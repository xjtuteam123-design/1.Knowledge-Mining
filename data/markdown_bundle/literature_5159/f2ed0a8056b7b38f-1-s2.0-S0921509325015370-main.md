ABSTRACT
Homogenization annealing reduces elemental segregation in high-entropy alloys (HEAs) and promotes uniform 
solid solution formation, thereby optimizing phase composition, stabilizing the microstructure, and enhancing 
both strength and plasticity. This study investigated the microstructural evolution, phase transformation, and 
mechanical properties of Co

Fe

characterization. The as-cast alloy exhibited high strength but low plasticity, with a coarse face-centered 
cubic (FCC) matrix (average grain size ~908.6 μ m) and 3.8 vol% of an irregular body-centered cubic (BCC) 
phase. With increasing annealing temperature and holding time, grain coarsening occurred, the fraction of low- 
angle grain boundaries decreased, and the pole density of FCC and BCC phases increased. At 900 
◦

from CP to DP. This transition was mainly attributed to the higher thermodynamic stability of the coarser bulk 
BCC phase formed via DP compared with the finer, needle-like BCC precipitates generated via CP. Compared 
with the as-cast alloy, all homogenized samples exhibited enhanced strength and elongation exceeding 25 %. The 
enhanced plasticity resulted from the activation of multiple slip systems, which facilitated more homogeneous 
dislocation motion. This study provides a cost-effective strategy for achieving strength – ductility synergy in 
CoFeMnNiTiAl HEAs and presents practical guidance for heat-treating high-strength, high-ductility components.
1. Introduction
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) redefine conventional approaches to 
alloy design. HEAs, composed of five or more elements in equimolar or 
near-equimolar ratios, exhibit high-entropy effects, lattice distortion, 
sluggish diffusion, and the “ cocktail ” effect. These intrinsic properties 
contribute to the excellent strength – ductility synergy, improved corro -
sion resistance, and high-temperature stability of the alloys [ 1 – 5 ]. 
Among HEAs, the Cantor alloy exhibits the potential for forming 
multi-principal element solid solutions, making the Co – Fe – Mn – Ni-based 
HEA system a research hotspot [ 6 – 9 ]. Studies have shown that the phase 
structure and mechanical properties of HEAs can be controlled through 
the addition of elements (such as Cu, Al, Ti, Nb, V, Si, C, N, and B) 
[ 10 – 15 ]. Owing to its strong negative enthalpy of mixing with Ni ( 22 
kJ/mol) and Co ( 19 kJ/mol), Al promotes ordering in the Fe – Ni – Co 
sublattice. This ordering drives a phase transformation from a single 
face-centered cubic (FCC) structure to a dual-phase body-centered 
(BCC)/FCC structure. In contrast, Ti increases lattice distortion energy, 
which promotes a dynamic multiphase balance, thereby enhancing 
strength and maintaining plasticity [ 16 – 19 ]. This study introduces 
bi-component Al/Ti additions into the Co – Fe – Mn – Ni matrix via a 
multicomponent synergistic regulation strategy. Through the synergistic 
effects of Al-induced BCC precipitation strengthening and Ti-enhanced 
lattice distortion, Co

After 900 – 2 tensile deformation, the Kernel average misorientation

heat treatment (as confirmed by the dislocation morphology in 
Fig. 13 g). Conversely, the KAM distribution for the 1000 – 2 condition 
( Fig. 13 c and d) indicates significantly improved uniformity. The 
extensive regions with high Schmid factor values confirm that high- 
temperature annealing activates multiple slip systems, promotes strain 
homogenization, and improves plasticity. Quantitative analysis reveals 
that the 1000-2 sample (1.1

high-temperature annealing promotes dislocation slip, activates addi -
tional slip systems, and reduces dislocation accumulation. Dislocations 
tend to accumulate along the boundaries of the BCC phase, forming 
dislocation walls ( Fig. 13 h). This is due to the preferential movement of 
dislocations along low-energy pathways at phase interfaces. This 
boundary-accommodated deformation reduces the overall resistance to 
dislocation motion and affects the yield strength [ 54 ]. In some cases, 
dislocation lines penetrate the phase boundary into the BCC phase 
( Fig. 13 i). These dislocations form pile-ups within the harder BCC phase 
( Fig. 13 j). The limited slip and dislocation accumulation in the BC phase 
contribute to a work-hardening effect. Although this phase plays a sec -
ondary role in overall deformation, the dislocation penetration mecha -
nism provides additional strengthening, which maintains the high yield 
strength of the alloy after homogenization. Notably, the dislocation 
penetration across phase boundaries observed in Fig. 13 i introduces 
additional slip paths, alleviates local stress concentrations, and pro -
motes the activation of multiple slip systems. This mechanism enables 
dislocation propagation into phases/grains with different orientations, 
further activating new slip systems. Moreover, dislocation penetration 
reduces deformation incompatibility between the FCC matrix and the 
BCC phase, thereby delaying the onset of strain localization. The synergy 
of these mechanisms significantly enhances the plastic deformation ca -
pacity of HEAs.

5. Conclusion
This study investigated the effects of homogenization annealing on 
the microstructure, phase evolution, and mechanical properties of 
Co
25
Fe
25
Mn
15
Ni
25
Ti
5
Al
5 
HEAs. The main conclusions are as follows. 
(1) The as-cast alloy comprises an FCC matrix and an irregularly 
shaped BCC phase. The BCC phase exhibits a volume fraction of 
3.8 % and an average grain size of 908.6 μ m. The alloy exhibits 
high strength but low plasticity ( σ
0.2 
= 436.36 MPa, δ = 11.98 %).
(2) Homogenization annealing preserves the dual-phase (FCC/BCC) 
structure. Increasing annealing temperature/time results in grain 
coarsening, a reduction in LAGBs, and an increase in the MRD of 
both phases. The needle-like BCC phases formed at 900 
◦
C 
gradually dissolve, while the irregular BCC phases expand via 
diffusion and coalescence, leading to increased size and reduced 
volume fraction. The precipitation mode of the BCC phase shifts 
from CP to DP.
(3) Under all annealing conditions, the alloy exhibits enhanced 
strength and ductility, with elongation exceeding 25 %. At 
1000 
◦
C for 2 h, the alloy achieves optimal strength – ductility 
synergy ( σ
0.2 
= 548.96 MPa, δ = 29.84 %). The enhanced 
ductility results from the activation of multiple dislocation mo -
tion mechanisms.
