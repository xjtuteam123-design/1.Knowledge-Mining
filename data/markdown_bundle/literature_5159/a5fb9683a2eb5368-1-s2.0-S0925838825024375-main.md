ABSTRACT
FeCrAl based alloys are considered as one of the most promising candidates for the advanced accident tolerance 
fuel (ATF) cladding in light water reactors (LWRs). The effects of Mo and Nb elements and ZrC nanoscale par -
ticles addition on mechanical property and thermal stability of Fe-13Cr-5Al alloys were investigated, and the 
strengthening mechanism was assessed by microstructure characterizations including TEM and EBSD. The 
strength of alloys has been effectively improved along with an acceptable ductility at temperatures from RT to 
800 
◦
C. Especially at 800 
◦
C, the ultimate tensile strength of FeCrAl-Mo-Nb-ZrC alloy has been improved to be 
131 MPa, which is 138 % higher than that of raw Fe-13Cr-5Al alloys, respectively. At the same time, FeCrAl-Mo- 
Nb-ZrC alloy maintains high strength and favorable ductility after an annealing at 1000 
◦
C for 20 h, indicating an 
ultra-high thermal stability. The enhanced high temperature strength and excellent thermal stability can be 
mainly attributed to the synergistic effects of the dispersion strengthening by nanoscale ZrC and alumina par -
ticles, solid solution strengthening by Mo and Nb elements and the grain refinement strengthening.
1. Introduction
Cladding materials, as the core components of the nuclear power 
reactor, directly determined the lifespan, safe reliability and economy of 
the nuclear power reactor [1] . As seen in the Japanese Fukushima 
Daiichi Nuclear accident in 2011, Zircaloy cladding used in light water 
reactors (LWRs) reacted with the hot steam under a high temperature 
condition ( > 800 
◦
C), generated a huge amount of heat and hydrogen, 
which led to the core melting and hydrogen explosion [2,3] . Hence 
exploring an advanced accident tolerance fuel (ATF) cladding material 
with lower oxidation rates in the high temperature steam environment is 
necessary.
Ferritic FeCrAl based alloys are currently considered as a replace -
ment alloy for the ATF cladding in LWRs owing to their excellent high- 
temperature water vapor oxidation resistance, good radiation resistance 
and corrosion resistance, stable thermophysical properties, and good 
mechanical properties compared with zircaloy cladding [4 – 9] . For 
example, the oxidation behavior of FeCrAl and Zr alloys after the high 
temperature excursions (1200 
◦
C for 2 h) indicate that FeCrAl alloys 
showed superior oxidation resistance compared to Zr-based alloys [10, 
11] . The oxidation rates of FeCrAl based alloys are two orders of 
magnitude slower than Zr alloys over a wide temperature range 
[12 – 15] . However, the tensile strength of raw FeCrAl alloys at 800 
◦
C is 
normally lower than 60 MPa, which directly reduces the safe reliability 
of the nuclear power reactor under the severe accident conditions [16, 
17] . In addition, due to the larger neutron absorption cross section of 
FeCrAl alloys, the thickness of tube wall needs to be reduced to as thin as 
0.5 mm to compensate for the neutron penalty [18] . Therefore, how to 
improve the high-temperature strength of FeCrAl alloys has been 
regarded as an important research topic.
As is well known, dispersion strengthening is regarded as an effective 
strategy for improving the high temperature strength and microstruc -
tural stability, which are attributed to the pinning effect of the addi -
tional second-phase particles on the dislocations and grain boundaries. 
For example, oxide dispersion strengthened (ODS) FeCrAl steels with 
different strengthening phases (such as Y

Similarly, solid solution strengthening can also make a contribution 
for improving the high temperature strength. It is found that smaller 
amounts of some high melting point elements such as Mo can enhance 
the high temperature strength of FeCrAl alloys owing to the solid solu -

mainly depends on the amount of Nb addition or the preparation 
methods. Hence to improve the high temperature strength of FeCrAl 
alloys, in this work, Mo and Nb elements as well as nanoscale ZrC par -
ticles were simultaneously introduced into FeCrAl alloys by employing 
the high-energy ball-milling technique combined with the spark plasma 
sintering (SPS). The microstructures, mechanical properties and the 
thermal stabilities of ZrC nanoparticles dispersion-strengthened FeCrAl 
alloys containing Mo and Nb were investigated.
2. Experimental
2.1. Materials preparation
Fe-13Cr-5Al-2Mo-1Nb-1ZrC (denoted by FeCrAl-Mo-Nb-ZrC, wt%) 
alloys were prepared via the high-energy ball-milling technique com -
bined with spark plasma sintering (SPS). High purity powders of Mo 
(average particle size ~2.0 μ m, purity ≥ 99.95 %), Nb (average particle 
size ~2.0 μ m, purity ≥ 99.9 %), ZrC (average particle size ~50 nm, 
purity ≥ 99 %) and Fe-13Cr-5Al (prepared by gas atomization method, 
average particle size ~10 μ m, purity ≥ 99.8 %) were weighed and mixed 
to acquire alloy precursors with the desired compositions. Here the 
chromium-to-aluminum (Cr/Al) concentration ratio in FeCrAl alloys 
was determined as 13:5 for further nuclear-grade FeCrAl alloy devel -
opment because of the optimal balance between oxidation resistance 
and radiation tolerance in FeCrAl alloys [31] . And the amounts of ZrC, 
Mo and Nb were determined as 1 wt%, 2 wt% and 1 wt% according to 
the optimized mechanical properties reported in the references, 
respectively [24 – 30] . The mixed powders were mechanically alloyed in 
a planetary ball mill for 24 h with a rotation speed of 320 rpm in 
high-purity Ar atmosphere. Then, the ball-milled powders were loaded 
in a graphite mold and sintered by spark plasma sintering method (SPS) 
in high-purity argon atmosphere. For SPS sintering, the samples were 
heated to 1150 
◦
C at a heating rate of 100 
◦
C/min, and held for 5 min 
under a pressure of 50 MPa, and then cooled down to room temperature 
at a rate of 100 
◦
C /min. The sintered sample with a relative density 
greater than 99.1 % was obtained with a diameter of 30 mm and a 
thickness of 7.4 mm. The sintered alloys were hot-forged at 1100 ℃ with 
a 60 % thickness reduction, and after that the relative density of 
FeCrAl-Mo-Nb-ZrC alloy is above 99.5 %.
In order to study the high temperature stability of microstructures 
and mechanical properties of FeCrAl-Mo-Nb-ZrC alloys, the samples 
were annealed at 1000 
◦
C for 1 h, 5 h and 20 h in high-purity argon 
atmosphere, respectively.
2.2. Microstructure and mechanical properties characterization
The tensile fracture surface of FeCrAl-Mo-Nb-ZrC alloys were 
examined by field emission scanning electron microscope (FESEM) 
(Sirion200, FEI, USA). The microstructures including orientation, grain 
size and distribution of FeCrAl-Mo-Nb-ZrC alloys were analyzed through 
the electron back scattering diffraction (EBSD, Oxford Instruments 
Nanoanalysis) patterns recorded using a HKL Nordlys EBSD system 
interfaced with a field emission gun scanning electron microscope (SEM: 
Sirion 200). Transmission electron microscope (TEM, JEM-2000 FX) was 
used to study the microstructure of FeCrAl-Mo-Nb-ZrC alloys, especially 
for the characteristic of second-phase particles in the matrix. Energy- 
dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS, INCA) analytical system installed 
on TEM was used to characterize the composition of nanosized particles. 
TEM samples were prepared by a Struers Tenupol-5 twin-jet electro -
polisher using 5 % HClO

4.2. High temperature mechanical properties enhancement of FeCrAl-Mo- 
Nb-ZrC alloys
It is reported that the UTS of Fe-13Cr-5Al alloy at RT and 400
◦
C is 
743 MPa and 592 MPa, respectively, both of which are high enough for 
application as cladding materials. However, their UTS at 800
◦
C is lower 
than 60 MPa, which reduce the safe reliability of the nuclear power 
reactor under the severe accident conditions. In a previous study, the 
UTS of Fe13Cr5Al-1ZrC alloy is 835 MPa at RT and 91 MPa at 800
◦
C 
[24] , which is about 12 % and 78 % higher than that of pure FeCrAl 
alloy. The strengthening mechanism in Fe13Cr5Al-1ZrC alloy is 
dispersion strengthening, which depends on ZrC nanoparticles dispersed 
not only at the grain boundaries but also inside the grains. By contrast, 
FeCrAl-Mo-Nb-ZrC alloy in this study has the highest UTS of 968 MPa at 
RT and 131 MPa at 800
◦
C. Comparing with the results at RT, the 
enhancement of UTS at 800
◦
C is more remarkable, which is almost 
138 % and 44 % higher than that of Fe13Cr5Al and Fe13Cr5Al-1ZrC 
samples, respectively. Based on these experimental results, it is 
concluded that the enhancement of high temperature mechanical 
property of FeCrAl based alloys results not only from the dispersion 
strengthening of high density ZrC and alumina nanoparticles but also 
from the solid solution strengthening effects of Mo and Nb elements. For 
dispersion strengthening, high density nanosized ZrC and alumina 
nanoparticles dispersed not only inside the grains but also in the grain 
boundaries. The former ones have the pinning effect on the dislocations 
and the latter ones have the pinning effect on the grain boundaries 
migration, which can both improve the tensile strength of FeCrAl based 
alloys during plastic deformation. Here it is important to point out that 
the original intention of the dispersion strengthening in 
FeCrAl-Mo-Nb-ZrC alloy in this study is mainly relied on ZrC nano-sized 
particles, rather than alumina particles. The appearance of alumina 
particles is detrimental to the steam oxidation resistance because of the 
composition decreases of Al elements soluted in FeCrAl based alloys. 
Hence in the future research, some measures should be taken to solve 
this problem, for example, on the one hand, the oxygen contents during 
the milling or SPS sintering processes should be strictly controlled to 
avoid the occurrence of oxidation reaction. On the other hand, the 
contents of aluminum in raw materials should be increased appropri -
ately to compensate for the decreases of Al elements. For solid solution 
strengthening, a small amount of Mo and Nb elements can cause big 
distortion of the matrix lattice and produce an elastic stress field to 
hinder the movement of dislocations for reinforcing the effect of solution 
strengthening. What is more, on the one hand, Mo elements could 
reduce the grain growth rate at a certain temperature, which is helpful 
for achieving fine-grained microstructure of FeCrAl-Mo-Nb-ZrC alloys. 
On the other hand, the pinning pressure of high-density nanoparticles 
dispersed on grain boundaries counteracts the driving force pushing the 
boundaries, which could also inhibit the grain growth during high 
temperature sintering process [35,36] . Hence the grain refinement 
structure of FeCrAl-Mo-Nb-ZrC sample is promoted by the synergistic 
effects of Mo and nanoparticles addition, which also play an important 
role for improving the strength of materials described by the Hall-Petch 
relationship.
Hence it can be concluded that the enhancement of mechanical 
properties including the yield strength and UTS for FeCrAl-Mo-Nb-ZrC 
alloy are mainly originated from the solid solution strengthening ef -
fects of Mo and Nb elements, the dispersion strengthening effects of ZrC 
and alumina nanoparticles and the fine-grain strengthening promoted 
by the synergistic effects of Mo and nanoparticles.

4.4. Expectation of FeCrAl-Mo-Nb-ZrC alloy
The optimized FeCrAl-Mo-Nb-ZrC alloy exhibits a UTS of 131 MPa 
and a TE of 20.4 % at 800 
◦
C, which are likely to satisfy the requirements 
of mechanical properties for advanced ATF cladding material. This 
paper can provide the experimental and theoretical supports for the 
mechanical property strengthening of the novel FeCrAl based ATF 
cladding in LWRs in future.
It is well known that one of the most important methods for 
designing radiation resistance materials involves introducing high 
density point defect recombination centers, referred as point defect sinks 
[40] . Numerous studies have verified that the introduction of nano -
particles can provide interfaces as effective sinks for irradiation-induced 
defects [41] . For example, Tungsten alloys strengthened by nanoscale 
ZrC particles exhibit enhanced irradiation resistance to neutron [42,43] . 
Therefore, it is expected that the addition of 1 % ZrC nanoparticles in 
FeCrAl-Mo-Nb-ZrC alloy could improve the irradiation resistance of raw 
Fe-13Cr-5Al alloy. Moreover, the additions of Mo, Nb elements and ZrC 
nanoparticles refine the grains, the large number of interfaces is also 
beneficial for the irradiation damage resistance. Combined with the 
good steam oxidation resistance ensured by the suitable mass ratio of Cr 
and Al elements in FeCrAl-Mo-Nb-ZrC alloy, it is expected that 
FeCrAl-Mo-Nb-ZrC alloy studied in this paper could be used in Gener -
ation IV reactors in the future, such as lead-bismuth fast reactor.
5. Conclusions
In this paper, solid solution strengthening elements such as Mo and 
Nb and dispersion strengthening nanoscale ZrC particles were intro -
duced into the raw Fe-13Cr-5Al alloys by employing the high-energy 
ball-milling technique combined with the spark plasma sintering 
(SPS). The mechanical properties of FeCrAl-Mo-Nb-ZrC alloys and the 
corresponding microstructures analysis were investigated. The main 
results could be summarized as follows: 
1) FeCrAl-Mo-Nb-ZrC alloy with the additions of Mo, Nb and ZrC 
nanoparticles in the range studied exhibits a high tensile strength 
and an acceptable ductility at each tested temperature (RT, 400
◦
C or 
800 
◦
C).
2) The UTS of FeCrAl-Mo-Nb-ZrC alloy measured at 800
◦
C is 131 MPa, 
which is 138 % higher than that of Fe-13Cr-5Al alloys.
3) FeCrAl-Mo-Nb-ZrC alloy maintains high strength and favorable 
ductility after annealing at 1000
◦
C for 20 h, indicating a high 
thermal stability compared with Fe-13Cr-5Al alloys.
4) The enhanced high temperature strength and excellent thermal sta -
bility can be mainly attributed to the synergistic effects of the 
dispersion strengthening by nanoscale ZrC and alumina particles, 
solid solution strengthening by Mo and Nb elements and the grain 
refinement strengthening.
5) FeCrAl-Mo-Nb-ZrC alloy with refined grains, higher strength, excel -
lent thermal stability and the potential high radiation resistance 
could be expected to be used in LWRs or even in Generation IV re -
actors in the future.
