ABSTRACT
Poor mechanical strength at high temperature is the key problem to limit the application of FeCrAl alloys as the
candidates for the accident tolerant fuel (ATF) cladding in LWRs. Fe-13Cr-5Al (wt %) alloys were strengthened
by adding solid solution element Mo and nanosized ZrC particles, and the strengthening mechanism was assessed
by microstructure characterizations including TEM and EBSD. Fe-13Cr-5Al-2Mo-1ZrC alloy has the highest ul-
timate tensile strength (UTS) and an acceptable ductility at each tested temperature (Room temperature, 400
◦
C
or 800
◦
C). Especially at 800
◦
C, the UTS of Fe-13Cr-5Al-2Mo-1ZrC alloy is about 124 MPa, which is 125 % and
34.7 % higher than that of raw Fe-13Cr-5Al (55 MPa) and Fe-13Cr-5Al-1ZrC alloys (89 MPa), respectively. Fe-
13Cr-5Al-2Mo-1ZrC alloy has the highest hardness of 306.3 HV, which is 12.8 % higher than that of Fe-13Cr-5Al
and 3.7 % higher than that of Fe-13Cr-5Al-1ZrC alloys, respectively. Furthermore, Fe-13Cr-5Al-2Mo-1ZrC
samples maintain high strength and favorable ductility after annealing at 1000
◦
C for 20 h indicating their su-
perior thermal stabilities. The excellent mechanical properties and superior thermal stabilities of Fe-13Cr-5Al-
2Mo-1ZrC alloy were not only attributed to the dispersion strengthen by nanosized ZrC particles, the solid so-
lution strengthening by Mo elements, but also the grain refinement structure promoted by the synergistic effects
of Mo and ZrC additions.

1. Introduction
Ferritic FeCrAl alloys, one of the most promising candidates for the
accident tolerance fuel (ATF) cladding materials used in light water
reactors (LWRs), have excellent high-temperature water vapor oxidation
resistance, better radiation resistance and corrosion resistance
compared with Zr cladding [ 1-3 ]. Many researches about the
high-temperature water vapor oxidation behavior of FeCrAl alloys and
Zr alloys indicate that FeCrAl alloys show excellent high-temperature
steam oxidation resistance to steam at 1000
◦
C [ 3-7 ], and furthermore
Pint et al. found that the oxidation kinetics of FeCrAl alloys were two
orders of magnitude below that of Zr alloys at high temperature of 1500
◦
C [ 8 ]. However, the high temperature mechanical strength of FeCrAl
alloys especially at 800
◦
C is normally lower than 60 MPa, which seri-
ously decreases its reliability under the severe accident conditions in the
nuclear power reactor and limits its use as the fuel cladding [ 9 , 10 ]. In
addition, the neutron absorption cross section of FeCrAl alloys is ten
times that of Zr-based alloys, the thickness of FeCrAl fuel cladding tube
needs to be reduced as thin as 0.5 mm to compensate the neutron pen-
alty, which is another reason for having to improve the high temperature
mechanical strength to compensate its thickness loss [ 11 , 12 ]. In a word,
the most urgent need is improving the high temperature mechanical
strength of FeCrAl alloys to satisfy the demands of ATF cladding.
As is well known, solid solution strengthening, fine grain strength-
ening and precipitation strengthening are commonly regarded as
effective strategies for improving materials strength. For FeCrAl based
alloys, it is found that addition of small quantities of reactive elements,
such as Mo [ 13 , 14 ], Nb [ 14-17 ], Y [ 18 ], Zr [ 19 ], etc., improve the high
temperature strength. Among them, Mo doping mainly acts as solid
solution strengthening element, however Nb doping does not only
enhance the solution strengthening but also promote the precipitation
strengthening because of Fe
2
Nb-type Laves phase particles precipitated
in FeCrAl alloys [ 14 ]. A previous study proved that FeCrAl alloy with 1.0
wt % Nb addition exhibited the highest ultimate tensile strength (UTS)
and an acceptable ductility at 700
◦
C, which were attributed to the
pinning effect of the dispersed dense and fine Fe
2
Nb-type Laves phase
particles, for example, the pinning effect on dislocations generates a
dislocation strengthening and the pinning effect on the grain boundaries
generates a fine-grain strengthening [ 17 ].
As Nb addition, Y and Zr mainly act as precipitation strengthening
elements and are used to prepare the oxide dispersion strengthened
(ODS) FeCrAl alloys with different strengthening phases, such as Y
2
O
3
and ZrC particles [ 19-21 ]. However, it is found that the coarser Y-Al
oxide particles (reaction of Al with Y) dispersed in ODS-FeCrAl alloys
with Y
2
O
3
particles can be detrimental to strength [ 22 ]. By contrast,
FeCrAl alloy with 1.0 wt % ZrC at 800
◦
C exhibits a higher tensile
strength because of the fine-grained structure achieved by the pinning
effects of ZrC nanoparticles [ 19 ]. In addition, ZrC has many other ad-
vantages, such as high melting point of 3540
◦
C, high hardness, low
thermal neutron absorption cross section of Zr and so on, all of which
make ZrC very attractive in the high temperature strengthening of
FeCrAl alloys.
Thus, in this work, in order to further improve the high temperature
strength of FeCrAl alloys, strengthening components such as Mo (solid
solution strengthening element) as well as nanosized ZrC particles
(precipitation strengthening phase) were both introduced into FeCrAl
alloys by employing the high-energy ball-milling technique combined
with the spark plasma sintering (SPS). The relationship between the high
temperature strength and strengthening components, their synergistic
effects between Mo and ZrC additions were investigated, and which are
helpful to clarify the high temperature strengthening mechanism.
2. Experimental
FeCrAlMo-ZrC alloy with a nominal composition of Fe-13Cr-5Al-
2Mo-1ZrC (wt %) was prepared by using the high-energy ball-milling
technique combined with spark plasma sintering method (SPS). High
purity powders of Mo (2 μ m, 99.95 % purity), ZrC (50 nm, 99 % purity)
and Fe-13Cr-5Al (33 μ m, 99 % purity, self-prepared by gas atomization
method) were weighed and mixed to acquire alloy precursors according
to the desired compositions. For comparison, Fe-13Cr-5Al and Fe-13Cr-
5Al-1ZrC alloys, denoted by FeCrAl and FeCrAl-ZrC respectively, were
both prepared by the same methods. The mixed powders were me-
chanically alloyed by high-energy ball milling in a planetary ball mill up
to 24 h at a rotation rate of 320 rpm. And then, the milled powders were
loaded into a cylindrical graphite die with an inner diameter of 30 mm,
and sintered by spark plasma sintering method (SPS) in a flowing high-
purity argon atmosphere. The samples were heated to1150
◦
C at a
heating rate of 100
◦
C /min, and sintered for 5 min under a uniaxial
pressure of 50 MPa, and then cooled to room temperature at a cooling
rate of 100
◦
C /min. At last, for achieving a higher density, the sintered
samples were hot-forged at 1100
◦
C with a ~60 % thickness reduction,
and after that the relative densities of above prepared FeCrAl based al-
loys were all above 99.5 %. The chemical compositions of above three
FeCrAl alloys are shown in Table 1 .
The prepared FeCrAl based alloys were cut and mechanically pol-
ished for tensile and microhardness testing. Tensile tests were carried
out by using an Instron-5967 machine with a strain rate of 1 × 10
– 3
s
-1
at
RT, 400
◦
C and 800
◦
C, respectively. The tensile testing samples were cut
with a cross-section of 1.5 × 1.0 mm
2
and a gage length of 5 mm. 5
samples were tested for each preparation condition. It was emphasized
that samples should be held at high test temperature for at least 20 min
to ensure a homogeneous temperature over the entire working length.
The microhardness testing was performed by Huayin 310HVs Vicker ’ s
microhardness tester. Similar with the tensile testing, at least 5 in-
dentations were made for each sample under a load of 200 g with a dwell
time of 15 s. The average value of test data was reported as the final
value for both tensile testing and microhardness testing.
The microstructures including orientation and grain size in FeCrAl
based alloys were analyzed through the electron back scattering
diffraction (EBSD). EBSD samples were cut and polished by using the
emery papers, polishing cloth and vibratory polishing in turn to obtain
high quality surfaces. EBSD mappings were measured with an acceler-
ation voltage of 13 kV and recorded using a HKL Nordlys EBSD system
interfaced with a field emission gun scanning electron microscope (SEM:
Sirion 200). The tensile fracture surface of FeCrAl based alloys were
examined by field emission scanning electron microscope (FESEM)
(Sirion200, FEI, USA). Transmission electron microscope (TEM, JEM-
2000 FX) was used to study the microstructure of the FeCrAl based al-
loys, especially for the characteristic of second-phase particles in the
matrix. TEM samples were prepared by a Struers Tenupol-5 twin-jet
electropolisher.
In order to study the high temperature stability of FeCrAl based al-
loys, FeCrAlMo-ZrC alloys were annealed at 1000
◦
C for 1 h, 5 h and 20 h
in high purity Ar atmosphere, respectively. It is of great significance to
investigate and evaluate their microstructure evolution, hardness and
tensile properties by using above characterization methods.

4. Discussion
In a previous study, the UTS of Fe13Cr5Al-1ZrC alloy is 835 MPa at
RT and 91 MPa at 800
◦
C [ 19 ], which is about 21 % and 69 % higher
than that of pure FeCrAl alloy. The strengthening mechanism in
Fe13Cr5Al-1ZrC alloy is precipitation strengthening, which depends on
ZrC nanoparticles dispersed not only at the grain boundaries but also
inside the grains. By contrast, FeCrAlMo-ZrC alloy in this study has the
highest UTS of 935 MPa at RT and 124 MPa at 800
◦
C. Comparing with
the results at RT, the enhancement of UTS at 800
◦
C is more remarkable,
which is almost 125 % and 34.7 % higher than that of Fe13Cr5Al and
Fe13Cr5Al-1ZrC samples, respectively. Based on these experimental
results, it is concluded that the enhancement of high temperature me-
chanical property of FeCrAl based alloys results not only from the pre-
cipitation strengthening of high density ZrC nanoparticles but also from
the solid solution strengthening effects of Mo elements.
For precipitation strengthening, high density nanosized ZrC particles
dispersed not only inside the grains but also in the grain boundaries. The
former ones have the pinning effect on the dislocations and the latter
ones have the pinning effect on the grain boundaries migration, which
can both improve the tensile strength of FeCrAl based alloys during
plastic deformation. What is more, the pinning pressure counteracts the
driving force pushing the boundaries, which could inhibit he grain
growth during high temperature sintering process and it is benefit for
improving the strength of materials described by the Hall-Petch rela-
tionship [ 25 , 26 ]. For solid solution strengthening, on the one hand, Mo
elements could reduce the grain growth rate at a certain temperature,
which is helpful for achieving fine-grained microstructure of FeCrAl
based alloys. On the other hand, a small amount of Mo elements can
cause big distortion of the matrix lattice and produce an elastic stress
field to hinder the movement of dislocations for reinforcing the effect of
solution strengthening.
Hence it is worth noting that except for the reported solid solution
strengthening effects of Mo elements and the precipitation strength-
ening effects of high density ZrC nanoparticles, the grain refinement
structure of FeCrAlMo-ZrC sample is promoted by the synergistic effects
of Mo and ZrC additions, which also play an important role for
enhancement of high temperature mechanical properties of FeCrAl
based alloys.
In addition, it is found that the UTS of Fe-13Cr-4.5Al-2Mo-1.5Nb
alloy is 692 MPa at RT and 95 MPa at 800
◦
C [ 17 ], the enhancement
of mechanical property also depends on solid solution strengthening
effects of Mo elements and the precipitation strengthening effects of
high density Fe
2
Nb Laves phases, however the grain size of Fe
2
Nb Laves
phases is about 200 nm, which is much larger than ZrC nanoparticles
added in this study. As well known, the precipitation particles with a
higher density and smaller size are necessary for having a better pre-
cipitation strengthening effect. Therefore, the ZrC precipitates particles
in this study contain both high density and a smaller size of 5 – 30 nm
resulting in highest strength improvement, while the strengthening ef-
fect of Fe
2
Nb Laves phases will be weaken in Fe-13Cr-4.5Al-2Mo-1.5Nb
alloy because of the coarser Fe
2
Nb particles.

[ 17 ]. Similarly, the ductility of Fe-13Cr-5Al alloys with different ZrC

5. Conclusions
FeCrAl alloy with the uniform dispersion of nanosized ZrC particles
and solid solution alloying elements Mo is prepared by employing the
high-energy ball-milling technique combined with the spark plasma
sintering (SPS), which exhibited refined grains, higher strength and
excellent thermal stability. The main results are summarized as follows:
1) FeCrAlMo-ZrC alloys in the range studied exhibit a highest tensile
strength improved by the addition of both Mo elements and ZrC
nanoparticles and have an acceptable ductility especially at elevated
temperatures.
2) The UTS of FeCrAlMo-ZrC alloy at 800
◦
C reaches 124 MPa, which is
125 % higher than that of raw FeCrAl alloys.
3) FeCrAlMo-ZrC samples maintain high strength and favorable
