Influence of Concentration Composition on Dispersion Strengthening 
Efficiency of V–Ta–Cr–Zr System Alloys
I. A. Ditenberg1 · I. V. Smirnov1 · K. V. Grinyaev1 · D. A. Osipov1 
Received: 30 January 2025 / Revised: 3 October 2025 / Accepted: 18 October 2025

Abstract
Using V–Ta–Cr–Zr system alloys, a study was carried out of the influence of the main phase-forming elements concentration 
on the features of the structural-phase state and the level of strength properties after various modes of thermomechanical 
treatment. It has been established that only part of the second phase, represented by the smallest nano-sized particles, is 
involved into dispersion strengthening and the effects associated with it. The transformation of the structural-phase state under 
the selected thermomechanical treatment conditions ensures high efficiency of dispersion plus substructural strengthening, 
which, in turn, helps to increase the strength of the alloy while maintaining a high level of ductility, both at room and at 
elevated temperatures. In addition, dispersity and volume-uniform distribution of stable ZrC-based particles released during 
thermomechanical treatment provide an increase in the thermal stability of the alloy due to the fixing of defect substructure 
elements by nanoparticles.
Keywords Vanadium alloy · Thermomechanical treatment · Dispersion strengthening · Mechanical properties ·

Introduction
One of the ways to increase the high-temperature strength 
and heat resistance of alloys based on refractory metals is 
to increase the efficiency of their dispersion strengthening, 
which is determined by the volume fraction, dispersity and 
nature of the distribution of non-metallic particles of the sec-
ond phases (carbides, oxides, oxycarbonitrides, etc.) based 
on interstitial elements (O, C, N) [1–7]. In addition, the ther-
mal stability of these particles themselves plays a significant 
role in providing high-temperature properties. This prob-
lem is especially relevant for vanadium-based alloys [8–20], 
characterized by a low melting point relative to the main 
representatives of this class (W, Ta, Mo, Nb), as well as low 
thermal stability of vanadium carbides [21].
The specificity of carbide strengthening of vanadium 
alloys is associated with the peculiarities of the interaction 
of carbon with vanadium. Compared to interstitial impuri-
ties such as oxygen and nitrogen, carbon is characterized by 
very low solubility in vanadium [ 21]. During the cooling 
process of the ingot, precipitation of second phases based 
on vanadium carbides (V2C) predominates. Thus, the low 
solubility of carbon and the specificity of phase formation 
in vanadium impose certain restrictions on the efficiency of 
dispersion strengthening of vanadium alloys with carbide 
particles. It should be noted that today there are no methods 
for additionally introducing carbon into the finished ingot, 
similar to internal oxidation [8].
An equally important feature is that during thermome -
chanical treatments (TMT) it is possible to transform only 
some part of the second phase precipitates into a nano-sized 
state [9, 10]. In fact, only a small volume fraction of these 
precipitates, from 3 to 20 nm in size, is involved into dis-
persion strengthening. In this regard, one of the ways to 
optimize the elemental composition of vanadium alloys is 
to increase the concentration of the active phase-forming

Thus, a general increase in the volume fraction of the stable

Vanadium alloys of the V–Ta–Cr–Zr system are consid-

V–Ti–Cr alloys [8, 15–20] due to the great potential of Zr-

and stability [9 , 10]. While V–Cr–Ti system demonstrates 
acceptable strength only up to 700–800 °C, Zr-containing 
vanadium alloys are characterized by higher strength proper-
ties at higher temperatures [10].
In this work, a study was conducted of the influence of 
the concentration of alloying elements on the efficiency 
of dispersion strengthening of vanadium alloys of the 
V–Ta–Cr–Zr system.
Materials and Methods
Vanadium alloys of the V–Ta–Cr–Zr system, produced by 
the JSC Bochvar High-Technology Research Institute for 
Inorganic Materials (Moscow), were used, the compositions 
of which are given in Table 1.
TMT-I and TMT-II modes were implemented similarly 
to what were used in works [9 , 10] devoted to V-Me(Cr, 
W, Ta)-Zr systems alloys. TMT-I consists of rolling by 
ε = 20% at room temperature and 1-hour vacuum annealing 
at 1000 °C. TMT-II, which provides nanostructuring of the 
initial heterophase structure, included homogenizing one-
hour vacuum annealing at 1400 °C and 4 cycles of “rolling 
at room temperature with deformation ε = 20% + vacuum 
annealing at 600–700 °C.” After TMT, stabilizing one-hour 
annealing in vacuum at 1100 °C was carried out.
Mechanical tests of dog bone-shaped samples with the 
gauge part dimensions of 13 mm × 2 mm × 0.7 mm were 
carried out by tension using PV-3012 M high-temperature 
vacuum testing machine at 20 °C, 800 °C and 900 °C in 
vacuum ~ 3 × 10−3 Pa at a speed of ≈ 3 × 10−2 mm/s. The 
measurements of mechanical properties were carried out 
from engineering stress–strain curves using at least 3 sam-
ples per point.
Electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) patterns were 
obtained in hexagonal dot mode using a Thermo Fisher 
Apreo 2 S (20 kV) scanning electron microscope equipped 
with an EDAX Velocity Super backscattered electron detec-
tion system. Kikuchi patterns formed by backscattered elec-
trons were automatically indicated by the EDAX APEX 
EBSD program. The resulting data array was processed 
using the EDAX OIM Analysis software.
Studies of the surface relief and fracture features of sam-
ples after tension at different temperatures were carried out 
using a Tescan Vega 3 SBH scanning electron microscope 
at an accelerating voltage of 30 kV.
Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) studies were 
carried out using JEOL 2100 (200 kV) electron microscope. 
Thin foils were prepared by double-sided sputtering with 
argon ions on “Model 1051 TEM Mill” ion milling and pol-
ishing system at an accelerating voltage of 5 kV.
Results and Discussion
Figure  1 shows the alloy 2 grain orientation maps, histo-
grams of the distribution of boundaries by misorientation 
angles and SEM images of the second phase particles in the 
secondary electrons after TMT-I and TMT-II after stabiliz-
ing annealing at 1100 °C.
Figure  1 a, d shows grain orientation maps of alloy 2 
after TMT-I and TMT-II. After TMT-I, some grains have 
a shape close to equiaxed and some are elongated, with a 
non-equiaxiality coefficient from 1:2 to 1:4 (Fig.a). Based on 
grain size, they can be divided into two main fractions: from 
a few micrometers to 30 µm and more than 30 µm. No gradi-
ent color is observed inside the grains, regardless of shape 
and size. The corresponding histogram of the distribution 
of boundaries by misorientation angles (Fig. 1b) shows that 
most boundaries are high-angle boundaries. Taken together, 
the results indicate that at a temperature of 1100 °C recrys-
tallization processes actively occur.
The grains orientation map of alloy 2 after TMT-II 
(Fig. 1d) differs significantly from the previous one. Firstly, 
large grains elongated in the rolling direction (RD) with a 
gradient color are observed, characteristic for the state after 
deformation treatment. Their width varies from 20 to 40 µm, 
and their length reaches several hundred micrometers. These 
grains alternate with clusters consisting of small grains 
(5–30 µm) with equiaxial or elongated in RD shapes. The 
non-equiaxiality coefficient of the latter varies in the range 
from 1:2 to 1:4. The corresponding histogram of the distri-
bution of boundaries by misorientation angles indicates an 
increase in the proportion of low-angle misorientations. It is

Electron microscopic images (Fig. 1c, f) show a line-by-

Using transmission electron microscopy, it was found that 
after TMT-II and annealing at 1100 °C the dissolution and 
redistribution of coarse particles is accompanied by the for-
mation of various fractions of fine particles. As can be seen 
even in bright-field images, their sizes can vary from a few 
nanometers to several tens of nanometers (Fig. 2). Particles 
larger than 100 nm can pin grain boundaries (Fig. 2b), while 
fine particles up to 20 nm can fix the elements of a defective 
structure like dislocation configurations such as networks 
and walls (Fig. 2c).
Dark-field images (Fig.  3a, b) present the high vol-
ume fraction of fine ZrC particles (from 3 to 20 nm) with 
almost uniform distribution. Such nanoparticles pin indi-
vidual dislocations distributed within the grain volume 
in a chaotic manner (Fig.  3a). To obtain a higher-quality 
image of the particles themselves (Fig.  3b), it is conveni-
ent to detect them in (200) reflections located close to the 
(110) matrix reflection (Fig.  3c). When tilting the sample 
in the TEM goniometer, such particles are clearly visible 
in the trace of the matrix contour when it leaves the precise 
reflecting position.
A developed defect structure remains in the material 
after stabilizing annealing at 1100 °C (Figs.  1d, 2c, 3a), 
even though this temperature is more than 0.6 of the mate-
rial melting point. It is well known that at such homolo -
gous temperatures, relaxation and even recrystallization 
processes should develop intensively; however, in this case, 
this is not observed, suggesting high thermal stability of the 
microstructure.

Comparison of stress–strain curves obtained at differ -
ent temperatures indicates a general tendency for strength 
to increase during the transition from TMT-I to TMT-II. 
In addition, a common feature of tensile deformation of 
alloy 2 after TMT-II, regardless of the test temperature, is 
a decrease in the relative contraction in the neck compared 
to TMT-I (Figure  4b, c, e, f, Table  2). It has been estab -
lished that the absolute and relative changes in the aver -
age values of the yield strength (Δ σ) for each temperature 
are individual. In particular, at 20 °C the average σ 0.1 value 
increases only by 24 MPa, which is 8%. At high tempera -
tures (800 °C and 900 °C), Δσ is 45.5 (27%) and 26.5 (15%) 
MPa, respectively. Against the background of the presented 
strength increase in the case of TMT-II, the dependence of 
ductility on the tensile temperature was revealed. At 20 °C, 
the average value of δ decreases by ~ 5%. Despite the maxi-
mum increase in the yield strength value, the ductility actu-
ally remains unchanged at 800 °C. At the same time, in the 
case of tension at 900 °C, against the background of a 15% 
increase in strength, the ductility decreases by half. It is 
important to note that after TMT-II, an increase in tensile 
temperature from 800 to 900 °C is accompanied by no more 
than a 5% decrease in strength, with almost no change in the 
ductility. Together with the results of structural investiga-
tions (Fig. 1d), this indicates a high thermal stability of the 
structural-phase state of alloy 2 after TMT-II.

interacted with carbon forming zirconium carbides) are 0.47

leads to an increase in σ by 27 MPa. But taking into account 
the fact that dispersion strengthening is provided only by 
nano-sized particles, it makes sense to estimate the volume 
fraction of fine-dispersed (f d, %) particles that provide the 
increment ∆σ.
As is known [5 ], the magnitude of Orowan stress 
(∆σ) is determined by the relation ∆σ ≈ Gb/λ , where 
λ ≈ R(2π)1/2(3f)−1/2 is the distance between particles lying 
in the dislocation slip plane, G is the shear modulus of the 
material, b is the Burgers vector of gliding dislocations, and 
f and R are the parameters of the heterophase structure that 
determine the volume content and sizes (radius) of particles 
of the second phases, respectively. Based on these formu-
las, fd can be estimated using the relation f d ≈ 2/3·π (R∆σ/
Gb)2. For vanadium a ≈ 0.303 nm, G ≈ 47.3 MPa [22], 
b = a/2 < 111 > ≈ 0.262 nm [22]. Let us recall that after 
TMT-II, the segregated particles of the stable phase based 
on ZrC are characterized by sizes from several nm to several 
tens of nm. In this case, dispersion strengthening is most 
effectively provided by particles with sizes from 3 to 15 nm, 
but it is not possible to take into account their fractional con-
tribution. In this regard, the estimates were carried out with 
a simplification, assuming that the entire contribution to the 
increment ∆σ is provided by particles of the same size. The 
obtained estimates of the volume fraction of fine particles 
(fd), depending on their sizes, are given in Table 3.

of defect substructure elements by particles helps to increase 
the thermal stability of the alloy.
Conclusions
Using the example of alloys of the V–Ta–Cr–Zr system, it 
is shown that when the strength properties of the material 
are increased by increasing the volume fraction of second 
phase particles, the effectiveness of dispersion strength-
ening and related effects is ensured by the smallest nano-
sized particles, the proportion of which represents only a 
small part of the precipitates. Dispersion and formation 
of a volume-uniform distribution of stable particles based 
on ZrC formed during TMT-II ensure the implementation 
of combined dispersion plus substructural strengthening. 
This transformation of the structural-phase state helps to 
increase the strength of the alloy while maintaining a high 
level of material ductility, both at room and at elevated tem-
peratures. In addition, due to the fixation of elements of the 
defect substructure, volume and grain boundaries by stable 
ZrC-based nanoparticles, the thermal stability of the alloy 
is increased. The strengthening effect depends significantly 
on the concentration of the main phase-forming element, so 
close attention should be paid to this.
