ABSTRACT
In this study, weldability and mechanical properties of dissimilar joint between Nb-1Zr and 321 stainless steel
produced by electron beam welding were investigated. The results indicated that the optimum parameters to
make this dissimilar joint possible are the electron beam o ﬀset of 50 –50 and the current of 13 mA. Because of
high melting temperature of Nb relative to 321 stainless steel and also high thermal conductivity of Nb, the
molten weld pool was drawn to 321 stainless steel side containing 85.1%Vol 321 stainless steel and 14.9%Vol
Nb. Non uniform regions were observed in the weld zone which were due to melt turbulence and its rapid
solidiﬁcation. The Fe
7Nb6 and Fe 2Nb intermetallic compounds formed in various regions of the weld zone di-
rectly govern the mechanical properties and weldability of the weld. Tensile strength of the weld was 170 MPa
which was equal to 60.5% of that in autogenous weld of Nb-1Zr alloy. Tensile strength of the autogenous weld of
Nb-1Zr alloy was 281 MPa. According to the Rosenthal's equations and measurements of the weld section, the
recrystallization temperature of the Nb-1Zr was identi ﬁed 1810 °C in the EBW condition.
1. Introduction
Niobium is a refractory metal which has high thermal stability and
good mechanical properties at elevated temperatures [ 1–4]. Generally,
Nb-based alloys are widely used in superconductors, high temperature
and corrosive environments [ 5,6]. These alloys are very reactive at
elevated temperatures. Thus, welding of these alloys should be per-
formed in vacuum or protective gas atmosphere. Welding of dissimilar
metals is always favorable for industries because of its potential ad-
vantages in saving materials and cost, reduction of weight, increase of
design ﬂexibility and improving performance of the product [ 7]. In
welding of dissimilar metals, particularly Nb alloys, there are many
scientiﬁc and technical challenges [ 8,9]. The techniques which are
available for dissimilar welding of Nb alloys include: explosive welding,
magnetic pulse welding, friction welding, brazing, laser beam welding,
electron beam welding (EBW), GTAW and plasma arc welding among
which two techniques of laser and electron beam welding generate
better results [ 10,11]. In dissimilar welding of Nb-alloys to steels, due
to high di ﬀerence in melting and evaporation points of the two metals
and also, intensive recrystallization of niobium at elevated tempera-
tures (particularly in HAZ), it is required to use the beams with con-
centrated energy which can melt both sides of the joint, simultaneously.
Formation of brittle and hard intermetallic compounds between iron
and niobium in these joints is very important issue which is one of the
most important factors making this dissimilar joint complex and di ﬃ-
cult [ 8,12].
EBW is a high power density welding method widely used in aero-
space, automobile, electronic and nuclear industries [ 13]. Due to very
high power density and consequently more depth to wide ratio in weld
zone, EBW creates very narrow HAZ and minimum residual stress in the
part [ 14,15]. Moreover, because of high concentration of electron
beam, the procedure can be controlled precisely. Presence of vacuum
chamber in this technique prevents formation of harmful oxide com-
pounds. In addition, fast cooling rate hinders bulky formation of brittle
intermetallic compounds [ 16,17]. Therefore, this technique is one of
the most appropriate techniques for dissimilar welding [ 18,19].
Budkin et al. [ 20] used EBW to join 2 mm Nb sheet to 12Cr8Ni10Ti
steel. They observed a continuous layer of Fe
2Nb at the interface of
contact in weld zone that deteriorated the mechanical properties of the
weld due to high brittleness and hardness. Zhao [ 21] used niobium as
an intermediate layer in hot-roll bonding of Ti6Al4V alloy and
Cr18Ni10Ti stainless steel. A layer of intermetallic compound between
Nb and weld zone was formed, in which several cracks were detected.
Vacuum soldering of Nb to 316 L was reported by Abhay Kumar [ 22]. In
the soldered sample, no brittle intermetallic layer formed in either side
of the joint, but the joint had a low tensile strength (about 120 MPa).

Compared to brazing and solid-state welding, fusion welding is suitable
for various joint types and obtains higher strength joints at elevated

Laser welding of Nb to 410 stainless steel associated with the Ni
intermediate layer formed by electro-spark deposition (ESD), was per-

of pure Nb to 410 stainless steel. K. Saito et al. [ 25] developed the
coalescence of stainless steel and niobium through hot isostatic pressure
(HIP). A. J. Palmer and C. J. Woolstenhulme [ 26] studied dissimilar

the eutectic alloy of Au-Ni (Au-18Ni) ﬁller to braze the metals. Sabirov
et al. [ 27] investigated the joint of Nb to S.S. created by explosive
welding method. They welded Nb tube to S.S. ﬂange through making
controlled explosion inside the Nb tube. After treatment of relieving
residual stress on welded parts, 250 MPa shear strength was achieved.
This special joint is used for accelerating cavities in superconductors.
Generally, in the context of dissimilar joint of Nb alloys to S.S.,
regarding very important applications that it has, too limited reports
have been published. In most of these reports, it is implied that its
application is restricted because of the complexity of methods, using a
variety of intermediate layers, low speed of the welding process, very
costly steps of the process and also impossibility of applying complex
geometries. In this work, for the ﬁrst time the dissimilar joint of Nb-1Zr
to 321 S.S. was made by electron beam welding, without using any
intermediate layer and only by adjusting the position of focused elec-
tron beam over each side of interface and controlling the weld dilution.
Oﬀsetting the beam position changes the contribution of base metals in
chemical composition of the weld metal in dissimilar beam welding
processes and consequently, results in di ﬀerent mechanical properties,
microstructure and weldability of welded joint [ 28].
2. Materials and methods
2.1. Base metals
In this study, 3 mm sheets of Nb-1Zr and 321 S.S. were used.
Chemical composition of the alloys was determined by atomic emission
spectroscopy (AES), as represented in Table 1.
To perform phase analysis of the substrate metals, XRD equipment
X'Pert MPD manufactured by Philips was used. To assess the tensile
properties of two substrate metals, ﬁrst, sheet samples were cut by
wirecut equipment according to ASTM E8/E8M standard. Then, they
were subjected to tension by the 50 Ton uniaxial tensile testing machine
SANTAM by strain rate of 0.001 s
−1 at 20 °C ambient temperature.
2.2. Electron beam welding
To determine optimum heat input for both sides of joint, some
coupons from both base metals with dimension of 15 × 15 × 3 mm 3
were prepared. On each of them, one welding line by di ﬀerent heat
input was created, so that the weldability and optimum heat input can
be extracted from obtained results. Afterwards, by considering appro-
priate amount of intermixing, dissimilar joint between Nb-1Zr and
321 S.S. was made. For this purpose, the joint was designed in such a
way that proportions of 75– 25, 50 –50, and 25– 75% of participation of
two base metals to be obtained and evaluated, subsequently the op-
timum proportion by which the weldability is acceptable could be
identiﬁed. Fig. 1 shows these three di ﬀerent modes of design,
schematically. Accordingly, 6 dissimilar joints were made as presented
in Table.2. 3 joints with optimum heat input for Nb alloy and 3 ones
with optimum heat input for 321 S.S.
Preparation of samples is especially important in welding two me-
tals by EBW. Due to narrow and low width and formation of keyhole in
EBW, the edges of joint should be completely aligned together and there
should not be any gap between the edges. To ful ﬁll this condition, 3 mm
sheets of two metals, were cut quietly ﬂat from the side that EBW to be
done by wire cut, polished by very ﬁne sand paper, and ﬁnally the joint
and surrounding areas were thoroughly washed by acetone. To design
and fabricate suitable ﬁxture for this joint, according to existing lim-
itations such as operability of joint design, being butt of welding geo-
metry, generating low residual stress, a bolt and nut ﬁxture were de-
signed and utilized. First, the sheets of two metals were drilled after
wire cut and polishing. Also, the steel straps of the ﬁxture with 2 mm
thickness were cut and drilled. Then, the two intended sheets were
stacked together and ﬁxed by book clip. 4 drilled steel straps were
placed in bottom and top of sheets and tightened by bolts and nuts.

2.3. Microstructural characterization and mechanical properties of the joint
To analyze microstructures of the joints, the samples were etched
individually in two steps, ﬁrst via a solution consisting of
4HF + 3H
2SO4 + 1HNO3 +5 H2O and second via a solution containing
68% glycerin, 16% HNO 3 and 16% HF for 40 s.

microscopy model-SZX2-ILLB and Philips scanning electron microscopy

were separated by wirecut and phase analyzed by XRD.

samples were drawn by uniaxial tensile test machine model-SANTAM at

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Characteristics of base metals
XRD analysis was used to identify the phases in Nb-1Zr alloy and
321 S.S. and obtained diagram is shown in Fig. 4. Referring to the XRD
diagram of the Nb-1Zr alloy, it can be observed that this alloy is
generally made of only one phase. Thus, the alloying elements in this
alloy are dissolved in the Nb-rich phase in the form of either interstitial
or substitutional (depending on alloying element) solid solution. Fih.4
also shows the result of XRD analysis of 321 S.S. considering the peeks
in the 321 S.S diagram it can be observed that this alloy is made of two
phases of delta ferrite and austenite. Depending on the mass ratio of
ferrite-stabilizer to austenite-stabilizer alloying elements, micro-
structure can be wholly austenitic or a mixture of austenite and ferrite.
This ferrite is originated from segregation of ferrite-stabilizer elements
(especially chromium) during solidi ﬁcation and thermomechanical
treatments. The solidi ﬁcation of 321 S.S. starts by formation of the
primary δ-ferrite which is the ﬁrst solid phase appears in the melt.
Continuing the solidi ﬁcation, due to instability of ferrite at lower
temperatures, austenite formed at the end of solidi ﬁcation at the
boundaries of ferritic solidiﬁ cation [ 29].

3.2. Autogenous welding on each of base metals
To obtain optimum parameters for dissimilar welding of Nb-1Zr to
321 S.S., several autogenous weld lines were created on each of Nb
alloy and 321 S.S. sheets by di ﬀerent welding currents. Then, according
to Table 2 and geometry of the joint, the current of 13 mA and 24 mA
were chosen as optimum currents for 321 S.S. and Nb-1Zr alloy, re-
spectively.
Fig. 6 shows the microstructure of the weld zone. In Fig. 6(a) cap-
tured from weld zone in the Nb alloy side, the cellular solidi ﬁcation
inside grains can be identi ﬁed from the spherical cavities. Principally,
Nb-based and all other refractory alloys will intensively undergo re-
crystallization and grain growth at elevated temperature. These grown
grains can be easily observed in its weld zone. Furthermore, inter-
cellular dendrites caused by micro-segregations appeared between so-
lidiﬁed cells [ 30]. Fig. 6(b) shows the weld zone in the 321 S.S. Two
phase microstructure of austenite and δ-ferrite is seen, so that the
austenite phase is predominant in the microstructure.
In the beginning of solidi ﬁcation, δ-ferrite is formed at ﬁrst and by
continuing solidi ﬁcation because of instability of ferrite at lower tem-
peratures; austenite is formed at ferrite's grain boundaries at the ﬁnal
stages of solidi ﬁcation. Both of lattice and skeleton morphologies of
ferrite can be observed in the weld zone of 321 S.S. Existence of ske-
leton ferrite is attributed to commonly high cooling rate in EBW. Lattice
morphology is formed instead of skeleton morphology due to limited
diﬀusion during ferrite - austenite transformation.

4. Conclusions
The dissimilar welding of 3 mm sheets of Nb-1Zr alloy to 321
stainless steel via the EBW technique with changing the o ﬀset of the
focused electron beam and heat input was studied. The most important
results are summarized below:
1- The microstructure of weld zone in the autogenously welded Nb
alloy revealed cellular solidi ﬁcation accompanying the formation of
intercellular dendrites caused by micro-segregation. In autogenous
welding of 321 stainless steel, the dual-phase of austenite + δ-fer-
rite was observed. Furthermore, due to the high rate of solidi ﬁcation
in EBW, both skeleton and lattice ferrite morphologies were de-
tected in the microstructure of the weld zone.
2- The sample S-Nb50-13 created under the parameters including:
oﬀset the electron beam with 50:50 deviation from the center and
13 mA current, presented the best results in terms of depth of pe-
netration, weldability and mechanical properties.
3- Due to the high melting temperature of Nb alloy compared to 321
stainless steel and also high thermal conductivity of Nb alloy, the
molten pool was drawn to the 321 stainless steel side and this side
participated more in the weld zone (asymmetric weld pool). 85.1
and 14.9%Vol of the weld pool was formed by 321 stainless steel
and Nb alloy, respectively.
4- The region in the weld zone adjacent to the 321 stainless steel was a
dual-phase region consisted of a primary Fe-rich phase and a eu-
tectic structure of Fe + Fe
2Nb. In the region far away from 321
stainless steel base metal, a region with entirely eutectic structure of
Fe + Fe
2 Nb was formed. The third region included a primary
Fe2Nb-rich phase and a eutectic structure of Fe + Fe 2Nb.
5- In the weld zone adjacent to Nb-1Zr alloy, there was a bright colored
narrow and continuous region next to the Nb-1Zr base metal, which
consisted of the Fe
7Nb6 intermetallic compound. The thickness of
this region was 50 μm.
6- The tensile strength of sample S-Nb50-13 was 170 MPa, which is
equal to 60.5% of that of autogenously welded Nb-1Zr alloy
(281 MPa).
7- All specimens of the tensile test were fractured from the weld zone
in vicinity of Nb-1Zr alloy. This is due to the formation of micro-
cracks within the hard and brittle Fe
7Nb6 compound formed in the
weld zone near the Nb alloy base metal. These micro-cracks acted as
nucleation and fracture inception sites in tensile testing of speci-
mens.
8- The island-like regions were observed within the weld zone in vi-
cinity of the Nb-1Zr base metal, in which the Nb content was more
and in the regions around them the Nb content was lower. Due to
the high turbulence of the melt during welding, parts of the wall of
Nb-1Zr base metal in the form of Nb particles were detached and
moved into the molten pool. Since the melting point of these par-
ticles is high and also the rate of solidi ﬁcation in EBW is high, these
Nb particles could not ﬁnd enough time to be melted and dissolved
in the molten pool and, after the end of solidi ﬁcation, remained in
the form of islands in the weld zone and in particularly in vicinity of
the Nb-1Zr alloy.
9- According to the Rosenthal's equations and micrographs of the weld
zone, the recrystallization temperature of Nb-1Zr alloy was de-
termined 1810 °C in the conditions of EBW; this value is presented
for the ﬁrst time for EBW of Nb-1Zr alloy.
