ABSTRACT
Cu-Cr-Nb alloys have garnered considerable attention for extreme thermal applications due to their exceptional 
combination of high strength and electrical conductivity. In this work, Cu-4 at.%Cr-2 at.%Nb alloy was fabri -
cated by selective laser melting and subsequently heat-treated to establish a multi-level dual heterostructure. The 
as-built material displayed a bimodal grain morphology, with coarse columnar grains occupying the melt-pool 
cores and fine equiaxed grains at overlapping boundaries, all reinforced by dispersed submicron Cr
2
Nb pre -
cipitates (~37.5 nm). Direct aging at various temperatures revealed that a 2 h hold at 450 
◦
C produced the 
optimal balance of hardness (~258 HV
0.3
) and conductivity (~46 % IACS). Under peak-aged state, the alloy 
exhibited an ultimate tensile strength above 900 MPa with ~14 % elongation, outperforming analogous Cu-Cr 
systems. Microstructural analysis showed that aging also generated coherent Cr nanoparticles (~4.9 nm), 
forming a dual-scale precipitate distribution. The results from in-situ tensile observations, together with electron 
microscope analysis, indicated that strain localized and stress concentrated near melt-pool boundaries, where 
fine grains and high dislocation densities promoted heterogeneous deformation. Quantitative strengthening 
calculations demonstrated that, in addition to the retained dispersion strengthening and dislocation hardening 
from Cr
2
Nb, significant Cr precipitation contributed to the overall strength. Furthermore, hetero-deformation- 
induced back stresses enhanced the work-hardening rate. These synergistic effects of bimodal grains and dual 
precipitates offer a promising strategy for designing next-generation copper alloys that achieve a superior 
strength-ductility-conductivity combination.
1. Introduction
Cu-Cr-Nb is a high-strength, high-conductivity alloy designed for 
extreme thermal environments [ 1 ]. However, Cr and Nb ’ s strong mutual 
affinity will lead to severe segregations of coarse primary Cr
2
Nb in 
conventional methods fabricated Cu-Cr-Nb alloys, thereby undermining 
the strength of Cu-Cr-Nb alloys and limiting their application [ 2 ]. For 
instance, melt-casting studies have shown centimeter-scale coarse sec -
ond phases under slow cooling (1 
◦
C/s) [ 3 ]. Although rapid solidifica -
tion via chill block melt spinning can suppress segregation and extend 
solid solubility [ 4 ], it requires complex post-processing steps such as 
high-pressure consolidation and metal cutting.
As a net-shape technology, selective laser melting (SLM) inherently 
provides extremely high cooling rates and localized melt pools, enabling 
fine microstructure and markedly enhancing alloy performance [ 5 ]. For 
example, SLM ’ s rapid solidification enabled the retention of ~47 nm 
amorphous nanoparticles within a Cu matrix, yielding a composite with 
> 1 GPa tensile strength and ~10 % elongation [ 6 ]. Similarly, SLM was 
used to fabricate a Cu-8 wt.% Fe alloy that suppressed liquid – liquid 
phase separation and confined second-phase particles to 10 – 300 nm, 
reaching strengths above 400 MPa [ 7 ]. In a Ti-Al-Nb alloy, SLM ’ s high 
cooling rate inhibited α
2
-phase decomposition, delivering superior 
properties compared to conventional processing [ 8 ]. Recently, research 
has extended to Cu-Cr-Nb alloy, including the fabrication of high- -
alloy-content Cu-9.6Cr-2.1Nb (at.%, the same as following) and 
comparative evaluations of different laser additive manufacturing

Laser-induced non-equilibrium solidification and element evapora -
tion have been reported to introduce local chemical fluctuations and 
microstructural inhomogeneities within SLM melt pools [ 10 ]. Unlike 
conventional routes, where heterostructures were created via process 
control plus post-annealing, SLM intrinsically generated these hetero -
geneous architectures [ 11 ]. Such heterogeneous architectures emerged 
in many systems — stainless steels [ 12 ], aluminum alloys [ 13 ], titanium 
alloys [ 14 ], high-entropy alloys [ 15 – 17 ], and superalloys [ 18 , 19 ] — and 
deliver exceptional combinations of strength, ductility, and 
work-hardening capacity unattainable by traditional methods. Widely 
dispersed regions of contrasting strength prove highly effective at 
overcoming the strength-ductility trade-off through heterogeneous 
deformation [ 20 ], making this strategy a promising route to simulta -
neously achieve ultrahigh strength and good ductility [ 1 ]. For example, 
eutectic high-entropy alloys with dual nanoscale phases accommodate 
plasticity in the soft matrix while the hard phase impedes dislocation 
motion, attaining an ideal balance of strength and ductility [ 21 ]. Simi -
larly, a CoCrFeMnNi composite engineered with a bimodal grain struc -
ture exhibited 22 % higher dynamic strength and excellent ductility 
compared to its homogeneous counterpart [ 22 ].
This work leveraged SLM ’ s ultra-high cooling rates to eliminate the 
coarse primary Cr
2
Nb phases found in as-cast Cu-4Cr-2Nb. By coupling 
SLM with direct aging, a dual-heterostructure was achieved, surpassing 
the performance of previous reports. The inherent small melt pools and 
rapid solidification produced a bimodal grain architecture — coarse 
columnar crystals in the melt-pool core and fine equiaxed grains at the 
pool boundaries — reinforced by dispersed submicron Cr
2
Nb. Systematic 
aging across different temperatures then precipitated nanoscale Cr 
alongside the existing Cr
2
Nb, forming a dual-scale precipitate network 
that pushed yield strengths beyond 904 MPa. The hierarchical mecha -
nisms — ranging from dislocations interactions with dual precipitates to 
heterogeneous deformation in the bimodal grain matrix — explained the 
alloy ’ s exceptional mechanical and electrical performance.

4. Discussion
4.1. Hierarchical microstructure formation and deformation behavior 
under varied strains
The microstructures of the AB and DAH Cu-Cr-Nb alloys exhibited 
pronounced spatial heterogeneity and bimodal distributions in grain 
size and precipitate size. On the top and side planes, MPBs and overlap 
zones contained finely equiaxed grains, while the melt-pool centers 
featured coarse columnar grains. In the AB state, the dispersed second 
phase consisted predominantly of 10 – 100 nm Cr
2
Nb particles. After 
aging, nanoscale Cr precipitates appeared alongside the Cr
2
Nb particles, 
creating a dual-scale precipitate architecture. Thus, the aged samples 
possessed a true “ dual-heterostructure ” , with bimodal heterogeneity in 
both grain morphology and second-phase distribution [ 40 ].
The unusual bimodal grain distribution arose from local variations in 
temperature gradient (G) and solidification rate (R). In classical solidi -
fication theory ( Fig. 8 ) [ 41 ], the cooling rate (G ⋅ R) set the overall grain 
size, while the G/R ratio dictated the growth mode — planar, cellular, 
columnar, or equiaxed dendritic [ 42 , 43 ].
In the melt-pool center, continuous laser heating generated a high G 
but a relatively low R, resulting in a high G/R ratio that promoted 
columnar or coarse equiaxed grains; however, the moderate cooling rate 
(G ⋅ R) yielded larger grain sizes. In contrast, at MPB and overlap regions, 
repeated scanning and remelting lowered G while R increased, due to 
heat conduction into previously solidified layers and nonuniform 
nucleation on the surface, thereby reducing G/R and raising G ⋅ R. This 
shift favored equiaxed dendrite nucleation and significant grain refine -
ment. In overlap zones, where multiple thermal cycles were 
superimposed, R was maximized and G minimized, producing an ul -
trafine equiaxed dendritic structure. Similar remelting-induced refine -
ment has been observed in overlapped melt-pool regions by Kumar et al. 
[ 44 ].
Furthermore, the high melting points and low diffusivities of Cr and 
Nb caused micro-segregation and “ constitutional supercooling ” at the 
melt-pool boundaries, which in turn boosted local nucleation rates and 
inhibited grain growth. These combined thermal and compositional ef -
fects produced the dual-scale, heterogeneous grain structure observ -
ed — an architecture known to enhance mechanical performance [ 42 ]. 
Similarly, Li et al. [ 45 ] reported ultrafine equiaxed grains at MPB in 
SLMed Cu-15Ni-8Sn alloys, driven by Sn-rich second phases.
The rapid thermal cycles in SLM not only determined grain 
morphology but also refined the second-phase particles. According to 
the calculated binary Cu-Cr
2
Nb phase diagram [ 2 ], at temperatures 
above 2000 K, Cr
2
Nb in the original powder can completely melt and 
form a homogeneous liquid phase with the Cu matrix. Upon cooling, 
prior to Cu solidification, fine Cr
2
Nb fragments re-precipitated. Owing 
to the extreme cooling rates of SLM, the resulting second phase was 
uniformly dispersed and significantly finer than the centimeter-scale 
precipitates typical of cast alloys or the micron-sized particles 
commonly observed in L-DED alloys [ 46 , 47 ]. Moreover, intense Mar -
angoni convection within the melt pool not only facilitated particle 
rearrangement [ 48 , 49 ] but also fragmented the second phase, further 
modifying its morphology [ 50 ]. Even when the original micron-scale 
Cr
2
Nb particles were only partially dissolved, intense Marangoni flows 
predominantly sheared them into tens-to-hundreds-nanometer frag -
ments [ 46 , 47 , 51 ]. These transformations were clearly evidenced by the 
contrast between Figs. 1e and 4 c. Additionally, the extreme cooling rates

4.3. Strain partitioning, hetero-deformation-induced hardening, and the 
bauschinger effect
Under the uniaxial tension of Cu-Cr-Nb specimens, the softer overlap 
zones were constrained by neighboring harder regions. Dislocations 
piled up at these interfaces, generating long-range back stresses that 
impeded further slip in the softer zones until the harder regions them -
selves began to yield at higher strain. Consequently, the entire structure 
work-hardened until dislocations could overcome these back stresses, 
resulting in a marked increase in the Cu-Cr-Nb alloy ’ s yield strength 
[ 86 ].
To probe the origins of this pronounced strain hardening and 
quantify the back-stress contribution, the LUR tests ( Fig. 12 a – c) were 
conducted to reveal the Bauschinger effect arising from internal stresses 
and dislocation accumulation. All Cu-Cr-Nb specimens exhibited clear 
hysteresis loops ( Fig. 12 b) — evidence of inhomogeneous deformation 
[ 87 ]. During unloading, reverse plastic flow ( σ
rev
) was initiated even 
while the nominal stress remained tensile. As strain increased, loop 
areas grew in both the AB and DAH states, indicating a strengthening 
Bauschinger response. Notably, the heterogeneous-deformation-induced 
(HDI) back stress also rose after aging: in the AB state, back stress near 
yield reached only ~248.5 MPa (52.6 % of flow stress), whereas after PA 
it climbed to 461.4 MPa (56.0 %) and, under higher strains, peaked at 
60.7 % ( Fig. 12 c). This amplified back stress highlighted how the 
dual-scale microstructure drove outstanding strain hardening — a key 
factor in the alloy ’ s high work-hardening coefficient [ 88 ]. The results 
demonstrated that aging further amplified the HDI effect during plastic 
deformation [ 86 ]. What ’ s more, after tensile loading, the dislocation 
density increased dramatically. Dislocation entanglement and accumu -
lation transformed many LAGBs into HAGBs, especially at melt-pool 
overlaps, where dense HAGBs further impeded slip. Once GNDs which 
were required to accommodate local strain gradients saturated these 
boundaries, they diffused into the columnar melt-pool grains, enhancing 
HDI hardening and markedly improving their strain-hardening capacity 
[ 88 ].

initiated at these high-stress concentration sites, ultimately leading to 
fracture ( Fig. 13c4 ).
