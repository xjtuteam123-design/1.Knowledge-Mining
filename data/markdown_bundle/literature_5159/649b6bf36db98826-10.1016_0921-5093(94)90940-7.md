High temperature performance of dispersion-strengthened A1-Ti alloys

Kwang-Min Lee* and In-Hyung Moon

Abstract 
Dispersion-strengthened A1-Ti alloys were prepared by the mechanical alloying (MA) process and hot consolidation 
method, and their high temperature mechanical properties and oxidation properties were investigated. The tensile 
strengths of MA AI-Ti alloys were substantially higher than those of conventional AI alloys such as 7075 and 2024 
grades at temperatures above 150 °C. The compressive strength and the yield strength of MA Al-8wt.%Ti at 350 °C with 
a strain rate of 1 × 10 4 s-~ were 233 MPa and 186 MPa respectively. The measured parabolic rate constant kp in 
oxidation of the MA Al-8wt.%Ti alloy at 600 °C was a much lower value, approximately 1.12 x 10-~3 g2 cm-4 s t, which 
revealed that the oxidation resistance of the MA alloy was much better than that of the 2024-TB AI alloy. 
1. Introduction 
The role of dispersoids has been focused on by a 
number of researchers as a key point for high tempera- 
ture applications of dispersion-strengthened alumin- 
ium alloys, because these alloys have usually exhibited 
better high temperature strength than conventional 
precipitation-hardened aluminium alloys (for example, 
the 2XXX or 7XXX series alloys). The dispersoid 
should be thermally stable and insoluble in the matrix 
even at temperatures near the melting point, and thus 
should act as barriers to dislocation motion to sustain 
their high temperature strength [1, 2]. 
Since the Al-rich intermetallic compound A13Ti has 
a low density (3.3 g cm-3), a relatively high melting 
point (1350 °C) and high elastic modulus (166 GPa) 
[3-5], titanium has been selected as the preferred alloy- 
ing element. Mechanical alloying (MA) of AI-Ti alloys, 
since it is a solid state process, offers the unique advan- 
tage of producing a homogeneous and fine dispersion 
of the thermally stable AI3Ti phase, which is an in- 
coherent tetragonal equilibrium phase, D022 type [6, 7]. 
We have observed the formation of nanometre-sized 
A13Ti intermetallic phases and discussed lattice match- 
ing between the intermetallic compound and the alu- 
minium matrix in our previous papers [8, 9]. 
In the present study, the AI-Ti alloys were prepared 
by the MA process and hot consolidation method, and

2. Experimental procedure 
Aluminium powder used for the experiment was 
supplied by Korea Nonferrous Metal Powder Co. It 
had an average particle size of 59 ~tm, with a purity of 
99.5% and a morphology of spheroidal shape. Tita- 
nium powder was of an irregular particle shape with a 
minimum purity of 99%, supplied by Johnson Matthey. 
8 and 12 wt.% Ti powders were added for the fabrica- 
tion of the MA AI-Ti alloys. MA was accomplished by 
using a batch attritor of 750 cm 3 capacity, a high energy 
ball mill, with 3/16 in stainless steel balls. The ratio of 
balls to powder was kept at 65 to 1. The homogeneous 
AI-Ti composite powders were prepared by the fol- 
lowing MA processing variables: addition of 1.5 wt.% 
stearic acid as a process control agent (PCA), milling 
velocity of 400 rev min- 1 and the milling time of 15 h. 
The MA AI-Ti powders were consolidated either 
by hot pressing or hot extrusion. After degassing treat- 
ment at 430 °C in a vacuum of better than 1.3 Pa for 
1 h, the cold compacted powders were subsequently 
hot pressed under a pressure of 200 MPa for 1 h. The 
hot extrusion was performed in a 200 tonf extrusion 
press at temperatures between 450 °C and 480 °C with 
a extrusion rato of 25 : 1. The cans were extruded into a

4. Conclusions 
(1) The tensile strengths of MA AI-8wt.%Ti and 
Al-12wt.%Ti alloys at 350 °C were 186 MPa and 232 
MPa respectively, exhibiting substantially higher 
strength than conventional A1 alloys such as 7075 and 
2024 grades at temperatures above 150 °C. 
(2) The compressive strength and the yield strength 
of MA AI-8wt.%Ti at 350 °C with a strain rate of 
1 x 10 -4 s-l were 233 MPa and 186 MPa respectively. 
(3) The measured parabolic rate constant kp in 
oxidation of the MA AI-8wt.%Ti alloy at 600 °C was a 
much lower value, approximately 1.12x10 -13 g2 
cm 4 s ~, which revealed that the oxidation resistance 
of the MA alloy was much better than that of the 2024- 
TB A1 alloy.
