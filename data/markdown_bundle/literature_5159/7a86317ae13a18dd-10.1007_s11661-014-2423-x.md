Microstructural Evolution and Mechanical Behaviors
of an Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Hf Alloy with 2 and 17 at. pct Cr
Additions at Room and/or High Temperatures
CHUNYAN YANG, LINA JIA, CHUNGEN ZHOU, HU ZHANG, and JIANGBO SHA
X-ray diﬀraction analysis, scanning electron microscopy, and transmission electron microscopy
were used to investigate the microstructures and orientation relationships (ORs) of Nb-16Si-
22Ti-2Al-2Hf-(2,17)Cr alloys (hereafter referred to as 2Cr and 17Cr alloys, respectively). The
mechanical properties of the two alloys at room and/or high temperatures were compared. The
2Cr alloy comprised Nb
SS and (a + b)-Nb5Si3 phases, while the 17Cr alloy consisted of Nb SS,
(a + b)-Nb5Si3 and Laves Cr 2Nb phases with a C15 structure. The b-Nb5Si3 and Laves Cr 2Nb
phases exhibited variable ORs with respect to the Nb SS phase. The Laves Cr 2Nb phase was
found to play a negative role on the fracture toughness at room temperature and on the
compressive strength at temperatures from 1523 K to 1623 K (1250 /C176 C to 1350 /C176 C). The frac-
ture toughness and the compressive yield strength at 1623 K (1350 /C176 C) both decreased from 14.4
to 10.3 MPa m
1/2 and from 300 to 85 MPa, respectively, when the nominal Cr content increased
from 2 to 17 at. pct. Finally, the fracture modes of these typical Nb SS/Nb5Si3 and NbSS/Nb5Si3/
Cr2Nb microstructures under bending and compression conditions at room and high temper-
atures were investigated and discussed.

I. INTRODUCTION
THE eﬃciency of jet engines depends strongly on the
highest temperature in the engines, i.e., the inlet tem-
perature of the high-pressure turbine. Recent researches
have shown that Nb-Si-based alloys have great potential
for overcoming the operating temperature barrier of Ni
superalloys and of improving the eﬃciency of jet
engines.
[1,2] Nb-Si-based alloys usually comprise ductile
Nb solid solutions (Nb SS) and stiﬀening Nb/Si silicides.
The addition of Cr forming Laves Cr 2Nb phase
improves the oxidation resistance. [3,4] In order to obtain
an adequate combination of properties, alloying ele-
ments, such as Mo, W, Ti, Cr, Al and Hf, have
frequently been added to the Nb-Si binary. Subse-
quently, the proportion of the Nb
SS,N b 5Si3, and Laves
Cr2Nb phases was adjusted and excellent toughness,
strength, and oxidation resistance at high temperatures
have been achieved separately.
[5–8]
The high-temperature strength of Nb-Si-based alloys
that is relative to the temperature capability is a
preferential issue to develop in these structural alloys.
W and Mo were found to be the most eﬃcient solid-
solution strengthening elements for Nb
[5,6] and the
strength has been improved signiﬁcantly by the addition
of W and Mo into the eutectic composition of the
Nb-10Ti-18Si ternary.
[6] With a 10 at. pct Mo plus
15 at. pct W addition, the as-cast Nb-10Ti-18Si alloy
showed yield compressive strength of about 350 MPa,
even at 1773 K (1500 /C176 C), while the compressive
strength was greater than 650 MPa for the directionally
solidiﬁed sample.
Alloying Nb-Si-based alloys with the elements Hf, Cr,
Al, and Fe might reduce oxygen diﬀusion and solubility
in Nb or form the oxidation-resistant phase Cr
2Nb,[9,10]
which might increase the environmental stability at high
temperatures. Two goals have been established for
Nb-Si-based alloys with higher Cr contents. The short-
term goal corresponding to a material thickness loss of
200 lm in 10 hours air exposure at 1643 K (1370 /C176 C)
has been successfully achieved in an Nb-Si-Cr-Ti-Hf-
Al-Ge
[8,9] alloy with an Nb SS/Nb5Si3/Cr2Nb microstruc-
ture. A further order of magnitude reduction in the
material thickness loss upon oxidation is still required [10]
in order to achieve the long-term goal, i.e., a material
thickness loss of 25 lm in 100 hours air exposure at
1588 K (1315 /C176 C).
It has been reported that the elements Ti and Hf,
directional solidiﬁcation technology, [6,11,12] as well as
thermo-mechanical processing (such as hot extru-
sion[13,14]), played a key role in the improvement of
toughness of Nb-Si-based alloys. Ti and Hf are the only
alloying elements that do not increase the brittle-to-
ductile transition temperature of Nb
[15]; on the other
hand, they are toughening and ductilising elements for
Nb. An improvement in the fracture toughness of
Nb-42.5Ti-15Si and Nb-40Ti-15Si-5Al alloys up to a
range of 13 to 16 MPa m
1/2 has been obtained, mainly
due to the addition of 40 at. pct of the toughening

II. EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURES
The purity of all raw elements used was 99.9 pct (in
mass) or higher (Nb 99.95 pct, Si 99.99 pct, Ti
99.95 pct, Al 99.99 pct, Cr 99.95 pct, Hf 99.9 pct).
Button ingots, each with a weight of 100 g, were
prepared by vacuum arc melting. The buttons were
heat-treated at 1648 K (1375 /C176 C) for 100 hours, fol-
lowed by furnace cooling. The microstructures of the
polished specimens were observed using scanning elec-
tron microscopy (SEM) (JEOL JXA-8100) in a back-
scattered mode, and the phase surface fraction was
statistically determined by quantitative metallographic
analysis of microstructure images taken in the BSE
mode. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) sam-
ples, 0.5 mm in thickness, were cut and then mechan-
ically ground to 50 lm. Foils, 3 mm in diameter, were
obtained from the thinned samples by punch and then
thinned using a GL-696F ion-milled instrument at an
angle of 15 deg to 20 deg with an applied voltage of
6 kV, current 0.15 mA to perforate. X-ray diﬀraction
analysis (XRD) analysis was performed on ingot sam-
ples to identify the phase constitutions using Cu K
(k = 0.15405 nm) radiation at 40 kV and 40 mA
(Rigaku D/Max 2500PC). Diﬀraction patterns, high-
resolution TEM (HRTEM) lattice images, and energy-
dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDXS) spectra were
obtained in a JEOL JEM-2100F microscope, to conﬁrm
crystal structure and compositions of phases, respec-
tively. Orientation relationships (ORs) were identiﬁed
by selected-area diﬀraction patterns (SADPs).
Single-notched 3-point-bending specimens 30 mm in
length, 6 mm in width, and 3 mm in thickness were used
for the fracture toughness measurements. A notch of up
to 3 mm was introduced by electrical discharge machin-
ing with a 0.2-mm diameter wire. The 3-point-bending
tests were conducted on an Instron 8801 testing machine
with a loading span of 25 mm and a crosshead dis-
placement rate of 0.1 mm/min. The compressive tests at
1523 K and 1623 K (1250 /C176 C and 1350 /C176 C) were con-
ducted in an argon atmosphere at a strain rate of
3 9 10
/C0 4 s/C0 1 using a Gleeble 1500 testing machine. The
temperature increase rate was 200 K (200 /C176 C)/min and
the specimens were held for 5 minutes at the testing
temperature, prior to testing, in order to homogenize the
temperature throughout the heating area. The compres-
sive cylinders were 8 mm in diameter and 12 mm in
length. All data (toughness, strength, phase composi-
tion, and fraction) for each alloy were obtained by
taking an average value of three to ﬁve samples.
III. RESULTS
A. Phase Constitution and Microstructure
Typical X-ray diﬀraction patterns of the two heat-
treated alloys are shown in Figure 1. It should be
pointed out that the constituent phases in the Nb-16Si-
22Ti-2Al-2Hf-(2, 17)Cr alloys are the multi-component
phases with solutes of Ti, Al, Cr, and Hf (but not binary
phase), and the X-ray diﬀractive patterns were detected
using alloy ingot (not alloy powder), therefore, the
diﬀractive peaks of the multi-component constituent
phases do not fully overlap with those of the binary
phases (ICDD ﬁles). For some diﬀraction peaks of the
silicides (a-Nb
5Si3 and b-Nb5Si3 phases) and the Cr 2Nb
phase are close to or even overlap with each other, all
these possible phases are marked on the X-ray diﬀrac-
tion proﬁles, and a primary judgment for the phase
constitutions is made here. It is clear that independent
peaks from the Nb solid solution Nb
SS, a- and b-silicide
(Nb)5Si3, and Laves (Cr) 2(Nb) phase exist in the 17Cr
alloy. The parentheses around Nb in the (Nb) 5Si3 and
(Cr)2(Nb) phases mean that Nb is partly replaced by Ti
and Hf, forming an (Nb, Ti, Hf) 5Si3 or (Cr)2(Nb, Ti, Hf)
phase, while those on Cr in the (Cr) 2(Nb) phase mean
that the site of Cr can be partially occupied by Si. For
convenience, the parentheses are going to be omitted. It
is recognized that the Nb
5Si3 may contain a-type and
b-type in the 17Cr alloy. However, the diﬀractive peaks
of the b-Nb5Si3 phase are dominant, because the peak
numbers and intensities of the b-Nb5Si3 phase are

IV. DISCUSSION
In this investigation, the phase constitution and the
microstructure of Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Hf alloy are chan-
ged dramatically (see Figures 1, 2) as the nominal Cr
content is jumped from 2 to 17 at. pct. These metallur-
gical changes aﬀect the behaviors of the current alloys.
A rule-of-mixtures (ROM) relating to phase fractions
and properties of the alloys with the Nb
SS/silicides
microstructure has been proposed primarily to estimate
the bulk properties, [5,15,28] in which the phase morphol-
ogy is not considered because the geometric parameters
of the constituent phase are complex and unavailable.
With a higher fraction of 70 pct of the brittle interme-
tallic compounds Cr
2Nb and Nb 5Si3, the 17Cr alloy
undoubtedly shows lower toughness than the 2Cr alloy
with a ductile Nb
SS-dominant microstructure of 60 pct
in fraction. Substituting the fractions of the Cr 2Nb
phase (22 pct), the Nb 5Si3 phase (48 pct) and the Nb SS
phase (30 pct), the toughness of 1.4 MPa m 1/2, for the
Cr2Nb phase, 3 MPa m 1/2 for the Nb 5Si3 phase and
30 MPa m 1/2 for the Nb-13Cr-30Ti alloy [15] (this com-
position is very close to that of the Nb SS phase in the
17Cr alloy) to the ROM, the toughness of 10.8 MPa m 1/
2 for the 17 Cr alloy is obtained, which is very consistent
with the measured data in this work. Again using the
ROM, the estimated toughness of the 2Cr alloy is about
19.1 MPa m 1/2, which is higher than the measured data
by about 4.7 MPa m 1/2. It is noticed that the Nb SS phase
in the 2Cr alloy has a Ti content of 23.5 at. pct; thus, its
toughness should be lower than the Nb-13Cr-30Ti alloy
with a 30 at. pct Ti, because Ti is a strong toughening
element for Nb.
[15,16] Therefore, using the ROM and the
toughness data of the Nb-13Cr-30Ti alloy for the
toughness estimation of the 2Cr alloy is overestimated.
Interestingly, the role of the C15 Cr 2Nb phase on
strength is found to be negative at 1523 K (1250 /C176 C) and
above. It was reported that the compressive strength of
the Cr 2Nb binary is about 300 MPa at 1673 K
(1400 /C176 C),[21] which is higher than that of the Nb SS/
Nb5Si3 microstructure (see Table I). The Cr 2Nb binary
should be a strengthening phase for the Nb-Si-based
alloys. Therefore, with a higher fraction of the stiﬀening
Nb
5Si3 and Cr2Nb phases, the 17Cr alloy should exhibit
greater strength at 1523 K (1250 /C176 C) and above, than
the 2Cr alloy with a lower fraction of the stiﬀening
Nb
5Si3 and Cr 2Nb phases. However, the results show
the opposite (see Table I). In this work, Ti, Hf, Al, and
Si solutes into the Cr 2Nb phase, resulting in a sharp
decrease of the melting point of this multi-component
Cr
2Nb phase, from 2043 K (1770 /C176 C) of the binary
Cr2Nb phase [18] to a temperature lower than 1773 K
(1500 /C176 C).[29] This suggests that at the testing tempera-
ture of 1523 K (1250 /C176 C) and above (closer to the
melting point of the multi-component Cr 2Nb phase), the
multi-component Cr 2Nb phase might become a soft
zone compared with the Cr 2Nb binary and thus,
decrease the bulk strength. On the other hand, the Cr
additions weaken the Nb-Si alloys at high temperatures.

V. CONCLUSIONS
A study of the microstructural evolution and mechan-
ical behaviors of the Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Hf-(2, 17)Cr
alloys has been made. The major conclusions are
summarized as follows:
1. With a 17 at. pct Cr addition, the Nb-16Si-22Ti-
2Al-2Hf alloy consists of the Nb
SS,N b 5Si3 and
Laves C15 Cr 2Nb phases; there is no Cr 2Nb phase
for a 2 at. pct Cr addition. The Nb 5Si3 and Cr 2Nb
phases exhibit variable ORs with the Nb SS phase.
2. The 2Cr alloy with a ductile Nb SS-dominant micro-
structure shows higher room-temperature toughness
and strength at 1523 K and 1623 K (1250 /C176 Ca n d
1350 /C176 C) than the 17Cr alloy with a silicide and
Laves-dominant microstructure. The multi-compo-
nent Cr
2Nb phase is detrimental to fracture tough-
ness and high-temperature strength.
3. Under bending, the stiﬀening Nb 5Si3 and Laves
Cr2Nb phase fractured in a brittle mode. The slight
river pattern appearance on the Nb SS phase surfaces
revealed a cleavage mode of failure.
4. Under high-temperature compression, collapse of
the stiﬀening Nb 5Si3 silicide and the Laves Cr 2Nb
phase dominates the failure of the Nb SS/Nb5Si3 and
NbSS/Nb5Si3/Cr2Nb microstructures. Cracks mainly
propagate within the Nb 5Si3 phase or the Nb 5Si3/
Cr2Nb mixture zone and they sometimes bowl the
NbSS phase or spread along the Nb SS/Nb5Si3 and
NbSS/Cr2Nb interfaces.
