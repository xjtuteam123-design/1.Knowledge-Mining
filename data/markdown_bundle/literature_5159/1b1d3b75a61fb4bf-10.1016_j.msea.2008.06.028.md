abstract
The effects of of C addition on microstructure and properties of Ni–Si–Cr ternary alloys were investigated.
The alloys were fabricated using self-propagating high-temperature synthesis (SHS) casting route. It was
found that C was mainly presented in the form of solid solution and Cr
XCY hard particles (Cr23C6 and Cr7C3).
Microstructure and properties of the alloys varied with the C fraction. The alloy containing 1.14 at.% C
showed ﬁne and uniform microstructure. Bending strength, compressive strength and fracture toughness
of the alloys reached the maximum value in 1.14 at.% C.

1. Introduction
Ni3Si is a L1 2 structured (ordered face centered cubic) inter-
metallic compound, it possesses anomalous yield strength as a
function of temperature, and specially, it shows excellent strength
and good oxidation resistance at temperatures up to 700
◦C [1,2].
However, Ni3Si exhibits brittle fracture and low ductility at room
and intermediate temperatures, thus, its utilization is restricted
[1–4].
Alloying can substantially improve the ambient and elevated
temperature strength and ductility of Ni
3Si [5–1 1]. By alloying with
chromium, the high-temperature ductility and the corrosion resis-
tance of Ni
3Si could be improved a lot [12–16]. Also, it was reported
that carbon doping could increase the ductile of the Ni 3Si alloys
[1,15]. So, it should be possible to improve the mechanical proper-
ties of Ni–Si–Cr alloys by the addition of C.
In my previous work, Ni–Si–Cr ternary alloys with various
amounts of Cr, which is far beyond the solubility limits in Ni 3Si,
were fabricated using self-propagating high-temperature synthesis
(SHS) casting route. The results indicated that the Ni–17.5Si–29.3Cr
ternary alloys possessed the best microstructure and properties,
details of the results were described elsewhere [16].
In the present study, expecting to further improve the
mechanical properties, thermite reaction (CrO

4. Discussion
When temperature in the reactor reached 270◦C, the igniter was
ignited and in turn ignited the two reactions below:
3XCrO3 + XCr2O3 + 8XAl + Y C = 4XCr + CrX CY + 4XAl2O3 (2)
3Ni + Si = Ni3Si (3)
Here, Cr2O3 in reaction (2) acts as a diluter and the ratio of Cr 2O3
to CrO3 is 1:3. CrXCY in reaction (2) is related to C content and may
be Cr23C6 or Cr7C3.
The combustion wave of the upper layer of (Cr–C) reaction prop-
agated immediately to the lower layer of (Ni–Si) reactants and
ignited reaction (3). The adiabatic temperature (T
ad) of the combus-
tion reaction (2) at 270◦C was calculated to be about 3200 ◦C when
no C element presented, it was a little higher (about 3300–3400 ◦C)
as the fraction of C increased. The Ni–Si reactants were ignited by
high temperature of reaction (2) and T
ad of this reaction at 270 ◦C
was about 1680 ◦C, which was much higher than the melting tem-
perature of Ni3Si. The two liquids, Cr–C and Ni–Si can miscible with
each other and spontaneously mixed. The temperature of the liq-
uid mixture was about 3000–3400
◦C as a result of a heat equation
of the two liquids at this time. Liquid Al 2O3 was immiscible with
Ni–Si–Cr–C liquid, and therefore separated from them and ﬂoated
on top of the liquid due to its low density [16,19,20].
According to the Ni–Si diagram (seeFig. 13) [21] and the melting
points of the products given in Table 2 , Cr should ﬁrst crystal-
lize in the liquids of the alloy at about 1900
◦C, then the Cr XCY
phases crystallized, Cr XCY might be Cr 23C6 or Cr7C3, which related
to C content. The hexagonal Ni 5Si2 with dissolved Cr and C crys-

The mechanical properties of the alloys are closely related to
the constitution and microstructure of the material. The alloys
with 1.14% C possessed the highest bending strength, compres-
sive strength and fracture toughness due to the effect of solution
strengthening of C addition and the ﬁne and uniform microstruc-
ture. As the content of C increased, Cr
23C6 and Cr 7C3 phases
gradually increased. Although these hard particles showed some
dispersion hardening effects and helped to improve the hardness
and wear resistance of the alloys, but they caused coarse and het-
erogeneous grains even microcracks and therefore decreased the
bending strength, compressive strength and fracture toughness.
The wear rate of Ni–Si–Cr-C alloys did not show monotonic decrease
with the C content. At the moment, the explanation could not be
clearly presented, but it was suggested that the wear rate for the
alloys with 3.35% C was higher than that for the alloys with 0.57% C
and 1.14% C might be associated with some changes in microstruc-
ture and its lower fracture toughness. The tribological properties of
Ni–Si–Cr–C alloys are under investigation now and the results will
be reported soon.
5. Conclusions
The effects of C addition on microstructure and properties of
Ni–Si–Cr ternary alloys are investigated. Results show that C mainly
presents in the form of solid solution and hard Cr
XCY particles
(Cr23C6 and Cr7C3). Microstructure and properties of the alloys vary
with C fraction. The alloy containing 1.14 at.% C possesses the high-
est bending strength, compressive strength, fracture toughness and
the lowest wear rate. The strengthen mechanism is mainly grain
ﬁning and solid solution strengthening of C.
