abstract
Alloy 617 is considered a primary structure material for building the intermediate heat exchanger (IHX)
for the very high temperature reactor (VHTR). In the present work, the effect of orientation of Alloy 617,
which may inﬂuence the engineering design and performance, was investigated by mechanical tests and
associated microstructural analysis. Tensile tests at various temperatures were performed on the speci-
mens at various angles to the rolling plane. The yield strength parabolically dropped once the sampling
direction deviated from the rolling plane, whereas the ultimate tensile strength decreased in a linear
trend. The mechanical anisotropy reﬂected by the loss of the transverse strengths exists throughout
the range of testing temperatures up to 1000 /C176C. The microstructural analysis revealed that the intrinsic
Bauschinger effect, the loss of transverse yield strength, is caused by mechanical ﬁbering in Alloy 617,
which is originally induced during hot rolling processing. The results also indicated that the inclusion
particles caused steep strain gradients during plastic deformations in Alloy 617, due to the dislocation
pileups on the interphase boundaries. The distribution of strained area is orientation dependent and thus
caused localized plastic deformations and early fractures in the transverse direction at low temperatures.
In addition, it was found that the strained area could be recovered by forming new grains, leading to an
oriented dynamic recrystallization (DRX) at high temperatures.

1. Introduction
Among all Gen-IV nuclear systems, the very high temperature
gas-cooled reactor (VHTR) is considered one of the most promising
in the U.S. due to its dual capacities of electricity generation and
hydrogen production. The VHTR is expected to operate at a high
temperature (/C24 950 /C176C) helium atmosphere for more than 60 years.
This poses great challenges for selection, examination, and qualiﬁ-
cation of possible structural materials for reactor components,
including gas ducts, pressure vessels, and the intermediate heat ex-
changer (IHX) [1,2]. In particular, the intermediate heat exchanger,
responsible for heat transfer from the primary helium loop to the
secondary loops used for electricity generation and hydrogen pro-
duction, is expected to experience a high pressure ( /C24 7 MPa) and an
aggressive atmosphere (corrosion and carburization/decarburiza-
tion) [3]. Various structural materials for the heat exchanger have
been studied for more than 30 years. The prospective structural
materials are Inconel 617 (or Alloy 617), Haynes
/C210230 (or Alloy
230), Incoloy 800H (or Alloy 800H) and Hastelloy XR (or Alloy
XR) [4]. Alloy 617 has been identiﬁed as a leading structural mate-
rial due to its exceptional creep strength at elevated temperatures
and relatively large experimental database for engineering design
[5]. Since Alloy 617 was introduced in the early 1970s, many stud-
ies of material properties have been carried out to test its potential
for use in the VHTR [6]. Creep and corrosion are the primary poten-
tial threats to structural materials in the VHTR environment. Creep
damages of Alloy 617 at high temperatures have been extensively
studied in air [6–12] as well as in a simulated reactor environment
[13–17]. Environmental effects, or corrosion damage, in the VHTR
environment has been studied by Bates [18], Graham [19], Christ
et al. [20,21], Jang et al. [22] and Cabet et al. [23–27]. Research
on short-term and long-term corrosion in high-temperature air
was reported by Jang et al. [22], Harper et al. [28], and Wright [29].
Although the mechanical properties and corrosion resistance of
Alloy 617 have been well studied, little research has focused on the
evaluation and characterization of the effect of orientation of Alloy
617. The effect of orientation on mechanical properties is signiﬁcant
in terms of engineering design and safety because the loss of
strength in one direction may impact the integrity of required com-
ponent shapes and cause localized failure. The effect can be exacer-
bated for components of the VHTR due to the harsh environment

heat exchanger for use in the VHTR may induce a potential issue
with respect to the mechanical directionality of the material. In
pre-conceptual designs of high temperature reactors, the compact
type heat exchanger was recommended to replace the traditional
shell-and-tube and helical coil type in order to take advantage of
the high temperature of the outlet gas and obtain a high efﬁciency.
The Print Circuit Heat Exchanger (PCHE), a compact type of heat ex-
changer, has been considered for use in the VHTR due to its large
heat transfer area from its miniature ﬂow channels and compacted
channel intervals [4]. The PCHE is typically fabricated by photo-
chemical etching on thin ﬂat plates and diffusion bonding. The de-
sign induces several possible material issues during long-term
operation. First, the shape of the plates will transform from rectan-
gular to trapezoidal as a result of the temperature gradient from the
inlet and outlet of the IHX [7]. The consequential thermal stresses in
the IHX can lead to creep damage for the structural material. Sec-
ond, the thermal transient may induce fatigue behavior [8], which
can dramatically reduce the service life of the material. Because
of the block-shaped design of the PCHE, these issues will not only
occur along the gas ﬂow channels, but they will also endanger the
transverse direction of the heat exchanger. Other components with
special geometry, such as the thick wall tubes under high internal
pressure [31], may also suffer the adverse effect of orientation with
dramatically lower strength in the transverse directions. Previous
studies have shown the microstructural inhomogeneity in the plate
form of Alloy 617. McCoy and King studied the mechanical proper-
ties of Alloy 617, and observed carbide stringers parallel to the roll-
ing direction [32]. Another anisotropic microstructure developed
during high-temperature tensile and creep tests was the preferen-
tial distribution of grain boundary carbides on boundaries normal
to the direction of the applied stress [33,34]. The understanding
of the impact of microstructural inhomogeneity on mechanical
properties is therefore necessary for the engineering design of reac-
tor components.
In this study, tensile testing of a plate form of Alloy 617 from
various sampling directions was conducted at temperatures up to
1000 /C176C. The results show that anisotropy inﬂuences the mechan-
ical properties of Alloy 617 at all testing temperatures, although
additional deformation mechanisms were activated at higher tem-
peratures. Following the mechanical tests, microstructural charac-
terization of Alloy 617 elucidated the impact of mechanical
ﬁbering on the material deformation at various temperatures.
Therefore, the effect of orientation on plastic deformations was
clariﬁed for both mechanical and microstructural aspects.
2. Materials and experimental procedures
The Alloy 617 plate was fabricated by Haynes International in
2005. The alloy was vacuum melted and electro slag re-melted as
a slab. It was then hot rolled a number of times with intermediate
anneals to a thickness of 1.020 00, and ﬁnally solution treated at
1177 /C176C for 37 min (Heat Number: 861758808). The chemical com-
position is shown in Table 1 .
Small tensile specimens with a gauge section of
1.49 mm(width) /C2 0.76 mm(thickness) /C2 7.60 mm(length) were
fabricated from the plate form of Alloy 617. The specimens were
sampled from different orientations with respect to the long-trans-
verse direction ( Fig. 1 ). Previous studies indicate that little or no
deviation of mechanical properties exists between the rolling (lon-
gitude) and the long-transverse direction of Alloy 617[35]. Thus, the
long-transverse direction was employed as the reference to study
the mechanical anisotropy and associated microstructure gradients
in the Alloy 617. Tensile tests were carried out with a strain rate
/C24 10
/C0 3/s using an INSTRON 1331 servohydraulic test system
equipped with a high temperature furnace. The strain rate was con-
trolled by the LabVIEW program linked to the loading frame. The
miniature design of the tensile specimens allowed the direct strain
measurement to be sufﬁciently accurate [36,37]. In order to main-
tain a stabilized and homogenous heat zone inside the furnace,
the testing temperature was maintained for /C24 40 min before begin-
ning the test. The temperature variation is ±3 /C176C during tensile
testing.
Transmission electron microscopy (TEM), scanning electron
microscopy (SEM), and electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD)
were employed to analyze the microstructural anisotropy of the
material both in the as-received condition and after plastic defor-
mations at various temperatures. The TEM samples were prepared
using typical electropolishing methods with a solution of 10% ace-
tic acid in methanol at /C0 45 /C176C and 22 V. The SEM samples were
etched for /C24 3 min using 10 ml acetic acid, 15 ml HCl, and 10 ml
HNO3. The EBSD samples were polished with SiC abrasive paper
down to 600 grit, polished further using 1 lm and 0.3 lm alumina
solutions, and ﬁnally ﬁnished with vibratory polishing using
0.02 lm colloidal silica. A focus was placed on the EBSD study.

the mechanical tests, microstructure analyses were conducted on
the specimens in the as-received condition and after tensile test-

study the effect of orientation on the fracture mechanism.

3.2. Microstructure analysis
TEM, SEM, and EBSD analyses were performed to discover the
source of the anisotropy and its effect on plastic deformations in
Alloy 617. Both the rolling and the transverse planes were charac-
terized in the as-received condition and after tensile testing.
3.2.1. Material characterizations
The triplanar montage generated from SEM images exhibits the
microstructure gradients in the three principle directions ( Fig. 5). A
great number of intrinsic carbide inclusions, composed of coarse Cr
and Mo carbides with a small amount of Ti(C, N), are presented as
white spots in Fig. 5 . Most of the coarse particles are M23C6 type
carbides from the TEM study ( Fig. 6 ). The lattice constant of this
FCC type carbide is 1.065 nm, or about three times the value of
the matrix. These intrinsic particles are not homogenously distrib-
uted after hot-rolling and annealing. The carbide inclusions are
shown as bands on the transverse plane (TP) and longitudinal
plane (LP), and grouped as clusters on the rolling plane (RP) ( Fig. 5).
Duplex grain structures were observed in Alloy 617 during both
the SEM ( Fig. 5) and EBSD analyses ( Fig. 7). Small grains ranging in
size from 10 to 30 lm were found in the inclusion-rich areas, and
large grains ranging in size from 40 to 100 lm were found in the
inclusion free areas. Similar to the band-shaped distribution of
inclusion particles, the alignment of crystals in the transverse
plane appeared to be parallel to the rolling plane. Most of the small
grains were located between two carbide bands ( Fig. 7 (b)). The
lamellar-shaped inclusions and the associated ﬁber-shaped grain
distributions are known as mechanical ﬁbering [31,38]. The EBSD
analysis also showed that both coarse and ﬁne grains are randomly
oriented, indicating no intrinsic texture in the as-received Alloy
617 ( Fig. 8 ).
3.2.2. Microstructural analysis for anisotropic deformations
Previous studies of Alloy 617 show that various mechanisms,
including dislocation sliding and climb, along with dynamic recrys-
tallization inﬂuence the process of plastic deformation to various
extents depending on the studied temperature [36]. The tempera-
ture impact can be seen from the results of mechanical testing
(Figs. 2 and 3 ). Alloy 617 has high strengths, with a small amount
of loss in strengths as the temperature increases to 800 /C176C. Consid-
erably lower strengths were observed at temperatures higher than
800 /C176C due to the activation of additional deformation mecha-
nisms, including dislocation climb and dynamic recrystallization.
800 /C176C can be considered the equicohesive temperature for Alloy
617. Above this temperature, grain boundaries no longer efﬁciently
impede the dislocation movement, and thus grains and grain
boundaries have comparable strength. For this reason, the analysis
of the effect of orientation on plastic deformations is further dis-
cussed separately according to low and elevated temperature
regions.
Figs. 9 and 10 show the results of EBSD analysis for the long-
and short-transverse specimens, respectively, which have been
tensile tested at room temperature. The sampling area for both
specimens is /C24 0.5 mm from the rupture edge. The crystallography
in Figs. 9 (a) and 10(a) shows the dominant texture after plastic
deformation is primary h111 i and secondary h001 i parallel to
the tension direction (refer to the legend in Fig. 7 (c)). A direct
observation of the developed texture comes from the intensity
mapping in the pole ﬁgures ( Fig. 11). The long-transverse specimen
shows a fully developed texture, which was also seen at higher
temperatures up to 700 /C176C [36]. In contrast, many more random
crystal orientations were observed in the short-transverse speci-
men, indicating that the material was not well plastically deformed
before rupture.

processes of Alloy 617. Based on the measurement of the maximum
misorientation in a grain, the strain contouring provides an
estimate of the amount of strain on individual grains during plastic
deformation of a material. Detailed procedures for the strain
contouring can be found in Ref. [39]. The results of strain contouring
for the long- and short-transverse specimens are shown in Figs. 9(b)

Following thermal aging for a specimen, microstructural analy-
sis can provide additional information on plastic deformations in
the short-transverse direction. Alloy 617 develops M 23C6 type pre-
cipitates during high temperature aging. These carbide particles
precipitate, diffuse, and coarsen during aging, then ﬁnally stabilize
in favorable nucleation sites, including interphase and high-angle
grain boundaries, due to their high free energy and large average
atomic volume [42]. SEM images show that the grain boundaries
became fully decorated by continuous intergranular carbide
‘‘chains’’ after aging ( Fig. 14 ). These ‘‘chains’’ were broken at grain
boundaries during plastic deformation. Fig. 14 shows a specimen of
Alloy 617 aged for 300 h at 1000 /C176C and then tensile tested at room
temperature. The tension direction is parallel to the short-trans-
verse direction (perpendicular to the inclusion bands in the pic-
ture). The intergranular carbide ‘‘chains’’ became disconnected in
areas lying between two inclusion bands, indicating that the plas-
tic deformations essentially localized in these areas. The discon-
nection of the carbide ‘‘chains’’ and corresponding grain
boundary elongation conﬁrms the EBSD microstructural analyses
that revealed the fact that only the areas between inclusion bands
became well deformed under a tension load, while the areas near
the inclusion were highly strained without complete deformation.
Above 800 /C176C, the ﬂow stress was found to rapidly drop once the
yield strength was reached. At this point, strain hardening cannot
occur due to additional deformation mechanisms, including dislo-
cation creep and dynamic recrystallization (DRX). The effect of ori-
entation on the deformation process for high temperatures is
therefore different from low temperatures. At a high temperature,
e.g. 1000 /C176C, the strains near the inclusion particles can be released
by the additional deformation mechanisms, particularly dynamic
recrystallization. However, the effect of orientation is not subse-
quently weakened according to the analysis of relative strengths
(Fig. 4 ). Fig. 15 (a) shows EBSD images of a long-transverse speci-
men after a tensile test at 1000 /C176C. A large number of recrystallized
ﬁne grains can be observed in the inclusion rich areas as well as in
the high-angle grain boundaries. The recrystallized grains have
various crystal orientations without a speciﬁc texture, whereas
the adjacent large grains obtained a developed texture (e.g.
h111 i) following plastic deformations. Compared to a large frac-
tion of recrystallized grains in the long-transverse specimen
(Fig. 15(a)), dynamic recrystallization in the short-transverse spec-
imen features a smaller number, but larger size, of recrystallized
grains ( Fig. 15 (b)). Also, the alignment of recrystallized grains is
approximately parallel to the inclusion bands in the short-trans-
verse direction.
3.2.3. Fractography
A study of SEM fractography was performed to examine the
fracture processes and reveal the causes of failure for different an-
gles in relation to the long-transverse direction. Fig. 16 shows the
fractography of specimens following tensile tests at room temper-
ature. For the specimens at 0 /C176and 30/C176angles to the long-transverse
direction, each individual grain can be clearly indentiﬁed in the
fracture surface, indicating an approximate intergranular fracture
(Fig. 16 (a) and (b)). For the specimen at 45 /C176, ‘‘dimples’’ with
slightly elongated troughs arise due to microvoids generated by
the shear stress acting on the carbide particles ( Fig. 16 (c)). ‘‘Dim-
ples’’ with equiaxed microvoids can be found in the specimens at
60/C176and 90 /C176(the short-transverse direction) ( Fig. 16 (d) and (e)).
In these cases, the microvoids are formed in association with frac-
tured inclusion particles and rapidly grow out under approxi-
mately uniaxial stresses.

4.2. Microstructural anisotropy
Two main sources of the anisotropy can typically be found in
polycrystalline materials [45]. The ﬁrst source is material texture
produced by strong deformation (e.g. cold work) that develops pre-
ferred crystal orientation, such as (1 1 0) h112 i rolling texture ( a
brass type) in the sheet form of face-centered cubic (FCC) metals
[31]. The second source is mechanical ﬁbering, composed of an align-
ment of inclusion particles and matrix crystals. Compared to cold
rolled products, hot rolled metals and alloys generally have more iso-
tropic mechanical properties. Solid solution annealing after hot work
also helps to minimize residual stresses. Therefore, no obvious tex-
ture was found in the as-received Alloy 617 (Fig. 8). However, signif-
icant mechanical ﬁbering was found in the plate form of Alloy 617,
reﬂected by the directionality of mechanical properties. During the
hot rolling process, the inclusion particles were developed with
asymmetric shapes and spatial distributions. The growth of recrys-
tallized grains was subsequently restricted by the inclusion particles,
and thus the new grains presented a speciﬁc alignment. Additionally,
the inclusion particles were not re-dissolved during solid solution
annealing. As a result, the microstructures featuring inclusion and
crystal ﬁbers were not modiﬁed during the annealing process. In
other words, the mechanical ﬁbering was preserved after solid solu-
tion treatment. So this microstructure, composed of carbide bands in
the transverse direction, was found extensively in the plate form of
Alloy 617 [16,17,32–34,46–48].
The mechanical ﬁbering in Alloy 617 attributes to an aniso-
tropic microstructure under tensile load. The inclusion particles,
seen as clusters in the rolling plane and as bands in the transverse
plane, induce large strain gradients during plastic deformations.
According to the dislocation model suggested by Ashby, a signiﬁ-
cant number of overlaps and voids occur at the grain boundaries
during plastic deformations [31]. Many statistically stored disloca-
tions are generated along with these defects and bring in non-uni-
form strains at the grain boundaries. The geometrically necessary
dislocations are therefore generated to correct the overlaps and
voids and to ﬁt the grains together, continuing plastic deforma-
tions. In the current study of Alloy 617, the high volume fraction
of carbide particles impedes the dislocation movement and conse-
quently causes dislocation pileups, generating a high density of
subgrain boundaries surrounding the particles. In this case, the
geometrically necessary dislocations cannot be generated to re-
lieve the non-uniform strains present at interphase boundaries.
Therefore as seen in the strain contouring, the highly strained areas
are concentrated on the interphase boundaries ( Figs. 9 (b) and
10(b)). In the short-transverse direction, this phenomenon is
ampliﬁed due to the steep strain gradient. Continuous deformation
can only occur in between two inclusion bands and is caused by lo-
cal deformation in these areas. The process is evident in the aged
specimen of Alloy 617 where intergranular carbide ‘‘chains’’ were
disconnected between two carbide bands as a result of localized
plastic deformations ( Fig. 14 ). However, the situation changes for
elevated temperatures. Due to the increased mobility of atoms,
subgrain boundaries transform into grain boundaries and new
grains are formed. The number, size, and distribution of dynamic
recrystallized grains strongly depend on the plastic strain. The
highly strained area after plastic deformations, which is also the
inclusion rich area in Alloy 617, is greatly subjected to dynamic
recrystallization. This explains why the dynamic recrystallized
grains are distributed in the same way as the strain contouring
at low temperatures ( Figs. 9 (b) and 10(b)).

Another important result of mechanical ﬁbering is the induction
of early failure by the ligament of carbides bands in tension load-
ing, which can be seen as a loss of tensile strength in short-trans-
verse specimens. This was found to only occur at lower
temperatures for Alloy 617, since at temperatures greater than
700 /C176C, fracture was initiated by triple junction cracks from grain
boundary sliding [36]. Two microstructural analyses completed
during the study indicated that specimens with various transverse
directions experienced failure at different levels of plastic deforma-
tions. The ﬁrst analysis is based on the observation of the preva-
lence of twin boundaries. As shown in Fig. 13 , the fraction of
twin boundaries in the as-received condition was 62.5% of the total
observed boundaries. This value dropped to 11.0% for the long-
transverse specimen after tensile testing, signifying that most twin
boundaries had been deformed into other types of boundaries. In
contrast, most twin boundaries were preserved in the short-trans-
verse specimen, with a fraction value of 32.8%. This indicates that
an early fracture occurred in the short-transverse specimen, which
stopped further plastic deformations.
The second analysis involves observation of texture develop-
ment. During the plastic deformation of Alloy 617 at low temper-
atures, crystallographic planes tend to rotate themselves to form
h111 i or h100 i textures to minimize easy glide. The amount of
crystals oriented to be parallel to the h111 i or h100 i direction,
or the texture development, can be used to examine the process
of plastic deformations. Thus, different levels of plastic deforma-
tions in the long- and short-transverse specimens after tensile test-
ing can be found either from direct observations in EBSD crystal
orientation mappings ( Figs. 9 (a) and 10(a)) or intensity mappings
in pole ﬁgures (Fig. 11). A fully developed texture in the long-trans-
verse specimen of Alloy 617 indicates that the material has experi-
enced a strong deformation during tensile testing. In contrast, the
texture was found to be undeveloped in the short-transverse spec-
imen, seen as a relatively smooth intensity distribution in the pole
ﬁgures (Fig. 11(b)). The undeveloped texture is a result of the early
fracture that occurred in the short-transverse specimen.
Fractography provides a direct approach to the study of various
fracture processes for specimens at various angles. Due to the high
volume fraction of inclusion particles in certain areas of Alloy 617,
the rupture of the specimen was generally caused by microvoid
coalescence from fractured particles. This type of failure usually
has a ‘‘dimple’’ feature on the fracture surface. However, an inter-
granular fracture was found in the specimens at 0 /C176and 30 /C176angles
to the long-transverse direction. The ‘‘dimple’’ feature does appear
in the fractured surface at 45 /C176with non-equiaxed microvoids, and
becomes severe at 60 /C176and 90 /C176with equiaxed microvoids. The re-
sults show the impact of mechanical ﬁbering is small for low an-
gles (0 /C176and 30 /C176), but becomes signiﬁcant for higher angles (45 /C176,
60/C176and 90 /C176). The fractography of fracture mechanisms in Alloy
617 for the studied orientations agrees well with the mechanical
tests and other microstructure analyses.
The effect of orientation can also be magniﬁed in the creep con-
dition for Alloy 617. Totemeier and Lillo reported that specimens of
MA 754 have a comparable tensile strength in the transverse direc-
tion to the strength value in the longitudinal direction. However, a
markedly lower transverse strength was found in the creep condi-
tion [44]. Thus, the effect of orientation for the structure materials
is important for long-term, high temperature environments in the
VHTR, where creep damage can occur. Signiﬁcant effort should be
placed into minimizing the effect of orientation and even eliminat-
ing the source of anisotropy in Alloy 617. High temperature anneal-
ing is a simple method to remove the inclusion particles. Kihara
et al. reported that all of the inclusion particles in Alloy 617 were
dissolved after aging at 1200 /C176C for 20 h [49]. However, the grain
size for the aged Alloy 617 was 0.36 mm, or more than twice the va-
lue in the as-received condition. Creep resistance increases as a re-
sult of grain size increase; however, the yield strength of the aged
alloy likely decreases due to the loss of strengthening by the ﬁne
grains. The corresponding loss in yield strength may fail to meet
the ASTM standards [50]. Hence, it will be a challenge to remove
inclusion particles from Alloy 617 by high temperature annealing
while still maintaining its mechanical properties.
5. Conclusions
In this study, the anisotropic mechanical properties of Alloy 617
were studied through tensile tests performed at various tempera-
tures. The dependency of plastic deformations on loading orienta-
tion was analyzed through several microstructural examinations.
The following conclusions can be drawn from the results of the
study:
1. Lower material strengths were observed in specimens with var-
ious angles of orientation to the long-transverse direction. The
yield strength parabolically dropped from the long-transverse
to the short-transverse direction, while the ultimate tensile
strength decreased in a linear trend. The anisotropy has compa-
rable impacts on Alloy 617 for temperatures up to 1000 /C176C. The
results of the study can be directly applied to the anisotropic
yielding criteria used in engineering simulation and design.
2. Mechanical ﬁbering attributes to the mechanical anisotropy of
Alloy 617. The inclusion particles that induced from solidiﬁca-
tion of the ingot were developed with asymmetric shapes and
spatial distributions, and preserved after solid solution treat-
ment. Mechanical ﬁbering causes an orientation dependence
of plastic deformations in the material, with varying perfor-
mances across the studied temperature range.
3. At low temperatures, Alloy 617 was not homogenously
deformed due to microstructure gradients. The inclusion rich
area, seen as clusters from the rolling plane and bands from
the transverse plane, could not be smoothly deformed, and thus
they obtained high strains under tensile load. The steep strain
gradient in the short-transverse direction caused an early frac-
ture, leading to a much lower tensile strength.
4. At elevated temperatures, the highly strained areas generated
by the dislocation pileups at barriers, e.g. interphase bound-
aries, were subject to dynamic recrystallization. The orientation
dependence of dynamic recrystallization with different dimen-
sions and alignments of recrystallized grains is a result of the
anisotropic spatial distribution of inclusion particles in Alloy
617. This leads to orientation dependent mechanical properties
at elevated temperatures.
