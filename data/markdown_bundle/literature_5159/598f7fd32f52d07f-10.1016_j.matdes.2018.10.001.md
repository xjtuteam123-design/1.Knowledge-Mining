Abstract 
The microstructure and room-temperature mechanical properties of directionally solidified and 
heat-treated Nb-16Si-23Ti-4Cr-2Al-2Hf-0/0.3/0.5Sc (at%) alloys were investigated. All alloys 
consisted of Nb-based solid solution (Nb ss), α -Nb5Si3 and γ -Nb5Si3 with preferred <001> 
orientation for the first two phases, and Sc was enriched in γ -Nb5Si3 phase. Sc-added alloys 
showed a more continuous Nb ss matrix in which the finer Nb5Si3 phase distributed uniformly. 
The volume fraction of both α -Nb5Si3 and γ-Nb5Si3 phase was decreased by Sc addition. Sc 
alloying reduced the Young’s modulus and nano-hardness of Nbss phase. 0.50 at% Sc addition 
enhanced the fracture toughness and tensile strength from 9.88 MPa m 1/2 and 430.3 MPa to 
25.95 MPa m1/2 and 1119.7 MPa. The improvement in fracture toughness was due to the better 
deformability of Nbss phase. The high tensile strength was attributed to the dispersed and refined 
Nb5Si3 phase, which was not degraded by the increased Nbss volume fraction since dislocations 
were not open within most Nbss phase. Besides, the strong interface cohesion was preferable 
for the strength of Sc-added alloys.

1. Introduction 
By incorporating Nb 5Si3 silicide into Nb ss phase, Nb -Si based alloys are formed with 
ductile/strengthening two-phase structure in which Nb5Si3 phase provides creep and oxidation 
resistance at high temperature, and Nbss phase supplies fracture toughness and ductility at room 
temperature [1–3]. Nb-Si based alloys show great potential for replacing Ni based superalloys 
due to high melting points, relatively low densities and excellent high-temperature strength [4]. 
However, their industrial applications are still restricted by the low fracture toughness and poor 
ductility [1,5]. In recent years, r oom-temperature mechanical properties have been improved 
through alloying and optimizing processing techniques. Kashyap [6–9] et al have studied the 
significant enhancement in mechanical proper ties and oxidation resistance for Nb-Si binary 
alloys with Ga, In and Mg additions. Multi-component Nb-Si-Ti-Cr-Al-Hf systems perform 
well at room and high temperature [1]. Ti and Hf are alloyed for improving toughness and 
tensile strength without increasing the brittle-to-ductile transition temperature [10,11]. Alloying 
with Cr and Al tends to cause cleavage fracture, while a high Cr or Al content can benefit the 
oxidation resistance [10,12,13]. A variety of processing techniques, such as vacuum arc-melting 
[2], directional solidification [5], powder metallurgy [11] and additive manufacturing [14], have 
been applied for fabricating Nb-Si based alloys. I t has been reported that the directional 
solidification, which results in the well-aligned microstructure with fewer horizontal grain 
boundaries, can improve both the room-temperature ductility and high -temperature strength 
simultaneously [3,5].

Chan [10,15,16] et al have proposed a theory to understand the ductility of Nb-Si based alloys, 
in terms of the ratios of surface energy to Peierls-Nabarro energy (γs/UP-N) and surface energy 
to unstable stacking energy (γs/γus). γs, γus and UP-N measure the resistance to cleavage fracture, 
dislocation nucleation and dislocation mobility. Contrary to γs and γus, UP-N is very sensitive to 
alloying additions. Fewer d + s electrons per atom will weaken the atomic bonding and thus 
lower the U P-N, which benefits the mechanical properties due to the higher mobility of crack-
tip dislocations in both Nbss and Nb5Si3 phase. Sc (3d14s2) has fewer d + s electrons compared 
with 5 in Nb (4d45s1). Therefore, Sc is likely to restrain the fracture by promoting the dislocation 
emission and the slip process at crack tips. Similarly, Ti (3d24s2) and Hf (5d26s2) are beneficial 
for mechanical properties while Cr (3d 54s1) has an opposite effect. Besides, Sc has been used 
in aluminum alloys to improve mechanical properties [17,18]. However, to our best knowledge, 
the effect of Sc on Nb-Si based alloys has yet remained unexplored, which arouses our interest. 
The main purpose of this study is to systematically investigate the microstructure, phase 
constitutions as well as room-temperature mechanical properties (Young’s modulus, nano -
hardness, fracture toughness and tensile strength) of Nb-Si based alloys with newly designed 
compositions. Excellent fracture toughness and tensile strength are essential for the security of 
manufacturing, assembling and operating. And valid data of modulus and nano -hardness for 
single phases can help understand the properties of Nbss/Nb5Si3 composites. Moreover, the 
toughening and strengthening mechanisms are discussed in details. We hope these results could 
be beneficial for the future application of Nb-Si based alloys.

Ingots with nominal compositions of Nb-16Si-23Ti-4Cr-2Al-2Hf-0/0.3/0.5Sc (at%) were 
prepared by vacuum non-consumable arc-melting within water-cooled copper hearth in argon 
atmosphere. Sc was added through the Al-Sc alloy since pure Sc has high chemical activity in 
air. Each ingot weighed 1500 g. These ingots were re-melted at least four times to ensure the 
homogeneity. Several rods were cut from the ingots by electro-discharge machining (EDM) and 
ground to remove oxidized surfaces. The rods were assembled in self-made ceramic crucibles. 
Directional solidification was carried out in the liquid-metal cooling (LMC) furnace, then these 
rods were heat treated at 1500 °C for 12 h under argon protection, followed by furnace cooling 
to room temperature. These samples were marked as the Master alloy, 0.30Sc alloy and 0.50Sc 
alloy respectively, according to their Sc addition.

Fracture toughness, KQ, was measured by three-point bending tests. The single-edge notched 
specimens were 30, 6 and 3 mm in length, width and thickness, with the length parallel to the 
solidification direction. The notch with a/w = 0.5 ( a: notch length, w: specimen width) was 
prepared by EDM . The bending tests were executed on the SANS hydraulic servo testing 
machine with a crosshead speed of 0.1 mm/min , the testing span was 24 mm . The fracture 
toughness was averaged from nine specimens. The plate-shaped tensile specimens, with a gauge 
length of 18 mm and a cross section of 3 × 2 mm2, were surface ground for measuring tensile 
properties. Three specimens were tested using Instron 8801 hydraulic servo testing machine. 
The tensile process was under a constant axial speed of 0.05 mm/min and the loading direction 
was parallel to the solidification direction.

3. Results 
3.1 Microstructure of directionally solidified Nb-Si based alloys 
Figure 2 shows XRD patterns of directionally solidified Nb-Si based alloys. Nbss and α-
Nb5Si3 are the main phases in either Sc-free or 0.30Sc and 0.50Sc alloys. Correspondingly, the 
peak intensity ratio I/Imax for crystal planes are listed in Table 1 [21–23]. According to Table 1 
and Figure 2, (400) remains stable as the principle plane, Sc addition leads to the reinforcement 
of peak intensity for other planes. The intensity of (006) plane gets quite close to that of (400) 
plane in 0.50Sc alloy. As shown in Figure 3, the bright phase is Nb ss and the gray phase is α -
Nb5Si3. Some α-Nb5Si3 phase is with square corners. The microstructure shows hypoeutectic 
morphology as implied by the large Nbss phase. Nbss phase forms firstly from the liquid metal, 
expressed as L → primary Nb ss + L1. On transverse sections, the worm-like α-Nb5Si3 strings

concentrated in the areas near Nb5Si3 phase. These areas still belong to Nb ss phase in spite of 
the higher contents of Si and other alloying elements (see Table 2). To better determine the 
distributions of various elements, the results of area scan and line scan are showed in Figure 5.

3.2 Microstructure of directionally solidified and heat-treated Nb-Si based alloys 
Figure 6 shows the directionally solidified and heat-treated microstructure, and EDS results 
are given in Table 3. Phases with different contrast correspond to Nbss, α-Nb5Si3 and γ-Nb5Si3. 
On transverse sections, no dramatic difference is observed for the heat-treated microstructure, 
except for the isolation degree of Nb5Si3 phase. Nbss phase interconnects to form a continuous 
matrix, while the Nbss matrix of Sc-added alloys exhibits higher continuity than that of Master 
alloy. And Nb5Si3 phase becomes finer after adding Sc. On longitudinal sections, the f ibrous 
Nb5Si3 phase distributes parallel to the growth direction . The directionally solidified Nb 5Si3 
phase with sharp corners tends to be blunted after heat treatment since this round morphology 
decreases the interphase boundary area and the total interfacial energy [3]. As shown in Figs.6 
(c-f), γ -Nb5Si3 phase is quite rare in Sc -added alloys. The amount of γ -Nb5Si3 phase is not 
influenced by heat treatment since γ-Nb5Si3 phase is stable during heat treatment [3]. By using 
ICP-AES, the retained Sc content in the heat-treated 0.30Sc and 0.50Sc alloys is 0.11 and 0.19

3.3 EBSD analysis 
EBSD is used to analyze the preferred orientation and phase amount on transverse sections. 
Figure 8 shows orientation maps and inverse pole figures (IPFs) of Nbss and α-Nb5Si3 phase. 
It can be seen that the orientation of Nbss and α-Nb5Si3 phase doesn’t change after adding Sc. 
The color discrepancy indicates the misorientation. However, no apparent color difference is 
exhibited by orientation maps, revealing that the orientation of the two phases maintains fixed 
during directional growth. The IPFs illustrate that the preferred orientation of both Nbss and α-
Nb5Si3 phase is <001>, which is parallel to the growth direction with a little deviation. That 
the orientation is preferred has be verified by the diffraction peaks of Nbss and α-Nb5Si3 phase 
in Figure 2. More details about phase amount are shown in Fig.8 (j), 0.5 at% Sc increases the 
amount of Nbss by 20% and decreases that of α-Nb5Si3 by 24.4%. As for γ-Nb5Si3 phase, the 
area fraction and growth orientation are influenced remarkably: the area fraction decreases 
from 4.5% to about 1% and the growth orientation gets disoriented with Sc addition.

4 Discussion 
4.1 Effect of Sc on fracture toughness 
ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT

## Page 23

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT
The ductile-phase toughening mechanism has been reported in many studies. Incorporating 
Nbss phase could maintain the high-temperature properties of Nb5Si3 phase while minimizing 
its negative impacts, like intrinsic brittleness and poor damage tolerance at room temperature 
[20]. This toughening mechanism is not very useful as envisioned in Master alloy: Nbss phase 
fractures with transgranular cleavage facets and no other toughening mechanisms act in 
Master alloy (see Fig.10 (a)). As a result, the well-aligned Nbss/Nb5Si3 microstructure cannot 
effectively prevent the crack propagation, leaving the low fracture toughness. However, the 
fracture resistance of Sc-added alloys is much larger than the planar fracture in Master alloy, 
as indicated by the sophisticated fracture surfaces, i.e., the tortuous crack paths along which 
there are more chances of encountering obstacles during the crack propagation. 
For Nbss/Nb5Si3 two-phase systems, the deformation of Nbss matrix is restricted by the 
incorporated Nb5Si3 phase [20]. The tearing ridges of Nbss phase have revealed the constraint 
(or stress triaxiality) imposed by Nb5Si3 phase [11]. As shown in Figure 6 and 7, the uniform 
distribution of refined Nb5Si3 phase leads Nbss matrix to be more continuous, which alleviates 
the constraint on Nbss phase [11] and thus makes Nbss phase bear greater plastic deformation 
[20]. Besides, it is difficult for the main crack to circumvent the continuous Nbss matrix with 
higher volume fraction (see Fig.8 (j)). So, Nbss phase is more potent to arrest the crack in Sc-
added alloys. As the load increases, the crack renucleates in Nb5Si3 phase at the front of the 
crack tip, leaving behind the bridging of Nbss phase to resist further opening (see Figs.10 (c) 
and (e)). That Nbss phase can be better deformed under less constraint stands the higher 
likelihood of the Nbss bridging [13]. Once Nbss phase bridges the crack, the crack propagation 
is stopped and thus requires larger driving force. The Nbss bridging also induces the crack 
ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT

## Page 24

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT
deflection, which promotes the fracture energy absorption along the tortuous paths. More 
interfaces are introduced due to the refined microstructure and they can benefit the fracture 
toughness by resisting the crack propagation as barriers [5]. The microcracks in Nb5Si3 phase 
could become crack sources. However, in Sc-added alloys (see Figs.10 (c-f)), most of them 
are arrested by Nbss phase, so they cannot extend continuously to accelerate crack propagation 
[12]. In addition, Nbss bridging and microcracks can redistribute the stress and reduce the 
stress intensity at crack tips, therefore blunt the main crack [13,14]. 
The influence of the isolation degree of Nb5Si3 phase on fracture behaviors is discussed 
based on the bright-field TEM images of dislocations, as shown in Figure 13. Dislocations are 
not observed within Nb5Si3 phase since Nb5Si3 is the hard phase and no slip systems can be 
opened at room temperature [34]. In Fig.13 (a), high-intensity dislocations are piled up at the 
corners of two conjunct Nb5Si3 phase. The hard Nb5Si3 phase is prone to break under the local 
stress concentration. It is possible that the crack source can further extend into the nearby Nbss 
phase and thus impair the fracture toughness. In Fig.13 (b), Nb5Si3 phase is surrounded by the 
Nbss matrix. Dislocations are observed at Nbss/Nb5Si3 interfaces, but the stress concentration 
can be better alleviated through the deformation of Nbss phase, which is proved by the 
existence of minor dislocations inside Nbss phase. As shown in Fig.13 (c), several Nb5Si3 
phases are isolated by Nbss phase but still close to each other. Dislocations are piled up in the 
regions between the two Nb5Si3 phases. Owing to the Nbss phase in the narrow gaps between 
Nb5Si3 phases, the stress concentration can be relieved more easily compared with the 
situation in Fig.13 (a). To sum up, since Nb5Si3 phase tends to distribute isolated in Sc-added 
alloys (see Figure 6 and 8), cracks cannot be initiated readily and thus avoid the early fracture 
ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT

## Page 25

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT
of Nb5Si3 phase, that is, Nbss phase is less affected by the constraint imposed by Nb5Si3 phase. 
Therefore, the fracture tolerance of Sc-added alloys is superior to that of Master alloy. 
 
Figure 13. Bright-field TEM images taken from the regions near the fracture in 0.50Sc alloy 
after three-point bending tests. 
 
In addition to the constraint that restricts the plastic deformation of Nbss phase and thus 
affects its fracture mode, the fracture behavior is also influenced by the property of single 
phases [20]. In Table 4, micromechanical properties of α-Nb5Si3 phase remain nearly 
unchanged, while Nbss phase can be better deformed since it becomes softer with relatively 
lower modulus after adding Sc. The higher deformability of Nbss phase can help reduce the 
local stress concentration to delay the crack initiation in Nb5Si3 phase [20]. It can also blunt 
the crack propagation by the local plastic flow [1,13], thereby increase the fracture toughness 
of Sc-added alloys. Another remarkable phenomenon is that interface separation occurs 
frequently in Sc-added alloys (see Figs.10 (d) and (f)). Ashby [20] et al have pointed out that 
limited interface separation is desirable for fracture toughness since it lowers the constraint 
level. Moreover, interface separation can blunt crack tips and induce crack deflection, more 
fracture energy would be absorbed along the tortuous crack propagation path [5]. So, interface 
separation is beneficial for fracture toughness. 
 
4.2 Effect of Sc on the tensile strength 
Nb-Si based alloys exhibit great room-temperature tensile strength with Sc addition . The 
ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT

## Page 26

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT
strength improvement in two-phase Nb-Si based alloys is usually attributed to the strengthening 
of hard Nb5Si3 phase [14,35] and/or the solid-solution strengthening of ductile Nb ss phase 
[6,13,20]. In general, tensile strength increases with the volume fraction of silicide under both 
room and high temperature conditions. By building back propagation artificial neural networks, 
Liu [24] et al predict that room-temperature tensile strength decreases as Nb5Si3 volume fraction 
drops from 40% to 30%. From Fig.8 (j), adding Sc reduces Nb5Si3 volume fraction, which is 
unfavorable to tensile strength [6]. And no new precipitated phases are found through the XRD 
and microstructure analysis. Besides, Nbss phase is not strengthened since Nbss phase get s 
softening after adding Sc (see Table 4). Without the solid-solution and hard-phase strengthening 
effects, there must be some other factors that contribute to the excellent tensile strength. 
Microstructure refinement has been known as the optimum method to improve both the 
tensile strength and fracture toughness at room temperature. As a brittle material, Nb-Si based 
alloys are very sensitive to microcracks. From Figure 6-8, adding Sc leads to the well-refined 
microstructure by changing the morphology and volume of Nb5Si3 phase. The deformation can 
be distributed more uniformly to avoid dislocation pile-ups, especially around Nb5Si3 phase, 
which restrains the crack initiation in the brittle Nb 5Si3 phase [11,34]. Since Nb5Si3 phase 
becomes isolated and dispersed, the surrounding ductile Nb ss phase could effectively arrest 
microcracks even if they are initiated in some Nb 5Si3 phase. As shown in Figs.12 (b-c), 
conspicuous cleavage facets are not observed due to the refinement of Nb5Si3 phase, and Nb5Si3 
phase is surrounded by the plastically deformed Nbss phase. It is less likely to form large crack 
sources to cause the catastrophic fracture. As a result, Sc -added alloys can be subjected to 
greater stress before fracture. Meanwhile, more interfaces parallel to the loading direction are 
ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT

## Page 27

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT
introduced as obstacles to resist interfacial sliding, which benefits the tensile strength [34]. 
By analyzing the distribution of dislocations in 0.50Sc alloy (see Figure 13), we find that 
there are few or even no dislocations inside Nbss phase, most of them are piled up near 
Nbss/Nb5Si3 and Nbss/Nbss interfaces. It can infer that dislocation sources are almost inactive 
within Nb ss phase, which explains why Sc-added alloys have excellent tensile strength even 
with a higher volume fraction of Nbss phase. The strength keeps increasing as dislocations are 
pinned by interfaces. As discussed above, the refined microstructure with isolated Nb5Si3 phase 
avoids the premature failure for Sc-added alloys. So, interface stress can increase continuously 
before fracture, leading to the interface separation (see Figs.12 (b-c)). However, for Master 
alloy, interface separation is overshadowed by the premature fracture. Therefore, if microcracks 
are not initiated in most Nb5Si3 phase, Nbss/Nb5Si3 and Nbss/Nbss interfaces play a predominate 
role in resisting interface separation and bearing the load. One important thing is that although 
interface separation is beneficial for toughness through lowering constraint level, inducing the 
crack deflection and absorbing more fracture energy during the deformation process, it actually 
introduces cracks into the microstructure, which induces the final fracture. Some studies [30,35] 
have shown that interface separation degrades the strength. In this study, we think that the 
interface separation is the precursor of fracture for Sc-added alloys. 
 
5 Conclusion 
A series of Nb -Si based alloys are fabricated with different Sc addition. The effect of Sc on 
microstructure, phase orientation, element contents and mechanical properties is systemically 
analyzed. Major conclusions can be drawn as follows: 
ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT

## Page 28

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT
(1) The constituent phases of all alloys are Nb ss, α -Nb5Si3 and γ -Nb5Si3. Sc is primarily 
partitioned in γ-Nb5Si3 phase. Sc alloying refines Nb5Si3 phase, leading Nbss matrix to be higher 
continuous. Both Nb ss and α -Nb5Si3 phase grow preferentially along <001>. The volume 
fraction of α-Nb5Si3 and γ-Nb5Si3 phase is reduced in Sc-added alloys. 
(2) For Nb ss phase, En and Hn are stable on transverse sections . En is reduced to some extent, 
and Hn decreases from 5.35GPa to 4.84GPa with 0.5 at% Sc addition. En and Hn of α-Nb5Si3 
phase remain nearly unchanged because of its intrinsic brittleness. 
(3) 0.30 and 0.50 at% Sc addition improves room -temperature fracture toughness from 9.88 
MPa m 1/2 to 22.88 and 25.95 MPa m 1/2. Th is enhancement is attributed to the refined and 
dispersed Nb5Si3 phase, the softer Nb ss phase as well as the interface separation , all of which 
alleviate the constraint on the plastic deformation of Nbss phase. 
(4) The refined microstructure with dispersed Nb5Si3 phase improves tensile strength from 
430.3 MPa to 1123.5 and 1119.7 MPa for 0.30Sc and 0.50Sc alloy. The strength is not affected 
by the higher volume fraction of Nbss phase since dislocation sources are inactive inside Nbss 
phase. Without the early initiation of microcracks in Nb5Si3 phase, Nbss/Nb5Si3 and Nbss/Nbss 
interfaces play a major role in bearing the load for Sc-added alloys.
