Eﬀects of Ru and Re additions on the high temperature ﬂow
stresses of Ni-base single crystal superalloys
A.C. Yeh, S. Tin *
Department of Materials Science and Metallurgy, University of Cambridge, Pembroke Street, Cambridge CB2 3QZ, UK
Received 6 September 2004; received in revised form 13 October 2004; accepted 29 October 2004
Available online 25 November 2004
Abstract
The strengthening eﬀects of Re and Ru additions in high refractory content single crystal Ni-base superalloys were investigated.
An electro-thermal mechanical testing (ETMT) unit was used to measure the high temperature ﬂow stresses of ﬁve experimental Ni-
based superalloys containing varying levels of Re and Ru. Flow stresses of single phase c and c
0 alloys with compositions represen-
tative of the constituent phases found in superalloy single crystals were also measured. Signiﬁcant increases in ﬂow stress were
observed with the addition of Ru or Re. For similar alloying levels in wt%, additions of Re were more eﬀective in strengthening
the single crystal alloy when compared to addition of Ru.

1. Introduction
Advanced Ni-base single crystal superalloys utilize
extremely high levels of refractory alloying additions
to enhance structural properties at elevated tempera-
tures. In these complex multi-component engineering al-
loys, additions of Mo, W and Re are commonly
incorporated to provide a high degree of solid solution
strengthening [1–3]. This allows remarkable improve-
ments in high temperature creep resistance. However, al-
loys containing high levels of these refractory alloying
additions are also susceptible to the formation of topo-
logical close packed (TCP) phases during service at high
temperatures [3–5]. Microstructural and chemical
changes associated with the precipitation of these refrac-
tory rich intermetallic precipitates degrade the structural
integrity of the component and results in a substantial
reduction of high temperature creep properties. Re-
cently, addition of Ru in Ni-base alloys has been used
to improve the microstructural stability and suppress
the formation of these TCP phases, resulting in much-
improved high temperature creep properties [6,7].
At lower temperatures, when creep performance is
not dictated by microstructural instabilities, the intrinsic
properties of the c and c
0 phases govern the deformation
characteristics. The strength of the alloy then becomes
dependent upon a number of factors including, stacking
fault energies (SFE), anti-phase boundary energies
(APB), lattice misﬁt and volume fraction of c

2. Experimental materials and procedures
High temperature ﬂow stresses were investigated
using ﬁve experimental single crystal Ni-base superal-
loys containing varying levels of Ru or Re. To minimize
the eﬀect of minor elemental additions (B, C, N, S and
O) and compositional variations due to processing,
chemistries of the experimental alloys ( Table 1 ), were
obtained by doping a vacuum induction melted (VIM)
solute-lean base alloy with varying levels of Re or Ru.
Other than the absence of Re from AC2000, levels of
refractory alloying additions are typical to those found
in other /C212third generation/C213single crystal Ni-base superal-
loys. The nominal compositions of the other four alloys
are identical with the exception of 3 wt% of Ru in
AC2001, 6 wt% of Ru in AC2002, 3 and 6 wt% Re in
AC2003 and AC2004, respectively.
All ﬁve alloys were solidiﬁed as single crystal rods
measuring 12.7 mm in diameter and 100 mm in length
using a modiﬁed laboratory-scale Bridgman furnace.
The as-cast samples were solution heat-treated in a vac-
uum furnace using a long, ramped cycle rising in steps to
1364 /C176 C, at which temperature the material was held for
20 h to ensure homogenization. The solution heat treat-
ment was then followed by a primary age for 5 h at
1140 /C176 C and secondary age of 16 h at 870 /C176 C.
Flow stresses of single phase c and c
0 alloys were also
investigated. The single phase c and c0 alloys were derived
from two nominally identical single crystal superalloys
with and without Ru additions, RR2101 and RR2100,
respectively. The compositions of RR2100 and RR2101
(in wt%) were Ni–12Co–2.5Cr–9.0W–6.4Re–6.0
Al–5.5Ta–0.15Hf and Ni–12Co–2.5Cr–9.0W–6.4Re–
6.0Al–5.5Ta–2.0Ru–0.15Hf, respectively. Atom probe
tomography (APT) and transmission electron micros-
copy (TEM) techniques were previously used to
determine the chemical compositions [10,11] associated
with the constituent phases ( Table 1 ). The single phase
c and c
0 alloys were also cast into single crystals using
the same modiﬁed Bridgman furnace. As-solidiﬁed crys-
tals of the c phase were homogenized at 1360 /C176 C for 48 h,
while the c0 crystals were homogenized for 48 h at
1280 /C176 C.
The miniature ETMT, Fig. 1 , developed at the Na-
tional Physical Laboratory, UK was used to evaluate
ﬂow stresses at high temperatures. Tensile specimens
with geometries measuring 40 · 1 · 1 mm were wire elec-
trode discharge machined (EDM) from the homoge-
nized single crystals. Samples were then subjected to
light grinding and polishing on all surfaces to ensure
both surface smoothness and good electrical contact
during the test. Coupled with a 1 kN load cell, the
ETMT system utilizes a DC current to provide direct
heating of the specimen. Thermocouples attached to
the center of the gage length monitor specimen temper-
ature and micro transducers are used to measure overall
displacement during deformation. Probes attached
across a /C24 2 mm section of the gage length record subtle

was assessed. No changes in the morphology of c

Ru. Micrographs depicting the structures of AC2002

the volume fraction of c

the overall volume fraction of c0 precipitates, but larger

4. Discussion
The high temperature mechanical properties of Ni-
base single crystal superalloys are primarily reliant on
precipitation and solid solution strengthening. With c0
precipitate volume fractions in advanced single crystal
superalloys approaching /C24 70%, little opportunity exists
for further optimization of the microstructures. Hence,
recent initiatives to increase strength and extend the
temperature capability of single crystal superalloys have
relied on solid solution strengthening of both the c and
c
0 phases. The incorporation of Ru into single crystal
superalloys has primarily been aimed stabilizing the
microstructure against the formation of TCP phases at
elevated temperature [12]. However, in this investiga-
tion, Ru additions were also shown to be highly eﬀective
as a potent solid-solution strengthening element.

The eﬀectiveness of the additions in strengthening the
experimental single crystal superalloys varied signiﬁ-
cantly. Although identical amounts of Re and Ru were
added in wt%, approximately twice the at.% of Ru has
been accommodated within the structure of the alloy
due to the density of Ru being signiﬁcantly lower than
that of Re. In spite of this, Re additions were clearly
demonstrated to be more eﬀective in increasing the ﬂow
stress of the alloy at the temperatures investigated. This
is interesting since elemental Re and Ru exhibit a num-
ber of similarities. In addition to forming complete bin-
ary solid solutions, both elements form hexagonal close
packed (HCP) structures and have extremely high melt-
ing points. Possibly due to the extreme partitioning
behavior, even small additions of Re are suﬃcient to en-
able substantial accumulation of Re within the c phase
to eﬀectively lower the SFE. Despite being present in
higher overall concentrations, less partitioning of Ru
into the c phase occurs resulting in only a modest de-
crease of SFE.
Results from the testing of the single phase c and c
0
alloys are consistent with these ﬁndings. The presence
of Ru in conjunction with Re in RR2101 c results in
additional strengthening when compared to RR2100 c.
Since some Ru is soluble within the intermetallic c0
phase, a modest amount of strengthening was also mea-
sured in RR2101 c0. Unlike Re, the Ru additions appear
to provide a certain amount of solid solution strengthen-
ing to both the c and c
0 phases. The eﬀects of these
alloying additions are likely to have implications on
the resulting creep performance of the single crystal
superalloys, particularly at low to intermediate temper-
atures (750 /C176 C–900 /C176 C) and corresponding high stresses
where deformation of both the c and c
0 phases may
occur.
The results from this study clearly show that Re addi-
tions are more eﬀective in strengthening single crystal
superalloys than Ru additions. However, unlike Re
additions that also promote the formation of deleterious
TCP phases at elevated temperatures, Ru additions can
be eﬀectively utilized in a number of diﬀerent roles. As
well as serving as an eﬀective solid-solution strengthen-
ing element in high refractory content Ni-base single
crystal superalloys, Ru additions are able to eﬀectively
stabilize the microstructure and suppress the growth of
Re-rich TCP phases. This leads to substantial increases
in creep rupture lives under conditions where micro-
structural stability is required. Other preliminary results
indicate that Ru additions also improve oxidation and
hot corrosion resistance. Experiments designed to quan-
titatively assess these eﬀects are currently in progress.
5. Conclusions
An ETMT unit was used to characterize the ﬂow
stresses of a number of experimental single crystal super-
alloys containing additions of Ru or Re. Flow stresses of
single phase c and c
0 alloys representative of the constit-
uent phases in actual superalloys were also measured.
The following conclusions could be drawn from this
work:
1. Tensile tests conducted at 900 /C176 C and 1100 /C176 C reveal
that both Re and Ru additions provide a high degree
of solid solution strengthening. Despite a lower over-
all content in at.%, Re additions were revealed to be
more eﬀective than Ru additions.
2. Additions of both Re and Ru to the experimental
alloys resulted in a decrease in the volume fraction
of c
0 precipitates.
3. Flow stresses corresponding to the c0 phase are three
to ﬁve times higher than the c phase at 900 /C176 C and
1100 /C176 C.
4. Ru additions appear to provide strengthening to both
the c and c0 phases. Along with a signiﬁcant increase
in ﬂow stresses of the single phase c alloy with Ru, a
modest increase in the ﬂow stress of the single phase
c
0 alloy was also measured.
