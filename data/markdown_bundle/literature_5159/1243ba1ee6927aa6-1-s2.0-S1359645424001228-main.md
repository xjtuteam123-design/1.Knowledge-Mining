ABSTRACT 
Developing strong-yet-ductile alloys with wide temperature capabilities is crucial for modern industrial and 
scientific applications; however, this is often difficult to realize using conventional wisdom. In this work, we 
innovatively designed a nanoparticle-strengthened high-entropy alloy (HEA) through elaborate compositional 
design and thermomechanical regulation. The designed HEA exhibits extraordinary mechanical properties across 
a wide temperature range ( 196~1000 
◦
C), resulted from the unique microstructures consisting of high-density 
hierarchical L1
2 
nanoparticles combined with serrated grain boundaries. A high ultimate tensile strength of 1355 
MPa combined with a ductility of 35.0 % were achieved when tested at 25 
◦
C. The tensile strength and ductility 
were enhanced simultaneously with the decrease of temperature from 25 to 196 
◦
C. More prominently, the 
intermediate-temperature intergranular embrittlement, a severe ductility loss at 600~800 
◦
C for most conven -
tional superalloys, is effectively suppressed even under high tensile stress, which is attributed to the high 
resistance to crack propagation of serrated grain boundaries. An apparent anomalous yielding behavior occurred 
at 700 
◦
C, leading to an ultrahigh yield strength of ~1045 MPa while maintaining a large ductility of ~22.1 %. 
Meanwhile, an excellent strength of 325 MPa can still be maintained even at 1000 
◦
C combined with a good 
ductility over 25 %. Systematic characterizations revealed that such a temperature-dependent mechanical 
behavior is highly related to various deformation substructures activated at different temperatures. This work 
would open a pathway to developing a series of advanced alloys with superior strength and ductility over a wide 
temperature range. 
1. Introduction 
To meet the harsh service requirements under extreme environ -
ments, designing strong-yet-ductile alloys with wide temperature ca -
pabilities is particularly essential for advanced modern industries, such 
as aviation, aerospace, cryogenic offshore, etc. [1 – 4] . However, such 
materials are difficult to screen out among conventional alloys, whose 
properties are usually limited to specific and relatively narrow tem -
perature ranges. For example, the typical ferritic and martensitic steels 
usually exhibit excellent tensile properties at room temperature, but 
suffer from severe embrittlement at cryogenic temperatures, which is 
caused by the deleterious grain-boundary segregation or the higher 
strength of the matrix than that of the grain boundaries [ 5 , 6 ]. The 
sinking of the Titanic ship is one of the catastrophic metallurgical ac -
cidents caused by low-temperature embrittlement. On the contrary, al -
loys with excellent properties at low temperatures often do not perform 
well at high temperatures, such as austenite steels [ 7 , 8 ]. Despite 
long-term efforts in alloy development, significant challenges remain in

Recently, the discovery of high-entropy alloys (HEAs) based on 
multi-principal elements is considered a flexible paradigm for devel -
oping novel alloys with desirable properties [ 9 , 10 ]. Up to now, various 
HEAs have been developed by tuning the chemical compositions and 
phase constitutions [11] . Nevertheless, there still exist some significant 
limitations among these HEAs. For example, HEAs with the single 
face-centered-cubic (FCC) phase structure have been demonstrated to 
exhibit excellent mechanical properties at room and cryogenic temper -
atures [ 12 , 13 ]. The low stacking fault energy (SFE) of the FCC HEA 
enables them to have higher twinning capability during deformation, 
leading to enhanced mechanical properties [ 14 , 15 ]. However, this 
strengthening effect is difficult to be activated at elevated temperatures, 
causing obvious softening [16] . Refractory body-centered-cubic (BCC) 
HEAs are considered as one of the most potential high-temperature 
structural materials, but their applications are greatly limited to 
room-temperature embrittlement and poor high-temperature oxidation 
resistance [ 17 , 18 ]. Precipitation-hardened HEAs were therefore devel -
oped to overcome the shortcomings of single-phase HEAs by introducing 
various kinds of hard intermetallic particles, such as σ and μ [ 19 , 20 ], 
Laves [21] , B2-NiAl [ 22 , 23 ], carbides [24] , and L1
2 
phase [25 – 27] . For 
example, the HEAs strengthened by B2 phase can maintain a good 
combination of strength and ductility from cryogenic temperatures to 
elevated temperatures [ 22 , 23 ]. Nevertheless, the working temperature 
of these types of HEAs is limited to 450 
◦
C due to the rapid softening at 
higher temperatures [ 22 , 23 ]. In contrast, the HEAs reinforced by 
coherent L1
2 
nanoparticles can potentially maintain decent strengths at 
higher temperatures [28] . Unfortunately, the biggest limitation of this 
type HEAs lies in severe intergranular embrittlement at intermediate 
temperatures (usually 0.5~0.7 Tm, T
m 
is the melting point) [ 29 , 30 ]. 
Such a serious embrittlement problem also widely exists in many other 
high-temperature structural materials, including Ni-based [31] , 
Co-based [32] , and Fe-based superalloys [33] . The abrupt and rapid 
rupture along grain boundaries is almost inevitable, especially under the 
high-stress level, which sets one of the strictest constraints on their 
practical applications. The main reason for the 
intermediate-temperature embrittlement may stem from the synthetic 
damage effects of penetrating oxygen and stress concentration near the 
grain boundaries [30] . High volume fraction of L1
2 
phase contributes to 
excellent strength but leads to an easy crack propagation along the grain 
boundary, which is difficult to compromise. Thus, it is extremely chal -
lenging to simultaneously obtain excellent strength-ductility synergies 
across a wide temperature range from cryogenic to elevated 
temperatures. 
To address this long-standing critical issue, we innovatively designed 
a new kind of HEA with unique hierarchical nanostructures via 
compositional design and thermomechanical regulation. This HEA ex -
hibits excellent strength-ductility combinations from 196 to 1000 
◦
C, 
resulted from high-density hierarchical L1
2 
nanoparticles and serrated 
grain boundaries. More prominently, this alloy can not only overcome 
the serious intermediate-temperature embrittlement, but also exhibit 
anomalous yielding behavior at 700 
◦
C, leading to an ultrahigh yield 
strength of ~1045 MPa and maintaining a large ductility of ~22.1 %. 
These findings will pave the way for developing high-performance alloys 
with superb mechanical properties over a wide temperature range.

The phase constitution was further verified by XRD. Consistent with the 
result calculated by Thermo-Calc, the designed HEA exhibits an “ FCC +
L1

The EBSD inverse pole figure (IPF) mapping ( Fig. 1 c) reveals the fully 
recrystallized equiaxial grains with an average grain size of 95 μ m. 
Instead of the straight grain boundaries, this HEA exhibits unique 
serrated grain boundaries ( Fig. 1 d). High-density primary flower-like 
precipitates can be observed in the interior of grains ( Fig. 1 e), which 
are formed during the slow cooling process from the high solid solution 
temperature. Besides the primary precipitates, secondary spherical 
precipitates were also formed within the matrix channels ( Fig. 1 f). Aging 
process at an intermediate temperature of 800 
◦
C contributed to the 
formation of secondary spherical nanoprecipitates. The average sizes for 
the primary and secondary precipitates are determined to be around 
1000 and 75 nm, respectively. Meanwhile, some elongated precipitates 
are distributed along the grain boundaries, contributing to the formation 
of serrated grain boundaries during the slow cooling process. 
The scanning TEM-bright field (STEM-BF) images and atomic scale 
resolution images were taken to further confirm the morphology and 
crystal structure of the precipitates. Similar to the SEM results, the 
designed HEA shows multiscale precipitates in the grain interior ( Fig. 2 ). 
Interestingly, some nanoparticles can be observed inside the primary L1
2 
precipitates ( Fig. 2 b), which cannot be well detected by SEM due to its 
low resolution. Atomic scale resolution images and corresponding Fast

primary and secondary precipitates show the ordered L1
2 
crystal 
structure, which are coherent with the FCC matrix. The nanoparticles 
formed in the primary L1
2 
phase were identified as the disordered FCC 
phase ( Fig. 2 e). This phenomenon is considered an inverse precipitation 
behavior during the aging process [37] .

4.2. Mechanism of the anomalous yielding behavior 
Besides the excellent ductility at intermediate temperatures resulted 
from the serrated grain boundaries, the anomalous yielding behavior at 
700 
◦
C also led to a significant increase in yield strength up to 1045 ± 15 
MPa. To reveal the root of this phenomenon, the dislocation structure of 
the sample at a strain of 5 % was examined by STEM under different tilt 
conditions ( Fig. 10 a~c). In addition to the dislocations gathered at the 
FCC-L1
2 
phase boundary, some long straight dislocations can also be 
observed inside the primary L1
2 
particles, which can be determined as 
the screw dislocations according to their parallel relationship with 
Burgers vector ( b = < 10 1 > ). Meanwhile, the enlarged STEM-dark field 
(STEM-DF) image (inset in Fig. 10 a) further confirmed that the < 10 1 >
screw dislocation inside the primary L1
2 
phase was dissociated into two 
superlattice partial dislocation pairs separated by the APB. 
The cross-slip behavior of the superlattice dislocation pairs is 
thought to be the main reason for the anomalous yielding behavior [50] . 
Thus, trace analyses along the Burgers vector ( b = < 10 1 > ) were con -
ducted under different tilting conditions to reveal the dissociated 
behavior. As presented in the STEM-DF images in Fig. 10 (b) and (c), 
three segments X, P, and C of the superlattice screw dislocations show 
varied separation distances under different tilting conditions, indicating 
they are dissociated on different planes. When the beam direction (BD) 
is along [ 11 1 ] direction, the segments X and C can be clearly separated. 
In contrast, segment P is hard to be distinguished, indicating that it is 
close to an edge-on condition ( Fig. 10 b). When the BD tilts to the 
[ 10 1 ] direction, segments X and P are clearly separated into two pairs. In 
comparison, segment C is overlapped ( Fig. 10 c). Under this tilting 
condition, the cubic cross-slip (010) plane is projected to an edge-on 
condition, leading to an overlapped superlattice dislocation. These re -
sults reveal that the segments P, X, and C are dissociated on the (111), 
( 11 1 ) and (010) planes, respectively. The superlattice dislocation in 
segment C appears to undergo a cubic cross slip from the primary py -
ramidal (111) plane to the cubic (010) plane. Moreover, since the cubic 
(010) plane is not the glide plane for L1
2 
phase, the cross-slipped 
superlattice dislocation is thus sessile or locked on it, causing a strong 
pinning effect on the further movement of dislocations, which is also 
called Kear-Wilsdorf (KW) lock [ 51 , 52 ]. Based on these analyses, we can 
conclude that the cubic cross-slip behavior is the main reason for the 
anomalous yielding behavior observed in the present HEA.

4.4. Implications for future alloy design 
Developing high-performance structural materials by alloy design 
has consistently been a hot but challenging topic for years. In this work, 
we obtained a strong yet ductile HEA across a wide temperature range 
through elaborate compositional design and thermomechanical regula -
tion. The design strategy is proposed based on the following two aspects. 
First, we solved the severe intermediate-temperature embrittlement via 
introducing serrated grain boundaries with high resistance to cracks 
propagation. The slow cooling rate together with large lattice misfit

5. Conclusion 
In this study, we designed a novel nanoprecipitation-strengthened 
HEA with superior mechanical properties across a wide temperature 
range ( 196~1000 
◦
C) through elaborate compositional design and 
thermomechanical regulation. The microstructural features, 
temperature-dependent tensile properties, as well as deformation and 
fracture mechanisms were studied in detail. Based on the results and 
analyses, some key conclusions can be drawn as follows: 
(1) The designed HEA shows unique microstructures consisting of 
high-density hierarchical L1
2 
nanoparticles combined with 
serrated grain boundaries. The volume fraction of L1
2 
phase is 
estimated to be 58.5 %. The L1
2 
phase is strongly rich in elements 
Ni, Ti, Al, Ta, and depleted in elements Co, Cr, and Fe, while the 
FCC phase shows a reverse trend of element partition. The 
serration of grain boundaries is attributed to the formation and 
movement of coarse primary L1
2 
particles near the grain 
boundaries during slow cooling rate from the solution tempera -
ture to the intermediate temperature. 
(2) Excellent tensile properties across a wide temperature from 196 
to 1000 
◦
C were achieved in the present HEA. The samples exhibit 
a high UTS of 1355 MPa together with a large ductility of 35.0 % 
at 25 
◦
C. The deformation is dominated by the dislocations within 
the FCC matrix and APB-coupled superlattice dislocations in the 
primary L1
2 
particles. Both the strength and ductility were 
enhanced simultaneously when deformed at 196 
◦
C, which can 
be attributed to the decrease of SFE and activation of intersected 
SFs. 
(3) The intermediate-temperature embrittlement at 700 and 800 
◦
C 
is significantly suppressed by engineering serrated grain bound -
aries. When deformed at this temperature range, although the 
grain boundaries suffered from serious stress concentration and 
environmental damage in the oxidizing atmosphere, the serrated 
grain boundaries offered high resistance to cracks propagation, 
completely eliminating the intergranular brittle fractures and 
resulting in large tensile ductilities of 16.2 – 22.1 %. 
(4) An obvious anomalous yielding behavior occurred at 700 
◦
C, 
leading to a high yield strength of ~1045 MPa. Such a pro -
nounced yielding anomaly arises from the cross-slip of super -
lattice dislocations from the primary (111) plane to the cubic 
(010) plane, which is driven by the anisotropy of the APBE be -
tween the (111) and (010) planes.
