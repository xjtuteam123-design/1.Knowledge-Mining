ABSTRACT 
Fully dense powder metallurgy (PM) Ti – 4Fe – 0.2O – 3Cu – 0.4Si alloy was produced employing spark plasma sin -
tering (SPS) and hot extrusion aiming to develop an inexpensive high strength dual-phase ( α + β ) Ti alloy. The 
microstructure consisted of globularized fine grain α phase dispersed in the β matrix having the sharp texture of 
< 10 10 > //extrusion direction. The tensile strength of alloy (~1060 MPa) is comparatively higher than the 
conventional Ti – 6Al – 4V alloy produced by a similar method. Based on theoretical calculations the grain 
refinement and solid solution phenomena were the principal strengthening mechanisms. Additionally, it was 
found that Si solute remarkably enhanced the microstructure stability (using in situ EBSD observation) and yield 
strength of alloy at high-temperature. 
1. Introduction 
Dual-phase titanium alloys are known as high-performance struc -
tural materials that are widely used in aerospace, automotive, and 
biomedical applications due to their exceptional strength-to-density 
ratio, corrosion resistance, and biocompatibility [ 1 , 2 ]. The Ti – 6Al – 4V 
is a well-established dual-phase Ti alloy, however, its lamellar 
morphology of α + β microstructure generally lower the ductility and 
work-hardening which consequently limit their applications compared 
to other metals such as steels [ 3 ]. In general, dual-phase Ti alloy with 
equiaxed or globular microstructure – obtained by thermomechanical 
treatment – is much preferred since it can exhibit higher static and dy -
namic mechanical properties and offer a better trade-off between 
strength and ductility compared to lamellar microstructure [ 4 ]. Several 
studies have been carried out on the strengthening mechanisms and 
high-temperature behavior of dual-phase Ti alloys. For instance, Liang 
et al. [ 5 ] studied the strengthening mechanism of basket-weave dual 
phase titanium alloys using Hall-Petch relationship. Recently the ther -
mal stability of additive manufactured dual-phase Ti alloy has been 
studied by Formanoir et al. [ 6 ]. Although this category of materials is 
used widely, their fabrication cost is affected profoundly due to utilizing 
expensive β stabilizer elements such as V, Nb, Ta, and Mo. In this study, 
we used ubiquitous materials (Fe, O, Cu, and Si) to fabricate 
high-performance dual-phase Ti alloys employing SPS known as 
state-of-art sintering method by which fully dense PM components can 
be produced [ 7 ]. The selection criteria of alloying elements was based 
on the literature and our studies on Ti-Fe based alloys which have been 
conducted in our lab at Osaka University.

4. Conclusion 
High strength dual-phase PM Ti alloys were produced using inex -
pensive ubiquitous elements. Hot extruded Ti – 4Fe – 0.2O – 3Cu – 0.4Si 
alloy showed the optimal tensile properties comprising globularized α 
phase in the β matrix. A theoretical evaluation of the potential 
strengthening mechanisms indicated that solid solution and grain 
refinement effects were the main strengthening factors and the calcu -
lated value was completely matched to the experimental result. For 
example, these dominant mechanisms contributed to the increase of 
125 MPa yield stress in Ti – 4Fe – 0.2O – 3Cu – 0.4Si compared to the con -
ventional Ti – 6Al – 4V alloy during tensile stress. Additionally, the effect 
of Si element on the high-temperature microstructure stability and yield 
strength improvement was evidenced using in situ EBSD observation 
and high-temperature tensile test.
