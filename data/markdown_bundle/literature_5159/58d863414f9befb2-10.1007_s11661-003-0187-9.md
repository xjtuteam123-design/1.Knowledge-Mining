Toughness and Strength Characteristics of Nb-W-Si Ternary
Alloys Prepared by Arc Melting
JIANGBO SHA, HISATOSHI HIRAI, TATSUO TABARU, AKIRA KITAHARA, HIDETOSHI
UENO, and SHUJI HANADA
This article describes the room-temperature and high-temperature mechanical properties and failure
modes of series Nb-W-Si alloys—Nb-10W, Nb-10Si, Nb-10Si-5W, Nb-10W-5Si, and Nb-10W-
10Si—prepared by arc melting. For the Nb-10W alloy, the microstructure was a monolithic Nb solid
solution (Nb
ss) with a grain size up to a few hundred microns, while the other four alloys consisted
of primary Nb ss and a eutectic of Nb ss/Nb5Si3 (5-3 silicide) as a result of replacing Nb with Si.
Among all alloys, the Nb-10W showed the highest fracture toughness of about 15.3 MPa 1/2 and
the lowest 0.2 pct yield compressive strength of 90 MPa at 1670 K. Conversely, the Nb-10Si-10W
had the highest 0.2 pct yield strength of about 330 MPa at 1670 K and the lowest fracture tough-
ness of 8.2 MPa
1/2. It is suggested that toughness is supplied by the metallic Nb ss phase, while
high-temperature strength is mainly provided by the brittle silicide phase. For the Nb-10W alloy
with the monolithic Nb
ss, intergranular cleavagelike crack propagation is the fracture mode at room
temperature, and dislocation movement within the grains and grain-boundary sliding are the domi-
nant modes of high-temperature failure. With two-phase Nb
ss/Nb5Si3 microstructures, the compres-
sive damage of all four alloys at high temperature was dominated by debonding of the interfaces
between the Nb
ss and the silicide; however, the fracture mode at room temperature is transgranular,
controlled by the primary Nb ss cleavage.

I. INTRODUCTION
WITH a relatively high melting point of about 2743 K
and room-temperature ductility, Nb is one of the most
promising refractory metals for future high-temperature struc-
tural materials, and it is a candidate to substitute for Ni-based
superalloys for service at extremely elevated temperatures.
Since the 1960s, many studies have been performed in an
effort to develop various Nb-based alloys.
[1,2,3] Approaches
to improving the high-temperature strength of Nb have
involved solid-solution strengthening, or forming composites
with Nb and intermetallics such as silicides
[3–12] and alu-
minides.[13–16] Systematic development of Nb/intermetallic
combinations, defined as in-situ composites, has been
reported extensively; among them, Nb-Al and Nb-Si binaries
are typical. The Nb-Al and Nb-Si binaries have equilibrium
phases of Nb solid solution (Nb
ss)/Nb3Al aluminide and
Nbss/Nb5Si3 silicide,[17] respectively, in which the Nbss offers
room-temperature ductility, and the aluminides and silicides
supply strength and creep resistance at elevated temperatures.
It is well known that structural materials used in advanced
aerospace or turbine engine systems must possess a bal-
ance of properties, including ductility and toughness at low
temperatures, strength and creep resistance at elevated tem-
peratures, as well as environmental stability. Significant work
on environmental stability has been directed toward pro-
ducing compact, adherent, and self-recovering oxide scales
on Nb-based alloys, or alloys with the elements Ti, V, Hf,
Cr, Mn, Si, and Be, to reduce oxygen diffusion and solu-
bility.
[2,3,19–22] Considering mechanical properties, the diffi-
culty seems to be serious in optimization of the toughness
at room temperature and of the strength to resist load at
elevated temperatures, because both these parameters are
usually incompatible. The strength of the Nb-based in-situ
composites at elevated temperatures, however, has been of
concern as a preferential issue and has been increased as
high as possible. This effort is for balancing strength and
toughness. Successful improvement in strength has been
achieved as high as 600 MPa even at 1770 K by alloying,
[9,31]
frequently accompanied by thermomechanical processing
such as directional solidification
[7,8,9] and hot isostatic press-
ing (HIP). [3,4,5] Extensive studies show that the bending
strength of the as-cast Nb-10Si alloy is 400 MPa at 1470 K
and is 250 MPa for an extruded specimen.
[4,5] The Nb-16Si
prepared by HIP retains a bending strength of about 400 MPa
at 1670 K, which is mainly attributable to the increase in
the volume fraction of Nb
5Si3 and closure of internal cracks
by HIP.[5] A directionally solidified Nb-24.7Ti-8.2Hf-2.0Cr-
1.9Al-16Si alloy shows a relatively high tensile strength of
about 370 MPa at 1470 K.
[18] Continuously increasing the
Si content toward the eutectic composition of 18 mol pct,
accompanied by 22 mol pct Ti prepared by directional solidi-
fication, produces compressive yield strengths of about 1000
and 450 MPa at 1470 and 1690 K,
[7,8,9] respectively. Further
alloying by Mo and W of the Nb-Si binary formed compo-
sitions of Nb-10Mo-10Ti-18Si and Nb-10Mo-15W-10Ti-
18Si, which have an outstanding strength level. Even at
1770 K, the compressive yield strengths of the Nb-10Mo-
10Ti-18Si and Nb-10Mo-15W-10Ti-18Si
[7–9,31] that were
prepared by arc melting and directional solidification are
550 and 650 MPa, respectively. The elements W and Mo
are most efficient for improving high-temperature strength,

low Si and W contents were studied.
II. EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURES
The Nb-W-Si ternary diagram at 298 K, close to the Nb
corner, is shown in Figure 1, in which the compositions
investigated in this work are denoted by solid dots. The com-
positions Nb-10W, Nb-10W-5Si, Nb-10W-10Si, Nb-10Si,
and Nb-10Si-5W can be classified into two Nb-10W-xSi and
Nb-10Si-yW series, for individual discussion of the effects
of W and Si on the mechanical properties. Elements with a
purity of 99.95 mass pct or higher were used. Button ingots
were prepared by arc melting in 1 atm argon 5 or 6 times
to homogenize the compositions throughout the ingots; these
were then arc melted again 2 to 3 times to cast the samples
into bars. The bars were finally annealed at 1870 K under
vacuum for 100 hours, which ensured the equilibrium
microstructures. The microstructures were observed using
a scanning electron microscope (SEM), and the compos-
ition of each phase was analyzed using energy-dispersive
X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) in the SEM. Vickers hardness

Fractographic observations of the crack-growth path and
fracture surface in Figure 5 reveal the fracture mechanism
of the Nb-10W alloy with the monolithic Nb
ss. As seen in
Figure 5(a), a crack initiated at the notch propagates in a
zigzag way, within which the crack turns with large angles
and extends a long distance in each deflection. Bridging
(arrows marked A in Figure 5(a)) and branching (arrows
marked B in Figure 5(a)) toughening mechanisms appear
during crack growth. By comparing Figure 5(a) with Fig-
ure 5(b), the angles and distances of each crack deflection
in the zigzag path are found to be in good agreement with
either the shape or dimension of the Nb
ss grains. Thus, one
can determine that the fracture is intergranular. The Nb ss
grains are polyhedrons; their sizes are up to a few hundred
microns. The arrow in Figure 5(b) points out the typical
feature of a crack advancing from one grain to the next one,
and the cleavage river pattern directs the fracture or crack
initiation. The fracture initiation may take place at the grain
boundary ahead of the main crack tip and force the main
crack growth by a bridging mechanism (arrow A in Fig-
ure 5(a)), where highly located plastic deformation ( i.e.,
tearing) may be exhibited. The crack also propagates con-
tinuously from one grain to the next (arrow C in Figure 5(a))
by concentration of dislocations on the grain boundary
(theory of dislocation concentration). This conclusion is
deduced for any inclusion related to the cleavage fracture
that has not been discovered at the fracture-initiation sites
or river pattern. Figure 5(c) shows a dense cleavage river
pattern traversing multiple facets in the crack-growth direc-
tion, where the ridges perpendicular to the crack-growth
direction reflect the undulant boundary within a grain. A
highly magnified view of the river-pattern region shown in
Figure 5(d) further reveals the multiple facets. It is evident
that the movement and concentration of the dislocations con-
trol the deformation of the Nb-10W alloy, but there is no
clear evidence of ductile fracture such as a dimple on the
fractured surface. This result also proves indirectly that the
Nb-10W alloy is an entirely monolithic microstructure, i.e.,
no small inclusions or precipitates were present. Toughening
of the monolithic Nb
ss is performed by the crack-deflection,
bridging, and branching mechanisms during crack propa-
gation through the intergranular fracture.

IV. DISCUSSION
As mentioned in Section III, toughness and strength conflict
with each other in the Nb-Si-W ternary. We have tried to
obtain a balance of low-temperature toughness and high-
temperature strength in this work, using the alloying strategy
implied in the previously developed Nb-22Ti-15W-10Mo-
18Si alloy, whose strength is 650 MPa even at 1770 K.
[9,31]
Proper compositions of the Nb-Si-W ternary suggested for
application depend on the synthesized requirements in prop-
erties, i.e., toughness, strength, and even specific strength
and environmental resistance. The response of mechanisms
and properties related to composition and microstructure
should be understood in detail prior to alloy design for the
engineering service. Discussions on the strengthening and
toughening mechanisms of the monolithic Nb
ss and Nbss/5-3
silicide microstructures are described subsequently.

1. The Nb-10W alloy displays a monolithic Nbss microstruc-
ture. By further Si additions, the 5-3 silicide phase appears
in addition to the Nbss phase. Nb-10Si and Nb-Si-W alloys
show a two-phase microstructure composed of the pri-

ss and a eutectic secondary Nbss and 5-3 silicide.
2. The metallic Nbss and the 5-3 silicide contribute to improve
room-temperature toughness and high-temperature strength,
respectively. With the monolithic Nb
ss, the Nb-10W alloy
has the highest fracture toughness of 15.3 MPa 1/2. The
Nb-10Si-10W alloy, however, shows the highest high-
temperature compressive strength, 600 and 330 MPa,
respectively at 1470 K and 1670 K.
3. The grain boundaries and interface play key roles in dam-
age and fracture at both room and high temperatures. The
Nb
ss/5-3 silicide interface in the Nb-Si-W ternaries is the
crack-initiation site; transgranular cleavage fracture and
interface debonding in a shear manner are the failure
modes at room and high temperatures, respectively. As
for the Nb-10W alloy with the monolithic Nb

cleavage, while dislocation movement within grains and
grain-boundary sliding dominate the high-temperature
compression damage.
