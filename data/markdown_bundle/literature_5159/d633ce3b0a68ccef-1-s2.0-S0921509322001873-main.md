1. Introduction 
‘High entropy alloy (HEA) ’ is a concept for developing complex 
multi-component metallic materials in a vast and unemployed realm of 
alloy compositions [ 1 – 4 ]. In recent years, a variety of novel HEAs with 
excellent ambient/high-temperature/cryogenic mechanical properties 
[ 5 – 11 ], remarkable corrosion/wear/irradiation resistance [ 12 – 17 ] , etc. 
have been designed based on this concept. The definition of HEAs could 
be composition-based (five or more principal elements, and each 
element has concentration between 5% and 35% [ 3 ]), or entropy-based 
(mixing/configurational entropy of ≥ 1.5 R ( R : gas constant, 8.314 
J/(mol ⋅ K))) [ 4 ]. Solid-solutions rather than intermetallic phases tend to 
form in this series of alloys due to ‘high entropy effect ’ [ 3 , 7 ], but such 
solid solutions are proved to have various degrees of metastability [ 18 ], 
which would be transformed to other phases under different pro -
duction/processing/operating conditions [ 19 – 21 ]. This unique and 
interesting characteristic has attracted increasing attentions in recent 
years, and is promising to significantly broaden the applications of HEAs 
[ 18 – 21 ]. 
Formation of fine-grain structure is a positive factor for enhancement 
in mechanical properties of HEAs [ 22 – 24 ]. However, it is noticed that, 
such structure generally requires complex fabrication strategies such as 
cold-rolling and heat-treatment, additive manufacturing, spark plasma 
sintering, etc. [ 22 – 28 ], which is to some extent time-consuming and 
energy-intensive, and if fine-grain structure could be easily formed in 
HEAs via casting or during cooling process after melting, it would 
greatly simplify the processing route to fabricate fine-grain HEAs, which 
would undoubtedly be beneficial to their development. 
Solid-state phase transformation is an effective way to form refined 
microstructure in HEAs and medium entropy alloys (MEAs) [ 29 – 32 ].

segregation of Al reaches a critical value, its diffusion through the 
boundary between FCC and BCC phases would be possible to (1) cause 
the instability of simi-coherent phase boundary, leading to arc-shape 
structure with much lower degree of coherent in phase boundary, or 
(2) increase the resistance of grain growth of FCC phase, which would 
cause the zigzag structure with the change in growing direction, or (3) 
the mixture of the conditions above, resulting in branched structure. 
This phenomenon could also explain the ORs between FCC and BCC 
phases that greatly deviate from ideal K – S OR, and based on the analysis 
above, the strategies for inhibition of the diffusion of Al, such as high 
cooling rate or minor alloying with proper elements, would be prom -
ising to eliminate the arc-shape, zigzag and branched structures, and 
such issue requires further investigation for clarification. 
Moreover, according to the microstructure formation and possible 
phase evolution process analyzed above, it is interesting that, although 
this HEA is Fe-based, the phase formation in this alloy is much more 
similar to typical HEAs rather than traditional Fe-based alloys. It is 
known that, FCC phase (Austenite) generally forms in traditional Fe- 
based alloys before BCC phase (Ferrite) during cooling [ 39 ], but in 
contrast, this Fe-based HEA shows BCC → FCC transformation during 
cooling, which has the similar trend compared to some Fe-containing 
HEAs [ 21 , 40 ], and ordered BCC phase also forms in this Fe-based 
HEA, which is commonly observed in various BCC phase in HEAs [ 21 , 
40 ]. Accordingly, it is reasonable for utilizing the phase formation rule 
of HEAs to understand the phenomenon of structural evolution of this 
Fe-based HEA. According to the theory on the correlation between VEC 
and phase formation, when the VEC is less than 8 and higher than 6.87, 
it is possible to form FCC + BCC structure [ 41 ]. Based on the calculation 
method given by Guo et al. [ 34 ], the VEC value of this Fe-based HEA is 
7.5, which locates in the region for the formation of FCC + BCC struc -
ture. However, based on previous investigations, the solid solution could 
be stabilized at high temperature by ‘high entropy effect ’ [ 3 ], and such 
structure could be retained using rapid-solidification although it has 
high VEC over the BCC-stable region [ 21 ]. After decomposition, the FCC 
and BCC phases have re-distribution of elements, and both of them could 
be more stable at low temperature. Therefore, it could be deduced that, 
the BCC solid solution is stable at high temperature, but this structure is 
metastable and has tendency for solid-state phase transformation and 
compositional segregation at lower temperature.

Our work proves that, it is available to realize fine grain formation 
without complex and time-consuming mechanical and heat treatments 
in HEAs. The formation of fine FCC grains caused by solid-state phase 
transformation in this Fe-based HEA during casting-related process is 
very beneficial for the development of this series of alloys due to cost/ 
energy/time-saving, and this phenomenon provides a motivation for 
the development of such novel series of HEAs. In addition, it is expected 
that the as-cast fine-grain HEAs would have great potential for explo -
ration, while further compositional optimization for tuning mechanical 
properties of FCC and BCC phases, interaction between two phases, and 
further refining grain size of these two phases, would be beneficial to 
enhance comprehensive mechanical properties of this series of HEAs, 
which requires detailed investigations in the future.
