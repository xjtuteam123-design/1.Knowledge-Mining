Two Fe-Al-Ti alloys with coherent aFe,Al (A2) + Fe 2AlTi (L21) microstructures have
been produced and the evolution of the microstructure with aging time has been studied by
light optical and scanning electron microscopy and hardness measurements. The
compressive flow strength, creep properties, brittle-to-ductile-transition temperatures
(BDTT), and oxidation behavior of the alloys have been evaluated. The results show that
the investigated alloys show good flow strength, high creep resistance, and good oxidation
resistance. However, their BDDT is high compared to binary Fe-Al-based alloys and
compared to other Fe-Al-Ti alloys no increase in creep resistance was achieved by
generating coherent microstructures. The latter effect is due to the breakup of the coherent
microstructures when the temperature varies because the compositions and consequently
the volume fractions of the phases vary markedly depending on temperature.
I. INTRODUCTION
Iron aluminide-based alloys are of great interest for
applications at high temperatures in corrosive envi-
ronments since they have low density, good corrosion
resistance, and high wear resistance and they are cost-
efficient as they do not contain strategic elements and
their production and processing is possible with equip-
ment readily available in industry.
1–3 The shortcomings
of these materials are their relatively low strength above
600
/C14 C and their brittleness up to elevated tempera-
tures.4–6 Therefore, considerable research activities have
been carried out in the recent past to improve these
properties. It has been shown that the strength at high
temperatures can be substantially improved by alloying
with Ti.
7–15 This improvement at least partly results
from the stabilization of the L2 1 structure—which is the
ternary equivalent of the binary D0 3 structure—at higher
temperatures by the addition of Ti. 16–19
Inspection of the Fe-Al-Ti system shows that a two-
phase equilibrium between disordered aFe,Al (A2) and
L21-ordered Fe 2AlTi exists, 19,20 where the mismatch of
lattice constants is sufficiently small to generate coher-
ent A2 + L2 1 microstructures.21 Aligned/coherent two-
phase microstructures with A2, B2, D0 3,o rL 2 1 have
been reported to occur in the Fe-Al-X systems with
X = Si, 22 Ti,21,23,24 V,25 Co,26 Ni(+Cr),27–30 Nb,7,31,32
and Ta. 6 By generating such microstructures, Fe-Al-
based alloys may be considerably strengthened at high
temperatures, but only few relevant investigations have
been performed.2,6,7,29,33,34
The aim of this study was to investigate the possibility
of producing coherent microstructures in the Fe-Al-Ti
system and to examine the resulting mechanical proper-
ties and the oxidation behavior of these alloys. For this
purpose, two alloys with compositions situated inside
the two-phase field A2 + L2
1 have been thoroughly
investigated.
II. EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURE
The alloys Fe-23Al-8.5Ti and Fe-21Al-10Ti (at.% is
used throughout if not stated otherwise) were produced
by vacuum induction melting using high purity Fe
(99.9 wt%), Ti (99.99 wt%), and Al (99.99 wt%). Rods
of 20 mm in diameter and 180 mm in length were pro-
duced by drop casting the melts into copper molds. The
alloys were heat treated at 1000
/C14 C for 500 h after which
they attained thermodynamic equilibrium, 20 that is, they
obtained a stable microstructure. After the heat treat-
ment, the samples were cooled to room temperature
(RT) in the furnace to avoid thermal cracking. Samples
for metallography, differential thermal analysis (DTA),
and mechanical and oxidation testing were cut by elec-
tro-discharge machining (EDM).

E. Evolution of microstructure and hardness
during aging at 800 oC and its effect on creep
To understand why the stress exponents vary during
the creep experiments at a given temperature, the influ-
ence of aging at 800
/C14 C for varying times on the devel-
opment of microstructure and hardness has been
examined. Figure 8 shows the evolution of the micro-
structure of Fe-23Al-8.5Ti after aging at 800
/C14 C for 1,
24, and 100 h. The micrographs clearly show that the
coherent microstructure already coarsened considerably
after annealing for 24 h at 800 /C14 C [Fig. 8(b)] and after
100 h [Fig. 8(c)] the initial microstructure has complete-
ly changed.
The effect of aging at 800 /C14 C for various times on the
evolution of the hardness can be seen in Fig. 9. The
hardness of Fe-23Al-8.5Ti decreases drastically within
the first 24 h which corresponds to the marked coarsen-
ing of the microstructure shown in Fig. 8. After longer
aging times, the further decay of the hardness is weak-
ened. The same evolution of hardness with aging time,
though less spectacular, is observed for Fe-21Al-10Ti.
The variations of the stress exponents during the creep
experiments are attributed to these significant changes
of the microstructures. Figure 8 shows that the initial
microstructures considerably coarsen and then disinte-
grate during aging at 800
/C14 C. The same happens during
the creep experiments. Therefore, the variations of the
stress exponents during the creep experiments are attrib-
uted to these significant changes of the microstructures.
The creep curves with stepwise increased load displayed
in Fig. 7 typically last about 300 h in total, hence, cover-
ing the aging times chosen here.

H. Comparison of the creep properties with
those of other Fe-Al-(Ti)-based alloys
In Table III, the creep resistance of the present alloys
at 650 and 750 /C14 C is compared to those of other Fe-Al-
(Ti)-based alloys of about the same Al content. The
creep resistance is defined as the stress that produces a
secondary creep rate of 10
/C0 7 s–1. No comparison can be
made with binary Fe-Al alloys as they do not show any
marked creep resistance at these temperatures. By con-
trast, the present alloys exhibit a notable creep resis-
tance; in fact they show higher stress values than a
directionally solidified Fe-Al-Nb alloy with coherent
A2 + C14 microstructure
49 whose creep resistance
equals that of the most advanced ferritic steels intended
for steam turbine applications. 6 However, an even higher
creep resistance has been observed for a Fe-Al-Ta alloy
with coherent A2 + L2
1 microstructure.6 This is related
to the fact that in the latter alloy the L2 1 precipitates are
much finer, that is, nano-sized, than in the present alloys.
Compared with other Fe-Al-Ti-based alloys with various
microstructures (Table III), the comparison shows that
the creep resistance generally increases with increasing Ti
content, that is, with an increasing volume fraction of L2
1.
In summary, the present alloys show a good creep resis-
tance, but compared with other Fe-Al-Ti-based alloys, no
additional strengthening effect of the coherent microstruc-
ture is observed, most likely because these microstructures
disintegrated quickly at test temperatures.

IV. SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS
Two Fe-Al-Ti alloys with coherent aFe,Al (A2) +
Fe2AlTi (L2 1) microstructures were produced and their
mechanical and oxidation behavior has been character-
ized. Compared to binary Fe-Al alloys, the present alloys
show much higher flow stresses at elevated temperatures
and remarkably high creep resistances. However, com-
parably high or even higher data have been observed for
Fe-Al-Ti alloys with higher Ti contents, that is, which
contained a higher volume fraction of L2
1. In addition,
the BDDT of both alloys is above 900 /C14 C in the as-cast
condition, which corresponds to the BDTT for other
Fe-Al-Ti alloys, but which is rather high compared
to the BDTT of binary Fe-Al alloys with about the same
Al content. The oxidation behavior of the two alloys at
800
/C14 C is excellent as it has been observed for the ma-
jority of Fe-Al-based alloys.
It had been hoped that generating coherent A2 + L2 1
microstructures may have a positive effect on increasing
the (creep) strength at high temperatures and on reducing
the BDTT. As the microstructures proved to be unstable
when the temperature was varied, no pronounced posi-
tive effect was observed. This observation complies with
that made for Fe-Al-Ni-Cr alloys where marked changes
in the compositions of the coexisting phases lead to a
deterioration of the properties, though in this case a
severe embrittlement of the alloys was observed due to
the formation of second and third generation precipitates
during cooling.
29,30 It has been found in the case of other
Fe-Al-X systems that coherent microstructures which
include L2
1 are not stable because this phase formed
only in a metastable manner, and so it has to be left open
whether stable coherent microstructures can be gener-
ated in Fe-Al-X alloys by which mechanical properties
can be improved.
