ABSTRACT 
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) with a face-centered cubic (FCC) lattice usually exhibit decent ductility but low yield 
strength at cryogenic temperatures. In order to address this issue, in this study, nanotwin structures containing 
high-density dislocations were prepared in a typical FCC Al
0.1
CoCrFeNi HEA by multiaxial cryogenic forging 
(MACF). At 77 K, the Al
0.1
CoCrFeNi HEA exhibits a unique combination of high tensile yield strength of 1263 
MPa and large ductility of 30% after five MACF cycles. The interplay between forging cycles and microstructures 
was explored, and the related strengthening mechanism related to the synergistic effect of nanotwins and dis -
locations was discussed. 
Face-centered cubic (FCC) high-entropy alloys (HEAs) have recently 
attracted considerable attention because of their outstanding fracture 
toughness and work-hardening ability at cryogenic temperatures [1 – 5] . 
However, the low yield strength of FCC HEAs limits their widespread use 
as engineering materials. 
It was reported that the existence of high-density nanotwins could 
effectively improve the yield strength of FCC alloys without sacrificing 
ductility [6 – 10] . The main strengthening mechanism was found to be 
the unique interaction between dislocations and coherent twin bound -
aries [11] . Recently, it was also reported that the multiaxial cryogenic 
forging (MACF) technology is a feasible method for generating 
high-density multiscale nanotwins inside metallic materials such as Ti 
[12] . Subsequently, Li et al. [13] innovatively prepared high-density 
nanotwins in a typical FCC Al
0.1
CoCrFeNi HEAs using the MACF tech -
nology, which significantly improved its cryogenic yield strength while 
maintaining decent ductility. However, the underlying strengthening 
mechanism responsible for the enhanced properties remains unclear. 
More critically, the effects of the nanotwin quantity produced by 
different forging cycles on the mechanical properties are also not fully 
understood. Therefore, in this study, nanotwin structures with different 
forging cycles were prepared in the Al
0.1
CoCrFeNi HEAs by the MACF 
treatment. The interplay between cryogenic mechanical properties and 
varied nanotwin structures prepared with multiple forging cycles were 
explored, and the related strengthening mechanism was discussed in 
detail. 
The Al
0.1
CoCrFeNi HEA specimens with a nominal composition of 
2.44Al – 24.39Co – 24.39Cr – 24.39Fe – 24.39Ni (in at. %) were fabricated 
by vacuum-induction melting a mixture of raw metals with 99.9 wt. % 
purity. The as-cast ingots were cut into rectangular specimens with a 
dimension of 30 mm × 30 mm × 25 mm, and then homogenized at 1473 
K for 24 h. After that, these specimens were subjected to a two-step 
thermal-mechanical processing, i.e., sequentially forged along three 
axes for five times (~16.7% cumulated height reduction imposed for 
forging on each axis) at room temperature, followed by annealing at 
1033 K for 20 min. As such, fine-grained (FG) Al
0.1
CoCrFeNi HEA 
specimens with an average grain size of 6.52 μ m were obtained. Sub -
sequently, these FG specimens were MACF-processed at the liquid ni -
trogen temperature. The details regarding the MACF process can be 
found in Ref. [14] . Each cycle of the MACF process consists of three 
passes. The MACF die was designed to impose an equivalent strain of 
0.182 during every pass on the specimen as per the equation, ε = ln 
( h
0

the final height of the processed specimen. Thus, the total strain induced 
after one MACF cycle (i.e., after three passes) is 0.546. Three different 
MACF treatments were applied, i.e., one, three and five cycles, and for 
simplicity, they were referred to as MACF-1, MACF-3 and MACF-5, 
respectively. The corresponding cumulative strains can be determined 
to be 0.546, 1.638 and 2.730, respectively.

Fig. 1 a shows the engineering stress-strain curves measured under 
tensile loading at the liquid nitrogen temperature, whilst Fig. 1 b com -
pares the yield strength and fracture elongation of all the specimens 
investigated. As shown in Fig. 1 a, the as-prepared FG specimen has good 
ductility of 71.6%, but low yield strength of 501 MPa. After one cycle of 
the MACF treatment, the yield strength is more than doubled to 1175 
MPa, but the ductility is still as high as 37.4%. For the MACF-3 spec -
imen, the yield strength further increases to 1257 MPa and the ductility 
is slightly decreased to 32.1%. As the forging cycles are further increased 
to five (i.e., the MACF-5 specimen), nevertheless, both the yield strength 
and ductility are almost unchanged (see Fig. 1 b). 
Fig. 2 shows the EBSD band contrast (BC) maps, inverse pole figure 
(IPF) maps and kernel average misorientation (KAM) maps of the MACF 
specimens, in comparison with those of the base FG specimen. Micro -
structures in all the FG and MACF specimens were further characterized 
by TEM, and the results are shown in Fig. 3 . As can be seen from Fig. 2 a1, 
a large number of annealing twins appeared in the FG specimen. For 
those MACF specimens, many fine deformation bands are present based 
on their BC maps, and some deformation bands are intersectional with 
each other ( Fig. 2 b1 – d1). According to selected area electron diffraction 
(SAED) analysis (bottom-right insets in Fig. 3 b), these bands are actually 
deformation twins. It is to be noticed that, due to the resolution limi -
tation, the EBSD method cannot detect very fine deformation twins. In 
addition, according to the analysis of the EBSD-IPF maps shown in 
Fig. 3 a2 – d2, it can be found that after the MACF processing, the 
deformed grains basically maintain their original morphology. Mean -
while, the proportion of low angle grain boundaries within the grains 
significantly increased as the forging strain increases. This observation is 
similar to that of the MACF pure titanium [16] . The underlying reason is 
due probably to the fact that the MACF specimens retain their initial 
dimension after deformation in three directions (i.e., the forging pro -
cess). Local misorientations in Fig. 2 a3 – d3 are closely associated with 
the geometrically necessary dislocations (GNDs), which are formed to 
accommodate the lattice distortion caused by the mismatch of active slip 
systems among neighboring regions of individual grains [17] . With the 
increase of forging cycles, the density of GNDs in the MACF specimens 
increases significantly and reaches the maximum in the specimen 
treated after three forging cycles. Note that a high density of GNDs was 
also observed near the nanotwin boundaries. In the FG specimen 
( Fig. 3 a), the recrystallized grains are largely dislocation-free. In 
contrast, each grain of the MACF specimen contains high-density 
nanotwins, as shown in Fig. 3 b – d. With the increase of forging cycles, 
the dislocation density increases and also reaches the maximum after 
three MACF cycles ( Fig. 3 c), which is consistent with the KAM results 
shown in Fig. 2 b3 – d3. Moreover, the high magnification bright field 
(BF) and dark field (DF)-STEM (scanning TEM) images of the MACF-1 
specimen are shown in Fig. 3 e and f, respectively. As can be seen, a 
large amount of dislocations have accumulated inside the deformation 
twins (as indicated by red arrows in Fig. 3 f). The high-density nanotwins 
reduced the distance of dislocation movement, thus significantly 
increasing the dislocation density in the MACF specimens. 
The MACF treatments produced a substantial increase in yield 
strength for the Al
0.1
CoCrFeNi HEA. According to the microstructure 
analysis shown in Fig. 3 , the yield strength improvement of the MACF 
specimens may mainly stem from dislocation and nanotwin strength -
ening. From the microstructural analysis (see Fig. 3 ), it can be seen that 
the MACF treatment has created a high density of nanotwins in just one 
cycle, and when the MACF cycles increase, dislocations accumulate in -
side the deformation nanotwins ( Fig. 3 f). Nevertheless, the ability of 
deformation nanotwins to pile up dislocations is limited, leading to an 
inappreciable increase of the dislocation density in the specimen treated 
after five forging cycles. The dislocation density of the MACF-1, MACF-3

with a high number of MACF cycles (e.g. MACF-3 and MACF-5). Similar 
phenomena were reported in Cu processed by cryogenic dynamic plastic 
deformation, in which the dislocation strengthening dominates and the 
strengthening effect from nanotwins is usually small ( < 100 MPa) [19] . 
In addition, Huang et al. [20 – 23] revealed that dislocation was far more 
important than twins in strengthening of twinning-induced plasticity 
steel. Therefore, effects of nanotwins on mechanical properties are 
twofold; first, existence of nanotwins enhanced the dislocation density 
of the MACF specimens, although its direct contribution to the yield 
strength is limited. As shown in Fig. 3 , a large amount of dislocations 
accumulated in the nanotwins after just one forging cycle. As the forging 
cycle increases to three, nevertheless, the dislocations inside the nano -
twins gradually saturated (see Fig. 3 ). Second, during cryogenic tensile 
deformation, the presence of high-density nanotwins reduced the dis -
tance of dislocation movement, alleviating the stress concentration, 
avoiding local plastic instability and ultimately achieving large 
ductility. Subsequently, it is interesting to note that the strengthening 
from nanotwins actually decreases with the MACF cycles, and stabilizes 
when the forging cycles reach three or above. This trend might be 
related to the detwinng process in the over-processed specimens, due to 
the interaction between gliding dislocations and twins in FCC metallic 
materials processed by severe plastic deformation [24] . As such, with a 
further increase of forging cycles, the concurrent occurrence of the 
twinning and detwinning leads to the unchanged nanotwin 
strengthening. 
Another interesting feature is that, even with a high density of de -
fects (i.e., dislocations and nanotwins), these MACF specimens, espe -
cially MACF-1, still showed decent ductility at cryogenic temperature 
( Fig. 1 a). Fig. 4 shows the microstructure of MACF-1 specimen after 
cryogenic tensile deformation. Compared with the undeformed micro -
structure (see Fig. 2 b1), it seems that the nanotwin density is almost the 
same after tensile loading ( Fig. 4 a). However, the stress distribution of 
MACF-1 specimen ( Fig. 4 b) became more uniform than that of the un -
deformed specimen ( Fig. 2 b3). Clearly, the interaction between uni -
formly distributed nanotwins and dislocations effectively suppressed 
stress concentration, which is beneficial to improve the ductility. Also, it 
was reported that nanotwins could increase the propagation path of 
cracks during the fracture process [ 3 , 16 ]. Therefore, high-density 
nanotwins created during the MACF process can delay the propagation 
of cracks, which is beneficial to improve the ductility as well. Never -
theless, with the increase of forging cycles, the nanotwin density did not 
change much ( Fig. 3 ), but the dislocation density increased, resulting in 
a ductility loss in MACF-3 and MACF-5 specimens.

0.1
CoCrFeNi HEA was processed with different 
cycles of the MACF technique, and a high density of dislocations and 
nanotwins was successfully introduced. As a result, the nanotwinned 
Al
0.1
CoCrFeNi HEA with one MACF cycles exhibited a unique combi -
nation of high yield strength and decent ductility at the liquid nitrogen 
temperatures. Our analysis indicates that the high yield strength is 
attributed to the synergistic effect of dislocations and nanotwins, whilst
