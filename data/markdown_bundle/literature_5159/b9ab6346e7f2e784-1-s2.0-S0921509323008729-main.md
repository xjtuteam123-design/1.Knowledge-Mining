1. Introduction 
Niobium alloys have been widely applied in aerospace and nuclear 
reactors such as the rocket nozzles and jet aircraft heat change tubes as 
an important high-temperature materials because of their high melting 
point, high specific strength, good plasticity, and excellent processing 
and welding performance [ 1 – 3 ]. The working temperature range is be -
tween 1200 
◦
C and 1400 
◦
C, however, at temperatures above 1000 
◦
C 
the pure niobium alloy shows a quick decrease in strength [ 4 ]. To 
improve the strength, refractory elements such as W, Mo, Ta, and Zr 
have been added to solute and enhance the niobium alloys by solid so -
lution strengthening [ 4 – 9 ]. For example, FS-85 (Nb – 11W-27.5Ta) and 
WC3009 (Nb-30Hf-9W) exhibited improved high-temperature yield 
strengths of about 0.2 GPa and 0.3 GPa at 1200 
◦
C, respectively [ 9 ]. 
Furthermore, second phases such as silicide and carbide have been 
introduced to enhance alloys by second-phase strengthening [ 10 – 17 ], 
however, the niobium alloys enhanced by carbide generally also show 
limited strength due to small C addition ( e.g. Nb – 1Zr-0.1C) [ 10 , 11 ]. 
Particularly, Nb – 10Mo – 10Ti – 18Si and Nb – 10Mo – 15W – 10Ti – 18Si al -
loys with a large number of eutectic silicide showed high yield strengths 
of 0.55 GPa and 0.65 GPa at 1500 
◦
C, respectively [ 13 ]. However, these 
reinforced niobium alloys exhibited high strength but low 
room-temperature plasticity [ 14 ]. For example, the Nb – Si alloys 
exhibited low compressive fracture strain of less than 1% [ 13 , 15 ], 
resulting in difficulties in preparation and assembly. 
Recently, multiprincipal alloy and carbide eutectic alloys have been 
shown to exhibit good strength and plasticity balance while they have 
good plasticity of the metallic phase and high strength of the hard car -
bide phase [ 18 , 19 ]. For example, Re
0.5

exhibited high compressive yield strength of 1.2 GPa and good plasticity 
of 10% at room temperature [ 19 ]. The formed carbide phase suppressed 
the grain growth of the primary BCC metallic phase and improve the 
plasticity by grain refinement strengthening. The BCC and monocarbide 
phases exhibited a semi-coherent interface with strong bonding, in 
which the cracks propagated from the interior of the phases and the 
deformed BCC phase relieved high local stresses to delay brittle fracture 
[ 20 , 21 ]. Inspired by the multiprincipal alloy and carbide eutectic alloys, 
we have previously designed and successfully prepared Nb

4.2. Strengthening mechanism 
By introducing alloying elements Mo and W along with hard carbide 
phases, the Nb
2
Mo
0.5
W
0.5
C
x 
alloys show improved strength and plas -
ticity. Among these alloys, C
0.25 
alloy with proper C addition exhibits the 
highest room-temperature plasticity and high strength. In general, 
increasing alloying elements and carbide phase leads to the strength 
improved by solid solution strengthening and second phase strength -
ening, however, at the cost of reducing plasticity [ 35 , 36 ]. The simul -
taneously enhanced strength and plasticity in the C
0.25 
is attributable to 
the fine grain strengthening due to the reduced grain size of the BCC 
phase in the C
0.25 
in compared with the C
0.05 
and C
0.15 
alloys, which is 
resulting from a strong resistance to grain growth by hard carbide phases 
( Fig. 1 ). Proper alloying elements in the BCC phase is crucial, and the 
BCC phase with less solid solution elements (W + Mo, 32.5 at.%) in C
0.25 
shows better deformation ability than that (W + Mo, 41.6 at.%) in C
0.5 
( Table 1 and Fig. 6 ), even if the BCC phase in C
0.5 
becomes smaller 
( Fig. 1 ). 
In addition to the good plasticity of BCC phase, the BCC/carbide 
interfaces also plays an essential role in the room-temperature 
strengthening from strong bonding and relieving interfacial energy. 
Generally, metal-ceramic composite materials ( e.g. Ni and Al
2
O
3
) are 
prone to failure from a weak phase interface due to its high energy and 
stress [ 37 ]. In the Nb
2
Mo
0.5
W
0.5
C
x 
alloys, the BCC/carbide 
semi-coherent interfaces are strong bonding with low interficial energy, 
which retain well after room-temperature compression and cracks 
propagate from the interior of phases ( Fig. 7 ). Particularly, the 
BCC/Nb
2
C interface in C
0.25 
exhibits smaller lattice mismatch and thus 
lower interfacial energy than that in C
0.5
, providing a strong strength -
ening effect combined with softer BCC phase. During deformation, 
cracks arriving at the interface reduce the elastic strain energy by lattice 
mismatch and slow down the crack extension. The soft BCC phase blunts 
crack tips and slow down crack propagation by relieving high local 
stresses ( Fig. 7 ). This good combination together slows down the crack 
propagation and results in excellent strength and plasticity of the C
0.25 
alloy.
