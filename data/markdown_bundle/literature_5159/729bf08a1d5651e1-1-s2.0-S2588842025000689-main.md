ABSTRACT
The microstructure evolution and stability of the directionally solidified superalloys with various contents of γ ′ 
phase forming elements were investigated. The increase of mass fraction of γ ′ phase forming elements from Al: 5 
%, Ti: 1 % to Al: 6 %, Ti:2 % dramatically increased the area fraction of eutectic and the inhomogeneity. 
However, the segregation ratio decreased with the increase of γ ′ phase forming elements, owing to the discharge 
of W and Mo. After heat treatments, the addition of γ ′ phase forming elements led to the formation of a four-layer 
layered structure, and the inhomogeneity could not be eliminated. The increased contents of γ ′ phase forming 
elements were detrimental to the structural stability, promoted the coarsening of the γ ′ phase, and increased the 
mismatch stress between the two phases in the dendrite.
1. Introduction
The development of nickel-based superalloys has gone through 
several stages: wrought superalloys, cast superalloys, directionally so -
lidified superalloys (DS superalloy) and single crystal superalloys (SC 
superalloy). Compared to single crystal superalloys, directionally so -
lidified superalloys are widely used in ground gas turbines due to their 
lower cost and higher yield rate. The Ni-based superalloy is mainly 
composed of γ phase and γ ′ phase within the crystal. Therefore, the 
mechanical strength of Ni-based superalloys is mainly enhanced through 
solid solution strengthening of γ phase, precipitation strengthening of γ ′ 
phase and grain boundary strengthening [ 1 ]. Researchers have previ -
ously focused on optimizing alloy design by targeting γ -phase 
strengthening elements, incorporating Re into the alloy, which can 
enhance the alloy ’ s temperature capacity by 30

Therefore, this study attempts to regulate the alloy microstructure 
from the perspective of precipitation strengthening by adjusting Al and 
Ti as γ ′ phase forming elements, with the aim of further optimizing the 
alloy ’ s performance by optimizing the synergistic interactions among 
constituent elements, thereby unlocking the full potential of the material 
system. The Al and Ti elements, which have precipitation strengthening 
effects in DS superalloy, play an important role in the microstructure 
and performance. Antiphase boundary (APB) appears in mechanical 
experiments of various alloys[ 9 – 12 ], and the increase in antiphase 
domain boundary energy is conducive to improving the strength of the

high-performance directionally solidified superalloys is proposed.
2. Experimental
Based on the general screening system of nickel-based directional 
solidification alloys, this study focused on the mass percentage and 
atomic percentage of Al and Ti elements, increasing the content of re -
fractory elements to promote the solid solution strengthening effect, 
controlling the Cr content to take into account both oxidation resistance 
and organizational stability, and finally using the thermodynamic phase

3. Results and discussions
3.1. As cast microstructure
The dendrite structures of as-cast DS1 and DS2 were shown in Fig. 1 , 
and the grain boundaries between columnar crystals were indicated as 
red lines. The dendrites in the same columnar crystal were oriented in 
the same direction. The distance between different dendrite cores in the 
cross-section was defined as the primary dendrite arm spacing. The 
secondary dendrites growing from the primary dendrite core were 
observed in the longitudinal section in Fig. 1 (c) and (d).
The primary dendrite arm spacing, secondary dendrite arm spacing 
and eutectic structure content of DS1 and DS2 were showed in Fig. 2 . 
The secondary dendrite arm spacing of the two alloys was almost the 
same, but there was a small difference in the primary dendrite arm 
spacing. Considering that the influence of Mo and W on the primary 
dendrite arm spacing was relatively small [ 35 ], it was inferred that the 
increase of Al and Ti promoted the increase of primary dendrite spacing. 
The most striking observation was that the significant difference in 
eutectic content between the two cast alloys. Lots of white eutectic were 
presented in the interdendrite as shown in Fig. 1 , in which the eutectic 
content in DS2 was almost non-existent, while the content of DS1 was 
nearly 40 %. Furthermore, as indicated by the yellow arrow in Fig. 1 (c), 
plenty of eutectic phases almost filled the among the interdendrite re -
gion, resulting in the absence of secondary dendrite precipitation. The 
phenomenon of high eutectic content in DS1 was consistent with the 
casting nickel-based superalloys in Refs. [ 29 , 36 ].
The SEM images of the dendrite structures of the two alloys were 
shown in Fig. 3 . The eutectic structure of DS1 alloy contained rare γ 
phases and mainly existed in the form of huge blocky γ ′ phase. However, 
the size of the γ ′ phases around the eutectic was not uniform. Some γ ′ 
phases around the eutectic were relatively large, while the γ ′ phases in 
other areas were smaller, surrounded by the larger γ ′ phases at the same 
time, as shown in Fig. 3 (b). As shown in Fig. 3 (c), the eutectic structure 
in DS2 was similar to the common eutectic structure in nickel-based 
superalloys [ 37 , 38 ]. The eutectic morphology contained mosaic-like 
and fan-like γ / γ ′ two-phase structures, as shown in Fig. 3 (d). The γ ′ 
phase on the outside of the eutectic structure solidified first and was 
larger in size, while the γ ′ phase in the center of eutectic solidified last 
and was smaller in size.
Several blocky particles adjacent to the large eutectic were detected 
in DS1, as marked by the white box in Fig. 4 . EDS mapping in Fig. 4
suggested that these were W and Mo-rich phases, in which the mass 
fraction exceeded 80 %. Since W and Mo were not prone to form the γ / γ ′ 
eutectic, they were rich in the remaining liquid after the precipitation of 
γ / γ ′ eutectic [ 39 ]. The EDS results, combined with HRTEM image and 
FFT image, showed that the W and Mo-rich phases were the same as the 
α (Mo, W) phases with body center cubic (BCC) structure in Refs. [ 28 ,
29 ], and the alpha phase was located at the edge of the eutectic structure 
and showed a [001]

3.2. Microstructure after heat treatments
The microstructures of the dendrite core and interdendritic space of 
the two alloys after heat treatment were displayed in Fig. 6 . The solution 
heat treatment of DS1 was conducted below the incipient melting tem -
perature as show in Fig. 6 (c), followed by a two-step aging treatment, 
yet the eutectic structure persisted corresponding to the sharp peak on 
the left in Fig. 6 (c), although this structure did not exist in the phase 
diagram in Fig. 6 (b). Optical microscopic photos (not included in the 
article) were used to quantify the eutectic structure content, revealing a 
decrease in the area fraction of the eutectic structure of DS1 after so -
lution treatment, from 38.4 % in the cast state to 26.5 %. However, the 
residual eutectic structure persisted and couldn ’ t be entirely eliminated 
by solution treatment. After heat treatment, as depicted in Fig. 6 (e), the 
size of γ ′ phase in the dendrite core grew relatively large (about 1000 
nm), with irregular two-phase structures forming, displaying low 
squareness and some degree of coarsening and connection. Additionally, 
a small amount of γ phase was found enclosed within the γ ′ phase, as 
indicated by the yellow arrow in Fig. 6 (e).
In the as-cast DS1, irregular γ ′ phases were still retained around part 
of the eutectic, as highlighted by the red arrow in Fig. 6 (d). However, a 
layered structure of eutectic → regular γ ′ phases → W and Mo-rich 
phases → irregular γ ′ phases formed around the eutectic of the other 
parts as shown by the yellow arrows in Fig. 6 (d) and (f). As illustrated in 
Fig. 6 (f), this layered structure consisted of layers divided by the yellow 
dashed line: the first layer being the shrunken eutectic due to reduced 
area fraction, the second layer being regular γ ′ phases with improved 
squareness compared to the dendrite core. The third layer, highlighted 
by the yellow arrow, indicated the presence of W and Mo-rich phases in 
the depleted γ ′ phase zone, whose compositions closely matched those 
showed in Fig. 4 . The fourth layer of irregular γ ′ phases was indicated by 
the yellow arrows in Fig. 6 (f). In summary, the schematic diagram of the 
layered structure of DS1 were shown in Fig. 7 (a), and each layer was 
surrounded by the next layer.
In the as-cast DS1 alloy, partially retained irregular γ ′ phases were 
observed adjacent to specific eutectic regions (red arrow, Fig. 6 (d)). 
Notably, a distinctive layered microstructure developed around other 
eutectic areas, comprising four characteristic zones delineated by yellow 
arrows and dashed lines in Fig. 6 (d)(f) 
1. A contracted eutectic core with reduced area fraction;
2. A secondary layer of regular γ ′ phases exhibiting enhanced 
morphological squareness compared to dendritic cores;
3. A tertiary W/Mo-rich phase layer within the γ ′ -depleted zone 
(compositionally consistent with Fig. 4 data);
4. An outer layer of irregular γ ′ phases.
As schematically illustrated in Fig. 7 (a), this concentric layered ar -
chitecture demonstrated sequential encapsulation where each structural 
zone circumscribes its predecessor.
In addition, as shown in Fig. 7 (a, b), a small amount of the W and Mo- 
rich phases still remained in the eutectic structure of the first layer. The 
W and Mo-rich in the first layer and the third layer in the layered 
structure were consistent with those observed in the as-cast DS1, being α 
(Mo, W) phases. The EDS results of two types of α phases were showed in 
Fig. 7 (c and d), in which area 1 represented the rod-shaped α phase in 
the third layer generated after heat treatment, while area 2 denoted the 
residual blocky α phase observed in the first layer.
The stark contrast between DS1 and DS2 becomes evident in their 
response to heat treatment. While DS2, despite having a lower solution 
heat treatment temperature than DS1, completely eradicates the eutectic 
structure owing to the only one sharp γ phase melting peak caused by the 
decrease of γ ′ phase forming elements content in the DSC curve in Fig. 6
(c), forming more regular γ ′ phases in both the dendrite core and 
interdendritic regions. The γ ′ phase in the dendrite core of DS2 exhibits 
higher squareness and an average size of about 320 nm, while in the 
interdendritic region, the squareness was lower, with an average size of 
about 450 nm. This phenomenon underscores the impact of excessive Al 
and Ti content. In DS1, these elements contribute to the appearance of a 
stubborn eutectic structure that persists even after heat treatment. This 
eutectic, resistant to elimination, fosters the precipitation of more im -
purities such as the α (Mo, W) phase. On the other hand, DS2 alloy, with 
a lower content of these elements, demonstrates the ability to eliminate 
the eutectic entirely through heat treatment, resulting in a more favor -
able microstructure with regular γ ′ phases.
In order to explain the layered structure and compare it with DS2 
alloy, line scan of Al, Ti, Mo and W was conducted on the as-cast state 
and heat-treated states of DS1 and DS2 alloys, as illustrated in Fig. 8 . The 
black dashed lines in Fig. 8 (a), (b) and (c) represented the interface 
between the eutectic and the external γ / γ ′ two-phase structure. As 
depicted in Fig. 8 (a), the scan line was relatively stable due to the low 
content of γ phase in the eutectic, with minimal changes observed in the 
content of the four elements. Two forms of γ ′ phase zones were observed 
outside the eutectic. One was the smaller γ ′ phases zone, indicated by the 
green dashed line in Fig. 8 (a), where the Mo and W was higher and the 
content of Al and Ti was lower than in the eutectic. The other was the 
larger γ ′ phases zone shown by the red dashed line in Fig. 8 (a), with 
lower Mo and W content and higher Al and Ti content. A small amount of 
α phases was present around the smaller γ ′ phase zone, as depicted in 
Fig. 3 , indicating that the formation of the small γ ′ phase zone resulted 
from the eutectic ’ s low solubility for Mo and W elements during solid -
ification, leading to the expulsion of Mo, W, and other elements from the 
eutectic structure, thus forming a W and Mo-rich γ / γ ′ two-phase struc -
ture. Due to the excessive Mo and W content in some areas, the α (Mo, 
W) phases shown in Fig. 4 were formed. Another γ ′ phase zone with 
larger size γ ′ phase formed in the early stage of solidification from the 
interdendritic melt, where the content of γ ′ phase-forming elements was 
higher.
Line scanning was performed on the layered structure formed after

zone. In the third layer of the layered structure, which consisted of short 
rod-shaped α phases, the distribution of the four elements was non- 
uniform due to the presence of a depleted γ ′ zone around the α phase. 
Al and Ti elements were mainly concentrated in the depleted γ ′ phase

3.3. The formation of the layered structure and α phase
The formation mechanism of the two types of γ ′ phases around the 
eutectic structure of the as-cast DS1 alloy and the four-layer layered 
structure after heat treatment was illustrated in Fig. 10 . In Fig. 10 (a), the 
cream section represents the interdendritic melt. Towards the end of 
solidification, the eutectic formed exhibited relatively low solubility for 
the γ phase-forming elements Mo and W [ 41 , 42 ]. Given the higher 
concentrations of Al and Ti in DS1 compared to DS2, the eutectic melting 
zone was more extensive, as evidenced by the larger area fraction of 
eutectic observed in the as-cast DS1. Consequently, a greater amount of 
Mo and W elements were expelled into the vicinity of the eutectic 
structure compared to DS2, resulting in the formation of small-sized γ ′ 
phases, as depicted in Figs. 3(b) and 10(b)(c) . However, due to the 
non-uniform discharge of Mo and W elements from the eutectic, the 
distribution of the small-sized γ ′ phase zone, surrounded by the 
large-sized γ ′ phase in the cast DS1 alloy, was irregular around the 
eutectic structure, with the large-sized γ ′ phase zone distributed in other 
areas surrounding the eutectic structure.
In the as-cast DS1 alloy, as depicted in Fig. 8 (b), high concentrations 
of Mo and W were distributed within the small-sized γ ′ phase zone, 
whereas the large-sized γ ′ phase zone outside the small-sized γ ′ phase 
zone exhibited elevated levels of Al and Ti. Consequently, during the 
heat treatment process, the Al and Ti elements diffused from the exterior 
towards the small-sized γ ′ phase zone, as illustrated by the purple arrow 
in Fig. 10 (e), while the Mo and W elements within the small-sized γ ′ 
phase region diffused outward, indicated by the red arrow in Fig. 10 (e). 
Due to the comparatively slow diffusion rate of Mo and W elements, 
their concentrations at the interface of the two regions increased at a 
slower rate, whereas Al and Ti elements diffused more rapidly [ 43 , 44 ], 
facilitating an increase in Al and Ti concentrations at the interface of the 
γ ′ phase. This ultimately led to the precipitation of the α (Mo, W) phase 
rich in W and Mo elements, as indicated by the deep blue arrow in 
Fig. 10 (e). Additionally, the small-sized γ ′ phase zone transformed into a 
regular γ ′ phase zone, while the large-sized γ ′ phase zone transformed 
into an irregular γ ′ phase zone.

4. Conclusions
The investigation into the organizational evolution, encompassing 
the formation of α phases and microstructural stability, was conducted 
on two directionally solidified superalloys with varying levels of γ ′ phase 
forming elements (mass fractions from 5 %Al + 1 %Ti to 6 %Al + 2 %Ti) 
and refractory elements. From this study, the following conclusions can 
be drawn. 
(1) An increase in the content of γ ′ phase forming elements signifi -
cantly augmented the presence of eutectic structures in the alloy, 
causing the expulsion of refractory elements to form impurities 
around the eutectic, albeit reducing the segregation ratio of the 
as-cast alloy.
(2) In the heat-treated DS1 alloy, a four-layer layered structure and α 
phases rich in W and Mo were identified. Despite the low total 
content of W and Mo, this phenomenon stemmed from the for -
mation of a second layer abundant in Mo and W, and a fourth 
layer rich in Al and Ti. The α phase precipitated between these 
layers due to diffusion.
(3) Elevated levels of γ ′ phase forming elements compromised the 
stability of the structure, resulting in pronounced coarsening and 
rafting of the precipitated phases post thermal exposure, while 
also promoting the precipitation of TCP phase.
(4) In subsequent directional solidification alloy designs, careful 
consideration should be given to ensuring that the sum of atomic 
percentages of the γ ′ phase-forming elements does not exceed 
15.7 %. Exceeding this threshold led to an increase in the contents 
of Al and Ti in the γ ′ phase, consequently reducing the solubility 
of refractory elements and prompting the precipitation of impu -
rity phases such as the α phase and the TCP phase, thereby 
compromising the uniformity and stability of the alloy.
