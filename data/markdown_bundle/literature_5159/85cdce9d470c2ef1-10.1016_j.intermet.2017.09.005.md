ABSTRACT
The alloying eﬀ ects of Ta and W on the phase composition, microstructure and room-temperature fracture
toughness of Nb-Si based alloys were investigated. The nominal compositions of Nb-Si based alloys were Nb-
15Si-24Ti-4Cr-2Al-2Hf (base alloy, at.%), Nb-15Si-24Ti-4Cr-2Al-2Hf-1Ta (1Ta alloy, at.%) and Nb-15Si-24Ti-
4Cr-2Al-2Hf-1W (1W alloy, at.%). Results showed that the base alloy, 1Ta alloy and 1W alloy were composed of
Nb solid solution (Nbss), αNb
5Si3 and small volumes of hexagonal γNb5Si3 phases. The elements of Ta and W
were primarily partitioned in the Nbss phases. The addition of Ta improved the fracture toughness of the base
alloy from 10.2 to 12.2 MPa m 1/2, but the addition of W reduced the fracture toughness to 8.2 MPa m 1/2. The
fracture of Nbss phases in the three alloys exhibited a cleavage fracture mode, and river patterns were created on
individual facets, while the Nb
5Si3 phases showed a brittle fracture mode with ﬂat and featureless fracture
surfaces. Diﬀerent with base alloy and 1W alloy, 1Ta alloy involved the occurrence of secondary cracking as well
as interface decohesion. The toughening mechanisms of Nb-Si based alloys with Ta and W additions were em-
phasized.
1. Introduction
As the alternative materials of current Ni-based superalloys for
turbine blade applications, Nb-Si based alloys have captured consider-
able attention, owing to their relatively low densities and outstanding
high temperature capabilities [1–4]. Nb-Si based alloys are featured by
hard intermetallic Nb5Si3 silicides embedded in Nb solid solution (Nbss)
matrix or vice versa, depending on their compositions [5–7].I ti s
known that for turbine blade applications, minimum toughness is re-
quired to survive the ﬁnal assembly into engines and resist damage
during service. Though striking achievements have been scored re-
cently, the insu ﬃcient fracture toughness of Nb-Si based alloys still
limits their industrial application.
Alloying has been considered as an e ﬀective method to improve the
performance of Nb-Si based alloys. The Nb-Si-Ti-Cr-Al-Hf multi-com-
ponent system has been recently developed to improve the overall
performance of Nb-Si based alloys [8–11]. Ti is added to the Nb-Si
based system to improve the intrinsic ductility of the Nbss phase as well
as the toughness of Nb-Si based alloys [2,12]. Certain amounts of Cr
and Al are alloyed to enhance their oxidation resistance [2,7].H fi s
added as it has a strong solid solution strengthening e ﬀect on the Nbss
phase [13]. But a further increase in the fracture toughness of Nb-Si
based alloys tends to be compromised by the degradation in the high
temperature strength or the oxidation resistance [2,14].
The fracture behavior of Nb-Si based alloys at room temperature
typically combines the initialization of cracks and the crack-trapping,
bowing and bridging processes [15–17]. As in situ composites, the
fracture behavior of Nb-Si based alloys may also be dominated by the
shear strength and the normal strength of phase interfaces [18]. It has
been found that the elements of Ti [12],H f [19],B [20] and Ga [21]
typically beneﬁt the fracture toughness of Nb-Si based alloys, while the
elements of Cr [22],A l [2,23],M o [24],R e [23] and Fe [11] degrade
the fracture toughness. Zhang et al. [20] reported a 2 at.% B addition
increased the fracture toughness of Nb-22Ti-16Si-5Cr-4Hf-3Al alloy, by
reﬁning the eutectic structures and increasing the Ti concentration in
Nbss, but higher content of B addition up to 5 at.% or 10 at.% reduced
the fracture toughness by increasing the volume fraction of brittle si-
licides. Zhang et al. [11] found that the Fe addition from 1 at.% to 6 at.
% resulted in the phase transition of Nb-Si based alloys from
Nbss + (α+γ)Nb
5Si3 to Nbss + Nb 4FeSi and the increase in the volume
fraction of silicides, leading to the decrease in fracture toughness.
To the best knowledge of the authors, the e ﬀect of Ta and W on the
fracture toughness of Nb-Si based alloy has not been investigated sys-
tematically, which is the aim of the current work. Combined with our

melted ﬁve times to ensure chemical homogeneity. Master alloy rods

3. Results
Fig. 2 shows microstructures of the base alloy, 1Ta alloy and 1W
alloy via DS. Combining with XRD results in our previous work [25],
the three Nb-Si based alloys are mainly composed of Nbss (light grey in
contrast) and αNb
5Si3 (dark grey in contrast). The base alloy, 1Ta alloy
and 1W alloy via DS all exhibit a hypoeutectic microstructure, as the
primary large Nbss phases are observed. It can be deduced that during
the solidi ﬁcation of the three Nb-Si based alloys, the primary Nbss
phases ﬁrstly crystallize from the melt, expressed as L→Nbss + L1.
Then the residual liquid melt reached the ﬁrst eutectic composition, and
the eutectic reaction L1→Nbss+αNb5Si3 is triggered. No signi ﬁcant
diﬀerence on the microstructure is shown for the three Nb-Si based
alloys via DS, suggesting the 1 at.% addition of Ta or W does not sig-
niﬁcantly alter the microstructure and phase constitution of Nb-Si
based alloys. In addition, small volumes of Ti-rich Nb
5Si3 phases in
black contrast are distinguishable in the three alloys. They are fre-
quently formed at the phase boundaries between Nbss and αNb5Si3.
Based on the XRD patterns shown in Fig. 3, the main phases of the
base alloy, 1Ta alloy and 1W alloy after HT at 1450 °C for 12 h remain
to be Nbss and αNb5Si3. Typical microstructures of the three alloys are
displayed in Fig. 4. It is observed that in the three alloys, the inter-
connected Nbss phases are the continuous matrix of Nb-Si based alloys
embedded with discontinuous silicides. On their longitudinal micro-
structures, the ﬁbrous silicides are presented paralleling to the DS di-
rection, and the silicides tend to be spheroidized to reduce the inter-
phase boundary area and the total interfacial energy [27]. Considering
no obvious chemical segregation is observed in the three alloys after
HT, the chemical compositions of Nbss, αNb
5Si3 and Ti-rich Nb 5Si3
have been measured by EPMA-WDS, as listed in Table 2 . It is observed
the addition of Ta or W alters the compositions of the Nbss phases,
especially the content of Si in Nbss. The addition of 1 at.% Ta results in
an 8% increase in the content of Si in Nbss, while the addition of 1 at.%
W leads to an 11% decrease, which is in accordance with other in-
vestigations [17,28]. As determined from the Nb-Si and Ta-Si binary
phase diagrams [29], the solid solubility of Si in Ta is higher than that
in Nb at the same temperature, i.e., the addition of Ta enhances the
solid solubility of Si in the Nbss phases. Though the solid solubility of Si
in W is also larger than that in Nb according to the W-Si binary phase
diagram [29], the addition of W typically reduces the solid solubility of
Si in the Nbss phases due to the slow di ﬀusion rate of W in Nb at high
temperature [17]. The content of Ti in Ti-rich Nb
5Si3 is approximately
twice as high as that in αNb5Si3. The element of Ta is largely parti-
tioned in Nbss phases (1.20 at.%), and the contents of Ta in αNb5Si3
and Ti-rich Nb 5Si3 are merely 0.72 at.% and 0.15 at.%, respectively.

mode along the loading direction. Secondary cracks are observed
nearby the main crack path in the base alloy, 1Ta alloy and 1W alloy. It
is worth noting that a branched main path during crack propagation is
distinguished in the 1Ta alloy, i.e., secondary cracking has been
promoted. In contrast, only one main crack propagation path is ob-
served in either the base alloy or the 1W alloy.
Fig. 8 shows the fracture surfaces of the base alloy, 1Ta alloy and
1W alloy after HT. It is observed that Nbss and silicides are fractured in
diﬀerent manners. The fracture of Nbss phases shows a cleavage mode,
as river patterns are presented on individual facets. The fracture sur-
faces of silicides are ﬂat and featureless, indicating a brittle fracture
mode. Secondary cracks are observed in the 1Ta alloy, but not visible in
the base alloy and the 1W alloy. More evidently, interface decohesion
between Nbss and silicides occurs during the fracture of the 1Ta alloy,
while insigni ﬁcant interfacial cracking is involved in the fracture of
either the base alloy or the 1W alloy. Fracture steps are also observed
on fracture surfaces of the three alloys, probably resulting from the
fracture of silicides as well as interface decohesion. This explains why
larger fracture steps are shown on the fracture surfaces of 1Ta alloy,
while shallower fracture steps are created on the fracture surfaces of
base alloy and 1W alloy. Fig. 9 shows the three-dimensional topo-
graphies of fracture surfaces of the base alloy, 1Ta alloy and 1W alloy.
Taking the base alloy as a reference, the fracture surface of the 1 Ta
alloy is created with more unevenness and larger fracture steps, and the
1W alloy shows a more ﬂat fracture surface.
4. Discussion
The concept of ductile phase toughening has been evidenced in Nb-
Si based alloys in many investigations [9,16,32]. The fracture tough-
ness of Nb-Si based alloys is signi ﬁcantly higher than that of the
monolithic Nb
5Si3 phases ( ∼3 MPa m 1/2) [12,33]. It has been shown
that the toughness of Nb-Si based alloys increases with the tensile yield
strength, Young's modulus, volume fraction and the radius of the duc-
tile Nbss phase [34]. Accordingly, a hypoeutectic composition, but
near-eutectic, is typically optimized for Nb-Si based alloys in this work
as well as other literature [15,16,27]. Fig. 10 (a) shows the schematic
crack propagation path of Nb-Si based alloys involving the crack de-
ﬂection and bridging, interface decohesion and secondary cracking
toughening mechanisms. For the base alloy, 1Ta alloy and 1W alloy
with Nbss being the matrix, the main crack propagates in a zigzag way
by crack deﬂ ection and normally is blocked within Nbss matrix, which
is governed by the above toughening mechanisms. The main crack
tends to uninterruptedly pass through the Nb
5Si3 particles ahead, and
they can not provide Nb-Si based alloys with additional bene ﬁcial eﬀect

on the fracture toughness [12,15]. Interface debonding between Nbss
and Nb5Si3 phases may also take place, when the main crack meets the
interface, which improves the fracture toughness of Nb-Si based alloys
and is known as the interface decohesion toughening mechanism.
As stated previously, the average volume fraction of γNb5Si3 in the
base alloy, 1Ta alloy and 1W alloy is 2.32%, 1.93% and 2.62%. Namely,
the volume fraction of γNb5Si3 in Nb-Si based alloys is altered by the
additions of Ta and W. The 1Ta alloy contains a lower volume fraction
of γNb
5Si3 and an improved fracture toughness. On the contrary, the
1W alloy has a higher volume fraction of γNb5Si3 and a reduced frac-
ture toughness. The fracture toughness of hexagonal γNb5Si3 is lower
than that of tetragonal αNb5Si3 due to its comparatively lower unstable
stacking fault energy and Peierls-Nabarro (P-N) barrier energy ( UP-N)
[35]. This explains the tendency that the fracture toughness decreases
with the increase of the volume fraction of γNb5Si3.
Secondary cracking is another e ﬀective toughening manner caused
by the addition of Ta ( Figs. 7 and 8). The local crack tip driving force in
1Ta alloy is typically reduced by secondary cracking, which results in
the change of crack front and the redistribution of local stress [36]. The
release of energy thus occurs, and the resistance of Nb-Si based alloys to
failure form fracture initiated by a preexisting crack is enhanced. This
can explain why the 1Ta alloy shows a higher fracture toughness.
Especially, interface decohesion has been observed in the fracture
surface of 1Ta alloy ( Fig. 8). A constrained volume is typically in-
troduced above and below the crack plain in Nb-Si based alloys by
interface debonding, resulting in the increase in the crack-opening
displacement and the constraint relaxation during fracture [37]. In-
terface decohesion between Nbss and silicides tends to take place at the
interfaces approximately paralleling to the main crack, i.e., the loading
direction [15,18]. Then the crack propagates along the interface, rather
than vertically to the interface, as the interfacial normal strength in Nb-
Si based alloys is larger than the interfacial shear strength [18]. The
crack vertical to the interface will not propagate along the interface. It
tends to be either deﬂ ected and bridged by Nbss or lead to the break of
brittle silicides. Besides, it has been well established that the fracture
toughness of particulate metal-matrix composites decreases with the
increase of the plastic constraint in the ductile phases [12,34]. The
signiﬁcant interface decohesion in 1Ta alloys may suggest the mitiga-
tion of the plastic constraint in Nbss phases, and the fracture toughness
is improved resultantly.
Sha et al. [17] reported the outstanding strengthening e ﬀect of W on
Nbss, due to the large mis ﬁt parameter of a W atom to a Nb atom and
resultant large lattice deformation, resulting in the decrease in the
elastic-modulus mis ﬁt between Nbss and silicides. Because of this, the
addition of W signi ﬁcantly improves the high-temperature properties of
Nb-Si based alloys, but embrittles the Nbss phases as well [17,38,39].I t
was reported that Cr and Al additions to Nb or Nb-Ti reduced the γ
s/UP-
N ratio by increasing UP-N, where γs is the surface energy, resulting in a
higher tendency for cleavage fracture and a lower fracture toughness
[12,35]. The addition of W probably has the same e ﬀect as Cr and Al,
i.e., increases either the U
P-N or the unstable stacking energy ( γus)o f
Nbss and reduces the fracture toughness of Nb-Si based alloys [12,40].
Further research is required to verify this opinion.
Our previous work [25] reported the oxidation resistance of Nb-Si
based alloys was improved by Ta addition, but damaged W addition.
Therefore, Ta is suggested to be alloyed to the Nb-Si based multi-
component system, and the addition of W should be avoided.

microstructure, consisting of Nbss, αNb
5Si3 and small volumes of
γNb5Si3. The elements of Ta and W were largely partitioned in the
Nbss phases. The addition of Ta reduced the volume fraction of
γNb5Si3 and raised the content of Si in Nbss. On the contrary, the
addition of W resulted in the increase of the volume fraction of
γNb
5Si3 and the decrease of the content of Si in Nbss.
