Interaction between c0 precipitate distribution and microstructure
homogeneity during hot deformation in a Ni-based superalloy

Abstract
The inhomogeneous microstructure of the Ni-based superalloys used for turbine disks was an intolerable defect for the
mechanical performance. The effects of the distribution of c
0 precipitate, forging temperature, and strain level on the
microstructure evolution of GH4730 alloy were investigated by EBSD during hot deformation. The results showed that the
heterogeneous factor peaked at the transition temperature from the single-phase to the double-phase region. The coupling
effect of heat and stress led to the heterogeneous precipitation and distribution of c
0 phase during the transition region,
which was the main reason for the formation of inhomogeneous microstructures. The coherent c0 phases of approximately
0.3 lm were diffusely distributed inside the large grains, increasing the grain strength, making recrystallization reﬁnement
difﬁcult, and thus forming large unrecrystallized grains. The incoherent c0 phases with a size of approximately 1.1 lm
located at the grain boundaries and pinned the grain boundaries, and thus the accumulated strain at the grain boundaries
caused the occurrence of discontinuous dynamic recrystallization and promoted continuous reﬁnement of the grains. The
microstructure evolution of new Ni-based superalloys during hot forging was focused, and the formation mechanism of
inhomogeneous microstructure and control measures was explained. A theoretical basis for improving the microstructure
homogeneity of the new cast and wrought superalloys was provided.

1 Introduction
Ni-based superalloys have been extensively used in high-
temperature rotary-engine components owing to their high-
temperature strength [ 1, 2], excellent mechanical properties
at elevated temperatures [ 3], and long-term organizational
stability [ 4–8]. As a driver for higher performance and
increased thermal efﬁciency of new aero engines, devel-
oping novel superalloys has become the major focus of
current research [ 9]. A series of new Ni-based superalloys
such as Rene65 [ 10], AD730 [ 11, 12], and VDM780 [ 13]
have been developed to provide better compromise among
high-temperature properties, process capability, and cost.
GH4730 alloy is the representative new Ni-based superal-
loys that are strengthened by the c
0 phase (approximately
40 vol.% c0) with excellent casting and forging process
route capabilities and an excellent combination of high-
temperature mechanical properties and cost [ 14–18].
According to the Hall–Petch relationship, a reduction in
the average grain size results in an increase in mechanical
strength [ 19]. Conventional Ni-based cast and wrought
(C&W) superalloys are transformed from cast ingot to
billet through a series of complex hot forging and heat
treatments to obtain a ﬁne and homogeneous grain struc-
ture [ 20]. The new Ni-based superalloys suffer from difﬁ-
culty in obtaining a uniform strain when processed in the
double-phase region, resulting in microstructure inhomo-
geneity [ 21]. The inhomogeneous microstructure in the
superalloys usually does not satisfy the microstructural
requirements of the product, which leads to a reduction in
the life and mechanical properties of the material [ 22].

The convoluted interactions between the hot forging
parameters (i.e., temperature, strain rate, and strain level)
[22–25] and microstructure homogeneity make achieve a
homogeneous and ﬁne-grain structure after hot forging
difﬁcult [ 26]. A signiﬁcant number of works have been
published on hot forging parameters and microstructure
evolution [ 27–30]. For example, Konkova et al. [ 21]
demonstrated the effect of strain level on the evolution of a
microstructure in AD730 alloy during hot forging, and their
study showed that large unrecrystallized regions could be
found at all applied strain levels, but the frequency
decreased as the strain level increased. Jia et al. [ 31]
reported that the strain rate signiﬁcantly affected the ﬂow
behavior and evolution of the microstructure of the
GH4169 alloy. As the strain rate increased from 0.01 to
1s
-1, both dynamic recrystallization (DRX) grain size and
DRX fraction decreased and reached a minimum value at a
strain rate of 1 s -1. At a strain rate of 1 s -1, microstructure
inhomogeneity was observed, which manifested as a mix-
ture of deformed grains, ﬁne recrystallized grains, and
large unrecrystallized grains. Then, the DRX fraction
gradually increased with a further increase in the strain rate
due to the high dislocation density and adiabatic temper-
ature rise. Liu et al. [ 32] studied the effect of deformation
temperature on the microstructure evolution and concluded
that deformation temperature signiﬁcantly affected the
DRX grain size and rheological stress. Increasing the
deformation temperature coarsened the DRX grain size and
reduced the rheological stress. Pe ´rez et al. [ 33] found that
intermediate heat treatments in the middle of a two-step
compression caused the ﬁne c
0 phase in the unrecrystallized
region to dissolve. The unrecrystallized grains were elim-
inated, and a homogeneous ﬁne-grain structure was
obtained. Although considerable work on the hot-forging
parameters and microstructure evolution has been per-
formed, further studies are needed to elucidate the rela-
tionship between the hot-forging parameters and the
evolution of the c
0 precipitates.
The microstructural homogeneity of Ni-based superal-
loys is closely related to the characteristic (size, morphol-
ogy, and distribution) of the c0 phase [ 34, 35]. Three
different sizes and morphologies of c0 precipitates are
distributed in the microstructure, namely, irregular granular
primary c0 phase, petaloid secondary c0 phase, and spher-
ical tertiary c0 phase [36]. For a cast billet, a homogeneous
and ﬁne-grain microstructure can be achieved using
numerous iterations of multidirectional hot forging [ 37].
During the thermomechanical processing history, Ni-based
C&W superalloys are usually initially forged in the single-
phase region. At this time, the alloy has a low resistance
and a homogeneous grain structure, but ﬁne grains cannot
be obtained to satisfy the microstructural requirements of
the ﬁnal products. Subsequently, hot forging is performed
in the double-phase region ( c ? c
0), and the c0 phase hin-
ders grain growth through Zener pinning. The size and
distribution of the c0 phase exert some inﬂuence on the
deformation behavior of the superalloys [ 29, 38]. However,
the size and distribution of the c0 phase are affected by the
hot forging parameters, and thus, the effect of the grain
boundary pinning is affected [ 39, 40]. Thus, the transfor-
mation from the single-phase region to the double-phase
region is characterized by two processes, namely, precipi-
tation and dissolution of the strengthening phase and
recrystallization and pinning of the strengthening phase,
which may be considered as the origin of the microstruc-
tural heterogeneity [ 33, 41]. The detailed transformation
process still needs to be further studied, and the evolution
of this process is vital for the processing of new Ni-based
superalloys.
The hot deformation behavior of the GH4730 alloy with
different hot forging parameters was investigated, and
focus was made on the inﬂuence of the c
0 phase on the
DRX behavior and microstructure evolution. After repe-
ated forging in the single-phase region, specimens were
selected from the middle bar blank for the hot compression
test. The effect of the hot deformation parameters on the
microstructure evolution was investigated to analyze the
evolution of the c
0 phase during forging from the single-
phase region to the double-phase region to elucidate the
causes of the microstructure inhomogeneity. This result
provided an experimental basis for the enhancement of the
hot forging process of the new Ni-based C&W superalloys.
2 Experimental procedure
2.1 Experimental materials
The ingots used in the experiment were produced using
vacuum induction melting, electroslag remelting, and
vacuum arc remelting. After homogenization, the ingots
were repeatedly forged at 1100 and 1130 /C176C as as-received
materials. The c0 solvus temperature of the GH4730 alloy
is 1094 /C176C. The nominal chemical composition of the
GH4730 superalloy is shown in Table 1. The specimens
with /10 mm 9 15 mm for the hot compression experi-
ments were obtained from the mid-radius of the billet.
2.2 Experimental method
Hot compression experiments were performed in an MTS
Criterion60 mechanical simulator at a constant strain rate
(0.1 s -1) and three different strain levels (0.357, 0.693, and
1.204). The selection of strain rates and strain variables
was determined based on past research work and parame-
ters used in industrial forging [ 27–30]. Because we

the deformation of the material is accompanied by an

increase in the internal energy, which disrupts the ther-
modynamic equilibrium of the microstructure [ 42, 43]. To
re-establish thermodynamic equilibrium and reduce inter-
nal energy, ‘ ‘dynamic recovery’ ’, ‘ ‘DRX’ ’, and ‘ ‘post-dy-
namic recrystallization (PDRX)’ ’ occur during hot forging
[44, 45]. Ni-based C&W superalloys have a slow dynamic
recovery and are more prone to DRX behavior. DRX is an
important approach to reﬁning the grain structure of Ni-
based C&W superalloys. DRX is a process in which
internal energy forces the formation and migration of
HAGBs, which results in the formation of new strain-free
grains in the deformed materials. DRX is mainly divided
into discontinuous dynamic recrystallization (DDRX) and
continuous dynamic recrystallization (CDRX) [ 46, 47].
DDRX is mainly nucleated by the strain induced in the
bow bending of the original grain boundaries [ 48, 49]. The
recrystallized grains grow by consuming the surrounding
unstable deformed grains. Recrystallization is achieved
when the entire deformed grains are occupied by recrys-
tallized grains. The recrystallized grains preferentially
occur at original grain boundaries and localized inhomo-
geneous deformation zones, which appear as a necklace
structure. DDRX requires excellent migration of grain
boundaries. The migration ability of GBs increases as the
deformation temperature increases.
CDRX occurs by the coalescence and growth of sub-
grains. During the deformation, dislocations increase and
slip under the stress, and dislocations accumulate when
they slip to original grain boundaries [ 38, 50]. When

which leads to an increasing density of dislocations within
the subgrain boundaries and the formation of HAGBs.
After the deformation of the materials has stopped, they
remain at high temperatures. The dynamically formed
nuclei and recrystallized grains in the materials continue to
grow and consume the stored energy [ 13, 51–54]. There-
fore, PDRX plays a signiﬁcant role in microstructure
evolution. During PDRX, the unrecrystallized grains in the
material can be fully recrystallized, the grains continue to
grow, and the grain size becomes dependent on the
deformation temperature.
The DRX mechanism of the GH4730 superalloy above
the c
0 solvus temperature is DDRX, which is achieved by
grain boundary bulging, as reported by Qu et al. [ 55]. As
shown in Fig. 7b, the DRX mechanism of the GH4730
alloy is DDRX when the strain level is low during the
deformation in the double-phase region, as demonstrated
by the grain boundaries and necklace structures. As shown
in Fig. 13a–e, DDRX also occurs at high strain levels when
the materials are forged in the double-phase region, but
orientation deﬂection of large unrecrystallized grains is
also shown in Fig. 13d, in contrast to the large grains with
no internal distortion shown in Fig. 8. Thus, the DRX
mechanism at high strain levels is dominated by DDRX
and supplemented by CDRX.
