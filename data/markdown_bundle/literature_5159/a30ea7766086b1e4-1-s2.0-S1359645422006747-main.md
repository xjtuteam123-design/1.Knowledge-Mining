Porous metals are lightweight and multi-functional at the expense of strength and ductility, requiring 
post-heat treatment to elevate mechanical properties. In this study, we report porous NiTi-Nb alloys fabri- 
cated via laser powder bed fusion, which achieved a synergistic strength-ductility enhancement by mod- 
ulating the eutectic microregion through heat treatment. After solution treatment with the aging process, 
porous NiTi-Nb alloys achieved ultra-high compressive strength (2 GPa) and good ductility (33%). Com- 
pared to the as-built sample, the strength was enhanced by 500 MPa, and the ductility was improved by 
12%. The effect of heat treatment on the eutectic microregion and the mechanism of strengthening and 
toughening were revealed. In the as-built sample, β-Nb existed mainly in a mesh-like morphology, while 
in heat-treated samples, the mesh-like β-Nb was damaged and appeared in an independent morphology 
(sphere-like or rod-like). Compared to mesh-like β-Nb, independent β-Nb enabled the adjacent matrix to 
have a more favorable twinning orientation, which could effectively release strain energy through stress- 
induced martensitic transformation. Moreover, the internal dislocation density of mesh-like β-Nb was 
relatively low, while independent β-Nb had a superior capacity to store dislocations. During the strain 
accumulation, the staggered twins and Ti 2 Ni precipitates together provided a continuous strain hardening 
capability, which ultimately led to an ultra-high compressive strength. This work can provide inspiration 
for porous metals to achieve a combination of high porosity and outstanding mechanical properties.

1. Introduction 
Porous metals are widely utilized in aerospace, automotive in- 
dustry, and biomedical implants due to their lightweight, high spe- 
ciﬁc strength, good damping property, and thermal resistance [ 1 , 2 ].

metals such as 316L stainless steel, CoCr alloys, CP-Ti, Ti-6Al-4V,

[ 8 , 9 ]. To tackle these drawbacks, heat treatment can improve me-

NiTi shape memory alloy (SMA) has a low elastic modulus, fa- 
vorable shape memory effect, and superelasticity that can be ap- 
plied in biomedical implant devices, actuators, and energy damp- 
ing structures [1] . The addition of Nb improves biocompatibil- 
ity and expands thermal hysteresis, making it more suitable for 
biomedical applications [11–13] . According to the NiTi-Nb pseudo- 
binary phase diagram, Nb will undergo the eutectic reaction with 
NiTi at high temperature (above 1150 °C), forming a quasi-binary 
liquid eutectic region with a composition close to Ni 38 Ti 36 Nb 26 
(at%) [14] . So far, the most widely used alloy in the Ti-Ni-Nb sys- 
tem is the Ni 47 Ti 44 Nb 9 (at%) bulk material, which has distinguish- 
ing properties such as high strength and wide thermal hysteresis 
[ 15 , 16 ]. The eutectic region plays a vital role in various properties 
of NiTi-Nb alloys, such as strength, ductility, and superelasticity. 
Therefore, it is hypothesized that the properties of NiTi-Nb alloy 
can be improved by modulating the characteristics of the eutec- 
tic region. Wang et al. reported that β-Nb with rod-like morphol- 
ogy can provide NiTi-Nb alloys with good ductility, high strength, 
and deformation coordination properties, while β-Nb with spheri- 
cal morphology can improve the superelasticity as well [ 11 , 14 ]. Re- 
cently, β-Nb with a mesh structure has been reported to enable 
NiTi-Nb alloy to obtain high compressive strength [ 17 , 18 ]. However, 
the studies on modulating the eutectic region to enhance the me- 
chanical properties of NiTi-Nb alloy are not comprehensive enough, 
and the inﬂuence mechanism of β-Nb morphology on the mechan- 
ical properties has not been revealed yet. 
As an SMA, the NiTi matrix will undergo stress-induced 
martensitic transformation (SIM) with increasing strain, and re- 
lease the strain energy through a series of twinning, reorienta- 
tion, and detwinning processes, leading to transformation-induced 
plasticity (TRIP) and twinning-induced plasticity (TWIP) effect [19–
22] . As a soft phase, β-Nb can play a role in storing dislocations, 
buffering microcracks, and avoiding catastrophic fracture [ 23 , 24 ]. 
Maintaining an adequate strain hardening capability while increas- 
ing strain is the key to synergistically improving strength-ductility 
[25] . As the strain increases, SIM will generate different types of 
twins in the matrix, these staggered twins can strongly impede 
dislocation movement, resulting in the twin boundary-associated 
strengthening effect [26] . Furthermore, heat treatment (aging) will 
introduce hard intermetallic precipitates, which can impede dislo- 
cation movement and lead to precipitation strengthening [ 27 , 28 ]. 
So far, there are limited reports on the synergistic enhancement of 
strength and ductility of NiTi-Nb alloys, and modulating the eutec- 
tic region appears to be a potential means for achieving this pur- 
pose. 
Here, we present porous NiTi-Nb alloys prepared by laser pow- 
der bed fusion (LPBF) with a porosity close to 30% and a reduced 
elastic modulus of 11.6–15.4 GPa, meeting the biomechanical suit- 
ability of most human cortical bones (7–30 GPa) [29] . To achieve a 
synergistic strength-ductility enhancement, we modulate the eu- 
tectic microregion by solution treatment and aging and ﬁnally 
achieve an ultra-high compressive strength (2 GPa) and a good 
ductility (33%) (MAG2 sample). Compared to the as-built sample, 
the strength was increased by 500 MPa, while the ductility was 
increased by 12%. The remarkable increase in ductility is attributed 
to the enhanced β-Nb dislocation storage capacity and more fa- 
vorable matrix twinning orientation. The precipitation strengthen- 
ing of Ti 2 Ni and the twin boundary-associated strengthening of 
staggered twins together ensure suﬃcient strain hardening rate to 
achieve a synergistic increase in strength and ductility. This work 
provides inspiration for achieving a combination of high porosity 
and superior mechanical properties for porous metals. 
2. Experimental 
2.1. Sample preparation 
Fig. 1 a shows the designed model of the porous cylinder, in- 
cluding its unit cell structure and the top view image. The porous 
cylinders were designed by Materialise Magics 24.0 software, and 
the body diagonals with nodes rounded unit cell structure was 
chosen, which was consistent with the I-WP structure from the 
triply periodic minimal surface (TPMS) series structures, with 
smooth curvature and no sharp corners [30] . This structure can im- 
prove cell penetration and proliferation while avoiding the stress 
concentration caused by the local curvature mutation, which is fa- 
vorable for obtaining high-strength porous metals. The unit cell 
size was designed as 800 μm, and as shown in the top view of 
the model, the regular circular pores were uniformly distributed 
with a pore size of approximately 300 μm, satisfying the biolog- 
ical requirements of orthopedic implants [31] . The porous cylin- 
der dimensions were /Phi14 × 6 mm with a height-to-diameter ra- 
tio of 1.5, meeting the requirements of uniaxial compression test 
specimens. The top view of the model shows a centrosymmetric 
cross-section without the potential risk of compressive instability. 
In addition, the ratio of the cylinder diameter to the pore size ex- 
ceeds 10, with a suﬃcient number of complete circular pores in 
the cross-section that the cylinder can be quite representative of 
the mechanical properties of the porous structure. According to 
the NiTi-Nb pseudo-binary eutectic phase diagram ( Fig. 1 b), the 
sub-eutectic composition of Ni 46.5 Ti 44.5 Nb 9 was selected. The pre- 
alloyed NiTi powder and Nb powder were added to the planetary 
ball milling jar and ball milled at a speed of 400 r/min for 2 h to 
ensure the powder was well mixed. The particle size of the ball- 
milled NiTi-Nb mixed powder was less than 50 μm, and it was 
mostly regular spherical ( Fig. 1 c). To examine the mixing effect, the 
mixed powder was characterized by SEM and EDS at high magni- 
ﬁcation, indicating that the Nb powder attached to the pre-alloyed 
NiTi powder successfully, which can guarantee the compositional 
homogeneity of as-built samples prepared by the subsequent ad- 
ditive manufacturing ( Fig. 1 d). The porous NiTi-Nb cylinders were 
prepared using the LPBF technique, and the laser scanning strategy 
is shown in Fig. 1 d. The LPBF equipment was an HK M125 with an 
accuracy of 0.1 mm, and the preparation process was protected by 
a high-purity argon atmosphere. The processing parameters were 
as follows: laser power of 200 W, the spot size of 120 μm, pow- 
der bed thickness of 30 μm, and scanning speed of 600 mm/s. The 
rotation angle between adjacent layers is 67 °. The schematic dia- 
gram of heat treatment is shown in Fig. 1 e. First, the as-built sam- 
ples were encapsulated through a quartz tube ﬁlled with argon gas 
to prevent oxidation. The samples were placed in a box-type resis- 
tance furnace for solution treatment at 10 0 0 °C for 0.5 h, followed 
by water quenching. The red and blue boxes showed that the su- 
persaturated solid solution samples were subjected to single-stage 
aging at high-temperature (450 °C) and low-temperature (250 °C) 
for 2 h. In addition, multi-stage aging processes combining high- 
temperature and low-temperature aging with different duration 
were adopted to treat the supersaturated samples, as shown in the 
green and purple boxes. The cooling method was water quenching 
for all aged samples.

3. Results 
3.1. Microstructural characterization at multiscale 
The apparent density and porosity of porous and solid samples 
are summarized in Table S1. The porosity of porous samples ranged 
from 26.3 to 28.1%, and the heat treatment had no signiﬁcant ef- 
fect on the porosity. Fig. 2 a shows the macroscopic morphology 
of an as-built sample. The SEM image of the surface shows that 
the circular pores were arranged regularly, which was consistent 
with the top view of the model, indicating that the preparation of 
complex porous structures was achieved ( Fig. 2 b). The overlap of 
the laser scanning path during the LPBF process resulted in over- 
lapping tracks on the surface [34] . In addition, the strut thickness 
of porous samples was measured by following Fig. 2 b, and the re- 
sults are summarized in Table S1. In this study, the strut thickness 
was approximately 410 μm, and the difference between individ- 
ual samples was negligible. XRD analysis reveals that the samples 
were primarily composed of NiTi matrix (B2 austenite with a small 
amount of B19 ′ twinned martensite) with β-Nb ( Fig. 2 c). In addi- 
tion, the Ti 2 Ni peaks were found in all heat-treated samples, indi- 
cating the successful introduction of precipitates by heat treatment 
[19] . The (110) B2 peaks shifted to lower degrees due to the increase 
in lattice constant of NiTi caused by the solid solution of Nb [35] . 
Impressively, the (110) B2 peaks of HAG and MAG2 samples shifted 
to a lower angle than the other two heat-treated samples, which 
reﬂected the bigger lattice constant. It was due to the higher solu- 
bility of Nb in the matrix during high-temperature aging compared 
to low-temperature aging, which increases the lattice constant of 
the matrix [36] . The (110) B2 peak angle of the as-built sample was 
relatively the lowest, related to the supersaturated solution caused 
by the rapid cooling in the LPBF process [37] . 
The heating and cooling curves, transformation temperature, 
and thermal hysteresis calculation results are shown in Fig. 3 and 
Table 1 . DSC results show a single-stage transformation (B2 ↔ B19 ′ ) 
during heating and cooling for all samples, with no R phase trans- 
formation [21] . The martensitic transformation start temperatures 
(M s ) of the heat-treated samples (-35.8—17.7 °C) were all higher 
than the as-built sample (-38.5 °C). Because these precipitates 
acted as stress concentration points, increasing local stress and 
making lattice shear more likely to occur ( Table 1 ), resulting in a 
higher M s temperature [ 38 , 39 ]. A f temperatures in Table 1 show 
that only the B2 phase was present in as-built and LAG samples, 
and no B19 ′ martensite existed. While a small amount of marten- 
site was present in the remaining three samples, as shown in 
Fig. 2 c. The thermal hysteresis (A s -M s ) of samples was greatly ex- 
panded (more than 10 °C), which was conducive to the orthopedic 
clinical application. The expansion of heat hysteresis is attributed 
to β-Nb and Ti 2 Ni precipitation during the aging process, which 
crystallographically hindered the shape memory cycle [15] .

3.2. Compressive properties 
Uniaxial compression tests show that introducing porous struc- 
ture can signiﬁcantly reduce the elastic modulus of NiTi-Nb al- 
loys (11.6–15.4 GPa) compared to the solid sample (28.0 GPa). In 
this work, nearly 30% porosity enables a decrease in elastic mod- 
ulus of approximately 50%, which allows the porous NiTi-Nb al- 
loys to meet biomechanical suitability with the majority of hu- 
man cortical bone. After heat treatment, both strength and duc- 
tility are distinctly improved compared to the as-built sample, 
with ultimate compressive strength reaching 2060 ± 21 MPa and 
strain reaching 33.0 ± 0.4% (MAG2 sample). The strength increases 
by about 500 MPa, while the ductility increases by 12% ( Fig. 7 a, 
Table 3 ). Furthermore, all porous samples exhibit a multi-stage 
fracture mode that differs from the majority of porous samples, 
but instead, like the solid sample, the stress gradually reaches a 
maximum and fractures completely. The local magniﬁcation curves 
below 10 0 0 MPa show that all samples exhibit an apparent stress 
plateau, conﬁrming the SIM occurrence in all samples (Fig. S1) 
[22] . The σSIM is signiﬁcantly lower in the heat-treated samples 
than in as-built samples due to the increased local stress from the 
precipitates introduced by the aging process, making the lattice 
shear more likely to occur under the same external stress values 
[19] . In addition, we note that the σSIM of porous samples is much 
lower than that of the solid sample. When the porous samples are 
stressed, some struts suffer from stress concentration, which leads 
to higher local stress and earlier SIM, resulting in lower σSIM .

4. Discussion 
The presented results show that heat treatment signiﬁcantly 
improved both strength and ductility of NiTi-Nb alloys. In the case 
of the MAG2 sample, the strength increased by about 500 MPa, 
and the ductility increased by 12% (compared to the as-built sam- 
ple). Now we consider how heat treatment can modulate the 
eutectic microregion and simultaneously strengthen and toughen 
NiTi-Nb alloys.

4.1. Effects of heat treatment on eutectic regions and the underlying 
mechanism 
First of all, the formation mechanism of the mesh-like β-Nb in 
the as-built sample needs to be revealed by the NiTi-Nb pseudo- 
binary phase diagram and the non-equilibrium solidiﬁcation of 
LPBF. Lu et al. reported ultraﬁne cellular grains in SLMed NiTi, since 
the cooling rate of the molten pool in LPBF can reach 10 4 –10 6 K/s, 
it leads to non-equilibrium solidiﬁcation and forms ﬁne equiaxed 
NiTi grains (B2) [75] . Fang et al. pointed out that a layer of colum- 
nar grains would form at the bottom of the micro-molten pool 
during the DED process, and subsequently, the grains would grad- 
ually tend to be equiaxed, resulting in a network structure in the 
eutectic region [76] . Similarly, in this work, during the LPBF pro- 
cess, the high-energy laser beam would melt the NiTi-Nb mixed 
powder into liquid, forming a micro-molten pool containing NiTi 
(L) and Nb (L). As the laser beam moves away, the mixed liquid 
rapidly cools down to the NiTi liquidus and NiTi grains will nucle- 
ate. When the temperature drops down to the eutectic point, the 
eutectic reaction (L → NiTi + β-Nb) will occur. β-Nb tends to nu- 
cleate at the NiTi grain boundaries and arrange along the equiaxed 
grains, forming the so-called mesh-like morphology [17] . The OM 
image shows that a large number of ﬁne equiaxed grains, with 
sizes mostly in the range of 1-3 μm, appear in the as-built sample 
(Fig. S5a), which is basically consistent with the size of the mesh- 
like β-Nb. 
After heat treatment, the NiTi grains became coarser, and the 
representative OM image of the heat-treated samples shows coarse 
NiTi equiaxed grains with sizes ranging from 3 to 10 μm (Fig. S5b). 
According to the results in Fig. 6 i and Table 2 , it is found that the 
mesh-like β-Nb was damaged and existed in sphere-like or rod- 
like morphology in the heat-treated samples compared to the as- 
built samples, and there were distinct differences in the contents 
of both kinds of β-Nb in different heat-treated samples. As pre- 
viously reported, during the solution treatment, the rod-like β-Nb 
with a high aspect ratio will gradually undergo fusion, spheroidiza- 
tion, and coarsening processes [77] . In NiTi-Nb alloys, the mesh- 
like β-Nb can be regarded as composed of independent rod-like 
β-Nb ( Fig. 6 h). After solution treatment and water quenching, the 
rod gradually transformed into a sphere, exhibiting fewer rod- 
like β-Nb and more sphere-like β-Nb. It is found that the sam- 
ples without aging at high-temperature for 1 h or longer (LAG 
and MAG1 samples) retained a large proportion of rod-like β-Nb 
( Fig. 6 b and d) by comparing the typical eutectic regions of the 
different heat-treated samples. However, in HAG and MAG2 sam- 
ples, almost no rod-like β-Nb phase was found (0.92 and 0.75%). 
This indicates that for the same supersaturated samples, the β- 
Nb precipitated from the matrix tended to grow spherically during 
high-temperature aging, while growth toward the rod morphology 
was suppressed. Furthermore, the total volume fractions of β-Nb 
in the heat-treated samples were all lower than that of the as-built 
sample, indicating that the heat-treatment can precipitate more Nb 
atoms from the matrix in the form of β-Nb, and reduce the super- 
saturated solid solution degree of Nb in the matrix, which is con- 
sistent with the (110) B2 peak shift phenomenon in the XRD pattern 
( Fig. 2 c) [78] . 
The LPBF process will result in Ni volatilization and Ti-rich ma- 
trix composition, which will preferentially precipitate Ti 2 Ni dur- 
ing the aging process [75] . When aging at the high-temperature, 
the atomic motion is intense, and the G-P region forms easily, 
leading to a higher nucleation rate [79] . However, because Ni has 
a high solid solution degree at the high-temperature, fewer Ni 
atoms will precipitate [80] . High-temperature aging will nucle- 
ate a large amount of Ti 2 Ni precipitates, but the supply of Ni 
atoms for growth is limited, obtaining ﬁner Ti 2 Ni precipitates. Low- 
temperature aging, on the other hand, can precipitate more Ni 
atoms from the matrix, supplying the growth of Ti 2 Ni. However, 
the atomic motion is not intense enough during low-temperature 
aging, and the nucleation rate of Ti 2 Ni is relatively low. There- 
fore, multi-stage aging was employed in this study, in which

4.2. Strength-ductility enhancement mechanism of heat treatment 
The contribution of such a characteristic eutectic region to the 
synergistic enhancement of strength and ductility should be clari- 
ﬁed by analyzing the microstructure evolution of heat-treated sam- 
ples during the deformation. When external stress is applied, the 
NiTi matrix can release strain energy through SIM and subsequent 
twinning, rather than accumulate dislocations to fracture [66] . The 
microstructure evolution of the matrix during deformation was in- 
vestigated in detail, and the crossover phenomenon between ⟨ 011 ⟩ 
type II twins and (11-1) type I twins, as well as deformation 
twins in detwinned martensite, were all uncovered ( Fig. 8 ). All of 
these can effectively pile up dislocations and achieve synergistic 
twin boundary-associated strengthening effects, providing contin- 
uous strain hardening capability [82] . Based on the differences in 
the microstructure evolution of the matrix around mesh-like β-Nb

and independent β-Nb, we ﬁnd that the matrix inside the mesh- 
like β-Nb had a more unfavorable twinning orientation than the 
outside. Since it is diﬃcult to release strain energy during the de- 
twinning process, it can only store dislocations internally ( Fig. 9 d). 
In contrast, the twinning orientation of the matrix around inde- 
pendent β-Nb was usually favorable, and the resulting detwinned 
martensite had better dislocation storage capacity, and can with- 
stand more plastic deformation ( Fig. 11 ) [74] . Therefore, the de- 
struction of mesh-like β-Nb into independent β-Nb by heat treat- 
ment can enable the adjacent matrix to have a more favorable 
twinning orientation, release more strain energy, and effectively 
enhance ductility. 
The morphology of β-Nb has a signiﬁcant impact on its behav- 
ior during plastic deformation. Sphere-like β-Nb bore plastic de- 
formation in the form of internal dislocations, and rod-like β-Nb 
stored dislocations at both ends. Both forms of β-Nb have a supe- 
rior ability to store dislocations, which helps to enhance ductility 
( Fig. 10 ) [83] . However, for mesh-like β-Nb, the internal dislocation 
density was relatively low, implying that mesh-like β-Nb cannot 
withstand much plastic deformation, resulting in poor toughening 
effects than independent β-Nb ( Fig. 9 ). Furthermore, mesh-like β- 
Nb disrupted the continuity of the matrix compared to the other 
two morphologies, and it also worsened the overall ductility. 
The distribution state of Ti 2 Ni has a considerable inﬂuence on 
its precipitation strengthening effect. From the HRTEM analysis, 
when Ti 2 Ni was distributed independently, it displayed a non- 
coherent interfacial bonding state with the matrix ( Fig. 5 m). The 
interface was relatively weak and led to the traditional Orowan 
strengthening mechanism, impeding dislocation movement or cut- 
ting it through to form a dislocation loop [84] . In this study, Ti 2 Ni 
tended to nucleate and grew at the interface of sphere-like β-Nb, 
where dislocations existing within the sphere-like β-Nb piled up 
at the interface and continuously provided strong strain harden- 
ing capability during the strain accumulation ( Fig. 10 ). However, 
when Ti 2 Ni nucleated between the discontinuous mesh-like β-Nb, 
it would be supplemented by more Ni atoms and grow up in this 
microregion. Then, it connected these β-Nb phases into a quasi- 
continuous mesh morphology, making the β-Nb less capable of 
storing dislocations and weakening the precipitation strengthen- 
ing effect [85] . In addition, the Ti 2 Ni precipitate acted as a hard 
and brittle phase and was prone to initiate cracks [86] . Fortunately, 
the diffusely distributed β-Nb phases can effectively buffer and in- 
ternally terminate cracks by generating dislocation entanglement, 
which can avoid the brittle fracture caused by catastrophic crack 
expansion ( Fig. 11 a). To sum up, only damaging the mesh-like β- 
Nb through heat treatment can optimize the twinning orientation 
of the matrix and enhance the dislocation storage capacity of β-Nb 
at the same time. While the ductility was enhanced by modulating 
the eutectic microregion, the twin-boundary assisted strengthen- 
ing by staggered twins and the precipitation strengthening of Ti 2 Ni 
together provided excellent strain hardening capability. Ultimately, 
a synergistic strength-ductility enhancement of NiTi-Nb alloy was 
achieved. 
It is noteworthy that the compressive strength of the porous 
NiTi-Nb alloys after heat treatment reached up to 2 GPa, not only 
due to the considerable enhancement of strength and ductility by 
heat treatment but also attributes to the unique fracture mode 
brought by the low porosity. Yang et al. performed ﬁnite element 
modeling of the Schwartz diamond gradient porous structure and 
pointed out that the smaller the volume fraction of the strut, the 
higher the local stress level under the same overall load and the 
more prone to failure [31] . Most of the previous-reported porous 
metals have a porosity of 50% or more, which would make the 
porous sample exhibit a multi-stage fracture mode, i.e., the par- 
tial struts fracture ﬁrst, the other struts fracture sequentially, and 
ﬁnally enters the densiﬁcation stage [31] . However, in this work, 
all porous NiTi-Nb samples did not exhibit multi-stage fracture 
mode, and instead, the stress gradually increased to the highest 
level with the increase of strain until fractured completely. Due 
to the low porosity (nearly 30%) and the uniform strut thickness 
(about 410 μm), the local stress in each portion of the strut was 
not much different under the external load, the stress concentra- 
tion degree was quite low and the internal stress distribution was 
relatively uniform (Table S1). Therefore, even under a large exter- 
nal load, the strut will not fracture prematurely due to excessive 
local stress but will fracture completely until the ultimate stress is 
reached. 
4.3. Differences in mechanical properties of eutectic microregions 
To explore the differences in the mechanical properties of three 
different characteristic eutectic microregions individually, three eu- 
tectic micropillars containing sphere-like, rod-like, and mesh-like 
β-Nb were milled by FIB, named sphere-like, rod-like, and mesh- 
like micropillar, followed by in-situ micropillar compression tests 
( Fig. 12 ). The sampling positions of the three micropillars are 
shown in Fig. 12 b, f, and j. The stress-strain curves of three 
micropillars demonstrate that the ultimate compressive strength 
of the micropillars differs signiﬁcantly ( Fig. 12 a). The rod-like 
micropillar has the highest strength ( ∼4 GPa), followed by the 
sphere-like micropillar ( ∼3 GPa), while the mesh-like micropil- 
lar has the lowest strength ( ∼2 GPa). Meanwhile, the stress-strain 
curves of all samples show apparent stress plateaus, indicating the 
occurrence of SIM. The σSIM of the rod-like and mesh-like mi- 
cropillars is similar, while the sphere-like micropillar has the low- 
est σSIM . Because of the size effect, the strength of micropillars 
is much higher than that of macroscopic specimens [18] . Further- 
more, during the compression tests, all micropillar samples exhib- 
ited no visible slip bands, only upsetting, demonstrating favorable 
ductility ( Fig. 12 c–e, g–i, and k–m). 
The reason for the signiﬁcant difference in compressive strength 
of micropillars needs to be revealed from three perspectives. First, 
the σSIM of the sphere-like micropillar is the lowest among the 
three micropillars. It is speculated that the ﬁner Ti 2 Ni precipitates 
will lead to greater local curvature, severer stress concentration, 
and easier lattice shearing. To conﬁrm this speculation, a supple- 
mentary BSE characterization was performed to measure the size 
and quantity of Ti 2 Ni precipitates in three different eutectic mi- 
croregions, as shown in Table S3. The results show that the aver- 
age size of Ti 2 Ni precipitates in the microregion with sphere-like 
β-Nb is 150 μm, which is the ﬁnest compared to the microregions 
with rod-like (200 μm) and mesh-like (188 μm) β-Nb, while the 
quantity per unit area (10 3 μm −2 ) is the largest. This can conﬁrm 
the statement that the microregion with sphere-like β-Nb mainly 
underwent high-temperature aging with a high nucleation rate of 
Ti 2 Ni but an insuﬃcient supply of Ni atoms, forming more and 
ﬁner Ti 2 Ni precipitates.

5. Conclusion 
In conclusion, porous NiTi-Nb alloys with ultra-high-strength 
(2 GPa) and good ductility (33%) were obtained through LPBF and 
post-heat treatment. The modulation of the eutectic microregion 
by heat treatment led to a remarkable increase in both strength 
and ductility, and the intrinsic mechanism was investigated. The 
main conclusions are as follows: 
(1) The β-Nb phase in as-built NiTi-Nb alloys exists mainly in a 
mesh-like morphology. After heat treatment, the mesh-like β- 
Nb is damaged and appears in an independent spherical or rod- 
like morphology. During high-temperature aging, the β-Nb pre- 
cipitated from the matrix tends to grow spherically, while the 
growth toward the rod morphology is suppressed. 
(2) The matrix inside the mesh-like β-Nb has a more unfavorable 
twinning orientation than outside. Since it is diﬃcult to release 
strain energy during the detwinning process, it can only store 
dislocations internally. In addition, the internal dislocation den- 
sity of mesh-like β-Nb is relatively low, resulting in poor tough- 
ening effects. 
(3) The twinning orientation of the matrix around the independent 
β-Nb is more favorable, and the strain energy can be well re- 
leased by SIM. Independent sphere-like β-Nb accumulates plas- 
tic deformation in the form of internal dislocations, and rod- 
like β-Nb stores high-density dislocations at both ends, which 
helps to enhance ductility. 
(4) The crossover phenomenon between ⟨ 011 ⟩ type II twins and 
(11-1) type I twins, as well as deformation twins in detwinned 
martensite, were revealed, all of which can effectively plug dis- 
locations. Ti 2 Ni tends to nucleate and grow at the interface of 
sphere-like β-Nb, and dislocations existing within the sphere- 
like β-Nb will pile up at the interface. Both staggered twins and 
Ti 2 Ni precipitates can provide continuous strain hardening ca- 
pability during the strain accumulation to achieve synergistic 
enhancement of strength and ductility.
