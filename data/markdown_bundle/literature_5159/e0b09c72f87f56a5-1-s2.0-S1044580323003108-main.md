ABSTRACT 
Here we fabricate a heterostructure in CrCoNi medium entropy alloy consisting of deformed substructures (the 
hard zone) and recrystallized ultrafine/fine grains (the soft zone). Mechanical tests reveal that the heterogeneous 
structure displays an exceptional combination of strength and ductility, especially at cryogenic condition, with a 
yield strength up to 1480 MPa and a uniform elongation of 28%. Such mechanical performance is primarily 
attributed to the zone boundary-dictated inhomogeneous deformation. Strain incompatibility-induced inter-zone 
interaction produces high internal stress near zone boundary, which leads to quick piling-up of geometrically 
necessary dislocations and consequently extra back stress strengthening. Cryogenic deformation further improves 
the internal stress, which helps to activate additional mechanical twinning and promotes the multiplication and 
interaction of defects, thereby contributing to high strain hardening at the large plastic stage. These findings 
suggest that heterostructured CrCoNi medium entropy alloy is a promising candidate for structural application 
over a wide temperature range. 
1. Introduction 
Excellent combination of strength and ductility over a wide tem -
perature range is highly desired for structural materials. However, 
except for a few special materials such as the cryogenic steels, certain 
austenitic stainless steels and cryogenic titanium alloys, most conven -
tional high-strength metals exhibit serious cryogenic brittleness, which 
largely restricts their engineering application. In past decade, multi- 
component high/medium entropy alloys (H/MEAs) have attracted 
much attention for their extradentary properties [ 1 – 7 ], such as excellent 
strength and fracture toughness [ 3 , 4 ], superior high temperature me -
chanical performance [ 5 ], good wear resistance [ 6 ] as well as irradiation 
resistance [ 7 ]. Notably, the face-centered cubic (FCC) structure H/MEAs 
appear as a new class of promising candidates for cryogenic application. 
For instance, at cryogenic temperature, the fracture toughness of some 
of them even attains K

yield strength of coarse-grained (CG) H/MEAs is generally lower than 
~400 MPa, although high configure entropy confers high friction stress. 
Through grain size refinement, the attainment of about 1-GPa yield 
strength is usually accompanied by a dramatic sacrifice of uniform 
elongation that even reduced to only ~2% [ 8 ]. 
Heterogeneous microstructure design, deploying heterogeneous 
constituent zones with dramatic difference in mechanical properties, has 
been recently reorganized as an effective approach to overcome the 
trade-off dilemma between strength and ductility [ 9 – 13 ]. For instance, 
the heterogeneous lamella Ti was reported to be as strong as the ho -
mogeneous ultrafine-grained (UFG) sample and simultaneously as 
ductile as the fully annealed CG counterpart [ 10 ]. The heterogeneous 
CrCoNi MEA with grain size spanning the nanometer to micrometer 
range displays a yield strength up to ~1.5 GPa while retain decent 
ductility (~33%) at cryogenic temperature, which is unattainable by the 
homogeneous structures [ 11 ]. This suggests that heterostructure design 
in H/MEAs may be a potential route towards strong-yet-ductile mate -
rials for cryogenic use [ 14 , 15 ].

with regard to the coupling of multiple potential factors affecting the

performed on tensile deformed samples at different interrupted strains. 
Analyses based on the stress-strain behaviors and microstructure evo -
lutions were carried out to reveal the deformation mechanisms. 
2. Materials and methods 
The equimolar CrCoNi MEA was prepared using arc-melting 
elemental ingredients with a purity > 99.9 wt% in a high-purity argon 
atmosphere. After homogenized by repeated melting, the ingots were 
hot forged into slabs with a thickness of 6.3 mm, and then subjected to 
multi-pass cryo-rolling to ~90% thickness reduction. The sheets were 
immersed in liquid nitrogen for 5 min before each rolling pass. No cracks 
were examined on the surfaces of cryo-rolled sheets. The thin sheets 
were then annealed at the critical recrystallization temperature (893 K) 
with a duration of 20 min to get partial recrystallization.

A FEI Quanta 650F field emission scanning electron microscope (FE-

3.2. Mechanical properties 
Fig. 3 displays the mechanical properties of as-prepared hetero -
structure at both ambient and cryogenic temperatures. At ambient 
temperature (293 K), it exhibits a high yield strength of 1080 MPa, a 
tensile strength of 1224 MPa and an exceptional uniform elongation of 
~17%. As expected, this strength-ductility combination is superior than 
homogeneous structures. For instance, at the same strength level, the 
uniform elongation of homogeneous UFG sample is only ~2% (the black 
dashed curves) [ 8 ]. This suggests that microstructure heterogeneity 
plays a critical role in retaining strain hardening. At cryogenic temper -
ature (77 K), strength and ductility are simultaneously enhanced, dis -
playing an ultrahigh yield strength of 1480 MPa, a tensile strength of 
1650 MPa and uniform elongation high up to 28% ( Fig. 3 (a)). Such 
temperature-dependent mechanical responses are typical for FCC 
structures, especially that with relatively low SFE, and the hetero -
structured MEA here is no exception [ 11 ]. A comparison of strain 
hardening rate is shown in Fig. 3 (b). Notably, the heterostructure

4. Discussion 
4.1. Strengthening and work hardening of the heterostructure 
In the deformation of the partial-recrystallized heterostructure, the 
dispersed hard NRZs serve as the strengthening phase analogue to the 
martensitic phase in dual-phase steel [ 44 ]. With increasing load to near 
global yielding, dislocation sources in the soft RZs should be activated 
first, whereas the NRZs maintains still elastic due to the higher elastic 
limit. The difference in Poisson ’ s ratio activates mutual constraints be -
tween zones, i.e., elastic-plastic inter-zone interaction, which complexes 
the stress state in the vicinity of zone boundary [ 9 , 16 , 45 ]. This 
microstructure heterogeneity-dominated yielding confers several 
unique micromechanics near zone boundary. First, it initiates the 
accumulation of GNDs near zone boundary to accommodate the strain 
incompatibility, as exemplarily shown in Fig. 4 (a). Second, the potential 
dislocation sources at or near zone boundary are preferentially activated 
( Fig. 4 (b)). This unique micromechanics collectively induce the forma -
tion of early hetero-zone boundary affected region (Hbar), contributing 
to extraordinary forest strengthening and back stress strengthening in 
the RZs [ 10 , 17 , 46 ]. In this regard, the dispersed distribution of NRZs 
should be a key microstructural factor for higher extra strengthening, 
since it enables higher density of Hbar [ 12 ]. This physics should be 
partially responsible for the observed high yield strength ( Fig. 3 (a)). 
On the other hand, the small grain size of RZs, 90% of them are 
smaller than 2.5 μ m ( Figs. 2 (c, e)), should be also a critical factor behind 
the high yield strength of heterostructure, as supported by Hall-Patch 
physics. At the low-strain stage soon after yielding, the plastic strain 
gradient accumulates near zone boundary continuously due to the inter- 
zone strain partitioning [ 10 – 12 , 16 ], and thus the GNDs-dictated hard -
ening develops quickly. This helps to maintain the high work hardening. 
Fang et al. [ 47 ] has also observed that such hetero-deformation induced 
hardening is very efficient at the low-strain stage. 
As strain continues, a large number dislocations and SFs multiplied 
and mutually interacted in recrystallized grains, which undertake most 
of the applied strain ( Fig. 5 (a)). As the enlarged image shown in Fig. 5 
(b), defects frequently interacted with the annealing twins, with part of 
them transmitted through twin boundary [ 22 ]. This could in turn hinder

4.2. Mechanical twinning-assisted strain hardening at cryogenic 
temperature 
Both the strength and strain hardening capability of heterostructured 
CrCoNi MEA have been dramatically improved when the deformation 
temperature decreases from 298 K to 77 K ( Fig. 3 ). In addition to the 
improved activation stress of dislocations, it is largely due to the addi -
tional deformation twinning-mediated plasticity. As shown in Fig. 6 , at 
~10% cryogenic plastic strain, deformation twins (DTs) are frequently 
observed in the recrystallized fine grains near hetero-zone boundaries. 
This may be attributed to the higher internal stress near zone boundary 
that imparted by deformation incompatibility induced inter-zone 
interaction, which may persist in the large strain stage as well 
[ 18 , 48 ]. Deformation twins in width of a few nanometers terminated to 
grain interior or another end ( Fig. 6 (a)). More interestingly, the 
newborn deformation twins are always coupled with 9R structures 
( Fig. 6 (b)), which originate from the dissociation of part of the inco -
herent twin boundaries (as marked by green arrow). Note that the 9R 
structure is consisting of a repeating unit of 9 {111} atomic layers, i.e., 6 
stacking fault planes and 3 normal stacking planes, which has a higher 
formation energy in comparison with DTs [ 49 – 51 ]. In these regards, 
microstructure heterogeneity can effectively promote the activation of 
high energy structures during cryogenic deformation, thereby enabling 
massive accumulation of defects at higher tensile strain. 
As cryogenic tensile strain increased to ~20%, DTs can be readily 
detected in a large part of recrystallized grains, including that in size of 
several hundred nanometers ( Fig. 7 (a)). At the same time, a large 
number of defects, including dislocations and SFs, multiplied and 
intersected significantly, partitioning the recrystallized zones into nar -
rower SFs/DTs networks (~2 – 10 nm) and/or laminates ( Figs. 7 (b, c)). 
These dynamic interactions can not only prevent dislocation motions 
effectively by decreasing the dislocation mean free paths, but also pro -
mote defect accumulation by facilitating microstructure refinement. The 
dislocation density has increased up to ~1.4 × 10
17
/m
2 
( Fig. 7 (d)). 
Thus, the strong dislocation motions and twinning behaviors collec -
tively induce a steady strain hardening at subsequent cryogenic plastic 
stage. 
Low temperature and microstructure heterogeneity collectively 
promoted the nano-twinning. The critical resolved shear stress for 
twinning is highly dependent on the grain size but insensitive to the 
temperature [ 20 – 22 ]. It has been widely suggested that mechanical 
twinning is hardly activated in CrCoNi MEA with UFG or FG micro -
structure [ 19 , 24 ]. For the present heterostructure, cryogenic tempera -
ture plays the role in enhancing the internal stress, especially in the 
vicinity of hetero-zone boundary, and thus provides an opportunity for 
mechanical twinning. Twinning is gradually activated in smaller and 
smaller grains with increasing tensile strain. DTs are efficient hardening 
agencies since they serve as strong barriers for propagating dislocations 
and SFs, and at the same time provide gliding passes for dislocations, 
known as the “ dynamic Hall-Petch effect ” [ 22 ]. This is the mechanisms 
why heterostructured CrCoNi displays a much higher strain hardening 
with decreasing temperature ( Fig. 3 ).
