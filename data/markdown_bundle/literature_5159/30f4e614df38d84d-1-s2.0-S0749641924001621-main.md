1. Introduction 
To increase the thrust-to-weight ratio and efficiency of aircraft engines, the turbine entry temperature continuously increases, 
resulting in an increasingly harsh service environment for turbine blades. Then the harsh environment would result in turbine blades 
operating under extreme cyclic conditions over a range of temperatures and complex stress states, in which the working temperature of 
the blade root, tenon, and inner wall of the air-cooled channel is intermediate temperatures (650 
◦
C~850 
◦
C) ( Darolia, 2019 ; Dye et al., 
2008 ; Kim et al., 2016 ). Therefore, the creep performance at intermediate temperatures is one of the key factors in the material se -
lection of turbine blades. 
Ni-based single crystal superalloys are considered the preferred material for turbine blades due to their excellent comprehensive 
properties ( Mukherjee et al., 2022 ; Reed, 2006 ). Considering [001] crystal orientation is the preferred direction of crystal growth in 
Ni-based superalloys and its great comprehensive performance, single crystal turbine blades usually are directionally solidified during 
the casting process with the crystallographic orientation [001] aligned with the blade stacking axis ( Tan et al., 2018 ; Zhou et al., 2019 ). 
However, during the directional solidification, the blade stacking axis does not always yield precisely the [001] orientation ( Tan et al., 
2018 ; Xiao et al., 2022 ). Fig. 1 a describes the relationship between the single crystal blade coordinate system and the

system. Although more results also suggested the activity of a single {111} 〈 112 〉 slip system would lead to orientation rotation, the

et al. (1990) had different views, suggesting that the creep anisotropy was related to {111} 〈 110 〉 and {001} 〈 110 〉 slip systems rather 
than {111} 〈 112 〉 slip systems. However the slip systems they thought could be active were inconsistent with deformation mechanisms 
dominated by stacking faults. Sass et al. (1996) thought that the poor creep life of samples near the [001]-[111] boundary was related 
to extensive single slip on {111} 〈 112 〉 slip systems or coplanar slip on {111} 〈 110 〉 slip systems resulting in high primary creep rates 
and creep strains. Yu et al. (2020) also thought the mechanism of anisotropic creep was attributed to the heterogeneous γ ′ precipitate 
deformation by {111} 〈 112 〉 slip. However, they failed to notice the influence of orientation rotation. In addition, some researchers 
were more concerned about the influence of {111} 〈 110 〉 slip systems ( Heep et al., 2022 ; Kakehi et al., 1993 ). Kakehi et al. (1993) also 
had similar findings and ascribed the effect of crystallographic loading directions on creep rates to different interface dislocation 
network configurations. Recently, Heep et al. (2022) proposed a new perspective that the creep anisotropy did not rely on dislocation 
networks or multiple planar fault slips, but was governed by dislocation reactions between γ -channel dislocation families with different 
Burgers vectors (a/2 < 110 > ) on the early stages creep strain accumulation. Unfortunately, they only considered the influence of a 
single type of slip system, {111} 〈 112 〉 or {111} 〈 110 〉 . In addition, they have mostly focused on creep fracture or the primary stage of 
creep, lacking analysis of different stages of creep. In fact, the activity of the {111} 〈 112 〉 slip system is closely related to the {111} 
〈 110 〉 slip system ( Drew et al., 2004 ; Qu et al., 2021 ; Rae and Reed, 2007 ; Zhang et al., 2024b ). Therefore, when studying the 
orientation sensitivity for the creep properties in near [001] oriented Ni-based single crystal superalloys, it is necessary to

3. Feasibility analysis of different orientation rotation models 
Although various theories have been proposed to explain the orientation sensitivity of creep life near [001]-orientated single crystal 
superalloys at intermediate temperatures, the orientation rotation theory proposed by MacKay and Maier (1982) seems to be the most 
reasonable. However, the effect of {111} < 110 > slip systems on orientation rotation has been ignored. In fact, the activity of the {111} 
〈 112 〉 requires (i) sufficient densities of at least two a/2{111} 〈 110 〉 families of dislocations in γ matrix, to enable the nucleation of the 
dislocation ribbons in accordance with the reaction given above, and (ii) sufficient resolved shear stress for the propagation of the 
a < 112 > ribbon ( Qu et al., 2021 ; Rae et al., 2001 , 2000 ). Therefore, the orientation rotation should comprehensively consider the

Fig. 2 a describes the illustration of orientation rotation process dominated by {111} 〈 110 〉 slip systems. The [001]-[101]-[111] 
standard stereographic triangle could be divided into several different zones according to the number of slip systems activated, 
such as single slip zone, double slip zone, and multiple slip zone. The multiple slip zone includes [010], [110] and [111] poles, [001]- 
[101], [101]-[111], and [001]-[111] boundaries are regions of double slip, and the internal area of the triangle only has one activated 
slip system ( Li et al., 1999a , b , c ; MacKay and Maier, 1982 ; Sun et al., 2022 ). Although these boundaries all belong to the double slip 
zone, three sides correspond to quite different double-slip orientations with differently active slip systems and corresponding dislo -
cation reactions during tensile deformation: i.e. the critical double slip ([001]-[101]), the coplanar double slip ([101]-[111]), and the 
conjugate double slip ([001]-[111]) ( Li et al., 1999a , b , c ). For the specimen in the internal area of the triangle, as only the (1 11) [011] 
slip system is activated, it will first rotate toward [011] direction. When rotating to the [001]-[111] boundary, the specimen will rotate 
along the [010]-[11 1] boundary under the action of conjugate double slip. Usually, when rotating to the position of [112], the 
specimen no longer rotates, as the effects of the two slip systems cancel out each other ( Li et al., 2022a ; MacKay and Maier, 1982 ; Qu 
et al., 2024b ; Ye et al., 2022 ). Then the illustration of orientation rotation process dominated by {111} 〈 112 〉 slip systems is shown in 
Fig. 2 b. The standard stereographic triangle would also be divided into three different zones. Similar to Fig. 2 a, [001], [101] and [111] 
poles also have multiple activated slip system, and the [101]-[111] boundary belongs to the coplanar double slip. Then the critical 
double slip includes the [001]-X, X-Y and Y-[101] boundaries, and the conjugate double slip includes the [001]-Y and X-[111] 
boundaries. The internal area in the standard stereographic triangle is the region of single slip, which is divided into two parts, 
[001]-X-Y and X-Y-[101]-[111]. In [001]-X-Y region, (111) [ 1 12] is the dominant active slip system, but for X-Y-[101]-[111] region, 
that is (1 11) [121] slip system ( MacKay and Maier, 1982 ; Qu et al., 2023a , 2024a ). The sample near [001] orientation is located in 
[010]-X-Y region. Hence, the specimen will first rotate to the [001]-Y boundary under the action of (111) [ 1 12] slip system. Then, the 
specimen will rotate to the [010] pole along the [010]-Y boundary under the action of conjugate double slip. 
For samples with a certain deviation angle from the [001] orientation, during the creep process at intermediate temperatures, the 
crystal orientation always rotates towards the [001] direction, hence it cannot be caused solely by the {111} 〈 110 〉 slip system. 
Therefore, three possible orientation rotation models are shown in Fig. 2 c. Model A is the direct rotation model, which satisfies most of 
the orientation changes before and after creep rupture. Model B is the model that the rotation is dominated by {111} 〈 112 〉 slip system. 
The sample will first rotate to the [001]-Y ([001]-[101]) boundary under single slip, and then rotate towards the [001] direction under 
conjugate double slip. Model C is the model that the rotation is dominated by both {111} 〈 110 〉 and {111} 〈 112 〉 slip systems. In Model 
C, the sample first rotates towards the [001]-X ([001]-[111]) boundary under the action of {111} 〈 110 〉 slip system. When the 
dislocation reactions between γ -channel dislocation families with different Burgers vectors cause the {111} 〈 112 〉 slip system to 
dominate deformation, the specimen will rotate under the action of {111} 〈 112 〉 slip system. It will also first rotate to the [001]-Y 
boundary, and then rotate towards the [001] direction. In these models, Model A cannot reflect the orientation rotation path and is 
also unable to explain the impact of deviation direction on alloy properties at the same deviation angle. Model B only considers the 
effect of {111} 〈 112 〉 slip system. Model C comprehensively considers the influence of {111} 〈 110 〉 and {111} 〈 112 〉 slip systems. 
Therefore, Model C seems more reasonable, but further proof is needed.

5. Discussions 
5.1. Orientation rotation path during creep at intermediate temperatures 
The orientation changes in Fig. 3 a indicates that the crystal orientation rotates from the [001]-[101] boundary towards the [001]- 
[111] boundary at the primary stage (from I to III), which is consistent with the orientation rotation law dominated by the {111} 〈 110 〉
slip system shown in Fig. 2 a. Although, some stacking faults have already appeared in the microstructure of the specimens at the 
primary stage, as shown in Fig. 3 c. However, the number of stacking faults is relatively less and has not reached the threshold that can 
cause orientation rotation. Recently, Qu et al. (2024b) also found that although there were some stacking faults, the dominant 
mechanism still was the movement of matrix dislocations during tensile deformation at intermediate temperatures. The result of Heep 
et al. (2022) also indicated that there were numerous matrix dislocations the early stage of creep. Therefore, the orientation rotation at 
the primary stage is dominated by {111} 〈 110 〉 slip system. 
At the secondary stage (from III to V) shown in Fig. 3 a, the crystal orientation rotates from the [001]-[111] boundary towards the 
[001]-[101] boundary. At the tertiary stage, the crystal orientation rotates towards the [001] pole along the [001]-[101] boundary 
until the specimen fractures. The rotation path of the specimen at the secondary and tertiary stages is consistent with the orientation 
rotation law dominated by the {111} 〈 112 〉 slip system shown in Fig. 2 b, and the orientation rotation at the secondary stage is 
dominated by {111} 〈 112 〉 single slip, while it is dominated by {111} 〈 112 〉 double slip at the tertiary stage. Although there are 
stacking faults along two orientations at the secondary stage, the density of stacking faults in the two directions is different. There are 
more stacking faults formed by the movement of the first activated {111} 〈 112 〉 slip system, which is also the dominant slip system 
during the secondary stage. Then, there are fewer stacking faults formed by the movement of the second activated {111} 〈 112 〉 slip

〈 112 〉 slip system. 
At the primary stage, the sample near the [001]-X boundary will rotate further away from the [001] pole and [001]-Y boundary 
under the action of {111} 〈 110 〉 slip system. Then, the sample need large rotation to reach the [001]-Y boundary at the secondary stage

lead to premature fracture ( Gupta et al., 2023 ; Qu et al., 2023a ). Therefore, the crystal orientation during the tertiary stage cannot 
rotate to the [001] pole. As shown in Fig. 5 , the closer the initial orientation is to the [001]-X boundary, the further away the 
orientation after the creep rupture is from the [001] pole.

6. Conclusion 
In summary, the orientation sensitivity of creep life in near [001] oriented Ni-based single crystal superalloys at intermediate 
temperatures has been unveiled in this work. The key findings can be summarized as follows: 
(1) During the creep process at intermediate temperature, the orientation rotation path indicates the primary stage is dominated by 
{111} 〈 110 〉 slip system, while both the secondary and the tertiary stages are dominated by {111} 〈 112 〉 slip system. In addition, 
the secondary stage is dominated by {111} 〈 112 〉 single slip, while the tertiary stage is dominated by {111} 〈 112 〉 conjugate 
double slip. 
(2) The creep life exhibits obvious orientation sensitivity in the vicinity of [001] orientation, and the creep life of samples near the 
[001]-[101] boundary was much longer than those close to the [001]-[111] boundary. For samples near the [001]-[101] 
boundary, the proportion of primary creep life to the total creep life is relatively small and it also has a low primary creep 
strain and strain proportion. In contrast, the sample near the [001]-[111] boundary exhibits a large primary creep strain and 
strain proportion. The percentage of creep life at the primary stage is also much larger than that of samples near the [001]-[101] 
boundary. 
(3) The reason for the orientation sensitivity of creep life near the [001] orientation is as follows: firstly, at the primary stage, the 
sample near the [001]-[111] boundary require a large deformation to generate sufficient matrix dislocations. Only a large 
number of matrix dislocations can react with each other to reach the threshold that can cause orientation rotation in the {111} 
〈 112 〉 single slip system, and then the secondary stage can begin. At the same time, the samples near the [001]-[111] boundary 
are subjected to {111} 〈 110 〉 double slip resulting in significant deformation and orientation rotation further away from the 
[001] pole and [001]-[101] boundary. Secondly, a large creep strain and rotation at the primary stage will require a larger 
rotation angle for the sample to reach the [001]-[101] boundary at the secondary stage, thereby initiating the double slip of the 
{111} 〈 112 〉 slip system. Finally, excessive rotation can lead to greater uneven deformation and exacerbate local stress con -
centration in the specimen, ultimately resulting in a poorer creep life of the specimen. 
(4) The deviation angle of 15
◦
from precise [001] is not an appropriate indicator to determine whether the blade is qualified. The 
deviation angle between the blade stacking axis (Z) and the [001] orientation not only needs to be controlled, but also the 
deviation direction needs to be controlled. A deviation angle of 15
◦
from [001] in [101] directions can be tolerated, while a 
deviation towards the [111] direction cannot be accepted.
