1. Introduction 
As a potential high-temperature structural alloy, Sb 
has been of interest because of its high melting temper- 
ature and relatively 101~ density. Unfortunately, Xb- 
base alloys exhibit very poor oxidation resistance and 
only moderate creep strength. Most of the existing 
commercial alloys rely primarily on creep strengthening 
due to solid solution hardening, particularly on those 
solutes with high melting points [l-4]. There are data 
which also suggest that both oxides and carbides may 
be effective in creep strengthening, provided that a fine 
dispersion may be introduced and that rapid particle 
coarsening could be avoided [l-G]. In order to achieve 
a finely spaced particle dispersion, precipitation harden- 
ing heat treatments, internal oxidation, and rapid so- 
lidification techniques have been used. These 
creep-strengthening approaches have met with limited 
success, principally due to softening caused by particle

4. Summary 
A Nb-10vol.%YzO? alloy has been processed using 
MA, and HIP consolidation at 1600°C. The resulting 
partially recrystallized microstructure consists of a 
necklace of fine recrystallized grains ( c 0.5-j urn) sur- 
rounding unrecrystallized grains which retain elongated 
heavily cold-worked subgrains. About 85% of the struc- 
ture is unrecrystallized, remaining in the form of dis- 
continuous islands. Such necklace microstructures have 
been previously observed in M-base alloys and require 
dispersed phase particles to retard recrystallization. 
When compared with either Nb or a powder-blended 
Kb-lOY,O, alloy, the MA Kb-10Y20j exhibits much 
superior yield strength and creep resistance at high 
temperatures (1050- 1350°C). In fact, on a density-com- 
pensated basis, the iMA Kb-10Y20j has elevated tem- 
perature-specific strengths which exceed those of the 
high-strength commercial Nb alloy, WC 3009. The 
primary source of strengthening in the MA Nb- 
10YzOj appears to be ‘indirect’ dispersion strengthen- 
ing in which the dispersoids stabilize a heavily 
dislocated ‘cold-worlced’ subgrain structure.
