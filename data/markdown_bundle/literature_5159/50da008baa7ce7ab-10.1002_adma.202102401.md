Refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) show promising applications at high 
temperatures. However, achieving high strengths at elevated temperatures 
above 1173K is still challenging due to heat softening. Using intrinsic material 
characteristics as the alloy-design principles, a single-phase body-centered-

1000 MPa at 1273 K) is designed, superior to other reported RHEAs as well 
as conventional superalloys. The origin of the high-temperature strength is 
revealed by in situ neutron scattering, transmission-electron microscopy, and 
first-principles calculations. The CrMoNbV’s elevated-temperature strength

mismatches, the insensitive temperature dependence of elastic constants, 
and the dominance of non-screw character dislocations caused by the strong 
solute pinning, which makes the solid-solution strengthening pronounced. 
The alloy-design principles and the insights in this study pave the way to 
design RHEAs with outstanding high-temperature strength.

mental friendliness in the aerospace industries requires materials 
with exceptional high-temperature strengths. The conventional 
high-temperature superalloys, such as Ni-based superalloys, could 
not meet the strength requirement at high operation temperatures 
toward and near their melting points. Therefore, it is imperative 
to design next-generation high-temperature or refractory alloys for

This RHEA has a high 0.2% yield strength of 1518  ± 38 MPa 
at room temperature (RT) (Figure  2a and Table S2, Supporting 
Information). Serrated flows were observed when the tempera-
ture exceeded 773 K, which could be due to the localized hetero-
geneous deformation, i.e., the interaction between dislocations 
and the local stress field caused by the rugged local atomic envi-
ronment.
[22] It is noted that the serrated flows become less pro-
nounced at 1273 K (≈0.5T m) compared to those at 773–1173 K, 
indicating that a diffusion-mediated deformation mechanism 
could become important, which could lead to the reduced duc-
tility and work hardening rate at 1273 K. Although the yield 
strength of this alloy gradually decreases with increasing tem-
perature, it retains as high as 1062  ± 21  MPa at 1273 K. Even 
after long-time annealing at 1273 K, the yield strength at 1273 K 
is still beyond 1000 MPa (Figure S1, Supporting Information). 
This high yield strength at 1273 K is superior to other reported 
single-phase BCC refractory HEAs as well as many conventional 
superalloys. The temperature dependence of the yield strength 
of this alloy and other reported BCC refractory HEAs and tradi-
tional alloys

a crystal structure with a higher melting point (T

The strong solute-pinning effect exerted on dislocations results 
from the distorted local lattice/local compositional fluctuations in 
this RHEA. The large values of both the atomic-size and elastic-
modulus mismatches are due to the addition of the Cr element 
because of its relatively small atomic radius (r = 1.28 Å) and large 
shear modulus (G = 115 GPa), compared to the other constituent 
elements (Mo: r = 1.4 Å, G = 20 GPa; Nb: r = 1.47 Å, G = 38 GPa; 
V: r = 1.35 Å, G = 47 GPa). Moreover, the addition of Mo, with the 
smallest shear modulus value of 20 GPa, is another factor contrib-
uting to the large modulus mismatch. Furthermore, the use of 
refractory elements, such as Nb and V, which have a weak tem-
perature dependence of elastic constants, offers opportunities to 
achieve high strengths at elevated temperatures. It can be seen that 
RHEAs containing Nb and V, such as MoNbTaVW and NbTaTiV, 
possess great high-temperature strengths.
[6,50] Therefore, elements 
that have large differences in lattice constants and shear moduli 
and stable high-temperature elastic constants should be considered 
when designing RHEAs. However, one should be cautious when 
introducing a large atomic-size mismatch because the phase sta-
bility of an alloy could be affected by a too large atomic-size mis-
match, which in turn deteriorates the materials’ high-temperature 
mechanical properties. For example, most previously reported Zr/
Hf-containing refractory HEAs that have very large local lattice dis-
tortions are not thermodynamically stable and decompose easily 
at intermediate temperatures, leading to obvious strength sof -
tening.
[9–12] Thus, predicting the phase stability in advance by first-
principles calculations and CALPHAD (CALculation of PHAse 
Diagrams) will help accelerate the design of advanced RHEAs. Fur-
thermore, concerning high-temperature applications of RHEAs, 
oxidation resistance is another critical property to be considered. 
Therefore, when designing advanced RHEAs, incorporating suit-
able contents of elements that can form stable and thick oxide layer 
at high temperatures, such as Al and Cr, can be considered.
3. Conclusions
In summary, three alloy properties leading to a superior high-
temperature strength, including the large atomic-size and 
elastic-modulus mismatches, the insensitive temperature-
dependence of elastic properties, and the dominance of non-
screw character dislocations, have been successfully achieved in 
the design of a CrMoNbV RHEA. RHEA-design principles that 
are centered around the incorporation of the aforementioned 
three alloy properties can be extended to other alloy systems 
that aim at achieving an exceptional high-temperature strength.
