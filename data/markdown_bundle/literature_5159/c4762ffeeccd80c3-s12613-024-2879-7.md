Abstract: To increase the processability and plasticity of the selective laser melting (SLM) fabricated Al–Mn–Mg–Er–Zr alloys, a novel
TiB2-modified Al–Mn–Mg–Er–Zr alloy with a mixture of Al–Mn–Mg–Er–Zr and nano-TiB2 powders was fabricated by SLM. The pro-
cessability, microstructure, and mechanical properties of the alloy were systematically investigated by density measurement, microstruc-
ture characterization, and mechanical properties testing. The alloys fabricated at 250 W displayed higher relative densities due to a uni-
formly smooth top surface and appropriate laser energy input. The maximum relative density value of the alloy reached (99.7 ± 0.1)%,
demonstrating good processability. The alloy exhibited a duplex grain microstructure consisting of columnar regions primarily and
equiaxed regions with TiB2, Al6
Mn, and Al3Er phases distributed along the grain boundaries. After directly aging treatment at a high tem-
perature of 400°C, the strength of the SLM-fabricated TiB2/Al–Mn–Mg–Er–Zr alloy increased due to the precipitation of the secondary
Al6Mn phases. The maximum yield strength and ultimate tensile strength of the aging alloy were measured to be (374 ± 1) and (512 ± 13)
MPa, respectively. The SLM-fabricated TiB2/Al–Mn–
Mg–Er–Zr alloy demonstrates exceptional strength and thermal stability due to the
synergistic effects of the inhibition of grain growth, the incorporation of TiB2 nanoparticles, and the precipitation of secondary Al6Mn
nanoparticles.
Keywords: selective laser melting; aluminum alloy; processability; mechanical properties; thermal stability
 
 
1. Introduction
Selective laser melting (SLM) is one of the most widely
used additive manufacturing (AM) technologies, extensively
researched in both academic and industrial because it offers
more design freedom, requires less machining, and allows for
quicker prototyping than traditional process technologies
[1–2]. Meanwhile, a high cooling rate of 103–107 K/s can be
achieved during the SLM, providing a chance to obtain high-
strength materials [3–4]. Aluminum alloys are widely used in
the manufacture of important parts in the aerospace field.
However, the application of SLM-
fabricated aluminum al-
loys has primarily been limited to traditional near-eutectic
Al–Si-based alloys [5]. Regrettably, the strength of these al-
loys is noticeably lower compared to conventional high-
strength aluminum alloys, thereby restricting their potential
applications.
In recent decades, the SLM-fabricated Sc- and Zr-
modi-
fied Al–Mg- and Al–Mn-based aluminum alloys have been
studied systematically [6–10]. These alloys demonstrate ex-
ceptional strength, with both the ultimate tensile strength
(UTS) and yield strength (YS) exceeding 500 MPa due to
fine grain strengthening induced by primary Al3(Sc,Zr)
particles and precipitation strengthening resulting from the
high-density secondary Al3Sc nanoparticles after direct heat
treatment at 300–
350°C. However, the disadvantage of high
raw material prices in those high-Sc-content (>0.5wt%) al-
loys is difficult to eliminate. To reduce the raw material cost
of the high-Sc-content Al–Mg-based alloys, Geng et al. [11]
developed a novel Al–Mg–Sc–Zr aluminum alloy with high
Mg-content (11.3wt%) and low Sc and Zr elements. The res-
ults revealed that the rapid solidification inherent in the SLM
technology led to a large amount of Mg dissolved in the α-Al
matrix, thereby substantially enhancing the strength of the al-
loy. However, the high content of Mg could lead to excess-
ive vaporization of Mg elements during the SLM process.

This, in turn, negatively affected the alloy’s processability
and mechanical property stability. Recently, Croteau et al.
[12] fabricated a new Sc-free Al–Mg–Zr alloy using SLM.
The results indicated that the primary Al3Zr particles had a
significant grain refining effect during the SLM process, res-
ulting in improved processability and mechanical properties
of the alloy. Moreover, the alloy demonstrated remarkable
mechanical stability even after annealing at 400°C, which
was mainly attributed to the precipitates of highly thermally
stable Al3Zr nanoparticles. The alloy exhibited the maximum
YS of about (365 ± 11) MPa and the maximum UTS of about
(389 ± 4) MPa. Zhou et al. [13] performed a study on the
processability, microstructure, and mechanical properties of
the AA5083+Zr alloy. The primary Al3Zr as a grain refiner
could effectively refine the grain size of alloys, thereby sup-
pressing the generation of solidification cracks in the alloy
during the SLM process. After direct aging treatment at
400°C for 2 h, an increase of 100 MPa in the YS of the alloy
was achieved due to the precipitation of secondary Al3Zr
particles. However, it should be noted that the strength of the
SLM-fabricated Al–Mg–Zr alloys was low. Specifically, the
UTS of those alloys was measured to be below 400 MPa.
Most recently, Geng et al. [14–15] reported a novel SLM-
fabricated Sc-free Al–Mn–Mg–Er–Zr alloy. This new alloy
exhibited high strength, including UTS and YS values of
(502 ± 3) and (327 ± 5) MPa, respectively. Unfortunately, the
SLM process window of the alloy was narrow, and hot
cracks were found in most of the samples. The processability
of the SLM-fabricated Al–Mn–Mg–Er–Zr alloy could be sig-
nificantly improved through Si alloying, but the obtained al-
loy had a lower plasticity [16].
Ceramic nanoparticles, such as TiB2, were often incorpor-
ated to enhance the SLM printability, strength, and plasticity
of 7xxx and 2xxx series aluminum alloys [17–18]. The
ceramic nanoparticles could promote the formation of a large
amount of equiaxed grains, thereby significantly mitigating
hot cracking during solidification. Typically, Sun et al. [18]
systematically investigated the influence of in-situ nano-TiB2
particles on the processability and mechanical properties of
the 2024Al alloy. The results indicated that the addition of
TiB2 successfully prevented the occurrence of hot cracks in
the alloys during the SLM process. The volume fraction of
pores decreased first and then increased with the TiB2 con-
tent increase. The sample with TiB2 content of 2wt% demon-
strated the lowest porosity among the tested samples. With
the increase of TiB2 particles, UTS increased continuously
and reached its maximum value at 8wt% TiB2. Wang et al.
[19] demonstrated that the addition of 3wt% TiB2 in the
SLM-fabricated 2024 aluminum alloy effectively improved
its strength and plasticity, primarily attributed to the grain re-
finement effect of TiB2 nanoparticles. In the present work, a
novel TiB2-modified Al–Mn–Mg–Er–Zr composite was pro-
duced by SLM. The results showed that the incorporation of
TiB2 effectively improved the SLM processability, mechan-
ical properties, and thermal stability of the alloy. 
2. Experimental 
2.1. Alloy powder
The powder with a composition of 6.01wt% Mn, 2.74wt%
Mg, 0.86wt% Er, 0.52wt% Zr, and Al balance, was prepared
by a nitrogen-protected atomization system. The size of the
powder ranged from 15 to 53 μm with average size of 35.6
μm. Nano-TiB2/Al–Mn–Mg–Er–Zr composite (2.00wt%
TiB2) powder was blended by mechanical mixing for 12 h at
a speed of 200 r/min. The mean size of TiB2 particles was
50 nm. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images of
TiB2/Al–Mn–Mg–Er–Zr mixed powder are shown in Fig. 1.
The composite powders appeared to be nearly spherical, as
shown in Fig. 1(a). Additionally, TiB2 particles were uni-
formly attached to the surface of the Al–Mn–Mg–Er–Zr
powder (Fig. 1(b)–(g)).

2.2. SLM process
The bulk specimens with a dimension of 55 mm (length) ×
15 mm (width) × 16 mm (height) were fabricated using EP-
M250 equipment with a spot size of 70 μm. During the SLM
process, laser power of 250 and 350 W, spot size of
70 μm, layer thickness of 30 μm, hatch spacing of 70 μm,
scanning speed of 800–1200 mm/s, and rotation angle of 67°
were used. Before the SLM process, the platform was pre-
heated to a temperature of 100°C. After the printing process,
the as-built specimens were subjected to aging treatment at
varying temperatures for a duration of 2 h. Additionally,

2.3. Characterization
The mass density of the specimens was determined
through the Archimedes method. Microstructure analysis of
the SLM-fabricated specimens was conducted via optical mi-
croscopy (OM), SEM (SM-6480), and transmission electron
microscopy (TEM, JEM-2010F, 200 kV), respectively. Be-
fore observing the microstructure using OM and SEM, the
samples were subjected to chemical etching with Keller’s re-
agent (1 mL HF, 1.5 mL HCl, 2.5 mL HNO3, and 95 mL dis-
tilled water) for approximately 15 s. TEM samples were pre-
pared using twin-jet electropolishing and subsequent ion-
beam thinning. X-ray computed tomography (X-ray CT, GE
v/tome/x m) was performed to analyze the morphology of
pores in the specimen. A LEXTOLS4000 laser scanning con-
focal microscopy (LSCM) was applied to measure the rough-
ness (Ra) of the top surface of the specimens. The specimen
underwent component analysis through the utilization of en-
ergy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX). The crystallo-
graphic orientation and grain size of the specimens were ex-
amined by electron backscattered diffraction (EBSD, OX-
FORD Nordlys Nano) using a step size of 0.3 µm. A Bruker
D8 Focus X-ray diffractometer (Cu Kα, 0.15406 nm) was
utilized to ascertain the phase identification of the specimens.
A Vickers hardness tester (HRS-150) was utilized to meas-
ure the microhardness of the samples. The loading and dwell
time were set to 2.94 N and 15 s, respectively. Tensile prop-
erties of the alloy were evaluated using a UTM5105 univer-
sal testing machine at a constant strain rate of 1 mm/min fol-
lowing the ASTM E8-04 standard. The microstructure of the
specimens and their mechanical properties were tested on the
specimens fabricated at a laser scanning speed of 900 mm/s
and a laser power of 250 W. 
3. Results 
3.1. Surface quality and processability
Fig. 2 shows the photographs of the TiB2/Al–Mn–Mg–
Er–Zr samples fabricated at various process parameters.
Most of the samples displayed a uniformly smooth top sur-
face except for the samples obtained under a laser power of
350 W with scanning speeds of 1100 and 1200 mm/s. This
observation was further confirmed by the SEM and LSCM
top-surface images (Fig. 3). As shown in Fig. 3, for the
samples fabricated at the laser power of 250 W with scan-
ning speed of 800–1200 mm/s and laser power of 350 W with
scanning speeds of 800–1000 mm/s, the smooth surface
could be observed. Only a few spherical spatters with dia-
meters measuring tens of micrometers were attached to the
surface (Fig. 3(a) and (c)). However, extremely uneven sur-
faces with larger balling and pits were observed for the
samples fabricated at a laser power of 350 W with scanning
speeds of 1100 and 1200 mm/s. The LSCM analysis indic-
ated that the Ra of the samples obtained at the laser power of
250 W initially increases and then decreases with the in-
crease of laser scanning speed. The minimum Ra value of the
samples was 22.758 μm at the speed of 800 mm/s, while the
maximum Ra value was 33.707 μm at the speed of 1000
mm/s (Fig. 3(b)). For the samples fabricated at laser power of
350 W, the Ra values showed a notable increase with the in-
creasing of laser scanning speed. At a laser scanning speed of
800 mm/s, the Ra exhibited a mere 19.144 μm. However,
upon raising the speed to 1100 and 1200 mm/s, the Ra values
rapidly increased to 44.671 and 131.26 μm (Fig. 3(d)), re-
spectively.
Fig. 4 shows the OM images of the TiB2/Al–Mn–Mg–
Er–Zr samples fabricated at various process parameters. The
samples displayed a characteristic stacking morphology,
which revealed the shape of the molten pool in the direction
of the building [20]. At the laser power of 250 W, only a few
metallurgical pores were observed in the samples (Fig. 4
(a)–(c)) [21]. The samples fabricated at the laser power of
350 W and scanning speeds ranging from 800–1000 mm/s
exhibited large amounts of metallurgical pores with large
sizes (Fig. 4(d)–(e)). For the samples fabricated at the laser
power of 350 W and scanning speeds of 1100 and 1200
mm/s, the large keyholes were observed (Fig. 4(f)).

4. Discussion 
4.1. Surface quality, hole, and processability
After TiB2-modified, the balling phenomenon and Ra
value in most alloys were significantly reduced in most of the
alloys in comparison to the SLM-fabricated Al–Mn–Mg–
Er–Zr alloy [16]. Numerous studies have demonstrated that
the incorporation of TiB2 into SLM-fabricated alloys can ef-
fectively mitigate liquid droplet coalescence, subsequently
suppressing the formation of large balling spatters [24]. At
the laser power of 250 W, the molten pool remains stable at
different laser scanning speeds. The upper surface of the
SLM-fabricated samples is flat, and the balling phenomenon
is weak (Fig. 3(a)). As a result, the Ra values of the samples
fluctuate between 22.758 and 33.707 μm (Fig. 3(b)). The ab-
sence of excessive balling in the alloy can effectively reduce
occurrence of unpredictable defects, such as keyholes, and
prevent the formation of cracks caused by stress concentra-
tion during the SLM process, which will enhance the pro-
cessability and consistency of the mechanical properties of
SLM parts [25]. Besides, the appropriate combination of
laser power and laser scanning speeds can reduce the size and
number of metallurgical pores in the alloys, thereby increas-
ing the relative density and processability of the alloy. The

4.2. Microstructure
According to EBSD (Fig. 6), a bimodal grain configura-
tion was observed in the SLM-fabricated TiB2/Al–Mn–Mg–
Er–Zr alloy. During the solidification process of the molten
pool, the presence of TiB2 and the formation of primary Al3Er
support the nucleation of α-Al, resulting in the formation of
fine equiaxed grains at the boundary of the molten. However,
as solidification progresses, the cooling rate of the melt starts
to increase, which hinders the formation of Al3Er. Con-
sequently, the number of nucleation particles present in the
melt decreases, resulting in an enlargement of the α-Al
grains. Additionally, the steep thermal gradient in the molten
pool induces the α-Al to grow in the opposite direction to the
heat flux and forms columnar grains with a <100> texture
[8,16]. Compared to the SLM-fabricated Al–Mn–Mg–Er–Zr,
the addition of TiB2 nanoparticles leads to a reduction in the
grain size (Fig. 6(a) and (b)). The reduction in grain size can
be attributed to two factors. Firstly, TiB2 nanoparticles act as
nucleation sites for the solidification of α-Al, leading to smal-
ler grains [28]. Secondly, the aggregation of nano-TiB2
particles at grain boundaries impedes grain growth through a
pinning effect [29]. As a result, the SLM-fabricated TiB2/Al–
Mn–Mg–Er–Zr sample showed a finer grain size. The se-
gregation of Zr at the TiB2/α-Al contact was attributed to the
low interfacial energy that is controlled by Gibbs’ adsorption
rule. It is widely believed that solute adsorption at the inter-
face between the liquid and the particle can reduce the inter-
facial energy, thereby decreasing the probability of particle
agglomeration [30]. In TiB2-modified aluminum alloys with
Sc- and Zr- free, the primary cause of grain refinement in
TiB2 particles is the formation of a monolayer of Al3Ti two-
dimensional compound on the TiB2 surface. This reduces lat-
tice misfit from 4.2% (with TiB2) to 0.09% (with Al3Ti)
[31–32]. However, the Zr adsorption at the TiB2/Al interface
in the current alloy reintroduces the lattice misfit to –4.2% as
Ti2Zr forms between TiB2 and Al. This partially hinders the
nucleation of α-Al. As a result, the grain refinement effect of
TiB2 in the SLM-fabricated Al–Mn–Mg–Er–Zr alloy was
weak, and the number of equiaxed grains was much less than
that of the other SLM-fabricated TiB2-modified aluminum
alloys [33].
After 400°C aging treatments, the excessive dissolution of
Mn elements in the α-Al matrix results in the precipitation of
Al6Mn nanoparticles and the coarsening of primary Al6Mn
(Fig. 11). Even after subjecting the alloy to aging treatment at
400°C for 18 h, the average grain size remained virtually un-
changed (Fig. 6). The high thermal stability of the grain
structure can be attributed to the presence of thermally stable
Al6Mn, Al3Er, and TiB2 particles. These particles can serve as
strong grain growth inhibitors to avoid recrystallization
and/or grain growth of the alloy [34].

due to their different coefficients of CTE, resulting in the
formation of high-density thermal mismatch dislocations at
the Al/TiB2 interface [36–37]. The different elastic modulus
between Al and TiB2 particles can generate strain gradients at
the Al/TiB2 interface and induce numerous geometrically ne-
cessary dislocations, which is beneficial for increasing the
strength of the alloy. Moreover, the presence of TiB2 particles
has a grain refinement effect due to its coherence with Al.
The high-density grain boundaries effectively hinder the
movement of dislocations, thereby further increasing the
strength of the alloy [38]. Grain refinement also increases the
overall grain boundary and interfacial area in the alloy, which
allows for more energy storage during plastic deformation.
The activity of grain boundaries can dissipate deformation
energy, resulting in improved plasticity of the material. Fol-
lowing 400°C aging treatments, the precipitation of second-
ary Al6Mn nanoparticles further intensifies the precipitation-
strengthening effect within the alloy. However, with an in-
crease in aging time, the coarsening of these nanoparticles
causes a decline in the alloy’s strength. In the current alloy,
the presence of TiB2 particles distributed within the grains
and along the grain boundaries exhibits a high resistance to
coarsening. The coexistence of these heat-resistant TiB2
particles effectively limits grain growth during the SLM pro-
cess and subsequent heat treatment through the Zener pin-
ning effect [39]. Furthermore, the presence of TiB2 also acts
as a barrier to the diffusion of Mn elements, thereby increas-
ing the precipitation temperature of the Al6Mn phase. This
leads to the enhanced strength of the alloy after undergoing
high-temperature aging treatment. The combined effect of
these factors contributes to the high thermal stability of the
SLM-fabricated Al–Mn–Mg–Er–Zr alloy. 
5. Conclusions
In this study, TiB2-modified Al–Mn–Mg–Er–Zr alloy was
produced via SLM. The processability, microstructure, and
mechanical properties of the alloy were studied. The main
conclusions can be summarized as follows.
(1) A nearly fully dense specimen with the maximum rel-
ative density of (99.7 ± 0.1)% was achieved at 250 W-800
mm/s process. All the samples were found to be free of
cracks, which can be attributed to the weak balling phe-
nomenon occurring on the surface of the alloy as well as the
grain refinement effect induced by the incorporation of TiB2.
(2) The SLM-fabricated TiB2/Al–Mn–Mg–Er–Zr alloy
demonstrated a bimodal microstructure. The fine equiaxed
grains were observed along the boundaries of the molten
pool, while the coarse columnar grains were present within
the molten pool. The mean grain size was about 4.15 µm.
The precipitates in the alloy were mainly Al6Mn, Al3Er, TiB2,
and possibly Mg-rich phases, which were distributed at the
boundaries of both equiaxed and columnar grains.
(3) After aging treatment, the primary Al6Mn phase in the
sample became coarser, while the secondary needle-like
Al6Mn phase precipitated from the α-Al matrix. Besides,
even after subjecting the sample to a temperature of 400°C
for a duration of 18 h, there was no observable increase in the
grain size of the alloy due to the Zener pinning effect of TiB2
particles.
(4) By leveraging the combined benefits of the inhibition
of grain growth, the presence of TiB2 nanoparticles, and the
precipitation of secondary Al6Mn nanoparticles, the SLM-
fabricated TiB2/Al–Mn–Mg–Er–Zr alloy exhibits impressive
strength and thermal stability. After 400°C-4 h aging treat-
ment, the alloy demonstrates exceptional strength properties,
including UTS of (512 ± 6), YS of (374 ± 1) MPa, and elong-
ation of (6.2 ± 0.5)%. The alloy’s strength surpassed that of
Al–Mn–Mg–Er–Zr and other Sc-free Al–Mg-based alloys
fabricated via SLM. This study provides a new way to manu-
facture high-performance aluminum alloys using low-cost
raw materials.
