Abstract
The fretting fatigue crack formation and propagation behaviors of Ni ‐based
single‐crystal (NBSX) superalloys are investigated in this paper. Subsurface
crack formation process is revealed by in situ fretting fatigue experiment. The
crack is observed to form on subsurface area, then propagates to the contact
surface. Inclusions in materials are found to have obvious effects on crack
propagation, and slip lines are closely related to the crack propagation direc-
tion. Crystal plastic finite element method (CPFEM) simulation is used to sim-
ulate crack formation position. The accumulative plastic strain peaks at the
edge of contact zone and the subsurface area. The results show that the CPFEM
simulation and in situ observation achieve good agreements.

1 | INTRODUCTION
Fretting refers to the microscale relative displacement in
a pair of contact surfaces subjected to an oscillatory load-
ing condition. 1-3 Severe stress concentration exists at the
edge of contact zone under fretting condition, where the
fatigue crack usually initiates.
4,5 The whole process involv-
ing crack formation, propagation, and final fracture is
defined as the fretting fatigue. 6
Fretting fatigue is one of the most important factors
contributing to the structure failure. The dovetail joint
between the turbine blade and disk employed in aero ‐
engine or gas turbine is a typical fretting condition,
and the fracture occurring at the turbine blade root will
cause aero ‐engine failure and aviation accident.
7-10
According to the statistics from US Air Force (USAF),
fretting fatigue occupies 1/6 of fatigue ‐induced aero ‐
engine failure, 7,11 thus the fretting fatigue properties of
turbine blade attract more and more interests from
researchers. Ni ‐based single ‐crystal (NBSX) superalloys
are widely used in manufacture of advanced turbine
blade for its excellent mechanical properties at high
temperature. This type of superalloy has outstanding
antifatigue properties due to removing the grain bound-
aries, a potential crack source from the material. There-
fore, the fretting fatigue properties of NBSX has become
a key problem noticed by various research institutions
currently.
12,13
Some researchers have paid attention to the crack
nucleation and propagation, 14 including the surface and
subsurface crack nucleation. 15 The surface crack that ini-
tiates on the contact surface is the most common case 9
(Figure 1A). Compared with plain fatigue, fretting fatigue
is easier to induce subsurface crack, which should be
attributed to the serious stress concentration in the sub-
surface caused by fretting contact. Generally, subsurface
crack forms below the contact surface, but its propaga-
tion path is in the observational plane (Figure 1B).
There are a very limited experimental observations and
researches on subsurface crack,
16 while most of them are
postexperimental observation, theoretical solutions, 17,18
and finite element simulation results. 19,20 In situ obser-
vation of subsurface formation and propagation during
fretting fatigue process is still in its infancy.
In situ scanning electron microscope (SEM) technique
gets more and more attention since it can reveal the
microstructure evolution process directly. However, the
design of in situ fretting equipment is difficult because
two loads of different directions need to be applied at
the same time. Nishida et al first developed the in situ
SEM observation device to reveal fretting fatigue pro-
cess.
21 Otsuka et al improved the macroscopic bridge ‐type
fretting equipment for the in situ SEM observation,
investigated the wear property of Ti ‐based alloy contain-
ing Hap coating, and captured the wear pattern in real
time.22 However, the material used in their research
was polycrystalline alloy, and the slip lines and crack for-
mation were not observed. Han et al conducted the in situ
SEM observation on the NBSX experiencing fretting test
and analyzed the influence of secondary orientation on
the slip line distribution and crack formation direction.
23
However, the in situ SEM observation on the subsurface
crack of NBSX is still rarely reported.
In the finite element simulation aspect, it should be
noticed that the contact zone is in the state of high stress
concentration, high stress gradient, and multiaxial stress,
which brings challenges to the finite element simulation.
Several researchers have done some explorations about
this.
24-28 Arnab et al used linear elastic fracture mechan-
ics (LEFM) based finite element simulation to predict
the propagation behavior of subsurface crack.
20,29 How-
ever, there has been little work done on the simulation
of fretting fatigue subsurface crack formation of NBSX.
Besides, many studies have pointed out that there are
obvious plastic deformations in the fretting contact zone,
and plasticity plays an important role in crack forma-
tion.
30,31 Recently, the crystal plasticity theory has been
developed, and the crystal plastic finite element method
(CPFEM) combined with advanced computational tech-
nology has been applied to the study of fretting. CPFEM

3.2 | Crack propagation period I: Main
crack retardation caused by the inclusion
In this section crack propagation behavior at the early
stage will be introduced. Figure 8A shows a carbide inclu-
sion at the front of the main crack, at which point the
crack growth rate begins to decrease significantly. As
shown in Figure 8B, the secondary crack is formed in
the carbide inclusion due to the local stress concentration
at the inclusion. It is noted that the direction of the sec-
ondary crack is along the elongated carbide inclusion,
rather than parallel to the direction of the main crack.
After the secondary crack penetrates the entire carbide
inclusion, it propagates forward and the propagation
direction turns to the direction parallel to the main crack.
Finally, the main crack restarts to propagate and
merge with the secondary crack (Figure 8C), and then
the crack tip of the secondary crack becomes the new
tip of main crack.
In summary, the observations in Figure 8 show that
the cracks are retarded at the initial stage of fretting crack
propagation. The inclusion at front of the crack plays a
role as a barrier of slip along the path of main crack. This
results in a decrease in crack growth rate. The high stress
is induced in the inclusion due to the main crack and
then secondary crack forms in the inclusion. When the
secondary crack propagates to a certain extent, the main
crack will continue to propagate forward and merge with
it. Figure 9 shows a graph of crack growth rate based on
image measurements, showing the significant effect of
inclusions on the main crack retardation.

3.3 | Crack propagation period II: Slip
lines along the crack
In this section, we will discuss the second period of crack
propagation, the accelerated propagation period. The
observation shows that the slip at the crack tip and the
dislocation accumulations play an important role in crack
propagation.
From Figure 11A, we can see that the crack as a
whole propagates along the 45° zigzag direction, and a
large amount of slip lines appear at the front of the
crack. For NBSX, the slip line is caused by the plastic
deformation. The internal dislocations slide along the
crystallographic slip planes to leave traces on the surface
of the material. The observation result indicates there
exists significant slip and accumulation of dislocation
at the crack tip. The slip line at the crack tip is also
along 45° inclined direction, and there are slip lines in
both ox and oy directions (Figure 11A). It also shows
that the direction of the slip line is closely related to
the crack propagation direction. Specifically, before the
crack reaches the position A in Figure 11A, the direc-

there are only a few isolated slip lines in the oy direc-

along the ox direction before the crack reaches the posi-
tion A, which is in agree with the general direction of
slip line. Then, after the crack reaches the position A,
the slip line in the oy direction significantly increases
(Figure 11A), which is closely related to the transition

are investigated by in situ observation and CPFEM simu-

process that the crack forms at the subsurface and then
propagate to the contact surface. In situ observation also
shows that in the initial stage of crack formation and
propagation, the inclusions have an important influence
on crack behaviors. When the inclusions are at the front
of the main crack, stress concentration occurs and sec-
ondary cracks form at the inclusions. In this process,
the propagation of main crack is retarded. When the sec-
ondary crack in the inclusion propagates and the stress is
released, the main crack will continue to propagate and
merge with it. In situ observation shows that the crack
forms and propagates along the 45° zigzag direction,
and there is obvious slip and dislocation accumulation
along the crack. It also shows that the slip line is closely
related to the crack propagation direction.
A CPFEM simulation model is developed to study fret-
ting fatigue of NBSX. A crystal plasticity constitutive rela-
tionship considering crystal slip behavior and cyclic
loading effect is employed in the simulation. Accumulative
plastic strain map is obtained, and the peak site matches
the crack formation position of in situ observation.
