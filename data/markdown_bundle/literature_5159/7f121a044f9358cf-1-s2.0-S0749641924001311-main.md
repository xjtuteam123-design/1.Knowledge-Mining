ABSTRACT 
As the operating temperature of jet turbine engines increase, creep becomes the life-limiting 
property for turbine disks and blades. At intermediate temperatures, around 600 – 800 
◦
C, 
microtwinning contributes significantly to creep strain in these alloys. Therefore, understanding 
how microtwins form and grow is critical to improving the creep life of future Ni-base superalloy 
components. In addition, exploring the effect of different alloying elements, such as Nb and Ta, on 
the formation of microtwins is critical for future alloy development. Several mechanisms of 
microtwinning have been proposed among which the Kolbe mechanism, based on thermally 
activated reordering, is believed to be dominant. In this work we employ atomistic simulation to 
investigate the effects of Nb solutes on the Kolbe mechanism. The simulation demonstrates that 
Nb atoms significantly slow down the reordering processes, explaining the experimentally 
observed improvement in the creep resistance. 
1. Introduction 
Ni-based superalloys are widely used as disk materials for jet turbine engines due to their high strength, microstructural stability 
and excellent resistance to creep, fatigue, and oxidation at elevated temperatures ( Reed, 2008 ; Rae et al., 2022 ). Evolving technologies 
require higher operational temperatures for engines in order to lower emissions and improve engine efficiencies. At these tempera -
tures, creep becomes the life-limiting property ( Smith et al., 2016a ; Cormier and Cailletaud, 2010 ). Microtwinning is one of the 
dominant creep deformation mechanisms in Ni-based superalloys at intermediate temperatures (600 
◦
C – 800 
◦
C) ( Smith et al., 2016a ; 
Unocic et al., 2008a ; Kovarik et al., 2009 ). These faults can shear through both the softer γ matrix and strengthening, ordered γ ′ 
precipitates, thus contributing significantly to the overall accumulated creep strain. 
The Kolbe mechanism (schematic diagrams are provided in Refs. ( Smith et al., 2016a ; Kovarik et al., 2009 ; Kolbe, 2001 ; Smith 
et al., 2015 )), based on thermally activated reordering, is currently widely accepted in the scientific community to explain the pro -
cesses of twin nucleation and growth during creep ( Unocic et al., 2008a ; Kovarik et al., 2009 ; Unocic et al., 2008b ). In this mechanism, 
the passage of two Shockley partials on adjacent {111} glide planes in γ ′ creates 2-layer complex stacking fault (CSF), characterized by 
a high number of unfavorable high-energy Al-Al nearest neighbor bonds. This 2-layer CSF can be reordered via thermally activated

strengthening effect of the γ ′ precipitates ( Chen et al., 2022a ). This γ softening effect is believed to promote twin formation and 
decrease the creep strength of the alloy. Conversely, studies exploring Ni-base superalloys with higher concentrations of slow diffusing 
elements (e.g., Ti, Nb, Ta, W, etc.) have found microtwinning formation to be depressed, thus leading to improved creep strength 
( Smith et al., 2021 ; Galindo-Nava et al., 2023 ). Nb and Ta alloying elements are also known η phase formers and experimental analysis

has now been observed in several studies ( Smith et al., 2021 ; Smith et al., 2016b ).

and W increase the energies of the antiphase boundary (APB) and complex stacking fault (CSF) ( Chen et al., 2022b ). These high energy 
defects form in γ ′ phase strengthening precipitates, providing shear resistance, when the precipitates are sheared by various dislo -
cation types. Furthermore, heavy, slow diffusing solutes (e.g., Nb) ( Divinski et al., 1998 ) are supposed to slow down the speed of 
reordering processes involved in the Kolbe mechanism for microtwinning. We also note that alloying additions may significantly affect 
the stacking fault energy (SFE) of the γ phase matrix ( Zhu et al., 2023 ). Lowering the SFE of the γ phase matrix would favor the 
nucleation of twins within the matrix ( Pandey et al., 2023 ; Ding et al., 2023 ). These effects were not considered in the present study 
and can be investigated in the future.

3. Semi-empirical potential 
In the present study, we chose to employ a Finnis-Sinclair (FS) ( Finnis and Sinclair, 1984 ) type potential for the Ni-Al-Nb ternary 
system. This choice was motivated by several reasons. First, the large simulation cell and long simulation time used to model the Kolbe 
mechanism as described in Section 2 require a computationally cheap semi-empirical potential. The cheapest semi-empirical potentials 
which can still reasonably well reproduce metallic alloy properties are the embedded atom method (EAM) ( Daw and Baskes, 1984 ) and 
FS ( Finnis and Sinclair, 1984 ) potentials. Second, an important part of the atomistic model construction for the MD simulation was 
prior MC/MD equilibration. Taking into account that the simulation cell included both γ and γ ′ phases, this equilibration could be done 
only with a semi-empirical potential which properly reproduces the solute partitioning between these phases. Since we have developed 
a Ni-Al FS potential ( https://www.ctcms.nist.gov/potentials/Download/2022 – Mendelev-M-I – Ni-Al/1/Ni-Al.eam.fs ) which well re -
produces the Ni-rich part of the Ni-Al phase diagram in the present study we extended this potential to the Ni-Al-Nb alloys ( https:// 
www.ctcms.nist.gov/potentials/entry/2024 – Mendelev-M-I – Ni-Al-Nb/ ).

In order to get the target ab initio data, we used the Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP) with PBEsol exchange-correlation 
functional ( Kresse and Hafner, 1993 ; Kresse and Hafner, 1994 ). The Monkhorst-Pack Γ -centered k-mesh was used for the Brillouin zone

( Johnson, 1988 ). The plane-wave energy cutoff (ENCUT) was increased to 650 eV. Collinear spin-polarized calculations (ISPIN = 2) 
were performed for magnetic systems, such as nickel. Formation energies were computed relative to the energies of elemental solids in 
their ground states ( Zarkevich, 2006 ). The simulation cells used to determine the Nb segregation energies on SESF and SISF contained 
10 and 8 atomic planes in the direction normal to the stacking fault planes. 
The liquid structures were simulated using AIMD with the time step of 2 fs for ionic motion (IBRION = 0; POTIM = 2.0). The Verlet 
algorithm was used to integrate Newton ’ s equations of motion. The electronic structure was computed in real space (LREAL = .TRUE.). 
The width (SIGMA) of Gaussian smearing (ISMEAR = 0) was increased to 0.172 eV for MD with ionic temperature T = 2000 K. Cubic 
supercells with large numbers of atoms (e.g., 256) were used. During equilibration, each supercell size was adjusted towards a 
reasonable pressure (less than 1 GPa) at the target temperature. Both the liquid pair correlation functions (PCF) and pressure were used 
in the potential development procedure. 
The potential development procedure used in the present study was described in Ref. ( Zhang et al., 2016 ). Here we want to 
emphasize two features of this procedure. The first feature is simultaneously fitting the potential functions for the pure elements (Nb in 
the present case) and the cross-functions. This approach allows us to better reproduce the alloy properties at the expense of the pure 
element properties. This makes sense for the present study where we focus on the effect of Nb on dislocation migration in Ni-Al alloys 
rather than on properties of pure Nb ( Mendelev et al., 2008 ). This is why the agreement with the target values listed in Table 2 and 
liquid PCF (see Fig. 3 ) is somewhat worse than one could expect from the state-of-the-art FS potential for a pure metal (e.g., see 
Ref. ( Mendelev et al., 2008 )). Nevertheless, the agreement is reasonable, and the potential reproduces the main Nb properties critical 
for the current study: the atomic radius is close to that of Al (via the correct reproduction of the bcc lattice parameter) and the high 
melting temperature which is associated with stiffer bonding involving Nb atoms.

4. Binary Ni-Al system 
The authors of the present study recently investigated the Kolbe microtwinning mechanism employing atomistic simulations 
( Borovikov et al., 2023a ). It was demonstrated that atomic diffusion-mediated reordering takes place in the immediate vicinity of the 
cores of twinning partials and proceeds in a highly concerted manner, directly influenced by propagation of the dislocations (see 
Fig. 5 ). The first leading partial entering the precipitate produces a complex stacking fault (CSF) which is energetically unfavorable 
(Al-Al nearest neighbor bonds (NNBs) are created). As discussed in detail in Ref. ( Borovikov et al., 2023a ), the second leading partial, 
gliding on the adjacent {111} plane, generates high energy Al-Al-Al NNBs sequentially, one after another, along the [1 0 -1] direction, 
as it glides inside the strengthening precipitate (from the right to the left in Fig. 5 ). Moreover, the corresponding reshuffling moves 
follow the same sequence, because each consecutive reshuffling move triggers the next one in the sequence: after completion of one 
reshuffling event, the next Al atom which is going to swap with Ni along the row, receives an extra Al bond (on top of the two NNBs 
already produced by the glide of the partials), that provides an extra driving force (assistance) to execute the reshuffling move (see 
Fig. 5 b and Ref. ( Borovikov et al., 2023a )). 
Fig. 6 and Fig. 7 show the deformation progress at different simulation times (0.1 μ s, 0.2 μ s, … , 0.6 μ s) for the upper and lower pairs 
of dislocations, correspondingly ( T = 1250 K). As can be seen, the double CSF produced by the glide of the partials inside the 
strengthening precipitate is completely reordered. Most of the reordering/reshuffling occurs in the immediate vicinity of the partial 
dislocation core, as it slowly propagates inside the precipitate. Fig. 8 shows the deformation progress of the same system for three 
different simulation temperatures at 0.5 μ s. These results allow to calculate the velocity of the partials for different simulation tem -
peratures (see Fig. 9 ) and estimate the activation energy: E

diffusion-mediated reordering processes that transform the high energy double CSF into a lower energy SESF. Using the results pre -

6. Conclusions 
In conclusion, using atomistic simulations, we investigated the effects of Nb solutes on the Kolbe mechanism for microtwinning in 
Ni-based superalloys, which is based on thermally activated reordering. Our results demonstrate that relatively small addition of Nb 
can significantly increase creep resistance, in agreement with experimental observations. The effect of Nb atoms is multifold. First, it 
increases the CSF energy due to Nb atoms making it more difficult for dislocations to enter the strengthening precipitate (and move 
inside it). Secondly, and more importantly, the slowly diffusing Nb atoms impede the reordering of the high energy double CSF. The 
presented results illustrate the complexities associated with atomic-scale processes involved in creep deformation, which is crucial for
