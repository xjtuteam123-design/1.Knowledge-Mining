Abstract: 
The paper reports evolution of two-phase microstruc ture quantitatively in a high entropy 
alloy of composition Co 37.6 Ni 35.4 Al 9.9 Mo 4.9 Cr 5.9 Ta 2.8 Ti 3.5 . A very high-volume fraction of 
ordered L1 2 precipitates forms below its solvus temperature of 1156 °C that cannot be 
suppressed during quenching. The quench microstruct ure contains nanometric bimodal size 
distribution of spheroidal and irregular precipitat es, both having L1 2 ordering (γ’) and a Co + 
Ni concentration close to 75%, thus representing a stoichiometry of (Ni,Co) 3 
(Al,Cr,Ta,Ti,Mo). The alloy shows hardening on agin g with a peak in hardness value at 900 
°C for 50 hours of annealing. The morphology of the precipitates at this stage evolves into 
cuboidal shape having rounded corners with a lattic e misfit of +0.22% with the matrix. A 
detailed study of the coarsening behaviour of these precipitates in the matrix of complex solid 
solution indicates that the coarsening follows a mo dified LSW mechanism with estimated 
activation energy of 360 ± 50 kJ/mol between 900 to 1000 °C. This is slightly higher than the 
activation energy of most of the solutes in γ Co matrix that does not contain heavy elements 
like W or Re. The high-volume fraction of the preci pitates together with coarsening 
resistance lead to an attractive high temperature s trength that is higher than many of the 
known superalloys. 
Keywords: High entropy alloy; γ′ precipitates; Coarsening kinetics; High temperatur e 
strength; Strengthening mechanism.

1. Introduction 
Although entropic stabilisation of phases is known for a long time, consciously designing a 
high entropy material for structural applications b y mixing many different elements is of 
recent origin [1–3]. In the realm of high temperatu re materials, multicomponent alloys are 
rather norm than exception. All the superalloys strengthened by solid solution contain several 
alloying additions. This is particularly true for c obalt based superalloys which still recently 
were strengthened only by solid solution hardening. A typical solid solution hardened Co-
based superalloy like Haynes 188 contains at least 6 elements ensuring higher entropy of 
mixing that provides stability at high temperatures [4,5]. Ordering, on the other hand can 
stabilize an alloy through lowering of the enthalpy and hence free energy. The emergence of 
L1 2 ordered precipitates forms the basis for the devel opment of widely used nickel based 
superalloys. It is recently established that with t he additions of aluminium, tungsten, Mo and 
Nb or Ta, a new generation of cobalt based superalloys can be developed where the alloys are 
mainly strengthened by the ordered precipitates [6– 9]. Ordering, however, tends to alter the 
entropic contribution and hence, can influence the high temperature stability. Thus, a judicial 
combination of elements can leverage both the contr ibutions and provides a pathway for 
developing high temperature high entropy alloys. 
Utilization of L1 
2 ordered precipitates in strengthening the high ent ropy alloy is of very 
recent origin and therefore very limited literatures are available. Of the available publications, 
majority of them focus on the phase transformation behaviour, γ′ morphology and mechanical 
properties at low temperature regime ( ≤ 900 °C) [10–19]. For example, Gwalani et al. have 
reported the effect of alloying additions such as A l, Ti and Cu and various thermo-
mechanical processing on the stability of L1 2 ordered precipitates in high entropy alloys 
[10,11,15,18]. An addition of Cu is shown to promot e the heterogeneous nucleation and 
stabilization of L1 2 ordered precipitates. These precipitates are only s table below 930 °C and 
transform to disordered γ phase [11]. Reports covering the mechanical aspect of these alloys 
have shown multi-fold increase in strength on intro duction of these precipitate in high 
entropy γ matrix [12–14,20–23]. However, due to limited micr ostructural stability of L1 2 
ordered precipitates in these alloys, the mechanica l properties including plasticity deteriorate 
at high temperature [10,13,21]. Therefore, to make the high entropy alloys suitable for high 
temperature applications, strategic composition des ign as well as studies on long term 
stability and coarsening kinetics of these precipitates at high temperature (> 900 °C) and their

4. Discussion 
The results presented above point to a new γ-γ′ strengthened high entropy alloy (HEACoNi) 
that shows very high γ′ dissolution temperature ( γ′ solvus) and excellent coarsening 
resistance. These will now be discussed in detail a nd compared with the contemporary high 
entropy alloys as well as state of the art Ni and Co-based superalloys. 
4.1 The evolution of microstructure in the two-phase high entropy alloy 
The microstructure indicate segregation of strong γ′ stabilizers such as Ni and Ta, Ti in the 
inter-dendritic regions and enrichment of γ formers such as Co and Cr in the dendrites. As a 
consequence, the inter-dendritic regions contain a uniform distribution of very fine scale L1 2 
ordered precipitates. Similar formation of γ′ precipitates in the dendritic and inter-dendritic 
regions in a high entropy alloy was recently reported by He et al. [13].

Solutionising heat-treatment at 1250 °C for 25h followed by water quenching could not 
suppress the formation of fine γ′ precipitates which now appear with bimodal size 
distribution. The bigger size precipitates evolve i nto a well-defined spheroidal shape, while 
smaller size precipitates remain irregular. Nucleat ion of very fine irregular shaped γ′ 
precipitate after solutioning heat-treatment is well reported in the Co and Ni-based superalloy

generally not observed either in high entropy alloy s or Co-based superalloys. The 
composition profiles of these precipitates suggest that the Co + Ni content of both spheroidal 
and irregular shaped γ′ precipitates are close to the content of the γ matrix, i.e. ∼ 75 at.%. This 
strongly suggests that a slight fluctuation in the composition of γ matrix may have led to the 
crossing of the nucleation barrier, a phenomenon ge nerally known as pseudo spinodal 
decomposition that may have led to the formation of fine irregular precipitates. The pseudo 
spinodal decomposition has been reported in variou s metallic systems such as Ti alloys, Au-
Cu-Pt, Au-Cu-Pd, Co-Pt and Ni-Fe-Nb-V alloys [41–47]. 
The heat treated alloy (900 °C for 50 h) shows γ/γ′ lattice mismatch of +0.22% similar to the 
Co-based superalloys [6,24]. In contrast, the Ni-ba sed superalloys with Ni 3(Al,Ti) type 
precipitates in γ- Ni matrix show negative lattice mismatch [48–50]. The alloying additions 
change the lattice mismatch by partitioning across the γ/γ′ interface. In the case of Ni-based 
superalloys, it decreases with Co addition. However , in the case of most of the Co-based 
superalloys, a positive mismatch could be observed with the Co based solid solution matrix 
with the alloying additions of elements like Ni, Ti , Mo, W, Ta, Nb [30,51]. Contrary to these 
superalloys, present high entropy HEACoNi alloy sho ws Ni rich γ′-precipitates of 
stoichiometry (NiCo) 3(Al,Cr,Ta,Ti,Mo) in Co rich γ- matrix with a positive mismatch. 
4.2 Evolution of γ′ morphology during heat-treatment at 900, 950 and 1000 °C 
During aging at 900 and 950 °C, the γ′ precipitate morphology remains cuboidal with 
rounded corner while aging at 1000 °C, the shape changes to spheroidal. The precipitate 
shape is governed by two competing factors. First i s the anisotropy of interfacial energy 
which alters the Wulff shape and hence the shape of the precipitates. The second factor is the 
elastic energy ( ∝ r 3) which can be both isotropic and anisotropic, aris ing due to γ/γ′ lattice 
mismatch. At aging temperatures of 900 and 950 °C, the precipitate shape remains cuboidal 
with rounded corner till 1000 h of aging, suggestin g that there is no further change in the

results in evolution of spheroidal shape of γ′ precipitates.

5 Conclusions 
The present work on the precipitation hardened high entropy alloy can be summarized by 
following points: 
1. High entropy alloy of composition Co 
37.6 Ni 35.4 Al 9.9 Mo 4.9 Cr 5.9 Ta 2.8 Ti 3.5 (HEACoNi) in the 
as cast form shows dendritic and inter-dendritic re gions with presence of very fine scale 
L1 2 ordered precipitates in the inter-dendritic region. 
2. Solutionizing heat-treatment followed with water qu enching of the as cast HEACoNi 
alloy leads to bimodal size distribution of γ′ precipitates with bigger precipitates (<r>, 28 
± 7 nm) evolve into a spheroidal shape, while small er precipitate remains irregular. The 
APT analysis on the solutionized sample shows both kinds of precipitates to have similar 
compositions with stoichiometry of (Ni,Co) 3(Al,Cr,Ta,Ti,Mo) 
3. Further isothermal heat-treatment of the HEACoNi alloy at various temperatures leads to 
evolution of γ′ morphology to round-cornered cuboidal shape at 900 and 950 °C and 
spheroidal shape at 1000 °C. The room temperature X-ray measurement predicts a 
positive 
γ/γ′ lattice mismatch value of 0.22% for the HEACoNi al loy subjected to heat-treatment 
at 
900 °C for 50 h. 
4. The temporal exponents of average precipitate size and number density evolution for the 
HEACoNi alloy during isothermal exposure at 900, 95 0 and 1000 °C are close to the 
values predicted by modified LSW based model and PV based model for the 
multicomponent system, suggesting matrix diffusion as the coarsening rate limiting step. 
5. The coarsening rate constant of the HEACoNi alloy i s comparable to heavy elements 
(such as W, Re and Ru) containing Ni and Co-based s uperalloys at 900 and lower at 950 
°C and above. Thus, the present high entropy alloy s hows excellent microstructural 
stability at elevated temperatures.
