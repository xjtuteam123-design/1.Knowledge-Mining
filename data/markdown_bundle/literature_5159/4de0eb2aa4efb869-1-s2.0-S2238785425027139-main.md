ABSTRACT
Laser additive manufactured (LAM-ed) copper alloys with post-heat-treatments own excellent thermal and me -
chanical properties at ambient temperature. Nevertheless, for high-heat-flux copper alloys, the lack of under -
standing on microstructural evolution and properties at elevated temperatures. Herein, thermal and tensile 
properties of LAM-ed copper-chromium-zirconium (CuCrZr) alloys at elevated temperatures were investigated. 
The remained tangled dislocations, dislocation walls, low stacking fault energies, irregular grain and subgrains, 
high-dense body-centered-cubic nanoscale Cr and Zr-rich particles, high fraction of high-angle grain boundaries, 
retarded partial recrystallization and then made the alloys maintain good tensile and thermal properties (tensile 
strength: 180 MPa, elongation: 6.1 %, thermal conductivity: about 290 Wm

◦
C. The present work 
achieved the synthesis of high-strength and high-thermal conductive copper alloys at elevated temperatures,

1. Introduction
Copper alloys, especially used as rocket engine combustion chamber 
parts, the heat sink of fusion reactor, etc., have attracted more attention 
due to their excellent thermal and mechanical properties when serving 
in extreme environment [ 1 – 4 ]. Laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) process 
has been widely used to fabricate such complex parts due to its unique 
feature of layer-by-layer fabrication and rapid cooling rate (10
4
– 10
7 
K/s) [ 5 , 6 ]. The process eliminates most of the constraints related to 
design and creativity, making it one of the most promising processes for 
different additive manufacturing (AM) processes [ 7 , 8 ]. Nevertheless, 
the fabrication of pure copper and its alloys through laser additive 
manufacturing (LAM) remains difficult due to high reflectivity to 
near-infrared (NIR) laser beams and significant heat dissipation during 
LAM process [ 9 – 11 ].
In our previous work [ 12 ], a comprehensive review on the AM-ed 
copper alloys (especially copper-chromium-zirconium (CuCrZr) and 
copper-chromium-niobium (CuCrNb) alloys) has been systematically 
conducted, including the application in aerospace fields, AM and 
post-treatment processes, the microstructural evolution, tensile and 
thermal properties, and future prospects. To best of our known, most of 
researchers focus on AM and post-heat-treatment processes, micro -
structure and properties at ambient temperatures. However, for alloys 
used as engine components, it is significant for AM-ed copper alloys to 
maintain excellent mechanical and thermal properties at elevated tem -
perature. So far, Zeng et al. [ 13 , 14 ] investigated the effect of tempera -
tures on tensile and thermal properties of LPBF-built CuCrZr (C-18150) 
alloys. The alloys exhibit poor mechanical and thermal properties ( ul -

3.2. Tensile properties at elevated temperature
Fig. 3 shows the tensile properties of LPBF-built CuCrZr alloy at 
elevated temperatures. The alloys exhibit excellent tensile properties at 
ambient temperature ( UTS : 585 MPa, EL : 14.4 %). Since stress relaxation 
may occur at heating process before tensile test, there were slightly no 
elastic stage at early stage of tensile testing. When elevating to 100 
◦
C, 
the tensile strength of the alloy decreased to 482 MPa, while the 
ductility improved ( EL :18.0 %). When temperature rose to 300 
◦
C, the

MPa, EL : 21.1 %). However, as the temperature increased to 600

both strength and ductility begun to decreased ( UTS : 180 MPa, EL : 6.1

ture elevated to 700 
◦

( UTS : 140 MPa, EL : 3.8 %). Similar phenomenon can be found in other 
most of alloys, including Cu-based, Nickel-based alloys etc [ 17 , 27 , 28 ].

significantly lower than that with {110} one.
When alloys were stretched at room temperature, the dislocations 
evolved to higher density due to strain hardening [ 37 ], rendering higher 
faction of LAGBs as illustrated in Fig. 10 (b). As for Str_600 one, initially, 
static recrystallization (SRX) occurred during heating process (600 
◦
C). 
As a result, the dislocations annihilated and dislocation density reduced. 
Subsequently, dynamic recrystallization (DRX) occurred during tensile 
deformation (600

Typically, DRX temperature is about one-third or one-half of melting 
point of metals. For high stacking-fault energy (SFE) pure Cu, the DRX 
temperature is about 300 
◦
C [ 38 ]. As for LPBF-built CuCrZr alloys in 
present work, the DRX temperature is higher. It is matter that DRX can 
influence mechanical properties of alloys greatly. DRX process depends 
on many factors, including temperature, strain rate, time, the initial 
grain size, dislocations, misorientation, crytal-orientation, precipitates, 
etc [ 39 , 40 ]. The above factors will be discussed as follows.
Thermal activated processes had trend to reduce crystal defects to 
reduce the free energy of system to keep the system stable. During 
heating process, SRX occurred, and dislocations arranged and annihi -
lated. The residual dislocation walls remained near the origin HAGBs. 
During tensile deformation at elevated temperature, DRX process 
occurred by the difference of internal energy. For low SFE Cu alloys in 
present work, at the beginning of tensile deformation, SFs can impede 
cross slip and climb of dislocations [ 39 ], whereby can retard DRX. 
Dislocation slipping mediated the tensile deformation at the initial 
stage. With the progress of tensile testing, GBs absorbed more disloca -
tions. Usually, for LPBF-built Cu alloys, the SFs led by LPBF process are 
in long-range order, like Ref [ 5 , 22 ]. The SFs in Fig. 7(d1) and Fig. 7(e) in 
this work are only several atoms in length, which are probably to be 
caused by tensile deformation at elevated temperature. As such, SFs 
mediated the tensile deformation at the final stage, as illustrated in 
Fig. 7 (d – f), whereby the alloy can achieve strength-ductility trade-off at 
elevated temperature.

The LPBF-built CuCrZr alloys in present work own excellent tensile

been discussed in detail, as aforementioned in Section 4.2 .
As for thermal properties, the alloys in our work own better thermal 
conductivity due to post-heat-treatments. The alloys in other research

5. Conclusion
The CuCrZr alloys processed by LAM and aging treatment in this 
work maintain good tensile and thermal properties at ambient and 
elevated temperatures. The microstructural evolutions had been care -
fully investigated after high-temperature tensile deformation. Some 
conclusions are summarized as follows. 
(1) The alloys exhibited good tensile properties ( UTS : 180 MPa, EL : 
6.1 %) at 600 
◦
C. The interaction of dislocations with dislocations 
(remained tangled dislocations, dense dislocation walls, low 
SFEs, irregular grain and subgrains), high-dense bcc nanoscale Cr 
and Zr-rich particles, high-angle grain boundaries, retarded 
recrystallization and then made the alloy maintain good tensile 
properties at elevated temperatures.
(2) The alloys exhibit excellent thermal conductivity and slightly 
decreased to about 290 W m
 1 
K
 1 
at 600 
◦
C due to the remained 
bcc nanoscale Cr and Zr-rich precipitates, and the decrease of 
high-density dislocations. The thermal conductivity decreased 
with increasing temperature since SRV and SRX occurred in 
turns, whereby the overaging occurred and precipitates aggre -
gated, and phonon scattering by crystal defects and umklapp 
occurred.
