Effects of Er on Microstructure and Mechanical Properties of 
Mg-Zn-Er-Zr Magnesium Alloys 
Qudong Wang a, Daquan Li, Qiang Li, Wenjiang Ding 
National Engineering Research Center of Light Alloys Net Forming, Shanghai Jiaotong University, 
1954 Huashan Road, 200030 Shanghai, China. 
 aemail:wangqudong@sjtu.edu.cn 
Keywords: Mg-Zn-Er alloy; Heat treatment; Microstructure; Strength; Hardness 
Abstract: Microstructure and mechanical properties of Mg-Zn-Er-Zr alloys were characterized in 
detail. The grain size of as-cast Mg-Zn-Er-Zr alloy was greatly decreased by the Mg-Zn-Er phases 
formed at grain boundaries. The addition of Er can increase the yield strength (YS) but decrease the 
ultimate tensile strength (UTS) and elongation of as-cast Mg-Zn-Zr alloy. The thermally stable 
Mg-Zn-Er phases were just partially dissolved into the matrix during solution treatment. And the 
addition of Er can prolong the precipitation process of Mg-Zn-Zr alloy. Solution-plus-ageing 
treatment can increase the strength of both the Mg-Zn-Zr and Mg-Zn-Er-Zr alloys, but the 
strengthening effect of Mg-Zn-Er-Zr alloy was greatly weakened, for the incompletely solution of 
Mg-Zn-Er phases. Er can greatly enhance the high temperature elongation of Mg-Zn-Zr alloy, but the 
increase of high temperature tensile strength was just a little. 
1. Introduction 
Lightweight magnesium alloys have attracted increasing interest in recent years for applications in the 
aerospace, aircraft and automotive industries [1]. The widely used magnesium alloys belonging to 
Mg-Zn-Zr (ZK) series, such as ZK60 have excellent deformability, good room temperature 
mechanical properties and low cost. However, the use of these magnesium alloys has been limited 
because of the poor high temperature properties. 
It has been reported that rare earth additions can improve high temperature mechanical properties 
of Mg-Zn-Zr magnesium alloys [2-4]. Especially the Mg-Zn-Y alloys containing quasicrystal 
icosahedral phase (I-phase) as a secondary solidification phase exhibit good mechanical properties at 
room temperature and at elevated temperatures [5-8]. 
The maximum solubility of rare earth metal erbium in magnesium is 6.56at.%, which is much 
larger than that of Y (3.59at.%) [9]. There may be larger solution and precipitation strengthening 
effect for Er in Mg alloys. And the ZnMgEr quasicrystal phase was also found [10]. So Mg-Zn-Er-Zr 
alloy is a promising candidate for novel high strength Mg alloys with quasicrystal phases. But there 
was almost no investigation on Mg-Zn-Er alloys. Therefore, Mg-Zn-Er-Zr alloys were investigated in 
the current work. Effects of Er on microstructure and mechanical properties of Mg-Zn-Er-Zr 
magnesium alloys during heat treatment were studied. 
Experimental method 
The chemical compositions of the alloys investigated in this work were Mg-6.13Zn-0.75Zr and 
Mg-5.59Zn-1.25Er-0.92Zr analyzed by inductively coupled plasma (ICP). 
Mg-Zn-Zr alloy was solutionized at 733K for 10h and Mg-Zn-Er-Zr alloy was solutionized at 
753K for 40h followed by quenching in cool water. The alloys were then isothermally aged at 423K, 
448K and 473K for different time. 
The process of precipitation during ageing was checked by hardness measurement. Hardness was 
measured using a Vickers-hardness tester with 30Kg load. The tensile properties at room temperature 
were tested in Zwick/Roell testing machine, and the high temperature tensile tests were carried out on

Results and discussion 
3.1. Microstructure 
The microstructures of as-cast and solutionized alloys were shown in Fig.1 (a)-(d). The average 
grain size of as-cast Mg-Zn-Er-Zr alloy is much smaller than that of as-cast Mg-Zn-Zr alloy. Er has 
great effects on grain refinement of as-cast Mg-Zn-Er-Zr alloy. The grains became a little larger after 
solution treatment in both alloys.

Fig.4 shows the DTA results of the as-cast Mg-Zn-Er-Zr alloy during the heating process. There 
are two endothermic peaks appeared during the process, one is at 734.28K and the other is at 811.43K. 
These two peaks may correspond to the dissolution temperatures of two Mg-Zn-Er phases. The lower 
one may correspond to X1, which was solutionized into the matrix during the solution process 
carrying out at 753K, the other may correspond to X2, which was still remained at grain-boundaries 
after solution treatment. 
3.3. Ageing curves 
The results of the hardness measurements during ageing for Mg-Zn-Zr and Mg-Zn-Er-Zr alloys are 
presented in Fig.5 (a) and (b). The Mg-Zn-Zr alloy has higher maximum hardness than Mg-Zn-Er-Zr 
alloy in the same ageing conditions. This may contribute to the better solution-plus-ageing effect in 
Mg-Zn-Zr alloy. In the same ageing temperature, Mg-Zn-Er-Zr alloy need a longer ageing time to 
reach the hardness maximum than Mg-Zn-Zr alloy. This indicates that addition of Er can prolong the 
precipitation process of Mg-Zn-Er-Zr alloy. According to the ageing curves, 448Kx16h for Mg-Zn-Zr 
alloy and 448Kx32h for Mg-Zn-Er-Zr alloy were chosen as the best ageing parameters. 
3.4. Tensile properties 
Fig.6 shows the tensile properties of Mg-Zn-Er-Zr alloy in different states at room temperature 
compared with Mg-Zn-Zr alloy. Mg-Zn-Er-Zr alloy exhibits higher YS but lower UTS and elongation 
in the as-cast state. UTS and YS of both the alloys increased after solution-plus-aging treatment, but 
the increments of Mg-Zn-Er-Zr alloy were lower than that of Mg-Zn-Zr alloy. The result was 
correspondent with ageing curves, and this may be caused by the thermally stable Mg-Zn-Er phases, 
which can not be completely dissolved into the matrix after the solution treatment. So the solution 
strengthening effect and the ageing strengthening effect of Zn and Er in Mg-Zn-Er-Zr alloy were 
weakened.

Fig.7 shows the tensile properties of Mg-Zn-Zr and Mg-Zn-Er-Zr alloys after solution-plus-aging 
treatment at elevated temperatures. Fig.7(a) shows that the UTS of both alloys decreased with 
increasing the test temperature. The elongation of alloys at elevated temperatures was shown in 
Fig.7(b). Mg-Zn-Er-Zr alloy exhibits much larger elongations than Mg-Zn-Zr alloy in all the elevated 
temperatures. This may be caused by the thermally stable Mg-Zn-Er phases, which act as strong 
pinning effects for grain boundaries at high temperatures, preventing the micro-crack propagation at 
large angle grain boundaries. Er has a pronounced effect to increase the high temperature plasticity of 
Mg-Zn-Er-Zr alloy. 
4. Conclusions 
(1) Er has a pronounced effect on grain refinement of as-cast Mg-Zn-Er-Zr alloy. The addition of Er 
can increase the YS but decrease the UTS and elongation of as-cast Mg-Zn-Er-Zr alloy. 
(2) Some thermally stable Mg-Zn-Er phases formed along the grain-boundaries, which can not be 
completely dissolved during the solution treatment. 
(3) Solution-plus-ageing treatment can increase the strength of Mg-Zn-Er-Zr alloy, but the 
strengthening effect was greatly weakened, for the incompletely solution of Mg-Zn-Er phases. 
(4) Er can greatly enhance the high temperature elongation of Mg-Zn-Er-Zr alloy.
