Ti-Fe-Sn-Nb hypoeutectic alloys with superb yield strength and
signiﬁcant strain-hardening

In this letter new Ti-Fe-Sn-Nb hypoeutectic alloy comprised of primary dendriticβ-Ti and ultraﬁne (β-Ti + TiFe)
eutectic was developed showing superior mechanical properties. The as-cast Ti67Fe27Sn3Nb3 (at.%) alloy exhibit-
ed exceptionally high yield stress (2.18 GPa) comparable to that of bulk metallic glasses. Most importantly, the
sample presented signiﬁcant strain-hardening (320 MPa) and enhanced plasticity. The slip deformation and dis-
location accumulation in the β-Ti dendrite contribute to the plasticity and the pronounced strain-hardening,
whereas the high strength stems from the ultraﬁne (β-Ti + TiFe) eutectic structure as well as the solution hard-
ening in the multicomponent system.

Nanocrystalline and ultraﬁne-grained alloys perform superior me-
chanical strength owing to the existence of a large volume fraction of
grain boundaries that impede further dislocation propagation [1,2].
These alloys present densely packed heterogeneous cluster structure
in atomic scale and exhibit highest yield stresses. However, they display
limited plasticity and lack of strain-hardening due to their highly local-
ized deformation mechanisms [3–5]. Attempts have been made to im-
prove the plasticity of the ultra ﬁne or nanocrystalline alloys by
introducing a composite structure formed by isolated crystalline den-
drites embedded in the nanostructured matrix [6,7]. With this motiva-
tion, the current work concentrates on the development of Ti-based
ductile-phase-reinforced alloys for achieving the combination of high
strength and excellent plasticity.
In a similar manner as nanocrystalline composite alloys, Ti-Fe based
eutectic alloys were investigated, where the matrix material is com-
prised of ultraﬁne eutectics instead of nanocrystalline phases [8]. A se-
ries of Ti-Fe and Ti-Fe-Co metastable alloys in arc-melted state
exhibited an excellent combination of strength (over 2.0 GPa) and duc-
tility (up to 16.5% compressive strain)[9–12], where Co was found to be
an effective alloying element to improve the plasticity. Later it has been
shown that the additions of Sn and Nb may further improve the me-
chanical performances of Ti-Fe eutectic and hypereutectic alloys [13–
18]. Recently a (Ti
70.5Fe29.5)93.15Sn3.85Nb3 (at.%) alloy exhibited
1.6 GPa yield stress and 2.36 GPa fracture stress at 15% compressive
strain [17]. Indeed these alloys present high fracture strength, which is
actually linked with pronounced strain-hardening after yielding. In
fact they exhibit much lower yield strength (1.3 –1.8 GPa) comparing
with their fracture strength[8]. Note that the yield strength is more im-
portant from the practical engineering application point of view. It was
reported earlier that Ti-Fe-Ta hypoeutectic alloys prepared by Cu-mold
casting showed higher yield stress exceeding 2.0 GPa[19]. However the
alloys expressed limited plasticity (≤5% plastic strain) due to the coars-
ened eutectics by the addition of Ta.
In this letter we present Ti-Fe-Sn-Nb hypoeutectic alloys consisting
of a ductile dendritic phase and ultraﬁne eutectics for the purpose of im-
proving the plasticity without sacri ﬁcing their excellent mechanical
strength. The basic design strategies are: (1) introducing ‘soft’ primary
β-Ti phase by selecting Ti-Fe hypoeutectic compositions; (2) a multi-
component alloy system was achieved by the addition of minor amount
of Nb and Sn, which have a tendency to form solid solutions and to re-
ﬁne the eutectic structure [11,13,20].
The alloys used in this work were prepared in a two-step process.

Fig. 4 shows the fracture morphologies of the alloys after compres-
sive tests. Ti 67Fe27Sn3Nb3 exhibited general ductile fracture ( Fig. 4a),
where the majority of β-Ti dendrites can be clearly distinguished from
the lamellar eutectic. The black arrows denote the deformation-induced
shear bands inside the dendrites. Some of the shear bands propagated
across the entire dendrite and stopped at the dendrite/eutectic bound-
ary. Harren et al.[26] pointed out that the formation of the macroscopic
shear bands was proceeded by the development of slip bands and ac-
companied with continuous strain-hardening, meanwhile the den-
drite/eutectic boundaries are strong barriers to shear transferring. Fig.
4b shows an enlarged dendritic area of Ti
67Fe27Sn3Nb3, where the
boundaries between the dendrites and the surrounded eutectic are
clearly seen. One can see network-like deformation bands homoge-
neously dispersed in the dendrites, which indicates that the plastic de-
formation was mainly operated by the ductile primary β-Ti in the
form of dislocation. This is in agreement with the TEM observation in
our previous work [12,25], where the dislocation-type of deformation
is mainly responsible for the plasticity and also plays a positive role on
the pronounced strain-hardening. On the other hand, the eutectic area
also displayed smooth fracture morphology inFig. 4b, although it is be-
lieved that no dislocation deformation occurred in the hard TiFe lamella
[12].I n Fig. 4c–d, Ti
65Fe26Sn6Nb3 and Ti66Fe26Sn3Nb3Co2 showed cleav-
age fracture due to the lack of a ductile reinforced phase. Actually both
alloys expressed some extent of ductility even in a limited level
(Fig. 3 ). The plasticity of the eutectic can be explained as easy slip
transfers from β-Ti to TiFe and both phases deform compatibly at a
stress lower than the cleavage stress [27].
One critical issue in the development of ultra ﬁne-grained or nano-
crystalline engineering materials is to improve the ductility without
sacriﬁcing their mechanical strength. The present Ti
67Fe27Sn3Nb3 ultra-
ﬁne hypoeutectic alloy was found exhibiting superb yield strength ac-
companied with signi ﬁcant strain-hardening and good plasticity. It's
worth emphasizing the scienti ﬁc and engineering signi ﬁcance since
most bulk metallic materials with high yield strength display moderate
strain-hardening and limited plasticity, e.g. Ti-based nanocrystalline al-
loys are characterized by high yield strength ( Hall-Petch relation) but
low strain-hardening due to the low density of dislocation encountered
during plastic deformation[28,29]. The good ductility of Ti
67Fe27Sn3Nb3
is mainly attributed to the primaryβ-Ti dendrites, where dislocation-in-

carriers of plastic deformation. In addition, the easy slip transfer fromβ-
Ti to TiFe in the eutectic matrix also contribute to plastic strain to some
extent, as one can see the plasticity (b6% plastic strain) expressed by the
alloys without ductile primary phases. The excellent mechanical

harder eutectic matrix.
In summary, Ti
67Fe27Sn3Nb3 ultraﬁne hypoeutectic alloy with good
plasticity and exceptionally high strength was developed using the
strategy of ductility reinforcement and microstructural toughening in
Ti-Fe based alloys. The two basic principles are: (1) designing the alloy
from the Ti-Fe hypoeutectic compositions for the purpose of introduc-
ing ductile β-Ti dendrites in a eutectic matrix to provide the plastic
strain; (2) re ﬁning the (TiFe + β-Ti) eutectic matrix to suppress slip
propagation and enhance the mechanical strength. The alloy presented
superb yield strength followed by signi ﬁcant strain-hardening. The
overall plasticity and the pronounced strain-hardening were attributed
to the primary β-Ti dendrites performing dislocation type of deforma-
tion, whereas the superior strength results from the hard TiFe lamella,
