New developed quaternary refractory superalloys
Xihong Yu *, Yoko Yamabe-Mitarai, Yoshikazu Ro, Hiroshi Harada
National Research Institute for Metals, Sengen 1-2-1, Tsukuba, Ibaraki 305-0047, Japan
Abstract
The novel idea for designing quaternary superalloys by two kinds of binary alloys with coherent structure was applied to Ir–Nb–
Ni–Al superalloys. Eight alloys were prepared by combining two sorts of binary alloys, Ir–Nb and Ni–Al, in di/C128erent proportions.
The e/C128ects of the mole fraction of Ir-based alloy or the L12 alloy on the microstructure and the 0.2% ﬂow stress of quaternary
alloys were investigated. Two sorts of coherent structure, fcc/L1 2–Ir3Nb and fcc/L1 2–Ni3Al, were observed in the quaternary
superalloys. The new developed quaternary Ir-based superalloys are of the advantages of Ir-based alloys and Ni-based superalloys.
The 0.2% ﬂow stress of Ir–Nb–Ni–Al at 1200 C could reach up to 350 MPa and the compressive strains were improved greatly

1. Introduction
The ‘‘refractory superalloys’ with a fcc and L1 2 two-
phase coherent structure were proposed and investi-
gated systematically by our group [1]. These alloys are
expected to have good high temperature strength, creep
resistance and oxidation resistance at high temperature.
Ir was selected as a base material because the melting
temperature of Ir (2447
C) is higher than that of Ni
(1455C). The results indicated that Ir-based alloys with
L12/fcc coherent structure were of great superiority at
high temperature strength. But, the intergranular frac-
ture in Ir-based alloys is a serious problem for applica-
tion; the ductility of Ir-based alloys are lower than 10%.
Other problems are high density and cost. In order to
improve the disadvantages of the Ir-based alloys, we
tried to solve these problems in di/C128erent approaches.
One is adding the third element to Ir-based binary
alloys, such as Ni [2]. Another approach is to develop
new alloys. We have proposed the idea to develop qua-
ternary alloys, i.e. the quaternary alloys are prepared by
mixing two sorts of binary alloys, Ir-based and Ni-based
alloys, which consist of fcc/L1
2 coherent structure [3].
Coherent structures of fcc/L1 2 are required for high
strengths at high temperature. The object was to com-
bine the high-temperature strength of Ir-based alloys
with the high ductility, low density (about 8.5 g/cm
3,
compared to 22 g/cm 3 for Ir), and relatively low cost of
Ni-based alloys. The microstructural evolution and
compressive properties at 1200C were investigated.

High purity Ir, Nb, Ni and Al were used to prepare
the quaternary Ir–Nb–Ni–Al refractory superalloys.

According to the ﬁrst stage results, the second stage was

obtain a volume in which only fcc and L1
2 phase exist.
It will be helpful to choose the composition just with
fcc/L12 coherent structure and beneﬁt to develop next
generation Ni-based superalloys. These alloys were
named as S020, S030, S040, S100 and S000. Alloy S000

3. Results and analysis
3.1. Microstructure
The observation of as cast samples showed that den-
drite structure existed in all investigated quaternary Ir–
Nb–Ni–Al alloys. The X-ray and composition analysis
indicated that the dendrite structure in S750 was L1
2–
Ir3Nb; two kinds of dendrite structure, L1 2–Ir3Nb and
fcc, showed in alloy S550 and S100; the dendrite struc-
ture in others alloys was fcc. Shown in Fig. 2 is the
microstructure of alloys heat treated at 1400
C for 168
h. Theses photos are arranged according to the location
of these alloys in sketch diagram shown in Fig. 1. After
heat treatment, the dendrite of fcc disappeared but L1
2–
Ir3Nb still existed. Phase analysis is also summarised in
Table 1. From the direction of (b) (g) (h), i.e. with the
increasing of Ir-based alloy, there was three-phase
equilibrium — fcc, L1
2–Ir3Nb and Ni 3Al — in the three
alloys. But the morphology of predominant L1 2–Ir3Nb
changed from particles to dendrite structure. In S050
alloys, some of the L1
2–Ir3Nb phase also presented in
lamella structure (see Fig. 2b). In the direction of (a)–
(f), the components of alloys changed from two kinds of
L1
2 phases (Ir 3Nb and Ni 3Al, Fig. 2a) to three-phase
equilibrium (fcc, Ir 3Nb and Ni 3Al, alloy; Fig. 2b–e)

the proportion of L1
2 alloy. Further investigation
showed that there also was three-phase equilibrium —
fcc, Ir
3Nb and Ni 3Al — in the alloy S000 at lower tem-
perature (lower than 1400 C). The TEM observation
indicated that fcc/L1 2–Ni3Al coherent structure could
be observed in all of these alloys and fcc/L1 2–Ir3Nb
coherent structure only observed in some interface
between fcc and plate-like Ir
3Nb. The fcc/L1 2–Ni3Al

suitable. The three-phase equilibrium (fcc, L1 2–Ir3Nb
and Ni 3Al) volume in the Ni-rich side of quaternary Ir–
Nb–Ni–Al provides a relatively larger room to develop
new alloys with two kinds of fcc/L1
2 coherent struc-
tures. And the two kinds of fcc/L1 2 coherent structures
must be beneﬁt to the high temperature strength.

4. Conclusions
The novel idea of designing quaternary superalloys
was applied for developing quaternary Ir-based super-
alloys. The quaternary superalloys were prepared by
two kinds of binary alloys with fcc/L1
2 coherent struc-
ture. Two kinds of coherent structure, fcc/L1 2–Ir3Nb
and fcc/L1 2–Ni3Al, were observed in the quaternary
superalloys. Three-phase equilibrium — fcc, L1 2–Ir3Nb
and L1 2–Ni3Al — were observed in a special volume.
Two-phase — L1 2–Ir3Nb and Ni 3Al — and one-
phase — fcc — also were observed in alloy S100 and
S000, respectively. The new developed quaternary Ir-
based superalloys are of the advantages of Ir-based
alloys and Ni-based superalloys. The 0.2% ﬂow stress of
Ir–Nb–Ni–Al at 1200
C could reach up to 350 MPa.
The compressive strains were improved greatly.
