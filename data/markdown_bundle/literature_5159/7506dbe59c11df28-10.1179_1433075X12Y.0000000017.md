Microstructure and mechanical properties of
directionally solidified and heat treated Nb–
14Si–24Ti–2Cr alloy
L. G. Wang, J. F. Weng, K. Guan, L. F. Su, L. N. Jia and H. Zhang *
In this work, the directional solidification of an Nb–14Si–24Ti–2Cr alloy (at-%) was performed at
1900uC with withdrawal rates of 1 ?2, 6 and 18 mm min 21, and the alloy was then heat treated (HT)
at 1450uC for 100 h. The microstructures and mechanical properties of the directionally solidified
and HT alloy were investigated. The phases present in the sample were confirmed to be Nb SS
dendrites, lath-like Nb 3Si, eutectic Nb SSzNb3Si and Ti enriched areas. As the withdrawal rate
increased, the effects of directional solidification became more evident, and the microstructure
became more refined. After the heat treatment, the microstructure was characterised by Nb SS
dendrites, residual Nb 3Si and lamellar eutectic Nb SSzNb3Si, which was decomposed from the
lath-like Nb3Si. An increase in the withdrawal rate resulted in a decrease in the room temperature
fracture toughness of the HT alloy. However, the room temperature compressive strength of the
HT alloy increased to 1593 ?9 MPa with increasing withdrawal rate, which was twice the value of
the compressive strength before heat treatment.
Keywords: Nb–Si, Directional solidification, Heat treatment, Microstructure, Mechanical properties
Introduction
Nb–Si based ultrahigh temperature alloys possess higher
melting points, relatively lower densities and attractive
high temperature strength and oxidation resistance
properties.
1 Therefore, they have become a material of
interest in the ﬁeld of ultrahigh temperature alloys.
Recent research has focused on alloying and processing
technologies for enhancing the combined properties.
2
Bewlay et al. 3–6 studied the microstructure and per-
formance of Nb–Si and Nb–Ti–Si alloys that were
directionally solidiﬁed (DS) at a low withdrawal rate
and then heat treated (HT). The study indicated that the
concentration of Si determined the volume fraction
of silicide and the high temperature strength. With
increasing Si concentration, the Nb–Si alloy changed
from hypoeutectic to hypereutectic. The addition of Ti
could enhance the room temperature fracture toughness
and the high temperature oxidation resistance.
7,8 Cr
played the role of activation in promoting the eutectoid
reaction of Nb 3Si and enhanced the high temperature
oxidation resistance. In the quaternary Nb–Ti–Si–Cr
alloy with an appropriate Cr content, the heat treatment
temperature and time for the disappearance of Nb 3Si
were reduced, whereas the high temperature oxidation
resistance was increased. 9–11 The Nb–Si–Ti–Cr–Al–Hf
alloys, such as the Nb–16Si–25Ti–2Cr–2Al–8Hf (at-%)
alloy from GE, 12 were developed on the basis of qua-
ternary Nb–Ti–Si–Cr alloy and have reached a balance in
the creep, oxidation and fracture resistance properties.
Controlling the morphology of Nb based solid solu-
tions and the intermetallic phases (Nb 3Si or Nb 5Si3)
has been a key issue for improving the mechanical pro-
perties of alloys. Directional solidiﬁcation could result
in Nb–Si based alloys with the desired morphology
for Nb based solid solutions and intermetallic phases,
eliminate horizontal grain boundaries and reduce
metallurgic defects.
13,14 The use of a higher solidiﬁcation
rate can further reﬁne the alloy microstructure and
improve the mechanical properties of the alloy. In view
of this, the Nb–14Si–24Ti–2Cr alloy was used for this
study. The effects of the withdrawal rate and heat
treatment on the microstructure and mechanical proper-
ties of the alloy are discussed.

Conclusions
The DS samples of the Nb–14Si–24Ti–2Cr alloy with-
drawn at rates from 1 ?2t o1 8 m m m i n21 consisted of
NbSS,N b3Si, Ti rich area and eutectic (NbSSzNb3Si) cells.
Both the Nb SS dendrites and the Nb 3Si blocks aligned
erectly along the growth direction. With increasing with-
drawal rate, both the Nb SS dendrites and the Nb 3Si
became relatively longer, whereas the width decreased.
The microstructures of the HT samples consisted of
NbSS dendrites and the (Nb SSzNb5Si3) phase with a
lamellar microstructure and residual Nb3Si. The NbSS in
all of the HT samples presented continuous dendrite
morphologies, and the edge of the Nb3Si3 blocks became
smooth. The solid solubility of Ti in NbSS increased, and
the components were homogeneous after heat treatment.
With increasing withdrawal rate, the room tempera-
ture fracture toughness of the HT samples decreased
gradually, whereas the room temperature compressive
strength of the DS samples increased. The room
temperature compressive strength of the DS samples
improved signiﬁcantly after heat treatment, with a
maximum strength of 1594 MPa for sample HT-18.
