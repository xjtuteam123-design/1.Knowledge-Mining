Effect of Co content on the as-cast microstructure and homogenization of Ni-Co-based superalloys
ARTICLE INFO
Keywords:
Ni-Co-based superalloy
Dendritic segregation
Homogenization
ABSTRACT
Ni-Co-based superalloys have emerged in recent years due to their excellent high-temperature capabilities. As the 
core element in these alloys, the influence of Co on microstructure characteristics and dendritic segregation 
remains unclear. Therefore, the as-cast microstructure and homogenization behavior of the alloys with varying 
Co content were investigated in this study. It was found that the Co element only exhibited a significant influence 
on the size and morphology of the γ’ phase for the as-cast microstructures. The addition of Co exacerbated initial 
dendritic segregation, it substantially improved the diffusion coefficients of alloying elements during homoge -
nization, resulting in an overall enhancement of homogenization efficiency.
1. Introduction
As the thrust-to-weight ratio of high-performance aerospace engines 
continues to rise, the demand for materials with enhanced temperature 
resistance correspondingly increases [1] . Superalloy is the primary 
material used for hot-end components of aerospace engines, its tem -
perature resistance capability is mainly elevated through composition 
design and optimization [2,3] . The high-temperature performance of 
nickel-based superalloys is typically improved by increasing the content 
of the precipitation phases, leading to higher concentrations of Al, Ti, 
Nb, etc. elements [4,5] . However, the continuous addition of strength -
ening elements leads to a gradual decrease in alloy plasticity and hot 
workability [6,7] . To overcome this challenge, the National Institute for 
Materials Science in Japan proposed the concept of Ni-Co-based super -
alloys [8] and successfully developed the TMW series of Ni-Co-based 
wrought superalloys [9 – 13] . Their research demonstrated that the 
Ni-Co-based superalloy could achieve a temperature resistance increase 
of 30
◦
C compared to the advanced wrought superalloy U720Li, without 
compromising hot workability [8,14] . Owing to their superior overall 
performance, these alloys have attracted considerable scholarly atten -
tion [15 – 24] . However, most studies have concentrated on the me -
chanical properties of Ni-Co-based superalloys [15 – 17,19] , with fewer 
investigations examining the segregation behavior and homogenization 
processes. Revealing the as-cast microstructural characteristics and 
evolution of elemental segregation during homogenization is a crucial 
step in material preparation [25 – 29] , which is critical for the cogging 
process and microstructural uniformity [30 – 32] . This step is especially 
vital for superalloys with high alloying element content, which exhibit 
heightened sensitivity to element changes [33 – 38] . The substantial 
addition of Co elements is expected to significantly impact the alloys. 
Therefore, this study explored the as-cast microstructural characteristics 
and segregation behavior during the homogenization process, aiming to 
reveal the influence pattern of Co element on the as-cast microstructural 
characteristics and the homogenization annealing process.

[26] , all alloys exhibited dendritic structures regardless of Co contents.

results revealed that SDAS remained nearly constant with increasing Co 
content, suggesting that Co has minimal influence on the extent of cast 
segregation. Previous studies have demonstrated that the SDAS is closely 
associated with the solidification process [39,40] . The similarity in

Observation of γ’ precipitates in the interdendritic region and 
dendrite core revealed that Co significantly influenced the size and 
morphology of γ’ phase. As Co content increased, the size of γ’ pre -
cipitates gradually decreased in both interdendritic regions and dendrite 
cores. Moreover, the sphericity of γ’ precipitates progressively improved 
with increasing Co content. At lower Co concentrations, γ’ precipitates 
in dendrite cores even exhibited splitting phenomena, which have been 
demonstrated to be closely related to the coarsening behavior [41 – 43] . 
These findings suggest that the Co element inhibits γ’ phase growth 
during the cooling process of Ni-Co-based superalloys, substantially 
refining γ’ precipitates under equivalent conditions. The XRD results 
confirmed that the as-cast microstructure primarily consisted of γ and γ’ 
phases across all alloy compositions, indicating that increasing Co 
content did not change the phase constitution. Additionally, γ + γ’ 
eutectic structures and carbide were observed at all Co concentrations. 
In summary, the addition of Co primarily affected the γ’ phase in the 
as-cast microstructure, with limited influence on the types of as-cast 
phases and SDAS.
To further investigate the influence of Co content on the γ + γ’ 
eutectic structure and carbides, elemental distributions were charac -
terized by EPMA, as shown in Fig. 2 . It was found that the carbide re -
gions exhibited high concentrations of Ti, C, and W regardless of Co 
content. It suggests that the carbide is enriched with Ti, C, and W, in -
dependent of Co content. The elemental enrichment pattern of the car -
bide closely resembles the descriptions of MC carbide in other studies 
[40,44,45] , leading to their identification as MC carbide. The γ + γ’ 
eutectic structures were all enriched with Ni, Ti, and Al elements under 
varying Co contents, which is similar to other Ni-based superalloys [44] . 
Thus, it can be inferred that Co has minimal impact on the elemental 
segregation behavior in the MC carbide and γ + γ’ eutectic structure.

tures, with results shown in Fig. 3 . It can be found that increasing Co 
content had minimal effect on the melting points, but significantly

The diffusion coefficients for various elements at different Co contents 
are presented in Table 1 . It can be observed that the diffusion coefficient 
of Ti is consistently greater than that of W under the same homogeni -
zation conditions, which is closely related to the difference in their 
atomic radius [50] . It was also found that the diffusion coefficients of the 
primary segregating elements Ti and W increased with increasing Co 
content. The results indicated that the addition of Co enhanced the 
diffusion capacity of Ni-Co superalloys, leading to the improvement of 
homogenization efficiency. The primary reasons for the increase in the 
diffusion coefficient of alloying elements due to the addition of Co are as 
follows. The addition of Co elements intensified the as-cast segregation, 
which increased the concentration gradient between dendritic arms and 
interdendritic regions. According to Fick ’ s law, an increase in the con -
centration gradient leads to an enhancement in the diffusion driving 
force. Moreover, studies have shown that the addition of Co reduced the 
solubility of γ’ -forming elements in the matrix. It has also been sug -
gested that the diffusion coefficient of solute atoms in the matrix is 
inversely proportional to their solubility [51] . Therefore, the smaller the 
solubility of alloying elements in the matrix, the larger their diffusion 
coefficient. The Ti and W elements are critical elements for the forma -
tion of the γ’ phase. With the addition of Co, the solubility of these el -
ements in the matrix gradually decreased, leading to an increase in their 
diffusion coefficients. Furthermore, some studies have indicated that the 
increase in Co content reduced the diffusion barrier for W, resulting in a 
higher diffusion coefficient [52] . Additionally, the increase of Co con -
tent may increase lattice distortion and reduce the interatomic dis -
tances, facilitating the diffusion of solute atoms. And the Co element 
may form weaker chemical bonds with other alloying elements, which 
decreased the binding energy of solute atoms in the lattice. The reduc -
tion in binding energy allows the solute atoms to move more easily, 
thereby enhancing the diffusion rate.
4. Conclusions
In conclusion, the influence mechanisms of Co content on the as-cast 
microstructure and homogenization behavior of Ni-Co-based superal -
loys have been elucidated in this study. It was found that Co primarily 
affected the γ’ phase in the as-cast microstructure, with minimal impact
