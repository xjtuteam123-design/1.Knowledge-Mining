abstract
Increasing service temperature of wrought Ni-based superalloy by increasing alloying de-
gree and fraction of g
0 phase has come to a bottleneck. In present work, an attempt is made
to make maximum use of carbide to strengthen Ni-based alloy with a simpler chemical
composition. Multiple morphology characterization methods and high temperature tensile
test show that the newly designed Ni(bal.)-20Cr-0.80C e12Ta (wt%) alloy can be strength-
ened by TaC and Cr-rich M
7C3 carbide and shows good ductility at 800 /C14 C. Phase trans-
formation characteristics detected by differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) and high-
temperature confocal laser scanning microscopy (CLSM) images shows that there is a
potential for dispersive precipitation of carbide in the alloy to get better mechanical
property.

1. Introduction
Ni-based superalloy is widely used in aerospace industry
attributed to good high temperature property and stability [ 1].
It is very likely that material development will continue to
push the maximum allowable service temperature of super-
alloy upwards [ 2]. However, given to the development history
of superalloy, major attempts have been made in increasing
alloying degree and g
0 phase content to obtain higher service
temperature [3]. The weight fraction of g0 phase in most up-to-
date wrought Ni-based superalloy can be higher than 50 wt%
[4]. Defects have also appeared, such as, huge expense by
addition of expensive rare metals [ 5], sacriﬁce of density,
microstructure control more difﬁcult [ 6]. Ni-based superalloy
has developed for a hundred of years and meets a bottleneck
for continuous increasing service temperature following
conventional way. Hence, it is essential to search for new
solutions beyond traditional understanding of Ni-based su-
peralloy. Attempts have been made in recent decades to try
other ways of designing metals for high temperature condi-
tion [7,8]. Joachim R€osler et al. [ 2] developed Co-Re-based alloys
in the frame of the Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft (DFG)
research group “Beyond Ni-based Superalloys ”. Research
group from Facult/C19e des Sciences et Techniques tried to modify
traditional Co-based superalloy by sufﬁcient use of carbide [ 9].
Among all the attempts, carbide strengthen metal-base
alloy seems to be a good solution. Carbon is common on
earth and carbide has been widely used in steel. Carbide is also
an important phase in wrought Ni-based superalloy, but it is

Motivated by these and based on previous investigation,

and theoretical guidance for new alloy design, which is
beyond traditional understanding of Ni-based superalloy.
2. Materials and experiments
The NiCrTa alloy investigated in present work is designed by
target composition of Ni(bal.)-20Cr-0.80C e12Ta (all in weight
fraction). A high C content is designed here for maximum
usage of carbide. The alloy was elaborated by foundry from
pure elements supplied by Trillion Metals Co.,Ltd. High fre-
quency induction furnace was used for foundry under an inert
atmosphere of pure Ar and the material was remelted 10 times
before poured into water cooled copper crucible. A solidiﬁed
ingot about 40 g was taken out from the furnace after total
cooling. The ingot was then wire-electrode cut for micro-
structure observation and property tests.
Samples for optical metallographic (OM) examination and
ﬁeld emission scanning electron microscopy (FESEM) obser-
vation were mechanically grounded and polished, then elec-
trolytically polished by a solution of 20% H

þ10 ml H 2SO4þ170 ml H 3PO4 for 5 s. OM image was taken by
Leica-DMR optical microscope. FESEM observation was carried
out on Zeiss Ultra 55 ﬁeld emission scanning electron micro-
scope. Tensile sample with dog-bone shape (the size is shown
in Fig. 2 (a)) was manufactured from the as-cast alloy and
tensile test was carried out on Instron 5582 universal testing

3. Results and discussion
Fig. 1 shows microstructure and phase characteristics of as-
cast NiCrTa alloy. It is composed of dendrites and large
amounts of dispersive carbides can be found on dendrite as
shown in Fig. 1 (a). Large blocky TaC carbide decorates on
matrix. Property diagram ( Fig. 1 (b)) calculated by JMatPro
software shows that major precipitation in the alloy is variety
of carbides. The weight fraction of carbides can sum up to
20 wt% in equilibrium state. High amount addition of C pro-
motes precipitation of carbide, which can be used as
strengthening phase. It should be noticed that the melting
temperature of MC carbide is higher than matrix and it can
precipitate directly from liquid during solidiﬁcation. Further
FESEM observation and energy disperse spectroscopy (EDS)
analysis show that interdendritic area is composed of Cr-rich
carbide particles as shown in Fig. 1(c) and (d).
To investigate mechanical property of the alloy, 800
/C14 C
tensile test with dog-bone shape sample (as shown in Fig. 2(a))
was carried out. The yield strength and tensile strength are
283.37 and 365.90 MPa with elongation of 25.8%. The alloy
shows good plasticity [ 14e16]. The fracture surface is deco-
rated with many dimples, showing ductile fracture charac-
teristics as shown in Fig. 2 (b). Fig. 2 (c) and (d) are
microstructure in the shoulder area. Skeleton and blocky TaC
carbide, together with Cr-rich interdendritic carbide, can be
found in the matrix, which is similar with the as-cast alloy
shown in Fig. 1 . Furthermore, some tiny clubbed particles
decorate in matrix after high temperature tensile test as can
be seen in Fig. 2(d) and (f). In the nearby area of fracture, cracks
can be found generated from TaC skeleton. While no void or
crack is detected on TaC carbide/matrix interface, indicating
good interfacial strength. TaC is a good strengthening phase
with high stability at high temperature [ 11,17]. The good
interfacial strength means it can play an important role in the
strengthening of NiCrTa alloy. Cr-rich interdendritic carbide
shows good deformation coordination during high tempera-
ture tensile test. The characteristics of carbides after tensile
test indicate promising effect in strengthening the alloy.

4. Conclusion
In present work, an alloy with composition of Ni(bal.)-20Cr-
0.80Ce12Ta (wt%) was designed to explore the possibility of a
new high-temperature alloy design direction. The addition of
0.8 wt% C promotes the precipitation of a variety of carbides,
which strengthens the alloy. The TaC/matrix interface shows
good interfacial strength and tiny M
7C3 carbide precipitate
dispersedly in matrix. Hence, the alloy shows good ductility
and strength at 800
/C14 C, providing a potential way for carbide-
strengthen Ni-based alloy for high temperature. This is
different from g
0 strengthened Ni-based superalloy. The
optimization microstructure has not been achieved in this
alloy system, further work is ongoing for heat treatment
optimization to get better mechanical property.
