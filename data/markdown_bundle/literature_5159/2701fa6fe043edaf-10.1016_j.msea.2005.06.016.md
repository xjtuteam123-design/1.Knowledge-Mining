Thermal shock cycling behavior of NiCoCrAlYSiB

Abstract
NiCoCrAlYSiB coatings were obtained using an arc ion plating technique on a directionally solidiﬁed Ni-base superalloy. Thermal shock
resistance of the bare and coated superalloys were investigated by cycling between high temperature (900, 1000 and 1050 ◦C) and room
temperature (forced water quenching). The microstructure evolution in coatings and the underneath substrates was studied. The results
showed that phase transformation from /H9253
′ to /H9253occurred at the surface of the coated and bare specimens due to the consumption of Al
and Ti by oxidation. A directional coarsening of the /H9253′ precipitates ( /H9253′ rafting) just beneath the /H9253′-impoverishing zone (or interdiffu-
sion zone) is observed. This kind of rafting is believed to come into being from the compressive stresses by temperature gradients due
to high cooling rate during cooldown. The relationship between microstructure evolution and cracks formation and propagation is also
discussed.

4.2. γ

A directional coarsening of the/H9253′precipitates is observed
when the materials is submitted to stress at high temperatures
or during a long period of annealing. It is driven by several
parameters: the applied stress, the temperature, the difference
between the elastic constants of the two phases and the lattice
parameter mismatch [7–10].
DSM11 possesses rather steady microstructures at high
temperatures. Fig. 8 shows the typical /H9253+ /H9253

tures in DSM11 during high temperature annealing without
external stresses. The results showed that the morphology of
the /H9253
′-cuboidal particles remained almost unchanged in this
directionally solidiﬁed Ni-base superalloy after enough time
except that the cuboidal /H9253
′particles became a little rounder.
No directional coarsening (rafting) can be detected.
In other literatures [12,19–21], it is considered that for
coated samples subsurface rafting is most likely to be pro-
duced by a combination of mechanical and chemical inter-
actions between the MCrAlY coatings and the bulk of the
superalloy. But in this test, the mechanical effect became the

the metals; the second, that between the coatings and the

compared to the metal matrix; thus, it provides negligible
mechanical constraint to the substrate. The induced stress can
be very little on the superalloys. The thermal expansion mis-
match between the coating and the underneath superalloys is
very small because they have similar chemical composition.
Therefore, this part can also be neglected. The other potential
sources of internal stresses result directly from the thermal
cycle itself. The main strain is formed due to the excessively
high cooling rates, which resulted in temperature gradients
in the specimens. The schematic illustration of the thermal
stresses in the cooled specimens is shown inFig. 9. The outer
layer is quenched to room temperature immediately and the
shrinking rate of the inner matrix is not as fast as that of the
outer layer. As a result, the surface layer provided enough
mechanical constraints on the substrates. Adversely, the outer
part experienced tensile stresses. In this study, extremely high
cooling rate is obtained by water quenching. So the outer
part of the specimens suffered rather large tensile load dur-
ing cooling. The value of the tensile stresses descended from
the surface of the specimen to the inner part because of the
decreasing temperature gradients. As for stresses during heat-
ing, the slow elevating temperature rate resulted in rather
small stresses that can be omitted compared to the stresses
during cooling.
As a result of a number of consistent experimental obser-
vations, two types of directional coarsening behavior in
[0 0 1]-oriented nickel-based single crystals have been iden-
tiﬁed [22]:
Type N: Elongated precipitates or “rafts” develop transverse
to the direction of the externally applied stress.
Type P: Elongated precipitates or “rafts” develop parallel to
the direction of the externally applied stress.
Type N behavior is usually associated with negative misﬁt
crystals stressed in tension, or positive misﬁt crystals stressed
in compression. Conversely, type P behavior is associated
with positive misﬁt crystals stressed in tension and negative
misﬁt crystals stressed in compression.
In this study, the directionally solidiﬁed Ni-superalloy
DSM11 superalloy has the similar positive lattice misﬁt value
as GTD111 superalloy for their similar composition[23], and
the orientation of this superalloy is [0 0 1]. From the rafting

4.3. Summarization of thermal shock cycling behavior
of coated specimens
According to the results in Parts I and II, we can obtain
the integrated picture of how DSM11 specimens with AIP
NiCoCrAlYSiB coatings performed during thermal shock
cycling. Fig. 10 schematically illustrated the concurrent pro-

following three aspects:
(1) On coating surface, the oxide scale failure is much

cracks formation and propagation occurred.
(2) The /H9253

5. Conclusion
From the present results, the following conclusions can be
drawn:
(1) During thermal shock cycling, the /H9253
′content in the coat-
ings decreased and the width of the interdiffusion zone
increased with the increasing of thermal cycling temper-
ature and time.
(2) The /H9253
′impoverishing and the formation and propagation
of cracks in the coating and superalloys had an internal
relationship. The cracks in coating and bare alloys were
always formed in the/H9253
′-denuded zone. The cracking and
/H9253′denuding in the metallic matrix accelerated each other.
(3) A directional coarsening of the /H9253′ precipitates just
beneath the /H9253′-impoverishing zone (interdiffusion zone)
is observed in the directionally solidiﬁed Ni-base super-
alloy for both the coated and uncoated specimens. This
kind of rafting is believed to come into being from the ten-
sile stresses by temperature gradients due to high cooling
rate during cooldown.
