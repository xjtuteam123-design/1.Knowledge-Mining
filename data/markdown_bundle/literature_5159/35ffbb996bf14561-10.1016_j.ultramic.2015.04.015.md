abstract
The analytical limitations in atom probe tomography such as resolving a desired set of atomic planes, for
solving complex materials science problems, have been overcome by employing a well-developed unique
and reproducible crystallographic technique, involving synergetic coupling of orientation microscopy
with atom probe tomography. The crystallographic information in atom probe reconstructions has been
utilized to determine the solute site occupancies in Ni – Al– Cr based superalloys accurately. The structural
information in atom probe reveals that both Al and Cr occupy the same sub-lattice within the
L1
2-ordered γ′ precipitates to form Ni 3(Al,Cr) precipitates in a Ni – 14Al– 7Cr (at%) alloy. Interestingly, the
addition of Co, which is a solid solution strengthener, to a Ni – 14Al– 7Cr alloy results in the partial reversal
of Al site occupancy within γ′ precipitates to form (Ni,Al) 3(Al,Cr,Co) precipitates. This unique evidence of
reversal of Al site occupancy, resulting from the introduction of other solutes within the ordered
structures, gives insights into the relative energetics of different sub-lattice sites when occupied by
different solutes.

1. Introduction
Nickel-base superalloys have been extensively used as materi-
als for high temperature applications because of their ability to
form coherent ordered γ′ precipitates within the face centered
cubic matrix ( γ), which enables the retention of high strength
close to their melting temperature [1]. The key factors, based on
thermodynamic and kinetic principles, for alloy development are
controlled partitioning of solute elements between the matrix ( γ)
and the precipitates ( γ′), and the solute site occupancy within the
γ′ phase. As the γ′ precipitates are coherent with the matrix, the
relative compositions of these phases determine the lattice co-
herency and subsequent high temperature microstructural chan-
ges and mechanical properties [2]. On the other hand, the site
occupancy of various solutes within the
γ′ precipitates can in ﬂu-
ence the ductility of the precipitates and the ordering energy,
which can affect the precipitation strengthening in Ni-base
superalloys [3]. Co is a main alloying element, present in many
commercially available Ni-superalloys [1]. Co has been reported to
increase the high temperature mechanical strength by reducing
the stacking fault energy of γ matrix [4]. It is also reported that
high level of Co addition can alter the partitioning behavior of
other solute atoms, preventing the formation of detrimental
phases [5].
There have been many attempts to study the sub-lattice site
occupancies of various solutes in ordered compounds using atom
probe tomography (APT) [3,6,7]. Over the last few decades, APT
has been the technique of choice for compositional analysis in
many metallic materials due to its inherent capability for the
element-speciﬁc quantitative probing at sub-nanometer scale. The
occasional high resolution in atom probe can resolve the in-
dividual lattice planes and various structural analyses in metallic
alloys including solute site occupancy in γ′ precipitates in Ni-base
superalloys have been reported earlier [3,6– 13].
The currently reported articles on advancement in atom probe
tomography for exploration of the crystallographic information,
retained in the reconstructions, are based on implementation of
algorithms like Fourier transforms and Hough transforms to the
atom probe dataset [14– 16]. These algorithms have successfully
indexed the crystallographic poles within the ion desorption im-
age, formed by accumulation of ions onto the detector, in pure

In some recent works, orientation microscopy has been cou-

21], but the use of this technique for precise site occupancy de-

and compositional information, obtained utilizing this method,
has determined the solute site occupancies in γ′ precipitates in
some Co-based superalloys [22].
In the present paper, a unique and reliable experimental ap-
proach reported by Meher et al. [23] has been employed, which
can extract crystallographic information in the APT datasets of
highly alloyed and polycrystalline superalloys by coupling or-
ientation microscopy with APT. This characterization method in-
volving the electron backscattered diffraction (EBSD) mediated
precise site-speci ﬁc sample preparation is an alternate method
enabling the crystallographic study of complex multi-phase ma-
terials, which is demonstrated in the present study of Ni – Al– Cr
and Ni – Al– Cr– Co alloys.
2. Experimental
The as-cast nominal Ni – 14Al– 7Cr (at%) and Ni – 14Al– 7Cr– 16Co
(at%) alloys were encapsulated in quartz tubes back ﬁlled with ar-
gon and were heated in a furnace at 1200 °C in the single γ phase
ﬁeld for 1 h, followed by water quenching. The homogenized
samples were encapsulated again, annealed for 256 h at 800 °Ct o
achieve near equilibrium phase compositions, and ﬁnally, water
quenched. Microstructural imaging and bulk composition de-
termination were carried out in a FEI Nova 230NanoSEM ﬁeld
emission gun scanning electron microscope equipped with back-
scatter detector. Electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) studies
were done on these samples prepared by standard metallographic
methods in a dual-beam focused ion beam (dual-beam FIB) in-
strument, the FEI FESEM NOVA 200, retro ﬁtted with EDAX Digi-
View IV EBSD camera. TSL OIM Data Collection and TSL OIM Data
Analysis 5.3 software were used for data acquisition and analyses
respectively. Since the γ′ precipitates typically exhibit a cube-on-
cube orientation relationships with the γ matrix, a γ grain close to
〈001〉 crystallographic orientation was identi ﬁed and the atom
probe samples were subsequently extracted from that region using
dual-beam FIB. APT was carried out using a local electrode atom
probe (LEAPTM 3000X) system in the voltage evaporation mode at
a temperature of 40 K, with an detection rate about 0.5 ion/pulse
and a pulse frequency of 200 kHz. Data analysis was performed
using the IVAS 3.6.6 software.
3. Results and discussion
Fig. 1 (a) shows a backscattered scanning electron microscopy
(SEM) image of a long-term annealed sample of Ni – 14Al– 7Cr (at%),
where the near spherical γ′ precipitates, with a high number
density, are uniformly dispersed in a γ matrix. The size scale of γ′
precipitates ranges from 150 to 200 nm. Fig. 1 (b) shows the pole
ﬁgure of the particular grain used for the site-speci ﬁc FIB lift-out,
that con ﬁrms an offset of 2 ° from the 〈001〉 pole. Fig. 1 (c) shows
the secondary SEM image of the atom probe tip, prior to the ﬁnal
ion-polishing step in the FIB, which shows that the alignment of
the γ/γ′ interfaces is nearly perpendicular to the axis of the tip, as
expected from the pole ﬁgure. Fig. 1(d) is the 2D desorption image
from the APT data, which exhibits a central 〈001〉 pole with four
other poles clearly exhibiting a four-fold symmetry. The appear-
ance of clear crystallographic poles in the desorption image of this
complex multi-component material, despite large differences in
the evaporation ﬁelds associated with these solute elements, is a
clear indication of a well-controlled evaporation sequence in this
experiment. Moreover, the coincidence of the position of the
central 〈001〉 pole relative to the center of the desorption image
indicates the minimal mis-orientation of the tip axis from 〈001〉
direction. Fig. 1 (e) shows the APT reconstruction, which clearly
exhibits a ﬂat horizontal γ/γ′ interface (14 at% Al iso-concentration
surface), con ﬁrming the accuracy of the proposed technique.
Fig. 2(a) shows a proximity histogram (proxigram) [24], created
using the 14 at% Al isosurface, clearly exhibiting the compositional
proﬁles for Al and Cr across the γ/γ′ interface. The summation of Al
and Cr content within the γ′ precipitates equals 25 at%. With the
assumption of an ideal stoichiometry for this L1 2 phase, the ratio
of Ni-sublattice sites (1/2,1/2,0 type) to Al-sublattice (0,0,0 type)
sites should be 3:1. Since the summation of Al and Cr equals
25 at%, the partitioning of solutes between the Ni and Al sublattice
sites appears to be such that both Al and Cr exclusively occupy the
Al-sublattice sites, while Ni atoms exclusively occupy the Ni-

sublattice sites, in the ordered γ′ precipitates. Additionally, it
should be noted that such a distribution of the Ni, Al, and Cr atoms
assumes the absence of any anti-site defects. Similar assumptions
of an ideal stoichiometry have been employed in previous APT
studies for determination of site occupancies of various solutes in
simple Ni-base superalloys [25, 26]. But, the complexity of this
problem increases in case of a non-ideal stoichiometry and in
multicomponent alloys, such as commercially available Ni base
superalloys, where there can be a strong in ﬂuence of interactions
between like and unlike solutes on the site occupancies [22, 25].
The estimation of solute site occupancies based on a simple dis-
tribution of atoms between the sub-lattice sites, using only the
composition of the γ′ phase, with absolutely no knowledge of the
solute distribution on different crystallographic planes, is most
likely to produce misleading interpretations for multi-component
superalloys. This complex problem can be accurately solved pro-
vided the crystallographic information within the γ′ precipitates is
available, permitting the individual atomic planes perpendicular to
the evaporation axis to be clearly resolved with high con ﬁdence.
Fig. 3(a) shows a schematic of the L1 2 ordered Ni3Al precipitate.
As Ni atoms occupy the face-centered position (1/2,1/2,0 type) in
the lattice, they are present on every {002} planes, while Al oc-
cupies the corner position (0,0,0 type) of the unit cell and are
therefore present on every {001} plane. Thus, the sub-lattice site
occupancies of various solute elements within γ′ precipitates can
be determined by their periodic appearance along the 〈001〉 di-
rection. As the lattice parameter of γ′ precipitates in various Ni-
base superalloys is usually close to 0.36 nm [1], the solute occu-
pying the Ni sub-lattice should appear periodically on every {002}
planes i.e. at 0.18 nm along the 〈001〉 direction. On the other hand,
the inter-atomic distance along 〈001〉 direction for solutes occu-
pying only the Al sub-lattice should be 0.36 nm. Fig. 3(b) shows an
enlarged section of the APT reconstruction of the Ni – 14Al– 7Cr
alloy, revealing the crystallographic planes perpendicular to the
〈001〉 pole within a
γ′ precipitate. In a series of atom probe ex-
periments of this alloy with variation of operating temperatures, it
was found that the stability of pure and mixed atomic planes
signiﬁcantly varies at temperature near to 60 K, which causes
degradation of depth resolution along 〈001〉 direction and the pure
atomic planes are not distinguishable from the mixed planes. But,
the ﬁeld evaporation rates of the mixed lattice planes with dif-
ferent atomic bonding and pure atomic planes with like bonding,
were nearly equal at or below 40 K, leading to a higher depth re-
solution along the 〈001〉 leading to the {002} planes being clearly
resolved. Hence, all the atom probe studies were carried out at an
operating temperature of 40 K. Additionally, the image compres-
sion factor (ICF) of all the reconstructions in this study has been
properly adjusted to match the theoretical inter-planar spacing of
the {002} planes.
In the present study, the commonly used algorithm for
constructing spatial distribution maps (SDM) for atom probe data
analysis has been employed for quantitative element speci ﬁc
probing along the 〈001〉 crystallographic direction. SDM, a deri-
vative of radial distribution function, is usually used to determine
the distribution of solute atoms in the z-direction as well as to
create a 2D map to locate atomic positions in the x– y lateral plane
[27, 28]. But, the success of SDM analysis is very much dependent
on the intrinsic atomic resolution of atom probe reconstructions.
The high resolution of atom probe datasets, obtained by utilizing
the present technique, is an ideal case for SDM analysis. Fig. 3
(c) shows the xz-SDMs corresponding to self-interaction of Ni, Al
and Cr separately. These SDMs, plotted on the plane de ﬁned by the
x- and z-axes, were created from a region of interest (ROI), placed
at the center of 〈001〉 pole and the angular offset between the ROI
and the actual lattice planes in the reconstructions was carefully
minimized so that the highest spatial resolution could be obtained
in the SDMs. The xz-SDM for Ni – Ni interactions reveals that the
minimum separation between the Ni atoms is 0.18 nm in the z-
direction. While the xz-SDMs for Al – Al and Cr – Cr interactions
reveal that the minimum separation between Al – Al or Cr – Cr
atoms is 0.36 nm.
Fig. 3(d) shows the SDMs along only the z-axis for Ni, Al and Cr
ions for better quantitative analysis, created from the same ROI.
The z-SDM conﬁ
rms the minimum Ni – Ni separation to be 0.18 nm,
conﬁrming the existence of atomic planes containing Ni in the APT
reconstruction, separated by 0.18 nm. Moreover, the intensity
variation at regular intervals in this plot can be directly interpreted
as periodic change in the density of Ni on alternating {002} planes
along the 〈001〉 direction. On the other hand, the z-SDM reveals
that the minimum Al – Al separation is 0.36 nm and the atomic
density on every plane almost the same. The z-SDM for Cr is al-
most identical to that of Al and the minimum Cr – Cr separation is
also 0.36 nm. This con ﬁrms that the Cr atoms have a strong ten-
dency for occupying the Al sub-lattice, which also substantiates
the previous reports of Cr site occupancy in Ni-base superalloys by
various experimental and computational studies [25, 29]. There-
fore, the stoichiometry of the ordered γ′ phase in this alloy is
Ni3(Al,Cr).
The site-speciﬁc sample preparation of Ni – 14Al– 7Cr– 16Co alloy
was carried out in a manner identical to that employed for the Ni –
14Al– 7Cr alloy. Fig. 4 (a) shows the pole ﬁgure of the particular
grain, which was used for atom probe sample preparation. The
pole ﬁgure con ﬁrms that the surface normal of the selected grain
is close to the 〈001〉 direction with an offset of 5 °. Fig. 4 (b) shows
the atomic planes of Al ions perpendicular to the 〈001〉 pole within
a γ′ precipitate. Fig. 4(c) shows the proxigram, created using 13 at%
Al isosurface, across the interface between γ and γ′ phases. The
proxigram indicates that since the Ni content within γ′ is about
65 at%, it is likely that this phase is non-stoichiometric.
Fig. 5 shows z-SDMs for individual atomic interactions within
the γ′ precipitates for the Ni – 14Al– 7Cr– 16 Co (at%) alloy. The
periodic variation in the density of Ni ions on every alternating
plane, separated by 0.18 nm distance, correlates well with the
basic L12 structure. The z-SDM of Al – Al interactions reveals that Al
is present on both Al-sublattice sites (0,0,0 type) and the Ni-sub-
lattice sites (1/2,1/2,0 type) in the ordered γ′ phase. Consequently,
Al is present on both pure and mixed atomic planes of γ′ pre-
cipitates in this particular alloy leading to a Al – Al minimum in-
terplanar separation of 0.18 nm. The signi ﬁcant amount of ionic
density of Al in the Ni rich atomic planes is a true experimental
indicator of elemental distribution in this alloy and does not ap-
pear to be associated with any of the known atom probe artifacts,
considering the high spatial resolution of this dataset. The den-

4. Conclusions
This paper applies a coupled EBSD-assisted dual-beam FIB
technique for site and orientation-speci ﬁc APT sample preparation
with the objective of determining the site occupancies of various
solute elements in a multi-component Ni-base superalloy. Using
this technique, the site occupancies of all relevant solutes within
the L1 2-γ′ precipitates in a ternary Ni – 14Al– 7Cr (at%) alloy and a
quaternary Ni – 14Al– 7Cr– 16Co (at%) alloy have been determined
and the following conclusions can be drawn:
1. The current approach, coupling EBSD-assisted FIB based sample
preparation with APT analysis, substantially enhances the
possibility of resolving the desired set of atomic planes in the
APT reconstruction in a reproducible manner.
2. In case of the Ni – 14Al– 7Cr alloy, Al and Cr occupy the same
sub-lattice within the γ′ phase, leading to the stoichiometry of
Ni3(Al,Cr).
3. In case of Ni – 14Al– 7Cr– 16Co alloy, the substantial amount of
Co addition in ﬂuences the Al site occupancy, with Al occupying
both sub-lattices of the L1 2-ordered γ′ phase, in this alloy.
Furthermore, both Cr and Co atoms occupy only one type of
sub-lattice site in this phase. The resulting γ′ stoichiometry is
(Ni,Al)3(Al,Cr,Co).
The use of EBSD-assisted FIB-based sample preparation can
substantially enhance the reliability of crystallographic informa-
tion obtained from APT reconstructions. As shown in this paper,
this technique has great potential in accurately determining the
site occupancies of solute atoms within the ordered precipitates in
complex multi-component alloy systems, and is therefore ex-
pected to have an impact on further development of ordered
precipitate strengthened metallic systems.
