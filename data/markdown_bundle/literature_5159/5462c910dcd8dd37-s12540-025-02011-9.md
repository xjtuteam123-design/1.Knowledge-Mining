Microstructure and Mechanical Properties of  Al0.5CoCrFeNi 
High‑Entropy Alloy Reinforced 6063 Aluminum Matrix Composites

Abstract
In this study, composite materials featuring various high entropy alloy contents and different sintering temperatures were 
prepared. The impact of process parameters on the microstructure, mechanical properties, strengthening mechanisms and 
fracture modes of the composite materials was investigated. The results show that with the increase of HEA particles and the 
sintering temperatures, these composites exhibit mechanical properties that are superior to those of the unreinforced 6063 
aluminum alloy. The comprehensive properties first increased and then slightly decreased. The properties were obviously 
optimized when the volume fraction of the HEA particles reached 7% and the sintering temperature reached 530 °C, with a 
tensile strength of 224.3 MPa and a tensile fracture elongation of 14.3%. The fractures of composite materials were mainly 
ductile fractures. The main strengthening methods for the composite materials were Hall–Petch strengthening and dislocation 
strengthening, by fitting the bivariate strength calculation model, it is found that the yield strength of the composite material 
can reach 623.6 MPa under ideal conditions.
Keywords Aluminum matrix composites · High entropy alloys · Spark plasma sintering · Microstructure · Mechanical 
properties
1 Introduction
Aluminum matrix composite materials are lightweight, pos-
sess high specific stiffness, have excellent high temperature 
performance, and exhibit good wear resistance and corrosion 
resistance. They have a wide range of applications in fields 
such as the automotive industry, aerospace, and electronic 
packaging. 6063 aluminum alloy, as one of the widely used 
aluminum matrix materials, has numerous uses in the area of 
construction profiles, irrigation tubes, and vehicle manufactur-
ing due to its good plasticity, moderate heat-treatment strength, 
excellent welding performance, and the gorgeous color after 
anodic oxidation treatment [1–3]. To further enhance the com-
prehensive mechanical properties of 6063 aluminum alloy, 
the introduction of reinforcing materials has become an effec-
tive strategy. Among them, particulate reinforced aluminum 
matrix composites have advantages such as good stiffness, 
strong fatigue resistance, excellent heat resistance and good 
machinability. In recent years, ceramic particles and metallic 
glasses are widely utilized as reinforcement materials [4–6]. 
The conventional ceramic particles exhibit poor wettability 
and a mismatch in thermal expansion coefficients with the alu-
minum matrix, making them prone to fragmentation or detach-
ment at the interface. Consequently, the composites produced 
display subpar plastic toughness [7, 8], the existing solutions 
are complex and costly. Although good interfacial bonding can 
be ensured when metal glass is used as reinforcement, metal 
glass has high brittleness and low crystallization temperature, 
poor interfacial stability during high-temperature sintering and 
poor sintering quality during low-temperature sintering [9, 10].

The appearance of high-entropy alloy (HEA) reinforcement 
solves the above problems. HEA is an alloy featuring multiple 
major elements made up of five or more metals, with each 
element's atomic percentage ranging from 5 to 35% [11, 12]. 
HEA has attracted much attention for its excellent mechanical 
properties, corrosion resistance and thermal stability. HEA has 
four primary effects, which are high entropy effect, hysteresis 
diffusion effect, lattice distortion effect and cocktail effect [13, 
14]. The introduction of high-entropy alloy as reinforcement 
into 6063 aluminum alloy aims to take advantage of their 
unique properties to enhance the overall performance of the 
composites, especially for applications in extreme environ-
ments. Spark plasma sintering (SPS) as a cutting-edge powder 
metallurgy technology, uses pulsed direct current and pressure 
to rapidly solidify metal and alloy powders, which can effec-
tively inhibit the growth of grains and improve the densities 
and properties of materials [15–17].
Wang et al. [18] incorporated FeNiCrCoAl3 HEA as rein-
forcement to optimize 2024 aluminum matrix composites. 
The compressive strength of the prepared composites was 
about 710 MPa. And the appearance of α(Al) phase and the 
reinforcement of FeNiCrCoAl3 particles endowed the mate-
rial with high compressive strength, and the strength was also 
related to the precipitated second phase Al2Cu and the Fe-rich 
phase. Lu et al. [19] selected CoNiFeAl0.4Ti0.6Cr0.5 HEA as 
reinforcing material and examined the influence of HEA on 
the characteristics of 7075Al composites, the high-entropy 
alloy exhibited excellent bonding at interfaces with the matrix 
and the suitable dislocation densities. This led to an increased 
modulus of elasticity, as well as enhancements in the strength 
and plasticity of the HEA/7075Al composites when compared 
to the SiC/7075Al composites, with average values recorded at 
171 GPa, 712 MPa, and 0.82%, respectively. Yuan et al. [20] 
created diffusion layers between Al0.5CoCrFeNi HEA and the 
pure titanium matrix using the spark plasma sintering method. 
The development of diffusion layers enhanced the hardness 
and Young's modulus of the composites, and found appropri-
ately increasing the sintering temperature was beneficial to 
enhance the overall mechanical properties of the materials. 
Zhang et al. [21] added different contents of HEAps (0.5, 1.5, 
3.0 and 5.0 wt%), the sample's hardness rose significantly from 
82.4 HV0.2 (AA2219) to 120.5 HV (5.0 wt% HEA/AA2019). 
The incorporation of cemented carbide significantly inhibited 
plastic deformation on contact surfaces while improving the 
wear resistance of the composites. However, too much HEA 
particles would produce aggregation phenomenon, which can 
reduce the hardness of the materials, aggravates the delamina-
tion of the wear interface and particle shedding.
Compared with high strength aluminum alloys (such as 
7075Al or 2024Al), 6063 aluminum alloy has lower initial 
strength, but its strength can be significantly improved by 
reinforcing with HEA particles. Meanwhile, this approach 
avoids the interfacial compatibility issues caused by the 
brittleness inherent in high strength aluminum alloys 
[22–24]. Additionally, the small difference in thermal expan-
sion coefficient between Al0.5CoCrFeNi particles and the 
6063 matrix is conducive to reducing stress concentration 
at the interface and promoting uniform strengthening [25, 
26]. When using Al-rich AlCoCrFeNi particles as rein-
forcements, brittle intermetallic compounds tend to form, 
while CoCrFeMnNi particles containing Mn easily cause 
grain boundary segregation. The Al0.5CoCrFeNi HEA has 
attracted attention due to its unique duplex phase structure, 
in which the FCC phase optimizes plasticity coordination 
ability and the BCC phase contributes to enhancing material 
strength [27, 28]. Therefore, selecting Al0.5CoCrFeNi HEA 
as the reinforcement and 6063 aluminum alloy as the matrix 
material enables the preparation of composites that integrate 
both plasticity and strength.
In this study, Al0.5CoCrFeNi HEA reinforced 6063 alu-
minum matrix composites were fabricated using the spark 
plasma sintering process, and investigate the effects of 
the high-entropy alloy content and sintering temperature 
on the microstructure, elemental distribution, mechani-
cal performance and reinforcement mechanism of the 
 Al0.5CoCrFeNi/6063Al materials. Exploring the effect of 
fracture modes and each strengthening mechanism on the 
mechanical properties of composites and the percentage 
of strengthening. A bivariate strength prediction model for 
HEA reinforced aluminum matrix composites was estab-
lished, aiming to obtain aluminum matrix composites with 
excellent comprehensive properties and predict the yield 
strength values of composites under ideal conditions, so as 
to meet the requirements of specific engineering applications 
and provide reference significance for related research.

2.2 Experimental Methods
2.2.1 Preparation of  (Al0.5CoCrFeNi)p/6063Al Composites
Calculate the mass of high-entropy alloy powder and 6063 
aluminum alloy powder, weigh the powder, in which HEA 
particles accounted for 1%, 4%, 7% and 10% respectively. 
The powder was mixed and mechanically alloyed using a 
planetary ball mill with a ball milling process of 80 min. 
The mixed powders were loaded into graphite molds and 
started to spark plasma sintering under vacuum conditions, 
with volume fractions of 1%, 4%, 7%, 10% of HEA parti-
cles when keeping the sintering temperature at a constant 
of 530 °C, and 480 °C, 530 °C, 580 °C when keeping the 
HEA particles volume fraction constant at 7%. The sintering 
pressure was 30 MPa, the heating rate was 100 °C/min, and 
the holding time was 6 min to obtain a cylindrical specimen 
of Φ 30 × 20 mm, which was prepared as shown in Fig. 2.

3 Results and Analyses
3.1 XRD Analysis
Figure 3 shows the XRD patterns of Al0.5CoCrFeNi/6063Al 
composites with different high-entropy alloy volume frac-
tions and different sintering temperatures. From Fig.  3a, b, 
it can be seen that four different phases may exist in the 
composites, which are Al0.5CoCrFeNi phase, 6063Al phase, 
 Al13Co4 type phase and Al9Co2 type phase. Combined with 
the results of elemental analysis in Sect.  3.4, the molar 
ratios of Al atoms to other atoms such as Co, Cr, Fe, Ni 
and others in the interface layer between the HEA parti-
cles and the 6063 aluminum ranged from 3.25 to 4.5, so 
in the region of the interface layer new phases were gener -
ated, including the Al13Co4 type intermetallic compounds 
 (Al13(CoCrFeNiMg) 4) and Al9Co2 type intermetallic 
compounds (Al9(CoCrFeNiMg) 2). The formation of these 
two intermetallic compounds can be attributed to the sub-
stitution of Co atoms in Al13Co4 and Al9Co2 by other atoms 
[29, 30].
With the increase of the volume fraction of high entropy 
alloy particles, the diffraction peak intensity of 6063Al 
gradually decreases, and the diffraction peak intensities of 
 Al13Co4 type phase and Al9Co2 type phase increase with 
the increase of the volume fraction of high-entropy alloy. 
When the sintering temperature was increased from 480 to 
580 °C, the interface layer between the high-entropy alloy 
and the 6063 aluminum matrix became thicker, the pre -
cipitated intermetallic compounds increased, and the dif-
fraction peak intensities of Al13Co4 type phase and Al9Co2 
type phase increased. The increase of the volume fraction 
of high-entropy alloy and the increase of SPS sintering 
temperature make the diffusion of different elements in the 
high-entropy alloy particles to the matrix intensify. Due to 
the different diffusion speeds and distances, the arrangement 
of the atoms in the lattice changed, and the 6063 aluminum 
matrix produced lattice distortion to different degrees, which 
resulted in the diffraction peaks of the various phases shifted 
to the right with a range of 1°–2°.

3.3 The Density and Relative Density of Composites
Table  3 shows the density parameters of 
 Al0.5CoCrFeNi/6063Al composites with different volume 
fractions of reinforcement, sintered at 530 °C. Due to the 
discharge plasma sintering process characteristics and the 
lubrication effect of the generated interfacial layer, the 
density of the composites is overall high. From the data in 
the table, it is found that the densities of the composites 
are decreasing with the increase of the volume fraction 
of high entropy alloy (HEA). When the volume fraction 
of HEA is 10%, the density of the composite is 97.5%, 
which is due to the agglomeration phenomenon of the high 
entropy alloy particles. Agglomeration affects the forming 
and sintering behaviors of the powders and leads to the 
non-uniform distribution of the reinforcement within the 
material, which reduces the density of the composite and 
also affects the mechanical properties of the composite.
Table  4 shows the density of Al0.5CoCrFeNi/6063Al 
composites with a HEA volume fraction of 7% at different 
sintering temperatures with HEA volume fraction of 7%. 
Due to the characteristics of low voltage and high current 
in the spark plasma sintering process, the powder is rap-
idly sintered and densified, the density of the composite is 
relatively high. The higher the sintering temperature, the 
fuller the element diffusion, and the closer the HEA particles 
are bonded to the matrix. When the sintering temperature 
increased from 480 to 580 °C, the density of the composites 
increased from 97 to 98.4%.

helping the powder to bond more tightly under mechanical 
pressure, promoting densification of the composites. As 
shown in Fig.  9b, the Vickers hardness of the compos-
ite increases from 65.88 HV0.2 (480 °C) to 80.76 HV0.2 
(580 °C) when the sintering temperature increases because 
the interface layer is generated by mutual diffusion of the 
high-entropy alloy and the matrix, and the thickness of 
the interface layer increases with the increase of the sin-
tering temperature. The highest hardness at a sintering 
temperature of 580 °C is due to severe interfacial reactions 
between the high-entropy alloy and the matrix. At this 
temperature, all elements of the high-entropy alloy diffuse 
into the matrix to form a solid solution, and the precipita-
tion of intermetallic compounds increases. The Vickers 
hardness of a pure 6063 aluminum alloy sample prepared 
by the same process is only 56.74 HV0.2 (530 °C). By 
adding HEA reinforcement and changing the sintering 
temperature, the hardness of the composites increased by 
11.3%–42.3%, and the effect of sintering temperature on 
the hardness of the composites was greater than that pro-
duced by the volume fraction of the reinforcement.

5 Conclusion
The Al0.5CoCrFeNi/6063Al composites were prepared by 
the spark plasma sintering process. The microstructure, 
elemental distribution, mechanical properties, strengthen-
ing mechanisms, and fracture mechanisms of the composite 
material under different high-entropy alloy contents and dif-
ferent sintering temperatures were investigated.
1. As the HEA content increases, the particles are distrib-
uted more evenly. HEA can slow down the flow of the 
material during sintering and forming, thereby increas-
ing the material's density. However, when there are an 
excessive number of reinforcing particles, agglomera-
tion occurs, which is detrimental to the comprehensive 
properties of the composite material.
2. The sintering temperature affects the thickness of the dif-
fusion layer between the HEA particles and the matrix. 
The interface thickness of the composite material is 
approximately 2–10 μm. When the sintering temperature 
is 530 °C, the composite material exhibits the optimal 
comprehensive mechanical properties. Its microhardness 
is 75.9 HV0.2, Young's modulus is 107.8 GPa, tensile 
strength is 224.3 MPa, and plasticity is 14.3%.
3. Based on the experimental results and theoretical formu-
las, a bivariate mathematical model for calculating the 
HEA reinforced 6063 aluminum matrix composite was 
fitted. According to the model results, under ideal con-
ditions, the theoretical yield strength of this composite 
material can reach 623.6 MPa, with a minimum value 
of approximately 93.5 MPa.
4. Hall–Petch strengthening and dislocation strength-
ening are the main strengthening mechanisms of the 
 Al0.5CoCrFeNi/6063Al composite material. The con-
