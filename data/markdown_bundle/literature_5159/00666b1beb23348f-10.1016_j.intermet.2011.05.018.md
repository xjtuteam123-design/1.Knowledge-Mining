abstract
In this paper, we report a signi ﬁcant improvement in mechanical properties of near eutectic Nb eSi alloys
by addition of Gallium (Ga) and control of microstructural length scale. A comparative study of two alloys
Nb-18.79 at.%Si and Nb-20.2 at.%Si e2.7 at.%Ga were carried out. The microstructure re ﬁnements were
carried out by vacuum suction casting in water cooled thick copper mold. It is shown that addition of Ga
suppresses Nb3Si phase and promotes b-Nb5Si3 phase. The microstructural length scale and in particular
eutectic spacing reduces signi ﬁcantly to 50 e100 nm in suction cast ternary alloys. Compression test
shows a strength of 2.8 /C6 0.1 GPa and plasticity of 4.3 /C6 0.03%. In comparison, the binary Nb-18.79 at.%Si
alloy processed under identical conditions exhibit coarser length scale (300 e400 nm) and brittle
behavior. The fracture toughness of Ga containing suction cast alloy shows a value of 24.1 1 /C6 0.5 MPaOm
representing a major improvement for bulk Nb eSi eutectic alloy.

1. Introduction
Intermetallic compounds based on refractory metals such as Cr,
Nb, Mo and W with high melting points ( >170 0/C14 C) are promising
for high temperature applications. Among these, silicides and alu-
minides have attracted industrial attention. However, many of
these materials are brittle in nature. Hence, successful imple-
mentation of these materials needs improvement in their tough-
ness both at room temperature as well as at elevated temperatures.
Among all the Nb, V and Cr based intermetallics, the ones belonging
to Nb eSi system have shown greater promise because of there
prospective future applications in gas turbine and aircraft engines
as a replacement of current Ni based super alloys [1,2]. Nb rich
NbeSi alloys have low density (6.6 e7.2 gm/cm
3) and high
temperature oxidation resistance [3,4]. At Nb rich end, the binary
NbeSi phase diagram [5] contains equilibrium low temperature
phase a-Nb5Si3, which on heating transforms to a high temperature
phase, b-Nb5Si3 depending on the composition. The crystallo-
graphic details of these phases are given in Table 1. The b-Nb5Si3
can also form directly from Nb rich melt during solidi ﬁcation. This
phase can react with residual melt to yield another phase Nb 3Si
through a peritectic reaction. The phase diagram also exhibits
a eutectic reaction between Nb solid solution and Nb
3Si. There
exists inconsistency in the literature about the exact location of the
eutectic point in the Nb eSi system. Bewlay et al. have observed
eutectic composition to be at 18.2 at.%Si for directionally solidi ﬁed
alloy [6], where as Massalski et al. have reported the eutectic point
at composition of 18.7 at.%Si [5]. The thermodynamic calculation,
by Schlesinger et al. on the other hand have indicated that the
eutectic exists at 17.5 at. %Si [7].
The binary Nb eGa phase diagram indicates that Ga has very
limited solubility ( <1 at.%) in Nb at room temperature [5]. The
solubility increases with increasing temperature. The Nb rich end in
the phase diagram shows formation of Nb
3Ga and Nb 5Ga3 phases.
The crystal structure of Nb 5Ga3 phase is identical to b-Nb5Si3.O n
the other hand Nb 3Ga is similar to Nb 3Si however, it has space
group P4 2/n. Table 1 summarizes the crystallographic information
of the phases in Nb eGa system. The Si eGa phase diagrams also
indicates negligible solid solubility of Ga in (Si), with a maximum
solubility of 0.1 at.% Ga near 1200
/C14 C [5].
The mechanical properties of Nb rich Nb eSi alloys were
experimentally studied by several investigators [2,8e12]. These
alloys are in generally brittle primarily due to the presence of sili-
cide phases. Reports of attempts, to improve the room temperature
strength along with ductility of the eutectic alloys are available
[2,8e12]. These studies indicate that by controlling the micro-
structure, it may be possible to improve the mechanical properties
of these alloys. The control on microstructure can be achieved by
controlling the growth velocity, thermal gradient and the alloy
composition during processing. Bendersky et al. [13].h a v e

2. Experimental details
We have prepared two set of alloys, a binary of Nb-18.7 at.% Si
located at Nb rich side (eutectic composition) and a ternary alloy
composition of Nb-16.5 at.%Sie4 at.%Ga using high purity elements
(Nb (99.9%), Si (99.999%) and Ga (99.9%)). As cast ingots were
prepared in a water cooled Cu hearth using an arc melting setup
operated in the presence of puri ﬁed Ar gas atmosphere. The alloys
were remelted ﬁve times to achieve complete homogenization. As
cast alloys were further remelted and suction cast in a water cooled
copper mold to obtain rods of 3 mm diameter. The composition
analysis was initially carried out using a ﬁeld emission scanning
electron microscope equipped with EDS detector (Sirion make, FEI).
The composition analysis of the individual phases con ﬁrmed using
the EDS attached to a ﬁeld emission transmission electron micro-
scope (Tecnai F-30) in thin ﬁlm mode and using a ﬁeld emission
electron microprobe (EMPA, JEOL make). For bulk composition,
analyses were carried out over at least ten large regions in different
samples. For binary alloys the analysis yield a composition of Nb-
18.79 /C6 0.6 at.%Si.
Our analysis indicates the loss of 1.3 at.% of Ga during melting
process of Ga added alloy. The ﬁnal average composition from ten
samples each of the two alloys are given in Table 2. The processed
samples were characterized by SEM, TEM, and X-ray diffraction to
obtain compositional, morphological and structural information.
EPMA with nanometer probe size is also used to determine the
exact local composition of primary as well as eutectic regions in the
microstructure. The X-ray powder scan was carried out at a step
size of 0.01

Fig. 1 shows the X-ray diffraction patterns obtained from the as

The as cast and suction cast samples were cut through trans-

typical SEM micrographs (using secondary detectors) of the as
cast ingot and 3 mm diameter suction cast rod of binary alloy Nb-
18.79 at.%Si are shown in Fig. 2 (a) and (c). The microstructures in

compared to 300 e400 nm for Nb-18.79 at.%Si eutectic (shown in
the inset of Fig. 2 (c, d)).
As we have observed the loss of Ga during melting, at least three
samples of each alloy were melted and suction cast to verify the

each measurement the probe current was adjusted to achieve dead
time between 20 and 30%. The peak positions were calibrated using
pure metals at different operating conditions before analyzing the
alloys. In order to obtain good statistic, the compositions were
measured at more than 10 different places with more than one
operating conditions.

3Si phase

This leads to shifts in composition of the remaining liquid enabling
it to enter the eutectic couple zone between Nb solid solution (Nbss)
and Nb 3Si. Our observation did not reveal any eutectoid structure
consisting of Nb ss and a-Nb5Si3. Clearly the kinetics of this solid
state diffusion reaction in sluggish and can easily be suppressed. In
contrast, Ga addition suppresses the formation of the Nb
3Si phase
and promotes nucleation of b-Nb5Si3 phase in alloys where Nb 3Si
phase is otherwise expected. The effect is seen in both arc melted as
well as suction cast samples emphasizing the role of Ga addition in
promoting this phase. Ga belongs to the same group as Al and is
expected behave in similar fashion. There are reports that Al
addition also stabilizes b-Nb5Si3 phase [16].
The different alloying elements behave differently in Nb eSi
system depending on crystal structure and phases stability. The
elements such as Ti, V, Al, Ge, and Hf partition to Nb 5Si3, where Ti, V
substitutes Nb in Nb5Si3 and Ge replaces Si. On other hand Al and Hf
goes in intermetallic solution [18,21e25]. The alloying elements

[18,21e25]. The B effect is entirely different than both of the cate-
gories. It goes into Silicides. All the above alloying elements
certainly changes lattice parameter depending on their solubility
[26]. The solubility of alloying elements depends on processing
parameter and heat treatment. The lattice parameter calculated

b-Nb5Si3. A very ﬁne probe current (1 nA) EDS attached to TEM

The analysis of the compositions of the primary and eutectic

during primary solidiﬁcation, the liquid in front of primary phase is

Nbss and b-Nb5Si3 in the eutectic (obtained by STEM mapping of the

implies that the diffusion coupling of the two eutectic phases

(ii) The diffusion of Nb and Si in the liquid is affected by the

Utilizing thermodynamic analysis, Abbaschian et al. have
explored the possible metastable extensions of the phases in Nb eSi

the absence of Nb3Si contains the Nbss-b-Nb5Si3 eutectic. Therefore,
suppression of Nb 3Si phase due to Ga addition leads to the devel-
opment of phases following the metastable phase diagram.

5. Conclusion
The present work reports the effect of addition of Ga and
subsequent non-equilibrium solidi ﬁcation processing on the
microstructure and properties of hypereutectic Nb eSi alloy. The
following are the major conclusions.
1. Addition of Ga suppresses the formation of Nb
3Si phase and
stabilize the b-Nb5Si3 phase. The ternary alloy Nb-20.2 at.%
Sie2.7 at.%Ga on suction casting yield dendrite-ultra ﬁne
eutectic (Nb ss-b-Nb5Si3) composite with nanometer length
scale.
2. The combination of these ultra ﬁne eutectic and primary
b-Nb5Si3 results in a high strength composite ( w2 GPa of
Ultimate Strength) with 4.3% plasticity. In contrast the binary
hypereutectic alloy (Nb-18.79 at.%Si) under identical process-
ing condition shows a strength 1.35 GPa and 0.2% plasticity. The
Ga addition also enhances the fracture toughness from
9.2 /C6 0.05 MPaOm to 24.1 /C6 0.5 MPaOm.
