ABSTRACT
We introduced an innovative methodology employing the high shearing dispersion technique (HSDT) to uni -
formly distribute Zr particles, creating a heterogeneous structure with more and finer fine-grained regions after 
extrusion, which enhanced the strength and ductility synergistically in Mg-3Gd-1Zr (VK31) alloys. Compared to 
non-high sheared VK31 (NHS-VK31) alloy, the high sheared VK31 (HS-VK31) alloy exhibited a 30 MPa increase 
in yield strength and a 7.9 % improvement in elongation. HSDT applied before solidification effectively dispersed 
and refined Zr particles, suppressed dynamic recrystallization nucleation and growth during extrusion, which 
contributing to the development of heterogeneous structures. The finer average grain size of the HS-VK31 alloy 
(3.64 μ m) was a key factor in the strength increase compared to the NHS-VK31 alloy (8.09 μ m). During defor -
mation of the HS-VK31 alloy, the more numerous and finer grains in fine-grained regions created favorable 
conditions for the activation of < c + a > dislocations, which were easily decomposed into partial dislocations 
( < c > dislocations and < a > dislocations) and stacking faults. The activation of high-density non-basal disloca -
tions and stacking faults in the fine-grained zones was primarily responsible for the improvement of ductility. 
Additionally, the more dispersed and finer Zr particles in HS-VK31 alloy could effectively alleviate stress con -
centration, preventing the deterioration of ductility. This work presented an advanced design paradigm for 
heterogeneous structures, providing novel insights and perspectives for the evolution of strength-ductility syn -
ergy in alloy development.
1. Introduction
Amid the growing emphasis on green transportation, lightweighting 
in the transportation sector has emerged as an essential strategy to 
mitigate energy consumption [1] . Magnesium (Mg) alloys, being the 
lightest structural materials, offer considerable potential for advanced 
applications in the automotive and aerospace domains [2,3] . However, 
the difficulty in attaining a high strength-ductility synergy in Mg alloys 
continues to pose a major challenge for researchers [4,5] . The poor 
ductility of Mg alloys can be attributed to the insufficient number of slip 
systems and the restrictive plastic deformation mechanisms inherent in 
their hexagonal close-packed (HCP) crystal structure [6] . The predom -
inant deformation mode of Mg at room temperature is basal < a > slip, 
which is easily activated due to its relatively low critical resolved shear 
stress (CRSS) [7,8] . Nonetheless, basal < a > slip provides only two 
independent slip systems, which do not satisfy the Von-Mises yield cri -
terion and cannot accommodate strain along the c-axis [7] . Tensile 
twinning as another important deformation mode, has a relatively low 
CRSS and facilitates limited ductility along the c-axis. Non-basal slip 
facilitates c-axis strain accommodation but has a much higher CRSS than 
basal slip, this prevents its activation at room temperature and greatly 
limits the ductility of Mg alloys [7,9 – 11] . To enhance strength, re -
searchers typically employ strategies such as the design of ultra-fine 
grains, high-density precipitates, or the development of strong basal 
textures [12 – 14] . However, high strength is frequently attained at the 
expense of ductility [15] . Consequently, the development of approaches 
to concurrently improve both strength and ductility has become a crit -
ical focus.
In recent studies, substantial efforts have been focused on improving 
the strength-ductility synergy of Mg alloys, with experimental findings

particles post-extrusion [21,22] . This implies that the size and distri -

heterogeneous structures.
As an effective grain refiner, Zr exhibits low solubility in Mg and 
predominantly exists as Zr particles in Mg alloys. Standard techniques 
for incorporating Zr into Mg melts typically involve the use of com -
mercial Mg-Zr master alloys [23] . Unfortunately, Zr particles in these 
alloys are typically large and prone to aggregation [24] . A number of 
studies have sought to refine the size of Zr particles in Mg-Zr master 
alloys through techniques such as equal channel angular extrusion 
(ECAE) [25] , friction stir processing (FSP) [26] , and tungsten inert gas 
arc re-melting (TIGR) [24] . However, the implementation of these 
pre-treatment methods adds complexity to the industrial production 
process of Mg alloys, thereby significantly lowering efficiency. Recently, 
Fan et al. [27 – 29] developed a high shearing dispersion technique 
(HSDT) that is applied before solidification, effectively achieving uni -
form dispersion of solute particles, reducing particle size, and improving 
the chemical homogeneity of the material. Kandemir et al. [30] pro -
duced relatively uniform recycled carbon fiber via HSDT, significantly 
improving the mechanical properties of AZ91 alloy. Numerous studies 
have demonstrated that HSDT, which is widely applicable, effectively 
disperses particles [31,32] . Therefore, it is reasonable to conclude that 
HSDT can be used to disperse and refine Zr particles, enabling greater 
control over heterogeneous structures.
In this work, we applied HSDT during the casting process to disperse 
and refine Zr particles, followed by extrusion processing to prepare a 
heterogeneous Mg-Gd-Zr alloy structure, resulting in synergistic im -
provements in both strength and ductility. We systematically investi -
gated the impact of Zr particle distribution on the formation 
mechanisms of heterogeneous structures and comprehensively analyzed 
the intrinsic mechanisms by which these structures enhance both 
strength and ductility. This study offers a novel approach for designing 
heterogeneous structures and provides technical insights for addressing 
the strength-ductility trade-off in Mg alloys.

4. Discussion
4.1. The formation mechanism of heterogeneous structure
The development of heterogeneous structures is fundamentally 
governed by alloy composition and the processes of nucleation and 
growth of dynamically recrystallized (DRXed) grains during thermo -
mechanical processing [17] . Zr, with its limited solubility and lack of 
ability to form secondary phases with Mg or Gd, is predominantly pre -
sent as discrete particles within the matrix. As shown in Fig. 4 , regions 
with higher concentrations of Zr particles exhibit finer grain structures, 
whereas other regions show coarser grains, where Zr particles are less 
abundant. These observations indicate that Zr particles have a signifi -
cant role in modulating the nucleation and growth of DRXed grains. The 
DRX mechanism can be mainly categorized into continuous DRX (CDRX) 
and discontinuous DRX (DDRX). The CDRX through dislocation rear -
rangement forms subgrains, and low angle grain boundaries (LAGBs) 
continuously absorb dislocations to eventually form high angle grain 
boundaries (HAGBs), resulting in the formation of new DRXed grains. In 
contrast, DDRX is a conventional DRX process that nucleates on jagged 
HAGBs and achieves grain growth by GB migration. Particle stimulated 
nucleation (PSN) is commonly observed to contribute to DRXed 
behavior in thermo-mechanical processes, but PSN usually requires that 
the particle size is larger than 1 μ m [33] . Given that the Zr particles in 
this study are typically smaller than this size threshold, they do not act as 
effective promoters of DRX. Instead, fine Zr particles are believed to 
significantly impede DRXed behavior. Fine Zr particles are effective in 
blocking the migration of both LAGB and HAGB, thus severely inhibiting 
the DRX process both CDRX and DDRX [34] . Additionally, Zr particles 
are known to pin grain boundaries, thereby impeding grain growth 
during the grain growth process. In regions where Zr particles are ab -
sent, nucleation and grain growth proceed without impediment, 
resulting in typical grain growth behavior. However, in regions with a 
high concentration of Zr particles, DRX and grain growth are severely 
hindered, while regions without Zr particles exhibit normal nucleation 
and growth. This disparity in grain growth rates leads to differences in 
grain size, contributing to the formation of a heterogeneous structure. 
The use of HSDT, with its superior ability to uniformly disperse particles, 
ensures that the as-cast HS-VK31 alloy has a more consistent Zr particle 
distribution. This uniform distribution results in a more dispersed, 
stream-like Zr particle zone during extrusion, promoting a higher pro -
portion of fine-grained areas. Additionally, the Zr particles can effec -
tively limit the growth of DRXed grains, meaning that the dispersed 
structure in the HS-VK31 alloy impedes grain coarsening more effec -
tively, thereby achieving a finer average grain size.
In terms of texture, both fine and coarse grains in the NHS-VK31 
alloy exhibit RE texture, while in the HS-VK31 alloy, only the coarse 
grains show RE texture, the fine-grained region displays a fiber texture 
with [10 10] planes parallel to the ED. Previous studies have pointed out 
that the formation of RE textures is mainly due to the PSN mechanism 
but required the alloy with coarse secondary phases at high RE content 
[35] . Considering that the PSN mechanism is not satisfied in the present 
study, this factor was excluded from the analysis. The element of Gd in

increased by 30 MPa compared to the NHS-VK31 alloy after the HSDT.

properties of Mg alloys include grain boundary strengthening, solid 
solution strengthening, texture strengthening and precipitation 
strengthening [39,40] . Considering that the NHS-VK31 and HS-VK31 
alloys have similar alloy compositions and overall texture types, solid 
solution strengthening and texture strengthening are not considered the 
primary contributors to the YS differences. HSDT caused significant 
differences in the Zr particle size and distribution. Fine Zr particles can 
effectively impede dislocation motion, thereby enhancing strength.

slip within the fine-grained region, thereby improving the strength of 
the alloy. Since HS-VK31 has a higher proportion of fine-grained regions 
compared to NHS-VK31, it is likely to generate greater back stress, 
thereby enhancing its strength.
4.3. Ductility mechanism

low CRSS and are easily activated at room temperature [8] . Non-basal

formed within the grains, and the predominant type of twins is tensile

4.4. The mechanism of synergistic enhancement of strength and ductility
Different grain structures play a crucial role in the simultaneous 
enhancement of strength and ductility. Grain refinement is considered 
one of the most effective approaches to improving strength [53] . The 
finer grain size of HS-VK31 compared to NHS-VK31 is the primary 
reason for its increased strength. Regarding ductility, the activation of a 
higher density of non-basal dislocations, including < c + a > and 
< c > dislocations, along with the formation of fine stacking faults in the 
fine-grained regions, are the key factors contributing to the superior 
ductility of HS-VK31 over NHS-VK31. In heterogeneous structures, the 
fraction of fine grains is critical in balancing strength and ductility. Liu 
et al. [54] investigated the effects of fine grain fractions of 0 %, 13.3 %, 
26.8 %, and 66.5 % on the strength and ductility of the AZ91 alloy. As 
the fine grain fraction increased, strength exhibited a monotonic rise, 
while the ductility initially increased and then decreased, achieving an 
optimal strength-ductility balance at 26.8 %. This trend arises because a 
higher fraction of fine grains introduces more grain boundaries, signif -
icantly enhancing strength; however, excessive grain refinement re -
duces the dislocation storage capacity, thereby impairing ductility. In 
this study, the HS-VK31 alloy possesses a more favorable fine grain 
fraction than NHS-VK31, which facilitates the synergistic improvement 
of strength and ductility.
5. Conclusion
In this study, we employed a novel method, HSDT, which effectively 
disperses Zr particles. After extrusion, a heterogeneous structure with a 
higher proportion of fine-grained regions and smaller grain size was 
successfully fabricated, leading to simultaneous improvements in YS and 
EL. The intrinsic mechanism behind the simultaneous improvement of 
strength-ductility and microstructure was systematically elucidated. The 
main conclusions are presented in the following sections:
1. The extruded NHS-VK31 alloy without HSDT exhibited a YS of 
102 MPa and an EL of 21.1 %. In comparison to the NHS-VK3 alloy, 
the HS-VK31 alloy showed a YS of 132 MPa and EL of 29 %, which 
resulted in a 30 MPa increase in YS and a 7.9 % improvement in 
elongation.
2. HSDT prior to solidification successfully dispersed and refined Zr 
particles in the as-cast HS-VK31 alloy. After extrusion, the dispersed 
Zr particles played a crucial role in promoting the formation of fine- 
grained regions, along with smaller grain sizes. Grain boundary 
strengthening was considered the primary mechanism driving the 
increase in YS.
3. During the tensile process, twinning exhibited a notable size 
dependence, occurring predominantly in coarse grains while being 
rarely observed in fine grains. Coarse grains with RE textures showed 
favorable orientations for basal dislocation activation, leading to the 
activation of some basal dislocations.
4. In the fine-grained region, the high flow stress during deformation 
contributed to the activation of < c + a > dislocations, which were 
easily decomposed into stacking faults and partial dislocation. 
Moreover, the fine-grained region of the HS-VK31 alloy displayed a 
fiber texture, which facilitated the activation of non-basal disloca -
tions. The activation of high-density non-basal dislocations and
