Abstract 
This study reports the d esign and development of ductile and strong 
refractory single-phase high-entropy alloys (HEAs) for high temperature applications, 
based on NbTaV with addition of Ti and W. Assisted by CALPHAD modeling, a 
single body-centered cubic solid solution phase was experimentally confirmed in the 
as-cast ingots using X-ray diffraction and scanning electron microscopy. The observed 
elementalsegregation in each alloy qualitatively agreeswith CALPHAD 
prediction.The Vickersmicrohardnesses(and yielding strengths) of the alloys are about 
3 (and 3. 5-4.4) times that those estimated f rom the rule ofmixture . 
WhileNbTaTiVWshows an impressive yielding strength of 1,420MPa with fracture 
strain of 20%, NbTaTiV exhibits exceptional compressi ve ductility at room 
temperature. 
Keywords: high-entropy alloys;refractory; single-phase;design;ductile

1. Introduction 
Structuralengineering materials with highstrength, acceptable ductility, and 
good thermal stability are highly desirable for critical applications[1, 2]. Conventional 
alloys have one principal element as the matrix, with minormodiﬁcations achieved by 
adding relatively small amountsof other alloying elements. This strategy typically 
optimizes a se t of physical or mechanical properties while retaining the base 
characteristic properties of the main alloying matrix element.Over the past decade, a 
newalloy design and development philosophy —high-entropy alloys (HEAs), or 
multi-principle-element alloys (MPEAs )—has attracted significant attention [3, 
4].Such alloys generallyconsist of four , or more element swith equimolar or 
near-equimolar ratios thatform face -centered cubic (FCC) [4], body-centered cubic 
(BCC)[5], or hexagonal close -packed (HCP) phase s[6, 7] .A shared rationale is to 
maximize theconﬁgurational entropy by increasing the number of the principal 
components, thereby, in theory, continuing to improve the thermal stability 
ofdisordered solid -solution phasesagainst intermetallics, especially at elevated 
temperatures. A number of HEAs have show ed a variety of favorable, and sometimes 
unusual properties such as high strength, high ductility, high fracture toughness at low 
temperatures, and thermal stability. (For recent comprehensive reviews on HEAs, the 
reader is referred to Refs. [8, 9].) 
Traditional refractory metals and alloys hold great potential in high 
temperature applications beyond the capability of the state -of-the-art Ni -based

superalloys, primarily due to their extremely high melting points, but they also suffer 
from low ductility at low temperatures and their oxidation resis tance is poor [10]. 
Recent development on refractory HEAs suggests that they may exhibit outstanding 
strength [11] and exceptional oxidation resistance [12]. The main objective of the 
present work is to explore the design potential of single-phase refracto ry 
HEAs,namely, NbTaTiV ,NbTaVW, and NbTaTiVW. These HEAs possess the BCC 
structure, resulting in alloys that are ductile and strong . The base alloy NbTaV was 
chosen since elements Nb, Ta , and V , separately, show excellent ductility at room 
temperature[13]. 
2. Computational methodologies and experimental procedures 
The CALPHAD calculations were performed using the TCNI7 
thermodynamic databasesupplied by ThermoCalcTM[14]. The database covers the 
complete binaries pertaining to nine refractory metals although it was initially 
developed for Ni -based superalloys. Previous CALPHAD modeling using this 
database demonstrated good agreement with experimental observations for various 
refractory BCC HEAs [15-17], but future development includ ing descriptions for all 
possible ternaryalloys in their complete composition ranges will definitely enhance 
the reliability of the database in the applications for HEAs. For guidance on 
CALPHAD modeling of HEAs, the reader is referred to recent publications[18-21]. 
Alloy ingots w ithnominal composition s of NbTaTiV , NbTaVW, and 
NbTaTiVW (compositions in at.%) were synthesized by vacuum arc melting of the 
equimolar pure metals (weight percentage purity ≥ 99.9%) in a Ti -gettered,

5. Conclusions 
In summary, three refractory HEA alloys, NbTaTiV , NbTaVW, and 
NbTaTiVW, were designed by CALPHAD modeling and synthesized by vacuum arc 
melting. A single BCC solid solution phase was observed in the as -cast alloys using a 
combination of XRD and SEM analyses. The following conclusions were reached: 
1) While NbTaTiV is very ductile and does not fracture up to 50% strain, 
NbTaTiVWhas a yielding strength of 1,420 MPa with a fracture plastic 
strain of 20%. 
2) The Vickersmicrohardness values(and the yielding strengths) of the HEAs 
are about 3 (and 3.5-4.4) times that those estimated from the rule ofmixture, 
suggesting contribution from matrix solid solution strengthening. 
3) The microsegregationbehavior of the element sisclosely related to the ir
