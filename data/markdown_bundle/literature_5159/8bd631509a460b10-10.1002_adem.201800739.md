Structure and Mechanical Properties of Low Doped-Zr
TC4 Alloy Prepared by Spark Plasma Sintering
Aijun Chen, Hao Deng, Longqing Chen, Yongqiang Wei, Zuxi Xia, and Jun Tang*
The effects of Zr-doping on TC4 alloy are investigated by mechanical milling
and subsequent spark plasma sintering (SPS) technique. The microstructure
and mechanical properties of the sintered ingots are studied. It is shown that
the microstructure of the alloys undergoes a
β-phase increase through low
Zr-doping, thus the mechanical properties of the studied alloy is improved.
Tensile tests are performed to investigate the strength of TC4-xZr alloys at
room temperature. The tensile strength increases and the ductility maintains
at a high level with increased Zr content. As a result, the TC4-5Zr alloy
exhibits the highest tensile strength (1106 MPa) and excellent ductility
(
δ ¼ 16.1%). It is demonstrated that low Zr-doping in combination with
mechanical milling and SPS provides an effective way to enhance the
mechanical properties of TC4 alloys.
1. Introduction
TC4 (Ti – 6Al– 4V) alloy has been widely employed in biomedi-
cine, automobile, aeronautics, and astronautics industries owing
to its excellent physical and mechanical properties, such as low
density, low elastic modulus, high speci ﬁc strength, high
strength-to-weight ratio, biocompatibility, superior erosion
resistance, etc.
[1– 4] However, this alloy has its own disadvantages,
including low hardness, dif ﬁculty in fabrication, and poor wear
resistance. Further improvement is anticipated in many
aspects.
[5,6] Particularly, alloy matrix component tuning and
ﬁne-grain strengthening are generally very effective.
Due to its excellent mechanical properties, re ﬁnement effect
and good corrosion resistance, Zirconium (Zr) is widely used as
a strengthening element. [7,8] As Zr and Ti
belong to the same group (IVB) in the
periodic table, they possess similar proper-
ties and are able to form a continuous
solid solution. Zr is considered as an
ideal strengthening element for titanium
alloys.
[9] The addition of Zr into Ti or Ti
alloys can lead to solution strengthening
and increase solid solubility of alloying
elements.
[10] Based on the equilibrium
phase diagram of Ti – Zr, Ti, and Zr
elements always completely dissolve each
other in the
α-phase and β-phase regardless
of the liquid or solid state. Furthermore,
the β-phase transition temperature ranges
from 605 to 882 /C14 C by varying the chemical
stoichiometric ratio. [11] A series of quater-
nary (ZrTiAlV) alloys has been fabricated
by casting, and the microstructure, phase
transformation, mechanical properties, and corrosion resistance
of these alloys under various heat treatment conditions
have been investigated.
[12– 34] These alloys showed promising
potential in the aerospace industry for their excellent mechanical
properties and good corrosion resistance.
[32] Although highly
doped Zr can increase the strength of alloy, the toughness
reduces.
[22] In order to obtain TC4 alloys with both good strength
and toughness, a series of TC4- xZr alloys with low Zr-doping is
fabricated in this work.
The TC4-xZr alloys can be prepared by ingot metallurgy (IM)
or powder metallurgy (PM). In general, IM may result in
inhomogeneities and segregations in microstructure, while no
segregation forms after PM, which includes a variety of
processes such as vacuum sintering, hot pressing (HP), hot
isostatic pressing (HIP), and spark plasma sintering (SPS), etc.
Among these techniques, SPS is quite promising in fabricating
Ti alloys.
[35,36] Compared with the conventional consolidation
techniques, SPS technique features the advantages of quick
processing, low temperature, high ef ﬁciency, high density, and
ﬁne graininess.
[37] In addition, high-energy ball milling (HEBM)
can signi ﬁcantly re ﬁne grains and increase powder activity. [38]
Once HEBM is combined with SPS process, a novel technique is
proposed for fabricating Ti and TC4 alloys. [38,39] Ultraﬁne-
grained TC4 alloy with excellent compressive yield strength has
been reported.
[40,41]
The present work investigates the microstructure and
mechanical performance of TC4- xZr alloys processed with
mechanical milling and SPS. The effects of Zr content on the
microstructure and mechanical performance of obtained alloys
are characterized. The resultant alloys with low Zr-doping
in this work show relatively good strength and ductility.

2. Experimental Section
The as-received TC4 alloy powders, which are produced by a
plasma atomization process, are spherical with a particle size
ranging from 15 to 45 mm. Zr is introduced into TC4 powders by
ball milling. The TC4 and Zr powders are mixed in stainless steel
tanks with stainless steel balls with diameters of 12 and 6 mm.
The ball to powder weight ratio is 10:1. The powders are mixed
for 50 h with a rotation speed of 350 rpm in an all-dimensional
planetary ball mill (QM-QX, apparatus factory of Nanjing
University, P. R. China) under a puri ﬁed argon gas atmosphere
(99.999%, 0.5 MPa). Approximately 10 g of the milled powders
are loaded into a high-density graphite die with an inner
diameter of 30 mm and compacted by SPS (LABOX-325, Japan)
in a vacuum of 5 Pa. During the sintering process, a pressure of
60 MPa is applied at a sintering temperatures of 850
/C14 C. The
powders are sintered at a ramp rate of 100 /C14 C min/C0 1 to the target
temperature and then dwelled for 5 min. Prior to the measure-
ments, the obtained sample is ground and polished by silicon
carbide (SiC).
The N and O contents of the sintered samples are acquired
by TC500 (LECO Co., USA). The density of SPS sintered
samples is calculated based on the Archimedes principle. The
morphologies of ball-milling powders and fracture surface of
tensile test specimens are characterized by a Scanning Electron
Microscope (SEM) (Inspect F50, FEI). The composite speci-
mens for optical microscope (OM) (LEICA DMI 5000 M) and
SEM observation are prepared by standard metallographic
methods. The polished surfaces of samples are etched by Kroll ’s
reagent (10% HNO
3, 5% HF, and 85% H 2O) before OM and
SEM observation. The crystal structure of the TC4- xZr
specimens is characterized by X-ray diffraction (XRD). The
phase transition temperature of TC4- xZr is measured by
differential scanning calorimetry (DSC). At room temperature,
tensile tests are performed by using an Instron 5892 testing
machine at a strain rate of 1 /C2 10
/C0 3 s/C0 1. The dimensions of the
tensile test specimen is 8 mm in length, 1.5 mm in width,
1.2 mm in thickness, with a corner radius for lower stress
concentration.

The O and N contents of the alloys may increase during ball
milling and sintering. The vast majority of the increased O, N
atoms in the sintered compacts may exist in solid solution, which

commercial Ti– 6Al– 4V alloys, the O equivalent should be below
0.38%. In commercial Ti – 6Al– 4V alloys, the O content is less
than 0.2% and the N content is less than 0.05%.[45] Therefore, the
O and N contents of the sample are tested. The results indicate
that the O and N contents of the sintered TC4- xZr alloys
are within the acceptable range and have negligible effect on the
mechanical properties of the alloys ( Table 1).
The crystallite size evolution corresponding to ball milling
process can be estimated by XRD measurements. In the case of
TC4 powders, the XRD pattern are shown in Figure 2. Compared
with the as-received powders, the peaks of the milled TC4
powders gradually broaden in width and decrease dramatically in
height (Figure 2a). Based on the Scherrer formula, this displays
obvious grain re ﬁnement and lattice distortion through ball-

[46] The average crystallite size of the powders after
different ball-milling time is calculated and shown in the
illustration. The crystallite size decreases to 16.7 nm after 45 h

powders (about 47 nm) (Figure 2b).
3.2. Characterization of the Sintered TC4- xZr Compacts
XRD measurements are performed to determine the phase
composition and grain size of the sintered TC4-xZr compacts, as
shown in Figure 3a. The phase peaks of sintered alloys become
sharper compared with the TC4 powders, indicating that certain
grains grew during the sintering process. This tendency is
conﬁrmed by the SEM images of the sintered TC4- xZr alloys
(Figure 4). By analyzing the grain morphology, the average grain
size of the TC4- xZr alloys is much larger than that of the TC4
powders (16.7 nm, calculated by XRD Figure 2b), due to
recrystallization and grain growth between the particles. In
the early stage of sintering, because of plastic deformation and
residual strain in ball-milling TC4 powders, heating the
compacts leads to primary recrystallization and new strained
grains. In addition, since the Joule effect is concentrated on the
neck conductive powder particles in the early stage of sintering,
the particle interface ’s local temperature should be relatively
high.
[47] In these overheated regions, recrystallization and grain
growth are accelerated, and larger grains are formed. During the
late stage of sintering, excessive free energy at the grain
boundary leads to the movement of the grain boundary, which
results in gradual increase in grain size. When the small crystal
grains grow into large grains, the interface area of the crystal
grains decreases, and the free energy at the crystal grain interface
decreases. At the same time, a few large grains exist in the
compact during the late stage of sintering, which tends to
become the secondary recrystallization nuclei, and the grain size
grows abnormally. The secondary recrystallization are mainly
due to the non-uniformity of the particle size for the ball-milling
powders and the high sintering temperature, followed by the
non-uniformity of the forming pressure.

α and β-phases. When the
compact is sintered at a temperature of 850 /C14 C, the diffraction

TC4-xZr alloys. With enhanced Zr dopant, the intensity peak of
β-phase (200) (around 2 θ ¼ 57.5/C14 ) increases (Figure 3b). Accord-
ing to the Zr– Ti binary phase diagram, the Zr content in the alloy
increases and the β-phase transition temperature decreases. It is

3.3. Mechanical Properties of Sintered TC4- xZr Compacts
The mechanical properties of the SPSed TC4- xZr ingots are
evaluated with uniaxial tensile tests. Compared with the SPSed
TC4, all the other sintered TC4- xZr ( x ¼ 1, 2, 5) alloys acquire
superior mechanical properties thanks to Zr-doping, as shown in
Figure 7 and Table 2.
The tensile strength of SPSed TC4- xZr ( x ¼ 0, 1, 2, 5)
increases from 920 to 1106 MPa, while the elongation decreases
from 24.6% to 16.1% (Figure 7). An investigation of high Zr-
doping shows that the mechanical test and microhardness of
hot-rolled TiZrAlV quaternary alloys increases with Zr content
ranging from 5 to 20 wt%, while the plasticity decreases
dramatically.
[22] In this study, higher ductility of TC4- xZr alloys
is obtained under low Zr-doping.
Generally, the mechanical properties of alloys are mainly
determined by microstructure and solid solution strengthening.
In the studied alloys, the solid solution strengthening of the Zr is
very obvious. Compared with TC4, enormous quantity of defects
is produced in the TC4-5Zr by the addition of Zr. According to
the Orowan relation, these defects cause lattice distortion, which

the alloy.

4. Conclusion
In this study, a series of TC4- xZr alloys with high strength and
excellent ductility is prepared by using mechanical milling and
SPS technique. The microstructure transformation of as-
received TC4 powders during ball milling and the phase
transition, structural evolution and mechanical properties of
TC4-xZr alloys obtained by SPS sintering are also investigated.
The conclusions are as follows:
1) According to the XRD patterns and the DSC results, the
phase composition of the TC4- xZr alloys undergo changes,
the phase transition temperature decreases and the
β-phase
increases with the increase in Zr content.
2) The tensile strength enhancement of the TC4- xZr alloy is
achieved by doping the Zr element. When 5 wt% Zr is added,
the tensile strength reaches a maximum value (1106 MPa) in
relation to the SPSed TC4 increase of more than 186 MPa.
3) The fracture manners of TC4- xZr alloys show ductile
fracture. Compared to the elongation (24.6%) of sintered
TC4, the elongation of the studied TC4-xZr (x ¼ 1, 2, 5) alloys
decreases and good ductility is thus achieved.
