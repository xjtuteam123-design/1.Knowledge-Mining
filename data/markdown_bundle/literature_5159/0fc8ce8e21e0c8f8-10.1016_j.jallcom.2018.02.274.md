Abstract 
Ti-Nb alloys are potential candidates owing to thei r excellent mechanical properties and high 
corrosion resistance for bioactive implant applicat ions compared to other metallic materials. The 
purpose of this work is to investigate the effect of the Nb amount on the microstructure, mechanical 
properties, corrosion behavior and hydroxyapatite (HA) formation ability of Ti-Nb alloys produced via 
powder injection molding. According to the phase/mi crostructure studies, including XRD analyses, 
optical microscope, and SEM imaging, all of the all oys are composed of α+β phases, and with the 
increment of Nb content, β phase stability increase s. Also, it is observed that Nb content has 
significant effects on the mechanical properties of the considered alloys. While the hardness / 
Transverse rupture strength values of Ti-Nb alloys (303-340HV, 992-440MPa) are higher than that of 
the titanium (269HV, 300MPa), the elastic modulus i s measured as lower than (100-115GPa) that of 
the titanium (132-140GPa). The increment of Nb cont ent causes to decrease of the hardness, 
bending strength and elastic modulus of Ti-Nb alloy s. Also, the addition of Nb contributed to the 
improvement of corrosion resistance and induced to increase of hydroxyapatite formation ability. 
Keywords: Metallurgical characterization, Titanium alloys, Powder methods, Biomaterials 
1. Introduction 
Load bearing applications such as hip or knee prost hesis should possess appropriate mechanical 
properties (mechanical values are targeted near the bone) and high corrosion resistance. 
Furthermore they should not cause any allergic or i nflammatory reactions in the human body for 
preventing the rejection of the implant from body a nd making it durable for a long period. Also, it is 
desirable that surface of the implant should be int egrated with the adjacent bone for 
osseointegration [1].

The most suitable metallic implant materials provid ing these mentioned attributes are Ti and its 
alloys. Among Ti based alloys, Ti-Nb alloys are oft en preferred due to their excellent properties [2]. 
Nb reduces the elastic modules of the Ti alloys because of its β phase stabilizing character [3]. In this 
way, lowering the elastic modulus of the alloy redu ces the effect of ‘stress shielding effect’ which is 
responsible for the losses of implants [4]. Moreover, by the addition of Nb to Ti alloys, it is possible to 
increase the corrosion and wear resistance due to the development of stable Nb 2O5 oxide on surface 
of Ti alloy [5,6]. Although Ti-Nb alloys have these superior properties, their osseointegration property 
is recognized as low due to their bioinert surface [7]. Thin connective tissue capsules form on the 
surface of alloy and don’t adhere to the implant su rface. Eventually, implant failure occurs[8]. 
Various studies have been completed to increase the bioactivity [9–12]. 
Implant production is a relatively difficult process because of the reasons such as the complex shape 
of the products, high cost, high melting temperatur es of the raw materials (Ti, Nb, Zr,Ta, etc.). 
Powder injection molding (PIM) is an appropriate ca ndidate method to overcome these mentioned 
problems. It is possible to produce net-shape produ cts by using PIM method, and no additional 
plastic forming process is required [13,14]. Also, high strength and dense products can be acquired, 
by selecting appropriate production parameters and binders [15–17]. 
In the literature, Ti-Nb alloys were produced using various powder metallurgy methods such as spark 
plasma sintering [18], press-sinter [19], powder in jection molding [19–21]. Zhao and colleagues [22] 
have examined the microstructure and mechanical properties of PIM Ti-(10-22)Nb alloys. Also, same 
research group has investigated the effects of the sintering temperature on the microstructural 
properties of the PIM Ti-16 Nb alloy [23]. Bidaux et al. [24] have studied the properties of the PIM Ti-
17Nb alloy. In previous studies based on Ti-Nb PIM alloys, corrosion behavior and in vitro evaluations 
are very rare [25]. Also, studies on the rheological properties of the PIM Ti-Nb alloys are very limited 
[16,17]. Therefore, to better understand such prope rties of Ti-Nb PIM alloys, the present study 
focused on mentioned alloys. 
In this study, the PIM technique was used to produce Ti and Ti-(16,28,40)Nb wt. % alloys. The effect 
of Nb addition on various properties on the Ti-Nb alloys was investigated. For this purpose, hardness 
and elastic modulus measurements, bending and corrosion tests as well as in vitro assessments were 
performed on the alloys produced.

3. Results and Discussion 
3.1. Rheology 
At the initial stage of the experimental works, rheology analysis was conducted to the powder-binder 
mixtures for understanding their flow properties at powder injection process temperature. It is 
thought that carefully controlling of the flowing a ttributes of the feedstock allows producing green 
parts without defects. Data of the rheological ana lysis can be used for preventing the faults those 
occur before or after molding, such as short shot, surface wrinkle, binder separation, flashing 
defects, [27]. Huang et. al. [28] reported that vis cosity of feedstock is sensitive to temperature and 
powder/binder ratio. So it is essential to conduct rheological analysis for each condition rather than 
using values reported in the literature. Viscosity vs. shear rate values of the feedstocks at various 
temperatures is given in Fig. 3. It is suggested th at irregular and sharp-edge morphology (Fig. 1) of 
the raw powders causes low flowability of the powders. However, rheology analysis of the feedstock 
(Fig. 3) revealed that feedstock shows pseudoplastic flow which is appropriate flow behavior for PIM 
process [29]. The term “Pseudoplastic flow” remarks a decreasing of viscosity with the increment of 
shear rate and temperature [30]. Sidambe et. al. reported the viscosity of the Ti feedstock (pure Ti + 
%31 binder) about 1000 Pa.s. at 120°C [31]. As ment ioned previously binder/ powder ratio and 
powder morphology have significant effect on the vi scosity of the feedstock. So it is speculated that 
choosing different powder/binder ratio is the main reason for achieving different viscosity values 
than similar previous work [31]. It is reported tha t viscosity of the feedstock should be lower than 
1000 Pa.s. for successful molding [32]. It is appar ent that viscosity of the feedstocks is lower than 
that of this mentioned value at all of the investig ated temperatures (Fig. 3) so it is concluded that 
viscosity of the feedstock is appropriate for producing fault-free green compacts.

 3.4. Mechanical Properties 
It is known that load bearing implant materials sho uld have high strength and low elastic modulus 
[1]. Low elastic modulus reduces ‘stress shielding effect’ problem and prevents implant loosening. 
Because of elastic modulus of β phase is lower than that of α phase, β stabilizer elements are added 
to Ti alloys to reduce the elastic modulus [47]. Nb is one of the most important beta stabilizer 
elements [48]. In this study, nanoindentation and u ltrasonic measurement techniques were used to 
obtain more accurate results due to the low ductili ty of the sintered alloys. It is observed that Nb 
addition to the Ti leads to decrease of the elastic modulus about 28 % (Fig. 8), this result is consistent 
with previous works[22,45]. Han et.al. investigated the properties of Ti-Nb alloys produced by ingot 
metallurgy and claimed that elastic modulus of the Ti could be lowered to 114-127 GPa from 132 
GPa by alloying with 5-10-15-20 % wt. Nb [44]. It is reported that increased content of the Nb 
reduces the carbon solubility of the Ti-Nb binary system [22]. So it is thought that adding 40 wt. % Nb 
caused the formation of the hard carbide phases bec ause of the low solubility of the C in the Ti-Nb 
matrix and, thus lead to higher elastic modulus and higher hardness values (Fig. 9) of the Ti40Nb 
alloy. It was therefore concluded that the alloys w ith 16 and 28 % wt. Nb is preferable for implant 
production as an alternative to Ti6Al4V alloy.

The hardness values and bending strength of the Ti- Nb alloys (303-325HV, 440-992 MPa) were 
measured as higher than that of Ti (269HV, 300MPa) due to solid solution hardening of the Nb 
element (Fig. 9). It is well known that elastic mod ulus is related to bond strength between atoms of 
the constituents. It is reported that , increment o f the Nb content in Ti-Nb alloys, the bonding force 
of the lattice decreases and thus hardness and bending strength values decrease accordingly [45,49]. 
Moreover, it is previously shown that there is a positive correlation between Nb content and porosity 
(Fig. 4), so increasing porosity is another reason for lower transverse rupture strength of Ti40Nb 
alloy. Han et. al. were measured the hardness of t he Ti5Nb and Ti20 Nb alloys as 358 and 332 HV 
respectively and stated that increasing amount of N b causes decreasing of the hardness [44]. 
Another study observed that sintering the Ti-35Nb alloy at 1500 °C leads to achieve a hardness of 390 
HV [50]. It is seen that obtained results are in good agreement with those given in relevant literature.

metallic ions are released into the surrounding tis sues and causes allergic-toxic reactions.

4. Conclusions 
The completed study has provided insight into the p roduction of Ti-Nb alloys by using Powder 
Injection Molding Technique. Effects of Nb addition on the mechanical properties, microstructural 
features, corrosion behavior and Hydroxyapatite dep osition ability of the Ti-Nb were investigated. 
From the completed work the following summary remarks can be made: 
 
• Rheological analyses showed that feedstock showed pseudo-plastic rheological behavior and 
it is determined that used powder/binder ratio (50 % -50 % vol.) is appropriate for producing 
parts with no-fault. 
• It is observed that the amount of porosity increase d because Nb has a lower diffusion 
coefficient than that of Ti in β matrix. Maximum p orosity (4.73 %) achieved from the alloy 
with maximum Nb content (40 % wt.) 
• Nb addition to the Ti caused gradual increasing of the β phase content, so β stabilizer effect 
of the Nb has been proven. 
• The addition of Nb has changed the mechanical prope rties as well as the other properties. 
With the addition of Nb to Ti, the elastic modulus decreased from 140GPa to 100GPa owing 
to an increase in the amount of β phase, while hardness increased from 269HV to 303-340HV 
and bending strength increased from 300MPa to 400-9 92MPa. Decreasing of the elastic 
modulus of the alloys found to be beneficial for su ppressing the stress-shielding effect 
between implant-bone interfaces. Furthermore, the i ncrement of mechanical properties is 
desirable for the load-bearing implants. 
• Due to the formation of Nb2O5 oxide, which is more stable than TiO2, the corrosion 
resistance of Ti-based alloy is increased as Nb added in SBF. Besides Nb addition triggers the 
formation of HAP on the surface of pre-treated Ti-( 16, 28, 40)Nb alloys, thereby creating a 
denser HAP layer and osseointegration feature devel oped as a result of bioactive surface 
formation.
