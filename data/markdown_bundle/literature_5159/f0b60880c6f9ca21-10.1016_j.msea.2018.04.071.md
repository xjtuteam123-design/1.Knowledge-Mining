ABSTRACT
The equiatomic CoCrFeMnNi high entropy alloy prepared by powder metallurgy has homogenous chemical
composition and microstructure. The mechanical properties of the CoCrFeMnNi high entropy alloy at the strain
rates (1 × 10 −4 s−1 to 0.1 s−1 and 1 × 10 3 s−1 to 3 × 10 3 s−1) and the temperature (298 K, 673 K and 1073 K)
were investigated. Results indicate that the yield strength of the CoCrFeMnNi high entropy alloy is in the range
of 350 –700 MPa, increasing sensitively with increasing the strain rates, especially at a high strain rate (larger
than 1 × 10
3 s−1). The serration behavior of the high entropy alloy is observed on the ﬂow stress curves of the
alloy deformed at a low strain rate of 1 × 10 −3 s−1 and the high strain rates (1 × 10 3 s−1 to 3 × 10 3 s−1).
Inﬂuences of the strain rate and the temperature on the serration behavior of the CoCrFeMnNi high entropy alloy
are discussed.
1. Introduction
High entropy alloys, called solid solution alloys, contain at least ﬁve
major elements in equal or near equal atomic percent [1]. The equia-
tomic CoCrFeMnNi high entropy alloy owns a single-phase solid solu-
tion with the face-centered-cubic (FCC) structure. Many researchers
present rising interest on the mechanical behavior and the application
of the CoCrFeMnNi high entropy alloy [2–7]. The strength of the
CoCrFeMnNi high entropy alloy is relatively low in as-cast state, only
around 200 MPa [7]. High entropy alloy are commonly prepared by arc
melting, mechanical alloying, laser cladding, and spark plasma sin-
tering [8–14]. The compositional segregation of elements and brittle
intermetallic are inevitable in the high entropy alloy ingots prepared by
vacuum arc melting [14–16]. Powder metallurgy is an e ﬃcient way to
prepare a high strength CoCrFeMnNi high entropy alloy with homo-
geneous compositions and microstructures [15]. Powders can be com-
pletely combined in a short time so that the microstructure without
coarsening [17], and the extremely fast cooling rates can also re ﬁne the
microstructure. In addition, the compositional segregation can be
avoided and the single phase can be stabilized with this fast cooling
rates [18].
A row of sharp or tooth-like projections appear on ﬂow stress curves
of solid materials are called serration behavior by Zhang et al. [8]. The
serration behavior in the plastic regime of the CoCrFeMnNi high
entropy alloy has been observed in both compressive and tensile tests
[7,16,19–22]. Zhang et al. [8] summarized that serrations observed in
the high entropy alloys during the compressive tests at the strain rate of
1×1 0
−3 s−1 seemed to be greater than those at strain rate of
1×1 0 −4 s−1 or 10 s −1, and were more visible than those at elevated
temperatures. Serration behavior is supposed to associate with Cottrell
atmosphere interaction with moving dislocations, slip band, and dy-
namic strain aging, i.e. the dynamic breakaway / locking of dislocations
from / by mobile solute atoms at intermediate temperatures [23–26].
Otto et al. [16] reported that serrations were commonly attributed to
dynamic dislocation pinning and breakaway, such as dynamic strain
aging. Yasuda et al. [23] presented that serrations may cause by dy-
namic strain aging and closely related to the solute atmosphere around
moving dislocations or stacking faults. Chen et al. [27] applied self-
similar random process and chaotic behavior to explain the mechanism
of serration behavior. Serrations in other traditional alloy had also been
investigated. For example, the serration behavior of dual phase steels at
various temperatures and strain rates between 1 × 10
−3 s-1 and 1 s −1
had been studied Bayramin et al. [28]. The results suggest that the
serrations can be linked to a periodic microstructural feature. In addi-
tion, Portevin-Le Chatelier (PLC) e ﬀect [29–33],d e ﬁned as continuous
serrated yielding in stress –strain curve in certain ranges of temperature
and strain rate, is another method to explain the serration behavior of
the high entropy alloy. The critical strain of the Portevin-Le Chatelier

2. Material and methods
The CoCrFeMnNi high entropy alloy was prepared by powder me-
tallurgy method. Chemical composition of the CoCrFeMnNi high en-
tropy alloy is listed in Table 1. The detail of the process was explained
in our previous paper [35].
Cylindrical compressive specimens with 4 mm in diameter or 6 mm
in diameter were machined from the sintered specimens by the line
cutting. At least 4 specimens were adopted for each kind of testing
conditions. The loading direction was parallel to the cylindrical axis of
these specimens. To ensure uniaxial compressive condition, the end
face of the compressive specimens were ground on each side with SiC
paper. During the process of the impact loading and the electrical signal
collection, we had adopted advanced waving plastic and anti-jamming
technique. Three di ﬀerent types of compressive test were adopted to do
mechanical testing, as follows. 1) Quasi-static compressive tests were
performed at ambient temperature (298 K) and at strain rates of
1×1 0
−4 s−1,1× 1 0 −3 s−1,5× 1 0 −2 s−1 and 0.1 s−1, for specimens

performed at strain rate of 1 × 10 −3 s−1 and at elevated temperatures
of 673 K and 1073 K, for specimens B and A, respectively. 3) Dynamic
compressive tests were performed at ambient temperature (298 K) and
at the strain rates of 1200 s

3.3. Microstructure of the deformed CoCrFeMnNi high entropy alloy
Fig. 6a shows that the initial CoCrFeMnNi high entropy pre-alloy
particles are sintered, and the primary particle boundaries still exist in
the powder metallurgy CoCrFeMnNi high entropy alloy. The micro-
structure of the deformed specimen is shown in Fig. 6 b. It can be seen
that the primary particle boundaries are elongated along the de-
formation direction.
Fig. 7 shows the scanning electron micrographs of the deformed
CoCrFeMnNi high entropy alloy specimens A (673 K, 1 × 10
−3 s−1), D
(298 K, 1 × 10 −3 s−1) and J (298 K, 2800 s −1). It can be seen that the
microstructure in the regions enclosed by the primary particle bound-
aries are relatively perfect. The cracks in specimens D (298 K, 1 × 10 −3
s−1) and J (298 K, 2800 s −1) are along the primary particle boundaries
(Fig. 7b and c), while no cracks can be observed in the specimen A
(Fig. 7a). Sintered particles are broken at the primary particle bound-
aries after the specimen J (298 K, 2800 s −1) are seriously deformed, as
shown in Fig. 7 d. The number of the continuous cracks in specimen J
are larger than those in specimen D (298 K, 1 × 10 −3 s−1).
Figs. 8 and 9 show the bright ﬁeld images of microstructure in the
deformed CoCrFeMnNi high entropy alloy specimen J (298 K,
2800 s−1). While the dislocations are marked by black arrows, the
secondary phase particles and the holes which left by the particles when
they fell o ﬀ during TEM specimen preparation are marked by white
arrows. Tangled dislocations and elongated dislocation cell structures
with heavy dislocation density are formed, as shown in Fig. 8 . Fine
scattered secondary phase particles with a size about 0.15 µm are ob-
served on both the boundary and within the deformed grains, and the
dislocations are pinned and blocked at the secondary phase particles, as
shown in Fig. 9. The energy dispersive spectrometer is performed to
analyze the element contents of Co, Cr, Fe, Mn and Ni in the region of
the secondary phase particles, as shown in Fig. 10 and Table 2. The
secondary phase particles are rich-Cr oxide phase ones. Therefore,
number of dispersed rich-Cr phase particles pin the dislocations in the
CoCrFeMnNi high entropy alloy.
From above analysis, the CoCrFeMnNi high entropy alloy prepared
by powder metallurgy method is characterized by ﬁne equiaxed grains
enclosed by the primary particle boundaries. The broken of the grains
in the primary particle boundaries and the cracks along the primary
particle boundaries are acting all the time creating homogeneous de-
formation and causing continuous changes in stress, and the pinning of
the dislocations in the grains enclosed by the primary particle bound-
aries in the CoCrFeMnNi high entropy alloy by number of dispersed
rich-Cr phase particles may cause the small serrations observed on the
stress-strain curves. The Portevin-Le Chatelier (PLC) e ﬀect take e ﬀects
for investigating the strain rate and temperature e ﬀects on the serration
behavior of the CoCrFeMnNi high entropy alloy. With the secondary
phase particles gathering at the arrested dislocations, the dislocations
are pinning and the motion of dislocations is intermittent. The pinned
dislocations need more stress to unpin the obstacles. And then the un-
pinning of dislocations will bring a drop in stress. The critical strain of
the Portevin-Le Chatelier e ﬀect marks the boundary between stable and
unstable ﬂow [30].
4. Conclusions
The serrations of the CoCrFeMnNi high entropy alloy prepared by
powder metallurgy method are observed on the ﬂow stress curves when
the specimens are deformed at ambient temperature and at strain rates
of 1 × 10
−3 s−1 or 1 × 10 3 s−1 to 3 × 10 3 s−1. Amplitudes of the
serrations of the powder-metallurgy CoCrFeMnNi high entropy alloy
are smaller than those of the as-casting CoCrFeMnNi high entropy alloy.
The yield strength of the CoCrFeMnNi high entropy alloy is sensitively
with the strain rates, especially at the high strain rates (larger than 1 ×
10
3 s−1).
The cracks generates along the primary particle boundaries and the
primary particle boundaries are broken when the powder-metallurgy
CoCrFeMnNi high entropy alloy is serious deformed. The small serra-
tions observed on the stress-strain curves of the powder-metallurgy
CoCrFeMnNi high entropy alloy are caused by the periodic pinning and
unpinning process of dislocations in the grains by number of dispersed
rich-Cr phase particles.
