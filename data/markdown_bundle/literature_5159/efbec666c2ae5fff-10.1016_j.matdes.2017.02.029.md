Abstract 
Low-activation structural materials for fusion applications beyond 700 oC are desired. 
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) with excellent properties at elevated temperatures are suitable 
for use in nuclear reactors. A newly deve loped V 35Ti35Fe15Cr10Zr5 HEA was fabricated by 
vacuum arc melting and the microstructure of as-cast and annealed sample was found to be 
composed of BCC solid solution phases. It was interesting to find that the yield strength of 
as-cast V 35Ti35Fe15Cr10Zr5 HEA increased with increasing the temperature below 500 oC, 
and it was much higher than that of vanadium alloy or martensitic steel for fusion reactors 
in the temperature range of room temperature to 900 oC. At 700 oC, the yield strength of 
V35Ti35Fe15Cr10Zr5 HEA was about 500 MPa higher than that of vanadium alloy or 
martensitic steel , showing promise for high temperature applications . V 35Ti35Fe15Cr10Zr5 
alloy had a relative low ductility below 700 oC, while the alloy exhibited a good ductility 
above 700 oC. 
 
Keywords: High-entropy alloy; Intermetallics; Microstructure; Strength; Hardness

1. Introduction 
Reduced activation ferritic/marte nsitic (RAFM) steels, such as F82H and CLAM 
steel, are considered to be the primary candidate first wall and blanket structural materials 
for fusion power reactors, because of their intrinsic low activation property, high resistance 
to neutron irradiation, high ductility and appropriate high temperature strength [1]. Those 
steels are iron -based alloy and have a body -centered cubic (BCC) phase structure, which 
makes its irradiation resistance is better than the face -centered cubic (FCC) steels [2]. The 
maximum operating temperature limit for RAFM steels is assumed to be around 550 oC, as 
its high temperature strength and thermal creep resistance decreases significantly above 550 
oC [3]. In order to improve the operating temperature of structural materials in fusion power 
reactors, extensive efforts have been made to develop advanced materials, and oxide 
dispersion strengthened (ODS) steels [4,5], vanadium alloys [6,7] and silicon carbide fiber 
reinforced silicon carbide matrix composites [8,9] have been develo ped and some 
progresses have been achieved. 
In the above mentioned traditional alloys, one or two elements are the principal 
elements, and other minor elements are used to optimize their properties. For example, the 
main element of RAFM steel is Fe (~85wt %), small amounts of Cr (~8wt%), W (~2wt%), 
C (~0.08wt%) and other elements are added in the alloy to achieve its mechanical strength 
by heat treatment. Recently, high -entropy alloys (HEAs), which are usually composed of 
five principal elements and the con centration of each composed element is between 5 and 
35 at.% [10-15], have attracted many researcher's attentions, because the alloy have 
excellent high -temperature strength, high hardness, and great thermal stability through

proper alloy design due to the effects of sluggish diffusion, lattice distortion and cocktail 
effect in high entropy alloy systems [10-17]. 
According to the Gibbs phase rule, in order to make HEAs have enough mixing 
entropy to form a simple single phase solid -solutions rather than comp licated intermetallic 
compounds, HEAs are generally designed to equal atomic proportions to obtain the largest 
mixing entropy. Due to the high -entropy effect, solid solution is easier to form than 
intermetallics during solidification process in HEAs [10-16]. The structure of HEAs is 
usually a simple structure, such as single BCC phase structure, FCC phase structure or 
BCC+FCC phase structur e. The strength of HEAs is expected to be higher than the 
conventional metallic alloys due to the strong solid solution effects. For example, 
AlCoCrFeNi and Ti CoCrFeNi HEAs showed very high room temperature compression 
strength, exceeding 1500 MPa [18]. Similar to conventional alloys, the strength of most 
HEAs decrease with increasing the temperature, and a rapid decrease in strength of HEAs 
occurs at temperatures above ~0.6 Tm, where Tm is the melting temperature, and at 1000 oC 
the strength of CoCrFeNi-based HEAs comes near to ~100 MPa [19]. 
Based on this new alloy conceptual design, in this paper, low activation elements Fe, 
Cr, Ti, V and Zr were chosen, to form an equiatomic VTiFeCrZr and a non -equiatomic 
V35Ti35Fe15Cr10Zr5 HEAs. Fe, Cr, Ti and V elements are selected mainly because of their 
reduced activation property which is required for alloys use for fusion reactors . Zr element 
is selected because it has a larger negative enthalpy of mixing with other selected elements, 
the value of Zr-Fe and Zr-Cr are -25 and -12 KJ/mol, respectively [20], making the alloy is 
easy to form solid solution. Also, the difference of atom ic radius between Zr and other

2. Experimental 
The VTiFeCrZr and V35Ti35Fe15Cr10Zr5 high-entropy alloys with a weight of 30 gram 
were prepared by vacuum arc melting with high purity elements (the purity of every 
element was more than 99.9%) on a water -cooled copper mold in the argon atmosphere. 
Ingots were melted at least five times t o ensure compositions homogeneous. The samples 
for microstructure evaluation were cut from the button ingots and polished with standard 
polishing procedures. A sample was cut from the as -cast ingot and annealed at 1000 oC for 
3 h in a vacuum furnace for mi crostructure comparison. Cry stal structure of as -cast alloys 
was analyzed by X -ray diffraction (XRD) using Cu Kα radiation, the tube voltage was 40 
kV and the tube current was 40 mA, with the 2θ ranging from 20 o to 100 o at a speed of 0.2 
o/min. A JSM-6490LV type scanning electron mi croscope (SEM) was used for 
microstructural analysis, and the micro -composition was analyzed by energy dispersive

Generally, solid solution criterion is used to measure the effect of atomic size to form 
a solid solution, and the entropy function criterion is to assess the effec t of mixing entropy 
and mixing enthalpy on the formation of high entropy alloys. According to the statistical 
results, if the value of Ω is higher than 1.1 and δ is smaller than 6.6%, the alloy may form a 
solid solution. The Ω and δ of VTiFeCrZr HEA are 2. 21 and 9.06%, respectively. Entropy 
function criterion meets the requirement while the solid solution criterion is not. Therefore, 
it may not form a simple solid solution in this alloy. Moreover, the difference of atomic 
radius plays an important role in alloy formation process, which determines the structure and 
phase of the alloy. As shown in Table 1, the radii of Ti and Zr elements are much higher than 
the other principal elements, as well as the average radius value of 139 pm. The maximum 
differential radius between Zr and Fe (or Cr) is 20.6%, which is higher than the standard 
value (12%) for the requirement of solid solution formation. As a result, adding too much 
amount of Zr in the VTiFeCrZr alloy is expected to produce very large lattice distortion 
energy and the combination of different elements is difficult. On the other hand, alloy with 
small amount of Zr, such as V35Ti35Fe15Cr10Zr5, the Ω and δ is 2.74 and 6.47%, respectively. 
Therefore, it is expected to form a simple solid solution. 
To confirm the empirical estimation, equiatomic alloy VTiFeCrZr and 
non-equiatomic alloy V35Ti35Fe15Cr10Zr5 were fabricated. As expected, equiatomic alloy 
VTiFeCrZr HEA was very brittle and broken after solidification . Therefore, the 
microstructure and mechanical properties have not been further investigated in this paper .

3.2 Crystal structure 
X-ray diffraction patterns of as -cast and annealed for V35Ti35Fe15Cr10Zr5 HEA are 
shown in Fig 1 . A group of diffraction peaks can be obviously observed, around the 
two-theta 41.43 o and 42.20 o, there is a strongest peak (110), corresponding to two phases 
(BCC1 and BCC2) , both with the BCC crystal structures, are identified in the alloy. The 
lattice parameters of these phases are 308 pm and 303 pm for V35Ti35Fe15Cr10Zr5 alloy in 
the as -cast state, respectively. After annealing at 1000 oC for 3 h in a vacuum furnace, 
V35Ti35Fe15Cr10Zr5 alloy is still composed of two BCC phases with the lattice parameter o f 
305 pm and 298 pm, respectively. Comparing the XRD parameters of as -cast and annealed 
samples, the decrease of the lattice parameter in annealed one may be attributed to the fast 
cooling rate during alloy fabrication for as -cast alloy, as the fast coolin g rate could cause 
large internal stresses generation in the sample, leading to the increase of the 
lattice distortion. On the other band, annealing at 1000 oC may r elease the internal stress 
partly, dissolve the atoms and finally decrease the lattice dist ortion [25]. The intensity of 
diffraction peak of BCC2 phase also increased after annealing. T his might be attributed to 
the increase of volume fraction of BCC2 phase. Also, annealing at high temperature could 
release the internal stress partly and decreas e the lattice distortion, leading to the change of 
diffraction peaks.

3.3 Microstructure 
The microstructures of as-cast and annealed V35Ti35Fe15Cr10Zr5 HEA were shown in 
Fig. 2. The microstructure o f the alloy was a typical solid solution dendritic crystal 
structure, mainly owing to the similar melting points and the infinite solid solution between 
principal elements of Ti and V . The chemical compositions of dendrite (DR) and 
inter-dendrite (IR) regions at as-cast and annealed states were given in Table 2, suggesting 
the alloy had high temperature stability at 1000 oC. It could be seen that, Ti and V were 
mainly concentrated in the DR regions, while Fe and Zr concentrated in the IR regions, and 
Cr homogeneously distributed both in DR and IR. The distribution o f elements could be 
explained by the mixing enthalpy. The mixing enthalpy between Fe and Zr is -25 kJ/mol 
(Table 1), which is the minimum one for the selected alloy system, meaning that Fe and Zr 
combine together much easier than other element pairs during solidification because of 
their good compatibility [26]. Therefore, Fe and Zr formed a solution with other elements, 
and the melting point could be lower than V , Ti and Cr. During solidification, V , Ti and Cr 
solidified firstly to form the dendrite, and the relative poor compatibilities of V and Zr, and 
Ti and Zr made the Zr-Fe-base low melting point solution repel to the inter-dendrite. As a 
result, the DR was rich in Ti and V , while Zr and Fe segregated in the IR after solidification. 
Although there were some differences between the mixing enthalpy of elements, they were 
small enough to form solid solutions rather than intermetallic compound, and the 
microstructure was typical dendritic microstructure. 
The TEM bright field images and the selected -area electron diffraction patterns of 
as-cast and annealed samples were presented in Fig.3. Two phases (region 1 and 2) were

3.4 Hardness 
The average hardness of as -cast V35Ti35Fe15Cr10Zr5 alloy is 554.3 Hv . The high 
hardness of V35Ti35Fe15Cr10Zr5 is mainly attributed to the solid-solution strengthening effect, 
which was observed in many other HEAs [27]. Ti, V and Cr can form solid solution, whose 
hardness should be higher than that of the pure Ti, V or Cr. The addition of Fe and Zr also 
could increase the alloy hard ness through the formation of solid solution and/or 
intermetallic phases. As shown in Table 1, the atomic radius of Zr is much larger than other 
elements. Therefore, the presence of Zr in the alloy makes the alloy possessing a large 
atomic distortion and i nternal force, and hence, contributing to the high hardness of the 
alloy. 
Annealing at 1000 oC for 3 h did not cause alloy softening. The hardness of 
annealed V35Ti35Fe15Cr10Zr5 alloy is 572.68 Hv, slightly higher than that of as -cast alloy, 
showing the a lloy has high softening resistance owing to solid solution strengthening 
common to HEAs. Generally, annealing leads to alloy softening due to the internal stress 
relaxation caused by the alloy defects at high temperatures as a result of fast atom diffusion.

However, if there are hard phases, such as intermetallic compounds, formed in the alloy 
during annealing, the hardness of alloy may enhance [28]. For the current HEA studied here,

Although the volume fraction of BCC1 and BCC2 is not known before and after annealing,

cause the formation of small amount of nano -size hard phas e and the increase of ordering

3.5 Compression properties 
Figure 4 shows the compressive stress -strain curves of V35Ti35Fe15Cr10Zr5 alloy at 
different temperature from room -temperature to 900 oC, and t he compressive properties at 
elevated temperatures are summarized in Table 3. At room temperature, the alloy exhibits 
high yield strength, but the ductility of alloy is low and the sample fractured after yielding 
soon, which is the typical fracture character for high strength BCC phase. However, it was 
interesting to found that the strength and ductility increased with increasing the temperature 
below 500 oC. For example, the compressive yield strength increased from 917.8 MPa to 
1238.1 MPa, and the compression uniform strain increased from 0.67% to 1.37%, when the 
temperature increased from room temperature to 500 oC. Beyond 600 oC, the alloy strength 
decreased significantly. At 700 oC, the yield strength was 788.7 MPa, and that at 900 oC was

To understand the mechanism for the incre ase of yield strength below 500 oC and 
the decrease beyond 500 oC, TEM experiments were carried out for the samples deformed 
at 500 oC and 800 oC, and the results were given in Fig.5. It can be seen from Fig.5 (a) that, 
at 500 oC, the distribution of dislocations was not uniform. The slip of d islocation seemed 
to be confined to a certain slip plane and piled up at the phase boundaries, which makes the 
dislocation hard to slip or cross-slip. Hence, the yield strength increased. On the other hand, 
at 800 oC (Fig.5 (b)), dislocations easy to move and distributed uniformly within the phase 
due to the effect of thermal activation, although they may slip along a certain direction 
initially. Therefore, the strength decreased. The unusual increasing yield strength with 
increasing temperature in the V35Ti35Fe15Cr10Zr5 alloy was similar to that observed in the 
intermetallic compounds [29].

3.6 Fracture morphology 
Compression curves show obviously that the alloy has a little plastic deformation 
before fra cture below 700 oC. Fracture surfaces for the compression V35Ti35Fe15Cr10Zr5 
specimens tested at room and elevated temperatures are shown in Fig.7. As shown in 
Fig.7(a), a typical river -like pattern was observed at room temperature, indicating the 
characteristics of cleavage fracture. It was noticed that specimens tested at 400 oC and 500 
oC macroscopically exhibited a typical shear fracture mode with a shearing angle of about 
45o, and fracture morphologies at 400 oC and 500 oC were shown in Fig.7(b) and (c ), 
respectively. Slip patterns on the fractured surfaces could be observed, suggesting a plastic 
deformation occurred during fracture. The roughness of fractured surface c ould also 
provide qualitative information about ductility. It could be seen that the roughness of 
fracture surface increased as the temperature increased, which was consistent w ith the 
engineering stress-strain curves shown in Fig.4. At higher temperature of 600 oC and 700 oC, 
the ductile feature during fracture was more evident on the fra cture surfaces, as show n in 
Fig.7(d) and (e). The fracture surfaces of 800 oC and 900 oC are given in Fig.7(f) and (g). 
Many flat shear planes seem to be induced by dynamic instability of a rapidly propagating 
crack front were observed. Along with this kin d of pattern, micro-scale pattern, which is 
generated by crack front waves in the beginning of crack initiation, was also found. It 
seems that local plastic zone s generated during fracture process , and this may lead to 
improve the ductility of the alloy.

4 Conclusions 
An equiatomic VTiFeCrZr and a non -equiatomic V35Ti35Fe15Cr10Zr5 HEAs were 
fabricated by vacuum arc melting and the microstructure and mechanical properties were 
investigated. The following conclusions can be drawn: 
(1) The as-cast VTiFeCrZr HEA was very brittle and broken after arc-melting, because 
its parameter of solid solutio n criterion did not meet the requirement to from a solid 
solution due to the large difference of atomic size between Zr and other elements and the 
relative large amount of Zr in the alloy. 
(2) The as-cast V35Ti35Fe15Cr10Zr5 HEA consisted of a combination of BCC1 and 
BCC2 solid solution phases. Annealing at 1000 oC for 3 h did not change the microstructure, 
while caused the partial dissolution of the primary BCC1 phase and the phase fraction of 
BCC2 increased, resulting in the hardness increased from 554.3 Hv to 572.68 Hv. 
(3) The yield strength of as-cast V35Ti35Fe15Cr10Zr5 HEA increased with increasing the 
temperature below 500 oC, a nd was much high er than that of candidate alloys for fusion 
reactors in the temperature range of room temperature to 900 oC. At 700 oC, the yield 
strength of V35Ti35Fe15Cr10Zr5 HEA was about 500 MPa higher than that of candidate alloys. 
Although the as-cast V35Ti35Fe15Cr10Zr5 alloy has a relative low ductili ty below 700 oC, the 
alloy exhibits a good ductility above 700 oC.

Highlights 
A new V35Ti35Fe15Cr10Zr5 high-entropy alloy with a BCC structure was designed and 
fabricated. 
The yield strength of as-cast V35Ti35Fe15Cr10Zr5 HEA increased with increasing the 
temperature below 500oC. 
At 700 oC, the yield strength of V35Ti35Fe15Cr10Zr5 HEA was about 500 MPa higher 
than that of candidate alloys. 
V35Ti35Fe15Cr10Zr5 alloy has a relative low ductility below 700 oC, while the alloy 
exhibits a good ductility above 700oC
