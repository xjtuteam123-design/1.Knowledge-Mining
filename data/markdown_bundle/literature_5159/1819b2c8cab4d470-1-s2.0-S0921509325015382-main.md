ABSTRACT
In order to achieve high strength and high conductivity of typical precipitation-strengthened CuCrZr alloys, the 
combination of plastic deformation (rolling at room and cryogenic temperatures) and aging treatment was 
applied in this study, and the influence of microstructure on the strength-conductivity trade-off in CuCrZr alloys 
was investigated. The results show that cryogenic temperature rolling (CTR, 196 
◦
C) combined with aging 
treatment (400 
◦
C for 3 h) achieves an optimal balance of mechanical (ultimate tensile strength (UTS) 605.74 
MPa) and electrical properties (83.42 % IACS). Moreover, the CTR + peak-aged (CTRA) samples exhibit a 15.71 
% enhancement in UTS with merely a 0.034 % reduction in electrical conductivity compared to room temper -
ature rolling + peak-aged (RTRA) samples. Cryogenic deformation of the CuCrZr alloy results in a finer grain size 
and higher dislocation density, while simultaneously promoting twinning deformation. The calculations results 
show that grain boundary strengthening and precipitation strengthening are the primary factors for the high 
strength of CuCrZr alloys. Furthermore, the superior yield strength (YS) of the CTRA sample (587.31 MPa) 
relative to the RTRA sample (484.31 MPa) can be attributed to three main factors: the higher hetero-deformation 
induced (HDI) strengthening effect, the finer grain sizes, and the formation of nanotwins. The predominant factor 
determining the electrical conductivity of the alloy is the concentration of Cr atoms in the matrix. Therefore, the 
strength of the CTRA sample significantly enhanced by grain refinement, twins, dislocations, and precipitates 
through hindering dislocation motion, with additional strengthening from heterostructures; meanwhile, the 
precipitations purify the matrix, maintaining high conductivity. The results of this paper reveal the influence of 
microstructure on the strength and conductivity of CuCrZr alloys, leading to a simultaneous improvement in both 
mechanical and electrical properties.
1. Introduction
In recent decades, the rapid advancement of high-speed railway 
technology has become increasingly remarkable, and further accelera -
tion (exceeding 400 Km/h) is an inevitable development trend [ 1 , 2 ]. 
The increasingly speed necessitate higher stability, strength, and con -
ductivity of copper-based alloys used in contact wires. The CuCrZr alloys 
(0.15 – 0.85 wt% Cr, 0.08 – 0.25 wt% Zr) retaining the superior electrical 
conductivity (over 80 % IACS) of pure copper, enabling high speed and 
efficient transmission of electrical energy, meanwhile its high strength 
(over 530 MPa) ensures the long-term operational reliability of contact 
wires [ 3 – 6 ]. The addition of trace Cr and Zr significantly enhances the 
strength of the alloy by controlling the precipitation strengthening 
phases [ 7 , 8 ]. Compared with other strengthening elements commonly 
used in high strength and conductivity copper alloys — such as Fe, Co, Si, 
and Ti — Cr and Zr have a comparatively smaller impact on the electrical 
resistivity of the copper matrix [ 9 ]. Furthermore, due to their limited 
equilibrium solid solubility (at room temperature) in copper, most of 
these elements (Cr and Zr) exist in the form of precipitates, allowing the 
alloy to maintain excellent electrical conductivity while achieving 
enhanced mechanical properties. However, the trade-off between 
strength and electrical conductivity limits the further enhancement of 
the mechanical strength of copper alloys while maintaining their high 
electrical conductivity [ 10 , 11 ]. To meet the growing application de -
mands, a systematic investigation of comprehensive property optimi -
zation of CuCrZr alloy is imperative.

The combination of plastic deformation and aging treatment is 
proved as the effective method to achieve high strength and conduc -
tivity of a CuCrZr alloy [ 12 , 13 ]. Therein, rolling deformation usually 
induces a great increase in dislocation density and promotes grain 
refinement, however as the rolling deformation approaches 90 %, it is 
difficult to further continue to improve the strength of materials [ 14 ]. 
Research indicated that cryogenic temperature deformation can effec -
tively suppress dislocation recovery, leading to an increase in disloca -
tion density, while simultaneously promoting twinning deformation, 
resulting in a strength improvement of approximately 60 MPa with only 
a slight decrease in electrical conductivity of about 2 % [ 12 ]. Twin 
boundaries can enhance the strength of alloys as high-angle grain 
boundaries and have a lower detrimental impact on the electrical con -
ductivity relative to other interfaces (e.g. high-angle and low-angle grain 
boundaries) [ 15 , 16 ]. Lu et al. [ 17 ]prepared a thin foil of pure copper 
with high density nanoscale twins through electrodeposition, achieving 
a high strength of 1 GPa while maintaining 97 % IACS conductivity. 
Therefore, twinning deformation strengthening an effective strategy to 
simultaneously improve the strength and electrical conductivity of 
copper alloys.
Furthermore, for typical precipitation-strengthened alloys such as 
CuCrZr, the formation of dispersed nano-scale Cr phases during aging 
treatment plays a critical role in obtaining the high strength and elec -
trical conductivity. Analysis by Chu et al. [ 18 ] on the high strength and 
high conductivity copper alloy showed that Cr precipitates strength -
ening contributed to over 54 % of the yield strength, and the high 
density of dispersed precipitates caused a minimal effect on electrical 
conductivity. Under external stress, these coherent (or semi-coherent) Cr 
phases remain relatively stable, effectively preventing the initiation and 
propagation of microcracks [ 19 – 21 ]. Additionally, the aging process can 
reduce the lattice defects within the deformed alloy microstructure, and 
both twin boundaries and dislocations can be used as nucleation sites for 
the formation of precipitates. This process not only improves strength 
but also reduces electron scattering caused by solute atoms [ 22 , 23 ]. Li 
et al. [ 24 ] achieved an ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of 712 MPa and 
an electrical conductivity of 70.2 % IACS through cryogenic tempera -
ture deformation (the reduction of 90 %) followed by aging treatment, 
primarily due to the synergistic strengthening effects of grain refine -
ment, precipitation strengthening, and deformation twins. However, 
how the rolling and aging process affects the number and size of the 
twins and precipitates, as well as their mutual interactions and their 
impact on the strength and electrical conductivity, these scientific 
problems need to be further investigated.
In this study, a CuCrZr alloy was prepared by adding a small amount 
of Cr and Zr elements into the pure copper matrix. By combining rolling 
and aging processes, the influence of deformation twins and precipitates 
on the alloy ’ s strength and conductivity was investigated. This study 
aims to establish effective strategies for controlling the size and distri -
bution of twins and precipitates, reveal their synergistic effects on me -
chanical and electrical properties, thereby enhance the comprehensive 
performance of the CuCrZr alloy.

grains and the twinning deformation in CuCrZr alloys. Therefore,

interaction of twins and shear bands play a key role in the grain 
refinement of copper alloys with increasing strain during cryogenic 
processing [ 26 , 31 , 32 ], and most nano grains originate from the frag -
mentation of twin lamellae [ 33 ]. The elongated grains mainly result 
from geometric effects induced by compression and extension during 
rolling [ 34 ]. Moreover, cryogenic conditions inhibit dislocation slip and 
annihilation, retaining a high dislocation density that improves the

Similar to grain refinement strengthening, twin boundaries can 
hinder dislocation motion and stimulate dislocation multiplication by 
reducing the mean free path of dislocation movement, leading to high 
strain hardening [ 36 , 37 ]. Consequently, twinning can also effectively 
enhance the mechanical properties of alloys [ 24 ]. Moreover, the elec -
trical resistivity of twin boundaries is much lower than that of 
high-angle grain boundaries, leading to the minimal impact on con -
ductivity [ 17 ]. The main factors influencing the formation of deforma -
tion twins include stacking fault energy (SFE), deformation temperature, 
and strain rate. Research has shown that trace Zr additions can effec -
tively reduce the SFE of copper alloy [ 38 , 39 ], indicating that low solute 
content also plays a crucial role in promoting twin formation [ 23 ]. The 
Zener-Hollomon ( Z ) parameter can be used to characterize the effect of 
strain rate ( ˙ε ) and deformation temperature ( T ) on twinning behavior. 
Dislocation activity dominates the deformation mode at low Z condi -
tions, whereas twinning behavior at high Z conditions. This parameter is 
expressed as [ 40 ]:

deformation. Reducing the deformation temperature to liquid nitrogen 
levels leads to a decrease in Z value, thereby stimulating the formation of 
nanotwins [ 40 ]. The thickness of twins is closely related to their 
nucleation and subsequent growth. Studies have reported that the low 
SFE and deformation temperature of copper alloys can restrict disloca -
tion cross slipping and climbing, not only leading to higher flow stress, 
which enhances the nucleation driving force, but also reducing the 
embryonic thickness of twins [ 41 – 43 ]. Therefore, a large number of 
ultrafine nanotwins are observed in the CTRA sample ( Fig. 6 ), whereas 
no obvious twinning phenomenon is observed in the RTRA sample.

5. Conclusion
In this study, CuCrZr alloys with high strength and excellent elec -
trical conductivity were prepared by controlling deformation tempera -
ture and aging treatment. By combining microstructure 
characterization, mechanical property measurement, and strengthening 
calculation, the synergistic mechanism for improving both strength and 
electrical conductivity was investigated, leading to the following 
conclusions: 
1) Through CTR followed by aging treatment (400 
◦
C/3 h), the alloy 
achieves a better balance among high UTS (605.74 MPa), hardness 
(218.6 HV), and electrical conductivity (83.42 % IACS). The UTS of 
the CTRA sample increases by 15.71 %, and the electrical conduc -
tivity decreases by only 0.034 % compared to the conventional room 
temperature deformation of the RTRA sample.
2) Compared to RTR, CTR results in finer grains, higher dislocation 
density, and the deformation of nanotwins. These microstructures 
provide more nucleation sites for precipitates in CuCrZr alloy. A 
large number of nano precipitated phases are present within the 
grains and at the grain boundaries of both RTR and CTR samples. The 
high dislocation density in the CTR sample facilitates additional 
diffusion pathways for Cr atoms, and thereby accelerates the for -
mation of bcc-Cr precipitates.
3) The calculation of strengthening factors results indicate that grain 
boundary strengthening and precipitation strengthening are the 
primary factors contributing to the strength enhancement of the 
CuCrZr alloy. The higher YS of the CTRA sample compared to the 
RTRA sample is mainly attributed to more effective HDI strength -
ening, grain refinement strengthening and twinning strengthening. 
Moreover, these microstructures have a minimal impact on electrical 
conductivity, which is mainly influenced by Cr atoms dissolved in the 
matrix, thereby enabling the CTRA sample to maintain good con -
ductivity through the precipitation of a large number of phases.
