a b s t r a c t 
Precipitation-strengthened medium/high-entropy alloys (MEAs/HEAs) have great potential for high- 
temperature applications. In this study, we designed a novel Ni45.9 Fe23 Cr23 V4 Nb3 Mo1 B0.1 (at.%) MEA alloy, 
hardened by the D022 (Ni, Fe, Cr)3 (Nb, V)-type nanoprecipitates, with an excellent strength-ductility com- 
bination from room to elevated temperatures. Speciﬁcally, the tensile strengths, at 700 and 800 °C, could 
be maintained as high as 845 and 589 MPa, respectively; meanwhile, elongations at all testing temper- 
atures exceeded 25 % without any intermediate-temperature embrittlement. The temperature-dependent 
deformation mechanisms were unraveled using multi-scale characterizations, which involved profound 
slip planarities, such as stacking fault (SF) networks and deformation twins (DTs). Furthermore, the crit- 
ical resolved shear stress (CRSS) to initiate SFs in both face-centered cubic (FCC) and D022 phases was 
evaluated, and the possible reasons for the origin of anomalous DTs at 800 °C were discussed in de- 
tail. The main ﬁndings demonstrate that the shearable D022 nanoparticles can provide the FCC matrix 
with considerable dislocation storage capacity, reinforcing strain hardening at ambient and intermedi- 
ate temperatures. This work provides fundamental insights into the controllable design and deformation 
mechanisms of high-performance D022 -strengthened MEAs/HEAs.

1. Introduction 
Harsh service conditions in the industry, particularly in 
the aerospace, power, and nuclear sectors, have long re- 
quired metallic materials with good strength-ductility balances 
over a wide temperature range. Recently, the rapidly de- 
veloping precipitation-strengthened medium/high-entropy alloys 
(MEAs/HEAs) have emerged as potential candidates for these en- 
gineering applications [ 1 , 2 ]. Different from the conventional wis- 
dom of adding few elements to a single primary element, the

A few recent studies have demonstrated this, one notable exam-

manufactured MEAs/HEAs at cryogenic and/or ambient tempera-

tures of these promising D022 -strengthened MEAs/HEAs have re-

example, the tensile elongations of D022 -strengthened Ni2 CoCr-

temperature (RT) to 12.8 % at 650 °C [ 18 , 19 ]. These results re- 
mind us that, given the remarkable hardening effect of D022 
nanoparticles, there is still much room for improving the strength- 
ductility synergy in D022 -strengthened MEAs/HEAs at elevated 
temperatures. 
Based on the above status, a novel D022 -strengthened MEA 
was designed in the present work, which exhibits an excellent 
strength-ductility balance over ambient and intermediate temper- 
atures. We systematically investigated its microstructures using 
multi-scale characterizations and conducted a comprehensive com- 
parison of its tensile properties with some other alloys (includ- 
ing MEAs/HEAs and Ni-based superalloys) at 700 and 800 °C. 
Particular emphasis was placed on elucidating its temperature- 
dependent deformation mechanisms, in particular concerning 
the interaction between D022 nanoparticles and stacking faults 
(SFs), which has rarely been reported in other D022 -strengthened 
MEAs/HEAs. The present study provided an effective strategy 
for the controllable design of D022 -strengthened MEAs/HEAs 
with enhanced strength and ductility for advanced structural 
applications. 
2. Materials and methods 
2.1. Alloy design 
Directed by the goal of strengthening MEAs applied at a wide 
temperature range, the present study designed a novel D022 - 
strengthened Ni45.9 Fe23 Cr23 V4 Nb3 Mo1 B0.1 (at.%) MEA based on the 
CALPHAD (Calculation of Phase Diagram) technique. Proportions of 
Ni and Fe, stabilizers of the FCC structure, were controlled to be 
about 46 and 23 at.%. A signiﬁcant amount of Cr (23 at.%) was 
added to promote oxidation resistance. Slight additions of V and 
Nb (4 and 3 at.%, respectively) promoted the formation of the D022 
phase with an ordered tetragonal structure [20] , which was de- 
termined by the Thermo-Calc software with a TTNI8 database, as 
highlighted by the yellow star in Fig. 1 (a). Since the δ phase with 
D0a structure is the end product of the metastable D022 phase, a 
low aging temperature of 750 °C was selected to suppress its oc- 
currence, as highlighted by the green circle in Fig. 1 (b). Finally, mi- 
nor Mo and B (1 and 0.1 at.%, respectively) were alloyed for solu- 
tion strengthening and strengthening grain boundary. In addition, 
Fig. 1 (b) indicates quite large “FCC + δ”d u a l - p h a s e as well as single 
FCC phase regions in the designed alloy system, implying consid- 
erable heat treatment temperature windows. So that the homoge- 
nized temperature can be chosen as 1150 °C, as highlighted by the 
blue circle in Fig. 1 (b).

times before drop-casting into a water-cooled copper mold with a

for 2 h to reduce elemental segregation. The obtained alloys were

2.4. Mechanical testing 
The mean microhardness of each aged sample was obtained 
from at least six measurements using a Vicker micro-hardness 
tester (Wilson, VH1102), with a load of 2 N and a duration of 
15 s. Flat dog-bone-shaped samples with a gauge dimension of 
12.5 mm × 1.0 mm × 3.2 mm were cut by electro-discharge ma- 
chining. All surfaces of the gauge section were ground to a 2500- 
grit ﬁnish using SiC paper. Ambient temperature tensile testing 
was performed at a strain rate of 1 × 10–3 s–1 on a Material Testing 
System (MTS, Alliance RT30) tension machine equipped with a 10- 
mm extensometer. High-temperature tensile testing was conducted 
using a SUST tension machine at the same strain rate. During the 
testing, three K-type thermocouples were used to precisely control 
the testing temperature within the furnace. At least three samples 
were tested for each condition to conﬁrm the data reproducibility.

3.3. Fractography analysis 
Specimens after tensile tests were carefully analyzed using 
EBSD and SEM to reveal the fracture behavior and associated 
mechanisms. Fig. 7 (a1–c1) presents the IPF maps and (111) pole 
ﬁgures close to fractured specimens tested at RT, 700 °C, and 
800 °C, respectively. Compared with the initial microstructure 
( Fig. 2 (b)), grains were elongated along the tensile direction and 
there was no signiﬁcant grain growth when deformed at both 
room temperature and elevated temperatures. However, it can be 
found from the inserted pole ﬁgures that crystal orientation devel- 
oped towards a ⟨ 111 ⟩ ﬁber texture, which resulted from the activa- 
tion of {111} slip systems in FCC metals during loading [53] . The 
corresponding kernel average misorientation (KAM) maps were 
employed to evaluate the distribution of geometrically necessary 
dislocations (GNDs), as shown in Fig. 7 (a2–c2). At these three rep- 
resentative testing temperatures, GBs and annealing twin bound- 
aries (ATBs) possess higher KAM values than those of grain inte- 
rior, implying a high-level density of GNDs near them. In addi- 
tion, it can be found that the dislocation density decreases with 
increasing testing temperatures, which may relate to the lower 
ﬂow stress due to dynamic recovery and recrystallization during 
high-temperature deformation. Subsequently, SEM images of the 
overall fracture surfaces reveal how fracture behaviors changed 
with the rising temperature, as shown in Fig. 7 (d). The green ar- 
eas represent ductile (transgranular) fracture, evidenced by numer- 
ous dimples ( Fig. 7 (e)); and the uncolored stand for brittle (in- 
tergranular) fracture, as shown in Fig. 7 (f). It is clear that the 
present alloy exhibits a fully transgranular fracture mode under 
650 °C while a mixed mode of transgranular and intergranular 
fracture can be observed over 700 °C. Yet, the area fractions of 
completely intergranular fracture were identiﬁed as only 3.76 % 
and 10.13 % when deformed at 700 and 800 °C, respectively. It 
demonstrates that this D022 -strengthened MEA has a strong re- 
sistance to intergranular embrittlement at intermediate temper- 
atures, which is diﬃcult to achieve in many other conventional 
alloys [8] . 
4. Discussion 
4.1. Temperature-dependent deformation mechanisms of the 
D022 -strengthened MEA 
To understand the superb combinations of strength and 
ductility, especially at elevated temperatures, the temperature- 
dependent deformation mechanisms will be discussed with the 
aid of systematic TEM investigations. First, the deformation struc- 
ture after fracture at room temperature was disclosed, as shown in 
Fig. 8 . It can be seen from Fig. 8 (a) that the deformation substruc- 
tures exhibit typical characteristics of planar slip, containing ﬁnely- 
dislocation network structures, namely the Taylor lattices, and the 
high-density dislocation walls (HDDWs). These dense dislocation 
walls and Taylor lattice domains can effectively inhibit dislocation 
motion, leading to a signiﬁcant strain-hardening effect [54] . The 
presence of microbands is also conﬁrmed by the insert of the SAED 
pattern along the [110] zone axis, as shown in Fig. 8 (b), which 
shows the spots were elongated owing to non-strict Bragg diffrac- 
tion [55] . Microbands are regions within dislocation walls that 
can accommodate the misﬁt orientation between the Taylor lattice 
domains, serving as an effective carrier of GNDs [54] . The well- 
developed microbands in the current alloy can therefore accom- 
modate a large number of planar dislocations and at the same time 
induce uniform plastic deformation by delocalizing stress through 
their intersections [56] . Moreover, the in-grain interactions of these

and micro-twins) [ 58 , 59 ]. During the initial deformation stage of 
FCC-type alloys with shearable precipitates, gliding of planar dislo- 
cations on {111} planes is identiﬁed as the dominant deformation 
mechanism [60] . Once some preceding dislocations shear the or- 
dered precipitates, the gliding of the trailing dislocations on the 
same slip plane will become easier due to the ‘‘glide plane soft- 
ening’’ effect [61] . Thus, when the alloy suffers from severe tensile 
deformation, the uniformly dispersed shearable precipitates tend 
to endow the FCC matrix with a considerable capacity for disloca- 
tion accumulation [62] , which will promote further gliding of pla- 
nar dislocations thus enhancing uniform deformation and postpon- 
ing the onset of necking due to the plastic instability. 
TEM results of the deformation substructures at 700 °C are 
demonstrated in Fig. 9 . It can be seen clearly from Fig. 9 (a) 
that, under the two-beam condition where (200) plane is ex- 
cited, gliding of SFs with continuous conﬁgurations on {111} planes 
are prevalent in the FCC matrix. Closer inspection further re- 
veals that the interactions of SFs further result in the formation 
of SF networks, as shown in Fig. 9 (b). In addition to impeding 
the movement of dislocations, these nano-spaced SF networks can 
also lead to the formation of immobile Lomer–Cottrell locks (L–
C locks) [7] , which can act effectively as Frank-Read sources for 
dislocation multiplication [63] . The high-density SFs and associ- 
ated SF networks are therefore responsible for the outstanding 
strain-hardening capacity of the present alloy that remains at 700 
°C. Furthermore, it is evident that the extension of these SFs is 
not hindered by the D022 nanoparticles, but shear through them, 
which is the same case as the sample tested at room tempera- 
ture. To achieve an energetically favorable slip path, the a /2 < 110 > 
full dislocation in the FCC matrix tends to decompose into two 
a /6 < 112 > Shockley partial dislocations, of necessity, between 
which the stacking fault must also be generated [64] . When the 
partial dislocations further encounter the D022 nanoparticle, the 
shearing of a /6 < 112 > partial dislocations leave the stable in- 
trinsic stacking faults (ISFs) within the D022 phase by overcom- 
ing the lowest energy barrier ( ∼2.3 mJ m–2 ) among all other fault 
types [ 65 , 66 ]. A representative HRTEM image in Fig. 9 (c) shows the 
ISFs inside the D022 phase, which are conﬁrmed by the inserted 
SAED pattern. Fig. 9 (d) gives a close view of the ISF induced by 
the a /6 [¯1 1¯2 ] partial dislocation, with a stacking sequence of …AB- 
CABABCABC… along the [¯1 11] close-packed plane. Further tilting 
the sample to the two-beam condition of [111] yields the BF TEM 
images shown in Fig. 9 (e, f), revealing the distinct interaction be- 
tween dislocations and D022 particles. Fig. 9 (f) clearly shows that 
D022 particles have been sheared by dislocations. In addition, due 
to the small volume fraction of the D022 phase, the dislocations are 
more likely to lie in separated particles during shearing, indicating 
a weak-pair coupling behavior [67] . Collectively, the strain hard- 
ening of the D022 -strengthened MEA at 700 °C mainly originates 
from the high-density SFs and SF networks. 
When deformed at 800 °C, the D022 -strengthened MEA shows 
a slow tendency of strain softening, yet it still maintains a consid- 
erable fracture elongation near 25 %. The tendency for the stress to 
fall is accompanied by a slight ﬂuctuation (see Fig. 6 ), which im- 
plies that the underlying deformation mechanism retards the sharp 
drop in engineering stress. TEM observations were then made on a 
sample stretched to fracture at 800 °C, the developed microstruc- 
tures could be grouped into two categories, i.e. stacking faults and 
deformation twins, as shown in Fig. 10 . Fig. 10 (a) demonstrates 
that stacking faults along {111} planes, with relatively intermittent 
conﬁgurations, are prevailed in the FCC matrix. The correspond- 
ing SAED pattern testiﬁes to the presence of SFs, in the diffraction 
pattern streaks occurred at both FCC and D022 reﬂections along 
directions perpendicular to the {111} planes, which indicates the 
shearing of D022 particles by SFs. DF image obtained by using one 
(002) superlattice reﬂection is presented in Fig. 10 (b), clearly re- 
vealing that D022 nanoparticles have been fragmented into slices 
along the {111} planes. The shearing of the D022 phase is further 
evidenced by the HRTEM image shown in Fig. 10 (c), demonstrating 
the penetration of a stacking fault from the FCC matrix into the 
D022 nanoparticle. The FFT patterns of the highlighted area show 
streaking along the direction related to that stacking fault in both 
the FCC and D022 reciprocal space. It is also interesting to note 
that, in addition to the prevailed stacking faults, deformation mi- 
crostructures are also accompanied by some DTs in a few grains. 
Fig. 10 (d) gives a typical BF TEM image displaying the SFs and DTs 
within the FCC matrix. To further elucidate how the DTs interact 
with the D022 particles, DF TEM images were captured by using 
different diffraction spots. By taking both (002)T twin and (002)P 
superlattice reﬂections (circled in green in Fig. 10 (d)), the captured 
DF TEM image in Fig. 10 (e) reveals the DTs with an average thick- 
ness of ∼14.4 nm and D022 precipitates with one variant. It is ev- 
ident that the DTs penetrated those nanoparticles located at their 
extension path (oval circled in yellow). This is proved by the DF 
image taken by only using (002)P precipitate reﬂection (circled in 
yellow in Fig. 10 (d)), as seen in Fig. 10 (f). It is shown that the frag- 
ments of the D022 particles were left behind after shearing by both 
SFs and DTs. Based on these, it is evidenced that the deformation 
microstructures at 800 °C are dominated by intermittent SFs and a 
few DTs, both of which exhibit the potential to shear D022 parti- 
cles. Such complex interactions between SFs, DTs, and precipitates 
make the present MEA resist the drastic softening and thus main- 
tain a large fracture elongation [9] . The reason for the presence of 
DTs in samples that fractured at 800 °C will be discussed later. 
The deformation mechanism evolution of the D022 - 
strengthened MEA from room temperature to elevated temperature 
is schematically summarized in Fig. 11 . At ambient temperature, 
HDDWs, and microbands together with SFs are dominant defor- 
mation modes. As the testing temperature increases to 700 °C, 
high-density SFs and SF networks are more prevalent in the 
current alloy. Stacking faults, with less continuous conﬁguration, 
accompanied by a few DTs are the main deformation microstruc- 
tures at 800 °C. Evidently, the D022 nanoparticles of the present 
alloy are invariably shearable when encountered with different 
dislocation conﬁgurations from room to high temperatures. Hence, 
these D022 nanoparticles, which allow dislocations to cut through, 
contribute to the excellent ductility of the studied alloy.

4.3. On the origin of DTs in the sample fractured at 800 °C 
Among all the detected deformation substructures, of particular 
note is the deformation twins found in the fractured sample at 800 
°C, which is usually observed in FCC materials at cryogenic temper- 
atures and/or high strain rates [76] . This is, of course, not a prece- 
dent as similar ﬁndings have been made in some Ni-based alloys 
[ 69 , 77 ] and other MEAs/HEAs [ 9 , 12 ] with relatively low SFE. How- 
ever, given the SFE of the present alloy determined in Section 4.2 , 
the critical uniaxial tensile stress ( σt = 6 . 14 γSF /bp ) [73] to initiate 
twinning is estimated to be ∼1167.8 MPa, which is higher than the 
ultimate tensile true stress at 800 °C. This suggests that DTs are 
unlikely to operate extensively at elevated temperatures. 
Several models with respect to the formation of DTs in FCC al- 
loys have been comprehensively reviewed by Christian and Ma- 
hajan [78] , with all-embracing a common theme that the decor- 
relation of full a /2 < 110 > dislocations is a necessary pre-cursor 
to deformation twinning. It is well believed that the resultant 
a /6 < 112 > Shockley partial dislocations with the same Burgers 
vector, namely twinning partials, propagate on multiple, adjacent 
{111} planes and leave overlapping SFs [79] . Usually, twinning par-

5. Conclusions 
In this work, a novel D022 -strengthened Ni45.9 Fe23 Cr23 V4 
Nb3 Mo1 B0.1 (at.%) MEA was designed and its microstructures, me- 
chanical properties, and deformation mechanisms were systemati- 
cally investigated. The main conclusions can be drawn as follows: 
(1) The present disc-like D022 nanoparticles possess an average 
long axis of 37.13 nm, an average short axis of 7.05 nm, a vol- 
ume fraction of 10.6 %, and a stoichiometry of (Ni, Fe, Cr)3 (Nb, 
V); 
(2) The ultimate tensile strength and fracture elongation of this al- 
loy is 845 MPa and 27.5 % at 700 °C, 589 MPa, and 24.3 % at 
800 °C, respectively, suggesting a promising property for high- 
temperature applications; 
(3) The critical resolved shear stress to initiate SFs in the FCC ma- 
trix can be estimated to be ∼410 MPa, which is obviously lower 
than the actual applied stress. The prevalent SFs in the FCC 
matrix can shear the D022 phase and leave ISFs inside it due 
to its ultralow SFE. Low-content shearable D022 nanoparticles 
provide the FCC matrix with higher dislocation storage capacity 
and promote uniform deformation of the current alloy; 
(4) The origin of anomalous DTs in some grains at 800 °C may be 
related to grain boundary precipitates induced localized stress 
concentration and locally low SFE and especially may stem 
from the high diffusion rate at GBs leading to a much higher 
probability of twinning partials cross-slip event.
