ABSTRACT
As the demand for high-temperature structural materials in modern industry ’ s continues to grow, refractory 
high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) have garnered increasing attention because of their excellent high-temperature 
strength and structural stability. However, their high density and room-temperature brittleness limit practical 
applications. The effects of TiZr content on the structural stability and mechanical properties of (TiZr)x(VNbMo) 
(100-x) lightweight RHEAs (TZ alloys) are investigated across different temperatures in this study. First- 
principles density functional theory (DFT) and ab initio molecular dynamics (AIMD) simulations are 
employed to analyze these effects. The calculation results show that increasing TiZr content improves the 
structural stability and ductility at room temperature but reduces the strength. At high temperatures, although 
the ductility improves, the structural stability and strength significantly decrease due to the lattice thermal 
expansion. By quantifying the local lattice distortion (LLD), this study elucidates the atomic-scale mechanism: 
TiZr content affects mechanical properties of TZ alloys through competitive lattice expansion and local structural 
disorder at different temperatures. This study provides a theoretical basis for designing new RHEAs with high 
strength, toughness, and thermal stability, and enhances understanding of the strength-ductility-stability balance 
in lightweight RHEAs.
1. Introduction
With the rapid development of industries such as aerospace, energy, 
chemical engineering, and metallurgy, traditional superalloys increas -
ingly face limitations under extreme conditions, struggling to meet the 
escalating demand for materials with exceptional high-temperature 
strength and structural stability. In recent years, refractory high- 
entropy alloys (RHEAs), inspired by the high-entropy alloy (HEA) 
design concept [ 1 – 5 ], have emerged as promising candidates due to 
their outstanding high-temperature performance, including high 
melting points, exceptional hardness, wear resistance, and superior 
strength retention at elevated temperatures [ 6 – 10 ]. For example, classic 
RHEAs such as NbMoTaW and NbMoTaWV exhibit a stable body- 
centered cubic (BCC) structure, maintaining structural integrity at 
temperatures exceeding 1600 
◦
C with a room-temperature yield 
strength surpassing 1000 MPa [ 11 ]. Even at 1400 
◦

strengths remain in the range of 400 – 600 MPa, significantly out -
performing conventional alloys like Inconel 718 and Haynes 230, which 
exhibit yield strengths below 200 MPa at 1000 
◦
C [ 11 ].
Despite these advantages, the widespread application of RHEAs is 
hindered by two critical challenges: low specific strength (high density) 
and significant room-temperature brittleness (low ductility). To address 
these issues, lightweight elements (e.g., Ti, Zr, V) have been introduced 
to partially replace refractory metals, achieving notable improvements 
in the specific strength and ductility. For instance, TiZrVMo alloy 
demonstrates a compressive yield strength exceeding 1300 MPa [ 12 ], 
while Nb0.75ZrTiV0.5 alloy exhibits a compressive yield strength of 
over 1000 MPa with a compressive plasticity of 40 % [ 13 ]. Similarly, 
TiZrNbMoV alloy achieves a compressive yield strength of 2222 MPa 
and a specific strength of 312 kPa ⋅ m

plasticity exceeding 20 % [ 14 ]. Other alloy systems further highlight the 
potential of lightweight elements addition such as Ti2ZrNbHfV and 
NbTa0.5TiZr0.28 [ 15 , 16 ]. However, it is worth noting that while 
lightweight elements improve mechanical properties such as specific 
strength and room temperature plasticity, they also bring some adverse 
effects. Lightweight elements like Ti may compromise structural sta -
bility and high-temperature strength because of their low melting points 
and weak interatomic bonding [ 7 , 9 , 17 ]. Additionally, the significant

The composition design of RHEAs determines the performance of 
alloys and is crucial for practical applications. To reduce reliance on 
costly trial-and-error experimentations, first-principles calculations, 
specifically density functional theory (DFT), have become indispensable 
for exploring the effects of elemental composition on the structural 
stability and mechanical properties. By analyzing lattice constants, 
formation energies, and elastic constants, DFT studies reveal atomic- 
scale mechanisms through tools such as density of states, differential 
charge density, and electron localization functions [ 18 – 23 ]. For 
instance, Hu et al. [ 20 ] demonstrated that V enhances the microhard -
ness of NbMoTaW by modifying charge density distribution, while Tong 
et al. [ 21 ] attributed the ductility improvement in NbMoTaWV to the 
severe lattice distortion and Zr-Zr metallic bonding induced by large 
atomic radius of Zr. Chen et al. [ 22 ] studied the phase structure and 
mechanical properties of Al/V-NbMoTiZr RHEAs and revealed the cor -
relation between the ductility and metallic bonds such as Ti-Ti, Zr-Zr, 
and Zr-Al through electronic structure analysis. Jin et al. [ 23 ] discovered 
that increasing V content can improve the yield strength of HfNbTaTiVx 
and unveiled the mechanism of the solid-solution strengthening (SSS) 
effect by V-induced local lattice distortion (LLD). Despite these ad -
vances, DFT calculations are inherently limited in simulating 
temperature-dependent behaviors, leaving the mechanisms governing 
high-temperature structural stability and mechanical properties 
unresolved.
To overcome the limitation of DFT, ab initio molecular dynamics 
(AIMD) has emerged as a complementary tool for simulating atomic 
dynamics and lattice evolution at elevated temperatures [ 24 – 27 ]. For 
example, Huang et al. [ 24 ] combined AIMD with special quasi-random 
structure (SQS) models to study the thermodynamic properties of 
AlNiCuCo, while Zhang et al. [ 25 ] applied AIMD and quasi-harmonic 
approximation (QHA) to calculate temperature-dependent elastic con -
stants of FCC Al. Chen et al. [ 26 ] used AIMD and QHA to calculate the 
elastic properties of FCC CoCrFeNi at different temperatures. Ali et al. 
[ 27 ] calculated the elastic constants of NiCoFeCrMn and its subsystems 
under various temperatures and pressures using AIMD. However, AIMD 
still faces significant challenges in practical applications, especially due 
to the computational burden caused by large systems and long simula -
tion times, which are further exacerbated by multi-component and high- 
entropy effects. Fortunately, recent advances in machine-learning force 
fields (MLFFs) have partially solved these issues of AIMD by enabling 
efficient simulations of large systems over extended time scales [ 28 , 29 ]. 
However, existing AIMD studies on RHEAs are still scarce, and it is 
necessary to further investigate the structural stability and mechanical 
properties of RHEAs at different temperatures and compositions to 
reveal the temperature composition performance relationship.
In this work, a TiZrVNbMo RHEA system is investigated. Considering 
the structural differences between hexagonal close-packed (HCP) ele -
ments (Ti, Zr) and BCC elements (V, Nb, Mo), structural stability, me -
chanical properties, and local lattice distortion (LLD) under different 
temperatures are elucidated by systematically varying the TiZr content. 
Combining DFT and AIMD simulations, SQS supercell models with 
specific compositions were constructed, the LLD levels were quantified, 
and their influences on the bulk modulus, shear modulus, elastic 
anisotropy, and fracture toughness were analyzed. This study not only 
provides a theoretical framework for designing RHEA with balanced 
strength, ductility, and structural stability, but also reveals the mecha -
nism of the influence of TiZr content on structural stability and me -
chanical properties at different temperatures.

3. Results and analysis
3.1. Structural stability
The structural stability of TiZrVNbMo RHEAs under different tem -
peratures was investigated by combining the DFT and AIMD simula -
tions. The equilibrium lattice parameters and structural stability at 0 K 
were determined through the EOS. Subsequently, the high-temperature 
structural evolution behaviour was studied through isothermal anneal -
ing simulations at 298 K, 673 K, 1073 K, and 1473 K. RDF analysis was 
used to further evaluate the lattice order and structural stability.
Fig. 2 presents the EOS analysis results and average lattice constants 
of TZ alloys. The energy-volume ( E-V ) curves, as shown in Fig. 2 a, are 
well-fitted by EOS and aligned closely with the DFT data, each dis -
playing a single minimum point. This indicates that the supercell 
structures of TZ alloys are stable at 0 K. The coordinates of these mini -
mum points corresponded to the volumes and energies of the balanced 
structures. Higher TiZr content correlates to larger volumes and en -
ergies. Fig. 2 b demonstrates the increasing trend of average lattice 
constants (Ave. L) with TiZr content. Ave. L by EOS was derived from the 
balanced structure volume in Fig. 2 a, while Ave. L by reference [ 32 ] and 
by calculation were based on r

During the AIMD annealing simulation process, the energy ( E ) and 
volume ( V ) per atom fluctuate due to the use of the Langevin thermostat 
to control the temperature. Figs. 3 and 4 depict the E and V per atom 
curves for each TZ alloy during annealing at various temperatures. The 
average values and fluctuations of E and V are influenced by the TiZr 
content and temperature. Usually, the more constant and less fluctuating 
E and V , the higher the structural stability. At 298 K, TZ10 displayed an 
initial decrease in E and an increase in V , suggesting structural insta -
bility. No such behavior was observed in other TZ alloys, implying 
enhanced room-temperature stability compared to TZ10. For a given 
TiZr content, the average values of E and V both increased with tem -
perature, accompanied by greater fluctuations. This phenomenon re -
flects the decrease in structural stability caused by the lattice thermal 
expansion and enhanced atomic vibration. Fig. 3 f and 4f summarize the 
average values and standard errors of E and V during annealing. At a 
fixed temperature, E and V increased linearly with TiZr content, 
consistent with the EOS results. The temperature increased the average 
and fluctuation range of E and V . At 1473 K, the E and V averages 
increased by 1.734/1.876/2.013/2.325 % (for E ) and 3.551/3.828/ 
4.231/4.398 % (for V ) compared to 0 K, respectively. The fluctuation 
amplitudes (relative standard errors) were 0.148/0.151/0.151/0.170/ 
0.158 % (for E ) and 0.725/0.797/0.829/0.915/0.922 % (for V ), 
respectively. These findings indicate that higher TiZr content correlates 
with greater E and V averages and fluctuation ranges, diminishing the 
high-temperature structural stability.

arrangement, which indicates stronger stability. To further evaluate the 
structural stability at different temperatures, the RDF of each TZ alloy 
supercell was calculated after annealing at various temperatures. Fig. 6
illustrates the RDF of TZ alloys compared to standard BCC. The stronger 
similarity in shape and position between the RDF peaks of TZ alloys and 
the standard BCC reflects higher structural order and stability. At a fixed 
temperature, higher TiZr content led to the merging of the first and 
second nearest-neighbor peaks (1st and 2nd NNPs), resulting in reduced 
peak sharpness and distinguishability, which signified the decrease of 
the structural order and stability ( Fig. 6 a – e). At 298 K, the 1st NNP of 
TZ10 splited into two peaks at 2.45 Å and 2.90 Å, corresponding to the 
atomic radii of V and Nb elements, indicating the structural trans -
formation and instability. In contrast, other TZ alloys at 298 K exhibited 
the RDF patterns similar to those at 0 K, reflecting higher structural 
stability. At 673 K, the 1st and 2nd NNPs of TZ10 remained distin -
guishable, while those of TZ25, TZ40, and TZ55 became less so, and the 
NNPs of TZ70 were highly indistinct. At 1073 K, the NNPs of TZ10, 
TZ25, and TZ40 overlapped significantly but remained identifiable, 
whereas those of TZ55 and TZ70 were indistinct. At 1473 K, the NNPs of 
TZ10 and TZ25 were still distinguishable, while those of other alloys 
were nearly merged, indicating the highly structural disorder. Fig. 6 f 
further highlights the temperature-dependent behaviour of the 1st and 
2nd NNPs of TZ alloys. For a given TiZr content, increasing temperature 
reduced the height of the 1st NNPs and diminished the distinguishability 
of the 2nd NNPs, reflecting the weakened structural order and stability. 
Additionally, at a fixed temperature, the NNPs shifted to the right at 
higher TiZr content, accompanied by broadening and merging, which 
were consistent with enhanced volume fluctuations during annealing.
The above results indicate that at room temperature, a certain TiZr 
content ( > 10 %) can stabilize the structural instability caused by phase 
transition, but excessively high TiZr content ( > 55 %) significantly re -
duces the structural order and stability. At high temperatures, the 
increased TiZr content enhances the structural disorder and lowers the 
temperature threshold for NNPs merging, thereby causing a notable 
drop in structural stability.

This study investigates the influence of TiZr content on the structural 
stability and mechanical properties of TZ alloys at different tempera -
tures. Structural stability was evaluated using EOS diagrams, E-V curves 
during annealing, and the merging of NNPs in the RDF. The findings 
reveal that while all TZ alloys exhibit stable static structures, their high- 
temperature stability decreases with increasing TiZr-induced structural 
disorder. Mechanically, the strength-related bulk and shear modulus are 
influenced by the lattice expansion and structural disorder. As TiZr

lattice distortion (LLD) was considered to have a significant impact on 
the mechanical properties of RHEAs containing TiZr, serving as an 
atomic level mechanism for achieving strength plasticity equilibrium 
[ 68 , 69 ]. LLD reflects the disorder of the lattice structure locally and 
measures the degree to which atoms deviate from the standard lattice 
position. This study quantifies LLD in TZ alloys to explore how TiZr 
content affects these properties. RDF analysis demonstrates that near- 
neighbour distances (NNDs) are closely related to structural disorder, 
with the distribution of the 1st and 2nd NNDs serving as a measure of 
LLD.
Fig. 8 illustrates the NNDs distributions of TZ alloys at different 
temperatures. The analysis of the annealed supercell shows that an in -
crease in TiZr content leads to an increase in the average NNDs values, 
which reflects the decrease in bulk modulus caused by lattice expansion. 
The widening distribution range of NNDs corresponds to the merging 
and broadening of the 1st and 2nd NNPs in the RDF, indicating 
heightened structural disorder and reduced stability. In the BCC lattice, 
the 1st NNDs represent the average distances between nearest- 
neighbour atom pairs. When nearest-neighbour atoms are in close con -
tact, the 1st NNDs equal to the sum of the radius of the central atom and 
the average radius of the 1st near-neighbour atoms. Based on the 
average lattice constants from Fig. 2 b, the average atomic radii of TZ 
alloys at 0 K were 1.378/1.394/1.412/1.431/1.450 Å, respectively. The 
average 1st NNDs values at 0 K, as shown in Fig. 8 f, were 2.763/2.795/ 
2.828/2.861/2.901 Å, which were exactly twice the average atomic 
radii, demonstrating that the 1st NNDs effectively captured the influence 
of TiZr content on lattice expansion. For different elements, the 1st 
NNDs should be positively correlated with the atomic radius of that 
element. At the same TiZr content, Zr has the largest atomic radius and 
therefore the largest 1st NNDs, while V has the smallest, consistent with 
the results shown in Fig. 8 a – e. Comparing the 1st NNDs values at 0 K 
across TZ alloys in Fig. 8 f, the maximum increase in the average 1st 
NNDs with increasing TiZr content was 0.138 Å, with elemental 1st 
NNDs values of 0.089/0.138/0.055/0.138/0.084 Å, respectively. The 
largest increases were found in Zr and Nb, which wear consistent with 
the average 1st NNDs results. This indicates that the 1st NNDs associated 
with Zr and Nb are more stable and less susceptible to compression from 
LLD, contributing most to the merging of the 1st and 2nd NNPs in the 
RDF.

Zr and Nb, while the additional increase in the 2nd NNDs stemmed from 
local lattice distortions caused by near-neighbour atom pairs deviating

the distribution ranges of both 1st and 2nd NNDs, with Ti and V

TiZr content alloys, this impact was mitigated due to the enhanced 
initial structural disorder at 0 K.

5. Conclusions
In this study, first-principles density functional theory (DFT) and ab 
initio molecular dynamics simulations (AIMD) were employed to 
investigate the structural stability and mechanical properties of (TiZr)x 
(VNbMo)(100-x) refractory high-entropy alloys (TZ alloys) with varying 
TiZr content under different temperatures. The key role of local lattice 
distortion (LLD) was revealed. The major conclusions are as follows: 
(1) At room temperature, as TiZr content increased, the fracture 
toughness of TZ alloys increased while the elastic anisotropy 
index decreased, indicating a significant enhancement in plas -
ticity. This enhancement was attributed to the increased LLD. The 
large atomic radius of Zr atoms broadened the distribution of 
near-neighbour distances, and the lightweight Ti atoms increased 
near-neighbour disorder. These factors jointly reduced the 
anisotropy of shear modulus and suppressed the crack initiation 
and propagation, thus alleviating the room-temperature brittle -
ness of traditional BCC refractory alloys.
(2) At room temperature, TZ10 with low TiZr content exhibited 
insufficient LLD, which triggered a spontaneous TRIP effect. This 
caused a transformation from the cubic to the orthorhombic 
crystal system, leading to structural instability. However, when 
TiZr content was increased to TZ25 and above, the enhanced LLD 
raised the energy barrier of shear deformation, suppressing the 
spontaneous initiation of the TRIP effect and maintaining the 
stability of the BCC structure.
(3) At high temperatures, increasing TiZr content led to a decrease in 
bulk and shear moduli, resulting in reduced high-temperature 
strength. This was attributed to the weakening of atomic in -
teractions caused by LLD-induced lattice expansion. Additionally, 
the effect of LLD on improving plasticity gradually saturated at 
high temperatures. For low TiZr content alloys (TZ10, TZ25, and 
TZ40), elastic anisotropy decreased with increasing temperature. 
But for high TiZr content alloys (TZ55 and TZ70), initial struc -
tural disorder limited the improvement in plasticity. At 1473 K, 
the plasticity of all TZ alloys became similar.
(4) As TiZr content increased, volume and energy fluctuations during 
annealing intensified. The RDF peaks merging temperature 
decreased from 1473 K for TZ10 and TZ25 to 1073 K for TZ40 and 
TZ55. These observations indicated accelerated high- 
temperature structural instability. The synergistic effects of se -
vere LLD caused by TiZr addition and enhanced atomic vibrations 
due to elevated temperatures were identified as the main reasons 
for the reduced high-temperature structural stability.
(5) Adjusting TiZr content allows for balancing strength, plasticity, 
and stability at different temperatures. For example, TZ25 and 
TZ40 offer high bulk modulus and excellent plasticity at room 
temperature without spontaneous TRIP effects. At high temper -
atures, their strength decreases relatively little, while plasticity 
improves significantly, indicating strong structural stability. This 
study provides a foundation for designing novel RHEAs with 
“room-temperature plasticity, high-temperature strength, and 
thermal stability” via composition design and the LLD 
mechanism.
