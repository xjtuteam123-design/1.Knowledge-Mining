ABSTRACT 
An ultra-high strength TiZrNbTa (TZNT) high-entropy alloy (HEA) with good plasticity was attempted as a new 
reinforcing phase, which is introduced in the highly conductive Cu matrix to fabricate high-strength conductive 
composites. This work utilized a combination of a two-step ball milling method (first HEA mechanical alloying 
followed by fabrication of TZNT HEA/Cu hybrid powder) and a spark plasma sintering process to prepare the 
bulk TZNT HEA/Cu metal matrix composites (MMCs). Remarkably enhanced mechanical properties of the 
designed TZNT HEA/Cu MMCs with increasing TZNT contents were characterized while the electrical conduc -
tivity declined. Compared to the strength of the copper matrix, the strength of the fabricated 50 wt% TZNT/Cu 
specimens increased up to 340% induced by the second phase strengthening effect. The combination of grain 
boundary strengthening and dislocation strengthening contributed relatively less to the overall strength 
improvement of the composites. The TZNT/Cu composites were found to be a new insight into the high-strength 
conductive material preparation by choosing TZNT HEA with superior strength as hard secondary phase in 
copper matrix. 
1. Introduction 
Due to its excellent electrical and thermal conductivity, copper and 
its alloys has become the best candidate for electrical applications [ 1 – 3 ]. 
However, they are usually unable to meet the growing requirements of 
practical applications because of their low intrinsic strength [ 4 ]. Today, 
there is a growing demand for advanced materials with superior 
strength, high electrical conductivity and excellent thermal stability in 
the aerospace, electronics and transportation industries [ 5 ]. The devel -
opment and deployment of high strength-conductive material have 
facilitated intense related research [ 6 , 7 ]. 
Metal matrix composites (MMCs) are constituted by the high- 
stiffness second phase and the soft matrix, and exhibit superior 
strength and excellent tribological performance [ 8 , 9 ]. Despite the 
incorporated reinforcing phase will degrade electrical conductivity of 
the copper matrix, compounding remains one of the most effective ways 
to settle the trade-off between mechanical and electrical properties [ 10 ]. 
The existence of hard and brittle particles distributed in soft metal 
matrix significantly contributes to the strength of pure metals [ 11 ]. 
Indeed, many works have been dedicated to improving the strength of 
copper by the addition of hard secondary phase, such as TiC [ 12 ], Al

advantage for enhancing mechanical properties, their applications are

IACS in 20 vol% TiC/Cu composites [ 12 ]. By adding 5 wt% Al

However, their application as reinforcements is constrained mainly due 
to the low crystallization temperature and poor plasticity [ 19 ]. Thence, 
a novel reinforcement that combines high strength with good plasticity 
and excellent thermal stability is considered for the design of high 
strength-conductive composites. Motivated by this deliberation, high 
entropy alloys (HEAs) are adopted as reinforcement phases in Cu and/or

Cu-based alloys in view of their simultaneous ultra-high strength and

HEAs, as a novel material, have been rapidly developed and widely 
researched in the past decades. There are four core effects about the 
HEAs proposed by Yeh, which are high entropy effect, severe lattice 
distortion, sluggish diffusion, and the cocktail effect [ 20 , 21 ]. HEAs are 
constituted by multi-principal elements with different atomic radii, each 
of which has an equal or near-equal atomic percentage for maximizing 
the configurational entropy. A large configurational entropy is benefi -
cial to stabilize the solid solution phase. Compared with common and/or 
single-element-based alloys, HEAs with a solid solution phase present 
superior mechanical properties, which gives rise to deformability and a 
severely distorted lattice to strengthen the material [ 22 ]. Huang et al. 
[ 23 ] have exhibited an attempt of AlCrTiV HEA particle-reinforced 
aluminum composites. With the addition of 3 wt% of HEA particles, 
the yield strength (YS) and ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of the com -
posites compared with pure aluminum (30.6 MPa and 57.8 MPa) are 
increased by 38% and 54%, respectively. Li et al. [ 24 ] have reported that 
a new kind of AlCoCrFeNiTi HEA is introduced into pure aluminum. 
When the addition content of HEA in aluminum melt reaches 3.0 wt%, 
the UTS is increased from 62 MPa to 152 MPa, and the YS is improved 
from 42 MPa to 152 MPa. Chen et al. [ 25 ] have found that AlCoNiCrFe 
HEA/Cu MMCs can be successfully fabricated by PM. Compared with the 
compression yield strength of Cu matrix composite without the AlCo -
NiCrFe HEA (150 MPa), the yield strength of 10 wt% AlCoNiCrFe/Cu 
(240 MPa) and 20 wt% AlCoNiCrFe/Cu (330 MPa) is improved by 160% 
and 220%, respectively. Although the HEAs can significantly improve 
the YS of matrix materials, the MMCs still exhibit relatively low YS, 
which may be caused by the low intrinsic strength of the adopted HEAs. 
The HEA reinforcing phase with superior high strength may provide 
more pronounced strengthening effect in soft matrix. It has been 
confirmed that the superior interfacial bonding integration between the 
HEA reinforcement and metal matrix facilitates the modification of the 
mechanical properties of the composites [ 4 , 26 ]. The advantage of 
interfacial bonding plays a key role in compensating for the interfacial 
electrical losses in Cu matrix composites. However, few studies excavate 
the potential application of HEA reinforced Cu composites for high 
strength-conductive material preparation. The effects of the incorpo -
rated HEA phase on the mechanical and electrical properties of the 
composites have rarely been explored. Based on our previous reports 
[ 27 ], the YS, compressive maximum strength, and plasticity of TiZrNbTa 
(TZNT) HEA are 2212 ± 38 MPa, 2292 ± 31 MPa, and 8.7% ± 0.2%, 
respectively. Furthermore, high melting point elements (Ti, Zr, Nb, Ta) 
alloying design favors avoiding unexpected effects of solid solution and 
microstructure evolution on electrical and mechanical properties for 
high temperature conditions. TZNT HEA may be an excellent candidate 
reinforcement for pure Cu to enhance the strength and maintain good 
electrical conductivity. The incorporation of TZNT HEA particles pro -
vides high strength and the rules for the contribution to YS and con -
ductivity are specifically clarified. The objective of the study is to design 
a stable combination of TiZrNbTa with ultra-high strength and Cu ma -
trix with high conductivity, focusing on obtaining superior strength 
while maintaining adequate electrical conductivity. 
Herein, the TZNT HEA reinforced Cu MMCs with high strength and 
conductivity were successfully fabricated by combining ball milling and 
spark plasma sintering (SPS) processes. TZNT HEA with ultra-high 
strength was introduced into pure Cu matrix with high conductivity to 
achieve superior strength and good electrical conductivity simulta -
neously. The TZNT/Cu MMCs with different contents of HEAs (0, 10, 20, 
30, 40, and 50 wt%) were designed, and microstructure, mechanical 
properties, and electrical conductivity were investigated. Theoretical 
models based on the rule of mixing are constructed to analyze the 
compressive strength and electrical conductivity variation trend of the 
prepared TZNT/Cu composites with the varying TZNT contents. 
2. Materials and methods 
2.1. Preparation of TZNT/Cu MMCs 
The designed TZNT HEA alloy with an equal atomic ratio was syn -
thesized by the mechanical alloying (MA) process using Ti, Nb, Ta, ZrH
2 
(Purity ≥ 99.5%) raw powders. The TZNT HEA alloyed powders were 
successfully obtained after a ball milling (BM, QM-3SP2, Nanjing Chis -
hun Technology Development Co. Ltd.) process of 40 h. The applied 
rotating speed was 350 revolutions per minute (rpm), and the weight 
ratio of the stainless-steel balls and powders is 15:1. The preparation 
procedures of TZNT HEA powders were detailed described in Ref. [ 28 ]. 
Pure Cu powders were obtained commercially by the physical vapor 
method and then mixed with the equal-atomic TZNT HEA powders using 
BM method. The mixture process runs at 250 rpm rotating speed for 5 h 
[ 29 ]. The TZNT/Cu MMCs with different contents of HEA (0, 10, 20, 30, 
40, and 50 wt%) were sintered under vacuum using a spark plasma 
sintering (SPS) system (SPS-VI, Shanghai Chenhua Science and Tech -
nology Corp., Ltd., China). Cylindrical specimens with a diameter of 15 
mm and a thickness of about 4 mm were finally obtained. All processes 
of TZNT/Cu MMCs preparation are depicted schematically in Fig. 1 . 
Previous investigations had demonstrated the feasibility of nano -
crystalline copper composites consolidated by SPS at given sintering 
parameters [ 29 , 30 ]. Therefore, the specific sintering parameters were 
set to 420

MPa for holding pressure to determine the grain refinement micro -

2.3. Mechanical and electrical properties

3.2. Phase and microstructure analysis of TZNT/Cu MMCs 
TZNT/Cu MMCs with various TZNT contents were fabricated using 
combining ball milling and spark plasma sintering processes, which has 
the advantage of controllable heating and holding process at the setting 
temperatures. It would be beneficial to minimize undesirable interaction 
between the metal matrix and the reinforcing phase by regulating the 
sintering process [ 33 ]. TZNT/Cu MMC was prepared by a 
low-temperature and high-pressure SPS sintering process to suppress 
excessive atomic interdiffusion at the interface of the two phases [ 29 ]. 
The XRD patterns of sintered TZNT/Cu MMCs with different contents of 
TZNT HEA are plotted in Fig. 4 . The diffraction peaks of crystalline 
copper are observed and diminish with the increasing HEA content. The 
characteristic halo peak corresponding to equiatomic TZNT HEA is 
identified in the XRD patterns, owing to the obtained complete solid 
solution phase after 40 h milling process. Fig. 5 shows the SEM micro -
graphs and EDS points scan energy spectrum analysis of the two-phase 
structure of TZNT/Cu MMCs samples after sintering. The surface 
morphology of 10, 30, 50 wt% TZNT/Cu MMCs are displayed in Fig. 5 
a-c) respectively. The elemental analysis of two different phases (spot A 
and B) is presented in Table 1 . The measured atomic ratios of Ti, Zr, Nb, 
and Ta elements of the HEA phase are 23.6, 25.18, 25.92, and 25.02 at.% 
respectively, consistent with the designed equal atomic ratio. 
Fig. 6 a) shows the SEM surface morphology of 30 wt% TZNT/Cu 
MMCs. EDS detects the distribution of different elements including Cu, 
Ti, Zr, Nb, and Ta on the surface of the 30 wt% TZNT/Cu MMCs, as 
exhibited in Fig. 6 b-f). The EDS results further illustrate that the bright 
region in Fig. 6 a) is the HEA phase and the dark region is the copper 
phase. The HEA particles are fine (ten microns or so) and uniformly 
distributed in the matrix, ensuring the effective connectivity of the 
copper network structure. The fabricated MMCs have two distinct pha -
ses with clear boundary lines. It can be seen from the images that the 
bulk TZNT/Cu samples are dense with no obvious two-phase interface 
gap and defeats in Fig. 5 d), indicating the good interfacial bonding 
status of the two phases. It would always be a prudent approach to 
develop TZNT reinforced Cu matrix composites with simultaneously 
improved electrical and mechanical performance by optimizing and 
adjusting interface properties.

5. Conclusions 
The TZNT HEA reinforced copper metal matrix composites were 
successfully fabricated through the utilization of a two-step ball milling 
and SPS sintering method. The addition of the TZNT hard particles 
reinforcement significantly improved the mechanical properties of the 
copper metal matrix, but decreased the electrical conductivity. The 40 
wt% TZNT/Cu MMCs exhibited a good combination: compressive 
strength of 882 ± 6 MPa, a plasticity of 15.7% and electrical conductivity 
of 33.0 ± 0.2% IACS. Simple models based on a rule of mixtures were 
successfully applied to correlate the variation of mechanical and elec -
trical properties with the increasing TZNT contents. A desirable 
strengthening effect was availed by adding TZNT HEA particles, the 
compressive yield strength of 50 wt% TZNT/Cu MMCs is improved 
340% compared with the pure Cu specimen. TZNT hard particles 
effectively pin the copper phase, leading to grain refining and disloca -
tion build-up effect in the matrix. Multiple strengthening mechanism of 
dislocation strengthening, grain boundary strengthening and the second 
strengthening were employed and promoted in the MMCs with the 
increasing contents of TZNT phase. This study provided insights into 
further performance improvement of high-strength-conductive mate -
rials by introducing new strengthening phase-high entropy alloy. Be -
sides, it also expanded the application potential of high-entropy alloy 
composites, offered a reference basis of high-entropy alloy composites 
preparation.
