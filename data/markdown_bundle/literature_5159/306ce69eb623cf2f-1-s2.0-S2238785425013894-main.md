ABSTRACT
Hot corrosion is one of the key factors affecting the long-term service of Ni-based single crystal superalloys. In 
this paper, the long-term hot corrosion test of a second-generation Ni-based single crystal superalloy was carried 
out in a high-temperature furnace. Then, the microstructure morphology and composition of the alloy were 
analyzed by a series of characterization methods, which clarified the evolution process of the corrosion/oxida -
tion layer and the attack of the corrosive salt. Meanwhile, EBSD analysis revealed that recrystallization was 
formed on the γ ′ -depleted layer, accelerating the cross-section evolution of the alloy. Finally, the effects of Al 
element content and salt amount on the hot corrosion behavior were investigated separately by the ingenious 
design of two groups of control tests, and the long-term hot corrosion behavior of Ni-based single crystal su -
peralloys is investigated by decomposition into salt-corrosion damage and alloy-oxidation protection behavior 
for the first time. Further, the salt corrosion-oxidation role mechanism of Ni-based single crystal superalloy in the 
long-term hot corrosion environment was revealed.
1. Introduction
Affected by the marine “ salt spray ” environment, the service life of

the engine combustion chamber, it reacts with the fuel containing im -

2. Experimental materials and procedures
The material used in the test is a second-generation Ni-based single 
crystal superalloy with [001] orientation after standard heat treatment, 
and the nominal chemical composition of the alloy is (wt.%): 7.62Co, 
5.02 W, 6.58Ta, 6.2Al, 7.05Cr, 2.93Re and Ni in balance. Then, the 
sample was finely machined by wire-electrode cutting with molybde -
num wire. The geometric size of the sample was 10 mm × 8 mm × 6 mm, 
where the [001] direction was parallel to the 10 × 8 mm plane. Sub -
sequently, the six sample surfaces were sanded with SiC water sand -
paper to 2000# and cleaned by ultrasonic wave with ethanol and 
acetone to remove oil stains on the surface.
The temperature of the long-term hot corrosion test was 900 
◦
C, 
which was achieved by a high-temperature furnace. The sample surfaces 
were coated with salt using the salt coating method prior to the long- 
term hot corrosion test. The detailed steps of the salt coating method 
were as follows: first, the sample was preheated to about 110
◦
Con the 
heating plate, and then the salt solution was atomized and sprayed on 
the sample surface with a spray bottle. Among them, the salt solution 
composition was 75 wt%Na
2
SO
4
+ 15 wt% NaCl + 10 wt% NaVO
3
, and 
the salt coating amount was 3 ± 0.1 mg/cm
2 
[ 31 ]. Then, the sample was 
placed in the corundum crucible, and the crucible was placed in the 
high-temperature furnace and heated to 900 
◦
C along with the furnace 
temperature. The test times were 50 h, 100 h, 200 h, 400 h, 600 h, and 
800 h, respectively. Then, take out the sample after cooling to room 
temperature in the high-temperature furnace. In order to reduce the test 
error, three parallel samples were carried out for each condition.
Based on the above test, two groups of control tests were designed. 
The control test I was a high temperature oxidation test without salt 
coating on the sample surface and the test times were 100 h, 200 h, and 
800 h. The test purpose was to discuss the role of oxidation in the long- 
term hot corrosion process by comparing the evolution of the product 
layer in hot corrosion and oxidation environments. The control test II 
was supplemented with coated salt 3 ± 0.1 mg/cm
2 
at 400 h and 600 h 
of long-term hot corrosion, respectively, and then continued hot corro -
sion until 800 h, named 800 h-A and 800 h-B. The purpose of the control 
test II was to clearly reveal the hot corrosion mechanism at the late stage 
of the long-term hot corrosion test. Considering that a certain amount of 
corrosive salt would be depleted over a long period of time, the control 
test II was performed by adding corrosive salt at 400 h and 600 h 
respectively. This would contribute to analyzing the influence of the 
adequacy degree of the amount of corrosive salt during the long-term 
hot corrosion process.
After hot corrosion tests, the macroscopic surface morphology of the 
sample was observed by OM (Leica DM2700 M), and the phase 
composition of the surface corrosion product was identified by XRD 
(Bruker D8 Advance) with Cu K α radiation at 40 KV. Then, the micro -

SEM 300) coupled with EDS (Oxford Instruments, X-Max
N

3. Result
3.1. Microstructure of the sample before hot corrosion
The diffraction pattern, morphology, and element distribution of the 
alloy are observed by transmission electron microscopy (TEM, FEI Talos 
F200X), as shown in Fig. 1 . Ni-based single crystal superalloy consists of 
the cube-shaped γ ′ phase and the γ phase as the narrow channel con -
necting γ ′ phase, both of which have a face-centered-cubic(fcc) struc -
ture. The elements Ni, Al, and Ta are mainly distributed in the γ ’ phase, 
the elements Cr, Co, and Re are mainly distributed in the γ phase, and 
the element W is evenly distributed in the two phases.

role and oxidation role. The hot corrosion behavior of the alloy can be

when the content of Al element is low, a continuous protective Al
