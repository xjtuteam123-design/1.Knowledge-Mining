Abstract 
W and Mo are added to the HfNbTaTiZr high entropy a lloy to improve its 
high-temperature performance. Microstructures, and mechanical properties of new 
refractory high-entropy alloys, HfNbTaTiZrW (H-W) a nd HfNbTaTiZrMoW 
(H-MoW), were studied. W and/or Mo additions broade n the two-phases temperature 
range and introduced two disordered BCC phases in c ontrast with the single BCC 
phase in HfNbTaTiZr. Attributing to the dual-phases microstructure and the strong 
solid solution strengthening effect, H-W and H-MoW exhibit substantially improved 
yield strengths at 25-1200 °C compared with HfNbTaT iZr. Particularly, at 1200 °C, 
H-W and H-MoW show yield strengths of 345MPa and 70 3MPa, which are much 
higher than 92MPa of HfNbTaTiZr. No phase change ha ppens during compressive 
tests at high temperatures, indicating good phase s tabilities of H-W and H-MoW 
alloys.

1. Introduction 
Since being proposed in 2004, high-entropy alloys ( HEAs) [1] has been 
increasingly investigated due to their high strengt h [2], high hardness [2], good wear 
resistance [3], good corrosion resistance [4], and excellent magnetic properties [5]. 
HEAs break up the design strategy of conventional a lloys and employ five or more 
elements as principal components (rather than just 1 or 2) at molar ratios of 5-35%. 
Refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) are mainly c omposed of the metal 
elements with melting points higher than 1650°C [6- 14]. The first two reported 
RHEAs are NbMoTaW and NbMoTaWV which were prepared using vacuum arc 
melting by Senkov et.al. in 2010 [6]. These two RHE As have a simple BCC 
disordered solid solution structure [6], and are st ructurally stable at high temperature 
[15]. Moreover, compressive yield strengths of thes e two alloys can still be 
maintained at 405 MPa and 477 MPa respectively at 1 600°C, which is obviously 
superior to traditional nickel-based superalloys [1 6, 17]. However, these two RHEAs 
have significant room temperature (RT) brittleness, and their fracture strains are less 
than 2% at RT [15]. To overcome the RT brittleness, Senkov et.al. designed a new 
RHEA, HfNbTaTiZr, and this alloy exhibits a compres sive strain >50% at RT [7]. 
This superior ductility of HfNbTaTiZr is completely different from the RT brittleness 
of other RHEAs with BCC structures [15]. HfNbTaTiZr RHEA also showed a stable 
single-phase BCC structure after annealing at 1200° C, but it decomposed into 
multiple phases after annealing at intermediate tem peratures (500-900°C) [18, 19]. Ta 
and Nb have BCC crystal structures through high temperature to RT, but Hf, Ti and Zr 
are BCC at high temperature and transform to HCP at 1760°C, 862°C and 882°C, 
respectively. Therefore, the single BCC phase in HfNbTaTiZr tends to decompose into 
three phases, the BCC matrix, BCC Ta-Nb-rich precip itates, and HCP Zr-Hf-rich 
precipitates after annealing at 700-800°C. The yiel d strength of HfNbTaTiZr is much 
lower than former RHEAs (NbMoTaW and NbMoTaWV), e.g ., its yield strength is 
only 92MPa at 1200°C [20], which renders this alloy less attractive for 
high-temperature applications. 
In this paper, W and Mo are added to the HfNbTaTiZr RHEA to improve its 
compressive strength, considering these two element s feature the highest melting 
points among the four refractory metals (W, Mo, Cr and V) [21]. The increased 
melting points are expected to elevate softening te mperatures of the HfNbTaTiZrW 
(H-W) and HfNbTaTiZrMoW (H-MoW) RHEAs, and microstructures and mechanical 
properties of novel H-W and H-MoW alloys are carefully studied in this paper.

2. Methods 
Equal-molar H-W and H-MoW RHEAs were prepared 
 with commercial purity 
(99.9 wt.%) Hf, Nb, Ta, Ti, Zr, W using vacuum arc melting in a water-cooled copper 
crucible under argon atmosphere. Alloys were remelt ed at least five times to ensure 
the compositional homogeneity. Compositions of allo ys were measured by an 
Inductive Coupled Plasma Optical Emission Spectrome ter (ICP-OES, Agilent 
ICPOES730) with a LECO ONH836 elemental analyzer (for O, N, and H) and a vario 
El cube elemental analyzer (for C), and were shown in Table S1 in Supplementary 
Information. Impurities in the two alloys are all < 0.01 wt.%. Solidification paths of 
alloys were predicted using a Thermo-Calc software and the TCHEA1 database [22]. 
Crystal structures were examined with a Rigaku Ultima III X-ray diffractometer using 
a Cu-Kα radiation and a scanning rate of 5°/min from 20° t o 100°. Microstructures of 
as-cast alloys were studied using an optical micros cope (OM), a scanning electron 
microscope (SEM, Hitachi S4800) equipped with an Ox ford Instruments INCA 
x-sight energy dispersive X-ray (EDX) and an Oxford HKL Nordlys II Electron 
Backscattered Diffraction (EBSD), and a transmissio n electron microscope (TEM, 
FEI Tecnai G2 F20). TEM samples were prepared using a FEI Helios NanoLab 600 
Dual-Beam focused ion beam (DB-FIB) system. Quasi-s tatic compression tests at 
room temperature were conducted on an Instron 5569 universal testing machine with 
a constant stain rate of 10 -3 /s, and compressive strains were measured by an 
extensometer. Samples of Φ4×6 mm were used. Compression tests at 800, 1000, a nd 
1200°C were carried out using a dynamic thermal sim ulation testing machine 
(Gleeble-3500) and the furnace chamber was evacuate d to 10 -3 torr before each test. 
Specimens of Φ6×9 mm were used. The heating rate is 300°C/min and the 
compressive strain rate is 10 -3/s. Compressive strains were obtained by directly 
measuring displacements of indenters. 
 
3. Results and discussions 
3.1 Thermodynamic simulation 
Fig. 1 shows solidification paths of HfNbTaTiZr, H- W and H-MoW alloys at the 
equilibrium state. It can be seen that the liquid H -W alloy (Fig. 1b) continues to 
solidify as a single BCC_A2 phase just below its li quidus (2295°C). As the 
temperature drops to 1740°C, the single BCC phase s tarts to decompose into 
BCC_A2 and BCC_A2#2. These two BCC phases coexist b etween 1740°C and 
971°C, and after then, the Laves and HCP phases form at the expense of BCC_A2 and 
BCC_A2#2. Five phases, i.e. BCC_A2, BCC_A2#2, Laves phase, HCP_A3, and 
HCP_A3#2, are predicted in this alloy at room tempe rature at equilibrium state. Note 
that phase fractions are not properly shown in Fig. 1b under 800°C, this is likely 
attribute to the limit of TCHEA1 database, e.g. it does not contain all ternary phase 
diagrams of refractory elements incorporated in the alloy. A similar solidification path 
can be found for the H-MoW alloy. Comparing with Hf NbTaTiZr, additions of the W 
and/or Mo reduce the temperature range of single-ph ase BCC (1847-1018°C, 
1784-1742°C, 1800-1544°C in HfNbTaTiZr, H-W, and H- MoW alloys respectively),

Strong strengthening effects of W and/or Mo additio ns can attribute to that i) W 
and/or Mo additions change the single BCC structure in HfNbTaTiZr into dual BCC 
structures which induces phase boundaries that can act as obstacles to dislocation 
motion and thus strengthen the alloy; and ii) W and /or Mo atoms increase the solid 
solution strengthening. This solid solution strengt hening effect is rather evident when 
comparing H-W and H-MoW alloys, specifically, both alloys show similar dual phase 
structures and grain sizes but H-MoW that has Mo ad ded exhibits higher yield 
strength. 
Improving strength by dual phases has also been fou nd in other HEAs. For 
example, it is reported that adjusting Zr, Al, and Ti compositions in AlNbTaTiVZr 
alloy can change the microstructure in the alloy in to either single BCC or dual BCC, 
and the yield strength of the dual BCC alloy is hig her than the single BCC one [32]. 
Another example is that replacing the W element in the MoNbTaTiW alloy with Zr, 
the crystal structure of alloy changes from single BCC of MoNbTaTiW to dual-phase 
BCC of MoNbTaTiWZr, and the yield strength increases [33, 34].

4. Conclusions 
W and Mo were added to the HfNbTaTiZr alloy to impr ove its high-temperature 
performance. Crystal structures, microstructures, a nd mechanical properties of new 
RHEAs, HfNbTaTiZrW (H-W) and HfNbTaTiZrMoW (H-MoW) were studied. The 
following conclusions can be drawn: 
(1) 
 Both H-W and H-MoW exhibit two disordered BCC phase s in each alloy, and 
they correspond to dendrite regions and interdendri te regions respectively. The 
dendrite BCC phase is rich in high melting point el ements (W, Ta, and Nb) 
whereas the interdendrite BCC phase is rich in low melting point elements (Hf, Zr, 
and Ti). Elements segregations are even serious in H-W than in H-MoW, which 
leads to larger different between lattice constants of the two BCC phases in H-W. 
(2) Comparing with HfNbTaTiZr, H-W and H-MoW exhibit hi gher yield strengths 
and much balanced mechanical properties at high tem peratures. The greatly 
improve strengths of H-W and H-MoW attribute to dua l BCC structures and the 
strong solid solution strengthening effect induced by W and/or Mo additions. 
(3) H-W and H-MoW alloys show better resistance to the high-temperature softening 
compared with HfNbTaTiZr. No new phases were presen ted in H-W and H-MoW 
after compressions at high temperatures up to 1200° C, indicating good phase 
stabilities of new alloys.
