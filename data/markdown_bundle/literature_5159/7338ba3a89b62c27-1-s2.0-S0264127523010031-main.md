ABSTRACT 
The self-healing mechanism has been newly applied in Ni superalloys to extend the creep lifetime during service. 
Quantitative selection criteria are proposed to screen the potential self-healing elements in Ni alloying system. Ti 
atoms and η -Ni
3
Ti precipitates are thus selected as the candidate healing atoms and the healing agent, respec -
tively. The ideal composition interval for Ti to achieve the effective healing effects is computationally calculated 
as 0.076 – 0.1 mol fraction. On this basis, SHNi (self-healing Ni alloy) has been developed and compared with 
nSHNi (no self-healing Ni alloy). The creep lifetimes of SHNi alloys are observed 8 – 10 times higher than that of 
nSHNi alloys. Microstructure characterizations have shown that η -Ni
3
Ti healing agents in SHNi alloys can form 
site-specifically at the cavity sites during service. Meanwhile, the creep cavities in SHNi alloys present much 
smaller average sizes during creep, and the cavity growth rates are generally one magnitude lower compared to 
that of nSHNi alloys. The excellent creep properties of SHNi alloys can be well ascribed to their creep cavitation 
resistance brought by the self-healing behavior. The effectiveness of self-healing mechanism in improving the 
creep properties has proposed a new strategy in developing novel Ni superalloys. 
1. Introduction 
Ni polycrystalline superalloys are well known as the most promising 
candidates for structural components used in the hot sections of aircraft 
engines and land-based gas turbines [1 – 3] . Nevertheless, the compo -
nents subjected to high temperatures and stresses during long-time 
service inevitably suffer material degradation. For an instant, creep 
cavities can form and distribute as isolated defects along grain bound -
aries, which will gradually get coalesced and lead to the deteriorated 
mechanical properties and reduced lifetime during subsequent service 
[4 – 9] . Due to the high cost of production of Ni-based alloy components, 
recently, the methods of repairing the defects of post-service compo -
nents have attracted great attention [10,11] . 
At present, the off-line treatment methods represented by hot 
isostatic pressing (HIP) treatment have been widely applied to rejuve -
nate the properties of post-service components [12 – 15] . Investigations 
show that the internal cavity density of components after hot isostatic 
pressing treatment can be significantly reduced. At the same time, the 
service life can be largely reset as well, when applying proper HIP 
processing parameters. However, recent studies have denoted that the 
performance of HIP-repaired alloys degrades more significantly 
compared to the pristine states at elevated service temperature [16,17] . 
Namely, the HIP-closed cavities are observed to be more vulnerable to 
nucleate defects, which can evolve with increased growth rates into 
cavity sizes during post-HIP service, compared to the initial states. 
Therefore, the off-line healing methods can hardly deal with the dy -
namic evolvement of creep cavities in Ni superalloys during service, 
while the in-situ repair of service defects could be the possible solution 
[12,16] . 
The concept of self-healing has been proposed as an alternative 
method to enhance the lifetime of a wide range of materials [18 – 22] , 
which have opened a new window for the in-situ control of the creep 
cavity growth in service conditions [23,24] . In self-healing metals, the 
defects can be repaired through the selective formation of 
compositionally-designed precipitations (so-called healing agents) by 
stimulating atom diffusion to those locations. The healing atom diffusion

In this work, quantitative criteria were proposed to screen the 
alloying elements with self-healing potentials in Ni superalloys. To 
ensure the effective healing behavior, thermodynamic simulations were 
made to obtain the composition intervals for the defect-site-specific 
precipitation of healing agents. Creep tests based on prototype alloys 
were operated to experimentally validate the self-healing phenomena, 
while the selective precipitation behaviors of healing agents as well as 
the corresponding evolution of creep cavities were quantitively char -
acterized. The effectiveness of the self-healing concept in prolongating 
the rupture time has been confirmed, which can shed light on the 
development of novel Ni superalloys with prolonged service lifetime.

damage sites and form a new phase (so-called self-healing agents), and 
therein intervene the growth of the cavities. To achieve the intrinsic self- 
healing by the directional movement of the atoms, a series of criteria 
need to be satisfied for selecting the alloying elements. These are, as 
shown in Fig. 1 : 1) the alloying system can be brought into a supersat -

2.2. The determination of target composition range with optimal self- 
healing behaviors

agents should match the formation of the cavities so that the healing 
agents can form specifically at the defect sites. Namely, the healing 
atoms are expected to be meta -stably solid solutioned in the matrix, but 
when the defects take place, the atoms can be driven to move orientally 
towards the defect sites and form healing agents afterwards. Therefore, 
to reach the selective precipitation as anticipated, the composition of 
healing atoms has to be manipulated.

5.1. The contributions of strengthening effects 
In the results section, the SHNi alloy was found to possess much 
prolonged creep lifetime compared to the nSHNi alloy, while great dif -
ferences in the growth behavior of creep cavities between these two 
alloys have been observed due to the selective formation of η -Ni
3

healing agents. However, before we ascribe the improved creep lifetime 
to the healing phenomena of cavities, it is necessary to exclude the 
possible strengthening effects caused by the alloying Ti atoms. 
The strength of creep ruptured samples for these two alloys was

been provided in Table 3 . To minimize the possible influence of the

samples is 205HV and 183HV for the SHNi and the nSHNi alloys,

‘pristine ’ hardness. The primary difference of around 20 HV in hardness

alloying levels, which can well explain the difference in creep behaviors

5.3. The mechanism of self-healing behavior 
The schematic diagram representing the self-healing mechanism is 
shown in Fig. 13 . According to the results in Fig.s 7 and 8, during the 
creep service, cavitation in the SHNi and the nSHNi alloys is initially 
confined to relatively isolated grain boundary facets which are sur -
rounded by undamaged creeping material. The cavity growth occurs by 
the stress-directed diffusive transport of voids to the cavity surface. In 
the nSHNi alloy, the voids can move unimpededly towards the cavity 
surface, leading to the unconstrained growth of the cavities with a 
speedy growth rate, and thereby the undesirable creep properties. In 
comparison, for the SHNi alloy that shows self-healing phenomena, the 
directional diffusion of Ti atoms towards the defect sites has been 
confirmed by the selective precipitation of η -Ni
3
Ti at the cavity vicinity, 
driven by the chemical driving force. According to Kirkendall effect, the 
oriented movement of atoms can intrinsically interrupt the trans -
portation of voids to the cavity surface, thereby preventing or retarding 
the growth of the cavities. The much-reduced cavity growth rates in the 
nSHNi alloy, as presented in Fig. 11 , have correspondingly led to their 
much-improved creep properties with retarded creep rates and prolon -
gated creep lifetime. The effectiveness of self-healing mechanism in 
enhancing the creep properties can shed light on the development of 
novel Ni superalloys. 
6. Conclusions 
1) Self-healing mechanism has been introduced into Ni superalloys in 
order to realize the in-situ recovering of defects. Quantitative se -
lection criteria considering the supersaturation state, diffusivity, 
atomic size and γ ʹ compatibility, are proposed to screen the alloying 
elements that process the self-healing potentials in the Ni system. Ti 
element has been consequently selected as the healing atom that 
meets all the requirements, while η -Ni
3
Ti can be considered as the 
potential healing agent. 
2) To thermodynamically realize the effective healing phenomena, 
namely the selective precipitation on the defect sites, the ideal 
composition interval for Ti atoms has been calculated based on the 
quantification of the precipitation energy barrier from interfacial 
energy and strain energy. Alloy compositions can be obtained which 
possess the chemical driving force that can stimulate the formation of 
the η -Ni
3
Ti phase exclusively at the free surface of the cavity sites 
during creep. 
3) Two prototype alloys, SHNi with self-healing phenomena and nSHNi 
with no self-healing effects have been developed to experimentally 
validate the self-healing concept. The results have show that the 
creep lifetime for the SHNi alloy is 8 – 10 times higher than that of the 
nSHNi alloy at 750 
◦
C for the different applied stress levels (120, 100 
and 80 MPa), confirming the effectiveness of the self-healing 
mechanism in prolongating creep lifetime. 
4) The microstructure of the creep ruptured samples in the SHNi alloy 
have proved that η -Ni
3
Ti phase can selectively precipitate at the vi -
cinity of the cavities rather than form at the grain interior or at the 
grain boundaries. 
5) The detailed characterization of the evolution of creep cavity size has 
been made on interrupt crept samples. Compared to the nSHNi alloy, 
the SHNi alloy possesses a much smaller average cavity size, as well 
as cavity growth rates one order of magnitude lower during the 
entire creep service. The excellent creep cavitation resistance of the 
SHNi alloy can be well ascribed to the site-specific precipitation 
behavior of η -Ni
3
Ti phase, where the accumulation of voids to in -
crease the cavity size can be interrupted by the directional diffusion 
of Ti atoms towards the defect sites. 
6) The effectiveness of self-healing mechanism in improving the creep 
properties has proposed a new strategy in developing novel Ni 
superalloys.
