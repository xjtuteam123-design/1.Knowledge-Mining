abstract
A compositionally modi ﬁed Ni–45Ti–5Al–2Nb–1Mo (at%) alloy was developed by simultaneously adding
Nb and Mo in NiTi –Al-based alloy and processed by forging isothermally. The as-forged Ni –45Ti–5Al–
2Nb–1Mo alloy consists of NiTi matrix, Ti 2Ni and dispersive (Nb,Ti) SS phases. Mo addition reduces the
solubility of Nb in the NiTi matrix and gives rise to the precipitation of numerous nanoscale (Nb,Ti) SS
phases (bcc structure) within the grains. The (Nb,Ti) SS phase is semi-coherent to the NiTi matrix with a
lattice mis ﬁt of 6.7%. The addition of Mo and Nb dramatically enhances the NiTi –Al alloy at high
temperatures, which is mainly attributed to the solid solution strengthening of Nb and Mo elements as
well as the precipitation strengthening of (Nb,Ti)
SS phases. The Ni –45Ti–5Al–2Nb–1Mo alloy is expected
to become a high-temperature structural material using at intermediate temperatures with strengths of
650 MPa at 923 K and 350 MPa at 1073 K.

1. Introduction
To meet the increasing demand of aerospace and automobile
industries for lightweight high-temperature structural materials,
investigations focus on lightweight and high-strength intermetallics.
Due to their low density, high speci ﬁcs t r e n g t ha n dr e a s o n a b l e
oxidation resistance, ordered intermetallic compounds based on
aluminides have been developed as high-temperature structural
materials for decades, such asγ-TiAl (L10)a n dN i A l( B 2 )[1,2].H o w e v e r ,
the applications of these ordered intermetallics are hindered by their
poor room-temperature ductility and hard deformability[3].
NiTi intermetallics as shape memory alloys have achieved great
success because NiTi has a unique shape memory effect and a
quite remarkable ductility among intermetallics. However, as a
high-temperature alloy, NiTi has several drawbacks, e.g., lower
strength at elevated temperatures and poor oxidation resistance
[4,5]. Koizumi et al. found that Al addition to substitute the Ti can
remarkably enhance the near-equiatomic NiTi alloys at room and
elevated temperatures, especially when the Al amount is high
enough to precipitate Ni 2TiAl phase coherent to the NiTi matrix.
At 1273 K, the compression strength of NiTi –Al alloys is the same
level as mid-grade superalloys such as U500 and U700 [6]. Our
recent investigations indicate that the substitution of Al for Ni or Ti
can reinforce NiTi-based intermetallics through the precipitation
of Ti 2Ni phase [7]. Hence, NiTi –Al-based alloys are considered as
potential structural materials for use at medium-temperature
interval.
Several studies were conducted to improve the integrative
mechanical properties of NiTi –Al alloys by adding quaternary
elements. In our previous work, it is reported that the addition
of larger-sized Mo into the NiTi –Al alloy will take effect of solution
strengthening and dramatically reinforce the alloy [8].I ti s
suggested that the amount of Mo addition in NiTi –Al alloys is
preferably not more than 1 at%. When Mo content reaches 1.5 at%,
Mo solid-solution phase precipitates and dramatically reduces its
room-temperature ductility. As potential high-temperature struc-
tural materials, oxidation behavior is a predominant factor of
affecting its application. Xu et al. found that Nb is a very effective
element to improve the oxidation resistance of NiTi and NiTi –Al
alloys at elevated temperatures [9]. Additive Nb slower the inward
diffusion of oxygen and the outward diffusion of intermetallic
elements, leading to the dramatic improvement of oxidation
resistance for Ni –50Ti–6Al–3Nb alloy 1073 K [10]. Moreover, the
Ni/Ti atomic ratio is another predominant reason for affecting the
mechanical properties and oxidation behavior of NiTi –Al based
alloys [1 1,12]. For example, the state of Nb either as a solid solution
or as precipitates depends on the Ni/Ti ratio and then shows
different in ﬂuences on the oxidation behavior.

strength and room-temperature ductility still exists in NiTi –Al

2. Experimental procedure
A 100 mm-diameter alloy ingot with the nominal composition
of Ni –45Ti–5Al–2Nb–1Mo (at%) was prepared by vacuum induc-
tion melting using a high-purity sponge Ti, Ni (99.95 wt%), Al
(99.98 wt%), Nb (99.98 wt%) and Mo (99.95 wt%). The ingot was
forged isothermally with 60% reduction at 1223 K. Microstructural
and mechanical specimens were cut using an electro-discharge
machine, annealed at 1373 K for 6 h, and then aged at 1073 K for
6 h followed by air cooling. After electro-discharge cutting, the
specimens were mechanically ground with SiC paper and then
polished with powdered emery. To investigate the effect of
compositional modi ﬁcation, the Ni –44Ti–6Al alloy was prepared
in the same way for comparison.
Tensile tests were conducted in air with an MTS880 machine
ﬁtted with an electric resistance heating furnace under an initial
strain rate of 3 /C2 10/C0 5 s/C0 1 from ambient temperature to 1073 K,
using cylindrical tensile specimens with gage length and diameter
of 25 mm and 5 mm, respectively. In the tensile tests at tempera-
ture no more than 873 K, an extensometer was used to measure the
elongation, while an electronic digital caliper with a precision of
0.01 mm was used at temperature higher than 873 K. Three speci-
mens were tested under identical conditions to obtain an average.
The constituent phases were identi ﬁed using a Regaku D/max
2200PC X-ray diffractometer via Cu Kα radiation. The microstructure
and fracture surface observatio ns were performed on a JSM-5600
scanning electron microscope (SEM) equipped with energy-dispersive
spectroscopy (EDS). A transmission electron microscopy (TEM) inves-
tigation was performed using a JEM 2100F microscope. Specimens for
TEM observation were prepared by standard twinjet electropolishing
using a solution of 20% perchloric acid and 80% methanol as an
electrolyte at 20 V and 243 K.

3.3. Mechanical properties
Fig. 5 shows the tensile stress –strain curves of as-cast and as-
forged Ni –45Ti–5Al–2Nb–1Mo alloy at room temperature (293 K).
For as-cast samples, fracture occurs before yielding during tension;
but after isothermal forging, an apparent yield is observed and the
elongation (EL) reaches 1%. The ultimate tensile strength (UTS) for
as-forged alloy is 1225 MPa, which is 50% higher than that for the
as-cast alloy. Therefore, the room-temperature tensile properties
of Ni –45Ti–5Al–2Nb–1Mo alloy are markedly improved by the
isothermal forging.
Fig. 6 illustrates the tensile properties of as-forged alloy at
different temperatures, including 0.2% yield strength (YS), UTS
and EL. As the temperature increases, the YS and UTS ﬁrst remain
unchanged, and then decrease gradually. However, the variation of
EL shows a contrary tendency. As the temperature rises, the value
of EL increases from 1% at 293 K to 17% at 923 K. By considering 5%
elongation as the signal of brittle to ductile transition taking place,
the brittle to ductile transition temperature (BDTT) is between
893 K and 923 K.
3.4. Fracture observation
Fig. 7 shows the SEM micrographs of the fracture surfaces after
tensile tests at different temperatures. It can be found that the
room-temperature tensile fracture surface is quite smooth, and the
fracture characterization is mainly brittle transgranular cleavage
[Fig. 7(a)]. It suggests that the crack propagation resistance is small
and the fracture propagates rapidly. From the corresponding back-
scattered electron (BSE) image [ Fig. 7 (b)], some white (Nb,Ti) SS
particles are observed on the grain boundaries and within grains.
The presence of (Nb,Ti) SS phases can prevent crack propagation
and make the stress redistribution. As temperature rises to 893 K,
just below the BDTT, the sample fractured after a certain plastic
deformation without apparent necking. The fracture mode is still a
cleavage fracture while the fracture surface becomes dimpled,
indicating the crack propagation resistance increases [ Fig. 7 (c)].
When tensile temperature reaches 923 K, the fracture morphology
is macroscopically rough and predominantly consists of equiaxial
dimples [ Fig. 7 (d)], which indicates that the deformation mode
transits to ductile fracture with necking. Hence, consistent with
the results of tensile tests, the BDTT for the as-forged Ni –45Ti–
5Al–2Nb–1Mo alloy is between 893 K and 923 K.
4. Discussions
In this study, the microstructure of as-forged Ni –45Ti–5Al–
2Nb–1Mo alloy after heat treatment consists of NiTi matrix, Ti 2Ni
and (Nb,Ti) SS phases. Compared with the Ni –50Ti–6Al–3Nb and
Ni–43Ti–6Al–1Mo alloys [8,10] a mass of dispersive ﬁne (Nb,Ti) SS
particles embedded in the NiTi matrix in Ni –45Ti–5Al–2Nb–1Mo
alloy. This is due to the simultaneous addition of Nb and Mo. As
reported, the solubility of Nb in NiTi alloy is more than 2% [18].

Because both Nb and Mo have the bcc structure and similar lattice
parameters, Mo addition to NiTi –Nb alloys will occupy some of the
Nb sites and reduce the solubility of Nb in the NiTi matrix. This
eventually gives rise to the precipitation of (Nb,Ti) SS particles.
Similar result has been found in Ni 44.5Ti46Nb9Mo0.5 alloy [19].
We compare the tensile properties of Ni –44Ti–6Al and
Ni–45Ti–5Al–2Nb–1Mo alloys, and ﬁnd that the addition of Nb
and Mo dramatically improves the tensile strengths of NiTi –Al
based alloy, especially at elevated temperatures. For example, the
value of YS is 650 MPa at 923 K for Ni –45Ti–5Al–2Nb–1Mo alloy,
which is double the value for Ni –44Ti–6Al alloy of 300 MPa.

The precipitation of nanoscale (Nb,Ti)
SS particles has a great
inﬂuence on the mechanical properties. As mentioned above, the
(Nb,Ti)SS phase is semi-coherent to the NiTi matrix. Hence, the
introduction of nanoscale (Nb,Ti) SS particles will serve as barriers
to dislocation motion and increase the resistance during tensile
deformation. Meanwhile, in Ni –45Ti–5Al–2Nb–1Mo alloy, Mo
tends to dissolve in the (Nb,Ti) SS phase ( Table 1 ), which causes
the enhancement of the (Nb,Ti) SS phase and raises the precipita-
tion strengthening effect. Fig. 8 shows the TEM image of the Ni –
45Ti–5Al–2Nb–1Mo alloy after tensile tests at 1073 K, in which
high-density dislocations passing through the precipitation are
observed. The numerous (Nb,Ti) SS particles introduce more inter-
faces and impede the dislocations movement.

phase, which can block the movement of slips in NiTi phase and
result in higher YS and UTS [7]. However, Ti 2Ni is brittle to the
detriment of ductility. Pan et al. reported that Ti2Ni is responsible for
the sharply reduced strength at high temperatures, especially those

5. Conclusions
(1) The microstructure of as-cast Ni –45Ti–5Al–2Nb–1Mo alloy is
composed of primary NiTi matrix, Ti2Ni, (Ti,Nb)2Ni and (Nb,Ti)SS
phases. Through isothermal forging, the grains are elongated
perpendicular to the forging direction and the eutectic structure
along the grain boundary is crushed, leading to the improve-
ment of room-temperature ductility.
(2) Mo addition reduces the solubility of Nb in the NiTi matrix and
results in the precipitation of numerous nanoscale (Nb,Ti)
SS
particles within the grains. The (Nb,Ti) SS phase with the bcc
structure is semi-coherent to the NiTi matrix and gives rise
to precipitation strengthening effect. Mo tends to enrich in
(Nb,Ti)SS particles and strengthens them.
(3) The YS and UTS of NiTi –Al-based alloys are dramatically
enhanced by the addition of Nb and Mo, which is attributed
to solid-solution strengthening as well as precipitation
strengthening of (Nb,Ti) SS phases. This alloy is expected to
become a high-temperature structural material for application
at intermediate temperatures with strengths of 650 MPa at
923 K and 350 MPa at 1073 K.
