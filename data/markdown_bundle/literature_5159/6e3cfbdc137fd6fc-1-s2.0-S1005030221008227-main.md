NiCoCr-based multi-component alloys have drawn much attention due to their exceptional ductility and 
strain hardening capacity. However, insuﬃcient strength-ductility synergy of NiCoCr alloy has always 
been an issue that prevents it from extensive applications. According to our previous research, the 
precipitation of γ”p h a s e can signiﬁcantly improve the strength-ductility synergy of this alloy system at 
room temperature. In this study, the effects of V addition on γ”p h a s e stability and high-temperature 
mechanical properties have been explicitly investigated. The results indicate that V addition can sta- 
bilize the metastable γ”p h a s e in this alloy system and prevent it from transforming into a stable δ
phase at grain boundaries upon 650 °C aging, resulting in improved mechanical properties at elevated 
temperatures. The speciﬁc strength of γ”-strengthened multi-component NiCoCr-based alloy can reach 
up to 86.2 MPa g −1 cm −3 at 650 °C, which is higher than those of Ni-based superalloys, IN 939 and 
Waspaloy. This work provides theoretical guidance for the novel design of γ”-strengthened alloy for 
high-temperature applications.

1. Introduction 
Recently, multi-component alloys, also known as high-entropy 
alloys (HEAs) or medium-entropy alloys (MEAs), have attracted 
worldwide scientiﬁc attention because of their unique compo- 
sitions and outstanding properties [1–6] . They usually contain 
three or more principal elements, and the atomic fraction of 
each component is about 5–35 at.% [7] . Multi-component alloys 
are expected to be attractive candidates for structural applica- 
tions at cryogenic and room temperatures due to their excellent 
low-temperature mechanical properties [8–10] . Among them, 
the equiatomic ternary NiCoCr alloy with a single face-centered 
cubic (FCC) structure has received extensive attention [11] , which 
exhibits superior mechanical properties at the temperature range 
from −196 °C to 400 °C, compared with most multi-component 
and multi-phase alloys [11 , 12] . One major reason is due to the low 
stacking fault energy (SFE) at cryogenic temperatures so that a

individual grains in this multi-component alloy during deforma-

the solid solution strengthening effect owing to the presence of 
solute atoms, including Cr, Co and Al etc. [14] . 
Commercial Ni-base superalloys also contain a signiﬁcant 
amount of Cr and Co for corrosion resistance and solid solution 
strengthening of alloy matrix [15] . Among them, the most typical 
NiCoCr-based superalloys are IN 939 and Waspaloy [16 , 17] . There 
is a widely accepted assumption in the ﬁeld of multi-component 
alloys regarding the existence of the “sluggish diffusion” effect. 
It indicates that the vacancy formation and migration kinetics in 
the multi-component alloy are slowed down by local variations 
in lattice potential energy caused by different types of atoms on 
each lattice site [18] . This could exert an obvious effect on the 
creep properties in alloys at elevated temperatures instead of cryo- 
genic temperatures, as the coarsening rate of precipitates [19] and 
creep deformation [20] is decelerated due to the low diffusion 
rate. However, it has been reported that the high-temperature

strength of single-phase FCC HEAs is relatively insuﬃcient due to 
the limitation of the solid solution strengthening effect [21 , 22] . 
Therefore, the strength of NiCoCr alloy drops dramatically to a low 
level when the temperature exceeds 650 °C. The yield strength of 
NiCoCr at 700 °C is only ∼141 MPa [23] , which is much lower than 
commercial metallic structural materials. For alloys with promising 
above-mentioned structure, it is beneﬁcial to improve the high- 
temperature mechanical properties of NiCoCr alloys in order to 
satisfy the requirement of practical utilization, which covers a 
broader temperature range, similar to austenite steels [24–26] and 
superalloys [27–29] . 
Precipitation strengthening is one of the most widely used 
strengthening methods in multi-component alloys, which is con- 
sidered an effective way for enhancing the mechanical properties 
at both low and high temperatures [30–36] . The combination of 
precipitation hardening and high work-hardening capability of 
the matrix [30] results in the excellent mechanical performance 
of precipitate-strengthened multi-component alloys at ambient 
temperature. One typical example is the extensively studied L1 2 - 
γ’ phase, as in the case of Ni-based superalloys with γ’-Ni 3 Al 
strengthening [27 , 28] . In 2018, Yang et al. [37] introduced the 
nano-sized coherent γ’ phase into FeCoNi-based multi-component 
alloys, and the precipitation of γ’ signiﬁcantly improves the 
mechanical properties. Since then the γ’-strengthening effects 
in multi-component alloys have been comprehensively investi- 
gated [38–43] . Zhao et al. [44] also added Al and Ti elements 
in NiCoCr alloy and obtained nano-sized L1 2 -(Ni, Co, Cr) 3 (Ti, 
Al) strengthened NiCoCr-based multi-component alloy. The yield 
strength of γ’-strengthened NiCoCr alloy is 70% higher than that 
of single-phase NiCoCr alloy. 
On the other hand, γ”p h a s e with ordered D0 22 structure, sim- 
ilar to γ’, is another coherent strengthening phase in commercial 
Ni-based superalloys. It has been proven that the strengthen- 
ing effect of γ”i s more signiﬁcant than γ’ phase on account 
of higher anti-phase boundary energy and lattice misﬁt (about 
∼2.9%) [45] . In the work of He et al. [46] , the strength of FCC –HEA 
Ni 2 CoCrFeNb 0.15 is greatly enhanced via precipitating γ”p h a s e 
within the FCC matrix. According to our previous research [47] , 
we successfully introduced γ” strengthening phase into NiCoCr- 
based alloys via Nb addition and further improved the mechanical 
properties through adding V element. The V-containing alloy 
we designed presents a yield strength of ∼900 MPa, while still 
maintaining a tensile elongation of over 40% at room temperature, 
exhibiting superior room temperature mechanical properties. 
However, researches on the stability of γ”p h a s e and high- 
temperature mechanical properties are still lacking, due to the 
metastability of γ”-Ni 3 Nb phase in multi-component alloys. Since 
Ni 3 V is the only stable D0 22 –Ni 3 X intermetallic phase in Ni-based 
alloys [48] , it can be postulated that V-addition could stabilize 
γ”p h a s e in the NiCoCr alloy system. Together with the coherent 
strengthening effect of γ” phase, an enhancement of strength and 
ductility synergy at elevated temperatures in this alloy system 
is also promising. As a result, the γ”p h a s e stabilities and high- 
temperature mechanical properties of γ”-strengthened NiCoCr- 
based alloy are investigated in detail in this paper. The microstruc- 
tural evolutions of γ”t o δ phase are also observed and discussed, 
providing further guidance for alloy design and heat treatment 
strategies of Ni-based alloys for elevated-temperature applications. 
2. Alloy design based on CALPHAD technique 
CALPHAD (Calculation of Phase Diagrams) technique [49] was 
applied to conduct compositional design for multi-component al- 
loys, which has been proved to be a feasible and reliable tool 
to evaluate phase stabilities in multi-component alloy systems 
[50–52] . Thermodynamic calculations were performed using 
Thermo-Calc Software (version 2016b 6.1.9357) with all default 
phases in the Ni-based superalloys database (TCNI8). In order to 
obtain a relatively large γ/ γ" two-phase region, the content of Co 
and Cr in the alloy should be precisely controlled because high Co 
content would lead to precipitation of Laves phase, and Cr would 
induce σ phase formation. Therefore, in this study, Ni 2 CoCr was 
used as a master alloy, and a strong γ"-forming element Nb was 
added to introduce γ" phase into the NiCoCr-based alloy. As shown 
in Fig. 1 (a), the addition of Nb causes the precipitation of δ phase, 
where the γ/ δ two-phase region expands with Nb content increas- 
ing. Previous works have proven that the metastable γ”-Ni 3 Nb 
phase would decompose into stable δ-Ni 3 Nb D0 a orthorhombic 
Pmmn phase after long-term aging [53–57] . 
It is noted that when the content of Nb exceeds 4 at.%, σ
phase tends to precipitate at low temperatures. Therefore, the 
content of Nb was determined as 4 at.%. As Ni 3 V was the only 
stable D0 22 Ni 3 X type intermetallic in Ni-X binary systems [48 , 58] 
and V did not lead to third phase precipitation as shown in 
Fig. 1 (b), therefore, V was added into Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb alloy to further 
stabilize the γ” phase. The dissolution temperature of δ phase 
ﬁrst increases and then decreases with the increase of V content, 
and the maximum value locates at 4–5 at.% V content. 
Based on calculation results, the alloys with compositions of 
Ni 48 Co 24 Cr 24 Nb 4 and Ni 48 Co 22 Cr 22 Nb 4 V 4 (at.%) were designed 
(marked as red dashed line in Fig. 1 (a) and (b)), and these two 
alloys were denoted as Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb and Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb4V alloys, 
respectively. 
3. Experimental procedure 
3.1. Material preparation 
The alloy ingots with target compositions were ﬂipped and re- 
melted at least 7 times using a vacuum arc melting apparatus, and 
the purity of raw materials used in this paper exceeds 99.95%. A 
grinding machine was used to remove the oxide layer of cobalt 
chunks before arc melting, and vanadium pieces were cleaned by 
dilute HF solution. The heat treatment process has been formu- 
lated based on thermodynamic calculations. The cast ingots were 
homogenized at 1200 °C for 8 h, and then cut into pieces with a 
thickness of 14 mm using an electro-discharged machine (EDM). 
The alloy pieces were cold-rolled into 2 mm with a total reduc- 
tion of ∼85%, and both samples were recrystallized at 1150 °C for 
8 min. For comparison, recrystallized samples were divided into 
two groups. One was aged at 650 °C for 12–72 h with the purpose 
of introducing the γ” precipitation, and the other group was aged 
at 10 0 0 °C to avoid the precipitation of γ”. All heat treatment was 
conducted in air, as the alloy samples are antioxidative due to high 
Cr content, followed by quick quenching into ice water to maintain 
the microstructure at high temperatures. 
3.2. Microstructure characterization 
A ﬁeld emission scanning electron microscope (FE-SEM, 
JSM7600F, JEOL, Japan) equipped with an energy dispersive spec- 
trometer detector (EDS, Oxford X-Max, Oxford, UK) was used to 
observe the microstructure as well as composition distribution. 
The specimens for SEM observation were ﬁrst ground using 400-, 
60 0-, 80 0-, and 150 0-grit SiC papers, and then chemo-mechanical 
polished using 50 nm colloidal silica. All samples were chemically 
etched (using 5 g FeCl 3 , 20 mL HCl + 100 mL C 2 H 5 OH) before SEM 
observations. 
Phase constitution detection and crystal structure investigation 
were carried out on a ﬁeld-emission transmission electron micro- 
scope (FE-TEM, Tecnai G2 F20 S-TWIN, FEI, USA) and X-ray diffrac- 
tion (XRD, MAX-2250, Rigaku D, Japan) with Cu K α radiation. TEM

The hardness values of samples were measured using an HVS-30–
30P Vickers micro-hardness tester with a loading force of 9.8 N and

4. Results and discussion 
4.1. Microstructure evolution 
The SEM images operated in the back-scattered electron (BSE) 
mode of Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb alloys aged at 650 °C for different time are 
displayed in Fig. 2 , where the triple grain boundaries (TGBs) im- 
ages with higher magniﬁcations are also shown in Fig. 2 (a1-d1). 
The grain sizes of alloys are obtained through image analysis. It can 
be seen from the images that the aging treatment at 650 °C has no 
signiﬁcant effect on the grain size of this alloy system, and the av- 
erage grain size of Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb alloy is about ∼72.5 μm. The 1150 
°C/8 min recrystallized alloy indicates an FCC single-phase struc- 
ture, and no precipitates are found at TGBs. After aging at 650 °C 
for 12 h, a small amount of nano-sized lamellar phases got pre- 
cipitated at the TGBs of Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb alloy. With the prolongation 
of aging treatment, more ﬁne lamellar precipitates are found at 
the TGBs. It is diﬃcult to quantify its chemical composition us- 
ing SEM-EDS (Fig. S1 in Supplementary Information) since it is on 
a nanoscale size. According to He et al. [46] and from Thermo- 
Calc calculations, it can be inferred that δ phase nucleation is easy 
to occur at grain boundaries in Nb-containing Ni-based superal- 
loys [59] . Metastable γ”-Ni 3 Nb phases in Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb alloy might 
transform into stable δ phases at temperatures over 650 °C sim- 
ilar to certain Ni-based superalloys [16 , 55] . In order to conﬁrm 
the grain boundary phases, TEM and XRD is further performed on 
the long-term (100 h) aged Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb alloy at 800 °C. Fig. S2(a) 
and (b) shows that a large number of lamellar phases are found 
in 800 °C aged Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb alloy, and the morphology is simi- 
lar to the grain boundary phase of 650 °C aged alloys as shown 
in Fig. 2 (d1). The selected area diffraction pattern (SADP) indi- 
cated that the lamellar phases in Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb are δ phases with 
D0 a structure, as shown in Fig. S2(c), which is the same as re- 
ported in Ref [60] . The XRD result of 800 °C aged Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb 
alloy also indicates that lamellar phases in this alloy system are 
δ phases, as shown in Fig. S2(d). Although a small amount of δ at 
grain boundary can improve the room- and medium-temperature 
strength of the alloys, it is invariably incoherent with the FCC ma- 
trix, which cannot confer strength at elevated temperatures [16] . A 
large amount of δ precipitation at the grain boundary would de- 
teriorate the ductility of the alloy. Therefore, the precipitation of δ
phase should be prohibited.

The formation processes of δ phase are summarized as fol- 
lowed: During aging at temperatures below the γ” solvus tem- 
perature, the dislocations in γ matrix interact with γ” particles, 
producing stacking faults within them. These faults lay along the 
close-packed (112) γ”p l a n e s . The δ phases would then nucleate 
at the stacking faults within γ”p r e c i p i t a t e s . With the prolonga- 
tion of aging time, the δ phases will gradually grow into γ matrix, 
and linkage of small δ phases leads to a formation of δ plates [61] . 
Moreover, previous research has shown that δ nucleation occurred 
ﬁrstly on grain and twin boundaries since the free energies of ac- 
tivation for the formation of δ nucleation at the grain and twin 
boundaries are relatively lower than that at intra-crystalline [60] .

nary systems [48] , and V has been proven to be a strong γ”f o r m - 
ing element in Ni-based alloys, mainly distributed in γ” phases

4.2. γ ”-D0 22 phase characterization 
The XRD patterns are depicted in Fig. 4 (a) and (b), where 650 
°C aged Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb and Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb4V alloys are both indexed 
to be a single FCC structure. The δ phase only exists at the TGBs 
of aged Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb alloy, and the volume fraction of δ phase is 
relatively low so that it is diﬃcult to ﬁnd. As a result, there are 
no obvious diffraction peaks of δ phase detected in aged alloys. As 
for the γ” phase, it has been reported that γ and γ” phases have 
similar lattice parameters, wherefore some of the diffraction peaks 
from γ and γ” phases may partially overlap. It is rather diﬃcult to 
identify them via XRD, but we have characterized the existence of 
γ”p e a k s using synchrontron radiation, as has been recently pub- 
lished in Ref [47] . The lattice parameters of γ phase in these two 
alloys have been calculated. The XRD results indicate that the FCC 
lattice parameter of Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb is 3.583 ˚A, while in V-containing 
Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb4V alloy, this value is 3.588 ˚A, slightly higher than that 
in V-free alloy. The reason for the higher γ lattice parameter in V- 
containing alloy is that the atomic radius of V is larger than those 
of Ni, Co and Cr.

4.3. Phase stability 
The high solvus temperature of strengthening phases in 
superalloys is indispensable to maintain the property at ele- 
vated temperatures. As one of the main strengthening phases 
in commercial Ni-based superalloys, γ”w o u l d cause signiﬁcant 
strengthening effects from room temperature to medium temper- 
atures (25–650 °C). But when the temperature exceeds 650 °C, 
metastable γ” phases in Ni-based superalloys get dissolved quickly 
[16 , 55] . The solvus temperature of γ”i n Ni-based superalloys is 
usually between 500 and 700 °C [69] .

loy is about 995 °C, and the δ-solvus temperature is 1088 °C. It 
has been reported that γ”→ δ transformation (metastable-stable 
transformation) will occur during long-term aging treatment be- 
low γ” solvus temperature [60] , which is also found in the present 
work as shown in Fig. 2 . The thermal stability of V-containing 
γ”p h a s e is improved compared to V-free γ”, indicating promis- 
ing mechanical properties at elevated temperatures. Moreover, the 
Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb alloy exhibits a relatively higher solidus temperature, 
which is about 1298 °C. Adding V slightly decreases the melt- 
ing point of this alloy, and the solidus of V-containing alloy is 
about 1291 °C. The incipient melting points of the presented al- 
loy are both higher than that of commercial Ni-based superal- 
loys IN 939 (1150 °C) [70] . The temperature difference between 
the dissolution temperature of precipitates and solidus tempera- 
ture of the presented NiCoCr-based alloys in this work is as high as 
∼254 °C, suggesting a larger heat treatment window and better hot 
processability. 
According to the results of DTA, in presented alloys, the γ”- 
solvus temperatures are below 10 0 0 °C, while the δ-solvus tem- 
peratures are between 1040 and 1090 °C. In order to verify the ex- 
perimental results determined from DTA curves, another 10 0 0 °C 
annealing treatment was performed. Fig. 6 (a) and (b) shows BSE 
images of 10 0 0 °C aged Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb and Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb4V alloys, 
and both alloys exhibit γ/ δ two-phase structure. In 650 °C aged 
Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb alloy, δ phase is only found at grain boundaries, while 
no δ phase in Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb4V alloy is found under the same heat 
treatment regime. The nano-sized γ”p h a s e homogeneously dis- 
tributes at intra-crystalline of both 650 °C aged alloys according 
to TEM results. But in 10 0 0 °C aged alloys, δ phases are found at 
both intra-crystalline and grain boundaries, suggesting that the γ”
phase at intra-crystalline has transformed to δ phase due to the 
high-temperature aging process.

4.4. Mechanical properties 
Vickers hardness has been measured for solid-solution treated, 
650 °C aged and 10 0 0 °C aged alloys, respectively as shown in 
Fig. 8 (a). The γ”-strengthened NiCoCr-based alloys in this work 
present a classic aging response for a precipitation-hardened al- 
loy. The Vickers hardness of solution-treated alloys and 10 0 0 °C 
aged alloys is almost the same, indicating that the γ”p h a s e com- 
pletely transforms into δ phase upon 10 0 0 °C aging. Because of 
the precipitation of γ” phase, the hardness of 650 °C aged alloys 
increases signiﬁcantly. The difference in microstructure (precipita- 
tion of δ phase at grain boundaries) of 650 °C aged alloys does 
not lead to an obvious difference in hardness. The hardness of 650 
°C aged Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb and Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb4V alloys is measured to be 
411 ±4 HV and 409 ±7 HV, respectively, which is 120% higher than 
that of solid-solution treated alloys and 10 0 0 °C aged alloys. 
Fig. 8 (b) depicts the comparison of mass densities of presented 
alloys and commercial Ni-based superalloys. The densities of 
Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb and Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb4V alloys were measured to be 8.31 
and 7.90 g cm −3 , respectively. The densities of γ”-strengthened 
Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb4V alloys are obviously lower than those of com- 
mercial Ni-based superalloys IN 939 (8.20 g cm −3 ) and Waspaloy 
(8.21 g cm −3 ) [16] since the content of Cr and Co of Ni-based su- 
peralloys is lower than that of alloys in the present investigation. 
Moreover, Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb4V alloy does not contain heavy elements, 
such as Ta (16.68 g cm −3 ), W (19.35 g cm −3 ) nor Re (21.04 g cm −3 ).

4Nb4V alloys, which decreases after V addition, resulting in a 
decrease in thermal stress during heating. It has been reported 
that reduced CTE of metallic materials helps to avoid welding 
cracks or assembly reset problems [71] . Fig. 9 (b) shows the yield 
strength versus temperature curves of 650 °C aged Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb 
and Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb4V alloys, and the curves of Waspaloy [16] and 
IN 939 [72] are also provided for comparison. The room tem-

those of Waspaloy and IN 939 ( ∼800 MPa). The curves of yield 
strength versus temperature of Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb and Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb4V 
alloys are above commercial Ni-based superalloys from room tem- 
perature to 650 °C, as shown in Fig. 9 (b). Detailed tensile proper- 
ties of presented alloys are shown in Table S1. The 650 °C yield 
strengths of Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb and Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb4V alloys are 704 MPa 
and 681 MPa, respectively. The yield strengths of Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb4V 
alloy are slightly lower than those of Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb alloy at room 
temperature and 650 °C, because the precipitation of δ phase 
is prohibited after V addition. Small amount of δ phase, which 
is mainly distributed at grain boundaries, can confer strength at 
room- and medium-temperature (25–650 °C) strength of Ni-based 
alloys [16] . Therefore, at room- and medium-temperature, strength 
of Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb alloy (grain boundary δ + ∼20% γ”+ γ matrix) is 
slightly higher than those of Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb4V alloy ( ∼30% γ”+ γ
matrix). The strengths of γ”-strengthened alloys at 650 °C are 
comparable with Waspaloy and IN 939 ( ∼700 MPa), suggesting that 
the γ”p r e c i p i t a t e in Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb and Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb4V alloys still 
possesses considerable strengthening effect at 650 °C. One major 
reason is that the main strengthening phase in Waspaloy and IN 
939 is γ’ phase, where strengthening is caused mainly through 
the necessity to disorder the particles as they are sheared, while 
γ” strengthens by virtue of high coherency strains in the lattice 
[16] . The strengthening mechanism of γ”a n d relatively high γ”- 
solvus temperatures in presented alloys result in the fact that this 
phase can provide very high strength at low to intermediate tem- 
peratures.

strength of V-containing alloy is about 383 MPa at 800 °C, which 
is 50% higher than that of V-free alloy. It is known that the 
coherent γ”p h a s e can provide a good high-temperature strength-

5. Conclusions 
In this work, the effects of alloying elements V to γ”- 
strengthened multi-component NiCoCr-based alloys on microstruc- 
ture evolution, phase stability and high-temperature mechanical 
properties have been investigated. The following conclusions can 
be drawn: 
(1) The addition of V into the γ”-strengthened Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb al- 
loy can reﬁne the γ grains and γ” phase. The grain size 
and size of γ”p h a s e can be decreased to ∼28.8 μm and 
about 15 nm ×6 nm after V addition. V addition signiﬁcantly 
increases the volume fraction of the γ”p r e c i p i t a t e s from 
∼20% to ∼30%. 
(2) The γ”-solvus and δ-solvus temperatures of Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb al- 
loy are 991 °C and 1045 °C, respectively. And the addition 
of V can slightly increase the γ”-solvus temperature and 
dissolution temperature of δ phases. The γ”-solvus and δ- 
solvus temperatures of Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb4V alloy are as high as 
995 °C and 1088 °C. The thermal stability of V-containing γ”
phase is improved compared to V-free γ”, indicating promis- 
ing mechanical properties at elevated temperatures. 
(3) The speciﬁc strengths of the V-containing γ”-strengthened 
multi-component NiCoCr-based alloy are higher than those 
of the Ni-based superalloys IN 939 and Waspaloy at 650 °C. 
In Ni 2 CoCr-4Nb4V alloys, the value is as high as ∼86.2 MPa 
g −1 cm −3 at 650 °C, while that of the Ni-based superalloys 
is only ∼84.1 MPa g −1 cm −3 . 
In summary, V addition can improve the stability of γ”p h a s e 
and high-temperature mechanical properties in NiCoCr-based al- 
loys. This work provides solid foundations for the further de- 
velopment of high-performance precipitation-strengthened multi- 
component alloys at elevated temperatures.
