ABSTRACT
Using high-entropy alloys (HEAs) as reactive materials remains challenging because optimising the interplay 
between their reactivity and strength – ductility trade-off is difficult. In this study, body-centred cubic (BCC) 
TiZrVNb
x 
HEAs (x = 0.16, 0.33, 0.75 and 1), with an Nb content of 5 – 25 at%, are produced by vacuum arc 
melting. The results show that the number of complex precipitates at the grain boundaries decreases with the 
increase in the Nb content. Nb
0.75 
exhibits remarkable grain refinement behaviour among the four alloys, 
achieving a high quasi-static yield strength and compressive strength of 990 MPa and 1211 MPa, respectively. In 
addition, it exhibits the highest dynamic yield strength of 2062 MPa at the strain rate of 3012 s
 1 
among the 
single-phase BCC refractory HEAs. Among the four alloys, Nb
0.75 
exhibits the lowest critical velocity for impact- 
induced reactions (17.56 m/s), which is attributed to the cleavage fracture triggered by dislocation pile-up and 
thermal softening effects. Therefore, the Nb
0.75 
alloy exhibits superior mechanical properties and energy release 
performance, having immense potential for use in the field of preformed fragments. This work provides a 
promising approach for the development of high-entropy energetic alloys with an optimal combination of 
reactivity and strength – ductility.
1. Introduction
Reactive materials (RMs) [1] belong to a novel type of high-energy 
structural materials, typically consisting of two or more solid re -
actants. Differing from explosives that are highly sensitive to ignition, 
they maintain the mechanical strength required for structural compo -
nents and exhibit a certain plasticity. Under external stimuli such as 
heat, electricity and impact loading, RMs have the ability to cause 
chemical interactions between elements or between elements and the 
environment, which can lead to explosions or burning that release a 
significant amount of energy [2,3] . RMs possess both kinetic energy 
penetration capability, similar to inert materials and chemical damage 
capability, making the study of the design and mechanical behaviour of 
RMs highly promising for applications involving efficient damage [4,5] .
Studies on metal-based RMs primarily focus on the metal-fluoride 
type, such as Al-PTFE [6] ; These materials release high energy and 
exhibit fast combustion propagation during the reaction process. How -
ever, their low strength limits their scope of application. Intermetallic 
compounds, such as W/Zr [7] , have high strength and generate signif -
icant combustion heat, ensuring good penetration performance. 
However, the formation of brittle Laves phases severely limits their 
processability. Metastable amorphous alloys, such as Zr-based [8] and 
Hf-based [9] amorphous alloys, exhibit high energy density and perform 
well under impact failure conditions. However, owing to their lack of 
periodic crystal structure, they cannot undergo dislocation-mediated 
plastic deformation under load, resulting in general brittleness and 
difficulty in deformation processing, which in turn affects the energy 
release. Therefore, identifying RMs that can ensure the activity of the 
structural materials while exhibiting remarkable mechanical properties 
is the primary research focus of current research.
In 2004, Yeh [10] proposed the development of a new type of alloy 
that is conceptually distinct from the traditional alloys. These alloys, 
referred to as multi-principal element alloys, are defined as alloys con -
taining five or more elements, with the concentration of each in the 
range of 5 – 35 %. With further advances in research, high-entropy alloys 
(HEAs) comprising four or more elements in approximately equal pro -
portions were developed [11] . The single-phase solid solution structure 
produced by the "high entropy effect" exhibits superior mechanical 
properties, such as high strength, high hardness and good plasticity, in 
contrast to traditional alloys [12,13] . Therefore, researchers have

3.3. Nano-indentation and quasi-static compression testing
Nano-indentation testing was carried out on a Berkovich indenter 
using the continuous stiffness measurement (CSM) technique. The 
modulus of elasticity and nano-hardness were obtained as shown in 
Fig. 5 (a). When the Nb content increases, the hardness of TiZrVNb
x 
initially increases and then decreases, reaching a maximum of 5.32 GPa 
in Nb
0.75
. A severely pronounced lattice distortion leading to solid so -
lution strengthening is responsible for the significant increase in the 
hardness of the alloys. The elastic modulus shows a minimal variation of 
11.9 % between the highest and lowest Nb content, indicating that 
variations in the Nb content do not significantly impact the elastic 
modulus. This suggests that the elastic modulus of these alloys is not 
sensitive to variations in the Nb content.
The stress – strain curve was acquired using a 9 μ m diameter plat- 
punch indenter as shown in Fig. 5 (b). These curves were compared 
with the true stress – strain curves obtained from quasi-static compres -
sion tests [ Fig. 5 (d)]. Table 4 lists the yield and compressive strengths 
and relative errors derived from the two testing methods. The analysis 
shows that as the Nb content increases, the yield and compressive 
strengths of the alloys follow a trend similar to that of hardness, initially 
increasing and then decreasing. All four alloys exhibit true strains 
exceeding 50 %. Among them, Nb
0.75 
shows the highest yield (990 MPa) 
and compressive strengths (1211 MPa). This may be attributed to the 
synergistic effects of grain boundary strengthening, grain refinement, 
and solid solution strengthening that hinder the motion of dislocations. 
The yield strength values obtained from nano-indentation testing show a 
trend consistent with those from quasi-static compression tests, with an 
average relative error of 26.1 %. This discrepancy primarily stems from 
the fundamental differences between the two methods: nano- 
indentation reflects the localised deformation response at the micro -
scale, whereas compression testing characterises the macroscopic bulk 
mechanical behaviour. Therefore, nano-indentation can be considered a 
preliminary, rapid and "non-destructive" method for determining the 
yield strength of alloys while selecting material properties.

4. Conclusion
In this study, TiZrVNb
x 
HEAs (x = 0.16, 0.33, 0.75 and 1) were 
designed and synthesised to investigate the effects of varying the Nb 
content on the phase structure, microstructure and mechanical proper -
ties of the alloys. The following are the core conclusions: 
1. Variations in Nb concentration alter the thermodynamic driving 
force during solidification. In Nb
0.75
, a surge in constitutional 
undercooling promotes significant grain refinement and an increase 
in the density of HAGBs, resulting in optimal mechanical perfor -
mance, with a nano-indentation hardness of 5.32 GPa, yield strength 
of 990 MPa and compressive strength of 1211 MPa.
2. Although slight variations exist between the yield strengths 
measured by nano-indentation and quasi-static compression testing, 
the two methods demonstrate a similar trend. This validates nano- 
indentation as a rapid, efficient technique for the qualitative char -
acterisation of phase-specific and bulk quasi-static mechanical 
properties of HEAs. Such capability provides deeper insights into the 
microstructure-property relationships, particularly in multiphase 
systems where the localised deformation mechanisms dominate.
3. The TiZrVNb
x 
alloys exhibit pronounced strain rate effects. Nb
0.75 
achieves a dynamic yield strength of 2062 MPa at 3012 s
 1
, resulting 
from the enhanced strain hardening owing to the ultrafine-grained 
microstructure. Concurrently, Nb
0.75 
has a lower adiabatic shear 
sensitivity, making it susceptible to fracture under dynamic impacts, 
thereby increasing the efficiency of the impact reaction energy
