Keywords: MIM(Metal Injection Molding), Inconel 713C, High temperature, Tensile property 
 
Abstract. Inconel 713C alloy was tried to manufacture by usi ng MIM(Metal Injection Molding) 
process. The high-temperature mechanical properties of MIMed Inconel 713C were also investigated. 
Processing defects such as pores and binders could be observed near the surface. Tensile tests were 
conducted from room temperature to 900°C. The resul t of tensile tests showed that this alloy had 
similar or somewhat higher strengths (YS: 734 MPa, UTS: 968 MPa, elongation: 7.16 % at room 
temperature) from RT to 700°C than those of convent ional Inconel 713C alloys. Above 800°C, 
however, ultimate tensile strength decreased rapidl y with increasing temperature (lower than casted 
Inconel 713C). Based on the observation of fractography, initial crack was found to have started near 
the surface defects and propagated rapidly. The sup erior mechanical properties of MIMed Inconel 
713C could be obtained by optimizing the MIM process parameters. 
 
Introduction 
Inconel 713C is a precipitation hardening-type allo y of Ni-Cr-Al-Mo-Nb composition with 
excellent high-temperature mechanical properties, c astability, oxidation resistance, and thermal 
fatigue resistance [1]. Therefore, it is used as material for power plant gas turbines and turbine blades. 
In addition, it is expected to be applied as component of car turbo chargers. Inconel 713C is generally 
produced through casting process. Although casting is more cost-effective compared to other 
processes and is able for mass production, it also has disadvantages, i.e., limited microstructural 
control and inhomogeneous mechanical properties among locations. 
MIM (Metal Injection Molding) is a process of fabri cating part shapes using metal powder and 
binders (mixing and injection molding steps) and pr oducing final products through de-binding, 
sintering, and part finishing steps [2-3]. As the a dvantages of the MIM process, the microstructures 
can be easily controlled, excellent properties can be had for parts manufactured through this process, 
and mass production can be done through this process. 
Note, however, that few studies have been conducted to date with regard to the mechanical 
properties of Inconel alloy parts made using the MI M process. In particular, almost no study dealt 
with high-temperature mechanical properties [4]. In this study, the room-temperature and 
high-temperature tensile properties of Inconel 713C fabricated using the MIM process were examined, 
and investigation of the effects of microstructures on-high temperature tensile properties was 
attempted.

Results and discussion 
Fig. 2 shows the microstructures of the Inconel 713C manufactured through the MIM process. As 
shown in the figure, process defects, pores, and bi nders were observed. These defects were found 
mainly on the surfaces of the manufactured specimens and were deemed to have occurred where the 
powder used in the MIM process was not completely sintered or where the de-binding process was not 
completed. As shown on the right side of Fig. 2, ar ound ~1 µm phases were distributed inside the 
specimens. Thus, the specimens could be said to hav e relatively even microstructures. In general, 
Inconel 713 is added with Al, Ti, and Nb for precip itation reinforcement; Cr and Mo are added as 
elements for solid solution hardening. Inconel 713C is known to be reinforced by precipitating 
γ′-Ni 3(Al, Ti) on γ-Ni matrix [1]. Approximately ~1µm phases were identified as γ′-Ni 3(Al, Ti) phases, 
which were major precipitated phases.

Fig. 3 shows the results of temperature tensile tests conducted at temperatures ranging from room 
temperature to 900°C. The Inconel 713C manufactured through the MIM process exhibited yield 
strength of 734 MPa, maximum tensile strength of 968 MPa, and elongation of 7.16 %. As shown in 
the stress-strain curve (a), some plastic deformation occurred after the yield point, and fracture rapidly 
took place thereafter. The results of the compariso n of the aforementioned tensile properties 
(symbols) with those of the casted Inconel 713C materials (dotted lines) [5] are shown under (b). The 
MIMed material in this study was found to have yield strength similar to that of the casted material at 
room temperature, but maximum tensile strength incr eased by around 14.6 %. As the temperature 
increased, although yield strength exhibited behavi or similar to that of the casted alloy, maximum 
tensile strength decreased faster. Maximum elongation was found to have relatively lower values than 
that of the alloy manufactured through casting proc esses. The results at 700°C showed that yield 
strength and tensile strength values at room temper ature were maintained. At higher temperatures, 
however, strength continuously decreased rapidly. E longation decreased until the temperature 
increased to 750°C and increased thereafter. Such f indings of elongation decreasing with increasing 
temperature in MIMed material are consistent with t he results of a study by B. Kloden, et al 
(suggesting only for 650°C and 900°C) [6]; the same results were obtained in casted Inconel 713C. 
Likewise, at 900°C, a plastic deformation region that had not appeared at other high temperatures ((a)) 
was observed in a relatively greater length.

Fig. 4 shows the tensile fracture surfaces deformed at room temperature and 700°C. It was found 
that initial cracks could be formed in vulnerable l ocal regions near the surface, propagating rapidly 
thereafter. At the starting point of cracks, as sho wn in Fig. 4, processing defects, pores, and binder s 
were importantly noted. In other words, in the case of this MIMed Inconel material, cracks are 
considered to initiate due to defects in microstruc tures and then propagate rapidly. These results are 
different from the results reported by B. Kloden, e t al, i.e., when tests are conducted at high 
temperatures, cracks are formed by carbide or oxide, which exhibits brittleness; hence the decreasing 
high temperature strengths and elongation. As such, in manufacturing conditions that were not 
optimized, the effects of the formation of carbide or oxide on high-temperature tensile properties were 
verified to decrease relatively, whereas the effect s of processing defects such as pores or binders 
increased further. In other words, it can be reasonably inferred that, when Inconel 713C parts are made 
using the MIM process, the control of process varia bles is most important, results in homogeneous 
microstructure without defects.

Conclusions 
In this study, Inconel 713C materials were manufactured using the MIM process. MIMed Inconel 
713 materials showed higher room-temperature tensil e strength than alloys manufactured through 
casting processes. As the temperature increased, ho wever, maximum tensile strength rapidly 
decreased, recording lower values compared to caste d alloys. The tensile destruction of MIMed 
Inconel 713C was found to have been caused mainly by cracks formed by defects appearing unevenly 
on the surfaces due to process variables. This conf irms anew the importance of optimization of 
process variables that can minimize defects in micr ostructures when Inconel 713C is manufactured 
through the MIM process.
