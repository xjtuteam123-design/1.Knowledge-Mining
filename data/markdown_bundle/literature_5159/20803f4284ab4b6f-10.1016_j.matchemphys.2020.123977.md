ABSTRACT 
To reduce or even eliminate the negative effects of grain boundaries (GBs) in nickel-based superalloys, the GB- 
segregation behaviors of 14 main alloying elements X of superalloys and their effects on the stability and fracture 
strength of Ni Σ 5 [001](210) GB were systematically investigated based on the concept of GB segregation en -
gineering (GBSE), using first-principles calculations. We found that 10 alloying elements can segregate towards 
the GB. Their GB-segregation tendencies increase in the order of W < Mo < Al < Mn < Cu < Ti < Ru < Ta < Hf 
< Zr, due to the increase in atomic radius and electronegativity differences between X and host Ni atoms. The GB 
energies of X -segregated GBs depend linearly on the GB segregation energies of solutes X , the stronger segre -
gation tendency of solute X could lower the GB energy and make the GB more stable. Except Cu, the segregations 
of the other 9 solutes increase the GB fracture strength. The segregation-induced GB stabilizing and strength -
ening mechanisms were further revealed on the electronic level. This work could be beneficial for manipulating 
the composition, structure and properties of Ni GBs and designing Ni-based superalloys with desired perfor -
mances based on the GBSE. 
1. Introduction 
Nickel-based superalloys are widely used in nuclear reactors and hot 
end parts of advanced aero-engines and gas turbines such as turbine 
disks and blades, due to their excellent mechanical and creep properties 
at high temperature [ 1 – 3 ]. Grain boundary (GB) is a common type of 
planar lattice defects in such superalloys [ 4 ]. For example, the coherent 
nano-twin boundaries belonging to a kind of special GBs, can emerge in 
both single-crystal and polycrystalline superalloys when micro-twins 
form at certain deformation temperatures and stresses [ 5 , 6 ]. 
Generally, GBs can hinder the dislocation motion and thus significantly 
strengthen the superalloys (i.e., Hall-Petch effect) [ 7 – 9 ]. However, GBs 
can also have negative effects on material properties. On the one hand, 
due to the excess energy of GBs, GBs are thermodynamically unstable, 
resulting in the migration or even disappearance of GBs under certain 
temperature and stress conditions. This further increases the structural 
and mechanical instabilities of superalloys [ 10 ]. On the other hand, GBs 
are usually the mechanically weak regions in materials as compared 
with grain interiors (hereafter termed the bulk phase environment). The 
local stress concentration induced by the limited motion of dislocations

such as fracture strength, toughness, fatigue resistance, ductility, creep 
resistance and so forth of nickel-based superalloys are largely deter -

fects of GBs and design superalloys with beneficial GB behaviors, it is 
essential to improve the thermodynamic stability and mechanical 
strength of GBs in nickel-based superalloys. 
Using the GB segregation behavior of solutes as a manipulation tool, 
which allows the chemical composition, structure and properties of GBs 
to be adjusted [ 22 ], Raabe et al. proposed the concept of grain boundary 
segregation engineering (GBSE) [ 23 ]. It has been proved that the GBSE 
is a successful method of enhancing the thermodynamic stability and 
mechanical strength of GBs [ 22 , 24 – 26 ]. For instance, using transmission 
electron microscopy and atom probe tomography, Rabbe et al. have 
observed that the formation of nanosized austenitic reversion layers at 
decorated martensite GBs is a result of the strong interfacial segregation 
of austenite stabilizing elements (Mn) and the release of elastic stresses 
from the host martensite. Upon sufficient Mn segregation, the austenite 
phase gradually becomes stabilized at the martensite GB and finally a 
ductile and tough martensite was obtained [ 22 ]. Zhao et al. have found 
that the alloying element Cu has a large driving force to segregate to Al 
GBs and its segregation leads to a significant decrease in the GB energy 
but an enhancement in the cohesion of Al GBs [ 24 ]. 
Based on the concept of GBSE, the GB segregation tendency of 
alloying elements in Ni-based superalloys and their effects on the 
structural and mechanical properties of GBs have been studied. Razu -
movskiy et al. found that elements Hf, Nb, Ta and B can segregate to the 
Ni Σ 5 [001](210) GB and improve the strengthening energy of the GB. 
These alloying elements had been successfully used to design a new 
powder metallurgy Ni-based superalloy with higher tensile strength and 
creep resistance [ 27 ]. Using first-principles calculations, Bentria et al. 
studied the segregation behavior of vanadium. Their results shown that 
the element vanadium, as an effective strengthening element for the Ni 
Σ 5 [001](210) GB, tends to segregate towards the GB and thus increases 
the tensile strength of GB by about 17% [ 28 ]. However, not all elements 
with the GB segregation tendency can strengthen the GB. It was found 
that the segregations of elements H and P in Ni Σ 5 [001](210) GB are 
harmful for the GB fracture strength [ 29 ]. V ˇsiansk ´a et al. observed that 
the interstitial segregation of Si enhances the cohesion of Ni Σ 5 [001] 
(210) GB, while the interstitial segregation of S, Ge, As and Se as well as 
the substitutional segregation of Ga, In, Sn, Sb and Te embrittle the GB 
[ 30 ]. Although the previous studies have been focusing on the segre -
gation tendency of alloying elements in Ni Σ 5 [001](210) GB, the 
relation of GB segregation behavior with atomic properties such as the 
atomic radius, electronegativity and so on is still unclear. Moreover, the 
effect of GB segregation of alloying elements on the thermodynamic 
stability of GB and the underlying mechanism of segregation-induced 
stabilizations have not been well elucidated yet. 
In order to provide guidance for stabilizing and strengthening spe -
cific GBs based on the promising GBSE, it should be indispensable to 
settle the foregoing issues. Hence, the influences of atomic radius and 
electronegativity differences between alloying elements X ( X = Zr, Hf, 
Ta, Ru, Ti, Cu, Mn, Al, Mo, W, Cr, Fe, Re and Co) and host atoms Ni on 
the segregation energy of alloying elements in Ni Σ 5 [001](210) GB are 
studied firstly in present work, then the effects of GB segregation of 
alloying elements X on the thermodynamic stability (GB energy) and 
fracture strength (work of separation) of the GB are systematically 
investigated. Finally, through analyzing the crystal orbital Hamiltonian 
populations (COHP) [ 31 – 35 ] and deformation charge densities, micro -
scopic mechanisms of segregation-induced stabilizing and strengthening 
effects on the Ni Σ 5 [001](210) GB are discussed at the electronic level. 
This work might be helpful for manipulating the composition, structure 
and properties of GBs based on the concept of GBSE and designing the 
Ni-based superalloys with desired performances.

Here (210) and [001] denote the habit plane index and the crystal Miller 
index of tilt axis of the symmetrical tilt GB model, respectively. 
A vacuum layer of 10 Å was added into the GB supercell to block the 
interaction between neighboring interfaces due to the periodic bound -
ary conditions [ 36 ]. By changing the distance between two grains in the 
GB supercell monotonically and calculating the total energy of corre -
sponding GB supercells, a stable GB supercell model with an optimal 
distance of 0.3 Å between two grains and the lowest total energy was 
obtained [ 24 ]. It should be noted that grains in the GB model should be 
thick enough to simulate the bulk phase environment or grain interior of 
a perfect Ni crystal. After a series of tests, 21 atomic layers with 2 atoms 
per layer for the GB supercell was considered to be sufficient in this 
work. As shown in Fig. 1 (c), a free surface (FS) model of Ni (210), which 
consists of same number of Ni atoms and same vacuum layer with the Ni 
Σ 5 [001](210) GB model, was built to simulate the bulk Ni crystal and 
calculate the GB energy, segregation energy and work of separation. 
2.2. First-principles total energy calculations 
All calculations were performed within the framework of density 
functional theory (DFT) by using the Vienna ab initio simulation package 
[ 37 – 39 ]. The projector augmented wave method (PAW) was employed 
to describe the electron-ion interactions [ 37 , 40 ]. The 
exchange-correlation functional was treated using the 
Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) form within the generalized gradient 
approximation (GGA) [ 40 ]. A reasonable value of plane-wave cut-off 
energy and k -points were selected after convergence tests [ 41 , 42 ]. The 
plane-wave cut-off energy was set to 350 eV and a Γ -centered k -mesh of 
6 × 3 × 1 was chosen to sample the Brillouin zone. For the relaxation of 
GB and FS models, the convergence criterions of energy and force were 
selected as 1 × 10
-5 
eV and 0.05 eV/Å, respectively, the spin polarization 
was set for all calculations [ 30 ]. During the relaxation calculations, 
atoms in outermost (top and down) three atomic layers of the GB and FS 
models were completely fixed.

4. Conclusions 
The segregation behaviors of 14 alloying elements X ( X = Zr, Hf, Ta, 
Ru, Ti, Cu, Mn, Al, Mo, W, Cr, Fe, Re and Co) in the Ni Σ 5 [001](210) 
grain boundary (GB) and their effects on the GB energy, fracture 
strength and electronic properties of Ni Σ 5 [001](210) GB were sys -
tematically investigated from first-principles calculations, in order to 
clarify the GB segregation tendencies of these alloying elements in 
nickel-based superalloys as well as the GB stabilizing and strengthening 
mechanisms induced by the GB segregation of alloying elements. Our 
results can be summarized as follows: 
(I) Except elements Cr, Fe, Re and Co, the other 10 alloying elements 
prefer segregating to the Ni Σ 5 [001](210) GB. Their GB segre -
gation tendencies increase in the order of W < Mo < Al < Mn <
Cu < Ti < Ru < Ta < Hf < Zr. The increasing segregation ten -
dency of alloying elements could be attributed to the increase in 
atomic radius and electronegativity differences between host Ni 
atoms and alloying elements.

(III) The GB segregations of elements Hf, Zr, Al and Mn mainly focus 
on improving the thermodynamic stability, while the GB segre -
gations of elements W and Mo significantly promote the fracture 
strength of Ni Σ 5 [001](210) GB. The GB segregation of Ta is 
found to maximize the equal enhancement of thermodynamic 
stability and fracture strength of the GB. 
(IV) The mechanism of segregation-induced GB stabilization is that 
the GB segregation of alloying elements causes a reduction of 
anti-bonding states occupying at high energy levels and an in -
crease of bonding states occupying at low energy levels below the 
Fermi level. For the GB fracture strength, the strengthening 
mechanism of GB segregation of alloying elements X can be 
mainly attributed to the increase in strength of X -Ni bonds at the 
GB.
