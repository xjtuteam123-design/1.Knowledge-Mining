Introduction Rapidly solidification (RS) is the most effective proc- ess for the development of elevated-temperature alumi- num Tensile strength of 300 MPa and more at 573 K is desired for the elevated-temperature aluminum alloys. Good workability that enables to work the alloys with maintaining the original mechanical properties is, moreover, required in the practical use. We have previ- ously reported that A193Ti3.5Fe3.5 RS powder metallurgy (P /M) alloy exhibited high tensile strength of 786 MPa, reasonable plastic elongation of 3.2%, high thermal sta- bility up to 600K, low density of 2.90 kg/m3, high Young's modulus of 91 GPa and low coemcient of ther- mal expansion of 20.2 x 10-6 The A1-Ti-Fe P/ M alloy is expected to be applied as elevated-temper- ature materials such as Al-Fe��Ce, Al��Fe-Mo, Al��Fe��V�� Si and Al-Cr-Zr RS P/ M alloys, because the Ti and Fe elements exhibit limited solid solubility and low diffusiv- ity in Recently, we have exhibited that the addition of 1.0 at%Cr and Mo to A193Ti3.5Fe3.5 RS p/ M alloy improved effectively its thermal stability(10) elevated-temperature strength(10) and wear resistance (11) The elevated-temperature strength was, however, short of the target. On the other hand, although workability is usually inconsistent with the elevated-temperature strength, it has been reported that nanocrystalline alu- minum alloys exhibited high-strain-rate superplastici- ty(12). The further investigation of the Al-Ti-Fe-(Cr, Mo) RS P/ M alloys with nanocrystalline structure is, there- fore, expected to obtain good workability and high t This Paper was Presented at the Autumn Meeting of the Japan Institute of Metals, held in Ehime, on September 28, 1998. elevated-temperature strength. In this paper, we will report the development of elevated-temperature Al-Ti- Fe-(Cr, Mo) P/ M alloys with a high strength of more than 300 MPa at 573 K and good high-strain-rate workability. II. Experimental Procedure RS powders were produced by a high-pressure helium-gas atomization, where the dynamic gas-pressure was 9.8 MPa. The powders that were sieved to less than 25 gm were first packed into a copper can with an inner diameter of 20 mm and an outer diameter of 23 mm, and then were degassed for 900 s at the same temperature as the extrusion temperature. The extrusion was performed at an extrusion ratio of 10, temperatures ranging from 623 to 823 K, and a ram speed of 2.5 mm/ s. The struc- ture of the extruded bulk alloys was investigated by X- ray diffractometry (XRD) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM). Tensile strength and elongation were investigated at a strain rate of 5 x 10-4 s -1 using an Instron testing machine. The strain-rate and tempera- ture dependence of the tensile properties was investigated for the evaluation of the deformation behavior under conditions of temperatures ranging from 473 to 823 K and strain rates ranging from 5 x 10-4 to 8 x 10-1 s -l The thermal stability was estimated by the change in ambient-temperature hardness, that was measured using a Vickers hardness tester, after the annealing at temper- atures ranging from 573 K to 823 K. X-ray diffraction study showed that the A192.5Ti2.5Fe2.5Cr2.5 RS P/M alloy predominantly con- tained fcc-Al and intermetallic compounds of A113Cr2 and A13Ti. In addition, some of the peaks could not be in- dexed to any known binary or ternary compound present in this system. Hence, the high tensile strength was obtained presumably due to both the refinement of grain size and the homogeneous dispersion of various phases. Moreover, the good ther- mal stability is presumably a result of low diffusion rates of Cr, Fe and Ti elements in aluminum.

Conclusions We have developed A192Ti3Fe3CrIM01, A192Ti3Fe3Cr2 and A192.5Ti2.5Fe2.5Cr2.5 RS P/M alloys that had high elevated-temperature strength, high thermal stability and good high-strain-rate workability. These RS p/ M alloys had ambient-temperature tensile strength ranging from 530 to 730 MPa and elongation ranging from 1 to 7%. The elevated-temperature strength of these RS P/ M alloys exceeded 300 MPa at 573 K. Especially, the A192.5Ti2.5Fe2.5Cr2.5 alloy exhibited about 320 MPa at 573 K. No change in the room-temperature hardness of these alloys was observed after the isothermal annealing for IOOh at 673K and for IOOOh at 623K. The A192.5Ti2.5Fe2.5Cr2.5 RS P/M alloy exhibited the elonga- tion of more than 100% at high strain-rates ranging from 10-1 to 100 s -1 at 823 K. Although the RS P/ M alloy didn't exhibit superplasticity, the deformability was enough to work the P/ M alloy into near-net shaped products industrially. The newly developed Al��Ti��Fe- (Cr, Mo) RS p/ M alloys are the promising candidate materials for elevated-temperature applications. Acknowledgement The author (Y.
