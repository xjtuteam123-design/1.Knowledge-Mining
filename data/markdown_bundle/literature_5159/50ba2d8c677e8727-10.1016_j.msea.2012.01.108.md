Phase transitions and compression properties of Ti 2AlC/TiAl composites
fabricated by combustion synthesis reaction

abstract
The phase transitions in the Ti–Al and the Ti–Al–C systems are investigated by the differential thermal
analysis (DTA) and X-ray diffraction (XRD). The results reveal that the addition of C not only leads to the
formation of the Ti
2 AlC phase but also lowers the formation temperatures of the TiAl and Ti 3 Al phases.
In the both systems, the combustion synthesis reactions are ignited at the melting point of Al, so the TiAl
alloy and the Ti
2 AlC/TiAl composites are successfully fabricated at a lower temperature (about 660 ◦C)
with an applied pressure. The yield strength, the ultimate compression strength and the work hardening
rate of the Ti
2AlC/TiAl composites are all higher than those of the TiAl alloy and increase with the increase
in the Ti 2AlC content. The average ultimate compression strength of the 6 vol.% Ti 2 AlC/TiAl composite is
179 MPa higher than that of the TiAl alloy without sacriﬁcing ductility.

1. Introduction
TiAl-based alloy ( /H9253-TiAl + /H92512-Ti3Al) is particularly attractive for
high temperature structural applications due to its low density,
high melting point, good creep characteristic and excellent oxi-
dation/corrosion resistance [1–4]. However, the low ductility and
fracture toughness at ambient temperature and the insufﬁcient
strength limit the practical use of the TiAl alloy [1,2,5]. The com-
posite technology is an effective approach to improve the strength
of the TiAl materials by introducing the stiff and hard parti-
cle reinforcements, such as TiB
2,A l 2O3,T i 5Si3. Nevertheless, the
strength improvement by addition of these particles is usually at
the cost of the material ductility [6–8].T i
2AlC ceramic combines
unusual properties of both metals and ceramics, that is, possesses
both ductility and stiffness [9–11]. Moreover, the density and the
thermal expansion coefﬁcient of the Ti
2AlC ceramic (4.11 g/cm 3
and 8.8 × 10−6 K−1) are close to those of the TiAl (3.8 g/cm 3 and
12 × 10−6 K−1) [10,11], which would largely avoid the phenomena
of segregation and reduce the inner stress between ceramic and
matrix during the fabrication of the TiAl matrix composites. There-
fore, the introduction of a small amount of Ti
2AlC particles into the
TiAl matrix to form Ti 2AlC/TiAl composite is expected to improve
the strength of the TiAl materials with no sacriﬁce in ductility.

The combustion synthesis reaction represents an in situ pro-
cessing technique for the fabrication of composites, which takes
the advantages of low energy requirement, cleaner particle–matrix
interface and high purity of the products [12,13]. In the present
study, the ignition temperatures in the Ti–Al and the Ti–Al–C sys-
tems are about at the melting point of Al, and the combustion
synthesis reactions would be completed immediately after the igni-
tion. If pressure could be applied just after the ignition of the
exothermic synthesis while the products are still at elevated tem-
peratures, the TiAl matrix composites could be synthesized and
densiﬁed simultaneously at the lower temperature (about 660
◦C)
compared with the reported works (>1000 ◦C) [1,14,15]. However,
the reactions taking place in such an in situ process are often com-
plicated and have not been well understood due to the Ti
2AlC
ceramic and TiAl matrix formed as a result of complex reactions in a
short time. Thus, we investigated the phase transitions in the Ti–Al
and the Ti–Al–C systems before the fabrication of the TiAl alloy and
the Ti
2AlC/TiAl composites. It is expected that the understanding of
the reaction behaviors in the two systems can permit a better con-
trol of the reaction process through the regulation of processing
parameters, and also provide a guide for producing the Ti
2AlC/TiAl
composites with tailored microstructures and properties.
Therefore, in this paper, the phase transitions in the Ti–Al
and the Ti–Al–C systems during combustion synthesis reaction
were investigated ﬁrstly by us, and then, the TiAl alloy and
the Ti
2AlC/TiAl composites were fabricated by the method of
the combustion synthesis reaction and hot press consolidation.

2AlC content on the compression

2. Experimental
The starting materials were made from the commercial pow-
ders of titanium (99.5% purity, ∼25 /H9262m), aluminum (99% purity,
∼74 /H9262m) and carbon black (99.9% purity). Elemental powder blends
corresponding to TiAl with 0, 2, 4 and 6 vol.% Ti 2AlC were mixed
sufﬁciently by ball milling for 8 h. The powder mixtures of the
TiAl and the 6 vol.% Ti
2AlC/TiAl were performed for DTA (Model
TA SDT-Q600, American) experiment in order to analyze the phase
transitions in the Ti–Al and the Ti–Al–C systems. A small amount
of the reactants weighing about 45 ± 5 mg were held in an alu-
mina crucible and heated to 1200
◦C at a heating rate of 30 ◦C/min,
and some interrupted runs were performed in order to obtain the
intermediate reaction products.
The combustion synthesis reaction and hot press consolidation
process was conducted in a self-made vacuum thermal explosion
furnace. The sufﬁcient mixed elemental powders corresponding to
TiAl with 0, 2, 4 and 6 vol.% Ti
2AlC were cold pressed into cylindrical
compacts using a stainless steel die. The powder compact, 28 mm
in diameter and approximately 36 mm in height, was contained in a
graphite mold. The mold was put into the furnace and then heated
at the heating rate of 30
◦C/min in a vacuum atmosphere. The tem-
perature in close to the center of the compact was measured by
Ni–Cr/Ni–Si thermocouples. When the temperature measured by
the thermocouples came to about the melting point of Al (660
◦C),
it then would suddenly rose rapidly, indicating that the sample was
ignited, and sample was quickly pressed just when it was still hot
and soft. The pressure (∼50 MPa) was maintained for 10 s and then
was cooled down to the ambient temperature.
The phase constituents of the DTA products and the bulk sam-
ples were examined by X-ray diffraction (XRD, Rigaku D/Max
2500PC, Japan) with Cu K/H9251radiation. The microstructure was stud-
ied using scanning electron microscopy (SEM, Evo18, Carl Zeiss,
Germany) and high-resolution transmission electron microscopy
(HRTEM, JEM-2100F, Japan). The cylindrical specimens with a
diameter of 3 mm and a height of 6 mm were used for compression
tests, and the loading surface was polished to parallel to the other
surface. The uniaxial compression tests were carried out under a
servo-hydraulic materials testing system (MTS, MTS 810, USA) with
a strain rate of 1 × 10
−4 s−1.
3. Results and discussion
3.1. Differential thermal analysis
Fig. 1 shows the DTA curves for the Ti–Al (Ti:Al = 1:1) and the
Ti–Al–C (6 vol.% Ti 2AlC/TiAl) powders. In the both systems, an
endothermic peak appears at temperatures close to 660 ◦C, cor-
responding to the melting of Al, and an exothermic peak follows
immediately at 714
◦C and 704 ◦C in the Ti–Al and the Ti–Al–C sys-
tems, respectively. In order to develop an understanding of the
phase transitions in the Ti–Al and the Ti–Al–C systems, experiments
quenched at about 640
◦C, 660 ◦C, 710 ◦C and 730 ◦C were carried
out in the TiAl mixtures, and at about 640 ◦C, 660 ◦C, 700 ◦C and
710 ◦C in the Ti–Al–C system, respectively. The products were sub-
sequently examined by XRD as shown in Fig. 2(a) and (b). A little of
Al3Ti phase formed in both systems at the temperature of 640 ◦C,
indicating that the solid reaction occurred between Ti and Al. When
the products heated to 660
◦C, a large quantity of Al3Ti phases were
detected in the both systems. It indicates that the presence of the
liquid aluminum phase promotes the formation of the Al
3Ti.

In the Ti–Al system, according to the XRD result of the products
quenched at 710 ◦C, the intensity of Al 3Ti increases progressively
while that of Al and Ti decreases. Clearly, the exothermic peak
resulted from the reaction between Al and Ti into the formation
of TiAl
3. The TiAl and Ti 3Al phases begin to appear at the products
quenched at 730 ◦C, indicating that the Al 3Ti further reacts with Ti
to form the TiAl and Ti3Al phases. Up to the temperature of 1200◦C,
the Al 3Ti absolutely transforms to the ultimate products TiAl and
Ti3Al phases. In the Ti–Al–C system, the endothermic phenomenon
of Al melting is neutralized by the dramatic heat release of the fol-
lowing exothermic reaction, and the slope of the exothermic peak
in the Ti–Al–C system is larger than that in the Ti–Al system. It indi-
cates that the exothermic reaction in the Ti–Al–C system is more
severe than that in the Ti–Al system, that is, the system releases
more heat and exhibits a larger exothermic rate due to the partici-
pation of C[16]. According to the XRD results shown inFig. 2(b), TiAl
and Ti
3Al phases as well as the ceramic phase Ti 2AlC are detected
in the products quenched at 700 ◦C and the content of the resid-
ual Al 3Ti phase is very low. Thus, the exothermic peaks may be
resulted from four reactions: (1) the formation reaction of Al 3Ti
from Al and Ti, (2) the formation reaction of TiAl and Ti 3Al from
Al3Ti and Ti, (3) the formation reaction of TiC from Al 3Ti and C [17],
(4) the formation reaction of Ti 2AlC from TiAl and TiC [18].I tc a n
be deduced that the liquid aluminum phase and the external heat
from the earlier exothermic reactions of Al
3Ti formation promote
the following reactions, leading to these reactions almost occurred
simultaneously. The products of the Ti–Al–C system quenched at
710
◦C mainly consist the ultimate phase TiAl, Ti3Al and Ti2AlC, and
the phase compositions of the Ti–Al–C system are not changed until
the temperature up to 1200
◦C. However, the intensity of Ti 2AlC
increases and which of Ti 3Al decreases from 710 ◦C to 1200 ◦C. It
indicates the increase of temperature improves the reactions to be
more complete in the Ti–Al–C system.

deformation mechanism of dislocation activity, that is, the strain

particles are all in the large block form and the content of them is

less than that of the ﬁne Ti

by the content of the Ti

levels indicates that the Ti 2AlC/TiAl composites possess a larger
capacity for dislocation storage compared with TiAl alloy. Thus,
the onset of destructive deformation of the Ti
2AlC/TiAl compos-
ites could be postponed, which also has the contribution to the
improvement of strength and the no sacriﬁce in the ductility of the
composites.
4. Conclusions
The addition of C not only results in the synthesis of the Ti 2AlC
phase but also lowers the formation temperatures of the TiAl and
Ti
3Al phase. The TiAl alloy and the Ti 2AlC/TiAl composites were
successfully fabricated at a lower temperature (about 660 ◦C) with
an applied pressure. The yield strength, the ultimate compression
strength and the work-hardening rate of the Ti
2AlC/TiAl composites
are all higher than those of the TiAl alloy and increase with the
increasing Ti
2AlC content. Moreover, the existence of the Ti 2AlC
particles resistances the work-softening effects of the TiAl alloy. The
average ultimate compression strength of the 6 vol.% Ti
2AlC/TiAl
composite is 179 MPa higher than that of the TiAl alloy without
sacriﬁcing ductility.
