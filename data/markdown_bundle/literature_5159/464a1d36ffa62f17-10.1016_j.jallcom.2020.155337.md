Abstract 
CoNi-base superalloys have recently attracted inten sive research interests due to their wider 
γ/γ′ domains and higher γ′ solvus temperatures compared to Ni-free Co-base su peralloys. In 
this work, two CoNi-base superalloys with Al conten t of 9at.% or 13at.% were designed with 
the aid of CALPHAD approach. The 9Al alloy exhibits a γ/γ′ dual-phase microstructure in its 
as-solidified and aged states, but the β-(Co,Ni)Al phase precipitates on the grain boundari es 
in the 13Al alloy. The measured T
γ′-solvus of the as-solidified 9Al and 13Al is 1238 °C and 
1220 °C, respectively, and the fraction of γ′ in the aged 9Al and 13Al is 64% and 80%, 
respectively. Tension tests at 900 °C reveal that t he Al content plays a vital role on 
high-temperature tensile properties of the current CoNi-base superalloys. The lower yield 
strength but a larger tensile strain of the aged 13 Al alloy, as compared with the aged 9Al 
alloy, are attributed to irregular shape of the sof ter γ′ precipitates and the easy formation of 
the local tiny cracks around the β particles. This implies that the existence of β particles can 
effectively relieve the grain-boundary brittleness of superalloys at high temperature. 
Keywords: High-temperature alloys; CoNi-base superalloys; γ/γ′ microstructure; 
High-temperature tension; β precipitates

1. Introduction 
Ni-base superalloys strengthened via the precipitation of the coherent γ′ particles in the γ 
matrix have been widely used in the aircraft engine s and power generating gas turbines [1-3]. 
A higher working temperature is always required to improve the engine efficiency, and even a 
10 °C increase in the working temperature of aircra ft engines could be regarded as a 
significant improvement [3,4]. Since the melting po int (1495 °C) of Co is 40 °C higher than 
that (1455 °C) of Ni, Co-base superalloys are consi dered as potential high-temperature alloys 
[3-6]. However, Co-base superalloys were commonly s trengthened by the precipitation of 
carbides and the solid solution strengthening [7,8] , which exhibit poor strengthening effects 
at high temperatures as compared with the Ni-base s uperalloys with the γ/γ′ microstructure. 
This hindered the exploitation and investigation of Co-base superalloys [3-6]. 
Since the discovery of the L1 2 γ′-strengthened Co-base superalloys in the Co-Al-X 
alloys [9], Co-base superalloys containing various L1 2 γ′-Co 3(Al, X) (where X can be Mo, Ta, 
Nb and Ti [5,10-17]) precipitates have been develop ed. However, the γ′-Co 3(Al, X) phases 
were revealed to possess low phase stability at hig h temperatures and tend to decompose into 
several equilibrium phases [18-20]. Furthermore, th e γ/γ′ dual-phase region of Co-base 
superalloys were revealed to be quite narrow in the composition range as compared with the 
Ni-base superalloys [5,13,21]. Therefore, Ni was added into Co-base superalloys to widen the 
γ/γ′ dual-phase region and enhance the phase stability of γ′-Co 3(Al,W) at high temperatures 
[22-28]. Currently, the CoNi-base superalloys attra ct intensive research interests, and many 
CoNi-base superalloys with the γ/γ′ dual-phase microstructure have been developed 
[25,26,29-40]. It has been shown that the addition of Al in CoNi-base superalloys can

In this work, we designed two CoNi-base superalloys of the γ/γ′ dual-phase

CoNi-base superalloys [18,29,40,43-55]. The effects of high Al content on the 
microstructures and tensile properties at 900 °C of the two CoNi-base superalloys were 
investigated. It is found that the Al content plays an important role on the fraction, solvus 
temperature, and morphology of the γ′ precipitates. The microstructure, and high-temperature 
tensile properties of the CoNi-base superalloys are also significantly altered.

CoNi-base superalloys with compositions listed in T able 1. The used ThermoCalc software 
with a TTNi8 database is capable to calculate the e volution of the equilibrium phases in the 
current Co-Ni-Al-Cr-Ti-W-Ta (-Mo) system. The ingot s of the two alloys were prepared by 
arc melting the mixture of pure metals in a high-purity argon atmosphere, and the ingots were 
melted four times for a better chemical homogenizat ion. And then the alloy ingots were

4. Discussion 
4.1. Effects of Al content on microstructures 
It is known that Al addition in Co-base or CoNi-base superalloys promotes the formation 
of L1 2 γ′ phase, and also stabilizes the metastable L1 2 γ′-Co 3(Al,W) phase at elevated 
temperatures [22,41,42]. As the Al amount increases from 9at.% to 13at.% in the current 
CoNi-base superalloys, the fraction of γ′ phase increases from ~60% to ~80%, respectively, 
as confirmed by the calculated phase diagram and th e experimental results. The calculated 
Tγ′-solvus (1215 °C) of 9Al is slightly lower than that (1225 °C) of the 13Al superalloys, see Fig. 
1. However, this is contradict to the experimental results. The measured Tγ′-solvus of 9Al and 
13Al is 1238 °C and 1220 °C, respectively. The chan ge in the Al content alters the partition 
coefficients of elements, as seen in Fig. 6. The de creased partition coefficients of the 
elements with high melting points, such as W and Ta , account for the decreased Tγ′-solvus from 
the as-solidified 9Al to the as-solidifed 13Al. 
Moreover, with the Al content increasing from 9at.% to 13at.%, the morphology of γ′ 
precipitates is also altered. In the aged 9Al super alloy, the γ′ precipitates are of cuboidal

## Page 14

11 
shape, but in the aged 13Al superalloy, the γ′ precipitates with much larger size are in 
irregular shape instead of perfect cuboidal shape a nd the γ channels are zigzag rather than 
straight as the gamma channels in 9Al, see Figs. 4( c) and (e) and Figs. 5(a) and (b). The 
difference in microstructures can probably be attri buted to the fact that the increase in Al 
content changes the partition coefficients of eleme nts between γ and γ′, which affects the 
precipitation kinetics of the γ′ phase, such as the diffusion rates of element and the interfacial 
energy [22,41,42]. 
It is noteworthy that the current ThermoCalc softwa re with a TTNi8 database is also 
incapable to predict the formation of β-(Co,Ni)Al phase in the 13Al superalloy, which exis ts 
in both of its as-solidified and its aged samples w ith a fraction of ~10%. In a fact, Al also 
promotes the formation of β-(Co,Ni)Al phase [43,44,56,57]. Generally, as the A l amount is 
less than 10at.%, a dual-phase γ/γ′ microstructure can be obtained in the Co-base or 
CoNi-base superalloys [18,29,40,43-54]. Since the i nvestigated Co-base or CoNi-base 
superalloys generally contain an Al content less th an 10at.% and possess a dual-phase γ/γ′ 
microstructure, the influence of high Al content an d the existence of the β phase on 
high-temperature has not been well investigated. Ho wever, the currently tensile tests at 
900 °C clearly reveal that a high Al content with t he existence of the β phase can 
significantly improve the high-temperature ductilit y of the superalloy, and the underlying 
mechanisms are discussed below.

current CoNi-base superalloys, the high-temperature tension-fractured samples were

observed in SEM, as shown in Fig. 8. Fig. 8(a) show s the side view of the fractured 9Al 
sample at a low magnification. It shows a jagged pa th of the facture surface, which has an 
average angel of ~45 ° to the loading direction. Also a long crack parall el to the loading 
direction can been seen, suggesting that the sample split during the last stage of tensile 
deformation. Under a higher magnification (Fig. 8(b )), it is clear that there are several big

reasonable to conclude that the formation of cracks parallel to the loading direction causes the 
facture of the aged 9Al samples. At high-temperatur e, the grain boundaries and the interfaces 
between γ/γ′ phases possess a lower strength as compared to tha t of the inner grains. The γ 
channels in the aged 9Al are straight, as seen in F ig. 4(c) and Fig. 5(a), suggesting straight 
γ/γ′ interfaces. The formation of the cracks along the grain boundaries and the straight 
interfaces between γ/γ′ phases parallel to the loading direction results i n the early fracture of 
the aged 9Al, which is lack of large plasticity. 
In comparison, Fig. 8(d) shows the side view of the fractured 13Al sample at a low 
magnification. It also shows a zigzag path of the f acture surface, but is almost perpendicular 
to the loading direction. No long cracks can be see n, and only many local tiny cracks 
homogenously distributed throughout the sample. Und er a higher magnification (Fig. 8(e)), it 
is clear that there are many local tiny cracks form around the β particles, which propagate 
alone the grain boundaries. These cracks are perpen dicular to the loading direction, 
suggesting they were formed under an I-type opening force instead of a splitting force. The

13 
local tiny cracks are homogenously distributed, and they do not percolate to form a long 
crack. The fractured surface of the aged 13Al alloy (Fig. 8(f) comprises not only smooth 
facets but also many rough regions, suggesting a mi xed cleavage alone the grain/phase 
boundaries as well as penetrating the grains. 
The intermetallic β phase with a B2 structure generally possesses a wo rse plastic 
deformability as compared with the γ/γ′ phase [58,59]. Therefore, some tiny cracks form 
around the β particles under the opening force, which locally p ropagate along the grain 
boundaries. Since the grain boundaries are just loc ally perpendicular to the loading direction, 
the formed tiny cracks are then restrained in a sho rt length of ~100-200 µm. In the current 
13Al superalloy, although the fraction of γ′ phase is much higher than that in 9Al, the easy 
formation of local tiny cracks around the β particles results in a relatively lower yield 
strength. Therefore, the increase of Al from 9at.% to 13at.% promotes the formation of the β 
phase on the grain boundaries, which causes an decr ease in yield strength but an 
improvement in ductility. As a fact, the severe gra in-boundary brittleness has been widely 
reported in the dual-phase γ/γ′ microstructure superalloys with a high volume frac tion of γ′ 
phase [60,61]. Therefore, the current microstructure of the aged 13Al with β-(Co,Ni)Al phase 
precipitated on the grain boundaries might be a goo d solution to relief the grain-boundary 
brittleness in superalloys. Additionally, the amoun t of precipitation strengthening elements, 
including Ti, W, and Ta in the γ′ phase in the aged 13Al is much less than that in t he aged 
9Al, see the lower partition coefficients in Fig. 6 . Therefore, the γ′ phase in the aged 13Al 
possess a lower hardness as compared with that in t he aged 9Al, which also accounts for the 
lower yield strength of the 13Al. The irregular sha pe of the γ′ precipitates and the zigzag γ

channels in the aged 13Al also prevent the formatio n of long crack along the straight γ/γ′ 
interfaces, as what happens in the aged 9Al superal loy. This is beneficial for the large

5. Conclusions 
In summary, two new CoNi-base superalloys with Al c ontent of 9at.% or 13at.% were 
designed in the Co-Ni-Al-Cr-Ti-W-Ta (-Mo) system wi th the aid of CALPHAD approach, 
and both alloys were predicted to possess a dual-ph ase γ/γ′ microstructure. The 9Al alloy 
exhibits a γ/γ′ dual-phase microstructure in its as-solidified as well as the aged state. However, 
β-(Co,Ni)Al phase precipitates on the grain boundari es in the as-solidified and the aged 13Al 
superalloy. The β particles are enveloped by the γ′ phase, and new γ′ phase precipitates inside 
the β phase during the heat treatments. The measured T
γ′-solvus of the 9Al and 13Al superalloys 
is 1238 °C and 1220 °C, respectively. The volume fr action of γ′ in the aged 9Al and 13Al is 
64% and 80%, respectively, and the volume fraction of β phase in the aged 13Al is ~10%. As 
compared with the yield strength of 559 MPa and a t ensile strain of 2.0% of the aged 9Al 
superalloy at 900 °C, the aged 13Al superalloy exhi bits a lower average yield strength of 496 
MPa and a much larger tensile strain of 6.3%. This can be attributed to the easy formation of 
the local tiny cracks around the β particles, the irregular shape of the softer γ′ precipitates and 
the zigzag γ′ channels in the 13Al superalloy. Therefore, the ex istence of β particles and the 
zigzag γ channels can effectively relieve the grain-boundary brittleness of superalloys.

Highlights 
1. Two new CoNi-base superalloys with Al content of 9at.% or 13at.% were designed. 
2. The 9Al superalloy possesses a dual-phase γ/γ′ microstructure with a high Tγ′-solvus of 
1238 °C. 
3. Besides γ/γ′ phases, β-(Co,Ni)Al phase forms on the grain boundaries in t he 13Al 
superalloy. 
4. The easy formation of tiny cracks around β particles accounts for the large plasticity of 
13Al. 
5. The existence of β particles can relieve the grain-boundary brittleness of superalloys.
