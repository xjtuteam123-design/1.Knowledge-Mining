In nickel-based superalloys with high volume fraction of /C130 precipitates,
dislocations have to experience high curvatures in order to enter narrow
channels by glide in the {111} planes of the fcc /C13 matrix. Observations of in situ
dynamic sequences performed in a transmission electron microscope on several
industrial superalloys have shown the occurrence of decorrelated movements of
Shockley partial dislocations, originating from perfect dislocation dissociation.
By evaluating the eﬀective stress acting on each one of these partial dislocations,
as well as their respective ﬂexibility, the possible occurrence of such movements
for some particular dislocation characters and channel widths is accounted for.
These movements can play an important role in the creep behaviour of these
materials in the low deformation rate regime.
1. Introduction
For several years, the basic plastic deformation mechanisms operating at elevated
temperature in nickel-based superalloys strengthened by /C130-Ni3Al type precipitates
have been studied through the use of in situ straining experiments in a transmission
electron microscope (TEM). With this technique, the only one which allows direct
observation of the evolution of the dislocation microstructure under stress and
temperature, we have already identiﬁed such mechanisms in single-phased /C130-based
monocrystals [1–3] as well as in single-phased /C13 monocrystals simulating the matrix
of various industrial single crystal superalloys [4].
The core structure of the a/2h110i dislocations propagating in the /C13 matrix of
superalloys has been described by a number of authors [5–9]. Saint-Antonin [10] has
shown that in a polycrystalline /C13//C13
0 superalloy (Astroloy), containing a volume
fraction of about 45% of precipitates with sizes within the range 20–100 nm, the
dissociation of single matrix dislocations which occurs in the /C13 phase at 650 /C14 C can

2. Materials and experimental technique
The chemical compositions of the MC2 and NR3 superalloys are given in table 1.
The MC2 nickel-based superalloy is used for single crystal turbine blade applications

The powder metallurgy NR3 superalloy was designed for compressor and

conducted at 25 /C14 C on NR3 specimens previously creep-strained at 700 /C14 C.

4. Discussion
The two types of observations carried out either post mortem or in situ on NR3 and
MC2, respectively, share a common point: they correspond to decreasing transient
stress states associated with relaxation. These dislocation conﬁgurations have seldom
been investigated in depth in the past. Moreover, to our knowledge, these move-
ments have never been reported in superalloys with a high volume fraction of /C13
0
phase. As a result, no explanation can be found until now for this mechanism, in
the literature.
Here, an analysis will therefore be put forward based on the following idea: for
a dislocation to propagate in a matrix channel, the eﬀective resulting force on the
dislocation segment must be in excess of the threshold force characterizing the
local geometry of the obstacle and generally called the ‘‘Orowan stress’’ in previous
models. Decorrelation of the partial dislocation movement occurs when the eﬀec-
tive force acting on the perfect dislocation does not allow it to propagate as a whole,
in the matrix channel, while this propagation is allowed for the leading partial
dislocation alone.
To quantify this, it is necessary to evaluate the eﬀective force acting on the
perfect dislocation and on each one of the partial dislocations. Then, for each dis-
location, the threshold force taking into account the ﬂexibility of each one of these
dislocations will be calculated.

More generally, a large panel of mechanisms is possible. When the stress is

geometry of the obstacle they are facing.
