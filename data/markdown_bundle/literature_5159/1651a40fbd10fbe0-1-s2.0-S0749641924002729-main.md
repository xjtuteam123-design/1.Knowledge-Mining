In precipitation strengthened alloys, apart from γ’ precipitate volume fraction, size, shape, and distribution, the chemical 
composition of the precipitate and the matrix will also dictate the deformation mechanisms ( Eggeler et al., 2021 ; Feng et al., 2018 ). 
The role of alloying chemistry was shown to have a pronounced effect on the dominant deformation mechanism of single-phase solid 
solution (SSSS) high entropy alloys at room temperature ( Laplanche et al., 2017 , 2016 ; Wei and Tasan, 2020 ). Laplanche et al. 
concluded that reducing stacking fault energy by manipulating the alloying chemistry could result in better performance of CrCoNi 
compared to CrMnFeCoNi ( Laplanche et al., 2017 ). Wei et al. experimentally demonstrated that reducing stacking fault energy (SFE) 
leads to faulting as the dominant deformation mechanism in the CoNiCrW alloy ( Wei and Tasan, 2020 ). Moreover, recent theoretical 
efforts have highlighted the important role of SFE of γ and γ’ in dictating the technologically crucial creep properties in multicom -
ponent Ni-based superalloys ( Breidi et al., 2018 ; Galindo-Nava et al., 2023 ). Breidi et al. have shown the significant role of solute 
elements in governing the SISF (superlattice intrinsic stacking fault) energy of γ’ and the precipitate shearing mechanism ( Breidi et al., 
2018 ). Additionally, the effect of lattice site occupancy in γ’ precipitates on the broader landscape of planar fault energies and its 
subsequent impact on deformation mechanisms has not been experimentally studied. In this context, it is of interest to study the effect 
of lattice site occupancy on the deformation mechanism in PSHEAs and the consequent effect on temperature-dependent mechanical 
properties.

2. Alloy design
This work employs the first principles density functional theory (DFT) approach as implemented in the Vienna ab-initio simulation 
package ( Fuchs and Scheffler, 1999 ; Kresse and Furthmüller, 1996 ). The electron-ion interactions were approximated by all-electron

Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) generalized gradient approximation (GGA) ( Perdew et al., 1996 ). Vienna ab initio Simulation Package 
(VASP) employs a plane wave basis set for all calculations, where the energy cutoff was set to 450 eV. The optimization of structures 
was performed using the conjugate gradient scheme with a convergence criterion of < 10
– 3

– 6

3. Materials and methods
3.1. Alloy preparation and heat-treatment
The designed alloys were prepared using constituent elements of 4 N (99.99 at %) purity. A 100 g batch of each alloy was produced 
using a laboratory-scale vacuum arc melting unit filled with argon and equipped with a water cooled copper hearth and a non- 
consumable tungsten electrode. The alloy was melted 6 to 8 times with flipping after each cycle to avoid macro-segregation of ele -
ments. The melted samples were subsequently cast into rods ( ϕ × l - 3 mm × 80 mm) and slabs ( l × w × t - 80 mm × 10 mm × 6 mm) 
using a vacuum suction casting unit attached with a water-cooled split copper mould. The cast rods were solutionized at 1175 
◦
C for 20 
h and aged at 900 
◦
C for 20 h followed by water quenching.
3.2. Electron microscopy (SEM and TEM) and synchrotron measurements
Microstructural characterization of solutionized and aged samples was carried out in a field emission gun equipped dual-beam 
SEM/focused-ion-beam instrument (FIB, FEI, Helios G4-UX) operated at 30 kV using secondary and backscattered electron de -
tectors. Bulk composition determination and elemental mapping of the cast and solutionized alloys were performed in an electron 
probe micro analyzer (EMPA, JEOL, JXA-8530F) with both energy and wavelength dispersive detectors (EDS and WDS). Controlled 
electron channelling contrast imaging (cECCI) was performed using an SEM (TESCAN MIRA 3) equipped with a solid-state BSE de -
tector and an EDAX Hikari EBSD camera, operating at a voltage of 20 kV and a working distance of 9 mm. The initial orientation of 
grains was obtained by electron backscattered diffraction (EBSD). Grains oriented close to [001] and [101] were chosen for the cECCI 
analysis, and orientation for two-beam conditions was obtained by using the TSL OIM software coupled with single-crystal software. 
SEM samples were prepared by mechanically grinding using SiC grit paper down to 10 μ m following polishing using diamond sus -
pension with particle size down to 1 μ m. Final polishing was achieved with colloidal silica with a particle size of 40 nm.
Diffraction, phase contrast, high-resolution imaging were carried out using a field emission gun source equipped transmission 
electron microscope (TEM, FEI, TITAN Themis) operated at 300 kV. Weak beam dark-field imaging on deformed samples was carried 
out using the T20 microscope (FEI, Tecnai TEM) opeated at 200 kV. TEM samples were prepared by first mechanically grinding the 3 
mm disc of 500 μ m thickness to 100 μ m. Final electron transparent foil was obtained by electropolishing (Fischione, TWIN jet) using an 
electrolyte of 95 vol% methanol and 5 vol% perchloric acid at a temperature and voltage of 40 
◦
C and 15 V, respectively.

3.3. Atom probe tomography (APT)
Atomic-scale compositional measurements were performed using atom probe tomography (APT, LEAP ™ 5000XS, Cameca In -

5. Discussion
The above results showcase the effect of alloying chemistry (varying Fe) on designing of γ ʹ strengthened high entropy alloys with 
improved mechanical properties. Despite the similar grain size, precipitate size, shape, volume fraction and γ / γ ʹ lattice misfit in both 
5Fe and 10Fe alloys, the mechanical properties at ambient and high temperatures are different. The different deformation sub- 
structures and the operative precipitate shearing mechanisms at different temperature levels are responsible for such observations. 
In the subsequent section, these findings will be discussed in detail.
5.1. Mechanical properties and deformation mechanisms
The UTS and elongation of currently designed alloys are compared with different structural alloys in the Ashby plot in Fig. 9 a. 5Fe 
alloy shows a better strength-ductility combination than those of engineering alloys. The UTS value for 5Fe is higher than reported 
high entropy alloys (including dual-phase and ultra-fine grain high entropy alloys) and comparable to precipitation-hardened (pH) 
superalloys with improved ductility.
It was shown that the change in alloying chemistry resulted in a simultaneous improvement in both strength (YS and UTS) and 
ductility at RT in the 5Fe alloy compared to the 10Fe alloy. The microscopic characterization of deformed microstructure revealed a 
different underlying micro-mechanism that rendered a difference in macroscopic behaviour at RT. The schematic of deformation

Second factor in the present study can be discounted as both alloys show a similar lattice misfit value. The third factor comes from 
the change in modulus value of γ ʹ due to alloying addition ( Wang and Wang, 2009 ). If the modulus values were to be loosely correlated 
with hardness, then a significant change in hardness value from 460 HV for the aged 5Fe alloy to 420 HV for the aged 10Fe alloy would 
highlight the effect of alloying chemistry. It is, however, also to be noted that the HV values cannot unambiguously paint the real 
picture in the current landscape, but it gives a trend on the effect of change in alloying chemistry.
Apart from precipitation strengthening, the role of solid solution strengthening of matrix cannot be discounted in rationalizing the 
difference in strength at RT of studied alloys. The matrix in both alloys is rich with Co, Ni, Cr and Fe with small content ( < 5 at %) of Al, 
Ti and Nb. However, the Fe content of 16.44 at % in the 10Fe alloy is higher than 8.74 at % in the 5Fe alloy. The recent report by 
Laplanche et al. concluded a significant effect of Fe content on the SF energy in CoNiCr and CoNiCrFeMn systems ( Laplanche et al., 
2017 ). The increase in SF energy with Fe and Mn addition has resulted in the higher resolved shear stress for twin formation, which 
resulted in a reduction in yield strength from 360 MPa in the ternary CoNiCr system to 265 MPa in the quinary CoNiCrFeMn system 
with similar grain size. In the present case, the higher Fe content in the 10Fe alloy would have similar effect on the strength contri -
bution from matrix. However, the detailed microstructural studies using TEM ruled out the presence of deformation twins in these 
systems.

6. Conclusions
In summary, the present work elucidated a pronounced effect of alloying chemistry on the macroscopic mechanical response of two 
newly designed alloys at various temperatures without significantly affecting microstructural constituents. Based on our results, the 
major conclusions of the present work can be laid out through following points: 
1. The combined density functional theory and thermodynamic calculations led to the design of two γ ʹ containing CoNiCrFe based 
high entropy alloy with varying Fe content (5Fe and 10Fe), where γ ʹ precipitates were stabilized by the addition of Al, Ti and Nb.
2. The designed alloys show low mass density of 8.01 and 7.95 g/cc for the 5Fe and 10Fe alloys. While increasing the Fe content 
significantly decreased the γ ʹ solvus temperature by 51 
◦
C from 1081 
◦
C for 5Fe to 1030 for 10Fe, it does not affect the γ ʹ precipitate 
size, shape, and lattice misfit.
3. The alloying chemistry has a pronounced effect on the tensile strength-ductility trade off at room temperature. 5Fe alloy shows a 
better combination of YS, UTS and ductility of 970 ± 25 MPa, 1535 ± 30 MPa and ~ 18 (%), respectively, in comparison to 850 ±
20 MPa, 1300 ± 35 MPa and ~ 15 (%), respectively, for the 10Fe alloy.
4. The microstructural characterization using cECCI and weak beam dark-field TEM imaging revealed that the 5Fe alloy primarily 
exhibited stacking fault activity within the γ matrix, with occasional shearing of precipitates. In contrast, the 10Fe alloy exhibited 
frequent shearing through faulting of precipitates. The DFT calculations revealed that the reduction in SISF energy with Fe atoms 
occupying the Ni sub-lattice sites rendered the easeier faulting of the precipitates in the 10Fe alloy.
5. Both 5Fe and 10Fe alloys exhibited a YS anomaly with temperature, with peak YS values of 1120 ± 30 MPa and 890 ± 25 MPa, 
respectively, at 670 
◦
C. Detailed microstructural characterization revealed precipitate shearing with SF and antiphase boundary 
formation in the 5Fe alloy, while precipitate shearing with only SF formation in the 10Fe alloy. Furthermore, the presence of sessile 
stair-rod configurations and dislocation segments on non-gliding (001) planes within the γ ʹ precipitate contributed to the high yield 
strength observed in the 5Fe alloy at 670 
◦
C. DFT calculations predicted that the interplay of superlattice intrinsic stacking fault, 
complex intrinsic stacking fault, and antiphase boundary energies favoured thermal-driven cross-slip of partial dislocations from 
the octahedral (111) plane to the (001) plane when Fe atoms occupied the Al sub-lattice sites (5Fe alloy).
