1. Introduction
TiAl alloys are promising light-weight high-temperature structural 
materials due to their high specific strength, low density, and good creep 
and oxidation resistance [ 1 , 2 ]. Using TiAl alloys to substitute Ni-based 
superalloys can effectively reduce the weight of aircraft engines, 
which is critical to the improvement of the engine performance [ 3 ]. It is 
also known that the manufacturing process of TiAl alloys is complex and 
of high cost due to their inherent brittleness and limited deformability at 
room temperature [ 2 ]. Therefore, developing high-performance joining 
techniques is very important for promoting the applications of TiAl al -
loys. For joining TiAl alloys, fusion welding (such as arc welding, laser 
welding, and electron beam welding) and solid-state welding (such as 
brazing, diffusion welding, self-propagating high temperature synthesis, 
and friction welding) have been reported [ 4 ]. Possessing the advantages 
of low heating temperature, low residual stress, and high joining accu -
racy, brazing is considered to be an appropriate method for the 
high-performance joining of TiAl alloys and has received special 
attentions.
At present, the brazing of TiAl alloys with Ag- [ 5 , 6 ], Al- [ 7 ], Ni- [ 8 ,
9 ], and Ti-based [ 10 – 16 ] brazing filler metals (BFMs) have been re -
ported. Compared with the TiAl alloy joints brazed with the Ag- and 
Al-based BFMs, the joints brazed with the Ti-based and Ni-based BFMs 
generally exhibited good mechanical properties at room and elevated

Moreover, the good chemical and physical compatibility between the 
Ti-based BFMs and the TiAl base metals could benefit the formation of 
sound joints [ 10 , 13 , 15 ]. By using the reported Ti-based BFMs, the 
resultant TiAl alloy joints exhibited high shear strength up to 333 MPa 
[ 10 , 12 , 15 , 17 – 23 ]. Therefore, Ti-based BFMs are considered to be an 
ideal choice for brazing TiAl alloys and have been most widely used. On 
the other hand, amorphous alloys have attracted great attention due to 
their unique structure and properties, and being used as novel BFMs is 
one of their practical applications [ 24 – 26 ]. By using the method of 
melt-spinning, alloys with good glass-forming ability (GFA) can be easily 
fabricated into continuous and flexible ribbons, which are advantageous 
to be used as BFMs in the aspects of net-shape preparation and conve -
nient assembly of preplaced preforms of the joints. Amorphous BFMs 
can also favor the properties of the joints due to their homogeneity in 
microstructure and composition, narrow melting temperature interval, 
and good wettability [ 12 , 24 – 26 ]. For improving the mechanical prop -
erties of the TiAl alloy brazed joints, it is of great significance to develop 
novel Ti-based amorphous BFMs.
Up to now, many Ti-based amorphous BFMs, such as Ti-Zr-Cu-Ni [ 11 ,
13 ], Ti-Zr-Cu-Ni-Co-Mo [ 10 , 12 ], and Ti-Zr-Ni-Nb [ 14 , 16 ], have been 
developed for brazing TiAl alloy. In these BFMs, Cu and Ni are the wildly 
used alloying elements for lowering the liquidus temperatures as well as 
improving the GFA to enable the preparation of flexible ribbons by 
melt-spinning. However, Cu and Ni with relatively high contents in the 
Ti-based amorphous BFMs would react with Ti or Zr to form brittle 
intermetallic compounds in brazing, deteriorating the mechanical 
properties of the brazed joints [ 10 – 13 , 15 ]. By applying novel BFMs with 
tailored compositions and optimizing brazing parameters, the formation 
of brittle intermetallic compounds in the TiAl joints could be suppressed, 
leading to improved mechanical properties [ 11 , 18 , 23 ]. However, a 
systematic investigation on how the BFM composition, particularly the 
addition of specific alloying elements, influences the microstructural 
evolution and mechanical performance of the brazed TiAl joints is still 
lacking. It is noticed that Nb is an important alloying element in TiAl 
alloys due to its effects of stabilizing the BCC/B2 phase and leading to 
the formation of the O-Ti
2
AlNb phase, which is beneficial to the me -
chanical properties of the alloys [ 27 , 28,29 ]. Therefore, it is proposed 
that developing novel Ti-Zr-Cu-Ni-Nb amorphous BFMs by replacing 
partial Cu and Ni in Ti-Zr-Cu-Ni amorphous BFMs with Nb would be an 
effective way to suppress the formation of brittle (Ti, Zr)-(Cu, Ni) 
intermetallic compounds and improve the mechanical properties of the 
TiAl alloy joints. Moreover, as the addition of the element Nb would 
influence the GFA and melting behaviors of the Ti-Zr-Cu-Ni(-Nb) alloys, 
the glass formation and thermal properties of the alloys should also be 
investigated.
In the present work, a series of amorphous/nanocrystalline Ti-Zr-Cu- 
Ni(-Nb) alloys, which can be prepared into flexible ribbons by melt- 
spinning, were developed. The alloy ribbons were then used as the 
BFMs for brazing the TiAl alloy, and the resultant joints exhibited sound 
metallurgical bonding and high strength. The GFA of the Ti-Zr-Cu-Ni 
(-Nb) alloys, and the microstructure and mechanical properties of the 
TiAl alloy brazed joints were studied.

The Ti-Zr-Cu-Ni(-Nb) BFM ribbon with the thickness of 40 μ m was 
preplaced between two TiAl alloy plates to form a sandwiched structure. 
The brazing temperature was set at 1223 K for the protection of intrinsic

a vacuum brazing furnace with a vacuum below 5 × 10

joints were cooled down to room temperature in the furnace.
The microstructure and elementary distribution of the cross sections 
of the brazed joints were investigated using an electron probe micro -
analyzer (EPMA) equipped with an energy dispersive spectrometer 
(EDS). The phases in the joints were determined by XRD and a trans -
mission electron microscope (TEM). By using the software ImageJ, the 
fractions of the phases in the TiAl alloy joints were estimated based on 
the backscattered electron images. The Young ’ s modulus (E) and 
nanoindentation hardness (H) of the phases in the joints were calculated 
from the nanoindentation tests by using a nanoindenter with a standard 
Berkovich tip. Compressive shear tests of the brazed joints were per -
formed on a universal testing machine at a compressive speed of 0.5 
mm/min. The fracture of the joints after compressive shear tests were 
examined with a scanning electron microscope (SEM) and EPMA, 
respectively.

decrease in Cu and Ni contents. Nevertheless, the crystalline peaks on 
the XRD patterns of the amorphous/nanocrystalline ribbons are inap -

sisting of the amorphous matrix and low amounts of nanocrystals.

tent, the ribbons with amorphous phase serving as the matrix are also 
continuous and flexible with a smooth surface ( Fig. 3 a), which is 
convenient for the assembly of preplaced preforms of the joints.

4. Conclusions
Amorphous/nanocrystalline Ti-Zr-Cu-Ni(-Nb) alloy ribbons were 
synthesized and used as novel BFMs for brazing TiAl alloy. The effect of 
Nb addition in Ti-Zr-Cu-Ni(-Nb) BFMs on the microstructure and me -
chanical properties of the joints were investigated and discussed. The 
main conclusions are as the followings: 
(1) Ti-Zr-Cu-Ni(-Nb) amorphous/nanocrystalline BFM ribbons were 
prepared by melt-spinning. With the increase of Nb content, the 
glass-forming ability of the alloys decreased due to the positively 
mixing heats of the atomic pairs Nb-Ti, Nb-Zr, and Nb-Cu. The 
critical ribbon thicknesses for achieving a fully amorphous

and below 20 μ m, respectively, while continuous and flexible 
BFM ribbons with thicknesses up to 40 μ m could still be obtained 
for the latter two compositions since the amorphous phase serves 
as the matrix of the ribbons.
(2) Sound TiAl alloy joints were successfully obtained by using the 
novel Ti-Zr-Cu-Ni(-Nb) amorphous/nanocrystalline BFMs. The 
braze zones of the joints exhibited multi-layer microstructure, 
mainly consisting of α 2-Ti

center. It is demonstrated that the addition of Nb to the Ti-based 
BFMs is an effective approach to tailoring the microstructure and 
enhancing the strength of TiAl brazed joints.
