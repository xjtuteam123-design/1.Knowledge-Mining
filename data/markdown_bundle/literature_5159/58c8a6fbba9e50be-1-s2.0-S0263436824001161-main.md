1. Introduction 
With the advancement of aerospace technology, ultra-high- 
temperature hot-end structural components have placed increasingly 
demanding expectations on the high-temperature strength of materials. 
However, current high-temperature-resistant materials are insufficient 
to satisfy these requirements [ 1 ]. For instance, in the case of a supersonic 
vehicle moving at high velocities within an atmospheric setting, there is 
a significant rise in temperature observed at the leading edge of the nose 
cone. When the velocity reaches Mach 8, the temperature of the vehi -
cle ’ s nose cone and leading edge of the wing can soar up to 2000 
◦
C [ 2 ]. 
However, high-temperature alloys based on nickel and cobalt will un -
dergo melting and subsequent failure if the temperature surpasses 
1200 
◦
C [ 3 , 4 ]. For traditional refractory alloys such as tungsten alloy 
and molybdenum alloy, the strength of most alloys is < 400 MPa after 
the temperature exceeds 1400 
◦
C, and the strength at 2000 
◦
C is 
generally lower than 100 MPa [ 5 ]. Current high-temperature materials 
cannot meet the material performance requirements of such ultrahigh- 
temperature components. Consequently, it is necessary to further 
improve the performance of ultra-high temperature materials, so that 
they can maintain high strength at temperatures above 1400 
◦
C and 
meet the harsh requirements of extreme environments. 
Refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) have drawn considerable 
attention and research due to their excellent ultra-high-temperature 
mechanical properties. For example, The NbMoTaW and NbMoTaWV 
RHEAs showed high yield strengths of 405 and 407 MPa at 1600 
◦
C,

temperature refractory alloy materials. In order to facilitate the com -

reported high-temperature resistant alloys, this study mainly introduces

2.2. Characterization of microstructure and mechanical property

ZEISS Corporation, Germany; SEM5000, CIQTEK, China) equipped with 
an energy dispersive spectrometer (EDS). 
The XRD specimens were roughly and finely ground with a series of 
sandpaper foils, and electrolytic polishing was then performed on the 
surface of the specimens to remove the surface stress and strain layers. 
The specimens for SEM observation were prepared by standard me -
chanical grinding and mechanical polishing procedures firstly. At the 
final step, specimens were etched in an acid solution (43% hydrochloric 
acid + 14% nitric acid + 43% hydrofluoric) for 30s at room-temperature. 
The specimens for compressive tests were cut into cylinders with Φ 5 
× 7.5 mm. The room-temperature compression tests were conducted 
using a testing machine (GNT1000Y, NCS Testing Technology Co., Ltd., 
China) with a constant loading speed of 0.45 mm/min. The compressive
