abstract
Microstructure and tensile properties of a high strength metastable beta titanium alloy, Ti –B20–0.1B
(wt.%), were studied. The alloy was double melted by vacuum arc melting and subsequently subjected
to forging and heat treatment in the
a + b phase region. The microstructural characteristics and
microstructure–property relationships were investigated by solution treatment at 780 /C176C/740 /C176C and
subsequent aging from 450 /C176C to 600 /C176C. The result showed that the strength of forged alloy was
improved by trace boron. The strength of the alloy, which was processed by solution treatment at
780 /C176C and subsequent aging, was higher than that at 740 /C176C. Whereas, the ductility, after alloy being
solution treated at high temperature, was lower than that at low temperature. Secondary
a phase was
precipitated incompletely. And precipitate free zone was found in the microstructure after alloy being
solution treated at 740 /C176C followed by short-time or low-temperature aging. Secondary
a phase grew
with the increase of the aging temperature and aging time.

1. Introduction
The studies, dating back to 1950s, were conducted on effect of
boron on the microstructure and the property of titanium alloy.
Early studies focused on the grain reﬁnement by trace boron addi-
tion (within the solubility limits) and on the improvement of the
stiffness and strength by signiﬁcant additions (to prepare titanium
matrix composites) [1]. The reaction between B and Ti produced
strong and stiff TiB whiskers. Therefore abundant addition of boron
leads to the production of substantial TiB which would improve
alloy strength and stiffness remarkably. In recent 20 years, fruitful
studies on titanium matrix composites have been conducted by
many researchers [2–4], and some commercial alloys have been
applied to occasions of wear-resistant parts [5,6]. However, short-
falls in mechanical properties such as poor ductility, limited the
application of titanium matrix composites in aerospace and auto-
mobile industry. So far, no commercial alloys have been applied
to structural parts.
Boron-modiﬁed titanium alloys attracted considerable interest
in recent years, for its promising structures and mechanical prop-
erties such as signiﬁcant grain reﬁnement [7,8], high strength,
ductility [9–11] and reasonable low cycle fatigue properties
[12,13]. Recent studies mainly focused on the effects of trace boron
on a + b titanium or near a titanium alloys [14–16]. Few literatures
can be found in the ﬁeld about the microstructures and properties
of metastable b titanium alloys modiﬁed with trace boron [17,18].
Effects of trace boron on the properties of Beta –21S and Ti–5553
were studied by Tamirisakandala et al. [17]. Both the yield strength
(YS) and the ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of two as -cast alloys
were improved by the addition of 0.1B wt.%. The ductility of
Beta–21S increased, while that of Ti–5553 decreased. Generally,
in the case of low stress and static loading, titanium alloys compo-
nents are manufactured from cast material, but in the case of high
stress and dynamic loading, the wrought form of materials are
applied [19]. This is mainly because coarse grains of as-cast alloys
were crushed by the forging process and the recrystallization micro-
structure was obtained during the subsequent heat treatment, which
improved the mechanical properties of alloys. Up to now, micro-
structures and mechanical properties of forged alloy modiﬁed with
trace boron has not been reported, so it is necessary to carry out such
researches. The matrix alloy is metastable b titanium alloy Ti–B20
(nominal composition: Ti–3.5Al–5Mo–4V–2Cr–1Fe–2Zr–2Sn, devel-
oped by Northwest Institute for Nonferrous Metal Research). Ti–
B20–0.1B alloys were prepared with the addition of 0.1B wt.% into
the above alloy. Based on that, effects of heat treatment on micro-
structures and properties of forged Ti–B20–0.1B alloy were studied.

2. Experimental procedure
Ti–B20–0.1B (wt.%) ingot ( U150 /C2300 mm) was prepared by
consumable vacuum arc melting (VAR). Ti, Al, Cr, Fe, Zr, Sn and B
were added in elemental form, Mo and V were added as Mo–Al
and V–Al master alloys. A series of samples were treated by solu-
tion treatment plus quench from 760 to 850 /C176C and the microstruc-
ture was examined, which determined that the a/b transus of
boron-containing alloys was 810 ± 5 /C176C. The ingot with 300 mm
in length and 150 mm in diameter was ﬁrst forged to 120 mm in
diameter billet after soaking in the b phase region at 950 /C176C for
2 h. The billet was soaked in the a/b phase region at 760 /C176C for
1.5 h and then forged to a U100 /C2600 mm bar. Samples from the
forged alloy were solution treated in an electric resistance furnace
at 740 /C176C/780 /C176C for 30 min followed by water cooling. After the
solution treatment, the samples were subjected to aging at tem-
peratures ranging from 450 /C176C to 600 /C176C for 0.5–8 h followed by
air cooling.
The samples for microstructure characterization were prepared
by standard polishing techniques and etched in Kroll’s reagent.
Microstructure characterization was performed on FEI Quanta
200 F scanning electron microscopy (SEM). Phase volume percents
were determined by ImageJ image analysis software on several
SEM photomicrographs. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM)
analysis was performed by Tecnai G2 F30. The tensile samples
were obtained from the rolled sheet by electric discharge machin-
ing (EDM), and the coarse surface was polished by silicon carbide
paper through a 1500–grit ﬁnish. Room temperature tensile
tests were performed at a tensile rate of 0.5 mm/min using an
Instron5569 testing machine. Hardness testing was performed
on a Vickers hardness tester using a 5 kg load with a diamond
indenter.

tive pinning of TiB to the grain boundary can prevent grains from
growing at high temperature [18], which improves the alloy form-
ability and beneﬁts the subsequent forging process. The massive
defects locating at grain boundary provide plenty nucleation sites
for primary a phase. By contrast, there is few lattice defects located

Such precipitation characteristics of primary a phase are also

the only option effective to break continuous grain boundary a

matrix alloy, and provides driving force for the subsequent recrys-

and dynamically recrystallized to attain globular morphology. So,
forged and solution treated alloys are composed of ﬁne equiaxed
and uniform a phase, as can be seen from microstructures. The
fraction of primary a phase is determined by recrystallization
annealing temperature. Compared with forged alloys, the fraction
of primary a phase increases after solution treatment below the
ﬁnal forging temperature, while decreases after solution treatment
above the temperature. In addition, the primary a phase can pin
prior b grain boundary and decrease its mobility, thus limits the
growth and the recrystallization of prior b grains [23].
Fine secondary a phase forms during aging. And size, fraction
and morphology of the aging-formed secondary a phase can be
controlled by regulating aging temperature and time [24,25].
Either rising aging temperature or prolonging ageing time will lead
to growth of secondary a phase, so secondary a phase after ageing
at high temperature or for a long time is the coarsest, as shown in
Figs. 3–6 . Besides aging temperature and aging time, the solution
treatment temperature will also inﬂuence the precipitation of sec-
ondary a phase. Occurrence of PFZ around globularized a in the
samples aged after treatment at 740 /C176C but not after treatment at
780 /C176C (as see from Fig. 5 a). Generally, PFZ occur because areas
next to already present a phase are more heavily b stabilized,
which makes precipitation of a phase difﬁcult. Solution treatment
will improve the stability of prior b grains, and decreases the driv-
ing force for the precipitation of secondary a phase in the subse-
quent aging process [21,23]. Compared with solution treatment
at high temperature, solution treatment at low temperature
improves the matrix stability, therefore PFZ could occur in 740 /C176C
samples instead of 780 /C176C. When the aging temperature is lower
or the ageing time is shorter, alloys have slower aging response.
Equilibrium volume fraction cannot be reached when aging at
low temperature (450 /C176C) for 2 h and at medium temperature
(550 /C176C) for 0.5 h, which leads to relatively less precipitation of sec-
ondary a phase (aging at 450 /C176C for 2 h) and the appearance of PFZ
(aging at 550 /C176C for 0.5 h) respectively. Element diffuses easily at
higher aging temperature. It leads to more secondary a phase
precipitation and result in complete precipitated aging microstruc-
ture. Generally, the size of primary and secondary a phase depends

4.2. Mechanical properties
The mechanical property of near b titanium alloy is sensitive to
size, morphology and distribution of primary and secondary a
phase [28,29], so it can be modulated in a wide range by adjusting
heat treatment process to control characteristics of a phase. The
ductility of aged near b titanium alloy is affected by the fractions
of equiaxed primary a phase produced by solution treatment at
different temperatures [22]. And the alloy strength is determined
by the volume fraction and the size of secondary a phase. Since
the total volume fraction of a phase (coarse primary a phase and
ﬁne secondary a phase) is ﬁxed by the alloy chemistry [30], the
fraction of primary a phase which is precipitated by solution treat-
ment at different temperatures inﬂuences the quantity of second-
ary a phase that is precipitated during aging. Compared with
solution treatment at high temperature, the ﬁnal aging microstruc-
tures contain more equiaxed primary a phase when solution trea-
ted at low temperature, which improved the ductility. Compared
with the samples solution treated at low temperature plus aging,
samples treated at high temperature contain more precipitated
secondary a phase, resulting in a higher strength and simultaneous
decrease in ductility. So, alloys get high strength and low ductility
after solution treatment at high temperature, but low strength and
high ductility after solution treatment at low temperature.
The size and distribution of secondary a phase which affects the
alloy mechanical properties in the ﬁnal microstructures are deter-
mined by aging treatment parameters (including aging tempera-
ture and aging time). Generally, with aging temperature rising
and aging time increasing, the size of secondary a phase grows
and the alloy strength decreases. The change of alloy properties
follows this tendency after alloy being solution treated at 780 /C176C
and then aging. Solution treatment at high temperature provides
great driving force for the precipitation of secondary a phase,
which contributes to the maximum value of alloy strength of the
sample aged at low temperature (450 /C176C) or for a short time
(0.5 h) (see Figs. 9b and 10b). By contrast, when solution treated
at 740 /C176C and then aged at low temperature, the driving force is
insufﬁcient for secondary a phase to precipitate, leading to the
appearance of PFZ which usually exist in heavily stabilized b alloys
such as b21S and Beta C [25,31]. The appearance of PFZ means that
it is an incomplete precipitation process and that equilibrium
volume fraction of secondary a phase is not achieved, which
reduces the alloy strength level (see Figs. 9a and 10a). By rising
the aging temperature and increasing the aging time, equilibrium
volume fraction of secondary a phase can be reached, and the alloy
strength reaches the maximum value, followed by over-aged
alloys.
As mentioned above, the high strength of forged Ti–B20–0.1B
alloys after solution treatment and subsequent aging is attributed
not only to secondary
a phase, but also to TiB to some extent. For
example, after solution treatment at 780 /C176C and then aging at
550 /C176C for 8 h, UTS and YS of Ti–B20 are 1422 MPa and
1368 MPa, respectively [32]. Under the same process conditions,
however, UTS and YS of Ti–B20–0.1B are 1470.2 MPa and
1425.2 MPa respectively, which are increased by 48.2 MPa and
57.2 MPa separately. It is also reported in other literatures
[9,33,34] that trace TiB improves the strength of alloys such as a
titanium alloys and a + b two phase alloys. The reinforcement of
trace TiB on strength of Ti–B20 is attributed to the load transfer
by the shear lag mechanism [35,36], which can be proved by the
fracture of TiB caused by its carrying (see Fig. 12 ).
The hardness test can effectively analysis the ageing response of
alloys. Compared with commercial near b titanium alloys Beta C
and Ti–15–3, the ageing response of Ti–B20–0.1B alloy is quicker
when aged at temperatures ranging from 450 /C176C to 600 /C176C. For
example, in similar aging temperature range, Beta C alloy takes
16–20 h to reach the maximum hardness value [37]. This is mainly

5. Conclusions
The effects of solution plus aging treatment on microstructure
and tensile properties of a high strength metastable beta titanium
alloy, Ti–B20–0.1B were investigated in this work. The major con-
clusions are as follows:
(1) The addition of trace B not only reﬁnes the grain size of
as-cast alloys, but also reacts with Ti. The production of the
above B and Ti, TiB, plays the carrying role during the tensile
process, thus improves the strength level of Ti–B20 alloys.
(2) The fraction of primary a phase is determined by the
solution temperature. The equilibrium volume fraction of
secondary a phase is indirectly affected by solution treat-
ment, and it inﬂuences the strength and the ductility of
aging alloys. The strength of alloys solution treated at high
temperature plus aging is higher than that at treated at
low temperature plus aging, while the ductility has the
reverse trend.
(3) After solution treatment at two phase region and then aging,
Ti–B20–0.1B alloy obtained a high strength over 1500 MPa,
with ductility at about 10%. Such a high strength is attrib-
uted to the ﬁne secondary a phase which precipitates
with the size of 1 lm. After solution treatment at 780 /C176C
and aging at 550 /C176C for 1–8 h, alloys possess an excellent
combination of strength and ductility.
(4) Ti–B20–0.1B alloy has rapid aging response. When solution
treatment is conducted at high temperature followed by
aging treatment for short time, the equilibrium volume frac-
tion of secondary a phase is reached, and alloy strength
reaches the maximum value.
