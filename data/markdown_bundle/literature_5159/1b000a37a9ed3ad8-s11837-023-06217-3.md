Medium-entropy alloys (MEA) have excellent mechanical properties and are
extremely suitable for applications such as aerospace. However, almost all
high- and medium-entropy alloys decrease in strength and ductility at high
temperatures. As one of the alternative materials for turbine blades of aircraft
engines, high/medium entropy alloys are inevitably subjected to coupling ef-
fects of high temperature and high strain rates during service. The present
work focuses on improving the mechanical properties of MEAs and brieﬂy
describes the mechanical properties at high temperatures and high strain
rates. Meanwhile, it integrates literature regarding the methods, including
adding high melting point solutes, nanocrystalline MEA and non-isoatomic
MEA, to improve the mechanical properties at high temperatures and high
strain rates. Finally, we propose the potential research direction and possible
problems medium entropy alloys may face in the future.
INTRODUCTION
Metal materials have excellent strength and
ductility and are currently one of the most widely
used material types. However, with the develop-
ment of advanced industry, the requirements for
high-performance structural materials are becom-
ing increasingly stringent, especially under extreme
conditions such as high and low temperatures, high
pressures, high deformation rates and high strain
rates in areas such as aerospace, national defense,
military, reactor industry and other ﬁelds; the
mechanical properties of metallic materials still
have signiﬁcant space for improvement. For exam-
ple, heat-resistant steel is the most widely used
high-temperature material at a working tempera-
ture of 550–600 /C176C. However, due to brittle carbide
precipitation and phase transformation,

mechanical properties of heat-resistant steel dete-
riorate sharply when the temperature rises above
600/C176C.

one basic element, such as Fe in steel, with a small
number of alloying elements (such as C and Cr)

According to the previous studies, almost all high-

and high strain rates during service. To further
understand the deformation mechanism of medium-

latest knowledge and provide valuable information

strain rate.

Adding High Melting Point Solutes
During the development of traditional superal-
loys, researchers have been working on the factors
that affect the high temperature mechanical
properties of high- and medium-entropy alloys. We
can ﬁnd clues for the strengthening methods of
HEAs/MEAs at high temperatures from the mate-
rial strengthening strategies used for conventional
high-temperature materials. Conventional high-
temperature materials widely used include oxide
dispersion-strengthened steels (ODS),
4,5 nickel-
based superalloys, 17–19 monocrystalline and poly-
crystalline TiAl intermetallics, 20,21 and heat-resis-
tant steels. 2,3 In the study of these high-
temperature materials, the second phase and high-
melting-point precipitates and solutes are key fac-
tors for obtaining high strength of materials under
high-temperature conditions. Since the formation of
the second phase and precipitation reduces the
conﬁgurational entropy, the addition of high-melt-
ing-point solutes seems to be the main choice for
improving the high temperature strength of HEAs/
MEAs without reducing entropy.
22
Jiang et al. 23 prepared a non-isoatomic single-
phase face-centered cubic structure Ni 2CO1-
Fe1V0.5Mo0.2 MEA with sub-millimeter-sized coarse
grains and uniform distribution of all alloy elements
(Ni, Co, Fe, V, Mo), indicating that it is a single -
phase solid solution without signiﬁcant element
separation or biphase and has severe lattice distor-
tion. Mo and V are common alloying elements in
high-temperature materials, which have high melt-
ing points, solid solution strengthening effects
and phase stabilization effects. Some HEAs and
conventional superalloys (steel, nickel-based super-
alloys, TiAl, etc.) exhibit a trend of strength ductil-
ity repulsion under high-temperature
environments.
2–4,17–19,21,24–32 The special multi-
principal component structure breaks the strength
ductility paradox of the Ni
2CO1Fe1V0.5Mo0.2 MEA
exhibits signiﬁcant strain hardening as well as
excellent ductility in a wide temperature range of
25–800/C176C, as shown in Fig. 1.
During testing at low strain levels, Ni 2CO1-
Fe1V0.5Mo0.2 MEA showed a higher strain hardening
rate at 800/C176C than at 700/C176C. Notably, the strain rate
sensitivity (SRS) varies with temperature. According
to Hart’s plastic instability criterion, 33 in the tem-
perature range of 25–800/C176C, Jiang et al.23 studied the
thermal activation process and SRS of MEAs through
strain rate mutation experiments. At 25 /C176C, MEAs
show a positive SRS effect, which means the stress
increases with the increase of strain rate. At temper-
atures in range of 400/C176C and 700/C176C, a negative SRS
value can be noted, while during the strain rate
mutation test at 800 /C176C, the SRS increases to a
positive state, and as the strain increases, the stress
ﬂuctuates strongly. These two observations can be
attributed to the dynamic strain aging (DSA) effect of
MEAs (DSA refers to the change in the microstruc-
ture and properties of materials over time under the
action of strain forces
34).
From the above phenomena, it can be concluded
that the presence of DSA has a signiﬁcant impact on
the tensile properties of Ni 2CO1Fe1V0.5Mo0.2 MEA.

Nanocrystalline MEA
It is well known that nanocrystalline (NC) alloys
have numerous excellent properties, 41 such as high
strength and hardness, competitive fatigue resis-
tance and outstanding radiation resistance, due to
their ultra-ﬁne grains and high-density grain
boundaries (GBs).
42–45 However, the thermal insta-
bility of NC materials severely limits their potential
applications at high temperatures and makes them
difﬁcult to process.
42,46 However, from a thermody-
namic and kinetic perspective, MEA/HEA has
unique advantages in stabilizing grain size. From
a thermodynamic perspective, lower grain boundary
energy in HEA/MEAs may reduce the thermody-
namic driving force for grain coarsening, thereby
stabilizing the grains.
38,47 In terms of kinetic sta-
bility, considering the presence of many different
adsorbents and solute drag effects, the coarsening
kinetics can be suppressed. 48,49 In addition, the
thermodynamic driving forces of grain growth and
precipitation can reduce the slow diffusion kinetics
in HEAs. 50 Based on this, if thermodynamic and
kinetic characteristics can be fully utilized, it will
provide HEA/MEAs with excellent performance at
high temperatures.
Deng et al. 51 designed and prepared a two-phase
(Cu-rich face-centered cubic and AlCoNi rich body-
centered cubic) NC AlCoCuNi MEA using mechan-
ical alloying (MA) and spark plasma sintering (SPS)
consolidation methods. This method avoids the
formation of grain coarsening and precipitation
during traditional casting processes. Through MA
and SPS process, an unprecedented thermally
stable two-phase NC AlCoCuNi MEA has been
fabricated. The material has an average grain size
of 46 nm, a compressive strength of 2.0 GPa, a
strain of up to 10.1% and a Vickers microhardness
of up to 645 HV. More importantly, research has
found that this dual-phase NC AlCoCuNi MEA can
maintain nanostructures and a high hardness of
about 580 HV even after annealing at 900 /C176C for
50 h. The critical temperature of about 900 /C176Ci s
signiﬁcantly higher than other copper-containing
nanocrystalline materials and MEAs.
52–56 To eval-
uate the thermal stability of the two-phase NC
AlCoCuNi MEA increasingly deeply, the experi-
ment further increased the annealing temperature
to 950/C176C, and the results showed that the hardness
value remained at a high level of about 508 HV, as
shown in Fig. 2. The microstructure of the fracture
surface shows that most of the nanostructures in
the MEA remain unchanged even after annealing at
950/C176C for 50 h. All experimental results show that
when the annealing temperature is < 900/C176C, the
two-phase NC AlCoCuNi MEA does have excellent
resistance to long-term annealing softening. The
extremely high thermal stability of thermally
stable substrates is attributed to a wide range of
thermally stable low-energy phase boundaries, low-
angle grain boundaries, high entropy and slow
diffusion effects.

that of nickel-based superalloys [in nickel-based
superalloys, cubic gamma ferrite precipitates have
an L12 structure (Cu3Au type), which is closely

strengthening mechanism for structural applica-
tions. The second phase particles will harden
through dislocation bypass mechanisms (Orowan
type) or particle shear mechanisms. In general, the
Orowan mechanism is effective when the size of the
precipitate reaches a critical value or is inconsistent
with the substrate. When the precipitate is small
and uniform enough, the shear mechanism will be
available. The high hardness value of HEAs is
attributed to lattice distortion effects, which are
caused by differences in atomic size, crystal struc-
ture and bond energy between constituent ele-
ments. As a MEA, Fe

strengthened by precipitation hardening of B2
phase, with a yield strength > 1000 MPa.

the oxide layer/base interface became rough after

In addition, oxide formation was observed at grain
boundaries near the surface, indicating the pres-
ence of internal oxidation. Although the element
mapping almost did not detect the presence of Co
and Ni in the oxide layer, the line contour results
successfully showed an increase of Co and Ni
concentrations within the oxide layer. As the expo-
sure time increases, the oxide layer after 48 h of
oxidation maintains a similar thickness to the oxide
layer after 24 h of oxidation, indicating that the
oxide layer on the surface of CrCoNi can remain
stable for longer exposure times. In addition, it was
observed that the oxygen content is extremely high
in and near the oxide layer.
Hu et al.
66 prepared a 20% volume fraction TiNi
ﬁber-reinforced aluminum alloy matrix composite
based on Al using a pressure inﬁltration method.
After pre-oxidation treatment in air, a thin layer of
oxide is formed on the surface of TiNi ﬁbers. The
results showed that with the increase of oxidation
time, the interfacial layer between TiNi ﬁber and Al
matrix became thicker, and the tensile strength and
elongation of the composite decreased. When the
oxidation time is 1 h, due to the strong interfacial
bonding between the Al matrix and TiNi ﬁbers, the
tensile strength of the material decreases with
increasing temperature. At a temperature of
423 K, stress does not induce the formation of
martensite, and only one yield plateau is found
during tensile deformation. The appearance of oxide
layers at high temperatures has a certain impact on
the mechanical behavior of materials. However,
there are few studies on the impact of the appear-
ance of oxide layers on the mechanical properties of
CrCoNi MEAs at high temperatures, and it is
necessary to study the corresponding impact.
Improvement of Mechanical Properties
of MEAs at Ultra-High Temperatures
The non-isoatomic single-phase FCC Ni
2CO1-
Fe1V0.5Mo0.2 MEA prepared by Jiang et al. 23 exhi-
bits signiﬁcant strain hardening and excellent
ductility in the temperature range of 25–800 /C176C,
despite its unique multi-principal component struc-
ture breaking the strength ductility paradox. How-
ever, when the test temperature is > 800/C176C, the
Ni
2CO1Fe1V0.5Mo0.2 MEA exhibits strain softening
and signiﬁcantly reduced ductility. As the temper-
ature increases from 25 /C176C to 900 /C176C, the yield
strength gradually decreases from 310 MPa to
191 MPa. When the temperature rises to 1000 /C176C,
the yield stress of the MEA decreases sharply to
107 MPa, showing a softening effect with increasing
temperature. Similarly, at 900 /C176C and 1000 /C176C, the
material loses its strain-hardening ability, and the
ultimate tensile strength decreases. At this point,
the MEA exhibits necking immediately after yield-
ing, resulting in negligible uniform plastic deforma-
tion. As the temperature rises from 25 /C176C to 800 /C176C,
the uniform elongation slowly decreases from 62%
to 32%. As the temperature increases from 25 /C176Ct o
1000/C176C, the failure strain gradually decreases from
73% to 47%.
A two-phase nanocrystalline AlCoCuNi MEA was
designed by Deng et al.
51 When the annealing
temperature is < 900/C176C, it has excellent long-term
annealing softening resistance, while after anneal-
ing at 1000/C176C for 10 h, its hardness decreases to 480
HV, indicating typical microstructure instability.
The study of the mechanical properties of two-phase
nanocrystalline AlCoCuNi MEAs at higher temper-
atures is still an unexplored ﬁeld.
Shivam et al.
57 prepared a non-isoatomic
Fe40Cr25Ni15Al15Co5 MEA by IM process. Due to
the formation of an intermetallic B2 phase in a
BCC matrix, the alloy exhibits limited values of
uniform compressive strain (3%) and total com-
pressive strain (5%) in the as-cast state. The
research on its mechanical properties also stopped
at 900 /C176C.
Currently, the research on the mechanical prop-
erties of MEAs at high temperatures is mostly
focused on temperatures < 1000/C176C. However, as
one of the alternative materials for turbine blades
of aircraft engines, the service conditions of MEAs
in ultra-high temperature extreme environments
are also inevitable. However, there are few studies
on improving the mechanical properties of MEAs
under ultra-high temperature conditions; more
research is needed to provide new ideas and direc-
tions for future research on MEAs.

CONCLUSION
This article reviews the disadvantages of mechan-
ical properties of MEAs under high-temperature
conditions, as well as the methods of improving
their mechanical properties by adding high melting
point solutes, designing nanocrystalline and non-
isoatomic MEAs. Although these methods have
achieved some important research results on
improving the high-temperature mechanical prop-
erties of MEA/HEAs, this review still proposes some
future research directions. Speciﬁcally, this article
raises the issue of MEA oxidation at high temper-
atures and the gaps in research at ultra-high
temperatures. These possible problems in the future
development of MEAs provide new ideas and direc-
tions for future research. Further breakthroughs in
these issues will be of great signiﬁcance for the
expansion of research in the application ﬁeld of
MEAs.
