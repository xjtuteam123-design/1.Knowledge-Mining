Abstract. The paper presents the transmission and scanning electronic microscope investigations of Ni-Al-Cr superalloy 
alloyed with additional Re and La elements. This superalloy is obtained by a directional solidification method. It is 
shown that such additional elements as Re and La result in formation of new phases in Ni-Al-Cr accompanied by 
considerable modifications of quasi-cuboid structure in its Jc-phase. 
Keywords: structure, phase composition, superalloys, alloying, quasi-cuboid 
INTRODUCTION 
The requirements for materials with appropriate service properties are being constantly improved. One of the 
most prospective researches related to the advanced high-temperature materials is the creation of alloys comprising 
intermetallic phases. (J + Jc)-phase-based superalloys belong to this type of materials, which J-phase is presented by 
a solid solution with the fcc structure, while the ordered Jc-phase has L12 superstructure [1]. Currently, superalloys 
are produced on the basis of Ni-Al alloy modified by different elements [2]. 
EXPERIMENTAL 
The aim of this work is to carry out the qualitative and quantitative investigations of structure, phase composition 
and morphology of higher-rhenium-content alloy with different La content. This alloy comprises such basic 
elements as ~70 ɚt% Ni, ~17 ɚt % Al, and ~5 ɚt % ɋr. Additional elements, such as Mo, W, Ta, Ti, Co with the total 
amount of ~7 ɚt %, namely 0.4 ɚt % Re and 0.006 and 0.08 ɚt % La, were used in this experiment. Two types of 
investigations were applied in this experiment: transmission electronic microscopy (TEM) and scanning electronic 
microscopy (SEM). The alloy was obtained by a directional solidification method. Its structure was studied in two 
states: 1) the original state after the directional solidification and 2) the state after annealing at 1150qɋ during 1430 h. 
The obtained alloy possessed a single-crystal structure (orientation [001]). 
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION 
TEM and SEM analyses show the combination of a number of phases in different states. Their qualitative and 
quantitative compositions depend on the thermal treatment (see Table 1). The phases observed in the superalloy can 
be classified as primary and secondary. This classification is based on their volume fraction, contributing part in the 
alloy, and stationary or single phase presence. As it follows from Table 1, Jc- and J-phases are primary, J-phase 
being a disordered solid solution with the fcc structure, and Jc-phase being an ordered phase with L12 superstructure.

Usually, these phases form the basic structure of practically all superalloys [2]. In the alloy under research, they 
are present in the form of Jc-phase quasi-cuboids separated by J-phase layers. All other phases are secondary, i.e. 
different phases are present in different states of the alloy. The volume fractions of phases in different alloy states 
are also given in Table 1, which shows the high volume fraction of Jc-phase in both states of the alloy. The amount 
of primary phases (J + Jc) in the original state of the alloy is larger than after its high-temperature annealing, because 
Ni is extracted by lanthanum (La) from J-phase more intensively to form Ni3La2 phase [3]. 
Let us consider ȕ-phase (see Table 1). This letter defines three phases: 1) ȕ-NiA with the crystal lattice parameter 
of ɚ = 0.288 nm and 2) AlRe with the same lattice parameter of ɚ = 0.288 nm. They both have CsCl structure and 
can be transformed to tetragonal phases of the type L1 0 [2, 4]. These phases are mutually soluble, can form solid 
solutions, and the presence of Ni–Al–Re triple diagram on their Ni–Al and Al–Re sides denotes their ability to form 
a large area connecting these positions. The authors indicate these phases by letter E since AlRe phase has no 
specific notation except for the composition. 
The next two ı and Ȥ phases included in Table 1, refer to topologically close-packed or Frank-Kasper phases [2, 
5]. They are present in the alloy due to Re presence. Actually, interaction of Re with Mo and W results in the 
emergence of ı and Ȥ phases: ı-phase is formed due to the high-temperature solidification, while Ȥ-phase—due to 
phase decomposition at lower temperatures or crystallization. Moreover, ı-phase is formed as a result of Re 
interaction with Co, Cr, Ni, Al, while Ȥ-phase is formed at its interaction with Mo and W and also with Co and Al. 
Finally, the last phase is presented by nickel lanthanide Ni
3La2. It is observed in the alloy only after annealing 
(see Table 1). The rest of the phases are presented by concentrated solid solutions formed within the large interval of 
concentrations and intensively contributing to all phase transformations [2]. 
As is known, the structure of superalloys is presented by a mixture of ( J + Jc)-phases. This mixture has a 
morphologically regular orientation and forms a quasi-single-crystalline structure. Active metals found in the alloy 
complicate its structure causing the formation of areas with disordered J + Jc-structure. First of all, Re and La are 
active metals in the alloy [3, 4]. The presence of Re is caused by the partial formation of ȕ-phase and the complete 
formation of ı- and Ȥ -phases. La is caused by Ni 3La2 phase formation (see Table 1). The formation of these four 
phases considerably complicates the structure of J + Jc quasi-cuboids. Since Re and La do not fill the alloy volume 
uniformly and are present only in local areas, merely a part of a J + Jc quasi-cuboid bulk turns to be disordered in all 
states of the alloy. The experiments allow the construction of alloy schemes that are shown in Fig. 1 and 2. 
As shown in Figure 3, the layers of Jc- and Ȥ-phases are parallel. Both phases have a cubic crystal lattice, size 
effects, however, produce their crystallographic disorientation [4, 5]. 
Let us consider the contact between E-phase and J + Jc-phase cluster (see Fig. 1). Experiments show that the 
interface between them is directed towards [011] Jc-phase. The chemical formula of E-phase is close to NiAl 2Re 
composition.

phase particles has the specific contrast and the finite thickness. Sometimes, V-phase creates a contrast of bending-
torsion. This effect is also observed near Ni 3La2 phase, but rather rarely. Particles of V-phase are elongated along 
several directions of the ¢110² type in Jc-phase, whilst particles of Ni 3La2 phase are elongated only by one direction

It should be noted that extinction contours of bending-torsion are not observed in the structure that indicates the 
absence of internal stresses in the alloy. This is because a good crystallographic conjunction between ( J + Jc) matrix 
and the formation of V-phase and lanthanum. This crystallographic conjunction allows supposing that Re and La 
alloying that provides solidification, does not create problems connected with internal stresses. Also, it means that a 
positive effect from Re and La alloying can be used in superalloys. 
So, the alloy obtained by the directional solidification method had three different states of phase morphology, 
namely: the ideal structure of ( Jc + J) quasi-cuboids containing F-phase layers and ȕ-phase of the there-component 
solution NiAl2Re. After the annealing, only two states were observed: the ideal structure and ( Jc + J) quasi-cuboids 
comprising the formation of V and Ni 3La2 phases. Also, the periodicity was observed in the location of space 
structures. Undoubtedly, the periodic structure of the superalloy strengthens the material in terms of the appropriate 
orientation. In other words, reserves for strengthening the superalloy material can be obtained at the mesolevel.
