Improvement of Mechanical Properties of Zr‑Based Bulk Amorphous 
Alloys by High Temperature Heat Treatment
Changwoo Jeon1  · Min Chul Jo2 · Juho Lee3 · Eun Soo Park3 · Junho Park4 · Sang Yong Shin5
Received: 2 May 2019 / Accepted: 28 July 2019

Abstract
Zr-based bulk amorphous alloys which were heat-treated from 673 to 1073 K were investigated, and compressive fracture 
mechanisms related with improvement of mechanical properties were investigated by observing how the type, size and volume 
fraction of crystalline particles which is generated during the heat treatment affected the fracture mode. The Zr-based bulk 
amorphous alloys contained nano-sized crystalline particles showed excellent mechanical properties of compressive fracture 
strength over 2.0 GPa and compressive plastic strain of approximately 1%. The fracture modes of the alloys are changed 
depending on the heat treatment temperature and their microstructure. When the alloy is heat-treated at 673 K, the nano-
sized Zr
2Cu particles are generated in amorphous matrix. According to the observation of fractured specimens, in the alloy 
heat-treated at 673 K, the fine and elongated vein patterns were well developed. The heat treatment temperature increased 
above T
x, the Zr2Cu particles as well as complex crystalline particles are consist in the heat-treated alloy, which lead to the 
brittle fracture and the alloy show the low strength of 605 MPa. In the fractured surface of the alloy, large cleavage facets 
were evenly developed. When the heat treatment temperature up near T
m, the size of Zr2Cu and complex crystalline particles 
increased to approximately 1 μm and the volume fraction of complex crystalline particles increased up to 52.3%. As the size 
of crystalline particles change, the size of facet decreased similarly to the size of crystalline particles.
Keywords Zr-based bulk amorphous alloy · High temperature heat treatment · Melting temperature · Fractography
1 Introduction
Bulk amorphous alloys have excellent properties such as 
high strength, high hardness, high stiffness, and high corro-
sion resistance because of their liquid-like structure [1 –4]. 
In spite of their excellent properties, their widespread 
applications have been limited due to some disadvantages. 
To produce amorphous alloys with their unique structure, 
equipment with a fast cooling rate and vacuum atmosphere 
is essentially required. Most of these types of equipment 
for bulk amorphous products have serious shortcomings on 
productions speed and expensive maintenance cost, there-
fore mass production of amorphous alloys has been limited. 
Because of the recently developed equipment that has com-
plemented these shortcomings, products of bulk amorphous 
alloys have become gradually commercialized [4, 5]. One of 
them with a cycle time of two or three minutes is possible to 
continuously produce 1–24 samples per a cycle.
Since the demand for enlargement and thin plate has 
been increased in industries, especially electronic devices, 
structural materials are required to have lightweight and 
high strength. Although Zr-based amorphous alloys show 
the high tensile strength of 1500–1600 MPa, their specific 
strength is approximately 280 MPa/(g/cm
3) which is rela-
tively higher than that of commercial Ti–6Al–4 V alloys 
or 7075 aluminum alloys [6 ]. In recently developed Zr 
amorphous alloys by heat treatments, the fracture strength 
is improved by forming crystalline particles in amorphous 
matrix [7, 8]. According to Wu et al. [7], the microstructure

2 Experimental
Master alloys of Zr56.7Al13Ni5Cu26.3 (ZANC) were fabricated 
by arc melting in a water-cooled copper crucible under the 
Ti-gettered argon (Ar) atmosphere. The cylindrical rods of 
their alloys with a diameter of 2 mm and a length of 50 mm 
were produced by arc melting and suction casting into a 
water-cooled copper mold. Thermal properties of the alloys 
were measured by a differential scanning calorimetry (DSC; 
Setaram Instrumentation, LabSys). The alloys were heated 
to their T
g temperature based on the results of thermal prop-
erties at a heating rate 5 K/min and held for 30 min at a 
vacuum pressure of 1.3 × 10
−5 torr. The samples, then, were 
reheated to peak temperatures from 673 to 1073 K at a heat-
ing rate 5 K/min and held for 30 min in a vacuum chamber. 
After heat treatment, all samples were cooled in furnace. 
The thermal properties and heat treatment conditions of the 
alloys are listed in Tables 1 and 2. The alloys were polished 
in diamond pastes (size; 0.25 m) and observed by a scanning 
electron microscope (SEM, model; JSM-6330F, Jeol, Japan). 
Crystalline phases and amorphous matrix were analyzed by 
X-ray diffraction (XRD, Cu radiation, scan rate; 2° min
−1, 
scan step size; 0.02°), and their average grain size was 
measured by an image analyzer program. The overall bulk 
hardness of the alloys was measured by a Vickers hardness 
tester (model; FM-700, Future-Tech Corp., Japan) under a 
300 gf load. They were machined into rod-type compressive 
specimens with a diameter of 2 mm and a length of 4 mm, 
and room-temperature compressive tests were conducted on 
these specimens at a strain rate of 5.2 × 10
−4 s−1 by a uni-
versal testing machine (model; 8862, Instron Corp., Canton, 
MA, USA) with capacity of 10,000 kg. In order to investi-
gate the compressive fracture mode, the fractographs of the 
fractured specimen was observed by an SEM.

3.2 Compressive Properties
Table 3 shows the Vickers hardness test results of the ZANC 
alloys. The hardness of the as-casted ZANC alloy is 499.4 
Hv. As the heat treatment temperature increases, the hard-
ness of the alloys also increases to 705.4 Hv in the ZANC 
alloy heat-treated at 923 K. Above 923 K, the hardness of 
the ZANC alloys decreases as the heat treatment temperature 
increases. The highest hardness of the ZANC alloy is associ-
ated with the type and size of crystalline phases.
Figure  3 presents compressive stress–strain curves 
obtained from the compressive testing machine and Table 5 
summarizes the compressive test results. The compressive 
fracture strength of the as-casted ZANC alloy is 1902 MPa. 
The heat-treated ZANC alloys below the glass transition 
temperature (T
g; 693 K) have strengths of over 2031 MPa 
and elongations of over 0.8%, which are higher than those 
of the as-casted ZANC alloy. The heat-treated ZANC alloy 
near the crystallization temperature (T
x; 763 K) have lower 
strengths and elongation than the as-casted ZANC alloy, and 
the heat-treated ZANC alloy at 823 K is worst among all the 
ZANC alloys. The strength of the heat-treated ZANC alloys 
near the T
m begins to increase, and the heat-treated ZANC 
alloy at 1073 K (20 K below the Tm) has a compressive 
fracture strength of 2029 MPa, which is similar with the 
heat-treated ZANC alloys below the T
g.
SEM fractographs of the fractured compressive speci-
mens are shown in the Fig.  4. The fractographs of the as-
casted ZANC alloy show vein-patterns which is the typical 
fracture mode in bulk amorphous alloy (Fig.  4a) [9 , 10]. 
In the ZANC alloy heat-treated at 673 K, the vein patterns 
less than 1 μm are formed, and some of them are elongated 
along the fracture direction (Fig. 4b). As the heat treatment 
temperature increases over the T
g, the inner surface of the 
vein pattern changes from the flat surface to the rugged sur-
face (Fig. 4c, d). The fractured surface of the ZANC alloys 
heat-treated above the Tx shows the vein pattern and rugged 
surface as well as the cleavage facet which is developed in 
the wide area (Fig. 4e). Since the heat treatment temperature 
increase near the Tm, cleavage facets with a size of less than 
3 μm are evenly developed in the fractured surface of the 
ZANC alloys. The size of the cleavage facets is decreased as 
the heat treatment temperature is closer to the T
m.
4 Discussion
Zr-based amorphous alloys generally show the quite limited 
ductility, even though they are under compressive loading 
condition [7 , 8]. When the stress beyond a certain limit is 
applied on the amorphous alloys, few shear bands are gener-
ated and a crack is rapidly propagated along the shear bands. 
The ductility can be limited without the developments of 
slips or twins which are essential to plastic deformation. In 
order to improve the ductility and strength of amorphous 
alloys, crystalline particles should be formed inside the

rapidly drop by brittle fracture [11]. In this study, though, 
the strength of the Zr-based amorphous alloys with fully

When the ZANC alloy heat-treated at 673 K is compared 
with the as-cast ZANC alloy, vein patterns are well devel-
oped in the fractured surface and the size of vein-pattern is 
smaller than the as-cast ZANC alloy. As the vein-pattern 
size decrease, the distance that shear band propagation is 
reduced, which means the fracture of the alloy is delayed 
[9–12]. Although the elongated vein pattern is found in the 
fractured surface, most vein patterns are densely developed, 
which are seen as evidence of increased compressive ductil-
ity. The crystalline particles were generated inside the amor-
phous matrix, which interrupted the propagation of shear 
bands [8, 13–17]. Increasing the heat treatment temperature 
to the T
g (693 K), the fracture surface of the ZANC alloy 
change under compressive load. As Fig. 1 shows, the Zr2Cu 
and complex crystalline phase peaks increase steeply, which 
is the evidence of increasing the generation of Zr
2Cu and 
complex crystalline particles [8]. Due to increase the Zr2Cu 
and complex crystalline particles, the inside of the vein pat-
tern changes smooth surface to rugged surface, even if the 
compressive ductility is maintained.
The ZANC alloys heat-treated at temperatures above the 
 T
x have the microstructure with crystalline phase. The grain 
sizes of the ZANC alloys increase as heat-treated tempera-
ture increases. The ZANC alloy heat-treated at 823 K has 
the microstructure of sub-micron size grains which include 
additional crystalline phase with volume fraction of 20.8% 
as well as Zr
2Cu phase with volume fraction of 63.5%. The 
hardness of the ZANC alloy has been increased compared 
to the heat-treated alloy below the T
g, but the compressive 
fracture strength is low of 600 MPa, which is about 1/3 level 
of the as-cast ZANC alloy. Additional phases generated by 
increasing heat-treated temperature are considered brittle 
particles [15, 18, 19]. In the fractured surface, cleave facets 
are observed in a wide area, which indicates that the shear 
crack occurred during the initial stage of compression test 
and rapidly propagated. This phenomenon is found in Zr-
based bulk amorphous alloys which are heat-treated above 
 T
x or for more than 40 min within glass transition tempera-
ture [19].
As the heat treatment temperature is increased near the 
 Tm, the compressive fracture strength of the ZANC alloy 
increases again. The ZANC alloy heat-treated at the temper-
ature below 20 K below the Tm shows the compressive frac-
ture strength of 2029 MPa which is similar to those of heat-
treated ZANC alloys below the T
g. This higher compressive 
fracture strength is not easily explained because most multi-
component alloys generated brittle eutectic phases after heat 
treatment [20–23]. Bulk amorphous alloys require different 
alloy designs and processes with commercial alloys to have 
a liquid-like structure at room temperature. The composition 
of the alloys has to be designed near the eutectic point and 
made with a fast cooling rate. Since heat energy is injected 
into the bulk amorphous alloys, the crystalline particles with 
eutectic phases are generated inside the amorphous matrix. 
These phases cause the brittle fracture, which leads to the

5 Conclusions
Microstructures and compressive properties of the Zr-based 
bulk amorphous alloys which were heat-treated from 673 to 
1073 K were investigated, and fracture mechanism related to 
the improvement of mechanical properties were examined.
(1) From the observation of fractured surface after com-
pressive tests, the alloy heat-treated at 673 K developed fine 
and elongated vein patterns in the fractured surface. The 
size of vein-pattern in the alloy heat-treated at 673 K was 
smaller than those of the other alloys. Most vein patterns 
were densely developed, which increased compressive frac-
ture plastic strain. The alloy heat-treated at 673 K showed 
the excellent compressive properties of compressive fracture 
strength of 2052 MPa and compressive fracture strain of 
1.3%.
(2) The compressive fracture strength of the alloy heat-
treated at 823 K decreased to 605 MPa. As the heat treat-
ment temperature increased to 823 K, the cleavage facets 
were developed in a wide area of the fractured surface, 
which degrades the mechanical properties of the alloys.
(3) The alloy heat-treated near the melting temperature 
increased in strength to 2029 MPa. The microstructure 
showed the crystalline phases whose average grain size is 
approximately 1 μm. Since the size of crystalline particles 
is grown to approximately 1 μm even in the fully crystal-
lized microstructure, the strength of the alloy increase and 
the size of cleavage facets reduces to similar to the size of 
crystalline particles.
