The eﬀects of ruthenium on the phase stability of fourth generation
Ni-base single crystal superalloys
Atsushi Sato *, Hiroshi Harada, Tadaharu Yokokawa, Takao Murakumo,
Yutaka Koizumi, Toshiharu Kobayashi, Hachiro Imai
National Institute for Materials Science, High Temperature Materials Group, 1-2-1 Sengen, Tsukuba Science City, Ibaraki 305-0047, Japan
Received 16 August 2005; received in revised form 24 December 2005; accepted 3 January 2006
Available online 3 February 2006
Abstract
The formation of topologically close-packed (TCP) phases in nickel-base single crystal superalloys causes considerable degradation of
the mechanical properties. It has recently been found that platinum-group metals can be eﬀective in controlling the precipitation of such
phases, and this extent of precipitation control requires further investigation. This study compares Ru-containing and non-Ru-contain-
ing single crystal superalloys. Scanning electron microscopy microstructural observations showed that the rate of TCP phase precipita-
tions decreased through Ru addition. Transmission electron microscopy microstructural observations showed that the P phase, one of
the TCP phases, was eliminated through the addition of Ru. The occurrence of this phenomenon will be discussed.

1. Introduction
The superior high temperature mechanical properties of
nickel-base superalloys make them the best candidates for
use in jet engines and industrial gas turbines. Higher oper-
ating temperatures are required to increase turbine output
and eﬃciency; thus, improvements in the high temperature
strength of superalloys are being actively sought.
To improve the high temperature strength, solid solu-
tion strengthening elements have been added to later gener-
ation superalloys at levels that are well above the solubility
limit imposed by the solvus of the c/c
0 two-phase region. In
long-term exposures to elevated temperatures, the forma-
tion of topologically close-packed (TCP) phases causes
serious deterioration in creep strength [1,2]. Recently, it
has been found that platinum-group metals are eﬀective
in controlling the precipitation of TCP phases [3,4].
In this study, Ru, a platinum-group metal, was added to
a third generation superalloy that contained an excessive
amount of Re. The combined area fraction of all TCP
phases present in the alloy specimens, after exposure to dif-
ferent conditions, was measured. The types of TCP phases
present in the Ru-containing and non-Ru-containing alloys
were identiﬁed through transmission electron microscopy
(TEM) observation.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. TCP formation
The combined area fractions of all TCP phases in each
alloy are shown in Fig. 1 . The precipitate area fractions
in all alloys are seen to increase with time, at all exposure
temperatures. An Ru-containing alloy, when exposed to
the same conditions as a non-Ru-containing alloy, can be
seen to have a considerably smaller precipitate area
fraction.

that Ru addition has on nickel-base superalloys, the nucle-

Fig. 2 shows the number of precipitates at each tempera-
ture for Ru-containing and non-Ru-containing alloys.

3.2. Nucleation rate
Precipitate nucleation rate depends on both the nucle-
ation frequency and the area fraction of alloy that is con-
ducive for precipitate nucleation ( Fig. 2 ). If one were to
assume that Ru addition merely decreases precipitate
nucleation frequency, then, in the case where a Ru-contain-
ing alloy and a non-Ru-containing alloy had inﬁnite high
temperature exposure, both alloys must have equal TCP
precipitate area fractions. Yet, this was not the case; when
specimens of both alloy types were exposed to a range of
high temperatures for 3000 h ( Fig. 1 ), the maximum
precipitate area fraction was found to always be lower
for the Ru-containing alloy. This result implies that Ru
addition reduces the area fraction of alloy that is conducive
for precipitate nucleation, and this subsequently decreased
the precipitate nucleation rate.
The composition diﬀerences between the dendrite core
region and the inter-dendritic region in TMS-138, after
exposure at 1000 /C176 C for 3000 h, were determined using
SEM–EDX ( Table 2 ). Although this alloy contains
2 wt.% Ru, refractory elements, especially Re and W, were
still found to segregate in the dendrite core regions, and
this led to TCP phase precipitation in the single crystal
superalloy.
Ru, Re and W are known to preferentially partition to
the c phase [12]. In this paper, it was found that Ru addi-
tion does not reduce the extent of segregation of the latter
elements into c; rather, it decreases the area fraction in the
dendrite core regions that is conducive for precipitate
nucleation. This is attributable to Ru’s ability to increase
the solubility limits of solid solution strengthening ele-
ments, particularly for Re and W.

The TEM bright ﬁeld image of a TCP precipitate that
formed in the non-Ru-containing alloy after exposure at
1100 /C176 C for 300 h is shown in Fig. 5 (a), together with its
diﬀraction pattern (inset). From diﬀraction patterns taken

In the non-Ru-containing alloy after exposure at
1000 /C176 C for 1000 h, needle-like and round precipitates were
observed. Examination of several diﬀraction patterns
determined that the precipitates of both morphologies were
the orthorhombic P phase.
Specimens of the 2 wt.% Ru alloy that were exposed to
1000 /C176 Co r1 1 0 0 /C176 C for 1000 h were examined for TCP
precipitates by TEM. In both specimens, a needle-like
precipitate was observed. In the 1000 /C176 C specimen, a type
of round TCP precipitate was also observed. From several
diﬀraction patterns, precipitates of both morphologies were
identiﬁed as the P phase. The TEM image of the TCP
phase observed in the 2 wt.% Ru alloy after exposure at
1000 /C176 C for 1000 h is shown in Fig. 5 (b), together with its
diﬀraction pattern (inset).
TEM observation showed that only the P phase precip-
itated in all specimens that were exposed at 1000 /C176 C; the
addition of Ru did not change the type of TCP phase that
precipitated. This indicates that the decrease in TCP pre-
cipitation due to Ru addition ( Fig. 1 ) can be regarded as
a decrease in P phase precipitation. Yeh et al. [14] has sta-
ted that Ru addition can control the precipitation of both r
phase and P phase. Cui et al. found on the contrary, that
Ru addition cannot suppress r phase precipitation [15].
Additionally, the P phase contains the highest Re concen-
tration out of all TCP phases [10]. These ﬁndings all indi-
cate that Ru expands the solubility limit of Re, thus
controlling the Re-rich P phase’s precipitation.

4. Conclusions
(1) TCP precipitation rate was substantially reduced
when 2 or 2.5 wt.% Ru was added to a third genera-
tion nickel-based superalloy, TMS-121.
(2) The nucleation rate of TCP precipitates decreased
considerably upon Ru addition; this is due to the
reduction in the area fraction of alloy that is condu-
cive for precipitate nucleation.
(3) Ru addition increased the solid solubility limits of
solid solution strengthening elements in c, particu-
larly for Re and W.
(4) Increases in solid solubility limits eliminated the pre-
cipitation of both the P phase and the R phase.
(5) Growth rate of the TCP phase decreased with the
addition of Ru. It is proposed that the diﬀusion coef-
ﬁcient between the TCP phase and the matrix inter-
face decreased with the addition of Ru.
