abstract
In this paper, the evolution of the g0 and h phases of new Ni eCo-based superalloy with
different Nb contents was investigated using a combination of thermodynamic calcula-
tions and high-throughput selective laser melting (SLM). Thermodynamic calculations
show that the increase of Nb content lowered the solubility temperature of the g
0 phase
and caused the precipitation of the h phase. The alloys with the Nb gradient content
prepared by SLM during aging at 800 /C14 C for 100h indicated that the maximum volume
fraction and size of the g0 phase reach the maximum at 3.0 e3.5% Nb. The elongated h
phases enrichment in Ni, Ti, and Nb atoms precipitate for the Nb content higher than 2.2%
Nb and prefer to grow longitudinally at the expense of the g
0 precipitates. Combining the
inﬂuence of the Al/(Nb þ Ta þ Ti) ratio on the h precipitates, the addition of Nb content in
the NieCo-based superalloys is less than 2.2%, which increases the high-temperature yield
strength and tensile strength.

1. Introduction
Ni-based superalloys are widely used in aero-engines due to
their excellent strength, fatigue resistance, and creep prop-
erties [ 1]. The increase in thermal efﬁciency of aero-engines
required the improvement of mechanical properties and
temperature-bearing capacity of the Ni-based superalloys by
the addition of alloy elements, such as Nb, Ta, Ru, and Hf [ 2,3].
These elements can generate a combination of solid-solution
hardening [ 4], precipitation hardening [ 5], grain boundary
hardening [ 6], or additional twin strengthening [ 7] and affect

(DO24 structure, Ni 3Ti), g00 and d (DO22 structure, Ni 3Nb), or
Laves phase segregation are often found in the Ni-based su-

Many works [11e13] were carried out on the alloy elements
on the phase transformation, microstructures, and mechani-

0 phase hard-
ener element in nickel-based superalloys, can reﬁne the g0

promote the homogeneous distribution of MC carbides [14,15].
In ATI 718Plus superalloy with 5.5% Nb, the new-type g0/g'0
coprecipitates with a sandwich conﬁguration are found and
the formation of the d phase might have advantageously crept
at temperatures higher than 649 /C14 C[ 16e18]. The g0 volume
fraction, size, and lattice misﬁt increased with a larger Al: Nb
ratio [19]. Carneiro et al. [20] discussed the effect of niobium on
the phase selection and phase chemistry of EP741NP alloy and
found that the alloy with unique mechanical and corrosion
resistance properties may be achieved. Antonov et al. [ 21]
showed rapid nucleation and growth of s phase precipitates
occurred and conglomeration of these precipitates led to a
complex grain boundary “basket-weave”type network for the
RRHT4 superalloy containing elevated levels of Nb. In the Nb-
modiﬁed Nimonic 80A alloy, the diffusion of Nb from g to g
0
phase made part of the ﬁne g0 particles coexist with the coarse
g0 particles, while the d-Ni3Nb precipitates in the g matrix
exhibited an orientation relationship with the blocky (Nb, Ti)C
[22]. In the Nb-modiﬁed MAR-M246 alloy, TCP precipitation
and carbide decomposition are prone to occur during high-
temperature exposure probably due to the higher internal
diffusivity of elements [ 23]. Asgari et al. [ 24] found that the h
phase nucleated within the g
0 precipitates and the mismatch
is accommodated by arrays of misﬁt dislocations at the h/g0
interface.
High throughput methodology via additive manufacturing
provided a new strategy to efﬁciently establish the
relationship between material compositions, microstructure,
and mechanical properties [ 25e27]. Wang et al. [ 28] con-
structed a comprehensive thermochemical database based on
high-throughput ﬁrst-principles phonon calculations and
applied it to the additively manufactured nickel-based IN718
superalloy to analyze the phase evolution with temperature.
Kong et al. [ 29] fabricated Inconel 718 with different Nb con-
tents via a high-throughput dual-feed system with laser metal
deposition and investigated a dual-feed system and investi-
gated the Laves phase segregation behavior. Weaver et al. [ 30]
demonstrate the high-throughput experiments design and
use of combinatorial samples produced on a commercial laser
powder bed fusion system to study 60 distinct process con-
ditions of nickel superalloy 625.
Recently a new Ni eCo-based superalloy with a service
temperature of over 750
/C14 C has been developed for turbine
disks, turbine blades, and receivers. However, the effect of Nb
on the microstructure in this alloy needs further study. In this
work, the effect of Nb on phase precipitation and micro-
structures of SLM Ni eCo-based superalloys through high-
throughput sample preparation was investigated for a better
understanding of the complex precipitates thermodynamic
and kinetics behavior.

structure and mechanical properties of the Ni eCo-based su-
peralloy. For the optimization of alloy compositions, the
equilibrium phase constituents and weight fraction were
calculated using the Thermo-calc software TTNi8 database.

According to thermodynamic calculation, the Nb gradient
samples of Ni eCo-based superalloys were fabricated by se-

alloy is prepared by vacuum induction melting and then
electroformed to obtain a bar with a diameter of 50 mm and

50 Gas Atomization Equipment. The atomization pressure is
4.0 MPa, the melt mass ﬂow rate is 10 kg/min, and the inner
aperture of the diversion is 4.5 mm. The purity argon of
99.999% is used. The powders were subsequently screened
and graded to obtain the particles of 15 e53 mm. Fig. 2 (a, b)
shows the microstructure of Ni eCo-based pre-alloyed and Nb
powders by aerosolization. The nominal compositions of
NieCo-based pre-alloyed powders are shown in Table 1.
Before selective laser melting the powders were dried in a
vacuum drying chamber and then protected by Ar gas during
the process to reduce the oxygen content in the bin. The
substrate is 316 stainless steel. The scanning strategy is
shown in Fig. 2(c), with laser power of 260 W, scanning speed
of 960 mm/s, scan spacing of 0.09 mm, the layer thickness of
0.03 mm, and energy density of 100.31 J/mm
3 in the process.
The preparation of Nb gradient samples is shown in Fig. 2(d).
After the SLM process, the samples were wire cut from a
316 stainless steel substrate, and some samples were aged
directly at 800
/C14 C for 100 h. The samples were sanded with
320e2000 grit sandpapers, then electrolytically polished in the
solutions of 80 ml CH 3OH and 20 ml H 2SO4 at 20 V for 1.0 A to
remove the surface stress layer, and ﬁnally etched in the so-
lutions of 15g CrO 3,1 0m lH 2SO4, and 150 ml H 3PO4 at 4.5V for
0.1A to make the precipitates clearer. The phase constituents
were performed using a D8 ADVANCE X-ray Diffractometer.
The microstructural examinations were conducted by a JSM-
7800F Scanning electron microscope (SEM) equipped with
Energy Dispersive Spectrometer (EDS), a JSM-8530F Electron
Probe MicroAnalysis (EPMA), and an F20 Transmission Elec-
tron Microscopy (TEM). The size of g
0 phases were calculated
by precipitation software. The mechanical performance speci-
mens with M12 /C2 65 mm were machined after heat treatment
and tested by NCS-NT1000 and NCS-RS6 equipments.
3. Results and discussion
3.1. As-built microstructure
According to the Thermo-calc calculations, the Nb gradient
samples were prepared by SLM. Fig. 3 shows the Nb gradient
samples analyzed by EPMA. The Nb content increases as an
exponential trend, ranging from 1.0% to 6.0%.
The Nb gradient samples during the SLM process involved
complex physicochemical changes that can produce various
types of defects [31,32]. Fig. 4 shows the surface of the samples
obtained by stereo microscopy. Many black dots observed in
the samples are the unfused Nb powders identiﬁed by EPMA,
which increased with the increase of Nb content. A certain
amount of porosity can be also observed due to the sputtering
in the process [ 33,34].

Conversely, as the ratio is lower than 0.85, additional Nb, Ta,

favorable nucleation and precipitation of the h phase. Fig. 12

observed. However, in the 2.0% Nb alloy, the ratio of Al/
(Nb þ Ta þ Ti) is 0.79 (lower than 0.85) and there is still no h
precipitation. This may be the combination of Co and Ti pro-
moting the formation of the g

It has also been reported that the increase of Co content in Ni-
based single-crystal superalloys can effectively suppress the
formation of the h phase and improve the microstructure
stability [40,42e44].

3.4. Mechanical properties
The precipitation of the h phase is deleterious to the micro-
structure stability and mechanical properties, which has been
proved by many authors [ 38,39]. Therefore, the mechanical
properties of 1.0%Nb, 1.5%Nb, and 2.0%Nb alloys in this study
was tested, as shown in Fig. 15 . The tensile samples with
different Nb content were prepared by selective laser melting
and heat treated at 1070
/C14 C for 4h by air cooling and aged at
650 /C14 C for 16h following by air cooling. The yield strength and
tensile strength at room temperature are similar. At high
temperatures, the increase of Nb content in the alloy in-
creases yield strength and tensile strength due to the increase
of g
0 precipitation volume fraction.
4. Conclusion
In this work, the effect of Nb on the microstructures of new
NieCo-based superalloys is investigated using selective laser
melting (SLM) to prepare high throughput gradient samples.
The equilibrium phase constituents were calculated using the
Thermo-calc software TTNi8 database. Based on the micro-
structural analysis, the following conclusions can be drawn.
1) The high throughout Nb gradient alloys in the range of
1.0%e6.0% were prepared by SLM according to Thermo-
calc calculations. The as-built alloys exhibit the appear-
ance of a striped substructure and cellular substructure
with the unfused Nb powders and few porosities in the
matrix.
2) During aging at 800
/C14 C for 100h, the g0 phase uniformly
precipitated in the matrix with a short bar shape. The vol-
ume fraction and size of theg
0 phase reach the maximum at
the 3.0 e3.5% Nb alloys. The h phase with DO 24 structure
starts to precipitate in the 2.2%Nb alloy and becomes from
needle to lumpy with the increase of Nb content.
3) The h phase nucleated from the g
0 phase that is located in
Ti and Nb-rich regions and preferred to grow lengthwise
rather than laterally at the expense of the g0 precipitates for
providing Ti and Nb atoms identiﬁed by EDS, resulting in
the precipitate-free zones around the h phase.
4) No h phase precipitated even if the ratio of Al/(Nb þ Ta þ Ti)
in the 2.0% Nb alloy is lower than 0.85. This may be the high
