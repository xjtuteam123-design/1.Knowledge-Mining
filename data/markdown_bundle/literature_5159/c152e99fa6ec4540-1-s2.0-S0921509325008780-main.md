ABSTRACT
Refractory multi-component alloys (MPEAs) are promising high-temperature structural materials, but the limited 
uniform elongations restrict engineering applications. Here, the mesoscopic-scale chemical composition fluctu -
ations (CCFs) were reported to increase the uniform elongation by 240 % and the fracture elongation by 44 %. 
CCFs effectively impede the dislocations movement and facilitate cross-slip. As strain rises, deformation-induced 
destruction of CCFs promotes the diffusion of Nb and Zr elements, releases local stress, and contributes to the 
multiplication of dislocations to achieve a uniform distribution and frequent cross-slip. Moreover, the local 
strength increasing from element diffusion also retards dislocation motion, providing additional self-hardening 
capacity. The hardening from the destruction of CCFs competes with the softening from cross-slip, thus main -
taining a high work-hardening rate at large strains while increasing ductility. This work demonstrates the unique 
effect of CCFs on the mechanical properties of refractory MPEAs and provides new insights for designing high 
strength-ductility alloys.
1. Introduction
Refractory multi-principal element alloys (MPEAs) consisting mainly 
of more than three refractory elements demonstrate remarkable high- 
temperature strength, exceptional resistance to high-temperature 
creep, and low neutron cross-sections, making them promising candi -
dates for advanced applications in aerospace engines and nuclear plants 
[ 1 , 2 ]. However, their limited room-temperature (RT) ductility restricts 
their practical use in various engineering applications [ 2 , 3 ]. Although 
TiZrHfNb and TiZrVNb MPEAs exhibit comparable RT ductility, their 
uniform elongations are typically below 5 %, due to insufficient sus -
tained work-hardening capacity at high strain levels [ 4 , 5 ].
Improving the uniform elongation of body-centered cubic (BCC) 
MPEAs remains a significant challenge in alloy design to this day. Face- 
centered cubic (FCC) MPEAs, such as CrCoNi and CoMnFeCrNi alloys, 
which maintain a high work-hardening rate due to deformation-induced 
nanotwins, but single-phase BCC MPEAs are unable to activate 
additional deformation mechanisms because of their high stacking fault 
energy [ 6 , 7 ]. In addition, during deformation, the planar slip of screw 
dislocations dominates, and this behavior makes it difficult to achieve 
dislocation multiplication due to the lack of new dislocation sources, 
leading to difficulties in sustaining a high work-hardening rate in 
single-phase BCC MPEAs [ 8 , 9 ].
Introducing heterogeneous structures is an effective strategy to 
enhance both the work-hardening rate and strength of BCC MPEAs [ 10 ,
11 ]. However, while increasing the work-hardening rate, the ductility 
inevitably decreases due to local stress concentrations [ 12 – 14 ]. To break 
this trade-off, local chemical fluctuation structures with minimal lattice 
mismatch have attracted much attention [ 15 , 16 ]. Local chemical fluc -
tuations can retard dislocation movement, leading to dislocation 
multiplication through the activation of multiple slip systems, to 
enhance both the strain-hardening rate and strength [ 9 , 17 ]. Addition -
ally, it promotes the cross-slip of dislocations, thereby increasing frac -
ture elongations [ 18 ]. In recent years, there has been a substantial

3.4. Effects of CCFs on dislocation evolution
To explain the increased work-hardening rate and ductility induced 
by CCFs, the slip traces of Ti35-HET and Ti35-HOM during deformation 
are shown in Fig. 6 (a – d). Both Ti35-HET and Ti35-HOM exhibit straight 
slip lines, indicating the presence of planar slip in both MPEAs [ 29 ]. 
However, wavy slip lines are observed in Ti35-HET at 4 % strain, and 
when the strain is increased to 10 %, there is a significant increase of 
wavy slip lines ( Fig. 6 (a and b)). In contrast, even at 10 % strain, only 
low-density wavy slip lines are observed in Ti35-HOM with predomi -
nantly straight slip lines, indicating that planar slip dominates the 
deformation process ( Fig. 6 (c and d)). This indicates that the CCFs 
promote the transition from planar slip to cross-slip, thereby enhancing 
ductility [ 30 ]. However, frequent cross-slip promotes local stress release 
and reduces dislocation storage, which can increase ductility but 
inevitably leads to a reduction in strain-hardening rate due to softening 
effects [ 31 , 32 ]. Therefore, to further investigate this anomalous 
work-hardening phenomenon, the dislocation structures were charac -
terized using TEM. Fig. 6 (e – k) illustrates the dislocation features of the 
Ti35-HET under different strains. Fig. 6 (e) shows the bright-field (BF) 
STEM image of 2 % pre-strained Ti35-HET, and significant dislocations 
cross-slip are observed without obvious planar slip bands. Unlike brittle 
BCC MPEAs, where straight dislocations are commonly observed, 
Ti35-HET exhibits characteristics of cross-slip even in the early stages of 
deformation [ 19 ]. With the increase of strain to 4 %, massive dislocation 
networks are formed which indicate that dislocation movement is 
obstructed, leading to interactions between dislocations ( Fig. 6 (f)). At a 
strain of 7 %, the planar slip bands are formed ( Fig. 6 (g)). Unlike planar 
slip in refractory MPEAs, which leads to narrow slip bands and rapid 
saturation of dislocation density, the nucleation and sliding of disloca -
tions are observed between slip bands in Ti35-HET ( Fig. 6 (h)) [ 33 ]. 
Therefore, additional work-hardening compensation is provided in stage 
II ( Fig. 3 (b)). At a large strain (10 %), multiple slip systems are activated 
( Fig. 6 (i)). The interaction of dislocations within slip bands of different

Dislocation slip is closely related to chemical heterogeneity. MPEAs 
containing local chemical fluctuation structures exhibit similar dislo -
cation characteristics because the continual slip of dislocations can 
destroy these structures [ 9 , 15 ]. Therefore, the changes in CCFs under 
deformation are investigated ( Fig. 8 ). In the undeformed state, the 
Nb-rich regions are approximately spherical and are surrounded by 
Zr-rich regions, forming CCFs ( Fig. 8 (a)). As strain increases, the 
element-enriched regions are gradually destroyed, and the region 
boundaries become indistinct, exhibiting a phenomenon of diffusion and 
merging, leading to reduced elemental segregation and promoting 
microstructural homogenization ( Fig. 8 (b and c)). At 18 % strain, 
elemental segregation can still be observed, but compared to the un -
deformed state, the scale of these segregations has increased while their 
intensity has significantly decreased ( Fig. 8 (d)). To further confirm the 
reduction in the degree of CCFs, Fig. 9 illustrates the EDS maps of 
Ti35-HET under 6 % and 18 % tensile strains, and after cold rolling by 
80 %. The elemental distribution gradually becomes more uniform with 
the increase of strain, which confirms that deformation promotes the 
diffusion of Zr and Nb from high-concentration regions to 
low-concentration regions respectively, thus reducing the degree of 
CCFs. Furthermore, the EPMA results of the undeformed sample and the 
fractured sample are shown in Fig. 10 (a and b). Compared with the 
undeformed state, the degree of segregation of Nb and Zr decreases 
significantly in the fractured sample. Additionally, a slight segregation 
of V is observed, and its distribution also becomes more uniform with 
deformation. This indicates that deformation promotes the diffusion of 
elements from enriched regions to depleted regions, not only Zr and Nb. 
And the schematic diagrams of the evolution of CCFs and dislocations 
with strain are shown in Fig. 10 (c). Due to the changes in CCFs during 
the deformation process, the anomalous hardening phenomenon in 
stage II can be effectively explained. The destruction of CCFs releases 
interfacial resistance and reduces high local stresses, which facilitates 
dislocation movement. Therefore, dislocation slip between slip bands 
has been observed ( Fig. 6 (h)). The secondary dislocations can prevent 
the rapid saturation of dislocations between slip bands and significantly 
increase the dislocation density. Moreover, due to the low local strength 
in the Nb-rich regions, the diffusion of elements inevitably leads to an 
enhancement of the local strength, thus producing a self-hardening ef -
fect. Furthermore, the true stress continues to increase with deformation

4. Conclusions
In summary, the effect of mesoscopic-scale CCFs on the mechanical 
properties of Ti

Unlike conventional wisdom, mesoscopic-scale CCFs in MPEAs can 
simultaneously increase the strain-hardening rate and ductility. The 
CCFs retard dislocation motion while promoting dislocation cross-slip in 
lower strain. As strain increases, the CCFs are gradually destroyed, 
releasing local stress concentrations and promoting dislocation multi -
plication. This process fully utilizes undeformed regions to promote a 
more uniform distribution of dislocations. With elemental diffusion, 
local strength is enhanced and resistance to dislocation slip gradually 
increases, providing additional work-hardening effects. And the inter -
action of dislocations between multi-slip bands further enhances work- 
hardening capability and promotes frequent cross-slip of dislocations. 
The hardening effect induced by elemental diffusion and the softening 
effect caused by cross-slip compete, maintaining a high work-hardening 
rate while enhancing ductility. As a result, the uniform elongation is 
enhanced by 240 %, and the fracture elongation is improved by 44 %. 
This work demonstrates that mesoscopic-scale CCFs are beneficial to the 
mechanical properties of refractory MPEAs and provides new insights 
for designing strong and ductile alloys.
