abstract
The high-cycle fatigue behavior of Ti46Al8Nb cast alloy with fully lamellar microstructure has been
studied at room temperature, 400 /C14 C, 600 /C14 C and 750 /C14 C. The result shows that temperature has great
inﬂuence on the fatigue strength of Ti46Al8Nb alloy. Compared with that at room temperature, fatigue
strength is signi ﬁcantly reduced at 400 /C14 C. However, at 600 /C14 C the fatigue strength is slightly improved. A
further increasing of temperature up to 750 /C14 C gives rise to larger scatter of fatigue life and produces
a more ﬂat S-N (stress amplitude versus number of cycles to failure) curve. Fractographic observations
indicate that fatigue cracks initiate at the interior casting defects and the lamellar colonies near the
surface. Mixed modes of delamination, translamellar fracture and stepwise fracture are observed on the
fracture surfaces at all temperatures.

1. Introduction
Fatigue characteristics of TiAl-based alloys have been studied
extensively in recent years. This interest has been motivated by the
anticipation of the potential use of these intermetallic alloys for
aerospace and automotive applications that demand certain reli-
ability and damage-tolerance requirements. There are three typical
microstructures of TiAl, i.e., lamellar microstructure, equiaxed g
microstructure and duplex microstructure [1 ,2].A m o n gt h e m ,t h e
fully lamellar microstructure is currently regarded as the most
attractive because the overall balance of properties is more suitable
for high-temperature structure [3,4]. It has been reported that the
fatigue properties of TiAl alloys at high temperature depends on
several factors, such as grain size [5e7],l a m e l l a rs p a c i n g[8],
temperature[7 ,9], environment[9] and alloying additions[10, 1 1]etc.
For example, Recina et al. [5] investigated the low cycle fatigue
properties of Tie48Ale2We0.5Si at 600
/C14 C and found that a duplex
ﬁne grained microstructure had superior properties in comparison
to a coarse grained lamellar structure. Larger isotropic hardening
and smaller Bauschinger effect were responsible for the superior
fatigue resistance of the ﬁne microstructure. Better low cycle
fatigue (LCF) properties of Ti e45Ale2Mne2Nb with ﬁne grained
lamellar microstructure was also found, which was ascribed to
a combination of higher yield strength, lower elastic modulus and
smaller sized lamellar colonies [6].
Chan et al. [7] studied the effect of temperature on the fatigue
life of TiAl alloys. At elevated temperatures the fatigue strength was
reduced compared with that at room temperature, which was
associated with lower yield and ultimate fracture strengths at high
temperatures. However, Christ et al. [9] found that the cyclic life-
time of Ti e47Ale2Nbe2Mn alloy with a duplex microstructure at
temperature ranged from 500 to 750
/C14 C was strongly affected by
the environment. In vacuum the number of cycles to failure
decreases with increasing temperature, while fatigue life increases
in air indicating an abnormal environmental effect [9].
In order to further increase the high temperature strength and
oxidation resistance of TiAl alloys, the third-generation
g-titanium
aluminide with alloying of Nb (5e10 at.% Nb) was developed[12].N b
is an essential additive in gamma TiAl alloys for high-temperature
applications and the Nb content in current TiAl alloys is increasing
[13,14]. Lin et al. have proven that the oxidation behavior of TiAl alloy
is signiﬁcantly improved by Nb addition[15]. In addition to the effect
of Nb on oxidation resistance, it was also found that Nb could
signiﬁcantly increase the high temperature strength of TiAl alloys
[10]. As for fatigue behavior, Kruml et al. [1 1]reported that both TiAl
alloys containing 2 at.% and 7 at.% of Nb with near lamellar micro-
structure had stable cyclic response at 750
/C14 C. Umakoshi [8] indi-
cated that twinning is harmful to the fatigue life of TiAl alloys with
a PST microstruture, because twinning accelerated the formation of
extrusions on the specimen surface. Highly concentrated residual

The barriers for the advance of practical applications of TiAl
alloys include not only the requirement of optimized properties
but also the high-cost of processing. Extrusion and forging have
been used to produce components for engine testing, but the
processing costs are very high. To accelerate the practical appli-
cation of TiAl for automotive application, e.g., engine valves, low-
cost processing technologies for casting should be developed
[16]. Casting offers the most cost-effective routes for the produc-
tion of complex shapes since investment casting is a well-
established process. Conventional gravity casting of this high
melting point material in induction skull melting (ISM) furnace
with the plasma arc melted material shows the defect of inef ﬁcient
ﬁlling. In the present research, the TiAl-based alloy was produced
by centrifugal casting in the vacuum induction furnace using the
CaO crucible, which offers a maximum superheat of approximately
180
/C14 C and much improved ﬁlling over normal cold wall induction
furnace [1 7].
The likely operating temperatures of TiAl-based alloys used as
intake and exhaust valves in automotive engine is 400 /C14 C and
750 /C14 C, respectively. Therefore it is necessary to investigate the
fatigue behaviors of the cast TiAl alloy at these temperatures.
Up to now, there have been only a few studies on the high-
temperature and high-cycle fatigue behavior of cast TiAl alloy
with a fully lamellar structure, especially at 400 /C14 C. In this paper,
the high-temperature fatigue behaviors of the third generation
Ti46Al8Nb alloys with full lamellar microstructure have been
investigated. Fractographic examinations were performed with aim
to understand the role of the microstructure on the fracture process
and fatigue life.
2. Experimental
2.1. Materials preparation
The material used in this study has a nominal composition of
Ti46Al8Nb. The ingot was manufactured by centrifugal casting into
a pancake. The feed stocks including sponge titanium, industrial
pure aluminium and metal niobium were melted in the vacuum
induction furnace using the CaO crucible. After casting, the
pancakes were hot isostatically pressed (HIP) at 1300
/C14 C under
a pressure of 150 MPa for 4 hours to reduce porosity.
2.2. Tension and fatigue testing at high temperatures
Tension and fatigue testing were carried out in an SHIMADZU
servohydraulic test machine (Model EHE-EB10-20L) at RT (room
temperature), 400 /C14 C, 600 /C14 C and 750 /C14 C in air. Schematic draw of
the fatigue and tensile specimen is shown in Fig. 1. The test pieces
were ground in the longitudinal direction with SiC-paper up to
1200 grits. For elevated temperature tests, both the specimen and
grip were enclosed in a resistance wound split furnace. Prior to
tension and fatigue test, the specimens were hold at the desired
temperature for 1 h. During the test, the temperature was
controlled within /C6 1
/C14 C. After the ﬁnal fracture, the specimens
were immediately fan cooled to room temperature to minimize the
oxidation of the fracture surfaces. Fatigue tests were carried out in
a load-controlled mode and the specimens were axially loaded in
triangle waveform with stress ratio of 0 and frequency of 20 Hz.
Fatigue tests were carried out up to 1 /C2 10
7 cycles. For tension tests,
the displacement rate was 0.001 mm/s.
2.3. Fractographic observations
Fractographic observations were performed using scanning
electron microscopy (FEI XL30 SEM). Longitudinal sections through
the fracture surface were also prepared and examined. The longi-
tudinal sections were ground, polished and etched for 5 seconds in
a solution of 10 pct HF, 30 pct HNO
3 and 60 pct H 2O.
3. Results
3.1. Microstructure of the alloy
Optical microscopic examinations reveal that the cast alloy has
a fully lamellar structure as shown in Fig. 2(a). The lamellar colony
sizes vary from tens of micrometers to several hundreds of
micrometers. The microstructure consists alternating layers of
ordered g TiAl (L10 structure) and a2 Ti3Al (D019 structure) layers as
shown in Fig. 2 (b), where the g lamellar and a2 lamellar are indi-
cated as the dark and bright area, respectively. Similar micro-
structure was reported in the literature [18]. These layers are
stacked in a way that the {1 1 1}g plane is parallel to (0001)a2 and the
closely packed direction on {1 1 1}g is parallel to those on (0001) a2
[19].
3.2. Tensile tests at high temperatures
The ultimate tensile strengths (UTS) of Ti46Al8Nb alloy at
different temperatures are listed in Table 1. The tensile tests indi-
cate that the cast Ti46Al8Nb alloy has poor tension ductility at the
temperatures up to 750 /C14 C. With the increase of temperature, the
UTS decreases ﬁrst and then increases slightly. The UTS is the
lowest at 400 /C14 C and is the highest at RT.

temperature. It has been reported that the discontinuous coars-

decomposed to g lamellar after 3000 hours [22]. In the present
investigation, microstructure instability of Ti46Al8Nb alloy at
750
/C14 C was not observed after fatigue tests up to 670,000 cycles as
shown in Fig. 4 (d). Meanwhile, no change of microstructure was
observed at 400 /C14 C and 600 /C14 C as shown in Fig. 4 (b) and (c)
respectively. This result indicates that the fatigued specimens
tested at high-temperature up to 750
/C14 C exhibit negligible micro-
structure change. In addition, the microstructure stability of
Ti46Al8Nb at 750
/C14 C may also be related to the shorter test time

3.5. Fractographic observations
3.5.1. Crack initiation and fracture modes
It is well known that the fatigue life of TiA alloys mainly
depends on crack initiation resistance [24]. The observations of
fatigue fracture surface were carried out with aim to identify the
crack initiation sites and the fracture modes. The fracture surfaces
of fatigue specimens tested at RT, 400 /C14 C, 600 /C14 Ca n d7 5 0 /C14 Ca r e
shown in Figs. 5 e8 respectively. It is clearly that most fatigue
cracks initiated at either the interior casting defects or the coarse
lamellar colonies near the surface with the orientation perpen-
dicular to the loading axis. The crack initiation sites are found to
be same at all temperatures indicating that crack initiation
mechanism does not depend on temperature. Fracture surface
observations also demonstrated that there was no fatigue stria-
tions and rubbing on the fracture surfaces. Multi-crack initiations
were frequently observed on the fracture surfaces at all temper-
atures. Typical examples are present in Fig. 7 and Fig. 8. The multi-
crack initiations may cause a decrease of the crack initiation
resistance.
Detail views of the fracture surfaces of Ti46Al8Nb alloy at
different temperatures are shown in Fig. 9 and Fig. 10. The results
indicate that fatigue fracture occurs by a mixed mode of delami-
nation ( Fig. 9 (a)), translamellar fracture ( Fig. 9 (b)) and stepwise
fracture (Fig. 9(c)). The mixed fracture mode has also been observed
by Malakondaiah et al. [25] for Ti e46Ale2NBe2Cr alloy with fully
lamellar structure at 650
/C14 C and 800 /C14 C. Large amounts of fracture
observations demonstrate that there is no obvious change in the
fracture modes with the increase of temperature.

3.5.2. Crack propagation paths
The crack propagation paths were observed on selected fracture
samples. The results are shown in Figs. 10e12. These results reveal
that there is no obvious plasticity deformation on the fatigue
fracture surface. In most cases, fatigue fracture proceeds with
formation of frequent branches, preferentially along the inter-
lamellar interface as shown in Fig. 10. Additionally, translamellar
cracking can also be observed. In Fig. 1 1, crack-wake ligament was
also formed during the crack propagation process. Similar results
were also reported in the literature [26]. Crack-tip measurements
indicated that the crack-wake ligaments can reduce the near-tip
stress intensity factor and improve fracture resistance if they
remain intact during loading [27]. However Chan et al. [28] has
indicated that these ligaments are ineffective in improving fatigue
crack growth resistance because they are more highly strained and
therefore exhibit a higher propensity to fatigue failure than near-tip
region of the main crack. When a crack reaches a colony boundary it
can grow into the contiguous colony without any dif ﬁculty if the
lamellar orientation between the colonies is similar or aligned in
the same direction. On the other hand, crack propagation can be
retarded at boundary between misoriented lamellar colonies and in
this case propagation along the colony boundary occurs as shown
in Fig. 10 and Fig. 12.
4. Discussion
4.1. Fatigue crack initiation
In the literatures several mechanisms have been proposed to
describe the fatigue crack initiation behavior of TiAl alloys. It have
been reported that the fatigue crack initiation sites for TiAl alloys
include: (i) extrusions [8], (ii) lamellar interfaces [27], (iii) lamellar
colonies [6] and (iv) casting defects [29].
In the present research, the fractographic observations have
demonstrated that there were two kinds of fatigue crack initiation
sites for Ti46Al8Nb alloy, i.e., the lamellar colony, and the casting
defect, as shown in Figs. 5 e8. Recina has concluded that fatigue
cracks initiated at the surface or subsurface near weak spots in the
lamellar microstructure. Such weak spots can be not only defects
such as pores/cavities or surface damage, but also lamellar colonies
oriented perpendicular to the loading direction [5,6]. Kruzic has
pointed out that crack nucleation occurred much more readily in
the lamellar microstructure compared to that of the duplex
microstructure. Crack initiation in the lamellar microstructure
occurrs preferentially at favourably oriented colonies where the
lamellar interfaces are subjected to the highest tensile stress
(interfaces w90
/C14 to the maximum tensile stress direction) or the
highest shear stress (interfaces w 45/C14 to the maximum tensile
stress direction) [30]. Shibata found that for the as-cast TiAl alloy
consisting predominantly of lamellar grains, fatigue cracks were
initiated from cast defects. The size of defects can have great
inﬂuence on the fatigue crack initiation life. Therefore, control of
the defect size is important in order to improve the fatigue life of
Ti46Al8Nb alloy [29].
There is no obvious macro-slip band found on the surface of the
fatigue specimen at present study. These results are consistent
with those reported in the literatures [29]. The classical model of
crack initiation which suggested that the PSB are a pre-
requirement for the formation of a fatigue crack may still be
applicable to the TiAl intermetallics, but on a much smaller scale
corresponding to a tiny cyclic plastic deformation zone [31]. For

properties of the large lamellar colonies. The relatively low resis-

high growth rate for the long fatigue crack suggests that the growth
of small fatigue cracks will be an important component of the total
fatigue life.
4.2. Effect of temperature on fatigue lives
The effect of temperature on fatigue lives of TiAl alloy has not
been fully understood. Some researchers have reported that the
fatigue resistance of TiAl alloy is not signi ﬁcantly degraded at
temperature up to 800
/C14 C [20]. However, this is not the case in the
present study. As shown in Fig. 3 the temperature has signi ﬁcant
inﬂuence on the fatigue live of Ti46Al8Nb alloy. The fatigue
strength is signi ﬁcantly reduced at 400 /C14 C. But the fatigue strength
at 600 /C14 C is slightly higher than that at room temperature. In the
literature, similar results have been reported by Pineau [33] for the
nickel-base superalloy. According to Pineau’s explanations, the high
temperature fatigue life of the nickel-base superalloys can be
determined by the interaction between the slip mode and oxida-
tion. At lower temperatures the microstructure is stable and
oxidation can be ignored. The fatigue property is mainly controlled
by mechanical deformation. At intermediate temperatures, the
microstructure is stable but the environment becomes increasingly
damaging. The high stresses associated with the stable structure
cause cracking of oxidized boundaries or oxide spikes and the life is
reduced. At high temperatures, even though oxidation is more
severe, deformation-assisted precipitate coarsening leads to
reduced stresses, a more damage resistant structure (increased
ductility) and an increase in life.
As for TiAl alloys, it was reported that the fatigue life of TiAl
alloys can be reduced at elevated temperatures compared to that at
ambient temperature, which is ascribed to the lower yield and
ultimate fracture strengths at high temperatures [7]. The reduced
fatigue strength at 400
/C14 C is probably associated with the lowest
UTS at the temperature as shown in Table 1 although the reason for
the lowest UTS is still not clear. At 600 /C14 C, the UTS is lower than that
at RT. Therefore, there must be some other factors to control the
fatigue behavior for Ti46Al8Nb alloy at 600 /C14 C. Cui et al. [21] have
investigated the low cycle fatigue properties of Ti47Al5Nb alloy
with a duplex microstructure at temperature range of 550 e750
/C14 C.
As test temperature increases, the fatigue resistance under small
strain amplitude increases owing to the improved deformability of
alloy matrix. Further increasing of the temperature to 750
/C14 C, larger
scatter of the fatigue lives occurs, which is explained in the
following section.
4.3. Scatter of the fatigue lives
As shown in Fig. 3 large scatter in fatigue lives are observed at all
temperatures with the largest one at 750
/C14 C. This scatter can be
rationalized by the following factors. First, the fatigue property of
the full lamellar TiAl alloy is strongly affected by the lamellar
orientation. The colonies with the lamellar oriented perpendicular
to the loading axis will became the crack initiation area and these
colonies could be treated as the ﬂaw. Second, the feature in the
lamellar microstructure such as lamellar width, direct and colony
size (grain size) are slightly different for different specimens
depending on the location of the ingot. The difference in micro-
structure cannot be avoided in every specimen, which may be
responsible for the scatter of the fatigue strength [34]. An increased
temperature has been suggested to cause the formation of a brittle
surface layer and induce embrittlement of TiAl alloy [35]. The
largest scatter and the lowest fatigue exponent at 750
/C14 C( Fig. 3)a r e
probably associated with this mechanism.
5. Conclusions
The high-cycle fatigue behavior of the Ti46Al8Nb cast alloy with
full lamellar microstructure has been studied at room temperature,
400 /C14 C, 600 /C14 C and 750 /C14 C. The conclusions can be drawn as follows.
