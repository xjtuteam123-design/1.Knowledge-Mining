ABSTRACT
Reducing Re content is a trend in the development of advanced Ni-based single crystal (SX) superalloys. In this 
research, the synergic effects of Co and Ta additions on the microstructural stability and γ / γ’ elemental parti -
tioning behaviors in low Re-containing Ni-based superalloys were investigated, utilizing a high-throughput 
diffusion multiple method after aging at 1100 
◦
C for 1000h, in combination with electron microscopy image 
analysis, quantitative EPMA, and nanoindentation. The results indicated that the microstructure remained stable 
γ / γ’ phases across a relatively broad range of Co and Ta contents. The synergic effect of Co and Ta on the γ’ 
volume fraction was different on the different concentration levels, and higher Co addition would counteract the 
positive effect of Ta addition. Moreover, the increase of Co and Ta contents altered the partitioning behavior of 
elements within the γ / γ’ phases. The partitioning of Re into γ phase was enhanced with appropriate Co and Ta 
contents, resulting in a peak of hardness of γ phase. While, the hardness of γ’ phase continuously increased with 
Ta additions. Therefore, moderate Co and Ta additions may be more suitable for optimizing the composition of 
the investigated low Re-containing Ni-based SX superalloy, considering both the microstructural stability and the 
strength of γ / γ’ phases. This study will be helpful for the accelerated development of low Re-containing Ni-based 
SX superalloys by adjusting the contents of other alloying elements to maximize the benefits of Re.
1. Introduction
Ni-based SX superalloys are broadly accepted for the turbine blades 
of aerospace engines due to their outstanding comprehensive perfor -
mances, which benefit from the large amount of alloying elements [ 1 ]. 
Among these elements, the refractory element Re is recognized for its 
substantial influence on high temperature creep resistance on Ni-based 
SX superalloys, mainly attributed to its enhancement in solid solution 
strengthening and interface strengthening [ 2 , 3 ]. As a result, the Re 
content has been increased to more than 6 wt% in advanced Ni-based SX 
superalloys [ 4 – 6 ]. However, the Re content of the newly developed al -
loys tends to be decreased, due to its negative impacts on the micro -
structural stability, cost and density of Ni-based SX superalloys[ 7 – 9 ]. 
Considering these factors, developing methods for the design and opti -
mization of low Re-containing Ni-based SX superalloys becomes crucial. 
Generally, the most extensively explored methods involve substituting 
Re with other well-known refractory elements, such as W and Mo. Ru 
et al. [ 10 ] has investigated the effect of substituting Mo for Re in the 
equal weight on high temperature stress rupture properties of Ni-based 
SX superalloys, and found that the 1100 
◦
C/137 MPa life keeps almost 
unchanged. Additionally, researchers were also on the lookout for novel 
substitutional elements for Re. For instance, Chen et al. [ 11 ] screened 
the possible Re-substitutional elements through interdiffusion co -
efficients and proposed that Os and Ir are potential substitutes for Re. 
Another, relatively less-explored method, involves adjusting key ele -
ments in the low Re-containing alloys to change the elemental parti -
tioning coefficients, thus maximizing the strengthening effect of Re 
without changing the overall Re content, as well as stabilizing and 
strengthening the γ’ phase. Although Re is mainly distributed in the γ 
matrix, a small amount can also partition into the γ’ phase [ 12 , 13 ]. If as 
much Re as possible is induced to enter the γ matrix through tuning 
other elements, particularly those that stabilize the microstructure and

Co is an essential alloying element in advanced Ni-based SX super -
alloys. However, the appropriate amount of Co in the Ni-based SX su -
peralloys is still ambiguous, for example, the Co contents of the 4th 
generation Ni-based SX superalloys TMS-138 and EPM-102 are changed 
from 5.9 wt% to 16.5 wt% [ 14 , 15 ]. Despite some ongoing debates, many 
studies consider Co as a microstructure stabilizer due to its ability to 
reduce the precipitation tendency of topologically close-packed (TCP) 
phase[ 15 – 17 ]. For example, Shi et al. [ 18 ] revealed that higher Co ad -
ditions reduced the TCP phase precipitation in Ru-containing Ni-based 
SX superalloys. Co has also been found to affect the volume fraction of γ’ 
phase. MacKay et al. [ 19 ] demonstrated that the volume fraction of γ’ 
phase showed a declining tendency as the Co content increased. How -
ever, Tan et al. [ 20 ] illustrated that the γ’ volume fraction maintained 
the same across alloys with varying Co contents. These suggested that 
the specific effects of Co could vary across different alloy systems and 
might be influenced by the synergic effects of other elements. Therefore, 
it is necessary to optimize the Co content during alloy design.
Ta is essential for the development of high-performance Ni-based SX 
superalloys, as it enhances their strength and ductility at elevated 
temperatures, while also improving their resistance to creep and hot 
corrosion [ 21 , 22 ]. Ta primarily concentrates in γ’ phase, significantly 
impacting both the volume fraction and thermal stability of γ’ phase [ 23 ,
24 ]. Wu et al. [ 25 ] noted that the addition of Ta could enhance the 
volume fraction of the γ’ phase. Gao et al. [ 26 ] has also demonstrated 
that Ta addition resulted in the refinement of γ’ precipitates and 
improved the thermal stability of γ’ phase. Moreover, it has been 
discovered that the strong distribution of Ta towards the γ’ phase could 
promote the distribution of Re towards the γ phase [ 27 ]. Although 
previous studies have documented the impact of Ta addition on the 
microstructural stability of superalloys, limited research has been pub -
lished on optimizing the Ta content for alloy design. As a whole, both Co 
and Ta play crucial roles in Ni-based SX superalloys. The synergic effects 
of Co and Ta potentially offer a way to simultaneously remain micro -
structural stability, maximize the benefit of Re and improve the strength 
of γ / γ’ phases, thereby enhancing the high-temperature performance of 
low Re-containing Ni-based SX superalloys.
The diffusion multiple method, as one of a high-throughput experi -
mental methods, can significantly reduce time and costs compared to 
traditional trial-and-error techniques[ 28 – 30 ]. In our previous works, 
this approach had been used to effectively explore the synergic effects of 
two or more elements on the microstructural stability and γ / γ’ elemental 
partitioning behavior of multi-component CoNi-based superalloys [ 31 ,
32 ]. In this work, such approach was utilized to explore the synergic 
effects of Co and Ta on the phase constituents, γ’ volume fraction and 
elemental partitioning behavior of low Re-containing Ni-based super -
alloys after aging at 1100 
◦
C for 1000h, trying to strengthen low 
Re-containing Ni-based superalloys by adjusting the contents of Co and 
Ta. This study is beneficial for the design and optimization of advanced 
Ni-based SX superalloys with low Re content.

2.1. Preparation of the diffusion multiple
The used diffusion couple was composed of two experimental alloys 
with variable amounts of Co and Ta, referred to as the 6Ta8Co alloy and 
10Ta16Co alloy, respectively. The nominal compositions of these two 
alloys were listed in Table 1 . The diffusion couple itself was denoted as 
6Ta8Co – 10Ta16Co, as shown in Fig. 1 . More details on the preparation 
of diffusion multiple can be found in Ref. [ 31 ]. Following a hot isostatic 
pressing (HIP) process at 1200 
◦

◦

diffusion couple was then subjected to a long aging at 1100 
◦

1000h. As a consequence, the alloys with different components were 
effectively bonded and underwent complete diffusion, leading to the 
formation of compositional and microstructural gradients. The region 
signed by red rectangle in Fig. 1 were analyzed in this study.

6Ta8Co – 10Ta16Co diffusion couple were obtained using a JEOL JXA-

EPMA beam spot was chosen to be 1 μ m to analyze the composition of 
the γ and γ’ phases at specific positions. It should be emphasized that the 
tested γ and γ’ phases were much bigger than 1 μ m.

ements such as Re, Ru, W and Mo etc. should be also optimized by using 
different diffusion multiple, to develop novel Ni-based SX superalloys 
with lower Re content.

In this work, the synergic effect of Co and Ta additions on the phase 
constituents, γ’ volume fraction and γ / γ’ elemental partitioning 
behavior in low Re-containing Ni-based superalloys with the normal 
composition range of 6Al-(8 – 16)Co-3.5Cr – 1Mo-3Re-3Ru-(6 – 10)Ta – 5W- 
Bal.Ni (wt%) were investigated by a diffusion couple after aging at 
1100

1) Increasing Co and Ta contents from 8.0 to 6.0 wt% to 16.0 and 10.0 
wt%, respectively, led to the formation of a stable ( γ + γ’ ) two-phase 
microstructure without any secondary phases, suggesting that a wide 
range of Co and Ta contents could be considered in the designing of 
Ni-based SX superalloys.
2) Co addition decreased the γ’ volume fraction, while Ta addition had 
an opposite effect. However, their synergic effect on the γ’ volume 
fraction was different on the different concentration levels, and

3) The additions of Co and Ta slightly changed the γ / γ’ partitioning
