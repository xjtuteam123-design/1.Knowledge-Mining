ABSTRACT
This study prepared MnFeCoNi high-entropy alloys with precipitates and high-density lattice defects (disloca -
tions, stacking faults, deformation twins, etc.) using powder metallurgy combined with deep cryogenic defor -
mation (DCD). The alloy demonstrates exceptional yield strength (1370 ± 56.64 MPa) and ultimate tensile 
strength (1463 ± 74.05 MPa), but exhibits limited elongation (3.18 %). Annealing the alloy at different tem -
peratures enables microstructure evolution, allowing precise control over the morphology, quantity, and dis -
tribution of defects. This achieves an optimal balance between yield strength (1209 ± 30.88 MPa) and 
elongation rate (8.51 %). The results indicate that the outstanding mechanical properties of high entropy alloys 
primarily arise from the synergistic strengthening effect of its nano-spherical σ phase and high-density lattice 
defects, including dislocations, stacking faults, and deformation twins.
1. Introduction
This serendipitous discovery of high entropy alloys (HEAs), typically 
composed of multiple metallic elements in equal or nearly equal pro -
portions, has triggered a surge in alloy design within the materials sci -
ence community [ 1 – 3 ]. The MnFeCoNi HEAs system, characterized by a 
single face-centered cubic (FCC) structure, has garnered significant 
attention for its exceptional strength and ductility at both room and 
cryogenic temperatures [ 4 – 6 ]. The low stacking fault energy (SFE) of 
FCC-phase HEAs facilitates the formation of stacking faults (SF) and 
twins during deformation, thereby enhancing mechanical properties [ 4 ,
7 ]. At 77 K, the yield strength and ultimate tensile strength of the 
MnFeCoNi HEAs system increase by 80 %, accompanied by a significant 
improvement in ductility [ 7 – 9 ]. The MnFeCoNi HEAs system exhibits a 
dislocation density as high as ~1016 m 2 when deformed at an ultra -
low temperature of 15 K. The exceptional combination of strength and 
ductility is attributed to the formation of SFs, twining, and serrations 
during cryogenic deformation [ 7 ]. At low temperatures, the HEA gen -
erates abundant nanotwins, while reduced atomic internal energy sup -
presses dislocation motion, significantly enhancing the material ’ s 
strength and plasticity. However, a single strengthening mechanism 
cannot achieve the ideal balance of strength and ductility. Collaborative 
mechanisms, including precipitation strengthening, grain/twin bound -
ary (GB/TB) strengthening, and heterogeneous deformation-induced 
strengthening, are often employed to enhance FCC alloys [ 5 , 10 – 14 ]. 
Low temperature promotes SF and twinning during the initial stages of 
plastic deformation assisted by pronounced B2-driven hardening. And 
the yield strength, ultimate strength, and tensile ductility of (NiCoCr) 
88Al10Ta2 alloy increase from ~1.1 GPa, 1.4 GPa and 20 % to ~ 1.5 
GPa, 1.8 GPa and 25 %, respectively [ 15 ]. Consequently, 
low-temperature deformation combined with the introduction of pre -
cipitates effectively enhances mechanical properties at room 
temperature.
Building on the aforementioned studies, this paper proposes a 
strategy that integrates multiple strengthening mechanisms to enhance 
the room temperature strength of precipitated FCC phase MnFeCoNi 
HEAs. The HEAs with precipitated phase were synthesized via me -
chanical alloying (MA) and vacuum hot-pressing sintering (HPS), fol -
lowed by annealing to optimize the microstructure and room- 
temperature mechanical properties after introducing high-density de -
fects through DCD. The relationship between microstructure and me -
chanical properties was subsequently established utilizing a universal

2. Experimental method
The high pure metal powder ( ≥ 99.95 wt%) of Mn, Fe, Co, and Ni 
with equal atomic ratio was milled for 20 h in a planetary ball mill under 
an argon atmosphere to produce HEA powders. To prevent severe 
welding of the metal powders, 1 wt% N-heptane was added to the ball 
mill tank. The collected HEA powders were then transferred to a vacuum 
hot-pressing sintering system (HPS, FUJIDEMPA HIGH-MULTI 5000). 
The entire sintering process was conducted under a low vacuum of 10-1 
Pa and cooled with the furnace after holding at 1050 
◦
C and 50 MPa for 
1h. The sintered bulk HEAs were immersed in liquid nitrogen and cooled 
to its temperature before being deformed under a hydraulic press. The 
overall thickness was reduced by 10 %. The deformed HEA samples were 
then annealed at 500 
◦
C, 600 
◦
C, 700 
◦
C, and 800 
◦
C for 30 min, followed 
by water quenching. These samples were labeled A500, A600, A700, and 
A800, respectively. The phases of HEAs were characterized using X-ray 
diffraction (XRD, Rigaku MiniFlex 600, Cu radiation, scan step size of 
0.02
◦
). The microstructure of HEAs was examined using electron back -
scattering diffraction (EBSD). Before analysis, the samples were pre -
pared through mechanical and electrolytic polishing. For transmission 
electron microscopy (TEM) analysis, the samples were wire-cut, me -
chanically polished to 50 μ m, and subsequently thinned using an ion 
beam. Rectangular dog bone specimens for tensile testing had a gauge 
length of 6 mm and a width of 1.5 mm. Uniaxial tensile tests at room 
temperature were performed at an engineering strain rate of 10-3s-1 
using a SHIMADZU AG-Xplus 50 KN tensile testing machine. To 
ensure data reproducibility, each sample was tested three times. Post- 
tensile test fracture surfaces were analyzed using scanning electron 
microscopy (SEM).

The TEM-EDX mapping was conducted on DCD and A600 HEAs to 
analyze the element composition of each phase and evaluate the effect of 
annealing on the elemental distribution in HEA samples, as shown in 
Fig. 2 . In Fig. 2 a, the microstructure of MnFeCoNi HEAs after DCD pri -
marily consists of a matrix phase and nano-scale precipitated phase ( σ 
phase). The nano-scale σ phase improves the alloy ’ s strength via the 
Orowan strengthening mechanism. The elemental distribution within 
the matrix is uniform, with no segregation observed, while the σ phase is 
predominantly enriched in Mn. Notably, C element is detected in the 
alloy. The C element originates from the control agent (n-heptane) used 
during ball milling. C element is primarily concentrated around the 
precipitate phase, as the negative mixing enthalpy of C and Mn elements 
( 66 kJ mol-1) suggests a stronger chemical affinity [ 17 ]. Fig. 2 b pre -
sents the TEM-EDX mapping of A600. The results show that the phase 
composition of the HEA remains unchanged after annealing, confirming 
that DCD followed by annealing at 600 
◦
C does not induce phase 
transformation. In summary, MnFeCoNi HEAs retain their matrix phase 
and σ phase.
Fig. 3 illustrates the microstructure of A600 HEAs. The selected area 
electron diffraction (SAED) pattern of the matrix Fig. 3 a confirms an FCC 
crystal structure. Fig. 3 b depicts the bright field image of the interface 
between the A600 HEA matrix and the σ phase particles. The interface 
between the HEA matrix and the σ phase particles appears well-bonded, 
with no evident defects such as voids. High resolution transmission 
electron microscopy (HRTEM) was used to analyze the interface be -
tween the HEA matrix and σ phase particles, as shown in Fig. 3 c. Fast 
Fourier Transform (FFT) analysis in Fig. 3 c reveals that the σ phase 
possesses an FCC structure with a lattice spacing of 0.229 nm. Addi -
tionally, the inverse fast Fourier transform (IFFT) image in Fig. 3 d in -
dicates significant lattice distortions and dislocations in the HEA matrix, 
σ phase grains and the transition zone between the HEA matrix and the σ 
phase particles.
Fig. 4 presents the grain orientation, recrystallization map, and 
kernel average misorientation (KAM) of MnFeCoNi HEAs. Following 
DCD subsequent annealing, the grain orientations of MnFeCoNi HEAs 
display a random distribution without significant texture formation 
( Fig. 4 a – e). The HEAs progressively transform from deformed (dis -
torted) grains to newly formed equiaxed grains. The recrystallization 
maps of MnFeCoNi HEAs, shown in Fig. 4a1-e1 , primarily consist of 
recrystallization (blue), sub-structured (yellow), and deformed grains 
(red). After annealing, a large number of substructures and recrystal -
lized grains appear in the MnFeCoNi HEAs. Furthermore, the number of 
recrystallized grains increases with the rise in annealing temperature. 
The annealed MnFeCoNi HEAs primarily consist of sub-structured and 
recrystallized grains, which reduce work hardening and enhance plas -
ticity to some extent [ 16 ]. Fig. 4a2-e2 shows the KAM of MnFeCoNi 
HEAs. As the annealing temperature increases, the KAM decreases, as 
observed in Fig. 4 . This decrease can be attributed to the retention of 
numerous residual dislocations at a low annealing temperature of 500

As the annealing temperature increases, the amount of precipitated σ

significantly increases the defect density in the matrix, creating nucle -

impedes dislocation slip during plastic deformation, forming dislocation 
cells around the particles. This leads to stress concentration at the 
σ -phase/matrix interface, promoting crack initiation [ 29 , 36 – 38 ]. With 
increasing strain, cracks propagate and eventually lead to fracture. 
Although σ -phase precipitation enhances the strength of HEAs, it also 
increases grain boundary brittleness, making crack initiation during 
plastic deformation more likely, a trade-off that remains challenging to 
resolve.
4. Conclusion
In summary, MnFeCoNi HEAs were prepared by powder metallurgy, 
and then subjected to DCD and annealing treatment. Precipitated phase 
and high-density lattice defects (such as dislocation, SFs, and nanotwins) 
were introduced into the HEAs. The mechanical properties, phase, grain 
size, dislocation density, and the interface between σ phase and matrix 
of HEAs were characterized. Some of the main conclusions are drawn as 
follows: 
(1) The mechanical properties of MnFeCoNi HEAs at room temper -
ature are seriously dependent on the annealing temperature. The 
yield strength and ultimate tensile strength of HEAs decrease 
from 1370 ± 56.64 MPa and 1463 ± 74.05 MPa to 1209 ± 30.88 
MPa and 1361 ± 20.72 MPa, respectively, after optimized 
annealing at 700 
◦
C for 30 min, while the elongation increases 
from 3.18 ± 0.54 % to 8.51 ± 1.10 %, achieving excellent syn -
ergistic properties of strength and ductility.
(2) The high-density lattice defects caused by DCD, such as GND, 
nanotwins and SFs, help to greatly improve the strength of 
MnFeCoNi HEAs. GND can improve the strength of the material 
by dislocation hardening. Nanotwins provide additional TB, 
which hinders dislocation movement, aggregates dislocations, 
and promotes the diffusion of defects. The SF-dislocation inter -
action promotes the storage of dislocations, reduces the average 
free path of moving dislocations, and promotes dislocation 
accumulation.
(3) Annealing treatment can adjust the number of lattice defects and 
σ phase, improve the plasticity of MnFeCoNi HEAs, and regulate 
the strain hardening behavior. MnFeCoNi HEAs with excellent 
strength and toughness (1209 MPa 、 8.51 %) can be obtained by 
selecting the appropriate annealing temperature (700 
◦
C).
