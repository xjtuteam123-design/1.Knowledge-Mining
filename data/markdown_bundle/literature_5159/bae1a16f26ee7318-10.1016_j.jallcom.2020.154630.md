abstract
ZrO2/Mo alloy bars are fabricated by hydrothermal method and powder metallurgy process, with their
microstructures, density, hardness, strength, reduction of area and elongation under ambient tempera-
ture investigated experimentally. The high temperature deformation stress of these alloy bars are tested
through thermomechanical simulator. Results show that the second phase (ZrO
2) in the microstructure
was ﬁne. As addition amount of the second phase increases, the microstructures of the matrix are
reﬁned, the density decreased while the hardness and strength are enhanced. Under high temperature,
the second phase in ZrO 2/Mo alloy bars can improve compressive stress of the alloy effectively by ﬁne-
grain strengthening and intra-granular and grain-boundary strengthening. At 1400 /C14 C, the deformation
stress of Mo alloy bars with 1.5% ZrO 2 can be 20% higher than that of pure molybdenum. The
strengthening effect of the second phase at high temperature are analyzed. The increase of strengthening
effect is actually the manifestation of the pinning effect on hindering recrystallization behavior. It is
shown that the improvement of mechanical properties of alloys at elevated temperatures should be
mainly attributed to the retardation of recrystallization by the second phase.

Molybdenum (Mo) is a refractory metal that keeps its body-
centred cubic (bcc) crystal structure from room temperature all
the way to its high melting temperature of 2610 /C14 C. Molybdenum
and its alloy bars possess high-temperature strength are widely
used as heating elements and structural parts in high-temperature
furnaces [ 1e3]. Through bending formation technology, molybde-
num bars can be fabricated into heating elements of various shapes
as required [ 4e6]. In addition, molybdenum and its alloy bars can
also be used as billets for manufacturing ion-implanted parts, high-
temperature tools, all kinds of small molybdenum crucibles, the
parts, molds and various structural components of molybdenum
electric light source product. But the recrystallization temperature
of molybdenum was lower than its neighboring metals, which has
limited its applications and processing performance of molybde-
num above 1000 /C14 C[ 7,8]. The elements or oxides are usually added
to improve the high-temperature strength of molybdenum alloys.
Recently, China has also initiated related research actively, as a
result of which, well-known varieties including Mo eLa2O3 alloys,
MoeAl2O3 alloys, and recently released ZrO 2/Mo alloys have been
developed. By means of hydrothermal method, the ultra- ﬁne
ceramic powder without agglomeration but in good crystalliza-
tion was prepared and then added into the precursor through
liquid-liquid method, so that the dispersion distribution of ZrO 2
was achieved. The ﬁnally prepared alloy, with ﬁne and dispersed
ZrO2 particles, can realize excellent strengthening effect. However,
researches about alloy performances still lacks, thus is of great
signiﬁcance and should be given more attentionc [ 9e11]. Studies
have shown that the ZrO 2 particles distributed at grain boundaries
and inside the grains. During sintering, the Mo grains grow. ZrO 2
particles could take a pinning effect, i.e., delay the recrystallization
progress through pinning effect of the second phase, and also
enhance high-temperature mechanical properties of the alloy
[3,14]. These are all bene ﬁcial for enhancing hightemperature
tensile stress and plasticity of the alloy.

We have studied the performances of sintered alloy compact
and the plates previously, thus focused on the room and high
temperature mechanical properties of the alloy bars in this paper
[3,12e14]. The studies were shown that the second-phase particles
can reﬁne the grains by promoting grain nucleation and hindering
grain growth. The re ﬁned microstructure will result in not only a
signiﬁcant increase in strength, but also abundant grain boundary
areas that can drive down the concentration of deleterious solutes
in the lattice [ 9]. Zirconia particles are uniform and ﬁne which
prepared by hydro-thermal synthesis [ 15e19]. The second-phase
particles in the alloys, has very important in ﬂuence on the me-
chanical properties of the alloys. Subjecting to certain deformation
processing, molybdenum and its alloy bars exhibit high density,
increased compactness and strength. However, low corrosion
resistance and expansion coefﬁcient of these alloy bars have limited
their usages as nozzles, bolts, supports, etc. Under corrosive con-
ditions [ 7e12]. Thus, the alloy usually requires speci ﬁc room and
high-temperature strength. To study the performances of alloy
bars, their mechanical properties were investigated under ambient
and high temperature respectively. In this paper, the high tem-
perature mechanical properties of the sintered compacts were
tested. The effect of the second phase on the microstructure and
mechanical properties of the alloys was investigated.
2. Material and methods
The precursors of Mo and ZrO
2 powder were fabricated by hy-
drothermal method respectively and then mixed well by stirring in
liquid environment. The composite powders for the alloy were
obtained by two-step reduction routine. ZrO 2/Mo composites were
subjected to powder metallurgy process. The pressure value was
280 MPa, holding 5 min (by cold andstatic pressing (CIP)). The
samples were sintered at 2000 /C14 C under H 2 atmosphere [20]. After
sintering, the diameter of the molybdenum rod was about Ø20 mm,
and the forged surface was polished to obtain a bar of Ø8 mm (hot
die forging, 1000 /C14 C). The composition of the alloys given in Table 1.
The microstructure of the bar was observed by Scanning Electron
Microscope (SEM), with the elemental distribution characterized
through Energy Dispersive Spectrometer (EDS). Room temperature
properties of the bars were also tested. Microhardness was
measured by microhardness tester. Samples were measured 20
times. The tensile specimen at room temperature is shown in Fig. 1.
The bar was processed axially into the size of Ø8 mm /C2 12 mm. The
stress and strain of the material at high temperature were simu-
lated with Thermal-mechanical simulator (Gleeble 1500D). Fig. 2 is
the structural scheme of experimental machine. During thermal
simulation process, a pure tungsten indenter of Ø20 mm /C2 40 mm
is used because the deformation resistance of molybdenum alloy is
high at high temperature. To avoid bulging problem during
compression, it is necessary to reduce the end face friction as much
as possible.
The experiment was carried out under vacuum to avoid end face
oxidation. When the indenter is compressing the sample, instan-
taneous cross sectional area of the sample was calculated auto-
matically, then force signal value was divided by it to obtain true
stress. The test machine records the true stress automatically, and
analyzes the relationship between true stress and true strain. The
sample size was Ø8mm /C2 12 mm. The experimental temperature
ranged from 1000 /C14 C to 1400 /C14 C at intervals of 100 /C14 C. The strain
rate was set as 0.1 s /C0 1. The experimental environment of experi-
ment was high vacuum.
Scanning electron microscope (VEGA3-SBH) and Octane Prime
spectrometer were employed to observe the structure of the bar.
The microstructure of the alloy was also characterized using a JEM-
2100 high-resolution transmission microscope. The material den-
sity was measured through Archimedes drainage method. Room
temperature properties were tested by AG-I 250 KN precision
universal material testing machine. Samples were measured 10
times.
3. Results
3.1. Microstructures of molybdenum alloy bars
The microstructure of sintered bar prepared from molybdenum
alloy is shown in Fig. 3 . No.1 presents refers to the sample of pure
molybdenum, while No.2 e5 correspond to samples with ZrO 2
addition amount of 0.1%, 0.5%, 1.0%, and 1.5%, respectively. EDS
ﬁgures show that the grains in the bar are irregular. It is con ﬁrmed
that the grain size of the alloy tends to decrease as the addition
amount of ZrO2 increases. The drag effect imposed by ZrO2 particles
hinders the grain-boundary migration to some extent, and the
eventual grain size is several micrometres. Relevant studies have
demonstrated that the second-phase particles added in the alloy
can promote grain nucleation and hinder grain growth, thus
reﬁning the grains [ 12e14,20e22]. The research about molybde-
num alloys doped with lanthanum oxide has shown that ﬁne grains
can improve hardness, strength and toughness of the alloy [ 9,21].
With further re ﬁnement of grain size, the strengthening effect of
grain boundary is enhanced. The dispersive distribution of nano-
oxide particles in the grain can hinder dislocation movement
effectively and improve the mechanical properties of molybdenum
alloys [3,20e23].

phase in ZrO
2/Mo alloy. It is demonstrated that the second phase
ZrO2 is precipitated and well dispersed in the alloy matrix, and the

It can be seen from Fig. 5(a) that a portion of ZrO 2 particles are
ﬁrmly embedded in the grain boundaries, and the deformation
resistance of the alloy is enhanced by this pinning action. Besides,
ZrO2 particles are also found inside the grains. As shown in Fig. 5(b),
ZrO2 particles appear in the molybdenum matrix, and there is a
circle of dislocation loops around them. Based on Orovan mecha-
nism, if the moving dislocation encounters non-deformable ZrO 2
particles, it would swerve due to the hindrance of ZrO 2 particles. As
the external force increases, the bending becomes more and more
severe to the extent that the dislocation line around ZrO 2 particles
meet again on the other side of ZrO 2 particles. Hence, positive and
negative dislocations cancel out, and a dislocation loop surround-
ing ZrO 2 particles is formed and kept. The other part of the dislo-
cations pass ZrO 2 particles and move forward. To bypass ZrO 2
particles, the dislocations need to overcome both the hindrance of
ZrO
2 particles and the reverse force of the dislocation ring to the

3.2. Room temperature mechanical properties of molybdenum alloy
bars
Fig. 6 shows that as ZrO2 content increases, the density of ZrO2/Mo
alloy bars gradually decreases. After forging the sintered compact of
the alloy, the compactness of the alloy increases. In the case of pure
molybdenum, its density is higher than that of the sintered compact
and reaches 10.07 g/cm3. Due to the limited deformation, the density
of the bar is between the plate and the sintered compact.
The hardness of ZrO 2/Mo alloy bars shown in Fig. 7 is between
those of the sintered compact and the plate. The hardness of ZrO 2/
Mo alloy bars with ZrO 2 addition exceeds that of pure molybde-
num, and ascends gradually with increasing ZrO 2 content. When
the addition amount reaches 1.5%, the hardness of the alloy bar is
enhanced by 12%.
Fig. 8 illustrates tensile strength and yield strength of molyb-
denum alloy bar under room temperature. It can be found that as
the addition amount of ZrO 2 rises, tensile strength and yield
strength of the bar go up signi ﬁcantly and this enhancement effect
becomes more obvious. With an addition amount of 1.5%, the
tensile and yield strength of the alloy bar is improved by 40% and
70%, respectively.

3.3. High temperature deformation resistance of molybdenum alloy
bar
Maximum deformation stress of the alloy bar is tested. As
shown in Fig. 10, the stress required for alloy deformation decreases
with ascending temperature. Due to the limited heating time, the
grain of the alloy undergoes a recovery process according to high-
temperature tensile analysis during the process from 1000 /C14 Ct o
110 0/C14 C, and the strength of the alloy decreases slightly. When the
temperature rises to 1200 /C14 C, partial grains inside the bar begin to
recrystallize, and the stress required for alloy deformation con-
tinues to decrease [ 3,23]. However, the temperature reaches
140 0/C14 C or higher, most of the grains inside the alloy have recrys-
tallized, and the stress for alloy deformation continues to drop [ 12].
As shown, maximum compressive stress at each temperature
ascends with increasing addition amount of ZrO 2, indicating that
the added ZrO 2 exhibits a good strengthening effect, while the
second phase strengthening can improve high-temperature
deformation resistance of the alloy effectively. At 1400 /C14 C, the
compressive stress of the alloy bar can be more than 1.2 times that
of pure molybdenum. After doping with ZrO 2, the grains in mo-
lybdenum matrix become tinier, and the number of Mo matrix
grains per unit volume rises. If the deformation value was constant,
the deformation would occur in more grains, and the grain defor-
mation be more uniform [ 9,22]. Besides, plentiful small-sized Mo
crystals can also reduce dislocation and crack density, the possi-
bility of cracking caused by stress concentration, and the initiation
of cracks [ 9,21,22]. Moreover, since numerous ﬁne ZrO 2 particles

4. Discussion
In order to study the strengthening effect of ZrO 2 particles, the
strengthening effect of the second phase is converted as follows:
m¼(S1eS2)/ S1
where m is the strengthening effect of second phase, S1 refers to
maximum stress of the alloy and S2 indicates maximum stress of
pure molybdenum.
Research shows that the dislocations move relatively rapidly
and the strength of molybdenum particles decreases at high
temperature. The grain boundary strength and grain strength
decrease at the same time. But the atomic migration rate at the
defect is faster, so the decrease rate of grain boundary strength is
much higher than that of grain strength. As an inorganic material,
Zirconia has good high-temperature strength. The temperature
have little in ﬂuence on strength of Zirconia. So it is necessary to
study the strengthening effect of second phase in alloy. Through
this, we can effectively analyze the change of strengthening effect
with temperature [ 21,22,25].
From Fig. 1 1, it can be seen that the strengthening effect of the
second phase increases with rising temperature, but does not
change much before 1200
/C14 C. The enhancement effect increases
dramatically after 1200 /C14 C. The recrystallization temperature of the
alloy is 1200 /C14 C. When the temperature reaches 1200 /C14 C, the matrix
begins to recrystallize. At this moment, the pinning effect of the
second phase not only improves mechanical properties of the
matrix, but also hinders the recrystallization behavior of the alloy,

as a result of which, the reinforcing effect of the alloy is enhanced.
With the increase of the addition amount, the hindrance effect is
more obvious, and the strengthening effect is also more signi ﬁcant.
The increase in strengthening effect is actually a manifestation of
the effect of pinning on hindering recrystallization behavior. It is
indicated that the improvement of mechanical properties of alloys
at elevated temperatures is mainly due to the retardation of
recrystallization by the second phase. This provides a direction for
future research of superalloys.
The micro-morphologies of pure molybdenum and ZrO 2/Mo
alloys are compared after high-temperature tests. Fig. 12 shows the
microstructure of pure molybdenum after compression test. The
plasticity of sample is poor at 1000 /C14 C and there can be seen a lot of
cracks on the cross section. With elevated temperature, the plas-
ticity of matrix is improved, and the cracks have been reduced
signiﬁcantly. The plasticity decreases with further increase of the
temperature. A large number of cracks appear under 1300 /C14 C, and
the plasticity decreases violently. When the temperature reaches
140 0/C14 C, the length and width of crack increase. As the temperature
continues to rise up, cracks become numerous and larger
eventually.
The microstructure of ZrO2/Mo alloys after compression test has
been observed. Fig. 13 shows a few cracks. Under 1000 /C14 C and
110 0/C14 C, the length of cracks is short and the number of cracks is
small. Many small holes can be seen in the ﬁgure. Above 1200 /C14 C,
the cracks begin to appear. The alloy plasticity has been greatly
improved.
This is because the grain re ﬁning effect by ZrO 2 particles can
improve the slip system of grain boundaries and dislocation. There
can be seen few cracks in the alloy at high temperature. The alloy
show a superior plasticity. Considering the strength and properties
of alloy, it can be deduced that adding ZrO 2 particles at high
temperature can reduce crack growth, re ﬁne grain size and delay
crack formation, thus improving the strength and plasticity of the
alloy. It is found that ZrO 2 in the alloy can effectively prevent
dislocation and crack growth. Speci ﬁcally, the high strength is ex-
pected from the grains and nanoscale particles ( Fig. 4 ), and the
excellent ductility is associated with intragranular ZrO 2 nano-
particles that help with the generation and storage of dislocations
without initiating cracks localized at grain boundaries ( Fig. 5 )
[9,26,27].
The microstructures after the experiment are observed and
shown in Fig. 14(a). There are a second particle in the middle of the
picture and many dislocations around it as the arrow points to. The
detachment of the second phase particles from the matrix is also
shown. In the experiment, with the deformation increasing, the
number of dislocations increases. When the number of dislocations
reaches a certain value, the second phase particle is separated from
the matrix. It can impede dislocation movement and result in the
strengthening effect. In Fig. 14 (b), there is a crack between the
second phase and the matrix. During high-temperature creep tests
of alloys, micro-cracks form and grow with the proliferation, slip-
page and accumulation of dislocation. TEM observation shows that
crack extension is mainly controlled by the eutecticums of intro-
granular structures with nano-micron ZrO 2 phases, resulting in
the existence of strong toughening mechanism from crack de ﬂec-
tion during fracture process of the alloy.
5. Conclusions
The second phases in ZrO 2/Mo alloy bars are ﬁne, and the mi-
crostructures of the alloy bar are re ﬁned as the addition amount
increases. The second phase in ZrO 2/Mo alloy bars at high tem-
perature can improve the high-temperature compressive stress of
