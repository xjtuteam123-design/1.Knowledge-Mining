ABSTRACT 
CoCrNi medium entropy alloy (MEA) has potential applications in cryogenic environment due to its strength and 
plasticity enhancement at low temperatures. However, the poor strength at the room temperature limits its 
widespread application. In this work, the alloying element, Tantalum (Ta), was chosen to strengthen CoCrNi 
MEA and CoCrNiTa
x 
MEAs with different Ta content were designed and prepared. The effect of Ta content on the 
microstructure and mechanical properties of the corresponding alloys were investigated. It was found that Ta- 
enriched Laves phase was formed due to the addition of Ta element. The alloy ’ s microstructure experienced 
from hypoeutectic to eutectic, and then to hypereutectic morphology evolution with increasing Ta addition. 
CoCrNiTa
0.4 
alloy was a fully eutectic microstructure with fine lamellar colonies. Its yield strength reached about 
2150 MPa, which was greatly improved compared with CoCrNi MEA. In order to understand the strengthen 
mechanism of CoCrNiTa
x 
MEAs, strength contribution of the alloy ’ s individual phase to the lamellar colonies was 
investigated by the high-throughput nanoindentation mapping technique. It is indicated that Laves phase had 
very high hardness increasing from ≈ 16.12 GPa to ≈ 18.14 GPa, almost 3 times than that of FCC phase ( ≈ 6.03 
GPa). The strengthening of CoCrNiTa
x 
MEAs should be attributed to the production of the hard Laves phase and 
lamellar colonies. 
1. Introduction 
CoCrNi medium entropy alloy (MEA), firstly reported by Gludovatz 
et al., in 2016, had excellent cryogenic mechanical properties with 
simultaneously enhanced in strength and plasticity [ 1 ]. CoCrNi MEA 
solved the problem of plasticity reduction of traditional alloys at cryo -
genic environment. However, its yield strength at room temperature is 
poor [ 1 ], which hinders its further engineering applications [ 2 – 5 ]. 
Therefore, improving its yield strength at room temperature is of great 
significance to broaden its further engineering application [ 2 , 5 ]. 
Alloying was an effective method to improve metals ’ strength at 
room temperature [ 2 , 6 – 10 ]. This method was widely used for structural 
materials, such as aluminum alloys, copper alloys, and magnesium al -
loys, etc. [ 6 , 11 – 13 ]. Some alloying elements, such as W, Mo, Al, and Si, 
etc., have also been chosen to improve the yield strength at room 
temperature of CoCrNi MEA [ 2 – 5 , 14 – 16 ]. Adding 3 at.% W into CoCrNi 
MEA would improve the yield strength by ~ 33% and maintained high 
work hardening ability [ 5 ]. The addition of the alloying element Al 
could form a multi-phase hierarchical microstructure, resulting in an 
effective increase in the yield strength of CoCrNi MEA [ 17 , 18 ]. In 
particular, when some refractory alloying elements were added into 
CoCrNi MEA, a colony lamellar would be formed with excellent ad -
vantages in balancing the strength and plasticity, which is named 
eutectic medium entropy alloy (EMEAs). The addition of refractory 
element Nb or Mo to CoCrNi MEA has been documented to form a colony 
lamellar and significantly improve the properties of CoCrNi MEA [ 2 , 14 ]. 
Tantalum (Ta) element is well-known to have a larger atomic radius 
and more negative mixing enthalpy than Co, Cr, and Ni elements [ 8 ]. 
The large atomic radius can form lattice distortion to strengthen alloy.

2. Experimental details 
2.1. MEAs preparations 
The nominal compositions of CoCrNiTa
x 
(x = 0.2, 0.4, 0.6 and 0.8 in 
molar ratio) MEAs were prepared by arc melting method. The purity of 
raw materials, Co, Cr, Ni, and Ta, was more than 99.95 wt%. The ingots 
were re-melted at least 5 times and turned over before each melting to 
ensure the uniformity of chemical composition. In order to minimize the 
melting loss of raw materials, the melting current was maintained at 
230 – 350 A. At the last melting, the melting current must be reduced 
gradually and kept at 50 – 80 A to eliminate the internal stress caused by 
rapid cooling. Then, the tested samples were processed by wire electric 
discharge machine, and their surface were further mechanically pol -
ished by diamond grinding (down to 1 μ m) and Al

3 
suspension (down 
to 0.04 μ m) for subsequent performance tests.

phase decreased from ≈ 3.61 Å to ≈ 3.52 Å, which was because Ta 
element dissolved into the FCC phase beyond the scope of maximum 
solution limit [ 23 ].

x 
MEAs

formed after addition of Ta into the CoCrNi MEA and their microstruc -
ture in the CoCrNiTa
x 
MEAs was influenced by different Ta content, 
which correspondingly affected the mechanical strength of CoCrNiTa
x 
MEAs (as shown in Fig. 5 a). Especially for the CoCrNiTa
0.4 
EMEA, it 
exhibited high strength combined with relatively large plasticity, indi -

strengthening of CoCrNiTa

MEA should be attributed to solid solution 
strengthening of Ta solution into FCC phase and second phase 
strengthening by precipitated hard Laves phase.

the individual phase to the lamellar colonies of CoCrNiTa

MEAs. 
The mappings of the nanoindentations in Fig. 7 are capable to

different morphology. The linkages between the microstructural fea -
tures and their corresponding properties of Young ’ s modulus and 
hardness were set through the thousands of indentations in the specific 
area of interest.

4. Conclusion 
In this research, alloying element Ta was chosen to add into CoCrNi 
MEA, and CoCrNiTa
x 
MEAs were designed and prepared to improve the 
yield strength of CoCrNi MEA. The effect of Ta content on the micro -
structure and mechanical properties of the corresponding alloys were 
investigated. Further, a revolutionary technique, high-throughput 
nanoindentation mapping was employed to analyze the strengthening 
mechanism on the nanoscale. Based on the obtained results and corre -
sponding analysis, the conclusions can be drawn as follows. 
(1) With increasing Ta content, the volume fraction of Laves phase 
increased gradually. The micromorphology experienced the 
transition from the hypoeutectic (x < 0.4) to the eutectic (x = 0.4) 
and finally to the hypereutectic structure (x > 0.4) in turn. 
(2) The yield strength and hardness were significantly improved by 
Ta alloying. CoCrNiTa
0.4 
EMEA had the greatest comprehensive 
performance. The largest hardness of ≈ 791.6 HV was obtained in 
CoCrNiTa
0.8 
MEA. 
(3) The Laves phase had highest hardness, which was almost ≈ 3 
times than that of FCC phase ( ≈ 6.03 GPa) according to the high- 
throughput nanoindentation mappings technology. The hardness 
of lamellar colonies had the medium hardness compared with 
FCC and Laves phases. 
(4) The strengthening mechanism of CoCrNi MEA includes solid so -
lution strengthening and second phase strengthening.
