1. Introduction
Intermetallic γ -TiAl alloys exhibit a favorable combination of char -
acteristics, including low density, high specific strength, specific stiff -
ness, and resistance to oxidation and creep [ 1 , 2 ]. These attributes render 
them promising lightweight structural materials for high-temperature 
applications, with the potential to replace Ni-based superalloys. Over 
the past two decades, various TiAl alloys have been effectively employed 
in low-pressure turbine blades (LPTB) for aero-engines, such as the cast 
Ti-48Al-2Cr-2Nb alloy (4822, at.%) used in the GEnx-1B engine and the 
wrought Ti-43Al-4Nb-1Mo-0.1B alloy (TNM, at.%) utilized in the GTF 
engine [ 3 , 4 ]. However, the demanding operational conditions faced by 
TiAl components, characterized by prolonged exposure to high tem -
peratures, stress, and oxidation, pose significant challenges to their 
broader adoption in the aerospace industry. Therefore, addressing the 
need to enhance the high-temperature comprehensive performance of 
TiAl alloys is a pressing issue that warrants further attention.
Composite technology represents a promising strategy to address the 
limitations of TiAl alloys by leveraging the advantages of both the ma -
trix and the reinforcement [ 5 – 7 ]. Research has demonstrated that TiAl

4. Conclusions
The influence of heat treatment on the microstructure evolution, 
high-temperature mechanical properties, and creep behavior of the as- 
cast Y
2
O
3
-bearing TiAl alloy was investigated in detail. The main con -
clusions can be summarized as follows: 
1. During heat treatment in the ( α + γ ) dual-phase field, micron-sized 
Y
2
O
3 
particles distributed within the lamellae serve as heteroge -
neous nucleation sites for the γ phase, thereby promoting the for -
mation of blocky γ phase through a discontinuous coarsening 
mechanism. The combination of elevated temperature conditions 
and an increase in crystal defects promotes the precipitation of Y
2
O
3 
nanoparticles, leading to the highest quantity of nanoscale re -
inforcements within the FL microstructure.
2. As the ratio of blocky γ phase to α
2
/ γ lamellae decreases, the high- 
temperature tensile strength tested at 800 
◦
C and the creep life 
tested at 800 
◦
C and 325 MPa gradually increase, while the high- 
temperature elongation and creep strain initially increase before 
subsequently decreasing. The HT3 sample, characterized by a nearly 
lamellar microstructure, exhibits the best high-temperature elonga -
tion and creep strain, whereas the HT4 sample with a fully lamellar 
microstructure demonstrates the highest high-temperature strength 
and creep life.
3. The excellent high-temperature elongation and creep strain observed 
in the HT3 sample can be attributed to the cooperative deformation 
of its lamellar structure in conjunction with the surrounding blocky γ 
phase. In contrast, the superior high-temperature strength and creep 
resistance of the HT4 sample result from the synergistic strength -
ening effect arising from a higher density of nanoscale Y
2
O
3 
pre -
cipitates and the greatest content of lamellar colonies, which 
effectively impede dislocation movement.
