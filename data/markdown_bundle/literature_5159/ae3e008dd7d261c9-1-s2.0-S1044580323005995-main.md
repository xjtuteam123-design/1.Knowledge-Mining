ABSTRACT 
The effects of Nb alloying on microstructural evolution and mechanical performance of a newly developed solid- 
solution strengthened Fe-Ni-based superalloy, considered as the candidate material for high-temperature com -
ponents manufacturing in fourth Generation (Gen IV) advanced nuclear reactor, were systematically investi -
gated. The results show that the increase in the Nb content obviously increased the volume fraction and average 
size of MC carbides, and the MC carbides more preferentially distributed at grain boundaries. The average grain 
size and low Σ coincident site lattice (CSL) boundary exhibited a gradually decreased trend with increasing Nb 
content. Meanwhile, the thermal stability (grain size and grain boundary precipitation) of the alloy can be 
improved notably via Nb alloying. The tensile strength of Nb-alloyed alloys at 750 
◦
C was enhanced significantly 
compared to that of Nb-free alloy, mainly due to the synergistic effect of solid-solution strengthening and fine 
grain strengthening. It was impressive that the variation of Nb content had no obvious influence on the yield 
strength, but gradually decreased the ultimate tensile strength. The detrimental effects of the decrease in Σ 3 
boundaries and intergranular precipitates were assumed to restrict the strengthening factors. The ductility of Nb- 
alloyed alloys decreased remarkably due to the changed dominant deformation mechanism from planar slip to 
wavy slip; however, it showed an increasing trend in high Nb-content alloys due to the occurrence of dynamic 
recrystallization and grain rotation. The present work establishes Nb alloying as an effective method to optimize 
the microstructure and mechanical properties of solid-solution strengthened Fe-Ni-based superalloys, and the 
appropriate Nb content is recommended to be controlled below 0.5 wt% for this alloy. 
1. Introduction 
Nuclear energy, as a kind of clean energy, plays an essential role in 
reducing carbon emissions and promoting socially sustainable devel -
opment. It has developed into the fourth Generation (Gen IV) advanced 
and innovative nuclear energy system, characterized by a good econ -
omy, high safety, and reliability [ 1 – 3 ]. The Gen IV nuclear reactors 
operate at higher temperatures and neutron irradiation environments; 
thus, the performance of some traditional heat-resistant steels can no 
meet such severe service conditions [ 4 – 6 ]. According to the Gen IV 
nuclear roadmap [ 7 ], solid-solution strengthened Fe-Ni-based superal -
loys are considered to be important candidates for use as structural 
materials for nuclear reactors (high Ni content can improve irradiation 
swelling resistance) [ 8 ]. Recently, a low-cost Fe-Ni-based superalloy 
with good elevated temperature performance and workability has been 
developed as the candidate material to manufacture key high- 
temperature components for Gen IV nuclear reactors [ 9 – 11 ]. 
As an important alloying element, niobium (Nb) is usually employed 
to improve microstructural stability and obtain ideal mechanical prop -
erties in the development of wrought superalloys. The improvement of

design, optimization, and practical employment of Fe-Ni-based 
superalloys.

4.2. Effects of Nb alloying on the deformation behavior 
4.2.1. The variation of strength 
The strength was apparently affected by the Nb alloying due to its 
influence on the microstructures. There were five key factors: (1) solid- 
solution strengthening atoms, (2) grain size, (3) grain boundary char -
acteristics, (4) grain boundary precipitates, and (5) MC carbides. These 
factors included strengthening factors and weakening factors which can 
restrict each other and result in the change in strength. 
Solute atoms can dissolve into the matrix for the multi-component 
alloy to cause local lattice distortion, resulting in a generated stress 
field. It can increase the resistance to dislocation motions to further 
increase the strength [ 39 , 40 ]. It is well known that Nb as a substitutional 
atom can provide a good solid-solution strengthening effect. Liu et al. 
[ 18 ] confirmed that Nb atoms played a critical role in the strengthening, 
contributing 15 MPa to the Nb-modified Ni-based alloy. In this study, 
besides Nb, Cr and Mo were also two important solid-solution atoms.

4.2.2. The variation of ductility 
The main reason for the remarkable decrease in the elongation of Nb- 
alloyed alloys can be attributed to the dominant deformation mecha -
nism from planar slip to wavy slip [ 54 ]. Actually, the deformation bands 
observed in Nb-free alloy were transitional morphologies of planar slip 
bands, which can be mainly attributed to the occurrence of dynamic 
recovery at high temperatures, leading to the initial formation of 
interface-like substructures. In addition, the appearance of deformation 
bands was also associated with the large deformation degree of the 
samples after tensile fracture, which can also make the planar slip 
feature less obvious. With the addition of Nb, the typical dislocation cell 
configuration represented the arrangement and annihilation of screw 
dislocations, demonstrating the activation of wavy slipping. This result 
was similar to the earlier work in Nb-stabilized Fe-Ni-based alloy re -
ported by Ding et al. [ 49 ]. Previous studies have also reported that with 
the increase of SFE, the slip mode in FCC metals would gradually 
transform from planar slip to wavy slip [ 54 , 55 ]. Thus, the change of 
dominant deformation mechanism may be due to the increase of SFE 
caused by Nb addition in this work. Moreover, the drop in the Σ 3 frac -
tion decreased deformation uniformity and cracking resistance, which 
was also not favorable for high-temperature ductility of Nb-alloyed 
alloys. 
It was indeed impressive that the ductility of alloys with high Nb 
content exhibited an increasing trend. This performance was probably 
affected by two factors: Firstly, the substructure of 1Nb alloy was 
composed of considerable amounts of sub-grains and recrystallized 
grains, suggesting that an enhanced DRX process took place, which 
contributed to the improvement of ductility. The occurrence of the DRX 
process can be attributed to the increased volume fraction and the 
average size of MC. A similar DRX phenomenon was also found in 316LN 
stainless steel [ 56 ], Alloy 709 [ 49 ], 25Cr-20Ni austenitic alloy [ 57 ], etc., 
Fig. 12 d shows that recrystallized grains formed near MC, indicating 
that MC can increase the DRX nucleation rate through the particle- 
stimulated nucleation (PSN) mechanism dependent on particle size 
( > 1 μ m) and strain. In this work, the large MC carbide size and strain 
during the tensile deformation can meet the nucleation condition of the 
PSN mechanism in the 1Nb alloy, which can also be confirmed in 
Fig. 15 d [ 58 – 61 ]. In addition, the high proportion of GBs and low 
fraction of Σ 3 boundaries in high Nb content alloys weakened the sup -
pression effects of DRX during high-temperature deformation. The 
results demonstrated that excessive Nb alloying can promote recovery 
and DRX, thus accelerating dislocation annihilation and decreasing 
dislocation density, which can be regarded as a weakening factor that 
affected the high-temperature deformation stability. 
Secondly, a new orientation < 001 > //RD gradually strengthened 
during tensile deformation as the Nb content increased from 0 to 1.0 wt 
%, as shown in Fig. 10 . Zhang et al. [ 62 ] have confirmed that grains with 
< 001 > orientation have the lowest strain energy in FCC materials. The 
driving force for the appearance of new orientation may be due to strain 
energy minimization. This phenomenon has also been observed in 
Ref. [ 29 , 63 , 64 ], illustrating that a large deformation occurred at GBs 
region and then led to grain rotation, thus ensuring the ductility stability 
of the alloys with high Nb content [ 65 ]. The analyzed results of the 
Schimid factor (SF) of {111} 〈 110 〉 slip system calculated by Channel 5 
software can support this view, as presented in Fig. 16 . It can be seen 
that Nb alloying significantly increased the faction of high SF in 0.5Nb 
and 1Nb alloys, indicating that dislocation slip became easier. This may 
be related to the enhanced activation of cross-slip, which can make the 
dislocation movement no longer restricted to a fixed primary slip plane.
