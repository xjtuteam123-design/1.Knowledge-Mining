ABSTRACT
Incorporating high-volume-fraction intermetallics into aluminum alloys is a typical way to enhance
both room- and high-temperature strength, yet often sacrifices ductility. In this study, a novel
Al–Si–Fe–Ni–Mn alloy with 35.6 vol.% and multi-scale heterogeneous IMCs was fabricated via pow-
der metallurgy and thermo-mechanical processing. The alloy achieves an exceptional balance of
room-temperature strength (477 MPa), ductility (8.2%) and high-temperature strength (277 MPa at
300°C), demonstrating remarkable strength-ductility synergy and superior heat resistance. This arises
from a hierarchical architecture that coordinates deformation across multiple size scales. The find-
ings presented herein provide fresh perspectives for the design of high-performance aluminum
alloys.
IMPACT STATEMENT
A high-volume-fraction, multi-scale IMCs-strengthened aluminum alloy with a heterogeneous
microstructure is designed and investigated, achieving significantly enhanced room-temperature
and high-temperature (exceeding 300°C) mechanical properties.

In conventional processes, such as casting, high-
volume-fraction secondary phases require high levels of
alloying elements. However, this can lead to coarsening
due to rapid dynamics, thereby may deteriorate plas-
ticity and strength. In the powder metallurgy process,
thecharacteristicsofsolid-statediffusion,combinedwith
the high shear rate during hot deformation, generate
a heterogeneous microstructure containing intermetal-
lic compounds at multiple scales. This enables the pro-
posed routes in this study to improve the tolerance of
the volume fraction of the secondary phase in the alloy,
which is critical for combining both high-temperature
androom-temperatureproperties.
The findings of this study provide new insights into
the microstructural design principles and methods for
heat-resistant aluminum alloys and other alloy systems.
The novel aluminum alloy developed holds significant
potential for demanding engineering applications, such
