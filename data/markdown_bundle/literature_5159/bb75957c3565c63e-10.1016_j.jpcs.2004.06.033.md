Nano-structure control in ODS martensitic steels by means of selecting
titanium and oxygen contents
S. Ohtsuka*, S. Ukai, M. Fujiwara, T. Kaito, T. Narita
Oarai Engineering Center, Japan Nuclear Cycle Development Institute, 4002 Narita-cho, Oarai-machi, Ibaraki-ken 311 1393, Japan
Accepted 19 June 2004
Abstract
The effects of mechanical alloying (MA) conditions on oxygen contamination were investigated to establish the manufacturing technology
of oxygen-controlled oxide dispersion strengthened (ODS) martensitic steel. High-temperature mechanical tests and microstructure
observations were performed in oxygen-and-titanium-controlled steels in order to demonstrate high-temperature strength improvement using
oxygen control. Oxygen content was drastically reduced by the puriﬁcation of MA atmosphere, Ar gas. It was shown that the lower speed
agitation at the beginning of MA was effective for the reduction of oxygen contamination because oxygen was mixed mainly at the beginning
of mechanical alloying due to the imperfect replacement of Ar gas. Dense distribution of oxide particle and signiﬁcant improvement in high-
temperature strength were achieved by applying 99.9999 wt% Ar in MA atmosphere and reducing oxygen contents. It was revealed that the
merely increasing Y
2O3 and titanium addition cannot improve the high-temperature strength. The excess oxygen is an important parameter
for making oxide particles ﬁnely and densely distributed as well as improving high-temperature strength.

1. Introduction
High-temperature strength and radiation resistance are
important properties for the advanced fast reactor (FR) core
materials because they are used in severe conditions such as
high-temperature environment around 700 8C and average
burn-up of 150 Gwd/t which is relevant to a peak neutron
dose as high as 250 dpa [1]. Oxide dispersion strengthened
(ODS) martensitic steel is noticed as a most prospective
candidate for the advanced FBR core materials because of its
excellent radiation resistance and high-temperature strength.
Japan Nuclear Cycle Development Institute (JNC)
showed that titanium (Ti) is indispensable for ultra-ﬁne
oxide particle dispersion and high-temperature strength
improvement [2]. Furthermore, recent efforts showed that
the high-temperature strength and microstructure can be
drastically improved by lowering excess oxygen (Ex.O)
contents in ODS martensitic steel [3,4]. Where excess
oxygen is deﬁned as subtracting oxygen contents (O) in
Y
2O3 from total O in steel, and is easy to be mixed in steels
during the manufacturing process. Then the manufacturing
technology for reducing the excess oxygen contents must be
established for steadily manufacturing high quality ODS
steels. In this study, the effect of mechanical alloying (MA)
conditions on excess oxygen (Ex.O) contents was examined.
Improvement of nano-scale oxide particle distribution was
also demonstrated by controlling excess oxygen contents.

2.2. Evaluation tests
The chemical analysis was conducted to examine the
correlation between manufacturing conditions and chemical
composition including oxygen. Oxygen and nitrogen were
analyzed by inert gas fusion method. The oxide particle
distributions in manufactured steels were observed using
transmission electron microscope (TEM), where the well-
annealed furnace-cooled steels were observed with the
intension of clearly observing oxide particles.
Since Vickers hardness (Hv) would have a close
correlation with oxide particle distribution and other
mechanical properties, Vickers hardness test was performed
after normalizing, normalizing-and-tempering and furnace-
cooling ﬁnal heat treatment with 1 kgf loading. The
softening by tempering was evaluated by subtracting
Vickers hardness (Hv) in the normalized-and-tempered
steels from that in the normalized steels. Uni-axial tensile
tests and uni-axial creep tests with gage size of 6 mm
diameter!30 mm length were conducted in the normal-
ized-and-tempered steels at a temperature of 700 8C.

2O3
on microstructure and Vickers hardness
As described later, some of manufactured steels have a
microstructure composed of not only equiaxed grain but
also elongated grain. Fig. 2 shows the TEM observation
results of oxide particle distribution in equiaxed grain.
Oxide particles in AT-1 are more ﬁnely and densely

and in Hi–TiYO containing higher excess oxygen, titanium
and Y

be achieved by applying ultra-high purity Ar (99.9999 wt%
Ar) gas and reducing excess oxygen, considering that
the difference between AT-1 and Hi–O in the contents of
particle-composed-elements (excess oxygen, yttrium
and titanium) is only excess oxygen contents. Excess
oxygen would be a critical parameter for ﬁne and dense
distribution of oxide particles rather than Y
2O3 and
titanium. On the other hand, a number density of oxide
particle looks little higher in Hi–TiYO than Hi–O, while
there is not signiﬁcant difference in their oxide particle size.
This would be caused by the higher Ti and Y
2O3 addition in
Hi–TiYO.

signiﬁcantly hardens the furnace-cooled steels and reduces

important role for keeping solute carbon (a typical austenite
former) in matrix, because it enhances titanium oxides
formation rather than titanium carbide. Therefore, either
descreasing excess oxygen or increasing titanium contents
enhance the solute carbon shortage through titanium carbide
precipitation, and suppress the alpha to gamma transform-
ation during the 1150 8C hot-extrusion, which leads the
generation of the elongated residual alpha-phase and
hardening of furnace-cooled steels.
In equiaxed grains, it was shown that ﬁner and denser
distribution of oxide particle can be achieved by making the
excess oxygen lower and titanium higher [4]. Finer and
denser distribution of oxide particles in equiaxed grain by
either decreasing excess oxygen or increasing titanium also
makes steels harder and suppresses the recovery of the high
density dislocation because oxide particles block the
dislocation motion at the tempering. The hardening of
furnace-cooled steel and the suppression of tempering-
induced softening by decreasing excess oxygen and
increasing titanium would mean the generation of elongated
grain and the shortening of oxide particle inter-spacing in
equiaxed grain, which would lead to enhancement of not
only hardness but also high-temperature strength.

3.3. High-temperature strength
Fig. 5 shows the uni-axial creep and tensile test results at
a temperature of 700 8C. The creep strength is certainly
higher in AT-1 than in Hi–O and Hi–TiYO. This would be
caused by the ﬁner and denser oxide particle dispersion in
AT-1 than in Hi–O. Although, more Y 2O3 and titanium are
added in Hi–TiYO than in Hi–O, there is not a clear creep
strength improvement in Hi–TiYO compared with Hi–O.
Referring to Fig. 5(b), AT-1 shows apparently higher short-
term strength (tensile strength, 0.2% proof stress) than
higher excess oxygen steel (Hi–O, Hi–TiYO) as similar to
the creep strength. Comparing Hi–O with Hi–TiYO, the
short-term strength in Hi–TiYO is higher than in Hi–O
although there was not a clear difference in creep strength
between them. The higher short-term strength in Hi–TiYO
than in Hi–O would be caused by denser distribution of
oxide particle due to the increasing Y
2O3 and Ti addition.
The prior gamma grain in Hi–TiYO may be smaller than in
Hi–O because the higher number density of oxide particle
suppresses the grain growth. The resultant larger grain
boundary sliding in Hi–TiYO may decrease the creep
strength of Hi–TiYO to the level of Hi–O.
4. Summaries
The effects of mechanical alloying conditions on
the excess oxygen contents were investigated to establish
the manufacturing technology of the excess-oxygen-con-
trolled ODS martensitic steel. Improvement of nano-scale
oxide particle dispertion was demonstrated by controlling
excess oxygen contents. The derived results can be
summarized as follows.
(1) It was shown that excess oxygen contents can be
drastically reduced by the puriﬁcation of MA atmos-
phere, Ar gas. The lower speed agitation at the
beginning of MA is also effective for the reduction of
excess oxygen contents.
(2) Dense oxide particle distribution and signiﬁcant high-
temperature strength improvement were achieved by
applying 99.9999 wt% Ar to MA atmosphere and
reducing excess oxygen contents.
(3) It was revealed that the merely increasing Y
2O3 and
Ti addition cannot improve the high-temperature
strength. The excess oxygen is an important par-
ameter for making oxide particles ﬁnely and densely
distributed as well as improving high-temperature
strength.
