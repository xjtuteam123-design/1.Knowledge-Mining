ABSTRACT
The quaternary equiatomic CoCrFeNi high-entropy alloy (HEA) with a face-centered cubic (FCC) structure ex -
hibits significant strengthening and strain hardening at cryogenic temperatures, making it highly promising for 
aerospace applications. However, its strength remains insufficient for engineering demands. In this study, a novel 
heterostructure comprising coarse-grained zones (~2.3 μ m), fine-grained zones (~0.9 μ m), and non- 
recrystallized zones was developed via a multi-step short-term annealing process, the total annealing duration 
was only 6.5 min. This microstructural design resulted in yield strengths of ~859 MPa at 293 K and ~1130 MPa 
at 77 K, with uniform elongations of ~22.6 % and ~35.2 %, respectively. The heterostructure leverages a distinct 
strain partitioning mechanism to maximize hetero-deformation-induced (HDI) strengthening and strain hard -
ening. The HDI effect is amplified at the cryogenic temperature, promoting deformation twinning and further 
enhancing strain hardening capacity, thereby achieving an exceptional strength-ductility combination. These 
findings highlight the critical role of heterostructures in governing the deformation mechanisms of HEAs, and 
developing high-performance cryogenic metallic materials.
1. Introduction
The strength-ductility trade-off remains a persistent challenge in the 
design of load-bearing structural materials [ 1 – 3 ], particularly at cryo -
genic temperatures. High-entropy alloys (HEAs), a product of the 
multi-principal elements design approach, have demonstrated an 
outstanding strength-ductility combination and fatigue resistance 
compared to conventional alloys [ 4 – 12 ]. Among numerous HEAs, 
equiatomic CoCrFeNi HEA is an important alloy due to its simple 
single-phase face-centered cubic (FCC) structure and excellent me -
chanical properties. It is shown that significant improvements in 
strength and ductility at 77 K can be achieved, with the yield strength 
increasing from 260 MPa to 480 MPa and the uniform elongation rising 
from 45 % to 55 % [ 13 ]. These enhancements were attributed to the 
enhanced Hall-Petch strengthening, dislocation strengthening, and a 
notable increase in nano-twins at 77 K. However, the weak 
solid-solution strengthening and limited lattice distortion effects result 
in exceptional ductility but moderate strength, limiting its potential for 
advanced industrial applications [ 14 – 16 ].
Grain refinement has been systematically investigated as a viable 
strategy to achieve strength enhancements [ 17 , 18 ]. For instance, the 
yield strength of an additively manufactured CoCrFeNi HEA can be 
enhanced from 552 MPa to 1173 MPa by simple grain refinement. 
However, this strengthening effect was accompanied by a substantial 
reduction in ductility, with the elongation decreasing dramatically from 
35.3 % to 4.9 % [ 19 ]. The grain refinement leads to a substantial in -
crease in the grain boundary density, which effectively impedes dislo -
cation motion by reducing the mean free path (MFP) of dislocations, 
which improves the critical stress required for dislocation activation, 
thereby contributing to the material ’ s strengthening [ 20 ]. Nevertheless, 
the constrained intragranular space significantly limits dislocation 
accumulation and mobility, resulting in diminished strain hardening 
capacity and ultimately leading to brittle fracture characteristics. The 
inherent strength-ductility trade-off associated with grain refinement 
poses significant challenges for engineering applications, particularly in 
achieving optimal mechanical performance.

This limitation has driven the development of innovative micro -
structural engineering approaches, among which heterostructured (HS) 
design has emerged as a particularly promising strategy [ 21 ]. This 
transformative architectural approach has demonstrated remarkable 
potential in enhancing the mechanical properties of many metallic 
material systems [ 22 , 23 ], including HEAs [ 24 ]. HS materials encompass 
various configurations, including gradient [ 25 , 26 ], layered [ 27 ], 
lamellar [ 28 ], and dual-phase structures [ 29 , 30 ], which have demon -
strated the successful application in various medium entropy alloy 
(MEAs) and HEAs.
More profoundly, the advantages of HS design extend beyond 
ambient temperature performance, demonstrating remarkable efficacy 
in enhancing cryogenic mechanical properties. It is reported that a 
partially recrystallized CoCrNi MEA achieved exceptional cryogenic 
performance, with a yield strength of 1.48 GPa and 28 % uniform 
elongation, significantly outperforming its homogeneous counterparts 
[ 31 ]. This enhancement is attributed to temperature-dependent strain 
partitioning mechanisms, where cryogenic conditions amplify interfa -
cial strain gradients and effectively suppress dislocation cross-slip, 
thereby leading to the generation of more geometrically necessary dis -
locations (GNDs), enabling sustained strain hardening capacity under 
low-temperature conditions [ 32 ]. While significant progress has been 
made in applying HS design to enhance the strength-ductility combi -
nation of HEAs, the interactions between HDI strengthening/hardening 
mechanisms and additional plastic mechanisms (such as deformation 
twinning) remain insufficiently elucidated.
In this study, a novel hierarchical HS CoCrFeNi HEA was successfully 
fabricated via multi-step short-term annealing. The effect of hetero -
structure on the mechanical properties and microstructural evolutions of 
CoCrFeNi HEA during uniaxial tensile testing were systematically 
investigated. Our results demonstrate that the hierarchical hetero -
structure imparts an exceptional combination of strength and ductility 
to the CoCrFeNi HEA, performing remarkably well at both room and 
cryogenic temperatures. Notably, the exceptional strength-ductility 
combination achieved at cryogenic conditions surpasses not only our 
homogeneous structured specimens but also outperforms numerous 
other HEAs strengthened through additional alloying elements or 
various carefully engineered heterogeneous structures, demonstrating a 
substantial breakthrough. The underlying mechanisms by which cryo -
genic temperatures amplify strain partitioning effects and HDI effect are 
further analyzed, offering new insights into optimizing the mechanical 
properties of metallic materials for low-temperature applications.

3. Results
3.1. Initial microstructure
Fig. 2 characterizes the partially recrystallized microstructure of the 
HS sample. The microstructural configuration exhibits an approximate 
core-shell morphology, where non-recrystallized zones (NRZ) are 
enveloped within the fully recrystallized zones (RZ), as shown in Fig. 2 a. 
Detailed examination reveals a combination of coarse-grained (CG) and 
fine-grained (FG) zones within the RZ ( Fig. 2 b – c). A noteworthy 
observation is the formation of high-density annealing twins (ATs) 
within the RZ ( Fig. 2 d). These ATs exhibit a maximum thickness of ~2 
μ m, and most twins nearly extend across the entire grain width. TEM 
analysis in Fig. 2 e provides direct evidence of well-defined hetero-zone 
boundaries (HBs) between RZ and NRZ, with striking microstructural 
contrasts: the RZ grains display relatively dislocation-free interiors, 
while the NRZ is characterized by dense dislocation networks and tan -
gles. As shown in Fig. 2 f, the four elements (Co, Cr, Fe, Ni) are uniformly 
distributed throughout the material. Quantitative analysis in Fig. 2 g – i 
indicates significant microstructural disparities between RZ (~73 vol%) 
and NRZ (~27 vol%): the RZ exhibits substantially lower Kernel average 
misorientation (KAM) values, indicative of extensive recovery during 
annealing, while the NRZ maintains high KAM values consistent with its 
severely deformed state. Statistical evaluation of grain size distribution 
in the RZ ( Fig. 2 i) reveals a bimodal structure, with CG zones (~40 vol 
%) averaging 2.3 ± 0.1 μ m and FG zones (~33 vol%) measuring 0.9 ±
0.1 μ m.
The formation of this unique hierarchical heterostructure is attrib -
uted to thermodynamically driven recrystallization behavior, wherein 
severe cold rolling produces a uniform yet stochastic distribution of high 
stored-energy zones that govern the discrete nucleation of recrystallized 
regions during subsequent annealing [ 33 – 36 ]. The controlled short-term 
annealing effectively restricts grain growth in recrystallized areas 
[ 36 – 40 ], while the retained unrecrystallized domains progressively 
undergo recrystallization at higher temperatures with systematically 
reduced annealing durations to prevent coarsening [ 35 , 41 ]. This 
sequential, temperature-dependent activation of recrystallization across 
microstructurally distinct regions [ 42 , 43 ], results in a well-defined hi -
erarchical heterostructure with optimized microstructural discreteness.
This methodology exhibits applicability beyond CoCrFeNi HEAs. 
Given the widespread use of rolling and annealing in metal processing, 
the approach shows significant potential for other rollable/annealable 
alloys through parameter optimization. For instance, a dispersed het -
erostructure in CoCrNi MEAs was obtained using similar thermo -
mechanical processing, achieving exceptional strength-ductility synergy 
[ 35 ]. These techniques are fundamentally based on conventional roll -
ing/annealing practices widely adopted in industrial metallurgy. 
Notably, our annealing treatment requires only 6.5 min total processing 
time. While initial parameter optimization is needed, subsequent 
transfer to standardized production ensures excellent manufacturability.

Fig. 4 presents the uniaxial tensile mechanical behaviors of both HS 
and homogeneous CoCrFeNi HEA samples at 293 K and 77 K, with 
corresponding mechanical properties quantitatively summarized in 
Table 1 and standard deviation of mechanical properties is presented in

1554 MPa (77 K) but suffer from limited uniform elongation below 2 %. 
When the grain size increases to ~2.4 μ m, the A600 sample shows

that cryogenic condition (77 K) significantly enhances both strength and 
ductility compared to room temperature (293 K), which is attributed to 
the substantially improved strain hardening capacity, as evidenced by 
the strain hardening rate curves presented in Fig. 4 c – d.
It ’ s worth noting that the specimen with grain sizes ~2 μ m exhibits 
the best strength-ductility combination among homogeneous samples. 
This observation is consistent with the work of Guo et al. [ 56 ]: precise 
grain size control at approximately 2 μ m has enabled an exceptional 
combination of mechanical properties in the CrCoNi MEA system, 
including a yield strength of ~800 MPa and uniform elongation 
exceeding 30 %. These results demonstrate that homogeneous structures 
exhibit inherent limitations: an exceptional strength-ductility combi -
nation can be achieved at an optimal grain size (~2 μ m), while further 
improvement is hard to be attained through grain size control.
The HS sample exhibits outstanding processing efficiency and me -
chanical performance, matching the ductility of the homogeneous A600 
sample (22.6 % at 293 K and 35.2 % at 77 K), while requiring only 6.5 
min of total annealing time, far less than the 2 h needed for the A600 
sample. Furthermore, the HS sample maintains yield strength equivalent 
to the A580 sample, with values of ~859 MPa (293 K) and ~1130 MPa 
(77 K). Quantitative Ashby-type analysis of the hierarchical hetero -
structure compared with other HEAs, presented in Fig. 5 a – b, reveals that 
the HS sample achieves an exceptional strength-ductility synergy, out -
performing all our homogeneous counterparts at both 293 K and 77 K. 
Particularly, it surpasses the A600 sample — the optimal homogeneous 
structure with a grain size of ~2 μ m [ 56 ] — highlighting the HS design ’ s 
effectiveness in overcoming inherent strength-ductility trade-off.

4. Discussion
The experimental results reveal that the HS CoCrFeNi HEA achieves 
exceptional mechanical performance at both 293 K and 77 K, manifested 
through three characteristics: (1) superior strength-ductility synergy, (2) 
enhanced HDI hardening, and (3) promoted deformation twinning ac -
tivity. To fundamentally understand the mechanisms underlying these 
properties, the following discussion will systematically address three 
critical aspects by correlating the deformation behavior with micro -
structural evolution. Furthermore, despite the compositional complexity 
of the alloy, the minimal atomic radius differences and comparable 
valence electron concentration among constituent elements result in a 
relatively simple solid-solution structure with limited strengthening ef -
fects [ 14 – 16 ]. This characteristic enables direct investigation of heter -
ostructure and deformation twinning mechanisms.

deformation, while the FG counterparts demonstrate significant slip 
length reduction [ 73 ]. This pronounced reduction in slip length effec -

have conclusively demonstrated that the critical twinning stress follows 
a grain size dependence analogous to the Hall-Petch relationship [ 75 ,
76 ]. Consequently, CG zones exhibit a higher propensity for twinning at 
cryogenic temperatures, leading to enhanced strain hardening and a 
more pronounced improvement in uniform elongation [ 77 ], as shown in 
Fig. 10 b.

tween different zones in the HS CoCrFeNi samples intensifies at cryo -

domains. The implications of this strain partitioning will be further 
elaborated in the following section.
4.2. Strain partitioning and HDI hardening
The strength disparity between different zones leads to progressive 
yielding, resulting in pronounced zonal heterogeneity in plastic defor -
mation between the RZs (CG + FG zones) and NRZs, namely strain 
partitioning [ 78 – 82 ]. As discussed in Section 4.1 , cryogenic conditions 
amplify this mechanical heterogeneity, further intensifying strain par -
titioning. The resulting strain gradients at HBs promote the accumula -
tion of GNDs [ 83 ], which generates long-range internal stresses — back 
stress in soft zones and forward stress in hard zones — contributing to 
HDI hardening [ 84 ]. The substantial accumulation of GNDs at HBs 
significantly enhances the work hardening rate in HS samples, leading to 
the pronounced upturn phenomenon observed [ 85 ]. Additionally, GNDs 
hinder the movement of statistically stored dislocations (SSDs), further 
enhancing strain hardening [ 86 , 87 ]. At cryogenic temperatures, the 
intensified strain partitioning leads to more pronounced HDI hardening 
and improved uniform elongation, as shown in Fig. 10 .
The extent of strain partitioning evolves throughout deformation, as 
suggested by both experiments and simulations [ 88 – 91 ]. In this study, 
the KAM obtained via EBSD is used to evaluate the degree of plastic 
deformation and elucidate strain partitioning behavior in HS samples, as 
shown in Fig. 11 . At the initial stage, a significant KAM contrast is 
observed between NRZs and RZs, as the later retain a deformed state 
with severe plastic strain from prior rolling. Given the inherently high 
initial KAM value of NRZs and its minimal variation during subsequent 
deformation, only the average values of KAM in CG and FG zones are 
calculated for analyzing strain partitioning in RZs during deformation, 
as illustrated in Fig. 12 .
Before deformation, the average KAM in the CG zone is lower than 
the FG zone, attributed to a higher degree of recrystallization. At 5 % 
strain, the KAM increment in the CG zone is significantly greater than 
the FG zone, indicating preferential plastic deformation in the CG zone, 
a trend more pronounced at 77 K. This observation aligns with the TEM 
results ( Fig. 8 ), which reveal that dislocation slip and deformation

three aspects.
First, the activation of deformation twinning requires a sufficient 
driving force. GBs typically serve as preferential sites for twin nucleation 
due to stress concentration effects [ 98 , 99 ]. In heterostructures, the 
intrinsic mechanical disparity between zones induces significant me -
chanical incompatibility during deformation, resulting in pronounced 
stress concentration at HBs, which facilitates twin formation. Moreover, 
the strain gradients at HBs effectively suppress fatigue crack initiation 
[ 96 ]. These HBs promote crack deflection, creating highly tortuous 
propagation paths [ 97 ], significantly reduce the stress intensity factor at 
crack tips and substantially enhance crack propagation resistance. 
Notably, the presence of FG zones and NRZs, which exhibit high 
strength, generates elevated local stress in the HS sample, promoting 
deformation twinning in the softer CG zones [ 100 – 103 ]. Consequently, a 
high density of DTs is observed in CG zones, as shown in Fig. 8 .
Second, the heterostructure comprises zones with distinct mechani -
cal properties, leading to deformation incompatibility and a more 
frequent occurrence of multi-axial stress state compared to the homo -
geneous sample. This promotes the activation of multiple twin systems 
within the heterostructure [ 104 ], as shown in Fig. 7 d. The formation of 
multiple twin structures, rather than parallel twin structures, is more 
effective in obstructing dislocation movement [ 105 ], thereby contrib -
uting to strain hardening. Moreover, twin intersections increase the 
stress required for twin growth, further improving the flow stress of the 
HS sample. The deformation incompatibility is more pronounced at 77 
K, leading to a greater prevalence of multiple twin structures and better 
strain hardening.

5. Conclusions
In this work, hierarchical HS and homogeneous CoCrFeNi HEA 
samples with varying grain sizes were fabricated via multi-step short- 
term annealing and conventional annealing, respectively. Their me -
chanical properties and deformation mechanisms at ambient and cryo -
genic temperatures were comparatively investigated. The key 
conclusions are summarized as follows: 
1. The HS sample exhibits a superior strength-ductility synergy 
compared to the homogeneous sample with the optimal grain size. 
Notably, the total annealing time is significantly reduced, contrib -
uting to improved energy efficiency and environmental 
sustainability.
2. At the cryogenic temperature, the mechanical incompatibility be -
tween different zones in the HS sample is intensified through two 
dominant mechanisms: an elevated Hall-Petch slope amplifying 
strength heterogeneity and amplified grain size-dependent twinning 
behavior difference exacerbating ductility heterogeneity, promoting 
strain partitioning and enhancing the HDI hardening.
3. The HS architecture markedly facilitates twin nucleation by inducing 
higher stress localization at HBs and promoting multi-axial stress 
state, with this effect further amplified at the cryogenic temperature.
