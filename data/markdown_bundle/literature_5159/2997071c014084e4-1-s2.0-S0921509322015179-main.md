ABSTRACT 
Sc and Zr additions in Al matrix offer enormous potential for strengthening at elevated temperatures due to 
formation of coarsening resistant precipitates. The high-temperature properties of supersaturated Al-0.4Sc-0.4Zr 
at% alloy, produced via melt-spinning and extrusion, were studied in creep and elevated temperature 
compression. Given the larger amount of solute resulting from rapid solidification, the elevated temperature 
compressive strength of the supersaturated Al-0.4Sc-0.4Zr at% was significantly higher compared to dilute Al- 
0.06Sc-0.06Zr at%. The operating deformation mechanism at elevated temperatures in both alloys was dislo -
cation climb, and the difference in threshold stress was assessed between Al-0.4Sc-0.4Zr at% and Al-0.06Sc- 
0.06Zr at%. The larger threshold stress in the supersaturated Al-0.4Sc-0.4Zr at% was attributed to the 
different microstructure due to the processing. The stress exponents and activation energies from the creep tests 
are consistent with the diffusion-controlled dislocation climb mechanism. The small grain size led to diffusional 
creep in the low stress regime in both alloys.

The low solubility of Sc and Zr in the Al matrix limits the precipitate

are potent strengtheners. When these low equilibrium solubilities (0.28

is Sc and Zr have different partitioning behavior [ 21 , 22 ], with eutectic 
Sc solute partitions to the interdendritic regions, while peritectic Zr 
solute stays within the dendritic cores [ 3 ]. 
One strategy for further strengthening is to increase the precipitate 
volume fraction by trapping larger solute fractions in the matrix through 
rapid solidification processing [ 23 , 24 ]. Rapid solidification techniques 
such as selective laser melting (SLM), gas atomization, and additive 
manufacturing have been utilized to increase the matrix solute levels

2. Experimental 
2.1. Sample preparation 
The dilute baseline Al-0.06Sc-0.06Zr at% composition was chosen as 
it doesn ’ t form primary precipitates using conventional solidification 
processing. This dilute alloy was vacuum induction melted and cast into 
a steel candelabra rod mold. No primary precipitates were found in the 
dilute alloy using SEM, which is consistent with the result reported in 
the previous work [ 3 ]. 
The supersaturated Al-0.4Sc-0.4Zr at% alloy was produced using 
melt spinning. A graphite crucible charged with 99.99% Al (Belmont 
Metals) and master alloys Al-1.2Sc at% and Al-3.2Zr at% (KBM) in the 
vacuum chamber of the melt spinner. The chamber was evacuated to 8

4. Discussion 
4.1. Hot strength properties 
The decrease in strength at elevated temperature is partially due to 
the change in temperature-dependent properties such as shear modulus, 
Young ’ s modulus, and anti-phase boundary energy. At the same time, 
the compressive strength of Al-0.06Sc-0.06Zr and Al-0.4Sc-0.4Zr 
converge with increasing temperature ( Fig. 1 ). This decrease might 
relate to the microstructure change beyond the aforementioned property 
changes. (1) The dominant strengthening in many precipitate-hardened 
aluminum alloys comes from the dislocation looping or shearing pre -
cipitates at ambient temperature. However, the dominant strengthening 
might change to others at elevated temperatures, such as Coble creep 
and Herring Nabarro creep, grain boundary sliding, and dislocation

4.3. High-temperature strengthening mechanisms

shearing, Orowan looping around precipitates, dislocation climb, or 
dislocation detachment. Precipitate shearing has not been observed in 
the Al-Sc-Zr alloys at either ambient or elevated temperature, while 
dislocation detachment is not observed with coherent precipitates [ 2 , 3 , 
27 ]. Orowan looping is the dominant room-temperature strengthening 
mechanism, resulting from a large number of non-shearable nano-sized 
precipitates [ 1 , 2 , 27 ]. Quantification of Orowan strengthening in 
Al-Sc-Zr alloys containing 2 – 3 nm precipitates is given by Ref. [ 30 ]:

5. Conclusions 
A supersaturated Al-0.4Sc-0.4Zr at% was produced via melt-spinning 
and extrusion. The high-temperature mechanical properties of dilute, 
cast Al-0.06Sc-0.06Zr at% and supersaturated Al-0.4Sc-0.4Zr were 
investigated. The active deformation mechanism during creep was 
found to dislocation climb with minor contributions from boundary 
diffusional creep. 
This work demonstrates: 
● Both Al-0.06Sc-0.06Zr and Al-0.4Sc-0.4Zr lost most of their ambient- 
temperature-strength when the temperature reached up to 300 
◦

(60% and 75% respectively). The strength differences between them 
became less significant at 300 
◦

● Dislocation climbing was the active strengthening mechanism in Al- 
0.06Sc-0.06Zr and Al-0.4Sc-0.4Zr at elevated temperature, as sup -
ported by the stress exponents and activation energies. Larger 
threshold stresses were found in high solute alloy Al-0.4Sc-0.4Zr due 
to the larger precipitate size and volume fraction. 
● Diffusional creep was thought to exist in both alloys at lower stresses, 
given the fine and thermally stable grain size in Al-Sc-Zr alloys.
