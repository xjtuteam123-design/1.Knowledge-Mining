abstract
In this study, Re alloying and self-generated hard phase enhancement were used to improve the strength
and toughness of W. The arc-melting W-based composite with 10 wt% Re and 1 wt% SiC addition is
composed of toughening W-Re matrix and ceramic particles (W 2C and W 5Si3). This composite having
high strain capacity (37.1%) at room temperature (more than 5 times of W), exhibits the excellent
hardness (5.04 GPa), yield strength (0.92 GPa) and fracture strength (1.78 GPa) due to the synergetic
mechanism, grain re ﬁnement, and good interfaces bonding state. The synergetic mechanism of Re and
SiC addition on strength and toughness was proposed.

1. Introduction
W-based materials feature several advantageous properties for
structural materials [ 1] and plasma-facing components [ 2,3], e.g.,
high melting point, good thermal conductivity, high creep resis-
tance, good high-temperature strength and low vapor pressure.
However, the low ductility of W at room temperature (RT) is one of
the most obstacles for its application [ 4]. Several approaches have
been developed to increase the ductility, e.g., alloying of W with K
[5], Ta [ 6,7], V [ 7], Nb [ 8], Mo [ 9], Re [ 2,9] and Ti [ 9,10]. Only a few
metals (Ta, V, Nb and Mo) form solid solutes in the complete frac-
tion range. However, recent studies with Ta, V, Ti, Nb and Mo, which
should be veriﬁed using a different fabrication route, have not led to
promising results. Hence, it seems unlikely that the ductility of W
would be improved by the formation of substitution solid solutions
other than by Re [ 11]. As reported, Re alloying could obviously in-
crease the toughness at only high Re concentrations close to the
limit of solubility (27%) [ 11e13]. Base on the density-functional
theory (DFT) analysis, Re alloying modi ﬁes screw dislocations
from a symmetric structure to an asymmetric core structure, and
reduces resistance in shear [ 12,14]. Experiments have con ﬁrmed
that small amount of Re addition (less than 9 wt%) could “soften” W
already [ 15e17], because Re alloying could decrease the Peierls
stress and increase the additional slip planes [ 11,12], e.g., the slip on
{1 12} planes could be promoted by Re addition [ 18,19]. Hence, Re
alloying is a good approach for improving the ductility of W. On the
other hand, ductility is also enhanced by using grain re ﬁnement,
which could be achieved by the addition of ceramic phase and the
process treatment of plastic deformation and thermal treatment.
Although more promising materials such as W-Y 2O3 [20], W-HfC
[21,22], W-TiC [ 23] and W-ZrC [ 24] were recently produced by
sintering and severe plastic deformation (SPD) in collaboration, the
fabrication processing with high failure rate and short die life is
complex and time-consuming [ 25,26]. Forming the ductility phase
in situ through high-temperature reaction is an available way to
enhance the ductility and toughness of W. For instance, W 2Zr [27]
and W5Si3 [28,29], those formed from the reactions of W, have been
veriﬁed as the toughness phases. Lee [ 29] showed that the ﬂexural
strength of SiC/W composite at RT was signi ﬁcantly increased due
to the bridging phenomenon by the toughening W 5Si3. As our
previous study shows [ 30], W-based material with 1 wt% SiC
addition signi ﬁcantly enhance the strain capacity due to the grain

2. Experimental details
W powder (99.9% in purity, 1mm in diameter; Aladdin, Shanghai,
China), Re powder (99.9% in purity, 1 mm in diameter; Aladdin,
Shanghai, China) and b-SiC powder (99.9% in purity, 1 mmi n
diameter; Aladdin, Shanghai, China) were used as raw materials.
Mixed powders (W þ10 wt% Re, W þ1 wt% SiC, and W þ1 wt%
SiCþ10 wt% Re, abbreviated as R10, S1, and S1R10, respectively)
were weighed and ball milled in a low energy ball milling apparatus
(QM-A, Xianyang, China). The powder mixture was dried in a vac-
uum at 323 K for 12 h after mixing and then pressed into pellets
(15 mm in diameter and 5 mm in thickness) at 30 MPa. Each pellet
was melted 4 times by arc-melting (ACM-SO1, DIAVAC, Japan) to
achieve uniformity of composition, and solidiﬁed on a water-cooled
copper base in an Ar atmosphere at 60 kPa, and obtaining the fully-
dense specimen. The melted specimens were cut into the speci ﬁc
size, i.e.
F2 /C2 5m m3 for compressive test, by an electric discharge
machine.
The microstructure and compositions were investigated by an
electron probe micro-analyzer (EPMA, JXA-8230, Japan) with a
wavelength dispersive spectrometer, and a transmission electron
microscope (TEM, JEM 2100, JEOL, Japan). The grain size was
analyzed by the software of Image-Pro Plus 6.0. The hardnesses of
the W-Si-C multi-phase composites were measured by a Vickers
micro-hardness tester (430SVD, Wolpert, USA) using a load of 1 kg.
The compressive strength and strain of the specimens were
measured by a mechanical testing machine (AG-100 kN, Shimadzu,
Japan) equipped with an extensometer (M926105BH40-03, Ame-
tek, USA) using a crosshead speed of 1 mm/min. Both the Vickers
micro-hardness and strength values were measured at RT and re-
ported as the averages of ﬁve measurement results.
3. Results and discussion
Fig. 1 shows the XRD patterns of R10, S1, and S1R10. W and W 2C
were only identiﬁed in both S1 and S1R10, and Re was not detected
in R10 and S1R10. There is a peak shift of W and no peak of Re in R10
and S1R10, indicating that Re solubilizes in W.
Fig. 2 (a)e(d) show the microstructures of pure W, R10, S1,
S1R10. The average grain size of W decreased from approximately
1070 to 350 mm and to 52 mm after adding 10 wt% Re (R10) and 1 wt
% SiC (S1), respectively. The W grain size in S1R10 is approximately
twice of that of S1, indicating that Re decrease the effect of grain
reﬁnement with both the addition of Re and SiC. The corresponding
element distributions for A of Fig. 2 (d) indicated that there are
more ceramic particles in metallic matrix of S1R10 than that of S1,
and Re mostly distributes in W matrix. Baud et al. reported that
W2C and W5Si3 were formed by the reaction of W and SiC at higher
1473 K [29,32]. Furthermore, according to the phase diagram of W-
Si-C system ( Fig. 3 [33]), W 2C and W 5Si3 segregates and form the
eutectic (W/W2C and W/W 5Si3) and peritectic (W 2C/W5Si3), which
mainly distributes along the W grain boundaries (GBs) [ 30]. In the
case of Re metal, there should be no reaction taking place at lower
than 1873 K as the previous study realized by Searcy [ 34,35].
Therefore, it can be initially speculated that S1R10 is mainly
composed of W-Re matrix and ceramic particles (W 2C and W 5Si3).
Comparing with S1, there are more ceramic particles (particle
diameter size, D z 10
mm) distributing in the matrix grains of
S1R10. This novel phenomenon might be related with the growth of
matrix grains, which is affected by the 10 wt% Re addition. Although
the presence of ceramic particles is bene ﬁcial to depress the grain
growth [20e23,30], the ceramic particles would be surrounded by
metallic grains when the matrix grains have the high growth rate.
For instance, this structure was observed in the W-based composite
of 0.5 wt% addition [30]. For S1R10, 10 wt%Re addition may increase
the DT between the liquidus and solidus [ 36] and decrease the
thermal conductivity [37,38], and consequently prolong the growth
time of matrix grains. On the other hand, as Roger reported, the
addition of Re increase the activation energy of the interface re-
action and decrease the reaction zone for W/SiC system [ 39],
resulting in a degradation of the grain re ﬁnement by reaction
products. Moreover, the matrix grain may have a high growth rate
due to Re has the same ðDSf =RÞ as W (1.16) at a rough interface
[30,40]. Here, DSf and R are the entropy of fusion and the universal
gas constant [ 41], respectively. These reasons induce the more
ceramic particles surrounded by the matrix grains.

compressive behavior of pure W, R10, S1, S1R10. As reported, the
hardness of arc-melting pure W and the fracture compressive
strength of SPS-W were 3.50 GPa [ 13] and 0.75 GPa [ 42], respec-
tively. The strain capacity of arc-melting W from the literature was
more than 5% [43]. In this study, the hardness in pure W is 3.57 GPa,
and the arc-melted W with the fracture compressive strength of
0.83 GPa and the strain capacity of 7% exhibits the typical brittle
fracture at RT, as shown in Fig. 4 (b). The comparison results show
that Re alloying decrease the Hv and compressed strength (yield
strength, s0:2 & ultimate compressive strength, UCS) due to the
solution softening mechanism of Re in W [ 15]. On the other hand,
all the Hv, compressed strength and the ductility were improved
with 1 wt% SiC addition [ 30]. It is important that the Hv of S1R10
exceeds that of R10 and S1, and showed the value of 5.04 GPa,
which is approximately 1.4 times higher than that of pure W.

To clarify the mechanism of strengthening and toughening, the
interfaces bonding state and the dislocation distribution in S1R10
were investigated by TEM. There are few dislocations in the un-
deformed S1R10 as shown in Fig. 8 (a). By comparison, the
compressive deformation introduced a high density of dislocations
in W-Re matrix, exhibiting various geometries, such as dislocation
networks and dislocation tangles as shown in Fig. 8 (b) and (c).
Moreover, varying degrees in deformation will induce the different
dislocation density due to the presence of strain gradient. In
Fig. 8(b) and (c), a mass of dislocations is in the metallic matrix, and
dislocation density increased signi ﬁcantly near the interfaces. The
seamless phase interfaces and some slip bands between W and
W5Si3 in compressed S1R10 were observed, as shown in C of
Fig. 8(c) and (d). No slip bands were found in the interface of W and
W2C, but the interface bonding is neat and even close to coherent,
as shown in D of Fig. 8 (c) and (e). For instance, (1 10) of W with
body-centered cubic (bcc) could be completed through the
coherent growth to (0002) of W 2C with close-packed hexagonal
(hcp), as shown in Fig. 8(e) and (f). It indicates that the integrating
state of phases interface is very dense, and the interface of W and
W5Si3 appears a good toughness. Therefore, the ﬁner grains, good
interfaces bonding state, and the toughening metallic matrix pro-
vide an excellent strain capacity of S1R10.
In general, the yield strength is the stress required to operate
dislocation sources and is governed by the presence and magnitude
of all the obstacles that restrict the motion of dislocations in the
matrix [46]. As Nan reported [ 47], in the dislocation description of
deformation in the metal matrix composites (MMC), four distinct
contributions to the ﬂow stress can be identi ﬁed. i. e. , the Orowan
stress ( DsOR ), kinematic strain gradient contribution ( DsKIN) and
the corresponding isotropic form to the ﬂow stress (DsISO), and the
strengthening from CTE mismatch ( DsCTE). In S1R10, the diameter
size of ceramic particles has reached approximately 10 mm
(Fig. 2(d)), and the ceramic phase was surrounded by dislocations,
as shown in Fig. 8(b)-(c). It indicates that the motion of dislocations
is blocked by big size ceramic phase, and forming the dislocations
pile-up and tangles [ 46,48]. However, the Orowan contribution is
the stress of pass a dislocation through an array of impeding par-
ticles [ 47]. Hence, the strength increase for S1R10 will through a
load bearing mechanism, rather than the formation of Orowan
dislocation loops, because of the preferential and the large size of
strengthening particles [ 49,50]. The fracture of the ceramic

4. Conclusions
In summary, Re and SiC were added into pure W to improve the
strength and ductility via the arc-melting method. The W-based
composite with 10 wt% Re and 1 wt% SiC additions (S1R10)
composed of W-Re metallic matrix and ceramic phases (W
2C and
W5Si3) that either segregated along the GBs or in the grain interior
of W-Re. The grain size of metallic matrix obviously decreased due
to the formation of W 2C and W 5Si3 at the GBs. S1R10 presented a
good interfaces bonding state, and exhibited the excellent hardness
and comprehensive properties due to the synergetic effect of Re
alloying and SiC addition. The synergetic mechanism may result
from the combined effect of toughening metallic matrix and in-
ternal particle strengthening in a long-range interaction between
particles and dislocations. Moreover, kinematic strain gradient
contribution is the most effective. In view of that the harder ma-
terial always has the lower strain capacity, the synergetic design
may be a promising approach to provide the composite with high
hardness, strength and strain simultaneously.
