abstract
Due to their excellent creep behavior and acceptable oxidation resistance at ultrahigh temperatures
multiphase Mo-based alloys are potential candidates for applications in aerospace engines and the power
generating industry. The resulting materials properties, as well as the microstructure of Mo eSieB ma-
terials, strongly depend on the manufacturing process. In the following paper we report on a new Mo eSi
eB alloy which was processed by crucible-free zone melting (ZM) from cold pressed elemental powders.
SEM investigations of the zone molten microstructure showed well-aligned arrangements of a three-
phase microstructure consisting of a Mo solid solution (Mo SS), and the two intermetallic phases Mo 3Si
and Mo5SiB2. First, high temperature mechanical properties, such as the compressive strength and creep
strength at about 1 100/C14 C, were evaluated and compared with a commonly used Ni-based superalloy and
a PM processed Mo eSieB material. In comparison to the PM processed reference alloy, the creep
resistance of ZM materials was found to be substantially improved due to the relatively coarse direc-
tionally solidi ﬁed microstructure. Thus, ZM alloys show great potential for applications at targeted
application temperatures of around 1200 e130 0

1. Introduction
Materials applied in aircraft engines and stationary gas turbines
have to withstand extremely high surface temperatures. To further
increase the thermodynamic ef ﬁciency of these machines, accord-
ing to the Carnot cycle, the gas inlet temperature needs to be
further increased e following a simple rule “the hotter the engine
the better ” [1]. Raising gas steam temperatures would then make
aviation more sustainable and reduces the environmental impact.
To reach the aim of an environmental sustainable use of rare re-
sources it is necessary to develop new high temperature materials
beyond the capability of Ni-based superalloys since commonly used
Ni-based alloys are limited to applications with a maximum surface
temperature near 1 100 /C14 C. An overview of different developments
in high temperature turbine materials is given elsewhere [1e3].
Beside new categories of high temperature materials such as Nb eSi
alloys [4,5],C oeRe alloys [6e10] and Pt-based alloys [1 1e14], which
were proposed over the last decades, multiphase refractory alloys
based on molybdenum are of central scienti ﬁc interest. Speciﬁcally,
Mo-based alloys containing Si and B have been under investigation
and development over a long period of time and a broad range of
materials are described in the references [15e19] leading to alloy
compositions which are located in the so-called “Berczik triangle ”
[20]. These alloy compositions consists of a three-phase micro-
structure with a ductile molybdenum solid solution (Mo SS, A2
structure) and the two intermetallic phases Mo 3Si ( A15 structure)
and Mo 5SiB2 (T2, D81 structure). Depending on the processing
technique, these materials provide acceptable room temperature
toughness, high temperature strength and a good oxidation
behavior due to the formation of a protective and self-healing
SiO2eB2O3 glass layer on the surface [21,22]. However, most
studies focused on powder metallurgy (PM) routes as an economic
alternative to the classic ingot metallurgy and extremely expensive
gas atomization processes [19,23e25].
PM Mo-materials can be processed using two different pro-
duction routes, one includes cold isostatic pressing (CIP), sintering
and hot isostatic pressing (HIP) while the second route uses the
ﬁeld assisted sintering technology (FAST) for consolidation. Both
PM processing routes, HIP and FAST, start typically either from
powder mixtures or mechanically alloyed (MA) powders of pure
Mo, Si and B. MA is seen as a key feature in these processing
techniques to obtain a desirable microstructure which consists of a

MoSS matrix. However, PM Mo-based alloys are ultra- ﬁne grained,
which is beneﬁcial in terms of oxidation resistance but unfavorable
for creep behavior. Nevertheless, the creep performance of as HIPed
materials can be increased by a heat treatment at high tempera-
tures to achieve homogeneity and larger grains [25].
The present work introduces an alternative processing route
using cold pressed powder mixtures which are consolidated by
directional solidiﬁcation via a crucible-free zone melting technique
(ZM). While there is an abundance of literature on directional so-
lidiﬁcation for various alloy systems [26e30], ZM of multiphase
MoeSieB materials is a rather new approach.
Mason et al. [31] investigated a directionally solidi ﬁed eutectic
composition consisting of MoSi 2eMo5Si3. By varying the solidi ﬁ-
cation velocities from 25 to 210 mm/h Mason and co-workers
achieved a lamellar crystal morphology parallel to the growth di-
rection. They proposed that the cooperative growth of MoSi 2 and
Mo5Si3 structures proceeded in a similar manner as the eutectoid
growth of pearlite in austenite. A more ﬁbrous Mo5Si3 morphology
was found to occur by adding 0.35 at% of erbium. A bene ﬁt in creep
behavior at 1300 /C14 C and higher temperatures was found as the
lamellar spacing of the MoSi 2eMo5Si3 eutectic was decreased [32].
Different multiphase ZM alloys with a T2/Mo SS and (Mo,Nb) SS/
T1/T2 eutectic microstructures which were consolidated using an
optical ﬂoating zone method were investigated by Ito et al. [33].
They achieved a T2/Mo SS two-phase microstructure in the alloy
Moe9Sie18B using a solidi ﬁcation rate below 5 mm/h. It was
observed that the Mo SS phase appears in two characteristic mi-
crostructures, either of a short rod-like morphology or as coarse
particles, respectively. The three-phase (Mo,Nb) SS/T1/T2 micro-
structure of the alloy (Mo,Nb)-19.5Si e3B could by re ﬁned by
increasing the growth rate up to 200 mm/h, which results in a
typical lamellar eutectic structure. However, no signi ﬁcant depen-
dence of the directionally solidi ﬁed microstructure on room tem-
perature fracture toughness and strength at 1500 /C14 C has been
observed by Ito et al.
Recently, Wang et al. [34] reported on the microstructure and
oxidation behavior of directionally solidi ﬁed Mo e10Sie14B with
the typical three phases appearing in the “Berczik triangle”. Coarse
microstructures were obtained at growth rates of 10 and 30 mm/h.
Due to the directional solidi ﬁcation this coarse microstructure re-
sults in a rather poor oxidation resistance caused by the cata-
strophic oxidation of large Mo SS regions (the so-called pesting
phenomenon). On the other hand, a re ﬁnement of the micro-
structure by increasing the growth rate corroborates with Ito et al.
[33] and thus leads to an increased oxidation resistance. However, a
distinct crystal growth orientation obtained by the processing route
of Wang and co-authors [34] is difﬁcult to distinguish, in contrast to
the new ZM alloy composition which will be investigated in the
present work.
A speci ﬁc comparison between an industrial PM route (CIP,
sintering, HIP) and an alternative ZM route has been reported
recently [35]. Speci ﬁcally, the microstructure and the mechanical
properties with respect to the brittle-to-ductile transition tem-
perature (BDTT) were assessed for various ternary Mo eSieB alloys.
As compared to the directionally solidi ﬁed morphology of Wang
et al. [34], the elongation of the microstructure parallel to the
growth direction was achieved for three-phase Mo eSieB alloys
containing 9 at% Si and 15 at% B [36], respectively. Due to a high
amount of intermetallic phases the three-point bending strength at
871 /C14 C of the eutectic ZM alloy Mo e9Sie15B reaches identical
values as an ultra- ﬁne grained PM alloy Mo e6Sie5B [35]. There-
fore, these initial results show the potential of a well-aligned zone
molten microstructure. Furthermore, such a directionally solidi ﬁed
microstructure will have advantageous effects on the creep per-
formance, especially at temperatures higher than 1 100 /C14 C where
ﬁne-grained alloys suffer from void accumulation on transverse
grain boundaries [37].
The present work focuses on a new three-phase Mo eSieB alloy
composition, namely Moe17.5Sie8B (at%), which was fabricated by
zone melting. Three different solidi ﬁcation velocities were chosen
for examination and the correlating microstructures will be
examined using optical and scanning electron microscopy (SEM) as
well as X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis. A comparison of the creep
behavior of the new ZM alloy, a PM alloy Mo e9Sie8B as well as a
commonly used Ni-based superalloy, CMSX-4, will be presented at
high temperatures ranging from 1093 to 1400 /C14 C. Additionally, the
deformed microstructures will be investigated.
2. Experimental procedure
The novel ZM alloy Mo e17.5Sie8B (at%) was produced from a
stoichiometric powder mixture of Mo, Si and B of 99.95%, 99.6% and
98% purity, respectively. The composition was chosen from ther-
modynamic calculations of isopleths located in the “Berczik trian-
gle” [20] by keeping the Si content constant and varying the B
concentration using the Pandat software. The aim was to ﬁnd a
ternary eutectic composition. After the cold pressing of the powder
it was compacted in an induction heated zone melting device
“Crystal 206 ” (Ukraine). Zone melting was performed in a high
purity He atmosphere using solidi ﬁcation rates of 40, 60 and
80 mm/h, respectively. Chemical compositions were veri ﬁed after
the directionally solidi ﬁcation using inductively coupled plasma
optical emission spectroscopy (ICP-OES). The results of chemical
analysis are listed in Table 1. Typical products are around 80 mm in
length, having a diameter of about 7 mm, as demonstrated in Fig. 1.

After solidi ﬁcation, specimens with a diameter of 3 mm and a
height of 5 mm were cut parallel to the growth direction using
electrical discharge machining (EDM) to perform compressive
creep tests. The creep strength was evaluated in a ﬂowing Ar/H 2
atmosphere (used for the tests at 1093 /C14 C) or in vacuum (for the
tests at 1200 and 1400 /C14 C) using a Zwick/Roell Z100 electro-
mechanical testing machine equipped with a Maytec furnace.
Creep testing was carried out by applying a constant true stress to
the specimen. After attaining a constant, i.e. steady-state creep rate
at a particular constant stress level the stress was increased until a
new steady-state was established. This involved a careful and
continuous monitoring of the creep strain during the entire test.
To investigate the microstructure, samples were taken from a
transverse and longitudinal crystal growth direction and
embedded in a cold hardening epoxy (Demotec 15). After subse-
quently grinding from 180 down to 1200 grit size the specimens
were ﬁnished by polishing with a 3 and 1
mm diamond suspension.
The microstructural observations were carried out using a SEM
microscope (FEI ESEM XL30 FEG) equipped with EDX. The SEM
images were typically obtained in the backscattered electron (BSE)
mode. The software ImageJ, was used to determine the fraction of
the microstructural constituents in dependence on the solidi ﬁca-
tion rate from the SEM-BSE images.
Powders for XRD analysis were prepared from directionally
solidiﬁed specimens. XRD measurements were performed using an
X’pert Powder X-ray diffractometer (PANalytical) with a Cu-K a
beam. The phase identi ﬁcation was obtained using the analysis
software X’Pert HighScore Plus (PANalytical). The oxygen content in
the new zone molten alloys was determined using a Bruker G8
Galileo ON/H analyzer.
3. Results and discussion
3.1. Directionally solidi ﬁed microstructure
During ZM a loss of Si and B was measured, Table 1, showing a
more pronounced evaporation of B from the melting zone during
directionally solidi ﬁcation which may be the reason for a slightly
lower volume fraction of the T2 phase in alloys solidi ﬁed at 40 and
80 mm/h. Table 2 summarizes the corresponding fraction of the
microstructural constituents, as obtained from the SEM images, and
the oxygen content of the alloys investigated in this study. Indeed,
the volume fractions of phases vary with the solidi ﬁcation rates,
although not have an obvious systematic tendencies that could be
linked directly to the elemental evaporation in Table 1.
The microstructure of the novel alloy is shown in Fig. 2 .I n
contrast to recent investigations of ZM MoeSieB alloys [34], a well-
aligned phase morphology can be obtained with the new alloy Mo-
17.5Sie8B under the chosen ZM parameters. Fig. 2 a, c and e show
micrographs that are taken parallel to the crystal growth direction
corresponding to 40, 60 and 80 mm/h, respectively. Fig. 2b, d and f
are taken from the transversal cross-section at each corresponding
crystal growth rate. In these images light gray phases correspond to
the Mo

corresponds to the T2 intermetallic phase. A near-eutectic alloy
composition is achieved wherein areas of lamellar shaped MoSS and
T2 phases are embedded into a Mo 3Si matrix. On the other hand,
larger, spherical areas of all three single phases can be observed,
too. These regions seem to vary in size and shape, depending on the
solidiﬁcation rate, i.e. Fig. 2 c shows large areas of Mo 3Si and in
Fig. 2e larger Mo SS areas can be seen which are either elongated in
growth direction or have a spherical shape, respectively. However,
in agreement with the literature [33],ar e ﬁned phase morphology

The values for the oxygen content in the ZM processed alloys are

3.2. Mechanical properties
The high temperature creep behavior of the novel ZM alloy, Moe
17.5Sie8B, was investigated and compared to a ternary PM alloy,
Moe9Sie8B, which also consists of a Mo SS/Mo3Si/Mo5SiB2 three-
phase microstructure having a continuous Mo SS matrix. Addition-
ally the high temperature deformation behavior was compared
against pure Mo [39] and the commonly used single-crystalline Ni-
based superalloy, CMSX-4, at possible application temperatures.
The corresponding Norton-plot is illustrated in Fig. 4a). In reference
to Mo eSieB alloys, the state-of-the-art single-crystalline CMSX-4
exhibits a much weaker creep behavior due to the well-known
thermal instability of the g/g0 microstructure leading to the so-
called rafting phenomenon, a directional coarsening of the g0
phase upon the applied creep load. However, the creep resistance of
the PM material above an applied stress of 100 MPa is comparable
to CMSX-4 at 1093
/C14 C while the ZM alloys are showing a different
behavior. The ﬁrst results are available for the alloy Mo e17.5Sie8B
grown at 60 mm/h where a signi ﬁcantly increased creep perfor-
mance was observed.
Furthermore, the creep behavior of ZM Mo-17.5Si e8B at tem-
peratures ranging from 1093 to 1400 /C14 C is compared with the
performance of CMSX-4 and a forged and re-crystallized three-
phase alloy, Mo e8.6Sie8.7B [40],i n Fig. 4 b. A typical temperature
dependent creep behavior can be observed; with increasing test
temperatures the creep performance drops signi ﬁcantly. In the
ternary alloy used for the present study MoSS is known as the phase
with the lowest creep resistance [33,39]. Thus, at 1093 /C14 C disloca-
tion movement which causes deformation should mostly be
contributed by the Mo SS phase. Hence, the creep resistance at this

Investigations of the deformed microstructures support the
conclusions, as described above. As an example, the microstructure
of Mo-17.5Sie8B deformed at 1200 /C14 C is shown in Fig. 6a and b, the
deformed microstructure at 1400 /C14 C is shown in Fig. 6 c, respec-

tough Mo SS phase. Additionally, crack trapping in the Mo SS phase
was also clearly identi ﬁed, which prohibits the crack propagation
through the material and, thus, would cause brittle failure. At
140 0 /C14 C the situation changes slightly since only the T2 phase
should deform in a brittle manner according to the investigations of

cracks as compared to 1200 /C14 C. Nevertheless, crack de ﬂecting and
crack trapping can also be observed. In this connection it is worth
noting that the specimen at 1200 /C14 C was deformed plastically by
about 10% while the specimen at 1400 /C14 C was deformed plastically
by only 5%.
4. Summary and conclusions
- A new ZM Mo eSieB alloy composition has been introduced in
the present paper. It can be concluded from the microstructural
investigations that the directional solidi ﬁcation process applied
in the present work is a useful approach to produce an in-situ
MoSS/Mo3Si/Mo5SiB2 three-phase microstructure from me-
chanically mixed powders.
- As compared to published results on PM processed alloys, a
remarkably low oxygen content was found in the ZM alloy, Mo-
17.5Sie8B, directionally solidi ﬁed at 40 mm/h, 60 mm/h and
80 mm/h, respectively.
- Due to the high volume fraction of intermetallic phases the
creep performance of the novel alloys solidi ﬁed at a crystal
growth rate of 60 mm/h could be improved as compared to the
state-of-the-art Ni-based superalloy CMSX-4 and PM Mo eSieB
alloys, even at very high temperatures. Dislocation climb was
found to be the operating creep mechanism by determining the
creep stress exponent n and the activation energy for creep Q.
- Furthermore, Mo e17.5Sie8B solidiﬁed at 60 mm/h satis ﬁes the
creep strength requirements for possible applications in aircraft
engines as proposed by Bewlay et al. [4].
