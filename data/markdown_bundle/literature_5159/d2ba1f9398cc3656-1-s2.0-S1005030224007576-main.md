Grain boundary engineering plays a signiﬁcant role in the improvement of strength and plasticity of al- 
loys. However, in refractory high-entropy alloys, the susceptibility of grain boundaries to oxygen presents 
a bottleneck in achieving high mechanical performance. Creating a large number of clean grain bound- 
aries in refractory high-entropy alloys is a challenge. In this study, an ultraﬁne-grained (UFG) NbMoTaW 
alloy with high grain-boundary cohesion was prepared by powder metallurgy, taking advantages of rapid 
hot-pressing sintering and full-process inert atmosphere protection from powder synthesis to sintering. 
By oxygen control and an increase in the proportion of grain boundaries, the segregation of oxygen and 
formation of oxides at grain boundaries were strongly mitigated, thus the intrinsic high cohesion of the 
interfaces was preserved. Compared to the coarse-grained alloys prepared by arc-melting and those sin- 
tered by traditional powder metallurgy methods, the UFG NbMoTaW alloy demonstrated simultaneously 
increased strength and plasticity at ambient temperature. The highly cohesive grain boundaries not only 
reduce brittle fractures effectively but also promote intragranular deformation. Consequently, the UFG 
NbMoTaW alloy achieved a high yield strength even at elevated temperatures, with a remarkable per- 
formance of 1117 MPa at 1200 °C. This work provides a feasible solution for producing refractory high- 
entropy alloys with low impurity content, reﬁned microstructure, and excellent mechanical performance.

1. Introduction 
Traditional high-temperature alloys have faced challenges in 
meeting the service requirements under extreme working condi- 
tions, such as ultra-high temperatures. Refractory high entropy al- 
loys [ 1 , 2 ], composed of multiple refractory metal elements, have 
emerged as a promising hot spot of research for metallic materi- 
als due to their exceptional high-temperature strength [ 3-5 ]. Their 
potential high-temperature performance enables promising appli- 
cations in aero-engines, gas turbines, and nuclear power plants. 
Among the reported refractory high-entropy alloys, NbMoTaW al- 
loy exhibits excellent resistance to high-temperature softening. 
For example, the yield strength of coarse-grained NbMoTaW al- 
loy prepared by vacuum arc melting (VAM) at 1200 °C can reach 
506 MPa [ 6 ]. However, the room-temperature compressive strain of 
NbMoTaW alloy is only 3 %, which restricts its practical application. 
In addition to the intrinsically brittle from chemistry aspect, a trace 
amount of oxygen is easily segregated at the grain boundaries to

room temperature. 
In order to address the aforementioned issues, researchers have 
conducted numerous investigations on the NbMoTaW-based alloy. 
The addition of Ti can reduce the valence electron concentration 
of the alloys and improve grain boundary cohesion. Therefore, 
the room-temperature plasticity of NbMoTaWTi alloys prepared by 
VAM is enhanced, and the compressive yield strength at 1200 °C is 
increased to 586 MPa [ 8 ]. The addition of Zr improves the room- 
temperature plasticity of NbMoTaW alloy through formation of the 
heterostructure [ 9 ]. Additionally, trace amounts of B and C atoms 
have proven effective in preventing oxygen atom segregation at the 
grain boundaries, thus mitigating the embrittlement [ 10 ]. More- 
over, appropriate inclusion of oxides or carbides as second phases 
within the alloy can improve the strength [ 11-13 ]. Notably, dif- 
ferent preparation methods signiﬁcantly inﬂuence the mechanical

Although powder metallurgy methods are beneﬁcial for im- 
proving the uniformity of composition, reﬁning grain size, and 
enhancing the mechanical properties of alloys, they are suscep- 
tible to introducing impurities during the preparation process 
[ 17-19 ]. Prolonged impact between the powder and the grind- 
ing media can diminish the purity of the powder and increase 
the probability of oxidation. Moreover, adding carbon-containing 
process control agents during milling tends to generate carbide 
impurities with refractory metal elements [ 20-22 ]. Furthermore, 
ensuring complete avoidance of air contact during subsequent 
treatment before sintering is not always feasible for powders, 
resulting in elevated oxygen content or even formation of various 
oxides within the prepared alloy. The presence of these impurities 
affects both microstructures and mechanical properties of alloys, 
hindering investigation of the intrinsic microstructural stability 
and mechanical properties of refractory high-entropy alloys. 
On the other hand, despite extensive research effort s aimed 
at enhancing the mechanical properties of NbMoTaW alloys, there 
is still a need for further improvement in their high-temperature 
strength. It is rarely reported that refractory high-entropy alloys 
can maintain a strength exceeding 10 0 0 MPa at 1200 °C. To achieve 
superior high-temperature strength while retaining a certain level 
of room-temperature plasticity, reﬁning the grain structure is one 
viable option. Compared to metals with an FCC crystal structure, 
BCC metals generally exhibit higher Hall-Petch coeﬃcients [ 23 ], in- 
dicating a more pronounced strengthening effect as grain size de- 
creases. Furthermore, with a constant oxygen content in the al- 
loy, reﬁning the grain size leads to an increase in grain bound- 
ary area and a decrease in oxygen concentration per unit area 
of the grain boundary. It should maintain relatively high interfa- 
cial strength and prevent premature failure caused by embrittle- 
ment along grain boundaries, thereby potentially improving room- 
temperature plasticity. 
To address these challenges, this work employs a rapid hot- 
pressing powder metallurgy technique at a relatively low sintering 
temperature to prepare an ultraﬁne-grained (UFG) NbMoTaW alloy. 
The UFG NbMoTaW alloy with low impurity content is achieved by 
utilizing highly eﬃcient mechanical alloying and inert atmosphere 
protection throughout the entire process from powder prepara- 
tion to sintering. The UFG NbMoTaW alloy exhibits an impressive 
room-temperature yield strength of 2386 MPa while demonstrat- 
ing improved plasticity compared with its coarse-grained counter- 
part. Even at 1200 °C, its yield strength remains above 1100 MPa. 
These exceptional mechanical properties position it among the 
highest levels for both NbMoTaW alloys and other reported refrac- 
tory high-entropy alloys. This work not only offers a solution for 
preparing refractory high-entropy alloys with a low impurity con- 
tent using powder metallurgy but also provides material support 
for applications in high-temperature environments. 
2. Materials and methods 
NbMoTaW alloy was prepared through mechanical alloying and 
rapid hot-pressing sintering. High-purity powders of Nb (parti- 
cle size ∼45 μm, 99.95 %), Mo (particle size ∼150 μm, 99.9 %), 
Ta (particle size ∼44 μm, 99.9 %), and W (particle size ∼12 μm, 
99.99 %) were utilized as the initial raw materials. Mechanical al- 
loying was performed using a vibrating high-energy ball mill with 
an isoatomic ratio for each element. This method is highly effec- 
tive in achieving mechanical alloying or even amorphous powder 
in a short duration [ 24 ]. Stainless steel balls and tank were uti- 
lized with a ball-to-powder mass ratio of 15:1 at a rotational speed 
of 470 r/min under an argon atmosphere. To further mitigate ox- 
idation of the powder, an inert Ar atmosphere was maintained 
throughout the entire process, spanning from powder preparation 
to sintering. This approach has proven successful in fabricating 
high-purity rare-earth metals and lithium alloys [ 24 , 25 ]. Rapid hot- 
pressing sintering was conducted at temperatures of 140 0, 150 0 
and 1600 °C with a constant pressure of 100 MPa to investigate the 
effect of sintering temperature. In detail, the milled alloy powder 
was loaded into a high-strength graphite mold and transferred to 
the sintering furnace under Ar protection. The heating rate during 
sintering was 75 °C/min, and once the target sintering tempera- 
ture was reached, it was maintained for 5 min. During cooling, the 
temperature decreased as a rate of 75 °C/min until reaching 800 
°C. Then, the cooling rate of the sample was controlled to be grad- 
ual till room temperature. The sintered samples have dimensions 
of 20 mm in diameter and a height ranging from 3 mm to 10 mm. 
Phases and crystal structures were analyzed using a Rigaku- 
Ultiam IV X-ray diffractometer (XRD) with Cu-target radiation 
( λ= 0.15406 nm). The microstructure and compositional distribu- 
tion were characterized using a scanning electron microscope 
(SEM, Gemini SEM 300) equipped with an X-ray energy spectrom- 
eter (EDS), as well as a transmission electron microscope (TEM, 
JEM-2100F). The grain size was determined using the electron 
backscattered diffraction (EBSD) technique. The relative densities 
of the alloys were determined by Archimedes method. 
The hardness was measured by the Vickers hardness tester 
with a load of 30 kg and a holding duration of 5 s. The 
room-temperature compression test was conducted using LD26.105 
microcomputer-controlled electronic universal testing machine 
(Force Test (Shanghai) Scientiﬁc Instrument Co., Ltd.) with a con- 
stant strain rate of 5 × 10−4 s−1 . An extensometer was utilized 
to ensure the measurement accuracy of the strain. The cylindri- 
cal samples utilized for the compression test have a height of 
5.0 mm and a diameter of 3.5 mm. The microcompression tests 
were conducted using a nanoindenter equipped with a diamond 
ﬂat-punch tip with a strain rate of 0.01 s−1 . The height-to-diameter 
ratio of the micro-pillar specimen was set at 2:1. The top speci- 
men had an approximate diameter of 2 μm, and the slope angle 
was controlled to be within 5 ° The high-temperature compression 
test was conducted on a German-DOLI-DDL200 mechanical prop- 
erty testing machine at a strain rate of 0.001 s−1 in Ar atmosphere, 
with temperatures ranging from 600 °C to 1200 °C. The cylindrical 
specimens have dimensions of 6.0 mm in height and 4.0 mm in 
diameter. 
3. Results and discussion 
3.1. NbMoTaW alloys prepared under whole-process inert protection 
Previous studies have demonstrated that elemental oxygen 
tends to segregate at grain boundaries of NbMoTaW alloy, result- 
ing in grain boundary embrittlement and a decrease in strength 
and plasticity [ 10 , 26 ]. To reduce the oxygen content, a protective 
Ar atmosphere was employed throughout the entire process from 
preparation of the alloy powder, loading the powder into the mold, 
and transportation to sintering. The XRD patterns of NbMoTaW al- 
loy powders under different ball milling durations are shown in 
Fig. 1 (a). A single-phase solid solution was not achieved in the 6 h 
milling as the diffraction peaks of Ta and W can still be observed, 
indicating incomplete dissolution of these elements into the ma- 
trix. Previous studies have demonstrated that the alloying order 
is inﬂuenced by the melting points of components [ 27 ]. Compo- 
nents with lower melting points exhibit weaker atomic bonding 
and higher diffusion coeﬃcients. Consequently, it takes longer to 
achieve alloying of Ta and W than Mo and Nb. When the ball 
milling time was extended to 12 h, a BCC single-phase solid so- 
lution could be formed. Subsequent prolongation of ball milling 
time does not signiﬁcantly alter the phase composition. The pow-

3.2. NbMoTaW alloys prepared without whole-process inert 
protection 
To indicate the importance of Ar protection during the entire 
process, NbMoTaW alloys are also prepared by a traditional pro- 
cess. In this process, a protective Ar atmosphere was employed for 
the preparation of the alloy powder, while loading the powder into 
the mold and transferring it to sintering were conducted in the 
ambient air environment. The alloy powders milled for 12, 14 and 
16 h were sintered at 1500 °C according to the above process. The 
XRD patterns of the prepared alloys are presented in Fig. 4 (a). It 
can be observed that the phases of NbMoTaW alloys remain consis- 
tent with increasing ball milling time. The primary phases consist 
of BCC structures accompanied by a small amount of Nb, β-Ta, and 
TaO2 phases. β-Ta is a metastable phase with the P42/mnm space 
group [ 28 ]. Previous studies have shown that even trace amounts 
of oxygen can induce the transformation from α-W to β-W [ 30 ]. 
Similarly, in our work, exposure of the powder to air resulted in 
relatively higher oxygen content in the powder. During the sinter- 
ing process, oxygen diffused into interstitial sites within Ta lattice, 
leading to the formation of β-Ta. Additionally, when impurity con- 
tent was high within the powder, Nb adsorbed impurity and pre- 
cipitated as Nb rich phase. If local oxygen content exceeded certain 
levels, Ta combined with oxygen forming TaO2 . It was also found 
that diffraction peaks corresponding to Nb and β-Ta did not di-

3.3. Mechanical properties at room temperature 
The hardness of NbMoTaW alloy prepared by VAM is 454 HV 
[ 6 ]. In the present work, WP-UFG-1400, WP-UFG-1500, and WP- 
FG-1600 alloys demonstrate signiﬁcantly improved hardness of 
664 ±19, 695 ±18, and 683 ±19 HV30 , respectively, due to their re- 
ﬁned grain structure. Remarkably, the high hardness achieved in 
this work for the NbMoTaW alloy is comparable with that reported 
for the alloy prepared via powder metallurgy containing rigid FCC 
compounds [ 28 ]. The compressive stress-strain curves at room 
temperature for WP-UFG-1400, WP-UFG-1500, and WP-FG-1600 al-

WP-FG-1600 alloys, the WP-UFG-1500 alloy exhibits signiﬁcantly 
improved compressive yield strength (2386 ±54 MPa) and fracture

Based on the aforementioned investigations, the correlation be- 
tween the macroscopic and microscopic mechanical properties of 
the prepared alloys in this study with their respective microstruc- 
ture, oxygen content, and relative density can be established. Con- 
sidering that the WP-UFG-1500 and UFG-1500 alloys possess sim- 
ilar grain sizes, the disparity in strength and plasticity arises from 
variations in grain boundary cohesion, which governs crack initi- 
ation at grain boundaries and subsequent propagation. The weak 
cohesion of grain boundary in the arc-melted NbMoTaW alloy 
has been conﬁrmed through micropillar compression tests con- 
ducted on bicrystalline alloys [ 35 ]. These tests revealed lower frac- 
ture toughness of the bicrystal samples compared to those of the 
single-crystal samples, indicating reduced energy required for the 
crack formation and propagation along the grain boundary. Three- 
dimensional atom probe tomography analysis combined with ﬁrst- 
principle calculations provided additional evidence for the weak- 
ening of grain boundaries due to oxygen segregation [ 10 ]. The re- 
sults in this study demonstrate that the higher strength of WP- 
UFG-1500 alloy relative to UFG-1500 alloy can be attributed to the 
enhanced cohesion of its grain boundaries due to a low concentra- 
tion of oxygen and oxides within the grain boundary regions. The 
ﬁne-grain strengthening effect relies on the interaction between 
dislocations and grain boundaries. A high-cohesion grain boundary 
can effectively impede dislocation motion without readily fractur- 
ing. Moreover, continuous loading induces substantial plastic de- 
formation within grains thereby improving the overall plasticity of 
the alloy. Conversely, stress concentration at a low-cohesion grain 
boundary tends to result in crack initiation and premature failure 
while limiting its ability to hinder dislocation motion. Therefore, 
compared to UFG-1500 alloy, WP-UFG-1500 alloy has concurrently 
improved strength and plasticity. 
As for the alloys prepared at different sintering temperatures 
(WP-UFG-1400, WP-UFG-1500 and WP-UFG-1600), the grain size 
increases with higher sintering temperatures. The ﬁner grains 
would facilitate a more homogeneous dispersion of stress in the 
matrix, thereby preventing excessive local stress concentration and 
inhibiting the formation and propagation of premature cracks. Con- 
sequently, higher loads can be applied to induce dislocation nu- 
cleation and motion. Compared with the grain size (3.49 μm) of 
WP-FG-1600 alloy, the ﬁner grain size (0.49 μm) of the WP-UFG- 
1500 alloys results in a higher density of grain boundaries, which 
enhances resistance against dislocation movement and improves 
yield strength [ 37 ]. In addition, under a speciﬁc oxygen content in 
the alloy, the increase in grain boundaries facilitates dispersion of 
segregated oxygen atoms and reduces their concentration at these 
interfaces, preventing embrittlement at these locations. Therefore, 
both yield strength and plasticity are enhanced in the WP-UFG- 
1500 alloy compared to the WP-FG-1600 alloy. However, despite 
further reduction in grain size (0.44 μm) observed in WP-UFG- 
1400 alloy, it exhibits reduced strength and plasticity compared to 
WP-UFG-1500 alloy due to its lower relative density. The higher 
porosity leads to premature failure under compression stress and 
undermines the effect of grain reﬁnement on the mechanical prop- 
erties.

4. Conclusions 
In this work, UFG NbMoTaW alloy with a low content of im- 
purity was successfully prepared by a rapid hot-pressing powder 
metallurgy method through a whole-process inert atmosphere pro- 
tection from powder preparation to sintering. The microstructure 
and mechanical properties of alloys prepared under different con- 
ditions were investigated. The main conclusions are as follows: 
(1) By controlling the oxygen content and implementing rapid hot- 
pressing sintering at lowered temperatures, UFG NbMoTaW al- 
loys with low impurity content were fabricated. The grain sizes 
can be reﬁned to less than 500 nm, providing a viable solution 
for producing refractory high-entropy alloys with relatively high 
purity based on the powder metallurgy strategy. 
(2) The UFG NbMoTaW alloy demonstrates a yield strength of 
∼2300 MPa at ambient temperature, and a high plasticity with 
a strain of 40 % is obtained for the UFG NbMoTaW micropil- 
lar. The greatly increased grain boundaries with high cohesion 
not only reduce brittle fractures but also promote intragranu- 
lar plastic deformations, contributing to simultaneous enhance- 
ment of strength and plasticity of the UFG NbMoTaW alloy. 
(3) The UFG NbMoTaW alloy exhibits superior high-temperature 
yield strength compared to other reported refractory high- 
entropy alloys, demonstrating a remarkably increased yield 
strength of 1117 MPa at 1200 °C. The ultraﬁne-grained struc- 
ture contributes 58 %−66 % in maintaining high strength of 
the NbMoTaW alloy at elevated temperatures. The excellent in- 
tegrated mechanical performance of the UFG NbMoTaW alloy 
renders it highly promising for applications requiring resistance 
against high temperatures.
