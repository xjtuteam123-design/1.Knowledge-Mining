ABSTRACT 
As structural materials candidates of advanced fission and future fusion reactors, oxide-dispersion-strengthened 
(ODS) alloys, which are often ultrafine-grained (with a grain size between 100 and 1000 nm), have attracted 
widespread interest in past decades. However, fabrication of fully dense bulk nanocrystalline ODS alloys has 
rarely been reported. In this work, a novel Zr-doped nanocrystalline 14YWTZ ferritic ODS alloy (Fe – 14Cr – 3W- 
0.4Ti-0.8Zr-0.3Y
2
O
3
) was prepared by mechanical alloying and high-pressure consolidation techniques. The high 
consolidation pressure (4 GPa) endows the bulk nanocrystalline 14YWTZ alloy with a nearly full relative density 
(~99.5%) and a reasonable tensile elongation. The consolidated nanocrystalline 14YWTZ alloy with an average 
grain size of ~50 nm shows an ultra-high compressive yield strength of ~2641 MPa. Besides, the alloy exhibits 
an exceptional thermal stability, i.e., limited grain growth occurs after annealing at a high temperature of 
1000 
◦
C. After hot rolling, the alloy retains nanocrystalline microstructures and exhibits an unprecedented 
tensile yield strength of 2730 MPa, far exceeding those of previous reported ferritic ODS alloys. The high thermal 
stability of the annealed nanocrystalline 14YWTZ alloys can be ascribed to the combined effects of the ther -
modynamic multicomponent co-segregation to grain boundaries and strong kinetic Zener pinning by high- 
density Zr – Ti – Y – O-enriched nanoparticles with an average particle size of ~3.18 nm and a high number den -
sity of ~3 × 10
24 
m
 3
. The outstanding strength of the present nanocrystalline 14YWTZ alloys primarily orig -
inates from the Hall-Petch strengthening of the nanocrystalline matrix, distinguishing them from conventional 
ODS alloys strengthened by dislocation forest and/or dispersed oxide nanoparticles. 
1. Introduction 
The development of advanced fission energy and future fusion en -
ergy systems strongly depends on high-performance structural materials 
applicable to extremely hostile conditions [ 1 ]. Structural materials for 
next-generation fission and fusion reactors must be stable at very high 
temperatures (up to 1000 
◦
C) and able to withstand intense irradiation 
(up to 200 dpa). Ferritic steels are widely used in nuclear reactors due to 
their excellent void swelling resistance. Particularly, oxide dispersion 
strengthened (ODS) nanostructured ferritic alloys (NFAs) are considered 
to be one of the best candidates for structural components such as fuel 
claddings of fast reactors [ 2 ] and blanket structural materials of fusion 
reactors [ 3 ] in advanced nuclear reactors. The high-density 
Y – Ti – O-enriched nanoparticles (NPs), several nanometers in diameter, 
and ultrafine-grained (UFG) matrix, typically 100 nm to 1 μ m, endow 
NFAs with higher strength, better thermal stability, and higher creep 
resistance in comparison to conventional ODS alloys [ 1 , 4 ]. Besides, the 
NPs/matrix interfaces and grain boundaries (GBs) can also act as 
effective sinks for irradiation-induced defects and improve the recom -
bination of vacancies and interstitial atoms, resulting in excellent radi -
ation tolerance. 
Introducing a high density of sinks, such as NPs [ 5 , 6 ], GBs [ 7 – 9 ], and 
interfaces [ 10 , 11 ] is an effective way to enhance the radiation tolerance 
of materials. In particular, nanocrystalline (NC) materials with a high

relative density (98.6 – 98.7%), damaging the strength and fracture 
toughness of consolidated materials [ 26 ]. 
Improving the thermal stability of NC materials is a critical factor to 
achieve bulk NC alloys using a powder consolidation method. Besides, 
for the advanced fission and fusion reactors, the operation temperature 
can reach ~1000 
◦
C [ 3 ]. The improvement of thermal stability can also 
enhance the resistance to heat-induced grain growth at such a high 
operating temperature. The kinetic stabilization approach [ 27 , 28 ] has 
been widely used to prepare ODS alloys, where high-density NPs can 
effectively pin GBs, inhibiting the grain boundary (GB) migration. 
However, the resultant grain size of ODS alloys is often between 100 and 
1000 nm [ 1,4,18,20 – 24 ]. It is worth noting that for many NC Fe based 
alloys, the refined NPs have been proved to be insufficient to stabilize 
the NC microstructure at high temperatures [ 14 , 17 ]. In comparison, 
recent theoretical [ 29 , 30 ] and experimental [ 31 – 33 ] studies have 
shown that NC alloys can be largely stabilized by a thermodynamic 
approach, i.e, their grain growth can be effectively inhibited by the 
adsorption of solute atoms to GBs, reducing the specific GB energy. For 
many NC Fe based alloys, the thermodynamic segregation of the doping 
elements was believed to be a primary cause for their high thermal 
stability [ 14 , 17 , 33 ]. 
Here, we synthesized novel bulk NC 14YWT ferritic ODS alloys by 
doping 0.8 wt% Zr (named as 14YWTZ hereafter) through a mechanical 
alloying and high-pressure consolidation technique. The 14YWT ferritic 
ODS alloys were developed in a collaboration between the Oak Ridge 
National Laboratory, University of California, Santa Barbara, and Los 
Alamos National Laboratory, aiming to achieve a good combination of 
strength, toughness and irradiation tolerance in a larger heat of NFAs 
[ 23 ]. Zr was often doped into ODS alloys to refine the particle size and 
increase the number density of NPs [ 25 , 34 – 37 ]. Meanwhile, Zr has also 
been reported to reduce the GB energy in Fe based alloys [ 17 , 33 , 38 ]. 
Thus, manipulating the microstructure of ODS alloys by Zr doping can 
be expected through combined utilization of the kinetic pinning and 
thermodynamic segregation mechanisms. On the other hand, the 
high-pressure consolidation technique has been successfully applied to 
fabricate hard materials, such as ceramic [ 39 ], intermetallic compounds 
[ 40 ], and nanomaterials [ 14 , 41 – 44 ]. Under high pressure, the plastic 
flow of the materials effectively improves the densification process. 
Furthermore, the oxide layer on the particle surface could also be 
effectively disrupted under the combined effects of high pressure and 
high temperature, facilitating the formation of direct inter-particle 
bonding [ 45 – 47 ]. Cai et al. [ 41 , 42 ] fabricated fully dense NC Mg al -
loys by high-pressure consolidation, confirming the effectiveness of high 
pressure on promoting the densification process. In particular, Du et al. 
[ 14 ] prepared bulk NC 304L stainless steel with a fully dense 
microstructure by high-pressure consolidation at 900 
◦
C. In addition to 
the exceptional thermal stability of the unique predesigned alloy, the 
reduced consolidation temperature and diffusion coefficient exerted by 
the high pressure are another important factor for the achievement of 
NC microstructure. As expected, through the combined stabilization 
strategy of thermodynamic segregation and kinetic pinning with the aid 
of high-pressure consolidation technique, we have achieved fully dense 
NC 14YWTZ ferritic ODS bulk alloys with an ultrahigh yield strength of 
up to ~2.7 GPa and an exceptional thermal stability of up to 1000 
◦
C.

3. Results 
3.1. Microstructure 
Fig. 1 (a) shows the morphology of the as-milled 14YWTZ alloy 
powders, revealing a relatively uniform distribution of particle size 
(average size 47 μ m). Fig. 1 (b) shows the XRD patterns of the as-milled 
alloy powders and the consolidated 14YWTZ bulk alloy. The as-milled 
powders are mainly composed of a body-centered cubic (bcc) struc -
tured ferrite phase. In addition, a weak second-phase diffraction peak at 
2 θ = 42.5
◦
is also observed, which was often identified to be derived 
from coarse oxide and/or carbide particles in ODS alloys [ 25 , 49 , 50 ]. 
After consolidation, both the ferrite matrix and the secondary phase are 
maintained, as shown in Fig. 1 (b). Fig. 1 (c) shows the polished SEM 
morphology of the consolidated 14YWTZ bulk alloy, revealing a fully 
dense microstructure after consolidation. Although the preliminary 
powder boundaries (PPBs) are observed, no large (a few micrometers) 
micropore can be observed. A high relative density of 99.5% is obtained 
for the consolidated bulk alloy, demonstrating the effectiveness of the 
high-pressure consolidation technique on powder densification process. 
Fig. 2 shows the microstructure of the consolidated 14YWTZ bulk 
alloy. The bright-field scanning TEM (STEM) image ( Fig. 2 (a)) shows a 
matrix composed of equiaxed nanograins with an average grain size of 
50 ± 15 nm ( Fig. 2 (b1)). Meanwhile, neither micro-pore nor nano-pore 
can be observed in the matrix inside the powders, as shown in Fig. 2 (a). 
It is worth noting that residual pores with a size ranging from a few 
nanometers to micrometers have been often observed in conventional 
ODS alloys [ 23 , 35 , 51 , 52 ]. To the best of our knowledge, the present NC 
14YWTZ alloy possesses the smallest grain size with a fully dense 
microstructure among previously reported ferritic ODS alloys. The 
high-angle annular dark-field (HAADF) image ( Fig. 2 (c)) reveals that 
prevalent NPs decorate the matrix of the consolidated NC 14YWTZ alloy. 
The average diameter and the number density of the NPs are 3.3 ± 1.9 
nm ( Fig. 2 (b2)) and 1.69 × 10
23 
m
 3
, respectively, yielding a volume 
fraction of 1.01%. The high-resolution STEM image ( Fig. 2 (d)) reveals 
that the NP has a fully coherent interface with the matrix, and the cor -
responding fast Fourier transform (FFT) pattern (shown in the inset of 
Fig. 2 (d)) indicates that the NP exhibits a bcc lattice structure. Mean -
while, it is worth noting that the NPs in the present NC 14YWTZ alloy 
exhibit a narrow unimodal distribution, as shown in Fig. 2 (b2), where 
the size of each NP is below 10 nm. For comparison, we also fabricated 
an ODS alloy without Zr addition (14YWT). The average grain size of 
this non-Zr alloy is 375 ± 182 nm ( Fig. 2 (e)), much larger than that of 
Zr-doping alloy ( Fig. 2 (a)). The average particle size of NPs in the non-Zr 
alloy is 6.7 ± 3.8 nm ( Fig. 2 (f)), much larger than that of Zr-doping ODS 
alloy ( Fig. 2 (c)). These results indicate that doped Zr largely refines both 
the grain size of matrix and the particle size of NPs. The refined size of 
NPs in the present Zr-doping ODS alloy is consistent with that in pre -
viously reported Zr-doping ODS alloys [ 25 , 34 – 37 ]. The refining effect 
on NPs has been mainly attributed to the increase of binding energy of 
yttria nanoclusters by Zr doping [ 36 , 53 ]. 
Occasionally, some coarse oxide particles can be also observed in the 
consolidated NC 14YWTZ alloy, as shown in Fig. 3 (a). Examination of 
more than 100 coarse oxide particles shows a particle size distribution 
ranging from 45 to 120 nm, averaging 107 nm. The coarse oxide par -
ticles have a low volume fraction, which constitutes less than 5% of the 
total oxide particles in the consolidated NC 14YWTZ alloy. Meanwhile, 
these coarse oxides are inhomogeneously distributed in the matrix, 
frequently exhibiting a string-like distribution feature, as shown in Fig. 3 
(a). Such a distribution feature is common in ODS alloys, where the 
coarse oxide particles are predominantly located along or near the PPBs 
[ 23 , 54 , 55 ]. The powder surface is inevitably exposed to air during 
processing, and thus the PPBs are often decorated by thin oxidized layer 
and/or coarse oxide particles after high-temperature consolidation. 
Despite the low volume fraction, these coarse oxide particles are 
considered as the main reason for the degradation in tensile ductility, 
impact properties as well as corrosion resistance [ 23 , 54 , 56 ]. The cor -
responding EDS elemental mapping results ( Fig. 3 (b – i)) reveal that these 
coarse oxides are Mn – Ti – O-enriched and Fe-depleted particles. Addition 
of Ti to ODS alloys can transform Cr oxides or carbide towards Ti oxides 
because of a greater affinity of Ti to O [ 57 ]. The introduction of Mn in 
the present NC 14YWTZ alloy can be mainly attributed to the wear of 
high Mn-containing milling balls (440C stainless steel) during the me -
chanical alloying.

The elongation of ferritic ODS alloys generally decreases with the 
reduction in grain size of the matrix, as shown in Fig. 11 . For NC ma -
terials, limited plasticity with a strain softening phenomenon in plastic 
deformation stage has been often reported [ 14 , 23 , 26 , 50 ]. This behavior 
also occurs in previously studied NC 12YWT alloy, which exhibits 
obvious strain softening and low plastic elongation (~2%) [ 26 ]. In 
comparison, the present NC 14YWTZ alloys exhibit a moderate softening 
behavior and a reasonable plastic elongation of ~4%, as shown in Fig. 9 
(b). The strain softening phenomena in NC materials has been attributed 
to the residual internal stresses due to the dislocation absorption at the 
GBs [ 91 ]. Microcracks in brittle and quasi-brittle NC materials have 
been also considered as a trigger for the strain softening [ 50 , 92 ]. It is 
worth noting that residual pores are ubiquitous in conventional ODS 
alloys, resulting from the entrapped gas during mechanical alloying 
and/or the insufficient densification [ 23 , 35 , 51 ]. During the 
high-temperature consolidation, grain growth occurs rapidly, resulting 
in the retention of the entrapped gas. However, for NC materials, GBs are 
proved to be an effective diffusion path for the trapped gas to dissipate 
away along the GB network [ 41 , 42 ]. The NC matrix and the ultrahigh 
consolidation pressure (4 GPa) endow the present NC 14YWTZ alloys a 
porosity-free microstructure, as shown in Figs. 1(c) and 2(a) . However, 
for the few reported ODS alloys with extremely fine grain sizes below 
200 nm (inset of Fig. 11 ), a minimum consolidation temperature 
(850 
◦
C) was applied to achieve bulk alloys without any further hot 
working. Thus, the achievement of a NC microstructure is at the sacrifice 
of the density and mechanical properties (especially elongation) of the 
consolidated bulk alloys. The extremely prudent processing route results 
in a low relative density with prevalent residual pores in the matrix. 
These pores, even on a nanometer scale, can be stress concentration 
points, promoting the initiation and propagation of microcracks under 
tensile stress and resulting in strain softening and rapid fracture. 
On the other hand, oxide particles have also been proved to be an 
origin for microcrack initiation, and lower the fracture properties of ODS 
alloys [ 23 , 26 , 54 ]. The size of NPs in the reported NC 12YWT alloys 
ranges from 10 to 34 nm whereas that in the present NC 14YWTZ alloy is 
as small as ~3.3 nm ( Fig. 2 ), which may largely reduce the sensitivity to 
crack initiation. Nevertheless, the coarse oxides preferentially distrib -
uting at PPBs would be a major concern. Microcracks can initiate readily 
at the interface between these coarse oxides and the matrix and pene -
trate rapidly throughout the microstructure, resulting in a limited 
plasticity of ODS alloys. The present NC 14YWTZ alloys were fabricated 
by mechanical alloying in a high-purity argon atmosphere and subse -
quent high-pressure consolidation process in a sealed environment. 
Thus, the amount of coarse oxides has been controlled to less than 5% of 
the volume fraction of total oxide particles. However, it is worth noting 
that significant uptake of oxygen still occurs, as shown in Table 1 . Thus, 
more prudent protections of materials during the whole processing 
procedure are expected to further improve the plasticity of the present 
NC 14YWTZ alloys. 
5. Conclusion 
(1) A novel Zr-doped NC 14YWTZ ferritic ODS alloy was prepared by 
mechanical alloying and high-pressure consolidation techniques. 
The high consolidation pressure (4 GPa) endows the bulk NC 
14YWTZ alloy with a nearly full relative density (~99.5%). 
(2) The consolidated NC 14YWTZ alloy with an average grain size of 
~50 nm exhibits an ultra-high compressive yield strength of 
~2641 MPa. Besides, the alloy exhibits an exceptional thermal 
stability, where limited grain growth occurs after annealing at a 
high temperature of 1000 
◦
C. After hot rolling at 800 
◦
C, the alloy 
retains NC microstructures and has an unprecedented tensile 
yield strength of 2730 MPa with a reasonable plastic elongation 
of up to ~4%. These strength values far exceed those of previ -
ously reported ferritic ODS alloys. 
(3) Zr – Ti – Y – O nanoparticles with an average particle size of ~3.18 
nm and a high number density of ~3 × 10
24 
m
 3 
are formed in 
the NC 14YWTZ ferritic ODS alloy. These nanoparticles can 
strongly pin the GBs, suppressing the grain growth during 
annealing. Besides, multicomponent co-segregation of alloying 
elements, such as Zr, Y, O, Mn, and C, to GBs can largely decrease 
the GB energy, reducing the driving force for grain growth. The 
high thermal stability of the annealed NC 14YWTZ alloys can be 
ascribed to the combined effects of the thermodynamic multi -
component co-segregation and kinetic nanoparticles pinning. 
(4) The outstanding strength of the present NC 14YWTZ ferritic ODS 
alloys primarily originates from the Hall-Petch strengthening of 
the NC matrix, distinguishing them from conventional ODS alloys 
strengthened by dislocation forest and/or dispersed particles. 
Besides, the fully dense microstructure resulting from the high- 
pressure consolidation of the present NC 14YWTZ alloys endow 
them with a reasonable tensile elongation, which is critical to 
their practical applications.
