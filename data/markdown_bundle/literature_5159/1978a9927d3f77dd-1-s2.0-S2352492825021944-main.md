ABSTRACT
Reaching advancements in the additive manufacturing of titanium aluminum (TiAl) alloys is a challenge owing 
to the difficulty in processing and improving their high-temperature mechanical properties, which hinders their 
broad application in the aerospace industry. In this research, a lightweight and high-strength Ti
2
AlNb-based alloy 
(Ti-22Al-25Nb, at%) was manufactured with a high pre-heating temperature of 830 ℃ (near the initial precip -
itation temperature of the O phase) by electron beam powder bed fusion (PBF-EB). The as-printed samples 
showed ultimate mechanical properties (803 ± 30 MPa) at 650 ℃ . The tensile strength and fatigue crack growth 
(FCG) tests were used to analyze the fatigue and fracture behaviors for short-term and long-term service safety. 
Furthermore, an extensive examination of the deformation and fracture mechanisms of the PBF-EB samples was 
conducted using advanced characterization methods. The results showed that the fabricated Ti
2
AlNb-based alloy 
had excellent strength at high temperature, which was attributable to unique microstructural features, including 
the near-ubiquitous formation of equiaxed β phase grains and a significant abundance of the O phase achieved 
through a higher pre-heating temperature. The fracture mode was observed to be mainly transgranular in PBF- 
EB-fabricated samples at both room temperature and at 650 ℃ . Meanwhile, spheroidized O phase hinders the 
deformation of the grain boundaries of β phase and the movement of dislocations, showing the strengthening 
effect of O phase under stress. Moreover, the activation of more slip systems and the decrease of dislocation 
density are beneficial to improve the plastic deformation of β phase at 650 ℃ .
1. Introduction
Titanium aluminide (TiAl) alloys, known as advanced high- 
temperature materials with unique physical and mechanical proper -
ties, are widely used in aerospace engineering [1] . Ti-48Al-2Cr-2Nb 
alloy was the first alloys to be applied in low-pressure turbine blades 
and turbocharger wheels [2,3] . However, the limited applicability of 
these alloys is primarily due to their inadequate mechanical properties 
at high temperatures [4,5] . Ti
2
AlNb-based alloys (Ti-22Al-25Nb, at%), 
which belong to a new generation of TiAl alloys, are expected to replace 
Ni-based superalloys at 600 – 750 ℃ owing to their low-density 
(5.3 g/cm
3
) and excellent high-temperature mechanical properties [6, 
7] .
The Ti
2
AlNb-based alloy is composed of three distinct phases: the 
continuous matrix phase, β /B2 (BCC); the primary strengthening phase, 
O (orthorhombic); and the secondary strengthening phase, α
2 
(HCP) 
[8 – 10] . Currently, the production of Ti
2
AlNb-based alloy primarily re -
lies on casting and forging methods, resulting in challenges when 
manufacturing components with intricate designs and often necessi -
tating additional machining processes. Additionally, the processing ca -
pacity of Ti
2
AlNb-based is poor, resulting in high production costs [11, 
12] . Additive manufacturing (AM) technique has undergone rapid 
development and is expected to change the forming method of materials 
in the future. While powder metallurgy has the capability to achieve 
fabrication close to the desired shape, the mechanical characteristics of 
the resultant materials often fall short of expectations due to composi -
tion segregation [13 – 16] . Zhang et al . [17] utilized laser deposition (LD) 
to fabricate a Ti
2
AlNb-based alloy, which exhibited unsatisfactory ten -
sile strength due to the presence of a brittle α
2 
phase and coarse grain 
size. Zhou et al . [1] found that the manufacturing of Ti
2
AlNb-based via 
laser beam powder bed fusion (PBF-LB) yielded an alloy with fine grains 
that did not contain a brittle α
2 
phase, and also displayed excellent yield

working temperature of the last two turbine blades), which was closely 
related to the volume fraction and morphology of the O phase [18] . 
Accordingly, how the O phase enhances the high-temperature strength 
and how to improve the volume fraction of the O phase merit further

Extensive research has been conducted on the enhancement effect of 
the O phase, with a focus on understanding the deformation mecha -
nisms. Liu et al . [19] discovered that the mechanical properties can be 
significantly improved through the large-scale generation of the O 
phase. Meanwhile, Polozov et al . [20] observed that the existence of the 
O phase exerted a marked strengthening effect on TiAl-alloy micro -
hardness. The primary contribution of the O phase lies in its ability to 
impede the dislocation slip motion in the B2 phase, resulting in accu -
mulation of dislocations [21] . Furthermore, it was observed by Jia et al. 
[22] that the proportion of the O phase rose from 36.7 % to 47.5 % with 
an increase in sintering temperature from 950 to 1200 ℃ , aligning 
closely with the findings reported by Chen et al . [23] . Jia et al. [24]
reported that the number of O phases and the coarse spherical-O phase 
increased after heat treatment (HT), which suggested that a reduced 
cooling rate enhances the formation of the O phase from the B2 phase. 
Nevertheless, the precipitation of the brittle α
2 
phase during high tem -
perature treatment might not be beneficial for enhancing the mechani -
cal properties of the Ti
2
AlNb-based alloy [18] . The precipitation of the O 
phase depends on the solidification time of liquid metal at the O-phase 
transition temperature [25] . Consequently, it is feasible to enhance the 
precipitation and proliferation of numerous O phases by adjusting the 
ambient temperature within the range of precipitation temperature for 
the O phase during AM. Electron beam powder bed fusion (PBF-EB) can 
be used for fabricating Ti
2
AlNb-based alloy due to the higher preheating 
temperature (above 1000 ℃ ) used compared with PBF-LB and LD (below 
200 ℃ ) [1,26] . However, there is a limited amount of research available 
on the development of Ti
2
AlNb-based alloy using PBF-EB technology.
Fatigue and fracture behaviors are important factors for both short- 
term and long-term service safety. Numerous studies have highlighted 
the significance of an alloy ’ s microstructure in influencing its fatigue 
and fracture characteristics. Zhang et al. [27] found that incorporating a 
significant quantity of lamellar O phases can enhance fatigue resistance, 
resulting in remarkable improvements in both strength and plasticity of 
as-cast Ti
2
AlNb-based alloy. Additionally, Fu et al. [28] demonstrated 
that the β /B2 phase can inhibit the rapid propagation of cracks due to its 
excellent plastic deformation ability. Meanwhile, in the study conducted 
by Zhang et al. [29] , it was observed that enhanced resistance to crack 
propagation and improved fracture toughness can be achieved through 
the presence of larger initial β grains, a higher number of subgrains, 
grain boundary O phases with irregular and non-continuous patterns, as 
well as thicker coarse lamellae within the multi-scale lamellae structure. 
Many studies to date have only investigated the fatigue and fracture 
behaviors of Ti
2
AlNb-based alloy at RT. Consequently, it remains un -
certain whether the distinctive microstructure observed in PBF-EB 
samples, characterized by a significant presence of O phases, can 
effectively address the issue of rapid strength and plasticity deteriora -
tion experienced at elevated temperatures.
In summary, some key questions relating to the manufacture of 
Ti
2
AlNb-based alloy using PBF-EB remain to be answered, including the 
following: 
(1) Can PBF-EB technology be used to fabricate Ti
2
AlNb-based alloys 
with excellent mechanical properties at 650 ℃ ?
(2) If yes, what will be the microstructure of these alloys and the 
nature of the associated performance-strengthening mechanism, 
and will a significant quantity of O phases be generated?
(3) What will be the fatigue and fracture behaviors of the PBF-EB- 
manufactured samples during both short-term and long-term 
service processes?
In this work, the tensile property and the fatigue crack growth 
property of Ti
2
AlNb-based samples fabricated via PBF-EB at RT and high 
temperature have been revealed, which was closely related to the 
microstructure of the O and β phases. The fatigue and fracture behaviors 
were analyzed using advanced microstructural characterization tech -
niques for service safety at RT and 650 ℃ . In addition, a detailed analysis 
was conducted on the deformation mechanism of the O and β phases. 
Finally, the essential mechanism of microstructure deformation of as- 
printed sample within the service temperature range was revealed. 
This study holds great significance for the application of Ti-Al alloys in 
high-temperature environments.

2.1. Material and methods
The initial metal powder produced by Guangzhou Sailong Additive 
Manufacturing Co., Ltd was manufactured through electrode induction 
melting gas atomization (EIGA) to produce a spherical powder suitable 
for PBF-EB. The particle size used for PBF-EB ranged between 75 and 
125 μ m. The chemical composition of both the powder and the as- 
printed samples were examined using universal testing machines 
(HORIBA EMGA 930, Thermo ICAP 7200), as indicated in Table 1 . The 
significant amount of Al and Nb loss in the PBF-EB samples was related 
to an increase in the duration of overheating within the molten pool with 
a high preheating temperature, which was unavoidable during the 
printing process. Germann et al. found that Al content was an important 
factor affecting the ordering of the β phase [30] . Specifically, Al was 
enriched in the O phase, whereas Nb was enriched in the β phase, 
increasing Al content was beneficial to the ordering of the β phase, thus 
significantly reducing the plasticity of alloy containing the β phase. At

O phase due to Al being enriched in the O-phase grain, which was 
beneficial to improving the high-temperature strength of the TiAl alloy

4. Discussion
4.1. Microstructural deformation mechanism
It is evident from the data presented in Fig. 4 (a) that the elastic 
modulus exhibited a decrease at elevated temperatures compared to 
room temperature. This observation can be inferred from the slope of the 
tensile curve, suggesting that the Ti
2
AlNb-based alloy undergoes a 
softening phenomenon when exposed to a temperature of 650 ℃ . To 
further analyze the deformation behavior exhibited by the O phase and β 
phase, TEM was used to better visualize the microstructure of the sam -
ple, as depicted in Fig. 7 (a). The findings suggest that high-density 
dislocation networks are formed inside the as-printed alloy. High- 
resolution (HR) TEM ( Fig. 7 (b)) demonstrated that these dislocations 
were mainly distributed on the matrix of the β phase. In general, the 
mobility of dislocations plays a significant role in determining the plastic 
deformation behavior of crystalline materials. To sustain motion, the 
dislocation needs to surpass the stress field generated by the existing 
dislocations. Geometrical phase analysis (GPA) was employed to 
determine the spatial distribution of two-dimensional strain within the 
interface region through the measurement of localized displacements in 
lattice fringes [39,40] . The ε
xx 
strain in Fig. 7 (b) illustrated in Fig. 7 (c) 
was larger within the β phase than within the O phase, indicating that 
strong lattice distortion was generated mainly in the β phase grain. Thus, 
the O phase mainly overcomes the flow stress caused by the dislocation 
in the β phase, and then plays a pinning role in the β phase, hindering the 
further deformation of the β phase. Moreover, the existence of numerous 
O phases can hinder the further movement of dislocations, which en -
dows the alloy with greater strength and poor plastic deformation at RT, 
as shown in Fig. 4 (e – f). Gupta et al . found that the flow stress decreases 
as the temperature increases [41] , which facilitates the movement of the 
dislocation and improves the coordination of deformation between 
grains and the plasticity of as-printed samples at 650 ℃ . Simulta -
neously, the O phase also deforms and fractures under stress, as depicted 
in Fig. 4 (f).
The deformation behavior of the O and β phases near the crack 
propagation path based on high-magnification TEM images after FCG is 
shown in Fig. 7 (d – f). A schematic diagram of the TEM sample after FCG 
is shown in Fig. 1 (f). It is evident from Fig. 7 (d) and Fig. 7 (e) that the 
strip-shaped O phase produces a larger deformation at high temperature 
than at RT, that is, the degree of spheroidization of the O phase is higher, 
which indicates that the O phase is also softened at high temperature. 
Fig. 7 (f) shows that the deformation and spheroidization of the O phase 
are induced under stress, which is related to the dislocation wall formed 
in the O phase ( Fig. 7 (a)). The course of strip-shaped O-phase spher -
oidization is summarized in Fig. 7 (g). Dislocations are easily generated 
inside the slab and pile up to form dislocation sub-grain boundaries. The 
β phase wedges into the phase boundary of the strip-shaped O phase as 
the O/O phase boundary gradually separates, resulting in the fracture 
and spheroidization of the O phase, as shown in Fig. 7 (e, f).

4.2. The dislocation slip behavior in the β phase
The plastic deformation of the as-printed samples relies on the slip 
system of the β phase based on the abundant presence of dislocations 
( Fig. 7 (a)). Fig. 8 (a) and Fig. 8 (c) show the actual slip line of the fracture 
surface after tensile testing at RT and 650 ℃ . As can be seen, the β phase 
had better plastic deformation ability with more slip systems at high 
temperature. The {110} < 111 > , {112} < 111 > , and {123} 
< 111 > slip systems, as the possible slip systems of the β phase (BCC), 
had maximum Schmid factors of 0.5, 0.44, and 0.45, respectively. Ac -
cording to Schmid ’ s law, these observations suggest that the β phase 
moves along the {110} < 111 > slip system at RT and 650 ℃ , as

4.4. Effect of pores on crack propagation behavior for as-printed sample
To provide additional clarification on effect of pores on crack prop -
agation behavior of the Ti
2
AlNb-based sample fabricated by PBF-EB. 
KAM images reflected the micro-deformation and residual stress on 
the surface of FCG sample based on Fig. 9 (b) – (c). High residual stress 
was distributed near the crack growth path, indicating that cracks were 
easy to be produced along the pores under stress inducement. However, 
the relative position between the pores and the crack propagation path 
was not uniform, as shown in the SEM image ( Fig. 10 (a)). Generally, 
cracks tend to deflect toward the pores with any angle except 90 degrees 
under the action of Von Mises stress [48] , as shown in Fig. 10 (b). In the 
case of constant pore diameter (2 r ), with the increasing distance ( D ) 
between the crack tip and the center of the pore, Von Mises stress de -
creases, resulting cracks deflect preferentially towards closer pores [49] , 
which was also one of the reasons why the crack propagation path was 
not straight. When crack was long enough, each external stress would 
result in a branch in the crack path, especially near the pores due to 
stress concentration, which favored the secondary cracks to be pro -
duced. Rezanezhad et al . have made a detailed analysis of this phe -
nomenon, and their research results are consistent with this work [48, 
49] .

comparing with the forged components [23,55,56] . The forthcoming

potential solution.
5. Conclusion
This work provided supporting data indicating the successful pro -
duction of the Ti
2
AlNb-based alloy through PBF-EB technique. The as- 
printed Ti
2
AlNb-based alloy (nearly all the equiaxed grains were 
perpendicular and parallel to the BD) possessed a UTS of 803 ± 30 MPa 
and an EL of 3 ± 0.5 % at 650 ℃ , which is competitive compared to 
other AM processes (such as PBF-LB and EPM et al.). The β phase wedges 
into the strip-shaped O phase as the O/O phase boundary gradually 
separates under stress, resulting in the fracture and spheroidization of 
the O phase. Spheroidized O phase hinders the deformation of the grain 
boundaries of β phase and the movement of dislocations, showing the 
strengthening effect of O phase under stress. Moreover, the activation of 
more slip systems and the decrease of dislocation density are beneficial 
to improve the plastic deformation of β phase at 650 ℃ . This alloy is 
expected to be a potential replacement for Ni-based superalloys (UTS =
1000 MPa for the wrought IN718 at 650 ℃ in AMS 5662) in high- 
temperature applications.
