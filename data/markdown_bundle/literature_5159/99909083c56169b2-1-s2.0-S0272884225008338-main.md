ABSTRACT
Understanding reaction behavior of the high-entropy solid/liquid interface induced by multicomponent disso -
lution is critical for the construction of reliable brazed joints for high-temperature service. The brazed joint of 
(HfZrTiTaNb)C high-entropy carbide (HEC) bonded with a simple CoCrFeNi high-entropy alloy was investigated 
at 1440 – 1550 
◦
C for 15 – 60 min. Under bonding temperatures, HEC grains dissociated into the liquid alloy, and 
the self-dissolved Zr atoms at the interface between the HEC grains and liquid alloy induced the formation of Zr- 
riched HEC phase (ZHEC) attached to HEC grains. The ZHEC phases were characterized by the increased Zr 
content to 21.44 at.% and the coherent orientation with the HEC. The CoCrFeNi alloy did not undergo phase 
separation after solidifying in the middle of the joint, resulting in a high-entropy interfacial structure, which 
remained stabilized in the joints over a wide range of process parameters. In addition, the shear strength and 
high-temperature performance of the joints were evaluated. A joint strength of 266 MPa could be obtained when 
brazed at 1460 
◦
C for 45 min. At an test temperature of 1000 
◦
C, the joint strength still retained 87 MPa, 
indicating its positive potential for high-temperature applications. Intergranular fracture of the CoCrFeNi under 
elevated temperatures was responsible for the decrease in joint strengths.
1. Introduction
High-entropy carbides (HECs) are considered to be a novel structural 
ceramic [ 1 , 2 ], in which the randomly arranged equimolar or 
near-equimolar five or more transition metal atoms in the cationic sites 
and the carbon atoms in anionic sites deliver a mixed entropy of higher 
than 1.5 R (R is the gas constant) [ 3 – 5 ]. Accompanied by high entropy 
effects, the multi-elemental nature and the sluggish diffusion in 
large-distorted lattices of HECs distinguish their improved damaged 
tolerance from those of conventional carbides [ 6 , 7 ]. Cocktail perfor -
mances of the HECs, such as the combination of higher hardness, 
strength, and remarkable thermal stability [ 8 – 10 ], allow them to be 
potential structural ceramics for extreme thermal environments in the 
aerospace, nuclear industry, and other fields [ 11 – 13 ]. The components 
in these fields inevitably involve complex shapes, which require reliable 
HEC joints that match the performances of HECs to advance further 
engineering applications.
Vacuum active brazing has been explored for the preparation of HEC 
components with metallurgical bonding interfaces in a process where a 
liquid alloy fills the gap between two HEC substrates, and the effective 
bonding frequently depends on interfacial wetting and reactions 
involving active elements [ 14 , 15 ]. For conventional alloys served as 
filler materials, TiNi-20at.% Nb eutectic alloy was the earliest alloy 
published for joining the (HfZrTiTaNb)C, and the interfacial bonding 
relied on the formation of the (Ta, Nb)C phase induced by active Ti, and 
the obtained joint with Ti-Ni and β -Nb eutectic microstructure per -
formed a strength of 201 MPa and resisted at 800 
◦
C for 2 h [ 16 , 17 ]. The 
active Zr and Ti atoms in TiZrNiCu alloy collectively participated in the 
interfacial reactions in the (VNbTaMoW)C joints, and the flocculent (Ti, 
Zr, Nb)C phase and fine high-entropy intermetallic compounds 
contributed to the enhancement of interfacial load-bearing capacity to 
193 MPa [ 18 ]. In addition to being derived from original liquid alloys, 
active atoms that facilitated interfacial wetting and reactions could be 
introduced by the altered compositions of liquid pure metals or inert

2. Materials and methods
The (HfZrTiTaNb)C high-entropy carbide substrate was fabricated by 
ball milling and hot-press sintering process. Five mono-carbide powders 
with equimolar ratios were homogeneously mixed and milled at 300 
rpm for 24 h. The ball-milled powder was dried and sieved, and then 
sintered at 1850 
◦
C for 30 min under a pressure of 30 MPa to obtain HEC 
bulk. The detailed processes of preparation and performance evaluation 
can be referred to our previous work. The HEC possessed high relative 
density ( > 99 %), Vickers hardness (24 GPa), and flexural strength (494 
MPa) [ 16 ]. The arc-melted FeCoCrNi high-entropy alloy followed a 
regular melting process under a Ti-gettered high-purity argon atmo -
sphere [ 34 ], and then it was sliced and hand-thinned to 300 μ m thick.

The irregular gray phases protruding from the edges of HEC grains 
(position D in Fig. 3 (b)) had a significantly higher Zr content of 21.44 at. 
% than that of the HEC, being designated as Zr-riched HEC phase 
(ZHEC). In contrast to the elliptical morphology of THEC, the ZHEC was 
distinguished by the structural features of epitaxial growth, and it sug -
gested that the appearance of ZHEC was associated with the interfacial 
reaction triggered by the active Zr atoms dissolved from the HEC into the 
liquid CCFN. The inverse pole figure (IPF) of ZHEC/HEC interface in 
Fig. 4 (a) revealed the consistency of lattice orientations between ZHEC 
and neighboring HEC grains. This result implied that the formed ZHEC 
phase also succeeded in the structural advantage of HEC, despite un -
dergoing complex dissolution, atomic diffusion, and reaction processes. 
The elemental line distribution from the HEC to the ZHEC grain interior 
was shown in Fig. 4 (b – c), and the variation of Zr contents followed a 
successive trend, indicating that the aggregation of active Zr and the 
following interfacial diffusion induced the transformation from HEC to 
ZHEC phase. At the edge of HEC grain, the presence of the THEC phase 
led to the elevated contents of Ta and the depression of the curve rep -
resenting Ti content. This phenomenon reflected the atomic diffusion 
within the lattices of HEC and the interfacial inter-diffusion between 
dissociated HEC grains and liquid CCFN, during which the Ta with a 
lower metallic vacancy formation energy than that of the other transi -
tion metal atoms was retained in the stabilized HEC lattices [ 38 , 39 ], 
leading to the formation of the THEC phase.

metal atoms in the HEC, the inherent disorder of these atoms was pre -
served, while the high solid-solution tendency of the reaction contrib -
uted to the emergence of the ZHEC/HEC and their high-entropy 
interfaces with CCFN.

4. Conclusions
Reliable HEC joints with distinctive high-entropy heterogeneous 
interfaces had been successfully fabricated by employing a CCFN alloy, 
and the evolution of the interfacial structure in response to brazing 
process parameters has been elucidated. In addition, the shear strengths 
and high-temperature performances of the HEC joints were assessed. 
The main conclusions obtained were as follows. 
(1) At bonding temperatures, the HEC dissociated along grain 
boundaries and dissolved intensely into liquid CCFN, and liquid 
CCFN penetrated the HEC substrate. The Zr atoms in the liquid 
CCFN served as active atoms and induced the formation of ZHEC 
phases dependent on the dissociated HEC grains, which was 
characterized by the increased Zr content to 21.44 at.% and the 
coherent orientation with the HEC. Due to the low solid solubility 
of Hf and Zr atoms in liquid CCFN, a certain amount of twinned 
Laves phases were formed in the brazing seam, where the liquid 
CCFN did not occur phase separation after solidification, result -
ing in a high-entropy interfacial structure of HEC/CCFN/HEC.
(2) When increasing the bonding temperature from 1440

size and amount of ZHEC phase, and the width of CCFN reduced 
from 37.5 μ m to 4.4 μ m due to the improved wettability and 
diffusion consumption. With the extension of bonding times from 
15 min to 60 min, the dissociated HEC grains significantly 
increased, and the strengthened penetration of liquid CCFN into
