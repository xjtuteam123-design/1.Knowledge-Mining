Double minimum creep in the rafting regime of a single-crystal
Co-base superalloy

To investigate whether the formation of rafts in Co-base superalloys has a hardening or softening effect, the single
crystal deformation behavior was studied in the high temperature/low stress creep regime. The large positive lat-
tice misﬁt of the investigated Co-base superalloy led to a pronounced directional coarsening of the microstruc-
ture normal to the stress axis during compressive creep at 950 °C/150 MPa. Two creep minima were observed
similar to Ni-base superalloys under tensile creep. Rafting was accompanied by a decreasing creep rate indicating
a hardening effect, although cutting of γ′ rafts by partial dislocations under the formation of stacking faults
occurred.

Precipitation strengthened Co-base superalloys have been recog-
nized as a promising new class of structural materials for high temper-
ature applications [1–3]. Although experimental ﬁndings show that γ′
strengthened Co-base superalloys share many similarities with the
well-established γ/γ′ Ni-base superalloys [4–6], the microstructural
evolution during high temperature creep and the deformation mecha-
nisms are different [7–11].
In general, the creep deformation mechanisms of single-crystal (SX)
superalloys depend strongly on the applied temperatures and stresses.
This becomes apparent in the different shapes of the corresponding
creep curves, in particular when the strain rate is plotted against the
plastic strain. Such creep curves of Ni-base superalloys exhibit a double
creep rate minimum in the low temperature/high stress creep regime
(e.g. 800 °C/654 MPa) [12]. In a recent study Wu et al. comprehensively
discussed the occurrence of two creep minima in this regime[13].I nt h e
medium temperature/medium stress creep regime (e.g. 950 °C/
241 MPa) the strain rate vs. strain creep curve exhibits only one mini-
mum, while in the high temperature/low stress creep regime (e.g.
1100 °C/140 MPa), again two minima were observed for Ni-base super-
alloys [12,14]. The latter was related to the formation of plate-like γ′
rafts normal to the applied tensile stress axis (N-type rafting), as illus-
trated in Fig. 1. N-type rafting occurs due to the negative γ/γ′ lattice
misﬁt, which is deﬁned as δ =2 ( a
γ′ − aγ)/(aγ′ +a γ), i.e. the lattice pa-
rameter of theγ′ precipitate phase, aγ′, is smaller than the lattice param-
eter of the γ′ matrix phase. The plate-likeγ′ rafts effectively prevent the
a/2〈110〉{111}dislocations to glide/climb around the γ′ precipitates
at elevated temperatures, while shearing of the γ′ phase takes place
only in the late creep stages at higher strains. In contrast, meander-
like γ′ rafts develop parallel to the tensile stress axis (P-type rafting)
in Co-base superalloys due to their positive lattice mis ﬁt; however, N-
type rafting occurs during compressive creep [8,10,15–17], see Fig. 1.
For further details on the relationship between the lattice misﬁt, the ap-
plied stresses and the direction of rafting the reader is referred to [18,
19]. Additionally, the γ′ precipitate phase in Co-base superalloys has a
lower resistance against shearing by dislocations. Thus cutting of rafts
mainly by a/3 〈112〉{111} type partial dislocations easily occurs in
the early creep stages[7,10,16]. So far no double minimum creep behav-
ior was observed forγ′ strengthened Co-base superalloys during tensile
creep in the high temperature/low stress rafting regime (e.g. 1000 °C/
137 MPa) [9,10,16].
This raises the question whether the formation of rafts can also have
a hardening effect in the high temperature/low stress regime of single-
crystal Co-base superalloys even though the deformation mechanisms
are different and shearing of the γ′ phase is easier. In order to under-
stand this, the compressive creep behavior of a SX Co-base superalloy
with N-type rafting was investigated in the present study. This enables
a direct comparison between Co- and Ni-base superalloys with similar
rafting type to assess the role of rafting and the different resistance of
the γ′ phase against cutting by dislocations in Co-base superalloys. Ad-
ditionally, the lattice mis ﬁt of the SX Co-base superalloy was studied,
because the knowledge of the γ/γ′ lattice misﬁt at creep temperature
is essential to understand the microstructural response to mechanical
loading during high temperature creep.

A quaternary Ta-containing SX Co-base model superalloy, hence-
forth referred to as ERBOCo-2Ta, of the composition Co-9Al-7.5W-2Ta
(in at.%) was investigated. A SX bar with a length of 120 mm and a diam-
eter of 12 mm was cast in a laboratory scale Bridgman unit. The melt
was homogenized for 0.5 h at 1550 °C and subsequently cast into the
mold that was preheated at the same temperature. The solidi ﬁcation
was performed at a withdrawal rate of 3 mm/min (for further details,
see [20]). The SX bar with a misorientation within 5° away from [001]
was solution heat treated at 1300 °C for 12 h followed by water
quenching and subsequently aged at 900 °C for 200 h to generate a γ/
γ′ microstructure. Differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) was per-
formed to determine the phase transformation temperatures using a
heating and cooling rate of 5 °C/min. Neutron diffraction (ND) experi-
ments were conducted at temperatures from 25 °C to 1100 °C using
the high-resolution powder diffractometer SPODI at FRM II in Garching,
Germany [21] to determine the lattice parameter of theγ and γ′ phase.
A cylindrical sample with a diameter of 6 mm was spark eroded from
the polycrystalline starter block of the SX bar and sealed in a Nb capsule
for the measurement. A Co-9Al-9W alloy (composition in at.%)[22] with
the same heat treatment was investigated for comparison. Further in-
formation on the ND experiments and the data evaluation can be
found in [6,23,24]. Cylindrical creep specimens with a length of
7.5 mm and a diameter of 5 mm were machined from the fully heat-
treated bar parallel to the solidi ﬁcation direction. Compressive creep
tests were performed at 950 °C and 150 MPa with the stress axis close
to the [001] orientation of the SX samples. Several tests were carried
out and interrupted at speciﬁc creep strains. The load was kept constant
during furnace cooling to room temperature. After standard metallo-
graphic sample preparation, the microstructure was characterized by
scanning electron microscopy (SEM) using a Zeiss Cross Beam 1540 EsB
FIB system. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) investigations
were performed on a Philips/FEI CM 200 operated at 200 kV. Electrolytic
thinning of the samples was conducted with a solution consisting of
55 vol% methanol, 37 vol% 2-butoxyethanol, 5 vol% perchloric acid and
3 vol% water at about −25 °C using a Struers Double Jet Tenupol-5.
The aged microstructure of the SX superalloy ERBOCo-2Ta is shown
in Fig. 2(a). After heat treatment at 1300 °C for 12 h and aging at 900 °C
for 200 h,ﬁne γ′ precipitates distributed homogeneously in theγ matrix
and no other phases were present. Theγ′ precipitates exhibited a cuboi-
dal morphology with an average edge length of about 0.144 μm. The
area fraction of the γ′ phase was ~64% which corresponds to a volume
fraction of the cubicγ′ precipitates of ~ 51%.Fig. 2(b) displays the ND de-
termined lattice parameters of theγ and γ′ phases as a function of tem-
perature. A gradual increase of both γ and γ′ lattice
parameters with
increasing temperature is clearly evident, while the γ phase shows a
more pronounced expansion as expected from its disordered nature.
The γ/γ′ lattice mis ﬁt δ, was + 0.74% at room temperature and
decreased to approximately + 0.40% at 1000 °C ( Fig. 2(c)). At 1100 °C
which is close to the γ′ solvus temperature of 1120 °C (as determined
by DSC), only fundamental reﬂections of the γ phase are found because
nearly all of theγ′ precipitates have been dissolved.Fig. 2(c) also shows
that the lattice misﬁt of the Co-9Al-9W alloy is in good agreement to the
room temperature misﬁt published in [1].I tc o nﬁrms that the addition
of Ta results in a much larger positive misﬁt over the entire temperature
range due to the strong partitioning of W and Ta to theγ′ phase [5].T h i s
is consistent with the ﬁndings by Tanaka et al. [10], who determined a
similar lattice misﬁt of the alloy Co-9Al-7W-2Ta-20Ni as a function of
temperature. The large magnitude of the lattice mis ﬁt in ERBOCo-2Ta
even at creep temperature was expected to cause high initial internal
stresses between the matrix and the precipitates and strongly affect
the rafting kinetics during creep.
Compressive creep experiments at 950 °C and 150 MPa were con-
ducted to investigate the high temperature creep behavior in the N-
type rafting regime. When the logarithmic strain rate vs the strain is
plotted, softening and hardening stages can be identi ﬁed in Fig. 3(a).
The creep curve is characterized by an initially decreasing creep rate
with a ﬁrst local creep rate minimum and is followed by a second
global creep rate minimum. Afterwards the creep rate increases
continuously until approximately 2.5% creep strain is reached. This
compressive creep behavior is similar to the tensile creep behavior
of a SX Ni-base superalloy CMSX-4 in the high temperature/low
stress creep regime with N-type rafting [12] (Fig. 3 (b)).

To directly correlate the creep curves with the microstructural evo-
lution and deformation mechanisms, additional creep tests were
interrupted shortly after the creep rate maximum and at the global
creep rate minimum, see Fig. 3(a). The crept microstructures of trans-
verse sections close to the (001) plane and longitudinal sections close
to the (100) plane were investigated. Fig. 4(a) and (b) show the crept
microstructure directly after the local creep rate maximum was
reached. Some γ′ precipitates are connected with each other along
[010] and [100] cube direction normal to the external stress axis, indi-
cating that rafting already started. The rafting direction is consistent
with N-type rafting, which is expected for ERBOCo-2Ta with a large pos-
itive lattice misﬁt( Fig. 2(c)) under compressive creep. The presence of a
lot of γ′ terminations and verticalγ channels indicated that rafting was
not ﬁnished yet ( Fig. 4(a) and (b)). This is con ﬁrmed by comparison
with the microstructure at the global creep rate minimum, where the
plate-like rafted microstructure has been fully developed with the
largely vanished verticalγ channels and a much lower fraction ofγ′ ter-
minations ( Fig. 4 (d) and (e)). Thus the microstructural evaluation
strongly indicated that the continuous rafting process was related to
the decreasing creep rate until the global minimum was reached. The
N-type rafting was thus appropriately considered as a strengthening
mechanism since the a/2〈110〉-type dislocations within the γ chan-
nels are blocked from climbing around the γ′ phase via elimination of
the vertical channels.
The hardening effect of N-type rafts seems to be similar to that in Ni-
base superalloys. After reaching the local minimum, dislocations cross-
slip and climb into the vertical γ channels which leads to the creep
rate plateau. With ongoing deformation and raft formation the rafts hin-
der the climb of dislocations and are not cut by dislocations before the
global minimum creep rate is reached [12]. In ERBOCo-2Ta as studied
here, however, the TEM investigations on the sample that was
interrupted directly after the local creep rate maximum revealed occa-
sional shearing of the γ′ rafts by partial dislocations leading to the for-
mation of superlattice stacking faults ( Fig. 4 (c)). These superlattice
stacking faults have two different orientations, indicating shearing of
the γ′ phase by partial dislocations operating on at least two {111} slip
planes. During compressive creep in Co-Al-W alloys at 850 °C and 900
°C, a/2〈110〉-type dislocations start to operate on {111} slip planes
in the horizontal channels [8]. They deposit at the γ/γ′ interfaces and
do not cut into γ′ pr
ecipitates to form anti phase boundaries (APBs)
like in Ni-base or CoNi-base superalloys [7]. However, they can react
with each other at the interface and dissociate to form a/3 〈112〉-
type dislocations, as also found by Tanaka et al. and Titus et al. on similar
quaternary Co-Al-W-Ta superalloys that were crept under tension at
1000 °C and 900 °C, respectively [7,10]. Such partial dislocations cut
the γ′ phase under the formation of superlattice intrinsic (SISF) or
superlattice extrinsic stacking faults (SESF). At the global creep mini-
mum, an increasing number of superlattice stacking faults was ob-
served. Fig. 4 (f) shows several stacking faults with different
orientations on different {111} planes (marked by arrows). Although

Therefore, it is reasonable to conclude that the rafting process, which
prevents the a/2 〈110〉-type matrix dislocations from entering the
vertical γ channels is the dominant hardening mechanism contributing
to the decreasing creep rate after theﬁrst local minimum. Recently, an-
other hardening mechanism in a quinary alloy Co-7Al-8W-1Ta-4Ti was
proposed in the medium stress/medium temperature regime (i.e.
420 MPa/900 °C) [9,25]. Two leading partial dislocations that create
superlattice stacking faults on different {111} planes can meet each
other, react and form a Lomer-Cotrell-type lock. This hardening mecha-
nism could also be active during creep of ERBOCo-2Ta at 950 °C/
150 MPa, however, it seems that it did not play the dominant role
here, because the rafts were not frequently cut after the local maximum
so it is less likely that two partial dislocations on different slip planes
met and interacted with each other. In comparison to the compressive
creep curves of this work, no pronounced second, global creep rate min-
imum was present in the creep curves of Co-Al-W-Ta-(Ti) superalloys
that were crept at 1000 °C and 137 MPa tensile stresses [10,16,17].
This can be a result of the different shape of the P-type rafts (mean-
der-like) compared to the N-type rafts (plate-like), see Fig. 1.A n o t h e r
reason could also be the higher creep temperature used in the previous
studies [10,16,17]. Higher temperatures or higher stresses may facilitate
frequent cutting ofγ′ rafts which diminishes the hardening effect of the
γ′ rafts. Recent investigations indicated that the precipitate shearing
process may need diffusion-assisted local elemental segregation within
the γ′ precipitates which is faster at higher temperatures and which can
impact the shearing processes as well [26,27].
In summary, the inﬂuence of rafting on the creep behavior has been
investigated in a Ta-containing single-crystal Co-base superalloy. The
large positive lattice mis ﬁt ranged from + 0.74% at room temperature
to + 0.40% at 1000 °C as measured by neutron diffraction, causing N-
type rafting perpendicular to the compressive stress axis during creep.
The creep behavior was characterized by an initial local creep rate min-
imum (~ 1 × 10
−8 s−1 at 0.22% strain) and a subsequent global creep
rate minimum (~2×10−9 s−1 at 0.76% strain) in the strain rate vs. strain
creep curve. The continuous decreasing creep rate after the local creep
rate maximum was accompanied by the formation of plate-like γ′
rafts which indicate that rafting is an effective strengthening mecha-
nism. These positive rafting effects are similar to that in Ni-base super-
alloys during creep in the N-type rafting regime, although shearing of
the γ′ phase by partial dislocations associated with the formation of
superlattice stacking faults occurred in the current study.
