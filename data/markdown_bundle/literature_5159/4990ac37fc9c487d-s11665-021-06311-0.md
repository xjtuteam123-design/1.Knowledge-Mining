Effects of Solution Heat Treatment Temperatures
on Microstructures and Mechanical Properties of SP-700
Ti Alloys
Yang-Chun Chiu, Jun-Hao Chen, and Sheng-Long Lee
Submitted: 25 May 2021 / Revised: 19 September 2021 / Accepted: 25 September 2021 / Published online: 5 November 2021
The aim of this study is to explore the effects of solution heat treatment temperatures on the microstructures
and mechanical properties of water-quenched SP-700 Ti alloys. The main objective is to determine whether
the tensile strength and ductility of the SP-700 Ti alloys conform to the quality requirements of armor
materials for aircraft, spacecraft, other transportation vehicles, sporting goods, and turbine blades.
Therefore, we have experimented with different solution heat treatment temperatures in order to obtain
alloys with more excellent tensile strength and ductility. The results showed that after SP-700 Ti alloys ( b
transus temperatures is 900 /C176C) were subjected to solution heat treatment and then quenched with water,
the microstructures of the high-temperature single-phase region (950 /C176C and 900 /C176C) were composed of the
soft residual b
r base phase and distributed, hard, and brittle needle-like a‘‘ martensite phase. The tensile
strength and ductility of this material were not good. In the comparatively lower-temperature dual-phase
region (750 /C176C), the microstructure of the water-quenched alloy was composed of a residual b base phase
and a pro-eutectoid a phase. Because no martensite phase occurred in the alloy, we observed no obvious
work hardening characteristics. However, it exhibited the highest impact toughness and hardness of all the
fabricated materials. By contrast, in the high-temperature dual-phase region (850 /C176C and 800 /C176C), because
the room temperature was still between the martensite start (M s) and martensite ﬁnish (M f) temperatures,
the microstructures of the alloys were composed of the softer br base phase, hard brittle pro-eutectoid a
phase ( ap) and a’’ martensite phase. With the ‘‘stress-induced martensite phase transformation’’ that
occurred during the tensile test, the SP-700 Ti alloys experienced a high strength increase and high ductility
characteristics. Especially, when the solution heat treatment temperature was 800 /C176C, the alloy had the
highest strength and ductility, and therefore best met the requirements of armor material standards (MIL-
DTL-46077).
Keywords martensite phase, mechanical properties, solution heat
treatment, SP-700
1. Introduction
The SP-700 (Ti-4.5Al-3V-2Mo-2Fe) titanium alloy is an
(a+b)-type dual-phase titanium alloy that contains more b-
stabilizing elements than the Ti-6Al-4V titanium alloy. There-
fore, its b-transition temperature ( b-transus at approximately
900/C176 C) is approximately 100 /C176 C lower than that of the Ti-6Al-
4V Ti alloy (Ref 1, 2). Because SP-700 Ti alloys have excellent
processability, superplasticity, mechanical properties, and heat
treatment capabilities (Ref 3, 4), they have been widely used in
ﬁelds such as the aerospace industry, military industry,
automobiles, motorcycles, sporting goods, and turbine blades
(operating temperature up to 550 /C176 C) (Ref 6).
Because the martensite ﬁnish temperature of SP-700 Ti
alloys is lower than room temperature (Ref 5), when the alloy is
solid solution heat treated in the b-single-phase region that is
higher than the b transition temperature (900 /C176 C) (Ref 6) and
then quenched to room temperature, a portion of the b phase
experiences martensite phase transformation, thereby forming
an orthorhombic a¢¢ martensite phase. The microstructure of the
overall alloy is an a¢¢ phase and a residual br phase base. When
the (a+b) dual-phase region is solid solution heat treated below
the b transition temperature, the microstructure is composed of
the pro-eutectoid a phase ( a
p), br phase, and a¢¢ martensite
phase (Ref 6, 7). When the solid solution temperature is lower,
the equilibrium b phase at the solid solution heat treatment
temperature contains higher b stabilizing elements, making the
martensite start temperature in the b phase lower than room
temperature (Ref 24). The b phase does not cause any phase
transformations during the water-quenching process. The
microstructure of the alloy at room temperature is composed
of the a
p and unchanged br phases. In addition, the room
temperature mechanical strength of the a phase of the Ti alloy
is higher than that of the b phase (Ref 22, 23). The more the a
phase content is, the higher the yield strength of the alloy
becomes. Therefore, the amount and morphology of the a
phase are the main factors affecting the yield strength of these
Ti alloys (Ref 8, 9).

If br phase exists in the titanium alloy after the solid solution
heat treatment and water quenching, the stress-induced marten-
site of br phase will produce a¢¢ martensite phase ( a¢¢ is a
metastable phase, so it is an orthorhombic structure.) during the
plastic deformation process (Ref 7). Thus, a signiﬁcant work
hardening phenomenon appears (Ref 10, 11). The ‘‘stress-
induced martensite phase transformation’’ of the br phase in the
SP-700 Ti alloy is determined by the M
d temperature, at which
the br phase begins to exhibit stress-induced martensite
transformation (M d > Ms) (Ref 12). If the M d temperature is
higher than room temperature, then the stress-induced marten-
site phase forms. In contrast, when the M d temperature is lower
than room temperature, then there is no stress-induced marten-
site phase transformation (Ref 13). During the tensile testing
process, when the stress-induced martensite phase transforma-
tion in the SP-700 Ti alloy is more signiﬁcant, the tensile
strength increases and the ductility improves (Ref 1, 14).
However, in previous studies, the effects of the solution
temperature on the microstructures and the mechanical prop-
erties of water-quenched SP-700 Ti alloys have not been
investigated.
In this study, solution heat treatments at different temper-
atures were used to evaluate their effects on the hardness,
tensile properties and impact toughness of SP700 titanium
alloys, and to understand the development of microstructures to
obtain the alloys that meet the mechanical property require-
ments of the titanium alloy armor material speciﬁcations (MIL-
DTL-46077) (Ref 15-17). It is hoped that the best solution heat
treatment temperature will have positive contributions to the
future application of SP700 titanium alloys (Ref 18).
2. Experimental Procedures
Commercially, room temperature cold extruded SP-700 Ti
alloy bars with 45 mm diameters were used as experimental
materials. Their components are shown in Table 1. After the
alloys were annealed at 720 /C176 C for 2 hours, they were solution-
treated in a protective atmosphere furnace at 950 /C176 C, 900 /C176 C,
850/C176 C, 800 /C176 C, and 750 /C176 C, respectively, for 1 hour, and then
water-quenched to room temperature.
An optical microscope (OM; Olympus BX60M), low-
vacuum scanning electron microscope (LV-SEM; Hitachi S-
3500N), and ultra-high vacuum ﬁeld emission scanning
electron microscope (FE-SEM; Hitachi S-800) were used to
observe the microstructures. ImageJ 1.45s software was used
for image analysis. Samples were taken in 10 different areas
with an area of 4800 lm
2 each to estimate the percentage of the
whole area occupied by the ap phase. The alloy phases were
identiﬁed by using x-ray powder diffraction (D8AXRD Bruker,
Cu Ka (k = 0.1542 nm). The diffraction surface was calculated
by using Bragg /C213s Law and a plane-spacing equation. The
hardness (Hv) was measured by using Vicker /C213s hardness (Load:
1 kgw, loading time: 10 seconds). An Instron-4505 tensile
testing machine was used to perform tensile tests at room
temperature. The tensile speed was 1.5 mm/min, and the tensile
test bars adopted ASTM E8/E8M 11 standards (Ref 19). A
Charpy impact testing machine was used to conduct impact
tests at /C050/C176 C, 25 /C176 C, 50 /C176 C, and 100 /C176 C. The standard
speciﬁcations for the impact test pieces adopted ASTM E23-
12c standards (Ref 20). Subsequently, the density of cavities
was quantitatively analyzed with a MA TLAB /C212 open source
toolbox software MTEX to quantitatively analyze the cavity
sizes.
3. Results and Discussion
3.1 Microstructural Observation
The optical microstructures of the water-quenched SP-700
Ti alloys in the b single-phase region after undergoing the
solution heat treatment at 950 /C176 C and 900/C176 C above the b-transus
temperature are shown in Fig. 1(a) and (b), respectively. Both
of these microstructures were remarkably similar, demonstrat-
ing dense and ﬁne needle-like a¢¢ martensite phases distributed
in the residual b base phase ( br). Following the high-
temperature b single-phase solution heat treatment, no ap
phase was present to obstruct the movement of grain bound-
aries, resulting in the rapid growth of b-phase crystal grains. It
can be clearly seen that the 950 /C176 C solid solution-treated alloy
had coarser grains than the 900 /C176 C solid solution-treated alloy
because a higher temperature resulted in faster crystal grain
growth.
The SP-700 Ti alloys in the ( a+b) dual-phase region were
subjected to solution heat treatment and water quenching below
the b-transus temperature; the microstructures are shown in
Fig. 2. Figure 2(a) shows that when the solid solution temper-

approximately 28% on the basis of the quantiﬁcation of the
phase composition. When the solution temperature dropped to
800/C176 C, the quantity of ap phase in the br base phase was greater

ap was approximately 62%. The microstructure was mainly the
ap phase with a strength higher than that of the b phase, as
shown in Fig. 2(c). Using a scanning electron microscope

3.2 Mechanical Property Tests
The relationship between the solution heat treatment and
hardness of SP-700 Ti alloys can be discussed in two parts.
Although trace amounts of martensite (or no martensite) may
be present in the ( a+b) dual-phase region when the alloys were
water-quenched after undergoing solution heat treatment at
750/C176 C-850/C176 C, the main microstructures of the alloys were the
pro-eutectoid a phase ( a
p) and residual b phase ( br). As the
solution temperature was increased, the volume fraction of the
softer br phase was higher and the harder ap fraction was lower,
as shown in Fig. 2. Therefore, the hardness of the alloy
decreased as the solid solution temperature increased, as shown
in Fig. 5. Because the hard, brittle, needle-like martensite a¢¢
phase existed in the b base phase when the solution temperature
was 900 /C176 C in the b single-phase region, as shown in Figure 1.
The hardness of this sample was higher than that of the water-
quenched alloy after undergoing solution treatment at 850 /C176 C.
Although the b phase grains were coarser when the solution
temperature rose to 950 /C176 C, the overall microstructure did not
greatly change, and the hardness was not signiﬁcantly different
from that of the water-quenched alloy after undergoing solution
heat treatment at 900 /C176 C.
The stress–strain curves of the water-quenched SP-700 Ti
alloys after undergoing solution heat treatment at room

the solid solution temperatures were 900 /C176 C and 950 /C176 C in the b
single-phase region, the alloy microstructures were hard, brittle,
needle-like martensite distributed in the br phase base, as
shown in Fig. 1, and no obvious stress-induced martensite

When the solid solution heat treatment was performed at
750/C176 C in the ( a+b) dual-phase region, the microstructure of the
alloy was composed of the ap phase and b phase, as shown in
Fig. 2. Because this sample contained the largest amount of the

work hardening occurred because the stress-induced martensite
phase transformation did not occur. The increases in the yield
and tensile strengths were approximately 170 MPa, but the
sample still demonstrated good ductility mainly because the
microstructure did not contain the hard, brittle martensite phase.
After undergoing solution heat treatment at 850 /C176 C and
800/C176 C in the ( a+b) dual-phase region, a signiﬁcant stress-
induced martensite phase transformation occurred during the
stretching process, and the work hardening rate of these alloys
increased. After undergoing solution heat treatment at 850 /C176 C,
the water-quenched alloy conformed to the required conditions
of a low yield strength and high strain hardening rate. This
alloy had a high ductility (17%) and high tensile strength
(1100 MPa), and the increases in the yield and tensile strengths
were approximately 390 MPa. The yield strength of the water-
quenched alloy after undergoing solution heat treatment at
800/C176 C was slightly higher than that of the water-quenched alloy
after treatment at 850 /C176 C because its microstructure contained
more a
p phases, as shown in Fig. 2, and it exhibited a good
high strain hardening rate. This alloy had a comparatively
higher ductility (19%) and higher tensile strength (1200 MPa).
The yield and tensile strengths were also increased to 450 MPa.
a¢¢ martensite phase and stress-induced a¢¢ martensite phase
were both fragile phases. When the solid solution heat
treatment temperature was over 900 /C176 C, the main object of the
tensile test was the a¢¢ martensite phase. Therefore, the ductility
performance was poor. However, at a solid solution heat
treatment temperature (800 /C24850/C176 C), the main object of the
tensile test was a soft and ductile a
p phase matrix. During the
stretching process, the br residual phase transformed into a
stress-induced a¢¢ martensite phase. Secondary hardening
(similar to dispersion strengthening) occurred in the soft and
ductile a
p phase matrix, thereby increasing the strength and
maintaining the ductility of the ap phase matrix.
In summary, the water-quenched alloy after undergoing
solution heat treatment at 750 /C176 C did not conform to the
required conditions of possessing a low yield strength and high
strain-hardening rate. The ductility (15%) and tensile strength
(1000 MPa) of this alloy were lower than those of the water-
quenched alloys after undergoing solution heat treatments at
850/C176 C and 800 /C176 C, but it had a higher ductility than the water-
quenched alloys obtained after undergoing solution temperature
treatment in the b single-phase region at 900 /C176 C and 950 /C176 C.
The alloys with a low yield strength and high strain
hardening rate exhibited high uniform deformability. Moreover,
these alloys showed a high ductility and high strength
enhancement characteristics during the stretching process. It
is worth noting that although the water-quenched alloys after
undergoing solution heat treatment at 850 /C176 C and 800 /C176 C
contained trace amounts of hard, brittle, needle-like martensite,
they did not signiﬁcantly affect the tensile ductility.
From the tensile test results shown in Fig. 6, it can be seen
that the mechanical properties of the SP-700 Ti alloys after
undergoing solution heat treatment in the high-temperature b
single-phase region were not ideal. Therefore, these results will
not be included in the subsequent discussion of the impact
toughness.
The impact toughness values of the water-quenched SP-700
Ti alloys after undergoing solution heat treatment in the ( a+b)
dual-phase region at different testing temperatures are shown in
Fig. 7. It can be seen that a higher solution heat treatment
temperature resulted in a higher volume fraction of the b phase
with an improved ductility. However, because of the water
quenching in the base, the ratio of the hard, brittle martensite
phase that was transformed from the b phase was also higher;
therefore, the impact toughness decreased as the solid solution
temperature increased. It can be seen from Fig. 7 that after
undergoing solution treatment at 750 /C176 C, this water-quenched
alloy had the best impact toughness because no martensite was
present. This indicates that the presence of needle-like, hard,
brittle martensite had a negative effect on the impact toughness
values of the other alloys after undergoing solution heat
treatments at 850 /C176 C and 800 /C176 C. It can also be seen that when
the temperature of the impact test piece increased, the impact
toughness also increased. However, if the temperature of the
test piece was lower than room temperature, it did not have any
signiﬁcant impact on the changes in the impact toughness.

3.3 The Evaluation and Suggestions of SP-700 Ti Alloys
Used in Armored Vehicle Plates
From the above experimental results, to meet the strength
and ductility requirements of the Ti alloy armor plate standard
speciﬁcations (MIL-DTL-46077; UTS = 120 ksi * 827 MPa,
EL% = 10%) (Ref 18), the SP-700 Ti alloys must be subjected
to the solid solution heat treatment followed by water
quenching in the dual-phase region ( <900/C176 C). When the heat
treatment temperature exceeds 750 /C176 C, the alloys exhibit a low
yield strength and high work-hardening rate, as well as a high
strength and high ductility. When the solid solution temperature
is 800/C176 C, the yield strength of the alloy increases and the work
hardening rate becomes more signiﬁcant. These alloys exhibit a
higher tensile strength and ductility. The results of this study
may be used as a reference for the heat treatment conditions of
SP-700 Ti alloys for application toward armored vehicle plates.
Table 3 shows the comparison of the mechanical properties of
SP700 Ti alloy and other armor plates. It can be seen that
SP700 is a practical material with excellent strength and
ductility.
