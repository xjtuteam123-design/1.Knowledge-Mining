1. Introduction
TiAlalloyshavebeenreceivingconsiderableattention
recently because of its attractive properties such as low
density, excellent high temperature strength and good
oxidation resistance [1,2]. TiAl base alloys of engineer-
ing interest usually is not in the composition range of
single g phase; a slightly oﬀ-stoichiometric composition
on the Ti-rich side results ing+a dual phase alloys. It
has been shown that microstructure control, in addition
to composition control, is the main factor to determine
the strength of the TiAl alloys [3,4]. Usually, the nearg
(NG) or duplex (DP) microstructures has superior ten-
sile ductility at room temperature, but fracture tough-
nessandhightemperature strengtharelow.Incontrast,
thelamellarmicrostructureshowsexcellentstrengthand
fracture toughness, with a low tensile ductility at room
temperature.
Kim [3] has reported that the high Nb containing
TiAl base alloy developed by Chen [5–12] is the ﬁrst
example for the development of high performance TiAl
alloy for high temperature application. The high Nb
containing TiAl alloy exhibited very high strengths at
both roomtemperatureandhigh temperatures[10].The
creepstrengthretentionis60–100
/C14Chigherthanthatof
conventional TiAl alloys. The eﬀects of microstructure
on properties for the high Nb containing TiAl alloys is
similar to that for the conventional TiAl alloys, but the
abnormal relationship between the room temperature
ductility and fracture toughness found in the conven-
tional TiAl alloys is absent [10]. Full lamellar micro-
structure exhibits excellent strength with best fracture
toughness and ductility. This paper investigated sys-
tematically the eﬀects of Nb and Al contents on the
microstructure and strength. What is the diﬀerence
between the eﬀects of these two alloying additions? The
detailed strengthening mechanisms of Nb in the TiAl
alloys will be reported in a separated paper.
