abstract
In order to balance the strength and lubricating properties of Co matrix alloy at high temperature, Co –Cr
matrix high-temperature self-lubricating alloys with addition of molybdenum and silver were fabricated
by powder metallurgy technology. The high-temperature tribological behavior of alloys sliding against
Si
3N4 ceramic ball were investigated by using a ball-on-disc high-temperature tribometer at 10 N and
0.19 m/s starting from room temperature to 1000 °C. Cr and Mo element showed the solid solution
strengthening effect on the matrix. The specimen with 8 wt% Mo and 9 wt% Ag showed the best tribo-
logical properties over a wide temperature range. The favorable tribological properties were attributed to
the combined action of hardness and the solid lubricants including Ag, metal oxides, molybdates and
chromates at all the tested temperatures.

1. Introduction
In high temperature environment, the mechanical parts must
possess excellent mechanical properties and high temperature
oxidation resistance in order to support the effective operation of
whole system as structural components in internal combustion
engines, airplane engine and rocket motor [1–4]. However, some
mechanical parts could contact and slide between them at high
temperature. Especially, the rolling contact bearings or sliding
components require high resistance to wear and friction at ele-
vated temperature. In order to solve this question, many efforts
had been made by many researchers. According to previous
reports [5–8], the addition of solid lubricants was an important
measurement to improve tribological properties of materials at
high temperature. BaF
2, CaF2 and BaF2/CaF2 eutectic were the most
important high temperature solid lubricants, which could provide
lubricating effect for mechanical parts from 500 °C to 1000 °C
[9,10]. The ﬂuoride reacted with other elements and formed high-
temperature solid lubricants on the worn surfaces due to high-
temperature reaction and tribo-chemical reaction on the rubbing
surfaces [5,11,12]. These kinds of non-metal solid lubricants evi-
dently improve the friction and wear behavior at high tempera-
ture. However, the addition of non-metal materials obviously
destroyed the mechanical strength of matrix [5,13]. Therefore, it is
important to select reasonable solid lubricant to balance the
strength and lubricating property of alloy at elevated temperature.
Cobalt-base alloy is a type of high temperature alloy, which has
been widely used as bearing applications in nuclear industry and
jet engines due to its excellent high-temperature strength and
high-temperature wear resistance [14–16]. The excellent resis-
tance of Co matrix alloys to wear and oxidation bene ﬁts from the
solid solution hardening, oxidation and carbide precipitation-
hardening by adding tungsten, molybdenum, graphite element,
etc. over a wide temperature range [17]. Stellite
s series alloys are
one of the most important high-temperature Co matrix alloys [18].
Birol [19] investigated the tribological properties of Stellite 6 alloy
at 750 °C. The wear resistance of Stellite 6 alloy showed better
wear resistance as compared with those of Inconel 617 and
X32CrMoV33 alloys due to the stable oxide layer without spalling
on the worn surfaces at 750 °C. Radu et al. [20] studied the effect
of yttrium (Y) on high-temperature wear behavior and mechanical
properties of Stellite 21 alloy. The obtained results showed that the
yttrium element reinforced the mechanical properties of oxide
scale as well as its adherence to the worn surface due to the for-
mation of glaze layer containing Y
2O3 so that the alloy showed
excellent high-temperature wear resistance. Wang et al. [21] also
reported the similar conclusion. Nowadays, the wear resistance of
Co matrix alloys mainly depends on the formation of glaze layer of
oxides on the worn surfaces. The absence of oxygen or low tem-
perature would result in severe wear of alloys because the oxides
do not form on the worn surfaces. However, the investigation of Co

In this study, a series of Co –Cr matrix self-lubricating alloys

2. Experimental procedure
2.1. Specimens
The sizes of the raw cobalt, chromium, molybdenum and silver
powder are about 55 mm, 72 mm, 75 mm and 60 mm, respectively.
The Co –21 wt% Cr alloyed powder was sintered in a vacuum fur-
nace and milled by using a high-energy mill in our lab. The mean
size of Co –Cr alloyed powder is about 45 mm. The components of
sintered alloys were given in Table 1 (denoted as CC, CM and CA).
The samples were prepared by powder metallurgy technology (P/
M). The corresponding mixed powders were milled by using a
high energy ball mill for 4.5 h. And then the mixed powders were
enclosed in a graphite mold and sintered in a hot-press-sintering
furnace at 1 150 °C. A compaction pressure of 30 MPa was held for
30 min. Finally, the specimens were cooled down to room tem-
perature. The sintered density of specimens was tested by the
Archimedes' method by using a balance. The sintered density was
the mean value of ten measurements. The Vickers hardness was
measured by using an MH-5 Vickers hardness instrument, using a
normal load of 300 g and a dwell time of 10 s. The hardness
reported here was the mean value of ten measurements.
2.2. Friction and wear tests
The tribological tests were carried out by a high-temperature
tribotester with a ball-on-disc con ﬁguration. The tribotester can
conduct the tribological test from room temperature to 1000 °C.
The frictional counterpart was Si 3N4 ceramic ball with a diameter
of 6.0 mm, a Vicker's hardness of 15 GPa and surface roughness
(Ra) of 0.01 mm. The sample disks were made of sintered alloys
with dimensions of 18 mm /C2 18 mm /C2 5 mm. Before each test,
surfaces of specimens were polished to an average surface
roughness (Ra) of 0.20 mm with 1500 grit emery paper, and then
cleaned with acetone. The testing temperatures were 22 °C,
200 °C, 400 °C, 600 °C, 800 °C and 1000 °C. The normal load and
sliding speed were 10 N and 0.19 m/s, respectively. The testing
time was 20 min and the sliding radius was 5 mm. The curves of
friction coef ﬁcient of specimens were recorded by computer. The
reported friction coef ﬁcients were determined according to the
stable stage during the whole test. The speci ﬁc wear rate W was
calculated as below:
W ¼ V= S /C1 FðÞ ð 1Þ
where V is the wear volume loss (mm 3), S is the sliding distance
(m), F is the normal load (N), so the speci ﬁc wear rate ( W)i s
expressed as mm 3/N m. All tribological tests were carried out at
least three times to make sure the repeatability of experimental
data at the same testing condition, and the average results were
reported. The ambient temperature is 22 °C with a relative
humidity of 35%.
The microstructures and worn morphologies of specimens
were observed by scanning electron microscopy (SEM, JSM-
5600LV, JSM-IT300) with energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS,
Inca X-Max). The phases on the worn surface of specimens were
examined by X-ray Diffraction (XRD, Philips X'Pert-MRDX-ray
diffractometer, 40 kV, 30 mA, Cu Ka radiation). The hardness of the
lubricating ﬁlm was tested using a MTS NanoIndenter @ G200
system ﬁtted with a Berkovich indenter using the continuous
stiffness measurement (CSM) option (Indentation depth: 1500 nm,
Load: 8 mN).

4. Conclusions
Cr and Mo elements form solid solution phases ( α, ε) with Co
element at high temperature. The hardness of specimen CM
increases due to the solid solution strengthening effect of Mo on
the cobalt matrix. The hardness of Co –Cr–Mo–Ag alloy decreases,
and silver is present in its elementary form in the matrix.
The Mo and Ag greatly in ﬂuence tribological properties of
cobalt alloys over a wide temperature range. The speci ﬁcw e a r
rates of specimens decrease with the addition of Mo and Ag. Above
600 °C, the specimen CM shows lower friction coef ﬁcient than that
of specimen CC because of the combined lubricating effect of
metal oxides, molybdates and chromates. The specimen contain-
ing Mo and Ag shows the lowest friction coef ﬁcient as compared
with those of other specimens at testing temperatures. Generally
speaking, the specimen CA keeps the best tribological properties
over a wide temperature range with the friction coef ﬁcient of
0.16–0.40 and the speci ﬁc wear rate of 2.1 /C0 6.8 /C2 10
/C0 5 mm3/m N.
It is attributed to the combined action of hardness and the solid
lubricants including Ag, metal oxides, molybdates and chromates.
The specimens show different wear mechanisms. The specimen
CA shows the plowing characteristics. Meanwhile, the samples
