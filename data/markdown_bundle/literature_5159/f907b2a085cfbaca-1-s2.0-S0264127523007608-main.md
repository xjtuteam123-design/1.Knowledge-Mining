ABSTRACT 
Developing Mg alloys with high strength-ductility synergy presents a major challenge. In this work, a novel Mg- 
6Zn-0.2Ca alloy prepared via rotated hard-plate rolling (RHPR) exhibits a simultaneous high yield strength (YS) 
of ~268 MPa and ductility of ~22%. The RHPRed alloy features a fine-grained structure containing high-density 
low angle boundaries (LAGBs) and densely distributed nano-coherent precipitates, contributing to a remarkable 
improvement of YS (~82 MPa) in comparison to the conventional rolling counterpart, while homogenizing 
tensile deformation. In the RHPRed alloy, the presence of ellipse-shape texture and grain-boundary disclinations 
adjacent to LAGBs promotes the activity of pyramidal < a > and < c + a > slip that governs the high ductility. The 
current work demonstrates the essential role played by high-density LAGBs and nano-coherent precipitates to the 
high strength-ductility synergy, which may provide insights into microstructural control strategy towards su -
perior performance of Mg-Zn-Ca alloys. 
1. Introduction 
With increasing demands for improving fuel efficiency in aerospace 
and automobile, Mg alloys as the lightest structural metallic material 
have attracted considerable attention [1,2] . However, Mg alloys suffer 
from insufficient strength and poor ductility in comparison to iron, Ti 
and Al alloys [3,4] , which impedes their widespread industrial 
applications. 
Zn is considered as one of effective strengthening alloying elements. 
Given that the solid solubility of Zn in α -Mg matrix is high (~6 wt.% at 
325 
◦
C), high alloying with Zn is desirable. In particular, as one of 
widely used Mg-Zn based alloys, rolled Mg-6Zn-0.5Zr (ZK60) alloy 
exhibited a high yield strength of ~247 MPa [5] . However, ZK60 alloy 
usually develops a strong basal texture during thermomechanical pro -
cess [6] , leading to the limited ductility and formability. Recently, Mg- 
Zn-Ca alloys have been extensively studied [7] . The addition of Ca 
contributes to weaken basal texture, promote non-basal slip and 
microstructure refinement [8] . Alloying with Zn and Ca has been proved 
as an effective approach in improving ductility by reducing the dispar -
ities in CRSS values between basal slip and non-basal slip [9 – 11] . 
Therefore, Mg-Zn-Ca alloys with a low cost are among popular Mg alloys 
for their potentially excellent mechanical properties. 
Nowadays, advanced processing routes have been applied in pre -
paring Mg-Zn-Ca alloys to improve mechanical properties, contributing 
to refining grains, modifying second phase and obtaining non-basal 
texture. Recently, a short-processed route, i.e., sub-rapid solidification 
followed by hard-plate rolling (HPR), was applied on hard-to-deform 
Mg-6Zn-0.2Ca alloys [12] . Not only was the formability guaranteed, 
but also grain refinement, fragment and uniform distribution of Ca- 
containing particles brought about a balance of high ductility (~23%) 
and YS (~213 MPa). Li et al. [13] found that cross rolling, where the 
rolling direction was alternately changed by 90
◦
per pass, could effec -
tively tailor the texture of Mg-2Zn-1.2Ca alloys, resulting in the incli -
nation of basal poles greatly away from the normal direction (ND). 
Hradilov ´a et al. [14] fabricated a ductile Mg-4Zn-0.4Ca alloy (~24%) 
via four-pass equal channel angular pressing (ECAP), resulting from 
microstructure refinement and fragmented phase. Although Mg-Zn-Ca 
alloys reported in literature [8,15 – 19] showed an excellent room- 
temperature ductility over 20%, their YS was not satisfactory, usually 
as low as 100 – 220 MPa, due to the insufficient density of strengthening 
phase and limited dispersion strengthening effect.

Conventional strengthening methods are always companied with the 
loss of ductility. Indeed, fine-grained structure has the limited capacity 
of storing dislocation [22] . Weakening basal texture could improve 
ductility but at expense of strength [23] . Precipitation strengthening is 
closely related to the inhibited effect on dislocation motion, thus dete -
riorating ductility once stress concentration occurs [24] . To overcome 
the trade-off dilemma, introducing low-angle grain boundaries (LAGBs) 
has been proposed as an efficient strategy in achieving high strength- 
ductility synergy [25] . Compared with high-angle grain boundaries 
(HAGBs), LAGBs exhibit better ability to tune the balance of strength 
and ductility [26] . In particular, LAGBs could impede dislocation motion 
and hence induce remarkable sub-grain refinement hardening [27] . 
Meanwhile, LAGBs enable to emit new mobile dislocations [25] and 
collectively migrate with their constituent dislocations [28] as vehicles 
of plastic deformation. As a result, the stress concentration can be 
alleviated by dislocation emission and GB migrations, thus homoge -
nizing deformation and improving failure tolerance [29] . 
In this work, owing to the ideal microstructural combination of fine- 
grained structure, high-density LAGBs and densely distributed nano -
precipitates, high strength-ductility synergy has been achieved in Mg- 
Zn-Ca alloy by rotated hard-plate rolling (RHPR). The as-fabricated 
Mg-6Zn-0.2Ca alloy exhibits a remarkable enhancement of YS (~82 
MPa) and superior ductility (~22%) in comparison to its conventional 
rolling counterparts. This work provides a novel processing route and 
microstructural control strategy into designing Mg-Zn-Ca alloys with 
high strength-ductility synergy.

3.1. Mechanical properties of as-rolled ZX602 alloys 
Fig. 2 a shows the room-temperature engineering tensile stress – strain 
curves of CR and RHPR samples. Noticeably, the RHPR sample possesses 
a superior synergy of YS (~268 MPa) and ductility (~22%) in com -

RHPR is efficient in preparing high-alloying ZX series alloys having both 
high strength and ductility.

4. Discussion 
4.1. The formation of high-density LAGBs 
Deformation strain and rolling temperature are two key factors 
affecting the evolution of LAGBs and HAGBs [38] . CR could introduce a 
large residual shear stress, owing to the large friction between the 
sample surface and rollers, especially without lubrication in present 
work. The large residual shear stress could provide the adequate driving 
force for recrystallization, leading to the formation of HAGBs during 
rolling. In contrast, during RHPR, the rolling sample was inserted be -
tween two hard plates ( Fig. 1 ). Considering the limited deformation of 
hard plates, the shear stress could be transformed into the planar strain 
compression along ND during RHPR [12,39,40] . Thus, more residual 
dislocations are expected to be generated under the large planar strain 
compression that become the sources for the formation of LAGBs 
eventually [38] . Concerning the larger planar strain compression, more 
LAGBs can be introduced and well-established in the RHPR sample. 
Moreover, rolling temperature also has a great influence on micro -
structural evolution, depending on dynamic recrystallization (DRX) and 
dynamic recovery. The rate of dynamic recovery can be calculated ac -
cording to the following equation [38] :

Apart from rolling temperature, dynamic recovery also depends on 
rolling strain [38] . For Mg alloys, there exists a competition between 
dynamic recovery and DRX. Higher degree of dynamic recovery in -
dicates more stored energy being consumed and lower driving force for 
DRX [42] . In this work, the large thickness reduction of the initial two 
passes (~50% and ~40%) can accumulate considerable stored defor -
mation energy during rolling, which provides the adequate driving force 
for DRX. Afterwards, considering the relatively low thickness reduction 
of next two passes (~30% and ~15%), dynamic recovery plays the 
predominant role in the rearrangement of dislocations and the forma -
tion of cell structures [27] .

4.2. The reason for enhanced ductility of the RHPR sample 
4.2.1. The high-density LAGBs associated with grain-boundary 
disclinations 
Concerning inhomogeneous deformation during RHPR, various 
types of crystal defects enable to be introduced, resulting in the 
nonuniform distribution of LAGBs. According to the topological theory 
of defects [43] , topological defects can be divided into two basic kinds, i. 
e., dislocation and disclination. The disclination with rotation axis as 
direction and rotation angle as magnitude can be quantified from the 
Frank vector. Similar to the accommodation effect of intragranular GND, 
disclinations contribute to coordinating lattice curvature adjacent to 
GBs. 
Fig. 9 displays the IPF maps and corresponding disclination and GND 
density of RHPR sample. Note that the Region A without LAGBs features 
both low disclination and GND density ( Fig. 9 c and e). Considering that 
GBs could introduce dislocation sinks [44] , GBs are capable of absorbing 
dislocations in grains [45] , leading to the annihilation of high-density 
dislocations near GBs as marked by arrows ( Fig. 9 c and e). However, 
high-density disclinations develop along LAGBs and regions adjacent to 
LAGBs accumulate substantial GND ( Fig. 9 d and f), suggesting that 
LAGBs can serve as favorable nucleation sites to emit new dislocations, 
which contributes to enhancing the multiplication of dislocations. The 
origin of disclinations is closely related to the interaction between dis -
locations and LAGBs, indicating that the presence of local high stresses 
in the Region B containing abundant LAGBs, which is necessary for the 
activation of non-basal slips [46] . Thereafter, LAGBs could impose a 
positive effect as vehicles of accommodating plastic deformation by 
dislocation emissions and GB migrations [29] , playing a critical role on 
the enhancement of ductility.

and decompose. Considering that a large misorientation gradient and 
high internal stress are generated during RHPR ( Fig. 8 ), not only could 
the formation of strain field adjacent to high-energy regions enhance the 
driving force for dynamic precipitation [50] , but also stimulate the 
redistribution of phase via decomposition, dissolution and re- 
precipitation [51] . As a result, numerous spherical MgZn
2 
precipitates 
are generated in the RHPR sample. 
Interestingly, coarsened spherical phases dominate and needle-like 
phases disappear in the RHPR sample, indicating that the finer spher -
ical phases grow at expense of larger needle-like ones, which is inverse 
to the well-known Ostwald ripening [52] . As proposed by Voorhees et al. 
[53] , the inverse ripening process associates with the decrease of 
interfacial energy and total elastic energy. In the present work, the 
absence of needle-like phases contributes to a reduction of interfacial 
energy and elastic self-energy [54] . Additionally, atom diffusion also 
plays an essential role in driving the inverse ripening [54] . The presence 
of more LAGBs in the RHPR sample could provide extra short circuit 
diffusion paths for Zn atoms. Thus, favorable diffusion of Zn atoms along 
LAGBs allows inverse Ostwald ripening to occur during RHPR. 
The shape and distribution of MgZn
2 
precipitates impose an impor -
tant influence on the tensile behavior of Mg-Zn-Ca alloys. SEM images of 
tensile fractured CR and RHPR samples are presented in Fig. 11 . Note 
that the fracture surface of CR sample contains a large number of 
cleavage planes and cleavage steps with sharp edges ( Fig. 11 a), indi -
cating that cleavage fracture is the dominant fracture mechanism. As 
domonstrated in the inserted image, micro-cracks are generated nearby 
needle-like particles and propagate along RD, resulting from decohesion 
or fracture of needle-like ones. Once these micro-cracks extend into 
adjacent grains and connect with each other, earlier fracture will occur. 
By contrast, numerous uniformly distributed dimples exist on the frac -
ture surface of RHPR sample ( Fig. 11 b), suggesting ductile fracture 
characteristics. Considering that spherical MgZn
2 
particles in the RHPR 
sample are as fine as nano scale and coherent with α -Mg ( Fig. 6 ), they 
can rarely introduce large stress concentration and serve as cracking 
source. Moreover, high-density LAGBs introduced by RHPR could emit 
dislocations into neighboring grains to relieve stress and homogenize 
tensile deformation [26,29] , thereby improving cracking resistance and 
delaying premature fracture. It demontrates that the spheroidization of 
particles and the introduction of high-density LAGBs via RHPR could 
effectively suppress the crack initiation and propagation.

5. Conclusion 
In this work, the introduction of high-fraction LAGBs (~50%) and 
densely distributed nano-coherent MgZn
2 
precipitates (~8%) via 
rotated hard-plate rolling (RHPR) allows for the development of a Mg- 
6Zn-0.2Ca alloy possessing a high strength-ductility synergy, i.e., YS 
of ~268 MPa and an elongation of ~22%, whose YS is enhanced (~82 
MPa) in comparison to the conventional rolling (CR) counterpart. 
Concurrently, high-fraction LAGBs and nano-coherent precipitates could 
effectively homogenize deformation, thus improving the cracking 
resistance. Interestingly, an ellipse-shape texture and high-density 
grain-boundary disclinations adjacent to LAGBs are generated after 
RHPR, contributing to the increasing activity of pyramidal < a > and < c 
+ a > slip. As a result, not only could abundant LAGBs provide 
remarkable sub-grain refinement hardening (~62 MPa), but also emit 
disclinations as vehicles of accommodating plastic deformation. The 
current study suggests a promising microstructural control strategy into 
designing Mg-Zn-Ca alloys with high strength-ductility synergy.
