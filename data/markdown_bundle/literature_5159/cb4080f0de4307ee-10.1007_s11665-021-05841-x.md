Effects of Aging Treatment on The High-Temperature
Mechanical Behavior of Laser-Welded High-Strength
Mg-Gd-Y Alloy
Lyuyuan Wang , Jian Huang, and Kehong Wang
Submitted: 1 November 2020 / Revised: 27 March 2021 / Accepted: 25 April 2021
The effects of aging treatment on the high temperature mechanical behavior of laser-welded Mg-Gd-Y alloy
were systematically studied. The microstructure and high temperature tensile properties of as-welded and
aging-treated joints were analyzed. The microhardness of the weakest part of the heat affected zone was
improved by 41% due to the precipitation of large amount of b¢ and the ultimate tensile strengths at room
temperature and 300 /C176C was increased by 11 and 8% by aging treatment. The elevated temperature tensile
properties of two types of joints decreased slightly from room temperature to 250 /C176C, then plummeted at
300 /C176C. The failure mechanism of aging-treated joints was the originating and propagation of cracks at the
interior of Mg 24(Gd,Y)5 by the local stress concentration when the temperature was lower than 250 /C176C,
whereas the failure mechanism at 300 /C176C was the deformation of ultraﬁne grains and unsta-
ble Mg 24(Gd,Y)5.
Keywords heat treatment, high temperature properties, laser
welding, Mg-Gd-Y alloy
1. Introduction
Mg-Gd-Y alloys in peak-aged condition have great poten-
tials to replace the Al-Si alloys in the automotive and aerospace
industries like gearbox casings, oil sumps, helicopter turbines
due to their good mechanical properties at elevated tempera-
tures (Ref 1, 2, 3). As one of the most important join methods,
welding process has been widely used in various industries.
Compared with TIG (Tungsten Inert-gas) and MIG (Metal
Inert-gas) welding, laser welding should be applied preferen-
tially to join different magnesium alloys parts in T6 condition
to reduce the heat effect on the precipitates with the advantage
of high welding speed and power density (Ref 4). However, it
was investigated that laser welding cannot absolutely prevent
the heat affected zone (HAZ) from being softened due to the
dissolution of precipitates despite much narrower HAZ (Ref 5).
To improve the mechanical properties of welded joints of
magnesium alloys, some reports on post-weld heat treatment
have been carried out. Niknejad et al. studied on the effects of
solution treatment on the microstructure and mechanical
properties of resistance spot-welded AZ61 and AZ80 alloy, it
was found that the strength of welds can be increased
signiﬁcantly due to the elimination of b-Mg17Al12 particles
and cracks propagation path in heat affected and fusion zones
of AZ61 and AZ80 welds (Ref 6, 7). Liu et al. (Ref 8) pointed
out that mechanical properties of the TIG welded AZ31B can
be signiﬁcantly improved due to dramatical decrease in the
number of the b-Mg17Al12 precipitates along with the GBs by
post-weld heat treated at 350 /C176 C for 1 h. Singh et al. (Ref 9)
studied on the microstructure and mechanical properties of
friction stir welded AZ91 and AZ61 magnesium alloys, it was
reported that tensile strength and elongation of welded joints
can be improved by post-weld heat treatment with more
homogeneous microstructure from the recrystallization of grain
structure and grain growth. In addition, Dai et al. (Ref 10, 11)
also claimed that mechanical properties of laser-welded NZ30K
alloys can also be signiﬁcantly improved based on the
formation of lots of nano-sized precipitates in the magnesium
matrix using solution treatment as well as aging treatment.
The high-strength Mg-Gd-Y alloy in T6 condition can be
satisfactorily welded with no macroscopic defects. However,
the mechanical properties of laser-welded joints at room
temperature were weakened due to the dissolution of the b¢
phase in the HAZ (Ref 12, 13). Some literatures reported that
the b¢ phase can be precipitated from the magnesium matrix for
the Mg-Gd-Y alloys by solution treatment as well as aging
treatment, and the decomposition sequence of a(Mg) supersat-
urated solid solution (S.S.S.S., cph) in the alloys with
increasing aging time was as follows: b’’ (DO 19) ﬁ b’
(cbco) ﬁ b1 (fcc) ﬁ b (fcc) (Ref 14, 15, 16). Proper post-
weld heat treatment can promote the performances of laser-
welded Mg-Gd-Y alloys signiﬁcantly if large amount of the b¢
phase can be precipitated from the supersaturate solid solution
part of the HAZ during heat treatment. The aging treatment
should be considered preferentially because of its the special
advantage of high efﬁciency, energy saving, little effect on
joints. However, no research about the microstructure evolution
of laser-welded Mg-Gd-Y alloy during the aging treatment has
been reported, especially in high temperature performance
evaluation for the aged-treated joints. In this paper, post-weld
aging treatment was used to improve the high temperature

2. Experimental Procedure
The hot-forged and heat treated (solution treated at 430 /C176 C
for 4 h and then aged at 225 /C176 C for 18 h) Mg-Gd-Y alloy sheets
with dimensions of 100 mm 9 50 mm 9 4 mm were chosen in
this experiment, which chemical composition is shown in
Table 1. Figure 1 shows that the microstructure of the base
metal (BM) consists of some equiaxed grains with a size of
about 50 lm (as shown in Fig .1 (a)), and high-density bamboo-
leaf-shaped b¢ phase can be observed from the TEM bright ﬁeld
(BF) image and selected area electron diffraction (SAED)
pattern taken along [0001] a zone axis in the Fig .1 (b) and (c) .
Before laser welding, the Mg-Gd-Y alloy sheets were
mechanically ground by abrasive paper to remove the surface
oxides and cleaned with acetone to remove grease and residue.
The butt-welding experiments of Mg-Gd-Y alloy were carried
out using a ﬁber laser system (IPG YLS-10000, IPG) with a
maximum output power of 10 kW. The experimental sheets
were ﬁxed tightly into a ﬁxture to ensure a zero gap. The
diameter of the laser beam focus is 0.72 mm, and the position
of the focal point was adjusted to 0 mm from the surface of
workpiece. The welding speed was set as 3.0 m/min and laser
power was set as 2.2 kW. The pure argon (99.99%) was used in
this experiment to protect the molten pool of the top and bottom
welds from oxidation with the constant ﬂow rate of 12 L/min
and 10 L/min, respectively. Based on preliminary tests results,
the post-weld aging treatment was performed at 225 /C176 C for 12 h
followed by cooling in air.
The laser-welded Mg-Gd-Y alloy joints were etched by a
solution of 4 vol.% nital for microstructure and morphology
observation by an optical (AxioCam MRc5, Carl Zeiss) and
transmission electron microscope (TEM) (JEOL-2100, JEOL).
The microhardness along the cross section of the aging treated
and as-welded joints were measured by a Vickers hardness
tester (Zwick Zh l, Zwick) with a load of 200 g and holding
time of 15 s. The distance between the microhardness measured
points is 200 lm. The high temperature tensile properties test
was carried out under a high temperature tensile test machine
(Zwick Z50, Zwick) with the condition of 1 mm/min in cross-
head speed at different temperature (room temperature, 150,
200, 250, 300 /C176 C). The tensile results were got based on three
samples at each temperature. The top and backsides of the
welded joints were minimally ground to achieve a smooth
surface before tensile tests. The tensile fracture surface of the
joints was also observed by a ﬁeld-emission scanning electron
microscope (NOV A NanoSEM230, FEI and JSM-7800F,JEOL
).
3. Results and Discussion
3.1 Microstructure
Section optical morphologies of the typical laser-welded
Mg-Gd-Y alloy in as-welded and aging-treated condition are
shown in Fig. 2. The macrostructure of two types of joints (as
shown in Fig. 2(a) and (d)) predicts that there is a clear dividing
line between the fusion zone (FZ) and HAZ. It is also observed
that the microstructure of the HAZ in as-aged condition is
different from that of the as-welded treated samples. However,
the FZ from both types of joints presents dark morphology. To
better understand the microstructural characteristics, the en-
larged optical microphotographs of the HAZ (Fig. 2(c)) and FZ
(Fig. 2f) are also shown in Fig. 2. Compared with the optical
micrographs of the HAZ between as-welded and aged treated
joints, it can be noted that the grain boundaries become clearly
identiﬁed, and the grain size can keep steady during aging heat
treatment (Fig. 2b and e). Figure 2(c) and (f) shows that the
microstructure characteristics of the FZ from two types of
welded joints consist of some ultraﬁne grains surrounding by
high density of divorced eutectic Mg 24(Gd,Y)5 phase (Ref 17).
In other words, there are not obvious changes in the FZ during
post-weld aging treatment. Because the temperature of aging
treatment (225 /C176 C) is much lower than the solution temperature
of the divorced eutectic phase for the Mg-Gd-Y alloy, the
morphology and distribution of the Mg 24(Gd,Y)5 phase remain
relatively unchanged during the aging process.
To identify the microstructure evolution of the HAZ during
the aging treatment, the TEM micrographs from the HAZ close
to the FZ of as-welded and aging-treated Mg-Gd-Y alloy joints
should be taken to ﬁnd the changes relating to the nano-sized
precipitates. Figure 3 shows typical TEM microstructure of the
HAZ close to the FZ from two types of welded joints along
[0001]a zone axis. From the BF image in the as-welded HAZ
(as shown Fig. 2(a)), only magnesium matrix can be observed.
Based on the corresponding SAED pattern (as shown
Fig. 2(b)), it can be concluded that the b¢ precipitates have
been dissolved completely into the magnesium matrix. Accord-
ing to traditional thermal cycle theory of laser welding, the
staying time at the high temperature (the solvus temperature of
b¢) is so long that the solute elements (Gd and Y) can diffuse
into the matrix completely, while too fast cooling rate can result
in no precipitates performing from the magnesium matrix
during subsequent cooling stage, compared to the aging
treatment. Figure 2(c) and (d) depicts the TEM pictures and
SAED pattern of the HAZ after aging treated at 225 /C176 C for 12
h. As shown in Fig. 2(c), some convex lens-like precipitates
(indicated by the white arrow) stagger at a certain angle. Based
on the SAED pattern shown in Fig. 2(d), the orientation
relationship between the precipitate and matrix identiﬁed is
[001] b’ // [0001] a, (100) b’// (2110 ) a, and the structure of the
precipitate is the cbco ( a =2 9 aa-Mg /C250.64 nm, b =8 9 d(10
10 ) a-Mg /C252.22 nm, c = c a-Mg /C250.52 nm) (Ref 14). It can be
predicted that large amount of b’ phases can be precipitated
from the magnesium matrix in the as-welded HAZ during the
aging treatment. Compared with the morphology and distribu-
tion of the b’ in the base metal (BM), the b’ precipitates in the
aging treated HAZ distributed unevenly presumably due to the
segregation of Gd, Y atoms in the as-welded HAZ before the
aging treatment process. The most important factor of the
segregation is that solute atoms (Gd and Y) have not enough

3.2 Mechanical Properties
Figure 4 shows the microhardness proﬁles measured along
the mid-thickness line and the high temperature tensile
properties contrast results of the 4-mm thick laser-welded
joints in the as-welded and aging treated condition. The
microhardness increases slowly with the distance away from
the fusion boundary in the HAZ for the as-welded joint. It can
also be note that the curve of the HAZ in the as-welded joint
appears a lowest hardness value (78 HV), which locate at the
boundary of the FZ. As the precipitation strengthen alloy, the
reduction of hardness of the HAZ of laser-welded Mg-Gd-Y
alloy in as-welded condition, shown in Fig. 4(a), is mainly
attributed to the dissolution of b¢ precipitates. Moreover, the
decreasing in the amount of the dissolved b¢ precipitates away
from fusion boundary in the HAZ also indicates the changing
laws of the microhardness. On the other hand, the microhard-
ness of the weakest part of the HAZ in the aging-treated joints
increases sharply to 110 HV and keeps a level close to the
hardness of the BM. This is beneﬁt from the precipitation of
high-density b’ in the HAZ, which is shown in Fig .3 (c).
In the case of microhardness distribution of the FZ from two
types of joints, the microhardness curve of aging-treated joints
shows a tendency toward slightly higher than that of as-welded
condition. This result is also in accord with optical microstruc-
ture of the FZ from two types of joints (shown in Fig. 2(c) and
(f)). As mentioned above, the FZ of two types of joints exhibits
some typical ﬁne equiaxed grains containing numerous brittle
divorced eutectic Mg 24(Gd,Y)5 phase. The ultraﬁne grains and
increasing amount of brittle Mg 24(Gd,Y)5 phase in the FZ are in
favor of the increase of microhardness. Compared with the
hardness between the HAZ and FZ from the aging-treated joint,
it can be predicted that this strengthen effects of b¢ precipitates
are much higher than that of grain boundary and secondary
phase.

4. Conclusion
The effects of aging heat treatment on microstructure and
high temperature properties of laser-welded Mg-Gd-Y alloy
were investigated. Based on the above investigation, the
conclusions from this paper can be summarized as follows:
1. The microstructure of the FZ in the aging-treated condi-
tion is very similar to the as-welded condition, which
consists of some ultraﬁne grains surrounding by high
density of divorced eutectic Mg 24(Gd,Y)5 phase. The
grain boundaries become clearly identiﬁed and the grain
size can keep steady in the HAZ during the aging heat
treatment.
2. The microhardness of the weakest part of the HAZ was
improved by 41% due to the precipitation of large
amount of b¢ phase by the aging treatment. The micro-
hardness of the FZ in aging-treated condition shows a
tendency toward slightly higher than that of as-welded
condition.
3. The ultimate tensile strengths of welded joints at room
temperature and 300 /C176 C was increased by 11% and 8%
after aging treatment, respectively. The elevated tempera-
ture tensile properties of as-welded and aging-treated
joints decreased slightly from room temperature to 250
/C176 C. With the temperature reaching 300 /C176 C, the strengths
of the two types of joints plummeted.
4. The cracks can originate easily at the interior of
Mg24(Gd,Y)5 phase in the FZ of aging-treated joints by
the local stress concentration, and the specimens exhib-
ited transgranular fracture during the tensile test at 250
/C176 C. The deformation of the ultraﬁne grains and unsta-
ble Mg 24(Gd,Y)5 phase in the FZ of aging-treated joints
at 300 /C176 C deteriorated the high temperature tensile prop-
erties.
