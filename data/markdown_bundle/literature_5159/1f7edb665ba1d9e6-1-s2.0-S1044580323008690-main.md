ABSTRACT 
The mechanical properties and deformation behaviors of a bimodal grain structured Inconel 625 alloy were 
systematically investigated over a wide temperature range from 25 
◦
C to 900 
◦
C. As compared with conventional 
Inconel 625 alloys, the heterogeneous Inconel 625 alloy exhibits superior strength-ductility synergy until 600 
◦
C. 
The yield strength, ultimate tensile strength and elongation at room temperature reach 617 MPa, 1057 MPa and 
56.9%, respectively. The grain boundary strengthening and twin boundary strengthening introduced by 
numerous fine grains result in high yield strength. Electron backscattered scattering diffraction and transmission 
electron microscopy results reveal different densities of geometrically necessary dislocations between coarse 
grains and fine grains. The strain gradients induced by incompatible deformation of bimodal grains produce large 
back stress work hardening, which not only significantly increases the ultimate tensile strength but also prevents 
early necking during tensile testing, improving tensile ductility. When temperature exceeds 600 
◦
C, stress 
relaxation induced by dynamic recrystallization significantly decreases the strength of alloys but increases its 
ductility. This study demonstrates the strength and ductility of Inconel 625 alloy can be simultaneously improved 
by introducing heterogenous lamella structure and the great mechanical properties can retain until 600 
◦
C. 
1. Introduction 
With the development of advanced industrial technologies, higher 
demands are placed on the comprehensive mechanical properties of 
structural materials, especially high strength-ductility synergy, which 
have attracted considerable attentions in the community of structural 
materials [ 1 – 4 ]. To date, considerable efforts have been devoted to 
design and prepare structural materials combining high strength and 
good ductility [ 5 , 6 ]. It is well known that the mechanical properties of 
metals and alloys depend on the chemical compositions and micro -
structures, and the microstructure can be adjusted by cold or thermal 
deformation processes and subsequent heat treatments in order to 
obtain desired specific properties without changing the chemical com -
positions [ 7 – 9 ]. For conventional metals and alloys, the strength can be 
improved by solid solution strengthening [ 10 ], fine grain reinforcement 
[ 11 ], precipitation hardening [ 12 ], etc., which introduce numerous 
solid solution atoms, grain boundaries and precipitates, respectively, 
impeding dislocation movement [ 13 ]. However, the increase of strength 
is generally accompanied with a drastic loss of ductility due to the strain 
concentration caused by the blocking of dislocation slips [ 14 ]. There -
fore, it is difficult to simultaneously improve the strength and ductility 
of structural materials and novel strengthening strategies are required to 
achieve more comprehensive mechanical properties [ 15 , 16 ]. 
The strength-toughness behaviors of metals and alloys heavily 
depend on the average grain size and grain size distribution. In general, 
the strength can be improved by decreasing grain size at least in the size 
range of tens of nanometers to tens of micrometers, which follows Hall- 
Petch relationship [ 17 ]. However, the ductility would severely lose 
when grain size decreases below one micrometer [ 18 , 19 ]. In contrast, 
although alloys with coarse grain structure have improved ductility, the 
strength is relatively low due to longer dislocation slip distance [ 17 – 20 ]. 
In recent years, based on the effects of grain size on the strength and 
ductility of metallic materials, it has been proposed that the combination 
of high strength and ductility can be achieved by a bimodal grain

Wang et al. prepared a pure copper with bimodal grain structure by 
rolling at liquid nitrogen temperature and low-temperature annealing, 
whose yield strength is six times higher than that of coarse-grained pure 
copper, and ductility is essentially the same [ 21 ]. The high strength 
comes from nanocrystalline and ultra-fine grains, while the uneven 
microstructure leads to the stable tensile deformation and good ductility 
[ 21 ]. Li et al. prepared a bimodal structured Co-Cr-based superalloy by 
moderate cold rolling and high temperature annealing, which exhibits 
higher yield and tensile strengths without any loss in elongation 
compared with the counterpart with uniform large grain structure [ 22 ]. 
Bimodal grain structures have been obtained in many metallic materials, 
such as pure titanium, aluminum alloys and magnesium alloys by dy -
namic recrystallization or static recrystallization [ 23 – 26 ]. In addition, 
besides the bimodal grain structure, some investigations have shown 
that lamellar structure can alleviate the plastic loss of ultra-fine grain 
and nanocrystalline metallic materials, and significantly improve the 
work hardening ability, thus achieving the combination of high strength 
and good ductility [ 27 , 28 ]. Sun et al. prepared a layered low carbon 
steel plate with bimodal grain size distribution by two-step warm rolling 
and annealing process [ 29 ]. Compared with counterpart with large grain 
size sample, the yield strength and tensile strength of the bimodal 
layered low carbon steel increased by 87.4% and 35%, respectively, 
with very small plastic loss [ 29 ]. This demonstrates that the mechanical 
properties of metallic materials can benefit from bimodal grain size and 
layered structure. 
Inconel 625 alloy is a typical nickel-based superalloy, which has been 
widely used in aerospace, petrochemical and energy industries due to its 
excellent tensile strength and fatigue resistance [ 30 – 33 ]. The great 
mechanical properties are mainly due to the solid solution strengthening 
mechanism induced by the addition of molybdenum and niobium and 
precipitation strengthening induced by γ’ phase (Ni3(Al,Ti)), etc.

4.2. Evolution of Back Stress During Tensile Test 
Although traditional strengthening theories can well predict the 
yield strength, it does not consider the incompatible deformation of 
bimodal grains and can not interpret the good ductility and high ulti -
mate tensile strength of the heterogeneous structured Inconel 625 alloy 
with small average grain size. Many previous investigations on hetero -
structured materials have pointed out that the back stress produced in 
the incompatible deformation processes of bimodal grains plays an 
important role for the great mechanical properties of heterostructured 
materials [ 1 , 69 – 72 ]. According to the Hall Patch relationship, the yield 
strength of FG is greater than that of CG [ 17 ]. Therefore, in the initial 
stage of plastic deformation, the CG layer first yields and undergoes 
plastic deformation, while the FG layer is still in the elastic deformation 
stage [ 1 ]. Due to the deformation incompatibility between the CG and 
FG regions, GNDs accumulation preferentially occurs at the CG/FG in -
terfaces, as show in Fig. 9 , generating long-range back stress at the CG 
and forward stress at the FG regions [ 73 ]. 
Since FGs are harder than CGs, the CG/FG interfaces can effectively 
block the GNDs. The FGs will retain elastic until the back stress is enough 
high in the CGs during the initial stage of plastic deformation. This re -
sults in the increase of global yield stress. During the subsequent 
deformation, the soft grains (CGs) will sustain higher plastic strain than 
the hard grains (FGs) under effects of back stress due to the longer slip 
distances and smaller hardness, which makes the CGs stronger. Since 
plastic strain needs to retain identical at the CG/FG interfaces to 
maintain continuity, which results in the strain gradient near CG/FG 
interfaces to accommodate the strain partition. High density GNDs will 
accumulate near CG/FG interfaces to accommodate strain gradient, as

5. Conclusions 
In this study, we systematically studied the mechanical properties 
and deformation behaviors of a layered bimodal grain structure Inconel 
625 alloy from RT to 900 
◦
C. The main conclusions can be summarized 
as follows: 
(1) As compared with conventional Inconel 625 alloys, the layered 
bimodal grain-structure Inconel 625 alloy exhibits superior 
strength and ductility synergy in the temperature range from RT 
to 600 
◦
C. The YS and UTS can reach 617 MPa and 1 GPa at RT 
with ductility of ~57%. The YS and UTS only decreased by 
157.11 MPa and 191.13 MPa, respectively, when the temperature 
increased from RT to 600 
◦
C. 
(2) When the tensile temperature is higher than 600 
◦
C, the strength 
of heterogeneous structure Inconel 625 alloy decreases sharply, 
and the elongation reaches the maximum value of ~101% at 
900 
◦
C. The decrease in strength and the improvement in ductility 
at 800 
◦
C and 900 
◦
C are attributed to stress relaxation induced by 
dynamic recrystallization. 
(3) The characterization results of EBSD and TEM at different strains 
of RT tensile test show that higher density of GND in FGs and the 
accumulation of GNDs at the heterogeneous interfaces (CG/FG 
interface). The HDI stress strengthening mechanism specific to 
the deformation of heterogeneous structures was verified through 
GND density calculations at different strains and LUR cyclic 
tensile test analysis. 
(4) The synergy of excellent strength and high ductility in the layered 
bimodal grain structure Inconel 625 alloy at RT to 600 
◦
C can be 
attributed to the HDI stress strengthening mechanism, which 
provides extra strain hardening and helps with retaining 
ductility.
