abstract
In this paper, the effects of Au on the microstructure and mechanical properties of NiAl intermetallic
compound were investigated. The results showed that the addition of Au in the NiAl alloy reﬁned the
NiAl grains obviously, but more addition of Au resulted in the formation of
a-AlAu2 phase in the alloy.
The composition tests revealed that the Au had low solubility in NiAl about 0.3 at%. Au alloying had
a moderate solid solution strengthening effect on NiAl at low temperature and a strong solid solution
strengthening effect at high temperature. In addition, Au alloying improved the room temperature
compressive ductility, elevated temperature strength and Vickers hardness of the NiAl alloy obviously.
The room temperature fracture surface of Au doped NiAl alloys exhibited transgranular cleavage
characteristics.

1. Introduction
B2 structure intermetallic compound NiAl alloys have shown
excellent potential properties as a new class of structural material
for high-temperature applications [1,2], because of their high
melting point, low density, good thermal conductivity and excellent
oxidation resistance. However, the poor fracture toughness and
ductility at room temperature (RT) and elevated temperature
strength limite the application of NiAl alloys. Considerable studies
have been done to improve NiAl’s mechanical properties through
alloying. Many researches [2–8] concentrate on the effect of
refractory elements, mainly including Hf, Ti, Nb, Ta, Mo and Cr etc in
polycrystal NiAl, which have been proved to be effective to improve
its elevated temperature creep strength by forming Heusler phase
(Ni
2AlX), Laves phase (NiAlX) and solid solution strengthening.
Considering the boron-doped Ni 3Al has a good plastic at RT [9,10],
the interstitial elements such as B, C, N, have also been added to NiAl
alloy to investigate their effect [1 1,12]. Unfortunately, B addition just
changes the fracture model from intergranular to transgranular, but
has no help to the RT plastic of NiAl alloy. In addition, some active
elements such as Y, Dy, Ho have been found to be able to reﬁne the
grain size and improve the oxidation resistance [13–16]. Although
alloying and processing technology have improve the RT toughness
or elevated temperature creep strength, NiAl alloys need to inves-
tigate further by alloying in order to attain a more reasonable
balance of RT toughness and elevated temperature strength.
The basic of chemical bonding for NiAl is the strong Ni-Al
interaction caused by the hybridization of Ni d, s- and Al p- orbits.
Previous study [17] shows that alloying NiAl by Fe, Co and Mo
signiﬁcantly inﬂuence the density-of-states in valence band of
intermetallic and the mechanical properties of NiAl alloys have
been improved accordingly. It is well known that signiﬁcant charge
redistribution occurs upon alloying [18] for noble metals with
nominally full d-bands, such as Au. On the other hand, Au has a very
high electric negativity for 2.54, while Ni for 1.91, Al for 1.61 [19].
Hence, Au addition should affect the charge redistribution of NiAl
intermetallic compound and then inﬂuence its microstructure and
mechanical properties. Moreover, the research on Au alloying
behavior in NiAl is few, so in the present work the investigation on
the effect of Au on the microstructure and mechanical properties of
NiAl intermetallic compound have been carried out.

2. Experimental
NiAl-Au alloys (listed on the Table 1 ) used for this investiga-
tion were arc-melted under an argon atmosphere from starting
materials of 99.9% Ni, 99.9% Al and 99.9% Au (quality percent),
using a non-consumable tungsten electrode. Each alloy button
was turned over and remelted more than ﬁve times to get
a homogeneous specimen. Since weight losses were generally less
than 0.5%, the alloy compositions were considered to be equal to
their nominal compositions. The alloy buttons were homogenized
at 1523 K for 24 h in evacuated silica capsules followed by furnace
cooling to RT. Their microstructure and phase constitutions were
characterized by optical microscopy (OM), scanning electron
microscopy (SEM) with energy dispersive spectrometer (EDS) and
X-ray Diffraction (XRD) and the compositions of constitute phases
were detected by EPMA-1610 electronic probe microanalysis
(EPMA). The foils for transmission electron microscope (TEM)
observation were prepared by the conventional twin jet polishing
technique using an electrolyte of 10% perchloric acid in methnol
at 253 K after mechanical polishing to 50
mm and cutting into disc
with a diameter of 3.0 mm. The TEM observation was performed
by a JEM-2010 transmission electron microscope operated at
200 kV.
Compressive specimens with the size of 4 /C2 4 /C2 6m m
3 were cut
from the homogenized buttons by electro-discharge machining
(EDM) and all major surfaces were mechanically ground with
600-grit SiC abrasive before compression test. The compression test
was conducted in air with a gleeble-1500 test machine at a constant
crosshead velocity corresponding to the initial strain rate of
1 /C2 10/C0 3 s/C0 1 from RT to 1273 K. The autographically recorded load-
time curves were converted to true stress-strain curves via the
assumption of the constant volume. Vickers hardness tests were
carries out on MICROMET micro-hardness tester using a load of
150–250 g and a dwell time of 15 s.
3. Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructure characterizations
The typical optical micrographs of the NiAl-Au alloys after
homogenization treatment are shown in Fig. 1. The average linear
intercept grain sizes of NiAl in the NiAl-0.1Au, NiAl-0.5Au and NiAl-
1Au are 150 um, 100 um, 70 um, respectively. The high magniﬁca-
tion SEM back-scattered images are given in Fig. 2 . It is clear that
the alloy contain two kinds of phase that are black NiAl matrix and
white second precipitates, which indicated that the Au addition
lead to the formation of the second phase in the NiAl-0.5Au and
NiAl-1Au alloys. For NiAl-0.5Au alloy, only a small amount of white
phase is found at the NiAl grain boundary partially. For NiAl-1Au
alloy, the second phase mainly distributes at the NiAl grain
boundary by forming a semi-continuous network, and there is
small particles exist inside the NiAl grain.
The compositions of the NiAl matrix and white second phase in
NiAl-0.5Au and NiAl-1Au tested by EPMA are listed in Table 2.W e
can see that Au has the solubility of about 0.3 at%, which has been
proved indirectly from the Au-rich phase precipitation in
NiAl-0.5Au alloy. The quantitative composition of the white phase
indicates that it is Au-rich phase with a high level of Al. The ratio of
Au and Al approximates to be 2:1. The structural characterization of
monophase NiAl and NiAl-1Au is characterized by using XRD
patterns and the results are shown in Fig. 3(a). It is clear that there
are more peaks in NiAl-1Au alloy compared with the NiAl.
Preliminary conﬁrmation reveals that these are the peaks of
a-AlAu2 phase. TEM observations further conﬁrm the conclusion
from the XRD test, as shown in Fig. 3 (b). The a-AlAu2 particles are

Fig. 5 summarizes the compressive yield strength of Au doped

and 673 K, the yield strengths of Au doped NiAl alloys increase with
the increase of Au content, but at 1073 K and 1273 K, the NiAl-0.5Au

The observations on the fracture surface of all alloys reveal that
the NiAl-0.5Au and NiAl-1Au almost have the similar characteris-
tics, so only the SEM images of NiAl-0.1Au and NiAl-1Au are shown
in Fig. 6 . NiAl-0.1Au alloy exhibits mainly transgranular cleavage
together with some smooth grain-boundary facets and some river
patterns. NiAl-1Au shows almost complete transgranular cleavage.
It’s well known that the intergranular fracture is the main failure
characteristics of the monophase NiAl compound at low tempera-
ture. While the Au addition changes the fracture mode from
intergranular to transgranular cleavage, and moreover the
compressive ductility of the NiAl-Au alloys improve obviously,
especially the NiAl-0.5Au alloy.

3.3. Microhardness
The results of Vickers hardness measurement in grain interiors
of all alloys are illustrated in Fig. 7. With increasing of Au content,
the hardness of NiAl increases obviously, which can be due to the
solid solution hardening of Au in NiAl. It’s speciﬁc that Au has
a strong solid solution hardening to NiAl. For example, 0.1% Au
addition makes the hardness of NiAl increased from 257 to 315 kgf/
mm2. NiAl-0.5Au and NiAl-1Au alloys have the similar hardness
although there was small amount of precipitation inside the grain
of NiAl-1Au alloy.
4. Conclusions
Based on the investigations on the microstructure and
mechanical properties of Au-contained NiAl alloys, the results are
summarized as follows:
(1) The addition of Au in the NiAl alloy reﬁnes the NiAl grains
obviously. More addition of Au results in the formation of
a-AlAu2 phase in the alloy.
(2) Au addition leads to a moderate solid solution strengthening at
low temperature and a strong solid solution strengthening at
high temperature to NiAl.
(3) Au alloying can improve the room temperature compressive
ductility and the compressive fracture surface exhibits trans-
granular cleavage characteristics.
