The Effect of Nb addition on microstructure, oxidation behavior and 
strength of some γ-TiAl alloys

Abstract 
The new generation γ-TiAl alloys contain higher amount Nb because of its effect on oxidation, strength and creep 
properties. This paper presents the effect of Nb addition in microstructure, oxidation behaviour and compression strength of tw o 
γ-TiAl alloys Ti-45Al-2Cr-7.5Nb-0.3B, Ti- 45Al-2Cr-10Nb-0.3B and comparison with th e properties of a standard gamma alloy 
Ti-48Al-2Nb-2Cr. The microstructural evolution was studied in as-cast, solutionized and aged conditions. The oxidation studies 
were carried out at 900 °C in air for various time intervals up to 200 hr s and the type of oxide layer formed have been 
characterized by X-ray diffraction (XRD). Th e composition and phase analysis in all th e three alloys were studied out electron 
probe micro analysis (EPMA). Finally th e strength and oxidation behaviour were correlated with both the composition and 
microstructure.

1. Introduction 
γ-TiAl alloys are superior to some Ni-base super alloys in terms of specific strength up to a temperature of about 
850 to 900°C [1] with good oxidation resistance and high elastic modulus. The poor ductility and sensitivity to 
processing conditions are the limiting factors of these alloys for aero-engine applications. These issues can be sorted 
out by producing fine grained microstructure with sufficient ductility and without preferred orientation. These alloys 
are normally produced by casting and certain new generations alloys can also be worked to some extent thermo-
mechanically.

The latest strategies focus on reducing the segregation and preferred orientation by reducing the Al content, 
adding β stabilizing elements such as Nb, Mo, V, Cr, Ta which results in solidification through β phase and also 
alloying with B which refines the grains by forming borides [2]. It is reported that the β solidifying alloys have finer 
grains [3] as against the coarse grained structure obtained normally in peritectically solidifying alloys of the earlier 
generations.The Nb containing γ-TiAl alloys developed is being used by GE and others successfully as turbine 
engine blades in aero engine [4]. It is known that strength and ductility of γ-TiAl decreases when exposed to air. 
Generally a harmful effect on the ductility of TiAl alloys has been shown to result from exposures as short as 2 hour 
at 700°C in air [5]. In the case of GE alloy, Ti-48Al-2Cr-2Nb, embrittlement was observed only below 200°C [6]. 
In the present work, two new TiAl alloys aimed for aero-engine applications have been studied. 
Compression strength of these alloys have been compared with the standard alloys at room temperature (RT) as well 
as at high temperature (800°C).The oxidation resistance of these alloys were also examined at 900°C in air. 
 
2. Experimental details 
The two new TiAl alloys with the nominal compositions (alloy1: Ti-45Al-7.5Nb-2Cr-0.3B, alloy2: Ti-
45Al-10Nb-2Cr-0.3B (at %) along with standard alloy Ti-48Al-2Nb-2Cr was melted in non-consumable vacuum arc 
melting furnace. The cast pancakes were HIPPED in ar gon atmosphere with the pressure of 120MPa at 1230 °C for 
4hrs and then cooled slowly. The melting was carried out six times to ensure chemical homogeneity. The pancakes 
of the alloys were cut into 2cm x 2cm cubes on high sp eed saw. The samples were coated with deltaglaze FB412 to 
prevent from oxidation and coated samples were solution treated (ST) at 1230 °C for 1hr and then air cooled. The 
ST samples were aged at 850 °C for 4 hrs and furnace cooled. The heat treated samples were cut and polished on 
various grit sized emery papers and finally polished on cloth, using 0.1 µm colloidal alumina. All the three alloys 
were characterized in as-cast, solution treated and aged conditions using scanning electron microscope (FEI make 
Quanta 400) and back scattered elect ron (BSE) images were recorded at various magnification. The as cast 
microstructures of the alloys are shown in Fig.1, whereas the fully heat treated microstructures are shown in Fig.2. 
The EPMA scans of the phases were carried out in heat treated conditions in order to identify the phases on a 
CAMECA make machine model No: SX-100 and are shown in Fig.4. The samples of 3mm thick and 10mm 
diameter were cut and heat treated at 900 °C for 5, 10, 25, 35, 50, 100, 150 and 200 hrs in air and furnace cooled in 
AURORA make heat treatment furnace. The change in weig ht of each sample was weighed by electronic balance 
after exposure. Finally a graph was plotted between ox idation time and weight gained by each alloy. The 
compression testing of all the three alloys in as-cast and solution treated and aged (STA) conditions have been 
carried out in Walter bai ag testing machine with sample dimensions of 5 mm diameter and 10mm height 
(cylindrical samples). Compression test was carried out with a crosshead spee d of 1mm/min with a 100kN load cell 
at room temperature (25°C) and at 800 °C.

3.3. Compression study: 
 
The compression test at room temperature and 800 °C at a constant strain rate was conducted to determine 
the strength of the different γ-TiAl alloys in this studys. The strength values obtained from the compression testing 
have been correlated with the microstructure and chemical composition. Yield strength of Ti-48Al-2Nb-2Cr 
(standard alloy) alloy is higher than that of ot her alloys at room temperature as well as 800 °C in both solution 
treated (ST) and solution treated and ag ed (STA) conditions (Table 2). The allo ys alloy 1 and alloy 2 portray better 
UTS (ultimate tensile strength) values at room temperature in ST condition. However, UTS values of all the alloys 
are same at 800 °C in solution treated condition. In STA co ndition the standard alloy shows higher UTS values than 
the other two alloys.

4. Discussion 
 In fully heat treated condition after HIPing, the two allo ys, alloy1 and 2 show a bi-modal type of microstructure 
consisting β phase at the boundaries and a fine lamella r structure inside the grains consisting of α
2 and γ. The 
standard alloy shows a lamellar microstructure with a larger grain size and the presence of β phase is also seen in 
this alloy in fully heat treated condition. 
EPMA line scan profile confirms that the alloy1, allo y2 and standard alloy contain three phases. The dark 
region is aluminium rich (TiAl), the bright region is Cr rich ( β) and the grey region (Ti3Al) is Ti and Al rich. Nearly 
the equal distribution of Nb in all the phases shows that the solid solubility of Nb is similar in all the phases. 
The oxidation in air at 900°C, shows the presence of TiN, Al 2O3 and TiO2. The presence of TiN layer was 
postulated to interrupt the establishment of continuous Al2O3 scale as oxidation proceeds; the TiN may subsequently 
oxidize to form TiO 2. This process normally results in the formation of intermixed Al 2O3/TiO2 scale rather than a 
continuous Al2O3 scale [7] and sometimes with remnants of TiN. The oxidation resistance of alloy2 with highest Nb 
content of 10 at. %.was found superior to the other alloys in this study. Nb has a valency greater than four, Nb5+. 
By substituting for the Ti4+ions in the TiO
2 structure, Nb reduces the number of anion vacancies and therefore 
reduces the oxygen diffusion rate [8]. Generally, TiO 2 is not stable on titanium alloys at high temperatures and the 
Al2O3 layer is not continuous on Ti 3Al, whereas the Al 2O3 layer is continuous on γ-TiAl and stable up to much 
higher temperatures [8]. This improved oxidation resistance of γ-TiAl alloys enhances its utility at higher 
temperatures in comparison to the conventional titanium alloys. 
The very fine lamellar structure of γ-TiAl with grain boundary β phase of the standard alloy after STA 
condition gives very good yield strength and ultimate tensile strength. These grain boundary β phase which is bcc 
crystal structure, is active at high te mperature and increase the number of slip system during compression and 
enhances deformation capability of the alloy. 
 
5. Conclusions 
 
1. The alloys 1 and 2 containing lower Al and higher Nb possesses a fine bi-modal microstructure in 
comparison to a coarser lamellar structure of the standard alloy. 
2. The γ-TiAl alloys studied in this investigation have good room temperature as well as high temperature 
compression strength. The standard alloy possesses a higher compressive strength in general. 
3. The high oxidation resistance of the alloy2 is due to the presence of higher Nb content, having higher 
valance Nb5+, thereby reducing the anion vacancies and resulting in lower oxygen diffusion rate.
