ABSTRACT
An ultra ﬁne grained (UFG) nickel base superalloy doped with 5 vol.%Y 2O3 nanoparticles, Alloy 718-5 vol.%
Y2O3, was fabricated by a powder metallurgy route which combines high energy mechanical milling of Alloy 718
machining chips, spark plasma sintering, hot extrusion and heat treatment, and its microstructure and me-
chanical properties at room temperature and 650 °C were studied. The study showed that the Y 2O3 nanoparticles
reacted with Al from the base alloy and transformed to Y 4Al2O9 nanoparticles (average diameter: 12.5 nm)
which were stable during heat treatment at 970 °C (0.78 Tm, where Tm is the solidus temperature of the alloy in
K). As a result of the high thermal stability of Y 4Al2O9 nanoparticles and their eﬀ ective pinning of the grain
boundaries, the UFG microstructure (average grain size: 179 nm) of the alloy was stable during heat treatment.
Grain boundary and nanoparticle strengthening rendered the heat treated Alloy 718-5 vol.%Y 2O3 alloy with a
notable room temperature tensile yield strength of 1870 MPa. It was demonstrated that the grain boundary
strengthening eﬀect associated with the UFG microstructure was still signi ﬁcant at 650 °C, but clearly decreased
from its level at room temperature. The grain boundary strengthening and nanoparticle strengthening eﬀ ects
which are likely to be independent of test temperature sustain a reasonably high tensile yield strength of
800 MPa at 650 °C, despite the absence of γ′ and γ″ precipitates in the UFG microstructure.
1. Introduction
Bulk ultra ﬁne-grained (UFG, grain sizes 100 –1000 nm) and nano-
crystalline (NC, grain sizes < 100 nm) superalloys have been widely
studied during the past two-three decades, partly because of reports of
signiﬁcant enhancements to their room temperature mechanical prop-
erties relative to those of their coarse-grained (CG) counterparts [ 1–4].
Interestingly, however, it is the fact that superalloys typically require
hours of solution heat treatment at high homologous temperatures
(> 0.8T
m, where Tm is the solidus temperature of the alloy) for
achieving precipitation hardening, which generally promotes rapid
grain growth, and thereby the bene ﬁts of a NC or UFG microstructure
are rapidly lost [ 4,5]. Meanwhile, it has been generally recognized that
grain boundaries weaken metals at elevated temperatures, so in the past
researchers tend to ignore the grain boundaries strengthening e ﬀects at
elevated temperatures, even for oxide nanoparticle stabilized UFG mi-
crostructures which are stable at the elevated temperatures.
It has been demonstrated that nanoparticle strengthening is a pro-
mising way to improve their structural thermal stability since nano-
particles would inhibit dislocation movement and grain boundary
sliding and migration in both NC/UFG metals and CG metals at elevated
temperatures [ 6–9]. In previous studies, thermally stable oxide

2. Experimental Procedures
Alloy 718 alloy chips ( Fig. 1(a)) produced by machining a rod of the
same alloy were blended with 5 vol% of Y
2O3 nanopowder (average
particle size: ~30 nm). The nominal composition of the Alloy 718 alloy
is shown in Table 1 [16]. The chips/powder blend was subsequently
milled using a planetary ball milling machine for 48 h with a speed of
500 rpm in hardened steel vials under argon to fabricate an NC Alloy
718-5 vol.%Y
2O3 powder, shown in Fig. 1(b). The chips and milled
powder were consolidated thermomechanically using a procedure in-
volving spark plasma sintering (SPS) at 960 °C, a pressure of 75 MPa
and a holding time of 10 min to fabricate cylindrical powder compacts.
Owing to the fast hating associated with SPS, the SPSed compacts were
prepared in a very short time duration, so the nanocrystalline structure
of the powder particles turned into an ultra ﬁne grain structure of the
SPSed compacts [ 17,18]. Then the SPSed compacts were hot extruded
in steel cans at 1120 °C using an extrusion ratio of 6:1 to prepare cy-
lindrical rods. Samples cut from the two as-extruded rods were heat
treated via solution treatment at 970 °C for 1 h followed by air cooling
(AC) and then aging at 720 °C for 8 h and 620 °C for 8 h followed again
by AC. The microstructures of the as-extruded and as-heat treated
(HTed) samples were characterized using a Rigaku Ultima IV X-ray
diﬀractometer (XRD) with Cu K α radiation, an Olympus BX51M optical
microscope (OM), a JEOL JEM-2800 transmission electron microscope
(TEM) equipped with an Oxford SDD Energy dispersive spectrometer
(EDS) detector, a Zeiss Auriga 60 scanning electron microscope (SEM)
equipped with an electron back-scattered di ﬀraction (EBSD) detector, a
Zeiss Merlin Compact SEM equipped with an Oxford NordlysMax
2 EDS
detector, and a FEI Quanta 3d SEM equipped with a transmission Ki-
kuchi di ﬀraction (TKD) detector. The TEM and SEM/TKD specimens
were prepared by mechanical dimpling and ion beam milling. The
tensile tests were carried out with a Zwick Z100 and Z050 universal
testing machine for room temperature and 650 °C testing respectively,
and using cylindrical specimens with a gauge length of 10 mm, and
cross-section diameters of 4 mm and 3 mm for room temperature and
650 °C testing. The tensile test specimens were cut along the extrusion
direction. The mechanical testing was performed at a strain rate of
5×1 0
−4 s−1 at both room temperature and 650 °C. Due to the lim-
itation of the amount of material, only two specimens were tested for
each sample and at each test temperature. The samples prepared with
the Alloy 718 chips and NC Alloy 718-5 vol.%Y
2O3 powder are here-
after denoted as A7 and A7Y respectively.

4. Discussion
4.1. Dispersoids and Thermal Stability
The majority of dispersoids in the A7Y alloy are Y 4Al2O9 nano-
particles. In our previous study, we found that the types of Y-Al-O phase
in nickel base superalloys are in ﬂuenced by the Y/Al ratios in the alloys
[23]. In the A7Y alloy, the measured Y, Al and O contents were 2.23 wt
%, 0.41 wt% and 0.98 wt% respectively, which give a Y/Al ratio of 5.4.
This very high Y/Al ratio makes Y 4Al2O9 nanoparticles to be the only
thermodynamically favorable phase that is likely to form [ 23,24]. In
previous studies on ODS alloys, Al has been recognized as a key con-
tributor to the coarsening of Y-M-O particles [ 15]. In our prior study on
another nickel base superalloy containing a higher content of Al, we
observed that the size of Y
4Al2O9 nanoparticles dramatically increases
during solution treatment. In contrast, the Y 4Al2O9 nanoparticles in the
A7Y alloy show an outstanding thermal stability, with their average size
increasing from 9.3 to only 12.5 nm after solution treatment at a rela-
tively high homologous temperature. The thermally stable Y 4Al2O9
nanoparticles eﬀectively inhibit the growth of the ultra ﬁne grains of the
γ matrix despite the high solution treatment temperature.
Based on the consideration that the thermal stability of the micro-
structure is derived from the Zener pinning e ﬀect [ 25], and the equi-
librium average grain size ( R), can be calculated by the following
equation:

taken as 12.5 nm, and f is the volume fraction of the Y 4Al2O9 nano-

growth and maintain the stability of the NC/UFG microstructure of the
superalloys beyond 700 °C. In this sense, the presence of dispersed na-
noparticles is a crucial factor for attaining a thermally stable UFG mi-
crostructure of the A7Y alloy.
4.2. Strengthening E ﬀects of the UFG Microstructure
The major strengthening e ﬀects of a metallic material come from
solid solution strengthening ( ΔσSS), grain boundary strengthening
(ΔσGB), precipitate hardening ( Δσp), dispersion strengthening ( ΔσD) and
dislocation strengthening ( Δσρ). The A7Y alloy has a considerable
content of Mo and Cr in the matrix, a substantial volume fraction of
dispersed nanoparticles and a UFG structure, so ΔσSS, ΔσGB and ΔσD are
signiﬁcant for the A7Y alloy. Beyond that, a large amount of δ pre-
cipitates form in A7Y alloy, so the strengthening e ﬀect (Δσδ) need to be
taken into consideration. Since Al and Nb formed oxide, nitride and δ
phase respectively instead of γ′ and γ″ precipitates, Δσp eﬀect is nearly
absent. Also, it is reasonable to assume that dislocation strengthening is
relatively weak for UFG superalloys compared with the grain boundary
and dispersed nanoparticle strengthening e ﬀects [ 28], hence it is rea-
sonable that Δσρ is not taken into consideration. Although it is well
established that strengthening mechanisms are not additive, one ap-

eﬀect would become more complex with increasing temperature due to
the faster dislocation activities associated with di ﬀusion. Such aspect
needs to be considered when calculating the additive e ﬀect of various
strengthening mechanism.
4.3. Ductility
The ductility of the UFG A7Y alloy is poor with an elongation to
fracture of 1.7% when being tested at room temperature. Shankar et al.
[1] have reported that a NC structured Alloy 718 alloy fabricated by
severe plastic deformation only achieved a maximum tensile strength of
~1400 MPa and ~1% total elongation. The NC and UFG materials are
intrinsic poor in ductility [ 34]. It is mainly imputed to the severely
reduction of dislocation accumulation in ultra ﬁne grains. Meanwhile,
an extraordinarily large population of grain boundaries with high in-
terface energies that would allow the cracks to propagate straightly
across the sample in an intergranular manner. Beyond that, the useful
uniform tensile deformation is close to zero for almost all NC and UFG
metals and alloys [ 34]. However, Sun et al. [ 2] recently reported that

primary reason for the relatively low ductility of the material at both
room temperature and 650 °C. However, the fracture mode transforms
from transgranular to inter granular with increasing the testing tem-
perature from room temperature to 650 °C.
5. Conclusions
A novel Alloy 718-5 vol.%Y
2O3 alloy with thermally stable UFG
microstructure has been fabricated by a combination of high energy
mechanical milling (HEMM), SPS, hot extrusion and heat treatment.
The investigation of the microstructure and room temperature and
650 °C mechanical properties of this alloy lead to the following con-
clusions:
1) The as-extruded A7Y consists of γ matrix, Y4Al2O9 nanoparticles and
(Nb,Ti)N particles, while δ precipitated instead of γ″ precipitated
formed during the heat treatment used in the study. The Y 4Al2O9
nanoparticles are ultra ﬁne and thermally stable with an average
diameter of 9.3 and 12.5 nm in the as-extruded and HTed samples,
respectively. The Y 4Al2O9 nanoparticles render the alloy with a high
thermal stability, with the average grain size of the matrix only
increasing slightly from 179 to 189 nm during heat treatment at a
high homologous temperature (0.78 T
m).
2) The Y 4Al2O9 nanoparticle strengthening and grain boundary
strengthening associated with the UFG microstructure of the A7Y
alloy results in a notable tensile yield strength of 1870 MPa at room
temperature. The grain boundary strengthening e ﬀect is still sig-
niﬁcant at 650 °C, but decreased from its level at room temperature.
The grain boundary strengthening e ﬀect together with the nano-
particle strengthening e ﬀect sustain a tensile yield strength of
800 MPa for the A7Y alloy at 650 °C, despite the absence of γ′ and γ″
precipitates in its microstructure. The relatively low ductility of the
A7Y alloy at both room temperature and 650 °C is likely due to weak
prior particle boundaries which allow formation of cracks during
tensile testing.
3) This work demonstrates that the grain boundary strengthening as-
sociated with UFG structure is still signi ﬁcant at 650 °C, suggesting
that the UFG superalloys doped with nanoparticles are potential
advanced structural materials for both room temperature and ele-
vated temperature applications.
