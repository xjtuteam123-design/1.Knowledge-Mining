ABSTRACT 
High strength-elongation synergy and its dependence on microstructure are the eternal themes of metallic ma -
terials including Ti alloys. In this study, the relationship between the microstructure evolution and mechanical 
properties of the Ti-25Zr-4Al-1.5Mn (TZAM, wt. %) near- α Ti alloy was systematically investigated after hot 
rolling at temperatures ranging in the whole solid solution region. The content of primary α phase decreased 
gradually with the increasing of rolling temperature. An excellent comprehensive mechanical properties with a 
yield strength of 1055 ± 10 MPa and a total elongation of 23.6 ± 0.6 % were obtained for the specimen after 
rolling at 750 
◦
C (single α phase temperature region). This great strength-elongation synergy is mainly due to the 
synthetic action of substructure strengthening, dislocation strengthening, basal random heterogeneity (BRH) 
texture, and suitable content of β phase. The α " martensitic phase was formed after rolling at temperatures of 800 
and 850 
◦
C ( α + β two-phase temperature region), resulting in a significant decrease in yield strength. However, 
remarkable work hardening with a rate of approximately 10 GPa occurred in both specimens. The whole evo -
lution process of primary α grains, namely dynamic recovery and recrystallization during hot-rolled is deeply 
discussed. When the rolling temperature increased to 900 
◦
C (single β phase temperature region), the dominated 
acicular α ′ martensitic phase with high dislocation density resulted in an ultra-high strength (1264 ± 12 and 
1488 ± 11 MPa for yield and tensile strength respectively) at the expense of plasticity. These findings revealed 
the microstructure evolution and its relationship to the mechanical properties of a TiZr-based alloy after the 
thermomechanical processing and helped to understand deeply the theory of metallic strengthening and 
elongation. 
1. Introduction 
Titanium and its alloys have high specific strength, remarkable 
thermal stability, excellent corrosion resistance, and good biocompati -
bility, playing a key role in the fields of aerospace, automotive 
manufacturing, biomedical, military, etc [ 1 – 3 ]. Although it has good 
plasticity, pure titanium is difficult to use as a structural material due to 
its low yield strength of 140 MPa [ 4 ]. To meet the application re -
quirements, it is necessary to develop new Ti alloys to optimize the 
balance between strength and plasticity. Elements of Ti and Zr, 
belonging to the same group (IVB) in the periodic table, have many 
similarities in physical and chemical properties like valence electron 
configuration. Moreover, those two elements can form an infinite solid 
solution in both phase regions of low and high temperatures. Based on 
these advantages, many scholars have invested in the development of 
Ti-Zr-based alloys [ 5 ], and some TiZr-based alloys with high strength 
such as Ti-Zr-Al, Ti-Zr-Al-V, Ti-Zr-Al-Nb, Ti-Zr-Nb-Sn, and Ti-Zr-B have 
been developed [ 5 – 10 ]. Although these alloys have a certain increase in 
yield strength compared to pure titanium, it is difficult to exceed 1000 
MPa and retain good plasticity (less than 15 % in terms of elongation 
after fracture). Therefore, there is a pressing need for further developing 
TiZr-based alloys with simultaneously enhanced yield strength and 
plasticity. 
Regulating the phase composition and microstructural morphology 
is an effective strategy for designing Ti alloys with excellent mechanical 
properties. In general. Ti alloys have α and β two solid solution phases 
under equilibrium conditions. In addition, the metastable phase formed 
by martensitic transformation is also of great significance. As the content

of the Ti alloys, but would lead to a sharp decrease in plasticity [ 12 ]. 
Therefore, to ensure the matching of yield strength and plasticity of Ti 
alloys, it is necessary to regulate the contents of different martensites. 
The microstructure of as-cast Ti alloys usually exerts negative effects 
on their mechanical properties due to their large grain size, high residual 
stress, and uneven chemical composition, etc. Therefore, a thermal- 
mechanical processing (TMP) such as hot rolling, hot forging, and hot 
extrusion, is usually used to regulate the phase distribution and micro -
structure [ 13 ]. Among these TMPs, hot rolling has received high 
attention due to its simple operation, low cost, and wide range of 
applicability [ 14 ]. During hot rolling, the phase constitution and 
microstructural morphology of alloys can be optimized by adjusting 
rolling temperatures and deformation amounts [ 15 ]. Ma et al. [ 11 ] have 
reported that a high yield strength while retaining a certain degree of 
plasticity can be obtained by controlling the contents of the equiaxed α 
phase and the secondary α ′ phase through hot rolling in the α + β phase 
region. Paghandeh et al. [ 16 ]. Have reported the temperature and strain 
regulation mechanism of Ti-6Al-4V alloy with different initial micro -
structure, and the difference of microstructure and phase is the main 
reason for the different mechanical properties. Murty et al. [ 17 ]. Have 
reported the refinement of the α and β phases in Ti-6Al-4V alloy through 
varying hot rolling temperatures, leading to notable improvements in 
both strength and ductility. Liu et al. [ 18 ]. Have studied the effect of 
microstructure on the mechanical properties of Ti-5Al-2Mo-5V-1Fe-1Cr 
alloy during hot rolling in the β single-phase region. As the rolling 
deformation increases, the alloy gradually transitions from coarse grains 
to fine recrystallized ones. Due to the formation of ω and α " phases 
during hot rolling, the tensile strength gradually increases while the 
elongation rate gradually decreases. There is currently a dearth of 
research on the hot rolling process of the new developed 
Ti-25Zr-4Al-1.5Mn alloy within the whole solid solution region. 
Achieving a harmonious balance between strength and ductility was 
proved to be difficult utilizing a TMP technology within a single-phase 
region, largely due to the limited microstructure evolution.

3. Results 
3.1. Phase composition and microstructure 
Fig. 2 shows the XRD patterns of TZAM specimens hot rolled at 
different temperatures. The 750-WQ alloy exhibited a typical dual-phase 
peak pattern, composed of α and β phases. Where the β phase was 
dominated by the (110) and (200) crystal planes. 800-WQ, 850-WQ, and 
900-WQ specimens were composed of a close-packed hexagonal α phase 
and α " phase. Since the α phase and the α ′ phase have the same structure 
[ 19 ], the diffraction peaks of the two phases are the same, and they 
cannot be specifically distinguished in XRD. So, hereafter the densely 
packed hexagonal α phase was used as a collective term for the α phase 
and the α ′ phase. The cleavage peak at the 2-theta near 62
◦

α " martensitic phase. A cleavage peak at the 2-theta near 34

martensitic phase. The asymmetric third peak of 850-WQ XRD pattern

micrographs of the hot-rolled TZAM alloys are shown in Fig. 3 . Based on 
the results of the phase transition temperature determined by DSC, it can 
be seen that the 750-WQ alloy was rolled and deformed in the α 
single-phase region. The 750-WQ specimen was composed of fine α 
phases, elongated α fibrous tissue along the rolling direction, and 
intergranular β phases ( Fig. 3 (a)). The microstructure morphology 
contains phases that were in agreement with the XRD test results. The 
grains in the 750-WQ specimen were relatively small. The rolling tem -
perature of 800 
◦

considered to be an intermediate phase between the α ′ and β phases. Due

At 850 
◦

microstructure of 900-WQ specimen underwent significant changes, the 
complete acicular α ’ / α " martensitic morphology replaced the equiaxed 
primary α grains and the original β grain boundary appeared ( Fig. 3 (d)).

3.3. EBSD analysis 
The EBSD technique was used to analyze the dynamic recovery and 
recrystallization of primary α phase in 750-WQ and 800-WQ specimens.

and grain orientation spread (GOS) maps. RD and TD respectively 
indicate the rolling direction and transverse direction in the diagram. 
Phase distribution diagram of the 750-WQ specimen in Fig. 5 (a) shows

14.2 %. Besides, almost only blocky primary α grains can be observed in 
addition to the green intergranular β phases. Comparing with the 750-

3.4. Mechanical properties 
Fig. 7 shows the tensile results of TZAM specimens rolled at different 
temperatures, as well as some reported mechanical properties for com -

from engineering and true stress-strain curves in Fig. 7 (a) and (b) and 
listed in Table 1 . As shown in Fig. 7 (a), with the increase of rolling 
temperature, the yield strength decreased first and then increased, the 
tensile strength continued to increase, the total elongation continued to 
decrease, and the uniform deformation elongation increased first and 
then decreased. Among all specimens, the 750-WQ specimen shows flow 
behavior similar to gum metal [ 32 ], which contrasts with the behavior 
of the other specimens. The 750-WQ has better comprehensive me -
chanical properties. The yield strength, tensile strength, uniform 
deformation elongation, and highest total elongation were 1055 MPa, 
1142 MPa, 8.8 %, and 23.6 %, respectively. As the rolling temperature 
increased, 800-WQ specimen and 850-WQ specimen showed 
double-yield behaviors. Relevant experiments have shown that there is 
the stress-induced β→ α " martensitic transformation or the martensite 
variants reorientating in the plastic deformation process [ 33 ]. The yield 
strength of the 800-WQ specimen reached 660 MPa, tensile strength, 
uniform deformation elongation, and total elongation were 1244 MPa, 
10.5 %, and 15.2 %, respectively. A lower yield strength of approxi -
mately 612 MPa appeared in the 850-WQ specimen. With a marginal

the whole fracture. The typical dimple-like fracture morphology was 
observed in all specimens of 750-WQ ( Fig. 8 (a)), 800-WQ ( Fig. 8 (b)), 
and 850-WQ ( Fig. 8 (c)). This was a characteristic of ductile fracture. 
From the macroscopic fracture image of 750-WQ specimen, The fracture 
morphology revealed two primary regions: the fiber region and the 
shear lip region [ 45 ]. The fibrous region of specimens in this work was 
selected for microstructure characterization. The dimples in the 750-WQ 
are large, deep, equiaxed, and evenly distributed. The fiber area is small 
and the shear lip area is large, thus showing an excellent total elongation 
of 23.6 %. The dimples of 800-WQ alloy and 850-WQ were shallower 
than that of 750-WQ, which also indicates less total elongation. 
Conversely, the macro-structure region of the 900-WQ alloy ( Fig. 8 (d)) 
shows no obvious shear lip region. Instead, numerous cleavage plat -
forms and a few shallow dimpled regions were observed, indicating a 
typical brittle fracture. This fracture pattern prevents the alloy from 
sustaining neck shrinkage strain, resulting in a total elongation of only 
3.9 %.

4.3. Effect of microstructure on mechanical properties and fracture
