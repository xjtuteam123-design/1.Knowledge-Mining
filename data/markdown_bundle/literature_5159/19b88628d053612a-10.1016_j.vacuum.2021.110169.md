ABSTRACT 
A η *-Ni
6
(Al,Ti)Nb strengthened CoCrNi-based medium-entropy alloy with partially recrystallized and hetero -
geneous grain microstructure was prepared. Block and lamellar η *-Ni
6
(Al,Ti)Nb phases were present in different 
regions. Excellent tensile properties were achieved with yield strength of 1.34 GPa, ultimate tensile strength of 
1.55 GPa and ductility of 9.2%. The deformation mode was dominated by stacking faults in all grains at the 
beginning of deformation and then relied on dislocation slip in the recrystallized grains. The high strain hard -
ening rate is due to the intense obstruction effect of the η *-Ni
6
(Al,Ti)Nb phase for dislocation movement and the 
hard-deformed non-recrystallized grains. Our findings provide a wider range of options for the selection of the 
precipitation phase. 
High entropy alloys (HEAs) and medium entropy alloys (MEAs), and 
in particular alloys with a face-centered-cubic (FCC) structure, have 
attracted extensive attention in recent years [ 1 – 3 ]. Many studies have 
focused on single phase FCC alloys because of their excellent properties 
[ 4 – 9 ]. Although FCC HEAs and MEAs possess many good properties, the 
strengths of these alloys, and particularly their yield strengths, are not 
sufficiently high to enable their use as structural materials. Strength -
ening these alloys while maintaining acceptable ductility is still a 
challenging task. Many effective strengthening methods such as het -
erogeneous structure strengthening, precipitation strengthening and 
partial recrystallization structure strengthening have been investigated 
for obtaining high strength and ductility. 
The formation of heterogeneous structure is an effective approach for 
achieving a good combination of strength and ductility [ 10 – 13 ]. Yang 
et al. [ 10 ] designed a three-level heterogeneous grain structure CrCoNi 
MEA with the structure of nanocrystalline grains, ultrafine grains and 
micro-grains that endows it with a high yield strength (YS) of 1150 MPa 
and high uniform elongation (EL) of 22%. Wu et al. [ 11 ] prepared an 
HEA with a combination of heterogeneous grains and non-recrystallized 
grains structure. Excellent strain hardening ability due to the complex 
heterogeneous structure is the main origin of the outstanding 
strength-ductility balance. In addition to the heterogeneous grain 
structure, heterogeneous precipitation behavior is also observed in some 
CoCrNi-based MEAs [ 12 , 13 ]. 
Precipitation strengthening has been proved to be a highly effective 
approach for strengthening FCC HEAs and MEAs. Based on the results 
obtained with Ni-based superalloys, γ
′
-Ni
3
Al phase was employed to 
strengthen FCC HEAs [ 14 – 22 ]. γ
′
-hardened HEAs show huge application 
potential as superalloys. Some results indicate that HEAs has better 
structure stability and lower γ
′

that the high temperature hardness of γ

some conventional Ni-based superalloys [ 14 , 18 , 19 ]. However, the re -
searches on HEAs with excellent synthetical properties are not enough. 
Besides, recent studies also proved that topologically close-packed (TCP) 
phases also can act as an effective strengthening phase by optimizing 
their size, distribution and volume fraction [ 23 – 26 ].

Tensile property results at room-temperature are presented in Fig. 3 
(a). Due to its single FCC phase, the A1 alloy shows low strength and 
excellent ductility with YS = 382 MPa, UTS (ultimate tensile strength) =
655 MPa and EL = 52%. After cold rolling and aging treatment, the 
strengths of the A2 alloy is greatly improved, with YS = 1.34 GPa, UTS 
= 1.55 GPa and acceptable ductility EL = 9.2%. The strain hardening 
rates of two alloys are shown in Fig. 3 (b). The A2 alloy has higher strain 
hardening rate than the A1 alloy which is the main origin of its high UTS. 
Fig. 3 (c) and (d) show the fracture morphology of the A2 alloy. A large 
number of dimples were observed. Based on the morphological char -
acteristics, three distinct regions can identified. The UFG region is filled 
with dimples. In the CG region, strip-shape dimples produced by the 
lamellar η * phase with a certain depth can be easily found as indicated 
by the blue arrows. Fig. 3 (d) shows the fracture of the NRG region. In 
this region, no dimples were observed due to the absence of η * phase. 
The deformation microstructures at different stages were studied by 
TEM in order to elucidate the deformation mechanism in the A2 alloy, as 
shown in Fig. 4 . Fig. 4 (a) and (b) show the early stage deformation 
structure at a strain of ~2% in the NRG region. NRG shows a high 
density of dislocations. No traces of dislocation slip were found. Dense 
stacking faults (SFs) can be observed in this region, as indicated by the 
green arrows. In the UFG region, SFs in two directions formed in the 
matrix, as shown by the red arrows in Fig. 4 (c). High resolution (HR) 
imaging and Fourier transformation (FFT) were used to verify that the 
black slice is the η * phase rather than deformation twin. SFs can also 
form in the η * phase (shown by the purple arrow). A few dislocations 
were observed in both the matrix and the η * phase, as shown in Fig. 4 (c) 
and (e) (red circles). Moreover, dislocation slip still did not occur. The 
CG region has the same microstructure as the UFG region, as shown in 
Fig. 4 (f). SFs formed in the matrix and the η * phase, and rare dislocations 
formed in the matrix but did not slip. When the strain was increased to 
5%, the deformation structure is characterized by dislocation slip along 
the {111} planes in both UFG and CG regions, as observed from Fig. 4 (g) 
and (h). Fig. 4 (h) shows that dislocations stacked in the matrix channel 
and the interface between the matrix and the η * phase. Meanwhile, 
crooked dislocations emerged in η * phase (blue circles in Fig. 4 (g) and 
(h)). In the NRG region, there are no slip traces or deformation twins, 
implying that for NRG it is difficult to deform due to the high stress 
caused by high density dislocations ( Fig. S5 ). 
Based on the results of deformation microstructure evolution, the 
deformation mechanism of the A2 alloy can be described as follows. At 
the early stage of deformation, the deformation is dominated by SF in 
three regions. η * phases in the UFG and CG regions are also deformed by 
SF. Although some dislocations were found in the matrix and η * phase in 
the UFG and CG regions, no evidence of dislocation motion such as slip 
bands or crooked dislocation was observed. With increased degree of 
deformation, dislocation slip in the matrix dominates the deformation.

The microstructure of the A2 alloy was divided into three regions 
according to the grain characteristics with each region making its own 
contribution to the total YS. For the UFG and CG regions, solid solution 
hardening, precipitation hardening and grain boundary hardening 
should be considered. For the NRG region, dislocation hardening is the 
main hardening mechanism. According to quantitative analysis (see 
supplementary material), increasing the area fraction of the NRG and CG 
regions will be benefit for the improvement of YS. The high UTS is 
attributed to the high strain hardening rate. The high strain hardening 
rate of the A2 alloy can be explained based on Fig. 4 . At the beginning of 
deformation, dislocation slip did not occur and deformation relied on SF. 
This cannot lead to severe deformation, so that deformation is difficult 
in the early stage. The strain hardening rate decreases as a result of the 
occurrence of dislocation slip, but it is still much larger than that of the 
A1 alloy. This due to the effect of the η * phases. Irregular block η * can 
effectively prevent dislocation motion in all directions, as shown in 
Fig. 4 (g). Lamellar η * is always parallel to a {111} plane and cannot act 
as a dislocation barrier when dislocations slide along the plane parallel 
to it. Thus, lamellar η * can only serve as an effective obstacle for dis -
locations that slip on the {111} planes that are not parallel to lamellar 
η *. Therefore, almost no dislocations pile up at the interface of the 
matrix and lamellar η * in Fig. 4 (g), but a pile-up of dislocations can be 
observed in Fig. 4 (h). On the other hand, the hardly deformed NRG is 
another nonnegligible factor contributing to the high strain hardening 
rate. 
In the present work, a precipitation-hardened CoCrNi-based MEA 
with partially recrystallized structure and heterogeneous grains was 
fabricated by cold rolling and heat treatment. Al, Ti, and Nb additions 
promote the formation of the η *-Ni
6
(Al,Ti)Nb phase. The deformation 
and strengthening mechanisms were investigated using TEM. The key 
conclusions were as follows: 
(1) A2 alloy has a complex structure with three regions called UFG, 
CG and NRG. η *-Ni
6
(Al,Ti)Nb is introduced into HEA/MEA in two 
forms, as irregular blocks in the UFG region and lamellar shapes 
in the CG region. High density dislocations are retained in the 
NRG region. 
(2) A2 alloy shows excellent tensile properties with YS~1.34 GPa, 
UTS~1.55 GPa and EL~9.2%. YS is the sum of the individual 
contributions from each regions. The area friction of UFG and 
NRG regions has significantly effect on the YS. 
(3) SFs dominate the deformation in all regions at the early stage. As 
the strain increases, the deformation relies on dislocation slip in 
the UFG and CG regions. NRG hardly deforms even in high strain 
situation. The high strain hardening rate is due to the SF, strong 
obstruction of dislocation movement and the hardly-deformed 
NRGs.
