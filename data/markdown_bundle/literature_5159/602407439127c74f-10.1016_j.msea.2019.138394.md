ABSTRACT
High entropy alloys with excellent mechanical properties have broad application scope and market demand.
However, a long-standing problem for these alloys is their low ductility. In this work, a novel alloy
(Ni6Cr4WFe9Ti) with ﬁne grained structure was prepared, which exhibited fracture strength of over 970 MPa and
tensile elongation as large as ~12.2% at room temperature. The processing route involved additive manu-
facturing via a selective laser melting technique which resulted in an optimal microstructure of ﬁne grains with
nanoparticles uniformly distributed in the matrix alloy. This approach can e ﬀectively re ﬁne the crystalline
structure and the coarse grain boundary precipitates, to provide a general pathway for manufacturing ﬁne
grained material with both high strength and ductility.
1. Introduction
High entropy alloys are a new type of metal materials, which con-
tain ﬁve or more principal elements with each elemental concentration
between 5 at% and 35 at% [ 1–3]. These alloys based on the severe
distortion in the crystal lattice and solid solution strengthening, which
endows them with high creep resistance, high strength and good tri-
bological properties. Thus, high entropy alloys are very attractive ma-
terials for many potential applications (including automobiles, sports
equipment, electronic equipment and aerospace applications) [ 1,4].
However, although the high con ﬁguration entropy in high entropy al-
loys helps to stabilize solid solutions (and inhibit compound phases),
previous studies have shown that most high entropy alloys consist of
multiple phases and even complex compounds [ 5,6]. In general, the
compound particles are inherently strong in high entropy alloys and
they can be easily hardened to high strength levels, but their limited
ductility at room temperature is a major drawback. Among the alloy
preparation methods, casting is widely employed to synthesize the new
kinds of high entropy alloy materials in recent years. However, the
alloy obtained by casting is frequently scrapped because of its coarse or
unequal grain size in the ﬁnal alloy microstructure. Especially, the
microstructural heterogeneity of compound phases (brittle and coarse
secondary phases) tends to introduce a localized stress-strain con-
centration and induces microcracks under the loading [ 7,8]. At room
temperature, the casting alloy (for example, Ni
6Cr4WFe9Ti casting
alloy, hereafter abbreviated as VAM-alloy, see composition analysis in
Table 1 ) usually displays a uniform tensile elongation of only a few
percent (VAM curve in Fig. 6a). Therefore, great e ﬀorts have been made
to improve the strength and ductility of high entropy alloys [ 9].
Improving the ductility of alloy materials has been the focus of
many studies in recent decades [ 10,11]. One approach to improve the
ductility of metal alloys is to control the system free energy, which
eﬀectively prevents the formation of brittle compound phases. How-
ever, unlike conventional metal alloys, high entropy alloys have several
principal alloy elements. Thus, complex thermodynamics and kinetics
problems are involved in the preparation of these alloys [ 12–15],
making it diﬃcult to control the formation of compound phases, such as
σ, μ and coarse grain boundary precipitates [ 16]. Recently, many re-
searchers have been trying to improve the ductility of metal alloys by
means of grain re ﬁning methods, and have obtained remarkable
achievements [ 17–19]. In general, micro-alloying approaches are used
for synthesis of ﬁne grained (micro/nano materials) materials [ 20,21].
Micro-alloying can greatly improve the comprehensive properties of the
alloy by adding rhenium (Re), tungsten (W), molybdenum (Mo), va-
nadium (V) and rare elements as alloying elements, which can form the
ﬁne and dispersive precipitates. They can e ﬀectively re ﬁne the grains
by promoting grain nucleation and hindering grain growth [ 22–24].

methods [ 26]. Therefore, this technique makes it possible to prepare
ﬁne grained alloys with nano-precipitates.
Herein, a non-equiatomic and cheap high entropy alloy was de-
signed, based on statistical analysis of the phase selection rules (the
atomic size polydispersity and the mixing enthalpy). Titanium and iron
elements were added to the high entropy alloy to improve the lattice
distortion and lower the alloy costs. A series of NiCrWFeTi alloys was
designed based on the ﬁrst-principles calculation, but this paper only
focuses on the Ni

additive manufacturing on the microstructure evolution and mechan-

2. Experimental procedure
The matrix alloy used in this study was commercially available gas-
atomized Ni
6Cr4WFe9Ti high entropy powder (99.7% purity) with a
nearly spherical shape and a mean particle size of 15– 53 μm. The
chemical composition of the Ni 6Cr4WFe9Ti high entropy powder is
shown in Table 1. Fig. 1 (Fig. 1) shows the scanning electron micro-
scope (SEM) image of Ni 6Cr4WFe9Ti high entropy powder. It can be
that most of the particles were sphere-shaped, which ensured good
ﬂowability and less porosity during selective laser melting (SLM).
Ni6Cr4WFe9Ti alloys were prepared by two di ﬀerent methods under
diﬀerent conditions. The ﬁrst alloy (hereafter abbreviated as SLM-alloy,
see nominal chemical composition in Table 1) was prepared by a self-
developed selective laser melting (SLM) 150 equipment with a build
chamber of 150 × 150 × 150 mm
3. (SLM 150, Xi'an Jiaotong Uni-
versity, China). The Ni 6Cr4WFe9Ti powders bed was selectively fused
by the ytterbium ﬁber laser (YLR-500-WC-Y11, Germany). The op-
timum experimental parameters were determined through orthogonal
tests as follows: laser power of 300 W, scanning speed of 2500 mm/s,
powder layer thickness of μm, and scanning space of 80 μm. The total
laser torch working area was protected by dynamic argon atmosphere
(with purity of 99.9%) to keep the alloy powders from being oxyge-
nated. Before starting the process, a three dimensional computer-aided
design (CAD) model was sliced into layers by Magics RP software. The
data of each layer contained the geometrical information along which
the laser scaned the respective cross section of the part, and the tensile
specimens were directly prepared (see Fig. 2). To decrease the loss of
alloying element, the gas-atomized Ni
6Cr4WFe9Ti high entropy pow-
ders were also used as starting materials for processing the second alloy
ingot (hereafter abbreviated as VAM-alloy, see nominal chemical
composition in Table 1). The powders were uniaxially pressed into
cylindrical specimens using a hydraulic press, with a compaction
pressure of 500 MPa. Then the compacted specimens were melted by
vacuum arc melting under argon atmosphere, (with purity of 99.9%),
on a water-cooled Cu hearth. The alloys were remelted at least ﬁve
times in order to improve homogeneity, and the ﬁnal alloy ingot was
prepared with dimension of 50 × 10 × 5 mm
3. For the mechanical test,
rectangular dog-bone shaped tensile specimens with gauge lengths of
20 mm (see Fig. 2) were prepared from the alloy ingot by electrical
discharge machining. Subsequently, the formed tensile specimens were
polished and cleaned in acetone using an ultrasonic cleaning machine
for 15 min.
For microstructural observation, the alloy specimens were ground
and polished using standard metallographic techniques to reveal grain
boundaries and ﬁne precipitates, some specimens were etched with a
solution composed of phosphoric acid and ethanol (in a volume ratio of
1:9). The phase was identi ﬁed using an X-ray di ﬀraction spectrometer
(XRD) (PANAlytical X'Pert ’S-MPD, Holland) with Cu-K ɑ
(λ = 0.154056 nm) radiation. The microstructures were characterized
using a Zeiss Gemini 500 FEG SEM and a JEM-2100Plus (200kv)
transmission electron microscope (TEM) coupled with energy dis-
persive spectrometer (EDS). Tensile tests were carried out on a servo-
hydraulic Instron testing machine with an extensometer at room tem-
perature.

3.2. Mechanical properties of the ﬁne grained Ni 6Cr4WFe9Ti alloys
The mechanical properties of the SLM-alloy and VAM-alloys at room
temperature are shown in Fig. 6. As expected, the SLM-alloy exhibited
excellent mechanical properties with the yield strength, ( σy)o f
~742 MPa, the ultimate tensile strength, ( σUTS) of ~972 MPa, the
elastic modulus, (E) of ~74.7 GPa and a total elongation, ( ε)o f
~12.2%. These values corresponded to a ~93% increase in yield
strength and a ~50% increase in ultimate tensile strength, compared
with the VAM-alloy ( σy = 385 MPa, σUTS=649 MPa, E = 137.7 GPa,
ε = 6.9%). The tensile ductility similarly increased by ~77% –~12.2%,
while the elastic modulus decreased by~46%. (compared with VAM-
alloy, SLM-alloy displayed both higher yield strength and higher elon-
gation to failure). The SEM images of the tensile fracture at di ﬀerent
magniﬁcations for the SLM-alloy and VAM-alloy are shown in Fig. 7.
Striking di ﬀerences were observed in the fracture characteristics asso-
ciated with di ﬀerent grain sizes of alloys. In the SLM-alloy, there were a
large number of dimples of extremely small size (see Fig. 7a). Inter-
estingly, most of the crystallized grains showed the same crystal-
lographic orientation, and the others were interwoven with the former
(see Fig. 7 b). Further analysis showed that grains of di ﬀerent orienta-
tions in SLM alloy were interwoven with each other (see Fig. 7c) in the
microstructure. This phenomenon was not observed in the VAM-alloy.
High magni ﬁcation morphology of the tensile fracture indicated that
the fracture mode of SLM-alloy belonged to intergranular fracture (see
Fig. 7c). In addition, some large and dense particles were clearly ob-
served in the microstructure, and the distribution and size of these
particles were the same as precipitate particles, which indicated that
the dense big particles were precipitates. In contrast to the VAM-alloy,
the tensile fracture surface was composed of ﬁne dimples (see Fig. 7d)
and the dimples were much larger than those in the SLM-alloy.
Therefore, the tensile fracture surfaces of the SLM-alloy and VAM-alloys
were quite di ﬀerent in terms of the grain size and distribution feature,
which in ﬂuenced on the fracture behavior of the alloys.
First, the origin of the high strength of the newly developed SLM-
alloy was analyzed. The interstitial and substitutional solutes existing
extensively in high entropy alloys can play an important role in im-
proving the strengths of alloy materials [ 41,42]. The interstitial or
substitutional atoms, such as W, Ti, Cr, Fe atoms, may enter the fcc
lattice interstitially, which causes interstitial solid solution strength-
ening. In our work, the intensity of γ diﬀraction peak in SLM-alloy was
obviously shifted, and the lattice parameter of γ was found to be
3.278 Å, and their lattice constant decreased, which indicate that
abundant atoms (especially the large atomic radius of Ti and W) were
dissolved into γ. Thus, solid solution strengthening can be the dominant
mechanism on the strengthening of SLM-alloy. Besides, it is well known
that ﬁne grain o ﬀers a higher volume fraction of grain boundaries,
which can e ﬀectively hinder the movements of dislocations during
deformation. Therefore, grain size re ﬁnement can improve the strength
of the alloy materials. The e ﬀect of grain size on the mechanical
properties of materials is best described by the Hall-Petch equation

In this study, the strategy was that the microstructure could be altered
to delay failure by reducing the grain size. As shown earlier, the matrix
grains and precipitated particles tended to form coarser grains in the
vacuum arc melting (see Fig. 4f). Also, the precipitated particles were
dispersed in the grain boundary, which introduced a localized stress-
strain concentration. The particles were prone to decohesion and frac-
ture during processing or loading (see Fig. 5j), degrading the ductility.
For the selective laser melting process, in contrast, extremely high
cooling rate and undercooling were easily obtained. This ensured that
the ﬁner grains were formed. The nanosized particles dispersed in the
grain boundary and grain interior served the dual purposes of blocking
dislocations and storing dislocations. The dislocation pinning and ac-
cumulation can be clearly seen in the TEM image (see Fig. 5h and i). As
a result, the matrix grains and precipitated particles were smaller in size
in the SLM-alloy. This alleviated the propensity for intergranular frac-
ture and thus increased the total elongation before failure.
4. Concluding remarks
In this work, Ni
6Cr4WFe9Ti alloys with ﬁne-grain structure were
successfully manufactured via an improved SLM mixing process that
enabled the control of grains in a nanostructured regime. The resulting
SLM-alloy exhibited outstanding tensile ductility, in terms of both the
total elongation to failure and the uniform elongation (both almost two
times that of VAM-alloy; see Fig. 6). Our ﬁndings suggest that selective
laser melting technique can re ﬁne the grain size signi ﬁcantly, and si-
multaneously improve the strength and ductility of alloys. It is expected
that the proposed ﬁne grains microstructure strategy would be equally
eﬀective for the preparation of other high entropy alloys materials.
