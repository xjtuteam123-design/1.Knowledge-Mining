Superior Mechanical Properties of AlCoCrFeNiTi x
High-Entropy Alloys upon Dynamic Loading
Z.M. Jiao, S.G. Ma, M.Y. Chu, H.J. Yang, Z.H. Wang, Y. Zhang, and J.W. Qiao
(Submitted August 18, 2015; in revised form November 25, 2015; published online December 29, 2015)
High-entropy alloys with composition of AlCoCrFeNiTi x (x: molar ratio; x = 0, 0.2, 0.4) under quasi-static
and dynamic compression exhibit excellent mechanical properties. A positive strain-rate sensitivity of yield
strength and the strong work-hardening behavior during plastic ﬂows dominate upon dynamic loading in
the present alloy system. The constitutive relationships are extracted to model ﬂow behaviors by employing
the Johnson-Cook constitutive model. Upon dynamic loading, the ultimate strength and fracture strain of
AlCoCrFeNiTix alloys are superior to most of bulk metallic glasses and in situ metallic glass matrix
composites.
Keywords constitutive equation, dynamic loading, high-entropy
alloys, mechanical properties
1. Introduction
Due to their versatile properties, including exceptional
damage tolerance, high thermal stability and hardness, great
fatigue resistance, excellent corrosion and wear resistance, and
signiﬁcant high-temperature compressive strength, (Ref 1-11),
high-entropy alloys (HEAs) hold the potential in a wide range
of applications for functional and structural materials. Unlike
traditional alloys, which are normally based on one or two
principal elements, HEAs are multi-principal component alloys
with at least ﬁve equiatomic or near-equiatomic alloying
elements (Ref 2, 12). However, it is of particular interest to note
that, despite containing multiple elements with different crystal
structures, HEAs actually exhibit a signiﬁcant degree of mutual
solubility. Only few multi-principal element alloys have solid
solution face-centered cubic (FCC) and/or body-centered cubic
(BCC) structures, while majority of the HEAs are multiphase,
containing both solid solution and intermetallic phases (Ref
13), which is often attributed to the conﬁgurational entropy
contribution to the total free energy (Ref 12).
For actual structural engineering applications of these alloys,
many kinds of extreme conditions other than quasi-static loadings
should be appreciated. For instance, they may be subjected to high-
speed dynamic loading. Previously, it has been found that the
AlCoCrFeNiTix system is primarily composed of the mixture of
A2 (disordered BCC) and B2 (ordered BCC) phases, may be a
Laves phase in the as-cast condition, and possesses excellent
room-temperature compressive mechanical properties upon quasi-
static loadings (Ref14-17). However, very limited studies focus on
the mechanical properties of HEAs upon dynamic loading. It is
now well known that the mechanical properties, such as yielding
stress, ductility, and ultimate strength, of materials will change
under different strain rates (Ref18). In this study, the deformation
behaviors of AlCoCrFeNiTix alloys upon quasi-static and dynamic
loadings were investigated by solid solution strengthening mech-
anism and strain-rate sensitivity, respectively. The Johnson-Cook
constitutive model (J-C model) is employed to depict quantita-
tively plastic ﬂows under dynamic compression. Additionally,
the ultimate strength and fracture strain of the present alloys and
some other high-strength bulk alloys upon dynamic loading are
compared.
2. Materials and Methods
Alloys ingots with nominal composition of AlCoCrFeNiTix (x:
molar ratio; x = 0, 0.2, and 0.4, denoted by Ti 0,T i0.2,a n dT i0.4,
respectively) were prepared by arc melting mixtures of pure metals
in a Ti-gettered high-purity argon atmosphere. These ingots were
remelted at least ﬁve times to ensure the chemical homogeneity and
injection cast into a water-cooled copper mold to obtain cylindrical
rods of U3 9 70 mm. The phase of the as-cast samples was
characterized by x-ray diffraction (XRD). The microstructure of
alloys was examined with a scanning electron microscope (SEM).
Cylindrical samples with an aspect ratio of 2:1 were prepared for
the quasi-static compression with a strain rate of 1 9 10/C0 3 s/C0 1 at
room temperature. The dynamic loading experiment was con-
ducted at room temperature using a split Hopkinson pressure bar
(SHPB) apparatus with a momentum trap. Cylindrical samples
with an aspect ratio of 1:1 were prepared for dynamic compression.

Normally, the bulk metallic glasses (BMGs) possess the
highest strength. However, controlled by solid solution
strengthening, with a second phase in some cases, the strength
of HEAs can be very high, and may be comparable to BMGs
(Ref 19). It is noted that these properties of the present alloys
are even superior to most of the high-strength bulk alloys
reported, such as BMGs (Ref 23) and metallic glass matrix
composites (MGMCs) (Ref 24). The excellent compressive
mechanical properties of Ti
0.4 alloy are mainly attributed to
solid solution strengthening mechanisms of Ti atoms and the
higher volume fraction of the B2 phase, which may result in
higher strength. For Ti 0 alloy, the ﬁve components are in
equiatomic ratio and have the same probability to occupy the
lattice sites to form BCC solid solution. As the Ti with larger
atomic radius occupies the lattice sites, the lattice distortion
energy will increase signiﬁcantly and the effect of solid solution
strengthening is enhanced. As a result, the yielding strength
greatly increases, and the plasticity slightly decreases (Ref 14).

So far, the dynamic compression of HEAs (Ref 29, 30),
BMGs (Ref 31-35), in situ (Ref 26, 36-39) and ex situ (Ref 27,
40-43) MGMCs, and Ni-based superalloys (Ref 21, 22, 44) has
been extensively investigated. Figure 5 summarizes the ultimate
strength plotted against the fracture strain for a series of the as-
cast HEAs, Ni-based superalloys, BMGs, as well as in situ and
ex situ MGMCs at room temperature upon dynamic loading. It
is noted that current HEAs exhibit higher ultimate strengths and
fracture strains than BMGs and in situ MGMCs upon dynamic
loading, which are mainly attributed to solid solution strength-
ening, the variation of phase content, and the thermally activated
mechanisms associated with the dislocation dynamics (Ref 25,
45-48). As reported previously, the strength of crystalline alloy
is strongly dependent on the activities of dislocations and
dislocation interactions become stronger at high strain rate,
which further enhances the ultimate strength of the alloys (Ref
25). Considering the deformation mechanisms of Ni-based
superalloys and dislocation slips and twinning dominate plastic
ﬂow when subjected to dynamic loading (Ref 21, 22). Twinning,
however, requires more applied stress than dislocation slip. At
high strain rates, dislocation multiplication is more rapid than at
lower strain rates and contributes to strengthening. The
deformation of BMGs at dynamic strain rates is prone to focus
on a major shear band, and the fracture stress reﬂects the true
failure behavior without the inﬂuences of a large number of
localized shear bands compared to those at quasi-static strain
rates, which is mainly responsible for fracture with little fracture
strain at dynamic strain rates (Ref 23). For the in situ MGMCs
upon dynamic compression, the multiplication of dislocations
within dendrites, which can result in plastic ﬂow of the
composites, competes against the applied strain exerted by
external loadings (Ref 26). Besides, the strength of the current
alloys is superior to that of most ex situ MGMCs. For the
tungsten-foam-reinforced composite, the ultimate strength is
3458 MPa and the fracture strain is 20.6% (Ref 43). Intrinsic
plasticity of the tungsten phase as a framework, together with
large work-hardening capacity, makes the ultimate strengths of
these ex situ MGMCs superhigh. However, simple casting and
improved yielding strength, together with prominent plastic
ﬂows and distinguished work-hardening capacity upon dynamic
loading, make the present HEAs more promising as new-
generation engineering materials.
