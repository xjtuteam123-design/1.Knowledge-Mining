1. Introduction 
In recent years, high-entropy alloys (HEAs) or multi-principal- 
element alloys have attracted extensive research interest and opened 
the doors to explore new complex alloy systems [ 1 , 2 ]. A number of HEAs 
reveal promising properties, such as high strength and toughness [ 3 – 7 ], 
exceptional corrosion and wear resistance [ 8 – 10 ], and good irradiation 
resistance [ 11 – 13 ]. Among these, the refractory HEAs (RHEAs) based on 
refractory elements have shown excellent thermal stability and soft -
ening resistance at high temperatures [ 14 – 22 ]. For example, two 
RHEAs, MoNbTaW and MoNbTaVW, retain high yield strengths of 405 
and 477 MPa at 1873 K, respectively [ 14 ], which are extremely superior 
to those of Inconel 718. Nevertheless, their high density and limited 
ductility at room temperature (RT) are not conducive to the malleability 
and widespread applications. Since then, a series of RHEAs with low 
density or good ductility were developed, such as AlCr
