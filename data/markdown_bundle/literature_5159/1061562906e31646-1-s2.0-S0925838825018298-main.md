ABSTRACT
The oxidation behavior and mechanism of Ni-Cr-W based and Ni-Cr-Fe based superalloys were investigated 
through isothermal oxidation experiments in air at 1000 
◦
C. The results indicate that the oxidation kinetics 
curves of both alloys generally follow a parabolic law, with the diffusion of ions in the oxide scale being the main 
controlling of the oxidation process. Despite the outer oxide scales of both alloys peel off after 300 h of oxidation, 
the inner oxide scales remain tightly intact and still maintain excellent oxidation resistance. The Ni-Cr-W-based 
and Ni-Cr-Fe-based superalloys exhibited weight gains of approximately 1.20 mg/cm² and 1.12 mg/cm², 
respectively. However, the presence of a dense and continuous Nb-rich layer within the oxide scale of Ni-Cr-Fe 
based superalloys promotes Cr
2
O
3 
formation, which further inhibits diffusion of other alloying elements and 
oxygen within the oxide scale. This also increases the bond strength between the oxide scale and matrix. 
Therefore, Ni-Cr-Fe based superalloys exhibit better oxidation resistance than Ni-Cr-W based superalloys in the 
later stages of oxidation. Additionally, the internal oxide Al
2
O
3 
particles are distributed along the grain 
boundaries of the alloy matrix, inhibiting the diffusion of other alloying elements and oxygen along the grain 
boundaries.
1. Introduction
Ni-based superalloys are extensively utilized as key structural ma -
terials in many fields, including aerospace, aircraft, and chemical in -
dustry, owing to their exceptional high-temperature strength, tissue 
stability, and resistance to oxidation [1 – 6] . Typically, superalloys are 
enriched with multiple elements like Nb, W, Mo, Cr and Al to enhance 
the γ -Ni solid solution, which not only are the mechanical properties of 
the alloy improved, but the oxidation resistance and high-temperature 
wear resistance are also improved [7 – 14] . For instance, Ni-Cr-W based 
superalloys use W as a key element to enhance solid solution strength -
ening, thereby improving the mechanical properties of the alloy at room 
and high temperatures [10,15] . W is an element that is prone to carbide 
formation, and finely dispersed carbides exhibit strong diffusion 
strengthening [16] . The precipitation of M
23
C
6 
carbides along the grain 
boundaries effectively inhibits grain boundary sliding, thereby 
enhancing the tensile strength [17] . The matrix strength at high tem -
peratures can be maintained by refractory elements such as Mo and W; 
however, the oxidation resistance of the alloys is compromised due to 
the volatility of their oxides [18 – 20] . The volatile oxides destroy the 
integrity of the oxide scale, leading to a loose and porous structure that 
fails to effectively prevent the outward dispersion of alloying elements 
and inward infiltration of oxygen [21] . While in Ni-Cr-Fe based super -
alloys, about 5 wt% of Nb is usually added. Xu et al. [22] demonstrated 
that Nb microalloying enhances chromium chemical activity within γ -Ni 
matrices while suppressing cationic outward diffusion of Fe and Ni,

The high-temperature oxidation properties of alloys are closely 
associated with their composition, and Ni-based superalloys encompass 
a diverse range of alloying elements that interact and significantly in -
fluence the formation of oxides. Alloys are susceptible to oxidation in 
complex high-temperature air environments, and a certain thickness of 
oxide scale will be formed on their surfaces. The presence of a contin -

of the alloy [27 – 29] . However, localized oxidation or spalling of this 
oxide layer can significantly impact the material ’ s service life. There -
fore, it is imperative to investigate the oxidation characteristics of 
Ni-based superalloys in high-temperature environments.

high temperature oxidation mechanism of the two alloys is clarified, 
which provides a basic theory for the use of the alloys in high temper -

Both alloy ingots were fabricated through the vacuum induction

mass gain method at a constant temperature of 1000

C. Prior to 
measuring their mass gain with an electronic balance accurate to 
0.01 mg, the specimens were cooled in ambient air until reaching room

tion of the oxidation scales. To identify phase and grain distribution, a

5. Conclusions
This paper investigates and compares the oxidation behaviors of Ni- 
Cr-W based and Ni-Cr-Fe based superalloys when oxidized at 1000 
◦
C for 
300 h, aiming to clarify the high-temperature oxidation mechanisms of 
these two superalloys. The main findings can be summarized as follows: 
(1) The oxidation kinetic curves of the two high-temperature alloys 
at 1000 
◦
C basically conform to the parabolic law, and the 
oxidation process is mainly controlled by the bidirectional 
diffusion of oxygen ions and metal ions in the oxide scale. As the 
oxidation time increases, the weight gain of the alloy increases, 
while the weight gain rate gradually decreases. After 300 h of 
oxidation, the oxidation weight gain of Ni-Cr-Fe based alloy is 
slightly smaller than that of Ni-Cr-W based alloy.
(2) The two superalloys formed a bilayer steady structure of Cr
2
O
3 
in 
the inner layer and spinel in the outer layer during the oxidation 
stabilization stage. Meanwhile the matrix undergoes internal 
oxidation and Al
2
O
3 
internal oxides are generated along the grain 
boundaries. After 300 h of oxidation, the outer layer of spinel 
oxide scale peeled off, but the inner layer of Cr
2
O
3 
oxide scale was 
still continuous and dense, which could still protect the matrix 
well, and both superalloys had good oxidation resistance.
(3) The presence of a Nb-rich layer in Ni-Cr-Fe based alloy promotes 
the formation of Cr
2
O
3 
oxide scale and inhibits the diffusion and 
internal oxidation of other alloy elements in the oxide layer. 
Moreover, the continuous accumulation of Nb-rich layer in the 
inner layer can increase the adhesion between the oxide scale and 
the matrix. Therefore, the oxidation resistance of Ni-Cr-Fe based 
alloy is slightly better than that of Ni-Cr-W based alloy during the 
high temperature oxidation process at 300 h.
(4) The presence of Al
2
O
3 
particles as internal oxides along the grain 
boundaries hinders the inward diffusion of oxygen and the out -
ward diffusion of metal ions, thereby enhancing the alloy ’ s 
resistance to oxidation.
