ABSTRACT 
High entropy alloys (HEAs) are a new kind of basic materials with excellent properties, which can be used in 
extreme service environment. In this paper, high temperature tensile properties of as-cast and forged CrMnFe -
CoNi high entropy alloy with a single-phase face-centered cubic (FCC) crystal structure were investigated. The 
coarse equiaxed structure was transformed into a fine structure with the average grain size of ~18.5 μ m after hot 
forging, and twinning and low angle grain boundaries (LAGBs) were observed in as-forged alloy. Tensile prop -
erties of the alloy in the as-cast and forged states were characterized in the temperature range of 25 – 950 
◦
C. An 
outstanding strength had been achieved at every range of temperature for as-forged alloy, especially at 650

the ultimate tensile strength and yield strength of the as-forged alloy increased by 40% and 158% compared with 
the as-cast alloy. Prominent serrations occurred from 350

C for as-cast HEAs and from 350

1. Introduction 
As a major breakthrough in the field of materials, high entropy alloys 
(HEAs) have attracted extensive attention during the past few years 
[ 1 – 3 ]. The traditional alloys usually take one or two elements as the 
main elements, but HEAs contain over four principle elements of 
roughly equal proportion [ 4 ]. There are four core effects in HEAs, 
including high entropy, severe lattice distortion, sluggish diffusion and 
cocktail effect [ 5 – 8 ]. HEAs are considered as a new generation of alloy 
materials with broad application prospects because of their excellent 
properties, such as high strength and toughness, good corrosion and 
wear resistance, outstanding radiation and fatigue resistance [ 9 – 12 ]. In 
order to promote their application in the field of structural materials, 
researchers make great efforts in achieving the strength-ductility 
trade-off [ 13 ]. A number of methods have been carried out to balance 
the strength and toughness of HEAs, including inducing transformation 
-induced plasticity [ 14 ], adopting hierarchically-structural heterogene -
ity [ 15 ], producing ordered oxygen complexes [ 16 ], and generating 
coherent nano-lamellar alloys [ 17 ], while the achievement was far from 
satisfied. 
The equiatomic CrMnFeCoNi HEA with a face centered cubic (FCC) 
structure was firstly reported by Cantor et al. and has attracted 
numerous interest due to their excellent ductility and fracture toughness 
at room and cryogenic temperature [ 18 ]. The major difference between 
CrMnFeCoNi HEA and conventional materials is that the strength and 
ductility of the material increase significantly with the decrease of 
temperature because of the formation of deformation-induced nano -
twins at low temperatures [ 19 ]. In addition, the CrMnFeCoNi HEA has 
good thermal stability and ductility at high temperature [ 20 ]. Never -
theless, the high temperature tensile strength of the alloy is poor,

There are several problems during the casting process of CrMnFe -
CoNi HEA, such as coarse dendrite structure, component segregation 
and shrinkage cavity, which seriously affect the mechanical properties 
of the cast alloys. In order to solve these problems, many thermo -
mechanical processing methods were applied, including annealing, 
forging and rolling. These methods are cost-effective and does not pro -
duce significant strain hardening [ 24 ]. Kuznetsov et al. [ 25 ] investi -
gated the tensile properties of AlCuCrFeNiCo HEA in both as-cast and 
forged states, and results suggested that the forged alloy showed su -
perplastic behavior in the temperature range of 1073 – 1273 K. 
Basically, mechanical components experience material removal 
processing, such as cutting, milling, and grinding et al., where they are 
subjected to thermal-mechanical coupling that changes their metallo -
graphic and mechanical properties, thus further affecting the machining 
process and produced surface quality. Research efforts mainly focused 
on improving the properties of CoCrFeMnNi HEAs at high temperature 
by alloying design and thermo-mechanical treatment up to the present. 
The deformation mechanism and mechanical behavior of materials at 
high temperature are not clear. Therefore, it is particularly important to 
reveal the deformation mechanism of materials at high temperature to 
guide the machining process. In this paper, high temperature tensile 
properties of as-cast and forged CrMnFeCoNi HEAs were investigated. 
The evolution of deformation mechanism with the increase of temper -
ature was studied. The obtained results can be used to establish 
processing-microstructure-properties relationships in CrMnFeCoNi 
HEAs, which may benefit the subsequent processing.

Basically, the serration mechanism can be attributed to Portevin-Le 
Chatelier (PLC) effect. The PLC effect refers to the plastic instability of 
materials under certain conditions, such as temperature, strain rate and 
strain. This effect is characterized by repetitive yielding on the stress- 
strain curve. For conventional alloys, PLC effect is generally caused by 
dynamic strain aging, which means the interaction between solute 
atoms and movable displacements [ 40 , 41 ]. Solute atoms fix and unpin 
the dislocations by using the lattice or dislocation pipes diffusion [ 42 ]. 
However, the mechanism cannot be used to explain the observations in 
the CrMnFeCoNi HEAs. For HEAs, every constituent element can serve 
as solute atoms and has the effect of sluggish diffusion. Therefore, a new 
mechanism was introduced, which suggests that solute atoms do not 
need to propagate over long distances, but only need to locally rearrange 
around the dislocation to capture the moving dislocation [ 43 ]. For the 
CrMnFeCoNi HEA, the serration features vary with the change of tem -
perature and strain. At room temperature, the solute atoms are difficult 
to overcome the energy barriers and rearrange itself to lock the mobile 
dislocations, so there is no serration. With the increase of temperature, 
atoms vibrate more and more rapidly that results in a higher probability 
of overcoming the energy barriers. By rearranging the atoms and locking 
the mobile dislocations, the serration appears. In addition, dislocations 
are pinned and unpinned more frequently at higher temperature, as 
shown in Fig. 7 (a). However, as the temperature continues to rise, the 
serration no longer occurs because the thermal vibration of the atoms is 
too great to effectively lock the dislocation.

Grain boundary sliding is activated at high temperature, leading to 
dislocation accumulation and local strain accumulation at grain 
boundary regions [ 46 , 47 ]. The cracks tend to initiate and propagate in 
the grain boundary regions with high dislocation density, leading to 
intergranular fracture [ 48 , 49 ].

4. Conclusion 
In this study, the high temperature tensile properties of as-cast and 
wrought CrMnFeCoNi HEAs were investigated. The major findings can 
be summarized by the following points: 
(1) CrMnFeCoNi HEAs before and after hot forging both exhibit a 
single FCC solid solution phase structure. Average grain size 
refined significantly from ~92.6 μ m to ~18.5 μ m. Abundant sub 
grains and annealing twins formed in the hot forging process, 
LAGBs increased by ~69% and Σ 3-type grain boundaries 
increased to ~40%. 
(2) The PLC effect is found between 350 
◦
C and 650 
◦
C for as-cast and 
from 350 
◦
C to 500 
◦
C for as-forged HEAs, respectively. The 
serration features vary from type-A to type C with the increase of 
temperature and strain. 
(3) An outstanding strength has been achieved at every range of 
temperature for as-forged samples. Especially at 650 
◦
C, the ul -
timate tensile strength and yield strength of the as-forged alloy 
increased by 40% and 158%, respectively. 
(4) Hot forging reduces the ductile-brittle transition temperature of 
CrMnFeCoNi HEAs, and the tensile properties of the as-forged 
alloy are less affected by temperature than those of the as-cast 
alloy. 
(5) Different deformation mechanisms are characterized for as- 
forged HEAs during tensile tests at different temperatures. At 
room temperature, the deformation mechanism is mixed dislo -
cation slipping and twinning. With the increase of temperature, 
the dominant deformation mechanism is wavy slip and disloca -
tion cells. At 950 
◦
C, dynamic recrystallization occurs, which 
counteracts the strain hardening effect and leads to strain soft -
ening immediately after yielding. 
In conclusion, the as-forged CrMnFeCoNi HEAs possesses 
outstanding mechanical properties at room temperature and at high 
temperatures up to 800 
◦
C in comparison with as-cast HEAs. The 
experimental results indicate that fracture mode changes from ductile 
fracture to brittle fracture at intermediate temperatures. These results 
provide theoretical guidance in the temperature control of the 
machining process to achieve the best cutting performance.
