High-temperature mechanical properties of as-extruded ZK60 
W. Wu C.M. Hong L.J. Chen Y. Wang L. Yang F. L i Z. Liu 
School of Materials Science and Engineering, Shenyang University of Technology, 
Shenyang 110023, P.R. China 
Keywords: magnesium alloy, high-temperature mechanical properties, fracture, flow stress 
Abstract. High-temperature tensile and fracture behaviors of as-extruded ZK 60 alloy were 
investigated. It was evident from the experiments that with decr easing temperature and increasing 
strain rate, the 0.2% offset yield strength and ultimate tensil e strength of the alloy increased while 
the elongation to failure decreased. The flowing stress of as-ext ruded ZK60 alloy during plastic 
deformation was proportioned to the reciprocal of temperature. At the initial strain rate of 5×10 -4s-1, 
the calculated active energy at 300°C was about 93.4 kJ/mol. 
Introduction 
As magnesium alloys have low density, high specific strength and good machinability, they attract 
special attention of researchers working in aerospace, sports a nd electronic industries [1] . Especially 
the need of weight reduction in automobile industry, which can significant ly decrease fuel 
consumption and CO 2 emissions, makes them substantial material of steel and aluminum alloys [2,3]. 
Tensile deformation behaviors of magnesium alloys at room and elevated temperatures have been 
studied for several decades, and a great amount of research result s have been documented [4,5]. It is 
also found that some magnesium alloys with fine grains can achie ve superplasticity in special 
temperature or strain rate ranges [6] . For magnesium alloys with hexagonal structure, superplastici ty 
is of both important academic interest and considerable industrial pote ntial, because it provides 
them with capability of forming complex parts. 
In this paper, high-temperature mechanical properties and superplastic-like deformation behaviors 
of as-extruded ZK60 alloy were investigated. Moreover, tensile fract ure behavior of the alloy under 
different testing conditions was analyzed. 
Experimental procedures 
The ZK60 magnesium alloy, a kind of Mg-Zn-Zr alloy, was used in this investigation, whose 
chemical composition (in wt%) was Zn 6%, Zr 0.7%, Si <0.2%, Cu <0.1%, Ni <0.001%, Fe <0.005%, 
and balance Mg. The ZK60 alloy was received as cast ingot from Ningxia Huayuan Magnesium Co., 
China. The ingots were machined into cylindric bars with a diamete r of about 110 mm and then 
hot-extruded at 350 °C employing an extrusion ratio of 35:1. The grain size in the ext ruded 
condition was about 7.5 µ m. 
Tensile specimens with 5 mm gauge diameter and 25 mm gauge le ngth were machined from the 
extruded bars. All tensile tests were performed on a computer-cont rolled universal testing machine 
at an initial strain rate range of 10 -4~5 × 10 -3s-1 and different temperatures in laboratory air. The 
specimens were heated in an electrical resistance furnace and held for about 15 minutes after 
reaching the testing temperature in order to ensure a uniform tem perature distribution along the 
gauge section of the specimen. Fractographic observation was conducted on t he alloy to clarify the 
fracture process.

Thus, Q can be calculated through the Equation (3). At the strain ra te of 5 × 10 -4s-1, the calculated

of easy diffusion in this material. 
Conclusions 
Based on the study on high-temperature mechanical performances, fracture behavior and 
superplastic-like characteristics of as-extruded ZK60 alloy, the following conclusions can be 
obtained. 
(1) For as-extruded ZK60 alloy, the fracture elongation of about 195% can be attained at 300 °C 
and initial strain rates on the order of 10 -4s-1. Elongation usually increases with increasing 
testing temperature or decreasing strain rate. 
(2) The ultimate tensile strength and yield strength of as-extruded ZK60 alloy generally increase 
with the decrease of testing temperature and the increase of strain rate.
