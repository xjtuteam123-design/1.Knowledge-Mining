ABSTRACT

advanced brazing filler metals. However, for TiAl-based alloy joining, currently developed filler alloys still

1. Introduction
Instead of traditional Ni-based superalloys used as elevated tem -
perature structural materials, TiAl-based alloys are highly attractive for 
industrial applications such as compressor components, due to their 
favorable combination of low-density and excellent high-temperature 
performance, with long-term safety service temperature of 760 – 850 
◦
C 
[ 1 , 2 ]. However, the intrinsic brittleness of TiAl-based alloys signifi -
cantly limits their workability and weldability [ 3 ]. To overcome these 
limitations, the development of appropriate joining technologies is 
essential for fabrication of complex TiAl components.
Despite substantial advancements in various joining technologies, 
including alternative joining filler alloy [ 4 ], brazing method [ 5 ] and 
brazing parameters [ 6 ] as well as controllable brazing process [ 7 ], 
achieving a balance between joint strength and brazing temperature for 
joining TiAl alloy remains as one major challenge. In particular, there is 
still a significant untapped opportunity for the further improvement of 
high-temperature joint strength compared to the reported filler alloys. 
Currently, the employment of Ag-based filler alloys gave a low melting 
point, whereas they seemed to be confused by their high-cost and the 
excessive growth of Al-Cu-Ti brittle reaction layer within the brazed 
joints [ 8 ], inducing poor high-temperature joint strength and low 
application temperature. In terms of traditional Ti-based brazing filler 
alloys, their elevated temperature joint performance was still insuffi -
cient due to the presence of Al-Ni (Cu)-Ti (Zr) brittle phases at the joint 
interface. Notably, Ti-Ni fillers reported by Si et al. [ 9 ] and Ti-Ni-Nb

Since its conceptual inception, multi-principal element alloys 
(MPEA) have continuously evolved as a dynamic frontier in modern 
materials science, with representative systems including Co-Cr-Fe-Mn- 
Ni [ 18 , 19 ], Cr-Mn-Fe-Ni-Cu [ 20 – 22 ], Al-Co-Cr-Fe-Ni [ 23 ], 
Ti-Zr-Hf-Nb-Ta [ 24 ], Co-Cr-Ni-Al-Ti-Zr [ 25 ]. Among these, the devel -
opment of MPEA as brazing filler material has proven to be a feasible 
solution to address the joining challenge according to the work by Li 
et al. [ 26 ], due to its nature characteristics of unique flexibility in 
composition design and collaborative optimization of joint microstruc -
ture and strength. When employed as a brazing interlayer, the MPEA 
filler effectively suppresses the formation of Al-Ni-Ti brittle intermetallic 
compounds within the brazed joint. Additionally, MPEA fillers of 
Ni-Co-Fe-Cu-Si-B by Ren et al. [ 27 ], Ti-Zr-V-Nb-Cu-Ni-Mn by Feng et al. 
[ 28 ] and Co-Cu-Fe-Ni-Ti-V by Zhang et al. [ 29 ] as well as 
Ti-Cu-Ni-Nb-Al-Hf alloy systems by Li et al. [ 30 ] have been investigated 
for the joining of different materials, signifying the research tendency in 
the development of new brazing filler alloys. Although MPEA fillers 
significantly increase the compositional complexity of the brazed joint 
[ 31 ], these currently reported brazing fillers can not offer sufficient 
strength for TiAl joints, primarily due to the introduction of the 
depressant element Cu and the presence of multi-element brittle phases 
at the interface. Therefore, new MPEAs should be encouraged to fully 
exploit their potential in joint strength improvement.
Based on the metallurgical considerations of alloying element in -
fluence on mechanical properties of TiAl-based alloy, and the melting 
point of Ti-Mn-Fe ternary alloy and Ni-Co binary alloy, Ti-Mn-Fe-Ni-Co- 
Cr filler alloy was proposed in the present study. Firstly, the melting 
point of the Ti-Mn-Fe-Ni-Co alloy system was calculated by the addition 
of Ni-Co alloy with various concentrations for identifying the theoretical 
eutectic point. On the basis of this computationally derived quinary 
eutectic alloy, near-equivalent concentration addition of Cr was incor -
porated for further strengthening the joint.
The dynamic wettability behavior of the brazing filler alloy was 
investigated. The correlation between micro-and-nano structure and 
tensile strength of the brazed joint was established. Except for the 
general method to study joining mechanism by transmission electron 
microscopy (TEM) and geometric phase analysis (GPA), the partitioning 
behavior of alloying elements within predominant phases was analyzed 
by 3DAP.

2.1. Metallurgical basis and design principle of Ti-Mn-Fe-Ni-Co-Cr 
brazing filler alloy

means to strengthen materials. Five kinds of alloying elements

4.3. Fracture mechanism of the brazed joint
The joint fracture mechanism can be explained by the microstruc -
tural mismatch between the brazing seam and the base alloy. The brazed 
joints shared a characteristic of dual-phase structure, with γ -TiAl phase 
of 33 – 47 vol% disorderedly distributed within B2 matrix, resulting in a 
distinct deviation from the microstructure of the parent alloy. Due to the 
crystallographic discrepancies between B2 and γ -TiAl phases, a hetero -
geneous interface formed at the brazing seam/base alloy interface. This 
structural mismatch was intensified under the external loading, causing 
the breakage along the joint interface at both room and high 
temperatures.
Taking the joint tested at 850 
◦
C as an example, crack propagation at 
the B2/ γ -TiAl interface within the brazing seam exhibits significant 
complexity and variability, characterized by crack deflection, bridging, 
and arrest. At the same time, the γ -TiAl within the brazing seam displays 
fine tear ridges. These findings should be favorable to enhancing frac -
ture energy and improving joint strength. Although the crack deflection 
can promote intergranular microcracking along the B2/ γ -TiAl interface, 
in certain cases it may induce transgranular microcracking within a 
specific phase. During loading tests, such microcracks can be bridged by 
shorter cracks, ultimately leading to joint failure governed primarily by 
transgranular fracture.
