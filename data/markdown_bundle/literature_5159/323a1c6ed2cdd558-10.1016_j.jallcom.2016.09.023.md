abstract
Quaternary Ni-Ti-Hf-Zr high temperature shape memory alloys containing different Zr content (0
e15 at.%) were prepared by arc melting and suction casting. The addition of Zr led to change in lattice
parameters and monoclinic angle of B19 0 martensite phase. The phase transformation temperature as
well as yield strength of the alloys gradually increased with increasing the Zr content. Moreover, the
alloys exhibited signi ﬁcant changes in phase transition and deformation behaviors. With the addition of
Zr up to 7 at.%, the alloys exhibited double yielding and two-step phase transition behaviors. In contrast,
alloys containing 10 and 15 at.%Zr revealed single yielding and one-step phase transition behaviors.
These distinctions on phase transition and yielding behaviors of the alloys were mainly related to the
structural evolution related to twinning mode in martensite phase of as-cast alloys originated from the
change of lattice distortion during martensitic transformation by increasing of Zr content. Through
detailed investigations on the microstructural features, thermal characteristics, mechanical properties
and structural evolution on deformation of the alloys, the in ﬂuence of Zr content on phase formation,
phase transition and deformation behavior of Ni-Ti-Hf-Zr high temperature shape memory alloys was
elucidated.

1. Introduction
NiTi shape memory alloys (SMAs) have been exhibited a
remarkable attractiveness from its shape memory effect (SME) as
well as superelasticity. The SME is originated from a diffusionless
transformation of B2 austenite phase to the B19 0 martensite phase
with change in temperature [1,2]. Another feature of SMAs is the
pseudoelasticity at certain temperatures and stresses by aging,
which is mainly related to the SME [3,4]. However, the NiTi SMAs
can be used only below service temperature of 373 K. This relatively
low transformation temperature of the NiTi SMAs limits use of
these alloys to application close to ambient condition. The devel-
opment of SMAs with higher transformation temperature is
strongly required for a broad range of application in aerospace,
automotive, biomedical, oil and gas industries and activating sen-
sors in ﬁre prevention devices. In order to increase the trans-
formation temperature of SMAs, some ternary alloy systems have
been developed with the addition of ternary alloying elements. The
additions of Cu and Mn have relatively little effect on increasing
transformation temperature of NiTi SMAs, however, these lead the
change of martensite phase from monoclinic to orthorhombic [5,6].
Other alloying elements addition, such as Hf, Au, Pd and Pt, have
been found to highly increase the transformation temperature
above 423 K ratable to amount of additional elements [7,8]. These
alloying elements have important roles to play in terms of

(HTSMAs), NiTiPd, NiTiPt and NiTiAu alloys are prohibitively
expensive for common use, whereas the NiTiHf alloy has been
regarded as a practical engineering application due to its lower
coasts. However, these NiTiHf HTSMAs have still awaiting solution
from relatively easy plastic deformation due to their low yield
strength at the room temperature [16e18]. It is well known that
cold rolling, thermo-mechanical processing, precipitate hardening,
solid solution hardening and grain re ﬁnement of polycrystalline
alloys are effective methods to improve the mechanical properties
of the SMAs [14,16,19e24]. The cold working method leads to
increasing the stress required for slip, which improves the strength
of the SMAs [16]. However, the most useful HTSMAs are ordered
intermetallic phase with limited plasticity at low and intermediate
temperatures, which make it dif ﬁcult to apply cold rolling method
to increase the strength. The precipitate hardening is a more
feasible method to improve strength of the HTSMAs [25]. The
precipitates embedded in matrix phase can act as obstacles in the
path of dislocations, which must then either cut through the pre-
cipitate or bypass it to proceed [26].
Recently, quaternary alloy systems have been reported, which
represent highly improved overall behavior compared with ternary
NiTiHf HTSMAs [27e33]. The addition of Pd has resulted in a very
high strength alloy that has high damping capacity of 35 J/cm
3 [29].
Moreover, the NiTiHfPd HTSMAs exhibit perfect superelastic
behavior with recoverable strain of 4.2% at the compressive stress
level as high as 2.5 GPa, also showing brittle manner in compres-
sion due to their limited plasticity [31,32]. This low workability is
one of the main problems for practical use. In more recently years,
other quaternary NiTiHfNb systems have been developed, which
represent large improved the cold workability by formation of a
soft Nb-rich b phase, although the transformation temperature in
thermal cycling under stress was decreased [33]. These previous
reports imply that the addition of alloying elements in ternary Ni-
Ti-Hf systems can improve the shape memory and mechanical
properties of the HTSMAs. Therefore, it is important to develop the
quaternary HTSMAs with other alloying elements to improve
overall properties.
In this study, we chose the ternary Ni50Ti38Hf12 alloy with low Hf
content as a starting material and Zr was selected as a fourth
alloying element due to its perfect solubility and similar electron
conﬁguration in the outer shells to Ti and Hf. The quaternary
NiTiHfZr alloys exhibited a gradual increase of transformation
temperature with the change of transformation path on heating
and cooling. Furthermore, the alloys revealed different yielding
behavior during deformation as Zr content. Here, we report the
inﬂuence of Zr content on phase formation, phase transition and
mechanical behavior of NiTiHfZr HTSMAs. In addition, the corre-
lation between structural characteristics and double/single yielding
behavior of NiTiHfZr HTSMAs is also discussed via combinatorial
investigations using systematic microstructural observations and
interrupting mechanical tests with different degree of deformation.
2. Material and methods
Ni50Ti38-xHf12Zrx alloy ingots with x ¼ 0, 1, 3, 5, 7, 10 and 15 at.%
were prepared by arc-melting with high purity elements under an
Ar atmosphere. Ingots were remelted at least 5 times to achieve
chemical homogeneity. As-cast samples were produced by suction
casting into a cylindrical rod-shape Cu mold with diameters of
2 mm and 50 mm in length. In order to analyze the phase and
structure of cast rod samples, X-ray diffraction (XRD; Rigaku-D/
MAX-2500/PC) with CuK a radiation and transmission election
microscope (TEM; Technai F20) were used. Thin ﬁlm specimens
were prepared by ion milling with liquid nitrogen cooling. Trans-
formation temperatures and phase transition characteristics of as-
cast samples were studied using differential scanning calorimeter
(DSC; Perkin-Elmer DSC8500) with heating and cooling rates of
0.67Ks/C0 1 and thermal mechanical analyzer (TMA; Perkin-Elmer
Diamond TMA) with heating rate of 0.33Ks /C0 1 under applied force
of 1.2 N. The cylindrical samples with 2:1 aspect ratio for
compression tests were prepared. Room temperature mechanical
properties were measured under compressive mode at a strain rate
of 1 /C2 10/C0 3s/C0 1. Nano-indentation experiments were conducted to
measure the hardness of as-cast samples in the load-control mode
with maximum load of 25 mN at a constant loading/unloading rate
of 3.3 mN/s using a Nano-indentation tester (CSM NHT-X). The
deformed samples with different degree of deformation were
prepared under compressive mode at a strain rate of 1 /C2 10/C0 3s/C0 1.
Structural characteristics of deformed samples were investigated
using TEM.
3. Results and discussion
3.1. Phase formation and phase transition behavior of as-cast
Ni50Ti38-xHf12Zrx alloys
Crystal structures of the as-cast NiTiHfZr alloys with different Zr
content were determined from XRD traces shown in Fig. 1(a). All
alloys exhibit monoclinic diffraction patterns of the B190 martensite
phase with no other crystalline peaks and positions of diffraction
peaks slightly shift with increase of Zr content. Lattice parameters
a, b, c and a monoclinic angle b of the alloys are calculated from the
XRD results in Fig. 1(a), which are plotted as a function of Zr content
in Fig. 1(b). Similar to the previously reported the effect of Hf on
binary NiTi system [34], Zr addition by substituting for Ti results in
an increase of a, c, b and a slightly decrease of b due to the atomic
radius difference between Zr and Ti. This result reveals that the
addition of Zr content up to 15 at.% in Ni 50Ti38-xHf12Zrx HTSMAs
leads to slightly change the lattice parameters of the monoclinic
structure of B190 martensite phase and causes no formation of any
other intermetallic phase.
To determine the in ﬂuence of Zr content on transformation
temperature and phase transition behavior, DSC tests were con-
ducted ranging from 300 to 950 K, as shown in Fig 2. Fig. 2(a) shows
the DSC curves of as-cast alloys containing 0, 3, 7, 10 and 15 at.%Zr
obtained from continuous heating method. All alloys reveal obvious
endothermic peaks corresponding to phase transformation from
B190 martensite to B2 austenite phase. The austenite start tem-
perature signiﬁcantly increases from 380 to 830 K with increasing
Zr content up to 15 at.%. For the alloys containing Zr up to 7 at.%,
DSC curves show two endothermic peaks representing double
transformation paths, whereas the alloys containing 10 and 15 at.%
Zr exhibit only one endothermic peaks indicating single trans-
formation path. In order to investigate the reversible phase trans-
formation behavior of the Ni 50Ti38-xHf12Zrx alloys, the DSC
responses are obtained during heating and cooling for 3 cycles, as
shown in Fig. 2 (b). The each alloy does not show signi ﬁcant dif-
ference on phase transformation temperatures and phase transi-
tion paths during 3 cycles. Zr free alloy also shows typical reversible
phase transformation behavior of HTSMAs on heating and cooling,
where the martensitic phase transformation from B19
0 martensite
to B2 austenite occurs in a two-stage process. The ﬁrst stage is
attributed to the B19 0 martensite / R-phase and the second stage
is for the R-phase / B2 austenite transformation [35]. The R-phase
transformation in NiTiHf HTSMA has been reported where the Hf
content is less than 16 at.% [36]. On the other hand, the alloy with
10 at.%Zr exhibits reversible phase transformation behavior

3.2. Room temperature mechanical properties and yielding
behaviors of as-cast Ni 50Ti38-xHf12Zrx alloys
The mechanical properties of as-cast Ni 50Ti38-xHf12Zrx alloys
with x ¼ 0, 3, 5, 7, 10, 15 at.% were determined by room temperature
compression and nano-indentation tests. The ﬁrst and second yield
strength, maximum compressive strength, plasticity and indenta-
tion hardness are summarized in Table 1 . Fig. 4 (a) shows the
compressive stress-strain curves of the as-cast Ni 50Ti38-xHf12Zrx
alloys with x ¼ 0, 3, 5, 7, 10, 15 at.%. The Zr free alloy exhibits the
compressive yield and fracture strength of ~638 MPa and
~2930 MPa, together with plastic strain of ~30.8%. Clearly, the
addition of Zr up to 5 at.% in the Ni 50Ti38-xHf12Zrx alloys improves
the compressive yield strength (~750 MPa) without signi ﬁcant
degradation of fracture strength (~2900 MPa) and plasticity
(~30.2%) as compared with Zr free alloy. For the alloys with 10 and
15 at.%Zr, the yield strength drastically increases to ~1 170 MPa,
whereas the fracture strength and plasticity suddenly decline to
~2370 MPa and ~10.9%, respectively, even though the microstruc-
ture of these alloys is similar to alloys containing 0 e5 at.%Zr. To
determine the hardening effect by Zr addition on these alloys,
nano-indentation was employed and the hardness of martensite
phases on each alloy was evaluated. Fig. 4(b) plots the typical load-
displacement curves of as-cast alloys containing 0, 3, 5, 7, 10 and
15 at.%Zr. With the increase of Zr content, the maximum and
remnant indentation depths are steadily decreased. This indicates
that the hardness of the martensite phases on each alloy consid-
erably depends on Zr content. Fig. 4 (c) shows the HIT and
compressive yield strength as a function of Zr content. A hardening
tendency of the martensite phase as Zr content is closely parallel to
that of yield strength of each alloy. By comparing the HIT and yield
strength, it is believed that the strengthening of the alloys is a result
of solid solution hardening of the martensite phase by substitution
of Zr for Ti [54]. Moreover, the drastic increase of yield strength and
hardness on alloys containing 10 and 15 at.%Zr is mainly related to
introducing additional very ﬁne internal twins in martensite vari-
ants as shown in Fig. 5(g)-(j). The atomic scale twin can improve the
strength of the alloys [56]. On the one hand, the alloys with
0e7 at.%Zr and alloys with 10 e15 at.%Zr reveal the signi ﬁcant dif-
ference on plastic deformation behavior, which is mainly related to
their yielding behavior. A typical double yielding behavior is
observed with the presence of two in ﬂection points, indicating ﬁrst
(marked by black arrows) and second (marked by red arrows)
yielding, from S-S curves of alloys containing 0, 3, 5 and 7 at.%Zr in
Fig. 4(a). On the other hand, the alloys containing 10 and 15 at.%Zr
show only one in ﬂection point (marked by black arrows) corre-
sponding to single yielding. The yielding behavior of the shape
memory alloys depends on the structural evolution of martensite
phase during deformation [40]. The correlation between structural
evolution of martensite phase and yielding behavior on the alloys
will be described in more detail through combinatorial in-
vestigations by interrupted compression tests and TEM observation
on each deformed sample in Section 3.3.

which can lead to change lattice distortion of the B19 0 martensite
phase. Accordingly, it is believed that this evolution on B190 unit cell
by Zr addition can introduce the (001) twin compound in
martensite variants.

less slip systems, which contributes to occurring deformation
twinning that occurs prior to dislocation slip because it is dif ﬁcult
to activate the slip systems in monoclinic systems [51]. Moreover,
deformation twinning is also related with the change of orientation
of lattice. When the elastic interactions arise between or inside
each martensite variant during deformation, it leads to the intro-
duction of (001) compound twins by rearrangement of martensite
plates in order to reduce the elastic strain [50]. This suggests that
the martensite plates on Zr free alloy have started to be rearranged
to the one including internal (001) compound twins while main-
taining <011> type-II twins upon ﬁrst yielding. For the second
yielded sample, the HPVs between neighboring martensite plates
indicating <011>
B190 type-II twins are still maintained, and some
martensite plates become narrower as shown in BF image in
Fig. 7(f). Moreover, the ﬁne HPVs in martensite plates observed on
ﬁrst yielded sample [see Fig. 7 (a)] disappear and dislocations
appear in some martensite plates on the second yielding stage. The
HRTEM image in Fig. 7 (g) obtained from dotted yellow rectangle
area in Fig. 7 (f) also shows only two parallel twin boundaries
indicated by dotted yellow lines corresponding to (01 1) twin
boundaries. Moreover, the FFT patterns in Fig. 7 (h)-(j) obtained
from i), ii) and iii) areas in Fig. 7 (g) represent one type twin mode
corresponding to <011>B190 type-II twins along the [1 1 1]M,[ 1 1 1 ]M//

4. Conclusions
In the present work, we studied the in ﬂuence of Zr addition on
phase formation, phase transition and mechanical behavior of
quaternary Ni 50Ti38-xHf12Zrx (x ¼ 0e15 at.%Zr) HTSMAs and also
investigated the correlation between structural evolution and
double/single yielding behavior of the alloys via combinatorial in-
vestigations using systematic TEM observations and interrupting
mechanical tests. The following important conclusions can be
drawn.
(1) The addition of Zr by substituting for Ti results in an increase
of lattice parameter a, c and monoclinic angle b without
change of the monoclinic structure of B19 0 martensite phase.
This can in ﬂuence to change of lattice distortion of B19 0
martensite phase.
(2) All alloys exhibit reversible martensitic transformation be-
haviors, and the austenite start temperature signi ﬁcantly
increases from 380 to 830 K with increasing Zr content up to
15 at.%. The phase transition behavior of the alloys also de-
pends on Zr content. The alloys containing 0e7 at.%Zr exhibit
double phase transition path (B19' 4 R 4 B2), whereas the
alloys containing 10, 15 at.%Zr reveals single phase transition
path (B19' 4 B2). These distinctions on transformation
process of the alloys are a result of the change of trans-
formation strain associated with lattice distortion during
martensitic transformation by Zr addition.
(3) Compressive yield strength gradually increases from ~640
to ~ 750 MPa by addition of Zr up to 5 at.% without signi ﬁcant
degradation of fracture strength and plasticity. For the alloys
with 10 and 15 at.%Zr, the yield strength drastically increases
to ~1 170 MPa, whereas the fracture strength and plasticity
suddenly decline to ~2370 MPa and ~10.9%, respectively. The
strengthening of the alloys is a result of the solid solution
hardening effect by Zr addition as well as formation of newly
atomic-scale internal twins inside martensite plates in alloy
containing 10 and 15 at.%Zr. The large difference in the
plasticity of the alloys is mainly related to their double/single
yielding behavior.
(4) The Zr free alloy consisting of the martensite plates with
<011>B190 type-II twins undergoes double yielding during
deformation. In the ﬁrst yielding stage, the internal (001)
compound twins regarded as deformation twins are formed
in martensite plates by inelastically rearrangement. In the
second yielding stage, internal (001) compound twins
disappear by de-twinning and the lattice dislocations are
generated. On the other hand, the as-cast alloy containing
10 at.%Zr composed of martensite plates with <011>
B190
type-II twins and very ﬁne internal (001) compound twins
exhibits single yielding behavior during deformation. The
very ﬁne internal (001) compound twins in the martensite
plates are de-twinned by reorientation of martensite plates
on yielding stage. Thereafter, plastic deformation occurs by
lattice dislocation without second yielding.
(5) The structural evolutions of as-cast alloys are mainly attrib-
utable to the change of lattice distortion during martensitic
transformation by increasing of Zr content, which is the key
to decide yielding behavior of the quaternary Ni-Ti-Hf-Zr
HTSMAs during compression.
