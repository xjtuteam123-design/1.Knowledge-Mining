Abstract. A brief review of recent research and development works of some advanced Ti alloys and Ni base 
superalloys in the Institute of Aeronantical Materials, Beijing (BIAM) is reported. In which, the tensile deformation 
and fracture characteristics in an ot-/~ Ti alloy, the creep behavior of Ti3A1 intermetallic alloy and effect of heat 
treatment and crystal orientation on the creep properties of a single crystal Ni base alloys DD3, are presented. The 
applications of the above mentioned alloys in aeronautic industry are described. 
1. Introduction 
It is well known that the materials, information and energy are the three major supporters of 
the modern civilization. The development of new materials not only serves as a substantial 
basis, but also plays an important pioneer roll for the progress of national science and 
technology. In the past decades, the advance of aircraft and space vehicle encourages the 
materials scientists and engineers to explore high performance materials with light weight, 
superior heat resistant properties and high degree of reliability. 
The Institute of Aeronautical Materials was founded in 1956, and now become one of 
the largest materials research and development centers in China. The chief missions of 
the Institute are to develop metallic and non-metallic materials, processing technology and 
testing techniques for aircraft and jet-engines. Over 700 scientific and technical items and 
projects have been accomplished and rewarded by the State and Ministry of Aeronautics 
and Astronautics. An overall view on the light metals research, development and commer- 
cialization at BIAM was introduced in Light Metals Ages by F.H. Froes [1]. In the present 
paper, some recent research and development works of high performance titanium alloys 
and nickel base superalloys for gas turbines as several typical examples are presented.

3. Creep rupture behavior of Ti3AI based alloy 
The intermetallic compound Ti3Al,owing to its superior strength-to-density ratio at elevated 
temperature, has been considering as one of most promising candidate material for aircraft 
jet-engines [4--6]. The material used in this was an super or2 alloy (Ti-A1-Nb-Mo-V). The 
specimens were prepared from a special double vacuum melting techniques, forged in 
alpha-2 + beta field and heat treated in around the beta transus to get different type of 
microstructures, as shown in Fig. 5. 
It was found that Widmanstatten structure emerges the most attractive creep fracture 
properties, where its stress-to rupture life lasts about 100-132 h under the loading condition 
of 700~ MPa with a total elongation of about 8-11%. The basket-weave structure 
also exhibits much better creep rupture properties, comparable to that of Widmanstatten 
structure. The creep rupture properties of duplex structure is not as good as expected. The 
creep rupture life at 700~ under 276 MPa is about 80 h. The creep rupture properties of 
equiaxed structure is the lowest.

studied under SEM and TEM. It is noticed that the creep cracks initiate at the free- or 
sub-surfaces of specimens for all four types of microstructures. However, the propagation

4. Influence of heat treatment and crystal orientation on creep rupture properties of 
A SC DD3 Ni-base superalloy 
The improvement of the performance of aircraft jet-engines demands higher operating 
temperature materials used for turbine blades and other high temperature structures and 
components. During past decades, nickel-base single crystal superalloys were developed 
for high performance jet-engines [7]. However, most nickel-base single crystal superalloys 
generally contain some rare and valuable elements, such as Ta and Re. From the economic 
point of view, a nickel-base single crystal superalloy, DD3, without Ta and Re was developed 
in BIAM [8, 9]. 
The chemical composition of DD3 alloy used in this investigation is 0.01C-9.5Cr-5.0Co- 
5.2W-4.2Mo-5.8AI-2.3Ti-NiBal. The casting billets were produced in an ISP2/3-DS vac- 
uum induction furnace using screw crystal selector and high rate solidification technique. 
These billets were solution treated at the temperature range of 1150 ~176 for a period 
of four hours and then aged at 870~ for 32 hours.

1. Effect of solution temperature on microstructure and mechanical properties 
Some typical microstructures of DD3 alloy under various solution temperatures are illus- 
trated in Fig. 9. It was observed that in the case of solution temperatures of 1190~ or 
lower, only a small part of as cast phase dissolved and the fine y' phase particles precip- 
itated during air cooling and aging (Fig. 9a, b). When the solution temperature increased 
to 1230~ only a small part of as-cast y' phase remained, as shown in Fig. 9c, and the 
cubic y particles precipitated during air cooling and aging, as shown in Fig. 12d. When 
the solution temperature reached 1250~ the as-cast y' phase completely dissolved, y-y' 
eutectic disappeared (Fig. 9e, f) and severe dentritic segreation was greatly relieved. When 
the solution temperature increased to 1250~ or higher, subgrain boundaries appeared and 
became more evident and wide with increasing the solution temperature (Fig. 9g). It has 
been found that the homogeneous and smallest y' particle size appeared in the specimens 
solution treated near 1250~ 
The effect of solution temperature on the stress-rupture life of DD3 alloy at 760~ 
and 980~ is shown in Fig. 10. The stress-rupture life increased with increasing solution 
temperature up to 1250~ and then decreased with further increasing the solution temper- 
ature. The improvement of stress-rupture life of DD3 alloy by heat treatment was quite 
evident. For example, the stress-rupture lives of the specimens (after 1250~ h, AC + 
870~ h, AC) under 785 MPa at 760~ and 221 MPa at 900~ are three times longer 
than that of as-cast DD3 alloy. The longest stress-rupture life of the specimens heat treated 
at 1250~ h, AC + 870~ h, AC, may be attributed to the optimum microstructure, 
i.e. the elimination of y-y' eutectic, the reduction of dendritic segregation and the smallest 
size of y' particle~ homogeneously distributed in y' matrix compared with another solution 
treatments.

The typical creep curves of DD3 alloy are shown in Fig. 11. It can be seen that the 
strains of primary creep of DD3 alloy are very small and an incubation period with very low 
creep rate appears at the testing condition 960~ MPa. Thus the creep rupture lives 
of DD3 single crystal alloy were significantly longer than those of equiaxed and columnar 
superalloys with similar compositions. The high resistance to high temperature creep may 
be ascribed to the following reasons: (a) higher volume fraction of y' precipitate (--,63%) 
due to high contents of yt phase forming elements, A1, Ti and Mo; (b) the relative low lattice 
misfit between y and y' phases (--~0.196%) and the entering of high melting point elements, 
W and Mo, into y' phase, making y' precipitates more stable during high temperature 
creep; (c) the absence of grain boundary strengthening elements, such as B, Zr, Ce and Hf, 
leading to high melting point of present alloy and making the high temperature solution 
treatment possible i.e. 1230-1250~ which can eliminate the weak area of y/yt eutectic 
and homogenize the distribution of alloying elements; (d) under proper heat treatment, the 
homogeneously distributed cube shaped fine yt phase of about 0.3/zm precipitate, which 
provide high resistance to creep deformation; (e) the formation of the raft structure of the 
specimens solution treated in the temperature range of 1230-1250~ during the early stage 
of creep, which is normal to the applied stress axis, would effectively inhibit or retard the 
dislocation motion [8].

5. Concluding remarks 
In this paper, some recent developments of investigations on microstructures and mechanical 
behaviors of some high performance materials, titanium alloys, nickel-base alloys and 
intermetallic Ti3A1 and Ni3A1 base alloys, are described. This report includes only a part of 
research and development works conducted in the Titanium Laboratory and the cast Ni-base 
Superalloy Laboratory of BIAM. However, it does reflect the present status and activities 
in the R&D of high temperature materials and their engineering application for advanced 
aircraft jet-engines during the past ten years. 
In order to fulfill the necessity of the advanced aircraft and space vehicles to ensure a high 
degree of reliability and longer service life, a number of high temperature materials have 
been developed in past decades. However, the nature, physical courses and micromechanism 
of high temperature behavior of these materials under service conditions have not been fully 
understood. A great deal of works remains to be done in material characterization, design 
criterion, processing parameter and quality controls during the engineering application and 
commercialization of these materials.
