ABSTRACT 
Intermediate temperature embrittlement (ITE) is very common in Ni and Ni – Co based superalloys, which seri -
ously restricts the application of these alloys. In this work, we developed a new Ni – Co based superalloy by 
increasing the content of Cr to eliminate ITE. The superalloy has a yield strength of ~950 MPa in the inter -
mediate temperature range (600 – 780 
◦
C), while also maintain a tensile elongation of ~15 %. Microstructural 
investigations showed that in the temperature range of 600 – 780 
◦
C, the microstructure of the alloy consists of 
nano-sized L1
2 
phase ( γ ′ ) distributing inside the γ matrix, and the deformation is dominated by anti-phase 
boundary (APB) shearing γ ′ precipitates, which makes the yield strength of the alloy decrease only slightly at 
600 – 780 
◦
C (compared to that tested at room temperature). More importantly, the element Cr is enriched along 
the grain boundaries (GBs) to form a protective layer without the formation of brittle topologically close-packed 
(TCP) phases due to the inverse two-stage aging design. In addition, the formation of a discontinuous precipi -
tation (DP) zone at the GBs can act as a stress buffer and introduce GB serrations to withstand intergranular 
cracking. 
1. Introduction 
Ni and Ni – Co based superalloys are widely used in aerospace, 
petrochemical, transportation and electrical fields [ 1 – 6 ]. However, a lot 
of investigations have shown that intermediate temperature embrittle -
ment (ITE) often exists in the temperature range from 500 to 900 
◦
C in Ni 
and Ni – Co based superalloys, with the maximum tensile elongation in 
this temperature range being only about 50% of the maximum tensile 
elongation in the low or high temperatures range [ 7 – 16 ]. It has been 
shown that ITE is a common feature of Ni and Ni – Co based superalloys, 
independent of manufacturing routes and strengthening mechanisms 
[ 17 , 18 ]. The investigation of Rhines and Wray [ 19 ] showed that Ni and 
its alloys have the minimum tensile elongation of only 7% at a tem -
perature of 600 
◦
C. It should be pointed out that ITE was not only 
observed in Ni and Ni – Co based superalloys, but also observed in many 
other alloys, such as Fe and Co based superalloys, TiAl based interme -
tallic alloys and copper alloys [ 20 – 25 ]. Since the service temperatures of 
most superalloys often includes the temperature range of ITE, the 
appearance of ITE poses a serious threat to the reliability of Ni and 
Ni – Co based superalloys, and becomes an urgent problem to be solved. 
At present, the explanation of ITE is based on intergranular precip -
itation, grain boundary (GB) shear or slip, GB segregation, decohesion of 
glide plane, gas phase embrittlement and dynamic strain aging (DSA) 
[ 18 ]. Currently, inert atmosphere protection, adjusting the alloy 
composition to remove the brittle elements and increasing the solution 
temperature are the most common methods to eliminate ITE [ 21 , 26 , 27 ]. 
For polycrystalline superalloys, regardless of the thermal stress damage 
mechanism, the failure characteristics are often crystal discontinuity 
[ 28 , 29 ]. Therefore, when evaluating the engineering potential of poly -
crystalline Ni and Ni – Co based superalloys, special attention should be 
paid to the GB regions. Firstly, GBs can act as effective barriers for 
dislocation slip transfer [ 26 ]. Thus, refinement of the grains can effec -
tively improve the strength of polycrystalline materials, (known as 
Hall-Petch strengthening) [ 30 ]. Secondly, GBs are also a weak point and 
the preferred crack nucleation sites, especially at high temperatures 
[ 31 ]. In most alloys, the damage preferentially accumulates in the GB 
region, which is the reason for the earlier initiation of intergranular 
cracks and related embrittleness [ 32 , 33 ]. 
The inclusion of GB beneficial elements, such as B, Ce and Cr, in 
superalloys can improve the GB strength by increasing the GB binding

In this paper, we designed a new Ni – Co based superalloy with a high

2. Experimental 
A Ni – 20Co – 5(Al + Ti) – 5W – 16.5Cr-2.5Nb (wt%) superalloy was 
fabricated by a three-step melting process including vacuum induction 
melting, electro slag remelting and vacuum arc remelting, respectively, 
and the supplier of the required elements of the alloy is Central Iron and 
Steel Research Institute. The samples were then subjected to heat

3. Results 
Fig. 1 a shows the XRD pattern of the initial state of the new Ni – Co 
based superalloy. The peak of γ + γ ′ can be clearly observed. No other 
precipitates can be detected or the amount of the precipitates is too small 
to be effectively detected by XRD. The annealed structure of the Ni – Co 
based superalloy contains typically the secondary γ ′ structure, while the 
primary γ ′ phase is not commonly observed. As shown in Fig. 1 b, dense 
and evenly distributed secondary γ ′ precipitates (the white circular 
spots) can be observed in the grain interior. The secondary γ ′ has an 
average diameter of ~50 nm (see Fig. 1 c), and a volume fraction of ~46 
%.

specimens tested at RT, 600, 680, 730, 780 and 880 
◦
C. When the tensile 
temperature is below 880 
◦

decreases only slightly with increasing the temperature, from ~1115 
MPa at RT to ~995, ~957, ~950 and ~946 MPa at 600, 680, 730 and 
780 
◦
C, respectively. The decreasing trend in the ultimate tensile 
strength (UTS) is similar to that of YS. It should be noted that the alloy 
can maintain a high work hardening degree at both tensile temperatures 
of 600 and 680 
◦

reaches 780 
◦

◦

does not show obvious ITE. In addition, when the tensile temperature of 
the superalloy reaches 880 
◦

Fig. 3 shows the fracture morphologies of the alloy after tensile 
deformation at different temperatures. It can be observed that there are 
a large number of deep but small dimples at the fracture surface of the 
alloy after tensile deformation at RT (see Fig. 3 a). This fracture surface 
can be regarded as a typical fracture of micropore accumulation, and is 
generally classified as a ductile fracture. At the same time, transgranular 
fracture can be observed (see red arrows in Fig. 3 b). As the tensile 
temperature increases to 680 
◦

that some intergranular fracture can also be observed at 680

with the ductility shown in Fig. 2 . When the test temperature increases 
to 780 
◦

mechanism is still transgranular fracture (see Fig. 3 e and f). It can thus 
be concluded that ductile fracture dominates the fracture procedure of 
the alloy from RT to 780 
◦

In order to study the coarsening behavior of the precipitates at 
different tensile temperatures, isothermal heat-treatment experiments 
were carried out. Fig. 4 shows the microstructures of the superalloy after 
isothermal heat-treatment at the same temperatures to the tensile testing

this temperature range is mainly dominated by the uniform precipita -

At these aging temperatures, from 600 to 780

that no other precipitates, particularly the TCP phases, were detected 
even with the high amount of Cr, it is reasonable to infer that the regular 
γ / γ ′ structure in this superalloy has a very good thermal stability and 
therefore has the potential for long-term high-temperature service. At 
the same time, it can be seen that the original trace of the GB is quite 
straight (shown by the green dashed line in Fig. 6 b), while the current 
trace is very curved (shown by the blue dashed line). The area between 
the original GB position and the current GB position is often referred to 
as the discontinuous precipitation (DP) zone [ 47 ], and the width of the 
DP zone channel in this superalloy is about ~65 nm. The curvature of 
the current GB trace line is directly caused by the resistance of the 
irregular GB precipitates [ 48 ]. This resistance or nail effect of GB pre -
cipitation on GB migration, often referred to as Zener drag, improves the 
stability of GB when exposed to heat [ 49 , 50 ]. Finally, we can observe 
that during the high temperature deformation process, the Cr element is 
segregated and highly overlapped with the DP region. Since the oxida -
tion resistance of Cr can help resist the so-called environment-assisted 
cracking, while not forming other miscellaneous brittleness at the GBs 
[ 51 ], it can be considered to be one of the reasons for improving the 
ductility of the superalloy.

4.2. Ductility 
For Ni and Ni – Co based superalloys at intermediate temperatures, 
the environmental attack of hydrogen and oxygen has always been the 
Achilles heel, and this important mechanism is called stress-assisted 
grain boundary oxidation (SAGBO) [ 51 ]. Environmental impact is the 
root cause of ITE of Ni and Ni – Co based superalloys. Because of oxygen 
infiltration, the GB is nailed, and the GB cannot slide, which leads to the 
embrittlement of the alloys. In another interpretation, GBs are weakened 
by oxygen adsorption, and stress concentration before dislocation 
accumulation at GBs or other obstacles can easily trigger cracks, which 
ultimately leads to low ductility. For the improvement of the ductility of 
the superalloy designed in this study, we propose the following three 
possibilities. Firstly, intergranular embrittlement is the main cause of 
brittle fracture during tensile deformation of Ni and Ni – Co based su -
peralloys. Cr is a necessary element to effectively improve the oxidation 
and corrosion resistance of Ni and Ni – Co based superalloys [ 35 , 36 ]. As 
shown in Fig. 6 b, during the high temperature tensile process, oxygen 
will rapidly diffuse inward along the high-energy GBs, and Cr will be 
deflected at the GBs to form a protective layer of the GBs, which plays an

the addition of Cr has the potential to form harmful TCP. Fortunately, we 
did not find the occurrence of brittle TCP phases at the GBs in this su -
peralloy, which is related to the inverse two-stage aging design, using 
lower temperature aging treatment at the first aging to introduce a 
higher volume fraction of the γ ′ phase, and using a higher temperature 
aging treatment at the second aging stage [ 8 ]. The brittle TCP phases at 
GBs can be eliminated due to the high temperature of the second stage 
aging. This is because with the increase of the aging temperature, the 
concentration of TCP formation elements at the GBs will decrease, thus 
inhibiting the nucleation of brittle TCP phase [ 55 ]. Nowadays, two-stage 
aging were widely used to inhibit the formation of brittle phases at the 
GBs in γ ′ -strengthened HEAs, which can effectively recover the ductility 
of the alloys at intermediate temperature [ 8 , 26 ]. In addition, γ / γ ′ 
coherence confers good strain transfer across the interface, while the 
multi-slip system in the FCC-based crystals ensures that the dislocation is 
easily passed not only within the γ phase, but also within γ ′ phase. 
Therefore, the DP region can be used as a nanoscale buffer zone where 
dislocation accumulates near the GBs, thus averaging the local stress 
levels. 
Secondly, the activation energy of Cr in Ni based superalloy is 289 
kJ/mol, which is higher than 275 kJ/mol of Ti, 284 kJ/mol of Co and 
268 kJ/mol of Al [ 56 , 57 ]. From the perspective of diffusion, the larger 
activation energy is conducive to delaying the coarsening of the pre -
cipitates and maintaining the size of the γ ′ precipitates. At the same time, 
Cr is the γ matrix forming element, and the addition of a large amount of 
Cr will minimize the difference of γ / γ ′ lattice parameters and decrease 
lattice misfit, which is conducive to maintaining the spherical shape of 
the γ ′ phase [ 41 , 58 ], and this is consistent with the observed phenom -
enon in Fig. 4 . Compared to the micron-sized precipitates, the 
nano-sized γ ′ precipitates can be maintained during the high tempera -
ture tensile process, which can provide effective strengthening for su -
peralloys. At the same time, the uniform distribution of the high density 
nano-precipitates reduces the stress concentration, which can prevent 
the appearance of cracks at the interface between the nano-precipitates 
and the matrix, and improve the strength of the superalloy without 
losing the ductility.

5. Conclusions 
In this study, by increasing Cr content and using inverse two-stage 
aging design, we developed a new Ni – Co based superalloy. The results 
show that the new alloy has no brittle TCP phase in the GBs and can 
effectively eliminate the ITE. The deformation mechanism of the su -
peralloy in the intermediate temperature range, from 600 to 780 
◦
C, is 
dominated by APB shearing γ ′ precipitates, so this superalloy can still 
maintain a high yield strength of about 950 MPa at the tensile temper -
ature of 780 
◦
C, which is only about 15 % lower than the YS tested at RT. 
Moreover, the addition of a large amount of Cr element reduces the 
lattice misfit of γ / γ ′ interface and makes the γ ′ precipitates maintain the 
size and morphology during the intermediate temperature tensile pro -
cess. At the same time, Cr segregation at GBs can form a protective layer 
without the formation of brittle TCP phases. Finally, the formation of DP 
zone of the GBs not only acts as a stress buffer, but also introduces GB 
serrations to withstand intergranular cracking.
