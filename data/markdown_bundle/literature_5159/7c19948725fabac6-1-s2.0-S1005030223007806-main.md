Refractory high-entropy alloys have recently emerged as promising candidates for high-temperature 
structural applications. However, their performance is compromised by the trade-off required between 
strength and ductility. Here, a novel W30 Ta5 (FeNi)65 refractory high-entropy alloy with an outstanding 
combination of strength and plasticity at both room and elevated temperatures is designed, based on 
the multi-phase transitions design strategy. The alloy comprises a body-centered cubic dendrite phase, a 
topologically close-packed μ rhombohedral phase, and a high-density coherent nano-precipitate γ′′ phase 
with the D022 structure (Ni3 Ta type) embedded in a continuous face-centered cubic matrix. Owing to pre- 
cipitation strengthening of D022 , the yield stress of the alloy is determined as high as 1450 MPa, which 
is a signiﬁcant improvement ( ∼100%) in comparison with the D022 -free alloy, without a loss of ductil- 
ity. This alloy exhibits an excellent high-temperature strength, with the yield strengths of 1300 MPa at 
600 °C and 320 MPa at 1000 °C. Detailed microstructural characterization using transmission electron mi- 
croscopy, high-angle annular dark-ﬁeld imaging, and three-dimensional atom probe tomography analyses 
indicated that this superior strength–plasticity combination stems from the synergy of a multiple-phase 
structure. These results provide a new insight into the design of RHEAs and other advanced alloys.

1. Introduction 
As new metallic materials with multiple principal elements 
(present at equimolar or near-equimolar ratios), high-entropy al- 
loys (HEAs) have attracted surging attention in the past decade 
[ 1–13 ]. Owing to unique atomic packing with a high chemical dis- 
order based on a topologically ordered lattice, HEAs exhibit su- 
perior mechanical properties, such as excellent cryogenic fracture 
toughness [ 7 , 8 ], exceptional self-sharpening [9] , outstanding resis- 
tance to hydrogen embrittlement [ 10 , 11 ], impact resistance [ 14 , 15 ], 
and radiation resistance [16] , with possible excellent performances 
in overcoming the strength–ductility trade-off [17–19] . On account 
of the high demand for high-temperature structural applications, 
this novel alloy design concept has recently been extended to de- 
velop refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) or refractory complex

ricated as promising alternatives to Ni-based superalloys, includ- 
ing NbMoTaW, VNbMoTaW, TaHfZrNbTi, and their derived compo-

crystal structure, RHEAs can achieve higher strength than tradi- 
tional metallic alloys at elevated temperatures. With the exception 
of the class of TaHfZrNbTi alloys, most RHEAs exhibit a relatively 
poor compressive ductility at room temperature, which limits their 
engineering applications [20–26] . Thus, there is strong demand for 
developing a new strategy to design high-strength RHEAs without 
sacriﬁcing ductility. 
Several different strengthening factors, such as phase transfor- 
mation, dislocation density, grain size, precipitates, phase bound- 
aries, and solid solutions, generally play important roles in met- 
allurgical strengthening mechanisms [28–32] . Of these, precipita- 
tion has a signiﬁcant effect on strengthening because of its dis- 
location shearing hardening and the Orowan strengthening effect, 
all of which have extensive applications in enhancing Ni-based su- 
peralloys [33–38] . In the development of Ni-based alloys, nano-

(Ni3 Ta type) structures due to the ordering of Al and Ta, respec-

two precipitation phases is ordering strengthening because disloca- 
tions have to cut through the precipitates. Additionally, the lattice 
misﬁt between precipitation phases and the matrix is important in 
reinforcement [33–35] . Owing to these factors, the strength is ef- 
fectively enhanced without causing a severe reduction in ductility. 
These studies suggest that the precipitation-strengthening mech- 
anism may have an enormous potential in the strengthening of 
HEAs. 
Several HEAs with a precipitation-strengthening mechanism 
have recently been reported to exhibit excellent tensile properties 
[39–47] . L12 -strengthened HEAs can be obtained by alloying with 
Al and Ti. Relying on the L12 Ni3 (Al,Ti) superlattice as a reinforce- 
ment, the strength can be signiﬁcantly enhanced. For example, Tao 
et al. [39] reported that L12 nano-precipitates can be formed in 
a (FeCoNi)96 Ti7 Al7 alloy, which enhanced the yield strength from 
300 MPa to 1100 MPa without reducing the ductility. Compared 
with the γ′ phase, the D022 ( γ′′ ) phase may possibly introduce 
a better strengthening effect due to its high anti-phase bound- 
ary (APB) energy and large lattice misﬁt [45–47] . However, up to 
date, only one HEA, with the composition Ni2 CoCrFeNb0.15 , has 
been successfully designed and prepared with D022 superlattice 
nano-precipitates [ 45 , 46 ]. In this study, the overall valence elec- 
tron concentration (OVEC) design strategy is proposed to introduce 
γ′′ precipitates in HEAs. Because of both ordering and coherency 
strengthening by γ′′ precipitates, the strength is signiﬁcantly en- 
hanced without causing a substantial sacriﬁce in ductility. Further- 
more, these superlattice precipitate phases play important roles 
in enhancing high-temperature strength. For instance, by alloying 
with Al and Nb, the γ′ phase can form in (FeCo2 Ni2 Cr)92 Nb4 Al4 , 
which signiﬁcantly enhances strength at elevated temperatures 
[48] . The aforementioned information indicates that the γ′′ phase 
can possibly act as an effective reinforcement for strengthening 
HEAs at both room and elevated temperatures. However, the D022 
structure is usually unstable at high temperatures in HEAs, and 
transformation may take place when the temperature is above a 
critical value. In CoCrFeNiNb0.25 , for example, the γ′′ phase is able 
to transform to the ε phase when the temperature exceeds 750 °C 
[47] . Thus, there is still lack of a successful design of RHEAs with 
γ′′ precipitates. Therefore, it is critically important for us to induce 
γ′′ nano-precipitates into RHEAs at high temperatures. 
In this research, a new non-equiatomic W30 Ta5 (FeNi)65 RHEA 
with high-density coherent D022 superlattice precipitates is de- 
signed. Based on the design strategy of multi-phase transitions in 
HEAs, a unique cast alloy containing body-centered cubic (BCC), 
face-centered cubic (FCC), and μ phases was prepared. In this case, 
nano-scaled γ′′ precipitates separated from the FCC matrix after 
annealing. Owing to the unique multi-phase structure, especially 
that of the γ′′ precipitates, the strength could be remarkably en- 
hanced without a loss of ductility. The yield strength of the alloy 
reached 1450, 1300, 1000, and 330 MPa at 25 °C, 600 °C, 800 °C, 
and 10 0 0 °C, respectively. By systematic microstructural analyses 
using high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM), 
high-angle annular dark-ﬁeld (HAADF) imaging, and atom probe 
topography (APT), the underlying synergy of the multiple strength- 
ening mechanisms was revealed. 
2. Material design philosophy 
Heat-resistant nickel-based superalloys and tungsten-based al- 
loys are widely used in high-temperature and radiation-resistance 
conditions [ 35–38 , 45–55 ]. The recently emerged design philosophy 
of HEAs has also achieved a great success. Here, using the design 
strategy of multiple phase transitions in HEAs, we have achieved 
a new RHEA with a multi-phase structure that combines the fea- 
tures of both nickel-based superalloys and tungsten-based alloys. 
Owing to its novel multi-phase structure, this alloy has the poten- 
tial to achieve outstanding mechanical properties at both room and 
elevated temperatures. 
W, Fe, and Ni were chosen as the base elements in this RHEA. 
Along with conventional tungsten-based alloys, the W–(Fe, Ni) sys- 
tem separates into different phases, which is caused by its wide 
miscibility gap [ 53 , 55 ]. Alloying of Ni/Fe in W is strictly limited to 
around 7 at.% because of the different crystal structures and strong 
immiscibility characteristics of W–(Fe, Ni) [53–55] . The strong im- 
miscibility between W and Ni/Fe has the merit in designing a 
multi-phase structure with different chemical compositions. Ac- 
cording to the W–Fe–Ni ternary phase diagram, the main phase 
structures of heavy tungsten alloys are the tungsten powder phase 
(BCC), the bonding phase (FCC), and a small amount of topolog- 
ically close-packed (TCP) phase [53] . Furthermore, the chemical 
compositions of the FCC phase in W-based alloys are similar to 
those of Ni-based superalloys [35] . This offers possibilities for com- 
bining the advantage of both nickel-based alloys and W-based al- 
loys to develop superior strength–ductility RHEAs. 
In traditional Ni-based alloys, the prevailing strengthening 
strategy is to derive enhanced strength from precipitation of the 
γ′ phase within the L12 structure or the precipitation of the γ′ 
phase within the D022 structure [ 35–38 , 47–51 ]. Here, γ′ precipi- 
tate strengthening was chosen as the target strengthening mech- 
anism due to a large APB energy and higher lattice misﬁt, when 
compared with the γ′ phase [46] . As in the HEAs and Ni-based 
alloys reported, it has been suggested that Ta or Nb can be used 
as an effective alloying element for the formation of D022 su- 
perlattice precipitation. Here, Ta was chosen as the alloying ele- 
ment. Owing to the low solubility of Ta, γ′′ precipitates of the 
Ni3 Ta type will separate out after aging from the supersaturated 
FCC matrix [ 34 , 38 ]. Moreover, Ta and W can be highly soluble to 
avoid the occurrence of additional intermetallic compounds [54] . 
By studying the alloying of different amounts of Ta in W–Fe–Ni- 
based alloys, we found that when the Ta content exceeded 5 at.%, 
the volume fraction of the TCP phase increased signiﬁcantly, which 
would cause serious deterioration in ductility. To balance the frac- 
tions of γ′′ precipitates and TCP, Ta was added at a level of 
5 at.%. Herein, following the aforementioned design strategy, a new 
W30 Ta5 (FeNi)65 RHEA was obtained. 
3. Experiments 
3.1. Sample preparation 
Bulk W30 Ta5 (FeNi)65 alloy ingot was prepared by arc melting 
of the pure metals (W, Ta, Fe, and Ni with purities > 99.99%) 
in argon atmosphere. The alloy sample was repeatedly melted at 
least 10 times to ensure uniform melting and improve its chem- 
ical homogeneity. After cooling, a bulk ingot with dimensions of 
50 mm × 50 mm × 20 mm was obtained. To obtain γ′′ precipi- 
tates, the as-cast samples were annealed at 1200 °C for 5 h, fol- 
lowed by furnace cooling. 
3.2. Compressive experiments 
Compression samples with a diameter of 4 mm and the length 
of 8 mm were prepared by wire electro-discharge machining. Both 
ends of the samples were polished with 10 0 0-grit SiC paper. Quasi- 
static uniaxial compression tests with a quasi-static strain rate of 
5 ×10−4 s−1 were performed using an MTS-810 universal materials 
tester at 25 °C, 600 °C, 800 °C, and 1000 °C. For comparison, the 
as-cast samples were also examined.

4.2. Compression properties at different temperatures

5. Discussion 
5.1. Precipitation mechanisms and multiple-phase structures 
During cooling from the liquid temperature to the liquid–solid 
transition temperature, phase separation takes place in the liquid 
matrix phase ( Lm ) [52–55] . Lm then decomposes into a W-based 
phase ( Sw ) with a BCC structure and a eutectic liquid phase ( Le ) 
that has high concentrations of Ni and Fe. The phase transforma- 
tion can be expressed as: 
Lm → Le + Sw (1) 
The BCC phase is the ﬁrst solidiﬁed during cooling. In this pro- 
cess, signiﬁcant element segregation occurs. The total contents of 
W and Ta (at.%) in Sw reached ∼88%, with small amounts of Fe 
and Ni ( Table 1 ). The principal components of Le are Ni and Fe 
with small amounts of Ta and W remained. As the temperature 
decreased to the eutectic temperature, further separation between 
Ta and Fe/Ni resulted in the eutectic transformation of Le . Le then 
decomposed into the Ni-based matrix ( SNi ) with the FCC structure 
and a TCP μ phase. The event associated with such a phase trans- 
formation can be expressed as: 
Le → SNi + μ (2) 
After the eutectic transformation, the principal elements in SNi 
were Ni and Fe, and their total content exceeded 90% (at.%). Dur- 
ing the eutectic transformation, Ta clustered together, leading to 
the formation of μ with the TCP structure. The main component 
of the μ phase is Ta, which was present as approximate 40% (at.%). 
Finally, almost all Ta segregated in the μ phase. This suggests that 
Ta is the major element involved in the subsequent eutectic reac- 
tion. This ﬁnding is further supported by comparing different con- 
centrations of Ta in W30 Tax (FeNi)70−x samples: with increased Ta 
content, the volume fraction of the μ phase increased signiﬁcantly. 
To obtain D022 superlattice precipitates, a sample of the mate- 
rial was annealed in Ni-based HEAs [ 34 , 45 , 46 ]. Differential scan- 
ning calorimetry results revealed an obvious endothermic peak 
near 1150 °C, indicating that a solid–solid phase transformation oc- 
curs. Thus, the phase transition involving the precipitation of the 
second phase takes place. This phase transformation can be ex- 
pressed as: 
SNi → SNi + D022 (3) 
During this phase transition, an ordered chemical transfor-

5.2. Multi-phase structure strengthening mechanism 
5.2.1. Synergistic strengthening of multiple phases 
Here, we ﬁrst discuss the strengthening mechanisms in 
the W30 Ta5 (FeNi)65 alloy at room temperature. Because of the 
multiple-phase structure ( Fig. 7 (a)), all phases participate in the

from as-cast samples with those of the annealed samples indicates 
that D022 nano-precipitates, formed in the FCC matrix in annealed 
samples, are the main strengthening phase. Additionally, The BCC

5 . 2 . 2 . γ′′ -strengthening at room temperature 
There are two main mechanisms of precipitation strengthen- 
ing: the dislocation shearing of precipitates and the Orowan by- 
pass mechanism [56–62] . Which of these two strengthening mech- 
anisms will occur depends on many factors, including the size of 
precipitate, coherency or incoherency, and its structural features 
[ 59 , 62 ]: all of these need to be determined by the structural analy- 
sis. APBs and stacking faults (SFs) were observed after compression 
at room temperature ( Fig. 8 (a) and (d)). Because of the low num- 
ber of SFs, their strengthening effect was omitted to simplify the 
analysis. 
First, we discuss the crystal and coherency structures between 
the γ′′ precipitates and FCC matrix. HRTEM images show the 
structure of FCC matrix containing nano-precipitates with a D022 
structure. Using FFT of the precipitates and FCC matrix ( Fig. 3 (a)), 
we could clearly distinguish the structures of the two phases. Dark- 
ﬁeld TEM images of γ′′ ( Fig. 3 (c) and (d)) show that the nano- 
precipitates occurred in two different directions. The different crys- 
tallographic orientations can be observed from the SAED pattern 
along the < 001 > of the FCC matrix ( Fig. 4 (a)). The HRTEM images 
show that there is a parallel relationship between the FCC matrix 
and γ′′ , such as < 0 01 >FCC // < 0 01 >D022 ( Fig. 4 (e) and (f)). Similarly, 
the HRTEM images indicate that there is no parallel relationship 
between the other phases. 
When the precipitated particles maintain a nanometer size 
and coherency with the matrix, dislocations shearing of precip- 
itates contribute to the precipitate-strengthening mechanism; in 
contrast, if the particle size is large or has an incoherent struc- 
ture with the matrix, the Orowan bypass mechanism takes ef- 
fect [62] . The size of γ′′ was approximately 10 nm, so the D022 
nano-precipitates maintained coherency with the FCC matrix dur- 
ing the deformation process [63] . Thus, precipitate shearing is con- 
sidered to be the dominant strengthening mechanism. To con- 
ﬁrm this, TEM observation of samples after compressive defor- 
mation was carried out ( Fig. 7 (b) and (c)). The motion of dis- 
locations suggests that these accumulated around the precipitate 
and sheared through the precipitates, by which dislocation shear- 
through strengthening occurred. 
Ordering hardening and lattice misﬁt are the main strength- 
ening factors of the γ′′ -strengthening mechanism. Owing to the 
lattice misﬁt, the motion of dislocations is hindered when γ′′ is 
sheared by dislocations, which signiﬁcantly enhances the strength. 
In the ordered D022 structure, APB will be formed after a single 
dislocation shear the precipitates ( Fig. 8 (a) and (c)). The APB en-

5.2.3. Strengthening mechanism at elevated temperatures 
On the basis of the previous studies, the mechanism will change 
from shear strengthening to the Orowan effect as the precipi- 
tates grow [60–62] . In this study, the size of γ′′ precipitates grew 
rapidly under the inﬂuence of both temperature and stress. To ex- 
amine the stability of the D022 structure at high temperatures, a 
long annealing treatment at 1200 °C for 48 h was carried out. The 
results show no change in the size of the γ′′ precipitates, which 
was almost same as that obtained for annealing at 1200 °C for 
5 h. This indicates that the alloy has a high thermal stability at 
high temperatures without external mechanical loadings. TEM re- 
sults show that the precipitate size did not change during com- 
pression tests at room temperature, which suggests that the stress 
alone does not promote the growth of precipitation. Thus, the in- 
ﬂuence of stress or temperature alone on the precipitate size is not 
obvious: precipitates growth following high-temperature compres- 
sion resulted from the cooperative effects of stress and tempera- 
ture [64–68] . 
Following high-temperature compression tests at 600 °C, 
800 °C, and 1000 °C, the precipitate size grew to 50 nm, 100 nm, 
and 120 nm, respectively ( Fig. 9 (b), (c), and (e)). HRTEM images 
( Fig. 9 (d) and (f)) show that the precipitate still maintained the 
D022 structure: only the size of the precipitate changed after the 
high-temperature compression. During compression at 10 0 0 °C, 
adjacent precipitates tended to connect without a change in the 
lattice structure ( Fig. 9 (f)). This leads to a transition from the 
shearing mechanism to the Orowan looping mechanism. When 
compressing at room temperature, only the dislocation shear- 
ing mechanism is prevailed. When the size grew to more than 
50 nm after compression at 600 °C, when both the shearing 
and Orowan mechanisms occurred. In compression at 800 °C, 
the average size of the precipitates grew further to 100 nm. At 
this point, the precipitation-strengthening mechanism was com- 
pletely dominated by the Orowan mechanism. This change of 
precipitation-strengthening mechanism induced the reduction of 
the yield strength at high temperature.

With the increase of the γ′′ precipitate size, /Delta1σpo is obviously 
decreased. In contrast, as the temperature increased, the number of 
slip dislocations increased and the slip resistance decreased, which 
is another reason for the decrease in strength [67–69] . The de- 
crease in the material yield strength can be reasonably explained 
from calculations of /Delta1σpo at different temperatures. In Ni-based 
superalloys, the directional coarsening of γ′ is known as ‘rafting’ 
[ 70 , 71 ]. After long annealing, the γ′ particles coarsen quickly. As 
a result, during high-temperature deformation, dislocations mainly 
move and accumulate along the γ channels, leading to a decrease 
of strength [72] . In contrast, a positive lattice misﬁt between γ and 
γ′ may generate a less restriction against the dislocation move- 
ment at elevated temperatures and the γ′′ precipitates can main- 
tain a relatively stable structure without any rapid growth. 
The Orowan mechanism has an effective strengthening effect, 
which brings about a higher strength of this alloy compared with 
that of Ni-based superalloys at elevated temperatures. When the 
temperature increases to 10 0 0 °C, dislocations become a channel 
for a fast element diffusion in Ni-based superalloys, which results 
in the rapid growth of γ′′ [ 49–51 , 73 ]. A similar process occurs in 
this RHEA. Under compression at 10 0 0 °C, adjacent γ′′ particles 
grew together and formed aberrant precipitates, which gives rise to 
the failure of the Orowan effect. As a result, the strength reduced 
sharply. The γ′′ precipitates in the RHEA, however, are much less 
concentrated than those in Ni-based alloys. Moreover, the phase 
transformation is commonplace in Ni-based alloys after anneal- 
ing at high temperature, in which γ′ changes into the γ phase 
and γ′′ transforms into the ε phase [ 50 , 52 ]. That does not happen 
in this alloy. γ′′ precipitates consistently retained the D022 struc- 
ture. Therefore, W30 Ta5 (FeNi)65 with the γ′′ -strengthening exhibits 
a higher strength at elevated temperatures.

6. Conclusions 
On the basis of the design strategy of a multi-phase transi- 
tion in HEAs, a novel RHEA with high-density coherent γ′′ nano- 
precipitates was designed. It exhibits an excellent combination of 
strength and compressive plasticity at both room and elevated 
temperatures. Several key conclusions can be drawn. 
(1) High-density nanoscale coherent γ′′ precipitates formed in the 
FCC matrix of W30 Ta5 (FeNi)65 after annealing at 1200 °C for 
5 h. After annealing at 1200 °C for 48 h, the size of γ′′ pre- 
cipitates did not changed, indicating that the precipitates show 
outstanding thermal stability owing to the D022 structure. 
(2) The γ′′ precipitates signiﬁcantly improve the material strength. 
After annealing, the yield stress of W30 Ta5 (FeNi)65 increased 
from 750 MPa to 1450 MPa at room temperature without de- 
terioration of the ductility. The dominant strengthening mecha- 
nism is shearing strengthening. The formation of APB after dis- 
locations shearing of precipitates is the main cause of strength- 
ening. 
(3) In high-temperature compression, the precipitation- 
strengthening mechanism changed from shearing to the 
Orowan mechanism as the temperature increased. When the 
deformation temperature was below 600 °C, the strengthening 
occurred by the shearing mechanism, this transformed into 
the Orowan mechanism when the deformation temperature 
exceeded 800 °C. The main factor leading to the change of 
strengthening mechanism is the change of particle size. The 
γ′′ -strengthening mechanism induced outstanding mechanical 
properties at high temperatures. 
In summary, the introduction of γ′′ precipitates into a multi- 
phase RHEA signiﬁcantly increased the strength without sacriﬁc- 
ing ductility. W30 Ta5 (FeNi)65 with a γ′′ phase shows outstand- 
ing mechanical properties and exhibits great potential for high- 
temperature structural applications.
