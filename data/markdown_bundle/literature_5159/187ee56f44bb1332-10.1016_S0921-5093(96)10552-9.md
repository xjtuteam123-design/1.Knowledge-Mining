Abstract 
High-temperature deformation of the C15 NbCr2-based intermetallics in ternary Nb-Cr-V and Nb-Cr-Mo alloy systems is 
observed in terms of microstructural and compositional effect, by compression testing and transmission electron microscopy 
(TEM). The observed 0.2% yield stress and stress-strain curve were largely dependent on the equilibrating b.c.c, phase (or the C15 
phase), their volume fraction and morphology. Among the observed alloys, the Nb-Cr-Mo alloys consisting of the C15 phase 
and 'Nb-rich' b.c.c, phase showed slightly enhanced 0.2% yield stress and largely decreased brittle-ductile transition temperature 
(BDTT) in comparison to the unalloyed single-phase C15 NbCr 2 alloy, indicating a better high-temperature mechanical property 
with a favorable balance of strength and fracture toughness. TEM observation indicates that high density of dislocations are 
observed above BDTT in both constituent phases in both alloy systems. Also, the Burgers vector and morphological feature of 
dislocations are determined. © 1997 Elsevier Science S.V. 
Keywords: High-temperature deformation; Laves phase; NbCr e alloy; Nb-Cr-Mo alloy system; Nb-Cr-V alloy system 
1. Introduction 
Laves intermetallics with AB2 stoichiometry [1] are 
currently developed for use as functional materials (i.e., 
magnetic materials, magneto-optical materials, hydro- 
gen storage materials, superconducting materials, etc. 
[2]), arid are also considered to be good candidates as 
high-temperature structural materials operating at high 
temperatures and in severe environments. Among a 
large number of Laves intermetallics, the NbCr2-based 
Laves intermetallics are attractive for high-temperature 
structural materials, because they have a high melting 
point (about 2000 K), low density (about 7.7 g cm-3), 
and potential resistance to corrosion and oxidation. 
The NbCr2 intermetallics undergo a phase transition 
from the C14 to the C15 structures at about 1870 K [3]. 
A few studies have been performed on deformation 
behavior and related properties of the NbCr2-based 
intermetallics, e.g., single-phase C15 NbCr2 [4,5], and 
the NbCr2 with a large volume fraction of Cr solid 
solution [6] and Nb solid solution [7]. It was shown that

those C15 NbCr2-based intermetallics have reasonably

From two points of view, ternary alloying is consid- 
ered to be an effective method of improving mechanical 
properties of the NbCr2-based intermetallics. One point 
is that the addition of some refractory elements to the 
C15 NbCr2 can improve deformability via facilitating 
deformation of the C15 structure itself, according to a 
recent analysis (based on a hard sphere model) for 
stacking sequence of the AB2 structure [8,9]. It was 
predicted that shear deformation along the {111} 
planes can be facilitated if the atomic diameter ratio, 
-RA/RB, between two constituent atoms, A and B, de- 
creases from a value in the binary NbCr2 intermetallics 
[8,9]. Fortunately, many refractory metal elements have 
been shown to have large solubilities in the C15 NbCr2 
intermetaltics [10]. Indeed, some elements such as Mo 
and V have been shown to improve the high-tempera- 
ture deformability of the C15 NbCr2 intermetallics [1 t-

Previous works have showed that V [11-13] and Mo 
[11,12] were actually effective in improving the high- 
temperature mechanical property of the C15 NbCr2 
Laves phase itself [11,12]. However, their strength levels 
were still Iow and ductile-brittle transition tempera- 
tures (DBTTs) were not reduced as much as we had 
expected. On the other hand, V and Mo are intersolu- 
ble to the two constituents Nb and Cr. Therefore, the 
C15 NbCr2 can be equilibrated directly with ductile 
b.c.c, solid solution without forming any intermediate 
compounds. Actually, duplex microstructures consist- 
ing of a variety of alloy compositions in two constituent 
phases have been obtained in the ternary Nb-Cr-V 
[13,14] and Nb-Cr-Mo alloy systems [14]. Thus, the 
addition of V and Mo to the NbCr2 intermetallics is 
considered to improve the mechanical properties of the 
NbCr~ intermetallics. In this work, high-temperature 
compressive deformation in the ternary Nb-Cr-V and 
Nb-Cr-Mo alloys, microstructures of which were 
shown to consist of the C15 NbCr, and b.c.c, solid 
solution [14], is investigated. Also, deformation mi- 
crostructures in the constituent phases are investigated 
by a transmission electron microscope (TEM). Empha- 
sis is placed on seeking those alloys (i.e., microstruc- 
tures) with an optimal balance of high-temperature 
strength and toughness. 
2. Experimental procedure 
Fig. 1 shows the isothermal phase diagrams at 1623 
K of two ternary alloy systems, Nb-Cr-V and Nb- 
Cr-Mo [14]. Alloy compositions of alloys prepared in 
this work are denoted in Fig. 1 and also listed in Table 
1. In both alloy systems, two types of duplex mi- 
crostructures were prepared to observe high-tempera- 
ture mechanical properties; alloy compositions 
consisting of C15 phase and Nb-rich b.c.c, phase, and 
those consisting of C15 phase and Cr-rich b.c.c, phase. 
Also, some alloys whose two constituent phases lie on 
the same tie line were prepared in order to investigate 
the effect of volume fraction of the two constituent 
phases on the mechanical properties. Details of speci- 
men preparation and heat treatment are described in 
[14]. 
Compression specimens with a dimension of about 
2.5 x 2.5 x 5 mm were cut by a precision wheel cutter 
from central regions of the button ingots with their axes 
parallel to the solidification direction. Lateral faces of 
the specimens were abraded on sufficiently fine SiC 
paper. Compression tests were carried out at various 
temperatures at an initial strain rate of 1.7 x 10 .4 s-1 
in a vacuum, using an Instron machine equipped with a 
vacuum chamber and a tungsten mesh heater. To ob- 
tain reliable 0.2% compressive yield stress and resultant 
brittle-ductile transition temperature (BDTT), data 
were plotted only when plastic deformation exceeded 
plastic strain of 0.5%.

ductile and softer at these temperatures than the 'Nb- 
rich' b.c.c, phase. The volume fraction of the 'Cr-rich' 
b.c.c, phase is thus an important factor affecting the 
0.2% yield stress and BDTT. It is concluded from Fig. 
l(a) that the 'Cr-rich' b.c.c, solid solution is basically 
more deformable at high temperatures than the 'Nb- 
rich' and the 'V-rich' b.c.c, solid solutions, because it 
has a lower melting point than the other two con- 
stituents, Nb and V. Also, the V elements are less 
soluble in the 'Cr-rich' b.c.c, solid solution than in 
the 'Nb-rich' and the 'V-rich' b.c.c, solid solutions. 
Consequently, in the alloy consisting of duplex mi- 
crostructure with the "Cr-rich' b.c.c, phase solid solu- 
tion, not only the 0.2% yield stress but also BDTT is 
lowered. 
3.i.2. Deformation microstructure 
Deformation microstructure was observed in some 
alloys deformed at high temperatures. For example, 
Figs. 4 and 5 show the TEM bright field images of 
deformation microstructure in two constituent phases 
of the CrsoNb40V10 and the Cr55Nb30Vi5 alloys, respec- 
tively. Both alloys were deformed at 1573 K up to 
relatively large plastic strain. The former alloy consists 
of the C15 phase and the 'Nb-rich' b.c.c, phase while 
the latter alloy consists of the C15 phase and the 
'Cr-rich' b.c.c, phase. In both phases of both alloys, 
high densities of dislocations were observed. The Burg- 
ers vector of the activated dislocations was identified to 
be ½(11t) in the b.c.c, phase and ½(110) in the C15 
Laves phase. The latter result is the same as that 
observed in the unalloyed single-phase C15 NbCr2 [4,5]. 
At high temperatures beyond BDTT, the C15 phase as 
well as the b.c.c, phase is thus plastically deformable. 
Here, it should be noted that a dislocation activated in 
the C15 phase was split into two Shockley partials, i.e., 
-~(112) bounded by a stacking fault (SF). However, the 
width of the SF between two partials was mostly nar- 
rower than that observed in the unalloyed single-phase 
C15 NbCr 2 [4,5] or the C15 phase in the ternary 
Nb-Cr-Mo alloys consisting of the duplex microstruc- 
ture (see Section 3.2). This result means that the SF 
energy in the C15 NbCr 2 is increased by the addition of 
V.

4. Conclusions 
The high-temperature deformation behavior of the 
C15 NbCr2-based intermetallics alloyed with V and 
Mo, which consist of duplex microstructure with b.c.c.

phase, was studied for microstructural and composi- 
tional effects, by compression testing and TEM. The 
following results were obtained in this study. 
(1) In the ternary Nb-Cr-V alloy system, the alloys 
consisting of duplex microstructure with the 'Cr-rich' 
b.c.c, phase showed decreased 0.2% yield stress and 
BDTT in comparison to the unalloyed single-phase C15 
NbCr2. On the other hand, the alloys consisting of 
duplex microstructure with the 'Nb-rich" b.c.c, phase 
showed decreased BDTT, although their 0.2% yield 
stresses remained at the same level as those of the 
unalloyed single-phase C15 NbCr2. 
(2) In the ternary Nb-Cr-Mo alloy system, the 
alloys consisting of duplex microstructure with the 'Cr- 
rich' b.c.c, phase showed considerably decreased 0.2% 
yield stress and BDTT in comparison to the unalloyed 
single-phase C15 NbCr2. On the other hand, the alloys 
consisting of duplex microstructure with the 'Nb-rich' 
b.c.c, phase showed slightly enhanced 0.2% yield stress 
and decreased BDTT. It is therefore demonstrated that 
the ternary Nb-Cr-Mo alloys consisting of the C15 
phase and the 'Nb-rich' b.c.c, phase have high-tempera- 
ture mechanical properties preferable to those of the 
other alloy system, alloy composition and microstruc- 
ture. 
(3) Above BDTT, a high density of dislocations was 
observed in each of two constituent phases in both 
alloy systems, and their Burgers vector and morpholog- 
ical features were determined.
