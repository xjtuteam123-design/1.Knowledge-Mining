ABSTRACT
Additive manufacturing (AM) has become a crucial technique for fabricating high-performance superalloys. 
K447A, a promising candidate for high-temperature applications, exhibits significant cracking issues during AM 
processing. In this study, a mixture of K447A and IN718 powders was processed via selective laser melting (SLM) 
to enhance mechanical properties and minimize cracking. The optimal composition was identified as KI28 (20% 
K447A, 80% IN718). Microstructural analysis revealed that KI28 exhibited the smallest laser melt pool area and a 
short-chain Laves phase morphology, reducing crack formation. Additionally, mechanical testing demonstrated 
that the increased IN718 content improved tensile strength and ductility. These findings provide valuable in -
sights for optimizing Ni-based superalloys through AM processing.
1. Introduction
Ni-based superalloys have attracted significant attention for hot- 
section components in the aerospace turbine engines and industrial 
gas turbines due to their exceptional oxidation and thermal corrosion 
resistance, along with an outstanding combination of high-temperature 
mechanical properties [ 1 – 4 ]. However, machining Ni-based superalloys 
remains challenging because of their high hardness and work hardening 
rate [ 5 ]. In recent years, selective laser melting (SLM), one of the most 
widely used Additive Manufacturing (AM) techniques, has been used to 
fabricate Ni-based superalloys, such as Inconel 625 and Inconel 718 
(IN718) [ 6 , 7 ].
Among Ni-based superalloys, K447A is a well-known non-weldable 
alloy that is highly prone to hot cracking [ 4 ]. This significantly increases 
the challenges and costs associated with fabricating complex compo -
nents from K447A. Existing strategies to mitigate cracking in additively 
manufactured K447A superalloys exhibit critical limitations. Zhang 
et al. [ 2 ] reduced hot cracking by 30 % through pulsed-wave laser duty 
cycle modulation, yet persistent elemental segregation and solidification 
shrinkage hindered full crack suppression [ 2 ]. Zhang et al. [ 4 ] employed 
shot peening to decrease surface crack density by 25 %; however, 
subsurface cracks and micro-void formation remained unresolved [ 4 ]. In 
contrast, Wei et al. [ 8 ] utilized laser surface remelting (LSR) to generate 
a remelted buffer layer (RBL) featuring refined dendrites (29.1 μ m vs. 
229.6 μ m spacing in cast substrate) and reduced elemental segregation.

2. Materials and methods
Two types of commercial powders, K447A and IN718, were pur -
chased from Avic Mette Additive Technology Co., LTD. The powders 
were mixed using a planetary ball milling for 8 h with the following 
weight fractions (wt. %): KI46 (K447A: 40 %, IN718: 60 %), KI28 
(K447A: 20 %, IN718: 80 %) and KI19 (K447A: 10 %, IN718: 90 %). 
These alloys were fabricated via SLM using an AFS-M260 machine under 
the following processing parameters: a laser power of 240 W, a laser spot 
diameter of 75 μ m, a scanning speed of 900 mm/s, a powder layer 
thickness of 40 μ m and a hatch spacing of 80 μ m. A bidirectional 
scanning strategy was employed, with a 67
◦
rotation between layers.
X-ray diffraction (XRD) was carried out on a PANalytical Xpert ’ s 
diffractometer with Cu K α radiation. Microstructure was characterized 
using an optical microscope and a TESCAN AMBER scanning electron 
microscope (SEM). An Instron 3365 universal electronic testing machine 
was used to determine the room-temperature mechanical properties. 
Each tensile result represents the average value obtained from three 
tensile samples.

A minimal heat-affected zone is desirable, as excessive heat dissi -
pation leads to microstructural coarsening, which degrades material 
properties [ 10 ]. Alloys with larger heat-affected zones are more prone to 
cracking. These results indicate that KI28 and KI19 are less prone to 
fracture. Overall, increasing the proportion of IN718 results in wider yet 
shallower molten pool, enhancing its ability to accommodate thermal 
stress and reducing crack formation.

tensile strength and promoting crack growth [ 11 , 12 ]. Therefore, the 
increased presence of the Laves phase may compromise both strength 
and plasticity, necessitating appropriate thermal treatment to mitigate 
its adverse effects [ 13 ].
Fig. 4 (a) presents the room temperature stress – strain curves of 
different samples. K447A exhibits significantly low strength and 
ductility, likely due to the extensive cracking formed during SLM pro -
cessing. In contrast, both strength and ductility enhance as the IN718 
content increases. Notably, KI28 demonstrates the most significant 
enhancement in mechanical properties among the alloys, with a yield 
strength (YS) of 784 ± 10 MPa, an ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of 
1105 ± 13 MPa, and an elongation of 23.7 ± 0.2 %. The tensile fracture 
morphology of each sample is also shown in Fig. 4 . In K447A and KI46 
( Fig. 4 (a) and (b)), cleavage planes are visible at the fracture surface, 
indicating a brittle fracture mode. Additionally, exposed pores and 
numerous open cracks, along with primary dendrite arms forming 
columnar tough dimples, were observed. These voids act as internal 
notches, leading to stress concentration and increased brittleness in KI46 
[ 14 ]. In contrast, KI28 and KI19 exhibit fracture characteristics, with 
fine, tough dimples at the fracture surface, contributing to a significant 
increase in elongation, as shown in Fig. 4 (c) and (d). However, the

4. Conclusions
In this study, Ni-based superalloys were successfully fabricated via 
selective laser melting (SLM) using mechanically mixed K447A and 
IN718 powders. The ball milling process preserved the crystal structure, 
ensuring compositional uniformity. An increased proportion of IN718 
led to a reduced melt pool area, thereby improving thermal stress ac -
commodation, and enhancing resistance to cracking, with KI28 and KI19 
showing the most stable morphology. KI28 exhibited a moderate
