1. Introduction 
Cu-based heat-resistant alloys are usually employed in high- 
temperature service environments due to their excellent thermal con -
ductivity, high-temperature strength, and high-temperature creep 
resistance [ 1 – 3 ]. High-performance of Cu-based heat-resistant alloys are 
necessary for liquid rocket engine combustion chambers, 
channel-cooled nozzles, and injectors [ 4 , 5 ]. The GRCop-84 alloy 
(Cu – 8Cr – 4Nb alloy), developed by the Green Research Center, has 
attracted extensive attentions recently [ 6 , 7 ]. And it has exceptional 
high-temperature creep resistance among the reported Cu – Al
2
O
3 
[ 8 ], 
Cu-Cr-Zr [ 9 ], Cu-Ag-Zr [ 10 ], and other copper alloys. 
Cu – Cr – Nb alloys are primarily strengthened by Cr
2
Nb particles that 
precipitated during the solidification and heat treatment process [ 11 ]. 
The finer the Cr
2
Nb particles, the higher the mechanical properties of 
the studied alloy. Primary particles are the sizable Cr
2
Nb particles that 
formed during the solidification. And secondary or tertiary particles are 
given to the fine Cr
2
Nb particles that formed during the heat treatment 
[ 10 ]. Compared with the fine particles precipitated during the heat 
treatment, the strengthening effect of the large-scale Cr
2
Nb particles 
precipitated during the solidification could be negligible [ 12 ]. The size 
of Cr
2
Nb particles precipitated during the solidification process of the 
alloy is correlated with the solidification rate [ 13 , 14 ]. Although it is 
possible to fabricate large-scale billets using the melting and casting 
method, the specific procedure is challenging to regulate. The powder 
metallurgy-fabricated alloys possess good room-temperature perfor -
mance and great material utilization, but the process is complicated and 
the billets size is limited. However, refining the large Cr
2

formed during the solidification process is Challenging. Researchers 
believed that the casting method was unsuitable for ingot fabrication, 
and rapid solidification methods must be employed to fabricate the 
Cu – Cr – Nb alloys [ 15 , 16 ]. Anderson et al. [ 10 ] reported a Cu – 8Cr – 4Nb 
alloy fabricated by the powder metallurgy method and found that the 
large primary particles were generated during the rapid solidification of 
the alloy, about 1 μ m. Additionally, particles of several hundred nano -
meters and less than 100 nm precipitated in solid solution and aging 
treatment, respectively. Li et al. [ 17 ] fabricated Cu – 4Cr – 2Nb alloys by 
cold spraying and vacuum plasma spraying, and they obtained fine

printing, and the largest (Cr, Fe)

In this work, one Cu-4.06Cr-1.25Nb alloy was fabricated by powder 
metallurgy, and the other one was fabricated by atmospheric melting.

on the samples. Nano-scale Cr and Cr-rich precipitates formed during

alloy fabricated by powder metallurgy showed higher room- 
temperature strength but lower high-temperature strength was 
discussed.

the Cr

strength of the alloy. While the increase in the solidification speed 
resulted in a limited reduction in particle size, and it was challenging to 
obtain nano-scale Cr
2
Nb particles to strengthen the alloy substantially.

4.2. High-temperature failure mechanism 
EBSD analysis of the fractures of ACH and SPS @ 750 
◦
C alloys 
treated by process III and tensile test at 500 
◦
C was conducted to char -
acterize the high-temperature plastic deformation and fracture mecha -
nism of the alloy, as shown in Fig. 13 . The grain size in the studied alloy 
fabricated by atmospheric melting method was larger. The alloy frac -
tured mostly through intragranular fracture. However, the grain size of 
the studied alloy fabricated by powder metallurgy had a bimodal dis -
tribution. The fracture mode was a mixed fracture of intragranular 
fracture and intergranular fracture. The crack source extended along to 
the fine-grained zone, indicating that the deformation mechanism 
would change at high-temperature. In addition to the slip of disloca -
tions, there would also be a slip of grain boundaries and torsion of 
grains. The fine grains could become crack sources during high- 
temperature tensile, and the cracks could propagate along the grain- 
refined zones, ultimately leading to material failure. Although the 
fabrication method of rapid solidification could refine the primary rich 
in Cr and Nb particles, its strengthening contribution was limited in 
comparison to the submicron-scale or nano-scale particles formed dur -
ing the heat treatment. However, the rapid solidification refined the 
grains of the matrix while refining the second phase particles, thereby 
declining the high-temperature mechanical properties. The mechanisms 
for high-temperature failure could be simplified as the grain-refined/ 
coarsened boundaries became the crack sources and crack propagation 
paths, which was also the reason for the fabrication of single crystal in 
superalloy. Ling Wang et al. [ 3 ] designed and fabricated a new 
precipitation-strengthened Cu – Cr – Nb – Zr alloy. The results showed that 
the regions distributed with smaller grains were susceptible to grain 
boundary crack initiation, and the Laves precipitates impeded the grain 
boundary crack propagation.

ACH alloy possessed higher strength at room temperature and elevated 
temperature. And at the room temperature, the precipitation strength -

good development prospect and high commercial value.
