ABSTRACT 
Ideal structural materials usually require excellent mechanical properties, high entropy alloys/medium entropy 
alloys (HEAs/MEAs) are considered to be the key material to achieve this goal. In the current work, a novel L1
2 
precipitation strengthened medium entropy alloy is designed. After rolling and recrystallization, the alloy which 
is then aged possesses a mechanical property of a yield strength of 1.09 GPa, an ultimate tensile strength of 1.5 
GPa and nearly 20% elongation at room temperature (the as-aged alloy). However, the plasticity of the as-aged 
alloy which is fully recrystallized is poor at high temperature. Then, the non-recrystallized alloy, which is 
directly aged after rolling, containing coarse lengthening grain with lower grain boundary energy, exhibits 
excellent high temperature mechanical properties. 
1. Introduction 
In recent years, high entropy alloys/medium entropy alloys (HEAs/ 
MEAs) have attracted extensive attention due to their promising prop -
erties [ 1 – 3 ]. The face centered cubic (FCC) HEAs/MEAs with 
outstanding ductility and excellent corrosion resistance are usually 
considered as a potential new structural material. However, the low 
yield strength at room temperature seriously limits its engineering 
application [ 4 ]. Thus, how to solve the problem of mismatch between 
strength and plasticity becomes a key factor for the large-scale practical 
application of FCC HEAs. In order to improve the strength of FCC 
HEAs/MEAs, various methods were proposed, such as solid solution 
strengthening [ 5 , 6 ], fine grain strengthening [ 7 , 8 ], dislocations 
strengthening [ 9 , 10 ]and second phase strengthening [ 11 , 12 ]. Among 
them, second phase strengthening is considered as the most effective 
strengthening method. However, improving strength by various second 
phases is usually at the expense of a lot of plasticity, such as μ phase 
[ 13 ], σ phase [ 14 ]and Laves phase [ 15 ], therefore strength-ductility 
dilemma tradeoff is still an urgent problem to be solved. 
Recent studies showed that the second phase with L1
2 
superlattice 
structure can greatly improve the strength of FCC HEAs/MEAs with 
sacrificing less plasticity to make the alloy have excellent strength- 
plasticity synergy at room temperature [ 16 ]. In addition, the L1
2 
pre -
cipitations strengthened HEAs/MEAs also have good thermal stability, 
which are a potential high temperature structural material [ 17 ]. Thus, in 
this work, we designed a new type of non-equimolar ratio medium en -
tropy alloy, which is Ni46Co22Fe22Al3Ti7(Al3Ti7 alloy). In our design 
strategy, Ni, Co, and Fe element are selected to form the matrix with FCC 
crystal structure of alloy, L1
2 
precipitates were introduced by alloying Al 
and Ti elements. Cr element is excluded from our designed alloy, to 
avoid the formation of brittle phase [ 18 ]. 
The present work is intended to study the microstructure and me -
chanical properties of Al3Ti7 alloy. And the microstructure of the as- 
cast, as-aged and non-recrystallized alloy are investigated. The mor -
phologies of L1
2 
precipitations are discussed as well. The room tem -
perature and high temperature tensile properties of the as-aged alloy is 
studied. Additionally, in order to improve the high temperature me -
chanical properties, the non-recrystallized processing is proposed, and 
the non-recrystallized alloy is tensile tested at high temperature.

2. Materials and methods 
2.1. Alloys preparation 
Pure metals Ni, Co, Fe, Ti and Al ( ≥ 99.9 wt %) were added into the 
vacuum suspension melting furnace in argon atmosphere, and the 
Ni46Co22Fe22Al3Ti7 alloy ingot (at.%, denoted as-cast Al3Ti7 MEA 
hereafter) was prepared by three repeating melting. The actual 
composition examined by X-ray fluorescence spectrometers was 
Ni45.2Co22.5Fe23.2Al2.4Ti6.7 (at.%). The as-cast Al3Ti7 alloy was 
homogenized at 1150 
◦
C for 8 h to make the alloy composition uniform. 
The homogenized alloy ingot was cut into 5 mm thick plate for cold 
rolling, through multi-step rolling, the plate is rolled into 1 mm thick 
sheet. Then the rolled sheet was recrystallized at 1100 
◦
C for 1.5 min to 
obtain fully recrystallized grains. Finally, the recrystallized sheet was 
aged at 700 
◦
C for 1 h and water quenched (denoted as-aged Al3Ti7 MEA 
hereafter). In addition, the other rolled sheet is treated by another heat 
treatment procedure which is just aged at 700 
◦
C for 1 h and water 
quenched, but without recrystallization treatment (denoted non- 
recrystallized Al3Ti7 MEA hereafter). The heat treatment procedures 
of the as-rolled alloy were summarized in Table 1 in detail. All heat 
treatments are carried out in air. 
2.2. Microstructural characterizations and mechanical tests 
The actual composition of the Al3Ti7 MEA was measured by X-ray

3.2. Mechanical properties 
The room temperature mechanical properties of Al3Ti7 MEA are 
shown in Fig. 5 . The engineering stress-strain curves of as-cast, as-aged 
alloy, and non-recrystallized alloy are shown in Fig. 5 (a). The yield 
strength and ultimate tensile strength of the as-cast alloy are 850 ± 40 
MPa and 1080 ± 60 MPa respectively, with the elongation of no more 
than 6 ± 0.5%. The as-aged Al3Ti7 MEA has the highest elongation 
which is about 18 ± 1%, with the yield strength improving to 1100 ±
35 MPa and the ultimate tensile strength up to 1.5 GPa (1520 ± 45 
MPa). The non-recrystallized Al3Ti7 MEA has the highest strength, but it 
also has the worst elongation. The true stress-strain curves and work 
hardening rate curves of Al3Ti7 MEA are shown in Fig. 5 (b). An obvious 
work hardening respond during the plastic deformation can be seen 
from the true stress-strain curves, especially the as-aged Al3Ti7 MEA. 
The work hardening rate curve of the non-recrystallized Al3Ti7 MEA 
only contains the first stage which is the transition stage from elasticity 
to plasticity. And the work hardening rate curve of the as-cast Al3Ti7 
MEA has the first and the second stage, with the highest work hardening 
rate. Only the as-aged Al3Ti7 MEA has a typical multi-stage work 
hardening rate curve.

reaction front (the interface of the DP and the CP), the larger the L12 
particle is. And at last, the DP forms to the chrysanthemum-like 
precipitation.

reduces the stress concentration on the interface between the FCC and

Al3Ti7 MEA is related to the continuous work hardening ability of the

5. Conclusion 
In the current work, a novel non-equiatomic Ni46Co22Fe23Al3Ti7 
MEA was fabricated. After systematically studying the phase composi -
tion, microstructure, mechanical properties at room and high tempera -
ture of the alloy, the following conclusions can be drawn. 
1. The Ni46Co22Fe23Al3Ti7 MEA is composed of equiaxed grains 
containing a large amount of L1
2 
nanoprecipitations. The size of the 
grain and the size of the L1
2 
particles in the as-aged Al3Ti7 MEA is 
much smaller than that of the as-cast Al3Ti7 MEA. 
2. The as-cast Al3Ti7 MEA has high room temperature strength, but the 
plasticity is poor. The as-aged Al3Ti7 MEA has excellent strength- 
ductility synergy at room temperature. And the non-recrystallized 
Al3Ti7 MEA has the highest room temperature strength and the 
worst elongation. 
3. The high temperature tensile properties of the as-aged alloy is no 
attractive, with not high strength and poor elongation. The brittle -
ness of the as-aged alloy at high temperature is maybe due to the 
high-volume fraction of grain boundaries. 
4. The non-recrystallized alloy with coarse elongated grain has better 
high temperature tensile properties than that of as-aged alloy, 
especially has higher elongation.
