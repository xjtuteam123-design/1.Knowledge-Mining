abstract
The microstructure and mechanical properties of as-cast Mg 97.1Zn1Gd1.8Zr0.1 (at%) alloy processed by
multi-pass equal channel angular pressing (ECAP) at 375 1C were investigated. The results show that
besides the shattering process of β phases and dynamic recrystallization (DRX) phenomenon, the
reﬁning and re-distribution process of ﬁne-lamellae LPSO phases is the other important feature of
microstructure evolution with increasing ECAP passes. Both strength and ductility of the alloy are
improved simultaneously with increasing ECAP passes. Notably, after 16 ECAP passes, the ultimate tensile
strength, yield strength and elongation to failure of the alloy can reach 387 MPa, 324 MPa and 23.2%,
respectively, which can be attributed to numerous units, mainly consisting of strip-distributed
β phase
particles, micro-sized and ultra- ﬁned grains, high-density dislocations, and a large number of 14H LPSO
microcells.

1. Introduction
Magnesium alloys based on the Mg –TM–RE (TM ¼Zn, Cu, or Ni;
RE¼rare earth metals) alloy system have attracted increasing
attention due to their excellent mechanical properties [1,2]. The
famous RS P/M Mg 97Zn1Y2 (at%) alloy exhibits extraordinary high
yield strength over 600 MPa at room temperature because of the
effects of grain re ﬁnement and the presence of a unique long
period stacking ordered (LPSO) structure [3]. Thereafter, several
conventional casting methods/processes have been applied to
fabricate the LPSO-containing magnesium alloys [4–6]. Homma
et al. have reported that the ultimate tensile strength (UTS) and
elongation to failure of Mg
95.5Gd1.8Y1.8Zn0.7Zr0.2 (at%) alloy, which
were prepared through an ingot metallurgy process with hot
extrusion and subsequent aging, can reach 542 MPa and 8%,
respectively [7]. Xu et al. have prepared a Mg –8.2Gd–3.8Y–
1.0Zn–0.4Zr (wt%) alloy by large-strain hot rolling and aging,
whose UTS and elongation to failure can reach 517 MPa and
4.5%, respectively [8].
However, although many LPSO-containing magnesium alloys
can achieve high strength over 350 MPa through conventional hot-
extrusion or rolling process, their processing time is relatively
long, and more importantly, most of their elongation values are
less than 10% at room temperature.
Equal-channel angular pressing (ECAP) has been proven to be
an effective method to re ﬁne the microstructure and improve the
corresponding mechanical properties of pure magnesium and
magnesium alloys [9–11 ]. In this paper, the magnesium alloy with
composition of Mg 97 .1Zn1Gd1.8Zr0.1 (at%) containing LPSO struc-
tures was ﬁrst prepared, and then was subjected to multi-pass
ECAP process. Finally, the relationships between mechanical prop-
erties and microstructures evolution were revealed by the tensile
tests and microstructure observations.
2. Experimental procedures
Alloy ingot with an actual composition of Mg
97.1Zn1Gd1.8Zr0.1
alloy (at%) was melted from commercial pure Mg and Zn (99.9 wt
%) ingots, Mg –30Gd (wt%) and Mg –33Zr (wt%) master alloys under
the protection of a mixed SF 6 (1 vol%) and CO 2 (99 vol%) atmo-
sphere in an electric resistance furnace at 750 –780 1C. The
chemical composition was determined by inductively coupled
plasma atomic emission spectrometry (ICP-AES). The billets for
the ECAP process with the dimensions of 19.6 /C2 19.6 /C2 40 mm3
were taken from the center of the ingot. ECAP process was carried
out using the die with a die angle of 90 1 and an outer arc angle of
01. The details of the ECAP process have been reported in Ref. [12].
The billets were subjected to 1, 4, 8, 12 and 16 ECAP passes,

Fig. 2 illustrates the OM and SEM images of as-cast, 4-pass and
16-pass ECAPed Mg 97 .1Zn1Gd1.8Zr0.1 alloys. In the as-cast alloy, the
network-like or island-like β-Mg5(Gd,Zn) phases with the coarse
size and skeleton structure (indicated by red arrows in Fig. 2 a and
d) and ﬁne-lamellae LPSO phases (indicated by white arrows in
Fig. 2 a) can be observed in the matrix. There is another rod-like
phase named the X-Mg 12Gd1Zn1 phase [13,14]. After 4 passes as
shown in Fig. 2 b and c, the primary β phases appear very brittle
and are easily broken into small particles, elongated along with
the shear direction. The original straight ﬁne-lamellae LPSO phases
also undergo different degrees of bending and form kink bands
(indicated by the red dotted line in Fig. 3 e) within the deformed
matrix. Furthermore, many micrometer-level equiaxed grains can
be observed, which are newly formed due to dynamic recrystalli-
zation (DRX). With increasing passes, the brittle β phases are
gradually broken into smaller particles and distribute in the matrix
more and more uniformly. After 16 passes, the alloy possesses a
homogeneous microstructure with complete recrystallization as
shown in Fig. 2c. Adjacent to the strip-distributed β phase particles
and DRX grains (marked by A in Fig. 2 f), there are numerous ﬁne-
lamellae LPSO microcells (indicated by red circles in Fig. 2 f) with
different orientations. It can be indicated that besides the shatter-
ing process of β phases and the DRX phenomenon, the re ﬁning
and re-distribution process of ﬁne-lamellae LPSO phases is the
other important feature of microstructure evolution of as-cast
Mg97 .1Zn1Gd1.8Zr0.1 alloy subjected to the multi-pass ECAP process.
Fig. 3 (a and b) illustrates the TEM images of the alloy after 16
passes. It presents that the α-Mg matrix after ECAP has been
severely re ﬁned and seems to be composed of numerous small
units (marked by red circle lines in Fig. 3 a). As shown in Fig. 3 b,
every unit mainly consists of ﬁve parts: β phase particles (trans-
parent region, marked by A), micro-sized grain (about 2 μmi n
diameter, marked by B), ultra- ﬁne grain (less than 1 μmi n
diameter, marked by C), ﬁne-lamellae LPSO microcells (marked
by D) and unde ﬁned particle (marked by E). Moreover, high-
density dislocations can be observed within these DRX grains.
Fig. 3 c shows the TEM image of ﬁne-lamellae LPSO microcell and
the corresponding selected area electron diffraction (SAED) pat-
tern. The SAED pattern demonstrates that the LPSO microcell has a
14H-type structure.
The engineering stress –strain curves of as-cast and ECAPed
Mg97 .1Zn1Gd1.8Zr0.1 (at%) alloys are shown in Fig. 4. After only ECAP
1 pass, both YS and UTS are increased by more than 100 MPa
compared with those of the as-cast one. Both the strength and
ductility are improved gradually and simultaneously with increas-
ing ECAP passes. After 16 passes, the YS, UTS and elongation to
failure reach 324 MPa, 387 MPa and 23.2%, which is 100%, 92.5%
and 364% higher than those of the as-cast one, respectively. Thus, a
magnesium alloy exhibiting good combination of strength
(4350 MPa) and ductility ( 4 20%) with also a high yield ratio
(YS/UTS) of 0.84 is successfully fabricated. These remarkable
improvements in mechanical properties can be attributed to grain
reﬁnement [15], dispersion of second phases [16] and gradually
increased LPSO microcells [17].
Fig. 5 illustrates the SEM images of fracture morphologies of
the tensile specimens. For the as-cast condition as shown in
Fig. 5 a, the fracture happens along the phase interface between
the β phases and the α-Mg matrix, where the fracture positions at
the phase interface and the cleavage planes are indicated by white
arrows. Up to 16 passes as shown in Fig. 5 b, a great number of
dimples appear on the fracture surface, which indicates the great
ductility improvement of the alloy. The small particles with the
size of about 2 μm, located in the bottom of the dimples as shown
in Fig. 5c, has been proven to be the broken β-Mg5(Gd,Zn) phases,
with the chemical composition of Mg –(5.7071.0 at%)Zn –
(9.7471.0 at%)Gd according to the EDS analysis.
4. Conclusions
Multi-pass ECAP process provides a simple and effective
procedure for microstructure homogeneity including both grain
reﬁnement and the re-dispersion of β phases. Meanwhile, the
transformation from β phases to LPSO phases is also promoted
according to the XRD results. The grain size after 16 passes is
reduced to 1 –2 μm due to the DRX effect. The tensile mechanical
properties are remarkably improved by the multi-pass ECAP
