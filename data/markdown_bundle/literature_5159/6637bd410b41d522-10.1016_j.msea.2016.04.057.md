abstract
The Nb-30Ti-18Al alloys doped with the amounts of 0 at%, 1 at% and 5 at% carbon were prepared by arc
melting and annealing. The effects of carbon addition on the microstructure and mechanical properties
of Nbss/Nb3Al in situ composites as well as the strengthening mechanisms were investigated. The results
showed that during annealing at 1200 °C, supersaturated Nb ss decomposed and Nb 3Al phase precipitated
in C-free alloy, while carbide decomposed with the precipitation of Nb 3Al in C-doped alloys. Further-
more, the orientation relationship of Nb 3Al phase and the surrounding (Nb, Ti)C was observed in Nb-
30Ti-18Al-5C (NA5) alloy. Additionally, with increasing the carbon content, the compressive strength of
alloys at high temperature were improved signi ﬁcantly ascribed to the occurrence of solid solution and
volume fraction increase of strengthen phase, while the fracture toughness decreased due to the volume
fraction decrease of Nb
ss.

1. Introduction
Due to excellent mechanical properties at the temperature
above 1200 °C, Nb-based refractory alloy has become an ideal
candidate superalloy material for high-performance structural and
advanced aerospace applications. Many researchers have focused
on the improvement of mechanical properties of Nb-Si alloy for
recent decades. So far, the isothermal sections at 1000 °C of Nb-Si-
Ti [1,2], Nb-Si-Al [3] and Nb-Si-Cr [4] have been produced.
Moreover, the inﬂuences of elements addition such as Ti, Cr, Si, Al,
Ta, Hf and Sn [5– 8] on the microstructure and mechanical prop-
erties of Nb-Si alloy were investigated. It was found that the so-
lubility of Al in Nb is large and it changes apparently with tem-
perature, which makes Nb-Al alloy possible for solid solution and
aging. As a strengthen phase of Nb-Al system, Nb
3Al exhibited a
yield stress of more than 900 MPa at 1200 °C with a strain rate of
1.67/C2 10/C0 4 s/C0 1 [9]. Additionally, a number of reinforcements such
as tungsten, molybdenum and tantalum [9] have been added to
Nb-Al alloys to improve the mechanical properties at high tem-
perature. In order to improve the fracture toughness of Nb-Al alloy
at ambient temperature, ductile niobium solid solution was in-
troduced [10,11 ]. Leonard et al. [12] investigated the phase equi-
libria at 1 100 °C in Nb-Ti-Al system, which is helpful for the
composition design to balance the high-temperature strength and
fracture toughness at ambient temperature. Nb-15Al-10(25, 40)Ti
alloys exhibiting a strain rate of 10
/C0 4 s/C0 1 at 900 °C were in-
vestigated [13], and the experimental results indicated that the
changes in microstructure and strength were attributed to the
content of Ti and aging conditions.
Due to the solid strengthening, some refractory elements such
as Mo and W were always added to Nb based alloys to increase
strength at elevated temperature [14– 16]. However, the refractory
elements would increase the density of the alloys. In order to al-
leviate this problem, some light elements such as boron and car-
bon were also introduced into Nb-based alloys. Moreover, these
light elements could also improve the mechanical properties of the
alloys. Ma et al. [14] reported that Nb-16Si-10Mo-15W alloy could
be strengthened by the addition of B due to the solution of B in the
strengthening phase. Given that the radius of C atom is smaller
than the B atom, the strengthen effect through solid solution of C
would be probably better than that of B.
Recently, much attention has been drawn to improve the mi-
crostructure and mechanical properties of a series of ternary Nb-
Ti-C alloys [17– 20]. However, rare reports on adding carbon to Nb-
Ti-Al alloys have been found in the open literature. It is still
equivocal that whether the carbon addition could improve the
mechanical properties of Nb-Ti-Al alloys and what the strength-
ening mechanisms are. The aim of this work was, therefore, to
demonstrate the effect of carbon addition on the microstructure
and mechanical properties of Nb
ss/Nb3Al in situ composites, and
identify the underlying strengthening mechanisms.

2. Experimental procedure
In the present study, high purity Nb, Ti, Al and TiC powders
were used as raw materials. According to the Nb-Ti-Al isothermal
section at 1200 °C [13], the base composition was selected in the
Nbss/Nb3Al two phases area, besides, the density of alloy was de-
signed as a range between 6 /C24 6.5 g/cm3. Thus, Nb-30Ti-18Al (at%)
was used as the base composition. The amount of 0 at%, 1 at% and
5 at% carbon was added to the base alloy by the substitution of
niobium. For indication, Nb-30Ti-18Al (at%), Nb-30Ti-18Al-1C (at%)
and Nb-30Ti-18Al-5C (at%) were represented by NA0, NA1 and
NA5, respectively. Alloy ingots were prepared by arc smelting on a
water-cooled copper crucible in argon atmosphere. In order to
ensure compositional homogeneity, each ingot was melted for ﬁve
times. Then, the heat treatment was conducted at 1200 °C for
10 min and 3 h, respectively. Finally, the prepared alloys were air-
cooled down to room temperature.
The phase and crystallinity were analyzed via X-ray diffraction
(XRD) using CuK
α radiation at 40 kV and 250 mA. The lattice
parameters were calculated by JADE 5 software. The micro-
structure of cast and heat-treated samples was characterized using
scanning electron microscopy (SEM), wave dispersion spectrum
(WDS) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM). SEM samples
were cut by electrical discharge machining (EDM) and polished to
a surface ﬁnish using 1 mm diamond paste, and TEM foils were
prepared by ion milling. The sample for secondary electron ob-
servation was etched at 25 °C for 1 min in a solution containing
4 ml HNO
3, 2 ml HF and made up to 16 ml using deionized water.
The volume fractions of phase in the alloys were calculated by the
quantitative image analysis using SEM micrographs, where ﬁve
SEM images were used for each alloy. The compression specimens
with a dimension of φ8m m /C2 12 mm were prepared and then
annealed at 1200 °C for 3 h in vacuum to evaluate the high tem-
perature strength. The compressive tests were carried out at
1200 °C in a computerized gleeble-3800 testing system to a plastic
strain of 5%, at a strain rate of 1 /C2 10/C0 3 s/C0 1 in vacuum. Three
compression samples were tested for each group alloy and the
average values were reported. Fracture toughness was determined
using a Vickers indentation technique, which could be calculated
according to the following equation [21].

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Phase transformation during annealing
Fig. 1 shows the XRD patterns of NA0, NA1 and NA5 alloys in as
cast and aging conditions. It presents that nominal compositions
(at%), actual compositions (at%) and constituent phases of Nb ss,
Nb3Al and (Nb, Ti)C were detected in the alloys, which are sum-
marized in Table 1 . Supersaturated niobium based solid solution
(Nbss) phase was observed in the cast carbon-free alloy due to the
high cooling rate. The cast structures of both NA1 and NA5 alloys
contain Nb ss phase and carbide (Nb, Ti)C. Furthermore, it can be
found that just NA5 presented Nb 3Al peak in as cast alloys, and the
intensity of Nb 3Al peak in all alloys increased after heat treatment
at 1200 °C for 3 h. It is seen from Fig. 1 that the alloys of NA1 and
NA5 after annealing consisted of Nb ss,N b3Al and a small amount of
(Nb, Ti)C, and no other phases could be identi ﬁed in the patterns.
3.2. Microstructure
This paper presents the average concentrations (at%) of ele-
ments for some phases of various conditions along with the ﬁg-
ures. However, it should be pointed out that only the phases
greater than 1 mm in size were analyzed.
3.2.1. Nb-30Ti-18Al (NA0)
Fig. 2 shows the microstructure of NA0 alloy in as cast and
annealed conditions. It can be seen from Fig. 2 (a) that the grain
boundary is clear, and no any phase precipitated in the as-cast
alloy. However, it is of interest to observe that after aging at
1200 °C for 10 min, Nb 3Al phase precipitated at the grain bound-
aries of NA0 alloy, as shown in Fig. 2 (b). When further increasing
the aging time to 3 h, the grain size and volume fraction of Nb 3Al
increased markedly. It is attributed to the further decomposition of
Nbss due to the phase equilibra at 1200 °C in the Nb-Ti-Al system.
Furthermore, the Nb 3Al appeared not only at grain boundaries but
also in the interior of Nb ss grains ( Fig. 2 (c)).
3.2.2. Nb-30Ti-18Al-1C (NA1)
Fig. 3 shows the backscattered electron micrographs of NA1
alloy. It indicated that with the addition of 1 at% carbon in as cast
alloy, (Nb, Ti)C mainly distributed at grain boundaries of Nb ss
grains ( Fig. 3 (a)). After aging at 1200 °C for 10 min, the Nb 3Al
phase was precipitated and a part of carbides were dissolved.
Comparing to the carbon-free alloy ( Fig. 2 (b)), more precipitated
Nb3Al phase was distributed in the interior of Nb ss grains ( Fig. 3
(b)). This might be associated with the higher growth rate of Nb 3Al
in NA1 alloy than that in NA0 alloy. Because of the different lattice
parameters of Nb ss and (Nb, Ti)C phases, the energy of Nb ss/(Nb,
Ti)C phase boundary is higher than that of Nb ss grain boundary.
After aging at 1200 °C for 3 h, most of (Nb, Ti)C phase appeared at
grain boundary. Meanwhile, the grain size and volume fraction of
Nb3Al increased.

3.2.3. Nb-30Ti-18Al-5C (NA5)
Fig. 4 shows the microstructure of NA5 alloy in various condi-
tions. It can be seen from Fig. 4 (a) that, both primary and sec-
ondary carbides were observed in as cast alloy. Ding et al. [18]
reported that eutectic products were observed in Nb-10.5 at% C
alloy. It indicated that the addition of Ti and Al in the alloy lowered
the eutectic C content. Besides, a part of Nb 3Al around the carbide
was observed owing to the decrease of Nb content. When the alloy
was annealed at 1200 °C for 10 min, due to the decrease of carbon
solid solubility in Nb ss with decreasing temperature, ﬁne carbide
was precipitated in Nb ss phase, while no obvious change was ob-
served in Nb 3Al phase, which could be seen from Fig. 4 (b). When
further increasing the aging time to 3 h, chemical compositions of
phases changed apparently. Reduction of niobium, aluminum and
carbon were observed in Nb ss, and inverse tendency was detected
in Nb 3Al. In this investigation, the results indicated a high solu-
bility of carbon in Nb ss and Nb3Al. Similar result was also reported
in Nb-Ti-C alloys [1 7].
According to Fig. 4, the microstructure of the NA5 alloy changed
during the heat treatment at 1200 °C: (i) Nb 3Al was precipitated in
carbide and the carbide was decomposed. (ii) Nb 3Al grows in Nb ss,
resulting in the increase of Nb 3Al volume fraction and decrease of
Nbss volume fraction. It can be seen clearly from a higher magni-
ﬁcation of the backscattered electron image shown in Fig. 4 (d).
The signi ﬁcant variations of Nb 3Al after heat treatment at 1200 °C
for 3 h could be mainly ascribed to 5 at% C addition. Firstly, the
increase of carbon indicates decrease of niobium, leading to the
increase of Nb
3Al volume fraction. Secondly, Nb 3Al could pre-
cipitate and grow in the primary carbide due to the 5 at% C
addition.
Fig. 5 shows the typical TEM micrographs and selected area
diffraction patterns (SADPs) of NA5 alloy aged at 1200 °C for 3 h. It
can be seen that ﬁne (Nb, Ti)C (90 – 100 nm in width and 300 –
400 nm in length) was precipitated in Nb ss, and its SADPs along
the zone axes of [1 ¯1 0] could be seen in Fig. 5 (a). Fig. 5 (b) shows
that rod-like Nb 3Al was precipitated in the (Nb, Ti)C of the Nb-
30Ti-18Al-5C alloy. The width and length of Nb 3Al phase was 70 –
130 nm and 300 – 600 nm, respectively. The phases of Nb 3Al and
(Nb, Ti)C in NA5 alloys were also identi ﬁed by SADPs analysis as
shown in Fig. 5(c), where Nb 3Al along the zone axes of [0 0 1] and
(Nb, Ti)C along the zone axes of [1 ¯1 0] are presented. The results
were in agreement with the composition of the phases found by
XRD analysis ( Fig. 1). It also revealed that the precipitates adopt a

3.4. Mechanical properties
Table 3 shows the volume percentage of Nb ss and the me-
chanical properties such as the average compressive 0.2% ﬂow
stress at 1200 °C and fracture toughness at ambient temperature
of the annealed alloys. With the increase of carbon contents, the
compressive strengths of alloys at 1200 °C signi ﬁcantly increased,
while the fracture toughness decreased. When the addition of
carbon was 5 at%, the compressive 0.2% ﬂow stress reached the
maximum value of 90 MPa, meanwhile the fracture toughness
reached the minimum value of 3.3 MPa m 1/2. The detailed ex-
planation will be presented as following.
In the present investigation, the presence of carbon sig-
niﬁcantly improved the compressive properties of Nb-30Ti-18Al
alloy. It should be associated with the following possible
mechanisms. Firstly, the carbon in Nb ss and Nb 3Al played a key
role in solution strength to improve the high temperature strength
of alloys. Carbon atoms dissolved in both Nb ss and Nb 3Al lattice
and located predominantly at substitutional sites, which decreased
the lattice parameters. The crystal lattice distortion caused by
carbon dissolution may increase the resistance to deformation.
Then, carbide as a strengthen phase could increase the high
temperature strength [17]. The more carbon content, the larger
volume percentage of carbide, and therefore the higher strength at
1200 °C was obtained. Lastly, the compressive strength is con-
cerned with the volume fraction of strengthen phase Nb 3Al. As
mentioned above, after annealed at 1200 °C for 3 h, the solid so-
lubility of carbon in Nb 3Al is higher than that in Nb ss, leading to
the increase of Nb 3Al volume fraction and improvement of the
compressive strength at high temperature.

to the decrease of Nb ss volume fraction. Fig. 6 shows the crack
propagation behavior of alloys annealed at 1200 °C for 3 h after
Vickers indentation test. For NA0 ( Fig. 6(a) and (b)) alloy, the crack
tends to de ﬂect and propagate preferentially within the brittle
Nb3Al. Besides, the occurrence of the crack bridging in Nb ss is
