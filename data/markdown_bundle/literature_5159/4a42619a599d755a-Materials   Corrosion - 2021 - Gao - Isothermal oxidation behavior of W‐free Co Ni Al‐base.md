Abstract
Co‐based superalloys with the advantages of high melting point, high ‐
temperature mechanical properties, and large resistance under hot corrosion
environment are potential candidates for turbine engine components. The
isothermal oxidation behavior of Co –Ni–Al‐based superalloy with the addition
of Mo and Cr in dry air at 1073 and 1173 K was investigated. The results
showed that similar three ‐layer oxides structures were composed of Co ‐
containing oxides, complex oxides rich in Co and Al, and Al 2O3 layer formed
on both 2Mo and 2Cr Co –Ni–Al‐based superalloys, and Mo ‐containing oxides
also existed in the subsurface. The oxides in the outer layer transferred from
Co
3O4 to denser CoO with the increase in temperature. The γ'‐free zone
formed under the Al 2O3 layer due to the depletion of Al. Co –Ni–Al‐based
superalloys need to conquer a large energy barrier at an earlier stage of oxi-
dation and exhibit good oxidation resistance at 1073 K.

5 | CONCLUSIONS
The oxidation behavior of W ‐free Co –Ni–Al‐based
superalloys up to 256 h in dry air was systematically
investigated at 1073 and 1173 K, respectively. The
following conclusions can be drawn as:
1. 2Mo and 2Cr Co –Ni–Al‐based superalloys show a similar
three‐layers oxides structure, in which the outer oxidation
layer is composed of Co‐containing simple oxides, while
the middle oxidation layer is mainly composed of Al
2O3
and complex oxides rich in Co and Al, the inner layer is
a nI O Zw i t hA l2O3 oxides.
2. The oxidation kinetics of Co –Ni–Al‐based superalloy
obeys the parabolic law and the formation mechanism
of oxides is dominated by the counter ‐current diffu-
sions of Co, Ni, Al cations, and O anions through the
Al
2O3 layer. Diffusion of O through pores and mi-
crocracks in the oxide layer promotes the internal
oxidation progress.
3. The parabolic mass gain rates of 2Cr and 2Mo su-
peralloys are 0.16 and 0.46 mg 2/cm4h at 1073 K, and
2.49 and 5.21 mg 2/cm4h at 1173 K, respectively. The
oxidation resistance of 2Mo superalloy is weaker than
that of 2Cr superalloy.
4. The calculated oxidation activation energies of 2Cr
and 2Mo alloys were 272.42 and 205.97 KJ/mol,
respectively. Co –Ni–Al‐based superalloys need
to conquer a large energy barrier during oxidation
and exhibit good oxidation resistance at
1073 K.
