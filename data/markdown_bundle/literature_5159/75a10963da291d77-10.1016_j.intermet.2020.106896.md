ABSTRACT 
A multi-step solution heat treatment regime of a Mo-rich Ni based single crystal superalloy (Alloy Mo) was 
designed, and the effect of each solution heat treatment step on microstructure, microsegregation degree and 
phase transformation characteristic temperatures of Alloy Mo was investigated by experimental analysis and 
dynamic calculation (via DICTRA). Homogenizations of microstructure and Re element in as-cast Alloy Mo were 
obtained via first step solution heat treatment (lowest temperature and shortest time) and last step solution heat 
treatment (highest temperature and longest time), respectively. In addition, appropriate diffusion length for 
dynamic calculation of homogenization of Re element was determined. Meanwhile, solution heat treatment 
increased the solidus temperature of as-cast Alloy Mo, while it had less effect on liquidus temperature and γ
0
solvus temperature. Moreover, as compared with W-rich single crystal superalloys, effects of solution heat 
treatment on both solidus temperature and γ
0
solvus temperature of Mo-rich single crystal superalloy were lower. 
Additionally, γ
0
solvus temperatures of Mo-rich single crystal superalloys were lower than those of W-rich single 
crystal superalloys. 
1. Introduction 
Ni based single crystal (SC) superalloy is an important material for 
hot end components in aero-engines and has been widely used in the 
production of SC turbine blades [ 1 – 6 ]. In recent years, in order to 
improve the mechanical properties of Ni based SC superalloys, a large 
amount of refractory elements (e.g. Re and W) have been added to SC 
superalloys [ 1 – 3 , 7 ]. Although the addition of Re and W elements 
effectively improves the high temperature creep properties of Ni based 
SC superalloy, the addition of Re and W leads to the following problems: 
(1) Higher density: Re and W are both high density elements, higher 
content of Re and W elements leads to a significant increase of 
density of SC superalloy, which is not conducive to the 
improvement of thrust-to-weight ratio of advanced aero-engines. 
Therefore, high density has become the bottleneck of the devel -
opment of advanced SC superalloy [ 8 ]. 
(2) Higher difficulty of solution heat treatment (SHT): on one hand, 
Re and W elements obviously segregated to dendritic region 
during solidification. On the other hand, the interdiffusion co -
efficients of both Re and W elements in γ -Ni are very low [ 1 ]. 
Therefore, the microsegeration of Re and W elements in as-cast 
SC superalloy is difficult to eliminate via SHT [ 9 , 10 ]. Taking 
1st generation SC superalloy CMSX-2 and 2nd generation SC su -
peralloy CMSX-4 as example, the content of Re elements 
increased from 0 to 3 wt%, and the SHT time correspondingly 
increased from 3 h to 18 h [ 9 ]. 
As mentioned above, Ni based SC superalloys with high W and high 
Re contents have the disadvantages of high density and high difficulty of 
SHT. Therefore, it is necessary to design a new type of SC superalloys 
with lower density [ 8 , 11 ], lower microsegregation degree and thus 
lower difficulty of SHT.

superalloys 
Table 3 summarized the SHT regimes (temperature range and total

typical 2nd and 3rd generation Ni based SC superalloys. 
As shown in Table 3 , in general, Re content increased from 2nd 
generation SC superalloys to 3rd generation SC superalloys. Therefore, 
in order to effectively eliminate the microsegregation degree of Re 
element, both temperature and time of SHT generally increased from 
2nd generation SC superalloys to 3rd generation SC superalloys. More -
over, as given in Table 3 , in order to avoid incipient melting during SHT, 
the highest temperatures of SHT were all lower than solidus tempera -
tures of as-cast SC superalloys. 
However, with regard to different SC superalloys, SHT regimes of 
which were obviously different. For example, as given in Table 3 , tem -
perature and time of SHT in CMSX series SC superalloys (i.e. CMSX-4 
and CMSX-10) were obviously higher than those of in Rene series SC 
superalloys (i.e. Rene N5 and Rene N6). That is to say, there were 
different SHT strategies for different series of SC superalloys. The 
elimination of IDPs and microsegeration (via SHT) will be discussed 
separately: 
(1) Residual IDPs were found to become a crack source under stress, 
and thus significantly deteriorated the creep properties of the SC 
superalloy [ 37 , 38 ]. Therefore, in order to ensure the service 
performance of SC superalloy, IDPs should be completely or at 
least basically eliminated after SHT. 
(2) With regard to microsegeration degree of alloying elements after 
SHT, different SC superalloys have different strategies, taking 
CMSX-4 and CMSX-10 as examples, both of them were solution 
heat treated for a relatively long time to effectively eliminate 
microsegregation degree of alloying elements [ 9 , 15 ]. However, 
the developers of Rene N6 believed that long-time SHT is not 
conducive to the microstructure stability and creep properties of 
Rene N6 [ 30 ]. 
On one hand, both Re and W elements were strong topologically 
close packed (TCP) phase forming elements [ 1 , 11 , 39 ], on the other 
hand, both Re and W elements had severe microsegregation degree in 
as-cast SC superalloys [ 9 , 15 – 18 ]. Therefore, the necessity of long-time 
SHT was closely related to composition of SC superalloy and/or the 
intrinsic TCP phase forming tendency. Take CMSX-10 and Rene N6 
(typical 3rd generation SC superalloys) as examples, the SHT time of 
CMSX-10 was very long (45 h), while TCP phases still precipitated after 
long-term aging [ 39 ]. In contrast, the SHT time of Rene N6 was very 
short (6 h), and TCP phase was not precipitated after long-term aging 
[ 30 ]. That is to say, with regard to SC superalloys with relatively high 
TCP phase forming tendency (e.g. CMSX-10), long-time SHT is necessary 
to effectively eliminate the microsegregation degree of Re and W ele -
ments, and thus decreased the TCP phase forming tendency of SC su -
peralloy during long-term service. In contrast, with regard to SC 
superalloy with relatively low TCP phase forming tendency (e.g. Rene 
N6), although Re and W elements still had relatively severe micro -
segregation degree after short-time SHT, due to relatively low intrinsic 
TCP phase formation tendency, the residual microsegregation degree 
had less effect on the microstructure stability of this kind of SC super -
alloy during long-term service.

been detailed discussed in Ref. [ 13 , 50 ]. 
On one hand, as compared with other alloying elements in Ni based 
SC superalloys, Re element has the lowest interdiffusion coefficient in 
γ -Ni [ 18 ]. On the other hand, Re element has severe microsegregation 
degree in as-cast Re-containing SC superalloy [ 9 , 10 , 15 – 18 ]. Therefore, 
the homogenization of Re element (via SHT) was very difficult [ 9 , 10 , 
15 – 18 ], even in as-cast Alloy Mo with relatively low microsegregation 
degree of Re element, high temperature and long term SHT was still 
necessary to basically achieve homogenization of Re element. 
Except for Re element, the homogenization process of W element also 
deserves attention. On one hand, W element also had severe micro -
segregation degree in as-cast SC superalloy [ 9 , 15 – 18 ], on one hand, the 
interdiffusion coefficient of W element in γ -Ni was only higher than that 
of Re element in γ -Ni (as shown in Fig. 6 ). Consequently, the homoge -
nization of W element was also relatively difficult, especially in some 
W-rich SC superalloys, e.g. in the W-rich (8.0 wt%) 2nd generation SC 
superalloy DD6, after three-step SHT, the microsegregation degree of W 
element was even severer than that of Re element [ 16 ]. 
It is worth noting that in multi-competent SC superalloys, interaction 
between different alloying elements might occur during the diffusion 
process (e.g. SHT), and thus affected mobility of atoms and interdiffu -
sion coefficients of alloying elements. Nevertheless, the interdiffusion 
coefficients of both Re and W elements in γ -Ni were much lower than 
other alloying elements (as given in Fig. 6 ), and thus other alloying el -
ements had less effect on the mobility of Re and W atoms, i.e. the 
diffusion behaviors of Re and W elements in Ni based superalloy were 
mainly determined by their own interdiffusion coefficients and their 
distribution behaviors (microsegregation degrees) in as-cast SC super -
alloy. Similar simplified processing had also been adopted in other in -
vestigations [ 18 , 51 ], and was proved to be reasonable. 
As mentioned above, as compared with W-rich SC superalloys, on 
one hand, the microsegregation degree of Re element in Mo-rich SC 
superalloys was lower, on the other hand, the interdiffusion coefficient 
of W element in γ -Ni is lower than that of Mo element in γ -Ni. Conse -
quently, substituting Mo for W decreased the difficulty of homogeniza -
tion of high refractory elements during SHT. 
3.5.3. Phase transformation characteristic temperatures 
The influence of SHT on liquidus temperature of Alloy Mo was 
limited, which was similar to previous studies on W-rich SC superalloys 
(CMSX-4 [ 21 ] and CMSX-10 [ 9 ]). 
Table 4 showed SHT regime, typical composition and SHT response 
of solidus temperature of some W-rich and Mo-rich SC superalloys. As 
given in Table 4 , CMSX-10, CMSX-4 and Alloy Mo were all solution heat 
treated at relatively high temperature and relatively long time. How -
ever, the increment in solidus temperature of W-rich SC superalloys 
(CMSX-4 [ 21 ] and CMSX-10 [ 9 ]) were obviously higher than that of 
Mo-rich SC superalloy (Alloy Mo). The reason was closely related with 
microsegregation degree of Re element in as-cast SC superalloys. On one 
hand, Re element significantly segregated to dendritic region [ 9 , 15 – 18 ]. 
On the other hand, based on Ni – Re binary phase diagrams [ 52 ], the 
addition of Re element increased melting point of γ phase. Therefore, in 
Re-containing SC superalloy, the melting point of dendritic region was 
higher than that of interdendritic region, i.e. initial melting point of 
interdendritic region was the solidus temperature of SC superalloy. 
Consequently, decrease microsegregation degree of Re element 
increased solidus temperature of as-cast Re-containing SC superalloy. As 
given in Fig. 9 , the microsegregation degree of Re element in Mo-rich SC 
superalloys was obviously lower than that in W-rich SC superalloys, and 
thus the difference between initial melting point of dendritic region and 
interdendritic region in as-cast Mo-rich SC superalloys was smaller than 
that of W-rich SC superalloys. As mentioned above, the influence of SHT 
on solidus temperature of Mo-rich SC superalloys was lower than that of 
W-rich SC superalloys.
