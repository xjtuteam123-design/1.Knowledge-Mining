ABSTRACT 
Refractory complex concentrated alloys (RCCAs) have great potential for applications in the nuclear industry due 
to their exceptional strength at high temperatures, as well as their fair corrosion resistance and abrasion resis -
tance. However, the limited ductility of RCCAs at room temperature hinders their processing and engineering 
applications. To cope with this challenge, a novel design framework was utilized, which integrated machine 
learning and bi-level optimization techniques, to design novel RCCAs for cladding materials. In order to address 
the challenge of a large composition space of RCCAs and the difficulty of establishing a direct model between 
composition and properties, bi-level optimization was employed. This was accomplished by establishing map -
ping relationships between composition-microparameters and microparameters-properties, respectively. 
Furthermore, Pareto optimization was implemented to achieve a compromise between high-temperature ulti -
mate strength and ambient temperature ductility. Ultimately, according to experimental validation, A1 alloy 
exhibited a superior compression strain limit (49.8 %) and ultimate strength (1994.6 MPa) at ambient tem -
perature. The desired RCCA was found to outperform existing materials in terms of target mechanical properties. 
This approach can not only uncover the indirect relationship between composition and properties but also 
achieve a trade-off between strength and ductility. 
1. Introduction 
Refractory complex concentrated alloys (RCCAs) constitute an 
innovative class of alloys, assembled from five or more refractory metal 
elements such as Ti, V, Cr, Zr, Nb, Mo, Hf, Ta, W, and others [1] . These 
metals constitute the main compositions of the alloy, and their molar 
fractions range from 5 ~ 35 % [2,3] .The diversity of composition 
combinations and constituent ratios in RCCAs furnishes a latent op -
portunity to obtain superior properties, like fair corrosion resistance and 
abrasion resistance, outstanding hardness and strength at room tem -
perature, high melting point, and excellent high-temperature strength 
over a wide temperature range from 800 ℃ to 1600 ℃ [1,4 – 7] . RCCAs 
have garnered significant interest from scholars, positioning them as 
potential contenders for next-generation high-temperature structural 
materials [8 – 10] . However, the vast composition space presents a 
challenge in alloy design, especially when aiming to achieve both 
enhanced and broad-ranging properties concurrently through “ trial and 
error ” or methods based on expert intuition [11 – 13] . Therefore, it is 
necessary to search for efficient and time-saving alloy design strategies. 
Some significant efforts have been made to utilize materials genome 
engineering to guide the process of alloy design, such as the calculated 
phase diagram (CALPHD) approach, molecular dynamics, density 
functional theory (DFT), and machine learning [14 – 18] . Zheng et al. 
predicted the elastic properties of seventy different compositions and 
discovered the optimized composition of a MoNbTaWV system by first- 
principles calculation [14] . In addition, researchers have also integrated 
machine learning (ML) and data mining into alloy design, aiming to 
enhance efficiency and reduce the research and development cycle 
[19 – 21] . By combining machine learning models with experimental 
validation, researchers have successfully demonstrated that the 
composition space of high entropy alloys can be effectively explored. 
Ujjawal et al. applied seven algorithms to predict phase formation in the 
CoCuFeNi

all of the testing data achieved an accuracy rate of over 80 % [19] . 
Machine learning enabled the efficient identification of novel alloy 
compositions and optimization of phase composition, leading to signif -
icant advances in property enhancement and alloy design. Brent et al. 
[22] employed a parameter-constrained high-throughput screening

process, leading to cost and time savings. 
2. Material and methods 
2.1. Design strategy 
A design technique combining Pareto optimization with BLO was 
applied to optimize both of ambient-temperature ductility and high- 
temperature ultimate strength in RCCAs systems, as shown in Fig. 1 . 
This design strategy consists of four major compositions: database 
construction, feature selection, creation of machine learning (ML) 
models, and bi-level optimization. Alloy compositions and microscopic 
parameters were gathered from publications to create the initial data -
base. Furthermore, feature descriptors were carefully selected to iden -
tify the microscopic information with the greatest influence on the 
ambient-temperature ductility and high-temperature ultimate 
strength. Once the database is featurized and normalized, several ma -
chine learning models were compared. The most accurate surrogate 
model was chosen to establish the relationship between microscopic 
parameters and mechanical properties. Additionally, a bi-level optimi -
zation problem was employed to search approximately for alloy com -
positions. In the upper problem, the constructed mappings between the 
selected descriptors and properties were used to find the Pareto solu -
tions for the two properties, as well as theirs corresponding Pareto 
optimal set of features. In the lower problem, genetic optimization al -
gorithm was used to search the search for the optimal combination of 
alloy composition that satisfied the mapping relationship between the 
feature descriptors and alloy compositions.

2.2.4. Multi-objective bi-level optimization 
Due to the discrete distribution of data in the composition space of 
the alloy sample, establishing a direct mapping relationship between 
compositions and properties is challenging. This challenge leads to low 
model accuracy and poor predictive performance. To resolve this issue, a 
bi-level optimization approach (BLO) was employed to establish an in -
direct mapping relationship between alloy compositions and mechanical 
properties. The BLO has discovered an optimal combination of decision 
variables that effectively tackled the trade-off of ductility at 25 ℃ and 
ultimate strength at 1000 ℃ . Typically, a bi-level problem consists two 
levels of optimization problems, one of which is nested within another. 
The lower-level optimization problem acts as a constraint for the upper- 
level optimization problem. Therefore, only the ones that comply with 
the lower-level optimum and fulfil the upper-level requirements simul -
taneously are deemed feasible [37,38] . In this study, the lower-level 
problem is influenced by the alloy compositions obtained, while the 
microscopic parameters acquired from the upper-level problem serve as 
output. Moreover, the obtained alloy compositions affect both the 
objective function and mechanical properties of the upper-level prob -
lem. Throughout this whole problem, microscopic parameters have been 
exploited as a bridge to establish mapping relationships between alloy 
composition and properties. 
Firstly, the upper-level problem focused on constructing distinct 
mappings between feature descriptors and mechanical properties. The 
feature descriptors, which capture the relevant mechanical properties of 
the alloys, were utilized as inputs to BPNN models to predict the me -
chanical properties of the alloys. Then, the NSGA-II algorithm was 
employed to conduct a global search and obtain the Pareto front, along 
with its corresponding Pareto optimal set of features for the two prop -
erties [39] . Here, the term “ Pareto ” refers to a set of ideal trade-off so -
lutions. Subsequently, the NSGA-II algorithm was executed to identify 
the Pareto optimal features that simultaneously satisfied the two 
mentioned properties. 
Secondly, the lower-level problem aimed to establish the mapping 
relationship between feature descriptors and alloy compositions. The 
feature descriptors obtained from the upper-level problem were used as 
targets for genetic optimization algorithms in order to search the cor -
responding alloy compositions. The genetic optimization algorithm 
utilized a process of random selection, crossover, and mutation to search 
for the optimal combination of alloy composition parameters that 
satisfied the desired mapping. By utilizing genetic optimization algo -
rithms, the compositions were iteratively adjusted and optimized in 
accordance with the mapping relationship between the feature de -
scriptors and alloy compositions. Through this iterative process, the 
compositions were refined to achieve a more precise representation of 
the mapping relationship. 
Overall, by addressing the optimization problems at the two afore -
mentioned levels, the desired alloy compositions were obtained, 
yielding values that closely approximate the Pareto optimal solutions 
and achieving a compromise between two conflicting mechanical 
properties. Furthermore, it demonstrated high levels of accuracy and 
predictive performance in establishing the correlation between feature 
descriptors and alloy performance.

3.3. Establishment of bi-level optimization 
The application of the bi-level optimization approach has made it 
possible to predict high-temperature ultimate strength and ambient- 
temperature ductility in the field of complex concentrated alloys. 
Since high-temperature ultimate strength and ambient-temperature 
ductility were two conflicting targets, improving either objective func -
tion on the basis of one of the non-dominated solutions may necessarily 
weaken at least one other objective function. By incorporating a 
crowding distance operator to maintain population diversity and an 
elitism strategy, NSGA-II can effectively balance the trade-off between 
maintaining diversity and preserving good solutions, whilst simulta -
neously expediting convergence [46] . Therefore, these Pareto solutions 
had the least number of conflicting objectives compared to other solu -
tions and provided a better choice space [47] , and the results were 
depicted by the blue points in Fig. 5 . The Pareto front line also showed a 
clear trade-off between ambient-temperature ductility and high- 
temperature ultimate strength, which were selected as the macro -
scopic performance objectives in this study. In addition, the use of multi- 
objective optimization improved the rationality of the alloy composi -
tion. And then, the composition that approximates the Pareto solutions 
the most was found out using a genetic algorithm, as shown by the red 
point in Fig. 5 . This demonstrates the practicality of using a bi-level 
optimization method to identify compositions that achieve a balance 
between objectives and closely align with desired performance criteria. 
Overall, the bi-level optimization method, by establishing a bridge 
through micro-level parameters, indirectly established the connection 
between composition and performance in complex concentrated alloys. 
The predicted properties of candidate alloys with the optimal compo -
sition obtained, however, still exhibit a certain gap from the Pareto 
optimal due to the two approximations. Based on the domain knowl -
edge, this approach is interpretable and scientific, providing a viable and 
effective method for attaining novel alloys with enhanced performance.

4. Summary 
In this study, a novel design strategy, incorporating Pareto optimi -
zation and BLO, was developed to identify novel RCCAs. Through 
feature selection, six microscopic parameters- G, F, Hv, D.G, Λ , Δ χ 
(Allen) were carefully selected as key input features for constructing the 
surrogate models. This approach facilitates to interpret the correlation 
between composition and mechanical property. Additionally, NSGAII 
algorithm was formulated to optimize these conflicting properties and 
search for the Pareto optimal solutions. As the predicted results shown, 
the designed alloys provide superior performance to apply for reactor 
vessel alloys in terms of thermal neutron absorption, high-temperature 
strength, ambient-temperature ductility, and hardness. Through exper -
imental validation, A1 exhibited an outstanding ductility-strength (US 
= 1994.8 MPa, ε
f
= 49.8 %). However, further experimental validation at 
high temperature is required to confirm the predicted superiority of A1- 
A5 and explore their potential applications. Overall, this method ac -
celerates the alloy design process, reduces the time and resources 
required compared to traditional trial-and-error methods, and simulta -
neously optimizes multiple conflicting properties, thereby advancing 
the field of material design.
