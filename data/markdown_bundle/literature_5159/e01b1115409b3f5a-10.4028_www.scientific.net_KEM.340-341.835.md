Keywords: Texture, internal variable theory, creep, Ti-6Al-4V alloy. 
Abstract. Textures developed during hot rolling process may affect the high temperature deformation 
behaviors of Ti alloys, but their relation has not been well understood or quantitatively analyzed yet. 
A series of load relaxation and creep tests for hot rolled Ti-6Al-4V alloy has been conducted in this 
work to clarify the effect of textures on the deformation behaviors of the alloy under 700 °C and the 
result was analyzed by using an internal variables approach. The internal strength σ* was found to 
vary significantly by the textures, but not by the temperature change, while the texture effect was found 
to decrease at higher temperatures. 
Introduction 
The deformation behaviors of Ti-6Al-4V alloy depend on microstructures as well as textures 
developed during the prior deformation processes. The α phase texture is generally known to strongly 
affect the deformation behaviors up to about 600 °C, having limited available slip systems under this 
temperature. The sheet products manufactured by a hot rolling process exhibit a strong deformation 
texture depending significantly on the rolling temperature, microstructure, and phase volume fraction 
of the constituent phases. It is therefore necessary to analyze the formation and evolution of texture 
from the various initial microstructures during hot rolling process to quantify the relation between 
texture and deformation behaviors. To this end, the texture of a Ti alloy was measured by XRD pole 
figure method and the deformation behaviors were analyzed by an internal variables approach [1].

rate α and internal strain rate ε are then regarded to develop through a mechanical activation process

Experimental procedures 
The commercial Ti-6Al-4V alloy was first homogenized at 1050 °C for 30 minutes before cooling 
with the various cooling rates, viz. water cooling (WC), air cooling (AC), and furnace cooling (FC) to 
produce different microstructures with various α phase thicknesses [3, 4]. The alloy was then hot 
rolled at 700 °C with 60 % reduction before annealing at 800 °C for an hour. After hot rolling, 
microstructures of the alloys were again observed by an optical microscope and the deformation 
texture of α phase developed during hot rolling was measured for (0002) plane by XRD pole figure. 
A series of load relaxation tests has been conducted to obtain the flow curves at the various 
temperatures between 500 °C and 700 °C for specimens with different orientations and cooling rates. 
Load relaxation test can provide stress-strain rate relations in a much broader range of strain rates with 
minimal plastic straining [5, 6], enabling to obtain the flow curves without changing the internal 
structure appreciably [7]. The relaxation data were then consequently analyzed utilizing the internal 
variable theory for inelastic deformation [1]. Creep tests were also performed to obtain the data in 
lower strain rate regime and the results were then compared with the relaxation test results.

Summary 
The effects of microstructures and textures on the deformation behaviors of Ti-6Al-4V alloy were 
examined in this study by performing a series of load relaxation and creep tests together with the pole 
figure measurement of deformation textures developed during hot rolling. The internal strength σ* 
was found to vary significantly by the textures, but not by temperature change, while the texture effect 
was found to decrease at higher temperatures. The effect of texture was also observed to decrease by 
raising test temperature or by reducing the applied stress in creep tests, suggesting that glide assisted 
dislocation climb was activated at higher temperature and lower stress.
