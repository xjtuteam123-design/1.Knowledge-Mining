Abstract: The effect of low temperature on tensile properties of a AlCoCrFeNi 2.1 
eutectic high entropy alloy (EHEA) was investigated in the present work. 
Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) showed that the initial as-cast 
microstructure consisted of B2 (ordered body centered cubic structure) and L1 2 
(ordered face centered cubic structure) phases. Upon tensile testing at temperatures 
ranging from room temperature (RT) to -196˚C (77K), the L1 2 phase became 
disordered, changing to a simple FCC (face centered cubic) crystal structure 
whereas the B2 phase maintained an ordered structure. An increase over 300MPa 
was observed in the ultimate tensile strength ( UTS) of the -196˚C tensile tested 
sample compared to the room temperature tensile tested sample, while keeping 
almost similar amount of total elongation. TEM results indicated an increase in the 
dislocation activity in the FCC phase as well as B2 phase in the -196˚C tensile 
tested sample compared to the sample deformed at room temperature.
Keywords: eutectic high entropy alloy, low temperature tensile test, 
nanoindentation, TEM

1. Introduction
A new class of the alloys has emerged in the last decade, which are termed as “high 
entropy alloys (HEAs)” by Yeh. et al. [1-3]. HEAs are defined as the alloys which contain 
at least five major elements, each having a concentration ranging from 5 to 35 at. % [1]. 
HEAs can often show rather simple phases, such as single phase FCC or BCC [4] or HCP 
solid solution [5, 6] as well as dual phase FCC+BCC structures [7, 8], which has been 
linked to high configurational entropy that decreases the free energy of solid solutions 
enough to stabilize their formation. HEAs have been reported to possess many promising 
properties, such as excellent cryogenic mechanical properties [9-11], outstanding wear 
resistance [2] and excellent thermal stability [12]. In order to further enhance the 
mechanical properties of HEAs, dual or multi-phase HEAs having mixtures of soft and 
hard phases have been investigated in a AlCoCrFeNi 2.1 HEA having a lamellar eutectic 
structure [13]. It has been also reported recently that thermo-mechanical processing can 
be effectively used for tailoring the microstructure and mechanical properties of the 
AlCoCrFeNi2.1 eutectic high entropy alloy (EHEA) [14, 15]. However there have been 
few works on the effect of low temperature on the mechanical properties of this alloy 
which is very essential for wider applications especially in cryogenic environments [16].

In this study, we investigate the effect of deformation temperature ranging from room 
temperature down to liquid nitrogen temperature, -196˚C (77K), on tensile properties of 
the AlCoCrFeNi 2.1 eutectic high entropy alloy (EHEA) and discuss the mechanical 
properties in relation with the nanoindentation hardness and microstructural 
characteristics. 
2. Experimental methods
The EHEA AlCoCrFeNi2.1 was prepared using arc-melting in a Ti-gettered high-purity 
argon atmosphere starting with high purity elements (≥99.9%). In order to ensure high 
level of chemical homogeneity, the melting process was repeated for at least five times. 
The melt was drop-cast into a copper mold with dimensions of 15 mm (width) x 90 mm 
(length) x 3 mm (thickness). Tensile tests were performed using a Shimadzu Autograph 
tensile test machine (Shimadzu Instruments, Japan) at temperatures ranging from room 
temperature to liquid nitrogen temperature (-196˚C) at an initial strain rate of 8.3 × 10 -4 
s-1 to characterize mechanical properties of the materials at different temperatures. Tensile 
specimens with a thickness of ~0.8mm having a gage length of 2 mm and gage width of 
1 mm were cut from the as-cast sample. Two tensile specimens were tested for each 
temperature. The gage length was marked by Vickers microhardness indents before

tensile testing to measure the elongation accurately after fracture [12]. Microstructural 
characterization was carried out using a JEOL JSM 7100F scanning electron microscope 
(SEM), a JEOL JEM 2010 transmission electron microscope (TEM) (JEOL Ltd., Japan) 
and a JEOL JEM-2100F microscope equipped with energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy 
(EDS) for chemical analysis. Samples for the TEM investigations were prepared using 
mechanical polishing followed by electropolishing (electrolyte: 90% ethanol + 10% 
perchloric acid). The TEM observations for tensile tested samples were performed from 
the transverse direction of the tensile specimens on cross sections of the gage part which 
were approximately 1mm from the fractured area. The nano-indentation mapping tests 
were conducted using TI950 Triboindenter (Hysitron, Minneapolis, USA). As in our 
previous report, indents were performed using a sharp cube-corner probe with an apex 
radius of curvature of approximately 40 nm (Hysitron, USA), in a 22 × 22 square grid 
with an indent-indent spacing of 100 nm (mapping area of 2.1 × 2.1 μm 2 ) [17]. During 
each indent, load on the probe was increased linearly from 1 μN to 500 μN over a period 
of 0.25 s, held constant for 0.25 s, and unloaded to 1 μN over a period of 0.25 s. After 
unloading, the probe was moved to the next indent position.

 Engineering stress-strain curves at different deformation temperatures are shown in 
Figure 1. The specimens showed similar shapes of stress-strain curves characterized by 
yielding, strain-hardening and fracture with little necking. At room temperature (RT), the 
AlCoCrFeNi2.1 alloy showed a yield strength ( YS) of 620 MPa, with ultimate tensile 
strength (UTS) of 1155 MPa, and total elongation of 20%. When the testing temperature 
decreased to -90˚C and -150˚C, the yield strengths increased to 675 MPa and 697 MPa, 
respectively, and tensile strengths increased to 1183 MPa and 1319 MPa, respectively. 
However, the total elongation did not change significantly from the RT deformation but 
maintained 18.3% and 19.5 % at -90°C and -150°C, respectively. When conducting the 
test at liquid nitrogen temperatures close to -196˚C, the yield strength further increased 
to 857 MPa, the tensile strength increased to 1461 MPa, while the total elongation kept a 
value of 17%. It should be noted that an increase of ~300 MPa in the tensile strength was 
achieved by decreasing the deformation temperature from RT to -196°C. The mechanical 
properties obtained are summarized in Table 1.

4. Discussion
The effect of deformation temperature on the tensile properties was investigated for the 
AlCoCrFeNi2.1 eutectic high entropy alloy (EHEA). It was found that with decreasing the 
deformation temperature the yield strength and tensile strength increased without a 
significant decrease in the total elongation. Similar results have been reported for other 
kinds of HEAs recently [9, 18]. Otto et al. [9] reported that a CoCrFeMnNi HEA having 
FCC single phase showed a similar temperature dependence of mechanical properties. 
Their specimen tested at liquid nitrogen temperature (-196˚C) exhibited the highest 
values of yield strength, ultimate tensile strength and elongation to fracture. The reported 
values of yield strength and ultimate tensile strength were ~580 MPa and ~1100 MPa 
respectively for a fine-grained CoCrFeMnNi HEA at -196˚C [9]. In the case of a CrCoNi 
medium entropy alloy (MEA) having FCC single phase, 50% increase in both yield

strength and ultimate tensile strength were reported when the test temperature decreased 
from RT to -196˚C [18]. The mechanical properties of the CrCoNi MEA were better than 
those of the CoCrFeMnNi HEA, and the yield strength and tensile strength reached to 
657 MPa and 1311 MPa, respectively, at -196˚C [18]. It should be noted that the present 
AlCoCrFeNi2.1 EHEA having a nano-lamellar structure composed of two different phases 
showed higher yield strength and ultimate tensile strength (857 MPa and 1461 MPa, 
respectively) at -196˚C than those of the CoCrFeMnNi HEA and the CrCoNi MEA [9, 
18]. The increased strength at -196˚C in the CoCrFeMnNi and CrCoNi alloys were 
considered to be related to nanoscale deformation twins formed during tensile testing at -
196˚C [9, 18]. According to the above mentioned reports, the nano deformation twins 
produced a high degree of work hardening resulting in high ultimate tensile strength and 
uniform elongation at -196˚C [9]. On the other hand, however, deformation twins were 
not observed in the present EHEA based on the TEM observations. A recent work by Lu 
et al. [16] also reported that twins were not formed in AlCoCrFeNi2.1 EHEA tensile tested 
at RT. According to their report, dislocations were observed in the FCC phase but no 
obvious dislocations were found in the B2 phase after tensile deformation. The result 
coincides well with our investigation. However, the present TEM investigation of the 
specimen tensile tested at -196˚C shows dislocation activities not only in the FCC phase

but also in the harder B2 phase (Figure 4(d)-(e)). The diffused interface boundaries 
decorated by many dislocations between FCC and B2 phases observed in the -196˚C 
deformed specimen (Figure 4 (a)) also indicate the high dislocation activity. The fact that 
the whole hardness distributions of the -196˚C deformed specimen shown in Figure 6 (c) 
shifted to the higher hardness side indicated that the B2 phase was strain-hardened. It is 
rather surprising that the B2 phase was plastically deformed without fracture. These 
results conclude that both soft FCC phase and hard B2 phase constructing the nano 
lamellar structure in the present HEA are plastically deformed and strain-hardened at -
196˚C, which can be related to the higher strength of the present EHEA at -196˚C than 
that at room temperature, even when nano-twins are not formed. 
 Another interesting feature reported in the CoCrFeMnNi and CrCoNi alloys was a 
monotonic increase in the total elongation with decreasing the deformation temperature. 
However, the present AlCoCrFeNi 2.1 EHEA did not show such a tendency. The total 
elongation of the EHEA did not show any considerable change when the testing 
temperature decreased from room temperature to -196˚C. The TEM results showed that 
the B2 phase remained ordered but the initial L12 phase became disordered FCC phase in 
the tensile deformation at both RT and -196˚C (Figures 3 and 4). It has been known from 
some earlier studies that disordered FCC phase is more ductile than the ordered L12 phase

[19, 20]. Another study on mechanical properties of ordered L1 2 Ni3Al and disordered 
FCC Ni3Al thin films reported that the disordered phase was found to have nearly twice 
the fracture strain as well as triple the fracture toughness compared to those of the ordered 
phase [21]. Those results suggest that the disordering of L1 2 phase during the tensile 
deformation improves the ductility of the HEA, resulting in the total elongation of 17% 
maintained even at -196˚C. The disordering of the L1 2 phase should be attributed to the 
plastic deformation of the phase, as a number of dislocations were observed within the 
FCC phase even at RT. It is, on the other hand, interesting that the B2 phase maintained 
the ordered structure even though the hard B2 phase was also deformed plastically to 
some extents and dislocation activities in B2 were clearly observed in the -196˚C 
deformed specimen.
5. Conclusions
The effect of deformation temperatures ranging from room temperature to -196˚C on 
the microstructure and mechanical properties of an AlCoCrFeNi 2.1 eutectic high entropy 
alloy (EHEA) was investigated in the present work. The as-cast EHEA showed a fine 
lamellar structure composed of L12 and B2 phases with a mean thickness of ~600 nm and 
~200 nm, respectively. It was found that both yield strength and tensile strength increased

## Page 19

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT
with decreasing the deformation temperature while total elongation maintained similar 
value of 17~20 % regardless of the deformation temperature. The yield strength and 
tensile strength at -196˚C were 857 MPa and 1461 MPa, respectively, and the increase in 
the tensile strength relative to RT reached ~300 MPa. At any deformation temperatures, 
the L12 phase became disordered FCC phase by plastic deformation in the tensile tests, 
while the B2 phase remained ordered. Nano-indentation hardness mapping indicated that 
both FCC and B2 phases were strain-hardened when tensile tested at -196˚C. The TEM 
observations of the deformed specimens confirmed dislocation activities in both phases 
at -196˚C. The high strength and large elongation at -196˚C of the present EHEA were 
considered to be attributed to the disordering of the L1 2 phase into a ductile disordered 
FCC phase and the strain-hardening of the hard B2 phase at low temperatures. The good 
tensile properties of this alloy would highly encourage their potential application in 
cryogenic environments.
