Various Al-containing ternary Laves phases with the hexagonal Cl4 structure 
and two-phase alloys of such Laves phases with the B2 phases NiAl or FeAl 
were screened with respect to lattice constants, thermal expansion, Young’s 
modulus, constitution, hardness, yield and creep in compression and bending at 
temperatures between ambient temperature and 1400°C. The Laves phases show 
higher strengths at higher temperatures than the superalloys, whereas their brittle- 
to-ductile transition temperatures are of the order of 1100°C. The alloying of 
the Laves phases with the less brittle B2 phases shows promise for developing 
high-temperature materials for applications above the temperature range of the 
superalloys. Copyright 0 1996 Elsevier Science Ltd

1 INTRODUCTION 
Inter-metallic phases are of high interest for develop 
ing new materials for structural applications at 
high temperatures. Most past and present develop- 
ments are based on the aluminides of titanium, 
nickel and iron which may be applied at tempera- 
tures up to the order of 1000°C because of insuffi- 
cient high-temperature strength and/or oxidation 
resistance at higher temperatures.‘.’ For higher 
temperatures - e.g. 1200°C - one has to rely on 
less-common intermetallic phases with higher 
strength at higher temperatures and still sufficient 
oxidation resistance. The Laves phases form the 
largest group of intermetallic phases with primarily 
metallic bonding and are hard and brittle with 
brittle-to-ductile transition temperatures of the 
order of 0.6 T,,, (T,,, = temperature of melting).3 
Reviews of the physical properties and the charac- 
teristics of the mechanical behaviour of the Laves 
phases are available.3 m5 
In particular there is a series of ternary Laves 
phases ABC, where A and B are transition metals, 
in particular refractory metals and iron-group 
metals, and C is either aluminium or silicon.6 Such 
phases may be advantageous as strengthening phases 
for high-temperature applications because they have 
sufficiently high melting temperatures; they are much

3. I. 3 Creep 
The creep characteristics of the studied Laves 
phases depend on the applied stress and tempera- 
ture as is illustrated in Fig. 5 for NbNiAl. Normal 
creep behaviour with decreasing creep rate during 
primary creep and extended secondary creep with 
constant creep rate was observed only at very high 
temperatures and low stresses whereas at higher 
stresses and lower temperatures the extended sec- 
ondary creep stage was preceded by a steep creep 
rate minimum with subsequent creep-rate increase, 
i.e. inverse primary creep. During the extended 
secondary stage damaging by microcracking is 
already observed which initiates the final tertiary 
creep. It is noted that much faster creep was observed 
for powder-metallurgically prepared NbNiAl with 
only normal primary creep, i.e. secondary creep 
was not observed even after more than 10% strain.

3.2.3 Creep 
The creep behaviour of the investigated two-phase 
alloys is closely analogous to the yield behaviour, as 
is visible in Fig. 16. After a short primary stage 
with a steep decrease of creep rate there is a rate 
minimum or first secondary stage which is more 
extended at lower stresses. Afterwards the creep rate 
increases to reach another apparent secondary stage. 
During this later stage, with larger strains abundant 
microcracking was observed. Ageing effects with 
precipitate coarsening by Ostwald ripening during 
creep were negligible. The observed creep behaviour 
is similar to that of the single-phase Laves phases 
(see Section 3.3) the more so, the higher the volume 
fraction of the Cl4 Laves phase is.
