ABSTRACT 
The initiation of microscopic defects and the microstructural stability are crucial factors in determining the 
tensile strength and failure mechanism of Ni-Co-Cr-based superalloys. This study involved the long-term thermal 
exposure of Ni-Co-Cr-based superalloys at 800 ℃ and their elevated-temperature tensile testing at 750 ℃ to 
investigate the microstructural and properties evolution. A 100 h-exposure led to the formation of a significant 
number of μ precipitates rich in refractory elements at the grain boundaries, particularly on the large-sized γ ′ 
precipitate surfaces. The intragranular γ ′ precipitates considerably coarsened with an increasing thermal expo -
sure duration while its volume fraction remained relatively constant. Cracks tended to nucleate preferentially at 
the μ phase interface during high-temperature tensile testing, leading to the breakage of the γ / γ ′ precipitates and 
formation of pores owing to crack propagation. The dislocation motion as a deformation response of the 
intragranular γ / γ ′ precipitates to tensile testing was dominated by a shearing mechanism, with a portion of the 
intragranular γ ′ precipitates deforming through dislocation shearing. The primary failure mechanism during 
high-temperature tensile testing changed from intergranular to intragranular with an increasing exposure time. 
Investigating the relationship between the phase evolution and failure mechanism will enhance our under -
standing of the fracture behavior of Ni-Co-Cr-based superalloy hot-end structural parts. 
1. Introduction 
Polycrystalline Ni-Co-Cr-based superalloys are widely used as tur -
bine disc materials in advanced aircraft engines because of their excel -
lent tensile strength and creep properties, which are mainly dependent 
on their microstructure [1 – 3] . Multiple refractory elements are added to 
these alloys to improve their strength and toughness to meet the 
increasing service requirements, such as the thrust-weight ratio [4 – 6] . 
Additionally, increasing additions of refractory elements, such as W and 
Mo, to Ni-Co-Cr-based superalloys exposed to extreme environmental 
conditions may cause brittle phases, especially the topologically 
close-packed (TCP) phase, to precipitate [7,8] . TCP phases often act as 
stress concentration sites where defects such as cracks may be formed; 
this phenomenon shortens the lifetime of structural parts [9,10] . The

particles with appropriate precipitation features, especially their size 
and volume fraction, are responsible for most of the strength of the su -
peralloys [11,12] . 
The long-term service of alloys generally degrades their micro -
structure owing to detrimental effects, such as the coarsening of intra -

on the essential mechanical properties of the structural parts [8,13] . The 
driving force for coarsening under an increasing exposure time is the 
transition between the interface and elastic strain energies [14,15] . 
Intragranular γ ′ with various sizes can affect the mechanical properties, 
such as tensile strength, owing to change in the critical shear stress [16] . 
In addition to intragranular γ ′ , significant TCP precipitation, which are 
intergranular defects, can also potentially deteriorate the mechanical 
properties owing to the depletion of solute atoms and provision of 
crack-initiating nucleation sites [7,9,17] . However, the contributions of 
intragranular γ ′ and intergranular precipitates to the grain interior and 
boundary strengths, respectively, are not yet clearly understood because

Long-term thermal exposure is a technological approach used to 
simulate the service conditions of aerospace structural components and 
analyze the stability of phases at elevated temperatures [7] . Several 
studies have investigated the microstructure and performance of su -
peralloys during long-term thermal exposure, such as the thermal sta -
bility of γ ′ precipitates and the formation of TCP phases [18 – 20] . Of 
further concern is that the shearing and bypassing stress of dislocation 
motion during tensile testing at elevated temperatures can be decreased 
due to the coarsening of intragranular γ ′ precipitates owing to long-term 
thermal exposure [21,22] . Concurrently, continuous long-term thermal 
exposure causes TCP phases, such as the μ phase, to precipitate at the 
grain boundaries (GBs), which causes significant risks at GBs during 
tensile deformation, such as the initiation and propagation of cracks [9, 
23] . Furthermore, the occurrence of TCP phases can be controlled by 
modifying the elemental ratio of the superalloy [24,25] . 
As stated above, the effect of the microstructure after long-term 
thermal exposure under various conditions on the deformation mecha -
nism and the interactions between the exposure conditions and failure 
sites during tensile testing have not been comprehensively investigated. 
This study revealed the evolution of inter- and intragranular precipitates 
under various thermal exposure conditions. The tensile deformation 
behavior at elevated temperatures, including the fracture characteris -
tics, initiation and propagation of defects, and dislocation motion 
modes, were also comprehensively studied. 
2. Materials and experiments 
A new Ni-Co-Cr-based superalloy turbine disc has been researched 
and developed owing to the continuous improvement in the aero-engine 
thrust ratio and the designed service temperature of the turbine disc. 
Therefore, a Ni-Co-Cr-based superalloy with design and over- 
temperature service temperatures of 750 and 800 ℃ , respectively, was 
used in this study, providing a reference for the required heat treatment 
temperature. The chemical composition of this alloy was as follows (wt 
%): Fe 1, Co 13, Cr 16, Mo 4, W 4, Al 2.1, Ti 3.7, Nb 0.7, and Ni balance. 
The superalloy was cast using vacuum induction melting, electroslag 
remelting in a protective atmosphere, and vacuum consumable remelt -
ing. The cast ingot was homogenized and forged into a 508 mm-diam -
eter rod. The rod was solution treated at 1120 ℃ for 2 h and then air 
cooled (AC) to room temperature. Subsequently, a two-stage heat 
treatment (680 ℃ 30 h/AC and 790 ℃ /16 h/AC) was conducted to 
obtain a standard heat-treated ingot. Finally, the ingot was machined 
into a turbine disc with a maximum diameter of over 450 mm and a disk- 
shaped morphology. 
Rod-like samples were selected from the tangential direction of the 
turbine disc, taken at the 3 R/4 position, and used for long-term 
isothermal exposure in heat treatment furnaces (KSL-1200). The sam -
ples were isothermally exposed at 800 ℃ for 10, 20, 50, 100, 500, 1000, 
2000, 5000, and 10,000 h. These samples were subsequently tensile 
tested using a universal testing machine (INSTRON 3382) with a 
± 100 kN capacity and using a tensile temperature, holding time, and 
tensile rate of 750 ℃ , 10 min, and 0.5 mm/min, respectively. The phase 
evolution as a function of the thermal exposure duration was examined 
using scanning electron microscopy (SEM; AMBER) and transmission 
electron microscopy (TEM; Talos F200X). The intragranular γ ′ precipi -
tate coarsening process was observed using secondary electron (SE) 
imaging, while the precipitation behavior and structural characteristics 
of the μ phase were studied using backscattered electron (BSE) imaging 
and TEM. SE and BSE specimens were prepared by electron polishing 
and electrochemical etching in a solution (H

3. Results and discussions 
3.1. Evolution of the γ ′ and μ precipitates in the isothermally exposed 
Fig. 1 (a) shows the evolution of the microstructure at different

The microstructure thermally exposed at 800 ℃ consists mainly of a γ

3.4. Effect of the μ and γ ′ phases on inter/intragranular defects 
The nucleation and growth of the μ phase consumes a significant 
amount of refractory elements, such as W and Mo, weakening the solid 
solution strength of the alloy. Therefore, the impact of the μ phase near 
the large intergranular γ ′ precipitates on the initiation and propagation 
of defects after long-term isothermal exposure was investigated, as 
shown in Fig. 5 . Two distinct regions were identified under the various 
exposure conditions, each located approximately 0.1 cm from the frac -
ture point. The large μ precipitates exhibited surface defects, whereas 
the small μ precipitates did not. Subsequently, the large μ phase pre -
cipitates exhibited two types of failure mechanisms owing to tensile 
deformation, namely, the formation of cracks (shearing of the μ phase) 
on the surface and the creation of crack propagation channels (bypassing 
the μ phase), as illustrated in Fig. 5 (a, b). This behavior can be attributed 
to the brittleness of the μ phase, which leads to stress concentration at 
the matrix interface. Furthermore, the segregation of solid solution el -
ements (Co, Cr) weakens the γ / γ ′ region near the μ phase precipitates, 
facilitating the formation of crack propagation channels, which has been 
reported in previous studies [29,30] . Kernel average misorientation 
(KAM) and Schmid factor analyses were used to predict the dislocation 
distribution density and grain deformation ability, respectively, as 
shown in Fig. 5 (c, d). The degree of stress concentration from blue to red

Long-term thermal exposure changes the critical failure stress of the 
intercrystalline and intracrystalline phases owing to the development of 
intergranular μ phases and intragranular γ ′ coarsening. Consequently, 
the failure sites and fracture morphology of the high-temperature tensile 
fracture cross sections varied under different thermal exposure condi -
tions, as illustrated in Fig. 6 . The samples with a brief thermal exposure 
exhibited multiple macrocracks with varying macrostructure in the 
fracture cross section, suggesting fracture initiation at the edge of the 
sample. A closer inspection of the fracture cross section revealed distinct 
morphological structures on the plane, which were divided into regions 
1 and 2 (see Fig. 6 (a, b)). The intragranular small-sized γ ′ precipitates 
were uniformly distributed after an 800 ℃ exposure for 100 h, indi -
cating the higher strength of the grain interior, which is less susceptible 
to damage during tensile deformation. However, numerous dislocations 
became pinned on the intergranular γ ′ / μ interface with a continuously 
increasing tensile deformation. Regions 1 and 2 of the fracture cross 
section clearly exhibited several intergranular secondary cracks and 
pores near the GBs. Additionally, the dimples formed after intragranular 
γ / γ ′ damage were small and shallow, suggesting more failure sites may 
arise at the GBs. The samples thermally exposed for 5000 h exhibited 
typical ductile fractures with large and deep dimples after high- 
temperature tensile deformation ( Fig. 6 (c, d)). Importantly, the num -
ber of intergranular cracks and pores decreased and the intragranular 
γ / γ ′ was fragmented into smaller pieces with an increasing tensile 
deformation, indicating that failure sites may preferentially form in the 
grain interior. 
Tensile strength is influenced by the microscopic plastic deformation 
related to the resistance to dislocation movement, whereas macroscopic 
plasticity depends on the resistance to damage accumulation. The 
relationship between the intragranular γ / γ ′ phase and dislocations 
during microscopic plastic deformation is an important area of study. 
This study demonstrated that microscopic plastic deformation during 
the 750 ℃ high-temperature tensile test after an 800 ℃ thermal expo -
sure for 10,000 h occurs through the intragranular γ / γ ′ phase ( Fig. 7 ). 
The dependence of the microscopic plastic deformation capacity on the 
intragranular γ / γ ′ phase results in the formation of numerous slip lines 
and several nanotwins, causing the deformation of intragranular γ ′ 
particles through a dislocation shearing motion ( Fig. 7 (a, b)). High 
resolution images of the intragranular γ ′ phase revealed SFs in various 
directions, which was confirmed by the SAED pattern analyses along the 
[-111] zone axis ( Fig. 7 (c, d)). This indicates the activation of different 
glide directions and the shearing of slip lines into the γ ′ precipitates, and 
that the shearing of the ordered γ ′ precipitates by the initial dislocations 
creates a pathway for subsequent dislocations [31,32] .

influenced by individual elements such as Cr, Co, Mo, and W [36] . The μ 
phase precipitates in the samples analyzed in this study were predomi -
nantly observed at the GBs, particularly near large intergranular γ ′ 
precipitates, which may be due to the segregation of Co, Cr, Mo, and W 
associated with the intergranular γ / γ ′ phase. Consequently, refractory 
elements diffuse towards the μ phase interface during thermal exposure, 
leading to the formation of hard and brittle μ precipitates. Additionally, 
the enrichment of refractory elements in the μ precipitates reduces the 
solid solution-strengthening effect in the γ matrix surrounding the μ 
precipitates [29,30] . As mentioned earlier, μ phase precipitation occurs 
during thermal exposure. Cracks often occur within the brittle μ pre -
cipitates or at the interface between TCP precipitates and γ / γ ′ phases 
during high-temperature tensile deformation. Subsequently, the cracks 
propagate towards the intergranular γ / γ ′ regions near the μ precipitates 
with continued tensile deformation, leading to the formation of pores 
( Fig. 8 (a)). Intragranular γ ′ typically undergoes coarsening during 
elevated temperature thermal exposure, which is characterized by a 
stable volume fraction, increased phase-interface distance, and 
decreased quantity. Dislocation shearing is the primary deformation 
mechanism in intragranular γ ′ precipitates during high-temperature 
tensile deformation, and involves numerous slip lines, SFs, and nano -
twins ( Fig. 8 (b)). 
The presence of brittle TCP precipitates in polycrystalline Ni-based 
superalloys often leads to cracks, which reduce the ductility of the su -
peralloys [7,17] . However, the elongation in this study was observed to 
initially increase and then stabilize with an increased thermal exposure 
duration. Therefore, it appears that the formation of the μ phase does not 
significantly affect the elevated-temperature tensile properties after 
prolonged thermal exposure. Conversely, the intergranular μ and γ ′ 
phases in samples with shorter exposure durations notably impacted the 
tensile properties, as evidenced by the fracture results ( Fig. 6 ). Conse -
quently, as the exposure time increased for this alloy, the primary failure 
locations may have transitioned from the GBs to the grain interior 
during high-temperature tensile testing. 
4. Conclusions 
Intragranular γ / γ ′ , intergranular large-sized γ ′ , and other precipitates 
play a significant role in the failure mechanism. This study investigated 
the phase transition and coarsening behavior of the microstructure of Ni- 
Co-Cr-based superalloys following long-term thermal exposure. A 
systematic and detailed analysis of the failure mechanism was con -
ducted using elevated temperature tensile testing on samples exposed to 
different temperatures. The following conclusions were drawn from the 
findings of this study: 
(1) During long-term thermal exposure at 800 ℃ , μ phases contain -
ing refractory elements precipitate around the large intergranular 
γ ′ precipitates and GBs and the small-sized intragranular γ ′ pre -
cipitates coarsen and maintain a stable volume fraction, resulting 
in the deterioration of the performance of Ni-Co-Cr-based 
superalloys. 
(2) γ ′ coarsening and the precipitation of the μ phase results in the 
reduction of the tensile strength, whereas the elongation firstly 
increases and then stabilizes with an increasing exposure time. 
Cracks preferentially nucleate at the μ phase interface and 
propagate to the γ / γ ′ precipitates to form pores, which affect the 
tensile properties. 
(3) The dislocation motion during the deformation response of the 
intragranular γ / γ ′ precipitates to tensile tests is dominated by a 
shearing mechanism, where a part of the intragranular γ ′ pre -
cipitates are deformed through dislocation shearing. The main 
dislocation motions during elevated tensile deformation include 
slip lines, SFs, and nanotwins. 
(4) The fracture morphology of the samples thermally exposed at 800 
℃ for short durations indicates an increase in the failure sites at 
the GBs during high-temperature tensile testing. However, the 
influence of the intergranular γ ′ and μ phases in the samples with 
longer exposure times on the high-temperature tensile strength 
diminishes. The intragranular γ / γ ′ precipitates are seemingly 
more susceptible to fracture after prolonged thermal exposure at 
800 ℃ .
