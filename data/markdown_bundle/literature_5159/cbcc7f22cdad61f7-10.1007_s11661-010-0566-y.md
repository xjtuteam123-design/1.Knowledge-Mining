Study of c/c¢ Interfaces in Nickel-
Based, Single-Crystal Superalloys
by Scanning Transmission Electron
Microscopy
BINGHUI GE, YUSHI LUO, JIARONG LI,
and JING ZHU
The interfaces of c/c¢ in crept Re-bearing, single-crystal,
nickel-based superalloys have been studied by scanning
transmission electron microscopy. Elements Ni and Ta
are congregated at the c¢ side, ahead of the Re enrich-
ment near interfaces, which shows the retarding eﬀect on
element diﬀusion during creep by Re enrichment. A
growth step exists at the c/c¢ interfaces with the apex of
kinks congregated with heavy atoms, which may slow
down interface displacement during creep.

The excellent creep properties of nickel-based super-
alloys at high temperatures have made them widely used
in the turbine blades of jet engines.
[1] In superalloys, the
interfacial structures of c/c¢ play an important role in
determining the mechanical properties of materials
through the lattice misﬁt and coherent strain. Many
studies have been performed on the structure of inter-
faces in nickel-based superalloys,
[2–7] especially on the
distribution of element Re and its existing manner near
interfaces, because Re is particularly beneﬁcial to solid-
solution hardening among all alloying elements. Warren
et al.
[2] was ﬁrst to ﬁnd that near interfaces, there is an
enrichment of Re by means of ﬁeld ion microscopy and
atom probe techniques; this ﬁnding has been conﬁrmed
by Yoon et al. [3] and Mottura et al. ,[6] respectively. By
means of scanning transmission electron microscopy
(STEM) and energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy
(EDS), Ge et al. [7] found that enrichments of Re and
W near interfaces are in the form of clusters. But it has
been reported also that the enrichment of Re near
interfaces is formed during cooling from the application
temperature,
[2,6] so it remains unclear whether the
enrichment of Re near interfaces is beneﬁcial to the
improvement of creep resistance of superalloys. In this
article, the c/c¢ interfaces are further studied by STEM,
high-angle annular dark ﬁeld (HAADF) image and EDS
to investigate the strengthening mechanism of Re-
bearing, single-crystal superalloys.
Diﬀerent from the TEM imaging, the HAADF image
is recorded with the oﬀ-axis HAADF detector, and the
HAADF technique can image with Z (atomic number)
contrast.
[8–11] There is no contrast reversal with crystal
thickness; thus, light and heavy atoms can be distin-
guished directly according to the diﬀerent contrast in the
HAADF image.
The nominal overall composition and the standard
heat treatment of the second-generation, single-crystal
superalloy DD6 has been described in References 12 and
13. The creep test was conducted at 1373K (1100 /C176 C)
under a stress of 140 MPa for 12 hours with the stress
axis parallel to [001] orientation. Disks with diameters of
3 mm suitable for TEM observations were punched out
of samples, and thin foils were prepared by electro-
chemical polishing and cryo ion milling. STEM obser-
vations were carried out on a FEI Titan* 80-300
electron microscope attached with an EDS system.
Figure 1(a) is one HAADF image of c/c¢ interfaces in
the crept single-crystal superalloy, and bright contrast
can be observed at interfaces. Element mapping was
carried out across interfaces corresponding to the area
indicated by the rectangle in Figure 1(a), and mapping
images of Al, Cr, Co, Ni, Mo, Ta, W, and Re are shown
in Figure 1(b), respectively. Enrichment of W and Re
can be observed near interfaces. To display the compo-
sition distribution across interfaces clearly, integrations
have been made along the direction parallel to the
interface, and the composition scatterings across inter-
faces are shown in Figure 2. Enrichment of Cr, Co, W,
and Re can be observed clearly near interfaces, espe-
cially W and Re, which is consistent with that in
Reference 7. It is noted that there are little peaks in the
distribution scatterings of Ni and Ta marked by arrows
ahead of the peaks of W and Re, which suggests that
there are congregations of elements Ni and Ta in the
area close to enrichment region of W and Re near
interfaces.
According to Figure 2 and the fact that creep is
controlled by element diﬀusion,
[6] it can be concluded
that enrichment of Re and W near interfaces hinders
the c¢ forming element diﬀusion into c phases during
creep and made the elements Ni and Ta congregate
ahead of the enrichments of Re and W. This conclusion
is supported from the experimental and theoretical
results
[14,15] that Re has the lowest diﬀusion coeﬃcient
among all alloying elements in single-crystal superalloys.
So although enrichments of Re and W near inter-
faces are formed during cooling from the application

As mentioned previously, alloying elements interdif-
fuse between c and c¢ phases during creep, which will
lead to the displacement of interfaces. As shown in
Figure 3(a) (the HAADF image in the crept single-
crystal superalloy), growth steps are indicated by
circles A and B. From the high-resolution HAADF
image as shown in Figure 3(b) (corresponding to area
A in Figure 3(a)), the apex of kink of interfaces is
composed of a higher concentration of heavy elements
compared with other interfacial areas because of its
brighter image contrast,
[8–10] which should be Re and
W according to Figure 2. This congregation of heavy
atoms is considered to slow down the displacement of
interfaces during creep and then retard the creep
process in the end.
Moreover, from Figure 3(c), which is the high-
resolution HAADF image that corresponds to area B,
a cluster of heavy atoms exists at the interface, which is
marked by an arrow. Changing the imaging focus, these
clusters approximately 2 nm in size and mentioned in
Reference 7 can be observed clearly in both c and c¢
phases. These clusters are displayed with high contrast
in Figure 3(d), the low-magniﬁcation HAADF image,
and a cluster exists at the center of area B. Moreover,
from Figure 3(c), which is the high-resolution HAADF
image, it can be observed that in the area of clusters,
there is no lattice distortion compared with the other
area, i.e., heavy atoms in these clusters, such as W and
Re, do not occupy the interstitial position but exist in
the face-centered cubic lattice sites. Actually, they are
just partially occupied, then the clusters are in the form
of Ni- X (X: heavy elements) alloy.
In conclusion, interfacial structures of the crept
Re-bearing single-crystal nickel-based superalloys have
been studied with STEM, HAADF, and EDS. Con-
gregations of c¢-formed elements Ni and Ta are found
ahead of enrichments of Re and W at c/c¢ interfaces,
which is considered to be the evidence of slowing
down the c¢ formed elements diﬀusion into the c phase
by Re. At interfaces, growth steps with the apex of
kink congregated with heavy elements including Re
have been found, which might retard the displacement
