Effect of TiC Addition on the Microstructure
and Mechanical Properties of Fe55Cr25Co10Ni10
High-Entropy Alloy by Laser Melting Deposition
Manufacturing
Zijian Wang , Fei Xing, Xiangyu Liu, Guojian Xu, Weijun Liu, and Hongyou Bian
Submitted: 12 September 2024 / Revised: 4 November 2024 / Accepted: 15 November 2024 / Published online: 28 January 2025
High-entropy alloy and its composite material show good development prospects and extensive application
potential in the aerospace ﬁeld. One of the most promising developments in additive manufacturing in the
20th century is laser melting deposition manufacturing, as it is commonly acknowledged. In this paper, the
effects of TiC ceramic particles on the microstructure, mechanical properties and friction and wear
properties of high-entropy alloy is thoroughly investigated. According to the results, the yield strength and
tensile strength of the high-entropy alloy at room temperature are 203 and 542 MPa, respectively, and it has
a single-phase FCC solid solution crystal structure with a clearly oriented coarse columnar crystal struc-
ture. When combined with TiC ceramic particles, it displays a composite structure comprising a single-
phase FCC solid solution crystal structure and TiC, which is mainly composed of columnar dendrites with
some cellular dendrites, and a little amount of unmelted TiC particles. The yield strength of the room
temperature tensile test is increased to 231 MPa (13.8% improvement over the TiC-free sample), while the
tensile strength rises to 608 MPa (12.2% improvement over the TiC-free sample). The detailed analysis
shows that the increase in yield strength is a result of the combined effects of ﬁne-grain strengthening and
Orowan strengthening mechanisms. In addition, the high-entropy alloy composite material exhibits supe-
rior wear resistance compared to the high-entropy alloy material. The frictional wear mechanisms of both
are dominated by abrasive and adhesive wear, accompanied by a small amount of oxidative wear. The
study/C213s ﬁndings offer a crucial theoretical foundation for the creation of high-entropy composite materials
through the use of laser melting deposition methods.

1. Introduction
High-entropy alloys are considered one of the most exciting
materials of the 20th century due to their excellent strength,
toughness, thermal stability, and wear resistance in a variety of
extreme environments (Ref 1, 2). These characteristics give
them the great potential for many applications in the future (Ref
3). However, the CoCrFeNi series of high-entropy alloys still
face challenges, such as relatively low yield strength, which
limits their industrial applications (Ref 4). Therefore, some new
strategies are required to fabricate high-performance high-
entropy alloys by adjusting the chemical compositions and
optimizing the manufacturing processes (Ref 5).
A lot of researchers have tried to add some metal elements
with larger atomic radii to high-entropy alloys in an attempt to
improve their mechanical properties (e.g., Al, Ti, Mo, etc.) to
them (Ref 6, 7). The introduction of these elements not only
induces a certain degree of lattice distortion, but also promotes
the phase transition of a face-centered cubic (FCC) structure
into a face-centered cubic (FCC) and body-centered cubic
(BCC) structure combination. This transformation increases the
dislocation movement resistance, thereby improving the alloy /C213s
strength. Additionally, another strengthening approach is
incorporating a second phase (such as carbides, borides, or
oxides) into the high-entropy alloys to form high-entropy alloy
composite materials (Ref 8, 9). The presence of the second
phase provides additional nucleation sites for grain formation,
promotes grain reﬁnement, and impedes dislocation movement,
thus further enhancing the strength of the materials. Bandar
AlMangour et al. investigated the effect of TiC content on 316L
by SLM, and the results showed that the hardness and wear
resistance of the alloy increased signiﬁcantly with increasing
TiC content (Ref 10). Cijun Shuai et al. investigated the
inﬂuence of in situ TiC on the wear resistance of Ti by LPBF
and the results showed that the content of in situ TiC increased
with the increase in graphite content, and then the hardness and

There are various traditional methods of manufacturing
high-entropy alloys, such as mechanical alloying, casting and
powder metallurgy (Ref 16). However, all these techniques lead
to the formation of coarse crystals due to slow cooling rates. As
a result, high-entropy alloys produced by conventional methods
often exhibit lower mechanical properties. As one of the
advanced additive manufacturing technologies, laser melting
deposition manufacturing (LMD) uses the laser as a heat source
and metal powder as a raw material to directly manufacture
metal parts by melting layer by layer, rapid solidiﬁcation and
layer-by-layer deposition of synchronously given metal powder
according to a predetermined processing path. Laser melting
deposition technology (LMD) enables the printing of proto-
types within a brief timeframe, as well as the reﬁnement of
grain size, and the reduction in elemental segregation, thus
signiﬁcantly improving material properties. For example,
Hamza Osman et al. fabricated CoCrFeNiMn-based composites
(CoCrFeNiMn + TiC) by using the selected laser melting
(SLM) technique, and it was found that the yield strength was
increased from 475.1 to 725.8 MPa after the addition of 5 wt.%
TiC (Ref 17). Y . L. Wang et al. fabricated CoCrFeNiMn matrix
composites (CoCrFeNiMn + TiB2) by using LMD technique.
The results showed that the tensile strength was increased from
552 to 861 MPa, and the yield strength was increased from 351
to 567 MPa after the addition of 5 wt.% TiB2, while the
elongation was decreased from 44 to 37% (Ref 18). Hongyu
Chen et al. studied the effects of the addition of nano-TiC on
the tensile strength of CoCrFeNiMn matrix composites with
different percentages of TiC by using the SLM technique. The
results showed that adding 1 wt.% nano-TiC will increase the
room temperature tensile strength from 705 to 931 MPa, and
the ductility will increase from 24 to 29%. When 2-3 wt.%
nano-TiC were added, the room temperature tensile strength
was increased from 931 to 1312 MPa, and the ductility was
decreased from 29 to 5%. In addition, nano-TiC could reﬁne the
grain and improve the microstructure homogeneity. The
addition of the second phase can also effectively inhibit
solidiﬁcation cracks and thus improve the mechanical proper-
ties (Ref 19). Y uanqiang Yan et al. fabricated FeCrAlY high-
entropy alloys by using LMD technology, and the addition of 4
wt.% TiC successfully suppressed the crack generation and
improved the mechanical properties of the materials. The
addition of TiC increased nucleation sites for grain formation,
facilitating the transition from columnar grains to equiaxial
grains, and promoting the expulsion of gas pores from the melt
pool, thereby enhancing material properties(Ref 20).
The constituent elements of high-entropy alloys are typically
expensive; therefore, exploring high-strength and high-tough-
ness high-entropy alloys composed of less costly elements is
more relevant for practical industrial applications. For this
reason, prior research focused on the effects of Fe and Cr on the
mechanical properties of the FeCrCoNi high-entropy alloy,
ultimately conﬁrming the matrix under investigation is
Fe55Cr25Co10Ni10 high-entropy. In this research, laser melt-
ing deposition (LMD) technology is successfully used to
fabricate the Fe55Cr25Co10Ni10 high-entropy and its com-
posite material. A detailed comparison of the microstructure,
mechanical properties, and tribological performance of the
high-entropy alloy and its composite material, and the strength-
ening mechanism of ceramic particles are described. The high-
entropy alloy composite fabricated by laser melting deposition
manufacturing technology is expected to be applied in the ﬁeld
of aero-engine combustion chambers.

2.5 Phase and Microstructure Analysis
The high-entropy alloy (T0) and the high-entropy alloy
composite material (T3) fabricated by laser melting deposition
are produced, and their X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns are
shown in Figure 3. According to the ﬁndings, the high-entropy
alloy (T0) has a single-phase face-centered cubic (FCC) solid
solution structure. In comparison, the face-centered cubic
(FCC) solid solution and TiC are combined to form a composite
structure in the high-entropy alloy composite material (T3) (Ref
21).
The unetched T0 and T3 specimens are observed at low
magniﬁcation by using an optical microscope (shown in
Figure 4). The results show that a small amount of porosity
is presented in the T0 specimen, while some unmelted second-
phase particles are found in the T3 specimen. Through the
magniﬁed observation and energy spectrum analysis by scan-
ning electron microscope, it can be conﬁrmed that a part of the
incorporated TiC particles has been melted and fused into the
matrix, while the other part is retained in the form of
compounds on the surface of the matrix.
The etched T0 and T3 samples are observed along the build
direction using optical microscopy and scanning electron
microscopy in order to further study the microstructure of T0
and T3, as illustrated in Figure 5(a-f). As can be seen from
Figure 5(a, c, e), the T0 sample is fabricated by laser melting
deposition and has a coarse columnar crystal with an obvious
direction. There are two primary factors that affect the size and
shape of the grains: the temperature gradient (G) and the
growth rate (R) (Ref 21). Generally, the larger temperature
gradient (G) and the smaller growth rate (R) promote the
growth of columnar crystals. In comparison, the smaller
temperature gradient (G) and the larger growth rate (R)
promote the formation of equiaxed grains (Ref 22). The laser
melting deposition process is a layer-by-layer additive manu-
facturing technique, where each layer is re-melted during
solidiﬁcation, leading to the re-melting of the grains from the
previous layer (Ref 23). Consequently, columnar crystals
continue to grow along the build direction in the re-melted
region of the molten pool. Figure 5(b, d, f) reveals that the T3
sample by laser melting deposition consists mainly of columnar
dendrites with a few cellular dendrites and a small amount of
unmelted TiC particles are presented. It is evident that the
microstructure of the T3 sample is signiﬁcantly more reﬁned
than that of the T0 sample. Due to the addition of TiC, it
provides a large number of nucleation sites for grain growth
and hinders the continuous growth of grains. The pinning effect
of TiC particles on grain boundaries inhibits the movement of
grain boundaries, thus effectively reﬁning the grains. This
reﬁning effect leads to the emergence of cellular microstruc-
tures in high-entropy alloy composite material, which further
enhances properties of the material (Ref 24).The details of these
phenomena will be further explored in the subsequent EBSD
analyses.

Figure 9 shows the fracture morphology of laser melting

testing. It is evident from the tensile fracture of T0 and T3 that

2.8 Friction and Wear Properties

particles to the metallic matrix, the composite material /C213s
hardness is greatly increased and provides hard wear points
for the metallic matrix. Secondly, the optimized process
parameters used in the laser melting deposition process ensure
a perfect bonding between the TiC ceramic particles and the
matrix, which further improves the wear resistance of the

the presence of oxygen, indicating that the wear mechanisms
are still abrasive, adhesive, and oxidative wear. Thus, the
metallic matrix /C213s wear resistance is greatly increased by the
addition of TiC ceramic particles.
3. Conclusions
In this paper, the effects of TiC ceramic particles on the
microstructure, mechanical properties and friction and wear
properties of Fe55Cr25Co10Ni10 high-entropy alloy is thor-
oughly investigated. The following conclusions are listed:
(1) Fe55Cr25Co10Ni10 high-entropy alloy and its compos-
ite material are successfully fabricated by laser melting
deposition (LMD) technology.
(2) The high-entropy alloy sample without TiC particles
addition exhibits a single-phase FCC solid solution crys-
tal structure with obviously orientated coarse columnar
crystal structure. In contrast, the high-entropy alloy with
the addition of TiC ceramic particles exhibits a compos-
ite structure (single-phase FCC solid solution crystal
structure and TiC), which is mainly composed of colum-
nar dendrites with some cellular dendrites, and a small
amount of unmelted TiC particles.
(3) Due to the addition of TiC, it provides a large number
of nucleation sites for grain growth and hinders the con-
tinuous growth of grains and it also leads to grain
reﬁnement. Additionally, the addition of TiC has signiﬁ-
cantly increased the proportion of high-angle grain
boundaries ( >15/C176 ) and weakens the (001) preferred ori-
entation, resulting in more dispersed grain growth.
(4) The yield strength and tensile strength of the high-en-
tropy alloy at room temperature are 203 and 542 MPa,
respectively. After adding TiC ceramic particles, the
yield strength increases to 231 MPa (13.8% improve-
ment over the TiC-free samples), and the tensile strength
increases to 608 MPa (12.2% improvement over the
TiC-free samples). The increase in yield strength due to
the addition of TiC ceramic particles is attributed to the
synergistic effects of grain reﬁnement and Orowan
strengthening.
(5) In the room temperature reciprocating sliding wear tests,
the friction coefﬁcient of the composite material with
TiC ceramic particles (0.46) is lower than that of the
high-entropy alloy sample without TiC (0.52). The wear
mechanisms of high-entropy alloy and its composite
mainly include abrasive and adhesive wear, and all of
them show different degrees of oxidative wear.
