ABSTRACT 
Additive manufacturing of high entropy alloys (HEAs) has attracted increasing interest. A high-strength Co-free 
HEA with eutectic structure was developed using selective laser melting (SLM) technology in this work. This 
material shows excellent mechanical properties over a wide temperature range, from 25 
◦
C to 500 
◦
C, owing to 
its ultra-fine eutectic cellular structure with BCC phase wrapped by FCC phase. Specifically, the Co-free HEA 
exhibits a room temperature tensile strength exceeding 1200 MPa, and a yield strength of more than 880 MPa. 
The strength results of this Co-free HEA represent a significant improvement compared to other Co-free HEAs or 
middle entropy alloys (MEAs) manufactured using SLM. Given their low cost, Co-free HEAs are highly promising 
for commercial large-scale applications, and this research suggests that this high-strength Co-free HEA with 
eutectic structure may have particular value in SLM manufacturing mold applications. 
1. Introduction 
High entropy alloys (HEAs) have attracted increasing interest due to 
their high strength, good thermal stability, and exceptional corrosion 
resistance, making them suitable for various applications [ 1 , 2 ]. Con -
ventional HEA fabrication techniques such as vacuum arc melting or 
induction melting followed by casting, as well as mechanical alloying 
followed by spark plasma sintering, have many disadvantages, espe -
cially because they require molds or postprocessing and are unsuitable 
for producing complex geometries [ 3 , 4 ]. Additive manufacturing (AM), 
also termed three-dimensional (3D) printing, produces parts directly 
from a 3D digital model via a layer-by-layer route, enabling the build-up 
of large-scale parts with complex geometries and internal features [ 5 , 6 ]. 
AM processes in HEAs are explored to meet the modern industrial re -
quirements for complex geometries, for example, directed energy 
deposition (DED), selective laser melting (SLM), electron beam melting 
(EBM), and direct ink writing (DIW). Among these AM techniques, SLM 
technology stands out for its high cooling rate and high precision [ 7 , 8 ]. 
However, the majority of SLM-ed (selective laser melted) HEAs reported 
in the literature contain Co, which is relatively more expensive than 
other elements commonly used, such as Ni, Cr, and Fe, making the 
investigation of Co-free HEAs promising for commercial large-scale 
applications, and some Co-free HEAs have been manufactured by 
traditional methods [ 9 , 10 ]. 
Although there have been a few reports on the preparation of Co-free 
HEAs or middle entropy alloys (MEAs) using SLM, the reported strength 
of these alloys is not particularly outstanding when compared with some 
high entropy alloys with Co addition [ 11 – 13 ]. The work of improving 
the tensile strength of SLM-ed Co-free HEAs is of great significance. 
Some researchers have made contributions to improving the strength of 
HEAs by different means including changing the composition of HEAs, 
comparing different processing technologies, and post heat treatment 
[ 14 – 16 ]. When using these processes, changing the composition of HEAs 
is a more direct and promising strategy. In the following sections, 
research is presented related to improving the strength of HEAs by 
adding Al and Ti.

significantly enhances the strength of the high-entropy alloys

possesses excellent strength, which is beneficial for engineering appli -
cations in SLM-manufactured molds.

4. Discussion 
4.1. Microstructure evolution 
From the SEM and EBSD photographs in Fig. 6 , Fig. 8 , and Fig. 10 , 
the bottom of the molten pool consists of large columnar grains while 
the center of the molten pool consists of fine equiaxed grains. This is 
because the bottom of the molten pool is nearer to the cool base plate 
and has a higher cooling rate which is beneficial to the growth of 
columnar grains [ 33 ]. Thus, the columnar grain size is as large as 10 μ m 
on average, which is much higher than the equiaxed grains at the molten 
pool center, approximately 1.5 μ m on average. According to the research 
of Guo et al. [ 34 , 35 ], at the center of the molten pool, the cooling rate is 
lower, and it tends to generate fine equiaxed grains. 
The X – Y plane shows a more homogeneous structure than the X-Z 
plane. This is related to more severe remelting during the SLM process 
and a more homogeneous temperature gradient on the X – Y plane. There 
is more severe remelting on the X – Y plane than on the X-Z plane. On the 
X-Z plane, the remelting is less when deeper into the molten pool with 
the laser energy decreasing along the depth. The result of Fig. 6 (b) is 
caused by the inhomogeneous remelting on the X – Y plane. Zhao et al. 
[ 36 ] found that more remelting results in more precipitation, and in this 
experiment, the precipitation is the BCC phase. The laser spot diameter 
is approximately 50 μ m, and the hatching space is 60 μ m. The inter -
melting track is approximately 10 μ m and is the most severe remelting 
area, which can explain the phenomenon of a banded area with a width 
of approximately 10 μ m with a relatively denser cellular structure in 
Fig. 6 (b). In Wu et al. ’ s [ 35 ] research, the temperature gradient on the 
X – Y plane is more homogeneous than that on the X-Z plane, which helps 
to explain the homogeneous structure on the X – Y plane.

The size of the BCC phase becomes larger, and the content of the BCC 
phase increases with increasing laser power, as shown in Fig. 7 . This can 
be explained by the high temperature during time increasing with 
increasing laser power when the scanning speed is the same [ 37 ]. A 
greater power supply on the specimen leads to a temperature increase in 
the molten pool, but the cooling rate changes little, so there is a longer 
high temperature during time from the initial precipitating temperature 
of the BCC phase to the overtemperature, which is beneficial for the BCC 
phase ’ s precipitation, and more BCC phase precipitates near the already 
precipitated BCC phase. As a result, the BCC phase becomes larger and 
the content of the BCC phase increases. 
The HAGB content on the X – Y plane is higher than that of the X-Z 
plane when comparing Fig. 8 with Fig. 9 . This phenomenon may also be 
caused by the more severe remelting on the X – Y plane. The SLM process 
is finished one layer by one layer. According to Lan et al. ’ s [ 38 ] research, 
the grain boundary angle enlarges during the melting and solidification 
process, and more severe remelting increases the HAGB content. 
Therefore, the HAGB content on the X – Y plane is higher than that on the 
X-Z plane. However, the FCC and BCC contents are almost the same on 
these two planes. On the X – Y plane, the temperature gradient is more 
homogeneous, so the BCC phase is more homogeneous. However, on the 
X-Z plane, the temperature gradient differs from the molten boundary to 
the molten center. Near the molten center of the X-Z plane, the BCC 
content is higher than that in the X – Y plane, but near the molten 
boundary, the BCC content is lower. When the FCC and BCC contents are 
calculated on average, the FCC and BCC contents are almost the same, 
which is reasonable.

The inhomogeneous structure of the as-built Fe32Cr33Ni29Al3Ti3, 
with columnar grains of low BCC phase content at the bottom of the 
molten pool and a cellular structure of the BCC phase wrapped by the 
FCC phase in the middle of the molten pool, is different from those of 
other SLM-ed eutectic high entropy alloys (EHEAs). Most SLM-ed EHEAs 
have a lamellar structure [ 28 , 34 , 40 ]. Guo et al. [ 34 ] found that the 
duplex microstructure of selective laser melted AlCoCrFeNi2.1 under -
went a morphological evolution from a lamellar structure to a cellular 
structure as the laser energy input decreased, but the cellular structure 
consisted of an FCC phase wrapped by a BCC phase. 
According to the research of Rinaldi et al. [ 41 , 42 ], the number of 
phases formed is related to the element content and G/R ( G is the 
temperature gradient, and R is the solidification rate). The content of Al 
in this HEA is relatively small compared to the reported HEAs, which 
limits the generation of the BCC phase [ 43 ]. Thus, the SLM-ed samples 
did not exhibit a completely eutectic structure. Furthermore, the value 
of G/R varies in different regions of the molten pool. At the boundary of 
the molten pool, the value of G/R is low, while at the center, it is high. A 
high G/R value is conducive to the precipitation of the BCC phase. 
Therefore, more BCC phase precipitation occurs at the center of the 
molten pool, forming a typical eutectic structure. However, at the 
boundary of the molten pool, G/R is extremely low, and BCC phase 
precipitation hardly occurs. 
The formation of a cellular structure requires a minimum degree of 
constitutional undercooling. The solute concentration in the liquid has 
to be minimized and combined with an extremely high velocity of the 
solidification front [ 44 ]. In the center of the molten pool, the cooling 
rate is lower than that in the molten boundary area, and constitutional 
undercooling is minimal [ 45 ]. However, a high velocity of the solidifi -
cation front still exists because of the high cooling rate of SLM. Under 
such circumstances, the cellular structure tends to form near the center 
of the molten pool. 
The cellular structure of Fe32Cr33Ni29Al3Ti3 is different from the 
SLM-ed AlCoCrFeNi2.1 cellular structure mainly because of the differ -
ence in element content. The cellular growth of the FCC phase occurs in 
the AlCoCrFeNi
x 
as-cast alloys with x over 2.4 and the FCC phase is 
wrapped by the BCC phase [ 46 ]. The EBSD results of Fig. 8 , Fig. 9 , and 
Fig. 10 show that the FCC phase proportion in Fe32Cr33Ni29Al3Ti3 is 
more than 70 % which is much higher than that in SLM-ed AlCoCr -
FeNi2.1, which is approximately 50 % [ 34 ]. The cellular growth of the 
FCC phase leads to the result of the BCC phase being wrapped by the FCC 
phase. 
The grain size refinement can be explained by the addition of Ti and 
the precipitation of the BCC phase. Numerous literature sources have 
reported that the addition of a moderate amount of titanium can refine 
the grain structure [ 47 , 48 ]. The BCC phase particles are smaller than the 
FCC phase particles according to the EBSD results. These factors lead to 
the refinement of the grain size.

5. Conclusions 
In this study, a wide range of selective laser melting (SLM) process 
parameters was investigated for the production of a eutectic Co-free 
high-entropy alloy with a composition of Fe32Cr33Ni29Al3Ti3. The 
as-printed material exhibited excellent mechanical properties. Specif -
ically, in the energy density range of 85 – 150 J/mm
3
, the density of the 
as-built samples exceeded 99 % without any micro-cracks. Notably, the 
HEA samples fabricated by SLM had a eutectic structure with BCC phase 
wrapped by FCC phase and the average grain size is less than 3 μ m. The 
refinement of the cellular eutectic structure resulted in exceptional 
mechanical properties, with yield and ultimate tensile strength at room 
temperature exceeding 880 MPa and 1200 MPa, respectively. Addi -
tionally, the material exhibited excellent elevated-temperature tensile 
mechanical properties between 300 
◦
C and 500 
◦
C. The as-printed 
samples in this study possess excellent strength and low cost and may 
have particular value in SLM manufacturing mold applications.
