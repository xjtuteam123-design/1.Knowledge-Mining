1. Introduction 
Due to the unique characteristics, high-entropy alloys (HEAs) have 
attracted intensive attentions in the research field in view of their po -
tential applications. In recent years, the enhancement of FCC FeCoCrNi- 
based HEAs has been extensively investigated [ 1 – 4 ]. The results [ 5 – 9 ] 
showed that the formation of precipitates, promoted by doping alloying 
elements and thermo-mechanical processing, is very effective to 
strengthen this kind of alloys. However, the precipitates could also 
easily result in a drop of ductility, which limit the alloys to achieve a 
superior tensile performance with desirable strength-ductility 
combination. 
The most used alloying elements in HEAs are Al and/or Ti [ 8 – 12 ]. 
These elements usually result in the formation of geometrical close- 
packed phases (GCPs, including γ’ -Ni
3

3

The lattices of these phases are coherent to the matrix. Generally, low 
lattice mismatch reduces the nucleation barrier to precipitates, thus 
enables and stabilizes nano-precipitates as shown in the work of Lu et al. 
[ 13 ]. By optimizing the Al and Ti concentrations, the coherent LI
2
- γ’ 
phase particles with radius of 9.7 nm can precipitate in the CoCrFeNi- 
based HEA [ 11 ]. The ultra-fine coherent precipitates are very favor -

The formation of the LI

contribution to the enhancement of HEAs with a promising combination 
of strength-ductility, in which Ti plays a key role in the γ’ phase pre -
cipitation, while Al enhances the stability [ 12 ]. 
Recently, some other alloying elements, represented by Mo, have 
been used to enhance HEAs [ 14 – 19 ]. The addition of Mo can produce 
different topologically close-packed phases (TCPs, including σ , μ and 
Laves phase, etc.), depending on its doping content and heat treatment 
parameters. Unlike Ni-based super alloys [ 14 ], these phases in FCC HEAs 
usually result in an effective increase of strength without causing brit -
tleness [ 15 ]. This is due to the fact that solid-solution FCC HEAs 
generally exhibit a very high plasticity that allows remaining a certain 
toughness after precipitation strengthening [ 16 ]. The result [ 17 ] 
showed that via thermo-mechanical processing, the σ and μ precipitates 
effectively strengthen the CoCrFeNiMo
0.3

the enhancement of HEAs with TCP precipitation can also be realized by

the HEA, simultaneously promote the formation of GCPs including γ’ , γ’

brought into play in enhancing the strength-ductility combination in the

Figure 1 presents SEM maps, EDS profiles and EDS maps of the ho -

displayed in the SEM maps. They show no apparent peak of intermetallic

Although the numerous coarse particles of (Ti, W, Mo)-rich phase

candidates for strengthening CoCrFeNi-based HEAs. Especially, the

and Nb elements. W, Mo and Ti are effective elements to strengthen the 
alloy with promising ductility via the formation of the (Ti, W, Mo)-rich 
phase in the FCC matrix. Adding Nb can further enhance the alloy via the 
formation of a higher volume fraction of (Ti, Nb)-rich phase. However, 
although the strengthening is effective via adding (WMoAlTi) and Nb, 
the practical significance is limited, because the yield strength is still 
very low.

nucleation and retards the growth of the σ phase. The partitioning 
of Nb makes γ’ phase precipitate with high nucleation rate and 
low growth rate. 
(3) The recrystallization is retarded due to the addition of trace Nb in 
the alloy, and there maintains large amounts of stacking faults 
and Lomer-Cottrell locks. The formation of these micro-defects 
benefits from a reduction of stacking fault energy due to the in -
crease of Mo content in the matrix, which is resulted from sub -
stitutions of W and Mo by Nb in the (Ti, Nb)-rich phase. 
(4) As trace Nb doped, the strength-ductility combination of the alloy 
is enhanced via the improvement of the heterogeneous micro -
structure, demonstrating that the addition of trace Nb is a 
promising method for enhancing the strength-ductility combi -
nation of this kind of alloy.
