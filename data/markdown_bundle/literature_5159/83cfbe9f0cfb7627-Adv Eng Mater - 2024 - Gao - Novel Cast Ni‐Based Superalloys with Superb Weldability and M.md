1. Introduction
Due to the outstanding mechanical performance and corrosion
resistance at elevated temperatures, nickel-based superalloys
have attracted extensive attention over the
past few decades and are, by far, the most
widely used high-temperature materials in
aeroengines.[1,2] In particular, the Inconel
718 (IN718) alloy, mainly strengthened
by the ordered Ni 3Nb ( γ 00) phase, consti-
tutes more than 50% of the worldwide pro-
duction of cast nickel-based superalloys. [3,4]
This alloy has been widely used in cast
components of aero and gas turbine
engines, due to its excellent tensile
strength, castability, and weldability. [5]
However, the instability of the predomi-
nant strengthening γ 00 phase under long-
term exposure at the temperature above
650 °C limits applicability. [2,6,7] To bridge
the gap in the service temperature of
IN718 alloy, cast IN939 polycrystalline alloy
strengthened by a high fraction of γ 0(Ni3Al)
precipitates was developed, which can be
used for the hot sections of aeroengines
served at ≈800 °C.[8,9] However, the weld-
ability of IN939 is insuf ﬁcient for repair
and remanufacturing. [10,11] Seeking a bal-
ance between the weldability and mechani-
cal properties of cast superalloys remains challenging since these
two properties are usually paradoxes.
The weldability of superalloys is affected by various intrinsic
mechanisms. For γ 0 phase precipitated superalloys, strain-age
cracking (SAC) is prevalent in the postwelding annealing treat-
ment because the γ 0 phase will precipitate shortly after solidi ﬁ-
cation and impedes the relaxation of thermal stress in the
solidiﬁed γ phase.[12] Solidiﬁcation cracking (SC) is bound to
occur if the remaining liquid is insuf ﬁciently ﬁlled in the inter-
dendrite and the solidiﬁcation shrinkage strain cannot be accom-
modated in the terminal stage of solidi ﬁcation.[13] Superalloys
with high strength and creep rupture life are required to develop
aeroengines with higher-temperature capacity. [14] One of the
effective approaches to achieve this object is to increase
the volume fraction of the γ 0 phase, which, however, raises the
SAC risk. [12] To compensate for the insuf ﬁcient precipitation
strengthening effect of the lower volume fraction of the γ 0 phase
in superalloys, another technical route can be adopted, which
involves enhancing the solid solution strengthening effect by
the addition of refractory elements. [15] However, the wide mushy
zone and microsegregation caused by refractory elements

Herein, innovative cast Ni-based superalloys that not only withstand temperatures
up to 800 °C but also offer superior weldability, robust tensile strength at both room
and elevated temperatures, and a reduced density are introduced. The new Ni-
based superalloys are optimized via calculation of phase diagrams-based high-
throughput thermal calculation and program-controlled composition screening.
A new multiperformance-oriented criterion is devised, encompassing quantitative
weldability indices, volume fraction of γ 0, electron vacancy number, density, sol-
idus temperature, and freezing range to optimize the alloy composition. Weldability
indices, rooted in the solidi ﬁcation crack initiation mechanisms, prove equally
applicable to both current interested alloys and commercial superalloys. The crack
length of the optimized alloy after the constrained welding test is only 3.08% of the
total weld length. It is unveiled that solidi ﬁcation cracking (SC) emerges as the
pivotal factor governing the weldability of these alloys. The mechanism of SC for
such superalloys is originated from the addition of Nb, Ti, Mo, and W, which
results in the microsegregation of the composition and the precipitation of (Nb, Ti)-
rich constituents or M(Ta, Nb, Ti)C carbides in the liquid ﬁlm of the central
interdendritic region. This work hopefully helps to ﬁnd promising candidates for
the combustion chamber of aeroengines and other high-temperature casts.

In this work, we aim to develop novel superalloys with supe-

multiperformance-oriented criterion. To illustrate the applicabil-

expected to enrich the superalloy systems, provide new ideas
for the ef ﬁcient and economical design of advanced materials
with multiple properties, and help establish the relationship
between weldability, castability, and strengthening of superalloys.
2. Methodologies for the Composition Designing
2.1. Composition Space
To develop new polycrystalline superalloys with desired proper-
ties, we designed two groups of superalloys based on different
screening criteria for composition optimization. Within these
groups, Ni and Co serve as the basic elements for the γ and
γ 0 phases; Co, Cr, W, and Mo constitute the γ-forming elements
responsible for solid-solution hardening; Al, Ti, Ta, and Nb
are the γ 0-forming elements that contribute to precipitation
strengthening; and C plays a crucial role as a grain boundary
(GB)-strengthening element, enhancing grain-boundary cohe-
sion and pinning. [13]
The compositional ranges of the ﬁrst group of superalloys
have been meticulously designed to cater to the SAC mechanism.
We set the lower limits of Al and Ti contents of 1.7 and 2.5 wt%,
respectively, to ensure an appropriate volume fraction of
γ 0 phase,[26,27] and the Nb and Ta contents were no more than
1.1 and 2.25% wt%, respectively, to ensure solid solution and pre-
cipitation strengthening. In addition, 0.65 –2.6 wt% of W and
0.35–1.4 wt% of Mo were added to improve the high-temperature
strength of γ phase. The second group of alloy composition
ranges was designed for the SC mechanism. Since solidi ﬁcation
crack initiation can readily lead to alloy cracking during the solid-
iﬁcation process, it is imperative to decrease the upper limits of
refractory elements (W, Mo, and Ta). The eutectic microstructure
is also taken into account as C element is added. The composi-
tion space and calculation resolution for the two groups of super-
alloys are listed in Table 1 . Based on this methodology, 65 536
and 77 760 alloys were allocated to the two distinct composition
spaces, respectively.
2.2. CALPHAD-based High-throughput Calculation
The application of the CALPHAD method for composition opti-
mization requires both thermodynamic databases (to provide the
Gibbs energies for individual phases) and computational
software (to calculate the equilibrium state). [21,28] In this study,
we employed Thermal-Calc (TC) software with the TTNI8
database[29] to evaluate the thermodynamic properties. To facili-
tate these massive calculations within the vast composition
space, we developed a computational software using Python lan-
guage to accomplish automatic input, data reading, storage, and
screening. We generated numerous batch ﬁles for CALPHAD
analysis, which were subsequently executed through the TC soft-
ware. Next, thermodynamic data were exported into individual
text ﬁles, and comprehensive summary ﬁles were created, com-
piling all data derived from the phase diagrams. The calculations
of the phase diagram were run between 600 and 1600 °C at inter-
vals of 10 °C. Then, the information including the solidus –
liquidus temperatures, as well as the number, volume fraction,
and types of phases varying with temperatures was output from
the phase diagram. The data extracted from each phase diagram
were then screened using our software according to precise cri-
terion. Through this rigorous process, alloy compositions that
met the speciﬁed criterion were identiﬁed. The CALPHAD-based
HTC employed in this study represents an innovative computa-
tional platform that integrates HTC, high-throughput screening,
and composition design criterion. Consequently, this approach
proves to be signi ﬁcantly more ef ﬁcient than traditional
trial-and-error methods, offering a substantial advancement in
the ﬁeld.

2.3. New Multiperformance-Oriented Criterion for Composition
Screening
In this work, we propose a new criterion for the composition opti-
mization of superalloys. The screening criterion is constructed
by comprehensively considering the weldability, strength,
organizational stability, castability, and lightweight, which is
composed of six quantitative metrics: weldability index ( ISAC
or ISC), volume fraction of γ 0 (Vγ 0 ), solidus temperature ( Ts),
solidus–liquidus temperature difference ( Tl/C0 s), electron vacancy
number (Nv), and density ( ρ). In the following context, the sub-
stantial indices for this criterion are speci ﬁed.
2.3.1. Weldability
Weldability Evaluation based on the SAC Mechanism : As men-
tioned in the introduction, the SAC mechanism has been used
to interpret the weldability, which is mainly related to the volume
fraction of γ 0 in superalloys. In this sense, the lower the volume
fraction of γ 0, the better the weldability. The γ 0 phase should be
regulated to an appropriate extent to ensure that superalloys have
sufﬁcient weldability, tensile strength, and creep rupture life.
Then the key point in designing a new superalloy is to optimize
the contents of γ 0 phase-forming elements: Al, Ti, Nb, and Ta.
Here we proposed a parameter, named ISAC index, to quantify
the weldability, which is expressed as
ISAC ¼ WAl þ 0.5WTi þ 0.3WNb þ 0.15WTa (1)
where WAl, WTi, WNb, and WTa represent the weight percents of
Al, Ti, Nb, and Ta, respectively.
Weldability Evaluation based on the SC Mechanism : For the cast
superalloys, the weldability is also affected by SC during solidi ﬁ-
cation. Moreover, the eutectic structure formed in the last stage
of solidi ﬁcation also affected the weldability. Therefore, we pro-
posed a new weldability assessment parameter, named as SC
index, which covers the main elements to affect the content of
γ 0 phase, the mushy zone width, and eutectic forming ability.
In contrast to the ISAC metric, we consider not only the
γ 0-forming elements such as Al, Ti, Nb, and Ta but also the ele-
ment C, which affects the eutectic microstructure, as well as the
elements W and Mo, which in ﬂuence the Tl/C0 s. Therefore, the ISC
index is expressed as
ISC ¼ WAl þ 0.6WTi þ 0.3WNb þ 0.15WTa
þ 1.2WC þ 0.26WMo þ 0.14WW
(2)
where Wi is the weight percent of element i.
2.3.2. Volume Fraction of γ 0 Phase
As mentioned in the above section, a certain content of Vγ 0 is
needed to meet the requirement of strengthening at high
temperatures. Thus, we set the lower and upper limits of the
γ 0 content to be 34% and 39%, respectively, at 600 °C for
high-temperature mechanical properties.
2.3.3. Solidus Temperature and Solidus –Liquidus Temperature
Difference
Mostafaei et al. reported that increasing Ta content from 3.2 to
6 wt% in CM247 LC superalloy added 25 °C to the Ts and opti-
mized the temperature-bearing capacity. [30] The addition of W,
Ta, and Mo can improve the temperature-bearing capability. [31,32]
The Ts index is closely related to the temperature-bearing capac-
ity of superalloys. Moosavy et al. [33] proposed a solidi ﬁcation
model for predicting castability in precipitation-strengthened
Ni-based superalloys and showed that alloys with shorter solidi-
ﬁcation temperature ranges have better castability and lower SC
susceptibility. In contrast, Tl/C0 s determines the wide freezing
range of an alloy and affects the microsegregation and precipita-
tion of some low-remelting temperature phases in the interden-
dritic regions.[34] Thus, the Tl/C0 s index designated here plays dual
functions (i.e., scaling the SC-related weldability and castability).
The Ts and Tl/C0 s thresholds in the screening procedure were
restrained to be 1260 and 60 °C, respectively.

3.2. Restraint Welding Test
A restraint welding test was performed, which was considered a
rigorous weldability test to evaluate weldability of alloys.
The plates, fabricated through investment casting, underwent
hot isostatic pressing and standard heat treatment before being
precision cut into 2 /C2 60 /C2 100 mm3 samples. For the welding
process, two plates were neatly stacked and securely ﬁxed
together using spot-weld positioning. This assembly was then
positioned on a water-cooled copper pad in preparation for tung-
sten inert gas welding, as illustrated in Figure 1. We opted for an
IN625 alloy with 1.2 mm diameter as a welding wire to effectively
attenuate the in ﬂuence of ﬁll materials on the SAC of the
assembly.[11] High-purity argon (99.99%) was used as the weld-
ing shielding gas. The gas ﬂow rate was maintained at a steady
10 L min/C0 1 throughout the welding process, while the welding
current was consistently kept at ≈95 A. Fluorescence nondestruc-
tive characterization was employed to meticulously analyze the
crack length in the welded specimen. This advanced technique
provided a comprehensive and reliable assessment of the weld
quality.
3.3. Microstructural Characterization
The metallographic samples, which were acquired subsequent to
the heat treatment, along with the weld cross sections, under-
went precise cutting followed by manual polishing using a
2.5 μm diamond paste. Then they were electrolytically etched
in a Cu 2SO4: HCl: H 2O solution at 8 V for 15 s to expose the γ 0
precipitates or chemically etched with a HF: CH 3COOH:HNO3
solution to expose the γ channel and facilitate statistical analysis.
The microstructures of the alloys were observed using a ZEISS
SUPRA 55 ﬁeld-emission scanning electron microscope (SEM)
in both the secondary-electron and backscattered electron modes.
The compositions of secondary phases and carbides were ana-
lyzed using SEM –energy-dispersive spectroscope (SEM –EDS).
Room-temperature (RT) tensile tests of these alloys were carried
out at a strain rate of 10 /C0 3 s/C0 1 using a DDL50 electronic testing
machine, while elevated-temperature tensile tests were carried
out at E45.105(UTC 2017-049) at a strain rate of 1.8 mm min /C0 1.
Stress rupture life tests were performed on an RDL30 electronic
creep machine under 815 °C/379 MPa and 870 °C/255 MPa con-
ditions. The variable γ 0 was quantitatively characterized using the
image analysis software Image-Pro Plus. At least three represen-
tative images were used to achieve statistical results. The area
fraction of the γ 0 phase was measured using image thresholding
according to the gray values. The measured area fractions in this
study were adopted to approximately represent the volume frac-
tions. The densities of the selected alloys were measured based
on the Archimedes principle. The melting and solidi ﬁcation
behaviors (phase transformation temperatures) of the selected
alloys were determined using differential scanning calorimetry
(DSC, NETZSCH STA 449C) in a temperature range of
600–1400 °C at a heating rate of 5 °C min /C0 1 with high-purity
Ar ﬂow. Baseline calibration runs were performed in an empty
crucible, and temperatures were calibrated in separate experi-
ments using standard reference material.

5. Discussion
5.1. Applicability of the Criterion for Screening Cast Superalloys
Overall, the aforementioned experimental results con ﬁrm the
effectiveness of our design-driven strategy, which is grounded
in a multiperformance-oriented criterion. We successfully
designed a new cast superalloy with comprehensive behavior,
rather than being unique in a certain performance. The SCR
alloy boasts excellent weldability, lightweight features, as well
as superior RT and high-temperature tensile properties superior
to those of IN939. The only aspect where it falls short is in terms
of creep resistance compared to the IN939 alloy; however, this is
attributed to the prioritization of weldability. In any case, the
stress rupture life of SCR alloy is suf ﬁcient for 800 °C
applications.

5.2. SC: The Principal Factor In ﬂuencing Weldability and Its

inﬂuencing the weldability of materials. Several criteria have
been established for formulating the chemical composition of
superalloys. For cast superalloys, especially equiaxed crystalline

in determining weldability, owing to their distinct solidi ﬁcation

Now, the in ﬂuence of alloying elements on the SC of the cur-
rent alloys can be elucidated. First, the addition of refractory met-
als W, Mo, Ta, and Nb increases the freezing range, which might
be the cause of the severe microsegregation and the precipitation
of some phases with low melting points in the interdendritic
regions.[34] Second, the behavior of the liquid ﬁlm formed in
the last stage of solidi ﬁcation is considerably affected by the
segregation of Nb, Ti, and Mo. With the development of
crystallization, the Ti, Nb, and Mo elements are excluded into
the interdendritic liquid phase. Finally, the eutectic reaction
L ! γ þ (Ti, Nb)C takes place. [44] The (Nb, Ti)-rich constituents
or precipitates formed in the liquid ﬁlm of the central interden-
dritic region may be pinned at the liquid channel to inhibit liquid
feeding. When the tensile stress caused by the solidi ﬁcation con-
traction obstruction is greater than the stress limit that the liquid
ﬁlms can withstand, the liquid ﬁlms between the dendrites are
strained to form cracks. [45,46] On the one hand, the formation of
MC carbides consumes large amounts of Ti and Nb, which
decreases the solid solution strengthening effect; on the other
hand, the carbides on the GB further enhance the susceptibility
to SC. Therefore, to obtain excellent weldability, it is essential to
control the composition of Nb, Ti, and Mo in alloys and the for-
mation of carbides in the interdendritic liquid ﬁlm.
Overall, considering the in ﬂuence of refractory elements on
SC, this article proposes a more precise criterion for the weldabil-
ity prediction of Ni-based superalloys. It is reasonable to consider
that the SCR alloy, developed using the multiperformance-
oriented criterion, can serve as a candidate structure material
for the combustion chamber and other components.
6. Conclusion
Based on the above results, conclusions can be drawn as follows.
1) New cast Ni-based superalloys with excellent weldability and
mechanical properties were developed using CALPHAD-based
HTC and program-controlled screening. The HTC results
indicated that there are indeed some trade-offs among various
properties for cast superalloys, for example, the mechanical per-
formance must be compromised when the best weldability is
achieved. 2) A multiperformance-oriented criterion composed
of the indices of weldability assessment index, the content of
γ 0, electron vacancy number, density, solidus temperature,
and freezing range was proposed for the composition screening
of polycrystalline superalloys. This criterion has been proved to
be effective not only for the currently optimized alloys but also for
various superalloys, including IN100, IN7C, IN738, IN625,
IN792, IN939, and CM247LC. 3) Susceptibility to SC was found
to be the principal factor affecting weldability. The mechanism of
SC for such superalloys was unveiled in view of compositional
aspect. The addition of refractory metals increased the freezing
range, resulting in severe microsegregation and the precipitation
of some phases in the interdendritic regions. The (Nb, Ti)-rich
constituents or MC carbides in the liquid ﬁlm of the central inter-
dendritic region enhanced SC susceptibility. To obtain excellent
weldability, the content of Nb, Ti, Mo, and W in the alloy must be
controlled. 4) The SCR alloy optimized in this study possesses
excellent weldability, castability, and lightweight property, as
well as superior RT and high-temperature tensile properties
as compared to those of IN939. Only the creep resistance is
compromised to make up for the best weldability requirement.
This novel alloy may be a promising candidate for the combus-
tion chamber of aeroengines and other high-temperature casts.
