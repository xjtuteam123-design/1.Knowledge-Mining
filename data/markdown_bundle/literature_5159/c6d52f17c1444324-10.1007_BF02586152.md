Phase Stability and Yield Stress of Ni-Base Superalloys
Containing High Co and Ti
C.Y . CUI, Y .F. GU, H. HARADA, D.H. PING, and A. SA TO
Ni-base superalloys containing high Co ( .20 wt pct) and Ti ( .5.5 wt pct) were designed in order to
study the effects of Co16.9 wt pct Ti addition on phase stability and mechanical property. These new
alloys, though they contained high Ti, mainly consisted of g and g9 phases. Ni 3Ti ( h) phase was
observed along the grain boundaries in some of the alloys. The formation of h phase was mainly
related to the Ti/Al ratio, Ti content, and alloy composition. Tensile and compression tests showed
that these new alloys exhibited higher yield stress than that of the baseline alloy, TMW-1(U720LI).
The possible strengthening mechanisms were discussed in terms of solid-solution and precipitation
strengthening effects by the Co16.9 wt pct Ti additions. Preliminary results show promising trends for
the development of new superalloys for turbine disc applications.
I. INTRODUCTION
ADV ANCED supersonic commercial and military air-
crafts will require turbine engines capable of sustaining
supersonic cruise for extended time periods. This will impose
severe challenges for the high pressure compressor and tur-
bine disks, which will operate with rim temperatures in
excess of 700 °C during the entire supersonic ﬂight. [1,2] To
achieve this goal, advanced Ni-base disk superalloys, such
as ME3 (General Electric, Cincinnati, OH), [1,2] NR3
(SNECMA, Futuroscope Chasseneual), [3] RR1000 (Rolls-
Royce, Derby),[4] and LSHR (NASA, Cleveland, OH),[5] have
been developed. These alloys are all strengthened by high
refractory element contents. To avoid the risk of elemental
segregations and unacceptable contents of carbonitride
stringers,[4] these heavily alloyed alloys are usually fabricated
using a powder metallurgy processing route, which is more
complex than a cast and wrought (CandW) processing route.
Recently, we proposed a new idea to design Ni-base disk
superalloy by mixing a UDIMET* Ni-base superalloy
*UDIMET is a trademark of Special Metals, Huntington, WV .
U720LI with a g/g9 two-phase Co16.9 wt pct Ti (hence-
forth referred to as the ‘‘Co-Ti’’) in various contents for
application at temperatures above 700 °C.[6,7] The results
showed that the additions of Co-Ti to U720LI can greatly
suppress the formation of topological closed packed
phases.[6] In addition, new alloys exhibit higher yield
strength than that of the baseline alloy, U720LI, at temper-
atures below 850 °C. However, they show similar strength
at the processing temperature about 1100 °C, which means
that these new alloys could be fabricated by the CandW
route. However, in some of these alloys, Ni 3Ti ( h)p h a s e
with a hexagonal close-packed structure is formed in the
interdendritic region. The h phase is reported to have a
deleterious effect on an alloy’s ductility. [8] Up to now, the
effects of Co-Ti contents on the formation of h phase and
mechanical properties are not clear.

In these alloys, the changes of the Ti/Al ratio (from 2.0
to 3.7), Ti content (from 4.8 to 7.4 wt pct), and alloy
composition (mainly Co and Cr) can be correlated with
the formation of h phase. The effects of Co-Ti on mechan-
ical property are discussed in accordance of strengthening
mechanisms. (1) Comparisons between TMW-1, TMW-4,
and TMW-6 are referred to as the ‘‘solid-solution strength-
ening effect of Co-Ti’’ (SSSE), because they possess the
same designed g9 volume fraction (45 pct) but different
Co-Ti contents in g and g9 phases. (2) The tie-line alloys,
TMW-7, TMW-6, and TMW-5, can clearly explain the pre-
cipitation strengthening effect (PSE) of g9. (3) TMW-1,
TMW-2, and TMW-5 contain various amounts of Co-Ti
in g/g9 phases and g9 volume fractions that are associated
with both solid-solution and precipitation strengthening,
i.e., mixed effect (ME).
The aim of this article is to characterize the microstruc-
tures and mechanical properties of alloys with high levels
of Co and Ti. In particular, the formation of h phase and
possible strengthening mechanisms are discussed.
III. EXPERIMENTAL
The alloys were fabricated by a vacuum induction melt at
Special Metals Corporation (Huntington, WV). The ingots
were homogenized at 1200 °C for 24 hours and subse-
quently hot extruded to 22-mm bar at about 1176 °C. The
extrusion ratio was controlled to be about 12:1 (henceforth
referred to as the ‘‘as-extruded’’ samples). The actual
chemical compositions were determined by X-ray ﬂuores-
cence at Special Metals Corporation, as listed in Table I.
This clearly shows that the chemical compositions of the
alloys were well controlled to the designed ones. The heat

4 h/air cooling (AC) followed by 650 °C for 24 h/AC and

V . DISCUSSION
A. F ormation of h Phase
Microstructural observations (Figures 3 through 5)
clearly show that, though these alloys contain higher Ti
than the current Ni-base disc superalloys, the alloys mainly
consist of g and g9 phases, independent of the solution
treatment temperatures. The h phase only forms in the alloy
TMW-5 with highest Ti/Al ratio and Ti content.
The formation of h phase is strongly related to the fol-
lowing factors: (1) Ti/Al ratio, (2) Ti content, and (3) alloy
composition. As for factor (1), it is known that in a Ni-base
superalloy, the stability of g9 Ni3X is dependent on Al Ti,
Nb, and Ta contents. [17] In a lower Ti containing alloy such
as Nimonic 263 (wt pct: Ti2.1, Al0.4), [18] the Ti-rich meta-
stable g9 can transform to the h phase during aging, where
the Ti/Al ratio is higher (5:1). In contrast, in a higher Ti
containing alloy such as GTD111 (wt pct: Ti4.86,
Al3.05),[19] the h phase can easily form with a lower Ti/
Al ratio (Ti/Al 5 1.6). In our alloys with the Ti/Al ratio
ranging from 2.0 to 3.7, the h phase is formed only in the
alloy TMW-5 with the highest Ti/Al ratio (3.7). As for
factor (2), the Ti/Al ratios in the alloys TMW-5, TMW-6,
and TMW-7 were 3.7, 3.5, and 3.6, respectively, but the
h phase is only formed in the alloy TMW-5 with the highest
Ti content (7.4 wt pct), showing that the Ti contents in the
new alloys are also important for the formation of the
h phase. As for factor (3), the main differences in alloy
compositions are the Co, Cr, and Ti contents. Compared to
the alloys (TMW-7, TMW-6, and TMW-5) with similar Ti/Al
ratio, the h phase does not form in the alloys TMW-6 and
TMW-7 with higher contents of Co and Cr. Probably, the
stability of Ni 3Ti is enhanced with a partial replacement
of Ni by Co or Cr atoms. The higher contents of Co
and Cr seemed to facilitate the formation of Co 3Ti or
(NixCo1/C0 x)3Ti phases. [20,21]
B. Yield Stress
Generally speaking, Ni-base superalloys derive their out-
standing properties from a combination of several principal
strengthening mechanisms: (1) solid solution, [22] (2) precip-
itation,[23] and (3) grain boundary strengthening. [24] As
listed in Table I, the elements such as C, B, and Zr for
grain boundary strengthening were almost the same in all
alloys investigated; thus, the effects of grain boundary
strengthening should be similar. Also, the grain sizes were
nearly the same under the same heat-treatment condition, as
shown in Figure 6; thus, the effects of grain size on strength
can be assumed to be the same. In addition, lower h phase
content ( #0.6 pct) in the alloy TMW-5 is believed to have
little effect on yield stress. [6]
Figure 9 shows the effects of Co-Ti addition on the yield
stress at 750 °C. In Figure 9(a), the compressive yield
stresses increased with increasing the Co-Ti contents inde-
pendent of the solution heat-treatment temperatures, show-
ing the solid-solution strengthening effect of Co-Ti. In
Figure 9(b), the compressive and tensile yield stresses at
750 °C also increased with increasing the g9 volume frac-
tion, showing the precipitation strengthening effect of g9.I n
Figure 9(c), the compressive yield stresses increased with
increasing the Co-Ti additions, showing the mixed effect.
The results showed that the strength increase could be
ascribed to the solid-solution strengthening of Co-Ti and
precipitation strengthening of g9.
Speciﬁcally, the concentration of Ti is more inﬂuential in
improving the yield stress and higher Ti/Al ratios are also
important for conferring strength. [25] It is reported that
increasing Co in the alloys has little effect on tensile prop-
erties.[26] For example, at room temperature, the yield stress
of Udimet700 increases slightly from 997 to 1146 MPa and
tensile strength increases from 1493 to 1550 MPa as Co is
increased from 0 to 17 wt pct.

C. V alidity of the Database
Table II lists the predicted equilibrium phases at 760 °C
using the Thermo-Calc program. Here, we only predicted the
main phases including g, g9, and h. The observed phases at
various conditions are also summarized in Table II. Micro-
structure observations showed that the h phase is only
observed in the alloy TMW-5 with the highest Ti/Al ratio
and Ti content. However, the Thermo-Calc program pre-
dicted that the h phase also exists in the alloys TMW-4,
TMW-6, and TMW-7. Even in the alloy TMW-5, the vol-
ume fraction of the observed h phase is much lower than
that of the predicted one, as listed in Table II. Thus, the
original database (Ni-RUMN) may not encompass all the
alloys with such high levels of Ti and Co. The possible
reasons may be related to the following: (1) the present
database is not suitable and (2) the present alloys are not
thermodynamically stable. Further works are in progress to
amend the database.
VI. CONCLUSIONS
The microstructure, phase stability, and mechanical
properties of the alloys containing high Ti and Co were
examined. The following conclusions were drawn.
1. The alloys with high Co and Ti contents mainly
consisted of g and g9 phases, independent of the heat-
treatment conditions.
2. The h phase with an hcp structure was only found in the
TMW-5 with the highest Ti/Al ratio and Ti content. The
formation of h phase was mainly related to the Ti/Al
ratio, Ti content, and alloy composition.
3. Some TMW alloys exhibited higher yield stress than the
base alloy TMW-1(U720LI). The strength increase was
mainly ascribed to the solid-solution strengthening of
Co-Ti and precipitation strengthening of g9.
