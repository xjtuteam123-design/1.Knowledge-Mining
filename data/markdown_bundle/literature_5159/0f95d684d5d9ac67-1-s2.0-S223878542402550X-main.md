ABSTRACT
Multi-component alloys have been confirmed to be excellent reinforcing phases in high-entropy alloy-based 
composite. However, achieving a balance between strength and ductility by controlling the reinforcement 
content remains a major challenge in the design of high-entropy alloy-based composites. In this study, CoCr -
FeMnNi HEA/NiCoFeAlTi HER high-entropy alloy composites were prepared using SLM technology. Interest -
ingly, gas-atomized NiCoFeAlTi high-entropy intermetallic (HER) powder was selected as the reinforcing phase, 
and the CoCrFeMnNi HEA matrix was used for comparison. The influence of the reinforcement content on the 
composite was investigated, and high-performance HEA/HER composites were obtained, with the strengthening 
mechanism elucidated. The results showed that the composite with 1 wt% HER reinforcement exhibited the 
optimal performance, with hardness, yield strength, ultimate tensile strength, and ductility at room temperature 
being approximately 226.9 HV, 562 MPa, 683 MPa, and 16%, respectively. The excellent mechanical properties 
at both room and elevated temperatures are attributed to the synergistic effects of heterogeneous microstructure 
strengthening, grain refinement, and precipitation strengthening. This work provides a foundation for the 
development of high-entropy alloy composites reinforced by high-entropy intermetallics.
1. Introduction
High-entropy alloys (HEAs), due to their excellent performances, 
have attracted research interest in recent years [ 1 – 5 ]. Typically, HEAs 
exhibit a simple solid solution phase [ 6 – 9 ], with CoCrFeMnNi HEA 
being one of the typical representatives showing a simple face-centered 
cubic ( fcc ) phase. CoCrFeMnNi HEA offers good low-temperature 
strength, hardness, wear resistance, and corrosion resistance, bringing 
significant application prospects [ 10 – 14 ]. However, CoCrFeMnNi HEA 
has the disadvantage of showing a lower yield strength both at room and 
high temperatures [ 15 – 17 ]. Three different methodologies have been 
adopted to improve and enhance the performance of CoCrFeMnNi HEA. 
The first method is to replace traditional/conventional fabrication 
methods with advanced manufacturing techniques like additive 
manufacturing (AM) for the fabrication of CoCrFeMnNi HEA. Kim et al. 
[ 18 ] achieved a yield strength of ~775 MPa for CoCrFeMnNi HEA 
fabricated using a laser-based powder bed fusion process (LPBF) 
otherwise commonly known as selective laser melting (SLM). The yield 
strength of the CoCrFeMnNi HEA fabricated by SLM is ~2.6 times higher 
than the yield strength (~293 MPa) achieved using conventional 
fabrication techniques like casting [ 19 ]. Therefore, obtaining hetero -
geneous structures through additive manufacturing to enhance the 
performance of high-entropy alloys represents an effective approach 
[ 20 , 21 ]. The second method involves thermal, mechanical, or

terms of high-level local process control, complex shapes, and extremely 
rapid cooling rates [ 35 – 38 ]. Therefore, the combination of material 
(CoCrFeMnNi HEA) and process (SLM), is expected to overcome the 
manufacturing bottleneck of high-performance in HEA-based compos -

multi-component alloys as reinforcing phases is still in its early stages. Li 
et al. [ 17 ] fabricated Fe-based amorphous alloy-reinforced HEA com -
posites using SLM. Interestingly, two new HEA phases were formed in 
the alloy, confirming the excellent compatibility between amorphous 
alloys belonging to multi-component systems and HEAs, making them a 
promising reinforcing phase. Meanwhile, due to the precipitation 
strengthening effect, the strength of such alloys exceeds 1000 MPa.
Based on the advanced SLM process, we propose incorporating 
multicomponent reinforcements into CoCrFeMnNi HEA to enhance their 
yield strength. Compared to conventional reinforcements (with ability 
to precipitation hardening), such as pure elements and ceramic particles, 
incorporating multicomponent reinforcements into multicomponent 
HEA powders is a most promising research direction. Accordingly, in 
this study, CoCrFeMnNi HEA was used as the matrix material, and 
NiCoFeAlTi was chosen as the reinforcing phase to fabricate the CoCr -
FeMnNi HEA/NiCoFeAlTi HER composites using SLM. The influence of 
the HER content on the microstructure and properties of the composite 
was investigated, and the factors affecting them were analyzed sys -
tematically. This study provides a foundation for the development of 
HEA composites (with HER-intermetallic phase).

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Influence of reinforcement content on the microstructure
3.1.1. Phase analyses
The X-ray diffraction spectra of the SLM-fabricated CoCrFeMnNi 
HEA and HEA/HER composites with different reinforcement contents 
are shown in Fig. 3 . It can be observed from Fig. 3 (a) that both the 
CoCrFeMnNi HEA and the composites consist of a single phase fcc 
structure. However, the SLM-prepared CoCrFeMnNi HEA exhibits strong 
diffraction peaks along the (200) direction, indicating preferential 
growth/texture along the (200) direction. In contrast, for the composites 
as the reinforcement content increases from 0.1 wt% to 2 wt%, the ratio 
between the (111) peak and the (200) peak in the HEA/HER composite 
increases, indicating increasingly pronounced preferential growth along 
the (111) direction.
The enlarged image of the (111) peak and (200) peak shown in Fig. 3
(b), reveals another important characteristic of the diffraction peaks in 
the HEA/HER composites as compared to the CoCrFeMnNi HEA. The 
diffraction peaks of the composite shift towards higher angles, which 
may be associated with a decrease in the lattice constants of the crystal. 
This can be attributed to two main reasons: on one hand, as compared to 
the CoCrFeMnNi HEA, the HEA/HER composite incorporates Al and Ti 
atoms, resulting in lattice distortion, and a decrease in lattice constants 
and on the other hand, compared to the matrix alloy, the composite 
possesses higher residual stresses, which lead to a reduction in the lattice 
constants of the composite. However, with the increase in the rein -
forcement content, no additional peaks nor peak shift is observed in the 
composite, indicating that the crystal lattice structure remains un -
changed with the introduction of the HER, and no reaction between the

3.2.2. Room temperature tensile properties
Fig. 7 presents the room temperature tensile stress-strain curves for 
the CoCrFeMnNi HEA and HEA/HER composites as a function of varying 
reinforcement content. The results reveal that the SLM-fabricated 
CoCrFeMnNi HEA exhibits the following strength levels: yield strength 
~545 MPa and ultimate tensile strength ~668 MPa with ~35% elon -
gation. With an increase in the reinforcement content, yield strength, 
ultimate tensile strength, and elongation of the composite initially in -
creases and then decreases, corroborating the hardness data. When the 
reinforcement content is increased to 1 wt%, the composite demon -
strates an optimal combination of strength and ductility, with a yield 
strength of 562 MPa, ultimate tensile strength of 683 MPa, and elon -
gation of 23%. Interestingly, adding 0.1 wt% reinforcement leads to an 
improved ductility (38%). According to the microstructural analysis, the 
improvement in the elongation may be attributed to the reduction of 
microstructural defects such as pores in the matrix along with grain 
refinement due to the introduction of reinforcement ( Table 2 ). Micro -
structural defects, including pores and inclusions, serve as the initiation 
sites for crack nucleation during tension, thereby affecting their 
ductility. As the reinforcement content increases, the grain refinement 
effect and solid solution strengthening effect of elements make positive 
contributions to the strength of the alloy compromising the ductility.
Fig. 8 shows the fracture morphologies of the CoCrFeMnNi HEA and 
HEA/HER composites as a function of varying reinforcement content 
after the room temperature tensile test. Fig. 8(a – a1) presents the frac -
ture morphologies of the CoCrFeMnNi HEA matrix. It shows the pres -
ence of large voids (~12 μ m) on the fracture surface. This may be 
attributed to the deformation and expansion of pores during the tensile 
test. Additionally, the magnified image shows the presence of dimples 
indicating ductile fracture as the primary mode of failure. When the 
reinforcement is added to 0.1 wt%, the fracture surface exhibits ridge- 
like protrusions, as shown in Fig. 8 (b,b1). Relatively flat surfaces are 
observed on the side of the ridges with an angle of inclination of 45
◦
between them. The ridges are accompanied by numerous micropores of 
varying sizes ( Fig. 8(b1) ), and some cracks are observed between the 
ridges. It may be concluded that the HEA/0.1 wt% HER composite un -
dergoes ductile fracture through micropore aggregation. As the rein -
forcement content increases, the inclination angle of the ridges increases 
until the ridges disappear completely. Additionally, the size of the mi -
cropores increases, gradually transforming into cavities, as illustrated in 
Fig. 8 (c and d). Notably, as depicted in Fig. 8(d1) , cracks extend from the 
cavities. This indicates that the thermal cracks tend to nucleate at such 
cavities. When the reinforcement content reaches 2 wt%, brittle fracture 
characteristics start to emerge.
As shown in Fig. 8 (e), small cleavage facets appear on the fracture 
surface, and some cracks and cavities are observed around these micro 
defects. The magnified image ( Fig. 8(e1) ) reveals the presence of step- 
like patterns on the cleavage facets, indicating a mixed fracture mode 
(ductile and brittle fracture coexist) in the HEA/2 wt% HER composite. 
These observations suggest that with an increase in reinforcement con -
tent, the fracture mode in the composites transforms from a completely 
ductile failure to a mixed fracture mode consisting of both ductile and 
brittle components.
3.2.3. High temperature tensile properties
CoCrFeMnNi HEAs offer excellent room temperature and low- 
temperature performance, while their tensile properties at high tem -
peratures are relatively poor. Therefore, the tensile properties of the 
HEA/1HER composite were tested at room temperature and elevated 
temperatures (at 873 K and 1173 K), as shown in Fig. 9 . The results 
demonstrate that the HEA/1 wt% HER composite exhibits superior high- 
temperature mechanical properties as compared to the matrix. At 873 K, 
the ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of the HEA matrix is ~487 MPa with 
an elongation of 17%. In contrast, the HEA/1 wt% HER composite 
achieves a significantly improved UTS of ~617 MPa (an increase of 
~27%), while maintaining a similar ductility. As the temperature in -
creases, both the HEA and HEA/1 wt% HER composites undergo high- 
temperature softening behavior. At 1173 K, the UTS and ductility of 
the HEA matrix is ~200 MPa and ~3%, respectively, exhibiting almost 
no ductility. In contrast, the HEA/1 wt% HER composite demonstrates a 
relatively better performance (UTS ~245 MPa and ductility ~ 3.5%).
The reasons for such strengthening in the composite can be multi -
faceted. Firstly, the addition of Ti and Al elements acts as solid solution 
strengthening. During high-temperature tensile deformation, these trace 
elements inhibit grain recrystallization behavior, thus partially sup -
pressing high-temperature softening behavior and impeding dislocation 
movement during deformation, thereby providing strengthening effects. 
Secondly, studies have shown that the addition of Al and Ti can improve 
the alloy ’ s resistance to high-temperature oxidation [ 41 ]. The addition 
of Al promotes the formation of Al
2
O
3 
oxide, which can protect the alloy 
from further oxidation and inhibit the formation of non-protective ox -
ides such as Cr
2
O
3 
[ 42 ]. During high-temperature tensile deformation, 
oxygen tends to diffuse along grain boundaries, leading to the genera -
tion of harmful products. Such oxides can lead to the formation of 
cracks, causing premature failure of the alloy. Therefore, Al plays a role 
in inhibiting oxidation and further enhancing the high-temperature 
mechanical properties.

the amount of internal stresses in the HEA/1 wt% HER composite is

higher dislocation density.

the melt pool boundaries, resulting in finer grains and higher residual 
stresses, consequently leading to the formation of high dislocation 
density. The KAM analysis plot reveals that the HEA/1 wt% HER com -

previous observations. This suggests that the HEA/1 wt% HER com -

melting process. Previous observations indicated that a high density of 
dislocations can alleviate stress concentration and release strain energy, 
thereby preventing the initiation of microcracks [ 49 ]. Consequently, 
this portion of residual stress contributes to the material ’ s strength and 
toughness.

4. Conclusion
This study focuses on the fabrication of CoCrFeMnNi HEA/NiC -
oFeAlTi HER composites using SLM technique. The influence of rein -
forcement concentration (HEA/0.1 wt% HER, HEA/0.5 wt% HER, HEA/ 
1 wt% HER, and HEA/2 wt% HER), on the microstructure and me -
chanical properties of the CoCrFeMnNi HEA matrix was investigated, 
elucidating the strengthening mechanism under the optimal parameters. 
The main conclusions are as follows. 
(1) Both HEA and HEA/HER composites exhibit a single-phase fcc 
structure, but the lattice constant of HEA/HER composites is 
found to be lower. The HEA-matrix contains macroscopic defects 
such as micropores, while the microstructure of HEA/HER com -
posites is denser. Both the HEA matrix and HEA/HER composites 
consist of equiaxed and columnar grains, with the HEA/1 wt% 
HER composite showing the smallest grain size (~0.35 μ m).
(2) The HEA/HER composites show higher hardness as compared to 
the HEA matrix. The composite with 1 wt% reinforcement ex -
hibits the highest hardness of ~227 HV. It also demonstrates the 
optimal combination of strength and ductility at room tempera -
ture, with yield strength, ultimate tensile strength, and uniform 
elongation are found to be ~562 MPa, 683 MPa, and 16%, 
respectively. As the reinforcement content increases, the fracture 
morphology changes from a ductile to a mixed mode of ductile 
and brittle fracture.
(3) The HEA/1 wt% HER composite exhibits excellent high- 
temperature tensile performance, maintaining ultimate tensile
