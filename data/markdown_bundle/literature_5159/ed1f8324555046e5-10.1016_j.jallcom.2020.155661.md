abstract
Using a total-energy method based on the density functional theory (DFT), the strengthening effects of
Mo, Ta, W, and Re on the ideal uniaxial compressive strength ( sICS) and elastic properties of Ni-based
single-crystal (NBSC) superalloys were systematically investigated using a model consisting of g/g0
multilayers. The ability of the ternary alloying elements to enhance sICS, the elastic constants, and elastic
modulus were in the order: Re > W > Mo > Ta. Our calculated elastic properties were consistent with
reported experimental results for commercial superalloys (e.g., CMSX-4 and TMS-26), as well as with
other reported computational results. Furthermore, the charge density difference of the g/g0 ternary
alloys during compressive deformation were analyzed to understand the strengthening mechanisms of
the alloying elements. The mechanical properties of the
g/g0 ternary model alloys were predicted by
modifying the ﬁrst-order rule of mixtures (ROM) with the alloying element concentrations, and the
determined values were in close agreement with the results of the ﬁrst-principles calculations.

1. Introduction
Ni-based single-crystal superalloys (comprising a precipitated
g0-Ni3Al phase coherently embedded in the continuous g-Ni matrix
phase) have exceptional high-temperature mechanical properties
and therefore, are promising high-temperature structural materials
that have been extensively used for advanced blade applications
[1,2]. The interfacial boundary between the face-centered cubic
(fcc) matrix g-Ni phase and the ordered L1 2 precipitated g0-Ni3Al
phase has an important hardening effect on the mechanical prop-
erties of superalloys [3e6]. Hence, it is important to have a detailed
understanding of the mechanical behavior of the g/g0 interface for
practical applications. In recent decades, the mechanical properties
(e.g., elastic behavior, stacking fault energies, and ideal strength) of
the matrix phase [7e13] and the precipitated g0 -phase [9,10,14e19]
have been widely investigated using ab initio calculations, which
has been aided by the development of computational methods and
increasing computational capability. However, theoretical quantum
mechanical study on the mechanical properties of g/g0 interfaces
has been limited.
The in ﬂuence of alloying elements (e.g., Re [ 20e23], Ru
[20,21,23], W [ 20,24], Ta [ 20], Mo [ 20], and Cr [ 20]) on the g/g0
interface strengthening of Ni-based superalloys has been investi-
gated via ﬁrst-principles calculations. Using density functional
theory (DFT), Sanyal et al. [ 25] studied the effects of O, H, and N on
the cleavage energies of g/g0 interfaces. Peng et al. [26] investigated
the inﬂuence of S and P on the cleavage rupture of the g/g0 interface
based on Grif ﬁth’s equation. Using g/g0 multilayers, Wang and
Wang [27] explored the elastic properties of Ni-Al binary superal-
loys with different g0 volume fractions. Wu and Wang [ 28] studied
the effects of Re, Ru, Cr, Mo, W, and Ta on the ideal tensile strength
of g/g0 multilayers using a total energy method. Ab initio calcula-
tions of the rupture strength of the g/g0 interface with and without
phosphorus were carried out by Tian et al. [ 29]. Research on the
elastic properties and mechanical strength is insuf ﬁcient for Ni-
based superalloys. The ideal compressive strength as well as
elastic constants and elastic modulus are essential parameters for
understanding the mechanical properties of solid structural

In general, the elastic properties and compressive strength of
two-phase materials are closely related to their constituent phases.
In addition to directly exploring the elastic properties of two-phase
materials via ﬁrst-principles calculations, many studies have
investigated two-phase materials using the rule of mixtures (ROM)
with promising predicted results [ 27,30e32]. In ceramic-metal
two-phase materials, Heish et al. [ 30] described the physical
properties of two-phase systems using the ROM. Wang et al. [ 27]
applied the ROM to the Ni-Al binary g/g0 two-phase model and
reliably obtained the elastic properties of the g/g0 two-phase
multilayer model at 0 K. Accordingly, whether the ROM can be
applied to predict the mechanical properties of Ni-based alloys for
alloying doped ternary two-phase superalloys needs to be
investigated.
In light of this background, quantum mechanical calculations
were performed in this work to investigate the effects of four
important strengthening elements (i.e., Mo, Ta, W, and Re) on the
ideal compressive strength and elastic properties of g/g0 interfaces.
Furthermore, modiﬁcations of ROM based on the alloying element
concentrations were used to predict the physical properties of the
g/g0 ternary model alloys.
2. Theory and computational details
2.1. Computational model
For the FCC-Ni and L1 2-Ni3Al phases, the weakest uniaxial
compressive direction is the <10 0> orientation. The interphase
boundary of the g/g0 two-phase system was experimentally and
theoretically observed to be coherent, with a simple [001]|[001]
relation [ 33,34]. Therefore, a 160-atom g-Ni[001]/g0-Ni3Al[001]
multilayer model (2[100]a0 /C2 2[010]a0 /C2 10[001]a0, where a0 is the
lattice constant) was used in this work, as shown in Fig. 1(a). The
vertical direction of the multilayer model contained 10 g0-(001)
atomic layers and 10 g-(001) atomic layers, and each vertical plane
included eight atoms. As reported in our previous study [ 35], Mo,
Ta, W, and Re prefer the Al site in g0 precipitates of the ternary
model NBSC superalloys. Hence, the alloying element X (X ¼ Mo, Ta,
W, and Re) was located at the center Al atom of the phase interface.

3. Results and discussion
Before investigating the uniaxial compressive behaviors of the
g/g’ Ni140Al19X multilayers, we calculated its lattice constants and
elastic properties. The lattice constant of the undoped g/g’ multi-
layer model (i.e., Ni-Al ternary alloys) was determined to be
3.544 Å. The lattice constants of the two-phase model increased to
3.546, 3.547, 3.546, and 3.545 Å after the addition of Mo, Ta, W, and
Re, respectively. The volume expansion caused by Ta was the
largest, while that by Re was the smallest. The lattice expansion of
the g/g’ layered model could be directly related to the atomic radii
of these four elements [ 44]. The tetragonal g/g’ multilayers shown
in Fig. 1(a) had six independent elastic constants, and the calculated
values were C 11 ¼ 240.21 GPa, C 33 ¼ 241.76 GPa, C12 ¼ 164.41 GPa,
C13 ¼ 159.51 GPa, C 44 ¼ 120.95 GPa, and C 66 ¼ 1 19.15 GPa,
respectively. Noticeably, the g/g’ multilayer demonstrated a cubic
symmetry, which is in good agreement with the experimental re-
sults reported by Ichitsubo et al. [ 45] and the DFT investigation by
Wang and Wang [ 27]. Hence, we applied cubic symmetry to
calculate the elastic properties of the g/g’ Ni-based alloys in sub-
sequent calculations. By this method, the elastic constants with
cubic symmetry for g/g’ Ni140Al20 multilayers were determined to
be 240 GPa, 157 GPa and 120 GPa for C 11,C 12 and C 44, respectively
(as displayed in Table 1). It is important to note here that one can
obtain the closest cubic elastic constants for supercell models by
using the projection technique introduced by Moakher and and
Norris [46], as demonstrated by previous studies [ 47e49]. And the
determined values for the undoped g/g’ multilayer model were
C11 ¼ 241 GPa, C 12 ¼ 161 GPa and C 44 ¼ 120 GPa, respectively.
Obviously, the elastic constants obtained by directly applied cubic
symmetry to calculate the elastic properties and by the projection
technique introduced by Moakher and and Norris [ 46] consist well
with each other for the g/g’ multilayers. Herein, we adopted the
former method to calculate the elastic constants in the present
study in order to reduce computational costs.

In addition to a material ’s elastic properties, investigation of the
ideal strength is also important for understanding the extent of the
dislocation core, creep, and the ductility and brittleness under
practical application [67e71]. The energy-strain relationship of the
g/g’ model (see Fig. 2 ) was calculated during [001] uniaxial
compression. Then, the stress-strain curve (plotted in Fig. 2 )w a s
derived by differentiating the energy of the system with respect to
the strain (see Eq. (1) in Ref. [17]). As displayed in Fig. 2, the strained
energies of Ni-Al binary alloys and Ni-Al-X (X ¼ Mo, Ta, W, and Re)
ternary alloys ﬁrst increase in a convex manner and then increase in
a concave manner with increasing compressive strain, exhibiting
an inﬂection at a strain of 10%. As shown in Fig. 2, the stress in the
Ni-Al binary alloy reached a maximum value of 6.258 GPa at a strain
of 10%. Hence, the ideal compressive strength, sICS, of the undoped
g/g’ two-phase alloy is 6.258 GPa. Compared with Ni-Al binary al-
loys, after doping with the alloying elements, the stress of the Ni-
Al-X (X ¼ Mo, Ta, W, and Re) ternary alloys increased over the
entire investigated strain range of 0% e16%. The ideal compressive
strengths were determined to be 6.556, 6.484, 6.570, and 6.640 GPa
for Mo-, Ta-, W-, and Re-doped g/g’ multilayers, respectively.
Similar to the elastic constants, the ability of ternary alloying ele-
ments to improve the ideal compressive strength was in the order:
Re > W > Mo > Ta.

strengthening by the alloying elements, the charge density differ-

compressive processes was further calculated. Fig. 3(a) and (b) plot
