Characterization of oxide scales to evaluate high temperature
oxidation behavior of Ni–20Cr coated superalloys

Abstract
Modern thermal spray processes such as plasma spraying are usually considered to deposit high-chromium, nickel–chromium coatings onto
the superalloys to enhance their high temperature oxidation resistance. The purpose of these coatings is to form long-lasting oxidation protective
scales. In the current investigation, Ni–20Cr alloy powder was deposited on three Ni-base superalloys; Superni 75, Superni 600 and Superni 601
by shrouded plasma spray process. Oxidation kinetics was established for the uncoated as well as the coated superalloys in air at 900
◦C under
cyclic conditions for 50 cycles by thermogravimetric technique. Each cycle consisted of 1 h heating followed by 20 min of cooling in air. All
the coated superalloys nearly followed the parabolic rate law of oxidation. X-ray diffraction (XRD) and scanning electron microscopy/energy
dispersive X-ray (SEM/EDAX) techniques were used to characterise the oxide scales. The coating was found to be successful in maintaining its
integrity with the superalloy substrates in all the cases. The oxide scales formed on the oxidized coated superalloys were found to be intact and
spallation-free in general. The XRD analysis revealed the presence of phase like NiO, Cr
2O3 and NiCr2O4 in the oxide scales. The XRD results
were further supported by the SEM/EDAX analyses.

1. Introduction
The high resistance of high-chromium, nickel–chromium
alloys to high-temperature oxidation and corrosion makes them
widely used as welded and thermally sprayed coatings in fos-
sil fuel-ﬁred boilers, waste incineration boilers, and electric
furnaces [1]. Modern thermal spray processes such as high
velocity oxyfuel (HVOF) and plasma spraying are often applied
to deposit high-chromium, nickel coatings onto the outer surface
of various parts of the boilers, e.g. tubes to prevent the penetra-
tion of hot gases, molten ashes, and liquids to the carbon steel
boilers tube [2]. Use of coatings is the preferred approach, even
when relatively hot corrosion/oxidation-resistant base alloys are
used [3]. Even if the material withstands the temperature with-
out coating, coating enhances the life period of the material in
that case. From an engineering point of view, the microstructure,
bond adhesion and oxidation performance of ﬂame-sprayed and

parts and heat treating jigs. Superni 601 has been developed as
high temperature heat resisting alloy.

ISIS300.
3. Results
3.1. Visual examination
Color of the oxide scale formed on the Ni–20Cr coated super-
alloy specimens during initial cycles of the air oxidation at
900
◦C was grey, which went on turning towards green as the
number of cycles increased, leaving a dark green scale at the
end of 50 cycles in all the cases. The scales were having macro-
crack-free surfaces with a lustrous appearance. The scale for
the coated Superni 75 showed little tendency for spalling in the
form of a ﬁne powder during the ﬁrst half of the study. The coat-
ing, in general showed good adherence to all the superalloys
during the course of the cyclic oxidation studies. However, the
coating suffered minor spallation along or near the edges of the
specimens, which was observed to be marginal. This spalling

4. Discussion
The plasma sprayed Ni–20Cr coatings have shown a typical
splat surface morphology with distinct splat boundaries, Fig. 1.
Presence of some oxides and voids is also indicated in the sur-
face microstructures. Wigren and Tang [15] have reported that
the microstructure of a thermally sprayed coating typically con-
sists of a multiphase matrix (often a mix between hard, soft and
amorphous), pores, oxides, delaminations, crack, grit residues
and unmelted particles. According to Bluni and Mardar[16] the
oxides may form due to the in-ﬂight oxidation during spraying
process and/or preexisting in the feed material. The latter reason
for the oxides formation in the structure of coatings under study
looks to be more relevant as the chances of in-ﬂight oxidation
are meager in case of the shrouded plasma spraying.
The high temperature cyclic oxidation of the Ni–20Cr coated
superalloys Superni 75, 600 and 601 proceeded by a diffu-
sion controlled mechanism as could be perceived from the
parabolic nature of the weight gain versus number of cycles
plots, Figs. 2 and 3. This indicates that the coating could act as
barrier to the oxidizing species during long durations of usage.
The minor deviations from the parabolic rate law as shown by
the coated specimens are in agreement with the ﬁndings of Choi
et al.[17], who attributed this scatter in data to the formation and
rapid growth of inhomogeneous oxides during oxidation process
of the plasma sprayed NiCrAlY coatings on Nimonic 80A. The
coated superalloys can be arranged in the following order with
regard to their oxidation resistance on the basis of cumulative
weight gain:
Superni 601 > Superni 600 > Superni 75
The XRD analysis indicates the formation of the phases like
NiO, Cr
2O3 and NiCr2O4 in the scales of coated superalloys.
The surface EDAX analysis for the oxidized specimens has also
revealed the presence of NiO in the top scale, which is further
strengthened by the greenish color of the scale [18]. The cross-
sectional EDAX analysis for the coated specimens also indicates
the formation of NiO as a main phase in the top scales. The
results are consistent with the ﬁndings of Sundararajan et al.[5].
The oxide Cr
2O3 may partially inhibit oxidation of the substrate
alloys by blocking the diffusion of the reacting species towards
the substrate alloys, as has been suggested by Stroosnijder et al.
[19] and Toma et al.[20]. Presence of the spinel NiCr
2O4 in the
oxide scales also helps to develop oxidation resistance as the
spinel phases usually have much smaller diffusion coefﬁcients
of the cations and anions than those in their parent oxides [21].
It could be seen fromFig. 2 that the major chunk of the over-
all weight gain was conceived only during the initial cycles of
the oxidation study in all the cases. This may be attributed to
the rapid formation of the oxides at the coating splat boundaries
and within open pores due to the penetration of the oxidizing
species along the splat boundaries/open pores during the early
cycles of the study, as has been proposed by Choi et al. [17]
and Niranatlumpong et al.[22] for the plasma sprayed NiCrAlY
coatings on Nimonic 80A and SS41 alloy substrates, respec-
tively. However, once the oxides are formed at the places of the
porosity and the splat boundaries, the coating becomes dense
and the diffusion of the oxidizing species to the internal por-
tions of the coatings gets slowed down and the growth of the
oxides becomes limited mainly to the surface of the specimens.
This, in turn, makes the weight gain and hence the oxidation rate
steady with the further progress of exposure time as has been
observed in the current study.
Further, the Ni–20Cr coating has been observed to be suc-
cessful in maintaining its continuous surface contact with all
the superalloy substrates under the study during the cyclic oxi-
dation run, as can be seen from the respective back scattered
images, Figs. 6–8. Moreover, it is clear from the cross-sectional
EDAX that oxygen has not reached the substrates in case of
Superni 75 and 601, whereas the amount of oxygen approach-
ing the substrates in case of Superni 600 is marginal. This reﬂects
the beneﬁcial effect of the coating as the substrate superal-
loys have not suffered oxidation. Furthermore, the oxide scales
for the Ni–20Cr coatings in general have shown very good
spallation resistance against the cyclic oxidation in air, which
indicates further effectiveness of the coatings under study as the

The dark phase as indicated in the bond coat regions of the

concentration of Al

frequency pulse detonation (HFPD) NiCrAlY and CoNiCrAlY
coatings onto the AISI 310 austenitic stainless steel samples at
a temperature of 1000

scales is also revealed by the cross-sectional EDAX analysis.
5. Conclusions
(1) The plasma sprayed Ni–20Cr coatings were found to be
useful in imparting high temperature oxidation resistance
to the Ni-based superalloys under investigation at the given
process parameters of plasma spray process.
(2) The plasma sprayed Ni–20Cr coating when subjected to the
cyclic oxidation in air at 900
◦C, was found to be successful
in maintaining its adherence with the substrate superalloys.
The oxide scales were also found to be intact and spallation-
free in general.
(3) In all the Ni–20Cr coated cases, the oxidation behavior was
found to be governed by parabolic rate law. Moreover, the
Ni–20Cr coating was found to successful to circumvent
the exposure of the oxygen to the substrate superalloys, in
general.
(4) The Ni–20Cr coating after exposure to the air oxidation
showed the presence of oxides of nickel and chromium, and
spinels containing nickel–chromium type mixed oxides.
