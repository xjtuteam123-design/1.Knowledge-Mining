ABSTRACT 
In order to improve the room and high temperature strength –ductility balance of ferritic 
Fe-Al-Ni based alloys strengthened by the B2-type NiAl precipitates, Fe-Al-N i-Cr-Mo alloys 
was developed. The microstructures and tensile properties of the alloys were examined 
focusing on Mo content. We found that the 2 at.% or 4 at.% Mo addition is effective in the 
improvement of the elongation of the Fe-Al-Ni based alloys at room temperature (RT) while 
maintaining high strength, which reflects the suppression of intergranular fracture by the 
strengthening of the bcc matrix by Mo addition. We also found that the RT tensile properties 
and deformation behavior of the alloys depend strongly on the volume fraction and the size of 
the NiAl precipitates. For instance, the yield stress decreases by the change in the primary 
slip direction from <111> to <001> and the formation of the Orowan loops with increasing 
the volume fraction and the mean diameter of the NiAl precipitates. In addition, the alloy 
with the appropriate Mo content exhibits a high yield stress above 660 MPa up to 923 K, 
owing to the solid-solution hardening by Mo addition and the low growth rate of the NiAl 
precipitates even at high temperatures. These findings indicate that the alloys have a great 
potential for steam turbines of advanced ultra‐supercritical thermal power plants. 
 
 
Keywords: Ferritic steels; NiAl precipitation; Tensile properties; Microstructure

1. Introduction 
 Ferritic heat-resistant steels such as 9-12Cr steels and modified 9Cr-1Mo steels are 
widely used for steam turbines of thermal power plants because of their low cost, high 
thermal conductivity and low thermal expansion coefficient [1 –3]. However, their mechanical 
properties limit the operational temperature to below 900 K, associated with the 
microstructural degradation at high temperatures [1,2]. Therefore, it is required to develop 
new ferritic heat-resistant steels, which can be used for advanced ultr a‐supercritical (A-USC) 
thermal power plants of which operational temperature is 973 K [2]. Ferritic heat-resistant 
steels strengthened by the NiAl precipitates have attracted much attention, as they address 
this concern. The NiAl is one of the B2-type intermetallics, which has high melting point 
(approximately 1200 K). The mechanical properties of the NiAl were studied and reviewed 
by Grala [4]. Teng et al . have investigated the microstructures and mechanical properties of 
Fe-Al-Ni based alloys which consist of a body-centered cubic (bcc) matrix and the NiAl 
precipitates [5,6]. They reported that the NiAl precipitates with 100 –200 nm in diameter are 
effective in the improvement of strengths at both ambient and high temperatures. Sun et al . 
examined the load partitioning between the bcc matrix and the NiAl precipitates by in-situ 
neutron diffraction and crystal plasticity finite element modeling [7]. Kapoor et al . reported 
the effects of Mn or Cu addition on the microstructures and mechanical properties of 
Fe-Al-Ni based alloys [8,9]. 
In our previous study, we found that the fine NiAl precipitates with a diameter of 10 –30 
nm significantly strengthen Fe-Al-Ni based alloys in the wide temperature range [10]. For 
instance, the yield stresses of Fe-23Al-6Ni (at.%) alloy single crystals containing the fine 
NiAl precipitates are higher than 1250 MPa and 500 MPa at room temperature (RT) and 923 
K, respectively [10]. This significant strengthening is caused by the difference in the primary 
slip systems between the bcc matrix and the NiAl precipitates. In general, the primary slip

system for bcc metals is {101} <111> slip, whereas that for the B2-NiAl is {110} <001> slip 
[11,12]. However, the fine NiAl precipitates in the bcc matrix are sheared by a pair of 
1/2<111> dislocations, when the single crystals are deformed at <149> orientation suitable 
for ( 
01 1)[111] slip (the Schmid factor is 0.5) [10]. This activation of <111> slip unfavorable 
for the NiAl precipitate results in huge hardening. On the other hand, if the loading axis is 
chosen to have <557> orientation favorable for (
10 1)[001] slip, the crystals are deformed by 
<001> slip [10]. In general, occurrence of <001> slip in bcc metals is impossible [11 ]. 
However, <001> dislocations move in both the bcc matrix and the NiAl precipitates at the 
orientation. This also leads to strong precipitation hardening. We term this phenomenon “slip 
frustration hardening (SFH)” because it is caused by frustration in slip direction between the 
matrix and precipitates [10]. Recently, we also found that the SFH works not only for 
Fe-Al-Ni based alloys but also for Fe-Al-Co based alloys containing the fine B2-type CoAl 
precipitates [13]. These reports predict that the SFH is expected to become a new 
strengthening mechanism for ferritic heat-resistant steels. 
However, it is known that RT ductility of the polycrystalline Fe-Al-Ni based alloys 
strengthened by the NiAl precipitates is significantly low, owing to an intergranular fracture 
[14]. In the Fe-Al-Ni based alloys, the strength near the grain boundary is considered to be 
equivalent to that of the bcc matrix without the NiAl precipitates, though the grain interior is 
strengthened by the precipitates. This results in the intergranular fracture. In order to 
overcome this limitation, it is necessary to increase the strength of the bcc matrix. 
Solid-solution hardening is one of the effective methods for improving the strength of the bcc 
matrix. Furthermore, in this approach, the solute atoms should be preferentially partitioned to 
the bcc matrix and not to the NiAl precipitate to increase the strength of the bcc matrix. From 
this point of view, Mo is one of the candidates for solid-solution hardening, because Mo tends 
to distribute into the bcc matrix owing to a low solubility limit in the NiAl precipitates [15].

Thus, in the present study, in order to improve the room and high temperature strength –
ductility balance of Fe-Al-Ni based alloys with the B2-type NiAl precipitates, Mo was added 
to the alloys. The microstructures and mechanical properties of the Mo-doped alloys were 
investigated focusing on Mo content. Moreover, the morphology such as volume fraction and 
size of the NiAl precipitates generally plays an important role in mechanical properties of the 
alloys [10]. Therefore, to optimize the morphology of the NiAl precipitates, the effect of the 
volume fraction and the size of the NiAl precipitates on the mechanical properties of the 
alloys was examined.

3.2 RT mechanical properties of Mo-doped AC alloys 
The hardness ( H) of the AC alloys was measured by the nanoindentation hardness tests 
which was performed for <100>-oriented grains (Fig. 5). The nanoindentation hardness 
increases linearly from 4.5 to 5.0 GPa with increasing Mo content up to 4 at.%, followed by a 
rapid increase in hardness. As mentioned above, the volume fraction and the size of the NiAl 
precipitates in the AC alloys is insensitive to Mo content (Fig. 3). Moreover, the indenter size 
is smaller than the D
m. Thus, one can conclude that the hardness increase of the AC alloys 
with Mo is caused by a solid-solution hardening of the bcc matrix by Mo atoms. 
Stress –strain (S–S) curves of the AC alloys tensile-deformed at RT are shown in Fig. 6.

2MoAC at RT is attributed to both SFH and the Orowan strengthening.

and the 4MoAC. This means that the fracture mode of the 2MoAC and the 4MoAC changes 
to brittle –ductile mixed fracture. This is because the strength near the grain boundary in these 
alloys is improved, associated with the solid solution hardening of the bcc matrix by Mo 
atoms. As a result, the intergranular fracture is partly suppressed, which leads to large plastic 
deformation of the 2MoAC and the 4MoAC at RT. However, further increase in Mo content 
results in an increase in D
f, and consequently, the Df of the 5MoAC is larger than the Dm. 
This means that a brittle transgranular fracture also takes place in the 5MoAC. As mentioned 
above, the hardness of bcc matrix of the 5MoAC is significantly increased by the solid 
solution hardening by Mo atoms. The hardness of the bcc matrix is so high that brittle 
fracture occurs in grain interior. This results in a decrease in EL of the 5MoAC pulled at RT, 
though the 
/g86y is highest among the AC alloys. 
Based on these results, it is concluded that the addition of appropriate amount of Mo (2 –
4 at.% in the present study) is effective in improving RT ductility of Fe-Al-N i based alloys 
while maintaining high strength. This is caused by the strengthening of the bcc matrix due to 
solid solution hardening by Mo atoms. However, excessive Mo addition ( Ӎ5 at.%) leads to 
the embrittlement of the bcc matrix, and consequently, decreases the RT ductility.

4.3 Deformation behavior of 2MoAC alloys at high temperatures 
The 2MoAC exhibits higher strength than some ferritic and austenitic heat-resistant 
steels as shown in Fig. 14. As mentioned above, the NiAl precipitates in the 2MoAC 
(correspond to stage II) are sheared by the 1/2<111> and <001> dislocations at RT (Fig. 8). In

addition, some coarse NiAl precipitates are bypassed by the 1/2<111> dislocations. At 923 K, 
the NiAl precipitates keep the f
p and the Dp approximately 7% and 30 nm, respectively, due 
to low growth rate of the NiAl precipitates (Fig. 15 (a)). Thus, the NiAl precipitates are not 
only bypassed but also sheared by dislocations during deformation even at 923 K (Figs. 15 
(b),(c)). Both the shearing and the bypass of the NiAl precipitates lead to high strength of the 
alloy at 923 K. It should be noted that the Burgers vector of the straight uncoupled 
dislocations at 923 K is [001] (Fig. 15 (b)). We have reported that the slip system for 
Fe-Al-Ni single crystals with the NiAl precipitates changes from <111> slip to <001> slip at 
923 K even at <149> orientation favorable for (

critical resolved shear stress (CRSS) for <001> slip of the NiAl phase is known to rapidly 
decrease with increasing temperature [12]. 
 
5. Conclusions 
The microstructures and mechanical properties of the Fe-Al-Ni-Cr-Mo alloys were 
investigated focusing on Mo content. Moreover, the effect of the morphology such as volume 
fraction and size of the NiAl precipitates on the mechanical properties was examined. The 
conclusions drawn in this study are given below: 
1. The hardness of the bcc matrix is increased by the solid solution hardening by Mo atoms. 
However, the influence of Mo addition on the morphology of the NiAl precipitates is 
insignificant. The misfit strain between the bcc matrix and the NiAl precipitates decreases 
with increasing Mo content. The small misfit strain leads to a low growth rate of the NiAl 
precipitates at high temperatures. 
2. The strength near the grain boundary in the Mo-doped alloys is improved, associated with 
an increase in strength of the bcc matrix by the solid solution hardening from Mo atoms. 
As a result, the intergranular fracture is partly suppressed by small amount of Mo addition,

## Page 20

19 
 
and consequently, the RT ductility is improved. 
3. The volume fraction and the size of the NiAl precipitates can be controlled by aging 
treatment in the temperature range of 473 –973 K without significantly influencing the 
microstructure of the bcc matrix. 
4. The RT tensile properties of the 2 at.% Mo-doped alloys depend strongly on the volume 
fraction and the size of the NiAl precipitates. The yield stress at RT is related to the 
dislocation type (1/2<111> or <001>) and the dislocation motion (shearing or bypassing). 
5. The 2MoAC alloys exhibit not only good RT strength –ductility balance but also excellent 
tensile strength, especially below 923 K. This is because the alloy is strengthened by the 
NiAl precipitates though SFH and/or Orowan strengthening. 
6. These findings indicate that the alloys have a great potential for steam turbines of A-USC 
thermal power plants, though further improvement of high temperature strength is 
necessary for the practical application of the alloys.
