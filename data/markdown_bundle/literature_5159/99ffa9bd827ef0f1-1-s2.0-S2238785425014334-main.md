Eutectic high-entropy alloys (EHEAs) have garnered significant attention due to their uniform and fine micro -

parameters indicates that the triple phases — FCC phase, Laves phase, and B2 phase — can be stabilized when 
conditions 6.7 ≤ VEC < 7.8, δ > 5 %, and Δ X > 7 % are met.
1. Introduction
Currently, strength and plasticity are two critical indicators for 
designing structural materials. However, there is often a tendency for 
these properties to be mutually exclusive, a phenomenon referred to in 
the literature as the “ strength-plasticity paradox ” [ 1 – 3 ]. Recently, it has 
been hypothesized that multi-major element alloys may hold the po -
tential to resolve this paradox. Professors Yeh and Cantor introduced a 
novel concept of high-entropy alloys (HEAs) to address the problem of 
strong-plasticity mismatch [ 4 , 5 ]. Unlike conventional alloys, HEAs are 
defined as alloys that contain at least five major elements, each present 
in a concentration range of 5 % – 35 % [ 6 ]. This concept is proposed to 
offer a vast compositional space for the design of metallic materials, 
along with significant potential for optimizing microstructural and 
mechanical properties. Compared to traditional alloys, HEAs have 
garnered significant attention in the field of metal materials due to their 
key benefits, exhibiting numerous outstanding properties such as high 
strength, excellent ductility, and superior wear and corrosion resistance 
[ 7 – 10 ]. Nevertheless, the insufficient fluidity during casting and the 
segregation of compositions in HEAs present considerable obstacles to 
their practical use, largely stemming from the variations in density and 
melting temperatures of the primary elements [ 11 ]. Lu et al. introduced 
the concept of EHEAs for the first time, utilizing the principles of 
eutectic alloys in the design of HEAs. This approach aims to develop new

are reported to be in-situ composites formed through solidification at 
eutectic temperatures. The presence of multiple elements can result in 
significant solid solution strengthening and lattice distortion, while also 
contributing to the refinement of the layered or rod-like microstructure. 
This enhances the castability of the material, providing a synergistic 
combination of strength and ductility, which makes it a preferred choice 
for high-temperature applications [ 12 – 14 ].
It is well established that EHEAs contain a greater variety of elements 
and a higher overall elemental content. These alloys exhibit complex 
interactions among multiple atoms. Additionally, due to the four pri -
mary effects associated with HEAs, the behaviors of elemental diffusion, 
solidification nucleation growth, and eutectic organization growth differ 
significantly from those observed in conventional alloys [ 15 – 18 ]. 
Nonetheless, achieving a balance between strength and plasticity has 
historically posed a challenge for structural materials. In recent decades, 
materials with non-homogeneous properties — such as bimodal, lamella 
structure, gradient grains, multiphase, and hierarchical microstructural 
configurations — have been developed to address the challenge of 
achieving strong plasticity [ 19 , 20 ]. Currently, two types of EHEAs are 
typically characterized by the presence of the FCC phase combined with 
either the B2 phase or the Laves phase. At room temperature, as-cast 
EHEAs (FCC + B2 phase) have excellent ductility, but the B2 phase 
has poor strength of its own, and the yield strength of such EHEAs is 
obviously insufficient [ 21 – 23 ]. Zhang et al. employed a normalizing 
treatment in air rather than a quenching treatment in water. This 
approach enabled the alloy to effectively accommodate dislocations 
during significant deformations, leading to work hardening and result -
ing in outstanding overall mechanical properties [ 24 ]. FCC phase and 
Laves phase type EHEAs have excellent yield strength but limited plas -
ticity. This is primarily attributed to the hard Laves phase intermetallic 
compounds, which are challenging to deform due to their inherent low 
plasticity [ 25 – 28 ]. He et al. found that when CoCrFeNiNb

high-temperature stability, which improved the overall mechanical 
properties of the alloys [ 29 ]. To tackle the common issue of 
strength-plasticity mismatch that frequently arises in EHEAs in their 
as-cast state, efforts have been made to introduce an additional phase. 
Under optimal conditions, the combination of three distinct phases has 
the potential to refine grain size and create a more finely spaced 
arrangement of eutectic phases. This progress creates exciting oppor -
tunities for the development of nanostructured eutectic alloy composites 
featuring micrometer-scale dendritic structures [ 30 ]. However, the 
design methods proposed by current researchers primarily focus on 
two-phase EHEAs. These methods include the simple mixing method 
[ 31 ], the enthalpy of mixing method [ 32 ], the infinite solid solution 
method [ 33 ], the pseudo-binary method [ 34 ], and the semiquantitative 
method based on CALPHAD [ 35 , 36 ]. However, there is a lack of 
reasonable and effective design methods for TEHEAs. It has been found 
that TEHEAs, featuring three different phase combinations, exhibit more 
complex microstructures compared to conventional alloys. This 
complexity is evident in the subtle variations of grain sizes within the 
eutectic zone, which predominantly manifests as rods and lamellae. 
Simultaneously, the intricate phase distribution, microdispersion of 
grains, and variability in size contribute to the remarkable mechanical 
properties of these alloys [ 30 , 37 ]. Such a microstructure not only pro -
vides strength support for the alloy but also offers a favorable balance of 
plasticity for plastic deformation, resulting in the potential to achieve 
significant advantages in material properties.
Due to the cocktail effect of high entropy alloys, we try to introduce 
B2 phase and Laves at the same time into FCC based EHEAs with the aim 
of designing scale EHEAs with micrometer-scale dendritic phase and 
nanostructured multiphase. This opens up a new research direction for 
the microstructure modulation of EHEAs. Research has shown that FCC 
+ B2 EHEAs can be created from CoCrFeNi and NiAl through a pseudo- 
binary approach [ 12 , 13 ]. Additionally, the combination of CoCrFeNi 
and Nb results in FCC + Laves phase EHEAs [ 25 , 26 ], while a B2 + BCC 
eutectic alloy is produced from NiAl and Nb [ 38 , 39 ]. Therefore, we have 
designed a triple-phase hypoeutectic HEAs that consists of an FCC phase, 
a NiAl phase, and a Laves phase within the CoCrFeNi – NiAl – Nb system, 
building upon our previous work [ 40 , 41 ]. Meanwhile, research has 
demonstrated that the optimal mechanical properties of EHEAs are 
frequently not found at the eutectic composition point [ 26 ]. Due to the 
high content of the Laves phase, which is detrimental to ductility, the Nb
