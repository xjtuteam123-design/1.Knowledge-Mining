Enhancing Mechanical Properties of Mg-6.0Gd-1.0Y-0.5Zr
Alloy by Thermomechanical Treatment
Meng Zhang, Hongge Yan, Qiang Li, Jihua Chen, Weijun Xia, Huaming Zhu, Bin Su, Jianling Hu, and Jiale He
Submitted: 24 January 2022 / Revised: 23 April 2022 / Accepted: 11 June 2022 / Published online: 19 September 2022
Thermomechanical treatment was conducted on the hot-extruded Mg-6Gd-1Y-0.5Zr (wt.%) alloy plates.
The effect of cold rolling reduction on mechanical properties at peak aging state was investigated. The
results show that the pre-rolling deformation can accelerate the aging process and shorten the peak aging
time. With the rolling reduction increasing from 0 to 15%, the aging time to reach peak decreases from 272
to 200 h. The peak strength of the aged alloy increases with the increase in rolling reduction. With the
reduction of 10%, the tensile strength, the yield strength and the elongation to rupture of the peak-aged
alloy at room temperature are 400, 344 MPa and 6.1%, respectively, while those at 240 /C176C are 310,
286 MPa and 11.6%, respectively.
Keywords aging, high temperature strength, Mg-Gd-Y -Zr alloy,
precipitation strengthening, thermomechanical
treatment
1. Introduction
In recent years, with the rapid development of aeronautics
and astronautics, there has been an urgent requirement for light-
weight high-strength magnesium alloys with favorable heat
resistance in these ﬁelds (Ref 1-6). The Mg-Gd-Y alloy system
is the most promising candidate due to the remarkable effect of
age hardening and excellent mechanical properties at both room
temperature and high temperature (Ref 7).
As a typical age-hardenable alloy, the aging precipitation
sequence of Mg-Gd-Y alloy is S.S.S.S(hcp) ﬁ b‘‘(D0
19) ﬁ
b’(cbco) ﬁ b1(fcc) ﬁ b(fcc) (Ref 8). The b’ phase that
precipitates on {11 2 0} with triangular distribution is the main
strengthening phase for the peak-aged alloy and contributes the
most to the strength (Ref 9, 10). Therefore, how to obtain the
uniformly distributed metastable aging precipitates is the key to
improve the strength and the fracture behavior of magnesium
alloys at room temperature and high temperature (Ref 11, 12).
In order to develop the Mg-Gd-Y alloy with high strength, the
relatively high contents of Gd and Y are added, which leads to
the high density and the serious deterioration of the plasticity.
However, the lower contents of Gd and Y result in the
prolonged aging time and the strength loss of Mg-Gd-Y alloy.
Moreover, the partial replacement of Gd by other alloying
elements and the regulation of the Gd/Y ratio cannot effectively
solve these problems (Ref 13, 14).
Thermomechanical treatment is a process involved with
strain hardening and phase transformation strengthening, which
is able to promote the precipitation of the second phases (Ref
5). It has been widely adopted to improve the properties of Al
alloy (Ref 15), Cu alloy (Ref 16), steel (Ref 17), etc. So far,
there are few reports available on the thermomechanical
treatment of Mg-Gd-Y alloys. Li et al. (Ref 18) have reported
that the peak-aged strength of the as-cast Mg-6.0Gd-1.0Y -0.5Zr
alloy experienced tensile pre-deformation and aged at 200 /C176 C
increases from 273 to 314 MPa and the peak-aged time
decreases from 128 to 48 h in comparison with T6 state.
Up to now, almost no studies have been reported on the
effect of thermomechanical treatment on high-temperature
mechanical properties of Mg-Gd-Y alloys. In this paper, the
mechanical properties of the Mg-6.0Gd-1.0Y -0.5Zr alloy plates
pre-deformed with different rolling reductions followed by
isothermal aging treatment are studied, with the aim of
achieving higher strength and shortening the peak-aged time.
Moreover, the effects of cold-rolling deformation and aging
treatment on the microstructure, mechanical properties of the
alloy as well as the related mechanisms are clariﬁed.

3.2 Effect of Pre-deformation on Aging Behavior
Figure 6 shows the age hardening curves of the cold-rolled
Mg-6.0Gd-1.0Y -0.5Zr alloys with different pre-strains aged at
175 /C176 C. Table 2 presents the detailed aging hardness results.
The as-extruded alloy has the hardness of 94 HV , which is
much higher than that reported in the literature (50 HV) (Ref
18). The dynamic precipitates and the small grain size
contribute to the high hardness of the alloy.
In addition, the peak-aged time decreases with the increase
in rolling reduction, and the peak-aged time is reduced from
272 h for the as-extruded state to 200 h for the alloy with the
rolling reduction of 15%. The aging process accelerates and the
peak hardness increases with the increase in the rolling
reduction, indicating that cold rolling can accelerate the aging
precipitation.

Gd and Y elements in the alloy and the saturated solid
solubilities of the elements at a given aging temperature.
Therefore, the volume fraction of precipitates changes slightly
during the thermomechanical treatment.
3.3 Effect of Pre-deformation on Mechanical Properties
Figure 11 shows the typical stress–strain curves of the peak-
aged alloys with different pre-strains at room temperature.
Table 3 offers the speciﬁc room temperature mechanical
property data. The ultimate tensile strength and the yield
strength of the peak-aged alloy increase obviously with the
higher pre-strain. The ultimate tensile strength increases from
374 to 407 MPa with the rolling reduction from 0 to 15%,
while the yield strength increases from 279 to 366 MPa.
However, the elongation to rupture decreases from 10.2 to 3%.
In light of strength and elongation, 10% rolling reduction plus
aging at 175 /C176 C for 212 h is the optimal process and the alloy
exhibits the yield strength of 344 MPa, the tensile strength of
400 MPa and the elongation to rupture of 6.1%. As shown in
Table 3, the as-extruded Mg-10Gd-3Y -0.5Zr alloy (Ref 32) and
the as-forged Mg-9Gd-4Y -0.5Zr alloy (Ref 33) have higher
contents of Gd and Y , but their mechanical properties in the
peak-aged state are lower than the counterparts of Mg-6.0Gd-
1.0Y -0.5Zr (GW61K) alloy after the thermomechanical treat-
ment. Therefore, the thermomechanical treatment has a positive
effect on the mechanical property of the alloy with the lower
RE content at room temperature.
Figure 12 shows the typical stress–strain curves of the peak-
aged alloys with different pre-strains at high temperatures.
Table 4 offers the speciﬁc high-temperature mechanical prop-
erty data. The high-temperature strength of the peak aged alloy
increases with the higher pre-strain, while the elongation is just
the reverse. As for the same pre-strain, the peak-aged alloy
exhibits lower strength with the increasing tensile temperature,
while the elongation increases slightly.
According to the previous analysis, the pre-deformation has
a positive effect on the dislocation density of the alloy (Table 1)
and the high dislocation density facilitates the dimensional
reﬁnement of the precipitates in the alloy. In comparison with
the tensile properties at room temperature, the strength of the
aged alloy at 240 /C176 C decreases obviously. As the tensile
temperature increases, the pinning effect of defects and
precipitates on dislocations is weakened. During peak aging,
b’ is the main strengthening phase in the alloy, and it is usually

3.4 Strengthening Mechanism of GW61K Alloy
by Thermomechanical Treatment
High-density dislocations introduced by the deformation
process provide nucleation sites for the precipitated phase of
the aged alloy and promote nucleation, thus shortening the peak
aging time and reﬁning the precipitates.
As shown in Fig. 10, the deformation process has little
effect on the volume fraction of the precipitated phase.
Therefore, the retained content of Gd and Y atoms in
magnesium matrix with different rolling reduction is nearly
same. Moreover, there is no signiﬁcant difference in the effect
of solid solution strengthening of the alloy with different rolling
reductions. As shown in Fig. 2, the grain size does not change
signiﬁcantly, and thus, it can be assumed that cold deformation
has little effect on the grain size. Therefore, the contribution of
the thermomechanical treatment to the strength of the alloy
mainly comes from deformation strengthening and precipitation
strengthening.

4. Conclusions
(1) The aging kinetics of Mg-6.0Gd-1.0Y -0.5Zr alloy plate
is signiﬁcantly improved by the thermomechanical treat-
ment. The peak aging time is shortened from 272 to
200 h and the hardness is increased from 123 to 125HV
with the rolling reduction increasing from 0 to 15%.
(2) Mechanical properties of Mg-6.0Gd-1.0Y -0.5Zr alloy
plate at room temperature, 150 and 240 /C176 C are obvi-
ously improved by the thermomechanical treatment.
(3) The mechanical property improvement of the alloy at
room temperature by the thermomechanical treatment
can be ascribed to the precipitation of b’ and high-den-
