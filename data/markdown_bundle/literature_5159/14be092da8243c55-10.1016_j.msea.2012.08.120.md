abstract
The deformation behavior and microstructure evolution of a high-strength Mg 93.96Zn2Y4Sr0.04 (at%)
alloy with long-period stacking ordered (LPSO) structure subjected to equal channel angular pressing
(ECAP) were systematically investigated. The results showed that, after equal channel angular pressing,
the alloy exhibited excellent mechanical properties with ultimate tensile strength of 408 and 375 MPa
at ambient and elevated temperatures, respectively. Such excellent mechanical properties could be
attributed to the long-period stacking ordered structure, dislocation pile-up in the bent LPSO structure
and appearance of subboundaries induced by dislocation tangles in the X-phase. These ﬁndings are
expected to provide the in-depth insights into methods for optimizing the mechanical properties of
magnesium alloys with LPSO structure.

1. Introduction
Magnesium alloys, as one of the lightest structural materials
with high speciﬁc strength, have become increasingly attractive
due to their applications in the automobile and aerospace indus-
tries [1,2]. However, their low absolute strength and poor high-
temperature properties hinder their widespread applications.
In the last decade, magnesium alloys with long-period stacking
ordered (LPSO) structure, exhibiting excellent mechanical proper-
ties and unique microstructures and accordingly attracting
progressive interest, have been dev e l o p e di nt h et e r n a r yM g – R E – Z n
systems (where RE represents rare earth elements) [3–6].
In 2001, Kawamura et al. used rapidly solidiﬁed powder metal-
lurgy processing to develop the Mg 97Y2Zn1 (at%) alloy, which
possesses an extremely high yield strength of 610 MPa with an
elongation-to-failure of 5% at ambient temperature[3].T h e s ee x c e l -
lent properties seem to originate not only from grain reﬁnement,
increase in solid solubility and homogeneity but also from a novel
precipitate with a long-period sta cking ordered (LPSO) structure.
With in-depth investigations of the long-period stacking ordered
structure, the processing method was generalized to Cu-mold casting
with an induction melting method and conventional casting as well
[4–6]. Meanwhile, some researches focused on the effect of plastic
processing on the magnesium alloys strengthened with LPSO struc-
ture using hot extrusion, hot-rolling processing and severe plastic
deformation (SPD) techniques[7–11]. Equal channel angular pressing
(ECAP), as one of the SPD techniques, has been widely used to
fabricate bulk UFG materials in the AZ and ZK series magnesium
alloys [12–15]. However, few works have been reported on the
fabrication of the magnesium alloys with LPSO structure using ECAP.
In our preliminary work [16],M g
93.96Zn2Y4Sr0.04 (at%) alloy
with long-period stacking ordered structure, was successfully
prepared by conventional permanent mold casting. A simulta-
neous increase in both strength and elongation-to-failure was
obtained. The ultimate tensile strength and elongation-to-failure
of the as-cast alloy at ambient temperature and elevated tem-
perature (200 1C) were 226 MPa and 4.6%, 205 MPa and 5.8%,
respectively. Notably, the formation of LPSO structure in the alloy
was found to maintain the ultimate strength at 200 1C at basically
the same level as at ambient temperature. However, the tensile
property of the as-cast alloy was not very high and some strength
improvement could still be made for the alloy.
Therefore, in the present work, a high-strength Mg93.96Zn2Y4Sr0.04
(at%) alloy with LPSO structure was processed using equal channel
angular pressing (ECAP). It is the aim of this study to reveal the
microstructure evolution during the ECAP process and the micro-
scopic mechanisms of the LPSO structure on strengthening of the
alloy.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructure of the as-cast Mg 93.96Zn2Y4Sr0.04 alloy
Fig. 1 shows the SEM micrograph and the XRD pattern of the
as-cast Mg93.96Zn2Y4Sr0.04 alloy, demonstrating a duplex microstruc-
ture. As shown inFig. 1(a), a continuous secondary phase appeared in
the matrix. Lamellar contrast could be observed in some regions of
the secondary phase. Standardless quantitative analysis of 5 different
areas indicated that the average chemical compositions of the matrix
and the secondary phase were Mg–1.3 70.3 at% Y–0.270.3 at% Zn
and Mg–8.670.3 at% Y–4.370.3 at% Zn–0.270.3 at%Sr, respectively.
Clearly, the matrix contained barely any Zn and the lamellar phase
was signiﬁcantly rich in both Zn and Y. The XRD pattern revealed that
there are both a-Mg and X-phase (Mg 12Y1Zn1) [17] in the as-cast
alloy, as shown in Fig. 1(b). According to the XRD pattern and EDS
analyses, the matrix phase and the secondary phase could be
identiﬁed as a-Mg phase and X-phase, respectively. The microhard-
ness of the X-phase was 107 Hv0.05, which was much higher than that
of the a-Mg matrix (83 Hv 0.05). Moreover, no micro-crack was
observed around the indentation in the X-phase during the Vickers
hardness test, suggesting that the X-phase was not brittle. Therefore,
the X-phase was a strengthening secondary phase, which had an
important reinforcement effect on the alloy.
In our previous work [16], the microstructure characteristics of
the a-Mg matrix and X-phase in the Mg 93.96Zn2Y4Sr0.04 alloy have
been analyzed systematically by TEM investigation. As shown in
Fig. 2 , the matrix was consisted of 2H-Mg with slices of stacking
faults (SF) formed by segregation of Zn and Y in the bulk Mg
grains. The X-phase was mainly composed of 18R LPSO structure
and some Mg segments sandwiched within it. This sandwich
structure corresponded to the interlayer structure of lamellae
region in the SEM image of Fig. 1 (a). It is reported that the softer
Mg nano-slice can be sheared along the kinking direction via
prismatic slip of 1/2[0 0 0 1] Mg when the deformation occurs [6].
As a result, more compatible deformability may arise from the
coexistence of the 18R LPSO structure and Mg nano-slice in the
lamellae region.

3.3. The mechanical properties of the as-cast and ECAP processed
Mg93.96Zn2Y4Sr0.04 alloys
The tensile property of the alloy was improved obviously after
being processed by 4 passes of equal channel angular pressing
(ECAP). As shown in Fig. 8(a), the ultimate tensile strengths of the
as-cast alloy at ambient temperature and elevated temperature
(200 1C) were 226 and 205 MPa, respectively, and they were
increased by 4 passes of equal channel angular pressing to 408
and 375 MPa, respectively. However, the elongations of the ECAP
processed alloy were only slightly increased. Unlike the elonga-
tion-to-failure, its microhardness (estimated as 123 Hv 0.05) was
much higher than that of the as-cast alloy, which may be related
to dislocation pile-up caused by the LPSO phase kink bands and
the particle phase precipitated in the a-Mg matrix. The DSC curve
for the as-cast Mg 93.96Zn2Y4Sr0.04 alloy ( Fig. 8 (b)) showed that
the melting points of the secondary phase and the alloy were
estimated to be about 543 and 615 1C, respectively. As reported by

The examination of microstructural evolution revealed that
high density dislocations emerge and dislocations pile up at kink
bands in the X-phase during ECAP process. In addition, the
appearance of dislocation boundary and subboundary (caused
by high density of dislocations and dislocation tangles) was favor
to the strengthening. Furthermore, the interface of the a-Mg and
X-phase was unique, which limited the dislocation pile-up in the
X-phase without being expanded to the a-Mg matrix.
Therefore, it is thought that the excellent tensile property of
the Mg93.96Zn2Y4Sr0.04 alloy can be attributed to the strengthening
of the long-period stacking ordered structure, dislocation pile-up
in the bent LPSO structure and appearance of subboundaries in the
X-phase.
4. Conclusions
A high-strength Mg
93.96Zn2Y4Sr0.04 alloy was developed
successfully by conventional permanent mold casting combined
with ECAP processing. The ultimate tensile strength and
elongation-to-failure of the ECAP processed alloy at ambient
temperature and elevated temperature (200 1C) were 408 MPa,
5.1% and 375 MPa, 5.7%, respectively. The Mg 24Y5 phase with the
diameter of 80–500 nm precipitated in the a-Mg matrix during
the ECAP process. The strengthening of the alloy was related to
the dislocations pile-up in the bent LPSO structure, dispersion
strengthen of the ﬁne precipitated phase and appearance of
subboundaries in the X-phase.
