abstract
AlNb2TiV, a novel, light-weight, refractory high-entropy alloy (RHEA), with high speciﬁc strength and
excellent room-temperature deformability is identiﬁed. As cast, it has a dendritic microstructure with
an ordered B2 phase, low density ( ~6.19 g/cm 3), excellent speciﬁc yield strength (167 MPa*cm 3/g) and
deformability (strain >1.0 in compression). Slip occurs by glide of 1/2 h111 i dislocations. Based on pre-
vious work, the Ti/Nb ratio of our RHEA falls in the optimal range for easy dislocation motion and good
deformability.

1. Introduction
The fundamentally new alloying concept behind the class of
‘‘high-entropy alloys” (HEAs) has inspired the investigation of
refractory alloys for high-temperature applications. Among these,
the refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) comprising mainly
refractory elements have the best potential for superior high-
temperature strength exceeding those of nickel-base superalloys.
Early representative RHEAs were the single-phase BCC VNbTaMoW
and TiZrHfNbTa systems [1,2], which exhibit some superior perfor-
mances; however, their densities were relatively high (12.36 and
9.94 g/cm 3). Recently a light-weight AlNbTiV RHEA was investi-
gated, which has low density (5.59 g/cm 3) and high speciﬁc yield
strength [3]. However, it exhibited poor room-temperature plastic-
ity in compression with work softening initiating almost immedi-
ately after yielding suggestive of cracking right from the start of
plastic deformation. These light-weight compositions need to be
further investigated to identify ways to improve room-
temperature plasticity.
To that end, we evaluated potential light-weight ductile RHEAs
based on the Al-Nb-Ti-V system. We varied the Nb fraction in the
AlNbxTiV system because of the intrinsic ductility of Nb, and con-
sidered additionally the modiﬁed Hume-Ruthery rules [4] and
the theoretical density ( qmix ¼

2. Experimental
Drop-cast ingots of the AlNb 2TiV (Al20Nb40Ti20V20 in at.%) RHEA
were prepared by arc melting mixtures of pure metals (>99.9 wt%)
in a high-purity argon atmosphere using the same arc melter and
similar procedures as described previously [5]. Density was mea-
sured be ~6.19 g/cm 3 following the Archimedes’ principle.
Phases were identiﬁed by a Shimadzu XRD-7000S X-ray diffrac-
tometer (XRD). Microstructures were observed in a Zeiss AURIGA
FIB-SEM scanning electron microscope (SEM) equipped with
energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) X-Max N after electro-
chemical polishing. Higher magniﬁcation microstructure analysis
was conducted using a JEOL JEM-2010-HT transmission electron
microscope (TEM).
Compressive tests were performed at an engineering strain rate
of 10/C0 3 s/C0 1 at room temperature in air and at 600, 800 and 1000 ℃
in Ar using cylindrical specimens with dimensions of U6 /C2 9m m3.

3. Results and discussion
The inset in Fig. 1 b shows the XRD pattern of the AlNb 2TiV
RHEA, which contains peaks corresponding to only the BCC struc-
ture. The lattice parameter was determined to be 325 ± 2 pm,
which is ~2% larger than that of the AlNbTiV RHEA (318 ± 3 pm
[3]). SEM-BSE image illustrates columnar dendrite grains with a
mean width >300 lm and dendrite-arm spacing of ~40 lm. Some
visible black particles were found to be rich in oxygen by EDS anal-
ysis, indicative of oxide inclusions, which has been frequently
found previously [6]. Elemental segregation is discernable by the
different contrasts across dendrites; nevertheless, SEM-EDS map-
ping ( Fig. 1 c) did not show evident composition ﬂuctuation. Two
positions located on a dendrite arm and in an interdendritic region
were further selected for SEM-EDS chemical analysis. Both regions
have very similar compositions with only a slight difference in the
Nb concentration ( ~1.5 at.%).

AlMo0.5NbTa0.5TiZr0.5 RHEA [7], in which the XRD analysis indi-

quently observed in Al-containing BCC-dominant HEAs. Zhang
et al. [8] suggested that Al stabilizes both the BCC and B2 struc-
tures, but the latter is more favored.
Representative stress–strain curves obtained in compression at
various temperatures are presented in Fig. 2a. Although it was pos-
sible to compress the specimens to very large strains ( ~1), here the
curves have been terminated at a maximum strain of 0.33 because,
beyond that point, the height-to-diameter ratio (aspect ratio) of
our specimens becomes less than 1 [9]. During the 600 /C176C test,
the thermocouple fell off at e ~ 0.2, so the test was stopped prema-
turely. At room temperature, the RHEA shows a very high yield
strength ( ry)o f ~1043 MPa. In spite of this high strength, signiﬁ-
cant compression was possible at room temperature, suggesting
that the AlNb 2TiV RHEA is very malleable. In contrast, its equia-
tomic counterpart (AlNbTiV) undergoes fracture at signiﬁcantly
smaller compressive strains [3]. An increase in temperature leads
to a decrease in ry, initially slowly to 694 and 612 MPa at 600
and 800 /C176C, respectively, and then rapidly to 144 MPa at 1000 /C176C.
The comparison of speciﬁc yield strengths ( rsy) between the
AlNb2TiV alloy and some previously reported RHEAs [1–3,10–13]
(Fig. 2 b) demonstrates that the AlNb 2TiV RHEA has a superior rsy
relative to most other RHEAs at the tested temperatures, except
the AlNbTiV and TiZrHfNbMo alloys. The AlNbTiV RHEA having
slightly higher rsy values typically exhibits brittle fracture at room
temperature [3] unlike the current RHEA which is signiﬁcantly
more deformable in compression. Similarly, the TiZrHfNbMo RHEA
also has low room-temperature ductility [12]. The TiZrHfNbTa
RHEA has good room-temperature ductility but with accelerated
oxidation behavior [14]. In comparison, the current AlNb 2TiV RHEA
possesses a good combination of density, strength, and
deformability.
To investigate the dislocation features, TEM analysis was con-
ducted on a compressed sample after a small plastic strain of
~0.01. TEM-BF micrographs ( Fig. 3 a) showing the typical disloca-
tion structure indicates that, in the early stage of plastic deforma-
tion, dislocations form pile-ups against grain boundaries. To
characterize the Burgers vectors of the dislocations, tilting experi-
ments were performed and, using the usual invisibility rules, the
Burgers vector of the BCC structure was identiﬁed as the typical
b ¼ a=2h111 i. A TEM-BF image under a two-beam diffraction con-
dition is shown in Fig. 3 b. To determine whether there is any
strain-induced phase transformation, as there is in some RHEAs
(e.g. Ta xHfZrTi [15]), XRD analysis was performed on a sample
compressed to e ~ 1.0, but there was no indication of any phase
transformation ( Fig. 3 c). In addition, deformation twins were not
detected after compression to a high strain, analogues to other
similar RHEAs [16,17]. In our current RHEA, deformation at room
temperature appears to be accommodated mainly by dislocation
activity.
Deformability at room temperature is related to the ease of
movement of dislocations. A strongly temperature-dependent
Peierls-Nabarro stress is generally considered to be a major factor
in the ductile-to-brittle transition of BCC metals and alloys at low
homologous temperatures. Chan [18] indicated that in Nb alloys,
adequate Ti contents (>24 at.%) are required for ductilization. The
Ti to Nb ratio in the current AlNb 2TiV RHEA is roughly consistent
with the optimal composition range. Davidson et al. [19] showed
that the intrinsic toughness of Nb-Ti alloys was not altered much
when Al was added. Some Nb-based alloys alloyed with V show
fairly large ductility in compression, such as the Nb–15A1–20V
alloy [20]. The VEC rule to predict intrinsic ductility of RHEAs by
Sheikh et al. [21] also indicated the ductility of the current RHEA.
4. Conclusions
The light-weight AlNb 2TiV RHEA investigated here shows a
dendritic microstructure with a B2 phase, low density ( ~6.19 g/
cm3), good speciﬁc yield strengths and exceptional room-
temperature deformability. The speciﬁc yield strength reaches
167 MPa*cm 3/g at room temperature and decreases with increas-
ing temperature.
