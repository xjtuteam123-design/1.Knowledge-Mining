Effect of Process on Microstructure and Elevated Temperature 
Mechanical Behavior of Multi-phase NiAl-Fe(Nb) Alloy 
Ping Han 1,a and Jianting Guo 2,b 
1School of Mathematics and Physics, Bohai University, Jinzhou 121013, China 
2Institute of Metal Research, Chinese Academy of Sciences, Shenyang 110016, China 
ahanpingbetty@yahoo.com.cn, bjtguo@imr.ac.cn 
Keywords : NiAl alloy; hot isostatic pressing(HIP); directional solidification; microstructure; 
mechanical behavior 
Abstract. The microstructure and elevated temperature mechan ical behavior of as-cast, HIPed and 
directionally solidified NiAl-Fe(Nb) Alloys have be en investigated. The results show that the 
microstructure of as-cast NiAl-Fe(Nb) alloy consist s of dendritic regions ( β) and interdendritic 
regions ( γ/γ′) with non-continuous NbNiAl(Laves) phase segregati ng at the interfaces of β and γ/γ′ 
phases. The HIP processing does not alter the micro structure of as-cast alloy. The longitudinal 
dendritic microstructure of the DS NiAl-Fe(Nb) allo y is regular. Room temperature elongations of 
as-cast and HIPed NiAl-Fe(Nb) alloys are all zero, however, 0.4% for the DS alloy. The strength 
and elongation of the HIPed alloy are higher than t hat of as-cast alloy. The yield strength and 
ultimate strength of the DS alloy are equivalent to that of as-cast alloy, but elongation of the DS 
alloy is higher than that of as-cast alloy, the elongation exceed 100% at 1273K, which indicates that 
the DS alloy can be deformed superplastically. 
Introduction 
NiAl-based intermetallics alloys are potential mate rials for high temperature structural 
applications, however, limited fracture toughness at room temperature and lack of creep strength at 
high temperature impede their use. An attractive ap proach to improve the ambinent ductility and 
toughness of NiAl is that the NiAl is alloyed by la rge amount of Fe element [1-9]. The extruded 
single phase Ni-30Al-20Fe alloy ( β phase) exhibited 6% tensile ductility and 800MPa yield strength 
at room temperature[1,2]. The room temperature elon gation of NiAl alloy can further be enhanced 
by embedding the toughed secondary phase( γ/γ′), which is γ-Ni phase containing γ′-Ni 3Al phase. 
The typical alloy is Ni-20Al-30Fe two phase alloy( β and γ/γ′), which possesses 20% tensile 
ductility at room temperature[3,4]. However, the hi gh temperature strength of Ni-20A-30Fe is 
rather poor. Thus, a Ni-20Al-27Fe-3Nb alloy, in whi ch the Fe has been partially replaced by the 
element Nb, was selected to improve the high temper ature strength by solid solutioning or 
precipitation strengthening. The aim of the present work is to investigate the effect of different 
processes including cast, cast+HIP and directional solidification on microstructure and elevated 
temperature mechanical behavior of multi-phase NiAl-Fe(Nb) alloy. 
Experimental procedure 
The Ni-20Al-27Fe-3Nb (at%, denoted as NiAl-Fe(Nb)) alloy was prepared by induction melting 
in vacuum from starting materials of 99.9% Ni, 99.9 % Al, 99.5%Fe and 99.0% Nb, cast rod is 
36mm in diameter. The cast rod was processed by hot isostatically pressing (HIP) at 1423K 
/100MPa for 2h in argon to eliminate microcracks. T he directional solidification was performed in 
vacuum at a pulling rate of 3mm/min, using a modifi ed Bridgman method with temperature 
gradient of 70-80K/cm through solid and liquid inte rface. The ingot is 16mm in diameter and 
220mm in length. Tensile specimens with a gauge sec tion of 2mm×2.5mm×16mm were 
electro-discharge machined, with the length paralle l to the direction of directional solidification. 
Tensile tests were performed at the SHIMADZU AG-250 KNE machine. Microstructures and 
fracture surfaces were examined by scanning electro n microscope (JSM-6301F) or by transmission 
electron microscopy (Philips TEM 420).

Results and discussion 
Microstructure 
The microstructure of as-cast NiAl-Fe(Nb) alloy is shown in Fig.1. Fig.1(a) shows the dendritic 
structure of β and γ/γ′ phases, which are coarse and irregular. The dendritic arm of isolated island is 
β phase, and the interdendritic region of connected skeleton is γ/γ′ phases. Fig.1(b) exhibits that 
there exist precipitates in the β and γ/γ′ phases. X-ray and EDS analyses indicate that β is 
(Ni,Al)(Fe,Al) phase, Fe atom substitutes Al atom, and occupies Al site, there are some Nb element 
in β phase, and acicular γ′ -(Ni,Fe) 
3(Al,Fe) phases precipitate in β phase. TEM centered dark field 
image from interdendritic region show that a disper sion of γ′ precipitate in γ matrix, as shown in 
Fig.1(c). Backscattered electron image and EDS analyses show that the white phases in the interface 
of β and γ/γ′ phases are NbNiAl(Laves) phase, as shown in Fig.1(d).

The microstructure of as-cast NiAl-Fe(Nb) alloy is stable, hot isostatic pressing or heat treatment 
of 1200°C×30h air cooling does not alter the micros tructure. No recrystallization occured because 
the samples were not deformed obviously during the hot isostatic pressing process. And the NbNiAl 
phase is much more stable than the Ni 
2AlZr (Heusler) phase of the NiAl-33.5Cr-0.5Zr alloy [10], 
which converts into Zr-rich phase after HIP process. 
The microstructures of transverse and longitudinal of the DS NiAl-Fe(Nb) alloy are shown in 
Fig.2(a) and (b), respectively. The DS alloy exhibi ts the typical dendritic structure, the black 
dendritic arm which is β-(Ni,Fe)(Fe,Al) phase arranges regularly along the direction of the 
directional solidification, as shown in Fig.2(b) , and the white interdendritic region is γ/γ′ phases. 
This kind of structure of relatively brittle dendri tic arms surrounded by relatively ductile 
interdendritic region, which is analogous to the cl adding during the extruding process, may be 
beneficial to gaining large elongation.

Effect of process on tensile property 
Comparison of tensile property at room and elevated temperature for the different processing 
NiAl-Fe(Nb) alloys are shown in Table 1. The as-cas t and cast+HIP alloys are brittle at room 
temperature, and fracture with no yield occurring, and the elongation of the DS NiAl-Fe(Nb) alloys 
at room temperature is 0.4%. The as-cast NiAl-Fe(Nb) alloy exhibits a certain ductility at 973K, and 
ductility increases evidently with increasing testi ng temperature. In addition, because of solid 
solution strengthening and precipitate strengthenin g resulting from the addition of Nb element, the 
yield strength and ultimate strength of the NiAl-Fe (Nb) alloy are much higher than that of the 
Ni-20Al-30Fe-Y alloy[11] at the same processing and testing condition.

Because the micro-defect was eliminated after the HIP treatment, the strength and ductility of the 
HIPed NiAl-Fe(Nb) alloy are higher than that of as- cast alloy, and the increase is large at low 
temperature. This indicates that the strength and d uctility of the NiAl-Fe(Nb) alloy are sensitive to 
micro-defect at low temperature, and the sensitivit y decrease at high temperature. The strength of 
the HIPed NiAl-Fe(Nb) alloy is the highest among the three processing alloys. 
The yield strength and ultimate strength of the DS NiAl-Fe(Nb) alloy are equivalent to that of 
as-cast NiAl-Fe(Nb) alloy, but the elongation of the DS NiAl-Fe(Nb) alloy is the highest among the 
three processing alloys, and the elongation exceeds 100% at 1273K, i. e. the DS NiAl-Fe(Nb) alloy 
exhibits superplastic deformation behavior. This is because the microstructure of the DS 
NiAl-Fe(Nb) alloy is regular, and the microstructure is that the brittle β phase is surrounded by γ/γ′ 
phase, which is analogous to the cladding of extrud ing process, therefore large elongation is 
obtained.

Conclusions 
(1). The microstructure of as-cast NiAl-Fe(Nb) allo y consists of dendritic regions( β) and 
interdendritic regions(γ/γ′) with non-continuous NbNiAl(Laves) phase segregati ng at the interfaces 
of β and γ/γ′ phases. The HIP processing or heat treatment of 1200°C×30h air cooling does not alter 
the microstructure of as-cast alloy. The longitudinal dendritic microstructure of the DS NiAl-Fe(Nb) 
alloy is regular. 
(2). The room temperature elongation for as-cast an d HIPed NiAl-Fe(Nb) alloys are all zero, 
however, 0.4% for the DS NiAl-Fe(Nb)alloy. The stre ngth and elongation of the HIPed alloy are 
higher than that of as-cast alloy. The yield streng th and ultimate strength of the DS alloy are 
equivalent to that of as-cast alloy, but the elonga tion of the DS alloy is higher than that of as-cast 
alloy, the elongation exceed 100% at 1273K, which i ndicates that the DS alloy can be deformed 
superplastically.
