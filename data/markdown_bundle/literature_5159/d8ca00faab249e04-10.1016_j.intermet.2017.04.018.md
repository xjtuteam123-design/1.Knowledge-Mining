ABSTRACT
Nb-Mo-Si based alloys have been prepared by arc melting in a water-cooled copper crucible under an argon
atmosphere. The eﬀ ects of Al, Cr, Hf and Ti additions on the phase components and stability, microstructures
and mechanical property of Nb-Mo-Si based alloys have been studied. The results indicated that the additions of
Al, Cr and Ti elements do not change the phase components of Nb-20Si-6Mo alloys, which are composed of Nb
solid solution (Nb ss) and β-Nb5Si3. The phase component is α-Nb5Si3 and Nb ss in Nb-20Si-6Mo-3Hf alloy. The
additions of Cr and Ti element make the Nb ss/Nb5Si3 eutectic morphology anomalous and coarsening. The
element segregation is obvious found with the additions of Hf and Ti. The enrichments of Hf and Ti change the
compositions of retained melt and promote the formation of ﬁne eutectic structure. After heating treatment at
1873K for 10 h, β-Nb
5Si3 phase translates into α-Nb5Si3 phase and γ-Nb5Si3 phase. The eutectic structures tend
to anomalous and coarsening. The Ti solid solution (Ti ss) phase was found in Nb-20Si-6Mo-20Ti alloy and the
formation mechanism of Ti ss phase was discussed. The high temperature (1523 K) compression strength of as
cast Nb-Mo-Si based alloys increased with the additions of Al, Cr, Hf, and decreased with Ti addition.
1. Introduction
Nb-Si based in-situ composites are considered for possible use in the
ﬁeld of high temperature structure materials due to their good
toughness and high strength [1,2]. It has become one of the most
promising candidate materials used at 1200 –1400 °C for the hot
components of jet engine [3].
The hot components of engine require super high temperature
material with high temperature strength, room temperature toughness
and oxidation resistance to achieve the balance of comprehensive
performance. However, the poor room temperature fracture toughness
and high temperature oxidation resistance of Nb-Si based ultrahigh
temperature composites cannot meet the requirements of the hot
components of engine [4]. The e ﬀects of Mo, Ti, Cr, Al, Hf and other
alloying elements on microstructures and mechanical properties of Nb-
Si based alloys were studied and reported in literature [5–10]. Mo can
strengthen the Nb
ss phase as a solid solution strengthening element
[11]. The room temperature fracture toughness of Nb-Si binary alloy is
signiﬁcantly increased by the addition of Ti [12]. The addition of Cr can
obtain Laves Cr 2Nb phase [13,14], which has the function of high
oxidation resistance and corrosion resistance. At present, Nb-Mo-Si
based alloys [15,16], Nb-Cr-Si based alloys [17,18] and Nb-Ti-Si based
alloys [7,14,19] have been developed.
It is worth noting that Mo addition can change the solidiﬁ cation
path of Nb-Si binary alloys. There are eutectic reaction L → Nb
ss + β-
Nb5Si3 (D8m) in Nb-Mo -Si ternary alloy system when Mo content is in
3 mol%∼45 mol% wide region [11]. There is much study on Nb-Mo-Si
based alloys. At present, the research mainly focuses on the inﬂ uence of
Mo on the alloy phase components, eutectic structure and properties of
Nb-Mo-Si based alloys [20–22].
However, the e ﬀects of alloying elements on Nb-Mo-Si based alloys
are less studied. This paper systematically studies the in ﬂuences of Al,
Cr, Hf and Ti elements on the microstructures and high temperature
compression strength of Nb-Mo-Si based alloys, and it is expected to
provide basis and reference for the improvement of comprehensive of
mechanical performance of Nb-Mo-Si based alloys.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Cast microstructures
Fig. 1 shows the XRD energy spectrum of the ﬁve alloys. As seen
from Fig. 1, only Nb
ss and β-Nb5Si3 exist in Nb-20Si-6Mo alloy, which
indicates that Mo addition tends to form β-Nb5Si3 [11,23]. The phase
components of Nb-20Si-6Mo-3Al alloy and Nb-20Si-6Mo-3Cr alloy are
the same with that of Nb-20Si-6Mo alloy. And only Nb ss and β-Nb5Si3
can be found. The Cr 2Nb phase was found in Nb-20Si-10Mo-3Cr alloy
[24], but no Cr 2Nb Laves in Nb-20Si-6Mo-3Cr alloy was observed,
which shows that the formation of Cr 2Nb phase may be a ﬀected when
the Mo content is relatively larger [24,25]. The addition of Hf can
change the phase components of Nb-20Si-6Mo alloy. Hf addition
promotes to form a large number of α-N b
5Si3 and almost no β-Nb5Si3
is formed in Nb-20Si-6Mo-3Hf alloy. Grammenos et al. [26] found that
adding 5% Hf in Nb-20Si-5Mo-3W alloy promotes the transformation of
Nb5Si3 from β to α, which also shows that the e ﬀect of Hf element on
the phase components of Nb-Mo-Si based alloy is higher than that of Mo
element. Ti-rich Nb
5Si3 phase is found with Ti addition in Nb-20Si-6Mo-
20Ti alloy. And the phases are composed of β-Nb5Si3 phase, Ti-rich
Nb5Si3 phase and Nb ss phase.
Fig. 2 shows the SEM micrographs of Nb-20Si-6Mo and Nb-20Si-
6Mo-3Al alloys. According to the results of XRD in Fig. 1 and EDS
analysis in Table 1, it shows that the primary phase of above two alloys
is β-Nb5Si3 phase, and the microstructures is combined by β-Nb5Si3 and
Nbss/β-Nb5Si3 eutectic. From Fig. 2(a–c), it can be seen that the shapes
of primary β-Nb5Si3 phase are various in Nb-20Si-6Mo alloy, such as L-
like shapes, square shapes and dendrite shapes ( Fig. 2(a)). There are
two kinds of eutectic structures in Nb-20Si-6Mo alloy: the eutectic I
which grows attached to the primary β-Nb
5Si3 phase and the eutectic II
which nucleates independently ( Fig. 2(b–c)). The eutectic I morphology
is anomalous. The eutectic II morphology tends to lamellar in some
degree, compared with that of eutectic I, as shown in Fig. 2 (c).
In Nb-20Si-6Mo-3Al alloy, primary β-Nb5Si3 phases mainly are
polygons. The dendrite shape is less seen and its morphologies are
inclined to form L-like shapes and square shapes, which are di ﬀerent
with Nb-20Si-6Mo alloy, as shown in Fig. 2(d). The eutectic structure
also has two types in Nb-20Si-6Mo-3Al alloy. The morphology of the
eutectic I is anomalous and the morphology of the eutectic II tends to
lamellar in Nb-20Si-6Mo-3Al alloy, as shown in Fig. 2(e –f). From the
above observation it shows that adding Al element will change β-Nb
5Si3
morphology, and promote the eutectic II morphology to lamellar in Nb-
20Si-6Mo alloy [24].
Fig. 3 shows the SEM micrographs of Nb-20Si-6Mo-3Cr, Nb-20Si-
6Mo-3Hf and Nb-20Si-6Mo-20Ti alloys. The microstructure morpholo-
gies in these alloys are signi ﬁcantly di ﬀerent, although there are
primary Nb5Si3 and Nbss/Nb5Si3 eutectic microstructure in these alloys.

Hf and O ratio is close to 1:2 through the EDS analysis of the white

alloy. More ﬁne Nb
ss/Nb5Si3 eutectics (A zone in Fig. 3(b)) are formed.
The magni ﬁcation is shown in Fig.(b'). The morphology of primary
Nb5Si3 phase is dendrite shapes.
From Fig. 3(c), it can be found that the morphologies of eutectic

Nbss/Nb5Si3 eutectic microstructure (B zone shown in Fig. 3(c)) is also

4. Conclusions
Based on Nb-Mo-Si based ultrahigh temperature alloys, the inﬂ u-
ences of Al, Cr, Hf, and Ti on the microstructures and mechanical
property of Nb-Mo-Si alloys were studied:
1. The Addition of Al, Cr and Ti elements do not change the phase
components in Nb-20Si-6Mo alloys. The alloys are composed of Nb
ss
phase and β-Nb5Si3 phase. The phase components are α-Nb5Si3
phase and Nb ss in Nb-20Si-6Mo-3Hf alloy. The e ﬀect of Hf addition
is stronger than that of Mo addition on the stability of α-Nb5Si3
phase.
2. The additions of Ti and Cr element promote the eutectic morphology
anomalous and coarsening in Nb-20Si-6Mo alloys. The element
segregation is obvious with the additions of Hf and Ti in Nb-20Si-
6Mo alloy. The enrichments of Hf and Ti change the compositions of
retained melt and promote the formation of ﬁne eutectic structure.
3. After heating treatment at 1873K for 10 h, β-Nb
5Si3 phases are
translated into α and γ-Nb5Si3 phase. Two kinds of Nb ss phase were
found after heat treatment. The eutectic structures are anomalous
and coarsening. And the regular surfaces of block Nb
5Si3 phases
tend to get rough. Ti solid solution phase was found in Nb-20Si-
6Mo-20Ti alloy. The Ti
ss phases are formed by the synergistic e ﬀect
of the phase transition of Nb 5Si3 and Mo element enrichment in Nb ss
