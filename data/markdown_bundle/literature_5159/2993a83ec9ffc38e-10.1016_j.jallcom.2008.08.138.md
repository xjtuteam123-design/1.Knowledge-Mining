abstract
A new method was employed to synthesize nano-sized ODS alloys using a light ball-milling and metallic
salts as precursor materials. The ball-milling of nitrate solution or the use of tiny nitrate particles as
precursor materials prior to its decomposition resulted in ﬁne and uniform distribution of Y
2O3 dispersoids
in the metallic matrix. The powders prepared by these methods can be easily processed using a simple
die-compaction/sintering technique, resulting in enhanced high temperature strength and ductility.

1. Introduction
The oxide dispersion-strengthened (ODS) alloys are one of
important materials for high temperatures uses. Compared to
precipitation-strengthened alloys, the ODS alloys have much higher
strength and creep resistance at high temperatures. This is due to
the ﬁne and uniformly dispersed oxide particles which are quite
stable beyond the melting point of the surrounding metallic matrix.
So far, mechanical alloying (MA) has been commonly used to fabri-
cate ODS alloys. However, the reﬁnement of hard microcrystalline
oxides to nanometer scale in a relatively ductile metallic matrix
is not easily achieved, requiring a long processing time. Owing
to this, expensive nano-sized oxides have been commonly used
as starting powders instead of micro-sized oxides, as in the orig-
inal MA technique by Benjamin [1]. Another inconvenience of MA
process is that the conventional powder metallurgical process of
die-compaction/sintering is difﬁcult for MA powders due to heavy
work-hardening induced during the high-energy ball-milling.
A variety of techniques has been recently reported to synthesize
nano-oxide particles via chemical methods using metallic salts such
as precipitation route [2], the sol–gel [3], hydrothermal routes [4],
pyrolysis/carbothermal treatment [5], reverse micelle method [6],
etc. These methods are relatively inexpensive and easy to control
the process.

To overcome the above-mentioned problem of MA powders, we
present a new method to synthesize nano-sized ODS alloys by a
combined method of light ball-milling and chemical methods using
metallic salts as starting materials. The examined alloy system was
nano-yttria (Y
2O3) dispersed-ferritic stainless steel, because this
alloy has recently received much attention due to its potential
application in interconnect materials of solid oxide fuel cells [7]
and cladding materials for the Generation IV nuclear reactors [8].
2. Experimental procedure

formed to Y 2O3 during processing. The preparation method of the additives is

4. Conclusions
In the present work, we have attempted a new method to syn-
thesize nano-sized ODS (Y
2O3 dispersed-ferritic stainless steel)
alloys: a light ball-milling of metallic salts (Y-nitrate) as a pre-
cursor materials. Various Y-nitrate precursors which decompose
to Y
2O3 particle during the process, were prepared by different
methods such as wet and dry milling, reverse micelle method,
and a modiﬁed pyrolysis. Wet milling of Y-nitrate resulted in a
more homogeneous distribution of dispersoids than dry milling.
Further improvement was made by a small addition of PEO to Y-
nitrate. A combined method of pyrolysis and light milling of nitrate
also resulted in a ﬁne and uniform distribution of dispersoids,
although milling was carried out in dry state. Among the exam-
ined method, both the wet-milled Y-nitrate + PEO (specimen E) and
the pyrolysis-processed specimen (G) exhibited the most optimum
mechanical property at 500
◦C: a tensile strength comparable to
conventional ODS alloys and a much higher ductility. The present
method is still preliminary, but show some advantageous features
over the conventional ODS alloys prepared by MA (high-energy
ball-milling) process. First, the powders prepared by the present
method can be easily and cost-effectively processed using a simple
die-compaction/sintering technique due to the employment of a
light milling, while conventional MA ODS powders requires expen-
sive hot-consolidation techniques such as hot-extrusion and hot
isostatic pressing to consolidate heavily work-hardened powders.
Second, the grain size of the present powders can be much larger
than the MA ODS powders due to a lower milling intensity, show-
ing the possibility of an easier plastic forming and a better creep
property.
