abstract
Motivated by the vital role played by boride precipitates in Ni-based superalloys in improving mechanical
properties such as creep rupture strength, fatigue crack growth rates and improved resistance towards
environmental embrittlement [1], we estimate fracture strength of Ni/boride interfaces through deter-
mination of their work of separation using ﬁrst-principles simulations. We ﬁnd that the fracture strength
of Ni/boride interfaces is higher than that of other commonly occurring interfaces in Ni-alloys, such
as Ni /Sigma1-5 grain boundaries and coherent Ni/Ni
3Al interfaces, and is less susceptible to oxygen-induced
embrittlement. Our calculations show how the presence of Mo in Ni/M5B3 (M = Cr, Mo) interfaces leads to
additional reduction in oxygen-induced embrittlement. Through Electron-Localization-Function based
analyses, we identify the electronic origins of effects of alloying elements on fracture strengths of these
interfaces and observe that chemical interactions stemming from electronegativity differences between
different atomic species are responsible for the trends in calculated strengths. Our ﬁndings should be
useful towards designing Ni-based alloys with higher interfacial strengths and reduced oxygen-induced
embrittlement.

1. Introduction
NiAl-based intermetallic compounds and superalloys constitute
a majority of structural materials used in high-temperature appli-
cations, for example, as blades and disks in turbine engines. The
basic microstructure of these alloys consists of a /CR-matrix, which
is a disordered FCC solid solution of Ni and other common alloying
additions, in which /CR
′,a nL 12 ordered Ni3Al-based intermetallic, is
dispersed as coherent precipitates. In addition to these, it is well
established [2] that carbides, oxides and borides form secondary
phase precipitates in Ni-based superalloys.
Susceptibility of Ni-based superalloys to environmental embrit-
tlement is well documented[3,4]. Ni-based superalloys experience
accelerated crack growth rates under the combined effects of oxy-
gen and temperature [1,5–8]. Addition of boron is found to be
beneﬁcial in reducing environmental embrittlement in various Ni-
based superalloys [1,9,10]. Rosler and co-worker[1] demonstrated
signiﬁcant life extension in INCONEL 706 by chemical modiﬁca-
tion of a surface zone with B, establishing that B was present
as boride precipitates in the matrix zone and played a key role
in this life extension. Xiao et al. [11] reported that addition of B

It was suggested that the beneﬁcial role of B was to counteract

Over the last decade, there has been a signiﬁcant amount of
theoretical work developing relationships between speciﬁc Ni-
based superalloy interfaces and their bulk mechanical properties.
For example, the /CR//CR

/CR/boride interfaces have not been reported as yet. In this work, ab-
initio calculations have been used to study the fracture strengths of
/CR/boride interfaces, and determine the effects of oxygen-induced
embrittlement in these interfaces.

3. Computational details
We use ﬁrst-principles Density Functional Theory [22,23] to
study the two structural models of Ni/M 5B3 (M = Cr, Mo) inter-
faces. Model I containing 108 atoms is studied using SIESTA code
[24], while Model II containing 52 atoms is studied using Vienna
Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP) [25]. VASP calculations are
carried out with the Perdew–Wang [26] form of Generalized Gra-
dient Approximation (GGA) functional with no dependency of
electronic spin. Projector-augmented wave potentials[27] are used
to describe the computationally expensive electron–ion interac-
tions, which permitted the use of a plane-wave basis set with
energy cutoff of 400 eV in the representation of the Kohn–Sham
wave functions. The Brillouin zone integrations are sampled with
a3 × 3 × 2 Monkhorst–Pack grid[28]. Ground-state atomic geome-
tries for all structures are obtained by minimizing the energy until
the magnitude of Hellman–Feynman forces [29] are less than a
tolerance of 0.01 eV/Å per atom. While using SIESTA, the electron
exchange-correlation is described by the GGA–PBE functional[30],
in conjunction with Troullier–Martins norm-conserving scalar rel-
ativistic pseudopotentials [31]. A double-/DLEplus polarization (DZP)
basis set is employed. The energy cutoff for the real space grid
used to represent the density is 100 Ry. The localization radii of
the basis functions are determined from an energy-shift of 0.01 eV.
Monkhorst–Pack meshes of 5× 5 × 1 sampling in the Brillouin zone
are used. In both codes, to ensure the precision of energies and
geometries, k-point and plane-wave cutoff energy convergence
tests are performed, showing convergence of total energies within
1–2 meV per atom.

I Ni/Cr

The effect of boride chemistry on interfacial strength is stud-

at the immediate vicinity of the Ni/Cr
5B3 interface are replaced by
Mo atoms (Fig. 2(b)). In the second case, Mo atoms are distributed
homogeneously in the boride supercell (Fig. 2(c)). Structural relax-

with an energy difference of 1.42 eV/cell. Following the same proce-
dure described above, O is introduced in three different sites along
the Ni/Cr
2.5Mo2.5B3 interface. O is still found to preferentially reside

4.2. Comparison of /CR/boride interfacial fracture strengths with
other interfaces
To validate the trends found in model I (simulated with SIESTA)
and to compare these results with our earlier work on /Sigma1-5 Ni
grain boundaries [17] and coherent Ni/Ni3Al interfaces [18], select
VASP calculations are carried out on structural model II of Ni/M5B3
(M = Cr, Mo) interfaces (Fig. 3). Again, O is placed at a single, ener-
getically favorable site, along the Ni/M 5B3 (M = Cr, Mo) interface.
In addition, Ni/Cr 2.5Mo2.5B3 interfaces were constructed with all
Mo atoms segregated at the interface, which was found energet-
ically favorable for model I in earlier SIESTA-based calculations.
Calculated cleavage energies are reported inTable 2. A comparison
with SIESTA-based calculations in Table 1 reveal good agreement
in that: (a) Ni/Cr
2.5Mo2.5B3 interfaces are found to be marginally
weaker than Ni/Cr5B3 interfaces, and (b) the extent of O-induced
embrittlement is reduced in Ni/Cr2.5Mo2.5B3 interfaces, relative to
Ni/Cr5B3 interfaces.
In multi-element Ni-based superalloys, microstructures con-
sist of multiple phases and complex interfaces including/CR//CRgrain
boundaries, /CR//CR′ interfaces and /CR/boride interfaces, which are all
likely to coexist in different proportions, depending on condi-
tions of alloying, processing and heat-treatment. Hence, to gain
a more complete understanding of the mechanical responses of
these alloys in terms of resistance to crack propagation through
interfaces/grain boundaries, it is insightful to compare the fracture
strengths of the various interfaces/grain boundaries. It is interesting
to note that the cleavage energies of Ni/M
5B3 (M = Cr, Mo) inter-
faces (5.15 and 5.08 J/m2) are found to be signiﬁcantly higher than
those reported for coherent Ni/Ni3Al interfaces (4.22 J/m2, Ref.[18])
and Ni-/Sigma15 grain boundaries (3.6 J/m 2, Ref. [17]), using the same
method. These calculations suggest that the fracture strengths of
the Ni/boride interfaces are intrinsically higher than that of Ni-/Sigma15
grain boundaries and coherent Ni/Ni
3Al interfaces, a ﬁnding that
is experimentally consistent with observations that addition of B
leads to improvement in creep rupture properties as well as reduc-
tion in fatigue-crack-growth-rates in Ni-based superalloys [2,12].
The cleavage energy of coherent Ni/Ni3Al interfaces, in the presence
of O, has been reported at 3.46 J/m2 [18], which is lower than that
of /CR/boride interfaces in the presence of oxygen (4.27–4.31 J/m 2,
Table 2). This result is supported by earlier experimental work on
IN706, where O-enhanced-crack-growth rate is reduced by increas-
ing the surface concentration of B, which forms boride precipitates
with matrix [1].

to be at the Ni/Ni 3Al interface over Ni/Cr 2.5Mo2.5B3 interface by

of O-induced embrittlement is much severe in Ni/Ni

than in Ni/M 5B3 (M = Cr, Mo) interfaces points to an extremely
interesting direction. The co-existence of these interfaces in a Ni-
based superalloy, added to the energetics of O-segregation and
embrittlement in these interfaces suggest that the only way to
retard the propensity of oxygen-enhanced crack growth along
interfaces in Ni-based superalloys would be to increase the propor-
tion of Ni/boride interfaces, i.e. to have more boride precipitates via
controlled alloying and processing methods.
4.3. Effect of composition of boride on /CR/boride interfacial
fracture strengths

origin of effects of boride chemistry on the fracture strength of
Ni/M

based on the Electron Localization Functions (ELF) (seeFig. 4) [32].
The relative interplay of electronegativities (EN) between the dif-
ferent atomic species (Ni, Cr, Mo, B, O) is found to hold a key to
their interfacial strengths. For pristine Ni/Cr

(EN of Cr = 1.66, EN of Mo = 2.16, EN of Ni = 1.91, EN of B = 2.04, in

in Pauling Scale) in the Ni/Cr

trons from Cr leading to charge localization around O atoms. It had
earlier been established [17,18] that charge localization around a
dopant at the interface results in increased ionicity at the interface,
thus weakening the interface. In contrast, in the Ni/Cr
2.5Mo2.5B3
interface, presence of electronegative Mo suppresses the strong
electronegative character of O to some extent, resulting in less
charge localization around O, which is reﬂected in the increased
strength of the Ni/Cr
2.5Mo2.5B3 interface, relative to its Ni/Cr 5B3
counterpart, in the presence of O.
The extent of charge localization around O atoms in these two
interfaces is evident in the ELF plots (seeFig. 4). It is also interesting
to note that although the starting positions of O atom are kept iden-
tical in Ni/Cr
5B3 and Ni/Cr2.5Mo2.5B3 interfaces, upon relaxation
O atoms approach Cr atoms, the most electropositive atom in the
Ni/Cr
5B3 interface. However, for the Ni/Cr 2.5Mo2.5B3 case, where
the interface is comprised of Ni, Mo and B atoms with Cr atoms posi-
tioned farther from the interface, O is found to move further from
Mo, positioning itself at an intermediate position between Ni and
B atoms. This shift in priority demonstrated by O atom is directly
related to the relative electronegativities of its nearest neighbor
atoms. This synergistic interplay between different alloying ele-
ments present in the interfaces is found to ultimately inﬂuence the
strength of these interfaces.
5. Conclusions
In summary, ﬁrst-principles calculations are used to determine
the fracture strengths of Ni/M
5B3 (M = Cr, Mo) interfaces, and exam-
ine the effects of oxygen-induced embrittlement on the strengths of
these interfaces. Two structural models of varied lattice mismatch
are studied using two different DFT codes, viz. SIESTA and VASP.
We show that (a) fracture strengths of Ni/boride interfaces are
higher than the Ni-/Sigma15 grain boundaries[17] and coherent Ni/Ni
3Al
interfaces [18] in Ni-based alloys, (b) Ni/boride interfaces are more
resistant towards O-embrittlement than coherent Ni/Ni 3Al [18]
and (c) (Cr,Mo)borides are more effective in reducing O-induced
embrittlement in Ni/M
5B3 interfaces, than their Cr-boride coun-
terparts. This result is found to be consistently true over the two
variants of structural models used to depict the Ni/boride inter-
faces. The cause and relative extents of embrittlement of Ni/M
5B3
(M = Cr, Mo) interfaces are found to correlate well with the lev-
els of ionic charge concentration around O, and can be understood
in terms of electronegativities of different atomic species at the
interface.
Future work in this area would involve exploring different ori-
entation relationships in/CR/boride interfaces and also incorporating
other variants such as/CR
′/boride interfaces, to develop a more com-
plete understanding of the effects of boride precipitates in the
microstructures of Ni-based superalloys. Keeping in mind the syn-
ergistic interplay between different alloying elements and their
effects on mechanical responses of interfaces, it would be interest-
ing to extend the current calculations beyond Ni to capture effect
of multi-element /CR-composition on interfacial strengths.
Our work provides a framework for fundamental understanding
of fracture strengths of relevant interfaces in Ni-based superalloys,
and should help in identifying alloying additions/processing routes
to increase the strengths and proportions of such interfaces. Such
approaches would work towards increasing the overall strength of
Ni-based superalloys and reducing their environmental sensitivity
during service.
