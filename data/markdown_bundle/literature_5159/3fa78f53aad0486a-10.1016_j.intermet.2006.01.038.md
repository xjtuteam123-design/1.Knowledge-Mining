Fe- and Co-based bulk glassy alloys with ultrahigh strength
of over 4000 MPa
A. Inoue a, B.L. Shen a,*, C.T. Chang b
a Institute for Materials Research, Tohoku University, Sendai 980-8577, Japan

Abstract
Since the ﬁrst synthesis of Fe-based bulk glassy alloys in Fe–(Al,Ga)–(P,C,B,Si) system in 1995, a number of Fe- and Co-based bulk glassy
alloys have been developed up to date because their alloys are expected to exhibit high mechanical strength and good soft magnetic properties.
The maximum diameter of Fe- and Co-based bulk glassy alloys exhibiting high fracture strength of over 4000 MPa is 5 mm for Fe–Co–B–Si–Nb
system and 3 mm for Co–Fe–Ta–B–Mo–Si system. The addition of a small amount of Nb or Ta is essential for the increase in their glass-forming
ability through the formation of network-like atomic conﬁgurations. The primary crystalline phase from supercooled liquid is a metastable
complex FCC Fe
23B6 or Co 23B6 phase and hence the change to the local atomic conﬁgurations leading to the precipitation of the metastable
Fe23B6 or Co23B6-type phase is thought to play an important role in the achievement of high glass-forming ability. The highest fracture strength
reached as high as 4250 MPa for Fe–Co–B–Si–Nb alloy and 5545 MPa for Co–Fe–Ta–B–Mo alloy. The fracture strength has a good linear
relation with Young’s modulus, glass transition temperature or liquidus temperature. It is, therefore, concluded that the origin for the ultrahigh
strength is attributed to the strong bonding nature among the constituent elements. Considering that Fe–Si–B amorphous alloy wires developed for
several years between 1979 and 1983 have been used as high strength materials for the last two decades, the newly developed high-strength Fe-
and Co-based bulk glassy alloys are expected to be used as a new type of ultrahigh strength material by utilizing the advantage points of much
higher strength and three-dimensional material form.

1. Introduction
It is important to develop a high-strength material in an
inexpensive alloy system because its development is expected
to cause the maintenance of a clean atmosphere on the earth
through the saving of fossil fuel as well as more efﬁcient
construction of national security. The most appropriate alloy
system among metallic materials is Fe-based alloys and the
highest tensile strength reported for conventional crystalline
Fe-based alloys is 3300 MPa for piano wire [1], when the
material size is limited to the bulk form with diameters above
1 mm. It is known that the high tensile strength is attributed to a
ﬁbrous duplex structure of a-Fe ferrite and Fe
3C cementite
carbide phases caused by heavy cold drawing and low-
temperature annealing treatments. For the last one decade,
we have tried to develop high strength materials with strength
of over 3300 MPa for Fe- and Co-based alloys in wire [2] or
bulk [3,4] form through the modiﬁcation to disordered atomic
conﬁgurations by using conventional inexpensive production
techniques in air, gas shield atmosphere or water. As a result,
the upper strength limit of glassy alloys was signiﬁcantly
increased to over 4000 MPa for Fe- [5–7] and Co-based [8–10]
bulk glassy alloys. It is thus expected that the Fe- and Co-based
bulk glassy alloys can be used as a new type of ultrahigh
strength material. We intend to review our recent results on the
formation, thermal stability and mechanical properties of Fe-
and Co-based bulk glassy alloys with diameters of 2–5 mm
exhibiting high strength of over 4000 MPa, with the aim at
applying their alloys to high strength practical materials.
Before the data for their bulk glassy alloys are presented, it is
meaning to introduce the feature of Fe-based amorphous alloy
wires with diameters up to 0.2 mm which have been used as
high strength materials for the last two decades [2], because the
understanding of the key points which had resulted in the
successful commercialization is expected to enable subsequent
practical uses of Fe- and Co-based bulk glassy alloys.

2. Fe-based amorphous alloy wires
The studies on the development of amorphous alloy wires in
Fe- [11,12], Co- [13] and Ni- [14] based alloy systems were
made for several years between 1979 and 1982. We used an in-
rotating-water spinning technique for the production of
amorphous alloy wires with a circular cross section [15]. The
technique was noticed to be appropriate for the production of
amorphous alloy wires with diameters ranging from 80 to
120 mm. Accordingly, it was essential to ﬁnd an appropriate
alloy composition at which an amorphous phase is obtained
even in the diameter range of 80–120 mm. Fig. 1 shows the
compositional dependence of the critical ribbon thickness for
the formation of an amorphous phase in Fe–Si–B ternary alloys
[16]. The signiﬁcant compositional dependence is recognized
and the largest critical ribbon thickness is obtained at the
limited composition of Fe
75Si10B15. Such compositional
dependence data of amorphous-forming ability were obtained
from the unique melt-spun alloy ribbon in which the thickness
was changed continuously in the range from 15 to 400 mm. The
unique melt-spun alloy ribbon was produced by a specially
designed melt spinning equipment [17]. In the equipment, the
rapidly rotating copper wheel with a circumferential speed of
50 m/s can be stopped within a very short time of 1–3 s. Such a
rapid stop has enabled us to produce the unique ribbon sample
with continuously changed thickness ranging from 15 to
400 mm. When the surface structure is observed with an optical
microscope, the precipitation of a crystalline phase can be
clearly detected, leading to the exact determination of the
critical ribbon thickness for the formation of an amorphous
phase. In comparison with the critical ribbon thickness for the
alloy series of Fe-20 at.% metalloid systems, the largest critical
ribbon thickness was obtained for Fe–P–C system, followed by
Fe–B–Si system [11,12]. However, we could not obtain the
large critical ribbon thickness for Fe–B–C system.
By choosing an optimum alloy composition with the
critical ribbon thickness of over 100 mm, we tried to
produce an amorphous alloy wire with a circular cross
section by the in-rotating-water spinning technique. In this
technique, the alloy melt is ejected into the water layer
formed inside a rotating drum and an alloy wire can be
formed in an appropriate melt spinning condition. The
scattering in the wire diameter has been reported to be less
than 1.3% in the case of optimum melt spinning condition.
The maximum diameter of the amorphous alloy wires
obtained by this technique was 270 mm for Fe
75Si10B15 [16],
in agreement with the compositional dependence of the
critical ribbon thickness for the formation of an amorphous
phase.
The resulting Fe
75Si10B15 amorphous alloy wires exhibit
good bending ductility which is deﬁned by a 180 8 bending
without fracture as shown in Fig. 2 [16] . A number of shear
deformation bands can be observed at the tip of the bent region.

3. Fe-based bulk glassy alloys
It is known that the ﬁrst synthesis of an Fe-based bulk glassy
alloy was made in Fe–(Al,Ga)–(P,C,B) system in 1995 [23].
Since then, a variety of Fe-based bulk glassy alloys have been
formed and their alloy components can be classiﬁed into the
following ﬁve groups as summarized in Table 1 .T h e
developments of their Fe-based bulk glassy alloys were made
in the order of Groups I, II, V, III and IV, as shown in the table.
Here, it is important to point out that Fe-based bulk glassy
alloys with high strength of over 3300 MPa in conjunction with
plastic strain can be obtained only in two alloy groups of I [24]
and IV [25], while the other Fe-based bulk glassy alloys are too
brittle to exhibit high mechanical strength of over 2000 MPa.

5. Dominant factors for high mechanical strength
Fig. 14 summarizes the relationship between fracture
strength ( sf) and Young’s modulus ( E) for the Fe- and Co-
based bulk glassy alloys, together with the data of other non-
ferrous bulk glassy alloys and conventional crystalline alloys.
It is noted that the data of the present Fe- and Co-based bulk
glassy alloys lie on the same linear line extrapolated from those
of the non-ferrous bulk glassy alloys. The agreement indicates
that the deformation and fracture modes of bulk glassy alloys
are independent of strength level and alloy component. In
addition, the high strength bulk glassy alloys are also ductile
enough to lead to an elastic elongation of 2%, as similar to that
for non-ferrous bulk glassy alloys with much lower fracture
strength.

6. Possible application ﬁelds
Considering that the Fe- and Co-based bulk glassy alloys
have the features of bulk material form, high fracture strength,
high crystallization temperature, large supercooled liquid
region and inexpensive material cost, it is expected that the
newly developed Fe- and Co-based bulk glassy alloys are used
in the following application ﬁelds, in addition to magnetic
materials which have been omitted in the present review paper:
(1) precision mold material, (2) precision imprint material, (3)
precision machinery material, (4) precision sensor material, (5)
surface coating material, (6) cutting tool material, (7) shot
penning material, (8) fuel cell separator material and so forth.
Considering that Fe–Si–B amorphous alloy wires have been
used for many years (more than 20 years) [2], the signiﬁcant
increase in material shape as well as the improvement of
mechanical strength is expected to enable practical uses of the
Fe- and Co-based bulk glassy alloys in various ﬁelds including
the above-described materials.
