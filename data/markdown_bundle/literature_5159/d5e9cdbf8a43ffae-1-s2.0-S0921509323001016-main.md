1. Introduction 
As one of the most emerging classes of new materials, high-entropy 
alloys (HEAs) have plenty of excellent properties, such as good ther -
mal stability, high hardness, distinctive electrical, impressive magnetic 
properties, and corrosion resistance [ 1 – 4 ]. HEAs are considered prom -
ising candidates for advanced high-temperature structural alloys, mag -
netic materials and so on [ 5 – 10 ]. However, the application of HEAs is 
constrained by insufficient high-temperature strength [ 11 ] and limited 
toughness [ 12 ]. Grain refinement [ 13 ], precipitation hardening [ 14 ], 
and transformation-induced plasticity [ 15 ] have been employed to 
improve the mechanical performance of HEAs for balanced mechanical 
performance. 
Among the HEAs investigated, Al – Fe – Co – Cr – Ni alloy is one of the 
most well-developed HEAs and receives much attention for its 
outstanding strength and ductility [ 4 , 6 , 16 ]. According to previous re -
ports [ 17 – 19 ], the Al content has a significant effect on the micro -
structure and mechanical properties of Al – Fe – Co – Cr – Ni HEAs. The 
alloy ’ s microstructure evolves from a simple face-centered cubic (FCC) 
structure to a body-centered cubic (BCC) + FCC structure and finally to a 
single BCC structure with increased Al content. Therefore, the Al ele -
ment ’ s content needs to be adjusted to achieve a balance of ductility and 
strength. The Al
0 ⋅ 3
CoCrFeNi HEA demonstrates excellent plasticity of 
50 – 80% elongation, but its yield strength at room temperature (RT) is 
only 300 – 600 MPa [ 20 – 22 ]. One possible strategy to improve its 
strength is to modify the microstructure and phase constitution by 
alloying new minor elements [ 23 , 24 ]. Gu et al. [ 25 ] revealed that the 
addition of Si could promote the formation of (Al, Ni, Si)-rich phase with 
B2 structure (BCC

Co, Ti)-rich B2 ordered precipitates, respectively [ 23 ].

5. Conclusions 
In this work, Al
0.3
CoCrFeNiTi
x 
HEAs with different Ti content are 
designed and studied on their microstructure and mechanical proper -
ties. The main conclusions are as follows. 
1. The precipitations in these alloys are L1
2 
and B2 particles. With the 
increased Ti contents, L1
2 
particles are coarsened. The uniform dis -
tribution of L1
2 
and B2 particles leads to a significant increase in 
strength with a bit of expense of plasticity. 
2. At RT, Ti0.075-A700 exhibits the highest product of strength and 
elongation of 48.8 GPa % (UTS = 1116 MPa, elongation = 43.7%). In 
comparison, Ti0.15-A700 displays the outstanding mechanical 
property at 700 
◦
C (product of strength and elongation = 25 GPa %). 
3. Quantitative calculation results revealed that the particle ( γ
′
) 
shearing mechanism is the predominant strengthening mechanism. 
The strengthening contributions from γ
′
and B2 phases to the yield 
strength of Ti0.15-A700 are 348.4 MPa and 147.8 MPa, respectively. 
4. At lower aging temperatures, the L1
2 
phase precipitates first, and 
leads to different B2 phase precipitation behaviors. When the aging 
temperature is 800 
◦
C, the L1
2 
phase is coarsened, and it is absent at 
900 
◦
C. Therefore, the 700 
◦
C aged alloys demonstrate outstanding 
mechanical properties.
