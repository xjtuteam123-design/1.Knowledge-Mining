THE STRUCTURE AND PROPERTIES OF 1.0-2.5 mm THICK 
SHEETS OF AK4-2ch HIGH-TEMPERATURE STRENGTH ALLOY 
L. P. Lantsova, V. V. Teleshov, 
M. N. Dmitrieva, Yu. I. Bykov, 
O. M. Sirotkina 
UDC 620.17:620.166:669.14.018.44 
AK4-2ch high-temperature strength alloy is a modification of AK4-1ch alloy and differs from it in nickel and 
iron content and also in additional alloying with zirconium. Plates, shapes, and forgings of AK4-2ch alloy 
have a high fracture resistance. In the literature there is insufficient information on the properties of AK4-2ch 
alloy sheets. It is of interest to determine the characteristics of fracture toughness and high-temperature 
strength for thin sheets, which are widely used in various structures. 
The possibility of use of aluminum alloys in the heated elements of aircraft structures depends upon such characteris- 
tics of their high-temperature strength as short- and long-term strength at service temperatures, stability of mechanical 
properties after long heating, and creep rate [ 1, 2]. The characteristics of design strength of semi-finished products including 
the fracture toughness coefficients K~c and K c, the rate of fatigue crack growth, and the low-cycle fatigue resistance, which 
determine the effectiveness of structures with cracks under conditions of static and cyclic loading, are of great value [3, 4]. 
AK4-1ch alloy of the A1-Cu-Mg-Fe-Ni system possesses a satisfactory combination of the above properties, 
which makes it the most commonly used high-temperature strength aluminum alloy. However, the large quantity of excess 
phases in the alloy leads to a low fracture toughness KIc and a relatively high fatigue crack growth rate after artificial aging 
[3, 4]. In a number of works, such as [5-7], it is shown that a decrease in iron and nickel content in AK4-1ch alloy to a low 
level promotes an increase in fracture toughness but has little influence on the characteristics of high-temperature strength. 
Further investigations of alloys of this system conducted by O. A. Romanova and V. N. Bobovnikovyi under the 
leadership of I. N. Fridlyander showed the possibility of improving the combination of properties of the alloys as the result 
of reducing the iron and nickel contents to 0.4-0.7% each and additional alloying with 0.15% Zr.

The analyses of the heats made it possible to evaluate the influence of the contents of copper and magnesium in the 
solid solution and also of the contents of iron and nickel forming in equal quantity insoluble A19FeNi phase. 
The melting was done in a 300 kg electrical furnace. The 160 x 540 mm flat ingots were cast in a sliding mold with 
filtration of the molten metal through a glass screen. To produce clad sheets the 140 mm thick milled homogenized slabs 
were rolled with 5 mm thick cladding layers of ADO alloy at 400°C in the transverse direction in four passes to a thickness 
of h = 80 mm and then in the longitudinal direction in eight passes to h = 6 mm. After process annealing at 420°C the 800 
mm wide sheets were rolled to room temperature to h = 2.5, 1.5, and 1.0 mm. 
In cold rolling of sheets to h = 1.5 and 1.0 mm process annealing was used. The degree of cold working e after the 
last anneal in rolling using different variations is shown in Table 2. The cold-rolled sheets were hardened from 530 + 5°C 
for 15 min and straightened by tension in the freshly hardened condition with a residual deformation of about 1.5%. 
Macrostructural analysis (Fig. 1) showed that in the whole cross section the structure of the cast ingots is fine-grained 
with fine pores in the peripheral zone. In the microstructure of the homogenized ingot there is a network of eutectic precipi- 
tates of insoluble A19FeNi phase bordering the dendritic cells with an average cross section of about 35/zm (Fig. 2). In this 
case the inclusions of excess phases are distributed individually and do not form significant colonies, which is characteristic 
of AK4-1ch alloy. The reduction in iron and nickel contents in the alloy is accompanied by a decrease in the content of 
excess phases in the structure. 
The rolled sheets in the heat treated condition have a fine-grained recrystallization structure (Fig. 3a, b). The average 
grain size in the investigated sheets, the average chord with orientation of the intercept in three directions, is shown in Table 
2. The structure with the minimum grain size (No. 2-3) is observed in sheets with h = 1 mm and in the sheet with h -- 1.5 
mm rolled before hardening with a maximum degree of cold working (variation 2-3).

which may be seen qualitatively in analysis of the microstructure of the sheets (Fig. 3c, d). The volume shares of the

Certain results of investigation of the kinetics of aging of sheets at 190°C are given in Table 3. The maximum 
strength properties are obtained after aging for 14-16 h and in this case the specific electrical conductivity 3' ~ 23 MS/re. 
However, the required mechanical properties are provided with rag = 12 h and 3' = 22 MS/m. As may be seen from Table 
3, sheets of heat 1 (rolling variations 1-1, 1-2, and 1-3) with a maximum copper content have higher strength properties. For 
the sheets of the remaining heats the strength properties are close. The iron and nickel contents in the alloy and also the 
degree of deformation in cold rolling did not have a significant influence on the properties. According to the data of [8] 
sheets of AK4-1chT1 alloy have a t = 400 N/mm 2, %.2 = 340 N/mm 2, and 8 = 7.0% in the transverse direction, which is 
close to the properties shown in Table 3. The investigation of the kinetics of aging of sheets of AK4-1ch alloy in [9] showed 
that the maximum in strength properties corresponds to 3' = 23.3 MS/m with an average specification content of Cu and Mg 
in the alloy. A comparison of these data with those given in Table 3 makes it possible to assume that the kinetics of aging of 
type AK4-1ch alloys does not depend upon the iron and nickel contents. 
Heats 2 and 4, which do not have significant deviations in copper and magnesium content from the average level, 
were used to determine the mechanical properties at different temperatures and also the toughness and high-temperature 
strength of sheets of AK4-2chAT1 alloy. Specimens of these heats were aged at 190°C for 12 h. An increase in test tempera- 
ture from -70 to + 175°C leads to a decrease in strength and an increase in plasticity (Table 4), which agrees with the data 
obtained for AK4-1chT1 alloy [8]. 
The mechanical properties of AK4-2chT1 alloy sheets in the transverse direction at room temperature after various 
holds at 125, 150, and 175°C are shown in Table 5. In all of the investigated cases loss of strength of the alloy is observed 
only after a hold at 175°C for 500 h.

rupture strength (stress at which failure of the specimen occurs during the specified hold time) were determined on longitudi- 
nal specimens after holds at 150 and 175°C for 100 and 500 h (Table 6). It was established that the level of high-temperature

The characteristics of crack resistance (fracture toughness Kc f, failure stress O-net cr, and fatigue crack growth rate) 
were determined on 200 mm wide longitudinal and transverse specimens with and without reinforcement in the area of crack 
growth (Table 7). The specimens tested without reinforcing have lower Kc f and O-net cr than those with reinforcing and the 
properties of AK4-2chT1 are lower in the transverse direction than in the longitudinal. Alloy 4 has higher crack resistance 
properties, which may be related to the lower content of excess phases [4]. 
The low-cycle fatigue resistance in repeated tension (R = 0) was determined from the number of cycles until failure 
Nf of longitudinal and transverse specimens cut in the form of plates with a hole (stress concentration factor 2.6) with a 
loading frequency of 5 Hz and a maximum cycle stress of 157 N/mm 2. The test results are shown in Table 8. 
General and exfoliation corrosion resistance, corrosion cracking, and intergranular corrosion tests showed that aging 
at 190°C for 12 h provides satisfactory anticorrosion properties of AK4-2chT1 alloy sheets (at the level of AK4-1chT1). It is 
not prone toward corrosion cracking with a stress of a = 0.9%. 2, has an insignificant (2-3 rating) tendency toward exfolia- 
tion corrosion, and does not require protection from intergranular corrosion. 
To produce parts of complex form, data on the critical degree of deformation (ecr) of sheets are necessary. The ecr 
value of heat 2 was determined with deformation of different degrees of 2.5 and 1.5 mm thick cold rolled sheets after harden- 
ing from 400°C from the results of structure investigation. Grain growth is observed in the sheets with a residual deformation 
of about 3.5-4.0%, which is more than ecr - 2% for AK4-1chT1 alloy. This may be explained by the presence in AK4-2ch 
alloy of dispersed inclusions of AI3Zr phase retarding grain growth. 
Conclusion. In comparison with AK4-1chAT1 alloy sheets AK4-2chAT1 alloy rolled sheets have close values of 
mechanical strength at room and increased temperatures but higher fracture toughness and lower fatigue crack growth rate. 
This makes it possible to recommend AK4-2chAT1 alloy in place of AK4-1chAT1 alloy for production of sheet.
