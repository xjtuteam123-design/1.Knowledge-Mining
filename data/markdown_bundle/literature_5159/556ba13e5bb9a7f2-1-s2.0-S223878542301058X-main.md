abstract
In this work, the effect of directional solidiﬁcation methods on solid solution windows in
two Ni-based single-crystal superalloys is investigated. The sample prepared using the
liquid metal cooling (LMC) method was found to contain smaller dendrite spacing, lower
dendrite segregation and eutectic, and a smaller size of g
0 phase than that of the high-
speed rapid solidiﬁcation (HRS) method. The alloy-2 contained higher Cr, Co and lowered
Re exhibited similar dendrite spacing and volume fraction of eutectic, and the lower
dendrite segregation and smaller size of g
0 phase than that of the other alloy-1 . The so-
lution treatment window is broadened by adopting the LMC method or changing the
composition to alloy-2. This is mainly realized by decreasing the lower limit temperature,
and the upper limit temperature remains unchanged. According to the eutectic only con-
tains the melt temperature, a similar eutectic is responsible for the unchanged upper limit
temperature. At this state, the lower bound of the temperature is the dissolution of the gʹ
phase in the dendrite core. The smaller size of g
0 phase and the elements ' partition ratio are
responsible for the change in the solid solution window.

1. Introduction
Nickel-based single-crystal superalloy is widely used in aero-
space because of its excellent mechanical properties [ 1,2].
Directional solidiﬁcation is traditionally used for preparing
nickel-based single-crystal superalloys [ 3e10]. In such a
complex alloy system, the solidiﬁcation method is often
uneven. The dendritic structure will appear in the cast alloy
obtained by this method, and the eutectic structure will be
produced in addition to g-gʹ phases [9e11]. To eliminate these
problems, solution heat treatment is commonly used to ach-
ieve homogenization [ 12e14]. Traditionally the treatment
proceeds during the solid solution window, which uses the
lower bound by the minimum temperature required for the

temperature for the incipient melting of the alloy [ 15e17]. In
addition, the alloy 's incipient melting temperature increases
with the eutectic dissociation. Thus, the solid solution win-

With the development of Ni-base single-crystal superalloy,
the content of refractory elements (Re, W, Mo, Ta, etc.) is
increasing to improve its mechanical properties at high tem-
peratures [20,21]. But this also brings the problem of the nar-
rower solid solution window, resulting in a decrease in the
impact temperature resistance of the alloy due to the difﬁculty
of heat treatment [22e25]. Therefore, how to broaden the solid
solution window has become an important research topic. In
preparing Ni-based single-crystal superalloy by directional
solidiﬁcation [ 21,26], the dendrite structure will be directly
affected by two parameters, i.e., the temperature gradient and
drawing rate. The high-speed rapid solidiﬁcation (HRS)
method traditionally contains a temperature gradient of
26e30K/cm and a drawing rate of 10 e27 cm/h [ 27]. The cast
microstructure and window would be changed by adjusting
these two parameters.
The researchers further develop the liquid metal cooling
method (LMC) by replacing the cooling medium with a liquid
metal to accelerate heat transfer using the characteristics of
high thermal conductivity and a low melting point of liquid
metal. The LMC method can increase the temperature
gradient by about three times and the solidiﬁcation rate by
about six times [ 27]. In this regard, the LMC method 's micro-
structure prepared differs from that of the HRS method in four
aspects. Firstly, the primary dendrite spacing of the alloy
prepared by HRS is larger than that of LMC [ 28e34]. Secondly,
the dendrite segregation decreases compared to the micro-
structure from HRS to LMC methods [ 35e38]. Thirdly, the
evolution of eutectic is not ﬁxed, it was found the eutectic
decreases and increases compared to the microstructure from
HRS to LMC methods in different alloys [ 39e41]. The last, the
size of the gʹ phase decreases compared to the microstructure
from HRS to LMC methods [ 36,42e45].
Although many studies compare the difference in the cast
microstructure between the alloys prepared using the HRS
and LMC methods, there are few reports on their effect on the
solid solution window. In this paper, the evolution of dendritic
structure, intergranular structure, and gʹ phase of Ni-based
single-crystal superalloy prepared by different methods are
studied by electron microscopy, and the relationship between
them and solid solution window is constructed.
2. Experiment procedure
This study used two third-generation single-crystal superal-
loys with high refractory element content as reference alloys.
Its nominal compositions are listed in Table 1 . The [001] ori-
ented single-crystal bars were solidiﬁed by using the HRS and
LMC directional solidiﬁcation methods. Similar to reference
[27], the LMC method adopts the temperature gradient by
about three times and the solidiﬁcation rate by about six times
than that of the HRS method. The size of the bars was F16

methods, respectively. The alloys were cut into cylindrical
specimens of F16 mm /C2 5 mm and F4m m /C2 5 mm by electric
spark cutting technology. The heat treatment was conducted
for 15 min in the temperature range of 1300 e1360
/C14 C. The air
cooling was adopted, which was also the most wildly used
cooling condition during the heat treatment. Scanning elec-
tron microscopic (SEM) examination was carried out using an
FEI-Quanta 650F ﬁeld emission scanning electron microscope

microscopic (TEM) analysis was carried out using an FEI-Talos
F2000X electron microscope equipped with an energy disper-

and composition. The phase size was measured using Image-

The above results show that the sample prepared using the
LMC method and alloy-2 contain a widening solid solution
window. The reasons behind these differences are discussed.

The varied diffusion rate of different elements causes

distance, the lower distance in the sample prepared using the
LMC method will produce lighter segregation [ 35,37]. In addi-

contribute to a short diffusion time. This can also lead to
lighter segregation in the sample prepared using the LMC
method. The lower dendrite segregation can further decrease
the volume of the eutectic [ 37]. The higher cooling rate also
contributes to the smaller size of gʹ phases and elements '
partition ratio for their lower growth time [ 40].
4.2. Effect of composition on the microstructure
The alloy-2 contained a similar dendrite spacing and volume
fraction of eutectic, and the lower dendrite segregation and
smaller size of g
0 phase than that of the alloy-1 . According to
formula (1), the dendrite size of the alloy depends on G, V k, D,
G and DT0. Among these parameters, G and V are determined
by the preparation methods and k, D, G, DT0 are determined by
the composition. The two alloys using the same solidiﬁcation
method contain the same G and V, thus the dendrite size of
the as-cast structure will not change due to the change of
these two parameters. The dendrite size decreased with the
addition of Cr, Co and Re, and the decrease of Re was the
highest [47e49]. The similar dendrite spacing of the two alloys
should relate to the offsetting effect of increasing Co and Cr
and decreasing Re. In addition, the similar dendrite structure
can also contribute to the same volume fraction of eutectic. As
a heavy element, the addition of Re will decrease the diffusion
coefﬁcient of elements in the alloy, thus inhibiting the diffu-
sion of dendritic elements to inter-dendrite after solidiﬁca-
tion, resulting in the aggravation of dendrite segregation
[50,51]. Co and Cr have high diffusion coefﬁcients to promote
element diffusion [46], thus reducing the dendritic segregation
[52,53]. In this regard, alloy-2 contains lower dendrite segre-
gation than alloy-1. The partition ratios of Cr and Re elements
in alloy-1 are lower than those of alloy-2 using the same
directional method, as shown in Fig. 6. According to the Re can
inhibit the coarsening of gʹ phase as its low diffusion rate and
segregated in the g/gʹ interface [ 54]. This makes the coars-
ening of gʹ phase inhibited in alloy-2.
4.3. Effect of microstructure on the solution heat
treatment window
The solid solution window's upper limit temperature in alloy-1
and alloy-2 are unchanged, with the preparation method
changing from HRS to LMC. Still, the lower limit temperature
decreases, so the solid solution window is broadened. Theo-

Thus, the lower bound does not seem to exist. According to the

5. Conclusions
This study revealed the effect of directional solidiﬁcation
methods on solid solution windows in two Ni-based single-
crystal superalloys. The experimental evidence and the dis-
cussions presented above enable the following conclusions.
1) The solution treatment window is broadened by adjusting
the directional method and composition. This is mainly
realized by decreasing the lower limit temperature, and the
upper limit temperature remains unchanged.
2) The sample prepared using the LMC method contained
smaller dendrite spacing, lower dendrite segregation and
eutectic, and a smaller size of g
0 phase than that of the HRS
method. The similar eutectic structure and the smaller size
of g
0 phase and elements' partition ratio are responsible for
the change in the solid solution window.
3) The alloy-2 contained higher Cr, Co and lowered Re
exhibited similar dendrite spacing and volume fraction of
eutectic, and the lower dendrite segregation and smaller
size of g
0 phase than that of the alloy-1. The similar eutectic
structure and the smaller size of g0 phase are responsible
for the change of the solid solution window.
