ABSTRACT
In this study, five different compositions of Multi-Principal Element (MPE) alloys with Al, Nb, and Zr, were 
designed to achieve a two-phase structure of α -HCP and β -BCC. Phase stabilities and yield strengths at various 
temperatures were investigated through heat treatment and high-temperature compression testing. Due to MPE 
alloying, the β -transus temperature varied significantly, and the alloys formed an α phase on a β -matrix phase 
after homogenization and aging treatments. This α phase formation contributed to the increased yield strength, 
combined with the strong solid-solution strengthening effect achieved through MPE alloying. Among the alloys,

1. Introduction
Titanium (Ti) alloys, known for their high specific strength and low 
density, are widely used in aerospace applications like turbine engines 
[ 1 – 3 ]. However, their strength degrades significantly at high tempera -
tures, limiting their use to below 600 
◦
C and driving research to improve 
high-temperature performance.
Ti alloys typically form hexagonal close-packed (HCP, α ) and body- 
centered cubic (BCC, β ) phases, depending on stabilizer content. These 
alloys are classified as α , β , or α + β types. Among them, metastable β 
alloys are promising for achieving high strengths [ 4 – 6 ]. These alloys 
stabilize in the β phase at high temperatures and transform to α + β 
below the β -transus temperature. Even though the level of improved 
mechanical properties differed for many factors: distribution, volume 
fraction, grain size and morphology, sizes and distributions of precipi -
tated α phase [ 7 – 12 ]. The strengths can be improved through the for -
mation of α phase on the β -matrix. Despite advancements in metastable β 
alloys, most studies focus on room-temperature properties, as main -
taining thermally stable α phase at elevated temperatures remains a key 
challenge. β stabilizers such as Nb, Mo, V, and Ta lower the β -transus 
temperature [ 13 ], complicating the retention of α phase and hindering 
strengthening by α phase under high-temperature conditions.
On the other hand, efforts to develop high-temperature materials 
have also explored high-entropy alloys (HEAs) [ 14 – 18 ]. More recently, 
Multi-Principal Element Alloys (MPEAs) have emerged, offering flexible 
composition and phase design while maintaining strengthening effects 
[ 19 , 20 ]. Unlike conventional alloys, which typically have a major 
element exceeding 70 %, MPEAs can have compositions where no 
element exceeds this threshold.
This MPE-alloying method has also been applied to Ti alloys. Li et al. 
reported that β stability increased in Ti – 6Al – 7Nb-xZr (x = 0, 5, 10, 15, 
20 wt%) alloys as the Zr content increased [ 21 ]. Zhang et al. demon -

In our past studies, the near- α Ti – 10Al – 2Nb – 2Zr (at. %) alloy (Ti- 
6Al-4Nb-4Zr, wt %), which exhibits α + β microstructures, showed 
variations in microstructure, such as equiaxed α phase, bimodal struc -

conditions, along with enhanced creep properties [ 23 – 25 ]. However, 
the compressive yield strength decreased with increasing temperature, 
falling below 600 MPa at 873 K [ 26 ]. This issue could potentially be 
addressed by adopting the MPE-alloying method.
Therefore, in this study, we designed MPE-TiAlNbZr alloys to obtain 
much higher solid solution strengthening by a higher concentration of 
alloying elements in a two-phase structure consisting of α -HCP and 
β -BCC phases, aiming to enhance their high-temperature performance. 
The alloy compositions are controlled between those that stabilize the β 
phase, as investigated in Refs. [ 21 , 22 ], and those that stabilize the α 
phase in our previous study [ 23 – 25 ]. The objective of this study is to 
clarify how MPE changes phase equilibria and how the resulting 
microstructure affects mechanical properties.

As mentioned above, all the alloys in this study exhibited a BCC 
matrix without martensitic transformation from β to α despite having 
higher Al content than conventional Ti alloys. Considering that the 
conditions for MPE-alloys (e.g., mixing entropy and δ -parameter) fall 
within the range for a solid solution phase, these alloys were expected to 
form a single-phase solid solution. While Alloy E initially showed a 
bright-phase formation after homogenization, its transformation 
behavior ultimately aligned with that of the other alloys, following 
typical processes observed in Ti alloys. This suggests that these alloys 
exhibit behaviors characteristic of both conventional Ti alloys and MEA 
systems.
3.2. Mechanical properties
Compression tests were subsequently conducted to evaluate their 
mechanical properties. All of the true stress-strain curves are exhibited 
in Fig. S2 in the supplementary file. In the homogenized samples, brittle 
deformation, characterized by a drastic drop in stress after yielding, was 
observed at 678 K in Alloy A and at 298 K in Alloy E; however, under 
other conditions, relatively ductile deformation behavior was indicated. 
The upper and lower yielding were observed at 773 K and 873 K in Alloy 
B and 873 K in Alloy C. As shown in Fig. 6 , significant solution of the α 
phase occurs during the compression test in Alloys B and C. Then, it is 
considered that dislocations started to overcome the α phase due to the 
shrinking of the α phase during dissolution and the strength decreased. 
In the aged conditions, samples of Alloy A were brittle at all testing 
temperatures. This is due to formation of the α phase. Other alloys 
exhibited brittle behavior up to 678 K, but they changed to ductile 
behavior at high temperatures.
Fig. 5 shows the compressive yield strengths of the homogenized and 
aged alloys at various temperatures. For comparison, the yield strengths 
of conventional Ti alloys are also presented as shaded regions in Fig. 5
(b). Those yield strengths values were obtained from technical data -
sheets provided by TIMET (Titanium Metals Corporation) [ 35 – 38 ].
For the homogenized conditions, as shown in Fig. 5 (a), the room- 
temperature yield strengths were approximately 900 MPa for all al -
loys. As the temperature increased, the yield strengths decreased. 
However, for homogenized Alloy D at 673 K, the yield strength exhibited 
an unusual drop for some reason, followed by a recovery. To understand 
this behavior, further investigation is necessary. Notably, homogenized 
Alloys D and E maintained strengths above 500 MPa even at 873 K. It 
was assumed that the α phase formed during the homogenization 
treatment would affect the yield strengths.
In Fig. 5 (b), it is evident that aging treatment enhanced the yield 
strengths through the formation of the α -HCP phase. Although strength 
reduction at elevated temperatures was similar to the homogenized 
samples, the aged alloys showed higher yield strengths up to 773 K. At 
873 K, Alloys A, B, and C had yield strengths comparable to their ho -
mogenized counterparts. In contrast, aged Alloys D and E exhibited 
significantly higher yield strengths, exceeding 680 MPa at 973 K. These 
alloys also demonstrated better resistance to high-temperature softening 
compared to conventional Ti alloys.
Fig. 6 shows the microstructures of Alloys A, D and E after 
compression tests at 873 K and 973 K. The α phase was clearly observed 
in Alloy A by aging treatment at 873 K, as shown in Figs. 2 and 3 , but the 
α phase partially dissolved after the compression test, as shown in Fig. 6 
(a) . Since the α phase formed at 973 K, the α phase should be stable at 
873 K. However, the observation indicates that the α phase becomes 
unstable at 873 K under stress. This behavior was more significant in 
Alloys B and C. In Alloy B, the β transus is between 750 and 873 K as 
shown in Fig. S1(a) . In Alloy C, the β transus is between 850 and 973 K as 
shown in Fig. S1(b) . The decrease in the α phase stable temperature 
range due to multi-element addition causes a drastic decrease in strength 
at high temperatures. Conversely, as shown in Fig. 6 (b) – (c), the α phase 
remained stable in the microstructures of aged Alloys D and E after 
compression testing at 973 K. This is supported by the microstructure 
homogenized at 1273 K, where formation of the α phase was observed, 
as shown in Fig. 3 (d) and (e). The α phase stabilization temperature 
increased with a decrease in Zr concentration in Alloys D and E. Another 
aspect contributing to the high strength at high temperatures in Alloys D 
and E is the formation of Ti
3
Al, which occurs due to the high Al content 
exceeding 10 at%. However, Ti
3
Al was not detected in the XRD patterns 
( Fig. 4 ).
In conclusion, the increase in yield strength, particularly at elevated 
temperatures, was strongly influenced by the stability of the α -HCP 
phase. Especially, Aged Alloys D and E demonstrated superior high- 
temperature strength compared to conventional Ti alloys, by success -
fully achieving the formation of β -stabled matrix and increased thermal 
stability of α -HCP phase.

3.3. Strengthening effects
To understand the effect of MPE-alloying on the yield strength of

4. Conclusions
In this study, to improve the high temperature strength of TiAlZrNb- 
MPEA, five different alloy compositions were newly designed to increase 
solid solution strengthening in HCP + BCC two phase MPEA. Their 
mechanical properties and strengthening effects were investigated. The 
results are as follows; 
(1) The MPE-alloying method successfully stabilized the β -BCC 
phase, with increased Al and Ti contents contributing to a higher 
β -transus temperature.
(2) After homogenization heat treatments, all alloys formed a β -BCC 
single phase matrix, and aging treatment formed α -HCP pre -
cipitates distributed.
(3) Alloys D and E demonstrated improved high-temperature yield 
strengths of 740 MPa and 970 MPa at 873 K, and 690 MPa and 
860 MPa at 973 K, respectively. This improvement was primarily 
attributed to α -HCP precipitation.
(4) Strengthening mechanisms were dominated by solid-solution and 
precipitation strengthening, while dislocation foresting and grain 
boundary strengthening played minor roles.
(5) The improved solid-solution strengthening effect on TiAlZrNb 
was mainly obtained from the content of Al and Zr.
