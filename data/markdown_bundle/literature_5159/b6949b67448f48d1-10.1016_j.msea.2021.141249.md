1. Introduction 
High-entropy alloy (HEA) is a novel class of material consisting of 
several principal elements with equiatomic or near-equiatomic ratios. 
HEA often shows simple crystal structures such as FCC and BCC, which 
results in promising mechanical properties [ 1 – 5 ]. The most extensively 
studied equiatomic CoCrFeMnNi HEA, referred to as the Cantor alloy, 
shows high FCC phase stability in a wide range of temperatures and 
promising mechanical properties with an excellent combination of 
strength, ductility, and toughness at both room temperature and cryo -
genic temperatures [ 6 – 9 ]. However, yield strength (YS) of the alloy at 
room temperature is rather low [ 9 , 10 ], and the previous research 
focused on improving YS of the CoCrFeMnNi HEA by grain refinement 
[ 11 ] and precipitation hardening by alloying additional elements 
[ 12 – 14 ].

solid solution. Prolonged annealing of the alloy at temperatures lower

3.4. Mechanical properties of cold-rolled and annealed HEAs 
Tensile behavior of the cold-rolled and annealed HEAs is provided in 
Fig. 6 . Table 5 shows the engineering YS, ultimate tensile strength 
(UTS), and total elongation (TE) of the investigated materials. HEA-600 
and HEA-700 show ultrahigh strength exceeding 1500 MPa of UTS, but 
fracture immediately after yielding. The poor ductility is mostly due to 
the large fraction of the unrecystallized area ( Fig. 1 ). HEA-800 shows a 
good combination of strength and ductility, i.e. 927 ( ± 36) MPa of YS, 
1063 ( ± 75) MPa of UTS, and 14 ( ± 1.5) % of TE. The ductility of HEA- 
800 is larger than that of HEA-600 and HEA-700 due to a higher frac -
tion of recrystallized grains of the former. HEA-900 and HEA-1000 show 
a relatively low strength but large ductility, i.e. TE = 46 ( ± 1.5) % for 
HEA-900 and 52 ( ± 3) % for HEA-1000, due to the fully recrystallized 
microstructures. The strain hardening rate of HEA-800 is lower 
compared to that of HEA-900 and HEA-1000, possibly due to the pres -
ence of unrecrystallized grains and σ phase ( Fig. 6 (c)). Fractography 
analysis in Fig. 7 shows that both HEA-800 and HEA-900 mainly exhibit 
dimple structures, indicating the characteristics of ductile fracture. 
However, HEA-800 also shows cleavage facets as indicated by the 
dashed red lines ( Fig. 7(a1) ). The fracture surface of the HEA-800 shows 
both the σ phase particles and TiN inclusions ( Fig. 7(a1) and (a2) ) while 
the fracture surface of HEA-900 only shows TiN inclusions ( Fig. 7(b1) ). 
HEA-800 shows combined characteristics of ductile and brittle fracture, 
thereby leading to lower ductility compared to HEA-900.

approximately 3 wt. % Al. This amount of Al present in the FCC matrix is 
expected to contribute to an increase in SFE compared to the CoCr -
FeMnNi HEA. Further, higher SFE of the investigated material compared 
to the CoCrFeMnNi HEA could lead to suppression of deformation 
twinning and characteristics of wavy slip. 
The TEM analysis also identified dislocation interactions associated 
with the B2 phase and the dislocation pattern in the B2 phase. Fig. 8 (d) 
shows dislocations in the matrix, the B2 phase, and near the B2/FCC

image indicates that most dislocations are parallel to the trace of the 
( 0 11 ) plane of the B2 phase, which is consistent with the reported slip 
plane of the B2 phase [ 28 ]. 
4. Discussion 
4.1. Formation of B2 and σ phases 
The present work reports two types of second phases, i.e. the B2 
phase enriched in Ni, Al, and Ti and the σ phase enriched in Cr and Fe. In 
the B2 – NiAl phase, Ti was reported to preferentially substitute for the Al 
lattice site [ 29 ]. Sidnov et al. [ 30 ] investigated the influence of Ti 
addition on elastic properties of the B2 – NiAl phase and reported that the 
addition of Ti in NiAl decreased Young ’ s modulus and would increase 
the ductility of the B2 phase. This suggests that the observed B2 phase 
enriched in Ni, Al, and Ti in the present work by Ti alloying could reduce 
the brittleness of the B2 phase. On the other hand, the σ phase can be 
formed in CrMn

FeCoNi HEAs when Mn exceeds a certain concentration

4.3. Strength-ductility balance of the partially recrystallized material 
Partial recrystallization has been extensively used to develop mate -
rials having superior strength-ductility balance. HEA-800 in the present 
work shows a good combination of strength and ductility. The ultrahigh 
strength is due to the combined effect of partially recrystallized micro -
structure and precipitation hardening. The fraction of recrystallized 
grain in HEA-800 is 0.694, determined from GOS analysis ( Fig. 1 (d)). On 
the other hand, HEA-800 has a larger fraction of second phase particles, 
leading to relatively low ductility. The increase in a volume fraction of 
second phase particles, incoherent with FCC matrix, could lead to the 
decrease of ductility of the material. Li et al. [ 44 ] investigated the in -
fluence of B2 and σ phase particles on the mechanical properties of the 
Al-containing CoCrFeMnNi HEA and reported that the large volume 
fraction of the small size of the second phase particles resulted in low 
ductility. The B2 – NiAl phase is known to have a brittle character. To 
overcome the brittleness of the B2 structure, the present work used Ti as 
an alloying element. With increasing Ti concentration in the B2 – NiAl 
structure, the character of metallic bonding which is ductile than ionic 
and covalent bonding could increase [ 30 ]. Therefore, the amount and 
structure of second phase particles should be properly controlled to 
develop precipitation hardened HEAs with a good combination of 
strength and ductility. Future work should focus on recrystallization 
annealing followed by aging to develop HEA with optimum precipita -
tion hardening and mechanical properties.
