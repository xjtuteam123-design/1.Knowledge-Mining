Materials Transactions, JIM, vol. 41, No. 11 (2000) pp. 1478 to 1481 Special Issue on Bulk Amorphous, Nano-Crystalline and Nano-Quasicrystalline Alloys 02000 The Japan Institute Of Metals High Strength and Good Soft Magnetic Properties of Bulk Glassy Fe��Mo��Ga��P��C��B Alloys with High Glass-Forming Ability Baolong Shen, Hisato Koshiba*, Hisamichi Kimura and Akihisa Inoue Institute for Materials Research, Tohoku University, Sendai 980-8577, Japan The effect of the addition of the transitional metal Mo, on the glass-forming ability, mechanical strength and magnetic properties of Fe78��xMoxGa2P12C4B4 glassy alloys was investigated. The addition of 4 to 6 at%Mo was found to be effective for the extension of the supercooled liquid region (ATx) defined by the difference between the glass transition temperature (Tg) and crystallization temperature (Tx). The ATx value is 30K for the Fe78Ga2P12C4B4 alloy and increases to 60K for the 4at%Mo alloy. These glassy ribbon alloys exhibit good soft magnetic properties of 1.13 to 1.34 T for saturation magnetization and 2.2 to 3.8 Alm for coercive force. Based on the large ATx values, we tried to prepare bulk glassy Fe74M04Ga2P12C4B4 rods in a cylindrical form with different diameters. The glassy single phase was obtained in the diameter range of 1.0 to 2.0 mm. These bulk glassy alloys also exhibit high saturation magnetizations of about 1.20 T as well as a high compressive strength of 2685 MPa. Introduction The synthesis of Fe-based glassy alloys with high glass- forming ability, a large supercooled liquid region before crys- tallization, high mechanical strength and good magnetic prop- erties has strongly been desired for the last three decades. However, the critical sample thickness for formation of an amorphous phase in a number of transition metal base alloys had been limited to less than about 50um up to the end of 1980s. The small maximum thickness resulting from the Iow glass-forming ability of Fe-based amorphous alloys has pre- vented the extension of application fields as magnetic materi- als. Consequently, great efforts have been devoted to search a new ferromagnetic glassy alloy with high glass-forming abil- ity for the last decade. For the last several years, multicompo- nent glassy alloys in the Fe��(Al, Ga)��(P, C, B, Ge, Si) and Fe�� (Nb, Mo, Zr, W)��B alloy systems which exhibit a large super- cooled liquid region reaching 58 to 88 K before crystallization and can be produced in a cylindrical form with diameters of 2 to 6 mm by copper mold casting have been found by Inoue group.1-4) The success has been attributed to the three em- pirical rules for the achievement of high glass-forming abil- ity.5-8) That is, (1) multicomponent alloy systems consisting of more than three elements, (2) significantly different atomic size ratios above 12% among the constituent elements, and (3) large negative heats of mixing among the elements. It has been also reported by Inoue et al. that the addition of Nb, Cr or Mo to the Fe��(Al, Ga)��(P, C, B) system is effec- tive for the further extension of the supercooled liquid region in the maintenance of good soft magnetic properties.9) More recently, bulk glassy Ni��(Nb, Cr, Mo)��(P, C, B)10) alloys with a large supercooled liquid region and high strength have been synthesized. Experimental Procedure Multicomponent Fe78_xMoxGa2P12C4B4 alloys were ex- amined in the present study. The ingots were prepared by induction melting a mixture of pure Fe, Mo and Ga metals, prealloyed Fe��C and Fe��P ingots, and pure B crystal in a pu- rified argon atmosphere. Their compositions are nominally expressed in atomic percentage. From the master alloy in- got, a ribbon with a cross section of about 0.020 x 1.0mm2 was prepared by melt spinning. Cylinders with diameters up to 2.0 mm and a length of 35 mm were also made by copper mold casting in an argon atmosphere. The amorphicity of the ribbons and cylinders was examined by X-ray diffractometry with Cu-Ka and optical microscopy (OM). The specific heat associated with glass transition, supercooled liquid region and crystallization was measured at a heating rate of 0.67 KIS with a differential scanning calorimeter (DSC). Magnetic proper- ties of saturation magnetization and coercive force were mea- sured with a vibrating sample magnetometer (VSM) and a B-H loop tracer. CompreSsive strength was tested at a strain rate of 2.2 x 10��4 s-l with an Instron testing machine at room temperature.

Discussion We discuss briefly the reason for the high glass-forming ability for the FeuM04Ga2P12C4B4 alloy. Based on the data of the changes in Tg and Tx with Mo content, the increase in A Tx is concluded to be attributed to the retardation of crystallization. The X-ray diffraction data of the melt-spun Fen-xMoxGa2P12C4B4 (x 0, 2, 4) glassy alloys annealed for 600s at 850K indicate that the crystalline structure is composed only of a-Fe, Fe3P, Fe3B and Fe3C phases. It is well known13' 14) that the Mo element has large negative heats of mixing against the other constituent element. There is a high tendency for Mo to form various kinds of compounds such as FexMoy, MoxGay, MoxBy and MoxPy However, we can't detect any kind of compounds containing Mo as a main constituent element, indicating that Mo element is dissolved into the crystallized phases. The dissolution of Mo into the crystallized phases implies the necessity of the redistribution of Mo from the supercooled liquid to the crystallized phases for the progress of crystallization. The redistribution is not always easy for Mo element with high melting temperature and large negative heats of mixing. The redistribution of Mo and the other solute elements such as Ga, P, C and B may be concluded to lead to the retardation of precipitation of the crystallized phases. 5. Summary A new glassy FeuM04Ga2P12C4B4 alloy with high glassy- forming ability, soft magnetic properties and high strength was synthesized. The large supercooled liquid region of 60 K before crystallization has enabled us to form bulk glassy al- loy rods in the diameter range up to 2.0mm by the copper mold casting method. The Tg and ATx of the bulk glassy al- loys are nearly the same as those for the corresponding melt- spun ribbons. These cast glassy alloy rods also exhibit high saturation magnetization of about 1.20 T. In addition, these bulk glassy alloys also exhibit high compressive strength of 2685 MPa. The combination of high glass-forming ability, good soft magnetic properties and high compressive strength for the glassy Fe74M04Ga2P12C4B4 alloy is encouraging for the future application of Fe-based bulk glassy alloys to bulk soft magnetic materials.
