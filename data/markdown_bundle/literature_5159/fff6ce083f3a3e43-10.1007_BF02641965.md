High-Temperature Strength and Ductility 
Increases of Ti-Mo-A! Alloys by Step Aging 
M. HIDA AND S. WEISSMANN 
Step-aging programs, based on principles of particle-dislocation interactions, were de- 
veloped systematically to obtain increases in the high-temperature strength and ductility 
properties of Ti-7 at. pct Mo-Al alloys. A triple-step aging program applied to Ti-7 Mo- 
16Al produced a yield stress ~o.2 = 1,500 MN/m 2, elongation to fracture EF = 4 pct at room 
temperature, and no.2 = 900 MN/m 2, CF = 12 pct at 600~ A two-step aging program re- 
sulted in no. 2 = 1,350 MN/m 2, E F = 5 pct at room temperature; no.2 = 800 MN/m 2, E F = 20 
pct at 600~ 
THE existence of a high-temperature, two-phase 
field, fi + TisAl, in Ti-7 at. pct Mo-A1 alloys and its 
phase relation at lower temperature to a three-phase 
field,/~ + a + TizA1, I-3 suggest that the mechanical 
and heat-resistant properties of these alloys could 
be optimized by designing an appropriate micro- 
structure through heat treatment. The importance 
of homogeneous dispersion and particle size of the 
Ti3A1 precipitates in controlling the slip mode and 
thereby conferring ductility to the alloys has already 
been demonstrated. 2 These features of the micro- 
structure will represent, therefore, necessary in- 
gredients in the design of the alloys. 
Studying Ti-35 wt pct Nb alloys, Mendiratta, 
Lfitjering and Weissmann 4 have shown that remark- 
able ductility and strength increases could be obtained 
by application of a step-aging progrmn which was so 
designed that a microstructure was formed, consist- 
ing of precipitated particles of different phases, which 
upon deformation of the alloys activated different 
mechanisms of dislocation-particle interaction. Thus 
ductility increases were achieved by inducing the dis- 
locations to bypass the large a particles, precipitated 
at elevated temperatures, while increases in yield 
strength were obtained by the cutting of smaller par- 
ticles precipitated at lower temperatures. 
In view of the many phase fields that exist in the 
Ti-Mo-A1 phase diagram, 1,3 and particularly in view 
of the fact that the ~ phase decomposes at lower tem- 
peratures into Ti3A1 + ~ particles, multiple-step- 
aging programs appeared quite feasible. These may 
be so designed that not only strength and ductility 
increases can be conferred upon the alloys but also 
improvements in high-temperature stability. The 
work described in this paper aims to accomplish this 
task.

RESULTS

the synergistic effect of various heat treatments, a 
systematic study of the effect of each individual heat 
treatment on mechanical properties and thermal sta- 
bility was carried out. The results were then com- 
bined to build up the multiple-step-aging sequence. 
The presentation of the results will generally follow 
this scheme.

DISCUSSION 
The underlying rationale for achieving the remark- 
able mechanical properties by the multiple-step-aging 
treatment lies in the creation of a microstructure that 
acquired the attributes necessary for high-tempera- 
ture strength and ductility. Thus: 
I) The first, short-time aging step at 960~ gave 
rise to the precipitation of Ti3A1 particles, suitably 
distributed in the ]3 matrix. This structural feature 
formed the backbone of the desired microstructure, 
since it induced, upon deformation, a dislocation by- 
pass mechanism and hence was responsible for the 
high ductility values. 
2) The second, short-time aging step at 800~ re- 
sulted in the precipitation of somewhat smaller 
+ Ti3A1 particles, which strengthened the grain and 
subgrain boundaries of the alloys. It is a well estab- 
lished principle that for the development of creep- 
resistant alloys the grain boundaries and subgrain 
boundaries should be strengthend by precipitation of 
fine particles. For example, in nickel-base super- 
alloys the strengthening of boundaries may be achieved 
by precipitation of Ni3Nb or Ni3Ta (Ref. 6) or by pre- 
cipitation of ThO 2 (Ref. 7). In the Ti-Mo-A1 alloys the 
intimate mixture of c~ + TiaA1 appears to be particu- 
larly suitable as the strengthening ingredient of the 
boundaries. 
3) The third step at 600~ involving long-time ag- 
ing for 24 h, precipitated fine a + Ti3A1 particles dis- 
tributed throughout the matrix, which by virtue of 
forming effective obstacles to the moving dislocations 
raised the yield stress of the alloy. It is well known 
that when the path of dislocations is controlled by 
large particles so as to induce a bypass mechanism, 
very small particles which the dislocations encounter 
in their path will be cut. This is particularly true 
when the volume fraction of the small particles is 
large and, therefore, the mean free separation of the 
particles is short. Such microstructure is responsi- 
ble for a large increase in the yield strength (Fig. 6).

It is because of the ability to induce homogeneous slip 
and concomitant ductility that the larger Ti3A1 and 
Ti3A1 + a particles, precipitated at elevated temper- 
atures, constituted the backbone in the design of the 
microstructure. When these larger particles were 
not initiated first into the microstructure or when 
aging was started in the lower region of the three- 
phase field,/3 + Ti3Al + a, without sufficient aging 
at elevated temperatures for this backbone structure 
to form, poor mechanical properties and thermal sta- 
bility were obtained (curves l, 2, 6 of Fig. 2). Hence 
to obtain strength, ductility and high-temperature sta- 
bility, the sequence in the step-aging program has to 
be directed always downward in the temperature scale. 
SUMMARY 
Step-aging programs were developed with the aim 
of optimizing systematically the high-temperature 
strength and ductility properties of Ti-7 at pct Mo- 
A1 alloys. Correlating microstructure to mechanical 
properties, the following results were obtained: 
1) The yield stress ao. 2 increased and the elongation 
to fracture e F decreased with increasing aluminum 
content of the alloys when single-step aging in the 
high-temperature phase field/3 + Ti3A1 was employed. 
These changes in mechanical properties resulted from 
the increaase of volume fraction of precipitated Ti3A1 
particles. 
2) Multiple-step aging programs were developed 
based on the following principles of controlled dis- 
location-particle interactions: 
a) A low-volume fraction of realtively large Ti3A1 
particles was first precipitated from the/3 + TisA1 
phase field to induce the moving dislocations to by- 
pass the particles and thus to confer good ductility 
properties on the alloys. 
b) Grain-boundary and subgrain-boundary strength- 
ening were subsequently achieved by precipitation of 
ot + Ti3A1 particles at the boundaries. 
c) Increases in yield stress were obtained by pre- 
cipitation of small ~ + Ti3A1 particles at lower tem- 
peratures to induce particle-cutting by the moving 
dislocations. 
3) Based on these principles, a triple-step aging 
program applied to Ti-7 Mo-16 A1 produced a cro. 2 
value of 1,500 MN/m 2 at room temperature and 900 
I~l/m 2 at 600~ while the ~/~ rose steadily from 4 
to 12 pct, respectively.
