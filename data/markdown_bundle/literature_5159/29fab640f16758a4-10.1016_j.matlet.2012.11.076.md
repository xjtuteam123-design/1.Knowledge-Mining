abstract
The microstructure and mechanical properties of Mg–8.40Gd–5.30Y–1.65Zn–0.59Mn (wt%) alloy were
investigated in this study. The phase composition of the alloy involved an a-Mg matrix and long-period
stacking ordered phases of Mg 12Zn(Y,Gd) with some eutectic phases. After aging at 200 1C for 50 h
following extrusion (extrusion ratio ¼ 11:1, extrusion temperature ¼ 400 1C), the alloy achieved high
strength (ultimate tensile strength ¼/C24 500 MPa, tensile yield strength ¼/C24 322 MPa) and good ductility
(elongation ¼/C24 10.0%) and exhibited excellent comprehensive mechanical properties. These properties
could be attributed to the numerous plate-shaped b0 phase precipitates in the a-Mg matrix, as well as
the small dispersed block-shaped long-period stacking ordered phases along the grain boundaries.

1. Introduction
Magnesium alloys with rare-earth (RE) and zinc elements have
attracted considerable attention in recent years. Research interest
in the applications of these alloys in high-performance light
structural materials has increased, especially in the aerospace
and automobile industries. Such alloys exhibit excellent strength,
good high-temperature creep properties, and remarkable preci-
pitation strengthening effects by aging [1,2].
Mg–RE–Zn alloys can be reinforced with long-period stacking
ordered (LPSO) phase, yielding high strength, ductility, and
deformation ability. Mg–RE–Zn alloys reinforced with LPSO
phases can show tensile yield strengths (TYS) from 342 MPa to
377 MPa, ultimate tensile strengths (UTS) from 372 MPa to
410 MPa, and elongations from 3% to 9% [3]. Mg–RE–Zn alloys
with heavy RE elements, such as Gd, Er, and Dy, among others,
can exhibit age-hardening and age-strengthening. Mg–12Gd–3Y–
0.6Zr (wt%) alloy ﬁrst subjected to conventional and hydrostatic
extrusion in two subsequent steps and then aged at 225 1C for 9 h
can show a high UTS of 510 MPa, TYS of 453 MPa, and elongation
of 4.0% [4]. Homma et al. successfully fabricated Mg–1.8Gd–1.8Y–
0.7Zn–0.2Zr (wt%) alloy with extraordinarily high strength. Such a
material had a UTS of 542 MPa, TYS of 473 MPa, and elongation to
failure of 8.0% [5].
While some alloys can achieve high strength, they cannot
simultaneously exhibit high strength and good ductility. Fang
et al. [6] indicated that Zr could signiﬁcantly reﬁne the grains of
as-cast Mg–Gd but could not restrict the growth of recrystalliza-
tion grains during extrusion while Mn showed grain reﬁning in
as-extruded alloy for
a-Mn precipitates, suppressing the recrys-
tallization grain growth. Also, Mn could reduce the difﬁculties in
the metallurgical process while compared with Zr.
In order to get better ductility, we use Mn instead of Zr as
the grain reﬁnement element in this work and investigated
the mechanical properties and age-strengthening behaviors of
Mg–RE–Zn–Mn alloys, where RE ¼Gd or Y.
2. Material and methods
Commercial Mg (99.95% pure), Zn (99.95% pure), Mg–30Gd
(wt%), Mg–25Y, and Mg–3Mn master alloys were used as starting
materials. Experimental Mg–8.40Gd–5.30Y–1.65Zn–0.59Mn
(wt%) (Mg–1.48Gd–1.65Y–0.70Zn–0.28Mn, at%), hereafter named
GWZM852, was prepared in an electromagnetic induction furnace
under an Ar atmosphere. The ingots were homogenized at 540 1C
for 4 h and then extruded using a 2500 T LXJ horizontal extruder
through two different sets of extrusion parameters: the ﬁrst
involved an extrusion ratio of 25:1 and an extrusion temperature
of 450 1C, and the second involved an extrusion ratio of 11:1 and
an extrusion temperature of 400 1C. The corresponding as-
extruded alloys were named GWZM852-I and GWZM852-II. After
extrusion, aging treatment was performed at 200 1C using a
resistance furnace.
The microstructural morphologies of the alloys were deter-
mined using a VegaIILMU SEM equipped with an EDS. Phase
compositions were analyzed by X-ray diffraction at scanning
angles from 20 1 to 90 1 and a scanning speed of 2 1/min using a

3. Results and discussion
The SEM image in Fig. 1 a illustrates the morphology and
distribution of the second phases of the as-cast GWZM852 alloy.
The average grain size was calculated to range from 20 mmt o
50 mm. The main second phases are gray phases; some bright
phases could also be observed. The XRD pattern of the as-cast
GWZM852 alloy shown in Fig. 1 b indicates that, aside from the
a-Mg matrix, the main second phase is Mg12Zn(Y,Gd) with some
eutectic phase Mg5(Gd,Y,Zn) and Mg24(Y,Gd,Zn)5. EDS results of
the gray phases in the alloy demonstrate that the average Mg/
(Zn,Y,Gd) atomic ratio of the phases is 6.17, close to the Mg/
(Zn,Y,Gd) atomic ratio of 6.0 in Mg12Zn(Y,Gd) phases. Therefore,
combining the results from the SEM, EDS, and XRD pattern, the
phase composition of GWZM852 alloy could be inferred to
include an a-Mg matrix and large block-shaped Mg12Zn(Y,Gd)
phases distributed in a discontinuous network with some small
block- and rod-shaped eutectic phases scattered at the edge of the
Mg12Zn(Y, Gd) phases.
The microstructures of the as-extruded GWZM852 alloys
obtained under different extrusion parameters are shown in the
SEM micrographs in Fig. 2. GWZM852-II exhibited ﬁne and homo-
geneous recrystallized grains with average grain sizes ranging from
5 mmt o1 0 mm. Given that higher extrusion temperatures could
accelerate grain growth during dy namic recrystallization, some
abnormal coarse recrystallized grains were scattered among the
main ﬁne grains of GWZM852-I. After the extrusion, larger block
shapes distributed in a discontinuous network through the LPSO
phases were broken into smaller dispersed block shapes distributed
in the grain boundaries of the as-extruded GWZM852 alloys.
Tensile stress–strain curves of the as-extruded and as-aged
GWZM852 alloys at 200 1C and different aging times shown in
Fig. 3 indicate that the alloys can exh ibit dramatic age-strength-
ening. GWZM852-II, which was without abnormal coarse recrystal-
lized grains, exhibited higher strength. The coarse recrystallized
grains in GWZM852-I decreased the alloy strength. Overall,
GWZM852-II exhibited better mechanical properties (UTS /C0/C24
378 MPa; TYS¼/C24 290 MPa; elongation¼/C24 10.7%); this improvement
in properties is attributed to the presence of uniform and ﬁne
recrystallized grains [7,8] and dispersed LPSO phases in the
Mg–Gd–Y–Zn alloy.

The plate-shaped precipitates formed on the prismatic planes of
the matrix in a dense triangular arrangement are vertical to the
basal plane of a-Mg. These precipitates effectively reduce the
inter-particle spacing and increase dispersion strengthening [9].
The high strength of magnesium alloys containing precipitate
particles resistant to basal dislocation shearing is associated with
the microstructures containing a high density of intrinsically
strong and plate-shaped precipitates with prismatic habit planes.
Therefore, the remarkable age-strengthening could be attributa-
ble to the plate-shaped b0 phase precipitates formed on the
prismatic planes of the a-Mg matrix. In addition, the excellent
mechanical properties of the alloys are closely related to the
existence of LPSO phases. LPSO phases with high elastic moduli
and Vickers hardness tend to suppress dislocation movements
[3,10–13]. In addition, the stable coherent interface between the
LPSO phase and the a-Mg matrix does not favor the formation of
nucleation sites for voids and/or microcracks during deformation
[13], which are important contributors to strength.
4. Conclusions
The phase composition of the as-cast Mg–8.40Gd–5.30Y–
1.65Zn–0.59Mn (wt%) alloy mainly involved an a-Mg matrix and
large block-shaped LPSO phase Mg 12Zn(Y, Gd) distributed in a
discontinuous network with some eutectic phases. After extrusion,
the LPSO phase was broken into dispersed smaller block shapes
distributed in the recrystallized grain boundaries. After aging at
200 1C for 50 h following hot extrusion at an extrusion ratio of 11:1
and an extrusion temperature of 400 1C, the alloy achieved high
