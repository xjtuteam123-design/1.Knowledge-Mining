ABSTRACT 
In this work, a novel and low-cost L1
2
-strengthened single crystal high entropy alloy (HEA) was prepared 
through the seeding method of directional solidification. The microstructures, tensile properties and deformation 
mechanisms of the single crystal HEA were systematically investigated. The volume fraction of L1
2 
precipitates 
after heat treatment was almost 80%, which was higher than that of conventional single crystal superalloys. The 
maximum yield strength, ultimate tensile strength and elongation, which were separately as 897 MPa, 1038 MPa 
and 37%, were obtained at the intermediate temperature of 800 
◦
C. Moreover, at higher temperature (900 
◦
C and 
1000 
◦
C), the HEA was able to maintain excellent performance, which was comparable to the commercial Ren ´e 
N5 second-generation Ni-based single crystal superalloy. Finally, the high performance was examined by the 
model of strengthening contribution, which proved that the high-volume fraction of L1
2 
precipitates in single 
crystal HEA was the primary reason for their significant precipitation and solid solution strengthening. Addi -
tionally, the formation of Lomer-Cottrell locks and stacking faults could enhance the high-temperature properties 
of the single crystal HEA. 
1. Introduction 
High entropy alloys (HEAs) are multi-component solid solution 
composed of five or more equimolar or near-molar atomic ratios of 
atoms [ 1 , 2 ]. It is found that the high entropy effect of the HEAs can 
generate stable solution and result in excellent comprehensive proper -
ties, including excellent deformation resistance, wear resistance, 
oxidation resistance and corrosion resistance [ 3 – 6 ]. In recent years, the 
design concept of HEAs provides a new idea for the study of traditional 
heat-resistant alloys [ 7 ]. In particular, the discovery of L1
2 
precipitates 
strengthened HEAs has been demonstrated a new paradigm for devel -
oping advanced structural material with advanced mechanical perfor -
mance. There are mainly attributed unique microstructure (L1
2 
precipitate + face-centered cubic (FCC) matrix) and special work- 
hardening behaviors [ 8 – 11 ]. Therefore, these L1
2 
precipitates 
strengthened HEAs are considered as promising material for heat- 
resistant components, and providing a pathway to solve thorny di -
lemmas in the field of structural material [ 12 , 13 ]. 
However, it was reported that these HEAs with low solvus temper -
atures and insufficient volume fractions of L1

The tensile deformation behavior of experimental alloy at various

1000

further after yielding. The stress decreased to a minimum value as strain 
accumulated. Subsequently, a slight hardening occurred before the alloy 
fractured. 
The strength and elongation trends at various temperature was 
demonstrated in Fig. 4 b. The yield strength (YS), ultimate tensile 
strength (UTS), and elongation of the single crystal HEA increased 
rapidly with the improvement of test temperature and then decreased. It 
was important to note that this behavior was temperature-dependent. As 
the tensile temperature rose from RT to 800 
◦
C, the YS increased 
remarkably to a peak value of 875 MPa. This anomalous yield behavior 
had been generally found that in most of L1
2 
ordered alloys the observed 
flow stress increase with increasing temperature was due to thermally 
activated cross slip from the {111} primary slip plane to the {001} cross 
slip plane [ 28 ]. Since the {001} plane was not the most densely packed 
plane, the super-dislocation segment located on it was difficult to move, 
thus preventing the super-dislocation segment remaining on the {111} 
plane from continuing to move. In addition, the occurrence of a large 
number of faults in the intermediate temperature and the formation of 
different types of interlayer structures hinder the movement of dislo -
cations, which is also the reason for the improvement of yield strength, 
which will be discussed in detail in the following part. Meanwhile, the 
UTS displayed the similar trend with YS and the maximum value was 
1036 MPa. The elongation of the alloy reached the highest value of 37%. 
It was worth noting that the alloy maintained both strength and ductility 
under moderate and high temperature conditions. At 1000 
◦
C, the alloy 
was still a high yield strength of 537 MPa and elongation of 32%. 
Notably, no transient brittle fracture occurred at intermediate temper -
ature due to the absence of softening deformation of grain boundaries.

4. Discussion 
4.1. Effect of alloying elements on the L1
2 
precipitate 
As shown in Fig. 2 , the microstructure of as-cast single crystal HEA 
exhibited regular dendrites arms and no deleterious phases (such as 
topologically close-packed phases (TCP)). Moreover, the volume frac -
tion (~80%) of L1
2 
precipitates was significantly higher than that of 
other single crystal superalloys. It attributed to the addition of strong 
L1
2 
precipitate-forming elements such as Al, Ti and Ta element with a 
total content of nearly 20% [ 31 ]. The Co element played a key role not 
only in solid solution strengthening, but also greatly increased the vol -
ume fraction and dissolution temperature of L1
2 
precipitation by 
reducing the solubility of Al and Ti in the FCC matrix [ 32 ]. Meanwhile, 
lower addition of Cr increased the volume fraction of the L1
2 
precipi -
tation, and reduced the precipitation tendency of TCP, which was a 
crucial element for alloys under high-temperature conditions [ 33 , 34 ]. A 
small amount of Mo had a strong solid solution strengthening effect on 
both the FCC matrix and L1
2 
precipitation [ 35 ]. Therefore, the addition 
of these elements could comprehensively regulate the organization and 
morphology of the alloy, which was conducive to improving the high- 
temperature mechanical properties of the alloy. In addition, as shown 
in Figs. 3 c and d, the L1
2 
precipitates and the FCC matrix exhibited both 
a high degree of coherence and positive lattice mismatch which 
contribute to a better high temperature resistance [ 36 ].

5. Conclusions 
In this paper, a novel and low-cost L1
2
-strengthened single crystal 
HEA was prepared through the seeding method of directional solidifi -
cation. The microstructures, tensile properties and deformation mech -
anisms were systematically investigated. The main conclusions were as 
follows: 
(1) The as-cast microstructure of single crystal HEAs exhibited reg -
ular dendrites arms, and no deleterious phases (such as TCP) were 
found. After heat treatment, the dendrite profile almost dis -
appeared and the (FCC + L1
2
) eutectic microstructure was 
absolutely dissolved. It exhibited the high density as approxi -
mately 80% L1
2 
precipitates uniformly precipitated in the matrix 
and area equivalent average diameter of L1
2 
precipitates as about 
280 nm. 
(2) The YS, UTS and elongation reached the maximum at 800 
◦
C, 
which were 897 MPa, 1038 MPa and 37% respectively. More -
over, the alloy was able to maintain high performance at 900 
◦
C 
and 1000 
◦
C. The experimental alloy exhibited excellent me -
chanical properties at elevated temperatures, surpassing those of 
most heat-resistant equiaxed-crystal L1
2
-strengthened HEAs and 
Co-based single crystal superalloys. Its performance is compara -
ble to that of the commercial Ren ´e N5 second-generation single 
crystal Ni-based superalloy. 
(3) The mechanical properties of single-crystal HEA depended on 
two factors at different temperature. The high-volume fraction of 
L1
2 
precipitates in single-crystal high-entropy alloys was the 
primary reason for their significant precipitation and solid solu -
tion strengthening at room temperature. Additionally, the for -
mation of different types of L-C locks and stacking faults could 
enhance the high-temperature properties of the single crystal 
HEA.
