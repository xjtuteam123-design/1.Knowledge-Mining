In order to characterize the fracture behavior of BT25y alloy at different temperature, in-situ scanning electron
microscopy of this alloy was performed during high temperature tensile deformation process. The results
showed that microcracks mainly initiated at α/α and α/β interfaces during both 600 °C and 650 °C tensile pro-
cess. However, the path of crack propagation varied at different temperature, it was related to the rapid decline
in interface strength with deformation temperature increasing. When deformed at 600 °C, interface strength was
higher than grain strength, cracks propagated into grain interior, the tensile specimen presented transgranular
fracture characteristic. As the deformation temperature increasing to 650 °C, interface strength was lower than
grain strength, cracks tended to propagate along interfaces, and the tensile specimen presented intergranular
fracture characteristic. Mechanical performance was sensitive to deformation temperature, with the temperature
increasing from 600 °C to 650 °C, the ultimate tensile strength fell by 200 MPa.

1. Introduction
(α + β) titanium alloys are attractive for aerospace and marine ap-
plications as structural materials due to their high speciﬁc strength, ex-
cellent thermal resistance, good room temperature plasticity, superior
fatigue and creep strength[1–5]. Among the explored (α + β) titanium
alloys, BT25y alloy (Ti-6.5Al-2Sn-4Zr-5Mo-1W-0.2Si, wt%) with out-
standing high temperature properties, is designed for high temperature
applications up to 600 °C as compressor disk and blade of advanced en-
gines [6]. A great deal of researches have been carried out on element
content, phase transformation, microstructural characteristics and
their effects on the mechanical properties of this alloy in the past few
years [7,8]. However, direct reports on observations of the microprocess

In-situ tensile specimens were machined from the bar via electric

3. Result and discussion
3.1. In-situ tensile curves
Typical tensile stress-displacement curves at high temperature are
shown in Fig. 3 . Various stress levels at which the test is paused for
SEM imaging are marked on these plots. The curves exhibit peak ﬂow
stress followed by continuous dynamic softening. Furthermore, ﬂow
stress decreases with increasing the tensile temperature, due to the mo-
tion of dislocation and interface become easier at higher temperature
[13,14]. The ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of BT25y alloy at 600 °C is
about 200 MPa higher than that at 650 °C.
3.2. Microcrack nucleation and propagation
In-situ SEM tensile deformation is adopted to study the fracture be-
havior of BT25y alloy at different temperature focusing on the
microcrack nucleation and propagation process. Fig. 4 shows the in-
situ SEM images of BT25y alloy taken at sequential load levels during
600 °C tensile process. Before the stress reaching UTS, there is no defect
could be observed on the surface of the specimen. As the strain increases
further, tensile stress decreases, microcracks nucleation alongα/α and
α/β interfaces are observed at stress about 300 MPa, as shown in
Fig. 4a. The size of nucleated microcrack is about 20 nm, however, the
maximum size of microcrack can be observed under the electron micro-
scope is about 500 nm. Therefore, the nucleated microcrack has grown
up when it begins to be found by SEM. The number of microcracks in-
creases with the strain increasing, and the microcracks mainly distrib-
ute along α/α and α/β interfaces (Fig. 4c). Red rectangle ﬁeld in Fig. 4
represents the same location, and this ﬁeld is ampli ﬁed as shown in
the top right corner ofFig. 4b, d. From this enlarged view, it can be clear-
ly observed that the crack propagates through the interior of α and β
phase. It's indicated that transgranular fracture happens in BT25y alloy
during 600 °C tensile process. Meanwhile, the width of crack increases
with the strain increasing.
Fig. 5 shows the in-situ SEM images of BT25y alloy during 650 °C ten-
sile process. This specimen exhibit the same phenomenon of
microcracks nucleation, as for the specimen deformed at 600 °C.
Microcracks nucleate along interfaces at stress about 135 MPa, as
shown in Fig. 5a. With the strain increasing, the number of microcracks
increases, and the microcracks also mainly distribute alongα/α an

interfaces, as shown in Fig. 5c and d. As the strain increases further, the
cracks propagate along interfaces. Unlike the 600 °C tensile process,
only intergranular cracks distribute in the specimen surface could be
observed in Fig. 5 e. With the tensile process continues, the width of
crack increases from tiny crack to cavity, as shown in the red circle

3.3. Mechanism of microcrack nucleation
For the bi-modal microstructure of titanium alloy, the ﬂow stress
of β phase is lower than that of α phase at high temperature [15].T h e
deformation begins from the soft β phase rather than the hard α
phase. In bimodal microstructure of titanium alloys, the α/β inter-
face has a near-Burgers orie ntation relationship ((0001) α//(101) β
and [2 /C221/C2210] α//[ /C221/C221/C221] β) [16] . As the strain increasing, deformation
transforms from β phase to α phase. Therefore only the dislocation
which slipped along the (110) β//[ /C221/C221/C221]β slip system in the β phase
can across the α/β interface easily, resulting in dislocations gliding
from β phase to α phase [10,17] .I tw i l lb ed i fﬁcult for dislocations
across the phase boundary when theβ and α phase do not meet the re-
lationship, which will produce large stress concentration at the inter-
faces. If the stress concentration is not relieved by the deformation of
the adjacent α phase, microcracks will nucleate along interfaces. In ad-
dition, β/β interface tends to be low angle in nature and thus does not
act as strong dislocation barrier nor slide readily [18–20]. Therefore,
microcracks tend to nucleate in α/α and α/β interfaces.

3.4. Mechanism of microcrack propagation
Fig.6 shows schematic diagrams of the microcrack nucleation and
propagation process of BT25y alloy under 600 °C tensile test.
Microcracks tend to nucleate at interfaces (Fig. 6a) during 600 °C tensile
process. Once the microcracks nucleate, they swiftly propagate into
grain interiors, as shown in Fig. 6b. With the strain increasing further,
cracks of adjacent grains coalesce into transcrystalline cracks ( Fig. 6c).
Fig. 7 shows the crack propagation behavior of the alloy during 650 °C
tensile process.Fig. 7b and c exhibit that the crack propagation direction
is along the interface. As the strain increasing further, cracks belong to
adjacent grains connect to each other, as shown in Fig.7d. Crack propa-
gation process at 650 °C of BT25y alloy is different with that at 600 °C. It
has been proven that the gliding of interface becomes easier with the
deformation temperature increasing, which will lead to a decline in in-
terface strength. Therefore, cracks propagation depends on the compet-
itive relationship between grain strength and interface strength. There
existing the critical temperature, when the deformation temperature
is below the critical temperature, interface strength is greater than
grain strength, then transgranular fracture occurs during deformation
process. However, when the deformation temperature is over the criti-
cal temperature, interface strength is lower than grain strength, inter-
granular fracture occurs. From the crack propagation processes at
different temperature, it can be concluded that the critical temperature
of BT25y alloy is between 600 °C and 650 °C. The decrease in interface
strength results in a sharp decline in 650 °C strength, compared with
600 °C strength. Which can be interpreted that the service temperature
of titanium alloy is usually below 600 °C
4. Conclusions
The fracture behaviors of BT25y alloy at 600 °C and 650 °C were eval-
uated using the in-situ tensile SEM surface observations. It was found
that:
(1) Microcracks primarily nucleated along α/α and α/β interfaces
during both 600 °C and 650 °C tensile process.
(2) Microcracks propagated into grain interior, and forming
transgranular cracks during 600 °C tensile process. However,
microcracks propagated along phase boundaries, and forming in-
tergranular cracks during 650 °C tensile process.
(3) The critical temperature, at which the grain strength equal to the
interface strength, is between 600 °C and 650 °C. The decrease in
interface strength results in a sharp decline in 650 °C tensile
strength, compared with 600 °C tensile strength.
