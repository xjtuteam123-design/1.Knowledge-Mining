Abstract 
Owning the excellent high temperature properties, l ow cost and density, 
Al-Co-Cr-Fe-Ni-Ti high-entropy superalloys (HESAs) represent a potential 
substitute for Ni-based superalloys. In this paper, the composition-dependent 
interdiffusivity matrices of face centered cubic (f cc) Al-Co-Cr-Fe-Ni-Ti HESAs at 
1473 K were first determined via a combination of diffusion multiple technique and 
HitDIC software based on the numerical inverse meth od. The correlations between 
the main interdiffusivities and the components sugg est that alloying elements play 
great impact on diffusion in HESAs. After that, com prehensive comparisons of the 
interdiffusivities and tracer diffusivities in present fcc HESAs with the literature data 
in fcc-based pure elements, lower-order alloys and traditional high-entropy alloys 
were conducted. The results indicate that the slugg ish diffusion effect exists for only 
some of the components in the present HESAs. Moreov er, comparisons of the 
diffusion coefficients between the HESAs and tradit ional Ni-based superalloys with 
refractory elements show that the interdiffusivitie s in the present HESAs are lower 
than most of the Ni-based superalloys with refractory elements, but higher than those 
with Ir, Re and Os. Furthermore, it is also found t hat refractory elements need to be 
added in order to further improve the creep resistance of the HESAs. 
Keywords : High-entropy superalloys; Al-Co-Cr-Fe-Ni-Ti; Diff usion; Diffusion 
multiple; HitDIC; Interdiffusivity

1. Introduction 
Due to their excellent high-temperature mechanical and physical properties, 
Ni-based superalloys play very important roles in a erospace, automobile, 
communication, energy and electronic industry secto rs [1]. In order to meet the 
increasing requirements of high-temperature propert ies, more additional amounts of 
refractory elements, such as W, Mo, Re, Ru etc., ha ve been added in Ni-based 
superalloys [2]. However, the increasing additions of refractory elements may lead to 
a series of side effects that cannot be ignored dur ing the development of 
new-generation Ni-based superalloys. On one hand, a dditions of expensive heavy 
elements can cause higher cost and higher density. On the other hand, Re addition can 
be prone to formation of casting freckle defects and bad castability of alloys [3]. Thus, 
one research hotspot is to explore new high-tempera ture alloys for replacing the 
Ni-based superalloys with addition of refractory elements. 
In 2015, a novel superalloy, i.e., AlCoCrFeNiTi-bas ed high entropy superalloy 
(HESA), was developed by Yeh et al. [4]. The compos ition space for such novel 
HESAs locates in between the traditional Ni-based s uperalloys and HEAs. HESAs 
were found to possess comprehensive mechanical prop erties attributed to their similar 
microstructure to the conventional Ni-based superal loys (i.e., fcc-γ matrix plus L1 2-γ′ 
precipitates) in combination with the four core eff ects of high-entropy alloys (HEAs). 
Meanwhile, with few or even no refractory elements, HESAs are with much lower 
density as well as lower cost than the traditional superalloys. Since its discovery, 
HESAs have drawn the increasing attentions in the f ield of materials. Up to now, 
some significant progress has been made, which grea tly promotes the rapid 
development of HESA as well. After the pioneering w ork from Yeh et al. [4], Tsao et 
al. [5-8] presented subsequent series of comprehens ive investigations on the

Nowadays, thermodynamic databases for HEAs are avai lable, like TCHEA-series

of the phase equilibria in different HESAs. By cont rast, no available atomic mobility 
database can be directly used for HESAs. The major hamper lies in lack of any 
experimental diffusivity information in HESAs available in the literature. 
Accurate determination of diffusivities in multicom ponent alloys is still 
challenging. Taking the interdiffusivity for instan ce, the traditional methods are 
widely used in binary and ternary systems, but are very difficult to be generalized to 
multicomponent alloys. Very recently, a numerical i nverse method was proposed in 
our research group for high-throughput determinatio n of the composition-dependent 
interdiffusion coefficients of multicomponent alloy s [13, 14]. Soon after that, based 
on the proposed numerical inverse method [13, 14], a software named as HitDIC 
(High-Throughput Determination of Interdiffusion Co efficients) [15, 16] was then 
developed. So far, HitDIC together with diffusion c ouple/multiple technique has been 
successfully applied to high-throughput determinati on of interdiffusivity matrices in 
different ternary alloys [17-22] and HEAs [23-25]. 
Consequently, fcc AlCoCrFeNiTi HESAs are chosen as the target in this paper.

2. Experimental procedure 
According to the experimental results from Yeh et a l. [4], four different alloys 
(denoted as alloy 1#, alloy 2#, alloy 3#and alloy 4 #) were prepared by arc-melting 
high-purity aluminum (purity: 99.99 wt.%), cobalt ( purity: 99.98 wt.%), chromium 
(purity: 99.95 wt.%), iron (purity: 99.95 wt.%), ni ckel (purity: 99.995 wt.%) and 
titanium (purity: 99.995 wt.%) in an arc melting fu rnace (WKDHL-I, 
Opto-electronics Co. Ltd., Beijing, China) under a high purity argon atmosphere with 
titanium as getter material placed in the arc chamb er, which was equipped with a 
non-consumable tungsten electrode and a water-coole d copper anode. All the samples 
were re-melted for at least four times to ensure homogeneity. The ingots were then cut 
into blocks of 4 mm × 4 mm × 8 mm respectively. Aft er being polished, the alloy 
blocks were sealed into vacuum quartz tubes, homoge nized in a high-temperature 
furnace (GSL1700X, Hefei Kejing Materials Technolog y Co., Ltd., Hefei, China) at 
1473±2 K for 168 hours, and quenched in ice water t o eliminate segregation and 
retain homogeneity. All the annealed alloys were th en verified to be in single fcc 
phase region by means of X-ray diffraction (XRD, D8 Advance, Bruker, Germany) 
and metallographic analysis. The XRD patterns for t he four alloys were given in Fig. 
1(a), which indicates that all the four alloys loca te in the fcc single phase region. In

4. Conclusions 
/circle6 The composition-dependent interdiffusion coefficien t matrices of fcc 
Al-Co-Cr-Fe-Ni-Ti HESAs at 1473 K were determined b y combining diffusion 
multiple technique and HitDIC software based on the numerical inverse method. 
The correlations between the main interdiffusivitie s and the components were 
carefully analyzed. Addition of Al and Ti component s can accelerate the 
diffusion in HESAs, while addition of minor Co and Fe decelerate. Addition of 
Cr component can accelerate the diffusion of Al, Co and Cr, and decelerate the 
diffusion of Fe and Ti. Besides, only Co component can decelerate its own 
diffusion, which might be an important component in decelerating the diffusion. 
/circle6 By comparing the interdiffusivities in present Al-C o-Cr-Fe-Ni-Ti HESAs with 
those in binary and ternary alloys, it can be concluded that sluggish diffusion only 
exists for Al, Co, Cr and Ti in fcc Al-Co-Cr-Fe-Ni- Ti HESAs. A similar 
conclusion can be drawn for the tracer diffusivitie s in the present HESAs alloys. 
Nevertheless, when comparing with traditional HEAs, only Al performs distinct 
sluggish diffusion effect, which indicates that com ponent type is an important 
factor influencing the diffusion in multicomponent alloys. 
/circle6 In view of bulk diffusion, the potential for high-t emperature creep resistance of 
Al-Co-Cr-Fe-Ni-Ti HESAs were fully assessed by comp aring the interdiffusion 
coefficients of the present HESAs with those of the Ni-based superalloys with 
refractory elements. It is concluded that the inter diffusivities in the present 
HESAs are lower than most of the Ni-based superallo ys with refractory elements, 
but higher than those in the Ni-based superalloys w ith Ir, Re and Os. A further
