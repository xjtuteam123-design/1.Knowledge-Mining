Dynamic recrystallisation of Mg–6Zn–2Nd–
0?5Zr alloy
H. T. Zhou *1, Z. D. Zhang 1 and Q. Li 2
Dynamic recrystallisation of Mg–6Zn–2Nd–0 ?5Zr alloy at temperature range from 543 to 693 K
and strain rate from 0 ?001 to 1 s 21 are investigated. Flow softening is found to occur and all flow
curves exhibit a peak with characteristics of dynamic recrystallisation and change significantly
with temperature and strain rate. As a result of dynamic recrystallisation, the grains are greatly
refined through dynamic recrystallisation, and the mean size of the recrystallised grain decreases
with decreasing temperature or increasing Z parameter. By comparison of the grain sizes, 593 K
is found to be the optimal deformation temperature. The yield strength and tensile strength of
extruded Mg–6Zn–2Nd–0?5Zr alloy are 320 and 379 MPa respectively, higher than those of ZK60
alloy.
Keywords: Mg–6Zn–2Nd–0?5Zr alloy, Hot deformation, Recrystallisation, Hot extrusion
Introduction
Mg alloys are of light weight, high modulus, excellent
machinability and superior damping capacity,1–3 and are
widely used in the aero and automobile industries.
However, it exhibits poor workability and poor ductility
at room temperature so that casting, especially die
casting, becomes the principal forming processing.
4,5 It
is well known that the mechanical properties of casting
Mg alloys are lower than that of deformed Mg alloys.
Therefore, deforming processes are necessary. Usually,
it requires hot deformation that gives rise to additional
(prismatic and pyramidal) slip systems in Mg alloys
which contribute signiﬁcantly to large deformation.
6,7
During hot deformation, dynamic recovery (DRV) and
dynamic recrystallisation (DRX) may occur so that
initial grains structure can be reﬁned greatly (initial
grains are replaced by new reﬁned grains) and the
deformation resistance can be reduced. Galiyev et al.
studied the relationship between DRX and deformation
mechanism of ZK60 alloy and pointed out that ultraﬁne
grains can be obtained during severe plastic deformation
and continuous DRX is associated with extensive cross-
slip due to the Friedel–Escaig mechanism in the inter-
mediate temperature range (473–523 K).
8,9 However,
few researchers have investigated the effect of microalloy
element on the deformation behaviour of ZK60.
In the present study, the authors conducted hot
deformation on the Mg–6Zn–2Nd–0 ?5Zr alloy and
recorded the ﬂow stress curves by which its DRX
behaviour was examined to clarify the microstructural
changes at strain rate 0 ?001 to 1 s
21. After selection of
the grain size, the optimal temperature for extrusion is
determined.
Experimental
The chemical compositions of the studied alloys are listed
in Table 1. The alloys were melted in a furnace under
protection of a mixed gas atmosphere of SF6 (1 vol.-%)
and CO
2 (bal.). When the temperature reached 1013 K,
molten metal was stirred for y300 s. After stirring, the
molten metal was hold for 15 min to allow inclusions to
settle to the bottom of the crucible. Then the molten metal
was poured into ingots with a diameter 90 mm at 1003 K.
The ingots were homogenised at 748 K for 24 h and then
hot compressed in a temperature range from 543 to 693 K
and a strain rate range from 0 ?001 to 1 s
21 on a Gleeble
1500 hot simulation machine. All specimens were
quenched in water in situ immediately after deformation.
Samples for microstructure observations were cut from
compressed specimens along the section parallel to the
compression axis and extruded specimens normal to
extruded axis. All these specimens were polished and
etched in the solution of 3% malic acid. Metallurgical
structure was observed by LEICA MEF4M. The mean
grain size was determined by liner intercept method.
Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) structure was
observed by H-800. After heated in induction furnace, the
alloys were extruded in a 3 MN vertical extruding
machine and the billet temperature is 573 K, ram speed
is 0 ?5–2 m min
21, extrusion rate is 14, and extrusion
temperature is 593 K. Then, the mechanical properties of
specimens were tested on a CSS universal electronic
material testing machine.

Typically, the ﬂow stress increases to a maximum and
then decreases to attain a steady state ﬁnally. Such ﬂow
behaviour is characteristic for hot working accompanied
by DRX, which can be described by the thermal
activation stored energy developed during deformation
controlled softening mechanisms.
9–11 With decreasing
strain rate or increasing temperature, the strain hard-
ening effect becomes weaker, while the degree of strain
softening becomes notable. As a result, the peak stresses
vary according to processing parameters, so do the peak
strains. Under a constant strain rate, the peak stress and
the peak strain increase with decreasing temperature.
Under the same temperature, the peak stress and the
peak strain increase with increasing strain rate.

Conclusions
1. The hot deformation behaviour of Mg–6Zn–2Nd–
0?5Zr alloy is interpreted with the activation energy of
167?17 KJ mol 21, and the stress exponent of 7 ?17.
2. Grain reﬁnement is attributed to DRX, and the
recrystallised grain size is related to Zener–Hollomon
parameter.
3. The yield strength and ultimate tensile strength of
Mg–6Zn–2Nd–0?5Zr alloy extruded at 593 K are 320
and 379 MPa respectively, which are higher that of
ZK60 alloy.
