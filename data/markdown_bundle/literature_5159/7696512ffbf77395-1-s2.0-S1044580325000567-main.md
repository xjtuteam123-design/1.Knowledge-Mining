1. Introduction
To enhance the carrying capacity of rockets, liquid rocket engines, 
especially the upper-stage liquid rocket engines, are developing towards 
greater thrust, higher specific impulse, and higher thrust-to-weight ratio 
[ 1 , 2 ]. Therefore, using nozzle materials with superior temperature 
resistance and lighter weight is an inevitable choice for the 
manufacturing of upper-stage engines [ 3 ]. Carbon fiber reinforced car -
bon matrix (C/C) composite, one of the ceramic matrix composites, has 
become the preferred material for the nozzle extension section of upper- 
stage engines due to their superior heat resistance, low density, and 
erosion resistance [ 4 , 5 ]. However, due to the shortcomings of C/C 
composite, such as poor processability, low toughness, and difficulty in 
manufacturing complex shaped components, they are commonly inte -
grated with metallic materials to form composite assemblies for utili -
zation [ 6 – 9 ]. Cobalt based superalloys strengthened by solid solution 
and second-phase precipitation exhibit excellent high-temperature 
resistance, corrosion resistance and excellent processing performance 
[ 10 ], and have been successfully applied in the combustion chambers of 
rocket engines [ 11 ]. With the maturity of C/C composite preparation 
technology and its application in aerospace systems, the joining tech -
nology of C/C composite and Cobalt based superalloys has become a 
crucial scientific challenge that urgently needs to be addressed in the 
new generation of aerospace field.
The bonding between C/C composite and Cobalt based superalloys 
faces two main issues: firstly, the joints often service at high- 
temperature and harsh environments, even above 1200 
◦
C, which re -
quires the joints to possess excellent high-temperature resistance 
[ 12 , 13 ]; secondly, The residual stress in C/C-superalloys joints, as well 
as the compound reaction layer at the C/C/interlayer interface, reduce 
the reliability of the joint [ 14 – 17 ]. So, the joining of C/C composite and 
Cobalt based superalloys need to address the challenges of poor heat 
resistance and low reliability.

solve the problems of poor heat resistance and low reliability.
Transient liquid phase (TLP) diffusion bonding, which combines the 
characteristics of brazing and diffusion bonding, utilizes the inter- 
diffusion between the interlayer and the metal to change the composi -
tion of the interlayer, resulting in isothermal solidification of the 
interlayer [ 25 , 26 ]. Following the homogenization process of the 
composition, the heat resistance of the interlayer (joint) is higher than 
the bonding temperature. So, TLP diffusion bonding has the character -
istic of “ low-temperature bonding/high-temperature servicing ” [ 27 ]. So 
far, several papers have studied the metals-metasl joints with TLP 
diffusion bonding, and a variety of bonding fillers, such as BNi2 [ 28 ], 
BNi5 [ 29 ] and FeNiCrSiB [ 30 ] were utilized. However, there are few 
reports on the joining of ceramic matrix composites and metals with TLP 
diffusion bonding [ 31 ].
TLP diffusion bonding usually requires long-term heat treatment to 
effectively change the composition of the interlayer. Wang pointed that, 
unlike double-interface diffusion observed in metals-metals joints, the 
isothermal solidification of the interlayer in ceramics-metals joints can 
solely be accomplished through inter-diffusion at the metal/interlayer 
interface (single-interface diffusion) [ 32 ]. This indicates the isothermal 
solidification process requires a longer time. As a result, it may lead to 
excessive interface reaction on the side of the ceramic, ultimately 
resulting in a decline in mechanical properties. So, the ceramics-metals 
joints joined with TLP diffusion bonding have a compatibility contra -
diction between the heat resistance and the mechanical properties of the 
joint [ 33 ]. Obviously, the C/C-Cobalt based superalloys joints prepared 
by TLP diffusion bonding also face the above-mentioned contradiction.
There are two aspects that need to be improved to solve the 
contradiction in TLP joints, one is the interface reaction behavior, the 
other is the isothermal solidification rate. The interface reaction 
behavior refers to the reaction of active element and C/C composite. Ti, 
Zr, Nb and Cr are strong carbide forming elements, they are commonly 
used as active elements in the fillers for joining C/C composite and 
metals [ 34 – 36 ]. They react with C/C and often form a continuous car -
bide reaction layers at the C/C/interlayer interface. However, the 
thickness of the reaction layers increase with the extension of holding 
time, which significantly exacerbate their brittleness [ 37 ]. So, it is not 
advisable to use strong carbide forming elements as active element in 
TLP diffusion bonding. Fe is a weak carbide forming element, it can react 
gently with C/C composite, it means that extending the holding time 
may not reduce the interface bonding strength but rather enhance it 
[ 38 – 40 ]. Therefore, Fe element is more suitable as the active element for 
TLP diffusion bonding of C/C composite and metals.
The isothermal solidification rate determines the holding time of TLP 
diffusion bonding. The faster the isothermal solidification rate, the 
shorter the holding time, which is more conducive to controlling the 
thickness of the reaction layer. To accelerate the isothermal solidifica -
tion, the filler requires two characteristics: one is the composition is 
prone to be changed through interface diffusion; the other is changes in 
composition can rapidly enhance the heat resistance of the interlayer. B 
element has a small atomic radius and is prone to diffusion, making it an 
ideal melting-point reducing element for TLP diffusion bonding [ 41 ]. 
Eutectic alloys have the characteristic of low melting point, its melting 
point is lower than that of their components. More importantly, changes 
in eutectic alloy composition can rapidly increase the heat resistance of 
the alloy [ 42 ]. So, the eutectic alloys with B element are ideal fillers for 
TLP diffusion bonding.
Based on the above design ideas, to achieved a heat resistant and 
high reliable C/C-Cobalt based superalloys joint, a novel eutectic Co- 
34.6Fe-4.5B (wt%) was designed to join C/C and Cobalt based super -
alloy GH5188 with TLP diffusion bonding. The superiority of this design 
include four points: firstly, the filler contains B element with a small 
radius, and the filler matrix is the same as the Cobalt based superalloy, 
indicating the composition of the filler is extremely prone to change 
through inter-diffusion; secondly, the melting-point of the eutectic alloy 
is lower than that of its components, and changes in its composition will 
inevitably increase its heat resistance; thirdly, Fe element will react 
gently with C/C composite, allowing the joint to be processed for longer 
time, which is beneficial for resolving the contradiction in TLP joints; 
lastly, a (Co) matrix interlayer with high-temperature resistance can be 
formed, enabling “ low-temperature bonding/high-temperature 
servicing ” . The microstructure evolution and formation mechanism of 
the joints were investigated via various characterization methods. The 
mechanical properties of the joints and the heat resistance of the in -
terlayers were tested and discussed in detail.

4.3. Microstructure evolution mechanism of the joint
Fig. 14 shows the microstructural evolution model of the C/C-(Co-Fe- 
B)-GH5188 joint, the bonding process of the joint could be divided into 
four stages as follows: 
(I) Physical contact: as shown in Fig. 14 (a), before the bonding 
temperature reached the melting point of the Co-Fe-B, there were 
physical contact between the fillers as well as between the filler 
and the substrate. The filler deformed plastically and contacted 
closely with base materials with the increase of bonding tem -
perature, but the diffusion of atoms were limited due to the low 
temperature.
(II) Atoms diffusion and reactions: as shown in Fig. 14 (b) and (c), 
when the temperature reached the liquidus of eutectic Co-Fe-B, 
the filler began melt and spread. At the same time, atomic 
diffusion occured between the filler and the base materials. Since 
the diffusion motivation stemming from the concentration 
gradient disparity, Co, Cr, Ni, W atoms from GH5188 and C atom 
from C/C composite diffused into liquids, and the Fe, B atoms 
from liquid filler diffused into superalloy. A small amount of C/C 
composite was dissolved due to the Fe
–
C eutectic reaction, and 
carbides were formed at the interface and inside the interlayer. 
Meanwhile, a small amount of superalloy was also dissolved 
during the initial holding period. As the diffusion of elements and 
chemical reactions continuously proceed, the composition of the 
filler matrix gradually deviated from the eutectic point.
(III) Isothermal solidification: B element, as the melting-reducing 
element in Co-Fe-B alloy filler, can significantly lower the 
melting point of the bonding filler. Moreover, due to its small 
atomic radius and fast diffusion rate, it is conducive to promoting 
inter-diffusion of elements during the bonding process, especially 
at the interface between the interlayer and the base metal. With 
the extension of holding time, as shown in Fig. 14 (d) and (e), due 
to the isothermal diffusion, the content of the melting-reducing 
element B in the interlayer gradually decreased. In addition, 
the composition of the interlayer matrix gradually deviated from 
the eutectic point. Therefore, the diffusion of B and the change in 
eutectic point composition lead to rapid isothermal solidification 
of the interlayer. As shown in Fig. 15 , the composition of the 
interlayer changed from eutectic point to high-temperature 
resistant (Co, Fe)
ss 
solid solution along the direction of the pink 
arrow. In the interlayer, the closer it is to the superalloy, the 
lower the content of B. Therefore, isothermal solidification 
occured first on the superalloy side. With the extension of the 
holding time, the solid phase gradually grew towards the C/C 
composite until the interlayer completely isothermal solidified. 
Compared to the solubility of C in the liquid phase, the solubility 
of C in the solid phase decreased significantly, a large amount of
