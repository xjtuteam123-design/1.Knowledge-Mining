1. Introduction
High strength to weight ratio is an essential criterion for design
and selection of a material for structural applications in trans-
portation and aviation industries. In this connection, Al-alloys are
the most widely used materials. It is known that the maximum
tensile strength level of age hardenable Al-alloys are limited to
about 500–600 MPa. In contrast, compressive strength of Al-alloys
can be increased to as high as 1200–1400 MPa in amorphous or
nanocrystal dispersed amorphous condition [1–3]. Among various
techniques, mechanical alloying has emerged as a versatile solid-
state synthesis route to develop amorphous alloys with metastable
microstructure [4–6]. Furthermore subsequent heat treatment of
the mechanically alloyed amorphous matrix product may enable
dispersion of nanocrystalline intermetallic phases [7,8]. Hot/warm
pressing or extrusion is a possible method to fabricate bulk samples

amorphous/nanocrystalline powders into fully dense bulk struc-

strength Al-alloys with amorphous, nanocrystalline or nano-
intermetallic dispersed amorphous microstructure. It is known that
mechanical properties of nanocrystalline materials are highly sen-
sitive to the presence of internal defects, heterogeneities and grain
size distributions, caused during the consolidation of the nanocrys-
talline powders [14–16]. Hence, the consolidation process needs
to be optimized for production of dense nanocrystalline materials
with narrow grain size distributions.
In the recent years, the hot isostatic pressing (HIP) and higher
pressure sintering (HPS) has emerged as a versatile technique to
obtain dense and near-net shape bulk product from nanometer-size
powders [17–19]. Hot isostatic pressing involves the simultaneous
application of an elevated temperature and a high-pressure (usu-
ally inert) gas in a specially constructed vessel or container [20].

internal pores or defects within a solid body collapse and weld

This involves the application of very high uniaxial pressure (upto
≈8 GPa) at elevated temperature only for a short period of time [19].
The processing time for both HIP and HPS is shorter compared to
that required for conventional sintering of cold-compacted pellets
(CCS). Therefore, grain growth is insigniﬁcant during either HIP or
HPS, leading to relatively higher yield strength and toughness in
the products of these processes than those found on CCS [22]. Den-
siﬁcation of compacts depends on many process parameters such
as temperature, pressure, and material parameters such as powder
size, creep properties and diffusion constants.
The principal objectives of the present investigation are: (i)
study of microstructure and mechanical properties of the in situ
bulk nano-intermetallic particle reinforced Al50Ti40Si10 amorphous
matrix composite prepared by HIP of the mechanically alloyed
powders; and (ii) comparative examination of the mechanical
properties obtained in products of HIP, HPS and CCS with empha-
sis on analysis of the effect of process parameters. This study is
relevant, particularly because little information is available in the
published literature on the mechanical behavior of these novel
in situ nano-intermetallic dispersed amorphous Al alloy matrix
composite. The mechanical properties evaluated include hardness,
Young’s modulus, compressive strength and compressive fracture
mechanism of the investigated composites.
2. Experimental procedure
Appropriate amounts of Al–Ti–Si elemental powders (with each
constituent having at least 99.5 wt% purity and about 50–100 /H9262m
particle size) in the nominal stoichiometry of Al50Ti40Si10 were
subjected to mechanical alloying in a Retsch PM 400 high-energy
planetary ball mill operated at 300 rpm with 10:1 ball to pow-
der weight ratio using tungsten carbide (WC) coated vial and balls
(10 mm diameter). Milling was carried out in wet (toluene) medium
to avoid agglomeration, retard oxidation, and prevent coating of
milling media (balls and vial) with Al. Fully amorphous Al50Ti40Si10
alloy was obtained after 10 h of mechanical milling.
Thereafter, the mechanically alloyed powder was sealed into a
steel can or cylinder in vacuum, and was processed in ultra-high
pressure HIP equipment in order to consolidate the powders. The
powder volume was heated in temperature range of 300–600◦C
under an isostatic pressure of 1.2 GPa in Ar gas for about 10 min.
Studies on the microstructural evolution, mechanism of solid state
amorphization and thermal stability of the Al50Ti40Si10 amorphous
alloy has already been reported elsewhere [23]. Density of the
compacted and sintered samples was measured using a helium
pycnometer (AccuPyc1330). Measurements were carried out on at
least 5 samples in order to ensure reproducibility and reliability of
the experimental data.
Identities of the constituent phases in the sintered samples
were determined by high resolution X-ray diffraction (XRD) using a
Panalytical X’Pert Pro diffractometer with Cu K/H9251 (0.1542 nm) radi-
ation. Microstructure and micro-composition of different phases of
the composite samples were investigated using ﬁeld emission gun
assisted scanning electron microscope (FESEM, Carl Zeiss, Supra
40) and high resolution transmission electron microscope (HRTEM,
JEOL 2100) with energy dispersion spectroscopy (EDS).
The average hardness and modulus of elasticity of the composite
samples were measured with the help of a nano-indentation tester
(Nanoindentor-XP, Nanoinstrument Incorporate, USA) at 500 mN

presented elsewhere [25]. The fracture surfaces of the composite
after compression test were studied using an FESEM.
3. Results and discussion
Fig. 1 shows the XRD patterns from the Al50Ti40Si10 elemental
powder blend subjected to mechanical alloying in planetary ball
mill for 1, 5 and 10 h, respectively. It is evident that the characteris-
tic peaks of the constituent elements in Fig. 1c disappear after 10 h
of mechanical milling. However, a low intensity and broad peak of
Al3Ti is found superimposed on the broad hump representing the
amorphous matrix. This phase aggregate corroborates the results
of a previous study dealing with mechanical alloying of the same
powder blend [26,27]. Fig. 2 shows the high resolution TEM image

indicate that the milled product at this stage contains amorphous
and nanocrystalline areas. Thus, Fig. 2 corroborates the XRD results
in Fig. 1 and conﬁrms that the microstructure consists of a mixture
of an amorphous and nanocrystalline Al3Ti phase.

It
 is intriguing that the porosity increases and the density
decreases as the temperature of pressing is increased beyond the
optimum temperature in case of both HIP (500 ◦C) and HPS (400 ◦C).
In HPS, a drop in density after sintering at 450 ◦C (from that at
400 ◦C) can be attributed to partial nanocrystallization of the orig-
inally amorphous matrix. It has been reported that a large area
fraction of grain boundaries and triple points reduces the density
[28]. Furthermore, it should be noted that a majority of the pores
is expected to be closed and located at grain boundaries or inter-
phase interfaces in the amorphous/nano-intermetallic composite,
as shown in Fig. 5a. As the pressure of the entrapped gas is inversely
proportional to the radius of pore and temperature, it is more dif-
ﬁcult to close by the application of uniaxial pressure at relatively
higher temperatures. Thus, it is possible to explain at least partly,
why the composite sintered at 450 ◦C has lower density than that
consolidated at 400 ◦C.
The amount of densiﬁcation is found to be relatively insufﬁ-
cient on formation of intermetallic phases, Al3Ti and Ti5Si3 from
the amorphous phase during HIP of Al50Ti40Si10 alloy. It is well
known that volume is decreased and density is increased on amor-
phous to crystalline transformation. In a more generalized manner,
this explanation can be presented as: (i) the intermetallic phases,
which are much harder than the powder particles with amorphous
structure, do not deform under the action of the applied pressure,
and such reduction in plasticity is responsible for the reduced com-
pactibility of the powders, as has been proposed by Senkov et al.
[29]; (ii) the nucleation and growth of crystalline intermetallic
phases within the amorphous matrix, which is assisted by ther-
mally activated diffusion of species, consumes the energy provided
by heating of the sample; and (iii) contraction in volume due to
formation of amorphous to nanocrystalline intermetallic transfor-
mation, if not accommodated by viscous ﬂow of the amorphous
phase can lead to formation of porosities, as shown in Fig. 5a. For
each of the aforementioned arguments mentioned above, it is nec-
essary to consider that nanocrystalline intermetallic precipitates
found in the products made by HIPing at temperatures in the range
of 300–500◦C are surrounded by sufﬁcient amount of amorphous
phase. However, a percolation threshold due to increase in volume
fraction of nanocrystalline intermetallic phases at the expense of
amorphous phase is reached at higher temperature, when densiﬁ-
cation becomes difﬁcult.

4.
 Conclusions
Composites with partially amorphous matrix were synthesized
by mechanical alloying of Al50Ti40Si10 elemental powder blend in
high energy planetary ball mill followed by hot isostatic press-
ing. The microstructure of composites sintered in the range of
300–600◦C have shown nano-size (≈10–30 nm) crystalline precip-
itates dispersed in amorphous matrix.
The major conclusions emerging out of the present study are:
(1) Hot isostatic pressing is an attractive way for consolidation of
mechanically alloyed powders into bulk nano-composite, as it
leads to higher product density compared to HPS and CCS. Max-
imum densiﬁcation is achieved for HIP at 500 ◦C, which is higher
than that for HPS.
(2) XRD analysis shows that mechanical alloying gives rise to amor-
phous/nanocrystalline microstructure after 10 h of milling.
Both XRD studies as well as TEM examination accompanied by
SAD analysis show the nanocrystalline intermetallics includ-
ing Al3Ti and Ti5Si3 dispersed in amorphous matrix, with the
crystallinity increasing with temperature of processing.
(3) The Al50Ti40Si10 composite shows optimum values of hard-
ness (8.61 GPa), compressive strength (1212 MPa), and Young’s
modulus (8.71 GPa) after hot isostatic pressing at 500 ◦C.
(4) The maximum hardness recorded for the product of HIP is found
to be greater than that in the materials processed by either HPS
or CCS. However, the maximum values of Young’s modulus and
compressive strength obtained for the product of HPS are found
to be superior to that processed by HIP.
(5) The ductility of the investigated nano-intermetallic dispersed
amorphous matrix composites is not satisfactory and failure
occurs within the elastic limit. Failure of this composite involves
formation of secondary cracks followed by their linkage, and
separation of clusters of nano-intermetallic dispersoids from
the matrix.
