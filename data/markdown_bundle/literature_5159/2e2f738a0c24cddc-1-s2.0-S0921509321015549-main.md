1. Introduction 
The demand for high-temperature structural applications beyond Ni- 
based superalloys in the aircraft and aerospace industry accelerates the 
development of refractory alloys [ 1 , 2 ]. The strength of metallic alloys 
generally undergoes a drastic deterioration at temperatures above 0.6 
T
m
, where T
m 
is the absolute melting point. Thus, refractory alloys with 
higher T
m 
can have increased the maximum service temperature. Con -
ventional refractory alloys are based on one refractory principal element 
with the addition of a small amount of other elements for optimizing the 
properties [ 3 , 4 ]. This strategy severely limits the development of re -
fractory alloys. High entropy alloys (HEAs), whose definition combining 
the composition-based and entropy-based definitions is the alloys with 
S
SS,ideal 
≥ 1.5R (where S
SS,ideal 
is the total configurational molar entropy 
in an ideal solid solution (SS) and R is the gas constant) and consisted of 
simple solid-solution phases [ 5 , 6 ], have been proposed to develop a new 
generation of structural materials. Specially, refractory HEAs (RHEAs) 
based on refractory elements with high-melting points have recently 
attracted intensive research attention, which are regarded as promising 
candidates for high-temperature structural materials [ 2 , 7 – 11 ]. This is 
because RHEAs usually possess simple phases and excellent mechanical 
properties, such as superior high-temperature softening resistance, high 
hardness and good wear resistance [ 7 , 12 ]. 
RHEAs were first proposed by Senkov et al., in 2010 [ 13 ]. WTaNbMo 
and WTaNbMoV RHEAs with a single disordered BCC solid solution 
phase were prepared successfully, which immediately attracted

persistent strain hardening. The RHEA shows strain hardening up to 
~0.22 true strain during deformation at 1073 K, after which the stress 
decreases gradually with increasing strain. While the RHEA deformed at 
1173 K and 1273 K undergoes the persistent strain softening after 
yielding, as shown in Fig. 3 . 
Extensive microcracks emanated from triple GBs have been observed 
in the RHEA after 1073 K compression. Fig. 6 (b and c) indicate that vast 
dislocations pile up at GBs, and resultant high stress concentrations in 
these regions facilitates the concentrated deformation along GBs. 
Because there is not sufficient dislocation mobility and diffusion to 
relieve stress concentration and coordinate deformation at 1073 K 
compression. Resultant stress concentration accelerates the formation 
and extension of microcracks, which are detrimental to deformation 
stability. In the meantime, abundant fine new sub-grains composed by 
LAGBs shown in Fig. 8 (b) are also the indication of the presence of high- 
internal stress and high-dislocation density along GBs. Therefore, dis -
locations glide and their interaction provide the strain hardening ability 
during initial deformation at 1073 K compression. As the strain 
increasing, high stress concentration at GBs promotes the microcracks 
generation. Meanwhile, abundant substructures produced by dislocation
