ABSTRACT
This study investigates the interactive effects of aluminium and tungsten on the coarsening kinetics of γ ′ pre -
cipitates in Ni-Al-W superalloys at high temperatures using phase-field simulations. Results reveal that Al-W 
interactions suppress coarsening while maintaining high γ ′ volume fractions. Additionally, high W concentra -
tions shift misfit from positive to negative, slowing coarsening while preserving γ ′ content. Increasing Al further 
inhibits coarsening in high-W alloys but is ineffective in low-W systems. Moreover, simulations under constant 
misfit reveal a saturation effect, where optimal Al – W ratios balance interfacial chemistry and elastic energy, 
effectively stabilizing γ ′ microstructures and minimizing coarsening.
1. Introduction
Single-crystal nickel-based superalloys are known for their excep -
tional high-temperature mechanical properties, which are largely 
attributed to the combination of the γ matrix and the γ ′ strengthening 
phase [ 1 ]. However, these properties can degrade significantly when the 
amount, size, or morphology of the γ ′ phase deviates from its optimal 
configuration at elevated temperatures [ 2 ]. Recent advances in alloy 
design have aimed to mitigate γ ′ coarsening in superalloys, with phase- 
field modelling emerging as a key tool for capturing high-temperature 
microstructural evolution. These simulations resolve key phenomena 
such as γ ′ coarsening [ 3 ], creep behaviour [ 4 ], and rafting [ 5 ], especially 
in ternary and multicomponent systems [ 6 ]. However, existing models 
still face challenges, such as accurately modelling the driving force for 
phase transitions and achieving a detailed description of systems near 
equilibrium, all while covering a broad compositional range.
In this study, we present a thermodynamically informed phase-field 
model to address gaps in understanding the coarsening kinetics of a 
ternary Ni-Al-W single-crystal superalloy at 1473 K. The Ni-Al-W system 
is chosen for its well-defined roles of alloying elements: aluminum is 
essential for γ ′ formation and stabilization, while tungsten influences 
lattice misfit and diffusion kinetics [ 7 ]. Despite their importance, com -
bined effects of aluminum and tungsten on γ ′ coarsening remain 
underexplored, particularly under high-temperature conditions where 
microstructural evolution accelerates. By integrating thermodynamic 
and kinetic parameters, our model provides a more accurate description 
of the interplay between alloying elements and their impact on γ ′ phase 
evolution.

between the γ matrix and γ ′ precipitates is influenced by the alloy 
composition, especially with increasing W content. As W content rises, 
the lattice misfit shifts from positive to negative values, and higher Al 
content further reduces misfit across all W levels. This change is driven 
by the redistribution of misfit strain and the role of elastic energy in 
resisting coarsening, rather than by the creation of a two-phase 
mismatch network. The transition from positive to negative misfit 
values as W content increases has significant implications for coarsening 
dynamics, leading to a more stable γ ′ phase with enhanced resistance to 
coarsening. Additionally, a more negative misfit correlates with slower 
coarsening kinetics, while near-zero misfit promotes isotropic γ ′ parti -
cles with spherical morphologies. Moreover, the transition from near- 
zero to negative misfit values induces γ ′ alignment along the 〈 001 〉
crystallographic direction ( Fig. 1 b and c ), emphasizing the role of misfit 
in driving microstructural anisotropy. These results highlight Al – W in -
teractions and misfit effects on coarsening; however, to isolate misfit ’ s 
role, following analysis under constant misfit conditions explores its 
interplay with coarsening kinetics.

elastic strain. W, as a slow-diffusing element, partitions to the γ phase 
and accumulates at the γ / γ ′ interface, modifying interfacial energy and 
acting as a diffusion barrier to slow coarsening. Its effect becomes 
stronger as W concentration increases, reinforcing γ ′ stability. Al, on the 
other hand, stabilizes γ ′ by influencing lattice misfit and interfacial 
chemistry, but its ability to reduce coarsening depends on W concen -
tration. When Al and W are present in an optimal ratio, they work 
together to maximize coarsening resistance. The synergistic interaction 
observed in the Ni – Al – W system is also present in Ni – Al – Mo [ 3 ], Ni-Al- 
Re [ 11 ] and Ni – Al – Ta [ 12 ] superalloys. Mo and Ta affect coarsening by 
slowing diffusion and impacting misfit strain, though their influence on 
lattice misfit is weaker than that of W. However, they remain effective 
diffusion barriers at the γ / γ ′ interface when combined with Al. Addi -
tionally, the substitution of Re for Al in the crystal structure, through 
solid solution, improves creep and coarsening resistance. This reinforces 
our key finding that coarsening resistance is not solely dependent on a 
single element but arises from the interplay between Al and a slow- 
diffusing species (W, Mo, or Ta).
4. Conclusions
This work demonstrates significant influence of Al and W in con -
trolling γ ′ coarsening in Ni – Al – W superalloys at high temperatures. 
Elevated W levels increase lattice misfit, promoting non-spherical γ ′ 
shapes while sustaining a high volume fraction. Al addition also reduces 
coarsening in high-W alloys but has limited effect in low-W systems. The 
results highlight that an optimal Al-W balance stabilizes γ ′ microstruc -
tures by regulating interfacial chemistry and elastic energy.
