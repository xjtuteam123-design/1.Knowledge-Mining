OF COMPLEXLY ALLOYED NICKEL ALLOYS USING EQUATIONS 
OF THE SYSTEM OF NONPOLARIZED IONIC RADII

Modern complexly alloyed high-temperature nickel-base alloys contain tip to 14 alloying elements. The 
complex composition ensures lhlfillment of strict and often contradictou ' requirements imposed on the 
materials of critical parts of gas turbine engines (GTE). However, multi-component alloying creates consider- 
able difficulties in the development of new compositions with specified characteristics or in the optimization 
of existing alloys. The present work is devoted to calculating the high-temperature strength of nickel alloys by 
means of the system of nonpolarized ionic radii (SNIR). 
The development of alloys requires evaluation of the ef- 
fect of the composition on the properties when the concentra- 
tion of several alloying elements is changed simultaneously. 
The solution to this problem is often sought using semi-em- 
pirical methods of representing the chemical composition of 
the investigated alloy in the fbrm of one or a few parameters. 
The parameters may be the type and amount of reinforcing 
phases, the temperature of their dissolution or segregation, 
the lattice parameters of the y'-phase and the matrix, the num- 
ber of electron vacancies, etc. [1 - 3]. 
The PHACOMP method [4, 5] is the most popular. It is 
based on the calculation of the mean concentration of elec- 
tron vacancies for the matrix solid solution, which makes it 
possible to evaluate the composition and to predict the segre- 
gation of the TPS-phases, which worsen the properties. We 
believe that the most essential drawback of the phase calcula- 
tion method is the insufficiently con'ect determination of the 
concentration of vacancies, the dependence of the calculation 
accuracy on the assumptions made on the distribution of ele- 
ments in the matrix and the reinforcing phases, and the lim- 
ited possibilities for evaluating the properties of the material. 
In [6, 7] the composition of the alloy is evaluated by the 
data of phase analysis based on the relation between the mean 
atomic mass of the alloying elements and the concentration of 
their valence electrons. 
Another approach envisages the use of the equations of 
the SNIR of the effective charges of the partners of the intera- 
tomic bond as a function of its length [8 - 10]. It is assumed 
that different crystallographic directions in densely packed 
lattices are characterized by different electron densities not

The purpose of the present work was to estimate the pos-

the alloying elements.

positions obtained by the method of vacuum induction melt-

Therefore, we should take into account one more parameter, 
namely, the size factor A, in order to characterize the compo-

Let us represent the compositions of alloys in the coordi- 
nates (A, Zy) (Fig. 2). As the degree of alloying is increased, 
the parameters A and Zy grow. For example, alloys 5, 7, 9-

and V. Analysis of the computed data allowed us to distin- 
guish three groups of alloys (straight lines in Fig. 2) for each

be seen from the data of Table 1 and Fig. 2 that alloys 1 - 4,
