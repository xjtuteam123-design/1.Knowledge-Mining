1. Introduction
Identiﬁcation and selection of suitable structural materials
possessing the desired high-temperature tensile properties and
corrosion resistance are of major challenge to successful implemen-
tation of a thermochemical process, known as the sulfur–iodine
cycle (S–I) to generate hydrogen using nuclear heat[1,2]. This pro-
cess consists of three chemical reactions, which can lead to the
generation of hydrogen (H
2) and oxygen (O2). The chemical species
are selected to create a closed loop, where water can be fed into
the process, H
2 and O2 can be produced and collected, and all other
reactants (iodine and sulfur dioxide) are regenerated and recycled,
as shown inFig. 1. An operating temperature of 950
◦C has been sug-
gested for the decomposition of sulfuric acid (H2SO4) to achieve a
maximum possible efﬁciency in H2 generation.
An austenitic iron–nickel–chromium–molybdenum (Fe–Ni–Cr–
Mo) alloy, commercially known as Alloy 718 (UNS N07718), was
identiﬁed as a candidate structural material for evaluation of its
tensile properties at temperatures up to 1000
◦C. The selection of
this alloy was based on its superior tensile properties at elevated
temperatures, and excellent corrosion resistance in many hostile
chemical environments [3,4]. This paper presents the results of
tensile testing of Alloy 718, showing anomalous increase in its
yield strength (YS) values at 700 and 800
◦C, a phenomenon known
as yield strength anomaly (YSA) of engineering metals and alloys
[5–7]. A mechanism of YSA of this alloy has been proposed, based
on the results of transmission electron microscopic (TEM) study.

2. Experimental procedures
Round bars of Alloy 718 were procured in a solution annealed condition. Even
though high tensile strength of conventionally aged Alloy 718 may be beneﬁcial
for high temperature applications, it could suffer from very poor ductility. Accord-
ingly, the test material was not subjected to ageing treatment following solution
annealing. They were austenitized at 954
◦C for 1 h followed by a water quench. The
chemical composition of Alloy 718 used in this study is given in Table 1. Smooth
cylindrical specimens were machined from heat treated bars in such a way that the
gage section was parallel to the longitudinal rolling direction. They had an over-
all length of 101.6 mm (4 in.), a gage-length of 25.4 mm (1 in.) and a gage-diameter
of 6.35 mm (0.25 in.), as shown in Fig. 2. A commercially available testing equip-
ment was used to determine the tensile properties of this alloy according to the
ASTM Designation E 8 [8] at a strain rate of 10
−3 s−1. A split furnace was used for
testing specimens at elevated temperatures. Nitrogen was continuously purged dur-
ing high temperature testing to prevent oxidation of the specimen surface. Three
B-type thermocouples were used to monitor the testing temperature. A linear vari-
able displacement transducer measured the extension at the gage section of the
tested specimen. Triplicate testing was performed at temperatures ranging from 30
to 1000
◦C, and the average values of the tensile properties were used in the data
analyses.
TEM and scanning TEM (STEM) were used to characterize dislocations generated
in a tensile specimen tested at 700◦C that exhibited the YSA behavior. Thin ﬁlm sam-
ples having thickness ranging from 0.10 to 0.15 mm, and a diameter of 3 mm were
used in TEM analyses. These samples were mechanically polished and subsequently
electropolished in a solution containing 5 vol.% perchloric acid and 95% ethyl alcohol
at −25
◦C under an applied potential of 55 V. Elemental analyses of these specimens
were performed by using energy dispersive spectrometry (EDS). Further, the extent
and morphology of failure of the tested specimens were determined using scanning
electron microscopy (SEM).

4. Summary and conclusions
The tensile properties of Alloy 718 were evaluated at tempera-
tures ranging from 30 to 1000◦C. Both dynamic strain aging (DSA)
and yield strength anomaly (YSA) phenomena were observed with
this alloy, when tested under tensile loading at speciﬁc temper-
ature regimes. A mechanism of DSA of Alloy 718 has been cited,
which resembles that of Ni-base alloys investigated earlier at the
authors’ laboratories. This paper presents the tensile data, eluci-
dating a basic understanding of the YSA phenomenon of Alloy 718.
The key results and the signiﬁcant conclusions derived from this
investigation are summarized below.
Reduced plastic strain and formation of serrations, observed in
the superimposed s–e diagrams, are the result of the DSA phe-
nomenon.
An anomalous increase in YS and reduced ductility (%El) at 700
and 800
◦C could be attributed to the occurrence of the YSA phe-
nomenon. Such anomalous behavior is the result of/H9253′′and /H9254phase
formation at these temperatures and their subsequent precipita-
tion within grains, causing enhanced hardening due to impaired
dislocation mobility and formation of Orowan loops around grain
boundaries.
The presence of/H9253
′′and /H9254phases was veriﬁed by the EDS spectra
that indicated a composition of Ni3Nb for both phases.
Super-dislocations formed at 700 ◦C could also account for the
anomalous increase in YS of this alloy.
The specimens tested at 30 and 600◦C exhibited ductile failures,
characterized by dimples in their SEM micrographs. On the con-
trary, dimpled microstructures and predominantly transgranular
brittle failures were observed at 700
◦C.
