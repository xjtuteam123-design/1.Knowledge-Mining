ABSTRACT
High-performance elastic materials are of significance in airplanes and space vehicles. It is usually a trade-off 
conundrum to achieve high strength and elastic limit (yield strength-to-elastic modulus ratio) simultaneously 
for engineering materials. In this work, we proposed an entropy-mediated approach to realize the synchroni -
zation of high strength and elastic limit for traditional titanium alloys. By the addition of Zr into Ti64 alloy, the 
entropy-mediated Ti
66
Zr
20
V
4
Al
10 
alloy exhibits tensile and yield strength up to ~1420 MPa and ~1200 MPa, and 
elastic limit of 1.3%, respectively, which are increased by 77 %, 77 % and 120 % compared to those of Ti64 alloy. 
The TEM structure study confirms that the grain boundary, solid solution and dislocation strengthening mech -
anism are responsible for the high strength. The model of first principles calculation shows that the elasticity is 
attributed to the high entropy and severe lattice distortion effect.
With the rapid advancement of new aviation equipment such as 
aerospace load-bearing structural components, high-strength springs 
and so forth, it is urgently desired for new titanium alloys with 
comprehensive strength and elasticity [ 1 , 2 ]. Nevertheless, simulta -
neously achieving high strength and large elastic limit, and/or low 
elastic modulus, is still a challenge in titanium alloys. In recent years, 
attempts have been made to improve the strength of Ti alloys by alloying 
method [ 3 – 6 ]. And first principles calculations have also been con -
ducted to predict the mechanical properties of titanium alloys [ 7 – 9 ]. It is 
noted that most of these works were mainly focused on the high strength 
and elastic modulus titanium alloys [ 10 , 11 ]. Exploring the new 
approach for synchronously achieving high strength and elastic limit is 
still an open question.
The development of medium/high-entropy alloys (M/HEAs) offers a 
novel approach to advancing traditional engineering materials and 
gaining insight into the nature of their structural and functional prop -
erties [ 12 – 15 ]. Notably, the high entropy effect and severe lattice 
distortion resulting from the randomness of the composition and atomic 
size mismatch among the components produce a substantial influence 
on the mechanical properties of M/HEAs [ 16 – 18 ]. In this work, the 
composition design criterion is to ensure the formation of a stable 
single-phase structure. Thus, we designed new entropy-mediated tita -
nium alloys with the nominal compositions of Ti

semi-manufactured production of titanium alloys, with a utilization rate 
of 80 %~ 90 % in the aircraft fuselage [ 19 ]. Given that Zr and Ti are in 
the same transition metal group and Zr has larger atomic size than Ti and 
V, Zr was regarded as the entropy-mediated component. Usually, Zr 
content has a low upper threshold in commercial titanium alloys. It is 
deduced that these entropy-mediated titanium alloys exist more 
randomness in atomic packing and severe lattice distortion, which are 
beneficial to promote strength and elasticity.

twinning. The literature indicates that the generalized stacking fault 
energy decreases with increasing Zr in Zr-Ti alloys, which promotes 
twinning formation [ 36 ]. The twinning alters the orientation of the 
crystal to convert the slip system into a more favorable position for 
slipping, gradually acting as another deformation mechanism to 
enhance strength for the alloys with more Zr atoms.

The elastic properties including shear, bulk and Young ’ s modulus, 
and Poisson ’ s ratio obtained by first principles calculation are shown in 
Table 3 . Both shear and bulk modulus are reduced, whereas Poisson ’ s 
ratio is increased with increasing Zr. Combined with shear modulus and 
Poisson ’ s ratio, Young ’ s modulus, expressed by E = 2G(1 + v) , is 
decreased as Zr increases. The low Young ’ s modulus means a large 
elastic limit under the same longitudinal stress. The elastic modulus 
represents the resistance to atomic displacement from equilibrium po -
sitions and is related to atomic bonding force. The above results indicate 
that both the average bond length between different atomic pairs and 
MSAD increase with increasing Zr. Fig. 4 (d) illustrates the inverse 
relationship between elastic modulus and MSAD. In other words, the 
unit cell undergoes lattice expansion distortion due to Zr doping, leading 
to an increase in interplanar spacing. This expansion weakens the atomic 
bonding force, resulting in a decrease in modulus. The reduction in peak 
intensity and shift in diffraction angles, along with the increase in lattice 
parameters of α ’ structure observed from the XRD pattern, further 
confirm the increase in interplanar spacing due to the lattice expansion, 
which corresponds to the change in modulus.
In conclusion, the synergistic enhancement of high strength and 
elasticity has been achieved for α -phase Ti-Zr-Al-V titanium alloys by 
entropy regulation and control. As Δ S
mix 
increases, the Zr20 alloy ex -
hibits the yield and tensile strengths, and elastic limit of 1200 MPa, 1420 
MPa, and 1.3 %, showing increases of 77 %, 77 %, and 120 %, respec -
tively, compared to the base Ti64 alloy. This alloy possesses the highest 
specific strength among all the referenced titanium alloys and the 
highest elastic limit among all α -phase titanium alloys. As Zr increases, 
the microstructure of the alloys gradually refines, and the deformation 
mode is transformed from dislocation slipping to mixed dislocation and 
twinning. The high strength of alloys is mainly attributed to the grain 
boundary, solid solution and dislocation strengthening mechanisms. 
Theoretical modeling has shown that the large elastic limit is due to the 
increased mean square atomic displacement and weakened interatomic 
bonding force caused by severe lattice distortion.
