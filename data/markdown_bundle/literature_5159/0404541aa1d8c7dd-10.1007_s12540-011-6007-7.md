Effect of Alloy Composition on High-temperature Bending Fatigue
Strength of Ferritic Stainless Steels
Yong-Sik Ahn* and Jeon-Young Song
Department of Materials Sci. & Eng., Pukyong National University
(received date: 14 May 2010 / accepted date: 15 February 2011)
Exhaust manifolds are subjected to an environment in which heating and cooling cycles occur due to the
running pattern of automotive engines. This temperature profile results in the repeated bending stress of exhaust
pipes. Therefore, among high-temperature characteristics, the bending fatigue strength is an important factor
that affects the lifespan of exhaust manifolds. Here, we report on the effect of the alloy composition, namely
the weight fraction of the elements Cr, Mo, Nb, and Ti, on the high-temperature bending fatigue strength
of the ferritic stainless steel used in exhaust manifolds. Little difference in the tensile strength and bending
fatigue strength of the different composition steels was observed below 600
°C, with the exception of the
low-Cr steel. However, steels with high Cr, Mo, or Nb fractions showed considerably larger bending fatigue
strength at temperatures of 800
°C. After heating, the precipitates from the specimens were extracted elec-
trolytically and analyzed using scanning electron microscopy energy dispersive spectrometry and transmission
electron microscopy. Alloying with Cr and Mo was found to increase the bending fatigue strength due to
the substitutional solid solution effect, while alloying with Nb enhanced the strength by forming fine inter-
metallic compounds, including NbC and Fe
2Nb.
Keywords: bending fatigue, stainless steel, alloying, microstructure
1. INTRODUCTION
Exhaust manifolds experience an environment with repeated
stresses due to engine vibration, as well as thermally induced
stress caused by heating and cooling cycles [1]. The high-
temperature bending fatigue strength is an important mate-
rial parameter affecting the lifespan of the part. The environ-
ment of exhaust manifolds has become more severe in recent
years due to the trend of increased engine power. In order to
improve the efficiency of automobile engines and reduce
their weight, the automobile industry is moving to replace
cast iron or cast steel manifolds with stainless steel ones.
Stainless steel has excellent high-temperature resistance to
corrosion and is advantageous in terms of weight reduction.
Further, the use of stainless steel can reduce the mass of the
manifold by 40 %, with the added benefit of bringing the
catalyst more readily up to working temperatures due to the
reduced heat capacity of the part. 
Each heating and cooling cycle caused by start-up/shut-
down of an engine results in substantial compressive and
tensile stress in the exhaust pipes. The thermally induced
bending stress eventually resu lts in fatigue, and we find
crack initiation and propagation, and consequently fracture.
Austenitic stainless steel has excellent corrosion resistance,
high-temperature strength, and formability, but has a higher
thermal expansion coefficient and is more expensive than
ferritic stainless steel [2]. Because of the low material cost
and relatively good high-temperature properties, 409L stain-
less steel, containing 11 % Cr, has been used in the manufac-
ture of exhaust manifolds [3,4]. However, there is a need for
steels with enhanced high-temperature strength and bending
fatigue strength, because of the increasing trend of the
exhaust gas temperature. Here, we report on the effect of the
composition of the alloying elements Nb, Mo, and Ti in
14Cr3Nb and 18Cr ferritic stainless steels on the high-tem-
perature bending fatigue strength.

3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
3.1. Microstructure
The as-received steels were in a hot-rolled and annealed
state. The grain size of the as-received 14Cr3Nb steel was
the largest of the specimens at 94 mm. There was no appar-
ent difference in grain size among the as-received steels con-
taining 18% Cr. The grain size of the steels did not significantly
change after heat treatment for 200 hours at 800
°C, as
shown in Fig. 2. From this, it follows that the grain size did
not increase during the bending fatigue tests, because the
longest test was 110 hours (corresponding to the fatigue limit
of 10
7

fatigue strength in this test range. 
3.2. High-temperature tensile test
The results of the high-temperature tensile tests are shown
in Table 2. In the 600°C tests, there was no remarkable dif-
ference in the tensile strength, yield strength, or elongation
among the different steels. In the 800°C tests, however, the
18Cr5Nb2Mo steel showed the highest tensile strength,
while the 14Cr3Nb steel showed the lowest strength and
the highest elongation. 14Cr3Nb steel contains a lower Cr
fraction, and has a grain size la rger than that of the other
steels. The 18Cr5Nb2Mo steel, containing 2 wt.% Mo
and a higher Nb content, sh owed the highest tensile and
yield strengths.

3.3. High-temperature bending fatigue test
Recent studies have reported that the addition of niobium
improves the high-temperature properties of ferritic stainless
steels [6-9]. Figure 3(a) shows the results of the bending
fatigue tests at 600
°C. The 14Cr3Nb steel containing a lower
Cr fraction showed the shortest lifetime before fracture, Nf,
while the other steels showed no distinct difference in Nf. As
shown in Table 1, all the steels, with the exception of 14Cr3Nb,
contain 18 wt.% Cr. 18Cr3Nb2Mo and 18Cr5Nb2Mo both
have 2.0 wt.% Mo, and include 0.3 and 0.5 wt.% Nb, respec-
tively. This means that the effect of the Nb and Mo fraction
on the bending fatigue strength at 600
°C is insignificant.
The results of the bending fatigue test at 800°C are shown
in Fig. 3(b). The 14Cr3Nb steel still showed a fatigue strength
lower than that of the other steels. 18Cr5Nb2Mo showed the
highest fatigue strength, and 18Cr5Nb (with a high Nb con-
tent, and with (Nb+2Ti)/(C+N)=48) showed a fatigue strength
lower than that of 18Cr3Nb2Mo, which contained 2 wt.%
Mo and a lower Nb content, meaning that alloying with Mo
enhances the bending fatigue strength more than alloying
with Nb. 
The 18Cr3Nb2Mo steel showed higher tensile and yield
strengths, and less elongation in the high-temperature tensile
test at 800
°C compared with 18Cr5Nb, as shown in Fig.
2(b). The results of the high-temperature tensile tests were in
agreement with those of the bending fatigue test. 14Cr3Nb
steel showed both the lowest high-temperature strength and
the lowest bending fatigue strength at 800
°C, which was
attributed to the lower Cr content. The 18Cr5Nb steel, con-
taining no Mo, showed the next lowest tensile and bending
fatigue strengths. 18Cr3Nb2Mo and 18Cr5Nb2Mo steels have
the same Mo and Cr content, but differing Nb content.
18Cr5Nb2Mo showed a higher tensile and bending fatigue
strength at 800
°C, which means that alloying with Nb
enhances the high-temperature mechanical properties. 
3.4. Analysis of precipitates

extracted residues of the 18Cr5Nb2Mo steel. The as-received
steel, shown in Fig. 4(a), contained a small amount of spher-
ical or granular shaped precipitates. After exposure to 800°C,
various shaped precipitates – including rods, plates, and
granular shapes, as shown in Fig. 4(b) - were observed. This
was the same in the other types of steel. Figure 5 provides a
TEM-EDS photograph of the carbon replica of the electro-
lytic-extracted residue from the 18Cr5Nb2Mo steel. The fine
round and granular particles were identified as Nb and Ti
MC Carbides, while coarse plate or rod-shaped precipitates
were identified as Fe
2Nb Laves phase. After heat treatment
at 800°C for 20 hours, the Fe2Nb phase formed the majority
of the precipitates in all the steels. The same result was
observed after heat treatment at 600
°C for 200 hours; how-
ever, the Fe2Nb particles were much finer than those formed

4. CONCLUSION
There was little difference in the tensile properties of the
different steels at 600 °C. Greater variation in the tensile
strength was observed at 800°C. 14Cr3Nb steel, which has a
relatively low Cr content, showed the lowest tensile strength
at 800
°C. The steels containing Mo and higher Nb contents
showed higher tensile strengths at 800°C. 
14Cr3Nb steel also showed the lowest bending fatigue
strength at 600°C, although there was little variation among
the other steels. The bending fatigue test at 800°C showed a
more significant difference, and the different steels could be
ranked in terms of the fatigue strength as follows, starting
with the highest: 18Cr5Nb2Mo, 18Cr3Nb2Mo, 18Cr5Nb, and
14Cr3Nb. 
(Ti,Nb)(C,N) particles were mainly found in the as-received
steels, regardless of the type of steel. Fe
2Nb phase was pre-
cipitated by heating at 800°C, and comprised a large major-
ity of the precipitates in the steel after 20 hours. 
The bending fatigue strength at 800°C was higher when
the Nb and Mo contents were larger. This was due to the
Fe2Nb Laves-phase precipitates and NbC particles. Most Mo
elements were not combined as precipitates but remained as
solute atoms.
