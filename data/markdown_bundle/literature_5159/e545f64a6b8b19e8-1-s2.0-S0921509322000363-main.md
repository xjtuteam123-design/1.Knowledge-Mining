ABSTRACT 
To improve the high temperature (up to 1100 
◦
C) strength of NiAl-based alloy, a method of the microstructural 
optimization for achieving network Cr(Mo) reinforcements was proposed. A network Cr(Mo) microstructure was 
successfully obtained by evolution of the ultrafine lamellas in the sintering of rapid-solidified powders. As a 
result, the micro-sized Cr(Mo) networks are formed at the sintering necks and nano-sized Cr(Mo) networks exist 
inside the powders. Moreover, the Cr(Mo) phases are interconnected and their network morphology is charac -
terized in 3D using micro-CT, as well as light optical tomography based on OM and SEM. Compared with other 
NiAl-based alloys, the sintered sample with network Cr(Mo) reinforcements has a considerably improved 
strength at high temperature. Based on detailed TEM analysis, the high temperature strengthening mechanism is 
discussed and the proposed network microstructure for NiAl-based alloy can provide synergetic strengthening 
effect, which is beneficial for achieving enhanced high temperature strength. 
1. Introduction 
The intermetallic NiAl has attracted great attention of both re -
searchers and industrials due to its high melting point, low density and 
excellent oxidation resistance, and it is considered as a promising 
candidate to take place of Ni-based alloys for high-temperature struc -
tural materials [ 1 – 3 ]. However, several severe shortcomings are still 
impeding the actual application of this alloy and the main bottleneck is 
the insufficient high-temperature strength of NiAl alloy (e.g. 55 MPa at 
980 
◦
C [ 4 ]) in comparison with the widely used Ni-based superalloys 
(173 MPa at 1000 
◦
C for GH170) [ 5 – 8 ]. Due to the complicated mech -
anisms of strengthening and deforming, it is challenging to design the 
NiAl-based alloys for improving the high-temperature strength. 
Considerable works have been devoted to overcome this bottleneck, 
such as alloying of the Cr, Mo or Ag elements [ 9 – 13 ], forming metal 
matrix composites (MMCs) [ 4 , 14 ], rapid solidification (RS) [ 15 , 16 ] or 
directional solidification (DS) [ 17 , 18 ]. In these methods, it is well 
documented that the microstructure optimization for strengthening will 
be a convincible way based on the previous experimental results. 
By considering the developments of the network composites, it is 
noted that forming the network structure is conceived to improve the 
strength. In order to improve the high-temperature performance of NiAl, 
introducing Cr(Mo) reinforcements or hard particles into NiAl matrix 
are extensive adopted. It is documented that the ultimate strength of 
NiAl-based MMCs by adding the hard particles is lower than that of the 
eutectic NiAl – Cr(Mo) alloy at 1000 
◦
C [ 4 , 7 , 14 ], which discourages the 
attempt to form network MMCs similar to previous works. In this case, it 
is more important to optimize the structure of Cr(Mo) in eutectic alloys 
and form the network Cr(Mo) reinforcements. Furthermore, it is also 
noted that the strengthening effect of different mechanisms in these 
alloys will change with the increase of service temperature, which leads 
to the insufficient strengths of MMCs in comparison with the eutectic 
NiAl – Cr(Mo) alloy. Therefore, a detailed understanding of the structure 
of reinforcements Cr(Mo) dependent strengthening mechanisms still 
remains unclear and more insights into this area are undoubtedly 
needed. 
However, based on the previous results, it is difficult to change the 
structure of the lamellas of NiAl and Cr(Mo) in the eutectic alloy by 
adding elements. According to recent researches, it is shown that the Cr 
(Mo) lamellas can grow up via heat treatment, which can lead to 
mismatch and the termination of Cr(Mo) lamellas and form an inter -
connected structure [ 17 , 19 , 20 ]. Meanwhile, it is also noted that this

On the other hand, it is well known that the rapid solidification is widely

In this paper, the NiAl-based alloy was prepared by hot-press sin -

The sintered alloy has an optimized microstructure of Cr(Mo) networks 
and the high-temperature strength is significantly improved. Further -
more, the high-temperature strengthening mechanisms of the alloy are 
discussed in detail.

rapid solidification method with nominal composition of NiAl – 28Cr-

LOT is a combination of serial sectioning and recording of light

by optical microscope (Leica DM4000M, Germany) and the Cr(Mo) 
networks with nano size was further characterized by SEM in a 
resolution of 0.029 and 0.015 μ m. 
To obtain the slices, the as-sintered samples were polished by a 
DECCA MP-2 polished machine using 3000 mesh sandpapers. On the 
surface, two Vicker diamond indents were made by a Vickers indenter 
(Model: HVS-1000, China) with a normal load of 1 kg and a dwell time of 
10 s for aligning the micrographs and measuring the amount of material 
removed after each step of polishing. The average depth of materials 
removed was ~80 nm for the optical microscope observation and ~40 
nm for the SEM observation. A total of 105 and 38 slices with a z-axis 
height of 8.4 μ m for the optical microscope observation and 1.5 μ m for 
the SEM observation were collected for 3D reconstruction. The contrast- 
selection of micro-scale Cr(Mo) phase in 2D original data was employed 
by Image J software and the 3D reconstruction, post processing and 
morphology analysis were employed by Dragonfly 4.0 software. 
Transmission electron microscopy (TEM, Talos F200X) equipped 
with selected area electron diffraction (SAED) apparatus and EDX 
spectrometer were conducted to identify the microstructure and the 
morphology of the dislocations in deformed samples. TEM foils were 
prepared by mechanical polishing of the cut discs with size of 3.0 mm in 
diameter and 40 μ m in thicknesses, followed by ion beam etching.

4.2.2. Evaluation of various strengthening mechanisms at high temperature 
Based on the microstructure characterization, several strengthening 
mechanisms including load bearing, Orowan strengthening and solution 
strengthening are analyzed. It is of interest to note that these mecha -
nisms show different strengthening effect at 1000 
◦
C as recorded in 
Fig. 8 , which indicates the importance for achieving the eutectic alloy 
dependent microstructural optimization rather than forming the MMCs. 
In order to discuss the strengthening mechanism at high temperature, 
the understanding of influence of temperature on these widely used 
strengthening mechanisms is still needed. 
For further discussion, these mechanisms are classified as two cate -
gories according to their strengthening effects: (1) The Orowan and the 
solid solution strengthening are regarded as the hindering of dislocation 
movement, and (2) the load bearing effect of reinforcements can 
accommodate more load during deformation. 
For the load bearing effect (2), the strengthening is based on the 
bonding between the reinforcements and the matrix. In this case, the 
classical model of mixed rule for the deformable reinforcements is still 
applicable for high temperature so as the bonding remains [ 29 , 30 ]. 
Accordingly, the decreased strength of composites with the increase of 
temperature can be mainly attributed to the weakened matrix or the 
reinforcements. Moreover, since the strength of the matrix deteriorates 
significantly at high temperature, the addition of in-situ deformable 
reinforcements with high strength and strong bonding (e.g. Cr(Mo)) can 
reinforce the material more considerably in comparison with the MMCs 
(hard-to-deform oxide or ceramic particles) that employs the Shear Lag 
model [ 31 ].

4.2.3. Advantages of network microstructure for achieving enhanced high- 
temperature strength 
Based on above demonstration, the advantages of the obtained 
network microstructure for strengthening is further discussed. 
For mechanism (1), the hindering of dislocation movement resulted 
from the refined Cr(Mo) network and solid solution atoms obtained by 
rapid solidification will strengthen the sintered alloy. In addition, the 
precipitates and IDs at the interface can also impede the dislocations, 
which show strengthening effect similar to Orowan mechanism. Since 
the nano-sized reinforcements can hinder the dislocations more effec -
tively [ 34 ], the sufficient nano-scale Cr(Mo) networks plays key role on 
hindering dislocations. 
As demonstrated by section 4.2.2 , the load bear mechanism (2) plays 
another key role for high-temperature strengthening. Although the load 
bearing effect can also be realized by adding hard-to-deform re -
inforcements with different sizes, the strong bonding between in-situ 
deformable reinforcements Cr(Mo) and NiAl matrix leads to more 
considerable strengthening in comparison with the MMCs, indicating 
the advantages of the microstructure obtained in this work. 
Furthermore, by considering that the NiAl – Cr(Mo) alloy fabricated 
by rapid solidification has similar high-temperature strength of the as- 
cast alloy with identical composition [ 16 ], this study changes the 
traditional lamella microstructure significantly and forms the network 
reinforcements. Owing to the better strengthening effect of the network 
structure, it leads to a considerable improvement of strength on service 
temperature than as-cast alloy. The micron-sized Cr(Mo) networks sur -
rounded the eutectic cells and the nano-sized Cr(Mo) networks inside 
the cells that can undertake the load will both effectively strengthen the 
alloy. Moreover, as shown by the 3D model, these interconnected 
double-scale networks are observed to connect with each other. Due to 
the stronger load bearing effect of the interconnected reinforcements

5. Conclusion 
In this work, the NiAl-based alloy with network Cr(Mo) re -
inforcements was achieved by rapid solidification and HPS. The ob -
tained alloy shows considerably improved strength at high temperature 
and the strengthening mechanisms have been analyzed. The main con -
clusions can be drawn as follows: 
1. The size of Cr(Mo) lamella in the obtained powders is ~90 nm. The 
Cr(Mo) phases in the intercellular regions transformed from the 
sintering necks are micro-scale and nano-sized Cr(Mo) phases exist 
inside the eutectic cells after sintering. However, the overheating 
during sintering leads to obvious growth of Cr(Mo) and the evolution 
of this network microstructure. 
2. The 3D morphology of the Cr(Mo) networks are analyzed by multi- 
scale characterization methods. For the sample sintered at 
1225 
◦
C/60 min, the results show that the micro-scale Cr(Mo) (~1.7 
μ m) in the alloy forms the interconnected networks and surrounds 
the C
p 
cells. In addition, the nano-sized Cr(Mo) (~166 nm) forms the 
nano-scale network inside the C
p 
cells. 
3. The alloy sintered at 1225 
◦
C/60 min with Cr(Mo) networks shows a 
considerable improvement of high-temperature properties, and the 
UTS and fracture strain of the sample at 1000 
◦
C are 405.4 MPa and 
0.162 respectively. Then, the UTS of sample sintered at 1300 
◦
C/30 
min with transformed Cr(Mo) networks decreases to 358.7 MPa at 
1000 
◦
C. 
4. For the sintered NiAl-based alloy, the strength reduces evidently at 
the temperature higher than T
d 
due to the climbing of dislocations. 
To further improve the strength at temperature above T
d 
(e.g. 
1000 – 1100 
◦

load bearing and hindering dislocation movement is required. For 
the sample sintered at 1225 
◦

network Cr(Mo) reinforcement leads to the highest strength at 
elevated temperatures.
