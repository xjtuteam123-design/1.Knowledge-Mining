Slow-Growing Titanium Dioxide on Ti-48Al Porous Alloy
Mediated by Nb and Cr Addition: Perspective via Local
Metal–Oxygen Bonding Strength
Fang Cheng, Wanyuan Gui, and Junpin Lin
(Submitted April 26, 2019; in revised form November 24, 2019)
TiAl porous alloys exhibit potential for application in high-temperature ﬂue gas ﬁltration, because of their
satisfactory mechanical properties and excellent high-temperature creep resistance. However, poor oxida-
tion resistance and corrosion resistance at temperatures exceeding 800 /C176C considerably limit the practical
applications of TiAl porous alloys. The disadvantages of TiAl alloys can be mitigated by adding a speciﬁc
amount of alloying elements to delay oxygen diffusion. In this study, two kinds of porous alloys, Ti-48Al and
Ti-48Al-2Nb-2Cr, are used to evaluate the high-temperature oxidation resistance and sulﬁdation resistance
of TiAl-based porous alloys. With the addition of alloying elements Nb and Cr, the high-temperature
oxidation resistance and sulﬁdation resistance of the TiAl alloys have greatly improved. The mechanisms
underlying phase transformation are also explained using the ﬁrst-principles theory. The excellent
antioxidation and anti-sulﬁdation properties of the Ti-48Al-2Nb-2Cr porous alloy are mainly attributed to
the markedly reduced free energy of Al 2O3 formation and suppress the presence of TiO 2 at elevated
temperatures, as explained by ﬁrst-principles calculations. The Ti-48Al-2Nb-2Cr porous alloy has advanced
the application of multiple alloying in high-temperature ﬂue gas ﬁltration.
Keywords high-temperature ﬁltration, oxidation resistance,
structural material, sulﬁdation resistance, TiAl-based
porous alloys
1. Introduction
The recovery and utilization of high-temperature ﬂue gas
from the chemical and power plants high ﬂue gas have become
an indispensable technique in contemporary society, because of
the large amount of excess heat and abundance of ﬂue gas.
However, the development of porous materials to purify high-
temperature ﬂue gas remains a signiﬁcant challenge, because of
its high-temperature (over 800 /C176C) and severe corrosion
environments. Current porous ceramics and porous metals
present disadvantages that are difﬁcult to address: for porous
ceramics, severe brittleness and poor workability, and for
porous metals, poor resistivity to high-temperature oxidation. In
addition, the high production cost involved in their complex
fabrication restricts their further development and application
(Ref 1-5). Ideal porous materials should not only exhibit good
mechanical properties and excellent workability, and they
should also be characterized by ultrastrong high-temperature
oxidation resistance and corrosion resistance.
Among numerous porous materials, TiAl porous alloys are
anticipated to become the next-generation high-temperature
structural materials for use in high-temperature ﬂue gas
ﬁltration, because of their high speciﬁc strength, high Young /C213s
modulus, and excellent high-temperature creep resistance (Ref
6-10). However, poor oxidation resistance and corrosion
resistance at temperatures exceeding 800 /C176C signiﬁcantly limit
the practical applications of TiAl porous alloys. Their insufﬁ-
cient oxidation resistance and corrosion resistance at high
temperatures are attributed to the simultaneous formation of a
non-protective TiO 2 ﬁlm and Al 2O3 mixture, rather than
continuous Al 2O3, during oxidation (Ref 11-13).
The drawback of TiAl porous alloys can be alleviated by
adding alloying elements, such as Nb, Ta, Y , Ni, and Si, to these
alloys owing to the ability of these dopants to delay oxygen
diffusion (Ref 8, 14-22). Among numerous alloying elements,
Nb incorporated into TiAl is the most effective method to
improve the oxidation resistance and corrosion resistance (Ref
8, 20), particularly for the high Nb-TiAl alloy. Nb addition can
effectively improve the oxidation resistance of TiAl alloys. Nb
was detected in the inner layer of TiO 2, but not in Al 2O3.T i O2
grew mainly by oxygen diffusion through oxide scales via a
vacancy mechanism. Nb substituted Ti in TiO 2 as a cation with
a valence of 5 in the inner oxide layers, which was expected to
decrease the oxygen vacancy concentration owing to elec-
troneutrality in the oxide. Such reduction is expected to
suppress the growth of TiO 2 in the inner oxide layer. The
growth stress induced by a fast-growing oxide is suppressed in
the oxide scale of high Nb-containing alloys, conferring
increased protection on oxide scales (Ref 23, 24). However,
high Nb content can increase production costs, and its
corresponding mechanism has yet to be determined. To
enhance the high-temperature oxidation resistance and corro-
sion resistance of TiAl alloys, a second alloying element (e.g.,
Ni and Si) is typically added to form a TiAl-based multicom-

TiO2 at elevated temperatures.
2. Experimental
2.1 Materials
Commercial Ti, Al, and Nb powders (99.9 wt.% purity)
with average particle size of about 75 lm were supplied by
Beijing DK Nano Technology Co., Ltd.
2.2 Instruments
X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were collected using the
TTR III multipurpose x-ray diffractometer instrument equipped
with CuKa radiation ( k = 0.15406 nm), and 2 h diffractograms
were recorded in the scan range of 10 /C176-90/C176. FESEM studies
were performed using the Zeiss Supra 55 instrument at an
acceleration voltage of 15 kV . The elemental compositions of
the samples were determined by energy-dispersive spec-
troscopy (EDS) with a takeoff angle of 36.5 /C176and live time of
2 min for further precision quantiﬁcation. The pore parameters
were collected by mercury intrusion porosimetry using a
Quantachrome AUTOSCAN-33 porosimeter.
2.3 Preparation of TiAl-Based Porous Materials
The Ti-48Al porous alloy was prepared using commercial Ti
and Al powders at an atomic ratio of 52:48, and the Ti-48Al-
2Nb-2Cr porous alloy was prepared using Ti, Al, Nb, and Cr
powders at an atomic ratio of 48:48:2:2. Two kinds of
ingredients were mixed (ball-to-powder weight ratio 3:1) in a
ball crusher by ball milling with 120 rpm for 10 h and then
pressed into green pellets (about 20 mm diameter) under
pressure of 200 MPa. Subsequently, a four-step heat treatment
(120 /C176C/1 h for vapor evaporation; 600 /C176C/3 h for Al and Ti
reaction; 900 /C176C/3 h for Al and Nb reaction and phase
transformation; 1350 /C176C/3 h for further phase equilibrium
transition) was employed to prepare the Ti-48Al and Ti-48Al-
2Nb-2Cr porous alloys under vacuum conditions.
2.4 Isothermal Oxidation and Sulﬁdation Test
Isothermal oxidation and sulﬁdation tests were conducted in
a quartz tube furnace. The isothermal oxidation behaviors of the
Ti-48Al and Ti-48Al-2Nb-2Cr porous alloys were observed at
900 /C176C for 100 h. All samples were weighed at various
intervals (5, 10, 20, 40, 60, 80, and 100 h) by using an
analytical balance with an accuracy of 10 /C05 g. The isothermal
sulﬁdation behaviors of the Ti-48Al and the Ti-48Al-2Nb-2Cr
porous alloys were observed at 900 /C176C for 10 h to evaluate the
high-temperature sulﬁdation resistance of the samples via
continuous aeration of SO 2 gas into the quartz tube furnace.
Excess SO 2 gas was recovered using supersaturated sodium
hydroxide. In addition to weight measurements, FESEM and
EDS were performed after isothermal oxidation and sulﬁdation
treatment (Fig. 1).
3. Results and Discussion
3.1 XRD Patterns of TiAl-Based Porous Alloys
The XRD patterns obtained from the surfaces of the Ti-48Al
and Ti-48Al-2Nb-2Cr porous alloys are presented in Fig. 2.
The Ti-48Al porous alloy mainly consists of two phases Ti 3Al
and TiAl, whereas the Ti-48Al-2Nb-2Cr porous alloys mainly
consist of three phases Ti 3Al, TiAl, and Nb 2Al. These ﬁndings
are consistent with our previous study (Ref 8, 21, 25, 26).
The XRD pattern of the Ti-48Al-2Nb-2Cr porous alloy
sintered in different temperature gradients is presented to
further investigate the effect of Nb and Cr addition on the phase
formation mechanism of TiAl porous alloys (Fig. 3). TiAl3 was
obtained at 600 /C176C via a diffusion reaction between Al and Ti
and then completely transformed into TiAl and Ti 3Al at 600-
900 /C176C. NbAl 3 was formed at 900 /C176C via a diffusion reaction
between Al and Nb and then transformed into Nb 2Al at 900-
1350 /C176C, which is a soluble solid in the interior of the matrix
(Ref 8, 21, 25, 26).
3.2 Pore Parameter of TiAl-Based Porous Alloys
The pore parameters (pore diameter, pore volume, pore area,
porosity, and density) of the Ti-48Al and Ti-48Al-2Nb-2Cr
porous alloys were analyzed by MIP are depicted in Table 1.
For the Ti-48Al porous alloy, an average pore diameter of
8.39 lm is observed from a Gaussian distribution, similar to the
results reported by Y ang. (Ref 8). However, for the Ti-48Al-
2Nb-2Cr porous alloy, the peak position of the pore diameter
occurs around 7.59 lm. The results suggest that by adding of
2 at.% Nb and 2 at.% Cr to the Ti-48Al porous alloy, the
average pore diameter distribution tends to be narrow. The pore
area, pore volume, and porosity of the Ti-48Al-2Nb-2Cr porous
alloy are slightly reduced relative to those of the Ti-48Al
porous alloy, because of the addition of Nb and Cr. This ﬁnding
is consistent with previous studies (Ref 6, 8).
3.3 High-Temperature Oxidation Resistance
Figure 4 presents the FESEM images of the surface
morphology of the Ti-48Al and Ti-48Al-2Nb-2Cr porous
alloys. A porous skeleton microstructure consisting of irregular
spherical particles was characterized via FESEM before
isothermal oxidation (Fig. 4a). After 900 /C176C/100 h isothermal
oxidation treatment, the pores of Ti-48Al porous alloy com-
pletely disappeared, owing to the formation of the TiO 2 and
Al2O3 mixture (Fig. 4c). With the addition of 2 at.% Nb and
2 at.% Cr (Fig. 4b), ﬂoccules or irregular white precipitates
appeared on the surface of the Ti-48Al-2Nb-2Cr porous alloy,
which were conﬁrmed to be the Nb 2Al phase in our previous

3.4 High-Temperature Sulﬁdation Resistance
The high-temperature sulﬁdation behavior of the Ti-48Al
and Ti-48Al-2Nb-2Cr porous alloys was also investigated
(Fig. 6). Figure 6(a) and (b) shows the changes in the
micropores of the Ti-48Al and Ti-48Al-2Nb-2Cr porous alloys
after the 600 /C176C/10 h isothermal sulﬁdation. The results
indicate that after the 600 /C176C/10 h isothermal sulﬁdation, the
Ti-48Al porous alloys showed multiple corrosion marks,
whereas the Ti-48Al-2Nb-2Cr porous alloys only showed
some spherical particles on the surface of the skeletons. As the
temperature of the isothermal sulﬁdation increased to 900 /C176C
(Fig. 6c and d), the pores of the Ti-48Al porous alloy
completely disappeared because of TiO 2 formation, whereas
most pores of the Ti-48Al-2Nb-2Cr porous alloy were retained,
with the alloy surface showing multiple corrosion marks.
The surface compositions of the Ti-48Al and Ti-48Al-2Nb-
2Cr porous alloys after 900 /C176C/100 h isothermal sulﬁdation are
presented in Table 4 and 5. Only Ti and O can be detected in
the Ti-48Al porous alloy, unlike in the Ti-48Al-2Nb-2Cr
porous alloy in which a small amount of Al can also be
detected. This ﬁnding indicates that Nb and Cr addition
increases the Gibbs free energy of formation in TiO 2 and
reduces the same in Al 2O3. Consequently, TiO 2 and Al 2O3 are
simultaneously formed on the surface of the Ti-48Al-2Nb-2Cr
porous alloy.
3.5 Slow-Growing Alumina Scale Mediated by Nb and Cr: As
Explained by First-Principles Calculations
Interatomic bonding strength/energy can be quantiﬁed using
stretching force constants (SFCs) after ﬁrst-principles phonon
calculations. Here, we found that the accelerated oxide scale
growth of a-Al2O3 and the delayed oxide scale growth of TiO 2,
mediated by Nb and Cr, occur via the strong Nb-O and Cr-O
bonding energy from the present SFC model applied to oxides
(Al2O3,T i O2,N b 2O5, and Cr 2O3, explained by ﬁrst-principles
calculations. All oxide supercells are given in Fig. 7, 8, 9 and
10).
The present model identiﬁes Nb and Cr as two elements that
promote a-Al2O3 oxide scale growth and delay TiO 2 oxide
scale growth, which agrees with the analyses of the bulk
modulus, melting point, and enthalpy of formation of oxides, as
well as our experimental observations. Force constants are
more suitable for characterizing bond strength between atomic
pairs, compared with electronegativity, which describes the
tendency of an atom to attract electrons toward itself. The
growth of Al 2O3 relates to oxygen diffusion via M-O bond
breakage by exceeding the diffusion activation energy Q of
oxygen. As shown in Fig. 11, the calculated SFCs at 0 K for
various metal–oxygen (M-O) pairs are based on the stable ox-
ides. Notably, the key SFC is the largest SFC (denoted by fs0)
with typically the shortest bond length r0, such as fs0 = 5.59
eV/A˚ 2 with r0 = 1.86 A˚ for the Al-O bond in bulk Al 2O3,
fs0 = 4.59 eV/A˚ 2 with r0 = 2.05 A˚ for the Ti-O bond in bulk
TiO2, fs0 = 14.49 eV/A˚ 2 with r0 = 1.74 A˚ for the Nb-O bond in
bulk Nb2O5, and fs0 = 5.09 eV/A˚ 2 with r0 = 1.61 A˚ for the Cr-
O bond in bulk Cr 2O3 (Ref 27, 28). All r0 values of Ti-O are
larger than those of Al-O based on the oxides. After the
addition of Nb and Cr, the SFCs become larger than the Al-O
bonds, but smaller than the Ti-O bonds. Therefore, for the Ti-
48Al-2Nb-2Cr porous alloy, Al 2O3 ﬁlms, rather than continu-
ous TiO2 ﬁlms, tend to form during high-temperature oxidation.

4. Conclusions
In this study, the high-temperature oxidation resistance and
corrosion resistance of Ti-48Al porous alloys and Ti-48Al-2Nb-
2Cr porous alloys were investigated. Compared with the Ti-
48Al porous alloys, the Ti-48Al-2Nb-2Cr porous alloy exhib-
ited superior high-temperature oxidation resistance and corro-
sion resistance. These results were mainly attributed to the
synergistic effect of Nb and Cr on suppressing the growth of the
non-protective inner layer TiO 2 and promoting the formation of
the protective layer of a-Al2O3 simultaneously, which agrees
with the analyses of the bulk modulus, melting point, and
enthalpy of formation of oxides, as explained by ﬁrst-principles
calculations. The results of isothermal oxidation and vulcan-
ization indicate that the Ti-48Al-2Nb-2Cr porous alloy exhibits
potential application in high-temperature ﬂue gas ﬁltration.
