ABSTRACT 
In this work, Cu-1.4 wt%Ta-Zr alloys with different Zr content (0, 0.1, 0.2 and 0.5 wt%) were fabricated by 
mechanical alloying combined with spark plasm sintering. The effect of Zr addition on the microstructures, 
mechanical properties and thermal stability of Cu-Ta alloy were characterized. Cu-Ta alloys composes of ul -
trafine equiaxed grains along with the uniformly dispersion of semi-coherent Ta nanoclusters and incoherent Ta 
coarse particles. As Zr is introduced into Cu-Ta alloy, the Ta precipitates were effectively refined, especially for 
the reduction of Ta coarse particles. The significant refinement effect of the grains is realized when Zr content 
reaches 0.2 wt%, and high-proportioned nano-sized grains are facilitated to form, along with the activation of a 
lot of twins and the formation of intermetallic composed of Cu and Zr. The dislocation density and strength 
increase remarkably with the increase of Zr content, and the highest strength (499 MPa for yield strength and 
600 MPa for ultimate tensile strength) is achieved as the Zr content is up to 0.2 wt%. Compared with Cu-Ta alloy 
(382 MPa for yield strength and 560 MPa for ultimate tensile strength), the significant increment of 31% for the 
yield strength is successfully realized by Cu-Ta-0.2 wt%Zr alloy. Cu-Ta-0.2 wt%Zr alloys exhibit the excellent 
thermal stability, and the hardness keeps stable even the sample was annealed at 700 
◦
C for 300 h, showing 
superior microstructural stability in comparison with Cu-Ta alloy (~4.5% reduction of the hardness after 300 h 
at 700

resistance, which have great potential application value in the field of future fusion reactor. 
1. Introduction 
Dispersion strengthened copper (DS-Cu) alloys were demonstrated to 
be favorable candidates for high heat flux applications components in 
International Thermonuclear Energy Reactor (ITER) [ 1 ]. Compared 
with ITER (~10 MW/m
2

ward for heat sink materials to serve under higher heat load of 
Demonstration Reactor (DEMO) (~20 MW/m
2

fusion reactor [ 3 , 4 ]. Cu-Ta alloys have been proved to possess many 
excellent properties, such as high mechanical strength, exceptional 
thermal stability as well as low creep rates, which may have great po -
tential application in the field of DEMO. Nanocrystalline Cu-10 at.%Ta 
alloys prepared by cryogenic ball milling followed by equal channel 
angular extrusion have been studied in detail by Darling et al. [ 5 – 7 ]. 
Fine grains with nanoscale and extraordinary microstructural stability 
were successfully obtained, which demonstrates that coherent Ta clus -
ters with atomic scale play an important role in preventing grain 
boundary migration and grain coarsening [ 5 , 8 ]. However, a number of 
coarse Ta particles reaching several tens nanometers can also be 
observed in the microstructure [ 9 ], which is detrimental to the 
improvement of properties of the alloy owing to incoherent interface. 
Meanwhile, the exorbitant price caused by high Ta content and 
complicated preparing methods severely limit the wide applications of 
Cu-10 at.%Ta alloys. Thus, it is necessary to reduce Ta content and 
simplify the preparing process. At the same time, the coarse Ta particles 
need to be reduced as much as possible to further improve the

2. Experimental details 
2.1. Materials and preparation 
The detailed preparation process of the alloys is shown in Fig. 1 . Pure 
Cu powders (99.8%, average diameter of 48 μ m), Ta powders (99.9%, 
average diameter of 45 μ m) and Zr powders (99.9%, average diameter of 
48 μ m) were used as initial materials. All the powders were weighed in 
the glove box under argon atmosphere and placed in the stainless-steel 
tank containing stainless-steel balls. The mechanical alloying process 
of the mixed powders was realized by high energy ball milling lasted for 
40 h under 350 rpm with the ball-to-powder weight ratio of 10:1. The 
obtained mechanical alloyed powders of Cu-1.4 wt%Ta, Cu-1.4 wt%Ta- 
0.1 wt%Zr, Cu-1.4 wt%Ta-0.2 wt%Zr and Cu-1.4 wt%Ta-0.5 wt%Zr 
were named as CT-0Zr, CT-0.1Zr, CT-0.2Zr and CT-0.5Zr, respectively. 
Then, all the powders were consolidated by spark plasm sintering at 
850 
◦
C for 7 min under 50 MPa, followed with hot rolling at 850 
◦
C with 
a height reduction of 70%.

3.1.2. EBSD results 
In order to investigate the characteristics of grains and grain 
boundaries of the alloys, the grain morphologies of CT-0Zr, CT-0.1Zr, 
CT-0.2Zr and CT-0.5Zr were observed in the inverse pole figure (IPF) 
maps from EBSD results, as shown in Fig. 3 (a)-(d), respectively. It is 
distinctly identified that all the samples are composed of fine and 
equiaxed grains without obvious preferential orientation. The statistical 
results of the average grain size of all the alloys are summarized in Fig. 3 
(e). Compared with CT-0Zr (541 nm), the average grain size of CT-0.1Zr 
is 976 nm, which reveals that the grain size increases with the addition 
of 0.1 wt% Zr. As Zr content increases to 0.2 wt%, significant grain 
refinement is successfully achieved in CT-0.2Zr, exhibiting the best 
refinement effect with the average grain size of 220 nm. In addition, a 
large number of nano-sized grains ( < 100 nm) were obtained in CT-0.2Zr 
compared with other samples. It might ascribes to the severe work 
hardening of the alloy promoted by the addition of Zr, and the cell 
structures might transform into grain boundaries, further promoting the 
formation of nano-sized grains [ 17 ]. When the Zr content is up to 0.5 wt 
%, the grain refinement effect is obviously weakened, and the average 
grain size reaches 600 nm, which indicates the effect of grain refinement 
provided by Zr becomes weak when the content exceeds 0.2 wt%. This 
result is almost consistent with the dislocation density acquired from 
XRD patterns ( Fig. 2 (b)), which proves that high content of Zr is detri -
mental to hinder dislocation movement and inhibit grain coarsening. 
Fig. 4 (a)-(d) display the proportion of high angle grain boundaries 
(HGBs) and low angle grain boundaries (LGBs) in the alloys. As it shown, 
all the samples are featured by high-proportioned HGBs. Based on the 
orientation distribution function (ODF) diagrams ( Fig. 4 (e)), the related 
texture in the alloys can be calculated. CT-0Zr mainly contains (110) 〈
111 〉 texture and strong (110) 〈 100 〉 Goss texture. CT-0.1Zr is featured by 
(001) 〈 120 〉 texture and (114) < 352 > texture. As Zr content is up to 0.2 
wt%, the ODF diagram of CT-0.2Zr exhibits strong (001) < 100 > cubic 
texture. As Zr content further increases, (001) < 120 > texture and strong 
(001) 〈 110 〉 rotated cubic texture can be observed in CT-0.5Zr. The above 
results reveal that recrystallization occurred in all samples with different 
degrees, and high-proportioned HGBs with low grain boundary energy 
possibly improve the microstructural stability of the alloys. For CT- 
0.1Zr, the slight grain coarsening occurred may be ascribed to the de -
grees of recrystallization and grain growth are more complete facilitated 
by a large driving force during hot rolling, which is provided by more 
deformation energy storage induced by the addition of Zr compared 
with CT-0Zr ( Fig. 2 (b)).

3.2. Mechanical properties 
The hardness values of all the samples are illustrated in Fig. 9 (a). The 
hardness value of CT-0Zr is 176 HV, which shows more significant 
strengthening effect than many other DS-Cu alloy systems, including Cu- 
Y
2
O
3 
[ 13 , 20 ], Cu-Cr [ 21 , 22 ], Cu-Ta [ 23 ]. As 0.1 wt% Zr is added into 
CT-0Zr, the hardness decreases slightly (170 HV), which is probably due 
to the slight coarsening of grains. When the Zr content is up to 0.2 wt%, 
the highest hardness value is obtained for CT-0.2Zr (192 HV). As Zr 
content increase to 0.5 wt%, the hardness decreases to 174 HV for CT- 
0.5Zr, which indicates the strengthening effect for mechanical prop -
erty begin to weaken when Zr content exceeds 0.2 wt%, which is 
consistent with dislocation density ( Fig. 2 (b)) and EBSD results ( Fig. 3 ). 
Obviously, as Zr content exceeds 0.2 wt%, the inhibition effect from 
precipitates for dislocation movement and grain coarsening becomes 
weak, and dynamic recrystallization and grain growth induced by a 
large deformation during the hot rolling process in CT-0.5Zr are more 
complete than in CT-0.2Zr, which results in a more significant softening 
in CT-0.5Zr. Therefore, with the grain growth, the dislocation density 
decreases significantly, resulting in an obvious reduction of mechanical 
properties of alloys. 
Tensile testing was conducted to further investigate the tensile 
properties of the alloys, and the tensile engineering stress-strain curves 
are shown in Fig. 9 (b). The detailed values of yield strength (YS), ulti -
mate tensile strength (UTS) and elongation (EL) of the samples are listed 
in Table 1 , and the variation trend of YS, UTS and EL for the alloys with 
the variation of Zr content is exhibited in Fig. 9 (c). The YS and UTS of 
CT-0Zr are 382 MPa and 560 MPa, respectively. It is believed that fine 
and dispersed Ta nanoclusters with coherent interface play the impor -
tant role in strengthening the alloy [ 24 ]. Both YS and UTS increase with 
Zr content increasing and the most significant strengthening effect is 
achieved by CT-0.2Zr (499 MPa for YS and 600 MPa for UTS), and ~ 
31% increment of YS is successfully realized in comparison with CT-0Zr. 
It may ascribes the following reasons: (I) the highest dislocation density 
and the most significant refinement of grains are obtained, which 
contribute the highest dislocation strengthening and grain boundary 
strengthening effects. (II) Ta precipitates are significantly refined with 
more formation of semi-coherent Ta nanoclusters, which effectively 
activate the shearing strengthening mechanism dominated by semi- 
coherent precipitates [ 25 ]. (III) the formation of intermetallic and the 
activation of high-density twins also play the important role in 
enhancing the strength [ 26 ]. As Zr content further reaches to 0.5 wt%, 
due to the weakened inhibition effect from precipitates for dislocation 
movement and grain boundary migration as Zr content exceeds 0.2 wt%, 
with the process of dynamic recrystallization, the grains grow obviously, 
which results in the significant softening of CT-0.5Zr during the hot 
rolling process. With the reduction of dislocation density ( Fig. 2 (b)) and 
obvious grains coarsening ( Fig. 3 ) during the hot rolling process, the 
mechanical properties are significantly weakened, and finally the 
decreased strength for both YS (427 MPa) and UTS (569 MPa) for CT- 
0.5Zr are obtained.

4. Conclusion 
In this work, Cu-1.4 wt%Ta-Zr alloys with different Zr content (0, 
0.1, 0.2 and 0.5 wt%) were prepared by mechanical alloying and spark 
plasm sintering followed by hot rolling, and the microstructures, me -
chanical properties, softening resistance and thermal stability were 
investigated in detail. The main conclusions can be drawn as follows: 
(1) The introduction of Zr significantly increase the dislocation 
density of Cu-Ta alloy, and the dislocation density firstly in -
creases and then decreases with the increase of Zr content. 
(2) The grains are effectively refined as Zr content reaches 0.2%, and 
high-proportioned nano-sized grains are formed, along with the 
activation of a lot of twins and the formation of intermetallic 
composed of Cu and Zr. 
(3) The ultrafine equiaxed grains along with uniformly dispersed 
semi-coherent Ta nanoclusters and incoherent Ta coarse particles 
is obtained by Cu-Ta alloy. Ta precipitates are significantly 
refined with the introduction of Zr, especially for the effective 
reduction of Ta coarse particles. 
(4) The YS and UTS increase remarkably with the increase of Zr 
content, and the highest strength (499 MPa for YS and 600 MPa 
for UTS) is achieved as the Zr content reaches 0.2%, which ex -
hibits the superior strengthening effect than the counterpart of 
Cu-Ta alloy (399 MPa for YS and 572 MPa for UTS).
