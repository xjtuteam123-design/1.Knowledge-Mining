Properties of the Ir 85Nb15 Two-Phase Refractory Superalloys
with Nickel Additions
Y .F. GU, Y . YAMABE-MITARAI, Y . RO, T. YOKOKAWA, and H. HARADA
The effects of nickel content on the properties of the polycrystalline Ir 85Nb15 refractory superalloy
were studied. To examine the possibility of replacing Ir with Ni in Ir85-XNb15NiX , we varied the nickel
content from 0 to 50 at. pct. The yield strength of the Ir 75Nb15Ni10 alloy was 2150 MPa at room
temperature, much greater than that of the binary Ir 85Nb15 alloy, and 728 MPa at 1473 K, similar to
that of the binary alloy. The fracture mode of an Ir 85-XNb15NiX alloy changed from predominantly
intergranular in fcc and L1 2 two-phase Ir85Nb15 to transgranular in the two-phase Ir 75Nb15Ni10 alloy
and a mixture of intergranular and transgranular in the other tested alloys when the Ni content exceeded
20 pct. The Ni addition also increased the compression ductility for Ni content up to 50 at. pct. The
maximum compression ductility was approximately 13 pct, obtained from Ir 65Nb15Ni20.I r85-XNb15NiX
two-phase refractory superalloys with X # 10 were superior to the binary Ir 85Nb15 alloy as an ultra-
high-temperature structural material in terms of strength, fracture behavior, and density.

III. RESULTS
A. Melting Temperature and Specific Gravity of the Ni-
Containing Alloys
We determined the effect of the addition of Ni on the
melting points of the Ni-containing alloys by rapidly heating
specimens containing various Ni concentrations to a series
of temperatures, up to 2273 K, and then holding them at
each temperature for 30 minutes. We inferred the melting
point of some Ni-containing alloys (Table I) from the indica-
tion of incipient melting in the samples observed by the
SEM. The specific gravities of the Ni-containing alloys
(Table I) were determined either by weighing and measuring
the dimensions of cylindrical samples or by weighing the
samples first in air and then in a liquid of known density,
ethylene glycol.

The importance of L1 2 morphology and particle size on
the mechanical properties of Ni-based superalloys has been
studied extensively.[24,25] When replacing Ir with Ni in the
Ir85Nb15 alloy, the first thing that must be considered is
keeping the L1 2 precipitate coherently or semicoherently
embedded in the fcc matrix. According to our research, there
was an optimal amount of Ni to replace Ir in the Ir
85Nb15
alloy. When the Ni content was below this optimum,
Ir85-XNb15NiX would keep the fcc/L1 2 two-phase structure.
The morphology and size of the L12 precipitates were nearly
identical to those in the Ir 85Nb15 alloy. The strength of the
alloy increased greatly at room temperature because of solid-
solution hardening, precipitate hardening, and GB strength-
ening. At the same time, the compression ductility of the
alloy changed little because the alloy still fractured in the
transgranular cleavage. However, when Ni content was
above the optimum, the morphology and size of the L1
2
phase changed significantly. The microstructure of this alloy
consisted of fine structure, which was composed of the small
size fcc and L1
2, or even orthorhombic-phase grains. The
effects of solid-solution hardening, precipitate hardening,
and GB strengthening would be offset by the change in the
morphology and size of the L1

points between the different phase grains. This offset may
be the major reason for the dramatic decrease in the strength
