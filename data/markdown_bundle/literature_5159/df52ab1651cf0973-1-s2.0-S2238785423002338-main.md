abstract
Due to its excellent deformability and high tensile strength, Ti eZreNb alloy is promising in
the ﬁeld of aerospace and national defense. However, the relatively low stability of
microstructure with temperature variation restricts its further application in navigation
system. In this work, the microstructure evolution and mechanical properties of Ti eZreNb
alloy is investigated after an annealing treatment. When the annealing temperature in-
creases, we observe the microstructure experiences recovery, recrystallization, reduction
of dislocation density, reﬁnement of a
0-prime martensite phases and dissolution of u-
phases. These behaviors lead to the release of distortion energy, and thus the improvement
of microstructural stability of Ti eZreNb alloy. In the meantime, mechanical properties
maintain after the enhancement of microstructural stability. Therefore, the propose
annealing process can improve the microstructural stability and keep the good mechanical
properties of Ti eZreNb alloy in further aerospace application.

1. Introduction
TieZreNb alloys have been widely used in aerospace, national
defense and military ﬁelds due to their favorable mechanical
properties (e.g., the excellent deformability and high tensile
strength) [ 1e5]. With the rapid development of space explo-
ration, the requirement of navigation accuracy is in urgent
need. It is known that the navigation accuracy of gyroscope is
extremely sensitive to its dimensional variation, a variation
less than 1% variation can result in a navigation error up to
thousands of meters, which may lead to catastrophic failures
and even air crashes [ 6e8]. Nevertheless, the dimensional
variation was found to be severely affected by the micro-
structural stability [ 9e12]. Therefore, to evaluate the naviga-
tion accuracy of gyroscope, attentions should be paid to
survey the factors affecting microstructural stability of
TieZreNb alloys.
Among various factors, the addition of alloy elements (e.g.,
Ta, Al and Fe) on microstructure of Ti eZreNb alloys has been
extensively studied [ 13e17]. For example, Konopatsky et al.
[13] found that appropriate replacement of Nb by Ta in
TieZreNb-X alloys could improve b-phase stabilization and
the corresponding mechanical properties. Tahara et al. [ 14]
also observed that, with the increase of N content in
TieZreNb alloy, the critical stress for slip deformation and
inducing martensitic transformation increased. In addition,
Zhang et al. [ 15] reported that with the addition of Al the unit
cell volume of high-temperature Ti eZreNbeAl alloy
decreased. Besides, previous studies have also revealed that
appropriate addition of Ta into Ti eZreNb alloys can (I) in-
crease b-phase stabilization and suppress u-phase formation;
(II) lead to solid solution strengthening and grain reﬁnement
strengthening [ 18,19]. Thereafter, the stabilization of micro-
structure and improvement of corresponding mechanical
properties can be realized by the appropriate addition of alloy
elements.
In addition to alloy design, the heat treatment, which acts
as an effective method to tailor the microstructure of Ti alloys
and other alloys, is known as another process for improving
microstructural stability [ 20e25]. For instance, Inaekyan et al.
[20] studied the formation rate, precipitate size, fraction and
distribution of u-phase after aging process, and found that
due to u-phase, even short-time aging leaded to drastic
embrittlement of Ti eNbe
Zr alloy. Sheremetyev et al. [ 21] re-
ported that after relatively high temperature annealing, the
dissolution of u-phases was observed, and consequently
improved microstructural stability of Ti e22Nbe6Zr alloy. The
above studies suggest that one can obtain uniform distribu-
tion of precipitates, stabilized grain structure or reduction of
defects after appropriate heat treatment. Thereafter, the
microstructural stability of these alloys can be increased to
some extent. However, the coupling inﬂuences of above fac-
tors (i.e., grain structure, defects and precipitates) remain
unknown. The objective of this work is to investigate the
microstructural stability of Ti eZreNb alloy and to evaluate
the corresponding mechanical properties under such in-
ﬂuences. Meanwhile, the underlying stabilization mecha-
nisms will also be explored.
2. Materials and experimental procedures
In present study, the raw material was b commercial hot-
rolled Ti e35Zre10Nb alloy with a dimension of 120 mm /C2
50 mm /C2 4 mm, provided by Ningxia Orient Tantalum In-
dustry Co., Ltd., China. To survey the microstructure and
mechanical properties, the raw material was divided into
several pieces using wire electrode machine. Thereinto, four
groups of samples for annealing at 550, 600, 650 and 700
/C14 C,
respectively, were sealed in the evacuated and argon-reﬁlled
quartz tubes placed in a tube furnace, and then heated to in-
dividual temperatures for 1 h (with a temperature error of
±3
/C14 C). After annealing, samples were quenched in water at
room temperature to retain the annealed microstructure.
The phase and crystallinity of alloys were analyzed via the
D/max 2500 X-ray diffraction (XRD) with CuK a radiation at
45 kV and 40 mA. In order to detect the variation of defects, the
dislocation density was analyzed using X-ray diffraction (XRD)
and Williamson Hall (WH) technique [ 26]. Microstructures of
annealed samples were performed on vertical sections
(perpendicular to the rolling direction) by electron back-
scattered diffraction (EBSD) using ZEISS EVO MA10 scanning
electron microscopy. Before EBSD, the samples were electro-
polished for around 1 min using a mixture of 10 vol%
perchloric acid and 90 vol% methanol at 0
/C14 C. The EBSD
scanning step size was set as 2 mm for a detailed study. The
data were analyzed by channel 5. Further microstructural
characterizations were examined by Tecnai G2 F20 trans-
mission electron microscope (TEM), at an accelerating voltage
of 200 kV. TEM samples were mechanically polished using
sand paper until their thickness equaling 60 mm, and then
polished by twin-jet electro polishing with a solution of 6 vol%
perchloric acid and 35 vol% methanol in butanol, at
/C0 30 ~ /C0 20
/C14 C and a voltage of 15 e20 V. In addition, tensile
strengths and elongation for annealed samples were
measured using the CSS-4400 electronic universal testing
machine, with a loading rate of 1 mm/min at room tempera-
ture. To obtain reliable results in such tests, ﬁve specimens
were used and the average value was adopted.
3. Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructural stability
TieZreNb alloy contains many phases in which they are
different in the microstructural features. Many material pro-
cesses, such as phase transformation, grain growth and defect
evolution are readily lead to microstructural evolution. In
other words, the microstructural stability can be determined
by microstructural evolution. Hence, as an attempt to inves-
tigate microstructural stability of Ti eZreNb alloy in this
study, attentions will be paid to microstructural evolution.

various temperatures. It can be seen that b-phases as well as
a

amount of u phases presented at low annealing temperature
(550 /C14 C). Moreover, when the temperature increased from

Based on the above results and analysis, it can be deduced
that the increase of annealing temperature improves the
microstructural stability. A schematic diagram related to
microstructural stability of Ti eZreNb alloy is shown in Fig. 5,
which indicates that second phases, grains and defects evo-

this alloy. The corresponding mechanisms can be interpreted
from the following aspects. Firstly, the increase of annealing
temperature resulted in microstructure recovery and recrys-
tallization, which involved stabilization of grain structure. As
can be seen in Fig. 2 , the average grain size decreased signif-
icantly from 550
/C14 C to 700 /C14 C. Additionally, according to Fig. 4,
the reduction of dislocation tangles can be also observed.
These results leaded to the release of lattice distortion, and
thus favored the microstructural stability [ 30,31]. Secondly,
the increase of temperature decreased the size of a
0-prime
martensite (see Fig. 3 ). The a0-prime martensite phases were
precipitated from b matrix after cooling process. Due to the
formation of these a0-prime martensite phases, many phase
interfaces were generated, and further resulted in the trans-
formation of distortion energy (from rolling) and thermal en-
ergy (from annealing) into interface energy. With the
reﬁnement of a

ergy increased, and thereby eliminated more distortion en-
ergy [ 32]. Due to the coupling inﬂuences of reﬁnement of a
0-
prime martensite phases, structure recovery and reduction of
dislocations, the distortion energy of the alloy decreased.
Therefore, the increase of temperature leaded to the micro-
structural stability of this alloy.

be improved, while the mechanical properties should also be
maintained to guarantee structural features. In this section,

Fig. 6 displays the corresponding tensile strength and elon-

decrease (total decrease ~8.1%) with the increase of tempera-

recrystallization after annealing, during which the area fraction

the tensile strengths at 650 and 700

respectively, which were still much higher than those for
common Ti alloys (e.g., ~690e~790 MPa for TieZreNbeFe alloys
annealed at 550 e900

annealed at 600 /C14 C[ 34]). The relatively high tensile strengths at
650 and 700 /C14 C were associated with both solid solution
strengthening and second phase strengthening. On one hand,
the high alloying elements (Zr and Nb) leaded to lattice distor-
tion, thereby hindered the movement of dislocations and
reinforced this alloy [ 35,36]. On the other hand, uniform dis-
tribution of ﬁne a
0-prime martensite blocked slip system [ 37],
and thus resulted in dislocation concentration and strength-
ened the TieZreNb alloy.
In addition, it can be seen that the elongation ﬁrstly
increased and then decreased slightly. At low temperature
(550e600
/C14 C), the dislocation tangle and embrittlement of u
phase reduce the plastic deformation, and thus decreased the
elongation [34,35]. With the increase of temperature to 650
/C14 C,
the elongation reached a peak value of ~18.5%, which was
higher than those of common Ti alloys annealed at ~600
/C14 C
(e.g., 7% for Ti e26Nb [ 33], 13% for Ti e20Nbe6Zr [ 38] and 13%
for Ti e24Nbe2Zr alloys [ 39]). Reduction of dislocation tangle
and u-phase in this alloy were the main reason for the
improvement of elongation (see Figs. 1 and 4 ). Furthermore,
the slight decrease of elongation should be attributed to the
needle-shaped a0-prime martensite. It was reported that, the
dislocations were liable to concentrate in the cusp of a0-prime
martensite during tensile test, and thus leaded to the poor
elongation [20,28]. Hence, it was conceivable that the elonga-
tion was ﬁrst positively and then negatively related to the
increase of temperature. In a word, under the coupling in-
ﬂuences of structure recovery, recrystallization, reduction of
dislocation density, reﬁnement of a
0-prime martensite phases
and dissolution of u-phase, the current heat treatment overall
resulted in slight decrease of tensile strength while improve-
ment of elongation of Ti eZreNb alloy.
4. Conclusions
In the present work, the microstructure and mechanical
properties of Ti eZreNb alloy after annealing process were
investigated. The main conclusions were as follows.
(1) Through the annealing treatments, the grain size was
gradually reﬁned from ~63 mm at 550 /C14 C to ~23 mma t
700 /C14 C. Meanwhile, the amount of the recrystallized and
substructured grains also increased from 1.23% to 76.4%
at low temperature to 6.91% and 87.6% at high temper-
ature, respectively. The increase of annealing temper-
ature promoted structure recovery and recrystallizat-
ion, which were in favor of microstructural stability.
(2) With the increase of annealing temperature, widths of
a
0-prime martensite phases were ~100 nm at 550 /C14 C and
then continuously decreased to ~30 nm (needle shaped)
at 700 /C14 C. The addition of annealing was also in favor of
the decrease of dislocation density, which was
~2.74 /C2 10
13 m/C0 2 at 550 /C14 C while ~0.48 /C2 1013 m/C0 2 at
700 /C14 C. Under the coupling inﬂuences of these micro-
structure evolutions, Ti eZreNb alloy after annealing
was at a state of low distortion energy, and thereby the
microstructural stability was improved.
(3) It should be pointed out that, after current annealing
process, the tensile strength (1158 MPa) and elongation
(18.5%) of Ti eZreNb alloy were still much higher than
those for common Ti alloys. This indicates that both
improved microstructural stability and good mechani-
cal properties can be achieved in the same time.
