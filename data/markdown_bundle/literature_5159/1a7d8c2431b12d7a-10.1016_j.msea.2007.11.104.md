abstract
Transient liquid phase diffusion bonding of Ti–22Al–25Nb (at.%) alloy with Ti–15Cu–15Ni (wt.%) was
performed. The factors inﬂuencing the microstructure and strength of the joints were studied. A suitably
long holding time and high bonding temperature would beneﬁt the formation of joints with homogeneous
compositions and high strength. Nb element is the mainly controlling one to the formation of composition-
homogenous bonding zone. The bonding zone of joint with a rapid cooling technology after dwelling at
bonding temperature is composed of B2 phase. Slow cooling technology is beneﬁcial to improving the joint
strength. The tensile strength at room temperature of the joint under the joining conditions of bonding
temperature 970
◦C for 90 min with a slow cooling technology reaches up to 1018 MPa, which is equal to
93% of base material tensile strength, obviously higher than the joint strength of 931 MPa with a rapid
cooling technology.

1. Introduction
Ti2AlNb-based alloy, also called O-phase alloy, is one of the most
promising material for high-temperature structural applications
in many ﬁelds, such as aviation, aerospace, automobile, energy,
etc., because of its low density, high modulus and favorable high-
temperature properties [1,2]. Developing joining processes so as
to fabricate heat-resistant high-quality joined structures is neces-
sary to its extensive applications. Up to now, however, there has
been no report about the joining technologies for Ti
2AlNb-based
alloy. Transient liquid phase (TLP) diffusion bonding combines the
advantages of brazing and diffusion bonding, and thus can fulﬁll
joining under a lower temperature for obtaining joints with good
high-temperature properties [3–6]. Recently, TLP diffusion bond-
ing method has been applied to join intermetallic alloys, such as
NiAl-, TiAl- and Ti
3Al-based alloys [7,8], but was not reported to
be applied for the joining of O-phase alloy. In this work, the TLP
diffusion bonding method was employed to join O-phase alloy.

3. Results and discussions
3.1. Effects of bonding parameters on the joint microstructures
It is well known that enough holding time ( t
B) is necessary for
sufﬁcient element diffusion between interlayer and base materials
during TLP diffusion bonding under suitable bonding temperature
(T
B). The effects of tB on the microstructures of joints with bonding
temperature 970 ◦C are shown in Fig. 2 a, b, d and e. A symmetri-
cal bonding zone with a relatively homogenous microstructures by
both a sufﬁcient inter-diffusion between the interlayer and base
metal and then an isothermal solidiﬁcation was obtained as the
holding time was longer than 10 min at bonding temperature of
970
◦C, shown in Fig. 2 d and e. Reversely, when the holding time
was too short, a liquid layer of the melting interlayer at the center
of the bonding zone was retained at bonding temperature, which
was transformed to a brittle layer during cooling after stopping the
heating, shown in Fig. 2a and b. The retained layer is called retained
liquid phase layer. Similarly, when the bonding temperature was
not enough high such as 950
◦C, a brittle retained layer was also
found, as shown in Fig. 2c.
In addition, by comparing the composition distributions in
Fig. 2 a, d and e, it can be found that Nb element distribution is
most non-homogenous because of its relatively larger atom-size,
leading to low velocity diffusion from the base materials to the
bonding zone. Therefore, Nb element is the mainly controlling one
for obtaining a composition homogenous bonding zone.
3.2. Effects of bonding parameters on the strength and fractures
of joints
For the TLP diffusion bonding of Ti–22Al–25Nb O-phase alloy,
the effects of the bonding parameters, t
B and TB, on the tensile
strength of joints are shown in Figs. 3 and 4 . The results indicate
that the optimal holding time is 90 min when the bonding temper-
ature is 970
◦C, as shown in Fig. 3. The maximum room temperature
strength reaches up to 931 MPa under the optimal diffusion bond-
ing parameter, which is equal to 85% of base material strength.
Too short holding time disbeneﬁt to the joint strength because of
the formation of the brittle retained layer, and the tensile test-
ing fracture mainly occurred at the retained layer, as shown in
Fig. 5 a. The strength increases with the holding time because of
the more homogenous bonding zone and higher interfacial bond-
ing strength between base material and bonding zone, and hence
