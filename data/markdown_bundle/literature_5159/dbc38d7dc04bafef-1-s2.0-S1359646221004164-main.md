Short-range ordering governs brittleness and ductility in W-Ta solid 
solution: Insights from Pugh’s shear-to-bulk modulus ratio

a b s t r a c t 
In this work, the effect of short-range ordering on the intrinsic brittleness and ductility deduced from 
Pugh’s shear-to-bulk modulus (G/B) ratio in W-Ta solid solution is investigated by ﬁrst-principles cal- 
culation. The results show that the G/B value, an indicator of brittleness, decreases as a function of Ta 
concentration in W-Ta solid solution. The short-range ordered structure displays a ductile behavior in 
comparison to ideal solid solution and atomic segregated structure in terms of G/B value. This ductility 
of short-range ordered structure originates from the reduction of the number of partially covalent W-W 
bonds and a centrosymmetric screening of W-W interactions in W-Ta solid solution. Our ﬁndings sug- 
gest that short-range ordering should play a role in the solid solution softening and irradiation hardening 
phenomenon in W solid solution, and could be included in the future design of ductile W alloys.

Short-range ordering is generally considered as one of the 
strengthening mechanisms for solid solution, since the formation 
of an ordered atomic environment between solute and solvent 
atoms has a stronger electronic interaction than the solvent atoms 
themselves [ 1 , 2 ]. In principle, the short-range ordered atomic en- 
vironment could also change the brittleness/ductility of solid so- 
lution phase through modulating the solute-solvent electronic in- 
teraction. How short-range ordering inﬂuence the intrinsic brit- 
tleness/ductility of solid solution phase, especially those solvent 
phases with intrinsic brittleness, has yet to be well understood. 
Tungsten (W) was considered as an intrinsic brittle metal, due 
to its near non-existent ductility at room temperature and its high 
ductile-to-brittle transition temperature (DBTT) [3] . However, the 
widely structural application of tungsten and its alloys, i.e., plasma 
facing components in fusion reactors, kinetic energy penetrators, 
ﬁlaments, etc., require a certain degree of ductility to ensure the 
workability and formability, as well as the capability to resist the 
external load [4] . Pugh’s shear modulus to bulk modulus ratio, 
G/B, which is widely adopted to reﬂect the brittleness/ductility of 
materials, indicates tungsten possesses intrinsic brittleness as its 
high G/B value (0.52) [5] . This value is the second highest (Next 
to Iridium (0.61), which is known for its brittleness.) among com- 
mon metals with cubic lattice. The formation of W solid solution

Indeed, a solid-solution softening phenomenon has been observed 
for some W solid solutions, known as the “Re effect” [ 6 , 7 ]. Never- 
theless, the solid-solution softening phenomenon is depended on 
solute concentration and distribution [ 4 , 7 , 8 ]. This raises an inter- 
esting question that how atomic concentration, more importantly 
how local atomic arrangement, inﬂuence the intrinsic brittleness, 
and thus the macroscopic solid solution softening or strengthen- 
ing. 
In this work, the effect of short-range ordering on the ductil- 
ity of W-Ta solid solution was studied by means of ﬁrst-principles 
calculation. Ta is next to W on periodic table and forms solid solu- 
tion with W over the whole concentration range. W-Ta solid solu- 
tion is currently under intense development intending to improve 
the mechanical properties [9] . Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package 
(VASP) [10] was adopted to calculate the total energy, electronic 
structure and elastic properties of W-Ta system. Projector aug- 
mented wave (PAW) method [1 1] and the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof 
(PBE) exchange-correlation functional [12] was used in the calcula- 
tion. The k points were sampled using the Monkhorst-Pack method 
[13] . The majority of short-range ordered structures were gener- 
ated by ATAT software [14] . Those structures created from ATAT 
software are similar to the Monte-Carlo (MC) method in the liter- 
ature [ 15 , 16 ], which evolves towards the ordered structures with 
lower formation energy. In order to cover as more short-range or- 
dered structure as possible, we intentionally adopted 2 × 2 × 2,

Fig. 2 a shows the shear modulus to bulk modulus ratio, G/B, of 
various structures of W-Ta system. The corresponding shear and 
bulk modulus are shown in Fig. S4, S5 and S6 of supplemen- 
tal material. As expected, the addition of ductile Ta decreases the 
G/B value. Almost all short-range ordered structures possess a G/B 
lower than linear combination of G/B of W and Ta from Vegard’s 
law. The G/B of short-range ordered structures spans over a large 
range when the W:Ta close to 1:1. For example, the lowest G/B is 
only 0.25 for B2 W 50 Ta 50 , while that can be 0.43 for another struc- 
ture at the same concentration. The SQS structures, which mimic 
the perfectly disordered alloy (a statistical model for ideal disor- 
dered alloy, the derivation is given in supplemental material and in 
reference [19] ), have a middle G/B value among those short-range 
ordered structures. A parabolic curve 0.2(x −1) 2 + 0.33, where x is 
the atomic concentration of Ta, similar to the G/B trend of SQS, 
was obtained through the least-square ﬁtting of G/B of all struc- 
tures. The vertex of ﬁtted parabola is close to 10 0 at. % Ta, indicat- 
ing Ta could reduce the brittleness of W-Ta solid solution.

That is to say, the more order the structure, the lower the G/B. 
The ordered B2 structure ( α1 = −1 ) which have zero W-W bonds

lowest is 0.28). This apparently deviates the linear relationship be- 
tween G/B and the short-range ordering α1 .

It is shown that the evenly distribution of W and Ta atoms in BCC 
lattice could reduce the G/B, thus the brittleness. On the contrary, 
when the centrosymmetry breaks, the G/B increases, despite the 
percentage of W-W bonds is the same. This indicates that the brit- 
tleness/ductility depends not only on the strong W-W bonds, but 
also on the direction of W-W bonds. 
The inﬂuence of short-range ordering on the ductility of W-Ta 
solid solution found above was further conﬁrmed by the electronic 
structure of W-Ta solid solution (as shown in Fig. 4 a). The charge 
density of W on {1 10} surface shows that the charge concentrates 
along the < 111 > crystallographic direction, indicating directional 
W-W bonding. The density of states (DOS) and the corresponding 
Crystal Orbital Hamilton Populations (COHP) shows partial covalent 
bonding feature in terms of the formation of deep pseudo-bandgap 
in DOS and distinct bond/anti-bond states in COHP (as shown in 
Fig. 4 b). Some short-range ordered structures could reduce the 
number of W-W bonds and breaks the directional distribution of 
W-W bonds. For example, short-range ordered B2 W 50 Ta 50 displays 
an evenly distributed charge density around Ta atoms which screen 
the strong W-W interaction. The DOS and COHP shows the reduc- 
tion of bonding electrons below fermi energy, which alleviate the 
covalent contribution from directional W-W bonds. 
The counterintuitive ductility of short-range ordered structures 
in W-Ta system could shed lights on another phenomenon of some 
W alloys, solid solution softening. In recent years, ﬁrst-principles 
calculations have revealed that the electronic interaction between 
solute atoms and solvent W atoms determines the critical resolved 
shear stress of the ½ < 111 > screw dislocation, and results in a 
solid solution strengthening or softening effect [ 6 , 21 ]. The dis- 
tribution of Re near the dislocation core leads to the softening 
of W solid solution as a result of the screening of strong W-W 
bonds [7] . The short-range ordered structure with higher ductil- 
ity could reduce the critical resolved shear stress the ½ < 111 > 
screw dislocation and should play a role in solid solution soften- 
ing. The ductility results from short-range ordering can also be 
related to the lower degree of irradiation hardening for W-Re-Ta 
alloy than W-Re and W-Re-Os alloys [ 8 , 22 ]. The hardening of W- 
Re and W-Re-Os alloys after irradiation was found originate from 
atomic segregation of Re and Os. Ta could reduce the segregation 
of Re and shows a lower degree of irradiation hardening. This is 
consistent with our results that the short-range ordered structure 
is more ductile than the atomic segregated structure ( Fig. 3 and 
the calculated hardness shown in Fig. S7 of supplemental material). 
Even though the intrinsic brittleness and ductility deduced from 
G/B provides clues for understanding those experimental obser- 
vations, the short-range ordering may leave an Antiphase Bound- 
ary (APB) behind the tail of the moving dislocation, causing the 
so-called short-range ordering strengthening. This strengthening, 
much smaller compared to the critical resolved shear stress (CRSS) 
of perfectly random alloy, may increase the brittleness of solid so- 
lution to certain extent. Besides, G/B value is derived based on the

In summary, we found the short-range ordering plays a key role 
in determining the ductility of W-Ta solid solution. The short-range 
ordered structures which have smaller number of W-W bonds and 
lower centrosymmetry parameter than ideal solid solution (per- 
fectly random alloys) display a ductile feature, while those with 
larger number of W-W bonds and higher centrosymmetry param- 
eter shows a brittle behavior in comparison to ideal solid solution. 
Our ﬁndings not only help us to understand the brittle/ductile be- 
havior of W solid solution, but also shed lights on the solid solu- 
tion softening/strengthening of W alloys.
