Microstructure and Room-Temperature Mechanical
Properties of FeCrMoVTi x High-Entropy Alloys
Jun Guo, Xuefei Huang, and Weigang Huang
(Submitted June 18, 2016; in revised form April 16, 2017)
FeCrMoVTix (x values represent the molar ratio, where x = 0, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, and 2.0) high-entropy alloys
were prepared by a vacuum arc melting method. The effects of Ti element on the microstructure and room-
temperature mechanical properties of the as-cast FeCrMoVTi x alloys were investigated. The results show
that the prepared alloys exhibited typical dendritic microstructure and the size of the microstructure
became ﬁne with increasing Ti content. The FeCrMoV alloy exhibited a single body-centered cubic
structure (BCC1) and the alloys prepared with Ti element exhibited BCC1 + BCC2 mixed structure. The
new BCC2 phase is considered as (Fe, Ti)-rich phase and was distributed in the dendrite region. With the
increase of Ti content, the volume fraction of the BCC2 phase increased and its shape changed from a long
strip to a network. For the FeCrMoV alloy, the fracture strength, plastic strain, and hardness reached as
high as 2231 MPa, 28.2%, and 720 HV , respectively. The maximum hardness of 887 HV was obtained in
the FeCrMoVTi alloy. However, the fracture strength, yield stress, and plastic strain of the alloys decreased
continuously as Ti content increased. In the room-temperature compressive test, the alloys showed typical
brittle fracture characteristics.
Keywords cleavage facture, high-entropy alloy, mechanical prop-
erties, microstructure
1. Introduction
The development of high-entropy alloys (HEAs), proposed
by Yeh et al. (Ref 1), has broken the conventional alloy design
concept and opened a new promising new area of research.
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) usually contain ﬁve or more
principal elements in equal or near-equal atomic ratios, where
each elemental molar ratio is more than 5% but less than 35%
(Ref 2). Due to the high-entropy effect, HEAs tend to form
simple solid solution structures (bcc, fcc or bcc + fcc mixed
structure) instead of intermetallic compounds (Ref 3, 4).
Moreover, HEAs exhibit a series of particular properties, such
as high strength and hardness (Ref 5, 6), good ductility (Ref 7,
8) and toughness (Ref 9, 10), and good resistance to wear (Ref
11), oxidation (Ref 12-14), and corrosion (Ref 15). Because of
these numerous advantages, HEAs have attracted the attention
of many researchers and offer huge potential for use as
engineering materials.
The elements Al, Ni, Fe, Cr, Co, Cu, Mn, Mo and V are
commonly used in multi-component HEAs. These elements
obviously affect the microstructure and properties of the
resulting high-entropy alloys. Depending on the different
combination or different amounts of these elements, the HEAs
may consist of single FCC, BCC solid solution phase, or
FCC + BCC mixed phases. Additionally, an intermetallic
compound phase may also form in HEAs. Al element was
shown to have considerable effects on the structures and
properties of high-entropy alloys due to its large atomic radius.
Studies found that with increased Al content, the crystal
structure of Al xCoCrFeNi (Ref 16), Al xCoCrCuFeNi (Ref 17),
and Al xCrCuFeNi2 (Ref 18) high-entropy alloys transformed
from FCC to mixed FCC + BCC and single BCC and the
hardness increased obviously. Furthermore, it was reported that
the addition of Si, Mo, Nb, and Zr (Ref 19-22) alloying
elements to the AlCoCrFeNi alloy led to the formation of small
amount of intermetallic compounds in addition to the BCC
solid solution phase, which increases the strength of the alloy,
but decreases the plastic strain. As a light and corrosion-
resistant metal, Ti has an atomic radius and electronegativity
similar to that of Al. Previous work has reported the addition of
Ti into either FCC CoCrCuFeNiTi x and CoCrFeNiTi x alloys
(Ref 23, 24) or BCC AlTiCrFeNiCu and AlCoCrFeNiTi x alloys
(Ref 25, 26) leads to the formation of intermetallic laves phase
in the FCC or BCC solid solution phase. This notably increases
the compressive strength but decreases the plasticity of the
materials. However, there have been limited studies about the
effect of Ti on BCC base alloys. Therefore in the present work,
we selected Fe, Cr, Mo and V elements to design quaternary
BCC FeCrMoV alloy. The effects of Ti element on microstruc-
ture and properties of FeCrMoV alloy were systemically
investigated to further understand the effect of addition of
alloying element Ti on the microstructure and properties of
BCC high-entropy alloys.

3.1 X-ray Diffraction Analysis
Figure 1 shows the XRD patterns of as-cast FeCrMoVTi x
alloys and shows that the alloys were composed of body-
centered cubic (BCC) phases. Only a single BCC phase was
present in the FeCrMoV alloy, which is deﬁned as BCC1 phase
with a lattice parameter of 2.9846 A ˚ . When the content of Ti
content x was equal to or greater than 0.5, a second BCC phase,
deﬁned as BCC2, was identiﬁed in alloys, as shown in Fig. 1.
The alloys consist of a relatively simple BCC crystal structure
lacking complex structure, which can be ascribed to the high-
entropy effect (Ref 2). Figure 1(b) shows the enlarged XRD
peaks of (110)
BCC in a narrow angle range to better illustrate the
effect of Ti contents on the XRD peaks position. The peak
position of (110) BCC1 shifts obviously toward lower angle
direction with increased Ti content. This indicates that the
lattice parameter of BCC1 phase increases slightly due to the
presence of Ti atom with larger radius in the solid solution
matrix (Ref 26). The lattice parameters of the BCC1 and BCC2
phases and the atomic radius of the elements in FeCrMoVTi
x
alloys are listed in Tables 1 and 2, respectively. The lattice
parameters of BCC1 and BCC2 phases increased when Ti
content increased. This suggests that there was enrichment of
the Ti element with larger atomic radius in the BCC solid
solution phase, and this hypothesis was supported by the EDS
result shown in Table 6. In addition, the relative intensity of the
BCC2 phase diffraction peaks increased with the increase in Ti
content. This trend is consistent with the increase in the volume
fraction of the BCC2 phase.

4. Conclusions
In this study, the microstructure and mechanical properties
of as-cast FeCrMoVTi x (x: molar ratio, x = 0, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, and
2.0) HEAs were investigated. A single solid solution phase
with a body-centered cubic (BCC1) crystal structure formed in
the FeCrMoV alloy and the alloys with Ti contain BCC1 and
BCC2 mixed phases. The BCC2 phase is considered an (Fe,
Ti)-rich phase and distributes in the dendrite regions. The
addition of Ti promotes the reﬁnement of the dendritic
microstructure of the alloys. FeCrMoV alloy exhibits better
compressive strength r
max of 2231 MPa, plastic strain e of
28.2%, and the Vickers hardness of 720 HV . With increased Ti,
the compressive strength and plastic strain of FeCrMoVTi x
alloys decreased continuously. However, the FeCrMoVTi x
(x = 0.5, 1.0, and 1.5) alloys have higher hardness values than
that of the FeCrMoV alloy. The FeCrMoVTi alloy exhibited the
maximum hardness of 887 HV . The observed room-tempera-
ture compressive fracture pattern of the FeCrMoVTi
x alloys
was typical quasi-cleavage or cleavage fracture.
