ABSTRACT 
Improving the strength of face-centered cubic high entropy alloy, CoCrFeNi, has been a research focus in recent 
years. In this paper, to further enhance its strength for engineering applications, a series of CoCrFeNi-(Nb, Mo) 
high entropy alloys were prepared using vacuum arc-melting. The synergistic effect of alloying by Nb and Mo on 
microstructure and mechanical properties of CoCrFeNi-based high entropy alloy were investigated. The criterion 
Md was used to evaluate the phase evolution for current alloy system. Through synergistic alloying with Nb and 
Mo, a new kind of Laves phase with hexagonal close-packed structure was formed. The Laves phase is semi- 
coherent with the face-centered cubic matrix. Furthermore, through adjusting the ratio of Nb and Mo, fine 
lamellar structure Laves phase was obtained. The synergistic precipitation strengthening and solid-solution effect 
lead to high yield and fracture strength of 426 MPa and 714 MPa, maintaining high elongation of 17.4%. 
Fracture observation shows that the face-centered cubic matrix of the alloy system is ductile fracture, and the 
Laves phase is cleavage fracture. The results of this research would provide a basis for strengthening face- 
centered cubic high entropy alloy through synergistic alloying effect. 
1. Introduction 
In recent years, high entropy alloys (HEAs) have attracted more and 
more concentration among material and engineering community 
because they show various exceptional properties, such as extraordinary 
strength [ 1 – 3 ], excellent mechanical properties at high temperature 
[ 4 – 6 ], fatigue resistance, corrosion and oxidation resistance [ 7 – 10 ]. 
Among them, single-phase face-centered cubic (FCC) HEAs have high 
ductility, and the balance between yield strength and ductility is rela -
tively easy through proper grain refinement or precipitation strength -
ening [ 11 , 12 ]. For instance, CoCrFeNi HEA has relatively low yield 
strength, but quite high elongation at failure which is about 50%, 
indicating that it has good uniform deformation and high work hard -
ening ability. This feature makes it a good base for further 
strengthening. Therefore, a lot of efforts have been made to enhance the 
strength of FCC HEAs [ 13 – 29 ]. 
An effective way to solve the problem of the low strength is to adopt 
other strengthening mechanisms, such as grain refinement [ 12 – 14 ] and 
precipitation strengthening [ 22 – 29 ]. Otto et al. [ 14 ] put effort to 
improve the strength of FCC HEA, FeCrNiCoMn, through grain refine -
ment and found that the yield strength of the alloy under ambient 
temperature would increase from 200 to 350 MPa when the grain size 
was decreased from 144 to 4.4 μ m. It suggests that grain boundary 
hardening could be occasioned in the FCC HEA. Nevertheless, it seems 
that the hardening is not remarkable. To further enhance the strength 
without losing too much plastic stability, we should rely on other 
mechanisms, such as precipitation hardening, which would require a 
modification of the chemical composition of the alloy.

and solid solution strengthening, respectively. However, the synergistic 
effect of alloying by double elements, Nb and Mo, on the microstructure 
and mechanical properties of FCC CoCrFeNi HEA are not clear. Through 
the synergistic effect, it is expected to obtain high performance HEAs 
with both strength and plasticity. 
Therefore, the synergistic effect of Nb and Mo alloying on the 
microstructure evolution of CoCrFeNi HEA and the relationship between 
mechanical properties and microstructure will be studied for the first 
time. The average value of the energy level of d-orbital Md could be used 
to evaluate the phase evolution for current HEA system. Through syn -
ergistic alloying, a new kind of Nb and Mo-enriched hexagonal close- 
packed (HCP) structure Laves phase semi-coherent with matrix in two 
directions was formed in the FCC matrix. In addition, different ratio of 
Mo and Nb will lead to different lamellar thickness of Laves phase. Due 
to the combined precipitation strengthening and solid-solution effect, 
the yield strength and fracture strength of the CoCrFeNi HEA were raised 
up to 426 MPa and 714 MPa, respectively. Meanwhile, it maintained a 
high elongation of 17.4%. The investigation would provide a guide for 
strengthen the FCC HEAs through synergistic alloying and design new 
HEAs with desired properties.

3.3. Mechanical properties 
Room temperature tensile tests were carried out to investigate the 
synergistic effect of Nb and Mo alloying on mechanical properties of the 
as-cast HEAs. The tensile engineering stress-strain curves for the present 
NM00~NM40 HEAs with different contents of Nb and Mo are shown in 
Fig. 8 and their relative properties are presented in Table 2 . The results 
show that combined Nb and Mo alloying can improve the yield and 
fracture strength of CoCrFeNi based HEAs. The yield and fracture 
strength of NM00 are 160 and 368 MPa, respectively. By adding Mo 
alone, the yield and fracture strength of NM04 are 420 and 646 MPa, 
respectively. The yield and fracture strength of the alloys are further 
enhanced with the addition of Nb elements in NM13, NM22, and NM31. 
In contrast, the tensile elongation decreases with the increase of Nb 
content. In NM40, both the strength and elongation reduce because of 
the alloy embrittlement. 
3.4. Fracture morphologies 
For the purpose of further investigating the fracture mechanism as 
well as the relationship between the microstructure and mechanical 
properties, fracture morphologies of the HEAs were characterized using 
SEM as shown in Fig. 9 . As shown in Fig. 9 (a), the fracture morphology 
of the NM00 HEA exhibits large dimples suggesting that its fracture 
mechanism is typical ductile fracture with excellent plasticity. For 
NM04, the fracture mode is ductile-brittle mixed fracture with a few 
dimples as shown in Fig. 9 (b). In NM13, NM22, NM31, and NM40 HEAs, 
two different kinds of fracture modes were observed for the FCC phase 
and Laves phase as shown in Fig. 9 (c) – (f). The Laves phase exhibits a 
cleavage fracture while the FCC phase displays ductile fracture 
morphology by necking into sharp lines without generating dimples. 
There are obvious cracks at the phase boundaries, indicating that due to 
the stress concentration, the crack first nucleated in the FCC and Laves 
phase boundaries, and then propagated in the Laves phase. The distri -
bution of ductile and brittle fracture region is consistent with that of FCC 
and Laves phases. Therefore, the fracture of ductile and brittle fracture 
can be evaluated by statistics of FCC and Laves phases [ 15 ]. As shown in 
Fig. 4 , with the increase of Nb content, the fractions of Laves increase as 
well as the brittle fracture.
