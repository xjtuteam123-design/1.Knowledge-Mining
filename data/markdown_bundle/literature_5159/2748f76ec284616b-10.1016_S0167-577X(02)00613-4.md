Solid-solution hardening of Ir by Pt and Ni
Y . Yamabe-Mitarai*, H. Aoki 1
National Institute for Material Science (NIMS, previously NRIM), 1-2-1 Sengen, Tsukuba, Ibaraki 305-0047, Japan
Received 9 October 2001; received in revised form 22 January 2002; accepted 24 January 2002
Abstract
To understand the solid-solution hardening effect of Pt and Ni to Ir, compressive strength and compressive creep properties
were investigated for Ir – Pt, Ir – Ni, and Ir – Pt – Ni alloys. The solid-solution hardening effect of Ni was higher than that of Pt
because the atomic size difference was larger in the Ir – Ni alloy than in the Ir – Pt alloy.

1. Introduction
Iridium has a high melting temperature of 2720 K,
high strength at elevated temperature, and excellent
corrosion properties. Thus, it is expected to have a
wide range of high-temperature structural applica-
tions. We have investigated the compression strength
behavior and deformation mechanism of Ir-based
alloys with fcc and L1
2 two-phase coherent structures
[1]. These alloys showed excellent strength above
1473 K; however, they were very brittle and failed
by the intergranular fracture mode [2]. This suggests
that the workability of two-phase Ir-based alloys
needs to be improved.
In a previous study, an Ir – 0.3 wt.% W alloy
showed good tensile ductility at elevated temperatures
and high strain rate; further, it showed resistance to
oxidation [3]. Thus, the Ir – 0.3W alloy was chosen for
application in space power systems. To further
improve the ductility of the Ir – 0.3W alloy, minor
elements were added to the Ir – 0.3W alloy [4 – 7].I t
became clear that Th and Ce are effective to signifi-
cantly improve the tensile ductility and impact elon-
gation. On the other hand, additions of Al, Ni, Cr, and
Fe do not affect the tensile ductility. Si is a harmful
element for the ductility of Ir – 0.3W alloys. However,
neither the strength above 1473 K nor the creep
properties have been investigated for these alloys.
Among platinum-group metals, Pt is known as a
ductile element whose tensile elongation at room
temperature is about 30 – 40% [8]. The tensile creep
behavior of Pt-based alloys solid-solution hardened by
Ir or Rh has been investigated [9 – 11].P t - b a s e d
alloys, containing Ir or Rh up to 30 at.%, are very
ductile, and sheet or wire samples can be produced.
Thus, a tensile creep test using wire samples was
performed. The stress-rupture strength of these Pt-
based alloys and pure Pt has been investigated at a

Previous research has demonstrated that addition of
an element to Ir is effective to improve ductility.
Furthermore, addition of elements is expected to result
in a solid-solution hardening effect. To improve the
ductility and increase the high-temperature strength
and creep properties of Ir, fcc single-phase Ir alloys
containing additions of ductile Pt and Ni were chosen
in this study. Both Pt and Ni are fully miscible to Ir
although a miscibility gap was observed in the Ir – Pt
system at low temperature [15,16]. A simple solid-
solution hardening effect is expected for single-fcc Ir
at high temperature. Recently, Ro et al. [17] showed
that the ductility of Ir-based alloys containing Pt or Ni
improved drastically compared with that of pure Ir. In
the space field, an alloy that shows a creep lifetime of
over 160 h at 1773 K under 100 MPa as well as good
ductility can be used in the manufacture of some parts
of a space-plane [18]. In this paper, the high-temper-
ature strength and creep behavior of the Ir – Pt, Ir – Ni,
and Ir – Pt – Ni alloys were investigated. The solid-
solution hardening effect of Pt and Ni to Ir and the
possibilities of these alloys as high-temperature struc-
tural materials are discussed.
2. Experimental procedure
Seven kinds of Ir – Pt, Ir – Ni, and Ir – Pt – Ni alloys
were prepared as 15 g button ingots by arc melting in
an argon atmosphere. Cylindrical samples that were 3
mm in diameter and 6 mm high were cut from the
ingot samples and heat treated at a temperature
between 1723 and 1923 K for 72 h until homogen-
ized. The nominal composition and heat-treatment
conditions are summarized in Table 1 . The whole
heat-treatment sequence of heating and cooling was
carried out within a vacuum furnace with a tungsten
mesh heater to prevent damage of the heater by
induction of air above 100 K. The grain size of the
samples was observed by optical microscopy after
electrolytically etching in an ethyl alcohol solution of
5% HCl. The lattice parameter of heat-treated bulk
samples was investigated at room temperature by X-
ray diffractometry. The lattice parameter was esti-
mated from each peak between 40 j and 120 j. Then
the lattice parameters from each peak were plotted as a
function of cos
2h and determined by least squares
refinements. After the heat treatment, compression
tests were carried out for one sample with the strain
rate of 10

Shimazu-50kNI testing machine. Compression test

3. Results
3.1. Lattice parameter and grain size
The lattice parameter change by addition of Pt or
Ni is shown in Fig. 1 . The lattice parameters of pure
Ir, Pt, and Ni are 0.3839, 0.3923, and 0.3524 nm,
respectively. The lattice parameters of the Ir – Pt alloys
increased with increasing Pt concentration and were a
linear function of the composition. These values are
consistent with previous data determined by XRD
[15,19]. On the other hand, the lattice parameter of
the Ir – Ni alloys decreased with increasing Ni con-
centration. However, the relationship showed a
slightly nonlinear dependence at low Ni concentra-
tion. The lattice parameter of the Ir – Pr – Ni alloy was
almost equivalent to that of pure Ir.
The grain size of heated samples is summarized in
Table 1. The grain size did not change by addition of
Pt and was larger than 1 mm. On the other hand, by
addition of Ni above 50 at.%, the grain size became
small, about 500 Am. The grain size of Ir – Pt – Ni was
also about 500 Am.
3.2. Mechanical properties
The compressive strengths of these alloys are
shown with the tensile strength of the Ir – 70 at.% Pt
by Lupton et al. [10] in Fig. 2 . Here, the accurate
strain could not be measured because only one sample
was used for the compression test at several temper-
atures. Thus, the elastic limit is plotted in Fig. 2 . The
single compression test at 1723 K was also performed
for some samples. It was found that the elastic limit
obtained by the single compression test was slightly
higher than the results in Fig. 2 . This shows that pre-
deformed structure in the samples by previous com-
pression test was released during the heating before
the next compression test. When compared with the
Ir – 25Pt and Ir – 25Ni alloys or the Ir – 50Pt and Ir –
50Ni alloys, the alloys containing Ni showed higher
strength through the testing temperature. The strength

fore, it is clearly shown that Ni is the most effective
element to achieve a solid-solution hardening effect.
For Ir – Pt alloys, the strength of the Ir – 25Pt alloy was
higher than that of the Ir – 50Pt alloy at low temper-

strengths of the Ir – 75Pt and Ir – 75Ni alloys were also
equivalent to those of the Ir – 25Pt and Ir – 50Pt alloys.
This suggests that there is no significant difference in
solid-solution hardening effect among these alloys at
high temperature.

4. Discussion
The lattice parameter change as a function of
composition of Pt or Ni showed that the size misfit
of Ni is larger than that of Pt in Fig. 1. Thus, the solid-
solution hardening effect was higher in the alloy
containing Ni than that in the alloy containing Pt, as
shown in Figs. 2 and 3 . The melting temperatures of
Ni-containing alloys are only 100 K lower than those
of the alloy containing the same amount of Pt. Thus,
the solid-solution hardening effect was still effective
up to 2073 K by addition of an element with a lower
melting temperature, as shown in Fig. 2. On the other
hand, the solid-solution hardening effect of Pt was not
as significant at high temperatures above 0.75, as
shown in Fig. 3 . This is because the small size misfit
between Ir and Pt is not an obstacle for diffusion. At
low temperature, the difference between the Ir – 25Pt
and Ir – 50Pt alloys can be observed in Fig. 3 . The
reason for this difference is not clear at this point.
The Ir – 25Pt, Ir – 25Ni, Ir – 50Ni, and Ir – 25Pt –
25Ni alloys showed high compressive strength. How-
ever, under the creep condition, only Ir – 25Pt, Ir –
50Pt, and Ir – 25Ni alloys showed a low creep rate.
Other alloys, Ir – 25Pt – 25Ni and Ir – 50Ni, showed a
higher creep rate than pure Ir. This is because the
grain size of these two alloys was smaller than that of
pure Ir, as shown in Table 1. Small grain size causes a
grain slip at high temperature under the creep con-
dition. Thus, the creep properties become worse in
these alloys. Another reason is that the creep stress
was higher than the elastic limit of these alloys. For
Ir – 25Ni, Ir – 25Pt, and Ir – 50Pt alloys, the grain size
was large above 1 mm and almost equivalent to that of
pure Ir. Their melting temperature was around 2273
K. Thus, solid-solution hardening was still effective
for compression creep properties. The reason that the
Ir – 50Pt alloy showed better creep properties than the
Ir – 25Pt alloy is not clear despite the fact that the
strength of the Ir – 50Pt alloy was below 100 MPa and
lower than that of the Ir – 25Pt alloy.
For other Ir – Pt alloys, the tensile creep properties
were investigated [ 9–1 1 ]. The 10-h stress-rapture
strength at 1973 K for Pt – 30 at.% Ir was 5.3 MPa,
higher than that of pure Pt, which was 0.9 MPa at
1873 K. However, this is lower than that of pure Ir,
whose 10-h stress-rupture strength is 17 MPa at 2073
K. This shows that, even though pure Pt is solid-
solution hardened by Ir, the Pt-based alloys are still
weaker than pure Ir. Our alloy, the Ir-based alloy with
Pt up to 50 at.%, showed higher creep resistance than
pure Ir. This suggests that Ir-based alloys are more
promising for use as structural materials. However, for
the Ir alloys with an fcc and L1

properties by use of solid solution-hardening effect.
5. Conclusion
The solid-solution hardening effect of Pt and Ni to
Ir was investigated. For the compressive strength, the
effect of Ni was higher than that of Pt because the size
misfit between Ir and Ni was higher than that between
Ir and Pt. For compressive creep properties, Ir – 50Pt
and Ir – 25Ni were excellent among the tested samples
because they had a high melting temperature of
around 2273 K and their grain size did not change
by addition of Pt and Ni.
