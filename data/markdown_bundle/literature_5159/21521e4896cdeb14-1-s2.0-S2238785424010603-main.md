ABSTRACT 
Tungsten-rhenium alloys with high-temperature strength and creep resistance are widely used in the cutting- 
edge scientific and technological sectors. However, W-5Re alloy with Re content of 5 wt% shows brittleness 
at room temperature, limiting its aviation temperature measurement. This work provides W-5Re-xB
4
C (x =
0~2.0 wt%) tungsten-rhenium alloys with high compressive performance by introducing B
4
C ceramic phase and 
regulating the B
4
C content. The alloys are composed of W(Re) solid solution, W
2
B phase and a few W
2
C. When 
increasing B
4
C addition, the grain size of the W(Re) matrix decreases from about 200 μ m to 17 μ m in W-5Re-2B
4
C 
and the content of intermetallic compounds increase to be about 12% in W-5Re-0.5B
4
C and 58% in W-5Re-2B
4
C. 
The W-5Re-0.5 wt% B
4
C alloy exhibits a high yield strength of 1.19 GPa and compressive plasticity with a plastic 
strain of 42.50%, while the W-5Re-0.75B
4
C alloy has a high yield strength of 1.52 GPa and good compressive 
plasticity of 22.52%, obviously higher than the reported W-5Re alloys. Fine-grain strengthening from the 
refinement of W(Re) and second-phase strengthening from intermetallic compounds simultaneously enhance the 
alloys, while good deformability of W(Re) metallic phase contributes to the room-temperature plasticity. This 
study provides necessary data accumulation and theoretical support for the application of tungsten-rhenium 
alloys. 
1. Introduction 
Tungsten alloys including tungsten-rhenium alloys are widely used 
in modern nuclear technology, aerospace technology, temperature 
measurement, and other cutting-edge scientific and technological sec -
tors due to their excellent properties such as high melting point, high 
temperature strength, creep resistance, good thermal conductivity, and 
high resistivity [ 1 – 4 ]. In the tungsten-rhenium alloys, W-5Re alloy with 
Re content of 5 wt% are widely used as a positive electrode material for 
thermocouples in aviation temperature measurement and industrial 
practice [ 5 ]. However, the W-5Re alloy shows brittleness at room tem -
perature [ 6 ], making it difficult to meet the requirements of aviation 
temperature measurement. 
A large amount of research have been conducted to improve the 
mechanical properties of the tungsten-rhenium alloys [ 7 – 11 ]. For 
example, Luo et al. increased the tensile strength of the W – Re alloy from 
190 GPa to 220 GPa at 2000 
◦
C by adding 1 wt% ThO
2 
[ 7 ]. Xu et al. 
introduced 1 wt%Ta in W-2Re alloy by vacuum arc melting and 
increased the hardness from 5.85 GPa to 6.93 GPa [ 8 ]. These works 
focus on the strength and hardness enhancement but ignore the ductile 
improvement. Further, Luo et al. added SiC to improve the strength and 
ductility of the W-5Re alloy [ 5 ]. SiC hard particles were introduced to 
react with the matrix and refined grain size, then the W-5Re-1wt% SiC 
alloy had a high yield strength of 0.77 GPa and a good plasticity of 
32.87%, indicating a good strong plasticizing effect of the ceramic 
phase. 
B
4
C has high melting point, high hardness, and high-temperature 
stability, which is usually used as ceramic reinforced phase in alloys 
[ 12 – 14 ]. In this work, we prepare high-performance W-5Re-xB
4
C alloys 
by regulating B
4
C content. The effect of B
4
C addition on the micro -
structure and mechanical properties of the W-5Re alloys is studied. 
Through change B
4
C addition to tune microstructure, the W-5Re-0.5 wt 
% B
4
C alloy exhibits a high yield strength of 1.19 GPa and compressive 
plasticity with a plastic strain of 42.50%, while the W-5Re-0.75B
4
C alloy 
has a high yield strength of 1.52 GPa and good compressive plasticity of 
22.52%.

4. Conclusion 
This work provides B
4
C enhanced tungsten-rhenium alloys. The ef -
fect of B
4
C addition on the microstructure and mechanical properties of 
the W-5Re-xB
4
C alloys are systematically investigated. The W-5Re-xB
4
C 
alloys contain W(Re) matrix phase and W
2
B and W
2
C intermetallics 
compounds. Increasing B
4
C addition, the grain size of W(Re) decreases, 
the content of intermetallic compounds increase, and the mechanical 
properties increase accordingly. The W-5Re-0.5B
4
C alloy shows the 
highest compressive strength of 1.99 GPa and the best plasticity of 
42.50%, while the W-5Re-0.75B
4
C alloy has a high yield strength of 
1.52 GPa and good plasticity of 22.52%. The high strength arises pri -
marily from fine-grain strengthening caused by refining W(Re) and 
second-phase strengthening from intermetallic compounds. The plas -
ticity is mainly attributable to the good deformability of the W(Re) 
metallic phase.
