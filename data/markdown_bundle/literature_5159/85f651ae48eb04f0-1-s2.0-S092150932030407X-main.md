ABSTRACT 
This study aims to understand the thermal stability and annealing hardening behavior of nanocrystalline Ni(Mo) 
alloys at the Mo solubility limit. The microstructural and microhardness evolution of these alloys is investigated 
at different annealing temperatures. A rather stagnant grain growth, along with the gradual crystallization of 
amorphous nanostructures, in the Ni(Mo) alloys has been observed at high temperatures. The Ni(Mo) alloys with 
Mo content above the solubility limit exhibit an abnormal secondary hardening behavior at high temperatures, 
accompanied by nanosized MoNi precipitates. It demonstrates that the residual amorphous nanostructures play 
an important role in the thermal stability of nanocrystalline alloys. It also implies that the addition of the solute 
up to a solubility limit in the nanocrystalline alloys can help achieve superior strength at high temperatures. 
1. Introduction 
Nanocrystalline materials are single or multi-phase polycrystals with 
grain sizes smaller than 100 nm [ 1 ]. Nanocrystalline materials exhibit 
several unique physical and chemical properties. The properties are 
generally superior to those of traditional coarse-grained materials; for 
example, the former exhibit higher creep resistance [ 2 ], higher strength 
[ 3 , 4 ], higher wear resistance [ 5 ], superplasticity [ 6 ], and higher catal -
ysis [ 7 ]. As promising structural materials, nanocrystalline metals and 
alloys have various applications in modern society. 
Considerable attention has been paid in recent decades to investigate 
the strength of nanocrystalline alloys upon annealing. Grain sizes and 
grain boundaries (GBs) are the major microstructural features influ -
encing the strength of nanocrystalline alloys [ 8 ]. However, the thermal 
stability of nanocrystalline materials is generally low due to the large 
volume fraction (up to 49%) of high-energy GBs [ 9 , 10 ]. Nanocrystalline 
materials tend to undergo rapid grain growth to eliminate GBs even at 
room temperature (RT) [ 11 ]. Introducing alloying elements into nano -
crystalline materials is a promising approach to suppress grain growth 
and thereby improve their thermal stabilities [ 12 ]. Furthermore, con -
trolling the grain size at the nanoscale through alloying could signifi -
cantly increase the mechanical strength of the materials because of the 
increased number of GBs that act as obstacles to sliding dislocations. 
Recently, increasing the GB stability of nanocrystalline metals, through 
GB relaxation and segregation, was found to be an effective approach to 
enhance strength [ 13 ]. 
Nickle – molybdenum [Ni(Mo)] alloys have attracted considerable 
interest because of their exceptional mechanical and electrocatalytic 
properties [ 14 , 15 ]. It is highly desirable to develop new methods for the 
synthesis of these alloys and to improve their properties. It is difficult to 
produce Ni(Mo) alloys using metallurgical methods due to the high 
melting point of molybdenum and the oxidation of Ni and Mo during 
solidification [ 16 ]. Electrodeposition is a convenient and simple manner 
to produce Ni(Mo) alloys with considerable compositions at low 
temperatures. 
For the present work, nanocrystalline Ni(Mo) alloys with solute Mo 
content around the solubility limit (13 – 17 at.%) were fabricated by 
electrodeposition. The microstructural and microhardness evolution of 
the Ni(Mo) alloys with different Mo content have been investigated 
comprehensively to reveal the thermal stability and annealing hard -
ening behavior of the alloys at different annealing temperatures. This 
work aims to explore the influence of amorphous nanostructures and 
precipitates on the thermal stability and strength of nanocrystalline 
alloys.

4. Discussion 
4.1. Alloying effects on the thermal stability of nanocrystalline Ni(Mo) 
alloys 
From a microstructure perspective, alloying Ni with Mo significantly 
enhances the thermal stability of nanocrystalline alloys. The thermal- 
induced grain growth behavior at elevated temperatures is a serious 
challenge in nanocrystalline materials, owing to the excess Gibbs energy 
associated with the large volume fraction of GBs. However, the addition 
of solute atoms can stabilize the nanostructure against grain growth 
[ 23 ]. The thermal stability of nanocrystalline pure Ni or Ni-based alloys 
has been investigated in many pieces of literature. The nanocrystalline 
Ni with an initial grain size of approximately 10 nm was reported to be 
already thermally unstable at 80 
�
C [ 24 ]. However, the nanocrystalline 
Ni
0.56
Cu
0.44 
alloys did not exhibit evident thermal-induced grain growth 
until the annealing temperature reached 350 
�
C [ 25 ]. Similarly, the 
abrupt grain growth was observed at 375 
�
C in the electrodeposited 
nanocrystalline Ni(Fe) alloys [ 26 ]. As shown in Fig. 2 b, the very fine 
nano-grains in the Ni(Mo) alloys exhibit limited growth at annealing 
temperatures up to 450 
�
C in this work, which is similar to Ni(W) alloys 
[ 27 ]. As we know, the GB diffusion plays a key role in the mechanism of 
the thermally-induced grain growth in nanomaterials. Previous research 
has revealed that there is no evident difference in the activation energy 
between grain growth and GB diffusion [ 28 ]. If the atomic diffusion 
along GBs is inhibited, the grains in most nanomaterials will become 
considerably more stable. Introducing additional alloying elements into 
nanomaterials can increase the activation energy for GB motion through 
Zener drag of the solutes or increase the stability of the GBs through 
solute segregation [ 12 , 29 ]. Because the Gibbs energy at the GB triple 
junctions is higher than that at the centers of GB regions, the Mo atoms 
tend to enrich at the GB triple junctions to lower the Gibbs energy of the 
system at elevated temperatures [ 30 , 31 ]. The Mo enriching at the triple 
junctions can further inhibit grain growth by pinning effects [ 32 ]. 
Therefore, very stagnant grain growth can occur in nanocrystalline Ni 
(Mo) alloys at elevated temperatures, compared with pure nano -
crystalline Ni. 
Furthermore, the alloying effect of Mo in the Ni matrix, through 
electrodeposition, can also contribute to several amorphous nano -
structures around nano-grains, as exhibited in Fig. 4 a and b. The GB 
wetting by the amorphous phase can occur because an interface between 
a crystalline phase and an amorphous phase has lower energy than an 
interface between two crystalline phases [ 33 ]. Our recent research (not 
published) also confirmed that the amorphous Mo-rich phase can wet

4.2. Mechanisms of annealing strengthening in nanocrystalline Ni(Mo) 
alloys 
The microhardness of the as-deposited nanocrystalline Ni(Mo) alloys 
exhibits a nonlinear relationship with the increase of Mo content. The 
grain size of the as-deposited Ni(Mo) alloys decreases with the increase 
in Mo content (see section 3.1 ). Compared with Ni
0.87
Mo
0.13 
alloys, the 
increasing microhardness of the as-deposited Ni
0.86
Mo
0.14 
alloys is 
caused by fine-grain strengthening, following the Hall – Petch relation -
ship [ 36 , 37 ]. The strengthening mechanism is based on dislocation 
pileups at GBs, which can hinder dislocation motion. Furthermore, it is 
worth noting that the enhanced solid-solution strengthening in nano -
crystalline alloys can significantly increase the hardness compared with 
coarse-grained alloys apart from grain-size effect [ 38 ]. However, as the 
Mo content continues to increase, a softening behavior is observed in the 
as-deposited Ni
0.83
Mo
0.17 
alloys. This deviation from the Hall – Petch 
relation has been reported in several nanocrystalline materials [ 39 , 40 ]. 
The nano-grains are believed to refine to a critical strength size as the Mo 
content continues to increase, where the nano-grains are perfect without 
dislocations. Below the critical strength size, the plastic deformation 
mechanism transforms from a dislocation slip to a GB mediated process, 
including GB migration, GB sliding, or grain rotation [ 41 – 43 ]. As the 
annealing temperature increases (above 450 
�
C), the stable nano-grains 
start to grow significantly and break through the critical grain size of 
softening (see Fig. 2 a and b); hence, the microhardness of annealed Ni 
(Mo) alloys exhibits a direct positive correlation with increase in Mo 
content. 
In regime I, the evident hardening behavior is independent of fine 
grains because the change in grain size is not significant during 
annealing (see Fig. 2 ). The annealing strengthening behavior in nano- 
grained alloys has been attributed to the GB stabilization, i . e ., the GB 
relaxation and the Mo segregation at GBs. According to Refs. [ 13 ], the 
plastic deformation of nanocrystalline alloys is strongly related to 
GB-mediated processes such as GB migration and grain rotation, which 
can be suppressed by the GB stabilization. The GB stabilization will 
change the dominant plastic deformation from the GB-mediated process 
to the generation of extended partial dislocations. However, the influ -
ence of amorphous nanostructures in nanocrystalline materials on 
annealing strengthening has not been mentioned. The dispersed amor -
phous nanostructures in the nano-grained alloys also can contribute to 
the GB relaxation and segregation of solute atoms (see section 4.1 ). The 
GB segregation of Mo atoms can increase the GB sites acting as nucle -
ation sites, pinning points, and absorption sites for dislocation [ 44 ]. 
Moreover, the storage of excessive free volume in the amorphous 
nanostructures can be released through crystallization upon annealing, 
which can strengthen the bonds between atoms and increase the 
microhardness [ 35 ].

5. Conclusions 
The nanocrystalline Ni(Mo) alloys with Mo content around the sol -
ubility limit in Ni were successfully fabricated by electrodeposition. 
Alloying Ni with Mo can significantly enhance the thermal stability of 
nanocrystalline at elevated temperatures. The Ni(Mo) alloys only exhibit 
one hardening behavior with the stagnant grain growth at annealing 
temperatures up to 500 
�
C because of GB stabilization caused by Mo 
segregations at GBs and crystallization of amorphous nanostructures. 
The Ni(Mo) alloys with Mo content higher than 14 at.% show an 
abnormal secondary hardening behavior at the annealing temperature 
of 700 
�
C. The nanosized MoNi precipitates dispersed in the matrix have 
been confirmed to be responsible for the secondary hardening due to the 
GB stabilization by Zener pinning effect at high temperatures. Finally, in 
conclusion, the stabilization of GBs can be achieved by the crystalliza -
tion of amorphous nanostructures in the nanocrystalline materials 
through GB segregation and relaxation, and the strength of the nano -
crystalline materials can be improved by the supersaturated solid solu -
tion at high temperatures through nanoprecipitation.
