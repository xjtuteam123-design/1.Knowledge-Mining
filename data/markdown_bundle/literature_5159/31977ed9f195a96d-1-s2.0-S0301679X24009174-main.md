1. Introduction
Wear is the process of continuous removal of surface material from 
moving component and is the inevitable result of friction. Wear is the 
most common type of failure of metallic materials, resulting in waste of 
material and energy [1 – 4] . Alloys can be used as engineered formable 
wear-resistant materials due to their high hardness, superior plasticity 
and easy processing [5 – 9] . Wear-resistant alloys are constantly being 
explored for use in a wide range of temperatures.
High entropy alloys (HEAs) [10 – 12] , possessing a design philosophy 
distinct from traditional alloys, can achieve high strength [13,14] , 
excellent high-temperature hardness [15,16] , and good oxidation 
resistance [17,18] through the "cocktail effect". These properties make 
them candidates for wear-resistant alloys over a wide temperature range 
[19 – 23] . Although some scholars have proposed theories regarding HEA 
design [24 – 26] , achieving excellent mechanical properties and wear 
resistance through compositional regulation remains a challenge.
Studies have elucidated that the integration of non-metallic ele -
ments, including C, Si, O, and B, into high entropy alloys can simulta -
neously improve their strength, hardness, and wear resistance [27,28] . 
Compared to other elements, Si alloying not only enhances the strength 
and toughness of high entropy alloys through interstitial solid solution 
effects and lattice distortion but also forms silicides with good wear 
resistance. Further, Si oxidation can reduce the oxidation rate of the 
HEAs, thereby curtailing material loss attributable to oxidative wear 
[29,30] . Gu et al. [31] reported that the mechanical properties of

Si results in the appearance of two BCC phases in the HEA with B2

diffusion rate and reaction rate of Si are faster compared to other sub -
strate elements [54] , and the kinetics of the Si-oxygen reaction may 
cause SiO ₂ to form preferentially in the early stages of high-temperature 
oxidation. Once SiO ₂ forms, its dense oxide film will prevent further 
oxygen diffusion into the HEA [29] , thereby limiting the oxidation of 
other elements in the initial stage.

for the changes in high temperature wear resistance at 800

2 
exhibits a higher melting point and 
greater thermal stability, endowing it with the ability to maintain a

tribo-layer formed by SiO

possesses a denser structure than that of Co, 
Fe and V oxides. On the worn surface of Si00 HEA, Fe

within the

rendering the surface tribo-layer denser and less susceptible to detach -

reduced accumulation of wear debris on the worn surface and improve 
wear resistance. On the worn surface of Si06 HEA, the tribo-layer is 
predominantly composed of SiO

propagation and interconnection of these cracks lead to the detachment 
of the tribo-layer from the worn surface.

of the Al

softening of the tribo-layer, resulting in more severe adhesive wear at

noticeably absent in the EDS mapping at 800 ℃ . Materials that possess 
higher hardness, strength, and toughness tend to exhibit greater resis -

of Si06 HEA, the primary component of the worn surface at high tem -

counterpart adheres to the softened oxides present on the worn surface, 
forming a transfer layer that prevents direct contact between the 
counterpart and the underlying HEAs during friction. This softening of 
the oxide layer also reduces shear strength, leading to a decrease in both 
friction coefficient and wear rate of the HEAs.

mismatch stress between the tribo-layer and the matrix, thereby alle -

contribute to the inhibited stress propagation and reduced stress transfer 
between the surface tribo-layer and the substrate.
4. Conclusion
This study investigates the effects of Si alloying on the microstruc -
ture, mechanical properties, and tribological performance of CoFe -
Ni
1.5
VZr
0.4
Si
x 
HEA, as well as their wear resistance mechanisms.
1. The Si alloying transforms the composition of the HEA from a 
mixture of FCC phase and Ni
7
Zr
2 
phase to a combination of FCC phase 
and silicides. The structure changes from a eutectic lamellar structure to 
a herringbone structure, eventually forming dendritic structures.
2. Si alloying significantly improves the high-temperature micro -
hardness of the HEAs. The addition of a small quantity of Si has the effect 
of enhancing both the plasticity and strength of the HEAs. However, as 
more Si is added, the strength of the HEA continues to increase while the 
plasticity decreases.
3. Si alloying does not significantly affect the friction coefficient of 
the HEAs, yet it markedly enhances the wear resistance of the HEAs. 
Below 400 ℃ , the wear rate first increases and then decreases with the 
increase of temperature, and the main wear mechanism is abrasive wear. 
Above 600 ℃ , the wear resistance of the HEAs shows improvement in 
tandem with rising temperatures, and the wear mechanisms are adhe -
sive wear and oxidative wear.
