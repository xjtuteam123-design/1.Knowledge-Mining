1. Introduction 
Refractory high entropy alloys (RHEAs) have been developed for 
about a decade from the use of all or part of the seven major elements Ta, 
Nb, Zr, Hf, W, Mo and Ti [ 1 – 6 ]. Among the RHEAs developed to date, the 
equiatomic TiZrNbTaMo RHEA has been proposed as a new candidate 
bio-metallic alloy due to its expected good biocompatibility and high 
strength [ 7 – 9 ]. However, the alloy exhibited no measurable tensile 
ductility and only limited compression ductility ( < 9%) at room tem -
perature (RT) in both the as-cast and as-annealed conditions [ 8 , 9 ]. This 
deficiency immediately excludes its application as a load-bearing 
implant alloy. To overcome this limitation, one strategy is to focus on 
reducing the valence electron concentration (VEC) through partial 
replacement of Ta, Mo and Nb with Ti and Zr [ 10 ]. This effort has led to 
the development of a Ti1.4Zr1.4Nb0.6Ta0.6Mo0.6 HEA ( δ , atomic 
mismatch , is 5.7%) with compression ductility being increased to ~32% 
at RT. Another strategy centres on reducing the δ of the TiZrNbTaMo 
RHEA ( δ = 5.5%) through partial or full replacement of Mo with Ti [ 10 ]. 
This is because Mo exhibits cytotoxicity at a high content and for this 
reason, Mo is typically less than 15 wt.% in biomedical Ti alloys [ 11 , 12 ]. 
This strategy resulted in Mo-lean Ti1.7ZrNbTaMo0.3 ( δ = 4.75%) and 
Ti1.5ZrNbTaMo0.5 ( δ = 4.98%) HEAs, both exhibiting ~30% 
compression ductility at RT. The exclusion of Mo from TiZrNbTaMo 
introduced the more biocompatible equiatomic TiZrNbTa medium en -
tropy alloy (MEA) with δ = 4.84%, which increased the compression 
ductility at RT from < 9% to ~48% [ 13 ]. Furthermore, the Ta content in 
this TiZrNbTa MEA (43.82 wt.%) exceeds the threshold of 40 wt.% for

This study aims to further explore the strength-ductility balance of 
these new MEAs in the as-cast state through reducing the value of δ for 
potential orthopaedic applications. At present, extra low interstitial 
(ELI) Ti – 6Al – 4V (wt.%) is the premier biomedical alloy, typically

their martensitic microstructure and surface defects. Therefore, they

overcome this latent risk, it requires that the base alloy in the as-cast

759 MPa, benchmarked against ELI Ti – 6Al – 4V [ 27 – 32 ]. At present, few 
highly biocompatible metallic alloys can meet this tensile ductility-yield 
strength expectation in the as-cast state [ 33 ].

3.3. Microsegregation 
Fig. 4 (a) shows a selected region of the alloy in the BSE mode for

melting point factor, the Miedema model [ 36 ] predicts that the mixing 
enthalpy ( Δ H

Nb and Ta, while Zr has a high positive Δ H

melting points, typical of RHEAs [ 8 , 38 , 49 – 52 ]. It is challenging to

melting-point elements. Such marked compositional inhomogeneity 
may affect the local oxide film nature as well as other properties. A 
practical approach to overcoming this drawback is to make the alloy into 
powder for AM.

related to the twin boundary density. Shear band traces, shown as black 
linear segments in Fig. 8 (c), appear more frequently in the grains than 
twin boundaries. Such fine lamella-structured micro-shear bands are 
observed in many grains, constrained by their grain boundaries. They 
can be regarded as a result of strain localization during significant 
plastic deformation. Similar micro-shear bands were observed in BCC 
alloys [ 82 ] and ductile HEAs [ 20 , 83 , 84 ].

In summary, the improved tensile ductility of the as-cast 
Ti35Zr15Nb25Ta25 MEA mainly arises from the reduced atomic 
mismatch ( δ ). The MEA exhibited a fine equiaxed grain structure, which 
is in favour of delivering a balanced combination of strength and 
ductility. During tensile deformation, the alloy displayed a marked 
reduction in HAGBs, accompanied by a noticeable multiplication of 
subgrain structures and LAGBs ( Fig. 8 (d)), indicative of the capability of 
undergoing significant plastic deformation. For example, the formation 
of fine lamellae-structured micro-shear bands, with their length being 
constrained by the GBs, can be regarded as a result of strain localization 
during significant plastic deformation. These combined effects enhanced 
the tensile ductility of the MEA, evidenced by the fine and severely 
distorted dimples on the fracture surfaces. 
4. Conclusions 
The following conclusions can be drawn from this experimental 
study on the as-cast Ti35Zr15Nb25Ta25 MEA. 
1) A new bio-applicable BCC Ti35Zr15Nb25Ta25 MEA has been 
designed by lowering the atomic mismatch of the equiatomic 
Ti25Zr25Nb25Ta25 MEA from 4.8% to 4.0%. It achieved tensile 
ductility of > 17% and yield strength of > 842 MPa in the as-cast 
state, above the respective requirements for mill-annealed medical- 
grade ELI Ti – 6Al – 4V and Ti – 6Al – 7Nb alloys. The MEA has a 
continuously connected Ta
2

5

content (45.74 wt.% > the critical 40 wt.%). The alloy has the po -
tential to be used as a micro-architectured implant alloy for additive 
manufacturing. 
2) The as-cast Ti35Zr15Nb25Ta25 MEA exhibited a single BCC phase 
with equiaxed grains in the size range of ~40 – 130 μ m. No clear 
texture was identified. However, the dendrites (trunks and arms) 
were markedly enriched with Nb and Ta while the inter-dendritic 
regions were noticeably enriched with Ti and Zr due to the clear 
difference in melting point and mixing enthalpy. 
3) The solidification grain boundaries (SGBs) in the as-cast MEA is 
mainly composed of high angle GBs with misorientation angles ( θ ) of
