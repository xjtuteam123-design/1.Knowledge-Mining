1. Introduction
The development of structural materials with both high strength and 
ductility across a wide temperature range is a critical requirement for 
advanced applications in aerospace, energy, and automotive industries. 
Conventional alloys, typically comprising a single principal element 
with minor alloying additions, frequently encounter a pronounced 
strength-ductility trade-off at elevated temperatures [ 1 – 4 ] due to 
enhanced dynamic recovery [ 5 , 6 ]. This inherent limitation highlights 
the urgent need for novel alloy design approaches to maintain robust 
mechanical performance under extreme conditions. Multi-principal 
element alloys (MPEAs), characterized by multiple principal elements 
in non-equiatomic proportions [ 7 , 8 ], offer exceptional mechanical 
properties across diverse environments [ 2 , 9 , 10 ].
Recent advancements have underscored the potential of precipitate 
modulation to enhance MPEA performance over broad temperature 
ranges [ 1 , 11 – 13 ]. For instance, in BCC-structured MPEAs [ 9 ], B2 phase, 
oxygen complexes [ 14 ], and local chemical ordering promote disloca -
tion multiplication by altering shear modes from planar slip to wavy slip 
and encouraging cross-slip. In FCC-structured MPEAs, coherent L1
2
-type 
multicomponent intermetallic nanoprecipitates promote multistage 
work-hardening by enhancing dislocation storage capacity and
