ABSTRACT
β-type titanium alloys have been extensively studied by researchers as the promising biomedical materials due to
their superior performance. Nb and Ta are candidate elements that can reduce the modulus and enhance the
strength of the titanium alloy while stabilizing theβ phase. It is crucial to study the diffusion phenomenon inβ-
TieNbeTa alloy for understanding the microstructure evolution in production process and developing new
alloys. In this work, the inter- and impurity diffusion coefficients in β-TieNbeTa alloy were determined using
diffusion couple technique combined with Whittle-Green and Hall methods. Based on thermodynamic descrip-
tion, the atomic mobilities in bcc phase in TieNbeTa system were obtained by assessing the experimental data
using CALPHAD (CALculation of PHAse Diagrams) method. In the optimizing procedure, the end member and
the binary description for TieTa and TieNb were reassessed. A self-consistent atomic mobility database for bcc
phase in TieNbeTa system was developed and examined by comparing the calculated diffusion coefficient with
the experimental results. Simulations of the diffusion couple were also implemented by DICTRA module in
Thermo_Calc software to evaluate the accuracy of the parameters.
1. Introduction
Titanium and its alloys have been widely applied in biomedical
implants for decades due to their excellent mechanical properties,
corrosion resistance and biocompatibility [1–5]. In the early stage, cp-
titanium and Ti6Al4V have been used in orthopaedic applications, in
spite of the fact that Ti6Al4V alloy was developed for aerospace ap-
plications. However, concerns on biotoxicity of Al and V serving as
long-term implantation have been raised [1,3,4,6]. In recent years, β-
type titanium alloys with body-centered cubic (bcc) structure have been
attracting increasing interest. β-type titanium alloys possess improved
wear resistance and fracture toughness compared with
+
alloys
while eliminating hazards such as implant loosening and bone resorp-
tion [3,4,6]. Studies have identified non-toxic elements such as Nb, Ta,
Zr and Sn as candidate elements that could reduce the modulus while
enhancing alloy strength [4,7,8]. Meanwhile, the shape memory effect
(SME) exists in β-type titanium alloy and its application in biomedical
industry is also being extensively studied [4,9]. Moreover, the addition
of Nb and Ta can stabilize the β-phase in titanium alloy [10,11] and
thus promising in development of novel low-modulus β-type titanium
alloy. The TieNbeTa system is attracting more and more attention and
several alloys related to this system have been developed [4,11–13].
Diffusion is a common but important phenomenon in materials
processing [14,15]. For example, it can be used to analyze segregation
during casting. During homogenization of the cast titanium alloy, the
knowledge of diffusion could helpful in making a heat treatment
schedule [16,17]. Ti, Nb and Ta are refractory metals with high melting
points. Power metallurgy (PM) method is an advanced method used to
produce titanium alloys, and yields significant advantages over tradi-
tional methods [4,18]. Diffusion information is critical to understand
element distribution during the sintering process. Studies have in-
dicated that diffusion also occurs in the shape recovery of shape
memory alloys, and kinetic models containing diffusion coefficients has
been established [19]. Moreover, it is also essential to determine the
thermodynamic stability of the alloy under long-term service condi-
tions. Therefore, it is necessary to fully understand the diffusion in-
formation in TieNbeTa system and render its assistance in develop-
ment of β-TieNbeTa biomedical alloys. So far, diffusion in β TieNb,
TieTa and NbeTa alloys has been intensively studied [20–22]. How-
ever, there is no report yet on the ternary TieNbeTa system.
For the past decades, the CALPHAD (CALculation of PHAse
Diagrams) method has been well developed as a powerful tool to un-
derstand and predict phase relations and microstructure evolution in
multicomponent alloys [15,23–25]. According to Andersson and gren

2. Experiments
Pure Ti, binary TieNb and TieTa alloys and Ternary TieNbeTa
alloys were produced from pure Ti (99.99 wt%), Nb (99.99 wt%) and Ta
(99.99 wt%) granules using arc melting furnace under an atmosphere of
argon. All the alloy compositions were selected in the bcc solid solution
area of TieZreTa system at 1373 K and 1473 K according to accepted
binary phase diagrams [35–37] (illustrated in Fig. 1).
The composition of these pure metals or alloys are listed inTable 1.
After six times of re-melting to attain homogeneity, the ingots were
annealed at 1473 K for 48 h to obtain microstructures with millimeter-
sized grain in order to minimize the effect of grain boundary diffusion.
The annealed ingots were cut into blocks with the size of 10

× 5

using Wire Electrical Discharge Machining (WEDM). All blocks
were ground and polished till mirror-like quality finish, then assembled
with appropriate pairs of blocks in Table 1 under vacuum at 1173 K for
3 h. The diffusion couples were sealed into quartz capsules filled with
pure argon and annealed for 48 h at 1373 K and 1473 K, respectively,
followed by quenching in ice water.

5. Conclusion
In this work, the inter- and impurity diffusion coefficients in bcc
TieNbeTa alloys at 1373 K and 1473 K were determined by diffusion
couple technique combined with Whittle-Green and Hall methods. The
atomic mobilities for bcc TieNbeTa system were investigated using
CALPHAD method. The end members for self- and impurity diffusion
were re-assessed using two-exp function. The TieTa and TieNb binary
systems were re-optimized due to different admissibility of experi-
mental data or different thermodynamic descriptions. On the basis of
the mobility parameters for unary and binary systems, the atomic
mobility parameters for bcc TieNbeTa were obtained by assessing the
experimental results in the present work using DICTRA module of
Thermo_Calc software. The composition-distance profiles and diffusion
paths for diffusion couples in the experiments were simulated and
compared with experimental results. The basic consistence between
experimental and predicted results demonstrates the effectiveness of
the parameters obtained in this work.
