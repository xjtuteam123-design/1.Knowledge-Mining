Ultraﬁne-grained nanocluster-strengthened alloys with unusually
high creep strength

The microstructure and mechanical properties of ultraﬁne-grained ferritic alloys containing nanoclusters (NCs) are investigated.
The NCs (diameter /C24 3 nm) as well as the grain size ( /C24 200 nm) remain stable at 1000 oC. Substantial Hall–Petch strengthening
occurs at room temperature. Surprisingly, the creep rate at 800 oC is up to a factor of 10 8 slower than that predicted for diﬀusional
creep. Possible reasons are the high NC coverage as well as Cr and W enrichment at the grain boundaries, and inhibition of self-
diﬀusion.

Ultraﬁne-grained (UFG) materials with grain
sizes of the order of 100 nm can be very strong at ambi-
ent temperatures [1]. Ultraﬁne grain sizes limit the
length of dislocation pile-ups and thus the magnitude
of stress concentrations at grain boundaries. As a conse-
quence, a high applied stress is required for slip trans-
mission to occur across grain boundaries (‘‘Hall–Petch
strengthening”). In iron, for example, the Hall–Petch
strengthening corresponding to a grain size of 200 nm
is 1340 MPa at room temperature [2].
At elevated temperatures above a homologous tem-
perature T/T
melting of /C24 0.4, ultraﬁne grain sizes usually
degrade the strength because the paths for diﬀusion of
atoms from grain boundaries in compression to those
in tension are short. Therefore, individual grains, and
thus the whole material, elongate gradually in the direc-
tion of an applied uniaxial stress, i.e. diﬀusional creep
occurs. Stress-induced diﬀusional mass transport occurs
also in ﬁne-grained superplastic deformation, which
allows forming at elevated temperatures and low ﬂow
stresses [3]. For example, the oxide dispersion-strength-
ened (ODS) alloy MA 6000 shows evidence for such
structural superplasticity [4]. MA 6000 has the nominal
composition Ni–15Cr–4.5Al–2.5Ti–2.0Ta–2.0Mo–
4.0W–0.01B–0.1Zr (wt.%)–1.1 Y
2O3 (vol.%). It contains
oxide particles such as Y 2O3 [4] and YAlO 3 [5] with
diameters of /C24 30 nm. In the hot-rolled condition, the
grain size of MA 6000 is /C24 260 nm, i.e. it is an UFG
material. At 1000 /C176C and a strain rate of /C24 0.5 s/C0 1 its ten-
sile elongation is /C24 300%, i.e. it is superplastic [4].
Mechanical alloying (MA) of iron alloys with Y 2O3
powder followed by consolidation can result in the for-
mation of nanoclusters (NCs) with unparalleled thermal
stability [6–8]. These NCs, with diameters of /C24 2–4 nm,
are dramatically smaller than typical oxide dispersion
particles and are far from the stoichiometric composi-
tion. Their formation is thought to involve binding be-
tween pre-existing vacancies and interstitial oxygen
together with the incorporation of Ti and Y [9]. The
MA processing not only results in NCs, but also in
ultraﬁne grains. Usually, ultraﬁne grains experience
rapid growth because the driving force for grain
boundary migration is proportional to the average grain
boundary curvature j, which is inversely proportional to

grain size of /C24 200 nm. This material condition will from

Compression specimens with a diameter of /C24 3m m
and length of /C24 5 mm were electrodischarge machined
and tested at diﬀerent temperatures in air. The testing
was performed under cross-head displacement control,
under extensometer displacement control with extensom-
eters near the specimen, or under constant applied load.
At ambient temperatures the UFG 14YWT alloy is ex-
tremely strong. Hoelzer et al. [8] reported a tensile yield
stress of /C24 1470 MPa at a strain rate of 10
/C0 3 s/C0 1. The com-
pressive yield stress of the present 14YWT alloy is
/C24 2040 MPa at this strain rate. Increasing the grain size
of 14YWT from 200 nm (UFG) to/C24 250 lm (LG) reduces
the room temperature yield stress by /C24 1200 MPa. This
reduction is consistent with the strength decrease of
/C24 1300 MPa predicted with Takaki et al.’s Hall–Petch fac-
tor K = 0.6 MPa m
1/2 for UFG iron [2], i.e. Hall–Petch
strengthening is an important, if not the decisive, mecha-
nism at ambient temperatures.
At intermediate temperatures of /C24 600 /C176C, the yield
stress of 14YWT drops distinctly [8]. The compressive
yield stress of the present alloy at 700 /C176C, 460 MPa, is
close to the previously measured tensile yield stress of
390 MPa [8]. Such strength drops at intermediate tem-
peratures are frequently observed in ODS alloys, such
as for example in PM 3030 [14]. They are due to a pro-
nounced reduction in the Hall–Petch strengthening
above /C24 650 /C176C (as seen by comparing the temperature
dependence of the yield stress for alloys with compara-
ble particle sizes and particle volume fractions, but dif-
ferent grain sizes) and a reduction in the direct particle
strengthening above /C24 700 /C176C (as seen by the drop in
the yield strength of a large grained ( /C24 1 mm grain size)
solid solution ODS alloy PM 1000 at /C24 700 /C176C) [14].
Since UFG 14YWT was annealed at 1000 /C176C prior to
testing and exhibits a very stable NC and grain size,
the distinct drop of its yield stress at /C24 600 /C176C cannot
be attributed to NC or grain growth. It is therefore rea-
sonable to assume that the decline of the Hall–Petch
strengthening mechanism at /C24 600 /C176C (without accom-
panying grain growth!) is a signiﬁcant factor responsible
for the observed strength drop. This decline may, for
example, be due to a thermally activated reduction in
the maximum stresses supported at the head of disloca-
tion pile-ups at grain boundaries.

What are the reasons for the low value of the mea-
sured diﬀusional creep rate? If the material forms the
oxygen–vacancy complexes proposed by Fu et al. [9],
i.e. if the concentration of individual vacancies is very
low, it is conceivable that the self-diﬀusion of iron is
inhibited. Also, if interfacial diﬀusion at the NC–matrix
interfaces is very slow, diﬀusionally accommodated slid-
ing of the grain boundaries would be slowed down, i.e.
the NCs would act as pegs that slow down grain bound-
ary sliding. Another potentially important issue is the
segregation of W and Cr at the grain boundaries of
14YWT [20]. W atoms are signiﬁcantly larger than Fe
atoms and this could contribute to slower grain bound-
ary diﬀusion. If W has this eﬀect it should be noted that
it would be unlikely to be of importance in materials
such as W-containing power plant steels, since these
are not UFG materials.
In the absence of diﬀusion data for 14YWT it is not
known whether the n /C24 1.5 regime for the UFG material
is due to rate control by the Coble or Nabarro–Herring
mechanism. It should also be pointed out that there are
complications in the interpretation of the creep proper-
ties of the LG material, since the NC population and
segregation of W or Cr at its grain boundaries may be
diﬀerent from that in the UFG material. Unfortunately,
because of the large grain size it would be diﬃcult to lo-
cate grain boundaries for examination by APT. Also,
although TEM studies of the dislocation structures in
the UFG materials have been carried out [12], the corre-
sponding information for the LG material is not avail-
able at this time.
In summary, 14YWT NC-strengthened alloys con-
tain an extremely high density of /C24 3 nm diameter NCs
with exceptional thermal stability. The NCs pin the
grain boundaries very eﬀectively, giving rise to an
UFG material with an unusually stable grain size. It is
therefore not surprising that very high ambient strength
values are observed. What is surprising is the remark-
ably high creep strength of the UFG material. Our ﬁnd-
ing is contrary to conventional wisdom which suggests
that UFG materials are weak and superplastic at ele-
vated temperatures. Possible reasons for the high creep
strength are the inhibition of Fe self-diﬀusion, as well
as the high coverage of the grain boundaries with NCs
together with Cr and W enrichment. If the mechanisms
governing the unusual creep strength of NC-strength-
ened materials can be exploited, the synthesis of poly-
crystalline materials with high-temperature strengths
closer to those of single crystals, but with the additional
beneﬁt of much higher ambient-temperature strength, is
conceivable.
