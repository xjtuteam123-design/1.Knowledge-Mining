ABSTRACT
Herein, we introduced a novel equiatomic MoNbTaTiSi refractory complex concentrated alloy (RCCA) with 
unprecedented ultra-high-temperature capabilities. Owing to its hypoeutectic structure, consisting of dominating 
tetragonal β -Me
5
Si
3 
and minor hexagonal γ -Me
5
Si
3 
phases, each holding specific orientation relationships with a 
proeutectic bcc phase, the alloy showed excellent softening resistance up to 0.74T
m 
with a record-high specific 
yield strength of 135 MPa*cm
3

◦

◦

The results of this study offer guidelines for achieving unparalleled performance in RCCAs, targeting for ap -
plications at extreme temperatures.
Refractory complex concentrated alloys (RCCAs) have emerged as a 
promising class of materials for next-generation high-temperature ap -
plications [ 1 – 4 ]. These alloys can form single-phase or multi-phase 
microstructures, each providing distinct advantages. Single-phase 
RCCAs with a body-centered cubic (bcc) lattice exhibit exceptional yet 
often contradictory properties, such as softening resistance up to 1600 
◦
C [ 5 – 8 ] or high tensile ductility down to -196 
◦
C [ 9 – 11 ]. In contrast, 
multi-phase RCCAs demonstrate superior specific strength (normalised 
to density), outperforming state-of-the-art Ni-based superalloys at 
comparable temperatures [ 12 – 14 ] while offering greater flexibility in 
performance tuning.
Most multi-phase RCCAs can be categorised into intermetallic- 
reinforced and ceramic-reinforced alloys. The intermetallic-reinforced 
RCCAs typically contain B2, Laves, Zr
x
Al
y
, and σ phases, delivering 
impressive specific strength, though their operational limit is con -
strained to 1000 – 1200 
◦
C due to rapid softening beyond this range 
[ 13 – 15 ]. In contrast, ceramic-reinforced RCCAs, particularly Si-alloyed 
variants, exhibit a compelling balance of mechanical properties and 
oxidation resistance [ 16 – 23 ]. However, their high-temperature strength 
often lags behind other ceramic-reinforced RCCAs [ 24 – 27 ], necessi -
tating further exploration of optimised compositions.
Most Si-alloyed RCCAs have been designed by simply adding Si [ 16 ,
17 , 19 , 28 – 32 ] or by varying metallic elements in a base Si-containing 
alloy [ 22 , 23 ], without intentionally targeting specific strengthening 
silicide phases. This aspect is critical, however, as evidenced by 
Nb-Si-based alloys [ 33 – 35 ]. Nearly all reported Si-alloyed RCCAs form 
the hexagonal γ -Me
5
Si
3 
phase [ 16 , 17 , 19 , 22 , 23 , 28 – 32 ], which demon -
strates limited high-temperature strength [ 33 , 36 ]. To enhance 
elevated-temperature performance, tetragonal α / β -Me
5
Si
3 
phases, typi -
cally stabilised by Mo and Ta additions, are preferable [ 37 – 41 ]. These 
elements, especially Mo, also impart strong solid solution hardening, 
retained up to 1500 
◦

5
Si
3 
formation [ 34 , 34 – 36 , 41 ], it remains essential for fracture toughness 
[ 34 , 43 ] and oxidation resistance [ 34 , 44 ]. Guided by these principles 
and ever-increasing demand for advanced high-temperature materials 
for structural applications in aviation, aerospace, and energy genera -
tion, we developed a novel equiatomic MoNbTaTiSi alloy that achieved 
a record specific yield strength of 135 MPa*cm
3
/g at 1200 
◦
C, surpassing 
all existing RCCAs, as well as demonstrated acceptable damage toler -
ance, superior phase stability and short-term oxidation resistance at 
1200 
◦
C.

essential to critically evaluate its potential use. During high-temperature 
operation, structural degradation due to phase instability often occurs, 
leading to severe consequences such as unexpected fracture caused by 
the precipitation of brittle phases [ 58 – 60 ]. The MoNbTaTiSi alloy 
showed superior tolerance to prolonged thermal impact, retaining its 
initial structure in terms of the phase composition, the volume fraction, 
morphology, and distribution of the corresponding phases after 100
