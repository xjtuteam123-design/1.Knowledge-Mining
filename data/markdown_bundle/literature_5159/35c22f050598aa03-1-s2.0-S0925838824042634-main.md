ABSTRACT
Lattice distortion, which is intended to increase the yield strengths of alloys, is central to alloy design. This study 
focuses on investigating the close connection between microstructure and mechanical properties of (CoNiV)
97
Al
3 
medium-entropy alloys (MEAs) in the as-cast and annealed states. The grain size and phase composition of the 
alloys were optimized by combining the large deformation cold-rolling and high temperature annealing treat -
ment, and the toughening mechanism of the alloys was evolved. After annealing at 900 and 950
◦
C, a small 
amount of diffuse B2 phase appears within the alloy, which is able to strengthen the alloy to obtain high yield 
strength. Since intermetallic compounds such as Ni3Al are extremely unstable, they will decompose during 
annealing at higher temperature (1000
◦
C), the alloy transforms into a single FCC phase. Therefore, the as-cast 
and annealed (CoNiV)
97
Al
3 
alloy exhibited excellent mechanical properties, including yield strengths of 
0.8 – 1.1 GPa, ultimate tensile strengths of 1.05 – 1.7 GPa, and an optimal elongation of 36.7 %. Combined with the 
mechanical properties and microstructure of the as-cast and annealed (CoNiV)
97
Al
3 
MEAs, the alloy strength -
ening was mainly attributed to the synergistic effect of dislocations, solid solution phases, and B2 brittle 
precipitated phases, while the good ductility was mainly due to the annealing twins. Thus, harmonizing multiple 
strength/toughness mechanisms can lead to a more optimal alloy strength – plasticity equilibrium point.
1. Introduction
In recent years, medium-entropy alloys (MEAs) and high-entropy 
alloys (HEAs) have become promising structural and functional mate -
rials due to their excellent mechanical properties, such as ultra-high 
tensile strengths [1 – 3] , excellent ductility [4 – 7] , and fracture tough -
ness [8 – 10] . MEAs and HEAs have attracted widespread attention in the 
field of materials science. Moreover, new alloy composition and struc -
tural designs and different heat treatment processes have gradually 
developed, yielding alloy systems such as CoCrNi [5,6,8,9] , CoNiV [4, 
11 – 14] , TiZrNbHf [15,16] , and TiZrNbTa [17 – 19] based alloys. The 
CoNiV MEA with FCC structure exhibits superior comprehensive per -
formance [12,14,20] . Its main advantages are its extremely high tensile 
and yield strengths and excellent ductility. It was found that this is due 
to the larger atomic radius of V, which leads to significant lattice 
distortion and a larger mismatch volume [21] . Luo et al. developed a 
CoNiV MEA with a GPa level of strength and ultra-high plasticity. 
Additionally, the CoNiV MEA has favorable hydrogen embrittlement 
resistance and corrosion resistance [22] . It may be widely used in spe -
cialty materials manufacturing, military industrial field, and petroleum 
exploration.
Previously, many researchers design high-performance alloys by 
adding trace alloying elements [23 – 25] , implementing multiple cold/ -
hot rolling [26] and heat treatments [27,28] , and adopting advanced 
preparation methods [28,29] . It is common to strengthen the mechan -
ical properties of CoNiV MEAs by doping with various elements. Adding 
Al to the CoNiV matrix with FCC structure can promote the precipitation 
of the L2
1 
Heusler phase, which has an ordered body-centered cubic 
(BCC) structure [20,30] . Nutor [31] believed that the distribution of FCC 
matrix phase and a small amount of B2 precipitated phase led to het -
erogeneous deformation during tensile loading process. Deformation 
constraints imposed by the B2 phase are counteracted by a certain 
dislocation accumulation on the structure. Therefore, the microstruc -
tures can effectively improve the mechanical properties of MEAs.

Doping with a moderate amount of Al in the CoNiV matrix alloy can 
effectively improve its mechanical properties [30] , and it can change the 
microstructure and phase composition of the CoNiV MEA, thus affecting 
its strength and ductility. In addition, different heat treatment processes 
can further improve the mechanical properties. Xiang et al. [32] studied 
the microstructures and mechanical properties of an as-cast and 
annealed TiZrNbTa-based HEA. The results showed that the yield 
strength of the TiZrNbTa HEA increased and then decreased with the 
increase in the annealing temperature.
In MEA and HEA systems, such as CoCrNi [33] , FeCoNi [34,35] , and 
especially the newly developed CoNiV MEA [21,36] , the strength and 
ductility are generally higher than those of polymetallic alloys. At pre -
sent, it is known that doping Al into the CoNiV MEA can effectively 
improve its mechanical properties. However, the comprehensive effects 
of the precipitated phases, such as Lomo – Cottrell (L – C) locks and 
annealing twins, on the strength and toughness mechanism of the MEAs 
have seldom been investigated. Therefore, in this study, the CoNiV MEA 
[37] with a FCC structure was selected as the matrix material, and its 
mechanical properties were improved by alloying, cold-rolling, and 
annealing treatment. The microstructure and mechanical properties of 
the Al-doped CoNiV MEA were systematically investigated after treat -
ment. The toughening mechanism through the joint action of the pre -
cipitation phases, layer dislocations, L – C locks, and annealing twins was 
also investigated.

3.1. Mechanical characterization

increases, the crystal structure within the metal can be rearranged to 
reduce or eliminate stress concentrations, allowing the residual stresses 
within the alloys to be evenly distributed. It can reduce the internal 
residual stresses, thus improving the plasticity of the material. The yield 
strength (YS) was above 1100 MPa for the sample annealed at 900

annealing at high temperatures increases the plasticity of alloys.
Fig. 2 b shows the corresponding true strain rate curves, which 
mainly showed three different stages. In the initial elastic deformation 
zone (stage I), the strain hardening rate (SHR) decreased rapidly. 
Entering the uniform plastic deformation zone (stage II) caused a gentle 
decrease of SHR, which delayed the plastic destabilization and promoted 
the enhancement of the plasticity. When the strength reached the ulti -
mate tensile value (stage III), the SHR gradually decreased and the alloy 
became extremely unstable internally, leading to fracture failure. The 
results showed that the samples treated at different annealing temper -
atures changed in multiple stages, and the maximum values of SHR for 
the sample annealed at 900
◦
C (~275 GPa) was higher than 950
◦
C 
(~160 GPa) and 1000
◦
C (~45 GPa) annealed alloys. The SHR of 1000
◦
C 
annealed alloy is the lowest in stage I. When the true strain was in the 
range of 5 % – 20 % (stage II), the SHR has a “ multiple-step ” decline. It 
suggests that multiple strengthening and toughening of the alloy occurs 
at this stage instead of dislocation-dominated deformation. In stage III, 
the SHR decreased with increasing strain, indicating that necking frac -
ture of the alloy occurred by tensile loading. Consequently, the multi- 
stage work-hardening rate over the strain range delayed the onset of 
the necking instability, resulting in a good balance of strength and 
ductility [38] . To further study the effect of the annealing temperature
