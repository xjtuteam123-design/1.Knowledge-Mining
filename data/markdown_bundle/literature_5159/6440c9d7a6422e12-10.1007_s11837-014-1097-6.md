High-temperature compressive properties of two TiC-added Mo-Si-B alloys
with nominal compositions of Mo-5Si-10B-7.5TiC (70Mo alloy) and Mo-6.7Si-
13.3B-7.5TiC (65Mo alloy) (at.%) were investigated. The alloys were composed
of four constituent phases: Mo solid solution (Mo
ss), Mo5SiB2, (Mo,Ti)C, and
(Mo,Ti)2C. The primary phases of the 70Mo and 65Mo alloys were Moss and T2,
respectively. The compressive deformability of the 65Mo alloy was signiﬁ-
cantly limited even at 1600 /C176 C because of the elongated, coarse primary T 2
phase, whereas the 70Mo alloy had good compressive deformability and a high
strength in the test-temperature range of 1000–1600 /C176 C; the peak stresses
were 1800 MPa at 1000 /C176 C, 1230 MPa at 1200 /C176 C, and 350 MPa at 1600 /C176 C. At
and above 1200 /C176 C, the peak stress values were more than double those of
Mo-6.7Si-7.9B, Ti-Zr-Mo, and Mo-Hf-C alloys. The plastic strain in the 70Mo
alloy at temperatures lower than the ductile–brittle transition temperature of
T
2 was generated by plastic deformation of not only Mo ss but also of (Mo,Ti)C
and (Mo,Ti) 2C. This work indicates that (Mo,Ti)C and (Mo,Ti) 2C play an
important role in determining the high-temperature strength and deformation
properties of TiC-added Mo-Si-B alloys.
INTRODUCTION
Mo-Si-B-based alloys are a leading candidate for
ultrahigh-temperature materials available above the
operating temperature of nickel-based superalloys
because of their high melting point, excellent high-
temperature (creep) strength, and moderate oxidation
resistance in the ultrahi gh-temperature range.
1–7
However, as these alloys have a higher density than
nickel-based superalloys2 and poor room-temperature
fracture toughness,7,8 their practical use is limited. To
improve the physical properties of Mo-Si-B-based
alloys, attempts have been made to include a fourth
element and control the microstructure.
9–16
We recently developed novel TiC-added Mo-Si-B
alloys17 using an arc-melting technique and found
that ﬁne microstructures of three- and four-compo-
nent eutectic phases such as Mo
ss +T 2 + TiC +
Mo2C, Moss +T 2 + TiC, and Moss +T 2 +M o2C with
a primary phase of Mo ss,T 2, or TiC depending on
the composition were created. The density reduced
to £9.0 g/cm3 as a result of the TiC addition, even
though the volume fraction of Mo ss was over 60%.
Alloys containing an elongated coarse T 2 primary
phase had a low deformability at 1400 /C176 C, whereas
alloys containing a Mo ss or TiC primary phase had
good deformability and a high strength of over
800 MPa at 1400 /C176 C along with a strain rate of
2.1 9 10/C04 s/C01. TiC-added, Mo-Si-B-based alloys
thus have a high potential to be used as ultrahigh-
temperature materials; however, most of the
mechanical properties are still unclear.
The purpose of this work was to study the com-
pressive properties of TiC-added Mo-Si-B alloys at
elevated temperatures. The temperature and
strain-rate dependence of the compressive strength
were investigated for two alloys developed in our
previous work,
17 and the properties are discussed in
comparison with those of a ternary Mo-Si-B alloy
reported by Alur et al.
18 and the heat-resistant
molybdenum alloys of Ti-Zr-Mo (TZM) and Mo-Hf-C
(MHC).

EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURE
The two TiC-added Mo-Si-B alloys had nominal
compositions of 70Mo-5Si-10B-7.5TiC (at.%) and
65Mo-6.7Si-13.3B-7.5TiC (at.%), respectively, and
correspond to the B
3 and A4 alloys of our previous
study.17 These alloys are referred to as 70Mo and
65Mo, respectively, throughout this article. The
70Mo alloy had a chemical composition of
70.65 ± 0.05 Mo, 4.8 ± 0.1 Si, 9.85 ± 0.05 B,
7.7 ± 0.1 Ti, and 6.95 ± 0.05 C, while that of the
65Mo alloy was 65.65 ± 0.05 Mo, 6.5 ± 0.01 Si,
12.95 ± 0.15 B, 7.7 ± 0.1 Ti, and 7.2 ± 0.01 C
(at.%). Both alloys were produced using a conven-
tional arc-melting technique in Ar atmosphere;
sample preparation details are given elsewhere.
17
The alloys were subjected to a heat treatment at
1800/C176 C for 24 h in Ar atmosphere. Microstructures
were observed using scanning electron microscopy
(SEM; JEOL JSM-7800F) by collecting backscat-
tered electron (BSE) images and transmission elec-
tron microscopy (TEM; JEOL JEM-ARM200F).
Thin foils for the TEM observation were prepared
using a focused ion beam (FIB) technique (JEOL
JIB-4600F) with a 30-kV Ga ion beam. Semiquan-
titative composition analyses of the constituent
phases were performed using energy-dispersive
x-ray spectroscopy (EDX) in TEM. Compression
specimens of about 2 9 2 9 4m m
3 in size were
electrodischarge machined from the center of the
alloy ingots after the heat treatment, and compres-
sion tests using an Instron 5982 machine were
conducted at elevated temperatures in a vacuum of
better than 10
/C03 Pa. Test temperatures were in the
range of 1000–1600 /C176 C, and the initial strain rate
was between 2.1 9 10/C05 s/C01 and 2.1 9 10/C01 s/C01. For
a comparison of the high-temperature strength, two
as-received molybdenum alloys of Mo-0.49Ti-
0.094Zr-0.037C (wt.%) (TZM) produced by H.C.
Starck Inc. and Mo-(1.0–1.3)Hf-(0.05–0.12)C (wt.%)
(MHC) produced by Plansee SE were employed.

CONCLUSION
In this study, the high-temperature compressive
properties of two TiC-added Mo-Si-B alloys with
nominal compositions of 70Mo-5Si-10B-7.5TiC
(70Mo) and 65Mo-6.7Si-13.3B-7.5TiC (65Mo) were
investigated, and the results were compared with
those of the Mo-6.1Si-7.9B ternary alloy reported by
Alur et al.
18 and the heat-resistant molybdenum
alloys TZM and MHC. TiC-added Mo-Si-B alloys
contain two carbides in addition to Mo
ss and Mo5SiB2:
(Mo,Ti)C with a NaCl structure and (Mo,Ti) 2C with
an orthorhombic structure. The compressive defor-
mability of the 65Mo alloy was signiﬁcantly limited
even at 1600 /C176 C, whereas the 70Mo alloy had good
compressive deformability over the test-temperature
range of 1000–1600 /C176 C. The high-temperature
strength of the 70Mo alloy was more than double
those of Mo-6.7Si-7.9B, TZM, and MHC at and above
1200/C176 C. The plastic strain of the 70Mo alloy at
temperatures lower than the DBTT of T
2 was shown
to be generated by plastic deformation of not only
Moss but also of (Mo,Ti)C and (Mo,Ti) 2C while
microcracking proceeds in T 2. Therefore, we con-
cluded that (Mo,Ti)C and (Mo,Ti) 2C play an impor-
tant role in determining the high-temperature
strength and deformation properties of TiC-added
Mo-Si-B alloys. The strain-rate sensitivity parameter
of the peak stress of the 70Mo alloy was approxi-
mately 0.05 at 1400 /C176 C in the strain-rate range
examined and 0.12 at 1500 /C176 C at strain rates of less
than 10
/C02 s/C01; the sensitivity parameter was similar
to that of MHC tested at 1400 /C176 C but considerably
different from that of the ternary Mo-6.7Si-7.9B.
