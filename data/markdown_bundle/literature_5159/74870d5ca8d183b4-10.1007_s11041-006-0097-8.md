A NEW BASE FOR CREATING
CASTABLE HIGH-TEMPERATURE SUPERALLOYS
E. N. Kablov, 1 O. A. Bazyleva, 1 and M. A. Vorontsov 1
Translated from Metallovedenie i Termicheskaya Obrabotka Metallov, No. 8, pp. 21 – 25, August, 2006.
A new basic composition for creating castable intermetallic refractory alloys ensuring elevated operating tem-
peratures is considered. Results of studies of alloys based on the Ni3Al intermetallic compound with different
alloying systems are presented. Ways for increasing the ductility, strength, and refractoriness are outlined.
INTRODUCTION
The prospects of high-temperature gas turbine engines
for aircrafts are directly connected with the gas temperature
at the inlet to the turbine. For example, when the gas tempe-
rature is increased by 50°C the efficiency of the turbine
grows by 12.5%.
Today the parts operating at a high heat load are pro-
duced from nickel-base alloys hardened with /c103/c162-phase. The
operating temperature of this class of alloys is limited to
1050°C due to their intense oxidation and softening caused
by dissolution and coagulation of the /c103/c162-phase at a tempera-
ture exceeding 1050°C. The structural stability of high-tem-
perature nickel alloys can be raised by introducing tungsten,
tantalum, and rhenium into their composition, but this in -
creases their density and cost. Their mechanical properties
can also degrade due to the danger of formation of topologi -
cally close-packed phases based on refractory alloying ele -
ments. For example, in several last decades the density of
castable nickel alloys has increased form 7.8 tons /c47m
3 (alloy
VZhL12U) to 8.8 tons /c47m3 (alloy ZhS32) while the total
content of expensive alloying elements increased from 8 to
18 – 20 wt.%.
The aim of the present work consisted in searching for a
new base for creating castable refractory alloys capable of
operating at 1200 – 1300°C and possessing a reduced density
and an economical chemical composition. Creation and use
of such alloys in gas turbine engines should raise their opera-
ting temperature and lower their mass and cost.
METHODS OF STUDY
We studied an alloyed Ni
3Al compound, which preserves
an ordered fcc crystal lattice (Ll 2 ) until the melting point
(1395°C), has a density of 7.5 tons /c47m3, and exists in binary
and more complex allying systems, as a base for creating
castable high-temperature refractory alloys. In contrast to
other intermetallic materials Ni
3Al possesses a margin of
low-temperature ductility and is characterized by anomalous
growth in the yield strength upon increase in the temperature
(a peak of the yield strength occurs at about 700 – 800°C).
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Studies of the microstructure and properties of composi-
tions based on the Ni
3Al intermetallic showed the following
regular features: in the binary and ternary Ni – Al – X sys-
tems (where X is used for Cr, Mo, and W) the compounds
crystallize (in the case of precision investment casting) with
a single-phase structure (Fig. 1 a ); the Ni
3Al compound
jointly alloyed with 5 wt.% Cr, 3 wt.% Mo, and 3 wt.% W
crystallizes by a eutectic reaction and yields a dispersed
heterophase structure in the bulk of the grains, which con -
sists of a hardened /c103/c162-phase (about 85 – 90 vol.%) and fine
interlayers of /c103-solid solution (about 10 – 15 vol.%) (Fig. 1b ).

in intermetallic materials with such a structure a satisfactory
ductility in tensile tests at room temperature ( /c100~5–
10 vol.%), because the straining occurs in the bulk of the
grains and dislocations move over the intermediate /c103-layers
[1]. However, at a high temperature (1100 – 1200°C) compo-

VIAM produces and supplies to consumers blends
for castable intermetallic alloys VKNA-1V (TU
1-595-3-664–2002), VKNA-4U (TU 1-595-3-664–2002),
VKNS-1VR (TU 1-595-3-783–2004), VKNA-4UR (TU
1-595-3-869–2004), and VKNA-25 (TU-595-3-893–2005).
According to the set of physical and mechanical properties
obtained within certification of aircraft materials, VKNA-1V
is recommended for parts of combustion chambers, flues,
gates of adjustable nozzle, and nozzle blades of small-size
GTE; VKNA-4U is recommended for uncooled rotor blades
and nozzle vanes of turbines; VKNA-4UR is recommended
for turbine nozzle vanes and gates of adjustable nozzle;
VKNA-25 is recommended for uncooled turbine blades.
Castable intermetallic alloys have satisfactory process
properties in casting of preforms for nozzle and rotor blades,
rotor inserts, gates of adjustable nozzles, nozzle blade sets, etc.
Alloy VKNA-4U has passed service testing in a com -
mercial TVD-20 engine in uncooled rotor blades for 5000 h
and 10,000 cycles. A decision on commercial production of
the alloy has been taken, which will reduce the mass of the
first turbine stage and increase the service life of this stage
by a factor of2–3 .

CONCLUSIONS
1. A new base has been developed for creating competi -
tive intermetallic alloys with a heterophase structure, operat -
ing temperature of 1200 – 1250°C, reduced mass, lower con-
tent of expensive alloying elements, and less laborious pro -
cess of fabrication of semiproducts and parts than in the ex -
isting commercial alloys.
2. In order to raise the strength and ductility of composi -
tions based on the Ni3Al intermetallic at room temperature, it
is necessary to create a heterophase structure in the bulk of
the grains with 10 – 15 vol.% /c103-solid solution.
3. In order to raise the density, ductility, and high-tem -
perature strength of the intermetallic compositions at a high
temperature, a heterophase structure should be created both
in the body of the grains and in dendrite spacing.
4. The determined structural features of intermetallic
compositions based on Ni
3Al have been used for creating a
series of intermetallic alloys of type VKNA that differ from
conventional nickel alloys by high operating temperatures,
high high-temperature strength, and an economical compo -
sition.
