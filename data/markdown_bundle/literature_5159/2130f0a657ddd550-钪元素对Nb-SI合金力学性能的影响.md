The microstructure and room-temperature mechanical properties of directionally solidi ﬁed and heat-treated
Nb 16Si 23Ti 4Cr 2Al 2Hf 0/0.3/0.5Sc (at.%) alloys were investigated. All alloys consisted of Nb-based solid solu-
tion (Nbss), α Nb5Si3 and γ Nb5Si3 with preferred 〈001〉 orientation for theﬁrst two phases, and Sc was enriched
in γ Nb5Si3 phase. Sc-added alloys showed a more continuous Nbss matrix in which theﬁner Nb5Si3 phase distrib-
uted uniformly. The volume fraction of both α Nb5Si3 and γ Nb5Si3 phase was decreased by Sc addition. Sc
alloying reduced the Young's modulus and nano-hardness of Nbss phase. 0.50 at.% Sc addition enhanced the frac-
ture toughness and tensile strength from 9.88 MPa m1/2 and 430.3 MPa to 25.95 MPa m1/2 and 1119.7 MPa. The
improvement in fracture toughness was due to the better deformability of Nbss phase. The high tensile strength
was attributed to the dispersed and reﬁned Nb5Si3 phase, which was not degraded by the increased Nbss volume
fraction since dislocations were not open within most Nbss phase. Besides, the strong interface cohesion was pref-
erable for the strength of Sc-added alloys.

1. Introduction
By incorporating Nb5Si3 silicide into Nbss phase, Nb-Si based alloys
are formed with ductile/strengthening two-phase structure in which
Nb5Si3 phase provides creep and oxidation resistance at high tempera-
ture, and Nbss phase supplies fracture toughness and ductility at room
temperature [1–3]. Nb-Si based alloys show great potential for replacing
Ni based superalloys due to high melting points, relatively low densities
and excellent high-temperature strength [4]. However, their industrial
applications are still restricted by the low fracture toughness and poor
ductility [1,5]. In recent years, room-temperature mechanical properties
have been improved through alloying and optimizing processing tech-
niques. Kashyap [6–9] et al. have studied the signi ﬁcant enhancement
in mechanical properties and oxidation resistance for Nb-Si binary al-
loys with Ga, In and Mg additions. Multi-component Nb Si Ti Cr Al Hf

Chan [10,15,16] et al. have proposed a theory to understand the duc-
tility of Nb-Si based alloys, in terms of the ratios of surface energy to
Peierls-Nabarro energy (γ

fracture, dislocation nucleation and dislocation mobility. Contrary toγs
and γus,U P-N is very sensitive to alloying additions. Fewer d + s elec-
trons per atom will weaken the atomic bonding and thus lower the
UP-N, which beneﬁts the mechanical properties due to the higher mobil-
ity of crack-tip dislocations in both Nb ss and Nb5Si3 phase. Sc (3d14s2)
has fewer d + s electrons compared with 5 in Nb (4d 45s1). Therefore,
Sc is likely to restrain the fracture by promoting the dislocation emission
and the slip process at crack tips. Similarly, Ti (3d
24s2) and Hf (5d26s2)

site effect. Besides, Sc has been used in aluminum alloys to improve me-
chanical properties [17,18]. However, to our best knowledge, the effect
of Sc on Nb-Si based alloys has yet remained unexplored, which arouses
our interest.
The main purpose of this study is to systematically investigate the
microstructure, phase constitutions as well as room-temperature me-
chanical properties (Young's modulus, nano-hardness, fracture tough-
ness and tensile strength) of Nb-Si based alloys with newly designed
compositions. Excellent fracture toughness and tensile strength are es-
sential for the security of manufacturing, assembling and operating.
And valid data of modulus and nano-hardness for single phases can
help understand the properties of Nb
ss/Nb5Si3 composites. Moreover,
the toughening and strengthening mechanisms are discussed in details.
We hope these results could be bene ﬁcial for the future application of
Nb-Si based alloys.
2. Experimental procedures
Ingots with nominal compositions of Nb 16Si 23Ti 4Cr 2Al 2Hf 0/0.3/
0.5Sc (at.%) were prepared by vacuum non-consumable arc-melting
within water-cooled copper hearth in argon atmosphere. Sc was
added through the Al-Sc alloy since pure Sc has high chemical activity
in air. Each ingot weighed 1500 g. These ingots were re-melted at
least four times to ensure the homogeneity. Several rods were cut
from the ingots by electro-discharge machining (EDM) and ground to
remove oxidized surfaces. The rods were assembled in self-made ce-
ramic crucibles. Directional solidiﬁcation was carried out in the liquid-
metal cooling (LMC) furnace, then these rods were heat treated at
1500 °C for 12 h under argon protection, followed by furnace cooling
to room temperature. These samples were marked as the Master alloy,
0.30Sc alloy and 0.50Sc alloy respectively, according to their Sc addition.

Berkovich) was used to evaluate Young's modulus, E

polished and kept parallel to the platform. Young's modulus and

Fig. 6 shows the directionally solidiﬁed and heat-treated microstruc-

blunted after heat treatment since this round morphology decreases
the interphase boundary area and the total interfacial energy [ 3]. As
shown in Fig. 6 (c–f), γ Nb5Si3 phase is quite rare in Sc-added alloys.

In addition to the constraint that restricts the plastic deformation of
Nbss phase and thus affects its fracture mode, the fracture behavior is
also in ﬂuenced by the property of single phases [ 20]. In Table 4 ,
micromechanical properties of α Nb5Si3 phase remain nearly un-
changed, while Nb ss phase can be better deformed since it becomes
softer with relatively lower modulus after adding Sc. The higher
deformability of Nbss phase can help reduce the local stress concentra-
tion to delay the crack initiation in Nb 5Si3 phase [20]. It can also blunt
the crack propagation by the local plastic ﬂow [1,13], thereby increase
the fracture toughness of Sc-added alloys. Another remarkable phe-
nomenon is that interface separation occurs frequently in Sc-added al-
loys (see Fig. 10 (d) and (f)). Ashby [ 20] et al. have pointed out that
limited interface separation is desirable for fracture toughness since it
lowers the constraint level. Moreover, interface separation can blunt
crack tips and induce crack de ﬂection, more fracture energy would be
absorbed along the tortuous crack propagation path [ 5]. So, interface
separation is beneﬁcial for fracture toughness.
4.2. Effect of Sc on the tensile strength
Nb-Si based alloys exhibit great room-temperature tensile strength
with Sc addition. The strength improvement in two-phase Nb-Si based
alloys is usually attributed to the strengthening of hard Nb
5Si3 phase
[14,35] and/or the solid-solution strengthening of ductile Nb ss phase
[6,13,20]. In general, tensile strength increases with the volume fraction
of silicide under both room and high temperature conditions. By build-
ing back propagation arti ﬁcial neural networks, Liu [ 24] et al. predict
that room-temperature tensile strength decreases as Nb
5Si3 volume
fraction drops from 40% to 30%. From Fig. 8 (j), adding Sc reduces
Nb5Si3 volume fraction, which is unfavorable to tensile strength [ 6].
And no new precipitated phases are found through the XRD and micro-
structure analysis. Besides, Nb ss phase is not strengthened since Nb ss
phase gets softening after adding Sc (see Table 4). Without the solid-
solution and hard-phase strengthening effects, there must be some
other factors that contribute to the excellent tensile strength.
Microstructure reﬁnement has been known as the optimum method
to improve both the tensile strength and fracture toughness at room
temperature. As a brittle material, Nb-Si based alloys are very sensitive
to microcracks. From Figs. 6–8, adding Sc leads to the well-re ﬁned mi-
crostructure by changing the morphology and volume of Nb
5Si3 phase.
The deformation can be distributed more uniformly to avoid dislocation
pile-ups, especially around Nb5Si3 phase, which restrains the crack initi-
ation in the brittle Nb 5Si3 phase [11,34]. Since Nb 5Si3 phase becomes
isolated and dispersed, the surrounding ductile Nbss phase could effec-
tively arrest microcracks even if they are initiated in some Nb 5Si3
phase. As shown inFig. 12(b–c), conspicuous cleavage facets are not ob-
served due to the re ﬁnement of Nb 5Si3 phase, and Nb 5Si3 phase is
surrounded by the plastically deformed Nb ss phase. It is less likely to
form large crack sources to cause the catastrophic fracture. As a result,
Sc-added alloys can be subjected to greater stress before fracture. Mean-
while, more interfaces parallel to the loading direction are introduced as
obstacles to resist interfacial sliding, which beneﬁt the tensile strength
[34].
By analyzing the distribution of dislocations in 0.50Sc alloy (see
Fig. 13), we ﬁnd that there are few or even no dislocations inside Nb
ss
phase, most of them are piled up near Nb ss/Nb5Si3 and Nbss/Nbss inter-
faces. It can infer that dislocation sources are almost inactive within
Nb
ss phase, which explains why Sc-added alloys have excellent tensile
strength even with a higher volume fraction of Nbss phase. The strength
keeps increasing as dislocations are pinned by interfaces. As discussed
above, the re ﬁned microstructure with isolated Nb 5Si3 phase avoids
the premature failure for Sc-added alloys. So, interface stress can in-
crease continuously before fracture, leading to the interface separation
(see Fig. 12 (b–c)). However, for Master alloy, interface separation is
overshadowed by the premature fracture. Therefore, if microcracks are
not initiated in most Nb
5Si3 phase, Nbss/Nb5Si3 and Nbss/Nbss interfaces
play a predominate role in resisting interface separation and bearing the
load. One important thing is that although interface separation is bene-
ﬁcial for toughness through lowering constraint level, inducing the
crack deﬂection and absorbing more fracture energy during the defor-
mation process, it actually introduces cracks into the microstructure,
which induces the ﬁnal fracture. Some studies [ 30,35] have shown
that interface separation degrades the strength. In this study, we think
that the interface separation is the precursor of fracture for Sc-added
alloys.
5. Conclusion
A series of Nb-Si based alloys are fabricated with different Sc addi-
tion. The effect of Sc on microstructure, phase orientation, element con-
tents and mechanical properties is systemically analyzed. Major
conclusions can be drawn as follows:
1) The constituent phases of all alloys are Nb ss, α Nb5Si3 and γ Nb5Si3.
Sc is primarily partitioned in γ Nb5Si3 phase. Sc alloying re ﬁnes
Nb5Si3 phase, leading Nb ss matrix to be higher continuous. Both
Nbss and α Nb5Si3 phase grow preferentially along 〈001〉. The vol-
ume fraction of α Nb5Si3 and γ Nb5Si3 phase is reduced in Sc-
added alloys.
2) For Nb ss phase, En and Hn are stable on transverse sections. En is re-
duced to some extent, and Hn decreases from 5.35 GPa to 4.84 GPa
with 0.5 at.% Sc addition. En and Hn of α Nb5Si3 phase remain nearly
unchanged because of its intrinsic brittleness.
3) 0.30 and 0.50 at.% Sc addition improves room-temperature fracture
toughness from 9.88 MPa m1/2 to 22.88 and 25.95 MPa m1/2.T h i se n -
hancement is attributed to the re ﬁned and dispersed Nb5Si3 phase,
the softer Nb ss phase as well as the interface separation, all of
which alleviate the constraint on the plastic deformation of Nb ss
phase.
4) The re ﬁned microstructure with dispersed Nb 5Si3 phase improves
tensile strength from 430.3 MPa to 1123.5 and 1119.7 MPa for
0.30Sc and 0.50Sc alloy. The strength is not affected by the higher
volume fraction of Nbss phase since dislocation sources are inactive
inside Nb ss phase. Without the early initiation of microcracks in
Nb5Si3 phase, Nb ss/Nb5Si3 and Nb ss/Nbss interfaces play a major
role in bearing the load for Sc-added alloys.
