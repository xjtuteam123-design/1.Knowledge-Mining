A novel precipitation-strengthened multicomponent Ni-rich Ni 46.23 Co 23 Cr 10 Fe 5 Al 8.5 Ti 4 W 2 Mo 1 C 0.15 B 0.1 Zr 0.02 
(at.%) high-entropy alloy (HEA) was designed with strain-hardening after yielding rather than strain- 
softening even at the temperature of 10 0 0 °C. Due to the rapid onset of intergranular cracking, the 
HEA with spherical precipitates and straight grain boundaries was brittle, exhibiting an inferior tensile 
strength of ~220 MPa, and an insuﬃcient uniform elongation of only ~1.9%. The serrated-grain-boundary 
architecture effectively overcomes this intergranular cracking issue. The resultant HEA with irregular- 
shaped precipitates and serrated grain boundaries showed a brittle-to-ductile transition, giving in a su- 
perior strength up to as high as ~260 MPa while maintaining a uniform elongation of ~6.5%. Such an 
improvement of the strength and ductility has been attributed to the enhanced resistance of the serrated 
grain boundaries to the intergranular crack nucleation and propagation. Our current results provide a 
new method for the innovative design of high-temperature structural materials with superior mechanical 
performances.

Heat-resistant metallic materials with high strength, suﬃcient 
uniform elongation, and environmental tolerances at high tem- 
peratures above 800 °C, are highly demanded for modern civil 
and industrial applications, such as blades and discs of gas tur- 
bine engines for aircrafts, helicopters, and rockets and land-based 
gas turbines [1] . Ni-based superalloys have long been the opti- 
mal candidates for these applications [1] . Further improvements 
in temperature capabilities and creep strength are now being ac- 
tively sought to improve the thrust-to-weight ratio and the fuel 
eﬃciency [1-3] ; however, this further improvement has now al- 
most reached their bottlenecks [3] . Recently, high-entropy alloys 
(HEAs) have emerged as a new class of structural materials [ 4 , 
5 ]. The developed HEAs usually consist of ﬁve or more major el- 
ements, and may also contain minor elements to optimize the 
properties with total conﬁgurational entropy of at least 1.5 R ( R is

Moreover, the nanoprecipitates-strengthened HEAs have suf- 
fered from a severe strain-softening issue [21] , e.g., the decrease 
of the stress with strain once the specimen yields, when stretched 
at elevated temperatures. Similar strain-softening issues were also 
often observed in other HEAs [22-25] , and many conventional 
wrought Ni-based superalloys [26-28] . The strain-softening prob- 
lem causes these materials susceptible to occasional overloading, 
and undesirable plastic instability. Once plastic deformation starts, 
a rapid and continuous necking to a point occurs, this inevitably 
leads to poor uniform elongation, bringing out serious service un- 
reliability and safety risk during their long-time durations. As the 
grain boundary becomes more weak than the grain interior with 
temperature [1] , another critical issue, rapid intergranular crack- 
ing, e.g., crack along grain boundaries [ 21 , 29 , 30 ], can be fore- 
seen to become more intense at high temperatures beyond ~800 °C. 
The fast intergranular cracking causes the specimen early fracture, 
which also induces poor uniform elongation. 
In this study, a novel precipitation-strengthened HEA was 
designed and expected to tolerate the operation temperatures 
at 10 0 0 °C and above. We ﬁrst carefully evaluated the Ni-Co- 
Cr-Fe-Al-Ti-W-Mo system aided by the CALPHAD (CALculation 
of PHAse Diagrams) technique with a TTNI8 database, and ﬁ- 
nally designed a novel multicomponent Ni-rich HEA (MNiHEA) 
Ni 46.23 Co 23 Cr 10 Fe 5 Al 8.5 Ti 4 W 2 Mo 1 C 0.15 B 0.1 Zr 0.02 (in at.% with a con- 
ﬁgurational entropy of 1.56 R ) ( Fig. 1 a). The criteria we used to 
screen out this target composition can be described as follows: 1) 
Al and Ti were added to hatch the formation of high-density L1 2 
precipitates for precipitation hardening, the relatively large content 
of Al was adopted for the generation of Al 2 O 3 ﬁlm to enhance the 
oxidation resistance; 2) 10 at.% of Cr was doped to form Cr 2 O 3 
ﬁlm for further improvements of hot-corrosion, and oxidation re- 
sistance at elevated temperatures; 3) W and Mo were alloyed for 
solid-solution strengthening of the matrix and for slow coarsening 
kinetics of the precipitates; 4) microalloying small amounts of C, 
B, and Zr was for the grain-boundary strengthening; 5) the con- 
centrations of Ni, Co, and Fe were adjusted accordingly to stabilize 
the FCC (face-centered cubic) matrix. Subsequently, serrated grain 
boundaries were introduced in MNiHEA to eliminate the strain- 
softening and rapid intergranular cracking issues. The microstruc- 
tures, tensile properties, and associated deformation and fracture 
mechanisms were systematically investigated. 
To start with, alloy ingots were synthesized by arc-melting a 
mixture of pure metals (purity ≥ 99.95%) in a Ti-gettered high- 
purity argon atmosphere. The ingots were ﬂipped and remelted 
at least four times to promote the chemical homogeneity, and 
then drop-cast into a water-cooled copper mold to give ﬂats 
with a dimension of 50 × 10 × 5 mm 3 . The as-cast ﬂats were 
ﬁrstly solution-annealed at 1200 °C for 2 h. The serrated-grain- 
boundaries (SEGBs) were introduced by furnace-quenching the 
solution-annealed ﬂats down to 800 °C inside the furnace with a 
slow constant rate of ~0.17 °C/s and subsequent cooling to am- 
bient temperature without power, followed by aging at 10 0 0 °C 
for 24 h (SEG-MNiHEA). As a comparison, the straight-grain- 
boundaries (STGBs) were manipulated by oil-quenching (~250 °C/s) 
the solution-annealed ﬂats down to room temperature followed 
by the same aging treatment (STG-MNiHEA). All aging treatments 
were ﬁnished by oil-quenching in the air atmosphere.

The elemental distributions of the SEG-MNiHEA specimen an- 
alyzed by EPMA and APT are shown in Fig. 2 . EPMA maps clearly 
revealed that the irregular-shaped precipitates were enriched in Ni, 
Al, and Ti elements, and depleted in Co, Cr, and Fe elements, while 
the matrix had the opposite distributions of these elements, re- 
spectively. Further quantitative APT analyses of the elemental dis- 
tributions at atomic-resolution are exhibited in Figs. 2 b–c. The en- 
richment of Ni, Al, and Ti atoms combined with the depletion of 
Co, Cr, and Fe atoms for the precipitates whereas the reverse ele- 
mental partitions for the matrix were found in atom maps of these 
elements ( Fig. 2 b), well in line with those from the EPMA analyses. 
Apart from these elements, W atoms were slightly enriched in the 
precipitates while Mo atoms had a more pronounced enrichment 
in the matrix was also disclosed ( Fig. 2 b). The chemical compo- 
sitions of the precipitates and matrix were quantitatively analyzed 
by a mean of proximity histogram calculated from a 45 at.% Ni iso- 
concentration surface ( Fig. 2 c), and the obtained results were sum-

partitioned, and W atoms were mildly partitioned into the precipi- 
tates by the magnitude of ~2.7 at.%, respectively. In contrast, the 
Co atom (~14 at.%) was signiﬁcantly depleted and the Cr atoms 
(~2.2 at.%), Fe atoms (~1.6 at.%), and Mo atoms (~0.9 at.%) were no- 
ticeably depleted in the precipitates, respectively. A contrary par- 
tition trend was found in the matrix. Given the stoichiometry of 
the L1 2 phase with the A 3 B-type structure, and the occupation 
trend of these elements in the multicomponent L1 2 lattice [ 32 , 
34 , 35 ], the precipitates can be identiﬁed as the multicomponent 
(Ni,Co,Fe,Cr) 3 (Ti,Al,Cr,W,Mo) L1 2 phase. 
The irregular-shaped precipitates and SEGBs were introduced 
simultaneously in the SEG-MNiHEA by the furnace-quenching, akin 
to those observed in conventionally Ni-based superalloys [ 33 , 36- 
38 ]. The formation of the irregular-shaped intragranular precipi- 
tates [36] was attributed to the coalescence of particles. The parti- 
cles formed ﬁrstly at the onset of precipitation coalesce with adja- 
cent ﬁner precipitates subsequently precipitated at lower tempera- 
tures during the slow cooling, and at 10 0 0 °C during the isother- 
mal aging. The formation of the SEGBs was associated with the 
relatively large intergranular precipitates ( Fig. 1 g). Their bound- 
ary side of the precipitate/matrix interface preferentially grows and 
coarsens more quickly toward grain boundaries [ 36 , 37 ] due to the 
strain energy difference between the matrix side and boundary 
side [37] as well as the rapid solute diffusion along grain bound- 
aries [38] . In sharp contrast, the growth and coalescence of the 
particles during the oil-quenching were signiﬁcantly suppressed 
for the relatively much faster cooling rate. When isothermally aged 
at the temperature of 10 0 0 °C, without large particles previously 
formed during cooling, the precipitates of the STG-MNiHEA spec- 
imen coarsen by the mechanism of Ostwald ripening [38] rather 
than the coalescence of each other. Such coarsening process leads 
to the spherical intragranular and intergranular precipitates.

Our detailed analyses of the fracture surfaces and lateral sur- 
faces of the failed SEG-MNiHEA and STG-MNiHEA specimens 
stretched at 10 0 0 °C uncover the underlying mechanism responsi- 
ble for this unusual SEGB toughening. Although these two speci- 
mens fractured essentially by the mode of intergranular fracture, 
their differences can be clearly observed ( Fig. 3 d). The fracture 
surface of the STG-MNiHEA specimen was featured with smooth 
grain-interior and STGB facets (the left inset in Fig. 3 d), while those 
of the SEG-MNiHEA specimen were relatively rough (the right in- 
set in Fig. 3 d), respectively. Moreover, we further found exten- 
sive intergranular cracks near the fractured end of the failed STG- 
MNiHEA specimen ( Figs. 4 a–d). Cracking along grain boundaries 
was found to be closely related to high strain localization, e.g., high 
strains at grain boundaries and very low strains within the grain 
interiors, as proved and unveiled by the EBSD KAM map ( Fig. 4 d). 
The occurrence of ﬁne recrystallized grains at the grain-boundary 
triple junctions (the inset in Fig. 4 c) concurrently evidenced the 
high strain localization because dynamic recrystallization prefer- 
entially occurs in high-strained regions [29] . The highly localized

strains at the STGBs may probably be produced by slip-assisted 
grain-boundary sliding, and the accumulation of dislocations at the 
vicinity of grain boundaries [ 18 , 39 , 40 ]. Such high strain localiza- 
tion suggests the STGBs with very limited capability to accommo- 
date strains, and thus, the poor resistance to the nucleation and 
propagation of the intergranular microcracks [21] . Once the inter- 
granular microcracks are initiated, they will expand readily and 
rapidly and therefore are easy to connect to form the straight in- 
tergranular crack path, leading to the fast fracture ( Fig. 3 b). 
The SEGBs remarkably eliminated this severe strain localiza- 
tion, as shown in Figs. 4 e–h. No secondary intergranular cracks 
and obvious strain localization as well as ﬁne recrystallized grains 
at them appeared in the fractured SEG-MNiHEA specimen. On 
the contrary, more pronounced strains were uniformly distributed 
within the grain interiors ( Fig. 4 h). The release of the intergranu- 
lar strain localization via the SEGBs can be attributed to the limit 
of grain-boundary sliding [39] , and the partition of strain into grain 
interiors instead of concentrating at grain boundaries [41] . Further- 
more, the SEGBs also suppress crack propagation because that the 
zigzag array of the initiated microcracks at the SEGB segments 
were diﬃcult to connect to form a serrated intergranular crack 
propagation path [41] . As a result, the SEG-MNiHEA specimen with 
the enhanced resistance to the intergranular crack nucleation and 
propagation showed a much superior uniform ductility and tensile 
strength than the STG-MNiHEA counterpart. 
In summary, we successfully developed a novel 
Ni 46.23 Co 23 Cr 10 Fe 5 Al 8.5 Ti 4 W 2 Mo 1 C 0.15 B 0.1 Zr 0.02 (at.%) MNi- 
HEA strengthened by coherent L1 2 precipitates using the 
computational-assisted CALPHAD method. The SEG-MNiHEA 
specimen overcame the issues of strain-softening and rapid 
intergranular cracking, exhibiting an unusual continuous strain- 
hardening, and a brittle-to-ductile transition with the improved 
tensile strength up to ~260 MPa, and uniform elongation up to 
~6.5%, respectively. Our results have demonstrated the powerful 
usefulness and effectiveness of high-temperature toughening 
precipitation-strengthened HEAs via the SEGB architecture. The 
method of slowing cooling applied here to introduce the SEGBs 
and irregular-shaped precipitates is simple and easy to control, and 
possible be readily adapted by the industrial process, and thus can 
be used to produce many SEGB-architectured HEAs and conven- 
tionally superalloys with an enhanced strength-ductility synergy. 
Therefore, our current ﬁndings provide a new approach for the 
future design of high-performance precipitation-strengthened 
HEAs and superalloys for high-temperature structural applications.
