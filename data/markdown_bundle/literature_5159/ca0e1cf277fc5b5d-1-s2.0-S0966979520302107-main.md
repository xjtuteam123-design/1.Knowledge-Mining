1. Introduction 
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) have attracted intense attention because 
of their remarkable properties [ 1 – 3 ]. It is generally accepted that single 
phase face-centered-cubic HEAs possess excellent ductility and unsat -
isfactory strength. Therefore, the methods for improving the strengths of 
the FCC HEAs have been intensely investigated. For example, Otto et al. 
[ 4 ] improved the yield strength of the FCC-CoCrFeMnNi alloy by 
reducing the grain size. However, the yield strength of this HEA was 
increased from 200 MPa to only 350 MPa with the decrease in the grain 
size from 144 μ m to 4.4 μ m, indicating that it is difficult to obtain a 
dramatic enhancement of the strength by grain refinement. On the 
other hand, many studies have indicated that precipitation strength -

3.2. Formation of σ phase and annealing twin 
In the A2 alloy, three types of nanoscale σ particles were formed after 
the cold rolling and annealing treatment. This can be explained as fol -
lows. On the one hand, Co
34.5

3.6 
alloy forms annealing 
twins during the high temperature treatment. On the other hand, 
attributable to the inhomogeneous of plastic deformation at a fine scale, 
the recrystallization speed of different deformed areas can be evidently 
different [ 26 , 27 ]. Therefore, recrystallized and non-recrystallized grains 
can exist at the same time during annealing [ 28 ]. Based on above 
analysis, the formation of different σ particles can be described 
reasonably as follows. In local area, recrystallization occurs and new 
recrystallization grains form. The boundaries of the annealing twins and 
recrystallization grains provide favorable conditions for σ phase nucle -
ation. Inside the non-recrystallized grains, the high density of disloca -
tions formed during cold rolling provides an abundance of nucleation 
sites. The phase growth after nucleation is mainly controlled by diffu -
sion, and the grain boundaries and twin boundaries provide prolonged 
diffusion pathways for the atoms. However, with the development of 
recovery, the dislocation density inside the grains decreases sharply, 
leading to a decrease in the diffusion path length. The grain boundary σ 
and twin boundary σ phase particles grow faster than the intra-grain σ 
phase, so that the boundary σ particles are larger than the intra-grain σ 
particles. According to Fig. 1 (d), a “ step-like ” twin boundary consisting 
of ITB and CTB was also present. In an FCC alloy, parallel CTB always 
consists of the {111} crystal planes. The formation of ITB arises from 
three Shockley partial dislocations launched by either a grain boundary 
or a junction of the twin boundary and the grain boundary [ 29 , 30 ]. 
3.3. Strength and ductility 
Due to the effect of the σ particles, YS and UTS improve by 181% and 
81%, respectively, while a high ductility is maintained. Nano particles 
can co-deform with matrix when dislocations cut through the hardening 
unit [ 31 ]. However, no shearing mechanism is observed during defor -
mation (Refer to Fig. 4 ). Non-deformed σ or μ particles were also found 
in CoCrFeNiMo series alloys [ 19 , 20 ]. It can be considered that reason -
able the σ phase cannot deform with the matrix and the high ductility of 
the alloy is caused by the FCC matrix. To understand the effect of the 
matrix on the ductility, the strain hardening rate and strain hardening 
exponent (n) of the A1 alloy were evaluated because its composition is 
close to the composition of the matrix of the A2 alloy, with the results 
shown in Fig. 3 . Strain hardening exponent (n) is used to assess the 
ability of the material to restrain crack formation, propagation and 
uniform deformation [ 32 ], and can be obtained from true stress-strain 
curve of the A1 alloy. The relationship of the true stress ( σ

factors for a high UTS. Fig. 3 (b) shows the strain hardening rate curves 
obtained for the two alloys. The evolution of the strain hardening rate of 
the A1 alloy presents the three typical distinct stages of decrease → in -

dislocation slip to twinning deformation in low SFE alloys is the main 
reason for the increase in the strain hardening rate [ 34 ]. Our results 
suggest that the matrix of the A2 alloy has a certain capacity for strain 
hardening as a result of deformation twins. By contrast, the A2 alloy has 
a very high strain hardening rate at early stage. To elucidate the strain 
hardening mechanism, the deformation microstructure of the A2 alloy is 
shown in Fig. 4 . At a strain level of 5%, the deformation microstructure 
is characterized by slip bands along the {111} planes, implying that 
dislocation slip dominates the deformation process. As non-shearable 
precipitates, σ particles effectively prevent the motion of dislocations, 
as illustrated by the dislocation pile-up around the σ particle shown in 
Fig. 4 (a). Moreover, a deformation twin was found at the same strain 
level, while no slip bands were observed implying that twin boundaries 
can also obstruct the motion of dislocations. These results suggest that 
the cooperative effects of the σ particles and deformation twins maintain 
the high strain hardening rate. Since deformation twins can also form in 
the A1 alloy, we argue that σ particles make a greater contribution to the 
high strain hardening rate at the early stage of deformation. With the 
increase of the strain to 12%, slip bands were found in the deformation 
twins, as indicated by red arrows in Fig. 4 . This finding is understand -
able because dislocations can break through CTB and slip into the twins 
with the increase in the external stress as shown by Lu et al. [ 35 ]. This 
should account for the decrease in the strain hardening rate at the later 
stage of the deformation.

4. Conclusions 
In summary, we fabricated a new Co
34.5
Cr
30
Ni
26.5
Al
5.4
W
3.6 
high 
entropy alloy. Nanoscale σ phase particles and coarse annealing twins 
were formed in the alloy after thermomechanical procedures. The alloy 
has excellent comprehensive mechanical properties with YS of 875 MPa, 
UTS of 1219 MPa and EL of 19%. The high ductility of the alloy is 
attributed to its FCC matrix with a high strain hardening exponent that 
promotes uniform deformation and suppresses crack propagation. The 
strong strain hardening capacity value of the alloy leads to its high UTS. 
TEM results show that the hindering of the motion of dislocations by the 
σ particles and the deformation twin boundaries is the main origin of the 
observed high strain hardening rate. Based on the results, the “ defor -
mation twins þ nano particles ” design concept was proposed for the 
future preparation of additional double-high HEAs.
