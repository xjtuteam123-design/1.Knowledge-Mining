We report the mechanical performance and microstructural characteristics of a Fe-Cr-Ni-Mn-N alloy at 
cryogenic temperatures. The exceptionally high yield strength of 1.5 GPa combined with a high strain- 
hardening rate and no deterioration in ductility at 4.2 K was displayed. The evolution of deformation mi- 
crostructure was examined using electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD), the transmission Kikuchi diffrac- 
tion (TKD), high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM), and aberration-corrected scanning 
TEM (STEM). The deformation microstructure mainly consisted of dislocation slip with L -C locks, {111} 
stacking fault formation, {111} deformation nanotwinning, and FCC → HCP shear transformation at 4.2 K. 
The occurrence of FCC-HCP shear transformation inside/near {111} twins to form γ- γtw - ε dual-phase 
structure induces a dynamic Hall-Petch effect that promotes the strain-hardening rate and enhances the 
strength-ductility combination. We believe that this alloy displays outstanding damage tolerance through 
a progressive synergy of deformation mechanisms leading to exceptional strength which provides a new 
insight into the commercialized development of high-performance alloys for cryogenic applications.

1. Introduction 
Developing high-strength metals has been a long-term chal- 
lenge for structural materials due to the accelerating demands for 
component safety and resistance to harsh environments, such as 
transportation and storage of cryogenic liquids, structural com- 
ponents in large hadron colliders (LHC) and fusion reactors, 
aerospace, and other ﬁelds, especially in-service in the liquid he- 
lium (4.2 K) [1–6] . The equiatomic CrCoNi-based medium-entropy 
alloys (MEAs) and high-entropy alloys (HEAs) attracted substan- 
tial attention during the last two decades, at present, developing a 
primitive understanding of those alloys has just started as a poten- 
tial structural material that may be used in many engineering ap- 
plications [7–13] . It is well accepted that one of the reasons for the

Conventional metals and alloys can be tuned to possess excel- 
lent mechanical properties. The twinning-induced plasticity (TWIP) 
and transformation-induced plasticity (TRIP) are usually employed 
to enhance the mechanical properties of the Fe-Mn-Cr, Fe-Cr-Ni, 
Fe-Cr-Ni-Mn-based austenitic stainless steels at room and cryo- 
genic temperatures [24–29] . Seeking to achieve excellent mechan- 
ical properties of these austenitic stainless steels, the alteration of 
elements such as Cr, Ni, Mn, Mo, and N to trigger the temper- 
ature deformation mechanism at the desired temperature range 
was performed. The increasing content of carbon and nitrogen 
(C + N) was found to improve the yield strength which is at- 
tributed to the diffusion of both elements and the solid solution 
strengthening mechanism. In recent years, researchers focused on 
the activation of multiple deformation modes to obtain high duc- 
tility and toughness while designing new advanced high-strength 
steels. Fe-Cr-Ni-Mn-N alloys were developed for the DEMO fu- 
sion reactor structural materials, the yield stress will become over 
1.2 GPa in case the (C + N) is over 0.3 wt% at 4.2 K with a 
fracture toughness of 150 MPa m1/2 [3] . Meanwhile, the solubil- 
ity of nitrogen in liquid austenitic stainless steel is mainly impor- 
tant and it increases with Cr, Mn, V, and Nb [ 30 , 31 ]. Addition- 
ally, nitrogen will also affect the SFE of austenite and change the 
deformation mechanisms. We know that the deformation mech- 
anism of the commercial 304/316 is the plastic deformation in- 
duced γ (FCC) to ε (HCP) and α′ (BCC) martensitic transforma- 
tion at cryogenic temperatures. Fe-Cr-Ni-Mn-N alloys show po- 
tential as cryogenic materials, but the relationship between their 
deformation behaviors and mechanical properties at 4.2 K has 
been rarely investigated. Therefore, a comprehensive understand- 
ing of the transformation mechanism is academically signiﬁcant 
and practically substantial to engineering applications of these 
steels.

2. Experimental methods 
The base materials are Fe-Cr-Ni-Mn-N alloys which were man- 
ufactured by HBIS (Hebei, China), the steelmaking and manufac- 
turing starts by melting the ingots in a Combined Oxygen Bottom 
blowing Maxhutte-Stainless process converter, followed by Vac- 
uum Oxygen Decarburization in an intermediate frequency furnace, 
casting the cylindrical ingots, ESR remelting into diameter billets, 
3D forging, and then the plate was solution-annealed at 1075 °C 
for 60 min followed by water quenching. The chemical composi- 
tion of the Fe-Cr-Ni-Mn-N alloy is shown in Table 1 . 
The microstructure characterization of the Fe-Cr-Ni-Mn-N 
austenitic stainless steel was examined using a scanning elec- 
tron microscope (ThermoFisher Verios 5UC) equipped with elec- 
tron beam backscatter diffraction (EBSD). The sample of the base 
materials was electropolished and scanned using EBSD (Oxford C 
Swift), and data processing including grain size, inverse pole ﬁg- 
ure (IPF) images, phase images, and grain boundary misorientation 
distribution (misorientation angle of > 15 ° for the high angle grain 
boundaries and misorientation angle of < 15 ° for the low angle 
grain boundaries) was performed on Aztec Crystal analysis soft- 
ware. The EBSD result indicates the microstructure of the base ma- 
terial is a single FCC-phase, with an average size of ∼ 30 μm, as 
shown in Fig. 1 . 
The round-bar shape tensile test specimens were cut from the 
center of the material with a diameter of 130 mm using electrical 
discharge machining with a gauge length of 35 mm, and a diam- 
eter of 7 mm according to the standard ISO 6892-4. The tensile 
tests were performed using an electronic universal test machine 
(MTS SANS Model CMT 5105) with a strain rate of 5 × 10–4 s–1 at 
300, 77, and 4.2 K. Three specimens were tested to determine the 
variability in mechanical properties of the materials at each tem- 
perature. The fracture surfaces of the tested specimens were ob- 
served using SEM and the microstructure evolution near the frac- 
ture area was analyzed by EBSD (ThermoFisher Verios 5UC). TEM 
foil samples were cut from the fractured pieces and thinned to 
electron transparency using focused ion beam (FIB) milling (Ther- 
moFisher Helios 5UX). Transmission Kikuchi Diffraction (TKD, Ox- 
ford symmetry S2) was performed on the same instrument us- 
ing an accelerating voltage of 30 keV with the foil positioned at 
–20 °C relative to the plane normal to the incident beam, and 
data processing was performed on Aztec Crystal analysis software. 
The deformation microstructure characterizations were analyzed 
using high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM, 
ThermoFisher Talos F200X) and aberration-corrected scanning TEM 
(STEM, JEOL JEM-ARM300F2).

3. Results and discussions 
3.1. Exceptional mechanical properties at cryogenic temperatures 
The representative tensile stress-strain curves of the single- 
phase FCC Fe-Cr-Ni-Mn-N alloy with the strain rates of 5 × 10–4 
s–1 at 300, 77, and 4.2 K are shown in Fig. 2 . An exceptional com- 
bination of strength and ductility (yield strength ∼ 1.5 GPa, tensile 
strength ∼ 1.85 GPa as well as uniform elongation of ∼ 36 %) is 
realized at a deformation temperature of 4.2 K, which is signif- 
icantly superior to that of yield strength and tensile strength of 
the samples at deformation temperatures at 77 K (yield strength 
∼ 1.08 GPa, tensile strength ∼ 1.6 GPa) and 300 K (yield strength 
∼ 0.46 GPa, tensile strength ∼ 0.82 GPa), but the uniform elonga- 
tion at 4.2 K is lower than those of 77 K ( ∼ 49 %) and 300 K ( ∼
51 %). The discontinuous, serrated plastic ﬂow behavior happened 
at 4.2 K, which is a common phenomenon reported in metastable 
austenitic stainless steels such as 304/316, etc. [32] . It can be seen 
that the amplitude of the stress drop increases to the maximum 
value of ∼ 500 MPa with increasing strain range from 30 % to 35 
% ( Fig. 2(a) ). In consistency with increasing yield strength and ul- 
timate tensile strength as the temperature decreases, the strain- 
hardening rate was simultaneously increased without any apparent 
loss of ductility as shown in Fig. 2(c) . 
3.2. Microstructure characterizations after fracture 
Fig. 3 presents the EBSD inverse pole ﬁgure (IPF) and ker- 
nel average misorientation (KAM) images for the deformation mi- 
crostructures of the tensile specimens near the fracture surface 
at 300, 77, and 4.2 K. Deformation twins and dislocation gliding 
are exhibited at different deformation temperatures. The equiaxed 
grains were completely stretched with an early necking that oc- 
curred at 300 K as compared to the deformation microstructures 
at 77 and 4.2 K, and the orientation of the deformation grains is 
random without strong orientation texture. The KAM is a comple- 
mentary method to analyze the dislocation density, and the KAM 
value quantiﬁes the averaged local misorientation around a partic- 
ular grain and correlates to the density of geometrically necessary 
dislocations (GNDs) [33–35] . It clearly illustrates that the averaged 
KAM value is higher at 300 K (2.25 °, Fig. 3(b) ) than those of both at 
77 K (1.75 °, Fig. 3(d) ) and at 4.2 K (1.86 °, Fig. 3(f) ). And misorienta- 
tions of the deformation microstructure at 77 and 4.2 K are almost 
similar and areas of the regions with KAM values greater than 4 °
are much less than that of 300 K. In addition, secondary cracks can 
be detected near the fracture surface ( Fig. 3(e) ), demonstrating the 
enhancement of the fracture resistance at 4.2 K. 
To observe the detailed features of the deformation microstruc- 
tures, the EBSD images scanned with a smaller step size of 
0.05 μm were performed. The microslip bands with multiple slip 
systems and deformation twins were dominant features of the de- 
formation microstructure at 300 and 77 K. The twin quantity and 
bundle thickness are much larger at 77 K ( Fig. 4(e) ) as compared to 
that of 300 K ( Fig. 4(a) ), which contributes to the enhanced cryo- 
genic strength and ductility at the same time. The higher dislo- 
cation density was conﬁrmed in Fig. 4(c) at 300 K as compared 
to 77 K ( Fig. 4(g) ) in KAM images, revealing that the disloca- 
tion movement dominates the deformation behavior at 300 K. The

along the twin boundaries. Firstly, the HCP ε-martensite embryos 
appeared to nucleate and grow on slip bands in grain interiors

cleated at grain boundaries or twin boundaries. Then the HCP ε- 
martensite grew along slip bands towards grain interiors such as 
the twin boundaries. Hence, the HCP ε-martensite transformation 
induced by activated dislocations was an additional factor that en- 
hanced the strength of Fe-Cr-Ni-Mn-N austenitic stainless steel at 
4.2 K.

Based on the TEM observation of microstructural evolutions 
during plastic deformation at 300 and 77 K, the deformation mech- 
anisms transfer from dislocation glide at 300 K to deformation 
twinning and limited HCP ε-martensitic phase transformation at 
77 K contributes to the enhanced mechanical properties with de- 
creasing temperature. It is consistent with the previously reported 
ductile CrMnFeCoNi HEA [38] . The deformation twinning is ac- 
tivated before the formation of dislocation cells due to cross- 
slip, which induces a dynamic Hall-Petch effect that promotes the 
strain-hardening rate, postpones necking instability, and enhances 
the strength-ductility combination ( Fig. 2(c) ). During the tensile 
testing, the formation of deformation twinning and ε-martensite 
continuously introduce coherent twin/phase boundaries that de- 
crease the mean free path of dislocations, known as the ‘‘dynamic 
Hall-Petch” effect. Such a deformation mechanism causes the high 
strain hardening rates and enhanced strength of Fe-Cr-Ni-Mn-N al- 
loy at 77 K as compared to that of 300 K ( Fig. 2(c) ) [18–21] .

The single-phase FCC Fe-Cr-Ni-Mn-N alloy experienced differ- 
ent deformation modes during the tensile test from 300 to 4.2 K, 
the summary of the deformation mechanism and strain harden- 
ing mechanism led to the ultrastrong strength of the Fe-Cr-Ni- 
Mn-N alloy at cryogenic temperatures as shown in Fig. 12 . The 
planar dislocation slip is the foremost and dominant deformation 
mode at 300 K ( Figs. 3(b) and 6(a) ), and the high density of dis- 
locations was found, leading to the formation extended stacking 
faults through dissociation ( Fig. 6 ( c , d )). Furthermore, a few nano- 
scale deformation twinning appeared in the deformed substruc- 
tures ( Fig. 6(e) ) which is consistent with the previous work on the 
formation of deformation twins [ 36 , 44 ]. As the deformation tem- 
perature decreases to 77 K, the deformation twinning with disloca- 
tion glide dominates the deformation mode, and limited nano HCP 
ε-martensitic transformations have appeared ( Fig. 8(f) ). It must be 
pointed out that the FCC → HCP shear transformation is accom- 
modated by successive gliding of Shockley partial dislocations on 
every two close-packed planes [45] . Due to the high density of 
SF formation, the faulted region in the FCC matrix can be trans- 
formed into an HCP nano-sized structure by adjusting the SFs by 
nucleation and sliding of Shockley partial dislocations. Moreover, 
the SFE is a prominent material property in FCC alloys that is con- 
sidered to control the activation of different deformation modes. 
Thus, the SFE decreases as the temperature decreases, and the ef- 
fect of the Hall-Petch is activated at low temperatures, therefore 
enhancing both the strength and the ductility.

ε-martensitic transformations. The dislocations dissociation and 
stacking faults interfere in coherent twin boundaries and may

4. Conclusions 
The Fe-Cr-Ni-Mn-N austenitic stainless steel exhibits excep- 
tional mechanical properties at cryogenic temperatures with a 
higher yield strength and tensile strength. The deformation mode 
at room temperature is planar dislocation slip and stacking fault 
formation. With decreasing the temperature, nanotwins and lim- 
ited nano-sized HCP ε-martensite formation were formed and be- 
came a predominant aspect of the substructure. The nanotwin-HCP 
ε-martensite nano-sized lamellae consist of nano-sized deforma- 
tion twins and narrow nano-sized HCP ε-martensite formed within 
the nanotwins. The cross slip of partial dislocations along the twin 
boundary provides a favorable site for the growth of the HCP 
phase. At cryogenic temperatures, the deformation nanotwinning 
and dislocations slip signiﬁcantly contributed to the alloy’s ultra- 
high strain hardening rate and great strength, meanwhile, the lim- 
ited strain-induced HCP ε-martensite transformation formed near 
coherent deformation twin boundaries, the formation of L -C locks 
and stacking fault play a minor role in strengthening the Fe-Cr- 
Ni-Mn-N alloy. The present study clariﬁes the deformation mech- 
anism and mechanical properties of Fe-Cr-Ni-Mn-N alloy at 4.2 K, 
thereby promoting their application as structural materials at ex- 
tremely low temperatures.
