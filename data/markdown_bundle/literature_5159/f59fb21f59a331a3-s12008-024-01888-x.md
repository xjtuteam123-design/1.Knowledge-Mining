Prediction of tensile behaviour of hybrid laser arc welded Inconel 617
alloy using machine learning models

Abstract
The current study deals with hybrid laser arc welding (HLAW) of Inconel 617 alloys under different heat inputs (0.516 kJ/mm
and 0.615 kJ/mm) whereas the room and high temperature tensile properties are considered as an output parameter. The high
temperature tensile tests are conducted at 650 °C, 700 °C, and 750 °C respectively. The effect of heat input on the tensile
properties of welded joints was comparatively investigated using tensile tests. The base metal shows superior tensile strength
(814 MPa) than the weld joints. The maximum high-temperature tensile strength of weld joints is 538 MPa was achieved at
the heat input of 0.516 kJ/mm and temperature of 750 °C. Temperature has a signiﬁcant effect on the tensile properties of
weld joints. A signiﬁcant drop in tensile strength was observed at higher temperatures and high heat input (0.615 m/min). In
addition, a signiﬁcant improvement of tensile strength at room temperature and low heat input (0.516 kJ/mm). This works deals
with the application of various regression analysis methods like Linear Regression (LR), Decision Tree Regression (DTR),
Random Forest Regression (RFR), Polynomial Regression (PR), Gaussian Process Regression (GPR) and Support V ector
Regression (SVR) were used for predicting the room and high temperature tensile behaviour of hybrid laser arc welded Inconel
617 alloy. The effectiveness of each model was evaluated in the training and validation stages and the results were compared
to experimental data. To assess the effectiveness of the designed models, the two quantitative standard statistical measures of
root mean squared error (RMSE) and R
2 were applied. The statistical performance factors of the models reveal the superior
performance of PR model was found better than LR, DTR, RFR, SVR, and GPR in predicting the tensile strength of joints.

11 Conclusion
Hybrid laser arc welding techniques were developed for
welding of Inconel 617 alloys. Machine learning models
were created using various regression algorithms; including
LR, DTR, RFR, PR, GPR and SVR to predict the high-
temperature tensile strength of the welded joints. The results
are as follow:
1. The base metal shows superior tensile properties than the
weld joints.
2. Due to lower heat input and faster cooling rate the maxi-
mum high-temperature tensile strength of 538 MPa was
achieved at the WFR of 10 m/min and temperature of
750 °C.
3. The decrease in both Yield Strength (YS) and Ultimate
Tensile Strength (UTS) as temperature increases from
room to high temperature (650 °C, 700 °C, and 750 °C)
because the material softens and the number of dimples
increases with temperature. Consequently, the fractures
of all the weldments are in ductile mode.
4. The fracture surfaces of the tensile specimens exhibited
a signiﬁcant presence of equiaxed dimples. Notably, the
number of these dimples increases as the temperature
increases.
5. Machine learning methods were employed to predict the
tensile properties of welded joints under selected weld-
ing parameters. The statistical performance metrics of
the models demonstrate the superior predictive capa-
bility of the polynomial regression model compared to
other machine learning models for forecasting the ten-
sile strength of hybrid laser arc weld joints.
6. For Ultimate tensile strength the prediction accuracy
using polynomial regression model is found closer to
experimental results with RMSE and R
2 values are
0.0051 and 0.99 respectively.
7. For yield strength the prediction accuracy using polyno-
mial regression model is found closer to experimental
results with RMSE and R 2 values are 0.0000799 and
0.9999 respectively.
8. For percentage elongation the prediction accuracy using
polynomial regression model is found closer to experi-
mental results with RMSE and R 2 values are 0.000364
and 0.999 respectively.
