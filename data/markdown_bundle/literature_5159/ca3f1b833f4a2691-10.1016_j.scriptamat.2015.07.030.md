Novel wrought c/c0 cobalt base superalloys with high strength and
improved oxidation resistance

abstract
New polycrystalline Co-base superalloys with c/c0 microstructure have been developed, which show
excellent mechanical properties and enhanced oxidation behavior. The c0 solvus temperature of these
alloys is comparatively low giving a large processing window. The yield strength of these alloys is similar
to that of wrought Ni-base superalloys. However, the creep strength at 750 /C176C is largely exceeding that of
the Ni-base superalloys Waspaloy and Udimet 720Li. Oxidation tests indicate that the oxidation
resistance is comparable to commercial Ni-base superalloys.

Co-base superalloys consisting of a fcc c Co solid solution hard-
ened by c0 Co3(Al,W) precipitates with L1 2 crystal structure have
attracted much attention since their discovery [1]. In the past,
other intermetallic L1 2 phases like Co 3Ti [2] or Co3Ta [3] were con-
sidered as reinforcing phase, but these phases were either not
stable at high temperatures [3,4], the c/c0 lattice misﬁt was too
large [2–4] or the c0 volume fraction was rather low. In some cases
also discontinuous precipitation occurred [2,4,5] which deterio-
rates the creep properties. In contrast, Co 3(Al,W) hardened
Co-base superalloys exhibit identical microstructures as c/c0
Ni-base superalloys and show very promising properties. Due to
the higher melting point of Co, they have higher solidus and liq-
uidus temperatures [6,7] and they show less segregation [7]. The
lattice misﬁt is moderate [1,8,9], the ﬂow stress is comparable or
even higher than in Ni-base superalloys above 900 /C176C [10] and
the creep properties are similar to ﬁrst generation Ni-base superal-
loys [6,11]. Moreover, the
c0 solvus temperature in Co-base super-
alloys is usually lower compared to that in Ni-base superalloys
though a higher c0 volume fraction can be achieved at typical appli-
cation temperatures of wrought alloys. This makes them very
interesting high temperature structural materials with high
strength that are suitable to be produced by conventional casting
and hot forming. However, the previously investigated c/c0
Co-base superalloys have a poor oxidation resistance [12] and high
density [13]. The goal of this work was to develop polycrystalline
c/c0 Co-base superalloys with good workability, high strength and
good oxidation resistance to compete with polycrystalline wrought
Ni-base superalloys.
Therefore, two Co-base superalloys, CoWAlloy1 and CoWAlloy2,
are investigated and compared with the commercial wrought
Ni-base superalloys Udimet 720Li and Waspaloy ( Table 1 ).
Both Udimet 720Li and Waspaloy exhibit a polycrystalline
two-phase c/c0 microstructure and are widely used in gas turbine
applications. Udimet 720Li is a high strength alloy due to the high
c0 fraction and Waspaloy has a comparably high creep strength for
its rather low c0 fraction [14,15].
To improve the properties of ternary Co–Al–W alloys several
alloying elements are added: Ni is used to stabilize the Co 3(Al,W)
phase and to widen the narrow c/c0 two-phase region [16]. The ele-
ments Ti, Ta and Hf partition to the c0 phase [17–20] so they
enhance the phase stability and increase slightly the c0 solvus tem-
perature [5,6,21]. Ti replaces W in c0 and is therefore used for low-
ering the density [5]. Ta is beneﬁcial for improving the c0 hardness
and the yield strength [10,20]. Cr is essential for oxidation and cor-
rosion resistance and partitions preferentially to the c matrix [1]
where it acts as solid solution hardener. B improves the grain
boundary strength, ductility [22] and oxidation resistance [12].S i
reduces the oxide scale thickness [12] and supports the formation
of chromia oxide scale by the formation of silica [23,24]. Zr and C
are used for grain boundary strengthening and formation of
carbides.

annealed at 1100 /C176C and hot rolled at Vacuumschmelze GmbH &

Differential scanning calorimetry analysis (DSC) was conducted
on disc specimens with 4 mm diameter and a mass of about
110 mg with a heating/cooling rate of 5 K/min using a Netzsch
STA 409 CD for determining the phase transformation tempera-
tures. The density was measured using Archimedes’ principle.
The characterization of the microstructure was carried out in a
Zeiss Crossbeam 1540 EsB focused ion beam microscope. Electron
backscatter diffraction was performed with a Nordlys2 detector
of Oxford Instruments for measuring the grain size. Samples were
cut from the hot rolled and heat treated bars, and subsequently
ground and polished. Compression tests were performed at RT,
600 /C176C, 750 /C176C, 800 /C176C and 850 /C176C on cylindrical specimens with
3 mm diameter and 4.5 mm length to determine the yield strength.
The high temperature creep strength was investigated in compres-
sion on cylindrical samples at 750 /C176C/620 MPa. The oxidation
behavior was characterized on polished samples by oxidation tests
at 750 /C176C for 1000 h and at 900 /C176C for 50 h.
The DSC results show that the temperature range between c0
solvus and solidus temperature, giving the solution heat treatment
and processing window, of both CoWAlloys is similar to that of
Waspaloy but much larger than that of Udimet 720Li ( Fig. 1 ). At
the same time the c0 fraction is higher than in typical wrought
Ni-base superalloys, although the c0 solvus is in the range or even
lower than that of Waspaloy and Udimet 720Li and similar to that
of the polycrystalline powder metallurgy Co–Ni superalloy
recently developed by Knop et al. [17].
This provides the possibility to produce high strength superal-
loys with good processability. The 40 /C176C higher c0 solvus tempera-
ture of CoWAlloy1 compared to CoWAlloy2 is due to the higher
contents of Ti and Ta [5,6,13,21].
After hot rolling and heat treatment both Co-base superalloys
show a recrystallized microstructure with ﬁne grains and car-
bides/borides at the grain boundaries ( Fig. 2 ). The median grain
size of both CoWAlloys is below 8
lm and similar to that of
Udimet 720Li, while Waspaloy has much bigger grains of about
33 lm. Primary c0 precipitates with a size of 0.5 lm and 0.2 lm,
that are useful to pin the grain boundaries during the sub-solvus
recrystallization heat treatment, and secondary c0 precipitates with
a size of about 60 nm are present in CoWAlloy1 and CoWAlloy2.
The different c0 shapes indicate a larger c/c0 lattice misﬁt in
CoWAlloy1 than in CoWAlloy2. This is mainly ascribed to the
higher content of Ti and Ta in CoWAlloy1 which do not only parti-
tion preferentially to c0 but also increase strongly its lattice param-
eter [5,9]. The density of both Co-alloys is around 8.8 g/cm 3 which
is much lower compared to other c/c0 Co-base superalloys [13]
with CoWAlloy1 having a slightly lower density as a consequence
of the stronger replacement of W by Ti.
Fig. 3 a shows the yield strength of all investigated alloys
together with literature data [10,29].
CoWAlloy1 and CoWAlloy2 possess a much higher compressive
yield strength than the conventional carbide and solid solution
hardened Co-alloy Mar-M509 [29] and c/c0 Co-base superalloy C
o–8.8Al–9.8W–2Ta [10] which is attributed to the presence of c0
precipitates and the smaller grain size, respectively. In comparison
to Udimet 720Li and Waspaloy a similar strength is achieved
although the c0 fraction is higher. This could be caused by different
deformation mechanisms and generally lower anti-phase bound-
ary (APB) and stacking fault (SF) energies, as found in single crys-
talline Co-base superalloys [30], that reduce the resistance of the c0
precipitates against penetration. At temperatures T P 800 /C176C
CoWAlloy1 exhibits an even higher yield strength compared to
Udimet 720Li. This shows that the strengthening effect of the c0
precipitates in Co-base superalloys is shifted towards higher tem-
peratures although the
c0 solvus is typically lower. Suzuki et al. [10]
observed similar results for a Co–8.8Al–9.8W–2Ta alloy, and con-
cluded that this is caused by the high density of stacking faults
formed in the
c0 precipitates above the peak temperature. Knop
et al. [17] stated that the observed shift of the breakdown of the
ﬂow stress anomaly to higher temperatures in their polycrystalline
Co–Ni superalloy is possibly caused by the high W content that
restricts diffusion and hence thermally activated slip in the c0
precipitates.
The excellent mechanical properties of both c/c0 Co-base super-
alloys become particularly evident when comparing the creep
properties at 750 /C176C( Fig. 3 b).

Waspaloy which is most likely due to the higher c0 fraction. But
they even have a superior creep strength compared to

minimum creep rate is about 30 (CoWAlloy1) respectively 2
(CoWAlloy2) times lower than that of Udimet 720Li. This means
that both CoWAlloys possess even signiﬁcantly higher creep
strength when the creep strength is related to the density, as the
density of the CoWAlloys is less than 10% higher than that of the
investigated Ni-base superalloys. The reason for this high creep
strength could be the high content of slowly diffusing W [31] that
retards diffusion controlled processes, the fact that less primary c0
particles are present at the grain boundaries compared to
Udimet 720Li, which means that the intragranular
c0 precipitate
fraction is much higher, or different creep deformation mecha-
nisms. In comparison with each other, CoWAlloy1 shows a signif-
icantly higher strength than CoWAlloy2 which can be a result of
the strengthening effect of Ti and Ta [10,20], which are also known
to increase the APB energies in Ni 3Al and thus the resistance
against shearing [32]. Further reasons could be the larger lattice
misﬁt or other effects such as differences in SF energies.
At this point it’s also worth to mention that it’s known from
Ni-base superalloys that single crystalline [33] and polycrystalline
[34] superalloys can exhibit a tension/compression asymmetry in
yield and creep strength. Sondhi et al. [34] ascribed the tension/-
compression asymmetry in the polycrystalline turbine disc
superalloy IN 100 to the internal stresses due to the different
lattice parameters of c and c0 . This means that the observed
mechanical properties may differ to those under tension, however,
since all alloys investigated in this study should have a positive
lattice misﬁt, such as Udimet 720Li [35], the order of the investi-
gated alloys in terms of tensile creep strength should hardly be
affected by this factor.
However, not only superior creep properties are required for
high temperature applications, but also a good oxidation resis-
tance. Both CoWAlloys exhibit a similar oxide scale morphology
(Fig. 4 a) and a strongly improved oxidation resistance compared
to previously investigated c/c0 Co-base superalloys [12,13]. The for-
mation of thick Co-oxides at the surface could be suppressed and
the upper oxide layer consists of a protective chromium oxide with
a thinner silicon oxide underneath. Si therefore seems to con-
tribute to the good oxidation resistance which is consistent with
literature [23,24]. The innermost oxide scale consists of an irregu-
lar but continuous alumina layer and no phase transformation of
the c/c0 microstructure has taken place which indicates good phase
stability. The oxide scale is more than one order of magnitude thin-
ner than that of the ternary c/c0 Co-alloy Co–9Al–9W [12] and even
slightly thinner than that of Udimet 720Li and Waspaloy after
annealing for 50 h at 900 /C176C( Fig. 4 b).
At lower temperatures of 750 /C176C the oxide scale morphology is
identical after heating for 1000 h and an oxide scale thickness of
only 10 lm and 6 lm for CoWAlloy1 and CoWAlloy2 is present
which shows again their good potential.
In summary, new c0 hardened Co-base superalloys with promis-
ing properties have been developed. Due to the very high solidus
temperature and low solvus temperature CoWAlloy1 and
CoWAlloy2 possess a large processing window. After hot rolling
and heat treatment, a microstructure with ﬁne grain size and
high c0 fraction of more than 50% is generated. The yield strength
of both CoWAlloys is quite high and that of CoWAlloy1 even
exceeding Waspaloy. At 750 /C176C both Co-alloys show excellent
creep strength with CoWAlloy1 being by far superior to the
investigated Ni-base superalloys. The oxidation resistance of
CoWAlloy1 and CoWAlloy2 is much better than that of previous
Co3(Al,W) hardened Co-base superalloys due to a high Cr content
and additions of Si. These results show that the newly developed
Co-base superalloys are promising cast and wrought alloys with
a high temperature capability in particular at temperatures above
700 /C176C.
