ABSTRACT
A novel Ti –Ni–Nb–Zr quaternary ﬁller alloy with the composition of Ti-(19~25)Ni-(15~25)(Nb þ Zr) (wt.%) was
designed. The ﬁller alloy was composed of (Ti,Nb)ss, (Ti,Zr,Nb)ss þ (Ti,Zr)2Ni, α-Ti and Ti 2Ni phases. It was
fabricated into ﬁller foil with a thickness of about 45 μm by a rapid solidi ﬁcation technique. The results indicate
that the liquidus temperature of the Ti –Ni–Nb–Zr brazing alloy was about 978 oC, and the brazing alloy presented
excellent wettability on TiAl substrate. The TiAl joint mainly consisted of β/B2 phase, Ti 2Al, Ti2AlNi and α2-Ti3Al
phases. The diffusion of Al atom from base metal to brazed seam led to the formation of Ti 2Al and Ti 2AlNi.
Considering that no previously references on XRD pattern of Ti 2AlNi compound can be found, Ti 2AlNi cast alloy
specimen was specially prepared and XRD peaks were specially labeled. The micro-hardness and bending strength
tests of the Ti 2AlNi phase were carried out, and the results were 761 HV and 192 MPa, respectively. The brazing
parameters of 1010 oC/10 min offered the joint shear strength of 280 MPa at room temperature, and the joints
exhibited tensile strength of 372 MPa at room temperature and 340 MPa at 750 oC, indicating that the newly
developed ﬁller alloy could offer a stable high-temperature strength.
1. Introduction
Due to its attractive properties such as relatively low density, high
speciﬁc strength and good oxidation resistance [ 1,2], TiAl alloy is
considered as one of the most promising high temperature structure
materials in aerospace ﬁeld, with long-term service temperature of 750
oC~800 oC, and it can be a promising material candidate to replace nickel
based superalloys when weight reduction and enhanced temperature
capability are critical for structural design [ 3,4]. As a typical application
example in aerospace ﬁeld, in metal thermal protection structures the
joining between Ni-based honeycomb and panels made by TiAl alloys is
needed. Generally speaking, the joint strength should reach up to
60~80% of the strength of TiAl alloys. However, its poor workability and
intrinsic high brittleness make it dif ﬁcult to manufacture large-scale and
complex components. In order to promote its practical utilization, joining
is an extremely important processing technology.
In the past over two decades, several welding and joining methods
had been applied to join TiAl alloys, including laser welding [ 5], electron
beam welding [6], diffusion bonding [ 7,8] and brazing [ 9,10]. Due to its
poor ductility and damage tolerance, it is believed that brazing is the
suitable joining technique. Nevertheless, intermetallic compounds easily
formed in the brazed joint because of the high reaction activity of TiAl
alloy. For instance, in TiAl joint brazed with AgCu [ 11] and AgCuTi [ 12]
ﬁller metals, Al –Cu–Ti system compounds frequently formed and the
joint properties at high temperatures were insuf ﬁcient.
Ti(Zr)-based ﬁller metals have good compatibility on TiAl alloy and
have been widely used to braze this material. For example, Lee et al.
studied the brazing of TiAl with Ti –15Cu–15Ni (wt.%) ﬁller at 1100
oC~1200 oC[ 13], and the joint compressive strength reached up to 319
MPa at room temperature. Ti-Zr-Ni-Cu [ 14], Ti-Zr-Cu-Ni-Co [ 15] and
Ti–Zr–Fe [16] system ﬁller alloys were also investigated, and the TiAl/-
TiAl joint shear strength varied from 220 MPa to 316 MPa.
In addition, Lin et al. studied the joining of TiAl intermetallics by
transient liquid phase (TLP) bonding technique using Ti/Ni ﬁller metal
[17].The Ti/Ni ﬁller metal consisted of pure Ti foil and Ni foil. To achieve
sufﬁcient diffusion effect, the bonding temperature high up to 1150 oC
was applied. TiNi –Nb(V) eutectic brazing alloys have been designed to
join TiAl alloy [ 18,19], but τ3-Al3NiTi2 intermetallics still formed in the
joint and they limited the joint strength. In refer. [ 20], high
Nb-containing TiAl alloy was joined by transient liquid phase (TLP)
bonding method using Ti –24Ni and Ti –29Fe (at.%) ﬁllers, and the ho-
mogenized joint microstructure could be obtained after annealing at
1000 oC for 168 h, but joint strength was not reported.

2. Metallurgical basis for composition design of Ti –Ni–Nb–Zr
quaternary ﬁller alloy
Based on the reaction characteristics between different elements and
previous studies, the Ti –Ni–Nb–Zr system ﬁller was designed to braze
TiAl alloy. Table 1 shows some Ti-base ﬁller metals designed and used in
the past decade. According to the Ti –Ni binary phase diagram ( Fig. 1a),
there is a eutectic reaction between Ti and Ni at 942 oC with eutectic
compositions of Ti-24 at.% (Ti-28 wt%) Ni [ 21]. Hence, The Ti-28 wt%
Ni alloy was used to braze TiAl alloy [ 22,23]. The solidus temperature
(Ts) and the liquidus temperature (T l) of this eutectic brazing alloy were
about 940 oC and 980 oC, respectively [ 22]. The addition of Ni into
Ti-based ﬁllers is bene ﬁcial to decrease their melting points.
Elements Nb and Ti are completely soluble with each other, and there
is a small amount of Nb atoms in the used TiAl alloy. The addition of Nb
in ﬁller alloys could contribute to the metallurgical bonding between the
TiAl base metal and ﬁller alloys. As can be seen in the Ni–Nb binary phase
diagram (Fig. 1b), an eutectic reaction between Ni and Nb occurs at 1175
oC with eutectic compositions of Ni-40.5 at.% Nb. In fact, in Ti –Ni–Nb

According to the above reports, the addition of Ni in Ti-based alloy is
beneﬁcial to decrease its melting point. However, it should be pointed
out that high content of Ni will increase the brittleness of brazing alloy
and promote the formation of containing Ni intermetallics in TiAl joints.
Therefore, the content of Ni should be specially controlled. Besides, High
content of Nb has an effect on the liquidus temperature of the ﬁller alloy
and the reaction during the brazing process.
Elements Zr and Ti have similar atomic radius and crystal structure,
and they are completely soluble with each other. The addition of Zr in Ti-
based ﬁller metals is widely applied in order to decrease the melting
point, such as Ti-Zr-Cu-Ni [ 14] and Ti –Zr–Cu–Ni–Co [15] brazing ﬁllers.
Hence, it is inferred that the addition of Zr would further decrease the
liquid temperature of Ti 40Ni40Nb20 (at.%) below to 1150 oC.
Hence, based on the reported Ti –Ni–Nb alloys, the in ﬂuence of ele-
ments on the melting points and the formation of brittle phases in joint,
Ti–Ni–Nb–Zr quaternary brazing ﬁller was proposed in this study.
Compared to the TiNi –Nb(V) eutectic brazing alloys [ 18,19], the content
of Ni has been decreased greatly. The content of Ni was lower than 25 wt
% and that of Nb was lower than 18 wt%.
Moreover, considering the oxidation resistance of TiAl alloy in high
temperatures and the in ﬂuence of heat process on the properties of this
alloy, the brazing temperature was looked forward to be lower than 1150
oC. The lower liquidus temperature means a lower brazing temperature,
which makes easier control of the dissolution and reaction between the
TiAl base metal and ﬁller alloy. And hence the liquidus temperature of
the Ti –Ni–Nb–Zr system ﬁller should not be exceeded 1100 oC.

4. Results and discussion
4.1. Characterization of Ti –Ni–Nb–Zr ﬁller alloy
The differential thermal analysis (DTA) result of the Ti –Ni–Nb–Zr
alloy is shown in Fig. 3a. As can be seen in the ﬁgure, four temperature
points of 899 oC, 924 oC, 941 oC and 978 oC were labeled at the curve. It
was inferred that the melting range of the Ti –Ni–Nb–Zr alloy was 899
oC~978 oC.
To further study the melting process and the wettability behavior, the
dynamic and static wettability tests on TiAl substrate were conducted. The
results in Fig. 3 b display that the contact angles decreased as the tem-
perature rises. During the heating process from 923oCt o9 5 0oC the contact
angles decreased quickly. At the temperature of 923 oC the contact angle
was 48.2/C14 (Fig. 4b). As the Ti –Ni–Nb–Zr alloy was heated to 950 oC, the
contact angle decreased to 27.8/C14 (Fig. 4c). However, when the temperature
increased from 950 oCt o9 7 8 oC the contact angle only slightly decreased
to 26.8/C14 (Fig. 4d). As the specimen was further heated the contact angles
decreased quickly again. When the temperature increased to 1010 oCt h e
contact angle decreased to the low value of 16.4 /C14 (Fig. 4e).
It is believed that the variation of contact angles was consistent with
the curve in Fig. 3a. As the Ti–Ni–Nb–Zr alloy was heated to 924 oC, some
phases in the alloy began to melt and reacted with the TiAl substrate. And
then the contact angle decreased quickly. As the temperature was lower
than 978 oC some phases in the Ti –Ni–Nb–Zr alloy were not melted
thoroughly. This limited the reaction and spreading of the alloy on TiAl
substrate. When the temperature was higher than 978 oC the
Ti–Ni–Nb–Zr alloy completely melted. And as can be seen in Fig. 4d the
silhouette of the alloy became hard to keep. Based on the experiments
and analysis, the liquidus temperature of the Ti –Ni–Nb–Zr alloy should
be 978 oC.
Fig. 3c shows the change of the contact angle on TiAl substrate with
the dwell time at 1010 oC. When the dwell time was 10 min and 20 min
the contact angles were 9.6 /C14 (Figs. 4f) and 7.4/C14 , respectively. As a whole,
Ti–Ni–Nb–Zr ﬁller alloy has a relatively low liquidus temperature of 978
oC and presented excellent wettability on TiAl substrate.

concentration gradient. And then γ-TiAl transformed to Ti 2Al phase.

Fig. 7 presents the element area distribution maps within the joint
brazed at 1010 oC for 10 min. Element Al ( Fig. 7c) uniformly distributed
in the whole joint. Although Al was absent in the original brazing alloy,
more than 20 at.% Al was detected in the joint centre. Comparing the
distribution maps of Al, Ni, Nb and Zr ( Fig. 7c~f), the diffusion of Al into
brazed seam was faster than that of Ni, Nb and Zr towards the TiAl alloy.
The reason may be that the diffusivity of atoms in molten state is much
higher than that in solid state. Moreover, on the basis of ref. [ 24], the
dissolution enthalpies of elements Ti, Ni, Nb and Zr in Al melt were
negative ( Table 4 ), indicating that Al tended to react with all the ele-
ments in the molten ﬁller and form compounds. And therefore, the dis-
tribution of Al in the whole brazed seam was relatively uniform.
As shown in Fig. 7 d~f, the distance of Zr and Nb diffusing into the
TiAl alloy was less than that of Ni atoms, demonstrating that the af ﬁnity
of Ni from the ﬁller alloy with the TiAl was rather strong. The data in
Table 4 illustrate that the dissolution enthalpies of Ni in melts of Ti and Al
were negative, which means that Ni could react with Ti and Al and form
the compounds. On the other hand, according to binary diagrams the
element Ti was soluble with Nb and Zr [ 36]. Hence, the reactivity of Nb
and Zr with TiAl base metal was lower than that of Ni. As a consequence,
the higher concentration of Ni was detected in microzone “1”.
4.3. Analysis of Ti 2AlNi phase
The determination of Ti2AlNi phase in microzone “4” was hard to ﬁnd
in corresponding literatures or XRD cards. It was reported that during the
glow discharge plasma brazing of Ti –45Al–2Nb–2Mn–1B alloy, a
different irregular type of particles with a chemical composition close to
Ti2AlNi formed in the joints [ 37]. However, further information was not
mentioned. In the present study, in order to analyze the Ti 2AlNi phase,
Ti2AlNi alloy with a nominal composition of Ti –25Al–25Ni (at.%) was
specially prepared through electric arc melting method, and the ingot is
shown in Fig. 8a. Fig. 8b displays its microstructure. Table 5 indicate that
the predominant phase of this alloy was Ti 2AlNi, but there also existed a
few of α2-Ti3Al particles dissolved with Ni. The cross section areas of
Ti2AlNi and α2-Ti3Al phases in Fig. 8 b were calculated through Photo-
shop software, giving the Ti 2AlNi area fraction value of about 94.6%. As
is known that the element Ti has a strong af ﬁnity with Al or Ni, Ti–Ni and
then Ti–Al phases can easily form during the electric arc melting process.
Therefore it was not strange to detect the small amount of α2-Ti3Al phase.

5. Conclusions
Based on the Ti –Ni and Ni –Nb binary phase diagrams and previously
reported TiNi –Nb brazing ﬁllers, quaternary ﬁller metal with a compo-
sition of Ti-(19~25)Ni-(15~25)(Nb þ Zr) (wt.%) has been designed to
be used as joining TiAl alloy.
(1) The microstructure of Ti –Ni–Nb–Zr ﬁller alloy consists of (Ti, Nb)
ss, (Ti, Zr)ss þ (Ti, Zr) 2Ni, α-Ti and Ti 2Ni phase.The liquidus
temperature of the ﬁller alloy is about 978 oC. And the
Ti–Ni–Nb–Zr ﬁller alloy presents excellent wettability on TiAl
substrate at 1010 oC.
(2) Sound brazed TiAl/TiAl joint microstructure has obtained under
the condition of 1010 oC/5~60 min. The TiAl joint is composed of
β/B2 phase, Ti2Al, Ti2AlNi and α2-Ti3Al phases. The diffusion of Al
leads to the formation of Ti 2Al and Ti 2AlNi. The Ti 2AlNi phase
displays a hardness value of 761 HV, and the Ti 2AlNi compound
specimen exhibits a three-point bending strength of 192 MPa.
(3) The average tensile strength of TiAl joints brazed with the
Ti–Ni–Nb–Zr quaternary ﬁller metal at 1010 oC for 10min is 372
MPa at room temperature, and the joint strength of 340 MPa is
retained at 750 oC, about 91.2% of the room-temperature
strength.
