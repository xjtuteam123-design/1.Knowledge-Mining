1. Introduction 
Cast polycrystalline nickel-based superalloys are widely used in high 
temperature components in turbine blades and aerospace fields owing to 
their excellent performance at elevated temperature and relatively low 
industrial production cost [ 1 , 2 ]. In nickel-based superalloys, boron and 
carbon are often added as grain boundary strengthening elements to 
improve high temperature strength and creep properties [ 3 – 6 ]. Conse -
quently, most polycrystalline nickel-based superalloys contain a certain 
amount of boron and carbon. Mar-M247 polycrystalline nickel-based 
superalloy is a type of solid solution and precipitation strengthening 
superalloy which is used extensively in gas turbine blades, rotors, sta -
tors, etc. And its excellent performances are related to the structural 
hardening induced by the ordered γ’ phase with L1
2 
structure [ 7 – 10 ]. 
For these precipitation and solid-solution strengthening nickel-based 
superalloys, there are often demands for bonding in industry produc -
tion and commercial applications. Among various kinds of bonding 
methods, transient liquid phase (TLP) bonding is an advantageous 
measure. For instance, the microstructures and mechanical properties of 
the joint bonded by TLP bonding are close to that of the base alloys, thus 
can avoid structural inhomogeneity. Meanwhile, it is economically 
attractive to use TLP bonding in welding thermal components [ 11 , 12 ]. 
During TLP bonding process, the intermediate layer containing high 
diffusion rate elements such as boron, silicon and phosphorus is sand -
wiched between the matrix. These high diffusion rate elements are also 
serviced as melting point depressant (MPD) elements of matrix that are 
added in the interlayer. Then, the entire assembly is heated to the 
bonding temperature higher than the melting point of intermediate filler 
while lower than the substrate in a vacuum furnace. On the bonding 
preservation at bonding temperature, the MPD elements in the filler

And it has previously been investigated the microstructural evolution

joints [ 11 , 27 , 28 ]. It had been proved that the TLP bonding was one of 
the most extraordinary methods for bonding superalloys. The pre -

important impact on the mechanical properties. However, there are still 
some unclear and controversial points about the microstructure and 
precipitation in the TLP joint of polycrystalline Mar-M247. Although 
post bond heat treatment is considered to be an effective method for 
removing precipitated phases that are deleterious to the mechanical 
properties of joints, there would still be some precipitation residues in 
the joint [ 11 , 29 , 30 ]. These precipitates easily become the fracture crack 
initiation sites. From TLP bonding process to post bond heat treatment, 
the precipitates in the joint would transfer from precipitation to anni -
hilation in order to produce the ideal TLP joint. So it has scientific and 
practical values to investigate the microstructure and precipitation 
behavior of TLP joints bonded for short holding time. In addition, the 
microstructural characteristics of precipitates in bonding regions could 
provide a significant basis for further post bond heat treatment. There -
fore, the investigation on the microstructure and precipitation behavior 
of TLP joints is of necessity.

attempted to reveal the formation mechanism of these types of pre -
cipitates as well. 
2. Materials and methods 
The base metal used in this work was polycrystalline Mar-M247 Ni- 
based superalloy. A commercial Ni-Cr-B amorphous foil MBF-80 with 
thickness of 40 μ m was employed as the interlayer alloy for TLP 
bonding. The nominal chemical compositions of Mar-M247 and MBF-80 
interlayer are listed in the Table 1 . Base metal with dimensions of 20 
mm × 20 mm × 20 mm was cut via electric discharge processing and

Microstructure characterization of the welded joints was conducted 
by using scanning electron microscopy (SEM), electron backscatter 
diffraction (EBSD) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM). Sam -
ples for SEM observations were prepared by mechanically grinding on 
abrasive paper followed by electropolishing in a solution of 10% 
perchloric acid and 90% under a voltage of 20 V at ambient temperature. 
SEM images and composition analysis were operated on a FEI Nova 
NanoSEM 450 SEM. And EBSD characterization was conducted on FEI 
Nova NanoSEM 450 operated at 20 kV with a step size of 1.2 μ m. TEM

grinding and subsequent twin-jet electrochemical polishing in the same 
electrolyte for SEM samples and 2) site-specifically using standard 
focused ion beam (FIB) lift-out and thinning methods on Helios PFIB 
CXe. Before FIB cutting, a layer of protective Pt layer was deposited on 
the surface in order to minimize Ga ion damage. The FIB lamellae were 
cut and positioned on Cu grids. Bright-field transmission electron mi -

pattern were performed on FEI Tecnai Spirit TEM T12 operated at

The nucleation mechanism of the eutectic dual-phase structure is still 
unclear at present. During the bonding process, some reports [ 32 ] sug -
gest that it seems to be more realistic to suppose that both solid/liquid 
equilibration and solid state diffusion processes carry on simultaneously 
rather than sequentially, while others [ 36 ] suggest that the solidification 
and solid state precipitation are in a certain sequence. However, 
comprehensively analyzing the results of EBSD and TEM, one possible 
mechanism might be used as an explanation. At the beginning of the TLP 
bonding, boron atoms would diffuse from the interlayer to the matrix, 
causing subsequent isothermal solidification of the joint. Meanwhile, 
due to the change of melting point, the dissolution of matrix makes the 
alloying elements in base metal like Ta and Hf to diffuse into the liquid 
phase, and this diffusion can be driven by concentration gradient. In 
insufficient bonding time to complete isothermal solidification process, 
a portion of the liquid phase in the vicinity of the matrix begin to solidify 
in an orientation that is close to the matrix. With the solidification of this 
portion of the liquid phase, solid phases discharge some solute atoms 
into the liquid phase, meanwhile there are still non-diffused elements in 
the centerline joint from the interlayer. When the temperature reaches 
the eutectic temperature, the remaining liquid phase in the centerline 
region finally solidifies into eutectic-type solidification. The eutectic 
nucleus near the matrix and solid solution side may grow in an orien -
tation close to the orientation of matrix. And it is speculated that the 
centerline liquid phase is generally supercooled, each grain in this re -
gion could nucleate and grow independently. These nuclei grow up until 
they get close to each other. At the same time, the solid solution grains 
on both sides of the matrix grow until reaching other grains. This growth 
and crystallization mechanism might lead to random orientation dis -
tribution of grains in the joint, while adjacent grains in contact with each 
other to grow up could have the cube-on-cube orientation relationship 
[ 37 ]. Based on the SAED patterns, the lattice parameter of the M

3.3. Microhardness distribution of TLP joint 
Microhardness distribution is a good indicator of mechanical prop -
erties of the joint. Fig. 8 shows the typical hardness distribution in the 
bonding joint. In the hardness distribution of TLP joint bonding for 5 
min, the hardness peak appears in the eutectic zone, which indicates that 
the NSZ has the highest hardness. Generally speaking, fractures always 
initiate at brittle precipitates sites and propagate along the weak-bonded 
interface between eutectic phases and matrix, and cause brittle fracture 
characteristics. Thus, the mechanical properties of joints with contin -
uous or discontinuous NSZ are usually unstable [ 28 , 59 ]. From NSZ, ISZ 
to DAZ, the value of hardness gradually decreases, and ISZ region is 
similar to a transition buffer zone on hardness. Intuitively, the hardness 
of ISZ is higher than that of DAZ and matrix. This may be due to the fact 
that the interdiffusion of Al, Mo and other matrix elements to ISZ in -
creases the precipitation hardening and solid solution hardening effects 
[ 15 ]. The hardness at the ISZ/DAZ interface is slightly higher than that 
of matrix, and this may be related to the precipitation of the dispersed 
granular and acicular borides at the ISZ/DAZ interface. Although the 
bonding temperature has reached 1230 
◦
C, the γ / γ’ matrix cannot 
consume so much B elements that B quickly reaches saturation [ 21 ]. The 
study of B segregation in austenite system proved this point [ 60 ]. B has a 
stronger affinity for the distribution to the precipitated phases rather 
than remaining in the matrix solid solution. Importantly, the enrichment 
of these hard precipitates is harmful to the mechanical behavior of the 
joint, especially the fatigue performance. Therefore, these precipitates 
are sought to be removed by extending the holding time and/or subse -
quent post bond heat treatment. In contrast to joints for short time of 5 
min, the hardness distribution of TLP joints for longer bonding time of 6 
h is also supplemented in Fig. 8 . The isothermal solidification process of 
the joints with longer holding time is more sufficient, and the precipi -
tated phases in the joints are less [ 11 , 28 ]. In the hardness distribution of 
joint for bonding time of 6 h, the hardness of the center at the original 
NSZ has been significantly reduced and becomes close to the hardness of 
matrix. The hardness of ISZ is also slightly higher than that of matrix but 
lower than that of DAZ. In this joint, DAZ corresponds to the highest 
hardness of the joint, but it is not much higher than that of matrix. The 
hardness distribution of joints becomes more homogeneous by extend -
ing the TLP holding time. And in the investigation on the relationship 
between the microstructure and mechanical properties of the TLP joints 
[ 61 ], bonding failure could be attributed to the insufficient γ’ formation.
