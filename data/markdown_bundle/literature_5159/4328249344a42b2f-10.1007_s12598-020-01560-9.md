Pseudo-spinodal mechanism approach to designing a near- β high-
strength titanium alloy through high-throughput technique
Li-Gang Zhang, Jing-Lu Tang, Zhen-Yu Wang, Jing-Ya Zhou, Di Wu* ,
Li-Bin Liu* , Patrick J. Masset
Received: 2 January 2020 / Revised: 25 March 2020 / Accepted: 29 July 2020
© The Nonferrous Metals Society of China and Springer-Verlag GmbH Germany, part of Springer Nature 2020
Abstract The main reason for the high strength in near- β
titanium alloys is the ultraﬁne precipitation of the acicular
secondary α phase in the β matrix. The purpose of this
study is to use the pseudo-spinodal mechanism to obtain
the ultraﬁne α phase for the design of a new high-strength
near-β titanium alloy. Thermodynamic calculations and
TC21-(TC21?15Mo) diffusion couple composition gra-
dient experiments were used to demonstrate that TC21 ?
3Mo alloy can undergo a pseudo-spinodal decomposition
to obtain the ultraﬁne α phase, resulting in a high-strength
alloy. By adjusting the heat treatment process to obtain a
bimodal microstructure, the alloy exhibits a good balance
between ultimate tensile strength (1351 MPa) and plasticity
(8.5% strain). Thus, it was demonstrated that the pseudo-
spinodal mechanism combined with a high-throughput
diffusion couple technique is an effective method for
designing high-strength titanium alloys.
Keywords High-strength titanium alloy; TC21 ?xMo;
Diffusion couple; Pseudo-spinodal mechanism;
Microstructure and mechanical properties
1 Introduction
Titanium alloys have been applied in diverse ﬁelds, espe-
cially in aerospace structure parts due to their high
strength, low density and excellent corrosion resistant
properties. β and near- β titanium alloys have become a
development direction of high-strength titanium alloys
because a good combination of strength and ductility can
be obtained due to the signiﬁcant strengthening effect after
aging treatment. The main reason for the high strength of β
and near- β titanium alloys is the ﬁne secondary α phases
precipitated in the β matrix. It leads to the formation of a
large amount of α/β phase boundaries within the β matrix,
which effectively hinder dislocation slip and increase the
alloy strength [ 1–8].
Recently, Boyne et al. [ 9] successfully explained the
formation of a dense population of ﬁne intragranular α
precipitates when the Timetal 21S alloy (Ti–15Mo–3Al–
2.7Nb–0.3Si, wt%) is step-quenched below a critical tem-
perature with the phase ﬁeld simulation results. When the
system is close enough to the point at which the α and β
phases have the same free energy, ﬂuctuation-assisted
partitionless transformation from β to α becomes the
dominant precipitation pathway. Consequently, a rapid
increase in nucleation rate occurs, resulting in a ﬁne dis-
tribution of numerous intragranular α precipitates. This
partitionless transformation can also be reached either by
variation of aging temperature at ﬁxed alloy composition or
by variation of composition at ﬁxed aging temperature and

Ti-5553 alloy (Ti–5Al–5Mo–5V–3Cr–0.5Fe). The precip-

spinodal nucleation composition, to obtain ultraﬁne α
phases in the microstructure and to improve the strength of
titanium alloys will be an effective research idea for tita-
nium alloys development.
However, until now, there are almost no cases of
designing high-strength titanium alloys using pseudo-
spinodal nucleation mechanism. Two reasons may be
mentioned to explain it: (i) the lack of high precision
thermodynamic database of multi-component titanium
alloy makes it difﬁcult to obtain the free energy curves of α
and β phase in the alloy accurately; (ii) the composition
range of alloys that can undergo pseudo-spinodal decom-
position is very narrow [ 10] and is difﬁcult to be found
accurately by traditional individual alloy composition
method. The development of high-throughput experimental
technology is conducive to rapid exploration of the
microstructure and properties of various alloy components.
A diffusion couple method, which can form a continuous
composition gradient in one sample, was used to ﬁnd the
critical Mo content at which the α and β phases have the
same free energy, and design the alloy which will form a
dense population of ﬁne intragranular α precipitates
throughout pseudo-spinodal nucleation mechanism
[11, 12].
In this study, we have designed a new near- β high-
strength alloy with the pseudo-spinodal nucleation mech-
anism and high-throughput experiment. The alloy pre-
served a dense population of ﬁne intragranular α
precipitates and high strength. More importantly, the alloy
obtained a good combination of strength and ductility by
improving heat treatment.
2 Experimental
Combined with the thermodynamic database developed in
our group [ 13–15], the Gibbs free energy curves of α and β
phases of TC21 ?xMo alloys at 600 °C were calculated
using the Thermo-Calc software. The TC21 titanium alloy
came from the ﬁnished ingot provided by Northwest
Research Institute of Non-Ferrous Metals, China. The alloy
samples of TC21 ?15Mo were fabricated through a vac-
uum non-consumable arc melting method in our laboratory.
The actual compositions of the two alloys are shown in
Table 1.
The TC21 and TC21 ?15Mo alloys were cut, grind and
polished. The TC21-(TC21 ?15Mo) diffusion couple was
produced by tightly stacking this block together (Fig. 1)
with a steel ﬁxture. The contact surface area of the diffu-
sion couple was 100 mm

estimated to be between 50 and 100 MPa. Then, the sam-
ples were treated at 1000 °C for 600 h under vacuum.
During this process, Mo diffuses between the two blocks
with different compositions, where Mo content continu-
ously changes with the diffusion distance obtaining a dif-
fusion couple. After that, the samples were heat-treated at
600 °C for 6 h. The hardness of the sample was measured
by Bu¨ hler 5104 microhardness system with indentation
load of 500 mN and holding time of 15 s every 150 μm
from 50 μm away from the boundary of the (TC21 ?
15Mo)-TC21 diffusion couple. Electron probe micro-ana-

alloy near the point. Microstructure analysis was performed
on a NOVATM Nano SEM 230 ﬁeld-emission scanning
electron microscope (FESEM) with acceleration voltage of
20 kV and operating distance of 5 mm. According to the
experimental results, the alloy composition with the highest
microhardness and a dense population of ﬁne intragranular
α precipitates was Ti–6Al–2Zr–2Sn–6Mo–1Cr–2Nb, which
was corresponding to TC21 ?3Mo and was selected as a
candidate alloy composition for further study.
Then, an ingot with 160 mm in diameter and composi-

phase transition point was measured and lay between 920

3 Result and discussion
3.1 Microstructure and properties of diffusion couple
Figure 2 presents the concentration of the composition of
different elements as a function of distance of (TC21 ?
15Mo)-TC21 diffusion couple. Obviously, the contents of
Mo and Ti change continuously with the diffusion distance,
and the contents of other elements (Al, Zr, Sn, Cr, Nb) are
almost invariant. The content of Mo starts from *18 wt%
and gradually decreases as the diffusion distance increases
until it approaches 2.7 wt%, with some initial ﬂuctuation,
while Ti content shows opposite tendency.
Figure 3 shows the change of microstructure of the alloy
with various Mo contents after heat treatment at 1000 °C
without aging treatment. In general, the martensite trans-
formation occurs in the matrix TC21 alloy during
quenching to obtain the α′ phase [16, 17]. As the content of
added Mo increases, the thickness of α′ phase gradually
decreases. When 3 wt% Mo is added, the single β phase
structure is obtained when the alloy is solid solution at
1000 °Ca sa β phase stabilizing element signiﬁcantly
inhibits the martensitic transformation. As Mo content
increases, the martensite transformation temperature
decreases. When the added Mo content reaches or exceeds
3 wt%, the β→α′ phase transition is completely sup-
pressed, and ﬁnally, a single β phase structure is obtained.
When the alloy with single β phase structure is subjected to
aging treatment, the precipitated α lamellar structure
mainly depends on the stability of the β phase [18]. Coarse
martensite phases in the TC21- xMo alloy are favored by
quenching from β phase region. In order to obtain dense
and ﬁne α phase and to further improve the organization, it
is necessary to explore the changes in the microstructure
and mechanical properties after aging treatment.
Figure 4 shows microstructures of the α phase with the
increase in Mo content. When the Mo content is low, thicker
α lamellar phases can be observed (Fig. 4a). As the Mo
content further increases, the α laths gradually becomes
thinner (Fig. 4b, c). When the adding Mo content is*3 wt%,
the ﬁnest α phases can be obtained (Fig. 4d). As the adding
Mo content further increases to 4 wt%, the thickness of α
laths becomes larger again (Fig.
4e). Subsequently, when
adding 8 wt% Mo in TC21 alloy, the alloy structure is close to
the single β phase structure.
The structural changes of TC21- xMo have been pre-
dicted and designed through thermodynamic calculations.
Based on the Ti-based alloy multi-component thermody-
namics database, the Gibbs free energy of the α and β
phases of the TC21 ?xMo alloy aging at 600 °C was cal-
culated (Fig. 5). The composition of TC21 ?3Mo (6 wt%
Mo in total) is calculated to be located at the junction.
When the alloy composition gradually deviates far from the
intersection point, the β→α transformation follows a par-
titionless transformation (below junction) or conventional
nucleation and growth of the equilibrium composition
(above junction). According to the theory of pseudo-spin-
odal mechanism, when the composition of alloy is at the
junction of the Gibbs free energy curves of the α phase and
the β phase, small ﬂuctuations in the composition cause a
difference in the Gibbs free energy of the two phases and
lead to a partitionless transformation from β to α followed
by decomposition. In the process of decomposition, the
concentrations of the solute lean and solute rich phases
evolve gradually toward equilibrium in a similar fashion as
in a true spinodal decomposition. In turn, it drastically
increases the nucleation rate and leads to a drastic increase

increasing the hardness of the alloy [ 19]. When the α phase

crack propagation. In addition, Wu et al. [ 20] ﬁnd that

The result is consistent with previous investigations.

[/C22111] β zone axis diffraction pattern from the sample after

aging. The elongated αs phase is dispersed uniformly in
thinner size.
A quantitative determination of thickness and content of
αs phases (Fig. 7) measured by Image J software is sum-
marized in Table 3. As the aging temperature increases
from 560 to 640 °C, the thickness of the αs phases increases
from 0.11 to 0.32 μm, while content of αs phases decreases
from 86.76 vol% to 65.53 vol%. This is because the
increase in the aging temperature will reduce the driving
force for the α phase precipitation, resulting in a reduction
of the nucleation rate. At the same time, the high aging
temperature increases the diffusion rate of alloying ele-
ments, thereby making the α phase easier to grow.
Figure 8a, c shows the microstructure after solution
temperature at 800 °C for 30 min and aging at 560, 600 and
640 °C for 6 h. Figure 8b shows a [012] β zone axis electron
diffraction corresponding to Fig. 8a. The diffraction spots
of the α phase are roughly distributed at 1/2{200} β, which
proves that the alloy microstructure only contains α and β
phases. As shown in Fig. 8a, c, d, the coarse α
p phases are
uniformly distributed on the β matrix, and many ﬁne αs
phases are precipitated around αp phases.
Table 3 shows the change of αs phase of the alloy
solution-treated at 800 °C plus different aging treatments.
There is a substantial difference among various aging
temperatures in terms of thickness of the αs phase. When
the aging temperature rises from 560 to 640 °C, the
intragranular αs phase gradually grows up from 0.06 to
0.13 μm, while content of α phase decreases from 17.71
vol% to 12.68 vol%. As the aging temperature is much
lower than the solid solution stability temperature, it is not
able to provide enough energy to signiﬁcantly change the
α
p phase which already has large layer thickness. Thus, the
main change of microstructure in the aging process is the
αs phase inside the grain [ 23–25].
Figure 9 shows the change of growing rate and driving
force of αs phase with the aging temperature. Obviously,
the growing rate rises with temperature increasing, while
the driving force gradually reduces. The decrease in
driving force means the lower nucleation rate which
results in the decrease in the content of α
s phases [ 26, 27].
In addition, the calculation results of the growth rate of the
αs phase show that the αs phase is easier to grow up when
the aging temperature increases, which is consistent with
the change of thickness of α
s phase.

the β phase grains, thereby increasing the plasticity and
allowing the alloy to coordinately deform adequately [ 33].

plus aging, the primary α phase improves the ductility of
the alloy so that the alloy strength can fully be released and
the local stress concentration can decrease. As a result, the
alloy can fracture after reaching the yield strength, thus
exhibiting better strength than the alloy solution-treated in
the β phase region.

4 Conclusion
In the current study, pseudo-spinodal nucleation mecha-
nism combined with high-throughput diffusion couple
technique was used for designing high-strength titanium
alloys. The microstructure and tensile properties of TC21 ?
3Mo alloy were analyzed in detail, and the result shows
