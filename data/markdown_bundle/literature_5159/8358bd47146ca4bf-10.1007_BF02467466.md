The need to provide high values of short-term mechanical properties in a wide temperature range, including 
ones exceeding the operational temperatures of turbine blades of gas turbine engines, is in the "shadow" of 
such characteristics as long-term strength and resistance to high-temperature salt corrosion. However, the 
attainment of high mechanical properties in corrosion-resistant high-temperature alloys cannot be treated as a 
requirement of secondary importance. The present paper is devoted to the properties of single-crystal nickel 
alloys alloyed with Cr, Co, W, Mo, Ti, AI, and Nb in various amounts. 
Parts of gas turbine engines (GTE) experience the effect 
of considerable temperature drops, especially when operating 
in transition regimes [1]. In addition, the temperature and 
power of the gas flow in actual service can increase consider- 
ably for a short time, and here the strength of the material ac- 
quires decisive importance for faultless operation of the en- 
gine. 
Multicomponent nickel alloys have a complicated hetero- 
phase structure. The high-temperature strength properties of 
nickel alloys are attained due to the high content of an inter- 
metallic "y'-phase based on Ni3(AI, Ti) and the degree of al- 
loying of this phase and the y-solid solution with strengthen- 
ing elements. 
It should be noted that when the content of Cr in a corro- 
sion-resistant nickel alloy exceeds 12- 13%, the amount of 
virtually all alloying elements has to be limited in order to 
eliminate the appearance of an embrittling or-phase with an 
unfavorable lamellar morphology in the structure of the alloy. 
The formation of a G-phase enriched with Cr, Mo, Co, and 
other elements causes depletion of these elements in the solid 
solution and hence decreases the corrosion resistance and 
strength characteristics of the alloy.

the search lbr an optimum alloying set is complicated by the 
opposite effects of the alloying elements on the corrosion re- 
sistance and the high-temperature strength. For example, in 
order to provide corrosion resistance, formation of a protec- 
tive oxide layer should occur with the participation of tita-

The production of GTE parts from nickel alloys by the 
method of single-crystal casting followed by heat treatment 
makes it possible to increase the strength parameters of the 
part both by improving the structure (absence of grain 
boundaries) and by optimizing the alloying set. The use of 
this method makes it possible to reduce the concentration of 
carbon and other strengthening elements at grain boundaries, 
which commonly decrease the melting temperature of the al- 
loy [4]. The preservation of the general laws of phase forma- 
tion in high-temperature nickel-base alloys in their produc- 
tion by the method of single-crystal casting is another posi- 
tive aspect. A drawback of the method consists in intensified 
segregation in single-crystal castings as compared to 
equiaxial polycrystalline castings [5].

nickel alloys that possess high corrosion resistance and high- 
temperature strength, we optimized the alloying system using 
the PttACOMP method for predicting structural stability [6].

The corrosion rate of the alloys was evaluated in tests 
conducted by a rapid method in a melt that consisted of a 
mixture of two salts, namely, 25% NaCI + 75% Na2SO 4, at 
900~ for 24 h. The results of the corrosion tests of the alloys 
(Fig. 3) show that the decisive role in providing resistance of 
the tested alloys to high-temperature salt corrosion belongs to 
chromium. The corrosion rates of alloys 2 and 3 with 15.1 
and 15.4% Cr were two orders of magnitude lower than for 
the rest of the alloys containing 13.0- 14.4% Cr. Molybde- 
num exerted a considerable negative effect on the corrosion 
resistance. The corrosion rate in alloy 5 with 5% Mo was 
higher than in alloys 1 and 4 with 2% Mo, although alloy 
5 had a more favorable content of Cr, Ti, and AI from the 
standpoint of corrosion resistance. 
Thus, the most rigid constraints on the composition of a 
single-crystal nickel alloy are imposed by the need to in- 
crease the corrosion resistance while preserving the structural 
stability.

CONCLUSIONS 
1. An increase in the chromium content in the tested sin- 
gle-crystal alloys from 13.0 to 15.4% is accompanied by de- 
celeration of high-temperature salt corrosion by two orders of 
magnitude and does not exert a noticeable effect on the val- 
ues of the short-term and long-term strength. 
2. In order to preserve the structural stability of the alloy 
the amount ofTi, AI, Co, and Nb additives should be reduced 
to a level that depends on their effect on the probability of the 
appearance of an undesirable o-phase. 
3. A reduction in the total titanium and aluminum content 
to 7 - 8% increases the degree of alloying of the strengthen- 
ing "/'-phase by the strengthening elements. An increase in the 
niobium content (to 0.8%) and the cobalt content (to 8%) 
causes additional solid-solution strengthening, which deter- 
mines the possibility of increasing the short-term and long- 
term strength of the nickel alloys. 
4. The use of over 2% Mo in the alloying system consi- 
dered is inexpedient from the standpoint of the strength char- 
acteristics and inadmissible from the standpoint of the corro- 
sion resistance and structural stability.
