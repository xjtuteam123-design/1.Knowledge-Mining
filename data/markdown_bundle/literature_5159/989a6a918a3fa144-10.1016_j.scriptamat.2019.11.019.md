In this work, a diffusion multiple consist of Ni-base superalloys with complex compositions rather than 
simple metals or compounds is introduce, to discover the new Ni-base polycrystalline superalloys in 
combination with high strength and microstructure stability. Additionally, machine learning based image 
recognition has been use to characterize the microstructure, especially the γ′ precipitates. The microhard- 
ness is also utilized to rank the mechanical properties of Ni-base superalloys with different compositions. 
Thus, the composition, microstructure, and property are integrated by the high throughput experiment 
to discover novel superalloys, which can also be applied to discover other alloys with complex chemical 
components.

It is acknowledged that Ni-base superalloys are still the ideal 
materials for manufacturing high temperature components in ad- 
vanced turbine engines from now on, even though the newly dis- 
covered high or medium entropy alloys show promising potential 
to commercially replace them in the unpredictable future [1–4] . 
Generally, developing superalloys with increasing strength and ser- 
vice temperature derives mainly from the requirements for boost- 
ing performance of the aero-engines, in terms of fuel eﬃciency and 
thrust-weight ratio. To realize that, over eight more kinds of al- 
loying elements are added into Ni-base superalloys than the origi- 
nal heat resisting alloy 80Ni20Cr. Thereby, the relationships among 
compositions, phases, and properties of Ni-base superalloys be- 
come increasingly complicated. As Reed et al. [5] exempliﬁed, to 
design a Ni-base single crystal superalloy added with eight or even 
more elements in the range of 0–10 wt.% and accuracy 1.0 wt.%, 
almost 10 8 experimental alloys need to be prepared and ranked, 
which can hardly be carried out by so-called “trial and error”
methods. 
To overcome abovementioned diﬃculties, numerous scientists 
have attempted to predict the composition dependent phase pre- 
cipitation and even mechanical properties before any physical ob- 
servation and test, based on the calculation of phase diagram

Thereafter, mechanical properties are modeled to optimize com-

be used to design new Ni-base superalloys with optimal target

also introduced as eﬃcient ways of Materials Genome Initiative to

enrich the thermodynamic database for CALPHD [16] . Moreover, 
the end quenching yielding gradiently distributed γ’ precipitates 
within a single specimen can be used to rank the feasibility of 
cooling method for Ni-base superalloys [17] . 
However, the majority of computationally aided models is 
based on known theories or well-established database, thus some 
potential alloys located in off-database or strengthened by un- 
known mechanisms may be missed. Additionally, present high 
throughput approaches have hardly integrated the composition- 
processing-microstructure-property to directly guide the design of 
novel alloys with complex chemistry. This work will introduce a

diffusion multiple consist of superalloys rather than simple metals 
or compounds in traditional ones [18] . Furthermore, machine 
learning based image recognition has been use to characterize the 
microstructure, especially the γ′ precipitates in Ni-base alloys. 
Thereafter the microhardness, as a micro-region analysis technique, 
is utilized to rank the mechanical properties of Ni-base superalloys 
with different compositions and microstructure and ﬁnd new al- 
loys. This integrated approach can also be applied to discover other 
novel materials with complex contents, such as Fe and Al alloys. 
Three superalloys with complex compositions are utilized to 
assemble a diffusion multiple. As listed in Table 1 , the detected 
compositions of the original alloys W2, and W3 have more con- 
tents of Co, and Mo with base alloy named W1, respectively. With- 
out adding minor elements like B, C, Zr, this diffusion multi- 
ple is aimed to discovery new polycrystalline superalloys for tur- 
bine disks. The preparation and characterization of the sample are 
schematically illustrated in Fig. 1 . In speciﬁc, the three cast su- 
peralloys were polished and placed in a Ni can, sealed by elec- 
tron beam melting in vacuum. Then the assembly was hot iso- 
statically pressed (HIPed) at 1180 °C and 150 MPa for 8 h to make 
the intimate interfaces among these pieces. Thereafter, the diffu- 
sion multiple was annealed at 1180 °C for 10 0 0 h in a vacuum glass 
tube to yield the long-range interdiffusion and gentle composition 
gradient. 
As mentioned there before, improving service temperature of 
these alloys becomes a major concern for next generation super- 
alloys. In speciﬁc, the working temperature of polycrystalline su- 
peralloys for turbine disks has increased from 750 °C to approx- 
imately 800 °C [19] , the microstructural stability at that extreme 
condition turns increasing signiﬁcant. Hence, the diffusion mul- 
tiple was ﬁnally annealed at 800 °C for 2000 h to exam the mi- 
crostructural stability at the elevated temperature. After that, the 
elemental composition at different areas was detected by Micro- 
XRF (X-ray ﬂuorescence). Moreover, microhardness at different re- 
gions was conducted by the automatic microhardness tester Qness 
Q10a, to evaluate the general strength of alloys with different com- 
positions, regarding the close relationship between hardness and 
yield strength [20 , 21] . 
Additionally, considering the A 3 B-type (wherein, A = Ni, Co, 
et al.; B = Al, Ti, Ta et al.) γ′ phase is the most commonly used 
strengthening phase in the polycrystalline superalloys, how to reg- 
ulate and stabilize the γ′ phase is deemed as a priority during de- 
signing novel Ni-base superalloys [22–24] . Thereby, the distribu- 
tions and morphologies in the sample are characterized by SEM 
and image recognition after chemical etching. 
The distributions of the major elements in the investigated do- 
main of the annealed diffusion multiples are illustrated in Fig. 2 . 
Speciﬁcally, 27.8 wt.% Co and 5.9 wt.% Mo are detected at the region 
of original W2 and W3 respectively, and the composition grades at 
the interface among W1, W2, and W3 are relatively gentle, because 
of the suﬃcient diffusion caused by the long-term annealing. Inter- 
estingly, the different concentrations of Co and Mo have moderate 
impact on the interdiffusion of other alloying elements. In speciﬁc, 
the γ′ formation elements, Al, Ti, and Ta are enriched at the Co- 
depleted regions, wherein the Ta is more likely to concentrate at 
Mo-rich areas. Moreover, the Cr and W are found segregated at 
the domains with less Mo and more Co, which is different with 
the trends of Al, Ti, and Ta. As the addition of Co decreases the 
contents of base element Ni, the change of it is right opponent to 
that of Co. 
As illustrated in Fig. 3 , the distributions and morphologies of γ′ 
in the different regions of the annealed sample dramatically vary 
with the change of compositions. For example, distributions of γ′ 
at P1 and P3, with compositions approximating to original W1 and 
W3 respectively, are bimodal and coarser, in comparison with the 
Co-rich area P1. It has ever been documented that adding Co would 
decrease the γ′ solvus temperature and thereby widen the heat 
treatment window [25–28] , thus the primary γ′ in high-Co areas 
dissolved into matrix during HIP and annealing at 1180 °C, then re- 
precipitated as secondary or tertiary γ′ with ﬁner size. Moreover, 
the reﬁned γ′ was also stabilized during the long-term anneal- 
ing at 800 °C, especially at the Co-rich areas, as the average par- 
ticle size at P1 keeps around 150 nm after annealing at 800 °C for 
20 0 0 h. Because of the high concentrations of the refractory ele- 
ments, the topologically close-packed (TCP) phases are detected at 
P3 where Mo reaches 5.9 wt.%, which are detrimental to the high- 
temperature performance of Ni-base superalloys [29] . The detailed 
discussion on the formation mechanism of TCP phases will be dis- 
cussed in the future work. 
The overall characterizations of γ′ at different regions with var- 
ious chemical compositions are plotted in Fig. 4 (a) and (b), corre- 
sponding to the area fraction and average particle size respectively. 
In general, the depletion of γ′ formation elements decreases the 
fraction of γ′ at Co-rich regions, where, however, the particle size 
is dramatically reﬁned and stabilized after annealing. To further in- 
vestigate the strength of different domains with composition vari- 
ation, the micro-hardness results are shown in Fig. 4 (c). In com- 
parison, the regions with higher Co, Mo contents present superior 
strength. For Ni-base superalloys, the precipitation strengthening 
and solid solution strengthening are the two major composition 
dependent mechanisms contributing to the overall strength. As Mo 
is a major solid solution strengthening element in Ni-base superal- 
loys, increasing that element will enhance these alloys. While ex- 
cessive such refractory element induces the precipitation of TCP 
phases at elevated temperatures and thereby deteriorate the mi- 
crostructural and mechanical stability, as shown in Fig. 4 (d). Even 
though the increased Co decreases the alloy tolerance for Al, Ti, 
Ta and deduces the γ′ fractions to some extends, the reﬁned 
and stabilized γ′ particles enhance the precipitation strengthening 
effects. 
In general, the contents of Co, Mo in base alloy W1 with less 
modiﬁcation on other elements are suggested to be in range of 20–
27.8 wt.% and 2.9–4.5 wt.% respectively, regarding both mechanical 
strength and microstructure stability.
