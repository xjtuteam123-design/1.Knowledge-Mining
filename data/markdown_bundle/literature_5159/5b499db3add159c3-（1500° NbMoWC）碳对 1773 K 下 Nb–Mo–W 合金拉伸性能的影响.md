Abstract
Ternary Nb±Mo±W alloys of various composition and carbon and oxygen contents were prepared to investigate the deformation and
fracture behavior by tensile and compression tests at 1773 K. The fracture mode changes from transgranular to intergranular with
increasing molybdenum and tungsten contents as substitutional elements of the VIA group, and with increasing oxygen content. A certain
amount of carbon addition to Nb±5Mo±15W alloy causes niobium carbide (Nb C) precipitation along grain boundaries and improves the
2
ductility accompanied by a change in the fracture mode. It is suggested that carbide precipitation and carbon segregation at grain
boundaries are responsible for the ductility improvement in carbon-doped Nb±5Mo±15W alloys. Ó 2002 Elsevier Science B. V . All
rights reserved.

2. Experimental procedure
The purities of the raw materials used in this study were
99.99 wt.% Nb, 99.99 wt.% Mo and 99.99 wt.% W. Alloy
buttons were prepared by arc-melting in an argon gas
atmosphere on a water-cooled copper hearth with a non-
consumable tungsten electrode. To ensure chemical homo-
geneity the alloy buttons were re-melted at least ®ve times.
Heat treatment of the alloy buttons for homogenization
was carried out in an argon atmosphere at 2073 K for 24 h
followed by furnace cooling. Since no detectable weight
change was found after arc-melting, evaporation of the
constituent elements was neglected in the expression of the
alloy compositions. The contents of interstitial impurities
were determined by the standard gas analysis method.

4. Discussion
As shown in Fig. 1, Nb± XMo±5W alloys exhibit
marked solid solution strengthening with increasing Mo
content in compression tests. Therefore, the low tensile
fracture stresses at high Mo contents are considered to
arise from accelerated intergranular fracture in tension,
based on the result shown in Fig. 2b. It is well known that
intergranular fracture in pure Mo is induced by grain
boundary segregation of interstitial impurities such as
carbon, nitrogen and oxygen, which lowers the cohesive
strength at the grain boundary [15±18]. Among these
interstitial impurities, oxygen is known to be the most
harmful element due to the formation of molybdenum
oxides with low melting points at grain boundaries such as
MoO [26]. Since Mo has almost no oxygen solubility at
