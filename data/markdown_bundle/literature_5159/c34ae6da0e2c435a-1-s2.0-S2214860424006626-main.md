ABSTRACT
To design new oxidation resistant Ni superalloys adapted for additive manufacturing, a computational design 
approach has been constructed in this work. A series of selection criteria and optimization criteria were defined 
and validated for composition screening, and the genetic algorithm was incorporated into the model to effec -
tively explore the compositional search domain. Accordingly, A new Ni superalloy, AMS-OR, has been developed 
with the optimal combination of printability, oxidation resistance and mechanical properties. Experimental re -
sults show that AMS-OR exhibits excellent printability, achieving crack-free samples across a rather wide range 
of printing parameters. The strength and ductility of AMS-OR alloy can well outperform the existing Ni com -
mercials as well. After oxidation test, a complete and stable Al
2
O
3 
oxide layer forms in the inner layer of the 
oxide scale with no spallation, demonstrating the favorable oxidation resistance of this alloy which is comparable 
to the commercial counterparts. The experimentally validated properties in additive manufacturing, mechanical 
properties and oxidation resistance of novel alloy confirm the effectiveness of the alloy design model in devel -
oping high-performance Ni superalloys, which provides a new pathway for developing novel printable alloys 
with excellent service performance.
1. Introduction
Metal additive manufacturing (AM) presents significant advantages 
in rapid prototyping and fabricating production with complex geome -
tries, surpassing the limitations of conventional manufacturing tech -
niques [1 – 3] . However, the persistent issue of cracking during the AM 
process has impeded its widespread adoption, particularly in crucial 
engineering alloys like Ni superalloys [4 – 9] . It is increasingly recognized 
that the Ni superalloys optimized for traditional manufacturing methods 
may not be compatible with the unique metallurgical requirements of 
AM [10 – 12] . Consequently, great efforts have been made to develop 
additive manufacturable Ni superalloys by constructing various alloy 
design methods. Some researchers have proposed the cracking suscep -
tibility criteria as indicator to classify crack formation, trying to mini -
mize the cracking likelihood through the criteria optimization [13 – 16] . 
Others more focus on the manipulation the selective segregation and 
partition of elemental during AM process, in order to inhibit cracking 
behaviors by minimizing the spatial variations of composition-induced 
features in the as-solidified microstructure [17,18] .
With the improved additive manufacturability, the service perfor -
mances of Ni superalloys were admittedly expected to be guaranteed in 
the alloy design routine. The detrimental impact of high-temperature 
oxidation and its associated damage mechanisms, such as oxidation- 
assisted cracking dwell fatigue crack growth and their interactions, 
underscore the need to prioritize oxidation resistance in alloy design 
[19 – 21] . In fact, oxidation is the predominant mode of damage on most 
service conditions, far exceeding the likelihood of excessive lengthening 
of the blade by creep. Thus, there is a growing emphasis on designing 
AM alloys with a specific focus on enhancing oxidation resistance, 
marking a new frontier in alloy development [22,23] .
Despite systematic investigations of the oxidation mechanisms of 
conventionally manufactured Ni superalloys (e.g., cast and wrought 
alloys), the design of additive manufacturable Ni superalloys with 
improved oxidation resistance remains limited. This is mainly due to the 
difference in oxidation kinetics between AM and cast/wrought Ni alloys. 
For instance, K ¨orner et al. highlights the difference in oxide layer ho -
mogeneity between selective electron beam melted (SEBM) and Bridg -
man investment casted CMSX-4 commercials, driven by the different 
element segregation behaviors [24] . Pedraza et al. report that the cation 
diffusion is blocked for the additive manufactured Inconel 718, while 
the outward diffusion is unimpeded in forged alloy instead [25] .

excellent additive manufacturability, favorable mechanical properties,

experiments.

2.2. Validation of the preset criteria
2.2.1. Validation of selection criteria
To demonstrate the validation of selection criteria employed in the 
alloy design model, thermodynamic calculations are conducted for the 
compositions of existing Ni superalloys, and it is determined to which 
degree multiple selection criteria are fulfilled by existing alloys, as 
shown in Fig. 2 . Five commercial Ni superalloys and five printable Ni 
superalloys are analyzed, with the compositions listed in Table S1 in 
Supplementary Materials [13,14,18,37] . The black dash lines indicate 
the value of the selection criteria and the patterned area mark the 
compositions that failed to meet the criteria. Fig. 2 (a) shows that almost 
all the existing Ni commercials meet the criteria for microstructural 
stability and mechanical properties, thus verifying the effectiveness of 
selection criteria in ranking promising candidates. For the printable 
alloys shown in Fig. 2 (b), the criterion of solid solution strengthening 
can be easily satisfied, while the criteria for undesirable phases and γ’ 
strengtheners can hardly be fulfilled for most printable alloys. The 
mismatch is as expected, since in the design routine of printable Ni al -
loys, the solidification path and weldability are more focused, where the 
service performances as well as the microstructure stability have been 
largely sacrificed. Anyway, this result implies that those selection 
criteria employed here are relatively strict, which further enable the 
formation of desirable microstructures.
2.2.2. Validation of optimization criteria
The validation of selection criteria for cracking susceptibility was 
also operated and shown in Fig. 3 . The tetragonal points indicate the 
currently-reported Ni alloys that can be well printed with no cracks 
observed, and the square symbols stand for the existing Ni heritage al -
loys highly susceptible to cracking during AM processing. As pointed by 
the black arrow, the additive manufacturable alloys can well situate at 
the left bottom corner of the Fig. 3 . The results show lower crack sus -
ceptibility is achievable at the reduced values of FR factor and SAC 
factor, indicating that the printable compositions are expected to be 
obtained by the combined minimization of these two factors.

5.4. Potential of the alloy design model in exploring other systems
It is noteworthy that the developed alloy design model can be 
established as the design methodology. Once the key target perfor -
mances can be “ translated ” into the thermodynamic criteria, the current 
model has the potential to be applied to other alloy systems, depending 
on the specific service requirements. For example, novel high-strength 
precipitation-hardening alloys, such as Al alloys and steels, could be 
developed using this model as well. By maximizing the contribution of 
precipitation strengthening and minimizing cracking susceptibility, this 
approach offers a new pathway for designing novel printable alloys with 
excellent service performance. This methodology not only broadens the 
scope of alloy design but also enhances the additive manufacturability of 
these materials, ensuring they meet stringent performance standards.
6. Conclusions
1. A computational design approach has been constructed to design 
novel Ni superalloys with decent additive manufacturability, me -
chanical properties, and optimized oxidation resistance. To achieve 
this, a series of selection criteria and optimization criteria were 
defined for composition screening, and their effectiveness were 
validated through thermodynamic calculations of existing Ni com -
mercials. A genetic algorithm was incorporated into the model as 
well to effectively probe the compositional search domain.
2. Using this approach, a new Ni superalloy, AMS-OR, was developed 
with an optimal combination of printability and service perfor -
mance. Experimental results show that the crack-free samples of 
AMS-OR alloy can be produced within a rather broad processing 
window, indicating high robustness in printability. The alloy also 
exhibits a superior combination of strength and ductility, out -
performing the existing Ni commercials.
3. After oxidation test, AMS-OR alloy forms a complete and stable 
Al
2
O
3 
oxide layer in the inner layer of the oxide scale, with no 
spallation observed. The newly designed alloy exhibits favorable 
oxidation resistance comparable to commercial counterparts.
4. The experimentally-validated properties in additive manufacturing, 
mechanical properties and oxidation resistance of novel alloy can 
prove the effectiveness of the alloy design model in developing novel 
Ni superalloys with high performance. This alloy design model 
provides a new pathway for developing novel printable alloys with 
excellent service performance.
