ABSTRACT
Nb-Si-based superalloy is one of the most promising candidate materials to replace the Ni-based superalloy due to 
its high melting point and low density. In this study, the economically viable Nb-30Ti- x Si- y Zr ( x = 10, 13, and 
16; y = 6, 12) quaternary alloys are designed. The results indicate that increasing the contents of Si can 
significantly raise the silicide fraction in the microstructure but without altering the phase composition. With the 
addition of Zr, the Nb
3
Si phase disappears, while the Nbss/ γ -Nb
5
Si
3 
eutectic structure becomes more prevalent. 
Furthermore, the microstructure of the investigated alloys shifts from hypoeutectic to hypereutectic. The Nb- 
30Ti-13Si-12Zr alloy exhibits a hypoeutectic structure, characterized by the absence of the Nb
3
Si phase and 
the presence of abundant lamellar eutectic formations. The addition of Si enhances high-temperature perfor -
mance, as the proportion of silicides shows a positive correlation relation with the elevated-temperature prop -
erties. Increasing Zr content improves both room-temperature fracture toughness and high-temperature strength. 
In the Nb-30Ti- x Si-12Zr alloys, large angle of crack deflection can be found in Nbss and eutectic regions, which 
can release the tip-stress to increase the fracture toughness. Additionally, Zr contributes to solid solution 
strengthening, improving the high-temperature compressive properties. The Nb-30Ti-13Si-12Zr alloy demon -
strates the best balance, achieving a fracture toughness of 14.68 MPa ⋅ m
1/2 
and a high-temperature strength of 
251.8 MPa.
1. Introduction
To enhance the thrust-to-weight ratio of gas turbine engines, 
advanced blade superalloys with superior high-temperature resistance 
are required. The design target for turbine inlet temperature has reached 
1800
◦
C [1] . Nb-Si-based ultrahigh-temperature alloys have garnered 
significant research interest owing to their superior properties, including 
a low density [2] , high melting point [3] , and exceptional 
high-temperature performance [4] , compared to conventional Ni-based 
superalloys. Consequently, among advanced materials for aerospace 
applications, Nb-Si in-situ composites currently represent the leading 
solution for future high-performance engines requiring exceptional 
thrust-to-weight characteristics [5 – 7] .
As an ideal candidate material for ultra-high temperature structures, 
Nb-Si alloys have their unique advantages but also face many chal -
lenges. Nb-Si-based ultra-high temperature alloys are primarily 
composed of a niobium solid solution (Nbss) and silicide phases, 
including Nb
5
Si
3 
and Nb
3
Si [8] . The Nbss phase exhibits excellent 
toughness but poor strength, while silicides contribute to enhanced 
high-temperature strength at the expense of fracture toughness due to 
their inherent hardness and brittleness. Consequently, optimizing the 
phase ratio to achieve balanced mechanical properties remains a key 
research focus. However, studies have revealed that Nb-Si alloys exhibit 
insufficient room-temperature toughness, leading to challenges in 
ambient-temperature processing and posing a critical limitation for 
aerospace applications [9,10] . Currently, microstructure and property

structures are properly managed [11,12] .
Since the late 1980s, the research on Nb-Si alloys by scientists has 
undergone a transformation from binary alloys to multi-component 
systems. In the early studies of the binary Nb-Si alloy system, re -
searchers observed that as the Si content increased, the primary Nbss 
coarse particles diminished, and a fine eutectic structure became pre -
dominant at the composition of Nb-18Si [13] . Subsequently, through 
extensive research on the alloying of Nb-Si-based in-situ composites, 
ternary, quaternary, and even more complex alloy systems have grad -
ually been developed [14 – 17] .
Ti is a crucial alloying element in Nb-Si-Ti-based in-situ composites 
[18] , as its concentration plays an extremely important role in the 
room-temperature fracture toughness of Nb-Si-Ti-based in-situ com -
posites [19] . Wang et al. [20] demonstrated that the addition of Ti to 
Nb-Mo-Si-based alloys leads to pronounced elemental segregation. 
During the melting process, Ti enrichment alters the composition of the 
residual melt. Furthermore, Ti addition modifies the morphology of 
primary silicides, making them more cubic, while coarsening the 
eutectic structure and altering its morphology. Studies have also indi -
cated that by decreasing the Peierls-Nabarro energy barrier for dislo -
cations, titanium effectively increases the intrinsic deformability of the 
niobium solid solution phase, leading to substantially improved tough -
ness of the material [21 – 23] . At the same time, replacing Nb with Ti can 
better save costs and improve the possibility of its wide application. 
However, due to its low melting point and role as a stabilizer of Nb
3
Si, 
excessive Ti content can degrade the alloy ’ s high-temperature 
performance.
Zr alloying provides an effective approach to optimizing the micro -
structure of materials, thereby improving both high temperature and 
room temperature characteristics of materials. Miura et al. [24] reported 
that a small amount of Zr influences the eutectoid decomposition 
behavior of Nb-Si alloys and accelerates the kinetics of Nb
3
Si eutectic 
transformation. Additionally, Zr has been demonstrated to increase the 
proportion of γ -Nb
5
Si
3 
in the silicide phases [25 – 30] .
In this study, a Nb-30Ti- x Si- y Zr ( x = 10, 13, and 16; y = 6, 12) alloy 
was designed. Compared with existing multicomponent systems, the 
quaternary alloy employs 30 at% Ti to replace Nb for cost reduction 
while introducing Zr to optimize both microstructure and mechanical 
properties. By systematically varying the Si and Zr contents, the syner -
gistic effects of silicide phases modifications in the Nb-Ti-Si-Zr quater -
nary alloy on its fracture toughness at room temperature and high- 
temperature compressive properties was investigated. The primary 
objective of this work is to develop a Nb-Si-based ultra-high temperature 
alloy with balanced room-temperature and high-temperature 
properties.

3.2. Room-temperature fracture toughness of Nb-30Ti-xSi-yZr alloys
The room-temperature fracture toughness serves as a critical mate -
rial parameter in assessing Nb-Si superalloy performance. Load versus 
displacement curves obtained during three-point bending tests of 
notched Nb-30Ti- x Si- y Zr alloys are illustrated in Fig. 7 (a), and all load- 
to-displacement curves of all samples are similar. During the loading 
process, the test load increases almost synchronously with the 
displacement, and then the load decreases rapidly with a small 
displacement, which accords with the characteristics of brittle defor -
mation [33 – 35] . The average fracture toughness at room temperature is 
shown in Fig. 7 (b), and it is evident that the proportion of Si and Zr can 
significantly affect the fracture toughness at room temperature The 
addition of Zr can improve the K
Q 
values of Nb-Si superalloys: all the 
average toughness values show the rising trend with Zr doping. For 
instance, the fracture toughness of the 13Si-6Zr alloy is 12.74 MPa ⋅ m
1/2

which is lower than the 13Si-12Zr alloy with 14.68 MPa ⋅ m
1/2

the increase of silicon introduces more brittle phases into the micro -

Among the investigated alloys, the 10Si-12Zr composition demonstrates

intrinsically related to the microstructural distribution. During the pri -

deflection and branching are frequently observed, with the hard silicide 
phases serving as preferential propagation paths. The observation that 
cracks propagate continuously through the silicide phases demonstrates 
that these hard and brittle components are ineffective in obstructing 
crack progression. In terms of toughening mechanisms [22,36] , the 
contribution of silicide phases to the overall toughness is negligible. The 
morphology and proportion of Nbss are the key factors determining 
fracture toughness. The ductile Nbss phase demonstrates superior stress 
accommodation capability. Consequently, the increase in volume frac -
tion and size of the Nbss phase is closely associated with the enhanced 
fracture toughness of Nb-Si-based alloys. The improvement in fracture 
toughness with decreasing Si content can be primarily attributed to the 
significant increase in both the proportion and size of the Nbss phase.
Microstructural analysis of the Nbss phase and silicide distribution 
reveals that when propagating cracks encounter the Nbss phase, they 
preferentially continue along the Nbss-silicide interface or through the 
silicide phases via various mechanisms including deflection, bridging, 
and branching ( Fig. 10 ). This propagation behavior facilitates energy 
dissipation through surface energy conversion, with greater energy 
consumption corresponding to improved fracture toughness. As 
demonstrated in Fig. 9 (d), the 10Si-12Zr alloy exhibits extensive crack 
deflection, bridging, and branching phenomena along the propagation 
path, accompanied by secondary crack formation, which correlates with 
its optimal fracture toughness performance. During crack propagation, 
the Nbss phase continuously dissipates crack energy until complete 
energy exhaustion and crack arrest. The continuous network of Nbss 
phase within the lamellar eutectic structure effectively consumes stress, 
impedes crack propagation, and consequently enhances fracture 
toughness [22] . The additional incorporation of Zr element facilitates 
the transformation of coarse Nb
3
Si phase into finer eutectic structures, 
resulting in a remarkable refinement of the microstructure and conse -
quently enhancing the overall mechanical properties of the material.
To investigate the correlation between microstructure and fracture 
toughness, the fracture surface morphology of samples after three-point 
bending experiments was analyzed. As illustrated in Fig. 11 , all speci -
mens exhibit quasi-cleavage fracture characteristics, with fracture sur -
faces consisting of cleavage planes and tear ridges. As shown in Fig. 9 (a) 
and (d), the 10Si alloys show the most pronounced surface undulation, 
exhibiting distinct fluctuations, tear ridges and dimples, with the size of 
the dimples resembling cellular structures. This morphological feature 
indicates superior energy dissipation during fracture, corresponding to 
enhanced fracture toughness [41] . With increasing Si content to 13 at% 
and 16 at%, the fracture surfaces progressively become smoother, sug -
gesting reduced fracture toughness, which is consistent with the 
three-point bending test results. The formation of tear ridges is associ -
ated with the fracture behavior of the eutectic structure [32] . Micro -
structural analysis reveals that the Nbss phase fails through cleavage 
fracture with the river patterns and tear ridge morphologies, indicating 
the ductile fracture behavior. In contrast, the silicide phases exhibit 
smooth, flat fracture surfaces dominated by cleavage planes, demon -

a significant increase in silicide fracture surfaces, particularly evident in

the Nb

content coupled with increased Zr concentration promotes enhanced 
interfacial debonding between Nbss and silicide phases, facilitating 
more efficient crack energy absorption.

Fig. 12 presents the representative flow curves and high-temperature 
compressive strength characteristics of Nb-30Ti- x Si- y Zr alloys under 
compression at 1200
◦

mation of Nb-Si alloys at elevated temperatures exhibits two phenom -

exhibits characteristic fluctuations during compression, indicative of the 
competing effects between work hardening and dynamic softening. In 
general, the stress value of the alloy at the initial deformation stage 
increases rapidly with increasing strain, as dislocations accumulate 
rapidly, exhibiting rapid work hardening. Thereafter, work hardening 
and dynamic softening reach equilibrium, and stress values peak at 
small strains. Following peak stress attainment, dynamic softening takes 
the dominant position. The alloy undergoes rapid stress reduction, and 
the curve becomes stable due to further dislocation accumulation during 
deformation. The obtained curve conforms to the characteristic flow 
behavior observed in typical hot compression deformation [44] . 
Furthermore, the steady-state flow stress remains substantially lower 
than the peak stress, indicating significant alloy softening.

the high-temperature compressive performance of the 16Si-6Zr alloy is 
significantly higher than that of the 16Si-12Zr alloy.

incorporation influence microstructural evolution, room-temperature 
fracture toughness, and high-temperature compressive property in Nb-
