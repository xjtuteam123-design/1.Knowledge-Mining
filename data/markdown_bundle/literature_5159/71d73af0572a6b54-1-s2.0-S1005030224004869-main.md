Lightweight high/medium-entropy alloys (H/MEAs) possess attractive properties such as high strength-to- 
weight ratios, however, their limited room-temperature tensile ductility hinders their widespread engi- 
neering implementation, for instance in aerospace structural components. This work achieved a transfor- 
mative improvement of room-temperature tensile ductility in Ti-V-Zr-Nb MEAs with densities of 5.4–
6.5 g/cm3 , via ingenious composition modulation. Through the systematic co-adjustment of Ti and V 
contents, an intrinsic ductility mechanism was unveiled, manifested by a transition from predominant 
intergranular brittle fracture to pervasive ductile dimpled rupture. Notably, the modulated deformation 
mechanisms evolved from solitary slip toward collaborative multiple slip modes, without signiﬁcantly 
compromising strength. Compared to equimolar Ti-V-Zr-Nb, a (Ti1.5V)3ZrNb composition demonstrated 
an impressive 360% improvement in elongation while sustaining a high yield strength of around 800 MPa. 
Increasing Ti and V not only puriﬁed the grain boundaries by reducing detrimental phases, but also tai- 
lored the deformation dislocation conﬁgurations. These insights expanded the applicability of lightweight 
HEAs to areas demanding combined high strength and ductility.

1. Introduction 
The lightweighting of structural materials has become a cru- 
cial strategy for energy eﬃciency and reduction of carbon diox- 
ide emissions in the transportation sectors [ 1 , 2 ]. Particularly in the 
aerospace ﬁeld, lightweight alloys with high strength-to-weight ra- 
tios (speciﬁc strength) have become indispensable to enable lighter 
and more capable aircraft and spacecraft [ 3 , 4 ]. For example, alu- 
minium and titanium alloys have been widely utilized in aircraft 
bodies, engine components, and space structures over past decades 
to meet performance requirements [5] . However, incremental im- 
provements to conventional alloys have nearly reached their lim- 
its [ 6 , 7 ]. To achieve next-generation lightweight aerospace struc- 
tures, novel alloy breakthroughs are needed that transcend tra- 
ditional alloy paradigms. Lightweight high/medium-entropy alloys 
(LH/MEAs) comprised of multiple principal elements have recently 
emerged as promising candidates [8–10] . LH/MEAs not only ex-

but also have remarkably high hardness, strength, speciﬁc strength,

Over the past decade, numerous LH/MEA systems have been 
discovered due to their low densities and high speciﬁc strengths. 
Among them, Al-Mg-Li-based alloys have the lowest densities 
( ∼2.7 g/cm3 ), but their application temperatures are limited by the 
low melting points of their constituents [ 13 , 20 , 21 ]. On the other 
hand, Ti-V-Zr-based alloys have higher densities ( ∼6.5 g/cm3 ), but 
they can withstand higher temperatures, owing to the presence 
of refractory elements [ 15 , 22-25 ]. These alloys are classiﬁed as 
lightweight refractory high/medium entropy alloys (LRH/MEAs), 
and they have demonstrated remarkable mechanical properties 
and thermal stability, making them promising candidates for 
lightweight and high-temperature applications [ 26 , 27 ]. For exam- 
ple, an NbTiVZr alloy demonstrated a yield strength over 1 GPa at 
room temperature and exceeded 800 MPa at 600 °C [28] , while 
an AlNbTiV alloy also exhibited a high yield strength exceeding 
1 GPa at room temperature, and over 600 MPa at 800 °C [29] . The

challenge in designing new alloy systems with balanced strength

Several strategies have been developed to enhance the room- 
temperature plastic deformability of refractory high-entropy al- 
loys (RHEAs). Introducing ordered oxygen complexes into TiZrHfNb 
RHEAs provides solid-solution strengthening and improves plas- 
ticity by pinning dislocations and inducing cross-slip [34] . The 
transformation-induced plasticity (TRIP) mechanism, utilizing a 
“metastable engineering” strategy to induce body-centered cu- 
bic (BCC) to hexagonal close packed (HCP) phase transforma- 
tions [ 35 , 36 ], has also proven effective in increasing plasticity and 
work-hardening capability. Grain boundary engineering through 
B/C substitution for “oxygen” at grain boundaries has addressed 
the room-temperature brittleness issue in NbMoTaW RHEAs [37] . 
Recently, tailoring local chemical ordering has emerged as a 
promising method to improve RHEAs’ room-temperature plastic- 
ity. By modifying planar-slip bands through enhanced B2-like lo- 
cal chemical ordering and generating excess lattice distortion upon 
deformation-induced ordering destruction, an exceptional combi- 
nation of gigapascal yield strength and nearly 50% elongation-to- 
failure has been achieved in a Ti-Zr-V-Nb-Al BCC HEA [38] . Addi- 
tionally, the addition of aluminum to the HfNbTiV alloy creates a 
negative mixing enthalpy solid solution that promotes hierarchical 
chemical ﬂuctuations across multiple length scales, acting as ef- 
fective barriers to dislocation motion and leading to a remarkable 
combination of roughly 20% tensile ductility and an ultrahigh yield 
strength of around 1390 MPa [39] . These results demonstrate that 
tailoring the alloy composition to modify microstructural features 
and alter dislocation motion behavior is an effective approach to 
enhancing the plastic deformability of BCC RHEAs. 
In this study, we aim to address the challenge of limited room- 
temperature tensile ductility in BCC LRMEAs. Drawing inspiration 
from our previously developed Ti3V2ZrNb alloy [15] , we propose 
tailoring the Ti and V ratios in the Ti-V-Zr-Nb LRMEA system to 
achieve a signiﬁcant improvement in the tensile ductility without 
compromising its strength, thereby overcoming the trade-off be- 
tween strength and ductility. Speciﬁcally, by modulating the Ti and 
V ratios in Ti-V-Zr-Nb alloys, we facilitate a transition from single 
slip to multiple slip modes, accompanied by a change from brit- 
tle intergranular fracture to ductile fracture. This approach signif- 
icantly enhances the room-temperature tensile ductility of the Ti- 
V-Zr-Nb MEAs. The optimization of alloy composition to modulate 
deformation behaviors and fracture modes offers new insights into 
the development of novel lightweight RHEAs with an optimal com- 
bination of high strength and ductility.

2.2. Sample preparation 
To prepare the equiatomic Ti-V-Zr-Nb and (Ti1.5V) x ZrNb ( x = 1–
5) high-entropy alloys, high-purity ( > 99.95%) constituent met- 
als including titanium, vanadium, zirconium, and niobium were 
weighed according to the designed compositions. The pure met- 
als were ﬁrst arc-melted under a high-purity argon atmosphere 
to allow for thorough intermixing and alloying. To ensure chem- 
ical homogeneity, the molten master alloys were ﬂipped and re- 
melted at least 5 times before they were drop-cast into a water- 
cooled copper mold to obtain plates measuring 10 × 80 × 5 mm. 
The as-cast alloy plates were cold rolled at room temperature in 
a two-high rolling mill through consecutive passes, achieving a to- 
tal thickness reduction of approximately 80% in a laboratory-scale 
two-high rolling machine. The cold rolled plates were subsequently 
recrystallized by vacuum annealing at 1073 K for 1 h, followed by 
air cooling.

measured approximately 12.7 mm in length, 3 mm in width and 
1 mm in thickness. Tensile testing was performed at room tem-

2.4. Microstructure characterization 
The phase constituents and crystal structures of the as- 
processed high-entropy alloy plates were determined by X-ray 
diffraction (XRD) using a Rigaku D \ max-2550 diffractometer with 
Cu Kα radiation operated at 40 kV. The microstructural evolu- 
tion before and after tensile testing was characterized by scanning 
electron microscopy (SEM) using a Gemini SEM 300 instrument 
equipped with electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) and energy 
dispersive spectroscopy (EDS, Oxford Instruments). EBSD mapping 
was performed at a step size of 1 μm to quantify the grain sizes, 
shapes, and orientations, and the average grain sizes were mea- 
sured from the area fractions using Image Pro-Plus software. 
Fractography of the tensile fracture surfaces was conducted by 
ﬁeld emission SEM (Hitachi instrument). The deformation and frac- 
ture mechanisms were further analyzed by transmission electron 
microscopy (TEM) using a JEOL JEM-2100F instrument operated at 
200 kV and equipped with an Oxford EDS system. 
Sample preparation for electron microscopy consisted of me- 
chanical polishing to a mirror ﬁnish, followed by electrochemical 
polishing. A specialized electrolyte containing 6% perchloric acid, 
34% butanol, and 60% methanol maintained at −40 °C was used 
to electropolish the EBSD and TEM specimens. The thin TEM foils 
were sliced from the gauge sections of the deformed samples and 
ground to a thickness of 50 μm before twinjet electropolishing.

3.3. Mechanical performance 
The tensile properties of the equiatomic Ti-V-Zr-Nb alloy and 
(Ti1.5V) x ZrNb ( x = 1–5) alloys were presented in Fig. 4 . All 
of the studied alloys exhibited high yield and ultimate tensile 
strengths, indicative of signiﬁcant solid solution strengthening. 
However, notable variations in tensile ductility were observed 
( Fig. 4 (a)). The Ti-V-Zr-Nb alloy exhibited the highest yield strength 
( σ0.2 ∼900 MPa) and ultimate tensile strength ( σUTS ∼957 MPa) 
strengths, yet limited tensile ductility ( Fig. 4 (a)), with only ∼5% 
uniform elongation (UE) and less than 11% fracture elongation (FE). 
In contrast, the (Ti1.5V) x ZrNb ( x = 1–5) alloys displayed improved 
ductility alongside slightly reduced strengths with increasing Ti 
and V content ( Fig. 4 (a)). Speciﬁcally, Ti1.5VZrNb demonstrated a 
signiﬁcant increase in UE ( ∼12%) and FE ( ∼20.3%), while experi- 
encing a decrease in σ0.2 (828 MPa) and σUTS (896 MPa) strengths 
versus equiatomic Ti-V-Zr-Nb. (Ti1.5V)2ZrNb alloy achieved a bal- 
anced combination of strength ( σ0.2 ∼877 MPa, σUTS ∼942 MPa) 
and ductility (UE ∼17.6%, FE ∼22.9%). 
Remarkably, (Ti1.5V)3ZrNb displayed the greatest tensile duc- 
tility with UE and FE of ∼23.3% and ∼34.8%, though σ0.2 
(782 MPa) and σUTS (850 MPa) strengths decreased slightly 
versus (Ti1.5V)2ZrNb. Intermediate properties were exhibited 
by (Ti1.5V)4ZrNb and (Ti1.5V)5ZrNb. (Ti1.5V)4ZrNb exhibited

values for (Ti1.5V)5ZrNb were 761/815 MPa and ∼14.8%/ ∼24.1%, re- 
spectively.

mechanisms of the alloys [42–44] , implying that the changes in 
Ti and V ratio affect the slip mechanisms, thereby inﬂuencing the 
overall mechanical performance of the alloys. 
3.4. Fracture morphology 
To understand the deformation mechanism, we conducted de- 
tailed postmortem SEM investigations of the fractured Ti-V-Zr-Nb 
and (Ti1.5V) x ZrNb alloys. As evidenced by the representative frac- 
ture morphologies, the failure fracture mode of the studied alloy 
sample is different, showing a transition from brittle intergranular 
to ductile fracture modes across the Ti-V-Zr-Nb and (Ti1.5V) x ZrNb

4. Discussion 
4.1. The effect of grain boundary puriﬁcation on improved tensile 
ductility 
To reveal the origins of brittle to ductile transition, EBSD anal- 
ysis examined the distributions of the BCC and HCP phases, as 
shown in Fig. 6 (a1 –f1 ), and the corresponding TEM characteriza- 
tion of the deformed grain boundaries is shown in Fig. 6 (a2 –f2 ). 
In the EBSD phase map of the Ti-V-Zr-Nb alloy ( Fig. 6 (a1 )), dis- 
continuous HCP phases were predominantly observed embedded 
along the grain boundaries. TEM-BF imaging veriﬁed ultraﬁne pre- 
cipitates ( ∼200 nm) decorating the grain boundaries ( Fig. 6 (a2 )), 
and STEM imaging of the grain boundary ( Fig. 7 (a)) indicated that 
the precipitates were granular or had a bench morphology. Corre- 
sponding energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) mappings in- 
dicated the grain-boundary precipitates were enriched in Zr com- 
pared to the matrix, while Ti, V, and Nb remained uniformly dis- 
tributed ( Fig. 7 (b–e)). The composition was conﬁrmed as Zr-rich 
(Zr: ∼51, Ti: ∼19; Nb: ∼14, V: ∼16, at.%) through EDS point anal- 
ysis ( Fig. 7 (f)). The corresponding selected area electron diffraction 
pattern (SAED) is shown in Fig. 7 (g), which could be indexed to an 
HCP structure in BCC matrix, agreeing with EBSD ( Fig. 6 (a1)).

Despite containing an ultraﬁne HCP precipitate fraction of less 
than 1.1%, which exerts a negligible inﬂuence on the strength, 
these inclusions had a deleterious impact on the fracture behav- 
ior of the Ti-V-Zr-Nb alloys. As shown in Fig. 6 (a2 ), microcracks 
initiated adjacent to the HCP precipitates after tensile deforma- 
tion, indicating that the HCP particles severely embrittled the grain 
boundaries. The formation of HCP precipitates along grain bound- 
aries was shown to induce intergranular fracture in many BCC al- 
loys [45–47] . This brittle intergranular failure stemmed from the 
high stress concentrations that developed at the interfaces be- 
tween the HCP precipitates and BCC matrix [48] . Once the mi- 
crocracks nucleated near the particles, unconstrained propagation 
rapidly ensued along the embrittled grain boundaries [49] . The 
applied tensile stress intensiﬁed the stress concentrations at the 
HCP/BCC interfaces, driving crack initiation [49] . Subsequently, the 
weak nature of the precipitate-decorated grain boundaries facil- 
itated rapid crack growth [50] . Ultimately, the presence of even 
a small volume fraction of the HCP precipitates along the grain 
boundaries in Ti-V-Zr-Nb alloys led to premature intergranular 
fracture. 
A similar phenomenon was observed in the Ti1.5VZrNb alloy, 
where minimal fraction ( ∼0.7%) of the HCP precipitates was ran- 
domly distributed along the grain boundaries, as revealed by EBSD 
phase mapping in Fig. 6 (b1 ). TEM further veriﬁed the presence of 
precipitates on the boundaries, with interparticle microcracks vis- 
ible in Fig. 6 (b2 ). Analogous to the Ti-V-Zr-Nb alloy, these grain 
boundary HCP particles had a detrimental effect on the fracture 
behavior of the Ti1.5VZrNb alloy ( Fig. 5 (b1 , b2 )). With further 
Ti and V addition, the grain boundaries became purer, with the 
disappearance of the HCP precipitates in the (Ti1.5V)2ZrNb alloy 
( Fig. 6 (c1 , c2 )). Likewise, in the (Ti1.5V)3ZrNb, (Ti1.5V)4ZrNb, and 
(Ti1.5V)5ZrNb alloys, EBSD maps showed only a BCC phase, with 
purer grain boundaries devoid of additional phases, as shown in 
Fig. 6 (e1 , f1 ). Corresponding TEM analysis of these alloys further 
conﬁrmed the puriﬁcation of the boundaries, without observed 
precipitates or microcracks ( Fig. 6 (e2 , f2 )). 
This substantial enhancement in the purity of the Ti-V-Zr-Nb 
alloy grain boundaries, attributed to the increased Ti and V con- 
tent, played a pivotal role in transitioning the fracture behavior 
of the alloys from brittle to ductile. The elimination of HCP pre- 
cipitates along the grain boundaries eradicated stress concentra- 
tions and crack initiation sites, which typically promote intergran- 
ular fracture. Consequently, fracture propagation occurred through 
a ductile dimple fracture mechanism within the BCC grains, as ob- 
served in Fig. 5 (c–f), resulting in improved ductility, as showcased 
in Fig. 4 (a).

EBSD analysis of the tensile fractured samples provided fur- 
ther insight into the microscopic deformation mechanisms of the 
representative Ti-V-Zr-Nb, (Ti1.5V)3ZrNb, and (Ti1.5V)5ZrNb alloys 
( Fig. 9 (a–i)). Speciﬁcally, the EBSD IPF map near the fracture sur- 
face of the TiV-Zr-Nb alloy ( Fig. 9 (a)) revealed pronounced defor- 
mation heterogeneities primarily concentrated at the grain bound- 
aries rather than within the grain interiors. This observation was 
corroborated by the presence of high densities of geometrically 
necessary dislocations (GND) near the boundaries ( Fig. 9 (d)). Con- 
versely, the relatively low dislocation densities observed within the 
grain interiors suggested limited dislocation slip activity within 
the grains. The localization of deformation at the grain bound- 
aries can be attributed to the restricted activation of easy slip sys- 
tems within the BCC grains of the Ti-V-Zr-Nb alloy [51] . Further- 
more, the absence of intragranular dislocation slip, coupled with 
the presence of grain-boundary precipitates ( Fig. 9 (g)), led to stress 
concentrations at the boundaries, ultimately resulting in intergran- 
ular fracture. The relatively low intragranular dislocation densities 
in the Ti-V-Zr-Nb alloy contributed to a low work hardening rate 
( Fig. 8 (b)), which, in conjunction with the propensity for intergran- 
ular cracking, adversely affected the ductility of the material. 
In contrast, the IPF map of (Ti1.5V)3ZrNb ( Fig. 9 (b)) showed ex- 
tensive intragranular plastic deformation features. The grains were 
severely tensile deformation and kinking, as evident by the ori- 
entation variations within the grains ( Fig. 9 (b), inset). The corre- 
sponding GND map ( Fig. 9 (e)) revealed high densities of curved 
and intersecting dislocation bands spanning the grain interiors, in- 
dicating to the activation of multiple slips. The interactions be- 
tween dislocations on different slip planes resulted in substan- 
tial dislocation forests and subgrains delimited by the low-angle 
boundaries, as shown in Fig. 9 (e) and (h). However, we did not ob- 
serve deformation twinning or transformations ( Fig. 9 (b, h)). The 
profuse dislocation storage enabled signiﬁcant strain hardening in 
(Ti1.5V)3ZrNb ( Fig. 8 (b)), while the dislocation substructures hin- 
dered crack propagation, enhancing ductility [52] . 
The IPF map of (Ti1.5V)5ZrNb ( Fig. 9 (c)) exhibited an interme- 
diate level of intragranular plastic deformation between Ti-V-Zr-Nb 
and (Ti1.5V)3ZrNb. The corresponding GND map ( Fig. 9 (f)) indi- 
cated substantial dislocations concentrated along the grain bound- 
aries. Within the grains, relatively straight and parallel disloca- 
tion bands indicative of planar slip were visible. Low-angle bound- 
aries formed in the high dislocation density regions near the 
grain boundaries, although no twin boundaries or stress-induced 
HCP transformations were observed ( Fig. 9 (i)). Low-angle bound- 
aries and planar slip promoted work hardening, with fewer dis- 
location densities than in (Ti1.5V)3ZrNb, leading to inferior work 
hardening ( Fig. 8 (b)). The combination of planar slip and reduced 
work hardening resulted in poorer tensile ductility compared to 
(Ti1.5V)3ZrNb. 
The Ti-V-Zr-Nb alloy exhibited single slip as the predominant 
deformation mode, as evidenced by the straight parallel slip bands 
in the GND map ( Fig. 9 (d)) and TEM-BF image ( Fig. 10 (a)). This 
could be attributed to the limited slip directions associated with 
the BCC HEA [53] . The restricted slip system interactions failed to 
facilitate extensive cross-slip and dispersion of the dislocations, re- 
sulting in strain localization along discrete crystallographic planes 
[51] . The strain localization resulted in deformation instability, as 
shown in Fig. 10 (b) and (e). The limited slip activity, along with 
conﬁned dislocation activity, contributed to the poor tensile duc- 
tility of the Ti-V-Zr-Nb BCC alloy.

ing plastic deformation, as revealed by the intricate dislocation 
networks in Fig. 10 (d). Typical planar slips, along with cross slips, 
were identiﬁed, as indicated by the arrows in Fig. 10 (d) and (e).

high dislocation density junctions. Additionally, dislocations with 
differing Bur gers vectors formed multiple reactions, as shown in 
Fig. 10 (f).

5. Conclusion 
In this work, increasing the Ti and V fractions in Ti-V-Zr-Nb 
system was found to improve the uniform elongation over three 
times, without markedly sacriﬁcing strength, achieving optimal 
combinations of strength and ductility. Detailed analyses revealed 
that higher Ti and V contents led to purer grain boundaries and 
the disappearance of HCP precipitates at the boundaries, chang- 
ing the fracture mode from intergranular to dimpled rupture. More 
importantly, Ti and V additions could alter the deformation mode 
from planar slip toward multiple slip activation. However, exces- 
sive Ti and V contents were undesirable, reverting to conventional 
planar slip and deteriorating ductility. Ti and V variations did not 
markedly inﬂuence the solid solution strengthening of the investi- 
gated Ti-V-Zr-Nb alloys but could dramatically improve their ten- 
sile ductility. Tuning the Ti and V compositions enabled multiply- 
ing ductility while retaining strength in the present alloy system. 
This study provided critical insight into tuning deformation mech- 
anisms through compositional tailoring, to design HEAs with opti- 
mal strength-ductility synergy.
