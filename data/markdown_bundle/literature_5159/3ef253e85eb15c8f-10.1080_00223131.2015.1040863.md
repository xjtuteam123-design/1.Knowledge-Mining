1. Introduction
Nuclear fuel cladding tubes, regarded as the first
confinement barrier, play a crucial role for the safe and
stable operation of nuclear power plants. Zirconium al-
loys have been used extensively as the fuel cladding ma-
terials in nuclear industry, due to their small thermal
neutron absorption cross section, high corrosion resis-
tance,andsufficientmechanicalperformance.Zr–Nbal-
loys,whichareusuallycomposedofan α-Zrmatrixand
β-(Nb, Zr) precipitates (PPTs), exhibit superior corro-
sion resistances compared with Zircaloy-4 and ZIRLO
[1]. They also show lower hydrogen absorption and ir-
radiation growth [2]. Therefore, they are considered to
be excellent fuel cladding materials in advanced future
nuclear plants. The development of materials for fuel
cladding tubes remains one of the key issues since the
introduction of a high burn-up design and an extended
fuel-cycle operation [3]. Modification of zirconium al-
loys has been considered as one of the most effective
ways to attain better performance cladding tube mate-
rials in recent years. Sn and Nb have been considered as
the main alloying elements in zirconium alloys, lots of

during a recrystallization process is controlled by the
driving force of the recrystallization and boundary
mobility [20]. The driving force is dependent upon the
stored energy of deformed matrix, including every kind
of defects, such as vacancies and dislocations. Since the
reduction of all experimental alloys was the same, to
make a compromise, the stored energy for all specimens
was assumed same. The boundary mobility between
the nucleus and the surrounding deformed matrix is
usuallyinfluencednegativelybytheZenerpinningforce
causedbyPPTsandthedraggingeffectcausedbysolute
alloyingatoms.Duetotheincreasedpiningforcecaused
by the increment on PPTs number density in higher
Mo-containingalloys, theboundary mobilitydecreased
with the increase of the Mo content. Meanwhile, it is
alsonecessarytoconsiderthedragging effectoftheMo
addition, which would reduce the boundary mobility
as well. This opinion was supported by the report from
Liu et al. [21], it is reported that the addition of Mo
could significantly reduce the inter-diffusion coefficient
of Zr, which is to say, the solute Mo atoms in Zr would
slow down the diffusion velocity of Zr atoms, leading
to the decrease of the grain boundary mobility. On the
whole, Mo addition enhances the Zener pinning effect
and dragging effect, both of which are attributable to
the retardation of recrystallization in Zr-Nb alloys.

4. Conclusions
Effects of the Mo addition on the microstructure
evolution and mechanical properties of Zr–Nb–Mo al-
loys were systematically investigated in this study. The
main conclusions can be summarized as follows.
(1) Effects of Mo on martensitic microstructure:
WidthoflathmartensitereducedwithincreasingMo
concentration, besides area fraction of lens martensite
increased with an increase in the Mo concentration.
(2) Effects of Mo on precipitation behaviors on Zr–
Nb–Mo alloys:
PPTs of a new type,β-(Nb, Mo, Zr), were identified
in Mo-containing Zr–Nb alloys. Mo addition reduced
the growth of PPTs, but had a positive effect on their
nucleation.
(3) Effects of Mo on recrystallization behaviors on
Zr–Nb–Mo alloys:
Mo addition retarded the recrystallization process
strongly, and reduced the grain size significantly in Zr–
Nb alloys.
(4) Effects of Mo on mechanical behaviors on Zr–
Nb–Mo alloys:
Mo addition enhanced the YS, UTS of Zr–Nb–Mo
alloysatroomtemperature,howeverreducedtheductil-
ity. The mechanical strength increment was interpreted
by three factors: solid solution strengthening, grain size
strengthening, and precipitation strengthening. Grain
size strengthening was the greatest contributor among
these factors.
In summary, even though continued effort is needed
to further evaluate and understand the new Zr–Nb–
Moalloys’performanceonvarioustestingenvironment,
suchas corrosion and irradiation. Sofar,thefindings in
thisstudyprovedunambiguouslythatZr–Nb–Moalloys
can be considered as a fuel cladding tube in future ad-
vanced nuclear plants, due to their outstanding advan-
tages such as the grain size reduction, more and smaller
precipitates, and higher strength.
