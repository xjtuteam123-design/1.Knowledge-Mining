Grain size effect on high-temperature fatigue properties of alloy718
Kazuo Kobayashi *, Koji Yamaguchi, Masao Hayakawa, Megumi Kimura
Materials Information Technology Station, National Institute for Materials Science (MITS/NIMS), Tsukuba 305-0047, Japan
Received 16 January 2004; accepted 2 September 2004
Available online 19 October 2004
Abstract
High-temperature fatigue properties of Alloy718 with different grain sizes were investigated. For the coarse-grain alloy, the fatigue
strength notably decreased beyond 10 5 cycles. The fatigue fracture had originated from facets of the austenite grains. The facets caused a
decreasing of fatigue strengths in a high-cycle region at high temperatures.

Keywords: Aerospace materials; Fatigue; High temperature; Internal fracture; Facet
1. Introduction
Nickel-base Alloy718 is widely used as heat-resisting struc-
tural materials, since it has high-temperature strength properties
in addition to an excellent workability and welding property
[1,2]. This alloy shows differences in austenite grain size
depending on manufacturing processes and heat treatment
conditions. The differences affect the strength properties of the
alloy, which needs to be understood for engineering purposes.
In this study, high-temperature fatigue properties of Alloy
718 with different austenite grain size were investigated in a
wide range from 10
2 cycles to 107 cycles. Between 102 and 105
cycles fatigue fractures initiated from specimen surfaces, while
beyond 10
5 cycles fractures initiated inside specimen surfaces.
Grain size effect on high-temperature fatigue properties of Alloy
718 could be explained by in terms of their fracture mechanisms.
2. Experimental procedure
2.1. Materials
The materials used were Alloy718 with fine- and coarse-
grains as shown inTable 1. These had almost the same chemical

grain alloy, respectively.
The high-temperature tensile strengths of the both alloys
are almost same values as those shown in Table 2.
2.2. Testing procedures
Fatigue tests were conducted using a servo-hydraulic fatigue-
testing machine. The load-controlled waveshape is sinusoidal
under a stress ratio ofR=/C01. The frequency is 10 Hz. Only for
the fine-grain alloy, strain control tests up to 10
5 cycles were
conducted.
The specimen shape is a cylindrical bar with 3 mm in
diameter and 13 mm in length. An electric resistance
furnace was used to heat the specimens. Fatigue tests were
conducted at 550 or 600 8C, depending on the projects of
the NIMS Space Use Materials Strength Data Sheet [3] and
the V AMAS Low Cycle Fatigue international project [4,5].
To observe crack initiation sites, a field emission-type
scanning electron microscope (FE-SEM) was used.

4. Discussion
The fatigue fracture appearances suggest that the fatigue
crack initiated at the specimen surface in the low-cycle region
as an ordinary fatigue fracture in both the alloys. Both the
alloys showed no notable differences in the normalized
fatigue strengths. On the other hand, in the high-cycle region,
the fractures were initiated from the facets.
As shown in Fig. 4 , it is thought that the facets were
formed by the slip-off along {111} slip plane (glide plane
decohesion). The facets were observed in both the alloys.
However, it is still not understood why this fatigue fracture
initiated from the facets, not at the specimen surface as in an
ordinary fatigue fracture.

non-metallic inclusion inside the specimen surface [7,8].A s

following equation:

5. Conclusions
Nickel-base Alloy718 with different austenite grain size
were tested for their fatigue properties at high temperatures.
The resultant conclusions are:
(1) The fatigue strength of the coarse-grain alloy
decreased notably beyond 10
5 cycles. The normalized
fatigue strength at 10 7 cycles was 0.32.
(2) The fatigue strength for the fine-grain alloy did not
notably decrease beyond 10 5 cycles. The normalized
fatigue strength of the fine-grain alloy at 10 7 cycles
was 0.51.
