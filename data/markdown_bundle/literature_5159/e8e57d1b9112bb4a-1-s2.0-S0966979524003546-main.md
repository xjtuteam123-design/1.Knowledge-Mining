Nb to the Fe-Co-Cr-Ni-based alloy system, and utilizes a model of the relationship between elements and phase 
structures. Finally, EHEAs are successfully designed with the help of phase diagrams, namely FeCo -

1. Introduction
The poor castability, phase separation, and lack of coordination be -
tween mechanical properties have affected the engineering application 
value of traditional high-entropy alloys (HEAs) [ 1 – 3 ]. EHEAs with low 
melting points, high thermal conductivity, and heterogeneous structures 
can effectively address these issues and can be easily adapted for 
large-scale industrial production and applications [ 4 ]. According to the 
microstructure, EHEAs can be divided into two types: one type utilizes 
pure hard phase eutectics as functional materials, while the other type 
employs alternating eutectics of hard and soft phases for load-bearing 
structures [ 5 ]. Comparatively, the latter holds more value in structural 
applications research.
Up to now, over 300 articles related to EHEAs have been published, 
but few of them address the design methods of EHEAs [ 5 ]. Existing 
design strategies for EHEAs are mainly based on “ eutectic points ” , such 
as the mixed enthalpy method [ 6 ], the simple mixed enthalpy method 
[ 7 ] and the pseudo-binary method [ 8 ]. These methods can only explore 
eutectic points of fixed alloy proportions, which is not efficient in un -
derstanding all eutectic systems. Dong et al. [ 9 ] early on noted that 
research should not only focus on one eutectic point in multi-element 
HEA systems (e.g., AlCoCrFeNi), but should also pay attention to 
“ eutectic lines ” or “ eutectic surfaces ” . Shafiei et al. [ 10 ] also studied 
eutectics in the AlCoCrFeNi system, with a focus on the concept of 
“ eutectic lines ” . Wu et al. [ 11 ] relying on machine learning methods, 
classified key elements, strongly correlated elements, and mixable ele -
ments, and successfully predicted the comprehensive eutectic compo -
sition of the AlCoCrFeNi HEA. Transitioning from “ eutectic points ” to 
“ eutectic lines/surfaces ” is a trend in designing new EHEAs. The 
research of the above scholars is based on existing binary/trinary

This study explores the microstructures and mechanical properties of 
a series of HEAs, focusing particularly on eutectic HEAs containing re -
fractory elements. Eutectic HEAs exhibit unique solidification behavior, 
with their microstructures consisting of both FCC and HCP phases. In 
binary phase diagrams, the eutectic line delineates the temperature and 
composition range where liquid and two solid phases coexist. The 
eutectic point corresponds to a specific composition and temperature, 
where the liquid phase crystallizes into two solid phases simultaneously. 
For multicomponent alloys, the eutectic surface represents the temper -
ature and composition range where liquid and multiple solid phases 
coexist, depicting the three-dimensional relationship between compo -
sition and temperature. Therefore, this paper aims to design 
structurally-functional EHEAs containing high-temperature-resistant 
alloy elements, elucidating the design steps from alloy element selec -
tion to establishing complete EHEAs, and thereby deepening design 
theory. This is of significant reference value for EHEA design through 
“ eutectic points ” . Additionally, obtaining novel six-element EHEAs is 
beneficial for enriching existing EHEAs databases and facilitating the 
derivation of “ eutectic lines/surfaces ” in subsequent studies.
2. Experimental procedures
2.1. Standard and process for the establishment of EHEAs
After nearly a decade of development, the casting of EHEAs has 
accumulated a wealth of data, prompting us to explore existing EHEAs in 
an attempt to achieve rapid design of high-performance structural 
EHEAs. Currently, EHEAs designed based on elements such as Co, Cr, Fe 
and Ni (or a combination thereof) can exhibit phase structures including 
FCC, BCC (Body-centered Cubic), Laves, IMC (Intermetallic Compound), 
and NMC (Nanocrystalline Matrix), as shown in Fig. 1 (a). Similarly, 
EHEAs designed based on refractory alloying elements can achieve 
similar types of phase structures. However, in refractory EHEAs 
(REHEAs), the FCC phase is replaced by carbides, which represent 
distinctly brittle and hard phase structures, as illustrated in Fig. 1 (b). 
Therefore, this paper aims to design structural EHEAs satisfying high- 
temperature performance by utilizing elements such as Co, Cr, Fe and 
Ni as a base, combined with the addition of high-temperature alloying 
elements and employing a rational design approach.
This paper selects the Fe-Co-Cr-Ni-based alloy system and conducts 
design work under the guidance of the (FCC + Laves) EHEAs database 
(gray area in Fig. 2 ) and empirical parameters (such as Δ H

A KUSITE K-ZGF-0.04 suspension smelting furnace was used for 
prepare alloy castings in the casting verification process. All the alloying 
powders have a purity of 99.0 – 99.5 % and granularity of 50 – 80 μ m. The 
thermal cycles during the smelting process are shown in Fig. 3 (a). High 
purity (99.999 %) argon was used as a shielding gas, and the smelting 
process was repeated four times to ensure that the compositions of the 
casted EHEAs were uniform. The casting samples were in a button shape 
with a weight of 100 g and a diameter of 40 mm, as shown in Fig. 3 (b). 
The design basis for the thermal cycling curve of the melting process 
shown in Fig. 3 primarily includes the alloy composition and its melting 
point, eutectic point and phase diagram, as well as key stages during 
melting and solidification. Firstly, the heating temperature of the ther -
mal cycling curve is designed higher than the melting point of all alloy 
components to ensure complete alloy melting. Subsequently, based on 
the eutectic point and eutectic temperature determined by the phase 
diagram, an appropriate holding time is set to observe and analyze 
eutectic reactions. During the cooling process, different cooling media 
(such as air, water, oil, etc.) and temperature control equipment are used 
to control the cooling rate. The combination of temperature-controlled 
furnaces, thermocouples, and control systems allows precise adjust -
ment of the cooling rate, ensuring process controllability and repro -
ducibility. Additionally, adjusting the size and shape of the sample can 
indirectly affect the cooling rate, further optimizing the formation of 
microstructure.

In the XRD characterization results, the Laves phase typically ex -
hibits distinct crystal structures, such as C15, C14 and C36 types, which 
are composed of larger and smaller atoms. The diffraction peaks of the 
Laves phase are characterized by multiple high-intensity peaks, with 
their positions related to specific lattice parameters. The lattice pa -
rameters of the Laves phase may depend on the constituent elements, 
resulting in different diffraction patterns for Laves phases with varying 
compositions.
In contrast, the HCP phase is a typical hexagonal crystal structure 
characterized by high symmetry and close packing. The diffraction 
peaks of the HCP phase generally exhibit a characteristic hexagonal 
symmetrical arrangement, with common peaks including (002), (100) 
and (101). The spacing and intensity patterns of the HCP phase 
diffraction peaks are also related to specific lattice parameters, showing 
a more regular spacing. While the Laves phase may present more peaks 
with uneven intensities, the HCP phase typically displays symmetrical 
and uniform peaks. The diffraction pattern of the HCP phase showcases 
hexagonal symmetry, whereas the Laves phase pattern may be more 
complex. XRD results corroborate the presence of both FCC and HCP 
phase structures, as shown in Fig. 4 (d) and (i) .

4. Discussion
EHEAs are designed to achieve alloys with both high strength and 
high ductility. According to existing literature [ 24 , 28 – 31 ], although it is 
possible to achieve a good balance between ultimate strength and 
ductility in EHEAs, it is extremely challenging to simultaneously have 
sufficiently high yield strength and ductility due to factors such as grain 
size or properties of strengthening phases. As early as 2015, Liu et al. 
from City University of Hong Kong [ 32 ] found that the CoCrFeNiNb
x 
(x 
≤ 0.412) alloy exhibited significant potential to become a 
high-temperature structural material due to its yield strength and plastic 
strain. More recently, F.P. et al. [ 33 ] utilized mechanically alloyed 
CoCrFeNiNb HEA to break the performance bottleneck of 
high-temperature resistant HEAs, achieving a maximum stress ( σ
max
) of 
up to 2.41 GPa. Although there are fewer reports on hexa-element 
EHEAs currently, all these data indicate that the new multi-element 
high-temperature resistant EHEAs system can expand the known struc -
tural application performance boundaries and enrich the existing EHEAs 
database. This allows the concept of designing eutectic structures to 
become an effective method for preparing high-performance HEAs, ul -
timately demonstrating significant potential for improving energy effi -
ciency and system performance in many specific fields.
The mechanical properties of the novel EHEAs designed in this study 
consistently exhibit a dual-phase synergistic response of FCC and HCP 
phases. In EHEAs, the enhancement of plasticity in the HCP phase may 
be influenced by the complex back stress state of the FCC phase. 
Furthermore, during compression deformation, the alternating phase 
structure becomes more heterogeneous, implying that dynamically 
strengthened heterogeneous structures lead to strong back stress effects. 
In relevant studies, the back stress model has been shown to enhance the 
plasticity of hard phases in nanostructures by mediating metastability, i. 
e., when dislocations accumulate at phase boundaries to a certain

The distribution of alloying elements in the two EHEAs is identical 
apart from the differences in Mo and V elements. It is well known that 
the composition of elements directly affects the microstructure of HEAs, 
thereby influencing their micro or macro mechanical responses, espe -
cially considering that the interface structure between FCC and HCP 
phases may also be influenced by eutectic growth patterns. On one hand, 
heterogeneous nucleation conditions require compatibility or semi- 
compatibility between phases at the interface [ 39 ]; on the other hand, 
due to the strongly coupled growth mechanism of EHEAs, selecting 
crystal planes that are dynamically favorable (i.e., low-energy phase 
interfaces) provides more opportunities for continuous nucleation [ 40 ]. 
Therefore, eutectic structures imply that the nucleation of initial FCC 
and HCP phases needs to be satisfied by low-energy phase interfaces 
between the two phases, otherwise, it would be difficult to produce 
semi-compatible or fully compatible interfaces. As proposed by Sutton 
and Balluffi [ 41 ], the high-density CSL layers corresponding to 
low-energy states are preferred. As mentioned above, lower lattice 
mismatch corresponds to lower interfacial energy, providing the most 
favorable crystallographic correspondence for crossing interfaces be -
tween two crystals [ 42 ]. In this case, the OR of the interfaces between 
the two phases has smaller lattice mismatch parameters, corresponding 
to shorter distances between atoms parallel or nearly parallel to the 
periodic planes in the low-energy state and high plane density in -
terfaces, with deviation angles less than 5
◦
between crystals. In other 
words, the parallel relationship of close-packed planes may be a 
convenient method to determine the low-energy phase interfaces and 
their orientation relationships (OR). Mo, with a larger atomic radius, 
differs significantly in size from other atoms, making it more likely to 
aggregate in the HCP phase [ 24 ], while V, with a smaller radius, tends to 
aggregate in the FCC phase. Therefore, lattice mismatch between the 
two phases and the variation in interfacial energy due to the occupation 
of different lattice positions by atoms are the main reasons for the two 
different matching modes. It is believed that heterogeneous phase in -
terfaces contribute significantly to the strength of EHEAs. This is mainly 
related to the mismatch strengthening effect of shear modulus and lat -
tice parameters, i.e., interface strengthening effects involving 
dislocation-interface interactions [ 43 – 46 ]. In dual-phase systems, the 
greater the difference in shear modulus between alternating layers, the 
greater the impedance to dislocation pile-ups [ 47 ]. This paper tested the 
mechanical performance indicators of the novel high-entropy alloys in 
high-temperature environments, and future research results are also 
eagerly anticipated.
EHEAs have garnered widespread attention due to their potential 
superior mechanical properties. Existing research indicates that the 
combination of FCC and BCC phases can achieve a balance between 
strength and ductility to some extent [ 4 , 8 – 10 , 13 , 16 , 22 , 29 – 31 ]. How -
ever, the introduction of the Laves phase often increases the brittleness 
of the material, especially under high-temperature conditions, which 
restricts the alloy ’ s ductility. Therefore, this study aims to alloy Mo, V 
and Nb elements to regulate the microstructure of Fe-Ni-Co-Cr-based 
alloys, exploring how to enhance the alloy ’ s ductility at high tempera -
tures while maintaining high strength. The focus of this research is on 
the combination of FCC and HCP phases, attempting to improve the 
mechanical properties of the alloy through the enhancement of inter -
phase interface characteristics.
The innovation of this study lies in systematically revealing the 
importance of FCC and HCP phases in the mechanical performance of 
alloys through experimental and computational analyses. This research 
offers a new perspective for the design of future EHEAs, particularly for 
performance optimization in high-temperature environments. Addi -
tionally, it addresses the lack of exploration of the HCP phase in existing 
literature, providing valuable references for the materials science com -
munity. This research not only enhances the high-temperature perfor -
mance of specific EHEAs but also establishes a new foundation for the 
design theories of HEAs.
