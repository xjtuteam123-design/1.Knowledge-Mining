materials for applications in counter balance weights, vibration dampers, gyroscopes, pendulums and so on.

deformability synergy of the novel high-density MCAs.
1. Introduction
High-density materials can be applied in a variety of fields, such as 
kinetic energy penetrators, gyroscope rotors, counter balance weights, 
radiation shields, vibration dampers [ 1 – 5 ]. Tungsten heavy alloys 
(WHAs), characterized by a dual-phase structure composed of 
body-centered cubic (BCC) W-grains and face-centered cubic (FCC) 
binder phase, are one of the typical high-density materials. They are 
mainly consisted of W and a small amount of transition elements 
including Ni, Fe and Co [ 6 – 8 ]. To fabricate the WHAs, liquid phase 
sintering (LPS) has been widely used [ 9 , 10 ]. However, to obtain 
densified and uniform microstructures, the temperature in LPS is fairly 
high and the holding time is long due to the high melting point of 
tungsten. Accordingly, grain growth is inevitable during the traditional 
LPS. This induces a critical issue as the coarse W-grains are usually 
detrimental to the deformability of WHAs at room temperature [ 11 – 13 ].
Efforts have been devoted to optimize the mechanical properties of 
WHAs by adjusting the microstructural features, such as BCC W-grain 
size, contiguity of FCC binder phase, volume fraction of BCC W-matrix 
and solubility of W in the binder phase [ 14 ]. Among these, grain 
refinement is one of the most effective methods. Fine grains can be 
achieved through adjusting parameters during synthesis by mechanical 
alloying [ 15 ], microwave sintering [ 16 ], spark plasma 
sintering/field-assisted sintering [ 17 , 18 ], laser additive manufacturing 
[ 19 ], etc. Thermomechanical processing after sintering, such as swaging 
[ 20 ], can be employed to further refine the grains [ 21 ]. Also, the 
appropriate addition of solute elements (e.g., Mo and Re) and ceramic 
particles (e.g., La
2
O
3
), can improve the mechanical properties of WHAs 
[ 11 , 22 , 23 ]. For instance, the addition of Mo can not only introduce solid 
solution strengthening, but also decrease the solid solubility of W in the 
ductile binder phase which in turn refine the W-grains [ 24 ]. In addition, 
the introduction of multicomponent high-entropy alloy (HEA) as a 
binder phase, or the mixing of multi-principal element to form 
tungsten-rich bulk multicomponent alloys (MCAs) has been recently 
demonstrated to be promising approaches for obtaining 
high-performance high-density materials [ 8 , 25 – 27 ]. However, devel -
opment of fine-grained high-density MCAs with enhanced performance 
via efficient processing technique is still challenging.

2. Experimental procedures
2.1. Alloy design
The first step of this work involves searching for appropriate alloy 
compositions. Although most works about conventional WHAs were 
performed with Ni/Fe = 7:3 (wt %) [ 4 , 13 , 24 ], in this work we reduce 
the ratio to nearly 1:1 for lower cost with fewer Ni. It has been reported 
that the appropriate addition of Mo can optimize the performance of 
high-density alloys by restricting the grain growth and facilitating the 
liquid phase penetration into the boundaries of BCC matrix [ 28 , 29 ]. In 
addition, sufficiently high Mo content may promote the precipitation of 
μ phase to provide additional strengthening effect apart from the solid 
solution strengthening by dissolved Mo in the BCC matrix [ 30 ]. 
Accordingly, the content of Mo was preliminarily determined to be 25 % 
(at %). According to the quotation from the supplier (Shanghai Shuitian 
Technology), the original price of pure Mo (~2.5 ¥ /g) is relatively high 
compared to W (~1.5 ¥ /g), Fe (~1 ¥ /g) and Ni (~1.5 ¥ /g). Nevertheless,

The approximately linear relationship shown in Fig. 12 confirms that 
the strengthening effect at yielding point in the present bulk MCAs is 
mainly contributed by the BCC matrix. More specifically, solid solution 
strengthening and grain/phase boundary strengthening are the main 
aspects. Solid solution strengthening in the BCC matrix of the present 
bulk high-density MCAs is realized mainly by the dissolution of high 
amount of Mo atoms into the W lattice, and a few Fe and Ni can also 
contribute by dissolving into the BCC structure ( Table 3 ).With regards to 
the grain/phase boundary strengthening, the refinement of BCC matrix 
grains plays an important role, which is related to the factors below: the 
sluggish diffusion in the W-Mo-rich MCAs; the nanocrystalline structure 
in the powders formed by repeated fracture and cold welding during 
high energy ball milling; the relatively low sintering temperature and 
relatively short sintering time. Moreover, the alloying of Mo can 
decrease the solid solubility of W in the FCC binder phase, thus impedes 
the dissolving-reprecipitation rate of W and contribute to the refinement 
of BCC matrix grains [ 24 ]. Further, Mo can facilitate the penetration of 
the liquid phase into the boundaries between BCC grains during 
high-temperature sintering and thus increases the fraction of phase 
boundary [ 28 ].
It should also be noted that solid solution strengthening effect in the 
FCC binder phase can also be significant since a substantial amount of 
Mo and W atoms have been dissolved in the Fe-Ni rich FCC lattice which 
can lead to significant lattice distortion hindering the dislocation motion 
[ 24 , 40 ]. The solid solution strengthening effect in FCC binder phase can 
diminish the mechanical contrast between the FCC binder and the BCC 
matrix, and thus is fairly favorable for the adaptive deformation of the 
phases.
The present bulk high-density MCAs also possess significant strain 
hardening ability and hence high compressive strain and ultimate 
strength. The underlying toughening effect is mainly contributed by the 
above discussed adaptive deformation of BCC matrix and FCC binder 
phase (Section 4.1 ). Note that the high fraction of phase interfaces is 
conductive to the adaptive deformation behavior, including the exten -
sive dislocation activities in BCC matrix, nano-twining and formation of 
9R structure in FCC binder. This can be confirmed by the fact that the 
strain hardening capacity of the bulk sample sintered at 1250

phase ( Figs. 8 and 9 a) can provide microcrack toughening effect by 
absorbing some mechanical energy and transferring the load [ 38 , 41 ].
5. Conclusions
In this work, fine-grained high-density MCAs with enhanced per -
formance were fabricated by mechanical alloying and sintering. The 
milled powders and sintered bulk alloys were investigated to compre -
hend the structural evolution during the powder metallurgy process. 
Mechanical behavior and the underlying deformation mechanisms were 
also systematically revealed. The following conclusions can be drawn: 
(1) With the optimized alloy design and powder metallurgy process, 
fine-grained (average grain size smaller than 3 μ m) bulk

matrix is W-Mo enriched, the FCC binder phase is Fe-Ni enriched, 
and the minor μ phase shows high concentrations of the four 
principal elements.
