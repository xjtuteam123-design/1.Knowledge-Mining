ABSTRACT
The substandard high-temperature performance of Magnesium (Mg) alloys has long been a critical issue limiting 
their use in industries such as aerospace and transportation. Stacking faults (SFs) can serve as effective dislo -
cation barriers for Mg alloys at elevated temperatures due to their excellent thermal stability at coherent in -
terfaces with the matrix. Currently, most methods for introducing SFs involve deformation techniques. In this 
work, high-density nano-spaced SFs and precipitates are introduced into WE43 alloy by laser-powder bed fusion 
(L-PBF) combined with heat treatment, leading to its superior mechanical properties demonstrated at both room 
and elevated temperatures. The ultimate tensile strengths of the T6 alloys at 25
◦
C, 200
◦
C, 250
◦
C and 300
◦
C were 
382 ± 5 MPa, 325 ± 3 MPa, 317 ± 6 MPa, and 275 ± 3 MPa, respectively, which were approximately 62 %, 
15 %, 28 % and 58 % higher than those of the as-deposited alloys. The microstructural evolution and 
strengthening mechanism of the laser-powder bed fused (L-PBFed) WE43 Mg with heat treatment alloy were 
systematically investigated. The excellent room temperature strength of the as-deposited alloy is attributed to the 
in-situ precipitated β ′ , β
1, 
and β phases. However, the as-deposited alloy exhibits a significant decrease in strength 
with increasing temperature, due to the presence of element segregation, grain boundary sliding, and poorly 
thermally stable precipitation strengthening. Following solid solution heat treatment, the element segregation at 
the melt pool boundary of L-PBFed WE43 was eliminated and the in-situ precipitated phases were dissolved into 
the Mg-matrix. In addition, the combination of rapid solidification during L-PBF and subsequent low-temperature 
solution heat treatment leads to the formation and preservation of a significant density of dislocations inside the 
grains. During prolonged aging treatment, dislocation facilitated the segregation of Zn atoms, leading to a local 
reduction in stacking fault energy (SFE). This reduction in SFE promoted the formation of high-density I

absence of additional 1/3[1 – 10] shear. The enhanced room and elevated temperature strength of heat treated L- 
PBFed WE43 Mg alloy can be primarily attributed to the synergistic strengthening effects of precipitates and 
nano-spacd SFs.

1. Introduction
With their low density, high specific strength, cost-effectiveness, and 
recyclability, Mg alloys hold considerable appeal for researchers in the 
field of lightweight materials [1] . Mg alloys are expected to be utilized in 
high-temperature components, such as aircraft trusses, aero engines, 
and pistons to realize the goal of lightweight. These components 
necessitate the alloys to maintain good thermal stability and strength 
within the range of 200 – 300
◦
C [2] . Recently, additive manufacturing 
(AM), as a promising manufacturing technique, has proven capable of 
fabricating complex components unachievable with conventional tech -
nology [3,4] . Meanwhile, Mg rare-earth (Mg-RE) alloys have significant 
advantages in room and high temperature mechanical properties due to 
the small atomic diffusion coefficients of RE atoms and precipitation 
strengthening [5 – 7] . The addition of RE elements, which exhibit poor 
diffusion capabilities, can effectively reduce the diffusion behavior of 
Mg atoms within grains and suppress grain boundary penetration, 
thereby slowing down intragranular diffusion. Furthermore, RE atoms 
can form high-melting-point secondary phases, which will effectively 
hinder dislocation movement. Therefore, Mg-RE alloys are considered 
one of the development directions for high-strength and 
high-temperature applications [8] . In recent years, several studies have 
prepared high Mg-RE alloys with excellent room-temperature strength 
via the AM process. Deng et al. [9] prepared Mg-10Gd-3Y-1Zn-0.4Zr 
alloys via L-PBF, reaching a strength of 400 MPa at room temperature 
after heat treatment. Hyer et al. [10] prepared dense WE43 alloys with a 
strength of 251 MPa through optimized process parameters and heat 
treatment. Tong et al. [11] prepared WE43 alloys with a strength of 
347 MPa through the in-situ heat treatment effect of the quasi-directed 
energy deposition. Based on the evaluation indicators of strength at 
room temperature, additive manufacturing seems to be one of the 
potentially promising techniques for high-strength Mg alloys. According 
to prior research, the additive manufacturing technique has certain 
negative effects on high-temperature performance.
As is widely recognized, laser powder bed fusion (L-PBF) manufac -
tured alloys often exhibit inherent defects including porosity and so -
lidification cracks, which are particularly pronounced in chemically 
reactive Mg alloys. A case in point is the study by Deng et al. [9] who 
observed substantial porosity and cracking in L-PBF processed 
Mg-10Gd-3Y-1Zn-0.4Zr alloys even with optimized parameters, attrib -
uting these defects to rapid solidification rates and associated thermal 
stresses. These microstructural imperfections become critically detri -
mental at elevated temperatures, as dislocation movement preferentially 
concentrates around pores and crack tips, creating stress concentration 
zones that accelerate material failure [12] . The layer-wise 
manufacturing mechanism of L-PBF also introduces additional chal -
lenges. First, element segregation frequently occurs at melt pool 
boundaries due to directional solidification patterns. Second, while 
grain refinement is typically considered advantageous in additive 
manufacturing, it paradoxically promotes grain boundary sliding at high 
temperatures, leading to strength degradation. This phenomenon was 
quantitatively demonstrated by Jia et al. [13] where 60 % fine grain 
boundaries in L-PBFed Al-Mn-Sc alloys accelerated dislocation annihi -
lation and reduced creep resistance. Regarding precipitation strength -
ening, in-situ thermal cycling during L-PBF generates metastable phases 
( β’ and β
1
) that initially enhance strength, as evidenced in GA151K and 
WE43 magnesium alloys [11,14,15] . However, these beneficial phases 
exhibit thermal instability. At elevated temperatures, β’ phases undergo 
rapid transformation to stable β phases, while existing β precipitates 
experience coarsening leading to solute depletion. This dual process not 
only diminishes precipitation strengthening but also creates 
precipitate-free zones (PFZs) that compromise matrix integrity [16,17] . 
These interrelated issues (porosity, element segregation, thermally 
activated dislocation movement, grain boundary sliding, and unstable 
precipitation phase) collectively restrict the high-temperature applica -
bility of L-PBFed Mg alloys.
As a precipitate-strengthened alloy, Mg-RE systems primarily rely on 
heat treatment to modify their microstructure and precipitate charac -
teristics for performance enhancement. Notably, high rare-earth Mg-RE- 
Zn alloys can develop heat-resistant LPSO structures through thermal 
processing. However, LPSO formation requires stringent compositional 
conditions, particularly high Zn and rare earth contents, which simul -
taneously increase thermal cracking susceptibility and material costs 
[18] . In recent years, researchers have found that SFs exhibit unique 
excellent thermal stability, effectively suppressing dislocation move -
ment at high temperatures [18] . Jian et al. [19] proposed that 
nano-spaced stacking faults (SFs) could provide a barrier to dislocation 
motion. Zhang et al. [20] demonstrated that introducing basal plane SFs 
in α -Mg grains could have a good strengthening effect at high temper -
atures, thereby improving the heat resistance of the alloy. SFs are 
generally believed to be caused by defects. SFs are generally believed to 
be caused by defects [21] , so most current research introduces 
high-density SFs in alloys through extrusion [18,22] . Unlike 
composition-dependent approaches, deformation-induced SFs can be 
generated independently of specific material compositions, thereby 
overcoming the inherent limitations associated with compositional 
constraints. Nevertheless, the deformation process presents significant 
challenges in fabricating complex-shaped components with high per -
formance characteristics. During the L-PBF process, there is a rather 
complex non-equilibrium solidification behavior (rapid solidification, 
remelting, and thermal cycles), which will lead to many defects and 
distortions in the metal [23,24] . Moreover, existing studies have 
demonstrated the presence of various in-situ precipitates in L-PBF Mg 
alloy samples, leading to a significantly uneven distribution of elements 
within the magnesium matrix. These defects, along with the varied 
elemental distribution, offer favorable nucleation conditions for the 
introduction and regulation of SFs at later stages [25] . Therefore, L-PBF 
is expected to improve high-temperature mechanical properties by 
introducing SFs without inducing plastic deformation and adjusting the 
material composition. This way is highly attractive for enhancing the 
high-temperature performance of L-PBFed Mg alloys. However, current 
reports on the fabrication of high-density SF-containing L-PBFed Mg 
alloys and their corresponding high-temperature mechanical properties 
are scarce. The defects and in-situ precipitates produced by L-PBF have 
failed to garner significant attention from most researchers [4] .
In this study, WE43 Mg alloy was fabricated via L-PBF. A novel 
solution-aging heat treatment were applied to reconfigure the micro -
structure and optimize the mechanical properties of the as-deposited 
alloys. The effects of heat treatments on the mechanical performance 
of L-PBFed WE43 alloys at room and elevated temperatures were sys -
tematically investigated. The effects of heat treatment on the evolution 
behaviors of grains, phases and defects were clarified by systematic 
microstructure characterization. The formation mechanism of the 
combined morphology between the β
1 
phase and SFs during heat 
treatment, as well as the corresponding strengthening mechanisms, 
were further elucidated. Based on the above analysis, the relationships 
between microstructure, strengthening mechanism and failure behavior 
of as-deposited and heat treated WE43 alloy were established. This work 
provides new insights into heat treatment protocols for L-PBFed WE43 
alloy, and offers an experimental and theoretical foundation for the 
application of L-PBFed Mg alloys in high-temperature environments.

3.2. Microstructure of as-deposited and heat treated WE43 Mg alloys
3.2.1. Macro morphology, grain, and texture evolution
Fig. 5 shows the macro optical morphologies and EBSD results of the 
L-PBFed WE43 alloy before and after heat treatment. As can be seen 
from Fig. 5 a, no obvious pores are found in the as-deposited WE43 Mg 
alloy, indicating that the alloy has low porosity. Before heat treatment, 
the as-deposited WE43 Mg alloy has typical melt pool characteristics of 
additive manufacturing after corrosion. Jia et al. [13] pointed out that 
the unique melt pool characteristics caused by element segregation were 
considered weak mechanical areas. During the deformation stage, stress 
was easier to concentrate on the melt pool boundary to induce cracks. 
After solution treatment ( Fig. 5 b and 5 c), the element segregation at the 
melt pool boundary was removed, which has positive implications for 
avoiding early fracture of tensile samples. In addition, the IPF map re -
sults show that the as-deposited alloy has a bimodal grain distribution 
( Fig. 5 d- 5 i). The coarse grains of the anomalously grown grains have 
high texture intensity, while the texture intensity of the fine grains is 
weak. The bimodal grain characteristics and the strong texture behavior 
of coarse grains are related to the thermal cycle and recrystallization 
during the L-PBF process. According to the findings of Yin et al. [26] the 
fast solidification rate effect of L-PBF would easily form fine grains; 
however, subsequent laser scanning would promote the growth of grains 
in the previous layers. When the n + 1 layer laser scans, part of the 
grains of the n layer are remelted or coarsened, and grow in the direction 
of the temperature gradient. Although fine grain strengthening mecha -
nisms can significantly improve mechanical properties, grain boundary 
instability mechanisms (grain boundary migration, grain boundary slip, 
grain boundary diffusion, and other grain boundary instability mecha -
nisms) can lead to grain boundary softening, eliminating the grain 
boundary strengthening effect. After T4 and T6 heat treatments, the 
grains are significantly coarsened, which increases the grain boundary 
slip resistance at high temperatures. The characteristic of fine grain area 
with weak texture intensity in the as-deposited alloy was eliminated, 
which led to a further increase in the texture intensity. According to the 
statistical results of the grains ( Fig. 6 ), after the T4 heat treatment, the 
proportion of fine grains decreases from 30 % to 18 %, and the pro -
portion of LAGB also increases. After peak aging, the alloy has a slight 
reduction in grain size compared with the T4 alloy, which may be caused 
by experimental errors. It can be seen that the heat treatment process 
coarsens the original fine grains and forms a stronger texture intensity, 
which is beneficial for texture strengthening and limiting the grain 
boundary slip behavior at high temperatures. Fig. 5 j- 5 l shows the KAM 
result of alloys in different states. The decrease in the error of the 
average value of KAM after heat treatment indicates that T6 heat 
treatment affects homogenizing stress. In addition, it was unexpectedly

Generally, alloys with FCC and HCP structures that have low SFE or 
nanocrystalline tend to form SFs during plastic deformation. Ban et al. 
[57] introduced a large number of SFs through liquid nitrogen dynamic 
plastic deformation. Jian et al. [19] found that as the thickness of the 
hot-rolled Mg alloy increased, the main defects in the Mg alloy transi -
tioned to SFs. In addition to applying shear stress, the alloying can 
effectively reduce the SFE of the alloy, further promoting the formation 
of SFs. At present, many researchers have confirmed that the addition of 
elements such as Y, Zn, and Yb elements could effectively reduce the SFE 
of Mg alloys [49,50,58] . Although the WE43 alloys contain Y and Zn 
elements, which may contribute to the reduction of the SFE, the Zn 
content of the WE43 alloy is very limited compared to traditional Mg 
alloys with high SFs densities [22,59] . Moreover, in this work, the 
content of Y was further reduced due to the presence of oxides. This 
makes it difficult to meet the thermodynamic and kinetic requirements 
for the formation of SFs in WE43 alloys. Very few studies reported the 
formation of high-density SFs in the WE43 alloys manufactured by 
traditional processes [60,61] . Interestingly, the high density of SFs 
found in T6 alloy does not seem to satisfy the traditional formation 
mechanism. Researchers generally believe that SFs are caused by crystal 
defects [62,63] . However, our investigation reveals that the formation 
of SFs in the T6-treated L-PBF alloy is not solely due to crystal defects 
caused by the rapid non-equilibrium solidification, another critical 
contributing factor is the lattice distortions induced by the dissolution of 
different precipitates. In the as-deposited alloy, we found a large number 
of phases composed of different elements in grains. Following T4 heat 
treatment, most of these precipitates dissolved into the Mg matrix. This 
dissolution process is inherently non-uniform, leading to localized 
compositional fluctuations and, consequently, lattice distortions in the 
surrounding matrix. Wang et al. [21] pointed out that the lattice 
distortion caused by solute atoms can produce a non-negligible level of 
strain and stress, which resulted in the generation of SFs in Mg-Zn-Y 
alloy without mechanical deformation. Zou et al. [64] also found 
atomic segregation serves as a source of dislocations. Therefore, the 
synergistic effect of non-equilibrium solidification and solution treat -
ment ensures the retention of a high density of crystal defects inside the 
grains, thereby creating the necessary conditions for SF formation. In 
traditional cast alloys or wire-arc directed energy deposition, the so -
lidification rate is relatively low. As a result, eutectic phases tend to be 
distributed at grain boundaries. During solution heat treatment, the 
dissolution of these eutectic phases primarily induces crystal defects 
(dislocations or vacancies) near the grain boundaries, rather than inside 
the grains. [65,66] . Therefore, in solution heat treatment, crystal defects 
(dislocation or vacancies) caused by eutectic phase dissolution are often 
distributed near grain boundaries rather than inside the grains. How -
ever, in L-PBF alloys, in addition to the significant increase in the density 
of internal defects during rapid solidification, the distortion effects 
caused by the dissolution of in-situ precipitated phases also promote the 
formation of SF throughout the grains [23,25] . It is also noteworthy that 
solution heat treatment in previous studies often led to dislocation 
reduction due to recrystallization. However, Zuiko et al. [67] pointed 
out that lower solution temperature can effectively reduce the migration 
rate of dislocations, thus retaining high-density dislocations before 
aging. Compared to the conventional heat treatment temperature of the 
WE43 alloy(above 500
◦
C), this study adopts a lower solution tempera -
ture of 450
◦
C. The lower temperature not only prevents excessive grain 
coarsening but also preserves crystal defects induced by lattice distor -
tion and rapid solidification, further promoting SF formation. STEM 
analysis ( Fig. 9 ) and EBSD results ( Fig. 5 ) confirmed that more dislo -
cations formed after T4 heat treatment, consistent with our hypothesis. 
Therefore, during the long-term T6 heat treatment, alloying elements 
would be noticeably gathered around SFs ( Fig. 10 h). This segregation of 
alloying elements lowers the SFE locally, making the formation of SFs 
energetically favorable. [54,68,69] .

strengthening mechanism are expected to be further applied in 
high-performance Mg alloys for high-temperature applications in the 
future. Additionally, the long-term service behavior and further 
fine-scale microstructural characterization of L-PBFed WE43 alloy at

and thermal stability of WE43 alloys under high-temperature 
conditions.

room and elevated temperature mechanical performances are signifi -

strengthening mechanisms are studied systematically. The following 
conclusions can be drawn:
