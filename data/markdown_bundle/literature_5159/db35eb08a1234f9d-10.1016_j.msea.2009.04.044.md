abstract
Turbopumps which pressurize fuels and oxidizers are generally used to operate liquid rocket engine for
space launch vehicles. It is considered very important to predict fatigue properties at elevated temper-
atures for the design and manufacturing of reliable turbopump systems. Hot isostatic pressing (HIP) is
one of the most promising manufacturing methods for gas turbine rotors. In this research, ﬁve kinds of
Ni-based superalloys (Inconel 718 HIP, Inconel 718 HIP and heat treated, MAR-M-247 HIP, MAR-M-247 HIP
and heat treated and Rene 95 HIP) were investigated at elevated temperatures. Original universal slopes
method, modiﬁed universal slopes method, Mitchell’s method, Bäumel and Seeger’s method, and Ong’s
method have been used for predicting the high temperature low-cycle fatigue performance. Among these
methods, Mitchell’s method showed the best adaptation results for the life prediction of Ni-based HIP
superalloys. A novel equation was suggested by modiﬁcation of Mitchell’s method to predict Ni-based HIP
superalloys at elevated temperature more accurately. The equation predicted well the low-cycle fatigue
of HIP superalloys as well as of cast Rene 80 at elevated temperatures.

1. Introduction
It is essential to develop a high powered light weight liquid
rocket engine for implementing space launch vehicles. To gain a
high thrust to weight ratio, most liquid rocket engines use turbop-
umps that pressurize fuels and oxidizers. Usually hot gas driven
turbine rotors are used to rotate the whole turbopump system;
therefore, it is very important to predict material behaviors at
elevated temperatures for designing and manufacturing a reliable
turbopump system. To develop a reliable turbopump rotor, nickel
and cobalt based superalloys are widely used. The development
of powder processed nickel-based superalloys for a gas turbine
engine has progressed since the 1970 s[1]. It has been demonstrated
that practically unworkable high strength nickel-based superal-
loys could be made readily workable by a combination of rapid
solidiﬁcation and isothermal metalworking technologies [2]. This
allowed the production of high strength powder processed discs
for high performance turbopump blades. With the powder metal-
lurgy technology, high strength alloys are ﬁrst atomized from the
melt into a powder which is subsequently consolidated into fully
dense compacts by hot extrusion or hot isostatic pressing (HIP)[1,2].
Consolidation produces compacts which have excellent mechani-

Ni-based HIP superalloys Inconel 718, MAR-M-247, and Rene 95
have been widely used for rotor turbine discs and blades [3]. These

they also have good moderate temperature low-cycle fatigue (LCF)

creep, stress rupture, fatigue and their combinations [4].
In this research, LCF tests of Ni-based superalloys for turbopump
turbine discs and blades have been performed and compared with
life prediction equations generally used in steels and aluminums to
estimate a reliable life equation.
2. Comparison of commonly used life prediction equations
For structural reliability, fatigue life prediction is very impor-
tant and it can be done with fatigue data. However, it is often
the case that fatigue data are not available. Due to a long testing
time and the high cost of fatigue testing, many researchers have
tried to obtain fatigue properties from simple tensile data. First,
Manson suggested the four-point correlation method and the uni-
versal slopes method [5], and some years later, Muralidharan and
Manson [6] suggested the new modiﬁed universal slopes method.
Mitchell [7] suggested another method focusing on steels, and

Bäumel and Seeger [8] developed another new method called the
uniform material law. Ong [9] examined widely used methods such
as the four-point correlation method, the original universal slopes
method, and the Mitchell’s method, and he observed that the four-
point correlation method and the original universal slopes method
give sufﬁcient results. Also, Ong suggested a modiﬁed four-point
correlation method.
Park and Song [10] tried to estimate the life prediction methods
using about 2700 data points of fatigue life from unalloyed steels,
low-alloy steels, high-alloy steels, aluminum alloys and titanium
alloys using sources such as the JSME data book, Materials Data for
Cyclic Loading on SAE data, and others. They showed the modiﬁed
universal slopes method, Seeger’s uniform material law, and Ong’s
modiﬁed four-point correlation method gave reasonably good life
predictions. Among these methods, the modiﬁed universal slopes
method gave the best results. On the other hand, the four-point
correlation method showed the worst correlation results for life
estimation.
Historically, fatigue life predictions of steels and aluminum
alloys at ambient temperature have been investigated by many
researchers; however, there was little research on fatigue life
prediction of nickel-based superalloys at elevated temperatures.
Consequently, a new estimation is needed for the adaptation of
such commonly used previous life prediction equations for ambient
temperatures.

3. Materials and testing methods
The chemical compositions of the materials are given in Table 1.
Three kinds of Ni-based superalloys (Inconel 718, MAR-M-247, and
Rene 95) have been used and fabricated through a hot isostatic
press (HIP). To ﬁnd the difference in properties due to heat treat-
ment, tests were also performed with heat treated Inconel 718 and
heat treated MAR-M-247. Therefore, ﬁve kinds of HIP materials
(Inconel 718 as-HIP, Inconel 718 HIP and heat treated, MAR-M-
247 as-HIP, MAR-M-247 HIP and heat treated, and Rene 95 as-HIP)
have been tested in this study. Tensile tests have been performed
at a strain rate of 2 × 10
−3/s in the temperature range from 600 ◦C
to 900 ◦C. Low-cycle fatigue tests have been performed at 627 ◦C
and 900 ◦C with strain control of 2 × 10−3/s. All specimens have
been manufactured according to ASTM E606-92, and temperatures
have been measured with a K-type thermocouple. A servo hydraulic
testing system with an induction heater was used for high tem-
perature loadings ( Fig. 1 ). An extensometer designed for elevated
temperatures with gauge length of 12 mm was used for the strain
measurement and control. Fatigue life has been deﬁned as a 10%
stress drop point of maximum stress amplitude.

8. Conclusions
For the reliability assessment of turbopumps in liquid rocket
engines, tensile tests and low-cycle fatigue tests of Ni-based HIP
superalloys (Inconel 718, MAR-M-247, Rene 95) were performed
at elevated temperatures, and the predictive accuracy of various
methods developed to estimate fatigue properties from simple ten-
sile data was quantitatively evaluated by using additional criteria
proposed in this work. The conclusions are summarized as follows:
(1) Life prediction methods (the original universal slope method,
the modiﬁed universal slope method, Mitchell’s method,
Seeger’s method and Ong’s method) were evaluated by the
method of the fraction of data, and the method of deviation.
(2) Mitchell’s method gave good predictions with the estimations
by fraction and by deviation. Other methods did not predict the
fatigue life.
(3) It was found that the modiﬁed universal slopes method which
gives the best results on steels and Ong’s method which gives
the best results on aluminum are not proper prediction methods
for Ni-based superalloys at elevated temperatures.
(4) A modiﬁed Mitchell’s method which is suggested in this
research gave the best results on correlation to HIP superalloys
and it predicted well the low-cycle fatigue of HIP superalloys as
well as of cast Rene 80 at elevated temperatures.
