ABSTRACT 
The effect of Si addition on the mechanical properties and deformation behaviour of the Cantor alloy in varying 
compositions from 1 to 5 atomic % has been investigated by experiments and first principle calculations. All alloy 
compositions exhibit single-phase FCC crystal structures with average grain size ranges from 125 – 140 μ m. Cantor 
alloy with 1 and 2 atomic % of Si shows an excellent combination of strength and ductility while 5 atomic % Si 
exhibits the lowest strength and highest ductility (⁓100 %). Detailed TEM analysis of deformed samples 
revealed the formation of high densities of closely spaced deformation twins in 1 and 2 atomic % Si at room 
temperature which was observed in Cantor alloy at cryogenic temperatures, while 5 atomic % Si shows the slip- 
mediated deformation with no observations of the twins. The dependency of the generalized stacking fault en -
ergy as a function of Si concentration was investigated by the first principle calculation for Si-containing alloys. 
The change in deformation mode from twinning for low Si content ( < 4 atomic %) to the dislocation-mediated 
slip for high Si content ( ≥ 4 atomic %) can be explained based on effective energy barrier, average interlayer 
distance, and interlayer distance distortion, which is in good agreement with experiments. The study showcases 
the unique contribution of Si in triggering multiple strengthening and strain hardening mechanisms by stacking 
fault mediated plasticity in Cantor alloy, thus highlighting the potency of minor alloying additions to single 
phase HEAs to achieve a large spectrum of strength-ductility paradigm. 
1. Introduction 
The development of new materials for advanced structural applica -
tions requires breaking the strength-ductility trade-off paradigm [1 – 4] . 
Globally, researchers are striving to overcome the strength and ductility 
paradox by exploring the unrevealed mechanism and microstructural 
evolution by tuning the alloy chemistry [ 5 , 6 ]. High strength and 
ductility can be achieved by introducing obstacles to the dislocation 
motion and by allowing the mobile dislocation to move under higher 
strain. To achieve this goal, compositional tuning plays an important 
role by contributing to lowering the stacking fault energy that facilitates 
the additional shear mechanisms such as twinning and martensitic 
transformations in addition to slips [7 – 9] . The recent advancement of 
the complex concentrated alloys (CCAs) or multi-principal multi-com -
ponent alloys [10 – 13] offers an extensive scope of the compositional 
tuning that can be used as an essential parameter for unlocking the 
multiple strengthening and strain hardening mechanisms to obtain a 
good balance between strength and ductility, excellent fracture 
toughness, damage tolerance, etc. Several single-phase high entropy 
alloys (ternary, quaternary, equiatomic, and non-equiatomic) show 
excellent mechanical properties at room temperature as well as cryo -
genic temperature [14 – 19] . The multidimensional compositional space 
offered by this new alloy design strategy is reasonably limitless, offering 
a unique opportunity to find the alloy chemistry for an optimum com -
bination of strength and ductility. 
In the present investigation, we investigate the effect of Si addition 
on the mechanical properties of equiatomic CoCrFeMnNi alloy also 
known as Cantor alloy. The Cantor alloy and its derivative have been 
extensively investigated in the last ten years [ 14 , 16 , 20 – 26 ]. It exhibits a 
spectrum of promising properties such as high-temperature structural 
stability [ 14 , 22 ] and excellent fracture toughness [23] . However, the 
room temperature strength is not much comparable to existing binary 
alloys. Attempts have been made to improve the room temperature 
mechanical property by the addition of some elements in varying 
amounts. Yin et.al. investigated the effect of V addition to Cantor alloy 
and there is a 2 to 2.5 times increase in the yield strength as compared to

% causes a decrease in stacking fault energy, promoting the planer 
motion of dislocations, requiring higher level stress rather than cross slip 
and thus causing an increase in strength [39] . 
In contrast, the goal of the current investigation is to increase the 
room temperature yield strength of the Cantor alloy by adding Si (1 to 5 
atom%) in progressive amounts while preserving the single-phase FCC 
crystal structure. The present study is carried out at a particular ther -
momechanical treatment (details were presented in the materials and 
methodology section) to investigate the effect of Si addition on the 
mechanical properties of the Cantor alloy. The experimental observa -
tions are best explained in the correlation of density functional theory 
(DFT) to comprehend the impact of the Si on the local lattice distortion 
in the vicinity of the silicon atom for the first nearest neighbor. None of 
the aforementioned investigations explain the experimental results in 
terms of local atomic configuration around Si atoms using first- 
principles calculations. In the present study, we observed that there is 
a continuous decrease in the local lattice distortion with Si concentra -
tion, which might cause the solid solution softening at the atomic level 
causing a decrease in strength and an increase in ductility. This is pri -
marily related to the decrease in Pierel ’ s stress. Besides, we also deter -
mined the SFE in the framework of Density-functional Theory. The SFE 
is a critical parameter that dictates the dominant plastic deformation 
mode, correspondingly affecting the mechanical properties. Low SFE 
enables the formation of stacking faults (SF) and twins. Twins facilitate 
the accumulation of partial dislocations causing a high degree of strain 
hardening thus causing a simultaneous increase in strength and ductility 
[40] . However, recent studies show that the SFE is not the only 
parameter that decides the competitive deformation mode of slip and 
twinning. Rather, the generalized stacking fault energy (GSFE) needs to 
be taken into consideration which is a combined representation of un -
stable stacking fault energy (USF), Intrinsic stacking fault energy (ISF), 
unstable twin fault energy (UTF), and extrinsic stacking fault energy 
(ESF) [ 40 , 41 ]. The physical interpretation of GSFE is the energy penalty 
between two adjacent planes when shearing inelastically in a particular 
direction on a given slip plane. 
Here, we report a detailed analysis of the structural and mechanical 
properties of equiatomic CoCrFeMnNi FCC alloy with progressive 
addition of the Si (1, 2, 3, 4, and 5 atomic %) by using the state-of-the-art 
material processing, testing, and characterization tools aided by the DFT 
calculation to estimate the GSFE and correlate to explain the underlying 
mechanism leading to excellent mechanical properties in the present 
alloys.

2.3. Methodology 
The supercell for the disordered structure was generated using a 
special quasirandom structure (SQS) [46] algorithm, for which we use 
the Alloy Theoretic Automated Toolkit (ATAT) [47] . We generated a 
nine-layer supercell for each composition. We consider different super -
cell sizes for each composition because of the constraint of the compo -
sitional space. The supercell sizes for each composition are given in 
Table 1 . We choose the supercell shape such that its sides have an 
orientation along the crystallographic direction [-110], [-101], and 
[111]: This is done to have convenience in generating stacking fault. For 
SQS generation, we took disordering up to the third nearest neighbor 
and considered clusters up to quadruplets. Multiple instances were run 
for each composition, and the selection of SQS was made based on the 
objective function. We use Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP) 
[48] for DFT calculations using Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) [49] 
functional. We chose an energy cutoff of 400eV which is based on our 
convergence tests, and for smearing, we use the first-order Methfes -
sel-Paxton [50] method with a smearing width of 0.1 eV. A conjugate 
gradient algorithm is used for carrying out volume relaxation using the 
convergence criteria of 10

and down magnetic spin in the whole SQS and performing a 
spin-polarized calculation.

4. Simulation Results 
The fault free FCC structure, which is a supercell with nine (111) FCC 
layers, is shown in Fig. 7 (a). Fig. 7 (b) and (c) show the supercell ’ s shear 
configurations after it was sheared along the red arrow-depicted 
displacement vector b which is in direction of [11 – 2] direction. The 
triclinic supercells explain the sheared configurations. The generalised 
stacking fault energy (GSFE), also referred to as the γ -surface, has been 
calculated using the supercell structure. The γ -surface is the energy

6. Discussion 
Role of silicon additions on the mechanical properties and de -

observed that with increasing Si concentration there is a decrease in 
strength and an increase in ductility. We have tried to explain it in terms 
of the local lattice distortion as a function of the Si content in the vicinity 
of each Si atom w.r.t. its first nearest neighbour using the first principal 
calculations. The Peierl-Nabarro barrier to the dislocation motion may 
reduce with increased Si concentration due to a decrease in local lattice 
distortion, which could result in the solid solution softening. It should be 
emphasised that solid solution softening, which is seen in various con -
ventional alloys, most notably BCC and HCP at lower temperatures, is 
the least understood phenomenon in FCC alloys [57] . This includes FCC

as they move across the crystal lattice. This is caused by the movement 
of a dislocation, which simultaneously breaks and forms local bonds. 
Here, we attempted to provide an explanation through DFT calculation 
finding that strength decreases as Si concentration rises. As shown in 
Fig. 10 (a), we estimated the local lattice distortion as a function of Si 
concentration ( Fig 10 (b)). As we can see from Fig. 10 (b), the increase in 
Si content gradually reduces local lattice distortion at the atomic level, 
allowing dislocations to move more easily and with less resistance, 
leading to a continuous decrease in strength and increase in ductility. 
While large local lattice distortion creates a landscape that is uneven or 
tortuous, dislocation can experience large amounts of frictional stress 
there and even occasionally become imprisoned in the deeper landscape, 
which necessitates enormous stress to overcome. 
The plastic deformation mechanism in low SFE materials is governed 
by the competition between slip and twin [60 – 63] . Cross slip is 
especially challenging in low SFE materials because the dislocations get 
impeded at twin boundaries [ 14 , 23 , 64 ]. The simultaneous occurrence 
and impediment of cross slip, which could result in the formation of 
HDDWs, Dislocations loops, Dislocations Cells, and Tangles, along with 
the twin results in a hierarchical microstructure at a higher strain level.

Recent research suggests that, rather than just the stacking fault 
energy, the generalized stacking fault energy (GSFE) represented by the 
USF, ISF, UTF, and ESF affects the deformation mechanisms in FCC 
materials. The traditional method of determining the deformation mode 
in materials (complete or partial dislocations and twinning) by 
employing SFE simply as a parameter is insufficient [ 61 , 62 , 65 , 66 ]. GSFE 
provides a detailed insight to understand the deformation mode tran -
sition in the framework of DFT. 
The greater propensity of entire dislocations separating into partials 
is indicated by the lower ratio of ISF to USF. The large difference be -
tween ISF and U.S.F. leads to the formation of the stacking fault, as we 
can observe the GSFE values from Table 3 . The deformations mechanism 
here is governed by the extended partial dislocations or stacking fault, 
also reported by Lizarraga et al. [67] . Still, they reported the experi -
mental results up to 10 % strain only. The generation of the stacking 
fault also promotes the FCC to HCP transformations with a progressive 
increase in strain. Still, the present study didn ’ t show any trace of the 
HCP phase investigated by the EBSD and TEM. Rather, it revealed 
deformation twins for different Si-containing alloys. If the FCC crystal is 
twinned, it can be qualitatively predicted using the twinnability 
approach in terms of relative energy barrier ( δ ). The energy barrier for 
the formation of micro twins (UTF) is lower than that for the formation 
of trailing partials (USF), according to the initial approximations from 
the GSFE curve and Table 3 . This suggests that the alloys will switch the 
deformation mechanism from dislocation-mediated slip to twinning 
mode.

7. Conclusion 
In the present investigation, the effect of Si addition in varying 
amounts (1, 2, 3, 4, and 5 atomic %) on the structural and mechanical 
properties of Cantor alloys have been discussed in conjugation with the 
Generalized Stacking Fault Energy calculated from the first principle 
calculation. The GSFE curves, relative barrier energy ( δ ), interlayer 
distance, and IDD have been estimated as a function of Si content and 
correlated with observed microstructure. Based on these following 
conclusions can be obtained. 
• The addition of the Si content in the progressive amount leads to the 
formation of single-phase FCC structures with almost similar grain 
sizes.
