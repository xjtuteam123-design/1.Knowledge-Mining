INTRODUCTION
The mechanical properties of single crystals of one of the
most refractory alloys ZhS32 chosen for the study have been
determined at temperatures lower than 1150°C [1]. At higher
temperatures, which are used for hot isostatic pressing, the
characteristics of strength and ductility of the alloy have not
been established. In the present work we studied the mecha-
nical properties of specimens of alloy ZhS32 at 1150 –
1250°C under conditions of testing for tensile and long-term
strength.
METHODS OF STUDY
Castable refractory alloy ZhS32 belongs to the class of
precipitation-hardening high-temperature carbon-bearing al -
loys based on nickel. The structure of the alloy is represented
by a nickel solid solution ( /c103-phase) alloyed with refractory
elements (W, Re, Ta, Mo) and precipitation-hardened by an
intermetallic /c103/c162-phase based on Ni
3Al (67 vol.%) with a tem-
perature of full dissolution tfd = 1273°C and by carbides due
to the presence of 0.15 wt.% C in the alloy. The chemical
composition of alloy ZhS32 and its critical points are pre-
sented in Table 1 [2].
Specimens with single-crystal structure and specified
crystallographic orientation [001] were cast by the method of
directed crystallization with seeding agents. Each specimen
was subjected to x-raying to control the crystallographic ori-
entation. The admissible deviation of the growth structure
from crystallographic direction [001] did not exceed 10°.
Tests for long-term and tensile strength were conducted for
specimens in the cast state (without heat treatment). The
specimens tested for long-term strength were flat and had a

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
In short-term tensile tests at 20°C D = 0.2 – 0.3. At
1250°C the parameter D1 was substantially higher than at
1150°C (Table 2).
Figure 1 presents typical strain curves for single crystals
of alloy ZhS32. It can be seen from Fig. 1 and Table 3 that
they have a normal temperature dependence of mechanical
properties, i.e., with growth in the test temperature the
strength characteristics decrease and the ductility characteris-
tics increase. Stages I and II characterized by a constant coef-
ficient of strain hardening are absent, and the entire curve in
the region of plastic strain corresponds to stage III of a sche -
matic “shear stress – shear strain” curve for single-crystal al-
loys [4]. At any test temperature the ultimate rupture strength
exceeded somewhat the yield strengths /c115
y and /c1150.2 , and the
specimens exhibited low uniform straining of 1, 2, and 3% at
a temperature of 1170, 1210, and 1250°C, respectively. The
ductility was determined primarily by the lumped strain due
to the formation of a neck. The form of the strain curves ob -
tained is similar to that of strain curves of single crystals of
the Ni
3Al intermetallic compound with the fcc lattice in a
temperature range of 820 – 1100°C [5]. Since the alloy pos -
sesses minimum uniform strain, we can hardly use the Hollo-
mon equation
S = A/c101n
(where S is the true stress, /c101is the true strain, n is the harden-
ing parameter, and A is a factor), and can determine only the
coefficient of strain hardening /c113.
At a deformation rate /c101=1 0 –3 sec –1 and temperatures
of 1170, 1210, and 1250°C the values of /c113amount to 30, 23,
and 8 MPa, respectively, which is much lower than the coef-
ficients of strain hardening of the Ni
3Al, Ni 3(Al, Cr), and
Ni3(Al, Ti) single crystals, which are equal to 400 – 700 MPa
in a temperature range of 400 – 700°C [5]. The difference is
explainable by the higher test temperature of alloy ZhS32.

The values of mechanical properties of single crystals of

the metal and to prevent the beginning of crystallization at
the same time.
CONCLUSIONS
1. In tensile tests of single crystals of alloy ZhS32 at a
temperature exceeding 1150°C the increase in the tempera -
ture is accompanied by decrease in the strength characteris -
tics and increase in the ductility ones.
2. With growth in the deformation rate at a specified tem-
perature the strength of the alloy increases. In the tempera-
ture range studied the ultimate rupture strength is insignifi-
cantly higher than the yield strength.
