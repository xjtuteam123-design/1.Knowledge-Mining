1. Introduction
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) were first proposed in 2004 by Yeh et al.
[1] and Cantor et al. [2] . By early definition, HEAs are composed of at 
least five elements, with each element ’ s atomic ratio typically ranging 
from 5 at% to 35 at%. Compared with traditional alloys, no matrix 
element exist in HEAs and thus provide a new and wide space for alloy 
design. Till now, many HEAs have been designed and successfully pre -
pared [3,4] , some of which have excellent properties, such as abrasive 
resistance [5] , fracture toughness [6] , corrosion resistance [7] , thermal 
stability [8] or catalytic performance [9] , etc.
Combined refractory elements with the concept of HEAs, in 2011, 
Senkov [10] proposed a new kind of alloys, namely RHEAs. RHEAs 
generally consist of elements with high melting point, such as Cr, Mo, 
Nb, Ta, Ti, V, W, Zr, etc. , and thus may possess outstanding mechanical 
properties at super-high temperature. The first proposed MoNbTaW and 
MoNbTaVW RHEAs have attracted particular attention and have been 
investigated widely due to the outstanding mechanical properties even 
up to 1600 ℃ [10] . This makes RHEAs promising candidates for military 
and aerospace applications. However, most reported RHEAs exhibit 
poor castability, and are easy to produce various casting defects, such as 
interdendritic shrinkage porosity, microstructure segregation and 
element segregation, etc [11] . Recently, Lin et al. successfully fabricated 
defect-free FeNiCrCoMo

manufacturing, and Liu et al. prepared defect-free Ni

exists in majority of HEAs, which obviously degrade alloys ’ mechanical 
performance at as-cast condition and increase difficulty or energy con -
sumption for subsequent process. In 2014, Lu [13] proposed the concept 
of eutectic high-entropy alloys (EHEAs), and effectively solve the 
problem. Regrettably, the eutectic strategy is only useful for dual-phase 
HEAs and is not applicable for RHEAs because most RHEAs consist of 
single-phase BCC rather than dual-phases.

When Δ T approaches zero, the alloy undergoes progressive layer-by- 
layer solidification, where the outermost layer preferentially forms a 
smooth equiaxed crystal layer. As a result, the downward flow of liquid 
metal encounters relatively low resistance. Subsequently, from the outer 
mold layer to the center, the alloy solidifies gradually into layers of 
equiaxed grains with increasingly larger sizes until complete solidifi -
cation. Conversely, when Δ T is large, the alloy in the outermost layer of 
the mold that preferentially solidifies undergoes mushy solidification, 
meaning that the alloy tends to segregate during solidification, which 
promotes the formation of dendrites. During casting, preferential so -
lidification of dendrites in the outermost mold layer leads to a rough 
inner wall. This roughness increases the resistance encountered by the 
subsequent downward flow of liquid metal, thereby affecting its fluidity. 
Based on this, the objective of this study is to design RHEAs with small 
Δ T or excellent castability in Ti-Zr-Al-Nb-V system, and systematically 
investigated their microstructure, mechanical properties, and structural 
stability.

4. Discussion
4.1. Fluidity and castability of HEAs
Due to the high number of constituent elements, the majority of re -
ported HEAs possess poor castability, i.e. poor mold-filling capacity. The 
castability of alloys is mainly affected by the fluidity of liquid metal as 
well as foundry technique. Here, we ignore the influence of complex and 
changeable foundry technique and focus on the fluidity of liquid metal. 
The fluidity of liquid metal depends on many factors, such as alloy 
composition, solidification mode, latent heat of crystallization, specific 
heat capacity, density and thermal conductivity, viscosity and surface 
tension, etc. Among all these factors, alloy composition and solidifica -
tion mode affect the fluidity most significantly.
The solidification modes is mainly affected by composition and 
temperature gradient near the solid-fluid interface, and can be divided 
into three types: gradually solidification (small Δ T or large temperature 
gradient), volume solidification (large Δ T or small temperature 
gradient) and mesosphere solidification (somewhere in between). From 
the perspective of chemical composition, it is known that pure metal and 
eutectic composition alloys generally have good fluidity, resulting from 
the extreme small crystallization temperature range ( Δ T ≈ 0). During 
the casting process at constant temperature, solidification occurs grad -
ually from the cold mold surface to the inside of the liquid metal. The flat 
and smooth liquid-solid interface slightly hinders the flow of liquid 
metal and ensure the good fluidity and castability. On the contrary, if the 
Δ T is large, solidification mode is volume solidification, solidification 
occurs in a range of temperatures, the outer dendritic crystal in contact 
with the mold surface appears first, then the dendrite arms will hinder 
the flow of the uncrystallized liquid metal inside and leads to a poor 
castability. Wider Δ T obviously results in wider solid-liquid coexistence 
zone, coarser dendritic crystal and the subsequent more serious 
composition segregation. Therefore, under similar solidification condi -
tion, composition segregation of HEAs mainly depend on the alloys ’ 
intrinsic factor Δ T . Considering the high melting point of RHEAs, it is 
difficult to determine Δ T values by experiment measures. For conve -
nience, microstructural characterization and composition segregation 
analysis can be adopted to estimate the Δ T and fluidity value. Specif -
ically, dendritic crystal at as-cast condition represents an obvious 
composition segregation and a large Δ T . Elements with high melting 
points tend to distribute in dendritic core, while elements with low 
melting points distribute in interdendritic region more. The composition 
segregation degree can be expressed as,
