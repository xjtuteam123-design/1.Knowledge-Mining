Chemically complex intermetallic alloys (CCIMAs) have emerged as promising materials for achieving ex- 
ceptional softening resistance at elevated temperatures, owing to their unique superlattice structures and 
elemental synergism. However, the lack of experimental endeavors and understanding regarding their 
temperature-dependent mechanical behaviors hinders their advancement for high-temperature applica- 
tions. Here, we conducted a systematic investigation on the mechanical properties and associated defor- 
mation mechanisms of an L12 -type Co-rich CCIMA using transmission electron microscopy. The Co-rich 
CCIMA exhibits superior strength across a wide temperature range of 70 0–90 0 °C, with an anomalous 
peak yield strength (YS) of ∼1.0 GPa at 700 °C, which surpasses most previously reported L12 -type inter- 
metallic alloys. This superior softening resistance can be primarily attributed to a combination of cross- 
slip hardening and segregation-assisted hardening. Speciﬁcally, massive Kear-Wilsdorf (K-W) locks formed 
by multiple cube cross-slips of screw dislocations prevail at 70 0–90 0 °C, providing strong barriers for dis- 
location movements and enhancing the yield strength (YS) accordingly. More interestingly, we revealed a 
segregation-assisted formation of superlattice stacking faults and nanotwins at the peak yield tempera- 
ture (700 °C). The interlocking of these substructures and the associated element dragging effect further 
impeded the propagation of dislocations, contributing to the observed yield anomaly. This work provides 
in-depth insight into the high-temperature performance and deformation behaviors of CCIMAs, which 
paves the way for the future development of novel heat-resistant structural alloys.

1. Introduction 
Intermetallic alloys with an L12 -ordered crystal structure ex- 
hibit low atomic mobility and unique dislocation dynamics, which 
enable them with an anomalous yield behavior at elevated temper- 
ature, namely increased rather than decreased yield strength (YS) 
with temperature increment [ 1–4 ]. Such unique properties, pri- 
marily attributed to cube cross slip and resultant immobile Kear- 
Wilsdorf (K- W) locks, make them promising candidates for high- 
temperature structural materials. However, most previous studies 
are mainly focused on chemically simple systems (binary or tri- 
nary), such as the Ni3 Al, Co3 (Al,W), Co3 Ti alloys, etc. These alloys 
usually have poor fabricability at ambient temperature, as well as 
insuﬃcient YS and phase stability at high temperatures, which has 
obstructed their practical engineering applications. For instance, 
the stoichiometric Ni3 Al has a high melting point, but its YS is

but a few of the polycrystalline L12 phases exhibit catastrophic en- 
vironmental embrittlement [ 7 , 8 ]. Though micro-alloying (such as 
Boron-doped Ni3 Al [ 5 ], Fe-alloyed Co3 Ti intermetallic compound 
[ 9 ], etc.) may optimize their plasticity to some extent, the perfor- 
mance enhancement of these conventional chemically simple L12 
intermetallic alloys remains quite limited due to their constrained 
compositional tailoring space.

chemically complex intermetallic alloys (CCIMAs), was proposed,

grain boundaries, leading to an order-to-disorder phase transfor-

tensile ductility [ 13 , 14 ]. 
Albeit the intriguing room-temperature mechanical properties, 
the deformation behaviors at elevated temperatures and the un- 
derlying mechanisms of CCIMAs have remained largely elusive. 
This knowledge is crucial for evaluating the potential of CCIMAs 
as high-temperature structural materials. To date, limited research 
works have been carried out, with only a few experimental in- 
vestigations focusing on microstructural modiﬁcations to allevi- 
ate intermediate-temperature embrittlement [ 12 ] and compressive 
creep properties of CCIMAs at elevated temperatures [ 15 ]. Besides, 
some theoretical studies have been undertaken concerning the pla- 
nar fault energies of the L12 -type (Ni,Co,Fe)3 (Al,Ti,Fe), suggesting a 
potential beneﬁt of the Co element in promoting planar fault for- 
mation [ 16 ]. However, so far, there is no experimental evidence 
supporting this ﬁnding, and its temperature-dependent impact on 
the deformation behavior remains unexplored. 
Therefore, the present work is designed to systematically in- 
vestigate the temperature-dependent tensile properties of the 
CCIMA over a wide temperature range. Its yield anomaly behav- 
ior and deformation mechanisms were carefully probed. We chose 
the previously-studied Co-rich CCIMA (Co47 Ni30 Al10 V5 Ta4 Ti4 )99 B1 
(at.%) as a model alloy. The results show that this Co-rich CCIMA 
exhibits ultrahigh YS across a broad temperature spectrum, outper- 
forming most previously reported alloys. More interestingly, in ad- 
dition to the conventional cube-cross-slip mechanism, we revealed 
a unique segregation-assisted yield anomaly at 700 °C, where the 
YS reaches a peak value of ∼1.0 GPa. This study delivers a fun- 
damental understanding of the high-temperature mechanical be- 
haviors of Co-rich CCIMAs and advances the design for higher- 
performance CCIMAs in the future. 
2. Experimental 
The Co-rich CCIMA with a nominal composition of 
(Co47 Ni30 Al10 V5 Ta4 Ti4 )99 B1 (at.%) was prepared by a mixture 
of high-purity materials ( > 99.9 %) in an arc-melting furnace 
under an argon atmosphere, as detailed in a prior study [ 13 ]. To 
ensure chemical homogeneity, all ingots were turned over and 
repeatedly melted at least eight times, and then cast into a water- 
cooled Cu mold to form plate samples. The as-cast specimens 
were homogenized at 1150 °C for 24 h under argon protection. 
Subsequently, a cycling thermo-mechanical process involving cold 
rolling with a thickness reduction of 20 % and then annealed at 
1050 °C for 15 min was implemented until a total cumulative 
reduction of 66 % was achieved. All heat treatments were ﬁnished 
by air cooling to ambient temperature. 
For mechanical testing, dog-bone-shaped specimens were fab- 
ricated using an electro-discharge machine. Specimens with 
gauge dimensions of 12.5 mm × 3.2 mm × 1 mm and 
15 mm × 3.5 mm × 1.2 mm were applied to ambient-temperature 
and high-temperature tensile tests, respectively. The surfaces of 
each test specimen were mechanically polished through a series of 
SiC paper up to 20 0 0-grit. The tensile tests were conducted over 
a wide temperature range of 25–10 0 0 °C at a constant strain rate 
of 1.0 × 10−3 s−1 using a Material Testing System (MTS) machine 
equipped with a high-temperature electric furnace. To eliminate 
the effect of temperature overshooting when the setting temper- 
ature was reached, extra few minutes were taken to stabilize the 
temperature and the temperature deviation was controlled within 
3 °C before the tensile test. At least three specimens were tested 
for each condition to ensure data repeatability. 
The initial microstructure was characterized using scanning 
electron microscopy (SEM, ZEISS Crossbeam 350) under backscat- 
tered electron imaging mode. More detailed initial microstruc- 
tures and the deformation substructures of the specimens af- 
ter tensile tests were characterized by ﬁeld-emission transmis- 
sion electron microscopy (TEM, Thermo Scientiﬁc Talos F200X) op- 
erated at an accelerated voltage of 200 kV. Elemental distribu- 
tions at various crystalline defects were investigated in scanning 
transmission electron microscopy (STEM) mode with a highly ef- 
ﬁcient Super-X energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS). TEM 
foils were mechanically ground to a thickness of ∼60 μm, and 
then electropolished using a twin-jet electro-polisher in a solution 
of perchloric acid (10 vol.%) and alcohol (90 vol.%) at 25 V and 
−15 °C. 
3. Results 
3.1. Microstructure of the as-prepared sample 
Fig. 1 shows the as-prepared microstructure of the present Co- 
rich CCIMA. The SEM image ( Fig. 1(a) ) revealed a fully recrys- 
tallized equiaxed grain structure with an average grain size of 
∼5.0 ± 1.2 μm. Apart from a few sporadically-dispersed borides, no 
other precipitates can be detected. High-resolution TEM (HRTEM) 
characterization was carefully performed to provide a more de- 
tailed insight into the microstructure. The CCIMA comprises a sin- 
gle L12 phase exhibiting an ordered superlattice grain interior and 
a disordered interfacial nanolayer (DINL) at the grain boundary, as 
conﬁrmed by the fast Fourier transform (FFT) images highlighted 
in the red and cyan squares in Fig. 1(b) , respectively. The elemen- 
tal distribution in the vicinity of an almost-edge-on grain boundary 
is shown in Fig. 1(c) . A noticeable segregation of Co, accompanied 
by the depletion of Ni, Al, and Ta is clearly observed. Ti and V do 
not exhibit a clear segregation preference. While EDS cannot de- 
tect boron directly, previous atom probe tomography (APT) results 
suggested that B would also be co-enriched at the grain boundary 
[ 13 ]. 
3.2. Tensile properties and yield anomaly 
Representative tensile engineering stress-strain curves of the 
present CCIMA at various temperatures are displayed in Fig. 2(a) . 
This CCIMA demonstrates outstanding room-temperature tensile 
properties with both high strength (YS of 0.79 ±0.01 GPa, ultimate 
tensile strength of 1.60 ±0.03 GPa) and a decent uniform elongation 
of 29.5 % ±0.7 %. More importantly, the CCIMA exhibits an extraor- 
dinary high-temperature YS over a wide temperature range, though 
it exhibits intermediate-temperature embrittlement. This could be 
possibly due to oxygen attack on grain boundaries [ 17 ], which re- 
mains an interesting topic for future study. The YS was plotted as a 
function of temperature, alongside the high-temperature mechan- 
ical properties of other L12 -type Ni- or Co-based simple or mul- 
ticomponent alloys [ 10 , 15 , 18–24 ] (see Fig. 2(b) ). Clearly, the YS of 
the present CCIMA initially increases with the increment of tem- 
perature from ambient temperature to 700 °C, demonstrating a 
strong yield anomaly response. Notably, it reaches a peak strength

4. Discussion 
Extensive studies agree that the yield strength anomaly of L12 - 
type intermetallic alloys is closely related to the thermally acti- 
vated cross slip of screw segments due to the anisotropy of APB 
energy on the {111} to {001} planes [ 38 , 39 ]. This process forms 
sessile dislocation portions and provides local pinning points to 
retard the motion of dislocations [ 30–32 , 40 ], known as cross-slip 
hardening. Indeed, detailed TEM analysis reveals that massive cube 
cross-slip events occur and K-W locks form after tension at 700 °C 
( Fig. 3 ), implying a strong cross-slip hardening takes place in the 
present CCIMA. The yield increment caused by cross-slip harden- 
ing is found to be linearly proportional to the atomic fraction of 
the alloying element in the Ni3 Al-type intermetallic alloy and can 
be modelled using Deruyttere and Gypen’s method [ 41–43 ]:

Interestingly, our TEM observation indicated that planar defects, 
i.e., deformation twins and SSFs, also occur during deformation 
at 700 °C. Their mutual interactions and resistance to dislocation 
motion can provide additional strengthening, potentially explain- 
ing the exceptionally high peak strength observed in this Co-rich 
CCIMA. Similar observations of SSF shearing and interaction for the 
yield anomaly at intermediate temperatures have been reported in 
a Co-Ti-Cr superalloy [ 45 ]. This planar-fault-mediated mechanism 
has also been found to strengthen other alloys, including Ni-based 
and Co-based superalloys, as well as compositionally complex or 
high entropy superalloys [ 46–48 ]. However, studies have shown 
that elements like Ta and Ni appear to increase the SSF energy of 
the L12 alloy, reducing the formation propensity of planar defects 
like twin and SSF [ 35 ]. This now raises the question of why these 
planar defects formed in our present CCIMA at 700 °C, despite the 
presence of a signiﬁcant amount of Ta and Ni. 
4.1. Planar defects formed at 700 °C deformation 
Previous studies on the high-temperature mechanical properties 
of Ni-based superalloys revealed that the formation of planar de- 
fects is generally related to the reduction in planar fault energies, 
which is often caused by a signiﬁcant local enrichment effect on 
the defects [ 49–51 ]. To investigate this phenomenon in our CCIMA, 
EDS compositional mapping across various planar or linear defects 
was carried out, as displayed in Fig. 7 . In the high-angle annular 
dark-ﬁeld (HAADF) images, most defects appear brighter than the 
surrounding matrix. The individual element maps show that Co has 
a tendency to strongly segregate at almost all defects after ten- 
sile tests at 700 °C, including the intersections of TBs ( Fig. 7(a) ), 
SSFs ( Fig. 7(b) ) and dislocations ( Fig. 7(c) ). However, Ni and Al ex- 
hibit noticeable depletion on these defects, while no clear segre- 
gation preference was detected for the other elements. This obser- 
vation aligns with previous research on creep-induced dislocations 
or stacking faults in Ni-, Co- or CoNi-based superalloys, where the 
enrichment of Co and depletion of Ni and Al have been frequently 
found at dislocations [ 52 ], APBs [ 53 ] and SSFs [ 53–59 ]. Thermo- 
dynamically, such selective elemental segregation is favored as Co 
would be expected to locate on Ni-sites along SSFs, leading to a re- 
duction in the fault energy or may even promote local phase trans- 
formation to a D024 or D019 structure [ 60 ]. Furthermore, given the 
high concentration of Co solute ahead of the partial dislocations or 
the TB intersections (red circles in Fig. 7 ) and the relatively low 
Co concentration at the fault plane, we proposed that the diffu- 
sion mechanisms of Co element to the fault occur through pipe 
diffusion along the fault plane. To support this hypothesis, we es- 
timated the diffusion distances of Co using different mechanisms. 
Based on the experimental data for Co diffusion in the Ni3 Al sys- 
tem [ 61 ], a value of D = 1.497 ×10−20 m2 s−1 is assumed for the 
bulk diffusion of Co in the present CCIMA at 700 °C. According 
to the relationship: x =

along dislocations is much faster than Co diffusion through the lat- 
tice, leading to an enrichment of Co at the defects. In contrast, the 
chemical distributions are rather uniform at 900 °C when pipe and 
lattice diffusion become relatively comparable. 
Additionally, a strong Co segregation was also observed at the 
stepped incoherent TBs (ITBs) ( Fig. 8(a) ). This Co enrichment ap-

these ITBs, as evidenced by the red circle in the BF morphology 
image and the SAED pattern ( Fig. 8(b) ). The HRTEM and FFT im-

crystal structures corresponding to the 9R and the L12 phases. This 
local phase transformation along ITBs further supports the idea of 
a reduced SFE caused by Co segregation. In summary, both thermo-

at 700 °C, which plays a crucial role in the unique planar-fault- 
mediated deformation mechanism and the corresponding yield 
anomaly.

Moreover, Co segregation can facilitate the propagation of the 
leading partial dislocations (Suzuki effect), promoting the forma- 
tion of planar defects (i.e., twins and SSFs) [ 56 ], as shown in Fig. 5 . 
Their mutual interactions and the interaction with the incoming 
dislocations may provide an additional strengthening effect [ 69 , 70 ]. 
Furthermore, Co segregation at TBs can also effectively lower the 
mobility of TBs and provide a strong pinning effect, leading to 
an exceptional TB strengthening that has been widely observed 
in many other alloys [ 71 , 72 ]. Besides, combined experiments and 
molecular dynamics simulations have shown that the 9R phase 
can resist the transmission of dislocations in alloys, which ren- 
ders them extraordinarily high hardness and compressive strength 
[ 73 , 74 ]. Nevertheless, due to its limited volume fraction, the con- 
tribution of the 9R phase on the yield anomaly behavior of the 
present CCIMA can be ignored. 
Fig. 9 schematically summarizes the temperature-dependent 
deformation mechanisms of the present Co-rich CCIMA. Superlat- 
tice dislocations predominantly glide on the {111} < 110 > slip sys- 
tems at all testing temperatures. With the increment of temper- 
ature, at 700 °C, there is an obvious yield anomaly, which could 
be mainly attributed to two factors. On one hand, frequent cube 
cross-slip events are activated during tensile deformation at 700 °C 
( Fig. 3 ). These events lead to the formation of sessile K-W lock 
conﬁgurations, which help the CCIMA resist softening at elevated 
temperatures –a problem commonly encountered in many other 
alloys [ 30 , 32 ]. More interestingly, on the other hand, in addition 
to the cross-slip hardening, segregation-assisted strengthening is 
also of great importance in the positive temperature dependence 
of the YS. More speciﬁcally, the segregation of Co to the disloca- 
tions and SSFs results in the Cottrell atmosphere or Suzuki effect 
( Fig. 7 ), imposing dragging effects on dislocation movement. Fur- 
thermore, such a Co enrichment to the SSF would further lower 
the SSF energy and facilitate the formation of deformation twins 
and even the 9R phase ( Fig. 8 ). These multiple structures together 
with the interactions between them form immobile lock conﬁgura- 
tions, which can act as strong obstacles for the gliding dislocations 
and contribute to further strengthening. No signiﬁcant changes 
in the volume fraction or distribution of the borides were ob- 
served after tensile tests at 700 °C. Given their low volume fraction 
( ∼2.6 %), we can reasonably rule out their inﬂuence on the anoma- 
lous yielding. Collectively, the YS of the present Co-rich CCIMA at 
700 °C can be assumed to be dominated by cross-slip hardening 
and segregation-assisted hardening. These two mechanisms result 
in the observed anomalous yield behavior and the exceptionally 
high YS ( ∼1.03 GPa). As the temperature further increases, the YS 
sharply decreases mainly due to the weakened atomic bonding ac- 
companied by a reduction in modulus and potential dislocation 
climb and recovery. Nevertheless, the presence of abundant K-W 
locks suggests that frequent cube cross slip remains the dominant 
deformation mechanism at 900 °C ( Fig. 6 ). This cross-slip activity 
helps to impede the movement of the dislocations and allows this 
alloy to maintain a relatively high YS ( ∼0.68 GPa) as compared to 
other L12 -type intermetallic alloys ( Fig. 2 ). 
5. Conclusions 
This study delved into the elevated-temperature tensile proper- 
ties and the corresponding deformation mechanisms of a L12 Co- 
rich CCIMA through a detailed TEM analysis. New insights were ob- 
tained and the main conclusions are summarized as follows: 
(1) The present L12 -type Co-rich CCIMA exhibits remarkable yield 
anomaly behavior during tensile deformation, with a peak yield 
strength of 1.03 GPa at 700 °C. Though the YS decreases 
as temperature further increases, an outstanding load-bearing 
ability with a high YS of 0.68 GPa at 900 °C can still be 
achieved, demonstrating a superior high-temperature softening 
resistance. 
(2) At the peak strength temperature (700 °C), pronounced cube 
cross-slip of screw dislocations and the resulting K-W locks 
could provide effective pinning points for the cross-slip harden- 
ing effect. Concurrently, the Co-segregation-assisted formation 
of nanotwins and SSFs, along with their mutual interactions 
with dislocations, additionally contributes to the yield anomaly 
and the associated exceptional YS observed in the CCIMA at 
700 °C. 
(3) At 900 °C, dislocation slip predominantly occurs on the 
< 110 > {111} slip systems and cubic cross-slip events remain 
prevalent, which is responsible for the retained high-stress level 
of the L12 Co-rich CCIMA at such an elevated temperature. 
In combination, these new ﬁndings underscore the extraordi- 
nary softening resistance of Co-rich CCIMAs at elevated tempera- 
tures. The insight gained into segregation-assisted yield anomaly 
may provide a fundamental basis for the development of novel 
high-temperature structural alloys.
