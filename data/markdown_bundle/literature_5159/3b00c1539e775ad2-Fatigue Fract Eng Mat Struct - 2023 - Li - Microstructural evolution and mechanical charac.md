Abstract
Recently, there has been a lot of interest in the AlCoCrFeNi 2.1 eutectic high-
entropy alloys (EHEAs), which can achieve a good balance of strength and
ductility. The relationship between the microstructure and mechanical proper-
ties of the alloy was established in this study by examining the microstructural
evolution during heat treatment of 500 –1000
/C14 C. The results show that the
alloy's microstructure remained unchanged when the heat treatment tempera-
ture was lower than 700 /C14 C. With further rise above 800 /C14 C, the B2 II precipitates
were inspected in the L1 2 matrix. Besides, L1 2II started to be precipitated from
the B2 matrix at 900 /C14 C. The tensile test results conducted at 20 –1000/C14 C
showed that both the yield strength and ultimate tensile strength decreased as
the temperature rose. As for the elongation, however, it increased dramatically
when the testing temperature was over 800
/C14 C, being attributed to the dynamic
recrystallization. The results obtained provided a fundamental understanding
of high-temperature properties for duplex HEAs.
KEYWORDS
eutectic high-entropy alloys, mechanical properties, microstructure, precipitate phase
Highlights
1. The B2 II precipitates were precipitated from the L1 2 at 800/C14 C.
2. The B2 II and L12II were precipitated from the L1 2 and B2 over 900 /C14 C.
3. The yield and tensile strength decreased sharply at 20 –700/C14 C and slowly at
800–1000/C14 C.
4. The elongation first remained stable at 20 –700/C14 C and then increased
sharply at 800 –1000/C14 C.

1 | INTRODUCTION
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) are a promising class of
materials for structural applications. 1–6 The design and
development of single-phase HEAs received the majority
of attention in the early stages of research. The tensile
yield strength of face-centered cubic (FCC) HEAs is,
however, frequently inadequate. In contrast, body-
centered cubic (BCC) HEAs, which frequently contain
considerable quantities of refractory BCC metals as

sustain extremely high strength across a broad range of
temperatures, at the cost of a sizable reduction in ductil-
ity.
7,8 The AlCoCrFeNi 2.1 eutectic HEAs (EHEAs) con-
tain conventional FCC and BCC layered structures. Due
to the back stress created when the two phases meet
during tensile deformation, it offers an excellent mix of
high strength and ductility.
9,10 Weak castability, liquid-
ity, and composition inhomogeneity typically limit
large-scale manufacturing of HEAs through casting.
However, during the casting process, kilogram-scale
eutectic AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 EHEA displays good
castability. 11,12
The AlCoCrFeNi 2.1 EHEAs has been studied for its
mechanical properties at different temperatures because
of its good castability, high strength, and high ductility,
which possess numerous possible applications. Under
tensile testing at room temperature, Gao et al.
13 investi-
gated the microstructure evolution of the as-cast AlCoCr-
FeNi2.1 EHEA and discovered that a high density of
parallel dislocations in soft FCC(L1 2) phases are pro-
duced in the form of (111) < 110 > planar slip systems.
Through nanoindentation experiments and micro-pillar
compression testing, the micromechanical behaviors of
as-cast AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 EHEA were also studied, 14 con-
firming that the B2 phase is more sensitive to strain rate
than FCC(L1 2) phase. Ashe et al. 15 investigated the tex-
ture evolution to determine how much the deformation
texture contributed to the softening/hardening of the
casted structure between 20 and 500
/C14 C. Zhang et al. 16
investigated the twin-plane boundary migration, interac-
tion of the deformation twin, and overall impact of the
twin on the alloy characteristics between 600 and 800 /C14 C.
At 900 –1050/C14 C, Zhang et al. 17 examined the effect of
strain rate sensitivity value m, plasticity, and two distinct
precipitates on recrystallization in the FCC matrix. Addi-
tionally, cold rolling, thermal mechanical treatment, or
cutting-edge synthetic methods have been used to
enhance the mechanical properties of AlCoCrFeNi
2.1
EHEAs.18–24
It has been demonstrated that through reasonable
preparation technology and warm and/or cold rolling
together with heat treatments, excellent comprehensive
mechanical properties can be obtained in AlCoCrFeNi 2.1
EHEAs.22,25,26 For example, after 90% cold rolling reduc-
tion and 1 h of annealing at a high temperature of 800 /C14 C,
the EHEA presents superior strength –ductility synergy.
Following thermomechanical processing, the tensile
strength of EHEA is about 600 MPa higher than that of
the as-cast state. While the harder B2 phase may con-
tinue to have an ordered lattice structure, the softer L1
2
phase will gradually become disordering. Additionally,
by reducing the rolling temperature to the liquid nitrogen
temperature, the yield strength and tensile strength of
AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 are further improved while the ductility is
kept at 14%. 21 Furthermore, it has been found that the
heterogeneous structure produced by warm rolling at
750
/C14 C endows EHEA with good strength and ductility
compared with those produced using cold rolling tech-
niques.22,23 In addition, the tensile mechanical behaviors
and directional solidification processes of the AlCoCr-
FeNi2.1 EHEAs have been studied to elucidate the defor-
mation mechanisms. 25–27 Along with strength and
ductility, the withdrawal rate also affects the deformation
behavior of lamellar microstructure. 25 Better ductility of
EHEAs is produced via recrystallization and deformation
twins, which show coordinate deformation effects.
16
Although many interesting results have been found,
certain questions still need to be answered. For instance,
the development of the alloy's microstructure at high
temperatures currently lacks essential knowledge. Fur-
ther research is also required on the relationship
between mechanical properties and microstructural
development since it is crucial to understand high-
temperature application conditions. Since the EHEA is
intended for high-temperature applications, it is neces-
sary to evaluate its microstructure and mechanical per-
formance at elevated temperatures, which will be
addressed in the current work. The heat treatment
experiments of EHEA were carried out at 600 –1000
/C14 C
and kept for 60 min to study the high-temperature
microstructural evolution. The EHEA was subjected to
tensile tests at 20 –1000/C14 C to explore the influence of
microstructural evolution on mechanical properties. The
scanning electron microscope (SEM) and electron back-
scattered diffraction (EBSD) analyses were used to con-
duct the phase identification and composition analysis.
The purpose of the current study was to establish a con-
nection between AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 EHEA's high-
temperature mechanical properties and microstructural
development. The gathered information will aid in a
deep comprehension of the hi gh-temperature mechani-
cal properties and fracture processes of EHEA, expand-
ing its potential applications.

2 | MATERIALS AND METHOD
In order to create alloy ingots with the nominal composi-
tion given in Table 1, the five elements Al, Co, Cr, Fe,
and Ni with a purity of more than 99.9% (wt.%) were first
produced by arc-melting and then remelted in a vacuum
casting furnace with directed solidification. A steady
growth rate of 6 mm/min was used to solidify the alloy
rods as they were cast into Φ50 /C2 500 mm.
The obtained alloy was heated to a certain tempera-
ture at 10
/C14 C/min, kept for 60 min, and then cooled in air
to evaluate the temperature-dependent microstructural
development. The primary determinant of the micro-
structure during heat treatment is the temperature. To
find out what reactions might take place during the heat-
ing process, differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) anal-
ysis using STA 449 F3 (Netzsch) was performed in the
temperature range of 0 –1200
/C14 C. The heat treatment for
1 h was at 500, 600, 700, 800, 900, and 1000 /C14 C, respec-
tively. The heat-treated samples were prepared for micro-
structural analysis using a standard polishing procedure.
SEM with an energy dispersive spectrometer (EDS) was
used to characterize the microstructure and analyze the
composition. Using EBSD, the phases in the samples
were also characterized morphologically and their crystal
structures were examined.
A testing device with a 25-ton capacity performed the
tensile tests (INSTRON 8850). The cylindrical ingots mea-
suring 50 by 500 mm were used to create the specimens
for microstructure and tensile testing (Figure
1A). The
original alloy and a schematic drawing displaying the
locations of the tested specimens are shown in
Figure
1A,B. The typical cylindrical tensile specimens
were used, which had a gauge length ( L) of 30 mm and a
diameter (d) of 5 mm. A closed loop three-zone resistance
furnace was used for heating the specimen. The specimen
temperature was monitored using three thermocouples
attached along the specimen gauge length indicating a
temperature gradient within ±3
/C14 C. The strain evolution
during the stress-varying loading was continuously mea-
sured by two symmetrically arranged rod-tube-type
extensometers. An in-house program recorded the strain,
load, and temperature signals. The rod-tube-type exten-
someters have an accuracy of 0.0001 and a data acquisi-
tion frequency of two times per second. To ensure a
uniform temperature distribution along the specimens,
the specimens were heated during testing to a specific
temperature (500, 600, 700, 800, 900, and 1000
/C14 C) at a
rate of 10 /C14 C/min, held for 30 min, and then stretched.
The tensile test was carried out at 0.02 mm/min until the
strain reached 2%. Following that, the tensile test was
performed at a rate of 2.5 mm/min until specimens were
fractured. Each condition was tested on three samples.
The slices with a thickness of 2 mm were cut from the
gauge length along the axial direction to inspect the
microstructure after tensile tests, as schematically shown
in Figure
1C.

With the increase in temperature, the evolution of
microstructure is mainly reflected in two precipitated
phases (B2
II and L1 2II). Exploring the mechanism of pre-
cipitate formation is crucial for understanding the micro-
structure evolution of EHEA. Li et al. 28 reported two
possible mechanisms leading to the formation of associ-
ated AlNi-rich L1 2 precipitates (B2 II) with increasing
temperature. First, spinodal decomposition in the HEA's
L1
2 matrix causes phase separation into nearby AlNi-rich
areas, which is followed by ordering within the AlNi-rich
regions to promote the precipitation of B2 II. Second, ini-
tial AlNi-clustering within the fcc matrix of the HEA, fol-
lowed by these AlNi-rich clusters acting as heterogeneous
nucleation sites for the AlNi-rich L1
2 precipitations
(B2II). Similarly, L1 2II is dominated by the same mecha-
nism, forming associated CoCrFe-rich B2 precipitates. To
further explore the precipitation sequence of the two pre-
cipitates, it was necessary to calculate their mixing
enthalpy. The lower the mixing enthalpy of the precipi-
tated phase, the easier it is to precipitate. The Miedema
theory and a geometric extended model were coupled by
Ouyang et al.
29,30 to predict the thermodynamic proper-
ties of multicomponent alloys. The enthalpies of mixing
for the B2
II and L1 2II were calculated to be /C0 10.12 and
/C0 7.45 KJ/mol, respectively, by adding the suggested
model. Due to its lower enthalpy of mixing, the B2 II was
given preference to precipitate upon thermal exposure in
our work.

3.2 | Mechanical properties of the
AlCoCrFeNi2.1 EHEAs tested at different
temperatures
Figure 8 shows the typical stress –strain curves of AlCoCr-
FeNi2.1 EHEAs tested at different temperatures. The yield
strength (YS) and ultimate tensile strength (UTS)
decrease monotonically with increasing temperature.
When the testing temperature exceeds 800
/C14 C, the elonga-
tion exhibits a distinct rise. The sample promptly frac-
tures after reaching the UTS below 800 /C14 C. Afterward,
over 800 /C14 C, the stress quickly approaches its maximum
and remains constant until the overall strain exceeds
10%.
The tensile properties (YS, UTS, and elongation) of
AlCoCrFeNi2.1 EHEAs tested at various temperatures are
presented in Figure 9. It is noticed that the maximum YS
and UTS are obtained at 20 /C14 C. When the temperature
exceeds 800 /C14 C, they show an evident downward trend.
However, the elongation displays a different trend. Low
temperatures (below 800
/C14 C) allow it to maintain stable.
However, the elongation dramatically rises at 800 –
1000/C14 C, indicating a rapid softening state.
To further comprehend the link between mechanical
property and microstructure as well as the fracture pro-
cesses of AlCoCrFeNi 2.1 EHEA, SEM characterizations
are conducted on the fracture surfaces (Figure 10). As
can be seen from Figure 10A–C, the fracture appearance
of the alloy after aged below 700 /C14 C is analogous to that at
20/C14 C. Some shallow dimples and river patterns can be
inspected on the fracture surface, suggesting a mixed
fracture mode. It is worth noting that the B2 phase has
high strength but low ductility, while the L1 2 phase has
great ductility but low strength. Consequently, a brittle-
like fracture is in the B2 phase while a ductile fracture is
in the L1 2 phase. Figure 10D reveals that when the exper-
imental temperature rose to 800 /C14 C, the number of dim-
ples on the fracture surface increased compared with that
below 700 /C14 C. The dimples enlarge when the testing tem-
perature is raised to 900 /C14 C and 1000 /C14 C (Figure 10E–F),
which verifies the improvement of plasticity.

4 | CONCLUSION
The AlCoCrFeNi 2.1 EHEA's temperature-dependent
microstructural development was studied, and a correla-
tion between the microstructural evolution and mechani-
cal properties was built. One can infer the following
conclusions.
1. The original alloy basically consisted of the L1
2 phase
(70%) and the B2 phase (30%). The microstructure of
the alloy heat-treated at 500 –700
/C14 C was similar to that
of the original alloy.
2. When the alloy was heat-treated at 800 /C14 C, a large
amount of B2 II precipitates were found in the L1 2
matrix. The B2 II and L1 2II were precipitated from the
L12 and B2, respectively, when heat-treated over 900/C14 C.
3. The yield and tensile strength decreased sharply at
20–700/C14 C and slowly at 800 –1000/C14 C. The moderate
decrease in strength was caused by the precipitate for-
mation. The elongation first remained stable at 20 –
700/C14 C and then increased sharply at 800 –1000/C14 C. The
dynamic recrystallization in the L1 2 matrix should be
the main reason for the sharp increase in elongation.
