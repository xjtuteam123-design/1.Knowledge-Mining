Correlation between mechanical properties and valence electron 
concentration for NbTiZrM (M = Hf, Ta, W) refractory high entropy 
alloys: an ab initio study
Min Yang1 · Lin Shao1 · Jia‑Ming Duan1 · Xiao‑Tao Chen1 · Bi‑Yu Tang1
Received: 13 October 2020 / Accepted: 19 March 2021 / Published online: 13 April 2021 
© The Author(s), under exclusive licence to Springer-Verlag GmbH, DE part of Springer Nature 2021
Abstract
On basis of systematical ab initio study of mechanical properties of body-centered cubic NbTiZrM (M = Hf, Ta, W) refractory 
high entropy alloys, the correlation between mechanical properties and valence electron concentration (VEC) is appreci-
ably revealed. The present investigation demonstrates that both the single-crystal elastic parameters and the elastic moduli 
of NbTiZrM alloys increase for M from Hf to W, similar to the variation trend of VEC. Hence, the strength and hardness 
of NbTiZrM alloys exhibit apparent correlation with the VEC. Because the Poisson’s ratio and the Pugh’s ratio suggest the 
alloys are more ductile for M from W to Hf, the lower VEC obviously correlates with the better ductility of NbTiZrM alloys. 
Moreover, with increasing VEC, the elastic anisotropies of NbTiZrM alloys are predicted to decrease, whereas the Debye 
temperatures of these alloys show increasing tendency. Noticeably, the VEC is an effective adjusting parameter to obtain 
excellent mechanical properties.
Keywords Refractory high entropy alloy · Valence electron concentration · Elastic properties · Debye temperature · Ab 
initio calculations
1 Introduction
Over the past few years, high entropy alloys (HEAs) have 
attracted significant attention because of their remarkable 
properties such as high strength, high hardness, excellent 
wear resistance and superior corrosion resistance [1 –3]. 
Typically, HEAs form simple solid solutions with body-
centered cubic (BCC), face-centered cubic (FCC), hexagonal 
closed-packed (HCP) crystal structure or a mixture of them 
[2]. Among them numerous high entropy alloys composed 
of refractory elements have been studied extensively, and 
these refractory high entropy alloys (RHEAs) possess gen-
erally good phase stability, exceptional mechanical strength 
and excellent high-temperature softening resistance [4 –8]. 
For example, the first reported BCC MoNbTaW and MoN-
bTaVW RHEAs exhibit superior strength up to 1600 °C, 
even higher than that of conventional superalloys [4 ], the 
new BCC  Ti1.89CrNbV0.56 and Ti2ZrHf0.5VNb RHEAs show 
high ductility together with impressive high-temperature 
strength [7 , 8]. Hence, RHEAs are expected to meet the 
rigorous requirements of high-temperature application in 
aerospace and other related industries.
Interestingly, more than half of RHEAs contain equi-
atomic concentrations of Nb, Ti and Zr, and the equia-
tomic ternary NbTiZr alloy is found to form single-phase 
BCC structure with low density and high hardness [9 ]. 
Based on equiatomic NbTiZr, many single-phase NbTiZr-
based quaternary BCC RHEAs have been researched, 
such as NbTiZrV [10– 12], NbTiZrMo [10, 13], NbTiZ-
rHf [14– 16], NbTiZrTa [17] and NbTiZrW [18] alloy. 
Thereinto, the NbTiZrV, NbTiZrMo and NbTiZrHf alloys 
have been subjected to both experimental and theoreti-
cal studies on mechanical properties. Although Nguyen 
et al. reported experimentally that NbTiZrTa alloy exhibits 
excellent combination of compressive yield strength and 
ductility [17], a comprehensive theoretical understanding 
of mechanical properties for the alloy is still lacking. It 
should be emphasized that the thorough understanding 
of mechanical properties is essential to optimal design of 
properties and the potential applications of materials. To

the refractory elements that make up the RHEAs above,

elements in the same period on the properties of alloys, we 
study systematically the properties of NbTiZrM (M = Hf, 
Ta, W) RHEAs in this work.
It should be stressed that the structures and proper -
ties of HEAs are closely related to valence electron con-
centration (VEC). Firstly, VEC can be used as an effec-
tive parameter to tune the crystal structure of the solid 
solutions. It is found that the FCC phase exists when the 
VEC ≥ 8, and both the FCC and BCC solid-solution phases 
are formed when 6.87 ≤ VEC < 8, whereas a single BCC 
solid-solution phase is presented when VEC < 6.87 [21]. 
In addition to predicting the phase structures of materi-
als, the VEC is also obviously related to the mechanical 
properties of RHEAs. Tian et al. show that VEC ~ 4.72 is 
an effective criterion for the elastic isotropy analysis of 
 TiZrNbMoVx (x = 0—1.5) RHEAs [22]. Sheikh et al. find 
VEC is a reliable parameter to design ductile RHEAs [23]. 
Nevertheless, the relationship between VEC and various 
mechanical properties of HEAs is not adequately uncov -
ered, so systematical research on the correlation of VEC 
with various mechanical properties of HEAs is very nec-
essary and valuable, which is conducive to guiding the 
design of alloys using the VEC as a preliminary indicator 
of mechanical properties.
In this present work, we employ ab initio exact muffin-tin 
orbitals method in combination with the coherent potential 
approximation (EMTO-CPA) [24] to investigate systemati-
cally the mechanical properties in elastic range for equia-
tomic NbTiZrM (M = Hf, Ta, W) RHEAs. Then the correla-
tion between mechanical properties and VEC of NbTiZrM 
(M = Hf, Ta, W) alloys is explored. The revealed correlation 
can be expected to provide valuable guidelines for designing 
of novel alloy with excellent mechanical properties.
2 Computational method
The first-principles calculations were performed using the 
exact muffin-tin orbitals (EMTO) method based on density 
functional theory and the coherent potential approximation 
(CPA) for describing disordered solid solutions [24]. The 
used software for EMTO-CPA calculations is EMTO5.8 
software [25]. The EMTO technique is an improved 
screened Korringa–Kohn–Rostoker approach, employ -
ing large overlapping potential spheres for an accurate 
description of the exact single-electron potential, and the 
full charge density method further improves the accuracy 
of full potential total energy [26]. The CPA implemented 
in the EMTO method is a very powerful mean-field tech-
nique, which treats the disordered systems by an effective 
medium within the single-site approximation to solve the 
impurity problem for each alloy constituent [27]. By CPA 
simulation of random solid solutions, the lattice site by 
random occupancy of different species in real alloy can 
be represented by a single site with the effective potential 
instead of a group of atoms. The EMTO-CPA approach 
has been successfully applied in the first-principles study 
of the physical properties of HEAs [28, 29].
In the present study, the EMTO basis set included 
s, p, d and f orbitals ( lmax = 4 ). The number of orbitals 
for the full-charge density was chosen as lh
max = 8 . The 
Kohn–Sham equation was solved using the EMTO to 
improve the calculation efficiency and ensure the cal-
culation accuracy of the total energy of the system. The 
exchange–correlation functionals local density approxima-
tion (LDA), generalized gradient approximation (GGA) 
of Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) and PBE for solids 
(PBEsol) [30] were chosen to calculate total energy of 
NbTiZrM (M = Hf, Ta, W) alloys. The tested results show 
that GGA-PBE exchange–correlation functional is more 
suitable for structural optimization and calculations of 
elastic properties for NbTiZrM (M = Hf, Ta, W) alloys, 
so the GGA-PBE exchange–correlation functional is 
used in the present calculation, in which the one-electron 
equations were solved within the scalar-relativistic and 
soft-core approximations. In order to ensure the accuracy 
of total energy calculation, the irreducible wedge of the 
BCC Brillouin zone was sampled by 21 × 21 × 21 Monk-
horst–Pack k-points mesh. The electrostatic correction 
to the single-site CPA was described using the screened 
impurity model [24] with a screening parameter of 0.6. 
The calculations were performed for static lattice neglect -
ing all thermal contributions.
The equilibrium volume V and bulk modulus B were 
extracted by fitting energy-volume points with Birch-
Murnaghan equation of state. For the cubic systems, 
there are only three independent elastic constants C 11,

3 Results and discussion

data are limited, and only experiment lattice constants

3.2 Elastic properties
Mechanical properties in small elastic strain range are 
described by elastic properties. The derived single-crystal 
elastic constants of BCC NbTiZrM RHEAs are tabulated in 
Table 3. Although the calculated elastic constants for NbTiZ-
rHf agree reasonably with previous theoretical investigation 
[16], no other theoretical and experimental data of the elastic 
constants for NbTiZrM (M = Ta and W) are reported so far; 
thereby, our theoretical results provide the valuable reference 
for the further research. Note that these elastic constants of 
NbTiZrM RHEAs satisfy the Born dynamical stability cri-
teria for cubic systems [37]: C11 + 2C12 > 0, C11 >|C12| and 
C44 > 0, suggesting that NbTiZrM RHEAs discussed here 
are mechanically stable.
As seen from the results in Table 3, for the studied alloys 
the C11 values are larger than C 12 and C 44, which means 
larger incompressibility under uniaxial strain along princi-
pal axes of NbTiZrM alloys. Furthermore, the C11 values of 
NbTiZrM alloys increase with atomic number of M element; 
thus, the order of incompressibility is NbTiZrHf < NbTiZ-
rTa < NbTiZrW. It is obvious that NbTiZrW alloy possesses 
the largest C11 value of 220.5 GPa, showing that the alloy is 
the most difficult to compress in the principal axes among 
these studied alloys. The C 12 also shows a slight upward 
trend for M from Hf to W; hence, the resistance along the 
crystallographic y axes to shear deformations in the x direc-
tion is in the order of NbTiZrHf < NbTiZrTa < NbTiZrW. 
Likewise, the C44 increases slightly with atomic number of 
M element for NbTiZrM alloys, indicating that the resistance 
to shear strain on the (100) plane of the alloys increases for 
M from Hf to W.
Tetragonal shear modulus C’ is also in sequence of 
NbTiZrHf < NbTiZrTa < NbTiZrW; thus, the tetragonal 
deformation resistance exhibits a similar change trend 
with C11, C12 and C44. In summary, the single-crystal elas-
tic parameters of NbTiZrM (M = Hf, Ta, W) alloys pre-
sent increasing trend with atomic number of M element. 
Interestingly, for M from Hf to W, the VEC of NbTiZrM 
increases from 4.25 to 4.75, and the single-crystal elastic 
parameters also increase with increase in the VEC. Hence, 
the single-crystal elastic parameters are evidently correlated 
with the VEC, as shown in Fig. 2. However, the correlation 
between VEC and the elastic parameters C 12 and C44 is not 
very strong.
The total density of state (DOS) for the NbTiZrM 
(M = Hf, Ta, W) RHEAs is shown in Fig.  3. As can be 
observed, for each HEA, the pseudogap appears around the 
Fermi level of the total DOS, and the Fermi level deviates 
from the bottom of the pseudogap and even approaches to 
the peak of pseudogap, which means that the studied alloys 
are metastable. For M from Hf to W, the deviation of the 
Fermi energy level from pseudogap bottom descends, and 
the electron number at the Fermi level reduces, and these 
characteristics demonstrate that the stability of NbTiZrM 
alloys is enhanced for M from Hf to W. Moreover, the peak 
near the Fermi level shifts toward the descending zone for 
M from Hf to W, and this trend may explain why NbTiZrW 
alloys have larger dynamical stability against tetragonal 
deformation (evaluated by larger C’) [22].

Again, all anisotropic elastic moduli of NbTiZrM alloys 
are correlated with the VEC, and the correlation is visually 
shown in Fig.  4. Because all anisotropic elastic moduli of 
NbTiZrM increase with increase of the VEC for M from Hf 
to W, the strength and hardness of NbTiZrM alloys evalu -
ated from B , G and E, naturally also exhibit an increasing 
trend in sequence of NbTiZrHf < NbTiZrTa < NbTiZrW. 
Therefore, the strength and hardness of NbTiZrM alloys are 
correlated with the VEC. This correlation is due to the fact 
that VEC reflects the changes of the metallic bonds and thus 
the changes of the elastic moduli [22].
Ductility/brittleness is critical characteristics of mechani-
cal properties of materials. The Poisson’s ratio ν as a fun-
damental elastic parameter not only reflects the stability of 
the solid against shear deformation, but also can predict the 
brittle and ductile nature of materials. The value of ν ranges 
normally from -1 to 0.5, and the larger ν value means lower 
stability against shear and better ductility or plasticity. The 
critical ν value for the brittle/ductile transition is 0.26 [39]. 
The Pugh’s ratio B/G is also widely used to assess the duc-
tility/brittleness behavior. Materials with B /G > 1.75 gen-
erally possess a ductile characteristic, whereas those with 
B/G < 1.75 are brittle [40]. As seen in Table 3, the values of 
parameters ν and B/G for the studied alloys satisfy the duc-
tility conditions, demonstrating that NbTiZrM (M = Hf, Ta, 
W) alloys exhibit ductile behavior. And more notably, both 
ν and B /G parameters for NbTiZrM alloys decrease with 
atomic number of M element. Clearly, this ductile behavior 
of NbTiZrM is also correlated with the VEC, as displayed 
in Fig.  4. As can be observed, the ductility declines with 
increasing VEC for M from Hf to W. The present results 
reveal that the VEC is the important tuning parameter to 
design the exceptional ductility of NbTiZrM alloys.

4 Conclusions
In summary, the structure, elastic properties and Debye 
temperature of BCC NbTiZrM (M = Hf, Ta, W) refrac-
tory high entropy alloys are investigated systematically by 
ab initio EMTO-CPA method. The theoretically predicted 
lattice parameters agree well with the available experi-
mental data and approximately meet the rule of mixtures. 
The formation enthalpies show that these high entropy 
solid-solution phases are metastable. The single-crystal 
elastic parameters and elastic moduli of NbTiZrM RHEAs 
increase with the increasing VEC, therefore, the obtained 
mechanical properties in elastic range are evidently cor -
related with the VEC, although the correlation between 
VEC and the elastic parameters C 12 and C 44 is not very 
strong. The ductility is studied in terms of the Poisson’s 
ratio and Pugh’s ratio, and the ductility of NbTiZrM alloys 
declines with increasing VEC. Furthermore, the elastic 
anisotropies of NbTiZrM alloys are predicted to decrease 
with increasing VEC, thus, the anisotropies degree of these 
alloys is also correlated with VEC. The derived Debye 
temperatures of NbTiZrM (M = Hf, Ta, W) alloys are also 
found to increase with increasing VEC. Unambiguously, 
the mechanical properties in small elastic range have 
explicit correlation with the VEC. Therefore, the VEC is 
an effective parameter to predict and design the excellent 
mechanical properties of RHEAs.
