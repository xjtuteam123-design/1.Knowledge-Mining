Abstract 
The microstructure and mechanical properties of Mg-Zn-Ho-Zr alloys have been investigated in detail. The grain size of the as-c ast 
Mg-Zn-Ho-Zr alloy was greatly decreased by the addition of Ho, and the grain growth during solution treatment was suppressed by 
Mg-Zn-Ho phases formed at grain boundaries. These thermally stable Mg-Zn-Ho phases could not completely dissolve into the matrix dur-
ing solution treatment, and the strengthening effect of solution-plus-ageing treatment weakened. The addition of Ho can greatly enhance the 
high-temperature elongation of the Mg-Zn-Ho-Zr alloy, but the increase of high-temperature tensile strength was just a little. 
Keywords: magnesium alloys; holmium; rare earth additions; heat treatment; microstructure; mechanical properties
 
 
 
1. Introduction 
The characteristic properties of magnesium alloys, such 
as low density, high specific strength/stiffness, good damp-
ing capacity, die-castability, and recycling potential make it 
become an ideal candidate for replacing heavier materials 
(steel, aluminum) in cars to meet the weight reduction de-
mand within the automotive industry [1-2]. The widely used 
magnesium alloys belonging to Mg-Zn-Zr (ZK) series, such 
as ZK60 and ZK61, have excellent deformability, good 
room-temperature mechanical properties, and low cost. 
However, the application of these magnesium alloys is lim-
ited because of the poor high-temperature properties. 
It has been reported that rare earth (RE) additions can 
improve the high-temperature mechanical properties of the 
as-cast Mg-Zn-Zr magnesium alloys [3-4]. Especially the 
Mg-Zn-Y alloys containing quasicrystal icosahedral phase 
(I-phase) as a secondary solid ification phase exhibit good 
mechanical properties at room temperature and at elevated 
temperatures [5-7]. Generally, there are three kinds of ter-
nary equilibrium phases in the Mg-Zn-Y system. They are 
W-phase (Mg
3Zn3Y2, cubic structure), I-phase (Mg 3Zn6Y, 
icosahedral quasicrystal struct ure, quasi-periodically or-
dered), and Z-phase (Mg12ZnY) [8]. 
The maximum solubility of rare earth metal holmium in 
magnesium is 5.05 at.%, which is much larger than that of Y 
(3.59 at.%) [9]. Therefore, it is reasonable that there may be 
larger solution and precipitation strengthening effects of Ho 
in Mg alloys. In addition, the ZnMgHo quasicrystal phase 
was also found in previous work [10-17]. Therefore, Ho also 
satisfies the formation conditions of the quasicrystal phases 
in the Mg-Zn-RE alloys. The Mg-Zn-Ho-Zr alloy is one of a 
number of promising candidates for novel high-strength Mg 
alloys with quasicrystal phases. However, there was almost 
no detailed information on Mg-Zn-Ho alloys. Therefore, 
Mg-Zn-Ho-Zr alloys were investigated in the current work. 
The effects of Ho on the microstructure and mechanical 
properties of Mg-Zn-Ho-Zr magnesium alloys during heat 
treatment were studied.

alloys were prepared by melting them in an electrical resis-

starting material, pure Zn was added during melting, and Ho

The Mg-Zn-Zr alloy was solution-treated at 733 K for 10 
h, and the Mg-Zn-Ho-Zr alloy was solution-treated at 753 K 
for 40 h followed by quenching in cold water. The alloys 
were then isothermally aged at 423, 448, and 473 K for dif-
ferent time intervals. 
The precipitation process during ageing was checked by 
hardness measurements. The hardness was measured using 
Vickers-hardness with a load of 300 N. The room tempera-
ture tensile properties were tested on a Zwick/Roell testing 
machine, and high-temperature tensile tests were carried out 
on a Shimadzu machine at an initial strain rate of 10
−3 s−1 at 
different temperatures from 423 to 573 K. The microstruc-
tures of the alloys were analyzed by light microscope (OM, 
LEICA MEF4M) and scanning electron microscope 
(JCXA-733) using an acceleration voltage of 25 kV. Ther-
mal analyses were performed using differential thermal 
analysis (DTA1600) with a heating rate of 1 K/s. Phase 
identification was completed by X-ray diffraction (XRD, 
D/MAX-IIIA). 
3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Microstructure 
The microstructures of the as-cast and the solution- 
treated alloys are presented in Fig. 1. The average grain size 
of the as-cast Mg-Zn-Ho-Zr alloy is much smaller than that 
of the as-cast Mg-Zn-Zr alloy. It can be concluded that Ho 
has great effect on grain refinement of the as-cast 
Mg-Zn-Ho-Zr alloy. 
 
Fig. 1. Microstructures of Mg-Zn-Zr and Mg-Zn-Ho-Zr alloys: (a) as-cast Mg-Zn-Zr; (b) solution-treated Mg-Zn-Zr; (c) as-cast 
Mg-Zn-Ho-Zr; (d) solution-treated Mg-Zn-Ho-Zr. 
The eutectic phases along grain boundaries dissolved into 
the Mg-matrix, and the grains became a little larger in the 
Mg-Zn-Zr alloy after solution treatment. However, in the 
Mg-Zn-Ho-Zr alloy, there were still lots of second phases 
that remained along grain boundaries, and the average grain 
size in the solution-treated mi crostructure was almost the 
same as that of the as-cast alloy. The possible reason is that 
the grain growth of the Mg-Zn-Ho-Zr alloy during solution 
treatment was suppressed by the undissolved second phases.

Fig. 4 shows differential thermal analysis (DTA) results 
of the as-cast Mg-Zn-Ho-Zr alloy during the heating process. 
There are two endothermic peaks appearing during the 
process: one is at about 732 K, and the other is at about 814 
K. The temperatures of these two peaks are much higher 
than the eutectic temperature of Mg-Zn binary alloys (613 K)

Because the as-cast Mg-Zn-Ho-Zr alloy contains two kinds 
of eutectic phases, namely, I-phase and W-phase, these two 
peaks may correspond to the dissolution temperatures of 
these two phases. I-phase disso lved into the matrix, and 
W-phase still remained at grain boundaries after solution 
treatment at 753 K, so the dissolution temperature of I-phase 
is about 732 K, and the dissolution temperature of W-phase 
is about 814 K.

tendency during ageing. There is a certain decrease of the 
maximum hardness with increasing ageing temperature. 
However, the Mg-Zn-Zr alloy has a higher maximum hard-
ness than the Mg-Zn-Ho-Zr alloy in the same ageing condi-

strengthening effect in the Mg-Zn-Zr alloy. When Ho is 
added to the Mg-Zn-Zr alloy, the thermally stable Mg-Zn-Ho 
phases (I-phase and W-phase) formed at grain-boundaries. 
These Mg-Zn-Ho phases cannot completely dissolve into 
the Mg matrix after solution treatment, as indicated in Figs.1 
to 3. There are still lots of Mg-Zn-Ho particles (W-phase) 
that remained at grain boundaries after solution treatment. 
As a result, the solubility of Zn in the Mg matrix decrease, 
and the ageing strengthening effect in the Mg-Zn-Ho alloy 
weakened correspondingly. In the same ageing temperature, 
the Mg-Zn-Ho-Zr alloy needs a longer ageing time to reach 
the maximum hardness than the Mg-Zn-Zr alloy. This indi-
cates that the addition of Ho can prolong the precipitation 
process of the Mg-Zn-Ho-Zr alloy. According to the ageing 
curves, ageing at 448 K for 16 h for the Mg-Zn-Zr alloy and 
ageing at 448 K for 32 h for the Mg-Zn-Ho-Zr alloy were 
chosen as the best ageing parameters.

3.4. Tensile properties 
Fig. 6 shows the tensile properties of the Mg-Zn-Ho-Zr 
alloy in different states at room temperature compared with 
those of the Mg-Zn-Zr alloy. The Mg-Zn-Ho-Zr alloy ex-
hibits higher ultimate tensile strength (UTS) and yield 
strength (YS) but lower elongation in the as-cast state. The 
UTS and YS of both the alloys increased after solution-plus- 
aging treatment, but the increment of the Mg-Zn-Ho-Zr al-
loy was lower than that of the Mg-Zn-Zr alloy. The result is 
correspondent with ageing curves, and this may be caused 
by the thermally stable Mg-Zn-Ho phases, which cannot 
completely dissolve into the matrix after solution treatment. 
Therefore, the solution strengthening effect and the ageing 
strengthening effect of the Mg-Zn-Ho-Zr alloy were lower 
than those of the Mg-Zn-Zr alloy.

elongation.

4. Conclusions 
(1) The Ho element has a pronounced effect on the grain 
refinement of the as-cast Mg-Zn-Ho-Zr alloy. The addition 
of Ho can increase the UTS and YS but decrease the elonga-
tion of the as-cast Mg-Zn-Ho-Zr alloy. 
(2) Some thermally stable Mg-Zn-Ho phases formed 
along grain boundaries, which cannot completely dissolve 
into the matrix during solution treatment. These phases can 
effectively suppress grain growth during solution treatment. 
(3) Solution-plus-ageing treatment can increase the 
strength of the Mg-Zn-Ho-Zr alloy, but the strengthening 
effect was weakened for the incomplete solution of 
Mg-Zn-Ho phases. 
(4) Ho can greatly enhance the high-temperature elonga-
tion of the Mg-Zn-Ho-Zr alloy.
