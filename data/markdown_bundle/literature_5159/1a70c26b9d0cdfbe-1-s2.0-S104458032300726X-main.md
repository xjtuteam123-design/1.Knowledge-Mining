ABSTRACT 
The high-temperature strength and compressive creep resistance of TNM B1-type TiAl alloy modified with Y
2
O
3 
nanoparticles are studied. The effect of equiaxed or lamellar microstructure on mechanical properties and 
deformation mechanisms is demonstrated. It is established that the lamellar microstructure provides higher 
strength compared to the equiaxed one at high temperatures. Microstructural studies of the samples, subjected to 
compressive creep, revealed that creep is governed by the dislocation mechanisms for the equiaxed alloy, while a 
combination of dislocation mechanisms, twinning, dynamic recrystallization, and dynamic recovery is typical for 
the lamellar alloy. In both types of alloys, Y
2
O
3 
nanoparticles successfully act as reinforcement at 800 
◦
C, while 
being prone to failure and facilitating microcrack nucleation at the nanoparticle/matrix interface at 1100 
◦
C. 
1. Introduction 
The advances in engine manufacturing and the increasing demand 
for engine power and efficiency have spurred the development of novel 
materials that would be simultaneously characterized by low density 
and improved elevated-temperature strength. Alloys based on TiAl/ 
Ti
3
Al intermetallic compounds meet these criteria [ 1 – 4 ]. The density of 
these alloys ranges from 3.9 to 4.2 g/cm
3
, which is approximately 
twofold lower than that of nickel-based superalloys. The high strength 
and creep resistance of these materials allow them to be used for 
manufacturing low-pressure turbine blades. These materials started to 
be intensively applied in manufacturing in 2006: the Ti – 48Al – 2Cr – 2Nb 
alloy (known under the GE4822 trademark) was the first of such ma -
terials and has formed the first generation of TiAl-based alloys [ 5 ]. Its 
improvement has given rise to second-generation Ti – (45 – 48)Al – (0 – 4) 
Nb – (1 – 2)Cr/Mo alloys [ 6 , 7 ] and third-generation 
Ti – 43Al – 4Nb – 1Mo – 0.1B alloys (the group of TNM-B1 alloys) [ 8 , 9 ]. 
Further research efforts have focused on enhancing the elevated- 
temperature strength of the alloys by adding dopants that stabilize the 
alloy structure. Yttrium and its oxide have recently been widely applied 
as such additives [ 10 – 13 ]. The effect exerted by Y
2
O
3 
nanoparticles is 
caused by growth inhibition of cast alloy grains, reduction of the mean 
size of γ -TiAl/ α
2
-Ti
3
Al lamellae inside the colonies, as well as hindering 
of dislocation motion, and Orowan strengthening of the matrix [ 14 – 16 ]. 
The γ -TiAl phase with an L10-type face-centered tetragonal lattice 
and α
2
-Ti
3
Al phase with a D019-type hexagonal crystal lattice are the 
key structural components of TNM-B1 alloys. Nb- and Mo-rich alloys 
typically contain the β phase with an A2-type bcc lattice as well. 
Depending on their composition and heat treatment modes, TNM-B1 
alloys can have an equiaxed [ 17 ], duplex [ 18 ], nearly lamellar [ 19 ] or 
fully lamellar [ 20 ] microstructure. The lamellar microstructure of the 
alloy is thought to be the most favorable for improving creep resistance, 
especially resistance to dislocation creep, since a thin lamella thickness 
reduces the mean free path of dislocations [ 21 ]. It is also possible to 
implement a similar strengthening mechanism for alloys with an equi -
axed microstructure by using nanoparticles. For this very reason, studies 
of the deformation behavior of equiaxed TNM-B1 alloys reinforced with 
Y
2
O
3 
nanoparticles are also of significant research interest. 
The effect of the β phase on the mechanical properties of TiAl-based 
alloys cannot be assessed unambiguously. On the one hand, several 
additional dislocation slip systems can be involved because of the 
presence of the β phase, thus resulting in increased plasticity and a 
broader range of service temperatures for the alloys [ 22 ]. On the other 
hand, the excessive content of the β phase and the formation of coarse 
grains of this phase at the interfaces with γ -TiAl, α 2-Ti
3
Al or colonies of 
their lamellae reduce the high-temperature strength and creep

3.5. Summary on deformation mechanisms of the TNY-Eq and TNY-Lam 
alloys 
The thermomechanical properties of TNY-Eq and TNY-Lam alloys 
reveal a distinct discrepancy between the underlying deformation 
mechanisms derived from the calculated stress exponent n from the 
Norton-Bailey equation and those observable in the morphology of the 
deformed samples. The values of stress exponent n are 3.24 – 7.81 and 
5.06 – 10.85 for TNY-Eq and TNY-Lam alloys, respectively. For both al -
loys at temperatures of 800 – 900 
◦
C, n goes beyond the ranges typical for 
the main deformation mechanisms during creep. In addition to the 
purely dislocation mechanisms, which are characteristic for n values 
ranging from 3 to 7, it has been established that TNY-Eq and TNY-Lam 
alloys exhibit deformation due to shearing and twinning. However, 
there is a lack of data in the literature regarding the correlation between 
these mechanisms and the stress exponent n. 
M. A. Morris et al. showed [ 46 , 47 ], that even higher stress exponent 
values can be reached during creep tests of TiAl based alloys – 13 and 19. 
Generally, this situation is not unique and is commonly observed during 
creep of metal-matrix composites reinforced with nanoparticles [ 48 ]. It 
can be explained by the presence of high internal stresses within the 
material and the necessity to overcome a certain threshold stress 
required to activate dislocation mobility. Most likely, the overestimated 
values of the stress exponent are associated with dislocation pinning at 
Y
2
O
3 
nanoparticles. 
Fig. 12 shows a scheme summarizing the main types of deformation 
of the TNY alloys with an equiaxed and lamellar microstructure. Having 
generalized the results presented above, one can infer that the micro -
structure type had a pivotal effect on the key patterns observed for 
deformation processes. The TNY-Eq alloy was characterized by dislo -
cation activity in creep. The exaggeration of service conditions and the 
raising of temperature from 800 to 1100 
◦
C will increase the number of 
dislocations several folds due to the activation of new slip systems and 
the emergence of slip bands inside the grains of the -TiAl phase in several 
directions. The growing internal stress caused by these types of defects 
will eventually lead to the formation of macrodefects, including cracks, 
and alloy failure. 
Y
2
O
3 
nanoparticles affect the creep resistance of the TNY-Eq alloy. 
They inhibit dislocation motion and prevent slip band propagation. 
However, their efficiency drops abruptly with temperature increasing to 
1100 
◦
C, as can be seen from structure degradation and nanoparticle 
destruction ( Fig. 8 d) as well as the drastically increased steady-state 
creep rate ( Fig. 4 a). 
Deformation of the TNY-Lam alloys with a lamellar microstructure at 
800 and 1100 
◦
C is typically characterized by a combination of dislo -
cation mechanisms and twinning. According to changes in the appear -
ance of lamellae inside the colonies, distortion of their shape, and 
subgrain formation as a result of polygonization, one can infer that 
dynamic recrystallization and dynamic recovery play a more important 
role as the test temperature is raised to 1100 
◦
C. 
In the TNY-Lam alloys, Y
2
O

nanoparticles also successfully fulfill 
their function at 800 
◦

ternal stress during creep at 1100 
◦
C causes mutual displacement of the 
lamellae, thus inducing microcrack formation at the site where they 
contact nanoparticles, which is a factor causing the loss of strength of the 
alloy.
