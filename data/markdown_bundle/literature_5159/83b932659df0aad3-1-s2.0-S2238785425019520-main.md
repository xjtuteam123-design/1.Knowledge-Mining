ABSTRACT
Two thermomechanical treatments, namely N (510 
◦
C/16 h) and C (510 
◦
C/16 h + 480 
◦
C/8 h), were employed 
in an extruded Mg-9Gd-4Y – 2Zn-0.5Zr alloy to reveal the influence mechanism of coordinated deformation of the 
14H-LPSO phase during the hot deformation process. Results revealed that the C-treated alloy(CC alloy), 
compared to the N-treated alloy(CN alloy), demonstrated a higher volume fraction of dynamic recrystallization 
(DRX) and reduced textural intensity after compression, which correlated with enhanced plasticity. In particular, 
the dynamic precipitation of the lamellar LPSO phase during deformation inhibited DRX nucleation and triggers 
the strong basal texture, in contrast to the behavior of pre-existing lamellar LPSO phases. These microstructural 
disparities induce the superior strength-ductility synergy of the CC alloy relative to CN alloy during high- 
temperature compression at 400 
◦
C. This study highlights mechanistic insights into the interplay of DRX, 
texture evolution, and crack initiation phenomena associated with the 14H lamellar LPSO phase.
1. Introduction
As a hot lightweight material, Mg alloy has high specific strength, 
specific stiffness and excellent vibration resistance, good shielding per -
formance against electromagnetic waves and good impact resistance, 
and has broad prospects in military, aircraft, vehicle manufacturing and 
other fields [ 1 – 3 ]. Recent studies have shown that the addition of rare 
earth elements such as Gd and Y to Mg alloys can cause atomic size 
mismatch and valence effect, which significantly affects the improve -
ment of Mg alloy properties [ 4 , 5 ]. Among a large number of Mg-RE(rare 
earth)alloys, Mg-RE-Zn series alloys containing the long-period stacking 
order (LPSO) phase have broad engineering application prospects. In 
2001, Kawamura [ 6 ] et al. developed Mg – Y – Zn series alloys by rapid 
solidification/powder metallurgy (RS/PM) methods and observed the 
LPSO phase structure. Their periodic layered stacking characteristics 
were confirmed by transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and X-ray 
diffraction techniques. The diversity of LPSO phases was confirmed by 
high-angle annular dark-field scanning transmission electron micro -
scopy (HAADF-STEM) analysis, including 10H,12R,14H,15R,18R,21R 
and 24R, among which the 18R and 14H-LPSO structures were the most 
common. It exhibits different morphologies under as-cast and 
heat-treated conditions as well as in different alloy compositions [ 7 , 8 ]. 
Among them, the 14H-LPSO structure is usually formed in solid-state 
reactions at high temperatures, The main structure of the rapid solidi -
fication process is 18R, and 10H is relatively rare [ 9 , 10 ]. The LPSO 
phase can effectively impede the movement of dislocations through a 
similar fiber reinforcement mechanism, thereby enhancing the 
Mg-RE-Zn series alloys [ 11 , 12 ]. It has been confirmed [ 12 , 13 ] that the 
LPSO phase generates a strengthening effect by refining the grain size of 
DRXed and hindering displacements, which makes an important 
contribution to the deformation behavior of the alloy. Shao et al. [ 14 ] 
believe that LPSO phase can change the expansion direction of micro -
cracks, inhibit the easy extension of microcracks to adjacent kink re -
gions, and thus improve their fracture toughness. Liao et al. [ 15 ] found 
that with the increase of the thickness of lamellar LPSO phase, the 
strengthening efficiency of the alloy gradually increased. Zhou et al. 
[ 16 ] studied that the orientation relationship between the LPSO phase 
and the tensile direction strongly affects the strength, ductility and

The samples of N and C alloys were compressed at 400

lamellar LPSO phases can simultaneously resist dislocation movement 
and grain boundary sliding, delay dynamic recrystallization (DRX), and 
thereby increase flow stress [ 28 ]. However, in this experiment, the CC 
alloy with initially dense lamellar LPSO phase distribution within the 
grains has a lower peak stress compared to the CN alloy without lamellar 
LPSO phase initially, indicating that the CC alloy is more prone to 
deformation at high temperatures. Meanwhile, the dense lamellar LPSO 
phase in the CC alloy hinders the sliding and climbing of dislocations. 
The lamellar LPSO phase enhances the strength of the alloy through the 
Orowan bypass mechanism. The LPSO phase has good stability at high 
temperatures and can effectively pin dislocations, delaying the occur -
rence of dynamic recrystallization [ 29 ]. However, the peak stress during 
the compression process of CC alloy is not as high as that of CN alloy. 
This is inconsistent with the research findings of H.T.Jeong et al. [ 18 ].

3.6. Twin behavior
As we all know, when Mg alloy is deformed at low temperature, 
twins are generally regarded as a supplementary deformation mecha -
nism, which can change grain orientation and promote continuous 
deformation, and become an important mechanism for coordinating 
plastic deformation [ 50 , 51 ]. In addition, at high strain rate, twins are 
the main deformation mechanism of Mg alloy [ 52 ]. Twin can provide an 
effective nucleation site for DRX [ 53 , 54 ], promote grain refinement and 
change texture, and effectively improve the mechanical properties of Mg 
alloys [ 55 – 57 ].

4. Discussion
4.1. DRX mechanism of two alloys
During the thermal deformation process, Mg alloy mainly relies on 
DRX for grain refinement, and the volume fraction of DRX region 
significantly affects the average size of GWZ942K alloy under different 
states [ 60 ]. In order to further clarify the DRX mechanism of GWZ942K 
alloy under two states, The {10 – 12} tension twin grains distributed 
differently in CC alloy were selected for analysis (grains of B and C in 
Fig. 7 (c) ), where grain B is the undivided grain, grain C is the grain to be 
divided. The results are shown in R1 and R2 in Fig. 13 . Obviously, the 
Undrxed region R1-R2 of GWZ942K alloy shows obvious color changes, 
and the orientation difference Angle statistics of straight lines AB and CD 
show that the orientation difference within the grain changes signifi -
cantly and the dislocation is active, resulting in obvious lattice rotation, 
indicating that the grain rotates to adapt to the deformation and facili -
tate the formation of kink more easily. At the same time, the texture is 
weakened by kinking, and the polar texture is continuously distributed 
in a certain range due to the deflection of the C-axis of the grain. In 
addition, the higher Kernel Averaged Misorientation (KAM) color dif -
ference represents the strain degree and local dislocation density in the 
deformation region to a certain extent [ 61 ]. In the thermal deformation 
process, more dislocation density and higher strain energy accumulate 
in the Undrxed region. The occurrence of DRX process in Mg alloy is 
closely related to the evolution of dislocation, and the dislocation 
movement and accumulation in the intragranular form a stress con -
centration area, resulting in the occurrence of kink. The occurrence of 
kink can be clearly seen from R1 (a-1) and R2 (b-1) in Fig. 13 . The kink 
provides a preferred location for DRXed nucleation, and the kink can 
form a kink band at a high strain rate, and the kink band further evolves 
into a DRXed nanocrystalline band [ 62 ], promoting the occurrence of 
DRX. The DRX process of Mg alloys usually includes CDRX and DDRX 
[ 60 , 63 – 65 ]. In general, CDRX occurs inside the grain without obvious 
nucleation and growth process. For example, the region S1 in R1 rep -
resents the CDRX region, while the grain G1 in R1 represents the small 
grains formed by the CDRX process. DDRX usually occurs at GBs, mainly 
due to the large orientation gradient formed between adjacent GBs 
under applied stress, and the adjacent grains are subjected to different 
strains during deformation, resulting in the occurrence of DDRX. For 
example, the region S2 in R1 represents the DDRX region, while the 
grain G2 in the R1 region represent the small grains formed by the DDRX 
process. GWZ942K alloy is refined by CDRX and DDRX during thermal 
deformation.
As can be seen from the analysis of Figs. 7 and 9 , CN alloy grains are 
only squashed and a small amount of CDRX occurs, while CC alloy the 
intragranular lamellar LPSO phase has a large number of kinks and 
fractures, resulting in a large amount of CDRX, which is connected with 
DDRX to segment the grains, resulting in without decrease in DRX vol -
ume fraction of CC alloy at high strain rate and twin grains appear.

4.2. Effect of LPSO phase orientation on microstructure evolution
Combined with OM and EBSD lattice orientation, the thermal 
deformation behavior of lamellar LPSO phase in different lattice orien -
tation grains is very different. As shown in Fig. 15 , when the c-axis of the 
grain is nearly parallel to the compression direction, the base surface 
rotates slightly to be parallel to the compression direction. When the 
grain c axis and the compression direction are at a certain Angle α , the 
grain base rotates towards the extrusion direction, and the lamellar 
LPSO phase will be kinked. The larger α is, the more easily the lamellar 
LPSO phase will be kinked, and the larger the kink Angle is, and even the 
lamellar LPSO phase will be fractured and broken. When the c axis of the 
grain is close to the perpendicular to the compression direction, twins 
are formed, and large strain and slip coordination plastic deformation 
are generated by stretching twins. While the lamellar LPSO phase of CN 
alloy appears in the process of lattice rotation, the weakening ability of 
the texture is far less than that of the prefabricated lamellar LPSO phase, 
and the kinking behavior of the lamellar LPSO phase of CC alloy 
randomly weakens the texture, resulting in completely different textures 
of the two alloys. The hard oriented grains in CN alloy are much more 
than CC alloy, which is not conducive to subsequent plastic deformation.
4.3. Crack initiation mechanism

shear or local rheological instability of the alloy. SAGA R P et al. [ 66 ] 
found that once instability defects occur, subsequent deformation is 
difficult to eliminate them. As shown in Fig. 5 , with the increase of the 
strain rate, intergranular cracks gradually increase (cracks in the white 
circle in Fig. 5 ), rheological instability occurs at the interface of DRXed 
and deformed grains, and adiabatic shear is formed at the interface of 
grains and massive phases. By comparing Fig. 5 (a), (b), (c) and Fig. 5 
(d) , (e), (f), it is found that the number of intergranular micro-cracks in 
CC alloy is much less than that in CN alloy.

provides a greater effect on the plastic forming of the alloy. At the same 
time, the design of reasonable LPSO phase spacing has become a key 
factor in the study of plastic strength and toughness.
5. Conclusions
(1) The prefabricated lamellar LPSO phase significantly advanced 
the softening behavior of the material, resulting in lower peak 
stress of the C alloy containing the LPSO phase compared with the 
N alloy without the lamellar LPSO phase. In the process of hot 
compression, the kinking phenomenon of CC alloy (including 
prefabricated lamellar LPSO phase) is more intense. This in -
dicates that the prefabricated lamellar LPSO phase not only re -
duces the strength of the material, but also affects its plastic 
deformation behavior by increasing the degree of kinking.
(2) With the increase of strain rate, the DRX volume fraction of CC 
alloy is much higher than that of CN alloy. This phenomenon can 
be attributed to the fact that the phase kinking of prefabricated 
lamellar LPSO promotes the nucleation of CDRX and works with
