ABSTRACT 
The tensile strengths of the as-extruded Mg – Al – Ca ternary alloys at both ambient and elevated temperatures are 
enhanced via adding a high content of Ca element up to 6 wt%. High-temperature strength of the present rare- 
earth-free Mg alloys can be even compared with those of the reported Mg-RE alloys. The as-extruded 
Mg – 6Al – 6Ca (wt. %, AX66) alloy exhibits a tensile yield strength (YS) of ~404 MPa and an ultimate tensile 
strength (UTS) of ~415 MPa at room temperature, and YS of ~225 MPa and UTS of ~265 MPa at 200 
�
C, 
respectively. For the AX66 alloy, the nucleation rates of dynamic recrystallized (DRXed) grains are larger than 
those of the AX62 and AX64 alloys due to the typical particle stimulated nucleation effect. A number of long-rod 
Al
2
Ca phase is dispersed at the grain boundaries of AX66 alloy, and a restricted degree of DRX is induced. 
Consequently, the majority of α -Mg matrix exists in the as-deformed state involving a high density of subgrains. 
The combined effects of grain refinement hardening, texture hardening and precipitation hardening guarantee 
the high strength. Specially, numerous micron-scale bulk (Mg, Al)
2
Ca phases are found to distribute among 
matrix, which play a critical role in maintaining the high thermal-resistance. 
1. Introduction 
Requirements for lightweight materials become urgent recently in 
the automobile industry due to the serious energy crisis and the critical 
environmental problems including the excessive CO
2 
emissions [ 1 – 4 ]. 
Magnesium alloys, as the lightest metallic structural materials, can meet 
this weight-saving demand, due to the obvious advantages of high 
specific strength, high specific stiffness and excellent damping resistance 
[ 5 – 9 ]. The wrought Mg alloys exhibit greater potential to be used in the 
vehicles components, considering the higher strength as compared with 
that of the die-cast Mg alloys [ 10 , 11 ]. However, the actual usages of the 
Mg alloys products are limited due to the still lower absolute strength, 
the higher processing cost and the poorer corrosion resistance than those 
of the competitive Al counterparts [ 12 – 15 ]. 
Nevertheless, there has been a great progress in the development of 
high-performance Mg wrought alloys in the past decades [ 16 , 17 ]. For 
example, Huang [ 18 ]et al. fabricated an ultra-fine grained 
Mg-2.1Nd-0.2Zn-0.4Zr wt. % alloy via extrusion, and an ultra-high YS of 
541 MPa has been achieved. A super high-strength Mg-Gd-Y-Zn-Mn 
alloy with tensile yield strength (YS) of 543 MPa was developed by Heng 
[ 19 ]. Zheng [ 20 ] et al. reported that the Mg-8Gd-1Er-0.5Zr alloy can 
exhibit the ultra-high YS of 518 MPa and ultimate strength (UTS) of 560 
MPa. However, high cost of RE elements, as well as the high relative 
density, render the massive usage of the Mg-RE based alloys unaccept -
able. Consequently, it become more desirable to develop new RE-free 
Mg wrought alloys with high strength. 
In this sense, the Ca-containing Mg alloys have received great at -
tentions due to the similar role of Ca with RE elements. And the Ca atom 
is proven to be effective in refining the α -Mg matrix with a trace amount 
of solute addition [ 21 ]. For example, Pan [ 22 , 23 ] et al. reported that a 
high YS of ~377 MPa can be achieved in the low-alloyed Mg-1.0 Ca (wt. 
%) binary alloy produced by conventional extrusion processing, and the 
high strength can be ascribed to the multi-scale substructures, including 
the ultrafine recrystallized grains, the mono-dispersive Mg

Fig. 1 shows the tensile stress-strain curves of the as-extruded AX62, 
AX64 and AX66 alloys at ambient temperature. AX62 alloy exhibits the 
yield strength (YS) of ~248 MPa, ultimate strength (UTS) of ~309 MPa

content, AX64 alloy displays a higher YS value of ~289 MPa and UTS

reaches 6 wt %, the YS and UTS values of the AX66 alloy increase 
significantly to 404 MPa and 415 MPa, respectively, while the EL is 
reduced to only ~1%. The detailed mechanical properties of the as-

4. Discussions 
4.1. Formation mechanism for the microstructures in Mg – Al – Ca alloys 
With increasing the Ca concentration, the average grain size of the 
as-extruded sample has been obviously refined, e.g., ~0.55 μ m in the 
AX66 alloy. Moreover, it is observed that volume fraction of the 
unDRXed grains increases when higher amounts of Ca atoms are added. 
The above grain structures should be correlated with both the micron- 
sized Al
2
Ca and/or (Mg, Al)
2
Ca phases and dynamic precipitations of 
nano-Al
2
Ca phases in as-extruded AX62, AX64 and AX66 alloys. In fact, 
the micron-sized Al
2
Ca and/or (Mg, Al)
2
Ca phases act as an additional 
barrier to the movement of dislocations, leading to the accumulation of 
dislocations, which leads to an increase of the nucleation rate around the 
second-phases. In this sense, the nucleation rate of DRXed grains is 
depend positively on the number density of the micron-sized phases, 
which follows the typical mechanism of particle stimulated nucleation 
(PSN). When the dislocation density around the precipitation phases 
reaches the critical value, the new crystal grains would be nucleated via 
the typical way of polygonization . In the AX66 alloys with highest 
amount of Ca content, the nucleation rate of DRXed grains becomes also 
larger than those of the AX62 and AX64 alloys. 
On the other hand, the increase of Ca concentration would also lead 
to formation of higher density of nano-particles, involving the rod and 
needle type nano-Al
2
Ca phases. And the presence of the nano-sized 
precipitates can in turn affect the DRX behavior. In particular, the 
pinning effect of the second phase particles on the grain boundaries can 
effectively inhibit the growth of recrystallized grains. For present 
Mg – Al – Ca alloys, a number of long-rod type Al
2
Ca phases are found to 
disperse at the grain boundaries of AX66 alloy, as shown in Figs. 7 and 8 . 
Consequently, a restricted degree of DRX is induced despite of the high 
nucleation rate of grain nuclei, and majority of the α -Mg matrix exists in 
the form of un-DRXed type containing a high density of LAGBs. For 
example, the as-extruded AX66 alloy shows a typical bimodal grain 
structure consisting of the fine DRXed grains of in average size of ~0.55 
μ m and the elongated un-DRXed grains ( Fig. 5 ). The result is also

4.2. Strengthening mechanism of Mg – Al – Ca alloys 
A high YS of ~387 MPa has been achieved in present as-extruded 
AX66 alloy containing the highest Ca concentration. And the high 
strength can be ascribed to both the grain refinement hardening, texture 
hardening and the precipitation hardening. Firstly, the grain size of the 
as-extruded AX66 alloy has been reduced to be in the range of 300 – 500 
nm, which can obviously increase the YS value according to the high 
slope value for Hall – Petch [ 52 ] relationship of Mg based alloys. Sec -
ondly, the EBSD results have shown that the as-extruded AX66 alloy 
possesses a strong fiber texture due to the high volume fraction of 
un-DRXed region. Moreover, the coarse unDRXed regions have a low 
Schmid factor for the basal slip, which means that the other deformation 
modes with higher critical resolved stress, such as the prismatic < a >
and/or the pyramidal < c þ a > slips would be activated to accommodate 
the strain. As a result, the yield strength level can be improved. How -
ever, the EL value is limited, ~1.3%, which follows the typical trade-off 
between the strength and ductility. Thirdly, numerous nano-sized Al
2
Ca 
could contributes the high strength of the alloy via the Orowan mech -
anism [ 53 ], where additional stress has to be used to overcome the 
obstacles from the hard nano-particles. Finally, a plenty of the long-rod 
and spheres phases distributes among the Mg matrix, which is similar 
with the in-situ composite materials, as shown in Figs. 7 and 8 . The 
dispersion of these micro-sized Al
2
Ca and/or (Mg, Al)
2
Ca phases can 
contribute to increase the yield strength by following the 
fiber-reinforcement hardening mechanism, which is similar as the LPSO 
phases in the Mg-RE-Zn alloys [ 54 – 56 ].
