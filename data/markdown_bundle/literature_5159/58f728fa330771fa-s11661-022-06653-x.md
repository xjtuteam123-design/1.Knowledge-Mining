Computational Design of Novel Ni Superalloys
with Low Crack Susceptibility for Additive
Manufacturing
HAO YU, JINGJING LIANG, ZHONGNAN BI, JINGUO LI, and WEI XU
Additive manufacturing (AM) has enabled the fabrication of innovative, geometrically complex
components of metallic materials with minimal material waste. However, the application of AM
in processing Ni superalloys remains challenging because of the high crack susceptibility. To
address this problem, a computationally design method was applied to design novel Ni
superalloys with low crack proneness as well as decent service performance. Diﬀerent types of
crack formation mechanisms during AM processing, i.e., the hot tear cracks and strain-age
cracks, have been considered, while a series of corresponding criteria to quantitively describe the
crack susceptibility were parallelly analyzed. To select the most proper criteria as optimization
index in alloy design regime, a series of prototype alloys with wide range of alloying elements
were printed, with their crack behaviors being quantitively captured. The freezing range
criterion and strain-age crack criterion were then determined as the optimization index in the
design routine. A genetic algorithm was applied to probe the compositional search domain,
while a Pareto front linking the hot tear crack criterion and strain-age crack criterion was
constructed by combining results of two separate optimization routes. Novel Ni compositions
with optimized crack resistance as well as decent microstructural features were thereby
identiﬁed. The new solutions are predicted to possess fairly better crack formation resistance
compared to existing Ni superalloys. Moreover, the newly designed alloys manage to
computationally outperform the existing printable alloys in all aspects of temperature
capability, creep properties and oxidation resistance.

I. INTRODUCTION
THE application of additive manufacturing (AM) in
metals has enabled the fabrication of components with
complex geometries, which can be hardly achieved
thought traditional manufacturing techniques.
[1] By
local melting of metal powder, thin layers of alloys
can be progressively added with greater precision, less
scrappage and enhanced design freedom. AM by now
has been considered as a new paradigm for the design
and production of high-performance components for
aerospace, medical, energy and automotive
applications.
[2–5]
Ni superalloys are well known as the most promising
candidate for structural components such as turbine
blades and vanes used in the hot sections of aircraft
engines and land-based natural gas turbines. [6] The
typical ‘‘brick and mortar’’ microstructure with high
amount of cuboidal c’ coherently embedded in a
solid-solution-strengthened c matrix guarantees their
high temperature performance, as shown in Figure 1(a).
However, the application of AM in Ni superalloys
remains challenging, since during AM processing, the
microstructural features of Ni superalloys often lead to
the strong proneness to cracking.
[7–9] The microstruc-
tures prepared by the conventional directional solidiﬁ-
cation (Bridgeman processing) and additive
manufacturing (the selective electron beam melting)

pared to the as-cast dendritic microstructure, which has
the primary dendrite arm spacing around hundreds of
microns, the as-printed microstructure of CMSX-4
fabricated by selective electron beam melting presents
cellular dendrite morphology with the primary dendrite
arm spacing <10 um in the crack-free region. The much

the further optimization for decent mechanical proper-

The coupled thermodynamic/genetic algorithm con-
cept to identify the composition for the best desired
property combinations has been proved a promising
method to solve the abovementioned problem. Genetic
algorithms have an algorithmic structure inspired by the
evolutionary process in nature system by following the
survival of the ﬁttest principle, which manages to
randomly generate the initial population and control
the heuristic evolution by probabilistic operators.
Therefore, genetic algorithms have been proved quite
eﬀective and relatively robust for multi-parameter and
multi-objective optimization. The alloy design task is
then formulated as an optimization problem by choos-
ing suitable alloy properties as goals to be minimized or
maximized. By deﬁning constraints to the optimization
problem, undesirable alloy compositions can be
excluded early, and the compositions are driven towards
desirable compositions. In this work, developing novel
additive manufacturable Ni superalloys with decent
service performance is the aim. The formation mecha-
nisms for hot tear cracks and strain-age cracks have
been considered to quantify the crack susceptibility. A
series of prototype alloys was manufactured with the
same processing parameters, while the proper criteria
that can best describe the crack behavior of prototype
alloys have been thereby selected as the optimization
index for alloy development. Three novel alloys have
been designed by employing the generic alloy-by-design
model, and their crack susceptibility, creep properties,
temperature capability and oxidation resistance have
been compared with Ni single-crystal commercial grades
and exiting printable Ni superalloy.
II. MODEL DESCRIPTION
A. Design Methodology
The design methodology used follows the ‘‘goal-
means’’ material design philosophy,
[21] which starts with
deﬁning the target properties required by the applica-
tion, and then the target microstructures are subse-
quently designed to obtain the desired properties,
according to metallurgical and mechanical principles.
Finally, to attain the tailored microstructure, the alloy
compositions are designed and optimized. In this work,
the requirements imposed by the intended use condi-
tions are converted to quantiﬁable criteria, which will be
linked to a speciﬁc composition via physical metallurgy
models with the guidance of thermodynamic principles.
In this case of printable Ni superalloys, the focused
desirable properties referred to the low crack suscepti-
bilities, while the mechanical properties are contributed
by the volume fraction of strengthening c’ phase.
Genetic algorithm was employed because of its eﬀec-
tiveness and relative robustness for multi-parameter and
multi-objective optimization. A similar model

B. Deﬁning the Go/No-Go Criteria for Mechanical
Properties
In this work, the mechanical properties of Ni alloys
were considered as constraint criteria to ensure the
performance of desirable solutions above a preset
minimum level. Within the scope of this work where
newly designed Ni alloys were to perform at the
temperature around 1173 K (900 /C176C), only c/c’ type
microstructures with rare detrimental phases such as
topologically close packed (TCP) phases were allowed.
Also, the volume fraction of strengthening c’ particles
had to be high enough to block the migrations of
dislocations in c matrix to guarantee the proper
mechanical properties. Therefore, two go/no-go criteria
are imposed at the start of the evaluation cycle for each
candidate solution:
(1) The equilibrium volume fraction of c + c’ phases
at service temperature 1173 K (900 /C176C) had to be
> 99 pct.
(2) Meanwhile, the amount of c’ strengthening phase
had to be > 40 pct.
After setting the constraints, the targeted microstruc-
ture with less undesirable microstructural components
could be obtained, while the mechanical properties of
newly designed alloys at high temperature could be
targeted. On the other hand, due to the rapid cooling in
AM, the c’ precipitations in as-printed matrix generally
adapts to a round shape with particle size <100 lm. The
precipitation of c’ particles will introduce great internal
stress and therefore greatly aﬀect crack growth behav-
ior, which will be discussed in detail in section 2.3.
C. Optimization Criteria for Crack Susceptibility
The candidate Ni superalloys are supposed to possess
decent crack resistance of either hot tear cracks or
strain-age cracks. To simulate crack behavior in alloy
design routine, the criteria that can quantitively describe
the crack susceptibility need to be established. As
mentioned above, diﬀerent types of cracks forming
during AM processing make a combined contribution to
crack vulnerability, yet with diﬀerent formation mech-
anisms. Therefore, the evaluation criteria for varied
cracks need to be deﬁned individually to clarify their
dedications
1. Criteria for hot tear cracks
The solidiﬁcation sequence and products during AM
processing under the non-equilibrium condition can be
readily described by the Scheil-Gulliver theory, provided
that a thermodynamic description for the multicompo-
nent alloy is available. The model assumes perfect
mixing in the liquid and no diﬀusion in the solid phase.
A theoretical limit exists with the assumption that there
is zero diﬀusion in the solidiﬁed material, but for the
speciﬁc case in this work, the simpliﬁcation is accept-
able because of the superfast cooling rate during the
solidiﬁcation process and the absence of fast diﬀusing
elements. To calculate the solidiﬁcation curve, the
temperature step of 1 K is selected.

crack susceptibility have been developed by researchers,
focusing on the formation mechanism of hot tear cracks
during solidiﬁcation. The model which employs the
freezing range (FR) as the key parameter for crack

2. Criteria for strain-age cracks
The strain-age cracks (SAC) in Ni superalloys gener-
ally occur during thermal cycling or during the subse-
quent heat treatment because of the superposition of the
remaining residual stress and a further induced stress
from the precipitation of the g’ from solid solution. To
quantify the proneness of strain-age cracks, an empirical
index has been proposed by Thompson and modiﬁed by
Reed, based on the assumption that the formation of c
compromises local ductility via eﬀective total fraction of
c forming elements: Al, Ti, Nb and Ta (wt pct).
[15,28]
This index is shown as follows:

During the cooling processing after solidiﬁcation, the
ductility of manufactured components will ﬁrst increase
when the microstructure goes through a (nearly)
homogenized region. The compositions with wider
homogenization range will have relatively more crack
resistance since the residual stress can thereby be more
easily restored. The ductility will sharply decrease when
secondary phase starts to precipitate, as it is well known
that the matrix becomes most vulnerable to strain-age
crack at the early stage of precipitations formation. The
critical temperature where ductility reaches the lowest

solidus by
Singh.[29] In this work, this critical temperature has
been deﬁned as the temperature to reach 80 pct mole
fraction of c matrix in solid states to better cater to the
crack formation mechanism. The strain rates introduced
by precipitation are determined by the change in
precipitation volume with temperature when assuming
that temperature decreases linearly with time.

III. MODEL APPLICATION
By setting FR and SAC as the optimization criteria,
either the minimization of hot tear crack tendency or the
reduction of strain-age crack proneness can be realized.
As the units of the FR and SAC optimization factors are
diﬀerent, the predicted contributions are not simply
numerically additive. Hence, we ﬁrst optimize the FR
and SAC independently; the optimization scheme can be
found in detail elsewhere.
[21,32] Then, all qualiﬁed
solutions from the two independent optimizations are
plotted together as the FR vs. SAC value, from which a
‘‘Pareto front’’ can be constructed. A Pareto front is a
curve where the critical information of two conﬂicted
properties is presented graphically. From the curve one
can derive the best possible trade-oﬀs between FR and
SAC.
In the design strategy, for every candidate composi-
tion possessing a desirable microstructure (fulﬁlling the
go/no-go criteria), a value for the non-optimized crack
susceptibility has also been obtained. The FR and SAC
factors of all accepted candidate solutions (around
21,000 solutions) are recorded and stored for further
analysis. The results are shown in Figure 6. The
solutions with the lowest FR value and minimized
SAC value have been selected and indicated by arrows.
Although neither of these two solutions meets the design
target of the present study, i.e., an optimal combination
of FR and SAC, the properties of these candidates are
still worth detailed analysis. To obtain the optimal
combination, a ‘‘Pareto front’’ has been constructed,
which is indicated by the dashed curve. The best
combination of FR and SAC can be found at the
turning point of the Pareto front, indicated by the
arrow. This solution only has a slightly higher value of
FR and SAC factors than the separate maxima.
Besides, the level of c’ volume fractions for the
desirable solutions has been shown as the color of the
dots. The color variations present a nearly horizontal
pattern, which indicates the sensitivity of c’ amount to
SAC factors. Compared to the eﬀect of solidiﬁcation
processing, it is also highly reasonable that alloys with
higher c’ volume fraction tend to become more vulner-
able to strain-age cracks. These observations indicate
that the current alloy design strategy is quite rational for
interpreting the crack formation mechanisms, with great
eﬃciency in searching for the optimal solutions.
The chemical compositions, phase conﬁgurations at
1173 K (900 /C176C), calculated freezing range and SAC
criteria of the three mentioned solutions are listed in
Table III. The alloys with the best FR, SAC and best
combination are labeled as Alloy FR, Alloy SAC and
Alloy Pareto, respectively. These alloys generally have a
high level of Al to keep the desirable volume fraction of
c’. Besides, each of the three newly designed alloys
possesses concentrations of alloying elements that are
highly diﬀerent from other, which indicates the complex
joint eﬀects of elements on the crack susceptibility. It is
interesting to point out that for Alloy FR, the alloying
level of Al reaches the upper limit of composition range,
while its Co level hits the lowest line. This result implies
the possibility to further improve the hot crack resis-
tance with narrower freezing range by widening the
search range of alloy concentrations for Al and Co.
Current observations demonstrate that the alloy design
approach is capable of balancing complex interactions
in a multicomponent system and can be used to achieve
a global trade-oﬀ between FR and SAC.
IV. DISCUSSION
A. Comparison of Crack Susceptibility Between Newly
Designed Alloys and Existing Alloys

V. CONCLUSIONS
1. A computational design method has been applied to
design novel Ni superalloys with low crack suscep-
tibility as well as decent service performance.
2. To define the proper criteria to quantify the crack
proneness, series of prototype alloys with varied
chemical composition have been employed and
additively manufactured with the same processing
parameters. Freezing range criterion and Strain-age
crack criterion have been selected as optimization
criteria that can best reproduce the crack behavior.
3. A genetic algorithm has been applied to probe the
compositional search domain, while a Pareto front
linking the hot tear crack criterion and strain-age
crack criterion was constructed by combining
results of two separate optimization routes. Novel
Ni compositions with optimized crack resistance as
well as decent microstructural features have been
identified. The new solutions are predicted to
