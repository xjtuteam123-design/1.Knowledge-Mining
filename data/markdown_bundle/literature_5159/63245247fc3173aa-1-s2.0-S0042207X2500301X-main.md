ABSTRACT
An advanced design strategy was employed to enhance the thermal stability of Mg-Gd-Y-Zn-Mn alloys by opti -
mizing the distribution of solute atoms at grain boundaries and phase boundaries. The developed Mg-4Gd-6Y- 
1.5Zn-1Mn alloy demonstrated remarkable yield strength and ultimate tensile strength of 266 MPa and 296 
MPa at 300 
◦
C. This exceptional performance can be attributed to the co-segregation of Gd, Y, Zn, Mn atoms at 
grain boundaries, which effectively mitigated grain coarsening and obstructed dislocation slip across boundaries. 
Furthermore, the Y and Zn elements segregated at the α -Mn particle boundaries, significantly suppressing the 
growth of α -Mn particles and promoting the formation of more α -Mn particles. The solute segregation at the α -Mn 
particle boundaries prevented the α -Mn particles from being sheared and impeding dislocation motion during 
high-temperature deformation.
In the pursuit of energy conservation and emission mitigation, 
magnesium (Mg) alloys, recognized as the lightest structural materials, 
are projected to find extensive applications in automotive, aerospace, 
and other industries [ 1 – 4 ]. However, the inherent thermal instability of 
Mg alloys poses a significant challenge, predominantly attributable to 
the vulnerability of interfacial regions such as grain boundaries (GBs) 
and phase boundaries (PBs) [ 5 , 6 ]. These GBs and PBs are highly dis -
torted regions characterized by high energy regions, rendering them 
susceptible to migration at elevated temperatures, which in turn leads to 
the coarsening of grains and secondary phases [ 7 ]. Some researchers 
have proposed that the coarsening of secondary phases, such as

severely undermines the high-temperature strength of Mg alloys [ 6 , 8 , 9 ]. 
Consequently, enhancing interfacial stability is crucial for mitigating 
interfacial migration and addressing the deficient thermal resistance of 
Mg alloys.
In general, the motion rate of interfaces in polycrystalline materials 
is intrinsically linked to the interfacial mobility and driving force for 
interfacial migration, both of which are attenuated as the interfacial 
excess energy is reduced [ 7 ]. Therefore, facilitating low-energy in -
terfaces emerge as a pivotal strategy to enhance interfacial stability. In 
accordance with the Gibbs adsorption isotherm, the segregation of so -
lute atoms at the interface mitigates interface energy, thereby dimin -
ishing the driving force and retarding interfacial migration [ 10 ]. 
Sauvage et al. [ 11 ] advocated the enhancement of the thermal stability 
of Al alloys through the induction of Ca elemental segregation at GBs. 
Hutchinson et al. [ 12 ] elucidated that AgMg segregation layers might 
inhibit the coarsening of Ω phases by impeding solute diffusion when the 
Al – 4Cu – 0.3Mg – 0.2Ag alloy was subjected to elevated temperatures. In 
the field of Mg alloys, researchers commonly investigate the influence of 
GBs and PBs segregation on grain size, texture, and ductility [ 13 – 16 ]. 
However, research on enhancing thermal resistance by modifying solute 
segregation at GBs and PBs has received little attention.
In this study, we introduce an innovative alloy design paradigm 
aimed at optimizing the distribution of solute atoms at GBs/PBs to 
enhance thermal stability. We successfully attained the exceptional 
high-temperature strength by meticulously designing the Gd/Y contents 
to indirectly affect the solute content at the GBs and α -Mn/matrix

largely unaffected by variations in Gd/Y content, as the total amount of 
RE elements remains constant.
As temperature increases, the atomic diffusion rate significantly 
rises, making GBs more prone to movement during high-temperature 
deformation [ 26 ]. The reduction in GB strength at high temperature 
can lead to the intensification of phenomena such as GB sliding, 
migration, and fracture [ 32 ]. However, GB segregation can enhance the 
bonding strength at GBs, thereby pinning the GBs and preventing these 
detrimental effects [ 26 ]. In Fig. 5 (a), a significant accumulation of 
dislocation formed near the GBs and the slip behavior of dislocations 
across the GBs was hindered in 4Gd-6Y alloy after the 300 
◦
C tensile 
testing. Moreover, the 4Gd-6Y alloy has demonstrated remarkable 
thermal stability; even after 100 h heating at 300 
◦
C, the grain size did 
not significantly increase ( Fig. 5 (b)). For the 4Gd-6Y alloy with the 
smallest grain size, there is a high possibility of a sharp decrease in 
high-temperature strength, and maintaining high strength is extremely 
difficult. The solute segregation of Gd/Y/Zn/Mn elements at GBs 
effectively inhibits grain growth and obstructs dislocation movement, 
enabling the 4Gd-6Y alloy to maintain high strength at 300 
◦

The α -Mn/matrix interfacial stability heavily influences the size and 
volume fraction of α -Mn particles, which is another crucial factor 
resulting in high thermal stability. Luo et al. [ 33 ] suggested that the 
energetically favorable negative formation entropy of Gd facilitates the 
formation of solid solutions within α -Mn particles, while the positive 
formation entropy of Y/Zn promotes segregation at the interface, this is 
consistent with our findings. As Y content increases, the interfacial 
concentration initially rises before declining, reaching its maximum in 
the 4Gd-6Y alloy. The decrease in α -Mn/matrix interfacial content 
observed in the 2Gd-8Y alloy may result from competitive interactions 
between the α -Mn particle and the LPSO phase. Researchers have pro -
posed that the excess of Y significantly lowers the stacking fault energy, 
favoring the formation of stacking faults [ 34 ]. This process encourages 
the aggregation of excess Y/Zn elements at the stacking faults,

In summary, we propose an innovative strategy to improve heat 
resistance by optimizing interfacial concentrations. With varying Gd/Y 
content, all alloys show a tendency for Gd/Y/Zn/Mn atoms to segregate 
at the GBs, with minimal differences in the overall solute content. The 
segregation at GBs effectively mitigates grain growth and obstructs 
dislocation across GBs at 300 
◦
C in the 4Gd-6Y alloy. Additionally, as the 
Y content increases, the concentration of Y/Zn elements at the α -Mn 
particle interfaces first increases and then decreases, with the 4Gd-6Y 
alloy having the highest concentration. This is due to the competition 
between the LPSO phase and α -Mn particles for the Y/Zn elements. The 
higher concentration of Y/Zn elements at α -Mn particle interfaces con -
tributes to the formation of high-density and small-sized α -Mn particles, 
while preventing their coarsening and shearing during high-temperature 
deformation.
