Abstract 
The finite temperature elastic properties of non-equiatomic refractory 
high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) HfNbZrTa1-xTix are investigated using the exact 
muffin-tin orbitals method in combination with coherent potential approximation and 
Debye-Grüneisen model , especially the effect of Ta and Ti addition is emphasized . 
Taking HfNbZr as a reference, the lattice constant s decrease when alloying with Ta, 
and continu e to decline slightly with replacement of Ta by Ti . Estimating from 
calculated elastic parameters, mere Ta addition leads to the high strength and stiffness 
accompanying evident lowering of ductility , while sole Ti addition bring s about the 
high ductility at the expense of reducing strength and hardness. With substitution of Ti 
for Ta, HfNbZrTa1-xTix alloys achieve a relatively high ductility accompanied a gentle 
decline in high strength and hardness. As temperature increases, although the obtained 
single-elastic and polycrystalline elastic parameters exhibit decreasing trend , the 
anti-softening ability is enhanced with substitution of Ti for Ta, and HfNbZrTa1-xTix 
alloys remain a n overall good combination of strength and ductility at high

1. Introduction 
As the unique engineering materials, the high -entropy alloys (HEAs) have 
triggered intensive research activities in materials science and engineering community 
due to the distinctive mechanical properties, such as high strength and hardness, 
outstanding thermal stability, excellent softening resistance, and high corrosion 
resistance etc.[1-3]. Among currently reported HEAs, two groups can be identified as 
the best for utility at high temperature [4]: (I) TM HEAs comprised of 3d -transition 
metals, and (II) RHEAs based on refractory element. In particular, the RHEAs are 
considered to be new superalloys for high temperature applications because of their 
high melting points, high strength and excellent high temperature anti-softening [5-8]. 
Thus, the study of their superior mec hanical properties at high temperature can 
provide a vast opportunity for the exploration and development of high -temperature 
structure application [9]. 
To tune the mechanical properties of alloys, addition alloying elements is often 
introduced into the single -phase hosts. Elements like Ti, Ta, V , Cr, Mo and Al, have 
been successfully added into HEAs system to assess the alloying influences on the 
structures and properties [6, 10-15]. As a typical additive element, Ti possess good 
ductility and high specific strength . It is shown that Ti addition is beneficial to the 
strength and compressive ductility of NbMoTaW and VNbMoTaW HEAs at 
room-temperature [12]. Also, addition of Ti is found to improve significantly the 
compressive yield strength of the FeCoCrNiMn HEA [16]. Ta with high melting point 
is also often added into alloys to enhance structural stability. It has been shown that Ta 
addition can promote the yield strength and hardness of CoCrFeNi or CoCu 0.5FeNi 
HEAs [13, 17].

Though most HEAs systems are composed of the main phase solid solution, the

the investigation of the main phase solid solution is crucial. Moreover, in 
compositional space of present study , no phase transition of non-equiatomic 
Hf-Nb-Zr-Ta-Ti RHEAs with bcc structure is observed to our best knowledge. 
Therefore, exploring high temperature mechanical properties of principal alloy phase 
in Hf-Nb-Zr-Ta-Ti system and the influence of alloying elements from th e theoretical 
study is valuable and necessary in the aspects of gaining more excellent performance 
in non -equiatomic compositions . At present, the coherent potential approximation 
(CPA) or special quasi -random supercell (SQS) method in combination with 
first-principle calculation have been widely applied to the investigation of the elastic 
mechanical properties of HEAs [9, 23, 24]. The CPA is a very powerful mean-field

single-site approximation to solve the impurity problem for each alloy constituent , 
whereas SQS technique involves large supercells constructed , and the atomic

2. Computational methods and details 
2.1. First-principles calculations 
The first -principles calculations were performed by using the exact muffin-tin 
orbitals (EMTO) method [25], and the Perdew -Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) 
exchange-correlation functional was employed to perform the self -consistent 
calculations [26]. In EMTO calcul ations, the single -site coherent -potential 
approximation (CPA) was adopted to treat random structure [27-29], and 
single-electron Kohn -Sham equations were solved with the scalar -relativistic 
approximations and soft-core scheme [30, 31]. The Green function was calculated for 
16 complex energy points used to integrate the valence states below the Fermi level. 
The Brillouin zone was sampled by a unif orm k-point mesh of 21×21×21 in present 
calculations. The screened impurity model parameter was set as 0.6, and the full 
charge density technique (FCD) was employed to obtain the total energy of systems. 
2.2. Elastic constants 
The equilibrium volume V0, the ground state total energy E and bulk modulus B 
were extracted by fitted Briche -Murnaghan equation of states (EOS). In order to

3. Results and discussion 
3.1 Elastic properties at 0K and influence of Ta and Ti addition 
The ground state properties of bcc HfNbZrTa 1-xTix(x=0-1) RHEAs at 0K is first 
computed, which can be used to assess the accuracy of the EMTO -CPA calculations. 
To obtain the equilibrium lattice constant s of HfNbZrTa1-xTix(x=0-1) alloys, the total 
energies E as a function of volume V is calculated and fit ted using Birch-Murnaghan 
equation of states, then the lattice constants are derived from total energy 
minimization. Fig. 1(a) shows the variation of the equilibrium lattice constants of 
HfNbZrTa1-xTix alloys as a function of Ti concentration. Taking the latt ice parameter 
(a=3.466Å) of original HfNbZr alloy as the reference, the lattice constant of 
HfNbZrTa alloy from sole equimolar Ta alloying decreases to 3.428Å. With 
substitution of Ti for Ta, the lattice constants of HfNbZrTa1-xTix continues to decrease. 
When mere addition of equimolar Ti, the lattice constant of HfNbZrTi decline s to 
3.422Å, which is in good agreement with the other theoretical value 3.419Å [23], but 
slightly smaller than the measured experimental data 3.444Å at room temperature [39]. 
This can be easily understood because the lattice constants of elemental crystals 
follow aTi< aTa< aNb< aHf< aZr [21, 40]. Meanwhile, the mixing lattice constants a mix 
calculated by the Vegard's rule [41] are also pictured in Fig. 1 (a). Evidently, the 
variation trend of lattice constants calcula ted by EMTO -CPA is similar to o nes 
calculated by Vegard's rule. The smaller lattice constant s of EMTO-CPA than a mix 
implies stronger interaction between added elements and other elements in alloys. To 
evaluate the stability of the alloys, the formation enthalpy as a function of Ti

4. Conclusion 
Through the EMTO -CPA calculations in combination with the quasi -harmonic 
Debye-Grüneisen model, the finite temperature elastic properties of non-equiatomic 
HfNbZrTa1-xTix alloys are investigated, and the influence of alloying element T a and 
Ti is exclusively highlighted. The present results show that addition of sole Ta into 
HfNbZr alloy can remarkably improve the strength and hardness whereas the ductility 
deteriorates apparently. With substitution of Ti for Ta, the strength and hardness are 
slightly lowered and the ductility is apparently improved. When about 50 at.% of Ta 
content is replaced by Ti, HfNbZrTa1-xTix alloy still remains a relatively high strength 
and hardness as well as a rather high ductility. With increase of temperature, the
