abstract
The origin of the superior high temperature strength of Ti-rich g-TiAl with addition of Nb is a highly
controversial issue. In this paper, we calculated the interaction energies between point defects in these
alloys using a ﬁrst-principles plane-wave pseudopotential method. It is found that, the nearest neigh-
bouring Nb atoms on Al-sublattice (Nb Al) repel whereas the next nearest neighbouring Nb Al atoms attract
each other, indicating that Nb Al atoms may form short-range ordered structure. Based on this ﬁnding, we
propose that the short-range order may be responsible for the high strength of the Ti-rich TiAl-Nb alloys.

1. Introduction
The Nb-containing TiAl alloys Ti-45Al-Nb were found to have
superior high temperature strength and excellent oxidation resis-
tance [1]. However, the origin of the high strength of these alloys is
highly controversial. Paul et al. [2] related the strengthening to the
low Al content of the alloys and the accompanying structural
changes, whereby the addition of Nb plays no role. In contrast, Zhang
et al. [3,4] ascribed the high strength to the solid-solution hardening
of the Nb additions due to the size mis ﬁt of Nb on the Al-sublattice.
Concerning the mechanism of the solid-solution strengthening,
Woodward et al. [5] have investigated the in ﬂuence of solid solu-
tions including alloying elements and antisites on the ﬂow
behaviour of g-TiAl, adopting a continuum model of the solute-
dislocation interaction energy with input parameters of the size
misﬁt and elastic constants from ﬁrst-principles calculations. The
theoretical solute-dislocation strength indicated that Al antisite
strengthens the alloy signi ﬁcantly, whereas Ti antisite does not.
This is in contrast to the experimental ﬁnding which showed that
solid-solution strengthening is more evident in Ti-rich than in Al-
rich alloys [3,4]. In addition, Nb is also recognized as a weak
strengthening solute from the calculations of Woodward et al.
Instead of considering the interaction between a single solute and
dislocation, Fröbel and Appel [6] proposed that, in off-stoichio-
metric TiAl alloys, point defects such as antisites and vacancies may
form point defect complexes which result in a variety of strain
ﬁelds depending on the mutual orientation of the constituent point
defects. The point defect complexes may move to the dislocations
through the so called anti-structural bridges (ASB) [7] and, there-
fore, inhibit the glide of the dislocations and thus strengthen the
alloys [6]. To understand the formation of the point defect complex,
a knowledge of the interaction between the point defects is crucial.
However, such interaction has rarely been addressed in literature.
Although Woodward et al. [8] have included the point defect
complexes in their model for the site-occupation of substitutional
solid solutions in TiAl, the interaction between the point defects
composing the complex was neglected since the model is based on
point defect gas approximation [9e13].
In this study, we calculated the interaction energies between the
point defects in
g-TiAl-Nb alloy using a ﬁrst-principles plane-wave
pseudopotential method. With the calculated energies, we attempt
to understand the origin of the high strength of the TiAl alloys
containing high concentration of Nb.
2. Calculation details
The point defect formation energy and the interaction energy
between point defects are calculated as the total energy differences
between supercells with properly chosen geometries and compo-
sitions, which will be described in the related part later in this
paper. For the defected system, we choose quite a large supercell
that is 3 /C2 3 /C2 2 of the crystallographic cell of
g-TiAl (see Fig. 1),
consisting of 18 sites for both Ti and Al-sublattice, so as to minimize
the interaction between the point defects in the nearby supercells
and to mimic the isolated point defect or point defect complex.

the short range order strengthening mechanism still cannot
account for the higher strength of Ti-rich alloys than that of the Al-

Since Nb Al atoms have much stronger short range ordering
tendency than Nb Ti, the short range order strengthening mecha-

the superior high strength of

supported by the suggestion of Paul et al. [2], i.e., the athermal
dislocation mechanism is responsible for the increased strength of
the Nb-containing alloys, since short range order strengthening is
not temperature/diffusion sensitive and may play a substantial
role at high temperature, dissimilar to the case of Snoek atmo-
sphere strengthening. We note that the short range order
strengthening is mainly ascribed to the interaction between Nb
Al
atoms, which supports the argument of Zhang et al., i.e., the
addition of Nb is critical for attaining the high strength of TiAl
alloys [3,4].

3.3. Electronic structure origin of the interaction between Nb Al
In order to understand the electronic structure origin under-
lying the interaction between Nb Al atoms with different inter-
distances, we calculated the local electronic density of states
(LDOS) of Nb Al and its nearest neighbouring Al atom as shown in
Fig. 2 . As seen from Fig. 2 , there exists a peusedo-gap around the
Fermi level for both the d-DOS of Nb Al and p-DOS of Al, which is
resulted from the hybridization between the d states of Nb Al and
the p states of Al, indicating strong covalent bonding between Nb Al
and its nearest neighbouring Al atom [22e25]. Since the nearest
neighbouring Nb Al atoms have one less Al coordinate than the
single NbAl, an additional non-bonding peak appears at the energy
of about 1 eV of the d-DOS of the nearest neighbouring Nb Al,
indicating weaker covalent bonding between the NbAl and Al atoms
for the nearest neighbouring Nb Al atoms than for the single Nb Al.
Therefore, the conﬁguration with nearest neighbouring Nb Al atoms
is less favorable in energy than that with Nb Al atoms far apart from
each other. This is why the nearest neighbouring Nb Al atoms show
repulsive interaction. For the d-DOS of the single Nb Al atom, we
observe a peak right at the Fermi level. This peak disappears for the
next nearest neighbouring Nb
Al atoms, which indicates a stronger
covalent bonding between Nb Al and Al for the next nearest
neighbouring Nb Al atoms than that for the single Nb Al. Therefore,
we get an attractive interaction between the next nearest neigh-
bouring Nb
Al atoms.
4. Summary
In summary, we calculated the interaction energy between point
defects in g-TiAl with and without addition of Nb using a ﬁrst-
principles plane-wave pseudopotential method. It is demonstrated
that Nb
Al and AlTi antisites have a strong tendency to order. Based on
this ﬁnding, we proposed that the short range order of NbAl and AlTi
might be the origin of the superior high strength of the Ti-rich TiAl
alloys with additions of Nb.
