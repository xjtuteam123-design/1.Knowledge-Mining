Introduction 
Superalloys for turbine blade applications have constantly been improved through chemistry 
modifications and better processing techniques. The improvement in mechanical strength is attri- 
buted partly to the increasing amounts of refractory elements such as Ta, W and/or Mo. For ins- 
tance, among the modern single crystal superalloys, PWA 1480 (i) contains 12 wt% Ta and CMSX-2 
(2) has 8% W and 6% Ta. More recently, the element Re has been shown to be an efficient streng- 
thener in creep both in the Ni-base eutectlc composites and single crystal superalloys (3, 4). 
It has also been reported that this element partitions solely to the matrix, retards the coar- 
sening rate of the ~' strengthening phase and increases the ~'/~' misfit (5). However, the physi- 
cal mechanisms responsible for the better creep resistance of the Re-contalning superalloys have 
not been elucidated. The purpose of this paper is to report and discuss the results of an atom 
probe' analysis on some single crystal superalloys in order to clarify the role of Re, on an ato- 
mic scale, in improving the creep strength. 
Materials and Experimental Procedure 
The chemical compositions of the single crystal alloys used in this investigation are given 
in Table i. The element Re was partly or entirely substituted for W in the reference alloys 
CMSX-2 and PWA 1480. Single crystals were grown parallel to the [001] orientation using the 
seeded technique under a temperature gradient of 250°C/cm and at a withdrawal rate of 
15 cm/hour. Laue back reflection technique was used to check the orientations. All specimens 
were given a solutionlng and homogenizing treatment between 1280 and 1325°C for 15 hours fol- 
lowed by air quenching and the following precipitation heat treatment : 
IIO0°C/4h/AC + 850°C/48h/ 
Specimens within 5 ° of [001] orientation were prepared both for the tensile creep tests and 
atom probe analyses of the ~ and ~' phases. The ~/~ microstructure of a typical single crystal 
alloy having about 70 vol.% of the ~" phase is illustrated in Fig. i. 
A FIM atom probe combines a field ion microscope in which an atomic resolution image of a 
specimen can be obtained with a mass spectrometer of single atom sensitivity. The principle of 
this technique is based on field ionization of a rare gas near the surface of a sharply pointed 
specimen and controlled field evaporation of surface atoms. The chemical nature of those ions 
evaporated from a small selected area is determined by means of time-of-flight spectrometry, the 
details of which can be found elsewhere (6). The unique advantage of FIM-atom probe, compared to 
the other microanalytical techniques, resides in its high spatial resolution coupled with the 
quantitative analysis capabilities. The lateral resolution at the tip surface can be varied from 
.5 to 5 nm and composition profiles can be obtained with an ultimate depth resolution of one 
atomic layer. The atom probe is therefore a unique instrument for the analysis of ~/~ super- 
alloys on a very fine scale. The details of the specific experimental conditions for the ana- 
lysis of Ni-base superalloys have been reported elsewhere (7).

Results and Discussion 
The stress rupture lives of the Re-modlfled versions of CMSX-2 and PWA 1480 alloys at 850°C 
are significantly increased compared with those of the base-llne alloys, despite the slightly 
lower volume fraction of the ~' phase in the modified alloys, as shown in Table 2. Further con- 
firmation of the beneficial role of Re in creep is provided at still higher temperatures. For 
instance, at 950°C under a stress of 240 MPa the Re-modlfled CMSX-2 has a stress rupture llfe of 
646 hours, whereas the standard CMSX-2 fails at 390 hours. These results unamblgously show that 
Re is indeed a potent strengthener of single crystal superalloys. In order to understand the ba- 
sic mechanisms responsible for this strengthening, an atom probe analysis of the ~ and ~' phases 
was undertaken both in the base-line alloys as well as in the Re-modlfled versions. Typical mass 
spectra of phases in the CMSX-2-4Re are shown in Fig, 2a,b. Since the precipitates have a higher 
evaporation field compared to that of the matrix (7), the Re atoms are predominantly field eva- 
porated as triple-charge ions in the ~' phase while both ionization states (Re 2+, Re 3+) are 
observed in the matrix (Fig. 2a). The prominent feature of these spectra is the presence of a Ta 
peak in the matrix. The phase compositions in both alloys are given in Table 3, clearly showing 
the increase of Ta content in the matrix of the CMSX-2-Re alloy. Statistical fluctuations are 
given by the standard deviation O'= Vc(I-C)/N where C is the measured concentration and N the 
number of collected ions. While the Ta level in the matrix of the CMSX-2 alloy is near zero, its 
concentration attains 0.5 at% ( 1.5 wt%) in the Re-modlfied version. This suggests that the 
presence of Re significantly increases the solubility of Ta in the gamma solid solution in such 
alloys. Because of the overlap existing between W and Re in the mass spectra, it is somewhat 
difficult to determine the concentrations of these elements very precisely. This led us to 
investigate a tungsten-free Re-contalnlng version of CMSX-2 (Table i) to assess the distribution 
of charge states for Re ions (Re3+/Re 2+) and the partitioning ratio for this element. These 
data were used to evaluate the Re and W concentrations shown in Table 3. It was again confirmed 
that the presence of Re in the tungsten-free ~4SX-2 alloy leads to an increase of the Ta content 
in the matrix, qomparable to that observed in the CMSX-2-dRe alloy. 
It is possible to deduce the volume fraction of the ~' precipitates from the phase composi- 
tions (Table 3) and the overall composition of the material. For the CMSX-2-dRe alloy, the cal- 
culated volume fraction is close to 60%. The volume fraction of precipitates measured in the 
CMSX-2 alloy after electrolytic extraction was found to be 68%. The lower value of the volume 
fraction in the Re-modlfied CMSX-2 is caused by the lower amount of W (4 wt%) in this alloy 
compared with that in the standard CMSX-2 (8% W). It is well known that W partitions equally to 
the ~ and ~' phases whereas Re preferentially segregates to the ~matrix. 
In order to establish a firm support for the observed improvement in creep resistance 
brought about by Re additions, it is important to study the spatial distribution of this element 
in the FCC solid solution. Although the spatial resolution of the atom probe is, in principle, 
sufficient to detect clusters smaller than 1.0 nm, it is often difficult to decide whether con- 
centration fluctuations observed in the composition profiles are statistically significant or 
not. Because of the small number of ions detected per desorbed layer for such a lateral resolu- 
tion, background noise induced by statistical fluctuations is particularly pronounced. One of 
the methods to avoid these uncertainties consists in analyzing the atom-probe data with the help 
of a histogram of the number of solute atoms (Re) which are detected in consecutive layers. Such 
a representation is shown in Fig. 3 and compared to a Polsson's random distribution (weak proba- 
bilities); the parameter of this distribution is taken equal to the averaged number of collected 
ions per evaporated layer ( A = 1.56). It is quite clear from these histograms that the number 
of layers (Nb) actually detected containing four or five Re atoms (solid lines) is greater than 
that expected from the random distribution (broken lines). By comparing the two distributions 
with the help of a statistical ~2 test, it can be concluded that a significant number of small 
Re-enrlched regions is present. Short range order therefore occurs for Re in the matrix of these 
alloys. 
The investigation was pursued on PWA 1480 and its Re-modifled derivative to confirm the va- 
lidity of the above results. The phase compositions for these alloys are given in Table 4. As 
expected, Re atoms are again observed to segregate preferentially to the matrix and its parti- 
tioning ratio is close to that measured in the CMSX-2-4Re alloy. It is, however, worth men- 
tioning that, because of the very high initial Ta content of PWA 1480 (4 at%), the concentration 
of this element in the matrix of the Re-free PWA 1480 (0.56 at%) is already high and close to 
that previously observed in the CMSX-2-Re alloy (0.52 at%). This indicates that in superalloys

containing a very high initial concentration of Ta, additions of Re would not change the parti- 
tioning coefficient for Ta. The volume fraction of the ~' precipitates in the Re-modlfled 
PWA 1480 deduced from the atom probe data is close to 70%. The fine scale fluctuations of Re ob- 
served in the matrix are illustrated by Fig. 4. In such composition profiles, the cumulative 
number of rhenium (or chromium) atoms is plotted versus the cumulative number of detected ions. 
The average slope of each curve gives the local concentration and the abscissa is proportional 
to the depth probed in the material. The rhenium profile clearly exhibits a small enriched re- 
gion extending over 1.3 nm. The lateral resolution being of the same order as the apparent size 
of the cluster, the observed composition has to be considered as an integrated measurement of 
the small cluster and the surrounding matrix. However, from simple geometrical considerations, 
it is possible to estimate the true composition of the cluster. Composition profile given in 
Fig. 4 may roughly he interpreted as a small particle crossed by the "analysis cylinder" (7). 
Assuming that the clusters are completely within the analyzed area, the Re concentrations 
computed from various clusters encountered fall between 20 and 90 at%. The presence of short 
range order for the Re atoms is hence unambigously confirmed. 
Conclusions 
This investigation, for the first time, provides a physical basis for the observed improve- 
ment of the stress rupture properties of Re-modlfled superalloys. Atom-probe microanalyses of 
various Re containing alloys clearly revealed the occurence of short range order in the matrix. 
Small Re clusters, about i.O nm in size, were detected in the Re-modlfied versions of CMSX-2 and 
PWA 1480 superalloys. Short range order is known to influence considerably the solid solution 
hardening in a number of materials (8). The Re clusters can act as more efficient obstacles 
against dislocation motion compared to the isolated solute atoms in a solid solution. It is 
thought that these clusters play a significant role in improving the creep strength of Re- 
containing superalloys.
