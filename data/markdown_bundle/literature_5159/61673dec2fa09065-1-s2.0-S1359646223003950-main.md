ABSTRACT 
A novel Mg-8Gd-2Y-1.5Zn-1Mn (GYZ-1Mn, wt.%) alloy with extraordinary high-temperature mechanical prop -
erties was developed by introducing RE/Zn segregation on α -Mn nanoparticles. Its ultimate tensile stress (UTS) 
and yield stress (YS) are 312 MPa and 248 MPa at 300 ℃ , which are 87 MPa and 52 MPa higher than the highest 
strengths of other Mg-Gd-Y-Zn alloys reported in the previous literature. The outstanding performance of GYZ- 
1Mn alloy was associated with the segregation of RE/Zn atoms on the α -Mn nanoparticles, which facilitated the 
formation of fine lamellar long period stacking ordered (LPSO) phase and impeded the coarsening of α -Mn. 
Consequently, retarded dynamic recrystallization behavior occurred in the GYZ-1Mn, resulting in fine grain size, 
high density of geometrically necessary dislocations and strong fiber texture intensity to strengthen the high- 
temperature mechanical properties. Hence, this work paved a novel path for the development of heat-resistant 
Mg alloys by introducing elemental segregation on nanoparticles. 
The urgent demand for lightweight and energy-saving materials has 
prompted an increasing interest in the development of magnesium (Mg) 
alloys, as they offer unique merits of low density, high specific strength 
and stiffness [1] . Nevertheless, the commercial Mg alloys, such as AZ91, 
have poor heat-resistant properties due to the thermally unstable

Al

phase [2] . To broaden the application, Mg alloys with excel -
lent high-temperature mechanical properties were intensively designed. 
Among them, the Mg- rare earth (RE)- Zinc (Zn) alloys exhibit satisfied 
properties both at room and elevated temperatures due to the formation 
of long period stacking ordered (LPSO) phase [ 3 , 4 ]. Although the LPSO 
phase shows better thermal stability than Mg matrix, it can be relatively 
unstable due to dynamic precipitation or dissolution at high tempera -
tures [ 5 , 6 ]. 
Recently, the researchers paved a novel insight to improve the me -
chanical properties of Mg alloys by introducing metal particles. It uses 
the precipitated metal particles to cause the strengthening effect. 
Alloying elements with lower solid solubility and higher thermal 
stability than Mg matrix are ideal candidates, such as manganese (Mn) 
[7] . Celikin et al. [8] found that the α -Mn particles improved the creep 
properties of Mg-Sr alloys at 150 – 225 
◦
C [9] . Homma et al. [10] re -
ported that Mn improved the creep properties of Mg-Al-Ca alloy, 
because it enhanced the aging response, caused by G.P. zone and Ca 
atoms. The promising heat-resistant Mg alloys usually contain substan -
tial amounts of RE and Zn, but the interactions between α -Mn particles 
and RE/Zn atoms remain unraveled. Although some researchers [11] 
claimed that Mn could promote the formation of LPSO phase via 
reducing the stacking fault energy (SFE), the underlying mechanism of 
the interactions between Mn and LPSO phase is far from adequate. 
In this work, an Mg-Gd-Y-Zn-Mn alloy with extraordinary high- 
temperature mechanical properties was developed by introducing 
elemental segregation on α -Mn nanoparticles. A fundamental under -
standing of the interactions between α -Mn and LPSO phase was revealed 
for the first time. This work puts forward a novel strategy to tailor 
elemental distribution on nano-sized reinforcements to develop Mg

Particular attention is given to the segregation of RE/Zn atoms in the 
α -Mn nanoparticles, which has not been reported in previous literatures 
and it deserves special interest to explore its effect. To elucidate the 
segregation of RE/Zn atoms, the formation enthalpies ( Δ H ) of Gd, Y and 
Zn atoms in α -Mn matrix were calculated using first-principle calcula -
tion. The doping solid solution models of Mn-X ( X = Gd, Y, Zn) were 
built (Fig. S5), the most negative Δ H indicates the solubility of the 
element in the Mn matrix is energetically favorable. The calculated Δ H 
for Gd, Y and Zn in Mn are 0.458, 1.467 and 0.190 eV/atom, respec -
tively, where negative enthalpy is only found between Gd and Mn. This 
indicates that Gd is energetically more favorable to form solid solution 
in Mn matrix than other elements. Hence, high contents of Gd atoms are 
segregated in Mn nanoparticles. Conversely, Y and Zn atoms are not 
likely to dissolve in Mn matrix, they tend to segregate at the α -Mg/Mn 
interface where has a higher diffusivity path. Although their contents 
are relatively low, their influences are non-negligible due to the large 
number of α -Mn nanoparticles in the GYZ-1Mn and GYZ-2Mn alloys.

could decrease the SFE and promote the formation of LPSO phase 
[ 20 – 22 ]. Secondly, Mn addition can also facilitate the precipitation of

In the GYZ-1Mn alloy, the RE and Zn atoms diffuse from the grain 
boundaries into the Mg matrix during homogenization, which tend to 
segregate periodically on the stacking faults in the grain interiors and 
form the fine lamellar LPSO phase [ 26 ]. In GYZ-2Mn alloy, the SFE 
decreased more significantly due to more α -Mn nanoparticles, thus more 
lamellar phase segregated and grew together to generate coarse lamellar 
LPSO phase. Besides, the Mg

alloy, which were frequently precipitated during thermo-mechanical

leads to an extension strain with the increase of Mn [ 29 ]. Therefore, the 
Gd/Y atoms with larger atomic radii are probably easily segregated to 
form the Mg

The formation of LPSO phase is affected by RE/Zn segregation, 
which in turn affected the DRX behavior during extrusion. Although 
α -Mn nanoparticles could retard the DRX behavior and delay the grain 
growth during extrusion [ 30 , 31 ], higher density of α -Mn nanoparticles 
in GYZ-2Mn alloy did not result in finer DRXed grains. The GYZ-1Mn 
alloy has profuse finer lamellar LPSO phase, which is effective in 
retarding the DRX behavior [ 32 ]. Contrarily, the GYZ-2Mn alloy has 
plentiful blocky and coarser lamellar LPSO phase, which promoted the 
DRX behavior by particle-stimulated nucleation (PSN) mechanism 
[ 30 – 32 ]. The GYZ-0.5Mn alloy has less Mn nanoparticles and less 
lamellar LPSO phase to inhibit the DRX behavior, but it also contains few 
coarse blocky LPSO phase to facilitate the DRX process. Hence, the 
GYZ-0.5Mn and GYZ-2Mn alloys show similar DRX behavior during 
extrusion. The crystal orientations changed from < 0001 > //ED to 
typical fiber texture. The < 0001 > //ED texture in RE-containing Mg 
alloys was constantly resulted from the activation of non-basal slip [ 33 ], 
whereas the fiber texture was rooted in the activation of basal slip [ 34 ].

The excellent heat-resistant performance of GYZ-1Mn alloy is closely 
related to the segregation of RE/Zn atoms on the α -Mn nanoparticles. 
The α -Mn nanoparticles have excellent thermal stability, which are 
capable of inhibiting the dislocation motions at elevated temperatures 
by Orowan strengthening [ 37 , 38 ]. The segregation of RE/Zn elements 
on the α -Mn nanoparticles is also beneficial to retard the particle 
coarsening and increase the thermal stability by relieving the strain, 
lowering the interfacial energy and decreasing the diffusion rate of 
atoms in nanoparticles. Meng et al. [ 39 ] also claimed that the 
co-segregation of Zn and Ca at the interface of Mn/matrix gave a higher 
thermal stability to the Mn precipitates by hindering their rapid growth 
at high temperature. 
Nevertheless, more α -Mn nanoparticles did not lead to further 
strengthening of mechanical properties in GYZ-2Mn alloy, because other 
factors also affect the mechanical properties. GYZ-1Mn alloy has the 
smallest DRXed grains, which are beneficial to enhance the mechanical 
properties by grain boundary strengthening [ 40 ]. The fracture micro -
structures (Fig. S3) also verify that the fine DRXed grains with more 
grain boundaries are beneficial to the mechanical properties at 300 ℃ 
and below. Since the deformation mechanisms of the GYZ-1Mn alloy are 
similar at RT and 250 ℃ (Fig. S3), barely no decline of UTS, TYS and 
elongation is observed at 250 
◦
C compared to those at RT. The GYZ-1Mn 
alloy also shows a high intensity of < 01 10 > //ED texture, which acts as 
hard orientation to resist basal and prismatic slips during deformation. 
Meanwhile, the unDRXed grains contain the highest density of GNDs, 
which are propitious to inhibit the subsequent dislocation movements 
resulting from the dislocation strengthening. Nevertheless, GYZ-2Mn 
alloy has coarser grains, weaker fiber texture and less density of GNDs 
than GYZ-1Mn alloy. Moreover, the additional formation of Mg
5
Gd 
particles at grain boundaries are not strong enough to reinforce the 
boundaries, they tend to deform via twinning and thus decreasing the 
boundary strength [18] . 
Our work provides a new strategy to develop heat-resistant Mg alloy 
by introducing appropriate elemental segregation on the metal particles. 
A heat-resistant Mg-8Gd-2Y-1.5Zn-1Mn alloy with ultra-high mechani -
cal properties at high temperatures was successfully developed by 
tailoring the RE/Zn segregation on α -Mn nanoparticles. Due to the 
segregation of RE/Zn atoms, the formation of fine lamellar LPSO phase 
was promoted in GYZ-1Mn alloy. Thus, the DRXed behavior was 
retarded, the grains was refined, the GNDs density was increased, and 
the texture intensity was aggrandized. Consequently, the GYZ-1Mn alloy 
showed outstanding mechanical properties at high temperatures.
