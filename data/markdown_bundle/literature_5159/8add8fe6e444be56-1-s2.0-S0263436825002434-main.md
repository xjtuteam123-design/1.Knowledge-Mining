ABSTRACT
High-performance W-based alloys, characterized by exceptional strength and ductility, are highly sought after for 
a variety of critical applications, including kinetic energy penetrators. However, achieving a significant increase 
in the strength of these alloys without severely compromising their tensile ductility remains a considerable 
challenge. In this study, we propose a Nb micro-alloying strategy to enhance the strength-ductility synergy in W- 
Ni-Fe alloys. The microstructure of the alloys was characterized by X-ray diffraction (XRD), scanning electron 
microscopy (SEM), and transmission electron microscopy (TEM), while the mechanical properties were evalu -
ated through tensile testing. This study investigates the effects of Nb content on the microstructure evolution, 
interfacial bonding characteristics, and quasi-static tensile behavior of tungsten heavy alloys (WHAs). The results 
demonstrate that the addition of 0.5 wt% Nb facilitates robust interfacial bonding of the W/matrix phase and 
reduces the sintering temperature of the alloy system, leading to structural refinement and subsequent im -
provements in both strength and ductility. The 90 W-0.5 wt% Nb alloy exhibited a 10 % increase in tensile 
strength and a 250 % increase in elongation, while no significant reduction in relative density was observed. The 
enhanced mechanical properties of the Nb-doped WHAs are attributed to fine-grained strengthening, solid- 
solution effects, and improved interfacial bonding strength. Furthermore, the well-bonded W/matrix interface 
and fine-grained microstructure play a critical role in inhibiting crack propagation, which is essential for 
achieving high performance. This study provides new insights into enhancing the mechanical performance of 
tungsten-based alloys and broadens the potential engineering applications of W-Ni-Fe alloys.
1. Introduction
Tungsten heavy alloys (WHAs), particularly W-Ni-Fe alloys, are dual- 
phase composite materials composed of a hard phase (tungsten parti -
cles) and a soft matrix phase ( γ -(Ni, Fe, W) [ 1 – 4 ]. WHAs exhibit high 
density, good strength, high hardness, a low coefficient of thermal 
expansion, and strong X-ray absorption capacity, making them exten -
sively utilized in aerospace, defense, nuclear, and medical industries 
[ 5 – 8 ]. The demanding service environments require WHAs to demon -
strate exceptional toughness in addition to elevated strength. The 
inherent brittleness of W and the relatively low strength of the matrix 
phase significantly limit the enhancement of the mechanical perfor -
mance of WHAs. The relationship between microstructure features and 
mechanical properties necessitates microstructural modifications to 
meet the performance requirements for diverse applications [ 9 ]. 
Consequently, the synergistic enhancement of strength and ductility has 
become a critical factor in the development of WHAs.
Previous studies on WHAs have demonstrated that the uniform 
introduction of fine dispersoid particles can effectively control W grain 
growth and reduce W solubility in the matrix phase [ 10 – 12 ]. Numerous 
studies have investigated the incorporation of Y

various critical applications, as demonstrated by studies on the effects of 
Re addition on the hardness of W-spheroids [ 17 ]. Overall, the afore -

Several studies have demonstrated that the mechanical properties of 
WHAs can be further enhanced through the addition of alloying ele -
ments [ 18 – 21 ]. Re has been shown to enhance the ductility of W par -
ticles and refine the W grain size in WHAs, thereby improving the overall 
strength of the alloy. However, the incorporation of Re reduces the 
interfacial bonding strength between W particles and the matrix phase, 
resulting in reduced ductility in the alloys [ 18 ]. The addition of Co to the 
γ -(Ni, Fe) matrix enhances the solubility of W and strengthens interfacial 
bonding, although it has a limited impact on the overall strength of the 
alloy [ 19 , 20 ]. Similar to findings in high-entropy alloys (HEAs) and Ni- 
based superalloys, Nb has been proposed as an effective alloying 
element for improving the mechanical properties of WHAs. Singh et al. 
[ 21 ] fabricated Nb-doped WHAs using the spark plasma sintering (SPS) 
technique to investigate the effects of Nb addition on microstructure 
evolution and mechanical performance. Additionally, 84 W-7 Ni-3 Fe-6 
Nb alloys subjected to a persistent liquid-phase sintering process exhibit 
outstanding hardness. Therefore, modifying W particles, enhancing the 
matrix phase, and refining the grain structure present significant chal -
lenges and are essential for simultaneously improving the strength and 
ductility of WHAs [ 22 ].
In this study, Nb was selected as the alloying element for the syn -
thesized W-Ni-Fe-Nb alloys due to its strong chemical affinity with the 
Ni matrix and its ability to form substitutional solid solutions in the 
matrix phase. After micro-alloying W, Ni, and Fe with Nb, the W-Ni-Fe- 
Nb alloys were fabricated using a powder metallurgy process. This study 
investigates the effects of Nb content on the microstructure evolution, 
interfacial bonding characteristics, and tensile properties of WHAs. The 
relationships between tensile strength, ductility, and microstructure 
were established. Furthermore, the effects of Nb micro-alloying on 
plastic deformation and fracture behavior were analyzed to elucidate 
the intrinsic mechanisms responsible for the strengthening and tough -
ening of the alloys. In particular, first-principles calculations and ther -
modynamic analyses were conducted to provide a detailed 
understanding of the strengthening mechanisms in Nb-doped WHAs.
2. Experiment materials and methods
2.1. Material preparation
In this study, elemental powders of W (Xiamen Tungsten Corpora -
tion, purity ≥ 99.98 %, 3 – 5 μ m), Ni (Shanxi BoNa, purity ≥ 99.50 %, 6 
μ m), Fe (Shanxi BoNa, purity ≥ 99.99 %, 5 μ m), and Nb (Shanxi Shihao, 
purity ≥ 99.99 %, 3 – 5 μ m) were used as the starting materials.
Nb was employed as the strengthening agent in the 90 W-7 Ni-3 Fe

Fig. 3 presents the XRD patterns of WHAs with varying Nb contents. 
The diffraction peaks corresponding to the W phase, which exhibits a 
body-centered cubic (BCC) structure, and the γ -(Ni, Fe) phase, which 
possesses a face-centered cubic (FCC) structure, were observed in all 
sintered alloys; however, niobium oxides and the rod-like precipitate 
phase remained undetectable due to their low concentrations. Notably, 
the diffraction peak of the γ -(Ni, Fe) phase showed a slight shift to lower 
angles with increasing Nb content, attributable to the solid-solution ef -
fect of Nb. EDS analysis revealed that Nb predominantly resided in the 
γ -(Ni, Fe) phase, with its solubility increasing as the Nb content rose. The 
atomic radius of Nb (198 pm) exceeds that of Ni (149 pm), Fe (156 pm), 
and W (193 pm). The incorporation of Nb atoms increased the lattice 
constant of the γ -(Ni, Fe) phase, causing the diffraction peaks to shift to 
lower angles [ 30 ]. Furthermore, the intensity of the diffraction peaks of 
the γ -(Ni, Fe) phase decreased in WHAs with Nb contents of 1.0 and 1.5 
wt%, possibly due to the presence of undetected phases within the 
matrix phase.

Compared to the 90 W alloy, Nb is uniformly distributed within the 
γ -(Ni, Fe) phase of the 90 W-0.5 Nb alloy ( Fig. 4 (b)), with no notable 
regions of elemental enrichment observed. The presence of Nb is evident 
in the 90 W-1.0 Nb and 90 W-1.5 Nb alloys, as shown in Figs. 4 (c) and 4

the elemental distribution maps of Nb and Ni in Fig. 4 (d) reveal the 
presence of (Nb, Ni)-rich regions, corresponding to the rod-like precip -

3.3. Mechanical properties
Fig. 8 (a) presents the tensile stress-strain curves for all as-sintered 
WHAs. The tensile strength and elongation of these alloys initially in -
crease and then decrease with increasing Nb content. The detailed me -
chanical properties are depicted in Fig. 8 (b). The 90 W alloy, fabricated 
via a solid-state sintering process, exhibited a tensile strength of 847 
MPa and an elongation of 10 %.
Among the Nb-containing alloys, the 90 W-1.0 Nb alloy exhibited the 
highest tensile strength of 932 MPa, whereas the 90 W-1.5 Nb alloy 
displayed the lowest tensile strength of 642 MPa and an elongation of 
nearly 0 %, indicative of brittle failure. The 90 W-0.5 Nb alloy exhibited 
an optimal balance of strength and ductility, with a tensile strength of 
924 MPa and an elongation of 35 %. The tensile results demonstrate that 
the incorporation of trace amounts of Nb provides both strengthening 
and ductilizing effects; however, higher Nb contents lead to a substantial 
reduction in strength and ductility.
3.4. Fracture morphology
Fig. 9 depicts the characteristic fracture morphologies of as-sintered 
WHAs following tensile testing. In the 90 W alloy ( Fig. 9 (a)), extensive 
W intergranular failure (W-W) and matrix tearing, exhibiting a discon -
tinuous island-like distribution, were observed on the fracture surface 
( Fig. 9 (a1)), consistent with the typical fracture mode of solid-state 
sintered WHAs. In the 90 W-0.5 Nb alloy ( Fig. 9 (b)), extensive W 
cleavage and an intact γ -(Ni, Fe, Nb) honeycomb structure were 
observed on the fracture surface ( Fig. 9 (b1)). W cleavage and matrix 
tearing are typically indicative of high strength and substantial ductility 
in WHAs [ 31 ]. In the 90 W-1.0 Nb alloy ( Fig. 9 (c)), numerous W 
cleavage facets are evident, accompanied by Nb ₂ O ₅ particles located 
between the W particles (indicated by green arrows in Fig. 9 (c1)). SEM 
( Figs. 2 (c) and 2 (d)) and TEM ( Fig. 7 (a)) characterizations demonstrate 
that Nb ₂ O ₅ particles predominantly reside within the matrix phase and 
act as crack initiation sites. The presence of oxide particles limits the 
plastic deformation capacity of the matrix phase during tensile testing, 
leading to a significant reduction in alloy elongation [ 32 ]. The fracture 
mode of the 90 W-1.5 Nb alloy ( Fig. 9 (d)) is characterized by W/matrix 
(W/M) interfacial debonding and W cleavage, with W/M debonding 
predominating ( Fig. 9 (d1)). High-magnification imaging of the fracture 
surface in Fig. 9 (d2) reveals the presence of NbNi ₃ distributed across the 
W particle surfaces. This suggests that these precipitates significantly 
degrade the overall mechanical properties of the 90 W-1.5 Nb alloy.
4. Discussion
The incorporation of Nb into the 90 W alloy enhances both the 
strength and ductility of the 90 W-0.5 Nb alloy simultaneously. SEM 
observations ( Fig. 2 ) reveal that the addition of Nb modifies the sin -
tering behavior of the 90 W alloy, benefiting to a lowering of the solidus 
temperature. Excessive Nb addition led to the formation of the NbNi ₃ 
intermetallic phase, significantly degrading the mechanical properties of 
WHAs. The underlying mechanisms are elaborated below.

Solid-solution strengthening induces significant lattice distortion 
and increases resistance to dislocation motion, thereby enhancing alloy 
strength [ 19 ]. Rupert et al. [ 47 ]reported that the modulus of pure Ni 
increases proportionally with W content, attributed to the rigid bonds of 
BCC W. Furthermore, as sintering temperature increases, more W and 
Nb atoms diffuse into the γ -(Ni, Fe) solid solution, leading to the sub -
stitution of specific sublattice sites in the matrix by solute atoms. This 
substitution causes lattice distortion by interacting with dislocations. 
The presence of W and Nb atoms modifies the elastic energy of dislo -
cations by altering local atomic size and modulus, thus impeding 
dislocation motion and improving the mechanical properties of WHAs 
[ 48 ].
The impact of Nb alloying on interfacial bonding properties was 
investigated using first-principles calculations, with the W(110)/ γ -(Ni, 
Fe)(111) phase interface selected as a representative model for in -
terfaces in the W-Ni-Fe system. Two W(110)/matrix(111) interface 
configurations with distinct stacking sequences were examined, as 
depicted in Figs. 11 (a) and (b). Notably, the charge density difference 
results, as shown in Figs. 11 (c) and 11 (d), reveal substantial charge 
transfer at the interface among W, Nb, and Ni atoms. Incorporating Nb 
atoms at the interface reduces the availability of free electrons, facili -
tating the formation of chemical bonds with neighboring atoms and 
enhancing interfacial interaction forces. In the W(110)/matrix(111) 
configuration, a higher concentration of Nb atoms near the interface 
increases the number of chemical bonds formed in this region, thereby 
strengthening interfacial bonding [ 49 ]. Furthermore, a pronounced 
hybridization peak between Ni-3d and Nb-4d states from 0 to 5 eV likely 
contributes to the beneficial effect of Nb on the interfacial strength of the 
W/matrix interface, consistent with the charge density results above 
[ 50 ]. This likely enhances the bonding strength of the W/matrix inter -
face, thereby improving the mechanical properties of Nb-added WHAs.
4.4. Fracture behavior analysis
The four alloys exhibit substantial microstructural variations, and 
the aforementioned findings suggest that plastic deformation during 
crack propagation is significantly reduced compared to that during crack 
initiation. Increasing Nb content reduces W-W contiguity in Nb-added 
WHAs, whereas the 90 W alloy displays the highest W-W contiguity. 
The W-W interface constitutes the most susceptible region, where 
microcracks are readily initiated and propagated under minimal loading 
[ 31 ]. The dominant failure pathway propagates along the weakest W/W 
interface ( Fig. 12 ).

5. Conclusion
In this study, fine-grained WHAs with a minor addition of Nb 
element have been successfully fabricated. The evolution of micro -
structure, mechanical properties, strengthening mechanisms, and frac -
ture behavior of WHAs was systematically analyzed. The primary 
conclusions are summarized as follows: 
(1) The microstructural analysis demonstrated that the addition of 
Nb effectively reduced the liquidus temperature of the 90 W-7 Ni- 
3 Fe alloy and promoted densification at lower temperatures, 
resulting in a fine-grained structure. TEM results revealed that 
the γ-(Ni, Fe, Nb) phase exhibited strong bonding with W grains; 
however, excessive Nb addition led to the formation of IMCs and 
oxides at the W/matrix interface, promoting crack initiation 
during deformation.
(2) The 90 W-0.5 Nb alloy demonstrates superior mechanical prop -
erties, with an ultimate tensile strength of 924 MPa and a total 
elongation of up to 35 %, which is attributed to fine-grained and 
solid-solution strengthening mechanisms. HR-TEM results reveal 
that the well-bonded W/matrix interface possesses high bonding 
strength and effectively facilitates stress transfer. Moreover, first- 
principles calculations demonstrated that the dual-phase inter -
face of the 90 W-Nb alloy exhibits minimal lattice distortion and 
significant charge transfer, indicating excellent interfacial 
bonding strength.
(3) In the crack propagation region, the 90 W alloy shows flat crack 
expansion paths. In addition, no obvious plastic deformation 
features were observed in the crack propagation, indicating that 
the crack propagation energy of the three alloys is rather low. 
However, the cleavage failure of W grains and the rupture of the 
ductile matrix are the primary fracture modes in the 90 W-0.5 Nb 
alloy, which are closely related to the Nb content in the alloy.
(4) The current research findings indicate the feasibility of designing 
WHAs that exhibit a combination of high strength and significant 
plasticity through microstructure refinement and solid-solution 
strengthening mechanisms. This approach offers new insights 
into the strengthening and ductilizing of refractory metals by 
introducing substitutional solid-solution elements.
