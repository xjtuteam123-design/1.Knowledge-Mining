Refractory complex concentrated alloys (RCCAs) have attractive 
properties for high temperature applications [ 1 , 2 ]. MoNbTaW was one 
of the first RCCAs whose microstructure and compressive yield stress 
versus temperature were measured experimentally; it is a single-phase 
body centered cubic ( bcc ) solid solution that retains its strength up to 
1600 
◦
C [ 3 ]. However, its room temperature brittleness and 
elevated-temperature embrittlement due to grain boundary oxides 
significantly hinder its potential for high-temperature applications [ 3 ,
4 ]. In contrast, bcc RCCAs containing a significant amount of hexagonal 
elements (Ti, Zr, Hf), such as the Senkov alloy (HfNbTaTiZr), achieve 
good tensile ductility at room temperature but are lacking in high 
temperature strength [ 5 ]. Recently, the Nb
45
Ta
25
Ti
15

has been reported to possess extremely high fracture toughness for bcc 
alloys at room temperature as well as excellent tensile ductility [ 6 ]. 
However, NTTH loses tensile yield strength significantly at elevated 
temperatures: σ

= 341 MPa at 950 
◦
C and σ
y 
= 140 MPa at 1200 
◦

In addition, high temperature dislocation and diffusion processes limit 
creep strength of metallic alloys significantly below their yield strength 
[ 7 ]. However, in precipitation strengthened materials like superalloys, 
dislocation glide processes do not significantly decrease creep strength 
below yield strength since precipitation strengthening is relatively strain 
rate independent [ 8 ]. In contrast, solid solution strengthening can be 
strongly strain rate-dependent [ 9 – 12 ]. As a result, in solid solution 
strengthened materials like nominally single-phase bcc RCCAs, ther -
mally activated dislocation glide processes can decrease their creep 
strength relative to yield strength significantly. This means that for a 
particular yield strength, precipitation strengthened materials are 
stronger in creep as compared to solid solution strengthened materials 
[ 13 ]. These hypotheses highlight the necessity for a systematic under -
standing of elevated temperature dislocation-mediated deformation 
mechanisms, as well as the strain rate dependence of creep strength in 
bcc single-phase RCCAs. This understanding is essential for developing 
physics-based models for more complex, precipitation-strengthened 
RCCAs.

solid solution strengthening (SSS) that arises from: a) the stress to move 
kinks across solute obstacles at the screw dislocation core, and b) stress

kink-kink collisions and the screw dislocation drags the jogs as it moves 
[ 11 ]. In this high-temperature model, the dipole dragging stress from 
the low temperature model is replaced by the jog dragging stress.

or jog height is fairly high, ~ 10 nm (~30 b ). At this height, the dipole
