ABSTRACT
In this work, an oxide-dispersion strengthened (ODS) FeCrNi medium entropy alloy (MEA) was prepared by low- 
energy ball milling and laser powder bed fusion (PBF-LB/M). The ODS FeCrNi MEA shows remarkable room- 
temperature tensile properties with yield strength of 878 MPa, ultimate tensile strength of 1070 MPa, and 
elongation of 30 %. Meanwhile, its high-temperature strength at 400 
◦
C, 600 
◦
C, and 700 
◦
C exceeds many 
typical ODS alloys, such as PM 2000 and ODS 316. The ultra-fine cellular structures and uniformly dispersed 
nano-Y
2
O
3 
particles in the PBF-LB/M ODS FeCrNi MEA are the main reasons for its high strength. Additionally, 
the FCC-matrix with a high deformation capacity and the semi-coherent interface relationship between the FCC- 
matrix and the nano-Y
2
O
3 
particles ensure adequate plasticity.
1. Introduction
Medium/high entropy alloys (M/HEAs) are new metallic materials 
composed of multi-element elements in equimolar or near-equimolar 
ratios, which have become important candidates for structural mate -
rials in the next-generation nuclear reactors due to their high strength, 
fracture toughness, and irradiation resistance [ 1 – 4 ]. However, low 
high-temperature strength is one of the main reasons limiting the 
application of M/HEAs [ 5 ]. Introducing stable nano-oxides, such as 
Y
2
O
3 
and Al
2
O
3
, to hinder the dislocation motion is considered an 
effective way to enhance the strength [ 6 , 7 ]. Powder metallurgy (PM) is a 
common preparation method for oxide-dispersion strengthened (ODS) 
M/HEAs. This method involves complex processes such as mechanical 
alloying, hot extrusion, cold-working, and heat treatment, making it 
difficult to prepare complex parts [ 1 ]. Therefore, it is an urgent need to 
develop new integrated forming techniques for the ODS M/HEAs.
Laser powder bed fusion (PBF-LB/M) is a commonly used metal 
additive manufacturing (AM) technique that enables near-net-shaping 
of complex parts [ 8 ]. With the advantages of high forming efficiency, 
high shape freedom, and good surface quality, PBF-LB/M technology has 
been used to prepare ODS M/HEAs [ 8 – 10 ]. Mechanical alloying is the 
main method to prepare ODS M/HEA powders, but it is difficult to meet 
the PBF-LB/M requirement due to the repeated crushing and welding 
process that reduces the sphericity and flowability of the powders. 
In-situ formation of oxide particles by introducing oxygen during the AM 
process is an alternative method to prepare ODS M/HEAs [ 8 – 10 ]. 
However, the relatively low concentration of oxygen introduced by this 
method makes it difficult to precipitate high-density oxide particles in 
the matrix alloy, so the strengthening effect is limited [ 8 – 10 ].
In this work, a short-time and low-energy ball milling method was 
performed to coat the FeCrNi MEA powders with nano-Y
2
O
3 
powders to 
introduce a high O content and eliminate the influence of the mechan -
ical alloying on the powder sphericity. The surface-coated powders were 
then subjected to PBF-LB/M to induce the formation of ultra-fine cellular 
structures and in-situ precipitation of nano-oxides. The microstructure 
in the PBF-LB/M ODS FeCrNi MEA was characterized, and the effect of 
the cellular structures and nano-oxides on the mechanical properties 
was analyzed. This finding aims to provide a new method to prepare 
high-performance ODS materials.

2. Materials and methods
The gas-atomized FeCrNi powder and nano-Y
2
O
3 
powder (~50 nm) 
were used as raw materials, as shown in Fig. 1 a and b. A low-energy 
planetary ball milling at 150 rpm for 5 h was used to coat the Y
2
O
3 
powder (0.5 wt%) on the surface of the FeCrNi powder, and the as- 
milled powder is provided in Fig. 1 c. The as-milled powder remains 
good sphericity, and the mean particle size increases from 17.9 μ m of 
FeCrNi to 28.8 μ m ( Fig. 1 d). The Y and O elements are uniformly 
distributed on the powder surfaces ( Fig. 1 e). The PBF-LB/M experiment 
for the ODS FeCrNi MEA was carried out on an FS121M machine with 
the following parameters: laser radius of 50 μ m, scanning speed of 800 
mm/s, laser power of 300 W, layer thickness of 0.05 mm, and hatching 
space of 0.09 mm. The PBF-LB/M bulks were then annealed at 400 
◦
C for 
3 h to release the thermal stress. The contents of metallic and non- 
metallic elements in the as-milled powder and the as-printed bulk 
were measured by an inductively coupled plasma mass spectroscopy 
(ICP-MS) and an instrumental gas analysis (IGA), respectively, and the 
results are listed in Table 1 . The as-printed bulk shows similar compo -
sition to the as-milled powder, indicating that there is almost no element 
loss in the PBF-LB/M process.
