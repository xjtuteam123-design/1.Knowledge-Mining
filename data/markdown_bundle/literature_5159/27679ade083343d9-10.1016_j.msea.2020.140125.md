ABSTRACT 
In this work, dynamic compression experiments are carried-out at both room temperature (RT = 298 K) and 
liquid nitrogen temperature (LNT = 77 K) to systematically investigate the dynamic mechanical responses of the 
Al
0 ⋅ 1
CoCrFeNi high entropy alloy (HEA). The yield strength significantly increases with the increasing strain rate. 
Even higher yield strengths are revealed when the temperature decreases from 298 K to 77 K. At cryogenic 
temperature, abundant nanoscale deformation twins are observed due to lower stacking fault energy (SFE). By 
introducing new interfaces in the grains and decreasing the mean free path of dislocations during deformation, 
the high density deformation twins that formed during dynamic deformation at 77 K lead to a higher degree of 
strain hardening rate. In addition, the low SFE results in widely dissociations and reactions of dislocations, which 
lead to the formation of Lomer-Cottrell (L-C) and Lomer dislocation locks. These immobile dislocation locks can 
act as both dislocation sources and barriers, which can also contribute to the outstanding strain hardening 
capability of the Al
0 ⋅ 1
CoCrFeNi HEA upon dynamic deformation at 77 K. 
1. Introduction 
Exploitation of resources and the exploration of unknown fields have 
demanded novel materials with excellent mechanical properties, which 
should meet the rigorous requirements of applications in extreme con -
ditions, such as liquefied gas storage at cryogenic temperature and dy -
namic impacts in aeronautical/astrospace industries, etc. For 
conventional metallic materials, a severe deterioration in ductility and 
toughness under cryogenic temperature environments has been reported 
in previous literatures [ 1 , 2 ]. Meanwhile, localized shearing instability 
often happens in metallic materials during dynamic impacting, which 
causes a sudden failure due to the rapid propagation of cracks inside the 
shear bands [ 3 – 6 ]. Therefore, it is a long-term challenge for the people 
to pursue the advanced structure materials with extraordinary 
strength-ductility synergy at some extreme application conditions. 
Until recently, a new class of alloys consisted of multiple principal 
elements are synthesized, whose complex compositions lead to a high 
configurational entropy ( > 1.36 R , R is the gas constant), on this account 
these alloys are named as high entropy alloys (HEAs) [ 7 ]. Such high 
entropy effect promotes the formation of simple solid-solution phase 
with single lattice structure in the HEAs, such as face-centered cubic 
(FCC), body-centered cubic (BCC), or hexagonal close-packed (HCP) 
crystal structures [ 7 ]. Moreover, the randomly distributed multiple el -
ements can cause severely distorted crystalline lattice and retarded 
atomic diffusion [ 8 ]. Due to these special structural characteristics, 
HEAs have been reported to exhibit many excellent mechanical and 
physical properties, including extraordinary fracture toughness, ultra -
high strength, exceptional ductility, significant resistance to corrosion, 
superconductivity and so on [ 8 – 13 ]. Furthermore, fruitful strategies, 
such as grain refinement [ 14 – 16 ], metastable phase-transformation

Except for the strong effect of temperature on mechanical properties 
of FCC based HEAs, it should be noted that the strain rate may also have 
a great effect on mechanical properties. During high strain rate loading, 
metallic materials may display some distinct behaviors, such as the 
localized shearing instability, which are different from those during 
static or quasi-static conditions [ 37 , 38 ]. Furthermore, because of the 
viscous drag effect, the dislocation motions are also quite different under 
dynamic conditions [ 38 ]. There are sporadical but valuable reports on 
the dynamic responses of FCC HEAs, which lay a good foundation for the 
applications on the dynamic impacting conditions, such as the acces -

strain rate tension originate from the significant strain rate sensitivity 
caused by the phonon drag effect as well as the presence of short-range 
orders and the extraordinary strain hardening capacity that caused by 
the dynamically formed deformation twins [ 44 ]. 
Despite these considerable efforts on FCC HEAs, very limited re -
searches have been reported so far on the mechanical responses at some 
coupling extreme conditions, such as high strain rate coupled with a 
cryogenic temperature, in particular, the underlying deformation 
mechanisms still remain largely uncertain. Therefore, the mechanical 
properties as well as the underlying deformation mechanisms upon 
dynamic loading at cryogenic temperature should be carefully investi -
gated because they are important indexes for the cryogenic applications. 
As a promising structural metallic materials, Al
0 ⋅ 1
CoCrFeNi high entropy

cryogenic temperatures [ 36 , 45 , 46 ]. Li et al. reported that the stacking

The low SFE leads to extensive dissociation and reaction of dislocations, 
which results in the formation of immobile Lomer-Cottrell (L-C) and

mulate dislocations [ 46 ]. In this study, we choose Al

results, it is shown that with the decreasing temperature, the transition 
of predominant deformation mechanisms causes much better hardening 
ability and plasticity. In the following sections, the deformation be -
haviors of this HEA under extreme loading conditions will be discussed 
in details according to the intensive observations on the microstructural 
evolution.

microstructure data after deformation that presented below and litera -
tures [ 37 , 41 ], such three different regions can be interpreted as follows. 
In region A, dislocation slip dominates the plastic deformation, the 
strain hardening rate decreases rapidly due to the increasing dislocation 
annihilation rate after dislocation initiated. Then the strain hardening 
rate gradually decreases due to the effect of tangled dislocations in re -
gion B. Thereafter, twining as an addition mechanism result in less 
decrease in the strain hardening rate in region C [ 37 ]. It is noteworthy 
that the formation of twins in region C does not lead to the increasing 
strain hardening rate as reported in the literature, which may be the 
result of the insufficient number of twins and the effect of thermal 
softening [ 37 , 50 ]. For dynamic deformation at 77 K, the curve of strain 
hardening rate versus true strain also decreases rapidly in region A 
because of the increasing rate of dislocation annihilation. However, with 
the increasing strain, the curve gradually increases in region B, which 
may attribute to the massive formation of twins, i.e., twining is a 
dominant mechanism in region B. In region C, the curve decreases again 
maybe owing to a decrease rate of twining evolution and thermal soft -

deformation of HEAs under quasi-static conditions [ 33 , 34 ].

Namely, there are two regions for YS with respect to strain rate, ther -
mally activated dislocation glide region and phonon drag affected 
dislocation motions region. Under quasi-static condition, the velocity of 
dislocations is so slow that the phonon drag effect can be ignored. Based 
on thermal activation dislocation motion analysis, increasing strain rate 
will lead to the less available time for dislocation to overcome obstacles 
so that the thermal activated energy will be less effective [ 37 , 38 , 41 , 50 ]. 
Accordingly, higher load is required for dislocation to overcome obsta -
cles. Under dynamic condition, however, dislocation velocity is much 
faster than that of quasi-static condition. The fast moving dislocations 
will cause phonons scattering and result in the increase viscosity of 
dislocation movement. Besides, the fast moving dislocations will 
generate a large amount of heat and lead to the local temperature 
changes [ 37 , 41 ]. This will cause an irreversible heat flow and depletes 
the dislocation energy. Therefore, the dynamic dislocation motions are 
subjected to viscous drag and lead to much higher strain rate sensitivity 
than those of quasi-static conditions.

up into a stair rod partial dislocation and a glissile partial dislocation 
that parallels to the twin boundary [ 58 ]. The glissile partial dislocation

boundary [ 58 ]. Fig. 7 (e) shows higher magnified inverse fast Fourier 
transformation (IFFT) image which exhibits a clear structure of SF

NiCoCr alloy recently [ 57 – 59 ]. Miao et al. found that the Shockley

of new partial dislocations on the twin boundary [ 59 ]. Therefore, the 
original coherent twin boundary will transform into a 3-layer HCP 
structure. Previous work has suggested that this kind of composite 
structures can be effective barriers for dislocation slip and contribute to 
a higher strain hardening rate [ 59 ]. Fig. 7 (d) shows the magnified 
one-dimensional IFFT image of the corresponding region in Fig. 7 (a) 
(orange box). Abundant dislocations at twin boundary can be seen. This 
is also an evidence for the above conclusion. 
Extensive L-C locks are also observed in this alloy upon dynamic 
deformation at 77 K. As shown in Fig. 7 (f), a large number of SFs 
(marked by orange arrows) in different directions and V-shaped inter -
action of lattice defects (marked by red circle) can be observed. The 
SAED patterns inserted in Fig. 7 (f) confirm that these SFs are parallel to 
(11 1) and ( 11 1) planes. The higher magnification micrograph of dashed 
rectangular area inserted in Fig. 7 (f) shows the V-shaped interaction of 
lattice defects more clearly. This kind of V-shaped morphology that 
composed by two SFs in different directions is a typical feature of L-C 
lock [ 46 ]. These L-C locks are formed by the reaction of two Shockley 
partial dislocations from two different slip planes, as reported by Yan 
et al. [ 60 – 62 ]. Fig. 7 (g) shows the IFFT micrograph, in which a L-C lock 
and corresponding Shockley partial dislocations are confirmed. The 
Burgers circuits of those dislocations (marked by white T in Fig. 7 (g)) 
and corresponding perfect lattices that have crystallographically 
equivalent steps are drawn via the well-established methodology [ 63 ]. 
High density of dissociated dislocations exists on two different slip 
planes, again due to the low SFE of current HEA, which results in a high 
possibility for the formation of L-C locks. It has been reported that the 
reaction of two Shockley partial dislocations will produce an immobile

hence is immobile [ 46 ]. Therefore, L-C locks can play a critical role in

4. Summary and conclusions 
In this study, dynamic mechanical properties of the Al
0 ⋅ 1
CoCrFeNi 
HEA at different temperatures (298 K and 77 K) have been investigated. 
The strain hardening behavior and corresponding microstructural fea -
tures during compression deformation at each situation have been 
studied. The main conclusions are: 
1. Under the condition of high strain rate, the current Al
0 ⋅ 1
CoCrFeNi 
HEA shows not only higher flow stress but also better strain hard -
ening ability with the decreasing temperature from 298 K to 77 K. 
The extreme loading conditions of high strain rate coupled with a 
cryogenic temperature can promote the formation of an abundant 
nanoscale deformation twins, which can significantly increase the 
hardening ability of the material, making it an alternative material 
for applications in such extreme environments. 
2. The dominated deformation mechanisms during high strain rate 
deformation change from dislocation slips to a mixed deformation 
pattern consisting of dislocation glide and twinning when loading 
conditions change from 298 K to 77 K. The high density twin 
boundaries can act as effective interfaces for the dislocations storage, 
thus stimulating extraordinary strain hardening. 
3. Under dynamic deformation at 77 K, abundant immobile L-C and 
Lomer dislocation locks are also observed in current Al
0 ⋅ 1
CoCrFeNi 
HEA. The formation of these immobile dislocation locks can effec -
tively impede the dynamic recovery of dislocations that caused by 
the cross slip and simultaneously can act as both dislocation barriers 
and sources, which can contribute to the larger strain hardening rate 
in dynamic deformation at 77 K.
