Keywords: Inconel alloy; TiAl alloy; Bonding; Multilayer thin films; Intermetallics; Sputtering 
Abstract. Joining nickel based superalloys to gamma-TiAl intermetallic alloys will contribute to a 
more efficient application of these advanced materials, particularly in extreme environments. In this 
study, Inconel alloy and gamma-TiAl are joined using as filler alternated nanolayer thin films 
deposited onto each base material. The nanolayers consisted in Ni/Al exothermic reactive multilayer 
thin films with periods of 5 and 14 nm deposited by d.c. magnetron sputtering in order to improve 
the adhesion to the substrates and to avoid the reaction between Ni and Al. Diffusion bonding 
experiments with multilayer coated alloys were performed under vacuum at 800ºC by applying 50 
MPa during 1h. Bonding was achieved in large areas of the centre of the joints where regions 
without cracks or pores were produced, especially when using multilayer thin films with a 14 nm 
modulation period. 
Introduction 
Nickel based superalloys and gamma-TiAl intermetallic compounds can be used to manufacture 
parts of automotive and aeronautical engines and turbines, which must serve under elevated 
temperature and chemical aggressive environments. The ability to join these materials to 
themselves and to other materials opens up the possibility to produce more complex parts. In 
addition, the successful application of these alloys in large structural parts also requires adequate 
joining. Unfortunately, intermetallic compounds and Ni based superalloys are difficult to join by 
conventional techniques such as fusion welding processes [1]. 
The literature has clearly demonstrated the possibility of joining titanium aluminides by solid-
state diffusion bonding [2,3]. Although few researches have been reported on the diffusion bonding 
of Ni based superalloys, Z. Guoge et al. succeeded in joining Inconel 718 using this process [4]. As 
a solid-state joining process, diffusion bonding avoids undesirable phase transformations and 
eliminates the problems of segregation, solidification cracking, and distortion stresses usually found 
in liquid phase welding processes. However, high temperatures and/or pressures are required to 
achieve sound joints by diffusion bonding. In order to assist in the bonding process it is possible to 
introduce thin foils between the materials to be joined. A novel procedure developed by the authors 
consists of introducing temperature reactive multilayer thin films as interlayer material. The 
feasibility of this procedure has already been successfully demonstrated by diffusion bonding of 
gamma-TiAl parts using Ti/Al multilayer thin films with nanometric modulation period (bilayer 
thickness) [5]. High quality joints were achieved indicating that they are promising filler materials 
for diffusion bonding at lower temperatures and shorter holding times [6]. The reactive multilayer 
thin films are made up of alternating nanolayers that exothermically react with each other at well 
defined temperatures, lower than those occurring in thin foils, due to their nanocrystalline character. 
The aim of this study is to establish a new procedure for the joining of superalloys and 
intermetallics. The objective is to promote sound joining using reactive multilayer thin films. Ni 
based superalloys and gamma-TiAl based parts will be coated by magnetron sputtering with Ni/Al

After reaction using temperature and pressure an intermetallic compound with nanometric grain size 
will be formed and it must promote the bonding between the coated parts. 
According to previous results, after deposition the Ni/Al reactive multilayers consist in alternated 
nickel- and aluminium-rich layers with nanometric grain sizes where the interfaces are solid 
solutions of Ni and Al as a result of intermixing [7,8]. The importance of the interfaces is a function 
of the multilayer period, containing the short period multilayers a large portion of interfacial region 
where intermixing occurs. On the other hand, by increasing the period of the Ni/Al thin films, 
undesired reaction products are formed besides the B2-NiAl intermetallic [7]. Therefore, the 
multilayer period must be optimised in order to have B2-NiAl as the only reaction product (short 
periods), but also have a low content of solid solution (high periods). For the present study two 
periods were selected as the most promising, one of 5 nm and the other of 14 nm. 
Experimental Details 
Materials. The Ni based superalloy used in this investigation is Inconel alloy 718 (IN718) 
exhibiting an equiaxed microstructure, while the other base material is a gamma-TiAl based alloy 
with a duplex microstructure. To guarantee a good contact between the base materials, a surface 
finishing by microgrinding was employed. The chemical composition of the bulk materials to be 
joined measured by electron probe microanalysis is shown in Table 1.

Ni/Al multilayer thin films were deposited onto IN718 and gamma-TiAl materials by d.c. 
magnetron sputtering. Layered films with nanometric period were produced from two pure metallic 
targets (Al and Ni) operating simultaneously. The Ni and Al single layer thickness is a function of 
the time that the substrates under rotation are in front of each sputtering target. Different periods 
could be achieved by adjusting the substrates’ rotation speed. The Al target operated at 4.4 x 10 -2 W 
mm -2 and the nickel target power density was adjusted in order to have a chemical composition 
close to 50:50 at.%. The total multilayer thickness is close to 2.5 micron and periods of 5 and 14 nm 
were used. The bottom layer was always Al for improving adhesion while at the top a thicker Ni 
layer was deposited (50 nm) to prevent oxidation. 
Solid-state Diffusion Bonding. The Inconel/TiAl dissimilar joints were fabricated by solid-state 
diffusion bonding after stacking two coated samples. The diffusion bonding experiments with Ni/Al 
thin films were performed under a vacuum level of less than 10 -2 Pa in a tensile machine equipped 
with a furnace. The samples were heated up to the diffusion bonding temperature (800ºC), and held 
for 1 hour, under a pressure of 50 MPa. Heating and cooling rates of 10ºC min -1 were used. In order 
to perform the microstructural characterisation of the bond interface, cross-sections of the joints 
were prepared using standard metallographic techniques. The interfaces were examined by high 
resolution scanning electron microscopy (SEM), under atomic number contrast, and chemically 
analysed by energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) at an accelerating voltage of 15 keV. 
Results and Discussion 
The as-deposited thin films consisted in alternated Al- and Ni-rich layers with nanometric grain size 
as evidenced in the transmission electron microscopy image (TEM) of Fig 1. During heat treatment 
the Ni/Al multilayer thin films evolve toward equilibrium due to interdiffusion and chemical

during heat treatment the grain growth is promoted, the grain size is still nanometric or sub-

multilayer thin films react during the diffusion bonding process, the layered structure disappears and 
the Β2 -NiAl ordered compound is expected to be the dominant product.

To the authors knowledge the diffusion bonding between Inconel 718 and gamma-TiAl was 
never conducted. Moreover, considering the melting point of the base materials the diffusion 
bonding temperature would be much higher (above 900 ºC). In fact, usually the typical diffusion 
bonding temperature is 0.7 T 
m, where T m is the melting temperature of the base material [4]. 
Therefore, the presence of the Ni/Al thin films as interlayer material had a definite role in 
decreasing the diffusion bonding temperature allowing sound joints to be produced at 800ºC.
