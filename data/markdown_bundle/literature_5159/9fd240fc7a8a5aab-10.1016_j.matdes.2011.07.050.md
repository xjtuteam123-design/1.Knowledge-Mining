Investigation on the relationship between intermediate temperature
embrittlement and intergranular precipitate in Ni(Bi) alloy

abstract
Ni and Ni-based superalloys generally exhibit intermediate temperature embrittlement (ITE), which is
usually interpreted by the evolution of intergranular precipitates. In this paper, the relationship between
ITE and intergranular precipitates was investigated in a binary Ni(Bi) alloy. The tensile ductility of Ni(Bi)
alloy shows evident ITE with a maximum between 700 /C176C and 750 /C176C. The microstructure of the sample
heat treated at 750 /C176C was observed to be free of precipitates within grains and at grain boundaries. The
interpretation of intergranular precipitates for ITE was then discussed and proven to be invalid.

1. Introduction
Ni-based superalloys have become essential materials in the
design and manufacturing of the hottest parts of modern aircraft
turbo engines [1–7]. Many experiments [8–10] demonstrated that
Ni-based superalloys retain a common feature, ‘‘intermediate
temperature embrittlement’’ (ITE), which means that the maxi-
mum elongation (EL) or reduction in area (RA) in tensile test at
intermediate temperature is much lower than those at low and
high temperatures. Since superalloys are operated generally in
the corresponding temperature range, ITE poses threat on the reli-
ability of aircraft turbo engines.
In 1925, Merica and Waltenberg [11] had already attributed the
detrimental effect of S on the ductility of Ni to the formation of
Ni–Ni3S2 eutectic ﬁlm at grain boundaries. Since then, many
researchers [9,12–14] considered the ITE of Ni-based superalloys
to be caused by the evolution of intergranular precipitates. The
main idea of the interpretation of intergranular precipitates for
ITE is: at low temperature, only a few small precipitates exist on
grain boundaries, which has little effect on the initiation of a crack
and the ductility of alloys; at the intermediate temperature, inter-
granular precipitates become larger, thereby leading to easy initia-
tion of a crack and low ductility of alloys; at high temperature, the
ductility is restored owing to the dissolution of the precipitates.
However, several experiments demonstrated that commercially
pure and impurity added Ni alloys [15–17] also show evident ITE.
In this case, the interpretation of intergranular precipitates is
doubtful. The present work was therefore carried out to clarify
the validity of this interpretation by a binary Ni(Bi) alloy with
emphasis on the veriﬁcation of intergranular precipitates. The re-
sults and conclusions can provide essential bases to understand
the fundamental of ITE and help to improve the compositions of
Ni-based superalloys.
2. Experimental procedure
A Ni(Bi) alloy was produced by vacuum induction melting
through mixing high purity Ni with adequate amount of high pur-
ity Bi. The ingot of the Ni(Bi) alloy with 25 wt ppm Bi was then de-
formed into a bar of 4 mm diameter. Afterwards, this bar was heat-
treated by three steps (455 /C176C/1 h + 600 /C176C/1 h + 1000 /C176C/0.5 h),
and ﬁnally quenched into iced water immediately. Tensile samples
with dimensions of 2 mm diameter and 6 mm gauge length were
cut from the quenched bar, as shown in Fig. 1 . Tensile tests were
performed in air by an INSTRON–1195 machine with the strain rate
of 10 /C02 s/C01 in the temperature range of 400–800 /C176C. All samples
were held at the corresponding temperature for 45 min prior to
tensile test. The elongation at fracture was used to determine the
ductility and six independent samples were used at each tempera-
ture to get a reasonable average.
The sample after tensile test at 750 /C176C was mechanically pol-
ished and the microstructure was observed by means of optical
microscopy (OM) (Keyence VHX-500F) and scanning electron
microscopy (SEM) (FEI Nova nano 230). In order to detect inter-
granular precipitates, a lamella with a grain boundary was pre-
pared by focused ion beam (FIB) (Zeiss 1540 EsB Crossbeam) lift
out technique for transmission electron microscopy (TEM) obser-
vation. It is well known that very dense dislocations will appear
in a sample after tensile test which hinders the observation of ﬁne
precipitates. Accordingly, the quenched bar followed by

3. Results
Fig. 3 presents the tensile ductility of the binary Ni(Bi) alloy. It is
clear that the ductility of Ni(Bi) alloy ﬁrst drops signiﬁcantly until
700 /C176C, and then increases rapidly above 750 /C176C. Thus, the Ni(Bi) al-
loy reveals evident ITE with maximum embrittlement between
700 /C176C and 750 /C176C. Fig. 4 exhibits the OM and SEM images of sam-
ples tensile tested at 750 /C176C. Fig. 4a is the OM images, which dem-
onstrates no large precipitates within grains and at grain
boundaries. Fig. 4 b is the SEM image, showing that there are no
intergranular precipitates in the size of submicrometers. Fig. 5 dis-
plays the TEM images of the lamella with emphasis on grain
boundary. Fig. 5a is the diffraction contrast image, which excludes
precipitates with the size in tens of nanometers. Fig. 5b presents a
high resolution electron microscopy (HREM) image, indicating that
the microstructure is completely free of even ﬁne precipitates.
4. Discussion
Fig. 5 shows the microstructure of the bar enduring the same
heat treatment as the samples of tensile tests at 750 /C176C. It proves
that there are no precipitates within grains and at grain boundaries
in the Ni(Bi) alloy. It is noteworthy that the tensile tests at 700 /C176C
and 750 /C176C took only about 30 s, which is much shorter than the
holding time of 45 min before tension. In this case, there should
be no intergranular precipitates formed during such a short time
of tensile deformation, especially when taking into account the
trace content of Bi. Consequently, the embrittlement of the Ni(Bi)
alloy cannot be linked to intergranular precipitates and the inter-
pretation of intergranular precipitates is not responsible for the
common feature of ITE in Ni and Ni-based superalloys. Addition-
ally, it must be pointed out that detrimental elements in the con-
tent of only several ppm can cause ITE. In order to improve the

The interpretation of intergranular precipitates contradicts not
only the experimental results of the Ni(Bi) alloy but also those of
Ni-based superalloys. The ITE of different Ni-based alloys were as-
cribed to intergranular M 23C6 [12,14],M 6C [12,14], d phase [13], c0
[14], oxides and MC carbides [9], respectively. However, these
ideas are also not applicable. In 1992, Miskovic et al. [18] studied
the ITE of the as-cast and heat treated conditions of IN 939 Ni-
based superalloy. A ductility minimum appears at 800 /C176C for the
as-cast condition while no ITE was found for the fully heat treated
condition (solution treatment and aging). In 2004, He et al. [8]
found that a ductility minimum of Ni-based superalloy M963
was observed at 800 /C176C under solution treatment and disappeared
under age treatment. In the same year, Sharghi-Moshtaghin and
Asgari [19] found the minimum ductility of IN-738LC superalloy
in standard heat treatment (SHT) condition to be located at
650 /C176C. However, no drop in ductility was observed for samples
aged for prolonged times (1500 and 3000 h). In 2007, Xia et al.
[20] found that the DZ951 superalloy after standard heat treatment
shows evident ITE (see Fig. 6 ) while ITE vanishes gradually as the
isothermal exposure time extends (100, 500, 1000 and 2000 h).
In 2009, the tensile properties of Ni-based superalloy 720Li were
measured by Gopinath et al. [21]. There is no ITE for this alloy after
two stages of long time aging. If the interpretation of intergranular
precipitates would be valid, it is inferred that these superalloys
should also exhibit ITE after long time of aging or exposure as well.
However, this inference deﬁnitely contradicts to the experimental
results. Besides, single crystal Ni-based superalloys, such as DD3
[22], CMSX-4 [23], and CMSX-10 [24], were proven to have evident
ITE. Obviously, the ITE of single crystal Ni-based superalloy cannot
be explained by this interpretation because of the absence of grain
boundary.
Based on the above discussions, it can be concluded that the
interpretation of intergranular precipitates is invalid for ITE of Ni
and Ni-based superalloys. Further investigations on the
segregation of Bi at grain boundary (polycrystalline Ni-based
superalloys) and phase interface (single crystal Ni-based superal-
loy) are in progress.
According to Fig. 5, it is known that the Ni(Bi) alloy with 25 wt
ppm Bi must be in single phase after heat treatment at 750 /C176C for
45 min. This ﬁnding is also helpful to determine the phase diagram
of Ni and Bi at very low content of Bi.
5. Conclusion
1. Tensile ductility of binary Ni(Bi) alloy reveals evident ITE with
maximum embrittlement between 700 /C176C and 750 /C176C.
2. OM, SEM and TEM observations with emphasis on grain bound-
ary fail to ﬁnd precipitates in Ni(Bi) alloy after heat treatment at
750 /C176C for 45 min.
3. The interpretation of intergranular precipitates cannot be used
to explain the general feature of ITE of Ni and Ni-based
superalloys.
4. The experimental results also yield a valuable data point to the
determination of the phase diagram of Ni and Bi in the very
dilute limit.
