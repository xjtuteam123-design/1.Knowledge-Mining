ABSTRACT 
The present paper has reported a comparative study on the influence of environmental temperature on the 
microstructural evolution and tensile mechanical behaviors between squeeze cast (SC) and gravity cast (GC) Mg- 
10Gd-3Y-0.5Zr (GW103K) alloys. The results revealed that for both alloys, the operative deformation mecha -
nisms at testing temperatures (25 – 300 
◦
C) were identified to be 〈 10 12 〉 extension twinning, 〈 10 11 〉
compressive twinning and 〈 10 13 〉 compressive twinning in addition to basal or non-basal slips. The elongation 
increased monotonically with increasing temperature within the range of 25 
◦
C to 300 
◦
C. However, the ultimate 
tensile strength (UTS) abnormally increased (for GC alloys) or keep constant (for SC alloys) with increasing the 
temperature from 25 
◦
C to 250 
◦
C, and decreased with further increasing the testing temperature to 300 
◦
C. 
Moreover, at all testing temperature, both the UTS and elongation of the squeeze cast material were improved 
relative to those of the gravity cast alloy. The underlying mechanism for the abnormal increase of strength within 
certain elevated temperature range was attributed to the insufficient strain hardening at lower temperatures that 
restrict further plastic deformation and lead to pre-mature fracture, and sufficient plastic deformation and 
softening at higher temperatures promote plastic deformation. The strengths improvements in squeeze cast 
GW103K alloy can be mainly ascribed to the preventing or limiting of crack nucleation sites and hindering of 
crack propagations through the reduction of micropores and increasing the amount of grain boundaries. 
1. Introduction 
The high strength and low density of Mg alloys render them as ideal 
candidate materials for applications that require high specific strength 
[ 1 – 3 ]. The increasing need in light casting components that can work at 
temperatures up to 300 
◦
C in certain areas of aerospace industry has 
greatly prompted the development of heat-resistant Mg alloys [ 4 ]. To 
that effect, inspection of the scientific literature shows that alloying Mg 
with: alkaline earth metal elements [ 5 , 6 ], transition metal elements 
[ 7 , 8 ], and rare-earth elements [ 9 – 11 ] reportedly lead to superior com -
binations of elevated temperature properties. Among these findings, 
alloying with heavy rare earth elements (REs) has been demonstrated to 
be an effective approach to improving the elevated temperature strength 
of Mg alloys [ 1 ]. 
As a near net shape forming process, squeeze casting has been proven 
to be an effective approach to improve the mechanical properties of Mg 
alloys [ 6 ]. In squeeze cast of Mg-RE alloys, Zhao et al. [ 12 ] found that 
the applied pressure during solidification of squeeze cast Mg-Gd-Zn al -
loys lead to considerable grain refinement effect and room temperature 
strength improvement, and similar results were also confirmed in Mg-Nd 
alloys [ 13 ]. Fang et al. [ 14 , 15 ] utilized rheo-squeeze cast process to 
fabricate the Mg-Y-Zn alloys and found that the as-cast and T6 heat- 
treated Mg-RE alloys can be further strengthened through squeeze 
casting. Our previous studies [ 16 , 17 ] showed that squeeze cast Mg-Gd-Y 
are potentially interesting for elevated temperature applications, but 
fundamental information on their microstructure evolution and me -
chanical behavior is lacking. 
In view of this lack of fundamental information, the objectives of the 
present study are twofold: First, we aim to provide fundamental insight 
into the elevated temperature tensile behavior of Mg-Gd-Y-Zr by 
comparing the materials processed via squeeze cast with conventional 
gravity cast. Second, to reveal the mechanisms that govern

3. Results 
3.1. Microstructures of GW103K alloy in T6 heat-treated condition 
Fig. 1 shows the optical micrographs of gravity cast and squeeze cast 
GW103K alloy in the T6 heat-treated condition. The phase composition 
of both alloys is nearly the same, and the phases present are: primary Mg 
phase, cubic phase (dark particles) randomly distributed along the grain 
boundaries, and petal like Zr-rich halos situated at the center of the 
grains (as labeled). A summary of microstructural details including the 
crystal structure, chemical composition and sizes of these phases were 
shown in Table 1 . It can be seen that the grain size was ~105 μ m in 
gravity cast and ~ 52 μ m in squeeze cast, respectively. The cubic phase 
(denoted as γ in Table 1 ) was identified to be REs rich FCC structured 
phase with a nominal composition of Mg
5
(Gd, Y) [ 19 ], which was 
mainly formed during solution heat treatment [ 16 ]. The Zr-rich halos 
were reported to be segregated Zr atoms through diffusion during non- 
equilibrium solidification and following heat treatments [ 20 ]. The 
morphology of the segregated Zr-core and Zr-rich halos are different in 
two alloys, which might be because the size of the critical heterogeneous 
nucleation site needed during solidification in squeeze cast is less than 
that in gravity cast. The precipitates in both alloys at peak-aged state 
(T6) were also confirmed to be the CBCO-structured (c-axis base- 
centered orthorhombic) β’ phase on the basis of TEM results as shown 
in Fig. 1 (c) and (d) and with a nominal composition of Mg
7
(Gd, Y) [ 21 ]. 
The sizes of β’ precipitate in both alloys are nearly equivalent according 
to quantitative statistic results [ 16 ].

4.1. The “ abnormal ” dependence of strength on temperature in Mg-Gd-Y 
alloys 
Generally, the strength of metallic materials decreases with 
increasing temperature. However, as indicated in Fig. 2 and previous 
studies [ 22 , 27 ], the tensile strengths, mostly the UTS, of precipitation 
hardened Mg-RE alloys are increased with increasing the atmospheric

discover the underlying mechanism in the deformation of Mg-RE alloys 
at elevated temperatures. 
In order to describe the abnormal strength improvement in UTS of 
Mg-RE at elevated temperatures, we define two variables here: the 
thermal hardener ratio (THR) and the fracture strength coefficient 
(FSC). The term thermal hardener ratio of the alloy at a certain tem -
perature is determined by the UTS that tested at the temperature divided 
by the UTS tested at room temperature (25

final point in the engineering stress-strain curve) divided by the UTS (the 
highest point). The calculated THR and FSC values of as-studied gravity 
cast and squeeze cast Mg-10Gd-3Y-0.5Zr alloys and other Mg-RE family

The FSC actually stands for the amplitude of strain hardening. If the 
tensile test is undergoing sufficient elongation before failure, the value

4.2. Toughening of microstructures in Mg-RE alloys through squeeze cast 
The results summarized in Fig. 2 show that the magnitude of the UTS 
(from the engineering stress-strain curves) of the squeeze cast GW103K 
alloy is superior to that of the gravity cast material at both room tem -
perature and elevated temperatures. The immature fracture of an alloy is 
usually caused by weak parts that act as nucleation sites for cracks 
formation and the channel for cracks propagation [ 29 ]. The toughening 
mechanism of squeeze cast alloy might be classified into two categories 
of reasons, the intrinsic and the extrinsic. In the current case, the 
intrinsic reason is the deformation mechanism of Mg-RE alloy performed 
at elevated temperatures, and the extrinsic reason is the microstructural 
differences that matters.

5. Conclusions 
In this study, we have comparatively studied the tensile behavior of 
GW103K alloy prepared by gravity cast and squeeze cast, the micro -
structure evolution together with the strength improvement mecha -
nisms were discussed, and the following conclusions can be made 
through experimental results: 
(1) In the gravity cast Mg-Gd-Y-Zr alloys, the ultimate tensile 
strength and elongation are both increased as the testing tem -
perature increases from 25 
◦
C ~ 250 
◦
C, while in squeeze cast 
alloys the strength is basically remain constant. With further 
increasing the testing temperature to 300 
◦
C, the strengths for 
both alloys were decreased drastically. When compared with that 
in gravity cast counterparts, the squeeze cast Mg-Gd-Y-Zr alloy 
shows an improved mechanical performance at testing tempera -
ture range of 25 
◦
C ~ 300 
◦
C. 
(2) The grains in squeeze cast alloy are deformed more severely than 
that in gravity cast alloy at all tested temperatures. Apart from 
basal and non-basal slips, the < 10 – 12 > extension twinning, 
< 10 – 11 > compressive twinning and < 10 – 13 > compressive 
twinning are also identified to be contributing deformation 
modes for gravity cast and squeeze cast alloys at temperatures 
from 25 
◦
C to 300 
◦
C. The squeeze cast alloy can induce more 
plastic deformation than gravity cast alloy at temperatures from 
25 
◦
C to 250 
◦
C via twinning. 
(3) The abnormal high strengths of Mg-Gd-Y-Zr alloys at elevated 
temperatures with respect to that in room temperature is mainly 
ascribed to the insufficient strain hardening at lower tempera -
tures that restrict further plastic deformation and lead to pre- 
mature fracture, and sufficient plastic deformation and soft -
ening at higher temperatures promote plastic deformation. 
(4) The improvements in tensile strength and ductility of squeeze 
cast Mg-Gd-Y-Zr alloy at elevated temperatures are mainly 
ascribed to the toughening of microstructures with reduced 
casting micro-pores and refined microstructures, both of which 
have restricted the crack initiations and propagations during 
uniaxial loading.
