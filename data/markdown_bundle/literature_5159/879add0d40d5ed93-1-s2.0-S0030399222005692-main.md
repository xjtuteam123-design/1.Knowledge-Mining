ABSTRACT

its microstructure, phase composition, texture, and tensile properties. The results indicate that both the micro -
structure of as-deposited and annealed alloys consists of phases Ti

1. Introduction 
TiAl alloys have a low density (4 g/cm
3
), a high melting point 
( > 1450 
◦
C), a high elastic modulus (160 – 180 GPa) and a high creep 
strength (~900 
◦
C). Recently, titanium alloy, nickel-based superalloys, 
and heat-resistant steels have been replaced by titanium alloy. There is 
great potential in aerospace and automotive engine manufacturing 
[1 – 4] . TiAl alloys with Nb atomic fractions greater than 5% are known 
as high Nb-TiAl alloys. Developing high Nb-TiAl alloys is a relatively 
new area of development for TiAl alloys. TiAl alloys exhibit greater 
oxidation resistance and high temperature creep strength than tradi -
tional TiAl alloys [5,6] . The Beijing University of Science and Tech -
nology has adopted investment casting technology in order to increase 
the ordering temperature of TiAl alloy by adding Nb. The service tem -
perature of TiAl alloys is 200 
◦
C higher than that of traditional TiAl 
alloys [7] . 
Due to the poor plasticity and mechanical properties of TiAl alloy at 
room temperature, it is difficult to produce high quality TiAl alloy with 
complex shapes by traditional forging, rolling, and welding methods 
[8,9] . This limits the wide application of TiAl alloy. The principal 
forming methods for TiAl alloys are powder metallurgy, precision 
casting, directional solidification, etc. It has been demonstrated that 
laser additive manufacturing (LAM) [10] offers significant advantages in 
the near-net forming of TiAl alloys. The properties and microstructure of 
high Nb-TiAl alloys have been studied using LAM technology by some 
scholars. According to Ismaeel et al. [11] , selective laser melting was 
used to investigate the effect of Nb on the microstructure and properties 
of γ -TiAl based alloys. When Nb was 7.0%, the comprehensive proper -
ties of the alloy were the best, including hardness of HV2000, strength of 
1390 MPa, and the degree of plastic deformation of 24.5% as well as 
tribological properties and high temperature oxidation resistance. 
Kan et al. [12] successfully prepared TiC reinforced nanocomposites 
with high Nb-TiAl alloy by using electron beam melting (EBM) tech -
nology. The TiC/Nb-TiAl nanocomposites sintered by electron beam 
have a microhardness of 393HV, which is 24% higher than the high Nb- 
TiAl alloy. Kan et al. [13] investigated grain morphology and texture 
control of a Ti-47Al-8Nb alloy fabricated by electron beam melting. An 
analysis of numerical simulations was conducted in order to determine 
the thermal gradient and the liquid-solid interface velocity, as well as 
the EBM process window for the development of columnar lamellar

2. Experimental process 
Titanium alloy TC4 was selected as the substrate. Its dimensions 
were 100 mm × 100 mm × 10 mm. Ti-48Al-2Cr-5Nb alloy powder was 
used as the experimental material, and its particle size range was 
53 – 100 μ m. The alloy powder was purchased from Zhonghang Maite 
Powder Metallurgical Technology Co., Ltd. The chemical composition 
(wt.%) of Ti-48Al-2Cr-5Nb alloy powder is 31.06Al, 2.49Cr, 11.35Nb, 
0.06O, 0.005N, and balance Ti. A SEM image of the alloy powder is 
shown in Fig. 1 .

Zhongke Yuchen Co., Ltd. The system consists of a fiber-coupled semi -

3.2. Microstructure characterizations of as-deposited alloy 
Fig. 5 illustrates the optical macrostructure of an as-deposited thin- 
walled Ti-48Al-2Cr-5Nb alloy sample. As shown in Fig. 5 , the macro -
structure of the as-deposited sample consists of columnar crystals. In 
addition, the alloy ’ s larger columnar grains are able to penetrate mul -
tiple deposition layers. The phenomenon is caused by the common 
structure of remelting of the lower surface and instantaneous heating of 
the upper deposition process, which refines the grains of the remelted 
part and forms the layer bands. 
Furthermore, the formation of columnar crystals occurs due to a 
small temperature gradient within the deposited layer, which makes it 
difficult for nuclei to form. After solidification and cooling, the direction 
of heat dissipation is opposite to the direction of deposition, so the 
nucleation and growth of the molten pool metal occurs as epitaxial 
crystallization [14] , which results in larger columnar crystals growing 
adjacent to the direction of deposition and their size is adequate to pass 
through multiple layers of deposition. 
Fig. 6 shows the chemical composition of the as-deposited and 
annealed samples. It can be seen from the figure that the contents of Al 
and Cr elements are relatively high in the lamella ( Fig. 6 (a, e, g)), but 
less between lamella ( Fig. 6 (b, c, d, f)). The content of Nb element 
changed little, indicating that Nb element was evenly distributed in the 
microstructure.

4. Conclusions 
Using Ti-48Al-2Cr-5Nb alloy powder as a raw material and opti -
mized process parameters, thin-walled samples of Ti-48Al-2Cr-5Nb alloy 
were successfully prepared by LAM technology, and the alloy samples 
were annealed at different temperatures. A study has been conducted on 
the effect of annealing temperature on the microstructure and properties 
of thin-walled samples of Ti-48Al-2Cr-5Nb alloy. The conclusions are as 
follows: 
The as-deposited Ti-48Al-2Cr-5Nb alloy thin-walled sample consists 
mainly of α
2
/ γ lamellae, and the volume fractions of the γ and α
2 
phases are 87.7% and 12.3%, respectively. The lamellar cluster size
