Abstract 
Effects of 2 wt% Yb addition on microstructures and mechanical properties of 
Mg −3.5Sm −0.6Zn −0.5Zr (SmZK410) casting alloy under as-cast, solution-treated and 
peak-aged conditions were thoroughly investigated. Yb addition has outstanding grain 
refinement effect, with grains being refined from ~48 µm to ~27 µm. Furthermore, the 
dominant intermetallic phase was changed from lamel lar Mg 
3Sm to divorced 
Mg 24 RE 5 by Yb addition. Moreover, Yb addition remarkably i mproves the alloy’s 
ageing hardening response. Transmission electron mi croscopy (TEM) observations 
indicate that the dominant precipitate in the peak- aged SmZK410 alloy is basal γ’’, 
while both fine prismatic β’’ and basal γ’’ along with a coarse tridentate precipitate 
which is consisted of ordered β’’/β’ stacking coexist in the alloy with Yb addition. 
These changes due to Yb addition result in clear im provement on both strength and 
ductility of the SmZK410 alloy at both room tempera ture and high temperatures. 
Thereby, the alloy with Yb addition exhibits high t ensile yield strength of 228 MPa 
and a satisfactory elongation of 5.7 %, thus more e xcellent strength-ductility balance 
than other conventional Mg −RE based casting alloys. 
Keywords: Magnesium alloy; Mechanical property; Transmission electron microscopy 
(TEM); Precipitate; Heat treatment.

3.1. Microstructure of as-cast and solution-treated alloys 
Fig. 1a and b shows the OM image of the as-cast SmZ K410 and SmYbZK4210

approximately 47 ± 6 
µm and 27 ± 4 µm, respectively. It is indicates that adding Yb 
has an excellent grain refinement effect on SmZK410 alloy. Note that Yb has 
relatively small solubility in Mg matrix [19]. Duri ng solidification, the α-Mg grain 
size is strongly related to growth restriction fact or (GRF). It is reported that the

to the formation of divorced eutectic. And it also results in early impingement of 
dendrites, thus decreasing interdendritic spacing. As a result, a higher constitutional 
undercooling induced by Yb segregation in the front of solid-liquid interface is 
responsible for the divorced eutectic in the SmYbZK 4210 alloy. Similar phenomenon

3.2. Ageing hardening behavior and precipitates 
Fig. 6 shows ageing hardening behavior of both allo ys during aged at 200 ºC, 
which indicates that the Yb addition into the SmZK4 10 alloy obviously enhances the 
aging hardening effect. Moreover, it can be seen th at the varieties of hardness values 
in both alloys present similar tendency, with the h ardness starting to decrease after a 
first monotonous increase, thus leading to peak har dness in spite of different hardness 
values and different aging time. Yb addition leads to an enhanced age hardening 
response with a higher peak hardness and substantia lly shortened time to peak 
hardness. During 200 ºC ageing, the SmYbZK4210 allo y achieved the maximum 
hardness of 98 HV after 15 h, against 89 HV after 2 0 h for the SmZK410 alloy. 
Rokhlin et al. [33] proposed that when two RE eleme nts from different groups 
(Ce-group and Y-group) are added together, each one will change the precipitation 
kinetics of the other one in Mg alloys, thus improv ing a precipitation strengthening 
effect. As an example, Li et al. [34] reported that Y addition into the Mg–2.8Nd–
0.6Zn–0.4Zr alloy promoted the clustering of solute s during the early-stage ageing. 
And they verified that such solute clusters is cond ucive to the formation of 
precipitates (such as β’’ phase), which is therefore responsible to a shorter peak time. 
In this work, Sm and Yb belong to Ce- and Y-groups, respectively. Thus, Yb addition 
into SmZK410 alloy would accelerate the clustering of solutes and improve 
precipitation strengthening effect, resulted in a s horter peak time and greater peak 
hardness are achieved in SmYbZK4210 alloy. Moreover , although the peak hardness 
of the SmYbZK4210 alloy is still slightly lower tha n those of the conventional WE43

Such phenomenon has been reported in many Mg alloys , such as AZ91 [58], WE54

the serrated flow is primarily attributed to the in teractions between dislocations and

compared to dislocation velocity at relative high t emperature, and any solute 
atmosphere formed can always move with dislocations causing disappearance of

dislocations are constantly pinned and unpinned by shearing of precipitates or 
interacting with solute atoms [19,60]. This cannot increase the work hardening 
exponent, instead decrease work hardening capabilit y. So when deformation 
encounters in the serration range, the necking phen omenon is more likely to occur. 
This will result in a loss of elongation. Consequen tly, the deterioration of the ductility 
of both alloys at 200 oC is largely attributed to such severe serrated flo w. In addition, 
lower elongations were obtained in both alloys at 3 00 oC, which is mainly due to the 
poor work hardening during high temperature deforma tion. Finally, it should be 
pointed out that Yb addition has little effect on s errated flow behavior for Mg −Sm

Highlights 
 A Mg −Sm −Yb −Zn −Zr alloy was developed and has excellent mechanical 
properties. 
 Grains are refined and intermetallic phases are modified by Yb addition. 
 Both basal and prismatic precipitates precipitated in the peak-aged alloy. 
 Tridentate precipitates with ordered β’’/β’ long-period stacking precipitated. 
 The underlying mechanisms for its outstanding mecha nical properties were 
revealed.
