Design of low mass density γ/γ ′ CoNi-based superalloys with promising high-temperature
mechanical properties
Prafull Pandey,* Namrata Mazumder, Mahander Pratap Singh, Chaitali Patil, Amit Sharma , Surendra Kumar Makineni,†
Dipankar Banerjee ,‡ and Kamanio Chattopadhyay §
Department of Materials Engineering, Indian Institute of Science, Bangalore 560012, India
(Received 7 June 2021; accepted 2 August 2021; published 2 September 2021)
We report new compositions of CoNi-based superalloys that have low mass densities (7.84–8.20 g /cc),
negative γ/ γ′ lattice misﬁt, and high γ′ solvus temperature up to 1094 °C with promising high-temperature
mechanical properties. The γ/ γ′ in the base Co-30Ni-10Al-5Cr alloy was stabilized with Nb and further
developed by varying Ti and Cr. The γ′ precipitates in these alloys are richer in Ni than the γ matrix phase,
unlike for γ′ in other CoNi-based superalloys. The atomic-scale compositional analysis indicates that the γ′
has a stoichiometry of (Ni, Co)3(Al, Nb, Ti, Cr). In these alloys, the γ′microstructure is stable at 900 °C up to
1000 h. In an alloy with a γ′ solvus of 1089 °C, the coarsening kinetics of γ′ at 900 °C follows the Lifshitz-
Slyozov-Wagner model with a rate constant of 6.6 ± 0.7n m3/s, comparable with high-density Co-Al-W-based
superalloys. The alloys also show an anomalous increase in strength, with temperature having a peak of 0.2%
proof stress (PS) and speciﬁc PS (SPS) of ∼710 MPa and ∼91 MPa g
–1cc, respectively, at 770 °C. The low
density and high strength make this class of alloys a potential and cost-effective alternative to the other Co-based
superalloys and comparable with Ni-based superalloys.

I. INTRODUCTION
Environmental concerns have driven the energy sector to-
ward increased efﬁciency of the energy conversion system
and advanced ultra-supercritical power plants that are ex-
pected to operate at higher operating temperatures in the
domains of 700–850 °C [ 1,2]. Currently, nickel-based super-
alloys are prime candidates to meet the requirements for
the high-temperature sections of these plants. Several al-
loys like Haynes 230, Haynes 282, IN 617, and IN 740
are actively evaluated for such applications [ 1,3–6]. In addi-
tion to the properties like high-temperature tensile and creep
strength, resistance to environmental degradation under the
harsh conditions that prevail at the ﬁreside, and supercritical
steam environments necessitated incorporating the increasing
amount of chromium in these alloys [1,7,8]. In addition to oxi-
dation resistance, resistance to sulﬁdation is another important
requirement for power plant application, where nickel-based
superalloys are limited by the formation of low-melting eutec-
tic of nickel sulﬁdes [9–12].
Co-based alloys have traditionally been used in extreme
environments, as in the combustion section of jet aero-engines
and land-based turbines. While these alloys have superior
resistance to sulﬁdation [ 13–15], most of these commercial
alloys are based on solid solution strengthening and are
limited by this approach. The discovery of L1
2 ordered co-
herent γ′ precipitates in Co-based alloys with the addition
of W [ 16,17] paved the way for developing classic γ/ γ′

required to stabilize γ′increases the density of the alloys be-
yond >9.4 g/cc. Recently, γ/ γ′Co-based alloys that contain
Mo in place of W were shown by Makineni et al. [18–20].
The densities and high-temperature mechanical properties
are comparable with many well-known Ni-based superalloys
[20–22]. The high cost of Co can be offset by using alloys
having lower density and thus provide speciﬁc properties that
enable use in weight-optimized conﬁgurations that gain an
advantage over Ni-based superalloys at the component level
in terms of actual consumption of materials.
In an evolution of our previous developments [18–20], we
now demonstrate a class of alloys with lower content of high-
density refractory elements which exhibit exceptional strength
at high temperatures. These alloys show good microstructural
stability and γ
′ coarsening resistance at 900 °C. Our design
approach is based on the recognition that the addition of Cr,
a γ stabilizer, not only promotes preferential partitioning of
Mo to the γ [Fig. 1(a)] but also enhances the partitioning of
Ti and Al to the γ
′. Previous work also allows us to evaluate
the effect of solute partitioning on the γ′solvus temperatures
[Figs. 1(a) and 1(b)][ 18–21,23]. Based on this information,
a base composition of Co-30Ni-10Al-2Nb was ﬁxed with a
combined addition of varying amounts of Cr and Ti to achieve
densities in the range of 7.84–8.20 g/cc. The γ
′ solvus tem-
peratures up to 1094 °C and γ′ volume fractions of 55–60%
were observed that are typical of forgeable alloys and make
them attractive in the temperature envelope of 700–900 °C.

III. RESULTS
A. Development of γ/γ ′ containing
Co-30Ni-2Nb-10Al-5Cr alloy
The BSE SEM image of the as-cast Co-30Ni-10Al-
2Nb-5Cr (10Al5Cr) alloy shows dark dendritic and eutectic
formation in the bright interdendritic region [see Fig. 2(a)].
In the present case, the nonequilibrium solidiﬁcation con-
dition leads to the rejection of solutes from the dendritic
region to the melt pool of the interdendritic region as the
dendrites grow. Finally, the last liquid solidiﬁes with rich
solute elements, leading to eutectic formation, as reported
in Ni-based superalloys [ 28,29]. The composition analysis
using WDS in EPMA reveals strong segregation of Nb in
the interdendritic region and weak segregation of Co, Cr, Ni,
and Al in the dendritic region (see Fig. S1 in the Supple-
mental Material [ 27]). The solutionizing heat treatment of
the as-cast alloy at a super-solvus temperature of 1200 °C
for 20 h leads to removing the microsegregation of solutes
[see Fig. 2(b)]. The BSE SEM micrograph shows equiaxed
grains of size 100 to 200 μm with the presence of anneal-
ing twins [shown by white arrows in Fig. 2(b)] that might
be due to the low-stacking fault energy of the alloy [see
Fig. 2(b)]. Figure 2(c) shows the BSE SEM image of the
10Al5Cr aged alloy (900 °C for 50 h). The micrograph shows
rod-shaped elongated γ
′ precipitates in the matrix. The se-
lected area electron diffraction pattern (SAEDP) along the
[001] zone axis from the alloy shows superlattice reﬂections
of L1
2 ordering and fundamental reﬂections from the face-
centered cubic (fcc) γ matrix [see inset of Fig. 2(d)]. The
TEM dark-ﬁeld micrograph using a 100 superlattice spot near
the [001] zone axis illuminates γ
′precipitates with a bimodal
distribution [see Fig. 2(d)]. The bigger γ′ precipitates are
formed during isothermal aging at 900 °C and are primary γ′
precipitates, while the ﬁne precipitates formed during contin-
uous cooling are secondary γ′precipitates. Figure 2(e) shows
the STEM high-angle annular dark-ﬁeld (HAADF) image
of peak aged 10Al5Cr alloy. The STEM energy-dispersive
x-ray spectroscopy (EDS) composition proﬁle across a pri-
mary γ

partitioning of Ni, Al, and Nb to γ′ while Co and Cr to γ
matrix [see Fig. 2(f)]. The qualitative STEM-EDS map in
Fig. 2(g) shows similar partitioning behavior of solutes across
secondary γ′precipitates. The observed partitioning behavior
of solutes is like Mo- and W-containing Co-based superalloys
[19,20,23,30].

IV . DISCUSSION
We demonstrated the design of low-density polycrystalline
γ/ γ′Co-based superalloys through optimization of Cr and Ti
and eliminating the use of high-density refractory metals such
as W, Mo, and Ta. The designed alloys have been evaluated
with respect to their RT and high-temperature strength, γ
′
solvus temperature, γ′ volume fraction, γ/ γ′ misﬁt, and the
stability of the microstructure at 900 °C.
Figure 8(a) shows an Ashby map of solvus vs density
for cobalt-based γ/ γ′ superalloys that highlights the unique
position of the current alloys. The strong partitioning of Al,
Ti, and Nb to the γ
′ together with the elimination of Mo
[that partitions to γ in this class of Cr containing alloys,
Fig. 1(a)] leads to a high solvus temperature and γ′ volume
fraction. The partitioning behavior also leads to a negative
γ/ γ

in a Co-based superalloy has not been reported. Earlier work
has shown that increasing the Cr content leads to a reduction
in the misﬁt through the partitioning of Cr to γ and results
in a signiﬁcant increase in the lattice parameter of γ [23].
The elemental partitioning data provided in Table II indicate
that the Cr content of γ is as high as 22 at. %, indeed, higher
than the Ni content in γ . Thus, even though Cr is known to
have a lower Vegard’s coefﬁcient with respect to Ni/Co [57],
its signiﬁcant content in γ inﬂuences the lattice parameter
strongly. While it is not clear whether the sign of misﬁt would

The addition of 2 at. % Ti signiﬁcantly increases the γ

of the Fermi level toward it. It results in the stabilization of
the γ

Quantifying the microstructures of the 10Al12Cr4Ti alloy

The variation of 0.2% PS with temperature for the
10Al12Cr4Ti alloy shows anomalous strengthening after
670 °C, i.e., the 0.2% PS increases up to a peak value of
710 MPa at 770 °C and drops at higher temperature. Similar
high onset temperature for anomalous strengthening is also
observed in some of the W- and Mo-containing Co-based
superalloys [ 21,40,41]. The high onset temperature was at-
tributed to lower complex stacking fault energy that makes
cross-slip of antiphase boundary-coupled dislocation difﬁcult
from {111} to {010} planes. The anomalous strengthening
beyond the onset temperature is due to the thermal activation
of the cross-slip that induces Kear-Wilsdorf locks [44,57].

and speciﬁc yield strength of the current alloys at RT and
870 °C. These are compared with the earlier reported γ/ γ
′
strengthened Co alloys with or without W. A signiﬁcant im-
provement in speciﬁc yield strength while retaining a high
solvus temperature is apparent. The results indicate that the
alloys developed in this paper exhibit a unique combination
of low mass density, suitable solvus temperature, and good
mechanical properties at high temperatures.
V . CONCLUSIONS
To conclude, here, we report low mass density CoNi-based
superalloys with primary constituents of Co, Ni, Al, Cr, and
Nb. The optimized and combined addition of low mass density
Cr and Ti led to the increase in γ
′ solvus temperature up to
1094 °C and a reduction in alloy density up to 7.84 ± 0.02
g/cc. In contrast to other Co-based superalloys that show
a positive γ/ γ′ misﬁt, the present alloys show a negative
γ/ γ′lattice misﬁt like the Ni-based superalloys. Atomic-scale
compositional analysis reveal γ′ precipitates to be richer in
Ni than Co, which is not commonly observed in other Co-
based superalloys. Another important ﬁnding is that Cr, an
essential alloy for high-temperature oxidation resistance, does
not show a signiﬁcant inﬂuence in the decrease of γ
′ solvus
temperature. Further, the alloys show anomalous increase in
strength with temperature that makes some of them superior
to other Co-based and many Ni-based superalloys at high
temperatures.
