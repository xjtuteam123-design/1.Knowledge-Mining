The microstructures and mechanical properties of novel multiphase Nb-based composites from Nb –Ti–C–B
system have been investigated. The microstructures were characterized using scanning electron microscopy,
transmission electron microscopy and X-ray diffraction. The composites contain Nb solid solution, (Nb,Ti)B
boride and (Nb,Ti)C carbide phases. The hybrid (Nb,Ti)B and (Nb,Ti)C together with Nb solid solution form
the network microstructure. The composites exhibit high hardness and strength while maintaining desirable
fracture toughness at room temperature. Boride and carbide phases effectively improve the strength and
hardness of the composites, while Nb solid solution toughens the composites. Higher boron contents increase
the hardness and strength, but decrease the toughness.

1. Introduction
Nb-based composites with a high melting point and a relatively
low density have received considerable attention in recent years as
one of the potential candidates of next generation high temperature
(1150–1450 °C) structural materials to replace the current Ni-based
or Co-based superalloys [1–5]. For the real application of these com-
posites, their strength, toughness, and creep resistance at high tem-
peratures should be comprehensively considered and tuned [6–8].
Besides, how to improve their oxidation resistance is also a challenge
as Nb-based composites suffer from a serious oxidation even above
500 °C [9,10]. In the recent investigations to balance excellent frac-
ture toughness and high strength, these composites were mainly
strengthened by the intermetallic compounds such as silicides
(Nb5Si3 and/or Nb3Si) and Cr2Nb Laves phases [6,7,11]. Simultaneous-
ly, Ti and/or Hf were also added to improve both the room tempera-
ture fracture toughness and oxidation resistance of these Nb-based
composites [7,8].
Niobium boride and niobium carbide possess some excellent
properties such as high melting temperature, high strength, high
thermal and electrical conductivity, and chemical stability [12,13].
Therefore, the (Nb,Ti)C carbide has been con ﬁrmed as the most ef ﬁ-
cient strengthening phase of Nb –Ti–C alloys at room temperature
and 1200 °C [14,15]. To date, no data for Nb-based composites
strengthened by the hybrid carbide and boride has been reported.
The objective of this study was to synthesize niobium carbide and ni-
obium boride in order to further improve the strength of Nb-based
composites. Ti was added for balancing strength and toughness of
the composites. The microstructure, and strengthening and toughen-
ing behavior of Nb-based composites were investigated and discussed
in details.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Phase identi ﬁcation and microstructure
The XRD patterns of as-cast samples are shown in Fig. 1. It shows
that, in each case, the sample contains W-type Nb solid solution
(Nbss), CrB-type (Nb,Ti)B boride and NaCl-type (Nb,Ti)C carbide
phases. The XRD results show that the boride and carbide of niobium
were synthesized using the arc-melting method. Ti is the solid solu-
tion element of Nb, and the experimental investigation by Ding et
al. [16] found that Nb-based alloy added by top 35 at.% Ti only
appeared single Nbss phase. When alloying with Ti in Nb –C alloys,
the Nb
2C carbide was changed to (Nb,Ti)C in Nb –Ti–C alloys added
by more than 20 at.% Ti [17]. The reported Nb15Ti5C and Nb20Ti5C
(at.%) alloys also further con ﬁrmed the existence of (Nb,Ti)C in
Nb-based alloys [14]. Moreover, Ti solution behavior in NbB has
been demonstrated by Borysov et al. [18]. Based on Nb –C–B phase re-
lation [19], the three phases, namely Nb 2C, NbB and Nb, existed in the
as-cast Nb-rich alloys. In this work, 20 at.% Ti was added, and then Ti
addition could result in the formation of Nbss, (Nb,Ti)C and (Nb,Ti)B
multiphase. The multiphase Nb-based composites were synthesized
according to the XRD results. Our results are in good agreement
with the previous reports.
Fig. 2 shows SEM backscattered electron images of as-cast sam-
ples. From the low magni ﬁcation images, it can be found that the
network-shape eutectic distributes around dendritic Nbss grain
boundaries in the microstructure of each sample, as shown in Fig. 2
(a and b). The network eutectic becomes big in the composite with
higher B content, and whose average thickness increases from
8.5 μm for Nb20Ti4C4B sample to 21 μm for Nb20Ti4C8B sample.
The high magni ﬁcation SEM backscattered electron images of the eu-
tectic in two samples are shown in Fig. 2 (c and d). In the eutectic, the
abundance ﬁne gray and dark grains distributing in Nbss matrix are
found. EDX results show that the B/(Nb + Ti) and C/(Nb + Ti)
mole ratios are around 0.5 in the gray and dark grains, respectively.
It indicates that the gray grains are (Nb,Ti)B, and the dark grains are
(Nb,Ti)C. Based on the low magni ﬁcation images, the image analysis
shows that the volume fraction of total (Nb,Ti)B and (Nb,Ti)C parti-
cles is about 11.6 and 18.1% in the Nb20Ti4C4B and Nb20Ti4C8B sam-
ples, respectively. TEM bright ﬁeld images with a detailed view of
(Nb,Ti)C and (Nb,Ti)B are shown in Fig. 3. From Fig. 3, it can be seen
that there is no interfacial reaction at the Nbss/(Nb,Ti)C and Nbss/
(Nb,Ti)B interfaces. Thus, it can be deduced that these interfaces are
stable, and the bonding is strong. The selected area diffraction pattern
of (Nb,Ti)C taken in the [114] zone of a face-centered cubic structure
(see the inset in Fig. 3 a), and which of (Nb,Ti)B was recorded along
the [110] zone of a B33 orthorhombic structure (see the inset in
Fig. 3 b). The results identi ﬁed by TEM are consistent with the XRD
analysis.
As discussed above, the network-shape eutectic distributes around
the coarse dendritic Nbss in the microstructure. In this work, the com-
posites were prepared using the arc-melting technique. The processing
temperature was so high that Ti, C and B were fully melted in the Nb liq-
uid. So, the production of the composites was based on a liquid –solid
process. The distinct eutectic network formed around Nbss dendrites
could be attributed to the solidi ﬁcation path. The recent data locates
the eutectic reaction of L ↔ (Nb) + Nb 2C + NbB in Nb-based alloys
from Nb–B–Cs y s t e m[19]. As stated above, Ti addition leads to the for-
mation of (Nb,Ti)C and (Nb,Ti)B in the composites. Thus, it could be
concluded that the coarse Nbss dendrites were ﬁrstly solidi ﬁed.
According to the binary M –Ca n dM –B (M = Nb, Ti) phase diagrams,
the C and B elements exhibit the less solid solubility in Nb and Ti. As
the temperature went down, the abundant C and B elements were
enriched in the interdendritic liquid around the primary Nbss. The eu-
tectic mixture of (Nb,Ti)B, (Nb,Ti)C and Nbss phases wasﬁnally formed
in the interdendritic region.
3.2. Mechanical properties
In order to evaluate the mechanical properties of the multiphase
Nb-based composites, the hardness, compression property, tensile
property and fracture toughness were tested at room temperature.
Table 1 lists the mechanical properties of the samples at room tem-
perature, including Vickers hardness (HV), 0.2% offset compression
yield stress ( σ0.2), ultimate tensile strength ( UTS), tensile elongation
(ε) and fracture toughness ( KQ). Fig. 4 shows the typical compression
and tensile stress –strain curves of each sample at room temperature.
From the stress –strain curves, the higher stress peak appears in
Nb20Ti4C8B sample. Namely, it can be stated that the strength of
the composites was improved by the addition of more boron. Further-
more, the compression tests were interrupted when the strain was up
to 25%. It is believed that the composites exhibit the excellent com-
pression ductility.
As reported in the literatures [14,15], the hardness of Nb20Ti12.5C
and Nb20Ti10C alloys are HV 253 and HV 204, and compression σ0.2
at room temperature are 580 MPa and 405 MPa, respectively. In this
study, for Nb20Ti4C4B sample, its hardness and compressionσ0.2 are re-
spectively HV 304 and 700 MPa at room temperature, which are much
higher than the reported results of Nb –Ti–C alloys. In addition, its ul-
timate tensile strength of 535 MPa at room temperature is superior
to other Nb-based composites/alloys such as Nb18Si5Mo5Hf2C com-
posite [20] and Nb5Mo15W alloy [21]. These results show that the

Fig. 5 shows SEM micrographs of tensile fracture surfaces of the
composites. The fracture surface displays the different failure modes
of the primary Nbss and the eutectic during the tensile process. The
primary Nbss shows smooth and ﬂat fracture surface containing
some river patterns and tiny avulsion edges. However, the fracture
surfaces of the eutectic are much rougher cleavage planes. Further,
the abundant tear-ridges of Nbss are also observed in the high
magniﬁcation images of the fractured eutectic surfaces. Obviously,
Nbss in the eutectic underwent the plastic stretching, and the ﬁnal
failure took place in a ductile manner to form the abundant
tear-ridges. The fracture process of the eutectic could consist of the
interface separation between Nbss and ceramic phases, plastic
stretching of Nbss and cleavage of ceramic phases. Moreover, the frac-
ture surfaces suggest that there are many cracks arrested by Nbss and
introduced into the ceramic phases or the interface. This implies that
the ceramic phases and the interface are the origin of crack initiation,
and Nbss performs as a crack arrester.

and then the secondary cracks were produced.
4. Conclusions
The multiphase Nb-based composites containing Nb solid solu-
tion, (Nb,Ti)B boride and (Nb,Ti)C carbide phases were successfully
synthesized using the arc-melting method. The hybrid (Nb,Ti)B and
(Nb,Ti)C phases distribute mainly in the interdendritic eutectic net-
work around the primary Nbss dendrites. Both hardness and strength
are enhanced due to the introduction of hybrid boride and carbide
particles. Higher B contents in the composites lead to lower values
of fracture toughness at room temperature.
