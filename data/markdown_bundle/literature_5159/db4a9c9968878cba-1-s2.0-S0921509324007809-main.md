ABSTRACT 
Refractory high entropy alloys are strong candidates for high-temperature structural materials, because of their 
high strength, excellent thermal stability and exceptional softening resistance under high-temperature. However, 
majority of the existing refractory high entropy alloys are seriously limited in widespread application, due to 
their severe brittleness at room temperature. In this study, we proposed a novel Al
x
HfTaTi body-centered cubic 
(BCC) refractory medium entropy alloy with remarkable strength-ductility combination at room temperature. 
Adjusting the atomic ratio of Al (from 0 to 0.5 at.%), we investigated the effect of Al on the structure-property 
characteristics of the alloy under as-cast as well as cold-rolled and annealed conditions. Compared with the as- 
cast samples, the rolled and annealed samples exhibit more excellent tensile properties due to the remarkable 
reduction of grain size resulted from recrystallization. The Al
0.3
HfTaTi sample annealed for 5 min exhibits 
outstanding comprehensive properties at room temperature, with a yield strength higher than 1000 MPa and a 
fracture elongation of up to 24 ± 1 %, respectively. Quasi in-situ EBSD analysis results reveal that, during tensile 
deformation, the grain size has a significant effect on the grain rotation and dislocation gliding. Small grains are 
more likely to initiate multiple slip bands, promoting the synergistic effect of grain rotation and dislocation 
gliding, thereby improving the ductility of the alloy. Our findings can provide new ideas for the development of 
refractory high entropy alloys with outstanding room-temperature tensile properties. 
1. Introduction 
Conventional alloys, which consist of one or two major elements, 
have been the research focus in the field of metal materials. Since the 
advent of high entropy alloy (HEA), as a new type of metal material, the 
design concept of traditional alloy composed of single main element has 
been revolutionized, and thus creating a new alloy system [ 1 – 3 ]. HEAs 
have multiple compositional possibilities, supported by appropriate heat 
treatment conditions, they provide new opportunities to design new 
alloys with outstanding performance, such as excellent mechanical 
properties, corrosion resistance, radiation resistance, etc [ 4 – 12 ]. Senkov 
et al. [ 13 ] first proposed the concept of refractory high entropy alloy 
(RHEA) in 2010. They applied the design concept of HEA to refractory 
elements and developed NbMoTaW and VNbMoTaW HEAs. As a new 
type of high-temperature structural materials, RHEAs possess significant 
high-temperature performances, due to their high melting temperature. 
For example, the yield strengths of NbMoTaW and NbMoTaWV [ 13 ] 
alloys are respectively 405 MPa and 477 MPa at 1600 
◦
C. 
HfMoNbTaTiZr alloy still maintains a yield strength of 556 MPa at 
1200 
◦
C [ 14 ]. The high temperature performances of these RHEAs even 
exceed conventional nickel based high-temperature alloys. Undoubt -
edly, RHEAs have become strong competitors among the structural 
materials used in aerospace and nuclear energy fields. 
However, most of the existing RHEAs are difficult to achieve plastic 
forming at room temperature due to their poor ductility and formability 
[ 15 ]. For example, the ultimate compressive strain of NbMoTaW alloy at 
room temperature can only reaches up to about 1.5 % [ 13 ]. In addition, 
oxidation resistance and lightweighting are also serious problems 
currently faced by RHEAs [ 15 ]. Al is an element that can form a dense 
oxide layer on the surface of alloys, thus the addition of Al usually results 
in effective enhancement of oxidation resistance [ 16 – 18 ]. Meanwhile, it 
also has the advantage of reducing density of the alloy [ 19 , 20 ]. There -
fore, Al has become a popular alloying element for the composition 
design of RHEAs. However, the majorities of current RHEAs containing 
Al have a trade-off between strength and ductility. Lin et al. [ 21 ] found 
that the addition of Al can significantly improve the compressive yield

Although the number of main elements in medium entropy alloys

HEAs [ 23 , 26 ]. For example, the ternary CrCoNi medium entropy alloy

strength and ductility of the ternary MEAs is easier to manage than the 
corresponding HEAs system [ 26 ]. 
Herein, we designed a HfTaTi refractory medium entropy alloy 
(RMEA) with excellent room temperature ductility. To further improve 
the mechanical properties of the alloy at room temperature and meet the 
needs of application in high-temperature conditions, we selected Al 
element as the fourth alloying element in the RMEAs. For the purpose to 
improve the comprehensive performance of the alloy while ensuring a 
satisfactory plasticity, we cold-rolled and annealed the as-cast alloy. We 
carried out systematic characterization and analysis of the microstruc -
ture and mechanical properties to the alloys in different forming states, 
aiming to explore the strengthening and deformation mechanism of the 
alloy. It has enlightening significance for the subsequent design of alloys 
with both conspicuous room temperature mechanical properties.

structure. In the Al

VEC of the alloy remains below 6.87, indicating that all three alloys tend 
to form BCC single phase solid solutions.

4. Discussion 
4.1. Deformation mechanism 
To investigate the deformation mechanism of the sample A3-5min, 
quasi in-situ EBSD analysis was performed on the sample during ten -
sile test and the corresponding results are shown in Fig. 9 . Fig. 9 (a) and 
(b) show the IPF maps of the selected region with the global tensile 
strain at 7.1 % and 14.9 %, respectively. It can be seen that, there exists 
crystal orientation gradient in individual grains (like the G1 region 
marked with black lines) of the deformed sample, especially when the 
global strain reaches to 14.9 %. Fig. 9 (c) shows the cumulative change in 
orientation along the section line marked in red and blue in the G1 grain 
at different global strain level (7.1 % and 14.9 %). It can be seen that 
with the increase of global tensile strain, the orientation speeding en -
hances within the G1 grain, which finally leads to the formation of 
substructures like low angle grain boundaries (LAGBs) in the sample. 
Fig. 9 (d) and (e) show the KAM maps and grain boundary maps of 
samples at different strain. LAGBs with the crystallographic misorien -
tation of 2 – 15
◦
were marked in red in the figure, while the high angle 
grain boundaries (HAGBs) with the crystallographic misorientation 
higher than 15
◦
were marked in black. With the global strain at 7.1 %, 
the local regions located near the grain boundaries firstly exhibit an 
increase of the KAM value, indicating a higher density of geometric 
necessary dislocation (GND) in these local regions. It demonstrates that 
strain concentration first occurs near the grain boundaries. There is no 
obvious strain concentration in the large-size grains, indicating that the 
stress at this time is mainly borne by the grain boundaries. When the 
strain increases to 14.9 %, a more enhanced strain concentration ap -
pears on the grain boundaries and some strain localization bands form 
inside the grains. The strain localization promotes the crystallographic 
rotation of the grains and leads to the formation of LAGBs inside the 
grains. At this point, the misorientation in the large-sized grains appears, 
and the strain concentration also appears inside grains, which indicates 
that strain concentration first appears at the grain boundary, then ap -
pears in the small-size grain, and finally appears in the large-size grain 
during tensile deformation. Fig. 9 (f) – (g) show the secondary electron 
(SE) images of the local region at different global strain. It can be 
observed that, when the global strain is 7.1 %, a large number of obvious 
slip bands appear on the surface of the sample, most of which are single 
slip and almost no multiple slip occurs. After the global strain increases 
to 14.9 %, the occurrence of multiple slips can be observed in the grains. 
Moreover, as the deformation enhances, multiple slip first appears in 
smaller grains and then in larger grains. 
In order to further reveal the influence of grain size on grain rotation 
and dislocations slipping during the deformation process, EBSD analysis 
were performed on the A3-60min alloy at different deformation stages.

5. Conclusions 
In this paper, we developed an Al
x
HfTaTi (0 ≤ x ≤ 0.5) refractory 
medium entropy alloys with a single BCC structure. After rolling and 
annealing, the Al
0.3
HfTaTi alloy exhibited excellent comprehensive 
properties at room temperature, with a yield strength above 1000 MPa 
and a total elongation of up to 24 ± 1 %, respectively. The plasticity 
originating from the multiple slip is more easily to be activated in small- 
sized grains, resulting in the combined effect of grain rotation and 
dislocation slip in the alloy. The yield strength of the alloy mainly comes 
from the modulus mismatch provided by the addition of Al and the 
refined crystalline strengthening resulted from recrystallization during 
annealing. This material has excellent comprehensive mechanical 
properties and cold rolling performance, and has the potential for 
application as structural materials.
