Abstract
In this study, the mechanical properties of Ti–13Nb–7Sn–4Mo (wt%) was improved through 90% cold rolling and subsequent aging treatment under 
different aging temperatures (500, 550, and 600°C) and times (30, 60, and 120 min). After aging treatment, the alloy showed a β + α″ + α structure, 
in which the α″ phase contributed to its low elastic modulus (58.5–73.4 GPa) and the nano α precipitates contributed to its high bending strength (> 
1230 MPa). The alloy exhibited the highest bending‑strength/elastic‑modulus ratio (×1000) (23.3) after 90% cold rolling and aging at 600°C for 30 min.
Introduction
In recent years, commercial biomedical alloys such as 316L 
SS, Co–Cr–Mo, and Ti–6Al–4V have faced doubts about insuf-
ficient mechanical properties, biocompatibility, and corrosion 
resistance. [1–3] Furthermore, the elastic moduli of the above 
three alloys (110–220 GPa) are much higher than that of human 
bone (≤ 30 GPa), and the stress shielding effect derived there-
from causes clinical problems. [4,5] Many studies have devel-
oped metastable β-type Ti alloys with low elastic modulus 
through alloy design.[6] However, the metastable β-type Ti alloy 
usually has the problem of insufficient strength. [7]
Work hardening is a common strengthening mechanism of 
alloys, which can increase the strength of alloys through dislo-
cations introduced by cold working. Moreover, the metastable 
β-type Ti alloy may undergo a stress-induced martensitic trans-
formation (β → α″) during the cold-rolling process, resulting 
in a decrease in the strength and elastic modulus of the alloy. 
Also, the amount of the cold rolling (driving force) required 
for the stress-induced martensitic transformation depends on 
(i) the compositions of the alloying elements, (ii) operating 
temperatures, and (iii) the microstructures of the alloy. [8] In 
addition, the α″ phase and initial β phase can be transformed 
into a finer β phase through the subsequent solution treatment, 
thereby achieving grain refinement.[9] Helth et al.[9] reduced the 
grain size of Ti–40Nb from 227 to 27 μm through cold rolling 
(86%) and subsequent heat treatment (800°C, 20 min), which 
significantly improved the strength of the alloy.
After the solution treatment, α phase precipitates from the 
supersaturated β matrix by aging treatment, but the grain size of 
the α precipitate is significantly affected by aging conditions. In 
general, overaging would result in the overgrowth of α precipi-
tates and reduce the α/β phase boundary areas, thereby reducing 
the strength of the alloy. However, the nano-sized α precipitates 
can enhance the strength of the alloy, but may not significantly 
increase the elastic modulus of the alloy.[10] Bahl et al.[11] per-
formed aging treatments on Ti–32Nb–2Sn alloy at 500–600°C 
for 6 h, and found that α phase precipitated along the grain 
boundaries of β phase, thus increasing the strength of the alloy. 
When aging at 600°C, the yield strength of Ti–32Nb–2Sn alloy 
decreased sharply due to the excessive growth of α-phase 
grains. Therefore, by cold rolling and appropriate aging treat-
ment, the nano-sized α phase can be precipitated in the metasta-
ble β-type Ti alloy, which is expected to achieve high strength, 
low elastic modulus, and good ductility of the alloy.
In this study, the structure and mechanical properties of the 
previously developed metastable Ti–13Nb–7Sn–4Mo (TNSM) 
alloy with low elastic modulus (54 GPa) [12] were investigated 
through cold rolling and aging treatment under various con-
ditions. Under appropriate aging treatments and the control 
of phase precipitation process, the strength of the alloy was 
improved and the optimal combination of strength and elas-
tic modulus was achieved. The phases, microstructure, and 
mechanical properties of TNSM alloy were also examined 
under various stages of treatment processes.
Experimental
The TNSM alloy was prepared from Ti (99.7 wt% pure), Nb 
(99.95 wt% pure), Sn (99.9 wt% pure), and Mo (99.95 wt% 
pure) using a commercial arc-melting vacuum pressure cast-
ing system (A-028, DAWN SHINE, Taiwan). The alloy ingots 
were flipped and re-melted five times for chemical homogene-
ity. The TNSM alloy was cold rolled using a double rolling

Results and discussion
Phase and microstructure
The XRD patterns of TNSM alloy under various conditions 
are displayed in Fig. 1 (a). TNSM-1 presented a single β 
phase, which was resulted from the large amounts of strong 
β-stabilizing elements (such as Nb and Mo) in the alloy. The 
90% cold-rolled sample (TNSM-2) exhibited a β + α″ dual-
phase structure, which was attributed to the stress-induced mar-
tensitic transformation during the cold-rolling process. During 
plastic deformation processes such as cold rolling or forging, 
α″ phase can be formed in metastable β-type Ti alloys. [14] 
When the [Mo]eq value of the alloy is between 5 and 11, the 
alloy tends to transform into α" phase through stress-induced 
transformation.[14] In this study, the [Mo]eq value of the TNSM 
alloy is 9.47. Also, TNSM-3 exhibited a β + α″ two-phase 
structure, which could be due to incomplete reverse transfor -
mation of α" phase during solution treatment. [15] All the aged 
alloys (TNSM-4 to TNSM-8) revealed a β + α″ + α three-phase 
structure. The precipitations of α phase can form in β-type Ti 
alloy after aging treatment above 500°C. [6,16,17] After aging 
between approximately 420 and 650°C, the stable α phase 
can be obtained from the transformation of the α″ phase or the 
metastable β phase. [18] However, it is not easy to confirm the 
existence of the α phase by XRD analysis, which may be due 
to the small grains (nanoscale) of the α phase or its insufficient 
volume fraction. A short time (< 120 min) aging can gener -
ally make the alloy precipitate nano-sized α phase.[10,19–21] The 
phase volume fractions of TNSM under different conditions 
obtained using XRD are shown in Table I.
The OM photos of TNSM alloy under various conditions are 
presented in Fig. 1(b)–(i). The as-cast TNSM alloy (TNSM-1) 
exhibited β equiaxed grains. After 90% cold rolling, the grains 
of TNSM-2 alloy were elongated along the rolling direction 
(horizontal). Furthermore, a small amount of fine needle-like α″ 
structure was observed in the OM photo (yellow arrow). After 
solution treatment, TNSM-3 alloy exhibited smaller equiaxed 
β grains than TNSM-1, which was attributed to the recrystal-
lization of the alloy during solution treatment. [9] After aging 
treatment, equiaxed β grains and a small amount of fine needle-
like α" structure (yellow arrow) could be observed in the OM 
images (TNSM-4 to TNSM-8). Similarly, α precipitates were 
not easily observed in OM due to its nano-sized particle size.

ing caused by cold rolling. However, the bending strength of

ment, which might be related to the elimination of the dis-

SEM observation
In this study, TNSM-8 with a β + α″ + α phase showed excellent 
mechanical performance, and its properties were highly related 
to the α and α″ phase. In order to confirm the existence of the 
α and α″ phase, the microstructure of TNSM-8 was observed 
using SEM (Fig. 3). The equiaxed grains were found in the 
alloy under low magnification image [Fig. 3(a) and (b)]. Addi-
tionally, the fine needle-like α″ phase located near the β phase 
boundary was observed under high magnification [Fig. 3(b), 
(c)]. At a much higher magnification [Fig. 3(d)], the α″ phase 
mainly nucleated at the β grain boundaries and grew into the 
grains. Under rapid cooling conditions (water quench), the α″ 
phase would nucleate heterogeneously at the β boundaries and 
appears as a thin lath-like morphology.[26,27] Also, the α″ phase 
formed by stress-induced transformation displayed a similar 
morphology. [28] In addition, fine α-phase precipitates were 
observed in the phase boundary between α″ and β, as shown in 
Fig. 3(d). Barriobero-Vila et al.[18] indicated that when aging 
treatment between 420 and 650°C, the precipitation of the α 
phase can form by heterogeneous nucleation at the β grain 
boundary or by the transformation from the α″ phase.
Conclusions
In this study, the structure and mechanical properties of 
Ti–13Nb–7Sn–4Mo were examined under 90% cold roll-
ing and the subsequent aging treatment at different condi-
tions (500, 550, and 600°C; 30, 60, and 120 min). The as-cast 
Ti–13Nb–7Sn–4Mo (TNSM-1) presented a single β phase. 
After 90% cold rolling, TNSM-2 alloy had a β + α″ structure. 
Also, TNSM-3 alloy showed a β + α″ phase after solution treat-
ment (850°C, 30 min), which may be due to the incomplete 
reverse transformation of α″ phase during solution treatment. 
All the aged alloys (TNSM-4 to TNSM-8) showed a β + α″ + α 
three-phase structure. The fine needle-like α″ phase nucleated 
heterogeneously mainly at the β grain boundaries and grew into 
the grains, whereas the granular α precipitates were observed in 
the phase boundary between α″ and β. Both TNSM-2 (solution 
treatment) and TNSM-8 (aging at 600°C for 30 min) had the 
highest bending-strength/elastic-modulus ratio (×1000) (23.3).
