Abstract 
 
The phenomenon of discontinuous precipitation (DP) leading to the formation of nano-rod FCC 
(γ) + L12 ( γ’) colonies, has been typically considered deleteri ous for mechanical properties. 
However, the present study shows clear evidence tha t substantially large fractions of FCC + 
nano-rod L12 microstructure within a thermo-mechanically processed high entropy alloys (HEA) 
or complex concentrated alloys (CCA) of composition Al 
0.2 Ti 0.3 Co 1.5 CrFeNi 1.5 , formed via 
recrystallization coupled with discontinuous precip itation, can lead to an excellent combination 
of room temperature strength and ductility. The ext ent of thermomechanical processing can be 
engineered to modify the phase transformation pathw ay from homogenous L1 2 precipitation to 
discontinuous L1 2 precipitation in the same HEA. This predominantly FCC + nano-rod L1 2 
microstructure exhibits a yield stress ~1.4 GPa, ul timate tensile strength ~1.6 GPa, and tensile 
ductility of ~14%, making it one of the best combin ations of room temperature tensile properties 
for FCC-based HEAs, that have been reported to date , as well as better than current generation

a temperature of 500°C in this alloy. However, at h igher temperatures (>550°C), the DP 
microstructures exhibit a rapid decline in strength and become less competitive as compared to 
microstructures consisting of homogeneously precipitated L1 2 within the FCC matrix.

1. Introduction 
 
Precipitation strengthened alloys have been the wor khorse for many structural applications, 
especially for high temperature applications [1,2]. In particular, the FCC+L1 
2 (γ/γ’) 
microstructure designed with Ni-base superalloys ha s been widely successful for critical 
applications such as turbine discs and turbine blad es [3]. However, the temperature capability of 
conventional superalloys appears to have been reach ed a limit and with it, the energy efficiency 
of gas turbines [4]. A radical change in alloy desi gn approach is thus necessary for making 
progress with high temperature materials. Yeh and C antor proposed a new alloy design strategy 
where multiple principal elements are alloyed toget her which led to the birth of High Entropy 
Alloys (HEAs) [5,6]. The term is later modified to Complex Concentrated Alloys (CCAs) to 
include alloys that cannot be technically categoriz ed as HEAs but are based on the same idea. 
The purpose of this new design strategy is to explo re the central regions of multi-component 
phase diagrams. 
 
Among the FCC based HEA/CCAs investigated, CoCrFeMn Ni and CoCrFeNi based alloy 
systems received much attention because of the inte resting mechanical properties [7,8]. The

addition of traditional L1 2 (γ’) formers like Al and Ti has resulted in precipitat ion strengthened 
HEA/CCAs that show promise for high temperature app lications [9,10]. However, L1 2 has not 
always been the equilibrium phase at high temperatu res with either Al or Ti addition. In an Al 
only containing alloy, Al 0.3 CoCrFeNi, Gwalani et.al. [11] reported a metastable L1 2 precipitation 
that exhibited approximately twice the tensile yiel d stress of single-phase condition of the same 
alloy. Thermomechanical processing of Al 0.3 CoCrFeNi has been exploited in the same study for 
precipitation of equilibrium phases. As a result, a triplex microstructure containing FCC, B2 and 
σ phases was produced that showed a good combination of tensile strength and ductility [11]. 
Addition of Ti to CoCrFeNi produced microstructures with topologically close-packed phases 
like (Cr,Fe) rich σ phase, (Ni,Ti) rich R phase and (Ti,Co) rich Laves phase [12]. The 
mechanical properties examined in compression altho ugh show increase in yield stress with Ti 
content, they exhibit poor ductility. 
 
Optimized addition of both Al and Ti, however, can yield the desired FCC+L1 
2 microstructure. 
He et.al. [10] reported an FCC+L1 2 microstructure in Al 2Ti 4(CoCrFeNi) 94 exhibiting a high yield 
stress of ~1005 MPa with 17% ductility. The microstructure consisted of fine recrystallized grain 
structure with cellular and spherical L1 2 precipitates. A yield stress of ~1028 MPa with ~46 % 
uniform elongation was obtained in Al 7Ti 7(CoFeNi) 86 alloy with a microstructure consisting of 
near spherical L1 2 particles in an FCC matrix [9]. Tsao et.al. [13] r eported a high entropy 
superalloy (HESA) of the composition Ni 47.9 Al 10.2 Co 16.9 Cr 7.4 Fe 8.9 Ti 5.8 Mo 0.9 Nb 1.2 W0.4 C0.4 (at%) 
with tensile, and creep properties at 982°C compara ble with some of the best Ni-base 
superalloys. Al, Ti and Hf containing CoCrFeNi based HESAs were reported by Zhang et.al [14] 
whose high temperature tensile properties were on p ar with Ni-base superalloys. The

discontinuous precipitation (DP) perform well at le ast at room temperature [10,20], most

FCC+L1 2 microstructure, although experimentally observed p reviously in Ni-base superalloys

Al 0.2 Ti 0.3 Co 1.5 CrFeNi 1.5 HEA. Thermomechanical processing was employed to s ystematically 
produce three microstructures with varying degrees of DP. The microstructures were 
characterized using SEM, TEM and APT and mechanical properties were measured via tensile 
testing at various temperatures.

Alloy with a nominal chemical composition Al

after heat treatments. However, quartz tube encapsu lation may have reduced the cooling rate of 
the samples. Microstructural characterization was d one using scanning electron microscopy or

at 200 kV/JEOL JEM-3000F HRTEM operating at 300 kV) was used to obtain crystallographic 
information from the phases present in the material . For analysis of compositional partitioning, 
atom probe tomography (APT) was performed using Cam eca LEAP 5000XS 3D Atom Probe

2. Results 
3.1 Thermodynamic modeling 
The solution thermodynamic CALPHAD based approach, using ThermoCalc (TCHEA4 
database) was adopted for alloy design starting fro m a base HEA/CCA composition of 
Co 
1.5 CrFeNi 1.5 . This base alloy composition was chosen to maximiz e the Al content for 
promoting intermetallic precipitation as reported p reviously [25]. The primary objective of the 
optimization process was to increase the phase frac tion and solvus temperature of the ordered 
L1 2 phase in order to increase precipitation strengthening in this HEA. However, the CALPHAD 
predictions indicate that simply increasing Al cont ent in the Co 1.5 CrFeNi 1.5 base alloy, increases 
the phase fraction of the B2 intermetallic phase. T he isopleth shown in Fig. 1(a) shows that with 
Co 1.5 CrFeNi 1.5 as the base alloy, the Al content can be raised up to 10 at.% while retaining the

The direct annealing approach adopted for the 60CRA and 90CRA conditions, results in

substantially affected by the heavy deformation cau sed by 60% thickness reduction. At the

bow-out in between the precipitates. The bowed-out boundaries further undergo thermally 
activated migration and the elastic coherency stresses at the interface with solute-enriched matrix

precipitation prior to the initiation of the recrys tallization process, then this region might not

4.4 Strengthening mechanisms at high temperatures 
The high temperature deformation mechanisms in prec ipitation strengthened alloys depend upon 
the volume fraction and the morphology of the stren gthening phase. The defect energies of these 
strengthening precipitates also play a crucial role in influencing the mechanical properties. 
Specifically, the absolute magnitudes of APB energy and the stacking fault energy decide the 
dominant shearing mode during the deformation. Thes e magnitudes are not only dependent on 
the composition of the ordered phase but also on the temperature of deformation. Further, in case 
of L1 
2 strengthened alloys, an increase in yield strength with temperature is well known. This is 
anomalous behavior in comparison with most material s where, a decrease in yield strength with 
temperature is experimentally observed [3,58,59]. In fact, such an anomalous increase in strength 
precisely makes them attractive for high temperatur e applications. The origin of such an 
anomalous increase resides in the anisotropy of the ordered L1 2 phase’s APB energy. Plastic 
deformation in these fcc based alloys, is accommoda ted by slip of ½<110> type dislocations 
along the {111} planes. While shearing the L1 2 ordered precipitates, pairs of these dislocations 
are coupled together, bound to each other by anti-p hase domains [60,61]. The spacing between 
these two dislocations is dependent on the magnitud e of the APB energy. Once inside the 
Journal Pre-proof

## Page 42

precipitate, either of these two dislocations may c ross-slip to the {100} type cube planes, owing 
to a lower APB energy associated with these planes. Since the second dislocation cannot break 
away independently, this leads to a sessile configu ration effectively “locking” the pair of 
dislocations, referred to as Kear-Wilsdorf locks [6 2,63]. The propensity of this cross-slip, 
increases with temperature, thus increasing the den sity of such locks which eventually leads to a 
higher yield strength. Therefore, the anisotropy in APB energy is critical for the anomalous 
increase in yield strength. Such an anomalous increase has not been observed in the present alloy 
which is indicative of a lesser anisotropy in APB e nergy along the cube planes ({100}) and 
octahedral planes ({111}). This anisotropy and the absolute value of the APB energy is 
extremely sensitive to the actual chemistry of the L1 2 ordered precipitate and thus tuning the 
precipitate chemistry suitably, offers an effective approach to engineer the deformation 
mechanism and in turn the mechanical properties of these alloys. The L1 2 ordered precipitates in 
the present Al 0.2 Ti 0.3 Co 1.5 CrFeNi 1.5 HEA, are of the type (Ni,Co,Fe) 3(Al,Ti,Cr) indicating 
complex multi-component site occupancies on the A a nd B sites of the A 3B L1 2 structure. Thus, 
the APB energy in these precipitates can be quite d ifferent as compared to typical γ’ precipitates 
in Ni-base superalloys. Additionally, in case of th e 90CRA condition the nano-rod morphology 
of ordered L1 2 precipitates is also expected to result in differe nces in the deformation 
mechanisms at both room and elevated temperatures, and needs further investigation. 
Nevertheless, the ordered L1 2 precipitates are stable at 500°C as predicted by t he phase diagram 
(refer to Fig. 1) and this work has shown that they can provide strengthening at elevated 
temperature while the HEA could still retain a good level of ductility. 
 
Conclusions 
Journal Pre-proof

## Page 43

1. Employing a thermo-mechanical treatment consisting of isothermal annealing subsequent 
to cold-rolling of an FCC-based HEA, leads to both a substantial grain refinement 
coupled with a change in the transformation pathway leading to discontinuous L1 2 
precipitation and a FCC + nano-rod L1 2 microstructure exhibiting a very high yield 
stress. 
2. Discontinuous precipitation of L1 2 is promoted in finer grains, since the FCC + nano-r od 
L1 2 colonies typically nucleate at grain boundaries. T he thermomechanical processing 
route adopted in this investigation, results in con current recrystallization of FCC grains 
and discontinuous precipitation of FCC+L1 2 nano-rod colonies at the FCC grain 
boundaries. 
3. Cold-rolling to 60% reduction in thickness, followe d by isothermal annealing at 
750°C/50hrs results in a partially recrystallized m icrostructure, comprising fine grained 
recrystallized regions promoting the discontinuous precipitation of FCC + nano-rod L1 2 
colonies and coarser non-recrystallized FCC grains exhibiting more spherical 
homogeneously nucleated L1 2 precipitates. 
4. Cold-rolling to 90% reduction in thickness, followe d by isothermal annealing at 
750°C/50hrs results in complete recrystallization of the HEA, with a uniform fine grained 
microstructure, consisting of discontinuously preci pitated FCC + nano-rod L1 2 colonies. 
This leads to a very high yield stress (~1396 MPa) and an ultimate tensile strength ~1586 
MPa, while maintaining a tensile ductility ~ 14%. 
5. High-temperature tensile testing revealed that the microstructure with discontinuous 
precipitation exhibited a yield strength of ~980 MP a at 500°C which is substantially 
higher than commonly used Ni-base superalloys. Howe ver, at temperatures >550°C, 
Journal Pre-proof

## Page 44

microstructure with homogeneously precipitated L1 2 perform better when compared to 
discontinuously precipitated L1 2. 
6. There was an excellent agreement between the compos itions of the L1 2 precipitates at 
750°C, predicted using solution thermodynamic CALPH AD models (ThermoCalc) and 
the experimentally measured APT values. Based on th e L1 2 composition, the site 
occupancies can be estimated to be of the type (Ni, Co,Fe) 3(Al,Ti,Cr). Composition of the 
remaining FCC matrix resembles that of a complex co ncentrated solid solution with 
nearly equiatomic Co, Cr and Fe, and marginally lower Ni content.

Highlights 
 
• The phase transformation pathway of L1 
2 precipitation can be engineered from

processing. 
• Discontinuous precipitation (DP) of FCC+ nano-rod L 1 2 colonies observed in an

to 500°C.
