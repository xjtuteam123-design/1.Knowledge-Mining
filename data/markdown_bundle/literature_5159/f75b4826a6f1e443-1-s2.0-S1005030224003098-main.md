Body-centered cubic Ti-Zr-Nb-Ta-Mo multi-principal element alloys (MPEAs), boasting a yield strength ex- 
ceeding one gigapascal, emerge as promising candidates for demanding structural applications. However, 
their limited tensile ductility at room temperature presents a signiﬁcant challenge to their processability 
and large-scale implementation. This study identiﬁes phase decomposition as a critical factor inﬂuencing 
the plasticity of these alloys. The microscale phase decomposition in these MPEAs during solidiﬁcation, 
driven by miscibility gaps, manifests as dendritic structures within grains. Closer examination reveals 
that the MPEAs with a pronounced thermodynamic propensity for phase decomposition are also suscep- 
tible to analogous phenomena at the atomic level. The atomic phase decomposition is characterized by 
the localized aggregation of some elements across nanometric domains, culminating in the establishment 
of short-range orderings (SROs). It is observed that phase decomposition for these MPEAs, occurring at 
both microscale and atomic scale, adheres to thermodynamic principles and can be predicted using the 
CALPHAD approach. The impact of phase decomposition on the plasticity of MPEAs fundamentally stems 
from the induced heterogeneities at three distinct levels: (1) Fluctuations in mechanical properties at the 
micron scale; (2) Variations in the strain ﬁeld at the atomic scale; (3) Bond polarization and bond index 
ﬂuctuations at the electronic scale. Consequently, the key to designing high-strength and high-plasticity 
MPEAs lies in maximizing lattice distortion while simultaneously minimizing the adverse effects of phase 
decomposition on the alloy’s plasticity (grain boundary cohesion). This research not only clariﬁes the 
mechanisms underpinning the ductile-to-brittle transition in high-strength Ti-Zr-Nb-Ta-Mo MPEAs but 
also offers crucial guidelines for developing advanced, high-performance alloys.

1. Introduction 
Designing alloys with multi-principal elements is a novel alloy- 
design strategy, which has been widely adopted in the search for 
alloys with superior mechanical properties [1–3] . The Ti-Zr-Nb- 
Ta-Mo MPEAs have recently drawn much attention for their po- 
tential application in the medical ﬁeld and extreme environments 
[4–8] . Although Ti-Zr-Nb-Ta-Mo MPEAs easily achieve high yield 
strengths close to or over 1 GPa at room temperature, one crucial 
bottleneck that hinders their processability and potential applica- 
tions is their low-to-no tensile ductility [9–12] .

In the quest to develop Ti-Zr-Nb-Ta-Mo MPEAs with an opti- 
mal balance of strength and ductility, extensive research effort s 
have been undertaken. Nakano et al. proposed the signiﬁcance of 
Mo equivalent (Moeq) and valence electron concentration (VEC) 
as key parameters for designing high-performance Ti-Zr-Nb-Ta- 
Mo MPEAs [13] . Leveraging this approach, they successfully engi- 
neered a Ti28.33 Zr28.33 Hf28.33 Nb6.74 Ta6.74 Mo1.55 (at.%) MPEA, charac- 
terized by its enhanced strength and ductility. Wang et al. [14] fur- 
ther contributed to this ﬁeld by establishing a correlation between 
the ductility of these alloys and their mixing enthalpy. They ob- 
served that alloys with a more negative overall mixing enthalpy 
tend to exhibit intrinsic brittleness. Additionally, it has been dis- 
covered that the ductility of Ti-Zr-Nb-Ta-Mo alloys can be sig- 
niﬁcantly improved by increasing the Ti content or reducing the 
Mo content [15] . In metallic materials, the macroscopic plasticity

manifested during actual deformation is determined not only by 
their intrinsic plasticity but is also signiﬁcantly inﬂuenced by mi- 
crostructural characteristics. The microstructure can alter the dis- 
tribution of stress, the movement of dislocations, and the behav- 
ior of grain boundaries, thereby impacting the overall macroscopic 
plasticity. Research on the plasticity of MPEAs has predominantly 
focused on the control and optimization of intrinsic plastic proper- 
ties. The solidiﬁcation microstructure, or as-cast microstructure, of 
MPEAs exerts a profound inﬂuence on their mechanical properties. 
However, the solidiﬁcation process of MPEAs is inherently com- 
plex due to the strong interactions among principal elements. Even 
minor changes in composition and cooling rate can substantially 
affect the solidiﬁcation microstructure. This is particularly perti- 
nent for high-strength Ti-Zr-Nb-Ta-Mo MPEAs, where any subtle 
microstructural alterations can have a pronounced impact on the 
alloy’s ductility [16] . 
A distinctive feature of the solidiﬁcation microstructure in Ti-Zr- 
Nb-Ta-Mo MPEAs is the occurrence of phase decomposition [ 17 , 18 ]. 
For instance, dendritic structures in as-cast Ti-Zr-Nb-Ta-Mo MPEAs 
are manifestations of phase decomposition [ 16 , 19 , 20 ], yet their role 
in the initial mechanical properties is understudied [ 16 , 21-23 ]. The 
dendritic structure can exert a certain impact on the initial me- 
chanical properties of the alloys [ 24 , 25 ]. Yao et al. [26] found that 
the ductile inter-dendritic regions enriched Ti and Nb are more 
prone to deform in response to the applied stress and can bear 
more strains than the strong and brittle dendrite, which leads to 
an enhancement of the ductility of the Tix NbMoTaW. Chen et al. 
[27] pronounced that the large discrepancy in the dendrite and 
inter-dendritic formed localized complex stress ﬁelds in the inter- 
dendritic, also made the stress distribution scatter, resulting in 
better plasticity in the (TiVCr)100- x Wx HEAs. Consequently, it be- 
comes evident that investigating the impact of phase separation 
microstructures on the mechanical properties of Ti-Zr-Nb-Ta-Mo 
MPEAs is of paramount importance. However, current research in 
this area remains somewhat superﬁcial, lacking an in-depth explo- 
ration of the relationship between phase decomposition and the 
mechanical properties of these MPEAs from perspectives such as 
lattice and atomic scales. 
Here, three presentative MPEAs (Ti35 Zr35 Nb15 Ta10 Mo5 (N15T10), 
Ti35 Zr35 Nb10 Ta15 Mo5 (N10T15), and Ti32.5 Zr32.5 Nb15 Ta15 Mo5 
(N15T15) with VEC < 4.5, featuring with high-strength and varying 
degrees of phase decomposition, were designed. Our objective is 
to undertake a comprehensive analysis of the microstructure and 
deformation mechanisms in these MPEAs, speciﬁcally focusing on 
unraveling the complex interplay between phase decomposition 
and their mechanical properties. This research is intended to 
provide pivotal insights for the design of alloys with superior 
mechanical performance. 
2. Experimental and computational details 
2.1. Materials and Methods 
High-purity metal billets ( > 99.95 wt.%) were used to prepare 
the Ti-Zr-Nb-Ta-Mo multi-principal element alloys (MPEAs). The 
billets were arc melting in an argon atmosphere, followed by drop- 
casting of the melt into a water-cooled copper mold (10 mm × 10 
mm × 50 mm). 
X-ray diffraction (XRD, RIGAKU-Ultima-IV) was conducted to 
identify the phase compositions of the alloys. The microstructures, 
elemental distributions, and tensile fracture morphologies at the 
mesoscale were meticulously characterized using a Zeiss Supra 
55 ﬁeld emission scanning electron microscope (SEM) equipped 
with Oxford electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) and energy- 
dispersive spectrometer (EDS) systems. The step size for EBSD test- 
ing was 0.87 or 0.06 μm. The EBSD samples underwent a se- 
ries of meticulous preparation steps, including polishing with 600, 
10 0 0, and 20 0 0-grit SiC papers, followed by electro-polishing us- 
ing HClO4 , CH3 (CH2 )3 OH, and CH3 OH solutions at 35 V and -20 °C 
for 90 s. The atomic-level characterizations of the alloys were con- 
ducted using an aberration-corrected transmission electron micro- 
scope (TEM), speciﬁcally the FEI Titan Cube 80-300, operating at 
300 kV. To prepare the TEM specimens, the samples were initially 
mechanically ground to a thickness of 50 μm and subsequently 
ion milled using a Gatan 695. The hardness was measured by a 
nano-indenter (Keysight G200, Agilent Technology, USA) with an 
in-depth control (20 0 0 nm). The loading time was maintained at 
15 s and the maximum load was maintained during 10 s for each 
sample. One hundred indents (10 × 10 arrays) were collected for 
each sample to give an average value. For each MPEA, a minimum 
of two parallel samples were tested to ensure adequate statistical 
data collection in the areas of interest. The tensile yield strength, 
Young’s modulus, and elongation of the alloys were obtained at 
room temperature on an MTS universal electronic tensile testing 
machine. The strain rate of the tensile test was set as 1 × 10−3 s−1 . 
The tensile samples were cut into dog-bone-shaped, in which the 
gauge length was set as 15 mm and the cross-section was 2.0 mm 
× 4.0 mm. At least four four samples were tested for each compo- 
nential condition to ensure reproducibility and statistics. The phase 
compositions and thermodynamic properties of the Ti-Zr-Nb-Ta-Mo 
MPEAs were calculated through the phenomenological Calculation 
of Phase Diagrams (CALPHAD) approach, using PANDAT software 
with the PanHEA database. 
2.2. First-principles simulation 
All ﬁrst-principles calculations were conducted based on den- 
sity functional theory (DFT), which was implemented in the Vi- 
enna ab initio simulation package (VASP) code [28] . To describe 
the electronic exchange–correlation energy, the generalized gradi- 
ent approximation of Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (GGA-PBE) [29] and 
the projector augmented-wave (PAW) [ 30 , 31 ] method was adopted. 
The DFT calculations involved the construction of two types of 
supercells, corresponding respectively to the bulk and the grain 
boundary (GB) portions of the N15T15 alloy. For the bulk models, 
each conﬁguration has 120 atoms in the supercell of 3 × 4 × 5 bcc 
unit cells. For the GB models, the /Sigma15(310)[001] GB with 160 atoms 
was used to mimic the GB. The kinetic energy cutoff of 450 eV was 
used with the total energy convergence of 10−5 eV was adopted. 
For the 3 × 4 × 5 cubic bulk supercell, the optimal supercell vol- 
umes, i.e., equilibrium lattice constants were found by varying the 
lattice constant and ﬁnding the energy minimum. Similarly, for the 
GB, the cell length was optimized by stretching/compressing the 
supercell in GB’s normal direction and ﬁnding the energy mini- 
mum, respectively. The 3 × 2 × 2 and 2 × 2 × 2 MonkhorstPack 
k-point mesh was used for the bulk and GB supercells, respectively. 
The atomic positions were allowed to relax until the forces on each 
atom were less than 0.02 eV/˚A while the shape and volume of the 
supercell were ﬁxed during relaxation. COBI and ICOBI for selected 
supercells were calculated using the computer program LOBSTER 
(Local Orbital Basis Suite Towards ElectronicStructure Reconstruc- 
tion) [32] based on the self-consistent electronic wavefunction cal- 
culated with VASP.

Fig. 1 (a–c) depicts backscattered electron (BSE) scanning elec-

principal element alloys (MPEAs), exhibiting characteristic den- 
dritic (light-gray, DR) arms and inter-dendritic (dark-gray, IDR) 
phases in the as-cast state. The N15T10 and N10T15 alloys, hav- 
ing identical Ti and Zr contents and differing only in the ratios of 
Nb to Ta, have similar dendritic morphology, shown in Fig. 1 (a, b). 
In contrast, the N15T15 alloy ( Fig. 1 (c)), with higher total (Nb, Ta) 
and lower (Ti, Zr) contents, demonstrates a higher proportion of 
inter-dendritic phases. XRD analysis conﬁrmed all samples are BCC 
structures ( Fig. 1 (d)). However, a gradual broadening and splitting 
of XRD peaks from N15T10 to N15T15 was observed ( Fig. 1 (e)), 
indicating increasing crystallographic heterogeneity among these 
MPEAs. Elemental analysis of DR arms and IDR regions compared 
to nominal composition (NC) is shown in Table 1 . The DR arms and 
IDR regions of the three samples exhibit compositional deviations 
from their NC to varying extents. The elemental distribution re- 
vealed enrichment of high atomic number and high melting point 
elements (i.e., Nb, Ta, Mo) in the DR arms, while lower atomic 
number and melting point elements (i.e., Ti, Zr) were predomi- 
nantly found in the IDR regions. This composition pattern aligns 
with the phase decomposition characteristics of the Ti-Zr-Nb-Ta- 
Mo alloy system. It implies that the dendritic structures within the 
grains of these alloys are primarily a result of phase decomposition 
phenomena. Fluctuations in the composition of the DR arms and 
IDR regions have led to signiﬁcant variances in the valence electron 
concentration (VEC) within these areas. Notably, in the N15T15 al- 
loy, the VEC in the DR arms exceeds 4.5, which adversely affects 
its plastic deformation capabilities [34] . Partition coeﬃcients, K , 
are used to quantify element segregation, as presented in Fig. 1 (f).

To further elucidate the inﬂuence of phase decomposition on 
the deformation behavior of Ti-Zr-Nb-Ta-Mo MPEAs, we conducted 
EBSD analysis on the tensile fracture microstructures of N15T10, 
N10T15, and N15T15 alloys. Fig. 4 (a–c) shows the EBSD-IPF map 
of the samples after fracture. The crystal structures were still in 
the BCC lattice, and no stress-induced phase transition occurred in 
the three alloys during tensile deformation. Note that a consider- 
able amount of kink bands (misorientation of the parallel bands 
was in the range of 37 °-46 °) were formed in the N15T10 alloy af- 
ter deformation. Kink bands are considered the third type of de- 
formation besides uniform dislocation slip and deformation twin- 
ning. In this case, the formation of kink bands is considered to al- 
leviate stress concentration through localized dislocation-mediated 
rotation and reduce the likelihood of fracture, thereby enhancing 
ductility without compromising strength. With increasing the Ta to 
Nb ratio (N15T10 to N10T15), the amount of kink bands has signif- 
icantly decreased. Meanwhile, the kink band vanished in N15T15 
alloy with higher Nb or Ta content. Fig. 4 (d–f) presents the ker- 
nel average misorientation (KAM) maps and corresponding ele- 
ment distributions. The KAM maps depicted the density of ge- 
ometrically necessary dislocations, and their values at the inter- 
dendritic regions (high Zr content) were higher than the dendritic 
arms (high Ta content), indicating that dislocations mainly accu- 
mulated at these places. Almost all grain boundaries are located in 
the inter-dendritic regions. Note that the N15T10 alloy has better 
deformation uniformity than that of the other two alloys, which 
tally with its weaker segregation and a narrow mechanical discrep- 
ancy between dendritic and inter-dendritic. In the case of N15T15 
alloy, the KAM maps at the dendrites and inter-dendritic region 
are minimal, indicating that the grains were hardly involved in de- 
formation during the tensile process, with deformation primarily 
concentrated in the grain boundary regions. Furthermore, notice- 
able intergranular cracking can be observed in N15T15 alloy, even 
with deformation levels of less than 2%. In the case of the N15T10 
and N10T15 alloys, even in the regions with the highest stress 
along the grain boundaries, there were no noticeable transgranu- 
lar cracks observed.

orientations. The N15T10 and N10T15 alloys, due to severe plas-

4.1. The origin of the phase decomposition 
Investigations into the microstructural evolution of the Ti-Zr- 
Nb-Ta-Mo multi-principal element alloys (MPEAs) were advanced 
through the execution of Calculation of Phase Diagrams (CALPHAD) 
for the N15T10, N10T15, and N15T15 MPEAs. Fig. 7 presents equi- 
librium phase diagrams of three samples. A board miscibility gap 
of these alloys led to the BCC phase decomposition into two BCC 
phases (BCC1 + BCC2) at intermediate temperatures, as shown in 
Fig. 7 (a). The N15T15 and N10T15 alloys exhibit much larger tem- 
perature ranges for immiscibility compared to N15T10, indicating 
phase decomposition is more prone to occur in MPEAs with higher 
Ta content. Additionally, variations in the Nb/Ta content also inﬂu- 
ence the concentrations of the BCC1 and BCC2 phases throughout 
the entire phase decomposition temperature range. The N15T15 al- 
loy with higher (Nb, Ta)-content consistently exhibited a higher 
concentration of the BCC1 phase within the corresponding tem- 
perature range. According to the XRD and SEM results ( Fig. 1 (a–
d)), it can be observed that the phase diagram calculations are 
consistent with the as-cast microstructure of the N15T10, N10T15, 
and N15T15 MPEAs. Hence, we can depict a schematic diagram of 
continuous cooling transformation (CCT) to reveal the as-cast mi- 
crostructure with these three MPEAs, as shown in Fig. 7 (b). The 
CCT curves were drawn according to microstructure characteriza- 
tion results ( Fig. 1 and Table 1 ) and CALPHAD simulation ( Fig. 7 (a)). 
It can be inferred that the as-cast MPEAs in this work solidiﬁed 
in a water-cooled copper crucible were subjected to phase de- 
composition (BCC1 + BCC2 phases), indicated by the red cool-

of the three alloys, we meticulously calculated the temperature- 
dependent atomic fraction for each constituent element in both 
BCC1 ( Fig. 7 (c)) and BCC2 ( Fig. 7 (d)) phases. The simulations re- 
veal a pronounced enrichment of Nb and Ta in the BCC1 phase, 
contrasted by a signiﬁcant accumulation of Ti and Zr in the BCC2

with the compositions derived from experimental observations

The formation of chemical short-range orderings (SROs) is a 
multifaceted process inﬂuenced by composition, thermodynamics, 
and kinetics [36] . In the Ti-Zr-Nb-Ta-Mo MPEAs, the SROs can 
be conceptualized as atomic-scale phase decompositions [5] . Their 
emergence aligns with the principles of thermodynamic phase di- 
agrams and the laws of mixing enthalpy [37] , which elucidates the 
easier observation of SROs in the N15T15 alloy. Herein, we have 
employed density functional theory (DFT)-based ﬁrst-principles 
calculations to decipher the relationship between the SROs and 
the total energy in the N15T15 alloy. A quasi-random bulk super- 
cell (denoted as Bulk-RD) alongside four bulk supercells exhibiting 
SROs, labeled Bulk-SRO x (where x ranges from 1 to 4), were sys- 
tematically constructed for the study. These supercells mirror the 
elemental composition found in the inter-dendritic (IDR) regions 
(i.e., in the vicinity of the grain boundaries) of the N15T15 alloy, 
as detailed in Table 1 . The Bulk-RD supercell utilized here was se- 
lected as the lowest energy conﬁguration from a pool of 30 ran- 
dom structures. Drawing on the STEM-HAADF images ( Fig. 6 ) and 
phase diagram calculations ( Fig. 7 ), we infer that the manifesta- 
tion of SRO in the N15T15 MPEA is attributable to the segregation 
of (Ti, Zr) and (Nb, Ta). The mixing enthalpy between elements is 
a crucial predictor of SROs. Positive mixing enthalpy indicates en- 
dothermic mixing, often reﬂecting repulsion or incompatibility be- 
tween elements, while negative values signify exothermic mixing 
due to attractive interactions. A mixing enthalpy near zero sug- 
gests neither signiﬁcant energy release nor absorption during mix- 
ing, implying neutral interactions where the outcome is inﬂuenced 
by external factors like temperature and pressure. It was observed 
that the mixing enthalpy values for the Ti-Zr and Nb-Ta atomic 
pairs are essentially neutral, which is substantially more negative 
compared to the Ti-Nb ( + 2 kJ/mol), Ti-Ta ( + 1 kJ/mol), Zr-Nb ( + 4 
kJ/mol), and Zr-Ta ( + 3 kJ/mol) pairs. Given the predominantly neg- 
ative mixing enthalpies between Mo and other elements and its 
relatively lower concentration in the model, the segregation of Mo 
is not primarily considered in this context. Therefore, the forma- 
tion of (Ti, Zr)-rich and (Nb, Ta)-rich regions is thermodynamically 
favored. The determination of the orderliness within the elemen- 
tal segregation structures for identical elements remains elusive. 
Consequently, we have considered four plausible SRO atomic con- 
ﬁgurations. The corresponding supercells for these atomic conﬁgu- 
rations are illustrated in Fig. 8 (a) and the Warren-Cowley SRO pa- 
rameters for key atomic pairs are delineated in Table 3 . Conﬁgura- 
tions Bulk-SRO1 to Bulk-SRO3 are characterized by varying extents 
of (Nb, Ta)-SRO. Both Bulk-SRO3 and Bulk-SRO4 conﬁgurations ex- 
hibit similar levels of (Nb, Ta)-SRO, but Bulk-SRO4 also shows an 
increased ordering in Ti-Ti and Zr-Zr atomic pairs. From the calcu- 
lated total energies of ﬁve bulk models with different atomic con- 
ﬁgurations, it is evident that the formation of SRO contributes to 
the reduction of system energy. Moreover, a higher degree of over- 
all order in the supercells correlates with lower system energy, as 
demonstrated in Fig. 8 (b). Consequently, we can ascertain that the 
chemically short-range ordered structure in the N15T15 alloy rep- 
resents a relatively stable conﬁguration, its formation being syner- 
gistically driven by thermodynamic and electronic behavior.

4.3. The relationship between phase decomposition and plasticity 
Elucidating the correlation between plasticity and phase de- 
composition behavior in high-strength Ti-Zr-Nb-Ta-Mo MPEAs can 
offer vital guidance for the design of advanced metallic materials. 
In this study, we observed the ductile-brittle transition in these al- 
loys, notably marked by a drastic reduction in plasticity with the 
addition of 5 at.% Nb/Ta (i.e., N15T15). The emergence of this phe- 
nomenon is closely associated with severe phase decomposition 
within the alloy. Firstly, the brittle N15T15 alloy exhibits a more 
pronounced phase decomposition tendency compared to the other 
two alloys with better plasticity. The signiﬁcant compositional ﬂuc- 
tuations within the grains at the micrometer scale, leading to high 
VEC and low plastic deformation capability in the dendritic regions 
for the N15T15 alloy (as shown in Fig. 2 ). During tensile testing, 
stress concentration was notably observed at the grain boundaries 
of this alloy ( Fig. 4 ), which is likely to cause early-stage cracking 
at these sites. Secondly, noticeable atomic-scale phase decomposi- 
tion (i.e., SROs) occurs in the regions surrounding the grain bound- 
aries of the N15T15 alloy. The SROs intensify the ﬂuctuations in 
the stress ﬁeld around the grain boundaries, signiﬁcantly hinder- 
ing dislocation movement and further accelerating the initiation 
and propagation of cracks along the grain boundaries [ 36 , 44 , 45 ]. 
However, it must be acknowledged that current atomic-scale anal- 
yses are still insuﬃcient to comprehensively explain the effect of 
SROs on the grain boundary strength of the N15T15 alloy. There- 
fore, ﬁrst-principles calculations are employed to elucidate the cor- 
relation mechanism between SROs and grain boundary embrittle- 
ment in the N15T15 alloy. 
Three representative /Sigma15(310)[001] grain boundary (GB) models 
with different atomic conﬁgurations were constructed, as depicted 
in Fig. 10 . The GB-RD model features atoms randomly distributed, 
selected as a more stable conﬁguration after screening a series of 
GB models created by SQS technique [46] . In the GB-SRO1 model, 
the characteristic is the absence of (Nb-Ta)-SRO at the grain bound- 
ary (three atomic layers away from the interface), resulting in an 
enrichment of Zr and Ti elements at the GB layer. Conversely, the 
GB-SRO2 model is characterized by the presence of (Nb-Ta)-SRO at 
the grain boundary, leading to a concentration of Nb and Ta ele- 
ments at the grain boundary cross-section. The work of separation 
( Wsep ) of a grain boundary, also interpreted as the GB’s ideal frac- 
ture strength, is deﬁned as the energy required for the separation, 
i.e., intergranular fracture, of a GB into two free surfaces in the ab- 
sence of other crystal defects. This metric is utilized to evaluate 
the inﬂuence of GB segregation of alloying elements on the ideal 
fracture strength of the GB. Utilizing the classic Rice-Wang model 
[47] , the work of separation of a GB is calculated as follows, 
Wsep = (EFS 1 +EFS 2 −EGB ) / 2 S (5) 
where EFS1 and EFS2 are the total energies of two newly created 
free surfaces after the GB fracture, the scaling factor 1/2 means 
that two GBs were broken in the GB supercell and S represents 
the area of free surfaces, thus four free surfaces were generated 
associated with the fracture. The total energies of the three GB 
models shown in Fig. 10 (b) reveal that the GB-SRO1 model, with 
Ti and Zr enrichment at the GB, has the lowest energy. In con- 
trast, the GB-SRO2 model, characterized by the presence of (Nb- 
Ta)-SROs at the GB, exhibits the highest system energy. This sug- 
gests that the atomic conﬁguration of GB-SRO1 is relatively stable 
and more likely to exist stably in actual crystals. As depicted in 
Fig. 10 (c), the grain boundary work of separation for the randomly 
distributed atoms in the GB-RD model is approximately 3.89 J/m ². 
The other two GB models with SROs show signiﬁcant differences 
in grain boundary strength. Speciﬁcally, the work of separation for 
the GB-SRO1 model is about 3.44 J/m ², indicating a decrease in 
grain boundary fracture strength by approximately 12% compared 
to the GB-RD model. For the GB-SRO2 model, the work of separa- 
tion is approximately 4.01 J/m ², indicating an increase in fracture 
strength by about 3% compared to the GB-RD model. Therefore, it 
can be inferred that the enrichment of Ti and Zr (with a greater 
number of Zr) at the grain boundaries of the N15T15 alloy leads to 
a reduction in its grain boundary cohesive strength.

5. Conclusions 
The microstructure, mechanical properties, and ductile-brittle 
transition mechanism of three Ti-Zr-Nb-Ta-Mo multi-principal el- 
ement alloys (MPEAs) with VEC < 4.5, featuring high-strength and 
varying degrees of phase decomposition, were investigated. The 
main ﬁndings are summarized as follows: 
(1) Micrometer Phase Decomposition: The Ti-Zr-Nb-Ta-Mo 
MPEAs inherently undergo phase separation during the 
solidiﬁcation process due to existing miscibility gaps. This 
phenomenon is prominently observed in dendritic mi- 
crostructures within the grain matrix. Notably, the increase 
of the Nb and Ta can exacerbate the phase decomposi- 
tion effect, especially with Ta, resulting in more marked 
compositional variances within the grain structures. 
(2) Atomic Scale Phase Decomposition: The phase decomposi- 
tion in Ti-Zr-Nb-Ta-Mo MPEAs occurs even at the atomic 
scale, predominantly as nanoscale elemental clustering 
(chemical short-range orderings, SROs). The formation of 
this phase decomposition aligns with the thermodynamic 
phase diagram rules, with a greater tendency for phase de- 
composition correlating with the emergence of SROs. More- 
over, ﬁrst-principles calculations indicate that the presence 
of SROs lowers the system’s energy. 
(3) Impact on Mechanical Properties: Phase decomposition 
at both micrometer and atomic scales signiﬁcantly im- 
pacts the mechanical properties of Ti-Zr-Nb-Ta-Mo MPEAs. 
Micrometer-scale decomposition leads to plastic deformation 
heterogeneity within the grains, causing stress concentration 
at grain boundaries. Atomic scale decomposition involves

tions in MPEAs. 
(4) Effects of Bond Strength: Enrichment of Ti and Zr ele- 
ments (particularly Zr) in certain regions results in increased 
atomic pair bond lengths, reduced electronic kinetic energy 
density, and a decrease in the ICOBI. Conversely, enrichment 
with Nb, Ta, and Mo elements leads to a decrease in the 
overall bond lengths, an enhanced metallic nature of bonds, 
and an increase in ICOBI. The bond length of atomic pairs 
is closely associated with the strength of the bond. Conse- 
quently, an appropriate concentration of Nb, Ta, and Mo ele- 
ments at grain boundaries is advantageous for enhancing the 
interfacial bonding strength, thereby reducing the likelihood 
of grain boundary embrittlement.
