alloys

1. Introduction
Refractory metal niobium (Nb) and its alloys have shown high
corrosion resistance [1] and good biocompatibility both in vitro [2]
and in vivo [3–6]. For instance, immersion of alkali treated niobium
in simulated body ﬂuid can induce bonelike apatite formation on
its surface [2]. A histomorphometric study of commercially pure
(CP) Nb and Ti implants in rabbit bone shows a more ¨positive
biocompatibility ¨of CP Nb in comparison to the CP Ti [3]. In vivo
implantation of Nb in both soft and hard tissues also shows that
Nb induces excellent osteogenesis [4,5] and osteointegration [6].
In addition, compared with stainless steel (magnetic susceptibility
/H9273 = ((3520–6700)×10−6) and cobalt alloys (/H9273 = ∼ 250) with high /H9273,
Nb-based alloys have low /H9273 (Nb = 237 × 10−6) and are magnetic res-
onance imaging (MRI)-compatible [1,7–9], which can thus avoid or
reduce device movement, heating, and development of an artefact
or distortion on the collected image. In addition, Nb is character-

is desirable for avoiding stress shielding effects [1,10].
Anti-infection is another critical requirement for orthopedic
and dental implants because implant-associated infection has been
one of the most serious complications for implant failure and
patient mortality [11]. Among the metallic elements, silver (Ag)
has demonstrated strong antibacterial activity [12–14]. In addi-
tion, the face-centered cubic (fcc) Ag with low shear strength has
also frequently been used as solid lubricant to reduce friction [15].
Therefore, Nb incorporated with a small amount of Ag, the resulting
Nb-Ag alloy, is expected to have a unique combination of adequate
mechanical properties, good corrosion and wear resistance, and
excellent biocompatibility and antibacterial activity, and might be
a promising candidate for biomedical implants.
Referring to the equilibrium phase diagram of Nb-Ag binary
system [16], Nb and Ag are highly immiscible due to large positive
enthalpy of mixing. Moreover, Nb has a high melting point of 2468
◦C and is ∼ 300 ◦C higher than the boiling point of Ag (2162 ◦C),
which makes it impossible to fabricate Nb-Ag binary alloys by
traditional melting method. Severe plastic deformation (SPD) via
high energy

nanometers and the obtained nanocrystalline ball-milled powders

the basis of detailed microstructure characterization, we then sys-
tematically evaluated the mechanical properties, wear resistance,
corrosion behavior, in vitro and in vivo antibacterial activity and bio-
compatibility to examine the feasibility of Nb-Ag two-phase alloys
for dental and orthopedic implant application.
2. Materials and methods
2.1. Alloy fabrication
Commercially pure Nb (1–5 /H9262m, 99.99 %, Alfa Aesar) and Ag (1–3
/H9262m, 99.99 %, Alfa Aesar) powders with nominal compositions of
Nb-1 at.% Ag and Nb-5 at.% Ag were used as the starting materials.
The powders were subjected to high-energy ball milling using a
SPEX 8000D mill at ambient temperature in an argon glove box for
12 h, respectively. Ball milling forces the atomic mixing of Nb and
Ag into a solid solution [23]. The ball-milled powders were then
compacted by SPS (SPS-211Lx, Fujidempa Kogyo. Co., ltd, Japan)
into bulk cylinders at 900 ◦C and under a pressure of 60 MPa in
vacuum. Pure Nb was also fabricated using the same procedure and
used as control.
2.2. Microstructure characterization
The phase of the obtained samples was characterized by X-ray
diffraction (XRD) on a Rigaku Smartlab-9 kW (Rigaku, Tokyo, Japan)
diffractometer in the 2/H9258 range of 30–85◦ using Cu-K/H9251 radiation (/H9261
=1.54 Å, 45 kV, 200 mA). The microstructure of the samples were
investigated by transmission electron microscopy (TEM), high res-
olution TEM (HRTEM), and high angle annular dark ﬁeld scanning
TEM (HAADF-STEM) using an FEI Tecnai F30 transmission electron
microscope operated at 300 kV. The TEM samples were prepared
by focus ion beam (FIB; Helios Nano LabTM 600i, FEI, USA) using the
standard lift-out technique. The corroded surface morphology and
composition were analyzed using scanning electron microscopy
(SEM; MIRA 3, TESCAN, Czech Republic) coupled with energy dis-
persive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS). The corrosion products were also
analyzed by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS; Thermo Scien-
tiﬁc K-alpha, Thermo Fisher Scientiﬁc, UK) with a monochromatic
Al K/H9251 source.
2.3. Mechanical properties
Cylindrical specimens with /Phi13 mm × 6 mm for compression and
pin-on-disk wear tests were cut using electrical discharge machin-
ing. Uniaxial quasi-static compression tests were performed with a
strain rate of 5 × 10−4 s-1 at ambient temperature using a universal
testing machine (CMT5105, MTS, China). The micro-hardness of the
as-sintered samples was measured using a Vickers diamond pyra-
midal indenter (HXD-1000TMC/LCD, Shanghai Taiming Optical
Instrument Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) under a load of 300 gf for 10 s.
2.4. Pin-on-disk wear tests

material. Prior to wear tests, both pin and contacting disk surfaces
were ﬁrst mechanically grinded with SiC paper down to 1200 grit

3.3.
 Tribological behavior
Fig. 3(c) shows the coefﬁcients of friction (CoFs) of Nb and Nb-
Ag alloys sliding against SS440C with a load of 5 N and velocity of
0.1 m/s as a function of sliding distance. The tribo-systems reach
steady-state wear at the sliding distance of approximately 150 m
and the average CoFs of Nb, Nb-1Ag and Nb-5Ag were measured
to be 0.85, 0.87 and 0.80, respectively. The measured wear rates of

1Ag alloy shows a higher CoF and wear rate, but the Nb-5Ag shows
apparently reduced CoF and wear rate.
3.4. Electrochemical corrosion behavior
Fig. 4(a) shows the potentiodynamic polarization curves of the
three groups of samples. The extracted corrosion current den-
sity (icorr ) from Tafel plots shows that the icorr decreases with the
increasing Ag content. The icorr of Nb-1Ag (6.46 × 10−7 A/cm2) is an
order of magnitude lower than that of pure Nb (5.15 × 10−6 A/cm2),
while the icorr of Nb-5Ag (2.26 × 10−7 A/cm2) is only about 30 % of
that of Nb-1Ag. This suggests that the Nb-5Ag alloy has the lowest
corrosion rate among these three groups of samples. The ﬂuctua-
tion in the anodic region of Nb-5Ag alloy suggests the formation
of pitting and the repassivation process on the surface [24,25]. An
obvious activation-passivation process was also observed in the
anodic region, which indicates that the Nb-5Ag alloy could easily
form a dense protective ﬁlm when immersed in PBS. Overall, Nb-
5Ag alloy has highest corrosion resistance compared to pure Nb and
Nb-1Ag alloy.

3.5. Corroded surface morphology and composition
Fig. 5 shows the surface morphology of pure Nb and Nb-Ag
alloy before and after polarization measurements. The number of
micropores apparently reduced with the increasing content of Ag,
indicating that the addition of Ag could enhance the density of
the as-sintered samples. After polarization test, the surface of pure
Nb (Fig. 5(a)2) shows not much difference from the as-sintered
sample (Fig. 5(a)1). The corrosion process is mainly localized in
the micropores [29]. Corrosion products were found around some
micropores (as marked by the arrows in Fig. 5(a)2), conﬁrming
that the chemical reactions have taken place inside the microp-
ores. High magniﬁcation SEM image taken from the region away
from the micropores (Fig. 5(a)3) shows uniform corrosion, except
for some nanopores generated during the sintering process. The Nb-
1Ag alloy also shows similar surface morphology before (Fig. 5(b)1)
and after polarization test (Fig. 5(b)2), while the high magniﬁcation
SEM image (Fig. 5(b)3) reveals that a small number of newly formed
round corrosion pits and rod-like inherently micropores. The corro-
sion pits were generated due to the galvanic corrosion between the
Nb phase and Ag phase. Thus, the corrosion mechanism of Nb-1Ag
alloy combines the pore corrosion and the galvanic corrosion away
from the micropores. As for the Nb-5Ag alloy, in contrast to the sur-
face of as-sintered sample with only a few micropores (Fig. 5(c)1),
a large number of newly formed corrosion pits were located on the
surface after polarization test (Fig. 5(c)2). High magniﬁcation SEM

4. Discussion
The refractory metal Nb has demonstrated great potential for
implants [1] both in vitro [2] and in vivo [3–6] due to its good bio-
compatibility and MRI-compatibility, but Nb often has low strength
in conventional grain size [22] and lacks of antibacterial ability. In
order to overcome these limitations, a novel nanostructured Nb-Ag
two-phase alloys with a minor amount of antibacterial element Ag
has been fabricated via a combination of high-energy ball milling
and SPS. Using pure Nb as a control, we have demonstrated that
the Nb-5Ag has signiﬁcantly enhanced mechanical properties, fric-
tion and wear performance, corrosion resistance, and antibacterial
ability. In vitro and in vivo evaluations also demonstrate that the
fabricated Nb-5Ag alloy has good biocompatibility. These combined
properties would enable the present Nb-5Ag alloy a promising can-
didate for dental and orthopedic implants.

4.3. Wear mechanism
Under unlubricated condition, wear resistance empirically cor-
relates with the hardness of the metallic material, as captured by
Archard equation [37], which states that the harder the material,
the higher the wear resistance, and the lower the wear rate. How-
ever, for two-phase alloys, Archard scaling frequently fails because
wear and friction performance also depend on the subsurface
microstructural evolution during wear. As found in our previous
studies [38,39], wear-induced SPD near the sliding surface can
lead to the spontaneous formation of self-organized nanolayered
microstructure, which can signiﬁcantly improve wear resistance
[38], but the formation of such nanolayered structure depends on
the initial size of Ag precipitates. In alloys with large Ag precipi-
tates, despite the reduction of hardness, SPD during sliding wear
results in the formation of stable alternating nanolayered struc-
ture which leads to low wear rate. In contrast, in the alloys with
small Ag precipitates, SPD during wear results in the dissolution
of the Ag precipitates, and forces the formation of supersaturated
solid solutions, with equiaxed nanograins below the sliding sur-
face, and thus leads to high wear rate [38,39]. In this study, with
the low Ag concentration at 1 at.%, the resulting Nb-1Ag alloy has
the Ag precipitate size of only ∼ 23 nm. Such small precipitates
would be dissolved due to the wear-induced SPD near the contact-
ing surface, and thus leads to high wear rate, despite high hardness.
However, for Nb-5Ag alloy, the precipitates reach 41 nm. This size is
sufﬁciently enough to induce the formation of self-organized alter-
nating Nb and Ag nanolayers [39], and thus leads to reduced friction
and wear.
4.4. Corrosion mechanism
As is known, Ag has much higher standard electrode poten-
tial of Ag (E0
Ag = 0.8 V for Ag+(aq.) + e ↔ Ag(s)) than Nb (E0
Nb =
-0.75
 V for Nb2O5(s) + 10H+ + 10e− ↔ 2Nb(s) + 5H2O) and is com-
pletely immiscible with Nb. Thus, the Nb-Ag two-phase alloys
would form micro-galvanic couples between Nb matrix and Ag
nano-precipitates, which should lead to more severe corrosion in
Nb. The present Nb-Ag two-phase alloys show much better corro-
sion resistance should be mainly attributed to the higher relative
density. For the alloys fabricated with powder metallurgy, relative
density is a critical factor to determine corrosion resistance because
larger real surface area would be exposed to the corrosive solution
and thus, the corrosion would be accelerated for material with low
density. Here, the measured relative densities of pure Nb, Nb-1Ag
and Nb-5Ag are 91.8 %, 95.1 % and 98.8 %, respectively. After adding
a small amount of Ag, the density of the as-sintered alloy was signif-
icantly improved. As shown in Fig. 5, more micropores were found
on the pure Nb surface and local corrosion was frequently found
inside the micropores. These micropores are drawbacks for the pas-
sive ﬁlms where the oxide layers are discontinuous compared with
the other area on the surface so that the corrosion processes would
prefer to occur in these sites due to the lack of protection. There-
fore, the relatively lower corrosion resistance of pure Nb should be
attributed to the local corrosion due to its low density. For Nb-1Ag
alloy, both local corrosion and galvanic corrosion in the microp-
ores may occur. Initially, the galvanic microcell between Nb matrix
and Ag precipitates can accelerate the corrosion of Nb. However,
the formed Nb2O5 passive ﬁlm would protect Nb from further cor-
rosion and then, corrosion would also occur in Ag. This is similar
to Nb-5Ag, but Nb-5Ag has higher density and the uniform dense
passive ﬁlm, composed of Nb2O5 and Ag2O as identiﬁed by XPS in
Fig. 6, would protect the alloy from severe corrosion. This explains
why Nb-5Ag alloy has higher corrosion resistance. The corrosion
mechanisms of the samples also explain the ions release proﬁles
in Fig. 7. It should be mentioned that despite that addition of Ag
could increase the relative density of Nb-Ag alloys and may further
enhance the corrosion resistance, the amount of Ag should be ded-
icatedly controlled since much more Ag addition would reduce the
mechanical properties of the alloy (as conﬁrmed in Supplementary
Fig. S2) and the overdose of Ag could also be toxic to mammalian
cells.
4.5. Antibacterial mechanism
As expected, with the antimicrobial Ag addition, Nb-Ag alloys
both have signiﬁcantly enhanced antibacterial activity against S.
aureus and E. coli. The antibacterial mechanism of Ag is still unclear
and in debate, but a generally recognition is that Ag ion can pen-
etrate the cell wall and subsequently change DNA molecules into
a condensed form to lost their replication ability and at the same
time reacts with thiol group in proteins, which induces the damage
or even the death of the bacterial [12,40,41]. Therefore, the released
amount of Ag ions in the solution plays a vital role for the antibacte-
rial activity of Nb-Ag alloys. However, when Nb-Ag alloys immersed
in PBS for 28 days, the Ag ion concentration for Nb-1Ag is larger than
Nb-5Ag alloy, which seems not to agree with the results of antibac-
terial ability. This phenomenon may be caused by the divergent
corrosion environment and the short culture time for antibacterial
evaluation. In a word, both Nb-1Ag alloy and Nb-5Ag alloys show
strong antibacterial activity compared with pure Nb. Moreover, the
Nb-5Ag alloy shows more stable Ag ion release in PBS than Nb-
1Ag alloy which can avoid the premature failure in clinical practice
and contribute to the sustainable release of Ag ion. Therefore, it
can be concluded that Nb-5Ag has the high antibacterial activity as
biomedical implants.
4.6. In vitro and in vivo biocompatibility
In vivo and in vitro evaluations have demonstrated that the
Nb-5Ag alloy has excellent biocompatibility and osteointegration
ability. The mechanism for the excellent bioactivity in alkalescency
body ﬂuid is as follows: (i) the metal surface was oxidized, forming
the surface oxides; (ii) Nb−OH groups are formed by the hydrol-
ysis reaction; and ﬁnally the abundant Nb−OH functional groups
induce bone mineral-like apatite nuclei formation [42], which can
grow spontaneously by absorbing the calcium and phosphate ions
in the surrounding ﬂuid [43]. Also, the terminal carboxyl and amino
groups of amino acids and proteins can bind with the surface
hydroxyls [6], which is conductive to the osteoblast proliferation.
It should be mentioned that there have been extensive studies
which reported that exposure of silver (ionic silver and silver par-
ticles) could result in cytotoxicity, genotoxicity, and inﬂammatory
response in human cells, including apoptosis [44], changes in gene
expression and DNA damage [45], but the toxicity of silver to human
cells is considerably lower as compared to bacteria [46]. In the
present study, Ag has a remarkable antibacterial ability, but no
dead cell was found for Nb-Ag alloys in vitro. This should be due
to the low Ag ion release rate which is far below the critical value
for cytocotoxicity [47–49]. Currently, there has been no available
clear health-based guideline value for silver nanoparticles or ions
concentration in human body. It is found that the minimum concen-
tration of silver which can induce cytotoxicity depends on cell-type
[50]. In vitro study shows that the acute cytotoxicity to mammalian

5. Conclusion
In this study, nanostructured Nb-Ag two-phase alloys with a
small amount of Ag have been fabricated by mechanical alloy-
ing and SPS. The microstructure was characterized in detail. The
mechanical properties, tribological performance, electrochemical
corrosion behavior, antibacterial ability and in vitro and in vivo bio-
compatibility were systematically evaluated. The key ﬁndings are
summarized as follows.
(i)
 Nanoscale Ag precipitates were located at the grain bound-
aries of ultraﬁne-grained Nb matrix. The fabricated Nb-1 at.%
Ag alloy has the matrix grain size of 200 nm and Ag precipitate
size of 23 nm, while the Nb-5 at.% Ag alloy has the matrix grain
size of 150 nm and Ag precipitate size of 41 nm.
(ii) The addition of 5 at.% Ag signiﬁcantly enhances the strength
and ductility of Nb. The fabricated Nb-5 at.% Ag alloy has an
excellent combination of strength-ductility, with compressive
yield strength of up to ∼ 1486 MPa and fracture strain of
∼ 35 %.
(iii) The friction and wear performance depend on the amount of
Ag addition. The higher Ag amount induced the precipitation
of larger Ag particles and would reduce the friction and wear.
(iv) The corrosion resistance was signiﬁcantly improved after
adding Ag into the Nb, due to the enhanced relative density
of the sintered alloys and the formation of a stable and dense
passive ﬁlm of Nb2O5 and Ag2O.
(v) The obtained Nb-5 at.% Ag alloy shows strong antibacterial
activity both in vitro and in vivo, with antibacterial rates of 99.3
% against E. Coli and 98.9 % against S. aureus, respectively.
(vi) In vitro and in vivo evaluations demonstrate that the present
Nb-5 at.% Ag alloy has good biocompatibility and osteointe-
gration ability.
