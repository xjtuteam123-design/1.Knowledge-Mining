1. Introduction 
Body centered cubic β Nb-Ti solid solution alloy possesses high 
melting point, high strength (~ 1000 – 1200 MPa at room temperature), 
moderate fracture toughness and good oxidation resistance. With the 
addition of Ti, the density of Nb-Ti alloy is generally 6.2 – 6.9 g/cm
3
, 
which is lower than conventional superalloys (8.1 – 8.6 g/cm
3
). There -
fore, Nb-Ti alloys have been considered to replace the traditional su -
peralloys in many fields, such as the surface of thermal protection 
system (TPS) [1 – 4] . Most of previous researches about β Nb-Ti alloy 
revolve around its production techniques and less have been carried out 
about its post-processing techniques such as joining [5 – 9] . It is therefore 
of great significance to develop a reliable joining technique for its 
practical application. 
Welding, diffusion-bonding and brazing are three joining techniques 
which have been commonly applied in the field of high temperature 
structure material joining. Brazing, as the most feasible and economical 
method, is widely adopted due to its relatively low joining temperature, 
the possibility of complex structure joining, dissimilar-material joining, 
high precision and better joint properties [10] . 
Selection of an appropriate filler alloy plays a key role in obtaining 
high-properties joints that can apply in special environments. It is well 
known that the serving temperature of Al, Ag or Au based brazed joints 
are usually below 500 ℃ [11] . For the high temperature application, the 
Ti and Ni based brazing alloys such as Ni-Cr [12] , Ti-Ni [13] , Ti-Cu-Ni 
[14] and Ti-Ni-Nb [15] have been widely employed to braze Ti
3
Al, 
TiNi, Ni-based superalloys and C
f
/SiC composites materials. Wu et al. 
[16] brazed Ti
50
Al
50 
and Ti-6Al-4V alloys with Ti-15Cu-25Ni and 
Ti-15Cu-15Ni filler alloys. The highest shear strength of 280 MPa was 
acquired by using Ti-15Cu-15Ni brazing alloy. However, the formation 
of Ti
2
Ni and Ti
3
Al phases deteriorates the shear strength of the joints. 
Qiu et al. [17] used Ti37.5-Zr37.5-Ni15-Cu10(wt%) brazing powders to 
braze the TiAl-based alloy with and without Mo particles. The results 
shown that the addition of Mo particles inhibited the eutectoid trans -
formation of β -Ti, which is beneficial to improve the bonding properties. 
Song et al. [10] brazed high Nb containing TiAl alloy 
(Ti-45Al-8.5Nb-(W, B, Y)(at%)) with Ti-Ni-Nb brazing alloy. It is found 
that dissolution style of TiAl substrate and interfacial morphology were 
significantly affected by the brazing temperature.

2. Experiment 
The Nb-Ti alloy with nominal composition of 50 Nb-30Ti-6Al-10 V- 
4Cr (at%) was received in the form of a cast rod with diameter of 80 mm. 
The Nb
10

65

20

40

melting with high purity Ti (99.7 wt%), Ni (99.98 wt%), Nb (99.7 wt%) 
under vacuum condition. All materials were washed with acetone and 
saturated alcohol solution for 10 – 15 min and then dried at room tem -
perature before vacuum melting. The ingots were remelted five times to 
ensure homogeneity, and the final weight loss of the brazing alloy less 
than 0.1 wt%. 
The Nb-Ti-Ni brazing filler alloys and Nb-Ti alloys were cut into 10 ×
10 × 0.2 mm
3 
and 10 × 10 × 2 mm
3 
respectively. Prior to the brazing 
process, the Nb-Ti-Ni filler alloys and Nb-Ti alloy were polished and 
cleaned ultrasonically. Then, the Nb-Ti-Ni filler alloys were placed be -
tween two Nb-Ti blocks. The brazing process was conducted in a vacuum 
furnace with a heating rate of 4 
◦
C/min under a vacuum of 5 × 10
 4 
Pa. 
As the temperature elevated to the brazing temperature, the brazed 
joints were maintained for 10 min. Finally, the brazed joints were cooled 
to room temperature with furnace under vacuum circumstance. 
Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) with associated energy 
dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) was used to characterize the microstruc -
ture and fracture morphology. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) 
was used to observe the morphology of the reaction phases in the joint, 
and the melting temperature of filler alloys was measured by DSC. The 
tensile strength of the brazed joints was tested on a universal testing 
machine at crosshead speeds of 0.5 mm/min.

The interfacial morphology is closely related to the dissolution of 
the Nb-Ti matrix, especially the concentration gradient of the Nb. 
As the Nb-Ti matrix contains 50 % Nb, the concentration gradient 
in the Nb-Ti/Nb
20
Ti

dissolution of the Nb-Ti matrix and the formation of connected 
micro-channels between the grains. In the meantime, the liquid 
filler alloy infiltrated into the Nb-Ti matrix through these micro- 
channels under the driven of capillary force, and thus formed the 
irregular interface. Additionally, the increased of the brazing 
temperature accelerated the atomic diffusion rate, leading to the 
increase in the dissolution rate of the Nb-Ti matrix. 
For the Nb-Ti/Nb
10

65
Ni

gradient of the Nb between the Nb

enhanced the dissolution of the matrix and increased the Nb content in 
the liquid filler alloy rapidly. The compositional change of liquid filler 
alloy caused the isothermal solidification, leading to the precipitation 
and coalescence of the (Nb, Ti) solid solution. Thus, the infiltration of 
liquid filler alloy was prevented by the (Nb, Ti) solid solution, and 
consequently formed a relatively flat interface. Besides, the dissolution 
rate of Nb-Ti matrix also increased with the increase of the brazing 
temperature in the Nb-Ti/Nb

temperature further elevated, the precipitation and coalescence of (Nb, 
Ti) solid solution inhibited the dissolution of (Nb, Ti) matrix.
