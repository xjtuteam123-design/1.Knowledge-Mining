Abstract 
To improve the yield strength of an FeCoCrNiMn high-entropy alloy (HEA), elemental Ti and C were doped into 
the alloy. Subsequently, an in situ synthesized carbides particle-strengthened HEA matrix composite was prepared 
by mechanical alloying (MA), followed by a vacuum hot-pressing sintering (VHPS) method. The TiC nanoparticles 
were distributed along the grain boundaries. The microstructure of the alloy contained a face-centered cubic (FCC) 
solid solution as the matrix phase and small amounts of TiC, M23C6 and M7C3 (where M=Cr, Mn, Fe) carbides. The 
addition of element al Ti and C significantly improved the room -temperature compressive yield strength of the 
FeCoCrNiMn HEA from 774 MPa to 1445 MPa (an 86.7% increase), accompanied by a decrease in the 
compressive strength and plasticity. Grain boundary strengthening and precipitation strengthening are the main 
strengthening mechanisms of the alloy doping with elemental Ti and C. 
Keywords: High-entropy alloy; Powder metallurgy; TiC nanoparticles; Microstructure; Mechanical properties 
 
1 Introduction 
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) based on chemical disorder represent a new class of materials and 
have attracted considerable attention due to their unique properties and related scientific importance 
[1-3]. Among various the HEAs, an equiatomic alloy of Fe, Co , Cr, Ni, and Mn is considered 
promising. This alloy exhibits outstanding ductility and fracture toughness at room and cryogenic 
temperature, and its fracture toughness exceeds that of the most other materials [ 4-7]. However, its 
yield strength is relatively weak [ 7]. Therefore, it is important to improve the yield strength of this 
alloy. 
The yield strength of the FeCoCrNiMn HEA can be improved by doping with alloying elements

## Page 3

or ceramic particles , or by refining the microstructure. Of these methods, doping with alloying 
elements, such as Al [8], C [9-11], modifies the chemical composition of the alloy and thus 
strengthens the alloy. A m icrostructural refinement can be obtained through thermomechanical 
treatment or powder metallurgy methods. A high Hall-Petch coefficient was reported for this alloy 
[7]; therefore, the production of a fine microstructure by thermomechanical treatment [4, 12 -16] or 
powder metallurgy [17-18] can effectively increase the yield strength of the alloy while maintain ing 
relatively high ductility. Specifically, powder metallurgy, which includes two steps (mechanical 
alloying (MA) and sintering ), is a more effective way to strengthen the alloy. A bulk FeCoCrNiMn 
HEA with ultrafine or nanograin sizes can easily be obtained through MA and subsequent sintering 
[17-18]. 
To further strengthen the alloy, Łukasz [19-20] et al. designed novel metal matrix nanocomposites 
with an FeCoCrNiMn alloy as a matrix and Al2O3 or SiC nanoparticles as a reinforcement phase. 
The alloys were manufactured by MA , followed by hot isostatic pressing sintering (HI PS). The 
addition of 5 wt% of Al2O3 or SiC nanoparticles effectively increased the room -temperature 
compressive yield strength of the alloy s, accompanied by a decrease in the plasticity and 
compressive strength. 
However, the ex situ inserted ceramic nanoparticles dispersal uniformity in the matrix metal is 
difficult to control, and the interface between the ex situ particles and the metal matrix is unstable 
and easily contaminated due to the poor wettability between them [ 21-23]. M oreover, the ex situ 
methods are complicated. In addition, a temperature increase can lead to faster grain growth of an 
FeCoCrNiMn HEA [18], which does not benefit the engineering applications of this alloy, especially 
at elevated temperatures. 
Fortunately, an in situ method is typically used to fabricate metal matrix composites with 
ceramic particles as a reinforcement phase. In situ method s are novel and have many unique 
advantages. For instance, the interface between ceramic and metal is generally cleaner and more 
compatible. Additionally, hard metals are thermodynamically stable with less degradation and yield 
final products with stronger interfacial bonding and elevated mechanical properties [24]. 
According to the characteristics of powder metallurgy and in situ technologies, combined with the 
high affinity of Ti toward C to easily form titanium carbide [25] and the good wettability between 
TiC and HEAs [26], an in situ synthesized carbides particle-strengthened FeCoCrNiMn HEA matrix

## Page 4

composite was prepared. In this paper, our objectives were to determine t he effect of elemental Ti 
and C additions on the microstructure and mechanical properties of the alloy and identify the 
strengthening mechanism. 
2 Experimental procedure 
Elemental blends of Fe, Co, Cr, Ni, Mn, Ti, C powders with purity higher than 99.9% were 
mechanically alloyed to synthesize FeCoCrNiMn and FeCoCrNiMnTi 0.3C0.3 (in atomic proportion s) 
HEAs, which are referred to as alloy TC00 and TC30, respectively . The MA process was performed 
in a high-energy planetary ball milling machine (QM-QX4L, Changsha Miqi Instrument Equipment 
Co., Ltd., China) using a stainless steel vial with stainless steel balls (of Φ5, Φ10 and Φ15 in 
diameter) at 250 rpm at a ball -to-powder weight ratio of 15:1 under highly pure argon. N-heptane 
was added during milling as a process controlling agent (PCA) for MA to avoid cold welding and 
prevent metal oxidation. During milling, the powder samples were removed by a vial opening inside 
a vacuum glove box after milling for 0, 5,10, 20, 30, and 45 h. The 4 5 h milled powders were first 
pretreated (100 °C for 1 0 h ) to remove t he PCA , then sintered by a vacuum hot -pressing furnace 
(ZT-40-20Y , Shanghai Chenhua Electric Furnace Co., Ltd., China) in a Φ20 mm -inner-diameter 
graphite mould. The heating rate was set at 8 °C /min, and subsequently the 45 h milled powders 
were sintered at 1000 °C for 1 h at a constant pressure of 5 0 M Pa, followed by cooling to room 
temperature inside the furnace. 
The phase structures of the alloys were characterized by an X-ray diffractometer (Bruker D8, 
Germany) at a scan rate of 2 °/min from 20° to 100°. The microstructures of the alloys were analysed 
by a n S-200 type (Hitachi, Japan) scanning electron microscope (SEM) with an accessory energy 
dispersive X -ray spectrometer (EDS) and a transmission electron microscope (TEM) with 
selected-area electron diffraction (SAED) performed on a JEOL JEM-2100 (Tokyo, Japan) operated 
at 200 KV . The compressive properties at room temperature were measured by a n MTS810 testing 
machine. The hardness values of the bulk alloys were measured by a n HXD-1000T microhardness 
tester. To ensure the accuracy of the measurement results, each sample was tested 10 times, and the 
mean values were calculated.

The XRD patterns of the vacuum hot-pressed sintered (VHPSed) bulks show an FCC solid solution 
structure as the matrix phase and a small quantity of carbides, as shown in Fig. 1c. Apart from the M23C6 
and M 7C3 carbides, the TiC carbides were also found in alloy TC30, which indicate s that during 
sintering, Ti and C reacted to form TiC carbide due to the high affinity between Ti and C. In addition, 
the small amount of the BCC solid solution phase disappeared after VHPS. It is possible that t he 
mechanical alloying powders are highly active, and a large amount of excess energy can be stored at 
the high-density dislocation s and the large number of nanocrystalline boundaries , which greatly 
reduced the activation energy required for phase transformation and thus led to the transformation of 
the BCC phase in to an FCC matrix phase [28]. The small amount of M 23C6 and M 7C3 carbides 
existed in the alloys, especially in alloy TC00, because C inevitably originates from the N -heptane 
medium and the stainless steel vials and balls used during the MA process, when the blend includes 
Cr, which has a high affinity to elemental C and easily forms carbides. A similar phenomenon was 
also observed in Ref. [17-20].

mechanical properties of the Fe CoCrNiMn alloy in this work are better than those obtained for the 
alloy in the as-cast state [20].

3.3 Strengthening mechanism 
Compared with alloy TC00, the yield strength of alloy TC30 increased by 86.7% and still 
exhibited a relatively high plasticity. The improved mechanical properties of alloy TC30 result from 
contributions from several strengthening mechanisms: grain -boundary strengthening, precipitation 
strengthening, twin-boundary strengthening, dislocation strengthening, and solid -solution 
strengthening. The final yield strength of the alloy, σ0.2, can be evaluated by summing the above 
contributions related to a single strengthening effect with the original yield strength of the 
unreinforced matrix, which is expressed as [32]: 
σ0.2 = σTC00 + ΔσG + ΔσP + ΔσT + ΔσD + ΔσS (1) 
where σ TC00 is the yield strength of the unreinforced matrix (alloy TC00) and Δσ G, Δσ P, Δσ T, ΔσD 
and ΔσS are strengthening contributions from the grain boundary , precipitates, twin boundary, 
dislocations and solid solution, respectively. In the following, the contributions of these mechanisms 
to the overall yield strength of alloy TC30 are individually analysed. 
 In general, grain-size refinement can improve the strength of an alloy because smaller grain sizes 
offer a higher volume fraction of grain boundaries, which can impede dislocation motion. The 
relationship between yield strength and grain size is well described by the classical Hall -Petch 
equation [33-34]: 
σy = σ0 + ky / d1/2 (2) 
where σy is the yield stress, σ 0 is the lattice friction stress, k y is the strengthening coefficient and d is 
the average grain diameter. The addition of elements Ti and C led to a microstructural transformation 
from coarse grains (0.3~0.7 μm in size) to ultrafine (~270 nm) and nanocrystalline (~100 nm) grains 
(See Fig. 3). Moreover, a high Hall-Petch coefficient was reported for this alloy [7]. Therefore, it can 
be deduced that grain-boundary strengthening played an important role in alloy strengthening.

 The precipitates in the matrix also have an important effect on the strength of the alloy. Generally, 
small and not -too-hard precipitates are sheared by the moving dislocations, as described as the 
Friedel effect. In a situation of bigger and stronger particles the moving dislocations pass them by 
bowing. The particle strength increases with increasing size until it can be shared by dislocations. At 
some point in the size increase, shearing becomes difficult, and the simpler option for dislocations is 
to pass them via the Orowan mechanism. This mechanism results in a reduction in strength with a 
further increase in the precipitate size. When the fraction of precipitates shear ed and bowed by the 
dislocations is equal, the alloys achieve peak strength [20]. In this work, approximately 
100-nm-sized TiC nanoparticles were distributed along the grain b oundaries of the FCC solid 
solution in alloy TC30 , and twins along with some defects seen as dark contrast features were also 
observed in the FCC matrix grains (Fig. 6b). According to the fast Fourier transform (FFT) pattern of 
the squared regions in Fig. 6c, strong reflections in the FCC at a zone axis orientation of [110] with 
twins on the {1 -11} plane are present. Moreover, the inverse fast Fourier transform (IFFT) pattern 
along the direction of the {111} crystal plane shows some dislocations and lattice distortion in the 
FCC solid solution (Fig. 6e). For the multi-element alloy, the stacking fault energy is relatively low, 
which enhanced twin formation. The stacking fault energy of a dissociated dislocation is relatively 
high causing blockage of its movement in the most active slip systems. In places where an 
accumulation of dislocations occur, most often at a grain boundary or an area of particles, the 
generation of twins occurs [20]. Note that the addition of hard particles enhanced the blocking of 
dislocation movement, which increases the probability of twin formation. When twins start

 Apart from grain-boundary strengthening and precipitation strengthening, twin-boundary 
strengthening, dislocation strengthening and solid-solution strengthening also contribute to the yield 
strength of alloy TC30. The analysis details are as follows. As mentioned above, the addition of 
elemental Ti and C led to more twins in the FCC solid solution. The strengthening effect of a twin 
boundary is similar to a grain boundary, which can also impede dislocation motion and thus 
strengthen the alloy during plastic deformation [36]. 
High density dislocations are usually introduced into the alloy powders during MA. After VHPS, 
the dislocation density in the bulk obviously decrease s compared that in the powders [ 37]. Despite 
this, there are still some dislocations in the alloy due to the effect of TiC nanoparticles (Fig. 6). The 
higher the dislocation density, the more obvious the effect of dislocation strengthening. Therefore, it 
is believed that dislocation strengthening also contributed to a certain yield strength value. In 
addition, a small amount of Ti existed in the FCC solid solution (Table 1), which can also produce 
solution strengthening. This is because Ti has a larger atomic radius than that of the other elements in 
the alloy , which can lead to lattice distortion in the FCC solid solution . In summary, the 
strengthening of the alloy was caused by elemental Ti and C additions, and grain boundary 
strengthening and precipitation strengthening are the main strengthening mechanisms of alloy TC30. 
4 Conclusions 
Powder metallurgy and in situ technologies we re used to successfully prepare a caibides 
particle-strengthened FeCoCrNiMn HEA . The add ition of element al Ti and C led to a fine 
microstructure composed of nanoscale grains (~ 100 nm) and ultrafine grains (~ 270 nm). Moreover, 
approximately 100-nm-sized TiC nanoparticles were distributed along the grain boundaries . The 
microstructure of the FeCoCrNiMn alloy with carbides particles contained an FCC solid solution as 
the matrix phase and small amounts of M23C6, M7C3 and TiC carbides. 
The FeCoCrNiMn HEA matrix exhibited a substantial work hardening effect resulting in a high 
compression strength of 2129 MPa and a good plasticity of 38.2%. However, its yield strength and 
hardness were only 774 MPa and 332 HV , respectively. The addition of element al Ti and C led to

indicated that elemental Ti and C additons have a significant st rengthening effect on the 
FeCoCrNiMn HEA. Grain boundary strengthening and precipitation strengthening are the main
