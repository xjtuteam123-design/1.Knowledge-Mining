ABSTRACT
During the service process, Ni-based superalloys undergo microstructure evolutions such as rafting, which
greatly affect its service performance. In the present study, a multiphase-field model coupled with crystal
plasticity was established to simulate the γ/γ' microstructure evolution of Ni-based superalloys during P-type
rafting. The anisotropic plasticity considering 12 octahedral slip systems was defined in γ matrix channel. The
PanNickel© database was used to calculate thermodynamic and kinetic data for phase transition and diffusion in
multicomponent superalloys. First, the model was validated by two benchmarks with and without consideration
of external load respectively. Then, the anisotropic of slip systems was analyzed. It was found that eight slip
systems A2, A3, B2, B4, C1, C3, D1, D4 were activated in gable channel first when the structure was compressed
in [0 0 1] direction. Besides, the elements distribution was studied. It can be seen that Al, Ti, Ta diffused into
precipitates while Co, Cr and Mo diffused away the roof channel when the structure was compressed in [0 0 1]
direction. Re and W mainly distributed in the γ/γ' interface because their slow diffusion rate. Finally, the P-type
raft of CMSX-4 superalloy was simulated, and the plasticity distribution and plastic behavior of the 12 slip
systems were discussed. The current model can be used to simulate phase transformation of multicomponent
superalloys where plasticity is strongly coupled.
1. Introduction
Due to the excellent creep strength, hot corrosion resistance and
oxidation resistance, Ni-based single crystal (SC) superalloys are widely
used in combustion chamber and turbine blades of aero-engines and
industrial gas turbines. Although techniques such as cooling channels in
the blade and thermal barrier coatings have been developed to reduce
the working temperature of blade, the harsh working environment still
imposes stringent requirements on the mechanical properties of the
superalloys. Under the condition of high temperature and external
loads, Ni-based SC superalloys undergo a slow and continuous de-
formation, i.e. creep, which greatly reduces the strength of the super-
alloys and shortens the service life of the blades. In the initial creep
stage, the heat-treated original cuboidal γ′ elongate in a certain direc-
tion, accompanied by the increase of the dislocation density in γ matrix,
which is called rafting. As shown in Fig. 1, the rafted structure can be
divided into N-type (the elongated direction is normal to the external
load) and P-type (the elongated direction is parallel to the external
load).
The lattice misfit of superalloys and the external load have great
impacts on the rafting type, which was given by Véron [1] as a mor-
phology map. To investigate the driving force and the influence of raft
phenomenon, some numerical techniques such as the Monte Carlo
method [2], phase field method [3], phase field crystal method [4] and
finite element based unit cell models [5] have been used to study the
microstructure evolution during rafting. Among these numerical
method, Monte Carlo method and phase-field crystal method main
focus on atomistic scale, while finite element based unit cell method
mainly studies macro performances like average strain, and describes
microstructure evolution by rough unit cell model. The phase field
method has been proved to be the most suitable approach for micro-
structure simulation in recent years because its simulation scale is
mainly in the micron range, consistent with the scale of γ/γ′ micro-
structure evolution. Besides, the phase field method can be easily
coupled with lattice misfit, inhomogeneous elastic energy, plastic de-
formation, dislocation activities and so on.

on the elastic inhomogeneity, discussing the effects of inhomogeneous

It is recent years, the plastic deformation is considered in phase field

stress, and examined the threshold value of strain for inducing rafting.
Cottura [20] simulated the plastic strain based on the J2 theory with
kinematic and isotropic hardening behavior considered, and proposed
that the formation of rafts is accelerated when plastic activity is taken
into account. Furthermore, plastic deformation is tightly associated
with the dislocation activity. Models coupled with crystal plasticity
framework and dislocation dynamic model were put forward accord-
ingly. Gaubert considered phenomenological viscoplasticity in phase
field model [21] and simulated the precipitates morphology of
011
-oriented Ni-based superalloys during creep tests [22]. Cottura
[23] considered dislocation-based constitutive laws in phase field
model and analyzed the plastic activities of 12 octahedral slip systems
of Ni-based superalloys under tensile stress. Wu simulated the evolution
of dislocation, investigated the relationship of dislocation density and
plastic strain under constant [24–26] and cycle loads [27], with the
continuum dislocation dynamics and phase field model combined. De-
spite some significant achievements, the previous models only con-
sidered plasticity in Ni-Al binary system. Hence, it is highly desirable to
combine phase-field model and crystal plasticity in multicomponent
systems.
The aim of the present work is to show how multicomponent mul-
tiphase-field model can be coupled with crystal plasticity framework
where the anisotropic of slip systems are taken into account, and study
the plastic activities and microstructure evolution of Ni-based super-
alloys during P-type rafting. In the following sections, the multiphase-
field model, crystal plasticity framework and the coupling method are
introduced. Then, the verifications of the model are carried out. Finally,
the anisotropic of slip systems and elements distribution are discussed,
and the microstructure evolution of CMSX-4 during P-type rafting is
simulated.

4. Simulation ofγ'-precipitates rafting
In this section, the multiphase-field model is applied to simulate the
microstructure evolution of γ'-precipitates under uniaxial compression
at 1373 K, which is consistent with the service temperature of Ni-based
superalloys. The cuboidal γ'-precipitates with the size of about 0.45 μm
are obtained as the initial microstructure after heat treatment simula-
tion. All the simulations have periodic boundary conditions. First, the
anisotropy of octahedral slip systems is discussed in a 3D simulation
with a single γ'-precipitate set at the center of γ matrix. It is indicated
that applied load has great impact on the stress distribution, thereby
influences the active slip systems and plastic behavior in γ matrix.
Then, the elements distribution is analyzed. It is found that the roof
channel gradually disappeared with γ'-formers (Al, Ti, Ta) diffusing into
precipitates and γ-formers (Co, Cr, Mo) diffusing away the growth path.
Finally, the microstructure evolution of γ'-precipitates in a negative
misfit superalloy, CMSX-4 is simulated with 2D method. The plastic
strain curve and the shear strain of 12 slip systems are depicted. It is
proposed that the plastic activities in gable and roof matrix channel are
different, which may provide an explanation of rafting driving force.

5. Conclusions
The microstructure evolution and plastic activities of multi-
component Ni-base superalloys during P-type rafting was studied in this
paper. For this purpose, a multiphase-field model coupled with crystal
plasticity framework was proposed. The mechanical equilibrium of γ
and γ' phase in the model was validated through comparisons to ana-
lytical solution. The main conclusions are listed as follows.
(1) The multicomponent multiphase-field model coupled with crystal
plasticity proposed in this paper provides a general framework to
study the microstructure evolutions of multicomponent superalloys
in which plasticity plays an important role.
(2) The anisotropy of 12 octahedral slip systems is taken into account.
When compressed along [0 0 1] direction, the primary slip systems
A2, A3, B2, B4, C1, C3, D1 and D4 are obviously activated in gable
channels, while the other four equivalent slip systems A6, B5, C5,
D6 are difficult to be activated.
(3) The elements distribution in roof and gable channel are different
when a uniaxial compression is loaded. With the gradually dis-
appearance of roof channel, γ′-formers (Al, Ti, Ta) mainly distribute
in γ′ phase, while γ-formers (Co, Cr, Mo) mainly distribute in gable
channel. Re and W gather at the γ/γ′ interface because of their small
chemical mobility.
(4) Plastic strain mainly accumulates in the gable channels in γ matrix,
and influences the driving force of microstructure evolution, which
is the main reason why CMSX-4 has a P-type raft when a 137 MPa
uniaxial compression is loaded.
