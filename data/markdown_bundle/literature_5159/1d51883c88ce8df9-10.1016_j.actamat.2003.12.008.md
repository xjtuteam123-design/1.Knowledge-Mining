Ultra-high strength above 5000 MPa and soft magnetic properties
of Co–Fe–Ta–B bulk glassy alloys

Abstract
Glassy Co43Fe20Ta5:5B31:5 alloy rods with glass transition temperature of 910 K, followed by a large supercooled liquid region of
72 K were produced in the diameter range up to 2 mm by copper mold casting. The glassy alloy rods exhibit ultra-high fracture
strength of 5185 MPa, high Young /C213s modulus of 268 GPa, high speciﬁc strength of 6.0 /C2 105 Nmk g /C0 1 and high speciﬁc Young /C213s
modulus of 31 /C2 106 Nmk g /C0 1. The fracture strength, speciﬁc strength and speciﬁc Young /C213s modulus obtained by compression test
are higher than all previous values reported for any crystalline and glassy alloys. The ultra-high strength of over 5000 MPa persists
in the temperature range up to 698 K. The fracture occurred on one major shear band which was declined by 44 /C176to the stress axis
implying the slight deviation from the maximum shear stress plane. Excellent formability is manifested by large tensile elongation of
1400% and large reduction ratio in thickness above 90% in the supercooled liquid region. The ultra-high strength alloy exhibits
simultaneously good soft magnetic properties: low coercivity of 0.25 A/m and an extremely high permeability of 550,000. The novel
alloy is promising as a new ultra-high strength material with good deformability and soft magnetic properties.

1. Introduction
The processing of metals and the development of new
alloys have accompanied and supported the develop-
ment of civilization from its earliest stages. Alloys in
bulk form have historically been cast from the liquid
state. Alloys of eutectic compositions have had wide
usage because of their low melting temperatures that
facilitate processing. However, such alloys in bulk form
usually consisted of crystalline structures with a periodic
atomic conﬁguration because of the extreme instability
of metallic supercooled liquid against crystallization
[1,2]. Such a structural limitation of metallic bulk alloys
was broken towards the end of the last century by the
discovery of stabilization phenomenon of supercooled
metallic liquids [3–5]. Bulk glassy alloys consisting only
of metallic components in Mg-, lanthanide(Ln)- and Zr-
based systems have been formed by conventional casting
processes and their maximum diameter reaches about
30 mm [6–8]. These bulk glassy alloys exhibit high me-
chanical strength combined with good ductility for Zr-,
Ti-, Cu- and Ni-based systems, high corrosion resistance
for Fe- and Ni-based systems, good soft magnetic
properties for Fe- and Co-based systems, high electro-
chemical discharge capacity for Mg-based alloys and
highly eﬃcient electrode properties for Pd-based alloys
[7,8].
When we focus on mechanical and magnetic prop-
erties reported for bulk glassy alloys, the fracture
strength is in the range of 1500–1800 MPa for Zr-based
alloys [4–8], 1700–1900 MPa for Ti-based alloys [9,10],
1900–2500 MPa for Cu-based alloys [11] and 2700–3100
MPa for Ni-based alloys [12]. On the other hand, the
highest fracture strength of bulk crystalline alloys has
been reported to be 2400 MPa for high alloy special steel
subjected to an optimum heat treatment and 3300 MPa

Very recently, we have found that the glassy Co–Fe–
Ta–B alloys in a bulk rod form of 2 mm in diameter
exhibit ultra-high strength reaching 5300 MPa by the
compression test. These Co-based alloys exhibit simul-
taneously extremely soft magnetic properties as well as
good viscous deformability at elevated temperatures
near the glass transition temperature. This paper reports
the synthesis of soft magnetic Co

glassy alloys with record ultra-high strength of over
5000 MPa, low coercivity of 0.25 A/m, ultra-high per-
meability of over 500,000 and good viscous deformabi-
lity leading to large elongation exceeding 1000%.
2. Experimental procedure
Multi-component Co–Fe-based alloy ingots with
composition of Co
100 /C0 x /C0 y /C0 zFexTayBz were prepared by
arc melting the mixtures of pure Co (99.9 mass%), Fe
(99.9 mass%) and Ta (99.8 mass%) metals and pure
crystalline B (99.5 mass%) in an argon atmosphere. The
alloy compositions represent nominal atomic percent-
ages. Bulk glassy alloys in a rod form with diameters up
to 3 mm and a length of 70 mm and in a ring form with
an outer diameter of 7 mm, an inner diameter of 3 mm
and a thickness of 1 mm were produced by an ejection
copper mold casting method. The as-cast and annealed
structures were examined by X-ray diﬀraction (XRD)
with Cu K a radiation and high-resolution transmission
electron microscopy (HRTEM). Two XRD scanning
modes which are continuous scanning and step scanning
methods are used. The glass transition temperature and
crystallization temperature were determined by diﬀer-
ential scanning calorimetry (DSC) at a heating rate of
0.67 K/s. Density was measured by the Archemedian
method using a chemical solution of toluene at 298 K.
Mechanical properties including Young /C213s modulus, yield
strength, fracture strength, elastic strain and fracture
strain were measured in the temperature range from 298
to 930 K by an Instron testing machine with a vacuum
chamber. For each temperature, ﬁve specimens were
tested, and the averaged data were used. The gauge di-
mension was 2 mm in diameter and 4 mm in length and
the strain rate was 5.0 /C2 10
/C0 4 s/C0 1. The strain was mea-
sured with a strain gauge meter. The yield strength was
deﬁned by the stress at the elastic strain of 0.02 for the
sample of not behaving plastic deformation. On the
other hand, for the sample of behaving plastic defor-
mation, the yield strength was deﬁned by the stress at
the plastic strain of 0.002. Vickers hardness was mea-
sured with a Vickers hardness tester under a load of
1.96 N. The generation of slip marking was made in the
temperature range from 298 to 873 K with a Vickers
hardness indenter under a load of 9.8 N by using the
same hardness tester. Deformation and fracture behav-
ior were examined by scanning electron microscopy
(SEM). Saturation magnetization was measured under
an applied ﬁeld of 1500 A/m with a vibrating sample
magnetometer (VSM). Coercive force, initial perme-
ability and maximum permeability were measured under
a ﬁeld of 3 A/m with a B–H curve tracer.

It was also shown that the primary crystallization
phase from the supercooled liquid was the metastable
complex face-centered cubic (Co,Fe)
21Ta2B6 with a large
unit volume. This phase can be regarded to be isomor-
phic to (Fe,M)
23B6 (M ¼ Nb,Ta) which has been identi-
ﬁed as the primary crystallization phase of bulk glassy
Fe–Nb–B and Fe–Nb–Ta–B alloys [18,19]. There have
been experimental data on the coordination numbers
and atomic distances of glassy Fe
60–70Nb10B20–30 alloys
obtained by anomalous X-ray scattering [28,29]. These
data indicate that the Fe–Nb–B glassy alloys have a
unique network-like structure, in which distorted trigo-
nal prisms consisting of Fe and B are connected with
each other through glue atoms of Nb, and its network-
like structure leads to the primary precipitation of the
metastable (Fe,Nb)
23B6 phase rather than separated
mixed phases. Although no data on local atomic con-
ﬁgurations have been obtained for the Co–Fe–Ta–B
glassy alloy, the similarities of the primary crystallization
phase and the alloy components allow us to presume the
formation of the similar network-like atomic conﬁgura-
tions. The network-like structure is diﬀerent from the
complex face-centered cubic phase and hence the pro-
gress of crystallization leading to the precipitation of
(Co,Fe)
21Ta2B6 requires long-range atomic rearrange-
ments of constituent elements. The necessity is presumed
to cause the high stability of supercooled liquid against
crystallization and enable the formation of the bulk
glassy alloy by the copper mold casting process. Besides,
the network-like atomic conﬁgurations seem to act as the
origin for high resistance against plastic yielding at room
temperature and elevated temperatures.

5. Summary
It was found that the Co
43Fe20Ta5:5B31:5 bulk glassy
alloy rod with a diameter of 2 mm exhibits glass tran-
sition temperature of 910 K, a large supercooled liquid
region of 72 K prior to crystallization, ultra-high frac-
ture strength of 5185 MPa, ultra-high Young /C213s modulus
of 268 GPa, ultra-high speciﬁc strength of 6.0 /C2 10
5 Nm
kg/C0 1 and ultra-high speciﬁc Young /C213s modulus of
31 /C2 106 Nmk g /C0 1. The fracture strength, speciﬁc
strength and speciﬁc Young /C213s modulus are higher than
all previous values reported for any crystalline and
glassy alloys. The ultra-high strength persists in the
temperature range up to 698 K. Excellent formability is
manifested by extremely large tensile elongation of
about 1400% and large reduction ratio in thickness
above 90% in one cycle pressing treatment in the su-
percooled liquid region. The ultra-high strength alloys
exhibit simultaneously good soft magnetic properties:
low coercivity of 0.25 A/m and extremely high perme-
ability of 550,000 at room temperature. The novel me-
tallic material is promising for future development as a
new ultra-high strength material combined with good
deformability and good soft magnetic metallic material.
