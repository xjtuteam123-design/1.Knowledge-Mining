ABSTRACT
The balance between strength and plasticity is the ultimate objective in the exploration of alloy design. This 
study focuses on achieving synergy between strength and toughness in the CrFeNiSi
0.2 
medium entropy alloy 
through various heat treatments to regulate the size and morphology of the σ phase. The results demonstrate that 
trace amounts of the σ phase began to appear after annealing at 800 
◦
C. The volume fraction of the σ phase in the 
alloy and the size of the phase gradually increased as the temperature rose from 800 
◦
C to 900 
◦
C. Specifically, 
the alloy exhibits an exceptional synergy between strength and plasticity after annealing at 900 
◦
C for 1 h, 
maintaining a plasticity of 18.6 %, with a yield strength of 630 MPa and keep an ultimate tensile strength 
exceeding 1 GPa. Subsequently, a short-term partial solution treatment was employed to eliminate the lath- 
shaped precipitates within the grains, further optimizing the volume fractions and morphologies of the σ 
phase, leading to superior mechanical properties. The plasticity increased to 21.1 %, the yield strength rose to 
722.3 MPa, and the ultimate tensile strength achieved to 1133.2 MPa, with the strain-energy density reaching up 
to 195.76 mJ/mm
3
. The σ phase is vital for high strain-energy density in the researched alloys, as it refines 
grains, enhances the strain gradient effect, and coordinates deformation. This finding paves the way for the 
further development of higher performance Si-added Co-free medium/high entropy alloy.
1. Introduction
As times progresses, the industrial sector has increasingly stringent 
requirements for the performance of metallic materials. Over the past 
decade, the emergence of medium/high-entropy alloys (M/HEAs) [ 1 – 3 ] 
has sparked a research boom in the academic community. This surge is 
primarily attributed to the exceptional mechanical properties of 
M/HEAs, which include a relatively high work-hardening rate [ 4 – 6 ], 
good plasticity [ 7 , 8 ] and low-temperature toughness [ 9 , 10 ]. Common 
M/HEAs predominantly consist of face-centered cubic (FCC), 
body-centered cubic (BCC) or hexagonal close-packed (HCP) structures 
[ 11 ]. M/HEAs with FCC structure have been widely studied due to their 
superior plasticity. However, M/HEAs typically contain precious metal 
elements (such as cobalt, Co). This significantly escalates the cost of 
industrial production. As a result, researchers are substituting expensive 
elements with low-cost ones. Within the MEA system, FCC-structured 
CrFeNi MEA, which evolved from austenitic stainless steels, has 
garnered considerable attention due to its remarkable toughness at both 
room and low temperatures [ 12 , 13 ]. Compared to commercial 304L or 
316L stainless steels, the CrFeNi MEA exhibits a higher chromium (Cr) 
content. Which Result in the performance disparities between the 
CrFeNi MEA and common stainless steels [ 14 , 15 ]. Nevertheless, despite 
the favorable plasticity associated with the FCC structure, its relatively 
low strength and hardness pose limitations for engineering applications.
Introducing precipitates into the FCC matrix can enhance the 
strength of the alloy while maintaining considerable ductility. For 
instance, Tang et al. [ 16 ] discovered that introducing a small amount of 
aluminum (Al) into the equiatomic CoCrFeNi alloy can cause the pre -
cipitation of ordered BCC phase, significantly enhancing the hardness of 
CoCrFeNi HEA. Seol et al. [ 17 ] investigated the mechanical properties of 
the FeMnCrCoNi HEA with the addition of 30 ppm boron (B) element 
and annealed at 800 
◦
C for 1 h. The results showed that the B element 
mainly segregated at the grain boundaries, playing a dual role in 
enhancing strength due to boundary strengthening and grain size

4. Conclusion
In this study, we conducted various heat treatment processes on the 
CrFeNiSi
0.2 
MEA to explore the relationship between microstructure and 
mechanical properties. The main findings were summarized as follows. 
(1) Different aging treatments significantly affect the content and 
morphology of the σ phase. When aged at 800 
◦
C for 1 h, the σ 
phase appears as ellipsoids no larger than 2 μ m. After aging at 
900 
◦
C for 1 h, it precipitates abundantly in a lath-like form 
within the grains while remaining an ellipsoidal shape at the 
grain boundaries, and the overall volume fraction of 12.5 %. 
Following a short solution treatment at 1100 
◦
C after aging at 
900 
◦
C for 1 h, the lath-shaped σ phase within the grains is almost 
dissolved. And the size of the σ phase at the triple junctions is 
reduced to approximately 400 nm with a regular spherical shape 
as well as a volume fraction of about 2 %.
(2) The mechanical properties vary significantly among different 
heat-treated alloys. Most samples exhibit a strength-plasticity 
trade-off, with the exception of the one subjected to short solu -
tion treatment. This particular sample demonstrates a yield 
strength of 722.3 MPa, a tensile strength of 1133.2 MPa, an 
elongation of 21.1 %, and a strain energy density of up to 195.7 
mJ/mm
3
.
(3) The σ phase play a crucial role in achieving high strain-energy 
density in the studied alloys. It refines the grains, enhances the 
strain gradient effect, and coordinates deformation during ten -
sion. Furthermore, the σ phase also improves the alloy ’ s strength 
and plasticity. For two phase alloys with precipitates, their 
optimal properties are determined by the matrix grain size, as 
well as the size and distributions of the precipitates.
