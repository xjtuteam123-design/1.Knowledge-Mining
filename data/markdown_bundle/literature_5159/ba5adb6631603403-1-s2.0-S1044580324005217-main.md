ABSTRACT 
Cu-Cr-Nb alloys has become a key material for ITER plasma facing components (PFC) due to its excellent thermal 
stability. In this study, Cu-3.3Cr-1.6Nb (wt%) alloy was prepared through in-situ casting using Cu
–
Cr and 
Cr
–
Nb master alloy. The agglomeration and coarsening of Cr
2
Nb particles in Cu-Cr-Nb alloy are improved. The 
effect of aging temperature on the microstructure and properties of rolled Cu-3.3Cr-1.6Nb alloy. The results show 
that the aged Cu-3.3Cr-1.6Nb alloy contains multi-scale precipitation phases distribution with micron-level 
Cr
2
Nb (0.2 – 2 μ m), Cr-rich phase (2.2 μ m), nano-level Cr
2
Nb (13.1 nm) and nano-level Cr (9.3 nm). After 
aging at 450 
◦
C for 0.5 h, the tensile strength and electrical conductivity of the aged alloy were 469 MPa and 
78.5% IACS, respectively. According to TEM and EBSD analysis, the Cr

aging and were pinned the grain boundaries to inhibit the growth of Cu matrix grains, which result in a good 
thermal stability of Cu-3.3Cr-1.6Nb alloy. This study provides a new method for preparing Cu-Cr-Nb alloy with 
high-strength, high-conductivity and high heat resistance by casting method. 
1. Introduction 
With the iterative renewal of fusion energy system, it has become 
urgent to develop a comprehensive performance material with high- 
strength, high thermal conductivity, and radiation-resistant for its core 
component heat exchanger under high-temperature service conditions 
[ 1 – 3 ]. As reported by NASA, the particle-reinforced Cu-Cr-Nb alloys 
have become the latest focus and are currently act as a preferred 
candidate material for ITER PFC [ 4 , 5 ]. In the Cu-Cr-Nb ternary system, 
the elements Cr and Nb can form a laves-type intermetallic compound 
Cr
2
Nb with good thermal stability, high melting point (1650 
◦
C) and 
extremely high hardness (1000 VHN) [ 6 , 7 ], which is conducive to the 
improvement of comprehensive performance. 
At present, the research on Cu-Cr-Nb alloys mainly involves opti -
mizing heat treatment process, increasing Cr content, adding alloy ele -
ments and innovative preparation method. As a precipitated reinforced 
copper alloy, optimizing the heat treatment process to improve the 
properties of the Cu-Cr-Nb alloy is the most common method. Guo et al. 
[ 8 ] designed a Cu-0.47Cr-0.16Nb (wt%) alloy, and the tensile strength 
and electrical conductivity of the alloy were significantly improved 
through two-step aging treatment. Meanwhile, Zhou et al. [ 9 ] designed 
the excess Cr element to prepare a Cu-4.06Cr-1.25Nb alloy with tensile 
strength of 440 MPa and electrical conductivity of 77.8% IACS, the 
precipitation of Cr element plays a great role in performance. The Cu- 
2Cr-0.5Nb alloy with excess Cr was also prepared by D. L. Ellis et al. 
[ 10 ], which result in a significantly improvement of tensile strength. In 
addition, Cheng et al. [ 11 ] studied the effect of Zr element on the 
properties of Cu-Cr-Nb-Zr alloy. The hardness and electrical conduc -
tivity of the alloy are 133.2 HV and 74.6%IACS, respectively, which is 
mainly attributed to the addition of Zr to promote the precipitation of 
Cr. Cu-Cr-Nb-Zn alloy was studied by Cai et al. [ 12 ], and found that Zn 
element can also strengthen the alloy by promoting the precipitation of 
Cr. 
Besides, the preparation methods of Cu-Cr-Nb alloy were also stud -
ied, which mainly include casting [ 13 ], chill block melt spinning [ 14 ], 
gas atomization [ 15 ], and so on. Among them, the casting method is

2. Experimental procedures 
The electrolytic copper powder (99.99%), pure Cr (99.9%) powder 
and pure Nb powder (99.95%) were used as raw materials. Firstly, 
Cu
–
Cr (Cu-6.6Cr, wt%) and Cu
–
Nb (Cu-3.2Nb, wt%) master alloys 
with designed compositions were prepared by vacuum induction 
melting and vacuum consumable melting, respectively. Based on 
composition design, a certain amount of two master alloys and pure Cu 
were heated to 1600 
◦
C, and then solidified in the water-cooled copper 
mold to prepare Cu-3.3Cr-1.6Nb alloy by vacuum induction melting. 
Finally, the as-cast alloy was rolled at room temperature with an 80% 
reduction and aged at 450 
◦
C, 500 
◦
C, 550 
◦
C, 600 
◦
C and 700 
◦
C for 0.5 
h, respectively. 
The composition of the as-cast Cu-Cr-Nb samples were determined 
via Agilent (ICP-OES 730). The electrical conductivity of the samples 
was measured using an eddy current conductometer (Sigma 2008B). HB- 
3000 Brinell hardness tester was used for hardness measurement, the 
load is 250 kg and the dwell time is 30 s. Scanning electron microscopy 
(SEM, Phenom Pure + , Merlin Compact, Zeiss) equipped with an elec -
tron back-scattered diffraction (EBSD) system was used to characterize 
the microstructure and fracture morphology of Cu-Cr-Nb alloy. X-ray 
diffraction (XRD-7000S) was used to identify the phases. Transmission 
electron microscope (TEM, FEI Tecnai G2 F20) was utilized to analyze 
the crystallographic structure of precipitates and lattice spacing at an 
accelerating voltage of 200 kV. A universal testing machine (HT2402) 
was used for tensile tests at room temperature with a tensile rate of 0.4 
mm/min.

4. Conclusions 
The Cu-3.3Cr-1.6Nb alloys were prepared by in-situ reaction as the 
design idea, and uses self-made CuCr and CuNb master alloys. By 
adjusting the aging process parameters, a multi-scale strengthening 
phases in the Cu-3.3Cr-1.6Nb alloy were formed, namely primary micro- 
scale Cr
2
Nb and micro-scale Cr, as well as aging precipitated nano-scale 
Cr
2
Nb and nano-scale Cr. After aging at 450 
◦
C for 0.5 h, the tensile 
strength and electrical conductivity of the alloy were 469 MPa and 78.5 
%IACS, respectively. The Cr
2
Nb particles do not grow up during the 
aging process and are pinned to the grain boundaries, inhibiting the 
growth of Cu matrix grains, thus making the Cu-3.3Cr-1.6Nb alloy have 
good thermal stability.
