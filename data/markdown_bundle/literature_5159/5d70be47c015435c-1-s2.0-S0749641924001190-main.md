ABSTRACT 
Precipitation strengthening is a crucial strategy for ensuring the overall performance of con -
ventional and multicomponent alloys to meet industrial demands. However, the mechanical 
properties of high-Cr-bearing alloys are often compromised by brittle Cr-rich precipitates at grain 
boundaries (GBs), leading to severe embrittlement. In this work, a multi-step thermomechanical 
process is employed to regulate discontinuous dynamic recrystallization (DDRX) and static 
recrystallization, achieving an ultrafine-grained microstructure. This optimized approach simul -
taneously impedes the continuous precipitation of the ordered L1
2 
nanocrystals within the matrix 
and actively encourages the synergistic discontinuous precipitations of submicron L1
2 
and Cr-rich 
σ particles at GBs, thereby enhancing (yield) strength and high-temperature thermal stability. The 
ultrafine grains facilitate uniform plastic deformation, characterized by pronounced parallel 
dislocation slip and stacking faults (SFs) within face-centered cubic (fcc) grains, while second- 
direction slips, SFs, and Lomer-Cottrell (L-C) lock networks near GB precipitates greatly alle -
viate stress concentration. Critically, the submicron L1
2 
particles enveloping σ precipitates at GBs 
also display plastic deformation via mechanical twinning and dislocations, effectively impeding 
rapid crack propagation along GBs. This research not only provides new insights into the 
ductility-strength balance in advanced alloys but also proposes a compelling route for optimizing 
biphasic precipitation, expanding the applicability of high-Cr multicomponent alloys.

1. Introduction 
Simultaneously increasing strength and ductility has always been a major focus in the development of advanced structural ma -
terials, particularly for various technological applications like aerospace and marine industries (Bai et al., 2022, 2023; Ding et al., 
2018; Feng et al., 2017; Li and Chen, 2021; Wei et al., 2014; Zhang et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2020). Recently, there has been growing 
interest in unique alloys containing multiple concentrated elements, known as multicomponent alloys, multi-principal element alloys, 
or medium- and high-entropy alloys (MEAs and HEAs), especially in the case of face-centered-cubic (fcc) type ones (Fu et al., 2023; 
Jiao et al., 2023; Li et al., 2023; Wagner and Laplanche, 2023; Wang et al., 2023), due to excellent mechanical properties. In general, 
grain refinement and precipitation strengthening are vital mechanisms for enhancing the low (yield) strength of these single 
fcc-structured multicomponent alloys by introducing optimal dislocation obstacles (Xu et al., 2020; Yang et al., 2021). The grain 
refinement and the formation of annealing twins often lead to an increased number of grain and twin boundaries (GBs and TBs), which 
hinder the dislocation motion and increase the resistance of materials to plastic deformation (Wang et al., 2020). For instance, Fu et al. 
(2022, 2023), Kishore et al. (2023), and Zhang et al. (2023) employed (surface) severe plastic deformation technologies, such as laser 
shock peening and high-pressure torsion, to achieve grain refinement including twins, thereby enhancing the strength-ductility 
synergy of multicomponent alloys. 
On the other hand, nanoprecipitates within the matrix can act as effective barriers and then impede dislocation motion, 
contributing to enhanced strength (Ming et al., 2018; Ye et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2024). Among these nanoprecipitates in multi -
component alloys, the ordered, coherent L1
2

Furthermore, in the pursuit of designing high-performance traditional and multicomponent alloys, chromium is widely 
acknowledged as an essential alloying element to ensure exceptional corrosion resistance, wear resistance, and high-temperature 
mechanical properties (Agustianingrum et al., 2020; An et al., 2020; Du et al., 2020; Gludovatz et al., 2016; Xu et al., 2016). 
Therefore, the designed high-performance alloys often contain a substantial amount of chromium. However, the thermomechanical 
process often results in the undesirable precipitation of Cr-rich brittle particles at GBs besides the formation of other nanoparticles in 
high-Cr-bearing alloys. Although this GB precipitation can increase hardness and wear resistance, it may compromise strength, 
ductility, and potentially corrosion resistance. For instance, Lin et al. (2015) reported that the formation of the σ phase in the

the formation of these brittle Cr-rich σ particles at GBs (Christofidou et al., 2019; Liu et al., 2016; Yi et al., 2021) introduces significant 
local stress/strain concentrations at the precipitation/matrix interface due to their incompatibility (Ming et al., 2019), leading to the 
early cracking along the interphase boundaries and thereby the room-temperature embrittlement (Cho et al., 2018; Stepanov et al.,

of principal elements Ni, Cr, and Mn separately into GBs reduces GB cohesion and promotes crack initiation along GBs, thereby 
deteriorating ductility at intermediate temperatures. In a word, the simultaneous achievement of outstanding comprehensive me -
chanical properties, including hardness, strength, and ductility, in high-Cr-bearing multicomponent alloys represents a significant 
challenge. 
To date, extensive efforts have been undertaken to weaken GB embrittlement caused by brittle precipitates at GBs through alloy 
composition design, optimization of heat treatment, and promotion of coherent precipitation within grains (He et al., 2019; Ming et al., 
2018; Ming et al., 2019; Tong et al., 2019; Zhao et al., 2017). Despite these efforts, progress in this area has been challenging. The GB 
precipitation of brittle Cr-rich particles is an inherent phenomenon in high-Cr-bearing multicomponent alloys during long-term service 
under intermediate-temperature conditions, which cannot be completely avoided. Therefore, it becomes imperative to develop a 
strategy that balances strength and ductility while still allowing for the precipitation of the Cr-rich phase to occur. Since micro-cracks 
prefer to initiate and propagate along GBs where brittle Cr-rich particles appear, inhibiting the rapid propagation of micro-cracks

4.1. Origin of submicron particles at GBs and grain refinement 
4.1.1. Microstructural evolutions of the homogenized and hot-extruded samples 
To elucidate the mechanisms underlying the formation of the final structure, schematic diagrams were employed to visually 
illustrate the evolution of the microstructures throughout the thermomechanical processing. It was reported that the ordered L1
2 
nanoprecipitates prefer to form coherently in the disordered fcc matrix after adding Al and Ti ( Du et al., 2020 ; Huang et al., 2021 ; Li 
et al., 2021 ; Zhang et al., 2022 ; Zhao et al., 2017 ). The high-temperature homogenization ensured chemical uniformity and slow 
furnace cooling allowed for sufficient nucleation and growth of L1
2 
particles ( Fig. 2 (f) and 10 (a)). In high-Cr-bearing multicomponent 
alloys, Cr-rich GB precipitation typically requires severe plastic deformation or long-term heat treatments at intermediate tempera -
tures ( Asghari-Rad et al., 2020 ; Otto et al., 2016 ; Pickering et al., 2016 ; Schuh et al., 2015 ). Therefore, in our case, only a few σ 
particles appeared at GBs in the L1
2
-strengthened homogenized sample. 
The chemically homogenized sample was then subjected to hot extrusion, which involved DRX and boundary migration ( Huang 
and Log ´e, 2016 ; Sakai et al., 2014 ; Savaedi et al., 2022 ). The occurrence of DRX is influenced by SFEs, thermo-mechanical processing 
conditions, initial grain sizes, second-phase particles, etc. ( Huang and Log ´e, 2016 ; Sakai et al., 2014 ; Savaedi et al., 2022 ). During hot 
deformation, the Co-Cr-Ni-(Al,Ti) multicomponent alloy system with low to medium SFEs prefers to undergo discontinuous DRX 
(DDRX) ( He et al., 2020 ; Khan et al., 2022 ; Yi et al., 2020 ; Zhao et al., 2017 ). In our scenario, non-uniform and short-term hot 
deformation prevented the complete achievement of DDRX, leading to a heterogeneous structure as reported by Pachla et al. (2017) . 
This structure is characterized by coarse non-DDRXed crystals and fine DDRXed grains ( Figs. 3 and 10 (b)) located between and within 
non-DDRXed crystals. As we know, the primary deformation mechanisms of metals and alloys during the high-temperature defor -
mation process include GB sliding, dislocation climb, dynamic recovery, and DDRX ( Huang and Log ´e, 2016 ; Sakai et al., 2014 ; Savaedi 
et al., 2022 ), while the subsequent DDRX tends to occur in dislocation accumulation regions (e.g., GB regions with significant 
dislocation pile-up, deformation band regions with a large number of dislocations). 
During the early stage of hot deformation, the initial nuclei of the DDRXed crystals should form on pre-existing GBs via strain- 
induced GB motion ( Huang and Log ´e, 2016 ; Miura et al., 1994 ; Sakai et al., 2014 ; Savaedi et al., 2022 ; Wusatowska-Sarnek et al., 
2002 ). The subsequent hot deformation can lead to fluctuating boundary shapes and dislocation accumulation, causing high dislo -
cation density gradients (Stage I in Fig. 10 (b)) and subgrain formation near the original GBs. Continued deformation aggravated local 
strain concentration, leading to sliding or shearing of partial GBs as well as the additional inhomogeneous strain (Stage II in Fig. 10 
(b)). This process promoted the formation of dislocation substructures or annealing twins near pre-existing GBs (Stage III in Fig. 10 
(b)), facilitating the separation of bulged portions from parent grains. These processes are commonly employed to explain the for -
mation of fine DDRXed grains ( Huang and Log ´e, 2016 ; Humphreys and Kalu, 1987 ; Miura et al., 1994 ; Nes et al., 1985 ; Sakai et al.,

4.1.2. Microstructural evolutions of the cold-rolled and annealed samples 
Upon cold rolling, all grains underwent further elongation along the ED direction, exacerbating deformation heterogeneity caused 
by pre-existing heterogeneous structures induced by hot extrusion. As a result, layered microstructures and deformation bands with 
various dislocation substructures ( Figs. 4 and 10 (c)) around heavily distorted GBs and second-phase precipitates, and within micro -
bands/shear bands can be observed. During annealing, static recovery occurs initially, involving the rearrangement of dislocation 
substructures and the release of stored energy ( Hornbogen and K ¨oster, 1978 ; Humphreys et al., 2017 ; Raabe, 2014 ). Subsequent static 
recrystallization nucleation predominantly took place discontinuously through the formation and motion of HAGBs ( Hornbogen and 
K ¨oster, 1978 ; Humphreys et al., 2017 ; Raabe, 2014 ) at GBs and within heavily deformed regions ( Fig. 10 (d)). The high density of 
dislocations and stored strain energy provide a fast channel for element diffusion upon recrystallization ( Fig. 10 (d)). Moreover, cold 
rolling, with an approximate total thickness reduction of 80%, is expected to produce diverse textures ( Haase and Barrales-Mora, 2018 ; 
Wang et al., 2020 ). During the early stage of static recrystallization, nuclei that initially formed at GBs remained components of the 
rolling texture, while annealing twins not only created pronounced twin orientations but also contributed to overall texture 
randomization ( Haase and Barrales-Mora, 2018 ). With prolonged annealing, a complete static recrystallization was achieved. More 
importantly, the L1
2 
nanoprecipitates in the samples before annealing already have an exceptionally heterogeneous distribution 
resulting from hot extrusion and cold rolling. The subsequent annealing treatments cannot fully dissolve pre-existing L1

nano -

5. Conclusions 
In this study, a multi-step thermomechanical process was proposed to manipulate the sizes and distributions of fcc grains and GB 
precipitations in high-Cr-bearing multicomponent alloys, which involves homogenization, hot extrusion, cold-rolling, and annealing. 
The findings regarding microstructures, formation mechanisms, and deformation behaviors are summarized below. 
(1) Hot extrusion transforms the coarse fcc homogenized structure into a heterogeneous one comprising fine grains and elongated 
coarse grains due to incomplete DDRX. The L1
2 
nanoprecipitates are still observed in the whole sample, whereas their presence 
becomes more pronounced within the DDRXed grains and within the heavily deformed regions between grains. The cold rolling 
further crushes grains, leading to the heterogeneous accumulation of dislocation substructures. Subsequent annealing causes 
static recovery and recrystallization, producing an ultra-fine grain structure with pronounced annealing twins. Static recrys -
tallization prefers to occur in the heavily deformed regions containing segregated L1
2 
nanoprecipitates and high-density dis -
locations, resulting in the formation of submicron L1
2 
precipitates. The emergence of these particles at GBs causes the depletion 
of nearby Ni, Al, and Ti elements, prompting the formation of Cr(Co)-rich σ particles in their vicinity. Ultimately, the significant 
consumption of Ni, Al, and Ti elements due to the GB precipitation of the L1
2 
phase leads to the absence of L1
2 
precipitates 
within the matrix. 
(2) The multi-step thermomechanical process significantly improves the Vickers hardness and tensile properties of the final sample. 
The Vickers hardness reaches 446 ± 10.6 HV, while the yield strength and ultimate tensile strength are enhanced to ~1552 MPa 
and 1773 MPa, respectively. Furthermore, the material exhibited a uniform elongation of around 9.7 %. Compared to those 
reported alloys containing Cr-rich σ precipitates at GBs, the present sample exhibits a better strength and ductility balance and 
superior high-temperature thermal stability. 
(3) The strengthening mechanisms were primarily attributed to grain refinement, dislocations strengthening, and biphase GB 
precipitation. The high-density GBs/TBs effectively impede dislocation motion, while pre-existing dislocations near GBs further 
increase the dislocation density within grains. The submicron particles at GBs also increase resistance to slip transfer across GBs. 
The above multiple factors lead to the increase of yield strength. Further plastic deformation induces dislocation pileup and 
entanglement, and abundant parallel stack faults facilitated by GBs/TBs within fcc grains. Annealing twins exacerbate plastic 
deformation near σ /fcc and L1
2
/fcc interfaces, leading to the formation of a cross-slip and L-C lock network. Moreover, micro- 
cracks easily form at the σ / σ and σ /L1
2 
phase interfaces, but the tough L1
2 
particles hinder their rapid linking via activating 
dislocations and deformation twinning.
