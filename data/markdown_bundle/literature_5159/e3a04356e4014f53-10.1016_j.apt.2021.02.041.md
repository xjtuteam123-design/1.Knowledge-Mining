abstract
Low mechanical strength, especially at high temperatures, is the key problem that limit the application of
FeCrAl alloys as the accident tolerance fuel (ATF) cladding materials. Dispersion strengthening by carbide
nanoparticles is an effective way to improve mechanical properties at high temperatures. In this work, an
ultraﬁne grained FeCrAl-0.6 wt.% ZrC alloys with excellent mechanical properties were fabricated suc-
cessfully by mechanical milling and spark plasma sintering. The effect of milling speed on powder char-
acteristics, microstructure and mechanical properties of FeCrAl alloys were investigated. The particle size
of the powders increase signiﬁcantly after milling at 400 rpm, while it has a lower oxygen content.
Increasing the milling speed decreased the resultant grain size and improved relative density.
Transmission electron microscope (TEM) demonstrated the nano ZrC particles uniformly distributed in
the matrix at higher milling speed, which effectively promotes grain reﬁnement and dispersion strength-
ening. The results of mechanical properties show that the tensile strength, percentage elongation and
hardness of FeCrAl-0.6 wt.% ZrC alloys at room temperature (RT) reached up to 1.05 GPa, 349.86 HV
and 12.1%, respectively, after milling at 400 rpm. It is worth noting that the FeCrAl-0.6 wt.% ZrC alloy also
exhibited a good high-temperature strength more than 110 MPa at 800 ℃, which is about 55.4% and
24.7% higher than previously reported FeCrAl-0.5 wt.% ZrC and FeCrAl-1.0 wt.% ZrC alloys, but the plas-
ticity is reduced. The results demonstrated that the excellent mechanical properties were not only attrib-
uted to the dispersion strengthen by nanosized ZrC, a good interface bonding between Fe matrix and
nanosized ZrC, but also the ultra-ﬁne grained structure induced by the milling process.

1. Introduction
As the core component of the nuclear power reactor, the perfor-
mance of nuclear fuel cladding directly determined the safety, reli-
ability and economy of the nuclear power reactor [1–3]. However,
the commercial zircaloy claddings used in the light water reactors
(LWRs) will react violently with high temperature steam under
high temperature (>800 ℃) in the case of the loss of coolant acci-
dent, resulting a large amount of heat and hydrogen, which in turn
reduce the safety margin of the nuclear reactor system and even
cause a disastrous security incident, such as the Japanese Fukush-
ima Daiichi Nuclear accident in 2011 [3–7]. Therefore, many
research projects on advanced accident tolerance fuel (ATF) clad-
ding materials with excellent high temperature performance were
proposed to improve the safety and reliability of the nuclear reac-
tor system under severe water-loss accident scenarios around the
world [2,4,8–10].
Among many candidate ATF cladding materials, FeCrAl alloys
have become one of the most promising candidate materials for
substituting the zirconium based alloys as the ATF cladding mate-
rials in the application of light water reactors (LWRs) due to its
superior oxidation resistance under high temperature steam envi-
ronment [9,11,12]. However, the high temperature mechanical
strength (800 ℃) of the traditional FeCrAl alloys is still less than
60 MPa because of the coarse second phases and grains, which
directly reduce the safety and reliability of the nuclear power

FeCrAl alloys have much higher thermal neutron absorption capac-
ity (about ten times) than zirconium based alloys, which needs to
further reduce the wall thickness to compensate the neutron pen-
alty and increase the high temperature mechanical strength to
compensate its thickness loss [4,6,9,14]. Therefore, it is imperative
to improve the high temperature mechanical strength of the FeCrAl
alloys.
In order to improve the high temperature mechanical strength,
some strategies such as solid solution strengthening, ﬁne grain
strengthening and precipitation strengthening are commonly used
and many beneﬁcial results have been obtained. Recently, the
FeCrAl based oxide dispersion strengthened (ODS) ferritic steels
with enhanced mechanical properties have been developed. How-
ever, the FeCrAl-ODS steels contain coarser Y-Al oxide particles due
to the reaction of Al with Y, which leads to a degradation of the
mechanical strength [16–18]. To overcome this issue, Ukai and Shi-
zukawa et al. [19–22] fabricated Zr-bearing FeCrAl-ODS steels by
co-addition of Zr and the results showed that Zr can preferentially
react with yttria to form ﬁner Y-Zr oxides and inhibit the precipi-
tation of coarse Y-Al oxides and therefore high temperature
mechanical strength was obtained. Mizuno and Shibata [23,24]
fabricated FeCrAl-ODS ferritic steels with CeO
2 particles instead
of Y 2O3 particles and the results showed that CeO 2 particles can
effectively improve the high mechanical temperature strength of
FeCrAl alloy. In addition, micro-alloy elements such as Nb, Mo
etc. were employed to produce Laves phase particles in the matrix
to achieve the precipitation strengthening [3,13]. However, the
strengthening effect of the Laves phase will be weakened due to
the smaller volume fraction and the coarser particles under high
temperature [25]. Gussev et al. [26] prepared FeCrAl alloys with
TiC and found that the addition of 1.0 wt.% TiC could effectively
limit the grain growth and obtain a ﬁne-grained structure. Among
many strengthening phases, ZrC has a very high hardness, and its
melting point as high as 3540 ℃, much higher than TiC (3160 ℃),
La2O3 (2215 ℃), Y 2O3 (2690 ℃), which makes ZrC very attractive
in strengthening the high temperature properties as the ideal rein-
forcements for FeCrAl alloys. In this work, an ultraﬁne grained
FeCrAl-0.6 wt.% ZrC alloys with excellent mechanical properties
were fabricated successfully by spark plasma sintering and
mechanical milling. In addition, the effects of milling speed on
the microstructure and mechanical properties of the FeCrAl-
0.6 wt% ZrC alloy were also investigated.

Fig. 2 shows the SEM morphologies and particle size distribu-

It can be seen that the morphology and particle size of FeCrAl pow-

The as-received FeCrAl powders exhibited a spherical morphology

milling speed to 400 rpm, the smaller particles were welded to

about 72.88 lm due to the cold welding effect as shown in
Fig. 4 g and h.
The oxygen content of as-received FeCrAl powders and as-
milled FeCrAl-ZrC powders at different milling speed was mea-
sured and the results are shown in Fig. 3 . The oxygen content of
FeCrAl-ZrC powders are 0.89 wt.%, 1.25 wt.%, and 0.14 wt.% at
the milling speed of 300 rpm, 350 rpm and 400 rpm, respectively,
which are higher than the as-received FeCrAl powders (0.036 wt.%).
Signiﬁcantly, Figs. 2 and 3 show that there is a close relationship
between the particle size and oxygen content of as-milled
FeCrAl-ZrC powders: the larger the particle size is, the lower the
oxygen content is. When other milling conditions remain
unchanged, the only signiﬁcant source of oxygen is the air environ-
ment outside of the milling pot. Mechanical milling will reduce
powder particle size of FeCrAl powders, and in turn increase the
speciﬁc surface area and surface energy, increasing the oxygen
adsorption capacity on the powder surface [29,30]. Thus, properly
increasing the milling speed leads to cold welding and coarsening,
which is beneﬁcial to reduce the oxygen content of the powders
and enhance the mechanical properties [31,32].

3. Conclusion
An ultraﬁne grained FeCrAl-0.6 wt.% ZrC alloys with excellent
mechanical properties were fabricated successfully by spark
plasma sintering and mechanical milling. The effects of milling
speed on the powder characterization, microstructure and
mechanical properties were investigated. The main conclusions
obtained are summarized as follows:
(1) The particle size of FeCrAl-ZrC powder increases signiﬁ-
cantly (a mean size of about 72.88
lm) after milling at
400 rpm, while the oxygen content is only 0.14 wt.%, which
is much lower than that at 300 rpm and 350 rpm.
(2) With the increase of the milling speed from 300 rpm to
400 rpm, the grain size of the FeCrAl-ZrC alloys is signiﬁ-
cantly reﬁned. When milled at 400 rpm, the FeCrAl-ZrC
alloys showed an uniform grain structure with an average
grain size about 0.86 lm. The relative density of FeCrAl-
ZrC alloys at the speed of 300 rpm, 350 rpm, 400 rpm are
98.20%, 98.60% and 99.86%, respectively. The higher milling
speed can make the nano ZrC particles more uniformly
distributed.
(3) With the increase of milling speed, the room temperature
