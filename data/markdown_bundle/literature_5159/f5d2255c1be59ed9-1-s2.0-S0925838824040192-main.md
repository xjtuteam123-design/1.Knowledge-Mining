ABSTRACT
Fe-B alloys are of interest due to their promising anticorrosion performance in Al melt, but new forms that are 
also ductile are needed for their widespread use. The challenge in developing Fe-B alloys that are both corrosion- 
resistant and ductile lies in the intrinsic tradeoff between B concentration and brittle-to-ductile transition tem -
perature. Here, we show a new strategy for the achievement of room temperature ductility in Fe-B alloys by 
architecting nano-lamellar structure under the laser remelting treatment. The test results indicate that the 
microhardness, yield strength and tensile strength of the nano-lamellae structural remelting layer are as high as 
877 HV
0.2
, 1012 MPa and 1123 MPa, respectively, and the uniform elongation is also enhanced to 4.51 % 
simultaneously. This work paves a new avenue for simultaneously improving the strength and ductility of 
eutectic alloys with brittle phase and widens the application scope for Fe-B alloys (B ≥ 3.0 wt percentage, wt%).
1. Introduction
During the Al production process, Al melt shows extreme activity 
with ferrous materials and the degradation of Fe-based components 
occurs after contact with the liquid Al, which is the bottleneck 
restricting the high-quality development of the aluminum industry 
[1 – 3] . Hence, many efforts have been made to develop the ferrous 
materials with desirable compatibility with liquid Al [4 – 8] . Recently, 
much attention has been focused on high boron ferrous alloys (B ≥ 3.0 wt 
%) due to their more promising anticorrosion performance in Al melt 
[9 – 12] . It is well documented that a large amount of continuous and 
stable borides (Fe
2
B) formed in the matrix can act as a physical barrier to 
inhibit the diffusion of Al melt into the matrix [11] , which makes the 
Fe-B alloys a leading candidate for bulk structures and protective coat -
ings for service in the extreme environments.
However, Fe
2
B phase with a body-centered tetragonal structure has a 
limited slip system at room temperature, which causes it to exhibit 
brittle deformation behavior under the action of external forces [13] . 
Additionally, the conventional cast high-boron ferrous alloys contain a 
large number of coarse lath-like primary borides and long needle-like 
eutectic borides, which can cause stress concentration and promote 
the initiation and propagation of microcracks, thereby seriously 
impairing the mechanical properties of alloys and limiting their wide -
spread application [14] . Hence, the chief concern over Fe-B alloys is how 
to address their inherent brittleness. According to our previous studies 
[14,15] , it is demonstrated that the control of the size and morphology 
of borides may be crucial for overcoming the intrinsic limitations of Fe-B 
alloys. The refinement of boride can effectively enhance their diffusion 
inhibition effect on Al melt and achieve a combination of strength and 
ductility. Ren et al. [16] found that the mechanical properties of high 
boron steel can be increased after the modification of microstructure by 
tuning solidification rate. Most recently, research has shown that 
nanolaminates could exhibit unique physical and mechanical properties 
that emerge as the thickness of individual layer declines to nanoscale 
[17] . Li et al. [18] reported that a brittle-to-ductile transition of eutec -
toid FeAl/FeAl
2 
alloys with lamellar structure occurs when the indi -
vidual lamellae thickness is reduced below 1 μ m. Wang et al. [19] stated 
that the as-cast micro-scale Al-Al
2
Cu eutectics exhibit brittle fracture of 
Al
2
Cu intermetallics, while the laser remelted alloys with nano-lamellar 
eutectics exhibit plastic deformation behavior in both phases after 
rolling at room temperature. Inspired by the above investigations, it can 
be expected that the architecture of nano-lamellar structure in the Fe-B

2. Experimental methods
The eutectic Fe-B alloy with compositions of Fe-3.8B (wt%) was 
fabricated in a 10 kg-capacity vacuum induction furnace with the charge 
materials of pure iron, ferroboron (Fe-18.8 wt% B) and cast as ingots 
with the dimension of 200 × 150 × 40 mm. Then, substrates of 150 ×
100 × 10 mm in size were cut from the ingots via wire-electrode cutting 
for LR treatment, followed by surface grinding using a grinding ma -
chine, cleaned with acetone and dried. A Trumpf TruLaser Cell 7020 
(TRUMPF SE + Co. KG, Germany) equipped with a TruDisk 3001 solid 
state laser was carried out for LR treatment, as shown in Fig. 1 . The 
specific laser process parameters are listed in Table 1 . The interval 
spacing between the two scans was 1.5 mm, and Argon shielding gas 
(flow rate of 20 SCFH) was used during the LR process to prevent 
oxidation.
The indentation test was conducted by a Vickers hardness tester 
(HDX-1000, Taiming Test Co., China) with a load of 200 gf for a dwell 
time of 15 s at room temperature. Each reported hardness value is 
determined as the average value of seven measurements, and the dis -
tance between each indentation is about 50 μ m. The tensile testing was 
conducted by a universal tensile testing machine (Instron 5569, Instron, 
US). Each result was taken from an average of three readings. The 
microstructure of the remelted layer and the as-cast substrate were 
characterized using a scanning electron microscopy (SEM; JSM-7800F, 
JEOL, Japan). Furthermore, more detailed information of the micro -
structural features was characterized using scanning transmission elec -
tron microscopy (STEM, FEI Talos F200X G2, Thermo scientific, USA) 
equipped with Super-X four SDD enhanced EDS detectors. High- 
resolution transmission electron microscope (HRTEM) analysis for the 
nano-lamellar structure was conducted on Schottky field-emission FEI 
Talos F200X G2 operated at 200 kV acceleration voltage. STEM samples 
were lifted out using a ultra-high-resolution FIB/SEM system (GAIA3, 
TESCAN, Czech). The foils were thinned with a 30 kV Ga beam to about 
250 nm and then polished at progressively lower voltages to gradually 
reduce the sample thickness to around 50 nm.

3.3. Effects of nano-lamellar structure on the comprehensive mechanical 
properties of the remelted layer
To characterize the comprehensive mechanical properties of the 
remelted layer, the Micro Vickers indentation tests at room temperature 
with 0.2 kgf loads on the surface of various remelted specimens and as- 
cast specimen were performed. As shown in Fig. 5 a-c, it is evident that 
many cracks of varying degrees appear around the indentation of as-cast 
Fe-B alloy (specimen A0) and specimen A1 and A2, whereas no visible 
cracks can be found during indenting for A3 and A4 in Figs. 5 d and e. 
After a careful observation of the cracks in Fig. 5 a-c, it is detected that 
they propagate in different modes. For specimen A0, the microcracks 
propagate and coalesce to form a long penetrating crack throughout the 
matrix. Unlike specimen A0, the zigzag cracks propagate in a step-like 
mode in the refined lamellar structure as illustrated in Fig. 5 b and c. 
Therefore, with the refinement of the eutectic boride and the reduction 
of the lamellar spacing, the crack initiation and propagation could be 
greatly delayed, if not totally inhibited. It is not until the nano-lamellar 
structures are formed in the remelted layer that the cracks on the 
indentation surface completely disappear, which implies that a marked 
transition from brittle to ductile response at room temperature can be 
observed with decreasing the interlamellar spacing. Additionally, Fig. 5 f 
gives the hardness of various specimens. In general, the hardness of the 
nano-lamellar structured specimens is remarkably enhanced and A3 has 
the highest hardness of 877 HV
0.2
, which is almost one time higher than 
that of A0 (427 HV
0.2
). The indentation test results indicate that the 
nano-lamellar structured specimens with decreased interlamellar 
spacing may exhibit simultaneously increased hardness and ductility.
