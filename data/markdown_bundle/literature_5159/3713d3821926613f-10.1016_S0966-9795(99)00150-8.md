Mechanical properties of Zr-based bulk glassy alloys containing
nanoscale compound particles
Akihisa Inoue *
Institute for Materials Research, Tohoku University, Sendai 980-8577, Japan
Abstract
For the last decade, bulk glassy alloys have been developed in a number of multicomponent alloy systems and have already
gained practical applications. Although the bulk glassy alloys in Zr-based systems have high strength and good toughness, it has
been found that the addition of special elements to the Zr-based glassy alloys causes the formation of a nanoscale mixed structure
consisting of nanoscale intermetallic particles embedded in a glassy matrix and the mixed phase alloys exhibit higher mechanical
strength and larger elongation as compared with the corresponding glassy single phase alloys. In particular, the bulk nanoscale
mixed phase alloys in the Zr–Al–Ni–Cu–Nb system produced by squeeze casting exhibit extremely high bending ﬂexural strength of
4400 MPa without fracture during the three point bending test. The mechanism for the formation of the nanoscale mixed structure
for the alloys containing special additional elements as well as the reason for the remarkable improvement in the mechanical
properties has been discussed on the basis of experimental results obtained by high-resolution TEM, nanobeam EDX spectroscopy,
atom probe FIM and small angle X-ray scattering, in addition to DSC, ordinary TEM and X-ray di/C128raction. # 2000 Elsevier
Science Ltd. All rights reserved.

1. History of bulk glassy alloys
Since the ﬁrst synthesis of an amorphous alloy in Au–
Si system by rapid solidiﬁcation in 1960 [1], a great
number of amorphous alloys have been produced in
various kinds of alloy systems for the last four decades
[2]. Almost all amorphous alloys found before 1990
have required high cooling rates over 10
5 K/s for glass
formation and the resulting sample thickness has been
limited to less than about 50mm [2]. The low glass-forming
ability has prevented the extension of application ﬁelds of
amorphous alloys. With the aim of eliminating the
limitation of the material thickness, a great e/C128ort has
been devoted to synthesize a bulk amorphous alloy in
the maintenance of good useful properties by various
consolidation techniques of amorphous powders [3,4].
However, there have been no successful data on the
formation of a bulk amorphous alloy with useful engi-
neering properties by the powder metallurgy techniques.
Among a great number of amorphous alloys containing
metallic elements, with Fe, Co, Ni, Ti, Zr, Al, Mg,
Ln(Lanthanide metal), Pd or Pt, etc., as a main con-
stituent element, it is known that only a Pd–Ni–P alloy
has a high glass-forming ability and can be produced
into a bulk glassy form with a thickness less than about
2 to 3 mm by water quenching of the ordinary molten
liquid [5] and the critical sample thickness increases sig-
niﬁcantly by repeated melting of the molten alloy ﬂuxed
with B
2O3 [6]. Fig. 1 summarizes the relation among

(3) negative heats of mixing among the main three

3. Features of structure and crystallization behavior of
bulk glassy alloys
It is important to clarify the reason why the alloys
with the three empirical rules can have high glass-form-
ing ability through the increase in the thermal stability
of the supercooled liquid against crystallization. By
using various identiﬁcation techniques of anomalous
X-ray scattering technique [22,23] and density measure-
ment [24], it has previously been reported that the spe-
cialized multicomponent glassy alloys have a new
structure with the features of highly dense random
packed atomic conﬁgurations, new local atomic conﬁg-
urations and long-range homogeneous atomic conﬁg-
urations [25–28]. That is, the increase in the packing
density has been conﬁrmed from the decrease in the
di/C128erence in speciﬁc density between the glassy and
crystalline states. The new local atomic conﬁgurations
which are di/C128erent from those for the corresponding
equilibrium crystalline phases have been clariﬁed from
the atomic distance and coordination numbers of each
atomic pair among the multicomponent in the glassy
and crystalline states [22,23]. The long-range homo-
geneous atomic conﬁgurations have been interpreted
from the results [22,23] that the feature of total inter-
ference function curve is not di/C128erent from that for
ordinary amorphous alloys requiring high cooling rates
over 10
5 K/s and is analogous to that for ordinary
metallic liquid phase.
It has further been conﬁrmed that the supercooled
liquid with the above-described structural features has a
single-stage crystallization process accompanying the
simultaneous precipitation of more than three crystal-
line phases. For instance, the Zr
65Al7.5Ni10Cu17.5 glassy
alloy has the sequent phase transition of the glass tran-
sition, followed by a large supercooled liquid region and
then a single exothermic peak due to a simultaneous
precipitation of Zr
2Ni, Zr 2Cu, Zr 2Al3 and ZrNi phases
[29]. The precipitation mode requires long-range atomic
rearrangements for the progress of crystallization from
the supercooled liquid in which short-range atomic
conﬁgurations corresponding to the four intermetallic
compounds are not included. Besides, the atomic rear-

4. Bulk glassy alloy systems containing nanocrystalline
phase
It was shown that bulk glassy alloys are produced by
choosing the alloys with the three empirical rules.
However, these bulk glassy alloys crystallize through a
single exothermic reaction, accompanying the simulta-
neous precipitation of more than three kinds of crystal-
line phases. If a new bulk glassy alloy exhibiting
nanocrystallization process is found, we can propose a
new direct production method of bulk nanocrystalline
alloys consisting of the processes of the formation of a
bulk glassy alloy by casting process, followed by
annealing leading to partial crystallization in the super-
cooled liquid region. There have been no data on the
formation of a bulk nanocrystalline alloy which con-
tains a remaining glassy phase and exhibits useful
properties. We have looked for a new glassy alloy with
high glass-forming ability and nanocrystallization pro-
cess. Based on the previous results on the formation of
nanocrystalline structure, we have already noticed the
following criteria for formation of nanocrystalline
structure from an amorphous phase, i.e. (1) multistage
crystallization processes, (2) high nucleation frequency,
(3) low growth rate, and (4) high thermal stability of the
remaining amorphous phase caused by redistribution of
solute element at nanocrystal/amorphous interface.
With the aim of developing a new alloy system satisfy-
ing these criteria, we examined the inﬂuence of addi-
tional elements on the crystallization behavior and
glass-forming ability for Zr–Al–(Ni,Cu) base glassy
alloys. The three types of additional elements were
selected, i.e. (1) Pd, Pt and Au elements with much lar-
ger negative heats of mixing against Zr, (2) Ti and Nb
elements with nearly zero heat of mixing against Zr, and
(3) Ag element with positive heats of mixing against Ni
and Cu [30]. The data of the Zr-based alloys containing
Pd, Au and Nb elements will be presented as typical
examples in the present review.

6. Mechanism for nanocrystallization
It was shown that the grain size of the intermetallic
compound decreases signiﬁcantly from about 450 nm
for the Zr–Cu–Al ternary alloy to less than 10 nm for
the Pd- and Au-containing alloys. We discuss the reason
for the signiﬁcant decrease in the grain size by the
addition of Pd or Au element [31,33]. We measured
exothermic DSC curves due to the precipitation of the
compound phases at di/C128erent isothermal annealing

9. Conclusions
We are successful in preparing the bulk glassy alloys
containing nanocrystalline particles with signiﬁcantly
improved mechanical strength by controlling the
annealing and cooling rate conditions. In particular, the
bulk nanocrystalline alloys prepared by the cooling rate
control process are promising for practical high-
strength materials because of the simple direct produc-
tion method. A further optimization of alloy compo-
nents and structure-control method is also expected to
fabricate a new material with other useful properties
through a unique structural modiﬁcation.
