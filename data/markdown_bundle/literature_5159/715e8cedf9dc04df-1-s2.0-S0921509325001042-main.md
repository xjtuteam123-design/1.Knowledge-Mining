ABSTRACT
We obtained the CoCrNi alloy composite with TiC fraction as high as 5 wt% by laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) of 
blended powder of Ti, C and CoCrNi medium entropy alloy (MEA). The as-built composite shows ultra-strong 
strength ranging from 2245 MPa to 1639 MPa and increasing fracturing strain from 8.5 % to 20 % at temper -
ature ranging from 77 K to 873 K. The significantly increased plasticity is mainly attributed to the higher and 
stabler straining hardening rate at elevated temperature compared to at room temperature, which is induced by 
the increasing number of deformation-induced nano-twins and stacking faults with the increasing deformation 
temperatures. This abnormal phenomenon is supposed to result from the increasing shearing ability of the 
stacking faults (SFs) on the γ’ phase with temperature and the strengthening effect of the nano-TiC along the cell 
walls.
1. Introduction
To meet extreme temperature conditions such as in outer space, 
nuclear industry, and aviation, high strength alloy over wide tempera -
ture is needed. The conventional alloy such as stainless steel, nickel- 
based super alloy cannot serve over wide temperature range, because 
they are designed just for specific temperature [ 1 – 3 ]. Recently 
strengthening CoCrNi-based MEA by precipitation of γ ′ , B2 or nano-TiC 
phase has been proposed to achieve high strength over wide tempera -
ture [ 4 – 7 ]. The strength as high as 1500 MPa at 298 K and 1200 MPa at 
873 K has been reported by these means [ 4 ]. However, further 
enhancement on the strength of the CoCrNi-based alloy over wide 
temperature is challenging because the difficulties during the fabrica -
tion of MEA with higher fraction of nanoprecipitations, such as the 
agglomeration of nano TiC or cracking susceptibility during the LPBF 
process [ 8 , 9 ]. We have previously eliminated the agglomeration of nano 
TiC in the CoCrNi composite containing high fraction of TiC by the 
in-situ alloying of TiC with Ti and C elemental powders during the LPBF 
process. As a result, the ultrahigh ambient strength up to 1800 MPa was 
achieved. However, the tensile behavior of the as-built composite from 
cryogenic conditions to elevated temperatures has not been 
investigated. Though nano-TiC reinforced alloys such as Ti-alloy, 
Al-alloy and steel shows potential to have better performance at high 
temperature or cryogenic conditions [ 10 – 12 ], the performance of the 
nano-carbide strengthened MEA over wide temperatures ranging from 
77 K to 1073 K still lacks understanding. In addition, the cell structure 
(especially the cells strengthened by the precipitations) in the LPBFed 
alloys has been reported to exert a strong influence on the ambient and 
high temperature mechanical behavior, while the 
temperature-dependent influence of the cell structure decorated by 
several kinds of precipitations, such as nano-carbides and γ’ phase, on 
the evolution of the defects, such as twins and SFs, is still ambiguous. We 
found in this research that the composite experienced an unusual in -
crease on the plasticity with increasing temperature, which was caused 
by the weakened restriction of nano-carbides and γ’ phase on the twins 
and SFs with temperature.
In this paper, we LPBF fabricated the agglomeration-free CoCrNi 
composite containing TiC as high as 5 wt% [ 6 ]. The CoCrNi + 5 wt%TiC 
composite shows ultra-high strength over wide temperatures range and 
increasing plasticity with increasing temperature. The underlying 
deformation mechanism relative to the cell structure was analyzed.

2. Materials and methods
The blended powders of CoCrNi pre-alloyed spherical powder 
(average size of 35 μ m), spherical Ti powder (average size of 15 μ m) and 
nano-C powder (average size of 26 nm) with CoCrNi: Ti: C weight ratio 
of 95:4:1 was tumbling mixed for 2 h and further balling milled for 2 h 
with a ball to powder ratio of 2:1 at 450 rpm. Then the blended powders 
were processed by the BLT s200 machine to perform the LPBF process. 
The blended powders were laser melted twice with laser power of 180 w 
and scan speed of 1000 mm/s, with hatch space of 80 μ m and powder 
layer thickness of 30 μ m. The interlayer rotation angle of scan direction 
was set to 67
◦
. The dog-bone shape specimens with gauge length and 
diameter of 15 mm and 3 mm, respectively, were machined out from the 
as-built blocks for tensile test, with gauge length along the transverse 
direction (TD), which was perpendicular to the building direction (BD) 
during the LPBF process. The specimens were tensile tested at 77 K, 298 
K, 873 K, and 1073 K, respectively, with straining rate of 0.001/s. 
During the cryogenic and elevated temperature tensile test, the speci -
mens were holding for 10 min at set temperature to ensure the uniform 
of temperature in the specimens. The microstructure was observed 
before and after the tensile test using scan electron microscopy (SEM), 
transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and electric backscattered 
diffraction (EBSD).

especially at 873 K. The fracturing strain of the as-built composite is

as-built composite dropped again, it is still higher than the value at 298 
K. So, generally speaking, the increase from room temperature to 
elevated temperature leads to the increased plasticity for as-built 
CoCrNi + 5 wt%TiC. In summary, the CoCrNi + 5 wt% composite shows 
ultrahigh strength over wide temperature, especially at 873 K, at which 
both the high strength and high plasticity were obtained.

The CoCrNi composite developed here shows a totally different 
temperature-dependent twining behavior from the CoCrNi MEA, which 
is very interesting. The temperature-dependent critical stress for initia -
tion of twinning in CoCrNi-based single phase MEAs is generally related 
to the stacking fault energy and grain size [ 26 ]. However, the calculated 
value(839 MPa) of twinning stress is far lower than the Yielding stress 
(1468 MPa) of the as-built CoCrNi + 5 wt%TiC composite at room tem -
perature, as shown in the Supplementary materials , which means the 
deformation induced twinning should fully developed during room 
temperature tension. In contrast, we didn ’ t find twins in the specimen 
fractured at 298K, as shown in Fig. 4 (c). So, this method is not suitable 
for explaining the twinning behavior of the CoCrNi + 5 wt%TiC com -
posite reported here.
To understand the abnormally increased deformation induced 
twinning with temperature, we noticed that there are some nano γ’ 
particles with ordered L1
2 
structure in the matrix, as shown in Fig. 2
(h – i). The γ’ phase may be formed through the combination of the Ni 
and Ti elements enhanced by the repeated intrinsic heating effect on the 
previously solidified matrix during the LPBF process. There has been 
reported that the γ’ phase in Ni-Co based superalloys and MEAs with low 
stacking energy can inhibit the twinning during deformation at room 
temperature [ 27 , 28 ]. During the deformation of these Ni-Co based al -
loys, the 1/2 < 110 > dislocations are usually dissociated to Shockley 
partial dislocations 1/6 < 112 > . When the Shockley partials shear the 
γ’ phase, the complex stacking faults (CSF) are produced [ 27 ]. The CSF 
is energetically unfavorable because the Ti elements are too close to 
each other, so the Shockley partials cannot easily shear into the γ’ phase, 
which was confirmed by Fig. 4 (d) in which the γ’ phase contained little 
SFs after tension at 298 K. This further inhibits the development of SFs 
and twins in the matrix at 77 K and 298 K, as shown in Fig. 4 (a – c). Four 
CSFs form pseudotwin structure, and through the reordering process of 
Ti and Ni atoms, a true twin is formed, thus, the high energy of CSFs can 
be reduced [ 27 ]. By this way the Shockley partials can easily shear into 
the γ’ phase and the SFs and twins can be developed. The atoms reor -
dering process can only be realized through the diffusion of the Ni and Ti 
atoms at high temperatures. Indeed, in the 873 K fractured sample, the γ’ 
particles were sheared into by many SFs and twins, as shown in Fig. 5 (d) 
and (i). Figs. 1 and 2 in the supplementary materials provide the EDS 
maps and the HRTEM image of the as-built CoCrNi + 5 wt%TiC com -
posite fractured at 1073 K, which further confirmed the γ’ particles (10 
nm in size) with composition of (Ni,Co)
3
Ti were sheared into by stacking 
faults. This further enhanced the development of twins in the matrix 
deformed at 873 K and 1073 K, as demonstrated in Fig. 5 (a) and (k). The 
increasing trend of twinning with temperature caused by γ’ phase is also 
observed for the newly developed Ni-Co-based superalloy containing 
high fraction of γ’ particles [ 29 ]. But the high-density twinning didn ’ t 
enhance the high temperature plasticity in Ni-Co-based superalloy. 
Their plasticity and strength simultaneously decreased in large at ~873 
K. In contrast, the nano-TiC particles are hard to shear by the SFs even at 
873 K, as shown in Fig. 5 (g), so they enhance the high temperature 
strength by Orowan bypass mechanism. In addition, the nano-TiC along 
the cell walls in the as-built CoCrNi + 5 wt%TiC composite suppresses the 
grain boundary brittleness at intermediate temperature. The combining 
effect of the nano-TiC and the high-density nano-twins contributes to the 
increased plasticity and straining hardening rate with temperature, 
especially at 873 K.
4. Conclusions
In summary, we obtained the agglomeration-free CoCrNi MEA 
composite with TiC fraction as high as 5 wt% by LPBF of blended powder 
of Ti, C and CoCrNi alloy. The as-built CoCrNi + 5 wt%TiC composite 
shows ultra-strong strength over wide temperatures. The plasticity in -
creases with the increasing temperature, which is attributed to the 
temperature-dependent initiation of nano-twins and stacking faults 
which induced high straining hardening rate at high temperature. This 
investigation provides new ways to develop the high-performance ma -
terials over wide range of temperatures, in particular, the materials with 
both high strength and high plasticity at 873 K.
