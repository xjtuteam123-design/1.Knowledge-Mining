ABSTRACT 
In this study, the deformation behavior of an equiatomic VNbTa medium-entropy alloy with a BCC single phase is 
studied by tensile tests at cryogenic temperatures. The results show that the alloy presents a strong strain 
hardening capability, which leads to a high ultimate tensile strength of 1930 MPa while maintaining a high 
fracture elongation of ~27%, without a ductile to brittle transition. The excellent cryogenic temperature me -
chanical properties of the present alloy are attributed to the cooperative of {112} 〈 111 〉 deformation twins, 
dislocation interaction, and entanglement caused by the continuing cross-slip of dislocation. The localized severe 
lattice distortions caused by the large atomic size misfit of elements among Nb, Ta and V play a major role in the 
cross-slip of dislocation. 
1. Introduction 
The development of deep space, deep earth, deep sea, and polar 
explorations demands advanced structural materials to adapt to extreme 
environments, such as cryogenic temperatures and elevated tempera -
tures [ 1 – 5 ]. Compared with face centered cubic high/medium entropy 
alloys (FCC- H/MEAs), the most body centered cubic (BCC) H/MEAs 
exhibit better strength because of a higher solid solution strengthening 
effect [ 6 – 10 ]. But the deficient tensile plasticity, especially at low 
temperatures because of the prominent ductile to brittle transition, 
blocked the cryogenic application of BCC-H/MEAs [ 11 – 15 ]. 
Kink bands, mechanical twins, and deformation-induced trans -
formations are condemned as primary mechanisms for tailoring the 
deformation behavior of BCC-H/MEAs [ 16 – 22 ]. For example, VNbTi 
MEA with a high fracture elongation of ~20% at room temperature was 
produced because of the twinning and kink bands during deformation 
[ 18 ]. Even so, the mechanical twinning, screw dislocation glide, and 
deformation induced BCC to ω phase transformation contributed to the 
tensile elongation of ~20.8% in TiZrHfNbTa HEAs at 77 K [ 20 ]. The 
composition design in near-equiatomic proportions leads to a severe 
lattice distortion in H/MEAs, which has been demonstrated to play a 
significant role in the deformation behavior of H/MEAs. Recently, the 
tuning of lattice distortion by composition optimization, such as 
increasing the misfit of atomic size, has been introduced into the 
strengthening and toughening of H/MEAs [ 24 – 26 ]. The underlying 
mechanisms can be attributed to factors such as the fact that the dis -
torted lattice increases the lattice friction by affecting the distribution of 
local stress, thus resulting in a strong resistance to dislocation motion, 
stacking faults, cross slip, dislocation pinning, and dislocation multi -
plication. It should be noted that the large difference in atomic size in -
creases the tendency to form intermediate compounds. Therefore, the 
tailoring misfit of atomic size should be carried out under the premise of 
forming primary solid solutions. 
Our previous work proved that the single BCC VNbTa MEA possessed 
an excellent combination of strength and ductility (the yield strength 
yield strength of ~925 MPa and fracture elongation ~26%) at room 
temperature [ 27 ]. The remarkable mechanical properties are mainly 
attributed to solid solution strengthening caused by large atomic size 
misfits of Nb, Ta, and V. Additionally, based on molecular dynamics 
(MD) simulations, Zhou et al. recently presented a cross-kink control 
screw dislocation deformation in VNbTa MEA, which could obtain an 
excellent combination of strength and ductility at different temperatures

2. Experimental methods 
The equiatomic VNbTa MEAs were produced by vacuum arc melting 
of pure elements (purity > 99.5%) under a Ti-gettered high-purity argon 
atmosphere. Ingots were flipped and completely remelted at least eight 
times to improve the homogeneity of all constituent elements. The 
melting alloy was dropped casting into a water-cooled copper mold with 
dimensions of 10 mm × 20 mm × 30 mm. The initial microstructure of 
only the BCC phase with an average grain size of 44.28 μ m can be ob -
tained [ 27 ]. Flat dog-bone-shaped samples with a gauge section of 6 mm 
(length) × 2.5 mm (width) × 1.5 mm (thickness) were sliced from 
rectangular samples using an electric discharge machine. The uniaxial 
tensile tests were conducted on a SUNS CMT 5105 testing machine with 
a strain rate of 2.5 × 10

at 77 K. The tensile samples and grips were 
immersed in a liquid nitrogen bath for 15 min before the test and held 
completely submerged during the test. All the tests were repeated at 
least 3 times to obtain reproducible tensile properties. The phase and 
microstructures of deformed samples were characterized by X-ray 
diffraction (XRD), scanning electron microscopy (SEM), electron back -
scattering diffraction (EBSD), and transmission electron microscopy 
(TEM). The XRD was performed with a scanning speed of 4 deg./min and 
a scanning angle of 20 – 100 deg. The samples for EBSD analysis were 
ground using SiC sandpapers, polished using a silica Nano-suspension 
solution with a granularity of 0.04 μ m, and then corroded by a mixed 
solution of 16.7% nitric acid, 33.3% hydrofluoric acid, and 50.0% 
glycerol. The samples for TEM examination were prepared by twin-jet 
electro-polishing of mechanically pre-thinned to 50 μ m foils. The de -
tails of the EBSD and TEM measurements can be found in other works 
[ 6 , 29 ]. The lattice distortion was analyzed using first-principles calcu -
lations based on density functional theory (DFT) using the Vienna ab 
initio simulation package (VASP) with the projector augmented wave 
(PAW). The special-quasirandom-structures (SQS) supercell containing 
54 atoms was constructed using a Monte Carlo search optimization al -
gorithm. Using the Monkhorst-Pack scheme, the 5 × 5 × 5 k-points were 
used for the Brillouin zone integration. Other simulation details refer to 
the literatures [ 25 , 26 ].

During the deformation of VNbTa specimens, the severe lattice 
distortion increases the local lattice friction, which can restrain dislo -
cation motion along the initial silp plane, inducing the cross-slips of 
basal dislocations to suppress strain localization. Eventually, the 
continuing cross-slip of dislocations causes serious dislocation in -
teractions, resulting in an intense strain hardening ability [ 47 – 49 ]. In 
addition, the severe lattice distortion also benefits to the activation of 
twinning deformation. As we know, twinning is a tuning mechanical of 
plastic deformation that can be activated when silp cannot be carried 
out, especially when BCC metals are deformed at cryogenic tempera -
tures. It has been reported that the twins in H/MEAs are formed pref -
erentially at the conjugate sliding plane via the formation and glide of 
Schockley partial dislocations layer by layer, derived from the 
continuing cross-slip and decomposition of full dislocations [ 24 ]. That is 
to say, the formation of high-density deformation twins during plastic 
deformation of VNbTa alloy at 77 K is also closely related to the cross 
slip induced by severe lattice distortion. Obviously, the unique lattice 
distortion effect of H/MEAs makes it more precise and continuous to 
optimize their mechanical properties by tailoring the lattice compared 
with the traditional methods, such as interface and cluster tuning. 
Therefore, the tuning lattice by composition optimization is a control -
lable and efficient strengthening and toughening method for H/MEAs.

5. Conclusions 
In summary, the cryogenic mechanical properties of the as-cast 
VNbTa MEA are investigated in the present study. An ultra-high yield 
strength and excellent strain hardening ability at cryogenic tempera -
tures are obtained for the VNbTa MEA. The tensile yield strength in -
creases from ~925 MPa at 298 K to ~1477 MPa at 77 K. The strain 
hardening exponent of ~0.76 gives rise to an ultimate tensile strength of 
1930 MPa while maintaining a high tensile fracture elongation of 
~27%. Additionally, no ductile to brittle transition is observed. The 
excellent cryogenic mechanical properties of alloys are ascribed to the 
synergistic effects of mechanical twins, dislocation interaction, and 
entanglement caused by the continuing cross-slip of screw dislocation. 
The large atomic size misfit of elements among Nb, Ta, and V inducing 
the local severe lattice distortion plays a critical role in the cross-slip of 
dislocation.
