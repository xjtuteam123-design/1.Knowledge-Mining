Abstract 
A FeCoNiCrTi 
0.2 high-entropy alloy strengthened by two types of coh erent nano-
precipitates but with the same composition was fabr icated, and its tensile properties at 
room (293 K) and cryogenic temperatures (77 K) and the corresponding defect-structure 
evolution were investigated. Compared with the sing le-phase FeCoNiCr parent alloy, the 
precipitation-strengthened FeCoNiCrTi 0.2 high-entropy alloy exhibits a significant 
increase in yield strength and ultimate tensile str ength but with little sacrifice in ductility. 
Similar to the single-phase FeCoNiCr high-entropy a lloy, the deformation behavior of 
this precipitation-strengthened FeCoNiCrTi 0.2 high-entropy alloy shows strong

1. Introduction 
As emerging structural materials, single-phase high -entropy alloys (HEAs) with 
equiatomic or near-equiatomic concentrations have r apidly garnered much attention for 
transforming the conventional alloy design concept by shifting the search space of useful 
alloys from the corners of phase diagrams to their center regions [1-5]. Some single-
phase HEAs with face-centered cubic (fcc) structure exhibit excellent resistance under 
irradiation [6-9] and outstanding mechanical perfor mance especially at cryogenic 
temperatures [10-13], making them strong candidates for engineering applications in 
extreme environments. For example, when the tempera ture decreases from room to 
cryogenic temperatures, the single-phase fcc HEAs m ade of 3d transition metals (e.g., 
FeCoNiCr and FeCoNiCrMn) show both enhanced strengt h and ductility due to the onset 
of twinning at cryogenic temperatures [11, 13-16], making them the toughest alloys. 
However, their applications are limited by their lo w yield strength (YS). Though these 
HEAs have a large fluctuation of local lattice dist ortion [17-19], experiments revealed 
that it only provides Labusch-type weak pinning on dislocation motion [19, 20]. An 
effective solution to the low YS is to incorporate other strengthening mechanisms, 
including grain refinement [21-23] and precipitation strengthening [24-35]. 
Strength and ductility are mutually exclusive prope rties in metals and alloys, and grain 
refinement and precipitation strengthening inevitably lead to a strength-ductility trade-off. 
These single-phase fcc HEAs, however, are extremely ductile, making balancing YS and 
ductility relatively easy through proper grain refi nement or precipitation strengthening. 
Several works have been conducted to improve the YS of the FeCoNiCr-based HEA by 
adding precipitate-forming elements, such as Mo, Nb, Cu, Al and Ti [24-35]. For the case

3.1 Microstructure 
The XRD pattern obtained from the aged FeCoNiCrTi 
0.2 HEA (denoted by Ti-HEA 
hereafter) was shown in Fig. 1a. It appears that th e aged Ti-HEA has only one phase with 
the fcc structure. The lattice constant of this fcc phase is 3.585 Å determined by the

constant of the aged Ti-HEA slightly increases by 0 .5%. Its microstructure was 
characterized by EBSD, as shown in Fig. 1b. We can see that the aged Ti-HEA has 
equiaxed grains but with non-uniorm grain sizes varying from 10 to 100 µm. The average

twins shown in Fig. 1b suggest that Ti-HEA also has a low SFE.

4.2 Deformation Mechanisms 
To understand the mechanism of the temperature-depe ndent plastic deformation in the 
aged Ti-HEA, it is insightful to examine the deform ation defects. Fig. 10 presents TEM 
images taken from the uniform deformation region (a way from the facture surface) in the 
sample deformed at 293 K. At 293 K, deformation occ urs mainly by dislocation activity 
up to fracture, accompanied by minor SFs. Deformati on twinning was not observed after 
sampling more than 20 grains. The dislocation struc ture is characterized by a 
configuration of planar glide along two non-coplana r slip systems, forming high-density 
dislocation walls with a spacing distance between 1 00 and 400 nm (Fig. 10a). These

5. Conclusions 
A L1 
2-structured, (Ni, Co) 3Ti-type precipitation-strengthened FeCoNiCrTi 0.2 HEA was 
fabricated. Microstructure was characterized by com bining XRD, SEM, APT and TEM. 
Its tensile properties at room temperature (293 K) and liquid nitrogen temperature (77 K) 
and the corresponding defect-structure evolution we re investigated by TEM. Based on 
our observations, the following conclusions can be drawn. 
(a) After aging at 1073 K for 1 h, precipitates with la mellar and spherical shapes were 
formed near the grain boundaries and in the inner g rains, respectively, due to 
heterogeneous and homogeneous nucleation behaviors. These two types of 
precipitates have the same composition, but the lam ellae have LPSO structures, 15R, 
12R and 4H, with chemical disorder, while the nano- particles are the ordered, L1 2-
structured γ′ phase.

(b) Compared with the single-phase FeCoNiCr HEA, the pr ecipitation-strengthened 
FeCoNiCrTi 0.2 HEA has a significant increase in YS and UTS but w ith a little 
sacrifice of ductility. Similar to the single-phase HEA, the deformation behavior of 
this precipitation-strengthened FeCoNiCrTi 0.2 HEA shows strong temperature 
dependence. When the temperature decreases from 293 K to 77 K, its yield strength 
and UTS are increased from 700 MPa to 860 MPa and f rom 1.24 to 1.58 GPa,

(c) When the temperature decreases from 293 K to 77 K, the deformation mechanisms 
change from the dislocation-dominant to SF-controll ed modes, indicating the reduced 
SFE with decreasing temperature. At 293 K, the defo rmation up to fracture in the 
precipitation-strengthened FeCoNiCrTi 0.2 HEA mainly occurs by the planar 
dislocation glide, accompanied by minor SFs. At 77 K, multiple-layered SF bundles

(d) Different from the single-phase FeCoNiCr HEA, twinn ing rarely happens in the γ′-
strengthened regions even when the aged FeCoNiCrTi 0.2 HEA is deformed at 77 K, 
suggesting that twin nucleation is impeded by the γ′ nano-particles. Microtwins, 
however, are observed in the regions strengthened b y chemically disordered lamellar 
precipitates, indicating that the chemical ordering of the γ′ nano-particles dramatically 
increases the energy barrier for twin nucleation.
