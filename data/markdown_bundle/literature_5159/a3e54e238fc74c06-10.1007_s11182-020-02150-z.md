THERMAL STABILITY OF MICROSTRUCTURE AND 
MECHANICAL PROPERTIES OF V–Me(Cr, W)–Zr ALLOYS AS 
A FUNCTION OF HEAT TREATMENT MODES 
K. V. Grinyaev,1,2 I. V. Smirnov,1,2 I. A. Ditenberg,1,2 UDC 548.4; 669:621.039; 669-17; 620.186.8 
A. N. Tyumentsev,1,2 and V. M. Chernov3 
Using the methods of scanning and transmission electron microscopy, a study is performed of the influence of 
high-temperature annealing on the parameters of structural-phase states of low-activation alloys of the V–
Me(Cr, W)–Zr system after different modes of thermomechanical treatments. By the active tensile loading at 
different temperatures the effect of the treatment regimes and final annealing temperatures on the strength and 
plasticity characteristics is investigated. The main factor s determining the thermal stability of plasticity of the 
structural-phase state and the mechanical properties of these alloys are discussed. 
Keywords: vanadium alloys, thermomechanical treatment, microstructure, mechanic al properties, thermal 
stability. 
INTRODUCTION 
The development of a new generation of low-activation vanadium-based alloys for nuclear-fission and 
synthesis reactors [1–16] is one of the most important issues of the present-day reactor materials science. Relying on the 
generalization of the literature data, we showed earlier [16] that heat resistance of vanadium alloys of the V–Ti–Cr 
system [1–10] is essentially limited by a rather small volume fraction of the strengthening fine-dispersed second-phase 
interstitial particles and their insufficient thermal stability . According to [7–16], an increase in heat resistance of 
vanadium alloys is ensured by a higher efficiency of di spersion strengthening due to a change of the elemental 
composition and the use of advanced treatment methods offering a possibility of forming in the material a large volume 
fraction of homogeneously distributed fine-dispersed non-me tallic particles with a high thermal stability. In [16], the 
first results were reported on designing the V–Me(Cr, W)–Zr– С alloys by their purpose-oriented doping with the 
interstitial (C, N, O) and substitutional (W, Zr) elements. Th e influence of the modes of thermomechanical treatment 
(TMT) on modification of the structural-phase state and level of strength properties was investigated. A high efficiency 
of the use of the modified treatment mode TMT-II was shown, which provides nanostructuring of the heterophase 
structure during realization of the ph ase transformation mechanism via the solution of metastable vanadium carbides 
followed by precipitation of a stable phase from the solid so lution. As a result, high dispersion strengthening effects 
were achieved, accompanied by an increased short-term strength and a retained considerable reserve of low-temperature 
plasticity.

In the present study, a comparative investigation is performed of the effect of TMT modes on the thermal

properties at different temperatures. 
EXPERIMENTAL MATERIALS AND PROCEDURE 
The compositions of the experimental alloys, received from SC VNIINM, Moscow, are listed in Table 1. 
The initial samples represented the sheets 1 and 3 mm in thickness, which were fabricated by a traditional TMT 
method (TMT-I) [16]); its major stages are listed below: 
1. Eight-hour homogenizing annealing of the ingot at a temperature of 1300°C. 
2. Extrusion (pressing) at an elevated temperature. 
3. Several rolling cycles and upsetting at room temperature with a few intermediate anneals at the temperatures 
of 950–1000°С. 
4. One-hour stabilizing vacuum annealing at the temperatures 1000 and 1100°С. 
TMT-II was performed after the third stage of TM T-I; it consists in one-hour annealing at 1400° С and several 
(at least 3) cycles “ ε = 30–50% rolling at 20° С + annealing at 600–700°C” [16]. The stabilizing one-hour vacuum 
annealing was performed at the temperature 1000 or 1100°С. 
After TMT-I and TMT-II, one-hour anneals were performed in vacuum at 1200, 1300, 1400 and 1500° С. The 
characteristics of the short-term strength (σ0.1) and plasticity (δ) of the samples were determined by the method of active 
tension in vacuum at 20, 800 and 900°С. 
The Electron Backscatter Diffraction patterns (EBSD) were obtained in a FEI Quanta 200 3D (30 kV) scanning 
electron microscope with a Pegasus anal ytical system. The data were obtained and analyzed using the TSL OIM data 
collection and TSL OIM data analysis software programs. 
The transmission electron microscopy (TEM) investiga tions were carried out in the Philips CM 30 TWIN 
(300 kV) and Philips СМ 12 (120 kV) electron microscopes. 
The foils for TEM examinations were prepared by the jet electrolytic polishing in a MIKRON-3М facility in an 
electrolyte (20% H2SO4 + CH3OH) at a voltage of 15 V. The sample surfaces for EBSD after mechanical grinding were 
electrolytically polished in the above-specified regime. 
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION 
Figure 1a presents an illustration of a typical pattern of the EBSD grain orientation maps of Alloy 1 after TMT-
I with a final annealing at 1000° С. It has been found out that the grains are nearly equiaxed in their shapes, and their 
sizes lie within the interval from 2 to 20 µm. The most probable grain size is ≈5 µm. 
Alloy 2 after TMT-I is charact erized by the presence of anisotropic grains (Fig. 1 b), whose dimensions in the 
rolling direction (RD) lie within the range of 2–50 µm, with the average size in this direction being ≈10 µm. In the 
direction perpendicular to RD these grains measur e from about 20 to 30 µm with the average size ≈5 µm. In certain 
coarse grains of Alloy 2 a gradient coloring is observed, wh ich implies the presence of stru ctural states with low-angle 
(discrete or continuous) misorientations. 
In the alloys, there are dislocation ensembles in the shape of coils and nets. It has to be noted that the 
dislocation density in Alloy 1 reaches 109–1010 cm−2, while in Alloy 2 it is an order of magnitude lower (10 8–109 cm−2). 
This fact indicates the absence of the collective recrystallization processes.

A different type of microstructure is formed in A lloys 1 and 2 after TMT-II with a final annealing at Т = 
1000°С (Fig. 1 c, d ). Both alloys are characterized by a high mi crostructural inhomogeneity. There are both coarse 
grains a few tens of micrometers in the width, elongated in the RD to 100 µm and more, and fine grains whose 
dimensions are within 10 µm. The latter either have anisotropic shapes and are predominantly elongated in the RD or 
have equiaxed shapes. Inside all of the coarse and fine anisotropic grains a gradient coloring is observed, while in the 
equiaxed grains there is no such coloring. The dislocation density is significantly (nearly an order of magnitude) 
increased, its values in Alloy 1 are as high as ≈5·1010 cm−2. 
The heterophase structure of both alloys after TMT-I and TMT-II with a final annealing at 1000° С is 
qualitatively similar and is presented by the main particle fractions: coarse (more than 1 µm), submicron (50–300 nm) 
(Fig. 2a, c) and nanosized (2–10 nm) (Fig. 2 b, d). An electron diffraction analysis demonstrated that the coarse, fine, 
and nanosized particles in both alloys have an FCC-lattice with the parameters ≈3.1, ≈4.2 and ≈4.6 Å and can be 
identified as VO, VC and ZrC, respectively [17]. 
After TMT-I, coarse particles, precipitated during coo ling of the ingot, in the course of rolling in the TMT 
stages arrange in rows parallel to the RD [16], while nanosized particles are distributed inhomogeneously (Fig. 2 b). In 
the case of TMT-II, a more homogeneous distribution of the fine-dispersed phase (Fig. 2 d) over the bulk is observed, 
which results from the effective transfor mation of the initial coarse precipitates of metastable vanadium carbides into 
stable ZrC particles.

It has been found out that in Alloy 1 afte r TMT-I and II with a final annealing at Т = 1000°С, a larger volume 
fraction of fine grains is typically formed compared to Alloy 2. This grain structure peculiarity and its high dislocation 
density are a consequence of a higher density of fine-dispers ed particles of the interstitial phases (O, C, N) formed after 
TMT, which, by pinning dislocations, represent important factors of a combined dispersion and substructural 
strengthening [18, 19]. 
The estimates made on the basis of the chemical comp osition data (Table 1) demonstrate that the volume 
fractions (f) of the second phase in Alloys 1 and 2 are ≈0.26 and 0.16%, respectively. Therefore, even in the case of 
partial dispersion of the coarse second-phase precipitate s under the same TMT conditions in Alloy 1 the fraction of 
nanosized particles is larger than that in Alloy 2. 
Annealing of the samples of both alloys, subjected to TMT-I, at 1100°C (0.63 Тmelt) results in the formation of 
structural states with the grains of nearly equiaxed shapes (Fig. 3 a, b) with the sizes from a few to tens of micrometers 
(with the average size being ≈8.5 µm). Inside these grains th ere is no gradient coloring, which indicates the absence of 
defect substructures with low-angle (discrete or continuous) misorientations. 
After annealing at 1100°C, the structure of Alloys 1 and 2, sub jected to TMT-II, is sti ll characterized by high 
inhomogeneity (Fig. 3c,d) as is the case at 1000° С. In both alloys, finer (from 3 to 30 µm) nearly equiaxed grains are 
observed to form at the background of the initial coarse grains of anisotropic shapes elongated in the RD. The gradient 
coloring is preserved in many of the coarse and fine grains. Summing up, the processes of recovery, polygonization and 
primary crystallization in Alloys 1 and 2 after TMT-I and annealing at 1100° С favor the formation of a more 
homogeneous structure compared to that formed by annealing at the temperature of 1000°C (see Fig. 1a, b). In the case 
of TMT-II, a partial recrystallization gives rise to the forma tion of interlayers made of fine grains between the initial 
anisotropic grains. 
An increase in the annealing temperature to 1200° С (0.67 Тmelt) is accompanied by an increase (to 12 µm) in 
the average grain size in Alloys 1 (Fig. 4a) and 2 (Fig. 4b) after TMT-I. 
After modification of Alloys 1 and 2 in accordance with TMT-II and annealing at 1200° С coarse grains 
elongated in the RD remain in them: in Alloy 1 – up to 50 µm (Fig. 4 c), and in Alloy 2 – up to 100 µm (Fig. 4d). At the 
background of these coarse grains, there appear nearly equiaxed grains measuring from 5 to 30 µm. From our 
viewpoint, these grains form as a result of fragmentation of th e initial anisotropic grains in the course of relaxation of 
the highly defective structure. The remaining coarse grains retain their gradient coloring.

SUMMARY 
It has been shown that the use of TMT-II for modifica tion of the heterophase structure of alloys of the V–
Me(Cr, W)–Zr system by solving metastable vanadium carbides, followed by precipitation of a stable phase from the 
solid solution, allows achieving high density of fine-dispersed (to 10 nm) zirconium-based particles homogeneously 
distributed over the bulk of the material. 
Such a transformation of the structur al-phase state ensures an effectiv e suppression of the processes of 
secondary recrystallization of these experimental alloys to 1 200°C inclusive. Doping with tungsten favors a decrease in 
the rate of precipitation of the second-phase particles along the grain boundaries and results in a higher thermal stability 
of the microstructure. 
Modification of the grain and heterophase structure of the V–Me(Cr, W)–Zr alloys during TMT-II gives rise to 
a considerable (to 40%) increase in the strength ( σ0.1) properties, with the acceptable pl asticity values retained. This 
increase is provided by a high density of nanosized particles homogeneously distributed over the bulk, which pin the 
defect structure elements, and by a higher efficiency of the combined dispersion and substructural strengthening. 
A higher thermodynamic stability of the non-metallic pha se particles in the V–Me(Cr, W)–Zr alloys together 
with the increased thermal stability of nanostructured and heterophase defect substructure ensure a significant (by about 
100°С) increase in the thermal stability of the short-term strength characteristics.
