Abstract 
Compared to conventional high temperature alloys, refractory high -entropy 
alloys (HEAs) have shown superior mechanical properties at room and elevated 
temperatures. Nevertheless, high density and consequent low specific strength restrict 
their engineering application. To address these issues, alloying light-weight Al can to 
some degree relieve these problems, and a series of AlxHfNbTiZr (x = 0 - 1.5 in mole 
ratio) HEAs were synthesized by arc melting technique. Alloying effects of Al on 
structural transition and mechanical properties were systematically investigated . The 
Al0HfNbTiZr HEA with excellent plasticity consists of a single random 
body-centered cubic (BCC) solid solution phase . When x reaches 0.75, new ordered 
ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT

## Page 4

BCC phase is formed, whose lattice constant is quite close to the random BCC phase. 
Under the premise of the small atomic size difference (~ 4.9%), high negative mixing 
enthalpy (<-15.63) and the low valence electron concentration (< 4.08) induced by Al 
addition promote the ordered structural transformation. Phase transition results in the 
drastic increase of compressive yield strength and microhardness accompanied by an 
evident plasticity reduction. Encouragingly, the compressive specific yield strength is 
gradually enhanced form 82.5 (x = 0) to 253.4 (x = 1.5) MPa cm 3 g-1, which is on 
account of the density reduction and distinct strengthening effect. 
Keywords: High-entropy alloys; Al addition; Mechanical properties; Phase transition; 
Specific strength.

1. Introduction 
Being d istinct from the conventional alloys containing one or two principal 
components, high -entropy alloys (HEAs) represent a new concept of multi-element 
alloys composed of at least four elements with equiatomic or near equiatomic 
compositions [1, 2]. Owing to the unique microstructure such as various major phases 
of body-centered cubic (BCC), face-centered cubic (FCC) or hexagonal close-packed 
(HCP) solid solutions [3-5], HEAs show excellent mechanical properties [6-8] and 
high corrosion resistance in different test conditions [9-11]. 
Recently, refractory HEAs containing refractory elements such as Mo, W, Ta, Nb, 
etc., have gained increasing attention by virtue of their high strength, good ductility 
and softening resistance at both room and elevated temperature s [12-14]. For 
example, the compressive yield strength (σy) of HfMoTaTiZr HEA was able to reach 
1600 and 855 MPa at room temperature and 1000 ℃, respectively [12]. Despite their 
excellent mechanical properties, the high density of refractory HEAs usually results 
in low ratios of strength to weight, which becomes a significant concern and severely 
restricts their special engineering applications. It is thus urgent in need of developing 
refractory HEAs with the lower density through adding the light elements. 
On the other hand, it is always desirable to further enhance the strength of HEAs 
for the sake of high ratio of strength to weight. It has been reported that the addition 
of certain elements can lead to the presence of new microstructures that largely boost 
the mechanical properties [15-18]. For example, Nb addition induce d the formation 
of hard Laves phase in AlCoCrFeNb xNi HEA s, which effectively resisted the 
dislocation motion and improved the strength [15]. The σy of HEA with addition of 
Nb0.5 was about 2473 MPa, exhibiting a significant increment of 1100 MPa compared 
to the AlCoCrFeNi HEA without Nb. It should also be noted that the plasticity was

severely depressed at the same time . With Mo addition, σy values of refractory 
HfMoxNbTaTiZr HEAs were improved from 1015 to 1512 MPa as x increased from 0 
to 1 [17]. Although no new strengthening intermetallic phase was formed, significant 
solid solution strengthening effect was confirmed in the simple BCC structure owing 
to the small atomic size and high Young’s modulus of additional Mo. 
The refractory Al xHfNbTiZrTa (x=0~1.0) HEAs all with single BCC solid 
solution have been reported [19]. In comparison to other four principal elements, Ta 
has a high melting temperature (3290 K) and high density (16.69 g/cm3), which lead 
to melting difficulty and increase of density (9.72~8.91 g/cm3) of the reported HEAs. 
Moreover, Ta-free HfNbTiZr as a medium-entropy alloy (MEA) also consisted of a 
single BCC solid solution [20], but they also face d the common problems of low 
ratios of strength to weight of HEAs. Motivated by the above limitations, we 
developed a high strength and low density AlxHfNbTiZr HEAs. Compared to matrix 
elements, Al possesses the lower density of 2.70 g/cm3 and higher negative mixing 
enthalpy (ΔHmix) compared with the other major elements. Through adjusting and 
optimizing the Al content, we aim at reducing the density of refractory HEAs and 
enhancing their specific strength . More importantly, the relativity of microstructure 
evolution and strength enhancement upon Al addition was also studied in detail.

3.1 Microstructures and morphologies of AlxHfNbTiZr HEAs. 
The X-ray diffraction patterns of AlxHfNbTiZr HEAs are shown in Fig. 1(a). The 
Al-free HEA consists of a single random BCC solid solution phase with lattice 
constant of 3.437 Å. As Al content gradually increases from x = 0.5 to 1.5, the peak 
positions of all BCC crystal planes shift towards high diffraction angle, indicating the 
reduction of lattice co nstant. The profiles of (110)BCC peaks were zoomed -in and 
shown in Fig. 1(b), from which the peak shift can be clearly observed . The peak shift 
of only about 1°(x = 0 ~ 1.5) demonstrates the significant effect of Al addition on 
lattice constant. This variety is owing to that Al atom has th e radiu s of 1.432 Å, 
which is smaller than of Al-free HEA (1.518 Å). In addition, the diffraction peaks for 
Al0.5 HEA are broadened compared to Al-free HEA, suggesting the severe lattice 
strain and the trend of phase separation. When Al content is increased to x = 0.75, 
ordered BCC solid solution phase appears and the minor (100) diffraction peak can 
be found at 25.9 ºaccordingly as a result of structural ordering . It reflects that th e 
solubility limit of Al for this random BCC matrix after rapid solidification is between 
x = 0.5 and 0.75, corresponding to an atomic content of 11.1 ~ 15.8 at.%. When Al 
content is beyond the boundary, ordered BCC phase starts to be presented in the 
random BCC matrix. The lattice constants of the random and ordered BCC phases are 
quite close to each other as evidenced by the overlap of some diffraction peaks of the 
two phases (such as (110) and (200) peaks). Further increasing the content of Al from 
x = 0.75 to 1.5 results in the elevated peak intensity of (100) diffraction planes and

4. Discussion 
4.1 Phase formation and transition 
As mentioned above, when Al content x is equal to or greater than 0.75, ordered 
BCC solid solution phase is formed. The ordered solid solution is a medium phase 
between typical solid solution and intermetallic compound, which has both metallic 
bond and covalent bond [22]. In accordance with some reports, Al element acts as a 
strong bcc stabilizer and causes the structural ordering transition in some HEAs [20, 
23, 24]. Al atoms with filled p-orbitals possess the high electron density and Fermi 
level [25]. Transition metals Hf, Nb, Ti and Zr have partially filled d-orbitals and 
easily obtain electrons from Al, resulting in the formation of covalent bonds through 
pd hybridization and therefore the presence of ordered solid solution or intermetallics 
[25, 26]. For example, Al addition induced the structural transformation from single 
FCC phase for CoCrFeNi HEA to a nano -lamellar mixture of random and ordered 
BCC solid solutions for AlCoCrFeNi HEA [27].

5. Conclusions 
In this study, a series of Al xHfNbTiZr ( x=0-1.5 in mole ratio ) HEAs were 
synthesized and characterized. Alloying effects of Al on structural transition and 
mechanical properties were systematically investigated. Strengthening and fracture 
mechanism and s pecific yield strength of HEAs were discussed. Based on obtained

results, tentative conclusions can be summarized as follows. 
1. Al-free HEA consists of a single random BCC solid solution phase . When the 
amount of Al exceeds the solubility in random solid solution (in the range of 11.1 ~ 
15.8 at.%), ordered BCC phase with lattice constant close to random one starts to be 
formed. High electron density and Fermi level of Al atom contributes to formation of 
covalent bonds. VEC and Ω-δ criteria seem to more precisely predict the formation of 
solid solutions. Low VEC value may be in favor of ordering structural transition of 
BCC phase. 
2. Al addition in the HEAs results in a notable strengthening effect. The compressive 
yield strength is generally enhanced from 706 MPa of Al-free HEA to 1738 MPa of 
Al1.5 HEA. Increasing Al proportion induces a reduction of plasticity. Strengthening 
mechanism includes solid solution hardening and ordered phase hardening. 
3. Al addition elevates the s pecific yield strength t o a great extent . Al1.5 HEA 
represents the highest compressive specific yield strengths of 253.4 MPa cm 3 g-1, 
about three times of Al-free HEA. Both the decreased density and strengthening 
effect make contribution, and the latter is the dominant factor.
