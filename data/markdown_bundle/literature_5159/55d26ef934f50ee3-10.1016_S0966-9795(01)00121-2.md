Eﬀects of high niobium addition on the mechanical properties
and high-temperature deformability of gamma TiAl alloy
Toshimitsu Tetsui*
Mitsubishi Heavy Industries Ltd, Nagasaki Research and Development Center, 5-717-1, Fukahori-machi, Nagasaki 851-0392, Japan
Received 5 July 2001; received in revised form 28 September 2001; accepted 1 October 2001
Abstract
The eﬀects of Nb on the mechanical properties, high-temperature deformability and some other basic properties of TiAl–Nb
alloys were investigated over a wide compositional range, and an appropriate composition for high-temperature applications was
investigated for both wrought and cast materials. The lattice parameter of Ti–50Al–xNb alloy, which is ag single phase alloy,
shows an anisotropic variation with Nb content. This lattice distortion is assumed to be responsible for the strengthening and
embrittlement of high-Nb ternary alloys. In the case of wrought materials, no appropriate composition exists in the high-Nb con-
tent region since wrought materials require good forgeability and high-temperature strength which are properties of antithesis. In
the case of cast materials, however, an appropriate composition exists in the vicinity of Ti–46Al–7.5Nb (at.%).# 2002 Elsevier
Science Ltd. All rights reserved.
Keywords: A: Titanium aluminides, based on TiAl; C: Isothermal forging; D: Microstructure
1. Introduction
Nb is an essential additive in gamma TiAl alloys from
the standpoint of oxidation resistance. Because Nb is
highly soluble in the TiAl alloy [1–3], unlike other addi-
tives, it can be added in substantial volume. Accordingly,
a large number of TiAl alloys [4–18], inclusive of a wide
range ofNb content, have been proposed so far. Because
the oxidation resistance of TiAl alloys is improved with
Nb addition [19–21], the amount of Nb to be added is
generally determined depending on the oxidation resis-
tance required. Since in the early stages of development
of TiAl alloys the application targets have been set for
low-temperature usage, such as low-pressure turbine
blades for jet engines, and exhaust valves for passenger
vehicle engines, oxidation resistance has not been a
particular priority, and Nb concentration in ﬁrst-gen-
eration TiAl alloys such as GE alloy [4,5], XD alloy
[6,7] and Daido alloy [8,9] was limited to under 2 at.%.
However, because applications for high-temperature
usage such as turbochargers for passenger vehicles have
been recently targeted [10–12], the Nb content in current
TiAl alloys [10–15] is increasing.
With respect to the eﬀects of high Nb content, while
those on oxidation resistance has been investigated to a
certain extent so far, few reports have discussed the
eﬀects on mechanical properties. Furthermore, deform-
ability at high-temperature is required if isothermal
forging is assumed as a manufacturing technique for
TiAl components, but the eﬀects of high Nb content on
this property are unclear. The present study considers
the eﬀects of high Nb content on the mechanical prop-
erties and high-temperature deformability, as well as on
some other basic properties over a wide compositional
range of TiAl–Nb ternary alloys. In addition, on the
basis of these results, appropriate compositions of tern-
ary alloys are investigated for both wrought and cast
materials.

3. Results
3.1. Speciﬁc weight
Fig. 3 presents the speciﬁc weight for each ternary
composition. Speciﬁc weight increases with Nb con-
centration, and that of 10Nb alloys is 1.1 times higher
than 0Nb alloys. Accordingly, in the case of rotating
components, the centrifugal stress of 10Nb alloys
becomes 1.1 times greater than that for 0Nb alloys, and
10Nb alloys are, therefore, required to be stronger by as
much as 1.1 times that of 0Nb alloys.

4.1.2. Mechanical properties
In the case ofg single phase alloys, although hardness
and tensile strength increased, ductility decreased with
Nb content. Thus, Nb is found to strengthen, but also
to embrittle the g phase. The anisotropic lattice para-
meter change by Nb is assumed to relate to this beha-
vior. In other words, lattice distortion caused by Nb is
considered to be responsible for this strengthening and
embrittlement of theg phase.
4.2. Appropriate composition of TiAl–Nb alloys
4.2.1. Nb concentration
Appropriate Nb concentration was investigated from
the results of this study for application for high-tem-
perature components in which the maximum possible
high-temperature strength and oxidation resistance are
ﬁrstly required and elongation is the next. As mentioned
above, although strength is improved, elongation
decreased with Nb content. Furthermore, from the oxi-
dation testing [23] that will be reported separately, it
was found that oxidation resistance of TiAl–Nb alloys is
improved up to 7.5 Nb. It is accordingly concluded that
7.5 at.% is an optimum concentration of Nb.
4.2.2. Al concentration
Al concentration should be considered independently
for wrought and cast materials. In the case of wrought
materials, from the results of the high-temperature
deformability test in this study, assuming standard iso-
thermal forging facilities, the only high-Nb ternary alloy
that can be forged is 50Al alloy. Thus, the applicability
of 50Al alloy was ﬁrst considered from the perspective
of tensile strength. Fig. 12 presents a comparison of
speciﬁc tensile strength between isothermally forged
50Al–7.5Nb, which possesses the highest strength, and
wrought Inconel 718. The speciﬁc strength of 50Al–
7.5Nb is lower at low and intermediate temperatures,
barely reaching equivalency near 800
/C14C. Because higher
speciﬁc strength than for current materials is the lowest
prerequisite condition for the application of TiAl, this
strength is insuﬃcient. Consequently, because forge-
ability and high-temperature strength are antithetical, it
is found that any appropriate composition for wrought

5. Conclusion
In thepresentstudy,theeﬀectsofNbonthemechanical
properties and high-temperature deformability and some
other basic properties were investigated over a wide com-
positional range of TiAl–Nb ternary alloys. On the basis
of these results, appropriate ternary compositions were
investigated for both wrought and cast materials. The
following results were obtained: (1) Although the phase
combination of ternary alloys below 15 at.% Nb is the
same as that for binary alloys, the addition of Nb
beyond this concentration causes the appearance of the
s phase. (2) Deformability of high-Nb alloys is depen-
dent on the phase combination. High-temperature
deformability of g+a
2 dual phase alloys is extremely
poor. Only g single phase alloys can be forged by stan-
dard isothermal forging facilities. (3) The lattice para-
meter of 50Al–0–15Nb g single phase alloys, shows an
anisotropic change with Nb content. This lattice distor-
tion is assumed to be responsible for the strengthening
and embrittlement of high Nb ternary alloys. (4) In the
case of wrought materials, assuming application for
high-temperature components, it is found that any
appropriate composition does not exist in the high-Nb
ternary alloys because of the requirement for the anti-
thetical properties of forgeability and high-temperature
strength. (5) On the other hand, in the case of cast mate-
rials, an appropriate composition exists in the vicinity of
Ti–46Al–7.5Nb (at.%).
