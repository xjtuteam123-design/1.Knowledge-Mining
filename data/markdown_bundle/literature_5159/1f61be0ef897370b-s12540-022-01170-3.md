Exploring the Heat Treatment Parameters, Microstructural Evolution, 
and Mechanical Properties of Ti–6Al–4V Alloy Fabricated by a Selective 
Laser Melting Process
Xing Gao1 · Ning Zhang1 · Yi Zhang1 · Mingrui Chen1 · Yan Ding2 · Bo Jiang1  · Yazheng Liu1
Received: 5 December 2021 / Accepted: 4 January 2022 / Published online: 18 May 2022 
© The Korean Institute of Metals and Materials 2022
Abstract
The effects of heat treatment parameters on the mechanical properties of a selective laser melting Ti–6Al–4V alloy were 
studied using an L9 (34) type orthogonal test. Results showed that the solution temperature and aging temperature were the 
most influential factors relative to the mechanical properties. The optimum heat treatment was then obtained as 920 °C/2 h/
water quenching + 550 °C/3 h/air cooling and the corresponding tensile strength was 1045.2 MPa and the elongation was 
13.6%. Compared to a solution temperature of 850 °C, more fine secondary α phase (αS) and higher geometrically necessary 
dislocations (GND) density were obtained when solution temperature was 920 °C, which promoted the increased strength 
by increasing the interface strengthening effect of αS/β and the dislocation strengthening. However, the microstructure of the 
continuously coarsening α GB resulted in decreased elongation when the solution was at 920 °C. As the aging temperature 
increased from 550 to 650 °C, the GND density was reduced and the microstructures of primary α (α P), αS with various 
crystallographic orientations, lamellar α, and equiaxial α P were coarsened. Therefore, the elongation of the specimen aging 
at a higher aging temperature increased while the strength decreased.
Keywords Selective laser melting · Ti–6Al–4V alloy · Orthogonal experiment · Solution temperature · Aging temperature
1 Introduction
Ti–6Al–4V alloy is a widely used engineering material for 
a variety of applications, such as aerospace and astronau-
tics, biomedical implants, automotive, marine engineering 
industries, etc., due to its combination of mechanical proper-
ties, exceptional corrosion and heat resistance, and superior 
biocompatibility [1 –6]. However, due to the low thermal 
conductivity, high chemical reactivity, and high deforma-
tion resistance of this alloy, its formability is poor. There -
fore, a Ti–6Al–4 V alloy manufactured with conventional 
methods (e.g., casting and forging processes) often requires 
much longer manufacturing time. In addition, it is difficult 
to fabricate complex structures [7 , 8]. Additive manufac-
turing (AM) technology, which uses a high-density and 
focused laser beam to melt a thin layer of metallic powder 
in a “layer-by-layer” methodology to directly fabricate dense 
metal parts with arbitrary complex structures, has recently 
attracted significant research attention. This technology has 
more cost-savings, higher production efficiency, and more 
efficient use of materials than conventional manufacturing 
technologies [1, 9]. As shown in previous studies [10– 13], 
the microstructures of a Ti–6Al–4V alloy fabricated by AM 
technology often consist of a large number of supersaturated 
fine acicular martensite /u1D6FC
/uni2032.var
 phases. Besides, in the manufac-
turing process, uneven residual stress can easily accumu-
late in the workpieces. Therefore, this AM-fabricated alloy 
mostly exhibited high strength and low plasticity [10, 11]. 
An appropriate heat treatment process could release the 
residual stress and stabilize the microstructure. Furthermore, 
the mechanical performance of the titanium alloys was also 
improved [14, 15].

°C/4 h/AC, a tri-modal microstructure including lamellar

Ti–6Al–4V alloy. The discontinuous αGB and the dispersed 
αS with various crystallographic orientations were obtained. 
The tensile strength and elongation were 865 MPa and 9.9%, 
respectively. Ren et al. [19] showed that the microstructure 
of a larger volume fraction of lamellar (α S + β) microstruc-
ture was obtained for a high-power laser solid (LSD) fabri-
cated Ti–6Al–4V alloy after solution and aging treatments of 
950 °C/2 h/AC + 550 °C/4 h/AC. The heat treatment process 
yielded a material with a tensile strength of 937.8 ± 11 MPa 
and a higher elongation of 17.8 ± 0.5%. As mentioned above, 
optimal heat treatment parameters were obviously different 
for Ti–6Al–4 V alloys fabricated by different AM strategies. 
The characteristics of the microstructure and the properties 
were also different. These differences may be derived from 
the differences in AM technologies, the corresponding pro-
duction process parameters (e.g., laser power and scanning 
velocities), and chemical composition variations. Therefore, 
applications of these heat treatment processes proposed in 
previous studies have been limited. Furthermore, some 
researchers showed that the elongation of addictive man-
ufactured (AM-ed) Ti–6Al–4 V alloys with different heat 
treatments are still lower than conventional Ti alloys due to 
the microstructure of basket-weave structure [11, 12, 17]. 
Consequently, for a given composition and AM technology 
with certain process parameters, the most appropriate solu-
tion and aging treatment parameters should be investigated 
and determined to obtain the best mechanical properties.
Additionally, the effects of solution and aging treatment 
temperature and time on the mechanical properties of a 
Ti–6Al–4V alloy fabricated by AM technology were also stud-
ied. Li et al. [20] reported that the tensile strength increased 
by 89 MPa while the elongation decreased by 5.5% when 
the solution temperature increased from 920 to 950 °C for a 
Ti–6Al–4V alloy fabricated by SLM. Zhang et al. [21] showed 
that the tensile strength increased by 190 MPa while the elon-
gation only decreased by 2.6% with increasing solution tem-
perature from 900 to 950 °C for a Ti–6Al–4V alloy fabricated 
via electron beam selective melting (EBSM). With respect to 
aging, as the temperature increased from 540 to 650 °C, the 
tensile strength decreased by 230 MPa while the elongation 
only increased 2.1%. Galarrraga et al. [22] stated that the ten-
sile strength of a Ti–6Al–4V ELI alloy fabricated by electron 
beam melting (EBM) decreased with longer aging tempera-
ture and time. The specimen aged at 550 °C for 8 h could 
provide a tensile strength of 1271 MPa. However, the speci-
men aged at 600 °C for 8 h presented a tensile strength below 
1200 MPa. Wang et al. [23] showed that the tensile strength of 
a Ti–6Al–4V alloy fabricated by laser three-dimensional (3D) 
printing increased by 27.5 MPa as the solution time increased 
from 0.5 to 1.5 h. The elongation increased by 16.1% as the 
aging temperature increased from 500 to 600 °C. When the 
aging temperature was 950 °C, the elongation increased by 
11.6% as the aging time increased from 2 to 6 h. Zhang et al. 
[24] showed that both tensile strength and elongation of the 
heat-treated Ti–6Al–4V alloy fabricated by laser solid forming 
slightly increased with increasing aging temperature (aging 
time below 4 h). At the same time, elongation increased from 
below 20% to 30% with increased aging time from 2 to 16 h. 
Considering these previous studies, the strength and ductility 
of the Ti–6Al–4V alloy fabricated by AM can be altered by 
adjusting the solution and aging treatment temperature and 
time. However, the effect and degree of the heat treatment 
parameters on the mechanical properties were different. It is 
difficult to determine which heat treatment parameter had the 
most obvious effect on performance improvement, let alone 
choose which main heat parameter to control as a way to 
obtain better compatibility of strength and plasticity. There-
fore, to investigate the sensitivity of heat treatment parameters 
to the mechanical properties of SLM-fabricated Ti–6Al–4V 
alloy, it is important to design the heat treatment processes. In 
addition, the relationship between the heat treatment param-
eters, microstructures, and properties should be clarified.
In this work, based on SLM technology, an L9 ( 34 ) type 
orthogonal test was used to design a heat treatment experiment 
for the Ti–6Al–4V alloy. The sensitivity of the heat treatment 
parameters to the mechanical properties of the SLM-fabricated 
Ti–6Al–4V alloy was studied. The heat treatment process pro-
viding the best mechanical properties was determined. Fur-
thermore, the influence of heat treatment on the microstruc-
ture of SLM-fabricated Ti–6Al–4V alloy was analyzed. The 
strengthening and plasticity mechanisms were also discussed.

Ti–6Al–4V alloys have significant anisotropies. The colum-

this direction. The round rod specimens with a diameter of

3 Results and Discussions
3.1 Orthogonal Experiments
As shown in Table  3, the mechanical properties of the 
specimens for orthogonal experiment including tensile 
strength, yield strength and elongation were obtained. The 
relationships between the various factors and indicators 
were shown in Fig. 3. It can be noted that the solution tem-
perature and aging temperature were the most influential 
factors on the mechanical properties while the time factors 
were slightly influential according to the range analysis 
method [28]. Among them, the aging temperature was the 
most influential factor relative to mechanical properties. 
With increasing aging temperature from 500 °C to 600 
°C, the tensile strength of the specimens decreased from 
1133.9 to 1027.1 MPa while the elongation increased from 
10.8 to 12.8%. To ensure a good combination of strength 
and plasticity, the aging temperature was set as 550 °C. To 
understand the effect of solution temperature on mechani-
cal properties, it can be seen that the tensile strength of 
the specimens increased while the elongation gradually 
decreased with increasing solution temperature. When the 
solution temperature was greater than 920 °C, its effect on 
tensile strength and yield strength was weakened. How -
ever, the elongation still obviously decreased with increas-
ing solution temperature. Therefore, a solution tempera-
ture of 920 °C was appropriate to obtain a high plasticity. 
In addition, the maximum tensile strength and elongation 
were obtained when the solution time was 2 h, as shown in 
Fig. 3a and c. When the aging time reached 3 h, the tensile 
strength of the specimen was the highest and the effect of 
aging time on the elongation was not strong. Consequently, 
the optimized heat treatment process to obtain the best

3.2 Verification Experiment
The verification experiment was conducted with the opti-
mum heat treatment 920 °C/2 h/WQ + 550 °C/3 h/AC to 
confirm that the obtained process truly optimized the 
mechanical properties of the SLM-fabricated Ti–6Al–4V 
alloy. The results are presented in Table  3. It can be seen 
that a tensile strength of 1045.2 MPa, yield strength of 
975.4 MPa, and elongation of 13.6% were obtained in the 
verification experiment.
The stress and strain curves of the SLM-fabricated Ti-
6Al-4 V alloy with different solution and aging treatments 
are given in Fig.  4. The requirements of the mechanical 
properties of SLM-fabricated Ti-6Al-4 V alloy in the fac-
tory is shown in Table  3. It can be seen that the specimens 
subject to 920 °C/2 h/WQ + 500 °C/4 h/AC, 950 °C/1 h/
WQ + 500 °C/5 h/AC, or 950 °C/2 h/WQ + 550 °C/3 h/
AC had higher strengths. They could all meet the strength 
requirements but their elongations were lower than 12%. 
After 880 °C/2 h/WQ + 600 °C/5 h/AC solution and aging 
heat treatment, the specimen had a considerable elongation 
while its strength was lower than 1000 MPa. The mechanical 
properties of the specimens in the verification experiments 
all met these requirements. It was indicated that an excellent 
combination of mechanical properties was in fact achieved 
with optimized heat treatment.

3.4 Mechanical Property Analysis
3.4.1 The Strengthening Mechanism
3.4.1.1 The Effect of Solution Temperature With increasing 
solution temperature, it can be observed in Fig.  5 that the 
average widths of α P and lamellar α both increased in the 
specimen with solution and aging treatment. The fraction 
of transformed β also increased with increasing solution 
temperature. In the meantime, there were more α S phases 
precipitated in the metastable supersaturated β [39].
According to the results of the orthogonal experiments 
of Sect. 3.1, the tensile strength of the specimens with solu-
tion and aging treatment generally increased with increasing 
solution temperature. Due to the increase in size of α P and 
lamellar α after solution treatment at a higher temperature 
of 920 °C, the number of interfaces of α/β was also reduced. 
Thus, the coarsening of the microstructure would lead to a 
decrease in interfacial strengthening effect [40]. However, 
there were more α S precipitated after solution treatment at 
a higher temperature of 920 °C. The fine microstructure led 
to an increase in the number of α S/β interfaces. The inter -
facial strengthening effect was relatively higher and led to 
improved strength. Meanwhile, the α/β interface phase pre-
sented between the α and β phases was observed in Fig. 14. 
The microstructure of the α/β interface phase could hinder 
dislocation motion and form deformed twin substructures 
during tensile testing, which led to a decrease in the effective 
slip length [41]. The strength of the specimen at a solution 
temperature of 920 °C was improved. As widely accepted, 
the mechanical properties were influenced by the level of 
dislocation density. A higher dislocation density easily made 
the dislocation intersect and interpenetrate with other dislo-
cations during dislocation movement. This was the reason

Figs. 5c and 8e, after heat treatment at a higher aging tem-
perature of 650 °C, the size of α P and lamellar α became 
further coarsened and the massive and equiaxial α P was 
obtained. Meanwhile, the size of αS was obviously coarsened

the number of α S/β and the α/β interface phases decreased 
when aged at a higher temperature of 650 °C. The effect of 
interfacial strengthening decreased. As shown in Fig.  9c–f, 
the KAM results show that the GND density is lower when 
aged at a higher temperature 850 °C. Based on the above 
analysis, it can be concluded that a higher aging tempera-
ture promoted the decreased strength mainly by lowering 
the interface strengthening effects of α/β and α S/β and the 
dislocation strengthening.

3.4.2.1 The Effect of  Solution Temperature As shown in 
Fig. 7c, there were continuous coarsening αGB formed after 
solution treatment at a higher temperature of 920 °C. The 
continuous coarsening α GB led to severe dislocation pile-
ups around the β grain boundaries. It would induce sig-
nificant stress concentration and increased nucleation site 
density of the micro-voids. Then, the fracture would occur 
easily at the continuous coarsening α GB [18]. As shown in 
Fig. 15c, the fracture was formed in the inner part of the 
continuous coarsening αGB. Secondly, the EBSD orientation 
maps showed that the smaller-sized lamellar α had vari-
ous crystallographic orientations with solution treatment 
at a low temperature of 850 °C (Fig.  8b). However, a part 
of the lamellar α had the same crystallographic orienta-
tion when the solution temperature increased to 920 °C, as 
shown in Fig. 8d. For the lamellar α with the same crystal-
lographic orientation, the dislocations can slip easily inside 
the lamellar α with the same crystallographic orientation. 
It increased to severe pile-up of dislocations at these lamel-
lar α boundaries. The plasticity of the specimen with solu-
tion treatment at a higher temperature of 920 °C decreased. 
In contrast, the small size of the lamellar α with various 
crystallographic orientations in the specimen with solu-
tion treatment at a lower temperature of 850 °C led to the 
homogeneous distribution of dislocations during the tensile 
process. The ductility of this alloy was improved [18]. As 
shown in Fig. 15e and f, the micro-voids were easily formed 
at the boundaries of the lamellar α with the same crystal-
lographic orientations, while the number of micro-voids in 
the zone of basket-weave lamellar α with smaller size was 
less. Furthermore, the KAM maps in the Fig.  9 showed 
that the GND density was higher when solution treated at 
a higher temperature of 920 °C. It can be indicated that the 
plasticity of the specimen with solution treatment at a higher 
temperature was lower than that of the specimen with solu-
tion treatment at a lower temperature. Generally, improved 
elongation is achieved while sacrificing strength [46]. The 
coarsening of the microstructure (e.g., α P and lamellar α) 
was obtained after solution treatment at a higher temper -
ature of 920 °C. It led to an increase in the effective slip 
of length of dislocation [47]. Hence, the plasticity further 
increased while the strength decreased. In conclusion, it can 
be inferred that the microstructure of continuous coarsening 
αGB and more lamellar α with the same crystallographic ori-
entation and a higher dislocation density were the reasons 
for the decreased elongation of the specimen after solution 
treatment at a higher temperature of 920 °C.
3.4.2.2 The Effect of  Aging Temperature As shown in the 
Figs. 5c and 8e, the size of αP and lamellar α became further 
coarsened and massive and equiaxial αP was obtained when 
aging at a higher aging temperature of 650 °C. As mentioned 
above, plasticity is related to the effectiveness of the slip dislo-
cation. The coarsening of the microstructure led to an increase 
in the effectiveness of the slip dislocation, which resulted in 
an increase in plasticity. As shown in Fig. 15d, the coarsening 
of the microstructure αP and lamellar α were lengthened and 
bended. It indicated that the deformability of the coarsening 
of the microstructure αP and lamellar α increased. Secondly, 
the fine αS became significantly coarsened, which presented 
as short rod-like or sphere-like morphologies (Fig. 7d). The 
short rod-like or spherical α phases had more slip systems and 
better deformation compatibility [39, 3]. Thus, the short rod-
like or spherical α phase could present better plasticity. Mean-
while, the EBSD orientation maps in the Fig. 8c showed that 
the αS had various crystallographic orientations when aged 
at a higher aging temperature of 650 °C. The αS with various 
crystallographic orientations increased the effective bounda-
ries to hinder dislocation movement. Thus, the distribution of 
dislocations was more homogeneous. The plasticity of this 
alloy was also improved. In addition, the GND density of the 
specimen with aging at a higher temperature of 650 °C was 
lower. The effect of dislocation strengthening was decreased 
while the plasticity of the specimen was increased [30]. 
Consequently, it can be concluded that the coarsening of the 
microstructure of αP, lamellar α and αS with various crystallo-
graphic orientations and lower dislocation strengthening were 
the reasons for the increased elongation of the specimens with 
aging at a higher temperature of 650 °C.
4 Conclusions
(1) The solution temperature and aging temperature were 
strongly influential factors on the mechanical prop-
erties. The optimum heat treatment process, i.e. 920 
°C/2 h/WQ + 550 °C/3 h/AC, was determined. The 
SLM-produced TC4 alloy with optimum heat treatment 
exhibited an excellent combination of mechanical prop-
erties, namely, tensile strength of 1045.2 MPa, yield 
strength of 975.4 MPa, elongation of 13.6%.
(2) With increasing solution temperature from 850 to 
920 °C, the size of α P was increased and the lamel-
lar α became coarsened. The number of fine α S was 
increased. The microstructure of continuous α GB pre-

650 °C, the size of αP and lamellar α further increased 
and part of the α P presented as an equiaxed morphol-
ogy. Meanwhile, the continuous αGB further grew and 
coarsened. The size of α S in transformed β obviously 
increased.
(3) More fine αS precipitated and higher GND density pro-
moted increased strength after solution treatment at a 
higher temperature of 920 °C by increasing the inter -
face strengthening effect of α S/β and the dislocation 
strengthening. The coarsening of the microstructure of 
αP, lamellar α, and α S were obtained when aging at a 
higher temperature of 650 °C and led to a reduction 
in number of the interface of α/β and α S/β. The GND 
density was also lower when aged at a higher tempera-
ture of 650 °C. The strength generally decreased by 
lowering the interface hindrance to dislocation with 
increased aging temperature.
(4) The continuous coarsening α GB and part of the lamel-

formed when solution at the higher temperature of 920 
°C. This induced significant stress concentration and 
increased the density of nucleation sites of the micro-
voids, and hence led to the decrease of the elongation 
of specimen with solution treatment at a higher tem-
perature. The lower GND density and coarsening of 
the microstructure of α S with various crystallographic 
orientation increased the elongation when aged at a 
higher temperature of 650 °C.
