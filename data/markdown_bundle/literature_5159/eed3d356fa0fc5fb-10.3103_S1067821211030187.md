INTRODUCTION
When metals are treated by cutting, a very high
temperature (up to 1100°C) can develop in the region
where they contact a cutter [1]. This leads to the oxi-
dation of the surface of the cutting edge, a decrease in
the mechanical strength of the cutter material, and an
increase in its adhesion to the treated material (and,
correspondingly , to a decrease in the efficiency of the
cutting tool).
The high-temperature strength of hard alloys (HA)
of the WC–Co–Ni–Re(Mn) system is mainly deter-
mined by the properties of the metal binder, in which
the interatomic bond strengthens upon the addition of
rhenium and, consequently , the melting point
increases. This fact also explains the structure of Co–
Re and Ni–Re phase diagrams, which are character-
ized by the peritectic melting of Co and Ni [2, 3].
The introduction of Ni, Re, and Mn into the
cementing metal (Co) compared with pure cobalt
without additives is able to increase the high-tempera-
ture strength and wear resistance of HAs due to the
formation of stronger interatomic bonds in them. It
should be noted that, according to [4], the addition of
Re in reality promotes an increase in the high-temper-
ature strength. This also indicates that the diffusion
processes in a complex cementing alloy is substantially
retarded by virtue of the increasing strength of the
metal bond.

Abstract—The regularities of oxidation of hard alloys (HA) in the “tungsten carbide–metal binder based on cobalt,
nickel, rhenium, and manganese” system are established. It is revealed that the introduction of Ni–Re or Ni–Mn
into the cobalt binder increases the temperature of the onset of active oxidation by 20–70°C and decreases its rate in
the following order: Co–Ni, Co–Mn (Co–Ni–Mn, Co–Re, Co–Ni) Co–Ni–Re Ni–Re. The
character of the temperature dependence of the bending strength of the HA with Co–Ni and Ni–Mn binders is
almost indistinguishable from that for the VK allo y , while the addition of Re into the binder abruptly
changes this character. The HA strengthens somewhat (up to 20%) up to temperatures of 600 °C for the
Ni–Re binder and 800 °C for the Co–Re binder; then its strength decreases but remains higher than for
materials with cobalt and nickel binders. The ma ximal strength of the HA of the WC–Co–Ni–Re sys-
tems is attained in the range t = 600–800 °C, which corresponds to the temperature of the working edge
of the cutting material.
Keywords: hard alloy , high-temperature strength, refracto riness, tungsten carbide, metal binder, oxidation,
cobalt, nickel, rhenium, manganese.

Refractoriness of Hard Alloys

alloys during heating is presented in Fig. 1, while the
influence of the composition of the metal binder on

sented below:
In regards to high-temperature strength, all HA
samples surpass the WC–Co starting alloy . The simul-
taneous introduction of Ni–Re or Ni–Mn into the
cobalt binder increases the onset temperature of its
active oxidation by 20–70
°C and decreases its rate in
the series Co–Ni, Co–Mn (Co–Ni–Mn, Co–
Re, Co–Ni) Co–Ni–Re Ni–Re. Thus,
nickel most strongly increases the high-temperature
strength of the HA, and then manganese and rhenium
follow . Although the composition of the oxide film was
not investigated, it seems likely that the protective role
o f N i l i e s i n t h e f o r m a t i o n o f a m o r e s t a b l e f i l m o f
nickel oxide when compared with cobalt oxide. This
relates to manganese and rhenium oxides to a lower
extent. Taking into account a high degree of volatility
of rhenium oxide and, correspondingly , a decrease in
the weight increment with the binder of the corre-
sponding composition, the contribution of rhenium to
an increase in high-temperature strength becomes the
smallest.
Such a behavior of HAs being heated in air is char-
acteristic of the metal binder with a complex composi-
tion. The causes of such an increase in the high-tem-
perature strength of hard alloys are the same as for
stainless and alloyed steels. This is caused by the fact
that the alloying element, which strengthens the lat-
tice and decreases the diffusion mobility of atoms,
enters the lattice of the host metal oxide forming the
films of complex oxides, which retard the diffusion of
oxygen [7]. It is problematic to considerably increase
the high-temperature strength of WC-based hard
alloys taking into account the physicochemical prop-
erties of tungsten oxide and its high content in these
alloys.

High-Temperature Strength of Hard Alloys
When selecting the material for the cutting tool, a
large high-temperature strength only is insufficient; a
high strength at t = 800–1000 °C is also necessary .
Hard alloys with the cobalt-based binder do not pos-
sess this property in this temperature range. In addi-
tion, the complex of these properties at the working
temperature of cutting (700–800
°C) is of great impor-
tance. Therefore, at the next stage of experiments, we
investigated the high-temperature strength of HAs
with various binders.
The results of tests in the region t < 1200°C in vac-
uum are presented in Fig. 2.
The character of the temperature dependence of
the bending strength for the HAs with the Co–Ni and
Ni–Mn binders is almost indistinguishable from that
for the VK8 alloy [9]. The addition of rhenium into the
binder abruptly changes the run of curves 
σbend(t). Up
to temperatures of 600 °C for the Ni–Re binder and
800°C for the Co–Re binder, the HA somewhat
strengthens (up to 20%), and then the value of σbend
decreases but remains higher than for the materials
with the cobalt and nickel binders up to t ≥ 1000°C.
The factor that promotes an increase in the high-
temperature strength is a decrease in the dislocation
mobility , which is realized as a result of alloying and
possible dispersion strengthening. Thus, the higher
strength of hard alloys that contain rhenium in the
metal binder is regular and quite expectable, and its
rise with an increase in temperature is most likely asso-
ciated with the features of destruction of the cement-
ing metal phase (pure cobalt and nickel) and their
alloys with rhenium.
The maximum strength of WC–Co–Ni–Re hard
alloys is attained in the range 
t = 600–800 °C, which
corresponds to the temperature of the working edge of
the cutting material. This indicates that the cutters
m a d e f r o m a l l o y s w i t h a m o d i f i e d b i n d e r p o s s e s s
higher working properties during the resistance tests of
the pilot party of cutting plates [10].
CONCLUSIONS
Rational alloying the cementing component of
hard alloys allows us to simultaneously solve two
important problems: the increase in the high-temper-
ature strength and the refractoriness of the WC-based
alloy , which in turn assumes the possibility of intensi-
fying cutting metals. The additional introduction of a
more scale-resistant component into the solid phase
(complex carbides and carbonitrides) can also improve
the working characteristics of hard alloys.
