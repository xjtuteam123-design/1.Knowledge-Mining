Abstract
A study using a transmission electron microscope was performed for the deformation microstructures of Nb-
20mol%Mo single crystals containing from 0.03 to 0.65 mol% oxygen and a Nb-40mol%Mo-0.05mol%O single crystal,
compressed to approximately 4% plastic strain at 298 K, 973 K and 1473 K. At 298 K, screw dislocations are predomi-
nant in all alloys. At 973 K, in Nb-20Mo-0.03O and Nb-40Mo-0.05O, the dislocation microstructure contains the same
amount of edge dislocations and small edge dipoles that tend to aggregate into clusters. In Nb-20Mo-0.65O, however,
the arrangement consists of long glide loops parallel to the screw direction. At 1473 K, edge dislocations are dominant
in all alloys. From the results, the following conclusions are made: (1) Oxygen atoms impede screw dislocation motion
at 298 K and 973 K; (2) The inﬂuence of oxygen on screw segments is signiﬁcantly strong at 973 K; (3) At 1473 K,
oxygen atoms impede edge dislocation motion. It is inferred that O-Mo atmosphere is formed around dislocations. 

4.3. The effect of O on dislocation arrangement
of Nb-20Mo alloys deformed at 973 K
Deformation microstructure in Nb-20Mo-O
alloys at 973 K exhibits the preference of screw
dislocation rather than edge dislocation. At 298 K,
screw dislocation is predominant with either Mo
or O addition at 298 K, but not with Mo addition
at 973 K. Obviously, the effect of O on dislocation
arrangement is stronger than that of Mo, and O
in Nb-20Mo impedes screw dislocation motion at
this temperature.
As shown in Figs. 9 and 10, dislocations elon-
gated parallel to the [111] direction and dislo-
cations bowing out are observed in Nb-20Mo-
0.65O. The long and bowing-out screw dislo-
cations suggest that the velocity of screw segments
is much lower than that of edge segments. In con-
trast, when the velocity of screw segments
increases, the movement of edge segments must
control the glide loop expansion. The staircase
ends of long screw dislocations may be explained
in terms of tearing off the screw dislocation from
pinning that frequently occurs at this temperature.
Debris and cusps may result from the cross slip-
ping of screw segments in an expanding loop to
some extent. On the other hand, the density of
cusps and elongated dislocations perpendicular to
[111] direction in Nb-20Mo-0.65O is evidently
smaller than in Nb-Mo alloys with lower O content
as seen in Figs. 7 and 8, suggesting that cross slip
hardly occurs. These evidences positively indicate
that O atoms in Nb-20Mo alloys impede screw dis-
location’s motion at this relatively high tempera-
ture. This effect of O is not observed in Nb and
Nb-Ta alloys at such high temperature [1,20].
A notable question has given rise to how O
atoms interact strongly with the screw dislocation
even at 973 K, at which edge dislocations are pre-
dominant in the low O content alloys. Since the O
strengthening does not appear in Nb and Nb-Ta
alloys, it is considered that Mo is also involved in
the interaction between O and screw dislocation.
That is, Mo-O interaction (the i-s interaction) with
screw dislocations occurs during deformation at
973 K in Nb-20Mo alloys.
Neither fringe contrast nor the decomposition of
dislocations was observed. Stacking-fault energy
of a screw dislocation is supposed to be too high
to generate in bcc lattice. It has been considered
that screw dislocation’s core in bcc lattice expands
to three-/H11021111/H11022directions [21: pp. 133; 22]. Inter-
stitial atoms can interact with a screw dislocation
due to tetragonal distortion around itself [23: pp.
38], which forms an atmosphere around screw dis-
location’s core [24]. In addition, serrations
observed at intermediate temperatures in Nb-20Mo
with a high O content [1] indicate that short-range
diffusion of O atom is related to dislocation
mobility. Thus, the i-s interaction in ﬂuences the
redistribution of interstitials under applied stress.
The results obtained in this study can be
explained by a mechanism proposed by Schoeck
and Seeger [25], which is slightly modi ﬁed con-
sidering the i-s interaction. They dealt with the for-
mation of “Snoek atmosphere” due to interstitial C
atoms around screw dislocations in the Fe-C sys-
tem. This strengthening mechanism is concerned
with the redistribution of interstitials on different
lattice sites in the stress ﬁeld of a dislocation.
When the stress is applied, an O atom will jump
to another site around an Mo atom to release its
tetragonal distortion. On the other hand, when a
screw dislocation moves under the exerted stress
and its core meets an O atom, the O atom will
be reoriented again in the stress ﬁeld of the screw
dislocation. As a result, the O atom jumps around,
the Mo atom reduces the energy of the system,
leading to the locking of the screw dislocation.
This mechanism needs only short-range diffusion
within lattice, although O diffusion in Nb-Mo
alloys will be harder than that in Nb because Mo
atoms arrest O atoms. The results of the orientation
dependence of c.r.s.s. in the previous study [4] also
suggests this mechanism.
4.4. Interaction between dislocations and O

5. Summary
Dislocation arrangement of Nb-20Mo single
crystals with various O contents and Nb-40Mo sin-
gle crystal with a low O content were studied to
investigate the combined effect of O and Mo atoms
on dislocations in Nb and Nb-Mo alloy. The results
obtained in this study are summarized as follows:
1. At 298 K, In Nb-20Mo with low O content, dis-
location microstructure of highly-developed
screw dislocations is observed. Also, micro-
structures consisting of screw dislocations are
observed in Nb-20Mo-0.65O and Nb-40Mo-
0.05O. Thus, it is indicated that Mo and O atom
interact with screw dislocations and impedes
their motion at 298 K. Consequently, solid sol-
ution strengthening due to Mo and/or O occurs.
2. At 973 K, the arrangement of Nb-20Mo and Nb-
40Mo with low O contents mainly consists of
edge dislocations. On the other hand, in Nb-
20Mo-0.65O, screw dislocations are dominant.
These results suggest strengthening mechanism
due to O is evidently different from that of Mo
addition. That is, the interaction between O
atom and a screw dislocation is stronger than
that between O atom and an edge dislocation at
973 K. The formation of Mo-O Snoek atmos-
phere is considered, which interacts with screw
dislocation’s core and impedes their motion.
3. At 1473 K, the dislocation arrangement consists
of edge dislocations, where strengthening due
to O still occurs in Nb-20Mo alloy. This result
suggests that O atom interacts with an edge dis-
location at this temperature. Dragging O atoms
whose diffusivity is lowered by the interaction
with Mo atom impede edge dislocations motion.
