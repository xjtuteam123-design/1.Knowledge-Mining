ABSTRACT
The eﬀ ects of Mo and Nb additions on the microstructure and mechanical properties of two FeCrAl alloys were
studied. Fine and uniform recrystallized grain structures (~ 20 –30 μm) were achieved in both alloys through
suitable annealing after warm-rolling. The formation of Fe 2Nb-type Laves phase precipitates in the Nb-con-
taining FeCrAl alloy eﬀ ectively stabilized the deformed and recrystallized microstructures. The Mo-containing
FeCrAl alloy exhibited strong γ texture ﬁber after annealing at 650 –900 °C, whereas the annealed Nb-containing
FeCrAl alloy had much weaker texture. Both strength and ductility decreased as the grain size increased in both
alloys.
1. Introduction
Iron‑chromium‑aluminum (FeCrAl) alloys have long been used as
high-temperature heating elements. Currently, wrought FeCrAl alloys
are under development as one of the promising candidates for accident-
tolerant fuel (ATF) cladding materials in light-water reactors (LWRs)
[1–5]. These alloys have shown excellent corrosion and oxidation re-
sistance in a high-temperature steam environment, compared with
current commercial zirconium alloys [6–8]. Their low radiation-in-
duced-swelling and tolerance of o ﬀ-normal conditions are also worth
noting [9]. Under development e ﬀorts for ATF FeCrAl alloys [4],
10–13 wt% Cr and 5 –6 wt% Al have been employed for improved
surface protection e ﬀects at both the service temperature and elevated
temperatures in accident-case scenarios. Small amount of alloying ele-
ments, such as molybdenum (Mo) and/or niobium (Nb), have also been
added to improve the strength and control microstructures of FeCrAl
alloys [4].
Because of their high Cr and Al content, FeCrAl alloys maintain a
body-centered-cubic (bcc) structure up to the liquidus temperature
[10,11], eliminating the chance of grain re ﬁnement through phase
transformation as in low ‑carbon steels. Rana et al. [12] reported that
Fe-Al interstitial-free (IF) ferritic alloys with ~ 6.8 –8.1 wt% Al exhibit
much coarser grains (~ 70– 90 μm) than a titanium-stabilized IF steel
(~ 20 μm) after similar thermomechanical processing routines. Coarse
grains in FeCrAl alloys not only are likely to cause premature failure in
seamless thin-wall tube fabrication of fuel cladding in LWRs [13] but
also are detrimental to mechanical properties of ﬁnal tube products
(e.g., strength, fracture toughness, and irradiation tolerance [14]).
Grain re ﬁnement can be employed as an e ﬀective strategy to increase
the deformability of alloys while improving their strength [15,16].
Texture as another aspect of microstructures also has signi ﬁcant
inﬂuence on the formability and in-service performance of FeCrAl al-
loys as fuel-cladding tubes in LWRs [17]. Properties such as the yield
strength, thermal and in-reactor creep strength, and fatigue and stress
corrosion cracking resistance are strongly associated with texture [17].
In a rolled plate, low‑ carbon and ferritic stainless steels usually display
strong recrystallization γ ﬁber
(i.e., ⟨111⟩//the normal direction [ND])
[18–21]. However, Fe-Al alloys show weak γ ﬁber as well as cube
texture components [12]. Therefore, the texture formation is another
concern during the development of FeCrAl alloys.
In this study, the microstructures (e.g., grain structure and texture)
of two FeCrAl alloys with small amounts of Mo or Nb (designated as
FrCrAl-2Mo and FeCrAl-0.7Nb, respectively) were investigated. The
mechanical properties of both alloys were also discussed by relating to
the microstructures. The results provide strategies and insights into
further development of FeCrAl alloys in order to achieve desirable
microstructures with balanced processability and properties.

3. Results and Discussion
3.1. Recrystallized Grain Structure
The microstructure of as-rolled alloys consisted of elongated grains
along the RD. Three deformation texture ﬁbers, namely α (⟨110⟩//RD),
γ (⟨111⟩//ND), and ⟨100⟩//ND ﬁbers, were observed, which agrees
with the deformation texture in low ‑carbon steels and stainless ferritic
steels [20,21,26]. Representative microstructures of both alloys sub-
jected to various annealing processes are displayed in Fig. 1. For Fe-
CrAl-2Mo alloy, recrystallized grains with an average size of
31 ± 13 μm (the error bar represents the standard deviation in the
grain size distribution) were achieved after annealing for 1 h at 800 °C,
as shown in Fig. 1a. Based on Fig. 1 (a–c), the grain size of FeCrAl-2Mo
alloy monotonically increased with increasing annealing temperature.
Annealing for 0.5 h at 1100 °C resulted in an average grain size of
165 ± 79 μm. All recrystallized grains in FeCrAl-2Mo alloy were uni-
form and in a slightly elongated round shape toward the RD, having
grain aspect ratios in the range of 0.55 to 0.6.
The microstructural evolution of FeCrAl-0.7Nb proceeded di ﬀer-
ently from that of FeCrAl-2Mo upon annealing because of the formation
of Laves phase below 1100 °C. When FeCrAl-0.7Nb alloy was annealed
at and below 900 °C, Fe
2Nb-type Laves phase particles pinned the
grain/subgrain boundaries of deformed grains, leading to mixed mi-
crostructures consisting of recovered and recrystallized grains. At
higher temperatures, the pinning e ﬀect of Laves phase particles became
weaker because of a lower volume fraction and higher particle coar-
sening rate. Consequently, most areas tended to recrystallize upon an-
nealing above 900 °C. Fig. 1(d –f) show the microstructures of FeCrAl-
0.7Nb annealed at 950 °C. Grains were e ﬀectively re ﬁned from several
hundred micrometers before the warm-rolling to 19 ± 14 μm after
annealing for 0.5 h at 950 °C. Bands with much larger grain sizes than
other areas were observed, such as the one indicated by arrows in
Fig. 1d, which strongly suggests that some deformed grains had many
fewer recrystallization nuclei than others [27]. The grains in FeCrAl-
0.7Nb stayed fairly stable at 950 °C. Very little grain coarsening was
observed after 24 h at 950 °C, as demonstrated in Fig. 1e. The SEM
backscattered electron image of the 24 h-annealed sample, shown in
Fig. 1f, indicates the Laves phase particles (bright contrast) mainly
distributing along grain boundaries. The composition of large particles
(~ 3 μm, annealed for 504 h at 950 °C) was determined by energy-dis-
persive X-ray spectroscopy in SEM to be Fe
58Nb22Cr9Si6Al5, which
conﬁrms that these particles were Fe 2Nb-type Laves phase. As shown in
Fig. 1(d –f), recrystallized grains in FeCrAl-0.7Nb were elongated along
the RD with grain aspect ratios of ~ 0.4. The microstructure of FeCrAl-
0.7Nb annealed at 1100 °C consisted of fully recrystallized, coarse
grains, similar to those in FeCrAl-2Mo in Fig. 1c, as a result of the
dissolution of all Laves phase particles into the bcc ‑iron matrix at
1100 °C.
Fig. 2 summarizes the grain size stability of FeCrAl-2Mo and FeCrAl-
0.7Nb annealed at 800– 1100 °C. At 800 °C, the grain size almost dou-
bled (from 31 ± 13 μmt o5 9±2 8 μm) when the annealing time
increased from 1 h to 360 h. The grain coarsening rate of FeCrAl-2Mo
increased as the annealing temperature increased in the studied tem-
perature range. The sizes of recrystallized grains in FeCrAl-0.7Nb were
more stable at 950 °C than those in FeCrAl-2Mo at 800 °C, as shown in
Fig. 2, indicating the pinning e ﬀect of the Laves phase particles in
preventing grain coarsening. It should be noted that abnormal grain
growth was observed in FeCrAl-0.7Nb after 120 h at 950 °C, indicating
that extended annealing could result in coarsening of the Laves phase
particles and cause them to lose their pinning e ﬀect on grain bound-
aries.
3.2. Recrystallization Texture
The ODF section with φ
2 = 45° ( Fig. 3) displays important texture
components in bcc materials. As shown in Fig. 3a, recrystallized grains
in FeCrAl-2Mo exhibited strong γ texture ﬁber with the main compo-
nent lying around {111} ⟨112⟩. The area fraction of recrystallization γ
ﬁber (within 15° tolerance) of FeCrAl-2Mo at 650 –900 °C was in the
range from 0.31 to 0.43 having an average value of 0.37 ± 0.04, and it
slightly decreased to ~ 0.28 ± 0.04 when annealed at 1100 °C. On the
other hand, the annealed FeCrAl-0.7Nb did not have dominant γ ﬁber
but showed weak ⟨100⟩//ND ﬁber ranging from {100} ⟨110⟩ to {100}
⟨100⟩ plus a Goss component (i.e., {110} ⟨100⟩ [18,28]), as demon-
strated in Fig. 3b. The preferred nucleation of γ-ﬁber grains at in-grain
shear-bands of deformed γ-ﬁber grains was reported to mainly con-
tribute to the strong recrystallization γ ﬁber in IF steels [18,29].A s
stated in Section 3.1 , Laves phase particles are likely to hinder the
nucleation or growth of γ-ﬁber grains, leading to the disappearance of
recrystallization γ ﬁber in FeCrAl-0.7Nb. The strong γ ﬁber in FeCrAl-
2Mo could bring processing di ﬃculties in thickness reduction during
tube fabrication owing to high Taylor factors of γ-ﬁber grains [30];o n
the other hand, FeCrAl-0.7Nb would be able to more easily accom-
modate applied strain along the thickness direction than FeCrAl-2Mo
due to random grain orientations.
3.3. Microstructure-dependent Mechanical Properties
Tensile tests at room temperature were performed on both alloys
with various grain sizes. Fig. 4a shows two representative tensile stress-
strain curves for FeCrAl-2Mo. Some tensile properties (i.e., yield stress
[YS], ultimate yield stress [UTS], uniform plastic strain [ ε
u], and failure
plastic strain [ εf]) are plotted as functions of the grain sizes in Fig. 4b
and c. For FeCrAl-2Mo, the YS values dropped ~ 9% from
479 ± 3.6 MPa to 437 ± 7.8 MPa as the grain size increased from 31
to 165 μm. When the grain size was below ~ 80 μm, FeCrAl-2Mo
showed ~ 0.15 uniform strain and ~ 0.35 failure strain consistently,
indicating that a stable, moderate deformability can be expected in the
range of the grain size. Dimples were also found on severely necked
fracture surfaces, suggesting ductile characteristics of the alloy with
relatively small grain sizes. However, brittle fracture occurred on some
or all tensile specimens when the grain size was larger than ~ 80 μm.
For example, all FeCrAl-2Mo tensile samples annealed for 0.5 h at
1100 °C (grain size ~ 165 ± 79 μm) suddenly fractured without
necking, resulting in a failure strain of 0.06 ± 0.04. Transgranular

4. Conclusions
In summary, the microstructures and mechanical properties of two
FeCrAl alloys under various annealing conditions were investigated.
The results outline guidelines to achieve desirable microstructures with
balanced processability and properties. The conclusions are as follows:
(1) Fine grain structure with an average size of ~ 20 –30 μm was
achieved in both alloys. Recrystallized grains of the Nb-containing
FeCrAl alloy were elongated along the RD with grain aspect ratios
of ~ 0.4. Deformed and recrystallized microstructures in the Nb-
containing FeCrAl alloy were stabilized owing to the pinning e ﬀect
of Laves phase particles along grain/subgrain boundaries.
(2) The Mo-containing FeCrAl alloy had strong γ recrystallization tex-
ture ﬁber after annealing at 650– 900 °C; however, recrystallized
grains in the Nb-containing FeCrAl alloy showed weak ⟨100⟩//ND
texture ﬁber and a Goss component at 950 °C.
(3) The solid-solution hardening of Al made a major contribution to the
overall strengthening in both alloys. The strength and ductility of
both alloys decreased as the average grain size increased. When the
grain size was below ~ 80 μm,
 both alloys showed ~ 15% uniform
plastic strain, indicating considerable deformability for processing.
