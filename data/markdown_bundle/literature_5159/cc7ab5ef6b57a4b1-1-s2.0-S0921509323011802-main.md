ABSTRACT 
A never-ending challenge for materials scientists is to design alloys with excellent strength-ductility combina -
tions. Here, a systematic study on B2 precipitation and mechanical behaviors of a high Co and Al content high 
entropy alloy (HEA) annealed at 700 – 1000 
◦
C was investigated. The alloy annealed at 700 
◦
C (denoted as A700) 
exhibited a high yield strength of 1820 ± 22 MPa and an ultimate tensile strength of 1884 ± 21 MPa, which can 
be mainly attributed to the dislocation, ultra-fine grain and B2-strengthening. With the annealed temperature 
increasing to 900 
◦
C (denoted as A900), the FCC matrix has been full-recrystallized, and the grain size of FCC 
matrix increases from 0.21 μ m to 2.8 μ m. The volume fraction of the B2 phase decreases slightly from 58% to 
53%. As a result, the A900 alloy displays a yield strength of 1038 ± 17 MPa and tensile ductility of 28 ± 2.3%. 
The deformation and strengthening mechanisms are investigated by microstructural characterization and 
theoretical calculations. This work will provide a basis for the composition and structural design of B2- 
strengthened HEAs. 
1. Introduction 
In order to meet the requirements of the industrial society, new alloy 
composition and structural design have been constantly developed. High 
entropy alloys (HEAs), also referred to as multi-principal element alloys 
(MPEAs), have received considerable interest due to their outstanding 
strength-ductility combinations at a wide range of temperature [ 1 – 5 ]. As 
is well claimed, the single face-centered-cubic (FCC) structured HEAs 
display excellent ductility at cryogenic and ambient temperatures by 
deformation induced twinning [ 6 – 8 ]. However, this type of HEAs has 
low yield strength at room and high temperatures, making it difficult to 
have engineering applications [ 9 – 11 ]. 
In order to break through the upper limit of strength, precipitation- 
strengthening HEAs have been successfully developed [ 12 – 19 ]. such 
as B2-strengthening and L1
2
-strengthening. B2 is a semicoherent pre -
cipitate, which has the higher interfacial energy than coherent precipi -
tate, and it is distributed along the grain boundaries [ 19 – 21 ]. The L1
2 
precipitates strengthened HEAs/MEAs can reach to the yield strength of 
2.0 GPa with 13 – 16% uniform elongation [ 22 , 23 ]. These results exhibit 
a very commendable strength-ductility synergy. However, the L1
2 
phase 
is unstable at high temperature and tend to dissolve. The 
B2-strengthened alloy shows a good mechanical stability at high tem -
perature, as reported in Ni based super alloy and lightweight steel 
[ 24 – 26 ]. Meanwhile, the brittleness of B2-strengthened HEAs at room 
temperature can be suppressed by controlling alloying elements and 
thermomechanical treatments [ 21 , 25 , 27 – 29 ]. For instance, Jiang et al. 
[ 20 ] propose a shear band-driven dispersion of nano-sized and semi -
coherent precipitates in CoNiV alloy, which show significant strength -
ening effects. In addition, the high density B2 strengthening ensures that 
the alloy retains good strength from room temperature to high tem -
perature [ 30 , 31 ]. The addition of Al element will promote the precipi -
tation of BCC/B2, but high content of Al addition will cause the brittle 
fracture of CoCrFeNi based HEAs [ 32 ]. To overcome the 
strength-plasticity trade-off of B2 strengthened alloys, we control the 
microstructure of CoCrFeNi based HEAs through careful composition 
design and heat treatment process to achieve ultra-high strength and 
good ductility. 
High content of Al can promote the precipitation of B2 phase in the 
FCC matrix. However, an Al content in excess of 16% will cause a drastic 
decrease in tensile plasticity of as cast FCC high entropy alloys [ 33 ]. 
Hence, to ensure the machinability of the alloy, we add 12% Al to the 
alloy. For Co-rich HEAs, the low lattice diffusion will promote the

Substantial stacking faults (SFs) can be observed in the ultra-fine grain of 
the A700 alloy ( Fig. 10 b and c). Fig. 11 a display the slip trace in the fine 
grains of the A900 alloy at 5%. After fracture, a high density of dislo -
cation was observed near the FCC/B2 interfaces, indicating that the B2 
phase is a strong barrier to dislocation motion ( Fig. 11 b). It is worth 
noting that no stacking faults were observed in A900 alloy through TEM 
characterization ( Fig. 11 c). The difference in grain size between A700 
and A900 alloys results in different deformation mechanisms [ 40 ]. 
When the grain size is reduced to ultra-fine grain regime, the dislocation 
free path is limited and the intragranular dislocation slip is suppressed 
[ 40 , 41 ]. The dislocation sources will transfer from the grain interior to 
the grain boundary. The deformation mode changes from in-grain slip to 
SFs and deformation twin nucleation at grain boundaries. Therefore, 
Fig. 10 b and c displays the SFs in the ultra-fine grains of FCC without 
dislocation pile-up. On the contrary, dislocation slip and dislocation 
multiplication within fine grains can proceed normally, interact with 
each other and form dislocation substructures [ 40 ]. Such as, dislocation 
pile-up and dislocation cells ( Fig. 11 b).

A novel B2-strengthened Co

HEAs with 
outstanding strength-ductility was developed in the present study. The 
microstructural evolutions, deformation mechanisms and strengthening 
mechanisms were systematically investigated and discussed for the B2- 
strengthened alloys. The main conclusions are drawn as follows. 
(1) The A700 and A800 alloys display a partially recrystallized 
structure, consisting of rod, particle shaped B2 phase and ultra- 
fine FCC grain (0.21 μ m and 0.72 μ m, respectively). As the 
annealed temperature increases, the volume fraction of the B2 
phase decreases from 58% to 40%. 
(2) With the synergistic effect of dislocation, fine-grain and B2 
strengthening, the A700 alloy has a yield strength of 1820 ± 22 
MPa and an ultimate tensile strength of 1884 ± 21 MPa. The full- 
recrystallized A900 alloy exhibits a yield strength and ultimate 
tensile strength of 1038 ± 17 MPa and 1246 ± 18 MPa, 
respectively. 
(3) The contribution of precipitation strengthening to the strength of 
A700 and A900 alloys is 784 MPa and 591 Mpa, respectively. The 
plastic deformation of ultra-fine grain is dominated by 
deformation-induced SFs in the A700 alloy, while dislocation slip 
dominates the plastic deformation of the A900 alloy.
