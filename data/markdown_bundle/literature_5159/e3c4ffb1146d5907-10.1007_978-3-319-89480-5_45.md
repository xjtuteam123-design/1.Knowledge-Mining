On the Effect of Alloying Additions
to the Ni-Cr-Al-Nb Dual-Superlattice
Gamma-Gamma Prime-Gamma Double
Prime Superalloys
P. M. Mignanelli, N. G. Jones, M. C. Hardy and H. J. Stone
Abstract Recent work has identi ﬁed a novel Ni-Cr-Al-Nb alloy capable of gen-
erating a γ-γ′-γ″ dual-superlattice microstructure with excellent microstructural
stability and tensile strength. In this study, we report on the effect of additions of
alloying elements that traditionally partition to the γ matrix. Attention is focussed
on their effects upon the microstructure, hardness and lattice mis ﬁto f
dual-superlattice superalloys. The results obtained from these studies indicate that
judicious alloying may further enhance the properties and stability of these
dual-superlattice superalloys and that they offer the potential of greatly increased
temperature capability compared to other alloys reinforced by γ″ precipitates.
Keywords Nickel alloys
⋅ Superalloys ⋅ Microstructure
Introduction
It is well understood that the temperature capability of Inconel 718, which is
reinforced by metastable γ″ precipitates, is limited to temperatures below 650 °C
by the coarsening of the γ″ precipitates and the transformation to the thermody-
namically stable δ phase [1]. Historical efforts to increase the temperature capability
have included minor chemical modi ﬁcations and alterations to heat treatments to
produce compact morphology precipitates [ 2]. Whilst such approaches have led to
improved thermal stability, the bene ﬁts are relatively modest. Recent research has
identiﬁed alloy compositions that generate a three phase γ-γ′-γ″ dual-superlattice
microstructure with excellent microstructural thermal stability, with respect to other
γ″ containing alloys, and greatly increased tensile strength over most superalloys,

In order to develop nickel-base superalloys to meet the large range of property
requirements, consideration must be made of alloying additions beyond that of the
Ni-Cr-Al-Nb quaternary system used to demonstrate the viability of the γ-γ′-γ″
microstructure [ 6]. The correlation between solid solution strengthening of the γ
matrix and the creep strength of nickel-base superalloys has been well established,
with improved properties being obtained with greater alloying concentrations,
provided the formation of secondary phases can be avoided [ 7–9]. This is crucial as
over-alloying of the γ matrix can lead to the precipitation of topologically close
packed phases, such as sigma and mu, which are detrimental to the alloys properties
[10, 11]. The use of Mo and W to increase the solid solution strengthening of the γ
matrix is the most common method of improving the mechanical properties of
polycrystalline nickel-base superalloys, given the very high levels of strengthening
conferred by each of these elements on a per atomic percent basis [ 12, 13].
In addition to Mo and W, Fe and Co are also commonly added to nickel-base
superalloys and are added in considerably greater quantities to ful ﬁl their different
roles. Additions of Fe convey two primary bene ﬁts to nickel-base superalloys.
Firstly, the very low cost of Fe relative to other alloying elements helps to reduce
the overall cost of the alloy. Secondly, by introducing Fe to an alloy it increases the
processability of the alloy. However, care must be taken with Fe additions to avoid
the formation of Laves phases [ 14, 15]. Co on the other hand is introduced to reduce
the stacking fault energy, which can lead to increased strength through a greater
extent of twinning [ 16]. Alloying with Co has also been recently applied to the
development of an Inconel 718 type alloy, where the Co is used to stabilise the δ
phase, leading to an alloy with a large forging window, despite an increased pre-
cipitate volume fraction, compared to Inconel 718 [ 17].
In order to optimise the performance of the γ-γ′-γ″ superalloys, increased
compositional complexity is required to deliver the range of properties needed,
beyond tensile strength, for structural components in high temperature applications.
To investigate the development of more advanced alloys we have studied additions
of Mo, W, Fe and Co to the base Ni-15Cr-4Al-6Nb at.% alloy of 1 and 2 at.%
(Mo and W) or 4 and 8 at.% (Fe and Co).
Experimental
To investigate the effect of alloying on the microstructure of γ-γ′-γ″ superalloys a
series of nine alloys with composition Ni-15Cr-4Al-6Nb-X (at.%), where X = Mo,
W, Co or Fe were manufactured. The material was produced as ∼50 g bars by
vacuum arc melting, using elements with more than 99.9% purity, and a procedure
of 5 inversion and melting steps to ensure homogeneity. The alloys were then
homogenised at 1200 °C for 4 h under vacuum to minimise micro segregation.
The composition of each alloy was determined using energy dispersive X-ray

the TOPAS diffraction software package [ 18]. Thermodynamic calculations were
completed using Thermo-Calc with the Thermotech nickel database TTNi8.

The heat treatment for 10 h was chosen to represent an aging heat treatment, shown
to generate a γ-γ′-γ″ microstructure in the base alloy, with a high hardness [ 4].
Similarly, 100 h at 800 °C was chosen to maintain the same exposure temperature
as the short treatment, whilst producing an accelerated assessment of the mi-
crostructural evolution, given the likely service window of these alloys would be
close to 700 °C. Previous work [ 4], has shown the coarsening after 100 h at 800 °C
to be comparable or greater than the coarsening observed for longer durations at
both 700 and 750 °C for the base alloy. These previous results indicate the con-
servative nature of this higher temperature exposure given that the greater coars-
ening of the γ′ and γ″ precipitates at 800 °C will lead to deterioration in the
mechanical properties, which would be expected to be overestimated by this higher
temperature exposure.
The microstructures of the base alloy and the alloys with γ partitioning additions
following heat treatment for 10 h at 800 °C are shown in Fig. 1. A comparison of
the nine alloys reveals that the alloys appear broadly similar, with both γ′ and γ″
precipitates present in the microstructure, and the alloying with Mo or Fe has had
the smallest effect. However, further small variations with composition can be
observed across the two other alloying systems. Alloying with W, and especially
the greater addition of 2 at.% W, lead to agglomeration of the precipitate phases,
and a conjoined γ′-γ″ precipitate network, as opposed to distinct boundaries
between the two phases. Conversely, alloying with Co has reduced the precipitation
of γ″ in the microstructure compared with the base alloy, and has thereby reduced
the connected nature of the precipitate phases.
In order to further assess the microstructural variations produced by the alloying
additions, following 10 h at 800 °C, X-ray diffraction experiments were completed
at the Diamond Light Source, seeking to measure the lattice parameter of the
precipitates and hence derive the lattice mis ﬁt between the constituent phases. The
results of the diffraction data analysis are presented in Fig. 2. The addition of Mo,
Fig. 2a, can be seen to have little effect upon the lattice parameter of the γ′ pre-
cipitates. However, it can be seen to increase the lattice parameter of both the γ
matrix and the a-axis of the γ″, whilst the c-axis of the γ″ can be seen to decrease. In
contrast, the addition of W, Fig. 2b, shows an increase followed by a decrease in
the lattice parameter of the γ matrix and the a-axis of the γ″, whilst the lattice
parameter of the γ′ and the c-axis of the γ″ are increasing and decreasing respec-
tively. The addition of Fe, Fig. 2c, to the base alloy shows a consistent trend across
all three phases (and by extension all four measured lattice parameters) as the lattice
parameters all increase with increasing Fe content. The addition of Co, Fig. 2d, is
similarly complex as that of W, as the γ matrix and γ′ lattice parameter decrease and
subsequently increase, whilst the a- and c-axes of the γ″ both increase with Co
content, albeit only by small amounts.

similar trends across the four alloy systems. The γ-γ′ lattice mis ﬁt, Fig. 2e, is
observed to decrease with increasing content of both Mo and Fe, whilst W increases
the lattice mis ﬁt and Co decreases before increasing, such that the values are quite

In order to better understand the results obtained from the microstructural
analyses, thermodynamic calculations were completed to investigate the predicted
partitioning of the alloying elements between the γ, γ′ and γ″ phases, Fig. 3.B y ﬁrst
considering the change in Ni content of the three phases, Fig. 3a–c, it can be seen
that the Ni decreases in concentration in all three phases, with increasing amounts
of the alloying additions. This result is unsurprising, as Ni is used as the balance
element in these alloys and hence all of the alloying additions are added at the
expense of the Ni. However, it should be noted that of the alloying elements added
to the base alloy, the Co addition is the most effective at reducing the Ni con-
centration in the γ′ and γ″ phases, Fig. 3b and c, both by raw amount and on a per
atomic percent basis. In contrast to the Ni concentration in the γ, the Cr concen-
tration in the γ, Fig. 3d, is increased by the addition of all of the alloying elements,
with only W producing a weak change, and it would be hoped that this increase
would lead to a greater resistance to oxidation and corrosion damage. This result
can be correlated with the Cr concentration in the γ′ phase, Fig. 3e, which is shown
to decrease with the increase in alloying additions, again with the exception of W,
whilst the γ″, Fig. 3f, contains very little Cr (<0.5 at.%) in all nine alloying
variations.
Consideration of the Al concentration, Fig. 3g–i, in all three phases reveals that
there is very little change in the Al content across the complete range of alloys

the relatively similar physical properties of Nb with Mo and W, as refractory

To assess the preliminary mechanical properties of the alloys, hardness mea-
surements were made following each of the two thermal exposures and the data are
presented in Fig. 5. Comparison of the data for each alloy revealed that there was
little variation in the hardness between the 10 h and 100 h heat treatments, indi-
cating the good thermal stability of these alloys. It can also be seen that the alloying
with Mo, W and Fe all led to increases in the alloy hardness, which increased with
larger additions of the alloying element. Conversely, the addition of Co produced a
decrease in the alloy hardness. Comparison of the rates of change in hardness, per
atomic percent, revealed that W was the most effective element at strengthening the
γ-γ′-γ″ alloy, whilst the addition of Mo was next most effective, followed by Fe and
the addition of Co leading to a decrease in hardness. However, the increase in
hardness conveyed by W additions comes at the expense of the morphological
stability of the γ′ and γ″ precipitates in the alloy, which is thought to be undesirable.
Similarly, the increased coarsening of the γ″ precipitates, brought about by addi-
tions of Co, is a likely cause of the observed decrease in hardness.

Conclusions
This work has shown that the γ-γ′-γ″ forming superalloys exhibit good solubility
for alloying elements, critical to improving the overall mechanical properties of this
new class of alloys. The addition of Mo and W led to substantial hardening of the
alloy, potentially leading to improved mechanical properties. However, for the
addition of W, this comes at the expense of the morphology of the precipitates
formed in the alloy and may have to be traded off against the potentially deleterious
precipitate network that is formed in alloys that contain an excess of W. The
addition of Fe and Co provided a small hardening increase in the case of Fe, but in
the case of Co, led to a deterioration in the γ-γ′-γ″ microstructure after exposure for
100 h at 800 °C and a consequent decrease in the alloy hardness.
