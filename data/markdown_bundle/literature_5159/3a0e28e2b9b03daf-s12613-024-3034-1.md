Abstract: Fe–Cr–Ni austenitic alloys are extensively utilized in the hot-end components of nuclear li ght water reactors, turbine disks, and
gas compressors. However, their low strength at elevated temperatures limits their engineering applications. In this study, a novel precipit-
ation-strengthened alloy system is developed by incorporating Al and Si elements into a FeCrNi equiatomic alloy. The results indicate that
the FeCrNiAlxSix (at%, x = 0.1, 0.2) alloys possess heterogeneous precipitation structures that feature a micron-scale σ phase at the grain
boundaries and a nanoscale ordered body-centered cube (B2) phase within the grains. An exceptional strength–ductility synergy across a
wide temperature range is achieved in FeCrNiAl0.1Si0.1 alloys due to grain refinement and precipitation strengthening. Notably, a yield
strength of 693.83 MPa, an ultimate tensile strength of 817.55 MPa, and a uniform elongation of 18.27% are attained at 873 K. The dislo-
cation shearing mechanism for B2 phases and the Orowan bypass mechanism for σ phase, coupled with a high density of nano-twins and
stacking faults in the matrix, contribute to the excellent mechanical properties at cryogenic and ambient temperatures. Moreover, the
emergence of serrated σ phase and micro-twins in the matrix plays a crucial role in the strengthening and toughening mechanisms at inter-
mediate temperatures. This study offers a novel perspective and strategy for the development of precipitation-hardened Fe–Cr–Ni austen-
itic alloys with exceptional strength–ductility synergy over a broad temperature range.
Keywords: high/medium-entropy alloys; medium temperature; mechanical property; serrated σ phase
 
 
1. Introduction
The search for materials with excellent mechanical prop-
e
rties across a wide temperature range has been a long-term
goal of the materials community. Alloys with high contents
of chromium (Cr), iron (Fe), and nickel (Ni) have served as
engineering heat-resistant materials for over a century,
primarily in the core components of nuclear light water react-
ors, turbine disks, and gas compressors [1
–2]. The m echanic-
al properties of these heat-resistant materials are notably af-
fected by service temperature, posing a considerable chal-
lenge in achieving an optimal strength–toughness synergy
across a wide temperature spectrum. The development of
medium- and high-entropy alloys (M/HEAs) has recently in-
troduced an innovative approach that differs from traditional
alloy design [3–7]. Reports indicate that single-phase face-
c
entered cubic (FCC) M/HEAs exhibit excellent low-tem-
perature mechanical properties, even at 4.2 K [8], and pos-
se
ss the best cryogenic fracture toughness reported to date
[9–12].
Num
erous studies have demonstrated that nearly all
single-phase FCC M/HEAs exhibit a remarkable reduction in
strength at elevated temperatures [13–16]. A single-pha se
matrix must be strengthened by second phases and precipit-
ates to maintain high strength [17–20]. Precipitation-re in-
forced microstructures are extensively employed in high-
temperature materials, such as Ni–Fe-based superalloys and
heat-resistant steels, where a single FCC matrix is
strengthened by the L12 phase and carbides [17–21]. The
pri
mary constituent elements of these alloys are Cr, Fe, and
Ni, while other solid solution elements such as V, Ti, and Nb
promote the precipitation of brittle carbides, enhancing the
mechanical properties of heat-resistant steel at elevated tem-
peratures [21–22]. However, the presence of carbides within
t
he matrix can lead to notable strain softening due to severe
interface cracking during deformation at high temperatures
[22], a phenomenon similar to that observed in particle-re in-
forced composites [23]. Additionally, the ordered L12 phase,
commonly utilized in superalloys, exhibits considerable em-
brittlement in the intermediate temperature range, although

2. Experimental 
2.1. Materials preparation
T
he FeCrNiAlxSix (at%, x = 0.1, 0.2) MEA ingots were
fabricated using a vacuum arc-melting furnace with mixtures
of high-purity metals (purity > 99.9wt%) under a pure argon
atmosphere. To ensure chemical uniformity, the ingots were
flipped and remelted several times before being drop-casted
into water-cooled copper molds, forming slabs with dimen-
sions of 2 mm × 10 mm × 100 mm. These as-cast plates were
homogenized at 1373 K for 5 h and then cold-rolled with a
total thickness reduction of 70%. These plates were sub-
sequently recrystallized at 1173 K for 1 h and water-
quenched. The incorporation of Al and Si elements enhances
the oxidation resistance of the material while simultaneously
reducing its density. However, FeCrNi MEA with high con-
tents of Al and Si (FeCrNiAl0.3Si0.3) exhibited notable brittle-
ness, thereby fracturing during the rolling process. Con-
sequently, the effect of Al and Si contents on the mechanical
properties of FeCrNi-based MEAs was limited to 5.9at%, re-
spectively (i.e., FeCrNiAl0.2Si0.2). 
2.2. Mechanical test
Dog-bone
 tensile specimens were machined from the re-
crystallized slabs with the tensile axis oriented along the
rolling direction using electrical discharge machining. The
gage dimensions (width, thickness, and length) of the quasi-
static specimens were 4 mm × 0.6 mm × 10.0 mm. All sur-
faces of the tensile specimens were mechanically polished
with 800# SiC paper. Quasistatic tensile tests were conduc-
ted using an Instron 5969 testing machine at a strain rate of
1 × 10−3 s−1 and temperatures from 77 to 973 K. Prior to con-
ducting uniaxial tests at 77 K, the specimens, along with the
tensile grips, were immersed in liquid nitrogen for 30 min
and remained submerged throughout the testing process.
Uniaxial tests performed between 873 and 973 K were con-
ducted in air within a temperature-controlled chamber. Dur-
ing this process, the sample was heated to the specified tem-
perature in the temperature-controlled chamber and held for
30 min. 
2.3. Materials characterization
X-ra
y diffraction (XRD) measurements were conducted
using Rigaku Ultima IV X-ray equipment operating at 40 kV
and 40 mA with Cu Kα radiation. Scanning was performed at
an increment of 0.01° and a rate of 4°/min over a range of
20°–100°. The microstructural characteristics were analyzed
using a JEOL JSM-7100F field emission gun scanning elec-
tron microscope equipped with back-scattered electron (BSE)
detectors, dual energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (Dual
EDS) detectors, and an electron backscatter diffraction
(EBSD) system. EBSD detections were conducted at an ac-
celeration voltage of 20 kV, with a selected scanning step
size ranging from 0.1 to 0.3 μm. EBSD specimens were elec-
tropolished using a mixed solution of HClO4 and C2H6O in a
volume ratio of 1:9, with an electric current of 0.8 mA at 298
K for 15 s. A JEM-2100F transmission electron microscope
(TEM) was employed to observe the microstructures at an
acceleration voltage of 200 kV. Slices that are 500 µm thick
were cut from the uniformly extended region of the fracture
specimen tensile at 77, 298, and 873 K. These slices were cut
parallel to the tensile axis and ground to a thickness of 50 µm
using 1000-grit SiC paper. Subsequently, a twin-jet electro-
polishing technique, along with Ar-ion milling treatment,
was used to thin the regions of interest to electron transpar-
ency.

of F

Fig. 3(a) shows the engineering stress–st rain curves of
FeCrNiAlxSix MEAs (x = 0, 0.1, 0.2) at 298 K. The addition
of Al and Si elements leads to a substantial increase in the ul-
timate tensile strength (UTS) and yield strength (YS) of the
FeCrNi MEA. Specifically, compared to the FeCrNi MEA
under the same treatment process [12

Fe

Fe
CrNi alloy, the grain size of the FeCrNiAl0.1Si0.1 MEA is

generally contributed to a substantial increase in the strength
of the Al, Si-added FeCrNi alloys. In Fig. 3(b), the Fe
CrNi-

3.3. Tensile behaviors of the FeCrNiAl0.1Si0.1 MEA at
77–873 K
The FeCrNiAl0.1Si0.1 MEA exhibits exceptional strength–
toughness synergy. Thus, the present work mainly focuses on
the mechanical response and underlying microstructure evol-
ution of FeCrNiAl0.1Si0.1 MEA. The mechanical properties of
the FeCrNiAl0.1Si0.1 MEA under tension loading were invest-
igated across a temperature range of 77–973 K, as shown in
Fig. 5(a). Compared to its mechanical properties at 298 K, the
Fe
CrNiAl0.1Si0.1 MEA exhibits an enhanced YS of 1125.58
MPa and UTS of 1252.31 MPa, along with an elongation of
14.8% at 77 K. At the intermediate temperature of 873 K, the
FeCrNiAl0.1Si0.1 MEA maintains a high yield strength of
693.83 MPa without sacrificing ductility. Interestingly, the
exceptional tensile strength exceeding 300 MPa and promin-
ent plasticity up to 56.35% are realized at the intermediate
temperature of 973 K. These findings demonstrate that the
FeCrNiAl0.1Si0.1 MEA possesses excellent mechanical char-
acteristics from cryogenic temperature to intermediate tem-
peratures. Fig. 5(b) summarizes the variation of yield strength
wi
th the temperature for a series of alloys, including FeCrNi-
Al0.lSi0.1 MEA, heat-resistant steels [41–46], and other MEAs
[47–48]. The yield strength of all alloys exhibits a marked
de
cline with increasing temperature, with the inflection point
occurring within the range of 473–673 K. Overall, the
strength loss at intermediate temperature is attributed to nu-
merous factors, including lower grain boundary (GB)
strength, GB embrittlement, growth of precipitate, stress in-
stability due to dynamic strain aging, and accelerated recov-
ery/recrystallization [16]. The FeCrNiAl0.1Si0.1 MEA demon-
strates exceptional thermal stability within the temperature
range of 298 to 873 K, as evidenced by its consistent yield
strength throughout this range. Different from the marked re-
duction in yield strength from 200 to 110 MPa at 298 and
473 K, respectively, of the FeCrNi MEA [49], the
Fe
CrNiAl0.1Si0.1 MEA exhibits a comparable yield strength of
693.83 MPa, a considerable tensile ductility of 18.27%, and
early strain hardening at 873 K compared to those at 298 K.
This finding implies that the incorporation of Al and Si ele-
ments remarkably increases the heat-resistance capability of
the FeCrNi MEA at moderate temperatures. This phenomen-
on can be primarily attributed to the structurally stable σ
phase without notable growth and deformation at 873 K, re-
vealing high-energy phase boundaries of the σ/matrix char-
acterized by high hardness and high elastic modulus [50].

 the microstructure observed at room temperature, B2

 an abundance of densely-packed deformation twin

bution compared to deformation at 298 K, as confirmed by
the corresponding SAED pattern. Under low-temperature
conditions, the further reduction in stacking fault energy and
the increase in stress level jointly promote the generation of a

4. Conclusions
In this study, the FeCrNiAlxSix (x = 0.1, 0.2) MEAs were
developed to generate an FCC matrix with σ + BCC precipit-
ates. Excellent mechanical properties across a wide temper-
ature range were obtained, and corresponding deformation
mechanisms were systematically investigated. The conclu-
sions can be summarized as follows:
(1) The yield strength of FeCrNiAlxSix (x = 0.1, 0.2)
MEAs is notably improved by 57% (400.31 → 628.43 MPa)
and 165% (400.31 → 1061.63 MPa) compared to FeCrNi
MEA. The yield strength of FeCrNiAl0.1Si0.1 MEA at 77 K is
doubled (1125.58 MPa), and the elongation remains 14.8%,
mainly due to precipitation strengthening, grain boundary
strengthening, and solid solution strengthening.
(2) After deformation at 873 K, the σ phase maintains
structural stability without notable growth or deformation.
The interaction between the serrated phases and micro-twins
in the matrix results in an excellent strength–ductility syn-
ergy at intermediate temperatures.
(3) During high-temperature deformation, the serrated σ
phase neither softens nor grows, thereby preserving the high-
temperature strength of the alloy. Additionally, the sawtooth
σ phase boundary mitigates stress concentration at the crack
tip due to its distinctive zigzag structure, effectively prevent-
ing crack formation and propagation, which delays the frac-
ture of the alloy.
