Computational-driven design of Ti-based medium entropy alloy for 
enhanced high-temperature performance above 600 °C 
Rujian Zhang 1, Zhaolong Ma 1,2,3,4*, Xiaoyue Li1, Haiyang Li1 and Xingwang Cheng1,2,3,4 
ABSTRACT The development of advanced titanium alloys 
capable of operating above 600 °C remains a critical challenge 
for aerospace propulsion systems, where conventional Ti al -
loys suffer from insufficient high-temperature strength and 
microstructural instability. Here, we propose a computation
-
ally driven design strategy for titanium-based medium-en -
tropy alloys (MEAs) that integrates thermodynamic phase 
prediction with mechanistically informed strength modeling, 
enabling systematic exploration of the Ti-Nb-Al-Cr qua -
ternary system. The optimized Ti 70Nb 10Al 15Cr 5 MEA exhibits 
exceptional performance metrics: 18% room-temperature 
ductility (as-cast), a yield strength of 520.7 MPa at 650 °C 
(post-aging), and an ultralow density of 4.76 g/cm
3 (45% 
lighter than Inconel 718). Microstructural characterization 
reveals a metastable single-phase BCC structure in the as-cast 
state, which transforms into a BCC/Ti 
3Al dual-phase system 
upon aging, with temperature-dependent precipitate mor -
phology and phase stability. The alloy demonstrates superior 
high-temperature strength retention up to 900 °C (>80 MPa 
yield strength), outperforming commercial titanium alloys 
(e.g., Ti-1100, TG6) and bridging the performance gap be -
tween conventional Ti alloys and nickel-based superalloys. 
This work establishes a multi-criteria design paradigm for 
entropy-engineered alloys, offering a viable pathway to light
-
weight, high-temperature structural materials for next-gen -
eration aerospace applications. 
Keywords: computational-driven design, Ti-based medium en -
tropy alloy, high-temperature tensile property 
INTRODUCTION 
The relentless pursuit of lightweight and high-temperature 
materials in aerospace engineering has reached a critical junc -
ture, driven by the urgent need to enhance thrust-to-weight 
ratios and operational efficiency of next-generation propulsion 
systems. While nickel-based superalloys have long dominated 
high-temperature applications, their inherent density (>8 g/cm
3) 
imposes severe limitations on weight-sensitive components. 
Titanium alloys, with their superior specific strength and 
inherent oxidation resistance, emerge as compelling alternatives, 
yet conventional Ti-based systems (e.g., Ti-6Al-4V) exhibit 
inadequate high-temperature resistance above 600 °C [1], fun
-
damentally constraining their service temperatures. This per -
formance gap has spurred global efforts to develop novel 
titanium alloys capable of bridging the 600–900 °C operational 
window—a regime where current materials either fail 
mechanically or become economically prohibitive. 
The advent of multi-principal element alloys, particularly 
high-entropy alloys (HEAs), has revolutionized alloy design 
paradigms by exploiting configurational entropy stabilization. 
Recent studies on Ti-containing HEAs, such as TaNbHfZrTi and 
NbTaTiZr systems [2,3] demonstrate improved high-tempera -
ture strength retention compared to conventional alloys. For 
example, Ti 45V 45Cr 5Al 5 exhibits a yield strength (YS) of 676 MPa 
at 600 °C [4], superior to conventional IMI834 (565 MPa at 
600 °C) [5]. However, these Ti-containing systems often com -
promise on density (>6 g/cm 3 [6]) or ductility (≤8% elongation 
at room temperature [7]), undermining their practical viability. 
For instance, the NbTiZrVTa HEA maintains 190 MPa YS at 
1000 °C but with a density exceeding 8 g/cm
3 [8], while the 
CrNbTiVZr alloy achieves 1230 MPa YS at 600 °C but suffers 
from <5% plastic strain at ambient conditions [9]. More fun
-
damentally, existing design approaches predominantly rely on 
empirical composition exploration [10,11]
, lacking systematic 
methodologies to navigate the vast quaternary/quinary compo -
sition space while balancing competing requirements: high- 
temperature strength, room-temperature ductility, low-density, 
and phase stability. The absence of predictive frameworks inte
-
grating thermodynamic stability with mechanical property 
models has resulted in suboptimal alloy performance. 
To address these limitations, we present a computationally 
driven design strategy for titanium-based medium-entropy 
alloys (MEAs) that synergistically combines thermodynamic 
phase prediction with mechanistically informed strength mod -
eling. By implementing this dual-filter methodology across the 
Ti-Nb-Al-Cr quaternary system, we finally gained an optimized 
Ti 70Nb 10Al 15Cr 5 MEA demonstrates outstanding performance 
metrics: 18% room-temperature ductility (as-cast) combined 
with 520.7 MPa yield strength at 650 °C (after aging at 650 °C ), 
along with an ultralow density of 4.76 g/cm
3 (45% lighter than 
Inconel 718). This work establishes a generalized paradigm for 
entropy-engineered materials, accelerating the discovery of 
lightweight alternatives to nickel superalloys in critical aerospace

EXPERIMENTAL SECTION 
The Ti aNb bAl cCr d alloys (a + b + c + d = 100 at%) were syn -
thesized by arc-melting high-purity Ti, Nb, Al, and Cr particles 
(
≥99.95 wt%) in a vacuum arc furnace under argon atmosphere. 
Each ingot was remelted eight times to ensure compositional 
homogeneity, with a pure titanium block pre-melted to scavenge 
residual oxygen. All as-cast alloys underwent initial mechanical 
property screening through room-temperature and 900 °C ten
-
sile testing. Based on these results, the Ti 70Nb 10Al 15Cr 5 alloy was 
selected for subsequent aging treatments and detailed char -
acterization. 
Tensile specimens were machined via electrical discharge 
machining (EDM) with gauge dimensions of 13.5 mm × 4 mm × 
1 mm (room temperature and 650–900 °C). Quasi-static tensile 
tests were performed on an LE5105 universal testing machine in 
ambient air, with a constant strain rate of 1 × 10 −3 s−1. Selected 
Ti 70Nb 10Al 15Cr 5 specimens were aged at 650, 700, 750, and 
900 °C for 8 h in a muffle furnace followed by water quenching 
(in all figures of this article, (Q) denotes water quenching). Post- 
aging mechanical evaluations included room-temperature tensile 
tests, Vickers hardness measurements (Qness 10A+, 500 gf 
load), and high-temperature tensile tests conducted at their 
respective aging temperatures (650, 700, 750, and 900 °C). 
For microstructural characterization, samples were mechani -
cally polished with SiC sandpaper up to 3000 grit, followed by 
colloidal silica suspension polishing. Phase analysis was con -
ducted using a Bruker D8 Advance X-ray diffractometer (XRD) 
with Cu-Kα radiation (
λ = 1.5406 Å), scanning from 20° to 85° 
at 1°/min. Microstructural observations were performed using a 
Zeiss Supra 55 scanning electron microscope (SEM) equipped 
with an energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) detector, 
operating in secondary electron (SE) and backscattered electron 
(BSE) modes. Electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) analysis 
was carried out using an EDAX Velocity Super detector to 
acquire crystallographic orientation maps, processed via 
AZtecCrystal software. High-resolution transmission electron 
microscopy (TEM) and selected area electron diffraction (SAED) 
were conducted on a FEI Talos F200X scanning/transmission 
electron microscope (S/TEM) operated at 200 kV, with samples 
prepared by focused ion beam (FIB) milling (TESCAN 
AMBER). 
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION 
Alloy design-screening via integrated theory and experiment 
The Ti aNb bAl cCr d alloys (a + b + c + d = 100 at%) were designed 
by systematically varying the atomic percentages of Ti, Nb, Al, 
and Cr within predefined ranges (≤80 at%, for each element) at 
5 at% increments, generating 969 candidate compositions. To 
prioritize alloys with balanced high-temperature strength and 
room-temperature ductility, a multi-stage screening strategy was 
implemented, as shown in Fig. 1. First, the combined Al + Cr 
content was restricted to ≤30 at% to suppress the formation of 
brittle Laves phases, as excessive Al or Cr promotes intermetallic 
compound precipitation [12,13]. Solid-solution strengthening 
models proposed by Toda et al. [14] (for room-temperature YS) 
and Curtin et al. [15] (for 900 °C YS) were then applied to 
calculate the theoretical YS for each alloy composition, retaining 
only alloys meeting strength thresholds (>500 MPa at 25 °C or 
>100 MPa at 900 °C), which reduced the candidate pool to 54 
compositions. It is noteworthy that these calculated strengths 
did not consider the contribution from microstructural char
-
acteristics such as grain size, which were deliberately excluded as 
constant parameters, based on the fundamental assumption of 
consistent single BCC phase formation across all compositions 
at room temperature and 900 °C where yield strengths were 
calculated. This assumption holds reasonably well given that: 
(i) typical alloys (e.g., Ti 70Nb 10Al 15Cr 5, Ti 70Nb 5Al 15Cr 10, and 
Ti 70Nb 15Al 10Cr 5, as discussed latter) exhibit single-phase BCC 
structure in the as-cast state at room temperature (Fig. S1), and 
(ii) the equilibrium solidification paths of them also indicate a 
single BCC structure at 900 °C (Fig. S2). Subsequently, Ther -
modynamic calculations using ThermoCalc with the TCHEA1 
database [16] further evaluated three critical parameters: melting 
point (prioritized higher values to enhance high-temperature 
strength [17]
), Lower limit of single-phase region (minimized to 
ensure microstructural stability with a wide temperature range 
[18]), solidification range (minimized to mitigate casting defects 
such as hot tearing and microporosity [19]). In addition, valence 
electron concentration (VEC, a low value optimized to enhance 
room-temperature ductility [20]) and density (<5 g/cm
3) are also 
evaluated using equations in Fig. 1. By setting these parameters 
with thresholds shown Fig. 1, two alloys with potentially 
superior performance were screened out for further study. 
Concurrently, a reference alloy was intentionally selected in this 
study to validate the predictive accuracy of the computational 
model. These alloys and corresponding calculated parameters 
are given in Table 1. The calculation details of room-tempera
-
ture and 900 °C YS are given in the Supplementary Information. 
The equilibrium solidification paths of the three alloys are 
shown in Fig. S2, from which the melting point, lower limit of 
single-phase region, and solidification range in Table 1 can be 
extracted.

Although as-cast Ti 70Nb 10Al 15Cr 5 exhibits a single-phase BCC 
structure, it might not be stable at higher temperatures. 
According to the molybdenum equivalency (MoE) [24], which 
has been widely used by traditional Titanium alloys for char -
acterizing the β-phase stability, the MoE of Ti 70Nb 10Al 15Cr 5 is 
calculated as 5.5 [25], indicating that this alloy is a near-beta 
alloy and its as-cast BCC structure is metastable. Therefore, to 
evaluate the high-temperature mechanical performances of 
Ti 70Nb 10Al 15Cr 5 at 650, 700, 750, and 900 °C, this alloy was 
subjected to annealing at the same temperatures for 8 h before 
tensile tests. XRD patterns in Fig. 4a reveal dual-phase con
-
stituents (BCC + Ti 3Al) across all annealing conditions, with no 
(100) diffraction peak corresponding to the B2 phase observed, 
indicating that the BCC phase remains disordered after aging. 
The EDS mapping (Fig. 4e and Fig. S3) and point analyses 
(Table 2) confirm that the Ti 
3Al precipitates possess relatively 
low Nb/Cr solubility. As illustrated in Fig. 4b, alloys annealed at 
650–750 °C contain high densities of needle-like Ti 3Al pre -
cipitates, whereas 900 °C annealing results in sparser bulk- 
shaped precipitates, suggesting temperature-dependent mor -
phological evolution.

It is noted that near-α titanium alloys are mainly employed for 
high-temperature applications, as employed by benchmarks 
including IMI834, Ti60, Ti1100, et al
., as given in Fig. 8. How-
ever, our alloys may offer distinct advantages for the target 
application temperature range (650–900 °C). First, conventional 
near-α titanium alloys undergo detrimental α
→β phase trans -
formation in this regime, compromising mechanical stability 
[41]
. In contrast, our β-alloy matrix maintains phase stability 
without polymorphic transformation. Second, the metastable β 
matrix in our as-cast alloy exhibits superior fracture strain (18%, 
Fig. 2a) compared to typical near-α alloys (2.1%–12.9% for 
IMI834 [5,42], 9%–11% for Ti60 [43] and 11.5%–13% for TG6 
[44]), significantly enhancing processability where traditional 
alloys show limited ductility. Third, high-temperature strength is 
augmented through high-volume-fraction hardening pre
-
cipitates (Ti 3Al in this study), which effectively strengthen the 
material by impeding dislocation slip and climb mechanisms at 
elevated temperatures [45]. Moreover, the yield strengths of our 
alloy reported in Fig. 8 correspond specifically to the annealed 
condition based on an as-cast coarse-grain microstructure 
(Fig. 3). These properties could be significantly improved 
through subsequent thermomechanical processing (cold/hot 
working) to refine the BCC grains and optimize Ti 
3Al pre-
cipitates. Further thermomechanical processing of this alloy, 
along with systematic investigation of additional properties such 
as oxidation resistance and creep behavior, will be the primary 
focus of our future research. 
CONCLUSIONS 
This study successfully demonstrates a computational-experi -
mental framework for designing titanium-based medium- 
entropy alloys (MEAs) with balanced room-temperature ducti
-
lity and high-temperature strength. The optimized 
Ti 
70Nb 10Al 15Cr 5 alloy achieves breakthrough performance, 
combining 18% as-cast ductility, 520.7 MPa yield strength at 
650 °C, and a record-low density of 4.76 g/cm
3, enabled by a 
metastable BCC matrix and controlled Ti 3Al precipitation. 
Aging treatments induce a temperature-dependent biphasic 
microstructure, where refined Ti 3Al precipitates enhance 
strength while retaining ductility through dynamic recovery 
mechanisms. Comparative analysis confirms the alloy’s super
-
iority over conventional Ti alloys across 650–900 °C, with yield 
strengths exceeding commercial benchmarks (e.g., Ti-1100 at 
600 °C) and maintaining functionality in extreme thermal 
regimes where Ti alloys typically fail. The dual-phase stability 
and scalable castability of this MEA highlight its potential to 
replace denser nickel superalloys in critical high-temperature 
components, while the integrated design methodology provides 
a universal strategy for accelerating the discovery of entropy- 
stabilized materials with tailored thermomechanical properties.
