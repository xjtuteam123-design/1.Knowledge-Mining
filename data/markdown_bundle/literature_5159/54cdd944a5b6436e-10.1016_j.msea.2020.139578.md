Abstract 
In this work we report new γ′ strengthened cobalt base superalloys that have low mass densities 
in the range of ~ 8.3 - 8.5 gm/ cm 
3, high solvus up to ~ 1128 °C and high specific 0.2 % proof 
strength of ~ 90 MPa.gm-1.cm 3 at temperatures up to 770 °C. The basic alloy composition is Co-
10Al-5Cr-2Ta that exhibits γ/γ′ microstructure on heat treatment. Electron diffrac tion and 
elemental partitioning across γ/γ′ indicate the γ′ has an ordered L1 2 structure with a stoichiometry 
Co 3(Al,Ta,Cr) coherently embedded in disordered face-c entered-cubic (fcc) γ-Co matrix. The γ′ 
structure is primarily stabilized by Ta addition. T he γ′ volume fraction and the solvus 
temperature of the base alloy was measured to be 14 % and 870 °C respectively. These values are 
significantly enhanced by the addition of 30at.% Ni (36% and 975 °C) and together with 4at.% 
Ti attaining the values of 63% and 1128 °C, respectively. Atom Probe measurements reveal bot h 
Ni and Ti strongly partition to γ′ precipitates enhancing the stability of the γ' while Cr partitions 
to the γ phase. At 900 °C after 200 hours of aging, the 30at.% Ni added all oy without Ti reveals 
formation of coarsened γ′ at the grain boundaries, directionally coarsened γ′ with wavy γ/γ′ 
interface and coagulated rod shape γ′ inside the grains. Addition of Ti (2 and 4 at. %), however, 
suppresses the formation of these features up to 1000 hours of aging at 900 °C. The feasibility of 
tuning the properties of the present alloys by allo ying additions makes them attractive for their 
further development. 
Keywords: Cobalt-based superalloys; Low mass density; Atom pr obe tomography; 
Microstructural stability; Mechanical properties.

1. Introduction 
Superalloys are used as material for the components in aero-based and land-based gas turbine 
engines where the temperature reaches beyond 1500 ° C. These alloys have a characteristic 
microstructure having cuboidal shape ordered L1 2 precipitates, termed as γ′ , coherently 
embedded in disordered face-centered-cubic (fcc) γ matrix phase, forming the basic architecture 
for most of the commercial Ni-based superalloys. Th ese are multicomponent with 8 to 12 
alloying elements that provide an optimum combinati on of high temperature mechanical 
properties and oxidation/corrosion resistance [1–6] . After decades of alloy development, the 
operation temperature of Ni-based superalloys is no w approaching its base element, Ni, melting 
temperature [3]. Hence, search for alternate alloy system is in progress that should have higher 
temperature capability and with similar mass densities as for Ni-based superalloys. 
Formation of γ/γ′ microstructure in Co-Al-W alloy system attracted r esearchers as an alternative 
with the possibility of higher temperature capabili ty than Ni-based superalloys since the base 
element Co has melting temperature higher by 40 °C than Ni [7]. The microstructure is similar to 
Ni-based superalloys that contains strengthening L1 2 ordered cuboidal γ′ precipitates with a 
stoichiometry of Co 3(Al,W) embedded in fcc γ-Co matrix phase [8,9]. Further alloying additions 
such as Ta, Ni, Ti, Nb, Mo, Re, Cr, Ru and B were o ptimized to increase mechanical properties, 
microstructural and environmental stability at high er temperatures [10–27]. For example, the γ′ 
solvus temperature increased from ~ 990 °C, for the base Co-Al-W alloy, up to ~ 1150 °C for 
Co-Ni-Al-W-Ta-Ti alloy. Some of alloy compositions show comparable creep properties to 1 st 
generation Ni-based superalloys. However, their mas s densities are above 9.5 gm/ cm 3, higher 
than Ni-based superalloys making them less promisin g for applications where weight/strength 
ratio is crucial. This is due to the essential requ irement of W addition to stabilize γ′ phase in Co-
Al-W system. 
In 2015, Co-based superalloys were developed that e xhibit the similar γ/γ′ microstructure 
however, without W addition [24,28–32]. The alloys were based on Co-Al with optimized 
addition of Mo and Nb/Ta. On heat treatment, the mi crostructure contains L1 
2 ordered cuboidal 
γ′ precipitates with the stoichiometry Co 3(Al,Mo,Nb/Ta) embedded in fcc γ-Co matrix. These 
alloys have mass densities in the range of 8.4 to 8 .6 gm/cm 3 with some of the compositions 
having comparable mechanical properties to Co-Al-W based superalloys at the ambient and

Recently, other Co-based alloy systems have also be en investigated looking for possibility of 
formation of stable γ/γ′ microstructure at higher temperatures, e.g. Co-Ti based with the addition 
of Cr or Mo or V [34,35] [36,37], Co-Al-V [38] and Co-Ge-W [39] alloys. However, these alloy 
systems show inferior γ/γ′ stability, lower γ′ solvus temperature and narrow processing window 
(smaller difference between solvus and solidus temp erature) than those of Co-Al-W and Co-Al-
Mo-Nb/Ta based systems. Later, Nyshadham et al. via DFT calculations predicted a narrow γ/γ′ 
phase field in the Co-Nb-V and Co-Ta-V systems [40] . Then it was experimentally validated by 
Tirado et al. [41,42]. Despite this new development s, aging of Co-Ta-V alloys at 900 °C beyond 
16 hours leads to dissolution of γ′ precipitates via discontinuous precipitation of la mellar γ-Co + 
C36-Co 3(Ta,V). Similarly, in Co-Nb-V alloy, the γ′ cuboidal precipitates transform to 
equilibrium needle shaped DO 19 ordered phase. 
In this work we show new Co-based superalloy compos itions with γ/γ′ microstructure and have 
γ′ solvus temperatures above 1100 °C, mass densities in the range of 8.3-8.5 gm/ cm 3 and 
microstructural stability at 900 °C up to 1000 hour s. We identified a quaternary Co-Al-Cr-Ta 
based alloy system that exhibits L1 2 ordered γ′ precipitate with the stoichiometry Co 3(Al,Ta,Cr) 
distributed in disordered fcc γ-Co matrix. This alloy was further developed by add ition of strong 
γ′ stabilizers, Ni and Ti, and their effect on γ′ solvus, partitioning behavior and mechanical 
properties were evaluated. The present study will c ommence further development of superalloys 
with improved properties.

3.3 The influence of Ti addition on the Co-30Ni-10Al-5Cr-2Ta alloy 
Figs. 4(a-c) show the microstructural comparison of Co30Ni, Co30Ni2Ti and Co30Ni4Ti alloys, 
respectively after aging at 900 °C for 50 hours. The BSE SEM micrographs reveal the duplex γ/γ′ 
microstructure is maintained and a significant increase in the γ′ volume fraction after Ti addition. 
The γ′ precipitates in Co30Ni2Ti alloy, Fig. 4(b), are al igned along a specific crystallographic 
direction and occasionally evolved into rod shape. This alignment of precipitates with or without 
any external stresses has been termed as rafting. T he directional alignment of precipitates along 
the elastically soft [100] direction has been reported in both W-free and W-containing model Co-
Al based superalloys and various Ni-Al based superalloys [21,52–57]. This alignment of γ′ along 
the soft [100] type directions is driven by the min imization of elastic interactions among the 
elastic anisotropic precipitates [58–60]. The incre ment in Ti content to 4 at.%Ti (Co30Ni4Ti 
alloy) reduces the propensity for directional align ment of γ′ precipitates. This can have direct 
correlation with the change in γ/γ′ lattice misfit. Note that, all the three alloys al so contain

3.6 Mechanical Properties 
The temperature dependency of 0.2% proof stress (PS ) of the alloys was evaluated by 
performing compression tests between 25 to 870 °C. Fig. 9(c) shows the 0.2% PS versus 
temperature plots for Co30Ni, Co30Ni2Ti, Co30Ni4Ti alloys and their comparison with other 
Co-based superalloys. All the investigated alloys s how a similar trend of 0.2% PS versus 
temperature variation with three distinct regions. The first region shows a negative dependency 
of 0.2% PS with temperature till 570 °C for all three alloys. The second region shows a y ield 
strength anomaly with positive dependency of 0.2% P S with temperature. Co30Ni and 
Co30Ni2Ti alloys show a peak in 0.2% PS at 770 °C, whereas Co30Ni4Ti alloy achieves a peak 
strength at 670 °C. Thus, the addition of 4 at.% Ti shifts the peak 0.2% PS temperature by 100 
°C. The observation of flow stress anomaly with temp erature has been reported in various γ/γ′ 
containing Ni and Co-based superalloys [10,11,17,71–73]. An activation of multiple slip systems 
with temperature assisted cross slip of super parti al dislocations from high antiphase boundary 
(APB) energy (111) octahedral plane to low energy c ubic (100) plane led to formation of KW 
(Kear-Wilsdorf) lock at elevated temperature. These locks further obstruct the motion of coming 
dislocation and thereby provides addition strengthe ning. Temperature beyond peak 0.2% PS, a 
third region of negative dependency of 0.2% PS with temperature is observed in all three alloys.

4 Conclusions 
The present work reports new γ/γ′ quaternary Co-Al-Ta-Cr based superalloys with low mass 
densities in the range of 8.3 to 8.5 gm/cm 3, solvus temperatures up to 1128 °C and good 
mechanical properties even at high temperatures. Th e key findings of this work can be 
summarized by following points: 
1. 
 The base alloy of composition Co-10Al-5Cr-2Ta after aging at 800 °C shows appearance of 
cuboidal shape γ′ precipitates having L1 2 ordered structure of stoichiometry Co 3(Al,Ta,Cr) 
that are coherently embedded in the fcc γ-Co matrix. 
2. An addition of Ni by replacing equal amount of Co i n Co-10Al-5Cr-2Ta alloy increases the 
γ′ solvus temperature. Co-30Ni-10Al-5Cr-2Ta alloy aft er aging at 900 °C for 50 h shows γ/γ′ 
microstructure having a γ′ solvus temperature of 975 °C. 
3. Ti addition to Co-30Ni-10Al-5Cr-2Ta alloy increases the γ′ solvus temperature up to 1128 
°C. Co30Ni2Ti and Co30Ni4Ti alloys show microstruct ural stability up to 1000 hours at 900 
°C with mass densities of ~ 8.30 ± 0.01 gm/ cm 3. 
4. Compositional analysis of γ and γ′ in Co30Ni, Co30Ni2Ti and Co30Ni4Ti alloys show 
partitioning of Ni, Al, Ta and Ti to the γ′ phase while Co and Cr partition to the γ phase. With 
increase in Ti content the partitioning of Ni, Al, and Ta to the γ′ decreases. The γ/γ′ lattice 
misfit also decreases with Ti addition. 
5. Co30Ni, Co30Ni2Ti and Co30Ni4Ti alloys show yield s trength anomaly at high 
temperatures. Due to low mass densities, these allo ys show higher specific 0.2% proof stress 
as compared to other Co-based superalloys at the temperatures of 25, 770 and 870 °C.

6. At 900 °C after 200 hours of aging, in Co30Ni alloy , coarse and bulky γ′ at grain boundaries, 
directionally coarsened and rod shape γ′ inside the grains were formed. Co30Ni2Ti and 
Co30Ni4Ti alloys show good microstructural stability up to 1000 hours.
