Summary
Creep rupture tests at 550°C lasting up to 16,000 h
habe been made with 13 test heats containing 0.15% C,
o to 2.4% Cr and 0.25 to 1% Mo as well as 0.22% C, 0 to
1.2% Cr, 0.5 to 1% Mo, and 0.15 to 0.35% V. The
steels with higher vanadium content exhibited with the
same chromium and molybdenum contents the lower
creep rate and consequently the higher creep resistance
and also the higher values of the 1% creep limit and
creep rupture strength for 1000 and 10,000 h. This
dependence was not so clearly pronounced with the
molybdenum-vanadium steels.
The lowest values of the creep rate have been found in
many instances already in the test stage with 0.2 to
0.5% creep strain, but with low-load tests repeatedly
only at higher creep strains of up to I % or more. This
was in some instances the case also at the highest loads
examined. The time elapsed to 1% creep strain amount­
ed in general to approximately one third of the time to
rupture, but steels sensitive to intergranular cracking
should be excepted.
