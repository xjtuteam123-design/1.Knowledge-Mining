1. Introduction 
The problem of energy always troubles the world. International 
Thermonuclear Experimental Reactor (ITER) is considered as the ulti -
mate solution for humanity to solve social energy and environmental 
problems. However, its harsh internal working environment makes only 
the tungsten (W) metal as the preferred material for the first wall of 
nuclear fusion. The investigation demonstrates that tungsten metal has 
excellent physical and chemical properties, such as high temperature 
strength, high melting point, high thermal conductivity, low sputtering 
yield, low thermal expansion coefficient, and low hydrogen isotope 
storage [ 1 – 3 ]. The excellent performance of tungsten makes it the 
preferred material for shielding components, deflectors, and 
plasma-facing armors in nuclear fusion devices [ 4 – 6 ]. However, tung -
sten metal also has the defects of poor radiation stability, high yield 
strength, low ductility, poor fracture toughness, low temperature brit -
tleness, and high ductile-brittle transition temperature. In this study, the 
method of alloying tungsten with different rare earth elements is 
intended to solve some of the current problems. Meanwhile, we inves -
tigate the mechanical and thermodynamic properties of tungsten alloy 
to achieve the purpose of improving the performance. Based on the 
literature investigation, doping different rare earth elements can 
improve the mechanical and thermodynamic properties of tungsten 
metal [ 7 – 22 ]. For example, doping W with different rare earth elements 
can improve its ductility, such as W – Cr, W – Ti, W – Hf, W – Zr, W – V, W – Ta, 
W – Co, W – Tc, W – Mo, and W – Re alloys [ 7 – 19 ]. Especially, doping rare 
earth element rhenium in tungsten can obviously reduce the 
ductile-brittle transition temperature [ 19 ]. Similarly, doping W with 
different elements can also improve its thermodynamic properties, such 
as W – Cr, W – Ti, W – Ni, and W – Cu alloys [ 7 , 20 – 22 ]. In addition, the W – Fe 
alloy [ 23 ] is an important connection component for connecting the first 
wall armor material to the structural steel. However, due to the influ -
ence of different thermal expansion coefficients, it is critical for the 
W – Fe alloy to become a stable joint that can withstand thermal stress. In 
other words, it is necessary to investigate the mechanical and thermo -
dynamic properties of W – Fe alloys with different Fe contents. In addi -
tion, the traditional alloy experiment design time is long and the cost is 
high. As a very mature research technique, the first principles method 
can be used to design materials and study the microstructure properties 
of materials [ 24 – 29 ]. Simulation calculations can be used to study

3. Results and discussions 
3.1. Crystal structure properties 
In this study, the structures of three W – Fe alloys with different Fe 
content are optimized. As shown in Fig. 1 , the three optimized structural 
models are all cubic crystals. Meanwhile, it is found that Fe atoms prefer 
to occupy a uniform and ordered W lattice position, indicating that W 
and Fe elements can be synthesized into atomic solid solutions. 
In order to clarify that the above mentioned phases can be stable in 
real systems, we evaluated the entropy contribution of a disordered 
system. We take the 12.5 at.% Fe concentration as our example and 
created a supercell that contains 128 atoms. The ordered configuration 
in the supercell is the same as the W
14
Fe
2
, while several other disordered 
configurations are tested. Our results shows that the total energy of the 
system in the ordered configuration ( 1562.788eV) is much lower than 
that of the disordered configurations (for example, we obtained total 
energies of 1562.756eV, 1562.667eV, 1562.676eV, 1561.699eV 
and 1561.551eV for different configurations). On the other hand, the 
entropy of the system can be evaluated with S = k

total number of the configurations. Therefore, we can estimate that the 
contribution of the entropy to the Gibbs free energy is around 1 eV under 
room temperature, which is clearly lower than that of the energy dif -
ference between the ordered and disordered configurations. Therefore, 
the disordered systems are not considered in this paper. In fact, we take 
W

dered configurations on mechanical properties, which are analyzed in 
detail in Section 3.3 . 
From Table 2 , some lattice parameters such as the lattice constant 
and cell volume are obtained after crystal structure optimization. It can 
be seen that the larger the Fe contents in the alloy, the smaller the lattice 
constant. The reason is that W has a large atomic radius, while Fe has a 
small atomic radius. In addition, the lattice constant of pure W is very 
close to its reference value [ 53 , 54 ], with only a small difference of

results are very reliable.
