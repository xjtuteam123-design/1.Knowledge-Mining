High-Temperature Mechanical Properties
of High-Purity 70 mass% Cr–Fe Alloy
M. Asahina, N. Harima, S. Takaki, and K. Abiko
Institute for Materials Research, Tohoku University, Sendai 980-8577, Japan
(Received January 23, 2001; accepted July 27, 2001)
Subject classification:62.20.Fe; S1.1; S1.2
An ingot of high-purity 70 mass% Cr–Fe alloy was prepared by high-frequency induction melting
in a high-purity argon atmosphere using a cold copper crucible. Its tensile properties such as hot-
ductility and tensile strength were measured, and compared with the results for a high-purity
50 mass% Cr–Fe alloy, a high-purity 60 mass% Cr–Fe alloy and a Ni-based super-alloy. The for-
mation of s-phase was also examined. The purity of a 70Cr–Fe alloy (70 mass% Cr–Fe alloy)
ingot is more than 99.98 mass% and the total amount of gaseous impurities (C, N, O, S, H) in the
70Cr–Fe alloy is 69.9 mass ppm. The strength of the 70Cr–Fe alloy is higher than those of the
60Cr–Fe alloy and the 50Cr–Fe alloy at the temperatures between 293 and 1573 K, without
decrease in ductility with increasing Cr content. The 70Cr–Fe alloy also possesses excellent high-
temperature ductility. The s-phase was not observed after aging of 3.6 Ms at 873 K. Consequently,
the 70Cr–Fe alloy is an excellent alloy as the base of super heat-resistant alloys.
1. Introduction
A new heat-resistant alloy is widely required for various industrial applications. For
example, the developments of jet engines for air-craft and of gas turbines for power
generation are mostly dependent on the advances in material qualities. Up to the pre-
sent, microstructure-controlled Ni-based super-alloys such as Inconel 718 or Rene 41
have been used for high-temperature service up to 1273 K. Ni-based super-alloys are
composed of various elements such as Ni, Cr, Fe, Mo, Nb, Ti and others; Ni, Cr and Fe
are used as base elements and Mo, Nb, Ti as the additive elements.
High Cr–Fe alloys are expected to be utilized as heat-resistant materials, because
they will have desirable high-temperature strength and desirable chemical properties
such as corrosion resistance and oxidation resistance. However, conventional Cr–Fe
alloys containing high chromium content have extremely low ductility [1] due to high
deformation hardening, 475
/C14C embrittlement [2] and the s-phase embrittlement [3].
Recently, Abiko and co-researchers found that the ductility of a 50Cr–Fe alloy [4–6]
and a 60Cr–Fe alloy [7] is remarkably improved by purification, and the formation of
the s-phase in the 50Cr–Fe alloy is suppressed by purification [8, 9].
In the present study, high-temperature mechanical properties and the formation of
the s-phase in a high-purity 70Cr–Fe alloy were investigated.

2.2 High-temperaturedeformationandtensileproperties
It is important to know the high-temperature mechanical properties for the develop-
ment of new heat-resistant alloys. The high-temperature mechanical properties such as
tensile strength and reduction in area at temperatures between 1273 and 1573 K were
measured by a gleeble test, which is one of tensile tests using a gleeble unit [11]. Speci-
mens 6.4 mm in diameter and 100 mm in length for the gleeble test were machined
from rods 7 mm in diameter. The specimens were annealed at 1273 K for 3.6 ks in a
vacuum of better than 6 /C210
––3 Pa, then quenched into oil at 273 K. Average grain size
of the specimens was 50 mm. The gleeble test was carried out at a strain rate 10 s ––1 on
specimens heated directly by an electric current passed through them for 50 s at various
test temperatures between 1273 and 1573 K in a vacuum of better than 2.7 /C210
––1 Pa.
A high-purity 50 mass% Cr–Fe alloy, a high-purity 60 mass% Cr–Fe alloy and a Ni-
based super-alloy type 718 were used as the reference of high-temperature heat-resis-
tant alloys.
The mechanical properties such as yield stress, tensile strength, elongation and reduc-
tion in area were measured by a tensile test at temperatures between 293 and 1073 K.
The tensile test was carried out at a strain rate of 4.2 /C210
––4 s––1 in a vacuum of better
than 6.7 /C210––4 Pa. Specimens 3 mm in diameter and 20 mm in gage length were ma-
chined from rods 7 mm in diameter. Each specimen was annealed at 1273 K for 3.6 ks in
a vacuum of better than 6 /C210
––3 Pa. Then, they were quenched into oil cooled to 273 K.
Average grain size of specimens was 50 mm. The microstructures of fractured specimens
after the gleeble test and the tensile test were observed by an optical microscope.
2.3 Formationof s-phase
A specimen of 6 mm in diameter and 40 mm in length was machined from a rod 7 mm
in diameter. It was quenched into oil cooled to 273 K after annealing at 1373 K for
3.6 ks under a vacuum of better than 6 /C210––3 Pa, and then aged at 873 K for 3.6 Ms
under a high vacuum of better than 2.7 /C210/C04 Pa in a quartz tube. The microstructures
of the specimen before and after aging were observed by an optical microscope. In parti-
cular, the surface and the grain boundaries of the specimen were carefully inspected.

4. Conclusion
The mechanical properties of the 70Cr–Fe alloy at temperatures between 293 and
1573 K and the formation of the s-phase were investigated.
1. The high-purity 70 mass% Cr–Fe alloy has excellent mechanical properties such as
high-temperature strength and hot-ductility.
2. The s-phase does not form in the high-purity 70 mass% Cr–Fe alloy even after
aging at 873 K for 3.6 Ms.
From these experimental results, it can be concluded that the high-purity 70 mass%
Cr–Fe alloy is an excellent alloy as the base of “super heat-resistant alloys”.
