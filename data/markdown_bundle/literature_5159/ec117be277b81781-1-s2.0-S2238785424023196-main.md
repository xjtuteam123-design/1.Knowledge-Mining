ABSTRACT
High entropy alloys (HEAs) exhibit high strength and excellent ductility at both room and low temperatures. The 
loss of ductility with increasing the tensile temperature above room temperature has been widely observed in 
single phase face-centered cubic structured HEAs. In this work, a N and Si doped FeCoCrNiMn-(N,Si) HEA with 
preexisting nano-sized Cr
2
N particles was fabricated by laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) and subsequent cold- 
rolling and annealing treatment. Tensile testing was conducted on the alloy at 298 K, 573 K, 773 K, 873 K, 
and 973 K, respectively. It has been shown that the FeCoCrNiMn-(N,Si) alloy with preexisting Cr
2
N phase ex -
hibits a steady ductility with increasing the tensile temperature. The intermediate temperature embrittlement is 
evaded in the FeCoCrNiMn-(N,Si) alloy via N-doping. The widely reported loss of ductility with increasing 
deformation temperature was avoided in present FeCoCrNiMn-(N,Si) HEA by shifting fast segregation of element 
Cr from GBs into preexisting Cr
2
N phase due to the high affinity between N and Cr elements, leading to the 
increases in the content and size of Cr
2
N particles after high temperature deformation.
1. Introduction
High entropy alloys (HEAs) or multiprincipal element alloys, as a 
new type of alloys developed recently, consist of five or more metallic 
elements with each amount of about 5 – 35 at.% [ 1 , 2 ] and have drawn 
wide attention due to the potential of application as structural materials 
with combined high strength, high ductility and good fracture toughness 
[ 3 – 5 ].
Although HEAs exhibit high strength and excellent ductility at room 
and low temperatures [ 6 – 8 ], however, recent studies on tensile me -
chanical properties of single phase HEAs with face-centered cubic (f.c.c) 
structure have shown a rapid decrease in ductility at the intermediate 
temperatures (673 K – 1073 K) [ 9 – 13 ]. Wu et al. [ 10 ] investigated a se -
ries of binary, ternary and quaternary alloys composed of two or more 
equiatomic elements Fe, Ni, Co, Cr and Mn. The results showed that all 
the equiatomic alloys exhibit a decrease in ductility with increasing 
temperature in the range of 77 – 673 K. Otto et al. [ 9 ] studied the tem -
perature dependent tensile properties of a quinary equiatomic CoCr -
FeMnNi HEA with three different grain sizes of 4.4 μ m, 50 μ m and 155 
μ m in the temperature range of 77 – 1073 K. The alloy with grain sizes of 
50 μ m and 155 μ m shows a ductility lost with increasing temperature in 
the range of 77 – 1073 K. While the ductility of the alloy with grain size of 
4.4 μ m decreases with increasing temperature in the range of 77 – 673 K. 
In addition, the loss of ductility with increasing temperature was also 
observed in a single phase Al
0.5
CoCrCuFeNi HEA [ 14 ].
It should be noted that although the loss of tensile ductility with 
increasing temperature has been widely observed in single phase f.c.c. 
structured HEAs, the study on the fundamental mechanism for such a 
loss is still very limited. A recently investigation indicated that the 
tensile ductility loss of equiatomic CrMnFeCoNi HEA might attribute to 
fast nanosegregation of some principal elements such as Ni, Cr and/or 
Mn into gain boundaries (GBs) [ 15 ]. This was verified by a recent 
investigation that a novel single phase CoFeNi
2
V
0.5
Mo
0.2 
HEA with f.c.c. 
structure shows an excellent combination of ductility and strength in a 
wide temperature range of 77 – 1073 K [ 16 ], and the unusual strain 
hardening ability and excellent ductility of the alloy might be due to the 
lack of fast segregation elements Cr and Mn along the GBs. According to 
these findings, we believe that a GB segregation engineering strategy 
could be used to evade ductility loss by migrating fast segregation of the 
principal elements from GBs into preexisting Cr-rich secondary phases.

Recently, laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) has been used to fabricate 
high performance HEAs [ 17 – 19 ]. Previous work has shown that doping 
interstitial N element into the FeCoCrNi HEA by (LPBF) can significantly 
enhance the room temperature mechanical properties through the for -
mation of a multi-level heterogeneous structure, including both the 
strength and ductility [ 20 ]. Unlike the easy formation of carbides when 
carbon element is added into the HEAs, the doping of nitrogen does not 
readily form nitrides in the HEAs due to its high solubility and slow 
kinetics in the formation of nitrides. However, cold deformation and 
subsequent heat treatment of N-doped HEAs can also introduce nitrides 
inside the matrix. For example, the thermo-mechanical processing led to 
the formation of Cr
2
N phase in the N-containing FeCrNiCoMn HEA, and 
the fraction of the formed Cr
2
N particles increases with the annealing 
temperature [ 21 ]. Our previous investigation also showed that 
cold-rolling plus subsequent annealing treatment can introducing uni -
formly distributed Cr
2
N particles in the FeCoCrNiMn-(N,Si) HEA fabri -
cated by LPBF [ 22 ]. The uniformly distributed nano-sized Cr
2
N particles 
significantly enhance the mechanical properties of FeCoCrNiMn-(N,Si) 
HEA at room temperature.
In this paper, to further understand the effect of N doping on the 
mechanical properties of HEAs at high temperatures, a N-doped 
FeCoCrNiMn-(N,Si) HEA with preexisting Cr
2
N phase was fabricated by 
LPBF and subsequent cold-rolling and annealing treatment. The tensile 
properties of the alloy were measured at the temperature range from 
298 K to 973 K. The corresponding microstructural evolutions of the 
alloy after tensile deformation at various temperatures were systemati -
cally investigated.
2. Experimental
The N-doped HEA with a composition of 21.9Fe ‒ 20.5Co ‒ 18.1Cr ‒ 
19.8Ni ‒ 15.5Mn ‒ 3.7N ‒ 0.5Si (at.%) was fabricated by a laser powder 
bed fusion (LPBF) equipment (FS271 M, China) under the atmosphere of 
nitrogen, and the detailed fabrication processing can be obtained in our 
previous investigation [ 22 ]. The fabricated plate-like samples were cold 
rolled from ~10 mm to ~1 mm at room temperature, with the reduction 
of 90% in thickness. Subsequently, the cold-rolled samples were sealed 
in vacuum tubes and then annealed at 800 
◦
C for 1 h, accompanied by air 
cooling to the room temperature. Flat dog-bone-shaped tensile speci -
mens with a gauge length of 24 mm and width of 4 mm were cut from 
the cold-rolled and subsequent annealed samples. Quasi-static tensile 
testing was conducted on an Instron 3369 instrument with a strain rate 
of 1 × 10
 3 
s
 1 
at 298 K, 573 K, 773 K, 873 K, and 973 K, respectively. 
The tensile specimens were held at the desired temperatures for ~10 
min before testing. Electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) was con -
ducted in an FEI Helios Nano Lab G3 UC scanning electron microscopy 
(SEM). The microstructures of the tensile deformed samples were 
analyzed by an FEI Titan G2 60 – 300 transmission electron microscopy 
(TEM) with a spherical aberration corrector equipped with X-Ray 
energy-dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) operating at 300 kV. The thin film 
samples for TEM observations were fabricated by mechanical grinding 
and then ion-thinning.

4. Discussion
For conventional metals and alloys with single phase f.c.c. structure 
(such as austenite steel and Cu – Al alloys), exceptionally high ductility 
can be observed in a wide range of temperatures [ 27 ], and softening 
phenomena are commonly observed as the deformation temperature 
increases. However, recent studies on single phase f.c.c. HEAs and MEAs 
have shown a rapid decrease in ductility at intermediate temperatures 
(normally from 673 K to 1073 K) [ 9 ]. It should be noted that the loss of 
ductility with increasing temperature has not been observed in N and Si 
doped FeCoCrNiMn alloy in present work. The tensile ductility of the 
FeCoCrNiMn-(N,Si) HEA at room temperature is approximately 40%. 
With the increasing tensile temperature to 573 K, 773 K, 873 K, and 973 
K, the ductility of the FeCoCrNiMn-(N,Si) alloy is 35%, 42%, 38% and 
80%, respectively. It can thus be concluded that the FeCoCrNiMn-(N,Si) 
alloy possesses steady tensile ductility at the temperature range from 
298 K to 873 K, and the yield strength and strain hardening rate at 573 K 
are even higher than those at room temperature. When the tensile 
temperature increases to 973 K, the alloy exhibits significantly softening 
with a ductility of about 80%.
After tensile deformation at temperature range from 573 K to 873 K, 
the grain size of FeCoCrNiMn-(N,Si) alloy remains similar (comparing 
Figs. 1 and 3 ) and the grain structure shows a fully recrystallized state. A 
slight refinement in the grain size can be observed in the sample 
deformed at 973 K. For the preexisting Cr
2
N phase, the size of some Cr
2
N 
particles in the alloy after tensile deformation at 773 K increases from 
around 50 nm to about 100 nm. At the tensile temperature of 873 K, 
more significant growth of Cr
2
N particles has occurred under the in -
fluence of thermal and mechanical coupling interactions. Both the 
content and size of the Cr

The phenomenon of significant ductility lost with temperature is 
known as intermediate temperature embrittlement (ITE). ITE has also 
been widely observed in Ni, Ni – Co and Co based superalloys, TiAl alloys 
and copper alloys [ 29 – 32 ]. The ITE in metals and alloys can be related to 
the precipitation of second phases at grain boundaries (GBs), GB shear or 
slip, segregation of impurity elements at GBs, absorption of hydrogen or 
helium and also dynamic strain aging [ 15 , 29 , 33 – 35 ]. For FeCoCrNiMn 
HEA, the ITE was reported to be owning to nanoclustering of Cr, Ni and 
Mn at GBs separately, particularly the Cr element, which reduces GB 
cohesion and promotes crack initiation along GBs [ 15 ]. In present work, 
the GBs of FeCoCrNiMn-(N,Si) HEA are very clean without any segre -
gation of principal elements (as shown in Figs. 5 – 7 ). The high affinity 
between N and Cr elements leads to the increases in the content and size 
of the preexisting Cr
2
N phase in the FeCoCrNiMn-(N,Si) alloy during 
high temperature tensile deformation, which in turn inhibits the fast 
segregation of Cr element into GBs and thus significantly improves the 
tensile ductility of the alloy and avoids ITE. Thus, the N-doping in 
FeCoCrNiMn HEAs can not only strengthen the alloy but also evade ITE 
via avoiding the GB segregation.
5. Conclusion
In this article, a FeCoCrNiMn-(N,Si) HEA with preexisting Cr
2
N 
phase was successfully fabricated by laser powder bed fusion, and sub -
sequent cold-rolling and annealing treatment. The intermediate tem -
perature embrittlement is evaded in the FeCoCrNiMn-(N,Si) alloy via N- 
doping. The FeCoCrNiMn-(N,Si) HEA with preexisting Cr
2
N phase ex -
hibits a steady ductility of ~35% in the tensile testing temperatures from 
298 K to 873 K. The widely reported loss of ductility with increasing the 
deformation temperature was avoided by shifting fast segregation of 
element Cr from GBs into preexisting Cr
2
N phase due to the high affinity 
between N and Cr elements, leading to the increases in the content and 
size of Cr
2
N particles after high temperature deformation.
