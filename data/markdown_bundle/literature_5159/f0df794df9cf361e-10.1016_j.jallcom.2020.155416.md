abstract
In the present study, the effect of Co addition on the microstructural features, martensitic transformation
beahviors and mechanical properties of Ti eVeAl alloys was investigated systematically. The results
revealed that phase constituents varied with Co content increasing, which can be summarized as follows:
sole a" martensite phase / coexistence of a" martensite phase and b austenite phase / fully b austenite
phase. Correspondingly, the reverse martensitic transformation temperature continuously decreased
with Co content increasing. Moreover, the moderate Co addition can improve yield strength and elon-
gation of Ti eVeAl alloys as a consequence of the solid solution strengthening effect. The maximum yield
stress of 81 1 MPa and superior elongation of 23.8% can be obtained in Ti eVeAleCo
2.0 and TieVeAleCo1.0
alloy, respectively. Besides, Co addition led to the signi ﬁcant improvement of the higher temperature
yield strength of quaternary Ti eVeAleCo alloys, compared with the Ti eVeAl alloy without Co addition.

1. Introduction
Recently, the high temperature shape memory alloys (HTSMAs)
have been paid more attentions due to their potential application in
the aerospace industry, automotive ﬁeld and so on [ 1e3]. Up to
date, the primary high temperature shape memory alloys are
composed of TieNi-(Hf, Zr, Au, Pt and Pd) HTSMAs, TiePt and TiePd
based HTSMAs, Zr eCu based HTSMAs, Ni eMn based HTSMAs, Ti-
based HTSMAs etc [ 4e13]. In comparison, Ti-based HTSMAs pro-
cess various kinds of advantages such as: the lower density and
superior machining properties [ 14]. For instance, Ti eVeAl alloys
not only have the lower density of 4.5 g/cm 3 and excellent pro-
cessing properties, but also show the perfect shape memory effect,
in the numerous Ti-based HTSMAs including Ti eCr, TieMo, TieNb
and Ti eTa HTSMAs ect [ 14e19]. In addition, Yang et al. have opti-
mized the chemical composition of Ti eVeAl alloys showing the
excellent shape memory effect. The results reveal that the optimal
Tie13 V e3Al alloy shows the high martensite transformation
temperature (Ap ¼ 389 /C14 C) and superior shape memory effect

2. Experiment
(Tie13 V e3Al)100-x-Cox (x ¼ 0, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0 at.%) alloys were
prepared using high purity titanium (99.99 wt%), vanadium
(99.995 wt%), aluminium (99.999 wt%) and cobalt (99.95 wt%) by
non-consumable vacuum arc melting under a higher-pure Ar at-
mosphere. Each ingot was re-melted ﬁve times to ensure the uni-
formity of chemical composition. The ingots were cold rolled with a
thickness reduction of 70%. The samples for the following charac-
terization were prepared by using wire cut electrical discharge
machining. The specimens inside sealed vacuum quarts tubes were
solution treated at 900 /C14 C for 2 h and then quenched into the ice
water. The phase structure of Ti eVeAleCo alloys with different
contents of Co was identi ﬁed by adopting x-ray diffraction (XRD,
Panalytical X ’PERT) with Cu K a radiation. The microstructure was
operated on an OLYMPUSPM optical microscope (OM). The samples
for OM characterization were mechanically polished and etched in
a solution containing 5%HF þ 5%HNO3 þ 90%H2O (volume fraction).
The phase transformation characteristics of Ti eVeAleCo shape
memory alloys were determined using a differential scanning
calorimetry (PE, Diamond DSC) with a cooling/heating rate of
10 0/C14 C/min. The tensile testing was carried out on an Instron-5569
tensile test machine with a strain rate of 0.2 mm/min at the room
temperature and higher temperatures. The method of character-
izing strain recovery features had been reported in our reported
literature [ 14,15]. Tensile specimens had a thickness of 1.5 mm, a
width of 2 mm and a gage length of 20 mm. Moreover, the tensile
specimens were ground and polished before tensile tests. The
fracture morphology of the Ti eVeAleCo alloys was characterized
by scanning electron microscope (SEM, FEI Quanta200FEG).
3. Results and discussions
Fig. 1 displays the room temperature XRD patterns of
TieVeAleCo shape memory alloys with various Co contents. It is
apparent that phase constituents of the solution treated
TieVeAleCo alloys are largely dependent on Co contents. Accord-
ing to the reported crystal structure of Ti eVeAl alloys [14,21], only
the diffraction peaks corresponding to
a" martensite phase with an
orthorhombic structure can be detected in Ti eVeAl shape memory
alloy without Co addition. The corresponding diffraction peaks can
be indexed as (1 10), (002), (1 1 1), (1 12), (022) and (1 12) of a"
martensite phase. In addition, the calculated lattice parameters of
a" martensite phase are as follows: a ¼ 0.31 1 nm; b ¼ 0.475 nm;
c ¼ 0.463 nm. As 0.5 at.%Co is added into Ti eVeAl alloy, the major
diffraction peaks corresponding to a" martensite phase vanish.
Merely (1 1 1) plane and (122) plane of a" martensite phase are
observed and their intensity becomes weaker. Besides, (1 10) and
(21 1) of body-centered cubicb phase also can be detected. Similarly,
both the a" martensite phase and b phase coexist in the Ti eVeAl
alloy added with 1.0 at.% Co addition. The difference is that the
intensity of (1 1 1) diffraction peak of a" martensite becomes much
weaker. With further increasing Co content up to 2.0 at.%,
TieVeAleCo alloy is completely changed into b phase. That is, the
evolution of phase constituents with the increasing of Co content is
as follows: a" martensite / a" martensite þ b phase / b phase.
Fig. 2 shows the optical images of the solution treated
TieVeAleCo alloys with the different Co contents. It can be seen
that the microstructure features of Ti eVeAleCo alloys changes
with the increasing of Co content. As Co content is 0 at.%, the
microstructure of Ti eVeAl alloy is characterized by the multiple
grains with the size of hundreds of microns. Moreover, the interior
of grain is featured with the single orientation a" martensite lath or
intersecting a" marteniste lath, as shown in Fig. 2 (a). The similar
microstructure also can be observed in the Ti eNbeZreSn alloys
[26]. With the Co content increasing, the amount of lath-like a"
marteniste becomes less and less. On the contrary, the number of
featureless grain containing b phase is becoming more and more.

When 2.0 at.%Co is added, the grain of Ti eVeAleCo alloy is
completely turned into the featureless b phase, as displayed in

Fig. 3 exhibits the DSC curves of Ti eVeAleCo alloys added with
the various Co contents. Obviously, only the reverse transformation
peak corresponding to a" marteniste /b phase transformation
during heating is observed in Ti eVeAleCo alloys, regardless of Co
content. No signi ﬁcant transformation peak is detected during
cooling for Ti eVeAleCo alloys, irrespective of Co content. The
similar phenomenon is also reported in Ti eNb based shape mem-
ory alloys, TieZr based shape memory alloy, and so on [27e29]. The
absence of exothermic peak in the present Ti-based shape memory
alloys may be closely related to the lower enthalpy of the forward
martensite transformation and the partial transformation of the b
phase to a00 martensite phase [ 29]. In addition, the endothermic
peak shifts towards to the lower temperature with Co content
increasing. This indicates that the reverse martensitic temperature
of TieVeAleCo alloys is reduced owing to the addition of Co. As Co
content is increased from 0 at.% to 2.0 at.%, the reverse martensite
transformation temperature (Ap) slightly decreases from 385.5 /C14 C
to 329.8 /C14 C,as shown in Fig. 4 . Similarly, the martensitic trans-
formation temperatures of Ti eVeAleCu alloys and Ti eVeAleFe
alloys are also reduced owing to the addition of quaternary Cu and
Fe element [21,30]. The difference is that Fe or Cu addition can lead
to a signi ﬁcant decrease of the reverse martensitic transformation
temperatures of Ti eVeAl alloys.
Fig. 5 shows effect of Co content on the room temperature
tensile stress-strain curves of Ti eVeAleCo shape memory alloys.
When the Co content varies, the deformation mechanism of
TieVeAleCo alloys also changes. It can be observed from the fea-
tures of stress-strain curves and the phase constituents of Ti eVeAl
alloys that the deformation mechanism of Ti eVeAl alloy without
Co addition consists of elastic deformation of martensite phase and
the continuous work hardening. With Co content increasing, the
deformation stage of Ti eVeAleCo alloys with the minor Co addi-
tion can be divided into three stages: elastic deformation of a"
martensite and b austenite, reorientation of martensite and the
stress-induced martensite transformation as well as the ultimate
plastic deformation. In addition, the minor Co addition results in
the extension of the elongation. However, the yield strength is
slightly reduced as a consequence of Co addition. With Co content
further increasing, the deformation stage of TieVeAleCo alloy with
2.0 at.%Co addition evolves into the elastic deformation of
b
austenite phase and the followed plastic deformation of austenite.
The distinction of their deformation mechanism can be attributed

to their difference in phase constituents of Ti eVeAleCo alloys with
various Co content. The higher content of Co leads to the
enhancement of yield strength and the reduction of elongation. The
maximum yield strength of approximate 81 1 MPa and the largest
elongation of 23.8% can be obtained in Ti eVeAleCo alloys by

improve the mechanical properties of Ti eVeAl alloy [ 21,30].
Fig. 6 represents the fracture morphologies of Ti eVeAleCo al-
loys with the different Co content. It is obvious that a great number
of dimples can be clearly observed in Ti eVeAleCo alloys, regard-
less of Co content. But the size of dimples in the Ti eVeAleCo
containing Co addition are slightly smaller. In addition, lots of
tearing ridges can be found, as marked by arrows, which may be the
grain boundary before tensile test. This means that the
TieVeAleCo alloys fracture mainly along the grain boundary due to
the lower-strength of the grain boundary. Besides, compared to
TieVeAl alloy, Co addition leads to the appearance of multiple
smaller dimples in the interior of larger dimple, which can be
attributed to the enhancement of matrix strength owing to the
solution strengthening.
Fig. 7 shows the strain recovery characteristics of Ti eVeAleCo
alloys with various Co contents at the pre-deformation strain of 4%.
It can be seen that the Ti eVeAleCo alloys can not completely re-
covery after 4% pre-deformation. The shape memory effect strain
and recoverable strain are 2.31% and 3.61% for the solution treated
and cold rolled Ti eVeAl alloy without doping Co, respectively.
Minor Co addition results in the slightly decrease of shape memory
effect strain and recoverable strain, which can be ascribed to the
decrease of amount of a" marteniste phase due to Co addition at
room temperature. The shape memory effect strain of Ti eVeAleCo
alloys with 0.5 at.%Co and 1.0 at.%Co is 2.25% and 1.95%, respec-
tively. However, no shape recovery corresponding to shape mem-
ory effect strain after heating is observed in Ti eVeAleCo alloy. As
Co content is 2.0 at.%, Ti eVeAleCo alloy fully changes into phase. It
can be concluded that forward martensitic transformation is far
lower than room temperature so that the alloy just undergoes
plastic deformation of b phase at room temperature. That is why
there is no shape recovery found in Ti eVeAleCo alloy doping
2.0 at.%Co.
Fig. 8 displays the high temperature tensile stress-strain curves
of TieVeAleCo alloys with the different Co contents. At the testing
temperature of 200 /C14 C, the elongation of Ti eVeAleCo alloys de-
teriorates as a result of Co addition, compared to the Ti eVeAl alloy
without Co addition. The elongation of Ti eVeAl and
TieVeAleCo2.0 alloys is approximate 25% and 10%. However, the
yield strength of Ti eVeAleCo alloy with minor Co addition almost
equals to the yield strength of Ti eVeAl alloy. The higher content of
Co can achieve a signi ﬁcant improvement in the high temperature
yield strength of Ti eVeAl alloys. Similarly, at the testing temper-
ature of 250 /C14 C and 300 /C14 C, the minor Co addition don’t improve the
yield strength of Ti eVeAl alloy. Inversely, the elongation of
TieVeAl alloys becomes worse, as the minor Co is added. Although
the elongation of Ti eVeAleCo alloys containing 1.0 at.%Co and
2.0 at.%Co becomes worse, the high temperature yield strength is
remarkably improved by adding the higher content of Co alloying
element. Moreover, the yield strength of Ti eVeAleCo alloys with
the adjustable Co content is higher than that of the reported Ti ePt
and Ti eNiePd based shape memory alloy [ 6,7,31,32]. In addition,
the improvement of yield strength is an effective approach to
obtain a better shape memory effect for the shape memory alloys
[31]. The increase in the yield strength can be attributed to the solid
solution strengthening due to Co addition. Herein, the atomic
radius of Co (0.167 nm) is slightly larger than that of Ti (0.132 nm),
which should be responsible for solid solution strengthening. By
solution strengthening effect, Co added Ti eVeAl alloys show the
lower fracture strain at room temperature and higher temperature.
When the Co content is ranging from 0 at.% to 1.0 at.%, the yield
strength is almost equal or slightly lower with the testing tem-
perature increasing. The similar phenomenon is also observed in
TieNiePdeCu alloys [ 31]. However, the yield strength of
TieVeAleCo alloy containing 2.0 at.%Co increases continuously
with the increased of testing temperature.

4. Conclusions
In the present study, the microstructure features, martensite
transformation behaviors and mechanical properties of quaternary
TieVeAleCo alloys were investigated. The results showed that the
amount of plate-like a" martensite gradually decreased and the
featureless b austenite became more and more with the Co content
increasing. Accordingly, the reverse martensite transformation
temperature of a"/b was also reduced owing to Co addition. The
ductility of Ti eVeAleCo alloys can be improved by adding mod-
erate Co content. In addition, the higher strength can be achieved in
the Ti eVeAleCo alloys with the tailored Co content as a conse-
quence of solid solution strengthening effect.
