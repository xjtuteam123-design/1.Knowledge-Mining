HIGH ELEVATED-TEMPERATURE STRENGTH OF Al-BASED
NANOQUASICRYSTALLINE ALLOYS
Akihisa Inoue and Hisamichi Kimura
Institute for Materials Research, Tohoku University, Sendai 980-8577, Japan
(Received January 22, 1999)
(Accepted February 18, 1999)
Abstract—A new icosahedral (I) quasicrystalline alloy was found around the Al-rich composition of Al84.2Fe7.0Cr6.3Ti2.5
by melt spinning. The I-phase consists of a spherical shape with a particle size of about 250 nm and contains a high density
of internal defects. The I-phase is a metastable phase and decomposes to Al1 Al13Cr2 1 Al23Ti9 1 Al13Fe4 phases in the
temperature range of 620 to 1005 K by continuous heating at a heating rate of 0.67 K/s. The use of further Al-rich
Al-Fe-Cr-Ti alloys enabled the production of metastable phase powders consisting mainly of Al 1 I phases in a wide
particle size fraction of 26 to 125
mm by high-pressure Ar gas atomization. The bulk Al93Fe3Cr2Ti2 alloys prepared by
extrusion of the 26mm powder at 673 K consist of Al, I and Al23Ti9 phases and the I-phase keeps a particle size of about
300 nm. The extruded bulk I-base alloy exhibits high elevated-temperature strength of 510 MPa at 473 K and 360 MPa at
573 K as well as high room-temperature strength of 650 MPa. The elevated-temperature strength exceeds the air-force goal
level (400 MPa at 473 K and 350 MPa at 573 K) for Al-based alloys. The high elevated-temperature strength and wear
resistance remain almost unchanged even after annealing at 573 K for 1000 h. The extremely good elevated temperature
strength is thought to result from high elevated-temperature strength of the Al-based I-phase itself containing Fe, Cr, and
Ti elements which have low diffusivities in Al phase. The success of developing the new type of high elevated temperature
strength Al-based alloys containing the I-phase as a main constituent phase by the use of the stoichiometric Al-Fe-Cr-Ti
icosahedral alloy is promising for the future development as a new high elevated-temperature strength material.©1999 Acta
Metallurgica Inc.
I. Introduction
It is generally known (1) that stoichiometric icosahedral quasicrystalline alloys can contain dislocations,
but the movement of dislocations in the quasiperiodic structure without periodicity is very difficult at
room temperature because it leads to the destruction of quasiperiodic lattice. Consequently, the
icosahedral alloys have been reported to exhibit high hardness (2), high Young’s modulus (3) and low
coefficient of thermal expansion (3) at room temperature and elevated temperatures below about 900
K. Simultaneously, the quasicrystalline alloys have a very brittle nature, and no appreciable plastic
elongation is recognized at room temperature and elevated temperatures (1). However, more recently,
Al-based alloys containing an icosahedral phase as a main constituent phase have been reported (4) to
exhibit high tensile strength and large plastic elongation by homogeneous dispersion of nanoscale
icosahedral particles embedded in a grain boundary-free Al matrix. It has further been clarified (5) that
the icosahedral particles include a high density of linear phason defects and approximate crystalline
phase fields resulting from the deviation of alloy components to Al-rich side and quenching-induced
strains. Consequently, the achievement of the high-strength and high-ductility icosahedral base alloys
has been thought (6,7) to result from the combination of the following three factors, i.e., (1) homoge-
neous dispersion of nanoscale icosahedral particles with diameters less than 100 nm, (2) the introduc-
tion of a high density of defects into the icosahedral phase leading to the short-range atomic
configuration similar to an amorphous structure, (3) the deviation of the composition of the nanoscale
icosahedral phase into Al-rich side, and (4) the formation of Al matrix without grain boundary. For

II. Experimental Procedure
Quaternary alloys in Al-Fe-Cr-Ti system was examined in the present study. The ingots of P/M (powder
metallurgy) alloys were prepared by induction melting Al-4.82 mass %Ti, Al-4.89 mass %Cr, Al-51.09
mass %Fe and pure Cr metal in an argon atmosphere. The ingots of melt-spun alloys were prepared by
arc melting pure Al, Fe, Cr and Ti metals in an argon atmosphere. From the alloy ingots, rapidly
solidified alloy ribbons with a cross-section of 0.02 3 1m m
2 were produced by the melt spinning
technique. Rapidly solidified powders were produced by high pressure gas atomization and then sieved
into different particle size fractions. By using the conventional powder metallurgy technique, bulk
cylindrical alloys with a diameter of 8 mm and a length of 300 mm were produced by extrusion of the
atomized powders at an extrusion ratio of 10 and an extrusion temperature of 673 K. The as-quenched
and extruded structures were examined by X-ray diffraction and optical and transmission electron
microscopy. Thermal stability was examined by differential scanning calorimetry at a heating rate of
0.67 K/s. Tensile mechanical properties were measured at a strain rate of 4.0 3 10
24 s21 in the
temperature range of room temperature to 673 K with an Instron testing machine. Vickers hardness was
also measured in the temperature range of room temperature to 773 K with a Vickers microhardness
tester under a load of 0.98 N for the ribbon sample and 9.8 N for the extruded bulk alloy. Wear rate
against a case hardening steel S50C (H
v 5 700) was measured in the sliding velocity range of 0.5 to
2 m/s with a wear testing machine.

IV. Discussion
4.1. New Icosahedral Al-Fe-Cr-Ti Alloy
We discuss the formation of a new icosahedral phase with the stoichiometric composition of
Al84.2Fe7.0Cr6.3Ti2.5. It is known that the icosahedral phase in Al-TM (TM5 IV to VIII group transition
metals) system is formed in Al-Mn, Al-Cr, Al-V, Al-Pd-Mn, Al-Cu-Fe and Al-Pd-Re systems (16).
However, no Al-based icosahedral phase containing Fe as the second constituent element has been
found, in spite of the importance of engineering the Al-Fe base system. Although a decagonal phase
is formed in a coexistent state with crystalline phases in melt-spun Al
8 0–8 5Fe15–20 binary alloys
(17), there have been no data on the formation of an icosahedral phase, even in the as-quenched
state. Furthermore, neither icosahedral nor decagonal phase is observed in melt-spun Al-Ti base

4.2. Achievement of High Elevated-Temperature Strength
It was shown that the volume fraction of the icosahedral phase in the Al-Fe-Cr-Ti alloys is about 45%,
which is nearly the same as those (60 to 70%) for the previously reported Al 92Mn6Ce2 (4) and
Al94.5Cr3Ce1Co1.5 (5) alloys. The smaller volume fractions indicate that the volume fraction of the
icosahedral phase is not a dominant factor for the increase in the high elevated-temperature strength.
The simultaneous dissolution of Fe, Cr and Ti elements into the icosahedral and Al phases suppresses
the grain growth of the two phases in the Al-Fe-Cr-Ti alloys subjected to the long-time annealing
treatment. Furthermore, the X-ray diffraction patterns of the extruded bulk Al-Fe-Cr-Ti alloys indicate
the precipitation of fine Al
23Ti9 compound which can act as an effective resistant phase against the
grain growth of the icosahedral phase as well as the deformation of the Al matrix. The effectiveness of
the Al
23Ti9 phase is also supported from the result that the compound particles have a small size of 10
to 60 nm and disperse homogeneously in the Al matrix. That is, the remarkable improvement of
elevated-temperature strength is attributable to the combination of the suppression of coarsening of the
icosahedral particles and the difficulty of the plastic deformation of the Al phase.
V. Summary
With the aim of developing an icosahedral base Al alloy with high elevated temperature strength, we
examined the possibility of forming an icosahedral single phase in Al-rich Al-Fe-Cr-Ti alloys contain-
ing Fe, Cr and Ti elements with low atomic diffusivity in Al phase. The results obtained are summarized
as follows.
