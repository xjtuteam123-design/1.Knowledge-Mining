Abstract
The effect of alloy composition on microstructure and mechanical properties of Nb /C1/Zr /C1/C ternary alloys were in vestigated.
Alloys were prepared arc melting and homogenized. The microstructure of the alloys were characterized by optical microscopy, X-
ray diffraction and electron probe microanalysis. Compression tests were conducted from room temperature to 1773 K to determine
the stress /C1
/strain curves and 0.2% flow stress. Fracture toughness of the alloys was determined using a single-edge-notched specimen
under a three-point bending test. The results indicate that the 0.2% flow stress of the Nb /C1/ZrC alloys increases with increasing ZrC
content from room to elevated temperature. However, the compressive ductility and fracture toughness decrease with increasing ZrC
content. A proper alloy design to obtain balance of high temperature strength and room temperature ductility is also discussed.

1. Introduction
Niobium-based alloys are one of the most promising
refractory metals for use at ultra-high temperatures,
because niobium metal has a high melting point of 2741
K and good ductility. Howe ver, the strength of Nb
decreases substantially at temperature above 1200 K. As
a result, various approaches ha ve been used to impro ve
the strength of Nb at ele vated temperature. Solid
solution strengthening of Nb-based alloys with addition
of some refractory elements such as V, Ta, Mo or W has
been reported [1,2]. The results showed that Nb/C1
/Mo /C1/W
alloys have excellent strength even at temperature above
1773 K. However, those alloys suffer from poor ductility
at room temperature. To impro ve both high tempera-
ture strength and room temperature ductility of Nb-
based alloys, se veral two-phase alloys containing solid
solution and intermetallics, for example Nb/Nb3Al [3,4],
Nb/Nb3Ir [5,6] and Nb/Nb 5Si3 [7 /C1/9] were in vestigated.
Recently, the effect of carbide addition on the mechan-
ical properties of Nb /C1/Mo /C1/W ternary alloys was also
investigated [10]. It was found that, with small amount
of carbide addition to the Nb /C1/Mo /C1/W ternary alloys,
both the 0.2% yield stress and the ductility increased
with increasing C or HfC contents. This was attributed
to the presence of carbides, which increased the grain
boundary strength and led to a change of fracture mode
from inter- to transgranular, thus impro ving the ducti-
lity of the Nb /C1
/Mo /C1/W alloys. More recently, Nomura et
al. [11] also in vestigated the mechanical properties and
creep beha vior of Nb /C1/Ti/C1/C ternary alloys containing
40 at.% TiC. The results showed that Nb /C1/40at.% TiC
alloy exhibited a very high strength at high temperature.
Similar results ha ve been reported for the Mo /C1/40at.%
ZrC alloy [12]. Howe ver, the mechanical beha vior of

2. Experimental procedure
Nominal compositions of the alloys employed in this
study were marked in the Fig. 1. The alloy compositions
were classified into two types, one is on the line
connecting Nb and ZrC stoichiometry (herby this group
of alloys were designated as stoichiometric Nb /C1
/ZrC
alloys), and the other one is in the region where Zr and
C atoms ratio is larger or smaller than 1 (herby this
group of alloys were designated as called non-stoichio-
metric Nb /C1
/ZrC alloys). High purity Nb (99.9% pure),
ZrC (95% pure) and NbC (99% pure) powders were used
as the starting materials. NbC is used only for the non-
stoichiometric alloy to control the C content. All alloys,
each about 25 g, were prepared by arc-melting the
powder mixture under argon. The mass loss before and
after melting was less than 0.1 mass%. The ingots were
homogenized at 2073 K for 24 h in an argon atmo-
sphere, followed by furnace cooling. Microstructure and
compositions of the alloys were examined by optical
microscopy and X-ray diffractometry (XRD), and
electron probe microanalysis (EPMA). The specimens
were made by electric-discharge machining (EDM) of
each annealed ingots to 3 /C29
/3 /C29/6m m 3. Compression
tests were conducted in air at room temperature and in
an argon atmosphere at high temperatures. Initial strain
rate was chosen to be 1.4 /C29
/10/C284 s/C281, and the compres-
sive deformation was up to 20% or till specimen
fractured.
The fracture toughness was determined by a three-
point bending test using the single-edge-notched beam
(SENB) specimens. The dimension of a specimen is 3 /C29
/
6 /C29/40 mm3. The notch was cut using a thin EDM wire
(f 100 mm) to about 1.5 mm in depth (a/w :/0.5). The
crosshead speed is the same as all the compression tests
and the span length is 24 mm. All fracture toughness
tests were performed at room temperature in air.
Fracture surfaces of the specimens were examined in a
scanning electron microscope.

3.1. Microstructure and phase identiﬁcation
Fig. 2 shows the optical micrographs of the stoichio-

Mixed microstructure of solid solution and carbide was
observed in the Nb /C1

observed in the Nb /C1/40at.% ZrC, Nb /C1/50at.% ZrC and
Nb /C1/60at.% ZrC alloys.
Fig. 3 shows the X-ray diffraction patterns of the
annealed stoichiometric Nb /C1/ZrC alloys. The results

confirmed to be (Zr, Nb)C through a compositional

which solidifies from metal liquid first, and Nb ss which
is located between dendritical carbides. In this case, the
carbide is the continuous matrix phase for the alloy.

behavior of Nb /C1/Zr /C1/C ternary system is controlled by
the alloy compositions. Fig. 12 shows the plot of the

5. Conclusions
On the basis of the abo ve systematic in vestigation of
Nb /C1/Zr /C1/C ternary alloys, the following conclusions
were obtained.
(1) In this study, microstructure of the alloy after
annealing consists of Nb solid solution and MC type
carbide, the carbide is (Zr, Nb)C. The microstructure of
the stoichiometric Nb /C1
/ZrC alloys can be classified into
two types: in the case of ZrC concentration is less than
30 at.%, the microstructure consists of mixed Nb solid
solution as the matrix phase and (Zr, Nb)C; in the case
of ZrC concentration is higher than 40 at.%, the
microstructure consists of carbide dendrite as the matrix
phase and Nb solid solution.
(2) The 0.2% flow stress of the stoichiometric Nb /C1
/
ZrC alloys increases with increasing ZrC concentration
from room temperature to 1773 K. Both fracture
toughness and compressi ve ductility decrease with
increasing ZrC concentration at room temperature.
Also, the 0.2% flow stress of all the alloys decreases
with increasing temperatures.
(3) The fracture toughness of the stoichiometric Nb /C1
/
ZrC alloys at room temperature decreases with increas-
ing ZrC concentration.
(4) Alloying the Nb with Mo appears to be a
promising approach to further increase the yield
strength of the Nb
ss.
