METHOD OF ANALOGY FOR ESTIMATING THE 
HIGH-TEMPERATURE LONG-TERM STRENGTH 
PROPERTIES OF METAL ALLOYS 
V. V. Krivenyuk UDC 539.4 
Analysis of physieochemical and mechanical properties, and also the structural features of different 
classes of heat-resistant and refractory metals points to the desirability of considering them with rapid esti- 
mation of service properties in the form of a strictly hereditary system. This system with prolonged force and 
thermal loading undergoes a series of successive changes, the result of which depends on loading history. One 
of the most intrinsic features for these changes is governed by the following hypothesis of equal significance: 
the condition and properties of a system at moment of time t under given external action conditions determine 
with equal significance the condition and properties of the system at moment of time interval t + At. 
The quite specific and rigid sequence of structural transformations and changes during prolonged high- 
temperature loading for many alloys is mainly accentuated by general principles for their production. From 
a theoretical point of view, the optimum condition is the existence of a solid solution at the solubility limit and 
precipitation during service at high temperature of finely dispersed intermetallics or introduced phases (car- 
bides, nitrides, and borides) which can block slip planes during deformation [1]. 
During service strengthening, structural elements are subjected to the same type of changes as a result 
of which there is a corresponding reduction in creep and delayed fracture. Such changes relate to the transfer 
of alloying elements from solid solution into carbides, carbide spheroidization and coagulation, concentration 
of carbides at grain boundaries, and changes in fine structure. It is important to note that the initial condition 
mainly governs subsequent material operation. For example, it was noted in [2] that structural differences 
created in one alloy by approximate heat treatment are not smoothed out during prolonged service, i.e., the 
nature of the changes observed mainly depends on initial steel structure. This is suggested, in particular, by 
the hypothesis of equal significance. Thorough verification of its validity is currently difficult. This is ex- 
plained by the fact that in rapid estimation of long-term service properties for structural materials, insufficient 
attention has been devoted to a reliable evolution of initial material condition. This is indicated by extensive 
use of preferentially paired correlation relationships between the properties sought, e.g., long-term strength 
a t on the one hand, and one of the initial properties such as ultimate strength crf, yield point ~y, or hardness 
HB on the other. Of course, it is difficult to work out whether one of these properties can reliably determine 
the initial condition of the test material. Therefore, in this case we consider the method of analogy for evalua- 
ting long-term strength properties [3, 4] as one of the possible examples for practical use of the equal signifi- 
cance hypothesis. 
According to this method, long-term strength characteristics for two metals with an identical type of 
crystal lattice at the same values of relative (homologous) temperature, ultimate strength ~f, relative elonga- 
tion 6 and reduction in area r after failure, and also impact strength a are approximately the same in absolute 
values. As a result of this, prediction of properties is accomplished in the following way. 
Short-term mechanical properties are established for a given material. Then, for example, an analog is 
sought in the reference literature, i.e., a material with an identical type of crystal lattice having at a com- 
parable temperature the same values of short-term mechanical properties, ~f, 6, r and a, and a value of a t 
established by experiment which will be sought for the test material.

The problem of estimating long-term properties from the short-term characteristics is governed mainly 
by the complexity of establishing a reliable multiple correlation between the set of initial properties and those 
sought. Generally, only mechanical properties are used as initial data. In this case we draw attention to the 
following example. In monitoring the mechanical properties of a steel 12KhlMF pipe operated at T = 570~ 
for 19,000 h, it was established that ~f = 24.5-25.7 kgf/mm 2, 5 = 26-35%, ,~ = 82-84.9~/o, a = 22-37.5 kgm/cm 2, 
With such marked scatter of properties 5 and a it is not possible to count on the correlation being considered. 
It is necessary to define typical values of 6 and a. This definition can apparently be effective with proper con- 
sideration of metal structure. Known methods for evaluating long-term strength properties do not generally 
consider structural data. For example, consideration of the structure is possible using the hypothesis of equal 
significance on the basis of analogy. With the existence of an appropriate analog, there is an additional pos- 
sibility of identification according to structure, and proof of alloy equipotentiality according to structure. Ac- 
cording to [14], an ideal version of equipotentiality is an identical volume content of strengthening phase, 
matching particle size and shape, and similar location of particles in the matrix. It is entirely natural that 
this equipotentiality should be substantiated on the basis of direct data, i.e., data about structure, and noton 
the basis of indirect data which relates to mechanical properties.
