Development of Protective Coating of MoSi2 over TZM Alloy 
Substrate by Slurry Coating Technique 
 
S.P.Chakraborty* 
Fusion Reactor Materials Section, Materials Group 
BARC, Trombay, Mumbai-400085, India 
 
Abstract 
TZM alloy possesses high strength at elevated temperature but due to high volatility of its oxide, MoO 3 the alloy undergoes 
catastrophic oxidation in air a bove 700 0C. In the present study, development of high temperature oxidation resistant MoSi 2 
coating over TZM alloy was investigated by slurry coating technique. As-coated TZM alloy by Si powder was exposed to 
thermal treatment at 1200 0C to develop MoSi 2 coating. Characterization studies like surface, cross section mor phologies, phase 
identification, evaluation of hardness, adhesive strength, oxidation resistance etc. were carried out to determine the quality of 
coatings. A double layered coating structure was obtained with thick outer surface layer of MoSi 2 (thickness ~ 60 µm) and thin 
interfacial layer of Mo 5Si3 (thickness ~ 5 µm). The coating showed good adhesion strength (~ 25 MPa) and stable oxidation 
resistance with negligible mass gain at 10000C in air.

1. Introduction 
High temperature structural materials are of great demand in the areas of aerospace, energy sector and chemical 
industries for their capabilities of working beyond 1000 0C where conventional super alloys fail. In this regard, TZM 
alloy of nominal composition, Mo-0.5Ti-0.1Zr-0.02 C (wt. %) is a promising candidate for high temperature 
applications in view of its high corrosion resistance, excellent mechanical properties and physical stability over a 
wide range of temperature up to 1500
0C [1-2]. However, unprotected TZM alloy is susceptible to high rate of 
oxidation in either air or oxidizing atmosphere when heated above 700 0C owing to the formation of a highly volatile 
species of MoO 3. This results in drastic deterioration of the mechanical properties of this alloy leading to structural 
failure. The detailed study as reported in the literature shows that the oxidation behavior of TZM alloy follows 
parabolic rate between 250-450
0C, linear rate above 400 0C, heavy mass loss above 650 0C via rapid volatilization of 
MoO3 [3]. However, despite being a high temperature alloy, TZM alloy finds limited applications as furnace liners, 
crucible, dies for hot forging super alloys etc. due to use of controlled atmospheres of either vacuum or argon. 
Therefore, development of oxidation resistant protective co atings are mandatory over TZM alloy shapes prior to use 
to widen its spectrum of applications identified in the ar eas of advanced thermonuclear reactors, heat exchangers 
and turbine blades of power industries either in controlle d environment or air atmosphere [4]. Slurry technique is 
simple, inexpensive, offers easy control of coating thic kness with good processibility and compositional flexibility. 
The as-coated material can easily infiltrate into the pores of the substrate forming metallurgically bonded coating at 
the interface after heat treatment which becomes the integral part of the substrate [5]. Molybdenum di-silicide 
(MoSi
2) is the most dependable coating material for refractory metals and alloys because it has an outstanding 
oxidation resistance up to a temperature of 1700°C due to the formation of a self-healing, glassy silica (SiO 2) layer 
that protects the substrate from oxidation and corrosion at elevated temperature [6]. In the present paper, the 
feasibility of developing Si rich MoSi 2 coating over TZM substrate was investigated by slurry coating technique. 
The as-coated samples were subjected to thermal treatment to develop MoSi 2 coating by allowing diffusion of Si 
from as–coated Si layer to chemically interact with Mo present in the TZM substrate. The coated structures were 
subjected to studies related to micro-structural and morphological characterizatio n of coating surfaces, cross 
sectional areas, phase identification, hardness measurement, adhesion test, oxidation performance etc. [7]. 
2. Experimental 
 
2.1. Preparation of substrate specimens 
 
TZM specimens of dimension, 50 mm dia and 2 mm thickness were first cut from as-received rolled sheet of TZM 
alloy in the form of circular disks. The disk specimens were then subjected to recrys tallization annealing treatment 
at 1200
0C for 2 hrs under argon for grain refinement and to relieve thermal stresses developed during hot rolling. 
Prior to coating, the edges of the specimens were ro unded off by grinding to remove corners to avoid any 
overgrowth of coatings in these areas. The surfaces were further polished up to 600 grit SiC paper to remove any 
oxide layers formed on the specimen surfaces. These specimens were then finally used as substrates for MoSi
2 
coating. 
 
2.2. Procedure 
 
High pure fine Si powder of -325 mesh size was used as the base material for coatings. Organic slurry was prepared 
in a solvent of methyl ethyl ketone (MEK) by adding small amount of binder (Poly vinyl alcohol, PVB) and 10-20 
wt% Si powder. The samples dipped into the slurry were covered with the coatings by single time immersion for 10 
to 30 min. with simultaneous agitation of the slurry by magnetic stirrer to accelerate the deposition process and 
distribute uniformly all over substrate. A schematic diagram of the slurry coating experimental set up and the 
corresponding surface shape of as-coated TZM alloy sample were shown in Fig. 1 (a) and Fig. 1 (b) respectively. 
The appearance of as-coated specimen in Fig. 1 (b) exhibited formation of grey coloured coating of Si uniformly all 
over the substrate surface by slurry coating technique.

As-coated samples were first heated to around 250 0C to ensure removal of all volatile organic matter and then 
subjected to a number of diffusion treatment experiments to develop MoSi 2 coating at 1000-1200 0C for 2-6 hrs 
heating under Ar atmosphere. Surface morphology, cross sections of the coatings was investigated by SEM 
(Scanning Electron Microscope) with EDS (Energy Dispersi ve Spectrum). Optical microstructure was examined by 
optical microscopy and cross sectional image and elemental distribution profile were studied by EPMA (Electron 
Probe Microanalysis).Coating structure was identified by XRD (X-ray diffraction) analysis. Isothermal oxidation 
test was performed over diffusion treated samples at temperature range of 600-1000
0C for 4-10 hrs duration in air. 
The surface hardness of the coatings was evaluated by microhardness tester. A Pull-off adhesion tester of Model 
PosiTest AT was used to evaluate the adhesive (pull-off) strength of coating by determining the greatest tensile pull-
off force that the coating can bear before detaching from th e substrate. Prior to testing, a pull-off dolly of 10 mm dia 
was glued to the coating surface under test and kept for overnight drying. The adhered dolly was then gradually 
applied pull-off force using hand pump with application of pull-off pressure ranging from 0-40 MPa. 
 
3. Results and Discussion 
 
Fig. 2 (a) presented the grain morphology of the as-recei ved rolled TZM alloy which showed typical microstructure 
in which grains were elongated in the direction of rolling. This grain structure was not suitable for coating due to 
presence of large thermally stressed grains which could lead to cracks in the coating. Fig. 2 (b) presented 
microstructure of post annealed TZM alloy which had demonstrated that rolled structure had undergone 
considerable grain refinement after annealing heat treatment relieving all thermal stresses developed during hot 
rolling. Hence, this annealed structure with suitable grain refinement was found suitable as substrates for developing 
coating and subsequently used for coating. The thermal treatment of Si coated samples at 1200
0C had resulted in the 
formation of MoSi 2 by inter diffusion of Si and Mo through the pores of TZM substrate (porosity~ 5%) to form 
MoSi2 by their mutual interactions as per the following metallurgical reaction. 
Mo + 2Si = MoSi2

4. Conclusion: 
 
Present paper demonstrates the technical feasibility of developing MoSi
2 coating on TZM alloy by Si slurry coating 
technique. Two layered coating structure was formed where large surface layer was made of MoSi 2and thin 
interfacial layer of Mo 5Si3. Maximum coating thickness of 60 µm was achieved for MoSi 2 and 5 µm for Mo 5Si3 
coating. Granular, hard and non-porous coating structure was obtained with adequate adhesive strength. Oxidation 
resistance was found to be stable at 1000
0C due to formation of SiO2 scale on the surface.
