Abstract 
Effects of additions of 
γ’ (L1 2)-forming elements (Ta, Ti and Ni; for creep 
property) and γ (FCC)-forming elements (Cr and Si; for oxidation r esistance) on the 
microstructural stability, oxidation resistance and creep properties of two-phase γ/γ’ 
Co-based superalloys based on the Co-Al-W systems h ave been investigated in order to 
see whether simultaneous achievement of sufficient oxidation resistance and creep 
property for the usage above 1000°C is possible or not for Co-based superalloys. A 
candidate composition ((Co 0.8 Ni 0.2 )-6Al-2W-2Ta-6Ti-8Cr-1Si) that provides relatively 
high γ’ solvus temperature and high γ’ volume fraction is found. The alloy with the 
candidate composition exhibits superior oxidation r esistance when compared to Cr-free 
alloys but the creep property is not as good as exp ected from the high γ’ solvus 
temperature and high γ’ volume fraction and is only comparable to those o f Ni-based 
superalloys of the first generation. The reason for the modest creep properties of the 
candidate alloy as well as possibilities for furthe r investigation to simultaneously 
achieve sufficient oxidation resistance and creep p roperty for Co-based superalloys are 
discussed. 
 
Keywords: Co-based superalloy; creep; oxidation resistance, phase equilibria; scanning 
electron microscopy (SEM)

1. Introduction 
There is an expectation for the development of Co-b ased superalloys that can 
withstand at much higher temperatures than Ni-based superalloys can, since the 
discovery of ternary L1 
2 (γ’) compound that can coexist with a solid solution based on 
face-centered cubic (FCC)-Co ( γ) in the Co-Al-W ternary system [1, 2]. This stems 
from the fact that Co has the melting temperature h igher than Ni by about 50 °C and 
that Co is elastically stronger than Ni [3-5] . In contrast to such expectation, however, 
the creep properties of Co-based superalloys so far developed are only comparable to 
those for Ni-based superalloys of the first generat ion (such as IN100, MM002 and so 
on) [6]. Many factors are pointed out to be responsible for the modest creep properties 
of these Co-based superalloys, although many of the m are all related to the low phase 
stability of the γ’ phase of these alloys [2, 3, 7-9]. Those include, for example, the low γ’ 
solvus temperature, low γ’ volume fraction at high temperatures, low high-te mperature 
strength of the γ’ phase, positive lattice misfit [2, 3, 7, 8, 10]. The increasing of the γ’ 
phase stability is very important to increase the c reep properties of Co-based 
superalloys, since the γ’ solvus temperature and the γ’ volume fraction are expected in 
general both to increase with the increase in the γ’ phase stability. Many alloying 
elements have therefore been examined to see how th ey alter the γ’ solvus temperature, 
γ’ volume fraction, lattice misfit as well as the hi gh-temperature compression strength 
[11-16]. Some elements, such as Ni, Ta and Ti are p roved to be effective in increasing 
the γ’ phase stability (both the γ’ solvus temperature and γ’ volume fraction) [11, 13, 14, 
17-19]. With such knowledge, Xue et al. [20, 21] ha ve recently succeeded to develop a 
new Co-based superalloy with high γ’ solves temperature (1131°C) and high γ’ volume 
fraction at high temperatures (80% at 1000°C). As a result, the creep property of this 
alloy (with a chemical composition of Co-7Al-8W-1Ta -4Ti) is considerably improved 
so as to be comparable to those of Ni-based superal loys of the second generation (such 
as RR2000, SRR99 and so on). This new alloy, however, suffers from a fatal shortage of 
oxidation resistance when used at temperatures abov e 1000°C. It is generally believed 
that additions of a large amount of Cr (~8 at.%) an d Si (~1 at.%) are mandatory for the 
oxidation resistance of Co-base superalloys to be used above 1000°C [22-25]. However, 
both Cr and Si are proved to be a γ forming element, thereby decreasing the γ’ phase 
stability (i.e., γ’ solvus temperature and γ’ volume fraction at high temperatures) [11, 14, 
26]. This means that additions of Cr of such a larg e amount to Co-based alloys (for 
oxidation resistance) without significantly decreas ing the γ’ solvus temperature and γ’ 
volume fraction at high temperatures (for creep pro perty) are a tough task to achieve. 
However, this has to be achieved for Co-based super alloys when used at high

In the present study, we investigate effects of co- additions of γ’-forming

order to see whether simultaneous achievement of su fficient oxidation resistance and 
creep properties is possible or not for Co-based superalloys for the usage above 1000°C. 
 
2. Experimental Procedures 
 Alloy compositions to be investigated were determi ned as described below. 
We first tried to determine the ‘base’ composition ((Co 0.8 Ni 0.2 )-aAl-bW-8Cr-1Si) with 
the fixed ratio of Co and Ni at 4:1 and with the fi xed composition of Cr and Si at 8 and 
1 atomic %. Alloy compositions are given in atomic % throughout this paper unless 
otherwise stated. The fixed ratio of Co and Ni at 4 :1 was employed based on the 
consideration that Ni additions increase the solid- solubilities of other elements such as 
Ta and Ti but too much Ni additions (at the level o f Co:Ni=1:1) reduce the lattice misfit 
between γ and γ’ phases down to nearly zero, which may lead to the lowering of creep 
properties. The fixed compositions of 8at.% Cr and 1 at.% Si, were employed for 
oxidation properties above 1000°C from literature s urvey [22-25]. We adjusted Al and 
W compositions so as to achieve the two-phase γ/γ’ cuboidal microstructure without any 
other phases and to increase the γ’ volume fraction as much as possible. Then, we added 
Ta and Ti independently or together to the base composition by substituting Al and/or W 
((Co 0.8 ,Ni 0.2 )-aAl-bW-xTa-yTi-8Cr-1Si), also so as to achieve the two-phase γ/γ’ 
cuboidal microstructure without any other phases an d to increase the γ’ volume fraction 
as much as possible. 
Ingots with the above-mentioned nominal composition s were prepared by 
arc-melting an appropriate mixture of pure elements under an argon atmosphere. The 
ingots were homogenized in an Ar-filled quartz tube at 1200°C for 24 h followed by air 
cooling, and then aged at 800°C or at 1000°C for 96 h followed by quenching. 
Microstructures and chemical compositions of the ph ases present were examined with a 
JEOL 7000 scanning electron microscope (SEM) equipp ed with a JEOL 
energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) detector . γ’ solvus temperatures for 
selected alloys aged at 800°C were measured by diff erential scanning calorimetry 
(DSC) at a heating rate of 10°C/min under an Ar gas flow. Single crystals were prepared 
for some selected alloys by the Bridgeman method at a growth rate of 10 mm/h. For 
comparison, single crystals of Co-8Al-7W-1Ta-4Ti de veloped by Xue et al. [20, 21]

3.1 Phase constitution and microstructures 
3.1.1. Base composition 
Phase constitution and microstructures of (Co 
0.8 Ni 0.2 )-aAl-bW-8Cr-1Si alloys 
were investigated to determine the base composition after aging at 800°C for specimens 
with varying Al and W contents. Microstructures for some selected alloys are shown in 
Figs. 1(a)-(c). The desired microstructure for the base composition after aging at 800°C 
is a two-phase γ/γ’ microstructure (hopefully with cuboidal γ’ precipitates) with a high γ’ 
volume fraction without any other phases. At compos itions of 8Al-8W and 9Al-9W, 
other phases are obviously incorporated; they are t he DO 19 phase in the 8Al-8W alloy

Fig. 1(d) shows a microstructure of the base (8Al-7 W) alloy after aging at

This indicates a relatively low γ’ solvus temperature of the alloy with the base

structure) is observed. The formation of the parall el raft structure is consistent with the 
fact that the lattice misfit is negative for all al loys. Although there is no clear evidence 
that the negative misfit is harmful to creep streng th [10], we believe so unless the γ’ 
volume fraction is high enough for the γ’ raft to be continuous along the tensile axis 
direction. The γ’ volume fraction is not such high for all the alloys. But, at the same time, 
the formation of the parallel raft structure is obs erved to be obstructed by deformation 
corresponding to slip on (111) (indicated with arro ws), as observed in our previous 
study of creep of Cr-free alloys [8]. Slip on (111) is observed to occur so as to penetrate 
γ’ precipitates and this occurs even in the 8Ti allo y in which the γ’ volume fraction at 
creep testing temperatures is sufficiently high. Al l these clearly indicate that the 
high-temperature strength of the γ’ phase for all the alloys is too low to give rise to an 
excellent creep property.

4.1. Ta and Ti additions

γ/γ’ two phases are

with (Co 0.8 ,Ni 0.2 )-aAl-bW-xTa-yTi-8Cr-1Si compositions, based on the results obtai ned 
in the present study. Ta is assumed to substitute e ntirely W, and Ti is assumed to 
substitute both Al and W in the corresponding figur es. When Ta or Ti is added 
Journal Pre-proof

## Page 15

individually, the solubility limit is larger for Ti than Ta. For both Ta and Ti individual 
additions, the γ/γ’ two phase stable region is wider at 800°C than at 1000°C. The extent 
of the narrowing of the γ/γ’ two phase stable region is much more significant for Ta 
additions than Ti additions. This is due to the app earance of the third phase (such as 
DO 19 and µ phases) presumably arising from the intrinsically low solubility limit of Ta. 
This is consistent with the fact that L1 2-Co 3Ta is metastable [36] while L1 2-Co 3Ti is 
stable. This means that although the Ta addition is more effective in increasing the γ’ 
solvus temperature than the Ti addition, the high-t emperature γ’ volume fraction cannot 
be increased as expected from the increased γ’ solvus temperature only by Ta additions. 
However, the γ/γ’ two phase stable region is kept widely at 1000°C for Ti additions, 
although Ti is not very effective in increasing the γ’ solvus temperature when compared 
with Ta. Because of the wide γ/γ’ two phase stable region achieved by Ti additions, 
there is some room where Ta that is effective in in creasing the γ’ solvus temperature is 
co-added with Ti (Figs. 11(c) and (f)) to achieve b oth high γ’ solvus temperature and 
high γ’ volume fraction, which is impossible to achieve w ith individual additions of 
either Ta or Ti. This is basically what is achieved in Cr-free Co-based superalloys by 
Xue et al. [20, 21] and is again confirmed to occur even these Co-based superalloys 
contain a large amount of Cr and Si for oxidation resistance. 
 
4.2. The possibility of simultaneously achieving su fficient oxidation resistance and 
creep property 
Additions of a large amount of Cr (and Si) are know n to mandatory for Co-based 
alloys to achieve sufficient oxidation resistance a t high temperatures more than 1000°C 
[22-24, 34] but, at the same time, this is expected to lead to destabilizing the γ’ 
strengthening phase because Cr is a strong γ former [11, 26, 28, 37] and thereby 
deteriorating the creep property. The present study is undertaken to answer a very 
simple question as to whether the deterioration of the creep property caused by the Cr 
(and Si) addition could be compensated by additions of γ’ stabilizing elements such as 
Ta and Ti. Indeed, we could find out some compositi on window to simultaneously 
achieve the high γ’ solvus temperature and high γ’ volume fraction in Co-based 
superalloys containing a large amount of Cr with (C o 0.8 ,Ni 0.2 )-aAl-bW-xTa-yTi-8Cr-1Si 
compositions. That is (Co 0.8 ,Ni 0.2 )-6Al-2W-2Ta-6Ti. However, the creep property of this 
Cr-containing alloy is inferior to other Co-based s uperalloys so far developed without 
Cr additions and is just comparable to those of Ni- based superalloys of the first 
generation, as shown in the Larson-Millar plot in F ig. 12. The conclusion drawn from 
the present study is thus that unfortunately, suffi cient oxidation resistance and creep 
Journal Pre-proof

## Page 16

properties for the usage above 1000°C is difficult to simultaneously achieve for 
Co-based superalloys. 
To be noted is that achieving the sufficient creep property is very difficult even for 
Cr-free alloys. While Xue et al. [20, 21] reported that the Cr-free 1Ta4Ti alloy exhibits 
the creep property close to those of Ni-based super alloys of the second generation, the 
alloy with the same composition prepared in the pre sent study exhibits only a modest 
creep properties comparable to those of Ni-based superalloys of the first generation (Fig. 
12). One of the main reasons for modest creep prope rties of Co-based superalloys is the 
low strength of the γ’ phase arising from the low phase stability [3, 8, 9, 19]. Many L1 2 
compounds such as Ni 3Al that is the strengthening phase in Ni-based supe ralloys are 
known to exhibit yield stress anomaly so that the s trength increases with the increase in 
temperature. This is known to occur by thermally ac tivated cross slip of superpartial 
dislocations with b (Burgers vector)=1/2<110>. Subdissociation of the superpartial 
dislocation into super-Shockley partial dislocation s with b=1/6<112> may occur with 
dissociation width depending on the energy of compl ex stacking fault (CSF), which is 
closely related to the L1 2 phase stability. When the L1 2 phase stability is high, the CSF 
energy is also high so that the width of subdissoci ation of the superpartial dislocation is 
negligibly small. This leads to a low onset temperature for thermally activated cross-slip 
of the superpartial dislocation and thereby to a lo w onset temperature for yield stress 
anomaly, as observed in Ni 3Al. This obviously leads to high strength at high 
temperatures. But, when the L1 2 phase stability is not high as in L1 2-Co 3(Al,W), the 
CSF energy is relatively low so that the width of s ubdissociation of the superpartial 
dislocation is not small. In that case, the formati on of constriction is needed for the 
superpartial dislocation to cross-slip, leading to a high onset temperature for thermally 
activated cross-slip and thereby to a high onset te mperature for yield stress anomaly 
[38-42]. Once the onset temperature for yield stres s anomaly is high, the 
high-temperature strength is accordingly low. If th is is the case, the addition of Ta and 
Ti (2Ta and 6Ti simultaneously) is not enough to increase the high-temperature strength 
of the L1 2 phase by increasing the L1 2 phase stability (CSF energy) and by reducing the 
onset temperature for yield stress anomaly of the L 1 2 phase. We believe this is a very 
difficult task to achieve at alloy compositions clo se to those investigated in the present 
study. But, we still see some possibility to achiev e this at compositions away from the 
present ones, such as those with high Ni contents l ike the Ni to Co ratio about 1:1 
(instead of 4:1 in the present study), because the solubilities of Ta and Ti (and also Cr) 
are reported to expand as the Ni content in Co-base d superalloys increases [14,26]. The 
results on these studies will be published elsewhere.

5. Conclusions 
In the present study, we investigate effects of add itions of 
γ’-forming elements (Ta, 
Ti and Ni; for creep property) and γ-forming elements (Cr and Si; for oxidation 
resistance) on the microstructural stability, oxida tion resistance and creep properties of 
two-phase γ/γ’ Co-based superalloys based on the Co-Al-W systems , in order to see 
whether simultaneous achievement of sufficient oxid ation resistance and creep property 
is possible or not for Co-based superalloys for the usage above 1000°C. The results 
obtained are summarized as follows. 
(1) The 8Cr1Si addition is essential to achieve sufficient oxidation resistance at 1000°C 
but deteriorates the stability of the L1 
2 phase. The γ’ solvus temperature of the Cr 
containing alloy, (Co 0.8 Ni 0.2 )-8Al-7W-8Cr-1Si is considerably low (927°C), which 
is much lower than the target temperature of 1000°C. 
(2) The γ’ stabilizing element Ta can be added by substituting W. Ta additions increase 
the γ’ solvus temperature significantly (at a rate of 37 °C per 1at.%Ta) but reduce 
the γ’ volume fraction at 1000 oC significantly due to the appearance of the third 
phase. 
(3) The γ’ stabilizing element Ti can be added by substituti ng both Al and W. Ti 
additions increase the γ’ solvus temperature (at a rate of 25°C per 1at.%Ti ) and the 
high-temperature γ’ volume fraction simultaneously. 
(4) A composition window is found to achieve the high γ’ solvus temperature (1112 oC) 
and high γ’ volume fraction (55 v.v.% at 1000 oC) with additions of large amount of 
Cr and Si through co-additions of Ta and Ti; (Co 0.8 Ni 0.2 )-6Al-2W-2Ta-6Ti-8Cr-1Si. 
While excellent oxidation resistance is observed fo r this alloy, the creep property is 
only comparable (rupture time 103 h at 1000 oC/137 MPa) to those of Ni-based 
superalloys of the first-generation 
(5) The deterioration of the creep property caused by t he Cr and Si-addition cannot be 
compensated by alloying Ta and Ti (and Ni). Further investigation on alloys with 
higher Ni contents where the solubility of Ta and Ti is larger is needed.
