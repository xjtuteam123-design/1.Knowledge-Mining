ABSTRACT
Despite having excellent mechanical properties, the applications of many Ni-
based alloys are limited owing to their modest yield strengths. Grain refinement 
has provided the opportunity for further strengthening, while also requiring 
significant and undesirable compromises in ductility. In this work, a novel Ni-
based alloy with a heterogeneous, partially recrystallized structure was designed 
by controlling the thermomechanical process after cold-rolling. The alloy exhibits 
a superior combination of ~ 2 GPa yield strength and ~ 9% tensile uniform elon-
gation, surpassing the room-temperature mechanical performance of most Ni-
based alloys reported in recent years. The ultrahigh strength originates from the 
synergistic strengthening effects of grain boundaries, high-density dislocations, 
and γ’ nanoparticles. Meanwhile, the considerable ductility is primarily ascribed 
to the improved strain hardening ability and delayed necking induced by two 
mechanisms: (i) the formation of high-density stacking faults, Lomer-Cottrell 
locks, and deformation twins in the recrystallized grains; (ii) the abundant dis-
locations pile-up at the interface between the γ’ nanoparticles and matrix. These 
findings suggest that the design of partially recrystallized structures has great 
potential to solve the strength-ductility trade-off in Ni-based alloys.

recrystallized structures possess yield strength

YS is insufficient and raises a growing need for 
enhanced mechanical properties of nickel-based 
alloys. Although grain refinement to the nanoscale 
can effectively elevate the YS of Ni-based alloys to 
2 GPa [9 ], sacrificing tensile elongations is inescap-

affected the safety of Ni-based alloys when they 
serve as the load-bearing structure materials. Such a 
strength-ductility trade-off severely limits the devel-
opment and application potential of Ni-based alloys. 
Thus, a new design strategy is urgently needed to 
improve the synergy between strength and ductility 
in Ni-based alloys.
Partially recrystallized structure, which is a kind of 
heterostructure architecture, has emerged as a practi-
cal approach to achieving strength-ductility synergy 
in various advanced metallic materials [11– 14]. For 
instance, the Inconel 740H superalloy with a partially 
recrystallized structure still has a uniform elongation 
(UE) of ~ 15% at a YS up to 1.1 GPa [15]. The single-
phase VCrMnFeCoNi high entropy alloy (HEA) with 
a partial recrystallized structure exhibits a high YS 
of ~ 1 GPa and UE of ~ 46% at cryogenic tempera -
tures [16], which is generally unattainable for a fully 
recrystallized structure. In a partially recrystallized 
structure, the non-recrystallized regions with refined 
grains maintain ultrahigh strength, while the recrys-
tallized region with coarse grains provides sufficient 
plastic deformation ability. Besides, the deformation 
heterogeneity between the recrystallized and non-
recrystallized regions produces strain gradient and 
geometrically necessary dislocations (GNDs) pile-ups 
at region boundaries, which improves strain harden-
ing [17]. Thus, an excellent combination of strength 
and ductility can be achieved. Inspired by these find-
ings, introducing the partial recrystallized structure 
into Ni-based alloys is expected to obtain the desired 
strength-ductility synergy.
In this work, some low-cost conventional heat 
treatments were employed after cold-rolling to form 
a novel partial recrystallized structure in the Ni-based 
alloy. The effects of the partial recrystallized structure 
on the mechanical properties and strain hardening 
were investigated through tensile tests and micro -
scopic characterizations. This study provides valuable 
insights into the development of high-performance Ni-
based alloys for engineering applications.
Experimental methods
The ingots of Ni-based alloys with the composition of 
 N i51.5Co20Cr16W5Al2.5Ti2.5Nb2.5 (wt.%) were prepared 
using the vacuum induction melting furnace. The 
design principle of the chemical components for this 
alloy is based on the role of each element. For example, 
adding Cr aims to improve oxidation and corrosion 
resistance by forming the Cr2O3 oxide film. However, 
excessive content of Cr (> 25 wt.%) may induce the 
formation of σ phases, deteriorating strength and 
ductility [18, 19]. Moreover, a high content of Co (~ 20 
wt.%) is designed to reduce the stacking fault energy, 
thus promoting the formation of deformation twins to 
enhance plastic deformability. Yet, it is critical to note 
that overabundant Co compromises the thermal sta-
bility [20]. Furthermore, the addition of W is intended 
to enhance high-temperature mechanical properties, 
although surpassing a 5 wt.% W threshold could pre-
cipitate the α-W phase that may deteriorate mechani-
cal properties [21]. Incorporating Al, Ti, and Nb, each 
at a concentration of 2.5 wt.%, facilitates the forma-
tion of dense L12-Ni3(Al, Ti) γʹ precipitates, thereby 
enhancing precipitation strengthening [22, 23].
The cast ingots with a dimension of 78 × 76 × 8 
 m m3 were homogenized at 1080 °C for 4 h, followed 
by cold-rolling (CR) with a thickness reduction rate 
of 96% (from 15.8 to 0.6 mm). The thin sheets were 
respectively annealed at the critical recrystallization 
temperature (950 °C, Fig. 9a) for 1 h and solution tem-
perature (1080 °C) for 4 h, in order to obtain a partial-
recrystallized structure (named by CRA sample) and 
a solution-treated (ST) structure (named as ST sam -
ple). Furthermore, both CRA and ST samples were 
subjected to peak-aging treatment at 650 °C for 24 h 
(Fig. 9b), followed by air cooling to attain a noticeable 
improvement in strength, called CRA-aging and ST-
aging samples. Figure 10 illustrates the heat treatment 
route for the present CRA-aging alloy.
The dog-bone-shaped samples with a size of 
10 × 2 × 0.6 mm3 were prepared using a wire-electrode 
cutting machine. The uniaxial tensile tests with a fixed 
strain rate of 5 ×  10–4 s−1 were performed at room 
temperature using the Shimadzu AG-10 KN testing 
machine. At least three independent tests were per-
formed on each kind of sample to ensure the accuracy 
of the test results. Microstructural analysis was per-
formed using various electron microscopes, includ-
ing the JEOL JSM 7200F scanning electron micros -
copy (SEM) equipped with electron backscattered

Strengthening and strain hardening 
mechanisms
Following the microsrtuctural characterizations of 
the partially recrystallized structure, the origins of 
the ultrahigh strength of the CRA-aging Ni-based 
alloy can be discussed. Figure 5 shows a schematic 
diagram of the strengthening mechanisms in the CRA-
aging alloy. First, according to the Hall–Petch effect 
[41, 42], the UFGs and NGs with nanoscale grain sizes 
can provide remarkable grain boundary strengthen-
ing ( /u1D70EGB , Fig. 5a) [43]. Furthermore, the high-density 
dislocations within the NGs contribute to forest dislo-
cation strengthening ( /u1D70ED , Fig. 5b) [44]. The asynchro-
nous deformation of UFGs and NGs produces strain 
partition and strain gradient at the interfaces, lead-
ing to the pile-up of GNDs before the macroscopic 
yielding, thereby contributing to hetero-deformation 
induced (HDI) strengthening (  /u1D70EHDI , Fig. 5c) [45–47]. 
Moreover, the presence of numerous γ’ nanoparti-
cles further impedes the movement of dislocations, 
thereby invoking precipitation strengthening (  /u1D70EP , 
Fig. 5d) [48–51]. Finally, different metallic elements in 
the Ni-based alloy introduce solid solution strength-
ening (  /u1D70ESS , Fig. 5e). To sum up, the ultrahigh YS 
observed in the CRA-aging alloy can be expressed as 
/u1D70EYS = /u1D70EGB + /u1D70ED + /u1D70EP + /u1D70EHDI + /u1D70ESS.
As shown in Fig. 1b, the exceptional ductility of the 
ultrahigh-strength Ni-based alloy can be attributed to 
its significant strain hardening ability. The deforma-
tion heterogeneity between non-recrystallized and 
recrystallized regions leads to HDI strengthening 
[52–54], but the strain hardening in the non-recrys-
tallized regions is limited. Therefore, our investiga-
tion focused specifically on the microstructures in 
the recrystallized regions to elucidate the underlying 
mechanisms of strain hardening in the CRA-aging 
samples. Figure 6 gives the TEM observations of the 
UFGs in the recrystallized regions at different applied 
strains. At an engineering strain of ~ 2% (Fig. 6a), a 
few thin stacking faults (SFs) were present. With the 
increasing applied strain, both the length and quan-
tity of SFs improved (Fig. 6b), and deformation twins 
(DTs) were generated owing to the relatively low 
stacking fault energy of this Ni-based alloy [55]. The 
formation of DTs is related to the reaction of overlap-
ping nano-sized SFs (Fig. 6b2). Moreover, the inter-
action between DTs and SFs leads to the generation 
of Lomer-Cottrell (L-C) locks (Fig. 6b2). As the strain 
further increased to ~ 8%, a high density of SFs on the 
two {111} slip systems, accompanied by abundant 
SF intersections, networks, and DTs (Fig. 6c1). Addi-
tionally, the interactions between SFs and DTs were 
enhanced and generated more L-C locks (Fig. 6c2),

The high-density γ’ nanoparticles within the matrix 
also contribute to the deformation stability and uni-
form elongation. Firstly, since the lattice parameters of 
the coherent γ’ nanoparticles and the matrix are simi-
lar, the relevant elastic interactions between the γ’ nan-
oparticles and dislocations are weak, which prevents 
the early crack initiation at the γ’ nanoparticles–matrix 
interface due to strain localization [39]. Secondly, 
Fig. 7 shows that when the applied strain reaches 8%, 
a large number of dislocations were observed near the 
interface between the γ’ nanoparticles and the matrix. 
The dislocation motions are hindered by the interface, 
which promotes dislocation accumulation [64]. The 
above features help maintain strain hardening capac-
ity during deformation and effectively suppress the 
onset of necking.
Fracture mechanism
Figure 8 shows the tensile fracture morphologies of 
the CRA and CRA-aging alloy. Their area reductions 
(RA) are 27% and 19% (Fig. 8a, c), respectively. Com-
pared to the relatively large and deep dimples in the 
CRA alloy (Fig. 8b), the dimples in CRA-aging alloy 
are small and shandow (Fig. 8d). It suggests that the 
aging treatment could affect the uniform deformability 
of the CRA-aging alloy. These findings indicate that 
the introduction of large amounts of γ’ nanoparticles 
by aging treatments has a slightly detrimental effect 
on the fracture behavior of the CRA-aging alloy. The 
γ’ nanoparticles have inherent brittleness and high 
strength, thereby inducing stress concentration and 
accelerating the growth of micropores [35, 65]. There-
fore, the ductility of the CRA-aging alloy is lower than 
the CRA alloy, as shown in Fig. 1.

Conclusions
This study indicates that the conventional heat treat-
ments following cold-rolling can yield a novel par-
tially recrystallized structure in the nickel-based 
alloy. The resulting Ni-based alloy exhibits a signifi-
cantly enhanced combination of YS (~ 2 GPa) and UE 
(~ 9%) compared to the fully recrystallized state. The 
exceptionally ultrahigh YS mainly originated from a 
synergistic effect of precipitation strengthening, dislo-
cation strengthening, grain boundary strengthening, 
and HDI strengthening. The generations of SFs, L-C 
locks, and DTs in the recrystallized grains specifically 
provide strain hardening capability, thereby preserv-
ing substantial ductility. Furthermore, the coherent 
nanoparticles can alleviate the strain localization and 
significantly mitigate the onset of necking. These piv-
otal findings contribute to a deeper understanding of 
the structure–property relationship in Ni-based alloys 
with high strength and toughness, and provide valu-
able insights for designing high-performance materi-
als for aerospace applications.
