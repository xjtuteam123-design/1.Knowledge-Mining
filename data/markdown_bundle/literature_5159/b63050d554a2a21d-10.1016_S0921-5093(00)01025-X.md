Characterization of Fe 3Al-based intermetallic alloys fabricated by
mechanical alloying and HIP consolidation
Su-Ming Zhu, Makoto Tamura, Kazushi Sakamoto, Kunihiko Iwasaki *
Japan Ultra -High Temperature Materials Research Institute , 573-3 Okiube, Ube, Yamaguchi 755-0001, Japan
Received 19 April 2000
Abstract
Fe3Al-based alloys with and without Ti addition were synthesized by hot isostatic pressing (HIP) of mechanically alloyed
nanocrystalline powders. An ultra-high pressure (1 GPa) HIP equipment was used in an effort to consolidate the mechanically
alloyed powders into fully dense compacts while retaining the nanocrystalline microstructure. The densiﬁcation of powders was
found to depend largely on the HIP temperature. Fully dense compacts with a grain size of about 200 nm were obtained after HIP
treatment at 1073 K. Mechanical properties of the compacted materials at room and elevated temperatures were determined by
hardness and compression tests. Ti addition signiﬁcantly increased the hardness and room temperature yield strength, but this
increase was accompanied by a reduction in ductility. The yield strength decreased monotonically with increasing temperature
with no positive temperature dependence observed. The high temperature deformation processes were investigated by examining
the strain rate sensitivity and the deformation microstructure. Compared with the binary Fe
3Al, the Ti-added alloy exhibited lower
strength but better deformability at temperatures above 1073 K. © 2000 Elsevier Science S.A. All rights reserved.

1. Introduction
Iron aluminides based on Fe 3Al and FeAl have at-
tracted considerable attention as potential candidates
for warm structural application and as promising sub-
stitutes for stainless steels and cast irons at room tem-
perature [1–3]. This is possibly because of their
availability of raw materials, conservation of strategic
elements (Cr as an example), relatively low density (as
compared with Fe- and Ni-based alloys), and excellent
oxidation and corrosion resistance. However, commer-
cialization of these intermetallics has been limited due
to their fabrication difﬁculty and poor mechanical
properties both at ambient and elevated temperatures.
In recent years, there has been considerable interest in
the synthesis and processing of intermetallic com-
pounds by means of mechanical alloying [4–10]. Me-
chanical alloying is a high energy ball milling process
that involves repeated welding and fracturing of pow-
der particles. This technique can produce ﬁne powders
with a nanoscale grain size and a homogeneous distri-
bution of disposoids, which is expected to improve the
ambient temperature ductility and high temperature
strength of intermetallic compounds.
A systematic investigation has been conducted by the
present authors to fabricate tribological Fe

metallic alloys by a mechanical alloying approach
[11,12]. Ti was chosen as the ternary alloying element
because it has been reported by Hawk et al. [13–15]
that addition of Ti to Fe

their wear properties. For example, the alloy with 10
at.% Ti substituting for Fe in Fe

The full beneﬁt of mechanical alloying can be pre-
served only if the subsequent consolidation process can
retain the nanoscale grain size. In fact, consolidation of
mechanically alloyed powders into full density compact
while restricting grain growth is a great challenge. This
is because full densiﬁcation of mechanically alloyed
powders generally requires exposure to high tempera-
tures, which tends to result in coarsening of the
nanometer-sized grains. In the present study, by using
an ultra-high pressure (1 GPa) hot isostatic pressing
(HIP) equipment, the mechanically alloyed powders
were consolidated at a temperature as low as possible in
an effort to fabricate full density compacts with
nanocrystalline microstructure. Microstructural charac-
teristics and mechanical properties of the compacted
materials were then examined.

3.3. Mechanical properties
Vickers hardness results of the compacted materials
are shown in Fig. 5. For comparison, hardness results
of cast alloys with the same compositions are also
given. The cast alloys are prepared by vacuum induc-
tion melting and have coarse grains (600 mm for 0Ti
and 100 mm for 10Ti alloy) [16]. The comparison clearly
shows that the HIP-compacted materials have signiﬁ-
cantly higher hardness than the cast counterparts.
Moreover, the Ti addition is more effective in increas-
ing the hardness of the HIP-compacted alloys than the
cast ones.
Fig. 6 shows the room temperature compressive
stress–strain curves of the HIP-compacted materials. It
can be seen that yield strength as high as 1.5 and 2.4
GPa is attained for 0Ti and 10Ti alloys, respectively.
Such high yield strength values are considered to result
from the ultra-ﬁne microstructure retained in the com-
pacts. It is also noted that the HIP compacts exhibit
considerable rupture strain (39% for the 0Ti alloy and
16% for the 10Ti alloy).

4. Discussion
The present study demonstrates that fully dense and
ultraﬁne grained Fe
3Al-based alloys can be fabricated
by HIP consolidation of mechanically alloyed
nanocrystalline powders at 1073 K under an ultrahigh
pressure of about 1 GPa. Due to the ﬁne grain size,
these compacted Fe
3Al alloys have signiﬁcantly higher
hardness values than their coarse grained cast counter-
parts. This study also shows that Ti addition signiﬁ-
cantly increases the hardness and room temperature
yield strength of the compacted Fe
3Al alloy. Such
strengthening effect has been previously reported by
other workers in cast Fe
3Al-based alloys, and was
attributed to solid solution hardening by Ti [20,21]. In
the present materials, two other factors are thought to
contribute additionally to the strengthening by Ti addi-
tion. One is the ordering strengthening effect, because
the Ti addition increases the degree of ordering of the
compacted Fe
3Al alloy. The other is the dispersion
strengthening effect. Ti addition also leads to the for-
mation of some ﬁne disposoids, which can act as obsta-
cles for dislocation movement during deformation. In
view of the small difference in grain size of the 0Ti and
10Ti alloys (Table 2), strengthening due to grain size
reﬁnement are considered to be unimportant.
With increasing temperature, the present alloys
shows monotonically decreased yield strength. The pos-
itive temperature dependence of yield strength usually
exhibited by cast Fe
3Al-based alloys is not observed.
The strength anomaly in Fe 3Al-based alloys is a very
complicated phenomenon, and is generally thought to
be associated with the degree of ordering and the
transition of dislocation mechanism with temperature
[22,23]. The absence of this behavior in the present
alloys is speculated to be due to the relatively low
degree of ordering. Another interesting point of the
present study is that the yield and the ﬂow strength of
the 10Ti alloy become lower than those of the 0Ti alloy
at temperatures above 1073 K. This result cannot be
explained either by the solution hardening, by the dis-
persion strengthening or by the ordering strengthening
already mentioned. In view of the different grain
growth tendency in the 0Ti and 10Ti alloys deformed at
high temperatures, microstructural stability and related
deformation mechanism are considered to account for
the observed result. It has been well documented that,
for the ﬁne grained materials, grain boundary sliding is
an important deformation mechanism [24–26]. Accord-
ing to these literature works, the temperature at which
the strengthening effect by Ti addition is reversed can
be interpreted as the one at which the deformation due
to grain boundary sliding becomes dominant. As a
result, the 10Ti alloy shows better deformability and
visible reduction in ﬂow stress at temperatures above
1073 K in comparison with the 0Ti alloy. The high
strain rate sensitivity close to 0.3 and lower activation
energy than creep activation energy also suggest that
grain boundary sliding plays an important role in the
deformation of the 10Ti alloy at temperatures above
1073 K.
5. Conclusions
Fully dense Fe
3Al-based compacts with a grain size
of about 200 nm were produced from mechanically
alloyed powders by HIP consolidation at 1073 K at an
ultra-high pressure of 980 MPa.
The compacted materials showed signiﬁcantly high
hardness and room temperature yield strength, together
with considerable rupture strain. Ti addition improved
the hardness and yield strength but this increase was
accompanied by a reduction in rupture strain.
The yield strength of the compacted materials de-
creased monotonically with increasing test temperature,
with no positive temperature dependence observed.
Finally, Ti addition improved the microstructural
stability of the compacted Fe
3Al alloy at elevated tem-
peratures. As a result, the Ti-added alloy showed lower
ﬂow stress but better deformability at temperatures
above 1073 K than the Ti-free one.
