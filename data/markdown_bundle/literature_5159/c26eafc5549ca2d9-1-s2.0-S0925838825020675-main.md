1. Introduction
The high entropy alloys (HEAs) have exhibit high strength over a 
wide temperature range and under a large strain state when its structure 
contains body-centered cubic (BCC). At the beginning, refractory high 
entropy alloys (RHEAs) are focused from 3d transition metals for high 
temperature applications with single-phase BCC structure [1,2] . How -
ever, their plastic deformation mode is quite different from that of 
metals with face-centered cubic (FCC) structure. This is mainly due to 
their crystallographic lattice characteristics and high lattice friction [3, 
4] . Due to the synergistic effect of solid solution strengthening and lower 
valence electron concentration, RHEAs often tend to exhibit comparable 
strength and plasticity with BCC structure [5] . Therefore, the HEAs with 
BCC structure can also achieve the synergistic increase of the strength 
and toughness by reasonable design. There are many ways to prepare 
refractory high entropy alloys, but additive manufacturing is believed to 
be a good choice. As one of the most promising and hot metal additive 
manufacturing technologies, laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) can realize 
the rapid formation of complex components effectively [6] . It has been 
proved that such technology shows a remarkable effect on enhancing the

The composition uniformity of alloy powder has great influence on 
the performance of molded parts in LPBF. Especially with refractory 
elements, the prepared powders are prone to component segregation if it 
is not handled properly. Nevertheless, the spherical powders required 
for additive manufacturing are usually prepared by traditional gas at -
omization method (GA). The advantages of GA are high yield, wide 
variety of powder and low cost. But it is easy to introduce foreign im -
purities into the powder prepared. Meanwhile, the alloy powder con -
taining high melting point elements (Nb, Cr, W, etc) is prone to severe 
segregation of components, heavily influencing the performance of 
printing parts. And the powder prepared by this technology contains 
more hollow powder. These bottlenecks limit its further development. 
Due to equipment temperature and process flow, it is difficult to ensure 
uniform composition of alloy ingot containing refractory elements. As a 
result, the resulting ingot contains refractory elements attached to the 
grain boundaries (partially or unalloyed) in the form of a single sub -
stance. Therefore, the alloy ingot has component segregation. Therefore, 
the spherical powders produced by GA are difficult to prepare RHEAs 
and ensure uniform composition [6 – 8] . How to control alloy powders to 
make them have the characteristics of high density, high sphericity and 
uniform composition has become the latest concern in the field of ad -
ditive manufacturing. [9 – 11] .
Based on the disadvantages of the traditional process of preparing 
refractory alloy powder, this paper provides an innovative idea. The 
RHEA spherical powders with uniform composition were prepared by 
mechanical alloying (MA) and High temperature remelting spher -
ification technology (HTRS). MA technology achieves the composition 
uniformity of RHEA powder through the combined action of mechanical 
milling (plastic deformation) and temperature raising (atomic diffusion) 
[11 – 13] . But the shape of these powders is generally polygonal granular, 
and the fluidity is poor [14 – 17] . In this paper, we are inspired by the 
idea of Temble ’ s droplet experiment: The single particle of powder is 
melted by plasma melt-blowing and then cooled to obtain spherical 
powder directly. Thus, the HTRS technology has been invented and 
verified as a wise way to solve the issue. In conclusion, MA can promote 
the alloying of refractory elements, while HTRS could change the 
powder morphology (spheroidization) [18,19] . The process obtained by 
combining these two processes is called dual process (DP). Additive 
manufacturing has the characteristics of personalized printing and 
customization. The production of alloy powder with special re -
quirements is low, and the general alloy on the market is generally 
difficult to be used directly.In order to adapt to the trend of individua -
tion and customization, it is very important to design and develop spe -
cial alloy and its preparation process in small batch. In addition, the 
self-developed technology of this project can significantly reduce the 
cost of powder production. This is an important way to promote the 
wider application of additive manufacturing.

high-entropy alloys, proving that our design concept is feasible [20] .

RHEAs with BCC structure through composition design control. It is 
believed that Nb can refine grain, improve wear resistance and tensile 
strength of alloy. In addition, Nb has high temperature strength and 
fatigue resistance. Thus, we have designed newly Nb

3. Results and discussion on powder production
3.1. Alloying degree analysis of alloyed powder
Generally speaking, the particle size of the alloy powder has been 
determined after the preparation of MA. The main reasons for affecting 
the particle size of the powder are milling time and rod material ratio. 
Under the milling process, the fragments and the flake ductility powder 
are welded together to form composite lamellar structure and the layer 
spacing decreases.The powder surface curvature increases, the diffusion 
distance decreases, and the diffusion is easier to carry out. After 
continued milling, the powder particles were further refined and the size 
was uneven. This is due to the continuous impact, deformation and shear 
of the composite layered structure of cold welding.
The high temperature remelting technique is inspired by Temble ’ s 
droplet experiment. Spherical powder was obtained by melting single 
particle through falling tube melting and cooling. Fig. 2 shows the SEM

3.6. Mechanical properties
In HEAs, the uneven distribution of chemical components causes the 
edge dislocation to be subjected to strong pinning forces at the nano -
scale. Instead of moving in a smooth, fast way, it moves in a slow, 
discontinuous way. In addition, the fluctuation of chemical composition 
will promote spontaneous nucleation of spiral dislocation twists [49] . 
This has a significant pinning effect on the kink expansion, resulting in 
the speed of the spiral dislocation being limited not by the kink nucle -
ation rate but by the kink expansion rate. Therefore, the motion of blade 
and screw dislocations in BCC HEAs is dominated by special activation 
behavior. The two kinds of dislocations need to overcome similar local 
barriers and show similar dynamic characteristics, being significantly 
different from traditional BCC metals.

(1) HEA-SLM are composed of a single BCC solid solution with uni -
form composition. This is mainly due to the mechanical alloying 
technology advantage. It combined effect of mechanical milling 
(plastic deformation) and temperature lifting (atomic diffusion) 
to achieve the composition homogenization. Such technology can 
promote the solid alloying of immiscible systems and further 
expand the solid solubility of insoluble elements in immiscible 
matrix.
(2) High friction coefficient can lead to considerable surface plastic 
deformation, thereby reducing the tribological properties of the 
material. According to the maximum principal stress distribution, 
the GCr15 ball is subjected to compressive stress at the front and 
tensile stress at the back during the sliding process. This proves 
that the stress behind the ball is similar to the stress in uniaxial 
tension. The cracking and falling off of GA-SLM surface with high 
friction coefficient are related to cyclic tensile stress.
(3) The yield strength (YS), ultimate tensile strength (UTS) and 
average elongation ( ε ) samples were 953 MPa, 1159 MPa and 
14.8 % respectively by the DP-SLM. An increase in the homoge -
nization of the elemental composition can hinder the sponta -
neous nucleation of the helical dislocation distortion. The 
resistance of the dislocation during motion is reduced. This al -
lows the dislocation to move more freely, thereby increasing the 
plastic deformation capacity of the alloy.
