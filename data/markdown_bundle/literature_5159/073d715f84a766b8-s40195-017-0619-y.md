Microstructure Characteristics and Mechanical Properties of Nb-
17Si-23Ti Ternary Alloys Fabricated by In Situ Reaction Laser
Melting Deposition
Wei Liu 1 • Hua-Ping Xiong 1 • Neng Li 1 • Shao-Qing Guo 1 • Ren-Yao Qin 1
Received: 12 April 2017 / Revised: 30 May 2017 / Published online: 6 July 2017
/C211The Chinese Society for Metals and Springer-Verlag GmbH Germany 2017
Abstract Fabrication of ternary Nb–17Si–23Ti alloys was attempted by in situ reaction laser melting deposition (LMD)
with dual powder feeding method from Nb-28 at.% Ti powder mixture and pure Si powder. The microstructures of the as-
deposited alloys were examined with scanning electronic microscope, and the phase constituents were analyzed by X-ray
energy-dispersive spectrometer and X-ray diffraction. Furthermore, the effect of laser power on microstructure charac-
teristics, microhardness and indentation fracture toughness was also investigated. The in situ reaction LMD process
resulted in remarkable reﬁnement of the microstructure. The as-deposited samples mainly consisted of Nb SS,
metastable (NbTi)3Si and Ti-rich Nb SS. With the increase in the laser power from 1000 to 2000 W, the Nb SS morphology
changed from discontinuous dendritic to near equiaxed, but the Ti-rich Nb SS phase tended to vanish. Furthermore, with the
increase in the laser power, the microhardness of as-deposited samples increased from 822 to 951 HV, while the inden-
tation fracture toughness was improved from 12.3 to 14.1 MPa m 1/2. The corresponding mechanism is also discussed.

reaction
1 Introduction
Nb–Si-based alloys have shown high potential to replace
Ni-based superalloys due to their high melting point
([1750 /C176C) and low density (6.6–7.2 g/cm 3) as well as
attractive speciﬁc mechanical properties at elevated tem-
peratures [1–4]. Therefore the Nb–Si alloys have become a
new generation of ultra-high-temperature structural mate-
rials with potential service temperatures of 1200–1400 /C176C
[3, 4]. In general, the Nb–Si-based alloys are composed of
NbSS (Nb solid solution) and silicide, in which the metallic
NbSS offers room-temperature ductility while the rein-
forcing silicide supplies strength and creep resistance at
high temperatures [ 5–7]. On the basis of many investiga-
tions, it has been recognized that the composition opti-
mization [8, 9] and the processing innovations [ 10, 11] are
very effective approaches for achieving a satisfactory bal-
ance between the properties of Nb–Si-based alloys.
Over the past few years, the inﬂuence of alloying ele-
ments such as Ti, Cr, Al, Hf, Mo and W on the
microstructure, mechanical properties and oxidation
behavior of Nb–Si-based alloys has been studied
[8, 9, 12, 13]. For example, it was found that Ti could
decrease the brittle-ductile transition temperature (BDTT)
[8, 13]. In particular, it was reported that the fracture
toughness of the as-cast Nb–16Si binary alloy was only
5.6 MPa m 1/2, but it was increased remarkably to
11.3 MPa m 1/2 when 27 at.% of Ti was added to the alloy
[13]. In fact Ti was considered as one of the most effective
toughening elements for the Nb–Si-based alloys.

To date, several processing techniques including vac-
uum arc melting [ 8, 9, 11, 15], induction melting [ 16],
investment casting [ 1, 22], directional solidiﬁcation (DS)
[6, 15, 17, 18] and powder metallurgy [ 10, 19] have been
successfully employed for the fabrication of Nb–Si-based
alloys. However, the fabrication of components with
complicated shape including hollow turbine blade for aero
engine is still a great challenge. It should be noted that laser
melting deposition (LMD), which is one kind of additive
manufacturing technologies, could produce near net shape
components with complex shapes and even with complex
internal features [ 20, 21]. Additionally, due to the rapid
melting and solidiﬁcation characteristics of the LMD
manufacturing process, it is easy to achieve ﬁne
microstructures and to decrease the composition segrega-
tion of Nb–Si-based alloys, a fact that should be favorable
to the improvement in the overall properties [ 20, 21].
However, so far, only a few researches have been reported
on the fabrication of Nb–Si-based alloys by additive
manufacturing method [ 22–24]. For instance, the Nb–
22Si–26Ti–6Cr–3Hf–2Al alloy was deposited by laser
directly forming from pre-alloyed powder, and the as-de-
posited alloy was mainly composed of Nb
5Si3 and NbSS,i n
which the former phase has both large (20–30 lm) parti-
cles and ﬁnely dispersed particles and the latter appears
only as ﬁnely dispersed white particles [ 22]. Additionally,
the Nb–18Si–24Ti–2Cr–2Al–2Hf alloy was built by
selective laser melting (SLM) using pre-alloyed powder.
The as-built alloy consisted of Nb
SS, a-Nb5Si3, b-Nb5Si3
and Nb 3Si phases [ 24]. However, so far, the studies con-
cerning the LMD-processing of Nb–Si-based alloys are far
insufﬁcient.
In this study, an attempt was made to fabricate Nb–
17Si–23Ti ternary alloys from the starting materials of pure
Nb, Si and Ti powders by the in situ reaction LMD method.
The effect of laser power on morphology, size and distri-
bution of the constituent phases of the alloys was exam-
ined. Microhardness and indentation fracture toughness
were evaluated as well, aiming at a better understanding of
the inﬂuence of the microstructure on the mechanical
properties.
2 Experimental
Powder particles of Nb (99.5 wt% purity), Si (99.9 wt%)
and Ti (99.5 wt%) with near-equiaxed morphology and
approximately 80 lm in average size were chosen as raw
materials, as shown in Fig. 1. A pure Nb plate with the
dimension of about 100 mm 9 100 mm 9 10 mm was
used as the laser deposition substrate.
Experiments of LMD were carried out using a 6 kW
optical ﬁber laser processing system with six-axes numer-
ical control working table and a synchronous powder fee-
der with two independent feeding units, which can
simultaneously deliver two different kinds of powder par-
ticles with diverse feeding rates.
Because the melting points of the Nb–Si-based alloys
are in excess of 1750 /C176C[ 1] and the melting point of pure
Nb powder is up to 2468 /C176C, high input energy should be
applied in the LMD process. The preliminary experiments
for fabrication of Nb–Si alloys by LMD using 500 W
showed that many unreacted raw powder particles still
retained in the as-deposited sample, indicating that a laser
power of 500 W was too low to prepare sound Nb–Si-
based alloys by LMD. Therefore, the inﬂuence of the input
energy was subsequently taken into account by comparing
different laser powers of 1000 and 2000 W. Based on the
preliminary experiments, the following optimal parameters
were obtained, as shown in Table 1. Argon was used as the
shielding gas and also as powder carrier gas for protection
of the molten pool. The oxygen content in the processing
chamber was less than 100 ppm.
A dual powder feeding method, as schematically illus-
trated in Fig. 2a, was used to deposit the Nb–Si–Ti alloy
samples. As shown in Fig. 2a, the laser beam and coaxial
powder feeding nozzle move as an integral unit so that the
powder particles from the two feeding units are delivered
coaxially into the laser molten pool. The pure Si powder
was delivered through one feeding unit and Nb ? Ti
powder mixture with the composition of Nb-28 at.% Ti was
delivered by the other feeding unit. By adjusting the
feeding rates of the two independent feeding units, the
powder mass ﬂow rates achieved were equal to 0.45 g/min
for the Si powder and 7 g/min for the Nb ? Ti powder
mixture (Table 1), respectively, so that the alloy with a
target composition Nb–17Si–23Ti could be produced by
LMD.
The laser scanning path is shown in Fig. 2b: the laser
beam moved in a zigzag way with the hatch spacing of
0.5 mm, and the area of 15 mm 9 15 mm approximately
in size was deposited in the ﬁrst layer, while in the sub-
sequent layers the laser scanning path was turned with 90 /C176
rotation layer by layer. The deposition thickness of each
layer was about 0.5 mm when the 1000 W laser power was

3 Results
3.1 As-Deposited Nb–17Si–23Ti Alloy Samples

ternary alloy specimens without macro-defects were suc-

method, as shown in Fig. 3. By comparing Fig. 3a and b, it
can be seen that the specimen fabricated using 1000 W

3.2 Microstructure Characteristics
Figure 4 shows the X-ray diffraction patterns of the Nb–
17Si–23Ti samples deposited by 1000 and 2000 W laser
power, respectively. The characteristic diffraction peaks of
bcc Nb (JCPDS No. 34-0370) and tetragonal Nb
3Si silicide
(JCPDS No. 22-0763) were identiﬁed, while no other
compounds were detected. This means that, regardless of
the laser intensity, the as-deposited Nb–17Si–23Ti samples
are mainly composed of the same two phases: Nb
SS and
Nb3Si. According to the Nb–Si–Ti ternary diagram [ 14],
the Nb3Si phase is metastable at room temperature. How-
ever, generally, the metastable Nb3Si phase existed in most
Nb–Si binary [6] or Nb–Si–Ti ternary [ 6] alloys fabricated
by cast methods owing to the incomplete or sluggish
eutectoid reaction [ 27]. Due to the rapid solidiﬁcation
characteristics of the LMD process and the stabilizing
effect of Ti on Nb 3Si at lower temperatures [ 13], the
eutectoid decomposition reaction, Nb 3Si ? Nb ? Nb5Si3,
was completely suppressed, and thus Nb3Si was retained in
the Nb–17Si–23Ti samples prepared by LMD.
The back-scattered electron (BSE) images of the
microstructure of the samples A and B are shown in Fig. 5,
where three phases are observed with clear contrasts of
bright, gray and dark phases. The EDS analysis results are
given in Table 3. By combining the XRD (Fig. 4) and EDS
(Table 3) results, it is conﬁrmed that in the BSE images
(Fig. 5) the bright and gray phases are Nb
SS and (NbTi)3Si,
respectively. Obviously they are the main constituents in
the as-deposited alloy. In addition, the dark phase particles
existing within the interdendrites are determined to be Ti–
rich Nb
SS by EDS analysis (Table 3; Fig. 5b).
It can be seen from Fig. 5a and b that in sample A
fabricated by 1000 W laser power, the constituent phase
NbSS is characterized by a discontinuous dendritic struc-
ture, with average size of about 3 lm in length and less
than 1 lm in width. The (NbTi) 3Si phase presents near
quadrilateral shape of about 2 lm in average size. Mean-
while, some Ti-rich Nb SS particles are found to exist
between the dendritic arms. It is deduced that they should
be formed from the lower-melting-point Ti solidiﬁed at the
end of the solidiﬁcation process and enriched in the inter-
dendritic regions. The Ti-rich Nb
SS phase existing in the
Nb–Si–Ti-based alloys was reported in other references
[28, 29] as well. It can also be found in Fig. 5a and b that
most of the interfaces between Nb SS and (NbTi) 3Si are
almost straight in the Nb–17Si–23Ti alloy fabricated by
1000 W laser power, which might be attributed to the
growth of the intermetallic (NbTi)
3Si phase in a typical
small-faceted structure in this case.
The BSE images of sample B in Fig. 5c and d show that
the microstructure is reﬁned and becomes more homoge-
neous by increasing the laser power from 1000 to 2000 W.
The (NbTi)3Si distributed continuously as matrix phase and
NbSS became ﬁner, with an average size 1 lm or less.
Interestingly, some of the extremely ﬁne (NbTi) 3Si parti-
cles of about 300 nm in average size were wrapped by the
Nb
SS. Meanwhile, very small amounts of Ti-rich Nb SS
particles (dark contrast, see the enlarged image in Fig. 5d)
existed at the NbSS/(NbTi)3Si interface. That is, the Ti-rich
NbSS particles tended to vanish when the laser power was
increased to 2000 W.

4 Discussion
4.1 Evolution Mechanism of the Microstructure
In the conventional cast Nb–Si-based alloys, the coarsened
microstructure presented the primary phase size up to tens
or hundreds of microns due to the slow solidiﬁcation rate
[7, 8, 15, 16]. However, in this study, the Nb–17Si–23Ti
alloys with rather ﬁne microstructure of a few microns
were achieved, signifying that the microstructure has been
reﬁned approximately by 1–2 orders of magnitude through
LMD. The LMD process is a kind of additive manufacture
technology based on the forming principle of scatter
accumulation [20]. The metal powder particles were fully
melted by the high-energy laser beam and solidiﬁed rapidly
as the laser beam moved away, and then the as-deposited
parts were built by overlapping the multi-tracks and multi-
layers. As we know, the thermal gradient, G, and the
solidiﬁcation velocity, V, are the most important parame-
ters which govern the solidiﬁcation microstructure of a
given alloy [ 34]. On the basis of the solidiﬁcation theory,
the ﬁneness of microstructure and the G and V values in the
solidiﬁcation process obey the following approximate
expression [35]:

During the LMD process, the solidiﬁcation velocity V is
related to the scanning velocity and the angle between the
direction of the moving laser source and the growth
direction. The thermal gradient G is given by the temper-
ature ﬁeld generated by higher intensity heat source of the
laser, while G in the molten pool has been reported to be as
high as 10 6 /C176Cm -1 [34]. However, during the process of
traditional directional solidiﬁcation, the thermal gradient
G of the liquid–solid interface in the steady state is of the
order of 10 4 /C176Cm -1 [6]. According to the expression ( 2),
due to the higher value of G, the microstructure of Nb–
17Si–23Ti alloys fabricated by LMD has been reﬁned. In
addition, the Nb, Si and Ti powder particles were used as
starting materials in this study. The laser-induced in situ
reaction of the powder mixture could also reﬁne the
microstructure of the Nb–Si–Ti alloy [ 36]. Therefore, the
remarkably reﬁned Nb–17Si–23Ti alloys were achieved by
LMD with dual powder feeding method.
Comparing the microstructure of the samples prepared
using a laser power of 1000 W (Fig. 5a, b) and 2000 W
(Fig. 5c, d), it is clear that the higher laser power is ben-
eﬁcial to the microstructure reﬁnement. In the process of
laser scanning line by line, the input energy can be used to
deﬁne the line energy density g (J/m) as g ¼ P=v, where
the P is laser power and v is scanning rate. According to
this equation, in the case of constant scanning speed, the
increase in the laser power can enhance the energy density
and then raise the temperature of the Nb–Si–Ti molten
pool, implying that the thermal gradient G would become
steeper with the laser power increasing. The previous
results of numerical simulation showed that the thermal
gradient along the depth direction of the molten pool
increased considerably from 10.6 to 21.7 /C176C/lm as the
laser power was increased from 150 to 300 W during the
additive manufacturing of AlSi10 Mg alloys by selective
laser melting (SLM) [ 37]. Once again, according to the
expression ( 2), larger G values are helpful for the
microstructure reﬁnement, and hence, ﬁner microstructures
would be achieved by higher laser powers. In addition, the
segregation could be also restrained by the steeper thermal
gradient [38], so that the Ti-rich Nb
SS nearly disappeared
with the increase in the laser power.
Figure 5 shows that the increase in the laser power not
only decreased the size of Nb SS but also changed its
morphology. The dendritic morphology degraded when the
laser power increased. As known, the mode of solidiﬁca-
tion is determined by the ratio between the thermal gra-
dient ( G) over the solidiﬁcation velocity ( V), G/V, which
controls the stability of the solidiﬁcation front [ 35, 39]. For
a given alloy, the higher the value of G/V is, the smaller the
undercooling tendency at the front of the liquid–solid
interface is, so that the microstructure is changed from
dendritic to cellular and then to planar. Therefore, the
morphology of Nb
SS in samples A and B changed from
dendritic to near-equiaxed (see Fig. 5). Additionally,

4.2 Toughening Mechanism of the Laser-Deposited
Nb–17Si–23Ti Alloys
To understand the inﬂuence of the constituent phases on the
fracture toughness of the Nb–17Si–23Ti alloys developed by
LMD, the crack paths at the tips of hardness indentation
were observed, as shown in Fig. 7. The Nb
SS and (NbTi)3Si
phases show different behaviors during the penetration of
the hardness indenter, indirectly indicating their inﬂuence on
the bulk toughness. In most cases when the microcracks
meet the Nb
SS phase, they bridge (arrows B in Fig. 7), or
deﬂect (arrows D in Fig. 7), or are arrested by the Nb SS
phase (arrows A in Fig. 7). Whereas, for the (NbTi)3Si, the
cracks generally propagate directly throughout it. This sug-
gests that the NbSS phase plays a key role in preventing the
crack propagation in the as-deposited Nb–17Si–23Ti alloys.
However, the (NbTi)
3Si cannot effectively stop the crack
propagation, so that the fracture toughness of monophase
Nb3Si is commonly less than 3 MPa m 1/2 [41].
As shown in Fig. 6, the sample B fabricated using higher
laser power exhibits higher Ki than the sample A (14.1 vs.
12.3 MPa m1/2). This result should be attributed to the
following reasons. For the microstructure of sample B, as
shown in Fig. 5d, some of the (NbTi) 3Si particles are sur-
rounded by the ductile Nb SS, which is beneﬁcial to the
fracture toughness. Additionally, previous studies have
shown that the Nb–Si-based alloys with extremely reﬁned
Nb grains may lead to a transition of the failure mode of
Nb
SS from cleavage to dimple/tear [19]. Therefore, the ﬁner
microstructure of the as-deposited sample B should have
contributed to the improvement of Ki to some extent. Fur-
thermore, the content of the Ti dissolved in NbSS of sample
B is higher than that of sample A (34.29 vs. 29.67 at.%, see
Table 2). Theoretical calculations have demonstrated that Ti
can impart fracture toughness resistance in the NbSS and Nb-
based silicides by altering the crystal structure to promote
slip by partial dislocations, reducing the Peierls–Nabarro
barrier energy (energy for starting the slip of dislocation)
and increasing the c
s/UP–N ratio, where cs is the cleavage
surface energy and UP–N is the P–N barrier energy [ 8].
Hence, with higher dissolved Ti content, the Nb SS in the
sample B was considerably toughened, which undoubtedly
led to a lower propensity for cleavage fracture and a higher
fracture resistance of Nb than those of the sample A.

fully obtained by in situ reaction LMD. The microstructure
characteristics and mechanical properties of the alloys were
studied. The conclusions may be summarized as follows:
