abstract
Equiatomic TiPt exhibits very high transformation temperatures ( /C24 1300 K) from B2 to B19 orthorhombic
martensitic phase. However TiPt exhibits negligible ( /C24 0–1 1%) shape memory effect and low strength
(/C24 450 MPa) in martensite and very low strength ( /C24 20 MPa) in B2 phase region. Then in this study, we
compared the high temperature strength and shape memory properties of Ti –50Pt by partial substitution
of Platinum (Pt) belonging to group 10 of periodic table with Cobalt (Co) belonging to group 9 and
Ruthenium (Ru) belonging to group 8 of periodic table respectively. Partial substitution of Pt with Co and
Ru was found effective in improving the high temperature strength and shape memory properties of
Ti–50Pt alloy. However partial substitution of Pt with Ru was found more effective than Co due to more
effective solid solution strengthening effect. Ti –45Pt–5Co and Ti –45Pt–5Ru alloys have potential for high
temperature shape memory materials applications.

1. Introduction
High temperature shape memory alloys exhibit shape memory
effect above 373 K and these alloys have potential to be used for
thermal power plants, aircraft tur bine engines, high temperature
actuators, ﬂow control valves for oil and gas applications in energy
exploration, thermal protection and switching devices etc. [1,2].
Equiatomic TiPt, TiPd and TiAu are c ategorized as high temperature
shape memory alloys as these allo ys exhibit thermo-elastic B19
orthorhombic martensitic phase transformation from B2 (ordered
bcc) phase above 700 K [1,3]. Equiatomic TiPt exhibits thermo-
elastic martensitic phase transformation at about 1300 K, /C24 400 K
higher than equiatomic TiAu [3,1 1,13,14]and /C24 500 K higher than
equiatomic TiPd [1,3] alloys. In this regards, equiatomic TiPt can be
used for much higher temperature applications compared to equia-
tomic TiAu and equiatomic TiPd high temperature shape memory
alloys [1,3,4,1 1,13,14,15]. Our research group recently reported that
Ti–50Pt exhibits negligible (/C24 0–1 1%) shape memory effect due to low
critical stress for slip deformation compared to stress require for
martensitic transformation [4,5]. Our research group also reported
that Ti–50Pt exhibits single yielding phenomenon and low strength
(/C24 450 MPa) in martensite and very low strength ( /C24 20 MPa) in B2
phase region [4,6].S oa l t h o u g hT i–50Pt exhibits thermo-elastic
martensitic phase transformation, it cannot be used for high tem-
perature shape memory materials applications due to negligible shape
memory effect and very low strength in B2 phase region.
One way to improve the high temperature strength of Ti –50Pt
alloy is through solid solution strengthening effect [7,8,15].
Recently we reported that partial substitution of Ti and Pt with
Zr and Ru respectively were found effective in improving the high
temperature strength and shape memory properties of Ti –50Pt
alloy due to solid solution strengthening effect, resulting increase
in critical stress for slip deformation and dominant detwinned
martensitic transformation [8]. Ru belonging to group 8 of periodic
table has 8 valence electrons (4d
75S1). Both Ru and Pt belong to
platinum group metals (PGMs) [9]. Atomic radius of Ru (0.130 nm)
is smaller than Pt (0.135 nm) however atomic radius of Co
(0.135 nm) is same as that of Pt [10]. Rhodium (Rh), Iridium (Ir)
and Cobalt (Co) belong to same group 9 of periodic table. However,
Rh and Ir are PGMs and Co does not belong to PGMs [9]. Co has
9 valence electrons (3d 74s2). Melting point of Ru (2607 K) is higher
than Pt (2045 K) and melting point of Co (1768 K) is lower than Pt.
Density of Ru (12.4 g/cm 3) and Co (8.9 g/cm 3) is lower than Pt
(21.3 g/cm3). So partial substitution of Pt with Ru and Co is
expected to decrease the density of Ti –50Pt alloy. Objective of
this study was to investigate and compare the effects of partial
substitution of Pt belonging to group 10 of periodic table with Co
and Ru that belong to group 9 and 8 of periodic table respectively
on high temperature mechanical and shape memory properties of
Ti–50Pt alloy.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Phase analysis
Room temperature XRD analysis of Ti–45Pt–5Co and Ti–45Pt–5Ru
is given in Fig. 1. XRD analysis showed that both solution-treated
Ti–45Pt–5Co and Ti –45Pt–5Ru have B19 orthorhombic martensitic
phase structure at room temperature. Optical micrographs of solution
treated Ti–45Pt–5Co and Ti–45Pt–5Ru are given in Fig. 2. Fig. 2 again
conﬁrmed only twinned oriented martensitic phase structure at room
temperature. So from room temperature XRD and optical microscopy
of Ti –45Pt–5Co and Ti –45Pt–5Ru, both have twinned oriented B19
martensitic phase structure at room temperature.
3.2. Transformation temperatures
Average martensite start ( Ms), martensite ﬁnish ( Mf), austenite
start ( As), austenite ﬁnish ( Af) transformation temperatures of
Ti–50Pt [4],T i –45Pt–5Co and Ti –45Pt–5Ru [8] measured from
differential thermal analysis (DTA) are given in Fig. 3 .I tw a s
analyzed from Fig. 3 that transformation temperatures of Ti –50Pt
were decreased by partial substitution of Pt belonging to group 10
of periodic table with Co belonging to group 9 and Ru belonging to
group 8 of periodic table. This showed that both Co and Ru
belonging to group 9 and 8 of periodic table respectively are
acting as B2 phase stabilizer in Ti –50Pt alloy. Decrease in M
s and
Mf per atomic% partial substitution of Pt with Co and Ru remained
the same. However decrease in As and Af per atomic% partial
substitution of Pt with Ru was found higher than partial substitu-
tion of Pt with Co. So difference in transformation temperatures
between Af and Mf that was 94 K for Ti –50Pt was increased to
1 19 K for Ti –45Pt–5Ru and 148 K for Ti –45Pt–5Co alloy. So
temperature hysteresis ( ΔT¼ Af /C0 Mf) was increased by partial
substitution of Pt with Co and Ru in Ti –50Pt alloy.

compressive strength for 20% applied strain at 50 K below Mf
(martensitic phase) for Ti –50Pt [4],T i–45Pt–5Co and Ti –45Pt–5Ru
are given in Fig. 5. As given in Fig. 5, high temperature compressive
strength of Ti –50Pt [4] in martensite phase was increased by
partial substitution of Pt with Co and Ru. However partial
substitution of Pt belonging to group 10 of periodic table with
Ru belonging to group 8 of periodic table was found more effective
in improving the high temperature compressive strength of
Ti–50Pt than partial substitution of Pt with Co belonging to group
9 of periodic table. One reason for this was related to size mis ﬁt
parameter related to difference in atomic radii of solute and
solvent atoms [7]. Here Ru having atomic radii of 0.130 nm has
large size mis ﬁt parameter with Pt having atomic radii of 0.135 nm
compared to Co having atomic radii of 0.135 nm [10], resulting
dominant solid solution strengthening effect by partial substitu-
tion of Pt with Ru compared to partial substitution of Pt with Co.
Figs. 4 and 5 also showed that Ti –45Pt–5Co and Ti –45Pt–5Ru
exhibited double yielding phenomenon when compression tested
at 50 K below Mf. However as already reported by our research
group in Ref. [4,6],T i –50Pt exhibits single yielding phenomenon.
Double yielding phenomenon for Ti –45Pt–5Co and Ti –45Pt–5Ru
clearly showed the increase in critical stress for slip deformation
by partial substitution of Pt with Co and Ru in Ti –50Pt alloy.
Fig. 5 showed that difference between ﬁrst yielding and second
yielding (310 MPa) was higher for Ti –45Pt–5Ru compared to
Ti–45Pt–5Co (1 16 MPa) alloy. Double yielding is the characteristic
of shape memory alloys [8,12,15] where ﬁrst yielding in this case
corresponds to transformation of twinned oriented martensite
(Figs. 1 and 2 ) to detwinned martensite upon mechanical loading
and second yielding is related to permanent deformation due to
slip [12]. So large difference between ﬁrst yielding and second
yielding for Ti –45Pt–5Ru showed formation of large amount of
detwinned martensite compared to Ti –45Pt–5Co. Higher second
yield stress for Ti –45Pt–5Ru compared to Ti –45Pt–5Co also
showed higher stress required for plastic deformation due to
permanent slip deformation. On the other hand, lower value of
ﬁrst yield stress for Ti –45Pt–5Ru compared to Ti –45Pt–5Co also
showed that the twinned oriented martensite transformed to
detwinned martensite at low stress value due to increase in critical
stress for slip deformation that is related to more effective solid
solution strengthening effect due to large size mis ﬁt parameter [7]
between Pt and Ru compared to Pt and Co.
High temperature compressive stress –strain curves of Ti –45Pt–
5Co and Ti –45Pt–5Ru [8] for 20% applied strain at 50 K above
Af (B2 phase) are given in Fig. 6 . Results of yield stress and
compressive strength for 20% applied strain at 50 K above Af (B2
phase) of Ti –50Pt [4],T i –45Pt–5Co and Ti –45Pt–5Ru are given in
Fig. 7. As given in Fig. 7, high temperature compressive strength at
50 K above Af of Ti –50Pt [4] in B2 phase region was improved by
partial substitution of Pt with Ru and Co. However partial
substitution of Pt belonging to group 10 of periodic table with
Ru belonging to group 8 of periodic table was found more effective
in improving the high temperature strength of Ti –50Pt in B2 phase
region compared to partial substitution of Pt with Co that belongs
to group 9 of periodic table. Still high temperature compressive
strength of Ti –45Pt–5Co and Ti –45Pt–5Ru was found lower than
their respective strength values at 50 K below Mf in martensitic
phase region (compare Figs. 6 and 7 with Figs. 4 and 5 ). Further
research is in progress to improve the high temperature strength
of near equiatomic TiPt based alloys in B2 phase region.

Results of recovery ratio due to shape memory effect of Ti –50Pt
[5],T i–45Pt–5Co and Ti –45Pt–5Ru [8] are presented in Fig. 9. Fig. 9
clearly showed that the partial substitution of Pt belonging to
group 10 of periodic table with Co belonging to group 9 of periodic
table as well as partial substitution of Pt with Ru belonging to
group 8 of periodic table was found effective in improving the high
temperature shape memory properties of Ti –50Pt alloy. This
improvement was related to double yielding phenomenon for
Ti–45Pt–5Co and Ti –45Pt–5Ru compared to Ti –50Pt that exhibits
single yield phenomenon [4], resulting dominant detwinned
martensitic transformation upon mechanical loading (5% strain)
and dominant reverse transformation of detwinned martensite to
B2 phase upon heat-treatment above Af. Improvement of critical
stress for slip has resulted improvement of shape memory proper-
ties by partial substitution of Pt with Co and Ru. It was also
analyzed from Fig. 9 that Ti –45Pt–5Ru exhibited higher recovery
ratio due to shape memory effect (45%) compared to Ti –45Pt–5Co
that has 36% recovery ratio due to shape memory effect. This
higher recovery ratio for Ti –45Pt–5Ru was related to higher value
of second yielding (665 MPa), large difference between second
yielding and ﬁrst yielding (310 MPa) compared to Ti –45Pt–5Co
whose second yielding was at 523 MPa and difference between
second yielding and ﬁrst yielding was 1 16 MPa.
4. Conclusions
Effect of partial substitution of Pt belonging to group 10 of
periodic table with Co and Ru belonging to group 9 and 8 of
periodic table respectively on high temperature strength and
shape memory properties of Ti –50Pt was compared in this study.
High temperature strength and shape memory properties of
Ti–50Pt were improved by partial substitution of Pt with Ru as
well as with Co. However partial substitution of Pt belonging to
group 10 of periodic table with Ru belonging to group 8 of periodic
table was found more effective compared to partial substitution of
Pt with Co that belongs to group 9 of periodic table in improving
the high temperature strength and shape memory properties of
Ti–50Pt alloy due to dominant solid solution strengthening effect.
We still could not achieve 100% shape memory effect and further
research is in progress to achieve 100% shape memory effect and
to further improve the high temperature strength of near equia-
tomic TiPt alloy especially in B2 phase region.
