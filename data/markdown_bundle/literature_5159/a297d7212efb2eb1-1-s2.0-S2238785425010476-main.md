ABSTRACT
The evolutionary behavior of precipitated phases is a key determinant of strain aging cracking sensitivity in 
superalloys. In multi-component superalloys, these behaviors are influenced by pre-weld conditions and heat 
input. The strain aging cracking (SAC) sensitivity of the heat affected zone (HAZ) of IN-939 superalloy with 
various pre-weld heat treatment conditions was studied. The SAC sensitivity of the IN-939 superalloy samples 
was evaluated through the strain-to-fracture test (STF) by monitoring the ductility and fracture time during the 
test. The SAC sensitivity influencing factors including microstructure evolution, phase precipitation behavior of 
IN-939 superalloys were analyzed by electron probe micro analyzer (EPMA) 、 in-situ and exit-situ TEM test. The 
results showed that the SAC sensitivity of overaged IN-939 superalloys is smaller than that of the solid-solution 
condition showing larger ductility and longer fracture time during the STF test. The fast precipitation of γ ′ phase 
in the IN-939 superalloy HAZ during the STF test is responsible for the large SAC sensitivity of the solid-solution 
condition. The SAC sensitivity temperature range is well consistent with the fast precipitation temperature of γ ′ 
phase.
1. Introduction
IN939 superalloy is a γ ′ precipitation strengthened nickel-based su -
peralloy developed by International Nickel Company in the late 1960s 
[ 1 ]. The alloy has excellent high temperature properties and oxidation 
resistance and is widely used in industrial and shipbuilding gas turbine 
blades with operating temperatures up to 850 
◦
C [ 2 – 6 ]. As a 
nickel-based high-temperature alloy, IN-939 has an L1
2 
ordered 
face-centered cubic (FCC) superlattice structure with higher mass frac -
tions of Al and Ti in addition to the γ matrix, which provide precipitation 
and order hardening for interacts with each other [ 7 ]. However, the 
substantial γ ′ precipitation created shrinkage stresses increasing the 
cracking sensitivity of IN-939 during heat treatment. It is common to 
increase the amount of this precipitated phase by the regulated 
elemental content to achieve excellent strength in nickel-based high- -
temperature alloys, but it also limits their formability and weldability, 
where the addition of Al and Ti contributes to the formation of the 
precipitated phase and improves the high-temperature mechanical 
properties of the alloys. However, the addition of alloying elements in 
turn has a detrimental effect on weldability, as the occurrence of these 
precipitated phases is closely related to crack formation [ 8 ]. With the 
inherent high temperature capability, IN939 alloys are very attractive in 
manufacturing large complex structures such as engine cases to replace 
the IN718 alloy which is used in temperatures below 650 
◦
C. However, 
as a precipitation hardened superalloy, IN-939 alloy possesses poor 
weldability which limits its wide application. With the increase of γ ′ 
phase content, the weldability of the alloy deteriorates, especially the 
crack sensitivity of the welded joint is increased [ 9 ]. A large number of γ ′ 
forming phases, such as Al and Ti, lead to the formation of microcracks 
in superalloys, especially in the HAZ during the welding thermal cycle 
[ 10 ].
The weldability of IN-939 is one of the important reasons to limit the 
extensive use of the alloy. Shaw et al. [ 4 ] performed butt welding on 12 
mm cast IN-939 plates, and the creep strength of the welded joint 
reached 80 % of the base metal. Gonz ´alez et al. [ 9 ] studied the micro -

heat treatment states. The results showed that the crack tendency of 
aging at high temperature is smaller than that of aging at low temper -
ature. Arhami et al.[ 11 ] [ 12 ] used Ni – Cr – B amorphous solder as the 
interlayer for TLP welding of IN-939. The results showed that the proper 
welding thermal cycle can improve the microstructure of the welded

The high strength superalloys with γ ′ precipitation hardening often 
have the SAC in HAZ [14] . It is known that several factors contribute to 
weld defects, including material composition (Al and Ti content), grain 
size, pre and post-weld heat treatment (PWHT) conditions. The SAC 
especially occurs in the post-weld heat treatment or high temperature 
service of the precipitation strengthened superalloy welded joint. Some 
scholars conducted researches on the formation mechanism of superal -
loy SAC in detail. Hughes et al. [ 15 ] believed that the welding residual 
stress is too late to be released during solution treatment, coupled with 
the aging stress generated by the precipitation of the strengthening 
phase, which leads to the excessive HAZ stress and the generation of 
SAC. Fawley et al. [ 16 ] found that SAC formation could be avoided by 
controlling the precipitation rate of the strengthening phase. At the same 
time, the researchers also found that the SAC sensitivity of the alloy can 
be reduced by reducing the content of impurity elements [ 15 ], the grain 
size [ 4 ], the hardness of the alloy before welding [ 11 , 16 ], and the 
welding heat input [ 17 ].
It is of great significance to study the crack sensitivity of IN-939 HAZ 
and its influencing factors. Nevertheless, the information available in 
the literature regarding the effect of PWHT and heat input on the 
microstructure and crack sensitivity of IN-939 HAZ is so scarce. In this 
paper, the SAC sensitivity of HAZ was studied by stress to fracture test 
based on the Gleeble system, and the microstructure of HAZ was studied. 
The influence of SAC sensitivity on pre-weld heat treatment and heat 
input was analyzed.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructure evolution
IN-939 is a precipitation-strengthened alloy. If the alloy is welded in 
a state of high hardness, its crack sensitivity will increase significantly 
[ 11 ]. Previous studies [ 18 ] have found that IN-939 has great weldability 
in the overaged state. The purpose of overaging is to reduce the hardness 
of the alloy and to release the residual stress generated in the welding 
process, thus reducing the crack sensitivity of HAZ.
According to the research of Brody et al. [ 18 ], the overaging treat -
ment process of IN-939 was determined as follows: holding at 1160 
◦
C 
for 4h after solution heat treatment, air cooling to room temperature, 
and then holding at 1050 
◦
C for 20h after furnace cooling to room 
temperature. The microstructure of IN-939 after solution and overaging 
treatment were observed, as shown in Fig. 4 a and b, respectively. Fig. 4 c 
shows the morphology of the η phase precipitated along the grain 
boundary of the overaged alloy. And Fig. 4 d shows the energy spectrum 
of the η phase.
Comparing Fig. 4 a and b, the microstructure of IN-939 at solution 
treatment and overaging treatment is significantly different. The main 
precipitations of the solutionized alloy are MC carbides. While the 
overaged alloy precipitates rough spherical γ ′ phase and aciculate η 
phase along the grain boundary. The depleted area of γ ′ phase can be 
observed at the interface between the η phase and the matrix, and some 
fine particles of γ ′ phase are attached to the side near the η phase. The 
precipitates free zone (PFZ) of the γ ′ phase is generated during the 
precipitation and growth of the η phase. When the γ ′ phase transforms 
into the η phase, the γ ′ phase near the η phase dissolves gradually, and 
then the PFZ of γ ′ phase is formed around the η phase. The EDS result in 
Fig. 4 d shows that the main elements of η phase are Ni and Ti, which are 
as same as γ ′ phase.
In order to further analyze the microstructure of IN-939 in two states, 
TEM was used to observe the microstructure in two states. The results 
are shown in Fig. 5 . Fig. 5 a shows the microstructure of solutionized 
alloy. As can be seen from Fig. 5 a, besides MC carbides, the solutionized 
alloy also precipitates small spherical γ ′ phase about 80 nm. Fig. 5 b 
shows the γ ′ phase morphology of the overaged alloy. The γ ′ phase size of 
IN-939 after overaging treatment is larger than the matrix.
The γ ′ phase in the solutionized alloy precipitates in the air cooling 
process. Due to the high content of γ ′ phase-forming elements in IN-939, 
the cooling rate of air cooling is slower than that of water cooling, so it is 
difficult to completely inhibit the precipitation of γ ′ phase in the alloy. 
Therefore, γ ′ phase will still precipitate in the solid solution state.

4. Conclusion
In this paper, the Gleeble testing machine was used to simulate the 
solutionized state and the overaged state of IN-939 HAZ, and the 
microstructure was characterized respectively. The SAC sensitivity of 
the alloy under different conditions was studied by the STF test. The 
following conclusions can be drawn from this work. 
1. The SAC sensitivity of the solutionized alloy is larger than that of the 
overaged alloy. The plasticity and fracture time of IN-939 in the 
solutionized state is smaller than that in the overaged state in the STF 
test.
2. In the simulated HAZ test, the microstructure of the solutionized 
alloy is basically unchanged, while the overaged alloy will undergo γ ′ 
phase and η phase back dissolution. The higher the peak tempera -
ture, the greater the degree of resolution. The γ ′ phase near the grain 
boundary is mainly dissolved by eutectic liquefaction, while the γ ′ 
phase inside the grain is dissolved by local dissolution. The η phase is 
dissolved back by partial dissolution.
3. In the STF test, the plasticity and fracture time of IN-939 have the 
lowest values in the temperature range of 850 
◦
C – 950 
◦
C, indicating 
that the SAC sensitivity is greater in this temperature range. The 
plasticity and fracture time of the overaged IN-939 decrease with the 
increase of the peak temperature of the thermal cycle.
4. The crack susceptibility of the alloy is influenced by the precipitated 
phase, and the acceleration of the γ ′ phase re-solution rate, the res -
olution of the η phase to precipitate the γ ′ phase and the trans -
formation of the carbides are all conducive to the decreased crack 
susceptibility.
