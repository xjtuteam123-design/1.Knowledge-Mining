ABSTRACT
High-Nb TiAl alloy is a preferred material for high-temperature applications due to its light weight and excellent 
mechanical properties. However, its inherent brittleness limits its machinability and wide range of applications. 
To overcome the obstacle of low machinability of TiAl alloys, a strategy of grain refinement and oxygen removal 
has been utilized to simultaneously increase the strength and plasticity of the alloys. In this paper, for the first 
time, an appropriate amount of LaB
6 
was introduced to the powder metallurgy TiAl alloy, and high-Nb TiAl- 
based composites with good strength and plasticity containing La
2
O
3 
and (Ti, Nb)B ceramic phases were syn -
thesized and prepared in situ by hot-press sintering. The results show that the coarse lamellar colony of 346.3 μ m 
was drastically refined to 71.2 μ m after the addition of 0.10 at.% LaB
6
. Accordingly, the ultimate tensile strength 
(UTS) rose by 26 % to 609 MPa. The fracture strain increment was 153 %, from 0.40 % to 1.01 %. This study 
presents an efficient method to achieve high-performance particle-reinforced toughened TiAl-based composite 
parts by rapid sintering using high-oxygen TiAl pre-alloyed powders.
1. Introduction
Titanium aluminides (TiAl) alloy has a low density (density is only 
about 50 % of nickel-based high-temperature alloys), good oxidation 
resistance, excellent high-temperature mechanical properties, as well as 
good creep resistance, widely used in aerospace and automotive fields 
[ 1 – 5 ]. Casting is a cost-effective method of producing TiAl alloys; its 
primary disadvantages are poor machinability, severe segregation, 
coarse microstructure, and limited ambient temperature plasticity, all of 
which strongly inhibit the extensive applications for TiAl alloys [ 6 – 8 ].
To overcome these disadvantages, a variety of microstructure con -
ditioning methods and processing methods, including hot deformation 
(forging, rolling, and extrusion) [ 9 – 13 ], additive manufacturing 
[ 14 – 16 ], powder metallurgy [ 17 – 19 ], and compositing [ 20 – 25 ], have 
been developed. Among these, powder metallurgy-prepared TiAl alloys 
have the advantages of non-segregation and fine uniform microstruc -
ture, excellent mechanical properties, and the near-net-forming tech -
nology of powder metallurgy overcomes the difficulties of TiAl 
processability [ 26 , 27 ]. This method is a very promising development 
direction for TiAl alloys at present. However, for a long time in the past, 
powder metallurgy methods were only used for sintering in the 
temperature range below the α -phase region to prepare fine and uniform 
near- γ , double phase, near-lamellar microstructures [ 28 – 30 ]. This is 
because, when held at the temperature of the α -phase area for a brief 
duration, the α grains develop quickly, forming a highly coarse lamellar 
microstructure ( > 300 μ m) upon cooling and having poor room tem -
perature mechanical properties [ 31 ]. However, lamellar is the preferred 
microstructure in the application of TiAl alloy because of its superior 
high-temperature mechanical properties [ 32 ]. Therefore, it is impera -
tive to prepare TiAl alloys with fine and uniform lamellar 
microstructure.
Recently, some studies have shown that composite technology 
applied to TiAl alloys can refine the lamellar microstructure and take full 
advantage of the excellent properties of the reinforcement, which is 
favorable for the performance of TiAl alloys [ 27 , 33 – 35 ]. For instance, 
Sun et al. [ 34 ] discovered that network-structured TiAl/boride com -
posites reduced lamellar colony size from 347 μ m to 53 μ m while 
increasing tensile strength by 200 MPa at ambient temperature over the 
TiAl matrix. Ti
2
AlC/TiAl composites with a hierarchical structure 
exhibit superior room and high temperature compressive performances 
than TiAl alloys, according to Liu et al. [ 35 ]. However, even though the 
lamellar microstructure and the compressive properties of TiAl were

Therefore, the design of TiAl-based composites with room-temperature

Normally, the lack of room-temperature ductility of TiAl alloys is 
partly attributed to the impurity content, especially the high oxygen 
content, so the removal of oxygen can further increase the plasticity of 
TiAl alloys [ 36 – 38 ]. Similarly, the refined microstructure facilitates the 
enhancement of the strength and deformation capacity of the TiAl alloy 
[ 39 ]. Some researchers have introduced rare earth elements and grain 
refining elements in the preparation of powder metallurgical titanium 
alloys. Rare earths are used to remove oxygen from the matrix using 
their oxyphilic properties, and grain refining elements such as B ele -
ments are used to pin the interface. The resulting rare earth oxides and 
B-rich precipitation phases can also play a role in strengthening the 
matrix [ 40 – 43 ]. Zhang et al. [ 41 ] showed that when LaB

Notably, Fang et al. [ 42 ] and Huang et al. [ 43 ] added LaB
6 
to casting and 
3D printing TiAl alloys, which optimized its room-temperature

ticity. However, the tensile properties were not given. Thus, it is vital to

In this work, we have innovatively fabricated TiAl-based composites 
by introducing LaB
6 
particles into Ti-47.7Al-7.8Nb alloys using powder 
metallurgy. The introduction of B elements refines the grains, and the 
rare earth La elements scavenges the oxygen in the matrix, which re -
inforces the TiAl-based composite and optimizes its deformability. 
Nano/micron borides and lanthanum oxides were implanted in the 
composites by the LaB
6

phases greatly optimized the sintered microstructure of TiAl-based 
composites and significantly improved their mechanical properties. At 
ambient temperature, the strong plastic mismatch problem of TiAl- 
based composites was overcome.

4. Discussion
In TiAl-LaB
6 
composites, the mechanism for the enhancement of 
strength and deformation capacity consists of three aspects. Firstly, the 
mechanism of twin strengthening and toughening. Previous studies [ 10 ,
57 ] have shown that the strengthening of TiAl alloys is owing to twin -
ning during cold deformation, which provides new interfaces that can 
impede dislocation motion. Twinning is similar to refining grain size, 
which helps to increase plasticity [ 4 , 10 ]. As illustrated in Fig. 5 , the 
presence of LaB
6 
promotes the increase of γ grains in TiAl/LaB
6 
com -
posites compared to TiAl-0LaB
6 
alloy. Generally, the cold deformation of 
TiAl alloys takes place mainly through dislocations and deformation 
twins in γ grains. Therefore, the increased γ grains in TiAl-LaB
6 
com -
posites provide more deformation sites for cold deformation and further 
increase the number of γ deformation twins as displayed in Fig. 10 (a – c), 
which strengthening and toughening the TiAl alloy. In addition, the 
added La element reacts in the matrix to form La
2
O
3
, which acts as an 
oxygen scavenger and facilitates deformation twinning, contributing to 
the increased plasticity of the alloy [ 58 – 60 ]. Consequently, TiAl-LaB
6 
composites have a higher tendency to develop deformation twins than 
the TiAl-0LaB
6 
alloy, contributing to their strength and plasticity.
Secondly, precipitate phase reinforcement. Fig. 10 (d) shows (Ti, Nb)

phases act as significant impediments to dislocation motion.

5. Conclusions
TiAl-based composites possessing both room temperature strength 
and plasticity were successfully fabricated by powder metallurgy. An in- 
depth study on how the introduction of LaB
6 
affects the mechanical 
performance and microstructural evolution of the TiAl-based compos -
ites was carried out. The following are the primary conclusions: 
(1) The coarse lamellar structure of the TiAl matrix is refined into 
fine near-lamellar and double phase microstructures by the 
addition of LaB
6
. Granular La
2
O
3 
reinforcements as well as some 
needle-like (Ti, Nb)B reinforcements are created when LaB
6 
re -
acts with the oxygen in the matrix.
(2) The grain refinement of boron and the oxygen scavenging action 
of LaB
6 
greatly enhance the room-temperature tensile ductility of 
TiAl matrix. The plasticity enhancement can be attributed mostly 
to increased dislocation and twinning activity as well as grain 
refining. The strain at fracture is increased by 153 % with a 
moderate addition of 0.10 at.% LaB
6
. More LaB
6 
addition into the 
TiAl matrix can improve the strength, but too high a content leads 
to premature fracture.
(3) This result overcomes the deterioration of TiAl alloy micro -
structures and properties induced by high sintering temperatures 
and long sintering times and highlights the potential of hot press 
sintering to rise the strength and deformation capacity of TiAl- 
based composites.
