Research results are presented on the KhN65KVMYuTB production alloy containing various Al and Ti 
contents" from the Elektrostal' plant. It is shown that it possible to significantly improve a number of service 
properties (short term strength, fatigue resistance and long term strength)for the KhN65KVMYuTB alloy that 
contains about 2.5% orAl and Ti, while preserving the other properties, particularly the hot ductility, at a 
satisfactory level. 
The excellent high temperature strength of the nickel-based deformable KhN65KVMYuTB (I~K78) alloy [1] that has 
been tested in metallurgical production is achieved by means of balanced alloying the nickel-chromium matrix with molyb- 
denum, tungsten, cobalt, niobium, aluminum, titanium, and hafnium [2, 3]. The basic strengthening phase for this alloy, as 
for all heat-resistant nickel-based alloys, is an intermetallic 3/-phase compound of the (Ni, Co, Cr)3 (A1, Ti, Nb) type. The 
amount of this compound depends on the total concentration of aluminum and titanium in the alloy. The total for these 
elements is 3 to 5% in the KhN65KVMYuTB alloy specification composition. As a rule, melting charge calculations are 
based on the mean specification concentration for each alloying element for serial metallurgical production of heat resistant 
nickel alloys. The research results show that when the aluminum plus titanium concentration in the alloy is at the upper 
specification level, an increase of ~20% is possible in certain of the service properties, while retaining the suitability for hot 
working at a suitable level. 
Table 1 shows the basic alloying element contents in the KhN65KVMYuTB alloy at the mean and upper specification 
composition levels. 
The alloy properties were determined on specimens cut from 115-mm diameter extruded bars, that were then heat 
treated* by heating to 1150°C for 3 h, cooled in air, heated to 800°C for 20 h, and cooled in air.

Results from low cycle fatigue testing* are given in Fig. 4 in the form of a cyclic strain diagram at 500, 650, and 
750°C, and indicate that the alloys with various total aluminum plus titanium contents have almost the same high deformation 
resistance (A~ = 0.5-0.6% on a basis of 10,000 cycles) which corresponds to the requirements for potential materials for 
turbine blades used in gas turbine installations. 
Tensile testing was carried out on a MTS machine at 1000-1250°C and a 5000 mm/min deformation rate. The 
mechanical properties of the test alloy containing various total aluminum plus titanium contents were virtually the same 
within the hot working temperature region (Fig. 5). It should noted that the alloy with ~;AI + Ti = 5 % exhibits a somewhat 
increased resistance to plastic deformation, particularly, at lower temperatures (1000-1100°C). 
Thus, it is not always necessary to develop a new alloy and the associated production technology in the search for a 
material with specific properties. This is demonstrated by the example of the KhN65KVMYuTB alloy. The proven metallur- 
gical production technology for this alloy with an upper alloying elements content enables a level of properties to be achieved 
that is better in relation to the properties for an alloy having a mean specification chemical composition. 
Conclusions 1. The KhN65KVMYuTB alloy has properties that are sensitive to variations in the chemical com- 
position within the limits of the turbine installations standard. 
2. The mechanical properties of the alloy with an upper level for the aluminum plus titanium content are 20% better 
than the corresponding properties for the alloy with a mean specification content for these alloys.
