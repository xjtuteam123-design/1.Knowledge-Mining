Mechanical Properties of Dispersion-Hardened P/M Al-Mn-Cu-Mg-Zn
Alloys at Elevated Temperature

Keywords: Al-Cu-Mg-Mn-Zn alloy, Mechanical properties at elevated temperature. Powder
Metallurgy, Rapid solidification, Intermetallic compound.
Abstract. In order to improve the high-temperature strength of an Al-Cu-Mg alloy, Mn was added at
supersaturation to form a high-density dispersion of an intermetallic phase. In the P/M Al-3.6Mn-
6.4Cu-3.6Zn-1.7Mg alloy (mass%), rod-like Al-Mn-Cu-Zn quaternary intermetallic phases (Q phase)
several hundred nanometers in length were dispersed in the matrix. The chemical composition of the
Q phase was determined by TEM/EDX to be 78.8Al-12Mn-8Cu-1.2Zn (at%). The crystal system,
space group, and lattice parameters of the unit cell were identified to be orthorhombic, Cmcm and a =
0.76, b = 2.11, c = 1.25 nm, respectively, by Rietveld analysis. Since the matrix of the alloy obtained
was of the Al-Cu-Mg-(Zn) system, age-hardening occurred by formation of a GPB zone at room
temperature and 448 K. At the peak level of age-hardening at room temperature, the tensile strength
at room temperature was 704 MPa, and the elongations were 8.0%. The high temperature strengths at
523 and 573 K were 319 and 141 MPa, respectively, and the elongations were 17 and 34%,
respectively.
Introduction
Many recent studies have attempted to improve the heat resistance of aluminum alloys in order to
enable their use as engine parts. In one technique, the intermetallic phase is abundantly dispersed by
adding a transition metal (TM) or rare earth metal in supersaturation utilizing powder metallurgy
(P/M) [1]. Since the diffusion velocities and solubilities of TM in Al alloys are very low, Al-TM
intermetallic phases generally have high thermal stability at elevated temperature. For example,
Al3Ni, Al6Fe, Al6Mn, Al9Co2, Al7Cr and Al3Ti particles formed by adding Ni, Fe, Mn, Co, Cr and Ti,
respectively, are utilized for increasing high-temperature strength [1]. However, there are few cases
that employed a higher-than-ternary multicomponent phase.
In the P/M Al-Zn-Mg-Cu-Mn alloy, with 4 mass% Mn added in supersaturation, a high-density
dispersion of the rod-like Al-Mn-Cu-Zn quaternary intermetallic phase (Q phase) is formed [2]. Since
this phase can be dispersed more finely than the binary phase Al6Mn, it is possible to strengthen the
alloy without a large reduction in ductility. The present study attempted to develop an alloy with
higher elevated temperature strength by dispersing this Q phase in an Al-Cu-Mg alloy with good
mechanical properties at about 473 K.

Tensile tests at room temperature were carried out at a strain rate of 3x10-4 s-1 with a Shimadzu
AG-50kNI. Tensile properties at 523 and 573 K were determined with a Shimadzu AG-100kND in
accordance with JIS-G0567. Hardness tests were carried out using an Akashi micro-Vickers hardness

equipped with an EDAX detector. TEM samples were prepared by a twin jet electro-polisher with a
HNO3:methanol = 3:1 solution at 243 K and 10 V. SEM observation was performed with a JEOL
JSM-6500F at 5 kV. XRD measurements were performed by a Rigaku RAD diffractometer using
Cu-Kα radiation, and the collected data were analyzed by using the Rietveld method with RIETAN-
2000 software to identify the crystal structure and the lattice parameter of the Q phase [4]. The
electrical conductivity was measured by a Hocking NDT Autosigma 3000DL tester; these
measurements were converted into thermal conductivity values using the Wiedemann-Frantz law.

This is presumably because, as in the initial design, the matrix of alloy No. 1 consists of Al solid
solution and that of alloys Nos. 2 and 3 consist of the Al-Cu-Mg system. The hardness of alloy No. 3
always shows a hardness that is 15 HV higher than that of No. 2 alloy at any given aging condition. It
is thought that the difference in hardness originates from the difference in the amount of dispersed
Mn intermetallic phase because the age-hardening behavior of alloys Nos. 2 and 3 is almost the same.
The amount of Mn intermetallic phase in alloy No. 3 is thought to be 1.2 times higher than that in
alloy No. 2 because the solubility of Mn in the aluminum alloy at both aging temperatures is nearly
zero and the amount of Mn addition is also 1.2 times larger. Upon aging at 448 K, the hardness of
alloy No. 1 tends to decrease slightly. This seems to be due to grain coarsening.

The tensile strength exceeds 300 MPa even in alloy No. 1, in which the precipitation hardening
phase is absent. This shows that the degree of dispersion strengthening of the Mn intermetallic phase
is very large. In alloys Nos. 2 and 3, in which precipitation hardening is observed in addition to
dispersion strengthening, the tensile strength exceeds 600 MPa. A tensile strength that exceeds 700
MPa is obtained in the case of alloy No. 3, temper T6.
For alloys Nos. 2 and 3, as is the case for the hardness, the strength for T6 temper is lower than that
for T4 temper by about 30 MPa. In contrast, the thermal conductivity for T6 temper is higher than that
for T4 temper by about 30%. These results imply that the precipitation hardening phase in the alloy
for T6 temper is more coarsely and more roughly dispersed than that in the alloy for T4 temper.

Microstructure
Fig. 2 shows the SEM image of alloy No. 3 for T4 temper. The rod-like Mn intermetallic phase,
which appears as white particles in the image, is aligned in the extrusion direction. This is assumed to
be the Q phase, which has been reported to precipitate in P/M Al-Zn-Mg-Cu-Mn alloys [2]. Most of
the Q phase particles are fine and about 500 nm in length. However, there are also relatively coarse Q
phase particles that are about 2 µm in length; these are assumed to be dispersed along the grain
boundaries. For alloys Nos. 1 and 2, in which the additive contents are low, the Q phase particles are
finer and a few hundred nm in length.

By TEM/EDX analysis, the average composition of the Q phase is determined to be about

Al-Zn-Mg-Cu-Mn alloys, which contains 4 at% Zn [2]. Thus, the matrix contains more Zn (~ 1.6

TEM/EDX results suggest that in the Q phase, Zn dissolves into the T1 phase (Al20Mn3Cu2),
which precipitates in the Al-Mn-Cu ternary system. The space group and point group of the T1 phase
is reported to be orthorhombic and Cmcm [5], respectively. Structure identification of the Q phase
was carried out on the basis of these structural data on T1 phase by using the Rietveld method. Fig. 3
shows the results. When the space group, point group, and lattice parameters were taken to be
orthorhombic, Cmcm, and a = 0.76, b = 2.11, c = 1.25 nm, respectively, almost all peaks were
successfully fitted.
The melting point of the T1 phase is reported to be 1293 K, which is much higher than that of
Al6Mn, namely 931 K [6]. Thus, since the degree of overcooling and the driving force for
precipitation is much larger than in the case of Al6Mn, the frequency of nucleation is much higher. As
a result, a high-density dispersion of a finer Q phase can be formed.

Fig. 4 shows the selected area electron diffraction (SAED) patterns of alloy No. 3 for T4 and T6
temper, respectively. SAED patterns are taken roughly along the <100>Al zone axis. As indicated

rod-like GPB zones extending along the <100> directions in this matrix are thought to form as a
result of T4 and T6 temper [7]. Although the matrix contains about 1.6% Zn, no diffraction spots
from the metastable phase that precipitates in the Al-Zn and Al-Zn-Mg alloy systems were

The particle size was defined as the diameter of the circle that has the same area as the area of Q phase
particle calculated on the basis of SEM images. During the 100-h anneal at 573 K, the Q phase
particles hardly grow, which shows that this phase has high thermal stability. However, XRD
analysis revealed the presence of Al2Cu and Al2CuMg formed upon annealing. Evidently, the GPB
zone, which formed as a result of T4 temper, dissolved at 573 K. This indicates that the large decrease
in tensile strength at 573 K is attributable to the decrease in the degree of precipitation hardening due
to the dissolution of the GPB zone.
Summary
In order to improve the strength of Al-Cu-Mg alloys, in particular the strength at elevated temperature,
a dispersion of an Al-Mn-Cu-Zn quaternary intermetallic phase was formed. The microstructure and
mechanical properties of the P/M Al-Mn-Cu-Mg-Zn alloys developed were investigated by using
several techniques.
Upon the addition of Mn at supersaturation, a fine rod-like intermetallic phase (Q phase), whose
average composition was 78.8Al-12Mn-8Cu-1.2Zn (mass%), was formed. The crystal structure of
the Q phase was identified as orthorhombic, Cmcm, and a = 0.76, b = 2.11, c = 1.25 nm by the
Rietveld method. Since the melting point of the Q phase is much higher than that of Al6Mn, a high-
density dispersion of submicron-sized Q phase particles can easily be formed. In the matrix, GPB
zones formed upon T4 and T6 temper. These zones have the same structure as the phase that
precipitated in the Al-Cu-Mg alloy.
Through a combination of dispersion strengthening of the Q phase and precipitation hardening of
the GPB zone, the strength of the P/M Al-3.6Mn-6.4Cu-3.6Zn-1.7Mg alloy (mass%) at 523 K was
improved to 319 MPa with sufficient ductility.
