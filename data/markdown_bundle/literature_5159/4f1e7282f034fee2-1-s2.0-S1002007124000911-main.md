ABSTRACT
To obtain a high-entropy alloy characterized by high strength and plasticity, (NiCoV) 100-xGax (x ¼ 0, 5, 7) was
successfully prepared, cold-rolled, and heat-treated. The microstructure was analyzed to correlate Ga content with
the performance of the system. The addition of Ga can produce alloying effects, including solid solution
strengthening effect, second phase precipitation strengthening effect, and layer misalignment energy reduction
effect. The experimental results show adding Ga elements can enrich Ni, Co, V, and Ga above the grain bound-
aries, causing the alloy to produce annealed twins inside. The alloy is strengthened mainly by precipitation, and
the formation of the precipitation phase effectively enhances the strength of the alloy. The low stacking fault
energy promotes the toughening of NiCoV but makes the plasticity of the alloy decrease. Still, the formation of
annealed twins effectively increases the plasticity, which makes the alloy harder but does not reduce the plasticity
too much. By comparing the experimental properties, (NiCoV)
93Ga7 showed the best mechanical properties at the
annealing temperature of 900 /C14 C, yield strength, tensile strength and elongation of 906 MPa, 1321 MPa and
21.36 %, respectively.
1. Introduction
High-performance materials are an important material basis for the
progress and development of human society. The traditional approach to
design alloy is to mix the major elements of one or two metals with
known properties, and then add minor elements to improve their prop-
erties. Yeh et al. and Cantor broke this mindset by designing HEAs. HEAs
typically have ﬁve or more primary elements with equal or near-equal
atomic concentrations, ranging from 5 % to 35 % of each element [ 1,
2]. The high entropy effect, slow diffusion effect, severe lattice distortion
effect and cocktail effect have been shown to play an important role in
the properties of HEAs [ 1,3–7]. One of the widely cited “core effects” is
the high entropy effect, which gives them single-phase stability, typically
represented by solid solutions of FCC or BCC solid solutions [ 8,9]. In
addition to the existing de ﬁnitions of high entropy alloys, there are still
some alloys that do not fall within these de ﬁnitions that are also referred
to as high entropy alloys [ 10]. This method deﬁnes whether the alloy has
high entropy by the magnitude of the mixing entropy [ 5,11]. This ex-
pands the scope of the de ﬁnition of high entropy alloys.
HEAs have many excellent properties, such as good antioxidant
properties [12,13], thermal stability [14], high temperature strength [15,
16], good fracture toughness [17–20], good corrosion resistance [21]and
excellent electrical conductivity [ 5]. This is why we are currently
exploring and studying high entropy alloys.
Recently, the team of Xiaogang Li [ 22] from the University of Science
and Technology Beijing discovered the medium-entropy alloy of NiCoV.
The tensile strength of the alloy is up to 1100 MPa, and the strain is about
85 %. Moreover, the low diffusivity of hydrogen and the deformation
twins that hinder crack propagation make the alloy remarkably resistant
to hydrogen corrosion. Furthermore, Nutor et al. [23] studied the entropy
alloy in NiCoV and found that the alloy was a completely disordered
lattice structure with a high layer fault energy and signi ﬁcant char-
ge/electron transfer between V and Co/Ni atoms [ 37–39]. Moreover, the
strain-hardening ability was improved at low temperatures so that the
strength and plasticity of CoNiV increased with the decrease of defor-
mation temperature below 4.2K. On the basis of the extremely high
fracture toughness of entropy alloy in NiCoV, Ga, Ni, and V elements
were enriched and precipitated at grain boundaries by adding a Ga
element to entropy alloy in NiCoV to improve the tensile strength of the
alloy. This is because the addition of Ga is bene ﬁcial for improvement of
the solid solution strengthening effect of the alloy and the second phase
precipitation strengthening effect. It also helps reduce the layer fault

2. Experimental details
2.1. Alloy design & preparation
The (NiCoV)100-XGax (x ¼ 0, 5, 7) series alloys (purity >99.9 %) were
prepared by arc melting furnace. Each sample was melted four times and
turned over before the next melting process to ensure chemical homo-
geneity. The resulting samples were cold rolled to approximately 20 % of
the original size and then cut with a wire cutter to obtain ﬂat dog bone-
style samples (length: 35 mm; width: 3 mm). Small squares of dimensions
10 mm /C2 10 mm were formed. The resulting drawn samples and squares
were annealed at 900 /C14 C, 950 /C14 C, and 1000 /C14 C for 1 h.
2.2. Microstructure characterization
The surface of the small square was polished with sandpaper from low
to high grinding to 2500 grain. The surface was etched with aqua regia
solution, the surface was rinsed with water immediately after etching for
10 s, and then the surface was cleaned with alcohol. Finally, the sample
microstructure was observed with XP-550 polarizing microscope made
by MikeVision. Experimental testing and analysis of the alloy specimens
were carried out with a D8 Advanced X-ray diffractometer from BRUKER,
Germany. The equipment was used to characterize the samples of the
prepared samples under a CuK α radiation source with a tube voltage of
40 kV, a scanning speed of 5 /C14 /min and a scanning range of 20 –80/C14 . After
polishing the surface of the tensile sample, the alloy specimen was tested
with a Zwick/Roell Z2050 universal material testing machine with a
tensile strain rate of 0.1 mm/s. The width and thickness of the tensile
specimen were measured with vernier calipers. The tensile fracture was
observed by a Quanta 200 scanning electron microscope (SEM), and the
microstructure of the alloy was analyzed by EBSD analysis. The samples
for TEM were ground to particles of approximate size 60 μm using coarse
sandpaper. It was put into a pitter to produce the samples of the size of
approximately 30 μm, and ﬁnally it was processed using an ion thinning
instrument and tested with HRTEM made in Japan. Elemental analysis of
alloys is done mainly with APOLLO X type energy spectrum analysis
(EDS) made by AMETEK.

ARS9000 digital micro Vickers hardness tester to test the local resis-
tance of the experimental model to the hardness of the alloy surface. The

3.2. Mechanical properties
Fig. 9 shows the tensile properties of (NiCoV) 100-xGax high entropy

after break for the alloy with 7 % Ga and 5 % Ga are 21.36 % and 23.71

increases with the addition of Ga elements. The strength of the alloy

elimination of the internal work hardening and stresses due to the in-
crease in annealing temperature, resulting in a gradual decrease in the
hardness of alloy specimens. This is also consistent with the results of

4. Conclusion
(1) The (NiCoV) 100-XGax series medium/high entropy alloy has a
stable face-centered cubic crystal structure, and the alloy still re-
tains a single face-centered cubic structure after 80 % cold rolling
and subsequent annealing and water cooling. It can be concluded
that (NiCoV)100-XGax high entropy alloy exhibits strong structural
stability.
(2) After annealing, the yield strength and tensile strength of
NiCoV100-XGax alloys increase signi ﬁcantly. With the continuous
addition of the Ga element, the strength of the alloy increases, but
the elongation after fracture decreases signi ﬁcantly. The tensile
fracture form is always the ductile fracture with many dimples.
The strength of the alloy sample decreases, as the plasticity in-
creases. This is because the annealing temperature gradually
eliminates the work stress and work hardening in the alloy
sample.
(3) According to TEM characterization results obtained for the
(NiCoV)100-XGax series high-entropy alloys, the precipitated NiC-
oVGa phase and the annealing twin structures exist in the alloy.
During the tensile test, NiCoVGa precipitated phase accelerates
the expansion of tensile crack and reduces the plasticity of alloy.
The annealing twins in the alloy prevent the extension of tensile
crack to a certain extent, so that the plasticity of the alloy
increases.
(4) Ga can effectively improve the solid solution strengthening effect
of the alloy, the second phase precipitation intensi ﬁes the effect,
and reduce the layer fault effect.
