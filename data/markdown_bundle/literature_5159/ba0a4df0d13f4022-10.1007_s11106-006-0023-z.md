INFLUENCE OF SILICIDE COATINGS ON THE MECHANICAL 
PROPERTIES OF THE NT-50 ALLOY 
P. I. Glushko, V. I. Savchenko, V. F. Trofimov, 
and B. M. Shirokov 
UDC 539.3:621.793 
We have investigated the effect of silicide coatings on the ultimate strength of NT-50 alloy over the 
temperature range −196…+1100°C. From 196 to 800 °C the ultimate strength of silicidated specimens 
was lower than that of the initial and annealed alloy whereas from 900°C it was slightly higher. We have 
also examined the effect of silicid e coatings on the relative elongation of NT-50 alloy samples. Over the 
temperature range −196…+1100°C, the relative elongation of specimens annealed in a vacuum of 
6.7 ⋅ 10−3 Pa at 1250 °C for 4 h was found to be lower than that of the silicidated specimens. This is 
attributed to the dissolution of the residual gases (O2, N2, H2, CO, and CO2) under vacuum annealing. 
Keywords: silicide, ultimate strength, coating, relative elongation, alloy, temperature, annealing. 
INTRODUCTION 
Because of its fairly high specific strength, producibility, and other physicochemical properties, niobium-
titanium alloys are of interest as a structural material for high-temperature technology. Because of their low heat 
resistance, however, no niobium alloy, including NT-50, can be used as structural material without effective protective 
coatings. According to data in the literature, silicides of refractory materials are the most promising for such coatings. 
The processes by which silicide coatings are formed, their heat resistance, and their strength characteristics have been 
studied in the temperature interval 20-1200°C [1-6]. We know of no data on how silicide coatings affect the mechanical 
properties of niobium alloys at temperatur es close to the liquid nitrogen temperature. This is why it is of scientific as 
well as practical interest to study the influence of silicide coatings on the mechanical properties of NT-50 alloy in the 
temperature interval from −196 to 1100°C. 
MATERIALS AND RESEARCH METHODS 
The mechanical properties of the NT-50 alloy were determined at temperatures from −196 to 1100 °C on an 
NIKMP-1246 installation in tensile testing of standard specimens (GOST 1497-81, GOST 11150-81). Silicidation was 
carried out in a silicon powder at a vacuum of 6.7 ⋅ 10−3 Pa at 1250 °C for 4 h [5]. The specimens were studied by 
metallographic, x-ray structure, and electron probe microanalysis (EPMA). We studied the influence of chemical and 
heat treatment on the mechanical properties of specimens in the initial state, silicidated, and also vacuum-annealed at 
6.7 ⋅ 10−3 Pa at 1250°C for 4 h in the absence of silicon. 
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION 
According to data from metallographic inspection of NT-50 alloy after silicidation, the coating formed consists 
of two clearly separated layers. From x-ray analysis and elec tron probe microanalysis we established that two phases of 
higher silicides (TiSi 2, NbSi2) are present on the surface of the silicidated alloy. The layer adjacent to the base has a 
composition similar to that of the lower silicide (NbTi)5Si3.

The tensile strength of NT-50 specimens vacuum-annealed for 4 h at 6.7 ⋅ 10−3 Pa and 1250 °C is higher than 
that of the initial specimens in the temperature interval from −196 to 1100 °C (Fig. 1). It increases from 1.1 times at 
−196°C to 2.5 times at 1100 °C. This is due to the solubility of the residual gases (O 2, N2, H2, CO, and CO 2) during 
vacuum annealing. 
The fracture of the annealed specimens differs in nature from that of the initial specimens. The latter undergo 
viscous fracture and the grains in deformation containment vo lume have traces of plastic deformation and are extended 
in the direction of the tensile stresses. In th e annealed specimens the surface layer about 200 μm thick brittle-fractures 
along almost the entire working surface and microtears are observed. The microhardness of the annealed specimens 
varies from 4300 to 2200 MPa over the depth of the layer. Acicular precipitates were detected in the surface layer by 
metallographic method. The increase in the microhardness and the formation of acicular precipitates in the surface layer 
are a consequence of the interaction of the alloy with the residual gases during the annealing. 
The tensile strength of silicidated specimens is lower th an that of the initial and annealed specimens up to 
800°C. The properties of silicide coatings depend on the temper ature during testing. At low temperatures the coating is 
brittle and this causes cracks to appear, which is why we chan ged the conditions of the tests, making them closer to the 
conditions under which notched specimens were tested. As the temperature is raised the silicide coatings become more 
plastic and their strength coincides with that of the initial coatings, but remains lower than in the annealed coatings. 
The temperature dependence of the relative elongation of the initial, annealed, and silicidated specimens is 
plotted in Fig. 2. Over the entire temperature interval studied the relative elongation of the initial specimens is higher 
than that of the annealed and silicidated specimens. The relative elongation of the initial specimens increases from 4% at 
−196°C to 26% at 20 °C and reaches 130% at 1000 °C. For annealed specimens the relative elongation at −196 to 20°C 
differs little, 2-3%. The relative elongation increases as th e temperature rises. Compari ng the values of the relative 
elongation of silicidated and annealed specimens, we can conclude that during the silicidation the interaction of the 
material of the base with the residual gases decreases beca use a silicide coating is formed in the early stage of the 
process. In the temperature interval from −196 to 750°C the relative elongation of the specimens annealed in a vacuum 
of 6.7 ⋅ 10
−3 Pa for 4 h is lower than in the silicidated specimens. Above 800 °C, e.g., at 1000°C, the relative elongation 
of the annealed specimens is 75% and th at of the silicidated specimens is 30%. The decrease in the relative elongation 
of the annealed specimens is due to th e interaction of the residual gases (O 2, N2, H2, CO, CO2) with the material of the 
base during vacuum annealing, while in the initial stages of silicidation that interact ion decreases because of the 
formation of the silicide layer.

CONCLUSIONS 
We have investigated how silicide coatings affect the ultimate strength and the relative elongation of NT-50 
alloy in the temperature interval from −196 to 1100 °C. We have found that the tensile strength of the alloy with a 
silicide coating in the interval of temperatures from −196 to 800 °C is lower than that of the initial and annealed 
specimens. Starting from 900°C the tensile strength of silicidated specimens coincides with that of the initial specimens, 
but is lower than that of the annealed specimens. That natu re of the variation of the te nsile strength is due to the 
properties of the silicide coatin gs as a function of the temperature of the tests. We have also established that from −196 
to 750°C the relative elongation of the annealed specimens is lower than that of the silicidated and initial specimens.
