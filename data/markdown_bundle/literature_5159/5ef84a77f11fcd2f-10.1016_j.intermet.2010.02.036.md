abstract
Ti-based bulk metallic glasses have attracted lots of attention of scienti ﬁc researchers due to their
lightweight, low cost and high speci ﬁc strength. But only a few Ti-based alloys have been reported
possessing large glass forming ability. Then, research on new Ti-based alloys with good glass forming
ability is necessary and important. In present work, rods of Ti 43.15Zr9.59Cu36.24Ni9.06Sn1.96 glassy alloy with
diameter of 3 mm have been fabricated by using copper mould casting method. It has been found that
this Ti-based bulk metallic glass possesses good mechanical properties at room temperature. The
compressive yield strength and the fracture strength of the glassy alloy are 2360 MPa and 2640 MPa,
respectively, together with the plastic strain of 2.24%. The present results show that adding an element or
increasing the amount of an element with large difference in atomic size ratio with other main
constitutive elements and more negative mixing heat with other main constitutive elements in an alloy
would bene ﬁt to enhance the glass forming ability of the alloy.

1. Introduction
As structural materials, Ti-based bulk metallic glasses (BMGs)
possess many great advantages such as good mechanical proper-
ties, low density, and high speci ﬁc strength and so on [1,2]. Thus
they are more attractive than other BMGs. However, similar to
other BMGs, limited glass forming ability (GFA) of most developed
Ti-based BMGs is still a big obstacle for their future practical
application as structural materials. In fact, enhancing GFA of glassy
alloys has been a challenging topic in the study of glassy alloy since
its discovery. During last decades, great effort has been devoted to
ﬁnd BMGs with large GFA and many signi ﬁcant results have been
achieved in many alloys systems [3e8]. For example, according to
the “confusion principle ”, the critical size for glass formation of
ZreTieCueNi could be enhanced to 50 mm after addition of Be
element [9]; by minor addition of heavy rare earth metals, “bulk
amorphous steel ” with centimeter dimension could be fabricated
[10,1 1]; with similar element's addition, Ln- and Pd-based glassy
alloys with critical diameter of 32 mm and 72 mm, respectively,
could be prepared [12,13]. As for Ti-based alloys, Ti
45Zr20Be35 and
Ti40Zr10Cu34Pd14Sn2 BMGs with the critical size of 10 mm have been
prepared [14e17], despite of that the critical size for most Ti-based
BMGs without Pd and/or Be addition is less than 3 mm. Then,
developing Ti-based BMGs with high GFA and free of Pd or Be
element is meaningful since containing expensive Pd or noxious Be
is quite disadvantageous for further industrious application
[18e22]. Recently, we have reported a new (Ti
0.45Cu0.378Zr0.10-
Ni0.072)100/C0 xSnx (x ¼ 0e8) glassy alloy system without containing
Pd or Be, which exhibit a critical size of 2 mm in preparation of
BMGs [23]. According to the confusion principle, we tried to
increase Ni element content in the previous Ti-based alloys, and
a new quinary Ti eZreCueNieSn bulk glassy alloy with high GFA
and good mechanical properties has been developed.

3. Results

3 mm and a length of 63 mm. Its XRD pattern is shown in Fig. 2 .
Except for broad diffraction peak, no distinctive sharp diffraction
peak is observed in the XRD spectrum of the sample. It indicates
that the rod sample possesses only glassy structure. The micro-
structure of the sample has also been examined with high resolu-
tion transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM). As shown in Fig. 3,
no crystalline phase has been observed during the examination and
the selected electron diffraction pattern (see the inset of Fig. 3 )
exhibits typical halo rings of amorphous structure. HRTEM obser-
vation has con ﬁrmed that the as-prepared sample is of full glassy
structure.

Table 2 lists the atomic radius data of the constituent elements
and the mixing heat among different elements of the S2 and S1
alloys [29,30]. The atomic radius of the elements is sorted in
descending order as follows: Zr > Sn z Ti > Cu z Ni. According to
their atomic radius, these elements could be divided into three
major groups as (Ti, Sn) e(Cu, Ni) e(Zr). Ni atom possesses the
smallest radius among the ﬁve elements and large difference in
atomic size ratio with Ti and Zr. Then, increasing Ni content would
enhance the packing density of atoms and clusters. In addition, the
mixing heat of Ni eZr and Ni eTi are /C0 49 and /C0 35 kJ mol /C0 1
respectively, which are relatively more negative than that of other
atomic pairs and the absolute values are relative large. It would
result in forming more stable or stronger atomic pair and/or clus-
ters. Therefore, the dif ﬁculty of atomic movement or diffusion
would be increased. Then, increasing Ni element content in the
alloy would beneﬁ t to enhance the stability of undercooled melt
and thermal stability of the glass. Thus it is bene ﬁcial to enhance
the glassy forming ability of the BMG, agreed with the result shown
in Table 1. In addition, the formation of strong atomic pair or cluster
would result in the increase of the critical shear stress and yield
strength, agreed with the present result.
The present results show that adding an element or increasing
the amount of an element with large difference in atomic size ratio
with other main constitutive elements and more negative mixing
heat with other main constitutive elements in an alloy would
beneﬁt to enhance the GFA of the alloy. Although the increase in the
critical size of the studied glassy alloy is not very large, it indicates
that it is an effective way for enhancing the GFA of alloys.
5. Conclusion
Through enhancing Ni content in an alloy of Ti
44.10Zr9.80-
Cu37.04Ni7.06Sn2.00, a new bulk glassy alloy of Ti 43.15Zr9.59Cu36.24-
Ni9.06Sn1.96, which possesses higher glass forming ability and
thermal stability than the former, has been developed. The newly
developed BMG possesses the supercooled liquid region of 50 K, the
yield strength of about 2360 MPa, the fracture strength of
2640 MPa, and the plastic strain of about 2.24%. It is believed that Ni
element possesses large difference in atomic size ratio with other
main constitutive elements and more negative mixing heat with
other main constitutive elements bene ﬁts to enhance the glass
forming ability of the alloy.
