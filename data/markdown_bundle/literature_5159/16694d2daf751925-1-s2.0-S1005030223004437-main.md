Grain reﬁnement has been found to be able to improve the strength of metals without reducing their 
plasticity or ductility. In this work, ultraﬁne-grained Ti6Al7Nb- x Cu ( x = 0, 3 wt%, 6 wt%, 9 wt%) alloys 
were fabricated based on the clinically used Ti6Al7Nb alloy via Cu alloying fabrication strategy combined 
with dynamic recrystallization (DRX) from martensite during hot work. Our results indicate that Cu alloy- 
ing is not only effective in reducing the width of martensite laths after quenching in the β phase region 
but also increases the nucleation rate of DRX during the high-temperature deformation. Additionally, the 
Ti 2 Cu phase that precipitates from the matrix can pin the grain boundaries and inhibit their growth dur- 
ing the high-temperature deformation. The optimal Cu concentration of Ti6Al7Nb- x Cu alloy is determined 
to be 6 wt%, and the optimal deformation temperature is determined to be 750 °C. At this Cu content and 
deformation temperature, the grain size is only 270 nm, the tensile strength is up to 1266 ±10 MPa, and 
the elongation is 17%. Compared with the commercial Ti6Al7Nb alloy, the ultraﬁne-grained Ti6Al7Nb-6Cu 
alloy has better overall mechanical properties, good biocompatibility, and unique antibacterial properties. 
This work provides a theoretical basis for fabricating the new antibacterial titanium-based alloys with 
high strength.

1. Introduction 
Titanium alloys have high speciﬁc strength and good corrosion 
resistance, which have been widely used in the biomedical ﬁeld. As 
titanium alloys become more widely used for clinical applications, 
some problems also occurred after long-term implantation. There 
still existed a few of incidences such as the fracture of implant dur- 
ing service. At the same time, clinical implantation failure due to 
bacterial infection should also be raised concern. Compared with 
the traditional coarse-grained metal materials, ultraﬁne-grained 
(UFG) metal materials not only have higher strength and hard- 
ness with good plasticity, but also have been reported to pro- 
mote the growth of cell in some work [ 1 , 2 ]. The development and

At present, bulk ultraﬁne-grained metal materials are mainly 
fabricated using methods of severe plastic deformation at low 
temperature, such as high-pressure torsion (HPT), multi-directional 
forging (MDF), equal channel angular pressing (ECAP), accumu- 
lative roll-bonding (ARB), etc. [3–5] . These methods all rely on 
high-power equipment and expensive molds, and the size of the 
fabricated materials is usually very small, i.e., it is diﬃcult to 
achieve large-scale fabrication of UFG titanium alloys with these 
methods. In contrast, the conventional hot forging, the most widely 
used metal processing and forming technology, can achieve bulk 
production of titanium alloys. However, its grain reﬁnement ability 
is weak, and with existing technology, the fabrication of titanium 
alloys with conventional hot forging equipment is limited to grain 
sizes of 3–5 μm [6] . Therefore, it is imperative to develop the 
UFG titanium alloys that are compatible with existing fabrication 
and processing methods, so as to make it possible to realize the 
low-cost and large-scale fabrication of ultraﬁne-grained titanium 
alloys.

In the conventional hot forging process, the grain reﬁnement 
behavior depends on the nucleation rate of dynamic recrystalliza- 
tion (DRX) and the growth rate of recrystallized grains. Firstly, the 
higher the nucleation rate of DRX, the more favorable for the grain 
reﬁnement during the forging process. The study of Chen et al. 
[7] showed that the higher the strain energy stored in the original 
microstructure before hot deformation, the higher the nucleation 
rate of DRX. The study of Wang et al. [8] showed that during the 
process of hot deformation, the activation of prismatic slip systems 
was more conducive to improving the nucleation rate of DRX than 
the activation of basal slip systems in titanium alloy. According to 
the above results, if martensite with higher strain energy is used as 
the original microstructure for hot deformation and the activation 
of prismatic slip systems is promoted by regulating the chemical 
composition of the titanium alloy and the hot deformation tem- 
perature, the nucleation rate of DRX can be greatly enhanced. Sec- 
ondly, the lower the rate of recrystallized grain growth, the more 
favorable for the grain reﬁnement during the forging process. The 
studies of Zhang et al. [ 9 , 10 ] and Chen et al. [ 9 , 10 ] showed that 
adding elements with high binding energy, such as W, Mo, Nb, Zr, 
into titanium alloys could effectively inhibit the growth of grains 
during the hot deformation process. The studies of Mohseni et al. 
[ 11 , 12 ] and Lin et al. [ 11 , 12 ] showed that the growth of grains dur- 
ing the process of hot deformation could be signiﬁcantly inhibited 
by adding secondary phase particles into the alloy, and the Zenner 
pinning force generated by these particles could offset the driving 
force of grain growth. According to the above results, replacing the 
toxic element V in Ti6Al4V alloy by Nb, which has better biocom- 
patibility and a higher binding energy [13–15] , to form Ti6Al7Nb 
alloy should also effectively inhibit the growth of recrystallized 
grains. Additionally, Cu alloying in titanium alloys allows for the 
formation of an intermetallic compound, Ti 2 Cu, that can pin grain 
boundaries, further inhibiting the growth of recrystallized grains. 
Based on the previous studies [16–19] , a new type of biomedi- 
cal Ti6Al7Nb- x Cu alloy ( x = 0, 3 wt%, 6 wt%, 9 wt%) was designed 
in this study, which was intended to enhance the nucleation rate 
of DRX during the process of hot deformation by optimizing the 
chemical composition, original microstructure and hot deformation 
conditions, and also to reduce the rate of grain growth in the pro- 
cess of hot deformation through adding Nb and Cu elements, so as 
to achieve an equiaxed UFG titanium alloy using the conventional 
hot forging equipment. On this basis, the microstructural stability 
of the UFG titanium alloy was evaluated, and its mechanical and 
biological properties were tested. The results in this study would 
have great signiﬁcance for the development of new generation of 
high-performance biomedical titanium alloys.

3.2. Flow behavior 
The as-quenched Ti6Al7Nb-xCu alloy was subjected to hot com- 
pression experiments at 750, 800, 850 and 900 °C with a strain 
rate of 1 s −1 . As can be seen from the morphology of stress–strain 
curves ( Fig. 2 (a–d)), all the materials underwent three stages: 
strain hardening (where the strain ε≈0–0.05), strain softening 
( ε≈0.05–0.6) and steady-state ﬂow ( ε≈0.6–1.2). In the strain hard- 
ening stage, the ﬂow stress increased rapidly with increasing the 
strain due to the proliferation of dislocations. In the strain soften- 
ing stage, the ﬂow stress decreased with increasing the strain due 
to the DRX. In the steady-state ﬂow stage, the effect of dislocation 
strengthening and DRX recovery softening reached a dynamic bal- 
ance, so that the ﬂow stress did not change with increasing the 
strain. According to the peak stress of each ﬂow curve and the 
steady-state ﬂow stress at ε= 1.0, the peak stress and steady-state 
stress maps are plotted to reveal the inﬂuence of deformation tem- 
perature and Cu content on the peak stress and steady-state stress 
( Fig. 2 (e, f)). The results show that the peak and steady-state stress 
of the Ti6Al7Nb- x Cu alloy decreases as the temperature decreases. 
The steady-state ﬂow stress of the Ti6Al7Nb- x Cu alloy decreases as 
the Cu content increases. 
3.3. Microstructures after hot compression 
SEM was used to observe the microstructures of the Ti6Al7Nb- 
x Cu alloys after hot compression at different temperatures ( Fig. 3 ). 
The compression direction (CD) is shown in the top left corner. 
When the temperature of hot compression was between 850 °C 
and 900 °C, the material contained a higher proportion of de- 
formed primary α phase. When the temperature of hot compres- 
sion was reduced to 800 °C, an equiaxed ultraﬁne microstructure 
could be obtained after hot compression. The grain size of the ma- 
terial could be further reﬁned with reduction of the temperature 
of hot compression to 750 °C. However, it should be pointed out 
that if the Cu content in the material was low (Cu content ≤3%), 
the deformed bands were easily generated in the microstructure, 
which was caused by the uneven plastic deformation in the mate- 
rial. Thus, it can be seen from the above analyses that the optimal 
hot deformation temperature for fabricating the UFG Ti6Al7Nb- 
x Cu alloy should be between 750 °C and 800 °C, and the evenly 
equiaxed ultraﬁne microstructure could be obtained by increasing 
the Cu content to more than 3%.

The Ti6Al7Nb- x Cu alloys were hot forged at the optimal 
deformation temperature determined by the hot compression

was the smallest, which is consistent with the SEM observa-

red region represents the α phase, the blue region represents 
the β phase, and the yellow region represents the Ti 2 Cu phase. 
When 0 ≤Cu content ≤6%, the microstructure of the material was 
mainly composed of α phases. When the Cu content was 9%, 
the Ti 2 Cu phase with size of 460 ±120 nm precipitated from the 
matrix.

3.5. Thermal stability of ultraﬁne-grained Ti6Al7Nb-6Cu alloy 
In order to investigate the thermal stability of the UFG 
Ti6Al7Nb-6Cu alloy, it was exposed at a temperature between 
600 °C and 750 °C for 1 h, and then the microstructure was 
characterized by EBSD and TEM. It can be seen from the inverse 
pole ﬁgure (IPF) ( Fig. 7 (a)) that when the thermal exposure tem- 
perature was below 700 °C, the α grain size almost always re- 
mained the initial grain size (270 ±43 nm). When the thermal ex- 
posure temperature was increased to 750 °C, the grain size grew 
to 900 ±57 nm. Thus it can be concluded that the critical insta- 
bility temperature of the microstructure should be 700 °C. The 
phase distribution map analysis ( Fig. 7 (b)) showed that the sizes 
of the β and Ti 2 Cu phases increased gradually as the thermal ex- 
posure temperature increased. Pole ﬁgure analysis results showed 
that when the thermal exposure temperature was below 650 °C, 
the α, β, and Ti 2 Cu phases always maintained a certain crystal 
orientation: (0 0 02) α∥ (110) β∥ (103)Ti 2 Cu ( Fig. 7 (c)).When the ther- 
mal exposure temperature rose to 700 °C, this relationship broke 
down. It can be concluded from the above analyses that although 
the α phase grains did not grow signiﬁcantly after thermal ex- 
posure at 700 °C, the β and Ti 2 Cu phases began to precipitate 
in abundance and the orientation relationship was destroyed as a 
result. Thus, the UFG Ti6Al7Nb-6Cu alloy should be thermostable 
at 650 °C. 
3.6. Mechanical properties of ultraﬁne-grained Ti6Al7Nb-6Cu alloy 
Mechanical properties of the UFG Ti6Al7Nb-6Cu alloy at room 
temperature were tested in comparison with the other experimen- 
tal materials fabricated in this study ( Fig. 8 (a)). It can be seen 
from the tensile curve that the UFG Ti6Al7Nb-6Cu alloy has ex- 
cellent strength and plasticity matching, with a tensile strength 
of 1266 ±10 MPa and an elongation of as high as 17%. Compared 
with the commercial Ti6Al7Nb alloy, the tensile strength and the 
elongation of the UFG Ti6Al7Nb-6Cu alloy are 40% and nearly 60% 
greater, respectively. Meanwhile, compared with the UFG titanium 
alloys and other medical titanium alloys reported in literatures, the 
present UFG Ti6Al7Nb-6Cu alloy has the best comprehensive me- 
chanical properties ( Fig. 8 (b)). 
3.7. Biological properties of ultraﬁne-grained Ti6Al7Nb-xCu alloy 
The antibacterial properties of the UFG Ti6Al7Nb- x Cu ( x = 0, 
3 wt%, 6 wt%, 9 wt%) alloys were evaluated, and the antibacte- 
rial rates of the materials were calculated using the commercial 
Ti6Al7Nb alloy as the control material ( Fig. 9 ). It can be seen that 
after the UFG Ti6Al7Nb-6Cu alloy was co-cultured with S. aureus 
for 24 h, only a small number of colonies were observed on the 
culture medium, while a large number of colonies existed on the 
culture medium of the commercial Ti6Al7Nb alloy. Quantitative 
statistical results showed that the antibacterial rate of the UFG 
Ti6Al7Nb-6Cu alloy was 97.4%, which was much higher than that 
of the UFG Ti6Al7Nb-3Cu alloy (71.2%), and only slightly lower than 
that of the UFG Ti6Al7Nb-9Cu alloy (99.2%).

4. Discussions 
Based on the fabrication strategy of decomposition of the 
martensitic microstructure under the condition of thermome- 
chanical coupling, this study successfully fabricated the UFG 
Ti6Al7Nb-6Cu alloy using conventional hot forging equipment, 
which not only possessed good microstructural stability, but also 
had excellent mechanical and biological properties. Thus it can 
be meaningful to further clarify the mechanism of Cu alloying 
for enhancing the nucleation rate of DRX, revealing the effects of 
Nb and Cu alloying on the inhibition of grain growth during the 
process of hot deformation and thermal exposure, and discuss the 
effect of Cu alloying and grain reﬁning on the material properties. 
4.1. Role of Cu alloying in enhancing the nucleation rate of DRX 
Cu alloying can signiﬁcantly enhance the nucleation rate of DRX 
because it leads to reﬁning the martensite laths, reducing the β
transition temperature, and promoting the activation of prismatic 
slip systems. 
Firstly, the martensite lath boundaries could provide nucleation 
sites for DRX during hot deformation of the initial martensite mi- 
crostructure [ 32 , 33 ]. The smaller the width of martensitic laths, 
the higher the number of lath boundaries, and thus the higher 
the nucleation rate of DRX, making it easier to obtain ultraﬁne 
grains [34] . When quenched in the β-phase region, due to the 
large difference in atomic radius between Cu ( r = 0.128 nm) and Ti 
( r = 0.147 nm), the supersaturated Cu solid solution in the matrix 
can cause strong lattice distortions. The adaptive lattice distortions 
allow a large number of variants to form in adjacent regions, so Cu 
alloying could signiﬁcantly reduce the width of quenched marten- 
sitic laths ( Fig. 1 (a)). When the addition of Cu exceeded 6 wt% 
in the studied alloy, the crystal structure of martensite began to 
change from the body-centered tetragonal α’ phase to the rhom- 
bohedral α’’ phase ( Fig. 1 (b)), and the reﬁning effect of Cu alloying 
on martensite lath width gradually decreased ( Table 2 ). Therefore, 
when the Cu content was 6%, Cu alloying achieved the greatest ef- 
fect on enhancing the nucleation rate of DRX in the present alloy. 
Secondly, as Cu is a stable element of the β phase in titanium 
alloys, the β transition temperature of the Ti6Al7Nb- x Cu alloy de- 
creased as the Cu content increased ( Fig. 1 (c), Table 2 ). Since the β
phase has a higher self-diffusion coeﬃcient than the α phase [35] , 
it is prone to both dynamic recovery and DRX during deformation. 
Therefore, the steady-state ﬂow stress of the experimental mate- 
rial decreased as the β phase proportion increased ( Fig. 2 (f)). The 
results of Zherebtsov et al. [36] on Ti6Al4V alloy showed that in- 
creasing the β phase proportion for a titanium alloy is conducive 
to reducing the ﬂow stress and promoting the uniform plastic de-

when the dominant deformation mechanism is prismatic slip, DRX 
nucleation is easier. Wang et al. [8] believed that this is because 
that there is only one basal slip plane (0 0 02), which can only 
generate the interfaces perpendicular to the compression direction, 
but not parallel to the compression direction. In contrast, prismatic 
sliding has three slip planes: (10 ¯1 0), (01 ¯1 0), (1 ¯1 00). This can pro- 
duce interfaces in multiple directions so as to promote the DRX 
nucleation. In this study, the dominant deformation mechanism in 
the Ti6Al7Nb- x Cu alloys was always prismatic slip, so the DRX nu- 
cleation occurred relatively easily in the materials, and thus the 
materials could obtain ﬁne grains after deformation ( Fig. 4 ). Ad- 
ditionally, Cao et al. [38] pointed out that the lower the c / a ra- 
tio of a hexagonal close-packed (hcp) crystal structure, the likelier 
it is if the dominant deformation mechanism is the prismatic slip 
rather than the basal slip. The addition of Cu in the Ti6Al7Nb al-

thus enhanced the nucleation rate of DRX in the Ti6Al7Nb-xCu al- 
loy. 
4.2. Role of Nb and Cu alloying in inhibiting grain growth 
A ultraﬁne-grained microstructure can be unstable and easily 
grows when exposed to a thermal source or stress. Therefore, the 
key to fabricate an ultraﬁne-grained titanium alloy is to preserve 
the ultraﬁne-grained microstructure formed in the process of hot 
deformation to room temperature. A study showed that the grain 
size of a Ti6Al alloy after hot deformation was about 40–70 μm 
[39] , while that of Ti6Al7Nb alloy under hot deformation at simi- 
lar temperatures was only 0.5–2 μm in the present study ( Fig. 3 ). 
It can be seen that the addition of Nb effectively inhibits the grain 
growth during hot deformation. Nb added to a titanium alloy is 
distributed uniformly in the α matrix in the form of a supersatu- 
rated solid solution. As Nb is an element with high binding energy, 
it reduces the self-diffusion coeﬃcient of atoms in the alloy matrix 
[10] and thus inhibits the diffusion and migration of grain bound-

4.3. Role of Cu alloying and grain reﬁnement on material properties 
Cu alloying and grain reﬁnement have signiﬁcant effects on the 
hot workability, mechanical properties and biological properties of 
the experimental materials. 
On the one hand, Cu alloying could reﬁne the martensitic 
laths and promote the precipitation of Ti 2 Cu phase in the matrix 
( Figs. 1 (a, b) and 6 (a, b)). The synergistic effect of lath boundary 
strengthening and precipitation strengthening led to an increase in 
the peak stress during hot deformation of the material ( Fig. 2 (e)), 
thus reducing the resistance to hot working. During the deforma- 
tion at room temperature, the tensile strength of the material was 
signiﬁcantly increased due to the interaction between the precipi- 
tated Ti 2 Cu phase and dislocations ( Fig. 8 (a)). After thermal defor- 
mation, a part of Cu will be solidly dissolved in β phase during air 
cooling, forming metastable β phase. The content of Cu is very im- 
portant. When the content of Cu is 3%, little Cu will dissolve into 
the β phase to form metastable β phase. Therefore, 3Cu alloy does 
not have Trip effect, and its uneven microstructure also leads to 
its poor plasticity ( Figs. 4 and 5 (a)). When the Cu content reaches 
6%, part of metastable β phase will be formed during the cool- 
ing process, and Trip effect will occur during the deformation at 
room temperature. The yield strength will be reduced to enhance 
the plasticity [43] , and the yield strength after reduction is close to 
that of 3Cu alloy. When the Cu content reaches 9%, the Cu content 
is too high. At the air cooling rate, a large number of Ti 2 Cu phase 
precipitates quickly, and very little Cu can help form metastable 
β phase in the β phase, without Trip effect. The lar ge Ti 2 Cu pre- 
cipitated in large quantities reduces its plasticity ( Fig. 4 ), resulting 
in poor plasticity. Cu alloying also offered the material antibacterial 
properties ( Fig. 9 ). This is mainly owing to the fact that continuous 
release of Cu 2 + ions from the surface of the material could inter- 
act with bacteria, change their permeability of the cell membranes, 
and act as a sulfhydryl group in respiratory enzymes near the cell 
membranes, resulting in the respiratory dysfunction. Finally, these 
membrane leakage and cytoplasmic outﬂow could kill the bacteria 
[ 17 , 44 , 45 ]. At the same time, Cu ions could also promote the dif- 
ferentiation of mesenchymal stem cells into osteoblasts and then 
the cell growth [ 46 , 47 ], i.e., the Ti6Al7Nb- x Cu alloys have excellent 
cytocompatibility ( Fig. 10 ). 
On the other hand, during the deformation process at room 
temperature, grain reﬁnement could not only increase the resis- 
tance of the material to dislocation movements, but also the strain 
could evenly distribute on each grain to increase the deforma- 
tion capacity of the material. Therefore, grain reﬁnement could si- 
multaneously enhance both strength and plasticity of the material 
( Fig. 8 (a)).The Ti 2 Cu phases can act as the “contact sterilization”
when bacteria are in contact with them [48] . Because the Ti 2 Cu 
phase was distributed along grain boundaries, reﬁning the grain 
size was equivalent to reﬁning the size of the Ti 2 Cu phase, thus in- 
creasing the number of Ti 2 Cu phases in contact with bacteria, and 
enhancing the antibacterial performance of the material. So grain 
reﬁnement is also conducive to improving the antibacterial perfor- 
mance of Cu-containing titanium alloys. Grain reﬁnement is also 
conducive to promoting cell adhesion on the surface of the mate- 
rial and accelerating cell proliferation and differentiation [ 49 , 50 ], 
thus further increasing the cell compatibility of the material. The 
antibacterial function of the UFG Ti6Al7Nb-6Cu alloy developed in 
this study can effectively reduce the risk of infection after implan- 
tation in human body, which is of great signiﬁcance for increasing 
the success rate of implantations. 
5. Conclusions 
In this study, UFG Ti6Al7Nb- x Cu ( x = 0, 3 wt%, 6 wt%, 9 wt%) 
alloys were successfully fabricated using a strategy of martensite 
thermomechanical coupling decomposition. The main conclusions 
are as follows: 
(1) Cu alloying could greatly reﬁne the width of martensitic laths 
after quenching of the Ti6Al7Nb- x Cu alloys in the β phase re- 
gion. When the Cu content was 6.0%, the width of the laths 
was down to 83 ±18 nm, which was one tenth of the thickness 
of the laths in the Ti6Al7Nb alloy after quenching. 
(2) The optimal Cu content in the Ti6Al7Nb-xCu alloy was 6 wt%, 
and the optimal hot deformation temperature was 750 °C, 
by which the grain size reached to 270 ±80 nm, the tensile 
strength 1266 ±10 MPa and the elongation 17%. 
(3) Ti 2 Cu phases precipitated along grain boundaries could effec- 
tively inhibit the grain growth in UFG Ti6Al7Nb-6Cu alloy dur- 
ing hot deformation. At the same time, the microstructural sta- 
bility of the material was signiﬁcantly improved when under- 
went the thermal exposure. Compared with UFG Ti6Al7Nb al- 
loy, the instability temperature of UFG Ti6Al7Nb-6Cu alloy was 
increased by 210 °C. 
(4) Cu alloying and grain reﬁnement offered UFG Ti6Al7Nb-6Cu 
alloy unique antibacterial activity and good cytocompatibility, 
which is expected to be a new generation titanium alloy for 
medical applications
