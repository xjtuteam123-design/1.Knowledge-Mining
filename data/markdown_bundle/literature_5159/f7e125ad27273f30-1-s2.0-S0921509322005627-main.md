ABSTRACT 
The lightweight AA2050 alloy exhibited an excellent specific strength in the T8 state. However, its widespread 
application was somewhat limited by the service temperature. In order to evaluate the suitability of the alloy 
operated at the high temperature, the high temperature mechanical properties (room temperature-300 
◦
C) of a 
T8 treated AA2050 alloy were systematically investigated in this work. The results reveal that the ultimate 
tensile strength decreased from 566 MPa to 262 MPa as the tensile temperature increased from room temperature 
to 300 
◦
C. Particularly, the rate of strength decline increased obviously at 300 
◦
C. The elongation increased first 
and then decreased, reaching the maximum (12.8%) at 250 
◦
C. The increase of tensile temperatures promoted 
the coarsening of T
1 
phases, the reduction of dislocation densities, and the decreasing fraction of deformation 
textures, responsible for the decrease of strengths. The high rate of strength decline at 300 
◦
C can be attributed to 
the T
1 
phase ’ s thickening. Meanwhile, as the tensile temperature increased from room temperature to 250 
◦
C, the 
increase of the elongation resulted from more grains with lower Taylor factor and the formation of precipitation- 
free zones. However, the sub-grain structure caused by dynamic recovery formed and T
1 
phases coarsened along 
sub-grain boundaries, which caused the elongation decline of the alloy at 300 
◦
C. 
1. Introduction 
The third generation Al – Cu – Li alloys have been widely used for the 
aeronautic and aerospace engineering in place of traditional Al alloys, 
due to their attractive mechanical properties, low density, low tough -
ness, and high corrosion resistance [ 1 – 3 ]. For the structural aerospace 
parts, both the lightweight and mechanical properties in the tempera -
ture range of 70 – 150 
◦
C were considered fundamental owing to the 
aerodynamic heating [ 4 ]. Additionally, the automotive parts have 
higher requirements than aerospace parts for the service temperature, 
which needs to be investigated up to 300

the high-temperature performance of Al alloys were primarily focused 
on Al – Zn – Mg – Cu, Al – Mg – Si and Al – Cu – Mg alloys by modifications of 
the alloy compositions and heat treatment schedules [ 6 – 8 ]. There were 
few reports on the high temperature properties of Al – Cu – Li alloys. 
However, Al – Cu – Li alloys are more competitive in comparison with 
traditionally heat-resistant Al alloys due to the existence of phases with 
the excellent thermal stability, such as T

2. Experimental methods 
A commercially available AA2050-T84 alloy sheet with ~40 mm 
thickness was employed in the present study, provided by Commercial 
Aircraft Corporation of China Ltd. The composition of the studied alloy 
is Al-3.51%Cu-1.01%Li-0.39%Mg-0.25%Zn-0.20%Ag-0.35%Mn-0.25% 
Zr (wt.%) measured by inductively couple plasma atomic emission 
spectroscopy (ICP-AES). The as-received alloy was first subjected to hot 
rolling to reduce the thickness from 40 mm to 6 mm after pre-heating the 
alloy at 525 
◦
C for 4 h, and then the sheet was rolled to 2 mm thickness 
through cold rolling processes. After solid-solution treatment at 525 
◦
C 
for 1 h and quenching in water, the sheet was then subjected to T8 peak 
aging treatment (aging at 160 
◦
C for 28 h after 5% pre-deformation 
through cold rolling). According to the standard GB/T 4338-2006 
[ 20 ], the tensile samples were cut along the rolling direction from the 
aged alloy and the sample size is shown in Fig. 1 . The high temperature 
tensile tests were conducted on an MTS810 tester with an MTS 653.02 
heating apparatus at room temperature, 100, 150, 200, 250 and 300 
◦
C, 
respectively with the accuracy of ± 1 
◦
C. The strain was measured by a 
high temperature mechanical extensometer with the ceramic extension 
rods and the gauge length of the extensometer is 15 mm. According to 
the Refs. [ 14 , 21 ], the mechanical properties of Al alloys were more 
temperature-sensitive with the reducing tensile rate. As a result, a low 
tensile rate (0.1 mm/min) was performed in order to present the effect of 
temperature more clearly. The samples were held at each temperature 
for 30 min before the tensile, and three samples per condition were 
tested. 
Fractography observations were performed in a Sirion 200 field 
emission scanning electron microscope (SEM) with an acceleration 
voltage of 20 kV and a working distance of 7 mm. A D/max 2500 in -
strument with an operating voltage of 40 kV and a current of 250 mA, 
was employed to measure dislocation density using CuK α radiation ( λ =
1.54 Å). The primary beam was monochromatized using a graphite 
monochromator tuned for the Cu K α 1 component, so that the Cu K α 2 
line was completely suppressed. The measurement parameters are as 
follows: over a 2 θ range of 5 – 145

Jade 9.0. Electron backscattered diffraction (EBSD) was conducted using 
an SEM equipped with XM4 Hikari at 25 kV and the TSL OIM Analysis 
5.31 software was used to analyze the EBSD data. Transmission electron 
microscopy (TEM), high-angle annular dark-field scanning transmission 
electron microscopy (HAADF-STEM) observations and drift correction 
energy-dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) mappings were carried out using a 
Tecnai G
2 
F20 operated at 200 kV. The specimens for EBSD and TEM 
observation were mechanically polished to a thickness of about 200 μ m 
and 80 μ m, respectively, and then double-jet polished in a 25% nitric 
acid and 75% methanol electrolyte solution maintaining the tempera -
ture at approximately 30 
◦

3.1. Mechanical properties 
Fig. 2 shows tensile stress-strain curves and graphs of mechanical

4.3. Effect of stretching temperature on the plasticity 
In previous studies on the high temperature properties of Al alloys 
[ 5 , 6 , 41 ], the alloy usually showed a better plasticity with the increase of 
tensile temperature, and the dimples tended to be larger and deeper for 
the corresponding fractures of the alloy. However, the average sizes of 
the dimples were similar as the tensile temperature rose from 250 
◦
C to 
300 
◦
C ( Fig. 3 (e and f)), and the corresponding elongation decreased in 
this paper. Obviously, the fracture behavior of the studied alloy was 
associated with the microstructural evolution under different tensile 
temperatures. 
For the deformations in polycrystalline materials, it is generally 
accepted that the deformation behavior takes place by multiple slips (in 
principle activation of up to 5 slip systems) to accommodate the shape 
changes imposed by the external load [ 25 , 53 , 54 ]. The Taylor factor can 
reflect the difficulty of polycrystalline materials to activate the slip 
system under stress [ 55 ]. The larger Taylor factor, the harder the crys -
talline grain of the corresponding orientation is, and the alloy is more 
difficult to slip. The result is that the grains with larger Taylor factors 
have a higher resistance to plastic deformation. On the contrary, smaller 
Taylor factors indicate softer crystalline grains of the corresponding 
orientation. These grains are more prone to slip, which causes the 
preferential deformation of these grains. Therefore, a few grains were in 
soft orientation during tensile at low temperature due to the low content 
of recrystallization textures. The dislocation rapidly accumulated in 
these grains, resulting in the initiation of cracks and reducing its elon -
gation. As the stretching temperature rose, more grains were in soft 
orientation because the proportion of recrystallization textures 
increased. As a result, the alloy deformed more uniformly and the stress 
concentration was reduced [ 56 ]. It was beneficial to the improvement of 
elongation.

Furthermore, the plasticity of the Al – Cu – Li alloy is also influenced by 
the precipitate behavior along grain boundaries and sub-grain bound -
aries. As shown in Fig. 3 , the fracture mode changed from intergranular 
fracture to transgranular fracture with the increase of stretching tem -
perature. The fracture mode is mainly related to the strength difference 
between matrix and grain boundary [ 57 , 58 ]. When the alloy was 
stretched at room temperature, the GBPs were continuous and the PFZ 
was hard to be detected. There was a larger strength difference between 
the matrix and grain boundaries. The dislocations moved to grain 
boundaries and formed the pile-up. This resulted in the stress concen -
tration and the crack tended to extend along grain boundaries. As the 
stretching temperature increased, the GBPs were coarsened and the PFZ 
formed, leading to the softening of grain boundaries. The strength dif -
ference between the matrix and grain boundaries was small. As a result, 
the crack growth changed to gentle transgranular expansion, causing the 
improvement of the plasticity. Meanwhile, it should be noted that the 
precipitation of T
1 
phases along sub-grain boundaries has adverse effects 
on the plasticity of the alloy [ 59 , 60 ]. As shown in Fig. 4 , T

(1) When the tensile temperature increased from room temperature 
to 300 
◦

to 262 MPa and the decrease was more obvious at 300 
◦
C. While 
the elongation increased first from 8%, reaching the maximum

◦
C, and then decreased to 11.3% at 300 
◦

(2) As the tensile temperature increased, the coarsening of T
1 
phases 
decreased the precipitation strengthening of the alloy, and the 
decreasing deformation textures and the increasing recrystalli -
zation textures led to the lower average Taylor factor and texture 
strengthening. Meanwhile, the drop of dislocation density caused 
the decrease of dislocation strengthening. The decline of 
strengths can be attributed to the decrease of these strengthening 
effects. Additionally, the thickening of T
1 
phases at 300 
◦
C caused 
the additional strength loss. 
(3) The higher tensile temperature promoted the coarsening of GBPs, 
causing the formation of PFZs. Meanwhile, the increasing 
recrystallization textures meant that more grains were in soft 
orientation. These two factors contributed to the increase of 
elongations from room temperature to 250 
◦
C. However, the sub- 
grain structures composed of low-angle grain boundaries formed 
and T
