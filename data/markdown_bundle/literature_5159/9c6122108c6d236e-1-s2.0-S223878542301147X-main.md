abstract
The inﬂuence of temperature on mechanical properties and fracture behavior of direc-
tionally solidiﬁed (DS) Ti47Al2Cr2Nb alloy was investigated in detail. Tensile tests and
single-edge notched bending tests were performed in the temperature range of room
temperature ( z25
/C14 C) to 900 /C14 C. The results revealed that both the yield strength and
tensile strength of DS alloy specimens were insensitive to temperature until 850 /C14 C. The
yield strength and tensile strength at room temperature were found to be 500 MPa and
610 MPa, respectively, which were close to those at 850
/C14 C (i.e., 534 MPa and 575 MPa). The
ductility increased with the increase in temperature and the ductile-brittle transition
(elongation >5%) occurred in the temperature range of 700 e800 /C14 C. Furthermore, the
fracture toughness initially increased with the increase in temperature and reached the
maximum value of 45 MPa m
1/2 at 850 /C14 C, followed by a decrease. It is worth emphasizing
that the DS Ti47Al2Cr2Nb alloy exhibited a brittle trans-lamellar fracture at room tem-
perature, which was similar to cleavage fracture. On the other hand, the fracture at high
temperatures was inﬂuenced by the propagation and coalescence of microcracks at the
lamellar interface, similar to micro-void accumulation fracture.

1. Introduction
g-TiAl alloys possess outstanding properties, such as low den-
sity, high speciﬁc strength and speciﬁc stiffness as well as good
oxidation resistance at high temperature [1e3]. Therefore, these
alloys are considered ideal candidates for replacing nickel-based
superalloys in aerospace applications, requiring high operating
temperatures (600e900
/C14 C). The microstructure signiﬁcantly in-
ﬂuences the mechanical properties of TiAl alloys. In particular,
the fully lamellar microstructure displays an excellent combi-
nation of strength and creep resistance at high temperatures
[4,5]. However, low room-temperature ductility and poor frac-
ture toughness limit the widespread application of TiAl alloys.
Systematic studies [ 6e8] have been conducted on the
deformation and fracture behavior of lamellar microstructure
using TiAl polysynthetically twinned (PST) crystals. The me-
chanical properties of laminated TiAl alloys are extremely
anisotropic with respect to the lamellar orientation. When the
lamellar orientation of the TiAl alloy is parallel or inclined to
the tensile orientation, it exhibits excellent room-temperature
ductility [ 9]. Previously, Chen et al. [ 10] reported that the
directionally solidiﬁed Ti e45Ale8Nb (at. %) alloy with aligned
lamellar structure exhibits an average ductility of 6.9% and
tensile strength of 978 MPa at room temperature. Moreover, its
creep life is up to 600 h, 10 times that of polycrystalline
Ti48Al2Cr2Nb alloy. Therefore, controlling the lamellar
orientation by directional solidiﬁcation (DS) is an effective
method to improve the room temperature ductility of TiAl
alloys. Extensive work has been carried out to achieve
columnar-grain TiAl alloys with lamellar orientation, parallel
to the growth direction by DS, using the seeding technology
[11,12] or controlling the solidiﬁcation path [ 13,14]. These
studies have focused on controlling the lamellar orientation by
optimizing solidiﬁcation parameters during DS of TiAl alloys.
The investigation on the mechanical properties of DS TiAl al-
loys at high temperatures is still limited though. Ding et al. [15]
studied the deformation behavior of DS Ti47Al2Cr2Nb alloy at
800
/C14 C. However, the mechanical properties of DS TiAl alloys at
other temperatures have not been systematically investigated
yet. In fact, the effect of temperature on mechanical properties
of DS TiAl alloys determines their operating environment
compared to other structural materials.
In this study, Ti47Al2Cr2Nb alloys were directionally so-
lidiﬁed at a low growth rate. The lamellar orientation of
columnar-grain was inclined at 0
/C14 and 45 /C14 to the growth di-
rection by controlling the solidiﬁcation path. Moreover, tensile
and three-point bending tests were performed in the tem-
perature range of room temperature (RT) to 900
/C14 C and the
inﬂuence of temperature on tensile properties and fracture
toughness of DS Ti47Al2Cr2Nb alloy was systematically
investigated. The fracture behavior at RT and high tempera-
ture (400 e900
/C14 C) was also analyzed in terms of fracture
morphology and crack propagation mode.
2. Experimental section
Ti47Al2Cr2Nb alloy prepared by using pure titanium (99.97 wt
%), pure aluminum (99.99 wt%), pure chromium (99.95 wt%)
and titanium /C0 50 wt% niobium master alloy. The titanium-
50 wt% niobium master alloy buttons were melted using pure
titanium (99.97 wt%) and pure niobium (99.95 wt%) in an arc
melting furnace [ 16]. After loading 15 kg of raw materials into
a cold crucible induction furnace, the chamber was evacu-
ated to approximately 5 /C2 10
/C0 2 Pa and then purged with pure
Ar gas. This process was repeated twice before the ﬁnal
backﬁlling step, which resulted in an Ar pressure of approx-
imately 500 Pa within the chamber. The alloy was melted at a
power of 300 KW for a duration of 5 min, followed by direct
solidiﬁcation in a graphite mold with an inner diameter of
150 mm to form a cast ingot measuring approximately
200 mm in height. Subsequently, long bar stocks were cut
from the master ingot, with each having a length and diam-
eter of 200 mm and 20 mm respectively, for feeding the melt
pool. The inductive cold crucible had a square inner cavity
(26 mm /C2 26 mm). Four-turn induction coil around the cru-
cible was fed with 50 kHz high-frequency current at the
output power of 50 kW. The furnace was protected by a vac-
uum argon gas atmosphere. During the DS process,
Ti47Al2Cr2Nb alloy was melted by induction heating to form
a melt pool in the crucible, which was stirred through elec-
tromagnetic force. Then, the DS ingot was drawn out from the
crucible and quenched into a Ga eIn alloy pool at drawing
rates of 3.33 mm/s, 10 mm/s, 16.7 mm/s and 25 mm/s, respec-
tively. Finally, DS ingots were obtained with a length of
z100 mm and a section size of 26 mm /C2 26 mm.

3. Results
3.1. As-cast microstructure
Fig. 1 (a) shows the microstructure of as-cast Ti47Al2Cr2Nb,
which was characterized by uniformly distributed coarse
lamellar colonies and a small amount of bright B2 phase and
gray g particles, mainly distributed around colony boundaries
and triple junctions. Furthermore, a few ﬁne white B2 phase
grains can also be seen within the lamellar colonies, as indi-
cated by arrows in Fig. 1 (a). Microstructure was further
observed by subsequent TEM analysis ( Fig. 1 (b)). The bright g
and gray B2 particles in the selected region in Fig. 1 (b) were
identiﬁed by the electron diffraction patterns in Fig. 1 (c) and
(d), respectively.
3.2. Microstructure of DS alloy
Fig. 2 shows the macro- and micro-structure of DS
Ti47Al2Cr2Nb alloy with a low growth rate of 3.33 mm/s. As
shown in Fig. 2 (a), coarser columnar grains parallel to the
longitudinal axis were obtained by DS at lower growth rate.
The morphology of columnar grains greatly depends on the
applied growth rate. Fig. 3 shows the relationship between
columnar grain diameter and growth rate. The average
diameter of columnar grain decreased from 4200 to 1200 mm
when the growth rate was increased from 3.33 to 25 mm/s. In
addition, at higher growth rates, columnar grains tended to
grow inclined toward the central axis, while at lower growth
rates, they tended to grow parallel to the central axis [ 17e19].
Thus, parallel coarse columnar grains were obtained at lower
growth rates.

zone, marked as “A” in Fig. 2 (a), which shows two adjacent
columnar crystals with 0

3.3. Inﬂuence of temperature on tensile properties
DS Ti47Al2Cr2Nb alloy with a low growth rate of 3.33 mm/s was
selected as the research object to study its high temperature
tensile properties. The following two factors were considered.
First, coarse columnar grains render better high-temperature
performance. Second, DS TiAl alloy does not produce 90
/C14 -
oriented lamellae under a low growth rate, which avoids
brittle fracture.
The effect of temperature (ranging from room tempera-
ture to 900 /C14 C) on tensile properties of the alloys is shown in
Fig. 5(a) and (b). The yield strength and tensile strength of DS
Ti47Al2Cr2Nb alloys were insensitive to temperature and
remained stable in the temperature range of RT to 850
/C14 C. The
yield strength and tensile strength at room temperature were
found to be 500 MPa and 610 MPa, respectively, which were
close to those at 850
/C14 C (i.e., 534 MPa and 575 MPa). However,
the yield strength and tensile strength of DS Ti47Al2Cr2Nb
alloy decreased sharply at 900
/C14 C. This indicates that the DS
Ti47Al2Cr2Nb alloy is suitable for practical applications in
the temperature range of RT to 850 /C14 C. Furthermore, DS
Ti47Al2Cr2Nb alloy exhibited limited ductility in the tem-
perature range of RT to 600
/C14 C. However, the ductility
increased to 2.57% and 8.88% at 700 /C14 C and 800 /C14 C, respec-
tively, indicating the occurrence of ductile-to-brittle
transition (elongation >5%), which is consistent with other
work [25]. After that, the ductility increased with the increase
in temperature. When the test temperature was up to 850 /C14 C,
the tensile curves of as-cast alloy and DS alloy displayed
obvious ductile deformation characteristics, and the corre-
sponding elongation values were 12.30% and 17.50%. At all
test temperatures, it can be seen that proper DS technology
improved the properties of TiAl alloy. Fig. 5 (c) presents the
tensile curves of as-cast Ti47Al2Cr2Nb alloy and DS
Ti47Al2Cr2Nb alloy at room temperature, respectively. Yield
strength, ultimate tensile strength and elongation of the as-
cast alloy were 390 MPa, 440 MPa and 0.66%, respectively,
which were all lower than those of DS alloy. Fig. 5 (d) shows
the tensile specimens after being fractured at different
temperatures. At RT and 600
/C14 C, no plastic deformation was
observed in the parallel section of the tensile specimens. At
850
/C14 C, the specimen exhibited obvious plastic deformation.
Fig. 6 presents the TEM images of the inner microstruc-
ture of broken specimen at RT. Both dislocation slip and
twinning deformation can occur concurrently in the g
phase under tensile load at room temperature [ 26e28].
Fig. 6 (a) shows the dislocation slips in the g phase lath. It
can be seen that some screw dislocations moved in rows,
where each dislocation followed the track of the preceding
one. Moreover, low density twinning was observed in the g
phase lath, as shown in Fig. 6 (a). The inset presents the
selected area electron diffraction (SAED) pattern of twin-
ning. The results show that the mechanical twin also hel-
ped the deformation of g phase, but the low density twin
had little effect on the overall deformation at room
temperature.
Fig. 7 (a) shows the fracture morphology of the ﬂat-plate
tensile specimen after being fractured at room temperature.
The DS tensile specimens exhibited typical trans-lamellar
fracture, and river-like patterns similar to the cleavage frac-
ture were also observed. Fig. 7 (b) shows the high-
magniﬁcation SEM images of the river-like patterns. Clearly,
the cracks existed at different positions in lamellae and the
river-like patterns formed steps at the intersection points of
these cracks.
TEM images demonstrate the deformation structure under
tensile load at 850
/C14 C( Fig. 8), where the load was applied in the
vertical direction. Compared with the deformation behavior at
room temperature, higher density dislocations and more
twins were activated within the g phase [ 29e31]. However,
due to the large number of dislocations in the given plane, it
was difﬁcult to resolve individual dislocations in Fig. 8 (a).
Fig. 8(b) shows these dislocations more clearly. It can be seen
that these dislocations arose from within the lamella and
were blocked by the twin interface. Moreover, after high
temperature deformation, the width of the twins also became
thinner (Fig. 8(c)).

after being fractured at 850
/C14 C, showing the trans-lamellar
fracture. Several bending lamellae were observed, indicating
that large plastic deformation occurred before fracture. In
addition, delamination also occurred during the high-
temperature tensile process. According to the fracture
morphology, it can be inferred that the fracture process at

Fig. 12 shows the propagation of the main cracks under
different temperatures. The insets present the digital photo-
graphs of the fractured specimens, showing that the defor-
mation increased with the increase in test temperature.
Hence, more energy was absorbed by plastic deformation
during crack propagation with increase in temperature. As
seen from Fig. 12(a), the cracks propagated across the lamellae
at room temperature. Moreover, the tortuous-zigzag crack
propagation path indicated that the lamellar interfaces
impeded crack propagation and the crack was forced to
change directions frequently. In this case, the toughening
mechanism involved small plastic deformation and the in-
crease in crack surface energy [ 32,33]. Fig. 12 (b) shows that
there was simultaneous crack propagation along the lamellar
layer and through the lamellar layer at 600
/C14 C. Delamination
cracks appeared due to the stress concentration at the crack
tip. Unlike the results at medium and low temperatures, the
main crack propagated along the lamellae at 800
/C14 C, as shown
in Fig. 12 (c). When the temperature ＞ 800 /C14 C, large
deformation occurred in front of the crack. The main crack
was blocked by the lamellar interface and propagated along
the lamellae. Compared with the room temperature specimen
showing negligible plastic deformation, the high-temperature
specimen was bent into a “V” shape and the gaps were largely
opened, as shown in Fig. 12 (c) and (d). Also, the main crack
propagated along the lamellae at 900
/C14 C. Thus, the toughening
mechanism at high temperature was mainly affected by the
plastic deformation of the crack tip. Thus, it is suggested that
the fracture toughness of DS Ti47Al2Cr2Nb alloy was
improved due to dislocation slip and twinning easily caused
by thermal activation at high temperature. The lamellar
interface completely prevented crack propagation across the
lamellae and the deformation process occurred at the crack
tip, implying that the plastic zone absorbed the energy. With
the opening of gap, the crack tended to propagate along the
lamellae. Thus, the improvement in fracture toughness at
higher temperatures can be attributed to more energy ab-
sorption by plastic deformation at the crack tip.

4. Conclusions
In summary, the mechanical properties and fracture
behavior of DS Ti47Al2Cr2Nb alloy were systematically
investigated in the temperature range of RT to 900
/C14 C. From
RT to 850 /C14 C, the yield strength and tensile strength were
maintained at 520 e540 MPa and 570 e590 MPa, respectively.
In addition, a low ductility value of z0.5% was observed in
the temperature range of RT to 600 /C14 C, which increased with
further increase in temperature. At room temperature, the
DS Ti47Al2Cr2Nb alloy exhibited brittle trans-lamellar frac-
ture, similar to cleavage fracture. At high temperatures,
the fracture occurred due to the propagation and coales-
cence of microcracks at the lamellar interface, similar to
micro-void accumulation fracture. The fracture
toughness initially increased with increase in temperature,
followed by a gradual decrease. The fracture toughness
reached the maximum value of 45 MPa m
1/2 at 850 /C14 C. Due to
the increase in plastic deformation at high temperatures,
the crack propagation mode changed from trans-lamellar
propagation to inter-lamellar propagation. The toughening
mechanism at medium and low temperatures
involved small plastic deformation and the increase in crack
surface energy. Moreover, toughening at high temperature
was mainly affected by the plastic deformation of the crack
tip.
