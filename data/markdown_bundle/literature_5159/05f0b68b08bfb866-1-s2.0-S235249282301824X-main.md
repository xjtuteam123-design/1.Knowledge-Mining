ABSTRACT 
Fe-Cr-Al alloy is considered as one of the most prospective accident-tolerant fuel cladding materials due to its 
excellently comprehensive properties. Irradiation performance evaluation of Fe-Cr-Al alloy is the key for suc -
cessful development of fuel-clad system with enhanced accident tolerance. However, the microstructural 
response is largely dependent on irradiated conditions. In the present work, molecular dynamic method is 
employed to simulate the cascade collision processes under high-energy irradiation and tensile deformation 
behaviors of Fe-Cr-Al alloys. The threshold displacement energies (TDEs) in Fe-Cr-Al alloys are calculated at 
different temperatures and along various directions, and the TDEs along different directions exhibit significant 
anisotropy. The effect of alloy compositions on the displacement cascade of Fe-Cr-Al alloys is systematically 
studied, as well as the direction of the primary knock-on atom (PKA), temperature, and PKA energy. At 300 K and 
700 K, no significant dependence between the number of surviving Frenkel pairs and PKA energies is observed in 
Fe-Cr-Al alloys under high-energy irradiation. High temperature and high-energy PKA could potentially lead to 
form large-sized interstitial clusters and a few small-sized vacancy clusters in Fe-Cr-Al alloys. Dislocations or 
dislocation loops can easily form at high temperatures and in high-energy irradiation. The effect of alloy com -
positions, temperatures and PKA energies on the tensile properties of Fe-Cr-Al alloys before and after irradiation, 
is systematically evaluated. By analyzing the corresponding yield strength, ultimate tensile strength and elastic 
modulus, Fe-Cr-Al alloys exhibit the characteristic of irradiation-induced hardening and embrittlement with the 
increase of PKA energy. As the temperature increases, the rigidity of alloys decreases while the irradiation 
embrittlement significantly increases. 
1. Introduction 
The Fukushima Daiichi nuclear accident has made it the hot topic to 
develop new accident-tolerant fuel (ATF) cladding materials in light 
water reactors (LWRs). Fe-Cr-Al alloy is considered as one of the most 
prospective ATF candidate materials due to its excellently comprehen -
sive properties, such as irradiation resistance [1] , low radiation-induced 
swelling [2] , high oxidation resistance under neutron irradiation and 
high temperature environments [3,4] . Irradiation performance evalua -
tion of Fe-Cr-Al alloy is the key for successful development of fuel-clad 
system with enhanced accident tolerance. Neutrons in fission or fusion 
reactors can kick atoms out of their original sites to generate point de -
fects such as vacancies and self-interstitial atoms. Despite the majority of 
point defects annealed through instantaneous in-cascade recombination, 
the surviving defects will suffer diffusion, further recombination, or 
merge into defect clusters [5,6] . By using transmission electron 
microscopy (TEM), irradiation defects in the 15Cr-ODS specimen prefer 
to disperse in the lattice matrix not to form dislocations or dislocation 
loops [7] . The phenomenon was also observed in Refs. [8] . Field et al. 
[9] showed that the main irradiation defects in Fe-Cr-Al alloy were black 
dots (also regarded as dislocation loops with small size), < 100 > and

The composition variously depended on the defect size, number density

Due to the difference in energy density between high and low-energy 
irradiation in the core region of cascade damage, it is necessary for

Irradiation could produce several changes in microstructure of Fe-Cr- 
Al alloys, thereby affecting their mechanical properties, which will lead 
to poor performance and even failure during normal operating condi -
tions. Yamamoto et al. [3] evaluated Fe-(10 20)wt%Cr-(3 5) 
Al-(0 – 0.12)Y alloys and suggested that, precipitation induced by irra -
diation dominated the hardening response of neutron irradiated alloys. 
The results also showed that the tensile properties were insensitive to the 
alloy compositions. Qiao et al. [1] revealed that the alloys became brittle 
after irradiation due to the increasing Al content. In addition, some 
experimental studies [3,13 – 15] have indicated that the irradiation 
conditions with different alloy compositions and temperatures have an 
impact on the microstructure and mechanical properties of Fe-Cr-Al 
alloy, and found that the hardening response of irradiated tensile 
specimens depended on the Cr content. For instance, Yamamoto et al. 
[3] indicated that the alloys with over 10 wt% Cr exhibited irradiation 
embrittlement, and the ductility and fracture toughness reduced due to 
the formation of Cr-rich α ’ phase. Field et al. [13] studied that an in -
crease in high Cr content ( > 10 wt%) will raise the density of the Cr-rich 
α ’ phase in Fe-Cr-Al alloys and enhance the hardening and embrittle -
ment of the alloys. Gussev et al. [14] indicated that increasing the Al 
content to over 5 wt% would improve oxidation resistance and yield 
strength (YS) of Fe-Cr-Al alloys, but increased the difficulty of welding 
and manufacturing the alloys. The results studied by Kondo et al. [15] 
showed that the YS, ultimate tensile strength (UTS), and elastic modulus 
of 12Cr-6Al ODS irradiated alloys increased as the PKA energy 
increased, exhibiting radiation hardening. The welding materials stud -
ied by Gussev et al. [16] exhibited radiation-induced hardening under 
low temperature irradiation. Nevertheless, the high temperature irra -
diation of the GE ’ s model alloy led to radiation-induced softening when 
tensile tested at 600 ℃ and 700 ℃ [17] . In summary, the radiation 
performance of the Fe-Cr-Al alloys is complex, with various mechanisms 
and factors to be considered, such as temperature, PKA energy, alloy 
compositions and so on. However, the complex influence of mechanical 
properties of alloys is time-consuming and expensive. The combination 
of experimental and simulation methods can effectively reduce costs and 
time, which requires efficient computational modeling to accelerate the 
design and application of new materials. MD method is one of the 
commonly useful tools to probe the change trend of stress, strain, energy 
and defect distribution in the process of tensile deformation of materials, 
and discusses the yield and fracture mechanisms of materials at the 
microscale [18,19] . Ye et al. [18] studied the stress, strain, defect dis -
tribution, dislocation density changes, and deformation mechanism of 
Fe-Cr-Al alloys using MD methods. The results showed that increasing 
temperature promoted the formation and growth of defects, leading to a 
decrease in elastic modulus and tensile strength with increasing tem -
perature. Dai et al. [19] studied the elastic constant of Fe-(1 15)wt% 
Cr-(1 5)Al alloys using MD methods and found that as the Cr content 
increased, the elastic constant decreased with the increasing tempera -
ture. Therefore, further systematic investigation on Fe-Cr-Al alloy for 
enhanced ATF application is required in details. 
In the present work, the threshold displacement energies (TDEs) of 
Fe-Cr-Al alloys are calculated at different temperatures and along 
various PKA directions. The displacement cascade processes of Fe-Cr-Al 
alloys under high PKA energy are investigated at the atomic scale. We 
analyze the influence of various PKA energies, temperatures, PKA di -
rections and alloy compositions on displacement cascades. High-energy 
irradiation damage largely changes the microstructure in Fe-Cr-Al alloys 
and the number of point defects, their distribution and morphology are 
related to the irradiation conditions and materials. In addition, the 
tensile properties of Fe-Cr-Al alloys before and after irradiation are 
studied. Under un-irradiated and irradiated conditions, the tensile per -
formance of Fe-Cr-Al alloys is primarily dependent on alloy composi -
tions, temperatures and PKA energies. 
2. Simulation details 
The displacement cascade under high-energy irradiation conditions 
and the tensile tests before and after irradiation in Fe-Cr-Al alloys are 
simulated by using the large-scale atomic/molecular massively parallel 
simulator (LAMMPS) software package. The embedded atom method 
potential in Fe-Cr-Al fitted by Liao et al. [20] through the Finnis – Sinclair 
(F – S) formulism, was used to describe the defect behaviors and the 
interaction potential between atoms. This parameterized potential has 
been successfully used to investigate the evolution of dislocation loops 
and the behavior of Cr-rich α ’ precipitates under cascade damage in 
Fe-Cr-Al alloy [8,11] . A cubic structure of bcc (body centered cubic) α -Fe 
was established, and Cr and Al atoms randomly replaced at the lattice 
positions of Fe to simulate Fe-Cr-Al alloys with different alloy compo -
sitions. Some simulation parameters used in this work, such as the PKA 
energies, PKA directions, temperatures, simulation box dimensions and 
the number of atoms, are listed in Table 1 . Initially, the modeling of 
Fe-Cr-Al alloy was built, which was divided into an inner block and an

Subsequently, a Fe atom located in the inner block was randomly

periodic boundary conditions (PBC) with a time step of 1 fs. As we all 
know, the value of time step can affect the simulation results. If the time 
step is too large, the simulation accuracy decreases because it cannot 
describe the interactions between atoms. If the time step is too small, the 
calculation time will be very long. In order to balance the efficiency of 
the cascade process caused by a time step, variable time step was 
employed for the cascade damage MD simulations. In this approach, the 
MD time step is inversely proportional to the maximum velocity of the 
atom. The time step is determined as follows. The maximum displace -
ment of atoms in each MD loop cannot exceed 0.02 Å, and the value of 
the time step is reset for each step. At the same time, the time for each 
step is not greater than 1 fs and not less than 0.0001 fs.

The evolution behavior of point defects generated by cascade dam -
age will promote the understanding of its response of microstructure and 
macro-properties to collision cascade environment in Fe-Cr-Al alloys. 
Point defects are mainly classified into three types: vacancies, in -
terstitials, and anti-site defects. The irradiation damage is usually re -
flected in the number of final surviving point defects and the size 
distribution of the final surviving point defect clusters. The point defect 
is identified based on Wigner-Seitz (W-S) analysis, which can be per -
formed by the Open Visualization Tool (Ovito) software. Defect cluster 
recognition is based on cluster analysis in Ovito. One of the main 
characteristics of radiation damage is the size distribution of defect 
clusters. The size of defect clusters resulting from collision cascades of 
energetic particles is an important feature to describe the microstruc -
tures of displacement cascades of irradiated materials. Defect cluster 
size is defined as the number of point defects belonging to it. In this 
work, the second nearest neighbor (2NN) and 3NN distances are taken as 
the cutoff radius of vacancy cluster and interstitial cluster, respectively. 
Since the generation and distribution of defect clusters are random, the 
reliable result is obtained on the basis of an average of 5 simulations for 
each PKA energy. Moreover, we can use the dislocation extraction al -
gorithm (DXA) to understand the dislocation configurations formed 
within the damage region.

3. Results and discussion 
The main goal of this work is to understand the effect of defect be -
haviors in Fe-Cr-Al alloys under high-energy irradiation and gain sig -
nificant insights into the mechanical performance related to the 
irradiation damage. In order to study the displacement cascade of Fe-Cr- 
Al alloys with high recoil energy, TDEs of alloy elements are first 
calculated. The dependence of alloy composition, PKA direction, tem -
perature, and PKA energy on the collision cascades of Fe-Cr-Al alloys 
under various irradiation conditions are demonstrated to provide the 
whole picture of displacement cascade. To get closer to evaluating actual 
commercial Fe-Cr-Al alloys, Fe-10Cr-4.8Al (wt%) is selected to study the 
effects of PKA direction, energy, and temperature on displacement 
cascade. 
3.1. Threshold displacement energy 
The values of TDE in Fe-10Cr-4.8Al have been calculated along 6 
different directions at 300 – 800 K. As shown in Fig. 1 , the value of TDEs 
strongly depends on the incident direction of PKAs, PKA atom types and 
temperatures. The TDEs of Fe and Cr are very small along the [100] 
direction, with the average values of 20 eV and 16 eV, respectively. The 
TDEs of Fe, Cr and Al atoms along [135] range from 40 to 99 eV, 
46 – 80 eV, and 22 – 53 eV in the temperature region between 300 and 
800 K, respectively. Obviously, the results indicate that the TDEs do not 
change significantly with increasing temperature. Dong et al. [22] 
studied that the resulting temperature increase might be negligible for 
energies up to 50 eV, it could influence TDE at much higher energies, 
such as 300 eV. In the present work, the PKA energy is 15 – 100 eV. 
Therefore, the effect of temperature on TDEs can be negligible under 
low-energy conditions, which is consistent with the results obtained 
from reference [22] .

cause interstitial atoms far away from vacancies and lead to the weak the 
recombination of interstitial and vacancy. The same phenomenon has 
also been found in studies on the TDE of other bcc structural materials 
[25,26] . Therefore, the TDEs exhibit significant anisotropy among the 
TDEs for the various incident directions of PKAs. 
From Fig. 1 , the TDEs of Fe along [135] at 400 – 800 K have the 
maximum except for [110] at 300 K. For Cr as PKA, the TDEs along 
[135] at 300 – 700 K have the maximum except for [110] at 800 K. The 
maximum of TDEs for Al is along [110]. When Fe atom is used as the 
PKA atom, the TDEs have a strong dependence on direction, while for Al 
atom, the opposite is true. It can be seen that the anisotropy along [110] 
and [135] directions is strongly dependent on atom types. The degree of 
anisotropy of Fe is the highest, while that of Al is the lowest. The reason 
may be that the relative atomic masses of Fe and Cr atoms are slightly 
different, which is about twice larger than that of Al atom. The results 
indicate that the TDEs for each type of PKA in Fe-10Cr-4.8Al alloy is 
highly dependent, and TDEs of Fe exhibit the largest degree of anisot -
ropy, while those of Al exhibit the smallest.

3.2.1. Effect of PKA direction on cascade damage 
Since the TDE of Fe appears to be highly anisotropic in Fe-10Cr-4.8Al 
alloys, the displacement cascades in different PKA directions are 
calculated for Fe as PKA atom. The difference in the atomic arrangement

3.2.2. Effect of temperature on cascade damage 
Considering the practical application and experimental embrittle -
ment temperature of Fe-10Cr-4.8Al alloy, a temperature range from 
300 K to 800 K is used to analyze the effect of temperature on the 
displacement cascade under PKA energy of 50 keV along [135]. The 
number of FPs as a function of time is shown in the Fig. 3 . As shown in 
Fig. 3 and Table 2 , the number of FPs rapidly increases and reaches its 
peaks at around 1.9 ps. Subsequently, the number of FPs begins to 
decrease and remains nearly constant at around 30 ps. The peak number 
of FPs increases with the rise of temperature from 400 K to 700 K and 
reduces at 800 K. As the number of FPs at the peak increases, the time 
required to reach the peak increases. It is interesting that the average 
number of final surviving FPs decreases with the increasing tempera -
ture. At high temperature, the irradiation defects could efficiently 
annihilate by accelerating migration of point defects and thus enhancing 
the possibility of vacancies-interstitials recombination. The cascade ef -
ficiency of alloys with different temperatures ranges from 0.38 to 0.81, 
as shown in Table 2 . It can be seen that the cascade efficiency initially 
increases and then rapidly reaches a maximum at 600 K. The cascade 
efficiency decreases above 600 K. The change of cascade efficiency is 
consistent with that of the TDE as a function of temperature. It indicates 
that temperature has a significant impact on the irradiation performance 
of Fe-Cr-Al alloys, affecting the final surviving FPs and the cascade ef -
ficiency during the irradiation damage.

3.2.5. Defect clusters and dislocations 
The dynamic behavior of defect clusters and dislocations is one of the 
important characteristics that affect the microstructure evolution during 
irradiation damage. Fig. 6 illustrates the four stages of 50 keV Fe PKA 
cascade damage in Fe-10Cr-4.8Al alloys at 300 K. In general, the cascade 
process can be divided into four stages: defect generation, appearance of 
thermal spike, defect annihilation, and cascade annealing to form sur -
viving FPs [6] . In the initial stage of cascade damage, defects are 
generated by collision events and rapidly increase, as shown in Fig. 6 (a) 
and (b). As shown in Fig. 6 (c), the number of vacancies and interstitials 
reach their peaks within 2 ps. Sequentially, interstitials and vacancies 
recombine and annihilate, and reach the stable state at around 30 ps, as 
shown in Fig. 6 (d) and (e). Defect recovery on the final stage, with the 
majority of point defects annihilating and few isolated point defects 
ultimately leaving, as shown in Fig. 6 (d). It is clear that the dominant 
defects are single interstitial and mono-vacancies in cascade annealing 
stage. However, displacement cascade combines with defect clusters 
nucleate and dissociation. Meanwhile, the clusters annihilation reduces 
to point defects dissociation. Therefore, the competition between 
nucleation and elimination of small defect clusters represents a dynamic 
process for defect cluster generation during displacement cascades. 
After analyzing the defects distribution of Fe-Cr-Al alloys under 
various irradiation conditions, we find that the accumulation of defects 
displays different types: near spherical dense type and non-spherical 
relatively dispersed type [30] . In this work, the defect accumulation in 
most simulated samples display non-spherical relatively dispersed type.

4. Conclusions 
The evolution of displacement cascades with high-energy PKA 
ranging from 50 keV to 65 keV and tensile properties for Fe-Cr-Al alloys 
are performed using MD simulation. The TDEs for Fe, Cr, and Al are 
obtained along various directions at different temperatures. We also 
analyze the effect of PKA direction, temperature, alloy composition, and 
PKA energy on cascade damage in Fe-Cr-Al alloys, as well as the atom -
istic mechanisms of cascade damages. The effects of alloy composition, 
temperature, and PKA energy on the tensile properties of the alloy are 
studied. Based on the present work, the statements are made as follows: 
(1) Due to the low TDEs, the effect of temperature on TDE can be 
negligible under low-energy conditions. The TDEs along the 
[100] direction are much lower than that along [135] and [110] 
directions. It indicates that the TDEs along different directions 
exhibit significant anisotropy. In addition, the TDEs along [110] 
and [135] directions are strongly dependent on atom types. 
Moreover, the TDEs of Fe exhibit the largest degree of anisotropy. 
(2) The displacement cascades of Fe-Cr-Al alloys under high-energy 
PKA atomic collisions are calculated. The larger TDEs will lead 
to the less final surviving FPs along different PKA directions. The
