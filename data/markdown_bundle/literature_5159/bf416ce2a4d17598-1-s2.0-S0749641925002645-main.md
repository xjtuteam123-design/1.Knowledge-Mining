ABSTRACT
High/medium-entropy alloys (HEAs/MEAs) are gaining attention for their superior mechanical
properties, especially in applications that require high strength and impact resistance. Among
these, body-centered cubic (BCC) refractory HEAs are notable for their high-temperature stability
and mechanical strength. However, achieving an optimal balance between strength and ductility
remains challenging. This study focuses on TiZrNbV BCC-HEA, which exhibits not only impressive
strength (yield strength > 1 GPa) but also good ductility (~9 % uniform elongation). Its plastic
deformation and strengthening mechanisms are investigated through uniaxial tensile tests, cyclic
load-unload-reload tests, and stress-relaxation tests. Key to understanding its mechanical
behavior is the evolution of dislocation structures, including mobile dislocations, geometrically
necessary dislocations (GNDs), and statistically stored dislocations (SSDs). The Kocks-Mecking
model is employed to examine hardening mechanisms. At low strains, heterogeneous
deformation-induced (HDI) hardening dominates, while forest dislocation hardening prevails at
higher strains. This work sheds light on the interplay of dislocation density evolution and
hardening mechanisms in achieving the high strength and ductility of alloys. These mechanisms
of dislocation synergistic evolution and strengthening are valuable for designing advanced alloys
with optimized mechanical properties, paving the way for high-performance materials in extreme
environments like aerospace, automotive, and energy industries.

BCC HEAs are characterized by better room and often high temperature strengths (Senkov et al., 2013; Senkov et al., 2010; Zhang
et al., 2025b), but lower ductility. The BCC HEAs composed of enough Mo, Ta and Hf, such as Hf-Ta-Ti-Nb-Zr and Mo-Nb-Ta-Ti-V
alloys, possess excellent room temperature strength, and their strength performance at high temperature is also prominent (Gong
et al., 2024; Qiu et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2025a). However, when these alloy samples are subjected to tensile testing
at room temperature, catastrophic fracture often occurs with very limited elongation, which greatly limits their applications (Cook
et al., 2024).
The TiZrNbV alloy has attracted considerable interest for its outstanding mechanical performance, maintaining ~10% uniform
elongation with a yield strength above 1 GPa (Wang et al., 2021) and exhibiting excellent delamination and dynamic fracture
resistance under high strain rates (Ma et al., 2025; Ren et al., 2021; Ren et al., 2023; Ren et al., 2025). Recent investigations on
Ti–Zr–Nb–V refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) have revealed that cold rolling and recrystallization annealing (CRRA) promote
strength–ductility synergy by activating multiple slip systems (Tian et al., 2024) and introducing heterogeneous lamellar structures
that enhance work hardening via heterogeneous deformation-induced (HDI) strengthening (Ma et al., 2024). Strategies such as partial
recrystallization and compositional tuning have further improved the balance between strength and ductility by combining
solid-solution, grain-boundary, and dislocation-based strengthening (Chen et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025b). While these studies have
deepened understanding of the deformation and strengthening behavior of RHEAs, most focus on empirical or processing-based im-
provements. A quantitative correlation between microstructural evolution and strengthening mechanisms in TiZrNbV alloys remains
insufficiently established.
The mechanical behavior of metallic materials is a fundamental focus in materials science, with strengthening mechanisms playing
a pivotal role during plastic deformation. To accurately predict deformation responses under various external loads, researchers
establish connections between the microscopic evolution of dislocations and the macroscopic mechanical behavior through
strengthening models. This is particularly critical for high-performance alloys and high-entropy alloys, where the mechanistic un-
derstanding of strengthening models elucidates the evolution of both strength and plasticity across different loading conditions.
The core of strengthening models lies in the mathematical characterization of the resistance to interaction between dislocations(Cai
et al., 2025; Huang et al., 2024; Peng et al., 2021) and microstructural features, such as grain boundaries(Feng et al., 2017; Jia et al.,
2025) and phase boundaries(Cao et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2025a). To simulate deformation with high precision, multiscale frameworks
that integrate dislocation density evolution, grain size effects, and back stress have been developed. Keller et al. (Keller and Hug, 2017)
employed the Kocks-Mecking model, which incorporates the average dislocation free path, to analyze the combined effects of grain
size and surface effects on strain hardening. Yang et al. (2023) proposed a constitutive model that incorporates the coupling of
dislocation density with thermal stress, GNDs, and dynamic strain aging, which successfully captures the deformation behavior of
TA18 titanium alloy. Qi et al. (2024) identified short transients in strain hardening behavior due to grain refinement, using a modified
Kocks-Mecking model in conjunction with finite element analysis. Feng et al. (2024) demonstrated that stress-induced phase trans-
formation can mediate hardening in high-entropy alloys, thereby extending the applicability of existing models.
During plastic deformation, the accumulation of mobile dislocations at phase and grain boundaries generates GNDs, which are a
significant source of back stress strengthening (Feng et al., 2017; Jia et al., 2025), particularly in multiphase or polycrystalline ma-
terials (Dong et al., 2015; Yang et al., 2023). Liu et al. (2019) revealed that the interactions between phase boundaries and dislocations
in duplex steels substantially enhance yield strength and improve resistance to plastic instability. Joshi (Joshi et al., 2023) compared
additively manufactured alloys with conventionally forged alloys, demonstrating that dislocation cells associated to dendrites work
synergistically to elevate yield strength through the combined effects of back stress and effective stress. While current Kocks-Mecking
models primarily focus on the evolution of forest dislocations, few frameworks have been developed that can simultaneously predict
the evolution of GNDs, SSDs, and mobile dislocations densities. The development of constitutive models for polycrystalline materials
remains a significant challenge in the field.
In this work, TiZrNbV HEAs with single-phase BCC structure was constructed. The mechanical properties and hardening mecha-
nisms were studied, particularly the heterogeneous deformation-induced (HDI) hardening and forest dislocation hardening mecha-
nisms were analyzed in detail. Then, the corresponding microstructural mechanisms for TiZrNbV alloy were studied. Finally, a
dislocation-based constitutive model was established, capable of predicting the evolution of the HDI stress and the dislocation
strengthening stress in material during the loading process. Additionally, the model can predict the evolution of GNDs, SSDs, mobile
dislocations, and the dislocation mean free path. This study provides a theoretical foundation for the material design and performance
prediction of high-entropy alloys.

3.1. Pre-loading microstructure
The inverse pole figure (IPF) of EBSD for as-cast TiZrNbV sample prior to tensile testing is presented in Fig. 1 (a). It is clearly shown
that the TiZrNbV sample has a large number of coarse-grained (CGs) structures and a small number of fine-grained (FGs) structures.
Moreover, the average grain sizes of the TiZrNbV sample are 163 ± 7 μ m show in Fig. 1 (b). The EDS-mapping show Ti, Nb, V elements

To summarize the observed evolution of dislocations and their interactions, plastic deformation proceeds through four charac-
teristic stages, as shown in Fig. 19 . At small strains, grain misorientation necessitates deformation compatibility, causing mobile
dislocations to slip within grains and accumulate at boundaries, which increases the GNDs density. With increasing plastic strain,
deformation compatibility improves, reflected by reduced grain misorientation and weakened strain gradients near boundaries,
leading to the stabilization of GNDs density. Concurrently, the SSDs density within grains rises continuously. Mobile dislocations
initially increase rapidly before stabilizing, primarily due to extensive dislocation multiplication that shortens the mean free path to its
minimum. With further straining, dislocation structures evolve from localized pile-ups near grain boundaries into more widely
distributed networks within grains, a homogenization process that reduces intergranular incompatibility and thereby enhances both
boundary compatibility and macroscopic ductility. The detailed analysis of each stage is presented as follows:

4. Conclusions
This study systematically examines the plastic deformation behavior and strengthening mechanisms of TiZrNbV alloy under
uniaxial tension. Using cyclic loading-unloading-reloading, stress relaxation experiments, EBSD and TEM analysis, we calibrate an
enhanced Kocks-Mecking model that quantitatively tracks the evolution of geometrically necessary dislocations (GNDs) density,
statistically stored dislocations (SSDs) density, mobile dislocations density, and dislocation mean free path. The modified Kocks-
Mecking model effectively correlates "loading strain – dislocation configuration – strengthening mechanisms", thereby providing
theoretical support for the strengthening-toughening design and performance prediction of refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs).
The model also distinguishes the contributions of hetero deformation-induced and forest dislocation strengthening to the mechanical
properties of alloys. This work introduces a new method for exploring dislocation evolution and material strengthening, offering
deeper insights into the high strength and ductility of alloys. The main findings are summarized as follows:
1. The TiZrNbV alloy achieves a notable synergy of high yield strength (~1.1 GPa) and uniform plasticity (9%). This stems from a
significant increase in mobile dislocation density during the elastic-plastic transition, followed by rising densities of GNDs and SSDs
during sustained plastic flow.
2. Plastic deformation reveals heterogeneous deformation and dislocation activity within the homogeneous microstructure. Early
strain localizes near grain boundaries, generating high GND concentrations and inducing pronounced hetero-deformation induced
hardening. As strain increases, intragranular deformation intensifies, marked by prolific SSDs activity and forest dislocation
hardening. When the plastic strain reaches 0.04, HDI strengthening contributes about 52% of the overall strength and approxi-
mately 63 % of the work hardening, while forest dislocation strengthening accounts for roughly 48 % of the strength and 37 % of
the work hardening. However, the contribution of HDI strengthening to work hardening decreases as the strain progresses, with
forest dislocation strengthening becoming the dominant contributor to both strength and work hardening at higher plastic strains.
3. The stabilization of mobile dislocation density, enhanced grain boundary compatibility, and improved deformation homoge-
nization act synergistically to drive the high-toughness mechanism. This synergistic effect is facilitated primarily by dislocation
homogenization, mediated via LAGB, which transitions dislocation structures from boundary pile-ups to distributed intragranular
networks with increasing strain. The intensification of dislocation homogenization is driven by an increasing number of active slip
