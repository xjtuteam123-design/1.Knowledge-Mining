Abstract
High-temperature structural metals remain in high demand for aerospace aircraft, gas turbine engines, and nuclear power 
plants. Refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) with superior mechanical properties at elevated temperatures are promising 
candidates for high-temperature structural materials. In this work, a WTaMoNbTi RHEA with adequate room temperature 
plasticity and considerable strength at 1600 °C was fabricated by vacuum arc-melting. The room temperature fracture strain 
of the as-cast WTaMoNbTi RHEA was 7.8%, which was about 5.2 times that of the NbMoTaW alloy. The alloy exhibited a 
strong resistance to high-temperature softening, with a high yield strength of 173 MPa and compressive strength of 218 MPa 
at 1600 °C. The WTaMoNbTi RHEA possessed excellent phase stability in the range of room temperature to 2000 °C. The 
dendritic grains grew into equiaxed grains after compression test at 1600 °C due to the dynamic recrystallization process 
at high temperature. This work presents a promising high-temperature structural material that can be applied at 1600 °C.

1 Introduction
The high-entropy alloys (HEAs) tend to form a single solid 
solution of multiple principal elements in equal or near-equal 
atomic ratios [1–3], which have attracted substantial research 
interest over the past decade due to their excellent properties 
in strength [4–7], wear resistance [8], radiation resistance [9, 
10], fatigue resistance [11], oxidation resistance [12], and 
corrosion resistance [13, 14]. Refractory high-entropy alloys 
(RHEAs), composed of refractory elements (e.g., W, Re, Ta, 
Mo, Nb, Hf, V, Zr, and Ti), show the potential as new high-
temperature structural alloys in refractory applications, such 
as aerospace aircraft, gas turbine engines, and nuclear power 
plants [15–18]. Ni-based superalloys, the most commercially 
used high-temperature alloys, almost melt at about 1200 °C 
[19, 20], while many RHEAs still maintain high strength 
at that temperature [21, 22]. For example, the strengths of 
NbMoTaW and NbMoTaVW RHEAs were over 400 MPa at 
1600 °C [19], which was far beyond the service temperature 
of Ni-based superalloys. However, both the NbMoTaW and 
NbMoTaVW RHEAs were intrinsically brittle at room tem-
perature [19]; thus, their practical application is hindered.
Recently, many works have been carried out to improve 
the mechanical properties of NbMoTaW RHEA by alloy -
ing. By adding Ti [ 23], Zr [ 24], Hf [ 24], B [ 25], and Si 
[26] to NbMoTaW RHEA, both the strength and plastic-
ity of the alloys have been improved significantly. Particu-
larly, the WTaMoNbTi RHEA showed good plasticity of 
11.5% at room temperature, which was significantly better 
than the 1.5% of the NbMoTaW RHEA [19, 23]. Besides 
the enhanced plasticity, the WTaMoNbTi maintained a

2 Experimental
WTaMoNbTi RHEA was prepared by vacuum arc melting 
in an argon atmosphere. The purities of W, Ta, Mo, Nb, 
and Ti were higher than 99.9 at%. The ingot was flipped 
and re-melted five times to ensure chemical homogeneity. 
The crystal structure was identified using a D8 ADVANCE 
X-ray diffractometer (XRD) with Cu Kα radiation. The 
microstructure and chemical composition were investigated 
by a scanning electron microscope (SEM) equipped with an 
energy dispersive spectrometry (EDS) detector. Cylindri-
cal specimens for compression tests were 5.4 mm in height 
and 3.6 mm in diameter. The compression tests were per -
formed using an Instron mechanical testing machine with a 
strain rate of 0.001  s−1. The high-temperature mechanical 
properties were measured by a mechanical testing machine at 
1600 °C in an argon atmosphere. The samples were heated to 
1600 °C at a rate of 10 °C/min and kept for 10 min, followed 
by compressing with a 25% height reduction at a constant 
strain rate of 0.001  s−1. The morphology of the deformed 
samples was examined by SEM. In order to examine the 
phase stability, the as-cast ingots were heated to annealing 
temperatures (1500 °C, 1600 °C, 1800 °C, and 2000 °C) at 
a rate of 10 °C/min, kept for 10 min, and furnace cooling in 
a vacuum graphite furnace.

Besides, no evidence of grain deformation was identified 
in the images.

5 Conclusions
The mechanical properties and phase stability of the WTaM-
oNbTi RHEA at elevated temperatures were studied. The 
deformation mechanism was discussed after the characteri-
zation of microstructures by using XRD and SEM analyses. 
The following conclusions were reached:
1. The compressive strength (1681 MP) and the frac-
ture strain (7.8%) of the as-cast WTaMoNbTi RHEA 
were about 1.4 times and 5.2 times that of the NbMo-
TaW RHEA. The alloy exhibited a strong resistance to 
high-temperature softening, with a high yield strength 
of 173 MPa and compressive strength of 218 MPa at 
1600 °C.
2. The WTaMoNbTi RHEA possessed high phase stability 
with a single BCC solid solution structure in the range of 
room temperature to 2000 °C. The dendritic grains grew 
into equiaxed grains after compression test at 1600 °C 
due to the dynamic recrystallization process in high tem-
perature. The high softening temperature preserves the 
high strength of WTaMoNbTi RHEA at 1600 °C.
3. This work presents a promising high-temperature struc-
tural material with good room temperature plasticity and 
considerable strength at 1600 °C.
