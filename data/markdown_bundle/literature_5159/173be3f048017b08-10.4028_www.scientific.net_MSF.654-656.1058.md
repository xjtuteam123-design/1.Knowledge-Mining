Keywords: Bulk metallic glasses, high entropy alloys, Bridgman solidification, dendrite/BMG 
composites, tensile elongation. 
Abstract. Bulk metallic glasses (BMGs) and high entropy allo ys (HEAs) have unique structures at 
the length scales of micro- and nano-metre, and exhibit unique properties, which make them potential 
materials for structural applications. The tensile ductility of dendrite/BMG composites can be greatly 
improved by Bridgman solidification. The BCC struct ured HEA with a composition of 
Ti 0.5 AlCoCrFeNi exhibits ultrahigh fracture strength whi ch is competitive to most of the BMGs, 
moreover, the strength can be sustained at high temperatures. The phase changes of HEAs are closely 
related to the atomic packing efficiency (APE). 
Introduction 
Recently, bulk metallic glasses (BMGs) and high ent ropy alloys (HEAs) were developed to be 
potentially used as the structural and functional m aterials [1-12]. Both of the two kinds of materials 
exhibit ultrahigh strength [2, 6]. However, BMGs are usually brittle and can not retain high strength 
up to high temperature, e.g. above the glass transi tion temperatures usually about 300-400°C Thus 
dendrite/BMG composites were developed to improve the ductility at room temperature, especially by 
controlling the morphology and the distribution of the dendrites using semi-solid and Bridgman 
solidification techniques [13-15]. The HEAs can com pensate for the materials of the BMGs for the 
high temperature applications. This is because the high entropy effect will be enhanced at the high 
temperature [6]. The phase change and the phase for mation of HEAs are mainly controlled by the 
atomic packing efficiency (APE) of the phases [9-12]. 
 
Experimental Procedures 
The HEAs and BMG composites were prepared by arc-me lting mixtures of high-purity metal 
elements in a Ti-gettered high-purity argon atmosph ere. The alloy was re-melted several times to 
improve homogeneity. The ingot was then re-melted under high vacuum (5×10 
-3 Pa) and suction cast 
into a water-cooled copper mould to obtain a cylindrical rod of diameter 5 mm and length 70 mm. 
The Bridgman solidification was carried out by plac ing crushed pieces of ingot into alumina 
tubes with an internal diameter of 3 mm and a wall thickness of ~ 1 mm. Then the alloys were heated 
to the semi-solid state, holding for 10 minutes, an d Bridgman solidification was carried out with 
withdrawal velocities of between 0.2 – 2.0 mm/s through a temperature gradient of ~ 45 K/mm into a 
water-cooled Ga-In-Sn liquid alloy. 
Quasi-static compressive tests were performed on 6 mm long sections cut from the 3 mm 
diameter cylinders (aspect ratio=2:1) with a MTS 809 materials testing machine at room temperature 
with a strain rate of 2 ×10 −4 s−1. 
The samples for tensile testing were machined into a dog-bone geometry, which had a nominal 
gage diameter of 2 mm and gage length of 13 - 15 mm. The tensile tests were conducted at a strain rate 
of 8 ×10 −4s−1 at ambient temperature using a CMT4105 universal e lectromechanical tension testing 
system. The cross-head with a constant displacement rate of 0.3 mm/min, and the percentage

Results and Discussion 
Dendrite/BMG composite by Bridgman solidification. The typical microstructure of the 
dendrite/BMG composite prepared by Bridgman solidif ication is illustrated in Fig. 1. It can be seen 
that dendrites are homogeneously dispersed in the g lass matrix. The inset of the Fig. 1 shows the 
typical XRD pattern of the samples fabricated by Bridgman solidification. The β-Zr phase peaks with 
a BCC structure and an amorphous hump can be seen in the XRD trace.

The room-temperature compressive engineering stress -strain curves of the composites 
synthesized with different withdrawal velocities V are shown in Fig. 2. All the composites exhibit 
fairly high yielding strength, fracture strength, a nd work hardening capacity. Especially, the 
composite synthesized with V= 1.0 mm/s exhibits the yielding strength, fracture s trength, and plastic 
strain of 1340 MPa, 2930 MPa, and 31.3 %, respectively. The lateral surface of the deformed sample 
with V= 1.0 mm/s is illustrated in the inset of Fig. 2, and patterns of profuse and regularly spaced shear 
bands are observed, in good agreement with the macr oscopic plastic strain. The shear bands have a 
dominant spacing of several microns in agreement wi th the typical dimension for BMG matrix 
composites [15]. 
Tension tests on samples with V=1.0 mm/s were perfo rmed. The percentage elongation after 
fracture was measured to be 7 % and the maximum tensile strength was 1530 MPa. Fig. 3(a) displays 
the lateral surface of the sample after tensile fra cture. It can be seen that obvious necking occurs 
compared with the sample before tension, illustrated in the inset of Fig. 3(a), which provides evidence 
of the definite tensile ductility. Fig. 3(b) shows a magnified portion near the fracture plane. Dense 
patterns of primary and secondary shear bands as we ll as microcracks along the shear bands are 
visible on the surface. The fracture surface is exh ibited in the inset of Fig. 3(b). Radiating vein 
patterns dominate the fracture surface and the veins propagate towards the outside, which is more or 
less like the failure mode under tension for the monolithic BMG.

Mechanical properties of the HEAs at high temperature. The high temperature compressive 
properties of the AlCoCrFeNi and Ti 0.5 AlCoCrFeNi alloys were studied, and the stress-strain curves 
are shown in the Fig. 4. It can be seen that the 0.2% proof strength, σ 0.2 , decreases with the increasing 
the testing temperature. The σ 0.2 of the AlCoCrFeNi alloy decreases from 1500 MPa at the RT 
temperature to 156 MPa at 1000 °C, especially at 500 °C, the σ 0.2 is 990 MPa, as shown in Fig. 4(a); 
while for the Ti 0.5 AlCoCrFeNi alloy, the σ 0.2 decreases from 2260 MPa at RT temperature to 191 MPa 
at 1000 °C, and at 500 °C, the σ 0.2 is 1450 MPa, as shown in Fig. 4(b); Thus the HEAs can sustain a 
high yielding strength even at 500 °C. 
Phase change of the HEAs. Fig. 5 shows the XRD patterns for the Al yCoCrFeNiCu 1-y alloys. The 
alloy CoCrFeNiCu has a FCC phase structure, after adding Al by subst itution for Cu, the phase 
changes from FCC to BCC with the increase of y from 0 to 1. Similar results were also observed in the 
Ti 0.5 CoCrFeNiCu HEAs. The BCC structure has lower atomic packing efficiency (APE) than the 
FCC structure, thus it can be concluded that the la rger atoms of Al lead to the phase transition from 
the structure of higher APE to lower APE. 
The chemical behaviors of the elements are also should be considered for the phase change of the 
HEAs. The chemical behaviors are usually characterized by the electronegativity, this is similar to the 
enthalpy of mixing ∆ Hmix . ∆ Hmix is often in a unit of kJ/mol, while the high entropy according to 
Yeh[5] is about 13 J/mol.K. For forming of the random solid solution [17], -15< ∆ Hmix <5, and at 1000 
K, the HEAs can be defined as Ω =1000 ∆ Smix /∆ Hmix >1. This criteria entropy is for the formation of the 
solid solution.

Summary 
Tensile elongation of 7% can be obtained for a dend rite/BMG composite fabricated by Bridgman 
solidification, the HEAs can sustain the high strength to at least 500 C, and the phase transition of the 
HEAs are mainly controlled by the APEs.
