Abstract 
High-temperature compressive strengths of titanium carbide (TiC) reinforced stainless steel 
composites fabricated by liquid pressing infiltrati on process were investigated at various 
temperatures. Effect of TiC on compressive strength of SUS431 was found to increase with 
increasing testing temperature. TiC reinforcement s uccessfully suppressed softening of 
SUS431 matrix, resulting in about 5 times higher st rength than that of SUS431 at 1050 °C. 
The novel SUS431 composites reinforced with uniform ly dispersed, high volume fraction 
TiC show great promise for applications in high-temperature environments. 
 
Keywords : Titanium carbide (TiC), Stainless steel, metal mat rix composites (MMCs), High-
temperature compressive strength

1. Introduction 
To develop infrastructure industries such as automo biles, aircraft, transportation 
equipment, and power plants, there is an increasing need for material that can dramatically 
reduce weight and improve durability at high temper ature compared to conventional steel 
materials. Due to tailored characteristics with uni que structural and functional properties that 
cannot be achieved in monolithic metal structures, composite materials have attracted 
scientific and industrial interest [1-9]. The addit ion of ceramic reinforcements, such as 
titanium carbide (TiC), titanium diboride (TiB 2), and niobium carbide (NbC) to a steel matrix 
has been considered a promising approach for improv ing mechanical and physical properties, 
such as strength, elastic modulus, hardness, and co efficient of thermal expansion (CTE) with 
large weight reduction, beyond what can be achieved through alloy design [10-21]. Among 
the various types of ceramic reinforcement, TiC is considered the best reinforcement for steel 
composites due to its high hardness, good wettabili ty, and low density [10-16]. However, due 
to agglomeration of reinforcement and defects intro duced during the fabrication process, 
mechanical properties of steel composites generally decrease as the volume fraction of 
ceramic reinforcement increases [22,23]. Furthermore, density mismatches are an obstacle for 
uniform dispersion of TiC particles in the steel ma trix by floating during the casting process. 
Recently, AlMangour et al. demonstrated the influen ce of the TiC reinforcemnet on the 
microstructure and mechanical properties of 316L MM Cs fabricated by selective laser 
melting [24,25]. Small TiC particle sizes and large contents considerably improved 
mechanical properties of TiC-316L composites. 
In our previous research, uniformly dispersed TiC–S KD11 composite was successfully 
fabricated by liquid pressing infiltration (LPI) pr ocess [13]. The composite has superior 
room-temperature mechanical properties such as wear properties and tensile and compressive 
strength. However, the oxidation resistance of the composite was less than those of stainless

steels such as SUS431 and SUS630 (17–4PH). Therefor e, oxidation resistance of TiC–
SKD11 composites fabricated by LPI process was also evaluated in the previous research 
[15]. Superior mechanical properties at elevated temperature also an essential requirement for 
metal matrix composites (MMCs) to be used as high-temperature structural materials. 
In this paper, we fabricated uniformly dispersed, h igh-volume fraction TiC–SUS431 
composites by LPI process. The effect of TiC reinfo rcements on the high-temperature 
compressive strength of stainless steel composite w as evaluated by Gleeble test at 600 ~ 
1050 °C using SUS431, TiC–SUS431 composite. 
 
2. Materials and procedure 
TiC powders (Changsha Langfeng Metal Materials Co., Ltd, 2500 mesh) and SUS431 
(SeAH css corp.) which has low carbon content and s uperior oxidation resistance at high 
temperature used as raw materials. In order to fabr icate the TiC–SUS431 composites, TiC 
powders were uniaxially pressured in a steel die at 80 MPa. After compaction, TiC preforms 
were sintered at 1200 °C for 60 min under argon atm osphere. The porosity of the fabricated 
cylinder-shape TiC preform (diameter 100 mm and height 50 mm) calculated 
by measuring the 
weight and size of the TiC preform with considering the density of TiC (4.93 g/cm 3) was about 40%. 
TiC–SUS431 composites were fabricated by LPI proces s, which has been developed to 
infiltrate molten metal into a ceramic preform via hydrostatic pressure, which leads to a 
uniform dispersion of reinforcement inside the stee l matrix [11,18]. The SUS431 was melted 
under vacuum of 10 −2 Torr in a chamber. The TiC preform was separately heated to 1600°C in 
an MgO mold in the same chamber. The molten SUS431 alloy was infiltrated into the pre-
heated TiC preform, and was then pressurized with a n argon pressure of 10 bar. No further 
thermal treatment was performed in this study. The density of the SUS431 and the TiC–
SUS431 composite was measured by the Archimedes met hod. The crystal structures of the

TiC powder, SUS431 and the composite were evaluated using X-ray diffraction (XRD; 
D/Max-2500, Rigaku). High-temperature compressive s trength of TiC–SUS431 composite 
specimen ( Ф8×L12mm) was also measured by Gleeble 3800 system u nder strain rate of 
0.001 s −1 at the temperature range of 600 ~ 1050 °C. The mic rostructure of the composite is

3.2 Microstructure of TiC-SUS431 composites 
 
 
Fig. 2. a) SEM image and b) XRD pattern of as-infiltrated TiC–SUS431 composite. 
 
Fig. 2 shows microstructures of as-infiltrated TiC– SUS431 composite fabricated by the 
LPI process at 1600 °C under 5 bar of Ar atmosphere . Average volume fraction of TiC 
particles measured from 10 SEM images by Image-J pr ogram was about 58%. This volume 
ratio of TiC in the composite is similar to the vol ume fraction of TiC in the preform, which is 
the result of infiltration of the SUS431 melt into the pore region of the TiC preform without 
collapsing the preform structure. Polished microstr ucture of the composite shows uniformly 
dispersed TiC particles in the SUS431 matrix. It ca n be seen that the TiC–SUS431 composite 
has uniform distribution of TiC with little contigu ity and few defects between TiC particles. 
When the distribution of reinforcement is uniform w ith small amount of contiguity between 
reinforcements, cracking propagation is minimized a nd excellent mechanical properties can 
be obtained. 
Furthermore, TiC particles in the SUS431 matrix had somewhat curved circular shapes, 
even the TiC powder used in this experiment had a f aceted and irregular shape. It is reported 
that if the contents of carbon (C) and titanium (Ti ) are low in pure liquid iron, dissolution of

## Page 9

TiC occurs at below the melting point of TiC (TiC melting point: 3065 °C) [26]. Also, Booker 
predicted that TiC could be dissolved in pseudo-bin ary TiC0.9-Ti0.015Fe0.985 when the 
temperature of liquid steel was higher than 1475 °C [27]. From the above discussion, it can 
be assumed that the surface of the TiC particles re acts with the liquid SUS431 and dissolves 
in the matrix. Finally, the dissolved Ti and C atom s were re-precipitated in the TiC/SUS431 
interface during the solidification process. XRD pa ttern of the TiC–SUS431 composite (Fig. 
2b) shows the presence of ferrite, austenite, and T iC phases in the TiC–SUS431 composite 
without impurity peaks. The result indicated that T iC–SUS431 composite without impurities 
and intermetallic compounds was successfully fabric ated by LPI process. However, the 
present of austenite peaks such as γ(111), γ(200) and γ(220) suggests that partial dissolution 
of TiC particles may have resulted in an increase i n the carbon concentration of the TiC–
SUS431 composites, resulting increase of the residual austenite.

In order to precisely evaluate the high-temperature strengths of the TiC–SUS431 
composites fabricated by LPI process, compression t ests were carried out using Gleeble 
equipment at 600, 750, 900, and 1050 °C. The compre ssive stress-strain curves of the TiC–
SUS431 composite and of SUS431, tested at elevated temperatures with a strain rate of 0.001 
s
–1, are shown in Fig. 6a–6d. Figure 6e also shows the results obtained by plotting the 
compressive strength of SUS431 and TiC-SUS431 compo sites as a function of testing 
temperatures. The high-temperature compressive stre ngths of TiC–SUS431 composites 
fabricated by LPI process were highly improved comp ared to those of the SUS431 alloy, 
regardless of temperature. Stress-strain curves of the TiC–SUS431 composites tested at 600, 
750, and 900 °C, showed deceased curves up to about 0.4 strain and then slightly increased 
again. If the cracks are propagated at an angle of about 45° from the load direction, the height 
of the fractured specimen would be the same at appr oximately 40% strain, which increased 
the overall cross-sectional area of the specimen. T herefore, it is expected that compressive 
strength of the TiC–SUS431 composites tested at 600 , 750, and 900 °C, slightly increased at 
over 0.4 strain as shown in Fig. 6a~6c. However, Ti C–SUS431 composite tested at 1050 °C 
showed a gradually decreased stress-strain curve in dicating ductile fracture with plastic 
deformation (Fig. 6d). 
The strengthening ratio of the TiC–SUS431 composite s against SUS431 (compressive 
strength of the composite ( σc)/compressive strength of the matrix ( σm)) was calculated using 
the results obtained in Fig. 6a-6d. The strengtheni ng ratios at 600 °C, 750 °C, 900 °C, and 
1050 °C were 2.62, 3.96, 4.18, and 5.10, respective ly. These results indicate that the 
strengthening effect of TiC increases as the testin g temperature increases in the TiC–SUS431 
system. While the strength of SUS431 decreased with increasing test temperature, uniformly

## Page 16

dispersed TiC, having high melting point and hardness, sustained the structural strength of the 
composite at the tested temperatures with the aid o f the good interfacial properties of the 
TiC/SUS431 matrix. Fig. 6g provides low-magnification optical photographs showing overall 
morphologies of the TiC–SUS431 specimens after comp ressive tests at various temperatures. 
All specimens are shear-cracked, but are not comple tely separated after the test. Conical 
shapes with fractured fragments attached due to she ar stress, for which direct cracks 
propagated at an angle of 45° from the load directi on, and brittle fractures were observed in 
the TiC–SUS431 specimen tested at 600 °C. Meanwhile , one main crack with lots of small 
cracks formed at approximately 45° from the compres sive loading direction are clearly 
observed on the side surfaces of the TiC–SUS431 specimens tested at 750 °C and 900 °C. On 
the other hand, several big cracks without small cracks propagated at approximately 45° from 
the compressive loading direction; these cracks were entirely generated in the deformed TiC–
SUS431 specimen tested at 1050 °C. Ductile fracture with plastic deformation of the TiC–
SUS431 composite indicates that this material is hi ghly applicable for high-temperature 
structural materials.

3.4 Fractography of TiC-SUS431 composites 
 
Fig. 7 fractured microstructures of the TiC–SUS431 composites tested at (a) 600 °C, (b) 
750 °C, (c) 900 °C and (d) 1050 °C, respectively. 
Figure 7 shows OM and SEM images of fractured micro structures of the TiC–SUS431 
composites after compression tests at elevated temp eratures. SEM images of TiC–SUS431 
composites tested at 600 °C show a majority of TiC cleavage fractures with no interfacial 
debonding between TiC and the steel matrix, indicat ing the excellent bonding between TiC 
and the SUS431 matrix (Fig. 7a). These results indi cate that the initial failure occurs in TiC

## Page 18

when the load transferred from the matrix to TiC ex ceeds the critical strength of TiC. TiC–
SUS431 composites tested at 750 °C and 900 °C were fractured in mixed mode with ductile 
rupture of the SUS431 matrix and cleavage fracture of TiC reinforcements (Fig. 7b, 7c). 
When the compression test was carried out at 1050 °C, almost no TiC particles were observed 
on the fracture surface, and a matrix was mainly ob served in the SEM image (Fig. 7d), which 
indicated that matrix failure is major parameter re sulting in plastic deformation. This result 
reveals that the strength of the SUS431 matrix is l ower than the interfacial bonding strength 
of TiC–SUS431 at about 1000 °C. Although, work hardening, dynamic recovery and dynamic 
recrystallization can lead to changes in the micros tructure and mechanical properties of the 
SUS431 matrix during hot deformation, the stress-st rain curve of the SUS431 (Fig. 6) tested 
at a relatively slow strain rate of 0.001/s suggest ed that effect of these phenomena on the 
strengthening of high volume fraction TiC–SUS431 co mposite is negligible. Therefore, the 
strengthening of the SUS431 matrix by addition of T iC particles was mainly attributed to the 
load-bearing effect of the reinforcement.

3.5 Distinction of TiC-SUS431 composites 
 
Fig. 8. a) Compressive stress-strain curves and b) specific compressive strengths of TiC–

Fig. 8 shows compressive stress-strain curves for T iC–SUS431 and Rene95, measured at 
1050 °C. The TiC–SUS431 composite showed a high compressive strength about 54% higher 
than that of Rene95, which is one of the highest st rength Ni based superalloys. Furthermore, 
the density of TiC–SUS431 (5.81 g/cm 
3) is about 29.3% lower than that of Rene94 (8.22 
g/cm 3). Therefore, it was confirmed that the TiC-SUS431 composite developed in this study 
has about 30% lighter weight than the Ni superalloy , as well as superior high-temperature 
compressive strength. 
 
4. Conclusions 
High strength TiC–SUS431 composites were successfully fabricated by a novel infiltration 
process. TiC–SUS431 composites showed higher ductil ity and increased compressive 
strength at elevated temperatures. Especially, comp ressive strength of the TiC–SUS431 
composite was found to be much better than that of SUS431. In the temperature range of 
600 °C to 1050 °C, TiC–SUS431 composites showed imp rovements in high-temperature

## Page 20

compressive strength values of about 2.6–5.1 times compared to those value of the SUS431 
alloy. Especially, ductility of the composite was greatly increased at about 1050 °C. Therefore, 
the TiC–SUS431 composite fabricated by unique infil tration process is a promising high-
temperature material.
