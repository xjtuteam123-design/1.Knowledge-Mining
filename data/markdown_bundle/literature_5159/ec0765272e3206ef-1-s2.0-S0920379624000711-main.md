1. Introduction 
As we all know, International Thermonuclear Experimental Reactor 
(ITER) is considered as the ultimate solution for humanity to solve social 
energy and environmental problems. However, its harsh internal 
working environment (such as high temperature load) makes only the 
metal tungsten (W) as the preferred material for the first wall of nuclear 
fusion. The reason is that tungsten metal has excellent physical and 
chemical properties, including high thermal conductivity, high tem -
perature strength, high melting point, low thermal expansion, low 
sputtering yield, and low hydrogen isotope storage [1 – 3] . The excellent 
performance of tungsten metal also makes it the preferred material for 
plasma-facing armors, shielding components, and deflector 
manufacturing in nuclear fusion devices [4 – 6] . However, tungsten metal 
also has the defects of high yield strength, poor radiation stability, poor 
fracture toughness, low ductility, low temperature brittleness, and high 
ductile-brittle transition temperature. In this paper, the method of 
alloying tungsten with different solute atoms is intended to solve some 
of the current problems. Based on molecular dynamics simulation, it is 
found that the type and number of solute atoms have obvious influence 
on the properties of the materials [ 7 , 8 ]. Therefore, Jiang et al. [ 9 , 10 ] 
investigated the effects of titanium doping on the behavior of carbon and 
hydrogen in tungsten, and concluded that titanium doping in tungsten 
can resist the reduction of mechanical strength caused by carbon and 
hydrogen impurities. At the same time, according to the literature 
investigation, doping W with different solute atoms can improve its 
mechanical and thermodynamic properties [11 – 27] . For example, 
doping W with different solute atoms can improve its ductility, such as

As shown in Fig. 1 , the crystal structures of four W-Mn alloys are 
optimized, and these structural models remain cubic crystals after 
optimization. Meanwhile, it is found that Mn atoms tend to occupy the 
uniform and ordered bcc W lattice position, indicating that W and Mn 
elements can be synthesized to form atomic solid solutions. 
From Table 2 , it is found that the lattice constant of W-Mn alloys 
decreases with increasing Mn content. The reason is that tungsten has a 
large atomic radius, while Mn has a small atomic radius. In addition, the 
lattice constant of pure W is very close to its reference value [ 57 , 58 ], 
with only a small difference of 0.19 % and 0.34 %, respectively, which 
indicates that the present calculation results are very reliable.
