Features of Heterophase and Grain Structure, and 
Mechanical Properties of V–Cr–Zr–Ta Alloy

Abstract. A comparative study into the influence of thermomechanical treatment mode on the structural-phase state and 
physical and mechanical properties of low activation vanadium alloys (V–Cr–Zr–Ta system) has been conducted. It is 
found that a modified thermomechanical treatment of this alloy increases the short-term strength at 20 and 800° С. It is 
shown that an increase in the stabilizing temperature of V–Cr–Zr–Ta alloy up to 1100° С provides the same mechanical 
properties as the properties of V–Ti–Cr system alloys after similar treatment and stabilizing at 1000°С. 
INTRODUCTION 
Low activation vanadium alloys are considered as prospective structural materials for nuclear and fusion energy 
systems of new generation because of their unique physical and mechanical properties [1–5]. This kind of materials 
is known for their special features, among which the long-term high temperature strength with maintained low 
temperature ductility should be highlighted [2, 4]. Nowadays, vanadium based alloys (V–Ti–Cr system [1–8]) used 
for ingot melting and fabrication are investigated most extensively. Structural-phase state modification methods are 
developed to improve the operational properties. By optimizing the elemental composition with the aim of 
increasing the thermal stability and high temperature strength, new vanadium based alloys of different system (V–
W–Ti, V–Ti, V–W–Y, V–Y, V–Y–Ti–Mo, V–Y–W–C, V–Y–W–Mo–TiC, V–Cr–W–Zr, etc.) are intensively 
developed at present [9–18]. 
This work is a comparative study into the influence of the thermomechanical treatment mode on the structural-
phase state and level of physical and mechanical properties of low activation vanadium alloys (V–Cr–Zr–Ta system). 
EXPERIMENTAL MATERIALS AND PROCEDURES 
The study was conducted with use of a vanadium alloy V–6.80% Cr–6.10% Ta–0.79% Zr–0.031% C–0.052% O–
0.009% N (mass %) (further V–Cr–Zr–Ta). Samples of the alloy were thermomechanically processed by mode I and 
II. Mode I includes several cycles of rolling at room temperature to ε ≈ 50% with 1-hour intermediate annealing at 
1000°C [6]. Mode II is 1-hour homogenizing annealing at 1400 °C and several cycles of rolling (to ε ≈ 20–30%) at 
room temperature with 1-hour intermediate annealing at 600–700 °C [18]. Stabilizing 1-hour annealing was 
conducted at 1100°C after thermomechanical treatment by the both modes.

Surface relief investigation and electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) analysis were performed with a scanning 
electron-ionic microscope FEI Quanta 200 3D with the analytical system Pegasus operating at the accelerating 
voltage 30 kV in the hexagonal point staging mode. The Kikuchi patterns formed by backscatter electrons were 
automatically indexed by the program “TSL OIM data collection”. The collected data was processed with the use of 
the software “TSL OIM analysis”. Microstructural investigations were made using a transmission electron 
microscope Philips CM30 TWIN operating at the accelerating voltage 300 kV. Thin foils for TEM were prepared by 
jet electropolishing with “MICRON-3 M” using a solution of 20 vol
 % H2SO4 and 80 vol % CH3OH at 10 V. 
Specimens for tensile test were made in the form of dumbbells with the dimensions 13 × 2 × 0.7 mm. Mechanical 
tests were carried out with a Polany type tensile test machine by active stretching with the strain rate ε ≈ 2 × 10–3 s–1 
in a vacuum of 3 × 10–3 Pa at temperatures 20 and 800°С. 
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION 
Microstructural observations were performed with a scanning electron microscope (SEM) and transmission 
electron microscope (TEM). It was revealed that a qualitatively similar structural state is formed in V–Cr–Zr–Ta 
alloys under study after thermomechanical treatment mode I (TMT I) (Figs. 1a, 1c) and mode II (TMT II) (Figs. 1b, 
1d). The grain structure combines fine crystalline fractions (size 3–5 µm) and coarse grains (size 30 µm and more). 
The scalar dislocation density inside the grains for the both thermomechanical treatment modes does not exceed 
values of order ~10
10 cm–2. 
The heterophase structure of the studied V–Cr–Zr–Ta alloys after TMT I and TMT II is characterized by three 
main fractions of second-phase particles: coarse (1–2 µm), medium (50–400 nm), and nanosized (2–10 nm). These 
particles are mostly Zr-based oxycarbonitrides. 
As follows from the mechanical tests, the short-term strength values ( σ0.1) for the alloy after TMT II are larger 
than for the alloy after TMT I by 17.5% at test temperature ( Tt) 20°С, and by 13% at Tt = 800°С (Table 1). It should 
be noted that this alloy after the both treatment modes possesses high ductility (up to 30%) for the both test 
temperatures.

It is established that the thermomechanical treatment mode does not influence on the fracture pattern that has a 
cellular failure character (Fig. 2). The refinement of the relief structure elements is observed with increasing strain 
temperature from 20°C (Fig. 2a) to 800°C (Fig. 2b). 
To find out the causes of the discovered differences in the alloy strength properties depending on the 
thermomechanical treatment mode, we performed a detailed comparative investigation of the defect and heterophase 
structure using a transmission electron microscope. The investigation revealed that the structural state with distinct 
equilibrium boundaries is the characteristic feature of the alloy after the both thermomechanical treatment modes. 
Apart from it, after TMT II there are regions where grain boundaries have high defect density (Fig. 3a). A 
polygonized structure is formed in some grains, with polygon sizes equal to tens of a micron (Fig. 3b). In our 
opinion, these features result from an inhomogeneous fine-disperse particle distribution after this treatment. 
Thus, the efficiency of thermomechanical treatment of V–Cr–Ta–Zr alloy by mode II is demonstrated, first, by 
the achievement of higher strength properties and, second, by a decrease in energy consumption due to reducing 
plastic deformation degrees and intermediate annealing temperatures. 
In comparison with V–4Ti–4Cr system alloy [18], the short-term strength values of V–Cr–Ta–Zr alloy at 20 and 
800°C after similar thermomechanical treatment modes are comparable (315 and 180 МPа, respectively), while the 
stabilizing temperature of the structural-phase state for the new alloy is 100 °C greater and equals 1100°C. The latter 
shows a higher thermal stability of V–Cr–Ta–Zr alloy. A distinctive feature of the new alloy is high ductility both at 
20 and at 800°C, which indicates the manufacturability of this alloy in a wide temperature range. 
SUMMARY 
Comparative study results showed that thermomechanical treatment by a modified mode provides an increase in 
the short-term strength level of V–Cr–Zr–Ta system alloy by 17.5% at 20°С and by 13% at 800°С. A higher thermal 
stability of the new alloy structural-phase state allows almost the same strength values to be achieved after thermal 
stabilization at 1100 °С in comparison with V–Ti–Cr system alloy. It is shown that V–Cr–Ta–Zr system alloy has 
high ductility (up to 30%) both at 20 and 800 °C, which indicates the alloy manufacturability in a wide temperature 
range.
