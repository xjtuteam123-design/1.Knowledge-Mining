abstract
Microstructures and mechanical properties of Co – 9.5Al– 5W– 16Ni– 6Cr– xTa (at% where x¼ 0, 1.8, 2.8,
named as 0Ta, 1.8Ta and 2.8Ta) alloys are investigated. Microstructures of heat-treated samples are
composed of
γ and cubical γ0 precipitates. The γ0 solvus temperature increases obviously with Ta
additions. The yield strength as well as creep strength is improved with the addition of Ta. Ta additions
affect the high-temperature tensile deformation mechanism. The γ0 phase of the three alloys is rafted
along the tensile axis direction during creep tests at 900 1C. The creep strength of 2.8Ta alloy is
approaching the level of a ﬁrst-generation Ni-base single-crystal superalloy.

1. Introduction
The discovery of the relatively stable γ0-Co3 (Al,W) phase with
L12 structure in the ternary Co – Al– W system by Sato et al. [1] has
generated substantial interest. A series of experimental [1– 6] and
computational [7] efforts have been done to study the micro-
structure stability and mechanical properties of the new Co-base
alloys, suggesting that Co 3(Al,W) can be practically used as the
strengthening phase. However, large amount of W ( /C24 25 wt%) is
added to stabilize Co 3(Al,W) in the Co – Al– W-base alloys, leading
to a high density, and the γ0 solvus temperature is lower compared
to Ni-base superalloys. A recent investigation by Shinagawa et al.
[3] indicates that substitution of Ni for W can stabilize the γ0 phase
and increase the γ0 solvus temperature. This provides a way to
decrease the heavy density. In the ternary Co – Al– W system, with a
2 at% Ta addition, the yield strength as well as the solvus
temperature of γ0 phase is increased obviously [4], whereas a
combined addition of certain amount Cr and Ta can signi ﬁcantly
decrease the γ0 solvus temperature as well as high-temperature
strength [4]. A combined addition of Cr and Ni to ternary Co – Al– W
system can improve the γ0 solvus temperature [5]. These results
reveal that the interactions between alloying elements affect the
microstructure and mechanical properties. So far, the effect
of Ta additions on the microstructure and mechanical behaviors
of a multinary Co – Al– W– Ni– Cr alloy system has not been shown
systematically. In this research, Co – 9.5 at% Al – 5 at% W – 16 at% Ni –
6 at% Cr was used as the basic composition, in which a portion of
W was substituted by Ni, and 1.8 at% and 2.8 at% Ta was added to
explore its effect on its microstructure and mechanical properties.

3. Results and discussion
γ0 Solvus temperature and microstructures: The transformation
temperatures of the three alloys are given in Table 1 . For
comparison, the transformation temperatures of Co-base super-
alloys with γ/γ0 two-phase microstructure are also given in
Table 1 . It can be seen that with the Ta additions the solidus and
liquidus decrease slightly, while the γ0 solvus temperature
increases from 930 to 1 100 1C obviously. In comparison with
2.8Ta and Co – 8.8Al– 9.8W– 2Ta (at%), with large amount of W
substituted by Ni, the γ0 solvus temperature of 2.8Ta alloy is
higher and the density is lower. These results suggest that Ta is
effective to improve γ0 solvus temperature in the present alloy
system.
The SEM micrographs of γ0 precipitates of samples after heat
treatments and creep deformation at 900 1C are shown in Fig. 1 .
After heat treatments, the three alloys are only constituted of γ
and cubical γ0 precipitates. The γ0 volume fraction of the three
alloys is close to 60% and the average size of γ0 precipitates
increases from 95, to 241 and 301 nm for 0Ta, 1.8Ta and 2.8Ta.
Due to the obvious variation of γ0 solvus temperature with Ta
additions, the three alloys were implemented with different
annealing temperatures. Alloying elements which lower the melt-
ing point of an alloy enhance the diffusivity [8]. As shown in
Table 1 , the melting points decrease with Ta additions. Thus, a
higher diffusion rate caused by elevated annealing temperature
and the addition of Ta results in the increase of γ0 precipitates size.
Directional rafting of γ0 precipitates occurred parallel to the
applied tensile axis in all crept specimens, and the rafts of the
three alloys vary in thickness, associated with the initial precipi-
tate size. According to Nabarro ’s prediction [9], for σ/C15 δ40 the raft
structure is oriented parallel to the external stress axis while the
situation is reversed for σ/C15 δo0, where σ is the externally applied
stress and δ is the lattice mis ﬁt. The rafting microstructures
suggest that the three alloys possess a positive γ/γ0 lattice
mismatch at high temperature.
Mechanical properties: The yield strength and creep strength of
the three alloys are shown in Fig. 2 . For comparison, a conven-
tional Co-base superalloy (L-605) that contains no γ0-Co3 (Al,W)
phase [10],a ﬁrst-generation single-crystal Ni-base superalloy
(SRR 99) [1 1] and two γ0-strengthened Co-base alloys [2,4] are
also included. It can be seen that the tensile strength and creep
strength of these γ0-strengthened Co-base superalloys are superior
compared to those of L-605, probably due to the γ/γ0 coherent
interface strengthening effect. The 1.8Ta and 2.8Ta alloys also
exhibit a three-stage temperature dependence of yield strength.
Increasing Ta content, the yield strength and creep life are
improved obviously. The yield strength of 1.8Ta and 2.8Ta alloys
is higher than that of Co – 9.4Al– 10.7W (at%), and the creep
strength of 2.8Ta alloy is comparable with that of Co – 8.8Al–
9.8W– 2Ta (at%), approaching the level of SRR 99. These results
suggest that Ta is effective to improve high-temperature proper-
ties in a multinary Co – Al– W– Ni– Cr alloy system.
Deformation microstructures: The dislocation con ﬁgurations of
these three alloys after tensile fracture and creep fracture at 900 1C
are shown in Fig. 3 . In tensile tests, for 0Ta and 1.8Ta alloys, the
main deformation mechanism is bypassing of γ0 particles by
dislocations, while for 2.8Ta alloy a high density of stacking faults
is observed in γ0 precipitates, indicating the shearing mechanism.
The channel width between γ0 precipitates of 0Ta is relatively
small, and a small mean surface-to-surface spacing between γ0
precipitates can restrict dislocation motion signi ﬁcantly, which
could add “pressure” to dislocations at the γ/γ0 interface and
enable dislocations to overcome the energy barrier preventing

deformation mechanism of 0Ta is bypassing of γ0 particles by
dislocations. The average γ0 precipitates size and channel width of
1.8Ta and 2.8Ta alloys are approaching, while the deformation
mechanism is different between the two alloys at 900 1C. A similar
variation of high-temperature deformation behavior with Ta
additions in ternary Co – Al– W system at 900 1C has been reported
in Ref. [4]. It can be inferred that the addition of Ta affects high-
temperature deformation behaviors in the present alloy system as
well. The stacking fault energy of Co 3(Al,W) can be increased by Ta
additions [7], which leads to the increment of the resistance of the
precipitate to initial shearing by partial dislocations and the
improvement of high-temperature strength. This illustrates the
improvement of high-temperature strength with Ta additions.
The major defect of the three alloys after creep rupture at
900 1C is dislocations bypassing γ0 precipitates. The resistance for
the motion of dislocations in γ channels is made up of three
contributions [13], the Orowan stress, solid solution resistance and
misﬁt stresses. Ta atoms dissolved in γ matrix can increase the
solid solution resistance. The addition of Ta can increase the γ/γ0
lattice mismatch [6], which will result in a higher mis ﬁt stress. It is
difﬁcult to determine the variation of the Orowan stress with Ta
additions since the channel width and constants related to the
Orowan stress are altered as well. At 900 1C, the major deforma-
tion of 0Ta and 1.8Ta during tensile tests is dislocations bypassing
γ0 precipitates and the yield strength of 1.8Ta is higher than that of
0Ta. This indicates that the addition of Ta can improve the
resistance of dislocations ’motion in γ channels, which contributes
to the improvement of creep strength.
4. Conclusions
Ta is effective to improve theγ0 solvus temperature and mechanical
properties in the present alloy system. With signiﬁcantly decreasing W
amount and alloying with Ni and Ta, the alloys remain comparable
strength with reportedγ0-strengthened Co-base superalloys.
