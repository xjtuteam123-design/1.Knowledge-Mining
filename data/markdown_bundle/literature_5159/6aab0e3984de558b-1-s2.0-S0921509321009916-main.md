ABSTRACT 
Gamma-TiAl matrix composites can function as promising high-temperature structural materials. In this study, to 
improve the poor ductility and insufficient strength of composites at high temperatures, they were successfully 
prepared via powder metallurgy by adding the Ti – 6Al – 4V alloy. The experimental results indicate that the soft 
phases of Ti – 6Al – 4V were surrounded by the hard phase of Ti – 48Al – 2Cr – 2Nb, and the special structure exhibited 
a unique strain hardening mechanism, which resulted in the high strength and ductility of the composites at high 
temperature. The 8 wt% Ti – 6Al – 4V composites were fractured with an elongation approximately 90.65% and 
tensile strength of 427.75 MPa at 800

than those of single unreinforced Ti – 48Al – 2Cr – 2Nb alloy. Moreover, with an increase in the deformation strain, 
the deformation behavior started from the Ti – 6Al – 4V area, which had good ductility. The dislocation movement 
was hindered by the Ti – 48Al – 2Cr – 2Nb area, and dislocation accumulation and grain deformation occurred, 
leading to strength increase. The stress concentration and high-density dislocations were stored in the soft 
phases, which delayed the initiation and propagation of pores and cracks. Moreover, besides grain sliding, 
evident dislocation activities occurred; therefore, the high ductility of the composites was obtained. 
1. Introduction 
Gamma-TiAl matrix alloys are ideal candidates for the manufacture 
of high-temperature structural materials because of their attractive 
properties, including high specific strength, light weight, excellent creep 
resistance, corrosion resistance and oxidation resistance [ 1 – 3 ]. How -
ever, their poor ductility and insufficient strength at high temperatures 
have severely limited their applications [ 4 , 5 ]. In recent years, much 
effort has been channeled toward improving the strength and ductility of 
γ -TiAl alloys. The addition of alloying elements [ 6 – 8 ] and the intro -
duction of reinforcement particles [ 9 – 13 ] into TiAl alloys have been 
adopted to improve the γ -TiAl alloy mechanical properties. However, 
γ -TiAl alloy is an intermetallic compound; thus the improvement effect 
of alloying on the strength and ductility of the γ -TiAl matrix alloy iss not 
effective. Given the limitation of alloying, composite technology has 
become more attractive, because it cannot only maintain the excellent 
inherent properties of the matrix, but also improve other properties by 
the addition of reinforcement [ 11 – 13 ]. 
To obtain both high strength and high ductility of γ -TiAl matrix 
composites or improve one property without trading off another, this 
method has become a research focus. In existing studies [ 14 , 15 ], the 
strength and ductility of materials have also been enhanced by intro -
ducing soft phases. Xu et al. [ 15 ] prepared a novel austenite-martensite 
dual-phase steel. The martensitic phase constrained the deformation of 
soft austenite, forming a strong skeleton frame with the soft austenite 
involved in the frame. The unique microstructure provided a combina -
tion of high strength and ductility. The yield strength was controlled by 
the deformation of the hard phase, and the plasticity was improved 
through the frame-structure effect. During the tensile process, the 
transfer of the stress and strain from the hard phase to a soft phase 
induced the cooperative deformation of the soft phase together with the 
hard phase. Wu et al. [ 14 ] reported a heterogeneous lamella structure in 
a Ti alloy produced via asymmetric rolling and partial recrystallization; 
the structure was characterized with soft micrograined lamellar 
embedded in the hard ultrafine-grained matrix. The ductility of the Ti 
alloy with a heterogeneous lamellar structure was higher than the 
ductility of ultrafine-grained metal, and the strength of the alloy was 
higher than that of conventional coarse-grained metal. This study was

In this study, Ti – 48Al – 2Cr – 2Nb powders and Ti – 6Al – 4V powders 
were used as raw materials. Among the several types of γ -TiAl alloys, the 
Ti – 48Al – 2Cr – 2Nb alloy shows great potential in mechanical properties, 
including good ductility, toughness, and creep resistance [ 19 , 20 ]. The 
Ti – 6Al – 4V alloy is a typical α + β phase alloy. The basic phases of the 
Ti – 6Al – 4V alloy are hexagonal close-packed structure and 
body-centered cubic structure; therefore, the alloy has the comprehen -
sive properties of α -Ti alloys and β -Ti alloys [ 21 , 22 ]. Moreover, 
Ti – 48Al – 2Cr – 2Nb powders were sintered to γ -TiAl matrix composites by 
adding Ti – 6Al – 4V powders. The good ductility of the Ti – 6Al – 4V alloy 
contributed to the high strength of the γ -TiAl matrix alloy. Furthermore, 
the high-temperature tensile properties of the γ -TiAl matrix composites 
were investigated. The special structure of the composites could 
improve its high-temperature strength and ductility; this study provides 
a new material for γ -TiAl matrix composites and can serve as a reference. 
2. Experimental procedure 
The gas-atomized Ti – 48Al – 2Cr – 2Nb pre-alloy powders (purity 
> 99.9 wt%, referred to as Ti4822) and Ti – 6Al – 4V pre-alloy powders 
(purity > 99.9 wt%, referred to as TC4) were used as raw materials in the 
present work. The Ti4822 powders were sufficiently mixed with 
different contents of TC4 powders (0, 6, 8, and 10 wt%) via ball milling

The back-stress plays an important role in the high strength and 
ductility of heterogeneous metals [ 25 ]. For γ -TiAl matrix composites, 
the observed high strength can be attributed to its composite structure. 
During tensile test, the soft TC4 alloy will start plastic deformation first. 
However, softer phase are constrained by surrounding hard TiAl based 
alloy, and many dislocations will be piled up and blocked at phase in -
terfaces. In these area, the back stress [ 26 ] will make it difficult for 
dislocations to slip in the TC4 alloy area until the surrounding TiAl based 
alloy area start to yield. And when sample reach yielding state, the back 
stress can significantly increase the flow stress of the TC4 alloy area. 
Finally, the tensile strength of the composites is improved. 
For high ductility of the composites, in the elastic deformation stage, 
the TC4 alloy and TiAl alloy deform elastically, which is similar to the 
unreinforced TiAl alloy. In this stage, TC4 alloy will produce plastic 
strain first, and TiAl alloy remain elastic deformation, therefore, these 
alloys cannot plastically deform freely. Due to continuous strain defor -
mation, so the soft TC4 alloy need to deform together with hard TiAl 
alloy, leading to synergetic strengthening to increase the strength of the

3.3. Deformation mechanism 
Fig. S7 shows the tensile fracture morphologies of the Ti4822 alloy 
and 8 wt% TC4 composites. The dimples of Ti4822 alloy ( Fig. S7a ) was 
shallow and uniformly distributed. Moreover, the details of the Ti4822 
alloy dimple ( Fig. S7b ) were similar to those depicted in Fig. S7 (a), and 
the TC4 area showed bigger, deeper, and rounder dimples than did the 
Ti4822 alloy. During deformation, the stress concentration could not be 
completely absorbed by the soft phase; the initial tightly bonded 
microstructure was gradually pulled apart, and the cavity was gradually 
expanded; then internal instability occurred, and finally the sample 
fractured. 
However, there were some differences between the fracture mor -
phologies of the Ti4822 alloy and the composites. The tensile properties 
of the composites were related to the TC4 addition: (i) As is generally 
known, the ductility of intermetallics is poor. With the increase in the 
TC4 content, the ductility of composites gradually increased. The TC4 
alloy area with good ductility was composed of the α -Ti and β -Ti phases. 
Moreover, the α + β phase was identified as the softer phase, which 
significantly reduced the strength of the composites. Therefore, the TC4 
content was an important factor. The composites will have the strength 
plasticity tradeoff. (ii) The crack propagation can be suppressed and 
deflected by the softer TC4 alloy. The decreased local stress concentra -
tion can further improve the composite ductility. (iii) The increased 
strength was mainly due to the high densities of dislocations and sub -

the findings are as follows: (i) Almost all the cracks ended at the TC4/

effectively hinder crack propagation, and thus improve ductility. (ii) The 
transfer of the dynamic load from the hard phase to a soft one forced the 
cooperative deformation of the soft phase and the hard phase at the 
same time. Furthermore, the cooperative deformation can effectively 
restrain the stress concentration localization, hindering the crack initi -

softer TC4 could more easily deform than the hard Ti4822 alloys, which

concentrated stress and high dislocation densities could be partly

cavity initiation at the interface boundaries, fi nally leading to improved

4. Conclusion 
In this work, γ -TiAl matrix composites with different percentages of 
Ti – 6Al – 4V powders (0 – 10 wt%) were fabricated via vacuum hot- 
pressing. The microstructure and high-temperature tensile properties 
of the composites were investigated, and the main conclusions are as 
follows. 
1. The γ -TiAl matrix composites may be the best way to solve the high 
strength and poor ductility of the γ -TiAl alloys. The soft phases of 
Ti – 6Al – 4V were surrounded by the hard phase of Ti – 48Al – 2Cr – 2Nb, 
and the special structure exhibited a unique strain-hardening
