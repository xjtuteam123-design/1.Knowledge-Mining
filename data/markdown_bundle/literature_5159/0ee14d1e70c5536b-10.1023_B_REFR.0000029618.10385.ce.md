A STUDY INTO THE MECHANICAL STRENGTH
OF THE WELDED JOINT BETWEEN A HIGH-TEMPERATURE
PIEZOCERAMIC AND A STRUCTURAL ALLOY
Yu. G . Zadorozhnyi1
Translated from Novye Ogneupory, No. 12, pp. 43 – 46, December, 2003.
The effect of diffusion welding parameters on the mechanical strength of a welded joint is assessed. The role
of welding regimes in the strength improvement of a welded metal-piezoceramic structure is discussed.
Sensors based on piezoceramics (PC) have gained wide
acceptance in measurement technology. These are accelero-
meters, fluctuating pressure sensors, force cells, to name but
a few. They exhibit high operational stability and a wide
range of performance characteristics. There are piezoelectric
sensors that are capable of sustaining temperatures as high as
500°C. They use TV-2 and TV-3-type piezoceramics based
on bismuth titanate.
A specific feature of high-temperature PCs is that they
use a low-melting metal — bismuth whose concentration in
the composition may be as high as 70 wt.%. During the dif-
fusion welding of a PC and bismuth, the metal performs as a
dopant for the surface ceramic layer. This results in the oc -
currence of a liquid phase at the contact of the welded mate -
rials and may cause failure of the welded joint. Diffusion
welding of the PC-metal type can be carried out through a
chromium barrier layer (BL) 0.1 – 1.5 /c109m thick [1, 2].
Our goal in this work was to gain a deeper insight into
the PC-metal diffusion welding technology with a view to
specifying conditions under which a maximum strength of
the welded joint could be reached.
The fabrication technology of all-in-one metal-ceramic
structures involves a series of operations where each opera -
tion exerts an influence on the mechanical strength of
the welded structure. For the joint TV-2 + Cr + Cu +
KhN67MVTYu-grade steel, a number of parameters can be
specified that affect the strength. These are polarization and
stabilization, the thickness of a copper compensation spacer,
PC surface roughness, welding temperature, compression
pressure, time, and chromium layer thickness.
To analyze technological implications in the mechani-
cal strength of a welded structure , a single-factor design
was used. The process parameters were fixed such as to pro -
vide maximum strength of the welded structure. To follow
the change in strength, one process parameter was varied
within a specified range. The measurements were carried out
on a welded structure in size identical with the actual dimen-
sions of a pulsating pressure sensor. The weld area diameter
was 5 mm. The height of the piezoceramic component was
3 – 6 mm. The tensile strength of the welded structure was
measured at a constant loading rate as recommended in [3].
During welding, the piezoceramic component is exposed
to compression pressure and to high temperature, which may
cause a change in the component’s stoichiometric composi -
tion and lead to a depolarization. As was shown by measure-
ments, an intense mass loss (over 1%) in the TV-2-type PC
accompanied by a change in stoichiometry was observed at
temperatures above 1213 /c17710 K. The TV-2 PC sustained
heating at 1153 K under a vacuum of 1.33 /c18010
–2 Pa and a
static compression pressure of up to 20 MPa for a period of
600 sec. These conditions are a marked improvement over
the conventional regimes of ceramic-to-metal diffusion
welding using a copper spacer [3 – 5]. Polarization and stabi-
lization of the piezoceramic component according to a stan -
dard polarization cycle [6] improved but slightly the me -
chanical strength of welded structure. The increase in
strength did not exceed 15%. Further thermal stabilization to
a maximum operating temperature of the component (773 K)
caused a slight decrease in strength of the welded structure.
The overall increase in strength due to polarization and stabi-
lization did not exceed 10%.

Copper spacer thickness . The use of a spacer 0.1 mm
thick requires an increase in static compression pressure. The
use of a copper layer with a thickness of 0.5 – 1.0 mm leads
to a decrease in strength. The highest strength is achieved us-
ing spacers of thickness 0.2 – 0.35 mm. Degraded strength of
the welded structure with a copper spacer of thickness less
than 0.2 mm may arise from the copper spacer strain because
of the insufficient flatness of the welded parts. This results in
an inadequate quality of the welded joint over the weld sur -
face and in a loss of productive time. Increasing the spacer
thickness to /c1790.35 mm causes a decrease in strength, possi -
bly because of a technological misalignment in the welded
structure. Therefore the preferred thickness of the copper
spacer should be 0.2 – 0.35 mm.
Thickness of the chromium barrier layer . This is es -
sential for the mechanical stress of a welded structure. The
strength of the diffusion-welded TV-2 PC – copper joint is
rather low and the welded structure may easily fail over the
contact weld surface as the welding upsetting (welding jig) is
dismantled.
Tests were conducted to show that placing a chromium
barrier layer with a thickness of up to 0.09 /c109m between the
PC and copper improves the strength of the welded joint
(Fig. 2). The failure of the welded structure (may it happen)
occurs over the contact surface of welded materials. In -
creasing the barrier layer thickness over 0.09 /c109m causes a
change in the mode of failure. In this case, the failure of a
welded piezoceramic metal structure occurs over the
piezoceramic component. The TV-2-type PC has a porosity
of 8% and the average pore size 0.2 – 1 /c109m across.
The chromium barrier layer (BL) sputtered over the PC
weld surface develops defects that come in part from the
sputtering technology and in part from the PC porosity. For a
BL thickness comparable with the size of micropores, the ad-
verse effect due to bismuth tends to be inhibited. This may
explain the improvement in strength of the welded joint as
the BL thickness is increased.
During diffusion welding, the material of chromium BL
interacts with the welded materials, which results in a deple -
tion (decrease) of the BL thickness. The diffusive dissolution
of chromium in PC results in the occurrence of chromium
oxide layers which, owing to their inhibitory behavior, set a
limit to the diffusion of bismuth into copper [2]. The diffu -
sive dissolution of BL in the weld materials sets a lower limit
to the chromium BL thickness. For the TV-2 + copper contact,
this limit is 0.09 /c109m (see the characteristic curve in Fig. 2).

ness 0.09 – 0.5 /c109m showed no improvement in strength. In
the BL thickness range 0.9 – 1.5 /c109m, the load capable of
breaking the welded structure showed a tendency to de -
crease. The failure occurs in PC. For the chromium BL thick-
ness of 2 /c109m, the sputtered layer tended to flake, possibly be-
cause of the mechanical stresses. The decrease in breaking
force as the chromium BL thickness increased from 0.9 to
1.5 /c109m is possibly associated with the combined effect of

Welding temperature. The strength of the TV-2 + chro-
mium + copper + KhN67MVTYu welded structure tends to
increase with welding temperature in the range of
1000 – 1073 K (see Fig. 2). Below 1053 K, the failure of
welded structure occurs in the material contact surface and
above 1053 K, in the PC. Presumably, this behavior is related
to the increase in mechanical strength of the weld which at
1053 K is higher than that of the TV-2 PC. Increasing the
temperature to above 1123 K causes a decrease in breaking
load. Possibly, this may be due to the growth of the transition
zone in the PC + metal structure (whose strength is smaller
than that of PC) and to the development of a microcrack net-
work on the PC surface because of the escape of volatile PC
components. A maximum strength is attained in the tempera-
ture range of 1053 – 1093 K.
Compression pressure. The strength of the welded joint
was observed to increase with pressure in the range of
10 – 18 MPa with a tendency towards stabilization (see
Fig. 2). Up to the compression pressure of 10 MPa, the
strength did not exceed 15 MPa, and failure of the welded
joint occurred partially in the material contact surface. At
pressures above 10 MPa, the failure occurred in the TV-2
PC. Further increase to over 25 MPa caused a strain in the
ceramic component, and the welded structure had to be re-
jected as substandard. A maximum strength was attained in
the pressure range of 12.4 – 17.4 MPa.
Welding time. The rate curves show a dependence on
the location of chromium BL in the welded joint (Fig. 3).
With BL deposited on the copper spacer by the thermal vac -
uum vaporization technique, a diffusion bonding (diffusion
welding) takes place between PC and chromium. Increasing
welding time from 60 to 600 sec causes an increase in
strength (Fig. 3, curve 2 ). With a welding time of 600 sec, a
maximum strength is attained. Further increase to 3600 sec
leads to a decreased breaking load (the failure takes place in
PC). The behavior with BL deposited on copper is analogous
to that observed in the welded joint of alumina ceramics and
metal [3 – 5]. With BL deposited on the TV-2 PC weld sur -
face, a bond via diffusion welding sets in between copper
and chromium. The initial contact between chromium BL
and TV-2 PC sets in already during the vacuum sputtering.
The strength in the welded joint attains a maximum on weld-
ing time 60 – 80 sec. Increasing welding time is accompa -
nied by a decrease in strength with a tendency towards de -
creasing stabilization (see Fig. 3, curve 1 ).
An analysis of the rate of formation of the chromium +
copper welded joint has shown that after a lapse of
10 – 30 sec the strength attained was 250 – 400 MPa, which
is 5 – 10 times the TV-2 PC strength (30 – 40 MPa). With a
welding time exceeding 60 sec, the chromium + copper bond
produced no effect on the interaction between PC and corro-
sion-resistant alloy. Using a chromium BL 0.2 – 0.3/c109m thick
to stop diffusion, a welding time longer than 1800 sec was
needed for a mechanically strong joint to form (see Fig. 3,
curve 3 ). In this case, the joint was observed to fail in
chromium.
Curves 1 and 2 in Fig. 3 show that the strength of the
PC + chromium joint decreases with welding time. Processes
involved in this behavior may be the occurrence of a new
phase in the transition zone or the aging of PC on exposure to
vacuum. Here the strength was observed to decrease with a
tendency towards stabilization. This gives reason to believe
that the evolvement of a new phase (in the PC + chromium
contact zone) is driven by the diffusion of reactants, which
was confirmed by the results of an analysis of the transition
zone in a welded joint [2]. The new phase tends to decrease
with increase in isothermal holding time at a high tempera -
ture, which is a potential benefit for the high-temperature
service of the welded joint.
A comparison of the techniques that were used to weld
the bismuth titanate-based PC — that is, the vapor deposition
of chromium BL onto PC or a copper spacer — shows that
the former technique allows one to reduce the time needed to
attain a maximum of strength in the welded joint by a factor
of 6 to 10. The actual welding time in this process varies
from 60 to 300 sec.
Chromium deposited by vacuum vapor sputtering forms
a metal film on the surface of the material. This film is thus a
replica of the microrelief of the surface on which it was sput-
tered. The physical contact between chromium PC and the
material was produced immediately during the vacuum de -
position. This provides favorable conditions for incipient
contact interaction between the materials. During diffusion

CONCLUSIONS
Deposition of a chromium barrier layer on the weld sur-
face of a piezoceramic improves the mechanical strength of
the welded joint and allows one to reduce the welding time
owing to the favorable conditions for the incipient contact
between materials.
Maximum strength in the welded structure TV-2 + Cr +
Cu + KhN67MVTYu is attained at temperatures of 1053 –
1093 K, compression pressure of 12.4 – 17.4 MPa, welding
time 60 – 300 sec, chromium layer thickness of 0.09 – 0.9/c109m,
and copper spacer thickness of 0.25 – 0.3 mm.
