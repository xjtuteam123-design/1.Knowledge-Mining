Shape Memory Effect in Quaternary NiTiHfPd Shape
Memory Alloys beyond 200 /C176 C
Soheil Saedi , Guher P. Toker, Hatim Raji, Emre Acar, Fumiya Watanabe, and Haluk Karaca
Submitted: 6 October 2021 / Revised: 9 January 2022 / Accepted: 31 January 2022 / Published online: 8 March 2022
In this study, the effects of minor palladium (3 and 5 at.%) addition to ternary NiTiHf shape memory alloy
(SMA) were investigated to design a high-temperature NiTiHfPd SMA. While most of the reported Ni-
TiHfPd SMAs contain (Ni+Pd)-rich compositions, the (Ti+Hf)-rich compositions have not been studied to
the same extent. This study investigates two new NiTiHfPd compositions and their shape memory effect at
elevated temperatures. To this end, two Ni
47.3Ti29.7Hf20Pd3 and Ni 44.3Ti31.7Hf20Pd5 alloys were fabricated
and characterized in terms of microstructure, transformation temperatures, and shape memory behavior in
as-cast and aged conditions. The results demonstrated that even a minor addition of Pd could drastically
alter the operating temperatures and shape memory effect of NiTiHf SMAs. The metallographic studies
revealed the presence of a high volume fraction of secondary phases in the microstructure of Ni
44.3-
Ti31.7Hf20Pd5 that consequently created a matrix leaner in (Ti+Hf) compared to Ni 47.3Ti29.7Hf20Pd3.A sa
result, the Ni 44.3Ti31.7Hf20Pd5 was found to be more responsive to precipitation hardening. The evaluation
of the shape memory effect of the alloys displayed remarkable strength and strain recovery capability of the
quaternary NiTiHfPd at a temperature range above 200 /C176C with thermal hysteresis as small as 25 /C176C.

1. Introduction
High-temperature shape memory alloys (HTSMAs) have the
potential to be employed in many critical applications.
However, the current HTSMA technology is still not as mature
as conventional SMAs. The popular binary NiTi alloys
although have outstanding strain recovery ability but cannot
be employed at elevated temperatures. Even though transfor-
mation temperatures (TTs) of binary NiTi alloys can be
adjusted by changing composition and heat treatment, their
operation temperature remains below 100 /C176 C. In addition,
since the strength of SMAs decreases with temperature the
plastic deformation becomes inevitable at elevated tempera-
tures (Ref 1). Several ternary alloys have been designed over
the years to overcome these shortcomings. Alloying with
elements such as Au, Pd, Pt, Hf, and Zr has shown to be
effective techniques to increase the TTs and also improve the
strength of NiTi (Ref 2, 3). Hf and Zr as alloying elements have
particularly attracted more attention due to their lower cost
compared to the other aforementioned precious metal elements.
NiTiHf SMAs are generally categorized as either Ti-rich or
Ni-rich alloys. Ti-rich NiTiHf compositions have higher TTs
but display poor shape memory responses (Ref 4). The other
important disadvantage of Ti-rich NiTiHf SMAs for actuation
systems is their large thermal hysteresis even under moderate
loading conditions (Ref 4, 5). Large thermal hysteresis is a
major limitation as it simply means longer response time,
slower actuation, and higher power requirement. Moreover,
large thermal hysteresis and low strength result in a lack of
superelasticity in Ti-rich NiTiHf SMAs. Enriching NiTiHf with
Ni content in the composition signiﬁcantly reduces the thermal
hysteresis (Ref 6-8). Ni-rich NiTiHf alloys exhibit good
strength, superelasticity, and shape memory effect (Ref 8).
The slightly Ni-rich composition Ni
50.3Ti29.7Hf20 (at.%) ex-
truded and aged at 550 /C176 C-3 h has been found to exhibit a 3.8%
shape memory effect under 500 MPa at above 200 /C176 C (Ref 7).
However, thermomechanical processes for NiTiHf-based alloys
are extremely challenging due to their brittleness. Quaternary
element addition to NiTiHf SMAs such as Al, Nb, and Cu have
been investigated to obtain a better balance of ductility and
strength (Ref 9-11). Pd is a comparatively less studied addition
due to the high cost of the element. However, NiTiHfPd SMAs
have been found to have extremely high yield strength. The
shape memory response experiments of the alloys under
considerably high loading conditions have revealed exceptional
strain recovery capability for this less known class of SMAs. Pd
addition has been also shown to be effective to decrease the
thermal hysteresis in NiTiHf SMAs (Ref 12, 13). The major
part of available NiTiHfPd studies in the literature focused on
(Ni+Pd)-rich compositions and most of them contain 5 at.% Pd.
For instance, polycrystalline Ni
45.3Ti39.7Hf10Pd5 has been
reported to show a transformation strain of about 4% at 500
MPa (Ref 12, 14). The single crystalline alloy of the same
composition has been found to have even higher strength and
could exhibit 2.2% strain recovery under an ultra-high stress

response, thermal hysteresis, and microstructure of both alloys
were studied. Moreover, the effects of heat treatments on the
TTs of the two SMAs were investigated, and the shape memory
response of the heat-treated alloys was characterized.
2. Materials and Process
NiTiHfPd alloys with two nominal compositions of Ni 47.3
Ti29.7Hf20Pd3 and Ni 43.3Ti31.7Hf20Pd5 at.% were fabricated by
the arc melting method and remelted at least three times to
ensure the homogeneity of the chemical composition. The
alloys were named NTHP-3 and NTHP-5, respectively, for
brevity. Micrographs of samples were captured using a
Keyence VHZ250R model optical microscope. EDS elemental
analysis and mapping of samples were acquired using a
scanning electron microscope (SEM), JEOL JSM-7000F. For
the microstructure study, samples were mounted using epoxy
resin and hardener mixture, polished with several steps, and
etched with a chemical solution of H
2O (82.7%), HNO 3
(14.1%), and HF (3.2%) (by volume). A semi-automatic
FEMTO 1100 /NANO polisher was used for sample prepara-
tions. X-ray diffraction patterns of samples were collected using
a Bruker D8-Discover Diffraction Unit (XRD). X-ray source
was Cu K a, and measurements were made at room temperature
with a wavelength of 1.54056 A˚ . This XRD system has a two-
dimensional x-ray detector (Bruker V antec-500) and was oper-
ated in theta-theta scan mode obtaining 3 frames of two-
dimensional intensity distributions. Each set of frames was later
integrated into a 1-D spectrum with DIFFRAC.EV A software
from Bruker. Vickers hardness of samples was measured using
Nextgen NG-1000DGEN micro-Vickers hardness tester. The
as-cast samples were aged for 3 hours at selected temperatures
ranging from 350 to 600 /C176 C in a vacuum furnace and then
water quenched. A Perkin Elmer Diamond Differential Scan-
ning Calorimetry (DSC) with the heating/cooling rate of 10 /C176 C/
min in a nitrogen atmosphere was used to measure the TTs. All
DSC samples were thermally cycled three times to stabilize the
transformation temperature, and the last cycle is presented in
the results section. Compression samples (4 9 4 9 8m m
3)
were cut out of the ingot by using a KNUTH electro-discharge
machine (EDM). Mechanical tests were performed using a
100 kN MTS Landmark servo-hydraulic test platform. The
samples were thermally cycled between martensite ﬁnish ( M
f)
and austenite ﬁnish ( Af) temperatures under compressive stress
with the heating and cooling rates of 5 /C176 C/min. The stress level
was raised incrementally, and the thermal cycling was repeated
until samples showed signs of irrecoverable strain.

between Pd content and M s temperature in the NiTiPd system

the range of 0-10 at.% and then increases for higher values of
Pd in the composition. Hence, a generous Pd addition to
NiTiHfPd will increase the TTs. This is evident from recently
published high entropy compositions of NiTiHfPd (Ni
35-
Ti30Hf20Pd15 and Ni 25Ti25Hf25Pd25) in which M s temperature
has been reported as high as approximately 480 and 580 /C176 C,
respectively (Ref 20), although a very poor shape memory
effect has been observed.
Figure 6 additionally revealed that TTs can be adjusted
using heat treatments. However, the studied compositions
exhibit a dissimilar type of response. The effect of precipitation
hardening on TTs of NTHP-3 was negligibly minor, while the
TTs of NTHP-5 were changed noticeably in response to the
same heat treatment. The alteration of TTs in NTHP-5 in
response to aging follows a similar trend to that of the binary
NiTi where they are initially decreased, then increase, and
decrease one more time as the aging temperature rises (Ref 22).
The unresponsiveness of NTHP-3 to aging should be attributed
to the highly (Ti+Hf)-rich matrix of the alloy. It was previously
shown that small precipitates with short interparticle distances
are formed in (Ni+Pd)-rich compositions aged at temperatures
below 400 /C176 C (Ref 16) which is in good agreement with the
results of the current study. When formed, these precipitates
lower the TTs due to their pinning effect where the nucleation
of martensite is impeded (Ref 16). Increasing the aging
temperatures to 400 and 550 /C176 C, increases the TTs as larger
Ni-rich precipitates with longer interparticle distances form and
deplete Ni from the matrix. The small and closely packed
precipitates of the samples aged at 350-550 /C176 C temperature

4.2 Shape Memory Response
Figure 9(a) illustrates the recovered and irrecoverable strains
under different loading conditions for both as-cast and aged
alloys derived from Fig. 5 and 8. The NTHP-5 sample has a
smaller transformation strain in the as-cast condition. As it was
shown in Fig. 1 and 2, the microstructure of untreated NTHP-5
contained large secondary phases. In such a microstructure, the
volume fraction of the matrix that can undergo phase transfor-
mation is decreased. As a result, the NTHP-5 sample displayed a
smaller recoverable strain than NTHP-3 which had a uniform
microstructure. It is also clear that the recoverable strains of the
alloys increase as the thermal cycling load is increased from 200
to 400 MPa. This increase happens as the stress favors more
martensite variants along the loading direction. When no more
martensite variant can be oriented along the loading direction, the
recoverable strain remains constant in response to the increase of

display smaller recoverable strains in contrast with as-cast alloys
which is the expected response and can be explained by the
formation of a high volume fraction of precipitates and the
change in martensite morphology. Since the precipitates cannot
transform, their increase in volume results in less transformable

positive side, precipitates create obstacles for dislocation motion.
Therefore, thanks to their hindering effect on plastic deformation
alloys are notably stronger so that the irrecoverable strain does
not initiate until 1000 MPa. Figure 9(b) compares the thermal
hysteresis of the as-cast and aged samples under stress. Although
the thermal hysteresis in SMAs, in the absence of plastic
deformation, may increase or decrease with stress depending on
the compatibility of the transforming phases (Ref 4), in stronger
alloys better stability is observed. It is evident that heat treatment
has been very effective to prevent or minimize thermoplastic
deformation and stabilize the shape memory effect; however, a
smaller hysteresis is obtained. Although high hysteresis is
favored for damping applications, low hysteresis SMAs can act
as more efﬁcient actuators (Ref 23, 24).
The studies concerning the effect of Hf indicate that
substituting Ti with Hf in the NiTiHfPd system leads to smaller
transformation strain and thermal hysteresis. The current study,
however, manifests that increasing the Pd content in the range
of 3-5% in Ni-sublattice of Ni
50.3Ti29.7Hf20 similarly decreases
the transformation strain. However, such minor Pd addition still
increases the strength and stability of the shape memory effect
substantially. It is also seen a signiﬁcantly lower hysteresis is
possible to obtain with careful adjustment of stoichiometry and
post-processing heat treatments. The studied NiTiHfPd SMAs
operate at above 200 /C176 C under 1 GPa stress with only 25 /C176 C
thermal hysteresis. Such small hysteresis can translate to a very
fast actuation response in these alloys. Since the matrix in both
of the alloys found to be rich in Ti+Hf, it can be concluded that
although (Ti+Pd)-rich NiTiHfPd compositions do not exhibit
good superelastic response, they are promising options for
actuation applications that require fast response and good
dimensional stability at extreme environmental conditions in
terms of stress and temperature.
5. Conclusions
Two NiTiHfPd SMAs were fabricated and characterized in
as-cast and aged conditions, and their microstructure and TTs
were discussed. The shape memory response and thermal
hysteresis of the alloys were examined at elevated temperatures
following a systematic aging study. The following conclusions
were made:
1. Minor addition of Pd to Ni
50.3Ti29.7Hf20 at the expense
of Ni was found to increase the M s temperature substan-
tially.
2. The TTs of NTHP-5 altered signiﬁcantly with heat treat-
ments, while no signiﬁcant changes were captured for
NTHP-3 after identical heat treatments. The observed be-
havior was attributed to the different contents of the ma-
trix of the alloys in (Ti+Hf).
3. Heat treatments increased the strength of alloys signiﬁ-
cantly; however, a smaller recoverable strain was pro-
duced by heat-treated samples. The aged NTHP-5 could
fully recover transformation strain under 1 GPa stress le-
vel which was ﬁve times higher than the full recovery
stress level of the as-cast sample.
4. An exceptionally stable and low thermal hysteresis of
25 /C176 C while performing at beyond 200 /C176 C was observed
which makes these alloys very promising for high-tem-
perature actuation applications.
