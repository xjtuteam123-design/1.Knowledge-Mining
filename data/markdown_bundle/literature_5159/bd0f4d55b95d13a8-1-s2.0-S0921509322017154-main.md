cations. The strategy that incorporating in-situ carbides into light RCCA system could broaden and inspire the 
design of lightweight and high-strength materials. 
1. Introduction 
High entropy alloys (HEAs) with multiple principal elements have 
attracted many researchers ’ attention due to their outstanding 
comprehensive properties like high strength and ductility [ 1 , 2 ], excel -
lent oxidation resistance [ 3 ], and high radiation damage tolerance [ 4 , 5 ]. 
Among them, the refractory high entropy alloys (RHEAs) mainly 
composed of nine refractory metal elements were proposed by Senkov in 
2010 [ 6 ]. RHEAs usually exhibit ultrahigh hardness and strength at 
elevated temperatures, which perform better than the traditional 
Ni-based superalloys. For example, WNbTaMoV RHEA showed a high 
yield strength of 477 MPa at 1600 
◦
C [ 7 ], AlMo
0.5
NbTa
0.5
TiZr presented 
attractive yield strengths of 745 MPa at 1000 
◦
C [ 8 ]. Due to their 
promising high-temperature mechanical properties, RHEAs can be 
regarded as promising candidates in the more extreme environment at 
elevated temperatures. However, the low room temperature (RT) frac -
ture strain [ 9 ] and high density [ 7 ] of some RHEAs have limited their 
applications, especially in the aerospace field. 
In recent years, it was revealed that decreasing the number of prin -
ciple elements had the potential to decrease density and enhance the 
plasticity of RHEAs without sacrificing the mechanical properties 
[ 10 – 12 ]. The novel design strategy broadened the range of possible alloy 
compositions, which makes more refractory complex concentrated al -
loys (RCCAs) with favorable properties designed and fabricated [ 13 , 14 ]. 
Through tuning the interfacial structure and chemistry of TiMoNb 
RCCA, the alloy showed exceptional wear resistance comparable to 
alumina whatever at RT or 600 
◦
C [ 15 ]. Pang et al. utilized 
boron-doping to induce grain refinement and change the 
dislocation-dominated deformation mechanism, improving the 
strength-ductility trade-off synergy of TiZrNb RCCA [ 16 ]. In the pursuit

2.3. Ab-initio calculation 
The formation energy of (Nb, Ti)C carbide was calculated on the 
Cambridge Sequential Total Energy Package (CASTEP) [ 24 ] based on 
the density functional theory (DFT). General gradient approximation 
(GGA) with Perdew-Becke-Ernzerhof for solids (PBEsol) [ 25 ] functional 
was employed to evaluate the exchange-correlation energy. Geometry 
optimization was performed using the Broyden – -
Fletcher – Goldfarb – Shanno (BFGS) minimizer. And the convergence 
criteria were set as: total energy tolerance of 5 × 10
 6 
eV/atom, 
maximum force tolerance of 0.01 eV/Å, maximum stress tolerance of 
0.01 GPa, and maximum displacement tolerance of 5 × 10
 4 
Å. 
Plane-wave cutoff energy was set at 900 eV and Brillouin zone sampling 
was set at 16 × 16 × 16 Monkhorst-Pack mesh.
