Microstructure and tensile properties of Ti-62421S alloy plate with
different annealing treatments
Yong-Ling Wang, Xiao-Yun Song*,

Song-Xiao Hui
Received: 29 July 2013 / Revised: 26 November 2013 / Accepted: 11 July 2014

Abstract Ti-62421S (Ti–6Al–2Sn–4Zr–2Nb–1Mo–0.2Si)
is a novel short-time using high-temperature titanium alloy.
The effects of annealing on microstructure and tensile
properties of Ti-62421S alloy plate were studied through
optical microscopy (OM), electron probe microanalysis
(EPMA), transmission electron microscopy (TEM), and
tensile tests. The results show that, with annealing tem-
perature increasing, the volume fraction of primary a (ap)-
phase decreases while that of transformed b (bt)-structure
and secondary a (as)-phase increases. The room-tempera-
ture strength and plasticity are insensitive to annealing
temperature. However, with annealing temperature
increasing, the tensile strength decreases at 550 /C176C, while
increases at 600 and 650 /C176C instead. It is suggested that, at
550 /C176C, the strengthening mechanism is mainly boundary
strengthening and the biggest contributor is ap-phase by
providing ap/b-boundary area. Above 600 /C176C, the
strengthening mechanism is grain strengthening, where as-
phase strengthens the b-phase.
Keywords High temperature titanium alloy; Ti-62421S;
Heat treatment; Microstructure; Tensile properties
1 Introduction
With the advantages of high speciﬁc strength within a
broad temperature range and excellent corrosion resistance,
high-temperature titanium alloys are widely applied in
aerospace ﬁeld [ 1]. By far, the typical high temperature
titanium alloys applied to operation are Ti-6242S [ 2],
Ti-1100 [3], IMI829 [4], IMI834 [5], BT18y [6], and Ti-60
[7]. These alloys are all based on Ti–Al–Sn–Zr–Mo–Si
system.
However, by far, the maximum service temperature of
these alloys is limited to 600 /C176C. At higher temperatures,
their long-term high-temperature strength and creep dra-
matically decrease. Though titanium aluminides (Ti3Al and
TiAl) are attractive for their high service temperature, poor
workability hinders their practical use [ 8, 9]. Therefore,
short-time using high temperature titanium alloy is pro-
posed, in which more b-stabilizing elements can be
accepted. Recently, a new alloy Ti–6Al–2Sn–4Zr–2Nb–
1Mo–0.2Si (Ti-62421S) is designed, in which 2.0 wt% Nb
elements are added to strengthen the alloy and keep high
plasticity meanwhile [ 10]. Previous researches prove that
Ti-62421S alloy has excellent workability and welding
properties [11]. This alloy can be manufactured into bars,
plates, and sheets, and has huge application potential in the
aerospace ﬁeld up to 650 /C176C in future.
Microstructure has a strong inﬂuence on the mechanical
properties of titanium alloy. Generally, a bimodal structure
exhibits better ductility and high cycle fatigue properties
due to the equiaxial grain. Whereas a lamellar structure
with large transformed bt-structure possesses better creep
and fatigue properties. Additionally, the microstructure is
largely related to heat treatment [ 12]. Many researches
concerning the relationships among heat treatment,
microstructure and mechanical properties were carried out

2 Experimental
The 6 mm thick Ti-62421S alloy plate was provided by
Baoti Group Co. Ltd. b-transus temperature ( Tb) of the
alloy was measured as 1,010–1,015 /C176C. The as-received
alloy plate was heated at 960 /C176C for 30 min and then hot-
rolled from 6 to 3 mm in thickness. The alloy was annealed
at 800, 900, 940, 960, 980, and 1,000 /C176C for 2 h, followed
by air cooling (AC).
The tensile test specimens with gage length of 50 mm were
cut along the rolling direction (RD). Room-temperature ten-
sile tests were carried out on a SHIMADZU AG-250KNIS test
machine with a strain rate of 3 mm/C1min-1.H i g h - t e m p e r a t u r e
tensile tests were performed on an Instron 5582 test machine at
550, 600, and 650 /C176C with a strain rate of 4 mm /C1min-1.
Microstructures were characterized by Axiovert 200 MAT
optical microscope (OM) and JSM-7001F scanning electron
microscope (SEM). The phase composition was analyzed
through JXA-8100 electron probe microanalysis (EPMA).
A JEM2100 transmission electron microscopy (TEM) was
used for TEM studies. Thin foils for TEM observation were
prepared in a twin jet electron-polishing unit, using 5 % per-
chloric acid ? 35 % butanol ? 60 % methanol solution at
-30 /C176C and a current of approximate 30 mA.

When annealing temperature reaches to 940 /C176C, as
shown in Fig. 2c, the rolling streamline becomes com-
pletely eliminated and ap-phase tends to be equiaxed. The
microstructure consists of bimodal structure, i.e., ap-phase
and bt-structure, and the as-phase in bt becomes obvious.
With the annealing temperature increasing from 940 to
1,000 /C176C, the volume fraction of ap-phase decreases par-
abolically from 63.9 % to 22.6 % (Fig. 3), while the size of
as-phase in bt becomes thicker and thicker. As temperature
increases, more Al atoms diffuse into b-phase, which will
generate more as-phases [17], and higher temperature also
makes as-phase thicker.
It is noted that as-phase in Ti-62421S alloy, generated
from b-phase during AC, is more and thicker than that in
Ti6242S (Ti–6Al–2Sn–4Zr–2Mo–0.1Si) alloy. As we
know, kb is a value weighing the stability of b-phase. Both
Nb and Mo elements are b-stabilizers, but the b-stabiliza-
tion effect of Nb is weaker than that of Mo. Therefore, the
kb of Ti6242S alloy (just containing 2 wt% Mo) is up to
0.182, while the kb of Ti-62421S is only 0.141, though it
contains 3 wt% Mo and Nb. Lower kb of Ti-62421S
represents weak stabilization effect on b-phase, and thus
leads to the generation of more and thicker as-phases
during AC [ 18].
3.2 Analysis of micro-area composition
Table 1 shows the composition of ap-phase and bt-phase
after different heat treatments. It can be found that in the
same specimen, the content of Al in ap-phase is higher than
that in bt, while the contents of Sn, Zr, Nb, Mo and Si are
less than that of Al. Al element is a a-stabilizer, so it tends
to be rich in ap-phase. Moreover, as b-stabilizing elements,
Mo and Nb with bcc structure tend to solute in bcc- b-
phase. The atomic radius of neutral elements Sn and Zr are
bigger than that of Ti, and then the solution in hcp- a-phase
with a higher density will cause bigger distortion, so they
tend to solute in bcc- b-phase with a smaller density.
In addition, with annealing temperature increasing, the
content of Al in ap-phase increases, while other elements’
contents decrease. But those in bt are just opposite. That is,
in bt structure, Al content decreases but the contents of
other elements increase. The relationships of composition
in both phases and temperature were calculated using
CompuTherm Pandat software based on the law of ther-
modynamics, and the results are shown in Fig. 4. It is well
consistent with the results of EPMA. This also veriﬁes the
former changing rule of microstructure in Fig. 2; when
annealing temperature ascends, the richer Al and poorer
Nb, Mo and Si result in more and thicker a
s-phase.

However, at 600 and 650 /C176C, the tensile strength

temperatures, boundary diffusion contributes to deforma-

weakened. Simultaneously, with the increase of annealing

et al. [12] reported, while lamellar as-phase becomes more
and thicker, fewer slip systems can be initiated, and then it
greatly hampers the migration of the dislocation and
improves the high-temperature strength. It can be seen in
Fig. 6 that most of a

ﬁes the contribution of as-phase to high-temperature
deformation. To investigate the detailed tensile deforma-
tion behavior, the microstructures of the alloy after tension
at 650 /C176C were observed by TEM. Figure 7 shows the
dislocation morphologies in bt of the sample annealed at
980 /C176C, in which the dislocations are concentrated at the
as/b-boundary apparently. It is known that the b-phase in
near a-titanium alloys presents considerably lower ﬂow
stress than a-phase. So the deformation begins with b-
phase, and then transfers to as, causing dislocations cross
the as/b-boundary. Finally, the dislocations are hindered by
the as/b-boundary and concentrated around phase bound-
ary. Therefore, more boundaries result in larger resistance
for dislocations motion. In other words, the a
s-phases
effectively strengthen b-grains at higher temperature. This
indicates that at 600–650 /C176C, the grain strengthening plays
a leading role. It is concluded that the equicohesive tem-
perature of Ti-62421S alloy is between 550 and 600 /C176C.
Above 600 /C176C, as temperature climbs, the difference
between grain strength and grain boundary strength
increases, which means that the strengthening effect of
lamellar as at 650 /C176C is more pronounced than that at
600 /C176C. So with annealing temperature increasing, the
increasing speed of tensile strength at 650 /C176C is faster than
that at 600 /C176C.
As shown in Fig. 5d, with annealing temperature
increasing, high-temperature elongation of the alloy basi-
cally presents a downward trend. The plasticity of a ? b
two-phase titanium alloy is mainly provided by ap-phase,
and the plasticity decreases with the decrease of ap-phase.
However, under 650 /C176C tension, owing to the higher tem-
perature, the dislocations are easy to move. Then for the
samples annealed at 940 and 960 /C176C, ap-phases connect
with each other, so that the cavity directly grows along the
boundary until fracture, instead of through the matrix [ 16].
Finally, it leads to the poor plasticity of the samples
annealed at 940 and 960 /C176C.
4 Conclusion
In this paper, the effects of annealing treatment on
microstructure and tensile properties of Ti-62421S alloy
plate were studied. The original microstructure of the as-
rolled Ti-62421S alloy consists of ﬁne a
p-phase and
intergranular b-phase. With the increase of annealing
temperature, the volume fraction of ap-phase decreases
while that of bt and as-phases increases. The content of Al
dissolved in ap-phase increases with annealing tempera-
ture, while the contents of Nb, Mo, and Si reduce. But
those in bt are opposite. These are related to the generation
of as-phase.
At room temperature, the tensile properties of
Ti-62421S alloy are insensitive to microstructure. But as
annealing temperature increases, the tensile strength
decreases slightly at 550 /C176C, whereas increases at 600 and
650 /C176C instead. At 550 /C176C, the strengthening mechanism is
mainly boundary strengthening and the biggest contributor
is a
p-phase by providing ap/b-boundary area. Above
600 /C176C, the strengthening mechanism is grain strengthen-
ing, where as-phase strengthens the b-phase.
