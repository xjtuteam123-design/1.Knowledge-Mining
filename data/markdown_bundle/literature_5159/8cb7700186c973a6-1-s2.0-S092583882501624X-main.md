ABSTRACT
Additively manufactured Ti-5Al-5V-5Mo-3Cr-1Zr alloy (Ti55531) has attracted broad interest in aerospace, due 
to its high strength and ductility. However, its mechanical properties at high temperatures (400 
◦
C to 600 
◦
C) 
remain an open question. This study utilized a combination of laser additive manufacturing and forging, followed 
by heat treatment, to adjust the microstructure. The relationship between the microstructure and mechanics is 
investigated by analyzing fracture mechanisms. It shows that heat treatment affects the mechanical properties of 
Ti55531 alloy. The mechanical properties after heat treatment at 400 
◦
C and 500 
◦
C were significantly superior 
to those without heat treatment. Notably, the heat-treated samples had ultimate tensile strength (UTS) values of 
997 MPa, yield strength (YS) values of 878 MPa, and elongation(EL) values of 11 % at 400 
◦
C. This study opens 
up a new way to prepare Ti55531 alloy, broadens the application of additively manufactured β -titanium alloys at 
high temperatures, and has important practical significance for improving the performance of additively man -
ufactured titanium alloys in high-temperature environments.
1. Introduction
Titanium alloys are irreplaceable in aerospace and energy industries 
due to their exceptional specific strength, corrosion resistance, and high- 
temperature stability [1 – 3] . Among them, the near- β 
Ti-5Al-5V-5Mo-3Cr-1Zr (Ti55531) alloy has attracted significant atten -
tion as a high-performance structural material, owing to its superior 
strength-ductility balance and processability [4] . In many application 
scenarios, high-temperature mechanical properties are critical perfor -
mance indicators for Ti55531 alloy. For instance, in critical components 
such as the main landing gear and engine brackets of the Airbus A-380 
[5,6] , this alloy is required to maintain stable performance under high 
temperatures, high pressures, and complex stress. Additionally, the 
Ti-5Al-5V-5Mo-3Cr alloy (a closely related variant) is widely used in 
fasteners, jet engine components, and the manufacturing of the Boeing 
787 ’ s tail cone [7,8] , demonstrating significant advantages in landing 
gear structures due to its exceptional load-bearing capacity and thermal 
stability [6] . However, titanium alloys fabricated via conventional ad -
ditive manufacturing (e.g., laser powder bed fusion, LPBF) often suffer 
from inhomogeneous microstructures and residual stresses [9 – 11] , 
leading to inadequate mechanical properties under high-temperature 
conditions (400 – 600
◦
C). Although post-processing techniques like 
forging have improved room-temperature performance, the mechanisms 
by which heat treatment regulates microstructural evolution and cor -
relates with high-temperature mechanical behavior remain unclear, 
hindering their application in extreme environments.
In recent years, hybrid manufacturing technologies (such as LPBF 
combined with forging) have provided new ideas for the microstructural 
control of titanium alloys by integrating the flexibility of additive 
manufacturing with the densification effect of plastic deformation. For 
instance, Martina et al. demonstrated that interlayer rolling in arc ad -
ditive manufacturing refines original β grains into equiaxed structures 
and reduces α -lamellae width [12] . McAndrew et al. highlighted the role 
of rolling force and roller protrusion radius in enhancing grain refine -
ment for Ti-6Al-4V WAAM components [13] . Zhang et al. proposed 
microcasting-forging additive manufacturing, which synchronizes ad -
ditive manufacturing with interlayer rolling to achieve grain refinement 
and improved mechanical homogeneity [14] . Pan et al. further revealed

In this study, Ti55531 alloy was fabricated via a hybrid approach 
combining LPBF and die forging. Specifically, a cubic specimen with 
dimensions of 100 × 100 × 100 mm³ was printed using an EOS M290 
laser powder-bed fusion device under optimized parameters. The prin -
ted specimen was then transferred to Deyang Wanhang Forging Co., Ltd. 
(a subsidiary of China National Erzhong Group Co., Ltd.) for forging 
treatment, followed by machining into a cuboid with protrusions to 
facilitate subsequent extraction of tensile specimens. Solution-aging 
heat treatment was applied to optimize the microstructure, and the ef -

insights into optimizing high-temperature properties of hybrid- 
manufactured titanium alloys, offering practical guidance for their 
application in critical high-temperature components such as aerospace 
engine parts.

The raw powders are spherical Ti55531 powders prepared by 
electrode induction gas atomization (EIGAed) process, with particle 
sizes in the range of 53 – 120 μ m ( Fig. 1 (c)), supplied by Luster Advanced 
Materials Technology Co. Ltd. in Chengdu. The chemical composition of

3.2. XRD physical phase analysis
Fig. 4 shows the XRD spectra of the Ti55531 alloy prepared by 
combining laser powder bed fusion and forging. The results indicate 
that, for the Ti55531 alloy samples prepared by this composite process, 
the main phase before heat treatment is the β phase with a body- 
centered cubic structure (bcc), and there is also a small amount of the 
α phase with a close-packed hexagonal structure (hcp). After heat 
treatment, distinct peaks of the α phase are detected from the XRD 
spectra. This implies that complex phase transformations occur in the 
microstructure of the Ti55531 alloy after heat treatment. After heat 
treatment, distinct α -phase peaks are observed, confirming phase 
transformation from β to α , consistent with thermodynamic predictions 
[18,19] .
3.3. Microstructure observation
From the scanning electron microscope images of the AMF alloy 
before and after heat treatment shown in Fig. 5 (a) and (b), it can be 
observed that compared with the As - AMF alloy without heat treatment, 
in the HT - AMF alloy, the primary α phase and the grain - boundary α 
phase have significantly coarsened, and the grain - boundary α phase has 
even transformed from a continuous state to a discontinuous state.
Using the ImageJ software, the volume fraction of the α phase, as 
well as the average length and width of the primary α phase and the 
grain - boundary α phase in Fig. 5 (a) and (b), were quantitatively sta -
tistically analyzed, and the results are presented in Fig. 5 (c) and (d). 
From the data, it can be seen that the volume fraction of the α phase in 
the alloy after heat treatment has increased significantly, from 37 % to 
59 %. Compared with the As - AMF alloy, the volume fraction of the α 
phase in the HT - AMF alloy has increased by 40 %. At the same time, the 
average size of the primary α phase has increased from 0.99 μ m to 3.69 
μ m, and the average width has increased from 0.33 μ m to 0.74 μ m. The 
average width of the grain - boundary α phase has also increased from 
0.63 μ m to 1.95 μ m, and its continuity has changed significantly.
These changes are closely related to the thermodynamic principles, 
kinetic mechanisms, and crystallization behavior during the phase 
transformation process [18] . They can be explained by the following 
three interrelated aspects:
3.3.1. Thermodynamic driving force
Before heat treatment, the Ti55531 alloy is in a metastable equilib -
rium state. Heat treatment provides additional energy to the system, 
reducing the free energy through solute redistribution [20] . Solute 
atoms such as Al and V, due to their low interfacial energy, will pref -
erentially accumulate on the surface of the primary α phase, promoting 
the growth of the α phase. During heat treatment, the diffusion coeffi -
cient of solute atoms in the β matrix increases, and the α phase is driven 
to migrate towards the grain boundary through volume diffusion, 
reducing the α / β interfacial energy, which is consistent with the LSW 
coarsening theory [19,21,22] .
3.3.2. Kinetic regulation
The high-temperature environment of heat treatment significantly 
increases the atomic diffusion coefficient. The diffusion of α -stabilizing 
elements to the grain boundary is accelerated, promoting the coarsening 
of the α phase [21] . The aging treatment at 600

boundary α phase change from continuous to discontinuous, weakening

of the α phase along specific crystallographic directions such as the basal

alloy, fully verifying the kinetic control effect.
3.3.3. Crystallographic anisotropy
In the hexagonal close-packed structure of the α phase, it will pref -
erentially grow along specific directions such as the basal plane ( 0001 )
and prism plane ( 10 10 ) [19,23] . During the aging process, the 
competitive growth of α variants at the grain boundary leads to 
non-uniform coarsening. This anisotropy originates from the differences 
in atomic packing density and diffusion rates on specific crystallo -

phase preferentially grows along the basal plane and prism plane of the 
hcp lattice, and the Burgers orientation relationship (BOR) controls the

the diffusion of solute atoms, resulting in an uneven distribution of 
α -stabilizing elements, which accumulate at the primary β grain 
boundary. At the same time, sub-grain boundaries and defects are 
introduced, becoming preferential nucleation sites for the α phase. The 
discontinuous α -phase clusters at the grain boundary of the As - AMF 
alloy are related to local solute segregation and defect-assisted nucle -
ation. After heat treatment, atomic diffusion causes these characteristics 
to evolve, reflecting the metastability of the as-printed microstructure.
According to the classical LSW theory [19,21] , this coarsening 
behavior is related to time and the coarsening coefficient. When the 
temperature increases, the thermal motion of atoms intensifies, and the 
diffusion of α -stabilizing elements to the grain-boundary α phase is 
accelerated. The growth rate of the grain boundary exceeds the trans -
formation rate of the α phase to the β phase, promoting the continuous 
coarsening of the grain-boundary α phase, which has an important 
impact on the alloy properties. Moreover, as the heat-treatment tem -
perature rises, the internal energy state of the alloy changes, further 
promoting the evolution of the microstructure. This is a complex dy -
namic process that requires comprehensive consideration of various 
factors.

overall plastic deformation of the material. Regarding stress concen -

initiation and propagation of micro-cracks. Meanwhile, under

micro-cracks. Moreover, the fracture process of the material is relatively

expansion of micro-cracks. It is worth noting that the number of dimples

is relatively small, their effect on relieving the stress concentration at the 
crack tip is limited, and it is difficult to effectively impede the propa -

smoothly. However, as the number of dimples increases, many dimples 
can disperse the stress at the crack tip, reducing the degree of stress 
concentration and then playing a certain inhibitory role in the propa -
gation of the crack. This slows down the propagation speed of the crack 
and changes the overall fracture behavior pattern.

powder bed fusion and forging. The differences in high-temperature 
mechanical properties of the alloy before and after heat treatment 
were systematically studied, and the following conclusions can be 
drawn.
