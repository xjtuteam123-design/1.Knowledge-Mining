ABSTRACT 
To clarify the different effects of alloying elements on the strength of Ni and Ni
3
Al, this study employs First- 
principles methods to investigate the effects of refractory elements Re, Ru, Ta, Mo and W on the generalized 
stacking fault energy and ideal strength of Ni and Ni
3
Al, respectively. The results reveal that the alloying ele -
ments Re, Ru, Ta, Mo, and W can help to improve the creep strength of Ni but reduce that of Ni
3
Al. Among these 
alloying elements, Re and Ru are more helpful for improving the strength of Ni, and for Ni
3
Al, Ru has the weakest 
enhancing effect on the strength of Ni
3
Al. Moreover, except for Ru, other alloying elements have more significant 
enhancing effects on the strength of Ni
3
Al. The electronic structure analysis shows that the d orbitals of alloying 
elements except Ru can form deep and wide pseudogap near the Fermi energy level, which causes the obvious 
enhancement of the strength of Ni
3
Al. Furthermore, the regular areas of charge distribution between atom Ru 
and its nearest neighbor atoms Ni are more vulnerable to be destroyed, resulting in Ni
3
Al – Ru owning a relatively 
lower ideal tensile strength and reaches its ideal tensile strength under a smaller strain. 
1. Introduction 
The continuous performance improvement of Ni-based superalloys is 
closely related to the reasonable material composition ( Reed, 2008 ; 
Long et al., 2018 ). Numerous experiments have shown that Re can help 
to improve the strength of Ni-based superalloys, hence, the proportion of 
element Re increases significantly, compared with the first generation of 
Ni-based single-crystal superalloys ( Kamaraj and Radhakrishnan, 2017 ; 
Darolia, 2019 ). Usually, Ni-based superalloys contain more than ten 
alloying elements, and the dissolution of these elements will lead to 
lattice distortion, which will hinder the dislocation movement and thus 
improve the strength of the alloy ( Van Petegem et al., 2013 ; Kurz et al., 
2013 ). As reported in experiments, the alloying elements will enter the 
matrix Ni phase and the ordered Ni
3
Al precipitate. So, it is difficult to 
determine the different effects of alloying elements on the strength of Ni 
and Ni
3
Al. 
Moreover, the alloying effect on the strength of Ni-based superalloys 
is a combination of multiple physical factors ( Unocic et al., 2008 ). In 
experiment, it is hard to determine the effect of a single physical variable 
on the strength of alloys. Theoretical calculations based on first principle 
method can cover this shortage. Several investigations have been 
devoted to further demonstrate the influence of various alloying ele -
ments on the strength of Ni-based superalloys ( Chen et al., 2019 ; Yang 
et al., 2018 ; Wen and Wang, 2017 ; Huang and Wang, 2017 ). For 
instance, as the unstable stacking fault energy ( γ
us
) calculated by the 
generalized stacking fault energy (GSFE) model can be used to quantify 
the energy barrier of dislocation motion ( Rice, 1992 ), it can also be used 
as an indicator of materials strength. With the analysis of GSFE, there are 
some researches on the influence of refractory elements Re, Ta, W, Ru, 
and Ti on the strength of Ni
3
Al, and it is found that the Re significantly 
increases the strength of Ni
3
Al, compared with other alloying elements 
( Yu and Wang, 2012 ). However, the GSFE model is mainly used to 
describe the nucleation pathways of a dislocation, and the calculated 
strength cannot fully reflect the strength of the system. 
Depending on the object of study, other computational strength 
models, such as the ideal strength model, are also used to quantify the 
strength of a material. The ideal strength model is used to calculate the 
ideal strength of a material without defects, and the model includes not 
only the elastic deformation, but also the plastic deformation. Based on 
the ideal strength model, the ideal tensile strength of Ni
3

2.2. Calculation parameter setting 
The Vienna Ab-initio Simulation Package (VASP) ( Kresse and 
Furthmüller, 1996 ; Kresse and Hafner, 1993 ) based on the density 
functional theory (DFT) was used for all the calculations. The 
ion-electron interaction was described by using the projector augmented 
wave (PAW) method ( Kresse and Joubert, 1999 ). The 
Perdew-Burke-Emzerhof (PBE) treated within the generalized gradient 
approximation (GGA) parameterized was adopted as the 
exchange-correlation functional ( Perdew and Wang, 1992 ; Ernzerhof 
and Scuseria, 1999 ). Throughout the calculation, in order to ensure 
convergent results, a sensitivity analysis for the setting parameters of 
VASP was performed. The wave functions were expanded using a cutoff 
energy of 350 eV for both the calculations of Ni-X systems and Ni
3
Al-X 
systems (X: Ru, Re, Ta, Mo, W). The effect of spin polarizations was also 
considered. By using a special k-point sampling of Monkhorst-Pack 
scheme ( Monkhorst and Pack, 1976 ), Brillouin zone integrations were 
approximated with Γ -centered 9 × 9 × 2 and 9 × 9 × 9 grids for the 
GSFE calculations and the ideal strength calculations, respectively. All 
the atoms were relaxed until the force on each atom was less than 10
 2 
eV/Å, and the convergence threshold of total energy was 10
 4 
eV. In 
addition, for the GSFE calculations, all the atoms were relaxed only in 
the direction perpendicular to the slip plane.

effects on the strength of Ni
3
Al. The enhancing effect of Ru on the ideal 
tensile strength of Ni
3
Al is even slightly lower than that on the ideal 
tensile strength of Ni, which implies that unlike other alloying elements, 
Ru may form some relatively unique bonding structures in Ni
3

3.3. Electronic structure information 
To further explain the reasons of the alloying effect on the strength of 
Ni and Ni
3
Al, Fig. 6 and Fig. 7 show the density of states (DOS) curves of 
the alloying atom and its nearest neighbor (1NN) atom in Ni and Ni
3
Al, 
respectively. For Ni-X (X: Ru, Re, Mo, W, Ta) systems, from Fig. 6 , there 
are several sharp peaks in the valence and conduction bands which 
originate from the contribution of the X- d and Ni- d orbitals. This strong 
hybridization between the X- d and Ni- d orbitals leads to an increase in 
the strength of Ni. In addition, all the hybridization peaks of alloying 
atoms Ru, Re, Mo, W and their 1NN atoms fall at the Fermi energy level, 
as shown in Fig. 6 (a) – (d). It means that compared with Ta atom, Ru, Re, 
Mo, and W can form much stronger bonds with their 1NN atoms Ni, 
which is beneficial to enhance the strength of Ni. This is consistent with 
what revealed by the GSFE and the ideal strength calculations, as shown 
in Figs. 2 (a), 5(a) and 5(c) . 
As for Ni
3
Al, when the alloying atoms X (X: Ru, Re, Mo, W, Ta) are 
added, these X atoms and their 1NN atoms Ni will lead to strong orbital 
hybridization, and the hybridization peaks also mainly originate from 
the contribution of X- d orbitals and Ni- d orbitals, as shown in Fig. 7 (a) – 
(e). It implies that the studied alloying atoms can also improve the 
mechanical strength of Ni
3
Al. By comparing Fig. 6 (a) – (e) and Fig. 7 (a)- 
(e), it is easy to find that the d orbital of all the alloying atoms Re, Ta, 
Mo, and W appears a deep valley so-called as pseudogap near the Fermi 
energy level, and its size is related to the strength of atomic bond ( Wu 
and Wang, 2015 ). This indicates that the alloying atoms Re, Ta, Mo, and 
W can form stronger bonds with their 1NN atoms in Ni
3
Al, which means 
these alloying elements obviously enhance the mechanical strength of 
Ni
3
Al. It well explains the stronger strengthening effect of these alloying 
elements on Ni
3
Al than on Ni. However, there is no pseudogap at Fermi 
level can be found for the Ru- d orbital. Therefore, the strengthening 
effect of Ru on Ni
3
Al is weaker than that of other alloying elements, as 
shown in Fig. 2 (b) – (d), Fig. 5 (b) and 5(d) . 
Interestingly, among the alloying elements, only Ru shows signifi -
cant changes in the strengthening order for Ni and Ni
3
Al. In order to 
further explore the unique role of Ru on the strength enhancement of 
Ni
3
Al, we took Ni
3
Al – Re as a comparison and plotted the charge density 
differences (CDD) of Ni
3
Al – Ru and Ni
3
Al – Re on the (010) plane in Fig. 8 
(a)-(d). It is clear from Fig. 8 (a) and (b) that the areas between Ni and 
alloying atoms are filled with a large amount of charges, which means 
both Re and Ru can form much stronger bonds with Ni than Ni – Al bonds 
in Ni
3
Al-X. Moreover, the charge distribution shows more obvious 
directional distribution between Re and Ni than that between Ru and Ni. 
It indicates that there forms a stronger covalent-like bond between 
atoms Re and Ni. When the system is loaded to a tensile strain ε = 0.08 
along [110] direction, there is a marked transfer of charge between Ru 
and Ni as shown in Fig. 8 (c). Partial of the charges are transferred to the 
areas between the second nearest neighbor (2NN) atoms Al and the 1NN 
atoms Ni, and the regular areas of charge distribution have been 
devastated. The corresponding stress at this point is the ideal tensile 
strength along [110] direction. 
While for Ni
3
Al – Re, under tensile strain ε = 0.08, the regular areas of 
charge distribution are not destroyed, as shown in Fig. 8 (b) and (d). 
Obviously, Ni
3
Al – Re can withstand a larger deformation than Ni
3
Al – Ru. 
In addition, under such a tensile strain, the charge distribution sur -
rounding atom Ru tends to be circular, that is because the covalent-like 
bond between Ru and Ni diminishes, thus showing more characteristics 
of metallic bond. However, the directivity of charge distribution sur -
rounding atom Re remains obvious, as shown in Fig. 8 (d). It indicates 
that the bond between Re and Ni still presents covalent-like bond, 
resulting in a higher tensile stress under the same strain for Ni
3
Al – Re.

4. Conclusions 
The generalized stacking fault energies (GSFE) and the ideal 
strengths of Ni-X and Ni
3
Al-X, where alloying element X = Re, Ru, Ta, 
Mo, and W, are investigated based on the First-principles calculations. 
The main results are summarized as follows: 
(1) The alloying elements Re, Ru, Ta, Mo, and W can help to improve 
the creep strength of Ni but reduce the creep strength of Ni
3
Al. 
Compared with other elements, Re and Ru are more helpful for 
improving the mechanical strength of Ni, and for Ni
3
Al, Ru has 
the weakest enhancing effect on the mechanical strength. 
(2) Except Ru, the elements Re, W, Mo, and Ta can enhance the 
mechanical strength of Ni
3
Al more significantly than that of Ni. 
This enhancement originates from the d orbitals of these ele -
ments, which can form deep and wide pseudogap near the Fermi 
energy level. 
(3) For Ni
3
Al, the alloying element Ru cannot form strong covalent- 
like bonds like other alloying elements. When loaded a series of 
tensile strains, the charges between Ru and the nearest neighbor 
(1NN) atoms Ni are easier to move to the interstices between the 
second nearest neighbor (2NN) atoms Al and 1NN atoms Ni. It 
makes the regular areas of charge distribution more vulnerable to 
be destroyed for Ni
3
Al – Ru, which results in a relatively lower 
ideal tensile strength and ductility.
