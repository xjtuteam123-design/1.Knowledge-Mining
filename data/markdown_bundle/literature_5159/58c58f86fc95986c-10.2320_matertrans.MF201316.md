Glass-Forming Ability, Corrosion Resistance and Mechanical Properties
of Zr 60¹xAl15Ni25TMx (TM = Nb and Ta) Bulk Metallic Glasses
Y . H. Li1, W. Zhang 1,2,+, C. Dong 1, S. Yamaura 2 and A. Makino 2
1School of Materials Science and Engineering, Dalian University of Technology, Dalian, 116024, P . R. China
2Institute for Materials Research, Tohoku University, Sendai 980-8577, Japan
The thermal stability, glass-forming ability (GFA), corrosion resistance and mechanical properties of Zr 60¹xAl15Ni25TMx (TM = Nb and
Ta, x = 0­6) bulk metallic glasses (BMGs) were investigated. The added Nb and Ta greatly increase the glass transition temperature of the base
BMG whereas reduce the supercooled liquid region. Minor addition of Nb enhances the GFA of the base alloy, and Zr 56Al15Ni25Nb4 BMG
exhibits the largest critical diameter of 20 mm. Additions of Nb and Ta ef ﬁciently improve the corrosion resistance of the BMGs in chloride-ion-
containing solutions due to the formation of highly protective surface ﬁlm. In addition, the compressive yield strength of the BMGs increases
gradually with increasing Nb and Ta contents. [doi:10.2320/matertrans.MF201316]
(Received February 15, 2013; Accepted June 5, 2013; Published July 12, 2013)
Keywords: metallic glasses, zirconium-based alloy, glass-forming ability, corrossion resistance, mechanical properties, alloying
1. Introduction
Bulk metallic glasses (BMGs) have been considered as the
promising structural and /or functional materials because of
their many unique physical and chemical properties. 1) For
instance, the BMGs are suitable to produce the separators of
the proton exchange membrane fuel cells (PEMFC) owing to
their high corrosion resistance, high strength, good toughness
and viscous ﬂow workability in the supercooled liquid
region, etc.
2) The Ni-based metallic glasses have been used to
make the separators for PEMFC, and the single cell with the
glassy separator exhibited good I-V performance.
2,3) The Zr-
based alloys are also promising the separators materials due
to their high glass-forming ability (GFA), large supercooled
liquid region, high strength with good toughness.
1) The Zr-
based BMGs with high GFA have been developed in the Zr ­
Ti­Cu­Ni­Be,4) Zr­Al­Cu­Ni5) and Zr ­Cu­Al­Ag6) multi-
component systems. It is noticed that these alloys contain
high Cu content, leading to decreases of the corrosion
resistance in aggressive media, especially in the chloride-ion-
containing solutions,
7,8) which limit their practical application
in the PEMFC 9) and other Chloride-ion-related environment.
In addition, the Cu-containing BMGs exhibit low plasticity
in spite of their high fracture strength. Consequently, it is
signiﬁcant to develop new Cu-free Zr-based BMGs possess-
ing high GFA, together with good corrosion resistance and
mechanical properties.
It has been reported that the addition of appropriate
amounts of Nb is effective for the increase in the GFA and
corrosion resistance of Zr-based Zr ­Al­(Cu, Ni) BMGs.
10­12)
The addition of Nb or Ta to Cu ­Hf­Ti BMGs also greatly
enhanced the corrosion resistance in the chloride-ion-
containing solutions due to the formation of the highly
protective Nb or Ta-enriched surface ﬁlm.
13,14) Recently,
we have found that Zr-based BMGs with dc more than one
centimeter can be synthesized in ternary Zr ­Ni­Al alloy
system, which exhibit high strength accompany with good
plasticity.
15) In this work, with the aim of developing new
Cu-free Zr-based BMGs possessing higher GFA, together
with good corrosion resistance and mechanical properties,
we investigated the effects of Nb and Ta additions on the
thermal stability, GFA, corrosion resistance and mechanical
properties of a Zr
60Al15Ni25 BMG.
2. Experimental
The master ingots with nominal compositions of
Zr60¹xAl15Ni25TMx (TM = Nb and Ta, x = 0­6, in atomic
percent) were prepared by arc melting of pure metals of Zr,
Al, Ni, Nb and Ta with a purity above 99.9 mass % in a
puriﬁed argon atmosphere. The ribbon samples with a cross
section around of 0.02 mm © 1.2 mm were fabricated by a
single-roller melt spinning facility, and the rod samples with
dimensions of 2 ­22 mm in diameter and 40 mm in length
were prepared by copper mold casting. The structure of the
samples was examined by X-ray diffraction (XRD) (Cu K ¡).
The thermal properties of the as-cast alloys were examined
by differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) at a heating rate
of 0.67 K /s. The liquidus temperature and melting behavior
were measured with a differential thermal analyzer (DTA)
at a heating rate of 0.33 K /s. The corrosion behavior of
the alloys was evaluated by electrochemical measurements,
which were conducted in a three-electrode cell using a
platinum counter electrode and an Ag /AgCl reference
electrode. Electrolytes of 1 N H
2SO4, 3 mass % NaCl and
1 N HCl solutions open to air were used. Potentiodynamic
polarization curves were measured at room temperature under
a potential sweep rate of 50 mV min
¹1 after open-circuit
immersion for about 20 min when the open-circuit potential
became almost steady. XPS measurements for surface
analysis of the specimens before and after immersion in
3 mass% NaCl solution were performed by photoelectron
spectrometer with monochromatized Al K ¡ excitation. The
compositions of the surface ﬁlm and the underlying alloy
surface were quantitatively determined with a previously
proposed method using the integrated intensities of photo-
electrons under the assumption of a three-layer model of
an outermost contaminant hydrocarbon layer of uniform

We evaluated the effects of minor Nb and Ta additions on
the corrosion resistance by electrochemical method. Figure 3
presents the polarization curves of the Zr
60¹xAl15Ni25TMx
(TM = Nb and Ta, x = 0­6) BMGs in 1 N H 2SO4 and
3 mass% NaCl solutions at 298 K open to air. In 1 N H 2SO4
solution, all BMGs exhibit spontaneous passivation with
passive current densities in the order of 10 ¹2 Am¹2, and no
distinct pitting corrosion can be found when the anodic
potential is up to 1500 mV (see Fig. 3(a)). The large passive
regions indicate that the present BMGs have good corrosion
resistance in 1 N H
2SO4 solution. The alloyed BMGs possess
obviously lower passive current densities than that of the
base alloy, suggesting the improved corrosion resistance.
In 3 mass % NaCl solution, the BMGs are spontaneously
passivated with current densities around 10 ¹3­10¹4 Am¹2.
However, the pitting corrosion is initiated with the increasing
of anodic potential (see Fig. 3(b)). The pitting potentials
(E
pit) and widths of passive region determined by Epit­Ecorr
(Ecorr: corrosion potentials) are listed in Table 2. It is seen that
the minor alloyed BMGs possess nobler Epit and larger
passive region than those of the base alloy. The BMG with
Nb = 6 possesses the highest Epit of 81 mV , and alloy with
Nb = 2 exhibits the largest Epit­Ecorr of 265 mV . In addition,
the added Nb and Ta lower both the anodic and cathodic
current densities of the base BMG (see Fig. 3(b)). In 1 N HCl
solution, the alloys dissolve rapidly by slight anodic polar-
ization and no self-passivation can be found from the
polarization curves. The E
pit became positive-shifting with
Nb and Ta additions, similar to that in the NaCl solution. The
increased Epit together with enlarged passive region indicate
that the BMGs with minor Nb and Ta alloyed show better
corrosion resistance in chloride-ion-containing solutions.

system, the atomic radii for Zr, Nb, Al and Ni are in the
sequence of 0.160, 0.147, 0.143 and 0.125 nm. It is then
anticipated that the mismatch of the atomic sizes in Zr ­Al­
Ni­Nb alloy system can produce an ef ﬁciently packed local
structure, which facilitates the formation of amorphous
phase.
20,21) In addition, the Nb addition into the Zr ­Al­Ni
alloy cause the generation of strong chemical short-range
ordering due to large negative heats of mixing between Al ­
Nb (¹18 kJ/mol), Ni­Nb (¹30 kJ/mol), Zr­Al (¹44 kJ/mol)
and Zr ­Ni ( ¹49 kJ/mol) atom pairs,
23) which can improve
the local packing ef ﬁciency and restrain the long-range
diffusion of atoms, and hence improve the GFA. 22) It should
be noted that Ta addition shows a negative effect on GFA,
although Ta element has a similar atomic radius to Nb and
large negative heats of mixing with Zr, Al and Ni elements.
With a high melting temperature of 3290 K, Ta element
is dif ﬁcult to dissolve completely in the alloys.
24) The
remaining Ta particles may induce the nucleation of the
competing crystalline phases during the casting and then
inhibit the amorphous phase formation. The Zr
2Ni and Ta
phases are precipitated accompany with amorphous for the
Zr56Al15Ni15Ta4 alloy, which supports the above assump-
tions. The similar results have been found in other Zr-based
alloy system.
24)
The minor additions of Nb and Ta elements improve the
corrosion resistance of the Zr ­Ni­Al BMGs, especially in the
chloride-containing solutions. To clarify the effect of alloying
elements on the corrosion resistance, XPS analysis was
performed for the Zr
56Ni25Al15Nb4 BMG specimens as-
polished mechanically in air, and immersed for 1 week in
3 mass% NaCl solution after polishing, respectively. The
XPS spectra of the specimens over a wide binding energy
region exhibit the peaks of Zr 3d, Al 2s, Ni 2p, Nb 3d, O 1s,
etc. The spectrum peaks from alloy constituents are
composed of peaks corresponding to their oxidized states in
a surface ﬁlm and the metallic states in an underlying alloy
surface.
25) The constituents in oxidized states related to the
surface ﬁlm are determined as Zr 4+,A l 3+,N i 2+ and Nb 5+,
respectively. The compositions of the surface ﬁlm were
calculated quantitatively by integrating the intensities of the
peaks as assigned to the oxidized states.
16,25) Figure 6 shows
the cationic concentration ( %) of surface ﬁlms formed on the
Zr56Ni25Al15Nb4 alloy as-polished and immersed in 3 mass %
NaCl solution for 1 week. The nominal concentration of the
constituents is also shown for comparison. It is seen that in
the surface ﬁlm formed by air exposure after mechanical
polishing, the cationic fractions of Zr
4+ and Al 3+ greatly
increase with a rate around 30 %, whereas Ni 2+ signiﬁcantly
reduces and Nb 5+ decreases slightly with respect to the
nominal composition. This fact indicates that the preferential
oxidation of Zr and Al occurs in the air-formed ﬁlm. When
the alloy is immersed in the NaCl solution, the cationic
fractions of Zr
4+,A l 3+ and Ni 2+ in the surface ﬁlm slightly
decrease as compared with those in air-formed surface, while
the fraction of Nb 5+ greatly increases with a rate of 33 %. The
results reveal that the surface ﬁlm is enriched in Nb with
respect to that in the as-polished ﬁlm or in the alloy. The
highly stable Nb-, Zr- and Al-enriched surface ﬁlm separates
the alloy from the corrosive solution effectively. The similar
results can be found for the Ta-added alloys as well.
Consequently, the present alloys exhibit excellent corrosion
resistance in chloride-containing solutions.

In the present work, additions of Nb and Ta greatly
increase the ·y and Tg of the based BMG. It has been reported
that the strength and Tg of BMGs show a good correlation. 26)
Tg is dominated by the bonding forces between the
constituent elements of the metallic glasses. As mentioned
in the paper, Nb and Ta elements have large negative heats of
mixing between Zr, Al and Ni elements, respectively, which
lead to stronger atomic bonding among the constituent
elements. Consequently, the additions of Nb and Ta increase
the strength and T
g simultaneously.
5. Conclusions
In the Zr 60¹xAl15Ni25TMx (TM = Nb and Ta) alloy system,
the glass transition temperature greatly increases by additions
of Nb and Ta, but the supercooled liquid region decreases.
The GFA of the base alloy is increased by addition of minor
Nb, the Zr
56Al15Ni25Nb4 BMG has the largest critical
diameter of 20 mm. Addition of Nb and Ta remarkably
improve the corrosion resistance of the BMGs in the
chloride-containing solutions, which due to the formation
of highly protective Zr-, Al- and Nb /Ta-enriched surface
ﬁlm. In addition, the compressive yield strength of the BMG
increases gradually with increasing Nb and Ta contents. The
present BMGs possess high compressive yield stress of
1791­1910 MPa and large plastic strain of 1.1 ­2.9%.
