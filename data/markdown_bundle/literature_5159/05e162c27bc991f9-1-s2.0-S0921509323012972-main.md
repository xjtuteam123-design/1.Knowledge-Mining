ABSTRACT 
Herein, a novel core-shell structured TiAl composite consisting of nanoparticles “ shell ” and a near fully lamellar 
“ core ” was designed using a Ti – 48Al – 2Nb – 2Cr alloy and Boron nitride nanoplates. By tailoring microstructure 
and nanoparticles strengthening, the TiAl composite exhibited a superior combination of strength and ductility at 
high temperature, which achieved an ultimate tensile strength of 514.2 MPa and an outstanding fracture strain of 
18.8 % at 850 
◦
C. 
1. Introduction 
TiAl alloys with excellent high-temperature performances were 
considered as one of the most promising candidates applied at 
650 – 850 
◦
C, which can reduce the weight of gas turbine engines for 
automobile and aerospace applications as much as ~50 % [ 1 , 2 ]. How -
ever, the insufficient strength and ductility of TiAl alloys at a high 
temperature limited its extensive applications [ 3 ]. To further improve 
the performance and service temperature of TiAl alloys, preparing TiAl 
composites via introducing various reinforcements was one of the most 
efficient practices, which was widely explored in recent years [ 4 ]. As a 
result, TiAl composites such as TiB
2
/TiAl, nano-Y
2
O
3
/TiAl, and 
multi-scale Ti
2
AlC/TiAl composites were fabricated, which presented 
excellent properties at room temperature and elevated temperatures 
[ 5 – 9 ]. The ceramic particles, especially the ceramic-nanoparticles could 
effectively refine and strength the alloys [ 8 , 9 ]. Liu et al. in-situ syn -
thesized nano-Ti
2
AlC/TiAl composite with excellent compressive 
strength and oxidation resistance at a high temperature [ 10 ]. 
It is well known that the microstructural characteristics is significant 
in affecting the mechanical properties of alloys [ 11 ], and the strength 
improvement by tailoring a special structure (such as heterogeneous 
structure, gradient structure and bimodal structure) without damaging 
ductility is expected [ 12 , 13 ]. A titanium-nitrogen alloys with a hetero -
geneous microstructure was proposed by Zhang et al. [ 14 ], which 
consists of a hard “ shell ” and a soft “ core ” , exhibiting high strength, high 
thermal stability and good plasticity. 
However, further insight into the contributions of nanoparticles and 
heterogeneous microstructure on the high-temperature tensile behavior 
of TiAl alloys was seldom reported, and the corresponding fracture 
mechanisms were not completely understood. In this work, a dual-phase 
TiB
2
– Ti
2
AlN nanoparticles reinforced TiAl composite with a novel core- 
shell structure was prepared by spark plasma sintering (SPS). The mi -
crostructures and high-temperature tensile properties were investigated, 
and the strengthening and toughening mechanisms caused by nano -
precipitates and the core-shell structure at high temperatures were 
revealed in detail.

4. Conclusions 
In summary, a novel core-shell structured TiAl composite with dual- 
phase TiB
2
– Ti
2
AlN nanoparticles reinforcement was successfully fabri -
cated, which presented a good combination of a high-temperature 
strength (514.2 MPa) and ductility (18.8 %) synergy at 850 
◦
C. The 
role of the nanoparticles “ shell ” in tensile fracture behavior was inves -
tigated detail. Firstly, the high-temperature strength was significantly 
improved due to the “ shell ” bearing more stress and accommodating 
deformation. Secondly, the stress concentration was amended by 
nanoparticles via pulling-out and forming large cavities, which delayed 
the fracture and resulted in a comforting strain.
