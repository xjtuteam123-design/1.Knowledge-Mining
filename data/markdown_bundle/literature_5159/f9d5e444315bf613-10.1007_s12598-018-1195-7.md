Microstructures and properties of Nb–Si-based alloys with
B addition
Liang-Shun Luo, Fu-Xin Wang, Xian-Yu Meng, Yan-Jin Xu, Liang Wang,
Yan-Qing Su* , Jing-Jie Guo, Heng-Zhi Fu
Received: 1 December 2017 / Revised: 23 January 2018 / Accepted: 30 November 2018
/C211The Nonferrous Metals Society of China and Springer-Verlag GmbH Germany, part of Springer Nature 2019
Abstract The Nb–16Si–18Ti– xB (at%, similarly here-
inafter, x = 0, 1, 2, 3) alloys were prepared by arc melting
in a water-cooled copper crucible. The inﬂuences of B
addition on their microstructures and properties were based
on the data of X-ray diffraction (XRD), ﬁeld emission
scanning electron microscopy (FESEM), and electronic
universal material testing machine. It is found that the
addition of B promotes the formation of a-Nb5Si3 phase
and suppresses the formation of Nb 3Si phase. B addition
also tends Nb–16Si–18Ti alloy to form the hypereutectic
structures. The content of silicide phases shows a trend of
ﬁrstly decreasing and then increasing in Nb–16Si–18Ti– xB
(x = 0, 1, 2, 3) alloys. The size of Nb solid solution (Nb ss)
phase increases in Nb–16Si–18Ti– xB( x = 0, 1, 2, 3)
alloys after heat treatment at 1523 K for 10 h. The room
temperature compression strength of Nb–16Si–18Ti alloy
increases ﬁrstly and then decreases with B addition. The
high-temperature compression strength of Nb–16Si–18Ti
alloy decreases ﬁrstly and then increases with B addition. It
is found that the volume and size of silicide phases have a
synergistic effect on the compression strength of Nb–Ti–
Si-based alloys.
Keywords Nb–Si alloys; B element; Phase stability;
Compression yield strength
1 Introduction
The front inlet temperature of new generation aircraft
engine turbine will reach 2073–2323 K. Even considering
the cooling effects of air-cooled blade structure and ther-
mal barrier coatings, the high-pressure turbine blades have
to bear 1473–1673 K. However, Ni-based high-tempera-
ture alloys cannot meet the requirement [ 1, 2]. Nb–Si-
based in situ composites would like to be used in high-
temperature structural materials because of their excellent
strength and toughness [3–8]. At present, the main research
is adding Ti, Cr, Al, Hf, Mo and other alloying elements to
improve their comprehensive mechanical properties
[9–17]. The addition of Ti signiﬁcantly improves room
temperature fracture toughness of Nb–Si binary alloys
[12, 18–20]. B is commonly used in Nb–Si-based high-
temperature alloys as alloying elements. The liquidus
projection of Nb–Si–B ternary system shows that the
addition of B promotes the reaction of L ? Nbss ? a-
Nb5Si3, and B can substitute Si atoms in a-Nb5Si3 phase
[20]. B also effectively improves the high-temperature
oxidation resistance, strength and fracture toughness of
Nb–Si-based alloys [21, 22]. Sha et al. [23] have found that
the strength and fracture toughness of Nb–10W–10Si
alloys are signiﬁcantly increased after adding 2at% B. Ma
et al. [24] have found that it has excellent high-temperature
strength when adding 5at% B in Nb–16Si–10Mo–15W
alloy which was prepared by arc melting and heat-treated at
1973 K for 48 h. The compression yield strength of Nb–
16Si–10Mo–15W–5B alloy reaches 880 MPa at 1773 K,
and the compressive yield stress remains 670 MPa at
1873 K.
From the above discussions, it can be seen that B
addition has many important effects on microstructure and

2 Experimental
Four Nb–16Si–18Ti– xB( x = 0, 1, 2, 3) alloys were pre-
pared by arc melting in a water-cooled copper crucible
under an argon atmosphere. The alloys were made of high-
purity Nb (99.9%), Si (99.9%), sponge titanium (99.6%)
and TiB
2 powder. Each ingot was remelted ﬁve times to
ensure compositional homogeneity. The four specimens
with the dimensions of 10 mm 9 10 mm 9 4 mm were
cut by electro discharge machining (EDM) to make the
microstructure analysis. The four specimens were cut in the
same sections of samples, respectively, in order to facilitate
microstructural observation.
Microstructures of as-cast and heat-treated alloys were
observed by a scanning electron microscope (SEM, FEI,
200F) in backscattered electron (BSE) mode. Electron
probe X-ray microanalysis (EPMA, JEOL, JXA-8230) was
applied to the analysis of phase compositions. X-ray
diffractometer (XRD, PANalytical, D/max-rB, Cu K a,
45 kV, 8 ( /C176)/C1min
-1) was employed to identify the con-
stituent phases presented in Nb–16Si–18Ti– xB( x = 0, 1,
2, 3) alloy. Image-Pro /C210Plus 6.0 image analysis software
was used to measure the particle size and volume fraction
of Nb ss phases and silicide phases. Three pieces of BSE
images in each component of the alloy were selected for
measurement, and the average values were calculated in
order to calculate accurately.
The specimens after grinding were loaded into high-
purity quartz tube which was pumped vacuum and ﬁlled
into 2 9 104 Pa Ar to make annealing heat treatment at
1523 K for 10 h, then cool to room temperature in the
furnace. The compressed samples were cut in the middle
area of the button ingots, and the three-dimensional size of
samples was 4 mm 9 4m m 9 6 mm. The sample sur-
faces were treated by mechanical polishing treatment with
sandpaper No. 1500. The room temperature compression
test was conducted using electronic universal material
testing machine (Instron-5500). The strain rate was
2 9 10
-3 s-1. The test was stopped when the specimens
were broken. The room temperature compression test of
each alloy composition was tested 3 times to keep the
accuracy. And the fracture surfaces were observed by
scanning electron microscope (SEM, FEI, Quanta
200FEG).
The size of specimens for high-temperature compression
test was U6m m 9 9 mm. High-temperature compression
experiments were carried out on the hot working simula-
tion test machine (DSI, Gleeble-1500D). The compression
temperature was 1473 K, and the true strain rate was
2 9 10
-3 s-1. The compression deformation value was
10%.

3.2 Compression properties
Figure 6 shows the room temperature compression strength
of Nb–16Si–18Ti alloys with different B contents. It is
found that the compression strength at room temperature
increases ﬁrstly and then decreases with B addition in Nb–
16Si–18Ti alloys. Figure 7 shows the fracture morphology
of Nb–16Si–18Ti– xB( x = 0, 1, 2, 3) alloy at room tem-
perature under strain rate of 2 9 10
-3 s-1. In Fig. 7, the
white tearing ridges are ductile Nb ss phases which have a
certain plastic deformation. The large smooth cleavage
facets or steps originating from primary silicide phase can
be found. It shows that silicide phases are brittle phases in
Nb–Si-based alloys and bulk silicide phase has signiﬁcant
effect on room temperature compression strength.
The effect of B addition on room temperature com-
pression strength of Nb–16Si–18Ti alloy can be interpreted
as that the compression strength is vastly affected by grain
size and morphology of silicide phases due to that the

4 Conclusion
Through the study of the phase composition, microstruc-
ture, high-temperature stability and mechanical properties
of Nb–Ti–Si as-cast and heat-treated alloys by adding B
element, the following conclusions can be drawn. After
adding B, L ? Nb
ss ? a-Nb5Si3 eutectic transformation
occurs in Nb–16Si–18Ti– xB( x = 1, 2, 3) alloys. The
content of silicide phases shows the trend of ﬁrstly
decreasing and then increasing. When the B content is
more than 3at%, the bulk Nb 5Si3 phase appears in Nb–
16Si–18Ti alloy. The size of Nb ss in Nb–16Si–18Ti– xB
(x = 0, 1, 2, 3) alloys increases after heat treatment at
1523 K for 10 h. It is found that the volume and size of
silicide phases have a synergistic effect on the compression
strength of Nb–Ti–Si-based alloys. The room temperature
compression strength increases ﬁrstly and then decreases
with B addition in Nb–16Si–18Ti alloys, and the room
temperature compression strength of Nb–16Si–18Ti–1B
alloy is the largest, reaching 2410 MPa. The high-tem-
perature compression strength decreases ﬁrstly and then
increases with B addition in Nb–16Si–18Ti alloys, and the
high-temperature strength is the largest in Nb–16Si–18Ti
alloy, reaching 620 MPa.
