Effect of Ni substitution for Ga on the polycrystalline Ni–Mn–Ga–Gd
high-temperature shape memory alloys

abstract
Ni53+xMn25Ga21.9/C0xGd0.1 (at.%) ( x = 0–5) alloys had been obtained by substituting Ga for Ni to increase
transformation temperature. The microstructure and phase transformation of polycrystalline Ni 53+x-
Mn25Ga21.9/C0xGd0.1 alloys had been investigated by XRD, SEM, TEM and DSC. The results showed that
the matrix of Ni 53+xMn25Ga21.9/C0xGd0.1 alloys were tetragonal martensite phase, and the face-centered
cubic c phase was formed in Ni 58Mn25Ga16.9Gd0.1 alloy. The transformation temperatures rose gradually
with the increase of Ni content. The compressive fracture strength and strain ﬁrstly increased then
decreased with the increase of Gd content when Ni content was less than 57 at.%. The generation of
ductile c phase improved the compressive fracture strength and strain of Ni 58Mn25Ga16.9Gd0.1 alloy
up to 2327 MPa and 51.7%, respectively. The complete recovery was obtained in Ni 53+xMn25Ga21.9/C0x-
Gd0.1 (x = 0–3) alloys when the pre-strain was 10%.

1. Introduction
Recently, high-temperature shape memory alloys (HTSMAs)
have attracted more and more interests in high-temperature ﬁled,
such as nuclear power plant, electricity plant, aerospace, oil and
gas exploration. Several HTSMA systems have been widely investi-
gated such as Cu-based alloy, Ni–Al alloy, Ti–Ni–X ( X = Hf, Zr, Pd),
Co–Ni–Ga alloy, Ti–Ta-based alloy and Ni–Mn–Ga alloy [1–10].
However, some problems are unsolved in these alloys. The martens-
itic transformation temperature of Cu-based and Ni–Al alloys are
unstable due to the precipitation of equilibrium phase at high
temperature [1,2]. Ni–Ti–Hf and Ni–Ti–Zr are too brittle to be hot-
processed [3]. Ni–Ti–Pd exhibits excellent mechanical properties
and outstanding shape memory effect (SME), but the high price of
Pd hinders its applications [4,5]. Although Co–Ni–Ga alloy has good
oxidation resistance and cyclic stability, the brittleness of polycrys-
tals limits its applications [6]. Ti–Ta-based alloy exhibits excellent
workability and high transformation temperature. However, the x
phase precipitation leads to unstability of martensitic transforma-
tion temperature [7].
During the past few decades, Ni–Mn–Ga shape memory alloys
have become an outstanding candidate for HTSMAs due to its wide
range of martensitic transformation temperature, large SME,
excellent thermo-stability and relatively low cost [8–10].
Ma et al. studied the microstructure and phase transformation of
Ni
50+xMn25Ga25/C0x (x = 2–11) shape memory alloys, and martensitic
transformation starting temperature (Ms) of this alloy was adjusted
in the range from 44.0 /C176C to 456.2 /C176C by adjusting Ni content [9].
Meanwhile, Ma et al. reported the SME of Ni 50+xMn25Ga25/C0x
(x P 4) single crystals. A SME of 6.1% in Ni 54Mn25Ga21 single crys-
tal with martensitic transformation temperature higher than
250 /C176C was obtained, and this SME remained stable even after
1000 thermal cycles [10]. It is known that polycrystalline alloys
are widely used in practical applications. Unfortunately, the
instinctive brittleness of polycrystalline Ni–Mn–Ga alloys seriously
prohibits its further development and applications.
Some methods were adopted to improve the mechanical
properties of polycrystalline Ni–Mn–Ga alloys, including second
phase strengthening [11–14] and grain reﬁnement [15]. Cu, Co,
Fe and Cr were added into polycrystalline Ni–Mn–Ga HTSMAs to
enhance mechanical properties, however, the occurence of ductile
c phase decreased the SME of Ni–Mn–Ga alloys [11]. Li et al.
improved the mechanical properties and SME of Ni 54Mn25Ga21
alloy by grain reﬁnement, but the mechanical properties and
SME were still lower than that of Ni
54Mn25Ga21 single crystal
[15]. It had been proven that rare earth (RE) addition can toughen
Ni–Mn–Ga shape memory alloys by grain reﬁnement [16–22]. Gao
et al. discovered that the addition of Gd, Dy and Y substituting Ga
obviously improved the mechanical properties of the Ni 50Mn29-
Ga21 ferromagnetic shape memory alloy [17–19]. Therefore, rare
earth addition becomes an effective method to improve the
mechanical properties of Ni–Mn–Ga HTSMAs. In our previous work
[23], the mechanical properties and SME of Ni 54Mn25Ga21/C0xGdx
alloys were investigated. When the Gd content was less than

2. Experimental
The nominal composition of the alloys studied were Ni 53+xMn25Ga21.9/C0xGd0.1
(x = 0–5). High purity nickel (99.99%), manganese (99.97%), gallium (99.99%) and
gadolinium (99.99%) were melted in a non-consumed vacuum arc furnace under ar-
gon atmosphere to prepare Ni
53+xMn25Ga21.9/C0xGd0.1 alloys. The ingots were re-
melted six times to ensure homogeneity. The samples ware annealed in vacuum
quartz tubes at 1073 K for 24 h, and then quenched into ice water.
XRD measurements were performed by using a Rigaku D/max-rB with Cu K
a
radiation. The phase transformation temperatures were determined by Perkin–El-
mer Diamond DSC with heating and cooling rate of 20 /C176C/min. The microstructures
of the alloys were surveyed by optical microscope, MX2600FE scanning electron
microscopy equipped with energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) analysis system
and FEI TECNAI G
2 20 STWIN 200 kV transmission electron microscopy (TEM)
equipped with a double-tilt cooling stage. Thin-foil specimens for TEM observation
were mechanically polished to about 100
lm and twinjet electropolished with an
electrolyte of nitric acid and methanol, 3:7 in volume, at around /C015 /C176C. The com-
pression tests were performed at room temperature on a Instron 5569 testing sys-
tem at a crosshead displacement speed of 0.2 mm/min, and the size of the sample
was about £3m m /C25 mm. The heights of the samples were measured before load-
ing (L
0), after loading ( L1), after unloading ( L2) and after heating to 600 /C176C for 2 min
(L3) by a micrometer with an accuracy of 0.01 mm. The pre-strain during compres-

3. Results and discussion
X-ray diffraction pattern of Ni 53+xMn25Ga21.9/C0xGd0.1 alloys at
room temperature are shown in Fig. 1 , and the corresponding
lattice parameters are summarized in Table 1 . Seven reﬂection
peaks of Ni 53+xMn25Ga21.9/C0xGd0.1 alloys can be indexed with the
tetragonal-structured martensite. The positions of this diffraction
peaks of martensite keep almost constant. It can be concluded that
the martensitic structure of Ni 53+xMn25Ga21.9/C0xGd0.1 alloys is not
changed due to the change of Ni composition. No other diffraction
peaks could be observed when Ni content is less than 57 at.%, while
several additional peaks appear when Ni content is 58 at.%. It can
be concluded that new phase was formed and conﬁrmed as face-
centered cubic-structured (fcc) c phase, with main diffraction
peaks of (1 1 1), (2 0 0), (2 0 2), (3 1 1) and (2 2 2), as shown in
Fig. 1 . Among the c peaks, the c(1 1 1) and c(2 2 2) peaks overlap
with the M(2 2 2) and M(4 4 4) peaks, respectively. The lattice
parameter of the c phase is calculated to be a = 0.3618 nm. As sum-
marized in Table 1 , the lattice parameters a and b contract while
the c axis elongates. Fig. 2 shows the optical micrographs of solu-
tion-treated Ni53+xMn25Ga21.9/C0xGd0.1 alloys, and the sampling posi-
tion of every ingot is same. It can be observed from Fig. 2 that the
microstructure of Ni 53+xMn25Ga21.9/C0xGd0.1 alloys are monophase
when the Ni content is less than 57 at.%. This is consistent with
the XRD result. The average grain size of Ni 53+xMn25Ga21.9/C0xGd0.1
(x = 0–4) alloys are about 100 lm, 40 lm, 50 lm, 75 lm and
90 lm, respectively. A new phase occurs when the Ni content is
58 at.%. The grain size of the Ni 54Mn25Ga20.9Gd0.1 alloy is far less
than that of Ni 54Mn25Ga21 alloy, which was studied before [23].
It can be inferred that the grain size of Ni 53+xMn25Ga21.9/C0xGd0.1 al-
loys are apparently reﬁned due to small amount of Gd addition.
In order to conﬁrm the structure of the martensite and the
second-phase in the Ni 53+xMn25Ga21.9/C0xGd0.1 alloys, TEM observa-
tion was carried out. Fig. 3 a displays the TEM bright ﬁeld image
of martensite in the Ni 53Mn25Ga21.9Gd0.1 alloy. It can be seen that
the bright ﬁeld images exhibit large stripe-like martensitic plates
with several microns in width, and the hair-like stripes can also
be seen in the big plates. Fig. 3 b shows the corresponding SAED
pattern. From the SAED pattern, the structure of martensite in
the Ni 53Mn25Ga21.9Gd0.1 can be indexed as tetragonal structure.
Fig. 3 c and e shows a TEM bright ﬁeld images of martensite and
c phase in Ni 58Mn25Ga16.9Gd0.1 alloy. It seems only the widths of
the martensitic plates exhibit some differences compared with
Ni53Mn25Ga21.9Gd0.1 alloy, which may be due to the sampling
position. The irregular second phase embedded in the plate-like
martensite is also observed. Fig. 3d shows the corresponding SAED
patterns taken from the matrix (area B). It can be indexed as tetrag-
onal structure. Fig. 3 f shows the corresponding SAED patterns
taken from the second phase (area C), which is indexed as fcc c
phase. The superlattice points in Fig. 3 f shows that the arrange-
ment of atomic in c phase is orderly along [1 0 0] zone axes.
According to the above results, it should be noted that the micro-
structure of matrix in Ni
53+xMn25Ga21.9/C0xGd0.1 alloys are tetrago-
nal-structured martensite, which are not changed with the
increase of Ni content and the small amount of Gd doping com-
pared with Ni 53Mn25Ga22 alloy [9]. When the Ni content is
58 at.%, the microstructure consists of two phases, including
tetragonal-structured martensite and fcc c phase. This is consistent
with the XRD and optical results.

4. Conclusion
The microstructure, martensitic transformation behavior,
mechanical properties and SME of Ni 53+xMn25Ga21.9/C0xGd0.1
HTSMAs have been investigated. The results demonstrated that
the grain size of Ni 53+xMn25Ga21.9/C0xGd0.1 alloys can be reﬁned by
small amount of Gd doping. When the Ni content is less than
57 at.%, the microstructure of the Ni 53+xMn25Ga21.9/C0xGd0.1 alloys
are the tetragonal martensite. The compressive fracture strength
and strain are ﬁrstly increases then decreases with the increase
of Ni content. The fcc c phase is formed when the Gd content is
58 at.%, and the compressive fracture strength and strain are
2327 MPa and 51.7%, respectively. The complete recovery is
obtained from the Ni 53+xMn25Ga21.9/C0xGd0.1 (x = 0–3) alloys with
10% pre-strain. The improvement in SME is mainly due to the grain
reﬁnement and solid solution strengthening.
