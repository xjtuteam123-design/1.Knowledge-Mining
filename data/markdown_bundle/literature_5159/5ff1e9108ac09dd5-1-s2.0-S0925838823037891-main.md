ABSTRACT 
A method for predicting fatigue life using damage variables coupled with multi-modal microstructure damage 
evolution was proposed after analyzing the weights of the multi-modal microstructure of directionally solidified 
Ni-based superalloys DZ125 in this paper. Mechanical property tests and metallographic observation tests were 
carried out, and the microstructure weights of the superalloys were quantified using principal component 
analysis (PCA) and entropy-based inter-criteria correlation (CRITIC) weight analysis. The relationship between 
mechanical properties and multi-modal microstructures of the superalloys was analyzed, and fatigue life was 
predicted using damage variables coupled with multi-modal microstructure damage evolution. The weights of 
multi-modal microstructures of Ni-based superalloys and the influence of multi-modal microstructural damage 
evolution on mechanical properties were quantified by taking into account the interactions among the micro -
structural factors and their combined effects, which were important references for the design and processing of 
the superalloy as well as the prediction of mechanical properties. 
1. Introduction 
Ni-based superalloy DZ125 (US ds rene 125) is one of the direc -
tionally solidified casting alloys with high performance levels developed 
independently. It has excellent thermal fatigue properties, good overall 
performance in medium and high temperatures, and has been exten -
sively used to manufacture turbine blades for advanced aero-engine hot 
components [1 – 3] . The microstructure of directionally solidified su -
peralloys is complex and diverse, including γ’ precipitates, γ / γ’ eutectic, 
carbides, topologically close packed (TCP) phases, creep cavities, etc. It 
is demonstrated that the multi-modal microstructure evolution of the 
superalloy has a significant impact on mechanical properties, and the 
various forms of microstructure evolution have different degrees of in -
fluence on the mechanical properties of the superalloy [2,4 – 6] . 
The microstructural damage of Ni-based superalloy DZ125 generally 
has the following types of evolution: coarsening and rafting of the γ’ 
precipitates [2,7 – 9] , carbide formation and evolution [10] , evolution of 
γ / γ’ eutectic [11] , generation of topologically close packed (TCP) phases 
[6] , and generation of creep cavities [12] . The γ’ precipitates coarse -
ning/rafting behavior gradually intensifies with increasing temperature 
and thermal exposure time during the service of superalloys, and the 
morphology, size, and distribution of γ’ precipitates significantly alter 
[2] . These parameters play a significant role in determining the me -
chanical properties of the superalloy and are most closely related to 
them [13] . It has been found that the volume fraction of γ’ precipitates 
affects the mechanism of dislocation and γ’ precipitates particles during 
creep, which in turn has an impact on the creep properties of the su -
peralloy [14 – 17] . Carbide is the main grain boundary precipitate in the 
service of superalloys, and during the heat treatment and use of the 
alloy, it changes the original composition, microstructure, morphology, 
and distribution of carbide, its volume fraction, scale, and morphology, 
affecting the mechanical properties of superalloys significantly [10,13, 
18] . It has been found that the size, number, and distribution of γ / γ’ 
eutectic have a direct impact on the mechanical properties of the alloy 
[19,20] . In his investigation into the formation of γ / γ’ eutectic, Mirak 
[21] found that the γ / γ’ eutectic of the single-crystal CMSX-4 superalloy 
tended to form destructive surfaces in tensile tests and that the presence 
of γ / γ’ eutectic weakened the mechanical properties of the superalloy. 
Hu [22] found that when the size of MC carbides and eutectic within the 
interdendritic region of K417G Ni-based superalloy is large, it is favor -
able for the initiation and expansion of cracks, and the cracks initiate 
within the carbides at the interdendritic and grain boundaries and at the 
edges of eutectic and expand laterally along the interdendritic and grain 
boundaries, which is one of the main fracture mechanisms for the

From the above large number of studies, it is found that the influence 
of microstructure on mechanical properties of alloys is typically 
analyzed from the perspective of a single factor by changing one factor 
at a time to analyze its effect on mechanical properties, while there are 
various types of microstructures affecting the mechanical properties of 
DZ125 Ni-based superalloys, and the evolution of microstructure 
morphology is a key factor in the change of material properties. The 
analysis of microstructure influence on the mechanical properties of 
materials will have more integrity if it takes into account the in -
teractions between variables and the combined action of multiple 
modes. Under the common influence of the multi-modal microstructure 
evolution, it is important to quantify the influence weights of the multi- 
modal microstructure of the material for the design and processing of 
the alloy as well as for the prediction of the mechanical properties of the 
material. By using inter-criteria correlation (CRITIC), Patel [24] calcu -
lated the weight of shape errors in engineering parts as well as process 
parameters. The importance of the parameters has been determined, 
increasing the precision of the predictions. For multi-objective optimi -
zation of process parameters for machining and producing aluminum 
alloys, Abas [25] performed ratio analysis using analysis of variance 
(ANOVA) and inter-criteria correlation (CRITIC) to identify the most 
important and contributing factors among all manufacturing parameters 
to obtain multi-response optimal cutting parameters. Principal compo -
nent analysis (PCA) was used by Patel [26] , Nguyen [27] , and Li [28] to 
quantify and determine the weight values of multiple process parame -
ters for 7075 aluminum alloy, 304 stainless steel, and Ti-6Al-4V, 
respectively. It was discovered that the selection of target weights was 
a better method for resolving the problem of identifying the optimal 
process parameters. The Critic method [29] and principal component 
analysis (PCA) method [28] used in the aforementioned study belong to 
the objective assignment method, which referred to the method of 
determining the weights of the indicators based on the relationship 
between the evaluation information given by the evaluator and the 
standard weights derived based on the available data measurements 
without the intervention of experts [30,31] . Entropy [32] , the analytic 
hierarchy process (AHP) [33] , and other methods of objective assign -
ment are also available. Because contrast intensity and conflict are taken 
into account when determining weights using CRITIC [34] , unlike other 
weight determination techniques, this information makes the use of 
criteria important to establish effective weights [25,30] . Although the 
importance of quantified weights has been seen in a large number of 
studies, the majority of current studies have conducted weight analysis 
from the perspective of processing and manufacturing of alloys, and few 
studies have quantified weights for damage evolution of multi-modal 
microstructures of Ni-based superalloys and the effect of damage evo -
lution of coupled multi-modal microstructures with quantitative weights 
on mechanical properties and fatigue life of superalloys. 
In this paper, a method for predicting fatigue life utilizing damage 
variables of coupled with multi-modal microstructure damage evolution 
was proposed after analyzing the weights of multi-modal microstructure 
in directionally solidified Ni-based superalloys DZ125. Firstly, the me -
chanical property tests at room and high temperatures and metallo -
graphic observation were carried out to obtain the morphological 
characteristics of the multi-modal microstructure of the superalloy, and 
the quantitative characterization and statistics were performed. Subse -
quently, the weights of the microstructure parameters and those of the 
multi-modal microstructure were quantitatively analyzed using prin -
cipal component analysis and entropy-based CRITIC weight analysis, 
and then the microstructure with the largest weight value was obtained. 
Finally, the relationship between the mechanical properties of the su -
peralloy and the multi-modal microstructure was investigated, and a 
method for forecasting fatigue life utilizing damage variables of coupled 
with the damage evolution of the multi-modal microstructure was pro -
posed. The weights of multi-modal microstructures of Ni-based super -
alloys and the influence of multi-modal microstructural damage 
evolution on mechanical properties were quantified by taking into ac -
count the interactions among the microstructural variables and their 
combined effects.

2.3.2. Criteria importance through inter-criteria correlation 
The entropy-based inter-criteria correlation (CRITIC) weights 
approach was used to analyze the microstructure weights [24] . The 
CRITIC method proposed by Diakoulaki is an objective assignment 
method that uses the contrast strength and conflict index as its two in -
dicators. The contrast strength is expressed using the standard deviation. 
If the larger the standard deviation of the data indicates the greater the 
fluctuation, the higher the weights will be. Conflict is expressed using 
the correlation coefficient; if the correlation coefficient between in -
dicators is high and the conflict is low, then the weight is low as well. 
The weights are calculated by multiplying the contrast intensity with the 
conflicting index and normalizing them to obtain the final weights. The 
advantages of the CRITIC weight analysis method are, firstly, weight 
allocation through standard deviation and correlation between features, 
which does not involve subjective judgment and reduces the possible 
subjective bias in the process of weight determination; secondly, it not 
only takes into account the variability of the features but also the 
independence between features, which makes the weight allocation 
more comprehensive and accurate; and thirdly, it does not require any 
additional data input or assumption of the premise. 
After obtaining the weights of the microstructure morphology pa -
rameters, the CRITIC entropy weighting approach was then chosen for 
the weighting analysis of the three types of microstructures. First, after 
analyzing the weight of the microstructure by the PCA, it was discovered 
that the test indexes did not meet the applicable threshold of the PCA, 
indicating that the PCA was not appropriate for use in analyzing the 
weight of the microstructure; second, because the CRITIC weighting 
analysis method was able to take into account all three types of infor -
mation at the same time for the analysis of the data, CRITIC weighting 
completely utilized the data objective attributes to facilitate the scien -
tific evaluation. Moreover, the entropy method was added at the same 
time, which could consider the size of the information. The CRITIC 
weights approach is suitable for analyzing data with certain correlations 
between indicators or factors, and the data is stable. The entropy 
weights method is used to determine the weight of indicators according 
to the dispersion degree between indicators, and the CRITIC method and 
the entropy weights method together can reflect the weight of indicators 
more objectively. In this study, the multi-modal microstructures had a 
certain correlation between them due to the bias effect of the elements. 
Thus, the CRITIC entropy weights method was used to analyze the 
weights of the three microstructures. The weights analysis process is

3. Results and discussions 
3.1. Microstructure morphology 
Fig. 2 depicts the microstructure morphology of the DZ125 alloy at 
300 h pre-processing. It is clear from Fig. 2 (a) that the material exhibits 
multi-modal microstructure characteristics, including γ’ precipitates, 
carbides, and γ / γ’ eutectic, as well as serrated grain boundaries. Car -
bides formed different morphologies during the solidification of the 
superalloy, and the morphology changed when affected by temperature 
and stress. The graph displayed that there were granular carbides, 
massive carbides, long strips of carbides, and chain carbides at grain 
boundaries. Typically, the morphology of γ / γ’ eutectic was mostly daisy- 
like or bare plate, and it can be seen from the graph that carbides and 
γ / γ’ eutectic were primarily dispersed in the region near the grain 
boundary. 
The microstructure morphologies of γ’ precipitates and the γ matrix 
phase can be clearly seen in Fig. 2 (b). The γ’ precipitates was a dark 
rectangular body, with the four corners of the rectangular body showing 
a rounded shape. A white area with a mesh-like structure made up the γ 
matrix phase. The fainter area was the γ matrix phase covered by the 
surface of the γ’ precipitates. 
3.1.1. γ ’ precipitate 
The coarse grains with jagged grain boundaries, a uniform cubic γ / γ’ 
microstructure, and discrete phases at grain boundaries are typical 
microstructural features of advanced Ni-based casting superalloys [2] . 
Fig. 4 shows the morphology of the γ’ precipitates evolution in a 
randomly selected observation area from the surface of a circular 
metallographic specimen with a diameter of 6 mm. As can be seen in 
Fig. 4 , the γ’ precipitate morphology in the dendritic at 0 h, 300 h, 
600 h, and 900 h of pre-processing after long-service damage of the 
Ni-based superalloy is depicted in Fig. 4 . As pre-processing time in -

degrees. The γ’ precipitates at 0 h are basically rectangular and uni -

radius of the four corners of the γ’ precipitate was gradually increased, 
while significant coarsening and joining occurred. The γ’ precipitates 
are depicted in Fig. 4 (d) at 900 h. The γ’ precipitates evolved intensely 
so that the cubic shape is invisible and the links form irregular polygons.

the alloy will gradually exhibit an elongation phenomenon while 
coarsening, and after a long period of action, further polymerization and 
interconnection will take place. The rafting phenomenon is not evident

3.1.3. γ / γ ’ eutectic 
Ni-based superalloy γ / γ’ eutectic was the product of non-equilibrium 
solidification in the solidification process, which was the result of so -
lidification segregation of superalloy elements. Cast Ni-based superal -
loys accelerate the interdendritic γ / γ’ eutectic due to severe 
solidification segregation, which has an impact on the improvement of 
superalloy properties. Fig. 1 illustrates the γ / γ’ eutectic morphology, 
which exists between the dendrites and is mainly in two states, radial 
and sieve-like. 
The driving force of γ / γ’ eutectic was the supersaturation of γ’ 
forming elements in the residual liquid phase. As the solidification 
proceeds, Al and Ti are biased in the dendritic region, which in turn 
leads to the formation of γ / γ’ eutectic between the dendrites at the later 
stage of solidification. In Fig. 10 , the SEM microscopic observation 
shows that the γ / γ’ eutectic is distributed among the dendrites of the 
superalloy. Comparative research revealed that the γ / γ’ eutectic was not 
only formed between the dendrites, but also that the eutectic formation 
position was related to the carbide position. The γ / γ’ eutectic was 
largely spread at the grain boundary of the carbide, and part of it was 
connected to the carbide.

3.2. Microstructure weights analysis 
3.2.1. Microstructure morphology parameter weights 
The above statistical analysis of the size distribution of different 
microstructure morphological parameters showed that for superalloys, 
each morphological parameter had an influence on the material, and the 
parameters were also correlated with each other. In order to determine 
which microstructure characteristic morphological parameters had the 
biggest impact on the weights, the weights of the five morphological 
parameters were analyzed and calculated using the weight analysis 
method in this section. 
The principal component analysis method was used to weight the 
different morphological dimensions of γ’ precipitates, carbides, and γ / γ’ 
eutectic. The results of the Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin (KMO) test and Bartlett 
tests, as well as the rate of cumulative variance contribution, are shown 
in Table 3 . It is generally considered that the KMO suitable for principal 
component analysis has a range of > 0.6 and the cumulative variance 
contribution of the initial eigenvalue variance of the principal compo -
nent over 1 exceeds 80 %, which is considered to reflect all indicator 
information. The calculation results are shown in Table 3 . 
The outer circles of the double pie chart in Fig. 13 depict the weight 
distribution of the five morphological parameters for the three different 
types of microstructures. It can be seen from the graph that the weights 
of area-polygon, feret-max, and VF for γ’ precipitates, carbide, and γ / γ’ 
eutectic were larger. As discussed in the previous section, these three 
morphological parameters had similar characterization significance to 
the microstructure of the material. Since the weight of VF was the 
largest, it was decided that VF was the most important factor for 
microstructure. The italicized values in Table 4 denote the maximum 
weights of microstructure morphology parameters. 
3.2.2. Microstructure weights 
The inner circle of the double pie chart in Fig. 13 displays the weight 
distribution of the three microstructures. According to the results of the 
previous analysis, the weights of VF in the microstructure morphology 
parameters were the largest, so they were used as the characteristic 
factor for the microstructure weights analysis. From the results of the 
inner circle distribution in Fig. 13 , the weights of the γ / γ’ eutectic in the 
microstructure without pre-processing were slightly larger than those of 
the γ’ precipitates, and the weights of the γ’ precipitates at all other pre- 
processing times were larger than those of the γ / γ’ eutectic. As a result, 
the weights of the γ’ precipitates was largest, followed by the γ / γ’ 
eutectic and then the carbide. As the pre-processing time increased, the 
weight of γ’ precipitates increased gradually, and the weight of γ / γ’ 
eutectic decreased gradually. The weights of the carbides remained 
essentially unchanged. Table 5 shows the weight values and damage 
variables for the three microstructures, with italics indicating the 
highest weights.

4. Conclusions 
In this paper, an analytical study was conducted to investigate the 
influence of weights on the multi-modal microstructure of DZ125 
directionally solidified superalloys, and the following conclusions were 
obtained: 
1. The multi-modal microstructure morphological weights of DZ125 
directionally solidified superalloy were analyzed utilizing principal 
component weight analysis method. The weights of area-polygon, 
feret-max, and VF for γ’ precipitates, carbide, and γ / γ’ eutectic 
were larger, with VF having the largest weight. 
2. The influence weights of the three microstructures were analyzed by 
using the entropy-based CRITIC weight analysis method, in which 
the largest weight was for the γ’ precipitates, followed by the γ / γ’ 
eutectic. As the pre-processing time increased, the weight of γ’ pre -
cipitates increased and the weight of γ / γ’ eutectic decreased.

4. Based on the continuum damage theory, a formula for the definition 
of damage variables was given utilizing the volume fraction of mi -
crostructures, and this formula was used to quantitatively charac -
terize the degree of total damage evolution caused by multi-modal 
microstructures to obtain the microstructure damage variables at 
different pre-processing times. It was discovered that the total 
damage variables exhibited an upward trend when pre-processing 
time increased. 
5. A formula for predicting the low-cycle fatigue life utilizing the total 
damage variables of multi-modal microstructure was proposed and 
predicted for the low-cycle fatigue life at room temperature and high 
temperature, respectively. The prediction formula showed good 
prediction accuracy and forecasted the fatigue life within an error 
band of ± 2. 
6. In this paper, the influence of multimodal microstructure weights 
considering time-series damage was discussed, and this work was 
intended to explore the causes of failure in materials and thus predict 
the fatigue life of materials. The relationship between the effect of 
multimodal microstructure weights on the fatigue crack initiation 
rules of the material should be discussed in detail in the future.
