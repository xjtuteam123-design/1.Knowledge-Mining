2. Experimental procedures
According to the Ir–Hf and Ir–Nb binary phase
diagrams [5], the solubility limitations of Hf and Nb in
Ir are 5mol% and 12mol%, respectively, at 1950/C176C.
Due to the unavailability of an Ir–Hf–Nb ternary phase
diagram, the nominal composition of Ir–3Hf binary was
carefully chosen as the base and 1, 3, and 5mol% Nb
were respectively added as the second solute to obtain
the saturated monolithic fcc solid solution at and above
1950 /C176C. Elements with 99.9mass% or higher purity were
used. Button ingots were prepared by arc-melting in a
vacuum ﬁve or six times to homogenize compositions
throughout ingots. The ingots were ﬁnally annealed at
2000 /C176C for 24h. The phases were identiﬁed at room
temperature using the high-resolution X-ray diﬀraction
technique (ﬁltered CuKa radiation, 40KV and 300mA)
with the RIGAKU RINT 2500, and the microstructure
was observed using a scanning electron microscope
(SEM). The lattice parameter was estimated using the
least squares method from the X-ray diﬀraction pattern.
The Vickers hardness (HV) and Young/C213s modulus were
measured under applied loads of 4.9N (500g) and
0.147N (15g), respectively. The Young/C213s modulus was
measured by the indentation method[10]. Seven to ten
points were taken for indentation to obtain an accurate
average. Compression tests were conducted at 1950/C176C
in a vacuum at an initial strain rate of 3·10
/C04s/C01 using
an Instron 8560 testing machine. In our compressive
testing,twotothreespecimensweretestedforeachalloy.
The samples were held in the furnace for 15min at the
testing temperature before the compressive test. The
dimensions of the compression test cylinders were 5mm
in diameter and 5mm in length, and the cylinder sur-
faces were ﬁnally polished using 800# SiC paper prior
to testing. In testing, graphite was used as the platen.
The strength of graphite at 2000/C176C is about 250MPa.
