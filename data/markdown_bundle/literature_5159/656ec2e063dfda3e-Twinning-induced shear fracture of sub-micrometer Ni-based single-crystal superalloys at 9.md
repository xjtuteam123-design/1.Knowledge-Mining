ABSTRACT
Topologically close-packed (TCP) precipitates and deformation twins significantly impact the high-
temperature performances of Ni-based single-crystal (SC) superalloys. However, their contributions
to failure of superalloys remain unclear. This study, utilizing in situ thermal-nanomechanical test-
ing at 900°C, reveals a twinning-induced shear fracture in sub-micrometer superalloys containing
TCP phases. Under < 110>
m tension, nanotwins nucleation dominates over the shear sliding at
pre-existing σ /matrix interface, inducing shear fracture via the twin boundary (TB) sliding. Density
functional theory calculations confirm that TBs exhibit weaker shear resistance than the σ /matrix
interfaces. These findings provide insights into twinning-induced premature failure of SC superalloys
under service conditions.

4. Discussion
Previous studies have shown that deformation twins
are prevalent in the intermediate-temperature deforma-
tion of SC superalloys and are highly dependent on the
loadingorientations[ 14,15,37–41].Kakehi[ 14]reported
prevalent twinning under [011] tension and [001] com-
pression at 700°C, contrasting with stacking faults dom-
inateddeformationsunderthereversedloadingorienta-
tions.ConsistentwithKakehi’sfindings,ourresultsshow
thatnumerousnanotwinsnucleateandthickenunderthe
similar conditions, i.e. tension along the [¯110]
m direc-
tion (Figure2(h)). Twinning in SC superalloy (mainly
L12-γ’) involves the shearing of a/6<112> type par-
tials (see the red arrow in Figure4(a)). However, the
successive glide of the partials on adjacent{111} planes
generateas-shearedpseudotwinswithhigh-energycon-
figuration of Al-Al neighbors (see the red dotted lines
in Figure 4(b)), which hinders the extensive propaga-
tion. This high-energy structure can be eliminated by
a thermally activated reordering [13,42]v i aat w o - s t e p
exchange,includingthefirstexchangeofNiandAlatoms
(see the blue arrow) and the second exchange of the
displaced Ni and another Al atom (see the magenta
arrow), thus transforming the pseudo twins into true
twins (Figure4(b)). Subsequent twin thickening occurs
through the sequential glide of a/6<112> partials on
the successive planes, enabling continuous twin propa-
gationacrossboth γ’and γ phases(Figure 4(c)).
Underfurtherstraining,twinsformnearthe σ/matrix
interface, and shear sliding along TBs is triggered due

5. Conclusion
In summary, utilizing a state-of-the-art in situ TEM
thermal-nanomechanical testing,wereportedtwinning-
induced shear fracture of sub-micrometer Ni-based SC
superalloys containing TCP phases at 900°C. Under
<110> tension, twins propagate across bothγ’a n d
γ phases, thickening through continuous glide of
a/6<112> partials. Shear sliding initiates at the σ/
matrix interface, but rapidly shifts to the TB following
twin formation near the phase boundary. This indicates
a preferential shear fracture dominated by TB sliding,
which is further accelerated by an orientation rotation.
