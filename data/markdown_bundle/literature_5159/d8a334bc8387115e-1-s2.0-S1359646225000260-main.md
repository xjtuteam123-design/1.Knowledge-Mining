ABSTRACT
Refractory high entropy alloys (RHEAs) have a high melting point and strength, exhibiting great potential for 
high-temperature applications. In this work, a novel single-phase Ti

5.3 g/cm

of ordered precipitates in the matrix phase. The severe lattice distortion increases the dislocation movement 
barrier and thereby promotes the dislocation multiplication during high-temperature plastic deformation, 
resulting in significant strain hardening. The strong solid solution strengthening originating from large modulus 
mismatch is mainly responsible for the enhanced high-temperature softening resistance.
With the rapid development of the aerospace industry, a harsher 
service environment requires metallic materials with stronger heat 
resistance in high temperatures [ 1 ]. Therefore, the high-temperature 
strength and stability need to be continuously advanced for the 
high-temperature alloys. However, conventional Ni- and Ti-based su -
peralloys, which are widely used in high-temperature conditions, are 
reaching their service temperature limits. In recent years, multiple 
principal-element alloys, which can also be called high entropy alloys 
(HEAs) [ 2 – 4 ], have provided new approaches to designing novel 
high-temperature structural alloys due to their inherent sluggish diffu -
sion effect and strong lattice distortions [ 5 ], which allow them to have 
excellent high-temperature mechanical properties [ 6 – 8 ].
Refractory high entropy alloys (RHEAs) with a body-centered cubic 
(BCC) structure have received widespread attention due to their high 
strength at high temperatures, good thermal stability, and softening 
resistance [ 9 ]. For example, NbMoTaW RHEAs have high strength at 
high temperatures, but with a high density of more than 13 g/cm
3 
and 
very poor plasticity at room temperature [ 10 ]. Recently, it was reported 
that the TiVCrZrNb-based light-weight RHEAs consisting of the group ’ s 
IV, V, and VI transition elements show good high-temperature properties 
with lower density [ 11 , 12 ]. A BCC single-phase NbTaTiHf RHEA has a 
high fracture toughness over a wide temperature range from low to high 
temperatures due to the formation of kink bands in the alloy, which can 
promote nonuniform strain deformation [ 13 ]. From many of the BCC 
single-phase RHEAs reported, the yield strengths are notable at 600 
◦
C to 
800 
◦
C due to the refractory solid solution structure, but when the 
temperature is higher the yield strength is insufficent [ 14 – 17 ]. It is 
noted that single-phase HEAs have a relatively low yield strength 
compared with dual-phase counterparts [ 18 ]. Moreover, the phase 
decomposition will occur in single-phase HEAs at intermediate tem -
peratures owing to the reduced high entropy effect stabilizing the phase 
structure [ 19 ].

To better reveal the deformation mechanism of Ti60Mo12 RHEA at 
high temperatures, the microstructures of the Ti60Mo12 RHEA are 
characterized after high-temperature plastic deformation. Fig. 4 shows 
the TEM images of 600 
◦
C deformed Ti60Mo12 RHEA. It can be observed 
that after plastic deformation at a high temperature, a high density of 
dislocations still exists in the deformed regions of the alloy. The dislo -
cation network structures were formed by planar multislip in Fig. 4(a) . 
The dislocation entanglement results from the dislocations cross-slip. In 
addition, the localized dislocation pinning phenomenon and the high- 
density dislocation loop structure can be observed in the deformed 
grain, as shown in Fig. 4(b) . The dislocation cell and a dense dislocation 
wall were formed in some severely plastic-deformed regions, as shown 
in Fig. 4(c) and ( d ). The average width of the dislocation wall spacing is 
~70 nm. Such a large number of dislocation activities can effectively 
hinder the movement of dislocations and the dislocation wall structure 
can be viewed as a subgranular structure leading to dislocation plugging 
[ 50 , 51 ]. Therefore, the Ti60Mo12 RHEA still has a strain-hardening 
ability and excellent resistance to high-temperature softening at 600 
◦
C. In addition to the series of dislocation activities observed above, 
dynamic recovery and recrystallization processes were also found in 
some of the plastically deformed regions. As shown in Fig. 4(e) and ( f ), 
there are obvious deformed regions (DG) and recrystallized regions (RG) 
in the deformed matrix. At the same time, the dislocation density in the 
recrystallization region has decreased significantly, resulting in the 
transformation of dislocation cells into subgrains and tiny equiaxed 
nanocrystals. The corresponding SAED pattern indicates that the nano -
crystals are the BCC structure.
