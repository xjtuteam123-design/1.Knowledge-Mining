Abstract
The (Nb)/Nb3Si eutectic alloys in the Nb–Si binary and Nb–Ti–Si ternary systems were unidirectionally solidiﬁed in an optical ﬂoating zone
melting furnace. The effect of solidiﬁcation rates on microstructures of the alloys is investigated. The coupled growth of (Nb) and Nb3Si is observed
in the binary alloy solidiﬁed at 10 mm/h. The ternary alloys exhibit the development of discontinuous (Nb) particles within the Nb3Si matrix. The
unidirectionally solidiﬁed alloys show improved fracture toughness and enhanced high temperature compressive strength as compared with the
arc-melted counterparts. The role of an aligned eutectic microstructure in toughening is discussed from the existing models based on the crack
bridging and the crack deﬂection.

1. Introduction
The third generation single crystal Ni-based superalloys are
used as turbine engine airfoils exposed to a high temperature
environment slightly over 1400 K. However further increases in
the temperature capability of the superalloys seem to be difﬁcult,
since the operating temperature is approaching to 90% of their
melting temperatures. Therefore the performance improvement
of aircraft engines will necessitate the development of a new
class of heat resistant materials.

considerable attention for potential high temperature applica-
tion [1,2]. According to the Nb–Si binary phase diagram [3],a
(Nb)/Nb
5Si3 two-phase microstructure forms through a eutectic
reaction (L→ (Nb) + Nb3Si) at 2193 K, and a eutectoid reaction

variables on the fracture toughness and high temperature com-

that operate in the unidirectionally solidiﬁed alloys are discussed
from the existing toughening models based on the crack bridging
and the crack deﬂection.
2. Experimental
Rod ingots with the dimensions of 11 mm in diameter and 70 mm in length
were prepared by arc-melting of high purity Nb, Ti, and Si under an Ar atmo-
sphere. The nominal alloy compositions were Nb–17.5at.% Si and Nb–10at.%
Ti–17.5at.% Si. The alloys were unidirectionally solidiﬁed in an optical ﬂoating
zone (OFZ) furnace at the rates of 10 and 100 mm/h under an Ar gas ﬂowing
atmosphere. Due to the sluggish diffusion, the high temperature phase Nb
3Si was
retained at room temperature after unidirectional solidiﬁcation. It is reported that
the nose temperatures of the TTT curves for Nb
3Si decomposition are 1773 K
for the Nb–Si binary alloys [31], and 1673 K for the Nb–Ti–Si ternary alloys
[27]. Therefore, the binary alloys are annealed at 1773 K, and the ternary alloys
at 1673 K for 500 h under vacuum after unidirectional solidiﬁcation. To min-
imize oxidation during annealing, alloys were wrapped with Nb foils prior to
annealing.
Microstructural observations were made by a scanning electron microscope
(SEM). The constituent phases of the alloys were identiﬁed by X-ray diffrac-
tometry (XRD), and energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS). Mechanical
properties of the alloys were evaluated by fracture toughness measurements and
compression tests. Fracture toughness was determined by four-point bending
tests (outer span: 21 mm, inner span: 7 mm) using chevron-notched specimens,
and analytical calculation using the Bluhm’s slice model[32]. The dimensions of
bending test specimen were 5 mm× 5m m× 30 mm, and the chevron notch was
introduced perpendicular to the solidiﬁcation direction using electro-discharge
machining (EDM) to set up the initial ligament length being 0.8 against the
width of the bending bar. The tests were carried out using an Instron type test-
ing machine under the cross-head speed of 8.3× 10
−7 mm/s. Compression tests
were conducted on rectangular specimens at temperatures up to 1673 K with a
nominal strain rate of 1.0 × 10
−4 s−1. The dimensions of the compression test
specimen were 2 mm× 2m m× 5 mm, and the loading direction was parallel to
the solidiﬁcation direction.

3.2. Fracture toughness and crack propagation behavior
The chevron-notch specimen is useful for measuring the
plane-strain fracture toughness of brittle materials, since a sharp
natural crack is produced at the early stage of loading, so that
no pre-cracking is required [32]. Due to the notch conﬁgura-
tion, however, the chevron-notch specimen is disadvantageous to
observe crack propagation behavior during bending tests. There-
fore in the present study, the chevron-notched specimens are
used for toughness evaluation, and the straight through notched
specimens are used for crack observations. For the Nb–17.5Si
alloys, the annealed alloys are not subjected to the mechani-
cal tests, since the decomposition of the high temperature phase
Nb
3Si has not completed after the annealing at 1773 K for 500 h.
Table 1summarizes the fracture toughness of the alloys deter-
mined by the chevron-notch fracture toughness measurements.
The arc-cast Nb–18.7Si alloy shows lower fracture toughness
than the arc-cast Nb–25Si alloy in spite of its higher volume
fraction of the (Nb) phase. The same tendency has been reported
by Bewlay et al. [4]. These results imply that, in the arc-cast
alloys with the eutectic or hyper-eutectic compositions, toughen-
ing mechanisms other than crack bridging are also contributing
to the work of fracture. The Ti doped ternary alloys generally
exhibit higher fracture toughness than the binary alloys. The
unidirectionally solidiﬁed alloys show higher fracture tough-
ness than the arc-melted counterparts. In addition, the tough-
ness value increases with decreasing the rate of unidirectional
solidiﬁcation. By comparison with the toughness of the unidi-
rectionally solidiﬁed Nb–10Ti–17.5Si alloys before and after
annealing, the annealed alloys obviously show higher fracture

indicates that the toughening from the crack bridging of (Nb) is
larger than that from the crack deﬂection, and the development
of (Nb) particles with higher aspect ratios can provide a better
opportunity for the (Nb) particles to contribute to crack bridging
toughening.
We have demonstrated that the continuity (or high aspect
ratio) of (Nb) particle is one of the important parameters that
determine the toughness of the alloys. However, the unidirec-
tionally solidiﬁed ternary alloys show higher toughness than the
binary alloys, though the ternary alloys show less continuity
(or lower aspect ratios) for (Nb) particles. The reason for the
toughness improvement by the Ti addition is the increase in the
size and ductility of the (Nb) phase. Microstructure observa-
tions clearly depict that the size of (Nb) particles is increased by
the Ti addition. As is indicated in Eq. (1), a larger sized ductile
particle can provide an alloy with higher toughness increment,
wherein the crack bridging mechanism is operative. In addition,
it has been shown that the addition of Ti enhances the ductility
of bcc solid solutions in the Nb–Ti–Cr [11,40–42], Nb–Ti–Al
[43], Nb–Ti [20], and Nb–Ti–Si [17,24] systems. Namely, the
number of (Nb) particles that contribute to the crack bridging
toughening would be comparably smaller in the ternary alloys
than in the binary alloys, but once the (Nb) particles bridged a
crack, larger amount of energy absorption can be expected from
the plastic deformation of the (Nb) particles.

for the full range of test temperatures because of the higher
volume fraction of the (Nb) phase, and the increased disloca-
tion mobility in the (Nb) phase. The unidirectionally solidiﬁed
Nb–10Ti–17.5Si alloys show higher compressive strength than
the arc-melted Nb–10Ti–20Si alloys [27]. Moreover the high
temperature strength is enhanced by lowering the rate of uni-
directional solidiﬁcation. It is concluded that a unidirectional
solidiﬁcation technique, especially at a low solidiﬁcation rate, is
effective in enhancing the strength of the alloys. The evaluation
of creep strength is planed for future work.
4. Conclusions
The (Nb)/Nb
3Si eutectic solidiﬁcation is controlled in the
Nb–Si binary and Nb–Si–Ti ternary alloys by means of unidi-
rectional solidiﬁcation. The slower solidiﬁcation rates yield the
coarser microstructures, as well as the better continuity in the
(Nb) growth toward the solidiﬁcation direction. The decomposi-
tion of Nb
3Si is kinetically much slower in the unidirectionally
solidiﬁed alloys than in the arc-melted alloys; probably because
impurities are removed by the zone reﬁning effect of unidirec-
tional solidiﬁcation. The development of continuous (Nb) rods
provides a better opportunity for the (Nb) phase to contribute
to the crack bridging toughening, and thereby, higher tough-
ness improvement. The development of a (Nb)/Nb
5Si3 lamellar
microstructure further improves the toughness of the alloys.
The high temperature compressive strength is also enhanced
by the application of a unidirectional solidiﬁcation technique to
the present alloys. The fracture toughness of 14.5 MPa m
1/2 at
room temperature, and the compressive strength of 580 MPa at
1673 K are achieved by a suitable microstructure control in a
Nb–17.5at.% Si alloy.
