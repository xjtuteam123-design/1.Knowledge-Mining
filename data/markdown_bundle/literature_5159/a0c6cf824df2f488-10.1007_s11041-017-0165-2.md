EFFECT OF REE ON THE PHASE COMPOSITION AND PROPERTIES
OF A NEW REFRACTORY MAGNESIUM ALLOY
OF THE Mg – Zn – Zr – REE SYSTEM
E. N. Kablov, 1 E. F. Volkova, 1 and E. V . Filonova 1
Translated from Metallovedenie i T ermicheskaya Obrabotka Metallov, No. 7, pp. 19 – 26, July, 2017.
A novel high-strength refractory alloy VMD16 of the Mg – Zn – Zr – REE system is described. The advan -
tages of the alloy with respect to the counterparts are considered. The processes of formation of phase compo-
sition and of structural evolution are studied. A fine dispersed subgrain structure is shown to exist in the de -
formable magnesium alloy. The combination of the specific morphology and topology of the intermetallic
phases with their thermal stability provides a high level of strength properties at room and elevated
(300 – 350°C) temperatures at a low anisotropy.
Key words: deformable alloy of the Mg – Zn – Zr – REE system, primary intermetallic phases,
thermal stability, strength properties at elevated temperatures, fine dispersed subgrain structure,
low anisotropy of mechanical properties.
INTRODUCTION
Deformable magnesium alloys are promising structural
materials applied in aircraft and spacecraft engineering due
to their low density (about 67% of the density of aluminum
alloys), high specific strength, rigidity, damping properties
and good weldability.
Progress in science and engineering demands a higher
level of mechanical properties and a good specific strength
of such alloys at normal and elevated temperatures [1 – 4].
In the recent years the problem of alloying of magnesium
alloys with rare earth elements (REE) has attracted much at -
tention. Introduction of REE into magnesium alloys pro -
duces a favorable effect on their structure and phase compo -
sition, which raises their strength characteristics (at room and
elevated temperatures) and improves their corrosion proper -
ties and the weldability [5 – 9].
In economically developed countries (Japan, Germany,
South Korea, etc.) magnesium alloys with REE and high
operating characteristics are employed in the leading indus -
tries (the aircraft, spacecraft, and defense ones) [10, 11, 14].
Starting with the middle of the twentieth century VIAM
has been a leading domestic center of research and develop-
ment of magnesium alloys [12, 13].
A novel refractory high-strength deformable magnesium
alloy VMD16 (of the Mg – Zn – Zr – REE system) has been
suggested recently. The alloying elements of the alloy belong
to the yttrium subgroup of lanthanides (Y) and the cerium sub-
group (La, Nd) and are contained in it in an amount not exceed-
ing 6 – 8 wt.% in total. The work has been performed within
implementation of scientific direction 8.4 “High-Strength Cor-
rosion-Resistant Weldable Magnesium and Castable Alumi -
num Alloys for New-Generation Articles for Aircraft Engi -
neering” (Strategic Directions of Development of Materials
and Technologies and their Processing until 2030) [1].
The aim of the present work was to study the effect of the
REE on the structure, the phase composition and the strength
and ductility properties of the novel magnesium alloy
VMD16 for subsequent employment of the alloy in units and
parts of the advanced aircraft and spacecraft industries.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
It is known that creation of refractory magnesium-base
alloys is complicated by the fact that magnesium alloys (ex -
cept for some magnesium-lithium ones) have a structure with
hexagonal close-packed (hcp) crystal lattice. The metals with
hcp lattice are less refractory than the metals with cubic lat -
tice at the same melting temperature. It has been shown by
A. A. Bochvar [12] that this is connected in the first turn with
the effect of inequality of the expansion factors in different
directions. Refractory alloy VMD16 has been developed
with the use of elements raising the refractoriness due to the
considerable strength of the interatomic bonding with mag-
nesium atoms (zirconium and the chosen REE), which has
provided formation of a favorable phase composition and
structure.
The study of the critical temperatures for specimens cut
from a forging showed occurrence of two phase transforma-
tions with endothermic effects, i.e., at 442 and 527°C
(Fig. 1). The temperatures of the phase transformations were
substantially higher than the heating temperature under ho-
mogenizing of the ingots and subsequent deformation. This
is confirmed by the fact that the phase composition of the al-
loy remains steady under the heating for homogenization and
heat treatment. A comparative analysis of the results of the
tensile tests of bars from alloy VMD16 and from domestic
and foreign counterpart alloys at elevated temperatures
(350 – 400°C) has shown that the novel alloy possesses
higher properties in the whole of the temperature range stu -
died (Fig. 2).
It should be noted that one of the most refractory foreign
alloys HK31A (USA) of the Mg – Zr – Th system has an ul -
timate strength 20 – 25% lower than VMD16 (Fig. 2).
The decisive role of the total alloying with REE in alloy
VMD16 exhibiting high values of ultimate strength at ele -
vated temperatures also follows from its comparison with
commercial alloy MA14 (Mg – Zn – Zr system). Alloy
MA14 starts to soften noticeably even when the temperature
exceeds 150 – 175°C (Fig. 2) [16]. The domestic high-
strength refractory alloy VMD10 (MA22) of the Mg – Zn –
Zr – REE system is inferior to VMD16 in the ultimate
strength at elevated temperatures (by 7 – 10% at 175 –
275°C) (Fig. 2).
Comparison of the new alloy with counterparts shows its
advantage with respect to all the characteristics studied. An
important circumstance is that the high level of the strength

bodies of grains (Fig. 3 a ). In the process of hot deformation

served in the structure of the deformed alloy (Fig. 3 b and c).

structure. The particles of individually singled out yttrium

lume fraction of the hydrides in the phase composition of the
alloy is very low, but their high microhardness and thermal
stability explain their contribution into the total hardening of

provide additional hardening of the alloy.
Finer methods of investigation with the use of transmis -
sion electron microscopy have made it possible to prove the
presence of a subgrain structure in the deformed magnesium
alloy (Fig. 6). This structure is represented by large-volume
polygonized regions with subgrains of a maximum size not
exceeding 500 nm. Subboundaries of these fine grains with
an orientation close to the basal planes of the crystal lattice
of magnesium (0001) are well discernible in the specimens
of a forging (in the transverse direction). Thus, the process of
plastic deformation may involve additional sliding planes.
The presence of a polygonized subgrain structure in the
alloy explains largely the fact of the good adaptability of the
alloy to manufacture in addition to the elevated strength
properties and the good ductility margin (specifically, the
elongation). It should be noted that the detected polygonized
subgrain structure is a distinguishing natural feature of alloy
VMD16.
Analyzing the results of the study of the effect of REE on
the structure and phase composition of alloy VMD16 we es-
tablished the following:
- the alloy possesses a thermally stable phase composi -
tion, which is confirmed by the temperatures of the phase
transformations, i.e., 442 and 527°C; this provides the pres -
ence of primary phases in the structure after high-tempera -
ture homogenizing and hot deformation;
– a feature of the phase composition is formation of
large fragments of a (Mg, Zn) 5REE eutectic component,
which seem to hinder active occurrence of diffusion pro -
cesses in the alloy and may block the motion of dislocations
at elevated temperatures;
– the formation of nanosize round particles of
intermetallics of type Zn 2(Zr, REE)3 including La, Nd and,
partially, Y in addition to the fine-grained (8 – 12 /c109m) and
equiaxed structure affects favorably the strength of the alloy
and its adaptability to manufacture, promotes leveling of the
properties over the volume of the metal, and is responsible
among other factors for the low anisotropy of the strength
properties;
– the presence of fine dispersed particles of yttrium and
zirconium hydrides formed by the REE elements due to in -
teraction with the impurity hydrogen and arranged uniformly
enough in the form of a fine net over the boundaries of
subgrains and partially in the bulk of grains produces an ad -
ditional strengthening effect;

CONCLUSIONS
The novel refractory and high-strength deformable alloy
VMD16 based on the Mg – Zn – Zr – REE system is supe -
rior to the domestic and foreign counterpart alloys with re -
spect to the characteristics of strength, endurance and refrac-
toriness and exhibits low anisotropy of the strength proper -
ties.
Using the obtained data on the effect of the REE on the
structure and phase composition of the new alloy we have
determined the special features of the morphology and topol-
ogy of the main intermetallic phases possessing a high tem -
perature of phase transformations. We have managed the to
detect the presence of a polygonized structure with subgrains
close in size to nanosize formations. Taken together, these
factors provide an elevated level of refractoriness and
strength and a good adaptability of the alloy to manufacture.
