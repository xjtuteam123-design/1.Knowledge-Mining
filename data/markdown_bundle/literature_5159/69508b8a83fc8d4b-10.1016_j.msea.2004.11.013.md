Abstract
The out-of-phase thermo-mechanical fatigue (OP TMF) of two Ni-based single-crystal (SC) superalloys, TMS-75 and TMS-82+, has been
investigated. Their different compositions speciﬁcally inﬂuence TMF properties. TMS-75 showed a longer rupture life than TMS-82+ at t*
(hold time) = 0, while TMS-82+ showed a longer life than TMS-75 at different hold times. The different TMF behaviors were analyzed and
were attributed, in TMS-82+, to the higher tensile strength at 400◦C and higher stress relaxation resistance at 900◦C. Some differences were
observed between the two alloys in slip band and the/H9253//H9253′structure. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) studies on the dislocations and
stacking faults (SFs) conﬁrmed the previous TEM results concerning the generation mechanisms of dislocations and SFs. The current work
indicates that TMS-82+ is appropriate for use in gas turbine engines under speciﬁc conditions.

1. Introduction
/H9253′precipitation-hardening Ni-based single-crystal super-
alloys, which have superior mechanical properties at elevated
temperatures, have been used as gas turbine blades and vans
for power plants and aeroengines. For example, TMS-82+
and TMS-75 were developed at the National Institute for Ma-
terials Science as modiﬁed second- and third-generation SC
superalloys [1–4]. TMS-82+ has high phase stability and ex-
cellent high-temperature creep strength [1]. Blades made of
these two superalloys have been tested recently by Toshiba
Ltd., in Japan. However, many issues remain unresolved with
respect to the application of high-temperature materials. One
of the critical issues is the thermal-mechanical fatigue (TMF)
[2–9]. The stresses due to TMF may arise from temperature
gradients in the airfoils during startup and shutdown opera-
tions or from gradients within the cooled airfoils. Efforts have
been made to improve creep and TMF properties of the SC

electron microscopy [2,5,7].
Under some speciﬁc working conditions, high-
temperature materials are subjected to OP TMF. For
example, gas turbine engines startup and shut down once
a day to adjust the consumption of electricity. During the
hold period, blades and vans are subject to compressive
stress. Therefore, it is important to know the behavior of the
superalloy during the hold time and the effect on the lifetime.
The objective of this work is to investigate in detail the
effects of composition and hold time on the TMF behaviors
of two SC superalloys, TMS-75 and TMS-82+.

4. Discussion
Thermo-mechanical fatigue of superalloys is a complex
process as many factors affect the fatigue results. As dis-
cussed below, analysis of some factors, such as the/H9253
′volume
fraction, the compositions of the /H9253and /H9253′phases, the yield
stress, the stress relaxation, and the shearing mechanism of
the /H9253
′precipitates, would be helpful in order to understand
the TMF and to design new superalloys.
/H9253′volume fraction is a key parameter in high-temperature
creep and TMF [3]. However, in the present study, TMS-
75 and TMS-82+ both have about 60 vol.% of /H9253′ precipi-
tates. Hence, the /H9253′ volume fraction is not a dominant pa-
rameter in the different TMF behaviors. Rather, the effect
of composition plays a more important role in the differ-
ent behaviors of the two superalloys. The previous results
indicated that different /H9253
′volume fractions may have differ-
ent stress relaxation behaviors [3], which lead to different
TMF rupture lives. In the present work, it has been demon-
strated that superalloys with the same /H9253
′volume fraction but
with different compositions also have different stress relax-
ation behaviors. It may be concluded that a high strain stress
relaxation resistance is a key to achieving good OP TMF
properties.
To clarify the effect of composition on TMF behavior, the
compositions of the /H9253and /H9253
′phases in TMS-75 were mea-
sured by an electron probe X-ray micro-analyzer (EPMA), as
shown in Fig. 10. Most elements prefer to partition into the/H9253
phase because Ni
3Al is a stable intermetallic compound with
low energy. A similar phenomenon has been found in other
Ni-based superalloys [14,15]. It is noted that the partitioning
ratio of Re (i.e., weight percent of Re in the /H9253phase over

present work suggests that high strength at a low temperature
in the tension stage of an OP TMF is effective for achieving
high TMF performance.
Gas turbine engines used for adjusting urban electric-
ity consumption are subjected to speciﬁc conditions, e.g.,
the engines experience startup and shutdown operations
once a day. TMS-82+ was developed as a modiﬁed second-
generation SC superalloy. It is cost competitive with high
phase stability, excellent high-temperature creep strength,
and good TMF properties under hold time conditions. Thus,
TMS-82+ is a promising substitute for commercial second-
and third-generation SC superalloys used in gas turbine
engines.

5. Summary and conclusions
The following summary and conclusions were obtained
from the studies on the thermal–mechanical fatigue of two
single-crystal Ni-based superalloys: (1) Although the TMF
property of TMS-82+ was poor without a hold time, it was
better than that of TMS-75 for TMF with a hold time.
(2) TMS-82+ showed higher tensile strength at 400
◦C and
higher stress relaxation resistance at 900 ◦C than TMS-75,
which led to a smaller plastic deformation in tension and
a longer rupture life in TMS-82+. (3) A relatively smaller
number of slip bands were observed in TMS-82+ than in
TMS-75 under identical conditions, and a light rafted /H9253//H9253
′
structure was observed in TMS-82+ for TMF with a hold
time. (4) The same deformation mechanism occurred in the
two superalloys. The previous results concerning the mech-
anisms of the formation of matrix dislocations and of shear-
ing the /H9253
′ precipitate by a single-matrix dislocation were
conﬁrmed.
