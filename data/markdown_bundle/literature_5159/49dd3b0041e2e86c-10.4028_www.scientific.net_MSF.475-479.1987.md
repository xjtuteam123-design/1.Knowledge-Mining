Keywords: martensitic transformation, B2, B19, PtTi 
Abstract. We have suggested B2-(Pt, Ir)Ti as high temperature shape me mory alloys. The phase 
transformation of (Pt, Ir)-50at% Ti from B2 to B19(2H) or 4H(4O) str uctures was investigated in 
our previous study. The microstructure suggested martensitic trans formation. In this study, thermal 
expansion measurement and cyclic compression test were performed for (Pt, Ir)Ti to investigate if 
the shape memory effect appears. High temperature strength wa s also investigated because phase 
transformation temperature of the (Pt, Ir)Ti is above 1273 K and high s trength is necessary as high 
temperature shape memory alloys in order to suppress dislocation motion and stabilize martenstic 
transformation. The potential of (Pt, Ir)Ti as high temperature shap e memory alloys will be also 
discussed. 
Introduction 
To develop high temperature shape memory alloys, a lot of efforts have been performed. For 
example, a systematic study of the effect of refractory e lements such as Nb, Hf, or Zr to NiTi 
indicates that the martensitic transformation temperature (Ms) , which is related to the shape 
memory effect, increased up to about 500 K from 400 K in NiTi [1-4]. It is also shown that 
platinum group metals such as Pt and Pd are effective element to increase the Ms of NiTi. The B2 
phase forms a continuous solid solution from the NiTi side to the PdTi or the PtTi sides at high 
temperature and the Ms of the PdTi and PtTi are 873 and 1303 K, respectively [5-7]. 
According to the phase diagrams, some of platinum group metals (PGM) base intermetallic 
compounds are B2 structure at high temperature and phase transformation occurs above 1273 K [8]. 
Then, we noted PGM base intermetallic compounds as high temperature s hape memory alloys. It is 
indicated that PtTi occurs phase transformation from the B2 to B19 phas es and showed typical 
martensitic microstructure, such as the lath or twining structures [7, 9]. This suggests that PtTi has a 
potential to be high temperature shape memory alloys. Other intere sting compound is IrTi which 
has the phase transformation from the B2 phase to the monoclinic phase [10]. The phase 
transformation temperature increases with decreasing Ti conc entration from room temperature to 
1923 K [8]. This suggests that combination of PtTi and IrTi may incre ase phase transformation 
temperature above 1273 K. In the previous study, the effect of Ir on phase transformation of PtTi 
was investigated [11]. It was found that the B19 phase was stable at room temperature in t he 
Pt-Ir-Ti alloys up to 37.5at% Ir. In the Pt-37.5Ir-Ti, the 4H(4O ) structure was also found in addition 
to B19 (2H) phase [12]. Although the phase at high temperature was not identified, phase 
transformation was found in DTA curves. The phase transformation te mperature increased with 
increasing Ir contents to 1473 K at 37.5at% Ir. However, it is not cl ear yet if this phase 
transformation is really martensitic transformation.

was performed to investigate if the martensitic transformati on occurs. Then, compression test at

Experimental procedure 
The alloys with nominal compositions, Pt-50Ti, Pt-12.5Ir-50Ti, Pt-25Ir -50Ti, and 
Pt-37.5Ir-50Ti (at%) were investigated. 10 g button ingots of each composi tion were prepared by 
the arc melting method using high-purity starting elements. Sa mples with the following size were 
cut from the ingots. The sample sizes for surface observation, m echanical test, and measurement of 
thermal expansion were 3mm in diameter and 1 mm in thickness, 3 mm x 3 mm x 6 mm, and 3 mm 
x 3 mm x 8 mm in size, respectively. They were sealed together in a vacuum with small amount of 
Ar gas in silica tubes. They were heated at 1523 K, which was highe r than the transformation 
temperature of all tested alloys for 24 hours in the furnace, fol lowing by ice water quenching. 
Surface observation was performed during heating with a heating rate of 10 K/ min under a high 
purity Ar atmosphere using a gold image furnace attached to a Co nfocal Scanning Laser 
Microscope (Lasertec corp.). Cyclic compression test was carr ied out at room temperature with the 
initial strain rate of 3 x 10 -4 /s using Shimadzu AG-I. General compression test was also perf ormed 
at 1273 K below phase transformation temperature with the initial st rain rate of 3 x 10 -4 /s. Thermal 
expansion behavior was observed for non-deformed and deformed samples wit h heating rate of 10

reorientation of martensite variants need higher stress for high Ir containing compounds.

Conclusions 
1. Mechanical properties of (Pt, Ir)-50Ti was investigated. 
2. Surface relief and stress-strain curves of (Pt, Ir)-50Ti sugges t that the phase transformation is 
martensitic transformation. 
3. Strength at 1273 K increased with increasing Ir contents from 160 to 750 MPa. This suggests 
that Ir is effective to strengthen PtTi. 
4. Thermal expansion measurement showed shape recovery of (Pt, Ir)-50Ti. 
5. The highest shape recovery ratio is shown in Pt-25Ir-50Ti. This indi cates that Pt-25Ir-50Ti is 
promising materials for a high temperature shape memory alloy.
