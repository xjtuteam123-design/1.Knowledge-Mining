Phase Decomposition and Elevated Temperature
Mechanical Properties of Zr–Ti–Nb–Ta–Sn Complex
Concentrated Alloy Annealed at Intermediate
Temperatures
YONGKANG ZHOU, SHUAI ZENG, YUHUI ZHU, BOWEN ZHAO, HUAN LI,
HONGWEI ZHANG, HAIFENG ZHANG, and ZHENGWANG ZHU
Refractory complex concentrated alloys (RCCAs) have attracted much attention due to their
unique design concept and mechanical properties at elevated temperatures. The researchers
found that the RCCAs are thermally metastable and could decompose in certain temperature
ranges. At present, the phase stability of these alloy systems is not completely clear. In this
work, the phase stability of single-phase body-centered cubic (BCC) Zr
46Ti13Nb26Ta10.5Sn4.5
alloy annealing between 600 /C176 C and 1000 /C176 C was evaluated, and the eﬀects of phase
decomposition on high-temperature mechanical properties were studied. Annealing at
1000 /C176 C, it is unstable and forms a second-phase precipitate with BCC structure (termed as
BCC2). After annealing at 600 /C176 C to 800 /C176 C, a new Zr–Sn-rich hexagonal-closed-packed (HCP)
phase was formed. At high temperatures (1000 /C176 C to 1200 /C176 C), the ‘‘ﬁshbone’’-shaped GB
precipitates can hinder the sliding of GBs and the intragranular precipitates hinder dislocation
movement causing a signiﬁcant increase in strength. These results indicate that the high-tem-
perature mechanical properties of RCCAs can be improved by optimizing the microstructure by
tuning the phase stability, which sheds light on designing new RCCAs with high performance.

I. INTRODUCTION
REFRACTORY alloys, including refractory com-
plex concentrated alloys (RCCAs) and refractory high
entropy alloys (RHEAs), have attracted widespread
attention as promising materials for elevated tempera-
ture applications.
[1–4] RCCAs are special refractory
alloys composed of three or more principal elements
(such as Cr, Hf, Mo, Nb, Ta, Ti, V, W, and Zr).
Additionally, the compositions can be modiﬁed via the
addition of other elements to reduce their density as well
as improve their properties. The high-temperature
properties of these alloys are comparable to that of
current Ni-based superalloys. For example, the
Ta
25Nb25W25Mo25 and Ta 20Nb20W20Mo20V20 alloys
were reported to have the strength of 405 MPa and
477 MPa at 1600 /C176 C.
[1,5] Based on refractory elements
and the design concept of RCCA, a large number of
unexplored phase spaces exist in complex concentrated
alloy systems.
[6,7] However, two key problems limiting
the practical applications need to be addressed, one is to
reduce the density of RCCAs through composition
selection, and the other is to design the ﬁne microstruc-
ture based on the idea of the ﬁne-scale c/c¢ micro+struc-
ture of Ni-based superalloys.
In the past few years, many eﬀorts have been made to
overcome the aforementioned issues. New refractory
alloys including TiZrHfNb,
[8] TiZrHfTa,[9,10] TiZrH-
fAl,[11] TiZrNbHfAl,[12] TiZrNbAl,[13] NbTiVAlHf-
TaW,[4] TiCrVAl,[14] and Al 0.5NbTa0.8Ti1.5V0.2Zr[15,16]
have been developed to show the relatively low density.
Additionally, as a typical equiatomic alloy, HfNbTa-
TiZr alloy is generally considered to be a single-phase
body-centered-cubic (BCC) solid solution in the as-cast
state as well as after heating treatment above ~ 1000 /C176

investigations on phase decomposition at intermediate
temperatures, but the inﬂuence of phase decomposition

temperatures, is rarely reported.
The present study is focused on the phase stability and
microstructural evolution in a newly developed
Zr

Zr–Ti–Nb–Ta system, a minor addition of Tin (Sn)

temperatures ranging from 600 /C176 C to 1000 /C176 C were
analyzed and the phase decomposition was discussed,
meanwhile, the eﬀect of phase decomposition on
high-temperature performance was also analyzed.
II. EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURES
The Zr
46Ti13Nb26Ta10.5Sn4.5 (at pct) alloy was pro-
duced by vacuum arc melting using high-purity metals
(purity over 99.9 wt pct) under a Ti-gettered argon
atmosphere. To ensure the chemical homogeneity of the
ingot, Zr–Ti–Sn and Nb–Ta intermediate alloys are
prepared ﬁrst, respectively. The intermediate alloy is
then melted into button ingots, which are remelted at
least eight times to ensure chemical homogeneity.
Rodlike samples with a diameter of ~ 10 mm and a
length of ~ 60 mm were produced using the copper
mold pouring technique in a high-purity argon atmo-
sphere. The cylindrical bar specimens were sealed in
vacuum quartz tubes and homogenized at 1400 /C176 C for
6 h, followed by water quenching. Subsequently, the
as-homogenized alloy was annealed at 600 /C176 C to 1000
/C176 C for 24 h and 600 /C176 C for 120 h, respectively. All
annealing treatments that were performed on the
as-homogenized alloy were followed by water quenching
to retain the phases formed at the annealing
temperatures.
Cylindrical specimens with a size of 8 mm in diameter
and 12 mm in height were cut from the as-cast and
annealed alloys for compression tests using an electric
discharge wire-cutting machine. And the uniaxial com-
pression tests were performed on the Gleeble 3800
thermo-simulation machine. Compression tests were
performed at a constant strain rate of 0.001 s
/C0 1 at
1000 /C176 C,1100 /C176 C, and 1200 /C176 C, with a true strain of 0.69
(50 pct reduction in the sample’s height). The samples
were then heated to the test temperature at a constant
heating rate of 10 /C176 C/s and held for 120 s to ensure
uniformity of temperature. At the end of the experi-
ments, the samples were immediately quenched to
ensure the high-temperature structure. To prevent oxi-
dation of the samples, all experiments were carried out
in a chamber ﬁlled with pressurized argon to minimize
sample oxidation.
Phase constituents of Zr
46Ti13Nb26Ta10.5Sn4.5 alloy
were examined by X-ray diﬀraction (XRD) with Cu-K a
radiation. The microstructure was observed utilizing a
scanning electron microscopy (SEM) operated in a
back-scattered electron (BSE) mode and a transmission
electron microscopy (TEM) with energy-dispersive
X-ray spectroscopy (EDS). An image-side Cs-corrected
JEOL 2100 F was further used for scanning-TEM
(STEM) imaging.

IV. DISCUSSIONS
A. Phase Stability
In the as-cast condition, there is an element distribu-
tion in the dendritic and interdendritic regions
(Figure 0.1 c), on the one hand, the presence of Ta
increases the solidiﬁcation gap and is a typical charac-
teristic of many RCCAs.
[6,25] On the other hand, due to
the high mixing enthalpy between Zr-X (X: Ti, Nb, and
Ta) and the diﬀerence in atomic size between Zr and X,
Zr tends to remain in the residual liquid during
solidiﬁcation and is enriched in the dendrite.
Accordingly, the two BCC phases determined by XRD
and TEM-EDS (Figure 6(a)) may represent Nb–Ta-rich
and Zr–Ti–rich regions, and the HCP phase may
represent the Zr–Sn-rich phase. Although most of the
reports have shown that a large number of RHEAs have
a single BCC structure after homogenization at high
temperatures, the RHEAs with BCC structure exhibit
phase decomposition during intermediate temperatures
annealing. According to the results, it can be determined
that the alloy is a disordered BCC single-phase structure
at 1400 /C176 C. In the current work, phase decomposition
was observed between 600 /C176 C and 1000 /C176 C and only two
BCC phases existed at 1000 /C176 C, while the HCP phase
disappeared in the XRD diﬀraction pattern, which may
be attributed to the Zr has changed from a low-tem-
perature stabilized HCP structure to high-temperature
BCC structure at 1000 /C176 C. It is known that Nb and Ta
elements have stable BCC structures over a wide
temperature range, while Ti and Zr elements exhibit
HCP structures at low temperatures and BCC structures
at high temperatures.
[27] In alloys containing Ta and Zr,
BCC phase separation is typical due to the inherent
repulsion between these elements over a range of
temperatures and compositions. The Ta-rich precipi-
tated phase and Zr-rich precipitated phase were
observed between 600 /C176 C and 1000 /C176 C can indicate that
the single BCC phase after solid solution treatment is
metastable. Omega phase is commonly observed in
Zr-rich metastable BCC alloys.
[28,29]

B. High-Temperature Mechanical Properties
At present, the eﬀect of precipitation and phase
decomposition on mechanical properties of RCCAs is
mainly concentrated at room temperature,
[43,44] and the
eﬀects at high temperatures are equally important and
need to be explored. The continuous softening behavior
during high-temperature deformation is generally con-
sidered to be a typical characteristic of dynamic recrys-
tallization (DRX).
[45] However, compared with as-cast
alloy, the ﬁne precipitates ( ~ 478 nm) in the grain can
hinder the dislocation movement to achieve the
strengthening eﬀect. Interestingly, the ‘‘ﬁsh-
bone’’-shaped GBs (Figure 5(c)) can inhibit the sliding
of GBs, which is also an important factor for the
increase in strength. In RCCAs, the decrease in solid
solution strengthening eﬀect can be compensated by an
increase in the high-temperature strength due to the
nucleation and growth of precipitated particles.
[46]

V. CONCLUSIONS
A novel low-density single-phase BCC Zr
46Ti13Nb26-
Ta10.5Sn4.5 refractory complex concentrated alloy was
prepared by vacuum arc melting and homogenization,
as the original state to investigate the decomposition of
the phase stability. The microstructure evolution of the
Zr
46Ti13Nb26Ta10.5Sn4.5 alloy after annealing at 800 /C176 C
and 1000 /C176 C for 24 h or 600 /C176 C for 24 h, and 120 h is
investigated. The high-temperature properties after
annealing at 600 /C176 C for 24 h (H600-24) and 120 h
(H600-120) were studied, and the strengthening mech-
anism was analyzed. The obtained results can be
summarized as follows.
(1) After homogenization at 1400 /C176 C for 6 h, the
alloy keeps a single BCC phase, and the
microstructure changes from dendrites to coarse
equiaxed crystals. According to our results of
annealing at 1000 /C176 C for 24 h, the BCC solid
solution is unstable at 1000 /C176 C. The matrix BCC
phase decomposes into a BCC1 matrix and
Nb–Ta-rich BCC2 phase, which forms a new
Zr–Sn-rich HCP phase at 800 /C176 C.
(2) A more complex microstructure was formed after
annealing at 600 /C176 C: isothermal for 24 h, contin-
uous precipitation occurs at the grain boundary,
especially the precipitated phase at the triangular
grain boundary is lamellar, and the scattered
spherical precipitates are within the grain. The
phase composition is BCC1 + BCC2 + slightly
HCP phases; isothermal for 120 h, the densely
distributed intragranular precipitations replace
the matrix, and the lamellae precipitated phases
at the grain boundary have developed into a
‘‘fishbone’’ shape. The phase composition is
slightly BCC1 + BCC2 + HCP phases.
(3) The strength of the as-cast alloy at 1000 /C176 C,
1100 /C176 C, and 1200 /C176 C is 135 MPa, 89 MPa, and
50 MPa, respectively. During high-temperature
deformation, the slip of the grain boundary leads
to softening behavior. The ‘‘fishbone’’-shaped GB
precipitation hinder the grain boundary sliding,
which is the main factor for the increase of
high-temperature strength.
