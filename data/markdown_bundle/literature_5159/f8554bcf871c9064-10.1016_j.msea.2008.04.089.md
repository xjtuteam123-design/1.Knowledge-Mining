abstract
A constitutive model for the mechanical behaviour of single-crystalline superalloys at high temperatures
has been developed. The model relies on the slip system theory and is able to predict rafting and its
inﬂuence on plastic ﬂow. The kinetics of rafting are assumed to be driven by the reduction of the inter-
nal stresses represented by the macroscopic back-stress. The rafting effect is incorporated in the model
through the dependence of the Orowan stress on the channel width. The model has been validated for
the alloy CMSX-4 at 950
◦C. The rafting part of the model has been calibrated by measurements of the
channel widths after several levels of creep strains and for several loads.

1. Introduction
Rafting, i.e., directional coarsening of the /H9253′ precipitates usu-
ally occurs in Ni-base superalloys under high-temperature creep
loading and has often been observed in turbine blades after ser-
vice [1]. This instability of the microstructure can also lead to a
signiﬁcant degradation of the monotonic and the cyclic strength of
these alloys [2]. Hence, modelling tools to predict the occurrence
of rafting in blades under service condition are highly desirable.
Micromechanical models of the /H9253//H9253
′ microstructure advanced in
the last decade (see, e.g. [3–6]) show that rafting is mainly due to
the anisotropy of the dislocation structure generated in the differ-
ently oriented /H9253//H9253
′interfaces. Indeed, the dissimilar superposition
of external stresses and misﬁt stresses in each channel type leads to
different dislocation densities in the three types of interfaces. This
is also demonstrated by calculations of the elastic energy stored in
the microstructure with a given creep strain[7]. It follows that the
driving force for rafting is related to the decrease of the internal
stresses in the microstructure or to their macroscopic counterpart,
the back-stresses.
Since the early 1990s, a large number of viscoplastic phe-
nomenological models for single-crystalline superalloys have been
derived from the slip system theory. These models can describe

the anisotropy of plastic ﬂow at high temperature (see, e.g.[8] and
[9]). However, they are not able to explicitly represent the cou-
pling between plastic ﬂow and rafting during long-term loading at
high temperature. On the other hand, quite detailed microstruc-
tural models of creep deformation including rafting have been
developed (see, e.g. [10]), which are able to fairly well cap-
ture the intricate creep behaviour in the range of temperatures
between 750 and 1100
◦C. However, these models have not been
applied to more general loading types (cyclic loading, stress relax-
ation) and are usually too complex to be amenable to structural
calculations.
A viscoplastic constitutive model for single-crystalline super-
alloys has been designed to predict rafting kinetics without
resorting to a computationally intensive microstructural analysis.
The mechanical part of the model has already been calibrated for
the superalloy SRR99 [7] and shown to describe the anisotropy of
the mechanical response of this alloy at high temperature. This
paper focuses on the extension of the model to rafting and the cal-
ibration of the rafting part with measurements of the widening of
the channels presented in a companion paper[11].
Speciﬁcally, the situation considered in this work corresponds
to the coalescence of cubic precipitates leading to plates per-
pendicular to one of the crystal axes, as usually observed under
uniaxial tension. The case of different morphological transforma-
tions, like that of sets of plates parallel to the load axis that
appear under uniaxial compression, is beyond the scope of the
paper.

2.3. Representation of the morphological changes during uniaxial
creep
To develop a model of the evolution of the /H9253′morphology it is
necessary to separate two phenomena:
- Homothetic growth of single /H9253′-precipitates, also called isotropic
coarsening.
- Coalescence of adjacent cubes, also called rafting.
Isotropic coarsening, which is simply a change of the scale, does
not alter the state of internal stresses in the structure. Hence, the
driving force for isotropic coarsening is essentially the reduction
of the overall interfacial energy. On the contrary, rafting induces a
signiﬁcant relaxation of the internal stresses[7].
Since the kinetics of both processes differs, two parameters are
necessary to characterize any current state of the /H9253

dependent. At the lower stress, a shift between the drop of the
Orowan stress and the onset of creep acceleration can be observed.
Creep acceleration after completed rafting might be due to addi-
tional recovery mechanisms such as glide or climb of dislocations
through the /H9253

at higher temperature. Indeed, creep acceleration subsequent to
rafting could be described in Ref. [10] by explicitly modelling cut-
ting of the /H9253

width still continuously increases after completed rafting due to
isotropic coarsening. As a consequence, the Orowan stress con-
tinues to decrease and the local shear stress /FS

theoretically sound, but the measurements in [11] or Fig. 2 show
that channel widening after rafting completion becomes relatively
