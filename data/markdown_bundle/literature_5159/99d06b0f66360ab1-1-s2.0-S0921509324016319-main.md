ABSTRACT
Herein, the high-temperature tensile deformation behavior in the range of 500 – 750 
◦
C for laser powder bed 
fusion (L-PBF) Ti-6.5Al-2Zr-1Mo-1V (TA15) alloy after annealing treatment has been investigated in detail. The 
experimental results show that the ultimate tensile strength (UTS) and yield strength (YS) of the sample decrease 
gradually with the increase of test temperature, while the elongation (EL) of the sample increases gradually. The 
reduction in strength can be attributed to the decrease in dislocation density. When the tensile temperature is 
below 550 
◦
C, a pronounced work-hardening phenomenon can be observed. At temperatures above 650 
◦
C, 
significant dynamic recovery and dynamic recrystallization effects lead to significant softening. When the tensile 
temperature is below 600 
◦
C, the fracture mode is transgranular fracture, while when the temperature is 650 
◦
C, 
the fracture mode changes to intergranular fracture. After annealing, L-PBF TA15 samples exhibit excellent 
mechanical properties at medium to high temperatures, which provides an important reference value for the 
application of L-PBF fabricated titanium alloy components in the aerospace industries.
1. Introduction
Titanium alloys are an indispensable material for key components in 
aerospace hot end applications due to their excellent strength-to-weight 
ratio, excellent weldability and excellent heat resistance [ 1 , 2 ]. Among 
all types of titanium alloys, near- α titanium alloys, such as Ti60 [ 3 ], 
IMI829 [ 4 ] and Ti-1100 [ 5 ] are widely applied in high-temperature 
environments, such as aerospace engine components, owing to their 
superior high-temperature mechanical properties and processability. 
Ti-6.5Al – 2Zr – 1Mo – 1V (TA15) alloy, a high-temperature titanium alloy, 
possesses a high content of α stabilizing elements, which endows it with 
excellent thermal stability, enabling its prolonged use at 500 
◦
C [ 6 ]. The 
mechanical properties at room and high temperature of TA15 alloys 
manufactured by traditional techniques such as forging and powder 
metallurgy have been extensively studied. For instance, Li et al. [ 7 ] 
found the microstructure, mechanical properties and fracture modes of 
powder metallurgy TA15 alloy within the temperature range of 
500 – 650 
◦
C, revealing the highest tensile strength and ductility at 
500 
◦
C, and a transition from intergranular to trans-granular fracture at 
600 
◦
C. Liu et al. [ 8 ] observed that, during tensile testing at 750 
◦
C, the 
flow stress of TA15 alloy sheets peaked at 10 % true strain, while the 
dislocation density continued to increase.
However, traditional manufacturing methods still confront 
numerous challenges when fabricating intricately shaped titanium alloy 
structural components, such as low material utilization and prolonged 
production cycles. The emergence of additive manufacturing technology 
offers an effective solution to solve the above challenges [ 9 , 10 ], since 
this technology enables near-net shaping of high-performance complex

3. Results
3.1. Microstructure of as-built and annealed TA15 alloys
Fig. 3 illustrates the XRD patterns of both as-built and annealed 
samples, both of which exhibited diffraction peaks characteristic of the 
hexagonal close-packed (HCP) structure. The HCP phases in the as-built 
and annealed samples can be identified as the α ʹ phase and the α phase, 
respectively. Extensive studies have found that the diffraction peak 
positions of two phases are extremely close, which is attributed to their 
similar lattice parameters [ 21 , 24 ]. The diffraction peak characteristics 
of β phase are also identified in samples after annealing. The full width at 
half maximum (FWHM) values for both α ʹ and α phases are presented in 
Table 1 . It can be seen from Table 1 that the α ʹ phase shows a shift to -
wards bigger angles compared to the α phase. In addition, as indicated 
by Table 1 , the FWHM of the as-built sample is greater than that of the 
annealed sample. Since the solute elements are not able to diffuse suf -
ficiently due to the rapid cooling rate during the processing, the α phase 
(Al) and β phase (Mo and V) stabilizing elements are extensively 
distributed within the α ʹ martensitic, forming a supersaturated solid 
solution with significant lattice distortion. Consequently, the diffraction 
peaks of the as-built TA15 samples are broader compared to those of the 
annealed sample. Besides, according to the Bragg equation, a decrease in 
lattice constant results in a shift of diffraction peaks towards higher 
angles [ 25 , 26 ]. Because the atomic radius of Al (0.143 nm) is larger than 
those of V and Mo (0.132 nm and 0.136 nm, respectively), the lattice 
constant decreases, causing the diffraction peaks to shift towards higher 
angles [ 27 ]. Therefore, the analysis of XRD diffraction peak angles and 
FWHM confirms that the as-built TA15 alloy consists of α ʹ phase, which 
can be decomposed into α + β after annealing treatment.

4. Discussion
4.1. DRV and DRX behaviors
As shown in Fig. 5 , there are two distinct types of tensile curves: one 
is that the flow stress decreases rapidly after reaching the peak, and the 
other is a slow decline. This can be attributed to work hardening and 
softening [ 44 ]. There are two types of softening mechanisms: DRV and 
DRX. During the tensile testing, the increase and accumulation of dis -
locations result in a rise in stress with the progression of strain. At 500 
◦
C 
and 550 
◦
C, the flow stress rapidly decreases after reaching peak. In the 
initial stage of tensile, the DRV process progresses slowly. The annihi -
lation rate of dislocations is lower than their multiplication rate, causing 
the continuous rise of dislocation density, which is reflected as a gradual 
increase in flow stress on the tensile curve. After the flow stress reaches 
its peak, dislocation entanglement leads to stress concentration, result -
ing in the initiation of cracks and the occurrence of heterogeneous 
deformation, which subsequently causes rapid fracture of the sample. 
With the increase of temperature (650 – 750 
◦
C), as the dislocation den -
sity reaches a critical value, the DRX eliminates dislocations through the 
transformation of high-angle grain boundaries, which results in 
softening. It was also noted that YS and UTS fell faster in the range of 
650 – 750 
◦
C, as shown in Fig. 6 . This can be attributed to the major 
softening mechanism shift from DRV to DRX.
4.2. High-temperature softening behavior
It is well established that the peak stress depends on various factors, 
such as strain rate, testing temperature, and structural parameters [ 45 ].

5. Conclusions
In this study, the softening mechanism and deformation behavior of 
annealed L-PBF TA15 alloy at high temperature were investigated. The 
primary research focus encompassed the analysis of GNDs, the activa -
tion of slip systems, fracture modes and the strength of grain boundaries. 
The main conclusions are as follows: 
(1) At 500 
◦
C, the TA15 alloy processed by L-PBF demonstrates 
exceptional high-temperature tensile properties, with a tensile 
strength of 686 ± 12 MPa and an elongation of 18.5 ± 2.1 %. 
However, as the testing temperature increases, its work- 
hardening capacity and yield strength progressively decrease, 
while the elongation rate gradually increases.
(2) The strength of the grain boundary decreases as the tensile tem -
perature increases. At temperatures below 600 
◦
C, the fracture 
pattern is transgranular, while at temperatures above 650 
◦
C, the 
fracture pattern changes to intergranular fracture.
(3) The sample exhibits a pronounced softening phenomenon at 
600 
◦
C. During the initial stage of tensile deformation, work 
hardening due to dislocation density remains the predominant 
mechanism. As the tensile temperature increases, the DRX effect 
intensifies, and the softening process becomes increasingly 
evident.
