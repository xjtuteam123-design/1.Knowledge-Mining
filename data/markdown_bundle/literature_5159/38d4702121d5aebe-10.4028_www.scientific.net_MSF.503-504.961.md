Effect of Severe Plastic Deformation on the Structure and Properties of 
the Age-Hardenable Mg-Sm Alloys

Keywords: Severe plastic deformation, Mg-Sm alloys, aging, submicrocrystalline structure, 
strengthening, specific electrical resistance. 
Abstract. In this work severe plastic deformation (SPD) was applied to magnesium base alloys of the 
Mg-Sm system (2.8-5.5 mass %Sm). These alloys are characteriz ed by high strength at elevated 
temperatures and high strengthening effect during aging. SPD wa s performed by torsion under 
pressure of 4 GPa at 20 and 200 °C to ε ∼ 6. SPD results in significant strengthening of the Mg-Sm 
alloys due to the formation of submicrocrystalline structure. In al l cases SPD accelerates the solid 
solution decomposition upon subsequent aging. The highest strengthening can b e obtained if the 
solution treated alloy is aged at 200 °C after SPD at room temperature. The state of high strength can 
be also reached if the following sequence of the operations is used: solution treatment + aging at 200 
°C up to maximum hardness + SPD at 20 °C + aging at 200 °C accompanied by Sm –rich phase 
precipitation in the submicrocrystalline matrix. 
Introduction 
Severe plastic deformation (SPD) is an effective method for strengthening of metals and alloys[1,2]. 
This method favorably affects many metallic materials of var ious compositions and structure, 
including Mg alloys [3-9]. Magnesium-base alloys are light str uctural materials and their strength 
properties are of great importance. This work included the investigat ion of the possibility of 
strengthening of magnesium-base alloys by SPD and aging. 
The work was performed on the perspective Mg-Sm system alloys characterized by high strength 
at room and elevated temperatures. The properties of the alloys t o a great extent are determined by 
their high strengthening during the decomposition of supersaturated m agnesium solid solution. So, 
the investigations of the Mg-Sm alloys allow one to establish t he joint effect of SPD and solid 
solution decomposition on strength properties. 
Experimental Procedure 
The phase diagram of the Mg-Sm system on the Mg side is presented in Fig.1 [10]. It shows relatively 
small solubility of Sm in solid Mg, decreasing with lowering temperature. Three binary alloys were 
used for the experiments. They contained 2.8, 4.5 and 5.5 mass %Sm. As the initial materials for the 
alloy preparation magnesium of the 99.96% 1 purity and samarium of the 99.83% purity (MG-96 and 
SmM-1 of Russian Grades) were used. The alloys were obtained by melting in electrical resistance 
furnace in steel crucibles under flux VI2 (Russian designation) c ontaining 38-46% MgCl 2, 32-40 
KCl, 3-5% CaF 2, 5-8% BaCl 2, 1.5% MgO, <8% (NaCl+CaCl 2). The flux preserved the melts from 
intensive oxidation and burning. The melts were poured into steel mould w ith three round cavities. 
Small ingots of 15 mm in diameter and of about 90 mm in length were obtained. The ingots were cut

Samples of the alloys without SPD and after SPD were studied b y specific electrical resistance 
measurements, microhardness measurements and microscopy examinat ion using optical and 
transmission electron microscopes. The specific electrical res istance was measured by the 
compensation method on the device based on the low-ohm potentiometer. The calculated error of the 
specific electrical resistance measurements was ±7%. To conduct the measurements, the strips ∼6 
mm wide and ∼10 mm long were cut from the round samples ∼0.30-0.34 mm thick. The 
microhardness was measured using diamond pyramid at a load of 20 g for 10 seconds at the middle of 
the radius of the sample. Before microhardness measurements the sample surface was polished using 
diamond paste (thin samples after SPD) or common wet method with Cr 2O3 suspension (bulk samples 
without SPD). The same procedure was used for the preparation of the s ample surface for optical 
microscopic observations. Transmission electron microscopy was perf ormed by the JEM 100C Х 
equipment with thinning of the samples in 20% HNO 3 solution in alcohol.

SPD at both room temperature and 200 °C resulted in significant strengthening of the alloys. In the 
Mg-Sm alloys, the microhardness after SPD at 200 °C is higher and the specific electrical resistance is 
lower than those after SPD at room temperature (Tab.1,2). These fac ts can be explained by partial 
decomposition of Mg solid solution during deformation at 200 °C, i.e. strain aging. Some increase of 
specific electrical resistance after SPD of the alloy M g-4.5%Sm aged preliminary at 200 and 300°C 
suggests a partial dissolution of the Sm-rich precipitates. For t he same heat treatment, the 
microhardness of the alloys increases with increasing Sm content. 
Effect of aging on specific electrical resistance and microhardness of the Mg-4.5%Sm 
After SPD the Mg-4.5%Sm alloy was aged isothermally at 200 °C for up to 64 hours. At this aging 
temperature, the decomposition of the Mg supersaturated solid solution usually causes strengthening 
and then softening. The solid solution decomposition rate was checked by specific electrical 
resistance and microhardness measurements. Results of the measurements are presented in Figs 2 and 
3. It is shown that during aging at 200 °C after only quenching + aging at 200 °C, 8 hours without SPD 
and after the same aging, but with SPD the additional solid solution decomposition occurs that results 
in decreasing of specific electrical resistance and increas ing of microhardness. SPD increases 
microhardness maximum and additional depletion of Mg solid solution. Afte r preliminary aging at 
300 °C, 6 hours without SPD additional aging at 200 °C resulted in insignificant changes in specific 
electrical resistance and microhardness. This fact correla tes with almost full decomposition of 
magnesium solid solution expected for aging at 300 °C. On the other hand, in the case SPD after aging 
at 300 °C, additional aging at 200 °C resulted in certain decrease in specific electrical re sistance 
showing an additional depletion of Mg solid solution and some increase in microhardness which 
confirmes an additional Mg solid solution decomposition. Thus, SPD intensifi es Mg solid solution 
decomposition and increases its strengthening effect. 
The highest strengthening was reached after aging at 200 °C when SPD was applied to the samples 
pre-aged at 200 °C.

Structure of the Mg-Sm alloys 
After quenching the alloys had typical structure with equiaxed gr ains of Mg solid solution. In 
optical microscope, Sm-rich precipitates could be seen only after aging at 300 °C, 6h. The grain 
boundaries were the places of the preferable precipitation. However, t he precipitates within grains 
were also seen. Transmission electron microscopy revealed specif ic features of the solid solution 
decomposition after aging at both 200 and 300 °C. After aging at 200 °C, the precipitation was 
revealed in the electron diffraction patterns indicating the GP-zone s formation and ordering. After 
aging at 300 °C, coarse plate-like precipitates were observed within grains. 
SPD resulted in the formation of submicrocrystalline structure of the Mg alloys with a grain size of 
100-200 nm. The remarkable feature of the structure formed was high disl ocation density and not 
distinct boundaries between grains. The typical structure is shown in F ig. 4a. If SPD was applied to 
the alloys after aging at 300 °C, the structure was changed, in general, by the similar way, but the 
coarse plate-like precipitates remained (Fig. 4b).

Conclusions 
1. SPD of the Mg-Sm alloys results in significant strengthening both after aging to maximum 
hardness (200 °C, 8 hours) and after overaging (300 °C, 6 hours). 
2. Compared with aging at 300 °C previous aging at 200 °C results in higher strengthening of the 
alloys due to finer Sm-rich precipitates. 
3. The highest strengthening effect can be reached after trea tment by the schedule: aging to the 
maximum hardness (200 °C, 8 hours) + SPD at 20 °C + aging at 200 °C. 
4. The high strengthening effect after SPD results from the f ormation of the submicrocrystalline 
structure of Mg solid solution. 
5. SPD intensifies the decomposition of Mg supersaturated solid solution upon aging.
