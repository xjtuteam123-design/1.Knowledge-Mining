1. Introduction
High-temperature structural materials are crucial for ensuring the 
safe operation of aerospace vehicles in high-temperature and high- 
pressure environments. The advancement of the aerospace industry 
has imposed higher demands on the ultimate service temperature of 
these materials [ 1 ]. For instance, the temperature of the fuselage front 
and wing leading edge of a hypersonic vehicle can reach 1800 
◦
C, while 
the operating temperature of a supersonic combustion ramjet engine 
approaches nearly 2000 
◦
C. Consequently, nickel-based superalloys are 
becoming increasingly inadequate to meet these requirements [ 2 ].
Refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) are characterized by excel -
lent corrosion resistance and high strength at elevated temperature 
[ 3 – 7 ]. They maintain stable mechanical properties and structural 
integrity under extremely high-temperature conditions [ 8 , 9 ]. RHEAs 
have a wide range of applications in high-temperature operating envi -
ronments, such as aerospace engines. Low-density RHEAs enable light -
weight structures while ensuring high performance and thermal stability 
[ 10 – 13 ]. AlMo
0.5
NbTa
0.5
TiZr, as a novel type of low-density RHEAs, 
exhibits high strength in high-temperature environments ( σ

◦
C). Compared to other refractory 
high-entropy alloys, the incorporation of Al not only reduces the overall 
density of the alloy but also enhances its machinability at ambient 
temperatures [ 14 ], making it particularly well-suited for structural 
components in high-temperature and high-pressure environments. 
Therefore, it holds significant application potential in the aerospace 
industry [ 15 , 16 ].

As the laser power increases, the intensity of the diffraction peaks

the temperature gradient (G) increases while the solidification rate (R) 
decreases. Since the laser power significantly affects the solidification 
rate (R) more than the temperature gradient (G), the decrease in the 
solidification rate (R) is much larger than the increase in the tempera -
ture gradient (G). The solidification rate (R) decreases by a value much 
larger than the increase in the temperature gradient (G). As a result, the
