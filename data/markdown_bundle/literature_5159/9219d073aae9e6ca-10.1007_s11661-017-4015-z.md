Effect of Co on Discontinuous
Precipitation Transformation with TCP
Phase in Ni-based Alloy Containing Re
QIANYING SHI, NING AN, JIAJIE HUO,
YUNRONG ZHENG, and QIANG FENG
The eﬀect of Co on discontinuous precipitation (DP)
transformation involving the formation of topologically
close-packed (TCP) phase was investigated in three
Ni-Cr-Re model alloys containing diﬀerent levels of Co.
One typical TCP phase, r, was generated within DP
cellular colonies along the migrating grain boundaries in
experimental alloys during aging treatment. As a result
of the increased solubility of Re in the c matrix and
enlarged interlamellar spacing of r precipitates inside of
growing DP colonies, Co addition suppressed the
formation of r phase and associated DP colonies. This
study suggests that Co could potentially serve as a
microstructural stabilizer in Re-containing Ni-base
superalloys, which provides an alternative method for
the composition optimization of superalloys.

Refractory elements such as W, Mo, and especially Re
are eﬀective solid solution strengthening elements in
Ni-based superalloys and increasing their content sig-
niﬁcantly raises the alloy temperature capability. [1]
However, the high level of these refractory alloying
additions strongly increases the degree of dendritic
elemental segregation and c matrix supersaturation,
resulting in the formation of topologically close-packed
(TCP) phases, such as P, r, l, and R phases. [2] Since the
TCP precipitates deplete the surrounding strengthening
elements from the c matrix and their brittle nature could
easily cause damage accumulation or cracking, TCP
phases are generally considered to be detrimental to the
mechanical properties.[3] The interaction between refrac-
tory alloying additions and Cr, which is indispensable
for the hot corrosion and oxidation resistance of
Ni-based superalloys, further increases the probability
of TCP phase formation.
One manner of TCP precipitation, discontinuous pre-
cipitation (DP) with cellular morphology, was observed
along high-angle grain boundaries and in the ﬁeld of
thermal barrier coatings in high refractory-containing
Ni-based superalloys.[4–8] DP transformation with TCP
precipitates has an adverse eﬀect on mechanical properties
since the ﬁne and ordered c/c¢ phases in Ni-based
superalloys transform to a coarse microstructure with
large columnar c and TCP phases, following the reaction:
ðcSS
matrix þ c0
precipitatesÞ!ð c0
matrix þ cprecipitatesÞþ TCP, where
SS represents supersaturated condition.[5–7] To date, lim-
ited studies have focused on the eﬀect of alloying elements
on DP transformation involving TCP precipitates in
Ni-based superalloys. Ru, the most well-known TCP
phase suppressor, was reported to signiﬁcantly retard the
DP-colony formation with TCP precipitates in Ni-based
single crystal superalloys by either increasing the solubility
of refractory alloying additions (especially Re) in the c
matrix or increasing the c/TCP lattice misﬁt.[7–9] However,
the addition of Ru increases the density and cost of
superalloys, thus of more practical interest is to explore
alternative elements that could play a similar role.
Interestingly, Co was found to inﬂuence the microstruc-
tural stability regarding the reduction of TCP phase
formation in Ni-based superalloys in a positive way.[10–14]
Nevertheless, very few stud ies were reported about Co
eﬀect on DP transformation with TCP phases in Ni-based
superalloys.
The current work is aimed to explore the inﬂuence of
Co addition on DP transformation with TCP precipi-
tates in Re-containing Ni-based superalloys. This
knowledge could provide a new insight to improve
microstructure stability for the further composition
optimization and coating design. To avoid the possible
interaction between elements and multiple phase con-
stitutions in multicomponent Ni-based superalloys,
which can contain ten or more alloying elements, a set
of model Ni-based alloys without c¢ precipitates and
carbides/borides is designed in this study. Three exper-
imental alloys, containing typical TCP-forming elements
Re and Cr as well as diﬀerent additions of Co, were
investigated to understand how Co aﬀects the DP
transformation in Ni-based alloys. Table I lists the

As a well-established solid state transformation, DP
reaction transforms one supersaturated matrix phase
into two phases, the equilibrium precipitated phase and
the solute-depleted solid solution, by a cellular man-
ner.
[16,17] In current study, the supersaturated c matrix in
experimental Ni-Cr-Re model alloys converted into r
phase and solute-depleted c phase through the decom-
position process of DP transformation, i.e.,
cSS
matrix ! cDP þ TCPðrÞ. The plate-like r phase in the
cellular structure appeared to be parallel to a speciﬁc
direction, as shown in Figure 1. This morphology
suggests that a certain crystallographic orientation
relationship exists between r phase and the matrix in
Ni-based superalloys: ð001Þr==ð1/C2211Þc and ½110/C138 r==½110/C138 c,
as has been reported previously. [2,18] The DP transfor-
mation involving r phase has also been observed in
Co-Cr-Re-based alloys during thermal exposure at 1373
K (1100 /C176 C), where ﬁne r phase precipitates with a
partly lamellar morphology in the grain interiors also
exhibit a speciﬁc orientation relationship with the HCP
matrix.
[19] Two classical mechanisms were proposed in
the literature to explain the initiation of DP, i.e., the
nucleation of boundary precipitates and concurrent
boundary migration. [20–22] Both mechanism I (precipi-
tate-induced boundary migration mechanism (‘‘pucker’’
mechanism) proposed by Tu and Turnbull
[20,21])a n d
mechanism II (migrating boundaries mechanism pro-
posed by Fournelle and Clark [22]) are possible depending
on the alloy systems and the experimental conditions. In
mechanism I, a precipitate/matrix habit crystallographic
relationship is the primary requirement to initiate DP,
and a curved boundary might lead DP to nucleate on
both sides of the same boundary.
[17,20,21] In mechanism
II, the initial boundary motion thermally activated is
autocatalytic, and one boundary will move only into one
direction—namely towards its center of curva-
ture.
[17,22,23] A DP reaction with S-morphology colonies
and the existence of r/c crystallographic relationship in
present alloys suggest that the DP cell initiation is more
analogous to mechanism I rather than mechanism II.
Therefore, the nucleation and growth process of DP
colonies in Ni-Cr-Re (-Co) alloys in this study could be
speciﬁcally illustrated in Figure 4. Firstly, original grain
boundaries provide heterogeneous nucleation sites
owing to the inherent mobility and high diﬀusivity
caused by the concentration of vacancies and disloca-
tions (Figure 4(a)). Then the TCP precipitates
preferentially form in certain locations of original grain
boundaries (Figure 4(b)). Because of the availability of
large numbers of equivalent r/c habit planes, it is
expected that r phase will form on one side or both sides
of the original boundary. Therefore, migrating bound-
aries bow into either one direction or both directions,
and those cellular structures exhibit S-morphology
(Figure 4(c)). Subsequently, the boundaries migrate
further into the adjacent grains. Meanwhile, r phase
continuously precipitates and grows in DP due to the
supersaturated condition in the reaction front (migrat-
ing boundaries), as shown in Figure 4(d). Solute-de-
pleted c matrix exists beside the r phase in DP colonies.
With extending the isothermal aging time, DP colonies
grow and connect with each other until they almost
occupy the original grains (Figure 4(e)).
Under the isothermal condition, the driving force for
DP formation process is spent to ﬁrstly initiate DP
reaction and subsequently sustain the steady state
growth.

In summary, discontinuous precipitation (DP) trans-
formation involving the TCP precipitation ( r phase)
occurred in three model Ni-Cr-Re alloys containing
diﬀerent Co additions during the aging process. DP
colonies were developed by migrating boundaries and
the following formation of cellular structures with
S-morphology. While increasing the Co content
enhanced the solubility of Re and decreased its super-
saturation in the c matrix, it also widened the inter-
lamellar spacing of r phase in DP colonies. As a result,
Co addition suppressed the formation of r phase and
DP colonies. It is expected that Co could serve as a
microstructure stabilizer for the compositional opti-
mization as well as the coating design of Ni-based
superalloys containing Re.
