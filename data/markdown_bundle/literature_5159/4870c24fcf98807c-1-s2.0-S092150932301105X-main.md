ABSTRACT 
The WNbMoTa-based refractory high entropy alloy (RHEA) is expected to become a new generation of high- 
temperature materials due to its high thermal stability, which can be used in aerospace, nuclear energy and 
other fields. In this paper, TN10 (TN is the abbreviation of Ti and Ni elements), TN15, TN20 and TN25 non- 
equimolar WNbMoTa-based RHEAs were designed based on phase engineering and formed by laser powder 
bed fusion (LPBF) technology. The solidification thermodynamics, microstructure and mechanical properties 
(compressive strength at 25 
◦
C) of the four alloys were analyzed, and the strengthening mechanism of TN20 alloy 
was studied. The compressive yield strength of the four alloys exceeds 2050 MPa, the ultimate compressive 
strength exceeds 2550 MPa, and the elongation exceeds 9%. The compressive yield strength of TN20 alloy is 
2513 MPa, the ultimate strength is 3127 MPa, and the elongation is 11.38%. The room temperature (25 
◦
C) 
compressive yield strength of TN20 alloy is the highest among the high entropy alloys studied so far. TN20 alloy 
is composed of BCC matrix phase and grain boundary phase containing three different structures, which are B2 
TiNi phase, Ni55Nb25Ti15Ta5 phase generating stacking faults and Ni70Ti20Nb10 secondary precipitated 
phase. The micrometer matrix phase and nanometer grain boundary phase of TN20 alloy show a network dis -
tribution in fine crystal region. After the matrix phase cracks due to high thermal stress, the flow TiNi-rich grain 
boundary phases precipitate to fill the cracks. The grain boundary phase with high proportion in this region is 
enhanced by the secondary phase and its nanoprecipitated phase. In other regions, stacking faults are generated 
by the Ni55Nb25Ti15Ta5 phases, which convert the thermal stress that induces crack into lattice defects of the 
crystal. On the other hand, the content of B2 phase is the highest in grain boundary phase, and the grain 
boundary between B2 phase and matrix phase is a coherent grain boundary, which realizes the high quality 
combination of grain boundary phase and matrix phase. The design and strengthening of WNbMoTa-based non- 
equimolar RHEAs are studied in this paper, which is expected to provide a reference for the design of a new 
generation of high entropy alloy. 
1. Introduction 
As an innovative material design idea, Professor Yeh, a scholar from 
Taiwan, China, proposed a new design concept of “ multi-principal 
element ” alloy in 2004 [ 1 ]. “ Multi-principal ” alloys are usually 
composed of four or more metallic elements in a solution of 5% – 35% of 
the atomic percentages, so they are also called high entropy alloys. High 
entropy alloy has high entropy effect on thermodynamics, lattice 
distortion effect on structure, hysteresis diffusion effect on dynamics and 
“ cocktail ” effect on material properties. Therefore, it has ultra-high 
hardness, high strength and high ductility, outstanding thermal 
stability, good wear resistance, corrosion resistance and oxidation 
resistance [ 2 – 9 ]. At present, the study of this kind of alloy has become a 
global hot spot, which has high research value and significance. 
Refractory high entropy alloys (RHEAs) are high entropy alloys made 
of high melting point elements W, Ta, Nb, Mo, Hf and Re, which are 
characterized by high strength and high density [ 10 , 11 ]. Compared with 
conventional high entropy alloys, the alloy can maintain high strength at 
high temperature and has good thermal stability. Senkov first designed 
WNbMoTa and WNbMoTaV RHEAs in 2010. The hardness of WNbMo -
TaV alloy is as high as 5250 MPa [ 12 ], and the compression strength of 
WNbMoTa at 1600 
◦

3. Results 
3.1. Alloy design 
The design of NbMoTaW-based REHAs needs to solve two problems. 
1) Whether the design idea of equimolar high entropy alloy can optimize 
the properties of the alloy; 2) How to solve the crack defects caused by 
high brittleness in NbMoTaW based RHEAs [ 19 – 21 ]. In this study, a 
design method of NbMoTaW-based REHAs on phase engineering was 
proposed to achieve the LPBF preparation of NbMoTaW-based REHAs 
with the best mechanical properties without defects. The first step is to 
complete the yield strength optimization design of NbMoTaW-REHA 
matrix phase. The second step is to complete the design of the grain 
boundary phase connecting the matrix phase to the matrix phase to 
resist cracks.

Studies have shown that the addition of the same proportion of Ti 
and Ni elements can inhibit the crack defects in the LPBF forming pro -

RHEAs were formed by adding 10% Ti and 10% Ni, 15% Ti and 15% Ni, 
20% Ti and 20% Ni, and 25% Ti and 25% Ni to the matrix phase with 
determined composition ratio, respectively. The four alloys were 
referred to as TN10, TN15, TN20 and TN25 respectively according to the 
Ti and Ni contents, as shown in Table 2 . As shown in Fig. 2 (a – d), 
JmatPro software was used to simulate the solidification process of all 
alloys, and it was found that all alloys formed three phase structures. 
The three phase structures are the matrix phase with a BCC structure, 
TiNi grain boundary phase of B2 structure, and a small amount of FCC 
structure TiNi grain boundary phase. The content of FCC phase is about 
3%. With the increase of Ti and Ni content, the content of BCC matrix 
phase gradually decreases, while the content of B2 grain boundary phase 
gradually increases. This indicates that the content of grain boundary 
phase which acts as binding matrix phase and matrix phase increases 
with the increase of Ti and Ni content. TN10, TN15, TN20 and TN25 
RHEAs were formed by the composition optimization of the matrix 
phase and the design of the grain boundary phase, which bonds the 
matrix phase and the matrix phase. 
3.2. Alloys forming 
Fig. 3 shows BSE and surface energy spectra of TN10, TN15, TN20 
and TN25 RHEAs formed using LPBF. From the macroscopic point of 
view, the morphology of the forming surface of all alloys is the classical 
molten pool morphology of LPBF forming. Under the action of high 
energy laser beam, the molten pool is formed and solidified rapidly, 
resulting in a very high temperature gradient (10
5 
K/s). Therefore, the 
microstructure in the middle of a single molten pool is relatively dense, 
which forms a fine grained region, where the two molten pools overlap, 
called the remelting zone. The microstructure tissue can grow again and 
is coarse. This area forms a coarse crystal zone. There are not too many 
unmelted particles (white granular) in TN10, TN15 and TN20 alloys, 
while there are more unmelted W and Mo particles in TN25 alloys. This 
is because the boiling point of Ti and Ni elements is lower than the 
melting point of the high melting point W element. And TN25 alloy 
contains a large number of Ti and Ni elements; in order to avoid a large

crystal region microstructure. On the other hand, the addition of Ti and 
Ni fills up the intergranular pores and cracks in the matrix phase under 
high thermal stress. The matrix phase forms the alloy skeleton, and the 
grain boundary phase binds the matrix together like a muscle. At the

5. Conclusions 
In this paper, TN10, TN15, TN20 and TN25 non-equimolar 
WNbMoTa based RHEAs were designed by means of matrix phase 
optimization and crack suppression, which were formed using LPBF 
technology. The solidification thermodynamics, microstructure and 
mechanical properties of all alloys were analyzed, with emphasis on the 
strengthening mechanism of TN20 alloy. The main conclusions are as 
follows: 
Through the solid solution strengthening model, whith the aim of 
maximizing the yield strength of the matrix phase of WNbMoTa-based 
RHEAs, the matrix phase composition with the best yield strength was 
obtained. The defects of WNbMoTa-based RHEAs in LPBF forming 
process were controlled by adding Ti and Ni elements. Therefore, TN10, 
TN15, TN20 and TN25 alloys were designed. In the four alloys formed 
using LPBF technique, the elements are evenly distributed, and there are 
no cracks or macrosegregation. All the alloys exhibit a dendritic struc -
ture with grain sizes smaller than 7 μ m. The grain size of the TN20 alloy 
is only 5.8 μ m. Through the compression performance test at room 
temperature (25 
◦
C), the yield strength of the TN10, TN15, TN20 and 
TN25 RHEAs exceeds 2050 MPa, the ultimate compressive strength 
exceeds 2550 MPa, and the elongation exceeds 9%. The compressive 
yield strength of TN20 alloy is 2513 MPa, the ultimate strength is 3127 
MPa, and the elongation is 11.38%. The room temperature (25 
◦
C) 
compressive yield strength of TN20 alloy is the highest among the high 
entropy alloys studied so far. 
After the TN20 alloy was formed using LPBF, the micrometer matrix 
phase and nanometer grain boundary phase showed a network of fine 
crystal region microstructure. This distribution of microstructure 
strengthens the bonding between matrix phase and matrix phase, and 
between the matrix phase and grain boundary phase of TN20 alloy 
firmly. After the matrix phase cracks due to high thermal stress, flowing 
Ti and Ni precipitate and form grain boundary phases to fill the cracks. A 
large area of grain boundary phase in this region is strengthened by 
secondary phases and their nanocrystalline precipitates. In other re -
gions, the Ni55Nb25Ti15Ta5 phase generates stacking faults, which 
convert the thermal stress that induces crack generation into lattice 
defects of the crystal. More importantly, the matrix phase and B2 phase 
with the largest proportion in the grain boundary phase show a coherent 
matching relationship, which strengthened the interface between them. 
In this paper, a new breakthrough in the properties of WNbMoTa-based 
RHEAs is achieved by phase engineering.
