ABSTRACT 
The impact behavior of the Ti – 6Al – 4V alloy has been investigated at varying temperatures (298 K, 223 K, 77 K) 
in this study. The results reveal a decrease in toughness with reduced temperature, with notable changes in 
energy distribution between crack initiation and propagation. The { 10 12 }
α 
twins formed in the studied sample 
under impact loading at 298 K and 223 K, and were absent at 77 K. The formation and propagation of twins 
contribute to the higher toughness of the 298 K and 223 K samples. These findings would provide valuable 
guidance to optimize the Ti – 6Al – 4V alloy toughness in low-temperature environments. 
1. Introduction 
Titanium alloy, a lightweight metal, has a high strength-to-weight 
ratio, excellent corrosion resistance, and good low-temperature plas -
ticity, making it a promising low-temperature structural material for 
applications such as ships, pipeline systems, and aerospace components 
[ 1 – 4 ]. For the safe application of these components, it is crucial to un -
derstand the impact performance of components with cracks, threads, 
and welds under impact loading under low-temperature conditions. It is 
well-known that the mechanical properties of metals are closely related 
to temperature [ 5 ]. Generally, the strength of metallic material increases 
as the temperature decreases, while its toughness varies due to different 
deformation mechanisms at different temperatures [ 6 – 8 ]. One of the 
challenges encountered in the development and application of 
low-temperature titanium alloys is their impact toughness at cryogenic 
temperatures. Revealing the microscopic control mechanisms of impact 
toughness at low temperatures and ensuring the safety and reliability of 
low-temperature titanium alloys are critical issues that need to be 
addressed. Furthermore, previous studies on the low-temperature per -
formance of titanium alloys have mainly focused on tensile properties 
[ 9 , 10 ], while research on low-temperature impact toughness is still 
lacking, severely hindering the application of titanium alloys in 
low-temperature environments. The impact toughness of titanium alloys 
under low-temperature impact loading, as well as their crack nucleation 
and propagation mechanisms are still not fully understood. Considering 
that Ti – 6Al – 4V alloy is the most commonly used titanium alloy, which 
can generally achieve the best match of strength and toughness through 
microstructural regulation. Therefore, we conducted a study on the 
crack nucleation energy, crack propagation energy, and impact tough -
ness of the Ti – 6Al – 4V alloy at three different temperatures: 298 K, 223 
K, and 77 K. Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM) was employed to 
characterize the impact fracture, while Electron Back-Scattering 
Diffraction (EBSD) was utilized to analyze the grain orientations near 
the fracture. The effects of different temperatures on the occurrence and 
propagation of impact-induced twins would be investigated. 
2. Experimental procedures

rods with 25 mm diameters was utilized. It has a chemical composi -
tion of 5.9% Al, 4.1% V, and balance Ti. To achieve a duplex micro -

3. Results and discussion 
The studied Ti – 6Al – 4V samples consists of equiaxed α + β phase

matrix of the sample is mainly contained α phase, with small amount of β 
phase.

temperatures, we have examined the impact fracture surface of the

seen from Fig. 3 that the fracture surface comprises two regions, namely, 
the crack propagation region (C) and the shear lip region (S). The per -

propagation. Fig. 3 (a1 - c1) display the magnified fracture morphology

consists of dimples and micro-voids, with the dimples at 77 K being 
shallower than those at other temperatures. A larger shear lip region

deformation during the impact process, thus absorbing more energy and 
resulting in a higher impact energy. This observation aligns with the 
experimental results shown in Fig. 2 (d). The 3D profiles of the fracture 
surfaces, analyzed through the three-dimensional OM, are depicted in 
Fig. 3 (d - f). In a three-dimensional OM, focus stacking is employed to

the loading direction. Conversely, the macroscopic fracture surface at 
77 K ( Fig. 3 (f)) is more linear and level than those at 298 K and 223 K, 
with a mere height difference of 1.18 mm. These 3D profiles of fracture

Orientation analysis of the impact fracture surfaces at varying tem -

Twin deformation processes often involve shearing, which contrib -
utes to the irregular morphology of fracture surfaces at 298 K and 223 K. 
The absence of twin deformation and other deformation modes near the 
fracture surface during the 77 K impact process resulted in a relatively 
flat fracture surface. For the cases at 298 K and 223 K, the higher impact 
toughness is mainly due to the coordinated deformation caused by 
dislocations and twins. When the system is subject to impact loading, the 
stress concentration at the crack tip activates multiple slip planes and 
twin systems, resulting in a more tortuous crack propagation path as 
shown by the red dashed lines in Fig. 4 (b, f, j). However, at low tem -
perature (77 K), the weakened lattice thermal vibration and increased 
lattice resistance prevent some slip systems from being activated due to 
their inability to reach the critical shear stress, thus reducing the number 
of active slip systems, and causing a more linear crack propagation path. 
This is primarily due to the decrease in coordinated deformation modes. 
The presence of multiple slip and twin deformation modes allows the 
sample to undergo more deformation, achieving better resistance of 
crack propagation and thus greater impact toughness, whereas in situ -
ations with less coordinated deformation, the impact toughness is lower 
[ 25 – 27 ].

observed that twins can readily traverse GBs when the MA is small, a 
phenomenon termed as twin transfer (TT). Conversely, large MA values 
impede twin growth, a phenomenon termed as twin blockage (TB). 
Fig. 4 (l) schematically represents these scenarios, and the incidences of 
TT and TB behaviors are quantified at the yellow arrows in Fig. 4 (d, h).

4. Conclusion 
In summary, this study presents a comprehensive investigation of the 
impact behavior of the Ti – 6Al – 4V alloy at three varying temperatures: 
298 K, 223 K, and 77 K. The OM and SEM microstructure analysis re -
veals that the Ti – 6Al – 4V alloy is composed of equiaxed α + β phase. The 
impact toughness, assessed at 298 K, 223 K, and 77 K, are 53.3 J/cm
2
, 
45.7 J/cm
2
, and 21.2 J/cm
2
, respectively, demonstrating a decline in 
toughness with temperature reduction. The evaluation of the impact 
fractures shows the formation of { 10 12 }
α 
twins under impact loading at 
298 K and 223 K, while no twins are observed at 77 K. The multiple twin 
and slip deformation lead to higher impact toughness of 298 K and 223 K 
samples. In addition, MA is found to significantly influence the occur -
rence of TT behavior, and when TT occurs, it can further release the 
stress within the system, which contributes to higher impact toughness. 
This work provides valuable insights into understanding the impact 
behavior of the Ti – 6Al – 4V alloy in low temperature environments, of -
fering guidance for its practical application under such conditions.
