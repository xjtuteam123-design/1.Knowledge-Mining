High temperature deformation of a Fe-based low nickel alloy
Hong Gao a, Gary C. Barber a,∗, Qi-An Chen b, Yuqiu Luc
a Department of Mechanical Engineering, Oakland University, Rochester, MI 48309, USA
b Central Iron and Steel Research Institute, Beijing 100081, China

Received 24 June 2002; received in revised form 18 December 2002; accepted 17 February 2003
Abstract
The high temperature deformation of a Fe-based low nickel alloy was investigated by using the Gleeble-1500 thermo-mechanical
simulation machine. True stress–true strain curves and microstructures were studied. The results show that the dynamic recovery is the
dominating softening mechanism during the high temperature deformation of the alloy. Twin crystals are produced by annealing for 2 min
after deformation. The addition of aluminum has little effect on the high temperature strength, but reduces the occurrence of dynamic
recrystallization and annealing twin grains. The recovery activation energies were calculated from the Zener–Hollomon parameter and
were found that it was to be higher for the alloy containing less aluminum.

1. Introduction
Nickel alloys that typically have more than 40% nickel
in weight have the characteristics of high electric resistiv-
ity and high resistance to oxidation. In order to reduce the
nickel content without sacriﬁcing the performance of nickel
alloys, a type of Fe-based low nickel alloy has been devel-
oped [1]. This alloy has similar anti-oxidation characteris-
tics as high nickel alloys (Ni > 60 wt.%). At the same time,
it has similar electric resistivity and temperature–resistance
coefﬁcient as medium nickel alloys (30 wt .% < Ni <
60 wt.%). The hot-deformation behaviors of the nickel-base
alloys were studied by experimental tests and modeling
[2,3]. However, little research has been done related to
the deformation characteristics at high temperature for the
Fe-based low nickel alloy. This subject is of importance
in manufacturing applications such as hot forging and
rolling.
In this paper, the compressive deformation behavior and
microstructure have been investigated experimentally by
using the Gleeble-1500 thermo-mechanical simulation ma-
chine [4]. This study includes high temperature plasticity
and inﬂuencing parameters.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Effects of deformation conditions on stress–strain
curves
Fig. 3 shows some true stress–true strain curves of the
Fe-based low nickel alloy containing 1.26 wt.% aluminum
at different temperatures and strain rates. Typically, the ﬂow
stress curves are ﬂat-topped without a distinct peak. This
indicates that the operative softening mechanism is domi-
nated by dynamic recovery rather than dynamic recrystal-
lization. Fig. 4 shows the true stress–true strain curves of the
Fe-based low nickel alloy containing 0.98 wt.% aluminum
at different combinations of temperatures and strain rates.
It can be seen that two types of stress–strain curves occur.
One is dynamic recovery [5,6] such as the deformation at
T = 850
◦C and ˙ε = 0.001 s−1 as shown in Fig. 4(a). No
ultimate or peak stress occurs for the dynamic recovery de-
formation. Another type is dynamic recrystallization such
as the deformation at T = 1250
◦C and ˙ε = 0.01 s−1 as
shown in Fig. 4(b). The ultimate stress occurs for the dy-
namic recrystallization deformation. When the deformation
temperature is below 1250
◦C, even though the deformation
strain rate is very low, the true stress–true strain curves show
no ultimate value. This suggests that dynamic recovery is
the major softening mechanism of the deformation of the
Fe-based low nickel alloy at high temperatures.
Both temperature and strain rate affect the stress–strain
curves of the Fe-based low nickel alloy. Dynamic recrys-
tallization occurs when the temperature is relatively high
such as at 1250
◦C and the strain rate is relatively low ( ˙ε =
0.01 s−1). On the other hand, dynamic recovery occurs when
the temperature is lower than 1250◦C even though the strain
rate is low at 0.001 s −1. The high temperature deformation
is governed by the competition between dynamic recovery
and recrystallization. Dynamic recovery occurs simultane-
ously with dynamic recrystallization. Sometimes dynamic
recovery dominates the high temperature deformation and
sometimes dynamic recrystallization dominates. As a result,
the deformation conditions affect the strength of the alloy
through different softening mechanisms.

4. Microstructure observation
Two post-heat treatments were employed to study the
annealing effect as seen in Fig. 2 . Fig. 5 shows the mi-
crostructures of the alloy at 1150 ◦C and 0.01 s −1. An-
nealing twins are produced after annealing for 2 min. For
the Fe-based low nickel alloy, it is difﬁcult for edge dis-
locations to climb and for screw dislocations to cross slip
due to the low stacking fault energy. However, twinning
increases with the decrease of stacking fault energy. There-
fore, annealing twins are produced during high temperature
deformation.
4.1. Effects of aluminum
In order to improve the oxidation resistance at high
temperatures, 0.9–1.7 wt.% aluminum was added into the
alloy. Fig. 6 shows the effect of aluminum on the yield
strength ( σ
0.2) and peak stress ( σp). Little difference was
found between the sample with 0.98 wt.% aluminum and
the sample containing 1.26 wt.% aluminum. This suggests
that the high temperature strength is not affected when alu-
minum is added to enhance the oxidation resistance of the
alloy.
Fig. 7 shows the microstructure of the alloy after being
deformed at 1250
◦C. The alloy containing 1.26 wt.% alu-
minum produces more recrystals and annealing twins than
does that containing 0.98 wt.% aluminum. The addition of
aluminum can increase the stacking fault energy of alloys.
Therefore, the activation energy for the dynamic recovery is
decreased for the alloy with greater amount of aluminum.
Recovery becomes easier to occur for the alloy containing
more aluminum. At the same time, recrystallization is rela-
tively inhibited.

It can be seen that the alloy containing less aluminum has
the greater recovery activation energy than that containing
more aluminum. The dynamic recovery threshold for the al-
loy with greater amount of aluminum is lower than that of
the alloy with less aluminum. As a result, it is easier for the
alloy containing a greater percentage of aluminum to pro-
duce dynamic recovery than that containing less aluminum.
This is expressed in the shape of the stress–strain curves in
Figs. 3 and 4 which agrees with the microstructure observa-
tion shown in Fig. 7.
5. Conclusions
The high temperature deformation behavior of a Fe-based
low nickel alloy was investigated by means of hot simulation
technology. True stress–true strain curves and microstructure
were studied. The recovery activation energy for this alloy
was calculated. The results show that the dynamic recovery
is the dominating softening mechanism during hot deforma-
tion of this alloy. Annealing twin crystals are produced due
to annealing for 2 min after deformation. The addition of
aluminum has little impact on the high temperature strength
while reducing the occurrence of dynamic recrystallization
and annealing twin grains. The recovery activation energy
for the alloy containing less aluminum is higher than that
for the alloy with more aluminum.
