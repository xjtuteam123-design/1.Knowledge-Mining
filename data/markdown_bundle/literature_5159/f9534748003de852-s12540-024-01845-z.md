1 Introduction
The pursuit of metallic materials that exhibit an outstanding 
balance of strength and ductility has long been a primary 
objective for challenging engineering applications, includ -
ing pipeline equipment, pressure vessels, high-strength 
fasteners, automotive bodies, and more [ 1–3]. However, 
strength and ductility are always contradictory: an increase 
in strength is often accompanied by a sacrifice of ductil -
ity [4–6]. To meet the increasing demand for new advanced 
high-performance materials, a lot of work has been focused 
on designing and creating high-performance metallic mate-
rials by tailoring element compositions, processing routines, 
and microstructures [7–9]. High and medium-entropy alloys 
based on multiple principal elements (three or more) in equi-
molar or near-equimolar ratios have been rapidly developed 
due to their exceptional mechanical properties compared to 
common metals and alloys [10–12].

Abstract
Combining high strength and good ductility is a long-standing and challenging goal for metallic materials in modern engi -
neering applications. Recently developed single-phase high and medium-entropy alloys, particularly those featuring het -
erogeneous microstructures, demonstrate superior strength-ductility synergy. Here, a single-phase CrCoNi-based medium-
entropy alloy (MEA) with heterogeneous microstructures is introduced through suitable thermo-mechanical treatment. The 
analysis systematically examines the development of heterogeneous microstructures and their corresponding mechanical 
properties. The tailored CrCoNi-based MEA demonstrated an exceptional balance between strength and ductility, with a 
yield stress of approximately 1160 MPa and fracture elongation of 29.3% at annealing 800 ℃ for 1 h. The analysis reveals 
heterogeneous microstructures consisting of variously sized deformed grains, recovery substructure, recrystallized grains, 
and annealing twins. These features are primarily influenced by the initial grain size, deformation texture, cold-rolling 
reduction, and annealing temperature. Elevated annealing temperatures enhance the transformation trend of the micro -
bands, introduced by cold rolling, into recrystallized grains. These findings not only provide a fundamental understanding 
of the formation mechanisms of the heterogeneous microstructure but also useful guidance for developing methods to 
tailor microstructures in high and medium-entropy alloys.

In general, single-phase FCC (face-centered cubic) 
HEAs and MEAs with fully recrystallized microstructures 
always exhibit good ductility and toughness under both 
quasi-static and dynamic loading conditions [13, 14]. Addi-
tionally, some single-phase HEAs and MEAs demonstrate 
excellent strength-ductility combinations at cryogenic tem -
perature, which leads to broad cryogenic application pros -
pects [15, 16]. However, their low yield strength, typically 
ranging from 200 MPa to 400 MPa at ambient temperature, 
limits their applications [17, 18]. Therefore, it is hard work 
to enhance the ambient temperature yield strength in MEAs 
and HEAs without significantly sacrificing ductility [ 19]. 
Currently, several strategies have been pursued to enhance 
the yield strength of MEAs and HEAs, such as composi -
tional adjustments [ 20, 21], pre-twinning [ 22, 23], grain 
refinement [ 24–26], and precipitation hardening [ 27–29]. 
These strengthening strategies resulted in increased yield 
strength but failed to maintain good ductility for HEAs 
and MEAs, particularly at room temperature. For example, 
severe plastic deformation (SPD) methods were proposed to 
reduce the average grain size to the submicron or nanoscale 
[30, 31]. Some ultrafine-grained (UFG) and nanocrystalline 
(NC) HEAs or MEAs can significantly enhance the yield 
strength over 1 GPa due to grain boundary strengthening but 
with unavoidable losses in ductility [32, 33]. During tensile 
deformation, the inability of UFG and NC HEAs and MEAs 
to accumulate dislocation density results in a low strain 
hardening rate, which corresponds to rapid tension instabil-
ity after yielding [ 34]. Therefore, the conflicting nature of 
strength and ductility remains challenging for the majority 
of strengthening approaches employed in HEAs and MEAs.
Recently, heterogeneous microstructure strengthening 
has emerged as a viable strategy to overcome the strength-
ductility balance in various metals and alloys. These micro-
structures have been successfully developed in a variety of 
materials, including copper alloys [ 35], aluminum alloys 
[36], titanium alloys [ 37], and steels [ 38], effectively 
addressing the strength-ductility trade-off. Yang et al. [ 14] 
prepared a bimodal grain structure ranging from nanome -
ters to microns in an MEA alloy and exhibited an excep -
tional strength and ductility balance. A novel hierarchical 
heterostructure was developed by Shi et al. [ 39] in ultra -
fine grains Fe20Co20Ni41Al19 eutectic HEA through thermo-
mechanical treatment. This HEA alloy demonstrates high 
ultimate strength and good ductility, attributed to its distinc-
tive multi-stage strain hardening originating from grain size 
and composition modification. Huang et al. [40] introduced 
a Co34.46Cr32.12Ni27.42Al3Ti3 MEA with dual heterogeneous 
structures comprising heterogeneous grain sizes and L1 2 
nanoprecipitate sizes, achieving a high ultimate strength of 
2.2 GPa and an elongation of 13%. In conclusion, this study 
emphasizes the introduction and investigation of hetero -
structures in CrCoNi-based MEAs.
Designing and creating the heterogeneous structure raises 
the topic of how the heterogeneous microstructure is formed 
and evolved in HEAs and MEAs under various processing 
methods. To address this question, Li et al. [ 41], Zhu et al. 
[42], Li et al. [43], and Bracke et al. [44] demonstrated that 
different domains (coarse, fine, recrystallization grains, and 
annealing twins) exhibit different nucleation and/or growth 
rates during the cold-rolling and short annealing process, 
which is a frequently used method for producing the het -
erogeneous microstructures. Due to the inhomogeneous 
nucleation and growth of these domains, different heteroge-
neous microstructures are eventually formed in metals and 
alloys. Furthermore, the nucleation of different domains is 
determined by the inhomogeneous strain energy, dislocation 
density, and twinning fraction, which are introduced by the 
lattice distortion in different slip systems during cold rolling 
[45, 46]. However, a systematic understanding of the het -
erogeneous microstructure nucleation and evolution mech -
anism, resolving the temperature-controlled and growth 
processes of various domains during short-time annealing, 
is still difficult work.
The CoCrNi MEAs are potential candidates for form -
ing heterogeneous microstructures due to their stable phase 
structure and excellent mechanical properties. In this work, 
a series of heterogeneous microstructures were produced 
in a CoCrNi-based MEA through appropriate cold roll -
ing followed by short-time annealing at different tempera -
tures. Microstructural analyses elucidate the evolution of 
domains through each thermo-mechanical treatment and 
their respective contributions to the heterogeneous micro -
structure. Additionally, the formation process is analyzed, 
and the evolution of the heterogeneous microstructure is 
distinguished to gain insight into the mechanisms govern -
ing its formation.
2 Experimental Details
2.1 Samples Preparation
The alloy, designated as Al1Ti2 samples with a nominal 
composition of Cr 32Co32Ni33Al1Ti2, were synthesized via 
arc-melting a blend of high-purity raw materials ( > 99.9% 
pure) in a Ti-gettered argon atmosphere. The Al1Ti2 ingot 
underwent at least four cycles of flipping and re-melting 
to ensure thorough mixing and compositional uniformity 
before being drop-cast into a Cu mold. Then, the cast ingots 
were homogenized in a vacuum at 1200 °C for 12 h fol -
lowed by water cooling. Subsequently, Al1Ti2 plates were 
subjected to cold rolling (CR) and annealing treatments at

Mechanical Tests
The microstructure of Al1Ti2 samples before and after cold 
rolling and short-term annealing at different temperatures 
were analyzed using equipment of different scales, includ -
ing Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM, JEOL 4700), 
Electron Backscatter Diffraction (EBSD, OXFORD Sym -
metry 2), and Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM, 
JEOL JEM-F200). The specimen surfaces for EBSD char -
acterization were first ground on various silicon carbide 
papers followed by further polishing with 0.25 μm diamond 
plate and 0.05 μm SiO2 suspension polishing solutions. Ele-
mental analysis was conducted via energy-dispersive X-ray 
spectroscopy (EDS) at 15 kV using SEM.
To further investigate the microstructure under differ -
ent thermo-mechanical treatments, slices for TEM analysis 
were obtained from the cold rolling samples using elec -
trical discharge machining (EDM). The Al1Ti2 samples 
were mechanically polished to a thickness of about 50 μm, 
then prepared using a twin-jet electro-polished in an acetic 
acid solution with 10% perchloric acid at 25 mA and − 30 
℃. TEM tests were performed with a JEM-F200 TEM at 
200 kV .
Dog-bone-shaped quasi-tensile samples with gauge 
dimensions of 12 mm × 2 mm × 1.2 mm were cut from the 
Al1Ti2 plates along the cold rolling direction. Tensile tests 
were conducted at ambient temperature using uniaxial test -
ing equipment with an engineering strain rate of 1 × 10− 3 
s− 1. To ensure data consistency, three specimens were tested 
for each condition.
3 Results and Discussion
3.1 Microstructures after Cold Rolling
Following the homogenization process, the Al1Ti2 sam -
ple underwent multiple passes of cold rolling, resulting 
in a 40% reduction in thickness to induce severe plastic 
deformation. After cold rolling, the surface (ND plane) of 
Al1Ti2 samples are characterized using EBSD. As shown in 
Fig. 2a, most coarse grains are elongated in the cold rolling 
direction, while dynamic recrystallization (DRX) results in 
the formation of many fine grains along grain boundaries 
(GBs) to accommodate strain. Fig. 2b shows the micro-tex-
ture evolution in the as-rolled sample. The φ2 = 0°, 45°, 65°

ponents such as E ({111} <110>), Brass ({110} <112>), 
Goss ({110} <100>), and S ({123} <634>). Brass and S

textures, typically formed during rolling processing [ 47]. 
Moreover, the E component had the highest intensity among 
the other texture components.
TEM characterization of CR samples (Fig. 3a) confirms 
the formation of high-density dislocation substructures, 
including shear bands, dislocation walls, and dislocation 
tangles, which are induced by extensive plastic deforma -
tion. A relatively high fraction of ∑3{111}60° deformation 
twins (DTs) is observed in Fig. 3c. In addition to the abun -
dant DTs, Fig. 3d also reveals the presence of numerous 
nano-twins and stacking faults (SFs), along with their cor -
responding fast Fourier diffraction patterns (FFT).

4 Conclusions
In summary, an Al-Ti MEA with a heterogeneous micro -
structure was prepared via simple thermo-mechanical treat-
ment. The heterogeneous microstructure evolution and 
mechanical properties were extensively studied. This work 
provides a systematic investigation of the creation and for -
mation of a heterogeneous microstructure. The main con -
clusions of this work are as follows:
(1) A series of heterogeneous microstructures, including 
deformed coarse grains, recovery substructures, recrystal -
lized grains, and annealing twins, were prepared in Al1Ti2 
MEA by appropriate thermal-mechanical processing. These 
microstructures include deformed coarse grains, recovery 
substructures, recrystallized grains, and annealing twins. 
This processing approach allows us to tailor the microstruc-
ture, enhancing the alloy’s properties.
(2) The CR800 sample exhibits a bimodal grain size with 
abundant dislocations, twins, L-C locks, SF parallelepipeds, 
and L12 nanoprecipitates sub-structures. This demonstrates 
an excellent combination of strength and ductility, with a 
yield strength of approximately 1160 MPa and a ductility of 
29.3%. Additionally, this tailored microstructure enhances 
strain hardening, promoting uniform deformation and 
increasing tensile strength and strain hardening capacity 
under tension conditions.

3.5 Formation Rules of Heterogeneous 
Microstructures
Fig. 11 illustrates the different domains of the experimental 
Al1Ti2 MEA after rolling and annealing. Introducing two 
or more domains with varying rates of change is crucial 
for creating heterogeneous microstructures. These domains 
include recrystallized grains, recovery substructures, 
deformed coarse grains, twin fraction, and twin density, 
each exhibiting distinct temperature responses. As the tem -
perature increases, the proportion of deformed coarse grains 
and recovery substructures decreases, while the opposite 
occurs for recrystallized grains and twin boundary fractions. 
The twin boundary density (the length of total twin bound -
ary in relation to the area of EBSD) initially increases, 
reaching a maximum of 2.698 μm− 1 at 800 ℃, as depicted 
in Fig. 11. When the annealing temperature is below 700 ℃, 
the recrystallized grains are too small to form a soft domain 
that contributes to a heterogeneous microstructure. Con -
versely, when the annealing temperature exceeds 700 ℃, 
the growth of recrystallized grains, twin boundary fraction, 
and twin boundary density becomes too rapid to maintain 
the hard domain. Large grains provide ample slip systems 
for dislocation movement, while small grains offer abundant 
grain boundaries that impede dislocation motion.
