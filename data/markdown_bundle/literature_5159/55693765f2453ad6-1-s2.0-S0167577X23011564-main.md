1. Introduction 
Nine to 12% Cr martensitic steels are considered to be the perspec -
tive materials for the tubes, pipes, and blades of fossil fuel power plants 
with the work temperatures of 600 – 620 
◦
C and pressure of 25 – 30 MPa 
[1,2] . These steels have tempered martensite lath structure (TMLS) after 
normalizing with following tempering. TMLS is characterized by small 
width of martensitic laths and high dislocation density that is stabilized 
by M
23
C
6 
carbides and MX carbonitrides [1 – 3] . High strength of these 
steels is provided by linear sum strengthening contributions due to 
martensitic laths, dislocations, particles and alloying elements in matrix 
[3] . Refinement of M
23
C
6 
carbides having the orientation relationships 
(ORs) with martensitic laths is key feature to stabilize the lath structure 
[1,4] . However, high creep resistance of the 9 – 12 %Cr martensitic steels 
is limited by the creep strength breakdown appearance that can be 
overcome by change in the alloying or/and heat treatment. In the pre -
vious works [5 – 7] , the improved creep resistance of the Re-containing 
10 %Cr-3 %W steel is provided by the formation of dense chains of 
M
23
C
6 
carbides and Laves phase along the low-angle boundaries of 
TMLS, which retain ORs up to 10,000 h of creep at 650 
◦
C. The loss of 
ORs because of the growth of grain boundary particles after 10,000 h 
leads to creep strength breakdown appearance [5] . The change in 
alloying of the Re-containing 10% Cr steel by decrease in W content and 
addition of Cu improves the short-term creep resistance, but this does 
not overcome the loss in creep resistance after 10,000 h [8] . The change 
in heat treatment providing the growth of prior austenite grains (PAGs) 
up to 50 μ m or more [4,7,9] is found to be an effective method for 
improving creep resistance. The change in alloying of the new Re- 
containing 10 %Cr-2 %W-0.8 %Cu steel should be supplemented by 
change in NT with the aim to overcome the loss in creep resistance. The 
aim of the present research is to report on the effect of NT on the 
structure and tensile properties of the Re-containing 10 %Cr-2 %W-0.8 
%Cu steel and to choose optimal NT for further creep tests.

4. Conclusions 
An increase in NT of the Re-containing 10 %Cr steel to 1100 
◦
C 
provides the + 10% increase in both YS and UTS. This is caused by 
improved particle strengthening due to refinement of M
23
C
6 
and NbX. 
Further increase in NT to 1200 
◦

the structure and tensile properties.
