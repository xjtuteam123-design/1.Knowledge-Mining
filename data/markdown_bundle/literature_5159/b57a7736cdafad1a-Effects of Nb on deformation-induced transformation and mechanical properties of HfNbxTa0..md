ABSTRACT 
Body-centered cubic (BCC) refractory HEAs which have shown interesting properties at elevated temperatures 
such as outstanding high-temperature strength, high oxidation and corrosion resistance, nevertheless, they are 
generally brittle at ambient temperature, which limits their practical uses. Application of the transformation- 
induced plasticity (TRIP) concept into these BCC HEAs like HfTaTiZr has confirmed to be an effective 
approach to addressing this critical challenge. To further reveal the underlying mechanisms and improve the 
overall properties, in this work, we attempted to reveal the interplay between the phase stability, strain-induced 
transformation and deformation in HfNbxTa0.2TiZr (x = 0, 0.15, 0.20 and 0.25) HEAs. It was found that proper 
addition of Nb could not only enhance the TRIP effect due to the reduced phase stability of the prior BCC phase, 
but also facilitate twinning in the product HCP (hexagonal close packing) phase at the late stage of deformation. 
As a result, the HfNb0.2Ta0.2TiZr HEA shows much enhanced mechanical properties, i.e., pronounced work 
hardening behavior and large uniform ductility of up to 26%, which surmounts most of refractory BCC HEAs 
reported previously. Our findings are important not only for understanding the deformation mechanism of the 
TRIP reinforced HEAs, but also for the future development of other high-performance HEAs. 
1. Introduction 
Recently, high-entropy alloys (HEAs) have attracted extensive in -
terests because of their high thermal stability and promising mechanical 
properties such as high hardness, oxidation and corrosion resistance and 
outstanding low-temperature mechanical properties [ 1 – 8 ]. Among 
which, BCC (body-centered-cubic) HEAs based on refractory elements 
exhibit extremely stable microstructure against temperature, which 
endows them great potential for high-temperature application [ 9 , 10 ]. 
However, most refractory BCC HEAs suffer from poor ductility at room 
temperature, impeding their practical uses [ 11 , 12 ]. 
Recently, substantial property improvement has been realized in 
BCC HfNbTiZr HEAs via introduction of interstitials such as oxygen to 
form chemical short-range ordering, successfully overcoming the so- 
called strength-ductility tradeoff [ 13 ]. Another effective route is to 
apply the transformation-induced-plasticity (TRIP) concept, as demon -
strated in several BCC HEAs, i.e., Hf

3. Results 
3.1. Mechanical properties 
Fig. 1 a shows typical true stress-strain curves for the as-cast 
HfNbxTa0.2TiZr (x = 0, 0.15, 0.20 and 0.25) HEAs containing a 
different amount of Nb. An obvious change in the strength and ductility 
can be seen as the Nb concentration varies. Alloy Nb0.25 shows yield 
strength of 748 MPa, fracture strength of 843 MPa and ductility of 
14.2%. With the decrease of Nb content (i.e., Nb0.2), the fracture 
strength and plasticity appreciably increase to 983 MPa and 31.8%, 
respectively. Nevertheless, the yield strength decreases to 607 MPa. 
With further decrease of Nb (i.e., Nb0.15), the tensile curve displays a 
double yielding behavior, as frequently seen in shape memory alloys 
[ 22 , 23 ]. The yield strength is reduced as low as 406 MPa, although the 
fracture strength is over 1000 MPa. Note that the alloy with no Nb 
addition shows the highest yield and fracture strength of 1206 and 1315 
MPa, respectively, but with the least ductility of 4.1%. Clearly, the 
mechanical performance of the current HEAs was significantly affected 
by the Nb concentration, and a unique combination of strength and 
plasticity was obtained in alloy Nb0.2. 
Fig. 1 b shows curves of the corresponding strain-hardening rate (d σ / 
d ε ) deduced from Fig. 1 a. As can be seen, Nb0 and Nb0.25 show a 
continuously decreasing stain-hardening rate, which indicates a 
dislocation-slip-dominated plastic deformation mechanism [ 24 ].

3.2. Microstructure 
3.2.1. The as-cast alloys 
EBSD images and XRD patterns of the as-cast alloys are shown in 
Fig. 2 . With the increase of Nb, phase constitution exhibits a transition 
from a nearly single HCP phase (i.e., Nb0) to dual phases of BCC and 
HCP (hexagonal close packed), i.e., Nb0.15 and Nb0.2. The intensity of 
the HCP peaks on the XRD traces continues reducing while that of the 
BCC peaks keep growing as the Nb content increases, suggesting that 
formation of the HCP phase is suppressed but that of BCC is promoted. 
When the Nb element increased to 7.2 at.% (i.e., Nb0.25), few amount of 
the HCP phase was detected from the EBSD image, and no signal of HCP 
was captured by XRD due to their tiny amount. 
As an example, details of the dual-phase structure were carefully 
analyzed in alloy Nb0.2. As shown in Fig. 3 a, along with the selected 
area diffraction (SAD) patterns, it is confirmed that the as-cast Nb0.2 
alloy assumes a dual-phase structure of HCP and BCC, which is consis -
tent with the EBSD and XRD results. The pre-existing HCP phase is 
thermally induced by displacive transformation during the quenching 
process. Furthermore, elemental partitioning of the dual-phase structure 
was analyzed in Nb0.2 by energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS), 
and the corresponding results are shown in Fig. 3 b. Clearly, no apparent 
elemental segregation is observed, indicating a uniform distribution of 
all the elements, confirming a diffusionless martensitic transformation 
during solidification. Moreover, homogeneous distribution of all con -
stituent elements suggests that both phases benefit from the same level 
of solid solution strengthening. Fig. 3 c shows the crystallographic 
orientation relationship between the BCC and primary HCP phase, 
which was verified to be {110} BCC//{0001} HCP by the corresponding 
pole figures. 
3.2.2. The deformed alloys 
To analyze phase evolution during deformation, EBSD phase maps of 
the Nb0.2 samples pre-strained to 8%, 16% and fracture are shown in 
Fig. 4a1 -a3, revealing the deformation-induced martensitic 
transformation as deformation proceeds. Clearly, as the strain increases, 
interwove HCP lamella plates embedded in the BCC matrix ( Fig. 4a1 ) 
gradually multiplies and coarsens ( Fig. 4a2 ), and ultimately dominate 
the microstructure ( Fig. 4a3 ). Fig. 5 presents the relative volume frac -
tion of the BCC and HCP phase as a function of applied strain. It is 
obvious that the fraction of HCP is gradually increased during tensile 
loading, indicating occurrence of the phase transformation from the 
metastable BCC to HCP. Fig. 4b1 and b2 show the corresponding EBSD 
band contrast (BC) images of Nb0.2 alloy pre-strained to 8% and 16%, 
respectively. It can be seen that the fraction of twins within HCP also 
gradually increased, as indicated by the white arrow. 
In order to reveal the mechanism of the involved phase trans -
formation, microstructure of a fractured Nb0.2 sample was carefully 
investigated by TEM, and the results are shown in Fig. 6 . After the tensile 
deformation, numerous parallel lath-shaped structures distributed in the 
matrix appeared ( Fig. 6 a). The SAD patterns of these areas ( Fig. 6 b and 
c) reveal that they have a HCP lattice. In addition, the SAD patterns 
( Fig. 6 e) of the magnified lath-shaped structure interior ( Fig. 6 d) reveal 
existence of the { 01 11 } twinning spots, which suggests the trace of 
twinning in the HCP phase. 
Fig. 7 shows SEM images of the cross-section and lateral surfaces of 
the fractured specimens with different Nb concentrations. As can be 
seen, as the Nb content is decreased, the quantity of dimples, either el -
lipse or equiaxed, increases but the size decreases, suggesting a more 
ductile deformation behavior. Moreover, extensive slip/shear bands 
were observed inside the grains of alloys Nb0.25, Nb0.2 and Nb0.15, but 
sparsely in Nb0. 
4. Discussion

mation in these HEAs. Based on the EBSD map analysis ( Fig. 2 ), the 
relative volume fraction for BCC and HCP phases of all the alloys prior to

as the Nb concentration decreases, the propensity to form BCC declines

HEA with the highest Nb content about 7.2 at.% (i.e., Nb0.25) renders a 
single BCC and the formation of HCP is completely inhibited. When less 
Nb is added (e.g., Nb0.2), the volume fraction of BCC reduces while HCP 
starts to form. As the Nb content is further decreased (Nb0.15), the 
volume fraction of BCC is further reduced and becomes negligible in the

could be used as the key descriptor to evaluate the SFE, and a smaller 
VEC often indicates a lower SFE [ 34 , 35 ]. The calculations show that the

stage of deformation and then giving rise to strain hardening through 
the dynamic Hall – Petch effect, i.e., the interface density increases 
constantly through the continuously formed twins [ 14 , 37 ]. Therefore, 
both facilitated phase transformation and twinning contribute to the 
sustained and steady work hardening rate ( Fig. 1 b), which postpones the 
onset of necking instability and homogenized the plastic flow, as shown 
in Fig. 7 b and f. Compared with HfTa0.5TiZr, the fracture strength (983 
MPa) and plasticity (31.8%) of Nb0.2 are further increased due to the 
addition of Nb. 
When the Nb content is further decreased (i.e., Nb0.15), formation of 
the HCP is facilitated at the expense of BCC during solidification ( Fig. 2 ). 
From Fig. 1 b, it is clear that the strain hardening rate of this HEA can be 
divided into three stages; stage I has a classical decrease which was 
observed in all the HEAs corresponding to the conventional elastic to 
plastic transition and dislocation-slip-dominated plastic deformation 
[ 16 ]. Note that the BCC stability in this HEA is further reduced, resulting 
in a low yield strength (406 MPa) and a high phase transformation ratio 
of BCC (~100%). As a result, most of the strain at the early stage of 
plastic deformation is accommodated by the TRIP effect, leading to an 
increasing work hardening rate up to ε ≈ 6% in the stage II ( Fig. 1 b). As 
the strain increases, the hardening effect resulted from phase trans -
formation tends to saturate and deformation is again dominated by 
dislocation slip, which yields a gradual decrease trend in the stage III. It 
has been suggested that plastic strain is preferentially accommodated 
inside the deformation induced HCP martensite [ 18 ], rather than in the 
original HCP formed during cooling. As shown in Fig. 1 b, the work 
hardening rate in the latter part of stage III shows a slow decline 
behavior until zero, rendering the fracture strength and plasticity to 
1003 MPa and 25.8%, respectively. This observation suggests superior 
plastic deformation capacity of the deformation induced HCP in Nb0.15, 
which is actually confirmed by dense slip bands on the lateral surface 
( Fig. 7 g), and small and dense dimples on the fracture surface ( Fig. 7 c). 
Although the TRIP effect of Nb0.15 is more obvious as compared with 
Nb0.2 alloy ( Fig. 1 b), its performance is not the best. In this particular 
alloy, the fraction of the prior BCC phase is reduced but the potency of 
the martensitic transformation is excessively enhanced. As a result, the 
work hardening rate quickly reaches the maximum, i.e., at early defor -
mation stage, and then quickly exhausted due to the limited parent BCC 
phase, resulting in a reduced tensile strength and plasticity. 
Finally, Nb0 alloy with no Nb addition consists of a single stable HCP 
phase. It has been reported that the thermally induced HCP in FCC HEAs 
is harder and less deformable [ 18 ]. Therefore, due to the lack of addi -
tional deformation mechanism other than dislocation slips and weak 
plastic deformation capacity, the work-hardening rate curve of this HEA 
shows a sharp decrease trend ( Fig. 1 b), similarly to that of TiZrHfAlNb 
[ 38 ]. Hence, a high yield strength ( ≈ 1206 MPa) and fracture strength 
( ≈ 1315 MPa) but very limited plasticity ( ≈ 4.1%) were obtained, as 
shown in Fig. 1 a. Consequently, the corresponding fracture surface ex -
hibits pronounced intergranular and trans-granular mixed fracture 
mode while lateral surface shows sparse slip bands ( Fig. 7 d and h), 
indicating a less ductile deformation behavior. 
The uniform elongation indicates the degree that work hardening 
counteracts a reducing cross sectional area during tensile straining, 
which marks the onset of diffuse necking and has often been taken as 
guide for the extent of useful stretching [ 39 ]. However, BCC HEAs for 
which plasticity is exclusively driven by dislocation glide and present a 
very low work hardening rate, bringing about rapid strain localization 
and low uniform ductility levels [ 40 ]. Here, the newly designed Nb0.2 
HEA, benefitting from not only the TRIP effect but also the twinning 
within the deformation induced HCP, demonstrates outstanding uni -
form strain of up to 26% (see Fig. 1 c), which surmounts most of other 
BCC HEAs [ 15 , 40 ]. It is worth noting that, the phenomenon about the 
beneficial effect of deformation twinning on the uniform elongation also 
has been reported in HCP Mg and its alloys [ 39 ]. 
As discussed above, it is clear that BCC metastability strongly de -
pends on the Nb content and influences the preferential deformation 
modes. Careful tuning stability of the prior BCC phase and the defor -
mation capacity of the product HCP phases opens up new opportunities 
to investigate the TRIP effects and its link with the level of work hard -
enability in relevant HEAs [ 41 , 42 ]. In the future, it would be interesting 
to compare effects of Nb with those of other BCC-stabilizing elements (e. 
g., Mo, V and Ta) and then establish alloying principles for further 
optimizing the performance of these refractory HEAs. 
5. Conclusions 
In conclusion, this work investigated effects of the BCC-stabilizer Nb 
on the phase constitution, phase stability, work hardening and me -
chanical properties of the HfNbxTa0.2TiZr HEAs. The results revealed 
that the proportion and stability of prior BCC and phase transformation 
ratio of BCC during deformation are strongly dependent on the amount 
of Nb. Decreasing the Nb content reduces the BCC phase stability and 
promotes the martensitic transformation from BCC to HCP upon loading. 
Moreover, additional mechanical twinning in the product HCP phase 
was also activated due probably to the lower SFE resulted from the Nb 
addition, giving rise to an enhanced sustained strain hardening rate and 
thus a superior combination of strength (983 MPa) and uniform ductility 
( > 26%), which surmounts the level of most BCC refractory HEAs re -
ported previously. This work provides a useful approach to further 
enhancing the comprehensive properties of refractory BCC HEAs; care -
ful tuning the stability of prior BCC phase and deformation capacity of 
product HCP phase is effective in overcoming diffusive necking and 
obtaining large uniform ductility.
