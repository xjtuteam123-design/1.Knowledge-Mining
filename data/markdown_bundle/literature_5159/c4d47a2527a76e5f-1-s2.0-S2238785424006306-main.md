ABSTRACT 
Designing compositions for novel alloys requires a fundamental grasp of the role of each alloying element in its 
properties. This study delves into the influence of alloying elements in Ultra-High Strength Steels on essential 
high-temperature properties, including thermoelasticity, thermal stress, and strength, utilizing first-principles 
phonon calculations. These properties are pivotal for steels used in high-temperature applications. Our investi -
gation quantitatively forecasts the impact of alloying elements on elastic properties, providing valuable insights 
for advanced alloy design in high-temperature environments. The introduction of alloying elements markedly 
amplifies the elastic modulus of Fe-based alloys. The Ni, Mo, Cr, and C elements significantly enhance the 
anisotropy of the alloy. While refining elastic modulus and mechanical properties, alloying elements induce 
thermal stress, posing a potential risk of high-temperature cracking. The quantitative analysis underscores the 
robust strengthening potential for C, Ni, Mo, and W elements, with interstitial C emerging as pivotal in enhancing 
mechanical properties. The findings of this study illuminate the diverse roles played by different alloying ele -
ments and offer theoretical guidance for the design of advanced Ultra-High Strength Steels. 
1. Introduction 
With the rapid advancements in aerospace, energy, and other in -
dustries, there has been an increasing focus on the high-temperature 
applications of Ultra-High Strength Steels (UHSS). These steels have 
garnered attention due to their exceptional mechanical properties, 
including high strength, fracture toughness, and resistance to creep 
[ 1 – 4 ]. Among these steels, the high Co – Ni secondary hardening steels, 
such as M54 [ 5 – 8 ], hold great significance. These steels comprise 
various alloying elements such as Co, Ni, C, Mo, V, W, Cr, and C. The 
presence of these elements facilitates the formation of second phases, 
namely M
2
C with high Mo and Cr contents, and MC with elevated levels 
of V. These phases exhibit a strong coherency with the body-centered 
cubic (bcc) Fe structure and precipitate on a fine scale, thereby 
contributing to both high strength and fracture toughness [ 9 – 12 ]. 
Additionally, certain elements dissolve in the matrix, thereby enhancing 
solid solution strengthening, cohesive grain boundary strength, and 
improving the steel ’ s resistance to hydrogen embrittlement [ 13 ]. 
UHSS formed by a multiphase microstructure containing martensite, 
austenite, bainite, and/or retained austenite sufficient in quantities to 
produce high strength and durability while maintaining formability. The 
variety of alloys and processing combinations can achieve a wide range 
of strength, ductility, toughness, and fatigue properties. Nevertheless, 
the extensive composition range enormously hinders the design of UHSS 
because the extended cycles are often associated with the trial-and-error 
method, and constructing experimental conditions for service under 
high temperatures is demanding. Therefore, understanding the impact 
patterns of mechanical properties by alloying elements is the key to 
designing UHSS with outstanding performance. The basic phase kinds of 
UHSS, such as dual-phase steel, martensite steel, bake hardening steel, 
etc., are α -Fe structure with bcc structure. Moreover, the alloying ele -
ments are usually treated as the solution soluted in the bcc Fe phase. 
Therefore, A quick design idea for UHSS is to understand the influence of 
each element on the mechanical properties of the matrix phase. How -
ever, limited research has been conducted on the response of bcc Fe, 
especially at high temperatures [ 14 ]. Achieving the realization of this 
concept through experimental means poses a formidable challenge, as it 
necessitates surmounting the influence of impurity elements, namely 
oxygen (O), nitrogen (N), and hydrogen (H), on the mechanical prop -
erties of steel. Despite their relatively low concentrations, the signifi -
cance of these elements cannot be overlooked, given their propensity to 
typically occupy interstitial positions. It can be solved by building a

Moreover, through meticulous exploration at the atomic level, we can

exert influence on the mechanical properties. This elucidation serves as

The alloying elements within the bcc matrix of UHSS predominantly 
function as solutes. The augmentation of mechanical properties attrib -
uted to these solute elements hinges significantly upon the solid- 
solution-strength (SSS) effects [ 15 ]. Hence, attaining a comprehensive 
comprehension of the SSS effects exerted by alloying elements in bcc Fe 
is imperative for advancing the mechanical properties of UHSS [ 16 ]. 
Significant progress has been achieved in understanding and quantifying 
the SSS effect of solute atoms, particularly concerning the hindrance of 
dislocation motion attributed to lattice misfit and modulus misfit be -
tween solute and matrix. Various models, including the strong-pinning 
model proposed by Friedel [ 17 ] and Fleischer [ 18 , 19 ], as well as the 
weak-pinning model by Labusch [ 20 , 21 ], have been employed to 
elucidate these effects. Recent enhancements to the Labusch model [ 22 , 
23 ], incorporating the Peierls-Nabarro model, have leveraged advances 
in computing capabilities. These models rely on material parameters 
such as lattice constants, shear modulus, or stacking fault energy for 
quantitative predictions of the SSS effect, underscoring the importance 
of detailed quantification of composition-dependent fundamental 
properties for optimizing material performance or predicting reliable 
SSS effects [ 24 ]. 
In this work, through meticulous first-principles calculations 
grounded in density functional theory, we have discerned the ground 
state properties of a bcc-structured binary Fe-based alloy at absolute 
zero (0K). These properties span a spectrum, encompassing energy, 
volume, magnetic moment, and pivotal elastic attributes, such as elastic 
constants and modulus. Employing the quasi-harmonic approximation 
method, we further delved into the realm of phonon calculations, 
enabling a nuanced exploration of the alloy ’ s thermodynamic charac -
teristics across a range of temperatures. Subsequent to this, employing 
the quasi-static approximation method, we quantified the alloy ’ s elastic 
properties at finite temperatures. Drawing from these findings, we 
engaged in a comprehensive discussion, shedding light on the alloy ’ s 
elastic temperature coefficient, thermal stresses, and the solid solution 
strengthening effects imparted by alloying elements. Our objective is to 
delineate the influence patterns of individual alloying elements on the 
mechanical properties of the Fe matrix. To achieve this, we utilize a 
model grounded in the Fe-based dilute model, deliberately overlooking 
interactions among alloying elements. This methodology stands as an 
archetypal guide for the judicious selection of alloying elements, steer -
ing the optimization of mechanical properties within the matrix phase of 
UHSS, with a particular emphasis on elevated temperatures.

All first-principles calculations are performed on the basis of the 
density functional theory (DFT) as implemented in the Vienna Ab Initio

augmented wave (PAW) [ 43 ] pseudopotentials and the generalized

(PBEsol) [ 44 , 45 ] are used for the exchange-correlation functional. After 
a strict convergence test, a plane-wave kinetic energy cutoff of 500 eV is 
adopted. And 15 × 15 × 15 k-mesh is chosen as the Monkhorst-Pack 
uniform k-point sampling in the Brillouin zone. The structure is fully

calculations are conducted at seven volumes around the 0 K equilibrium 
volume with a volume interval of 4.5%. 
The phonon calculations are performed using a finite differences 
approach. The supercell size is composed of 2 × 2 × 2 alignment of cubic 
bcc unit cell, where each unit cell includes two Fe atoms. The schematic 
diagram of the magnetic moments of the ferromagnetic and para -
magnetic states is given in Fig. 1 (a). The calculations primarily employ 
the exact muffin-tin orbital (EMTO) method in conjunction with the full- 
charge-density technique. This approach is implemented within the 
framework of Green ’ s function formalism [ 17 ] and can effectively 
handle alloys with chemical and/or magnetic disorder using the 
coherent potential approximation [ 46 ]. EMTO employs the muffin-tin 
approximation, dividing the crystal potential into smooth and radial 
components, allowing for precise electronic structure descriptions near 
atomic cores. In contrast, conventional DFT utilizes plane wave basis 
sets and treats the crystal potential as a continuous function, sacrificing 
some accuracy near atomic cores for computational efficiency. In this 
work, the EMTO method is mainly used to calculate the elastic constants 
of iron-based alloys after paramagnetic transition. The mechanical and 
thermodynamic properties of ferromagnetic Fe-based alloys are realized 
by PAW-PBE. To validate the accuracy of our EMTO calculations for 
ferromagnetically ordered Fe. For the EMTO-coherent potential

3.1. Lattice parameters and magnetic moments 
Energy and volume calculations are initially conducted for Fe-based 
alloys featuring a spectrum of solid-soluble alloying elements. The 
outcomes are illustrated by the circles in Fig. 2 (a), where distinct colors 
delineate different solid-soluble elements. Subsequently, the energy- 
volume relationship are clarified through fitting procedures utilizing 
the BM-EOS, as plotted by the solid line in Fig. 2 (a). In accordance with 
the fitting outcomes, the data point with the minimum energy along the 
E-V curve aligns with the equilibrium volume of the alloy in its ground 
state. The computational results indicate that the substitution of solid 
solution atoms noticeably increases the equilibrium volume of the Fe 
matrix. Furthermore, Fig. 2 (b) illustrates the variation of the magnetic 
moment of the binary alloy with volume. The results demonstrate that 
elements such as Mo, Cr, W, and V significantly reduce the magnetic 
moment of alloys. This phenomenon is ascribed to the doping of these 
elements, inducing an expansion in the alloy volume and subsequently 
giving rise to a monotonic augmentation in the magnetic moment. In 
contrast, although Ni and Co lead to a marginal increase in volume, their 
robust ferromagnetic properties contribute to an enhancement in the 
alloy ’ s magnetic moment.

4. Conclusions 
In this study, we employed first-principles calculations alongside 
quasiharmonic and quasistatic approaches to investigate the elastic and 
thermal properties of bcc Fe-based alloys. The phonon spectrum analysis 
confirmed the structural stability, with C exhibiting pronounced har -
monic vibration, while the other alloying elements had minimal effects 
on enhancing vibrations. The results revealed that the solid solution of 
alloying elements increases the elastic modulus of Fe-based alloys, with 
the interstitial solid solution of C showing more significant enhance -
ment. Additionally, Ni, Mo, Cr, and C elements notably enhance the 
anisotropy of the Fe-based alloy. Furthermore, the solid solution of 
alloying elements reduces the temperature dependence of ferroalloy 
elasticity considerably. 
Thermal stress calculations indicated that while alloying elements 
enhance the elastic modulus of Fe-based alloys, thereby improving their 
mechanical properties, they also elevate the thermal stress at high 
temperatures, leading to potential cracking under such conditions. 
Finally, a quantitative description of solid solution strengthening 
demonstrated that C, Ni, Mo, and W elements exhibit high strengthening 
potentials due to their large misfit factors, indicating substantial 
strengthening abilities. Remarkably, the solid solution of the interstitial 
atom C resulted in a significantly higher increment compared to other 
solid solution atoms, emphasizing its significant role in enhancing the 
alloy ’ s mechanical properties.
