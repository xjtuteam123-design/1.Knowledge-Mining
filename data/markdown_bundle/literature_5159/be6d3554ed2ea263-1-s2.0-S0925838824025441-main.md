1. Introduction
Achieving a higher temperature in turbine engines is crucial for
improving their thermal efficiency, which is severely restricted by
traditional turbine engine alloys due to their limited service tempera-
tures [1 – 3] . Meanwhile, it is crucial for high-temperature alloys to
possess strong resistance to oxidation. Unfortunately, many
high-temperature alloys generally contain refractory metal elements,
such as Ta, Zr, Hf, W, and V, which exhibit poor oxidation resistance.
Previous studies have revealed that the V element is preferentially
oxidized to volatile V
2
O
5
[4] , and elements like Nb and Ta are severely
oxidized above 400
◦
C [5] . Besides, group IV (Ti, Zr, and Hf) metal ele-
ments tend to bond with oxygen, which typically exists in the form of
interstitial atoms or combines with metal atoms to create metal oxides.
This results in severe oxidation of the alloy, further leading to a signif-
icant degradation of its mechanical properties in high-temperature en-
vironments [6] . As a result, it is essential to develop new
high-temperature structural materials that achieve a favorable combi-
nation of high-temperature strength, ductility, and oxidation resistance.
In this regard, Senkov et al. [7] first proposed refractory high entropy
alloys (RHEAs) for high-temperature applications based on the idea of
high entropy alloys (HEAs). As potential high-temperature alloys,
RHEAs have received widespread attention owing to their excellent
high-temperature strength and thermal stability. For instance, MoN-
bTaW and MoNbTaVW RHEAs have demonstrated yield strengths of
405 MPa and 477 MPa, respectively, at the high temperature of 1600
◦
C
[8,9] . Furthermore, Han et al. [10] reported a novel TiNbMoTaW RHEA,
which exhibited remarkable phase stability even after annealing at
1200
◦
C for 24 hours. However, the high density and cost of these RHEAs
have limited their industrial applications. Recent studies have demon-
strated that rational compositional design can achieve a desirable me-
chanical performance. As an example, Li et al. [11] designed a
TiNbMo
0.5
Al
0.225
RHEA with low density and excellent mechanical
properties at both high and room temperatures by optimizing the
composition. In this context, the rational compositional design is a valid
way to develop new RHEAs; hence, we will systematically investigate
the impacts of alloying elements on the mechanical properties of RHEAs
in this research.

In addition to mechanical properties, oxidation resistance is also
vital for RHEAs in actual applications. As previously mentioned, RHEAs
containing Ti, Zr, Nb, etc., generally exhibit poor oxidation resistance,
which needs to be further improved to promote safer and more extensive
use in high-temperature environments.
To the best of our knowledge, the common antioxidant mechanism of
RHEAs is to form a dense protective oxide layer on the surface by the
rational compositional design. So far, adding elements such as Cr, Al, or
Si seems to be an effective way to improve the high-temperature
oxidation resistance of RHEAs [12 – 16] . For example, Gorr et al. [17]
reported that the addition of Si to NbMoAlCrTi RHEAs results in the
formation of a protective SiO
2
oxide layer at the interface between the
metal substrate and oxide scale, which prevents oxygen diffusion and
further improves oxidation resistance. In addition, the SiO
2
acts as a
nucleation site for the formation of a homogeneous protective oxide film
that inhibits oxygen and metal ion diffusion. In a similar vein, Pei et al.
[18] investigated the impact of Al and Si on the oxidation behavior of
TiZrV
0.5
Nb
0.5
RHEA at 1000
◦
C and found that the addition of Si pro-
motes the formation of a homogeneous, continuous, and highly adherent
protective layer of niobium oxide on the oxidized surface. and, intro-
ducing Al results in the formation of an Al
2
O
3
protective layer. This
compact structure effectively inhibits the inward diffusion of oxygen.
Accordingly, the rational compositional design is also an effective
method to develop new RHEAs with superior oxidation resistance.

balance with a precision of 0.1 mg to measure the weight of each
oxidized specimen. To minimize errors, we selected three specimens

4.2. The formation mechanism of the oxides
Oxidation products provide crucial physical evidence for under-
standing the oxidation process of alloys and judging the diffusion
behavior of elements. Therefore, it is vital to study the mechanism of
oxide formation, particularly in RHEAs, as it differs from pure metals.
Each component in RHEAs forms one or more oxides at high tempera-
tures, and some oxides sublimate at high temperatures, while others are
unstable and decompose at elevated temperatures. Mutual dissolution
between the oxides should also be taken into consideration. Addition-
ally, simple oxides can transform into complex oxides through solid-
phase reactions. The mechanism of oxide formation in RHEA is
believed to be controlled by oxidative adsorption equilibrium, nucle-

in the alloy and the standard free energy of oxide formation [28] .

4.3. Oxidation-resistant mechanism

it has been observed that incorporating more Si content in the alloy
enhances its ability to resist oxidation. In the base alloy Si0, the alloy
exhibits a distinct linear oxidation, while the addition of Si elements
causes the alloys to transition from linear to parabolic oxidation. For
linear oxidation kinetics, the oxidation process is controlled by the rate
of the gas-metal interface reaction, which indicates that the oxide scale
is porous and unprotective. Conversely, parabolic oxidation kinetics
suggest that the oxide scale ’ s element diffusion becomes the primary
factor controlling the rate, in which case a relatively dense and pro-
tective oxide scale is formed to reduce the oxidation rate [41,42] .
