ABSTRACT
Cracking defects forming during additive manufacturing (AM) process in Ni superalloys have posed great 
challenge in application, since the spatial distributions of AM cracks are generally complex and lack regularity. In 
this work, investigations on the mechanisms governing the spatial distribution of cracks have been conducted 
based on a self-developed Ni superalloy AMS-nDB (Additive Manufacturable Superalloys with no Distribution of 
boron). The microstructural features including grain size, grain boundary misorientation, precipitates, and pri -
mary dendrite arm spacing (PDAS) distribution of have been characterized and simulated systematically. The AM 
cracks in as-printed samples are observed to locate exclusively in the bottom region in vicinity of the substrate. 
By excluding the possible effect of residual stress, grain size and grain boundary misorientation, the formation of 
unevenly distributed cracks in the alloy has been attributed to the heterogeneous precipitation behaviors. More 
specifically, detrimental Cr
2
B borides precipitate in the bottom region of the samples along the crack path, 
leading to the hot-tear cracking. The formation of borides is due to the different elemental segregation behaviors 
along the building direction, originating from the variations in the temperature gradient. Based on the unveiled 
cracking mechanism, the printing strategy has been optimized accordingly, which manage to manipulate the 
precipitation behaviors and effectively eliminate the cracking. The findings provide guidance for microstructure 
optimization and cracking suppression in AM-ed Ni superalloys.
1. Introduction
Ni superalloys exhibit excellent creep resistance and decent corro -
sion resistance at elevated operating temperatures, making them 
extensively applied in turbine blades for aerospace engines and gas 
turbines [ 1 – 4 ]. As the demand for aircraft engine components continues 
to rise, the geometric complexity of these components also increases. 
Meanwhile, the inherent high strength and significant work-hardening 
rates of nickel superalloys, pose challenges for conventional 
machining methods [ 5 – 7 ]. As an alternative, the technique of additive 
manufacturing (AM) can provide fewer processing steps with greater 
geometric freedom and higher material efficiency, showing great ad -
vantages in terms of energy savings and cost reduction [ 8 – 11 ]. However, 
the application of AM for high performance Ni superalloys remains 
challenging due to the high cracking susceptibility during AM process, 
which can severely impair the performance of these alloys [ 12 – 14 ].
Extensive research has been conducted on the cracking mechanisms 
in AM-ed Ni superalloys, which may potentially take place during so -
lidification or solid states. Chauvat et al. found that high-angle grain 
boundaries (HAGBs, misorientation > 15
◦

cracking rather than low-angle grain boundaries (LAGBs, misorientation

γ - γ ′ eutectic precipitates [ 16 , 17 ], γ -Laves eutectic precipitates [ 18 ], 
carbides [ 19 ] and borides [ 15 , 20 ], can form remelted liquid films, 
leading to the formation of cracks during AM solidification or remelting. 
Furthermore, the precipitation of the γ’ phase results in the accumula -
tion of residual stress and a reduction in the plasticity of the matrix, 
which can also facilitate the formation of cracks [ 21 ]. Namely, the 
cracking behaviors of Ni superalloys during AM process are primarily 
determined by the microstructure of the as-printed states.

cess, the as-printed microstructure of Ni superalloys generally present 
significant spatial heterogeneity. For instance, Li et al. reported that in

In this work, systematic investigations on the mechanisms governing 
the spatial distribution of cracks and the correlation with the as-printed 
microstructure of Ni superalloys have been conducted. The micro -

precipitates, and PDAS distribution of a self-developed Ni superalloy 
AMS-nDB have been characterized and simulated in details. The origins 
of the unevenly distributed microstructures and the underlying cracking 
mechanisms were revealed as well. Accordingly, the printing strategies 
have been successfully optimized and applied to reach the crack-free 
samples. Current results in this work shed light on the microstructure 
optimization and cracking suppression in AM-ed Ni superalloys.

3. Results
3.1. The spatial distribution and characteristic of cracks
Fig. 3 (a) shows the OM splices in the transverse section of the etched 
AMS-nDB sample. The local metallographic images showing the defects 
and dendrite morphology at various heights along the building direction 
are presented in Fig. 3 (b – g), corresponding to the red and black boxes in 
Fig. 3 (a). The dendritic morphology of the samples varies slightly with 
height, with dendrite size showing a trend of first increasing and then 
decreasing. A detailed analysis of the dendritic morphology will be 
presented in Section 4.3 . It is apparent that the cracks are exclusively 
distributed in the bottom region (g region) of the sample, in the vicinity 
of substrate with a maximum crack length less than 1 mm. The obser -
vation of site-specific distribution of cracks in AMS-nDB alloy deserves 
some further rationalization, while the corresponding microstructure as 
well as the underlying mechanism are also worth exploring.
Cracks in AM-ed Ni superalloy are typically classified into two cat -
egories according to the formation mechanisms: (i) hot-tear cracks 
(occurs during the solid-liquid phase transition) and (ii) strain-ageing 
cracks (occurs in the solid-state phase transition stage) [ 15 , 21 , 28 , 29 ]. 
The typical morphology of hot-tear cracks can be characterized by 
asymmetrical sides, which exhibit solidification dendrite arms 
morphology with large gaps between the separation surfaces (attributed 
to insufficient liquid feeding and residual stress), and precipitates along 
crack path, which are associated with the remelting of 
low-melting-point phases. In contrast, strain-ageing cracks present a 
straight, symmetrical morphology and lack both precipitates and den -
dritic structures. The morphology of the cracks was characterized in 
detail to identify the crack formation mechanisms, as shown in Fig. 4 . 
Fig. 4 (a – b) shows the large gap between the two cleavage surfaces of the 
crack with asymmetric edges, presenting the typical characteristics of 
hot-tear cracks. Meanwhile, no typical strain-ageing cracks features 
were found in the sample. Fig. 4 (c) shows a large number of film-like 
precipitates distributed along the crack path (marked by white ar -
rows). The cracks in Fig. 4 (d) exhibit the morphology of solidification 
dendrite arms (indicated by yellow arrows) confirming the presence of 
hot tear cracking. A detailed discussion on the formation mechanisms of 
hot-tear cracks will be presented in the subsequent sections.
3.2. The spatial distribution of primary precipitates
To elucidate the relationship between the unevenly distributed 
cracks with the microstructure, the site-specific phases and corre -
sponding element mappings were characterized by SEM, as shown in 
Fig. 5 . In the crack region, as shown in Fig. 5 (f) (corresponding to region 
g in Fig. 3 (a)), nearly equiaxed precipitates (marked by green arrows) 
are observed to precipitate near the crack and along the crack path, 
which are enriched in Cr and poor in Nb, Ta, or Al. As the building height 
increases ( Fig. 5 (e), corresponding to region f in Fig. 3 (a)), the number 
of cracks significantly decreases, accompanied by changes in the pre -
cipitate morphology. Specifically, the Cr-rich precipitates gradually 
decrease, and Nb and Ta-rich phases emerge. The precipitate 
morphology transitions from nearly equiaxed to a combination of 
equiaxed and stripe-like structures. Fig. 5 (a – d) shows the morphology 
and elemental distribution of the precipitates in the middle and top re -
gions of the sample, where no cracks are observed (corresponding to 
regions b-e in Fig. 3 (a)). The precipitates are solely Nb-rich and Ta-rich 
phases, distributed in the interdendritic regions, while the Cr-rich phase 
is no longer present. Furthermore, the presence of γ’ strengthening 
phases was observed at various heights. The pronounced correlation 
between the spatial distribution of precipitates and cracks ( Figs. 3 and 5 ) 
suggests that heterogeneous precipitation behavior may be the origin of 
the uneven distributed cracks in the AMS-nDB Ni superalloys.

to segregation. Element segregation behavior significantly influences 
the precipitation behavior of phases [ 42 , 43 ]. Therefore, it can be 
considered that the refined dendrite structure with low PDAS at the 
bottom region results in a weak element segregation, The lower 
concentration of Nb and Ta in the inter-dendrite zones suppresses the 
precipitation of (Nb, Ta)C. The formation of low-melting-point Cr ₂ B

the building height increases, the larger PDAS exacerbates Nb and Ta 
segregation, promoting (Nb, Ta)C formation and suppressing Cr ₂ B pre -

impact of element content on the (NB, Ta)C phase transformation 
driving force, calculated by Thermo-Calc as shown in Fig. S4 , also sup -

an important factor contributing to the occurrence of cracks in the alloy 
[ 44 , 45 ]. Finite element simulation of the stress distribution in the 
original samples was performed, and the results are shown as the black 
line in Fig. S5 . It can be seen that although stress decreases with 
increasing building height, the maximum stress in the crack-free regions 
shows only a slight reduction compared to the cracked regions, the 
similar residual stress levels in cracked and crack-free regions suggest 
that residual stress is not the primary factor contributing to the uneven 
distribution of cracks in the alloy. Therefore, it can be suggested that in 
AMS-nDB alloys, residual stress may not be the primary factor influ -
encing the cracking. In conclusion, the fundamental cause of the het -
erogeneous precipitation behavior is the uneven temperature gradient 
resulting from the unique thermal history of the L-DED process. Un -
derstanding the mechanisms behind the uneven distribution of the 
microstructure can provide valuable guidance for subsequent process 
optimization.
4.4. Substrate preheating
The abovementioned results indicate that the primary factor 
contributing to the uneven crack distribution is the variation in pre -
cipitates caused by the non-uniform temperature gradient. Thus, regu -
lating the temperature gradient during the AM process may inhibit the 
precipitation of Cr ₂ B, thereby mitigating cracking behaviors. To this 
end, substrate preheating was employed to reduce the temperature 
gradient at the bottom of the alloy, aiming to decrease the incidence of 
cracks in the samples. In this work, the substrate preheating was oper -
ated by scanning the substrate with laser before building the sample, 
without powder feeding. To determine an appropriate temperature 
gradient, simulations are necessary to identify the optimal preheating 
process. Prior to the experiments, the temperature gradient in the bot -
tom region of the alloy with different numbers of scanning layers was 
simulated and the results are shown in Fig. 12 . The maximum temper -
ature gradient in the crack-free region was used as the target tempera -
ture gradient for the experiment. The target temperature gradient,

gradient at the bottom of the alloy, leading to an increase in PDAS in this 
region and subsequently influencing elemental segregation behavior.

differences in the types of precipitates, the reduction in stress is not 
considered the main reason for crack inhibition.
5. Conclusion
In this study, AMS-nDB Ni superalloy samples were fabricated using 
L-DED technique. The factors contributing to the unevenly distributed 
AM cracks, as well as the underlying mechanisms have been clarified. 
Correspondingly, the printing strategies have been optimized and 
applied to reach the crack-free samples. The main conclusions are 
summarized as follows. 
(1) In the L-DED state, the cracks are confined to the bottom region in 
the vicinity of the substrate. The cracks exhibited the morphology 
with solidification dendrite arms accompanied with the film-like 
precipitates along the crack path, which are identified as hot-tear 
cracks forming during solidifications.

(2) The formation of unevenly distributed cracks is primarily

(3) The different precipitation behaviors in the as-printed micro -
structure can be ascribed to the different element segregations 
behaviors, which is due to the variations in the temperature 
gradient along the building direction. Differences in the tem -
perature gradient induce the changes in PDAS as well as the 
segregation behavior of Nb and Ta, which ultimately leading to 
the different precipitations along the building heights.
(4) Based on the unveiled cracking mechanism, substrate preheating 
was employed to reduce the temperature gradient at the bottom 
of the alloy. Results show that reheating process can effectively 
eliminate cracking. The formation of detrimental Cr ₂ B borides 
has been effectively suppressed and (Nb, Ta)C precipitates as the 
alternative, along with the PDAS increased from 9.63 to 17.06 
μ m. The results manage to corroborate the proposed mechanism 
linking temperature gradient, precipitate distribution, and crack 
formation, which can provide guidance for microstructure opti -
mization and cracking suppression in AM-ed Ni superalloys.
