Abstract
The microstructure and mechanical beha vior of se veral Nb /C1/Mo /C1/ZrC alloys were in vestigated from room to ele vated
temperatures. Alloys with different compositions were prepared by arc-melting and homogenized. The resulting microstructure
was characterized by optical microscopy, X-ray diffraction and electron probe microanalysis. Compression tests were performed to
determine the stress /C1
/strain curve and 0.2% flow stress of each alloy. The results indicated the compressi ve yield strengths of Nb /C1/
ZrC alloys were impro ved significantly with Mo addition from room to ele vated temperature. The impro vement of strength in the
Nb /C1/Mo /C1/ZrC alloy is mainly attributed to solid solution strengthening of the matrix.

1. Introduction
Niobium-based alloys are one of the most promising
refractory alloys for high temperature structural appli-
cations. Howe ver, the strength of niobium decreases
substantially at temperature abo ve 1200 K. As a result,
various approaches ha ve been used to impro ve the
strength of niobium at ele vated temperatures. These
include solid solution strengthening [1,2], composite
strengthening with intermetallic compounds such as
Nb
3Al, Nb 3Ir and Nb 5Si3 [3 /C1/7], as well as composite
strengthening using carbide phase [8 /C1/10]. In a pre vious
work reported by Tan et al., the effect of alloys
composition on microstructure and high temperature
properties of Nb /C1
/ZrC ternary system were studied [11].
The results showed that the 0.2% flow stress of the Nb /C1/
ZrC alloys increases with increasing ZrC content from
room temperature to ele vated temperatures. Howe ver,
the compressi ve ductility and fracture toughness de-
crease with increasing ZrC content. Also, the strength
improvement of Nb /C1
/ZrC alloys at ele vated tempera-
tures is not adequate. As a result, the addition of Mo is
considered in order to further impro ve the high tem-
perature strength of the Nb /C1/ZrC alloy. Mo was selected
because it may improve the strength of Nb alloy through
solid solution strengthening, and Mo is not easy to form
carbide.
The study is to report the results of an investigation to
examine the potential of Nb /C1
/Mo /C1/ZrC system as a new
heat-resistant alloy. The mechanical properties of se v-
eral Nb /C1/Mo /C1/ZrC alloys were in vestigated from room
temperature to 1773 K.
2. Experimental procedure
Nominal compositions of the alloys in vestigated in
this study were listed in Table 1. The ZrC contents in the
alloys were fixed to 20 and 40 at.%, respecti vely, and
with varying Nb and Mo concentration. High purity Nb
(99.9% pure), Mo (99% pure) and ZrC (95% pure)
powders were used as the starting materials. All alloys,
each about 25 g, were prepared by arc-melting under
argon. The mass loss before and after melting was found
to be less than 0.1 mass%. The ingots were homogenized
at 2073 K for 24 h in an argon atmosphere, followed by
furnace cooling. Microstructure and compositions of the
alloys were examined by optical microscopy, X-ray

4. Discussion
The effects of alloy composition on the microstructure
and mechanical behavior of several Nb /C1/Mo/C1/ZrC alloys
were examined in this study. From microstructure
observation, it is known that the microstructure of
Nb /C1/Mo /C1/20at.% ZrC and Nb /C1/Mo /C1/40at.% ZrC alloys

from the other alloys in this study. In Mo /C1/20at.% ZrC
and Mo /C1/40at.% ZrC alloys, the microstructure is
composed of ZrC and eutectic of Mo and ZrC. Suzuki
et al., reported the microstructure in the eutectic area by
TEM [12], it was reported that fine ZrC particles with
different sizes and irregular shapes, which are formed
through the eutectic reaction, are dispersed intergranu-

The results obtained from this study clearly illustrate
that the compressi ve yield strength of Nb /C1/ZrC alloy
was improved significantly with Mo addition from room
to ele vated temperature. Generally, the strength of a
two-phase alloy depends on the volume ratio, shape,
distribution of these two phases, and the strength of
each phase. Since the volume fraction and shape of
carbide phase was kept to be constant when the ZrC
concentration is fixed, the impro vement of strength in
the Nb /C1
/Mo /C1/ZrC alloy is mainly attributed to solid
solution strengthening of the matrix. It is ob vious the
Mo exhibits a strong solid solution strengthening effect
on the strength of (Nb, Mo)
ss. Tan et al. ha ve reported
that the high temperature strength of Nb /C1/Mo alloy
increases with increasing Mo content even at 1873 K due
to solid solution strengthening [1,2]. It appears that the
maximum solid solution effects can be achie ved when
the Nb/Mo ratio is around 1. The relationship between
the alloy composition and high temperature strength
obtained from this study can be used to design Nb /C1
/
Mo /C1/ZrC alloys for high temperature applications.
5. Conclusions
In the present work, the microstructure and high
temperature strength of Nb /C1/Mo /C1/ZrC alloys were
investigated, the results are summarized as follows:
(1) The microstructure of Nb /C1/Mo /C1/20at.% ZrC and
Nb /C1/Mo /C1/40at.% ZrC alloys are composed of (Nb,
Mo)ss and (Zr, Nb)C, respecti vely.
(2) The microstructure of Mo /C1/20at.% ZrC and Mo /C1/
40at.% ZrC alloys is composed of primarily-solidified
ZrC and eutectic of Mo and ZrC. Both alloys exhibit a
hyper-eutectic structure.
(3) The compressi ve yield strength of Nb /C1
/ZrC alloy
was impro ved significantly with Mo addition at room
and elevated temperature. The impro vement of strength
in the Nb /C1/Mo /C1/ZrC alloy is mainly attributed to solid
solution strengthening of the matrix.
(4) In the Nb /C1/Mo /C1/20at.% ZrC alloys, the Nb /C1/
40at.% Mo /C1/20at.% ZrC (with Nb/Mo ratio is about 1)
consistently exhibits the highest strength from room to
elevated temperatures.
