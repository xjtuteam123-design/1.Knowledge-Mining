abstract
The tensile properties and microstructure of hot extruded GW84 alloy stretching at 200 /C14 C,
250 /C14 C and 300 /C14 C were investigated. The results showed that the alloys had excellent high
temperature mechanical properties, when stretching at 250 /C14 C, the ultimate tensile
strength of alloy reached peak value, 393 MPa, and the tensile strength stretching at 300 /C14 C
reached 354 MPa. During high temperature tensile process, the microstructure markedly
changed, which is not recognised in previous researches. Stretching at 200
/C14 C, the pre-
cipitate phase was still metastable b0 phase. However, when stretching at 250 /C14 C, the nano
b0 precipitate transformed into nano b phase ( b0 / b), and the precipitation mechanism is
mainly inoculation nucleation of b phase. When stretching at 300 /C14 C, the number of b
precipitates increased signiﬁcantly at triangular grain boundaries, which grew up to sub-
micron particles, and precipitation mechanism is mainly growth and coarsening.

1. Introduction
Magnesium (Mg) alloys have many excellent properties, such
as low density, high speciﬁc strength, good electromagnetic
shielding and shock absorption, and have been widely used in
ﬁelds like transportation industry, military and electronics
products [ 1e3]. At present, Mg eAl system alloys, like AZ91,
AZ80 and AM60, are currently main achieving large-scale
commercial application alloys [ 4e6]. However, applications
for Mg eAl system are limited by the poor elevated tempera-
ture properties due to b-Mg
17Al12 and the eutectic temperature
of b phase is only 437 /C14 C, which can not effectively inhibit
grain boundary slip at high temperature [ 7,8]. When service
temperature exceeds 125 /C14 C, the strength of Mg eAl system
alloys decreases rapidly, therefore, they are difﬁcult to be used
in key parts of automobile engines and transmission mecha-
nisms [9,10].

not been clearly and emphatically studied. In this paper, the
microstructure and tensile strength of hot extruded Mg-8Gd-
4Y-1Sm-0.5Zr (wt%) alloy under T5 (peak-aging) condition
were analyzed, which could provide important theoretical
guidance for controlling mechanical properties and heat
resistance mechanism of Mg-Gd-Y alloys at high temperature.
2. Materials and methods
The original as-cast GW84 (Mg-8Gd-4Y-1Sm-0.5Zr, wt%) ingots
were prepared using pure Mg (99.95%), Mg eGd (30%), Mg eY
(30%) and Mg eZr (30%) (wt%) master alloys. These master al-
loys were heated in a crucible after removing surface stains
and oxide layer and the CO
2 and SF6 with a volume ratio of 99 :
1 were introduced simultaneously as protective gases. After
these materials were heated to 760
/C14 C for 10 min, the molten
alloy was cast into a steel mold with diameter of F 80 mm and
cooled to form GW84 ingot. The disc plate samples with F
50 /C2 45 mm were cut from the ingots, which were heated at
525 /C14 C for 8 h and then quenched into cold water. Before
extrusion, the samples were hold at 500 /C14 C for 30 min, and the
extrusion process parameters were as follows: extrusion ratio
l ¼ 10, extrusion speed, v ¼ 0.2 m/s, forming F 15 mm cylin-
drical bars. Then the extruded bars were quenched into cold
water immediately.
The hot extruded alloy specimens were processed into
sheet tensile specimens by wire cutting and the dimension of
tensile specimens is shown in Fig. 1 a. After aging treatment
(T5) for 200
/C14 C /C2 96 h, the tensile samples were cooled at room
temperature, reaching peak value and the surface of these
aged samples were polished. Then tensile test was carried out
on SHIMADZU AG-1250 N universal testing machine with high
temperature incubator at 200
/C14 C, 250 /C14 C and 300 /C14 C respec-
tively, with the tensile speed 1 mm/min. The aging process
and tensile process are shown in Fig. 1 b and these samples
were quenched into cold water immediately after stretching
process.
The tensile failure specimens were ground and polished
using ethanol, and then observed using emission scanning
electron microscope (SEM). The polished surfaces were etched
with double spray electrolytic instrument using etchant of
50 ml perchloric acid and 450 ml ethanol at /C0 30
/C14 C~ /C0 25 /C14 C
and 1 A, which were prepared for EBSD observation, which
was performed on JSM-7800 F. Further detailed morphologies
were observed by JEM-2100 high resolution transmission
electron microscope (HRTEM). The TEM specimens were pre-
pared by punching F3 mm discs with a thickness of 200 mm,
grounding to 70 mm thickness and with double spray electro-
lytic instrument reducing thickness with etchant of 50 ml
perchloric acid and 450 ml ethanol at /C0 30
/C14 C~ /C0 25 /C14 C and 1 A.

4. Conclusion
The tensile properties and microstructure of hot extruded
GW84 alloy stretching at 200
/C14 C, 250 /C14 C and 300 /C14 C were
investigated. The conclusions of the research could be
concluded as follows:
1. The tensile strength of hot extruded 84 alloy reaches the peak
value at 250
/C14 C, 393 MPa, and the elongation is 6.2%, which is
higher than that at 200 /C14 C. The tensile strength at 300 /C14 C still
reaches a high value, 354 MPa and the elongation is 9.8%. Both
the nano b
0 and b precipitates could effectively prevent the
grain boundary slipping during high temperature tensile and
thus improve the high temperature tensile strength.
2. The precipitated phase at grain boundary and in grain does
not change stretching at 200
/C14 C, which is stillb0 phase. When
stretching at 250 /C14 C, the semi elliptical nano b phases
(Mg5(Gd,Y,Sm)) particles about (20e30) nm /C2 (62e96) nm are
formed at adjacent grain boundaries. However some nanob0
precipitates still exist at grain boundaries; When tensile at
300 /C14 C, the b precipitates grow up and form sub-micron long
strip particles at adjacent grain boundaries and b pre-
cipitates at triangular grain boundaries increase obviously.
3. The metastable phase, b0 phase is very sensitive to tem-
perature and could complete the transition process to stable
phase b rapidly at certain temperatures. Within the tensile
process at 250
/C14 C, part of metastable phases transform into
stable phases ( b0/b), and the formation of b phase mech-
anism is mainly nucleation.; When stretching at 300/C14 C, all b0
phases transform into b phases and the precipitation mode
of b phase is mainly growth and secondary nucleation.
