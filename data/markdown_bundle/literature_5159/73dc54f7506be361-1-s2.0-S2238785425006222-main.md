ABSTRACT
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) with body-centered-cubic (BCC) structure usually displayed high compressive 
strengths, but completely brittle fractured upon tensile test at room temperature. Here, we report a directly cast 
Co-free Al
0.8
CrNiMn
2
Fe
2.5 
HEA with hierarchical microstructure and high tensile strength. This low-cost HEA 
consists of a Fe – Cr-rich disordered BCC (A2) matrix containing uniformly distributed high-density Ni – Al-rich 
ordered BCC (B2) ultra-fine precipitates (~198 nm) and secondary B2 nanoprecipitates (~13 nm). Owing to the 
hierarchical coherent precipitates, the duplex BCC-structured HEA displayed a remarkable tensile yield strength 
of ~907 MPa and an ultimate tensile strength of ~1328 MPa together with an appreciable failure strain of ~9.6 
% at room temperature, which is superior to most conventional alloys and HEAs in as-cast condition. The study 
introduces an effective approach for fabricating alloys with nanostructures, utilizing casting methods to achieve 
the targeted characteristics based on a well-thought-out compositional design.
1. Introduction
As a newly emerging brand of metallic materials, high-entropy alloys 
(HEAs), also referred to as multi-component alloys (MCAs) or complex 
concentrated alloys (CCAs), break the conventional alloys design 
concept based on one or two principal elements, which greatly broaden 
the research field of alloy design [ 1 – 3 ]. Owing to the unique high en -
tropy effect, HEAs are preferred to be composed of multi-component 
solid-solution phase with simple crystal structure, e.g., the 
face-center-cubic (FCC) phase, body-center-cubic (BCC) phase, 
hexagonal-close-packed (HCP) phase, or their mixtures [ 2 – 4 ]. Over the 
past decade, HEAs have attracted an impressive amount of attention due 
to their extraordinary properties that are not available in conventional 
alloys, including excellent strength-ductility balance over a wide tem -
perature range [ 5 ], outstanding wear resistance [ 6 ], good oxidation 
resistance [ 7 , 8 ] and prominent corrosion resistance [ 9 ].
Unfortunately, regarding the mechanical properties of as-cast HEAs, 
FCC-structured alloys usually exhibit high ductility but low yield 
strength. In contrast, the BCC-/HCP-structured HEAs are pretty strong 
but accompanied by disappointing brittleness, which is unfavorable for 
a structural material [ 10 – 12 ]. To date, various conventional physical 
metallurgy strategies have been undertaken to strengthen FCC-based 
HEAs. These strategies include dislocation strengthening strategy, 
various grain refinement strategies, introducing high-density pre -
cipitates, as well as engineering heterogeneous grain structure [ 13 – 15 ]. 
For example, Fu et al. created a single FCC Fe

BCC-structured HEAs potential as a viable option for accident-tolerant

4.3. Deformation mechanisms
To explore the fundamental deformation mechanisms of the Co-free 
Al
0.8
CrNiMn

Fe

structure, TEM was employed to track the evolution of deformation 
substructures under differing strain levels. Upon achieving a tensile 
strain level of approximately 1.6 %, the applied load was removed, 
allowing for an analysis of the dislocation distribution within the HEA, 
as depicted in Fig. 7 . It is clear that a scattered distribution of disloca -
tions is present within the A2-structured matrix, while no dislocation 
structures are discernible within the ultrafine B2-structured precipitates, 
suggesting a greater yield strength of the ordered B2 phase in compar -
ison to the disordered A2 phase.
Elevating the tensile strain level to the point of fracture (approxi -
mately 9.6 %), the dislocation configuration and distribution within the 
Al
0.8
CrNiMn
2
Fe
2.5 
HEA were further examined, as illustrated in Fig. 8 . A 
significant increase in dislocation density is observed in the A2 phase, 
indicative of the rapid multiplication of dislocations in response to 
increased tensile stress. Furthermore, dislocations are not only seen 
interacting and entangling but also accumulating at the boundaries of 
the ultrafine precipitates, suggesting that the hard B2 precipitates act as 
formidable barriers to dislocation motion. Indeed, precipitates are 
anticipated to strengthen the solid solution matrix either through 
dislocation shearing mechanisms or the Orowan bypassing mechanism, 
which is contingent upon the interplay between dislocations and 
precipitates.
In this study, the shearing mechanism is more dominant in certain 
smaller nanosized B2 precipitates, while the bypass mechanism is pri -
marily active in the larger ultrafine B2 precipitates. Surprisingly, the 
magnified views showcased in Fig. 8 (d) furnish direct evidence of dis -
locations shearing through the precipitates, revealing that the B2 phase 
is endowed with the ability to withstand a considerable degree of plastic 
deformation.
