Modeling anisotropic mechanical properties and creep 
behavior of Ni(/u1D6C4)/Ni3Al(/u1D738/uni2032.var ) single crystal superalloys at high 
temperatures
G. Tolooei Eshlaghi · Amir R. Khoei

Abstract This resear ch studies the atomistic phe -
nomena causing anisotropy and orientation depend -
ence of mechanical characteristics and creep defor -
mation in Ni( γ)/Ni3Al(/u1D6FE/uni2032.var ) superalloys with single 
crystals at extreme temperatures. For a wide variety 
of temperature conditions, stress, and load directions, 
MD simulations are used. As a result of this inves -
tigation, we can better understand how dislocations 
and elevated-temperature diffusional processes con -
tribute to creep at the tiny scales. Tensile simulations 
are performed first at constant strain rates of 5 × 1009 
and 1 × 1010 1/s. At stress values between 1.5 and 5.0 
GPa, creep simulations are then carried out. Tem -
peratures ranging from 1100 to 1700  K are applied. 
Additionally, the crystallographic orientations [010], 
[110], and [111] are considered to be loading direc -
tions. Dislocation accumulation in γ channels, shear -
ing of γ′ precipitates, micro-twinning, as well as dis -
location climbing through diffusion, which allows 
them to circumvent precipitates, and  γ′ rafting, which 
is a term used to refer to the coarsening of precipi -
tates that occurs in a directional manner, are all exam-
ples of deformation processes seen in each orienta -
tion model. This research shows that the findings are 
in accord with the experimental evidence. Constant 
strain rates in the steady-state creep phase are derived 
for each creep model. Power-law equations are used 
to forecast strain rates based on temperature and other 
variables such as stress exponent. Based on the values 
for stress exponent, the map for creep deformation of 
different load orientation models is presented.

Introduction
As the demand for gas turbine engines has increased 
dramatically over the last several decades, the devel -
opment of superalloys is essential. Researchers have 
come up with a variety of ways to increase gas tur -
bines’ thermodynamic efficiency. In practice, this 
means raising the temperature of the gas flow so that 
combustion may occur at an elevated temperature 
with less gas consumption. Aero-engine’s internal 
parts, such as the blades of the turbine, encounter 
hot gas frequently and complicated stress and strain 
circumstances must be endured at elevated tempera -
tures. Turbine blades, for example, are often exposed 
to high-temperature gas in aero engines and must 
survive high stress and strain conditions. Resistance 
to the extreme environment necessitates the use of 
materials that can endure creep, fatigue, oxidation,

Existing high-temperature Ni-based superalloys 
have been developed experimentally and via expen -
sive trial and error. Extensive experimental tests have 
been carried out to characterize the Ni-based super -
alloys in terms of mechanical and creep characteris -
tics. Computer modeling and simulation, on the other 
hand, may give a more precise insight into atomic 
mechanisms and shed light on in-depth informa -
tion on creep deformation processes that is difficult 
to obtain via experimental means. Using computer 
simulations, we may discover new knowledge on 
creep features, and design industrial turbines and air -
craft engines with fewer safety factors. Several meth -
ods exist for modeling nanomaterials’ mechanical 
properties [ 51–55], e.g., quantum mechanics (QM), 
molecular dynamics (MD), and finite element method 
(FEM). Nevertheless, interfaces play an important 
role in creep resistance, and because creep processes 
operate on extremely tiny length scales, the con -
tinuum models cannot adequately describe the cor -
responding physical phenomena. Thus, in Ni-based 
superalloys, MD simulations of the mechanisms of 
creep deformations are necessary to depict the atom -
istic characteristics of microstructure as a result of 
creeping across a wide variety of temperatures and 
stress conditions [56].
The creep deformation of Ni-based superalloys 
has been the subject of very few MD simulations. 
The molecular dynamics studies of phase interfaces 
in Ni-based superalloys and misfit dislocations evolu-
tion have been conducted by Wu et al. [ 57, 58]. The 
effects of tensile stresses at a range of temperatures 
on the (100), (110), and (111) phase interfaces were 
simulated. They demonstrated that each phase inter -
face has a unique dislocation network that becomes 
irregular or vanishes as the temperature or stress 
rises. Furthermore, a regular network of dislocations 
implies that the interface is stable and that the stabil -
ity and strength of the network decrease as tempera -
ture rises. This work was followed by a study to see 
whether loading direction affects the development 
of misfit dislocations [ 59]. A misfit dislocation net -
work is easily destroyed when a perpendicular force 
is exerted on the (100) interface; however, in the case 
of multiaxial stresses, there is less risk of structural

In this study, MD simulations of Ni( γ)/Ni3Al(/u1D6FE/uni2032.var ) 
superalloys in three dimensions are performed to 
investigate the mechanical characteristics and creep 
deformation processes using the EAM potentials 
of Mishin using the large-scale atomic/molecular 
massively parallel simulator (LAMMPS). The MD 
simulations of this study are simplified by exclud -
ing alloying elements such as Re and Ru, as well 
as components like W and Co, which are only pre -
sent in trace amounts in real Ni-based superalloys. 
The matrix ( γ ) and pr ecipitate phases ( /u1D6FE/uni2032.var ) ar e there -
fore thought to be composed of pure Ni and Ni3Al, 
respectively. To obtain the mechanical properties of 
these Ni(γ)/Ni3Al(/u1D6FE/uni2032.var ) superalloys, tensile simulations 
are performed at constant strain rates of 5 × 1009 and 
1 × 1010 1∕s . To investigate how temperature affects 
the deformation of the microstructure, various tem -
peratures are applied ranging from 1100 to 1700  K. 
Moreover, loading directions parallel to the crystal -
lographic orientations [010], [110], and [111] are 
investigated to explore the anisotropic mechanical 
properties. Tensile simulations elucidate the deforma-
tion mechanisms responsible for the anisotropy of Ni(γ
)/Ni3Al(/u1D6FE/uni2032.var ) super alloys. Similarly, the direction of 
loading affects the type of active deformation modes 
in creep observed at certain conditions of stress 
and temperature. Hence, the accumulation of creep 
strain is explored under various stress and tempera -
ture regimes for loading directions aligned with the 
crystallographic orientations [010], [110], and [111] 
at temperatures between 1100 and 1700 K with stress 
values of 1.5 to 5.0 GPa. There is a dominating defor-
mation mechanism that regulates creep behavior in 
all three creep stages. The active mechanism for the 
[010] loading orientation is /u1D6FE/uni2032.var precipitate shearing at 
lower temperatures and stresses. Climbing of disloca-
tions and /u1D6FE/uni2032.var precipitate rafting become predominant 
processes when temperature or stress rises. Also, 
micro-twinning is initiated in the [110] orientation 
model. In response to an increase in either tempera -
ture or stress, the dominant mechanism is converted 
to dislocation climb and rafting. Additionally, the 
[111] orientation model exhibits dislocation climb -
ing and rafting under conditions of high stress and 
temperature; otherwise, precipitate shearing occurs. 
Increased temperatures and levels of stress, according 
to the findings of this study, lead to more creep strain 
accumulation and a substantially higher strain rate. 
OVITO, an open-source visualization tool, is used to 
analyze the creep processes for each loading model 
[65]. Furthermore, the power-law equation is used to 
forecast the constant strain rate associated with the 
secondary phase of creep. The creep behavior and 
performance of various loading orientation models 
are also compared, and for each loading direction, a 
deformation map for creep behavior is presented.

often occur on the order of hours; however, MD 
simulation is limited to small time scales in the order 
of micro-seconds due to the considerable compu -
tational cost [ 73]. Consequently, elevated tempera -
tures, applied stresses, and strain rates are taken into 
account in the MD analysis of this study to discover 
creep mechanisms on shorter time scales [ 74, 77]. In 
addition, the high computational cost of MD severely

Conclusions
The research field presented in this article has the 
potential to yield substantial breakthroughs in the next 
advancements in gas turbines and aircraft engines. 
The first part of this study employs the LAMMPS 
software, with the Mishin potential being used to 
perform three-dimensional MD simulations on Ni( γ
)/Ni3Al(/u1D6FE/uni2032.var ) sing le crystal superalloys to examine 
mechanical property anisotropy. Dislocation networks 
and mechanical characteristics are studied in relation 
to temperature, strain rate, and loading direction. As 
the temperature rises, the elastic modulus and the 
yield stress drop. The elastic modulus and the evolu -
tion of dislocations are not affected by the changes 
in applied strain rate, but do contribute to changes 
in yield stress and plasticity. Increase in strain rate 
results in an increase in yield strength, but decreases 
in plasticity and ductility. When loaded in the [110] 
orientation, Ni( γ)/Ni3Al(γ
�
 ) super alloys’ tensile 
strength is the least. This observed anisotropy implies 
differences in active deformation modes and strong 
orientation effects. The dislocation network retains 
its regular mosaic structure during the initial load -
ing period. As loading proceeds in the [010] direc -
tion, the matrix dislocation shear precipitates through 
damaged misfit dislocation networks at the interfaces, 
whereas micro-twinning is the mechanism underlying 
strain growth in the [110] loading direction. Addi -
tionally, precipitate shearing and dislocation accumu -
lation in the channels are the primary strain increase 
mechanisms in the [111] loading orientation.
In paper’s second section, the creep deformation 
mechanisms were explored using MD simulations. It 
was determined how the creep strain evolved and how 
variations in the active creep deformation processes 
occurred as a result of changing various parameters, 
such as temperature, tensile stress, and the loading 
direction. Stress magnitudes ranging between 1.5 and 
5.0 GPa, temperatures ranging from 1100 to 1700 K, 
and three loading orientations including [010], [110], 
and [111] were among the circumstances being 
assessed. The centro-symmetry parameter and Voro -
noi topological types were used to detect and associate 
deformation mechanisms with creep behavior using 
OVITO. Several distinct tensile creep processes of 
Ni(γ)/Ni3Al(/u1D6FE/uni2032.var ) super alloy have been discovered for 
three loading orientations comprising [010], [110], 
and [111], at stresses and temperatures between 0.5 
and 5.0 GPa and 1100 and 1700  K, respectively. 
Through analysis of topological types, it was dem -
onstrated that the creep anisotropy exists as a result 
of changes in deformation modes between the vari -
ous orientations. At intermediate temperatures and 
stress conditions, shearing of the /u1D6FE/uni2032.var particle is the 
active creep deformation process in the first model 
with [010] orientation as the loading direction. It is 
consistent with experimental evidence. It is noticed 
that as temperature and applied stress increase, the 
combination of lattice mismatch and applied stresses 
increases dislocations in channels normal to the load -
ing axis. As a consequence, edge dislocations climb 
and circumvent the precipitates, while screw disloca -
tions cross slip and shift between slip planes to avoid 
precipitates. Subsequently, rafts form perpendicular to 
the stress direction as a result of atom diffusion neces-
sary for the dislocation climb process. As a result, the 
tertiary phase of creep is also noticed. Creep strain ini-
tiates micro-twinning as the primary creep deforma -
tion mechanism at low temperature levels in the sec -
ond set of simulations, which model the direction of 
loading [110], resulting in strain growth. Apart from 
micro-twinning, dislocations cannot shear the precipi-
tate by increasing the temperature, owing to the high 
energy APB. As a result of the increased temperature, 
atom diffusion is facilitated, activating cross-slip and 
glide-climb processes. Rafting is a consequence of 
this diffusion, which causes significant strain rise and, 
ultimately, microstructural collapse. Finally, precipi -
tate shearing in the [111] loading direction is found in 
the model at low temperatures and low stresses. How-
ever, it has been observed that the atomic diffusion 
in matrix leads in dislocation glide-climb and creep 
strain growth when severe temperatures or stresses 
are applied. As a result, a raft-like micro-structure is 
formed. Tertiary creep causes the strain rate to grow 
considerably till the ultimate failure point.
Ni(γ)/Ni3Al(/u1D6FE/uni2032.var ) super alloys’ steady-state creep rates

the power-law creep equation. In this way, the appar -

onset are identified. In order to estimate how long it 
will take a part to achieve a specified value of strain 
and then become incompatible with the geometry, it 
is necessary to know the steady-state creep strain rate. 
Thus, for determining the approximate strain rate of 
steady-state creep phase, n and Q must be specified 
for each temperature and stress condition. Slope is
