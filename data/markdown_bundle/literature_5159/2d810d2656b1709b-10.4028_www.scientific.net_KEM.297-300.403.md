Keywords: Ti-Al-Nb ternary alloy, Oxidation behavior, oxidation resistance, mechanical properties 
Abstract. Three types of Ti-Al-Nb ternary alloys ar e obtained by arc-melting and heat treatment, 
which are γ-TiAl single phase alloy, γ-TiAl + α2-Ti3Al duplex phase alloy, and γ-TiAl +α2-Ti3Al 
+Nb2Al multiple phase alloy. The phase stability is studied using optical microscope, X-ray 
diffractometer and electron probe microanalyzer. Th e oxidation behavior of three Ti-Al-Nb ternary 
alloys with different microstructures was investigated at 1273K using interrupted oxidation test in air. 
The compression test was carried out at 298K-1373K . The oxidation resistance of Ti-Al-Nb ternary 
alloy at high temperature was found to be better than that of the binary Ti-Al alloy. Among the three 
Ti-Al-Nb ternary alloys, the two-phase alloy with γ+α2 has the best oxidation resistance and 
mechanical properties. The existence of α2 could enhance the oxidation resistance of the alloy at high 
temperature. On the contrary, the presence of Nb-enriched phase such as Nb 2Al would decrease the 
oxidation resistance at elevated temperature due to the formation of Nb 2O5, which would accelerate 
the exfoliation of oxide. 
Introduction 
The TiAl intermetallic compound is an attractive material for high-temperature structural appli-
cations because of its low density, high melting poi nt and high specific strength at elevated tem-
perature [1, 2, 3]. However, the low RT duc tility, fracture toughness and insufficient Oxidation 
resistance above about 1123K etc. are the primar y obstacle which has prevented the immediate 
application of this material [2] . Microstructure control and alloy modification are two major routines 
to improve the high-temperature performance of TiAl -base alloys. Nb, W, Si, Mo and Ta appear to 
improve the high temperature oxidation resistance of TiAl-based intermetallic alloys with niobium 
being the most effective alloying element. Because Nb is highly soluble in the TiAl alloy [1, 2, 3], 
unlike other additives, it can be added in substantial volume. So Nb improves the microstructure and 
mechanical property while it improves the oxidation resistance. 
The objective of this paper is to investigate oxi dation behavior and mechanical properties of 
Ti-Al-Nb ternary alloys with different microstructure. 
Materials and experimental procedure 
The main purpose of the composition design was to prepare the Ti-Al-Nb ternary alloys with 
single-phase, two-phase and multi-phase microstruc ture. According to various Ti-Al-Nb ternary 
phase diagrams [4, 5], the nominal composition of the three alloys are Ti-47.5Al-5Nb, Ti-43Al-14Nb, 
Ti-40Al-20Nb (hereafter designated as alloy A, a lloy B, alloy C). High purity Ti (99.9% pure), Al 
(99.9% pure) and Nb (99.9% pure) were used as th e raw materials. All alloys were prepared by 
arc-melting using a tungsten electrode under argon . Each ingot was remelted four times to ensure the

All the ingots were homogenized at 1473K for 5hours in vacuum and cooled down to room

The interrupted isothermal experiment was carried out in high-temperature resistance. The 
experiment medium was static air and the temp erature was holding at 1273K. The specimens were 
laid obliquely in quartz crucible to assure that th e six sides of the alloy ha d the full contact with the 
air. Three specimens of each alloy were used for oxidation experiment in order to get the average 
mass change. Oxidation mass gain analysis was a dopted. The specimens with quartz crucible were 
periodically removed from the furnace, air cool ed, weighed, and returned to the furnace every 
10hours. The total oxidation time was 100hours. Th e mass gain weighed by electronic balance 
(accuracy at 0.1mg). 
The compression test were carried out at a strain rate of 2 ×10-4s-1 at 298K-1373K in INSTRON 
8562 testing machine.

Oxidation kinetics. Fig. 2 shows the oxidation kinetic curves of the three alloys at tested 1273K. 
The mass gains of the three alloys after interrupted oxidation test at 1273K for 100hours were all less 
than 8mg/cm2 (TiAl). In particular, the γ+α2 two-phase alloy (alloy B) showed the best oxidation 
resistance with a mass gain of only about 2.5mg/cm 2. Shind and Anada had reported that the mass 
gains of Ti-Al binary alloys oxidized at 1073K for 100hours was about 1-3mg/cm 2 [ 6, 7, 8]. The 
mass gains of a binary TiAl alloy used in this study was found to be 69mg/cm2 at 1273K for 100 hours. 
The results suggest that the oxidation resistance of Ti -Al-Nb ternary alloys were superior to that of 
the Ti-Al binary alloys. 
 
 
Fig. 2 Kinetic curves of interrupted oxidation of high Nb content TiAl-based alloy at 1273K 
 
After oxidation test at 1273K fo r 100h, the outer layer of all specimens was all grayish-white. 
Also, some exfoliation oxide scales were found in the crucible. However, it should be noted that time 
to exfoliation and the amount of exfoliation oxides for the three alloys were different. Alloy B had the 
least amount of exfoliation oxide scale, while all oy C had the most. The exfoliation of oxide scale 
occurred after oxidation at 1273K for 30 hours for a lloy A and C. The exfoliation did not occur for 
alloy B until after 90 hours of oxidation test. 
Analysis of oxide scale. The microstructure and composition across the oxide scale for the three 
alloys were analyzed using X-ray diffraction, SEM/EDS and EPMA. The results are summarized the 
following sections. 
Fig. 3 (a) shows the cross-section SEM micrograph of alloy A. The thickness of oxide scale was 
found to be about 15-17µm, and it stratified into two layers: the external scale (dot mark 3) and the 
internal scale (dot 2) which was close to matrix ( dot 1). The dot 4 indicated the partially spalled-off 
oxides next to mounting resin. The EPMA spectra of alloy 1 across the oxide scale was shown in Fig. 
7, it revealed that inner scale contained Ti, Al a nd Nb, where the concentration of Ti was relatively 
small. 
According to these results of EDS, EPMA analysis and X-ray diffraction, it is confirmed that the 
inner scale was a mixed oxide of Al2O3 and TiO2, and the outer scale was mainly composed of TiO2. 
Fig. 3 (b) shows the cross-section SEM micrograph of alloy B. Area 1 shows metal substrate, dot 2 
marks the oxide scale, and dot 3 indicates the partially exfoliation oxides next to the mounting resin. 
Both results of EDS and EPMA show that the oxide scale contained little Ti and Nb. X-ray diffraction 
result also showed that the oxide scale primarily composed of Al 2O3 and a minor amount of TiO 2. 
Alloy B had the best oxidation resistance in this study, which maybe due to the formation of a stable 
protective Al2O3 scale. 
The oxide scale of alloy C was composed of a multi-layered scale and the thickness of about 35µm 
as shown in Fig. 3 (c), dot 1 represents substrate, dot 2, 3 and 4 were all oxide layers, dot 5 indicated 
the mounting resin. The alloy exhibited apparent exfoliation of oxide scale from the matrix. EDX 
results showed that the oxide scale was composed of TiO 2, Al2O3 and Nb2O5, near dot 3, the oxide 
scale were mainly TiO 2 and Al 2O3, near dot 2, the oxide scale again become to TiO 2, Al2O3 and 
Nb2O5. It means Nb2O5 promote the oxide scale exfoliation.

The above results clearly show that the oxidation behavior of Ti-Al-Nb alloys is superior to that of 
the binary Ti-Al alloy. In generally, the free energy of formation of TiO
2 and Al2O3 in Ti-Al binary 
system is very close. At the same time, the activ ity of Al shows negative deviation, so the selective 
oxidation of Al is difficult to take place at high temperature [9]. As a result, the alloy forms a mixed 
oxide of Al2O3 and TiO2, rather than the protective oxide of Al 2O3. The structure of TiO 2 is porous 
and, thus, is the fast diffusion cha nnel of oxygen. At high temperature TiO 2 is less protective than 
Al2O3 even if the TiO 2 were not porous due to the higher concentration of point defects. Also the 
adhesion of the Al 2O3 +TiO2 mixed oxides to the substrate is poor, which result in poor oxidation 
resistance of the binary Ti-Al alloy. Adding a sma ll amount of Nb in the alloy could increase the 
activity of Al, suppress the growth of TiO2, promote the formation of protective oxide scale of Al2O3, 
and thus reduce the oxidation rate of the alloy. The a ddition of Mo or Sb to Ti-Al binary alloys has 
similar effect [10]. 
The above results also show that the oxidation beha vior of Ti-Al-Nb alloys is influenced by the 
microstructure and alloy composition. In the single-phase alloy (alloy A), the non-protective oxide of 
TiO2 dominated the outer oxide scale. Therefore, the formation of the inner oxide scale and the 
stability of Al2O3 in the inner oxide scale should control the oxidation rate. The γ+α2 two-phase alloy 
(alloy B) has the best oxidation resistance in this study. According to several previous studies in 
Ti-Al binary alloys, the oxidation resistance impr oves with aluminum content, so the oxidation 
resistance of α
2 is generally inferior to that of γ [6, 7, 8]. However, in this study of Ti-Al-Nb ternary 
system, the oxidation resistance of γ+α2 two-phase alloy was found to be better than γ single-phase 
alloy. The improvement of oxidation resistance may be attributed to the higher amount of Nb present 
in the γ and α2 phases. 
The results of EPMA elemental analysis for both alloy A and B after annealing show that the Nb 
concentration in alloy A was detected to be around 5.1at%. In the alloy B, the Nb in the γ and α2 
phases were found to be 13.3at% and 19.4at%, respectiv ely. Nb has been considered to be the most 
effective element to improve the oxidation resist ance of TiAl [11, 12]. As a result, the oxidation 
resistance of γ phase in alloy B is expected to be better than that of alloy A because of its higher Nb 
concentration. Similarly, oxidation resistance of the α2 phase in Ti-Al-Nb ternary alloy should be 
better than that of α2 phase in Ti-Al binary [11, 12]. 
It is also obvious that the Nb 2O5 emerged in both alloy C when the Nb content in the Ti-Al-Nb 
alloy is 20at%, resulting in the exfoliation of oxide scale. The exfoliation of oxide scale of alloys C 
must be related to the Nb-enriched phase of Nb2Al. The poor oxidation resistance of Nb-based alloy 
and Nb-containing intermetallics such as Nb 3Al and Nb 2Al are mainly attributed to the weak 
adhesion between matrix and Nb2O5 formed even at 1173k [13]. The results obtained from this study

Compression test. Fig. 4 shows the stress-strain curves of the three alloys at RT, 973K, 1173K 
and 1373K respectively. Although compression defo rmation of alloy A can be observed, the 
specimens failed to be compressed in the experiment. Stress-strain curves at 1173K and 1373K show 
alloy A was compressed successfully. This means that single phase γ-TiAl has no ductility at tem-
peratures below 973K. The result is in agreement with previous reports [14]. Alloy B was compressed 
successfully in the full temperature range, a nd no cracking was observed on the surface of the 
specimen. Alloy C was successfully compressed at temperatures above 973K.

Fig. 5 shows the relationships between yield strength and temperature for the 3 alloys. Properties 
of other correlated alloys are also displayed. At low temperatures, yield strength of the alloy has 
almost the same values to those reported by refere nce [14], but the values are much higher at high 
temperatures. This phenomena was resulted from higher concentration of Nb in γ-phase solution. At 
temperatures from RT to 1373K, yield strengths of alloy B and alloy C are much higher than that of 
alloy A. This comes to the conclusion that mu lti-phase microstructure strengthening mechanism 
effectively improves high-temperature properties of TiAl alloys. At 1373K, yield strengths of 3 other 
alloys were almost softened to the same ma gnitude. For TiAl alloy, yield strength at high 
temperatures can be effectively improved by adding Nb compared with by adding Mn or V [15].

Conclusions 
The oxidation resistance of the Ti-Al-Nb ternary all oy at high temperature is better than that of the 
Ti-Al binary alloy. Among the three Ti-Al-Nb ternary alloys investigated in this study, the two-phase 
γ+α2 alloy has the best oxidation resistance because higher Nb concentration in both phases. The 
presence of Nb-enriched phase such as Nb2Al and Nb3Al would decrease the oxidation resistance at 
elevated temperature due to occurrence of Nb2O5 which would promote the exfoliation of the oxide 
scale. The yield strength of single phase γ-TiAl alloy A was 450MPa at RT, it remained the value 
until 1200K. The yield strength decreased quickly above 1200K. At RT, the yield strength of two 
phase alloy B and multi phase alloy C were 1150M Pa and 950MPa respectively. These values de-
creased slowly as temperatures increased. At temperatures above 1000K, the values decreased faster. 
At 1200K, yield strength still remained at 600MPa. When temperatures went above 1200K, the yield 
strength dropped down rapidly. 
Two phase alloys presented high yield stre ngth at high temperatures and good compression 
ductility at RT.
