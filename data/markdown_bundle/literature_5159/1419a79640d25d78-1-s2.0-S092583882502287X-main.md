ABSTRACT
Titanium alloys are widely used in impact-resistant applications due to their high specific strength and excellent 
dynamic mechanical properties. In this study, a hetero-structured (HS) titanium alloy containing tungsten (W) 
was fabricated using powder metallurgy and hot extrusion. The dynamic mechanical properties and deformation 
behavior were investigated over a wide range of strain rates (0.001 – 3000 s ⁻ ¹) at room and elevated temperatures. 
The W-containing HS Ti alloy (Ti-W alloy) featured a fibrous heterostructure composed of W-rich and W-lean 
structures. Compared to conventional homogeneous titanium alloys, the Ti-W alloy exhibited significantly 
enhanced strain-rate hardening capability, achieving a strength of approximately 2 GPa at a strain rate of 
3000 s ⁻ ¹ . This improvement is primarily attributed to the increased dislocation generation and accumulation 
induced by the heterostructure under high strain rates. Additionally, the Ti-W alloy maintained the high strain- 
rate hardening effects up to 973 K, effectively resisting localized shear deformation. This is mainly due to the 
enhancement of thermal conductivity and thermal stability induced by the addition of W, which mitigates 
temperature rise in adiabatic regions and suppresses the softening effect caused by recrystallization. This study 
provides new insights into the design of impact-resistant titanium alloys by introducing alloying elements and 
heterogeneous structures.
1. Introduction
Titanium and its alloys have wide applications in aerospace, marine 
and automotive industries, thanks to their high performances, including 
high specific strength, toughness, and good corrosion resistance [1,2] . 
During the practical service, extreme conditions such as high-speed and 
thermal impacts are frequently encountered, which can threaten the 
structural reliability of the materials. The development of titanium al -
loys in the past decades has highlighted the importance of the dynamic 
behavior, where the structural failures are closely linked to the micro -
structural changes and thermal softening upon the generation of adia -
batic shear band (ASB) [3 – 6] . Adiabatic shear localization, as the prime 
failure mechanism, was commonly observed in materials subjected to 
dynamic impacting [7,8] . Since the large deformation abruptly takes 
place within extremely short time and inside a limited space, a sharp rise 
of localized temperature will lead to a process of thermal-plastic insta -
bility, accompanied by the formation of ASB [9,10] . The ASBs that act as 
the precursors to material failure during the adiabatic shear localization 
process, are greatly influenced by the thermal conductivity of materials. 
It has been reported that adiabatic shear dissipation energy has a posi -
tive correlation to the thermal conductivity of material [11 – 13] . For 
example, the decreased thermal conductivity in pure titanium resulted 
in a significant drop of the width of ASB [14] . Composition optimization 
was also reported to reduce susceptibility to ASB, for example, by 
introducing graphene nanosheets [11] or Cu-rich phases [8] . In their 
studies, the reduced susceptibility to ASB was closely related to the high 
thermal conductivity that transferred more heat from the shear zone. 
Therefore, it can be seen that local thermal softening plays an important 
role in dynamic behaviors, which should closely relate to the alloy 
composition [5,15] . However, few studies attempted to mitigate its in -
fluence by adding alloying elements with effective heat-transfer capa -
bility, leaving this strategy very potential one [16] . Tungsten (W) is 
renowned for its high thermal conductivity and exceptional thermal 
stability at elevated temperatures [17,18] . There is no formation of

The susceptibility to ASB formation can be also decreased by 
improving mechanical properties, since the adiabatic shear localization 
is closely related to insufficient work-hardening ability [23] . Li et al. 
[24] reported that the combination of high work hardening capability 
and moderate thermal softening effect were beneficial to improve 
adiabatic shear localization resistance. Yang et al. [25] proposed that 
combined stress-induced martensitic transformation and twinning 
enhanced the impact energy absorption, resulting in delaying the 
propagation of ASB. High strain hardening behavior demonstrated a 
tortuous and bifurcated path to consume more impact energy, thereby 
improving the resistance to formation and propagation of ASB [23, 
26 – 28] . Recently, hetero-structured (HS) materials that offer a favorable 
combination of strength and ductility due to hetero-deformation 
induced (HDI) hardening, have shown potential to absorb more defor -
mation energy than their homogeneous counterparts [29 – 31] . Zhang 
et al. [32] reported that the design strategy of hierarchically ordered 
coherent interfaces conferred a nano-martensite titanium alloy to 
consume significant deformation energy. In our previous studies [21, 
22] , a series of Ti-W alloys with compositional gradient were designed 
and prepared through tailoring the partial diffusion of W particles using 
the powder metallurgy method. The Ti-20W alloy exhibited high 
strength, good ductility, and high work hardening capability under 
quasi-static loading, which was attributed to the multiple deformation 
mechanisms occurring in the gradient interface [22,33] . However, few 
studies to date reported the enhancement of the resistance to adiabatic 
shear localization via introducing heterostructures, and there is a 
limited understanding on how heterostructures influence impacting 
deformation. While the HS materials may introduce microstructures 
with different mechanical responses, it is thereby critical to examine the 
shear localization behavior and microstructural evolution of HS tita -
nium alloys during the dynamic loading, which helps to understand the 
underlying mechanism of their dynamic deformation behaviors.
In this study, a Ti-20W alloy with a compositional gradient was 
fabricated by controlling the partial diffusion of W particles through the 
powder metallurgy process. The compositional gradient structure was 
further evolved into a fibrous heterostructure composed of W-rich and 
W-lean structures via hot extrusion. The dynamic mechanical properties 
and deformation behavior of the Ti-20W alloy with fibrous 
heterostructures were systematically investigated over a wide range of 
temperatures (298 – 973 K) and strain rates (0.001 – 3000 s ⁻ ¹).
2. Materials and methods
2.1. Materials fabrication
Fig. 1 (a) illustrates the fabrication process of the Ti-20W (weight 
fraction, wt%) alloy. High-purity Ti powder (99.9 %, < 45 μ m) and W 
powder (99.9 %, 2 – 4 μ m) were mixed for 6 hours using a V-shaped mixer 
under an argon atmosphere. The resulting powder mixture was then 
compacted into a cylindrical billet ( φ 120 mm) by using cold isostatic 
pressing (CIP) at a pressure of 180 MPa for 60 s. The W powders were 
uniformly dispersed within Ti powders. Subsequently, the billet was 
sintered at 1350 ℃ for 4 hours in a vacuum to promote partial diffusion 
of W particles. The diffusion zone was then elongated into a fiber-like 
structure through hot extrusion at 950 ℃ with an extrusion ratio of 
16:1, followed by air cooling. As a result, a fully dense Ti-W alloy rod 
with a final diameter of 30 mm was obtained.

4. Discussion
4.1. Strain-rate hardening behavior
Compared to some traditional Ti alloys, the Ti-W alloy in this work 
exhibited a high strain-rate hardening effect. The addition of W element 
may play a significant role in enhancing strain-rate hardening capability 
of Ti-W alloy. It is known that the solid solution of W in Ti matrix can 
enhance the hardness. Therefore, there is a difference of mechanical 
behavior between W-rich and W-lean structures in the Ti-W alloy due to 
different content of W. Recently, the non-uniform stress and strain have 
been also demonstrated to occur in HS alloys under high strain rate 
condition, where the hard domain can bear higher stress and the soft 
domain can undergo a larger plastic strain [45,46] . Consequently, 
bundles of dislocations can be rapidly generated and accumulated near 
the hetero-interfaces to accommodate the deformation [5,47] . Similarly, 
it is possible that the harder W-rich structures can bear higher stress and 
restrain the plastic deformation of the W-lean structures during the 
impacting process, leading to a strain gradient between the 
hetero-interfaces. The high-density dislocations could be sharply pro -
duced and accumulated near the interface of W-rich and W-lean struc -
tures, leading to a high strain-rate hardening effect. Therefore, the 
heterogeneous structures could help Ti-W alloy to obtain a high 
strain-rate hardening capability.
At elevated temperatures, such as 973 K, the Ti-W alloy maintained a 
high yield strength of 730 MPa at 3000 s ⁻ ¹ ( Fig. 4 (c)), demonstrating a 
high thermal stability. W element is known for its high melting point and 
thermal conductivity [48] . The addition of W can suppress the recrys -
tallization process during the dynamic deformation, thereby improving 
resistance to shear localization. During the dynamic impacting process, 
the occurrence of adiabatic shear localization in materials can be 
attributed to the complementary rotational dynamic recrystallization 
mechanism [49] . Upon dynamic impacting, numerous dislocations 
rapidly accumulate, and then form sub-grain boundaries under shearing 
stress. This drastic deformation within extremely short time and inside a 
limited space can induce a rise in the localized temperature. Subse -
quently, due to the coupling of temperature rise and shearing stress, the 
sub-grains break up and reorientate to form equiaxed nano-grains [50] . 
In this study, the influence of W addition on suppressing recrystalliza -
tion process can be explained by two aspects. Firstly, the W addition can 
increase the melting point and thermal conductivity of the Ti matrix, 
which effectively decreases the localized temperature rise, thereby 
suppressing recrystallization process. Secondly, the dissolution of W 
atoms can induce significant lattice distortions in the Ti matrix, which 
increases the friction stress to impedes dislocation motion. Conse -
quently, the formation of sub-grains and recrystallized nano-grains is 
effectively constrained. Therefore, W addition suppresses the recrystal -
lization process during the dynamic deformation, thereby improving 
resistance to shear localization. At elevated temperatures and high strain 
rates, the W-rich structures are less prone to dynamic recrystallization 
compared to the W-lean structures. This mitigates dislocation annihi -
lation induced by dynamic recovery and dynamic recrystallization. 
Meanwhile, the strain gradient between the W-rich and W-lean struc -
turers could help to generate and accumulate more dislocations, thereby 
maintaining a high hardening capability and high strength of the Ti-W 
alloy.

5. Conclusions
The dynamic deformation behaviors of Ti-W alloy have been 
comprehensively investigated under varying temperatures and strain 
rates. The main conclusions are as follows:
(1) The Ti-W alloy featured a fibrous heterostructures composed of 
W-rich and W-lean structures. Such heterogeneous structures were 
beneficial to obtain a high strain-rate hardening capability. Conse -
quently, the Ti-W alloy achieved a high strength of approximately 2 GPa 
at a strain rate of 3000 s ⁻ ¹ .
(2) Compared to traditional homogeneous Ti alloys, the hetero- 
structured Ti-W alloy exhibited a high strain-rate hardening capability 
and thermal stability in a wide range of temperatures and strain rates. It 
was attributed to the coordinated deformation of heterogeneous struc -
tures and constrained dynamic recrystallization caused by the addition 
of refractory element.
(3) The width of the adiabatic shear band in the Ti-W alloy was 
significantly larger than that of conventional homogeneous Ti alloys. 
This improved resistance to shear localization is associated to the 
enhanced thermal conductivity and restrained thermal softening 
induced by W alloying.
