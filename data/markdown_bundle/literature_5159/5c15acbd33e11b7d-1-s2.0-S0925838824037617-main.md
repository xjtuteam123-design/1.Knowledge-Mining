1. Introduction
Refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs), which have significant po -
tential for applications in aerospace and other fields, typically comprise 
at least five refractory metal elements in concentrations ranging from 
5 % to 35 %. These alloys possess enhanced thermal stability and are 
capable of withstanding higher service temperatures [1 – 4] . Nonethe -
less, their brittleness at room temperature has been a major constraint 
on their processing and utility since their introduction [1,4 – 7] . For 
instance, the WNbMoTa and WNbMoTaV alloys exhibited 
room-temperature fracture strains of only 1.7 % and 2.6 %, respectively 
[4] . Consequently, numerous strategies have been developed to mitigate 
this issue, with adjusting the alloy ’ s constituent elements and altering 
the fabrication technique proving particularly effective [8 – 10] .
The "cocktail" effect suggests that the properties of the alloys are 
dictated by the nature of the constituent elements and their interactions 
[11 – 13] . Modifying the type and content of constituent elements in al -
loys can influence the composition and distribution of phases, thereby 
achieving a reasonable improvement in the properties of alloys [14,15] . 
Research showed that the addition of elements with intrinsic brittleness, 
such as Mo and W, to alloys containing Nb, Ti, Zr, and Hf, results in a 
reduction in the toughness of the aforementioned elements [16,17] . 
Furthermore, a reduction in the valence electron concentration ( VEC ) 
has been demonstrated to enhance the toughness of RHEAs [16 – 18] . Rao 
et al. [19] identified HfMoNbTaTi alloys that satisfy important me -
chanical property requirements among a wide range of 
Cr-Hf-Mo-Nb-Ta-Ti-W-Zr alloys using the latest theories and extended 
thermodynamic tools. Yang et al. [20] prepared HfMoNbTaTi alloys by 
vacuum arc melting (VAM). The alloy has a room temperature 
compressive yield strength and plastic strain of 1713 MPa and 12 %,

The mechanical properties of materials are not only affected by their 
constituent elements but also inseparable from their microstructure. 
Currently, VAM is the dominant method for preparing RHEAs [10,21] . 
However, the as-cast alloys prepared by VAM usually exhibit coarse 
dendritic grains accompanied by elemental agglomeration, which is 
detrimental to their mechanical properties [1,6,22] . The powder met -
allurgy (P/M) process provides a route effective to surmount these de -
fects. Amongst the numerous P/M methods, the mechanical alloying 
(MA) and SPS process stands out for the production of fine-grained 
ductile RHEAs [11, 23 – 25] . The MA process allows the production of 
nanocrystalline solid solution powders with a homogeneous composi -
tion [11,24] . The SPS technology is an advanced powder sintering 
technology that uses energised electrodes to apply a specific sintering 
power source and pressing pressure to the sintered powder, which is 
discharged to activate, thermoplastic deform and cooled to complete the 
production of high-performance materials, which is capable of greatly 
reducing the sintering time and avoiding the coarsening of the grains by 
virtue of the rapid heating and cooling characteristics [24,25] . For 
instance, the microstructure and mechanical properties of WNbMoTa 
alloys prepared by the MA and SPS process exhibited notable enhance -
ments in comparison to those of the as-cast alloys [8] . The average grain 
size of the WNbMoTa alloys was reduced from 200 μ m to 0.88 μ m, 
accompanied by an increase in the compressive yield strength and 
plastic strain by 132.51 % and 546.15 %, respectively. In a previous 
study [26] , the authors prepared fine-grained HfMoNbTaTi alloys via 
the MA and SPS process, which demonstrated an increase in 
room-temperature compressive yield strength by 35.73 % and plastic 
strain by 120.33 % in comparison to the as-cast alloys. Consequently, the 
MA and SPS technique holds considerable promise for the preparation of 
fine-grained RHEAs with uniform composition and the effective 
improvement of their microstructure and mechanical properties.
Building on prior research [26,27] , this study first replaced some of 
Mo in the HfMoNbTaTi alloy (referred to as the A1 alloy) with Nb, which 
exhibits toughness characteristics, to enhance the toughness of the alloy. 
Subsequently, Ti, with its low VEC and density, was incrementally 
substituted for the high-density Ta, to further improve the toughness of 
the alloy while reducing its density. Given the advantages of the MA and 
SPS process in the field of on-demand material preparation, a series of

component content has no significant effect on the crystalline size of the 
pre-alloyed powders. In contrast, when Nb was utilized to replace some 
Mo, the lattice strain and lattice constant of the pre-alloyed powders 
underwent a significant change. The lattice strain decreased from 
1.35 % in the A1 pre-alloyed powders to 1.18 % in the A2 pre-alloyed 
powders, while the lattice constant increased from 3.30506 Å to 
3.31536 Å ( Fig. 1 (d-e)). This is because the atomic radius of Mo is 
smaller than that of the other elements ( Table S1 ), and replacing it with 
Nb can reduce the difference in atomic radius and weaken the lattice 
strain. Furthermore, according to Vegard ’ s law, a decrease in the content 
of Mo elements with smaller lattice constants will inevitably lead to an 
increase in the lattice constant of the alloys [31,32] . Conversely, the 
replacement of Ta with Ti, which has a similar lattice constant, has less 
impact on the lattice constant of the alloys. When the R- value is 
increased to 5/3 (A3 pre-alloyed powders), the lattice strain is mini -
mized. Subsequently, the lattice strain gradually increases as the R- value 
increases.
Fig. 2 presented the particle morphologies and SEM-EDS elemental 
distribution maps of the A2-A5 pre-alloyed powders. The SEM-EDS 
elemental distribution maps indicated that the elemental distribution 
of the pre-alloyed powders was relatively uniform, with no evidence of 
elemental agglomeration. The content of each component was deter -
mined by ICP-OES, and the results were presented in Table 2 . The 
content of each component in the A2-A5 pre-alloyed powders was 
consistent with the nominal composition, and the Fe element within the 
pre-alloyed powders was approximately 1.5 at%. Furthermore, the pre- 
alloyed powders exhibited a minor presence of O and C elements ( Fig. S1
(a)). The content of the elements O and C in the pre-alloyed powder is 
about 2.5 at% and 0.4 at%, respectively. This indicates that the MA 
process not only improves the solid-state solubility and promotes the 
uniform distribution of elements, but also enables the on-demand 
preparation of ultrafine crystalline alloy materials. As the R- value 
increased, the powder particles transformed from a spherical to an 
irregular shape, accompanied by a gradual increase in particle size. The 
above phenomenon indicates that an increase in the R- value improves 
the plasticity of the pre-alloyed powders. To further substantiate this 
assertion, the particle size and powder yield of A1-A5 pre-alloyed 
powders were subjected to statistical investigation, with the results 
presented in Fig. S1 (b-c). The particle size exhibited a notable increase 
from 6.26 μ m (A1) to 20.37 μ m (A5) as the R- value increased. 
Conversely, the powder yield decreased from 61.3 % to 42.8 %. This is 
because the increase in the R- value improves the plasticity of the pre- 
alloyed powders, resulting in powder particles being able to withstand 
greater impact potential energy without fragmentation and increased 
cold welding with the milling medium.
