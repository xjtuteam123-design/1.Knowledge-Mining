1. Introduction
A promising approach to higher strengths and ductility in mag-
nesium alloys is by using icosahedral Mg3Zn6RE quasicrystalline
phase (i-phase) which exists stably in Mg–Zn–RE alloys (rare earth
element RE = Y, Gd, Tb, Dy, Ho or Er) [1,2]. Not only does the
i-phase has properties such as very high hardness and low sur-
face energies [3], it also forms a two phase equilibrium with the
/H9251-Mg phase [4]. The i-phase, which forms interdendritically, is dis-
persed in the alloys through thermomechanical processes such as
hot rolling or extrusion, which also reﬁnes the grain structure to
achieve high strength and ductility, as well as a weak texture and
isotropic mechanical properties in tension and compression [5–12].
We showed that the mechanical properties of these wrought alloys
are limited by their grain size to a yield strength (YS) of about
300 MPa, corresponding to the ﬁnest grain size of about 1 /H9262m [11].
Recently we have shown that by an improved casting and extru-
sion, by which the quasicrystal phase is dispersed more effectively,
YS of about 400 MPa can be obtained in the ﬁne grained alloys [13].
Likewise, with a more effective dispersion of the strengthening
phases in the alloy, very high strengths should be possible even
without a very ﬁne grain size. One possible method would be to at
ﬁrst solutionize the alloy at a temperature higher than the i-phase
eutectic, and then to reprecipitate it ﬁnely. A ﬁne reprecipitation
of the i-phase would occur during a high temperature extrusion

By high temperature solutionizing, the i-phase is partly dissolved
and partly transformed to a pre-eutectic hexagonal phase called
H phase (Zn,Mg)5Y [15]. At high temperatures, formation of the
ternary phase cubic W-Mg3Zn3Y2 can also occur [16]. The result-
ing microstructure and mechanical properties have been studied.
We show high tensile yield strengths of ∼350 MPa without reﬁning
grain sizes to the order of 1 /H9262m.
2. Experimental procedure
An alloy with composition Mg–6 at% Zn–1 at% Y was made by
melting high purity elements with a mixture of cover gases CO2
and SF6, and cast in steel molds. The alloy was solutionized at
480 ◦C for 24 h in a mufﬂe furnace with inert atmosphere, followed
by extrusion at 430 ◦C and 380 ◦C (referred hereafter as alloy e430
and e380, respectively) with an extrusion ratio of 25:1, into 8 mm
diameter rods. The alloys were subsequently aged at 150 ◦C in an
oil bath. Microstructures were observed by optical microscopy and
transmission electron microscopy (TEM). Samples for TEM were
prepared by precision ion milling and observed with JEOL 2000FX-
II and FEI Tecnai G2 F30 microscopes, the latter attached with
STEM-HAADF and EDS analyzers. Tensile and compression tests
were performed on Instron machines at a strain rate of 10−3 s−1.
Tensile specimens had dimensions of 3 mm diameter and 15 mm
gauge length. The compression specimens were of 8 mm in height

3.
 Results and discussion
The solutionized alloy had a grain size of over 300 /H9262m, as shown
in Fig. 1a. A eutectic structure occurred in between the grains
(Fig. 1b). The presence of the icosahedral i-phase, the cubic W-
phase and the pre-eutectic H-phase [15] was conﬁrmed by electron
diffraction patterns. These phases were also identiﬁed by chemical
analysis by EDS. A HAADF image is shown in Fig. 1c. EDS spec-
tra from the points marked A–D are shown in Fig. 1d. Area ‘A’
in between the eutectic structures is the supersaturated matrix,
whose approximate composition was determined to be Mg–10 at%
Zn. The i-phase is identiﬁed in the lamellar region B, whose com-
position was analyzed to be about 21 at% Mg, 70 at% Zn and 9 at%
Y. Position C was detected to be the H phase (∼16 at% Mg, 60 at%
Zn, 24 at% Y). In its vicinity at D, elemental yttrium phase was also
detected (∼97 at% Y).
After extrusion, the average grain size was 20 ± 6 /H9262m for e430
and 13.8 ± 6 /H9262m for e380, as observed in Fig. 2a and b. A dark banded
region running vertically along the extrusion direction contains
submicron size i-phase particles. In some places, very large particles
of ∼20 /H9262m size are observed, some of which are indicated by arrow-
heads. These were determined to be the W phase. Distribution of
the W-phase particles in the matrix was found to be irregular and
they were spaced 100 /H9262m or more apart. A part of one such W phase
particles is shown in Fig. 3a. Its [1 0 0] diffraction pattern is inset.
The W and i-phase are related by the equation liq. + W → Mg + i at
448 ◦C [4]. Thus, a eutectic mixture of Mg + i can transform to the
W phase upon heating to a temperature above 448 ◦C. On cooling,
i-phase
 can form on the interface of the W-phase with the matrix,
as evidenced in Fig. 3a. A composite diffraction pattern from the W
phase and the i-phase layer is shown in Fig. 3b. TEM micrographs in
Fig. 2c and d show the grain structure and secondary phases in the
extruded alloys after ageing. In case of e430, about 200 nm size par-
ticles occurred continuously along all the grain boundaries, while
in case of e380 the i-phase particles did not occur on all the grain
boundaries.
Transformation of the phases was studied by DSC. DSC curves
for the solutionized and extruded alloys (with ageing) are shown in
Fig. 4. On heating, a shallow peak (H1) at about 300 ◦C and another
(H2) at about 460 ◦C were observed, which correspond to the disso-
lution of the /H92521′precipitates and the i-phase eutectic, respectively.
Upon cooling, three peaks are observed in the eutectic range, at
about 451 ◦C (C1), 436.5 ◦C (C2) and 420 ◦C (C3). These multiple
peaks suggest site speciﬁc nucleation of the i-phase, such as on
grain boundaries and in the matrix. The extrusion temperature of
430 ◦C is within the temperature range of the eutectic, while 380 ◦C
is below it. This may explain the formation of the i-phase particles
on the grain boundaries in the e430 alloy but not much in case of
the e380. The C1 peak does not occur in the alloy extruded at 380 ◦C,
therefore it can be associated with nucleation on the grain bound-
aries. Peak C4 (at about 330 ◦C) corresponds to the formation of the
/H92521′ precipitates. The formation of the i-phase at the grain bound-
aries resulted in a solute depleted zone, as evidenced in the HAADF
micrograph of Fig. 5a. The precipitation of the i-phase will be more
favorable on the grain boundaries because yttrium has very low
solubility and diffusion rate in the matrix.
A dispersion of about 50–100 nm size i-phase particles occurred
in the matrix, some of which are marked by arrows in Fig. 5a. These
were conﬁrmed to be the i-phase from their compositions deter-
mined by EDS analysis, but some of these particles also appeared

It is noticed that elongations to failure are poor, at nearly 12% in
tension in the as-extruded alloy and 8% after ageing. In compression
they are 14–16%. The reason for the low ductility was identiﬁed to
be the large particles of W-phase (Fig. 3) which act as inclusions
because of their large size. To dissolve the W phase a very high
temperature treatment is required. An alternative is to restrict the
size of these particles so that they do not act as inclusions, by hav-
ing alloys of lower alloy contents. The latter approach has been
successful and will be reported elsewhere.
In the as-extruded condition, the e430 alloy showed a yield
asymmetry ratio (YAS) of 0.80. After precipitation, it decreased to
0.72, because of the precipitation [13]. In the e380 alloy, the asym-
metry is lowered from about 0.84 in the as-extruded condition to
about 0.73 after ageing.
These mechanical properties are among the best reported for
magnesium alloys, and unique considering that they are obtained
with grain size of over 10 /H9262m. Comparable strength and ductility
have been reported in extruded Mg97ZnRE2 alloys [19,20], which
are in the same alloy system as studied here, but use another ternary
phase Mg12ZnRE [21] for strengthening. However, the Mg97ZnRE2
alloys have twice the amount of RE element than the alloy studied
here, and their grain size are reported to be much ﬁner.

4. Conclusions
A process is described for obtaining very high strengths in a
Mg–Zn–Y alloy containing quasicrystalline phase. The i-phase is
partly dissolved by solutionizing at a high temperature and then
re-precipitated ﬁnely during a hot extrusion. During solutioniz-
ing, the i-phase dissolves partially, and partially transforms to a
pre-eutectic phase H. The cubic W-Mg3Zn3Y2 phase also forms in
this process, which remains untransformed. This high tempera-
ture process results in a highly supersaturated matrix, which gives
a very high ageing response on ageing. Due to the nano-size i-
phase precipitated on the grain boundaries and in the matrix, and
a high density of precipitation of Mg–Zn phase (/H92521′precipitates) in
the matrix, very high tensile yield strengths of over 350 MPa and
compressive yield strengths of about 250 MPa were obtained with
relatively large average grain sizes of 14–20 /H9262m. The elongation to
failure was limited due to large size cubic W-phase particles which
form during the high temperature solutionizing.
