ABSTRACT 
In the temperature range 77 – 290 K, the microhardness of high entropy alloy CoCrFeNiMn ( С antor alloy) samples 
in three structural states was measured: (i) initial coarse-grained samples (ii) nanocrystalline samples obtained 
by high pressure torsion (HPT) at room temperature and (iii) nanocrystalline samples processed by HPT at liquid 
nitrogen temperature. With a decrease in temperature from 290 K to 77 K, the microhardness of all types of 
samples monotonically increased by about 40 – 60% which indicates the thermally activated nature of plastic 
deformation under the indenter. The highest microhardness was observed in nanocrystalline samples obtained by 
the torsion at room temperature. It is shown that the ratio of Vickers microhardness to the conventional yield 
strength remains constant in the range of 77 – 290 K, and that the ratio value depends on the microstructure of 
samples. In the coarse-grained Cantor alloy samples the normal indentation size effect was observed: the 
microhardness increased with a decrease of the indenter penetration depth; the obtained dependence is discussed 
in the framework of geometrically necessary dislocations model. In the HPT-nanocrystallized samples in the low 
load range, an essential microhardness drop with the load decreasing was observed, being a result of mechanical 
polishing of the sample surface, which destroys the microstructure created under the torsion and weakens the 
surface layer. 
1. Introduction 
One of the current problems of solid state and materials science is the 
development of new structural materials, which have high strength 
characteristics and sufficient plasticity. High value of ultimate strain is a 
particularly important criterion for the possible application of new 
materials at low temperatures, where high-strength materials often 
become brittle [ 1 ]. 
To improve the important characteristics of plasticity and strength of 
metallic materials, such as the microhardness, yield and ultimate 
strength, usually the alloying of metals was applied [ 2 , 3 ]. The formation 
of substitutional or interstitial solid solutions with uniformly distributed 
impurity atoms, as well as the formation of coherent and incoherent 
precipitates in the alloy, leads to a significant strengthening of the alloy. 
In this case, the mechanical characteristics of the alloy depend not only 
on the concentration of the alloying element, but also on thermal 
treatment, which changes the dispersion of the inclusions [ 3 ]. 
In recent years, the processing of metals by various methods of severe 
plastic deformation (SPD), such as equal channel angular pressing and 
high pressure torsion, acquired an important role. The use of these 
methods makes it possible to significantly refine the microstructure of 
materials [ 4 – 6 ]. According to the Hall-Petch relationship, the value of 
the yield strength is inversely proportional to the square root of the grain 
size [ 7 , 8 ], therefore the use of plastic deformation, in particular SPD, 
provides a strong increase in the yield strength. As a rule the hardening 
effect is significantly enhanced if the SPD treatment is carried out at low 
temperatures [ 9 , 10 ]. 
Recently, a new class of materials was developed - high-entropy al -
loys (HEAs) with an fcc lattice, which have high initial values of me -
chanical characteristics (modulus of elasticity, yield and ultimate 
strength) and sufficient plasticity even at liquid helium temperatures 
[ 11 , 12 ]. A feature of these alloys is a large number of constituent ele -
ments (at least five), which are approximately in equiatomic ratio, that 
leads to high distortion of the crystal lattice [ 13 ]. To obtain even higher

4. Discussion 
The results obtained in this paper indicate that the use of the HPT 
method is promising for hardening of the Cantor alloy. The mechanical 
characteristics of the alloy, in particular the microhardness, rapidly in -
crease with an increase in the shear strain γ ( Fig. 3 ), which is propor -
tional to the number of revolutions during torsion and to the distance 
from the disk center. However, the hardening process quickly reaches 
saturation ( Fig. 3 ), since at γ > 50 – 70 an increase in shear strain is no 
longer accompanied by an increase in the number of lattice defects 
(dislocations, twins) and by further structure refinement [ 17 ]. The 
resulting nanocrystalline alloy cannot be further strengthened by 
increasing the number of revolutions. As a result, the microhardness of 
the Cantor alloy after HPT increases approximately 2.5 – 3 times when 
measured in the temperature range T = 77 – 290 K ( Fig. 4 ). 
HPT at liquid nitrogen temperature resulted in lower values of me -
chanical characteristics than HPT at room temperature. The micro -
hardness of samples subjected to cryo-HPT turned out to be about 10% 
lower than that of the samples after RT-HPT ( Fig. 3 ), and this situation 
persisted in the whole temperature range of microhardness measure -
ments T = 77 – 290 K ( Fig. 4 ). The reasons for such a paradoxical effect of 
the temperature at which the torsion occurred on the structural state of 
the samples are not completely clear. Possibly, at very high degrees of 
torsion strain, deformation processes, including martensitic trans -
formations, proceed with different intensities at various temperatures. 
This assumption was confirmed in our structural observations [ 17 ]. 
Correct measurements of microhardness and investigation of the 
influence of various factors (temperature, concentration of impurities, 
etc.) on it presuppose the use of such loads on the indenter, the value of 
which does not affect the microhardness and does not distort the results 
obtained. In this paper, the dependences H

types of samples of the Cantor alloy. It is shown that these dependences 
have a different character for coarse-grained and nanocrystalline sam -

In this paper, for the first time the temperature dependence of the 
microhardness of a high-entropy alloy was obtained ( Fig. 4 ). For all 
types of samples of the Cantor alloy, the microhardness changes 
monotonically in the temperature range 77 – 290 K. It indicates that the 
mechanisms of plastic deformation under the indenter are preserved in 
the whole temperature range studied. A strong increase in microhard -
ness with decreasing temperature, which was observed for both coarse- 
grained and nanocrystalline samples ( Fig. 4 ), agrees with the concept of 
a thermally activated nature of plastic deformation under the indenter. 
Earlier, on the same samples in a wide range of low temperatures, we 
measured the yield strength and the strain rate sensitivity of the flow 
stress [ 19 ]. Based on these results, a rigorous thermoactivation analysis 
was performed and possible physical mechanisms controlling the plastic 
flow in the Cantor alloy were discussed. It is natural to assume that the 
value of microhardness and its dependence on temperature ( Fig. 4 ) will 
be determined by the action of the same mechanisms. In addition, we 
had a unique opportunity to compare the values of the microhardness 
and yield strength of the Cantor alloy in a wide temperature range T =
77 – 290 K. A detailed discussion of this issue is carried out below in 
Section 4.2 . 
4.1. Indentation size effect 
As illustrated in experiments performed on a large number of ma -
terials, the value of the indentation load (the depth of indenter pene -
tration), as a rule, can have a significant effect on the measured value of 
microhardness. This effect is called the ‘indentation size effect ’ . It is 
usually observed in the region of low loads and consists in an increase in 
microhardness as the indentation load decreases. This effect can have 
various natures [ 24 – 34 ], but most often two reasons lead to it: the 
introduction of geometrically necessary dislocations (GNDs) during the 
indentation of crystalline materials that ensures the formation of an 
indent [ 32 – 34 ], and the violation of the homogeneity of the surface 
layer caused by mechanical treatment of the sample surface before 
indentation [ 30 , 31 ].

5. Summary & conclusions 
1. For the first time, the micromechanical properties of CoCrFeNiMn 
high-entropy alloy (Cantor alloy) have been studied in the temper -
ature range 77 – 290 K. The materials of which the microhardness was 
measured, had different structural states: (1) initial coarse-grained 
samples with a grain size d = 4 μ m, (2) nanocrystalline ones ob -
tained by HPT at room temperature (RT-HPT) with grain size d ≈
40 – 80 nm, and finally (3) at liquid nitrogen temperature (cryo-HPT) 
with grain sizes d ≈ 50 – 100 nm, respectively. 
2. It was found that with a decrease in temperature from 290 K to 77 K, 
the microhardness of these samples monotonically increases by 
40 – 60%, which indicates the operation of thermally activated 
mechanisms of the plastic deformation of the material under the 
indenter. 
3. At all studied measuring temperatures T = 77 – 290 K, the materials 
subjected to RT-HPT had higher microhardness values than the 
materials subjected to cryo-HPT. This result confirms but also ex -
tends previous findings of the strength of these two structural states 
[ 17 , 18 ] which were done at 290 K and 77 K only. 
4. At room temperature, the usual indentation size effect was observed 
in coarse-grained samples of Cantor alloy: a decrease in the depth of 
the indenter penetration h (decrease in the load on the indenter P ) is 
accompanied by an increase in microhardness in the region of small 
penetration depths. This dependence H
