Abstract
The influence of V contents (0.6 wt%, 0.8 wt% and 1.0 wt%) on the microstructure and creep behavior of a Nickel-based 
superalloy was investigated. The results revealed that the V content exerted a significant impact on the morphology of carbide. 
Notably, in the alloy containing 0.8 wt% V, coarse blocky M6C carbides formed adjacent to MC carbides, while in the 1.0 wt% 
V alloy, fine granular M6C carbides exhibited a nearly continuous distribution along grain boundaries (GBs). The influence 
of V content on creep properties exhibited significant variations depending on temperature. At 650 °C/1010 MPa, the 1.0 
wt% V alloy, containing a high density of granular M 6C carbides, demonstrated enhanced intergranular bonding strength, 
which contributed to prolonged creep life. In contrast, at higher temperatures (750 °C/620 MPa and 800 °C/500 MPa), GB 
mobility was activated, making GB slip the dominant creep mechanism. The near-continuous distribution of M 6C carbides 
in the 1.0 wt% V alloy restricted GB deformation compatibility, promoting stress localization and an increased density of 
micropores along GBs. As a result, the 0.8 wt% V alloy, with its discrete M6C carbide distribution, exhibited superior creep 
resistance at elevated temperatures.

1 Introduction
The unique high-temperature mechanical properties and 
excellent processing performance of Ni-based superalloys 
have rendered them extensively utilized in aeroengines 
[1–3]. The turbine disc is a crucial thermal component of 
an aerospace engine. The achievement of higher engine per-
formance can be facilitated by elevating turbine inlet gas 
temperatures and compression ratios [ 3–5]. Therefore, to 
meet the requirements of advanced aero-engines, developing 
novel turbine disc superalloys with higher temperature-bear-
ing capacity has become a major challenge. Creep resistance 
is a critical factor governing the safe operation of turbine 
discs under elevated temperature conditions. Recent research 
efforts have consequently prioritized developing novel Ni-
based wrought superalloys with enhanced creep performance 
through precise compositional and microstructural optimiza-
tion [4–6]. Wang et al. [5] investigated the effect of carbon 
content on the microstructures and stress rupture properties 
of a polycrystalline Ni-based superalloy. The results showed 
that the number of carbides increased with carbon content.

Zhao et al. [12] investigated the secondary crack propaga-

to meet the increasing performance demands of modern 
aeroengines. To ensure reliable performance under extreme 
conditions, extensive research focuses on enhancing the 
creep resistance of superalloys.
Experimental studies have consistently identified GBs as 
the predominant weak link for creep deformation in poly -
crystalline superalloys at elevated temperatures [12–14]. GB 
reinforcement can be achieved through two complementary 
strategies: (1) microalloying with GB-strengthening ele-
ments (e.g., B, C, Zr); (2) controlling heat treatment to opti-
mize grain size and carbide precipitation [15, 16]. Kim et al. 
[17] adjusted the fractions of M 23C6 and M C carbides at 
GBs by designing a heat treatment regime for Inconel 740H 
superalloy. The results showed that the stress exerted on the 
GB can be effectively released by the decomposition of MC 
carbides into M23C6, thereby enhancing the creep resistance 
properties of the alloy. Zhou et al. [18] optimized the grain 
size and secondary carbides of a Ni-based superalloy by 
increasing the solution temperature, and obtained a desired 
microstructure of the alloy which exhibited outstanding 
creep property. The heat treatment solely aims to optimize 
the microstructure, while further improving creep properties 
requires compositional optimization. C, B, and Zr are effec-
tive microalloying elements that enhance creep resistance 
by strengthening GB [19, 20]. He et al. [21] demonstrated 
that GB carbides such as M 6C and M 23C6, whose forma -
tion is influenced by carbon content, actually benefit creep 
resistance. Li et al. [22] improved the stress rupture proper-
ties of the K4750 alloy by increasing its carbon content. 
However, excessive carbon content led to the formation of 
larger carbides, which could become potential crack ini-
tiation sites during stress rupture testing. Kontis et al. [ 23] 
demonstrated that boron addition primarily promotes the 
formation of Cr-rich M5B3 borides in a new polycrystalline 
superalloy, while simultaneously suppressing the precipita-
tion of Cr-rich M23C6 carbides. Zr is a typical GB-purifying 
element that can refine both grains and carbides [24]. While 
carbides and borides play a crucial role in improving creep 
resistance, excessive C and B additions promote GB carbide/
boride coarsening, which ultimately degrades mechanical 
properties. This limitation has motivated the exploration of 
alternative alloying strategies to optimize superalloy per -
formance. Vanadium (V) acts as an effective strengthening 
element in Ni-based superalloys. Although typically added 
in limited quantities, V has been successfully incorporated 
in several aeroengine alloys within both American and Rus-
sian alloy systems [25–27]. Studies have shown that V can 
effectively modify the alloy's microstructure by altering 
carbide morphology and γ′ phase characteristics, leading to 
enhanced mechanical properties [25]. Several studies have 
confirmed the beneficial effects of V on creep resistance 
[25–27]. However, the fundamental mechanisms responsible 
for these improvements remain incompletely understood. A 
systematic investigation of the impact of V on microstruc-
tural evolution and creep behavior in Ni-based superalloys 
is therefore critically needed.
To address the need for improved creep-resistant materi-
als, we investigate a vanadium-alloyed Ni-based superalloy 
developed for turbine discs. The alloy's remarkable thermal 
stability at 800 °C positions it as a promising candidate for 
advanced aeroengines with elevated service temperature 
requirements [28–30]. This study systematically investigates 
the microstructural evolution and creep behavior of this Ni-
based superalloy, elucidating the fundamental mechanisms 
by which V influences high-temperature creep resistance. 
The findings provide crucial insights for optimizing turbine 
disc alloy design, demonstrating how strategic vanadium 
additions can enhance creep resistance while maintaining 
cost-effectiveness through precise microstructural control.

3 Results
3.1 Initial Microstructures
Figure  1 presents the grain microstructures and γ ′ phase 
morphology for alloys with varying V contents (0.6 wt%, 
0.8 wt%, 1.0 wt%) following solution and aging heat treat-
ments. Quantitative analysis reveals that the 0.8 V and 
1.0 V alloys exhibit slightly coarser grain sizes (12–14 μm) 
compared to the 0.6 V alloy (~ 10 μm). The γ ′ phase dis-
tribution displays a characteristic bimodal morphology: (1) 
primary γ′ precipitates (> 500 nm) predominantly located 
at GBs (Fig.  1d-e), and (2) secondary γ ′ phases distributed 
throughout the grain interiors (Fig.  1f-h). Statistical meas-
urements indicate average secondary γ′ sizes of 116 ± 10 nm, 
94 ± 10 nm, and 98 ± 10 nm for the 0.6 V, 0.8 V, and 1.0 V 
alloys, respectively. While a modest reduction in secondary 
γ′ size occurs with increasing V content, the comparable 
dimensions between the 0.8 V and 1.0 V alloys suggest that 
this effect diminishes at higher concentrations. These results 
collectively demonstrate that V additions within this compo-
sition range exhibit limited influence on both grain growth 
kinetics and γ′ phase evolution in the investigated Ni-based 
superalloy.
Microstructural analysis (Fig. 2) reveals two distinct car-
bide populations: (1) irregular blocky MC-type carbides rich 
in Ti and Nb, and (2) M 6C-type carbides rich in Mo exhib-
iting both fine granular and larger blocky morphologies. 
Energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) results (Fig. 3) 
confirmed these compositional distinctions. The Mo-rich 
M6C carbides appear as white particles along GBs, while the 
(Ti, Nb)-rich M C carbides present as large, blocky phases 
with a characteristic lumpy gray contrast. Darker contrast 
particles correspond to primary γ′ precipitates, as annotated 
in Fig. 2. Notably, the 1.0 V alloy demonstrates a substantial 
increase in granular M 6C carbide density compared to the 
other compositions, forming an essentially continuous GB 
network.
While MC carbide distribution shows some heterogeneity, 
their size and population density remain relatively constant 
across all V content alloys, with area fractions of approxi-
mately 0.3%–0.35% and average particle sizes around 
3–5 μm. A significant microstructural observation is the 
consistent spatial association between large blocky M6C car-
bides and MC carbides in both 0.8 V and 1.0 V alloys, with 
notably higher M6C carbide prevalence in the 0.8 V compo-
sition. Importantly, the principal microstructural influence of 
V content in this alloy is mediated through its effect on GB 
M6C carbide formation and distribution patterns.

4 Discussion
4.1 Effect of V Content on the Microstructure
As demonstrated by the aforementioned results, the effect of 
V manifests predominantly through its impact on carbides. 
To determine the V distribution within the microstructure, 
SEM–EDS analysis was performed to quantify V concen-
trations in MC carbides, blocky M6C carbides, and γ matrix 
(Table 2). The results revealed that the V concentration in 
MC carbides remained relatively constant regardless of the 
alloy's overall V content and was consistently lower than 
that in the matrix. In contrast, M6C carbides exhibited higher 
V concentrations than the matrix, with their V content 
increased proportionally with the alloy's V content. These 
findings suggest that V played a beneficial role in promot -
ing the precipitation of M 6C carbides. It is well established 
that the formation of M6C carbides occurs through elemental 
diffusion and interfacial segregation processes [36]. Dur -
ing heat treatment, Mo preferentially segregates to GBs and 
reacts with carbon from decomposing MC carbides to form 
M6C carbides. In this study, the spatial association between 
large blocky M6C carbides and MC carbides suggests an in-
situ transformation mechanism from MC precursors.

4.3 Creep Deformation Mechanisms
The dominant creep mechanisms exhibit significant temper-

creep conditions, thermal activation enables two concur -
rent processes: (1) dislocation climb assisted by multiple 
slip systems [39, 40], allowing dislocations to circumvent γ′ 
precipitates, and (2) enhanced GB mobility that facilitates 
coordinated plastic deformation through boundary migra-
tion. These mechanisms collectively contribute to increased 
creep strain accumulation. Conversely, under low-temper -
ature creep conditions, severely constrained GB mobility 
necessitates intracrystalline dislocation slip as the predomi-
nant deformation mechanism. Under the investigated creep 
conditions of 650 °C/1010 MPa, the thermal–mechanical 
coupling exhibited distinct characteristics. The thermal driv-
ing force remained insufficient to activate GB migration, and 
the applied stress preferentially promoted dislocation nuclea-
tion and glide. This stress-dominated regime led to progres-
sive dislocation accumulation at γ /γ′ interfaces. When the 
interfacial dislocation density reached a critical threshold, 
the stress concentration enabled dislocations to shear into 
the γ′ precipitates. This cutting process generates SFs within 
the ordered γ′ phase, representing a key deformation mecha-
nism under these conditions.
Additionally, GB carbides are a critical factor influenc-
ing the creep life of Ni-based superalloys. The underly -

tion mechanisms. When creep at a lower temperature 
(650 °C/1010 MPa), extensive dislocations and SFs are 
generated within the grains. These defects subsequently slip

ultimately acting as initiation points for creep fracture and 
degrading the alloy's creep resistance. This mechanistic 
understanding explains why the 0.8 V alloy demonstrated 
the lowest creep life and minimal strain accumulation under 
650 °C/1010 MPa conditions.
In contrast, the 1.0 V alloy showed markedly different 
behavior. Although it contained larger carbides than other 
alloys, its nearly continuous network of M6C particles along 
GBs provided two key benefits: (1) enhanced intergranu -
lar cohesion, and (2) effective redistribution of localized 
stresses. These synergistic effects contributed to significantly 
delayed creep rupture. Notably, microstructural characteri-

precipitates throughout the creep process, highlighting the 
unique role of M 6C distribution in governing creep per -

this alloy exhibits distinct creep mechanisms at intermedi-

tures, deformation is primarily governed by the formation 
of stacking faults, whereas dislocation slip dominates the 
high-temperature regime. These differences in deformation 
mechanisms consequently alter the role of grain boundary 
carbides. Tai et al. [43] investigated the effect of carbide 
content on deformation mechanisms and creep behavior of a 
wrought superalloy. The SFs and microtwins formed at ser-
vice temperature, while the amount of GB carbides increased 
with higher carbon content. These carbides hindered GB 
crack formation and effectively improved creep properties. 
The creep deformation mechanism and the carbide content-
creep performance correlation are consistent with this study. 
Therefore, under the investigated deformation conditions, 
the creep resistance of the alloy demonstrates a positive cor-
relation with the number of GB carbides.

(750–800 °C), GB sliding becomes the dominant deforma-

M6C carbides comprising both fine and coarse precipitates 
effectively restricts GB mobility, thereby improving creep 
resistance and prolonging service life. Nevertheless, micro-

tially occurs along M C/γ-matrix interfaces, indicating this 
remains the primary failure pathway despite the improved 
GB stability. Li et al. [44] also demonstrated that the nuclea-

the creep damage in this alloy. Stress concentration result-

sliding, primarily accounts for GB micropore nucleation.

although carbide precipitation strengthens GBs, excessive 
carbide continuity ultimately deteriorates creep resistance.
5 Conclusions
The influence of V contents (0.6 wt%, 0.8 wt% and 1.0 wt%) 
on the microstructure and creep behavior of a Ni-based 
superalloy under various temperature and stress conditions 
was comprehensively investigated. The main conclusions of 
this work are as follows:
1. There are two types of carbides in this Ni-based superal-
loy, blocky (Nb, Ti)-rich MC carbides distributed within 
grains or grain boundaries (GB), granular or blocky

alloy, larger blocky M 6C carbides precipitate near M C

nearly continuously at GB in the 1.0 V alloy.
2. The transgranular fracture is the primary mechanism 
for creep deformation of this Ni-based superalloy at 
650 °C/1010 MPa. When elevating creep temperature 
to 800 °C, micropores can easily form along GB, lead-
ing to a dominant fracture mechanism characterized by 
micropore accumulation.
3. When creep at 650 °C/1010 MPa, the dominant creep 
mechanism was attributed to stacking faults within 
grains. The presence of a significant quantity of M 6C 
carbides in the 1.0 V alloy enhanced inter-grain bonding 
forces, thereby effectively prolonging the creep life.
4. During creep at 750 °C/620 MPa and 800 °C/500 MPa, 
GB movement was easily activated, and GB slip became 
the dominant creep mechanism. The nearly continuous 
distribution of M6C carbides in the 1.0 V alloy hampered 
the coordinated deformation ability of GBs, thereby 
intensifying stress concentration at these boundaries and 
providing additional sources for micropores. Conversely, 
the 0.8 V alloy, with more discretely distributed M 6C 
carbides, exhibited an extended creep life. Thus, a V 
content of approximately 0.8 wt% is more suitable for 
these Ni-based superalloys.
