In combination of the Virtual Fields Method and MoiréInterferometry, a novel method to characterize the elastic 
constants of single crystal Ni-based superalloys, called the Orientation-Identiﬁed Virtual Fields Method (OIVFM), 
is proposed in this study. The most outstanding advantage of the OIVFM is that it is able to characterize all the 
elastic constants of a single crystal Ni-based superalloy using a single test without the given parameter of crystal 
orientation. In this paper, the theory of the OIVFM is ﬁrstly introduced in detail to explain the identiﬁcation 
process. Simulations and error analyses are conducted to verify the feasibility of the OIVFM. The simulation 
results match well with the reference values and the error is less than 1 GPa for all the elastic constants. Finally, 
a single crystal Ni-based superalloy (DD407) is used for the real experiments. Moiré Interferometry is applied 
to measure the full-ﬁeld strain, with which the OIVFM is then utilized to calculate the elastic constants of the 
material. The experiments provide satisfactory results and show a small discreteness. Therefore, it is concluded 
that the proposed OIVFM can be successfully applied to characterize all the elastic constants of single crystal 
Ni-based superalloys. By using the OIVFM, it is not necessary to determine the crystal orientation in advance, 
and as a result, the eﬃciency of experiment can be improved and its costs can also be reduced. 
1. Introduction 
Ni-based single crystal superalloys have been widely utilized in ad- 
vanced aircrafts for their excellent performance under harsh service 
conditions. The existing literatures indicate that Ni-based single crystal 
superalloys have superior creep and fatigue strength along with good 
oxidation resistance, which makes them an ideal material for aircraft 
engines [1,2] . 
In order to evaluate the properties of a Ni-based single crystal su- 
peralloy, measurement of its mechanical constants is necessary. For 
isotropic materials, standard tests, such as tensile and shear tests us- 
ing strain gauges or extensometers, are utilized to determine their me- 
chanical constants. However, for a single crystal alloy, there are more 
requirements and diﬃculties in the measurement when the standard 
tests are performed. Firstly, the single crystal alloy is anisotropic [3] . In 
the standard test, the specimens should be manufactured in diﬀerent di- 
rections to obtain all the mechanical constants, and thus the test cost is 
high, and the eﬃciency is low. According to the existing research [3–5] , 
the orientation of the single crystal alloy has a signiﬁcant inﬂuence on 
its mechanical properties. Thus, the orientation should be determined 
before the measurement. There are several techniques to measure the

orientation of a single crystal alloy. However, these techniques normally 
require expensive equipment such as electron-backscattered diﬀraction 
(EBSD) and x-ray diﬀraction (XRD) [6,7] . Finally, given that the fabri- 
cation of a single crystal alloy is diﬃcult [8] , the specimens used for the 
standard tests have an extremely high cost. 
Therefore, in order to measure the mechanical constants of Ni-based 
single crystal alloys more eﬃciently and cost-eﬀectively, a novel method 
has been developed in this work combining the Virtual Fields Method 
(VFM) [9] and Moiré Interferometry (MI) [10] . The VFM, which was 
proposed in 1989 [9] , is known as a powerful method to determine 
all the elastic mechanical constants from the full ﬁeld deformation in 
one test. The VFM has been applied in orthotropic materials, such as 
composites, to obtain the four in-plane elastic constants at the same 
time [11,12] . Besides, it has been utilized to characterize paper board 
[13] , biomaterials [14] , thermal barrier coatings [15] and other materi- 
als [16–18] .Therefore, the VFM is eﬀective for mechanical characteriza- 
tion of anisotropic materials and is a promising method for application 
with Ni-based single crystal alloys. 
However, when applying the VFM to single crystal alloys, there are 
still some diﬃculties. One of them is that the properties of a single crys- 
tal alloy are orientation-dependent. In traditional VFM, if the orientation

elastic constants of a Ni-based single crystal alloy when the orientation 
of the single crystal alloy is unknown. Firstly, the constitutive model 
of Ni-based single crystal superalloy is introduced and the principle of 
the OIVFM is explained in detail. Then, simulated experiments are con- 
ducted numerically to verify the feasibility of the OIVFM and the ac- 
curacy of the proposed method is also analyzed. After that, real exper- 
iments on a Ni-based single crystal alloy are performed and the elastic 
constants are measured by the OIVFM and Moiré Interferometry. The 
experiments provide reasonable results and show a very small discrete- 
ness.

However, for a single crystal alloy, the crystal orientation must be 
determined in advance to determine the oﬀ-axis angle ( 𝜃). The measure-

in this work, the OIVFM is proposed to determine the crystal orientation 
from the VFM calculation. 
The idea of the OIVFM is based on the sensitivity of the calculated 
results to the virtual strain ﬁelds. In the VFM calculation, if there are 
few errors in the real strain ﬁeld, the selection of virtual strain ﬁelds 
will have little inﬂuence on the results. Then, after the strain ﬁeld along 
the coordinate axis ( ɛ xx , ɛ yy , ɛ xy ) is measured, a random value of the 
oﬀ-axis angle is used to obtain the strain ﬁeld along the principle axis 
( ɛ 11 , ɛ 22 , ɛ 12 ) using Eq. (10) . If the correct oﬀ-axis angle is selected, the 
calculated results using diﬀerent virtual strain ﬁelds will not have a sig- 
niﬁcant diﬀerence. Therefore, the crystal orientation can be determined 
by ﬁnding the oﬀ-axis angle which can minimize the variance between 
the results obtained with diﬀerent groups of virtual strain ﬁelds. The 
detailed procedure of the OIVFM is explained in Fig. 2 . 
From Fig. 2 , ﬁrstly, the step of the orientation angle, the searching 
range of the orientation angle, and the number of virtual strain ﬁelds are 
given as known quantities. Then, an initial orientation angle is used for 
the calculation. With the orientation angle and a group of virtual strain 
ﬁelds, a group of mechanical parameters can be calculated by the VFM. 
As n groups of virtual strain ﬁelds are used for the calculation, n groups 
of mechanical parameters can be obtained. After that, the variance of n 
groups of mechanical parameters is calculated and stored. Finally, the 
orientation angle that can minimize the variance of n groups of mechan- 
ical parameters is the correct crystal orientation.

4.2. Discussion 
Experiments on a single crystal Ni-based superalloy (DD407) are con- 
ducted and the results are presented in Table 5 and 6 . 
Table 5 provides the orientation angles and elastic constants of 
the material obtained by the OIVFM under three diﬀerent loads. From 
Table 5 , it can be observed that the calculated orientation angles with 
three diﬀerent loads show a very small discreteness and the error is less 
than 3.7 ∘. In terms of elastic constants, the errors of the three constants 
( Q 11 , Q 12 , Q 66 ) are all less than 6 GPa. 
Then, three groups of results with diﬀerent values of rigid-body ro- 
tation when the load is 818 N are listed in Table 6 . The error of the 
orientation angles is less than 0.7 ∘ and the errors of all the three con- 
stants ( Q 11 , Q 12 , Q 66 ) are less than 3 GPa. 
The results in Table 5 and 6 verify the feasibility of the application 
of the proposed method OIVFM to real experiments. Both the orienta- 
tion angle and elastic constants are calculated in one mechanical test 
without extra procedures, such as EBSD or XRD, to measure the orien- 
tation angle. Additionally, in order to explain the rationality of the cal- 
culated orientation angle, we have used another identiﬁcation method 
to calculate the angle of the experimental specimens [29] . The identiﬁ- 
cation method proposed in [29] is based on the fringe patterns, which 
has the advantages of automatic, better robustness, and high reliability. 
The angle calculated by the inversion method in [29] diﬀers from the

The calculated results with diﬀerent loads are similar although there

symmetrical, as shown in Fig. 10 and 12 . On the other hand, for the 
loading conﬁguration in this experiment (a diametrically compressed 
disk), strain concentration occurs around the top and bottom part of 
the specimens. The fringes are very dense in these areas, which induces 
some errors when calculating the strain ﬁeld. 
5. Conclusion 
In this work, the OIVFM is proposed to calculate the elastic constants 
of single crystal Ni-based superalloys with high eﬃciency and accuracy. 
The theory of the OIVFM is explained in detail. Simulated and real ex- 
periments are conducted as the application of the OIVFM. According to 
the results, the following conclusions can be drawn: 
(1) To measure the mechanical properties of a single crystal alloy 
when the orientation angle is unknown, the theory of the OIVFM 
is proposed. Combining a diametrically compressed disk conﬁgu- 
ration and MoiréInterferometry, the experimental plan is formu- 
lated in detail. Then, simulated experiments and error analyses 
are conducted. The results show that the OIVFM is able to calcu- 
late the orientation angle and all the three elastic constants at the 
same time and the errors of all the constants are less than 1 GPa. 
(2) The elastic constants of a single crystal Ni-based superalloy 
(DD407) are characterized using the proposed method (OIVFM) 
and Moiré Interferometry. The experimental results show very 
small discreteness and the errors of all the constants are less than 
6 GPa. Therefore, with the OIVFM, all the three elastic constants 
of a single crystal alloy can be obtained in one test and it is un- 
necessary to measure the orientation angle in advance, which can 
save a substantial amount of time and cost. 
(3) The OIVFM is proposed for mechanical characterization of 
anisotropic materials. The most outstanding advantage is that it 
can calculate all the in-plane elastic constants even when the con- 
stitutive relation is not fully known. Furthermore, the OIVFM is 
a promising method for application with other anisotropic mate- 
rials, such as composites and additive manufacturing materials.
