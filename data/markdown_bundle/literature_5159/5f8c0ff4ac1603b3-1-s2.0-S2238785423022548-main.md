abstract
The dilemma concerning the trade-off between the strength and ductility of alloys has
persistently been a signiﬁcant problem in engineering applications. Conventional techniques
such as grain reﬁnement, severe plastic deformation, and precipitation strengthening usually
increase strength at the expense of ductility. High entropy alloys with dual-phase structures,
on the other hand, generally display excellent strength-ductility synergy. In this study, we
attempted to mix pre-alloyed powders of two distinct phases (body-centered cubic - bcc and
face-centered cubic - fcc) to fabricate AlCoCrFeMnNi HEAs with a dual-phase structure by
selective laser melting. Furthermore, we investigate the impact of varying heat treatment
temperatures (900
/C14 C, 1000 /C14 C, and 1100 /C14 C) on its phase composition, microstructure, and
mechanical properties. The results show that with an increase in the heat treatment tem-
perature, the volume of the fcc phase increases gradually and the volume of the bcc phase
decreases. The columnar structure in the fcc phase transforms into a cellular structure, and a
spinodal decomposition is observed with the bcc phase. As the heat treatment temperature
increases, the yield strength decreases while the elongation substantially increases, with the
samples heat-treated at 1100
/C14 C showing the best comprehensive properties (yield strength
~700 MPa, tensile strength ~950 MPa, and ~15% ductility).

1. Introduction
High entropy alloys (HEAs) consist of ﬁve or more elements,
with each element having a nearly equal atomic composition
(varying between 5% and 35%) and all these elements can be
considered as solute atoms [ 1e5]. Surprisingly, the combina-
tion of multiple elements does not lead to the formation of
complex and numerous intermetallics, but instead generates
disordered or ordered solid solution structures (mostly with a
single phase) including face-centered cubic ( fcc)[ 6,7], body-
centered cubic ( bcc)[ 8,9], or hexagonal close-packed ( hcp)

their superior properties that outperform the traditional
alloys, such as high hardness and strength, good wear
resistance, excellent oxidation resistance, and good
high-temperature stability [ 16e25]. As a result, HEAs have
immense application potential in various industrial sectors.
The conﬂict between the strength and ductility of alloys
has been a long-standing problem in engineering applications
[26,27]. It has been found that fcc-structured HEAs exhibit good
toughness but low strength, whereas alloys with a bcc struc-
ture have high strength but poor ductility. In recent years,
various attempts have been developed to address the
strength-ductility trade-off [ 28e31], heat treatment is one of
the effective ways to modify the microstructure and improve
the mechanical properties of HEAs [ 32e37]. Niu et al. studied
the annealing of Al

Additive manufacturing of high-entropy alloys primarily
relies on traditional strengthening mechanisms, including
grain reﬁnement [ 39], solid solution strengthening [ 40,41],
dislocation strengthening [ 25], and precipitation strength-
ening [ 42]. Additionally, post-process heat treatment also
plays a critical role in alloys fabricated by additive
manufacturing (AM) techniques, such as selective laser
melting (SLM). It is utilized to eliminate metallurgical defects,
and residual stress, and to optimize material properties by
tailoring microstructures through phase formation [ 43].
Several studies have investigated the processing of metallic
materials including HEAs produced through AM, which have
shown superior mechanical properties compared to those
obtained from conventional manufacturing techniques such
as melting or casting [ 44e48]. However, there are no detailed
reports available for HEAs processed by AM and subsequently
treated by post-heat-treatment. Accordingly, in this work, we
have successfully fabricated AlCoCrFeMnNi HEA by adjusting
the ratio of two different kinds of pre-alloyed HEA powders for
the ﬁrst time [ 49]. Furthermore, to investigate the effect of
heat treatment temperature on the microstructure and me-
chanical properties of the considered HEA. Inspired by the
previous reports on the AlCoCrFeNi series high-entropy alloys
[32e34,36,37], the HEA samples were heat-treated at 900
/C14 C,
1000 /C14 C, and 1100 /C14 C, respectively. The phase evolution and
mechanical properties of different heat-treated samples were
discussed systematically.
2. Experiment
2.1. Sample preparation
The feedstock material comprised of pre-alloyed powders of
equimolar AlCoCrFeNi and CoCrFeNiMn (VILORY, Jiangsu,
China) was produced via inert gas atomization. The powder
particles show a particle size distribution ranging between
15 mm and 53 mm, with an average diameter of 36 mm. To
fabricate the HEAs with a composite structure, the Al
11.1-
Co20Cr20Fe20Ni20Mn8.9 (Al0.5) alloy was designed, which is a
mixture of AlCoCrFeNi (52.9 wt%) and CoCrFeNiMn (47.1%
wt.%), respectively. Both the HEA pre-alloyed powders were
mixed using the planetary ball mill, with a speed of 200 rpm
for 1 h, which ensures the powders were uniformly mixed. An
HBD-100 printer (from HBD, Guangdong, China) was used to
produce the Al
0.5 HEAs, and the optimized processing pa-
rameters were as follows: layer thickness e 30 mm, hatch
spacing e 70 mm, laser power e 50 W, scanning speed e
300 mm/s. Post-heat treatments were conducted in a JXR1400-
20 mufﬂe furnace with the following temperatures: 900
/C14 C,
1000 /C14 C, and 1100 /C14 C, respectively for 1 h. The heat-treated
samples were cooled in air and the procedure is illustrated
in Fig. 1.

2.2. Microstructural characterization
The top (cross-section) and side (longitudinal) surfaces of the
SLM fabricated Al 11.1Co20Cr20Fe20Ni20Mn8.9 samples were
subjected to phase analysis using an X-ray diffractometer
(XRD, 18 kW D/MAX2500V þ/PC) with a copper target (of
wavelength l ¼ 0.15406 nm). In this experiment, the samples
were scanned for the 2 q range 20
/C14 e100/C14 with a scan speed of
6/C14 /min and a step size of 0.02 /C14 . The tube voltage and current
were set at 40 kV and 250 mA, respectively. Prior to the XRD
scan, the bulk specimens were polished ﬂat using 1000-grit
sandpaper. For the microstructural observation, the samples
were polished according to standard metallographic proced-
ures and were etched with aqua regia for 15 e20 s. The
microstructural observation was carried out using a scanning
electron microscope (SEM) (HITACHI SU-1500). The chemical
composition of the specimens was analyzed by energy
dispersive spectroscopy (EDS). The grain size distribution was
detected using electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD). Prior to
testing, the samples were electrolytically double-blasted with
a 10% perchloric acid solution to remove the presence of
possible surface stresses. The microstructure of the SLM
sample after 1000
/C14 C heat treatment was characterized using a
JEM-2010F transmission electron microscope (TEM).
2.3. Mechanical testing
The direction of the tensile specimens is perpendicular to the
printing direction (X-Z). Dog-bone-shaped tensile specimens
were cut from the bulk printed parts by electrical discharge
machining with a thickness of 1.2 mm, gage length of 14 mm,
and 3 mm width. The experiment was conducted using a
strain rate of 1 /C2 10
/C0 3 s/C0 1 and a tensile speed of 0.84 mm/min
at ambient temperature (~25 /C14 C). An extensometer was used
to measure the deformation using the tensile test. All tests
were repeated at least three times to assert the reproducibility
of the samples. The fracture surfaces after the tensile tests
were characterized by SEM.
3. Results and discussion
3.1. Diffraction analysis
The X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of Al 0.5 alloy in the as-
SLM condition and after various post-heat treatments are
presented in Fig. 2 (a). Both the powder and as-SLM samples
show the coexistence of both bcc and fcc phases. The details of
(111) fcc and (110) bcc peaks are extended in Fig. 2(bef). Like the
as-printed SLM samples, coexistence of bcc (A2 and B2) and fcc
phases was observed in the SLM samples after all three post-
heat treatment conditions. However, after heat treatment, the
intensity of the fcc peaks signiﬁcantly increases. With an in-
crease in the heat treatment temperature, the volume of the
fcc phase increases at the expense of the bcc phase. At the
highest considered temperature (1100
/C14 C), the intensity of the
fcc peaks reaches the maximum and the bcc peaks reaches
the minimum.
In the SLM-fabricated samples, a peak for Al 2O3 was
detected, possibly due to the formation of oxide splatters at
the trajectory edges during the SLM process. Additionally, for
the samples heat-treated at 900
/C14 C, apart from the bcc and fcc
peaks, there exists a peak of the intermetallic compound
sigma (s) phase, which is hard and brittle [ 13,50]. The s phase
consists of a tetragonal structure concentrated mostly with Cr
and Fe phases, which may increase the hardness of the alloy,
but hampers the fracture toughness and tensile ductility.

In Fig. 8, it can be observed that annealing twins occurs in

0.5 SLM alloy heat-treated at 1100 /C14 C.
Reports show that annealing twins are a common
crystallographic feature in various deformed and subse-
quently annealed fcc metals and alloys (such as Cu [ 60] and Ni
[61,62]) with low and medium stacking fault energies (SFEs). In
addition, twin boundaries (TBs) can act as slip barriers as well
as active sources for dislocation multiplication and inﬂuence
mechanical and physical properties in metallic materials
[63e67]. The presence of twin boundaries in the matrix can
hinder dislocation movement during plastic deformation
thereby offering a strengthening effect.

gation. The 1000 /C14 C heat-treated sample exhibits a sYS of
~850 MPa and an ultimate strength (sUTS) of ~1020 MPa with a ε
of ~7.7%. On the other hand, the sample heat treated at 1100 /C14 C
shows a sYS of ~700 MPa and a sUTS ~950 MPa with a ε of ~15%.
The fracture surfaces of the Al0.5 alloy both in the as-
printed SLM state and after heat treatment can be seen in
Fig. 11. The fracture morphology of the as-printed SLM sample
shows the following features: a river-like pattern and a tough
dimple structure formed by the distribution of both bcc and fcc
phases, as shown in Fig. 11 (a and b), indicating a mixed frac-
ture mechanism operating in these samples that is composed
of both brittle and ductile features. After heat treatment,
cleavages in the fracture surface are absent and only dimples
are present suggesting a change in the fracture mode from
hybrid to ductile mode.
4. Conclusions
In the present study, AlCoCrFeMnNi high-entropy alloy sam-
ples were prepared by selective laser melting, and the alloy
was subsequently heat treated at 900
/C14 C, 1000 /C14 C, and 1100 /C14 C,
respectively for 1 h followed by air-cooling. The effects of heat
treatment on its phase composition, microstructure, and
mechanical properties were investigated systematically. The
following conclusions can be drawn from the present study:
(1) The Al
0.5 HEA, prepared by SLM using mixed powders as
raw materials, consists of a dual-phase composite
structure composed of fcc and bcc (A2/B2) phases. The
distribution of the fcc and bcc phases corroborates the
vortex ﬂow in the melt pool caused by Marangoni con-
vection, where the fcc phase is rich in Mn and the bcc
phase is rich in Al.
(2) After heat treatment at 900
/C14 C, 1000 /C14 C, and 1100 /C14 C, the
phase composition and microstructure of the Al 0.5 alloy
changes signiﬁcantly. As the heat treatment tempera-
ture increases, the fcc phase gradually increases, while
the volume of the bcc phase decreases. The columnar
structure in the fcc phase transforms into a cellular
structure after heat treatment, and the bcc phase un-
dergoes a spinodal decomposition, resulting in a net-
like interwoven structure. The grains observed along
the top surface (cross-section) were small and randomly
distributed, while the side surface (longitudinal)
exhibited elongated grains that spanned multiple layers
of the melt pool. After heat treatment, the fcc phase
shows grain growth, and the precipitation of the fcc
phase in the bcc structure breaks the elongated grains
into cellular ones.
(3) With an increase in the heat treatment temperature,
the yield strength decreases, while the elongation
signiﬁcantly increases, resulting in an excellent
strength-ductility combination in the alloy. The
1000
/C14 C heat-treated sample has a yield strength of
~850 MPa and a tensile strength of ~1020 Mpa with a
ductility of ~7.7%; while the 1100
/C14 C heat-treated
sample has a yield strength of ~700 Mpa and a tensile
strength of ~950 Mpa with a ductility of ~15%. The
fracture mode operating in the SLM sample is a hybrid
mode or mixed mode consisting of both brittle and
ductile features and after heat treatment, the fracture
mode changes predominantly to ductile mode.
