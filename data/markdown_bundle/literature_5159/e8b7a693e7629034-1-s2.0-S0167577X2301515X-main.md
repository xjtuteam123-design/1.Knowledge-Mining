ABSTRACT 
Refractory high entropy alloys are an obstacle to their use as engineering applications due to their low room 
temperature ductility and high density. In the present work, a new low-density Nb-Mo-Zr-Ti RHEA was prepared 
by vacuum arc melting method. The as-cast alloy exhibits a single BCC phase with lower density and higher 
hardness of 7.4331 g/cm
3 
and 522 HV. The compressive yield strength of 1462 MPa at room temperature, peak 
strength of 2158 MPa, and strain at break of 24.6 %. The specific yield strength of this alloy is the highest among 
all other reported RHEAs, and the ultra-high specific yield strength and excellent strong plastic synergistic effect 
provide important experience in designing and preparing lightweight structural materials for practical 
applications. 
1. Introduction 
In recent years, the rapid development of high entropy alloys, 
especially refractory high entropy alloys (RHEAs) can form a single solid 
solution structure and has excellent wear and corrosion resistance, high 
temperature oxidation resistance, mechanics and other properties and 
become a hot spot for researchers [1 – 4] . However, refractory high- 
entropy alloys generally have high densities ( > 10 g/cm
3
) and poor 
room-temperature ductility, which limits their use in aerospace appli -
cations. Senkov et al. [5] first developed two refractory high-entropy 
alloys, NbMoTaW and NbMoTaWV, which maintain a single BCC 
structure, and are currently hot spots in the research field of refractory 
high-entropy alloys. However, their higher-than-expected densities 
(13.75 g/cm
3
) and lower-than-expected room temperature ductility 
(fracture strain is only 2.6 %), which seriously limits its further devel -
opment and engineering applications in aerospace and other fields [6] . 
2. Material and methods 
The alloy with nominal composition quaternary equiatomic Nb-Mo- 
Zr-Ti LRHEA was fabricated by WK-II type non-self-consuming vacuum 
arc furnace using pure metals of Nb, Mo, Zr and Ti ( > 99.9 % wt %). The 
alloy ingots (60 g by weight) were remelted at least 8 times, and in 
addition, the alloy ingots were kept in a liquid state for 10 min during 
each melting and were turned before each remelting to achieve chemical 
homogeneity. Equilibrium phase diagrams were constructed using 
thermal - calc (2020b) software and TCHEA3 (high entropy) database. 
The physical phase was examined by X-ray diffractometer of type 
D8 & advance. The microstructures of the specimens were characterized 
using a Super 55VP scanning electron microscope with a companion 
piggybacked energy spectrometer (EDS) and electron backscatter 
diffraction (EBSD). 
The hardness was tested using a TMHV-1000Z microhardness tester, 
the specimen was subjected to an indenter load of 100 g, the holding 
time was 15 s, 15 points were measured, and the average value was 
calculated after removing the maximum and minimum values. The 
density of the alloy was determined by Archimedes method, and the 
specimen was averaged after 5 measurements in the same state. The 
compression test was carried out using an ETM 105D universal testing 
machine at room temperature with a strain rate of 1x10
-3
/s and a sample 
size of Ф 4 × 6 mm.

Fig. 2 (a-c) shows the BSE map, grain size map, XRD map, kernel- 
averaged orientation deviation (KAM) map and inverse polarity figure 
(IPF) of NbMoZrTiAl LRHEA. The as-cast microstructure of NbMoZrTi 
LRHEA can be seen in Fig. 2 (a), which shows that the microstructure of 
the alloy is a typical dendritic organization, with grey and black areas 
corresponding to the dendritic region (DR) and inter-dendritic region 
(IR), respectively. The elemental distribution mapping of the alloys in 
the figure shows that the high melting point Nb and Mo elements are 
enriched in DR, the low melting point Zr is enriched in IR, and Ti ele -
ments are almost uniformly distributed in both DR and IR. The differ -
ence in the elemental distributions between DR and IR may originate 
from the difference in the melting points of the elements. In addition, the 
thick layers of dark-grey Zr-rich separates the grains of light-grey Nb- 
and Mo-rich phases from each other, and some GBs are not completely 
wetted by the melt (non-zero contact angle). The reason for this may be 
the so-called incomplete GB wetting [9] . Fig. 2 (b) shows the grain size 
distribution of the alloy, and the average grain size of the alloy was 
calculated to be 34.1 μ m. Fig. 2 (c) shows the XRD pattern of the alloy, 
which is a single-phase body-centered cubic structure, which is in 
accordance with the phase organization predicted by the phase diagram. 
The IPF plot shows an alternating distribution of large and fine equiaxed 
grains, and this grain size distribution is mainly due to the repeated 
remelting, reheating, and cooling during the melting process. And 
bonding of different grains and energy differences between elements 
affect the overall mechanical and thermal properties of the alloy [10] . 
The crystal growth direction in the IPF is not homogeneous, and the 
crystal growth direction is not well defined. In the KAM diagram, the 
local orientation deviation is closely related to the geometrically 
necessary dislocations, and the green color approximates the presence of 
dislocations, which can be seen in the figure mainly within and near the 
grain boundaries. Similarly, the color in the KAM diagram reflects the 
magnitude of the Schmidt coefficients, and the yellow color indicates 
lower Schmidt coefficients and red color indicates higher Schmidt co -
efficients, which indirectly determines the quality characteristics of the 
material. 
Fig. 3 (a) shows the room temperature compressive properties of 
NbMoZrTi LRHEA. The compressive strength of the alloy is about 1462 
MPa, the peak strength is 2158 MPa, the strain at break is 24.6 %, and 
the average hardness is 522 HV. NbMoZrTi LRHEA with a single BCC 
phase has an excellent specific yield strength, which may be attributed 
to the large difference in the atomic size between the Zr element and the 
Nb, Mo, and Ti elements, and thus the formation of the BCC solid so -
lution phase causes severe lattice distortion, which results in a signifi -
cant solid solution strengthening effect. The enhancement of hardness 
and strength of the alloy comes from the solid solution strengthening 
effect of BCC. The fracture morphology of NbMoZrTi LRHEA is shown in 
Fig. 3 (b, c). The fracture surfaces of this alloy are all relatively flat, and 
the fracture shows a river pattern, which is a typical cleavage fracture 
morphology and exhibits brittle fracture [11] . Specific strength is 
defined as the ratio of alloy strength to density, and the specific yield

4. Conclusions 
The microstructure and properties of lightweight high specific yield 
strength NbMoZrTi LRHEA were investigated. It was determined that 
the matrix phase has a single BCC structure. The density and hardness of 
the alloy were 7.4331 g/cm
3 
and 522 HV, respectively. the peak strength 
was high at 2158 MPa with 24.6 % strain at break. It exhibits excellent 
specific yield strength at room temperature compared to most other 
alloys reported so far.
