ABSTRACT 
Non-refractory elements, like Al, can enhance both room-temperature tensile strength and ductility of refractory 
high/medium alloys (RH/MEAs) due to the formation of ordered nanodomains changing the dislocation glide 
character. To elucidate their influence at higher temperatures, we reported here the tensile behaviour and 
microstructure response of a Al
7.5
(NbTiZr)
92.5 
(at.%) RMEA having a bcc (body-centred cubic) matrix with 
embedded B2 (ordered bcc) nanodomains at 298 – 1073 K, and compared the results obtained with those of a bcc 
NbTiZr alloy. Detailed analysis showed that profuse B2 nanodomains confined the dislocation motion in localised 
bands at T < 873 K, intensified the Portevin-Le Chatelier effect at 473 and 673 K, and enhanced the strength by ~ 
20 – 30 % at T ≤ 873 K, while losing the strengthening ability at 1073 K. These results manifest that ordered 
nanodomains originating from the non-refractory alloying can improve the medium-to-high temperature me -
chanical performance of RH/MEAs. 
1. Introduction 
Since their introduction in 2010, solid-solution body-centred cubic 
(bcc) refractory high/medium-entropy alloys (RH/MEAs) have become 
a hot-topic theme in the materials science community due to the ability 
to retain an excellent strength up to 1900 K that makes them promising 
for applications beyond the temperature limit of nickel-based superal -
loys [1 – 4] . Early studies attributed this property to the sluggish diffu -
sion, one of four “ core ” effects of high-entropy alloys [2] . But later works 
showed, by calculations firstly [5] and further experimentally [6] , that 
the strength of such RH/MEAs could be controlled by edge dislocations. 
Meantime, a large portion of less strong yet more ductile RH/MEAs, 
belonging to a Hf-Nb-Ta-Ti-V-Zr family, is deformed by a glide of 
a
2
< 111 > screw dislocations [7 – 14] . At low temperatures, the strength of 
these RH/MEAs can be enlarged by increasing the chemical complexity 
due to solid-solution hardening [ 11 , 13 , 15 ]; in this case, modulus rather 
than lattice distortions appear to be stronger obstacles for dislocation 
motion [15] . However, the number of constitutive elements does not 
affect the rate-controlling mechanisms of plastic deformation at cryo -
genic or room temperature, which are kink/kink-pair nucleation [11] or 
thermally activated glide of screw dislocations overcoming 
Peierls-Nabarro stress barriers [13] , respectively. In the 
medium-to-high-temperature regime, the strength of Hf-Nb-Ta-Ti-V-Zr 
RH/MEAs can be controlled by different mechanisms. For instance, in 
a NbTiZr RMEA, the strength is attributed to cross-kinks unpinning [14] 
that coincides with the theory of screw dislocation strengthening [16] . 
In turn, dynamic strain ageing, a manifestation of Portevin-Le Chatelier 
(PLC) effect, is claimed as the main strength contributor in a HfNbTaTiZr 
RHEA at 573 and 673 K [17] . 
One of the most interesting findings related to Hf-Nb-Ta-Ti-V-Zr RH/ 
MEAs is the positive effect of non-refractory elements on the room- 
temperature mechanical properties of these alloys, associated with the 
creation of additional obstacles for dislocations motion. Specifically, 
oxygen additions to a HfNbTiZr RMEA change the dislocation glide 
character from planar to wavy due to the formation of ordered oxygen 
complexes, which doubles tensile ductility and increases strength by ~ 
50 % [18] . The Al-induced B2 ordering also affects the spatial distri -
bution of dislocations [ 19 , 20 ]. In the deformed bcc RMEAs, dislocations 
tend to be arranged homogeneously, while localised dislocation bands 
are formed in Al-containing counterparts with bcc + B2 and B2-ordered

4. Discussion 
The results obtained show some interesting effects of the Al alloying 
on the NbTiZr RMEA. Namely, Al increased the strength at T ≤ 873 K, 
intensified the PLC effect at 473 K and initiated it at 673 K, changed the 
rate-controlling mechanism of plastic deformation at 673 K, and 
confined the dislocation motion in DBs at T < 873 K. The most plausible 
origin of these features is the B2 ordering that presents in a form of 
profuse ordered nanodomains. Such entities attract much attention, as 
they are considered to be a source for the unique properties of RH/MEAs 
[ 26 , 28 , 30 , 36 , 38-40 ]. Theory says that ordered nanodomains can stand 
as shearable obstacles that effectively hinder the initial dislocation 
motion [41 – 43] . In the case of the Al

passing a transition point during cooling [63 – 67] . In some alloys, these

Lastly, some comments on the intensification of the PLC effect with 
Al additions should also be given. In the NbTiZr alloy, the serrations 
were suggested to originate due to the contamination by interstitials 
[14] . Yet, in some Al-containing B2-ordered RMEAs, the PLC effect was 
initially speculated [69] and then experimentally [20] confirmed via 
TEM studies to be caused by the pinning-unpinning of dislocations by B2 
domains. A close look at TEM data obtained in the current work revealed 
that the interaction of dislocations with B2 nanodomains varied with the 
temperature. This is clearly seen at 473 K (at this temperature, the most 
pronounced manifestation of the PLC effect was observed) where the 
bent/pinned dislocation lines were decorated with B2 nanodomains 
(Fig. 5(g)). Thus, we infer that the PLC effect in the Al
7.5
(NbTiZr)
92.5 
alloy can stem from both the contamination by interstitials and the 
presence of B2 nanodomains. Such a combination appears to perplex the 
dislocation movement at 473 K more pronouncedly than one single 
mechanism does, thereby multiplying the serrations and increasing their 
amplitude compared to the NbTiZr alloy (Fig. S2(b), Supplementary 
material). Note that the PLC effect does not allow evaluating the V* at 
this temperature. In turn, the high value of the V* at 673 K that sug -
gested the switching of rate-controlling mechanisms from the over -
coming of Peierls-Nabarro stress barriers to cross-slip and intersections 
of dislocations was likely an anomaly that emerged due to the operation 
of the PLC effect, as no significant difference between the microstruc -
tures deformed in the range of 298 – 673 K was found. Still, the reasons 
for changing the interaction character of dislocations with B2 nano -
domains depending on the temperature are not completely understood 
yet. These are goals for future studies.
