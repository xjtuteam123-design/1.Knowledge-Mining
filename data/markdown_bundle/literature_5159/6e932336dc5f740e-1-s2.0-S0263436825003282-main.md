ABSTRACT
The poor compatibility between Mo-based and Nb-based alloys poses significant challenges for high-temperature 
brazing. This study demonstrates that introducing Pd into Ni filler metals substantially improves the tensile 
strength (135.0 MPa vs. 126.5 MPa for pure Ni) and toughness of Mo-5Re/Nb-1Zr joints. Through multi-scale 
characterization and density functional theory (DFT) calculations, we reveal that Pd promotes the formation 
of a ductile (Mo,Nb) solid solution ( β phase) by reducing the Nb diffusion barrier in Mo by 59.4 % (from 1.06 eV 
to 0.43 eV). The resulting heterogeneous microstructure, comprising alternating β , NiNb, and Nb(Pd,Ni) ₂ phases, 
enables crack deflection and back stress strengthening. These findings establish a microstructure design para -
digm for refractory alloy joints through filler metal engineering.
1. Introduction
In the aerospace and nuclear energy sectors, material selection for 
structural components is critical due to the extreme service conditions 
including ultra-high temperatures, intense radiation, and severe corro -
sive environments [ 1 ]. These demanding conditions necessitate mate -
rials with exceptional performance characteristics. Refractory alloys, 
characterized by their high melting points, superior strength, and 
resistance to corrosion, are considered optimal for such applications. 
Molybdenum ‑ rhenium (Mo
–
Re) alloys, in particular, are poised for 
broad utilization in components subjected to high-temperature service 
due to their remarkable high-temperature stability, outstanding corro -
sion resistance and anti-irradiation stability [ 2 – 4 ]. Niobium zirconium 
(Nb
–
Zr) alloys, while sharing the beneficial attributes of refractory 
alloys, exhibit a comparatively lower density [ 5 ]. This feature is ad -
vantageous when Nb
–
Zr alloys are joined with Mo
–
Re alloys, as it can 
reduce the overall structural weight and enhance component perfor -
mance significantly. The successful joining of Mo
–
Re to Nb
–
Zr alloys is 
essential for meeting the specific demands of extreme service conditions 
and plays a crucial role in enhancing the structural reliability within the 
aerospace and nuclear energy industries [ 6 ].
Research on the joining of Mo
–
Re alloys is relatively scarce in the 
existing literature. Various welding techniques have been employed for 
the joining of molybdenum (Mo) and its alloys. In the case of fusing 
welding, intergranular embrittlement of the weld metal, induced by 
welding thermal cycle, is a significant factor limiting the application of 
molybdenum alloys. The exceptionally low solubility of oxygen, nitro -
gen and carbon in molybdenum leads to pronounced segregation of 
interstitial atoms to grain boundaries and other defects [ 2 , 7 , 8 ]. For 
instance, Liu et al. [ 9 ] utilized the laser lap welding technique to join 
50Mo
–
50Re thin sheets. However, the resulting joint exhibited distinct 
gas porosity and cracking near the interface. The fracture surface 
revealed characteristic features of brittle intergranular failure, likely due 
to the presence of brittle oxygen-containing compounds along the grain 
boundaries, which compromised the integrity in the weld heat-affected 
zone. Additionally, excessively high welding temperature can lead to 
grain coarsening in the weld heat-affected zone, potentially detrimental 
to the joint mechanical performance.
Regarding vacuum brazing, a high vacuum can mitigate the adverse 
effects of oxygen segregation on the intergranular weakening.

Metallurgical compatibility between the filler metal and base mate -

Aurum (Au), Silver (Ag), Platinum (Pt) and Palladium (Pd) have a large 
solubility with many other constituents, which may promote the for -
mation of solid solutions in the brazing seam, thus enhancing the 
toughness of the joint [ 18 ]. Among these precious elements, Pd can

Au and Pt. Li et al. [ 19 ] investigated the brazing of C

600 
◦

C

The joint strength tested at 1000

has promise in modifying phase equilibria and maintaining high-

In the context of high-temperature applications, Ni-based filler 
metals are particularly suitable for the brazing of Mo
–
Re and Nb
–

This suitability is partly attributed to the eutectic reaction of Ni and 
Niobium (Nb) at 1175 
◦

–
Nb binary system [ 21 ], which 
facilitates effective metallurgical bonding between the filler metal and 
the substrates. If Pd is added, Ni
–
Pd binary phase diagram indicates Ni 
and Pd can form an immiscible system [ 22 ]. It means the formation of a 
(Ni,Pd) solid solution is theoretically feasible in the seam, further 
enhancing the potential for robust and reliable joints in severe high- 
temperature service environment. This study compared the joints 
brazed with pure Ni foil and Pd-modified Ni-based filler metal and dis -
cussed how Pd tunes interfacial reactions, diffusion kinetics, and 
deformation modes to achieve strength-toughness synergy of Mo-Re/ 
Nb-Zr joints.

2.2. Simulation methodology
First-principles DFT [ 23 ] was used to elucidate the formation 
mechanism of specific phases on the atomic scale in the present work. 
Vienna Ab-initio Simulation Package (VASP) [ 24 ] equipped with the 
projector augmented wave (PAW) [ 25 ] method was adopted. The plane- 
wave cutoff energy was set to a constant value of 400 eV throughout the 
calculation. For the exchange correlation functional, the generalized 
gradient approximation (GGA-PBE) [ 26 ] was used. A 54-atom of 3 × 3 
× 3 bcc-based supercell and a corresponding 5 × 5 × 5 Monkhorst-Pack 
k-point mesh were used in the calculation. The structure was fully 
relaxed with respect to lattice vectors and atomic positions by mini -
mizing the energy.

3.4. The microstructure modification mechanism
The addition of Pd significantly alters the joint microstructure, with β 
emerging as a major phase when Pd is incorporated into the original Ni. 
As analyzed above, NiNb is the main phase in the Mo-5Re/Ni/Nb-1Zr 
joint. The predominant difference in the joint microstructure after Pd 
modification, apart from Pd-containing phases, is the β phase. The NiNb 
phase remains consistently present in the brazed joint, independent of 
Pd addition. The Gibbs free energies of NiNb, β and MoNi at 1220 
◦
C are 
 102.09 kJ/mol, 91.32 kJ/mol and 80.04 kJ/mol, respectively 
( Fig. 12 ). NiNb has the lowest free energy among the three phases, 
indicating a strong tendency for Ni and Nb to combine [ 45 ]. The higher 
free energy of β suggests that it is less stable than NiNb. Therefore, β is 
not detected in the seam when only Ni foil is used, while the addition of 
Pd promotes its formation. β phase occupied 47 vol% in Pd-modified 
joints versus nearly 0 % in pure Ni joints, confirming the role of Pd in 
stabilizing metastable phases. MoNi, a common phase in Mo-Ni-Nb 
ternary system [ 46 ], is not generated in either condition due to its 
higher free energy, indicating that the reaction between Mo and Ni is 
more difficult to proceed.
β phase plays a definitive role in the mechanical properties of the 
joint. It is primarily concentrated on the Mo-side distribution within the 
brazing seam, indicating that its formation is associated with the diffu -
sion of Nb into Mo. To investigate the formation mechanism of β and 
identify the effect of Pd addition, the diffusion behavior of Nb atoms was 
comparatively studied by DFT using the following supercell configura -
tions: pure Mo, Mo with a single Ni atom incorporated, Mo with a single 
Pd atom incorporated and Mo with both Ni and Pd atoms incorporated 
( Fig. 13 (a-d)). The orange spheres represent Mo atoms, magenta spheres 
represent Nb atoms, black spheres represent vacancies, blue spheres 
represent Ni atoms and green spheres represent Pd atoms.

4. Conclusions
This study investigates the application of Pd-modified Ni-based 
fillers to optimize the Mo-5Re/Nb-1Zr joint system, which convention -
ally exhibits stress concentration issues due to coarse NiNb-dominated 
microstructures when brazed with standard Ni fillers. Joining experi -
ments were performed at 1220 
◦
C using both pure Ni and Ni
–
Pd filler 
metals. A systematic comparison was conducted between the baseline 
(Ni filler) and Pd-modified joints, encompassing microstructural char -
acterization, mechanical property evaluation, and fracture behavior 
analysis. Local stress-strain distributions were quantified to correlate 
with enhanced mechanical performance. Complementary DFT calcula -
tions were implemented to unravel the synergistic interplay between Pd 
and Ni in governing heterogeneous microstructure evolution. The 
principal findings are summarized as follows: 
(1) The Mo-5Re/Nb-1Zr joint brazed with pure Ni foil at 1220

exhibits a biphasic microstructure (Mo

(2) In the pure Ni-brazed joint, interfacial decohesion dominates as 
cracks propagate preferentially along the Mo-5Re/NiNb inter -
face. Conversely, the Pd-modified joint exhibits phase boundary- 
controlled cracking originating from NiNb grains. Notably, crack 
deflection and bridging phenomena occur at β phase interfaces, 
enhancing fracture toughness.
