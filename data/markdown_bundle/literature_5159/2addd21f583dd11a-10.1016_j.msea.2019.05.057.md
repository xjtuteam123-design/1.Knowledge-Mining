Abstract 
The microstructure-mechanical property relationship s of a non-equiatomic FeMnCoCr 
high entropy alloy (HEA), which shows a single face-centered cubic (fcc) structure in the 
undeformed state, have been systematically investig ated at room and cryogenic 
temperatures. Both strength and ductility increase significantly when reducing the 
probing temperature from 293 K to 77 K. During tens ile deformation at 293 K, 
dislocation slip and mechanical twinning prevail. A t 173 K deformation-driven athermal 
transformation from the fcc phase to the hexagonal close-packed (hcp) martensite is the 
dominant mechanism while mechanical twinning occurs in grains with high Schmid 
factors. At 77 K athermal martensitic transformatio n continues to prevail in addition to

1. Introduction 
During the past decade, growing attention has been placed on high entropy alloys 
(HEAs) owing to their promising mechanical properti es, such as high strength, good 
ductility, and superior fracture toughness particul arly at cryogenic temperatures [1-3]. 
These excellent properties originate not only from the solid solution strengthening 
associated with the severe lattice distortion [4-7] but also from the activation of 
deformation-induced twinning and/or martensitic tra nsformation [8-12]. The equiatomic

FeCoMnCrNi HEA with a stable face-centered cubic (f cc) structure, also referred to as 
Cantor alloy, shows an increase in both strength an d tensile ductility with decreasing 
temperature between 293 K and 77 K [2,3]. Its high work-hardening capability at 
cryogenic temperatures has been associated with mechanical nano-twinning [3], an effect 
which delays the onset of necking. Activation of me chanical twinning and the resulting 
improvement in strain hardening at cryogenic temper atures have also been reported for a 
non-equiatomic VCrMnFeCoNi HEA with fcc single-phas e microstructure [12]. When 
relaxing the initially rather strict concept of pha se stabilization through configurational 
entropy maximization [13,14], additional strain har dening capacity can be triggered by 
enabling deformation-induced transformation of the metastable austenitic phase, 
producing increased ductility. This approach has re cently been proven useful for 
overcoming the strength-ductility trade-off and rea lized in a new class of metastable 
single and dual-phase HEAs [9,11,15-18]. In this ma terial class a gradual 
deformation-dependent increase in the fraction of m echanical twins and 
austenite/martensite phase boundaries provides mult iple planar obstacles that reduce the 
mean free dislocation path. Further strain hardenin g comes from accommodation of 
deformation effects that often accompany athermal t ransformations to compensate for 
misfit. Both phenomena create significant internal stresses and an increased dislocation 
density [19] and hence efficiently contribute to th e overall strain hardening of the alloys. 
A specific design target in such metastable HEAs li es in achieving a compositional state 
where the onset of athermal transformation phenomen a does not occur at the beginning 
of macroscopic loading but rather in advanced deformation regimes. The rationale behind

The composition-dependent stacking fault energy (SF E), deformation temperature and

Numerous investigations on Fe-Mn based austenitic t ransformation and

transformation (SFE ≤20 mJ/m 2). The temperature-sensitive deformation behavior i s 
intrinsically related to the SFE which also varies with temperature. This effect is due to 
modest differences in the individual vibrational an d magnetic entropy contributions 
pertaining to the two phases while the configuratio nal entropy should be identical for the 
two states. Recent transmission electron microscopy (TEM) measurements and ab initio 
calculations for an equiatomic FeCoMnCrNi alloy hav e revealed that the SFE indeed

capacity and ductility. Modification of the composi tions of HEAs along these 
metastability design guidelines thus offers an effi cient path for adjusting the SFE and 
optimizing the mechanical behavior of Ni-free alloys at various temperatures.

2. Experimental 
 The nominal chemical composition of the studied H EA is Fe 40 Mn 40 Co 10 Cr 10 (atomic 
percent, at. %). The alloy was cast in a vacuum induction furnace in Ar atmosphere using 
pure metals (purity >99.9 wt. %) followed by hot fo rging at 1200 ℃. Subsequently, the 
alloy was hot-rolled at 900 ℃ to reach a thickness reduction of 50% and homogeni zed 
at 1200 ℃ for 2 h followed by water quenching. The as-homoge nized alloy has an 
average grain size of ~500 µm, and is hereafter referred to as coarse-grained ( CG) 
material. Some of the hot-rolled plates were furthe r cold-rolled to a thickness reduction 
of 60 ％ using several passes and subsequently annealed at 900 ℃ in a salt bath furnace 
for 3 min followed by air cooling. These plates hav e an average grain size of ~8 µm, 
hereafter referred to as fine-grained (FG) material. It should be mentioned that the size of 
a grain refers to that of the fcc matrix grain containing twins and hcp martensite laths. 
Flat dog bone-shaped tensile specimens with a cross -section area of 4×2 mm 2 (gauge 
part) were sectioned from the alloy plates using el ectrical discharge machining. The 
gauge length of the specimens was 35 mm. After remo ving the oxides and defects from 
the specimen surfaces by mechanical polishing, unia xial tensile tests along the rolling 
direction (RD) of the specimens were conducted usin g a MTS tensile stage equipped 
with a cold chamber controlled by liquid-nitrogen. Tensile loading was performed at an 
initial strain rate of 1×10 -3 s -1 at different temperatures, i.e., 293 K, 173 K and 7 7 K, 
respectively. For both, CG and FG materials, three specimens were tensile tested at each 
temperature.

the current fcc non-equiatomic FeMnCoCr HEA and the Mn content has been increased 
to 40 at.%. The SFE which quantifies the excess ene rgy due to the stacking fault formed

5. Conclusions 
We studied the microstructures, temperature-depende nt deformation mechanisms and 
mechanical properties of a non-equiatomic FeMnCoCr HEA with initial fcc crystal 
structure under tensile loading at 293 K, 173 K and 77 K, respectively. The conclusions 
are ： 
(1) Strength and ductility significantly increase w ith decreasing temperature. When 
deformed at 293 K, the alloy with an average grain size of ~8 µm exhibits the yield 
strength of 272 MPa, ultimate tensile strength of 4 81 MPa and maximum elongation of 
47%. At 77 K we observed the yield strength of 567 MPa, ultimate tensile strength of 
1003 MPa and maximum elongation of 65%. 
(2) At 293 K dislocation slip and mechanical twinni ng are the prevalent deformation 
mechanisms. At 173 K, austenite to ε-martensitic transformation occurs and mechanical 
twinning is also primarily activated in some grains . At 77 K deformation-driven athermal 
transformation continues to prevail in addition to dislocation slip and twinning. 
(3) As the deformation temperature drops, the decre ased stacking fault energy together 
with the enhanced flow stress of the fcc matrix is responsible for the formation of 
martensite under cryogenic deformation. The enhance d strain hardening capacity is then 
attributed to the variation of deformation mechanis ms from dislocation slip to twin 
formation and further to phase transformation. The dislocation mean free path is reduced 
by the profuse fine martensite bundles and the dens e twin boundaries, contributing to the 
alloy’s enhanced strength at cryogenic temperatures.

(4) The joint contribution of martensitic transform ation and mechanical twinning 
explains the excellent strength-ductility combinati on of the alloy when deformed at 
cryogenic conditions. Such a state where athermal d eformation mechanisms can be 
jointly activated upon loading is achieved by a met astability alloy design approach where 
the required low stacking fault energy is realized by the reduced Ni and the increased Mn 
contents relative to the equiatomic FeCoMnCrNi composition.
