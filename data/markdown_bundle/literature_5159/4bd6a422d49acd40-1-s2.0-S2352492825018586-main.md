1. Introduction
Near- α titanium alloys have drawn considerable attention in 
advanced aero-engine and rocket propulsion materials due to their low 
density, good creep strength, and high temperature resistance [1,2] . 
Additive manufacturing (AM) provides a paradigm shift from invest -
ment casting, enabling radically different processing through direct 
computer-aided design (CAD) system [3] . This approach confers a high 
accuracy and flexibility near-net-shape technique for fabricating large 
and complex Ti parts. However, conventional near- α Ti alloys used for 
600 ~ 650

[4] , Ti1100 [2] , and Ti65 [5,6] , exhibiting insufficient structure stability 
and mechanical properties at additive-manufactured state. All these 
near- α Ti alloys strategically combine both low-melting-point elements 
(notably Sn, T

resulting in the deterioration of mechanical properties. Furthermore, in 
Sn-containing near- α Ti alloys, structural stability is substantially 
compromised by thermal decomposition of β phases [8] . Extensive ef -
forts have been devoted to optimize the performances of the Ti alloys 
through proceeding parameter control and composition design. He et al. 
[9] prepared Ti65 alloys using different laser powers, and found that 
higher power input favors to reduce defect density. Li et al. [10] found 
that employing an interlayer rotation scanning strategy effectively 
reduced the aspect ratio of lamellar α phases, consequently diminishing 
the anisotropy of the strengths. Chi et al. [11] developed exceptional 
densification in Ti60 alloy, with a relative density of 99.84 %, via 
optimized laser powder bed fusion parameters. Zhu et al. [8] designed 
an additive-manufacturing-oriented near- α Ti-5.5Al-11.2Zr-4.8Ta 
-1.6W-0.5Si alloy featuring fully-equiaxed β grains through strategic 
substitution of Ta for Mo/Nb, exhibiting an ultimate tensile strength of 
703 MPa at 600

Ti-6.5A1 – 2.5Sn-9Zr-0.5Mo-1Nb-1W-0.3Si- x Sc ( x , 0 – 0.5 wt%) alloys, 
and found that the thermal stability and creep resistance improved by Sc 
addition. To improve the structural stability and mechanical properties 
of near- α Ti alloys, instead of massive changing the forming strategies, 
the critical lies in controlling the alloy composition. Therefore, it is

The enhanced strength of the AZT6113 alloy primarily stems from its 
highly stable β phase and bimodal basket-weave microstructure. The 
β -phase stability is achieved through Zr, Mo, Nb, Ta, W and Si co- 
alloying. Another beneficial factor to strengths for the alloy is the 
appearance of bimodal basket-weave microstructure. Fine lamellar α 
display a weakened texture, which enhances plasticity. Meanwhile, 
coarser α grains exhibit a strong basal texture that generally impedes 
dislocation slip, contributing to strength. The precipitation of α lamellae 
with diverse crystallographic orientations further increases tensile 
strength. Additionally, a high density of geometrically necessary dislo -
cations (GNDs) accumulates near primary β -grain boundaries ( Fig. 9 b), 
providing additional strengthening. Further strengthening arises from 
silicide formation along α / β interfaces, which hinders dislocation mo -
tion. Collectively, the strengthening mechanisms in laser-additive- 
manufactured AZT6113 alloys are divided into grain-boundary 
strengthening, solid solution strengthening, and dislocation 
strengthening.
