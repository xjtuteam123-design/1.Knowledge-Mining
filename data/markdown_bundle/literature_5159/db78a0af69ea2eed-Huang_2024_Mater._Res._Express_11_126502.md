Abstract
This article uses EB furnace to prepare TA10 alloy ingots with higher purity. Studying their hot
deformation behavior and microstructure evolution can provide theoretical support for practical
processes.The hot deformation behavior of TA10 alloy smelted via an electron beam furnace has been
studied using thermal deformation experiments, the experiments were conducted from 800°Ct o
1,050 °C, from 0.01 s
−1 to 1 s−1 and a reduction to 60%. According to Arrhenius and Zener-Hollomon
models, the constitutive equation can be constructed. TEM and EBSD aimed at exploring the
microstructures and microstructural evolution, respectively. When deformed at the temperatures
below the phase transition point, grain boundaries converted to large-angle gradually. The textural
strength reduced when the strain rate increased. Besides, the recrystallization rate was relatively small,
mainly dynamic recovery, and there was a tendency to turn into dynamic recrystallization. At the
temperature higher than the phase transition point, the textural strength increased, the recrystalliza-
tion rate was small, and the dynamic recovery was partially enhanced.
1. Introduction
TA10 alloy (Ti-0.3Mo-0.8Ni) has better resistance to crevice and pitting corrosion than industrial pure titanium
due to the lower cathode overvoltage caused by the addition of trace amounts of molybdenum and nickel[1– 5].
The excellent corrosion resistance observed in chloride solution, weak reductive acid, sulfuric acid and
hydrochloric acid makes it popular in heat exchangers, tubular reactors and distillation equipment[4, 6– 10].
Hot working is the key process used to obtain theﬁnal samples of TA10 alloy[11, 12], and the
microstructures and properties of the resulting materials are signiﬁcantly inﬂuenced by the synthesis parameters
[13– 16]. The changes in the microstructures of the metal in various thermal deformation circumstances[17– 19]
have been obtained using experimental simulation methods, and theﬂow stress is used to construct some
practical theoretical formulas[16, 20]. Zhang et al [16] discussed the hot deformation behavior of Ti-6Al-4V
with three distinct initial microstructures, and found thatﬁner grains were formed via continuous dynamic
recrystallization rather than transforming theβ starting microstructure and bimodal starting microstructure
when the original microstructure wasα′ martensitic, and the average grain size was∼400 nm. Gao et al [21]
discussed the ﬂow behavior of TA15 alloy with various starting microstructures. The results showed that the
ﬂow stress presents clear softening phenomena at the non-uniform microstructure state. Besides, the
phenomena were more distinct than lamellar starting microstructure due to the presence of equiaxedα.L i et al
[19] identiﬁed the ﬂow behavior of Ti-5Al-2Sn-2Zr-4Mo-4Cr with equiaxed and lamellar original
microstructures, which indicated that the samples with a lamellar starting microstructure exhibit higherﬂow

2. Materials and methods
2.1. Experimental material
The material employed in the work was TA10 alloy, its chemical and measured composition are shown in table1.
The content of Mo and Ni obtained using plasma emission spectrometry(ICP-OES) were 0.28% and 0.85%,
meeting the standard. The ingots were obtained by smelting in an electron beam cooling bed furnace(EB)
provided by Yunnan Titanium Industry Co., Ltd, China.
2.2. Experimental method
2.2.1. Identiﬁcation of the phase transition temperature
The β transformation point was measured using differential thermal analysis and metallographic method in this
study.
Differential thermal analysis method: powder drilled from alloy ingot was used to test the phase transition
point using a STA449F3 differential thermogravimetric analyzer(NETZSCH, Germany) over a temperature
range of 30– 1100 °C. The heating rate was 10°C min−1, protected by an argon gas atmosphere.
Metallographic method: The samples(10 mm × 10 mm × 10 mm) were heated to certain temperatures at an
interval of 10 °C, kept for 20 min in a vacuum furnace(YFX16/120– 6), quenched immediately with water after
being removing and then cut out to observe the metallographic microstructures.

3.2. Determination of theβ transformation point of TA10 alloy
The temperature thatα-Ti completely transforms intoβ-Ti in the thermal equilibrium state is theβ
transformation point of the titanium alloy, which is vital in hot working and heat treatment process[22, 23]. The
phase transition temperature in different batches may differ by 20°C– 40 °C or more due to the composition
ﬂuctuations formed during the smelting process. Therefore, theβ transformation point of TA10 alloy was
measured to determine the temperature range of the subsequent hot compression experiments. Differential
thermal analysis and a metallographic method were used and the standards issued by the Ministry of
Aeronautics and Astronautics utilized.

3.3. Single-pass isothermal compression behavior of TA10
3.3.1. Effect of the deformation temperature on theﬂow stress of TA10 alloy
Figure 9 shows the true stress– strain curves obtained for cylindrical TA10 alloy specimens at different
conditions; the ﬂow stress of the samples goes up to a maximum or peak value with an increase in strain, and
then most of the samples decreases. The dislocation density continuously increases in the process, further
movement is hindered by the entanglement of dislocations. Theﬂow stress sharply increases with strain at this
stage [25]. With further deformation, the dislocation density attains a certain value, softening phenomena, such
as dynamic recovery or lamellar kinking, occur inside the samples[18, 26, 27]. These phenomena enhance with
an increase in the amount of strain, so the increase in theﬂow stress slows down or its value decreases.
At a range of 800 to 850°C, the increase leads to the decline of theﬂow stress signiﬁcantly, which may due to
the dislocation movement and an increase in the number ofβ phases observed at higher temperatures[20]. The
stress increases at 900°C, as shown inﬁgure 9(c). The heat generated during isothermal compression makes the

3.4. Isothermal hot deformation microstructure of TA10 alloy
3.4.1. EBSD analysis of the isothermal single-pass microstructure of TA10 alloy
EBSD characterization was carried out at 850, 900, 950 and 1050°C with 0.1 s
−1 to study the dynamic
recrystallization, dynamic recovery, grain boundary and texture of the isothermal single-pass microstructure.
Figure 14 suggests the microstructures become coarse when the temperature rises, which may because the rise of
the driving force for grain growth.
Figure 15 indicates the grain size observed at different temperatures and the same strain rate. There are many
ﬁne equiaxed grains with sizes in the range of 2– 4 μm in the alloy at 850°C and 0.1 s
−1, and the maximum grain
size was ∼15 μm (ﬁgure 15(a)). The grain size increases at 900°C and 0.1 s−1, and the maximum size was
∼20 μm (ﬁgure 15(b)). From ﬁgure 15(c), many ﬁne grains in the range of 2– 4 μm were observed in the alloy at
950 °C and 0.1 s−1, and the maximum was∼24 μm. The maximum grain size was∼26 μm at 1,050 °C and
0.1 s−1, as demonstrated inﬁgure 15(d). The grain size of TA10 alloy increases with temperatures, may attribute
to the matrix microstructures in the sample before the deformation change from theα to β phases, the
deformation ability of theβ phase being stronger, and the driving force for grain growth being larger at higher
temperatures.
3.4.2. Grain boundary and recrystallization analysis of TA10 alloy after isothermal single-pass thermal deformation
As given inﬁgure 16, the green segments stands for the low-angle grain boundaries and black segments represent
the high-angle. Figure17 displays the recrystallization diagrams of TA10 alloy observed at diverse deformation
temperatures and the same strain rate. Blue refers to the recrystallized grains, yellow refers to the substructure,
and red refers to the deformed grains. Figures18(a) and (b) give the columnar statistics of the large and small
grain boundaries, and recrystallization observed at distinct deformation temperatures and the same strain rate,
respectively. The climbing and slip motions make dislocations entangle to form cellular structures or subcrystals
in dynamic recovery and many low-angle exist. However, there is no or a few low angle grain boundaries
observed during dynamic recrystallization. Therefore, dynamic recrystallization and dynamic recovery can be
demonstrated by the proportion of low angle grain boundaries observed. Almost all of the grains contain green,
which suggests the impact of the dynamic recovery is strong, as exhibited inﬁgure 16.
At a strain rate of 0.1 s
−1, the proportion of the low-angle grain boundaries increases from 50.4 to 67.2%
(50.4, 65.1, 67.1 and 67.2%) as the deformation temperature increases from 850 to 1,050°C (850, 900, 950 and
1,050 °C). However, the high-angle decreases from 49.6 to 32.8%(49.6, 34.9, 32.9 and 32.8%).
Some grains do not contain low-angle grain boundaries even the temperature rises, which suggests the
enhancement in the dynamic recovery and reduction in the dynamic recrystallization during the process. EBSD
was carried out to verify the proportion of dynamic recrystallization because it can not only be determined by the
large and small angle grain boundaries. At a strain rate of 0.1 s
−1, the consequences prove that the proportion of
the recrystallized grains was 1.3%, the substructure was 8.2% and the deformed matrix was 90.5% at 850°C; the
proportion was 0.8, 7.9 and 91.3% at 900°C, 0.4, 8.9 and 90.7% at 950°C and 0.4, 24.4 and 75.2% at 1,050°C.
Therefore, the dynamic recovery is dominant in the hot deformation process of TA10 alloy. The effect of
dynamic recovery enhances and the dynamic recrystallization reduces with the increase of the deformation
temperature.
