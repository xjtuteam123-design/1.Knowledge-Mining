ABSTRACT 
This study investigated the high-temperature tensile properties and deformation mechanisms of a solution 
treated Ni – Fe-based alloy ranging from 600 to 750 
◦
C. The solution treated GH4070P (HT700P) alloy exhibits 
superior yield strength of 490 MPa and ultimate strength of 593 MPa at 700 
◦
C. The major carbides in the alloy 
are the blocky MC and discrete-distributed M
23
C
6
. The MC and M
23
C
6 
carbides at the grain boundaries (GBs) can 
impede crack propagation, yet concurrently intensify the stress concentration. The transgranular and inter -
granular fractures are observed. Above 700 
◦
C, micro-cracks emerge around M
23
C
6 
carbides at GBs and then are 
interlinked to form lengthy secondary micro-cracks, resulting in the intergranular fracture. The precipitation of γ ′ 
precipitates during tension at 700 and 750 
◦
C confers the alloy to a higher strength. 
1. Introduction 
Thermal power generation plays a critical role in energy consump -
tion. Nevertheless, coal-fired power generation will unavoidably bring 
about greater environmental strain. While producing top-notch power, 
it will still discharge a considerable amount of carbon dioxide and other 
greenhouse gases. The increasing demand for energy conservation and 
reducing CO
2 
emissions are driving the development of a new genera -
tion of advanced ultra-supercritical (A-USC) boiler technology. The A- 
USC technology is currently aiming to increase the operating tempera -
ture to 700 – 760 
◦
C and the main steam pressure to 37.5 MPa, which is 
expected to result in a process efficiency of over 50% to current gener -
ation plants and a reduction of 40 – 50% CO
2 
emissions as compared to 
current generation boilers [ 1 , 2 ]. Precipitation-hardened Ni – Fe-based 
superalloys are promising candidate materials for power plant super -
heater and reheater tube applications [ 3 – 6 ], due to their low cost, high 
creep strength, good corrosion resistance, and high microstructural 
stability at intermediate temperatures [ 7 – 9 ]. 
The excellent mechanical properties of Ni – Fe-based alloys are 
attributed to the strengthening effect of γ ′ precipitates and carbides 
[ 10 – 13 ]. Carbides are important strengtheners that offer several ad -
vantages, such as excellent thermal stability, no increase in relative 
density and good availability, resulting in carbon playing a key role in 
providing grain boundary strengthening in Ni-based superalloys 
[ 14 – 16 ]. The inclusion of carbon and carbide-forming alloying elements 
typically results in the formation of two types of carbides, MC carbides 
enriched with Ti, Nb, or Zr and the Cr-rich M
23
C
6 
carbides, which pre -
cipitate preferentially at the grain boundaries (GBs). He et al. [ 17 ] 
revealed that the precipitation of M
23
C
6 
carbides effectively obstructs 
dislocation motion and impedes grain boundary sliding, consequently 
improving the fracture strength of the alloy. Meanwhile, they also 
pointed out that the interface of MC carbides with matrix at 800 
◦

main site for crack initiation. Song et al. [ 18 ] reported that the trans -
formation from MC to M
23
C

of Haynes 282 superalloy. Our previous research [ 19 ] indicated that the 
precipitation of M

C

property of Ni – 30Fe-based superalloy. The size of M
23

during creep, and the morphology of M

4. Discussion 
This study investigates the tensile properties and deformation 
mechanisms of the Ni – Fe-based GH4070P alloy, which is designed for 
700 
◦
C A-USC main steam and reheat steam pipelines. By comparing 
with the Ni – Fe-based alloys at present [ 36 , 37 ], about 40% Fe and only a 
few percentages of Co and Mo (wt.%) have been added into the 
GH4070P alloy. Such a total amount of Co and Mo could increase the 
volume fraction of γ ′ phases and effectively slow down the creep rate of 
the alloy, and therefore maintain sufficient high-temperature strength 
[ 38 , 39 ]. In addition, GH4070P alloy shows good microstructural sta -
bility, and no harmful phases such as η and σ were observed during 
long-term thermal exposure. 
The nucleation and propagation of micro-voids and cracks at GBs 
were closely associated with stress concentration and grain boundary 
sliding [ 40 ]. It has been reported [ 41 – 43 ] that carbides could absorb 
solid-solution strengthening elements from the γ matrix and precipitate 
at GBs, which led to a decrease in the bonding strength at the matrix/ -
carbide interface. Moreover, the difference in the plastic deformation 
ability between the carbides and γ matrix causes stress concentration. 
Consequently, the MC and M
23
C
6 
carbides at GBs provide the potential 
sites for the nucleation of micro-voids and cracks during deformation.

It is generally accepted that the occurrence of slip deformation relies 
on the motion of dislocations [ 44 , 45 ]. In γ ′ precipitation-strengthened 
Ni – Fe-based superalloys the microstructures of alloys in different 
heat-treated states are varied, resulting in a more diverse and complex 
interaction between dislocations and precipitates. The strength of 
Ni – Fe-based alloys is mainly determined by the precipitation of γ ′ pre -
cipitates and carbides, and the grain size. The microstructures of sam -
ples tensile tested at 700

As shown in BF TEM images in Fig. 10 a and b, the dislocations with 
Orowan looping were observed. Nonetheless, at this level of magnifi -
cation, the γ ′ precipitates are not distinctly discernible. Hence, the high- 
resolution TEM (HRTEM) images, Fig. 10 c and d, show the precipitation 
of nano-structured γ ′ precipitates within γ grains, as confirmed by the 
fast Fourier transformations (FFTs) shown in Fig. 10 e and f. In Ni – Fe- 
based alloys, the γ ′ precipitate is the main strengthening phase, 
providing a precipitation hardening effect that hinders dislocation slip 
via the pinning effect during deformation. Therefore, the GH4070P alloy 
exhibits good high-temperature property despite the existence of 
numerous secondary cracks at GBs. 
5. Conclusions 
The tensile properties and microstructural evolution of a solution 
treated Ni – Fe-based GH4070P superalloy at high-temperature were 
studied. Based on the SEM, EBSD and TEM investigations, the following 
main conclusions can be drawn: 
(1) During the high-temperature tension, variations in the YS with 
temperature show a similar trend as those of UTS before 700 
◦
C. 
Above this temperature, the YS increases progressively with 
temperature and reaches to 490 MPa at 750 
◦
C. Whereas, with 
regard to the UTS, a peak value of 593 MPa appears at 700 
◦
C, and 
then the strength decreases with temperature. 
(2) The MC and M
23
C
6 
carbides at GBs can impede the connection 
and extension of micro-cracks, but also augment the stress con -
centration at GBs, providing the potential sites for the inception 
of micro-voids and cracks during deformation. At 700 
◦
C, the 
micro-cracks are emerged around the M
23
C
6 
carbide at GBs, and 
then interlinked to form lengthy secondary micro-cracks. 
(3) The mixed mode fracture of granular and intergranular fractures 
occurs in the experimental alloy during tensile deformation. The 
intergranular fracture becomes the predominant when the tem -
perature exceeds 700 
◦
C. 
(4) The precipitation of γ ′ precipitates within the γ matrix in the 
tensile samples at 700 
◦
C and 750 
◦
C enables the alloy to maintain 
a higher strength despite the presence of a large number of sec -
ondary cracks at GBs.
