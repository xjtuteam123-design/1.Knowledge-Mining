ABSTRACT 
To demonstrate the concept of lightweight refractory high-entropy alloys (LRHEAs) with remarkable strength 
and ductility at room- and high-temperatures, we designed and fabricated novel Ti
1.6
ZrNbAl
x 
( x = 0.3, 0.4, 0.5 
and 0.65) LRHEAs with a relatively low density ( < ~5.9 g/cm
3
). The presence of B2 coherent precipitates and 
their interactions with moving dislocations is proposed to be responsible for the measured high tensile yield 
strength ( > 800 MPa) and satisfactory tensile ductility ( > 10%) at room-temperature. The compressive peak stress 
exceeding 500 MPa at 600 
◦
C supports the potential application of these LRHEAs as high-temperature structural 
materials. Our work provides a framework that can be used to guide the design of high-performance LRHEAs for 
high-temperature applications. 
1. Introduction 
The demands of aerospace and aircraft industries for designing new 
lightweight metallic alloys with high strength and sufficient ductility at 
both room- and high-temperatures are growing significantly [ 1 , 2 ]. In 
the past decade, research on refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) has 
shown remarkable high-temperature strength, even superior to Ni-based 
superalloys [ 3 – 7 ]. Unfortunately, most RHEAs suffer from disappointing 
room-temperature ductility and high densities, significantly limiting 
further engineering applications. For example, the initially proposed 
NbMoTaW and VNbMoTaW RHEAs show extremely high compressive 
peak stresses with higher than 1.2 GPa at room temperature but very 
limited plasticity ( ＜ 2%) [ 8 , 9 ]. Moreover, due to the high density of the 
constituent elements, the density of these two RHEAs exceeds 10 g/cm
3
. 
So far, many light elements, such as Ti, Cr, V, and Al, have been adopted 
to design RHEAs with relatively low density, namely lightweight RHEAs 
(LRHEAs). Several newly developed LRHEAs systems, e.g., 
Cr – Nb – Ti – V – Zr, Al – Nb – Ti – V, Al – Cr – Mo – Ti – W, exhibit low densities 
less than 7 g/cm
3 
along with high compressive yield strength [ 2 , 10 – 18 ]. 
However, due to the intrinsic brittleness or the brittle second phase 
contained in the matrix, these LRHEAs show limited room-temperature 
tensile ductility. Thus, developing high-strength LRHEAs with sufficient 
tensile ductility is a key to their engineering application. 
The Hf-Nb-Ta-Ti-Zr RHEA system has been demonstrated to possess 
enough tensile ductility at room temperature but still with relatively 
high density and low strength [ 19 , 20 ]. Herein, it will be an effective 
method to meet the requirements of LRHEAs by adjusting this alloy 
system, including its composition and microstructure. Coherent pre -
cipitation strengthening, as one type of precipitation strengthening, is a 
feasible strengthening method to significantly improve the strength and 
ductility simultaneously, as verified in Ni-based superalloys, 
face-centered cubic HEAs, and a few Al-containing RHEAs [ 21 – 24 ]. 
These studies suggest that proper chemical composition optimization 
may offer the opportunity for the combinations of high strength and 
ductility of LRHEAs. 
In this study, we first focused on the Hf-Nb-Ta-Ti-Zr RHEAs system 
by removing costly Hf and Ta elements and further increasing the con -
tent of Ti. The second step was to add an appropriate amount of Al to the 
chosen RHEA to induce the formation of B2 coherent precipitates. 
Finally, we successfully designed novel LRHEAs with the compositions

3. Results and discussion 
Microstructures of the as-cast LRHEAs were first characterized by 
EBSD, as shown in Fig. 1 . The inverse pole figure (IPF) maps and PF 
maps suggest that their grain-orientation distributions are random, with 
no apparent micro-texture. To quantitatively describe the grain struc -
ture of the as-cast LRHEAs, the average grain aspect ratio (GAR) was 
calculated. The low value of GAR, less than 1.5, indicates that these 
alloys are composed of typical equiaxed grains. Additionally, the back- 
scattered SEM images at low- and high-magnifications of the as-cast 
LRHEAs and the distributed mappings of the Al0.5 LRHEA ( Fig. S1 in 
supplementary information) further confirmed the homogeneity of the 
composition, revealing the absence of bulk secondary phases and 
dendrite structures. The mean grain sizes were measured from the grain 
orientation maps, and the statistical values of the Al0.3, Al0.4, Al0.5 and 
Al0.65 LHEAs are ~409 μ m, ~464 μ m, ~434 μ m and ~301 μ m, 
respectively. 
As displayed in Fig. 2 (a), dark-field TEM (DF-TEM) image of the 
Al0.3 LRHEA with corresponding SAED pattern along [110] zone axis 
reveals the presence of B2 and BCC phases. Profuse near-spherical B2 
nanoparticles with bright contrast can be clearly distinguished in the 
BCC matrix, with an average diameter of ~6.8 nm and a volume fraction 
of ~5.9% (see inset in Fig, 2(a)). Fig. 2 (b) shows a high-resolution TEM 
(HRTEM) image containing BCC matrix and B2 nanoparticles of the 
Al0.3 RHEA, indicating a fully coherent relationship between these two 
phases. Fast Fourier transform (FFT) of the yellow square region pre -
sents a BCC-structured diffraction pattern, whereas that of the white 
square region reveals a B2-type superlattice pattern. Fig. 2 (c) shows the 
inverse FFT image transformed by the B2 superlattice reflection spots in 
the FFT image in Fig. 2 (b), providing the existence and distribution of 
this ordered structure, indeed revealing the assertion of full coherence 
between the precipitate and matrix. As Al content increases to 10 at.%, 
the nanosized morphology of the Al0.4 LRHEA is almost identical to that 
of Al0.3 LRHEA, as shown in Fig. 2 (d); however, the average diameter 
and the volume fraction of B2 nanoparticles were increased to ~9.3 nm 
and ~16.6 vol%, respectively, suggesting an increase in the degree of B2 
ordering (see inset in Fig, 2(d)). Further increasing Al addition signifi -
cantly changed the microstructure of the Al0.5 LRHEA, which exhibited 
a much more complex structure, as shown in DF-TEM image ( Fig. 2 (e)). 
SAED pattern along the [100] zone axis confirmed the existence of 
typical B2 superlattice reflections. Combined with bright-field TEM 
image ( Fig. S2 in supplementary information) of the Al0.5 LRHEA, the 
average diameter and the volume fraction of B2 phase were estimated to

Interestingly, the HAADF-STEM image and corresponding elemental 
maps of Al0.3, Al0.4 and Al0.5 LRHEAs show that there are no abrupt 
changes in the composition of the two phases, as shown in Fig. 2 (g) and 
Fig. S2 . This could be attributed to nanoscale fluctuations during the 
phase transformation. Specifically, the initial rearrangement of atoms 
within the cubic lattice occurs, followed by rapid development into 
nanosized domains, all without any noticeable long-range diffusion of 
elements [ 25 ]. Fig. 2 (f) shows BF-TEM image of the Al0.65 LRHEA along 
the [110] zone axis. Clearly, the presence of numerous anti-phase 
boundaries (APBs) along with strong superlattice reflections, indicate 
that the Al0.65 LRHEA consists of a single B2-structure phase. A similar 
morphology can also be found in some RHEAs, i.e., Ta/MoTiCrAl and 
AlTiVCr RHEAs [ 26 , 27 ]. 
Based on the microstructural observations, for the Al0.3 and Al0.4 
LRHEAs with relatively low Al content, the phase transition during 
cooling after solidification can be described as Liquid → Liquid + A2 → 
A2 → A2 + B2 , in which the B2 phase precipitate from the A2 matrix. 
Hence, the final microstructure is the BCC matrix and the B2 second 
phase, suggesting the precipitation mechanism is non-classical nucle -
ation and growth. In the case of the Al0.5 LRHEA, the addition of Al 
leads to the rapid growth of B2 domains and the formation of a structure 
similar to APBs. Considering that the volume fraction of B2 phase has 
exceeded 50%, we can assume that the Al0.5 LRHEA is composed of the 
B2 matrix and the BCC second phase. In addition, this precipitation 
behavior was also observed in some super-saturation solid solution al -
loys and high Al content Ni – Cr – Al superalloys [ 28 ]. For the Al0.65 
LRHEA with high Al content, the discrete B2 domains grew continuously 
and then formed APBs during cooling. To further understand the for -
mation mechanism of this BCC/B2 dual-phase structure, the elemental 
composition of the BCC phase as a function of temperature during 
equilibrium conditions is plotted, as shown in Fig. S3 . During solidifi -
cation, the BCC phase is enriched in Ti and Nb and depleted in Al and Zr. 
Since Ti and Nb with similar atomic radius prefer to form BCC solid 
solution, while Al and Zr with large negative enthalpy of mixing ( 44 
kJ/mol) tend to form ZrAl-type B2 secondary phase, as verified in some 
Al-containing RHEAs. Jensen et al. determined the B2 phase enrich in Al 
and Zr elements in the Al

4. Conclusions 
In summary, we successfully designed and fabricated a series of 
Ti
1.6
ZrNbAl
x 
LRHEAs with BCC/B2 coherent nanostructure and superior 
mechanical properties in the as-cast state. The addition of Al changes the 
phase formation of the current LRHEAs from a BCC + B2 dual phase 
structure to a single B2 phase structure, as well as the morphology of B2 
precipitates from near-spherical (when x = 0.3, 0.4) to a complex 
structure (when x = 0.5). Due to the interaction between coherent B2 
nano-precipitates and dislocation motion, the strength and ductility of 
the investigated LRHEAs effectively improved, thereby supporting the 
suggestion that these may be candidate materials for applications in the 
aerospace field. Therefore, our work provides a novel strategy for the 
development of LRHEAs with excellent room- and high temperature 
mechanical properties.
