1. Introduction 
The rapid development of advanced machinery in aerospace, jet 
engines, gas turbines, and nuclear applications industries require a 
corresponding development of materials with high mechanical and 
excellent tribological behaviour [1,2] . Multi-principal components al -
loys (MPCAs), usually called high-entropy alloys (HEAs) have recently 
gained a massive attention worldwide due to their excellent strength, 
high thermal stability, anti-corrosive, and anti-oxidative properties 
under harsh operating conditions [3 – 5] . HEAs have also attracted rich 
attention due to their superior tribological performance in a wide tem -
perature range [6 – 9] and load range [10,11] . The exceptional me -
chanical properties together with the unlimited development space of 
HEAs have made them potential materials for future applications where 
conventional steels, Co-based alloys, and Ni-based alloys reach their 
limits [12] . 
Eutectic high entropy alloys (EHEAs) belong to a group of HEAs that 
has attracted a widespread attention, since the first introduction of 
AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 
which possesses outstanding strength-ductility synergis -
tic mechanical properties [13 – 17] . The AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 
EHEA has a 
balanced lamellar structure between a hard BCC(B2) phase and a ductile 
FCC(L1
2
) phase. Gao et al. [18] has investigated the microstructural 
origins of high strength and good ductility of this EHEA. They found that 
the high density of Cr-enriched coherent nano-precipitates in B2 phase 
contributed to the high tensile strength while the high density of parallel 
dislocation arrays and massive stacking faults in FCC phase contributed 
to the large tensile elongation. The relationship between microstructural 
features and chemical compositions of a HEA or EHEA is very compli -
cated [19,20] . For example, formations of FCC and BCC phases in the 
Al
x
CoCrFeNi HEAs are strongly affected by the Al content, and only FCC 
phase was obtained when Al content was very low or only BCC phase 
was obtained when Al content was very high [20] . This special micro -
structure results in an achievement of both high strength and ductility 
from the EHEA hardly found in conventional steels and HEAs [16,17] . 
The outstanding strain/stress tensile properties of the AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 
EHEA can be maintained up to 700 
o
C, signaling a potential for 
multi-phases HEAs as future metallic materials. Since then, multiple 
studies have focused on the development of EHEAs for various appli -
cations. The first direction focused on processing and microstructural 
modification for mechanical properties enhancement of AlCoCrFeNi
2.1

engineering an ultrafine-grained duplex microstructure of AlCoCr -
FeNi
2.1

the thermomechanical processing behaviour of AlCoCrFeNi

temperature range of 25 – 500

2. Experimental materials and method 
2.1. Material preparation 
In this study, commercial pellets of Al, Co, Cr, Fe, and Ni with a 
purity of 99.5 wt% were used as raw materials to fabricate two EHEAs 
with nominal compositions of Al
0.8
CrFeNi
2.2 
and AlCoCrFeNi
2.1
, 
respectively, by conventional arc-melting method. The mixed pellets 
were placed into a clean vacuum chamber of the arc-melting furnace 
with an in-place chill casting onto a water-cooled copper hearth. The 
chamber was sealed and then vacuumed to ~0.01 MPa. The chamber 
was then flushed with high-purity argon air and repetitively evacuated 
to eliminate the retained oxygen within the chamber. During arc- 
melting, two titanium getters were first melted to further absorb the 
remained oxygen to avoid the oxidation of pellets. Then, the mixed 
pellets were melted. In order to achieve a chemical composition ho -
mogeneity within the as-cast EHEAs ingots, melting was repeated for at 
least five times. These EHEA ingots were cut into small pieces or discs for 
microstructural characterizations, mechanical properties measurements 
and tribological tests using wire cutting technique. 
2.2. Mechanical properties measurement 
In order to compare the mechanical properties between two EHEAs, 
both Vickers hardness and tensile tests were conducted at room tem -
perature. The hardness was measured using a Matsuzawa Via-F auto -
matic Vickers hardness tester with a load of 10 N and a dwell time of 10 
s. At least 20 separate hardness measurements were conducted for each 
EHEA sample. Tensile tests were performed at a strain rate of 1 × 10
-3 
1/ 
s on an Instron 8801 universal testing system, with a sample gauge 
length of 20 mm and sample thickness of 1 mm. The standard of ASTM 
E8 for uniaxial tensile test of metallic materials was followed and tensile 
tests were performed under a strain rate control mode [39] . Three 
samples were tested for each EHEA in order to confirm the reliability of 
their tensile properties. 
Moreover, nanoindentation tests were also performed using a Hysi -
tron TI950 Triboindenter system to evaluate the influence of tempera -
ture on the nano-hardness of both EHEAs. A Berkovich diamond 
indenter with a tip radius of 150 nm was used for the nanoindentation 
tests, which were conducted at RT, 150, 300, 450 and 600
o
C. The 
heating rate of the chamber was set to 20
o
C/min, and the system was 
stabilized for 10 min at the setting temperature prior to nanoindentation 
tests. At least 25 separate indentations with an array of 5 × 5 were done 
at each temperature for a given sample under an argon gas protection 
atmosphere. The nanoindentation loading-holding-unloading strategy 
applied in the experiments [40 – 42] were used in this work, namely with 
the maximum force of 1000 μ N and loading/unloading rate of 200 μ N/s. 
The indentation load-displacement relationship was analyzed according 
to the Oliver-Pharr approach with the details available in the previous 
study [40] .

4. Conclusions 
A comprehensive study on tribological behaviour of a Co-free cost- 
effective Al
0.8
CrFeNi
2.2 
EHEA system, including detailed analysis of 
wear, friction, and oxidation behaviours in a wide range of temperature 
from RT to 900
o
C has been investigated. Simultaneous comparisons 
with AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 
EHEA have been thoroughly conducted in terms of 
characterizing both the mechanical and tribological performances. The 
conclusions are as follows: 
(1) The high temperature nanohardness between 150 and 600
o
C for 
as-cast Al
0.8
CrFeNi
2.2 
are found to be higher than those for 
AlCoCrFeNi
2.1
, both in terms of the FCC and the BCC(B2) phases, 
indicating an overall greater strength and thermal-softening 
resistance of the Co-free EHEA at elevated temperature. The 
significant increase in Ni content in both FCC and BCC(B2) phases 
of the Co-free EHEA and the formation of Cr-rich phase nano- 
precipitates inside the BCC(B2) phase might be addressed for 
this enhancement in high temperature hardnesses of 
Al
0.8
CrFeNi
2.2
. 
(2) The Co-free Al
0.8
CrFeNi
2.2 
exhibits a lower COF as compared to 
AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 
at elevated temperature above 600
o
C. The 
different in COF is significantly high at 900
o
C, where an average 
COF of ~0.29 is recorded for Al
0.8
CrFeNi
2.2 
but ~0.44 for 
AlCoCrFeNi
2.1
, equivalent to ~34.1% reduction. 
(3) Al
0.8
CrFeNi
2.2 
exhibits a lower wear resistance than AlCoCr -
FeNi
2.1 
at RT, but a higher wear resistance when temperature 
exceeded 300
o
C. Particularly, the wear rate of Al
0.8
CrFeNi
2.2 
at 
600 and 900
o
C is only about 35.4% and 76.2%, respectively, of 
that of AlCoCrFeNi
2.1
. 
(4) The tribolayers of Al
0.8
CrFeNi
2.2 
at 900
o
C comprise an oxide 
layer on the top and a sub-layer tribo-bed containing ultrafine- 
grain particles. These two layers promote a significant enhance -
ment in wear protection of the tribolayers, as compared to 
AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 
where a mixing pattern of these two layers is 
observed. Furthermore, the thicknesses of the tribolayers in the 
case of Al
0.8
CrFeNi
2.2 
are found to be wider at elevated temper -
ature between 600 and 900
