ABSTRACT 
In this study, two-step heat treatment is conducted to adjust the microstructure and mechanical properties of Nb- 
Si alloy with addition of Y. The time and temperature of second-step heat treatment are studied to optimize the 
process. According to the microstructural observation and the tests of mechanical properties, it is confirmed that 
the two-step heat treatment can significantly improve the fracture toughness (about 30.3%) of Nb-Si alloy 
without sacrifice of tensile strength which also increases slightly. The improvement can be attribute to the 
adjustment of microstructures by the two-step heat treatment: Compared to the silicide after single-step heat 
treatment, the volume of silicide after two-step heat treatment reduces by 40.6%; The silicides with sharp 
corners tend to be spheroidized by the two-step heat treatment. The proper micro-cracks introduced by the two- 
step heat treatment are also the reason for improvement of fracture toughness which can promote the crack 
deflection and crack bridge to toughen the Nb-Si alloy. The optimal fracture toughness about 32.6 MPa·m
1/2 can 
be obtained after the first-step heat treatment at 1450 °C for 12 h and the second-step heat treatment at 1100 °C 
for 10 h. 
1. Introduction 
Niobium silicide (Nb-Si) based alloy as a kind of in-situ composites 
has became a candidate for the next generation of superalloys because 
of its excellent properties such as high melting point (> 1750 °C), low 
density (6.6–7.2 g/cm3), good fracture toughness at room temperature 
and high strength at high temperatures [1–3]. Nb-Si based alloys consist 
of niobium-based solid solutions (Nb ss) and silicides (Nb 5Si3, Nb 3Si, 
etc.), of which the Nbss provide room temperature fracture toughness as 
the matrix and the brittle silicides provide strength at high temperature 
[4–7]. And the proportion of two phases will affect the overall prop -
erties of Nb-Si based alloys. 
Several investigations have been conducted to improve the prop -
erties of Nb-Si alloys. Above all, Nb-Si binary alloys have been designed 
to Nb, Si, Ti, Cr, Al, Hf multi-alloys and thus exhibited relatively good 
comprehensive properties [
1,4,8]. There are also further studies fo -
cused on addition of micro-alloying elements (W, Ta, Mo, Ho, Dy, Y, B, 
C, V, Ge, Sn, Fe, In), which can optimize some performances [
2,9–22]. 
However, there are some disadvantages in alloying: the increased cost 
of materials and the difficulty of improving multiple performances si -
multaneously. For example, Song Zhang et al. proves that the element 
of B or Cr promotes the formation of hypereutectic microstructures. 
And Yunlong Li et al. also reveals that the addition of Zr results in the 
appearance of primary γ-Nb 5Si3 and significant hypereutectic micro -
structures. And it is confirmed that the high volume fraction of silicides 
adversely affects the fracture toughness of the alloy [
23]. Therefore, 
some researches focus on the effect of heat treatment on properties of 
Nb-Si alloys. Nb
3Si phases not only reduce the room temperature 
fracture toughness of Nb-Si alloys, but also become unstable below the 
eutectoid temperature as metastable phases [24]. Annealing heat 
treatment can be used to eliminate the metastable phases, but it re
-
quires a long heat treatment time to effectively reduce the metastable 
phases [
25]. It also can be noticed that current heat treatment methods 
are simplistic and there are few studies on the two-step heat treatment 
of Nb-Si alloys. Jia's research shows that the two-step heat treatment 
can refine the Cr
2Nb to nanometer level, thereby improve the tensile 
strength and high temperature oxidation resistance of the alloy [26]. 
However, the systematic optimization of two-step heat treatment pro
-
cess still requires further studies. In addition, the influence of the two- 
step heat treatment on improvement of both tensile strength and room 
temperature fracture toughness also needs further studies.

2. Experiment 
2.1. Material preparation 
Directionally solidified (DS) Nb-15Si-24Ti-4Cr-2Al-2Hf-(0.3, 0.6)Y 
(at.%) based alloys were subjected to two-step heat treatment at dif
-
ferent temperatures and times. The alloys were prepared by vacuum 
non-consumable arc melting, the button-shaped alloys were remelted 
five times to ensure uniformity, and master alloy rods with 14 mm in 
diameter were prepared by electrical discharge machining (EDM). The 
rods were assembled in a Y2O3 crucible which was nominally 18 mm in 
outside diameter, 15 mm in inside diameter and 220 mm in length. 
Liquid metal cooling directional solidification was carried out in a 
high vacuum directional solidification furnace. When the furnace 
chamber was evacuated to a vacuum level of 1 × 10
−3 Pa, the high 
purity (99.99 vol%) argon was backfilled into the chamber to minimize 
the contamination and volatilization. The directional solidification was 
carried out at 1980 °C and a rate of 500 μm/s. And the compositions of 
impurities in the directionally solidified alloys were shown in Table 1. 
The first heat treatment was carried out in a high vacuum 
(< 1.0 × 10
−3 Pa) heat treatment furnace at 1450 °C for 12 h with the 
heating rate of 12.5 °C/min. Then the samples were cooled down to 
room temperature in the furnace. The second-step heat treatment was 
conducted in a tube furnace at 1000 °C, 1100 °C, or 1200 °C respectively 
with the heating rate of 12.5 °C/min. And the second-step heat treat
-
ment was for 10 h and 20 h respectively, which was shown in Table 2
. 
Before the second-step heat treatment, all the samples were sealed in 
silica glass tubes which were evacuated to a vacuum level of 
1 × 10 −3 Pa and then filled with high purity (99.99 vol%) argon to 
0.3 atm. After the heat treatment, the samples were cooled down to 
room temperature in the furnace. 
2.2. Tests of mechanical properties 
The fracture toughness K
Q was measured in a three-point bending 
test. The length, width and thickness of the one-sided notched specimen 
are 30, 6 and 3 mm, respectively. The length is parallel to the solidi -
fication direction. A notch having a / w = 0.5 (a: slit length, w: sample 
width) was prepared by EDM. The bending tests were carried out by a 
SANS hydraulic servo test machine with a crosshead speed of 0.1 mm / 
min and a test span of 24 mm. The fracture toughness of the three 
samples was averaged. 
Tensile tests were carried out by an Instron 8801 hydraulic servo 
tester with extensometers. The stretching process is at a constant axial 
speed of 0.05 mm/min, and the loading direction is parallel to the so
-
lidification direction. The tensile strength of the three samples was 
averaged. The samples are plate-shaped with a gauge length of 18 mm 
and a cross section of 3 × 2 mm
2 for measuring tensile strength. 
2.3. Microstructural characterization 
The phases on the transverse section were identified by X-ray dif -
fraction (XRD; Rigaku D/MAX-2500 X-ray diffraction, Cu K α). The 
microstructures and crack propagation paths were observed by an 
electron probe microanalyzer (EPMA; JEOL JXA-8100) equipped with 
an energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) in the back-scatter electron 
(BSE) mode. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM; JEM-2100) was 
used to observe morphology of separated silicides. The fracture surface 
was observed by a scanning electron microscope (SEM; JEOL JSM- 
6010) in secondary electron (SEI) mode. The fracture surface roughness 
and three-dimensional images were measured using a laser confocal 
microscope (LSCM; OLS5000). The average area and volume of the 
Nb5Si3 phase was measured and calculated by Image-Pro Plus 6. 
3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Microstructures of the alloys before and after heat treatment 
Fig. 1 shows the X-ray diffraction patterns of the different heat- 
treated alloys. Both single-step and two-step heat-treated alloys are 
mainly composed of Nbss and α-Nb 5Si3. As for single-step heat treat -
ment of HT1, there are three phases Nbss, α-Nb5Si3, γ-Nb5Si3 whether it 
is an alloy with 0.3Y or 0.6Y addition; However, as for two-step heat 
treatment of HT2, the γ phase of the 0.6Y alloy remains after the two- 
step heat treatment, while the γ phase of the 0.3Y alloy disappears after 
the two-step heat treatment (HT2-1, HT2-2). Therefore, it can be con -
cluded that the two-step heat treatment can eliminate the γ-Nb 5Si3 in 
Nb-Si alloys and the addition of 0.6 at.%Y element inhibits this process. 
Fig. 2 shows the microstructures of directionally solidified Nb-Si 
alloys without heat treatment. The bright, gray and dark phase is Nbss, 
α-Nb5Si3 and γ-Nb5Si3 respectively. The microstructures show the hy -
poeutectic morphology indicated by the large portion of Nbss phase. 
The Nbss phase forms preferentially from the liquid metal, expressed as 
L → primary Nbss + L1 [
28]. And there are gray shadows distributed 
on the edge of Nbss, indicating the elements segregation. The dark γ- 
Nb5Si3 also appears in junction of Nbss and α-Nb 5Si3. In the long -
itudinal section, the Nbss and α-Nb 5Si3 phases grow in the axial di -
rection. The Nbss phase exhibits an undeveloped dendrite, and the α- 
Nb5Si3 phase grows in the residual space between the Nbss phases in an 
irregular shape. 
With the analysis of EDS as shown in Table 3, it is obviously that the 
γ-Nb5Si3 phase is rich in Hf, and the content of Ti and Si is also rela -
tively high. It is indicated that the segregation of Hf and Ti promotes 
the formation of γ-Nb 5Si3 phase. And some studies attribute it to the 
segregation of low-melting-point Hf and Ti elements in the boundary of 
the solidification interface [29,30]. There are distinct shaded areas 
around the Nb
5Si3 phase as indicated by the arrows in Fig. 2. It is the 
result of non-equilibrium directional solidification, which means it is 
difficult for the solute to completely diffuse in such a short solidification 
period. And if the solute is further concentrated in the vicinity of the 
Nb
5Si3 phase, it tends to form shaded areas. 
Fig. 3 shows the transversal and longitudinal microstructures of the 
heat-treated Nb-Si alloys, and the EDS results are exhibited in Table 4.

3.2. Tensile strength of the 0.6Y alloy after heat treatment 
Fig. 6 shows the tensile curves of 0.6Y alloy after the two-step heat 
treatment. And corresponding average ultimate tensile strengths are 
calculated as shown in Table 5
. Compared with the single-step heat 
treatment, two-step heat treatment can slightly improve the ultimate 
tensile strength. And HT2-2 sample exhibits the best ultimate tensile 
strength (1102 MPa) which increases 3.3% compared to the samples 
after single-step heat treatment. However, when the temperature of 
second-step heat treatment continues to increase, the ultimate tensile 
strength decreases to 1058 MPa (1200 °C) and has no significant dif -
ference with the single-step heat treatment. In addition, there is no 
significant improvement when the time of second-step heat treatment 
increases from 10 h (HT2-1) to 20 h (HT2-4). 
Fig. 7 shows the fractography of 0.6Y samples after two-step heat 
treatment. The macro fractures are relatively flat indicating typical 
brittle fractures, and there are some undulations on the microscopic 
surface. The partial fracture of 0.6Y alloy exhibits the ridge shape, 
which is the result of plastic deformation. Most of Nb 5Si3 phases are 
cleavage fractures, and some have ridge shapes. The temperature of the 
second-step heat treatment also affects the tensile fracture of the alloy. 
As the heat treatment temperature increases, the undulation, interface 
separation and micro-cracking become more significant as shown in 
Fig. 7. 
3.3. Fracture toughness of the alloy after heat treatment

different heat treatment. For both 0.3Y and 0.6Y alloys, it is obviously

more significant for the 0.6Y alloy. In addition, the fracture toughness 
values of HT2-1 and HT2-4 are basically the same indicating the frac
-
ture toughness is insensitive to the time of second-step heat treatment.

heat-treated samples, the back scattered electron images of crack ex

micro-cracks in the silicide phases at the crack extension tip of 0.6Y 
alloys compared to 0.3Y alloys. And there are also more micro-cracks in 
the alloys after two-step heat treatment compared to the alloys after 
single-step heat treatment. The fracture toughness can be improved by 
the micro-crack toughening mechanism [34]. The crack tip propagates 
through these micro-cracks, which can effectively absorb the fracture 
energy and reduce the driving force of crack propagation. And the 
micro-cracks promote the crack propagation to multiple paths, effec -

mary, after the two-step heat treatment, there are more micro-cracks in 
the silicides, which can absorb the fracture energy and cause crack 
deflection when the crack propagates. At the same time, crack bridging 
also plays a role in toughening. Therefore, the fracture toughness of the 
alloys is improved. 
Fig. 11 is a schematic diagram of crack propagation and deflection.

Nb-Si alloys which will develop to extended micro-cracks under 
loading. When the crack tip extends to the vicinity of γ-Nb 5Si3, cracks

original micro-cracks. Therefore, there is a certain deflection reducing 
the driving force of crack propagation as shown in Fig. 11(c). When the

to the fact that Nbss can provide enough toughness to prevent crack. 
Instead of simple crack propagation, the crack bridge also leads to crack 
deflection as shown in Fig. 11(c). Finally, the sample breaks after

4. Conclusion 
In this study, different heat treatment processes are carried out to 
adjust the microstructure and mechanical properties of Nb-Si alloy with 
the addition of Y. Without the sacrifice of tensile strength, the fracture 
toughness can be improved significantly. The main conclusions can be 
drawn as follows: 
(1) The two-step heat treatment can refine the microstructure effec -
tively which is the result of silicide separation. Compared to the 
silicide after single-step heat treatment, the volume of silicide re -
duces by 40.6% as for HT2-2. And the silicides with sharp corners
