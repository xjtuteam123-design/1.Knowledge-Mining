ABSTRACT
Rhenium (Re), as a characteristic solid-solution strengthening element, is commonly added to Ni-based single 
crystal superalloys (Ni-SXs) to stabilize the microstructure at high temperatures. However, Re tends to deplete 
from the γ matrix and form Re-rich topologically close-packed (TCP) phases under thermal conditions, 
compromising the high-temperature mechanical performance of Ni-SXs. Understanding Re migration during TCP 
nucleation is thus critical for enhancing Ni-SXs ’ high-temperature stability. In this study, we introduced the 
concept of "atomic diffusion-pair" control in a multi-element system and unveiled, at the atomic scale, for the first 
time, that chromium (Cr) in the γ matrix facilitated Re diffusion, serving as an elemental precursor for TCP- σ 
phase nucleation. Using in-situ scanning transmission electron microscopy (STEM) heating experiments and 
atomic-resolution characterization, we observed that Cr atoms initially segregated into Cr-rich face-centered 
cubic (FCC) phases at elevated temperature via defect assistance. These segregations promoted Re co-segregation 
in the same location, forming a Re-rich FCC phase that subsequently transformed into a Re-rich hexagonal close- 
packed (HCP) structure, identified as the structural precursor to the σ phase. Geometric phase analysis and 
electron energy loss spectroscopy revealed that this Cr-Re pair diffusion is primarily driven by both local stress 
fields and inter-element charge transfer, exemplifying strain-electronic co-regulation in metallic diffusion sys -
tems. These findings offer new insights into TCP nucleation mechanisms governed by element interactions and 
provide valuable guidance for tailoring alloy design and phase stability optimization in Ni-SXs.
1. Introduction
Ni-based single crystal superalloys (Ni-SXs), owing to their 
outstanding high-temperature mechanical properties, have become key 
materials for manufacturing gas turbine blades in aero engines and 
power plants [ 1 – 5 ]. Due to unique solid solution strengthening effects, 
rhenium (Re) has been added to advanced generations of Ni-SXs to 
achieve superior high-temperature performance. Among tens of alloying 
elements, the Re content is considered one of the characteristics of 
different-generation Ni-SXs [ 6 , 7 ]. The so-called “ Re effects ” are mainly 
rooted in its low diffusion rate, substantial atomic radius, and 
cluster-like distribution. These factors are beneficial for impeding 
dislocation motion and enhancing solid solution strengthening at 
elevated temperatures [ 7 – 15 ].
However, a critical issue arises when some Re-rich topologically 
close-packed (TCP) phases form in Ni-SXs under prolonged high- 
temperature conditions [ 16 – 18 ]. These phases deplete large amounts 
of Re from the γ matrix (e.g., ~28 at. % in TCP- σ vs. ~5 at. % in γ ), 
forming brittle intermetallic compounds and causing a considerable 
reduction in solid solution strengthening in the matrix [ 19 – 21 ]. It in -
dicates that Re, as the slowest diffusing element in nickel, has its 
diffusion resistance compromised under specific conditions [ 22 – 28 ].

Understanding why and how Re behaves is not only a breakthrough in 
elucidating the nucleation mechanism of TCP phases but also crucial for 
guiding the advanced alloy design of next-generation Ni-SXs.
The primary issue is determining when and why Re begins to diffuse 
significantly. However, this is not a straightforward matter. While the 
precipitation of TCP phases from the matrix typically occurs at a specific 
peak temperature [ 18 , 29 – 31 ], elemental diffusion or structural evolu -
tion of the matrix (such as γ coarsening, changes in lattice misfit and 
dislocation configuration, etc.) may initiate at earlier stages or across a 
broader temperature range. So it is essential to investigate the matrix 
alterations before TCP phase nucleation for a more comprehensive un -
derstanding [ 32 – 42 ]. In addition, although Re exhibits the lowest 
diffusion rate due to its substantial atomic radius and unique D -electron 
bonding, its diffusion behavior was highly sensitive to factors such as 
local lattice stress fields, crystal defects, and local chemical environment 
[ 43 – 51 ]. In fact, in multicomponent systems like Ni-SXs, element in -
teractions at elevated temperatures are particularly strong, which may 
result in a highly complex synergy between local stress fields, chemical 
inhomogeneities, and other factors.
Studies have demonstrated that internal stresses can alter vacancy 
concentrations and diffusion barriers in the alloy [ 48 , 52 , 53 ]. Addi -
tionally, it is worth noting that other crystal defects, such as disloca -
tions, stacking faults, and interfaces, can significantly influence local 
stress fields and diffusion pathways, leading to elemental enrichment in 
specific regions [ 15 , 54 – 58 ]. Such chemical inhomogeneities may also 
induce charge transfer, altering the electronic structures of constituent 
elements and atomic bonding energies [ 46 , 47 ]. As a result, diffusion 
barriers are modified, affecting both diffusion rates and pathways. In 
Ni-SXs, the interactions between Re and other alloying elements (e.g., 
Cr, W, Ru) may further modulate Re migration and aggregation through 
synergistic or antagonistic effects [ 59 – 63 ]. However, a clear under -
standing of these mechanisms remains elusive.
In this study, advanced atomic-scale structural and chemical char -
acterizations, accompanied by in-situ scanning transmission electron 
microscopy (STEM) heating experiments, were employed to investigate 
the diffusion behavior of Re in Ni-SXs. The results indicated that Re 
diffusion in Ni-SXs at elevated temperature was facilitated by Cr atoms 
through the Cr-Re diffusion-pair, presenting a phase transition process 
from Cr-rich face-centered cubic (FCC) phases to Re-rich FCC phase, Re- 
rich hexagonal close-packed (HCP) phase, and eventually the σ phase. 
Geometric phase analysis (GPA) and electron energy loss spectroscopy 
(EELS) revealed that Re migration was regulated by strain-electronic 
synergy. These findings offer new insights for understanding the diffu -
sion behavior of Re in the structural evolution of Ni-SXs at elevated 
temperature, specifically how inter-element interactions facilitate the 
nucleation of TCP phase, which is valuable for guiding the design and 
processing of future Ni-SXs.

3.5. Strain-electronic co-regulated Cr-Re diffusion-pair
Now, it was interesting to know why and how the inter-element 
interaction between Cr and Re is aroused. We first performed geo -
metric phase analysis (GPA) and electron energy loss spectroscopy 
(EELS) to investigate the evolution of local strain fields and electronic 
structure characteristics during elemental migration. Fig. 9 A illustrated 
strain distribution features at interfaces between three intermediate 
phases and the γ matrix. It showed that the Cr-rich FCC phase exhibited 
significant tensile stress along the y-axis in the intermediate phase re -
gion, and the Re-rich FCC phase demonstrated tensile stress concentra -
tion along the x-axis on the matrix side, while after transformation to 
HCP structure, localized tensile stress fields became weak, which 
emerged within a narrow range near the interface.
This strain evolution pattern indicated that the intermediates, where 
Cr (atomic radius: 1.27 Å) aggregated, were under tensile stress due to a 
remarkable lattice mismatch. These strain fields attracted larger atoms. 
The subsequent Re-incorporation (Re atomic radius: 1.97 Å) effectively 
relaxed internal tensile stress via atomic size compensation, inducing 
stress field reversal that transferred principal tensile stress to the matrix 
side [ 73 ]. Ultimately, HCP phase formation triggered interfacial stress 
redistribution, significantly reducing stress levels in both phases. This 
modification of lattice distortion fields constituted a critical driving 
force for Re migration.
In addition, it was found that the charge transfer during this process 
may also play a role. The EELS results in Fig. 9 B delineated the electronic 
state evolution of Cr and Re in γ matrix, intermediate phase, and σ phase, 
respectively. Compared with the γ matrix, Cr in Cr-rich FCC in -
termediates exhibited positive shifts of + 0.3 eV (L ₂ -edge) and + 0.4 eV 
(L ₃ -edge), whereas it presented reverse shifts in the σ phase. Conversely, 
Re displayed a more pronounced negative shift ( 0.4 eV at M
5
-edge) in 
Re-rich HCP intermediates, transitioning to a positive shift in the σ 
phase.
These charge transfer behaviors suggested that Cr atoms exhibited an 
electron gain tendency, while Re atoms showed an electron loss ten -
dency. This complementary charge distribution promoted the associa -
tion of Cr-Re atomic pairs through Coulombic interactions, providing an 
electronic structural explanation for the electrochemical attraction 
mechanism between Cr and Re.

4. Conclusion
In summary, this study provided critical insights into the roles of Cr 
and Re during the TCP- σ phase nucleation in Ni-SXs at elevated tem -
peratures through a combination of in situ STEM heating experiments, 
multiscale TEM characterization, and 4D-STEM techniques. Our results 
revealed a previously unrecognized Cr-Re diffusion-pair mechanism, 
where Cr segregation, not merely Re accumulation, drove TCP nucle -
ation, thereby challenging the classical "Re toxicity" paradigm. It 
demonstrated that the Cr-Re pair diffusion was governed by the syner -
gistic interplay between local stress fields (from geometric mismatch) 
and charge transfer effects (electronic interactions), representing a rare 
case of strain-electronic co-regulation in metallic diffusion systems. 
Furthermore, it was acknowledged that the actual driving forces in such 
complex alloys were not singular but involved multiple competing fac -
tors. The dominant factor governing the microstructural evolution
