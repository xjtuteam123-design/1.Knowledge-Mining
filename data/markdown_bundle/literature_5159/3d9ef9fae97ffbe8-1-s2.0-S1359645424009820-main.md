ABSTRACT
Eutectic high-entropy alloys (EHEAs) have favorable mechanical properties at room temperature but limited 
strength at elevated temperatures. Here we report a novel approach to remarkably enhance both room- and 
elevated-temperature mechanical properties of EHEAs via nanoscale co-precipitation and heterostructure engi -
neering. We found that the addition of Nb to an Al – Co – Cr – Fe – Ni EHEA not only triggers the precipitation of L1
2 
nanoprecipitates in the FCC phase but also induces the co-precipitation of α ’ and Laves nanoprecipitates in the B2 
phase, resulting in the formation of a hierarchical heterostructure. The precipitation strengthening from the L1
2
, 
α ’ , and Laves nanoprecipitates and the hetero-deformation induced strengthening elevate the yield strength to 
1076 MPa at room temperature and 905 MPa at 700 
◦
C while maintaining a high ductility of 10% – 50% in this 
temperature range. First-principles calculations were used to evaluate the intrinsic energetics of the multicom -
ponent FCC and B2 phases, and the results reveal that both phases can accommodate plastic deformation via a 
dislocation slip mechanism. The dislocation interactions in the two phases and the hetero-deformation induced 
strengthening contribute to the large strain hardening of the alloy at room temperature. At 700 
◦
C, the increased 
atomic mobility facilitates the movement of dislocations in the deformable B2 and FCC phases, and the defor -
mation also induces grain boundary sliding and dynamic recrystallization, which together substantially enhance 
the alloy ductility at elevated temperatures. The strategy of nanoscale co-precipitation and heterostructure en -
gineering can be applied to other materials for achieving excellent mechanical properties.
1. Introduction
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) containing four or more principal ele -
ments display unique structures and properties due to complex in -
teractions among constituent elements [ 1 , 2 ]. This innovative alloy 
design concept has opened new avenues for material exploration and 
property optimization. It has been reported that many HEAs exhibit 
exceptional mechanical and chemical properties, such as high strength, 
hardness, and oxidization/corrosion resistance. These materials are also 
found to have pronounced thermal stability at elevated temperatures 
due to the high entropy effects and sluggish diffusion, making them 
promising candidates for elevated-temperature structural applications 
[ 3 , 4 ]. Among them, eutectic HEAs (EHEAs) are very attractive and have 
received considerable attention because of their in-situ formed com -
posite structures and near-equilibrium nature. Lu et al. [ 5 ] designed an 
AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 
EHEA consisting of a hard B2 phase and a soft 
face-centered cubic (FCC) phase, in which the former offers high 
strength and the latter contributes to good plasticity. The mixed struc -
ture results in a good combination of high strength and ductility at 
ambient temperature. However, the FCC/B2 EHEAs generally exhibit a 
notable softening at elevated temperatures. For example, the yield 
strength of the AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 
alloy is only ~100 MPa at 700

3.2. Grain structure
Fig. 2 a and b exhibit the EBSD phase maps of the Nb-free and Nb- 
containing alloys, respectively. Both alloys consist of a duplex struc -
ture comprising BCC-type (depicted in red) and FCC-type (depicted in 
green) phases. The volume fraction of the BCC- and FCC-type phases 
were measured respectively to be 59% and 41% for the Nb-free alloy and 
53% and 47% for the Nb-containing alloy. EBSD IPF images ( Fig. 2 c and

by an oriented nucleation or oriented growth mechanism [ 27 , 28 ]. For 
the Nb-free alloy, the average grain size of the BCC-type and FCC-type 
phases are 6.5 ± 0.7 and 4.5 ± 0.5 µ m, respectively. For the 
Nb-containing alloy, the average grain size of the BCC-type and 
FCC-type phases are 5.8 ± 0.5 and 3.7 ± 0.3 µ m, respectively, implying 
that Nb does not induce a significant change in grain size. Supplemen -
tary Fig. S2 exhibits the FCC/BCC phase boundaries in the Nb-free and

3.3.1. Precipitates in the B2 phase
HAADF-STEM was performed to investigate the detailed micro -

Representative micrographs together with corresponding elemental 
mapping are displayed in Fig. 3 . In both alloys, high densities of nano -

alloy, the STEM-EDS maps show the co-segregation of Fe and Cr, sug -

precipitates are formed in the B2 phase of the Nb-containing alloy. One

± 0.3 nm, and the other is Nb-enriched precipitates with an average 
radius of approximately 3.6 ± 0.2 nm ( Table 2 ).

5. Conclusions
In this study, we present the development of a new FCC/B2 EHEA 
with excellent strength and ductility at both room and elevated tem -
peratures via nanoscale co-precipitation and heterostructure engineer -
ing. The precipitation behavior, mechanical properties, and deformation 
mechanisms at room and elevated temperatures were thoroughly 
investigated. Our main findings are: 
1. The addition of Nb effectively enhances the yield strength of the 
FCC/B2 EHEA at both room and elevated temperatures. The Nb- 
containing alloy exhibits a yield strength of 1076 MPa and an elon -
gation of 11% at room temperature. It maintains a yield strength of 
905 MPa with an elongation-to-failure of approximately 50% at 700 
◦
C, demonstrating high resistance to intermediate-temperature 
embrittlement.
2. The Nb addition not only triggers the formation of high number 
densities of L1
2 
precipitates in the FCC phase but also induces the co- 
precipitation of Laves and α ’ -(Fe,Cr) precipitates in the B2 phase, 
forming a hierarchical heterostructure. Additionally, the Nb addition 
makes the B2 and FCC phases more dispersed and increases the 
number density of heterophase boundaries.
3. Quantitative evaluation of the strengthening mechanism indicates 
that the precipitation strengthening from the L1
2 
and Laves pre -
cipitates and the enhanced HDI strengthening are major contributors 
to the yield strength increment of the Nb-containing alloy relative to 
the Nb-free alloy.
4. First-principles calculations on the intrinsic energetics of the multi -
component FCC and B2 phases reveal that both phases can accom -
modate plastic deformation via a dislocation slip mechanism. The 
dislocation interactions in the two phases and the HDI strengthening 
contribute to the large strain hardening of the alloy at room tem -
perature. At 700 
◦
C, the increased atomic mobility facilitates the 
movement of dislocations in the deformable B2 and FCC phases, and 
the deformation also induces GB sliding and dynamic recrystalliza -
tion, which together substantially enhance the ductility of the alloy 
at elevated temperatures.
