The high-temperature sections of radiative coils in pyrolysis systems used for ethylene production are fabricated
from carbide-hardened heat-resistant austenitic alloys of base composition 45Kh25N35S2. The long-term strength of the
materials used or recommended is a basic mechanical property used for functional validation of the pipe system in such coils
at operating temperatures of 800–1100°C. Tests of the long-term strength of the new, heat-resistant 45Kh26N33S2B2 alloy
developed by the Prometei Central Research Institute for Structural Materials at various temperatures indicate that the new
alloy has virtually the same long-term ultimate strength (for a test duration of 10000 h) as the best foreign heat-resistant alloys
in this class. 
Studies have shown that long-term static load at high temperature leads to significant structural changes and the for-
mation of new phases (with various morphologies) that affect the heat resistance.
In this paper, electron microscopy was used to determine the nature of the structural changes in 45Kh26N33S2B2
alloy, and the nature and chemical composition of proeutetoid constituents, based on tests of samples of this material for long-
term strength at high temperature.
In the initial, as-cast state, high-carbon heat-resistant refractory steels and alloys are thermally unstable; when the
end products are used under high temperature and static load, this thermal instability leads to the formation of proeutectoid
constituents and affects the service characteristics of the materials used for the fabrication of reaction coils in ethylene pr o-
duction units.
Previous studies [1] showed that at typical operating temperatures for the radiant coils (800–1000°C), the newly
developed alloy has higher long-term strength than standard alloys of this class, meaning that radiant coils fabricated from
the new alloy would have a longer service life. Comprehensive studies indicate that the improved heat resistance of the new
alloy is due to its chemical composition, which supports the formation of thick shells of complex phases with high thermal
stability [2] along the boundaries.
The purpose of this paper is to determine the mechanism by which doping (chemical composition) of the alloy
affects the behavior of the alloy under service conditions, and explain the different behavior of the alloys during long-term
strength tests at high temperature, i.e., at [typical] pipe operating temperatures.

tics located along the boundaries of the dendritic crystallites was studied in detail using transmission electron microscopy. In

Conclusion. The research performed in this paper suggests that the improved operational specifications for the new
45Kh26N33S2B2 alloy relative to those for the standard 45Kh25N20S2 steel result from the following:
• higher thermal stability of intergranular carbides and chromium carbonitrides, achieved through enrichment (up to
1–4%) with refractory elements – Mo and W;
• formation of thermally stable NbC and Cr 23C6 intergranular carbides along with plate-shaped austenite + Cr 7C3
eutectics in the initial state;
• formation of new Ni–Nb–Si, Cr–Fe–Ni–Nb and Cr–Ni–Nb–Si composites in grain-boundary phases during long-
term thermal holds;
• the disperse internal structure of grain-boundary features consisting of complex conglomerates of micronal and
submicronal carbonitride and intermetallic phases.
All these factors cause the reinforcing framework of grain-boundary precipitates to have higher stability, and there-
fore cause the resulting alloy to have improved operating specifications.
