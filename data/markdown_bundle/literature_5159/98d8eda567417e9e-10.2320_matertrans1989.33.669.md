Masumoto Institute for Materials Research, Tohoku University, Sendai 980, Japan An A188Ni9Ce2Fel alloy with ultrahigh tensile fracture strength exceeding 950 MPa in the temperature range from room temperature to 573 K was found to be obtained by rapid solidification in the structural state where the nanoscale fcc-AI particles without internal defects disperse homogeneously in the amorphous matrix. The ultimate tensile strength is as high as 1560 MPa at room temperature and 970 MPa at 573 K. The high strength values were obtained in the mixed structure where the volume fraction of AI phase was about 25%. It is notable that the strength values at room tempera- ture and 573 K are about 3 and 15 times, respectively, as high as those for conventional age-hardening type aluminum alloys. The extremely high strengths are presumably due to the dispersion strengthening resulting from the homogeneous dispersion of the nanoscale AI particles without internal defects. Furthermore, the excellent elevated-temperature strength is also presumed to originate from the high resistance of the defect-free AI particles against the softening as well as the particle growth. Introduction Recently, light metal-based alloys in Al- and Mg-based systems have attracted increasing attention because of increasing social needs for lightweight high-strength materials. However, the elevated-temperature strength for the Al- and Mg-based alloys has been believed to be rather poor because of low melting and boiling tempera- tures of their base alloys. It has been reported(l) that high mechanical strength retention of 300 MPa at 573 K is one of the goal in the development of heat-resistant Al-based alloys. The present authors have that the ten- sile strength reaching 1560 MPa at room temperature is achieved for Al-based amorphous alloys containing fcc- Al particles with a particle size of 3 to 5 nm. Based on the experimental data(4) on the absence of internal defects in the nanoscale fcc-Al particles in the high-resolution transmission electron micrographs, the achievement of the ultrahigh tensile strength has been to result from the dispersion strengthening due to the nanoscale fcc-Al particles without internal defects. It is to be expected that the dispersion strengthening due to the defect-free fcc-Al particles is effective even at elevated temperatures below the crystallization temperature of the amorphous matrix and the good elevated-temperature retention of strength is achieved in the mixed phase state. II. Experimental Procedure An alloy ingot with composition A188Ni9Ce2Fel was prepared by arc melting of mixtures of pure Al, Ni, Ce and Fe metals in a purified argon atmosphere. Rapidly solidified ribbons with a width of 1.5 mm and various thicknesses ranging from 15 to 30 gm were prepared by a single roller melt-spinning technique and the volume frac- tion of fcc-Al phase (Vf) was controlled by changing either the rotation speed of copper roller with a diameter of 200 mm in the range of 25 to 83 s -l (1500 to 5000 rpm) or the ejecting temperature of molten alloy. Rapidly solidified structure was examined by X-ray diffrac- tometry, scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) techniques. The TEM samples were prepared by thinning elec- trolytically the melt-spun ribbons in a solution of 25% nitric acid and 75% methanol at 243 K. Crystallization temperature (Tx) and heat of crystallization were measured by differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) at a heating rate of 0.67 K/ s. The Vf in the melt-spun ribbons was determined from the ratio of the amount of heat released during heating from room temperature to 850 K to the heat of crystallization in an amorphous single phase. Yield strength, tensile fracture strength and elongation were examined by an Instron-type tensile testing machine at an initial strain rate of 8.3 x 10-4 s -l

I V. Summary The mixed structure consisting of fcc-Al particles with a size of 3 to 10 nm embedded in an amorphous matrix was found to form by controlling the cooling rate of an A188 Ni9Ce2Fel alloy from the melt. No contrast revealing the existence of internal defects in the fcc-Al particles is seen in the high-resolution electron micrograph, in- dicating the formation of the defect-free particles. When the volume fraction of the homogeneously dispersed Al particles is about 25%, the Of increases to 1560 MPa which is about 1.5 times as high as the highest value for the amorphous single phase, accompanied by an increase of elongation, E and Hv. The significant increase in Of is presumably due to the dispersion strengthening of the nanoscale Al particles without internal defects. The ultimate tensile strength (0B) also shows high values at elevated temperatures and keeps a high level of 970 MPa even at 573 K. It is notable that the 0B at 573 K is about 15 times as high as that for conventional age-hardening type Al-based alloys. There were no appreciable increases in particle size and interparticle spacing of the Al par- ticles even in the ledge area which was deformed at 425 K. It is therefore presumed that the extremely high elevated-temperature strength for the mixed phase alloy is due to the high structural stability and the high mechanical property retention at elevated temperatures for the nanoscale fcc-Al particles without internal defects.
