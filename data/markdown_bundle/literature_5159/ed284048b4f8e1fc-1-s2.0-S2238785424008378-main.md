ABSTRACT 
Ni-based single-crystal superalloys are normally prepared by directional solidification. Due to different solidi -
fication sequences, Ni-based single-crystal superalloys exhibit a typical dendritic arm structure, and the element 
segregation in the dendritic arm can affect the high temperature mechanical properties of the alloy. Solid so -
lution treatment has a certain extent to reduce the inter-dendritic and dendritic arm between the concentrations 
of the elements, thereby changing the alloy micro-nano view parameters to improve the high temperature me -
chanical properties of the alloy. In this study, nanoindentation experiments were carried out on the inter- 
dendritic and dendritic arm of the cast and solid solution samples. The micromechanical parameters were 
calculated. The amounts of changes in elemental concentration were discussed with the results of the energy 
dispersive spectroscopy and electron probe microanalysis, so as to analyse the relationship between the 
micromechanical parameters and the changes in elemental concentration. The results show that solid solution 
treatment has a significant impact on the elastic modulus, with an increase of 114.7% of the inter-dendritic 
region and an increase of 142.7% of the dendritic arm. However, solid solution treatment has little effect on 
the hardness, with no significant change in the hardness of the inter-dendritic region and an increase of 43.86% 
of the dendritic arm. Solid solution treatment has a more significant effect on improving the segregation behavior 
of Al and Co elements, with the segregation coefficients of Al and Co elements in the inter-dendritic region 
decreasing and increasing by 36% and 19%, and the segregation coefficients of Al and Co elements in the 
dendritic arm region decreasing and increasing by 29% and 34%. 
1. Introduction 
The polycrystalline Ni-based high-temperature superalloys prepared 
by traditional methods usually have casting defects such as small angle 
grain boundaries and large angle grain boundaries. The introduction of 
grain boundaries can reduce the service life, elongation, and cross- 
sectional shrinkage of the alloy during usage, thereby affecting its 
high-temperature mechanical properties. Therefore, in recent years, 
directional solidification methods have been used to manufacture new 
Ni-based single-crystal superalloys to eliminate the adverse effects of 
grain boundaries [ 1 – 3 ]. However, during the directional solidification 
process, due to the solidification sequence and chemical element 
segregation, Ni-based single-crystal superalloys exhibit a typical den -
dritic arm structure, in which a large number of refractory elements such 
as Re, Mo, and W are enriched in the dendritic arm, and Al, Ta, and other 
elements are enriched in the inter-dendritic [ 4 – 7 ]. This chemical

inter-dendritic and dendritic arm but also the formation of defective 
pores, which adversely affects the high-temperature creep properties of 
the alloy [ 8 – 10 ]. In order to reduce the difference in elemental content 
between the inter-dendritic and dendritic arm regions, a homogenisa -
tion heat treatment is usually used to reduce the difference in elemental 
concentration between the two regions [ 11 – 13 ]. Although the differ -
ences in elemental concentration between the inter-dendritic and den -
dritic arm regions can be minimised by homogenising heat treatment, it 
is still difficult to achieve complete elemental homogenisation of 
Ni-based single-crystal superalloys by heat treatment, especially those 
containing more solid solution strengthening elements [ 14 , 15 ]. There -
fore, for single-crystal superalloys, elemental distribution in -
homogeneity will always exist and affect the micromechanical 
parameters and even the high-temperature creep properties of the alloy. 
In recent years, research on the dendritic arm structure of Ni-based 
single-crystal superalloys has mainly focused on the following di -
rections. Firstly, the creep characteristics of the dendritic arm and inter- 
dendritic region are different due to element differences. In Xia ’ s [ 16 ] 
work, it was found that as the time of solution treatment increased, the 
overall non-uniformity of element distribution could be further reduced, 
and the creep energy of the two different structures tended to be 
consistent. At the same time, the evolution mechanism of dislocation 
networks during the creep process was discussed. Secondly, the X-ray 
diffraction (XRD) method and the finite element method are used to 
analyse the residual stresses on the dendritic arm structure, and discuss 
the trend of stress distribution from the dendritic arm core to the den -
dritic arm arms. Epishin [ 17 ] used a combination of experimental and 
simulation methods to calculate the magnitude and distribution of re -
sidual stresses in the dendritic arm and inter-dendritic tissues. The re -
sults showed that the residual stresses in the two regions were mainly 
caused by the thermal contraction effect, which indirectly proved the 
existence of the dendritic arm stresses through the microscopic 
morphology transformation. Thirdly, there are issues related to lattice 
mismatch caused by element segregation in dendrite. By measuring the 
mismatch in different parts of the dendritic arm structure, Bruckner [ 5 ] 
shows that the differences in lattice mismatch in different regions are 
mainly caused by the segregation of refractory elements. He also dis -
cussed the measurement and characterization of the two-phase lattice 
mismatch in superalloys with a high content of refractory elements. 
Although the research on dendritic arm structure is relatively mature, 
most studies are related to creep properties, and there is still relatively 
little research on the mechanical parameters of dendritic arm structures 
at the micro and nano levels. 
From the existing studies, the main methods applied to measure the 
micro and nano-mechanical parameters of Ni-based single-crystal su -
peralloys include the nanoindentation method [ 18 , 19 ], in-situ stretch -
ing [ 20 , 21 ] and the finite element method [ 22 , 23 ]. In 2003, Drust [ 23 ] 
used the finite element method to simulate conical indentations in 
two-phase elastic-plastic materials and investigated the effects of par -
ticle shape and aspect ratio embedded in the matrix material on the 
deformation behaviour and hardness of the indentation. The results 
showed that even if the contact radius reaches about 70% of the radius of 
the particles, the properties of the particles are still well determined. In 
2004, Durst [ 24 ] used the nanoindentation method to test and calculate 
the hardness and elastic modulus of three different types of Ni-based 
single-crystal superalloys. The results showed that the hardness of the 
matrix phase increased with the increase of refractory element con -
centration, while the hardness of the precipitation phase did not change 
significantly. At the same time, the elastic modulus increased with the 
increase of refractory element concentration in all three different ma -
terials. In 2014, I. Povstugar [ 25 ] measured the two-phase hardness of 
Co-based alloys by combining atomic probe tomography with nano -
indentation. The results showed that the increase of Ta element con -
centration increases the hardness of the γ ′ phase, while the hardness of 
the γ phase remains basically unchanged, whereas the change in 
hardness of the γ ′ phase can be attributed to the replacement of Al and W 
atoms in the lattice by Ti or Ta atoms in the lattice. In summary, most of 
the existing research on the micro and nano mechanical parameters of 
Ni-based single-crystal superalloys is focused on γ / γ ′ observation and 
calculation of phases, while there are few measurements and calcula -
tions of the micro-mechanical parameters of the dendritic arm structure. 
In this study, in order to further investigate the effect of solid solution 
treatment on the mechanical parameters of the dendritic arm structure, 
the mechanism of the influence of elemental segregation on the me -
chanical parameters of the dendritic arm structure is discussed. Nano -
indentation experiments were carried out on the as-cast and solid 
solution treated alloy samples, respectively. The main parameters of the 
samples in different states were calculated based on the load displace -
ment curves and the classical Oliver-Pharr method to obtain the elastic 
modulus, hardness, and other relevant micromechanical parameters. 
The results show that the solid solution treatment has a significant 
improvement effect on the elastic modulus, in which the elastic modulus 
of the inter-dendritic region is increased from 72.990 GPa to 156.727 
GPa, an increase of 114.7%, and the elastic modulus of the dendritic arm 
region is increased from 73.091 GPa to 177.408 GPa, an increase of 
142.7%. The effect on the hardness is not as obvious as that on the elastic 
modulus, with no significant change in the hardness of the inter- 
dendritic region, and the hardness of the dendritic arm region is 
increased from 4.188 to 6.025 GPa, which is an increase of 43.86%. 
From the microscopic point of view, the micromechanical parameters of 
the alloy mainly depend on the degree of elemental enrichment and 
concentration differences between different structures, and the solid 
solution treatment is able to reduce the elemental concentration dif -
ferences to a certain extent. Reflecting on the heat treatment process 
through the experimental results, the selection of an appropriate heat 
treatment process will have obvious guiding significance for the 
enhancement of the high-temperature mechanical properties of Ni- 
based single-crystal superalloys.

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Element analysis results 
Based on previous academic research, we can learn that there is a 
large amount of Al, Ta, Ti, and other γ ′ phase forming elements 
concentrated in the inter-dendritic regions, and Re, Co, W, and other 
solid solution strengthening elements concentrated in the dendritic arm 
regions. Therefore, the volume fraction of γ ′ phase in the inter-dendritic 
region is much higher than that in the dendritic arm region. Due to the 
significant difference of the elemental concentration between the two 
structures, there is also a significant difference in the volume fraction of 
γ ′ phase between the two tissues, with γ ′ phase volume fraction in the as- 
cast inter-dendritic region at about 70%, and γ ′ phase volume fraction in 
the dendritic arm region at about 50%. Solid solution treatment can 
further homogenise the distribution of elements in the two structures, 
reduce the difference of elemental concentration in the two structures, 
make the volume fraction of γ ′ phase in the two structures more similar, 
and then affect the micromechanical parameters of the alloy. This study 
mainly focuses on the calculation of micromechanical parameters of 
inter-dendritic and dendritic arm structures and the analysis of influ -
encing factors, and discusses the specific effects of solid solution treat -
ment on the micromechanical parameters of the alloy. The main 
equipment used for EDS analysis in this manuscript is four high-

3.2. Indentation point of as-cast sample 
Schematic and enlarged views of the indentation points in the inter- 
dendritic and dendritic arm regions of the as-cast samples are shown in 
Figs. 8 and 9 , respectively, where (b) to (f) are enlarged views of posi -
tions 1 to 5 in the schematic. The experimental method used for the as- 
cast samples is a fixed-depth nanoindentation experiment, with a 
maximum indentation depth of 1 μ m. From the two-phase SEM 
morphology analysis, it can be seen that the shape and size of the γ ′ 
phase in the as-cast sample are irregular, and there are secondary 
spherical precipitates in some of the γ ′ phase, which is due to the exis -
tence of a sequential order of γ ′ phase precipitation in the directional 
solidification process. During the precipitation and coarsening process 
of the γ ′ phase, the primary γ ′ phase is first precipitated and coarsened 
relatively completely during the directional solidification process. The 
secondary γ ′ phase is coarsened relatively completely during the direc -
tional solidification process, while the secondary γ ′ phase is coarsened 
incompletely due to the late precipitation time and insufficient coars -
ening time, which results in the secondary γ ′ phase showing a spherical 
shape [ 31 , 32 ]. Observation of the morphology of the indentation point 
reveals that some voids can be observed in the indentation point, as 
shown by the green boxes in Figs. 8 and 9 , which may be internal defects 
in the sample. In nanoindentation experiments, the indentation defects 
will more or less adversely affect the measured micromechanical 
parameters.

regions. The same is true for the inter-dendritic and dendritic arm re -

3.5. Inter-dendritic and dendritic arm mechanical parameter results and 
discussion 
Using the OP method, the relevant calculations of hardness and 
elastic modulus of the load-displacement curve of the as-cast and solid 
solution samples were obtained, which are shown in Tables 7 and 8 . 
In order to compare the hardness and elastic modulus of the inter- 
dendritic and dendritic arm regions of the two state samples more 
obviously, the relevant parameters were graphically represented, as 
shown in Fig. 14 . From the graphical data, the average values of hard -
ness and elastic modulus of the dendritic arm region of the as-cast 
samples are 4.188 GPa and 73.091 GPa, respectively, while the 
average values of hardness and elastic modulus of the inter-dendritic 
region are 4.317 GPa and 72.990 GPa. The average values of hardness 
and elastic modulus of solid solution samples were 6.025 GPa and 
177.408 GPa for dendritic arm region, 4.421 GPa and 156.727 GPa for 
the inter-dendritic region. Comparing the as-cast samples, it can be seen 
that there is no significant difference between the inter-dendritic and 
dendritic arm regions in terms of hardness and elastic modulus. After 
solid solution treatment, the hardness of the dendritic arm region is 
greatly enhanced, increasing from 4.188 GPa to 6.025 GPa, which is 
43.86%, while the hardness of the inter-dendritic region has no signif -
icant change, this may be related to the differences in solute concen -
tration and element diffusion between inter-dendritic and dendritic arm. 
Rehman [ 36 ] and Zhang [ 19 ] studied the differences between the 
hardness and elastic modulus of the topological dense phase, γ phase, 
and γ ′ phase. The results showed that the content of difficulty melting 
elements had a significant effect on the hardness. Yang [ 37 ] and Zhang 
[ 38 ] use the atomic probe tomography and nanoindentation experi -
ments to illustrate the effects of Re and Ru element content on the 
hardness of the two phases. In addition, the solidification rate and the 
recovery conditions have a certain impact on the elastic modulus and 
hardness [ 39 , 40 ]. On the contrary, the elastic modulus was greatly 
improved both in the dendritic arm and inter-dendritic region, with an 
increase of 142.7% in the dendritic arm region and 114.7% in the 
inter-dendritic region. According to the existing studies, it is known that 
the inter-dendritic regions are enriched with more elements such as Al, 
Ta, Mo, Ni, Ru, etc., and the dendritic arm regions are enriched with 
more elements such as Re, Co, Cr, W, etc., while the solid solution 
strengthening elements such as Re, Co, Cr, etc., have a greater influence 
on the parameters of hardness and elastic modulus. Drust [ 24 ] stated 
that the local hardness and elastic modulus in the matrix phase can be 
viewed as a function of the chemical composition. As the concentration 
of refractory elements increases, the hardness and elastic modulus in -
crease. Povstugar [ 25 ] has suggested that Co
3
(Ta/Al), formed after the 
replacement of the W atom position in the γ ′ phase cell of Co-based alloys 
by Ta/Al, has a higher elastic modulus. Combined with the SEM image 
analysis of the as-cast samples, it can be seen that there are more eutectic 
structures and secondary γ ′ phases in the as-cast samples, which often 
contain a large amount of elements. The solution treatment can dissolve 
the eutectic structure and coarse γ ′ phase, and change the solute con -
centration difference between the inter-dendritic and the dendritic arm, 
which has a certain effect on the micromechanical properties of the 
alloy. In addition, atoms with larger atomic radii are not easy to pass 
through the γ / γ ′ phase interface, for example, Co, Cr, Re, etc. The solid 
solution treatment can provide the driving force required for elemental 
diffusion and promote the elemental diffusion process between the two 
phases or even between the inter-dendritic and dendritic arm regions, 
which will have an effect on the micromechanical parameters of the 
inter-dendritic and dendritic arm regions. 
4. Conclusions 
In this paper, the following conclusions are drawn after conducting 
nanoindentation experiments, EDS, and EPMA on the inter-dendritic 
and dendritic arm regions of the alloys in the as-cast and solid solu -
tion states, respectively: 
1) Solid solution treatments dissolve eutectic structure in cast samples 
due to elemental agglomeration in the directional solidification 
process, changing the solute concentration in inter-dendritic and 
dendritic arm.
