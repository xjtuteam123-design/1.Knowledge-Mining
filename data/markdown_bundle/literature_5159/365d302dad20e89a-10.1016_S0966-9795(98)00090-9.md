Abstract
Mechanically alloyed and blended Nb–Al–N powders were sintered by the spark plasma sintering process, and their micro-
structure and mechanical properties were investigated. All of the Nb–Al–N compacts consisted of phases in the Nb–Al system in
which the Nb 2N phase was dispersed. The microstructure of blended powder compacts was much coarser than that of mechanically
alloyed powder compacts. The compacts obtained by sintering powder produced by crushing blended powder compacts have ﬁner
microstructure, higher hardness, and higher fracture toughness than blended powder compacts. The strength of Nb–Al–N compacts
increases with increasing the fraction of AlN added to the Nb powder, while their fracture toughness at room temperature decrea-
ses. As for the Nb–Al–Mo and Nb–Al–W system, the e/C128ect of solid-solution hardening of W was larger than that of Mo, and Nb–
15Al–40Mo compact has the highest strength at room temperature and 1273 K among Nb–15Al– xMo compacts. # 1999 Elseiver
Science Ltd. All rights reserved.

1. Introduction
Nb–Al alloys have been considered as a candidate for
high temperature structural materials because of their
high melting points and low densities compared with Ni
base superalloys [1–5]. In general, Nb alloys are strength-
ened by dispersing carbides and nitrides or adding Mo
and W, but there have been very few studies concerning
the e/C128ects of nitrides and alloying with Mo and W [2].
Powder metallurgical technique is one of the best
methods for obtaining ingots of Nb–Al alloys because it
is very dicult to prevent Al from Al evaporation dur-
ing melting [6,7]. In general, the hot press and HIP
methods are applied for obtaining compacts of various
alloys. But in the case of Nb–Al alloys there is a serious
problem of oxidation because of a long sintering time.
Moreover, there are few appropriate materials for the
capsules used for HIP.
Recently, the spark plasma sintering (SPS) process
has received much attention as a method of making
better ingots easily because sintering is performed for
a very short time [8–10]. In this study, we made
an attempt by the SPS method to obtain Nb solid
solution, Nb
3Al, Nb 2Al and Nb 3Al2N compacts, in the
which the Nb 2N phase is dispersed, from blended and
mechanically alloyed (MA’d) Nb–Al–N powders pre-
pared by Nb and AlN powders. At the same time we
prepared Nb–Al–Mo and Nb–Al–W compacts from
blended powders and mechanically alloyed powders
made from Nb, Al, Mo and W powders. All of the
obtained compacts microstructure and mechanical
properties at room temperature and high temperatures
have been investigated. By using these results, we esti-
mated the di/C128erence between blended powder compacts
and mechanically alloyed powder compacts with respect
to their microstructure and mechanical properties, and
evaluated the most desirable method of obtaining
ingots.

2. Experimental procedures
As for the Nb–Al–N system, the investigated compo-
sitions were Nb–8Al–8N, Nb–10Al–10N, Nb–12Al–
12N, Nb–16Al–16N, Nb–22Al–22N and Nb–36Al–36N
(at%) (Fig. 1), [11]. Blended powders were prepared by
blending Nb powder (99.99% pure, ÿ325 mesh) and
AlN powder (99.8% pure, ÿ300 mesh) for 0.5 h in a
hexane-ﬁlled rotational ball mill, while MA of Nb
powder and AlN powder was carried out in an argon
atmosphere for 6 h by using a planetary ball mill (Fritch
P-5). Stainless steel vials (500 ml) and balls (20 mm
diameter) were used for MA. The weight ratio of steel
balls to powder was 8:1. These powders were con-
solidated to the size of 20 mm diameter and 5 mm
thickness by SPS-2050 (made by Sumitomo Coal
Mining) at temperatures between 1623 and 1973 K
under a pressure of 49 MPa in a vacuum of 6 Pa for 5
min. Temperature was measured at the surface of a
carbon mold during sintering. Sintering of MA’d Nb–
22Al–22N and Nb–36Al–36N powders were performed
by setting them in a carbon mold in Ar gas atmosphere
and placing the molds in the SPS machine as soon as
possible, because the powders easily ignited in air. After
sintering, X–ray di/C128raction (XRD), density, microvick-
ers hardness, and fracture toughness [12,13] measure-
ments were made. Microstructures were observed by
SEM. In addition, some samples (2  2 5 mm) were
cut from compacts and compressed at 1473 K at a strain
rate of 1.7  10
ÿ4/s in the vacuum of 1.3  10ÿ2 Pa to
examine their stress–strain behavior.
As for the Nb–Al–Mo [14] and Nb–Al–W [2] systems,
the investigated compositions were Nb–15Al– xMo
(x=0, 20, 40, 60, 85) and Nb–15Al–20W. Blended
powders were prepared by blending Nb (99.99% pure,
ÿ325 mesh), Al (99.9% pure, 10 mm) and Mo (99.9%
pure, 3 mm) powders or Nb, Al and W (99.9% pure, 2–3
mm) powders for 0.5 h in a hexane-ﬁlled rotational ball
mill, while mechanical alloying of the elemental pow-
ders was carried out in an argon gas atmosphere for 4 h
by using a planetary ball mill. The prepared powders
were consolidated by SPS-2050 at 1773 K under a pres-
sure of 49 MPa in a vacuum of 6 Pa for 5 min. After
consolidation, examinations of the microstructure and
mechanical properties of Nb–Al–Mo and Nb–Al–W
compacts were made, similar to the examinations of
Nb–Al–N compacts.
3. Results and discussion
3.1. Nb–Al–N system
XRD patterns of MA’d Nb–Al–N powder compacts
consolidated at 1773 K are shown in Fig. 2. All of the
MA’d powder compacts consisted of the phases expec-
ted by Fig. 1. Similar results were obtained for MA’d
powder compacts consolidated at 1623 K and blended
powder compacts consolidated at 1773 K.
Fig. 3 shows densities of Nb–Al–N compacts and
theoretical density [11,15,16] as a function of Al and N
content. The density of compacts decreases with
increased Al and N contents. For the compositions of
Nb–8Al–8N to Nb–22Al–22N, it did not depend on the
powder preparation process and sintering temperature,
and its relative density was from 98% to 99%. How-
ever, Nb–36Al–36N compacts, which were consolidated
from MA’d powder at 1623 K and from blended pow-
der at 1773 K, have lower densities than the other Nb–
36Al–36N compacts because the former two compacts
have not been suciently consolidated. On the other
hand, the relative density of the AlN powder compact
sintered at 1773 K was 91%, while the AlN compact
sintered at 1973 K had a relative density of nearly 100%
(3.26 g/cm
3) [17,18].

4. Conclusion
In this study, compacts with various compositions of
Nb–Al–N, Nb–Al–Mo and Nb–Al–W have been
obtained from blended and MA’d powders by the spark
plasma sintering process and their microstructure and
mechanical properties were investigated. The obtained
results and conclusion are shown as follows.
1. By sintering blended and MA’d Nb–AlN powders,
it is possible to obtain Nb–Al alloy and the
Nb
3Al2N compacts in which the Nb 2N phase is
dispersed.
2. Microstructure of MA’d Nb–Al–N powder com-
pacts were much ﬁner than that of blended powder
compacts. Moreover, the grain size of Nb–Al–N
compact got smaller with increasing the fraction of
AlN powder added to Nb powder.
