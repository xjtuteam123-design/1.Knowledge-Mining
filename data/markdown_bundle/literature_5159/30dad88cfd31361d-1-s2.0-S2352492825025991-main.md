ABSTRACT
This study systematically investigates the effects of a two-step warm rolling process, involving sequential passes 
at different temperatures with controlled thickness reduction ratios, on the microstructure and mechanical 
properties of Fe
50
Mn
30
Co
10
Cr
10 
high-entropy alloy. The alloy ingot, prepared by vacuum arc melting, underwent 
single-pass warm rolling at a higher temperature with a significant thickness reduction, followed by two-pass 
warm rolling at a lower temperature with a finer reduction ratio. This tailored thermomechanical processing 
effectively refines the microstructure and promotes phase transformation behaviors critical for mechanical 
performance. After two-pass warm rolling, the yield strength increased from 578 MPa to 794 MPa, and tensile 
strength improved from 824 MPa to 934 MPa, while elongation remained stable. Microstructural analyses via X- 
ray diffraction (XRD), electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD), and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) 
reveal that the two-step warm rolling induces deformation-driven FCC to HCP phase transformation, increased 
Σ 3 twin boundary density, and complex grain boundary networks. The synergistic activation of transformation- 
induced plasticity (TRIP) in the FCC phase and twinning-induced plasticity (TWIP) in the HCP phase, combined 
with grain refinement and dislocation accumulation, results in a balanced strength-ductility characteristics. 
Interrupted tensile testing confirms the stability and evolution of these microstructural features during defor -
mation. These findings reveal that the two-step warm rolling approach promotes a balanced strength-ductility 
profile in metastable Fe
50

1. Introduction
While the potential applications of high-entropy alloys in the aero -
space field are increasingly demonstrating significant value with the 
continuous advancement of science and technology, it is essential to 
recognize the challenges faced by conventional alloy systems. Conven -
tional alloy systems typically consist of a single dominant element (e.g., 
Fe-based or Al - based alloys) with minor alloying additions for perfor -
mance optimization. However, this compositional design paradigm 
faces increasing limitations in achieving synergistic improvements in 
strength and ductility. In response to these limitations and to explore 
new possibilities for advanced materials, in recent years, multi-principal 
element alloys (MPEAs) have emerged as a novel alloy design strategy 
[1 – 5] , breaking the single-principal-element constraint of traditional 
alloys. By incorporating multiple principal elements in near-equiatomic 
ratios, these alloys form single-phase solid solutions with face-centered 
cubic (FCC), body-centered cubic (BCC), or hexagonal close-packed 
(HCP) structures [6] , resulting in unique mechanical properties. 
Nevertheless, early MPEAs based on equiatomic designs still suffer from 
persistent "strength
–
ductility trade-offs" [7,8] . To address this chal -
lenge, researchers have proposed nonequiatomic design strategies that 
manipulate the stoichiometric ratios of principal elements to introduce 
gradient or multiscale microstructures in the phase composition and 
deformation mechanisms.

Among the various nonequiatomic systems, the Fe-Mn-Co-Cr-based 
high-entropy alloys, particularly those with compositions designed to 
achieve metastable FCC phases, have attracted considerable attention 
due to their potential to activate transformation-induced plasticity 
(TRIP) and twinning-induced plasticity (TWIP) effects [9] . Since the 
groundbreaking report of the Fe
50

have been devoted to this system. Subsequent studies have focused on 
tailoring its hierarchical microstructures and optimizing the metastable 
phase-transformation mechanisms through advanced 
thermo-mechanical processing strategies, including multi-directional 
forging, warm rolling, and multistage annealing [10,11] . These pro -
cesses have enabled the engineering of nanoscale lamellar structures 
with multilevel grain architectures, achieving remarkable combinations 
of yield strength up to 1.3 GPa with ductility exceeding 60 % elongation, 
representing a significant improvement in strength-ductility product 
over conventional alloys. Furthermore, compositional innovations, 
particularly interstitial carbon doping [12] and precise phase stability 
control, have been employed to enhance the synergistic TRIP and TWIP 
effects, contributing to sustained strain hardening. Current de -
velopments demonstrate that this alloy system successfully integrates 
multiple strengthening approaches-encompassing dual-phase heteroge -
neous structures, nanotwin networks, and dynamic reverse phase 
transformations-establishing itself as a paradigm material capable of 
achieving ultrahigh tensile strength ( > 1.5 GPa) while retaining 
considerable plasticity ( > 35 % elongation) [13,14] .
Despite these advances, the application of warm rolling-a particu -
larly industrially viable thermo-mechanical processing route-in the 
context of metastable Fe-Mn-Co-Cr HEAs remains relatively less 
explored and warrants deeper investigation. Warm rolling, conducted at 
intermediate temperatures (typically 0.3 – 0.5 Tm), offers a unique 
advantage by enabling dynamic recovery suppression to facilitate suf -
ficient dislocation accumulation (strengthening) while preventing 
excessive work hardening and cracking often associated with cold roll -
ing [15,16] . This balance is crucial for managing the stability of meta -
stable phases central to TRIP/TWIP effects. Pioneering work by Huang 
et al. [15] demonstrated that warm rolling in the 200 – 400 ℃ range could 
produce gradient nanostructures in steels, achieving an exceptional 
combination of 2.2 GPa strength and 16 % elongation through disloca -
tion cell refinement and retained austenite stabilization. The success of 
this approach has been extended to other advanced alloy systems, 
including TWIP steels [17] , titanium alloys [18] , and notably, some 
high-entropy alloys [19,20] . For instance, Li et al. [16] reported that 
warm rolling at 400 ℃ of stable CoCrFeMnNi Cantor HEAs induced hi -
erarchical nanotwin networks and dislocation substructures, resulting in 
a tensile strength of 1.5 GPa with 25 % ductility. However, a key limi -
tation often observed in single-stage warm rolling is the potential 
inability to simultaneously optimize all microstructural features-such as 
complete recrystallization, precise phase fraction control, and defect 
structure engineering-necessary for the utmost strength-ductility syn -
ergy. The controlled thermal activation during warm rolling enables 
dynamic recovery suppression while preventing excessive work hard -
ening, creating an optimal balance between defect accumulation and 
plasticity accommodation [21,22] . These successes raise an important 
question: Could a multi-stage warm rolling strategy be more effectively 
applied to metastable Fe-Mn-Co-Cr dual-phase HEAs to better harness 
their inherent TRIP/TWIP capabilities while introducing additional 
strengthening through tailored defect engineering? Although the 
TRIP/TWIP mechanisms have been documented in prior studies, our 
work demonstrates for the first time that the Fe

explore new possibilities for advanced materials, in recent years, 
multi-principal element alloys (MPEAs) have emerged as a novel alloy 
design strategy, breaking the single-principal-element constraint of 
traditional alloys.

processing-microstructure-property relationship model was established

synergistic control of deformation-induced defects and recrystallization 
kinetics.
