ABSTRACT
Anti-phase boundaries (APBs) significantly influence the yield strength and creep resistance of CoNi-based su -
peralloys. However, the effect of Ni on the anti-phase boundary energy (APBE) of the γ’ phase remains under 
debate. In this work, a comprehensive investigation combining density functional theory (DFT), cluster expan -
sion (CE), and Monte Carlo simulations was carried out to clarify this issue. The analysis of equilibrated con -
figurations reveals that Ni plays a key role in suppressing anti-site defect formation, thereby enhancing the 
stability of the γ’ phase. In addition, short range order (SRO) phenomena were observed within the γ’ phase, 
further stabilizing the γ’ phase. With respect to APBE, both Ni and Ta are found to increase the APBE within a 
reasonable compositional range. Notably, their co-addition exhibits a synergistic effect, leading to a further 
increase in APBE. However, in CoNi-based superalloys, an increased Ni content leads to a reduced Ta concen -
tration in the γ ′ phase, resulting in a decrease in the APBE. These findings offer new insights into the compo -
sitional tuning of APBE and provide a solid foundation for designing alloys with controlled shear deformation 
mechanisms in γ’ phases.
1. Introduction
Ni-based superalloys are a class of materials known for their excep -
tional resistance to mechanical and chemical degradation, even under 
extreme oxidative and corrosive conditions at high temperatures. 
Consequently, they have been found in widespread use in aero-engines 
and industrial gas turbines [1] . The superior high-temperature 
strength of Ni-based superalloys is mainly attributed to the precipita -
tion strengthening provided by the coherent γ’ phase [2,3] . In contrast, 
conventional Co-based alloys exhibit inferior high-temperature strength 
due to the lack of precipitation strengthening from the γ’ phase. The 
discovery of a coherent γ’ phase in the Co – Al – W system marked a sig -
nificant breakthrough in the development of Co-based superalloys [4] . 
Owing to the higher melting point of Co compared to Ni, γ’ -strengthened 
Co-based alloys are considered promising candidates to replace 
conventional Ni-based superalloys in aero-engines and industrial gas 
turbines. However, the γ’ phase in Co – Al – W is metastable and prone to 
decomposition at elevated temperatures [5] . To improve its thermal 
stability, a substantial amount of Ni is typically introduced, which has 
led to the development of CoNi-based superalloys [6,7] . However, the 
high-temperature mechanical properties of CoNi-based superalloys are 
only compared to those of the first generation of Ni-based superalloys. 
To further enhance the high-temperature mechanical properties of 
CoNi-based superalloys, it is essential to understand the deformation 
and shearing mechanisms of the γ’ phase [8,9] .
Studies have reported that the addition of Ni to CoNi-based super -
alloys alters the shear mechanism, resulting in deformation behavior 
that differs from both Co- and Ni-based superalloys. For example, Titus 
et al. [10] found that the γ’ phase is sheared by unit a/2[110] disloca -
tions, leaving Anti-phase boundaries (APBs) in its wake in CoNi-based

However, the APBE obtained in experiments is by measuring the 
spacing between partial dislocations, which can be associated with 
significant systematic errors [16 – 19] . On the other hand, although 
direct density functional theory (DFT) calculations can be used to 
determine the alloying effects on the APBE [20 – 23] , it is difficult to 
capture the effects of compositional variation due to the high compu -
tational cost. Alternatively, cluster expansion (CE), when used in com -
bination with DFT calculations and Monte Carlo (MC) simulations, 
provides a satisfactory approach to addressing the effects of multiple 
alloying elements and temperature on the APBE. Essentially, CE ex -
presses the configurational-based energy in a polynomial form of the 
occupation states [24] . These coefficients are obtained by the DFT 
calculated energies. Once the CE model is accurate enough, the total 
energy of a system can be easily calculated.
In this study, a combined approach of DFT calculations, CE, and MC 
simulations is employed to investigate the compositional dependence of 
the APBE. The Co-Ni-Al-Ta system is specifically focused on as a case 
study, as superalloys based on Co-Ni-Al-Ta have shown remarkable 
mechanical properties and thermal stability, making them highly 
promising for future advancements [5,25] . Firstly, the stability of the γ’ 
phase is determined, followed by a discussion on the short range 
ordering (SRO) of phase stability and its effect on mechanical properties. 
Subsequently, a systematic investigation was conducted to assess the 
effects of Ni concentration and temperature on the APBE. The influence 
of Ta content at varying Ni levels was further analyzed. Finally, based on 
the equilibrium composition of the γ’ phase in CoNi-based superalloys, 
the APBE was calculated to rationalize the intrinsically lower APBE 
observed in these alloys. These findings offer theoretical insights for 
tailoring the shear mechanism in CoNi-based superalloys.
2. Method
A combination of methods was employed to investigate and analyze 
the system, as shown in Fig. 1 . First, a series of DFT calculations were 
performed to generate a dataset of different configurations, each asso -
ciated with its corresponding energy. Using this dataset, a CE model was 
developed to capture the complex relationship between the atomic 
configurations and their energies. To further explore the system ’ s 
behavior, thermally equilibrated MC trajectories were generated based 
on the CE model. These MC simulations allowed for sampling the system 
at various impurity concentrations and temperatures, providing con -
figurations that reflect the system ’ s thermodynamic state. Finally, the 
equilibrated structures obtained from the MC simulations were used to 
sample the APBE at different temperatures and impurity concentrations 
statistically. This statistical analysis provided valuable insights into the 
system ’ s behavior. The methods employed in this study are described in 
detail, providing a thorough account of the research procedures and 
techniques used.
2.1. First-principles calculation
Configurations for DFT calculation were generated using the Alloy 
Theoretic Automated Toolkit (ATAT) code [24] . Beginning with 4-atom 
supercells on the FCC lattice, a series of random configurations were 
generated through random substitution of lattice sites by Co, Ni, Al, and 
Ta atoms. With a focus on CoNi-based superalloys, configurations were 
constrained to have a Co + Ni concentration exceeding 50 at%. After 
calculating some of these structures with VASP, a CE model was fitted. 
This model was then utilized to identify low-energy configurations 
across various compositions. If such configurations were not included in 
the dataset, their energies were calculated via DFT and subsequently 
added to the dataset. This process was iterated multiple times until the 
model achieved satisfactory accuracy and no new low-energy configu -
rations were found. In total, 4731 samples were calculated in this study.

shearing.
In alloy design, the ability of Ni to expand the γ / γ’ two-phase region 
allows high-Ni CoNi-based superalloys to tolerate higher additions of Ta, 
W, Ti, and similar elements. A moderate increase in Ta alongside Ni 
helps maintain a high APBE, thereby improving the alloy ’ s mechanical 
properties.
This study focuses solely on the anti-phase boundary energy (APBE); 
however, in practice, γ’ shearing also involves other planar defects such 
as stacking faults and microtwins. Therefore, future work should sys -
tematically investigate the effects of alloying elements on the energies of 
various types of planar defects. Such efforts will help to better control 
the γ’ shearing mechanisms in alloy design and further enhance the 
mechanical performance of the alloys.
5. Conclusion
This study investigates the influence of Ni and Ta concentrations on 
the APBE of the γ’ -(Co, Ni)
3
(Al, Ta) phase by a combination of DFT, CE, 
and MC methods, a relationship that remains unclear when examined 
solely through experimental studies.
The MC simulations conducted to obtain reveal that Ni plays a 
crucial role in suppressing anti-site defects induced by Al, thereby 
enhancing the stability of the γ’ phase, particularly at elevated tem -
peratures. In addition, SRO is observed within the γ’ phase, which 
contributes to improved strength, albeit at the expense of ductility. 
Notably, the existence of SRO in the γ’ phase has often been overlooked 
in prior studies. Given the multicomponent nature of CoNi-based γ’ 
phases, the potential for SRO characteristics similar to those observed in 
high-entropy alloys deserves further investigation and discussion.
To systematically evaluate the effect of composition on the APBE, a 
range of compositions was explored. The results indicate that increasing 
the Ni content leads to a noticeable increase in the APBE. Furthermore, a 
simultaneous increase in both Ni and Ta contents — at the expense of Co 
and Al — yields a synergistic enhancement of the APBE. To gain further 
insight into the relatively low APBE values observed in CoNi-based γ’ 
phases, four representative compositions were selected based on 
experimental data. It was found that a higher Ni concentration tends to 
reduce the Ta content within the γ’ phase. Although Ni contributes 
positively to APBE, its effect alone is insufficient to fully counteract the 
reduction caused by the loss of Ta. Importantly, Ni is also found to
