ABSTRACT
Understanding the relationship between deformation behaviors and mechanisms is significant for 
the processing and application of metastable β titanium alloys. Here we aim to investigate and 
evaluate the abnormal yield strength and strain softening of a Ti-15.1Mo-2.77Nb-3.1Al-0.21Si 
alloy at room temperature. This alloy exhibits a high yield strength of 970 MPa, followed by 
the continuous stress drop behavior in the entire engineering strains (or true strains of 0.018 ~ 
0.056). Digital image correlation (DIC) reveals that the flow stress drop results from local strain 
softening associated with a local increase in strain rate, instead of Lüders strain. The pinning 
between dislocations and Si atoms as well as other interstitial atoms at and near grain boundaries 
is mainly responsible for the high yield strength. Subsequently, dislocations originating from 
grain boundaries can easily slip in a planar pattern along the {110} 〈 111 〉 slip systems, resulting 
in a continuous stress drop. In addition, both the low density of dislocations within β grains and 
large grain size also provide favorable conditions for dislocation slip over a long distance. This 
study reveals the mechanisms of both high yield strength and strain softening in the metastable β 
Ti alloys.
1. Introduction
In general, the vast majority of metallic materials including metals and alloys tend to exhibit strain-hardening behavior during 
plastic deformation due to various effects, such as dislocation strengthening ( Yao and Min, 2022 , Devaraj et al., 2016 , Huang et al., 
2022 ), twinning-induced plasticity (TWIP) ( Lai et al., 2018 , Q. Wang et al., 2024 , Tang et al., 2020 ), transformation-induced plasticity 
(TRIP) ( Li et al., 2021a , Li et al., 2021b ) and hetero-deformation induced (HDI) strengthening ( Zhang et al., 2023a , Zhu and Wu, 2023 ) 
at room and cryogenic temperatures. In contrast, it is common for engineering stress-strain curves to exhibit a continuous flow stress 
drop and strain softening after yielding point at elevated temperatures, which can be attributed to softening mechanisms such as 
dynamic recovery, dynamic recrystallization, grain growth and phase transformation behaviors ( Savaedi et al., 2022 ). In addition, 
some studies have revealed that metallic materials, after severe plastic deformation, can immediately display a higher yield strength, 
followed by a continuous stress drop and low ductility. This is due to the hardening saturation effect associated with completed 
dislocation strengthening and other strengthening behaviors ( Vinogradov et al., 2018 , Zeng et al., 2016 ). Apart from plastic instability 
phenomena like the Lüders strain and Portevin-Le Chatelier (PLC) strain ( Zou et al., 2022 , Xu et al., 2022 ), tensile stress-strain curves 
are generally characterized by an increase in flow stress at the initial plastic stage at room temperature.

Metastable β titanium (Ti) alloys are widely used to produce structural components, especially in aerospace applications due to 
their lightweight and outstanding strength-ductility combinations ( Li et al., 2022 , Mantri et al., 2018 , Lonardelli et al., 2007 , Zheng 
et al., 2017 , Zhang et al., 2022a , Li et al., 2024 ). This type of Ti alloy consists of a full body-centered cubic (BCC) β phase after solution 
treatment in the β phase region and water quenching or fast cooling in air. The engineering stress-strain curves of several metastable β 
Ti alloys with a full β phase generally present low yield strength (about 400 ~ 700 MPa) and continuous strain hardening behaviors 
after the termination of the elastic deformation due to some effects, such as dislocation strengthening, TWIP and/or TRIP at room 
temperature ( Lai et al., 2018 , Sun et al., 2015 , Wang et al., 2022 , Luo et al., 2018 ). The low yield strength can be attributed to the 
relatively low critical shear stress for martensite ( α ˝ and/or α ʹ ) phase transformation and mechanical twin (common {112} 〈 111 〉
and/or {332} < 113 > ) activation within β grains compared with dislocation activation ( Lai et al., 2018 , Zhang et al., 2023b ).
However, a few metastable β Ti alloys, such as solution-treated β -21s alloy ( Liu et al., 2021 , Xu et al., 2016 , Lei et al., 2020 , Kumar 
et al., 2019 , Lei et al., 2022 ) and other β Ti alloys ( Bhattacharjee et al., 2007 , Markovsky et al., 2015 , Wang et al., 2016 ) indirectly 
achieve a peak stress after the elastic deformation, followed by gradual flow softening behavior manifested by the stress drop curves 
with increasing strain at room temperature. These Ti alloys mentioned above present high yield strengths ranging from 800 MPa to 
1000 MPa. Moreover, the martensite phase and twins cannot be found in the samples with large plastic strains. Similarly, the yield 
strength of metastable β Ti alloys is also affected by four common behaviors, including element solid solution, precipitation particle, 
dislocation action and boundary barrier ( Zhao et al., 2022 ). In addition, plastic deformation activation (or yielding starting) of the 
metastable β Ti alloys also occurs through martensite transformation, to mechanical twinning formation, and further to dislocation 
activation and slip with increasing β phase stability, because the β phase is a metastable phase at room temperature. The martensite and 
mechanical twinning can be characterized by comparing the microstructural evolution before and after yielding. Some studies have 
pointed out that yielding is dominated by dislocation slip, including new dislocation formation and conversion of immobile dislo -
cations to mobile dislocations ( Kolli et al., 2015 ). However, dislocation activation and slip are hard to clarify due to the diversity of 
dislocations present in the sample before tensile testing. Additionally, while flow softening has been noted in previous studies, it has 
not been systematically investigated. It may be a mistake to assume that the stress drop originates from normal necking. The un -
derlying mechanisms of stress drop from macroscopic deformation and microstructure need to be elucidated in more detail to provide a 
theoretical foundation for the mechanical processing and forming of this type of Ti alloy.
In this study, we revealed high yield strength and stress drop mechanisms of the β -21s alloy with completely equiaxed β grains 
subjected to tensile deformation at room temperature. Macro-digital image correlation (DIC) and micro-DIC methods were used to 
explore the strain evolution and localization. Moreover, Electron back-scattered diffraction (EBSD) was used to characterize the slip 
traces, microstructural evolution, and crystal orientation. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and aberration-corrected TEM 
(AC-TEM) under two-beam conditions were utilized to investigate the formation and motion of dislocations.

3.1. Initial microstructure and strain behavior
Fig. 1 and Fig. S1 (Supplementary material) show the microstructure of the initial β -21s alloy. EBSD results originate from the 
rolling thickness plane and the rolling plane. The number of grains is sufficient to statistically analyze microstructural features such as 
grain size, crystallographic orientation, and heterogeneity. According to the grain size image ( Fig. 1 a) and inverse pole figure (IPF)

3.2. Microstructural evolution during plastic strain
Fig. 3 indicates heterogeneous plastic deformation macroscopically. Fig. 4 shows the microstructure of the β -21s alloy after tensile 
deformation by EBSD observation. It can be ascertained that deformation-induced martensitic transformation and mechanical twin -
ning within β grains do not occur using EBSD characterization on the six regions of the fractured sample ( Fig. 4a ), with strains ranging 
from 0.032 to 0.588, which implies no TRIP and TWIP effects in this Ti alloy during tension. We have not included crystallization 
images in Fig. 4 because we are certain that there is no dynamic recrystallization. According to the Von Mises criterion, at least five 
active and independent slip systems are required for uniform deformation. For a BCC crystal structure, {110} < 111 > , {112} 〈 111 〉 , and 
{123} 〈 111 〉 slip systems are more important because they are easily activated at room temperature ( Xu et al., 2020 ). Fig. 4b and c
present the Schmid factor of slip systems {110} 〈 111 〉 and kernel average misorientation (KAM) characteristics with increasing strains, 
respectively. Fig. 4d exhibits KAM value and Schmid factor for {110} 〈 111 〉 slip systems with increased true strains ranging from 0.032 
to 0.558. KAM value increases from 0.27 to 1.26, while Schmid factors for {110} 〈 111 〉 slip systems remain stable at about 0.46. The 
Schmid factors for {112} 〈 111 〉 and {123} 〈 111 〉 slip systems also remain stable at about 0.41 and 0.43, respectively. This means that 
the preferential slip systems are {110} 〈 111 〉 .

4. Discussion
According to the above experimental results, two unconventional phenomena should be explained for this metastable β Ti alloy 
composed of fully equiaxed β grains: high yield strength (970 MPa) and flow stress drop after yielding (or strain softening). In this 
section, we first discuss the high yield strength of this Ti alloy. In the second part of the discussion, we discuss the mechanism of flow 
stress drop.
4.1. High yield strength mechanism
In general, the yield strength of metals and alloys can be estimated by some strengthening mechanisms including solid solution 
strengthening, precipitation strengthening, dislocation strengthening and boundary strengthening ( Zhao et al., 2022 ). The initial 
microstructure of β -21s alloy before tension consists of fully equiaxed β grains with an equivalent diameter of over 30 μ m. Moreover, 
mobile dislocations have been mostly removed in β grains during the solution process, coupled with low-density dislocations at β grain 
boundaries ( Fig. 6e and f ). The solid solution strengthening from the relationship between dislocations and substitutional alloy ele -
ments is weak. Besides, the precipitation strengthening can be ignored, because the nano ω and α precipitates are not found (Fig. 1d). 
Therefore, the four strength mechanisms are not crucial reasons for the high yield strength.

5. Conclusions
This work aims to explore the microstructural origins responsible for the high yield stress and strain softening in a metastable β type 
Ti-15.1Mo-2.77Nb-3.1Al-0.21Si alloy at room temperature from macro/micro-strains and microstructures based on experimental 
characterization and numerical calculation. Detailed conclusions can be drawn as follows: 
(1) This Ti alloy consists of fully equiaxed β grains without any other phases. This Ti alloy exhibits a yielding peak of 970 MPa at the 
starting stage of plastic deformation, followed by the continuous stress-drop behavior in the engineering stress-strain curves 
(0.018 ~ 0.056 in true strains). There is no martensite transformation or mechanical twin formation during plastic deformation.
(2) It is noted that the grain boundaries are enriched with Si and O atoms and some immobile dislocations. Moreover, the numerical 
calculation also suggests a pinning effect for the strength contribution. A low density of dislocations and dislocation pinning 
from Si atoms and other interstitial atoms at and near grain boundaries are responsible for the high yield strength.
(3) Macro-DIC reveals that the flow stress drop results from local strain softening associated with a local increase in strain rate, 
instead of Lüders strain. From a microscopic perspective, the initial plastic strain and dislocation slip originate from grain 
boundaries and propagate into grains along the 〈 111 〉 direction. The strain softening can be attributed to the decreased critical 
stress for the dislocation planar slip.
(4) After a certain amount of plastic deformation, the dislocation tangle and pile-up can provide positive conditions for dislocation 
strengthening to achieve a work hardening rate above 0. Dislocation tangle and pile-up mainly originate from the dislocation 
cross and multiple slip.
