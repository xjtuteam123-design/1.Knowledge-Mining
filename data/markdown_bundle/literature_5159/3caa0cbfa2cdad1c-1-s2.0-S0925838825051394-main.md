ABSTRACT
Achieving both high strength and ductility remains a challenge in dual-phase Mg-Li alloys. Although the high 
strength has been achieved through heat treatment or conventional deformation techniques, these methods result 
in low ductility. The Mg-8Li-3Zn-0.5Y alloy was processed using low-strain room-temperature rotary swaging 
(RS), demonstrating outstanding mechanical performance in this work. Tensile testing revealed that the swaged 
alloy exhibits a yield strength (YS) of 208 MPa, a high ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of 271 MPa and a high 
fracture elongation (FE) of 26.3 %. The high strength originates from plastic deformation-induced fragmentation 
of Mg
3
Zn
3
Y
2 
phases, numerous MgLi
2
Zn nano-precipitates and massive accumulated dislocations. Grain 
refinement constitutes the primary strengthening mechanism. Meanwhile, good ductility is attributed to β -Li 
phases with fine dynamically recrystallized grains aligned along the extrusion direction (ED). Furthermore, β -Li 
phases with body-centered cubic (BCC) structure provide enough slip systems, which facilitate coordinated 
plastic deformation. In addition, the RS process significantly affects the strain hardening capability of the alloy 
by increasing resistance to dislocation motion. Our findings propose a viable strengthening approach in indus -
trially relevant ultralight Mg-Li alloys.

Several studies have employed the plastic deformation techniques to 
strengthen Mg-Li-Zn-Y alloys. Wang et al. [14] recently prepared 
Mg-8Li-6Zn-1Y alloy using the cross-directionally rolling process, which 
exhibited a UTS (218 MPa) and FE (20.7 %). Cross-rolling effectively 
fragmented the bulk I phase and promoted their uniform distribution, 
thereby enhancing the effect of dispersion strengthening. Wei et al. [15]
obtained a YS (312.4 MPa) and FE (14.2 %) in Mg-8.5Li-7.5Zn-1.5Y 
alloy using the liquid nitrogen cryogenic rolling processing, attrib -
uting high YS to the dislocation cutting of semi-coherent MgLi
2

nano-precipitates. Wang et al. [16] obtained Mg-8Li-6Y-2Zn alloy with a 
UTS (210 MPa) and FE (27 %) by heat treatment and rolling. The 
18R-LPSO phase introduced by heat treatment improved the strength 
and ductility of the alloy. However, some plastic deformation technol -
ogies like forging, rolling and extrusion have only limited improvement 
for strengthening Mg-Li-Zn-Y alloys. Conversely, rotary swaging (RS) as 
an industrial net-shape-forming process with low cost has received sig -
nificant attention in recent years [17,18] . Wei et al. [19] recently pro -
duced Mg-7Li-4Al-1.5Sn-0.5Bi alloy with a high strength of 351 MPa 
using thermal extrusion and RS at room temperature. High strength and 
elongation are attributed to high-density dislocations and finer grains. 
However, as one of the severe plastic deformation (SPD) technologies, 
the RS is rarely used to process dual-phase Mg-8Li alloys, especially in 
the study of microstructure and mechanical properties after deformation 
and the mechanism of high strength and ductility.
This study achieves a balance between the strength and ductility of 
Mg-8Li-3Zn-0.5Y alloy through thermal extrusion at 250 ℃ followed by 
low-strain room-temperature RS processing. Microstructure evolution 
and strengthening mechanisms of Mg-8Li-3Zn-0.5Y alloys were inves -
tigated in detail, establishing RS as a viable industrial strategy for ul -
tralight Mg-Li alloys.

stress state characterized by biaxial compression superimposed with 
uniaxial tension. Progressive strain accumulation during RS refines 
grains to ultrafine grains or even nanograins, thereby strengthening the 
alloy ( Fig. 11 (a) and (b)). Concurrently, the stress induced by RS 
reduced α -Mg band widths and promoted β -Li grain refinement in Fig. 4 . 
Owing to differences in crystal structure, the grain refinement mecha -
nisms of α -Mg phase and β -Li phase are different under the stress of RS 
processing. The Von-Mises criterion stipulates that uniform plastic 
deformation in polycrystals necessitates the activation of five or more 
independent slip systems via dislocation glide [45] . However, 
HCP-structured α -Mg phase possesses limited independent slip systems 
at room temperature. Consequently, supplemental < c + a > pyramidal 
slips and deformation twinning become essential for compatible strain 
accommodation. As shown in Fig. 11 (e) and (f), extensive deformation 
twins are evident in the as-swaged alloy. Twinning-mediated lattice 
reorientation transforms initially unfavorable slip orientations into 
favorably aligned configurations, facilitating further plastic flow. They 
can stimulate the further slips of grains and then enhance the defor -
mation of as-swaged alloy [46] . In summary, the grain refinement 
mechanism of the α -Mg phase can be divided into three main stages 
during RS. First, massive deformation twins were introduced to the alloy 
at the beginning of the RS. Second, initial grains are refined into smaller 
grains and sub-grains with the increase of strains. Third, a homogenous 
structure with equiaxed ultrafine grains was formed [47,48] . Therefore, 
the α -Mg phases primarily enhance the strength of the swaged alloy 
through these refinement mechanisms.
Conversely, the BCC structured β -Li phase is generally considered to 
possess sufficient slip systems, which enable provide multiple directions 
for crystal slip. As deformation proceeds, dislocation density escalates 
rapidly. Consequently, dislocation interactions intensify during move -
ment, resulting in dislocation tangles in Fig. 11 (d). These tangled dis -
locations are gradually arranged into an approximately planar high- 
density dislocation wall, dividing the original grains into many dislo -
cation cells or blocks. Progressive dislocation accumulation at cell 
boundaries and dislocation walls generates increasing misorientation. 
The deformation is further enhanced, and the cell block structure 
evolves into a more regular lamellar sub-grain structure. The grain size 
continues to decrease, and the orientation difference increases greatly. 
The lamellar structure continues to become thinner, and a key transition 
occurs. The equiaxed (non-elongated) grains begin to form in some 
areas. Finally, the deformation reaches a critical point, the grain 
refinement process reaches a balance, and an average grain size is no

contributed good plasticity of the swaged alloy through slipping.
The results of tensile tests indicate that there is still a persistent 
strength-ductility trade-off in this alloy. Strength elevation coincides 
with ductility reduction as deformation intensifies. The work hardening 
is one of the reasons which leads to the universal paradox. During the RS 
processing, amplified lattice friction against dislocation motion elevates 
deformation resistance. Therefore, the strength of the swaged alloy is 
improved and the ductility is reduced. Despite this, compared with other 
Mg-8Li alloys, the Mg-8Li-3Zn-0.5Y alloy still exhibits high strength and 
ductility after the RS process in this study.

5. Conclusions
This work successfully designed and fabricated a novel Mg-8Li-3Zn- 
0.5Y alloy exhibiting a superior strength, employing room-temperature 
RS processing. Microstructure evolution and resultant mechanical 
properties across different processing states (as-cast, as-extruded and as- 
swaged) were thoroughly characterized. The main conclusions of the 
investigation are as follows:
(1) The swaged Mg-8Li-3Zn-0.5Y alloy shows excellent mechanical 
properties with a YS of 208 MPa, a UTS of 271 MPa and an FE of 26.3 %. 
This represents a strength increased by 114 MPa and 56 MPa compared 
with the as-cast and as-extruded alloy, respectively. Although the 
ductility decreased progressively with deformation level, it still main -
tains good ductility.
(2) The enhanced strength primarily originates from significant grain 
refinement, and massive three-leaf arc-shaped Mg
3

3
Y
2

scale spherical MgLi
2
Zn phases of swaged alloy. The strengthening 
mechanisms are refinement strengthening, precipitation strengthening, 
and work hardening strengthening. The grain refinement is the most 
important factor.
(3) The retained ductility is due to the fact that the blocky α -Mg 
phase was elongated with a streamline shape and dynamic recrystalli -
zation occurred in β -Li phase grains during the thermal extrusion. BCC 
structured β -Li phases have enough slips to coordinate the plastic 
deformation and improve ductility in subsequent swaged processing. 
The Zn precipitated at the grain boundary also contributed to the 
ductility.
