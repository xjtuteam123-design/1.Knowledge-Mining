ABSTRACT 
The microstructure and mechanical properties of IN718 alloy fabricated by laser metal deposition were inves -
tigated. The continuous dendritic Laves generated by Nb/Mo microsegregation is dissolved by homogenization, 
the strengthening phase γ " with dimension about 30 nm precipitates during full heat treatment. The ambient 
temperature tensile strength of laser metal deposited (LMDed) IN718 alloy is enhanced by heat treatment, and 
the elevated temperature mechanical properties vary with heat treatment temperature and forming direction. 
Laves dissolution and complete/partial recrystallization depend on heat treatment, stress release and Nb/Mo 
concentration gradient. The anisotropy of elevated temperature mechanical properties of LMDed IN718 alloy is 
caused by the combined effect of directional grains size, higher relative content of texture, superior intergranular 
strength and residual Laves particles. The fracture mechanism of LMDed IN718 alloy varies with heat treatment 
temperature. The LMDed IN718 alloy with full heat treatment exhibits superior elevated temperature mechanical 
properties at 650 
�
C along building direction. The elevated temperature tensile fracture surfaces consist of 
cleavage plane and/or dimples, the microvoid coalescence (MVC) occurs in the alloy with full heat treatment, the 
intergranular (IG) fracture and cleavage plane or quasicleavage (QC) exist in the alloys with 980STA and double 
ageing.

3.3.3. Elevated temperature tensile fracture 
Fig. 9 shows the elevated temperature tensile fracture along different 
forming directions of LMDed IN718 alloys. The fracture surfaces of 
LMDed IN718 alloys almost consist of cleavage plane and/or dimples. 
The morphologies and size of the dimples in the LMDed IN718 alloys 
with corresponding heat treatment are roughly equivalent. Comparing 
with HT1 alloy ( Fig. 9 a and b), the intergranular (IG) fracture and the 
cleavage plane or quasicleavage (QC) occur in the fracture surfaces of 
HT2 alloys ( Fig. 9 c and d) and HT3 alloys ( Fig. 9 e and f). The cleavage 
plane size in the HT3 alloy is relatively large, and the amounts and depth 
of the hollow and raised regions in the fracture surface of HT3 alloy are 
more than that of HT1 and HT2 alloys. 
The increment of the elevated temperature mechanical properties is 
verified by the content and morphology of residual Laves in the heat- 
treated LMDed IN718 alloy. The fracture characteristics relate to heat 
treatment temperature and microstructure morphology. The change in 
solubility of Nb/Mo in austenite ( Fig. 3 ) signifies Laves re-precipitation 
during solid solution and double ageing ( Fig. 5 c and d). The residual 
Laves facilitates the intergranular facture to form fracture step during 
elevated temperature plastic deformation, and then the elevated tem -
perature plastic deformation is restrained by brittle Laves particles. The 
remaining directional microstructure in solid solution and double 
ageing generates the variation of elevated temperature mechanical 
properties and fracture surfaces along different forming directions. Only 
dimples representative of microvoid coalescence (MVC) are found in the 
fracture surface of HT1 alloy with the highest ductility along 
Z – direction, indicating that the elevated temperature fracture mecha -
nism of LMDed alloy is ductile fracture [ 46 ].

4. Discussion 
4.1. Grain size 
The directional grains are generated along X – direction and 
Z – direction during successive laser metal deposition, as shown in 
Fig. 10 . The grain sizes along X – direction rang 5 – 90 μ m and the corre -
sponding sizes along Y – direction are about 1 – 60 μ m in the horizontal 
section (HS, X – Y plane) ( Fig. 10 a). In the cross section (CS, Y – Z plane) 
( Fig. 10 b), the grain sizes along Z – direction are about 100 – 1300 μ m that 
are much larger than that of Y – direction 10 – 130 μ m. The grain aspect 
ratios (length-to-width ratio) of LMDed IN718 alloys are in the range of 
1 – 14, and the grain width and grain size frequency in the cross section 
(CS) are larger significantly than that of horizontal section (HS), as 
shown in Fig. 11 . 
The relationship among elevated temperature tensile strength of 
LMDed IN718 alloy along different directions is σ (Z) > σ (X) > σ (Y) (as list 
in Table 5 ), and the corresponding grain size relationship is d (Z) > d (X) >
d (Y). The results contradict with Hall-Petch law due to the breakdown of 
Hall-Petch law in micro/nano-crystalline materials and at elevated 
temperature [ 47 , 48 ]. The strength of metallic materials depends on 
intergranular strength and grain boundary strength that decrease with 
increasing temperature. The grain boundary strength is weakened 
significantly at elevated temperature by accelerated atomic transition at 
the grain boundary. Therefore, the relatively large grain size promotes 
the stability of elevated temperature tensile strength of LMDed IN718 
alloy. 
Nevertheless, the fracture mode of HT1 alloy is ductility fracture, and 
only dimples appear in the fracture of HT1 alloy along Z – direction. The 
fracture mode varies to intercrystalline fracture in the HT3 alloy, and the 
mixed mode ductile-brittle fracture occurs in the HT2 alloy. It deduces 
that the elevated temperature mechanical properties of LMDed IN718 
alloy are improved by increasing grain size and aspect ratio along the 
external stress direction.

5. Conclusions 
(1) The microstructure of LMDed IN718 alloy consists of dendritic 
Laves and austenite. Laves dissolution and complete/partial 
recrystallization depend on heat treatment temperature, stress 
release and Nb/Mo concentration gradient. The strengthening 
phase γ " precipitate with dimension about 30 nm during full heat 
treatment. 
(2) The ambient temperature tensile strength of LMDed IN718 alloy 
enhances from 953 MPa to 1334 MPa by full heat treatment. The 
fracture mode is ductile fracture, and the fracture surface pre -
sents as the dimples with fracture step. 
(3) The elevated temperature mechanical properties vary with heat 
treatment temperature and forming direction. The LMDed IN718 
alloy with full heat treatment exhibits reasonable elevated tem -
perature tensile properties at 650 
�
C along the building direction, 
followed by laser scanning direction and overlapping direction. 
The elevated temperature tensile fracture almost consists of 
cleavage plane and/or dimples, the microvoid coalescence occurs 
in the HT1 alloy, the intergranular fracture and the cleavage 
plane or quasicleavage appear in the HT2 and HT3 alloys. 
(4) The anisotropy of elevated temperature mechanical properties is 
caused by directional grains size d (Z) > d (X) > d (Y), higher relative 
content of the texture (001)[110], intergranular strength 
improved by full heat treatment or 980STA, and residual Laves 
particles.
