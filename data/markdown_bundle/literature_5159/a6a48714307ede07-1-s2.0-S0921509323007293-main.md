ABSTRACT 
In this study, the effects of Zr addition on the microstructure and mechanical properties of arc melted Nb-16Si- 
22Ti-2Al-2Cr (at.%) alloy are investigated. Zr obviously promotes the eutectoid decomposition of Nb
3
Si and 
formation of γ -Nb
5
Si
3
. The volume fractions of reticulate-like Nbss/ α -Nb
5
Si
3 
eutectics and lamellar-like Nbss/ 
γ -Nb
5
Si
3 
eutectics increase with Zr addition. The elastic modulus and nanohardness of constituent phases are 
changed by the Zr addition, which can affect the toughness of the alloys. The fracture toughness increases at Zr 
content of 2 at.% (14.15 MPa m
1/2
); however, then drops with the increase in the Zr content from 4 to 8 at.%. 
Finally, the fracture toughness increases abnormally to 14.03 MPa m
1/2 
when Zr content is up to 10 at.%. The 
high-temperature compression strength at 1200 
◦
C decreases with increasing Zr content, but increases when the 
content of Zr is more than 6 at.%. The 10Zr alloy achieves a balance between room-temperature fracture 
toughness and high-temperature compression strength. Moreover, the orientation relationships between Nbss 
and Nb
5
Si
3 
change due to the addition of Zr and high-temperature compression. 
1. Introduction 
The increase in the thermal bearing capacity of turbine engine ma -
terials improves both the thrust to weight ratio and thermal efficiency of 
aircraft engines, which is crucial to improve the mechanical properties 
and energy utilization efficiency of aircrafts. Currently, the service 
temperature of the third-generation single crystal superalloys is limited 
to below 1200 
◦
C, which no longer fits in the service temperature range 
of the materials in the hottest part of an advanced turbine engine [ 1 ]. 
Therefore, it is urgent to find a new-generation ultra-high-temperature 
resistant material at higher temperature. Compared with other 
high-temperature materials, the Nb-Si based alloys have become 
promising alternative materials because of their high melting points 
( > 1750 
◦
C), low density (~6.6 – 7.2 g/cm
3
) and excellent 
high-temperature strength. However, the low room-temperature frac -
ture toughness and severe oxidation at high temperature limit the in -
dustrial application of Nb-Si based alloys. 
The Nb-Si based alloys are well known as in-situ composites that 
consist of ductile phase (Nbss) and brittle phases (silicides, such as Nb
3
Si 
and Nb
5
Si
3
). The ductile Nbss phase can endow the composite with 
room-temperature fracture toughness, while brittle silicides phases 
ensure high-temperature strength and creep resistance. Noteworthily, 
Nb
3
Si is a high-temperature phase and exhibits inferior creep resistance 
in the reported studies [ 2 , 3 ]. Besides, Nb
3
Si is usually retained at room 
temperature in the binary Nb-Si alloys because of the sluggish eutectoid 
decomposition at 1770 
◦
C. The prolonged high-temperature treatment 
should be used to promote the eutectoid decomposition of Nb
3
Si to 
obtain stable phases (Nbss and α -Nb
5
Si
3
). However, the eutectoid 
transformation requires more than 100 h for completion at 1500 
◦
C [ 4 ] 
according to the time-temperature-transformation diagram for the Nb-Si 
binary alloys. The binary Nb-Si phase diagram indicates the presence of 
two allotropic forms of Nb
5

3

5

5

Notably, to maintain a balance among the room-temperature frac -
ture, high-temperature strength and oxidation resistance is the key in 
the practical application of high-temperature structural materials. Many 
researchers have tried to solve this issue by utilizing various methods, 
among which alloying is a very effective approach to improve these 
properties of Nb-Si alloys by regulating the solidification microstructure. 
The Nb-Si based alloys have gradually experienced from binary to 
multielement systems in the past studies [ 1 , 13 ]. The titanium (Ti) 
element can not only improve the room-temperature toughness, but also 
enhance the high-temperature oxidation resistance when it is added to 
Nb-Si alloys. However, the added content of Ti is generally below 24 at. 
% because excessive Ti can decrease the melting point of the Nb-Si alloys 
[ 14 , 15 ]. Besides, alloying elements such as chromium (Cr) and 
aluminum (Al) have been preferentially selected to ameliorate the 
oxidation resistance [ 16 ]. Moreover, zirconium (Zr) can increase the 
kinetics of the eutectoid decomposition of unstable Nb
3
Si phase [ 4 ], and 
it can obviously change the microstructure and ameliorate the me -
chanical properties of Nb-Si alloys. For example, Tian et al. [ 17 ] found 
that the addition of Zr led to the increase in the coarseness of primary 
Nbss phase and promoted the formation of the lamellar eutectic struc -
ture. Moreover, the hardness of Nbss and Nb
3
Si phases was improved as 
well as the yield strength at room temperature increased with increasing 
Zr content. Interestingly, Zr led to the formation of γ -Nb
5
Si
3
, as reported 
in numerous related literature studies [ 7 , 17-23 ]. The formation of 
γ -Nb
5
Si
3 
was studied in the past decades, wherein it was reported mainly 
in the Nb-Si-Ti system alloys with Hf or Zr [ 7 , 8 , 17 ]. Although the effects 
of Zr addition on microstructures and mechanical properties of Nb-Si 
alloys have been investigated, systematic in-depth explorations are 
still demanded. Therefore, Zr is expected to achieve the balance between 
room-temperature fracture toughness and high-temperature compres -
sion strength in the multicomponent Nb-Si-Ti-Al-Cr system alloys. 
Meanwhile, the effect of Zr on microstructural evolution is also studied 
herein. 
2. Experimental work 
2.1. Material preparation 
Herein, six button ingots of Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr- x Zr ( x = 0, 2, 4, 6, 
8 and 10 at.%) were produced, which were marked as 0Zr, 2Zr, 4Zr, 6Zr, 
8Zr and 10Zr alloys, respectively. These alloys were melted by the 
vacuum non-consumable arc furnace with tungsten electrode under a 
high-purity argon atmosphere in a water-cooled copper crucible. The 
raw materials with high purities were selected as follows: Nb 99.9 wt%, 
Si 99.99 wt%, Ti 99.4 wt%, Al 99.9 wt%, Cr 99.4 wt% and Zr 99.9 wt%. 
Total weight of each prepared ingot was about 100 g, which was turned 
over and remelted six times to ensure compositional homogeneity. 
2.2. Microstructural characterization 
The cubic specimens with dimensions of 6 × 6 × 3 mm
3 
were cut 
from the ingots by electro-discharge machining (EDM) for analyzing the 
microstructure and constituent phases. The field-emission scanning 
electron microscope (FESEM, Quanta 200FEG) was used to characterize 
the microstructure and analyze the solid solution of alloying elements in 
the constituent phases. The X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis was used to 
test the crystal structure of the constituent phases with a Pixel3d de -
tector using Cu K
α 
radiation. The volume fractions of the constituent 
phases were measured by using the Imagepro 6.0 software. 
Thin foils for transmission electron microscopy (TEM) observation 
were prepared by a conventional method, i.e., the disks with a thickness 
of 0.3 mm and diameter of 3 mm were cut, then mechanically ground to 
about 50 μ m using sandpaper, and finally ion-milled. TEM (talos f200x 
operating at 200 kV) was used to analyze the microstructure as well as 
the dislocations in high-temperature deformed specimens. The crystal -
lographic properties were characterized by electron back-scattered 
diffraction (EBSD) method.

5. Conclusions 
In this study, the effects of Zr addition on the microstructure and 
mechanical properties of arc melted Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr (at.%) alloy 
were systematically investigated. These conclusions can be drawn as 
follows: 
1. The addition of Zr evidently affects the microstructure and constit -
uent phases of Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr alloy, which possesses primary 
α -Nb
5
Si

addition of Zr, Nb

2. Among the Zr-added alloys, only 2Zr and 10Zr alloys exhibit higher 
fracture toughness than 0Zr alloy, mainly because of the higher 
elastic modulus of Nbss and the dimples and interface decohesion 
formed by the low constraint degree on Nbss phase. 
3. The high-temperature compression strength at 1200

more than 6 at.% owing to the changes of eutectic structures and 
composition. The 10Zr alloy achieves a balance between toughness
