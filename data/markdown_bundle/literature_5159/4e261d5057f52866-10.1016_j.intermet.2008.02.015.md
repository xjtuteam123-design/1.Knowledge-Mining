Ni-base superalloys using the cluster variation method

abstract
Effects of alloying element-content on equilibrium segregation at and adjacent to the (100) g/g0 interface
in Ni-base superalloys were analyzed by using the cluster variation method incorporated with tetra-
hedron approximation and the phenomenological Lennard–Jones potential. Results suggest that Al-site
occupiers in the g
0 phase such as Mo, Re and W were enriched near the interface on the side of the g
phase. On the other hand, Co, which prefers to substitute for Ni site, exhibited minimal segregation in the
g phase. The enrichment of the refractory elements such as Mo, Re and W around the interface was
moderated, because Co addition slightly diluted refractory elements throughout the g phase.

1. Introduction
Ni-base single-crystal superalloy is a key material for a high-
temperature turbine section in an aero engine. Such Ni-base
superalloy is partially creep-strengthened by solid-solution
strengthening due to additions of refractory elements such as Mo, Re
and W. However, the excessive alloying of these elements promotes
precipitation of Topologically Closed-Packed (TCP) phases that de-
grade Ni-base superalloy’s mechanical properties. It has been
reported that additions of Co, Ru or Ir can be beneﬁcial in terms of
hindering the TCP precipitation [1–5]. This associated underlying
mechanism, which may be dependent of the element of Co, Ru and Ir,
is controversial although there have been various investigations
[1,3,6–9]. One of the possible factors for enhancing the phase sta-
bility could be the suppression of equilibrium element segregation
at the g/g0 interface by the addition of Co, Ru or Ir. This might be
because that interfacial segregation could induce the nucleation of
TCP phases. However, the equilibrium element segregation around
the g/g0 interface has not been previously well-understood.
Many atom probe microanalyses have been conducted on g/g0
interfacial chemical proﬁle in multi-component Ni-base superal-
loys, focusing on the local equilibrium at the g/g0 interface [10–13].
Blavette et al. reported Cr enrichment together with Al depletion in
the g phase close to the interface [10]. Murakami et al. [1 1]and
Warren et al. [12] showed Re enrichment in the g matrix around the
g/g0 interfaces. However, even atom probe microanalysis has a dif-
ﬁculty identifying crystal structure at the interface because the
doped quantities of refractory elements are small, i.e., the associ-
ated statistic errors in the measurement are large.
The present study was carried out to provide basic un-
derstanding on the effect of element quantities at the (100) g/g0
interface under the equilibrium state. As for the addition effects of
Co, Ru and Ir on the interfacial proﬁle, published thermodynamic
data for Ru and Ir, which is necessary for the accurate determination
of atomic interactions, are limited. So, only the effect of Co was
focused in the present article. In order to clarify the content effects
of refractory elements and Co on the interfacial segregation, Ni–Al–
Mo, Ni–Al–Co, Ni–Al–Mo–Re–W and Ni–Al–Co–Mo–Re–W systems
were investigated using the cluster variation method (CVM).

3. Results and discussion
The effect of Mo content on the interfacial proﬁle was in-
vestigated by increasing Mo concentration in ternary Ni–Al–Mo
systems with a concentration of Al ¼ 16 at% at 1 100/C14 C. Fig. 2 shows
Mo and Al concentrations on each site for each plane parallel to the
(100) g/g0 interface. On the right of the interface, L1 2 structure of g/
g0 phase exhibited the alternation of Al-rich planes with Al-de-
pleted planes. On the left, all (100) planes in the disordered g phase
have the same concentration. It can be seen in Fig. 2(a) and (b) that
bulk concentrations of Mo and Al at Al site in 45th plane are 6.7 at%
and 82.8 at%, respectively, indicating that 10.5 at% of Ni occupies Al
site in the g0 phase. On the other hand, site fractions of Mo and Al at
Ni site in the g0 phase are very small because they preferentially
occupy Al site [18,19,24]. Mo and Al concentration proﬁles around
the interface show that the segregation at the g/g0 interface region
extends over 10 atomic layers in Ni–Al–Mo systems. This means
that the change of the composition between the bulk g and g0
phases is not sudden in Ni-base superalloys due to the atomic in-
teraction. Such concentration transition between g/g0 phases was
already conﬁrmed by previous studies with atom probe analyses
[6,7,13]. Mo has a strong tendency to occupy Al site in the g0 phase.
Therefore, the Mo concentration at Al site of g0 phase even in the
interface is large as shown by arrows B.

in g0 phase also increased, although that at Ni site did not change

The decrease of Re enrichment at the interface is especially obvious

moderation of their equilibrium enrichments at the g/g0 interface.
Such moderation effect at the interface suggested one of the pos-

Co addition although it is noteworthy that its current g/g0 interface
was assumed to be coherent. The effect of g/g0 lattice misﬁt on the
concentration at the interface is our future work.
4. Conclusions
Numerical analysis using cluster variation method was carried
out to clarify the element-content effects on the chemical proﬁles
around the (100) g/g0 interface in multi-component Ni-base su-
peralloys. This investigation led to the following conclusions.
(1) Increase in site occupancy of Mo for Al site in the g0 phase
resulted in an increase of the enrichment of Mo in the g phase
close to the interface.
(2) Co exhibited very little solute segregation throughout the g
phase.
(3) The enrichment of the refractory elements such as Mo, Re and
W around the interface was moderated due to the Co addition
because its addition slightly diluted refractory elements
throughout the g phase.
