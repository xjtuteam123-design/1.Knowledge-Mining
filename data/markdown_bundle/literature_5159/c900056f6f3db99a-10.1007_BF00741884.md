The high protective properties of thermal diffusion protective coatings are obtained 
with a certain content of the diffusants in the layer. It has been shown that, for examD!e, 
the first zone of an aluminosilicide coating must contain 26-28% A1 and not less than 6-8% Si 
[i, 2]. Such a coating reliably protects high-temperature strength nickel alloys from high- 
temperature oxidation and corrosion in long high-temperature holds, especially in agressive 
gaseous media [3]. This is the result, first, of the fact that at the operating temperatures 
there is spontaneously created a stable silicide diffusion barrier between the coating and 
the alloy being protected, providing a significant increase in coating life, and, second, of 
the fact that as the result of microalloying the ~-AI=O3 oxide with silicon the concentration 
of point defects in the oxide film is decreased,accompanied by a decrease in its diffusion 
permeability. In addition, as the result of the reaction of silicon with the refractory ele- 
ments contained in the alloy entering into the coating and the formation of silicides there 
is a decrease in the harmful influence of these elements on the heat resistance and corrosion 
resistance of the coatings [4]. 
However, with a repeated change in temperature the protective properties of a!uminosi!i- 
cide coatings become partially poorer. Under conditions of thermal cycling as the result of 
the occurrence of additional thermal stresses the quite dense oxide layer spalls and falls 
from the surface, as the result of which the process of redistribution and burning off of the 
aluminum is accelerated. 
A further increase in the properties of aluminosilicide coatings may be obtained by al- 
loying with a third element having a favorable influence on the adhesion properties and mor- 
phology of the protective oxide film. It is known that the addition of rare-earth metals to 
alloys containing nickel, aluminum, and chromium provides a significant improvement in their 
heat resistance [5]. Several different mechanisms of the positive influence of rare-earth 
metals have been proposed. The main one of them is an increase in the adhesion of the sur- 
face oxide films. In addition, rare-earth metals, possessing a high affinity for oxygen,

The change in coating composition from aluminide to modified aluminosilicide provides a 
significant increase in its heat resistance and especially in its corrosion resistance. The 
results of microstructural investigations showed that after a lO0-h hold in molten sodium 
sulfate the aluminide coating is completely corroded and the specimens are coated with a thick 
layer of corrosion products. On the normal aluminosilicide coating there is observed partial 
penetration of corrosion into the outer zone (the corrosion front is limited to a light band 
of y'-phase) and on the modified coating layers of corrosion failure are not observed at all 
(Fig. i). 
Figure 2 presents the results of measurements of the thermally stimulated electron emis- 
sion and the thermal luminescence from the coatings after heat-reslstance tests at IIO0°C. 
The addition of the third alloying addition (lanthanum) to the aluminosilicide coating 
has practically no influence on the kinetics of the change and the~amount of the emission 
flow (Fig. 2). Apparently, the La +s ions do not replace cations in the ~-AI20~ lattice and 
do not influence the point defect concentration in it. 
However, the thermal luminescence, which is related to the content of ~-AI=O3 phase on 
the surface of the coating, is significantly higher for the modified coating than for the 
aluminide and the simple aluminosilicide (Fig. 2), which may indicate the favorable influence 
of lanthanum and to some extent of silicon on the adhesion of the protective oxide film. This 
is confirmed by the results of investigation of the structure of the protective oxide films 
with the use of a scanning electron microscope. Alloying with silicon and, especially, with 
silicon and lanthanum together promotes the formation on the surface of the aluminide coat- 
ing in high-temperature holds of a quite developed, dense, and nonpeeling oxide film (Fig~ 3). 
For example, long-termheat-resistance tests for 2000 h with the application of a large 
number of thermal cycles and removal of the samples for weighing each 50 h showed that for 
the aluminosilicide coating with lanthanum in any stage of the tests a significantly smaller 
quantity of oxidation products falling from the surface of the specimens to the bottom of the 
crucible than for the simple aluminosilicide and particularly for the aluminide coating is 
characteristic. As the result, as shown by micro-x-ray spectral investigations, the concen- 
tration of aluminum in the modified coating after operation is always higher, which provides 
its longer life under service conditions.

DAMAGE OF VZhLI2UALLO¥ TURBINE BLADES 
F. D. Kiselev and V. V. Shestakov UDC 620.192.7:669.245 
The gas-turbime blades are the most heavily loaded parts of gas-turbine engines and to 
a great degree determine their operating reliability. 
The processes of service damage of turbine blades, which are dependent upon such parame- 
ters of the gas-turbine engine as the temperature and stresses under steady conditions, the 
time of operation under different conditions, the rate and amplitude of the temperature dif- 
ferentials, the number of times started up and shut down, and the level of dynamic loads, some- 
times lead to early failure of the blades during service, which may cause failure of the gas- 
turbine engine. 
In determining the reasons for failure of turbine blades it is necessary to investigate 
the character of this failure and the factors causing the occurrence of a crack and promot- 
ing its development. 
The most common failure of turbine blades is fatigue. 
The investigations were made on actual aluminized blades of stage I of a gas-turbine en- 
gine of VZhLI2U alloy (0.14-0.2% C, 8.9-10.5% Cr, 13-15% Co, 5.0-5.7% AI, 4.5-4.7% Ti, 2.8- 
3.2% Mo, 1.1-1.8% W, 0.8-0.9% Nb, 0.7-0.8% V) after different operating times in engines of 
a single type. The character of failure was determined by fractographic and metallographic 
methods in ac=ordance with the criteria presented in [I]. The quality and structure of the 
blade material were investigated by methods of metallographic and spectral analyses, and the 
condition of the hardening y'-phase by electron-microscopic analysis on replicas and also di- 
rectly on a Stereoscan-600 scanning electron microscope. For investigation of the microdis- 
tribution of the elements in the zone adjoining the fracture micro-x-ray structural analysis 
on a Cameca mieroanalyzer was used, and to reveal possible cracks in the investigated blades, 
dye penetrant, edd~current, and Ultrasonic inspection methods. 
The investigation results showed that on the leading edges of the blades there is multi- 
ple cracking. During the investigation some of the cracks were opened. The surface of the 
fractures of the open cracks of segmentary form (maximum depth of crack propagation up to 
0.5 mm) was covered with a dense layer of oxides of gray color and has a smoothed, as if 
"fused," relief with a small amount of folding and weakly expressed fatigue lines (Fig. la), 
which is an indication of the thermal fatigue character of their origin [I].
