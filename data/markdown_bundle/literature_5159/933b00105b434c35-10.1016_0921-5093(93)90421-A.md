Abstract 
The following article is part of an investigation on the effect of Ta addition to Ni-based superalloys. This part deals with 
the creep properties. Two commercial alloys (Nimonic 80A and Waspaloy) and two experimental alloys containing Ta 
have been studied. It was found that Ta significantly improves the creep resistance only if the ageing treatment is carried 
out to develop fully the size and volume fraction of the 7' precipitates. Ta addition also affects the values of Q and n, i.e. 
the activation energy for creep and the stress exponent. 
1. Introduction 
One of the very first Ni-based superalloys in service 
was Nimonic 80A. Its high temperature strength and 
creep resistance are contributed by the 7' precipitates 
and by carbides, mainly of the type M23C 6. Extensive 
research has been conducted on this and other Ni- 
based superalloys during the last 35 years [1-15] in 
order to improve their creep resistance by improving 
their microstructure. The volume fraction, distribution 
and shape of the 7' precipitates have been modified. 
Also, the concentration of carbides which may cause a 
decrease in ductility has been controlled and their 
location has been confined mainly to grain boundaries. 
Grain boundary carbides surrounded by 7' precipitates 
caused further creep strengthening, because of 
depressing void formation resulting from grain 
boundary sliding. Additional improvement has been 
achieved by means of increasing the Ti:AI concentra- 
tions [16]. There were also attempts to replace Ni by 
Co [17] or by other elements [18-21, 23, 25, 26]. How- 
ever, the effect of these elements on creep strengthen- 
ing was not conclusive. A very significant improvement 
in creep strength and a prolongation of creep life were 
achieved more recently by the addition of alloying 
elements such as C, B, Zr and Hf. It was found [24] that 
the addition of these elements to a series of Ni-based 
superalloys resulted in extending the creep life five

times at intermediate temperatures. The major con- 
tributor in this respect was Hf. 
The effect of Ta on the creep resistance of Ni-based 
superalloys has been studied by various investigators 
[22, 27-30]. Their conclusion was that most of the Ta 
goes into the y' phase (70%-80%) and that the rest can 
be found in the carbides or dissolved in the matrix [22]. 
Accordir~gly, Ta increases the volume fraction of 7' 
[27-30] and, therefore, significantly increases the creep 
strength and creep life. 
It was decided to carry out an investigation of the 
kinetics of the nucleation and growth of 7' precipitates 
and their effect on creep strength, including four experi- 
mental alloys containing Ta as well as commercial Ni- 
based superalloys. The nucleation and growth studies 
are published elsewhere [31, 32], while the contribu- 
tion of Ta addition to the improvement in creep 
properties is the subject of this study. The investiga- 
tions of the nucleation and growth kinetics of 7' precip- 
itates were performed on a larger number of alloys; 
however, the creep study presented here involves only 
two commercial and two experimental alloys.

4. Discussion 
The following discussion is based on the results of 
this study, together with the information obtained from 
the investigation of the microstructure, especially the 
kinetics of the nucleation and growth of the 7' precipi- 
tates [31, 32]. 
In the under-aged conditions, only a few creep 
experiments were performed and only one experiment 
with Nimonic 80A under the relatively high stress of 
370 MPa. Therefore, one can only compare the creep 
results of alloy 77 with those of Nimonic 80A, and 
their SSCRs are almost identical under the same condi- 
tions of stress and temperature. 
As mentioned before, 70%-80% of the Ta goes into 
the 7' phase and, therefore, its full contribution to

creep strengthening comes into effect only when the 
alloys are fully aged. Solution treatment followed by 
ageing at 750 °C for 20 h produced spherical ~' preci- 
pitates in a relatively low concentration with an average 
diameter of 10 nm [32]. Accordingly, the Ta-containing 
alloys 72 and 77 do not exhibit better creep resistances 
than that of Nimonic 80A (see Table 2) if the ageing is 
done at 750°C. However, the improved creep resis- 
tance of alloy 77 compared with that of alloy 72 in the 
under-aged condition is probably due to the addition 
of Co, W and Mo, which are matrix strengtheners. 
Ageing at 900 °C for 20 h results in a completely 
different microstructure [32]. In all the alloys, the 7' 
precipitates become cubic with an average size of 40 
nm, which is four times larger than the diameter of the 
spherical precipitates obtained with the ageing treat- 
ment at a lower temperature. The volume fraction of y' 
precipitates is also higher [32] and, accordingly, the 
interprecipitate distance is smaller. However, there is a 
difference between the commercial and the Ta-contain- 
ing alloys, since the volume fraction of y' precipitates 
in the Ta-containing alloys is higher [32]. Therefore, 
the dominant factors in improving the creep resistance 
of the Ta-containing alloys are the volume fraction and 
morphology of the precipitates, together with the high 
concentration of Ta in the precipitate itself. 
Again, one should consider strengthening resulting 
from a mismatch in lattice parameter in the matrix, 
owing to soluble elements such as Co, W and Mo. The 
small difference between the SSCRs in alloys 72 and 
77 proves that the addition of 2 wt.% Mo, 2 wt.% W 
and 9 wt.% Co to alloy 77 has a relatively minor effect 
on the creep strengthening when the alloys are fully 
aged. 
Comparing the creep resistance of fully aged 
Waspaloy with that of Nimonic 80A proves that substi- 
tuting Ni by Co is unjustified. This has also been 
reported by other investigators [1, 3, 8, 9, 19]. The 
atomic mismatch between Co and Ni is relatively small 
(1%) and, therefore, it contributes only a little to matrix 
strengthening. However, the addition of Co at the 
expense of Ni reduces the volume fraction of 7' preci- 
pitates and enhances the formation of unstable M23C¢~ 
carbides in grain boundaries.

5. Conclusions 
The results of this investigation can be summarized 
as follows. 
(1) Ta addition to an Ni-based superalloy, such as 
Nimonic 80A, significantly improves its creep resis- 
tance, provided that the ageing treatment causes full 
development of the size and the volume fraction of the 
7' precipitates. 
(2) Replacement of Ni by Co results in inferior 
creep strength (compared between Waspaloy and 
Nimonic 80A); however, the addition of Ta to the Co- 
containing alloy causes substantial hardening. 
(3) The addition of Ta causes increases in the Q and 
n values and, therefore, its creep-strengthening con- 
tribution is more pronounced at lower temperatures 
and stresses. 
(4) As a result of a parallel study, it has been 
observed that the addition of Ta to an Ni-based super- 
alloy increases the volume fraction of 7' precipitates 
and improves their stability, and, therefore, contributes 
to creep strengthening.
