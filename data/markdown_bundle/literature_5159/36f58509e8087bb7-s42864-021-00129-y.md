Composition design of high yield strength points in single‑phase 
Co–Cr–Fe–Ni–Mo multi‑principal element alloys system based 
on electronegativity, thermodynamic calculations, and machine 
learning

Abstract
A method which combines electronegativity difference, CALculation of PHAse Diagrams (CALPHAD) and machine learning 
has been proposed to efficiently screen the high yield strength regions in Co–Cr–Fe–Ni–Mo multi-component phase diagram. 
First, the single-phase region at a certain annealing temperature is obtained by combining CALPHAD method and machine 
learning, to avoid the formation of brittle phases. Then high yield strength points in the single-phase region are selected by 
electronegativity difference. The yield strength and plastic deformation behavior of the designed Co14Cr30Ni50Mo6 alloy 
are measured to evaluate the proposed method. The validation experiments indicate this method is effective to predict high 
yield strength points in the whole compositional space. Meanwhile, the interactions between the high density of shear bands 
and dislocations contribute to the high ductility and good work hardening ability of Co14Cr30Ni50Mo6 alloy. The method is 
helpful and instructive to property-oriented compositional design for multi-principal element alloys.
Keywords High entropy alloys · Multi-principal element alloys · Yield strength · Electronegativity difference · 
CALculation of PHAse Diagrams · Machine learning
1 Introduction
High entropy alloys (HEAs), or multiple principal ele-
ment alloys, have developed from equiatomic or near 
equiatomic concentrations to non-equiatomic solid solu-
tion islands in multi-component phase diagrams [1 ], 
which greatly explores the composition space for metals 
and alloys. HEAs have attracted extensive attention due 
to huge opportunities for discovering novel properties and 
their combinations; however, it is still a big challenge to 
search the points with requested properties in the center 
region of the multi-component phase diagram, due to their 
chemical complexity. For example, the yield strength is an 
important mechanical property for three-dimensional (3d) 
transition element HEAs, and many attempts at composi-
tion designs have been tried to improve the yield strength 
mainly by experimental methods [2 ], but exploration is 
limited by now. Recently, an efficient strategy for a yield 
strength targeted design of 3d HEAs has been proposed, 
based on the atomic-level pressure approximation [3 ]. The 
atomic-level pressure of 3d HEAs is dominantly driven 
by the charge transfer, which can be approximated by the 
electronegativity difference among the constituting ele-
ments. Solid-solution strengthening in HEAs is in direct 
proportion to the atomic-level pressure difference, and also 
proportional to electronegativity difference [4 ]. It is worth 
noting that HEAs with multi phases are always loss of 
ductility, and therefore, prediction of the region for single

Full-field calculations for temperatures of phase

algorithms have been developed to predict the phase for -

HEAs, and these applications of ML are successful. In 
this study, first, phase diagrams calculations and machine 
learning are combined to predict the single-phase region 
in the whole configuration space for widely investigated 
Co–Cr–Fe–Ni–Mo multi-principal element alloys. Then, 
the electronegativity difference and temperature of phase

to predict the composition points with high yield strength 
in single-phase multi-principal element alloys. Lastly, vali-

This paper presents a high-throughput method for 
screening and designing single-phase multi-principal ele-
ment alloys with desired mechanical properties. To obtain 
better mechanical properties, the composition rang of 
Co–Cr–Fe–Ni–Mo system alloy with higher yield strength 
was given. In addition, some of the predicted results are 
verified by some experiments. Such research will contribute 
to the development of mechanical learning in prediction of 
new composition alloys.
2 Experimental
2.1 Component screening details
About 600 datasets of phase decomposition temperatures 
were obtained using CALPHAD calculations by changing 
the elemental content randomly in the Co–Cr–Fe–Ni–Mo 
system. It means multi-principal element alloys own a single 
phase when the heat treatment temperature is higher than 
the phase decomposition temperature. Thermodynamic cal-
culations were performed by JmatPro v 9.0 software using 
the nickel-based superalloy database. Then, the datasets 
are transferred for training the ML model, to predict the 
phase decomposition temperatures in the whole configura-
tion space of the Co–Cr–Fe–Ni–Mo system. Support vector 
machines (SVMs) are chosen for building the ML models. 
A set of design parameters [10, 11] is used to improve the 
prediction accuracy, as shown in Table  1. Predicted tem-
peratures of the phase decomposition for 101,402 composi-
tion points are obtained. The concentrations are constrained 
within 0 ~ 50 at.% for Co, Cr, Fe, Ni and 0 ~ 20 at.% for Mo.
2.2 Research procedure
The alloys were prepared by arc-melting the mixtures of 
high-purity (99.9 wt.%) elements under an argon atmos -
phere, subsequently homogenized at 1200 °C for 10 h. 
The homogenized ingots were cold-rolled with a thick -
ness reduction of 66%, followed by annealing at 920 °C 
for 0.5 h and then water quenching. The tensile proper -
ties were conducted using a SHIMADZU AGS universal 
electronic tensile tester with an engineering strain rate of 
1×10−3  s−1. At least three tensile specimens for each alloy 
were tested at room temperature. Dog-bone-shaped tensile 
specimens with a gauge length of 10 mm, thickness of 
1 mm and width of 5 mm were machined from recrystal-
lized sheets by electric discharge machining. Microstruc-
ture characterization was performed by a scanning electron 
microscope (SEM, JEOL 7100F). The average grain sizes 
are determined by the conventional mean-linear-intercept

3 Results
3.1 Component screening and prediction
The datasets based on CALPHAD calculations are trans -
ferred for training the ML model using SVMs, as shown in 
Fig. 1. The regression coefficient R of the ML model with-
out or with design parameters (Table  1) is 0.97 and 0.993, 
respectively. The prediction accuracy is improved when the 
design parameters are used in the ML models, as shown in 
Fig. 1b. The correlation of element contents and CALPHAD 
temperatures of phase decomposition are shown in Fig.  1c, 
and the results indicate that the phase decomposition occurs 
at a higher temperature when the contents of Cr and Mo 
increase. The reason is that more Cr and Mo in HEAs trend 
to form sigma phases or other intermetallic phases [12, 13].
Based on the optimized ML model, the phase decompo-
sition temperatures of 101,402 composition points for the 
Co–Cr–Fe–Ni–Mo system are predicted. It is beneficial to 
obtain single-phase multi-principal element alloys when the 
heat treatment temperature is higher than phase decomposi-
tion temperatures. The correlation of electronegativity dif-
ference and predicted temperatures of phase decomposition 
is shown in Fig.  2a. Element contents versus electronega-
tivity differences are plotted at the predicted temperatures 
of 900 ± 50 °C in Fig.  2b, and the results indicate the Fe 
content tends to zero, and Ni content tends to 50 at.%, as 
the electronegativity difference approaches the maximum. 
It is known that the yield strength is positively related to 
electronegativity difference [3]. Then the optimized element 
contents for the high yield strength and single phase are 
obtained, which is Co 10 ~ 20 at.%, Cr 25 ~ 35 at.%, Fe 0 ~ 5 
at.%, Ni 45 ~ 50 at.% and Mo 4 ~ 8 at.%, as the electronega-
tivity difference is between 0.132 and 0.136. Furthermore, 
frequency maps on the concentration distribution of different 
elements in filtered alloys with the electronegativity differ -
ence between 0.132 and 0.136 are shown in Fig. 3. It can be 
seen that the range of concentration distribution is clearly 
reduced. The main distribution of Fe is close to the mini-
mum value of 0, and Ni is close to the maximum value of 
50 at.%. Other elements of Co, Cr and Mo are distributed in 
the center regions. 
The interaction between every two elements among the 
frequency maps are analyzed, as shown in Fig. 4. Obviously, 
the relationship between Cr and Mo is in an inverse propor-
tion. Both Cr and Mo are the formation elements of interme-
tallic phases [11, 12]; therefore, the total amount of the two 
elements is limited. On the other hand, the electronegativity

3.2 Mechanical properties analysis based 
on prediction results
Two compositions of Co14Cr30Ni50Mo6 and Co20Cr24Ni50Mo6 
are selected to evaluate the proposed method. The electron-
egativity difference of Co14Cr30Ni50Mo6 is 0.136, which is the 
highest value as predicted temperatures of phase decomposi-
tion are about 900 °C. After rolling and annealing, single face 
centered cubic (FCC) phases are obtained in the two alloys, 
as shown in Fig. 5a–c. The yield strength of Co14Cr30Ni50Mo6 
alloy with a grain size of 17.1 μm is 472 MPa, as shown in 
Fig. 5d. Typical TEM micrograph of the Co14Cr30Ni50Mo6 
alloy is shown in Fig. 6, and the results indicate there are no 
nano-precipitates in the matrix. Thus, the main contributions 
to yield strength are solution strengthening and grain bound-
ary strengthening. The reported results of yield strength and 
electronegativity difference of single-phase Co–Cr–Fe–Ni–Mo 
alloys are summarized in Table 2. The alloys with the similar 
preparation process and grain size are selected for comparison. 
It indicates the higher yield strength are obtained when the 
electronegativity difference is higher, as the average grain size 
of recrystallized Co–Cr–Fe–Ni–Mo alloys are all about 20 μm. 
To our knowledge, the method to screen chemical points with 
higher yield strength for single-phase Co–Cr–Fe–Ni–Mo 
multi-principal element alloys is proved to be valid.

4 Discussion
The prediction of yield strength for HEAs is a tough prob-
lem owing to the highly non-linear relationship among 
composition, processing and performance. As a result, the 
compositional design of high yield strength HEAs mainly 
relies on trial and error, or intuitions. For example, minor 
additions of Mo in the equal-atomic HEAs [16] or higher 
contents of Co and Cr [19] is adopted to improve the yield 
strength. Surely, the yield strengths of HEAs are improved 
based on the addition of elements with a higher metallic 
radius. However, a rapid and accurate method to predict 
the higher yield strength points is needed. It is practical 
to utilize specific elemental information that sufficiently 
represents the underlying atomic information. Recent stud-
ies indicate that the composition inhomogeneity in the 
atomic level is almost inevitable in single-phase HEAs 
[20], and the dislocation movement faces a rugged atomic 
and energy landscape, which results in finer dislocation 
cell structures [21], nano-twinning during deformation 
[22]. Dislocation cell, twin induced plasticity (TWIP) 
or transformation-induced plasticity (TRIP) effects [19] 
occur to ensure the ductility of HEAs with different stack -
ing fault energies. Therefore, the high yield strength is a 
key factor to develop structural material for HEAs. In this 
work, electronegativity difference is effective to predict 
the potential good candidates with high yield strengths, 
meanwhile, an accurate prediction of single-phase region 
based on the CALPHAD and machine learning is neces-
sary to avoid the brittle intermetallic compound. Machine 
learning plays an important role to accelerate materials 
research [23– 30].
Kinetics of intermetallic compounds, such as sigma 
phase, are worthy to be mentioned [31, 32]. There are 
overwhelming experimental observations indicating inter -
metallic phases are formed after prolonged exposures in 
the intermediate temperatures in high entropy alloys. Wu 
et al. [33] have investigated the transition temperature 
between FCC single phase and “FCC + σ’’ via long-term 
annealing experiments in (CoCrFeNi)-Mo x alloy system. 
The results indicate the transition temperatures of Mo0.1 
and Mo0.2 alloy are about 900–1000 °C and 1000–1100 °C, 
respectively. The transition temperatures of Mo0.3, Mo0.4,

5 Conclusion
In this work, the high yield strength region in the 
Co–Cr–Fe–Ni–Mo system is obtained by combining elec-
tronegativity difference, CALPHAD and machine learning. 
The prediction accuracy of phase decomposition tempera -
ture is improved when the design parameters are used in the 
ML models, which is beneficial for the phase prediction at a 
certain temperature. Then, the optimized ranges of element 
contents for the high electronegativity difference and single 
phase are obtained. Moreover, when the Mo and Fe are 6 
at.% and 0 at.%, respectively, there are wider concentration 
ranges for Ni and Co to meet the designed requirement. The 
yield strength of single-phase Co14Cr30Ni50Mo6 alloy with a 
grain size of 17.1 μm is 472 MPa, with an electronegativity 
difference value of 0.136. The formation of the high density 
of shear bands and dislocations, and the interaction between 
the two, contribute to the high ductility and good work hard-
ening ability. The results show a satisfactorily compositional 
design ability. The developed method can be applied to other 
HEA systems for the screening of high yield strength points. 
The method is beneficial to design advanced HEAs with 
combinations of structural and functional properties in the 
future, and to accelerate the discovery of new materials.
