ABSTRACT
A cost-effective Ni-Fe-Cr-Al-based medium entropy alloy was designed using the CALPHAD approach and syn -

room-temperature mechanical properties were evaluated. An excellent strength-ductility synergy of > 1000 MPa

1. Introduction
The discovery of high-entropy alloys (HEAs) [1,2] pushed materials 
scientists to explore the vast compositional space for the development of 
multicomponent high entropy materials with unprecedented properties 
at a wide range of temperatures [3 – 9] . The exploration for the promising 
high-temperature mechanical performance of HEAs led to extensive 
investigation in two groups of alloy systems, namely (i) 
refractory-element-based HEAs (RHEAs), and (ii) transition-metal-based 
HEAs (TM-HEAs). The microstructure of RHEAs generally consisted of a 
disordered body-centred-cubic (BCC) matrix, often 
precipitation-hardened by the ordered B2 intermetallics [10] . This new 
alloy system generally exhibited phase separation and microstructural 
destabilization during prolonged exposure at intermediate temperatures 
[11] . On the contrary, several TM-HEAs mimicked the microstructure of 
superalloys consisting of disordered face-centred cubic (FCC)matrix 
precipitation hardened by ordered L1
2 
intermetallic (also called 
High-Entropy Superalloys (HESAs)) [12] . Furthermore, since its incep -
tion, TM-HEAs became a topic of intense research interest as they 
exhibited microstructural stability at elevated temperatures with suc -
cessful suppression of undesirable topologically closed packed (TCP) 
phases [12] [13] . The microstructural stability was attributed to the 
domination of entropy of mixing over enthalpy of mixing at elevated 
temperatures. Moreover, at low temperatures, the dominance of 
enthalpy of mixing over the entropy of mixing might lead to phase 
separation due to microstructural instability. In addition, nanoscale 
phase separation has been observed in several TM-HEAs under 
high-resolution atom-probe tomography (APT) characterization at room

microstructural stability at intermediate temperatures to assess the 
microstructure evolution from room temperature to high temperatures. 
Furthermore, the role of the carbides (if any) should be assessed to

4.2. Mechanical behavior at 450
◦
C
The alloy shows an appreciable yield strength (YS) of 475 MPa and a 
UTS of 900 MPa accompanied by an impressive ductility of 40 % at 
450
◦
C (refer Fig. 8 ). The flow stress shows a serrated behavior at 450
◦
C 
indicative of the popular Portevin-Le Ch ˆatelier (PLC) effect [43] . This 
PLC effect is often observed at intermediate temperatures in steels [44] , 
aluminium-magnesium alloys [45] and superalloys [46,47] . This is 
attributed to the dislocation locking and unlocking during the tensile 
tests in the studied alloys. One of the widely accepted reasons for this 
PLC effect is the dynamic strain ageing (DSA) phenomenon. Initial 
conceptualization of this phenomenon stated that carbon diffusion to the 
dislocation cores leads to energy minimization of the overlapping solute 
atoms and the dislocation strain field. This theory was initially postu -
lated for C-containing ferritic steels, which was later extended to the 
FCC superalloys. However, an interstitial solute atom or substitutional 
solute atom is responsible for the dislocation pinning is still a topic of 
immense research interest [46] . It was stated that the pinned dislocation 
requires more stress to move from the Cottrell atmosphere and once free 
can glide at relatively low stresses. This phenomenon depends on both 
temperature and strain rate. Furthermore, recent works of Cui et al. [47]
discuss the effects of decreasing stacking fault energy on the PLC effect 
in superalloys and stated that decreased SFE introduces several stacking 
faults and/or twins in the specimens. Literature suggest an inverse 
dependence of SFE on the increasing temperature [48,49] . This leads to 
the onset of DSA phenomena in the alloys within a certain temperature 
regime. Furthermore, the type of serrated flow behavior (Type A, B, C or 
D) also changes based on the faults/twins in the initial microstructure of 
the tensile test specimen. Jena et al. [50] studied the serrated flow 
behavior in Udimet 720Li at 350
◦
C and 450
◦
C, where Cr segregation 
was observed to be responsible for the serrated flow at 350
◦
C. Whereas, 
serrated flow behavior at 450
◦
C was mainly due to segregation of both 
Cr and Co at the dislocation core. The authors expect that this 
solute-defect interaction is responsible for this serrated flow behavior at 
450
◦
C, though a detailed analysis to evaluate the solute atom (inter -
stitial or substitutional) and the defect (faults and/or dislocations) is 
beyond the scope of the present study.

its effect on the high-temperature mechanical properties was assessed. 
The microstructural stability was assessed at an intermediate tempera -
ture range varying from 300
◦
