1. Introduction 
With the rapid advancement of industrialization, traditional mate -
rials have been difficult to adapt to the harsh environment such as high 
temperature, high pressure, and multiple stress [ 1 ]. Because of titanium 
alloys have high damage tolerance, high strength, high toughness, low 
density, excellent corrosion resistance, high temperature resistance and 
biocompatibility, it has gained widespread application in the aerospace, 
shipbuilding, medical, and military industries [ 2 – 5 ]. The common 
two-phase titanium alloys are mainly characterized by four types of 
structures: equiaxed structure, basket-weave structure, duplex structure, 
and Widmannstatten structure [ 6 – 9 ]. The titanium alloy with 
basket-weave structure exhibits excellent mechanical properties at room 
and high temperature. However, some challenges remain in the per -
formance of titanium alloy under high temperature. For example, the 
aggregation and growth of microstructure causes influence on perfor -
mance under the high temperature [ 1 ]. 
The Northwest Institute of Nonferrous Metals Research of China has 
developed new titanium alloys with high damage resistance, excellent 
high-temperature performance and corrosion protection. TC21 has been 
successfully employed in the aerospace industry for the fabrication of 
critical components, including engine casings and landing gear/wing 
joints, with the nominal composition of 
Ti – 6Al – 2Sn – 2Zr – 3Mo – 1Cr – 2Nb – 0.1Si. By adding a small amount of Al, 
Zr, Sn, Mo, Cr, Nb and Si to Ti, the comprehensive performance can be 
significantly improved [ 10 ]. 
Additionally, TC21 as a typical α + β titanium alloy, mainly composed 
of α phase with hexagonal close-packed (HCP) structure and β phase 
with body centered-cubic (BCC) structure. With the adjustment of heat 
treatment temperature, the structure will change greatly. Once the

The mechanical properties of α + β titanium alloys are highly sensi -
tive to even slight variations in microstructure, hard to get an exact 
correlation [ 11 ]. By improving the heat treatment method, the compo -
sitional distribution can be altered to improve the properties [ 12 ]. It is 
widely recognized that fine grain strengthening could achieve these 
properties by hindering dislocations [ 13 – 16 ]. Therefore, it is crucial to 
examine the influence of heat treatments on the microstructures and 
properties of the titanium alloy TC21. 
Impact and tensile properties are important indicators for charac -
terizing damage tolerant alloy materials. TC21 alloy usually contains 
equiaxed, spherical primary and lamellar α -phase within β -matrix. Of 
these, the equiaxed and lamellar α -phases exhibit relatively high 
strength and ductility [ 17 , 18 ]. For TC21 alloy, the basket-weave struc -
ture with the long and thin lamellar α phase inside the β -grains inter -
laced and woven into the basket-weave, has excellent comprehensive 
performance, such as creep properties, toughness and plasticity [ 19 ]. 
The toughness and tensile properties of the basket-weave structure are 
often influenced by several key characteristic variables, such as the β 
grain size, or thickness, volume fraction, morphology and aspect ratio of 
α -phase [ 20 , 21 ]. However, there are a limited number of studies 
exploring the correlation between the microstructure, the high tem -
perature tensile and the impact toughness of basket-weave structure 
TC21. 
Therefore, in this study, TC21 alloy was subjected to two solution 
treatments, followed by aging treatment to obtain basket-weave struc -
ture with different microstructure characteristics. In order to optimize 
the properties of the basket weave structure, the relationship among the 
microstructure such as the β grain size and α -phase thickness, impact 
properties, room-temperature and high-temperature tensile properties 
of TC21 titanium alloy was investigated. This study will provide an 
important guideline for controlling the heat treatment of TC21 titanium 
alloy, as well as for studying the mechanical properties corresponding to 
different morphologies, in order to obtain titanium alloys with excellent 
tensile properties.

3. Results and discussion 
The OM and TEM microstructures of the TC21 alloy under different 
heat treatment conditions are shown in Fig. 4 . The initial state of the 
material is the basket-weave structure. From Fig. 4 (a), it ’ s obvious that 
the alloy maintains the basket-weave structure after subsolvus solution 
treatment. But the α phase distribution of supersolvus solution shows 
difference under the optical microscope in Fig. 4 (b), the α phase within 
the grains is relatively fragmented, even some α phase similar to the 
Widmannstatten structure have grown from the grain boundaries to the 
interior of grains in parallel. Meanwhile, some coarse α phase distrib -
uting at β grain boundaries and within grains can be observed after 
supersolvus treatment. The β grain size of TC21 after subsolvus treat -
ment is about 150 μ m, while that of supersolvus treatment is about 400 
μ m. And it was observed that the triangular grain boundaries were very 
straight after supersolvus treatment. This implies that solid solution 
treatment above the phase transition temperature results in significant 
growth of the β grains. By comparing the TEM images, the average 
thickness of lamellar α phase in the grains after subsolvus treatment is 
about 0.8 μ m, while the lamellar thickness of α phase after supersolvus 
treatment is even thinner, mainly around 0.4 μ m. In summary, after 
treatment at supersolvus treatment, the β grain size reaches 2 – 3 times 
that of subsolvus treatment, while the thickness of the lamellar α -phase 
is only half. Furthermore, in Fig. 4 (c), the α -phase obtained from the 
subsolvus treatment is clearly visible woven into a staggered mesh, 
whereas in Fig. 4 (d) the supersolvus treatment elongated α -phase is not 
visibly woven and there are many fragmented and short α -phase. 
The XRD patterns of TC21 alloy after subsolvus and supersolvus

Fig. 7 shows the room temperature tensile test result of TC21 alloy 
after solution + aging treatments. At room temperature, the tensile 
strength of the TC21 sample treated at subsolvus treatment reached as 
high as 1070 MPa, the 0.2 % yield strength also reached 930 MPa, and

was no plastic strain, which presents obvious brittle fracture feature 
[ 27 ]. 
According to Fig. 4 (b), it ’ s clear that Widmannstatten structure 
grown from the grain boundary into the grain in the supersolvus sample. 
The Widmannstatten structure has reported to cause poor plasticity, and 
the growth of Widmannstatten structure at the grain boundaries could 
lead to along-granular fracture and reduced plasticity [ 30 ]. In addition, 
for the supersolvus alloy, incomplete basket-weave structure, the severe 
coarsening of β grains and some coarse α -phase in the grain boundary, 
also resulting in a significant decrease in plasticity and toughness at 
room temperature.

high-energy interfaces, with severe microstructure deformation and 
high energy consumption [ 28 , 29 ]. Obviously, compared to supersolvus 
treatment, the density of dimples for TC21 after subsolvus treatment 
heat treatment is larger and there are more shear lips and tear edges.

and it is not difficult to judge from the fracture that the supersolvus 
sample exhibits cleavage fracture and intergranular fracture at room

it is well known that fine grain strengthening achieves higher properties 
by inhibiting dislocation propagation [ 36 ]. When analyzed in the 
context of Fig. 4 , the β -grains obtained by heat treatment of supersolvus 
are coarse and difficult to inhibit dislocation slip effectively, while the 
α -strengthened phase is fine and short and does not weave into a mesh as

brittle fracture. β -phase and basket or layer α can be considered as 
strength control units according to the Hall-Petch formula [ 32 – 34 ].

In conjunction with studies done by others, Guo et al. [ 2 ] discovered 
that as β grain size of TC4 rose, its plasticity decreased noticeably. Nii -
nomi et al. [ 39 ] found that β -grain size affects the plasticity of the alloy, 
but has a lesser effect on the strength. In this study, strength, plasticity 
and impact toughness all decrease as the β grain size raise and the 
thickness of lamellar α phase decreases. Overall, the basket structure 
with larger lamellar α phase in finer β grains has better room tempera -
ture mechanical properties. 
The basket-weave α phase near the β grain boundary increases the 
total grain boundary area and reduces dislocation accumulation at the 
grain boundary, which helps to reduce stress concentration, allowing the 
material to withstand higher stresses and reduce intergranular brittle 
fracture [ 40 , 41 ]. Moreover, the basket-weave structure of the α phase 
can cause a longer crack tortuosity path, which further increases the 
plasticity and impact toughness of the alloy.

4. Conclusions 
In this study, two different microstructures of TC21 titanium alloy 
were obtained after two different heat treatments and the effects of 
microstructure on its tensile strength, impact properties, and hardness 
were investigated. The main conclusions are drawn as follows: 
1. After subsolvus solution treatment, a clear basket-weave structure 
was obtained since the lamellar α phase was clearly intertwined and 
woven into a mesh. While the α phase of the supersolvus sample was 
shorter and thinner, and did not form an obvious basket-weave 
structure, even some Widmannstatten α structure and coarse 
α -phase in the grain boundary were observed at the grain bound -
aries. In addition, the β grain size of the subsolvus sample was ~150 
μ m, the thickness of the α lamella was about 0.8 μ m, while the β grain 
size of the supersolvus sample was significantly coarsened to ~400 
μ m, and the thickness of the α lamella was around 0.4 μ m. 
2. At room temperature, both samples of subsolvus and supersolvus had 
high hardness ( > 500HV). Meanwhile, subsolvus sample had higher 
strength toughnessand plasticity. Its tensile strength reached as high 
as 1070 MPa with the elongation of 8 %. While superbsolvus sample 
exhibited brittle fracture without yielding phenomenon. These two 
samples showed different tensile fracture morphology, subsolvus 
sample had many dimples, shear lips, and tearing edges, with long 
cracks exhibiting a transgranular fracture morphology. The fracture 
surface of the supersolvus samples showed a brittle fracture 
composed of both intergranular and cleavage fractures. Therefore, 
subsolvus sample with the basket structure in finer β grains had 
better overall room temperature performance than supersolvus 
samples. 
3. Compared to room temperature tensile properties, for both subsolvus 
and supersolvus samples, at 400 
◦
C tensile, there were almost no 
decrease in tensile strength which still maintained about 1000 MPa, 
and plasticity was significantly improved, suggesting that the 400 
◦
C 
high temperature tensile properties and stability of TC21 alloy after 
both heat treatments were excellent. 
4. With the tensile temperature increasing to 600 
◦
C, the alloys after 
both heat treatments appeared obvious softening, the tensile 
strength significantly decreased to about 500 MPa as elongation 
increased to ~60 %. The competition of dynamic recovery and the 
work hardening resulted in tensile curve showing an M-shape. The 
fracture of high temperature tensile had a lot of dimples and dislo -
cations. The deformation of α phase was severe, subsolvus samples 
exhibited wave shaped and Z-shaped α phase, and supersolvus 
samples showed thinner and shorter α phase fragment.
