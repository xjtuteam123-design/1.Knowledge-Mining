ABSTRACT
The influence of grain boundary engineering (GBE) on the mechanical properties of FeCoCrNi high-entropy alloy 
(HEA) at different temperatures was systematically examined. An optimal GBE process, namely cold rolling (with 
5 % reduction) followed by annealing (at 900 
◦
C for 4 h), was ascertained to markedly modify the grain boundary 
character distribution (GBCD) in the alloy, and significantly increase the fraction of special boundaries ( Σ 3- Σ 29) 
to as high as 82.4 %, and meanwhile, the connectivity of random high-angle grain boundaries (RHAGBs) has 
been effectively disrupted. Such a modified GBCD leads to an improvement in room-temperature tensile ductility 
without loss of strength, but to a simultaneous enhancement in strength and ductility at high temperatures 
(600 
◦
C – 800 
◦
C). The improved properties result mainly from the inducement of abundant Σ 3 boundaries that 
effectively inhibit intergranular crack initiation and propagation during plastic deformation. Also, the GBE 
process optimizes deformation uniformity, mitigates dynamic recovery and recrystallization and suppresses 
dynamic strain aging at high temperatures, further facilitating more stable and homogeneous plastic deforma -
tion. This study has offered a detailed perspective on how GBE affects the plastic deformation and damage 
behavior of FeCoCrNi HEA at different temperatures, thus providing a novel pathway to improve the mechanical 
properties of HEA especially at high temperatures.
1. Introduction
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) have garnered significant attention due 
to their novel alloy design strategy and exceptional mechanical prop -
erties [ 1 – 3 ]. Unlike traditional alloys comprising primarily a single 
principal element, HEAs are typically composed of multiple principal 
elements in equiatomic or near-equiatomic ratio. Owing to the high 
entropy effect, these alloys tend to form a single-phase solid solution. 
Recently, several comprehensive reviews [ 3 , 4 ] have summarized the 
advancements in mechanical behavior of HEAs, further confirming their 
potential for structural applications across a wide temperature range. In 
these HEAs, the mechanical property of the single-phase face-centered 
cubic (FCC) FeCoCrNi HEA exhibits a unique temperature dependence. 
Specifically, the mechanical properties become significantly improved 
as the deformation temperature decreases, particularly below room 
temperature [ 5 – 7 ].
However, at mid-to-high temperatures, the mechanical properties of 
FeCoCrNi HEA exhibit a significant degradation with increasing tem -
perature [ 8 , 9 ]. For examples, Yang et al. [ 9 ] investigated the tensile 
properties of FeCoCrNi alloys in the temperature range of 673 K – 1073 K 
and observed a consistent decline in both strength and ductility with 
increasing temperature. Similar trends have also been reported in other 
FCC-structured HEAs, such as CoCrFeMnNi and CoCrFeNi, where a 
ductility loss with increasing temperature has been consistently 
observed, underscoring the pervasive nature of this issue in HEAs [ 3 ]. 
Such high-temperature induced performance deteriorations pose a 
notable challenge to the practical application of FeCoCrNi-based HEAs. 
Although the underlying mechanisms driving the deteriorations are 
multifaceted, grain boundary behavior plays a rather critical role, as 
elevated temperatures exacerbate grain boundary sliding and increase 
the likelihood of intergranular cracking [ 10 ]. In addition, 
FeCoCrNi-based HEAs are prone to dynamic recovery (DRV) and

the alloys. [ 8 , 11 ]. The reason for the reduction in strength and ductility 
is primarily attributed to the decrease in dislocation density caused by 
DRV and DRX [ 12 ]. Moreover, dynamic strain aging (DSA) or 
Portevin-Le Chatelier (PLC) effect at intermediate-to-high temperatures 
has been reported to occur in HEAs during tensile plastic deformation, 
which is characterized by distinct serrated flow in stress-strain curves [ 8 ,
9 ]. The occurrence of PLC effect not only suppresses plasticity but also 
negatively impacts the thermal stability, posing additional challenges 
for maintaining mechanical performance in high-temperature environ -
ments [ 13 – 15 ]. The degradations in strength and ductility must 
threatens the practical applications of these HEAs, and therefore, 
addressing these issues is crucial for enhancing their service life.
Nowadays, investigations on the improvement in the high- 
temperature mechanical properties of FeCoCrNi-based HEAs still 
remain relatively limited. Available studies suggested that the high- 
temperature strength of HEAs can be enhanced by adding refractory 
elements such as Nb through solid solution strengthening and precipi -
tation hardening [ 16 , 17 ]. However, the relevant 
precipitation-hardening strategy is often accompanied by the formation 
of precipitates at grain boundaries (GBs), where the induced stress 
concentrations during plastic deformation can lead to premature inter -
granular cracking [ 18 – 20 ]. Furthermore, it is worth noting that the 
existing studies have primarily focused on the effects of elements and 
second phases on alloy properties, but often overlooked the interactions 
between GBs and dislocations during plastic deformation. Actually, the 
character of GBs can significantly influence dislocation slip behavior, 
which in turn affects alloy properties. Therefore, optimizing the grain 
boundary character distribution (GBCD), also known as grain boundary 
engineering (GBE), should be an effectual approach to improving the 
mechanical performances of HAEs, especially at high temperatures.
GBE has emerged as an effective strategy for improving the me -
chanical performance of alloys by optimizing the GBCD. By increasing 
the fraction of low-energy boundaries, such as Σ 3 boundaries, while 
reducing the fraction of random high-angle grain boundaries (RHAGBs), 
GBE may enhance grain boundary stability and mitigate issues such as 
grain boundary sliding and intergranular cracking at elevated temper -
atures. These optimizations have proven effective in improving both 
room temperature and high-temperature properties of traditional alloy 
systems like austenitic stainless steels and Ni-based superalloys by 
reducing intergranular failure [ 21 , 22 ]. Additionally, in certain 
high-Cr-bearing multicomponent alloys, refined strategies to control 
grain boundary precipitates have also been shown to improve ductility 
and thermal stability [ 23 ]. This further highlights the critical role of GB 
design in mitigating high-temperature embrittlement. GBE is particu -
larly valuable for mitigating the detrimental effects of DRV and DRX, 
thereby enhancing the hardening ability of alloys at elevated tempera -
tures [ 24 , 25 ]. Although GBE has been successfully applied in various 
conventional alloy systems, its application in HEAs, particularly in the 
FeCoCrNi system, is still rarely reported.
In the present work, we investigate the feasibility and effectiveness 
of GBE in the FeCoCrNi HEA, focusing on the scientific issue whether 
GBE can improve the tensile properties of FeCoCrNi at both room and 
high temperatures. By optimizing the GBCD, we seek to enhance the 
strength and ductility across a range of temperatures, thereby broad -
ening the potential applications of FeCoCrNi HEA.

3. Results
3.1. Microstructure of BM sample
Fig. 1 a shows the XRD results of the BM sample, indicating that it has 
a single-phase FCC structure. Fig. 1 b-d illustrates the grain orientation, 
GBCD, and the connectivity of the RHAGB network of the BM sample. 
The microstructure of the BM sample consists predominantly of equi -
axed and randomly oriented grains, with an average grain size 
(including twins) of approximately 8.7 ± 0.1 μ m, as shown in Fig. 1 b. 
Due to the low stacking fault energy (SFE) of FeCoCrNi HEA, a consid -
erable number of annealing twins (ATs) are observed in the micro -
structure, as shown in Fig. 1 c. However, most of ATs are relatively small, 
terminating within a single grain, with only a few spanning across entire 
grains. Consequently, the SBs do not significantly interrupt the network 
connectivity of RHAGBs, as demonstrated in Fig. 1 d. In this context, the 
fraction of SBs is relatively low, approximately 43.2 ± 1.2 %.
3.2. Microstructures of FeCoCrNi HEA under different cold rolling 
reductions
Fig. 2 shows the microstructures of the FeCoCrNi HEA cold rolled 
with the reductions of 3 %, 5 %, 7 %, and 10 %. It is apparent that the 
deformation microstructures of the cold-rolled FeCoCrNi are predomi -
nantly featured by planar slip dislocation structures, encompassing 
stacking faults (SFs), planar slip bands and deformation twins (DTs). 
Additionally, Fig. 2 clearly demonstrates a gradual shift in the micro -
structural characteristics from planar slip structures towards the 
appearance of DTs. At the reduction of 3 %, the microstructure reveals 
the presence of SFs and initial characteristics of planar slip bands, as 
illustrated in Fig. 2 (a, a ’ ). As the reduction is increased to 5 %, both the 
visibility and the number of planar slip bands and SFs become more 
pronounced ( Fig. 2 b – b ’ ). Upon reaching 7 % reduction, the morpho -
logical features of planar slip bands and SFs are further accentuated 
( Fig. 2 c – c ’ ). At the reduction of 10 %, while planar slip bands are 
exceptionally distinct, there is a noticeable reduction in the number of 
SFs observed, alongside the emergence of significant DTs ( Fig. 2 d – d ’ ). 
Moreover, as cold rolling deformation progresses, an increase in dislo -
cation density is noted, coupled with a denser arrangement of planar slip 
bands. At elevated levels of deformation, planar slip bands manifesting 
in various directions are observed simultaneously. The microstructural 
patterns exhibited are highly similar to those observed in some low SFE 
FCC alloys, which can often be optimized in terms of GBCD through 
thermo-mechanical processing (TMP) [ 29 ].

After different amounts of cold rolling deformation, the residual 
stress generated is stored within the alloy. During annealing at 900 
◦
C, 
the strain energy combined with the energy brought by high tempera -
ture drives the migration of RHAGBs, leading to the rearrangement of 
atoms in local areas. When RHAGBs encounter SFs or planar slip bands 
during migration, the rearranging atoms absorb a large number of SFs or 
dislocations in planar slip bands, triggering a transition from the normal 
sequence of closely packed atomic planes ( … ABC … ) to the inverse 
sequence ( … CBA … ), thereby inducing the formation of ATs. Simulta -
neously, the abundance of SFs and planar slip bands in the deformed 
microstructure, along with the high lattice distortion characteristic of 
HEAs, can provide additional activation energy for the formation of ATs. 
This also explains why ATs often form at the trailing edge of RHAGBs. 
Consequently, at a deformation level of 3 %, the minimal deformation 
does not generate adequate strain energy to facilitate the migration of 
RHAGBs, leading to insufficient growth of partial TRDs, as depicted in 
Fig. 3 (a,a ’ ,a"). Additionally, the reduced scale of SFs and planar slip 
bands at this deformation magnitude contributes to a decreased for -
mation of ATs, which ultimately detracts from the effectiveness of GBCD 
optimization. With increasing cold rolling reduction (at 5 % and 7 %), 
the proliferation of SFs and planar slip bands, accompanied by more 
strain energy driving grain boundary migration, result in a higher 
probability of forming more ATs, thereby enhancing the effectiveness of 
GBCD optimization. At a reduction of 10 %, however, when RHAGBs 
encounter more structurally stable DTs during migration, the migration 
will be hindered, thereby inhibiting the growth of TRDs and suppressing 
the formation of ATs.
Additionally, different cold rolling reductions result in different 
recrystallization nucleation rates. With the increase in deformation 
amount, the nucleation rate during annealing also continuously in -
creases. Therefore, at 3 % reduction, some TRDs cannot be fully evolved, 
while at 7 % and 10 % reductions, the extensive recrystallization limits 
the space for TRD evolution and the TRDs compete with each other, 
leading to a decrease in the GBCD optimization parameters. Therefore, 
5 % cold rolling reduction is the optimal prior strain for the GBCD 
optimization of the current FeCoCrNi alloy.

4.3. Influence of GBCD optimization on tensile properties
As mentioned earlier, the GBE sample exhibit an enhanced ductility 
compared to the Non-GBE sample at different temperatures, and the 
tensile strength shows a progressive enhancement with increasing 
temperature, which is closely related to its improved strain-hardening 
ability as reflected in the strain-hardening rate curves ( Fig. S2 ). To 
more intuitively illustrate the improvement in plastic deformation due 
to the GBE optimization, the comparisons of KAM value distribution 
versus the relative frequency are provided in Fig. 12 a. Obviously, the 
peak values for the GBE samples are lower than those for the Non-GBE 
samples and are shifted more towards the lower right side of the 
figure, which is attributed to the more uniform deformation occurring in 
the GBE samples.
The optimization of GBCD primarily improves the mechanical 
properties of the alloy in the following ways. Firstly, after GBE optimi -
zation, a higher fraction of SBs effectively improves the uniformity of 
strain distribution during deformation. This is mainly because, as 
demonstrated in Fig. 9 (b ’ ,d ’ ) and 10 (b ’ ,d ’ ), SBs (primarily Σ 3 AT 
boundaries) can obstruct some dislocation slips while allowing others to 
pass through [ 12 , 39 , 40 ]. Consequently, the dislocation pile-up at these 
SBs is less severe compared to that at RHAGBs. Fig. 12 b-d show 
high-resolution TEM (HRTEM) images and the corresponding fast 
Fourier transform (FFT) patterns of the local region at the AT boundaries 
in the GBE sample after tension at 700 
◦
C. The periodicity in atomic 
intensity observed in the HRTEM images and the diffraction spots in the 
FFT pattern along the [011] zone axis indicate the presence of a twin 
structure. The HRTEM image in Fig. 12 b allows for geometric phase 
analysis (GPA) to study the microstrain in the region [ 41 ], and the 
relevant Fig. 12 e clearly shows dislocations crossing the twin boundary. 
Some previous studies [ 42 , 43 ] have also demonstrated that mobile 
dislocations can cross low-energy CSL GBs with a greater symmetry.
Secondly, the optimization of GBCD impacts the dislocation slip 
behavior during plastic deformation. A high fraction of SBs facilitates 
the continuous multiplication of dislocations, as confirmed by the fact 
that the GBE samples maintain a certain level of strain hardening 
capability even in the later stage of tensile deformation ( Fig. 8 ). In 
contrast, in the Non-GBE samples, a substantial accumulation of dislo -
cations at RHAGBs severely restricts dislocation multiplication, leading 
to a more pronounced and premature decrease in the strain hardening 
rate.

◦

noticeable serration flow phenomena. These serrations are one of the 
characteristic features of the PLC effect. The unstable plastic flow leads 
to uneven strain localization, which is normally undesirable in industrial 
processes [ 44 – 46 ]. In general, these serrations are classified into three 
types based on their shape characteristics. Type A serrations are periodic 
with large, sparse intervals and rapid stress drops, Type B are fine, 
high-frequency fluctuations around the normal curve, and Type C occurs 
below the normal stress-strain curve [ 47 ]. At 600

Type A serrations. As the strain increases, there is a trend towards Type B

Non-GBE sample. As the temperature increases to 700 
◦

plastic deformation continues, the serrations of the Non-GBE sample

GBE effectively suppresses the serrated behavior ( Fig. 7 c). The PLC ef -

This suppression of the PLC effect through GBCD optimization can be 
attributed to several key factors. The increased proportion of low-energy 
boundaries, such as Σ 3 and other CSL boundaries, greatly enhances the 
uniformity of plastic deformation [ 22 ], and meanwhile, GBE-optimized 
grain boundaries reduce obstacles to dislocation slip by decreasing the 
density of RHAGBs. This reduction in dislocation impediments would 
weaken the pinning of dislocations by solute atoms during plastic flow, 
as fewer dislocations become trapped at obstacles, thereby mitigating 
DSA, the primary driver of the PLC effect [ 47 , 52 , 53 ]. By facilitating 
smoother dislocation motion and reducing strain localization, GBE 
suppresses the PLC effect and further contributes to the improved 
high-temperature ductility and deformation stability.
5. Conclusions
The effect of GBE on the mechanical properties of FeCoCrNi HEA has 
been systematically investigated, focusing on optimizing the GBCD in 
the alloy to enhance the performance at room and high temperatures. 
The main conclusions are summarized as follows. 
(1) Thermo-mechanical process applied to the FeCoCrNi HEA can 
effectively optimize its GBCD. Specifically, after 5 % cold rolling 
and subsequent annealing at 900 
◦
C for 4 h, the f
SBs 
can be sur -
prisingly raised to as high as 82.4 % ± 0.7 %, and such a high f
SBs 
disrupts effectively the connectivity of RHAGB networks.
(2) The interactions of RHAGBs with the cold-rolling induced 
stacking faults and planar slip bands bring about the formation 
and growth of ATs during annealing. ATs themselves are also 
interacted with planar slip bands during annealing, further 
facilitating their growth and extending TRDs across grains. 
However, excessive cold rolling deformation or prolonged 
annealing duration lead to the formation of stable DTs or over- 
recrystallization to hinder the growth of TRDs, ultimately 
reducing the effectiveness of GBCD optimization.
(3) The optimized GBCD significantly enhanced the mechanical 
properties of the alloy. At room temperature, the GBE sample 
exhibits an improved ductility while maintaining tensile strength. 
At high temperatures (600 
◦
C – 800 
◦
C), the GBE samples 
demonstrate a simultaneous enhancement in strength and 
ductility.
(4) At room temperature, the improvements in mechanical proper -
ties are primarily attributed to a high fraction of SBs induced by 
GBE treatment, which greatly suppress intergranular crack initi -
ation and propagation by effectively interrupting RHAGB con -
nectivity, and enable more uniform strain distribution during 
deformation. At high temperature, in addition to suppressing 
crack initiation and propagation and promoting coordinated 
deformation, GBE also mitigates DRV and DRX behavior to sta -
bilize the microstructure, and suppresses the PLC effects during 
plastic flow, thus promoting more stable and homogeneous 
plastic deformation.
