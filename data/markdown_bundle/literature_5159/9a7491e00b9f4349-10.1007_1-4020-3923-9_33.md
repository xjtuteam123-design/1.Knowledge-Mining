Improving the Mechanical Properties of Ti-6Al-4V Alloy by 
Equal Channel Angular Pressing
L.R. Saitova1, I.P. Semenova*1, G.I. Raab1, R.Z. Valiev1,
T.C. Lowe2, Y.T. Zhu2
1Ufa State Aviation Technical University, Ufa, Russia 
2Los Alamos National Laboratory, Los Alamos, NM 87545, USA 
Abstract 
In this study, we attempted to enhance the strength of the two-phase Ti-6Al-4V alloy 
using severe plastic deformation (SPD) and thermo-mechanical treatment. It was 
established that structure refinement by ECAP significantly improved the strength of 
this alloy. The variation of the initial structure before ECAP was found to 
significantly affect the microstructures and mechanical properties after ECAP. 
Lower ECAP temperature resulted in finer microstructure, higher density of 
crystalline lattice defects, and consequently higher strength. Up to 40% of 
improvement in strength has been obtained in ECAP-processed Ti-6Al-4V alloy, 
compared with its annealed state. 
Introduction 
The Ti-6Al-4V alloy is widely used in various industries as a structural material due 
to its high specific strength and excellent corrosion resistance. Its strength can be 
enhanced further by conventional thermal and thermo-mechanical treatments. 
However, although capable of improving the strength of Ti-6Al-4V alloy, these 
treatments also usually sacrifice the ductility, which is one of the most important 
properties for structural applications. 
It is well-known that ultrafine-grained (UFG) structures with grain sizes less 
than 1 Pm could significantly improve mechanical properties [1,2]. One of the most 
promising methods to produce UFG structure in metals and alloys is equal-channel 
angular pressing (ECAP), which allows to preserve the same geometrical dimensions 
of billets [1-3]. Processing two-phase Ti-6Al-4V by ECAP is a complicated task. 
First, this alloy has very low deformability at room temperature. To overcome this 
difficulty, we have to do the ECAP processing at elevated temperatures and to use 
large intersection angles of die channels [4-6]. Moreover, the phase components and 
the morphology of metastable phases precipitating during the ECAP have great 
influence on the workability of the alloy. 
This paper presents the results of the study of ECAP and thermo-mechanical 
treatment effect on the UFG microstructure formation in the Ti-6Al-4V alloy and its 
mechanical properties.

It is seen that the contribution of solid-solution strengthening ( Vss), which is 
caused by dissolving all alloying elements and, can be regarded as constant for all 
the states of the alloy. The contribution of grain-boundary strengthening constitutes 
up to 40% of total strength, which is caused by the formation of barrier for 
dislocation motion in the form of new grain and subgrain boundaries during ECAP 
[7,8]. The further extrusion gives additional contribution to the increase of yield 
stress of the alloy by the dislocation strengthening mechanism. 
Conclusion
This study has allowed us to establish that ECAP in combination with thermal and 
thermomechanical treatment contributes to the formation of UFG structure in the Ti-
6Al-4V alloy. Strength was increased up to 1360 MPa while retaining a ductility of 
about 12%. 
The microstructural refinement produced by ECAP is the main cause for the 
improved strength. The subsequent thermomechanical treatment gives additional 
contribution to the alloy strength due to dislocation density increase and further 
microstructural refinement. 
It was shown that one more way to enhance strength and ductility of the 
deformed billets was the prior thermal treatment to form fine-lamellar microstructure 
before ECAP.
