Abstract 
An equiatomic refractory high-entropy alloy (RHEA) NbMoTaWRe is prepared by mechanical alloying (MA) and spark 
plasma sintering (SPS). The effects of mechanical alloying and sintering behaviors on the microstructure and properties 
of the RHEA are investigated. After ball-milling for 30 h, the metastable and supersaturated MA powders with the body-
centered cubic (BCC) structure are obtained. Then, the MA powders are sintered using the SPS method under the sintering 
temperature range of 1700–1900 °C, and the C atoms and WC introduced by the MA process reacts with the metastable and 
supersaturated Ta/Nb phase of the MA powers to form the face-centered cubic (FCC) structure (Nb, Ta)C particles along 
the BCC matrix boundaries during the SPS process. The NbMoTaWRe alloy sintered at 1800 °C consisted of BCC matrix 
and FCC-type (Nb, Ta)C particles has high compactness (porosity fraction is 0.32%), fracture strength (2630 MPa), plastic 
strain (6.82%), and hardness (992 ± 20 HV). These excellent properties of this RHEA are mainly attributed to the combina-
tion of multi-effects, including sintering densification, grain refinement strengthening from the refined sizes (3.80 μm) BCC 
matrix, precipitation strengthening from the (Nb, Ta)C particles, solid solution strengthening from multi-principal elements 
and interstitial solid solution strengthening from C atoms dissolving into BCC matrix.
Keywords Refractory high-entropy alloy · Spark plasma sintering · Microstructure · Mechanical properties
1 Introduction
Refractory high entropy alloy (RHEA) containing high 
melting point metals (such as W, Mo, Nb, Ta etc.) shows 
excellent thermal stability and high mechanical properties 
at elevated temperature [1, 2], which has a good application 
prospect under the working conditions above 1200 °C. For 
example, the NbMoTaW and NbMoTaWV show the high 
yield strength at elevated temperature; their compressive 
strength at 1600 °C is 405 MPa and 477 MPa, respectively 
[3]. However, the NbMoTaW and NbMoTaWV alloys exhibit 
room temperature brittleness with plastic strains of approxi-
mately 2.0% and 1.5%, respectively, which limits their fur -
ther application.
Appropriate alloying design is an important method for 
improving the mechanical properties of metals. The element 
with high melting temperature (including Cr [4 , 5], Ti [6 , 
7], Zr [8 ],etc.) is often added into or used to replace the 
component of the NbMoTaW alloy system to suppress brit-
tleness and improve the mechanical properties. For exam-
ple, by adding Ti into NbMoTaW RHEA, the ductility and 
yield strength of the TiNbMoTaW alloy increase from 1.9 to 
11.5% and from 996 to 1455 MPa, respectively [7]. Besides 
the above-mentioned refractory metal elements, the element 
Re with the second highest melting point (approximately 
3180 °C) is also an important element of the refractory 
alloy family. Due to the Re ductilizing effect, element Re 
is added into tungsten refractory alloys to improve the duc-
tility and plasticity, and it can reduce the ductile-to-brittle

SPS processes. Our work will provide a good reference for 
understanding the mechanical alloying behavior and sinter-
ing behavior of the microstructure and properties of NbMo-
TaWRe RHEA. In addition, it also provides a fine-grained 
NbMoTaWRe RHEA with high strength and good ductility.
2 Experimental
The equiatomic NbMoTaWRe RHEA bulk samples were 
synthesized by using MA and SPS processes as shown in 
Fig. 1. The NbMoTaWRe powders were prepared from pure 
Mo, Nb, Ta, W, Re element powders (purity approximately 
99.5%) using a high-energy ball mill machine (QM-3SP4). 
In order to prevent powder oxidation, the whole process was 
carried out under high purity argon. The tungsten carbide 
vials and tungsten carbide balls were used as grinding media 
during ball milling. The ball-to-powder weight ratio was 
20:1, and the milling speed was 400 r·min−1. The mechani-
cally alloyed powders were prepared into bulk samples with 
a size of φ10 × 10 mm by using the SPS-211HT sintering 
system under a high purity argon atmosphere. The sinter -
ing conditions were sintering temperature of 1700–1900 °C, 
holding time of 20 min, and compaction pressure of 50 MPa, 
respectively.
The phase compositions and crystal structures of the sam-
ples were analyzed by an X-ray diffractometer (XRD, Bruker 
D8). The morphology of the mechanically alloyed powders 
and the microstructure of the SPS samples was observed 
by scanning electron microscope (SEM, JSM-7900F) with 
an electron back-scattering diffraction (EBSD). The phase 
crystal structures and microstructure of the SPS samples 
were further analyzed by transmission electron microscopy 
(TEM, Talos F200XG2) with an energy dispersive spec-
trometer (EDS). The hardness of the SPS bulk samples was 
measured by using a Vickers hardness tester (TMHVS-1000) 
under a load of 1000 gf. Room temperature compression 
tests were carried out using a WDW-100 universal testing 
machine with φ3 × 6 mm cylindrical samples at a strain rate 
of 103·s1. The nanoindentation tests were used to analyze the 
nano-hardness of the SPS samples. The indentation measure-
ments were carried out according to the procedure that the 
Berkovich indenter was loaded at a rate of 40.00 mN·min−1, 
followed by 10 s of holding time at the maximum load of 
20.00 mN, and then unloaded at a rate of 40.00 mN·min −1.

3 Results and Discussion
3.1 Characterization of the Mechanical Alloying 
Powders
Figure  2 shows the XRD patterns and the morphologies 
of the MA powders. From Fig.  2a, it is clear that the dif-
fraction peaks of each elemental metal gradually decrease, 
and the peak intensity of the BCC solid solution phase 
increases with increasing ball-milling time. In addition, 
the diffraction peaks of WC caused by exfoliated media are 
observed. After ball milling for 10 h, as shown in Fig.  2a, 
the peak intensity of Nb element decreases, and some dif -
fraction peaks tend to weaken and widen, indicating plastic 
deformation, element diffusion and solid-state phase trans-
formation among the powders. After ball-milling for 30 h, 
the diffraction peaks of the elemental metals were replaced 
by the diffraction peaks of a single BCC structure, indi-
cating that the mechanical alloying was completed. From 
the variation of diffraction peaks at different ball-milling 
times, it can be predicted that the alloying order of the 
powders is Mo → Nb → Ta → W → Re. Previous work has 
shown that the elements with low melting points have a 
faster dissolution rate than high melting point elements, 
and those with similar structure could be easier to diffuse 
and form solid solutions with each other [23]. Therefore, 
in this study, Nb and Mo elements have higher alloying 
rates due to they have lower melting point. In contrast, the 
Re element has the lowest alloying rates, given that it has 
the second-highest melting point and the hexagonal close-
packed (HCP) crystal structure which is different from 
the other elements. The morphology of 30 h ball-milling 
powders show that the fine and uniform MA powders is 
obtained (Fig.  2b). The size distribution of the particles is 
mainly distributed between 0.5 and 10 μm, and the average 
size is about 4 μm.

The element distributions of the NbMoTaWRe SPS 
sample have been determined by EPMA map as shown in 
Fig. 4. It shows that the NbMoTaWRe SPS sample consists 
of the BCC matrix phase (bright regions shown by blue 
arrow) and FCC particle phase (dark regions shown by red 
arrow). In addition, it also shows that the BCC matrix phase 
is composed of almost equal atomic ratios of W, Mo Re 
and small amount of Nb and Ta elements. In contrast the 
FCC precipitation phase (dark regions) is enriched with 
Nb and Ta elements. Non-metallic element C is distrib-
uted in both BCC and FCC phases, and the FCC precipi -
tation phase has a much higher C content than the matrix 
(BCC phase), which indicates that only a small portion of 
C atoms is dissolved in the BCC solid-solution phase, and 
the most of them react with Ta and Nb to form the FCC 
precipitates. Previous reports showed that the C atoms 
introduced by the addition of stearic acid during the ball-
milling process tend to react with metastable mechanical 
alloying RHEA powders to form carbide (FCC type) at high 
sintering temperature [24]. In this NbMoTaWRe RHEA, 
the enthalpies of mixing for C-Nb, C-Ta, C-Mo, C-W, and 
C-Re are −102 kJ·mol −1, −101 kJ·mol −1, −67 kJ·mol −1, 
−60 kJ·mol −1, and −42 kJ·mol −1, respectively [25], indi-
cating that the binding strength between C and Nb or Ta is 
the strongest. Therefore, the metastable Ta and Nb phases 
in the mechanical alloying powders can react with C atoms 
to form carbides [26]. Since Ta and Nb have a similar atom 
radius, electronegativity, and carbide-forming ability, they 
can replace each other’s lattice position in their respective 
carbides, resulting in the formation of (Nb, Ta)C. In addi-
tion, according to the XRD results of the MA powders and 
sintered samples mentioned above in Fig.  3b, the peaks of 
WC in the MA powder disappear after the sintering process, 
indicating that WC particles undergo phase transformation 
during the sintering process. Gibbs free energy of forma -
tion between Nb/Ta and C is significantly lower than that of 
Mo/W and C [27]. Therefore, compared with Mo and W, Ta 
and Nb may react more easily with WC: WC + X = W + XC 
(X = Nb, Ta). A similar reaction had been reported as that 
XC + Ti = TiC + X (X = Mo, Nb, V, Ta) during the prepara-
tion of TiC/RHEA [28].

 Nb5+3d 5/2 spin–orbit peaks of the Nb2O5 phase, while the

Figure 8 displays the bright-field TEM morphology and 
the corresponding selected area electron diffraction (SAED) 
patterns of the sintered NbMoTaWRe alloy. Figure 8a shows 
the bright-field TEM micrograph, indicating that the micro-
structure of NbMoTaWRe bulk consists of the BCC matrix 
and FCC phase. As can be seen from Fig. 8b, some disloca-
tions are observed in the BCC matrix, indicating that the 
activation of plastic deformation occurs in the BCC matrix, 
while there are almost no slip dislocations and plastic defor-
mation in the FCC phase. These results are in accordance 
with the nanoindentation results (shown in Fig.  7a). It is 
considered that relatively easier dislocation movement in the 
BCC matrix provides good ductility and plasticity. In addi-
tion, it is observed that a large number of dislocations accu-
mulate at grain boundaries, suggesting that the FCC phase 
inhibits dislocation slip to improve the strength. Figure  8c 
shows the corresponding EDS maps of component elements 
distribution, implying that the FCC phase is rich in Nb, Ta, 
and C elements, which is in accordance with the EPMA 
results. According to the SAED results shown in Fig. 8d and 
e, the lattice parameters of the BCC phase and FCC phases 
are a1 = 0.3458 nm and a2 = 0.4801 nm, respectively.

The current research work shows that the NbMoTaWRe 
sintered samples consisted of BCC matrix and FCC-type 
(Nb, Ta)C particles exhibit different mechanical properties 
with the changes of sintering temperature. The relationship 
between the microstructure and mechanical properties of 
the NbMoTaWRe sintered alloy should be discussed in 
detail. It is well known that sintering densification has a 
significant effect on the properties of the sintered samples, 
and low sintering densification leads to poor mechanical 
properties of sintered alloys [20, 29]. In our work, the 
NbMoTaWRe bulk sample sintered at 1700 °C with a large 
number of micropores shows low sintering densification, 
leading to the lowest fracture strength. When the com-
pressive load acts on the bulk sample with many resid-
ual micropores, the stress is easy to concentrate in the 
micropore area, which makes the crack easier to initiate 
and propagate [31]. After the sintering temperature rises to 
1800 °C, the micropore ratio decreases rapidly to less than 
0.4%, which indicates that the sample has been completely 
densified. For completely densified samples,the strength-
ening mechanisms, including fine grain strengthening, 
solid solution strengthening, interstitial solid solution 
strengthening, and precipitation strengthening, become 
the main factors on the yield strength of the NbMoTaWRe 
bulk sample sintered at 1800 °C and 1900 °C. Therefore, 
the effects of these strengthening mechanisms on the sam-
ples sintered at 1800 °C and 1900 °C will be discussed in 
detail.

4 Conclusions
In summary, an equiatomic NbMoTaWRe RHEA bulk is 
obtained via a combination of the MA and SPS processes. 
After 30 h ball milling, the supersaturated single BCC solid 
solution with fine particle size (size less than 3 μm) of NbMo-
TaWRe MA powder is obtained. Then NbMoTaWRe RHEA 
bulk sintered at optimal 1800 °C shows the high compact-
ness (porosity fraction is 0.32%), excellent fracture strength 
(2630 MPa), plastic strain (6.82%), and hardness (992 ± 20 
HV). During the SPS process, the C atom and WC introduced 
by the MA process can react with the metastable Ta/Nb phase 
of the MA powers to form FCC-type (Nb, Ta)C particles along 
the BCC matrix boundaries. The sintered NbMoTaWRe sam-
ple reveals excellent mechanical properties mainly due to the 
mixed-phase of the fine size BCC solid solution matrix phase 
and FCC-type (Nb, Ta)C particles. The BCC matrix phase 
has multi-effects to help the excellent mechanical properties 
because of grain refinement strengthening from the small sizes 
BCC matrix phase, solid solution strengthening from multi-
principal elements, and interstitial solid solution strengthening 
from C atoms dissolved in BCC matrix. Moreover, the FCC 
particles have ultra-high hardness and exhibit a precipitation 
strengthening effect to contribute high strength and hardness. 
The excellent properties of this NbMoTaWRe RHEA make it 
to be potential structural material for future application.
