Ordering Reactions in Ni-AI-Mo-Ta and Ni-AI-Mo-W Superalloys 
J.C. WILLIAMS and P.L. MARTIN 
The ordered structures formed in two experimental nickel base superalloys have been determined using 
selected area electron diffraction. Upon quenching from 1300 °C, the alloys contained ordered 3`' 
precipitates (L12 structure) and the matrix exhibited diffuse intensity at {1~0} positions, indicating the 
presence of short range order. The high refractory metal content of the alloys caused the Dla, DO22, 
and Pt2Mo prototype structures to form in the matrix following aging at 600, 700, and 800 °C. The 
detailed structural effects of the Ta and W quaternary additions are similar to those observed in 
Ni3(Mo, A1), Ni3(Mo, Ta), and Ni3(Mo, W) ternary alloys. The decomposition products observed in 
the quaternary alloys studied can be explained by considering the partitioning of solutes between the 
3" and the matrix. 
I. INTRODUCTION 
THE efficiency of the gas turbine relies heavily on high 
operating temperatures made possible through the use of 
nickel-base superalloys. The dramatic development of the 
turbine has been paralleled by the increasing capability of 
this class of alloys to withstand these high temperatures. The 
metallurgical effects of the high operating temperatures are 
exacerbated by the corrosive environment and the high 
stresses imposed during service. Consequently, the interplay 
between complex microstructures and properties of these 
alloys has been extensively studied. 
The application of the principles of physical metallurgy to 
the development and refinement of nickel-base superalloys 
has led to multicomponent alloy compositions (sometimes 
containing as many as 12 intentional additions) and complex 
multiphase microstructures that utilize all known strength- 
ening mechanisms. For simplicity, however, the micro- 
structural constituents of the superalloys can be broken 
down into three broad groups: (1) the fcc (y) matrix, (2) the 
noncoherent precipitates such as gross intermetallic phases 
or carbides, and (3) the phases forming coherent precipi- 
tates. The maintenance of coherency by the latter group is 
possible since they are usually superlattices of the fcc ma- 
trix. The most common and important of these is the ordered 
3`' phase which is based on the Ni3(A1, Ti) composition and 
has the L12 structure. 
Other coherent phases can be formed in alloys contain- 
ing refractory elements, such as INCONEL* 718, where

IV. DISCUSSION 
The carbides in RSR 185 will be ignored for the purposes 
of analyzing the NixMo ordering reactions. High refractory 
element contents usually promote formation of M6C or M ~2C 
carbides. 2°'21'22 M6C formation could account for -1.2 at. 
pct Mo + W with the nominal 0.2 at. pct carbon addition 
present in RSR 185. This complicates the comparison be- 
tween RSR 185 and RSR 143 but does not negate it. 
In the following discussion, the principles developed 
from the analysis of the phase equilibria in ternary 
Ni3(Mo, X) alloys t°'t~ will be used to explain the appearance 
of the phases observed in the y phase of Ni-A1-Mo-X super- 
alloys. The y matrix composition can be calculated using 
the elemental partitioning data in the literature. 22 If the 
elements in the 7' (and the carbides for RSR 185) are 
subtracted from the overall composition, the remaining 
elements are roughly in the ratio Ni3(Mo, X) where X is 
A1 plus either Ta or W. This is an independent rationale for 
using the ternary Ni3(Mo, X) alloys as models for the 7 
phase ordering.

The similarity between the quaternary superalloys and the 
ternary Ni3(Mo, X) alloys begins with the quenched state. 
The [001] and [112] zone patterns shown in Figures 2 and 3 
clearly illustrate the presence of {1½0} diffuse intensity. This 
diffracted intensity is characteristic of the SRO state and 
originates in the y matrix. It is similar to the initial state 
observed in the Ni3(Mo, X) alloys. 10 
The {1½0} maxima observed in the superalloys are notably 
weaker in intensity compared to the ternary Ni3(Mo, X) 
alloys. The small volume fraction of the Y phase matrix 
(illustrated in Figure 4) limits the volume of short range 
ordered material. If the Y' volume fraction is 60 pct, this 
would decrease the SRO intensity in the patterns by the 
same amount. In the superalloys, the {1½0} diffuse intensi- 
ties appear to be more circular than those seen in the ter- 
naries, i.e., without the faint extensions toward the Pt2Mo 
locations (along (420) directions) shown schematically in 
Figure 1. At first glance, this could be indicative of the SRO 
occurring in Ni4Mo which is characterized by a nearly 
spherical {1½0} as opposed to Ni3Mo which has been re- 
ported to have (420) extensions from the {1~0}- .16'23 How- 
ever, the diffuse edges of the (already faint) {1½0} will be 
decreased in intensity by the small volume fraction of the y 
phase. Thus, the exact shape of the SRO intensity cannot be 
determined without quantitative isointensity profiles or low 
angle X-ray scattering data.

The LRO phases formed in RSR 143 at 700 °C are the 
same as those observed at 600 °C. The diffraction patterns 
from specimens aged at 700 °C (Figure 9) show the DO22 
and the Dla structures along with profuse (420) streaking. 
Following 10 hours of aging, these streaks emanate from all 
of the DO22 reflections and extend almost continuously 
through reciprocal space. This could be explained by a pre- 
cipitate shape effect, but closer examination of the streaks

D. Quenched and Aged at 800 °C 
The response of RSR 143 to aging treatments at 800 °C 
is significantly different from that noted at lower tem- 
peratures. The diffraction patterns in Figure 13 show the 
DO22 structure only; 800 °C is apparently above the Dla 
solvus. The shape of the DO22 reflections can be explained 
with reference to the dark-field image in Figure 14. The 
DO22 precipitates form as laths on the orthogonal { 100} faces 
of the y' particles. This DO22-L12 precipitate morphology 
has also been seen in INCONEL 718. 2 It suggests that the 
DO22 has either heterogeneously nucleated or preferentially 
coarsened on the y' cuboids. In either case, the distribution 
is significantly different from that formed at lower tem- 
peratures and effectively eliminates spinodal transformation 
mechanisms. 
The RSR 185 alloy forms the same LRO coherent struc- 
tures at 800 °C as at lower temperatures. The morphology of 
the Pt2Mo precipitates has not changed (Figure 16). The 
DO22 precipitates, however, occur as laths on the y' faces in 
a manner analogous to that seen in RSR 143. As was the 
case at 600 and 700 °C, it was not possible to differentiate 
between heterogeneous nucleation and spinodal ordering 
mechanisms for the Pt2Mo phase. 
The grain boundaries of the RSR 185 undergo a dis- 
continuous coarsening in which the coherent y', DO22, 
and Pt2Mo precipitates are replaced by noncoherent y' 
and orthorhombic Ni3Mo. As shown in Figure 18, the 
angles and d spacings measured on electron diffraction 
patterns taken from grain boundary precipitates are in ex- 
cellent agreement with those calculated from Ni3Mo lat- 
tice parameters. 12.19 
This study has shown that a fundamental understanding of 
ordering reactions in complex alloys can be gained through 
the careful choice of a model system. Specifically, ternary 
Ni3(Mo, X) alloys can be used to rationalize the ordered 
structures which form in Ni-A1-Mo-X superalloys. 
V. CONCLUSIONS 
The phases formed in RSR 143 and RSR 185 can be 
rationalized on the basis of Ni3(Mo, X) ternary ordering. The 
Ta + A1 in the y matrix of RSR 143 increases the stability 
of the DO22 structure as noted for the A1 and Ta ternary 
alloys. The W in RSR 185 stabilizes the Pt2Mo structure as 
in the W ternary alloy.The elemental partitioning of A1 may 
be affected by the quaternary element additions; the A1, Ta, 
and W content in the y matrix would control the NixMo 
precipitation. This explanation will require detailed ele- 
mental partitioning data in order to be confirmed. 
The ordering mechanisms for NixMo in the quaternary 
superalloys cannot be unambiguously determined by con- 
ventional TEM due to the small volume fraction of the y 
phase. However, the precipitate morphologies and distribu- 
tions observed after aging at 600 and 700 °C are consistent 
with either homogeneous nucleation or spinodal ordering 
mechanisms. Following treatments at 800 °C, the lath mor- 
phology of the DO22 precipitates is suggestive of hetero- 
geneous nucleation on the y' particles.
