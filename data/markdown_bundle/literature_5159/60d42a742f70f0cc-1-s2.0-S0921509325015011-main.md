ABSTRACT
Gamma prime ( γ ′ ) rafting poses a significant challenge to the long-life design of wrought superalloys operating 
above 800 
◦
C. This study revealed a fundamental difference in the element diffusion mechanisms during γ ′ rafting 
in wrought superalloys and single-crystal superalloys. In single-crystal superalloys, element diffusion is propelled 
by the different stress states between γ / γ ′ phases, which stems from γ / γ ′ interfacial dislocations. Conversely, in 
wrought superalloys, it has been observed that matrix stacking faults at the γ / γ ′ interface directly promote the 
diffusion of γ ′ forming elements. Subsequently, the rafting process is completed by matrix dislocations. To 
suppress the γ ′ rafting, the stability of γ ′ phases was enhanced through the optimization Ti and Ta contents. 
Consequently, the grain boundaries migration and γ ′ rafting process was effectively prevented due to the bimodal 
distribution of γ ′ phases with grains. Besides, the addition of Ta increased the lattice misfit of secondary γ ′ phase 
and simultaneously impeded dislocation motion, thereby further enhanced the creep resistance of the alloy. This 
research provides theoretical foundations for the development of high-temperature, long-life wrought superal -
loys, thereby guaranteeing the safety and reliability of power systems in advanced aero-engines.
1. Introduction
Ni-based wrought superalloys are widely used in key components of 
engines due to their excellent high temperature mechanical properties 
[ 1 , 2 ]. With the increasing demand for engine performance, there is an 
urgent need for wrought superalloys capable of sustained use at tem -
peratures exceeding 800 
◦
C [ 3 , 4 ]. However, the γ ′ phases will form a 
rafting structure under the prolonged influence of temperature and 
stress in single-crystal superalloys (SC-alloys) and a few polycrystalline 
superalloys (directional solidification alloys and cast alloys) [ 5 – 8 ]. This 
microstructure can expand the γ matrix channels and reduce the dislo -
cation hindering effect, which is one of the primary factors influencing 
the creep rupture life of superalloys [ 9 , 10 ]. Therefore, enhancing the 
microstructural stability of the alloy and delaying the rafting process of 
γ ′ phases are crucial for improving its mechanical properties.
Understanding the rafting mechanism of γ ′ phase and proposing 
targeted improvement strategies are effective measures to enhance alloy 
performance. The γ ′ rafting mechanism has also been widely reported in 
SC-alloys [ 11 – 13 ]. It is generally accepted that the difference of stress 
states at different γ / γ ′ interfaces can serve as a driving force for 
elemental diffusion [ 14 – 16 ]. The rafting direction and misfit are directly 
correlated in SC-alloys [ 13 , 14 , 17 ], when under tensile stress, negative 
misfit will power γ ʹ particle transform into N-type rafting which the 
rafting γ ʹ is perpendicular to the stress axis, positive misfit will drive γ ʹ 
phases evolve into P-type rafting which is parallel the stress axis. For 
compress stress, the rafting situation conversed. Yang et al. [ 18 ] have 
also reported that the rafted γ ′ phase is detrimental to creep performance 
in a Ni-based superalloy. Results showed that some existed 
super-dislocations located in rafted γ ′ phases and the misfit stress release 
at γ /rafted γ ′ interface resulting weakened the γ /rafted γ ′ interface 
pinning effect for dislocation movement, and decreased the creep 
resistance of rafted γ ′ phases. The γ ʹ rafting phenomenon occurs not only 
in single-crystal superalloys but also in certain polycrystalline superal -
loys. Fan et al. [ 19 ] investigated the effect of rafted γ ′ on fatigue 
behavior in a directionally solidified Ni-based superalloy, results 
showed that with the rafting state aggravated the fatigue life decreased

single-crystal superalloys [ 23 ]. During high-temperature creep, Re

contents require synergistic optimization to enhance microstructural 
stability while suppressing TCP phase precipitation. In this study, the γ ′ 
rafting mechanism of a Ni-based superalloy has been revealed, and the 
high-temperature creep resistance of the alloy has been significantly 
enhanced through optimized Ti and Ta alloying.

4.2. Effect of component regulation on the stability of γ ʹ phase
The composition-optimized alloy exhibited only dimensional and 
morphological changes in its γ ʹ phases without rafting formation after 
prolonged high-temperature creep. This behavior can be attributed to 
the unique bimodal γ ʹ phases distribution within grains, consisting of 
discretely dispersed spherical secondary γ ʹ precipitates interspersed with 
finer tertiary γ ʹ particles. The secondary γ ʹ phases serve as the primary 
strengthening constituents, which during high-temperature creep un -
dergo coarsening through the absorption of surrounding tertiary γ ʹ 
particles. This microstructural evolution progresses through three 
distinct stages: 1) discrete spherical secondary γ ʹ distribution; 2) 
diffusion-controlled Ostwald ripening; 3) densely packed cuboidal γ ʹ 
arrangement. The coarsening process can be accomplished solely 
through atomic thermal motion, whereas the rafting phenomenon re -
quires more stringent conditions. In addition to thermal activation, 
rafting necessitates an additional driving force provided by the lattice 
misfit stress gradient between γ and γ ʹ phases to initiate and complete 
the rafting process [ 14 – 16 ]. Intuitively, reducing interparticle spacing of 
γ ʹ phases would decrease the required diffusion distance for atomic 
rearrangement, thereby the rafting process would more easily. In 
composition-optimized alloy, the secondary γ ʹ phases exhibit a discrete 
distribution with wide interparticle spacing. The limited atomic diffu -
sion distance consequently resulted in coarsening and cuboidal trans -
formation without rafting behavior. In contrast, the initial alloy exhibits 
a unimodal γ ʹ phase distribution consisting of closely packed spherical 
secondary precipitates. The inherently small interparticle spacing be -
comes even shorter after brief coarsening, thereby significantly pro -
moting rafting behavior in initial alloy.
During prolonged high-temperature creep, both coarsening and 
rafting of γ ʹ precipitates are inevitable. Fig. 12 illustrates the γ ʹ phase 
evolution during long-term creep of the two tested alloys. The rafting of 
γ ʹ phase requires undergoing coarsening and directional coarsening 
processes, with the control of the former stage being critical. Although 
the composition-optimized alloy exhibits greater size and lattice misfit 
in secondary γ ʹ phases, it significantly retards the coarsening stage, 
thereby delaying rafting initiation. Since rafting microstructures are 
universally recognized as more detrimental than mere γ ʹ coarsening, this 
delayed transformation constitutes one of the primary factors contrib -

alloy.
Gao et al. [ 36 ] have investigated the influence of Ta content on 
microstructure stability and creep behavior of a Ni-based single crystal 
superalloy. The results showed that Ta addition increased absolute 
misfit of the alloy, and reduced the effective diffusion coefficient, which 
was beneficial to improve the microstructure stability and creep prop -
erty under high temperature and low stress condition. Kim et al. [ 37 ] 
have investigated the influence of γ ʹ precipitate morphology and size on 
the creep behavior of IN738LC superalloy under conditions of high 
temperature and low stress. The results showed that specimens with a 
bimodal distribution with large cuboidal γ ʹ and small spherical γ ʹ , 
exhibited the longer creep life compared to those with a unimodal dis -
tribution with spherical γ ʹ precipitates, which was mainly contributed to 
the distinguish of deformation mechanism. The main deformation 
mechanism in bimodal distribution specimen is isolated SF within γ ʹ 
phase, while, the deformation mechanism in unimodal distribution 
specimen presented extended SF traversed several γ and γ ʹ phases. 
However, both of the bimodal and unimodal distribution specimens 
occurred rafting structure under lower stress.

sents bimodal distribution within grains (secondary and tertiary γ ʹ 
phases), while, under the same creep conditions and time, no rafting 
phenomenon was observed in the alloy, nor was any directional coars -
ening detected. Therefore, the bimodal distribution of γ ʹ phases within 
grains not only enhanced the creep resistance, but also inhibited the 
rafting process effectively. Besides, the large secondary γ ʹ phases also 
effectively suppress the formation of PFZs along GBs, which also

5. Conclusions
The γ ʹ rafting mechanism and microstructure stability control by 
optimizing Ti and Ta elements of a Ni-based superalloy were investi -
gated. It revealed the γ ʹ phase rafting process in the wrought superalloy. 
Besides, by regulating the Ti and Ta elements, the morphology of the γ ʹ 
phase was changed (from a unimodal distribution to a bimodal distri -
bution), effectively suppressing the grain boundary migration phe -
nomenon and the formation of γ ʹ rafting, and significantly improving the 
high-temperature low-stress creep performance of the alloy. The main 
conclusions could be drawn as follows. 
1. Under prolonged high-temperature and low-stress creep condition, 
the initial alloy exhibited γ ʹ rafting phenomenon. The rafting direc -
tion is predominantly aligned with the 〈 100 〉 crystallographic di -
rection, and exhibits multi-directionality owing to the equiaxed 
grains structure, which mainly parallel to the stress axis.
2. The matrix SFs at γ / γ ʹ interface on the different {111} planes adja -
cent to γ ʹ phase provide a rapid diffusion pathway for elemental 
transport. This leads to the diffusion of γ ʹ phase forming elements 
towards the {200} plane which is the γ ʹ growth front, ultimately 
resulting in the completion of γ ʹ phase rafting guided by dislocations.
3. The composition-optimized alloy exhibits a bimodal distribution of γ ′ 
phases within grains (secondary and tertiary γ ʹ phase). This unique γ ʹ 
phase distribution prolongs the coarsening process of secondary γ ʹ 
precipitates, and significantly suppresses γ ʹ rafting formation, 
thereby greatly enhancing the thermal stability of γ ʹ phases. 
Furthermore, the larger-sized secondary γ ʹ precipitates effectively 
hinder grain boundary migration and prevent the formation of 
precipitate-free zones (PFZs), leading to remarkable improvement in 
the alloy ’ s high-temperature creep resistance.
4. The Ta addition in composition-optimized alloy increased the misfit 
of γ / γ ʹ phases, and impeding dislocation motion while simulta -
neously promoting the formation of L-C locks. Finally, the high 
temperature creep resistance of this Ni-based superalloy has been 
further improved.
