ABSTRACT 
In this study, we designed and developed a combined cable wire made of NbMoTaNiCr multi-principal alloys to 
meet the demand for high-temperature structural materials. On this basis, the gas tungsten arc welding (GTAW) 
wire arc additive manufacturing (WAAM) experiments of NbMoTaNiCr multi-principal alloys were conducted, 
and the microstructure and mechanical properties of the formed layer were analyzed and tested. The result 
showed that the formed NbMoTaNiCr multi-principal alloys layer at room temperature mainly exhibited a face- 
centered cubic (FCC) structure with a small amount of μ -phase. The microstructure exhibited the formation of 
fine and uniform dendrites, with certain dendrite segregation, accompanied by the changed elemental distri -
bution. The high-temperature structural phases of the formed NbMoTaNiCr multi-principal alloys layer were 
BCC, FCC, B2, and μ -phase; the average hardness of the formed layer at room temperature was 911 HV, higher 
than that of all other systems with a solid-solution structure reported in the literature. At room temperature, the 
yield strength of the formed layer was 545.5 MPa, and the fracture strain was 9.5%. At 750 and 1100 
◦
C, the 
fracture strains of the formed layer was 6.5 and 6.8%, respectively, while the corresponding yield strength of the 
formed layer was 597 and 490 MPa, much better than that of the conventional high-temperature alloy Inconel 
718 (200 MPa) at 1000 
◦
C.

space, nuclear, and military industries. However, current Ni-based high- 
temperature alloys are suitable only for applications below 1150

materials. Therefore, it is critical to design and develop materials with 
better high-temperature resistance.

Currently, there is an increasing number of reports dealing with the 
research and development of RHEAs using additive manufacturing 
methods, but they are mostly performed based on powder materials. 
Compared with metal powders, wires have the advantages of a high 
utilization rate (close to 100%), no environmental pollution, no oxida -
tion, and easy storage. The use of wire forming is more economical and 
affordable, and the size range of products produced using wires is much 
larger than that using powder fusion technology. However, RHEAs 
exhibit poor ductility at room temperature and cannot be drawn for wire 
forming. Therefore, Liu et al. [ 30 ] innovatively designed and developed 
a MoNbTaWTi RHEA cable-type welding wire, and the MoNbTaWTi 
RHEA forming experiment was performed using WAAM. The structure 
of the prepared MoNbTaWTi RHEA forming layer was a single BCC 
phase with an average hardness value of 533 HV
0.2

high-temperature alloy. This work provided a new idea and method for 
the rapid preparation of high-entropy alloys [ 31 ]. Shen et al. [ 32 ] also 
used this idea and method to conduct a study on high-entropy alloy 
WAAM forming, and the prepared AlCoCrFeNi HEA exhibited a 
compressive strength of 2.8 GPa and a compressive plasticity of 42%, 
which was 4.1 and 11.7% higher compared to the specimens prepared 
by casting, respectively. 
WAAM is a green manufacturing technology that employs an electric 
arc, created between a tungsten electrode and a substrate, as a heat 
source to melt a welding wire and synchronize the fused wire, exhibiting 
the advantages of high forming efficiency, high material utilization, 
large manufacturing part size, and low manufacturing cost compared 
with laser and electron beam additive manufacturing technologies [ 33 , 
34 ]. During the forming process of WAAM, when the cable wire is used 
as an electrode, a coupled self-rotating arc is formed due to the twisted 
structural characteristics of the cable wire, which helps enhance the 
forming quality. However, since the HEAs cable wire is twisted, exhib -
iting different properties, the stability of the arc in the wire electrode 
forming process is challenging. For this reason, the forming experiments 
of the NbMoTaNiCr were performed using the GTAW rotary WAAM 
technology based on the design and development of the NbMoTaNiCr 
cable wire. The microstructure and mechanical properties of the 
NbMoTaNiCr were also studied. The technical method described in this 
paper can completely solve the arc stability problem in the forming

2. Experimental design and scheme 
2.1. Materials and equipment 
The substrate material was 304 stainless steel with a size of 200 mm 
× 100 mm × 10 mm. The NbMoTaNiCr combined cable wire used in this 
research has a diameter of 1.6 mm (as shown in Fig. 1 (b)), which is used 
as the filler material (i.e material to be deposited). The combined cable 
wire is composed of 3 Nb wires, 2 Ni – Cr alloy wires, 1 Ta wire, and 1 Mo 
wire, a total of 7 thin metal wires with a diameter of 0.5 mm each, with 1 
central and 6 peripheral wires. The purity of each single elemental wire 
material used to make the combined cable wire was > 99.9%.The 
nominal elemental composition of the cable welding wire is shown in 
Table 1 . The shielding gas used for deposition was 99.999% high-purity 
argon. The experiments were performed at room temperature (25 
◦
C). 
The forming equipment was a self-designed GTAW rotary arc addi -
tive manufacturing forming system, which mainly consisted of an GTAW 
welding machine (EWM), the main frame and moving part, a control 
system, an electrode system, a gas protective atmosphere system, a 
cooling system, a wire feeding system, and other components. 
2.2. Experimental scheme 
The single-pass multilayer forming experiment was implemented. 
Before the experiment, the substrate surface was pretreated with a hand- 
held sander to remove the oxide layer until the surface metallic luster 
appeared. Then, the substrate was ultrasonically cleaned in an acetone 
solution and wiped with alcohol, and it was placed into a drying oven at 
100 
◦
C for 1 h. During the forming process, the process parameters were 
optimized through preliminary orthogonal process optimization exper -
iments: current 110 A, electrode spacing 2 mm, wire feeding speed 6 
mm/s, overlaying speed 110 mm/min, and a gas flow rate of 19 L/min.

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Macroscopic morphology 
A photograph of the experimentally prepared NbMoTaNiCr is shown 
in Fig. 2 . The formed weld bead is uniform, and the surface quality is 
good, without obvious surface cracks, pores, and other defects. This 
indicates that the technical scheme and process parameters used in this 
paper can be successfully used to prepare high-quality NbMoTaNiCr. 
3.2. Microstructure 
Fig. 3 shows the XRD pattern of the prepared NbMoTaNiCr. There are 
five XRD peaks. The analysis shows that the diffraction peaks correspond 
to the face-centered cubic (FCC) structure, indicating that the NbMo -
TaNiCr is the main solid phase with the FCC structure, showing a small 
amount of the μ -phase. At the same time, the diffraction peaks of the 
alloy exhibit narrow and sharp peaks, indicating the alloy ’ s high crys -
tallinity. This confirms that the technical method proposed in this paper 
can solve the problem of preparation of the HEAs wire, reaching the high 
preparation quality of RHEAs. High mixing entropy and large atomic 
size difference are the main reasons for amorphous formation. High 
mixing entropy increases the mutual solubility between the constituent 
metal elements, and large atomic size differences lead to topological 
instability, resulting in the formation of amorphous phase [ 35 ].

Since the prepared specimens all have a single FCC structure, the 
hardness increase is related to the grain size. In this experiment, the 
GTAW arc additive manufacturing process was used. Compared with the 
vacuum arc melting method, the molten pool solidification is a non- 
equilibrium solidification-crystallization process during the forming 
process, so the grain is small; meanwhile, as the arc rotates, the melt 
pool is reciprocally stirred by the arc and the centrifugal force, so the 
cooling rate increases during the preparation process, resulting in a 
larger internal subcooling and a higher nucleating mass in the melt pool, 
which refines the grain size of the dendrites. Therefore, the hardness of 
the formed layer of the RHEAs prepared by the rotating GTAW arc ad -
ditive manufacturing process is higher. At the same time, due to the 
large size difference between atoms, the multiple principal alloying el -
ements induce a high degree of lattice distortion and a significant solid 
solution strengthening effect when forming a single-phase solid solution, 
which leads to high hardness. In addition, the forming layer alloy con -
tains μ -phase, which is a topological dense phase (TCP) with hard and 
brittle properties at room temperature, also contributing to the alloy ’ s 
hardness.

4. Conclusions 
The NbMoTaNiCr was prepared by GTAW rotary WAAM technology, 
and its microstructure and mechanical properties were studied and 
analyzed. The main conclusions can be derived as following: 
(1) The twisted stranded wire + GTAW rotary arc additive 
manufacturing process can achieve the rapid forming preparation 
of HEAs. The phase structure of the NbMoTaNiCr is mainly 
composed of the solid solution with an FCC structural solid so -
lution and a small amount of μ -phase at room temperature. The 
high-temperature structural phase is composed of comprises BCC, 
FCC, B2, and μ -phase. The FCC phase exhibits relatively good 
plastic toughness at room temperature, which is favorable for 
forming, while the BCC and μ -phases exhibit excellent high- 
temperature mechanical properties; thus, this phase change is 
favorable for forming and application of high-temperature resis -
tant materials.
