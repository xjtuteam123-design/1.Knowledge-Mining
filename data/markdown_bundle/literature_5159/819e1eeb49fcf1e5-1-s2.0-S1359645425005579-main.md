We monitored how LCO affects the deformed substructure via TEM 
using compression deformed 1200-MPEA ( Fig. 2 and 3 a-e). No phase 
transformation or deformation twinning was detected. Copious planar- 
slip bands along {112} were observed ( Fig. 2 a and 3 a). The running 
dislocations concentrated in these bands can be attributed to the slip- 
plane softening phenomenon in alloys with LCOs [ 35 , 38 , 51 – 53 ]. That 
is, a strain-concentrated “ dislocation channel ” is formed, which is a 
result of the destruction of LCOs in the running path, by the repeated 
glide of dislocations along the primary slip-plane, as shown in Fig. 2 . 
Subsequent dislocations tend to run and accumulate in these channels. 
As the rolling strain increases, the tangling and jamming of dislocations 
increase the density of dislocations inside the band, some of which 
overflow out of the band via cross-slip [ 54 ]. ( Fig. 3 b, c) and tangle with 
each other around the channels to widen the deformation band [ 55 ]. 
Moreover, some overflowing dislocations open up new “ dislocation 
channels ” , again via slip-plane softening ( Fig. 3 d, e), which will evolve 
into new bands. All the dislocations have a high propensity to be 
confined within the multiple primary or secondary (and higher order) 
“ dislocation channels ” along {112} and {110}.

We next comment on the strengthening offered by the chemical 
heterogeneities introduced. The compositional undulation with a 
wavelength on the scale of 9 nm escalates the “ nano-cocktail ” -like 
environment in our MPEA, setting up rugged pathways and even traps 
for the travelling dislocations. The local environment in front of the 
moving dislocation line varies markedly due to composition variation. 
The activated dislocation segments need to detrap from some of the local 
regions where they are energetically favorable to reside. The propaga -
tion of dislocation then requires a nanoscale segment detrapping (NSD) 
mechanism, increasing the resistance to dislocation motion. The 
chemically-variable and complex landscape not just elevates the 
strength due to bumpy dislocation ride, but more importantly, it en -
courages stick-slip conducive to dislocation stall, facilitating tangling 
and accumulation [ 37 ]. The work hardening rate gets retained as a 
result of this heightened propensity for defect accumulation. This strain 
hardening helps to spread the strain throughout the material undergoing 
plastic deformation, delaying severe strain localization such as drastic 
necking.
4. Conclusion
In summary, this work delivers four novelty points/messages. The 
first take-home message is that, we have developed a facile preparation 
method of heterogeneous nanostructured alloys. Two sequential steps 
were used for thermomechanical processing, beginning with the gen -
eration of nanoscale structural heterogeneity via intensified local 
deformation bands created during cold rolling, followed by ageing 
induced chemical partitioning/heterogeneity again on the nanoscale. 
Both structurally and chemically, a heterogeneous nanostructured alloy, 
HNA-( S + C ), is derived via these two simple processing steps in the 
lightweight MPEA. Our second point is that, the strength hike is signif -
icant via our new heterogeneity design, which pushes up the hardening 
and therefore a record-high specific strength unprecedented for com -
mercial Ti alloys and MPEAs. Severe plastic flow instability is avoided 
such that the high strength (yield strength ~ 1.7 GPa) is accompanied by 
a respectable tensile ductility of 10.5 %. Third, note that all the above 
are accomplished via a novel alloy design for a simple-structured (in this 
case BCC) solid solution. Our microstructure design resorts to an un -
usually high population density of nanoscale heterogeneities inside, 
which elevate strength without having to involve hard/brittle pre -
cipitates that are expected to jeopardize ductility at the strength level 
reached by our alloy. Fourth, in terms of general applicability, our 
strategy is expected to offer a new route towards high-performance al -
loys. This is because many other MPEA solid solutions, which, being 
highly concentrated, may often be prone to the emergence of hetero -
geneous structural/chemical patterns during plastic flow or thermal 
ageing. Such features, especially when they are developed profusely on 
nanoscale, can be taken advantage of to tailor/gain properties, as we 
have demonstrated in the alloy system discussed above.
