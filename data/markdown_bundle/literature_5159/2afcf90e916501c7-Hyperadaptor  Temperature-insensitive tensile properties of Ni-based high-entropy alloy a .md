ABSTRACT
This study introduces the concept of ‘Hyperadaptor’ alloys, materials engineered to maintain con-
sistent performance across a wide range of environmental conditions. We present a Ni-based high-
entropy alloy (HEA) that exhibits minimal sensitivity to temperature variations from 77 K to 873 K,
maintaining both strength and ductility due to nano-sized L1
2 precipitates, coarse grain, and high
stacking fault energy. The temperature-independent mechanism of dislocation slip-induced plastic-
ity further supports its stability. Our findings highlight the potential of Ni-based HEA for demand-
ing applications in aerospace, automotive, and energy sectors, establishing them as pioneering
materials in alloy development.
HIGHLIGHTS
• Introduces the concept of ‘Hyperadaptor’ materials, which exhibits minimal environmental sen-
sitivity, and demonstrates Ni-based high-entropy alloys (HEAs) as prime examples, showing
consistent mechanical properties across a wide temperature range from 77 K to 873 K.
• Demonstrates minimal temperature sensitivity of Ni-based HEA through analyses of nano-sized
L1
2 precipitates and high stacking fault energy, enhancing strength and ductility.
• Employs various characterization techniques, including EBSD, ECCI, and TEM, to illustrate the dis-
location slip-induced plasticity and precipitation hardening, achieving temperature-independent
mechanical performance.
• Validates the potential of Ni-based HEAs for critical applications in the aerospace, automotive, and
energy sectors, emphasizing their consistent mechanical properties under extreme temperature
fluctuations.

IMPACT STATEMENT
This study introduces a ‘Hyperadaptor’, exhibiting a Ni-based high-entropy alloy with consistent
mechanical properties from cryogenic to high temperatures. Our findings demonstrate the alloy’s
crucial insensitivity to temperature variations in its deformation behavior.
1. Introduction
Theemergenceofhigh-andmedium-entropyalloys(H/
MEAs) represents a significant milestone in materials
science and engineering, offering outstanding mechan-
ical properties and thermal stability [1–5]. These alloys,
composedofmultipleprincipalelements,contrasttradi-
tional alloy design, which typically relies on a predom-
inant element. The increased configurational entropy
enhances phase stability and overall mechanical perfor-
mance in various environments, including temperature
variations [6–10], corrosion [11–13], hydrogen embrit-
tlement[12,14,15],andirradiation[ 16,17].
We introduce the concept of ‘Hyperadaptor’—
materials engineered for minimal sensitivity to a wide
range of environmental stimuli. Unlike conventional
materials, which are optimized for narrow tempera-
ture ranges—for example, Invar alloys for cryogenic to
roomtemperatureapplicationsandsuperalloysforhigh-
temperatureenvironments—Hyperadaptorsexhibitcon-
sistent performance across cryogenic, room, and ele-
vated temperatures. In high-demand industries such as
aerospace, automotive, and energy, where fluctuating
environmental conditions often require the use of mul-
tiple materials or supplementary components like coat-
ings, multilayer structures, or cooling systems to ensure
thermal stability. Hyperadaptor materials aim to replace
thesewithasinglesolution.Bymaintainingperformance
and stability across a wide temperature range, this inno-
vationcouldsignificantlyenhancetheefficiencyandreli-
ability of such systems, offering an optimized approach
forhigh-performanceindustries.
As the first example of a Hyperadaptor, this study
presentsaNi-basedHEAspecificallydesignedtoexhibit
reduced temperature sensitivity in its tensile properties.
Ni-based HEAs are notable for their face-centered cubic
(FCC) and L1
2 phases, known for their excellent ther-
mal stability. Minor addition of Al and Ti promotes
the formation of nano-sized L1
2 precipitates, enhanc-
ing thermal stability and ensuring athermal mechanical
properties [18–23].
The high Ni content in the alloy plays a crucial
role in increasing the stacking fault energy (SFE) [24].
In materials with low to moderate SFE, deformation
mechanisms can shift with temperature changes—for
example, while dislocation slip may dominate at room
temperature, twinning-induced plasticity (TWIP) or
transformation-induced plasticity (TRIP) may occur at
cryogenic temperatures [9,25]. In materials with suffi-
ciently high SFE, these transitions are effectively sup-
pressed, preventing the activation of TWIP and TRIP
mechanisms. As a result, dislocation slip remains the
dominant deformation mechanism across the wide tem-
perature range, leading to dislocation slip-induced plas-
ticity, including planar slip, Taylor lattices, dislocation-
dense walls, and microbands. This combination of high
SFEandnano-precipitatestrengtheningensuresthatthe
Ni-based HEA maintains consistent deformation behav-
ior, making it an excellent candidate for applications
requiring stability across a wide range of thermal condi-
tions. Further details regarding the alloy design strategy
areincludedinthesupplementary materials.
Building on this foundation, our study addresses a
criticalknowledgegapregardingtheperformanceofNi-
based HEAs across a wide temperature spectrum—from
77 K to 873 K. By investigating the temperature-
insensitivetensilepropertiesofaNi-basedHEA,weval-
idate its role as a Hyperadaptor, capable of meeting the
dynamicrequirementsofmodernindustrialapplications.
The findings highlight the potential of these HEAs to
functionreliablyundervariedenvironmentalconditions,
making them ideal candidates for advanced engineering
applications where both consistency and durability are
paramount.

4. Conclusion
This work introduced the term ‘Hyperadaptor’ alloy to
describematerialswithminimalenvironmentalsensitiv-
ity, as exemplified by Ni–HEA. Demonstrating similar
yieldstrength,ductility,anddeformationbehavioracross
a vast temperature range from 77 K to 873 K, the as-cast
Ni–HEA demonstrates its ability to maintain consistent
mechanical properties due to temperature-insensitive
strengthening mechanisms. The precipitation strength-
ening mechanism contributed to 43.5% to 62.2% of the
totalYS,anddislocationslip-inducedplasticityisnotably
insensitivetotemperaturechanges.Thismicrostructural
strategyensuresthatthealloyexhibitsstablemechanical
properties across a broad thermal spectrum, positioning
Ni–HEA as an ideal choice for critical applications in
sectors facing significant thermal variations. By further
optimizing elemental compositions for commercial via-
bility, a single Hyperadaptor material could potentially
replace the multiple materials or supplementary systems
