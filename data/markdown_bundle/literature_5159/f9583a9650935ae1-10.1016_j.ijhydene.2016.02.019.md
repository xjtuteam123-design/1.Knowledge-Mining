abstract
This paper demonstrates advantages of using tungsten single crystal as structural material
in science absorbing industry in comparison with polycrystalline material. High-
temperature short-term static strength and creep resistance characteristics of poly-
crystalline tungsten and single crystal alloy were obtained and compared. The results of
comparative studies of the mechanical properties of polycrystalline and monocrystalline
tungsten e 4% tantalum alloy are presented.
It is shown that monocrystalline tungsten based materials have both high-temperature
strength and plasticity which makes them most suitable for use under high temperatures
and high mechanical stresses. Use of monocrystalline tungsten as a structural material
makes it possible to prolong the service life and avoid the failures resulting from embrit-
tlement and strain. Moreover, thermo mechanical treatment of monocrystalline tungsten
enhances its high-temperature strength. However, it is necessary to carry out additional
experimental research to choose the appropriate thermo mechanical treatment conditions.

Introduction
Tungsten, molybdenum and their alloys are perspective
structural materials of modern technical facilities, such as
nuclear power installations, fast and high-temperature re-
actors, ITER metal mirrors, cathodes of powerful X-ray tubes
etc. [1] The basic requirement for such facilities structural
materials is the ability to operate at high temperature
(1500e2500
/C14 C) and mechanical stress, in contact with
aggressive media and radiation exposure. Structural materials
of high-temperature and high-loaded systems must have
such properties as high tensile strength, high ductility and low
creep at elevated temperatures. It means that tungsten based
materials can be used as high temperature materials in many
ﬁelds of science and technology due to the high rates of long-
term strength and creep resistance. However, increasing
temperature and operation time lead to deterioration of
properties of polycrystalline tungsten, so it is needed to
enhance the material creep strength.
Almost all materials are naturally polycrystalline. Grain
boundaries pose the weakest point of the polycrystalline
materials: they accumulate the most harmful impurities and
defects and cause cracks formation, resulting in destruction of

Monocrystalline tungsten has a number of advantages in
comparison with polycrystalline material [1,4]. Pure single
crystal has:
/C0 no grain boundaries, so it has a number of unique physical
properties that are absent in metals and alloys with poly-
crystalline structure;
/C0 the absence of recrystallization and grain boundary frac-
ture at room and high temperatures;
/C0 stability of structure and properties up to the pre-melting
temperatures (polycrystalline grain growth promotes the
release of impurities on the surface, which leads to sharp
degradation of mechanical properties);
/C0 low temperature brittle eductile transition (providing good
machinability);
/C0 high corrosion resistance in contact with nuclear fuel (ox-
ides, carbides and nitrides of uranium and plutonium),
plasma and coolant (liquid metal e Li, Na, K or gas e H
2, Cs);
/C0 anisotropy of the thermal, mechanical and chemical
properties (ability to control the properties);
/C0 high resistance to radiation and laser damage (concentra-
tion of impurities in monocrystalline materials is up to 2 e3
orders of magnitude lower than polycrystalline);
/C0 low level of gas impurity release at high temperatures;
/C0 phenomenal heat resistance at working load above 10 MPa
and temperature over than 1500 /C14 C (creep rate of the single
crystal to 3 e4 orders of magnitude lower than poly-
crystalline material);
/C0 low e ﬂow of fuel components and ﬁssion products
through the fuel element jacket (the monocrystalline
jacket serves as a diffusion barrier).

To forecast the capability of a technical object (e.g. nuclear
power plants) the ultimate strength or yield strength of
structural materials are extremely important. Main parame-
ters which determine the strength of the structures are the
tensile strength, yield strength and the curve of probability of
failure under loading obtained on the basis of experimental
data on the tensile, bending, breaking, etc.
Therefore, the main aim of this study is to obtain experi-
mental data on the comparative study of mechanical
properties: high-temperature short-term static strength,
creep resistance and high-temperature deformation of tung-
sten with poly- and monocrystalline structure (tungsten e 4%
tantalum alloy).
Moreover, obtained data are valuable for investigation of
the impact of high-temperature deformation processing on
improvement of high temperature strength. It is very desir-
able that thermo mechanical treatment with subsequent
annealing would improve the high-temperature strength of
the material preserving its monocrystalline structure and
consequently e its plasticity. This paper presents the results
of comparative studies of the mechanical properties and
deformation behavior at short-term loading of mono-
crystalline and polycrystalline tungsten.

Formation of substitutional solid solution (solid phase
hardening) by doping single crystal with the elements such
as Mo, Ta, Re, Nb results in signiﬁcant improvement of its
creep resistance and high-temperature strength at above
0.5 Tm.
Dope of single crystal results in changing of the creep curve
shape in the region of transient stress: the creep mechanism
transforms from dislocation-diffusion into ductile dislocation
climb which can be characterized by increasing of the stress
exponential index and results in signiﬁcant growth of the
creep resistance of monocrystals.

Conclusions
The comparative research of mechanical properties of tung-
sten polycrystalline and W eTa single crystal alloy is con-
ducted. It is shown, that single crystal has greater high
temperature strength and retains plasticity at temperature
above 1500 /C14 C. Plastic deformation of single crystal was
20e30% and the failure of specimen occurred with necking
down, while polycrystalline sample suffered brittle failure.
Doping of refractory monocrystalline metals to 4 at. % results
in signiﬁcant enhancement of their strength. After all, the
steady creep rate of doped materials is by 3 order of magni-
tude lower, then in pure materials.

Based on the results, following recommendation can be
made: under pressure treatment the behavior of materials is
greatly inﬂuenced by the crystallographic orientation.
Thermally stable polygonal structure obtained as a result
of the ﬂat-rolling and heat treatment can be effectively used to
improve the high temperature strength of single crystal at
temperature above 0.5 T melting. Despite all the advantages of
machining, the modes and patterns of pressure treatment
should be chosen carefully, paying special attention the pro-
cess temperature and crystallographic orientation.
Plasticity of the single crystal decreases as a function of
orientation from 95 to 25% of total deformation without failure
of the sample in the series (001) [110] (110) [001] (001) [100] (110)
[110]. The propensity of single crystals to geometric broad-
ening of the sample and distortion of the single crystal
structure increases in the same order [6].
