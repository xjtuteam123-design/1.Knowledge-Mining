ABSTRACT 
A Ni-7.9Cr-5.9W-22.2Mo-1.4Ti (wt.%) superalloy was designed and prepared to have simultaneously high 
densities of nanoscale C11
b 
and D0
22 
superlattices within the disordered Ni-based austenite matrix. As a result, 
both strength and ductility were enhanced significantly at the elevated temperature as compared to the Ni-8Cr- 
6W-22.5Mo superalloy with only the nanoscale C11
b 
superlattice. This is ascribed to not only co-existent pre -
cipitation strengthening of nanoscale C11
b 
and D0
22 
superlattices but also their combination deformation 
twinning induced strength and plasticity. Such a novel mechanism to overcome high-temperature strength- 
ductility trade-off would shed some light on designing advanced Ni-based superalloys. 
The Ni-Cr-W/Mo-based superalloys with excellent corrosion and 
creep resistance at high temperatures have drawn great attention [1 – 4] , 
because they possess great potential in the field of nuclear and chemical 
industry [5 – 7] . The previous literature revealed that nanoscale super -
lattice phases, including C11
b
, D0
22 
and D1
a
, precipitate through the 
ordering reaction can enhance significantly yield strength [8 – 11] . At 
present, most of the researches focus on investigating the evolution of a 
single superlattice and its effect on the mechanical properties [12 – 16] . It 
has been found that the D0
22 
superlattice phase has a strong precipita -
tion strengthening effect compared with the C11
b 
phase due to its higher 
stability, while the C11
b 
phase is excepted to have better ductility 
because it has a higher number of operative twining systems, which is an 
essential factor contributing to ductility when most of the slip systems in 
the parent disordered fcc structure are suppressed after the ordering 
reaction [12] . Thus, it is expected that the combination of C11
b 
and D0
22 
phases in the Ni-Cr-W-Mo-based superalloys might defeat the conflict 
between strength and ductility. 
The size and thermal stability of the superlattice phase are important 
factors for the final mechanical properties. Verma et al. [17] developed a 
superalloy (Ni
73.2
Cr
7.4
Mo
9.0
W
1.8
Ti
8.6
) with coexisting C11
b 
and D0
22 
phases, but the size of the C11
b 
phase is two orders of magnitude higher 
than that of D0
22 
phase, leading to no improvement in strength and 
sacrifice ductility. In addition, extensive studies have been conducted on 
the room temperature mechanical properties of Ni-Cr-W/Mo-based al -
loys [17 – 20] but not the elevated temperature. Nevertheless, enhancing 
the strength and ductility of the engineering structure materials simul -
taneously at elevated temperature is a key goal for their applications. 
In this paper, we designed and prepared a Ni-Cr-W-Mo-Ti superalloy 
with high densities of nanoscale C11
b 
and D0
22 
phases by adjusting the 
contents of W, Mo, and Ti. The extraordinary combination of high- 
temperature strength and ductility was found after proper processing. 
The deformation mechanisms of the C11
b 
and D0
22 
phases at elevated 
temperature was studied in detail. 
It has been proved that in the commercial Haynes alloy (Ni-8Cr-6W- 
22.5Mo), the high Mo concentration can enhance significantly both 
precipitation kinetics and the thermal stability of the C11
b 
(Ni
2
(Cr, Mo)) 
phase [ 21 , 22 ]. Thus, Ni-8Cr-6W-22.5Mo was chosen for the base alloy. 
In order to introduce the nanoscale D0
22 
phase simultaneously, minor Ti 
was added in Ni-8Cr-6W-22.5Mo alloy because the combined effect of Ti 
and W can facilitate and stabilize the D0
22 
phase based on the modified 
Engel-Brewer model [ 17 , 23 ], while 1.4 wt.% Ti was chosen because the 
higher content of Ti promotes the formation of a large Ni
3
Ti interme -
tallic compound and sacrifices the ductility [15] , the detailed design 
process can be found in the Supplementary material.

As far as the twinning mechanism is concerned, the occurring of the 
deformation twinning in coarse grained face-centered cubic metals ( > 1 
μ m) is generally believed to follow the well-known pole mechanism 
[24 – 27] , which requires an obstacle for the activation of the full dislo -
cation separation process [28] . The average grain size of the matrix 
phase in the aged Ni-7.9Cr-5.9W-22.2Mo-1.4Ti alloy was found to be 
~18.9 μ m ( Fig. 1 (b)), and the nanoscale C11
b 
and D0

high number density formed in the matrix can be treated as obstacles to 
dislocations ( Fig. 1 (e) and (f)). Therefore, deformation twinning in the 
superalloy is believed to occur via the pole mechanism. In this mecha -

The occurring of deformation twinning in the Ni-7.9Cr-5.9W- 
22.2Mo-1.4Ti alloy at 650 
◦

cut through the high density nanoscale C11

compared with that in the Ni-7.9Cr-5.9W-22.2Mo alloy. In addition, the 
twin boundaries can be regarded as internal obstacles to the motion of 
dislocations that may have a strengthening effect similar to that of grain 
boundaries. While the higher ductility of the Ni-7.9Cr-5.9W-22.2Mo- 
1.4Ti alloy at 650 
◦
C is mainly due to the following reasons. Firstly, 
the growing of the deformation twin requires the Shockley partial 
dislocation glide on the twinning plane, which is similar to the glide of 
the full dislocation and provides a deformation mode to accommodate 
plasticity [ 24 , 29 – 31 ]. Secondly, the nano-twin boundaries offer path -
ways for dislocation glide and make the dislocation motion easier on a 
twin boundary than that in the matrix, which produces an increase in the 
ultimate tensile elongation and enhances ductility [ 32 , 33 ]. From all that 
mentioned above, it is the co-existence of higher density nanoscale C11

and D0
22

ning themselves that contribute to the simultaneous enhancement of 
high-temperature strength and ductility in the age-treated 
Ni-7.9Cr-5.9W-22.2Mo-1.4Ti alloy. 
In conclusion, the addition of Ti to the Ni-8Cr-6W-22.5Mo superalloy 
can facilitates the formation of nanoscale C11
b 
and D0
22 
superlattice 
phases, contributing to improving yield strength by precipitation hard -
ening. The occurrence of high-temperature deformation twinning in the 
matrix, C11
b 
and D0
22

tions glide on the twinning boundary, which also offers channels for 
dislocation motion. It is the slip of both full and partial dislocations 
induced by high-temperature deformation twinning that leads to 
enhanced ductility and meanwhile maintains improved strength.
