ABSTRACT
Segregation plays a crucial role in crack initiation in Ni-superalloys, yet the surface segregation of Al, Hf, and Ta 
in these alloys has not been previously documented. This study investigates the melt pool microstructure, phase 
distribution, defects, and mechanical properties of CM247LC Ni-superalloys fabricated through laser-based 
powder bed fusion (L-PBF). The findings indicate that during solidification, misoriented columnar dendrites 
create segregation channels that facilitate the flow of Al and refractory elements to the melt pool surface, 
resulting in the formation of bubble-shaped Al
2
O
3
+ Ta(Hf)O
2 
primary surface segregation oxide. Subsequently, 
the depression and Marangoni flow induced by the next laser scan transform these primary segregation oxides 
into line-shaped redistributed oxides. These redistributed oxides, when positioned between the scan tracks of 
subsequent layers, become difficult to remelt and remain as unmelted oxides within the sample, thereby 
compromising its high-temperature performance. By parameters optimisation, the high-temperature tensile 
properties of 1105 MPa tensile strength and 8.5 % elongation in the horizontal direction, and 1100 MPa, 15 % 
elongation in the vertical direction were achieved.
In recent years, laser powder bed fusion (L-PBF) of Ni-superalloys, 
has garnered significant research interest due to the increasing de -
mand for high-temperature components with intricate structures [ 1 ]. 
Among them, the directionally solidified (DS) Ni-superalloys exhibit 
excellent high temperature performance and high susceptibility to 
cracking due to their high Al and Ti content [ 2 ]. Researchers have 
explored various strategies, including parameter optimisation [ 1 ], 
composition modification, and post-processing to minimise cracking. 
Despite these efforts, producing crack-free difficult-to-weld 
Ni-superalloys with excellent high-temperature performance via L-PBF 
remains a formidable challenge [ 3 , 4 ].
The dendrite boundary elements, Hf and Ta, have significant impact 
on the crack formation and the distribution of phases [ 5 , 6 ]. Notably, 
they elevates the potential for grain boundary segregation, with some 
studies correlating the formation of liquation cracks to the γ - γ ′ eutectic 
[ 7 , 8 ]. As such, forming equiaxed grains or reduce Hf content were 
proposed to avoid cracking. Yet, these solutions compromise the high 
temperature performance [ 9 , 10 ]. Additionally, the misorientation, 
thermal and compositional gradients in Ni-superalloys could also lead to 
segregation channels or freckle defects [ 11 ], which profoundly impacts 
mechanical performance, pose significant challenges for non-destructive 
testing, and resist elimination through heat treatment methods [ 1 ].
The aforementioned segregation is significantly exacerbated in the 
additive manufacturing (AM) process [ 12 ]. Wang et al. [ 13 ] conducted a 
study on the Nb segregation in IN718 superalloys, observing 
droplet-shaped Nb segregations in the interdendritic regions. Cloots 
et al. [ 14 ] reported that Zr segregation at misoriented grain boundaries 
acts as a precursor to cracking in L-PBFed IN738 superalloys.

Fig.4 shows the formation and evolution of segregation during L- 
PBF. Fig.4a shows the γ matrix typically exhibits a 〈 001 〉 direction along 
the building direction due to heat flex and preferred orientation of f.c.c. 
lattice. The segregation of Al, Hf and Ta typically occurs around the 
secondary dendrite tips, which can be blocked by the dendrites above, 
thus accumulating between the columnar grain boundaries. The upward 
migration of oxidation products is primarily driven by their lower 
density compared to the surrounding molten material, which contrib -
utes to the segregation of elements like Al, Hf, and Ta in these regions 
[ 21 ]. In this case, a small amount of the η or γ ′ phase precipitate at the 
〈 001 〉 low misorientation columnar grain boundary, with a size of 
around 100 nm width as shown in Fig. 4b and c. These phases exhibit 
brittleness at low temperatures, leading to grain boundary cracking 
during cooling in L-PBF process [ 9 ].

mulation of grain boundary elements. The flow of these segregated el -
ements towards the melting boundary is facilitated by Marangoni flow, 
solidification dynamics, and dendritic channeling, with the in -
compatibility between the oxide and metal phases, as well as surface 
tension in the liquid state, further influencing their segregation and 
distribution. Such misorientation become stronger when higher energy

This study reports, for the first time, an Al/Hf/Ta rich segregation in 
L-PBFed Ni-superalloys, which significantly impacts mechanical per -
formance at elevated temperatures. Two types of segregation oxides, 
primary and redistributed segregation oxide, develop at different stages 
of fabrication. The primary segregation oxide, which arises through 
segregation channels between misoriented dendrites, exhibits a bubble- 
shaped morphology on the melt pool surface. During the remelting of 
the subsequent scan pass, it is redistributed along the melt boundary by 
laser depression and Marangoni flow, adopting a line-shaped 
morphology. Fabricating CM247LC samples with low power and me -
dium E
L 
parameters reduces the redistributed segregation, enhancing 
the elevated temperature mechanical properties to 1100 MPa UTS with 
15 % elongation in the vertical direction, and 1105 MPa UTS with 8.5 % 
elongation in the horizontal direction.
