ABSTRACT 
This study investigates the high-temperature performance of Laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) – 
Haynes 230 alloy with trace La addition, addressing the issue of high-temperature ductility loss and 
establishing the relationship between microstructural evolution and mechanical properties at 
elevated temperatures. The La-containing deposited sample exhibited a 6% increase in tensile 
strength and a 27.2% improvement in elongation at room temperature. At 900°C, the elongation 
increased from 15.6% to 60.4% after hot isostatic pressing (HIP) treatment. The study also discusses 
the shift in deformation mechanisms, highlighting the formation of composite precipitates, 
nanotwins, and stacking faults. Notably, the stacking faults changed direction significantly when 
passing through annealing twin boundaries, which contributed to enhanced ductility. As the 
temperature increased, dislocation density decreased, and thermally stable La₂O₃ precipitated 
uniformly in the matrix. DFT calculations confirmed the formation of an amorphous layer at the 
La₂O₃ – matrix interface, which acted to pin dislocation sources, improving high-temperature 
ductility. The strengthening effect was primarily attributed to the inhibition of dislocation slip by 
the composite precipitates, annealing twins, and La₂O₃ precipitates. This study provides new 
insights into the optimisation of LPBF – Haynes 230 alloy for high-temperature applications.

1. Introduction
As modern industry continues to demand higher perform -
ance from materials under elevated temperatures, the 
development of alloys with exceptional high-temperature 
strength and ductility becomes increasingly important [ 1 ]. 
Superalloys are widely used in the aerospace industry, pri -
marily for aerospace engines and their high-temperature 
components that must operate under extreme high-temp -
erature and high-pressure conditions for extended periods 
[ 2 , 3 ]. These alloys exhibit excellent oxidation resistance, 
corrosion resistance, and high-temperature strength, 
maintaining stability and reliability in harsh environ -
ments-something that is difficult for other alloys to 
achieve [ 4 , 5 ]. Common superalloys include IN718, IN625, 
and Hastelloy X. However, the operating temperature for 
IN718 is around 650°C [ 6–8 ], IN625 around 800°C [ 9 , 10 ], 
and Hastelloy X around 870°C [ 11–13 ], while IN738 
operates at temperatures around 900°C [ 14 , 15 ]. Although 
these alloys offer robust performance, their usage temp -
eratures present certain limitations. Consequently, 
Haynes 230, with a usage temperature up to 1000°C, has 
garnered increasing attention [ 16 ].
The internal structure of aerospace hot-end com -
ponents, such as engines, is highly complex and requires 
advanced manufacturing processes to meet design and 
performance demands. LPBF is an advanced additive man -
ufacturing technology that offers several advantages, 
including high precision, greater design flexibility, high 
material utilisation, and shorter production cycles [ 17 , 18 ]. 
LPBF enables the precise fabrication of complex geome -
tries, making it particularly suitable for aerospace appli -
cations. However, microcracks are often generated 
during the LPBF-Haynes 230 process, which limits its 
development [ 19–22 ]. To address additive manufacturing

effective post-processing technique that can efficiently

However, micro-segregation occurs during the HIP 
process, and the size of precipitated phases increases, 
which is detrimental to high-temperature mechanical 
properties [ 30 ]. In recent years, rare earth elements 
have increasingly been added to superalloys to 
enhance their performance. Among these, La, as a repre -
sentative rare earth element, can be incorporated into 
the powder preparation process of Haynes 230. It can 
form stable oxides at grain boundaries, improving the 
alloy oxidation resistance, promoting grain refinement, 
and enhancing high-temperature strength and ductility 
[ 31–33 ]. However, the specific mechanisms of La’s 
effects in superalloys remain underexplored.
This study investigated the effect of adding 0.5 wt.% La 
to the original composition of Haynes 230 alloy. The objec -
tive was to suppress microcracks and maintain high-temp -
erature tensile strength and ductility through HIP. 
Specifically, the research explored the role of trace La in 
enhancing the alloy’s ductility, addressing the issue of 
plasticity loss in LPBF-Haynes 230. The findings aimed to 
provide theoretical insights and technical support for the 
further development of superalloys in the LPBF field.
2. Material and experiments
2.1. Crack inhibition and high-temperature 
tensile testing
In this study, Haynes 230 powder with the addition of 
0.5 wt.% La was prepared based on the composition of 
the original Haynes 230 alloy powder using the gas atomi -
sation process. 0.5 wt.% was identified as an optimal con -
centration that balances improved plasticity and economic 
efficiency. The detailed composition is shown in Table 1 . 
The composition analysis was performed using inductively 
coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS).
Haynes 230 samples were fabricated using LPBF tech -
nology, with the equipment model being EP-M250. The 
process parameters for fabricating Haynes 230 using 
LPBF were: a laser power of 300 W, a scanning speed 
of 1100 mm/s, a hatch spacing of 0.11 mm, and a layer 
thickness of 0.05 mm. The elemental composition in 
the as-deposited state showed no significant change. 
After sample preparation, HIP treatment was applied 
to eliminate defects from the additive manufacturing 
process and improve density. The specific parameters 
for the HIP process were as follows: pressure of 
180 MPa, temperature of 1210°C, and processing time 
of 2 h. The process flow is depicted in Figure 1 . During 
the HIP process, microcracks were closed under high 
temperature and pressure, and solute diffusion pro -
moted the healing of microcracks. Additionally, carbides 
underwent phase transformations, resulting in a more 
stable microstructure. After HIP treatment, the material’s 
microstructure recrystallized, becoming more uniform, 
and the density was significantly improved. However, 
since Haynes 230 superalloy is primarily used in high- 
temperature environments, its high-temperature mech -
anical properties, after additive manufacturing and 
defect elimination, require further investigation.
First, tensile mechanical tests were conducted on 
samples before and after microcrack elimination at 
different temperatures using a 5982 testing machine. 
The high-temperature tensile tests were performed in 
accordance with the GB/T 228.2-2015 standard, with 
test temperatures set at 23, 500, 700, 900, and 1100°C, 
and a strain rate of 0.001 s
−1
. To verify the effectiveness 
of the trace La addition, room-temperature tensile tests 
were conducted on LPBF + HIP samples prepared from 
both the original powder and the modified powder.

3. Results
3.1. High-temperature tensile properties
High-temperature performance tests were conducted 
on samples after crack closure. Comparison of the 
room-temperature tensile results for the original and 
modified compositions (containing La) showed that 
the modified composition samples had significantly 
better tensile performance, as shown in Figure 3 (a). For 
the as-deposited samples ( Figure 3 (b)), the room-temp -
erature tensile strength was 807.1 MPa, with an 
elongation of 30.8%. At 500°C, tensile strength 
decreased to 643.6 MPa, a reduction of 15%. As the 
temperature increased further, tensile strength contin -
ued to decrease. At 900°C, due to the presence of micro -
cracks, the yield strength of the sample was not 
obtained.
The mechanical properties improved significantly 
after eliminating additive manufacturing defects. For 
HIP-treated samples, the room-temperature tensile 
strength was 855.6 MPa with 58% elongation ( Figure 
3 (c)). Compared to the as-deposited samples, elongation 
increased by 27.2%, and strength improved by 6%. At 
500°C, elongation increased by 28.5%, and tensile 
strength by 5%. At 700°C, elongation increased by 
27.1%, and tensile strength by 2%. Notably, at 900°C, sig -
nificant work hardening occurred after yielding, with 
elongation increasing by 44.8%, greatly enhancing 
high-temperature performance ( Figure 3 (d)). The mech -
anical properties at 900°C also met ASTM standards for 
Haynes 230 ( Figure 3 (e)). At 1100°C, the yield strength 
of the sample was measured. Table 3 shows the

3.3. Effect of temperature on microstructure 
evolution
To investigate microstructure evolution at different 
temperatures with La addition, a detailed analysis was 
performed. In the as-deposited state, distinct melt pool 
and sub-grain boundaries were observed without 
evident precipitates ( Figure 5 (a)). After crack closure, 
melt pool boundaries disappeared, recrystallization 
occurred, and precipitates became more prominent 
( Figure 5 (b and f)). The main precipitates were large car -
bides ( Figure 5 (c1)), while fine nano-precipitates, likely 
La₂O₃, formed within grains, though further confirmation 
is needed. With increasing temperature, grain size grew, 
and statistical analysis showed that precipitate size at 
grain boundaries increased under LPBF + HIP conditions, 
indicating enhanced elemental diffusion at higher temp -
eratures ( Figure 5 (g)).
To study the effect of temperature on elemental dis -
tribution with La addition, elemental distribution analy -
sis was performed at different temperatures ( Figure 6 ). In 
the as-deposited state, the elements were uniformly dis -
tributed. After crack closure, regions with precipitates 
showed depletion of Ni and Cr and enrichment of W 
and C. La₂O₃ is at the nm scale, which could not be 
characterised at this scale. Statistical analysis of La 
content in the precipitates showed an increasing trend 
with temperature, peaking at 900°C. At 1100°C, La 
content slightly decreased, likely due to the shorter frac -
ture time, which reduced La segregation time ( Figure 
5 (h)).
To further clarify the impact of La addition on micro -
structure evolution, TEM analysis was performed on the 
clamping sections of high-temperature tensile samples.

3.4. Effect of temperature on fracture behaviour
To investigate the effect of La addition on fracture 
behaviour during high-temperature tensile testing, frac -
ture surface and composition analyses were conducted. 
At 23°C, shallow dimples appeared on the fracture 
surface, indicating ductile fracture, with Ni and Cr 
enriched and W concentrated in the precipitates 
( Figure 10 (a)). At 500°C, the elemental distribution was 
similar, showing ductile fracture with visible dimples 
( Figure 10 (b)). At 700°C, dimples remained, but the O 
content increased ( Figure 10 (c)). The reduction in Ni, 
Cr, and W suggested oxide layer formation, but oxi -
dation was not the primary cause of fracture. At 900°C, 
oxide formation became more pronounced, influencing 
fracture behaviour ( Figure 10 (d)). At 1100°C, rapid oxide 
formation decreased surface quality, leading to fast frac -
ture ( Figure 10 (e)).

4. Discussion
After HIP treatment, the improvement in plasticity 
was primarily attributed to crack closure, with a sig -
nificant reduction in dislocation density and a shift 
in the deformation mechanism to stacking faults 
and deformation twinning [ 35 ]. Compared to existing 
studies, plasticity was further enhanced by the 
addition of La. The key difference lies in the fact 
that, after HIP treatment, the precipitation of the 
matrix composite phase, the formation of twins, and 
the uniform distribution of La₂O₃ nanoscale precipi -
tates at 900°C collectively determined the defor -
mation mechanisms and their corresponding effects 
during plastic deformation. Each of these aspects is 
discussed in detail below.

4.3. Mechanism of La₂O₃ effect on high- 
temperature tensile properties
In Figure 15 (a–e), near the fracture surface, dislocation 
motion and annihilation accelerated with increasing 
temperature, leading to a decrease in dislocation 
density and a reduction in the material’s high-tempera -
ture tensile properties. However, at 900°C, the material 
exhibited similar ductility to that at 23°C. Microstructural 
analysis revealed that nanoscale particles were uni -
formly dispersed at 900°C ( Figures 15 (f) and 7 (n)), and 
elemental distribution confirmed these particles to be 
La₂O₃ ( Figure 15 (g)).
Figure 16 (a) illustrated the role of La in microstruc -
tural evolution. In LPBF-prepared deposited samples, 
no La₂O₃ precipitation was observed. After HIP treat -
ment, high temperature and pressure promoted 
element segregation and the initial precipitation of 
La₂O₃. After exposure to 900°C, La₂O₃ particles were uni -
formly dispersed throughout the matrix. La₂O₃ enhanced 
high-temperature tensile performance in three ways: (i) 
it formed stable La₂O₃ oxides with high thermal stability 
[ 39 , 40 ]; (ii) dispersed La₂O₃ particles acted as obstacles to 
dislocation motion, providing particle strengthening 
effects at 900°C; (iii) when uniformly distributed, La₂O₃ 
particles evenly distributed stress during deformation, 
reducing stress concentration and improving material 
ductility.
However, the above mechanisms could not fully 
explain why the elongation at 900°C reached 60.4%,

4.5. Synergistic effects on the enhancement of 
high-temperature plasticity
In the Haynes 230 alloy with added La prepared by LPBF, 
the enhanced high-temperature plasticity after crack 
closure is mainly controlled by the synergistic effect of 
composite precipitates, twins, and the uniform distri -
bution of La₂O₃ nanoparticles, in addition to crack 
closure. Figure 18 illustrates the mechanism behind 
the enhancement of high-temperature tensile perform -
ance. After HIP treatment, the size of the precipitates 
increased, and composite precipitates formed, signifi -
cantly increasing the area obstructing plastic defor -
mation, which was absent in the as-deposited 
microstructure. Figure 14 (a) shows that the precipitates 
effectively hindered deformation twin growth. The 
primary precipitate, M
6
C, had a coherent relationship 
with the matrix, exhibiting low misfit strain [ 44 ]. The sec -
ondary precipitate, M
23
C
6
, alleviated stress concen -
tration in the matrix through SFs ( Figure 13 (a4)), 
enhancing microstructure stability. The increase in twin 
density played a key role in improving plasticity. Anneal -
ing twin boundaries and their precipitates blocked dislo -
cation movement ( Figures 7 (h) and 14 (f–g)), and SFs 
propagation was restricted at the twin boundaries 
( Figure 14 (h and i)). Nanoscale twins within the precipi -
tates also contributed to enhanced plasticity. The defor -
mation mechanism shifted from dislocation tangling in 
the as-deposited state to deformation twinning, 
leading to improved elongation. A key factor was the 
dispersed La₂O₃ in the LPBF + HIP microstructure. The 
amorphous layer at the La₂O₃-matrix interface pins dislo -
cation slip, promotes dislocation multiplication, and 
enhances plasticity. The precipitation of rare-earth 
oxide, influenced by temperature, significantly improved 
mechanical properties and offers guidance for enhan -
cing the high-temperature performance of LPBF-fabri -
cated superalloys in future research.
5. Conclusion
This study investigated the high-temperature perform -
ance of Haynes 230 alloy fabricated by LPBF after crack 
suppression. For the first time, the issue of high-temp -
erature plasticity loss in this alloy was successfully 
addressed through the addition of La, and a correlation 
between high-temperature microstructural evolution 
and mechanical properties was established. The main 
conclusions are as follows: 
1. After HIP treatment, the as-deposited samples with 
La addition exhibited a 6% improvement in high- 
temperature strength and a significant 27.2% 
increase in elongation at room temperature. At 900° 
C, the elongation increased dramatically from 15.6% 
to 60.4%.
2. With the transition of deformation mechanisms, a 
large number of composite precipitates formed in 
the matrix, accompanied by the development of 
nanoscale twins and stacking faults. Additionally, 
annealing twins with different grain boundary 
configurations were generated. The stacking faults 
underwent significant directional changes as they 
crossed annealing twin boundaries, collectively con -
tributing to the improvement in plasticity.
3. As temperature increases, dislocation density 
decreases. La readily combines with oxygen to form 
thermally stable La₂O₃ particles. DFT calculations 
confirm the formation of an amorphous layer at the 
La₂O₃-matrix interface, promoting dislocation multi -
plication. The uniform dispersion of La₂O₃ particles 
significantly enhances ductility at 900°C. Additionally, 
temperature regulation effectively promotes La₂O₃ 
precipitation.
4. The strengthening mechanism was primarily deter -
mined by the combined effects of composite precipi -
tates, annealing twin boundaries, and the uniform 
dispersion of La₂O₃ particles, which collectively 
impeded dislocation motion.
