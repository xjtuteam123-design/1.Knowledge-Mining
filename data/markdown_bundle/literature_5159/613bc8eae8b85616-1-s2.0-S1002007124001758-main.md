ABSTRACAT
Refractory high-entropy alloy (RHEA) usually exhibits a high melting point and hence a very high deformation
resistance at high temperatures. However, the relatively poor plasticity at room temperature, i.e., only few RHEAs
displaying as-cast tensile ductility, strongly limits the applications of RHEAs as engineering materials. In this
work, we show a huge tensile ductility observed in an as-cast TiVNbTa RHEA (~40 % fracture elongation)
accompanying with a high yield strength (~800 MPa), which are rarely reported properties for RHEAs. The as-
cast alloy shows a simple body-centered cubic (BCC) structure with dendrites. Ductile fracture with many dimples
is the main fracture mechanism, while no twinning and deformation induced phase transition was observed. The
uniform plastic deformation mainly relies on the planar and cross-slip of dislocations. The present result suggests
the huge ductility potentials for RHEAs, providing a clue for designs future high performance RHEAs with good
ductility.
1. Introduction
Owing to the fast development of advanced industrial equipment in
various ﬁelds, structural materials with more and more excellent me-
chanical properties are in high demand. In the past decade, a great deal of
efforts was devoted to achieving advanced alloys with high strength and
plasticity. For instance, one of excellent approaches for achieving high
performance steels is the microstructure design via regulating processing
parameters based on thermodynamics and kinetics in phase trans-
formation and the criterion of generalized stability [ 1,2]. In recent years,
high-entropy alloy (HEA), a new concept in alloy design and develop-
ment, has opened up new design paths for advanced materials [ 3].
Among HEAs, alloys constituted with refractory metal elements like Mo,
Ti, V, Nb, Hf, Ta, Cr, W, etc., are called refractory high-entropy alloys
(RHEAs) [ 4–8]. RHEAs usually have high melting points and
body-centered cubic (BCC) structure, displaying excellent hardness and
strength at room temperature (RT) and high strength and ductility at high
temperatures [9–11]. Besides the promising mechanical properties, some
RHEAs also exhibit good storage capacity of H and He, and high resis-
tance to radiation damage [ 12,13], thus RHEAs are expected to show
broad application prospects in high-temperature structural components
in nuclear industry, aerospace and other ﬁelds [14].
Senkov et al. [ 15] ﬁrst reported single-phase BCC NbMoTaW and
VNbMoTaW RHEAs, which exhibit good phase stability up to 1400 /C14 C,
and also good high temperature strength. At 1600 /C14 C, the yield strengths
of NbMoTaW and VNbMoTaW are 405 MPa and 477 MPa, respectively,
suggesting very good resistance to high-temperature softening. However,
the two alloys are relatively brittle at RT. The compressive plasticity is
less than 10 % [ 15,16]. Later, a ductile HfNbTaTiZr RHEA with
single-phase BCC structure was developed [ 17]. Despite a decrease in
yield strength, the RT compression plasticity exceeds 50 %, which
signiﬁcantly distinguishes it from the common brittle RHEAs. The suc-
cessful development of HfNbTaTiZr provides ideas for the design of high
temperature alloys with good RT mechanical properties.
Nonetheless, the vast majority of current REHA related researches are
limited to compression properties, which usually cannot accurately
reveal the fracture mechanisms under opening tensile loading, one of the
most dangerous loading modes in engineering components [ 18–20]. The
lack of studies on tensile properties is mainly due to the poor RT tensile
plasticity of many RHEAs. Equiatomic TiVNbTa is one of the few
single-phase BCC RHEAs with both excellent high-temperature strength
and compressive ductility. Lee et al. performed the high-temperature

2. Experimental methods
TiVNbTa RHEA was prepared from high purity (99.95 wt%) Ti, V, Nb,
Ta by vacuum levitation melting (VLM), with ingot size of Φ50*60 mm.
Electromagnetic stirring in the VLM process allows the molten liquid
alloy to be fully mixed, with a uniform and accurate composition. In
addition, during the melting process, the alloy is suspended in the cru-
cible, so that pure alloy can be prepared without pollution. A Bruker D8
x-ray diffractometer (XRD) with Co - kα radiation was used to charac-
terize the crystal structure at a scan rate of 5 /C14 /min over a 2Theta angle
range of 30 /C14 –130/C14 . The microstructure and elemental distribution were
observed using a Tescan Vega 3 scanning electron microscope (SEM)
combined with electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) and energy
spectrometry (EDS). RT tension and compression was tested using an
Instron 5967 machine at a strain rate of 5 /C2 10/C0 4s/C0 1 on plate specimens
with a gauge dimension of 3 /C2 1.9 /C2 1 mm and rectangular specimen
with a size of 2 /C2 2 /C2 4 mm, respectively. To ensure the reproducibility of
the experiment, both tensile and compression were repeated at least
three times, and the strain was detected using a video extensometer. In
order to obtain the high-temperature (HT) mechanical properties of the
VLM TiVNbTa alloy, a Gleeble 3500 testing machine was used for
compression test at HT. The test temperature was 1273 K and the sample
size was 10 mm diameter by 15 mm high. The preparation process for HT
samples was similar to those for tension and compression at RT. For
comparison with related studies, the strain rate was set to be 10 /C0 3 s/C0 1.
Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) was used to analyze the
microstructure of the undeformed and deformed alloys. For the prepa-
ration of TEM samples, slice samples with a diameter of 3 mm were
mechanically ground to the thickness of 40 μm and further thinned by a
Gatan precision ion polishing system.

The plastic deformation mechanism was also investigated by TEM. As
shown in Fig. 6(a), in the undeformed VLM TiVNbTa alloy before tension,
we observed only single BCC grains, grain boundaries and a few dislo-
cations, but no other complex structures. Observations of the micro-
structure after deformation reveal that only dislocations are generated
within the alloy after RT tension (see Fig. 6(b)–(d)). In the deformed VLM
TiVNbTa sample, not only long straight dislocation lines were found, but
also L-shaped curved dislocation lines were observed. Long straight
dislocation lines are generally created by screw dislocations driving
along the slip plane. The L-shaped dislocation lines are the direct evi-
dence of dislocation cross-slip that might be caused by large lattice
distortions [51]. The activation of cross-slip gives the dislocations more
space to move, resulting in higher plasticity and the accumulation of
higher dislocation densities. The selected area electron diffraction
(SAED) pattern of the alloy after deformation shows that the alloy re-
mains a BCC structure, consistent with the XRD results. As shown in
Fig. 6 (b)–(d), the dislocation density is signi ﬁcantly increased after
deformation, in accordance with the dislocation mediated deformation
mechanism. Dozens of dislocations were observed in Fig. 6 (b) ;i n Fig. 6
(c) the dislocations proliferated and entangled to form dislocation cells;
and in Fig. 6 (d) the entire ﬁeld of view was full of entangled dislocations.
Combined with the dislocation features in the TEM images and the slip
band morphologies on the sample surface, we found that the excellent
elongation of VLM TiVNbTa should be mediated only by dislocation
activities, including both the planar dislocation glide and the cross-slip of
dislocations.

ture surface seems to be not uniform (see Fig. 6 (b)–(d)). This may be
caused by the ununiform plastic deformation degrees among grains with
different orientations. To examine this assumption, nanoindentation test
was performed on different grains. EBSD was used to determine the
orientation of each grain ﬁrstly, and the nanohardness values at 6 /C2 3
lattice positions were tested, as shown in Fig. 7 (a). The resulted map of
nanohardness display in Fig. 7(b) and the load-depth curves were plotted
in Fig. 7(c). It if found that the nanohardness values of the two differently
oriented grains #17 and #18 are 2.61 GPa and 4.55 GPa, respectively,
with a relatively huge difference in hardness of 1.94 GPa. It is well known
that the critical stress required for the dislocation slipping is highly
dependent on the Schmidt factor, which leads to a large effect of grain
orientation on plasticity. Orientation mismatch among different grains
leads to inevitable differences in the order and degree of plastic defor-
mation in the tension process, which may explain the difference in
dislocation density observed by TEM in different grains ( Fig. 6 ). How-
ever, this relatively large hardness difference, leading to the ununiform
plastic strain distribution, may be harmful for the global plasticity.

It is also noted that the excellent ductility of the as-cast RHEA alloy
can be attributed to the high generalized stability in the VLM method
[53–58]. On the one hand, the VLM method has a tendency to promote
the undercooling of solidi ﬁcation by hanging the melt above the copper
crucible to suppress heterogeneous nucleation [ 59,60], as opposed to arc
melting. In undercooled multi-component alloys, both the constitutional
and the thermal undercooling [ 61] generate an increase in the thermo-
dynamic driving force ΔG for the interfacial velocity of dendrite growth,
which facilitates a higher level of the solid concentration [ 53]. This re-
duces the concentration of bulk liquid and mitigates the occurrence of
segregation. On the other hand, compared with the mainly thermal
diffusion under higher undercooling, the dendrite growth controlled by
both constitutional and thermal diffusion under medium undercooling
enhances the generalized stability for interfacial velocity [ 62], which
reduces the interfacial velocity and inhibits the redistribution of solute at
the dendrite interface [ 53]. This reduces the interfacial concentration on
the liquid side and decreases the inter-dendritic segregation. As seen in
Fig. 1 , although the ingots are dendritic microstructure, no signi ﬁcant
elemental segregation is seen in the EDS mapping diagrams, indicating
that the elements are distributed uniformly. It is found that the BCC alloy
with more uniform microstructure and composition has a lager lattice
distortion [19]. Chen et al. found that in BCC HEAs, the mobility of both
edge dislocations and screw dislocations is correlated with lattice
distortion [ 63]. The lattice distortion affects not only the activation of
kink nucleation and extension that controls the motion of screw dislo-
cations, but also the local pinning strength of edge dislocations. As a
result, the velocity difference between the screw dislocations and the
edge dislocations decreases with increasing lattice distortion, and a
smaller velocity difference represents a better intrinsic plasticity of the
alloy. The large lattice distortions in the VLM TiVNbTa RHEA thus hinder
the opening and proliferation of screw dislocations, and slow down the
slip velocity difference between the edge - screw dislocations, and
eventually yield a good uniform elongation.
According to the SEM and TEM results, the plastic deformation of the
VLM TiVNbTa in the uniform deformation stage is dominated by the
planar slip and cross-slip of the dislocations, no twinning or phase tran-
sition was observed. On the other hand, in the non-uniform deformation
stage of the tensile process (i.e., after necking), only the grains in the
necking region undergone large plastic deformation. In this case, the
plastic deformation is still mediated by dislocation mechanism, but also
affected by the evolution of micro-voids.
It is well known that the stress state in the necking region is no longer
uniaxial tensile state, but a triaxial tensile stress state due to the
conﬁnement in the transverse direction. The triaxial tensile stress will
promote the formation and growth of micro-voids in ductile alloys,
leading to the softening and the ﬁnal fracture. From the work-hardening
curve shown in Fig. 2 (b), one can see that the decrease of the work-
hardening rate after necking is not so quickly. This is consistent with
the large non-uniform elongation observed in the stress-strain curve. The
relatively low softening rate may originate from two aspects: the still
high hardening ability and the sluggish growth of micro-voids. Recently,
it has been reported that in some RHEAs [ 64,65] which exhibited
remarkably high toughness, kink bands were observed near the fracture
surface or in front of crack. The activation of kink bands would release
the stress concentration, and also shift grains to orientations where dis-
locations are more prone to slip, thus result in further blunting of crack
tip and the enhancement of toughness. Because of the similar triaxial
stress state between necking region and crack tip, kink bands are
assumed to be happened in the present VLM TiVNbTa and to contribute
the non-uniform elongation. On the other hand, the growth of
micro-voids in RHEA may be much slower comparing with traditional
alloys. Molecular dynamics simulation by Sills et al. [
66] vividly sug-
gested that the growth of micro-voids is accompanied by the annihilation
of dislocations on the surface of void, and thus the dislocation mobility
directly affects the growth rate of voids. It has been found that in RHEAs
dislocation moves much slower than conventional alloys [ 67], hence a
slow micro-void growth is expected.
4. Conclusion
In this work, we observed super RT tensile ductility in the as-cast VLM
TiVNbTa RHEA. The RHEA shows a good match of high strengths and
large tensile elongations ( σy ¼ 804 /C6 24 MPa, σuts ¼ 864 /C6 12 MPa, εu ¼
12.8 /C6 0.6 %%, εf ¼ 40.1 /C6 2.1 %). Under compression, the RHEA shows
a yield strength 800 /C6 2.5 MPa, displaying no tension-compression
strength asymmetry. Moreover, at 1273 K, the alloy maintains a yield
strength of 330 MPa and exhibits a compressive strength of 423 MPa at
30 % strain. The TiVNbTa has a low VEC value of 4.75 and the VLM
sample shows also a high generalized stability, suggesting the intrinsi-
cally ductile nature of the VLM TiVNbTa. The fracture elongation value of
the present alloy is comparable with the most ductile RHEA among all the
RHEAs with certain tensile ductility. The as-cast alloy exhibits a simple
BCC phase and dendritic structure, but no signi ﬁcant elemental segre-
gation. Both planar slip and cross-slip of dislocations were observed,
which interpret the plastic deformation mechanisms. Micro-void coa-
lescence dominated ductile fracture is the main fracture mechanism, and
no twinning and phase transition are observed. Since the coarse-grained
microstructure of the as-cast alloy, it is believed that the mechanical
properties of the VLM TiVNbTa can be enhanced in the next-steps by
further thermo-mechanical treatments. The present study suggests that
some RHEAs (e.g., TiVNbTa) may have great ductility potentials, while
the proper processing of RHEAs is of vital importance for realizing of the
