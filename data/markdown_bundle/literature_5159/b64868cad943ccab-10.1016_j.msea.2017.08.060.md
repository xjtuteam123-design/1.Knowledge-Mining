Abstract 
Ti-Fe-Sn-Nb alloys comprised of single bcc phase were developed showing excellent 
mechanical s trength and superior plasticity. The alloys exhibited increased yield 
strength as a function of Fe content, where the Ti80Fe14Sn3Nb3 alloy presented the 
highest yield stress (1.88 GPa) comparable to that of Ti -based nanocrystalline alloys. 
Moreover, a significant strain-hardening (520 MPa) was achieved along with the plastic 
deformation. The excellent mechanical strength was enhanced by the supersaturated β-
Ti and the high density of lattice defects that restrict the dislocation motion and the high 
β-phase stability. 
Key words: Ti-Fe-Sn-Nb, mechanical properties, beta-Ti alloy, β-stability

The design of high -performance engineering alloys is driven by optimizing the 
combinations of mechanical properties in terms of strength, ductility and fracture 
toughness. Beta-type Ti alloys retaining a single bcc structure are very promising 
materials with respect to their unique mechanical features e.g. low elastic modulus, high 
strength-to-weight ratio and superio r plasticity [1-3]. The deformation mechanisms and 
the corresponding mechanical properties of beta-type alloys are closely related to the 
stability of β-phase, which is characterized by the ability to avoid the martensitic 
transformation upon quenching or deformation. The deformation mechanism changes 
from dislocation slip to deformation twinning and/or martensitic transformation with the 
decrease of β-phase stability [ 4, 5]. Molybdenum Equivale ncy (MoE) wa s used to 
quantitatively estimate the amount of β-stabilizers required for preventing martensitic 
transformation, where a pproximately 10 wt.% Mo is sufficient for stabilizing the β-
phase during quenching . Fe acts as a much stronger beta-stabilizer that the equivalent 
effect can be acheived with as low as 3.5 wt.% Fe. 
Most of the Ti-Fe based alloys studied so far are in the vicinity of Ti-Fe eutectic 
compositions [6, 7]. Various hyper - and hypo -eutectic β -Ti and TiFe alloys were 
produced and studied. These alloys exhibit superb mechanical strength but less-exciting 
ductility (˂ 15 % compressive strain) because almost no plastic deformation occurs in 
the TiFe phase [7]. In our recent work, it has been suggested that ductility of the Ti-Fe-
Sn-Nb hypoeutectic alloy mainly stems from the primary β-Ti dendrites which display a 
dislocation-type of deformation mode [8]. In this sense, it would be interesting to 
develop a new class of Ti-Fe-Sn-Nb alloys completely containing only β-Ti phase with 
high plasticity. 
In this letter we report a series of Ti-Fe-Sn-Nb alloys comprised of supersaturated β-Ti 
with high phase stability, which exhibit not only superior plasticity but also significantly 
enhanced mechanical strength. The excellent properties were obtained by an efficient 
one-step casting procedure even without extra thermomechanical processing. The basic 
design strategies are: (1) single β-Ti structure should be retained by rapid cooling and 
by selecting the Fe content lower than the Ti-Fe eutectoid point (15 at. % Fe) to avoid 
the formation of brittle TiFe phase; (2) multiple solute elements e.g. Nb and Sn , are

Master alloys wit h the nominal composition s (in at. %) of Ti80Fe14Sn3Nb3, 
Ti82Fe12Sn3Nb3 and Ti84Fe10Sn3Nb3 were prepared by arc-melting using tungsten non -
consuming electrodes on a water -cooled copper hearth under a Ti gettered argon 
atmosphere. Cylinder samples with a diameter of 3 mm were fabricated by induction 
melting and Cu-mold casting in an argon atmosphere. The phase composition of the as-
cast rods was exa mined by X -ray diffraction (XRD) with Cu-Kα radiation. The lattice 
strain of the alloys was estimated from the obtained XRD patterns by TOPAS software. 
The fracture surfaces were studied using a scanning electron microscope (SEM) 
equipped with a field emission gun. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) 
investigations were carried out using a JEOL JEM 2010 microscope operating at 200 
kV and equipped with an EDXS of 0.1 keV resolutions. The sample for TEM were 
thinned first mechanically (to ∼10 µm thickness) and subsequently by an ion polishing 
technique (to electron -beam transparency) with liquid N 2 as a coolant . Compression 
tests were carried out on a uni versal testing machine at a strain rate of 1.0× 10 -4 s-1, 
where a strain gauge was used to calibrate and measure the true strain during 
compression. The tested specimens with a diameter of 3 mm and a length of 6 mm were 
cut from the as -cast rods. The top and bottom contact surfaces of t he samples were 
lubricated by boron nitride (BN) powder spray to reduce the effect of barreling under 
compression, which is a standard procedure to reduce friction forces between the 
sample and the tungsten carbide (WC) plates.

Fig. 3 shows the fracture morphologies of the alloys after compression tests. The 
Ti84Fe10Sn3Nb3 sample displayed a large shear crack inclined at 45° toward the load 
direction and a clear barreling instead of crashing to pieces (inset of Fig. 3a). Deformed 
grain boundaries are visible at th e outer surface (as denoted by the white arrows) due to 
the severe interaction against the slip bands . High density of the slip bands (black 
arrows) is clearly observed, and the direction of these bands shows no special 
relationship with the compression direction. Such high density of slip bands is believed 
to contribute most to the superior plastic deformation and the pronounced strain -
hardening [15, 16]. Fig. 3b shows the fracture surface inside the crack of 
Ti84Fe10Sn3Nb3, where nano-scale melting spots induced by shear fracture are 
distributed on the fracture surface. It is quite similar to the fracture-induced melting in 
the nanostructured composite alloys observed by He et al. [17, 18] , which r evealed a 
substantial temperature increase in the localized shear bands. Fig. 3c illustrates the 
fracture surface of Ti82Fe12Sn3Nb3, where network-like deformation bands 
homogeneously dispersed on the surface. Similar deformation bands were also observed 
on the fracture surface of Ti 80Fe14Sn3Nb3 at a higher magnification (Fig. 3d). The 
deformation bands are oriented in a specific direction and the spacing between each 
band is about 100 nm or less. We are not able to explore the structural details of these 
nano-scale deformation bands. Nevertheless, they are believed to correlate to the 
dislocation activity of the present alloys.

The superb yield stress (1.88 GPa) of Ti80Fe14Sn3Nb3 is well supported by the 
predominant slip deformation i.e. high stability to prevent from the activation of 
twinning. The 〈 〉 twinning is believed to improve the plasticity but result in

but still located on the slip side , meaning that slip keeps operating as the main 
deformation mode. This alloy showed a lower yield stress (1.6 GPa) compared to that of 
Ti80Fe14Sn3Nb3, which is a ctually superior or comparable to that of most Ti-based 
nanostructured composite alloys [17, 1 8]. Ti84Fe10Sn3Nb3 with lowest Fe content is 
located close to the slip/twinning boundary suggesting both slip and deformation 
twinning can be triggered in this alloy, which is well corresponded to its moderate yield 
strength and the best plasticity. The excellent mechanical strength of the 
multicomponent alloys is partly attributed to the solution hardening of the 
supersaturated β-Ti and most importantly to the effect of precipitation harden ing by the 
formation of pre -precipitates. The lattice strain, dislocations and distorted domains 
coursed by the pre-precipitates act as strong barriers restricting dislocation propagation 
[22]. On the other hand, A minor volume fraction of α” phase seem not to play an 
important role. The highly-stabilized β-Ti plays a significant role on the elevated yield 
strength. The superior plasticity mainly stems from t he dislocation-mediated slip in the 
single bcc phase . Furthermore, the accumulation of dislocations and interactions of the 
different slip systems [15] contribute to the obvious strain-hardening.

In summary, new beta -type Ti-Fe-Sn-Nb alloys have been developed showing a 
combination of high strength, significant strain -hardening and superior plasticity . The 
highly-stabilized Ti80Fe14Sn3Nb3 alloy exhibited the highest yield stress (1.88 GPa) , 
which is elevated by the slip deformation mode. Moreover, significant strain-hardening 
(520 MPa) was observed along with the entire plastic deformation (34 % plastic strain). 
The successful development of the current alloys provides an ef ficient method for the 
design of strong β-type Ti alloys.
