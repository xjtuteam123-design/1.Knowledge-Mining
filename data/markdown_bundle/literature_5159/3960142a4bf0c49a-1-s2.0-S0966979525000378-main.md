1. Introduction
The extraordinary reliance of human society on other metallic ma -
terials for daily survival means that advancements in metallurgy have 
the potential to impact the quality of life. They have been used in 
everything from big famous constructions, buildings, bridges, and cars 
to tiny common culinary utensils, and medical gadgets because of their 
superior qualities, and affordable prices. An alloy intended for high 
temperature applications must possess a stable phase at high tempera -
tures and provide high temperature strength. In addition to the phase 
stability and strength at high temperatures, the deterioration of mate -
rials due to oxidation is also a significant issue as ambient temperature 
rises during service. Therefore, developing high-entropy materials 
(HEMs) as alternative materials for various applications in the aerospace 
and defense industry and structural applications in different machinery 
is crucial to prioritize oxidation resistance for sustainability and 
longevity. This is necessary to prevent unintended failures and minimize 
capital loss, as well as to enhance mechanical properties [ 1 – 3 ].
Ni or Co-based alloys are prominently operated at temperatures 
ranging from 700 to 1200 
◦
C as a part of different components in gas 
turbine engines owing to their excellent high-temperature mechanical 
properties and oxidation resistance [ 4 – 7 ]. As far as oxidation resistance 
is concerned, conventional Co, Ni alloys prefer to form stable Cr or 
Al-containing oxide layers at higher temperatures which suggests the 
importance of either Al or Cr in the high temperature alloy system 
[ 8 – 10 ]. However, traditional superalloys have major limitations of 
density along with lower thrust-to-weight ratio, thermal efficiency, and 
fuel efficiency of aero engines due to the presence of rare-earth and 
refractory elements in these alloys [ 11 , 12 ]. To overcome these limita -
tions, researchers have come up with a concept of high entropy super -
alloys (HESAs) wherein the benefit of the cocktail effect in HEAs is 
exercised [ 1 ]. The major limitation of these studies is again the higher 
density of these HESAs owing to concentrated amounts of costly and 
heavy constituent elements like Ta, Nb or Hf in them [ 13 , 14 ].
It is thus, important to lower the density of these high temperature 
(HT) alloys by eliminating heavy refractory elements and replacing 
them with high melting point, low-density elements like vanadium (V) 
[ 15 – 17 ]. It is well established from the literature that, V is a critical 
element in superalloys to engineer high temperature strength by pro -
moting the formation of phases like V

Considering this, the present work attempts to reduce the density of a

(HTCAs) to understand the oxidation kinetics and mechanisms. A brief 
study of phase change, hardness and compressive yield strength as a 
function of V addition is also performed to know the processing abilities 
of these alloys.

3. Results and discussion
Fig. 1 (a – e) shows EBSD inverse pole figures (IPF) for HT-CAs as a 
function of V content. Single phase equiaxed grain structure evolved up 
to 15 at. % V which has subsequently changed to a two-phase structure 
beyond 15 at.% suggesting good solid solution forming ability of V till 
15 at.%. as shown in Fig. 1 (a – c) and the X-ray diffractograms Fig. 1 (g). 
Moreover, the grain morphology was found nearly wavy typically 
highlighting the signature of the as-cast state for V-5, V-10, and V-15 
alloys ( Fig. 1 (a – c)) [ 32 ]. The microstructure of V-20 is classical 
multi-phase forming γ -f.c.c., Cr-Ni-Si containing tetragonal and V
3
Si 
phases in an as-cast state ( Fig. 1 (g) ) [ 33 , 34 ]. V-25 alloy showed the 
formation of a tetragonal phase additional to f.c.c. phase as suggested by 
the XRD in Fig. 1 (g) .
The microstructural evolution shows that V-lean alloys tend to form 
single-phase solid solutions in the Ni-Co-Cr-Fe-Si matrix. In contrast, V- 
rich alloys tend to promote phase separation where V is mainly sepa -
rating with Cr and Si from the matrix [ 35 ]. Thus, 15 at. % V becomes 
critical in transiting the single-phase regime to multiphase regimes of 
these HT-CAs. Moreover, the separation of Cr and Si along with V in 
V-rich alloys is mainly associated with the negative enthalpy of mixing 
of the former with V as shown in Fig. S2 of the Supplementary file [ 35 ] 
and thus is very prominent in V-25 alloy having highest V content. The 
detailed study of this microstructural mutation beyond 20 at. % V will be 
carried out in our subsequent work. It is observed in the literature that, 
the presence of tetragonal phases deteriorates the mechanical response 
of the alloys due to limited slip systems but increases the hardness. 
Hence, it is expected that an increase in V will increase the hardness and 
alloys beyond 25 at. % V will not only be hard but also brittle due to the 
presence of extremely higher fraction tetragonal phases [ 33 , 34 ].

5, V-10, V-15, V-20, V-25, V-30) is presented as a function of vanadium 
concentration. The data reveal a gradual increase in hardness from V-5 
to V-20, with values rising incrementally from 163.32 ± 9 HV to 252.39 
± 11 HV. However, beyond V-20, a sharp escalation in hardness is
