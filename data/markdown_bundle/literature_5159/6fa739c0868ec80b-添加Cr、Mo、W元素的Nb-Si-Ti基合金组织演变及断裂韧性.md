1. Introduction 
Nb – Si based alloys have potential to replace nickel-base superalloy 
in high-temperature structural applications due to their excellent high- 
temperature strength and low density compared with nickel-base su -
peralloy [ 1 – 3 ]. In Nb – Si based alloys, the body centered cubic structure 
of Nb solid-solution (Nbss) phase provides the room-temperature 
toughness, while the brittle intermetallic Nb
5
Si
3 
phase provides 
high-temperature strength [ 4 , 5 ]. However, the insufficient fracture 
toughness and ductility of Nb – Si based alloys hinder their application. 
Previous studies have reported that the fracture toughness of Nb – Si 
based alloys can be significantly improved by optimizing alloy compo -
sition and processing technologies [ 6 – 8 ]. The goal of these methods is to 
improve solution strengthening, microstructure, and constituent phases. 
Alloying is an effective method to improve the microstructure and 
mechanical properties of NbSi based alloys. In recent years, many 
alloying elements have been added into Nb – Si alloys to improve their 
poor high-temperature oxidation resistance and room-temperature 
fracture toughness, and some alloying elements can change the solidi -
fication path, promote the eutectoid reaction of Nb
3

Nb

eutectic, and affect phase constitutions [ 8 – 15 ]. Element Ti 
addition has been found to improve the room-temperature fracture 
toughness of Nbss phase due to the solution strengthening, and a greater 
number of element Ti has usually added into Nb – Si based alloys, and 
developed into the ternary Nb – Si – Ti based alloys [ 8 – 12 ]. Element Cr is 
usually added into Nb – Si based alloys for improving oxidation resistance 
and fracture toughness; however, the heavy addition of element Cr 
promotes the formation of Laves phase of Cr
2

considered to be harm to room-temperature fracture toughness [ 11 – 14 ]. 
Element Mo addition has been found to promote the formation of a 
unique microstructure that only contains Nb

in Nb – Si based alloys, and element Mo provides the solution strength -

of Cr and Mo, which has been studied recently in detail, element W 
addition increases the volume fraction of Nb
5
Si
3 
phase and decreases the 
element content of Si in Nbss phase [ 17 ]. 
Despite alloying elements of Cr, Mo and W have been studied in 
detail, including the microstructure, phase constitutions and mechanical 
properties, but the common influences of these elements on mechanical 
properties and microstructure in Nb – Si – Ti based alloys have not been 
well understood. Therefore, the effects of alloying elements of Cr, Mo 
and W on microstructure and mechanical properties are analyzed in this

2. Materials and methods 
The intrinsic brittleness of NbSi alloys limit its application. The 
hypoeutectic NbSi alloys generally have higher room-temperature 
fracture toughness compared with hypereutectic alloys, since the 
hypoeutectic NbSi alloys usually have high Nbss content. 16 at.% Si was 
selected in this study for balance the room-temperature strength and 
fracture toughness. Peierls-Nabarro barrier energy of Nbss phase can be 
reduced by addition of Ti, which is beneficial to the room-temperature 
fracture toughness of NbSi alloys. However, Ti base solid solution will 
form when the Ti content exceeds 25 at.%, which is harm to fracture 
toughness [18 – 20]. Therefore, Nb – 16Si – 20Ti is selected as the base 
alloy in this paper. The master alloy ingots with the different nominal 
compositions of Nb – 16Si – 22Ti, Nb – 16Si – 22Ti – 8Cr, Nb – 16Si – 22Ti – 
6Mo, Nb – 16Si – 22Ti – 2W, Nb – 16Si – 22Ti – 2W – 8Cr, Nb – 16Si – 22Ti – 6Mo – 
8Cr, Nb – 16Si – 22Ti – 2W – 6Mo and Nb – 16Si – 22Ti – 2W – 6Mo – 8Cr were 
prepared using the vacuum non-consumable arc-melting. All alloys were 
melted five times to homogenize the compositions of ingots. Micro -
structures were observed by the scanning electron microscopy (SEM, 
Quanta 200FEG) in the backscattered electron mode (BSE). The phases 
constitution was analyzed by the X-ray diffraction (XRD) under 40 kV at 
the scanning speed with 8
◦
/min, using the Cu-K α radiation. The elec -
trode discharge machine was performed to cut 2.0 mm deep slit notches 
in specimens for room-temperature fracture toughness testing using an 
electronic universal mechanical testing machine (Instron5569). The 
computational method details have been previously described in 
Ref. [ 18 ]. The volume fractions of various phases present in investigated 
alloys were calculated by the image analysis software on microstruc -
tures using SEM.
