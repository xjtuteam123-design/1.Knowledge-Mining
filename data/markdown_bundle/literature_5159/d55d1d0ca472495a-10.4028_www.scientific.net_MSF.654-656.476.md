Microstructure and High Temperature Strength in Fe 3Al Base Alloys 
Containing Fine Carbide Particles 
Satoru Kobayashi 1, a , Ryo Makihara 2,b and Takayuki Takasugi 1,2,c 
1Osaka Center for Industrial Materials Research, Institute of Materials Research, Tohoku University

Keywords: Fe 3Al, precipitation strengthening, high temperature strength 
Abstract. The precipitation of carbide particles and high temperature strength in a Fe 3Al base alloy 
with C, Cr and Mo were investigated. The κ -Fe 3AlC (E2 1) and M2C (B8 1) phases precipitated in the 
temperature range between 1000 °C and 600 °C in periods up to 10 h. The κ -Fe 3AlC phase tended to 
precipitate in a film-like morphology on grain boundaries by annealing at relatively high 
temperatures. The M 2C phase was dispersed in a fine needle shape within grains by annealing at 700 
°C. The film-like κ precipitates covering grain boundaries as well as finely dispersed M 2C particles 
was suggested to contribute to strengthening at 600 °C. 
Introduction 
Fe 
3Al-based alloys with B2- or D0 3-ordered structures are considered to be promising structural 
materials for high temperature applications due to excellent high temperature corrosion resistance in 
oxidising and sulphidising atmospheres [1], low materials costs and relatively low density compared 
to steels [2, 3]. One of the major obstacles in bringing these alloys into application is their poor 
strength at high temperatures above 600 °C [2, 3]. 
Precipitation strengthening is one of the effective methods to improve high temperature strength. 
Precipitation may occur in grain interior (GI) and on grain boundaries (GBs). Strengthening due to 
fine particle distributions in GI was extensively studied in Fe 3Al alloys [4]. However, to date few 
studies on the contribution of GB precipitates have been carried out [5]. 
Phase equilibria and the kinetics of carbide phase formation were previously reported in the 
Fe 3Al-C-Cr-Mo systems [6-8]. The present paper investigated the morphology of carbide phases 
formed on GB and in GI, and their contributions to high temperature strength in a Fe 3Al base alloy 
with C, Cr and Mo. 
Experimental Procedures 
The nominal composition of the alloy studied was Fe-27Al-0.6C-2.0Cr-0.9Mo (at.%). This alloy was 
prepared by arc melting under an argon atmosphere and casting as a 100g ingot button. The ingot was 
hot rolled and cut into pieces. The pieces were heat-treated at 1200 
°C, in the A2(bcc) single-phase 
field, for 10 min and followed by water quenching. Water quenched samples were subsequently heat 
treated at temperatures between 1000 °C and 600 °C for periods from 1 min to 10 h. 
Microstructure of the heat treated samples was observed with a scanning electron microscope 
(SEM) and a transmission electron microscope (TEM). For microstructure observation, the 
heat-treated samples were cut and the cross-sections were ground, mechanically polished with Al 2O3 
polishing powders down to 0.1 µ m, and finally electropolished. TEM samples were prepared by 
twin-jet polishing.

Results and Discussion 
The Morphology of Carbide Precipitates. Figure 1 shows the TTT diagram for the formation of the 
carbide phases in the Fe 
3Al A2(B2) matrix phase. The grain size of the matrix phase was 0.7 mm. 
Two types of carbide phases precipitated: κ -Fe 3AlC (E2 1) and M2C (B8 1) phases. The formation 
kinetics of the κ phase was faster than that of the M 2C phase in the entire temperature range 
heat-treated. Figure 2 shows the morphology of κ precipitates in the alloy heat-treated at a relatively 
high temperature of 1000 °C. The κ phase precipitated in a film shape on the GBs (Fig. 2). The 
coverage of the precipitates on the GBs was more than 90 % by annealing at 1000 °C. The κ phase 
precipitated in a needle shape in GI at a low density (Fig. 2). Figure 3 shows the morphology of the 
M 2C phase formed on GBs and in GI in the alloy heat-treated at a relatively low temperature of 700 °C 
for 1h. The M 2C phase precipitated in a grain shape on the GB, and the coverage of the precipitates 
was less than 50% (Fig. 3(a)). The TEM bright field image and selected area diffraction pattern 
indicates that the M2C phase precipitated in GI with a needle shape, gathering on the lines which align 
on the [100] directions of the matrix phase (Fig. 3(b)). The averaged spacing of the lines was 360 nm. 
The mechanism of such precipitation is not well understood, but it might be related to the nucleation 
site of the κ phase which precipitates in advance and dissolved at 700 °C [9].

following microstructures were created: 
 
(1) GI M

(2) GI M 2C particles with grain-shaped M2C particles partially covering GBs, as shown in Fig. 3. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
The microstructure (1) was created by heat treatment process (a)+(b)+(c) and the (2) by (a)+(c) in the 
TTT diagram (Fig. 1). In the microstructure (1), the grain boundaries are covered by film-like 
κ 
precipitates and M 2C grains (Fig. 4(a)). The distribution of M2C particles in GI was more 
homogeneous in (1) than in (2), and the interparticle spacing was about 400 nm, a little wider in (1) 
than that in (2) (see Table 1).

mechanism. Orowan stress ( ∆σ o) can be expressed as 
 
 ∆σ o = 2.4Gb/ λ (1) 
 
where G is the shear modulus of the matrix phase, b is the Burger’s vector and λ is the mean 
interparticle spacing. The ∆σ o values were calculated, by taking G = 54 GPa, b = 0.25 nm and the

sample with small interparticle spacing (i.e., with the microstructure (2)). The higher 0.2% proof 
stress in the microstructure (1) would therefore be caused by the film-like κ phase covering GBs.

Summary 
In the present paper we investigated the precipitation of carbide particles and high temperature 
strength in a Fe 
3Al base alloy containing C, Cr and Mo. The results are: 
(1) The κ -Fe 3AlC (E2 1) and M2C (B8 1) phases precipitated in the temperature range between 1000 °C 
and 600 °C in periods up to 10 h. 
(2) The κ -Fe 3AlC phase tended to precipitate in a film-like morphology on grain boundaries by 
annealing at relatively high temperatures. 
(3) The M 2C phase was dispersed in a fine needle shape within grains by annealing at 700 °C. 
(4) The film-like κ precipitates covering grain boundaries as well as finely dispersed M 2C particles 
was suggested to contribute to strengthening at 600 °C.
