Abstract: Titanium and titanium alloys exhibit low density, high strength and excellent
corrosion resistance, and have become workhorses in modern aerospace and chemical
processing industries. Recently, Ti-6Al-4V alloy has been manufactured through metal
injection molding (MIM). MIM is widely utilized in various manufacturing industries
due to the near-net-forming process, especially in the preparation of difficult-to-deform
high-temperature alloys. Sintering temperature is vital to ensuring good tensile mechanical
properties. In this work, Ti-6Al-4V alloy was sintered under various sintering temperatures,
from 1150 to 1250 ◦C. The effect of sintering temperature on the mechanical properties was
evaluated. The tensile test results exhibited that a yield strength of 896.35 MPa, an ultimate
tensile strength of 980.83 MPa and plastic elongation of 9.61% were produced at 1150◦C for
4 h; a yield strength of 877.23 MPa, an ultimate tensile strength of 981.92 MPa and plastic
elongation of 7.74% were produced at 1200 ◦C for 4 h; and a yield strength of 923.33 MPa,
an ultimate tensile strength of 1002.24 MPa and plastic elongation of 6.57% were produced
at 1250 ◦C for 4 h.
Keywords: metal injection molding (MIM); Ti-6Al-4V alloy; sintering temperatures; ten-
sile properties
1. Introduction
Titanium and titanium alloys are widely used in the manufacturing of some key com-
ponents in the aerospace, weaponry and marine fields, and in the chemical industry and
medical treatment, due to their high specific strength, low density, unrivaled corrosion
resistance and good oxidation resistance at relatively high temperatures, which can effec-
tively improve the safety and reliability of engineering equipment [ 1–5]. Titanium and
titanium alloys are relatively expensive materials, and their traditional processing, such as
machining and casting, greatly increases their cost [6]. In addition, owing to poor workabil-
ity, it is difficult to use them to produce parts with complicated shapes. Therefore, in the
processing of titanium and titanium alloys, near-net-shape technology has played an im-
portant role [7,8]. Metal injection molding (MIM) is suitable for the large-scale production
of small and complex-shaped titanium and titanium alloy parts [9–13], as it combines the
versatility and high productivity of plastic injection molding with the sintered powder met-
allurgy technique [14,15]. This method with low production costs has attracted widespread
attention in the metal industry due to its near-net-shape technology and high material
utilization. [16–18]. In recent years, MIM has become an advanced processing method

for the low-cost production of complex-shaped titanium and titanium alloy parts [19,20].
During this process, the feedstock is obtained by mixing titanium powder with suitable
binders. After mixing, the feedstock is injected into a mold through an injection molding
machine. Then, the binders are extracted from the molded parts during the debinding
process. Finally, the debound part is sintered at an appropriate temperature, for a suitable
duration and in an appropriate atmosphere to obtain parts with acceptable mechanical
and physical properties [21]. In high-performance structural applications, titanium alloys
composed of α and β structures are usually suitable. The most commonly used alloy in this
category is Ti-6Al-4V , accounting for 50% of the total usage of titanium alloys. This alloy is
highly favored due to its outstanding workability and overall mechanical properties.
As mentioned earlier, a typical MIM process consists of four processing steps: feed-
stock formulation (formulating binders), injection molding, debinding and sintering [22].
Nowadays, some researchers focus mainly on formulating binders through metal injection
molding. Choosing the appropriate binder system is crucial for the success of MIM tech-
nology. This is because the performance of the binder can affect the distribution of metal
particles, the forming process and the final performance of the sintered parts [ 18]. Ferri
et al. selected a wax-based binder composed of 60 wt.% paraffin, 35 wt.% ethyl acetate
and 5 wt.% stearic acid to prepare Ti-6Al-4V alloy with a tensile strength of 800 MPa and
an elongation of 15% [23]. Guo et al. utilized a binder composed of 63 wt.% paraffin wax,
12 wt.% polyethylene glycol, 14 wt.% polyethylene, 10 wt.% polypropylene and 1 wt.%
stearic acid homogenized with Ti-6Al-4V alloy powder for injection molding [24]. A critical
loading of 72 vol.% was obtained by the feedstock based on the binder. However, sintering
temperature is the most influential on the properties of MIMed titanium alloys [25]. Ergül
and Özkan sintered Ti-6Al-4V alloy under vacuum at different temperatures and for differ-
ent durations [12]. The maximum ultimate tensile strength was obtained at 1275 ◦C for 10 h.
Mohamad Nor et al. optimized the sintering parameter to prepare Ti-6Al-4V alloy [ 26].
They found that a tensile strength of 934.33 MPa could be attained when Ti-6Al-4V was
sintered at 1200 ◦C for 1 h, but no information on elongation was given. Obasi et al. found
that the maximum temperature is important in terms of the tensile strength of Ti-6Al-4V
alloy [27]. They prepared Ti-6Al-4V alloy with yield strength of more than 750 MPa, UTS of
more than 860 MPa and plastic elongation of about 14.3% when the maximum temperature
was 1350 ◦C. Wang and Shi used a wax-based binder composed of 1 wt.% stearic acid,
35 wt.% low-density polyethylene and 60 wt.% paraffin wax homogenized with Ti-6Al-4V
alloy powder for injection molding [28]. The authors found that the viscosity of the raw ma-
terial decreased with the extension of mixing time but increased with an increase in powder
loading when studying its rheological properties. However, only a few people studied
the effect of sintering temperatures on the mechanical properties and microstructure of
injection-molded Ti-6Al-4V alloy.
Therefore, in the present study, the effect of sintering temperature on the relative
sintered density, microstructure characteristics and mechanical properties of Ti-6Al-4V
alloy prepared through MIM was systematically investigated. Three sintering temperatures
of 1150 ◦C, 1200 ◦C and 1250 ◦C were employed.

The granular material was injected using a Battenfeld BA 250 CDC injection mold-
ing machine. The injection molding parameters were as follows: the injection pressure
was 80 MPa, the holding pressure was 65 MPa, the injection speed was 50 mm/s and
the injection temperature and mold temperature were 180 ◦C and 90 ◦C, respectively.
The debinding stage can be divided into two steps, namely the first debinding step for
removing low-molecular-weight polymer and the second debinding step for removing
high-molecular-weight polymer [15]. In the first debinding step, the low-molecular-weight
polymer is removed, and interconnected pore pathways are formed to facilitate the removal
of the high-molecular-weight polymer in the second debinding step. In this study, the
debinding stage was divided into catalytic and thermal debinding. Oxalic acid was used to
remove the majority of the binders for 4.5 h at 130 ◦C. Then, drying took place in a 50 ◦C
oven for 6 h. The final removal of the remaining binder and subsequent sintering were
carried out in a single-step heat treatment run (Table 3). Sintering was performed in a
vacuum of 10−5 mbar at 1150 ◦C, 1200 ◦C and 1250 ◦C, respectively, with a holding time of
4 h with 5 ◦C/min heating and cooling rates.

3.2. Microstructure and Porosity
Figure 4 shows SEM images of cross-sections of the tensile test specimens of Ti-6Al-
4V sintered at 1150 ◦C, 1200 ◦C and 1250 ◦C, respectively. All the samples exhibit the
typical microstructure of Ti-6Al-4V alloy irrespective of the sintering temperature. The
microstructure consists of lamellar α/β colonies. The α-lamellae appear dark gray, while
the β-phase between the α-lamellae is visible in a light gray color [ 31]. Moreover, the
pores are visible as black dots. The existence of points in the microstructure of sintering
may be related to incomplete debinding and removal of residual adhesives during the
sintering process. However, it has been reported that points in the microstructure of Ti MIM
components can lead to a decrease in tensile properties [32]. The shape of the pores sintered
at 1150 ◦C (Figure 4a,b) is mainly rectangular, with a maximum size of 2 µm × 12 µm. At
1200 ◦C and 1250 ◦C, the shape of the pores is mainly circular, with a maximum diameter of
10 µm. However, the number of pores decreases with an increase in sintering temperature.
This observation agrees with the relative density. The colony size was measured as 95 µm,
105 µm and 130 µm on average when the sintering temperature is 1150 ◦C, 1200 ◦C and
1250 ◦C, respectively.

3.3. Tensile Test Properties 
Figure 5 shows the statistical results of th e room-temperature tensile properties of 
specimens at diﬀerent sintering temperatures. The results of the tensile tests are also sum-
marized in Table 6. The MIM-processed spec imens sintered at 1150 °C show a yield 
strength (YS) of 896.35 MPa, an ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of 980.83 MPa and plastic 
elongation of 9.61%, which are similar values to the specimens sintered at 1200 °C (a yield 
strength of 877.23 MPa, an ultimate tensile strength of 981.92 MPa and plastic elongation 
of 7.74%). When the sintering temperature is 1250 °C, it leads to a signiﬁcantly higher yield 
strength of 923.33 MPa, an ultimate tensile strength of 1002.24 MPa and low plastic elon-
gation of 6.57%. The tensile properties of Titanium alloys produced by powder technology 
are determined by the alloying elements and their contents, impurity contents, relative 
sintered density and microstructure [33]. Th e yield and ultimate tensile strengths gener-
ally increase with an increase in relative sintered density (decreasing porosity) and with 
an increase in the impurity content [33]. The lower the porosity in MIM, the be tter the 
yield strength and ultimate tensile strength [34], which was the case in this work. On the 
other hand, Bian et al. obtained higher yield stress and ultimate tensile stress due to a high 
content of O [27]. In their work, Ti-6Al-V alloy samples with 4000 ppm oxygen were fab-
ricated through metal injection molding, and a signiﬁcantly higher yield strength of 905 ± 
10 MPa and ultimate tensile strength of 960 ± 20 MPa were obtained. In this work, the 
higher density of the specimen and impurity contents are the main reasons for the be tter 
mechanical properties of the sintered specimen [35]. However, in this work, it is suggested 
that sintering at a lower temperature on MIM-processed Ti-6Al-4V can obtain better com-
prehensive mechanical properties, which is in accordance with the investigations of other 
scholars [27]. The reason is that the microstructure became coarse because of sintering at 
higher temperatures, which might have deteriorated the plastic elongation.

4. Conclusions 
Herein, the inﬂuences of sintering temperatures on the microstructure and mechani-
cal properties of Ti-6Al-4V alloy fabricated through MIM were investigated at sintering 
temperatures of 1150 °C, 1200 °C and 1250 °C. The conclusions were as follows: 
1. The relative density of MIM-processed Ti-6Al-4V alloys increases with an increase in 
sintering temperature, and a maximum relative density of 97.919% is achieved at 
1250 °C. 
2. The oxygen contents of the specimens after sintering at 1150 °C, 1200 °C and 1250 °C 
are 0.287 wt.%, 0.288 wt.% and 0.323 wt.%, respectively.

4. Conclusions
Herein, the influences of sintering temperatures on the microstructure and mechani-
cal properties of Ti-6Al-4V alloy fabricated through MIM were investigated at sintering
temperatures of 1150 ◦C, 1200 ◦C and 1250 ◦C. The conclusions were as follows:
1. The relative density of MIM-processed Ti-6Al-4V alloys increases with an increase
in sintering temperature, and a maximum relative density of 97.919% is achieved
at 1250 ◦C.
2. The oxygen contents of the specimens after sintering at 1150 ◦C, 1200 ◦C and 1250 ◦C
are 0.287 wt.%, 0.288 wt.% and 0.323 wt.%, respectively.
3. The microstructure consists of lamellar α/β colonies and black dots, and the number
of pores decreases with an increase in sintering temperature. The colony size increases
with increasing sintering temperature.
When the sintering temperature is 1250 ◦C, it leads to a significantly higher yield
strength of 923.33 MPa, an ultimate tensile strength of 1002.24 MPa and low plastic elonga-
tion of 6.57%. However, when the sintering temperature is 1150 ◦C, the MIM-processed
specimen exhibits better comprehensive mechanical properties for Ti-6Al-4V alloy, with
a 896.35 MPa yield strength, a 980.83 MPa ultimate tensile strength and 9.61% plastic
elongation. The reason is that the microstructure of Ti-6Al-4V alloy is finer and the oxygen
content is lower.
