Thermally Stable Heterogeneous-Grained Refractory High-Entropy
Alloy Offering Superior Strength from 77 to 973 K

ABSTRACT: Achieving a robust strength−ductility balance
across a wide temperature range remains a major challenge for
refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs). In this study, we design a
cold-workable NbTaTiHf-based RHEA with a thermally stable
heterogeneous grain structure created through thermomechanical
processing. This tailored microstructure enables exceptional
mechanical performance, from cryogenic (77 K) to elevated
temperatures (973 K), achieving tensile strengths exceeding 1.8
GPa at 77 K and maintaining over 900 MPa at 973 K. The primary
mechanism responsible for this performance is a highly refined
heterogeneous microstructure, which is rendered thermally stable
even after extended annealing at high temperatures by the
concentrated refractory elements that entail sluggish kinetics. As
such, the hetero-deformation-induced (HDI) strengthening mechanisms can be maintained effectively even in high-temperature
regimes. Our work offers a practical design approach for next-generation RHEAs with superior mechanical properties across a wide
range of temperatures.

From the perspective of microstructural design, heteroge-
neous structured metallic materials have recently attracted
substantial attention due to their superior mechanical perform-
ance and broad processing adaptability. These materials consist
of grains, lamellae, or phases with markedly different
mechanical properties, including strength, hardness, and
strain-hardening capacity.
15−18
This microstructural inhomo-
geneity promotes hetero-deformation-induced (HDI)
strengthening and strain hardening, through which the material
exhibits a superior combination of strength and ductility. HDI
strengthening has been extensively demonstrated in various
alloys, particularly at room and cryogenic temperatures.
19−21
Microscopically, plastic strain incompatibility at the interfaces
between hard and soft regions leads to the accumulation of
extra geometrically necessary dislocations (GNDs), which add
long-range internal stresses, namely, back stress in the softer
domains and forward stress in the harder ones. This stress
coupling contributes to nonlocal kinematic hardening, thereby
promoting strain hardening behavior and delaying the onset of
strain localization and necking.
22,23
As a result, heterogeneous
structures can effectively alleviate the conventional strength−
ductility trade-off observed in homogeneous materials,
achieving superior combinations of mechanical properties.

As shown in Figures 1a,b, the HS alloy features a
heterogeneous microstructure with an area-weighted average
grain size of ∼8 μm, consisting of ultrafine (<1 μm, recovered
and partially recrystallized), fine (fully recrystallized), and
deformed (limited recovery after cold work) grains, which
promote hetero-deformation-induced (HDI) strengthening
across a broad temperature range. The fully recrystallized
grains display low grain orientation spread (GOS) values
(<2°), while incompletely recrystallized grains show signifi-
cantly higher GOS values, as shown in Figure S4a. The
incompletely recrystallized grains, in a partially recovered state,
retain dislocations (Figure 1c and Figure S4b), similar to prior
observations in heterogeneous lamella-structured titanium.
29

illustrated in Figures 1d,e. X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis in
Figure 1f confirms that both the HS and FR alloys maintain a
single-body-centered-cubic (BCC) phase, indicating excellent
phase stability during thermal processing.

The mechanical properties of the NTTHRB-HS RHEA were
evaluated over a broad temperature range, from cryogenic to
973 K (see Methods in the Supporting Information for
details). As shown in Figure 2a, the room-temperature tensile
performance of the HS and FR alloys reveals the beneficial
effect of the microstructural heterogeneity. At room temper-
ature, the HS alloy exhibits a yield strength of approximately
1100 MPa and an elongation to failure of ∼14%, representing a
∼21% increase in yield strength compared to the FR
counterpart. Importantly, this enhancement is obtained
without premature fracture or significant loss of ductility,
confirming that the NTTHRB-HS alloy retains an excellent
strength−ductility synergy. These observations are consistent
with prior reports on HDI strengthening in heterogeneous-
structured metals.
17,23
Figure 2b further demonstrates the outstanding tensile
properties of the HS alloy across the full testing temperature
range. At cryogenic temperature (77 K), the HS alloy achieves
an exceptional yield strength exceeding 1.8 GPa while retaining
a fracture elongation of ∼10%, highlighting an excellent
synergy between strength and ductility. With increasing
temperature, the alloy maintains robust tensile strengths of
956 MPa at 623 K and 974 MPa at 773 K. Notably, even at
973 K, the tensile strength remains high at 917 MPa,
demonstrating excellent high-temperature mechanical stability.
In the intermediate temperature range (623−973 K), the alloy
exhibits similar tensile strength, indicating a remarkable degree
of temperature insensitivity�consistent with previously
reported trends in other high-performance RHEAs.
30−32

room temperature, a well-defined work-hardening rate plateau
is observed in Figure 2c, contributing to the improved

superalloy.

To further investigate the thermal stability of the
heterogeneous structure, the HS alloy was annealed at 973 K
for durations ranging from 2 to 16 h. The microstructural
evolution of the alloy was characterized through EBSD; the
results are presented in Figure 3. As shown in panels (a) to (c),
the heterogeneous microstructure�comprising fine, ultrafine,
and deformed grains�remains largely intact throughout the
annealing process, even after extended thermal exposure.
Specifically, the structural heterogeneity observed in the 2 h
annealed specimen (Figure 3a) is still clearly visible in the 8 h
(Figure 3b) and 16 h (Figure 3c) annealed specimens. The fine
grains continue to exhibit a fully recrystallized state, while the
ultrafine grains retain partial recovery with dislocations present,
further supporting the structural stability of the heterogeneous
arrangement over time.

tensile strength after each annealing period. Figure 3d shows
that the tensile strength of the HS alloy remains nearly

treatment. This minimal variation in strength demonstrates
that the alloy retains its microstructure, even under prolonged

The persistence of the heterogeneous-grained microstruc-
ture, even after prolonged exposure to high temperatures, can
be attributed to two key factors. First, the high melting points
of the constituent elements in the NTTHRB RHEA, such as
Nb, Ta, Hf, and Re, ensure the alloy’s overall thermal stability,
preventing significant grain coarsening or phase transitions that
are typically seen in alloys with lower melting points under
similar thermal conditions. Second, the sluggish diffusion
kinetics, inherent in complex concentrated alloys such as
RHEAs, restrict the migration of grain boundaries, thereby
inhibiting rapid grain growth and preserving the heterogeneous
structure at high temperatures. These characteristics set the
NTTHRB RHEA apart from other heterogeneous-structured
materials, where microstructural coarsening often occurs at
elevated temperatures due to faster atomic diffusion and the
presence of lower-melting-point elements. Together, these
factors ensure that the engineered heterogeneous structure
remains thermally stable and structurally robust even under
extended high-temperature exposure.
To elucidate the underlying mechanisms responsible for the
excellent mechanical performance of the HS RHEA across a
wide temperature range, loading−unloading−reloading cyclic
tensile tests were conducted to assess the presence and
evolution of HDI stress at different temperatures. As shown in
Figure 4a, the true stress−strain curves obtained at 298 and
623 K exhibit clear hysteresis loops during each unloading−
reloading cycle, indicative of a pronounced Bauschinger effect
at both temperatures (see enlarged view in Figure 4b). The
evolution of the HDI stress (σ

At both 298 and 623 K, the HDI stress increases
progressively with plastic strain, thereby contributing to the
observed strain hardening. Specifically, the HDI stress during
the first loading−unloading−reloading cycle was measured to
be 507 MPa at room temperature and 409 MPa at 623 K�
substantial values that play a critical role in elevating strength.
The coexisting HDI stress and effective stress underpin the
alloy’s superior strength. Notably, the persistence of a
significant HDI stress even at elevated temperatures provides
a key mechanism for maintaining high strength in the

It is important to note that the plastic response in
heterogeneous structures involves two types of dislocations:
statistically stored dislocations (SSDs) and GNDs.
46
Unlike
SSDs, which generate short-range stresses and contribute to
dislocation hardening, GNDs accommodate the strain
incompatibility between soft and hard domains.
47
Both SSDs
and GNDs contribute to flow stress by impeding dislocation
motion. While strain hardening in homogeneous deformation
is primarily governed by SSDs, GNDs play a more dominant
role in the plastic deformation of heterogeneous materials.
48,49
The persistence of this highly refined and heterogeneous
grain structure, even at elevated temperatures, facilitates the
continued activation of HDI strengthening during high-
temperature deformation, which helps explain the alloy’s
remarkable strength retention even at 973 K. The GND
density maps (Figures 5a

concentrations at the grain boundaries and interfacial regions
relative to grain interiors, reflecting pronounced plastic strain
gradients.
14,29
To further elucidate these findings, TEM
analysis of the fractured specimen post-deformation at 973 K
(Figures 5d,e) was further performed, which reveals dense
dislocation tangles formed near the soft−hard interface. In
non-homogenous deforming alloys, plastic flow typically
initiates in the softer regions and progressively extends into
the harder regions with increasing strain.
17
Consistent with this
mechanism, the fully recrystallized grains were nearly
dislocation-free prior to deformation (Figure 1c), but TEM
analysis of a specimen strained to 2% at 973 K (Figure S8)
revealed a marked increase in dislocation density within the
soft grains near soft−hard interfaces, consistent with the
conventional HDI model.
17,18
After tensile straining to
fracture, both soft and hard regions were observed to have
been actively involved in deformation, each showing significant
dislocation accumulation (Figures 5d,e). The heterogeneous
structure with refined grains and high density of stored
dislocations, which remains stable across the tested temper-
ature range, contributes significantly to the alloy’s high yield
strength (see analysis in Supplementary Text S1) and
enhanced tensile properties at elevated temperatures.
50,51
The superior mechanical performance of the heterogeneous-
grained NTTHRB RHEA at elevated temperatures is rooted in
its thermally stable and architecturally refined microstructure.
Even after prolonged annealing at 973 K, the heterogeneous
grain structure remains largely intact, indicating high thermal
stability�a critical prerequisite for elevated-temperature
structural applications. This structural resilience enables the
continued HDI strengthening to persist up to 973 K, where the
NTTHRB-HS alloy retains a tensile strength exceeding 900
MPa�surpassing most reported RHEAs that typically suffer
rapid strength degradation at elevated temperatures. Also, the
persistent strain partitioning and the accumulation of GNDs at
the interfaces between non-homogeneously deforming regions
promote the synergy of strength and ductility from cryogenic
to elevated temperatures. Importantly, the design strategy of
stabilizing heterogeneously refined structures is generalizable
to other RHEA chemistries. The vast compositional design
space of RHEAs provides opportunities to tailor metastable
phases and thermomechanical processing windows,
25
establish-
ing grain structure engineering as a general paradigm for
optimizing the performance of refractory alloys. Furthermore,
the NTTHRB-HS alloy developed here highlights the need for
future studies on oxidation resistance and fracture toughness�
properties essential for ensuring service life, structural
reliability, and damage tolerance�thereby supporting its
potential deployment in extreme service environments.
52
In summary, a cold-workable NbTaTiHf-based RHEA with
a thermally stable heterogeneous microstructure has been
successfully developed by using a tailored thermomechanical
processing route. The resulting heterogeneous-grained alloy
not only demonstrates excellent tensile properties at room
temperature but also maintains outstanding strength−ductility
across a broad temperature range�from cryogenic (77 K) to
elevated temperatures (973 K). The alloy achieves a
remarkable balance between high strength and ductility
throughout this wide temperature range, significantly out-
performing several conventional high-performance alloys,
including other RHEAs. This superior performance is
attributed to the thermally stable heterogeneous-grained
structure (grain boundaries and dislocations accumulated at
the boundaries between different-sized grains) and the
resultant HDI strengthening, which persist in the refractory-
element-enriched alloy. Notably, such a strengthening remains
effective even at elevated temperatures, enabling robust
strength retention. These findings underscore the importance
of alloy design strategies that combine high-melting-point base
elements with minor alloying additions to stabilize the refined
heterogeneous grains and dislocation structures, maintaining
desirable deformation mechanisms across a broad temperature
range. This study demonstrates the effectiveness of HDI
strengthening in single-phase RHEAs and presents a viable
pathway for designing next-generation structural materials for
extreme service environments.
