Abstract. The effect of solution and aging temperatures on mi crostructure and tensile property of 
Ti-5Al-5Mo-5V-3Cr-1Fe Alloy was investigated. The r esults showed that the tensile strength 
lowered, when the solution and aging temperature ro se respectively. The alloy with different heat 
treatments showed better tensile strength totally, i.e. the minimum level of ultimate and yield strength 
passed 1280 MPa and 1245MPa. The highest ultimate t ensile strength could reach 1445 MPa. After 
(α+β) solution and aging treatment, the microstructure consists of primary α phase and transformed β 
phase with fine secondary α phase precipitating to improve the tensile strength effectively. 
Introduction 
Over the past few years, beta titanium alloys have continued on an increasing basis to be a very 
important class of materials. These alloys are very useful for structural forgings applications due to 
their high strength –to-density ration, good harden ability and corrosion resistance. These materials 
can obtain highest strength level throughout precipitating fine alpha phase in the final aging treatment 
[1-3] . 
Ti-5Al-5Mo-5V-3Cr-1Fe is a high strength beta alloy based on Timetal 555 
(Ti-5Al-5V-5Mo-3Cr-0.6Fe). Timetal 555 is a high st rength near-beta titanium alloy designed for 
large size forgings such as structural airframe app lications due to its highest strengths while still 
retaining reasonable ductility [4-10] . However, the works on the microstructure and prop erty of 
Ti-5Al-5Mo-5V-3Cr-1Fe alloy are limited. In this paper, the effect of solution and aging temperature 
on microstructure and property was investigated by using samples continuously air cooled and aged at 
various temperatures. 
2. Experimental 
Ti-5Al-5Mo-5V-3Cr-1Fe alloy was received from Baoji Titanium Industry Co., Ltd, China , 
having the following chemical composition (wt%): 5.23Al, 5.02Mo, 4.85V, 2.81Cr, 0.86Fe, 0.014C, 
0.0010H, 0.101O, 0.018N, balanced Ti. The material ingot was produced by casting it twice in the 
consumable electrode vacuum furnace into a cylindrical mold of 150 mm in diameter, hot forged in 
the β phase field, and then rolled into a billet 36mm in diameter in the (α+ β) phase field, followed by 
air cooling to room temperature. The original micro structure of the alloy is presented in Fig.1. The 
mean β transus temperature was determined by metallography and x-ray diffraction as 840 ℃±5 ℃. 
All mechanical properties of heat treated samples w ere examined in this article at room 
temperature. Standard samples of 5 mm in diameter a nd 25 mm in gauge length were used to 
determine the tensile properties. Solution heat tre atment was carried out at various temperatures in 
(α+β) region as 800 ℃, 810 ℃and 820 ℃ for 1 h and air cooling with subsequent different aging 
temperature as 550 ℃ , 580 ℃ and 610 ℃ for 4 h. After polishing, the samples were etched using 
10%HNO 3, 5%HF, and 85% H 2O for conventional optical observation. Characteriz ation of the 
resulting microstructure was carried out with scanning electron microscopy (SEM).

3. Results and discussion 
3.1 Microstructures 
The microstructure of heat treated materials are sh own in Figure 2. As solution temperature are 
below the β transus temperature, all the microstructures consist of globular primary α phase (α
p) and 
transformed β phase in which fine second α phase (α s) precipitated during aging treatment. The α p 
phase particles distribute in the β matrix uniformly. Solution temperature controls th e size and 
quantity of αp phase , which influences the ductility. It can be seen from Fig.2 (a, d, g) that the size of αp 
phase increase not obviously with the solution temp erature increase. As aging temperature increase, 
intragranular αs phase coarsening, resulting in lower strength but higher ductility. αs phase precipitates 
along grain boundaries and in the grain. The volume fraction and morphology of the α s phase 
precipitates control the strength level.

3.2 Tensile Properties 
The tensile property of the alloy in this study is summarized in Table 1. The strength and ductility 
are determined by solution temperature and aging te mperature. The data shows that the ultimate 
strength and yield strength decrease but elongation and reduction of area increase as aging 
temperature increase. The strength reaches maximum level solution treatment at 820 ℃ and age 
treatment at 550 ℃. Solution temperature has very little effect on el ongation and reduction of area. 
Solution temperature increasing decreases high stre ngth level except aging at 580 ℃ for 4 h, i. e. 
solution treatment at 810 ℃and 800 ℃ for 1h slightly decreases this high strength from 1445MPa to 
1435MPa and 1425MPa. The ultimate and yield tensile strengths varied from 1280 MPa to 1445 MPa 
and 1245 MPa to 1420 MPa respectively. The elongati on and reduction of area varied from 7.0 to 
12.0% and 17.0 to 22.0% respectively. It is observe d that increasing aging temperature can produce 
better ductility. That is certainly due to the coarsening of precipitated αs phase.

4. Conclusion 
1) As the solution temperature increase, the quanti ty and size of primary α phase of microstructure 
increase, resulting in lower tensile strength but higher ductility slightly. 
2) Increasing of aging temperature resulted in the increase of ductility and reduction of tensile 
strength,, which is attributed to the coarsening of precipitated α phase.
