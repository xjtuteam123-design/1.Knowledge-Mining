abstract
Nb–Ti–C–B in-situ composites with W addition were fabricated by arc-melting method under an argon
atmosphere. The microstructure and mechanical properties of the composites at room and high
temperature were investigated, and the strengthening mechanism of W-added composites was
discussed in detail. Microstructural observation shows that the composites contain primary Nbss
dendrites and interdendritic eutectic network, and W addition re ﬁnes primary Nbss and coarsens
boride phase. The hybrid (Nb,Ti,W)C and (Nb,Ti,W)B second-phases are formed in the eutectic network
of W-added composites. The hardness, room- and high-temperature compression properties were
evaluated to determine the strengthening effect of W-added composites. The results indicate that the
hardness and compressive strength of the composites are improved by W addition, which increases with
increasing W content. Though the compressive strength of the composites decreased at 1200 1C, the
increased W content was bene ﬁcial to high temperature strength improvement. The strengthening effect
of W-added composites is mainly attributed to solid solution and grain re ﬁnement strengthening.

1. Introduction
With the rapid development of aeronautic, astronautic and
nuclear technologies, a more ef ﬁcient combustion system operat-
ing at higher temperatures is anticipated, which in turn requires to
design a new class of heat-resisting materials capable of operating
at temperatures higher than current capability [1]. Although Ni-
based superalloys are employed widely for high temperature
productions, they have not satis ﬁed the need for next generation
high performance engine because their operating temperature
capability is less than 1 150 1C (this temperature reaches to 90% of
their melting points) [2]. Ideally, the new heat-resisting materials
should have a substantially higher melting point. Meanwhile, a
reasonable balance between strength, creep and oxidation resis-
tance at operating temperature together with good toughness
should be comprehensively considered and tuned for the real
application of these materials [3].
The refractory metal Nb has a melting temperature of 2468 1C,
and also has a lower density (8.57 g/cm
3) than that of Ni and any
other refractory metals such as Mo, W and Ta, thus making it an
attractive candidate as replacement of nickel notwithstanding the
sensitivity of niobium to oxygen [4]. In the last two decades, Nb-
based in-situ composites have received considerable attention
owing to their excellent properties, and several processing tech-
niques have been successfully employed to prepare such compo-
sites, including arc-melting [5,6], hot-pressing sintering [7,8], self-
propagating high-temperature synthesis [9] and spark plasma
sintering [10], etc. In such composites, the reinforcements are in-
situ synthetized and incorporated into the ductile Nb matix, which
are thermodynamically stable, and the matrix-reinforcements
interfaces are likely free of contamination and thermodynamically
compatible. Thus, the high-temperature strength of Nb-based
composites can be improved by stiffen second-phases, and low
temperature deformability and fracture toughness can be held by
the ductile matrix. As the reinforcements, some niobium com-
pounds containing Cr
2Nb [3],N b5Si3 [6,1 1],N b4Fe3Si5 [1,7], various
carbides [4,12], have been successful synthetized in niobium solid
solution (Nbss) matrix. Especially, it is attractive that two or more
stiffer hybrid second-phases have been selected as the reinforce-
ments of Nb-based composites according to the recent work,
resulting into some superior properties. For instance, Sha et al.
[2] reported that both of higher compressive 0.2% yield strength at
1200 1C and Vickers hardness appeared in arc-melted Nb –Mo–Ti–
Si–C in-situ composites reinforced by hybrid (Nb,Mo,Ti) 5Si3 silicide
and (Nb,Ti)C carbide. Yu et al. [1] employed hot pressing sintering
method to synthesize Nb –16Si–2Fe in-situ composites consisting

For Nb-based composites, most of the transition metals, such as
W, Mo, Ti, Hf and Ta, were dissolved into the Nb matrix to improve
their mechanical and anti-oxidation properties by means of solid
solution strengthening, controlling atomic transport kinetics, con-
trolling the lattice parameter of the bcc matrix, and/or in ﬂuencing
dislocation mobility, etc [3,1 1,13,14]. According to a systematic
study of binary Nb solid solution alloys reported by Begley et al.,
the alloying elements such as Mo, W and Zr embrittle Nbss, can
raise the ductile /C0 brittle transition temperature (DBTT) of Nb,
while Hf and Ti do not increase DBTT [15]. The alloying with Mo
and/or W produced strong solid-solution strengthening on Nb,
and this effect persisted to very high temperature [1 1,16].T i
addition enhanced both ductility and fracture toughness of Nbss,
while improvement in oxidation resistance was achieved by Ti
and/or Hf additions [8,14]. However, Ti addition reduced the
melting point of Nb-based materials, which was detrimental to
the high-temperature strength [17]. The alloying element
(s) controlled one or more properties, which often had a con ﬂict-
ing in ﬂuence on the other(s). Accordingly, it is important to
optimize the chemical composition of the composites to balance
the strength, ductility and oxidation resistance. Meanwhile, the
content, geometry, type and distribution of the reinforcements
should be considered together.

high strength, and high thermal conductivity and chemical stabi-

Single Nb 2C or (Nb,Ti)C carbide exhibited its ef ﬁcient high-

the composite substantially drops down at 1200 1C [4]. The
purpose of present study was to evaluate mechanical properties
of Nb –Ti–C–B composites with different W contents at room and
high temperature. Microstructural characterization of different W-
added composites has also been investigated in detail to under-
stand the alloying effects of W.
2. Experimental procedure
The starting materials were Nb (99.99% purity), Ti (99.99%
purity), W (99.95% purity), C (99.85% purity) and B 4C (99.85%
purity). The samples with nominal composition of Nb 20Ti4C4BxW
(x: 0, 2.5, 5, 7.5 and 10 at%), which termed as 0W, 2.5W, 5W, 7.5W
and 10W samples, respectively, were arc-melted in a water-cooled
copper crucible with a non-consumable tungsten electrode under
a pure argon atmosphere. Under argon atmosphere, an appro-
priate amount of pure titanium was melted before the samples
were arc-melted every time, in order to eliminate the remaining
oxygen and nitrogen impurities from the atmosphere. Each ingot
was turned over and remelted ﬁve times to ensure the chemical
homogeneity of the samples.
X-ray diffraction (XRD) was carried out to determine phase
constitutions of the composites via Rigaku D/max-2200 diffractometer
with monochromatic Cu-Kα (λ¼1.540562 Å) radiation and graphite
monochromator operated at 40 kV and 200 mA. The data were
collected through a step scanning mode with a 0.02 1 step in a
201r2θr901 diffraction angle range. Microstructural observation
was studied by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) using a FEI
Quanta 200FEG ﬁeld emission gun microscope, operating at 20 kV
and equipped with energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX), back-
scattered electron detector (BSE) and electron backscattered diffraction
(EBSD) detector. Thin foils for Kikuchi patterns analysis and transmis-
sion electron microscopy observation were prepared using a precision
ion polishing system (Leica/Bal-Tec RES 101). Kikuchi patterns analysis
was performed by SEM-EBSD, and orientation imaging microscopy
(OIM) (EDAX-TSL, Inc.) software was used to index the Kikuchi
patterns. A Tecnai G2 F30 ﬁeld emission gun transmission electron
microscope (TEM), operating at 300 kV, equipped with an EDX system,
was used for selected-area electron diffraction (SAED) analysis and
bright ﬁeld TEM observations. Bulk Vickers hardness (HV) was
evaluated under an applied load of 9.8 N and a load-dwell time of
10 s on a Model HV-1000 micro-sclerometer, and eight indentations

4. Conclusions
(1) Nb –Ti–C–B in-situ composites added by W from 2.5 at% to 10 at
% consist of body-centered cubic Nbss (W), face-centered cubic
(Nb,Ti,W)C carbide and base-centered orthorhombic (Nb,Ti,W)B
boride phases. The microstructures of composites with and
without W addition are composed of primary Nbss dendrites
and interdendritic network. The hybrid (Nb,Ti,W)C and (Nb,Ti,
W)B second-phases distribute in the interdendritic network of
W-added composites. Comparing with the composite without
