Effect of Cr Additions on Toughness, Strength,
and Oxidation Resistance of an Nb-4Si-20Ti-6Hf Alloy
at Room and/or High Temperatures
JIANGBO SHA, JUN LIU, and CHUNGEN ZHOU
The mechanical properties and oxidation resistance of Nb-4Si-20Ti-6Hf- yCr alloys (where
y = 6, 10, 14 at. pct) with an Nb SS-dominant Nb SS/Nb3Si/Nb5Si3 microstructure at room and/
or high temperatures were primarily studied. It was found that at room temperature, fracture
toughness was decreased by Cr additions, the fracture mode of the Nb SS phase was intergra-
nularly cleavage, and the silicides (Nb 3Si and Nb 5Si3) fractured in a brittle manner. It is
interesting that strength as a function of Cr content is dependent on temperature. Up to 1423 K
(1150 /C176 C), the strength was improved by Cr additions, whereas above that temperature, the
strength decreased. As the Cr content increased from 6 to 14 at. pct, for example, the yield
strength r
0.2 increased from 198 to 258 MPa at 1423 K (1150 /C176 C) but decreased from 109 to
83 MPa at 1623K (1350 /C176 C). Cr additions cannot improve the oxidation resistance of this Nb SS-
dominant microstructure considerably; the weight gain at 100 hours of air exposure at 1523 K
(1250 /C176 C) was 255 mg/cm
2 at a nominal Cr content of 6 at. pct and 220 mg/cm 2 at a Cr content
of 14 at. pct.

I. INTRODUCTION
THE use of refractory metals in the hottest parts of
engines and power plants has been an attractive subject
in recent decades for improving energy eﬃciency and
reducing emission of CO
2 gas.[1–3] Nb is one of the most
promising refractory metals for the next generation of
ultrahigh-temperature structural materials and has the
potential to service at extremely high temperatures over
the operating temperature of Ni-based superalloys of
about 1423 K (1150 /C176 C). It is well known that the
structural materials applied in advanced aerospace or
turbine engine systems must possess a balance of
properties, including ductility and toughness at low
temperatures, strength and creep resistance at elevated
temperatures, and environmental stability (oxidation
resistance). To enable Nb-based alloys to achieve
balanced properties, the concept of multiphase alloys
has been developed. It involves the incorporation of
ductile Nb solid solution, Nb
SS, with stiﬀening interme-
tallics, such as silicide Nb 5Si3
[4–14] and Laves phase
Cr2Nb,[15–17] to form a multiphase Nb SS/Nb5Si3/Cr2Nb
microstructure, in which the Nb SS phase oﬀers room-
temperature toughness and the silicide Nb 5Si3 and
Cr2Nb phases supply strength and oxidation resistance,
respectively, at elevated temperatures. Thus, adjusting
the proportion and morphology of the Nb SS,N b 5Si3,
and Cr2Nb phases in the Nb-Si-based alloys by alloying
or thermomechanical processing may obtain the bal-
anced properties that meet the requirements of ultra-
high-temperature structural materials.
The strength of the Nb-based alloys at elevated
temperatures, however, has been of concern as a
preferential issue and increased as high as possible.
Increasing Si content, i.e., volume fraction of the high-
strength and high-stiﬀness sicilides, is the simplest way
to improve the high-temperature strength of the Nb-Si-
based alloys. Extensive studies show that the bending
strength of the as-cast Nb-10Si alloy is 400 MPa at
1470 K (1197 /C176 C) and is 250 MPa for an extruded
specimen.
[5,6] The Nb-16Si prepared by HIP retains a
bending strength of approximately 400 MPa at 1670 K
(1397 /C176 C).
[6] Signiﬁcant improvement in strength has
been achieved by additions of W and Mo into the
eutectic composition of Nb-10Ti-18Si ternary. [12] With
10 at. pct Mo and 15 at. pct W additions, the Nb-10Ti-
18Si alloy prepared by directional solidiﬁcation shows
the yield strength greater than 650 MPa even at 1770 K
(1497 /C176 C). This is probably the highest strength among
the Nb-Si-based alloys that have been reported so far. W
and Mo are most eﬃcient solid-solution strengthening
elements for Nb.
[10–14]
Work on environmental stability has been directed
toward alloying with elements Hf, Cr, and Al to reduce
oxygen diﬀusion and solubility in Nb or form the
oxidation against phase Cr
2Nb. Nb-Si-Cr-Ti-Hf-Al-
Ge[15–17] alloys aimed to improve oxidation resistance
at high temperature have been developed. For the
oxidation resistance, short-term and long-term goals
have been established for the Nb-Si-based alloys with

Nb-10Si-2Fe alloy, an improvement in the fracture
toughness of the hot-extruded + heat-treated sample by
approximately 90 pct is obtained.
[20]
The aforementioned review strongly suggests that a
breakthrough in the high-temperature strength, room-
temperature toughness, and high-temperature oxida-
tion resistance has been individually made in various
compositions of the Nb-Si-based alloys. However, no
composition alone can meet requirements of high-
temperature structural materials synchronously in
the room-temperature toughness, high-temperature
strength, and oxidation resistance because these param-
eters are usually incompatible. To alloy design, we must
understand the limitations of strength, toughness, and
oxidation resistance of the Nb-Si-based alloys or the
relationship between comprehensive properties, phase
constitutions, and microstructures. So, a wide range of
Si and Cr contents should be used to obtain various
proportions of the Nb
SS, silicides, and Cr 2Nb phases. In
this work, two approaches for improving the room-
temperature toughness of the Nb-Si-based alloys were
employed. They are (1) reducing Si content from
18 at. pct (eutectic composition) to 4 at. pct, i.e.,
decreasing the volume fraction of the stiﬀening silicides;
and (2) doping a ﬁxed content of Ti and Hf at 20 at. pct
and 6 at. pct, respectively, for toughening. A moderate
strength at room and high temperatures was supported
by a 4 at. pct Si addition. Various Cr contents (6, 10,
and 14 at. pct) were added to understand the compre-
hensive eﬀect of Cr on properties of the Nb-Si-based
alloys at room and high temperatures, which is still
unknown. Thus, the toughness, strength, and oxidation
resistance of an Nb-4Si-20Ti-6Hf alloy with Cr addi-
tions is investigated in this study.
II. EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURES
Button ingots of the Nb-4Si-20Ti-6Hf-(6,10,14)Cr
alloys (referred to 6Cr alloy, 10Cr alloy, and 14Cr
alloy, respectively, hereafter) each with a weight of 100 g
were prepared by arc melting ﬁve or six times in an
argon atmosphere under a pressure of ~0.5 Pa. The
purity of all raw elements used was 99.9 pct (in mass) or
higher. The ingots were annealed ﬁnally at 1648 K
(1375 /C176 C) in vacuum for 100 hours and then furnace
cooled to ensure quasi-equilibrium microstructures.
X-ray diﬀraction analysis was performed on bulk
samples to identify phase constitutions using Cu K a
radiation at 40 KV and 40 mA (Rigaku D/Max 2200PC,
Rigaku, Japan). The microstructures and fracture sur-
faces were observed using a scanning electron micro-
scopy (SEM, JEOL JXA-8100, Japan Electron Optics
Laboratory Co. Ltd., Japan) under the backscattered
mode, and the phase compositions were analyzed using
an energy-dispersion X-ray spectroscopy (Oxford instru-
ments, UK) in the SEM. Bulk Vickers hardness ( Hv) was
measured under an applied load of 9.8 N on a digital
HXZ-1000 microsclerometer (Shangguang Microscopy
Co. Ltd., Shanghai, China) with a loading-maintaining
time of 15 s, 11 Hv data were taken for each sample to
obtain an average value. Single-notched three-point-
bending specimens were used instead of single fatigue-
cracked specimens for toughness measurements. A notch
was introduced with up to a/w = 0.5 ( a is the notch
length and w is the specimen width) by electrical
discharge machining with a wire diameter of 0.2 mm.
The three-point-bending specimen is 30 mm in length,
6 mm in width, and 3 mm in thickness. The three-point-
bending tests were conducted on an Instron 8801 testing
machine (Instron, UK) with a loading span of 25 mm
and a crosshead displacement rate of 0.1 mm/min. Three
specimens were tested for each alloy.
The compressive tests at room temperature were
conducted on an Instron 8801 machine at a strain rate of
3 9 10
/C04 s–1. The dimensions of the polished compres-
sive cylinders were 3 mm in diameter and 5 mm in
length. The high-temperature compressive tests (at
1423 K, 1523 K, and 1623 K [1150 /C176 C, 1250 /C176 C, and
1350 /C176 C]) were conducted in an argon atmosphere at a
strain rate of 1 9 10
–3 s–1 using a Gleeble 1500 testing
machine (DSI, Poestenkill, New York). The temperature
increase rate was 200 K/min (200 /C176 C/min) and the
specimens were held for 5 minutes at the testing tem-
perature before testing to homogenize the temperature
throughout the specimens. The dimensions of the
compressive cylinders are 8 mm in diameter and
12 mm in length. All specimens for the three-
point-bending and compressive tests were polished

NbSS phase, whereas the black Nb 3Si strips adhere to
the Nb 5Si3 network. It should be noted that in the
annealed samples the volume fraction of the Nb 5Si3
phase is greater than that in the as-cast samples, which

The average values of the bulk Vickers hardness ( Hv)
and fracture toughness ( KQ) of the annealed samples are

The fractured surfaces of the annealed 6Cr alloy
displayed in Figure 3 reveal the fracture mechanisms of
the typical Nb SS-dominant Nb SS/silicides microstruc-
tures. The river pattern, i.e., active facets, was found on
the Nb SS phase, which illustrates that a cleavage mode
takes place in the Nb SS phase. From the direction of the
cleavage river pattern, the crack propagation path can
be conﬁrmed. The Nb
SS grains are observed clearly from
the macromorphology of the fractured surfaces in
Figures 3(a) and (b). It is evident that the crack
propagates wavily and the distances of each crack
deﬂection of up to a few hundred microns in the wave
are found to be in good agreement with the dimensions
of the Nb
SS grains showed in Figure 2. Both Fig-
ures 3(a) and (b) suggest strongly that the fracture is
intergranularly cleavage, and the boundaries of the
NbSS grains should be the favorable sites for the crack
initiation and growth. In Figure 3(b), a typical feature
of the crack propagating from one grain (grain I) to its
next one (grain II) can be observed. It is interesting that
opposite growth of the crack is found also in the grains
II and III because the river pattern in the two grains is
rightabout. It is deducted that a crack on grain II
meeting a crack on grain III at the boundary results in
the crack growth. In addition, decohesion between the
Nb
SS grains III and IV was observed in Figure 3(b),
which means that secondary cracking or branching
mechanism occurs during crack propagation, where
the secondary cracking or branching is out of plane.

IV. DISCUSSION
In this work, the nominal Cr content is variable and
both the Cr concentration in the Nb SS phase and volume
fraction of the brittle silicides increases with an increase
of the nominal Cr content. These metallurgical changes
may aﬀect the bulk toughness at low temperatures,
strength at elevated temperatures, and environmental
stability (oxidation resistance) of the Nb-Si-based alloys.
Increasing Cr concentration in the Nb
SS phase may
decrease the fracture toughness of the Nb SS phase
because Cr additions raise the BDTT of Nb. [18] It was
reported that adding Cr to Nb results in an increase in
the Peierls–Nabarro barrier energy (energy for starting
slip of dislocation) and a decrease in the c
s/UP–N ratio,
which leads to a higher propensity for cleavage fracture
and a lower fracture resistance of Nb, where the cs is the
surface energy and the UP–N is the P–N barrier energy. It
is undoubted that for a microstructure with the ductile
and brittle phases, an increasing volume fraction of the
brittle phase is negative for the bulk toughness.
In metallurgy, both the solid-solution, hardened Nb
SS
matrix and the high-stiﬀening silicides contribute to the
bulk strength of the Nb-Si-Ti-Hf-Cr alloys at room
temperature. The silicides network on the Nb
SS bound-
aries may also act as a stiﬀness frame that bears the
service loads and with an increase of its volume fraction
the bulk hardness or strength increases undoubtedly. To
understand the solid-solution hardening eﬀect at room
temperature, a small applied load of 0.15N was used to
measure the Hv value of the monolithic Nb
SS phase
accurately. As expected, the Hv of the annealed Nb SS
phase increases considerably from 331 to 377 as its Cr
concentration changes from 6.71 at. pct to 15.42 at. pct
(corresponding to the nominal Cr content changes from
6 to 14 at. pct). It was reported that the Hv of the pure
Nb was of about 82, [24] so the increment of the Hv from
82 of pure Nb to 331 of the Nb SS phase comes from the
solute concentrations of 0.88Si + 22.94Ti + 5.33Hf +
6.71Cr (see compositions of the Nb SS phase in the 6Cr
alloy in Table I). Another increment in the Hv of the
NbSS phase from 331 up to 377 is basically attributive to
an increase of the Cr concentration in the Nb SS phase
from 6.71 at. pct to 15.42 at. pct (Table I). The previous
analysis suggests strongly that Cr additions strengthen
the NbSS phase, which is favorable to the bulk strength of
the Nb-Si-based alloys at room temperature.
From Figures 4 and 5, it is known that eﬀect of the Cr
content on the strength of the Nb-4Si-20Ti-6Hf alloy is
also dependent on temperature. Actually, there is a
disadvantage for strength of the Nb-4Si-20Ti-6Hf
alloy when Cr is added. Cr additions decrease the
liquidus of Nb; for example, the melting point of Nb
decreases from 2720 K to 2573 K (2447 /C176 C to 2300 /C176 C)
as the Cr concentration in Nb increases from zero to
15.42 at. pct.
[25] With multiple solutes of Ti, Hf, Cr, Al,
and Si, the Nb SS phase might have an actual melting
point lower than the Nb-Cr binary solid solution. This
ﬁnding suggests that the temperature capability of the
multiple-component Nb-Si-Ti-Hf-Cr alloys with the
Nb
SS-dominant microstructure decreases with an
increase of the nominal Cr content.

After all, the strengthening and weakening
mechanisms coexist in the Nb-Si-based alloys with Cr
additions. Which one is dominant for the bulk strength
depends on the nominal Cr content and testing temper-
ature. It is believed that a higher testing temperature
(closer to the liquidus of the Nb
SS phase) may be
favorable for the weakening mechanism. At 1423 K
(1150 /C176 C) and below, the solid-solution hardening of the
NbSS phase and high-stiﬀening silicides hardening may
exceed the weakening eﬃciency caused by decreasing of
the melting point of the Nb SS phase by Cr; the bulk
strength shows an increasing tendency with Cr. At
1523 K and 1623 K (1250 /C176 C and 1350 /C176 C), the weak-
ening behavior caused by the aforementioned disadvan-
tage of the Nb
SS phase (comparing liquidus of the Nb SS
phase in the 14Cr alloy with that in the 6Cr alloy) may
dominate over the solid-solution hardening of the Nb SS
phase and the high-stiﬀening silicides hardening, result-
ing in decreasing of the bulk strength of the Nb SS-
dominant microstructure with Cr. The fact that the
strength of the 10Cr and 14Cr alloys decreases at
temperatures above 1423 K (1150 /C176 C) vary faster than
that of the 6Cr alloy (Figure 5) supports the previous
deduction. However, subsequent work concerning on
the transition mechanism and accurate temperature
from strengthening to weakening of the Nb-Si alloys
with Cr additions is still needed, which would be the
theoretical base for the composition design and engi-
neering service.

alloys, the silicides exhibit better oxidation resistance
than the Nb

V. CONCLUSIONS
A study of the microstructure, mechanical properties,
and oxidation resistance of the Nb-4Si-20Ti-6Hf-
(6,10,14)Cr alloys led to the following conclusions:
1. The microstructure consists of the Nb
SS,N b 3Si,
and Nb 5Si3 phases, with no Laves Cr 2Nb phase.
The Nb SS phase is the matrix and dominates over
the Nb 3Si and Nb 5Si3 silicides.
2. Cr decreases the room-temperature fracture tough-
ness KQ. As the nominal Cr content increases from
6 at. pct to 14 at. pct, the KQ decreases from
20.67 MPa Æm1/2 to 13.83 MPa Æm1/2. The fracture
mode of the Nb SS phase is intergranularly cleavage,
whereas the silicides fracture in a brittle manner.
3. At 1423 K (1150 /C176 C) and below, the strength increases
with the nominal Cr content; whereas at 1523 K
and 1623 K (1250 /C176 C and 1350 /C176 C), the strength
decreases. As the Cr content increases from 6 at. pct
to 14 at. pct, for example, the r
0.2 increases from 653
to 1030 MPa at room temperature, and from 198 to
258 MPa at 1423 K (1150 /C176 C); it decreases from 176
to 158 MPa at 1523 K (1250 /C176 C), and from 109 to
83 MPa at 1623 K (1350 /C176 C).
4. The oxidation resistance of the Nb-4Si-20Ti-6Hf-
(6,10,14)Cr alloys with an Nb SS-dominant micro-
structure increases somewhat with Cr. The mass
gain of the alloy exposed at 1523 K (1250 /C176 C) for
100 hours changes from 255 to 220 mg/cm 2 as the
Cr content increases from 6 to 14 at. pct.
