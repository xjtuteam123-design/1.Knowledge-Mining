Microstructural characterization, formation mechanism and fracture behavior of the needleδ phase in Fe–Ni–Cr
type superalloys with high Nb content (GH4169, equivalent to Inconel 718) have been quantitatively investigat-
ed in this research. The typical microstructures of δ phases with the stick, mixed and needle shapes obviously
present in Inconel 718 after the isothermal upsetting at the temperature of 980 –1060 °C with the initial strain
rate of 10−3–10−1 s−1. It is found that the shape of the δ phase has a great effect on the mechanical properties
of the alloy, viz., the stick δ phase behaves good plasticity and the needleδ phase has good strength. In addition,
the needle δ phase can be used to control the grain size as it can prevent grain growth. The combined effect of the
localized necking and microvoid coalescence leads to the ﬁnal ductile fracture of the GH4169 components with
the needle δ phase. Both dislocation motion and atom diffusion are the root-cause for the needle δ phase to be
ﬁrstly separated at grain boundary and then at sub-boundary. The formation mechanism of the needle δ phase
is the new ﬁnding in this research. Furthermore, it is the primary mechanism for controlling the needle
δ phase in Fe–Ni–Cr type superalloys with high Nb content.

1. Introduction
Due to its excellent performance at a wide range of working temper-
ature up to 650 °C [1,2], GH4169 (Inconel 718) is the most important
Fe–Ni–Cr type superalloy used for manufacturing high temperature
components in the aerospace industry. In this type of superalloy, γ
(Ni) is the matrix, while γ′ (Ni3AlTi) and γ″ (Ni3Nb) precipitates are
the strengthening phases [3]. In practice, the major strengthening
phase is γ″, and only a small volume fraction (b 25%) is needed to trans-
form GH4169 into a high-strength superalloy. In contrast, the volume
fraction of theγ′ phase in GH4169 is less than 5% and the strengthening
contributed by this phase is incidental. Andδ (Ni3Nb) is the equilibrium
phase of γ″ and is thermodynamically more stable than the γ″ phase
[4,5]. The lattice parameters of γ′, γ″ and δ phases are summarized in
Table 1 [6]. A typical precipitation time temperature (PTT) diagram for
this type of superalloy is shown in Fig.1 [7,8]. The precipitation of the
δ phase often occurs in the temperature range of about 750 –1020 °C.
At lower temperature (below 900 °C), the precipitation of the δ phase
is preceded by the γ″ phase, but the δ phase precipitates directly from
the γ matrix at higher temperature [9,10]. It has been shown that
most rapid precipitation of the δ phase occurs at the temperature of
about 900 °C [11]. When the temperature is higher than 980 °C, most
δ phases dissolve [12]. Since the δ phase has a great effect on the me-
chanical properties of the superalloy, the effects of isothermal upsetting
parameters on the microstructural evolution are thus explored and
discussed in this research. Furthermore, the fracture behavior of the
needle δ phase is quantitatively investigated to address the issue why
the needle δ phase has good strength. Finally, the formation mechanism
of the needle δ phase is presented in this paper.

behaves good fracture strength?

tor for their reliability in applications, and fracture strength is one of
the most important mechanical properties for structural components
[22,23]. Consequently many researches [24–26], largely experiment,
have been conducted and focused on the fracture behavior of various
superalloys with different compositions, heat treatments, and corre-
sponding microstructures.
Study of the initiation of fracture cracks is focused either on initiation
from free surfaces or near defects (voids, carbides or ceramic inclusions)
that are present in the superalloys during the manufacturing process
[27,28]. Research on crack propagation is more focused on the rate of

4.2. Formation mechanism of the needle δ phase— how to control needle
formation?
The formation mechanism of the needleδ phase is the newﬁnding in
this research. Furthermore, it is the primary mechanism for controlling
the needle δ phase in the Fe–Ni–Cr type superalloys with high Nb con-
tent. Generally, the combined effect of plastic deformation and phase
transformation leads to the separation of the needle δ phase at grain
boundaries. The schematic diagrams inFig. 5 illustrate the precipitation
mechanism of the needleδ phase in hot working of GH4169 superalloys
at high temperature, which reveals that the needleδ phase separates at
grain boundary (1st) and sub-boundary (2nd).

(1) Separation of the needle δ phase at grain boundaries (1st: γ →
needle δ phase).
Grain boundary sliding occurs at high temperature, which can re-
sult in very large strains without any obvious internal deforma-
tion of grains, except at the grain boundaries to accommodate
grain sliding. With the increase of deformation, dislocations pile
up at grain boundaries. And metastable structure with high
distortion energy will be built up when enough dislocations
pile up at grain boundary. Meanwhile, atom diffusion would
take place in the enclosed region of the metastable structure.
The δ phase formation elements (e.g. Nb in the γ matrix) diffuse
towards the metastable structure, which leads to formation of
stable structure and the offset of distortion energy. When the
whole grain boundary is covered with needle precipitate, the δ
phase begins to separate in the grains.
(2) Needle δ phase separates at sub-boundary (2nd: γ′ → needle
δ phase).
Since the needle δ phase enhances the strength of grain bound-
aries, plastic deformation is carried out inside the grains instead
of the grain boundary. The effect of the γ′ phase on dislocation
motion should be paid more attention. When plastic deforma-
tion is carried out at the temperature slightly below the complete
solution temperature of the γ′ phase, dislocations will pass the
big γ′ phase by a circling mechanism and leave a dislocation
loop. If the deformation temperature is much lower than the so-
lution temperature, theγ′ phase would hinder most dislocations
in such a way to build up more dislocation loops[34]. Due to dis-
location propagation and tangle around the largeγ′ phase, dislo-
cation wall is built up as presented inFig. 6.M e a n w h i l e ,w i t ht h e
increase of plastic deformation, sub-boundary formed in the
grain via connection of dislocation walls with others. Similar
to microstructural mechanism at grain boundaries, element
diffusion of the δ phase would take place in the grains when
there are dislocation walls or/and metastable structure in sub-
boundaries.
Summarily, the combined effect of plastic deformation and phase
transformation leads to the separation of the needle δ phase at grain
or/and sub-boundaries. Actually, both dislocation motion and atom
diffusion are the real reasons that result in the separation of the needle
δ phase ﬁrstly at grain boundaries and secondly at sub-boundaries. The
formation mechanism of the needleδ phase is the newﬁnding in this re-
search. Furthermore, it is the primary mechanism for controlling the nee-
dle δ phase in Fe–Ni–Cr type superalloys with high Nb content. However,
the microstructural evolution in needle δ phase separation needs to be
further investigated by usingﬁnit

5. Conclusions
(1) The δ phases in stick, mixed and needle shapes obviously present
in the typical microstructures of GH4169 superalloys processed
by isothermal upsetting at the temperature of 980 –1060 °C
with the strain rate of 10−3–10−1 s−1.
(2) The shape of the δ phase has a great effect on the mechanical
property of GH4169 superalloy. The stick δ phase behaves good
plasticity, while the needle δ phase has good strength.
(3) The needle δ phase can be used to control the grain size of
GH4169 components, since the needle δ phase plays a great
role to prevent grain growth. Also, the needle δ phase can
enhance the fracture strength.
(4) The combined effect of the localized necking and microvoid
coalescence leads to the ﬁnal ductile fracture of the GH4169
superalloys with the needle δ phase.
(5) Both dislocation motion and atom diffusion are the real reasons
that the needle δ phase ﬁrstly separates at grain boundaries
and secondly at sub-boundaries. The formation mechanism of
the needle δ phase is the new ﬁnding in this research. Further-
more, it is the primary mechanism for controlling the needle δ
phase in Fe–Ni–Cr type superalloys with high Nb content.
