ABSTRACT 
Micro-twinning is the major creep deformation mechanism in Ni-based superalloys at temperatures above 700 
◦
C. Recent experiments suggest that superlattice stacking faults in γ ′ phase may serve as precursors for twin 
formation. Segregation of alloy elements to these precursors may have a significant effect on the formation and 
extension of micro-twins. Using atomistic modeling, we investigated and explained the effects of Cr on these 
processes. Our results indicate that depending on the site preference of Cr in Ni
3
Al γ ′ phase, two drastically 
different deformation behaviors can be expected. Occupying Al sites, Cr significantly accelerates deformation 
twinning. Cr on Ni sites, on the other hand, suppresses twin growth and slows down the high temperature 
deformation creep. These results help to rationalize the experimentally observed puzzling effects of alloy 
composition on creep resistance. 
Ni-based superalloys are high temperature, high strength creep 
resistant structural materials that are widely used in hot sections of the 
aircraft turbine engines. The unique microstructure of these alloys in -
cludes γ ′ precipitates coherently embedded in a γ matrix, which is a Ni- 
based solid solution. Micro-twinning is one of the major deformation 
mechanisms in Ni superalloys at intermediate temperatures (600 
◦
C – 
800 
◦
C) [ 1-4 ]. Over the last few decades several mechanisms of twin 
nucleation and growth were proposed [ 5-12 ]. The Kolbe mechanism 
(see schematic diagrams in Refs. [ 1 , 7 , 13 ]) for micro-twinning, based on 
thermally activated reordering, is currently widely accepted in the sci -
entific community to explain these processes [ 13-15 ]. In this mecha -
nism, 1/6 〈 112 〉 partial dislocations shear the precipitates, moving in a 
correlated manner, as dislocation pairs on adjacent {111} glide planes. 
As originally proposed by Kolbe [ 7 ], high energy double complex 
stacking faults (CSF), created by pairs of partials, transform into lower 
energy L1
2 
structures via thermally activated reshuffling. Recent ex -
periments suggest that superlattice stacking faults in the γ ′ phase may 
serve as precursors to twin formation [ 1-3 ]. Segregation of alloy ele -
ments to these precursors may have a significant effect on the formation 
and extension of micro-twins. In particular, an increased segregation of γ 
phase formers (e.g., Co, Cr) to superlattice intrinsic/extrinsic stacking 
faults (SISF/SESF) and twin interfaces in the γ ′ phase was observed 
experimentally in less creep resistant alloys (e.g., ME3), which are sus -
ceptible to micro-twinning under creep deformation conditions [ 2 , 3 , 12 , 
16-18 ]. 
The authors of the present study recently investigated the Kolbe 
micro-twinning mechanism employing atomistic simulations. It was 
demonstrated that atomic diffusion-mediated reordering takes place in 
the immediate vicinity of the cores of twinning partials and proceeds in a 
highly concerted manner, directly influenced by the propagation of 
dislocations [ 19 ]. In the present Letter, we concentrate on the effect of 
Cr on twin formation and growth in Ni superalloys. Employing a recently 
developed simulation methodology [ 19 ] along with a new 
Finnis-Sinclair (FS) [ 20 ] potential for Ni-Al-Cr [ 21 ], specifically devel -
oped for this study, we investigated the effect of Cr on the Kolbe 
mechanism for micro-twinning in Ni superalloys. All simulations were 
performed using the LAMMPS simulation package [ 22 ]. Visualization of 
the molecular dynamics (MD) simulation snapshots and some of the 
post-processing analysis were performed using the OVITO package [ 23 , 
24 ]. The simulation system used in Ref. [ 19 ] (see Fig. 1 a) was modified 
for this study by introducing ~ 6 % of Cr inside the precipitate at/near 
the glide planes of the dislocations (see the highlighted regions in 
Fig. 1 b). These regions mimic volumes with increased concentration of 
Cr due to segregation to superlattice stacking faults and twin interfaces 
in the γ ′ phase, which have been observed experimentally in less creep 
resistant alloys (e.g., ME3) [ 25 ].

currently not available, we manually created regions with high Cr

mechanism. For consistency purposes, two different simulation models 
were used: in one model, Cr atoms were placed on Al sites (in regions 
with high Cr concentration); in the other model, the same number of Cr

complex stacking fault (CSF) which is energetically unfavorable (Al-Al 
nearest neighbor bonds (NNBs) are created), while the second partial, 
gliding on the adjacent {111} plane, enters the precipitate and generates 
a second CSF on top of the pre-existing one, left by the first partial [ 19 ]. 
The high energy double CSF reorders into a superlattice extrinsic 
stacking fault (SESF) via vacancy mediated thermally activated diffu -
sion, as originally proposed by Kolbe [ 7 , 19 ]. 
The effect of Cr atoms on the Kolbe mechanism (see Fig. 2 ) strongly 
depends on the site preference of Cr in the Ni
3
Al-based γ ′ precipitates (Cr 
on Ni sites, or Cr on Al sites). The site preference of Cr within the γ ′ phase 
is still under debate and could depend on the alloy composition and heat 
treatment [ 27 , 28 ]. Our results indicate that Cr on Ni sites inhibits the 
reordering of high-energy Al-Al nearest neighbors (created by the 
coupled pairs of partials propagating through a γ ′ precipitate), impeding 
the glide of the coupled twinning partials inside the γ ′ precipitate (see 
Fig. 2 c). On the other hand, occupying Al sites in γ ′ precipitates, Cr 
significantly accelerates deformation (see Fig. 2 d). This can be ratio -
nalized as follows. Due to the non-uniform distribution of Cr, patches of 
γ phase are produced near preexisting stacking faults, or twin interfaces. 
As a result, dislocations “ fly ” through those patches, significantly 
accelerating creep (see Fig. 2 d).

Next, we consider the experimentally obtained creep curves for 
several Ni-based superalloys with different elemental compositions and 
relate the observed trends to the simulation results presented in this 
study. A new disk superalloy (TSNA-1) [ 18 ], that leveraged the ordered 
phase formation along faults to improve creep strength [ 4 , 16 ], was 
recently developed at NASA Glenn Research Center. The development 
process included testing of several alloys (TSNA-1, … , TSNA-6, along 
with two current state-of-the-art Ni-based superalloys: ME3 and LSHR). 
The compositions of these alloys are provided in Table 1 . All the alloys in 
this series had very similar total concentrations of slowly diffusing γ ′ 
phase forming elements (e.g., Nb, Ta, etc.). According to experimental 
observations, alloying with these elements considerably improves the 
creep performance of Ni-based superalloys [ 2-4 ]. On one hand, these 
alloying additions increase the energies of antiphase boundaries (APB) 
and complex stacking faults (CSF) [ 30 ]. These high energy defects form 
in γ ′ phase strengthening precipitates, providing shear resistance, when 
the precipitates are sheared by various dislocation types. On the other 
hand, slowly diffusing solutes (e.g., Nb) [ 31 ] are supposed to impede the 
reordering processes involved in the Kolbe mechanism [ 32 ].

The creep tests revealed an extremely sharp increase of alloy strength 
above a critical concentration of slowly diffusing γ ′ phase forming ele -
ments (see Fig. 5 a). This puzzling experimentally obtained result can be 
rationalized if we consider the effect of Cr content on the creep prop -
erties. Fig. 5 b demonstrates that the least creep resistant alloys (ME3, 
LSHR and TSNA-5) have the highest Cr concentration. The trend of 
increasing creep time to 0.3 % strain with decreasing Cr concentration is 
also well pronounced (see Fig. 5 b). Cr occupies Al-sites of Ni
3
Al-based 
precipitates in these alloys and segregates at superlattice intrinsic/ 
extrinsic stacking faults (SISF/SESF) and twin interfaces in the γ ′ phase 
(see Fig. 5 c) [ 4 , 25 ]. So, the experimentally observed behavior (see 
Fig. 5 b) agrees very well with what we observe in our simulations (see 
Fig. 2 d). 
It should be noted that in the present study we performed MD sim -
ulations to explore the effect of Cr on the Kolbe mechanism for micro- 
twinning in Ni-based superalloys. This was done by intentionally 
choosing the simulation setup where the Kolbe mechanism was the only 
one which could operate. However, while the Kolbe mechanism is 
considered to be the dominant deformation mechanism for these alloys 
at the intermediate temperatures, other deformation mechanisms can 
also be active. Additional effects can be associated with the influence of 
Cr on the dislocation propagation in the γ phase, on the formation of 
carbides, σ phase, etc. While these effects are well beyond the scope of 
the present study, they should be considered in the future investigations. 
Only the consideration of all these effects can provide a comprehensive 
picture of the overall effect of Cr on the creep properties. 
In conclusion, using atomistic simulations we demonstrated that 
depending on the site preference of Cr in Ni
3
Al γ ′ phase (Cr on Ni sites, or 
Cr on Al sites), two drastically different deformation behaviors can be 
observed. Cr on Al sites promotes twin growth via the Kolbe mechanism, 
degrading the high temperature creep properties. Cr on Ni sites, on the 
other hand, suppresses twin growth and slows down the high tempera -
ture deformation creep. Using the obtained results, we rationalized the 
experimentally observed puzzling effects of elemental composition of 
the alloy on creep resistance. Finally, we note that Cr site preference in 
the γ ′ phase of Ni based superalloys can possibly be manipulated by 
tuning the alloy composition, thus, providing an opportunity to design 
the next generation of Ni superalloys with improved creep resistance.
