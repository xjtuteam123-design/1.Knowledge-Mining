abstract
The simultaneous consideration of multiple conﬂicting properties in alloy optimization design is neces-
sary yet remains challenging. A comprehensive strategy of machine learning (ML) and multi-objective
optimization based on thermodynamic simulation data was proposed to accelerate the composition
design of Ni-based superalloys. The microscopic parameters were determined by Pearson correlation
analysis and domain knowledge as the key affect factors of tensile strength and elongation. The Multi-
objectives Evolutionary Algorithm (MOEA) was adopted to search the well-built surrogate by ML meta-
heuristically for the Pareto front of three objectives and its responding Pareto optimal solution set of
composition. Furthermore, nine high-performance superalloy samples selected from the obtained
Pareto front were well veriﬁed by fabricating and testing in the laboratory. Specially, a new composition
among the nine as-fabricated samples was the best one according to the pre-deﬁned design preferences
with the
c0 solvus temperature, c0 volume fraction, and TCP phases content approximating to 1210 /C176C,
65%, and 0.01%, respectively. This intelligent cooperation strategy based on ML and MOEA extends the
methodology for multi-composition and multi-property design materials, which can optimize multiple
conﬂicting objectives simultaneously rather than do one by one.

superalloy materials, it is essential to research the multi-
composition, multi-phase, multiple strengthening mechanism,
and multiple technologies of superalloys comprehensively and
coherently in the design process [5–7]. Ni-based superalloys con-
sist of c phase and c0 phase with face-centered cubic and L1 2 struc-
tures, respectively [8]. The comprehensive properties of Ni-based
superalloys such as rupture strength, creep resistance and high-
temperature strength are inﬂuenced by solution strengthening,
precipitation strengthening, and other multiple strengthening
mechanisms [9]. The diversity of elements and microstructures
in Ni-based superalloys results in a puzzle in the composition
design when pursuing more and better properties simultaneously.
Traditional experimental design strategies based on ‘‘trial and
error” are time-consuming and expensive. In addition, the objec-
tives considered concurrently in these strategies are very few
due to the optimization techniques. To tackle with this problem
in the paper, the Machine Learning (ML) was applied to design
superalloys for building a multi-task surrogate model accurately
at an affordable computational cost [10]. Then, the multi-
objective optimization evolutionary algorithm was adopted to
search the Pareto front of the well-built surrogate for obtaining
the potential optimal compositions of superalloys.
The supervised machine learning was widely applied in the
design of superalloys to ﬁnd the microstructures such as
c0 solvus
temperature [11], c0 volume fraction [12], and wide c/c0 phase
zone [13] for improving mechanical properties in terms of creep
resistance [14] and creep life [15], etc. However, after the highly
nonlinear relationship among composition, microstructure, and
performance mapped by the ML, an exhaustive search was exe-
cuted on the built surrogate model to ﬁnd the enumerated and sat-
isﬁed candidates as possible based on a performance threshold
ﬁlter. Such a strategy is not efﬁcient enough in a high-
dimensional composition space and multi-performance space,
and even misses the optimal solution easily when the search step
is not set appropriately.
Furthermore, when faced with conﬂicting performances of
superalloys, a single ML model could not take into account simul-
taneously, but only establish multiple ML models to optimize per-
formances one by one. To achieve the best comprehensive
properties of materials, it is inevitable to face multiple constraints
and conﬂicting properties [16–18]. The multi-objective optimiza-
tion method could effectively solve nonlinear and multi-
dimensional material design problems. As a meta-heuristic opti-
mization evolutionary algorithm, the Evolutionary Algorithm (EA)
could carry out a global search with the characteristics of natural
selection, survival of the ﬁttest, and species genetics [19,20]. Thus,
a Multi-objective Evolutionary Algorithm (MOEA) possesses the
searchability of global probability optimization, which could
replace the violent search method for ﬁnding the global and opti-
mal solution of the surrogate model built by ML [21]. Therefore,
multi-objective optimization has been marginally employed in
process parameters designing of various materials to balance mul-
tiple mutually constrained or conﬂicting process parameters [22–
26]. For Ni-based superalloy, the addition of
c’ phase forming ele-
ments such as Al, Ti, Ta, Nb and Hf are beneﬁcial to the formation of
c’ precipitation phase and the increase of c0 solvus temperature.
But the addition of refractory elements such as Ta, Nb, Hf will pro-
mote the formation of TCP phases, which are the origin and aggre-
gation of cracks. Therefore, optimizing multiple conﬂicting
properties is a challenge in design of Ni-based superalloy. How-
ever, until now, the application of MOEA in the design process is
rarely reported according to our best knowledge. The combination
strategy of ML and MOEA is expected to solve the complex nonlin-
ear relationship between compositions and performances by carry-
ing out a global multi-objective optimization.
Herein, an ML and MOEA combined strategy is proposed to fur-
ther comprehensively and effectively design Ni-based superalloys.
To achieve Ni-based superalloys with high c0 solvus temperature,
high c0 volume fraction, and low topologically closed-packed
(TCP) phases content, the coherence and reversibility relationship
from compositions to microscopic parameters was established
based on thermodynamic calculation data and domain knowledge.
The MOEA was responsible for searching the Pareto optimal com-
positions that could compromise multiple conﬂicting objectives
simultaneously on a multi-task surrogate model built by ML. Fur-
thermore, the properties of newly found compositions were well
veriﬁed by fabrication and test experiments. This strategy could
effectively improve the design efﬁciency and save time and cost
of experiments. In addition, it is also suitable for extending to other
material designs when facing multiple conﬂicting properties to be
optimized simultaneously.
2. Method
2.1. Calculation method
2.1.1. Design strategy
A design strategy based on ML and MOEA in Fig. 1 was applied
for Ni-based superalloys to optimize multiple microscopic param-
eters such as the
c0 solvus temperature, c0 volume fraction, and TCP
phases content. These microscopic parameters were screened to
ﬁnd the most inﬂuential microscopic information with tensile
strength and elongation. By setting the composition space of 9 ele-
ments (Cr, Co, W, Mo, Al, Ti, Ta, Nb, Hf, except for C ﬁxed at 0.01%
and Ni as the rest element) of Ni-based superalloy, there were
56,909 compositions sampled randomly and uniformly from the
Thermo-Calc, which was a thermodynamic software for calculating
the equilibrium phase content and phase transformation tempera-
tures in batch. After comparing the errors of multiple ML algo-
rithms, a surrogate model was established by Back Propagation
Neural Network (BPNN) for mapping the compositions and micro-
scopic parameters nonlinearly. Moreover, the MOEA was used for
searching the global multi-objective Pareto front and the region
of interest. The objectives were empirically selected as c0 volume
fraction (Vc0), c0 solvus temperature (T c0), and TCP phases content.
The constraints were the processing window and liquidus temper-
ature (Tl). Afterward, the microscopic parameters of candidates in
region of interest were veriﬁed by simulation of Thermo-Calc soft-
ware, and the microscopic parameters of nine optimized alloy were
veriﬁed by experiment. Finally, a new material, as a preferred sam-
ple, was obtained to satisfy objectives and constraints. Otherwise,
the veriﬁed data would be substituted into the workﬂow for feed-
back and iteration to ﬁnally ﬁnd the Pareto optimal solution set of
the composition.

2.1.3. Pearson relevance screening
The key microscopic parameters related to high-temperature
tensile strength and elongation were determined according to
the experience-driven domain knowledge. The raw data was cho-
sen from more than 30 compositions and 900 /C176C tensile strength
and elongation data of medium axial cast superalloys in the CHINA
SUPERALLOYS HANDBOOK [30]. According to the domain knowl-
edge, 14 quantitated microscopic parameters were selected and
divided into the following three categories as (i) phase volume
fraction: c phase, c0 phase, carbides (M 23C6,M 6C, MC), TCP phases
(including P phase, r Phase, ETA phase, and MU phase); (ii) phase
transformation temperatures: solidus temperature (Ts), liquidus
temperature (Tl), freezing range (Tl-Ts), c0 solvus temperature
(Tc0 ) and the processing window (Ts-T c0); (iii) lattice structure:
c/c0 mismatch [1]. According to the alloy composition of 30 groups
of medium axial cast superalloys, 14 microscopic parameters
above were calculated by the Thermo-Calc software. The Pearson
correlation coefﬁcient between the microscopic parameters and
the tensile strength and elongation at 900 /C176C was calculated
respectively. Finally, ﬁve microscopic parameters with the greatest
correlation of high-temperature tensile strength and elongation
were selected as T c0,V c0, Tl, TCP phases, and the processing win-
dow (Ts-T c0). Therefore, V c0,T c0 , and TCP phases content were
selected as the objectives of MOEA, while the processing window
(Ts-Tc0) and Tl were chosen as the constraints.

The trained model with the minimal error and the highest accu-

ML models mentioned above, the BPNN model achieved the best
performances in terms of error and accuracy on the sample data
simulated by the Thermo-Calc software. The BPNN model was con-
ﬁgurated with the elements, Ni, Al, Ti, Ta, Nb, Hf, Cr, Co, W, Mo, as
the inputs and the microscopic parameters, V c0,T c0, TCP phases
content, as the multi-task outputs. The Sigmoid function was used
as the activation function to map the neuron nodes in the input
layer into those in the hidden layer. The hidden layer to the output
layer was used as the linear output. The number of hidden layers
and hidden neurons in the BPNN model was adjusted by hand.
After several trials, the BPNN model with a single hidden layer
and 24 hidden neurons performed as the best structure according
to the training criterion in terms of minimal error and regulariza-
tion. The compositions in the sample data were normalized (i.e.
scaling the numerical range from 0 to 1) to avoid deviation
incurred by the different scales of variables so as to improve the
accuracy of the model. Furthermore, the ensemble learning tech-
nique can fuse multiple base models to decrease the prediction
error. As a result, there were 20 well-trained BPNN base models
which were selected for ensemble learning to reduce the bias of
the prediction results.
2.1.5. Multi-objective optimization
The MOEA realized a global search to obtain the Pareto front of
three objectives and its responding Pareto optimal solution set of
compositions. Here, Pareto means an ideal state of resource alloca-
tion with respect to multi-criteria. The Non-dominated Sorting
Genetic Algorithm with elite strategy (NSGA-II) [31] as an imple-
mentation of MOEA was employed to search the Pareto front of
the chosen BPNN surrogate model. The optimization objectives
and constraints were determined by the experience of domain
knowledge to search candidate compositions globally. The pre-
deﬁned preferences of the optimized objectives, V
c0,T c0, and TCP
phases, were speciﬁcally expected as close as possible to 65%,
1210 /C176C, and 0.01%, respectively. While the constraint thresholds
of the processing window (Ts-T c0) and Tl were speciﬁcally greater
than 40 /C176C and 1300 /C176C, respectively. The NSGA-II with its default
parameter settings was carried out to ﬁnd the Pareto optimal com-
positions that met the abovementioned multiple objectives and
constraints at the same time. The epsilon dominance [32] tech-
nique for improving the algorithm diversity was applied in the
NSGA-II to search for the better distributed Pareto front of the
tree-objective surrogate model. As a result, nine candidate compo-
sitions were obtained by uniform sampling for alloy preparation
and micro performance veriﬁcation.
2.2. Experimental procedure
Nine candidate compositions obtained by ML and MOEA were
veriﬁed by experiments. Raw metals with purity higher than
99.95% were applied. The oxides and impurities on the surface of
the raw metals were removed before processing. The alloy button
ingots were prepared by vacuum arc melting. To ensure the homo-
geneity of the alloy composition, every 100 g alloy was melted at
least six times and was homogenized by the magnetic stirring of
a vacuum arc furnace. The transformation temperatures were mea-
sured by TG-DSC (METTLER1100LF) within the high purity Ar ﬂow.
The samples in 4 mm /C2 4m m /C2 1 mm for DSC were tested within
the temperature range of 50–1400 /C176C at a heating rate of 5 /C176C min/C0 1.
The linear interpolation method was employed to measure Tl, Ts,
and Tc0 based on the DSC heating curves. After ultrasonic cleaning,
the as-cast ingots were subjected to solution heat treatment within
1180–1220 /C176C for 4 h followed by air cooling and subsequently
aged at 900 /C176C for 4 h followed by water cooling in Mufﬂe furnace.
All samples were metallographically prepared using standard tech-
niques and etched for a few seconds. In secondary electron imaging
mode, the microstructure was characterized by a ﬁeld emission
scanning electron microscope (FE-SEM, JEOL JSM-7900F). The alloy
compositions were measured by Energy-dispersive X-ray Spec-
troscopy (EDS) detector. The c0 volume fraction was calculated
from the mean value of ﬁve different microstructure images by
Image-Pro plus 6.0 software.
3. Results and discussion
3.1. Calculation
3.1.1. Pearson correlation parameter
In this work, the TCNi9 database of Thermo-Calc was used to
calculate the equilibrium thermodynamic data of compositions
for 30 brands of medium axial cast alloys. Then, Pearson correla-
tion screening was carried out between the microscopic parame-
ters and the tensile strength and elongation at 900 /C176C of Ni-
based equiaxed cast superalloy brand, respectively. The top three
correlated microscopic parameters on tensile strength were found
from Fig. 2 (a) as V
c0,T c0, and Tl. While on the elongation, the top
three microscopic parameters with the highest correlation were
analyzed from Fig. 2(b) as V c, the TCP phases content, and the pro-
cessing window (Ts-T c0 ). Among them, there was a strong correla-
tionship between V c0 and V c because their correlation coefﬁcient
was greater than 0.9 according to these 30 brand alloys. Therefore,
one of them, i.e., the former V c0 in this work, could express the fea-
ture in nature enough for characterizing the performances of ten-
sile strength and elongation. Thus, the ﬁve microscopic
parameters, T c0 ,V c0 , Tl, TCP phases content, processing window
(Ts-Tc0), were ﬁnally selected to characterize the tensile strength
and elongation of the superalloy to be optimized.
3.1.2. Optimization objectives determined by domain knowledge
Among the ﬁve microscopic parameters screened by the Pear-
son correlation analysis, the best high-temperature creep strength
and high-temperature rupture strength could be obtained when
the c0 volume fraction was around 65% [33–35]. Thus, the c0 vol-
ume fraction at 65% was regarded as one of the optimization objec-
tives. Besides, a higher c0 solvus temperature indicates to maintain
the stability of superalloy at a higher service temperature. Yet, the
subsequent heat treatment [36] of the designing superalloy is dif-
ﬁcult to put into practice if the c0 solvus temperature is set too high
in melting. Therefore, the c0 solvus temperature is considered at
1210 /C176C as the second optimization objective [37–39]. The additive
elements for solid solution strengthening such as W and Ta could
improve the high-temperature strength, but too high content
would promote the formation of TCP phases. Existing at the grain
boundary, TCP phases become the origin and aggregation of cracks
which would sharply decrease the strength of the superalloy [40–
42]. Therefore, it is indispensable to control the formation of the
TCP phases in the design of Ni-based superalloys. However, if the
optimization objective of the TCP phases was set to zero, the con-
tent of solid solution elements would be seriously compressed.
Apparently, it is not compliant with the domain knowledge-
driven design principle. Therefore, a very small TCP phases content,

3.1.3. Establishments of ML and MOEA
The comparisons of performance evaluation for multiple ML
algorithms were shown in Fig. 3 . The metrics about MAE, MSE,
RMSE, and R 2-score values of BPNN and SVR on three objectives
(Vc0 ,T c0, and TCP phases content) were signiﬁcantly lower than
those of GP, RBFN, and MLR. And the R 2 values of BPNN and SVR
were signiﬁcantly higher than those of the other three regression
models, which meant that the errors of BPNN and SVR were smal-
ler and the accuracy of BPNN and SVR were higher than those of
the others. The R 2 values of SVR were 0.5629, 0.7926, and 0.9338
on three objectives, respectively, while the R 2 values of the BPNN
were 0.9788, 0.9623, and 0.9268 respectively. The comparison of
evaluation parameters showed that the accuracy and the predic-
tion ability of BPNN were much better than those of SVR. An BPNN
model is a network interconnected by artiﬁcial neurons, where the
information moves forward unidirectionally, from the input nodes
(composition) to the hidden nodes, and ﬁnally to the output nodes
(microscopic parameters), as shown in Fig. 4. The BPNN is a kind of
multilayer FNN trained according to the error backpropagation
algorithm. BPNN is very suitable for mapping the nonlinear rela-
tionship between complex compositions and polyphase
microstructures. In addition, compared with other algorithms
established by multiple single-objective regression models, the
single multi-task neural network could train multiple outputs as
the prediction objectives at the same time, which is very suitable
for solving multi-objective optimization problems of material
design in this work.
The optimization objectives and constraints of superalloy
design for MOEA were determined according to the domain knowl-
edge. Here, the three objectives, V c’, Tc’, and TCP phases content,
were expected to be close as possible to their design preferences
at 65%, 1210 /C176C, and 0.01%, respectively, and the two constraints,
processing window (Ts-T c0) and Tl, were set higher than 40 /C176C
and 1300 /C176C. With these objectives and constraints in hand, a glo-
bal optimization was carried out by the NSGA-II, one instance of
MOEA, to ﬁnd the well-distributed Pareto front and its responding
Pareto optimal solution set for the candidate compositions of new
Ni-based superalloy. As a result, the three-objective Pareto front
was obtained by the NSGA-II in Fig. 5 (a). There were 200 candidate
compositions within the Region of Interest (ROI) in Fig. 5 (b). These
new compositions were concentrated around the design prefer-
ence point at (65%, 1210 /C176C, 0.01%). The candidate compositions
marked in blue hollow circles should be ﬁltered out because they
did not meet the two constraints. The 133 candidate compositions
marked in blue solid circles were the constraint-satisﬁed and non-
dominated optimal solutions which could be considered as the
new candidate superalloy materials to be veriﬁed experimentally.

3.2. Validation
After a preliminary veriﬁcation by the Thermo-Calc software,
the microscopic parameters of the above nine Ni-based superalloy
candidates were veriﬁed by experiment. The phase transformation
temperatures of 9 candidates were measured by DSC. Take candi-
date D6 as an example, which met the design preference of three

4. Summary
An intelligent design strategy based on ML and MOEA was pro-
posed for discovering new Ni-based superalloys in this work. Five
microscopic parameters associated with tensile strength and elon-
gation were screened by Pearson relevance analysis to be used as
the objectives and constraints for building the surrogate model
by ML. The global search algorithm MOEA was realized for opti-
mizing multiple objectives in terms of V c0,T c0, and TCP phases con-
tent under the constraints of the processing window and Tl
simultaneously. Nine candidate compositions from the Pareto front
obtained by MOEA were uniformly chosen to verify the micro-
scopic parameters of the optimized compositions. The phase trans-
formation temperatures and phases content veriﬁed by
experiments could prove the high accuracy of the surrogate model
based on the error evaluations. Finally, a Ni-based supperalloy
