ABSTRACT 
The short communication presents a synergistical reinforced Ti – 48Al alloy with multiple Ti – Al intermetallic 
compounds which was prepared with irregular pre-alloyed powders by vacuum sintering at low temperature 
(1250
◦
C). With simple composition, this alloy has tensile and yield strength of 593 ± 8 MPa and 435 ± 5 MPa at 
room temperature, and more than 510 MPa and 490 MPa at 700
◦
C, reached the level of Ti – Al alloys with 
complex composition. 
1. Introduction 
TiAl-based alloys are one of the most attractive candidates for high- 
temperature structural materials with low density, excellent creep 
resistance and oxidation resistance at high temperature [ 1 , 2 ]. From the 
first-generation alloy Ti – 48Al – 1V – 0.3C discovered by the P & W labora -
tory to the third-generation alloy Ti – (45 – 47)Al – (1 – 2)Cr – (1 – 5)Nb – (0 – 2) 
(W,Ta,Hf,Mo,Zr) – (0 – 0.2)B – (0.03 – 0.3)C – (0.03 – 0.2)Si – (0.1 – 0.25)O, the 
development trend of TiAl-based alloys has clearly shown high alloying 
and complex composition [ 3 , 4 ]. It is of great significance for the green 
sustainable development of TiAl-based alloys to reduce the use of 
alloying elements and improve the properties by microstructure 
adjustment. 
For this purpose, a variety of microstructure control methods have 
been developed. For example, coarse-grained pure Ti with nano-twin 
structure is prepared using cryoforging, with a tensile strength up to 
2 GPa [ 5 ]. Cu – Ag solid solution alloy composed of nanotwins and 
nanograins has been prepared by dynamic plastic deformation, and the 
tensile strength is as high as 870 MPa [ 6 ]. In-situ Al
2
O
3 
enhanced TiAl 
alloys is prepared by introducing oxygen atom into the matrix during the 
diffusion process [ 7 ]. For TiAl alloys, the reaction between Ti and Al is a 
reaction-diffusion process, and forms Ti
3
Al, TiAl, TiAl
2
, TiAl
3 
and other 
phases [ 8 , 9 ]. When the temperature rises to near the melting point, 
these phases gradually disappear and transform into a two-phase 
microstructure including Ti
3
Al and TiAl phase [ 8 – 11 ]. Therefore, it is 
difficult for conventional methods to strengthen TiAl alloys by the in -
termediate phases. 
Inspired by this, high-performance Ti – 48Al alloy reinforced by 
multiple Ti – Al intermetallic compounds were prepared by low- 
temperature sintering using pre-alloyed powders in this paper. Multi -
phase composite Ti – 48Al pre-alloyed powders were prepared by solid- 
liquid diffusion of TiH
2 
and Al powders. Low sintering temperature 
avoided the decomposition of fine TiAl
2
, TiAl
3
, Ti
3
Al
5 
and other inter -
mediate phases generated during the diffusion process. Accordingly, the 
cooperative reinforcement mechanism was clarified.

3.1. Mechanical properties 
As shown in Fig. 1 , the ultimate tensile strength (UTS) and yield 
strength (YS) of as-HIPed alloy at room temperature are 593 ± 8 MPa 
and 435 ± 5 MPa, respectively. At elevated temperature, the UTS are 
550 ± 6 MPa (500

elongation (EL) increases. Moreover, YS showed a trend of increasing 
first and then decreasing, which is a phenomenon known as yield stress 
anomaly (YSA) to intermetallic compounds [ 12 – 14 ]. YSA is related to 
thermally activated cross-slip of dislocations and the resulting disloca -
tion locking, and thus the yield strength will increase when the dislo -
cation density decreases [ 12 , 13 ]. The two main slip systems for plastic 
deformation of TiAl alloys are < 110 > {111} and < 101 > {111}, which 
become active above 700 K and are slip resistant at room temperature 
[ 12 , 14 , 15 ]. Therefore, Ti – 48Al alloy has the highest yield strength 
around 500
◦
C. 
Fig. 1 (b) compares the tensile properties of γ -TiAl based alloys re -
ported in literatures [ 16 – 22 ]. Ti – 48Al alloy prepared in this paper has 
excellent tensile properties without adding alloying elements. Room 
temperature UTS is significantly higher (above 50%) than that of other 
γ -TiAl based alloys containing Cr, Nb, Cu, and Co elements, while YS is 
increased by more than 25%. Besides, the strength at 700
◦
C is also 
significantly improved, especially YS increasing by more than 40% 
[ 18 – 22 ]. The properties of Ti – 48Al alloy significantly improve without 
adding alloying elements, which shows great development potential of 
low cost.

4. Discussion 
4.1. Phase formation mechanism 
About the Ti and Al diffusion process, Ti and Al particles will diffuse 
into each other below the melting point of Al and react at the particle 
interface to form TiAl
3 
phase [ 23 ]. When Al exists in the system, TiAl
3 
phase will exist in the alloy as the only Ti – Al intermetallic compound [ 8 , 
9 , 23 – 25 ]. It is commonly known that Ti has almost no solid solubility in 
Al or TiAl
3 
phase, while Al has great solubility in Ti, and Al is the most 
mobile element in Al-rich intermetallic compounds [ 8 , 11 , 24 ]. As a 
result, the diffusion in the Ti – Al binary system is mainly dominated by 
the mutual replacement between Al atoms and vacancies. This leads to 
the aggregation of a large number of vacancies at the interface, which 
eventually evolves into kirkendall pores at the interface. This problem is 
solved in this paper by preparing pre-alloyed powders. Due to the ex -
istence of kirkendall pores, loose and porous bulk samples formed by the 
reaction of TiH
2 
and Al powders are easily broken into fine pre-alloyed 
powders. In this study, the pre-alloyed powders have a fine particle size 
(D
50 
= 10 μ m). Consequently, the difficulty in sintering densification is 
greatly reduced in the subsequent sintering process. 
Once all Al atoms are transformed into TiAl
3
, Al will continue to 
diffuse from TiAl
3 
to Ti with the increasing temperature, and interme -
tallic compound layers such as Ti
3
Al, TiAl, TiAl

[ 8 , 9 ]. The formation of Ti
