abstract
The age-hardenable Mg-4.7%Y-4.6%Gd-0.3%Zr (wt%) alloy has been studied after high pressure torsion
(HPT) at room temperature, 200 °C and 300 °C. The formation of partially nanocrystalline structure upon
HPT results in substantial strengthening relative to the undeformed state of the alloy. The research
showed that the strength of the HPT processed alloy could be further improved by aging.

1. Introduction
Magnesium alloys are attractive materials due to their low
density and high strength. The addition of rare earth (RE) elements
remarkably improves mechanical properties of magnesium alloys
[1]. Therefore, many scienti ﬁc investigations have been carried out
to develop high-strength magnesium alloys [2]. Recently, the WE-
type Mg-Y-RE-Zr alloys were successfully tested in medicine as
biodegradable implants [3–5]. Among magnesium alloys, the Mg-
Y-Gd-Zr alloys are most promising compared to conventional WE
type alloys, because the Mg-Y-Gd-Zr alloys exhibit higher strength
[6–11 ]. The development of ultra ﬁne grained (UFG) structure is an
efﬁcient way to obtain high-strength magnesium alloys. Several
techniques based on severe plastic deformation (SPD) effectively
provided a UFG structure and superior properties of magnesium
alloys [12–22].
The aim of this work was to investigate the effect of SPD by
high pressure torsion (HPT) on the microstructure and mechanical
properties of a commercial Mg-Y-Gd-Zr alloy with small contents
of RE metals [23].
2. Experimental
The Mg-Y-Gd-Zr alloy bar was produced by hot extrusion of a
round ingot at 430 °C to an extrusion ratio of /C24 13. The alloy was
prepared by melting in an electrical resistance furnace under inert
atmosphere. To produce the alloy, we used pure magnesium
(99.9%) and the Mg-Y, Mg-Gd, Mg-Zr master alloys. The chemical
composition of the alloy (4.71% Y, 4.58% Gd, 0.31% Zr, and Mg as a
balance) was determined using an ULTIMA 2C inductively coupled
plasma atomic emission spectrometer.
Before the SPD treatment, the hot-extruded bar was annealed
at 510 °C for 4 h and quenched in water to room temperature (RT).
The SPD was performed by HPT of disk specimens of 20 mm in
diameter with Bridgman anvils at RT, 200 °C, and 300 °Ca tar o -
tation speed of 1 rpm under a quasi-hydrostatic pressure of 6 GPa
to 10 revolutions. The HPT was performed in a “groove” /C24 0.9 mm
deep. The true strain on the half-radius of the disks is
ε¼5.7. The
HPT samples were also isothermally aged at 175 or 200 °С for up
to 128 h to verify the ability of the alloy for additional hardening
due to a precipitation process.

3. Results and discussion
The cast structure of the alloy consists of a magnesium matrix
and a nonequilibrium second phase corresponding to the Mg 24(Y,
Gd)5 compound [24] (Fig. 1a). Before hot extrusion, the alloy was
homogenized for the dissolution of the Mg 24(Y,Gd)5 phase. The
microstructure of the hot-extruded bar consists of ﬁne re-
crystallized grains and deformed coarse grains elongated along the
extrusion direction ( Fig. 1b). Annealing at 510 °C for 4 h and sub-
sequent quenching result in complete recrystallization and grain
growth to an average grain size of /C24 50 mm( Fig. 1c).
The degree of deformation induced by HPT is known to in-
crease with the distance from the sample center [25]. This effect
leads to inhomogeneity of the structure and properties. Fig. 2 a
shows the microhardness inhomogeneity of the sample after HPT
at RT along the diameter on the surface that was in contact with
the ﬁxed deforming anvil and on the movable side that was in
contact with the rotating deforming anvil. The microhardness rises
from the center to the edge of the sample. Microhardness dis-
tribution on both surfaces is almost the same, except for slightly
higher microhardness of the sample edges on the surface that was
in contact with the ﬁxed deforming anvil. In this case, the mi-
crohardness can be higher due to hardening of the surface as a
result of friction. The analysis of the microhardness distribution
after HPT at 200 °C on the sample surface contacting the ﬁxed
anvil also shows a decrease in microhardness from the edge to the
center as well as a reasonable distribution symmetry ( Fig. 2 b).
Subsequently, the microhardness was measured on the half-radius
position on the surface contacting the ﬁxed deforming anvil.

A high microhardness level is achieved after HPT. The highest
microhardness corresponds to the HPT at RT, and the microhard-
ness level decreases with an increase in the deformation tem-
perature ( Fig. 3 ). The subsequent aging leads to an increase in
microhardness.
The thermal stability of the HPT-induced strengthening of the
alloy was estimated from the heating temperature dependence of
microhardness. The alloy was heated to temperatures of 100 –
400 °C with an interval of 50 °C and held for 1 h at each tem-
perature ( Fig. 4 ). With an increase in temperature to 100 °С, mi-
crohardness did not change because this temperature is
sufﬁciently low for structural transformations. In the temperature
range between 100 and 225 °С, the microhardness is likely to
increase due to the precipitation hardening. However, heating of
the samples to temperatures above 250 °С decreases the micro-
hardness because of the grain growth and precipitate coarsening.
After heating to temperatures above 400 °С, the microhardness of
the HPT samples becomes nearly the same as that of the un-
deformed sample. The level of strengthening by SPD and aging is
higher than that of obtained by conventional quenching and
subsequent aging, though their thermal stability is the same
(250 °C). Thus, the HPT-induced strengthening is retained after

We studied a possibility of strengthening the HPT processed
alloy upon aging at temperatures of 175 or 200 °С due to the
decomposition of magnesium supersaturated solid solution. Fig. 5a
and b presents the data on the microhardness of the alloy in
various initial states. Generally, the microhardness evolution upon
aging goes through a maximum, and the precipitation hardening is
replaced by subsequent softening caused by the growth of pre-
cipitates. Moreover, the SPD should accelerate the precipitation
process and decrease the temperature corresponding to the mi-
crohardness maximum [16]. In the case of the Mg-4.7%Y-4.6%Gd-
0.3%Zr alloy, no microhardness peak is reached in the undeformed
and HPT processed alloy even after aging for 128 h. The micro-
hardness of the alloy increases during the entire aging time and
reaches a “quasi-steady” state corresponding to 1600 MPa ( Fig. 5 a
and b). However, the hardest HPT sample reaches the “quasi-
steady” state of microhardness slightly faster than the quenched
sample. These data suggest the acceleration of the decomposition
of magnesium solid solution after HPT. With an increase in the
aging time, the electrical resistivity of the alloy decreases. This
decrease corresponds to the precipitation of yttrium and gadoli-
nium from the magnesium matrix ( Fig. 5c and d). The precipitation
process continues during aging and microhardness values after
longer aging time reaching a “quasi-steady” state. The method of
microhardness is less sensitive to structural transformations
compared to the method of electrical resistivity. A “quasi-steady”
state can be explained by the fact that microhardness may not
display simultaneous precipitation of new coherent phases that
increase microhardness and the growth and the loss of coherence
of the particles formed in the previous stages of aging that de-
crease microhardness. In general, the changes in microhardness
and electrical resistivity with an increase in the aging time at 175
and 200 °
С are identical.

deformation twins of different sizes and the regions of nano-grains
of 20–30 nm in size are present. We distinguished the shear bands
and the deformation twins analyzing the electron diffraction pat-
terns and comparing the dark ﬁeld and the bright ﬁeld images. If
the deformation twins were present in the structure, the addi-
tional twin re ﬂexes were observed in the electron diffraction
patterns (Fig. 7a). The shear bands were identi ﬁed by the dark ﬁeld
images obtained in the main matrix re ﬂexes. The presence of high-
angle boundaries is con ﬁrmed by ring-like electron diffraction
patterns with a number of point re ﬂexes (Fig. 7 b, c, e, and f). The
formation of nano-sized grains inside the shear bands and twins is
also observed. Aging at 200 °

Mg-4.7%Y-4.6%Gd-0.3%Zr alloy upon aging is caused by the for-
mation of metastable phases such as the ordered D019 β″ phase
and the base-centered orthorhombic β′ phase. The metastable
phases are coherent with the magnesium matrix and are ﬁne at
the aging stage corresponding to relatively short holding time. We
failed to reliably distinguish and identify the metastable phases
formed in the HPT processed alloy because of nanoscale sizes of
the structure constituents and a high dislocation density.
Therefore, strengthening induced by HPT at RT is due to the
structure re ﬁnement by reducing grain size to a nanoscale level,
the formation of the shear bands and deformation twins, and an
increase in total dislocation density. When the HPT temperature
increases, the strain hardening is reduced because of the grain
growth and a decrease in the total dislocation density. A certain
grain growth occurs upon aging. The increase in microhardness
upon aging is the result of the precipitation hardening, but we
should allow for softening caused by the grain growth.
The alloy quenched from 510 °
С after holding for 4 h is char-
acterized by UTS ¼280 MPa at elongation of 18% ( Table 1 ). After
HPT at RT, all samples exhibit brittle failure in the elastic region.
After HPT at 200 °С, the UTS is as high as 475 MPa, but the elon-
gation decreases to 2.5%. An increase in the HPT temperature to
300 °С leads to a decrease in strength to UTS ¼375 MPa at an
elongation of 5.5%. Fig. 8 presents the tensile stress-strain curves
for the samples processed by HPT at 200 °С and 300 °С. The alloy
samples after HPT at RT and 200 °С and subsequent aging at
200 °С for 16–24 h were also tested, but they also exhibited brittle
failure. However, according to the microhardness data, aging after
HPT leads to additional hardening.
4. Conclusions
1. A partially nanocrocrystalline structure with a grain size of 20 –
90 nm is formed in the Mg-4.7%Y-4.6%Gd-0.3%Zr alloy upon
severe plastic deformation by high pressure torsion (HPT) at
room temperature or 200 °
С.
2. The ability of the Mg-4.7%Y-4.6%Gd-0.3%Zr alloy after HPT at
room temperature for additional strengthening upon aging at
175 or 20 0 °С was shown by measurements of electrical re-
sistivity and microhardness.
3. The partially nanocrystalline structure of the Mg-4.7%Y-4.6%Gd-
0.3%Zr alloy with an average grain size of 60 –90 nm obtained
upon HPT at 200 °С leads to a high-strength state correspond-
ing to UTS ¼475 MPa at an elongation of 2.5%.
Prime novelty statement
Severe plastic deformation by high-pressure torsion (HPT) of
the Mg-4.7%Y-4.6%Gd-0.3%Zr alloy at the temperatures of 20 –
200 °С results in formation of partially nanocrocrystalline struc-
ture with the grain sizes of 20 –90 nm. HPT leads to signi ﬁcant
hardening of the Mg-4.7%Y-4.6%Gd-0.3%Zr alloy. Aging at 175 –
200 °С results in additional strengthening. Partially nanocrystal-
line structure of the Mg-4.7%Y-4.6%Gd-0.3%Zr alloy with an aver-
age grain size of 60 –90 nm obtained by HPT at 200 °
С leads to a
high-strength state of UTS ¼ 475 MPa.
