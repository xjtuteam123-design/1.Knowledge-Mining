Abstract. In order to replace Ti-Ni shape memory alloys, new biomedical s hape memory alloys 
have been developed which are composed of beta titanium and nontoxic el ements only. In this 
paper, experimental results of mechanical and shape memory propertie s are reported for the Ni-free 
Ti-18mol%Nb shape memory alloys containing 3mol% of 13-group and 14-group te rnary elements 
in the periodic table. The ternary elements selected are Al, G a, In, Ge and Sn. It was found that 
the solution treated alloys exhibit good shape memory effect but al most no pseudoelasticity at room 
temperature. Ultimate tensile strength and elongation to fail ure at room temperature are ranged 
from 250 to 710MPa and from 13 to 21%, respectively, depending on the kind of t ernary elements. 
Effect of solution hardening on strength is discussed. 
 
Introduction 
α , α/β , near-β and β titanium alloys become widely used in biomedical applications, espe cially for 
practical hard tissue implants, due to their superior biocompatibilit y, corrosion resistant, mechanical 
properties. In addition, low modulus of β titanium alloys, which is comparable to human bone, is 
attractive in terms of suppression of stress shielding. Then, several structural β titanium alloys 
equipping with both low modulus and high fatigue resistance have been proposed and applied 
practically such as Ti-6Al-2Nb-1Ta [1] and Ti-29Nb-13Ta-4.6Zr [2]. F rom the viewpoint of 
functional metal-base biomaterials, Ti-Ni shape memory alloys becomes widely used as stents, 
guidewires and orthodontic wires due to the unique shape memory and super elastic effect [3]. 
However, the anxiousness of Ni-hypersensitivity of Ti-Ni is conc erned. At present, no other 
practical biomedical shape memory alloys than Ti-Ni are known, then, new toxic-element-free 
shape memory alloys should be developed for advanced medical devices. A ccording to the 
background, our group has systematically innovated and developed several kinds of Ni-free 
titanium-base shape memory alloys such as Ti-Nb-Sn [4], Ti-Nb-Al [5], Ti-Nb-Ge [6], Ti-Mo-Al [7] 
and Ti-Mo-Ga [8]. Recently, the superelastic strain reaches 4.7% in a thermomechanical-treated 
Ti-Nb-Al [9] and the mechanism of large superelastic strain is revealed in terms of the texture 
formation [10]. Other related researches were the damping properti es [11], the anisotropy in 
Young’s modulus [12], and tensile properties of a cold-rolled Ti-Nb-Al [13]. These titanium-base 
shape memory alloys are classified into β titanium alloys, thus, these alloys are hopeful for practical 
biomaterials. Most of these titanium shape memory alloys are composed of Ti, binary additions 
such as Nb and Mo for β stabilizer, and 13- or 14-group elements such as Al in the periodic tabl e. 
However, no comparison in mechanical and shape memory properties was m ade in terms of the 
kind of ternary additions. Then, in this paper, the tensile properties of these Ni-free shape memory 
alloys at room temperature (RT) are compared each other. The b ase materials are selected to be 
Ti-18mol%Nb and the amount of ternary additions is constant of 3mol%.

Experimental Procedure 
The nominal compositions of the Ti-Nb base alloys were 79mol%Ti, 18m ol%Nb and 3mol% of 
ternary additions. The ternary elements selected were Al, Ga and In belonging to 13-goup and Ge 
and Sn belonging to 14-group in the periodic table. The alloys are called Ti18Nb3Al, for e xample, 
hereafter. These alloys were prepared by Ar arc melting m ethod with non-consumable W 
electrode in Ar-1%H 2 atmosphere using high purity starting elemental materials w ith 99.9% in 
purity at least. The ingots were homogenized at 1273K for 7.2ks in vacuum, and then cold-rolled 
to be 95% reduction in thickness. The reduction corresponded to 0.4mm in thic kness. A solution 
treatment at 1273K for 1.8ks in vacuum was carried out. No chemical ana lysis was carried out 
since little weight change was recognized during processing. It is noted that an oxygen content of 
another Ti-Nb-Al alloy made similarly is around 200ppm determined by atomic absorption 
spectrometry, therefore, the oxygen content of these alloys was thought to be comparable. 
Mechanical and shape memory properties were evaluated using bending tests and tensile tests. In 
the bending tests, plate specimens with 20mm in length, 2mm in width a nd 0.3-0.4mm in thickness 
were made by cutting, and deformed in a round shape at RT followed by unloading and heating by a 
conventional righter for shape recovery. The applied and residual stra ins were calculated to be 
d/(2 r+d), where d and r are the thickness of the specimen and curvature radius of specimens, 
respectively. The maximum applied strain was approximately 6% at surface. In tensile tests, the 
gage size of the specimens was 10mm in length and 1mm in width, and the specimens were cut 
from the sheet and damaged surface was eliminated by polishing. The tensil e tests were conducted 
at RT under the stain rate of 5x10 -4/s. In order to evaluate pseudoelasticity, a cyclic loading and 
unloading tensile test was selected where the applied strain incr eased by 2% per cycle with 
increasing the number of loading-unloading cycles. 0.2% flow stress (
σ 0.2 ) was employed as 
yield stress (or stress for inducing martensite or stress for reorientation of martensite variants). 
The tests were conducted up to failure, and ultimate tensile str ength (UTS) and elongation to failure 
(ε F) were evaluated. For the phase identification and microstruct ural characterization, θ -2θ X-ray 
diffractometry was conducted at RT using PAnalytical X’pert M PD with CuK α, and optical 
microscopic observation and 
transmission electron microscopic 
observation were done at RT. 
However, the details of the results 
obtained by XRD and TEM will be 
reported elsewhere.

Summary 
The shape memory and mechanical properties at room temperature we re evaluated for 
Ti-18mol%Nb alloys with 3mol% of ternary elements by the bending t ests and the cyclic 
loading-unloading tensile tests with strain increment of 2%. The t ernary elements were selected to 
be Al, Ga, In, Ge and Sn which belong to 13- and 14-goups in the periodic tabl e. It is found that 
almost perfect shape recovery appears in all the alloys but almost no pseudoelasticity appears at RT. 
By phase identification and microstructural observation, the apparent phas e at RT is α ” martensite 
regardless of the kind of ternary elements. Yield stress, ulti mate tensile strength and elongation to 
failure at room temperature are ranged from 120MPa to 230MPa, from 250MPa to 710MPa and 
from 13% to 21%, respectively, depending on the kind of ternary elements. The highest yield 
stress and UTS are achieved by the Ge addition. Although the effec t of solution hardening on 
strength by ternary additions was discussed, no relationship betwee n lattice mismatch and strength 
was recognized in this work.
