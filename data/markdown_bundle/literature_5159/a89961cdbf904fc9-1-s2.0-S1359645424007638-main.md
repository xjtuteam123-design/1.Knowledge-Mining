ABSTRACT
The strength of Pt-Al superalloys can be effectively improved using a coherent structure composed of a disor -
dered γ phase and an ordered γ ′ phase. However, γ ′ -Pt
3
Al has a cubic phase only at high temperatures and a 
tetragonal phase at low temperatures, thus breaking the coherent interface and deteriorating its mechanical 
properties. Here, high throughput first-principles calculations and rational experiments were conducted to 
discover novel γ - γ ′ Pt-Al superalloys. The alloying strategy stabilizes the γ ′ phase through the local charge 
distortion (LCD) or the local lattice distortion (LLD). Alloying elements, such as, Ti, Hf, and Ta, occupy the Al site 
in combination with Pt, making the spherical shape of change density in Pt
3
Al showing obvious directionality, i. 
e., LCD. Therefore, the stability of the γ ′ phase is proposed at a lower alloying element concentration (3.125 at. 
%). However, alloying elements near Pt, e.g., Ni, Co, and Ru, tend to occupy the Pt site and induce LLD to 
stabilize the γ ′ phase at higher alloying element concentration (12.5 at.%). The shape of the change density is 
similar to that of Pt
3
Al, and no obvious LCD is found when alloying elements occupy the Pt site. LCD-induced 
phase stability is observed exclusively at the Al site, whereas LLD-induced phase stability is solely present at 
the Pt site. The Pt-12Al-6X ( X = Hf, Ti, Ta, Cr, and Ni in at.%) alloys were prepared, among which Pt-12Al-6Hf 
possessed a stable γ ′ phase, a lower lattice misfit between γ and γ ′ , and a higher precipitation strengthening, 
resulting in its hardness (442.1 H
V
) surpassing that of Ni-based superalloys IN718 (413.6 H
V
). The γ ′ phase in Pt- 
12Al-6Hf was characterized by high-resolution transmission electron microscopy, suggesting that the Pt site of 
the γ ′ phase was occupied by Pt, while the Al site was shared by Pt, Al, and Hf. This is consistent with the 
calculated results.
1. Introduction
Platinum (Pt) based superalloys are promising materials for glass 
bushing, thrust chambers, and rocket nozzles, which are used at tem -
peratures reaching up to 1500 
◦
C, owing to the high melting point (1768 
◦

However, the tensile strength of Pt is low (125 MPa at room tempera -
ture) [ 9 ]. Solid solution strengthening or oxide dispersion strengthening 
(ODS) has been used to enhance the strength of Pt-based superalloys. For 
example, the 10-h stress rupture strength at 1873 K for Pt, Pt-30 wt.% 
Rh, and Pt-30 wt.% Ir were 0.9, 5.6 and 10.0 MPa, respectively [ 11 ]. The 
100-h stress rupture of ODS Pt-10 wt.% Rh at 1723 K is 10.0 MPa [ 11 ]. 
Nevertheless, the prohibitive cost of Rh and the poor machinability of 
ODS Pt-based superalloys pose a significant impediment to the 
continued development of Pt-based superalloys. The superalloys, e.g., 
Ni-based superalloys, exhibit distinctive mechanical properties and 
environmental resistance owing to the coherent cubic γ ′ phase (Ni

2.2. Details of first-principles calculations
All first-principles calculations were performed using the Vienna Ab 
initio Simulation package (VASP) [ 34 ]. The electron-core interaction 
was evaluated using the projector augmented waves (PAW) method 
[ 35 ], and the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) of Per -
dew – Burke – Ernzerh (PBE) was chosen to describe electron 
exchange-correlation interaction. The cutoff energy was taken as 1.3 
times the higher value than the VASP default energies for all elements in 
the alloys of study. The convergence criteria of self-consistency for 
electronic and ionic steps were at least 10
– 6 
eV/atom and 0.001 eV/Å, 
respectively. The 10 × 10 × 10, 6 × 6 × 6, and 4 × 4 × 1 k -point meshes 
were used for the 2 × 2 × 2 (32 atoms), 3 × 3 × 3 (108 atoms), and 2 × 2 
× 4 (96 atoms) supercells, respectively. All crystal structures were fully 
relaxed with respect to the atom positions, cell shape, and cell volume.

The strength of the γ - γ ′ alloy originates mainly from the solid solution 
strengthen (SSS) and precipitation strengthening [ 60 ]. The SSS origi -
nates from the lattice misfit and modulus misfit. The SSS can be esti -
mated using the first-principles calculations and the Labusch model 
[ 60 ]. The lattice misfit factors were evaluated, ranging from 0.003 to 
0.049, while the modulus misfit factors were quite different, ranging 
from 0.068 to 1.966, among which the Pt-12Al and Pt-12Al-6Ni alloys 
show the larger modulus misfit. However, the high modulus misfit 
comes from the fact that the alloy elements greatly reduce the elastic 
modulus of the γ phase in Table 2 , which softens the material and is 
unfavorable to improving the strength of the material. Fig. 15 (a) shows 
the SSS of Pt-12Al-6X ( X = Hf, Ti, Ta, and Cr). Ta was dissolved into the γ 
phase, giving it the highest shear modulus and enhancing the strength, 
showing agreement with the nanoindentation results, i.e., the γ phase of 
Pt-12Al-6Ta has the highest hardness.

dislocation looping mechanism. Previous studies reported that the shear 
mechanism acts on small precipitates, whereas the Orowan dislocation 
ring mechanism acts on large precipitates [ 61 ]. The size of the precip -

scale. Therefore, the dislocation looping mechanism plays a dominant 
role. Moreover, the contribution of the dislocation looping mechanism 
to strength was evaluated using Orowan strengthening ( Δ σ

strength of the Pt-based superalloys can be improved dramatically by 
imitating the γ - γ ′ two-phase microstructure, as in Ni-based superalloys. 
Pt-12Al-6Ta has a high hardness in γ and γ ′ phases, but its Vickers

Pt-12Al-6Ti (386.4 HV). This may be due to its reticular microstructure.
4. Conclusions
The present work was conducted to address the instability of the 
cubic Pt
3
Al phase and discover novel γ - γ ′ Pt-Al superalloys based on the 
phonon spectrum, formation enthalpy, mixing enthalpy, partitioning 
enthalpy and lattice constants. The instability of cubic Pt
3
Al at room 
temperature originated from the weak Pt-Al atomic pairs and the 
negative SFCS of the Pt-Pt atom pairs. The alloying strategy resulted in 
the variation of local atomic environments, such as LCD or LLD, 
enhancing chemical bonding and stabilizing the cubic Pt
3
Al phase. The 
alloying elements stabilizing the L1
2 
phase depend on their concentra -
tions and types. The energy priority of Pt
3
Al was evaluated using the 
formation energy, and the mixing enthalpy was used to judge whether 
the alloying element was easy to dissolve. The linear fitting coefficient of 
the lattice constant ( k ) and partitioning enthalpy were introduced 
concurrently to evaluate the lattice misfit. The alloying elements Hf, Ti, 
Ta, Cr, and Ni were selected for experimental characterization. The γ - γ ′ 
two phase microstructure was measured in Pt-12Al-6X ( X = Hf, Ti, Ta, 
and Cr), among which the Pt-12Al-6Hf alloy exhibited a lower lattice 
misfit, higher volume fraction of γ ′ phase and precipitation strength -
ening, resulting in a hardness surpassing that of Ni-based alloy IN718. 
The present work promotes the development of novel Pt-Al superalloys 
with enhanced mechanical properties and stability that can be used in 
extreme environments.
