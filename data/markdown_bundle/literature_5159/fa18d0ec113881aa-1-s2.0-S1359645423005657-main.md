ABSTRACT 
Cast & wrought Ni-based superalloys are required for manufacturing high-performance components in gas tur -
bine engines. Their excellent high-temperature properties and corrosion resistance are brought about by their 
complex microstructure of a γ -matrix, intra- and intergranular γ’ precipitates, carbides, and borides. The most 
highly-alloyed superalloy grades provide the high-temperature strength required for the design of next gener -
ation gas turbine engines but their processability remains challenging due to grain boundary (GB) cracking. A 
common mitigation approach is the addition of B and C to improve GB cohesion and strength. However, their 
detailed roles in GB segregation and precipitation and the link to GB properties during processing remains largely 
unexplored. 
We provide a systematic study on decorated GBs in the cast & wrought Ni-based superalloy Ren ´e 41 with B and 
C additions. Tensile testing reveals that B additions reduce its ductility due to GB cracking but slow down grain 
growth during heat treatments more effectively, than C additions. GB-M

1. Introduction 
Cast & wrought Ni-base superalloys are high-temperature resistant 
materials essential to manufacture both static and rotating components 
in gas turbine engines [ 1 , 2 ]. Current requirements on their 
high-temperature strength include yield strength of 600 MPa at tem -
peratures up to 800 
◦
C for several 1000 h of service [ 3 , 4 ]. However, 
more efficient future gas turbine engines require designs utilizing the 
most highly alloyed grades [5 – 7] , which are limited in their manufac -
turability, e.g. due to grain boundary (GB) cracking [8 – 10] . Addressing 
this issue is critical to retain the economic competitiveness of cast & 
wrought processed Ni-based superalloys. For example, highest strength 
grades are processed via more expensive powder metallurgy routes [1] . 
Designing superalloys for cast & wrought processing has the potential to 
widen their applications and reduce production costs. As a common 
mitigation approach, microalloying elements such as B and C are added 
to improve mechanical and/or manufacturability properties via GB 
cohesion and strengthening, however, this may create complex GB mi -
crostructures [11 – 13] :
