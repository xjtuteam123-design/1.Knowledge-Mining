1. Introduction 
With the development of new generation aviation propulsive system, 
the requirements of higher thrust-to-weight ratio and greater combus -
tion efficiency facilitate the continuous exploration of refractory metal 
intermetallic composites (RMICs) to become a candidate material for 
aeroengine hot component [ 1 , 2 ]. Among the most representative are the 
Nb
ss
/silicide in situ composites, superior physical properties and excel -
lent comprehensive mechanical performances, these factors contribute 
to the ascendency and also exhibit an extensive application prospect

Therefore, no matter Nb

3.4. Fracture behavior and damage evolution 
Under the different annealing conditions, the crack propagation 
paths of three-point bending measurements are displayed in Fig. 10 . 
After conventional heat treatment, a relative straight crack can be 
observed in the CHT alloy ( Fig. 10 (a)), only minor bridge connection is 
visible near the crack root ( Fig. 10 (b)). But as the gradient annealing 
proceeded, the crack propagation and toughening behaviors appear 
varieties. Along the loading direction, a lot of microcracks can be seen 
adjacent the main crack path as well as the obvious deflection phe -
nomenon ( Fig. 10 (d)). These microscopic crack deflections gradually 
accumulate to macroscopic crack bowing; comparing with other heat 
treatment processes, the maximum angle of bowing is generated in the 
GA-1 alloy owing to the gradient annealing at 850 
◦

(c)). In the GA-2 alloy, the gradient annealing process produces a 
marked effect to bifurcate and eventually form the secondary crack 
( Fig. 10 (f)) which also can be seen in Fig. 10 (g). While for longer-time 
gradient annealing, the characteristics of crack propagation in GA-4 
alloy ( Fig. 10 (i) and (j)) are essentially similar to that of CHT alloy.

(a)) and GA-4 alloy ( Fig. 11 (i)). Clearly, the failure processes of Nb
