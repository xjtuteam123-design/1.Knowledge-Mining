HIGH-TEMPERATURE STRENGTH OF REFRACTORY METALS, ALLOYS
AND COMPOSITE MATERIALS BASED ON THEM.
PART 1. TUNGSTEN, ITS ALLOYS, AND COMPOSITES
V. K. Kharchenko and V. V. Bukhanovskii UDC 539.4
The results of investigations of the mechanical characteristics of tungsten, alloys and composite
materials based on it, which are used in space-rocket hardware, have been generalized. The
investigations were carried out under short-time, sustained static and low-cycle loading conditions
on small time bases in a temperature range of 290–3020 K.
Keywords: tungsten and its alloys, composite materials, short-time strength, long-term strength, one-second creep,
plasticity, correlation relationship.
Introduction. Refractory metals and alloys and composite materials (CMs) based on them, which possess a
unique set of physicomechanical characteristics, are widely used in aircraft equipment and space-rocket hardware
(SRH), in the manufacture of parts and assemblies which are acted upon by high loads and temperatures, aggressive
gaseous media and other unfavorable operation factors [1–3]. Among the main requirements to so critical articles are
high reliability and economy. This can be ensured by a package of measures, among which great importance is
attached to increasing the accuracy of the strength design of construction, which ensures the predetermined margin of
its load-carrying capacity. This is possible on the basis of the knowledge of the properties of the material under the
conditions that most closely resemble the operation conditions, with allowance for its structure parameters and
influence of technological factors. Among the refractory metals, tungsten has the highest high-temperature strength,
corrosion and erosion resistance in aggressive media and the highest melting point (3695 K).
The present paper reviews and generalizes the results of investigations of the mechanical characteristics of
tungsten, alloys and CMs based on it, which are used in SRH, under short-time and sustained static loading
conditions on small time bases in a wide high-temperature range; the investigations have been carried out during
some years at the Pisarenko Institute of Problems of Strength (IPS) of the National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine
(NASU).
Mechanical Characteristics of Tungsten and Its Alloys under Short-Time Static Loading. Works on
creating laboratory facilities and developing procedures for the investigation of the mechanical characteristics of
refractory metallic materials under different loading conditions at high temperatures were started at the IPS under the
guidance of the Member of the NASU G. S. Pisarenko in the 1960s. In the first stage of investigations, the
temperature dependences of strength, ductility, hardness and elastic moduli for commercially pure refractory metals
(99.95%), such as tungsten, tantalum, molybdenum, niobium, and low alloys based on them, obtained by casting,
powder metallurgy (PM) and vacuum arc melting (VAM) in the initial state and after heat treatment, had been
determined on the basis of a methodological base created. The results of investigations are described in [4–7].
In the 1970–1980s, the problem of increasing the load-carrying capacity of various parts and structural
members of SRH became vital. Bench tests resulted in crack formation in them, which caused failure. This called for
a detailed study of physicomechanical properties because of the peculiarities of the macro- and microstructure of the
materials of articles of different batches, which is formed after hot rolling, casting, annealing, deposition of

Mechanical Characteristics of AVMG-1, VNDS -1, and VDMP Tungsten-Copper Pseudoalloys.
Tungsten-copper pseudoalloys (TCPAs) are used, thanks to their unique physicomechanical properties, as structural
materials for SRH parts, which are operated under the action of intensive heat flows. Their normal operation under
such extreme conditions is possible through self-cooling and heat stress relaxation, which are inherent in CMs
consisting of a refractory tungsten skeleton and copper-filled pores. The melting and evaporation of copper from
parts is accompanied by a strong endothermic effect, which prevents the overheating of the refractory skeleton of
structural member. The composites VNDS-1 and VDMP are fabricated by impregnating a sintered porous tungsten
skeleton with copper and AVMG-1 by the hot pressing of a tungsten and copper powder mixture [14–17].
In the works [18, 19], the characteristics of the elastic moduli ( E, G) of tungsten-copper pseudoalloys in the
temperature range 77–2770 K had been determined for the first time (Fig. 3). As a result of an analysis of
experimental data, formerly unknown laws of variation of these characteristics had been established. The studies of
the strength and ductility of TCPAs were carried out in a wide temperature range (up to 3300 K) depending on CM
composition (density, refractory constituent and copper content), manufacturing technique, ambient pressure, the
peculiarities of deformation and failure owing to development of microcracks (Fig. 4).
An analysis of plots of strength and ductility characteristics against temperature for tungsten-copper
composites fabricated by different manufacturing techniques with allowance for their connection with material
fracture micromechanisms allowed their optimal compositions, which were obtained by impregnation and hot
pressing, to be determined with allowance, among other things, for the chemical composition and fineness of starting
powders. The process of deformation of pseudoalloys at high temperatures took place by the brittle loosening of the
material with the formation of grain-boundary microcracks. The strength of the composites VNDS-1, VDMP, and
AVMG-1 differs because of different temperature conditions of sintering of the refractory skeleton and its
impregnation with copper, as well as other technological peculiarities. Thanks to the results obtained, search was
started for ways of increasing the strength and ductility of pseudoalloys by increasing the strength of W–W interfaces
to the crystallite strength level, in particular through the microalloying of tungsten grains with rare-earth metals.

To “keep” copper in specimens in high-temperature mechanical tests of pseudoalloys, a new procedure was
developed, and a NDT-10 setup was created for the mechanical testing of CMs, containing low-melting constituents,
at an increased ambient pressure of 10 MPa in a wide temperature range (up to 2800 K) [7]. It was shown that the
evaporation of copper from pseudoalloys before mechanical tests reduces the strength of the tungsten skeleton by a
factor of 1.1–2.3 (depending on alloy grade) in the entire temperature range and increases its ductility in the
high-temperature region.
Regressive models describing the strength of the pseudoalloys VNDS-1, AVMG-1, and VDMP with
allowance for composition and temperature were proposed, and the composition of these materials was optimized for
critical density.
CONCLUSIONS
1. Physicomechanical characteristics have been determined, and laws of deformation and fracture of
tungsten, alloys and CMs based on it under short-time and sustained static loading on small time bases in a wide
temperature range with allowance for the influence of the chemical composition of material, structure, technological
and operational factors have been established.
2. The results of investigations were utilized in the choice of composition, optimization of the manufacturing
technique for tungsten-based materials and in strength designs of critical parts and structural elements of SRH
objects.
