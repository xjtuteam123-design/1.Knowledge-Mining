ABSTRACT
Advanced nuclear reactors require materials with excellent performance under harsh conditions. 
Molybdenum (Mo) and its alloys are key candidates for cladding and core structural applications 
due to their high-temperature properties. However, balancing strength and plasticity in Mo alloys 
remains challenging, as most alloying elements focus on strengthening with less obvious tough -
ening effects. The complex interactions of alloying elements are difficult to fully understand 
through traditional testing alone. To address this, materials genetic engineering leverages data- 
driven approaches to predict and optimize material properties, accelerating R & D cycles. In this 
study, a BPNN regression model optimized by Bayesian methods was developed based on a 
dataset of 177 Mo-based alloy samples with 38 compositions, focusing on tensile strength and 
elongation at elevated temperatures. The model achieved an R
2 
value of 0.83 and was used to 
predict the effects of various single and multi-element additions on alloy performance. Results 
indicate that Re (~15 wt%), Zr (~0.08 wt%), and Ti (~0.2 wt%) additions, with minimal N 
content ( < 0.0025 wt%), optimize the strength and toughness of Mo alloys at 800 
◦
C.
1. Introduction
The core structural materials such as cladding in advanced nuclear reactors face harsh service environments such as high tem -
perature, neutron irradiation and liquid alkali metal corrosion. The comprehensive performance of molybdenum and its alloys is one of 
the bottlenecks restricting the development of advanced reactors. Molybdenum and its alloys are key candidate materials for future 
special nuclear reactor cladding due to their high melting point, high thermal conductivity and good compatibility with alkali metals. 
However, the tensile strength of pure molybdenum at room temperature is about 650 MPa and the elongation is 0 %. It has problems 
such as low room temperature plasticity, insufficient high temperature strength, recrystallization brittleness and irradiation embrit -
tlement [ 1 – 3 ]. As with other metals, alloying can significantly improve molybdenum ’ s high-temperature properties [ 4 ]. Solid solution 
strengthening and second phase strengthening are the two most typical methods. For solid solution strengthening, experimental results 
show that the possibility of alloying molybdenum while keeping the metal deformable is extremely limited, and the total amount of 
added elements other than W and Re does not exceed 1 % (mass fraction). Trace amounts of Ti and Zr will form Mo-Ti and Mo-Zr solid 
solutions in molybdenum grains, causing lattice distortion and playing a role in solid solution strengthening. The solid solution of Ti 
will change the lattice constant of Mo, significantly improving the brittleness of Mo. At the same time, due to the strong affinity of Ti for 
O, oxide particles are formed inside the alloy, which can not only reduce the formation of voids and purify oxygen atoms, but also

2. Data collection and processing
The research data in this paper were obtained through dual channels of experimental testing and literature data mining, covering 
multi-dimensional heterogeneous information on molybdenum-based alloy composition design, process parameters and mechanical 
properties. The original data set contains 452 groups of samples, of which there are 152 pure Mo alloy (Mo content ≥ 99.99 %) 
samples, accounting for 33.6 %; 124 Mo-xRe alloy (Re content 0.5 %~50 %) samples, accounting for 27.4 %; 56 TZM alloy (Ti: 0.4 % 
~0.55 %, Zr: 0.01 %~0.1 %, C: 0.02 %~2%) samples, accounting for 12.4 %; 56 ODS-Mo alloy samples, accounting for 12.4 %; 20 Mo- 
xSi alloy (Si content 0.1 %~1%) samples, accounting for 4.4 %; 16 Mo-xLa
2
O

for 3.5 %; 15 Mo-xLa alloy (La content 0 %~2%) samples, accounting for 3.3 %; There are 6 Mo-xZrC alloy (ZrC content 0.6 %) 
samples, accounting for 1.3 %; 5 Mo-xW alloy (W content 80 %) samples, accounting for 1.1 %; and 2 Mo-xNb alloy (Nb content 3 % 
~6%) samples, accounting for 0.4 %. It can be found that the Mo-based alloys in the data set are mainly divided into Mo-xRe and TZM 
alloys. Adding Re to Mo-based alloys can significantly improve the strength, plasticity and high-temperature stability of the alloy at the 
same time. This effect is called the Re effect. Due to its good Re effect, the Re element can be added to the Mo base at a ratio of 1 % to 45 
% to form a Mo-xRe alloy. In the TZM alloy, Ti, Zr and C are all added in trace amounts.
The data set contains 41-dimensional feature variables, which are 38 element composition features, including: major element 
features: mass percentage (wt.%) of Mo, Re, Ti, Zr, etc. and trace element features: doping concentration (ppm level) of interstitial 
elements such as C, O, N, etc.; 1 process and environmental parameter: tensile test temperature (25 ~ 1200 ℃ ); 2 target variables: room 
temperature/high temperature tensile strength (UTS, MPa), elongation (Elongation, %).
During the data collection process, some variables will have some missing values and outliers. Such data used for modeling will 
result in poor prediction effect and low prediction accuracy of the model, and it is impossible to obtain a prediction model that is 
helpful for actual production. In this paper, data quality analysis and cleaning mainly include missing value processing and outlier 
detection. First, multiple interpolation methods can be used to fill in missing data with missing component content. The interpolation 
range is limited based on physical constraints (such as the Mo content is 100 % minus the value of other component contents) to avoid 
generating non-physical feasible solutions. For systematic missing data caused by experimental equipment errors and failure to obtain 
experimental data, including the missing of target variables tensile strength and elongation, the method of directly deleting this data is 
adopted. After cleaning, the effective sample size is reduced to 182 groups. In the outlier processing stage, this study first implements 
data cleaning for the problem of alloy composition deviation. The nominal composition range of molybdenum-based alloys (Mo 
content ≥ 50 at.%) is combined with the solid solubility limit of the Mo-W binary phase diagram (W ≤ 30 at.%) to identify the 
abnormal composition samples in the original data set. It was found that the W content of 5 groups of samples was abnormally high 
(80.2 ± 2.4 at.%), and its alloy system has exceeded the scope of molybdenum-based alloys and is closer to the W-Mo master alloy 
system. Based on the principle of homogeneity of material system and the definition of research boundaries, this study performed strict 
data deletion operations to remove the samples that did not meet the composition constraints mentioned above to ensure the con -
sistency of the chemical composition of the data set. After cleaning, the number of valid samples was reduced to 177 groups. This 
processing strategy effectively maintains the feature space integrity of the molybdenum-based alloy material genome and provides a 
normalized data set that meets the thermodynamic equilibrium conditions for subsequent machine learning modeling. Next, some data 
with abnormal target values were checked, and abnormally high strength values (such as UTS > 1800 MPa of Mo-45Re alloy at room 
temperature) and negative elongation values (instrument recording errors) were removed. It was found that both target values in the 
177 groups of data obtained in the previous step met the specifications. Finally, in the normalization processing stage of alloy 
composition data, this study discretized the data for the common composition interval characterization problem in the data, and 
preferentially selected the upper limit of the interval as the feature input. The upper limit of the composition corresponds to the 
maximum solid solubility threshold under the irradiation environment, which can effectively evaluate the material failure risk under 
extreme working conditions. Finally, after cleaning, the number of valid samples was reduced to 177 groups, and the data stan -
dardization and integrity were improved to 100 %.

3. Machine learning models and the corresponding implementation
In Section 2 , the collected Mo alloy dataset is preprocessed, including feature selection, data cleaning and normalization, to ensure 
the input data quality of the model. Faced with the characteristics of small data sets, low dimensions and large number of features in 
the current materials field, shallow models such as decision trees [ 24 ], random forests [ 25 ] and backpropagation (BP) neural networks 
[ 26 ] in current machine learning models are widely used because they are relatively simple, easy to understand and implement. With 
the rapid development of data science and materials informatics, the application of these models is gradually evolving towards more 
advanced and complex methods to better capture the complex relationship between material properties and structure.
In this paper, we used BP neural network, a powerful machine learning tool, to conduct in-depth regression modeling analysis on 
complex material performance data sets. BP neural network is an ideal choice for processing multivariate and nonlinear relationship

This paper designs a multi-layer BP neural network structure. By adjusting the number of hidden layers, the number of nodes, and 
the activation function, the model architecture is optimized to adapt to the complex changes in material properties. First, the objective 
function is established, which receives two parameters, units and epochs, which represent the number of hidden layer units and the 
number of training rounds in the neural network, respectively. Inside the function, a sequential model bp_model is first established, and 
then two fully connected layers and one output layer are added according to the parameter settings, and the model is compiled using 
the Adam optimizer. Next, the model is fitted using the training data, and the model performance is evaluated on the validation set, 
returning the mean square error on the validation set as the value of the objective function. In the training stage, the gradient descent 
method is combined with the back-propagation algorithm to iteratively update the network weights and biases to minimize the error 
between the predicted value and the actual value.
If the BP neural network does not optimize the weights and thresholds, the accuracy of the model is limited, and the prediction 
effect is poor. The Bayesian optimization method has been recognized as the most advanced technology in the field of hyperparameter 
optimization, namely State of the Art (SOTA) [ 27 ]. Bayesian optimization first establishes prior knowledge about the global behavior 
of the objective function (usually represented by a Gaussian process), and then updates this prior knowledge by observing the output of 
the objective function at different input points to form a posterior distribution. Based on the posterior distribution, the next sampling 
point is selected. This selection must consider both the previously observed optimal value and the globally unexplored area. This 
selection strategy is usually defined by a so-called acquisition function, such as the most used expected improvement. In this way, 
Bayesian optimization can not only effectively search the hyperparameter space, but also guide the search based on existing knowl -
edge, avoiding many useless attempts.
The stacked network structure is built using Keras ’ Sequential Model. The first fully connected layer (Dense Layer) contains 64 
neurons, and the activation function is ReLU. Then the dimension of the input feature is added, and X_train.shape [ 1 ] represents the 
dimension of the input feature. The number of neurons in the second fully connected layer is determined by the function parameter 
units. ReLU is still used as the activation function. The output layer has only one neuron, which is used to output a value for regression 
tasks, and the default linear activation is used. In the process of compiling the model, the mean square error (MSE) is used as the loss 
function, and the Adam optimizer is used. In addition to calculating the loss, the MSE indicator is also measured during training/ 
evaluation. When training the model, the training set (X_train, y_train) is used for model training. After each epoch, the model per -
formance is evaluated using the test set. The number of training rounds is specified by the function parameter epochs. Each time the 
weight is updated, batch_size (after repeated several times, it was found that batch_size = 64 is more stable) samples are used as a 
batch. After training is complete, the test set (X_test, y_test) is used for final evaluation. The evaluation index is then returned. Overall, 
the constructed model function uses units and epochs as hyperparameters, dynamically creates and trains a Keras neural network 
model, and then returns the MSE index on the test set. In the Bayesian optimization process, different units and epochs values are 
repeatedly passed into this function to try to find the hyperparameter combination that minimizes MSE.

4. Interpretability to ML models
To gain a deeper understanding of the “ black box ” of machine learning models, we used the SHAP method to explain how features 
affect the target mechanical properties. Lundberg et al. [ 29 ] proposed a method for explaining the predictions of machine learning 
models, SHAP, which is a method inspired by game theory. SHAP generates a value (also called a SHAP value) for each input feature, 
which indicates how the feature contributes to the prediction of a specified data point; some factors have a positive impact on the 
prediction probability, while others have a negative impact on it. For each mechanical property, we selected a database with the 
highest prediction accuracy of the ML model for further SHAP analysis.

In the numerical correlation of Re element in alloying elements, Re element is negatively correlated with tensile strength and

value and the Spearman correlation. As a refractory metal element, Re can effectively strengthen grain boundaries. The essence of 
grain boundary strengthening lies in the hindering effect of grain boundaries on dislocation movement. The segregation of Re atoms at 
grain boundaries can significantly improve the strength of grain boundaries. This strengthening mechanism is particularly significant 
at high temperatures, because grain boundaries usually appear as weak links at high temperatures, and the addition of Re can 
effectively improve this problem. However, the role of Re in solid solution may be opposite to grain boundary strengthening. When Re 
exists in the molybdenum matrix in the form of solid solution, the difference between its atomic radius and molybdenum may cause 
lattice distortion, which will hinder dislocation movement to a certain extent, thereby playing a strengthening role. However, if the Re 
content is too high, it may cause excessive local lattice distortion, which will reduce the plastic deformation ability of the material, 
which is manifested as a decrease in strength. The SHAP value reflects the overall contribution of Re in model prediction, including its 
comprehensive effect at different temperatures and different compositions. Since Re has a dual role in grain boundary strengthening 
and solid solution softening, the SHAP value may capture more of the positive contribution of Re in grain boundary strengthening. 
However, the Spearman correlation analysis is only based on the overall trend of the data, which may reflect more of the weakening 
effect of Re in the solid solution, resulting in a contradiction between the positive SHAP value and the negative Spearman correlation. 
Re has a dual role in grain boundary strengthening and solid solution softening in molybdenum alloys. This complex metallurgical 
mechanism may be the reason for the contradiction between the SHAP value and the Spearman correlation. In practical applications, it 
is necessary to comprehensively consider the Re content and its mechanism of action under different environments to optimize the

In this study, under small sample conditions, a Bayesian optimized BPNN regression model was designed to predict the mechanical
