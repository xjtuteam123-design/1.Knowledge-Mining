1. Introduction
Metastable β titanium alloys have been
attracting increasing attention in the last
few decades as structural components for
use in the aerospace, naval, and automobile
industries.[1] This is due to their advan-
tages, including high tensile and fatigue
strengths, high hardenability, and excellent
formability compared with α/β titanium
alloys.[2] However, these alloys have also
some disadvantages compared with α/β
titanium alloys, including relatively higher
costs (due to the more expensive β stabil-
izers), a greater possibility of segregation
during melting, low plasticity, and poor
fatigue properties of the weldment.
Moreover, their mechanical properties
and microstructure instabilities are sensi-
tive to minor variations in hot working con-
ditions or heat treatments, and design data
are lacking.[2,3] Such shortcomings resulted
in a limited application of metastable β titanium alloys. Thus,
more investigations on these alloys should be conducted to pro-
mote their widespread application. The Ti –5Al5Mo5V3Cr1Zr
(Ti-55 531) alloy, which is a variant of the analogous
Ti–5Al5Mo5V3Cr0.5Fe (Ti-5553) alloy,[4] is a relatively new meta-
stable β titanium alloy with high strength and toughness based
on the Ti–5Al5Mo5V1Cr1Fe (VT22) alloy.[5] In contrast to the Ti-
5553 alloy, the thermomechanical processing ability of Ti-55 531
alloy can improve slightly with the addition of Zr element and
lower Fe and O elements without affecting its strength. [6,7]
Indeed, despite this small difference, the Ti-55 531 alloy is almost
the same as the Ti-5553 alloy. These two alloys are typically used
as the replacement of the Ti –10V2Fe3Al (Ti-1023) alloy used for
thick-section forgings in the aircraft industry (e.g., for landing
gears, ﬂap trunks, linkage units between wings, and hanging
slash).[8,9]
There have been many studies on similar types of alloys
involving thermomechanical processing, [4,8,10] solid-phase
transformation,[11–16] the relationship between microstructure
and properties, [3,17,18] and their welding or cyclic deformation
behavior.[7,19–21] For instance, Warchomicka et al. [4] studied
the hot deformation behavior of Ti-55 531 alloy and determined
its mechanism of restoration active of deformations.

The work aims to reveal relationship between solution temperature, micro-
structure, and mechanical properties of Ti – 5Al5Mo5V3Cr1Zr (Ti-55 531) alloy.
The alloys are prepared via solution processing in a temperature ranging from
Tβ /C0 100 °C (where Tβ is β-transus temperature) to Tβ þ 100 °C for 2 h; and then
air cooled, aged at 600 °C for 6 h, and air cooled again. Results indicate that
solution temperature range (from Tβ /C0 100 °C to Tβ þ 100 °C) can be divided into
following ﬁve ranges according to performance of Ti-55 531 alloy. The alloy
presents excellent mechanical performance combining high strength and good
ductility for solution temperatures ranging from Tβ /C0 50 °C to Tβ /C0 30 °C and
from Tβ þ 10 °C to Tβ þ 30 °C. Interestingly, the Ti-55 531 alloy exhibits a dra-
matically enhanced strength, while ductility drops rapidly as solution temperature
exceeds Tβ þ 70 °C. The enhanced strength ’s main reason is that grain boundary
α phase changes from continuous to discontinuous with increasing solution
temperature; nevertheless, the increased size of β grains results in a reduction in
ductility. Thus, continuity of grain boundary α phase has a dramatical in ﬂuence
on performance of Ti-55 531 alloy.

Indeed, only a limited amount of data of this alloy is available for
the design of components. Thus, the purpose of this work is to
reveal the effect of the solution temperature on microstructural
characteristics and mechanical properties of Ti-55 531 alloy and
understand its microstructure –properties relationship. With this
aim, in the present work, specimens were solution treated for 2 h
at different temperatures ranging from Tβ /C0 100 °C (where Tβ is
the β-transus temperature) to Tβ þ 100 °C and air cooled (AC);
they were then aged at 600 °C for 6 h and AC again.
Furthermore, the size and volume fraction of the α-phase
(including αp particles and αs lamellae) and the parameters of
the retained β matrix were quantitatively analyzed using the
IPP 6.0 image analysis software. Subsequently, the relationship
between tensile properties and microstructure parameters of all
specimens after different heat treatments was carefully dis-
cussed. Moreover, tensile properties of Ti-55 531 alloy were also
compared with Ti –6Al4V alloy as a function of solution temper-
ature ranging from Tβ /C0 100 °C to Tβ þ 100 °C, respectively.
Interestingly, in comparison with Ti –6Al4V alloy, tensile perfor-
mance of Ti-55 531 alloy as a function of solution temperature
ranging from Tβ /C0 100 °C to Tβ þ 100 °C exhibits a greatly
different phenomenon, owing to the special microstructure-
performance relationship of T i-55 531 alloy. These results
could provide a support for the design of engineering
structures and promote the wid er application of this alloy in
engineering.
2. Experimental Section
The experimental materials were Ti-55 531 and Ti-6Al4V alloys
forged-bar billets with a diameter of about 350 mm supplied
by the Northwest Institute for Nonferrous Metal Research in
China. The chemical composition test results were basically
the same as the results of our previous work in the study by
Huang et al. [25]. Specimens with a diameter of 11 mm and a
length of 75 mm were cut from the bars for subsequent heat
treatments. The solution parameters were selected based on
the Tβ of Ti-55 531 alloy ( Tβ ¼ 830 /C6 5 °C) and Ti-6Al4V alloy
(Tβ ¼ 965 /C6 5 °C). All specimens of Ti-55 531 alloy were placed
in a solution at a temperature ranging from 730 to 930 °C
(Tβ /C0 100 °C to Tβ þ 100 °C) for 2 h and AC; subsequently, they
underwent an aging treatment at 600 °C for 6 h and were AC. All
specimens of Ti-6Al4V alloy were placed in a solution at a tem-
perature ranging from 865 to 1065 °C (from Tβ /C0 100 °C to
Tβ þ 100 °C) for 2 h and water quenched; subsequently, they
were aged treated at 550 °C for 6 h and were AC. The details
of heat treatment procedures of Ti-55 531 and Ti-6Al4V alloys
are shown in Table 1 and 2, respectively.
Cylindrical tensile specimens with a center diameter of 5 mm
and gauge length of 25 mm were machined after the above heat
treatments according to the Chinese standard GB/T228.1-2010.
Tensile tests were carried out using an INSTRON 5975 testing
machine at room temperature with an initial strain rate of
0.5 mm min/C0 1 (10/C0 3 to 10 /C0 4 s/C0 1). The reported performance
data corresponded to the average of three individual tests to
improve the accuracy. More details on the tensile testing can
be found in our previous work. [26]
Microstructures and fracture surfaces of specimens were
investigated via optical microscopy (OM; Axio Vert A1), scanning
electron microscopy (SEM; JSM-6460 and JSM-6700 F), and
transmission electron microscopy (TEM; JEM-200CX). The vari-
ous scale features of all types of α and β phases were quantita-
tively characterized using the IPP 6.0 Image analysis software.

α/β interphase increases upon increasing the amount of the
βtrans microstructures. As a result, the strength of the alloy
increases with increasing solution temperature. Moreover, the
softer αp phase plays a primary role in the deformation process
of the alloy, which promotes higher ductility of the alloy. [28]
Therefore, the alloy with numerous αp particles indicates high
plasticity level in this range. On the other hand, the βtrans
microstructure is so hard that it cannot easily transfer the defor-
mation from the nearby softer αp particles. Thus, the ductility of
the alloy decreases upon increasing the βtrans fraction in range (1).

In range (3), from Tβ /C0 30 °C to Tβ°°C (800 to 830 °C), the
strength of the alloy dramatically declines with increasing solu-
tion temperature, while its ductility remains almost constant or
decreases slightly. This phenomenon can be explained by the two
following reasons. First, as mentioned earlier, when the solution
temperature is close to Tβ, the size of the prior β grains starts to
increase with increasing solution temperature, as demonstrated
in Figure 3. According to the “Hall–Petch” relationship, the
strength of the alloy decreases with increasing grain size.
Therefore, the decrease in the strength of the alloy with increas-
ing the prior β grains size can be described by the “Hall–Petch”
relationship. Second, the residual αp phase will gradually be res-
olution treated into the β matrix until all αp particles disappear as
the solution temperature increases during solution processing
(Figure 2). Subsequently, on the one hand, the αs lamellae
coarsen due to the absence of the limiting αp particles during
aging (Figure 1c –f ). The coarsening αs lamellae have a longer
effective slip length, which can improve the plasticity of the
alloy.[24,29] Thus, the alloy still exhibits good and stable ductility,
although the increase in the grain size leads to a slight reduction
in its plasticity. In addition, the volume fraction of the αs lamellae
would also decrease due to the coarsening αs lamellae, which
results in a decrease in the number of αs/βr interfaces, which
provide obstacles to the dislocation movement. [30,31] Therefore,
the hardness of βtrans in range (3) decreases. On the other hand,
the volume fraction of the β stabilizers relatively decreases as a
consequence of the increase in the amount of the α stabilizers in
βtrans microstructure in range (3). Therefore, the lower solid-
solution hardening effect results in the alloy exhibiting lower
strength. As a result, the strength of the alloy declines strongly.
In range (4), from Tβ to Tβ þ 70 °C (830 to 900 °C), the evolu-
tion of mechanical properties of the Ti-55 531 alloy with temper-
ature is similar to that observed in range (3). The only difference
is that αp particles do not appear in this range due to fact that the
solution temperature is always higher than Tβ. Therefore, the
strength of the alloy decreases with increasing solution temper-
ature due to the increase in the β grain size.
In range (5), from Tβ þ 70 °C to Tβ þ 100 °C (900 to 930 °C), it
is interesting to note from Figure 5 that a dramatical change in
the mechanical properties in Ti-55 531 alloy starts from the solu-
tion temperature of 900 °C. As the solution temperature
increased further above this temperature, the strength of the
alloy dramatically increased, while the ductility dropped rapidly.
This phenomenon of the Ti-55 531 alloy is totally different from
that of other Ti alloys, such as the Ti-6Al4V alloy. Figure 6 shows
the trend of mechanical properties of the Ti-6Al4V alloy as a func-
tion of the solution temperature in the range from 865 to 1065 °C
(from Tβ /C0 100 °C to Tβ þ 100 °C). The temperature was kept for
2 h, followed by water cooling, aging at 500 °C for 6 h, and air
cooling. It can be found that the strength and ductility of the
Ti-6Al4V alloy vary slightly at a solution temperature above
Tβ þ 70 °C. This interesting result can be preliminary explained
as follows. It is well known that as the solution temperature
reaches Tβ, microstructures of Ti-55 531 alloy consist of fully
lamellar microstructures, which are composed of αs lamellae
and the residual β matrix after aging treatment. As the solution
temperature increases, the alloy produces numerous αs lamellae,
whose density eventually results in the growth of αs lamellae
being hindered. Thus, the αs lamellae are reﬁned with increasing
solution temperature. In this temperature range, αp particles are
completely dissolved in the β matrix, which leads to an increase
in supersaturation. During the aging process, a large amount of
αs phase precipitated from the β matrix. Previous studies[32] indi-
cated that the strength of β titanium alloys can be enhanced
either by increasing the volume fraction of αs phase or re ﬁning
αs phase. The αs lamellae produce a high density of αs/βr inter-
faces, which provides a barrier to the dislocation movement and
causes extra dislocation pinning effect. [31] Moreover, the βtrans
microstructure is so hard that the strengthening of the alloy
primarily depends on amount of the βtrans microstructure. In
contrast, the size of β grains increases with increasing solution
temperature, when the temperature exceeds Tβ.[33] However, the
decrease in the strength caused by the β grain size is smaller than
the increase in the strength caused by αs/βr interfaces. Thus, the
alloy solution treated at a temperature in range (5) exhibits a dra-
matically enhanced strength. However, the strength and plastic-
ity usually show the opposite behavior, that is, the plasticity
decreases with increasing strength. Most importantly, the
increased size of β grains leads to a reduction in the ductility.
Therefore, the alloy strength increased sharply, while the ductil-
ity declined rapidly in range (5). However, the real reason for this
behavior is very complex, and the mechanism will be further dis-
cussed in the following sections.

4. Discussion
The tensile performance, deformation, and fracture mechanism
of alloys treated at different solution temperatures ranging from
Tβ /C0 100 °C to Tβ þ 100 °C were analyzed. The results show that
the alloy presents an excellent combination of the highest-level
strength and good ductility in solution temperatures ranging
from Tβ /C0 50 °C to Tβ /C0 30 °C and from Tβ þ 10 °C to
Tβ þ 30 °C. Moreover, the alloy solution treated at a temperature
ranging from Tβ /C0 50 °C to Tβ /C0 30 °C has a better performance
than that solution treated at a temperature ranging from
Tβ þ 10 °C to Tβ þ 30 °C. Most importantly, it is very interesting
that the strength of the alloy started to dramatically increase, as
the solution temperature exceeds 900 °C, while the ductility
dropped rapidly. This may be caused by the difference in micro-
structural parameters. Furthermore, the microstructural features
were found to depend greatly on the solution temperature.
Therefore, the relationship between the solution temperature,
microstructural characteristics, and fracture mechanism is fur-
ther discussed in this section.
4.1. Effect of the Solution Temperature on Microstructural
Features and Deformation Mechanism of the Ti-55 531 Alloy
It is well known that microstructure affects the properties of an
alloy.[23] Thus, to tune the morphology, volume fraction, and dis-
tribution of all phases by changing the treatment temperature,
the activation, multiplication, and movement of dislocations will
also be affected; this is an effective method to control the strength
and plastic deformation of titanium alloys. [34,36] The alloys solu-
tion treated at temperature in ranges (1) –(3) exhibited the
bimodal microstructure (Figure 1c –f ), while the typical lamellar
microstructure was formed for the other solution temperature
ranges (Figure 1g –j). Previous works [26,30,34] revealed that the
bimodal microstructure of Ti-55 531 alloy is primarily controlled
by the {10( –)10} prismatic slip and {0001} basal slip of the glob-
ular αp phase. This phenomenon was also found in other α/β and
metastable β-titanium alloys.[28,37] In addition to slipping, shear-
ing and {10( –)11}<11(–)20> twinning of the coarsening αs
lamellae are the other main deformation mechanisms of the
lamellar microstructure. [38,39]
To further reveal the effect of the solution temperature on the
deformation mechanism and performance of Ti-55 531 alloy,
defect structures of the alloys treated at various temperatures
(730, 780, 860 and 920 °C) after tensile deformation were inves-
tigated via TEM, as illustrated in Figure 9 . A large number of
piled-up dislocations were primarily observed in the equiaxial
αp phase for the alloy solution treated at 730 °C (Figure 9a)
due to the deformation mechanism mainly controlled by globular
αp phase in the bimodal microstructure. [34] Moreover, high-
density dislocations were obstructed by the αp/βtrans interfaces,
which would otherwise propagate and stack along the globular
αp particles. Furthermore, a few dislocations were also observed
in the acicular αs laths; however, the density of dislocation is
lower than that in globular αp phases (Figure 9b). Figure 9c,d
shows defect structures of the alloy solution treated at 780 °C
after tensile deformation. Both the volume fraction of αp particles
and the size of αs laths of the alloy treated at 780 °C are lower than
those of the alloy treated at 730 °C due to the increase in solution
temperature. However, a similar variety in the distribution of dis-
locations was found in the microstructure after tensile deforma-
tion (Figure 9c). Moreover, a few parallel straight multiple slip
lines inside the globular αp phase were also detected
(Figure 9d). With increasing solution temperature, the αp par-
ticles gradually redissolved in the β matrix. Subsequently, a large
number of the αs phase separated from the β matrix during
aging. As the solution temperature reached Tβ, almost all αp par-
ticles vanished after heat treatment. As previously mentioned,
the primary deformation of this lamellar microstructure with
no αp particles occurred on the softer αs lamellae.[26] The defor-
mation mechanisms of the lamellar microstructure have been
controlled by slipping, shearing, and deformation twins of the
αs lamellae.[33] Therefore, only high-density dislocations were
detected inside the coarse αs lamellae (Figure 9e,f ). Thus, many
αs lamellae have a close arrangement, which leads to the
distortion of the αs lamellae after tensile deformation
(Figure 9e). Furthermore, the special orientation of some αs
lamellae, which leads to the movement of dislocations, was
greatly hindered.[40] Therefore, some αs lamellae were character-
ized by a high density of dislocations during deformation, as
shown in Figure 9g,h.
The results show the occurrence of microstructural changes
from the bimodal to the lamellar microstructure with increasing
solution temperature, which causes changes to the deformation
mechanism of the alloy. It is evident that the tensile deformation
of the bimodal microstructure mainly depends on globular αp
phase, which is similar to the results reported by Shao et al. [24]
As mentioned earlier, the alloy treated at a temperature in range
(5) exhibits the acicular αs phase with a nanoscale and close
arrangement, which hinder the movement of dislocations. [41,42]
Previous studies [3,33] indicated that the strength of Ti alloys is
inversely proportional to the solution temperature. As a result,
the strength was greatly enhanced at a high solution tempera-
ture, especially above 900 °C.

4.3. Effect of the Solution Temperature on the Tensile
Properties of Ti-55 531 Alloy
Tuning the solution temperature to control the morphology, size,
and distribution of the α and β phases in the microstructure is an
effective method to tailor the strength and plastic deformation of
materials.[23] The Ti-55 531 alloy treated at a solution temperature
from 780 to 800 °C exhibits excellent mechanical performance
combining the highest strength (UTS: /C25 1300 MPa) and good
ductility (El /C25 10%) (Figure 5). When the solution temperature
is below /C25 780 °C ( Tβ /C0 50 °C), the strength of the alloy greatly
increases with increasing solution temperature, while the plastic-
ity dramatically decreases. This is because the volume fraction of
the harder βtrans increases with increasing solution temperature,
while the amount of the softer αp phase declines.[3] Upon increas-
ing the solution temperature from 800 °C ( Tβ /C0 30 °C) to 900 °C
(Tβ þ 60 °C), the strength of the alloy starts to decline. However,
the plasticity of the alloy still remains stable at above 8%. This is
because the prior β GBs and continuous GB α are present in this
temperature range, which are the weakest features of Ti-55 531
alloy under tension. [44] Moreover, although the large sizes of β
grains could result in reduction in the plasticity of the alloy,
numerous deformation twins can be detected inside the coarsen-
ing α lamellae under tension. Twinning promotes the continue
movement of some hindered dislocations and improves the plas-
ticity of the alloy. [26] The enhancement in the plasticity promoted
by twinning is equal to or slightly greater than the decline in the
plasticity caused by the increase size in β grains. Thus, the alloy
exhibits good plasticity in this solution temperature range.
Most importantly, the minimum strength of Ti-55 531 alloy
with lamellar microstructure was achieved when the solution
temperature reached 900 °C. However, the strength exhibited
a sharp increase when the solution temperature was in the range
from 900 to 930 °C (Figure 5). Correspondingly, the ductility of
the alloy gradually declined to a very low level (about 4%). This
interesting change in the performance of Ti alloys has never been
reported before. To further investigate the effect of a higher solu-
tion temperature on the mechanical performance of Ti-55 531
alloy, the microstructure –performance relationship for alloys
treated at three different solution temperatures (860, 900, and
930 °C), including the transpoint temperature, is discussed as
follows.
It is well known that the crack initiation of lamellar micro-
structure mainly occurs at the β GBs or continuous GB α, coars-
ening αs lamellae inner, and αs/βr interface.[45,46] Moreover, the
crack nucleation occurs most frequently at β GBs or continuous
GB α.[44] In general, the GB α phase can obviously in ﬂuence the
mechanical properties of the alloy through its quantity and con-
tinuity; in particular, continuous GB α can dramatically reduce
the plasticity of Ti alloys. [19] Numerous works [7,26,30,44] reported
that the GB α is the favorite crack initiation site of the lamellar
microstructure under tension, torsion, and fatigue deformation.
GB α is softer than the β grain, and the strain mismatch between
adjacent β grains results in the strain concentrating at GB α,
which subsequently leads to microviod nucleation and coales-
cence at GB α. However, in fact, the GB α has two effects on
the plasticity of the Ti alloys. On the one hand, the soft GB α
easily leads to the crack initiation at the GBs. However, on
the other hand, the GB α with relatively good plasticity can
induce the crack tip closure and prevent the crack propagation
to a certain extent, especially the microcrack propagation.
Therefore, a few dimples can be detected at the GB on the frac-
ture surface of lamellar microstructure; such a phenomenon is
known as the intergranular dimple fracture. [39] In this work,
when the solution temperature increased from 860 to 930 °C,
the GB α gradually became discontinuous and even disappeared
at a temperature above 900 °C (Figure 11). Therefore, the ductility
of the alloy treated below 900 °C with the continuous GB α is
higher than that of the alloy treated above 900 °C with no GB
α. Furthermore, the size of the β grains increased (Figure 3),
which resulted in the strength of the alloy declining with solution
temperature (Figure 5). Nevertheless, the GB α ﬁlms changed
from being continuous to discontinuous when the temperature
exceeded 900 °C. In addition, numerous ﬁne αs platelets
appeared at the GBs during the ﬁnal aging process
(Figure 11c). A large number of αs platelets at the GBs broke

5. Conclusion
The whole solution temperature range (from Tβ /C0 100 °C to
Tβ þ 100 °C) can be divided into ﬁve ranges according to the
mechanical performance of Ti-55 531 alloy. The alloy with the
best mechanical performance combining high strength and good
ductility is obtained in two solution temperature ranges, namely
range (2): from Tβ /C0 50 °C to Tβ /C0 30 °C and range (3): from
Tβ þ 30 °C to Tβ þ 50 °C. The alloy treated at a temperature in
range (2) exhibits UTS of 1296 MPa and an El of /C25 10%. The
UTS of the alloy treated at a temperature range (3) is
1200 MPa and its El is /C25 10%. The alloy treated at a temperature
range (2) presents the highest strength in conjunction with good
ductility in the whole temperature range.
The fracture mechanism of Ti-55 531 alloy is mixed over the
whole solution temperature range. However, the primary frac-
ture mechanism changes from the transgranular mechanism
of microvoid nucleation and accumulation to form dimples to
the brittle cleavage fracture mechanism with increasing solution
temperature.
Interestingly, Ti-55 531 alloy exhibits dramatically enhanced
strength, while the ductility drops rapidly as the solution temper-
ature exceeds 900 °C. With the increase in the solution tempera-
ture, the GB α changes from continuous to discontinuous, which
causes the formation of numerous ﬁne αs platelets at the β-GBs.
Meanwhile, the high density of αs/βr interface hinders disloca-
tion movement, which leads to the higher strength of the alloy.
The increased size of β grains results in reduction in the ductility.
