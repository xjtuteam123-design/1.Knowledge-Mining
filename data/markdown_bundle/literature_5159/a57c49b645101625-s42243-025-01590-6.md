Abstract
Tantalum (Ta) alloys have been widely utilized in rocket, air-breathing engines, and airframe applications. However,
traditional Ta alloys suffer from insufﬁcient strength at ultra-high temperature, making it challenging to satisfy the design
requirements for next-generation aerospace equipment. We report a novel strategy to design Ta alloys with superior
mechanical properties by integrating the multi-principal element concept with compositionally complex carbides. By
introducing multiple refractory elements and C, the resultant alloys displayed a thermally stable microstructure consisting
of two phases. With the increasing C contents, the microstructure evolved from hypoeutectic to eutectic and then to
hypereutectic. These varying microstructural characteristics inﬂuenced crack blunting and dislocation accumulation
behaviors, leading to different softening resistance at 1600 /C176 C and plasticity at room temperature. Beneﬁting from the
strengthening effects of solid solution and compositionally complex carbides, these alloys exhibited a high strength
of *600 MPa at 1600 /C176 C, signiﬁcantly superior than that of traditional Ta alloys.

1 Introduction
High-temperature (HT) structural materials with excellent
mechanical properties are urgently needed to meet the
continuously increasing demands for engineering perfor-
mance and energy efﬁciency in various sectors, including
energy, aerospace, and nuclear industries [ 1, 2]. Tantalum
(Ta), characterized by its high melting temperature
(T
m
Ta = 2996 /C176 C, surpassed only by rhenium and tung-
sten), excellent thermal shock resistance, and notable plas-
ticity, has been considered as a promising candidate
material for HT applications [ 3, 4]. Nevertheless, the
strength of Ta decreases signiﬁcantly ( \ 50 MPa) at tem-
peratures above 1600 /C176 C, which greatly restricts its broader
industrial application. Various strategies have been adopted
to enhance the strength of Ta. For example, by alloying
with refractory elements such as W and Hf, the strength of
Ta–10W and T111 (Ta–8W–2Hf) alloys was
increased *3.2 times via solid solution strengthening [ 5].
The strength and creep resistance were improved in
ASTER-811C (Ta–8W–1Re–1Hf–0.025C) alloy through
the synergistic effects of solid solution strengthening and
the second-phase precipitation [ 6]. However, due to the
limited addition of alloying elements, these Ta alloys still
exhibit insufﬁcient strengths and diffusion-dominated
softening at HT.
The recently emerged multi-principal element alloy
(MPEA) design paradigm, or commonly referred to as high
entropy alloys (HEAs), has been proposed as a promising
approach for designing novel HT structural materials with
remarkable mechanical properties [ 7–11]. By incorporating
multiple elements, MPEAs can achieve high conﬁgura-
tional entropy and a complex local atomic structure. These
characteristics lead to sluggish atomic diffusion and sig-
niﬁcant local lattice distortions. Given that the Gibbs free
energy ( G = H–TS, where H represents enthalpy; S repre-
sents entropy; and T represents temperature) determines the
thermodynamic stability of alloys under constant pressure,

Introducing the multi-principal element concept in Ta
alloys can facilitate the design of high-performance HT
structural materials. Firstly, the high Tm and great plasticity
of Ta are beneﬁcial to overcoming the common limitations
of low Tm and poor RT ductility observed in existing
MPEA systems [ 21, 22]. Secondly, the incorporation of
multiple elements provides an effective strengthening
effect for traditional Ta alloys [ 23]. Although some Ta-
containing MPEAs have been developed [ 7], their RT
plasticity remains extremely poor because of the extensive
addition of metallic elements, and further improvements in
strength at ultra-high temperatures are required. Additional
research is essential for optimizing the composition and
microstructure of Ta alloys to fully realizing their potential
for HT applications. Among various strategies, the intro-
duction of compositional complex carbides (CCCs) has
proven to be an effective way to enhance the mechanical
properties of MPEAs [ 24–27]. By constructing dual-phase
structures with alternating lamellar arrangements of alloy
and carbide phases, a great combination of RT and HT
mechanical properties can be obtained [ 28].
Inspired by these studies, we have developed novel Ta
alloys. As an illustrative example, several representative

2 Materials and methods
C0.5, C1.0, and C1.3 alloys were prepared through vacuum
arc-melting under a high-purity argon atmosphere. Ta, W,
Mo, and Nb particles with a purity of 99.9 wt.%, along with
C powder, were chosen as the raw materials and weighted
according to the designed compositions. These raw mate-
rials were placed in the water-cooled copper crucible, with
low-melting-point materials positioned at the bottom and
high-melting-point materials placed at the top. Before the
vacuum arc melting process, the sample chamber was
evacuated to a vacuum level exceeding 5 9 10
–3 Pa, fol-
lowed by the introduction of high-purity Ar gas (purity of
99.999%) as both a protective medium and arc-burning
medium. During the melting process, the current was
maintained 350 -450 A to ensure complete melting of all
raw materials. To obtain ingots with homogeneous chem-
ical compositions, the melting procedure was repeated 8
times, with each melting lasting at least 2 min. Before each
melting cycle, the ingot was ﬂipped and tilted approxi-
mately 40 /C176 . Throughout the whole melting process, high
purity Ti was employed as a getter to remove residual gases
within the chamber. The resulting ingot exhibited a
dimension of approximately /20 mm 9 8 mm.
The samples for microstructural characterizations and
mechanical property measurement were subsequently
extracted from the ingots via electrical discharge machin-
ing. RT compressive tests were performed on an Instron
machine using samples with dimensions of /2m m 9 4
mm at a strain rate of 1 9 10
–3 s-1. A strain gauge was
installed to ensure precise measurement. Each alloy was
subjected to at least 3 independent tests to ensure the
reliability of the data. A vacuum furnace in conjunction
with a Zwick KAPPA 100 dynamic thermal simulation
testing machine was employed to carry out compression
tests at a temperature of 1600 /C176 C. To eliminate the inﬂu-
ence of oxidation on mechanical properties, the sample was
processed in a vacuum HT furnace. The system was ini-
tially evacuated to a pressure below 1 Pa, followed by the
introduction of high-purity Ar gas (purity of 99.999%). The
air-washing process was repeated at least 3 times to ensure
minimal residual oxygen content in the vacuum furnace.
The vacuum furnace maintained a high-purity Ar

3 Results
3.1 Mechanical performance
The compression engineering stress–strain curves of C0.5,
C1.0, and C1.3 alloys at RT and HT are displayed in Fig. 1,
which indicates that the mechanical performance is greatly
affected by C contents. As shown in Fig. 1a, among these
alloys, C0.5 alloy illustrated the highest plasticity of
14.0% ± 0.5% with a 0.2% offset engineering yield
strength as high as 1820 ± 21 MPa and an ultimate
strength of 2872 ± 42 MPa at RT. With the increasing C
contents, the yield strength increased to 2090 ± 30 MPa
(C1.0) and 2390 ± 55 MPa (C1.3), while the compressive
strength showed no severe changes (2869 ± 60 MPa for
C1.0 alloy and 2738 ± 58 MPa for C1.3 alloy). However,
RT plasticity decreased continuously, which was
8.1% ± 0.3% for C1.0 alloy and 2.5% ± 0.2% for C1.3
alloy.
All alloys showed an engineering strain [ 35% without
fracture at 1600 /C176 C (Fig. 1b). For C0.5 alloy, the yield
strength was 595 ± 20 MPa, which was nearly 4.5 times
that of the traditional T-111 Ta alloy [ 5]. C0.5 alloy also
exhibited great resistance to HT softening, as indicated by
its nearly constant strength with increasing strain. C1.0
alloy had the same level of yield strength as C0.5 alloy,
while the yield strength increased to 715 ± 33 MPa for
C1.3 alloy. However, signiﬁcant softening occurred during
the deformation process of C1.0 and C1.3 alloys. It can be
observed that after initial yielding, the engineering stress
gradually decreased with the increasing strain.
We compared HT yield strength and RT plasticity of the
designed alloys with traditional Ta alloys and previously
reported Ta-containing refractory MPEAs, and the results
are shown in Fig. 1c[ 5, 6, 14, 29–32]. Compared with
traditional Ta alloys that exhibit limited strength at ultra-
high temperatures, the designed CCC-strengthened Ta
alloys process signiﬁcantly higher strength at 1600 /C176 C.
Besides, the designed Ta alloys exhibit greater RT plas-
ticity than Ta-containing refractory MPEAs. Especially,
C0.5 alloy shows the best combination of HT strength and
RT plasticity. It can also be concluded that the mechanical
performance of CCC-strengthened Ta alloys can be tai-
lored by changing C content. Speciﬁcally, increasing C
content leads to the improved HT strength, while RT
plasticity is reduced.
3.2 Microstructure characterization
The microstructure of CCC-strengthened Ta alloys was
systematically investigated to reveal the origins of their
mechanical properties. XRD patterns (Fig. 2a) of the
designed Ta alloys showed that all alloys contain a BCC,
(space group Im
3 m) and a hexagonal close-packed (HCP,

C atoms occupying alternating layers of octahedral inter-

results also showed that the peak intensity associated with
HCP phase enhances with higher C content, which suggests
an elevated fraction of HCP phase.

content inﬂuences the microstructural morphology. For

characterized by dendritic primary BCC phase and ﬁne
dual-phase structures containing interwoven BCC and face-
centered cubic (FCC) layers (Fig. 2b). The fractions of
BCC and HCP phases were 81.6% ± 2.2% and
18.4% ± 2.2%, respectively. With higher C contents, the

4 Discussion
4.1 Mechanism of microstructure formation
As illustrated in Fig. 8 [35], Ta and other metallic elements
(Nb, Mo, W) possessed comparable atomic radii ( R) and
nearly zero chemical mixing enthalpy ( DHmix). The afﬁnity
for atomic bonding formation among these elements is
relatively weak and they tend to form a continuous solid
solution. In contrast, signiﬁcant differences in R and highly
negative DH
mix are observed between C and the metallic
elements, especially Nb and Ta. This indicates that the
thermodynamic driving force for forming chemical clusters
between C and the metallic elements in the liquid phase is
much stronger. Binary chemical clusters of C–Nb, C–Mo,
C–Ta, and C–W prefer to form independently with negli-
gible interaction between them. Therefore, this multicom-
ponent system can be considered as a pseudo-binary C–
(Nb, Mo, Ta, W) system.

4.2 Strengthening mechanism
Compared with traditional Ta alloys, the designed Ta 3-
WNb0.5Mo0.5Cx alloys exhibited excellent strength, which
comes from the combination of the multi-principal element
concept with the introduction of compositionally complex
carbides. The alloys consist of primary BCC or HCP phase,
along with an eutectic structure formed by interwoven
BCC and HCP lamellae. BCC phase is a refractory MPEA,
in which Ta, W, Nb, Mo, and C elements are randomly
distributed across the lattice positions. Due to its high
chemical complexity, there are more severe lattice distor-
tions in BCC phase compared to the single-principal ele-
ment Ta alloys. The signiﬁcant elastic interaction between
the local stress ﬁelds of component atoms and dislocations
in BCC phase leads to more pronounced solid solution
strengthening. Moreover, the dual-phase microstructure
plays a crucial role in enhancing the strength. After

Due to the incorporation of brittle CCCs, the plasticity
of the alloys is reduced compared to traditional Ta alloys.
However, it exceeds the plasticity of most Ta-containing
refractory MPEAs. Prior studies have demonstrated that an
increase in Mo and W content leads to reduced RT plas-
ticity in Ta–W–Nb–Mo systems [ 37–39]. In the proposed
Ta alloys, the ductile Ta element exhibited a higher con-
centration within BCC phase, while the brittle Mo and W
elements were present at relatively low levels (Table 1).
Consequently, BCC phase exhibits enhanced ductility
compared to previously reported refractory MPEAs.
Moreover, the surrounding HCP-M

niﬁcant constraints on the plastic deformation of BCC
phase, thereby improving the plasticity of BCC phase.
For CCC-reinforced Ta alloys, variations in C content
lead to distinct microstructure characteristics, which sub-
sequently inﬂuence RT plasticity. Speciﬁcally, as C con-
tents increase, the fraction of BCC phase decreases, leading
to a reduction in plasticity. In C0.5 alloy, the primary bulk
BCC phase functions as an effective soft crack-arresting
layer, passivating the tips of cracks (Fig. 5a). The is
because that the semi-coherent BCC/HCP phase interface
can exhibit strong bonding and enable efﬁcient strain
transfer. The dense and uniformly distributed interfaces
reduce stress concentration and induce a pronounced back-
stress hardening effect within the matrix. C1.0 alloy exhi-
bits a microstructure composed of interwoven BCC and
HCP-M
2C lamellae. The signiﬁcantly reduced size of BCC
phase severely limits the capability to arrest cracks. When
deformed at RT, once the crack nucleates within the hard
and brittle secondary CCC phase, it quickly propagates
through the material, traversing the entire dual-phase
structure, and ultimately results in catastrophic failure.
The microstructure also plays a signiﬁcant role in
affecting the strain hardening and softening behavior at
1600 /C176 C. Usually, the high-temperature deformation
behavior of alloys is governed by the competition between
strain hardening, which is caused by dislocation aggrega-
tion, and dynamic softening, which arises from dynamic
recovery or recrystallization. In the designed alloys, BCC
metallic phase exhibits better deformation capability, and
the phase interface hinders dislocation motion. During HT
deformation process, continuous nucleation, accumulation,
and interaction of dislocations occur in BCC phase near
BCC/HCP interface as strain increases, resulting in strain
hardening. Simultaneously, the degree of dynamic recov-
ery intensiﬁes with increasing dislocation density, resulting
in the formation of LAGBs and sub-grains.

5 Conclusion
In this study, we have harnessed the favorable composi-
tional effects of multi-principal element systems and the
incorporation of CCCs to develop novel Ta alloys with
promising mechanical properties. These alloys consisted of
a refractory MPEA phase with a BCC structure and CCC
phase with an HCP structure. As C content increased, the
microstructure changed from hypoeutectic to eutectic and
then to hypereutectic. The signiﬁcant solid solution
strengthening effect arising from multiple elements, com-
bined with the dense and dispersed interfaces that effec-
tively impede the movement of dislocations, contributes to
the high strength. At 1600 /C176 C, the multi-component char-
acteristic and the semi-coherent phase interface with low
interfacial energy ensure microstructural stability. How-
ever, the increasing C content led to reduced volume
fraction of BCC phase, which compromised the ability to
blunt cracks and accumulate dislocations, causing HT
softening and decreased RT plasticity. This work provides
theoretical guidance and experimental evidence for
designing HT structural materials with excellent mechan-
ical properties. Further investigations are necessary to
study the scalability and industrial applicability of the
alloys.
