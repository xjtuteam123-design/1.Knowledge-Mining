1. Introduction
Selective Laser Melting (SLM) has emerged as a promising additive 
manufacturing technique for fabricating complex metallic components 
with superior mechanical properties. A common challenge in various γ ′ 
phase reinforced nickel-based superalloys fabricated via SLM is the 
prevalent occurrence of micro-cracks, which limit their suitability for 
SLM processing [ 1 ]. In contrast, the GH4099 alloy has attracted wide -
spread attention owing to its excellent SLM processability and high- 
temperature strength, rendering it highly suitable for aerospace and 
energy applications [ 2 , 3 ]. However, the rapid heating and cooling cycles 
inherent to the SLM process often result in unique microstructural 
characteristics that differ significantly from conventionally manufac -
tured counterparts[ 4 , 5 ].
Post-processing heat treatments, particularly solution treatment, 
play a crucial role in homogenizing the microstructure and relieving 
residual stresses in SLM-fabricated components [ 2 ]. The solution tem -
perature significantly influences the dissolution of strengthening pre -
cipitates, grain growth behavior, and recrystallization mechanisms, 
which ultimately dictate the mechanical performance of the material 
[ 6 – 8 ]. Despite extensive research on conventional processed GH4099 
alloy, the effects of solution temperature on SLM-fabricated components 
remain insufficiently documented.
Previous investigations into additively manufactured alloys have 
demonstrated that variations in solution temperature can significantly 
influence grain size, precipitate morphology, and phase stability [ 9 ]. 
Recent studies have shown that SLM-produced GH4099 responds 
differently to solution treatments compared to wrought or cast variants 
[ 2 ]. A well-calibrated heat treatment can refine the microstructure, 
enhance homogenization, and ultimately lead to improved yield 
strength and tensile properties.
This study aims to systematically investigate the effect of solution 
temperature on the microstructural evolution and resultant mechanical 
properties of GH4099 alloy fabricated by SLM at elevated temperature, 
providing valuable insights for optimizing heat treatment of SLM- 
fabricated GH4099 components.

3. Results and discussions
Fig. 1 (a-f) reveals significant differences between the two heat 
treatments. SA-1 produced columnar grains with an average size of 72 
μ m, aligned with the build direction, displaying 38.3 % high-angle grain 
boundaries (HAGBs) and 61.7 % low-angle grain boundaries (LAGBs). In 
contrast, SA-2 resulted in less elongated, more coarsened grains with an 
average size of 92 μ m, an increased proportion of HAGBs (45.1 %), and a 
more pronounced bimodal grain size distribution.
These microstructural differences suggest that higher solution tem -
perature promotes recovery and grain growth. The higher percentage of 
HAGBs in SA-2 typically enhances ductility by providing more effective 
barriers to dislocation movement and facilitating grain boundary sliding 
during deformation. Additionally, the less elongated grain morphology 
reduces mechanical anisotropy, potentially enabling more uniform 
plastic deformation.
Fig. 2 (a-d) reveal distinct differences in the γ ʹ precipitate morphol -
ogies after the two different heat treatment regimes. Fig. 2 (a) displays a 
dense distribution of γ ʹ precipitates with predominantly elongated, 
ellipsoidal morphologies of SA-1. The precipitates show directional 
alignment, likely influenced by residual strain fields from the SLM 
process. The size distribution appears broad (approximately 100 – 300 
nm in length, with an average size of 133.2 nm) with a mix of both 
elongated and some rounded particles, as shown in Fig. 2 (c). The 
elongated shape suggests coherency strains at the γ / γ ʹ interface, 
reflecting incomplete coarsening during the aging process.
Fig. 2 (b) exhibits a predominantly spherical γ ʹ precipitates with more 
uniform size distribution of SA-2. These precipitates appear smaller 
(approximately 50 – 200 nm in diameter, with an average size of 107.9 
nm) and more regularly shaped compared to those in SA-1, as shown in 
Fig. 2 (d). The distribution is more homogeneous with clearer separation 
between individual precipitates, suggesting more complete precipitation 
and coarsening.
The higher solution temperature in SA-2 resulted in more complete 
dissolution of pre-existing γ ʹ precipitates and other phases during sol -
utioning. This provides a more homogeneous supersaturated solid so -
lution, promoting uniform nucleation and isotropic growth of 
precipitates during the subsequent aging treatment, resulting in the 
spherical morphology. The elongated precipitates in SA-1 suggest that 
the 1080 
◦
C solution treatment was insufficient to fully eliminate the 
directional microstructural features and residual stresses inherited from 
the SLM process. These residual strain fields likely influenced the pre -
cipitate growth direction during aging.

4. Conclusions
This study systematically investigated the effects of solution treat -
ment temperature on the microstructure and high-temperature me -
chanical properties of GH4099 alloy fabricated by Selective Laser 
Melting. The results demonstrate that solution temperature significantly 
influences precipitate morphology, grain boundary characteristics, car -
bide evolution, which collectively determine the mechanical perfor -
mance at elevated temperatures. The 1120 
◦
C solution treatment proved 
optimal, producing a microstructure characterized by uniformly 
distributed spherical γ ʹ precipitates, more homogeneous carbide net -
works, a higher proportion of high-angle grain boundaries, and reduced 
crystallographic texture. This microstructural configuration resulted in 
superior mechanical properties at 900 
◦
C, with 26 % higher yield 
strength, 17 % higher ultimate tensile strength, and 41 % greater 
elongation compared to the 1080 
◦
C treatment.
