ABSTRACT
This study investigates autogenous diode laser welding of two differently strengthened Fe-Ni-Cr super -
alloys: solid-solution strengthened Incoloy 800 and precipitation-strengthened Incoloy A286. Effects of 
laser power, welding speed, and defocusing distance on weld bead geometry, microstructure, and 
mechanical properties were comprehensively analyzed. Butt weld joints produced with optimum and 
similar heat input conditions achieved joint efficiencies comparable to their base metals. Microstructural 
analysis of fusion zone revealed columnar dendrites of γ-(Ni,Fe) in both alloys, with A286 showing 
segregation of Ti, Cr, and Mo intermetallics at interdendritic regions, while Incoloy 800 exhibited refined 
columnar/equiaxed dendrites with chromium carbides and Fe-Ni-Ti intermetallics. Mechanical testing 
indicated enhanced yield strength but reduced impact toughness and marginal hardness gain in A286 
welds, whereas Incoloy 800 welds maintained similar hardness to the base material. Overall, the study 
demonstrated utilization of fiber-coupled diode laser welding for producing butt-joints in both super -
alloys, supporting their potential adoption in high-temperature applications.

Materials and methods
The base materials (BM) used in the current study are solid- 
solution strengthened (Incoloy-800 termed as SSA) and pre -
cipitation-strengthened (Incoloy-A286 termed as PSA) Fe-Ni- 
based superalloys. The chemical compositions of the as- 
received PSA and SSA in their solution-treated conditions 
are given in Table 1 . The 4-mm-thick plates, with dimensions 
of 200 mm × 100 mm were solution annealed (A286: 980°C/ 
1 h, oil quenching, and 800:1050°C/2-h oil quenching) prior to 
welding with a diode laser. The Plates to be butt-welded were 
cut using EDM and subjected to edge preparation by grinding 
to 1.0-micron Ra surface finish, maintaining a gap as less as 
60 µm in between, them. The plates were thoroughly cleaned 
with acetone before laser welding.

process which is influenced by critical processing parameters, 
to achieve joint efficiency and sound quality. Consequently, 
before proceeding with actual butt-joint welding experimenta -
tion of both PSA and SSA superalloy materials, bead-on-plate 
(BOP) welding experimentation was done on PSA by varying 
critical parameter of defocusing distance in both + and – direc -
tion, a crucial factor in optimizing laser welding process. 
Furthermore, identifying focus in diode laser welding is crucial 
for effective coupling entailing maximum weld penetration 
and consistent melt-pool stability. For the purpose, welding 
experimentations in BOP configuration were conducted by 
changing the focusing distance in the range of −5 to +5 mm 
with a constant laser power of 3.0 kW and a welding speed of 
16 mm/s (to entail appropriate power density and interaction 
time) to produce weld penetration from conduction-mode of 
partial penetration to keyhole mode with full penetration.

Conclusions
The study demonstrated successfully, utilization of high-brilliance 
energy-efficient diode laser for welding of both precipitation 
strengthened and solid solution strengthened Fe-Ni-Cr-based 
superalloys, with effective optimization of processing parameters 
comprising welding speed and de-focusing distance to produce of 
joint efficiencies on par with their respective of base metals (BM). 
When welded at similar heat input condition, weld penetration 
depth with melt fusion area was always higher in solid-solution 
strengthened alloy (SSA) joint than in precipitation strengthened 
alloy (PSA) joint and attributed to low thermal conductivity and 
thermal diffusivity. While, hardness in weld fusion microstructure 
marginally improved in PSA joint, no improvement in fusion 
zone hardness observed in SSA joint. Microstructure analysis 
indicated that both laser welded joints predominantly comprising 
of columnar dendrites of γ-Ni,Fe,Cr (matrix) with segregation of 
carbides of Ti, Cr, Mo in inter-dendritic regions of PSA and 
pockets of brittle intermetallic compounds (laves) in SSA.
At optimum laser welding conditions, joint efficiency of both 
superalloy weldments produced were on par with that of base 
metals, with the fracture failure location of PSA joint being in BM 
region, as compared to its location at weld fusion region in SSA. 
Impact testing of weldments indicated reduction in absorbed 
energy of both welded joints when compared to their BM coun -
terparts. Overall, the study demonstrated the successful single- 
pass diode laser welding of PSA and SSA with 100% joint effi -
ciency and its suitability for adoption in fabrication of compo -
nents used in high-temperature environment of aerospace, 
nuclear reactor, and other allied engineering sectors.
