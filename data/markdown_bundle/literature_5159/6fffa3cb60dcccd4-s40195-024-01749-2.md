Influence of Substituting W for Nb or Hf on Solidification Behavior 
of a Typical Co–Ni–Al–W Based Superalloy

Abstract
Understanding the effects of various elements on solidification behavior is crucial for designing the composition of 
γ’-strengthened Co-based superalloys and is fundamental for controlling the as-cast structure and formulating subsequent 
heat treatment processes. This research investigated the effects of replacing 1 at.% W with 1 at.% Nb or Hf elements on the 
solidification behavior of Co–Ni–Al–W-based superalloys. The findings revealed that substituting W with Nb and Hf resulted 
in a notable decrease in both the solidus temperature (T S) and liquidus temperature (TL). Specifically, the substitution of W 
with Nb lowered T S from 1353 °C to 1332 °C and T L from 1383 °C to 1368 °C, while replacing W with Hf decreased T S 
from 1353 °C to 1330 °C and TL from 1383 °C to 1366 °C. Moreover, both Nb and Hf element are positive segregation ele-
ment, while Nb decreases and Hf increases W segregation, respectively. During the final solidification stage, the substitution 
of W with Nb resulted in the formation of eutectic (γ + γ’), Co3Ta, and a small amount of μ-Co7Nb6 phase, while replacing 
W with Hf resulted in the formation of the Laves phase and β-CoAl phase. The solidification paths of the three alloys were 
confirmed based on the result of differential scanning calorimetry, isothermal solidification experiment and Thermo-calc 
simulation. These results offer a theoretical basis for the composition design and optimization of heat treatment processes 
for Co–Ni–Al–W-based superalloys.
Keywords Co–Ni–Al–W-based superalloys · Solidification microstructure · Segregation · Solidification path
1 Introduction
In recent years, high-temperature alloy materials have been 
consistently developed and improved [1 , 2]. In this con-
text, the research and development of novel superalloys 
has shifted from pursuing higher operating temperatures 
to developing special superalloys characterized by high 
functionality and environmental adaptability. Co-based 
superalloys, known for their high weldability, hot corro-
sion resistance and thermal fatigue properties, offer a novel 
direction for developing highly corrosion-resistant structural 
materials for aerospace engines and gas turbines. Recently, 
γ’-strengthened Co–Ni–Al–W based superalloys were shown 
to have similar high temperature properties of second-gen-
eration Ni-based single crystal superalloy, which proves 
their development potential [3, 4]. The successful evolution 
and development of Ni-based superalloys have inspired the 
design of Co-based superalloys. Previous studies have shown 
that Nb is a γ ’ phase-forming element can increase the γ ’ 
content in Ni-based superalloys [5–7]. Nb also increases the 
misfit between the γ’ phase and the γ phase. Appropriate Nb 
addition can enhance tensile and creep properties. Alloys 
with Nb addition also exhibit superior oxidation resist-
ance compared to Nb-free alloys [8 ]. The addition of Hf 
can improve the fluidity of the melt in interdendrites during 
solidification, reduce hot tearing, enhance castability and 
welding performance, and prevent solidification defects [9 , 
10]. The presence of Hf promotes the formation of massive

MC phases [11– 13]. Compared to Chinese script and strip 
carbides, massive M C carbides can inhibit micropores and 
cracks formation, enhancing the stress rupture life of the 
alloy [14, 15]. In addition, segregating Hf elements at grain 
boundaries can reinforce their strength and enhance the 
medium-temperature creep properties of the alloy [16, 17].
Srinivasan et al. [18] utilized the manifold learning 
method to demonstrate that Nb and Hf can substitute W in 
stabilizing the Co3Al phase, performing a role similar to 
W element in γ ’-Co3(Al, W). Researchers have attempted 
to introduce Nb and Hf into Co-based superalloys to rep-
licate the positive effects seen in Ni-based superalloys. 
Wang et al. [19] proposed that adding 4 at.% Nb enhances 
the ambient temperature and 800 °C yield strength of 
the Co–9Al–9W–2Ta alloy. Zhang et al. [20] found that 
Hf may facilitate the quick elimination of the C15-Laves 
phase by encouraging its transformation into Hf-rich M C 
carbides during the solution treatment in Co-based superal-
loys. Existing research has primarily focused on enhancing 
the γ’-Co3(Al, W) precipitation temperature and stability 
through compositional optimization. However, there is lim-
ited research on the solidification behavior of Co–Ni–Al–W 
based superalloys.
The solidification characteristics of superalloys, includ-
ing solidification path, element distribution coefficient, and 
phase transition temperature, are essential for alloy engi-
neering applications. The solidification phase transforma-
tion behavior of Co-based superalloys is more complex 
than Ni-based superalloys [21]. Zhou et al. [22] determined 
that the solidification path of Co–7Al–8W–1Ta–4Ti qui-
nary alloy is L → L1 + γ → L2 + γ + Laves → L3 + γ + Lave
s + (β + γ’)e → L4 + γ + Laves + (β + γ’)e + γ’ → γ + Laves + 
(β + γ’)e + γ’. Zhou et al. [23] noted that the Laves phase in 
Co–Ni–Al–W-based superalloys promotes the formation of 
the μ phase during solid solution treatment, making solution 
treatment more challenging. Liu et al. [24] noted that add-
ing Mo can decrease alloy density with minimal impact on 
the γ/γ’ microstructure. However, excessive Mo can lead to 
the formation of a needle-like μ phase (Co3Mo). Ding et al. 
[25] identified the solidification path of Co–Al–W–Ti super-
alloy through quenching. The phase transition route was 
determined as: L → L + γ → L + γ + (β + γ’) → L + γ + γ’ + 
(β + γ’) → γ + γ’ + β + (β + γ’). Zhou et al. [26] found that the 
Al content increases and the W content decreases will sup-
press the Laves phase formation and increase the ( β + γ’) 
eutectics fraction in Co–Ni–Al–W based superalloy.
Adding alloy elements can significantly impact the solidi-
fication behaviors of Co-based superalloys. A well-designed 
solidification microstructure is crucial for developing heat 
treatment process to improve the mechanical properties of 
the alloy. This study examines the impact of Nb and Hf 
on the solidification microstructure, dendrite segregation, 
phase transformation temperature and solidification path for 
Co–Ni–Al–W alloy using transmission electron microscopy 
(TEM), differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) analysis, 
isothermal solidification experiments and Thermo-calc sim-
ulation, which could build foundations for optimizing alloy 
component in Co–Ni–Al–W based superalloys.

2.2 Differential Scanning Calorimetry (DSC)
DSC tests were performed using an ultra-high temperature 
comprehensive thermal analyzer STA449F3 to identify 
phase transformation temperatures of three alloys. The sam-
ples for the DSC test were cut from the master alloy with 
dimensions of Φ3 mm × 2 mm. The upper, lower and circum-
ferential surfaces of samples were polished with sandpaper 
until the metallic luster was visible. All experiments were 
conducted in a dynamic high-purity Ar atmosphere using a 
high-purity Al2O3 crucible to reduce oxidation effects. The 
specimens underwent a heating and cooling cycle as fol -
lows: ambient temperature → heating to 800 °C at 30 °C/
min → isothermal holding at 800 °C for 10 min → heating to 
1450 °C at 10 °C/min → isothermal holding at 1450 °C for 
10 min → cooling to 800 °C at 10 °C/min → cooling to ambi-
ent temperature at 30 °C/min. The selected cooling rate of 
10 °C/min aligns with industrial casting processes. Accord-
ing to general principles [27, 28], the solidus is derived from 
the heating curve, and the liquidus is derived from the cool-
ing curve.
2.3 Isothermal Solidification Experiment
Isothermal solidification experiments were conducted on 
three alloys: Base, Nb, and Hf, to understand their solidifica-
tion processes. Samples measuring Φ 15 mm × 15 mm were 
cut from the master alloy ingot. To ensure sample purity 
and prevent interference from impurities, they were care-
fully polished with 180# sandpaper until a metallic luster 
was achieved, eliminating all cutting marks. The samples 
were wrapped in alumina powder and a silica sol mixture to 
prevent oxidation during isothermal solidification experi-
ments. The isothermal solidification experiment was con-
ducted in a 1600 °C rapid heating box-type electric furnace 
with a temperature accuracy of ± 1 °C. Figure  1 illustrates 
the isothermal solidification experimental process, which 
includes placing the sample in an Al2O3 crucible, heating 
it to 1450 °C, and maintaining this temperature for 15 min 
to ensure uniform melt temperature. Subsequently, cool the 
molten sample at a rate of 10 °C/min to reach the speci -
fied isothermal solidification temperature range determined 
from the DSC experimental results. After cooling to the 
specified temperature, the sample was kept for 10 min and 
then quenched in cold water to achieve a near-equilibrium 
structure.
To determine the volume fraction of residual liquid in the 
isothermal solidification samples, five SEM images were 
randomly captured at 100 × magnification from various areas 
of each sample. The Image-Pro software was used to meas-
ure the volume fraction of the residual liquid phase. The 
electron probe microanalyzer (EPMA) JXA-iHP200F was 
utilized to analyze the chemical composition of the solid 
and liquid phases at various isothermal solidification tem-
peratures to study element redistribution between the phases 
during solidification. To ensure accurate results, a minimum 
of three randomly selected points were tested at each posi-
tion and then averaged.

3.2 DSC Analysis
Figure  5 shows the heating and cooling curves obtained 
through DSC for the alloy Base, alloy Nb, and alloy Hf, 
used to determine phase transformation temperatures. 
Table  3 lists the phase transition temperatures obtained 
from DSC curves. For alloy Base, the liquidus and solidus 
were 1383 °C and 1353 °C, respectively, while the forma-
tion temperature of M 3B2 was 1141 °C. Compared to the 
model alloy, replacing 1 at.%W with 1 at.%Nb reduced the 
liquidus temperature ( TL) to 1368 °C and the solidus tem -
perature (TS) to 1332 °C. The phase transition temperature 
of the eutectic (γ + γ’) decreased from 1256 °C to 1177 °C, 
and the phase transition temperature of M C decreased 
from 1351 °C to 1337 °C, while Co3Ta formation tempera-
ture was 1100 °C. When replacing W with 1 at.%Hf, the 
liquidus temperature (T L) decreased to 1366 °C, the soli-
dus temperature (T S) dropped from 1353 °C to 1330 °C, 
the phase transition temperature of M C dropped from 
1351 °C to 1343 °C, and the phase transition temperature 
of the eutectic (γ + γ’) decreased from 1256 °C to 1125 °C, 
while Laves phase and β phase formation temperature were 
1173 °C and 1138 °C, respectively. The solidification tem-
perature range can be equal to T L minus T S. The addition 
of Nb and Hf can broaden the solidification temperature 
range compared to the alloy Base (30 °C).

→ L4 + γ + MC + eutectic(γ + γ’) + Co3Ta → γ + MC + eutect
ic(γ + γ’) + Co3Ta + μ.
Figure 9g–i illustrates the solidification structure of alloy 
Hf quenched at different temperatures. As shown in Fig. 9g, 
at 1150 °C, the residual liquid phase surrounded the Laves 
phase and β phase, the Laves phase and β phase precipitated 
delicately within the residual liquid phase during quenching. 
At 1140 °C, the bulk Laves phase and β phase formed near 
the residual liquid phase as shown in Fig. 9h, with solidifica-
tion reactions of L → L1 + Laves and L → L1 + β. At 1130 °C, 
the eutectic γ + γ’ forms as depicted in Fig. 9i, with the solid-
ification reaction being L → eutectic (γ + γ’). In summary, 
the solidification path of alloy Hf can be summarized as L 
→ L1 + γ → L 2 + γ + MC → L3 + γ + MC + Lave s → L4 + γ + 
MC + Lav es + β → γ + MC + Laves + β + eutectic (γ + γ’).

4.2 Relationship Between Element Segregation 
and Phase Transformation
The element segregation and phase transformation of Base 
alloy was first discussed. The formation of the intermetallic 
phase depends on the segregation behavior of the forming 
elements in the residual liquid phase [ 34]. As depicted in 
Fig. 8a, Al achieved a k value less than 1, suggesting it acted 
as a positively segregating element, enriched in the residual 
liquid phase. Thus, eutectic (γ + γ’) (Al-rich) can be formed 
between dendrites. It is known that the solubility of B in the 
γ matrix is pretty limited. B is also a kind of positive seg-
regation element [35] and strongly segregated in the liquid 
phase. Accompanied with the formation of eutectic (γ + γ’), 
considerable Ta and W are also continuously repelled into 
the residual liquid, which intensifies the enrichment of 
M3B2 (rich in Ta, W) forming elements in the residual liq-
uid phase, resulting in considerable M 3B2 formed near the 
eutectic (γ + γ’). The isothermal solidification experiment 
(Fig. 9) indicated that M3B2 formed at the final solidification 
stage, with a formation temperature ranging from 1140 °C 
to 1130 °C.
As shown in Fig. 8a, b, substituting Nb for W obviously 
decreases the segregation degree of W and increases the 
segregation of Al element, while its effect on segregation of 
Ti, Ta elements is negligible. The distribution coefficient of 
Nb is significantly less than 1 indicating that Nb can be very 
seriously enriched in the residual liquid phase, which leads 
to the precipitation of the L12-Co3Ta phase (rich in Ta and 
Nb) and μ-Co7Nb6 phase (rich in Nb, Ta, and W) between 
dendrites. Furthermore, as a γ’ formation element, Nb addi-
tion promotes the formation of eutectic (γ + γ’) and increase 
their volume fraction, which agree well with relevant result 
in Ni-based superalloy [36]. Since M3B2 is generally formed 
in the final solidification stage, and the formation of μ phase 
and Co3Ta phase has consumed considerable Ta and W ele-
ments in residual liquid phase, therefore M3B2 will not pre-
cipitate in the Nb alloy.
Compare with the model alloy, substituting Hf for W 
increases the segregation degree of W element and decreases 
the segregation degree of Al element, while also have little 
effect on other elements. Hf element is a typical positive seg-
regation element, and tends to enrich in residual liquid. The 
MC carbide morphology change from script-like to blocky, and 
composition become much richer in Hf element compare to 
model alloy. Chen et al. [11] suggested that high Hf content 
in MC carbides increases their interfacial misfit with matrix 
and makes them blocky. Laves phases form when the elemen-
tal concentration of Ta and Hf in the residual liquid reach the 
required concentrations. During the growth of the Laves phases, 
the Al and Ti contents in the residual liquid phase increase 
and cause the Al-rich β phase (19.5 at.%) formation around the 
Laves phases. When the temperature further reduces, eutectic 
(γ + γ’) (10.0 at.%), less Al-rich than the β phase, will precipi-
tate in the residual liquid phase and solidification terminate.
