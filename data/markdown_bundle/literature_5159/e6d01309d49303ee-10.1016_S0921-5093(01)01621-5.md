Abstract
The /p98-(Nb, Ti) solid solution phase in the Nb–Ti–Al system has recently been observed to extend to Al concentrations as high
as 40 at.%. Five alloys with compositions (in at.%) of Nb– xTi–15Al, where x =17, 28.3 and 34, Nb–25Ti–25Al and
Nb–24Ti–40Al were investigated. The /p98-transus temperatures, lattice parameters and /p98/p147B2 ( /p98o) ordering temperatures are
reported. Site occupancy preferences of the B2 lattice as a function of alloy composition were examined through the atom location
by channeling enhanced microanalysis (ALCHEMI) technique with results presented as ordering tie lines. Ti substitution for Nb
on Nb sublattice sites was observed in all of the alloys, with the strength of partitioning between Ti and Al atoms being dependent
on alloy composition. A correlation between the partitioning behaviors of the atomic species and the Nb:Ti ratio of the alloy
compositions was observed, along with changes in the lattice parameters and microhardness. © 2002 Elsevier Science B.V. All
rights reserved.

1. Introduction
Investigation of materials for high temperature appli-
cations has led to the development of numerous ternary
and quaternary alloy systems. Dilute solute additions to
the phases contained in these alloys can produce
changes in mechanical properties without necessarily
inducing phase transformations. Knowledge of site oc-
cupancy preferences in the ordering behavior of differ-
ent phases is valuable for both predicting and analyzing
mechanical properties, as well as in evaluating thermo-
dynamics and kinetics. Improvements to the mechanical
properties of TiAl and Ti
3Al based alloys, for example,
have been observed with the addition of 5 at.% Nb, as
a result of the substitution of Nb on Ti sublattice sites
in those structures [1].
The solubility range of the /p98(bcc.) solid-solution
phase in the Nb–Ti–Al system has recently been ob-
served to extend further into the ternary diagram than
predicted earlier [2]. The /p98phase in these alloys exhib-
ited B2 ( /p98
o) ordering, in which the ordered bcc crystal
structure can be represented as two sublattices by the
occupation of atoms on alternating {200} planes. As
part of a larger investigation of Nb-rich alloys of the
Nb–Ti–Al system, the site occupancy preferences in
the B2 Nb solid solution phase were examined in ﬁve
alloys through atom location by channeling-enhanced
microanalysis (ALCHEMI), a diffraction based tech-
nique developed by Spence and Taftø [3] for determin-
ing the crystallographic site preferences, distributions
and types of substitutional impurities within a
crystal.

excessive transformations from occurring in the /p98//p98o
phase. While treatments were kept short, some precipi-
tation of metastable O –Ti2AlNb was observed in sev-
eral samples. Anti-phase boundaries (APB ’s) used to
determine the changes in ordering with temperature
were imaged under dark- ﬁeld two beam conditions,
using g=100 type re ﬂections. The average size of the
ordered domains were determined from a random
throw method of ﬁve dipoles, the domain sizes being
based on the number of APB inceptions over the length
of the dipole, corrected for magni ﬁcation. A decrease in
the average domain size with increasing temperature
indicates that ordering had occurred on cooling. The
effects of temperature and cooling rate on APB size in
the Nb –28.3Ti–15Al alloy are shown in Fig. 3. The
cast and HIP’ed structures were produced through slow
cooling from above the transition temperature. A large
APB size was produced following a brief aging at
950 °C, while a dramatic decrease was observed after
aging at 1000 °C, which indicates that the ordering
temperature lies between these temperatures.
The /p98//p98
o transition temperatures were observed to
decrease from above 950 °C for the 34 and 28.3Ti
alloys to above 900 °C for the 17Ti alloy. The trend in
decreasing transition temperatures with decreasing Ti
concentration towards the binary Nb –Al system, was
in agreement with the measured transition temperature
of 800 °C for Nb –10Al by Menon et al [11]. The
increase in ordering temperatures with Ti concentration
appeared to be associated with the increase in Ti substi-
tution of Nb on Nb sites in the B2 structure.
3.3. Lattice parameters and microhardness of the /p105o
phase
The lattice parameters of the /p98o phase, calculated
using Cohen ’s method from the location of the dif-
fracted peaks, are plotted against composition in Fig.
4a. The {100} superlattice re ﬂections were not observed
in any of the XRD scans, suggesting a relatively low
degree of long range order occurring in the /p98
o phase. A
shift in the lattice parameter with composition was
observed, with a more signi ﬁcant reduction occurring
with the increase in Al content than in the decrease in
Nb:Ti ratio; the latter due to the closer atomic size
matching between Nb and Ti. Microhardness of the /p98
o
phase also showed a dependence on composition, as
illustrated in Fig. 4b. For alloys with 15 at.% Al,
decreasing the Nb:Ti ratio led to a decrease in hardness
from 302 kg mm
−2 in Nb –17Ti–15Al to 272 kg mm −2
in Nb –34Ti–15Al.
4. Conclusions
The site occupancy preferences for the B2 ordered
solid solution phase in ﬁve Nb –rich Nb –Ti–Al alloys
were investigated through the ALCHEMI technique.
The trends in the site occupancy preferences have been
determined from the slopes of the ordering tie-lines and
the changes in measured compositions and K
/p104ratios
between the s100 /p660 and s100 /p920 channeling conditions.
In all the alloys, Ti substitution for Nb on Nb
sublattice sites was determined based on the directions
of the OTL ’s.
A change in the ordering preferences from Ti and Al
separation to Nb and Al separation was measured
between the Nb –34Ti–15Al and Nb –28.3Ti–15Al al-
loys. Similar ordering behavior was observed occurring
between the Nb –34Ti–15Al and Nb –24Ti–40Al al-
loys. Tendency for separation between Al and Nb was
greatest in the Nb –25Ti–25Al alloy.
An increase in the ordering temperature of the solid
solution phase was observed with increases in Ti con-
centration, an increase in the amount of Ti led to a
corresponding decrease in the microhardness of the
alloys.
