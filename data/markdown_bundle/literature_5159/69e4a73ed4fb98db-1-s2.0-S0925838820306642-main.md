abstract
A series of refractory Re xNbMoTaW high entropy alloys were developed with the addition of Re into the
NbMoTaW alloy. The Re xNbMoTaW alloys are composed of body-centered cubic (BCC) matrix phase and
particle phase and could maintain the phase structure up to their melting points. All the alloys possess
precipitations due to the higher surface energy in powders raw material than rods. The proper addition
of Re could improve both the strength and ductility of the alloys, and Re 0.5NbMoTaW alloy exhibits the
best mechanical properties. As the Re addition increases from 0 to 0.5, the yield strength of the alloys is
improved from 1058 MPa to 1 147 MPa, while the room-temperature ductility increases from 2.6% to 7.0%.

1. Introduction
High entropy alloys (HEAs) were ﬁrstly launched by Yeh et al. in
2004, and the main principle is to maximize the con ﬁgurational
entropy by increasing the number of principal components [ 1,2].
Due to their high mixing entropy, these alloys tend to form single-
phase structures with high symmetry, such as FCC (face-centered
cubic), BCC (body-centered cubic), and HCP (hexagonal close-
packed) [ 3,4]. The high mixing entropy is also anticipated to
enhance the stabilization of solid solution phases and impede
dislocation motion, thereby improving the mechanical properties.
Consequently, HEAs have been demonstrated to exhibit a variety of
exceptional properties such as high hardness, outstanding wear
resistance, good structural stability, high-temperature strength, etc.
[5,6].
Since metallic alloys for high-temperature load-bearing struc-
tures and thermal protection systems remain in high demand for
aerospace applications, HEAs composed of high melting point ele-
ments, such as HfNbTaZr [ 7], CrMoNbTa [ 8], CrNbVZr [ 9]o r
NbMoTaW [ 10e12] element groupings, have been explored. In
these HEAs, NbMoTaW and VNbMoTaW produced by vacuum arc-
melting exhibit excellent yield strength of 506 MPa and
735 MPa at 1200 /C14 C, respectively [ 12]. However, the alloys showed
limited room temperature ductility with the plastic strain of about
2.0% and 1.5%, respectively, which might be a limitation for further
industrial applications.
It is well known that alloying has long been used to achieve
better properties than individual metals. Alloying typically involves
the addition of relatively small amounts of secondary elements to a
primary element, which as obstacles to dislocation motion and
resulting in increased strength or hardness [ 13]. For example, Han
et al. have successfully synthesized TiNbMoTaW and TiVNbMoTaW
refractory HEAs with better room-temperature ductility by adding
Ti element [ 14]. The addition of ductile Ti with a large atomic size
improves the strength and fracture strain of HEAs but reduces the
overall melting temperature of HEAs due to the low melting tem-
perature of Ti. Dong et al. [ 15] improved the mechanical properties
of AlCoCrFeNi alloy by adding 0.2 at% V. The compressive strength
and plastic strain were increased from 2865 MPa to 3298 MPa and
22.7%e26.8%, respectively.
Element Re has a small atomic size and a high melting tem-
perature. The addition of Re can raise the strength and improve the
ductility of W by reducing the Peierls stress due to the changes in 1=

2. Experimental procedures
Raw materials Re, Mo, Ta, Nb, and W were in the form of metallic
powder with purities of all above 99.95% and an average diameter
of 40 mm. Metallic powders were mixed in corresponding ratio.
RexNbMoTaW alloys (x¼ 0, 0.5 and 1 in molar ratio, denoted as Re0,
Re0.5 and Re1, respectively) were prepared by vacuum arc melting
the mixture of the corresponding elements. The powders were well
mixed by manual grinding, compressed under the pressure. Arc
melting of the alloys was on a water-cooled copper plate conducted
under a protective argon atmosphere. To achieve a homogeneous
distribution of elements, the arc-melted pellets were ﬂipped and
remelted ﬁve times. All the alloy ingots were about 8 mm in
thickness and about 20 mm in diameter.
The phase purity of the arc-melted alloys was determined by X-
ray Diffraction with Cu-K
a radiation (Rigaku Ultima III, Japan). The
diffraction pattern was recorded from a 2 q (diffraction angle) range
from 20 /C14 to 140 /C14 with a step size of 0.02 /C14 at a rate of 4 /C14 min/C0 1.
Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM, FEI the Quanta 250 FEG, US)
was used to analyze the microstructure after alloys etched by the
mixture of hydro ﬂuoric acid, nitric acid and distilled water (V HF:
VHNO3:V H2O ¼ 1: 1: 2). The average grain size was statistically
analyzed using quantitative metallography by the software of
Image-Pro Plus 6.0 based on more than 200 grains and 500 parti-
cles, respectively, from the BSED-SEM images of the sintered
samples. Electron probe microanalyzer (EPMA, JOEL JXA-8230) was
used to analyze the element distribution and chemical composi-
tions. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM, JEOL JEM-2100F)
equipped with energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) was
used to analyze the microstructure at 200 kV. The thin foil samples
were cut from the cross-section of alloys, mechanically polished,
and then ion-beam thinned down.
The Vickers hardness was measured on polished cross-section
surfaces under a 1 kg load applied for 15 s. Each specimen was
tested at room temperature and reported as the averages of ﬁve
measurement results. The room-temperature compressive proper-
ties were conducted on an Instron 5966 testing machine at a con-
stant loading speed of 0.5 mm/min. The specimens for compression
tests were in the shape of cylinder withF 2m m /C2 4 mm. At least ﬁve
samples were tested for each composition on the same testing
condition.
3. Results
3.1. Phase prediction
In this work, FactSage 6.4 software (GTT-Technologies, Ger-
many) [ 19] has been used to construct the phase diagram of the
RexNbMoTaW refractory alloys shown in Fig. 1a. A single BCC phase
is predicted to form below the melting point of the Re xNbMoTaW
alloys and remains stable at a broad temperature range. However,
an HCP (hexagonal close-packed) phase is expected to occur as the
temperature decreases to about 1000 /C14 C. As the alloys were melted
with quick cooling rate, the non-equilibrium solidi ﬁcation is
simulated. The non-equilibrium mole fraction versus temperature
for the Re 1.0NbMoTaW alloy is shown in Fig. 1b. The BCC phase is
found to form without phase transformation.
Several physical parameters have also been proposed to predict
the phase formation and structural stability in HEAs [ 20e25]. From
a thermodynamic point of view, and in particular, derived from
Hume-Rothery rules, Guo et al. [ 20] suggested a criterion for
achieving solid-solution phases in HEAs using three parameters,
DHmix (enthalpy of mixing), d (atomic radius difference), and DSmix
(entropy of mixing). Using available data from various HEAs sys-
tems, the criteria for forming solid solutions in HEAs were deter-
mined to be: 0 /C20 d /C20 8.5, /C0 22/C20 DHmix/C20 7 kJ/mol, and
11/C20 DSmix/C20 19.5 J/(K $mol). An additional parameter U was also
proposed [21], which supposed that the effect of entropy of mixing
must overshadow the enthalpy of mixing in order to favor the
formation of high-entropy phases. From the available literature, it
was concluded that the criteria for forming solid solution are
U /C21 1:1 and d /C20 6:6% [ 21]. The other parameter g (the ratio be-
tween the solid angles of the smallest and largest atoms) was
introduced by Wang et al. [22]. It is an indicator to reveal the atomic
packing mis ﬁtting and topological instability. The application of g
in phase selection in HEAs and superalloys indicates that it is more
efﬁcient at distinguishing solid solutions from intermetallics than
the previous polydispersity parameter of atomic size difference d.
And g < 1:175is one of the necessary conditions for determining the
solubility of multicomponent alloys. In order to form single-phase
solid solution (SPSS), parameter L (L ¼ DSmix=d2) has been pre-
scribed by Singh et al. and the criteria is L > 0.96 [23]. A new model
using high-throughput computation of the formation enthalpies
DHf of possible binary compounds has also been developed to
predict the speciﬁc combinations of elements that are most likely to
form SPSS [25]. It shows that single solid solution would be formed
under the condition of -TannDSmix < DНf < 37 meV/mol, where the
annealing temperature is Tann ~0.6Tm, Tm is melting temperature.
Table 1 shows the calculated values of parameters d, DHmix,
DSmix, U, g, L, and DHf of Re xNbMoTaW according to different
proposed prediction models. All the parameters except DHf are in
the range of forming a solid solution for Re xNbMoTaW alloys.
Meanwhile, the alloys tend to form single-phase solid solutions
according to the SPSS forming rule. For the formation enthalpy DHf,
-TannDSmix for Re0, Re0.5 and Re1 are calculated to be /C0 193 meV/
mol, /C0 222 meV/mol and /C0 228 meV/mol, respectively. Thus, the
DHf of ReTa and MoTa compounds are at the minimum boundaries.
Meanwhile, no MoTa and ReTa compounds are formed according to
the MoeTa and ReeTa binary alloy phase diagrams [ 26]. Therefore,
combining the calculation of phase diagram and physical parame-
ters prediction, it is expected to single phase Re xNbMoTaW alloys.

4. Discussion
4.1. Phase formation
Through the theoretical prediction, single BCC solid solution
would be expected to form in the Re
xNbMoTaW alloys. However,
BCC solid solution and (Nb, Ta)-rich phase are formed in the ob-
tained microstructure, which is inconsistent with the predicted
one. Previously, Senkov et al. [ 12] reported the NbMoTaW alloy
with a single BCC phase produced using arc melting of chips. The
NbMoTaW HEA (donated as A1) in this study was fabricated using
raw materials in powder con ﬁgurations, which probably results in
the formation of (Nb, Ta)-rich precipitations.
In order to explain the occurrence of (Nb, Ta)-rich particles, we
fabricated another sets of NbMoTaW alloys, for which the re-melted
time was increased from 5 times to 10 times. The SEM images of
NbMoTaW alloys melted for different times are shown in Fig. 9.A s
increasing the re-melted times, the NbMoTaW alloy changes to be a
single BCC phase. Therefore, the precipitation of the (Nb, Ta)-rich
particles is mainly due to the raw materials of powders. It is
widely known that powders have more surface energy than rods

5. Conclusions
In this study, novel refractory Re xNbMoTaW alloys were
designed and synthesized by arc melting the mixture of pure
metallic powders. From the theoretical prediction, single BCC solid
solution would be formed in the Re xNbMoTaW alloys. The
RexNbMoTaW alloys are actually composed of BCC solid solution
and (Nb, Ta)-rich particle phase with tetragonal structure. With
increasing Re addition, the grain size of BCC matrix decreases and
then increases and Re 0.5NbMoTaW alloy has the smallest one. The
difference between the calculated and experimental results might
be due to the usage of powder raw materials, which has high sur-
face energy. The hardness, strength, and ductility of the
RexNbMoTaW alloys increase ﬁrstly and then decreases. The
Re0.5NbMoTaW alloy reaches the maximum hardness, yield
strength, compressive strength and fracture strain of 567 ± 9 HV,
1147± 10 MPa, 1465 ± 18 MPa, and 7.01 ± 0.30%, respectively. The
strength enhancement of the Re xNbMoTaW alloys can be inter-
preted by the second phase strengthening and ﬁne grain
strengthening effect. The improved ductility can be understood by
the ﬁne grain strengthening mechanism. The excellent mechanical
performance and high thermal stability of these HEAs make them
hold potentials for applications as structural materials.
