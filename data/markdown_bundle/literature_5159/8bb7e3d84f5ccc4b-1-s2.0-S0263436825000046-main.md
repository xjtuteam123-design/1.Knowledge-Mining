ABSTRACT
Light Refractory High Entropy Alloys (LRHEAs) with good high temperature phase stability are important for
overcoming material failures in high temperature environments. In this work, NbMoZrTi LRHEAs were designed
and prepared using the vacuum arc melting technique. The microstructure and mechanical properties of
NbMoZrTi LRHEAs were investigated before and after annealing at different temperatures (600
◦
C, 800
◦
C and
1000
◦
C). The results show that both cast and annealed NbMoZrTi LRHEAs have a single-phase BCC structure and
exhibit excellent high-temperature phase stability. The microhardness of the alloy reaches up to 591.18 HV
0.2
after prolonged annealing at 600
◦
C, while the residual stresses are gradually released and the hardness decreases
with increasing annealing temperature. Compression test results show that as the annealing temperature in-
creases, the yield strength of the alloy increases and then decreases, the yield strength of the specimen annealed
at 600
◦
C is the highest (1538 MPa), the ultimate compressive strength is the highest (2193 MPa), the high
hardness and high strength are mainly attributed to the solid solution strengthening effect of the alloys. As the
increase in annealing temperature further eliminates the internal stresses in the alloy, the plastic strain first
decreases and then increases to 27.6 % in the annealed specimen at 1000
◦
C. This paper demonstrates the
excellent high-temperature phase stability of NbMoZrTi LRHEA through the study of NbMoZrTi LRHEAs with
different annealing temperatures, and proposes the technical applications of NbMoZrTi LRHEAs in the high-
temperature fields of aerospace and nuclear energy.
1. Introduction
In 2004, Yeh et al. [ 1 ] broke the traditional design concept of alloys
dominated by one or two elements and proposed for the first time the
concept of high-entropy alloys (HEAs), which extended the design of
alloys from the corners of the phase diagram to the intermediate region.
Conventional alloying theory suggests that the number of phases in an
alloy increases with the number of alloying components, and that when
the number of components is high, the phases formed are mostly hard
and brittle intermetallic compounds. However, the multi-major element
properties of HEAs give them the unique high-entropy effect, hysteresis
diffusion effect, lattice distortion effect and cocktail effect [ 2 – 5 ], which
make HEAs mostly simple solid solution structures. Since the concept of
HEAs has been proposed, it has attracted considerable attention from
researchers. To meet the needs of aerospace and other high-temperature
fields, scientists from various countries have conducted in-depth
research on the mechanical behavior and strengthening mechanism of
HEAs, and designed a variety of refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs)
with excellent performance. In 2010, Professor Senkov [ 6 ] of the US Air
Force Laboratory designed NbMoTaW and NbMoTaVW RHEAs based on
the design concept of high entropy alloys by mixing a variety of high
melting point elements in an isoatomic ratio for the first time. The
compressive yield strengths of the two alloys were found to be 504 MPa
and 477 MPa, respectively, at 1600
◦
C. At this temperature, conven-
tional high temperature alloys would have already melted. In addition,
both alloys have better strength than Ni-based high temperature alloys
at the same temperature. Since then, more and more researchers have
turned their attention to the design and development of RHEAs have
designed RHEAs such as MoNbVTa [ 7 ], WTaMoNbSi
0.75
[ 8 ], NbTaTiVW
[ 9 ], HfNbTaTiZr [ 10 ] and TiNbMoCr [ 11 ]. They exhibit more excellent
mechanical properties than conventional alloys (such as high strength,
high hardness, excellent low-temperature strength and fracture tough-
ness) [ 12 – 14 ], as well as good corrosion resistance, wear resistance and
high-temperature stability [ 15 – 17 ]. Therefore, RHEAs have great
application prospects in the field of engineering and become one of the
research hotspots of metallic materials [ 18 – 21 ].

The strength of an alloy is the result of a variety of strengthening
methods, of which fine grain strengthening, diffusion strengthening,
solid solution strengthening and dislocation strengthening are the main

previous results, it can be seen that after the annealing treatment, Zr

distortion is intensified, increasing in the hardness of the alloy. As the
temperature rises, the high temperature internal stresses in the micro-
structure are eliminated, resulting in a decrease in the hardness value of
the alloy. In summary, the NbMoZrTi LRHEAs retain a stable micro-
hardness after heat treatment, indicating that the alloys have good
resistance to softening and can be used in high temperature applications.
The engineering stress-strain curves and compressive properties of the
NbMoZrTi LRHEAs in the as-cast and annealed conditions are shown in
Fig. 12 (a) and Table 5 , respectively. HT0 has a yield strength of about
1462 MPa, an ultimate compressive strength of about 2158 MPa, and a

the yield strength increases and then decreases, while the ductility de-
creases and then increases. HT600 has the highest yield strength of
about 1538 MPa, and the fracture strains of HT800 and HT1000 are
increased to 26.8 % and 27.6 %, respectively.
The high hardness and strength of HT600 are mainly due to one of its
intrinsic properties, i.e., solid solution strengthening by severe lattice
distortion. In the NbMoZrTi LRHEAs the number of atoms of the four
elements is equal, so the atoms of each element can be considered as
solute or solvent atoms. Atoms with different atomic sizes and properties
will interact with each other to distort the BCC lattice, causing the for-
mation of a local elastic stress field, which will impede the movement of
dislocations, thus giving the alloy high strength values [ 47 ]. The
decrease in yield strength and increase in ductility of HT800 and
HT1000 are attributed to the fact that the annealing heat treatment
effectively removes the stresses within the metal alloy and rearranges
the metal grains, resulting in a decrease in strength and an increase in
ductility. This is consistent with the results of previous dislocation
density analyses.

(1) The phase formation of the HT0 from room temperature to high

the results showed that the HT0 did not undergo any phase
transition from room temperature to high temperature. The mi-
crostructures of the NbMoTiZr LRHEAs in both the as-cast and
annealed states were found to consist of a single disordered solid
solution BCC phase and formed a typical dendritic crystalline
microstructure, with no coarsening of the grain size with
increasing annealing temperature. It is shown that annealing has
no significant effect on the phase composition of the alloy and
that the alloy has good thermal stability.
