Evaluation of High-Vacuum Annealing and Hot Isostatic

CARTER FIETEK,1 ERIC BRIZES ,1,2 and JUSTIN MILNER1
1.—NASA Glenn Research Center, Cleveland, OH 44135, USA. 2.—e-mail: eric.t.brizes@nasa.gov
Niobium alloy C-103 is a refractory alloy used in the manufacturing of
spacecraft propulsion components for its mechanical properties at elevated
temperatures. Fair weldability makes C-103 additively manufacturable
(printable) utilizing laser powder-bed fusion (L-PBF). Printed C-103 parts are
often subjected to hot isostatic pressing (HIP) to obtain a microstructure with
fewer defects and more isotropic properties. Brief exposures to high-temper-
ature environments are common for C-103 parts; experimentally, a high-
vacuum annealing (HVA) process was employed to identify changes from
temperature effects apart from the HIP process. This study compares the
effects of HVA and HIP processes on the microstructure (i.e., grain size,
morphology), formation of precipitates, microhardness, and anisotropy of C-
103 fabricated by L-PBF. The results showed recrystallization and texture
changes occurred during HIP but not HVA, contributing to retained, albeit
altered, anisotropy in the HVA-ed material. The microhardness of HVA-ed C-
103 was greater and more anisotropic than HIP-ed for annealing tempera-
tures below 1200 /C176C. Hafnium oxide precipitates were observed in all the
samples and which coarsened with increasing HVA temperature. Oxide area%
determined via image analysis showed a strong negative correlation with
microhardness. Overall, this paper presents novel ﬁndings on the effects of
HVA and HIP on C-103 printed via L-PBF and forms a basis for future post-
processing of C-103 parts.
INTRODUCTION
Niobium alloy C-103 is a refractory alloy histor-
ically used in the manufacturing and design of high-
temperature aerospace propulsion components. C-
103 consists of niobium alloyed with 10 wt.%
hafnium, and 1 wt.% titanium (Nb-10Hf-1Ti). This
alloy exists in a predominately single-phase, body-
centered cubic structure, has excellent ductility,
and maintains a density less than 9 g/ml.
1 Histor-
ically, this alloy has been chosen for its superior
mechanical properties at elevated temperatures,
and for its ability to be formed through traditional
sheet-forming methods like rolling, deep-drawing,
and spinning.
2
Fair weldability characteristics have made C-103
amenable for use in additive manufacturing (AM)
processes like laser powder-bed fusion (L-PBF),
which can reduce production costs by producing
near-net-shape parts.
3 Unlike sheet-forming meth-
ods, L-PBF uses a pre-alloyed spherical powder that
is deposited in ﬁne layers by a bladed or rolling
recoater and fused by a high-powered laser to a
build platform or previously constructed layers.
When processing materials by L-PBF, large thermal
gradients occur that can contribute to the formation
of residual stresses and to an anisotropic grain
structure with elongated columnar grains oriented
along the build direction.
4 Adjustments to AM
processing parameters and feedstock material may
mitigate unwanted microstructures and residual
stresses. However, it is often more effective to heat-
treat and post-process AM C-103 parts to relieve

Effects of high-temperature environments and
heat-treatment of Nb-alloys have been studied.
Schmidt et al. studied the effects of heat treatment
on various Nb alloys in both vacuum (10
/C05–
10/C07 torr) and inert gaseous environments, and
found that signiﬁcant contamination from O, N,
and C occurred regardless of the environment,
leading to a reduction in strength.
8 Taylor further
explored Nb-Hf alloy contaminations of O, N, and C
during heat-treatment,
9 and found that O 2 was
soluble and stable up to 6 wt.% at 1775/C176C; however,
above this temperature, any oxygen content would
cause catastrophic oxidation. When N was present,
either interstitial N
2 or hafnium nitride (HfN)
would be formed depending on the Hf content. Like
N additions, when C was available, interstitial
carbon, NbC, and HfC could form depending on
the Hf content. Overall, both nitride and carbide
contents depended signiﬁcantly on annealing time
and temperature with signiﬁcant growth above
1650/C176C.
Savitskiy et al. studied the recrystallization of
niobium alloys.
10 In their work, cast material was
subjected to cold-compression, with comparisons
made to hot-rolled Nb. Both processed materials
were annealed to recrystallization and studied
microstructurally via X-ray analysis and hardness
measurements. They found that material subjected
to higher deformation recrystallized at lower tem-
peratures, indicating that recrystallization occurs
more rapidly when the crystal lattice is deformed.
Signiﬁcant differences were found in recrystalliza-
tion temperature between cold-deformed, and hot-
rolled materials. The cold-deformed recrystalliza-
tion temperature was found to range from 1125/C176Ct o
1150/C176C, while the hot-rolled recrystallization tem-
perature was 1550/C176C. They found that the purity of
Nb showed the strongest effect on recrystallization
temperature, with O, N, and C impurities sharply
increasing the recrystallization temperature. Grain
size was shown to have little impact on hardness,
but considerable effect on ductility.
Bonesteel et al. explored the effects of internal
oxidation on a Nb-1W-1Zr alloy.
11 Existing oxygen
impurities were present in the sheet, and the sheets
were annealed at various temperatures for extended
times. Oxide precipitates were observed at 800 /C176C
with much larger precipitate zones observed at
1200/C176C. Additionally, the formation of oxides
increased the recrystallization temperature of the
alloy. Applying these observations for Nb-1W-1Zr
alloy oxidation to C-103 may not merit an equal
comparison, because the much larger Hf content in
C-103 (10 wt.%) changes the chemistry, formation,
and diffusion behavior of high-temperature oxides.
Awasthi et al. conducted a study of the mechan-
ical properties and microstructural characteristics
of AM C-103 with comparisons to wrought speci-
mens.
5 In their study, tensile specimens were
manufactured in the transverse direction, ﬂat on
the build platform, and heat-treated at 1000 /C176C
before removal. The yield and ultimate strengths
of the AM specimens were somewhat greater than
the wrought, although elongation to failure was
signiﬁcantly reduced. Similarly, Mireles et al. stud-
ied the microstructure and mechanical properties of
AM and wrought C-103 at elevated temperatures.
12
They found that an annealing temperature of
1100/C176C resulted in recrystallization of the wrought
samples but not those produced by L-PBF. The
higher recrystallization temperature for the AM
specimens was likely due to the formation of HfO
2,
which formed as a secondary phase precipitate
leading to Zener pinning preventing grain growth.13
The conclusion of Mireles et al. stated that smaller
grains in the samples manufactured by L-PBF led to
higher mechanical strength even at elevated
temperatures.
Although previous studies have yielded insight
into the effects of high-temperature environments
and heat-treatments of C-103 and other Nb refrac-
tory alloys, much is still unknown about the effects
of high-vacuum annealing (HVA) on powder-based
printed C-103, especially when compared to HIP
processing. This study compares the effects of HVA
with those of HIP on the (1) microstructure (i.e.,
grain size, morphology), (2) formation of precipi-
tates, (3) microhardness, and (4) anisotropy. The
microstructure and mechanical behavior of both
HVA-ed and HIP-ed specimens are compared to that
of wrought C-103.

Heat Treatments
Hot Isostatic Pressing
A set of printed specimens was subjected to HIP.
During HIP, specimens were individually wrapped
in Ta foil to prevent oxidation and were loaded into
a high-pressure inert-gas environment and held at a
temperature of 1500 /C176C. After the HIP cycle, the
specimens were removed and inspected for evidence
of oxidation. No discoloration of the specimens or
the Ta foil was present, and the Ta foil retained its
ductility, indicating that no signiﬁcant oxidation
occurred during HIP. Additionally, the measured
oxygen content (as given in Table I), remained
consistent in the as-printed and HIP-ed specimens.
High-Vacuum Annealing
Sets of printed specimens were subsequently
heat-treated in a high-vacuum furnace, as shown
in Fig. 3. All the specimens were cleaned with
ethanol and individually wrapped in Ta foil to
prevent oxidation; individually wrapped specimens
from each printed orientation were wrapped
together in a larger Ta foil package.
Three thermocouples were placed equidistant
across the length of the package along the top of
the specimens. The package was then cinched shut
using Ta wire. This process was repeated seven
times for seven individual heat treatment, ranging
from 900 /C176C to 1500 /C176C in 100 /C176C increments. Each
package was then suspended into the center of the
furnace using tungsten wire and secured at the
bottom to maintain central alignment. For each
heat treatment, the specimens were brought to a
vacuum of 10

temperature to the target temperature at a rate of
17/C176C/min. After the desired temperature was
reached and sustained for 1 h, the heater was
turned off and the setup allowed to cool to ambient
conditions under vacuum overnight. The thermal
history for the seven HVA heat treatments are
shown in Fig. 3. Each package was then removed
from the furnace, disassembled, and inspected for
evidence of oxidation. No discoloration or changes to

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Microstructure
SEM imaging was performed for all the studied C-
103 processing conditions. EDS and EBSD mapping
were conducted on the as-printed, HIP-ed, HVA-ed
(at 900 /C176C, 1200 /C176C, and 1500 /C176C) and wrought sam-
ples. EBSD average statistics for grain area, aspect
ratio, equivalent circle diameter (ECD), length,
grain orientation spread (GOS), and maximum
orientation spread (MOS) are provided in Table II.
EBSD inverse pole ﬁgure maps are shown in Fig. 4.
The EBSD maps show that all the powders were
fused to form a dense, single-phase, polycrystalline
material. The as-printed and HVA-ed material show
an elongated structure with ﬁne grains around 11–
12 lm in ECD. The HIP-ed specimens show signif-
icantly larger (237.33- lm ECD), more equiaxed,
grains compared to the as-printed material. The
wrought specimens show equiaxed grains that are
only slightly larger than those measured in the as-
printed and HVA-ed material (19.54- lm ECD).
Precipitates
Higher-magniﬁcation SEM images revealed pre-
cipitates in all the heat-treated conditions, although
predominately in HVA-ed specimens above 900 /C176C.
High-magniﬁcation SEM images are shown in
Fig. 5.
The observed precipitates migrated and coars-
ened at the grain boundaries as temperature
increased, indicating that a stable oxide is formed
from the base material. The standard enthalpies of
formation for prominent oxide phases (at 298.15 K)
of NbO
2, HfO 2, and TiO 2 are /C0796.785 kJ/mol,
/C01117.63, kJ/mol and /C0945.0 kJ/mol respec-
tively.17,18 A more negative standard enthalpy of
formation indicates that the compound is more
stable and formation more favorable; as such,
HfO
2 can be expected to be the main oxide formed
in C-103. This presumption is conﬁrmed by previous
studies5,12 and through EDS maps showing a larger
concentration of O and Hf within the precipitates
and near the grain boundaries. Figure 6 shows the
EDS for the HVA-ed specimen at 1500 /C176C.
Image analysis was conducted on SEM images
at 9 10 k magniﬁcation to quantify the oxide area%
(Table III). Non-linear growth in oxide area% with
temperature was observed from the as-printed to
1200/C176C, where the oxide area became saturated
close to 1.3%. The oxygen that forms the precipi-
tates entered the bulk Nb matrix both from the
environment during printing, and from trapped
oxygen/oxides on the surface and within the volume
of the powder. It is unlikely that additional oxygen
is absorbed during high-vacuum annealing as
shown by the insigniﬁcant oxygen change in the
measured HVA chemical compositions (Table I).

a strong negative correlation to the oxide area

directly related to the very ﬁne (unobservable)

large and located at grain boundaries, no longer
providing additional strengthening. As a result, the
hardness decays from as-printed to 1200 /C176C, where
the oxides have sufﬁciently coarsened and disper-
sion strengthening is no longer present. The Pear-
son correlation coefﬁcient ( r) for hardness behavior
and oxide area fraction was found to be /C00.981,
where a perfect negative correlation would corre-

Anisotropy
Material anisotropy is explored through EBSD
and hardness testing. Changes in microstructure
texture with processing and respect to the BD and
TD were identiﬁed through comparisons of the pole
ﬁgures in the as-printed, HVA-ed, and HIP-ed
conditions (Fig. 8).
The as-printed and HVA-ed specimens have
nearly identical textures aligned with the BD,
indicating the presence of anisotropic properties.
The HIP-ed and wrought specimens have a more
random texture, indicating more isotropic proper-
ties. It is evident that recrystallization occurred in
the HIP-ed specimen, as shown by the presence of
signiﬁcantly larger and more equiaxed grains com-
pared to the as-printed in Fig. 4, and the signiﬁcant
texture changes shown in Fig.8. Other sources have
observed that other alloys produced via L-PBF may
recrystallize during HIP but not (at least to the
same magnitude) during annealing.
19,20 The higher
recrystallization temperature of AM C-103 com-
pared to that of the wrought samples has been
observed before, and is likely due to pinning of grain
boundaries by Hf oxides.
12 These observations mir-
ror those found previously,10 and indicate that both
deformation and the amount of precipitate oxides at
the grain boundaries play a signiﬁcant role in the
recrystallization behavior of C-103.
Additionally, the shape of the grains may impact
the anisotropic material properties; as such, the
ratio of hardness in the BD (indentation on the
plane shown in Fig. 2a) to the hardness in the TD
(indentation on the plane with a normal direction
along the recoater direction shown in Fig. 2a) and
the granular aspect ratio are plotted with annealing
temperature in Fig. 9. Measurements of granular

as no signiﬁcant changes in grain structure were

CONCLUSION
This study compared the effects of HVA and HIP
on the (1) microstructure (i.e., grain size, morphol-
ogy), (2) formation of precipitates, (3) material
hardness, and (4) anisotropy of AM C-103 using L-
PBF. Overall, the experimental ﬁndings from this
study on the HVA and HIP processes indicate that
signiﬁcantly different mechanisms occur in the
microstructural and mechanical property changes
to C-103 during these two post-processing methods.
Therefore, the HVA and HIP processes cannot be
used interchangeably, but both may serve as viable
options in future material engineering endeavors to
obtain the desired microstructural and mechanical
properties. From the study, the following conclu-
sions can be drawn:
1. Recrystallization was not observed in any of the
HVA-ed (900–1500 /C176C) specimens, but was ob-
served in the HIP-ed material. All the specimens
showed the formation of Hf oxide precipitates
which coarsened at the grain boundaries with
increasing temperature.
2. Hardness in both the BD and TD had a strong
negative correlation with perceived oxide area
fractions quantiﬁed by image analysis, indicat-
ing that the as-printed material beneﬁts from
dispersion strengthening from very ﬁne oxides
