ABSTRACT
Developing 9Cr oxide dispersion-strengthened (ODS) Fe-based superalloy combining elevated-temperature 
strength and oxidation resistance was a challenge for advanced nuclear applications. A new 9Cr ODS ferrite- 
martensite superalloy was designed via Al- and Si-alloying, aiming to improve the comprehensive perfor -
mance at elevated temperatures. The microstructure, mechanical properties and oxidation resistance of 9Cr ODS 
superalloy were investigated in detail. 1.5Al1Si (1.5 wt% Al and 1 wt% Si) alloy achieved the simultaneous 
improvement of mechanical properties and oxidation resistance compared with other alloys at elevated tem -
peratures. Multi-scale characterization revealed that the 1.5Al1Si alloy exhibited a refined dual-phase ferritic- 
martensitic structure (average grain size: 1.47 μ m) with coherent Y

1. Introduction
The gas cooled fast reactor (GFR) is a type of reactor that utilizes fast 
neutrons and a closed nuclear fuel cycle for effective nuclear conversion. 
The reactor is cooled with helium or carbon dioxide, and its outlet 
temperature reaches 850 
◦
C [ 1 ]. The passivation protective layer formed 
by the interaction of alloying elements with oxygen will play a key role 
in the service life of the nuclear fuel cladding material (NFCM) [ 2 – 4 ]. 
Furthermore, NFCM with good mechanical properties can avoid cata -
strophic nuclear leakage in gas-cooled reactors to the greatest extent 
[ 5 , 6 ]. Therefore, it is of great significance to study the oxidation resis -
tance and mechanical properties of NFCM.
Oxide dispersion strengthened (ODS) Fe-based superalloys possess a 
high density of nano-oxide particles within the matrix, which imparts 
excellent high-temperature mechanical properties. Consequently, ODS 
Fe-based superalloy demonstrates promising application potential in 
nuclear fuel cladding materials (NFCM) [ 7 , 8 ]. The martensitic structure 
in 9Cr ferritic-martensite (F-M) superalloy enhances the radiation 
resistance and mechanical properties of the alloy, and is extensively 
utilized in nuclear reactor materials. [ 9 ].As a result, 9Cr F-M ODS alloy 
possesses both good mechanical properties and radiation resistance 
[ 10 ]. However, Shen et al. [ 11 ] reported that FeCr

Aluminum (Al) is usually introduced into 9Cr steels to improve

that in 9Cr steel with an Al content of less than 1.9 wt%, it was

with increasing Al content. Thus, to enhance the alloy ’ s radiation

Silicon (Si) is also often introduced into 9Cr steel as an auxiliary 
antioxidant element (such as CLAM, T91 and Eurofer 97 steels) [ 18 – 20 ].

Xu et al. [ 5 ] reported that the formation of silica in 9Cr ODS steel by 
adding 1 wt% Si can promote the formation of Cr-rich oxide layer. 
However, Li et al. [ 21 ] showed that in order to form a continuous and 
dense silica protective layer, the Si content should not be less than 4.83 
wt% in 9Cr steel. Although the silica protective layer can be continuous, 
the thermal expansion coefficient between silica and the substrate is 
very different, which eventually leads to a large internal stress during 
the cooling process, and the risk of oxide layers spalling is greatly 
increased with the increase of Si content [ 22 ].At the same time, the high 
percentage of Si facilitates the coarsening of the Laves phase, leading to 
a significant enhancement in the brittleness of 9Cr steel [ 23 – 25 ].
Based on the above analysis, introducing high levels of Al (greater 
than 1.9 wt%) or Si (greater than 4.83 wt%) individually fails to 
simultaneously achieve mechanical properties, radiation resistance, and 
oxidation resistance in 9Cr F-M ODS superalloy. The synergistic effects 
of Al and Si in enhancing oxidation resistance while maintaining me -
chanical performance remain unreported in 9Cr ODS Fe-based superal -
loy. The simultaneous addition of Al and Si is expected to promote the 
formation of martensite and improve oxidation resistance. Previous

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructure of the ODS Fe-based superalloy
Fig. 1 (a-f) show that the sizes of 1.5Al1Si and 2.5Al alloy powders 
are similar (~30 μ m), but 2.5Si powder is increased compared to the 
other two alloys at the same mechanical alloying time (48 h). Li et al. 
[ 31 ] reported that high percentage of Si can promote dynamic recovery, 
and reduce the powder ’ s work hardening rate during mechanical 
alloying (MA). For the ODS alloy with a high percentage of Si (2.5Si 
alloy), the coarsening of powder may be also related to the reduction of 
work hardening rate. The reduced work hardening rate makes cold 
welding of powders dominate in the mechanical alloying process, 
eventually causing the 2.5Si alloy powder to coarsen. In addition, it is 
found from Fig. 1 (g) that the alloy elements of 1.5Al1Si alloy are evenly 
distributed after MA.
Fig. 2 is SEM images of matrix in 9Cr ODS alloys. The 2.5Si and 
1.5Al1Si alloys are composed of ferrite and martensite dual-phase 
structure, while the 2.5Al alloy is composed of ferrite and a small 
amount of martensite. The structural units of martensite including pri -
mary austenite grain (PAG) and martensite packet can be clearly 
observed. The EBSD of Fig. 3 (a) also show that the grains of 1.5Al1Si 
alloy present equiaxed and columnar morphology, mainly containing 
ferrite and martensite. The average grain size is 1.47 μ m, and no obvious 
coarsening grains are found. However, the coarse ferrite in 2.5Si alloy 
( Fig. 3 (b)) increases obviously, which eventually leads to the increase of 
grain size. The size of 2.5Al alloy presents a bimodal distribution, 
including fine equiaxed ferrite and coarse residual ferrite. The grain size 
of 9Cr ODS alloy is controlled by the proportion of martensite and re -
sidual ferrite. The increase of volume fraction of martensite and residual 
ferrite will lead to the coarsening of matrix grain size. In Fe
–
Cr alloys, 
the inhibition effect of Al on the formation of martensite is more obvious 
than that of Si [ 9 ].The high content of Si (more than 1 wt%) induces the 
coarsening of the precipitated phase at the ferrite grain boundary, which 
reduces the pinning effect of the precipitated phase on the grain 
boundary and results in the coarsening of the residual ferrite [ 23 ]. 
Therefore, the 1.5Al1Si alloy exhibits fewer martensite and residual 
ferrites compared to the 2.5Si alloy, resulting in significantly smaller 
grain sizes. Furthermore, Xu et al. [ 9 ] reported that when Al content 
exceeds 2.5 wt%, Al has a stronger effect than Si on promoting the 
stability of ferrite phase region. Therefore, 1.5Al1Si alloy has more 
martensite and less residual ferrite, ultimately achieving grain sizes 
comparable to those of the 2.5Al alloy. Based on the above analysis, it is

3.2. Mechanical properties at elevated temperature
As depicted in Fig. 5 (a), the 1.5Al1Si alloy exhibits a significant 
enhancement in tensile strength at 550 
◦
C, achieving a remarkable 648 
MPa. This value is 37 % higher than that of the 2.5Al alloy and 11 % 
higher than that of the 2.5Si alloy, underscoring its superior high- 
temperature performance. The yield strengths of 1.5Al1Si, 2.5Al and 
2.5Si alloys are 549, 387 and 489 MPa, respectively. Notably, the 
1.5Al1Si alloy demonstrates improved elongation (5.6 %) compared to 
the 2.5Si alloy. However, its elongation remains lower than that of the 
2.5Al alloy, attributable to the near absence of martensite in the 2.5Al 
alloy ’ s matrix. Fig. 5 (c) shows that compared with 9Cr ODS and non- 
ODS alloys reported in the literature [ 32 – 37 ], 1.5Al1Si alloy can 
significantly enhance tensile strength at 550 
◦
C. We achieved the 
improvement of mechanical properties of 9Cr F-M alloys via optimizing 
the percentage of Al and Si at elevated temperature. Fig. 5 (b) shows that 
the tensile and yield strength of 1.5Al1Si alloy at 850 
◦
C are 92 and 77 
MPa, and the elongation reaches 13.7 %. Our work proves that 1.5Al1Si 
alloy can still achieve good strength-ductility matching in more extreme 
environments.
The fracture morphologies at 550 
◦
C of Fig. 6 show that different 
numbers of dimples are formed in the three alloys, of which 2.5Al alloy 
is the most, while 2.5Si alloy is the least. Many coarse precipitates are 
produced at the fracture of 2.5Si alloy, but not obvious in 1.5Al1Si alloy 
and 2.5Al alloys. EDS point analysis in Fig. 6 (h) shows that the particle 
is carbides. It has been reported that Si acts as a critical nucleation site, 
accelerating the coarsening of carbides in high-Si alloys [ 38 , 39 ]. This 
phenomenon is particularly pronounced in 2.5Si alloy, where elevated Si 
content significantly promotes the growth of carbides. Ultimately, the 
micrometer-sized coarse carbides in the 2.5Si alloy induced brittle 
fracture in the alloy under external force. A high density of dimples are 
uniformly distributed across the fracture of the 1.5Al1Si alloy ( Fig. 6 (g)) 
tested at 850 
◦

elevated temperatures, atomic diffusion rates increase exponentially, 
enabling grain boundary sliding to readily occur. Concurrently, the 
resistance to dislocation motion decreases substantially, allowing dis -

systems. These thermally activated processes collectively contribute to 
the strength-ductility trade-off: reduced tensile strength (92 MPa) 
coupled with enhanced elongation (13.7 %) in the 1.5Al1Si alloy under 
extreme thermal conditions [ 40 ].

4. Conclusions
New 9Cr ODS Fe-based superalloy was designed via Al and Si- 
alloying to improve the oxidation resistance and mechanical proper -
ties at elevated temperature. The microstructure and the mechanical 
properties of the 9Cr ODS alloys were investigated, respectively. At the 
same time, the high-temperature oxidation resistance and the micro -
structure of oxide scale were comprehensively characterized. Key find -
ings included: 
(1) 1.5Al1Si alloy achieved the simultaneous improvement of me -
chanical properties and oxidation resistance at elevated temper -
ature. The tensile strength of 1.5Al1Si alloy was significantly 
higher than those of 2.5Si and 2.5Al alloys at 550 
◦
C, reaching 
648 MPa, while maintaining a high elongation of 5.6 %. At the 
same time, 1.5Al1Si alloy had the lowest weight gain at 850 
◦
C, 
only 0.79 mg/cm
2
, simultaneously possessing outstanding anti- 
spalling ability compared with 2.5Al and 2.5 Si alloys.
(2) The inhibition effect of Si on the formation of martensite is 
significantly less than that of Al. 1.5Al1Si alloy maintained the 
ferrite-martensite dual phase structure. Excellent mechanical 
properties were mainly attributed to the smaller grain size and 
the formation of martensite in 1.5Al1Si alloy by optimizing the 
proportion of Al and Si. At the same time, the finely dispersed 
Y

improved high temperature mechanical properties by the sup -
pressing dislocation motion.
(3) A concrete Cr

Cr was prevented from oxidation in the substrate due to the fact 
that Al and Si preferentially reacted with a small number of O 
ions diffused inward. More Cr ions subsequently diffused outward

the 1.5Al1Si alloy. The inlaid alumina-silica protective layer
