abstract
The room-temperature fracture toughness and high-temperature compression strength are investi-
gated in directionally solidiﬁed Nb–Mo–Si-based alloys with aligned Nb ss /Nb5 Si3 lamellar structure. The
fracture toughness is higher as grain size is reduced or annealing at 1300 ◦ C for 100 h is applied. The
normalized work of rupture ( /USl ) in the in situ Nb ss /Nb5 Si3 lamellar structure is modeled and calculated
to range from 0.10 to 0.38. Besides, the Nb ss and Nb 5Si3 are ﬁrmly connected on the fracture surface.
Thus, the Nb ss /Nb5 Si3 interface is strongly bonded. Either reducing interface toughness or enhancing
Nbss toughness is advantageous for interface decohesion. In the annealed Nb–17Si–10Mo–0.1Ga alloy,
a small amount of Nb ss /Nb5 Si3 interface decohesion is observed because the annealing increases the
toughness of Nbss . At 1250 ◦ C and 1350 ◦ C, the maximum compression strength is 620 MPa and 520 MPa,
respectively.

1. Introduction
The necessity of raising operation temperature experienced by
turbine blades to enhance propulsion and save energy creates a
great demand for advanced heat-resisting materials. Nb–Si alloys
are competitive in high-temperature applications [1]. Refractory
intermetallic Nb
5Si3, has high strength and creep resistance, and
Nb is tough and ductile. The Nb–Si in situ composites, comprised
of Nb solid solution (Nb
ss) and Nb5Si3, are expected to achieve bal-
anced mechanical properties. However, Nb–Si binary alloys exhibit
low room-temperature toughness [2]. There are two main fac-
tors, which impair toughening by Nb
ss. First, dissolved elements
strengthen Nb ss signiﬁcantly. Second, the constraints imposed by
neighboring Nb5Si3 restrict deformation of Nb ss [2].
In two-phase systems, the toughness is determined not only by
fracture resistance of each phase but also by interfacial constraint,
which is expressed by the normalized work of rupture, /H9273[3]. The
/US value increases with decreasing interfacial constraint. When the
interface is weak-bonded, little constraint is imposed on the ductile
phase by interface decohesion, which promotes plastic deforma-
tion and fracture energy absorbed [3]. Moreover, weak interface
generates crack deﬂection, which is a popular toughening mecha-
nism in composite materials [4]. Thus, weak interface is beneﬁcial
for improving toughness and ductility. Conversely, strong interface
is advantageous for strength.

2. Experimental procedures
Some 1 kg ingots, with nominal compositions of Nb–18Si–10Mo,
Nb–17Si–10Mo–xGa ( x = 0.1, 1) and Nb–17Si–10Mo–3Al (at.%,
unless otherwise stated), respectively, were fabricated with high
purity raw materials by non-consumable arc melting in an argon
atmosphere and were re-melted ﬁve times to ensure chemi-
cal homogeneity. The Ga was wrapped in a Nb foil to reduce
evaporation. Some rods of 9 mm in diameter were cut from the
ingots using electro-discharge machining and then ground to
remove oxidation surface. The samples were directionally solid-
iﬁed in an OFZ furnace [7]. It was referred to as an as-grown
sample. The rotation speed of feedrod was 10 r/min and the
seedrod didn’t rotate. The withdrawal rate was 5 mm/h. The
as-grown samples were encapsulated in silica tubes and then
annealed at 1300
◦C for 100 h, followed by air cooling to room
temperature.
The microstructure was observed by scanning electron
microscopy (SEM). Composition analysis was carried out using
energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS). The macrostructure was
examined by optical microscopy (OM) after etching the sample
with an agent of 10 ml H
2SO4,1 0 m lH N O3, 10 ml HF, and 30 ml
water. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) was operated at
200 kV on specimens prepared by mechanical grinding and Ar-ion
milling.
The fracture toughness ( K

slice model [9], was measured at room-temperature by four-point
bending test using chevron-notched specimens under cross-head
speed of 0.1 mm/min. The outer span was 21 mm and inner
span was 7 mm [10]. The dimensions of bending specimen were
5m m× 5m m× 30 mm. The hardness of Nb
ss was evaluated by
nano-indentation on Nano Indenter DCM machine with an inden-

are given.
The compression tests were conducted on specimen of

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Effect of grain size on fracture toughness
Aligned Nbss/Nb5Si3 lamellar structure is obtained in as-grown
sample (Fig. 1). The bright phase is Nb ss and dark phase is Nb 5Si3.
Also, divergent columnar grains, or cone-shaped grains to be exact,
are observed on a macroscopic scale due to a convex liquid/solid
interface ( Fig. 2 ). Therefore, grain size, i.e., diameter of the col-
umn grain, increases with growth length. As for deﬁnition of grain,
it means a portion with the same crystallographic orientation in
single-phase alloys, but no explicit concept is established for eutec-
tic alloys. Kraft suggested that a eutectic grain was a portion in
which crystallographic orientation and metallographic orientation
of each phase had ﬁxed angular relationship one to another [11].
Since the grain size is varied along the rod, effects of grain size
on fracture toughness are examined ﬁrstly. Two bending samples
were derived from a rod of as-grown Nb–17Si–10Mo–0.1Ga alloy,
as schematically shown in Fig. 2. The distances between the notch
and initial growth position are 5 mm and 30 mm, respectively. They
are accordingly regarded as ﬁne- and coarse-grained samples, with
grain sizes of about 1 mm and 6 mm in diameters, respectively. The
fracture toughness is 11.5 MPa √
m for the ﬁne-grained sample and
9.3 MPa √m for the coarse-grained sample. The ﬁne-grained sam-
ple bears many eutectic grains, while the coarse-grained sample
behaves as a single grained one. It is evident that the toughness of
polycrystalline Nb–Si alloy is higher than that of single-grained one.
It is plausible to state that the fracture toughness increases as the
grain size decreases because the fractographic observation reveals
relatively macroscopic roughness in the ﬁne-grained sample and
ﬂatness in the coarse-grained sample ( Fig. 3(a and c)). The fracture
toughness is associated with tortuosity of crack path or roughness
of fracture surface. It is suggested that microcracks formed ahead
of the main-crack tip in individual grains, as schematically shown
in Fig. 4 . When the main crack propagated, the portion between

3.5. High temperature compression strength
The high temperature compression tests were carried out at
1250
◦C and 1350 ◦C using as-grown Nb–17Si–10Mo–3Al alloys.
The maximum compression strength is 620 MPa and 520 MPa,
respectively ( Fig. 7 ). It was reported that the maximum com-
pression strength of as-grown Nb–10Ti–17.5Si was 450 MPa and
250 MPa at 1200
◦C and 1400 ◦C, respectively [18]. Both alloys have
similar composition, component phases and process, but different
morphology. In the latter, random-orientated Nb
ss/Nb5Si3 lamellar
structure coexisted with some coarse primary Nbss [18]. Some pos-
sible reasons are responsible for the higher compression strength
in the present alloy. First, the high interfacial constraint could
restrict dislocation movement and deformation in Nb ss. Second,
the interface of aligned lamellar structure is approximately par-
allel to loading direction. In this case, shear stress at the interface is
very low. So interface sliding is reduced during high temperature
deformation and microcracks initiated at the interface are avoided.
Besides, Nb
5Si3 developing a [0 0 1] texture along the growth direc-
tion [7] and solid solution hardening by Mo in Nb ss also enhance
compression strength [19].
It can be seen from the stress–strain curves that the stress
drops gradually after it reaches the maximum strength, approach-
ing to a stable strength, which is 420 MPa and 250 MPa, respectively
(Fig. 7 ). The stable strength levels respond to creep-resistances.

4. Conclusions
Aligned Nb ss/Nb5Si3 lamellar structure has been achieved
in as-grown Nb–Mo–Si-based alloys. Mechanical properties of
room-temperature fracture toughness and high-temperature com-
pression strength are evaluated in this study. It is found that
fracture toughness increases with decreasing grain size. It is
revealed that when the Ga content is 0.1%, it shows higher fracture
toughness than Ga-free alloy, but excessive Ga content is harmful
to toughness. The annealing at 1300
◦C for 100 h is beneﬁcial for
fracture resistance.
A model for calculating interfacial constraint in lamellar sys-
tem is proposed and the normalized work of rupture ( /USl)i nt h e
in situ Nb ss/Nb5Si3 lamellar structure is calculated to range from
0.10 to 0.38. Besides, fractographic observation indicates that the
Nb
ss and Nb5Si3 are ﬁrmly connected. Thus, the Nb ss/Nb5Si3 inter-
face is highly constrained. The TEM investigation shows that the
interface is zigzag, which might retard interface sliding and deco-
hesion. This is possibly an intrinsic reason for the strong-bonded
interface.
Either reducing interface toughness or enhancing Nb
ss tough-
ness is advantageous for interface decohesion. In the annealed
Nb–17Si–10Mo–0.1Ga alloy, the fracture surfaces of Nb
ss are
stretched and a small amount of Nb ss/Nb5Si3 interface decohesion
is observed. The annealing decreases the hardness and increases
the toughness of Nb
ss. Both doping and annealing exert no evident
inﬂuence on the interface cohesion.
At 1250 ◦C and 1350 ◦C, the maximum compression strength is
620 MPa and 520 MPa, respectively. The stresses ﬁnally reach stable
strength of 420 MPa and 250 MPa, respectively. The high compres-
sion strength is resulted from strong interface, aligned lamellar
structure, Nb
5Si3 texture and solid solution hardening by Mo in
Nbss.
