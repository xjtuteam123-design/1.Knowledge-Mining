abstract
Alloy 617 is considered a leading structural material for next generation nuclear power plants due to its
good corrosion resistance and exceptional strength at high-temperatures. In the present work, a comple-
mentary study of deformation mechanisms in Alloy 617 was performed via tensile testing at tempera-
tures up to 1000 /C176C. Dynamic strain aging characterized by serrated ﬂow leading to temperature
independent yield strength was observed at intermediate temperatures. At temperatures higher than
700 /C176C, the material’s strength reduced dramatically as a result of the predominance of dislocation creep
and dynamic recrystallization. The changes in deformation mechanisms were established by direct obser-
vations of Electron Backscatter Diffraction mappings and by the analysis of texture development based on
the pole ﬁgure mappings. Recrystallized grains were found to preferentially grow in particle-rich areas
and on high-angle grain boundaries. Finally, fracture was initiated from particle cracks at temperatures
up to 700 /C176C and by triple junction cracks from 800 to 1000 /C176C.

1. Introduction
Global energy demands have been predicted to double by the
year of 2030. The development of green energy sources to replace
traditional fossil fuels, thereby limiting the emission of green-
house gases and reducing global warming effects, is a signiﬁcant
and urgent worldwide objective. Nuclear power is a prominent en-
ergy source for the future due to factors of reliability, safety and
economic performance. The very high temperature gas-cooled
reactor (VHTR) is considered one of the most promising Gen-IV nu-
clear systems. A unique feature of the VHTR is its dual capacities of
highly efﬁcient electricity generation and thermochemical hydro-
gen production [1]. By taking advantage of a high outlet tempera-
ture, hydrogen can either be generated by the reformation of steam
from natural gas or from the iodine–sulfur (I–S) process [2]. An-
other evolutionary improvement of the VHTR is the net plant efﬁ-
ciency, which can exceed 50% at 1000 /C176C, a major improvement
compared to /C24 33% efﬁciency in current light water reactors
(LWRs). Design parameters, including the operating temperature,
were recently modiﬁed in new pre-conceptual designs, mainly
due to material performance limits [3]. Even though the predicted
outlet temperature was reduced from 1000 to 950/900 /C176C, the
operating conditions for the VHTR still pose a great challenge for
structural materials of the intermediate heat exchanger (IHX), with
a requirement to withstand a high pressure ( /C24 7 MPa) and aggres-
sive atmosphere (with corrosion and carburization/decarburiza-
tion) for over 60 years [4]. Alloy 617 has been identiﬁed as a
leading structural material due to its exceptional creep strength
at elevated temperature and plentiful experimental data for engi-
neering design [5].
Alloy 617 is a solid-solution strengthened austenitic alloy,
introduced in the early 1970’s as a tungsten-free, nickel-based al-
loy with lower weight and cost compared to tungsten-containing
alloys [5]. Numerous studies on Alloy 617 have been carried out
since it was chosen as a candidate structural material for high tem-
perature reactors (HTRs). The corrosion behavior of Alloy 617 has
been studied by Bates [6], Graham [7], Christ et al. [8,9], Jang
et al. [10] and Cabet et al. [11–15] in simulated HTR/VHTR atmo-
spheres. Corrosion behavior has also been studied by Jang et al.
[10], Harper et al. [16] and Wright [17] in high-temperature air
for various exposure times. Additionally, time dependent deforma-
tion, or creep, is a primary concern for major components with
long service life (up to 60 years) in a VHTR. Numerous creep dam-
age assessments of Alloy 617 have been performed in both air
[5,18–25] and simulated reactor environments [26–28]. Other
mechanical properties, such as tensile, fatigue, creep-fatigue inter-
actions and fatigue crack initiation and growth, have been ana-
lyzed in different environments as well [27,29–34].
Rahman et al. recently studied the deformation behavior of Al-
loy 617 at 600 and 800 /C176C, with a focus on the dynamic strain aging
effect [35]. Additionally, Roy and Marthandame studied anomalous
increases in yield strength (YS) at 800 and 900 /C176C, which can be
attributed to the precipitation of M 23C6 [32]. The precipitation
behavior of Alloy 617 during long-term thermal aging has been
studied by Wu et al. [36]. This study featured the development of
the time-temperature transformation (TTT) diagram for the alloy
according to existing data. The current study presents the results

2. Experimental procedures
The Alloy 617 sample used during the study was supplied by
Haynes International in the form of a one inch thick plate, which
was hot worked and solution treated at 1177 /C176C for 37 min (Heat
Number: 861758808). The chemical composition is shown in
Table 1 .
The current prospective design of the IHX for the VHTR limits
the thickness of the chosen material to a range of 0.2 mm to
2m m [41]. Considering a compatible testing geometry, miniature
specimens were used to study the tensile response ( Fig. 1 a). The
same size Alloy 617 specimens have been employed to study the
grain boundary engineering and irradiation effects of the alloy [40].
During this study, Alloy 617 specimens were taken in the short
transverse (ST) and long transverse (LT) directions from both the
rolling and transverse planes ( Fig. 1 b). Tensile tests with a strain
rate /C24 10/C0 3/s were conducted at temperatures from room temper-
ature (RT) to 1000 /C176C using an INSTRON 1331 servohydraulic test
system equipped with a high-temperature furnace. Temperature
was maintained for /C24 40 min after heating up to the desired value
in order to ensure a stabilized and homogenous heat zone. The
temperature variation is ±3 /C176C during testing.
Microstructural analysis was performed on a JEOL 7000F scan-
ning electron microscope (SEM) coupled with EBSD capabilities.
The SEM samples were etched for /C24 3 min using 10 ml acetic acid,
15 ml HCl and 10 ml HNO 3. The EBSD samples were polished with
600 grit SiC abrasive paper, followed by 1 lm and 0.3 lm alumina
solutions, and ﬁnished with vibratory polishing using 0.02 lm col-
loidal silica.

3. Material characterization
The triplanar montage generated from SEM images illustrates
the Alloy 617 microstructure in the three principle directions re-
lated to the rolling direction ( Fig. 2 ). The intrinsic particles in the
alloy are composed of coarse Cr and Mo carbides, including
M23C6 and M 6C, with a small amount of Ti(C,N). The microstruc-
tural anisotropy becomes evident by comparing the three princi-
ple planes: the rolling plane (RP), the transverse plane (TP) and
the longitudinal plane (LP). The carbides appear strung out as
bands on the TP and LP, but are grouped as clusters on the RP.
All of these particles were initially induced from solidiﬁcation
of the ingot. The particles were asymmetrically shaped and redis-
tributed during hot-roll and were not re-dissolved during solid
solution annealing. As a result, the microstructure characterized
by large particles and crystal ﬁbers, or mechanical ﬁbering , was
preserved after solid solution treatment. Therefore, this micro-
structure that is composed of carbide bands in the transverse
direction can be found extensively in the plate form of Alloy
617 [24,25,34,36,42].
Due to the high volume fraction of carbides in the particle-rich
areas (i.e. particle clusters in the RP and particle bands in the TP
and LP), characteristics of individual grains were partially blocked
and difﬁcult to analyze while solely using SEM imaging. Therefore,
complementary to the SEM analysis, EBSD studies were conducted
to examine microscopic features in these regions. Fig. 3a shows the
duplex grain structures in the Alloy 617 sample: small grains with
sizes ranging from 10 to 30 lm in the particle-rich areas and large
grains with sizes ranging from 40 to 100 lm in the particle-free
areas. During solid solution annealing, the particles exert a pinning
pressure on grain boundaries and impede their movement/migra-
tion ( Zener pinning ) so that the crystal growth is much slower in
particle-rich areas. Hence, ﬁne grains are preserved in the parti-
cle-rich areas after annealing, whereas coarse grains are developed
in the particle-free areas. This accounts for the duplex distribution
of grain size in the Alloy 617. Fig. 3b shows the EBSD analysis of the
areas between particle bands on a transverse plane. The carbide
bands segregate the grains into two layers, presenting a typical
microstructural alignment of the mechanical ﬁbering in Alloy 617.
In this regard, grain growth toward the short transverse direction
during high-temperature annealing was impeded by the carbide
particles. Therefore most of the large grains were located between
carbide bands in transverse planes after solution treatment ( Figs. 2
and 3 ).
The EBSD analysis of the Alloy 617 samples also showed that
/C24 60% of the grain boundaries belonged to the twin boundary cat-
egory (or coincidence site lattice P3 grain boundaries). The frac-
tion of twin boundaries was very high and even comparable to
the value of Alloy 617 after grain boundary engineering ( /C24 64%)
[40].

4. Results and discussion
Fig. 4 a shows the stress–strain diagram of Alloy 617 in the LT
direction. The yield strength maintained a relatively high value
(>300 MPa) from RT to 800 /C176C. Similar to yield strength, the de-
crease of ultimate tensile strength (UTS) from RT to 700 /C176C is mod-
erate. Above 800 /C176C, the stress rapidly dropped once the yield point
was reached. The tensile responses in the LT direction using mini-
ature specimens at various temperatures are very similar to the re-
sults found during experiments on standard size specimens
[32,35], thus indicating little if any size effects.
Fig. 4 b shows the tensile properties of Alloy 617 in the short
transverse and long transverse directions, indicating that the ori-
entation effect covers nearly the entire range of studied tempera-
tures, particularly from RT to 800 /C176C. Considerably diminished
tensile properties were observed in the ST direction. The difference
in the yield strength between the ST and LT directions is /C24 80 MPa,
remaining nearly constant from RT to 800 /C176C. This difference value
decreases at temperatures above 800 /C176C. The difference in the ulti-
mate tensile strength is /C24 150 MPa at room temperature and be-
comes smaller as the studied temperature is increased. More
detailed discussion about the orientation effect can be found in
Ref. [43]. Even though large anisotropy exists in two sampling
directions, the fundamental deformation behaviors remain similar.
Therefore, the orientation effect will not inﬂuence the following
discussion that is based on tests and examinations for LT direction
specimens.
4.1. Tensile properties from RT to 700 /C176C
At room temperature, the stress–strain curve for tensile tests on
Alloy 617 is smooth and continuous without apparent serrations.
With increasing testing temperature, the stress–strain curve be-
comes serrated and unstable ( Fig. 4a). These serrated stress–strain
curves can be described by the Portevin–LeChatelier effect from dy-
namic strain aging, which suggests that aging occurs during defor-
mation [44]. During plastic deformations, dislocations become
locked by solute atoms and can only be released when sufﬁcient
external stress is applied. This ‘‘drag and release’’ process is dis-
played by the serrated ﬂow in a stress–strain curve. The dynamic
strain aging was evident for Alloy 617 at temperatures ranging
from 300 to 700 /C176C( Fig. 4(a)), with larger serrated ﬂow amplitudes
at higher studied temperatures. The amplitude of the stress oscil-
lations were /C24 8, /C24 10, /C24 15, /C24 18 and /C24 20 MPa at 300, 400, 500,
600 and 700 /C176C, respectively.

occurrence of the yield strength anomaly phenomenon that is pos-
sibly caused by the precipitation of M 23C6 type carbide [32].

(>800 /C176C) because of the involvement of additional high-tempera-
ture deformation mechanisms and the accompanying loss of strain
hardening. 800 /C176C can be considered the equicohesive temperature
for Alloy 617. Above this temperature, grain boundaries are unable
to impede dislocation movement. Hence, matrix and grain bound-
aries have comparable strength. High-temperature creep induced
by thermal assisted dislocation glide and climb becomes a major
deformation mechanism. The changes in deformation behavior at
different temperatures are evaluated from observations of fracture
micromechanism ( Fig. 5 ). During tensile testing at temperatures
below 800 /C176C, dislocations in the alloy do not have sufﬁcient mobil-
ity to climb over carbide particles. Instead, the dislocations pile up
on the interphase boundaries between matrix and carbide particles
(Fig. 6 ). As the number of dislocations increases, sufﬁciently large
stresses are generated and placed on the particles. The particles be-
gin to crack and produce voids during plastic deformation ( Fig. 5a).
The coalescence of the crack voids ﬁnally results in pre-mature
failure during tensile testing. This can be seen in the stress–strain
diagrams for temperatures up to 600 /C176C( Fig. 4a), which show that
elongation is small beyond the maximum stress (UTS) (i.e. little
necking occurs). In contrast, dislocations attain a high thermal-
activated mobility at elevated temperatures (>700 /C176C). At these
high temperatures, particle pinning is not as effective and efﬁcient
compared to low temperatures because many dislocations with
enhanced mobility are able to bypass the particles by dislocation
creep or cross-slip. Furthermore, dislocation boundaries can trans-
form into low-angle subgrain boundaries during high-temperature
deformation [45]. This recovery process partially releases the
strained areas (and also the dislocation energy) closed to the inter-
phase boundaries, and thus decreases the stresses that are placed
on the particles. As a result, few particle crack voids develop at
temperatures above 700 /C176C for the alloy. Instead, intergranular
fracture initiated by triple junction cracks became the major frac-
ture mechanism ( Fig. 5 b). Intergranular fracture is a combined re-
sult of grain boundary sliding (controlled by dislocation creep) and
grain boundary cavitation. Cavity formation was found to depend
on the locally applied stresses and grain boundary surface energy
that relates to the coherence of the boundary. The cavity formation
can be described by the critical radius for stable cavity develop-
ment, rc, where rc =2 cs/r, and cs is the grain boundary surface en-
ergy and r is the local tensile stress normal to the grain boundary
[46]. Due to a relatively high strain and corresponding high stress
level, grain boundary cavities form and coalesce to produce grain
boundary cracks. Failure ﬁnally occurs at wedge cracks located at
grain boundary triple points ( Fig. 5 b).
Another important phenomenon that occurs during high-tem-
perature deformation is dynamic recrystallization (DRX). Fig. 7
shows the SEM and EBSD analyses of a tensile specimen after test-
ing at 800 /C176C. In order to differentiate between the recrystallized
grains and carbides, both alloy matrix and carbides were scanned,
recognized and mapped during EBSD analysis ( Fig. 7 b and c). A
very small number of recrystallized grains with a size of /C24 1 lm
or smaller were found in grain boundaries near a triple point crack.
No apparent recrystallization was seen in other areas. This indi-
cates that dynamic recrystallization rarely occurs during plastic
deformation at 800 /C176C. The new recrystallized grains showed no
tendency for producing speciﬁc crystal textures.
Fig. 8 shows dynamic recrystallization that occurred during ten-
sile testing at 1000 /C176C. Due to a higher temperature, both the num-
ber and the size of recrystallized grains are much larger compared
to the Alloy 617 sample tested at 800 /C176C. After signiﬁcant plastic
deformation, the recrystallized grains develop various crystal ori-
entations, whereas the adjacent large grains attain a texture (e.g.
h111 i). Additionally, a great number of twin boundaries are gener-
ated during dynamic recrystallization due to the alloy’s low stack-
ing fault energy [47]. Compared to a large fraction of recrystallized
grains in the necking zone ( Fig. 8b), dynamic recrystallization that
occurs 2 mm beyond the necking zone is much less intense with a
smaller number of recrystallized grains ( Fig. 8 a). This detail indi-
cates the strain dependence of dynamic recrystallization. Fig. 8 b
and c show a great number of ﬁne grains that nucleated in a parti-
cle-rich area. Particles in alloys, particularly large particles
(>1 lm), have a strong inﬂuence on the recrystallization kinetics,
as well as the recrystallized grain microstructure and texture. Dur-
ing the plastic deformation of Alloy 617, the high volume fraction
of particles strengthens this area by impeding dislocation move-
ment. Dislocation pileups in the vicinity of a particle create regions
of high dislocation density and a large orientation gradient. These
regions are deﬁned as particle deformation zones, or PDZs [45].A

Additionally, although the strain was greater than 0.5 when the

than the un-recrystallized area. This can be attributed to the solu-
tion content of Alloy 617. The grain boundary motion is partially
inhibited by solute atoms that adsorb at the grain boundary. There-
fore, a steady state recrystallized microstructure was not achieved
despite relatively high strain and temperature conditions during
the tests.
The texture development reﬂects different plastic deformation
processes at various temperatures. Fig. 9 shows the texture analy-
sis after tensile tests at 200, 800 and 1000 /C176C. The pole ﬁgure and
inverse pole ﬁgure show that the preferential orientation is
h111 i (primary) and h001 i (secondary), with viewing directions
parallel to the tensile direction for Alloy 617. The texture ap-
proached completed development during low-temperature defor-
mation. Fig. 9 a shows an intense h111 i texture followed by a
weaker h001 i texture developed during testing loading at 200 /C176C.
In contrast, the texture during tensile tests at 800 and 1000 /C176C
did not completely evolve, even though higher strains (greater
elongations) were achieved compared to the tests at lower temper-
atures. Fig. 9 b and c exhibit more evenly mapped intensity at 800
and 1000 /C176C, respectively. The texture usually developed to pro-
mote easy cross slip of dislocations. Since higher temperatures
activated additional deformation mechanisms, including disloca-
tion creep and dynamic recrystallization, texture development
was not as prominent as it was at lower temperatures.
Dynamic recrystallization is considered an effective metallurgi-
cal process for grain reﬁnement during hot deformation. In the as-
received Alloy 617, ﬁne grains within the particle clusters were
recrystallized during hot-roll. When applying further strain at high
temperatures, the grains in these regions were more reﬁned during
recrystallizations. In fact, ﬁne grains were not favorable in terms of
creep resistance for high-temperature long-term application.
Moreover, hot-rolling can result in a strong mechanical anisotropy
in Alloy 617 ( Fig. 4 b), which may impact the integrity of required
component shapes and may cause localized failure. In order to de-
velop desired mechanical properties for Alloy 617 to be used at
very high temperature systems, better control of the hot deforma-
tion for the alloy will be required for developing a suitable distri-
bution of the large particles and the following grain reﬁnement.
Finally, dynamic recrystallizations not only occurred during
short-term high-temperature deformation, but also were found
in long-term creep tests. Chomette et al. has found the presence
of grain boundary migration and recrystallization in Alloy 617 dur-
ing creep tests at 850 and 950 /C176C [49]. Dynamic recrystallization
during creep has also been observed by Sharma et al. [20,23].
The dynamic recrystallization behavior studied during the current
work has been found to be similar to the one observed in long-term
creep conditions. This indicates that dynamic recrystallization is
possibly independent of deformation time, and thus the analysis
in the current study can be extended to the long-term creep condi-
tions at high temperatures.
5. Conclusions
This study presents the results and analysis of research of the
deformation mechanisms of Alloy 617 for tensile loading at tem-
peratures up to 1000 /C176C. During tensile tests at temperatures from
300 to 700 /C176C, the yield strength was found to be independent of
temperature as a result of additional strain hardening induced by
dynamic strain aging. Higher temperatures activated additional
deformation mechanisms, including dislocation creep and dynamic
recrystallization, leading to a dramatic decrease of the strength at
the studied temperature higher than 800 /C176C. Consequently, the
fracture mechanisms changed from particle cracks at temperatures
up to 700 /C176C to triple junction cracks from 800 to 1000 /C176C. A small
number of recrystallized grains were observed in the highly
strained area connected to triple junction cracks during the tensile
tests at 800 /C176C. Dynamic recrystallization became predominant at
1000 /C176C. A great number of recrystallized grains were observed in
highly misoriented regions within grains, including regions near
large particles and pre-existing high-angle grain boundaries. Final-
ly, even though the strain was greater than 0.5 during high-tem-
perature deformation, steady state recrystallized microstructure
was not reached in Alloy 617.
