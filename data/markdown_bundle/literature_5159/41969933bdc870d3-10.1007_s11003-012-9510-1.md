IMPROVEMENT OF THE MECHANICAL CHARACTERISTICS OF Т110 ALLOY BY 
OPTIMIZING ITS THERMOMECHANICAL AND HEAT TREATMENT 
P. E. Markovs’kyi 1 and O. H. Molyar 1,2 UDC 669.295 
We study the influence of thermomechan ical treatment developed for two-phase α+β-titanium alloys on 
the microstructure and mechanical characteristics of a new Т110 titanium alloy. As a distinctive feature 
of the proposed method, we can mention the fact that, prior to hot deformation, the β -solution treatment 
is performed at a temperature of the single-phase β -region with subsequent cooling at a strictly con-
trolled rate as a result of which it becomes possible to eliminate the negative influence of the initial 
coarse structure of the α-phase of cast material on the final microstructure and form in the alloy a homo-
geneous dispersed microstructure with the structur e of phases similar to a globular structure in the 
course of subsequent plastic deformation at a temper ature lower than the temperature of completion of 
the polymorphic transformation Tβ by 50–70°С. As a result, both the strength and plasticity character-
istics increase substantially and simultaneously. The subsequent strengthening thermomechanical treat-
ment with a somewhat elevated temperature of heating for hardening guarantees the increase in the 
strength up to the maximum high value (1366 MPa) atta ined for the standard (furnace) methods of heat 
treatment for sufficiently high levels of plasticity. The indicated balance of strength and plasticity is 
unique for titanium alloys treated by using the standard furnace methods of heating. 
Keywords: high-strength titanium alloys, strength and pl asticity, thermomechanical and heat treatment. 
Titanium-based alloys are unique st ructural materials for various bran ches of industry, including the aero-
space engineering, where the requirement s to the specific strength are especia lly strong [1, 2]. The strength of 
titanium alloys is determined not only by their chemical composition but also by the presence of some phases 
and microstructures formed, in turn, in different stages of production: from melting of the castings to the final 
heat treatment (HT) of fini shed products [2, 3]. As an important structure-forming factor, we can mention the 
thermomechanical treatment (TMT), which is completely responsible for the sizes of β -grains and crystallo-
graphic textures and, together with HT, determines the morphology and dispers ity of the intragranular structures 
of these alloys. The resulting values of the mechanical and operating characteristics of titanium products also 
depend on some specific properties, in particular, on their weldability determined by the chemical composition 
of the alloys and fairly often insufficient to meet the requirements of desi gners [4]. Taking into account these 
requirements, the researchers of the Paton Institute of Electric Welding of the Ukrainian National Academy of 
Sciences and the ANTONOV State Enterprise developed a new Т110 titanium alloy (up to 6 wt.% Al, 3.5–4.8 Nb, 
0.3–0.8 Zr, 1.5–2.5 Fe, 0.8–1.8 Mo, 0. 8–2.0 V, balance Ti) aimed at combining high weldability with a balance 
of mechanical properties for which the ultimate strength of welded products in all zones is not lower than 
110 kg/mm 2 [5, 6]. According to the generally accepted relati on [7], the total level of its alloying may attain 
10.6 wt.% in the molybdenum equiva lent for the maximum content of β -stabilizers. Thus, by using the opti-

Materials and Methods of Investigation 
We tested a 12-mm-thick plate of Т110 alloy obtaine d by the method of electron-beam melting and rolled at 
the “Titan” scientific production plant (“Тitan” SPP) of th e Paton Institute of Electric Welding. The alloy had 
the following chemical composition (wt.%): 5.2 Al, 3.6 Nb, 1.7 Fe, 0.9 Mo, 1.0 V, 0.4 Zr, balance Ті. Thus, the 
contents of elements stabilizing the β-phase were minimum and, hence, th e molybdenum equivalent was as low 
as 7.51 wt.%. The specimens 10 × 12 × 60 mm in size were cut out from th e plate to study the microstructure 
and phase composition. The specimens we re also subjected to HT [10] and tensile tests both in the intact (see 
Table 1, state 1) and thermally harden ed (state 2) states. Specimens 12 × 12 × 80 mm in size were cut out from 
a different part of the plate and subject ed to TMT in the modes described in [8 ]. This type of treatment included 
the preliminary β -solution treatment at 1000 °С for 40 min with subsequent controlled cooling and rolling in 
profile rollers to a diameter of 8 mm at a temperature lower than the temperature of completion of the polymor-
phic transformation by 50–80 °С (Tβ ) ; in this case, the total strain was equal to 65%. Then the specimens were 
annealed at 800°С for 2 h (state 3). Some of these specimens were thermally hardened in a resistance furnace in 
air (state 4). We investigated the microstructure of th e alloy by the metallographic method with the help of an 
OLYMPUS ІХ70 microscope. The pha se composition was studied in a STADI diffractometer. Cylindrical 
specimens with a diameter of 4 mm and a length of the working part of 25 mm were tested in an INSTRON-
3376 machine according to the ASTM E8M standard by using at least three specimens for each state.

Results and Discussion 
The microstructure of the alloy after rolling performed at the “Titan” SPE is characterized by the presence 
of large α -phase platelets substantially deformed but ground in the process of rolling only partially and located

within the boundaries of nonrecrystallized large grains of the β -phase formed in the process of crystallization of 
the casting (Fig. 1a). A similar structure is observed in cast titanium two-phase α+β -alloys deformed at tem-
peratures of the upper part of the α+β -region (somewhat lower than the temperature of completion of the po-
lymorphic transformation Tβ ) with levels of strain that do not exceed 60–70% [11, 12]. This microstructural 
state is characterized by a relatively low level of mechan ical characteristics (see Table 1, state 1) and the ulti-
mate strength σu does not attain 110 kg/mm 2 (1078 MPa). The first stage of the hardening HT included heat-
ing to temperatures of the upper part of the two-phase α+β -region and quenching, which does not enable us to 
get principal changes in the microstructure of alloy. Af ter final aging, we detect large primary particles of the 
lamellar α -phase (its content attains ∼ 45%) and the dispersed mixture of the α - and β -phases responsible for 
the hardening effect is located betwee n these particles (Fig. 1b). Due to the relatively low fraction of the second 
structural component in the to tal volume of the material ( ∼ 55%), we managed to increase the strength of the 
alloy by at most 247 MPa as compared with the initial deformed state, whereas the unfavorable lamellar mor-
phology of the primary α-phase and the large sizes of β -grains led to relatively lo w parameters of plasticity 
(state 2 in Table 1). 
It is known [2, 3] that, in order to increase the strengt h and plasticity characteristics of titanium alloys, it is 
necessary to realize the transformation of the lamellar α -phase into the globular phase with simultaneous re-
crystallization of the β -grains with formation of the α+β -microstructure of equiaxial type. If the coarse-
grained lamellar microstructure of the cast material is rega rded as the initial state, this can be attained solely by 
the multistage TMT with a total level of plastic strains higher than 80%. The transformation of this initial mi-
crostructure into the globular structure can be intensif ied by using the approach developed at the Institute for 
Metal Physics of the Ukrainian National Academy of Sciences (IMF) [8], i.e., the β -solution treatment at tem-
peratures of the single-phase β -region. This enables us to dissolve the massive lamellar α -phase and remove 
the negative influence of the initial microstructure. Du e to the decay of the high-temperature metastable β -
phase according to a specific mechanism (the martensitic tr ansformation runs with a partial participation of the 
diffusion processes of redistribution of the alloying elements [13]), the subs equent procedure of controlled cool-
ing at a rate of ∼ 70°С/sec leads to the formation of a highly dispersed ′α+ ′′α -microstructure, which does not 
inherit the morphology of the phase structures obs erved in the initial state (see Figs. 1а, с). 
The subsequent TMT at 800°С with a total level of strains that does not exceed 65% promotes the formation 
of a more dispersed and homogeneous microstructure, which is closer to the equiaxial structure (Fig. 1d). The 
indicated transformation of microstructu re of the alloy enables us to enhance the characteristics of both strength 
(by 120 MPa) and plasticity as compared with the initial stat e (cf. states 3 and 1 in Table 1). Note that the best 
results can be obtained for higher levels of strains but, in the analyzed case, the level of strains is limited by 65% 
due to the insufficiently large thickness of the initial plate. 
The temperature of heating of the alloy for quenching (850 °С) was chosen by the authors from the view-
point of minimizing the decrease in the characteristics of pl asticity. However, it is too far from the temperature 
Tβ . As a consequence, we get a relativel y large fraction of the residual primary α -phase, which does not par-
ticipate in the thermal hardening and, hence, a relatively insignificant elevation of stre ngth. Since the alloy sub-
jected to TMT has a larger plasticity margin, we us ed a temperature of heating for quenching closer to Tβ 
(880°С). This enabled us to reduce the fraction of the primary α -phase to ∼ 35%. As a result, the ultimate 
strength increased to 1366 MPa for a fairly satisfactory leve l of plasticity. It is also worth noting that, as a result 
of this hardening heat treatment, the ultimate strength increased by 291 MPa as compared with the deformed and 
annealed states (see states 4 and 3 in Table 1), whereas the standard HT gave an increment of 247 MPa for al-
most the same plasticity (states 2 and 1).

The increment of strength of the initial alloy (state 1 in Table 1) after HT was 408 MPa and both the abso-
lute value of strength and its increment can be regarded as extremely high results for titanium alloys subjected to 
the standard (furnace) hardening HT [1–3]. 
By comparing the engineering tensile stress–strain diagrams for the specimens subjected to thermal harden-
ing (Fig. 2), it is shown that, afte r the proposed modes of TMT and HT, the alloy not only exhibits higher 
strength parameters but is also charact erized by a more uniform distribution of plastic strains in the entire stress 
range, i.e., it is less susceptible to the localization of strains than the ma terial after standard treatment. The pro-
posed modes of TMT and HT not only substantially incr ease the strength and plasticity as compared with the 
procedures of treatment used by the developers of the alloy but also significantly (s ometimes, even by an order 
of magnitude) decrease the statistical spread of their valu es. In [8], this was explained by the formation of a 
more dispersed microstructure, which is also more hom ogeneous in the sizes of the phase components of the 
microstructure. 
CONCLUSIONS 
In the present work, it is shown that, by optimizing th e modes of thermomechanical and heat treatment, we 
can substantially improve the microstructure and mechan ical characteristics of the new Т110 titanium alloy. 
The proposed TMT includes the preliminary β -solution treatment at temper atures of the single-phase β -region 
followed by the controlled cooling at a certain rate, whic h enables us to remove the negative influence of the 
coarse initial microstructure of the casting and form a homogeneous dispersed microstr ucture close to the globu-
lar structure in the course of subsequent hot plastic de formation. As a result, we get a simultaneous significant 
improvement of the strength and plasticity characteristics. In addition, this enables us to make, in the course of 
subsequent heat treatment, the temperat ure of heating for quenching as close to the temperature of completion of 
the polymorphic transformation as po ssible and, hence, attain extrem ely high levels of strength ( σu = 
1366 MPa) with sufficiently high plastic ity characteristics after final aging. As a distinctive feature of the mi-
crostructural states obtained after the optimized TMT and HT, we can mention the minimum statistical spread of 
values of the mechanical characteristics.
