Tensile properties of L1 2 intermetallic foils
fabricated by cold rolling
Polycrystalline L12-type Ni3(Si,Ti), Ni3Al and Co3Ti alloys
prepared through thermomechanical processing from arc-
melted ingots were successfully cold-rolled to thin foils
with a thickness of less than 200 lm. The cold-rolling with
over 90 % reduction in thickness was possible without
providing intermediate annealing. The cold-rolled foils
showed high tensile strength ( *2 GPa) at room tempera-
ture although no plastic elongation was observed. The ten-
sile strength of the annealed foils generally decreased ac-
quiring a certain level of fracture strain. Room temperature
fracture strain increased with increasing annealing tempera-
ture, and reached to 30 – 40 % by a high temperature anneal-
ing at 1173 K. Among three kinds of intermetallic alloys,
the Ni 3(Si, Ti) foil annealed around at 900 K exhibited an
extremely high tensile strength and yield strength (over
2 GPa) with a reasonable fracture strain. Also, it was found
that the fully-recrystallized Ni 3(Si, Ti) and Co 3Ti foils
showed a strength anomaly at intermediate testing tempera-
ture. The observed tensile properties, especially tensile
strength at low temperature as well as at high temperature
for the present L1
2 intermetallic foils, were found to be
superior to those for the conventional alloys such as nickel
based alloys and stainless steels.
Keywords: Ni
3(Si, Ti); Co 3Ti; Ni 3Al; L1 2 structure; Cold
rolling; Tensile properties
1. Introduction
In most of intermetallic compounds and related alloys, plas-
tic deformability especially at low temperature is very poor
because slip deformation is difficult due to their complex
crystal structures. By contrast, the L1 2 structure derived
from A1 (fcc) structure is composed of a relatively simple
crystal lattice, and therefore slip deformation is expected
to occur even at low temperature. However, the polycrystal-
line L1 2 ordered intermetallic alloys have for a long time
suffered from a propensity to intergranular fracture. This
drawback has been improved by micro- [1] and macro-al-
loying [2]. It is well known that low temperature ductility
of an L1
2 Ni3Al polycrystal can be improved by boron dop-
ing [1]. Also, an L1 2 Ni3(Si, Ti) alloy, which has been de-
veloped by Ti addition to an L1 2 Ni3Si alloy, shows fairly
good ductility at low temperature, as well as increased
strength at high temperature [2]. On the other hand, an L1
2
Co3Ti alloy which has its solid solution range approxi-
mately from 20 at.% to 24 at.% Ti shows considerable duc-
tility at low temperature without applying micro- and
macro-alloying methods [3]. These three alloys exhibit the
high flow strength increase associated with positive tem-
perature dependence of flow strength (i. e., strength anom-
aly) as well as good corrosion and/or oxidation resistance,
and thereby are considered to possess high potential for
use as high temperature structural materials. In addition,
fabrication by cold rolling has been applicable to these L1
2
alloys using their good plastic deformability.
Hirano et al. have recently fabricated thin foil of boron-
free Ni3Al with a thickness of below 100 lm by cold rolling
using directionally solidified single crystals [4 – 6]. They
found that the heavily cold-rolled Ni 3Al with a thickness
of 23 lm showed a very high fracture tensile strength
(1.3 – 1.8 GPa) at room temperature and has some bending
ductility in spite of almost no plastic tensile elongation [7].
The Ni3Al foils are suggested to be used for high tempera-
ture structural applications such as automotive catalyst sup-
ports and micro-chemical reactors. However, preparing sin-
gle crystals is not favorable in terms of industrial scale and
cost. Additionally, the initial orientation of the single crys-
tal must be chosen to ensure good cold rolling [8]. More
recently, it has been reported that a nanocrystalline Ni
3Al
foil was produced from an as-cast coarse-grained sheet by
heavy rolling at liquid nitrogen temperature and subsequent
recrystallization, and also that this foil showed an ex-
tremely high yield stress ( *2.6 GPa) at room temperature
[9, 10]. However, whether or not other L1
2 alloys such as
the Ni3(Si, Ti) and Co 3Ti alloys are fabricable to thin foils
by a similar method has not yet been reported.
In this study, we employed conventional polycrsytalline
ingots of the Ni 3(Si, Ti), Co 3Ti and Ni 3Al alloys as starting
materials. These ingots were thermomechanically treated
and then cold-rolled to thin foil with a thickness of below
200 lm. The purpose of this paper is to present room- and
high-temperature tensile properties of the cold-rolled and
annealed foils of these L1
2 alloys.
2. Experimental
The nominal compositions of the Ni 3(Si, Ti), Co 3Ti and
Ni3Al ordered alloys were Ni 79.5Si11Ti9.5 doped with
50 wt.ppm boron, Co 78Ti22 and Ni 76Al24 doped with
500 wt.ppm boron (expressed by at.% except for boron con-
tent), respectively. The purities of raw materials used were
99.99 wt.% Ni, 99.9 wt.% Ti, 99.999 wt.% Si, 99.9 wt.%
Co, 99.99 wt.% Al and 99.9 wt.% boron. These ordered al-
loys were prepared by arc melting in an argon gas atmo-
sphere on a copper hearth using a non-consumable tungsten
electrode. Button ingots were melted more than three times

All the Ni 3(Si, Ti), Co 3Ti and Ni 3Al alloys were success-
fully cold-rolled to more than 90 % reduction in thickness
without edge cracks. Figure 1 shows a typical example of
the thin foils obtained by the present fabrication process.
Figure 1a shows the appearance of the 90 % cold-rolled
Co3Ti foil with a thickness of 200 lm. The surface of the
foil is very smooth with a shiny metallic luster, like ordin-

foils were further cold-rolled to thin foil. Figure 1b shows

3.2. Tensile property and fracture mode
Figure 7 shows the nominal stress versus nominal strain
curves for the 90 % cold-rolled and annealed Ni3(Si, Ti) foil
with a thickness of 200 lm. Also, the data for the as-homo-
genized ingot is included in this figure, as reference for the
unrolled alloy. Tensile testing was conducted at room tem-
perature in air. The cold-rolled foil shows a high fracture
strength of *2 GPa although no plastic elongation is ob-
served. When the cold-rolled foils were annealed, tensile
strength generally decreased accompanied by a certain lev-
el of fracture strain. Fracture strain increases with increas-
ing annealing temperature, and reaches *30 % with a high
temperature annealing at 1173 K. Yielding phenomena,
i. e. serrations in stress vs. strain curves, are recognized in
the foils annealed at 973 K and 1073 K. Here, it should be
noted that intermediate temperature annealing (i. e. around
at 900 K) yields an extremely high tensile strength of over
2 GPa with a reasonable fracture strain. Thus, fracture
strain as well as tensile strength of the present foils is
strongly dependent on annealing temperature. Also, it is
noted that both tensile strength and 0.2 % proof stress for
the cold-rolled and annealed foils are apparently high com-
pared with those for the as-homogenized ingot (i. e., un-
rolled alloy): for example, tensile strength of the 873 K –

strength, 0.2 % proof stress and fracture strain of the 90 %

relatively high tensile (fracture) strength ( *2.2 GPa) com-
pared with the Ni 3(Si, Ti) and Ni 3Al foils. For the annealed
foils, the Ni 3(Si, Ti) foil shows generally higher tensile
strength and 0.2 % proof stress than the other two alloys.
Concerning tensile elongation, none of the cold-rolled foils
show plastic elongation while the annealed foils show
fracture strain depending on annealing temperature. The
Ni3(Si, Ti) and Ni 3Al foils annealed above 873 K show
fracture strain of 6 – 30 %. In the Co 3Ti foil, annealing at
1173 K yields an extremely high fracture strain of *40 %,
although annealing at 1273 K results in reduced fracture
strain possibly due to the environmental embrittlement for
the coarse grained microstructure. The increased fracture
strain for the annealed foils is apparently due to recrystalli-
zation because recrystallization occurs above 873 K irre-
spective of alloys, as clearly seen in the hardness change
(Fig. 2). Here, it is again found that the Ni 3(Si, Ti) foil an-
nealed at intermediate temperatures shows higher fracture
strain than the cold-rolled Ni 3(Si, Ti) foil: the Ni 3(Si, Ti)
foil annealed at 823 – 873 K show a very high tensile
strength of *2.2 – 2.3 GPa and 0.2 % proof stress of 1.9 –
2.0 GPa, accompanied by distinct fracture strain of 2.0 –
5.5 %. When comparing hardness and strength properties
for the Ni 3(Si, Ti) foil, hardness shows a peak around at
700 – 800 K while tensile strength shows a peak around at
823 – 873 K. The small discrepancy of peak temperature be-
tween hardness and tensile strength may be attributed to the
difference in the deformation mode: the former data are ba-
sically obtained in compression mode and therefore do not
so much depend on microstructure but flow behavior of
the alloy while the latter data are obtained in tension mode
and therefore depend on not only microstructure but also
flow behavior of the alloy.
Figure 9 shows SEM fractography of the 90 % cold-
rolled and fully-recrystallized foils, which were tensile-de-
formed in air at room temperature. For the fully-recrystal-
lized foils, the fracture surfaces show quite ductile-like
dimple patterns irrespective of alloys, corresponding to
large fracture strain (Fig. 8). On the other hand, the fracture
surfaces of the cold-rolled foils primarily show dimple pat-
terns although the depth of dimple is quite shallow. It is
very interesting to note that although the cold-rolled foils
show no fracture strain, their fracture modes are not com-
pletely brittle but somewhat ductile-like. This result implies
that the present cold-rolled foils are still plastically deform-
able and could be further cold-rolled without providing in-
termediate annealing.

When comparing the tensile data tested in air and
vacuum, fracture strain and tensile strength of both the
Ni3(Si, Ti) and Co 3Ti foils deformed in air is reduced while
0.2 % proof stress is insensitive to the testing environment
(atmosphere). This is due to environmental embrittlement.
It has been well known that many intermetallic compounds
and alloys are susceptible to moisture-induced embrittle-
ment, i. e., the so-called environmental embrittlement tak-
ing place at ambient temperature. The Ni
3(Si, Ti) [18 – 22],
Co3Ti [23 – 25] and Ni 3Al [26] alloys have been shown to
be embrittled when deformed in air, resulting in reductions
in tensile ductility and fracture strength. In this study, the
environmental embrittlement was found to be less signifi-
cant in the Ni 3(Si, Ti) foil than in the Co 3Ti foil. In the
Ni3(Si, Ti) alloys [27] as well as the Ni 3Al alloys [1, 28,
29], it has been reported that the doped boron preferentially
segregates to the grain boundaries and inhibits the harmful
action of hydrogen similarly segregating to the grain
boundaries, resulting in the suppression of intergranular
fracturing. However, the environmental embrittlement of
the boron-doped Ni 3(Si, Ti) foil was not fully suppressed,
as shown in this figure. This result may be explained by an
assumption that the doped boron content is not sufficient
to fully suppress the environmental embrittlement and/or a
too low strain rate used in the present test permits the harm-
ful action (i. e., kinetics) of hydrogen.

4. Conclusion
Polycrystalline L12-type Ni3(Si, Ti), Ni 3Al and Co 3Ti alloys
prepared through thermomechanical processing from arc-
melted ingots were successfully cold-rolled to thin foils with
a thickness of below 200 lm. The cold-rolling with over
90 % reduction in thickness was possible without providing
intermediate annealing. All the alloys showed large work
hardening by heavy cold-rolling. The cold-rolled foils
showed high tensile (fracture) strength ( *2 GPa) at room
temperature. During annealing of the cold-rolled foils, the
hardness showed a broad peak and then decreased with in-
creasing temperature. Room temperature tensile strength
generally decreased with increasing annealing temperature,
acquiring a certain level of tensile elongation. Room tem-
perature fracture strain increased with increasing annealing
temperature, and reached to 30 – 40 % with high temperature
annealing at 1173 K. In the Ni
3(Si, Ti) foil, intermediate
temperature annealing (i. e. around at 900 K) yielded an ex-
tremely high tensile strength and 0.2 % proof stress (over
2 GPa) with a reasonable fracture strain. Also, it was found
that the fully-recrystallized Ni 3(Si, Ti) and Co 3Ti foils
showed a strength anomaly at intermediate testing tempera-
ture. The observed tensile properties of the present L1 2 inter-
metallic foils at low temperature as well as at high tempera-
ture were found to be superior to those for the conventional
alloys such as nickel based alloys and stainless steels.
