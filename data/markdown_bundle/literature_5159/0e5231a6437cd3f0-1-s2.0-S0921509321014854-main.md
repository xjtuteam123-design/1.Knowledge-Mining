ABSTRACT 
Graphene and its analogous 2D nanomaterials demonstrate high potential as reinforcements in bulk metallic 
materials with high performance, but homogenous dispersion within metal matrices remains considerable 
challenging. Here we report the successful fabrication of Ni superalloys from graphene oxide (GO)-decorated 
spherical Ni superalloy powder. The GO-reinforced Ni superalloys show an exceptional combination of yield 
strength and tensile ductility. The high yield strength arises from collective effects of specific microstructures, 
including good interfacial bonding between GO and the matrix, uniformly-distributed precipitates, and GO- 
induced twin boundaries. The good ductility is attributed to a sustainable work hardening ability of GO- 
reinforced Ni superalloys by the addition of GO, enhanced by and dislocation-precipitate interaction and 
stacking fault hardening mechanisms. This work reveals a novel strategy to obtain GO reinforced metallic ma -
terials with high strength and high ductility. 
1. Introduction 
Ni superalloys have been widely applied in aero-engine components 
because of their high mechanical properties, corrosion and oxidation 
resistance [ 1 ]. The excellent mechanical behavior is related to their fine 
microstructures mainly comprising γ and γ
′
phases. At elevated tem -
peratures, the components are commonly cooled by air, where Ni su -
peralloys with high thermal conductivity are desired to avoid hot spots 
and to alleviate thermal stress [ 2 ]. Meanwhile, for the improvement in 
fuel efficiency of Ni superalloy components, a reduction in sliding fric -
tion of Ni superalloys during relative motion is required. Recently, 
studies demonstrates that graphene shows high potential in the 
improvement of thermal conductivity of Ni composites [ 3 ] and in the 
reduction of friction of Ni superalloys [ 4 ]. 
Since the discovery of graphene by Geim and Novoselov in 2004 [ 5 ], 
studies on one-atom thick carbon-based nanomaterial have 
unprecedentedly increased worldwide for developing novel and inter -
esting phenomena in fundamental scientific research [ 6 – 9 ]. Excellent 
mechanical properties associated with a typical large ratio of 
surface-to-volume and a small mean free path between neighboring 
graphene platelets offer possibilities for the exploration of graphene 
reinforced composites in the forms of ceramics, metals and polymers 
with superior mechanical properties for advanced engineering applica -
tions [ 9 – 18 ]. For the graphene-reinforced bulk materials, a homogenous 
distribution of graphene in composites is critical to the full potential of 
graphene layers as their enhancement additives that block dislocation 
motion. However, compared with polymer and ceramic bulk counter -
parts, challenges still remain for metallic materials to obtain uniform 
bulk materials with well-dispersed graphene layers and high interfacial 
strength between graphene and the metal matrix. Herein, in order to 
gain full potential applications of graphene-based material in enhancing 
Ni superalloys, a method to prepare homogeneously-distributed

In this study, we focus on the fabrication of Ni superalloys with 
homogenous dispersion of graphene oxide (GO), and on the effect of the 
homogenously-dispersed GO on the microstructure and mechanical 
behavior of the alloys. In this regard, we report the effect of GO deco -
ration on mechanical properties of Ni superalloys from GO-decorated 
spherical alloy powders prepared by spark plasma sintering (SPS) at 
different isothermal conditions with a constant heating rate. The focused 
ion beam (FIB) technique was carried out to obtain thin foils at the GO/ 
Ni interface position for observations of the microstructure of samples 
after the SPS process. This study also aims to evaluate the impact of 
different isothermal conditions on the strengthening effect of GO (0.025 
wt%), to show high potential of a small amount of GO (0.025 wt%) in the 
remarkable improvement of mechanical properties from densification of 
GO-decorated spherical particles.

For the simulation of the tensile process, FE models for the evalua -

To help understand the effect of GO with/without precipitate and the 
distributions in terms of uniform and non-uniform on the stress and 
strain state of Ni superalloys during uniaxial tension processes, simu -
lations were built by the FE technique to estimate stress and strain levels 
of the three cases including uniform precipitates, uniform GO and pre -
cipitates, and non-uniformly distributed GO and precipitates, Fig. 9 . The 
constitutive parameters and tension data of the simulations were based 
on the experimental results of Ni superalloy samples. The stress level of 
Ni superalloy during the tensile deformation was obtained from the FE 
simulations. The simulation results indicate that stresses in the Ni su -
peralloy with GO reinforcements are lower than those in the Ni super -
alloy without GO ( Fig. 9 a and b). Higher stress concentration can be 
observed in the sample without GO, where the minimum and maximum 
local stresses are 4.1% and 2.9% higher than that of the sample with 
uniform GO. This reveals GO reinforcements can alleviate the local 
stress concentration during the tension process. Additionally, compari -
son of stress levels between superalloys enhanced by uniform GO with 
precipitates and by non-uniform GO with precipitates indicates that non- 
uniform distribution of GO with precipitates can increase the local stress 
concentration during the tensile test ( Fig. 9 b and c). Lower local stress 
concentration likely helps superalloys to reach higher overall fracture 
strength and thus improved ductility. 
Fig. 10 gives elastic strain states of Ni superalloy specimens with 
uniform precipitates, uniform GO and precipitates, and non-uniform GO 
and precipitates during uniaxial tension. The lowest elastic strain values 
have been recorded for samples enhanced by uniform-distributed GO 
and precipitates at a given stress level, indicating a high strengthening 
effect of uniform GO and precipitates. The uniform distribution of GO 
and precipitates can help to generate a uniform reinforcing and bonding 
forces over the whole matrix [ 37 ], which would lead to high stresses to 
reach given strain levels. Furthermore, the uniform distribution may 
result in a smooth transition of the applied loads which leads to 
sustainable work-hardening capabilities ( Figs. 5 c and 8 ). 
The above analyses based on FE simulation results reveal that uni -
form GO and precipitates demonstrate the best ability to strength the 
matrix of Ni superalloy specimens and to alleviate local stress concen -
tration. The decreased stress concentration in Ni superalloy samples also 
has a critical effect on preventing earlier failure such as that observed in 
bare Ni superalloy samples. 
5. Conclusion 
In summary, Ni superalloy composites with a combination of high 
yield strength and high ductility were obtained by SPS consolidated GO- 
decorated Ni superalloy powder. This metallurgical approach enables 
the bulk materials with good bonding of GO reinforcement, which is 
applicable to other engineering metallic materials. The outstanding 
mechanical properties originate from good interface bonding between 
GO nanosheets and the matrix, uniformly-distributed precipitates, and 
GO induced twin boundaries. Theses specific microstructures demon -
strate an effective processing technique for next-generation high-per -
formance materials, including not only structural materials, but also 
functional materials applied in electronic components.
