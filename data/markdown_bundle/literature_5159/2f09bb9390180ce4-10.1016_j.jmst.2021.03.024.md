Super-high strength NbTaTiZr high-entropy alloys (NbTaTiZr HEAs) have been successfully fabricated by 
the mechanical alloying (MA) with spark plasma sintering (SPS) technology, which is 2-fold compared 
with that of NbTaTiZr HEAs prepared by vacuum arc melting (VAM). After the SPS process, the bulk NbTa- 
TiZr alloy samples are provided with dual-phase body-centered cubic (BCC) structure and nanoscale grain 
size about 500 nm that is obviously smaller than that of NbTaTiZr HEA fabricated by VAM. When the sin- 
tering temperature is 800 °C, the compressive fracture strength is the highest reaching at 2511 ± 78 MPa. 
When the sintering temperature is 10 0 0 °C, the fracture strain is the highest reaching at 12.8%, and com- 
pressive fracture strength and yield strength also reach at 2274 ± 91 MPa and 2172 ± 47 MPa, respec- 
tively. The excellent mechanical properties of bulk NbTaTiZr alloy samples are attributed to the merits of 
MA and SPS, and the collaboration effect of ultra-ﬁne grains strengthening, solid solution strengthening 
and interstitial solid solution strengthening.

1. Introduction 
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) proposed by Yeh since 2004 have 
been rapidly developed in the past decades and triggered wide re- 
search for the researchers [1] . According to the concept of HEAs, 
HEAs are constituted by the multi-principal elements, which is 
provided with equal or near-equal atomic percent for maximiz- 
ing the conﬁgurational entropy that is beneﬁcial to stabilizing the 
solid solution phase [ 2 , 3 ]. Because HEAs contain multi-principal el- 
ements with different atomic radii, it is tended to form complex 
arrangements and unique atomic structure. It is worth mention- 
ing that a multi-principal alloy system easily forms intermetallic 
compounds with a complex microstructure that will lead to a sig- 
niﬁcant decrease in mechanical properties [4] . Fortunately, accord- 
ing to many literature reports, many kinds of HEAs are provided 
with simple solid solution phase of body-centered cubic (BCC) or 
face-centered cubic (FCC) microstructure [5] , which emerges excel- 
lent mechanical properties. There is a generally accepted explana- 
tion that the large conﬁgurational entropy of HEAs is beneﬁt to 
stabilize the simple solution phase and simultaneously suppress 
the formation of intermetallic [ 6 , 7 ]. Therefore, HEAs are provided 
with a large number of advantages compared with traditional al-

the mechanical properties are measured at room temperature. Be- 
sides, the synthesis method of other HEA systems is frequently

Powder metallurgy (PM) as an effective candidate to fabricate 
bulk alloys has been reported in recent years [ 19 , 20 ]. Generally 
speaking, PM is provided with two procedures, which are mechan- 
ical alloying (MA) and spark plasma sintering (SPS), respectively 
[21] . As bio-HEAs, the constituent elements have been severely re- 
stricted due to the demand in terms of biocompatibility. These bio- 
HEAs are usually comprised of some refractory elements with high 
melting temperature, such as Ti, Zr, Hf, Nb, Ta, Cr, Mo etc. [22–26] . 
MA has obvious advantages in terms of synthesis refractory alloys, 
which reduces the alloying temperature and simpliﬁes the prepa- 
ration process of alloys [27] . Through MA method, we can obtain 
the solid solution powders with ultra-ﬁne grain and homogeneous 
composition [28] . SPS technology, as a rapid consolidation tech- 
nique for metals and amorphous materials, has features of rapid 
heating and cooling to synthesis bulk alloy samples with high den- 
sity. It is obvious advantage that SPS technology will not cause 
grain coarsening due to a short sintering time [ 29 , 30 ]. We have 
successfully developed bio-HEAs with excellent mechanical perfor- 
mance and good biocompatibility, which is combined MA method 
with SPS technology. 
Herein, based on the PM (MA + SPS) technology, a NbTaTiZr HEA 
with ultra-ﬁne grains and homogeneous microstructures was suc- 
cessfully obtained. The alloying process of ball-milling powders, 
microstructural characteristics after SPS at different sintering tem- 
peratures, mechanical properties (Young’s modulus, Vickers micro- 
hardness and compressive properties) at room temperature and 
electrochemical behaviors of the HEAs, Ti-6Al-4 V alloy and com- 
mercial pure Ti in Hanks solution were systematically investigated 
in this study. 
2. Materials and methods 
The raw powders including Ti, Nb, Ta, ZrH 2 with a purity of 
99.5% that produced in the Aladdin and Topmaterials are mixed 
in stainless steel vials. The composition of alloy with an equal 
atomic ratio has been designed. Particle sizes of raw powders are 
all about 45 μm ( −325 mesh), and the raw powders have been 
protected under high-purity Ar atmosphere during the mechani- 
cal alloying process. The stainless-steel balls have been utilized as 
the ball-milling media, and the weight ratio of ball and powder is 
15:1 and the milling speed of planetary ball miller (QM-3SP2, Nan- 
jing Chishun Technology Co., Ltd.) is 350 rpm. To avoid introduc- 
ing impurities, a process control agent (PCA) was not used during 
the entire mechanical alloying process. The bulk samples at differ- 
ent sintering temperatures were obtained by SPS (SPS-VI, Shanghai 
Chenhua Science and Technology Co., Ltd., China), and the sinter- 
ing temperature range is between 700 °C and 1100 °C with an axial 
loading pressure of 30 MPa under vacuum. 
XRD (Bruker D8 Advance powder X-ray diffractometer) with 2 θ
in the range of 20 °−90 ° has been utilized for the phase analysis of 
alloyed powders and bulk alloy samples. Morphology information 
and element distribution of these bulk samples are obtained by 
SEM (Zeiss SUPRA-55), TEM (JEM-3200FS, JEOL). Mechanical prop- 
erties of all bulk NbTaTiZr samples are measured using the elec- 
tronic universal material testing machine (Shimadzu, AGX-VD, 50 
KN) with an engineering strain rate of 5 × 10 −4 s − 1 under uniax- 
ial compression. Electrochemical workstation (Wuhan Corrtest In- 
struments Co., Ltd.) has been utilized to measure the corrosion be- 
havior of the bulk NbTaTiZr alloy samples at 37 °C in Hanks’ so- 
lution that is mainly constituted by NaCl and glucose as shown in

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. XRD and chemical composition analysis of the mechanically 
alloyed powder 
The XRD patterns of NbTaTiZr HEA powders for various ball- 
milling time are shown in Fig. 1 (a). When the ball milling time is 
15 h, the characteristic peaks of NbTaTiZr HEA powders are prelim- 
inarily formed. The characteristic peaks of NbTaTiZr HEA powders 
can be formed within a short time. This is due to the reduction of 
the alloying temperature in the MA process [27] . Simultaneously, 
the diffraction peak of ZrH 2 has been observed. With the increase 
of ball milling time, the diffraction peaks of ZrH 2 gradually disap- 
pear, indicating that a complete solid solution phase is obtained 
after 40 h. During the alloying process, we can observe that the 
diffraction peaks of ZrH 2 offset to 2 θ degree peaks of higher and 
the characteristic peaks of NbTaTiZr HEA powers shit to lower. Ac- 
cording to previous report [31] , selecting a certain atom as a refer- 
ence, the atoms with smaller atomic radius will make the 2 θ de- 
gree peaks of reference atoms shift to higher in the alloying pro- 
cess and the incorporation of atoms with smaller atomic radius 
will make the 2 θ degree peaks of reference atoms shift to lower. 
As the ball milling time at 40 h, only the characteristic peaks of 
NbTaTiZr HEA powers can be observed, which contain (110), (200), 
(211), (220) facets. Therefore, we can deduce that the ﬁnal NbTa- 
TiZr HEA powders are completely deformed into a simple solid 
solution with a BCC structure [ 32 , 33 ]. In addition, accompanied 
with the prolonging of ball milling time, the intensity of diffrac- 
tion peaks is gradually decremented and the diffraction peaks are 
gradually broadening. This phenomenon is mainly due to the for- 
mation of reﬁned grain size and high lattice strain. Moreover, the 
production of high lattice strain is caused by the severe plastic 
deformation, which is induced by the size mismatch effect, addi- 
tion grain boundary fraction and addition dislocation density [18] . 
According to the Debye-Scherrer formula, the grain sizes of alloy 
powders with identical interval milling time have been calculated 
utilizing the X-ray peaks of the BCC phase. The calculated results 
are shown in Fig. 1 (b). Accompanied with the interval prolonged 
of ball milling time, the grain size is overall showing a decreas- 
ing trend. As the alloyed powders become hardening due to the 
nano-scale grain size and severe lattice strain, the alloy powder at 
this time becomes facilitate fracture and can be reﬁned in a short 
time [19] . When the ball milling time is 40 h, the grain size of BCC 
phase is approximate 2.90 nm, which is slightly decreased com- 
pared with the ball milling time of 35 h (3.05 nm). It is indicated 
that the grain reﬁnement and cold welding of the BCC phase have 
reached a dynamic balance when the ball milling time arrives to 
40 h. After the time of achieving dynamic balance, the grain size 
of the BCC phase cannot be signiﬁcantly changed to achieve fur- 
ther reﬁnement by increasing the ball milling time [34] . The mor- 
phology and size distribution of alloy powders at ball milling time 
of 40 h are shown in Fig. 1 (c). The alloy powders at ball milling

3.2. Phase and microstructural analysis of bulk HEAs 
The XRD patterns of the bulk NbTaTiZr HEAs with different sin- 
tering temperatures are shown in Fig. 2 (a). Through the SPS pro- 
cess, the diffraction peaks of the bulk alloy samples become in- 
tense and sharp compared with initial alloy powders due to the 
grain coarsening and stress releasing [36] . Simultaneously, all the 
bulk NbTaTiZr HEA samples show obvious dual-phase BCC struc- 
ture that named as BCC1 and BCC2 phase, as shown in Fig. 2 (b). 
The lattice parameter of the sintering temperature at 10 0 0 °C is 
calculated and the result is 3.356 ˚A, which is similar to 3.364 ˚A 
calculated by Vegard’s law. 
Back scattered-electron (BSE) images of the bulk NbTaTiZr HEAs 
sintered at different temperatures are shown in Fig. 3 . As we all 
know, light elements like Ti and Zr are concentrated in the black 
area in the BSE mode, while the distribution of heavy elements 
like Ta and Nb is the opposite. The BSE images of all bulk alloys 
contain two different contrast, which is dark and bright, respec- 
tively. Furthermore, the values of /Delta1H ij for the elemental pairs Ti- 
Nb ( + 2), Ti-Ta ( + 1), Nb-Zr ( + 4), and Ta-Zr ( + 3) are positive, and 
it can be seen from the binary phase diagram feature of Ti, Nb, Ta 
and Zr that there are miscibility gaps [ 3 , 12 ]. Therefore, it can be 
concluded that the bulk alloys have a dual-phase structure, which 
is composed of a Ti (Zr)-rich phase and a Ta (Nb)-rich phase. All 
bulk alloys have nano-scale grain size that is beneﬁted from the 
obtaining of nanocrystalline alloy powders by MA and the rapid 
consolidation of SPS technology in a short time, and the grain size 
of bulk alloys is gradually coarsened with the increase of the sin- 
tering temperature, as shown in Fig. 3 (b-f). When the sintering 
temperature is 700 °C, there are visible microporous that are ob- 
served in the Fig. 3 (a). The appearance of micropores is mainly due 
to the low sintering temperature which leads to insuﬃcient sinter- 
ing, which will reduce the mechanical properties and density of 
the bulk alloy [37] . 
When the sintering temperature is 10 0 0 °C, the scanning trans- 
mission electron microscopy (STEM), FESEM and corresponding 
EDS elemental mapping are adopted to further discuss the element 
distribution and content of constituent elements in different con- 
trast regions. There are obviously different Z contrasts of BCC1 and 
BCC2 phase, as shown in Fig. 4 (a). According to the elemental dis- 
tribution of the constituent elements in the bulk alloy sample, the 
element distribution is obviously enriched. Ti and Zr elements are 
concentrated in the dark contrast area to form the secondary BCC2 
phase which is named as Ti (Zr)-rich phase, while Nb and Ta ele- 
ments are mainly concentrated in the bright contrast area to form 
the main BCC1 phase which is named as Ta (Nb)-rich phase, as 
shown in Fig. 4 (b, c, e, f). Simultaneously, the quantity of differ- 
ent Z contrasts region is analyzed by the FESEM and correspond- 
ing EDS elemental mapping. The results in Fig. 4 (d) further conﬁrm 
that the dark contrast area is due to the element enrichment of Ti, 
Zr, with the rough of 28.87 at.% Ti and 30.82 at.% Zr, and the light 
contrast area is due to the element enrichment of Nb, Ta, with the 
rough of 29.44 at.% Nb and 27.78 at.% Ta, which is coincided with 
the results of BSE and EDS mapping. 
Bright-ﬁeld TEM image and its partially enlarged image of 
the NbTaTiZr HEA sintered temperature at 10 0 0 °C are shown in 
Fig. 5 (a, b), respectively. According to this result, different con- 
trasts are shown in the bright-ﬁeld TEM images, indicating that 
the NbTaTiZr HEA matrix consists of different phases including Ta 
(Nb)-rich and Ti (Zr)-rich phases. There are ultra-ﬁne grain sizes 
between 50 nm and 500 nm, which is smaller than that of NbTa- 
TiZr HEAs fabricated by VAM that can be obtained in previous lit- 
erature [16] . Fig. 5 (c, d) shows the selected area electron diffrac- 
tion (SAED) patterns of the Ta (Nb)-rich region and Ti (Zr)-rich re- 
gion, respectively. Combined with the corresponding XRD pattern, 
these SAED patterns manifest that the Ti (Zr)-rich and Ta (Nb)-rich 
phases together form a solid solution phase of the NbTaTiZr HEA 
[ 21 , 38 ]. The lattice parameter of the (110) facet for Ta (Nb)-rich 
phase is smaller than that of Ti (Zr)-rich phase due to the larger 
atom radius of Ti, Zr than Nb, Ta, and calculated to be 2.450 ˚A and 
3.496 ˚A, respectively [39] .

Excellent mechanical performances of NbTaTiZr alloy system 
synthesized by PM are attributed to strengthening mechanisms 
that contain the solid solution strengthening [48] , grain boundary 
strengthening and interstitial solid solution strengthening [ 49 , 50 ]. 
Solid solution strengthening that can be predicted by Labush’ 
model endows with the higher strength for HEA compared with 
single-element-based alloys [ 51 , 52 ], which is beneﬁted from solid 
solution strengthening effect that is attributed to the solute atoms 
enriched around dislocations and hampering transfer of disloca- 
tions [52] . Grain boundary strengthening with reducing grain size 
has obvious effect to improve the compressive strength and hard- 
ness. The smaller the grain size is, the higher the grain boundary 
density will be. Ultra-ﬁne grain size can effectively hinder dislo- 
cation slip to vicinity grains and improve the strength of HEAs 
[ 53 , 54 ]. According to the classical Hall-Petch formula ( δs = δi + 
k y d −1/2 ), the inﬂuencing factors of yield strength mainly include 
the total resistance of dislocations, Hall-Petch coeﬃcient and av- 
erage grain size. The Hall-Petch coeﬃcient of the BCC phase is 
the largest compared with FCC and HCP phase. Therefore, the 
ﬁne grain strengthening effect for the BCC phase is more effec- 
tive than FCC and HCP phases. In this study, the grain size of all 
alloy samples is provided with nanoscale size (~500 nm), which is 
ﬁner than that of alloy prepared by VAM. Interstitial solid solution 
strengthening may contribute to the excellent mechanical proper- 
ties, which is due to the introduction of the hydrogen atom from 
the raw powder ZrH 2 . As a solid solution, HEAs constituted by 
multi-principal elements with different atomic radii are provided 
with severe lattice distortion. Hydrogen as interstitial atoms is in- 
troduced into the matrix, which further increases the lattice dis- 
tortion due to its smaller atomic radius than other constituent ele- 
ments. Based on the factor, the lattice distortion caused by hydro- 
gen atoms can not only prevent the movement of dislocations but 
also improve the mechanical properties of the alloy [ 41 , 55 ]. There- 
fore, the bulk alloy samples are provided with excellent mechani- 
cal properties, which is beneﬁted from the collaboration effect of 
above three strengthening mechanisms.

4. Conclusions 
After a ball milling process of 40 h, the NbTaTiZr HEA alloyed 
powders have been obtained, which is provided with nanocrys- 
talline. Combined with SPS technology, the bulk NbTaTiZr HEAs 
are successfully fabricated at different sintering temperatures. The 
microstructures of bulk NbTaTiZr alloy have been characterized, 
which indicates that it has a nanoscale grain size (~ 500 nm) 
and a dual-phase BCC structure (Ti, Zr-rich and Nb, Ta-rich). These 
nanoscale grain size and homogeneous microstructures are at- 
tributed to the advantages of MA and SPS. 
Beneﬁting from the advantages of the mechanical alloying (MA) 
with spark plasma sintering (SPS) technology and the collabo- 
ration effect of the solid solution strengthening, grain boundary 
strengthening and interstitial solid solution strengthening, the bulk 
NbTaTiZr alloy samples exhibit excellent mechanical properties. 
When the sintering temperature is 800 °C, the compressive frac- 
ture strength is the highest reaching at 2511 ± 78 MPa, and there 
is no plasticity. When the sintering temperature is 10 0 0 °C, the 
fracture strain is the highest reaching at 12.8%, and the compres- 
sive fracture strength and yield strength are 2274 ± 91 MPa and 
2172 ± 47 MPa, respectively. The yield strength of bulk NbTaTiZr 
sample sintered at 10 0 0 °C is more than 2-fold compared with 
the NbTaTiZr HEA prepared by VAM. At same time, this sample 
exhibits excellent comprehensive electrochemical performance in 
Hanks’ solution at 37 °C.
