abstract
A eutectic Fe-15 wt.% Ti alloy was found to consist of a ferrite phase and ultra ﬁne Fe 2Ti lamellar phase.
The ultra ﬁne lamellar structure was discovered to be thermally stable at temperatures up to 600 /C14 C.
Furthermore, the alloy also exhibited a very high yield strength (780 MPa) at this temperature so that it
can be considered as a potential engineering alloy for structural applications at this temperature regime.

1. Introduction
Eutectic alloys present a two phases microstructure, which can
be arranged microscopically with different patterns such as ﬁne
lamellar or rod-like structures[1e4]. For instance, lamellar structure
has been found in a eutectic Al eCu system (CuAl2 phase embedded
in Al matrix) while rod-like structure was discovered in a eutectic
AleFe system (Al 6Fe phase embedded in Al matrix) [5]. Recently,
ultraﬁne eutectic alloys have attracted tremendous research atten-
tions as they can provide ultrahigh strength and enhanced ductility
[6e13]. For instance, Schlieter et al. [6] reported that an ultra ﬁne
eutectic Ti eFe alloy exhibited a compressive strength of 2200 e
2700 MPa and a plastic strain of 7 e19% in compression. Das et al.
[7] found that another ultra ﬁne eutectic Ti eFe alloy possessed
a strength of 1900 MPa and a plastic strain of around 13%. Louzguine
and Kato [10] reported an ultra ﬁne Ti eFe alloy exhibited
a compressive strength of 2220 MPa and a compressive strain of
6.7%. Besides eutectic alloys, the ultra ﬁne lamellar structure can be
also found in eutectoid alloys. One of the most well known eutectoid
alloys is pearlite. The ﬁne structure formed by the thin ﬁlm of ferrite
and Fe3C lamellas provides the pearlite very high yield stress at room
temperature. This type of lamellar microstructure can be interpreted
as a micro-composite [14]. It was showed by Embury and Fisher [15]
that the length scale that controls the mechanical properties of
lamellar structure is the inter-lamella spacing. Smaller lamellar
spacing leads to higher yield stress.
Besides their applications at ambient temperatures, some
special ultra ﬁne lamellar structures, which are thermally stable,
can also ﬁnd their applications at intermediate and high tempera-
tures. For instance, TieAl alloys with an ultra ﬁne lamellar structure
have been successfully used to produce low-pressure turbine
blades of aero-engines, which clearly demonstrates the potentials
of lamellar intermetallic compounds for intermediate and high
temperature applications [16,17]. In general, Ti eAl alloy possesses
both Ti3Al (a2) and TiAl (g) phases, where g phase is the matrix and
a2 phase forms the lamellar structure [18,19]. The ultraﬁne lamellar
interspacing is smaller than 1 mm and the thickness of the a2 phase
is about 100 nm [20,21].
The present paper aims at exploring other potential lamellar
intermetallic systems for potential applications at intermediate and
high temperature applications. A Fe-based eutectic FeeTi alloy with
a lamellar structure was selected as the ﬁrst model material. The
microstructure and mechanical properties at different tempera-
tures of this eutectic alloy were investigated.

2. Experiments
A 2.64 kg ingot of binary Fe-15 wt.% Ti alloy was prepared under
vacuum in an induction furnace. The alloy was melted in an alumina
crucible and casted in a steel mould followed by air cooling. The ingot
had a size of 140/C2 70 /C2 28 mm. This eutectic composition is selected
according to the binary Fe eTi phase diagram shown in Fig. 1 [22] .
This eutectic composition is expected to provide a mixture of a ferrite
phase (Body Centred Cubic, BCC) and a Fe 2Ti phase (Hexagonal
Close-Packed, HCP) during slow solidi ﬁcation. Characterizations of
the microstructure and the chemical composition of the as-cast alloy
were performed by X-Ray Diffraction (XRD), Electron Back Scattered
Diffraction (EBSD) and Energy Dispersive X-Ray Spectrometer (EDS).
These experimental techniques were used in order to con ﬁrm the
presence of the Fe2Ti phase after casting. The EBSD technique allows
the accurate determination of the size of the lamellar structure. In
order to study the thermal stability of the microstructure, samples
with a dimension of 50 mm/C2 60 mm /C2 80 mm are heat treated in an
air furnace at 3 different temperatures (500, 600 and 700 /C14 C) for
various times (0.5, 1, 5 and 24 h) followed by water quenching. The
samples are then subjected to a long-time polishing to remove the
surface layer which was oxidized during the heat treatment. A
surface layer with a 2 mm thickness was removed by such polishing.
Then, all samples were subjected to normal polishing procedures
with a 1 mm diamond pastes ﬁnishing step, followed by Vickers
hardness measurement. The yield stresses of as-cast alloy at
different temperatures were measured by compression tests using
a Gleeble3500 machine at a quasi-static strain rate. For the
compression tests, the samples were heated from room temperature
to the desired testing temperature within 1 min and hold at such
temperature for 5 min prior to the compression tests. The
compression sample has a diameter of 10 mm and a length of 12 mm.
3. Results and discussion
XRD results on the as-cast alloy are shown in Fig. 2 . The
indexation of the different peaks in Fig. 2 conﬁrms the presence of
BCC ferrite and HCP Fe
2Ti, which agrees well with the prediction by
the phase diagram shown in Fig. 1.
Fig. 3 presents the EBSD maps of the as-cast alloy. The micro-
structure consists of a ferrite matrix (in blue) and the lamellar
eutectic Fe2Ti phase (in red). The mean thickness of these lamellas
is 0.6 mm. The lamellar interspacing is also below 1 mm. Such
ultraﬁne lamellar structure is a typical microstructure of eutectic
alloys [5]. The EDS analysis for the as-cast alloy is presented in
Fig. 4 . The results support the previous observation regarding the
presence of the intermetallic Fe 2Ti phase. As it is observed on the
maps, regions where no Ti is observed are rich in Fe, while regions
rich in Ti also contain Fe.

As a comparison, steels for intermediate temperature applica-
tions such as Oxide Dispersion Strengthened (ODS) steels exhibit
a yield stress of 400 MPa at 600 /C14 C [25], while the current as-cast
FeeTi alloy possesses a yield stress of 780 MPa at the same
temperature. Furthermore, it is thermally stable at this temperature
as demonstrated in Fig. 5 . Therefore, the current eutectic Fe eTi
alloy may be considered as a potential candidate for applications
at such temperature regime. At higher temperature, the current
eutectic alloy may not be a good candidate as its yield stress drops
rapidly at temperatures above 600 /C14 C.
4. Summary
The present paper characterized the microstructure and
mechanical properties of the eutectic Fe-15 wt.% alloy. The alloy
presents an ultra ﬁne lamellar structure consisting of BCC ferrite
matrix and lamellar HCP Fe 2Ti intermetallic phase. Both inter-
lamella spacing and Fe 2Ti lamella thickness are found to be
smaller than 1 mm. Its high yield stress as well as good thermal
stability at 600 /C14 C make it being a potential candidate for structural
applications at such intermediate temperature regime.
