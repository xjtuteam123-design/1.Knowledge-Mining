ABSTRACT
Enhancing the toughness of TiAl alloys remains a critical problem urgently requiring resolution. This study 
demonstrates that the incorporation of 5 vol% ductile Nb particles into the Ti-45Al-8Nb matrix via the spark 
plasma sintering (SPS) technique simultaneously enhances strength and toughness. The microstructure evolution 
at the interface between the Nb particles and the matrix was investigated, while the micro-mechanical properties 
of the interface phases and the toughening mechanisms were analyzed. The results indicate that a multi-layer 
interface structure is formed between Nb particles and TiAl powders through atomic diffusion reactions 
involving Nbr/ σ / σ + B2/B2 + γ /matrix zones. The (B2 + γ ) reaction layer achieves a gradient transformation of the 
elastic modulus between the residual Nb particles and the matrix (165 GPa → 150 GPa → 147 GPa → 140 GPa), 
thereby alleviating the deformation inhomogeneity at the interface. The Nb/TiAl composite prepared at 1250 
◦
C 
exhibits excellent room-temperature compressive strength (2417 MPa) and fracture toughness (16.85 MPa m
1/2

representing 14.8 % and 13.1 % enhancements compared to pure Ti-45Al-8Nb alloy. These enhancements pri -
marily originate from plastic tearing, interfacial debonding, and bridging of the residual Nb particles. The tough 
B2 and γ phases in the Nb-rich region improve toughness by reducing fracture energy through crack deflection 
and branching.
1. Introduction
With the development of advanced aerospace equipment aiming for 
lightweight and high strength, TiAl alloys have attracted considerable 
attention due to their low density, high specific strength, excellent high- 
temperature strength, and outstanding creep resistance [ 1 – 3 ]. TiAl al -
loys are employed to manufacture engine components like low-pressure 
turbine blades and exhaust valves, which enhance the fuel efficiency of 
turbine engines by reducing weight [ 4 , 5 ]. Nevertheless, disadvantages 
such as brittleness and low toughness at room temperature limit their 
widespread application [ 6 – 8 ]. Hence, improving the toughness of TiAl 
alloys is a critical problem that deserves to be solved.
Studies demonstrate that alloying TiAl alloys with W, Mn, and Nb 
elements improves their high-temperature strength. However, the 
improvement in room-temperature plasticity and toughness remains 
limited [ 9 – 11 ]. The introduction of ductile reinforcements into the TiAl 
matrix has been shown to concurrently improve room-temperature 
plasticity, toughness, and high-temperature strength [ 12 – 14 ]. Owing 
to their high toughness and deformation characteristics, ductile metals 
can effectively enhance the toughness of TiAl alloys. Current research on 
ductile metal-reinforced TiAl matrix composites is primarily categorized 
into two types: metal particle-reinforced and metal fiber-reinforced 
systems [ 15 – 17 ]. Zhou [ 18 ] et al. prepared Ta fiber-reinforced TiAl 
composites via vacuum hot-pressing technology, significantly strength -
ening and toughening the TiAl alloy. The main toughening mechanisms 
included plastic deformation of Ta fibers and debonding at weakly 
bonded interfaces. Li [ 19 ] et al. realized the microstructure modification 
of TiNb fibers by aging treatment, resulting in a significant increase in 
the tensile strength and toughness of TiNb/TiAl composites. Generally, 
Metal particles present a more viable option as toughening agents for 
TiAl alloys compared to metal fibers, which require high preparation 
costs and complex composite processes.

elastic modulus and nano hardness of the interfacial reaction products. 
Additionally, room-temperature compression and three-point bending 
tests were carried out on the Nb/TiAl composites to evaluate their me -
chanical responses, and the strengthening and toughening mechanisms 
of Nb particles were discussed. This work provides experimental insights 
and a theoretical framework for the toughening design of TiAl alloys.

3.2. Mechanical properties of Nb/TiAl composites

mechanical properties of composites. Nanoindentation testing is used to 
characterize the micromechanical properties of the Nb/TiAl composite 
interface phases, including elastic modulus and hardness, as presented 
in Fig. 7 . Fig. 7 (a) shows the indentation morphology of each phase. The

observed. In addition, the hard B2 and γ phases in the Nb-rich regions

characteristics such as interface debonding, Nbr particle bridging, crack 
branching, and deflection can halt crack initiation and propagation by 
consuming the fracture energy at the crack tip [ 33 , 34 ]. Consequently, all 
these mechanisms contribute to improving the fracture strength and 
toughness of Nb/TiAl composites.
