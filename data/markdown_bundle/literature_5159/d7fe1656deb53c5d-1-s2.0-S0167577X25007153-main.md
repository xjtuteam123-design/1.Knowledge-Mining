ABSTRACT
GH4099 superalloys with typical dendritic/cellular structures were manufactured and studied the microstructure 
evolution after hot isostatic pressing (HIP) and heat treatment (HT) using laser-powder bed fusion (L-PBF) and 
laser-directed energy deposition (L-DED), respectively. The results indicate that due to grain size and distribution 
differences, L-PBF alloy has higher strength and L-DED alloy has better plasticity at room temperature. In 
contrast, at high temperatures, the strengthening of grain boundaries is weakened, and the strength of the two is 
similar.
1. Introduction
Additive manufacturing (AM) of Ni-based superalloys has rapid so -
lidification rates, which may result in the formation of specific micro -

microstructures limit the performance, effectively improving mechani -

fusion (L-PBF) and directed energy deposition (L-DED), which use 
powder spreading and powder feeding methods, respectively. L-PBF and 
L-DED have excellent application prospects as AM technologies, with the

Fig. 3 a shows the tensile curves and mechanical properties of two 
alloys at RT, with L-PBF alloy exhibiting high strength compared to L- 
DED alloy. At RT, grain boundaries make an excellent contribution to 
strength by hindering the movement of dislocations. Grain refinement 
strengthening can effectively enhance strength, as explained by the Hall- 
Petch formula [ 11 ]. Grain size determines the mechanical properties 
between two alloys. Compared with L-DED alloy, L-PBF alloy has finer 
grains, resulting in higher strength. This inevitably leads to low plas -
ticity. Fig. 3 b shows the comparison and mechanical properties distri -
bution range of traditional, L-DED, and L-PBF GH4099 alloy at RT 
[ 6 , 12 – 14 ], It is evident that alloys formed by AM exhibit superior me -
chanical properties. GH4099 superalloy undergoes plastic deformation 
by activating stacking faults and dislocations to reduce strain concen -
tration. For AM alloy, the high-density dislocation attracts M elements 
segregation, which leads to the generation of intragranular carbides and 
twin nucleation after heat process. Compared with traditional GH4099 
superalloy, The AM alloy has obtained additional intragranular carbide 
pinning and twinning boundary hindrance, thus possessing higher me -
chanical properties. Fig. 3 c shows the tensile curves and mechanical

at high temperatures is mainly based on intragranular precipitation 
phase dispersion and the pinning of intergranular carbides to hinder the 
initiation and migration of dislocations. Due to the softening of precip -
itated phases, the mechanisms of dislocation migration, bypassing and 
cutting, occur simultaneously. Twinning and stacking faults can release 
energy during the tensile process [ 9 ]. Although the grain size distribu -
tion and characteristics of the two alloys are different, and the L-PBF 
alloy has fine grains with full twinning, while the L-DED alloy has 
bimodal grain distribution and fine grains twinning. Grain boundaries 
will become weak links, and cracks will initiate and propagate along the 
grain boundaries. The strengthening of the precipitated phase inside the 
crystal weakens, while the Intragranular carbides provide higher 
strength. The difference in plasticity depends on the grain size, so stress 
will concentrate and crack at the grain boundaries, ultimately leading to 
failure.
4. Conclusions
In summary, GH4099 superalloy with excellent performance was 
prepared by L-PBF and L-DED. Compared with traditional processes, 
Laser-AM alloys exhibit increased intragranular carbides after heat 
treatment, rapid condensation leading to element segregation and 
dislocation entanglement, as well as residual stress induced twin 
nucleation, effectively improving mechanical properties. The larger 
coarse grain size of L-DED alloy limits its strength (100 MPa lower than 
L-PBF alloy). At high temperatures, the grain boundaries will weaken 
the difference in strength, but the plasticity of L-DED alloy is low (about 
half of L-PBF alloy), which is equivalent to limiting the further appli -
cation of this technology. This study will provide a feasible route and 
theoretical guidance for the microstructure and mechanical properties 
of AM Ni-based superalloys.
