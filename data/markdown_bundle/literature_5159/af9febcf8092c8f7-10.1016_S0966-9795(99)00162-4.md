Microstructure and mechanical behavior of the DS Ni–Al–Hf alloy
Z.P. Xing *, Y.F. Han, M.G. Yan
Institute of Aeronautical Materials, Beijing 100095, PR China
Abstract
The Ni–30 at% Al–1 at% Hf alloy was prepared by a modiﬁed Bridgman method. The microstructure and mechanical properties
of the alloy were studied. The results show that directional solidiﬁcation (DS) of the alloy at 4 mm/min resulted in a two-phase
(b+g0) alloy which consisted of a single crystal b matrix and g0 (Ni3Al) reinforcements. Homogenization at 1280C/6 h followed by
air cooling or oil quenching resulted in the b-NiAl phases in the alloy mostly transformed to NiAl martensite (M-NiAl). Further-
more, the ambient temperature fracture toughness of the alloy was over 20 MPa mp in as-solidiﬁed state. With the solid solution
strengthening of hafnium and second /C130 phase strengthening, the high temperature strength of the alloy was also improved sig-
niﬁcantly. Annealing at 1280C for 6 h did not inﬂuence obviously the high temperature strength of the alloy, but decreased its
ambient fracture toughness signiﬁcantly when the cooling rate was fast enough. The microstructure/mechanical property relation-
ships were discussed. # 2000 Elsevier Science Ltd. All rights reserved.

1. Introduction
The B2 type intermetallic compound NiAl is being
considered as a potential alternative to superalloys for
advanced engines. This is because B2-NiAl has a high
melting point (1638
C), low density (5.90 g/cm 3), a high
Young’s modulus (240 GPa) and excellent oxidation
resistance at high temperature. Additionally, the ther-
mal conductivity of NiAl is 4–8 times greater than that
of Ni-base superalloys [1]. But it has not been widely
used due to its limited low temperature ductility and
toughness and poor elevated temperature strength.
Although creep strength has successfully been improved
by precipitation strengthening [2,3] or particulate com-
posite [4–7], it is necessary to simultaneously improve
toughness and creep resistance of this material to make
it competitive with currently used superalloys. One
approach to achieve these goals would be to move out
of a single-phase ﬁeld into a multiphase ﬁeld [8–10].
Noebe et al. [11] directionally solidiﬁed Ni–30 at% Al
two-phase ( b+g
0) alloy and obtained up to 8% elonga-
tion when the ductile phase was continuous. In addi-
tion, substantial compressive strength rising with
temperature to 600
C of this alloy was obtained. How-
ever, very little information is available on the room-
temperature fracture toughness of such alloy. The
behavior of the alloying additions in NiAl two-phase
alloy has not been reported.
In this paper, Ni–30Al–1Hf alloy was prepared by a
modiﬁed Bridgman method. Its mechanical properties and
microstructure in several conditions were investigated.
2. Experimental procedures
The Ni–30Al–1Hf ingots ( 25 mm in diameter and

crosshead speed was 0.3 mm/min. The tensile specimens
with gauge dimensions of 525 mm were machined
from the ingots and tested in uniaxial tension at high
