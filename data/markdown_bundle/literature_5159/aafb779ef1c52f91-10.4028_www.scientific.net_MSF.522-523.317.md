Abstract. The 4th and advanced generation Ni-base single crystal superalloys, which contain large 
amounts of refractory metals for strengthening and platinum group metals, e.g., Ru, for TCP-phase 
prevention, show excellent high-temperature strengths. However, these alloying elements seem to 
decrease high-temperature oxidation resistance. In this study, Ni-base superalloys with various 
amounts of Ta, Re and Ru were examined in isothermal and cyclic exposures at 1373K to investigate 
the effect on the oxide growth rate and resistance to scale spallation. Structures of the oxide for the 
alloys were analyzed by XRD, SEM and EDX, and the oxidation kinetics is discussed. Ru and Re 
were found to degrade the oxidation resistance by the vaporization of their oxide. Ta-rich oxide in the 
spinel layer affects to stabilize ruthenium and rhenium oxide in the scale and improve the oxidation 
resistance of Ru-containing Ni-base superalloys. 
Introduction 
Ni-base superalloys have excellent high-temperature strength and oxidation resistance and 
improvements in its properties are expected to lead to the increase of the output power and efficiency 
of jet engines and gas turbines. Additions of solid solution strengthening elements, such as Re, W and 
Mo, to Ni-base superalloys lead to the precipitation of Topologically Close Packed (TCP) phases and, 
consequently, the decrease in the high-temperature strength 
 [1, 2]. 4th generation Ni-base superalloys 
contain 2 mass% Ru, which improves the stability of microstructure [3] and results in an excellent 
high-temperature strength [4-6]. On the other hand, these alloys are likely to have lower oxidation 
resistance than the previous generations of superalloys due to the nature of the alloying elements, such 
as Mo, Re and Ru. The improvement of high-temperature oxidation resistance is one of the important 
issues for practical use of these superalloys. There are some papers about the oxidation experiment of 
conventionally-cast superalloys [7-10], 1st generation single-crystal superalloys [11, 12] and 2nd 
generation single-crystal superalloys [13]. However, few studies have so far been made at the 
oxidation resistance of the latest Ni-base superalloys containing Ru. 
In our previous research, the oxidation behavior of Ru-containing Ni-base superalloys with various 
amounts of Hf was examined [14] and it has been clarified that the addition of Hf to the superalloys is 
effective to improve the oxidation resistance by affecting to the adhesiveness of the scale at the 
interface of the oxide and the substrate. In order to add a large amount of Hf to the alloy without 
degrading the mechanical properties, Ta was substituted with Hf. The volume fraction of gamma 
prime is kept constant by substituting Hf with Ta, because Hf and Ta are both a gamma prime forming 
elements. So, it is important to know a role and necessity of Ta in the oxidation property of Ni-base 
superalloy. From these points of view, the oxidation behavior of Ni-base superalloys with various

Experimental Procedure 
The 4th generation Ni-base single-crystal superalloy TMS-138, which was developed by the 
collaboration of National Institute for Materials Science and Ishikawajima-Harima Heavy Industries 
Co., Ltd. and has an excellent high-temperature strength and stability of microstructure [6], was used 
as the standard alloy. Single-crystal alloys of TMS-138 and Ta-removed TMS-138 were cast into 
single-crystal rods by using the Directional Solidification (DS) furnace. They were then homogenized 
using a standard 2-step treatment in an argon atmosphere. Alloys with various amounts of Hf, Re and 
Ru were prepared by arc melting, using the standard alloy TMS-138. The compositions of samples 
used in this study are presented in Table 1. Sulfur contents of alloys were measured by chemical 
analysis and were about 3 ppm. Each alloy was cut into specimens of 10mm in diameter and 5mm in 
height and the surface of the specimens were polished by 0.05µm Al 
2O3 particles. 
 Cyclic oxidation examination was performed in the air. Specimens in porcelain crucibles were 
exposed to 1373K for 1 h and cooled to room temperature for 1 h in one cycle. Their increases in 
weight were measured after each cycle. Isothermal oxidation examination was also carried out at 
1373K for 0.1h, 1h and 10h. Oxide scales were identified by X-ray diffraction (XRD). Cross-sections 
of the scales were observed by scanning electron microscope (SEM) and energy dispersive X-ray 
spectroscopy (EDX).

Conclusion 
Ni-base superalloys with various 
amounts of Hf, Ta, Re, Ru and Y were 
examined in cyclic and isothermal 
exposures at 1100˚C and the following 
were clarified: (1) Ruthenium and 
rhenium degrade the oxidation 
resistance. This may be caused by the vaporization of their oxide. (2) Tantalum improves the 
oxidation resistance. Tantalum oxide, which is actually in the form of nickel tantalum oxide, exists in 
the oxide layer and affects to stabilize ruthenium and rhenium oxide in the scale. For the practical use
