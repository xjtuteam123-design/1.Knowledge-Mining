ABSTRACT
This study investigates the synergistic effects of Gd microalloying (0.01 at.%) and thermomechanical processing 
(50 % cold-rolling, 1123 K annealing) on the cryogenic performance of CoCrFeNiV
0.4 
alloys. The Gd0.01CR850 
alloy exhibits superior strength (1055.5 MPa) and ductility (55.7 %) at 77 K, demonstrating a successful balance 
between strength and toughness at low temperatures.
1. Introduction
High-entropy alloys (HEAs), as a novel class of multi-principal 
element alloys, offer diverse compositional possibilities and superior 
mechanical properties [ 1 , 2 ], which have attracted extensive attention 
due to their broad application prospects across various fields [ 3 – 6 ]. 
Among numerous HEA systems, CoCrFeNi-based alloys have emerged as 
a research focus in cryogenic engineering due to the exceptional 
low-temperature stability of their face-centered cubic (FCC) structure 
[ 7 – 10 ]. Achieving a balance between strength and toughness in alloys 
has long been a goal pursued by researchers [ 11 – 13 ]. However, con -
ventional FCC-based HEAs typically exhibit high ductility but insuffi -
cient strength, limiting their practical utilization [ 14 , 15 ]. Consequently, 
achieving a synergistic enhancement of strength and plasticity at cryo -
genic temperatures through compositional design and processing opti -
mization has become a critical challenge hindering the engineering 
application of these alloys [ 16 – 19 ].
Alloying with rare earth (RE) elements provides a promising strategy 
to address the limitations of CoCrFeNi-based alloys [ 20 – 22 ]. The 
introduction of RE elements such as gadolinium (Gd) increases atomic 
size mismatch among constituent elements, inducing severe lattice 
distortion and thereby enhancing mechanical properties primarily 
through grain refinement [ 23 – 25 ]. Interfacial chemical compatibility 
[ 26 , 27 ] and stacking fault energy [ 28 , 29 ] manipulation play critical 
roles in determining the cryogenic/elevated-temperature performance 
and strength-ductility synergy of materials. Recent research [ 30 ] show 
that the addition of trace Gd enhances the strength of Gd
0.05
CoCrFeNi 
alloy while maintaining its plasticity through grain boundary strength -
ening and precipitation strengthening. Furthermore, it reduces the 
effective stacking fault energy (SFE) of the FCC matrix due to lattice 
distortion and local chemical heterogeneity. These findings suggest that 
Gd alloying holds potential for further improving cryogenic mechanical 
properties. The low-temperature performance of HEAs is closely linked 
to their multiscale microstructures, with thermomechanical processing 
being a key method to control grain morphology, dislocation structures, 
and precipitate distribution [ 31 , 32 ]. For CoCrFeNi-based alloys, the 
coarse columnar grains in the as-cast state often lead to anisotropic 
behavior and low-temperature embrittlement. Cold rolling refines the 
grains by introducing high dislocation density and shear band forma -
tion, while subsequent annealing optimizes recrystallization kinetics 
and elemental segregation, thus improving the strength-ductility bal -
ance [ 33 – 35 ].
Despite the growing interest in HEAs for cryogenic applications, 
research on the effects of rare-earth microalloying, particularly with Gd, 
on the low-temperature performance of CoCrFeNi-based HEAs remains 
limited. While numerous studies have focused on the mechanical 
properties of HEAs in various temperature ranges, the specific combi -
nation of Gd microalloying with thermomechanical processing to

argon atmosphere, with the melt flipped multiple times to ensure 
compositional homogeneity. The as-cast samples were homogenized at 
1423 K for 2 h, followed by cold rolling (CR) with a 50 % reduction in 
thickness (denoted as Gd0CR and Gd0.01CR). The samples were then 
annealed at 1123 K for 2 h (CR850) with air cooling (denoted as 
Gd0CR850 and Gd0.01CR850). This annealing temperature was 
selected based on preliminary experiments, which indicated a balanced 
enhancement in grain refinement, precipitate distribution, and grain 
boundary stability, leading to significant improvements in strength and 
ductility under cryogenic conditions. Therefore, this study primarily 
focuses on the CR850 samples, and results for other annealing condi -
tions are not discussed.

3. Results and discussion
Fig. 1 presents the EBSD analysis results for the as-cast, CR, and 
CR850 samples. The as-cast samples exhibit relatively large grains, 
while the cold-rolled samples show significantly elongated grains. After 
annealing at 1123 K, the grains are notably refined. In all conditions, the 
average grain size of the Gd0.01 alloy is smaller than that of the Gd0 
alloy, with the Gd0.01 alloy ’ s average grain size after heat treatment 
reduced to 6.2 μ m. Furthermore, the fraction of high-angle grain 
boundaries (HAGB) in the Gd0.01 alloy consistently exceeds that of the 
Gd0 alloy across all conditions, reaching an impressive 97.3 % after heat 
treatment. This indicates that Gd addition effectively refines the grain 
size and increases the proportion of HAGBs, which likely contributes to 
enhanced strength and ductility. These findings are significant, sug -
gesting that Gd microalloying not only refines the microstructure but 
also improves grain boundary stability, making the alloy more suitable 
for high-strength, high-ductility applications at low temperatures. This 
approach of achieving grain refinement and strengthening through 
microalloying and thermomechanical processing is consistent with 
previous studies [ 38 , 39 ]. However, our findings provide further insight 
into the specific role of Gd in enhancing these effects, particularly in 
relation to cryogenic performance.
Fig. 2 illustrates the EBSD phase maps of the Gd0.01 alloy under 
different processing conditions and presents the TEM characterization of 
the Gd0.01CR850 alloy. The phase maps (a)-(c) highlight significant 
structural evolution, particularly grain refinement and the formation of 
Gd-rich HCP precipitates at grain boundaries, which are consistently 
observed across different processing states. The Gd-rich HCP pre -
cipitates, marked by white circles in (a1), (b1), and (c1), play a critical 
role in strengthening the alloy through grain boundary pinning and 
dislocation interaction [ 40 , 41 ].

Fig. 3 (a) and (b) depict the stress – strain curves of Gd0 and Gd0.01 
alloys in their as-cast, cold-rolled, and annealed states at 200 K and 77 K. 
The as-cast samples exhibit high ductility but relatively low strength, 
consistent with the inherent characteristics of unprocessed high-entropy 
alloys. The lack of strain hardening of the cold rolled samples during 
tension caused the observed lower toughness. On the other hand, the 
annealed samples achieve an excellent balance between strength and 
toughness. Interestingly, both alloys demonstrate enhanced strength and 
toughness at lower temperatures. At cryogenic temperatures (77 K), the 
Gd0.01 alloy demonstrates significant improvements in mechanical 
properties, including yield strength, ultimate tensile strength (UTS), and 
fracture elongation, likely due to cryogenic-induced strengthening ef -
fects, as indicated by prior studies [ 44 , 45 ]. The enhanced dislocation 
density and grain refinement resulting from Gd microalloying and 
thermomechanical processing contribute significantly to the alloy ’ s 
improved toughness without sacrificing strength.
Fig. 3 (c) and (d) provide a quantitative comparison of the UTS and 
fracture elongation of the alloys under two cryogenic conditions. The 
bar charts reveal that while the UTS values of Gd0 and Gd0.01 alloys are 
nearly identical across all processing states, the Gd0.01 alloy consis -
tently demonstrates superior toughness. This trend is most notable in the 
cold-rolled state at 200 K, where the Gd0.01 alloy achieves a fracture 
elongation of 17.4 % (compared to only 6.2 % for the Gd0 alloy), despite 
their UTS values being nearly the same (1147.6 MPa vs. 1146.6 MPa). 
This highlights the unique role of Gd in enhancing the alloy ’ s defor -
mation capacity without compromising its strength.

Fig. 4 (a) shows the formation of dislocations and deformation twins 
in the Gd0 CR850 alloy after cryogenic tensile testing at 77 K. The pink 
box highlights the SAED pattern of the twins. Stacking faults are 
observed between adjacent twins ( Fig. 4 (b)), indicating local lattice 
distortion. Triple junctions, as seen in Fig. 4 (c), are regions of lattice 
discontinuity that are prone to crack nucleation, suggesting limited 
plastic compatibility and potentially reduced fracture toughness. These 
observations align with previous studies [ 47 , 48 ] that show the detri -
mental effects of such lattice discontinuities on fracture behavior.
Remarkably, the Gd0.01CR850 alloy exhibits Gd-rich HCP phases 
containing embedded nano-twins as evidenced by the corresponding 
SAED pattern (highlighted by the blue rectangle in Fig. 4 (d)). To the best 
of our knowledge, the nano-twin structures within Gd-rich precipitates 
observed in this study have rarely been reported. Nonetheless, this 
phenomenon is consistent with the twinning-dominated strengthening 
and toughening mechanisms for low-temperature performance in HEAs, 
as documented in recent studies [ 49 , 50 ]. Fig. 4 (e) shows high-density 
dislocations and extensive cross-slip configurations, while Fig. 4 (f) 
presents high-resolution imaging confirming these as Lomer-Cottrell 
(L-C) locks. These L-C locks reflect the activation of multiple slip

In addition to deformation twins ( Fig. 4(g1) ), kink twins are 
observed in Fig. 4 (g), which arise from deformation incompatibility 
between the Gd-rich phases and the matrix. This incompatibility induces 
localized lattice rotation, leading to the formation of kink twins. Non- 
planar (wavy) twin boundaries are evident in Fig. 4 (h), with the green 
box showing their SAED pattern. This behavior has also been noted in 
earlier works [ 52 , 53 ], which reinforces the relevance of our findings in 
the broader context of HEA research, particularly in relation to the in -
fluence of Gd microalloying on low-temperature mechanical properties. 
This waviness is attributed to Gd segregation at boundaries ( Fig. 2 ), 
which generates a chemical pinning effect that impedes interface pla -
narization. Dense stacking faults are also distributed around these wavy 
twin boundaries ( Fig. 4 (i)), as confirmed by the SAED pattern in the 
yellow box. These findings suggest that the synergistic interplay be -
tween twins and stacking faults effectively disperses strain energy and 
inhibits early-stage crack propagation, further enhancing the material’s 
toughness. The schematic illustrations of the cryogenic microstructures 
for Gd0CR850 and Gd0.01CR850 alloys are presented in Fig. 4 (j).
Despite the valuable insights gained from this study, some limita -
tions must be acknowledged. The investigation is based on a single Gd 
concentration (0.01 at.%) and specific thermomechanical processing 
parameters. Expanding the study to include a wider range of Gd con -
centrations and processing conditions would provide a more compre -
hensive understanding of the alloy’s performance at cryogenic 
temperatures. Furthermore, while this study addresses the alloy’s me -
chanical behavior under cryogenic conditions, the long-term stability 
and performance under realistic operational environments, such as 
thermal cycling or fatigue, remain unexplored. These factors are 
essential for assessing the practical applicability of Gd-modified HEAs in 
extreme conditions.
4. Conclusion
This study explores the synergistic effects of Gd microalloying and 
thermomechanical processing on the cryogenic mechanical properties of 
CoCrFeNiV
0.4 
HEAs. The results demonstrate that Gd significantly re -
fines the grain structure and induces the formation of Gd-rich phases, 
which enhance dislocation density and strengthen grain boundaries, 
thereby improving strength at cryogenic temperatures. Additionally, Gd 
facilitates the formation of nano-twins, which impede crack propagation 
and enhance ductility. These combined effects successfully address the 
strength-ductility trade-off typically observed at low temperatures, of -
fering a novel approach to optimizing HEA performance in extreme 
environments. These findings provide critical insights into the mecha -
nisms governing low-temperature behavior in HEAs and lay the 
groundwork for designing advanced materials for cryogenic 
applications.
