ABSTRACT 
High entropy alloy (HEA) can be used as binder phase to improve the properties of cemented carbide due to its 
high hardness, high strength and high temperature softening resistance. TiC-Fe-AlCoCrFeNi cemented carbide 
was prepared by vacuum sintering, and the effect of AlCoCrFeNi high entropy alloy content on the micro -
structure, room temperature properties, friction and wear and high temperature hardness was studied. By 
analyzing the microstructure and properties of cemented carbide, it is found that adding HEA can refine grains 
and strengthen properties. With the increase of HEA content, the room temperature hardness, fracture toughness 
and flexural strength of cemented carbide all showed a trend of first increasing and then decreasing. When the 
HEA content was 7.5% (TiC 7.5 H), the room temperature properties of cemented carbide are the best, and the 
room temperature hardness, fracture toughness and flexural strength of 1610HV, 10.7 MPa ⋅ m
1/2 
and 945 MPa 
respectively. According to the high temperature hardness test, TiC 7.5 H cemented carbide has good high 
temperature properties, the hardness at 800 ℃ is 920HV, and it maintains 57.14% of the room temperature 
hardness. Through the analysis of friction and wear experiments, it is found that cemented carbide has three wear 
forms: abrasive wear, adhesive wear and oxidation wear, and TiC 7.5 H has the lowest friction coefficient and 
wear rate and the best wear resistance. 
1. Introduction 
Cemented carbide is composed of hard phase with high hardness and 
high melting point and bonding phase with high toughness and low 
melting point [1] . Cemented carbide has excellent mechanical proper -
ties, such as high hardness, high strength and wear resistance, and has 
been widely used in cutting tools, grinding tools and wear-resistant parts 
[2 – 4] . At present, Fe, as a conventional binder of cemented carbide, can 
improve the wear resistance and fracture toughness of cemented car -
bide. It has the advantages of low cost and no toxicity, and is widely used 
in cemented carbide [5,6] . However, the wettability of Fe to hard phase 
TiC is poor, and the high temperature properties of TiC-Fe cemented 
carbide are poor, and the addition of Fe will reduce the allowable 
"carbon window", which makes it difficult to adjust the carbon balance 
of the alloy to avoid the defects in the final microstructure and affects 
the properties of cemented carbide [7 – 9] . It is necessary to find several 
ways to improve the properties of TiC-Fe cemented carbide. 
In recent years, researches [10 – 13] found that high entropy alloy 
(HEA) has a series of excellent properties, such as high hardness, high 
strength, wear resistance, excellent high temperature properties and 
thermal stability, etc. Because of its excellent properties, the properties 
of cemented carbide can be improved by adding HEA. HEA is an alloy 
composed of five or more elements, and the atomic percentage of each 
element is between 5% and 35%, and the mixed entropy is greater than 
1.5 R(R is the gas constant) [14] . There are four core effects of HEA: high 
entropy effect, delayed diffusion effect, severe lattice distortion effect 
and cocktail effect [15] . The high entropy effect means that the high 
entropy alloy system has a high mixed entropy value, which will pro -
mote the formation of simple solid solution phase and inhibit the for -
mation of intermetallic compounds, especially at high temperature [16] , 
which is one of the reasons why the HEA has excellent high temperature 
properties. Delayed diffusion effect can produce phenomena such as 
precipitation of nano-phase and formation of amorphous phase, which 
can improve hardness and toughness, and delayed diffusion effect can

In this study, TiC-Fe-AlCoCrFeNi cemented carbide was prepared by 
vacuum sintering. The effect of AlCoCrFeNi content on the microstruc -
ture, room temperature properties and high temperature hardness of 
TiC-Fe cemented carbide was studied, and the influence mechanism of 
HEA on the microstructure and mechanical properties of TiC-Fe 
cemented carbide was studied and analyzed. This study provides a di -
rection for improving the room temperature performance, especially the 
high temperature hardness of TiC-Fe cemented carbide, solves the 
problem of poor room temperature performance and high temperature 
hardness of TiC-Fe cemented carbide, and reveals the influence mech -
anism of HEA on room temperature performance and high temperature 
hardness of TiC-Fe cemented carbide. This research can promote the 
application scope of TiC-Fe cemented carbide in wear resistance field 
under high temperature.

3.2. Grain size analysis 
In order to study the influence of HEA content on the average grain 
size of cemented carbide, the average grain size was calculated by the 
grain size diagram of cemented carbide with different HEA content in 
Fig. 5 . It can be found that the average grain size of TiC-Fe-HEA de -
creases from 4.39 µ m to 3.91 µ m and then increases to 4.26 µ m with the 
addition of HEA content from 0% to 10%. When HEA content is 7.5%, 
the average grain size of TiC is the smallest, and its average grain size is 
3.91 µ m. The addition of HEA can inhibit the growth of TiC grains. The 
grain growth mechanism and the reasons why HEA inhibits grain growth 
are as follows. 
Cemented carbide sintering can be divided into four stages: dewax -
ing pre-sintering stage, solid-phase sintering stage, liquid-phase sinter -
ing stage and cooling stage. The grain growth of cemented carbide can 
be divided into two stages: aggregation recrystallization and liquid 
phase recrystallization. Firstly, agglomeration and recrystallization 
occur in the solid phase sintering stage, and TiC forms a large particle 
through diffusion and agglomeration in the solid phase sintering stage.

3.3. Room temperature properties 
Fig. 6 shows the room temperature properties of TiC-Fe-HEA 
cemented carbide: relative density, Vickers hardness, fracture tough -
ness (K
IC
) and flexural strength. It can be seen from Fig. 6 (a) that the 
relative density of TiC-Fe-HEA cemented carbide decreases from 99.59% 
to 96.43% with the increase of HEA content from 0% to 10%. The 
relative density decrease is related to porosity. Pore is the incomplete 
contact between the components in the liquid phase sintering process, 
which leads to gaps between interfaces. When the bonding phase melts 
into liquid phase, with the help of external pressure and the surface 
tension of the liquid phase itself, the sintering fluid phase flows to make 
up the gaps. However, some gaps are not completely filled, which forms 
pores [27] . The reasons for the decrease of relative density of 
TiC-Fe-HEA cemented carbide are as follows: First, the vacuum sintering 
method is adopted in this study. Under the vacuum condition, although 
the residual gas in the sintered body can be effectively removed, it is not 
conducive to the flow of binder liquid phase in the sintered body and the 
filling of gaps. Secondly, when HEA is added, the hysteresis diffusion 
effect of HEA will hinder the flow of liquid phase, which will make the 
gaps not completely filled and increase the generation of pores. More -
over, the serious lattice distortion effect and hysteresis diffusion effect of 
HEA will hinder the shrinkage of pores and affect the rearrangement 
densification process, which will increase the number of pores in 
cemented carbide. 
Fig. 6 (b) is the Vickers hardness diagram of TiC-Fe-HEA cemented 
carbide. From Fig. 6 (b), with the increase of HEA content from 0% to 
10%, the Vickers hardness of TiC-Fe-HEA cemented carbide increases 
from 1169HV to 1610 HV, and then decreases to 1425 HV. When the 
content of HEA is 7.5%, the highest hardness of cemented carbide is 
1610 HV, which is 37.72% higher than that of cemented carbide without 
HEA. The improvement of hardness of steel cemented carbide mainly 
comes from the strengthening of fine grains and the strengthening of 
bonding phase. This is because the average grain size of the cemented 
carbide decreases with the addition of HEA. When the content of HEA is 
7.5%, the average grain size of cemented carbide is the smallest 
(3.91 µ m). Due to the effect of fine grain strengthening, the decrease of 
grain size will lead to the increase of hardness. At the same time, the 
added HEA as a binder phase has high hardness itself, which will 
strengthen the binder phase, and the addition of HEA will lead to severe 
lattice distortion effect, which will enhance the effect of solid solution 
strengthening, thus strengthening the bonding phase and finally 
increasing the hardness of cemented carbide. However, when the con -
tent of HEA is more than 7.5%, the hardness of cemented carbide begins 
to decrease, because with the addition of HEA, the pores increases, 
which will reduce the hardness of cemented carbide. And when the HEA 
content is more than 7.5%, the average grain size of cemented carbide 
increases, and the effect of fine grain strengthening weakens, which also 
leads to the decrease of hardness.

yellow ellipse) and intergranular fracture (indicated by red ellipse). 
Furthermore, TiC-Fe-HEA cemented carbide has two fracture mecha -
nisms, transgranular fracture and intergranular fracture, which can 
generate greater fracture energy, thus improving mechanical properties 
[21] . 
To sum up, although the addition of HEA will reduce the relative 
density of cemented carbide, it will obviously improve the room tem -
perature properties of cemented carbide, and the room temperature 
properties of cemented carbide are the best when the content of HEA is 
7.5%. When HEA content is greater than 7.5%, the room temperature 
properties of cemented carbide are decreased. Therefore, the room 
temperature properties of cemented carbide can be improved by adding 
proper amount of HEA. 
3.4. High temperature hardness 
According to the room temperature and 800 ℃ hardness of cemented 
carbide with different HEA content in Table 4 , the change trend of 
hardness at 800 ℃ is similar to that at room temperature. With the in -
crease of HEA content from 0% to 10%, it increases from 568HV to 
920HV, and then decreases to 816HV. When HEA content is 7.5%, the 
800 ℃ hardness is the highest. Compared with the room temperature 
hardness, the 800 ℃ hardness of TiC0H, TiC 2.5 H, TiC5H, TiC 7.5 H 
and TiC10H cemented carbides decreased by 51.41%, 52.47%, 45.64%, 
42.86% and 42.74% respectively. It was found that with the addition of 
HEA, the decrease rate showed a downward trend. 
The reasons for the change of the 800 ℃ hardness of TiC-Fe-HEA 
cemented carbide with HEA content are as follows: First, the effect of 
temperature on the hardness of cemented carbide is generally related to 
its own dislocation slip [20] . Under the high temperature environment, 
when the cemented carbide is subjected to external force, firstly dislo -
cations are formed in the metal binder, then dislocations will accumu -
late at the edge of the grain boundary of TiC hard phase, and when 
accumulated to a certain extent, they will enter the TiC hard phase, and 
finally plastic deformation will occur in the stressed area of cemented 
carbide [30] . After adding HEA into TiC-Fe cemented carbide, the 
stacking fault energy of HEA is relatively low, which is relatively easy to 
form twins and relatively difficult to slip. However, there is an active slip 
system in the stressed area, and the stacking fault energy of dislocation 
in the slip system is relatively high, and HEA binder will hinder its 
sliding movement [20] . Secondly, the HEA grains are irregular and 
wrinkled. When cemented carbide slips under stress, it will hinder the 
slip. In addition, Fe binder is easy to soften at high temperature, while 
HEA has high temperature softening resistance. The addition of HEA will 
improve the overall high temperature softening resistance of TiC-Fe 
cemented carbide. Therefore, the addition of HEA will improve the 
800 ℃ hardness of TiC-Fe cemented carbide.

4. Conclusion

conclusions: 
(1) The core-rim structure appears in TiC-Fe-HEA cemented carbide,
