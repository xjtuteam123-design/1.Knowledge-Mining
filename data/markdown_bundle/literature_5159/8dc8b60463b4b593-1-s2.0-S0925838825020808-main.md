ABSTRACT
The excess addition of Ru and Re is negative to the practical engineering application of Ni-based single crystal 
(SX) superalloys, and using substitute elements that have similar roles to Ru and Re to reduce their additions is a 
potential way to balance the cost and microstructural stability. In this study, two elemental substitutions were 
designed: (1) partial substitution of Ru by Co; (2) simultaneous increase of Co content and partial substitution of 
Re by Mo, to explore the precipitation of topologically close packed (TCP) phases in Ni-based superalloys 
following heat treatment at 1100 
◦
C for 1000 h. To improve efficiency and reduce costs, a multi-component 
diffusion multiple experimental method rather than traditional single crystal or small button methods was 
employed. The results demonstrated that both elemental substitutions facilitated a transition in the micro -
structures of the studied diffusion couples from a three-phase microstructure consisting of ( γ + γ ´ + TCP) to a two- 
phase microstructure comprising ( γ + γ ´ ). Notably, despite elemental substitutions, the TCP phase remained P-type 
with substantially unaltered composition. Analyses showed that reducing the supersaturation of the γ phase and 
its lattice mismatch with the P phase, thereby decreasing the precipitation driving force while increasing 
resistance, accounts for the suppression of P phase precipitation by such elemental substitutions. Based on the 
experimental results, the potential substitution solutions were proposed with reduced Ru and Re contents, 
respectively. This study will provide valuable insights for designing and optimizing high-performance advanced 
Ni-based SX superalloys with lower Ru and Re contents.
1. Introduction
The increasing thrust-to-weight ratio and efficiency of aeroengines 
have led to high requirements for developing advanced Ni-based SX 
superalloys with higher temperature capability [1] . Re is an essential 
alloying element in the design of high-generation Ni-based SX superal -
loys due to its strong solid solution strengthening ability [2 – 4] , 
considerable melting point, as well as decreased diffusion coefficient [5, 
6] . As a result, the Re content is progressively increasing to over 6 wt% 
in the third-generation and recently produced alloys [7 – 10] . However, 
adding too much Re can raise the cost and density of alloys [11] . More 
importantly, such excessive Re addition also undermines the long-term 
microstructural stability of Ni-based SX superalloys by promoting the 
formation of brittle and harmful TCP phases [12,13] . In order to address 
the matter of weakened microstructural stability in Re-containing alloys, 
Ru has been introduced since the fourth-generation Ni-based SX 
superalloys, which can affectively suppress the precipitation of TCP 
phases [14 – 16] . Nevertheless, Ru is strategic element with limited 
availability and its high price significantly increases the cost of Ni-based 
SX superalloys [17] . Thus, it is of interest to find substitute elements that 
have similar roles to Ru and Re to reduce their additions in Ni-based SX 
superalloys.
Walston et al. [8] found that adding more Co could enhance micro -
structural stability and lessen TCP phase precipitation during the 
development of advanced Ni-based SX superalloy EPM102. Shi et al. 
[18] indicated that Co addition suppressed the formation of σ phase, 
which is one typical TCP phase. Although there are different explana -
tions for the mechanism by which Co suppresses TCP phase precip -
itation — such as decreasing the partitioning ratios of refractory alloying 
additions (especially Re) in the γ matrix [19] or enhancing diffusion 
coefficients [20] — these experimental results provide a foundational 
basis for substituting Ru by Co. On the other hand, Mo, like Re, can

The conventional studies typically employ small-button melting or 
single-crystal casting to investigate alloying effects, yet these trial-and- 
error approaches inherently struggle to achieve simultaneous micro -

cost and time. In addition, the CALPHAD method has been a valuable 
tool, but its accuracy in complex multi-component Ni-based superalloys

approach was also used in our previous studies [25] , which shows sig -

stitution of Ru by Co, and simultaneous increase of Co content combined 
with partial substitution of Re by Mo on precipitation of TCP phases in 
Ni-based superalloys. For these purposes, the high-throughput diffusion 
multiple experimental method was adopted. We carefully designed a 
multi-component diffusion multiple within a relatively broad composi -
tional space, then subjected it to heat treatment at 1100 
◦

Based on the experimental results, the reasonable elemental substitution 
solutions were determined. This study will be helpful in guiding the 
development of high-performance advanced Ni-based SX superalloys 
with reduced Re and Ru.
2. Experimental methods
2.1. Preparation of the diffusion multiple
The experimental diffusion multiple consisted of three alloys, as 
shown in Fig. 1 . The nominal compositions (wt%) of the three alloys in 
experimental diffusion multiple are listed in Table 1 . The middle alloy 
represents a Ni-based superalloy with a composition of 0Co-5Ru-1Mo- 
6.5Re-6Al-3.5Cr-6Ta-5W-Bal.Ni (wt%), named as the base alloy. In 
order to analyze the effects of substituting Ru and Re, the base alloy was 
intentionally designed to have notably elevated concentrations of Ru 
and Re, alongside reduced Mo levels and the absence of Co. The left alloy 
named as alloy 16Co3Ru was made from the composition of the base 
alloy by increasing Co content to 16.0 wt% and reducing Ru content to 
3.0 wt%. Similarly, the right alloy named as alloy 8Co4Mo3Re was also 
formed from the composition of the base alloy by adjusting Co, Mo, and 
Re contents to 8.0 wt%, 4.0 wt%, and 3.0 wt%, respectively. The 
diffusion couple formed by the base alloy and alloy 16Co3Ru was 
referred to as the Base-16Co3Ru diffusion couple which was used to 
investigate the effects of partial substitution of Ru by Co. The diffusion 
couple composed of the base alloy and 8Co4Mo3Re alloy was referred to 
as the Base-8Co4Mo3Re diffusion couple, which was used to determine 
the effects of increasing in Co content while partially substituting Re by 
Mo. A pure Ni shell was used as a cartridge and encapsulated by electron 
beam welding. The surfaces were then tightly bonded by hot isostatic 
pressing at 1200 
◦

treatment with a final temperature of 1330 
◦
C and prolonged heat 
treatment at 1100 
◦

the research zones in this work.
2.2. Characterizations and analyses of the diffusion multiple
The diffusion multiple after heat-treated was sectioned longitudi -
nally, parallel to the top and bottom of the Ni shell. The cut surfaces 
were then ground and polished following standard metallographic 
procedures. The microstructures of the Base-16Co3Ru and Base- 
8Co4Mo3Re diffusion couples were observed and gathered via a field- 
emission scanning electron microscope (ZEISS SUPRA 55) in back 
scattered electron imaging mode. The area fraction of TCP phase in the 
( γ + γ ´ + TCP) three-phase region, as well as the thickness and volume 
fraction of γ ´ phase in the ( γ + γ ´ ) two-phase region, were determined for 
the two diffusion couples in 10 μ m steps along the diffusion direction.
To correlate the composition to the local microstructure in Base- 
16Co3Ru and Base-8Co4Mo3Re diffusion couples, a JEOL JXA-8230 
electron probe microanalyzer (EPMA) was employed to identify the 
elemental concentration at different regions. To measure the average 
composition of the local zone, the EPMA ’ s beam point was configured as 
a line segment measuring 1 × 40 µ m. The each “ line ” was paralleled to 
the contact interface of the diffusion couple, and measured continuously 
with a 10 μ m step size along the diffusion direction.
The TCP phase types within the two diffusion couples were charac -
terized utilizing samples acquired via a focused ion beam (FIB) method 
with an FEI Helios Nanolab 600i instrument. Subsequently, phase 
identification occurred with the help of an FEI Talos Transmission 
Electron Microscope (TEM), which operated at 200 kV, and three sets of 
selected area electron diffraction (SAED) patterns were acquired for 
each individual sample. The composition of the TCP phases was 
analyzed through EPMA. For the γ phase composition, either EPMA or 
atom probe tomography (APT) techniques were employed based on the γ 
phase size. EPMA was used when the γ phase size was greater than 1 μ m, 
whereas APT was implemented for sizes smaller than this threshold.
CALPHAD (Calculation of Phase Diagrams) is a popular used 
computational method for predicting the phase stability and trans -
formation of materials under different conditions. In this study, the 
commercial thermodynamic software PANDAT with the Ni-based ther -
modynamic database PanNi2018 was used to compute the phase con -
stituents of the investigated two diffusion couples and the solid solution 
solubility of alloying elements in Ni at 1100 
◦
C.

3.4. Composition of γ phase
The TCP phases usually precipitate from the γ matrix in Ni-based 
superalloys. In this research, the composition of γ phase was analyzed 
with the partial substitution of Ru by Co (Base-16Co3Ru diffusion 
couple), or as the increase in Co content along with the partial substi -
tution of Re by Mo (Base-8Co4Mo3Re diffusion couple). It is noteworthy 
that the composition of γ phase in the Base-16Co3Ru diffusion couple 
was determined using APT owing to the small size of the γ phase. 
Conversely, the composition of the γ phase in the Base-8Co4Mo3Re 
diffusion couple was analyzed using EPMA as the average size of the γ 
phase exceeded 1 μ m. Fig. 5 (a) displays the composition of γ phase at 
 20 μ m and at the center of alloy 16Co3Ru in the Base-16Co3Ru 
diffusion couple. These two positions were along the direction of 
increasing Co content and decreasing Ru content. The results illustrated 
that the contents of Re, Ru, Cr, W and Al in the γ phase decreased while 
the content of Co increased with the partial substitution of Ru by Co. 
Still, there was minimal change in the contents of Mo and Ta. Similarly, 
the compositions of γ phase along the direction of increasing Co and Mo

4. Discussion
4.1. Comparison of the diffusion multiple method with the traditional 
methods
In numerous studies, the small-button melting [26,27] and 
single-crystal casting [28] methods have always played a very important 
role in investigating the impacts of alloying elements, duo to these bulk 
samples can provide key parameters beyond the microstructure, such as 
alloy density and mechanical properties. However, these traditional 
methods show their limitations with the number of elements to be 
optimized increases. On the contrary, although the diffusion multiple 
method is mainly used to obtain microstructural information (which is 
of great importance for Ni-based superalloys), it stands out in terms of 
cost control and experimental efficiency improvement, and has 
remarkable competitiveness, especially in exploring the complex inter -
action effects of elements and optimizing the alloy composition. Addi -
tionally, the diffusion couple method has also been employed to 
investigate diffusion-related properties [29] . In this research, the two 
diffusion couples enabled rapid evaluation of phase evolution across 
broad compositional gradients with elemental substitution. The 
( γ + γ ´ + P) three-phase region changed to ( γ + γ ´ ) two-phase region with the 
partial substitution of Ru by Co, or as the increase in Co content along 
with the partial substitution of Re by Mo ( Fig. 2 ). Moreover, the critical 
content of Co and Ru for forming a stable ( γ + γ ´ ) microstructure were 
found to be 6.9 wt% and 4.4 wt% respectively, in the Base-16Co3Ru 
diffusion couple. Similarly, the critical content of Co, Mo and Re for 
forming a stable ( γ + γ ´ ) microstructure were 4.9 wt%, 2.6 wt% and 
4.7 wt% respectively, in the Base-8Co4Mo3Re diffusion couple. Appar -
ently, the diffusion multiple approach presented significant advantages 
for analyzing the precipitation trends of TCP phase and identifying the 
boundary between the ( γ + γ ´ + TCP) phase region and ( γ + γ ´ ) phase region 
across a broad range of compositions with multiple elemental variations. 
It is helpful to provide guidance for the design of low Re and Ru Ni-based 
SX superalloys with constrained experiments and costs.

4.3. Effect of elemental substitution on the type and composition of TCP 
phase
The type of TCP phase is related to the composition of Ni-based SX 
superalloys. Huo et al. [30,31] demonstrated that the concentrations of 
Co, Mo and Cr markedly affected the types of TCP phases observed in 
Ru-containing SX superalloys. For example, the TCP phase precipitated 
in highly supersaturated Ni-based SX superalloys with high Co content 
(~15.0 wt%) tended toward the Co-rich R phase after prolonged ther -
mal exposure at 1100 
◦
C [30] , whereas a higher Cr/Mo ratio facilitated 
the formation of the σ phase and a lower Cr/Mo ratio encouraged the 
emergence of the P phase in highly supersaturated SX superalloys with 
low Co content (~7.0 wt%) [31] . Hobbs et al. [32,33] also found that 
adding Ru to a third-generation SX superalloy SRR300D had negligible 
effect on the types of TCP phases formed. In our research, the concen -
tration of Co in the ( γ + γ ´ + TCP) three-phase region of Base-16Co3Ru and 
Base-8Co4Mo3Re diffusion couples was always below 7.0 wt% ( Fig. 3
(a1) and (b1)). Meanwhile, Cr was about 3.5 wt%, Mo varied from 1.0 to 
2.6 wt% and Ru changed from 4.0 to 5.0 wt% ( Table 1 , Fig. 3 (a1) and 
(b1)). This composition range was characterized by low Co and low Cr 
/Mo ratio, and the TCP phase in this range was always P-type ( Fig. 4 ), 
which is broadly consistent with the findings of Huo et al. [31] . In 
summary, the partial substitution of Ru by Co, or as the increase in Co 
content along with the partial substitution of Re by Mo, did not affect the 
type of TCP phase in the investigated composition range.
Furthermore, there are different opinions about whether Ru enters 
TCP phase in the Ni-based superalloys. A study by Yeh et al. [34] on 
Ru-containing Ni-based SX superalloys showed that the P phase did not 
contain Ru. Hobbs et al. [32] quantified the P phase precipitated in alloy 
SRR300D + 3Ru and concluded that the solubility of Ru in the P phase 
was very limited. However, Shi [19] and Huo et al. [30,31] analyzed the 
composition of the TCP phases precipitated in Ru-containing Ni-based 
SX superalloys, showing the presence of Ru in the σ , P and R phases. In 
our research, the results of two diffusion couples suggested that a high 
level of Ru (up to 8.1 wt%, Table 2 ) was present in the P phase. This 
observation indicated that although Ru has been expected to have a 
suppressing impact on the TCP phases in Ni-based superalloys, it also 
entered the P phase. In addition, there was no apparent alteration in the 
composition of P phase ( Table 2 ), while the area fraction of P phase was 
decreased ( Fig. 3 (a2) and (b2)) and finally disappeared, with the partial 
substitution of Ru by Co, or the increase in Co content along with the 
partial substitution of Re by Mo. Accordingly, further investigation is 
needed to understand why the P phase gradually disappeared with the 
elemental substitution in the investigated diffusion couples.

4.5. Elemental substitution solutions of Ru and Re
The current experimental results of the Base-16Co3Ru and Base- 
8Co4Mo3Re diffusion couples indicated that the elemental substitu -
tion could obtain a stable ( γ + γ ´ ) microstructure ( Fig. 2 ). Therefore, the 
reasonable elemental substitution solutions with lowered Ru and Re can 
be identified from these two diffusion couples.
Besides the precipitation of the TCP phase, the microstructural sta -
bility of Ni-based SX superalloys also depends on the stability of the γ ´
phase. The volume fraction of γ ´ phase is closely related to the high- 
temperature mechanical performance of superalloys [42] . Yokokawa 
et al. [43] suggested that for optimal creep properties of Ni-based SX 
superalloys under high-temperature, low-stress creep conditions, the γ ´
volume fraction at 1100 
◦
C should be approximately 60 %. Furthermore, 
the coarsening of γ ´ phase is also responsible for the properties degra -
dation during long-term service at high temperatures [44 – 46] . The cur -
rent study on the stability of γ ´ phase in the ( γ + γ ´ ) two-phase region of the 
Base-16Co3Ru diffusion couple revealed that there was minimal change 
in the coarsening of γ ´ phase, while the volume fraction of the γ ´ phase 
decreased from when Ru was partially substituted by Co ( Fig. 3 (a3)). It is 
to be noted that the γ ´ volume fraction varied roughly from 68 % to 57 %, 
which is in the higher range and therefore will not become an over -
whelming factor affecting the mechanical properties. Therefore, it is 
crucial to balance the volume fraction of the γ ´ phase and the amount of 
Ru substitution by Co in the Base-16Co3Ru diffusion couple. Finally, the 
average composition at 40 ～ 50 μ m in the Base-16Co3Ru diffusion 
couple was derived as the optimal alloying solution S1 for partial sub -
stitution of Ru by Co, i.e., 10.2Co-3.6Ru- 1.0Mo-6.5R -
e-5.9Al-4.6W-4.0Cr-6.0Ta-Bal. Ni (wt%). This alloy exhibited a 60 % γ ´
volume fraction and reduced Ru content by 1.4 wt% in comparison to 
the base alloy. Similarly, the results of the Base-8Co4Mo3Re diffusion 
couples indicated that the γ ´ volume fraction did not change much with 
increasing Co and substituting Re by Mo ( Fig. 3 (b3)). However, there 
was an increase in the coarsening of γ ´ phase, indicating a decrease in its 
stability ( Fig. 3 (b3)). As a result, the critical composition at the phase 
region boundary (at position 100 μ m) was selected as the optimal 
alloying solution S2 for the partial substitution of Re by Mo, i.e. 
4.9Co- 4.9Ru -2.6Mo-4.7Re -5.9Al-4.6W-4.0Cr-6.0Ta-Bal. Ni (wt%). This 
alloy reduced Re content by 1.8 wt% in comparison to the base alloy, 
and maintained the stability of the γ ´ phase as much as possible.

5. Conclusion
The present study utilized the diffusion multiple method to examine 
the effects of partially substituting Ru by Co, or increasing Co content 
along with partially substituting Re by Mo, on the precipitation of TCP 
phases in Ni-based superalloys containing Re and Ru, after heat treat -
ment at 1100 
◦
C for 1000 h. The specific conclusions can be deduced as 
follows:
1) Both the Base-16Co3Ru and Base-8Co4Mo3Re diffusion couples 
consisted of a ( γ + γ ´ + TCP) three-phase region and a ( γ + γ ´ ) two-phase 
region partially substituting Ru by Co, or increasing Co content along 
with partially substituting Re by Mo. Compared with the CALPHAD 
method, the diffusion multiple approach presented significant advan -
tages in analyzing the phase constitutes and identifying the phase region 
boundaries in both diffusion couples.
2) The partial substitution of Ru by Co, or the increase in Co content 
along with the partial substitution of Re by Mo, did not change the type 
of TCP phase in the three-phase region, which was always P phase. The P 
phase contained a high level of Ru content of about 8 wt% and the 
elemental substitution had a minimal effect on the composition of the P 
phase.
3) The partial substitution of Ru by Co, or the increase in Co content 
along with the partial substitution of Re by Mo, led to the decrease of 
supersaturation in γ matrix, decreasing the driving force and increasing 
the resistance for the precipitation of the TCP phase, thus resulting to the 
disappearance of P phase at the boundaries of the ( γ + γ ´ + TCP) three- 
phase region and ( γ + γ ´ ) two-phase region in both diffusion couples.
4) Based on the current results, the potential alloying solutions with 
lowered Ru and Re respectively were proposed. This study will be
