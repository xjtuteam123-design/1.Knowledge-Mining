Abstract 
Mo-ZrO 2 alloys were prepared through hydrothermal synthesi s and powder 
metallurgy process. Room temperature microstructure s of these alloy plates were 
observed, while high temperature tensile strength a nd plasticity of them were tested 
above 1000 °C using a Gleeble-1500D thermal simulat ed test machine. Results have 
shown that ZrO 2 particles were fine in the alloy and distributed a t grain boundaries 
and within the grains. The second phase of ZrO 2 particles demonstrated a pinning 
effect to retard recrystallization progress and thu s enhance high temperature tensile 
stress and plasticity of the alloys effectively. Be sides, the strengthening effect of the 
second phase in the alloy could also effectively im prove its high temperature tensile 
stress. Under 1400 °C, maximum tensile stress of Mo -1.5%ZrO 2 alloy was 1.5 times 
that of pure molybdenum, while the alloy plasticity also increased dramatically. The 
area shrinkage of Mo-1.5% ZrO 2 alloy at 1400°C was 5 times that of pure 
molybdenum.

2 particles; tensile stress; plasticity 
1. Introduction 
Molybdenum has various advantages such as high melt ing point (2620 °C), high 
elastic modulus (320~350GPa), excellent electrical and thermal conductivity, low 
coefficient of expansion (5.03×10 -6/K), and good resistance to acid, alkali and liquid 
metals[1, 2]. Therefore, molybdenum and molybdenum alloys are widely used in steel, 
metallurgy, machinery, chemical, atomic energy, ele ctronics, aerospace and other 
industries[3]. Meanwhile, the recrystallization temperature of molybdenum was lower 
than its neighboring metals, which has limited its applications and processing 
performance of molybdenum above 1000 °C. Alloying e lements or oxides are usually

Material and methods 
The precursors of ZrO 2 and MoO 3 were prepared by hydrothermal synthesis 
method, and then conversed to composite powders of Mo and ZrO 2 through a 
two-stage reduction process. This study was publish ed[15, 17]. Mo-ZrO 2 composites 
were fabricated by powder metallurgy technology, wi th the components of alloys 
given in Table 1. Subsequently, the samples were ro lled to a thickness of 1 mm under 
different temperatures, such as hot rolling at 1400 oC, warm rolling at 1000 oC, cold 
rolling and process annealing. Except temperature, the rolling processes and 
conditions for all samples are consistent. The plas tic deformation of each sample was 
above 95%. Samples for high temperature tensile tes ts were selected on the rolling 
plate of No. 1-5 specimen, and high temperature ten sile experiments were conducted 
along the length of the plate, with sample sizes shown in Fig. 1. 
To study high temperature tensile properties of the se alloy plates, a Gleeble 1500D 
numerically controlled dynamic thermo-mechanical ph ysical simulation testing 
machine was employed. SEM was used to observe micro structures and fracture 
structures of the plates before and after tests.

3. Results 
3.1 Microstructure and properties of Mo-ZrO 2 alloy at room 
temperature 
The second-phase particles can refine the grains by promoting grain nucleation and 
hindering grain growth. These refined microstructur es would result in a significant 
increase in strength, and also abundant grain bound ary areas that can reduce the 
concentration of deleterious solutes in the lattice [11]. Microstructures of the alloys in 
Figure 2 showed that alloy grains varied significan tly with increasing ZrO 2 content. 
ZrO 2 played a critical role in grain refining, making f ibers in the processed plate 
shorter and narrower. As ZrO 2 content increased, more spalled pits began to appe ar, 
for the grains of the alloy after ZrO 2 refinement were small and easy to peel off when 
the sample was dealt with[15, 18]. 
Figure 3 demonstrated mechanical properties of the rolled alloy plate at room 
temperature. As indicated, both tensile strength an d yield strength of the plate 
increased with the increase of ZrO 2 amount. The addition of second-phase ZrO 2 
particles could effectively improve room-temperatur e strength and yield strength of 
the alloy. ZrO 2 particles have exhibited a pinning effect during th e process of the 
deformation and fracture of the alloy[13, 19]. 
3.2 High temperature tensile properties of Mo-ZrO 2 alloy plates 
High-temperature strength and plasticity of Mo-ZrO 2 alloy plates were investigated 
at 1000°C, 1100°C, 1200°C, 1300°C, and 1400°C using a Gleeble 1500D numerically 
controlled dynamic thermo-mechanical physical simul ation testing machine. Fig. 4 
and Fig. 5 demonstrated their maximum tensile stres s and area shrinkage under high 
temperatures, respectively. As the temperature rose , dynamic recovery and 
recrystallization processes began to occur in alloy structures, due to decreased

strength of alloy matrix under high temperatures. H owever, different from the 
phenomenon upon high temperature annealing, the lim ited heating intervals and time 
in high temperature tensile tests made alloy struct ures unable to recover or 
recrystallize completely. From Fig.4, it can be observed that with the increase of ZrO 2 
addition, high temperature tensile stress of the pl ate showed a significant rising trend. 
The addition of ZrO 2 could significantly enhance maximum tensile stress of the alloy 
at high temperature. To be more specific, maximum t ensile stress of Mo-1.5% ZrO 2 
alloy was 1.4 times that of pure molybdenum plate a t 1000 °C, and 1.5 times at 
1400 °C. The high temperature strengthening effect of ZrO 2 particles was prominent. 
These added ZrO 2 particles could retard recrystallization process o f the alloy by 
pinning effect, but enhance maximum tensile stress of the alloy through strengthening 
effect of the second phase[4, 20, 21]. 
Due to the instability of heating range, only area shrinkage of the heating part can 
be employed as an index for measuring alloy plastic ity and shown in Fig. 5. As the 
temperature increased, the plasticity of pure molyb denum and the plate with 0.1% 
ZrO 2 addition both showed a descending trend, while tha t of the plate with trace 
amount of ZrO 2 only declined slightly. Combined with above-mentio ned 
recrystallization behavior of front plate, pure mol ybdenum began to recrystallize at 
1000°C. Due to limited time, the alloy could only c omplete the recovery phase during 
this dynamic recovery and recrystallization process. However, with rising temperature, 
more and more microstructures in the alloy finished dynamic recrystallization process, 
which ultimately led to decreased plate plasticity. Combined with the fracture analysis, 
it can be found that pure molybdenum plate complete d recrystallization at 1300 °C, 
while under 1400°C the tissues of pure molybdenum p late continued to grow up and 
its plasticity declined further. 
The plasticity of the alloy plate with 0.5% and hig her ZrO 2 addition increased 
initially, and then decreased as heating temperatur e rose. The second particles in the 
grain interior is an effective approach to increase the strength and simultaneously 
improve the ductility of alloys, because the intrag ranular particles can generate, pin 
down and thus accumulate dislocations within the gr ains[11]. Because the hindrance 
effect of the second phase to recrystallization enh anced recrystallization temperature, 
maximum plasticity of the alloy sample appeared at 1200 °C. The sample recovered 
under this temperature. In the stage when recrystal lization was impending, as the 
temperature increased, the hindering effect decreas ed, and the alloy began to 
recrystallize, making the alloy plasticity drop obv iously. Area shrinkage of the alloy 
with 1.5% ZrO 2 addition at 1400 °C was about 5 times that of pure molybdenum. The 
alloy plate still had good plasticity at 1400 °C.

3.3 High Temperature Tensile Fracture Analysis of M o-ZrO 2 Alloy 
Plates 
The heating time was short in high-temperature tens ile test, and the heating area 
was relatively small. Alloy strength at the heating position decreased dramatically to 
form a fracture. Fig. 6 showed SEM photos of a high -temperature fracture in pure 
molybdenum plate. The tensile fractures under 1000 °C, 1100 °C, 1200 °C, 1300 °C, 
and 1400 °C were observed, with the fracture patter ns analyzed. Meanwhile, dynamic 
recrystallization process of the plate during high temperature experiment was 
analyzed with high-temperature mechanical properties of the plate. 
When the temperature was below 1200 °C, recrystalli zation could not complete 
entirely due to inadequate heating time and tempera ture. Compared with the fracture 
of pure molybdenum plate, it can be figured out tha t a large number of dimples were 
still present in the alloy at 1200 °C. Pure molybde num plate demonstrated obvious 
plastic fracture, but recrystallized grains appeare d partially on the fracture at 1200°C 
due to local temperature instability. From the enlargement of area A and B in Fig. 6(c), 
plastic fractures were dominant, while recrystalliz ation has begun to occur in a few 
areas. When the temperature reached 1300 °C, crysta l grains of the alloy have 
completely recrystallized, with obvious recrystallized grains and intergranular fracture 
morphology appeared. Up to 1400°C, the grains grew further and brittle fractures 
became apparent. Pure molybdenum plate has recrystallized partially at 1200 °C. 
The area shrinkage in Fig. 8 demonstrated that the alloy plate had good plasticity. 
Mo-1.5%ZrO 2 alloy plate was selected to observe high temperatu re plasticity of the 
alloy. Fig. 5 showed high temperature fracture of t his plate with plentiful dimples. As 
the temperature rose up to 1400°C, a large number o f dimples were still observed to 
indicate plastic fractures. 
Area A and B in Fig. 8 (e) were enlarged and shown in Fig. 9, implying that the 
alloy has begun to recrystallize. More specifically , plastic fracture was still identified 
at A position with obvious presence of dimples and second-phase particles. On the 
contrary, the fracture at B position was recognized as brittle, for fine second phase 
ZrO 2 particles were observed on grain surfaces of the f racture under 100,000 times of 
magnification. 
It can be observed from Fig. 10 that ZrO 2 particles distributed at grain boundaries 
and inside the grains. The second phase predominant ly dispersed in the grain interior

to serve the dual purposes of blocking dislocations and storing dislocations[11]. These 
ZrO 2 particles could take a pinning effect, i.e., delay the recrystallization progress 
through pinning effect of the second phase, and als o enhance high-temperature 
mechanical properties of the alloy[7, 11, 13, 15, 2 2]. These were all beneficial for

boundaries. Through these two existence forms, the pinning effect of ZrO 2 could 
improve the strength of the alloy; meanwhile, ZrO 2 that hindered expansion of 
dislocation and crack could improve the plasticity of the alloy. 
4. Discussion 
Mo-ZrO 2 alloys with superior high-temperature mechanical p roperties were 
prepared through hydrothermal synthesis and powder metallurgy process. Under 
1400 °C, maximum tensile stress of Mo-1.5%ZrO 2 alloy was 1.5 times that of pure 
molybdenum, while the alloy plasticity was also gre atly improved, demonstrating that 
these two performances could be enhance effectively at the same time. Plastic-brittle 
transition after high-temperature stretching was po stponed, for the fracture of 
Mo-1.5%ZrO 2 alloy at 1400 °C was still plastic. The area shrinkage of Mo-1.5% ZrO 2 
alloy at 1400°C was 5 times that of pure molybdenum . SEM and TEM 
characterizations show that fine ZrO 2 was distributed inside molybdenum grains while 
slightly larger particles were distributed on grain boundaries. Through these two 
existence forms, the pinning effect of ZrO 2 could improve the strength of the alloy; 
meanwhile, ZrO 2 that hindered expansion of dislocation and crack c ould improve the 
plasticity of the alloy.
