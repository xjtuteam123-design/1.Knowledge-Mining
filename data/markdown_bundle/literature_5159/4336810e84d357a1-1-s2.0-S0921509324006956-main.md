ABSTRACT 
High Gd content Mg alloys are expected to overcome the poor heat resistance of magnesium (Mg) alloys. 
However, the low ductility limits their applications. Nano-precipitates is a viable method to enhance the strength 
without seriously plummeting ductility. In this study, ultrafine and densely arranged dislocation cells, with an 
average size of 27 nm, were obtained in Mg-11.5Gd-2Y-0.5Zr-0.5Ag-0.5Nd alloy after hot rolling. During the 
aging process, the dislocation cells facilitated the precipitation of fine and high-density β’ . In the peak-aged 
(225 
◦
C, 15 h) alloy, the volume fraction of β ′ could reach 20.3 %, and the average length and thickness of β ′ 
are 10 and 5.9 nm, respectively. The alloy with such a microstructure exhibited a yield strength (YS) of 403 MPa, 
and an EL of 8.3 % at room temperature. YS of the alloy was still 357 MPa, 310 MPa, and 206 MPa at 200 
◦
C, 
250 
◦
C, and 300 
◦
C, respectively. The high YS is attributed to grain boundary, dislocation, solid solution, and 
precipitation strengthening, among which precipitation strengthening is dominant. Moreover, the nano- β ′ could 
be sheared, thereby coordinating the plastic deformation. Therefore, the present alloy achieved high YS while 
maintaining good ductility. 
1. Introduction 
Reducing the weight of industrial products is a renowned strategy to 
release energy crises and decrease carbon emissions. Mg – Gd based al -
loys are one of the lightest metal structural materials with excellent 
mechanical properties, consequently, they have wide applications in 
aerospace equipment [ 1 – 6 ]. Wan et al. prepared an ultrafine nano -
crystalline Mg-8Gd-3Y-0.4Zr (wt.%) alloy, with an average grain size of 
80 nm, the yield strength (YS) at room temperature (RT) reached 650 
MPa [ 7 ]. Recently, stricter requirements have been imposed on Mg al -
loys for application at elevated temperatures (ET). However, YS of 
nanocrystalline materials dramatically decreases because of grain 
boundary slide and grain growth [ 8 , 9 ]. Therefore, there is an urgent 
need to develop other strengthening methods. Li et al. [ 10 ] revealed that 
increasing the Gd content through an extreme short-time solid solution 
treatment before aging treatment could increase the density of the 
precipitation phases, resulting in a 50 MPa increase in YS at RT. When 
the Gd content increased from 6 % to 12 % in the Mg-xGd-3Y-0.5Zr (wt. 
%) alloy, the volume fraction and size of β ′ increased, and the 
Mg-12Gd-3Y-0.5Zr (wt.%) alloy showed higher YS at RT and ET. How -
ever, the coarse- β ′ results in poor ductility [ 11 , 12 ]. High Gd content Mg 
alloys are the most promising lightweight heat-resistant alloys if their 
poor ductility can be improved. 
In recent years, some scholars revealed that nano-precipitates can 
overcome the tradeoff between YS and ductility [ 13 – 18 ]. Specialized 
dislocation arrangements (cells, networks, and loops) are known to 
promote the precipitation process and expected to refine precipitates, 
offering a means to improve mechanical properties. Dislocation net -
works in Fe – Cr – Ni – Al alloy promoted the formation of nano β -NiAl, 
increasing the YS from 929 MPa to 1659 MPa, whereas the elongation 
(EL) slightly decreased from 15.3 % to 14.7 % [ 19 ]. Dislocation net -
works, which act as pipe diffusion channels, can accelerate the diffusion 
of solute atoms into dislocation cores [ 20 ]. This accelerated diffusion 
explains the aging kinetics enhancement when dislocation substructures 
exist. Li et al. [ 21 ] reported that dislocation substructures formed via hot 
deformation in Mg-8Gd-3Y-0.5Zr (wt.%) alloys. Moreover, the addition 
of Ag and Nd enhanced the stability of the dislocation substructure 
through a pinning effect [ 22 – 24 ], indicating that a high-density (ultra -
fine) dislocation substructure can form in hot-deformed Ag and Nd 
doped Mg alloys with high Gd content. The dislocation substructure is 
expected to promote the precipitation process that enhances the 
ductility of high Gd content Mg alloys.

In order to reveal the effect of dislocation substructure on aging 
precipitation and enhance ductility of high Gd content Mg alloys, we 
prepared a Mg-11.5Gd-2Y-0.5Zr-0.5Ag-0.5Nd (wt.%) alloy. The dislo -
cation cells were introduced through hot rolling. During the following 
aging process, the dislocation cells served as prior nucleation regions of 
the β’’ and promoted the formation of fine and high-density β ′ in peak- 
aged alloy. This study demonstrates that the fine β ′ could participate 
in dislocation slip, decreasing the resistance of dislocation slip. As a 
result, the peak-aged alloy has a good combination of YS and ductility at 
RT, as well as maintains adequate YS at ET.

3. Results 
3.1. Microstructures of homogenized and rolled alloys 
An initially studied plate with dimensions of 200 mm × 80 mm × 20 
mm was first homogenized annealing. Fig. 1 a shows the inverse pole 
figure (IPF) map of the homogenized alloy. The alloy mainly comprised 
equiaxed crystals with an average grain size of approximately 54 μ m. 
The (0002) pole figure (PF) exhibited a maximum intensity value of 4.4, 
indicating random grain orientation ( Fig. 1 b). Kernel Average Misor -
itation (KAM) map shows a low dislocation density, with an average 
KAM of 0.33 ( Fig. 1 c). 
After hot rolling with 50 % reduction, the average grain size was 
refined to 25 μ m. A small number of twins, approximately 0.9 %, formed 
during the rolling process ( Fig. 1 d). The texture intensity decreased to 
3.9 ( Fig. 1 e), and the average KAM increased to 1.17, indicating that a 
high density of dislocations remained after hot rolling ( Fig. 1 f). 
TEM images of rolled alloy are shown in Fig. 2 . Periodically arranged 
ultrafine dislocation cells arrangement can be observed in Fig. 2 a and b. 
The average size of dislocation cells is 27 nm, with minimum and 
maximum sizes of 9 and 90 nm ( Fig. 2 f), respectively. There are only 
diffraction spots from a Mg alloy grain in the SAED pattern ( Fig. 2 c), 
which shows a common orientation of each cell and Mg matrix. More -
over, no additional diffraction spots were found in the SAED map, 
indicating no precipitation phase in the rolled alloy. HRTEM and cor -
responding Inverse FFT maps indicate that the dislocation cell consists 
only of edge dislocations ( Fig. 2 d and e). In summary, the dislocation 
cells have the same orientation in a grain, and have a coherent rela -
tionship with the Mg matrix.

4.3. Effect of dislocation cell on aging precipitation 
In the rolled alloy, there are densely arranged dislocation cells with 
an average size of 27 nm. In the RA1 (early-aged) alloy, β’’ nucleated 
preferentially around the cell boundary ( Fig. 4 b). β ′ successively trans -
formed from β’’ during the aging process, as a result, some β ′ also 
nucleated around the cell boundary. In the RA15 (peak-aged) alloy, cell- 
like precipitation bonds can be found ( Fig. 5 a). In the RA32 (over-aged) 
alloy, β precipitated along the grain boundary. However, β ′ was also the 
main precipitation phase inside the grain ( Fig. 7 c). During the aging 
process, the variation in mechanical properties can be reflected by the 
evolution of precipitation phases. The dislocation cell-induced aging 
precipitation is shown schematically in Fig. 11 . Fine β’’ , with a volume 
fraction of approximately 8.9 %, precipitated in the RA1 alloy, and the 
YS increased by 33 MPa, whereas the EL decreased from 16.6 % to 12.2 
%. After aging for 15 h, fine and high-density β ′ served as the main 
strengthening mechanism, and coordinated with dislocation slip. Thus, 
the peak-aged alloy had a YS of 403 MPa and adequate ductility. After 
peak aging, β ′ remained in RA32 alloy. However, its size increased while 
the volume fraction decreased. Moreover, β successively precipitated 
along grain boundary. Hence, YS of the RA32 alloy decreased to 375 
MPa and its EL decreased to 2.7 %.

to fine and high-density β’ . The precipitation strengthening of β ′ is the 
main contributor to YS, and fine β ′ could participate in dislocation slip,

The fine and high-density β ′ in RA15 alloy is mainly attributed to 
ultrafine and densely arranged dislocation cells. On the one hand, ul -
trafine and dense dislocation cells reduce the energy required for 
nucleation and provide nucleation sites. On the other hand, dislocation 
cells act as diffusion channels for Gd atoms, effectively facilitating 
precipitation. Similar dislocation substructures have been reported by 
previous researchers in Mg – Gd based alloys and pure Mg [ 66 – 68 ]. This 
specific dislocation pattern, like ‘Talyor lattice ’ , was observed to be 
formed by irregular dislocation arrays after annealing, which indicates 
that the formation of dislocation cells is related to dislocation motion 
during the annealing stage. And the dislocation substructure comprises 
< c > screw dislocations with alternating signs and an array of < c + a >
dislocations [ 68 ]. Previous studies have shown that dislocation sub -
structure can enhance mechanical properties [ 67 ], yet, the impact on 
aging precipitation has not been thoroughly examined. Herein, we 
reveal the mechanism by which dislocation substructure (cells) tailor 
precipitation to enhance mechanical properties, and shed some light on 
the design of advanced Mg – Gd based alloys.
