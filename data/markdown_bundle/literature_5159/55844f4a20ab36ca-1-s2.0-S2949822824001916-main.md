ABSTRACT
The applications of Nb-Si-based alloys for new-generation engines are heavily influenced by their strength and
toughness. Therefore, accurately and effectively predicting fracture toughness and compressive strength is
crucial for accelerating the research and development of Nb-Si-based alloys. To achieve this, this work estab-
lished a database based on Nb-Si-based alloy data fabricated by arc melting. It contains 259 samples with fracture
toughness, of which 124 groups have compressive strength information. Machine learning methods were then
employed to construct the prediction models between alloying elements, fracture toughness, and compressive
strength. The result indicated that Random Forest (RF) and K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) regression models were
suitable for predicting Nb-Si-based alloys ’ fracture toughness and compressive strength, respectively, and the
generalization ability was proved by the Nb-16Si-20Ti-xB
4
C (x = 0, 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 1.0 at%) alloys fabricated in
previous work. Therefore, the RF and KNN prediction models were used for the alloying design of five-element
Nb-(16 20) Si-(20 24) Ti-(0 6) Al-(0 6) Cr alloys (at%) with compositions ranging from hypoeutectic to
hypereutectic zones. Through these models and high-throughput optimization algorithms, Nb-16Si-24Ti-3Al-6Cr
alloy with excellent strength and toughness matching was selected as the optimal material from 1225 candidate
materials. Experimental results showed that the actual fracture toughness (11.9 MPa ⋅ m
1/2
) and compressive
strength (2333.5 MPa) of Nb-16Si-24Ti-3Al-6Cr alloy matched the predicted values with less than 15 % errors.

After more than three decades of research, more than twenty tran-

2. Experimental
2.1. Database creation
The data on Nb-Si-based alloys fabricated by arc melting was
collected from Web of Science, ScienceDirect, Springer, CNKI, and other
relevant publications. During the data collection, Image-Pro, Photoshop,
and other software were used. The data reliability was confirmed
through empirical judgment by professional knowledge of Nb-Si-based
alloys to assess the sources and information (such as the composition,
microstructure, and properties of Nb-Si-based alloys). Then, a database
containing compositions and properties of Nb-Si-based alloys was
established (shown in Fig. 1 ), in which 259 samples have fracture
toughness and 124 groups have compressive strength information.

2.3. Machine learning processes
The data were divided into the training set and testing set, with 30 %
of the data used for the testing set. The training set was trained using six
common machine learning regression algorithms: KNN, DTR, RR, LR,
SVR, and RF [29,33,34] . The models ’ parameters were optimized using
the 5-fold cross-validation method, and the prediction model was built
after the parameter adjustment. Finally, the algorithm with the highest
prediction accuracy was selected from six regression algorithms as the
fracture toughness and compressive strength prediction models,
respectively. The accuracy of the prediction models was verified using
data from previous parallel experiments [32] for fracture toughness and
compressive strength.
Then, through the high throughput composition design method
combined with the fracture toughness and compressive strength pre-
diction models, the fracture toughness and compressive strength of the
multivariate Nb- (16 20) Si- (20 24) Ti- (0 6) Al- (0 6) Cr alloys (at
%) with compositions ranging from hypoeutectic to hypereutectic zone
were predicted with each step length of 1 at%. The distribution results of
fracture toughness and compressive strength in the full component
range were constructed, and the data within the entire composition
range were screened using appropriate boundary conditions. After
selecting the optimal alloy composition, samples were fabricated and
verified, and the framework of the design process is shown in Fig. 2 .

2.5. Preparation of new alloys
The new alloy components with good strength and toughness were
screened out by the fracture toughness and compressive strength pre-
diction models, and the ingots were fabricated in a non-consumable
copper crucible under an argon atmosphere (DHL400). The raw mate-
rials used in this study were Nb, Si, Ti, Al, and Cr powders with a purity
of more than 99 % (all the powders were purchased from Sinopharm
Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd.). The powders were weighed according to
the selected composition proportion and then ball milling for 6 h. The
powder mixture was pressed into cylinders with a diameter of 10 mm
and a height of 10 mm under a pressure of 10 MPa. The mass of the
prefabricated alloy ingot is about 65 g. The alloy ingot was remelted five
times to ensure uniformity, and the weight loss rate was less than 0.5 wt
%.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Correlation analysis
Two representative algorithms, Maximum Information Coefficient
(MIC) and Pearson Correlation Coefficient (PCC), were employed to
analyze the correlation between the input characteristics (alloying ele-
ments) and output characteristics (fracture toughness and compressive
strength) of Nb-Si-based alloys. The MIC coefficient reflects the linear or
nonlinear relationship between the input and output features in the
high-dimensional big dataset. In contrast, the PCC coefficient reflects the
linear relationship between the input and the output features. The
output range of PCC is 1 to + 1, where 0 represents no correlation.
Negative values indicate a negative correlation between the input and
output features; positive values indicate a positive correlation and ab-
solute values indicate the magnitude of the correlation. The range of
MIC values is between 0 and 1, where higher values indicate a stronger
correlation. The alloying elements that dominate the fracture toughness
and compressive strength of Nb-Si-based alloys can be determined by
comparing the results of two correlation coefficients.
Fig. 3 shows the correlation analysis results between the input fea-
tures (alloying elements) and output features (fracture toughness and
compressive strength) from the MIC and PCC algorithms, as well as the
ranking of each alloying element. For Fig. 3 a, the five alloying elements
with the largest MIC values were Nb, Al, Hf, Cr, and Zr, while the five
alloying elements with the largest |PCC| values were Si, Ti, Zn, Mo, and
C ( Fig. 3 a). Among them, Ti, Zn, C, Mg, Zr, Al, and Hf were positively
correlated with fracture toughness, whereas Cr, Mo, and Si were nega-
tively correlated with fracture toughness, as shown in Fig. 3 c. The
measured MIC values, except for Nb element, range from 0.024 to 0.335,
with |PCC| values ranging from 0 to 0.47. Since both values were
relatively small, it can be confirmed that the correlation between the
alloying elements (input features) and the fracture toughness (output
features) was not close.
In terms of compressive strength, the top five alloying elements with
the highest MIC values were Si, Hf, Mo, Cr, and Al. Meanwhile, the top
five alloying elements with the highest |PCC| values were Mo, Nb, Zr,
Ga, and Cr ( Fig. 3 b). Among these elements, Mo, Zr, Ga, Cr, Si, Hf, and Al
showed positive correlations with compressive strength, while Nb
exhibited a negative correlation with compressive strength, as shown in
Fig. 3 d. The measured MIC values ranged from 0.068 to 0.45, and the |
PCC| values ranged from 0.01 to 0.42. Since both values were relatively
small, it indicates that in addition to alloying elements, other factors,
such as microstructure characteristics, affected the compressive strength
of Nb-Si-based alloys. The ductile Nbss phase and the hard silicide phase
(( α , β , γ )-Nb

Through the long-term research on the alloying of Nb-Si-based al-
loys, preliminary progress has been made in the addition range of
various elements. Considering the balance between room-temperature
and high-temperature performance, the proportion of ductile phases
should not be too high. When the proportion of ductile and brittle phases
in Nb-Si-based alloy reaches 6:4, both room-temperature fracture
toughness and high-temperature strength achieve good balance, so the
content of Si is usually limited to 16 – 20 at%. Ti is an important element
in improving the fracture toughness of Nb-Si-based alloys, which re-
duces the Prierls-Nabarro energy barrier [20,44,45] . Nevertheless,

usually 20 – 24 at%. Al is an element that significantly improves the

strength. Currently, the range of various element additions in Nb-Si-

4. Conclusion
A database of Nb-Si-based alloys fabricated by arc melting was
established by collecting data from the publications. The relationship
between input and output features in the database was studied through
correlation analysis. Six commonly used regression algorithms were
used to predict Nb-Si-based alloys ’ fracture toughness and compressive
strength. The strength and toughness design of multivariate Nb-Si-Ti-Al-
Cr alloys was carried out using the optimal machine learning model. The
main conclusions are as follows:
(1) The main input features (alloying elements) affecting the room
temperature fracture toughness and compressive strength of Nb-
Si-based alloy were determined through MIC and PCC correlation
analysis. With the increase of input features, the prediction ac-
curacy of each model increases first and then tends to stabilize.
RR and LR have lower prediction accuracy than other machine
learning algorithms, indicating that the relationship between
alloying elements and properties cannot be simplified into a
linear relationship.
(2) Using various machine learning algorithms to analyze the influ-
ence of alloying elements on fracture toughness and compressive
strength at room temperature, the RF model shows superior ac-
curacy in fracture toughness prediction compared to other
models. In addition, the KNN model is more suitable for
predicting alloy compressive strength, possibly due to the influ-
ence of the amount of data in the database.
(3) A total of 1225 groups of Nb-Si-Ti-Al-Cr with composition
ranging from hypoeutectic to hypereutectic zone were predicted
by RF and KNN prediction models, and Nb-16Si-24Ti-3Al-6Cr
alloys with excellent strength and toughness matching were
screened out by boundary conditions, with the fracture toughness
of 11.9 MPa ⋅ m
1/2
, and compressive strength of 2333.5 MPa.
