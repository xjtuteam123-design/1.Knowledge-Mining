constrained by their low strength and poor wear resistance. This study modifies the microstructure and prop -

1. Introduction
Over the past decade, high-entropy alloys (HEAs) have garnered 
significant attention from researchers, particularly for their mechanical 
properties [ 1 – 3 ]. The highly disordered solid-solution microstructure of 
HEAs imparts excellent mechanical properties. The mismatch in atomic 
size and modulus among multiple elements causes severe lattice 
distortion [ 4 ], thereby enhancing mechanical performance through 
solid-solution strengthening. FCC-structured HEAs exhibit excellent 
ductility. However, their relatively low strength and poor tribological 
properties limit their practical applications [ 5 , 6 ]. To overcome these 
limitations, researchers have focused on designing multiphase HEAs. 
Intermetallic compounds, with their complex crystal structures, provide 
high strength and hardness but suffer from poor plasticity [ 7 , 8 ]. 
Multiphase HEAs strengthened by intermetallic phases (Im) can achieve 
superior strength and a favorable combination of overall properties 
[ 9 – 11 ]. Nevertheless, designing multiphase HEAs containing Im re -
mains challenging due to the compositional complexity and the un -
known properties of multiple principal elements in these alloys [ 2 , 12 ].
The Laves phase, a prevalent intermetallic compound, is widely 
employed to strengthen HEAs [ 13 , 14 ], particularly those with a FCC 
structure. Studies have demonstrated that niobium (Nb) addition can 
induce a structural transformation from a single-phase FCC to 
dual-phase or multiphase configurations [ 13 – 18 ]. This transformation 
significantly enhances alloy strength through mechanisms such as solid 
solution strengthening, grain boundary strengthening, and 
second-phase precipitation, albeit typically at the expense of ductility. 
Current research primarily focuses on single-phase FCC HEAs. For 
instance, Sunkari et al. [ 9 ] explored the impact of Nb on the micro -
structural evolution, phase stability, and mechanical properties of the

Existing studies are predominantly centered on single-phase FCC 
HEAs (e.g., CoCrFeNi or CoCrFeMnNi), where phase transformation 
behavior can be predicted using thermodynamic parameters such as 
mixing enthalpy ( Δ Hmix), atomic size difference ( δ ), and electronega -
tivity difference ( Δ χ ) [ 20 – 22 ]. This provides a theoretical basis for the 
tailored addition of Nb. In contrast, the mechanism of underlying Nb 
influence in complex multiphase systems (particularly those with a 
dual-phase FCC matrix) lacks systematic investigation. To address this 
research gap, the present study employs a dual-phase FCC-type CoCu -
FeMnNi high-entropy alloy [ 23 ] as the matrix. By adding varying Nb 
contents, the influences of Nb on the alloy of microstructural evolution, 
mechanical properties, and tribological performance were systemati -
cally investigated. The research aims to elucidate the strengthening ef -
fect of Nb on the relationship of composition-microstructure-property 
within the CoCuFeMnNi system and to evaluate its potential for opti -
mizing the strength-ductility trade-off. The anticipated outcomes are 
expected to provide novel strategies and a theoretical foundation for 
designing high-performance multiphase high-entropy alloys.

An enlarged observation of the primary FCC diffraction peak 
revealed a shift toward lower angles. This shift was analyzed using the 
following Bragg ’ s equation: 
2d sin θ = n λ (1) 
where d is the interplanar spacing, λ is the wavelength of the incident 
electron beam, and θ is the angle between the incident diffracted beam 
and the crystal plane. With increasing Nb content, the lattice constants 
corresponding to the FCC diffraction peaks were observed to be 3.610 Å, 
3.620 Å, 3.629 Å, 3.652 Å, and 3.656 Å, respectively. The increase in the 
FCC lattice constant with rising Nb content can be attributed to two 
factors. First, the atomic radius of Nb (0.143 nm) is relatively large 
within this alloy system, and the incorporation of such large atoms into 
the solid solution causes significant lattice expansion. Second, Nb pro -
motes the formation of the Laves phase with Co and Fe, which possess 
smaller atomic radii. Consequently, the reduced Co and Fe content in the 
FCC solid solution phase further contributes to the lattice constant in -
crease (Supplementary Material). These combined effects result in an 
increased FCC lattice constant, which shifts the diffraction peak to lower 
angles.

To investigate the fracture mode of CoCuFeMnNb
x
Ni alloys, SEM 
was employed to examine the fracture morphology. Fig. 8 displays the 
fracture morphology of these alloys. As shown in Fig. 8 (a), the Nb0 alloy 
exhibited clear dimples. In the Nb0.25 alloy ( Fig. 8 (b)), dimples were 
also observed alongside a small number of tear ridges. The presence of 
tear ridges is attributed to the formation of the Laves phase. As a hard 
and brittle phase, the Laves phase acts as a nucleation site for cleavage 
cracks, which propagate in a cleavage mode, forming small cleavage 
facets. Subsequently, owing to the significant presence of the FCC phase, 
the cracks propagate in a ductile manner, creating tear ridges. 
Comparing the dimple sizes in Fig. 8 (a) and (b), the dimples in the Nb0 
alloy were larger than those in the Nb0.25 alloy, indicating that the Nb0 
alloy possesses greater resistance to localized instability and exhibits 
better plasticity [ 8 ]. As the Nb content increased, the fracture mode of 
the Nb0.5 alloy transitioned significantly from ductile fracture to 
quasi-cleavage fracture. As shown in Fig. 8 (c), small micropores were 
observed on the fracture surface, which did not fully grow and coalesce 
due to the rapid shear fracture of the Laves phase. The fracture surfaces 
of the Nb0.75 and Nb1 alloys ( Fig. 8 (d) and (e)) exhibited 
quasi-cleavage fracture characteristics, including tear ridges and cleav -
age steps. Furthermore, the Nb1 alloy displayed larger cleavage facets, 
which can be attributed to the excessive Laves phase content, resulting 
in brittle fracture characteristics in its mechanical properties [ 40 , 83 ].

4. Conclusion
CoCuFeMnNb
x
Ni ( x = 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, and 1) HEAs with varying 
Nb contents were prepared by arc melting, and their microstructures and 
properties were analyzed using XRD, SEM, and TEM. The findings are as 
follows. 
1. The addition of Nb promoted the formation of the Laves phase, 
causing the phase structure of CoCuFeMnNb
x
Ni alloys to transition 
from a dual-phase FCC + Cu-rich FCC structure to a multiphase 
structure (FCC + Cu-rich FCC + Laves). The Laves phase content 
increased with rising Nb content.
2. Phase parameter analysis revealed that Δ χ
Allen 
and M
d 
parameters 
effectively identify the formation of intermetallic compounds in 
transition group HEAs. Specifically, Δ χ
Allen 
≥ 11.3 % and M
d 
≥ 0.93 
were conducive to the formation of such compounds.
3. The addition of Nb altered the solidification sequence of the alloy. Nb 
readily formed the Laves phase with Fe and Co, which solidified first. 
Subsequently, the Cu-rich FCC phase formed between the Laves and 
FCC phases. The FCC phase was enriched with Ni, the Cu-rich FCC 
phase with Cu and Mn, and the Laves phase with Fe, Co, and Nb.
