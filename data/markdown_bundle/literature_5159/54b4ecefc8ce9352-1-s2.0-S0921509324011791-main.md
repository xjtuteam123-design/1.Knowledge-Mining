ABSTRACT
Refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) have garnered widespread attention as a novel class of alloys with 
promising applications in high-temperature environments. However, their limited ductility has constrained their 
engineering application. In this study, a Ti
40
Zr
40
Nb
10
Ta
10 
base alloy with enhanced ductility was chosen, and 
various amounts of Al were added to investigate the microstructure and mechanical behavior of these alloys. The 
addition of Al aimed to trigger the cocktail effect while simultaneously enhancing the specific strength of alloys. 
Scanning electron microscopy and transmission electron microscopy were employed to investigate the phase 
composition and microstructure, revealing that the addition of Al promoted the formation of a B2 structure. With 
the incorporation of Al, both the yield strength and hardness of alloys increased. When the Al content reached to 
8 at.%, the alloy achieved an optimal strength-ductility balance (yield strength 1030 MPa, elongation 13.4 %). 
The strengthening contribution was calculated and indicated that solid solution strengthening and precipitation 
strengthening are the two main methods. While the excellent ductility is contributed by the cross-slip of dislo -
cations, the presence of kink bands accommodated dislocation slip and reduced stress concentration, enhancing 
the elongation. This study reports a system of RHEAs with excellent room-temperature strength-ductility balance, 
shedding light on its potential engineering applications.
1. Introduction
Over the past decade, refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) have 
attracted considerable interest due to their exceptional high- 
temperature properties [ 1 – 6 ]. RHEAs exhibit higher yield strength, 
even at exceptionally elevated temperatures, making them potential 
candidates for replacing traditional superalloys [ 2 , 7 ]. However, certain 
drawbacks have hindered their widespread applications in engineering. 
Many RHEAs face challenges in ductility, showing poor strain hardening 
at room temperature [ 8 ]. Although numerous RHEAs with high strength 
have been developed, most of their mechanical properties have been 
studied through compression tests [ 9 – 11 ]. Only a small subset of RHEAs, 
specifically the Ti-Zr-Hf-Nb-Ta and its subsystems, have had their tensile 
properties reported, showcasing a favorable strength-ductility balance 
[ 12 – 16 ]. Among RHEAs, the Ti-Zr-Hf-Nb-Ta alloys stand out as some of 
the most thoroughly investigated and promising candidates.
The focus of the reported Ti-Zr-Hf-Nb-Ta alloys lies in their 
outstanding tensile ductility. In recent years, several researchers have 
elaborated on conventional deformation mechanisms in RHEAs, such as 
dislocation slip, twinning, stress-induced martensitic transformation, 
microband-induced plasticity, among others [ 17 – 20 ]. Additionally, 
numerous efforts have been dedicated to manipulating deformation 
mechanisms to enhance ductility. For instance, Lei et al. proposed that 
doping 2 at% O to TiZrHfNb RHEA results in preferential interaction 
between O atoms and Ti/Zr, leading to the formation of chemically 
short-range ordered structures based on (Ti, Zr, O), referred to as or -
dered oxygen complexes (OOCs) [ 21 ]. Specifically, OOCs cause dislo -
cations to deviate from their paths during movement, promoting wave 
slip of dislocation and thus enhancing dislocation multiplication.

The addition of Al is common in HEAs and can trigger a cocktail 
effect, leading to interesting properties [ 10 , 23 – 28 ]. On the one hand, 
the addition of Al promotes the formation of the Body-Centered Cubic 
(BCC) phase, imparting excellent room temperature and 
high-temperature strength to the alloy. On the other hand, the combi -
nation of Al with elements such as Ti and Zr can form a chemically 
short-range order (SRO), which hinders the movement of dislocations 
and promotes dislocation multiplication. The evident cross-slip bands 
alleviate the accumulation of stress in dislocations and delay fracture. 
The strong interaction between SRO and dislocations significantly in -
fluences deformation behavior and mechanical properties, exhibiting 
outstanding overall mechanical performance [ 29 ]. Furthermore, the 
addition of Al enables the attainment of alloys with outstanding specific 
strength by reducing the density while simultaneously increasing the 
yield strength. However, excessive Al can lead to the formation of 
intermetallic compounds and reduce ductility, making the effective 
utilization of Al a crucial research challenge.

The element segregation can be also discussed from the perspectives of 
atomic radius and enthalpy of mixing. Zr enrichment at inter-dendritic 
regions can be attributed to its larger atomic radius. Zr and Ta have 
positive mixing enthalpy, indicating that the two elements do not tend to 
segregate together. With the addition of Al, Al is enriched in the inter- 
dendrites, aligning with the distribution of Zr and Nb. Generally, there 
is a certain relationship between elemental segregation and the mixing 
enthalpy of elemental pairs. The mixing enthalpy for Al-Zr is 44 kJ/ 
mol, while the mixing enthalpy for Al-Ta is only 18 kJ/mol. A higher 
negative mixing enthalpy indicates a greater tendency for elements to 
segregate and cluster together during the solidification process.
Furthermore, EBSD technology was employed to conveniently 
determine the grain size and phase constitution of the RHEAs, as shown 
in Fig. 3 . From the EBSD inverse pole figure (IPF) maps, it can be 
observed that the addition of Al leads to a reduction in grain size. The Al- 
0 alloy consisted of coarse grains and the average grain size is approx -
imately 96 μ m. With increasing Al content, the average grain sizes for 
the Al-5, Al-8, and Al-10 alloys were 92 μ m, 51 μ m, and 56 μ m, 
respectively. This reduction may be attributed to the promotion of 
nucleation by the addition of Al, allowing for more nuclei during the 
solidification process and resulting in grain refinement [ 30 ].

particles are sufficiently fine and maintain coherency.

contribution to plasticity is far less significant compared to dislocation 
slip, resulting in lower elongation compared to the Al-8 alloy.
