1. Introduction
Lightweight Al – Cu alloys are widely utilized in aerospace appli-
cations due to their high speci ﬁc strength, production ﬂexibility,
and low density.[1– 5] Nevertheless, with the industrial demand for
high-temperature heat-resistant aluminum alloys, traditional
Al– Cu alloys have been dif ﬁcult to meet the requirements of
use.[6] Above 250 °C, the main strengthening phase θ 0 in the
traditional Al – Cu alloy coarses rapidly, and the grain boundary
pinning effect weakens, resulting in a sharp decline in alloy prop-
erties. To improve the high-temperature properties of Al – Cu
alloy, obtain the microstructure with high thermal stability
and restrain the coarsening of θ 0 metasta-
ble phase, it is necessary to carry out the
composition design and microstructure
optimization. Currently, the researchers
mainly enhanced the high-temperature
properties of the alloy by adding alloying
elements,[7] through micro-alloying, the
solidiﬁcation conditions of aluminum
alloys can be adjusted, thus controlling
the microstructure, [8] which process was
an ef ﬁcient way to manufacture heat-resis-
tant aluminum alloys. [9– 11]
Ni element has a great role in enhancing
the high-temperature performance of alu-
minum alloys. [12] Ni eutectic can reduce
thermal tearing during casting, [13] and
the Al-Ni eutectic is extremely thermally
stable.[14– 16] According to Feng et al. [17]
an increase in Ni content resulted in a
22 MPa increase in tensile strength at
350 °C. In contrast, the elongation
decreased by 0.8%. This ﬁnding highlights
the signiﬁcant impact of Ni content on the
mechanical properties of the material
under high-temperature conditions. At
present, adding Ni into casting Al – Cu alloy
can obtain Al – Cu– Ni alloy with superior high-temperature
strength,[18] which can be used in the service environment of
200– 300 °C for a long time, such as China ’s ZL208 and the
UK’s RR350 alloy, which has been widely used in the parts of
aircraft engines. In this series of alloys, Ni and Cu usually form
complex intermetallic compounds with high melting points,
which are generally Al 3CuNi, Al 7Cu4Ni, and Al 3Ni phases. At
the grain boundary, a closed network structure is formed by these
phases, thus resulting in the alloy ’s remarkable mechanical
properties under high temperatures. [19]
The addition of trace transition metal elements (TMs) can
form complex high-melting intermetallic compounds on the
grain boundaries, hinder sliding of grain boundaries and grain
rotation under applied stress, [20,21] and form stable nanoscale
precipitates, and inhibit the coarsening of thermal stable phase
θ 00/θ 0 and the transition to θ phase. By adding Zr, V, Sc transition
elements to cast Al-Cu alloys, Li et al. [22] found that the addition
of TMs can hinder the transition from θ 00 to θ 0, and enhance the
resistance of θ 0 to coarsening during prolonged heat exposure,
augment the mechanical characteristics of the alloys at high
temperatures. In a study conducted by A.Shyam et al. [23]
Al5CuNiMnZr alloy was prepared through microalloying Mn

components, but the casting defects are dif ﬁcult to avoid. In this research, the
local defects of casting heat-resistant Al6.5Cu2Ni0.3Ti0.5Zr0.25V alloy parts are
repaired by the arc-directed energy deposition method. To examine the mech-
anism of the microstructure in the repaired zone of the heat treatment condition
on the high-temperature characteristics, the microstructure evolution of the
repaired zone before and after heat treatment is analyzed. The results indicate
that strati ﬁcation exists in the sedimentary layer, however, the overall grain
distribution is relatively uniform. After heat treatment, the microstructure of the
repaired zone is changed, Cu element distribution becomes uniform and dendrite
segregation disappears; simultaneously, the grain in the repaired zone mainly
exists as high angle grain boundaries, which improves the fracture resistance
ability of the material. At 350 °C, the average tensile strength, yield strength and
elongation are 120.0 MPa, 98.6 MPa and 12.9%, respectively, the tensile strength
reaches 94.1% of the matrix. Dimples abound on the fracture ’s exterior, and the
fracture form of the repaired zone is microporous aggregate fracture. δ-Al3CuNi
and γ-Al7Cu4Ni enhance the grain boundary strength, and θ 0-Al2Cu diffuses from
the α-Al matrix during aging, which is the main reason for the excellent
high-temperature performance of the repaired zone.

The material used in this study was cast

cracks, and other casting defects. Based on the current problems,
WAAM, which stands for Wire Arc Additive Manufacturing, is
regarded as an ef ﬁcient solution for repairing parts. This is on
account of its noteworthy advantages such as molding stability
and process ﬂexibility. Wire þ arc additive manufacturing
(WAAM) enables the deposition of multiple layers of material
to achieve the desired shape and form of the part. It is a promis-
ing technology that is widely used in various industries, such as
aerospace and automotive. [24] Li et al. [25] used mechanical groov-
ing and WAAM to repair surface defects of parts, by giving the
relationship between the dip range of the groove and the geome-
try used for the pass, the automatic repair of machining and
WAAM integration can be realized, and favorable repair quality
is obtained. Zhou et al. [26] used the WAAM technique to repair
TC17 and found that the repaired zone formed an excellent met-
allurgical bond with the matrix. The strength of the deposited
weld metal exceeds 88% of the substrate. Vishnukumar
et al. [27] used WAAM to repair the corroded surface of the
AA5052 structure. Corrosion rates of WAAM-treated samples
were found to be 0.18 – 0.20 mm year/C0 1, the repaired zone has
excellent corrosion resistance.
Although convincing results were achieved with the use of
WAAM to repair component defects, some studies have
shown[28] that the repaired zone tensile strength will decrease
with the increase of heat input and a coarse precipitated phase
will be formed in the semi-melting zone, and the failure location
of the repaired zone is concentrated in this zone. At present, heat
treatment is considered to be an effective way to solve the
aforementioned problems. Heat treatment can improve the
microstructure of the repaired zone and effectively enhance
the repaired zone ’s strength and durability. [29– 31] As far as
we know, less extensive research has been conducted on the
impact of heat treatment on the performance of the
Al6.5Cu2Ni0.3Ti0.5Zr0.25V alloy. Especially studies after defect
repair. Therefore, it is of great signi ﬁcance to heat treat the
repaired alloy to achieve the desired microstructure.
In this research, the tungsten inert gas (TIG) arc-directed
energy deposition method was used to repair the local defects
of casting heat-resistant Al6.5Cu2Ni0.3Ti0.5Zr0.25V alloy. The
microstructure evolution of the repaired zone before and after
heat treatment was analyzed, to investigate the mechanism of
the microstructure in the repaired zone of the heat treatment
state on the high-temperature properties.

γ phase can provide more obstacles to resist creep and improve
the stability of the repair zone at high temperatures.
