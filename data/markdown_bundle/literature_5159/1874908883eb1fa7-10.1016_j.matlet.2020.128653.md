abstract
A novel Fe40Co40Ni10Si10 high entropy alloy (HEA) was designed and fabricated via copper mold casting. It
is found that, this alloy is mainly composed of ﬁne lamella-like BCC phase, as well as minor droplet-like
FCC phase. This HEA exhibits excellent yield strength of ~1.1 GPa, compressive plasticity of ~38.3%, sat-
urated magnetization of 172 emu/g and Curie temperature of 1169 K, which has signiﬁcant improvement
compared to those of the reported ferromagnetic HEAs, indicating good comprehensive performance
combining excellent mechanical and magnetic properties. This study proposes a new approach for the
design of high-performance HEAs for functional applications.

1. Introduction
High entropy alloys (HEAs) are a class of newly developed
advanced metallic materials, which have unique phase formation
principle and outstanding mechanical properties as well as surface
properties [1–4]. HEAs were initially deﬁned as the alloys with ﬁve
or more principal elements [5], i.e., CrMnFeCoNi, CrFeCoNiAl, etc.,
but during the past decades, the boundary of HEAs has been signif-
icantly broadened, and various novel non-equimolar alloys have
been regarded as HEAs although they have signiﬁcant deviation
from the traditional deﬁnition, such as Fe 40Mn40Co10Cr10 [6].
Besides, the HEAs could not only exhibit crystalline structures,
but also be composed of amorphous phases [7]. In recent years,
the functional properties of HEAs, especially magnetic properties,
have attracted increasing attentions [8–13]. Several ferromagnetic
HEAs have been proved to exhibit good combination of mechanical
and magnetic properties, especially high plasticity and relatively
low coercivity ( Hc), i.e. FeCoNiAl0.2Si0.2, FeCoNiMn 0.25Al0.25,
FeCoNiCr0.2Si0.2, etc. [11–13]. However, although these reported
soft magnetic HEAs have good comprehensive performance in
these aspects, their yield strength (
ry) values are
generally < 350 MPa [11–13], which limits them to the applications
without severe stress, and their saturated magnetization ( Bs)
values still need to be largely improved compared to traditional
soft magnetic alloys utilized in such conditions, i.e., silicon steel
and Fe-based amorphous alloys, etc.. It is noticed that, high ry is
expected in the development of magnetic materials for the applica-
tion with stringent requirement, such as the rotors in advanced
high-speed motor [14], and based on previous studies, HEAs show
great potential to obtain excellent ry [15], therefore it deserves fur-
ther attentions on developing ferromagnetic HEAs with ultrahigh
ry that could be more competitive in challenging applications.
Moreover, excellent Bs and Curie temperature ( Tc) are also impor-
tant factors for the application of ferromagnetic materials due to
the realization of high power with small volume of the engine at
higher temperature. Therefore, the aim of this work is to design a
HEA with excellent combination of ultrahigh ry, Bs and Tc.
It is known that, large proportion of ferromagnetic Fe, Co, Ni
would generally lead to high Bs in HEAs [8,10], and according to
the theoretical investigation from Feng et al., Fe and Co could have
better effect on enhancing Bs compared to Ni due to their larger
magnetic moment values [16]. Moreover, formation of BCC phase
instead of FCC phase is beneﬁcial for enhancing strength in the
HEAs mainly composed of Fe, Co and Ni [10,17], and according to
the studies from Guo et al. on valence electron concentration
(VEC), it is deduced that decrease in Ni would lower the VEC value
in ferromagnetic HEAs, which may be beneﬁcial for the formation
of BCC phase [18]. In addition, Fe and Co have higher Tc compared

2. Experimental
Alloy ingots with nominal composition of Fe 40Co40Ni10Si10 were
synthesized via induction melting the mixtures of raw materials
(purities above 99.9 wt.%) under a high-purity argon atmosphere.
Rod-shape specimens ( U2 /C230, in mm) were produced via
copper-mold casting under high-purity argon atmosphere. An
Empyrean X-ray diffractometer (XRD) was utilized for testing crys-
tal structure. A FEI QUANTA-200 scanning electron microscope
(SEM) was employed to observe the back-scattered image of the
cross-section of the specimen, and the Helios G4 CX was used for
electron back-scattered diffraction (EBSD) test. A Shimadazu AG-
IC 20/50KN testing machine was utilized for examining compres-
sive mechanical properties of rod specimens ( U2 /C24, in mm),
and at least 5 specimens were tested. A Lakeshore 7404 vibrating
sample magnetometer (VSM) was used for the test of hysteresis
loop of the specimens. A TGA5500 thermogravimetric analyzer
(TG) with magnetic ﬁeld was utilized for testing Tc of the rod
specimens.
3. Results and discussion
Fig. 1 (a) and (b) display XRD pattern and SEM image of the
cross-section of Fe 40Co40Ni10Si10 rod specimen, respectively. From
XRD result, sharp Bragg peaks corresponding to BCC phase are
clearly observed, while a weak FCC peak could be identiﬁed. More-
over, the specimen shows dendrite + droplet-like inter-dendrite
structure, and from the EDS results (listed in Table 1 ), the inter-
dendrite region is slightly enriched in (Ni, Si), while the dendrite
has higher Fe and Co concentrations compared to those of the
inter-dendrite region.
Fig. 2(a) and (b) show phase map and IPF image of Fe 40Co40Ni10-
Si10 rod specimen taken from EBSD, respectively. Based on Fig. 2(a),
this specimen is mainly composed of BCC phase, and minor FCC
phase locates between BCC phase, indicating that the dendrite
and droplet-like inter-dendrite regions shown in Fig. 1 (b) have
BCC and FCC structure, respectively. As shown in Fig. 2 (b), the
BCC dendrites are composed of ﬁne lamella structure with the
width of ~1 lm and length of several micrometers. Fig. 2 (c) and
(d) plot orientation difference between neighbored grains, and
there is 50–60 /C176misorientation between BCC lamellas, indicating
that such structure may be caused by twinning during cooling
[21], while low-angel boundaries located in the FCC or BCC phase
are possibly sub-grain boundaries in Fig. 2 (a).

According to the results above, Fe 40Co40Ni10Si10 has excellent
mechanical properties including ultrahigh ry and good ep, as well
as outstanding magnetic properties including very high Bs and Tc.
It is noticed that, except almost fully BCC structure of this alloy,
the formation of very ﬁne lamella structure possibly contributes
to ultrahigh ry of this HEA according to Hall-Petch correlation
(ry = r0 + kd-1/2, where r0 is frictional stress, d represent the aver-
age grain size, and k is a constant) [24]. Besides, its good plasticity
is possibly caused by the low concentration of elements which
have strong interaction with Fe, Co and Ni (such as Si) [25]. Further,
this HEA shows high Bs that is comparable to various Fe-based
metallic glasses (MGs) and composites, and although it has rela-
tively lower ry than Fe-based MGs [26], its high ep may be an
advantage for further applications. In addition, it should be noticed
that its Hc is about 2.5 kA/m, and our future work would focus on
reducing Hc [27] to signiﬁcantly improve the soft magnetism of this
HEA.
4. Conclusion
In summary, a ferromagnetic Fe 40Co40Ni10Si10 HEA was
designed according to the strategy of increasing concentration of
elements which have high magnetic moment and could be
beneﬁcial for the formation of BCC phase. Our work shows that,
this HEA produced via copper mold casting displays excellent Bs
of 172 emu/g, Tc of 1169 K, ry of ~ 1.11 ± 0.02 GPa and ep
of ~ 38.3 ± 5.5%, demonstrating outstanding combination of mag-
netic and mechanical properties. This work provides a new
approach for further development of HEAs for functional
applications.
