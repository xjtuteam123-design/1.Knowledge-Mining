1  Introduction
Austenitic stainless steel has been widely used in different 
fields ranging from petroleum, chemistry, energy to elec -
trical power. At high temperature, mechanical property 
and behavior of the steel are generally dependent upon 
the temperature and time, thus the high-temperature 
 performance of austenitic stainless steel is a widespread 
concern, because the austenitic stainless steel can be used 
as heat-resistant steel in many situations. In order to 
achieve the numerical simulation of material performance 
and relevant parameters by computer, the key point is to 
obtain the model of numerical calculation. The construc -
tion of mathematical models based on the analyzing and 
sorting of the experimental results and relevant theories is 
an important job for material’ calculation design, which is 
of great significance to the engineering applications. Dai 
et al. [1–3] have been working in the construction of math-
ematic model for the calculation design of austenitic steel, 
and have solved some problems concerning the calcula-
tion models of design system of the austenitic stainless 
steel applied to engineering. Herein this paper presents 
the numerical simulation and calculation of the high- 
temperature performance of austenitic stainless steel.
2 Result and discussion
2.1  The effect of alloying agents on room 
temperature strength
The solution strengthening of austenite is different with 
that of ferrite. Solute atoms cause spherically symmetric 
distortions in face-centered cubic (FCC) crystal lattice, af-
fecting the austenitic stacking fault energy, and forming 
Suzuki atmosphere. Generally, the effect of alloy contents 
on the austenitic stainless steel is linear [4, 5]. Generally 
the strengthening influence of interstitial atoms N and C 
are the strongest, whereas the ferrite forming elements 
Mo, V and Si, as well as the substituted austenitic forming 
elements Mn and Co are the weakest. Ni element acts as 
the softener for the solid solution. [6, 7]
Currently there are a few of formulas for calculating 
the room-temperature strength of the austenitic stainless 
steel. Irvine [5] had analyzed the effect of alloy contents in 
88 types of 18Cr-8Ni austenitic stainless steel upon the 
room-temperature strength, and obtained an empiric 
formula with the method of statistical regression.
Theoretically, except alloy contents, the size of crystal 
grains, the number of twin crystals and the second-phases 
also take effect on the strength. However, because the 
matrix is FCC, the effect is weaker. Besides, data of the size 
of crystals and the number of twin crystals are not pro -
vided in the experimental results in most literatures. Ac -
cording to the universal international standard for heat- 
resistant austenitic stainless steel, grain sizes are generally

tion movement in macroscopically. The process of thermal 
activation follows Arrhenius equation, so Arrhenius law is

2.4 Discussion
It is taken into consideration that the solid solution struc-
ture of steel in room temperature is austenitic. The check-
ing calculation with the expression in Ref. [28, 29] shows 
that all austenitic stainless steel in high-temperature solid 
solution has no δ ferrite, and the martensite transforma-
tion points M
s and Mεs are both below room temperature.
The room-temperature strength of the austenitic steel 
depends mainly on C and N, and other substitutional ele-
ments are of little effect. The room-temperature strength 
of the steel is the function of alloying agents, and the 
strength of the steel reduces while its temperature rises, 
which is a change following exponential rule. Except for 
few data with big errors, the obtained expressions can be 
used to calculate the predicted strength of austenitic steel 
with different compositions and in different temperature 
ranges. Besides, some data outside the statistical samples 
are collected, and the calculated results are satisfactory, 
as presented in Table 2.
Because of some differences in the methods used by dif-
ferent researchers, there is a range of fluctuation in the re-
search data, which causes big errors in the calculated values 
from regression. The factors affecting material plasticity are 
many and complicated, so the errors between calculated 
values and test values are greater than those of strength.
