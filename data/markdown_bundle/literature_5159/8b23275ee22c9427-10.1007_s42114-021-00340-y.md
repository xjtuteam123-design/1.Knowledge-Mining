Abstract
Both the dual-phase and multi-phase high-entropy alloys show excellent mechanical properties. The dual-phase FeCoCrMn 
alloy consisted of alternating lamellar face-centered-cubic (FCC) and sigma (σ) phases was developed in this work. The FCC 
and σ phase were identified to be FeCoMn-rich and Cr-rich phase, respectively. The alloy showed high compression yield 
strength of 1431 MPa at room temperature, which was attributed to the specific lamellar structure and high volume fraction 
σ phase (46%). The ductility was limited (compression strain was about 1%), due mainly to the inherent brittleness of σ 
phase. The compression properties at elevated temperatures were also evaluated. The yield strength and compression strain 
at 400 °C were 758 MPa and 12.6%, respectively. The yield strength at 800 °C was 181 MPa. The strengthening mechanisms 
of the alloy at different temperatures were discussed based on the microstructural characterization. The improved thermal 
activation process and the strength decline of both the FCC and σ phases at the elevated temperatures may be responsible 
for the decreased yield strength and increased ductility. In addition, the comparison of yield strength between FeCoCrMn 
alloy and other high-entropy alloys was also made.
Keywords High-entropy alloys · Microstructure · Mechanical Properties · Deformation mechanisms
1 Introduction
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) have obtained considerable 
attention due to their exciting structures and excellent prop-
erties [1, 2]. The severe lattice distortion effect and sluggish 
diffusion effect in HEA systems give the alloy structural sta-
bility and good mechanical properties [3–5]. The mechanical 
properties had a strong relationship with the crystal struc -
ture, microstructure, and the compositions of the HEAs 
[6]. The microstructure usually depends on the constituent 
elements of the alloy [ 7]. The additions of aluminum (Al) 
[8–13] and copper (Cu) [14, 15] into the HEA matrix were 
found to be effective in increasing the alloy strength.
In the original HEA design concept, phase separation has 
been regarded as an undesired phenomenon as the configu -
rational entropy is insufficient for stabilizing a single solid-
solution state. Also, the alloy with a body-centered-cubic 
(BCC) structure generally exhibits high strength but limited 
ductility. In contrast, the alloy with a face-centered-cubic 
(FCC) structure shows excellent ductility but low strength. 
Thus, alloys consisting of FCC and BCC could obtain good 
strength and ductility combinations. For example, the eutec-
tic HEAs consisted of FCC (L12) and BCC (B2) phases 
exhibited a remarkable combination of the ultimate tensile 
strength and ductility [16–21]. Based on the transformation-
induced ductility-assisted (TRIP) effect [ 22], Li et al. [ 23, 
24], combining the solid-solution strengthening effect and 
the TRIP effect, developed a novel HEA Fe50Mn30Co10Cr10, 
which successfully realized both the excellent strength and 
ductility [25]. As reported by Zaddach et al. [26], the stack-
ing fault energy (SFE) was a key atomistic quantity deter -
mining the TRIP phenomenon. Low SFE FCC alloys were

HEAs were relatively low [ 26]. The SFE of the HEAs can 
be lower if the content of high-SFE-element Ni reduced. The

Moreover, SFE was seriously temperature-dependent. In 
other words, the TRIP HEAs could exhibit excellent prop -

hardening behavior when aged at intermediate temperatures 
because of the formation of the sigma phase ( σ) [28].
Similar to conventional alloys, the strength of most HEAs 
decreased with increasing temperature, and a rapid decrease 
in the strength of HEAs frequently occurred at temperatures 
above ~ 0.6 Tm, where Tm was the melting temperature [29, 
30]. However, due to the limited-slip plane, the dislocations 
piled up at the phase boundaries and were hard to slip or 
cross-slip. Hence, the yield strength of dual BCC phases 
HEA V35Ti35Fe15Cr10Zr5 increased with increasing the tem-
perature below 500 °C but with limited ductility [ 31]. This 
alloy also showed reasonable ductility of more than 12% 
beyond 700 °C, due to thermal activation. The results above 
indicated that the mechanical performance of alloys mainly 
depended on their strengthening mechanisms. The remark -
able ability of strength retention at elevated temperatures 
could enhance the mechanical properties of HEAs and make 
them have a potential for structural applications.
In order to achieve an HEA with strength and ductility 
combination, a dual-phase FeCoCrMn HEA was fabri -
cated in this paper. The compression properties of the alloy 
were evaluated at elevated temperatures up to 800 °C. The 
strengthening mechanisms of the alloy at different tem -
peratures were discussed. The comparison of yield strength 
between FeCoCrMn alloy and other HEAs was also made.
2 Experimental
The four-component equimolar FeCoCrMn ingot was pre -
pared by arc-melting and subsequently furnace-cooled. The 
purity of elements was more than 99.9 wt.%. Ingots with a 
weight of 30 g were remelted at least five times to ensure 
chemical homogeneity [32]. Electrical discharge machining 
wire cutting and polishing were used to prepare the samples 
for microstructural characterization and mechanical property 
evaluation.
The crystal structure of the as-cast alloy was analyzed 
by X-ray diffraction (XRD), ranging from 30 to 100° with 
a speed of 2°/min. Optical microscopy (OM), scanning 
electron microscope (SEM, Sigma-Zeiss Gemini 500), and 
transmission electron microscope (TEM, Tecnai G2 F30 
S-TWIN) were used for microstructure analysis. The micro-
composition was analyzed by energy dispersive spectrometry 
(EDS). The fracture surface of compressive samples was 
also examined by SEM operated in the secondary electron 
mode. The volume fraction of phases formed in the alloy 
was estimated from SEM and TEM images taking on five 
different regions (by point counting) and assuming that this 
was the same as the volume fraction, using the IPWIN60 
software. Mechanical properties were characterized by 
compression test in a universal material testing machine 
(Shimadzu AG–X 100KN), from room temperature (RT) 
to 800 °C, with a strain rate of 2.5 × 10−3  s−1 [33, 34]. The 
surface of compression sample was polished with sandpaper 
up to a surface roughness was about 5 μm. The diameter and 
height of the compression sample were 3 mm and 6 mm, 
respectively. The samples were prepared by ion milling for 
the TEM observation to evaluate the deformation behavior.

of the phase is indexed to be σ phase, which is in agreement 
with previous reports [ 35]. The σ phase has a tetragonal

3.2 Microstructure
The microstructures of as-cast FeCoCrMn alloy obtained 
by OM, SEM, and TEM are shown in Fig. 2. A uniform and 
fine lamellar microstructure characteristic were observed in 
the optical image (Fig.  2a), similar to the microstructures 
reported by others [ 36, 37]. The interlamellar spacing was 
about 1 μm. The alternating lamellar phases showed a clear 
compositional contrast, corresponding to FCC/σ dual-phase 
constitution from XRD results. The detailed chemical com-
positions of FCC lamellae and σ lamellae regions by SEM 
Energy Dispersive Spectrometer (SEM-EDS) are listed in 
Table  1. The alternating plates of the σ and FCC phases 
were formed in the alloy (Fig.  2b). Fe and Mn distributed 
relatively uniformly in both FCC and σ regions. The σ lamel-
lae were found to contain much more Cr content than the 
FCC lamellae. The Cr is one of the two main elements (Cr 
and Mo) for the forming of σ phase [38]. The FCC lamellae 
contained more Co elements.
The TEM images, selected-area electron diffraction 
(SAED) patterns, and the chemical compositions of two 
selected grains obtained by TEM-EDS are given in Fig. 2c. 
The parallel-lath-like dual-phase microstructures were 
observed clearly. The thickness of the lath was about 0.2 μm. 
The volume fraction of σ phase was calculated to be around 
46%. The EDS analysis revealed a composition of 31.98 at. 
% Cr, 22.10 at. % Fe, 22.69 at. % Co, and 23.20 at. % Mn, in 
σ phase. The content of Cr in this region was predominantly 
high. On the other hand, the content of Fe (26.82 at. %), Co 
(27.29 at. %), and Mn (25.36 at. %) was high in the FCC 
phase. Therefore, the SAED and TEM-EDS results showed 
that the Cr-rich phase was σ phase, and the FeCoMn-rich 
phase was FCC phase.
It has been reported that elemental separation was asso -
ciated with the mixing enthalpy and melting point of the 
elements [ 39]. As shown in Table  2, the mixing enthalpy 
between element pairs is negative, which indicates that 
they have the excellent ability to bond together. However, 
the mixing enthalpy between the Cr and Mn pair was 5 kJ/
mol, which is larger than that of other elemental pairs. 
Hence, Cr and Mn may repel each other during the solidi -
fication process. Based on the fact that the solidification 
order of elements was similar to their melting points, the 
Cr element solidified firstly while the Mn element was the 
last. Thus, the Cr element nucleated first from the liquid to 
form the Cr-rich phase at the beginning of solidification. 
Then, Co, Fe, and Mn elements subsequently solidified to 
form the FeCoMn-rich phase. In addition, the slow diffu -
sion rate of the Cr element also played an essential part 
in the formation of the Cr-rich phase [ 40]. As reported by 
Christofidou et al. [ 35], Ni was found to affect the phase 
stability of CrMnFeCoNix HEAs significantly. It performs 
as a suppressing element on the σ phase formation process 
in the HEAs system.

The lamellar structure has been observed in many 
alloys. For example, the TiAl alloys exhibit a fully lamel -
lar structure, which forms in the α-matrix during cooling 
after a high temperature (> 1300 °C) heat treatment via the 
α → L(α/γ) → L(α2/γ) [41]. The Blackburn relationship is 
noticed between the α-TiAl (hexagonal close-packed, HCP) 
and γ-TiAl (FCC), which indicates the appearance of α/γ 
lamellar structure formed by eutectoid transformation [ 42]. 
According to the intercritical annealing and subsequent 
warm rolling, a lamellar structured ultrafine grain dual-phase 
steel was obtained from the low-carbon martensite steel [43]. 
The ultrafine structure is composed of alternate ferrite and 
martensite strips (i.e., lamellar structure) paralleled to the 
rolling direction, and the strips are composted of very fine 
grains in an average size of 0.96 μm. In the HEAs systems, 
a two-phase microstructure with nano lamellar eutectic mor-
phology has been investigated in a cobalt-base HEA. In the 
present FeCoCrMn alloy, the alternating lamellar structure 
was observed in as-cast alloy. The eutectic composition of 
the lamellar structure alloy should be evidenced. The forma-
tion mechanism of the two alternating lamellar phases in 
the as-cast FeCoCrMn alloy should be investigated deeply 
in the future.
In Cr-containing HEAs, the formation of σ phase could 
be predicted by the average valence electron concentration 
(VEC) parameter, which was proposed by Tsai et al. [ 44]. 
Alloys having VEC values between 6.88 and 7.84 are prone 
to σ phase formation either in the as-cast state or during 
aging at suitable temperatures. In addition, element Ni has a 
significant effect on the phase stability of the CrMnFeCoNix. 
Ni could suppress the formation of σ phase, and σ phase 
was observed in CrMnFeCo during solidification [ 35]. As 
far as our alloy FeCoCrMn is concerned, the VEC was 7.5. 
Besides, element Ni was not added to our alloy. According 
to the result reported above references, the formation of σ 
phase is highly possible in our alloy. In order to control the 
content of FCC and σ phase for our alloy, it could be realized 
by adjusting the content of alloying element or addition of 
Ni into the alloy. However, controlling the content of FCC 
and σ phase was beyond the scope of this paper.
3.3 Compression properties
The compression engineering stress-strain curves of the 
FeCoCrMn alloy from room temperature (RT) to 800 °C 
are shown in Fig.  3, and the related properties are sum -
marized in Table  3. The alloy exhibited high compression 
yield strength (1431 MPa) but limited ductility (compression 
strain was about 1%) at RT. The sample fractured under the 
stress of 1613 MPa and formed a brittle fracture surface. 
The high yield strength was attributed to the specific lamel-
lar structure and high volume fraction σ phase (46%). The 
limited ductility was due mainly to the inherent brittleness 
of σ phase.
The strength of the alloy decreased with increasing tem -
perature; for example, the compression yield strength was 
758 MPa at 400 °C. The compression uniform strain rose 
from 1 to 12.6%. At 500 °C, the yield strength was 648 MPa, 
and that at 600 °C was 475 MPa. On the other hand, with 
increasing the temperature, the samples had no fracture 
after the compression strain reached more than 12.4%. The 
higher deformation temperature might increase the effects 
of dynamic softening due to the improved thermal activation 
process [ 45]. The strength decline of both the FCC and σ

The relatively low strength and high ductility beyond 500 °C

alloy decreased compression strength and increased ductility

The comparison of yield strength between the FeCo -
CrMn alloy and the HEAs randomly consisting of several 
elements from Fe, Co, Cr, Mn, and Ni is given in Fig.  4. 
The data of yield strength were obtained from the refer -
ences [46]. The yield strength of the FeCoCrMn alloy was 
much higher than that of other four-component alloys in 
the test temperature range. These remarkable mechanical 
properties are due to its dual-phase lamellar structure, 
mixed with fine FeCoMn-rich (FCC) and hard Cr-rich 
(sigma) phases. Thus, more phase boundaries and smaller 
fine grains resulted in a lesser area and more blocks 
for dislocation moving, which could enhance the yield 
strength of the FeCoCrMn alloy. The deformation behav -
ior is sensitive to the modes of dislocation slip within the 
crystals during the forming process. Several researches 
have shown that dual-phase interfaces in nanosized lami -
nated metals can be the sources or sinks for dislocations 
[47], and hence, the material mechanical properties can 
be improved through specific interactions with other dis -
locations and defects, like twins and vacancies, within the 
crystals.
To observe the morphology of the deformed micro -
structure and fully understand the mechanisms of the 
strength-ductility combination for the FeCoCrMn alloy at 
elevated temperatures, TEM experiments were carried out 
for the samples deformed at RT, 400 °C, and 800 °C, and 
the results are given in Fig.  5. Dislocations are distributed 
obviously in the FCC phase. The interaction between dis -
locations was evident with a relatively high dislocation 
density at RT (Fig.  5a), indicating that the dislocations 
were confined and glide hardly between two hard lamel -
lar σ phases during deformation. Dislocations piled up 
and formed dense stacking faults at the phase boundaries, 
making the dislocation hard to slip or cross-slip. Besides, 
several twinning was found in the FCC phase, which may 
increase the flow stress by blocking mobile dislocations. 
The presence of twinning could impede the slip of dislo -
cations and then enhance the strength. Hence, the alloy 
exhibited high compression strength but limited ductility 
at RT.
The formation of twinning is usually observed in the 
alloys with low stacking faults energy (SFE), and twinning 
usually forms at the low temperature of ~ 0.3 Tm (Tm is the 
melting point). According to the result of differential scan -
ning calorimetry (DSC), the melting point of the FeCoCrMn 
alloy was 1350 °C. So, the twinning might be generated at 
about 400 °C. It can be seen from Fig.  5b that, at 400 °C, 
the distribution of the dual phase was not uniform. Dense 
dislocations gathered at the σ phase and caused the forma -
tion of stacking faults. Hence, the alloy maintained relatively 
high strength and great ductility with increasing the tem -
perature. However, due to the effect of thermal activation 
and the strength decline at elevated temperatures, disloca -
tions were easy to move and distributed uniformly within 
the phases at 800 °C. The energy of plastic deformation was 
absorbed by the σ regions and increased the alloy ductility. 
Therefore, the formation of stacking faults was considered 
the reason for the ductility increase in the FeCoCrMn alloy. 
The mechanism changed from dislocation-mediated ductility 
to nano-twinning, which contributed to increased ductility 
and relatively high strength at high temperatures.

4 Fracture surface
Compression curves revealed the limited plastic deforma -
tion at RT and 400 °C. Macroscopically, it was noticed that 
the fracture occurred along the middle axis (the cracks grow 
from top to bottom of the sample) at RT, while it exhibited 
a typical shear fracture mode with a shearing angle of 45° 
at 400 °C. As shown in Fig.  6a, the fracture surfaces are 
river-like patterns, corresponding to the typical cleavage-
fracture characteristics. The fracture surface of the brittle 
σ phase should be a smooth plane. The red dotted lines 
represent the outline of the σ phase. The insert in Fig.  6a is 
the enlarged view of the fracture surfaces, corresponding 
to the BSE image in Fig.  2b. The yellow arrows represent 
the microcracks on the σ phase. It is evident that the frac -
tures mainly occurred in the sigma phase at RT. During the 
fracture process, cracks were nucleated at one end of the 
σ phase and then propagated to the other end by leaving a 
river-like fracture surface. This fracture mechanism resulted 
in high strength but limited ductility at RT. In Fig.  6b, it 
was obvious to notice the tongue-like patterns, with a more 
prominent ductile feature. At 400 °C, cracks mainly nucle -
ated at the phase boundaries and then propagated along the 
outline of the σ phase boundaries. Additionally, some local 
ductility zones were generated during the fracture process, 
and this trend could improve the ductility of the alloy to 
some extent.
It is found that the cleavage fracture dominates the fracture 
at RT, while the fracture at 400 °C is the dimple-like struc -
ture, which is attributed to more plastic deformation work at 
400 °C.

5 Conclusions
In this paper, the microstructure, compression proper -
ties, and deformation mechanism of a newly developed 
FeCoCrMn high-entropy alloy were investigated, and the 
conclusions could be drawn as follows.
(1) The dual-phase FeCoCrMn alloy was composed of 
alternating lamellar FCC and σ phases. The FCC and σ 
phase were confirmed to be the FeCoMn-rich phase and 
Cr-rich phase, respectively.
(2) The alloy showed high yield strength of 1431 MPa 
but limited ductility at room temperature. The yield 
strength decreased with the increase of temperature, 
while the ductility remained adequate at elevated tem -
peratures.
(3) Brittle fracture was occurred at room temperature for 
the alloy during compression test, while ductile fracture 
feature at elevated temperatures was observed.
