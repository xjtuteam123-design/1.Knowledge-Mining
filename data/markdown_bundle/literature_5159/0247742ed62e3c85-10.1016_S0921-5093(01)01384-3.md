Superplastic behaviour of ultraﬁne-grained Ti–6A1–4V alloys

Abstract
Superplastic behavior of the ultraﬁne-grained (UFG) Ti–6A1–4V alloy produced by high pressure torsion (HPT) has been
studied. High elongations (more than 500%) have been observed in this alloy during tensile tests at relatively low temperatures
and high strain rates. At the same time, the superplastic behavior of this alloy has several speciﬁc features such as the relatively
low values of strain rate sensitivity of ﬂow stress and signiﬁcant strain hardening. Moreover, it was shown that the alloy,
processed by HPT, demonstrates an outstanding room temperature strength about 1500 MPa after superplastic deformation.

1. Introduction
The possibility of obtaining ultraﬁne-grained materi-
als (UFG) with grain size less than 1 /p109m has great
interest for investigations on superplasticity. It is well
known that superplasticity is often characterized using
the generalized constitutive equation [1]:

development of methods of severe plastic deformation
(SPD) for processing of UFG materials is very impor-
tant. These methods are advantageous in forming UFO
structure in bulk materials including commercial metal-
lic alloys and intermetallics [2–5].
In the last few years the enhanced superplasticity
associated with low temperature and /or high strain

and also by equal channel angular extrusion (ECAE)
has been demonstrated. Recently the current authors
have seen the potential for signiﬁcant improvement of
superplastic characteristics in severely deformed UFO
Ti–6A1–4V alloy [11]. In the present work, we report
the results on mechanical behavior of this superplastic
alloy, data on its structural investigations that are
important for understanding the mechanisms for en-
hanced superplasticity, and an analysis of the inﬂuence
of superplastic deformation on the mechanical proper-
ties of this alloy.

Another feature of the UFG alloy after superplastic
deformation is the absence of any voids and we could
not detect any cavitation after high elongation even
before fracture. However, it is well known that a cavita-
tion is common process during usual superplastic defor-
mation [17]. Perhaps the enhanced grain boundary
diffusion that has been observed in such UFG mi-
crostructure [3] acts to relax the local stress concentra-
tion that is needed to nucleate cavities at the grain
boundaries. Or the sizes of cavities were below the
detection limit and fracture occurred before they
coalesced.
4. Discussion
The obtained results demonstrate that high super-
plastic elongations have been observed in the Ti –6A1–
4V alloy processed by HPT during tensile tests at
relatively low temperatures and high strain rates. At the
same time, the superplastic behavior of this alloy has
several speci ﬁc features. Let us consider them in more
detail.
First of all, it is necessary to note that according to
TEM investigations the HPT process leads to strong
reﬁnement of microstructure. It has been reported that
materials subjected to SPD by HPT are internally
strained and contain a high density of different types of
lattice defects [3]. Formation of well-de ﬁned UFG mi-

microstructural aspects associated with formation of

At the same time, the observed values of strain rate
sensitivity in the alloy, especially at high strain rates
(10
−2 –10−1 s−1) where they are in the range of 03 –0.4
(Fig. 6), are signiﬁcantly lower than for usual superplas-
ticity where they are in the range of 0.5 –0.6 [12 –14]. It
is well known, that high strain rate sensitivity of ﬂow
stress is necessary for stable ﬂow and high elongations
[15,16]. However, how it is shown above, the high
elongations (more than 500%) have also been achieved
in investigated Ti –6A1–4V alloy after HPT. Such un-
usual superplastic behavior of the alloy at the reduced
strain rate sensitivity can most likely be explained by
taking into account signi ﬁcant hardening of the alloy
during the deformation. According to Hart ’s suggestion
[18] the stability of ﬂow can be achieved by increasing
the strain rate sensitivity of theﬂow stress as well as from
strain hardening. Presumably, both of these factors favor
high elongations in UFG alloy during this deformation
at relatively low temperatures and high strain rates.
An important aspect of microstructural evolution in
the Ti–6A1–4V alloy, processed by severe plastic defor-
mation, is the very high strength obtained after super-
plastic deformation, which is outstanding for this alloy.
Most microcrystalline alloys on the other hand demon-
strate a decrease in strength at room temperature after
superplastic deformation because of strain induced grain
growth or cavitation [13,16]. It is possible that this
increase in room temperature strength after superplastic
deformation in this UFG Ti alloy is associated with lack
of cavitation as well as with the smallness of the retained
grain size. However, this aspect needs to be investigated
fully in our ongoing future investigation.
