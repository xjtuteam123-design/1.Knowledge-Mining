abstract
In order to assess Al–Si alloy as potential high temperature structural material, the tensile and wear
properties of Al–Si alloy was investigated at various temperatures from ambient to 350◦ C. Al–Si alloy was
fabricated by stir casting technique. Microstructural studies showed that reasonably uniform distribution
of silicon particles. It was found that the tensile strength (strain rate = 10
−3 s−1 ) of alloy was decreasing
with increase of temperature. The ﬂow stress also decreased with increase of deformation temperature.
The effect of temperature on tensile behaviour of the alloy had been evaluated and fractographs revealed
that fracture behaviour of Al–Si alloy changed from brittle to ductile mode with increase of temperature.
The increase in wear resistance of Al–Si alloy with increasing temperature was observed from wear tests.
The oxidational wear was predominantly occurred during sliding.

1. Introduction
Aluminum silicon alloy is widely used as a material for sliding
components in automobile, aviation and other sectors. The signif-
icant research work on Al–Si alloy matrix composites have been
carried out to reduce the weight of engines and to improve the tri-
bological properties of such composites [1]. The weight reduction
has become key issue for automotive manufacturers. Al–Si alloys
with low silicon can be used with cast iron cylinder liners in auto-
motive engine blocks due to less weight, intrinsic low wear and
scufﬁng resistance [2].
Al–Si alloy is a well-known cast alloy with high wear resistance,
low thermal expansion coefﬁcient, good corrosion resistance and
improved mechanical properties at a wide range of temperatures.
Silicon imparts high ﬂuidity and low shrinkage, which result in
good castability and weldability. Silicon additions also improve
casting characteristics by improving feeding and hot tear resis-
tance. The silicon-rich phase is hard, so the hardness of the alloy is
increased with Si content. Due to high silicon content along with the
uniform distribution of the primary silicon in the microstructure,
improved wear resistance is achieved, making the alloy particu-
larly suitable for use as engine components, such as engine blocks.
The wear rate and friction coefﬁcient depend strongly on the testing
method, testing environments, testing variables, alloy manufactur-
ing route and the alloy constituents. Many researchers reported the
importance of oxidation during wear of metallic materials and the
role of oxide scale [3]. The wear resistance was lower in the case of
Al–Si alloy and Al–Si alloy reinforced with large Si or SiC particu-

main impurity element present in aluminum silicon alloy and it
produces deleterious effects on ductility and corrosion resistance
of the alloy [13].

2.3.1. Tensile and hardness tests
Tensile test at different temperatures was conducted as per

ried out by universal hydraulic testing machine (Model: DARTEC)

which the material begins to deform plastically, was determined
by ﬁnding the point where a line parallel to the linear region, offset

the tensile tested specimens were studied with the help of scan-

tests were conducted in accordance with ASTM E10-00 standards
using a Brinell hardness testing machine. The hardness test was
conducted using a steel ball indenter of 10 mm diameter and load

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructural observations
Fig. 1 shows the microstructure of Al–Si alloy with appropri-
ate modiﬁcation treatment. The effect of Al–5Ti–1B master alloy
as modiﬁer is clearly observed from the microstructure of Al–Si
alloy (Fig. 1). This master alloy contains a large number of insolu-
ble boride particles, which effectively nucleate a great number of
aluminium grains.
The modiﬁer nucleates the silicon to permit a ﬁne distribution
of the primary crystals. The addition of modiﬁer suppressed the
growth of silicon crystals within the eutectic, providing a ﬁner
distribution of lamellar relative to the growth of the eutectic.
The precipitated primary silicon particles embedded in a eutectic
matrix consisting of continuous phases of aluminum and silicon.
Silicon plates are fragmented to ﬁne particles and distributed uni-
formly throughout the matrix. The hardness of the alloy is found to
be 97 BHN.

3.3. Wear analysis with varying temperature
The inﬂuence of temperature on wear rate of Al–Si alloy in iden-
tical testing condition is shown in Fig. 8. It is observed from the plot
that the wear rate is decreased with increasing temperature. Al–Si
alloy is weakened structurally beyond 250
◦C and hence the test
was stopped due to gross damage of pin at 300 ◦C. The formation of
oxide ﬁlm at high temperature reduces the wear rate by avoiding
direct metal to metal contact. The oxide ﬁlm is removed due to con-
tinuous sliding action which results in direct metal to metal contact
and exposing new areas to environment. Tearing and formation
of oxide layer is a continuous process which results in decrement
of wear rate when the temperature is increased. There is another
mechanism possible for the reduction of wear rate of alloy when
the temperature is increased. The agglomerated clusters of oxide
wear debris produced due to tearing of oxide layers during sliding
of surfaces are subjected to thermal stresses and compaction by
applied pressure at high temperature.
At the same time, the sintering of ﬁne wear debris is also
occurred due to temperature and applied pressure. The rate of sin-
tering is increased with increase of the temperature which results
in formation of solid smooth hard surfaces, termed as ‘glazes’ [12].
The glaze layers protect the sliding surfaces by long time and the
wear rates reduced. The failure of glaze layer leads to formation
of oxide layer and tearing of oxide layer, which results the wear
debris again sintered and process is repeated. It is evident from the
wear trends the glazing layer effect is not inﬂuenced at room tem-
perature. Fig. 9 shows the effect of sliding distance with coefﬁcient
of friction of alloy. Before attaining the stable region, a running-in
period duration is observed before the stable value of coefﬁcient
of friction followed by strong ﬂuctuation in the friction coefﬁcient
during some initial sliding distance.

4. Conclusions
The high temperature behaviour of Al–Si alloy was studied and
the results are summarized as follows:
• The tensile strength and ﬂow stress of Al–Si alloy is decreasing
with the increase in temperature.
• The % of elongation of Al–Si alloy is decreased till the tempera-
ture is 200
◦C and then increasing with increase in temperature.
This is due to the segregation of impurities in the grain bound-
aries which is causing brittleness with increase in temperature.
This ductility reduction at elevated temperature is due to grain
boundary cavitations. The iron impurity also reduces the tensile
strength with increase of temperature.
• The strain hardening exponent ( n) value is decreasing with the
increasing temperature due thermal softening phenomenon.
• The tensile fractographs of Al–Si alloy tested up to 200
◦C shows a
brittle transcrystalline fracture. The slightly partial mixed mode
is identiﬁed at a temperature of 250
◦C. The fracture behaviour
of Al–Si alloy changes from brittle to ductile mode with till the
temperature reaches 250 ◦C. The ductile fracture occurs more
frequently with an increasing temperature, associated with an
increased dimple density.
• Wear resistance of the alloy is increased linearly with increasing
the operating temperature. This effect is due to the oxide ﬁlm
and glazing layer formation on sliding components more rapid at
higher operating temperatures. These layers prevent the direct
metal to metal contact of sliding surfaces during sliding.
• The alloy sustain under identical sliding condition up to 250
◦C,
beyond this temperature, the pin sample is squeezed out from
the disc during test.
• SEM micrographs of worn surface clearly support the wear mech-
anisms of alloy. Wear mechanism of alloy at temperatures less
than 100
◦C is delamination followed by partial abrasive wear
which leads plastic deformation. The mechanism of oxidative
wear dominates above 150
◦C for alloy at high temperature.
