1. Introduction
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) represent a groundbreaking class of 
advanced metallic materials that have garnered substantial attention for 
their exceptional properties including high strength and hardness, 
excellent structural stability, and enhanced corrosion resistance [1] . 
Unlike conventional alloy systems that based on a single principal 
element, HEAs are designed through combination of multiple principal 
elements (typically five or more), following the fundamental principles 
of configurational entropy [2,3] . This unique design approach enables 
the formation of stable single-phase solid solutions with relatively sim -
ple crystal structures, while exhibiting complex atomic scale in -
teractions that contribute to their exceptional material properties [4] .
The growing demand for advanced superalloys in power generation 
and aerospace applications has driven the development of novel mate -
rials with exceptional mechanical properties and superior high- 
temperature performance [5 – 7] . Refractory high-entropy alloys 
(RHEAs), which compose of high melting point elements (W, Ta, Mo, Hf, 
Nb, Cr, V, Zr, and Ti etc.), have emerged as promising candidates due to 
their remarkable strength and corrosion resistance under elevated 
temperatures [8,9] . In 2010, Senkov et al. [9] developed NbMoTaW and 
NbMoTaV RHEAs, which maintain a single-phase BCC structure at 
temperature up to 1600
◦
C while demonstrating superior 
high-temperature mechanical properties compared to conventional 
nickel-based alloys. However, subsequent RHEA systems such as 
TiZrHfNbTa and WNbMoTaV [10] face limitations in iindustrial appli -
cations due to their high density caused by the presence of numerous 
heavy elements (e.g., W, Ta). To address this challenge, researchers 
reduced the alloys density through the incorporation of lightweight el -
ements (Al, Ti, Zr, V, etc.). This approach has successfully generated of a 
large number of novel lightweight RHEAs, including CrNbTiZr [11] , 
AlNbTiV [12] , and AlNbTiZr [13 – 15] , with densities typically below 
8 g/cm³ and superior strength characteristics compared to traditional 
RHEAs.
Extensive studies [13,16,17] have established that AlNbTiZr RHEAs 
exhibit superior oxidation resistance, excellent wear resistance, and 
exceptional mechanical properties, positioning them as highly prom -
ising candidate material for advanced engineering applications.
