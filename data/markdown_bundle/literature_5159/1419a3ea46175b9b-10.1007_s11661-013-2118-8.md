Effect of Alloying Elements on Nb-Rich Portion of Nb-Si- X
Ternary Systems and In Situ Crack Observation of Nb-Si-Based
Alloys
SEIJI MIURA, TORU HATABATA, TAKUYA OKAWA, and TETSUO MOHRI
To ﬁnd a new route for microstructure control and to ﬁnd additive elements beneﬁcial for
improving high-temperature strength, a systematic investigation is performed on hypoeutectic
Nb-15 at. pct Si- X ternary alloys containing a transition element, Fe, Co, Ni, Cu, Ru, Rh, Pd,
Re, Os, Ir, Pt, or Au. Information on phase equilibrium is classiﬁed in terms of phase stability of
silicide phases, a Nb
5Si3,N b 4SiX,a n dN b3Si, and the relationship between microstructure and
mechanical properties both at room temperature and high temperature is investigated. All the
additive elements are found to stabilize either a Nb5Si3 or Nb 4SiX but destabilize Nb 3Si. A
microstructure of Nb ss/a Nb5Si3 alloy composed of spheroidized a Nb5Si3 phase embedded in
the Nb ss matrix is eﬀective for toughening, regardless of the initial as-cast microstructure. Also
the plastic deformation of Nb ss dendrites may eﬀectively suppress the propagation of longer
cracks. High-temperature strength of alloys is governed by the deformation of Nb ss phase and
increases with higher melting point additives.

I. INTRODUCTION
RECENTLY, Nb-Si alloys have attracted attention
as a candidate of high-temperature materials for replac-
ing Ni-based superalloys because they exhibit superior
high-temperature strength to the superalloys.
[1–6] As
shown in Figure 1, the Nb-Si binary system is composed
of several intermetallic compounds, which have suitable
properties for high-temperature use, such as high
melting points and high strength. Among them, a
Nb5Si3 is the most promising candidate as a phase
dispersed in Nb ss matrix for strengthening. However,
the lack of room-temperature ductility and high-tem-
perature oxidation resistance are major drawbacks of
Nb
ss/a Nb5Si3 two-phase alloys for practical applica-
tions. For improving room-temperature toughness of
the Nb ss/a Nb5Si3 two-phase alloys, a microstructure-
control technique has been proposed, which includes the
formation and decomposition of Nb 3Si phase, i.e.,a
eutectic reaction (L ﬁ Nbss+Nb3Si) and eutectoid
reaction (Nb 3Si ﬁ Nbss+a Nb5Si3),[7–9] which is sche-
matically shown in Figure 2 as ‘‘Route 1.’’ Fine a
Nb5Si3 lamellar plates are formed through the eutectoid
decomposition reaction of Nb 3Si, and then spheroidized
by extended heat treatment. It is well known that cast
iron with spherical graphite phase has many excellent
material properties such as tensile strength, toughness,
fatigue resistance, wear resistance, and others, which are
much better than that with ﬂake-like graphite. With a
similar microstructure composed of spheroidized a
Nb
5Si3 strengthening phase embedded in Nb ss matrix,
i.e., Nb ss network, an improvement of toughness is
expected. Therefore, the intermetallic phase Nb 3Si is the
key phase in this microstructure control (Route 1) that
was proposed for an alloy with a composition of Nb-
18.1 at. pct Si-1.5 at. pct Zr-100 ppm Mg previously
[7]
and applied for other several alloys.
For further improvement of mechanical properties, an
alternative route for microstructure evolution is pur-
sued. The addition of Mo and W were reported to form
typical eutectic microstructures composed of Nb ss/a
Nb5Si3 or Nb ss/b Nb5Si3.
[3,10–12] This implies that these
elements stabilize Nb ss and Nb 5Si3 phase ( a or b)
relative to Nb 3Si phase. Nb ss/a Nb5Si3 ﬁne eutectic
microstructure may turn into spheroidized microstruc-
ture because of a larger area of interphase boundaries in
eutectic lamellar structure than in spheroidized micro-
structure. This microstructure evolution may be alter-
native technique, called ‘‘Route 2’’ shown in Figure 2,
for obtaining a similar microstructure with the result of
Route 1, which is composed of spheroidized a Nb
5Si3
phase embedded in the Nb ss network. Such microstruc-
ture evolution is already implied in Nb-Si-Mo alloys. [11]
To extend the possibility of the microstructure control
of Nb-Si-based alloys through Route 2, the eﬀect of
additives on the as-cast microstructure and phase
equilibria at a certain temperature should be investi-
gated, as well as the mechanical properties. A part of the
periodic table is shown in Figure 3. The eﬀect of the
addition of elements such as Cr, Mo, W, Hf, and Ti
around Nb on the phase equilibrium and mechanical
properties of Nb-Si-based alloys has been investigated

II. EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURES
The composition of alloys including Fe, Co, Ni, Ru,
Rh, Pd, Re, Os, Ir, Pt, and Au as X were Nb-15 at. pct
Si-3 at. pct X. The composition of an alloy with Cu was
Nb-15 at. pct Si-1 at. pct Cu because of low solubility of
Cu in Nb ss. Additionally, alloys composed of Nb-15 at.
pct Si-6 at. pct X (X = Fe, Ni, and Co) were also
prepared.
Alloys were arc melted in Ar atmosphere on a water-
cooled copper hearth. To ensure the homogeneity, ingots
were turned and remelted at least ﬁve times. For the
investigation of phase equilibria in alloys, a part of ingots
were subjected to heat treatment at 1923 K (1650 /C176 C) for
100 hours in high-purity Ar-ﬂow atmosphere with Ta foil
wrapping. All samples were polished with Al
2O3 powder
(0.1 lm in diameter), and the microstructure was
observed using a scanning electron microscope (SEM;
JEOL-JXA-8900M; JEOL Ltd., Tokyo, Japan). The
composition of each constituent phase was analyzed by
using an electron probe microanalyzer (EPMA-WDX)
calibrated using pure elements. X-ray diﬀraction (XRD;
X’Pert MPD; Philips, Amsterdam, the Netherlands;
CuKa radiation, voltage of 40 kV and current of
40 mA) was also employed to investigate the crystal
structure of constituent phases. Diﬀerential thermal
analysis (DTA) was carried out on several alloys using
a Rigaku Thermoﬂex (Rigaku International Corp.,
Tokyo, Japan). A cylindrical specimen with approximate
diameter of 3 mm and length of 4 mm was heated up to
1773 K (1500 /C176 C) in an Al
2O3 crucible and cooled at a
rate of 0.17 K/s under Ar-ﬂow atmosphere.
As the ﬁrst step of investigation of the mechanical
properties, each alloy heat-treated at 1923 K (1650 /C176 C)
for 100 hours is subjected to a simple three-point
bending test that was performed on a sample with a
dimension of 0 :9 /C22:0 /C210:0 mm with a three-point
bending apparatus composed of a piezoelectric actuator
(N-214; Physik Instrumente GmbH & Co., Karlsruhe,
Germany) and a force sensor (type 9193; Kistler
Instrumente AG, Winterthur, Switzerland) under a
confocal laser scanning microscope (CLSM; type
1LM21, LaserTech Co. Ltd., Yokohama, Japan). Sup-
port pins and a load pin for three-point bending tests
were made of Al
2O3, and the spans between the support
pins are 8 mm. The speed of load point displacement is
1 lm/s. Deformation and crack propagation was
recorded by a video camera recorder with a video rate
of 30 frames/s attached to the CLSM. The applied load
for the bending test was recorded every 0.01 seconds for
room temperature (RT) testing or 0.1 seconds for
1473 K (1200 /C176 C) testing by a data acquisition system
(Yokogawa Electric Co., Tokyo, Japan). The tempera-
ture measurement by R-type thermocouple is calibrated
by observing the melting of an Au ball (0.3 mm in
diameter) in a slit groove at the top of the load pin while
heating the bending specimen.

Type 1 and Type 3 elements belong to the ﬁfth and
fourth period of the periodic table, respectively, and
Type 2 elements belong to the sixth. The elements are
classiﬁed in terms of the stability of silicide phases.
Generally, elements that belong to the same group, not
the same period, show a similar alloying behavior
because the number of the valence electrons has a large
eﬀect on the bonding between constituent elements. The
current results show that other factors such as atomic
size are dominant for the stability of phases.
In Type 1 alloys, a eutectic microstructure composed
of Nb
ss and Nb 3Si is found, essentially the same with
that of binary alloy. However, in these alloys, Nb 3Si
tends to decompose just in the as-cast condition,
suggesting that Ru, Rh, and Pd accelerate the eutectoid
decomposition Nb
3Si ﬁ Nbss + a Nb5Si3 by destabi-
lizing Nb3Si phase to some extent. On the other hand, a
eutectic microstructure composed of Nb ss and Nb 5Si3 is
found in Type 2 alloys. These elements are concluded to
stabilize Nb5Si3. In both Types 1 and 2, the similar Nb ss/a
Nb5Si3 microstructure is obtained in heat-treated alloys,
while in alloys containing Pt or Ir, there is a third phase
that is much brighter than Nb solid solution phase in
ﬁgures. From the phase composition and the result of
XRD patterning, those are concluded to be an A15-
Nb
3X (X = Pt or Ir) with a small amount of Si
substitution. Also in the heat-treated alloy with Os, a
thin dark phase in Nb
ss phase is observed. As a large
amount of oxygen (10 to 20 at. pct) is detected from the
area, the results of the heat-treated Os alloy might be
strongly aﬀected by oxygen contamination.
In Type 3 alloys including Co, Fe, and Ni, Nb
4SiX
compound is a matrix after the heat treatment and Nb
solid-solution phase tends to spheroidize. Other silicides
such as Nb
3Si and Nb 5Si3 are scarcely found in the heat-
treated alloys. At the edge of Nb 3Si matrix in as-cast
alloys, decomposition seems to start, suggesting the
destabilization of Nb 3Si phase by Fe, Co, and Ni. It is
also reported that Nb 4SiCu appears in Nb-Si-Cu ternary
system.[32] In the current study, the amount Cu is 1 at.
pct, much smaller than other alloys. The small addition
may be the reason why the Nb-Si-Cu alloy has Nb ss/a
Nb5Si3 two-phase microstructure after the heat treat-
ment. However, Cu should be classiﬁed into Type 3, not
into Type 1.
Based on the microstructure and chemical composi-
tion measurement by WDX, the Nb-rich portion of the
isothermal sections of Nb-Si- X at 1923 K (1650 /C176 C) is
evaluated as shown in Figure 5. Although the Os alloy is
heavily contaminated by oxygen, the evaluated phase
diagram is also shown. The additives X classiﬁed into
either Type 1 or Type 2 (Ru, Rh, Pd, Re, Os, Ir, Pt, and
Au) tend to show low solubility in a Nb
5Si3 phase. In
Type 3 alloys, the composition area of Nb 4SiX is found
to be much wider than the composition ranges for Fe,
Co, and Ni as reported previously, [24,28,30] which results
in large Nb/Nb 4SiX two-phase areas in these diagrams.
The solubility of Type 3 elements (Fe, Co, Ni, and Cu)
in Nb
ss is much smaller than that of Types 1 and 2
elements (Table I).
In alloys with Ir and Pt, Nb ss/a Nb5Si3/A15-Nb3X
three-phase equilibrium is found. The 1923 K (1650 /C176 C)
isothermal section of Nb-Si-Ir proposed by the current
study is similar to the 1473 K (1200 /C176 C) isothermal
section.[25] Both Nb 3Ir and Nb 3Pt show limited but not
negligible solubility of Si, and the amount of Nb is
almost kept to be constant. This result suggests that Si
substitutes for X. A similar behavior of Si solubility in
A15-Nb
3X phase was reported on Nb-Si-Sn system. [33] It
has been widely accepted that elements B and C occupy
the same atomic site in an ordered structure in a
continuous solid solution A
x(B, C) y existing along a
constant A composition line between two binary com-
pounds A xBy and A xCy in A-B-C ternary phase dia-
gram.[34] Metastable A15-Nb 3Si compound has been
reported to be produced by sputtering, [35,36] electron
beam evaporation,[37] chemical vapor deposition, [38] and
splat cooling (liquid quenching). [39] Therefore, Si may
play a role similar to that of X in A15-Nb 3X by
occupying the site for X.
B. Effect of Alloying Elements on Mechanical Properties
Figure 6 shows the results of bending tests of heat-
treated alloys at room temperature. No plastic defor-
mation region is observed and sudden cracking occurs at
the maximum value of the bending maximum stress. The
crack growth rate is evaluated to be faster than 30 mm/s
because the crack propagated by about 1 mm within 1
frame (video rate is 30 frames/s). The nonstraight crack
path was developed along the interdendritic region. It is
considered that the dendrites of ductile Nb
ss phase
suppress long crack development.
Alloys with Cu, Pd, and Au show larger maximum
stress than other alloys. The microstructure of these
alloys is distinguished by well-spheroidized a Nb5Si3
together with Nb ss network. Although the heat-treated
alloys show similar microstructure and mechanical
behavior, Pd is a Type 1 element while Au is a Type 2
element. (Although the element of Cu is classiﬁed into
Type 3 in the current study, the microstructure evolu-
tion of the Cu alloy is the same with that of Type 1
alloys.) This suggests that the obtained microstructure
governs the mechanical properties, regardless of the
initial as-cast microstructure, i.e., the route for micro-
structure evolution. The microstructure of alloys with Ir
and Pt is seemingly the same as that of alloys with Cu,
Pd, and Au, but brittle A15 phase connects a Nb
5Si3,
resulting in rather low connection of Nb ss network,
which might be a reason for lower maximum values of
bending strength. Similarly, alloys with Co, Fe, and Ni,
including brittle Nb
4SiX as the matrix, show relatively
low maximum stress. Also, the maximum stress of the
Ru alloy might be aﬀected by brittle a Nb
5Si3 matrix. It

IV. CONCLUDING REMARKS
A systematic investigation is performed on hypoeu-
tectic Nb-15 at. pct Si- X ternary alloys containing
transition elements, Fe, Co, Ni, Cu, Ru, Rh, Pd, Re, Os,
Ir, Pt, or Au. Information on phase equilibrium is
classiﬁed in terms of the stability of silicide phases, and
the relationship between mechanical properties and
microstructure is investigated.
The as-cast microstructures of alloys are found to be
classiﬁed into three categories:
Type 1 Nb ss=Nb3Si ! Nbss=aNb5Si3: Ru; Rh; Pd
Type 2 Nb ss=Nb5Si3 ! Nbss=aNb5Si3: Re; Os; Ir; Pt; Au
Type 3 Nb ss=Nb3Si ! Nbss=Nb4SiX: Fe; Co; Ni; Cu
The as-cast microstructures of Type 1 and Type 3
alloys include a eutectic microstructure composed of
Nb
ss and Nb 3Si, which is essentially the same with that
of binary alloy, but the decomposition rate of Nb 3Si is
accelerated by additive elements. A eutectic microstruc-
ture composed of Nb ss and Nb 5Si3 is found in Type 2
alloys. These elements are concluded to stabilize a
Nb5Si3 and destabilize Nb 3Si. The microstructure of
Type 3 alloys after the heat treatment is composed of
Nb
ss and Nb4SiX compound (X = Co, Fe, and Ni). As a
result, no additive elements stabilizing Nb 3Si are found.
The substitutional behavior of Si, Pt, and Ir in A15
structure is discussed. Type 1 and 2 elements are
predominantly partitioned into Nb
ss rather than Nb 5Si3
phases, while Type 3 elements are into Nb 4SiX.
A microstructure of Nb ss/a Nb5Si3 alloy composed of
spheroidized a Nb5Si3 phase embedded in the Nb ss
matrix is eﬀective for toughening, regardless of the
initial as-cast microstructure, i.e., the route for micro-
structure evolution. Also, the plastic deformation of
