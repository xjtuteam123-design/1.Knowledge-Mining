ABSTRACT
In this study, the CoCrFeNiMn
0.75
Cu
0.25 
high-entropy alloy (HEA) was systematically investigated to elucidate 
the effects of different annealing temperatures on the microstructure and mechanical properties of the face- 
centered cubic (fcc) structural alloy, renowned for its exceptional strength-ductility synergy. After annealing 
at 1273K, the HEA possesses an excellent combination of high strength (~700 MPa) and high plasticity (~45 %). 
This work establishes a temperature-dependent microstructural evolution mechanism in CoCrFeNiMn
0.75
Cu
0.25 
HEA, focusing on the Cr-rich precipitation and annealing twin formation. At lower annealing temperatures 
(973K), the precipitations impede dislocation motion, thereby enhancing the yield strength of HEA. In contrast, 
at higher annealing temperatures (1273K), the alloy develops a microstructure dominated by high-angle grain 
boundaries (HAGBs) (~92 %) with abundant annealing twins. In-situ electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) 
characterization was conducted on the 1073K and 1273K annealed samples to analyze microstructural evolution 
during tensile deformation. The plastic deformation of the rolled alloy after annealing at 1073K (CRA-1073K) is 
primarily governed by dislocation slip, characterized by a high slip band density and a rapid slip band expansion 
rate. In contrast, the CRA-1273K sample exhibited a lower initial slip band density and slower propagation rate in 
the early deformation stages. By leveraging in-situ EBSD characterization, this study not only elucidates the 
strengthening mechanisms and microstructural evolution of CoCrFeNiMn
0.75
Cu
0.25 
HEA, but also demonstrates 
the novelty of capturing real-time microstructural responses – such as slip band formation and twinning – during 
deformation. These insights provide a solid foundation for optimizing thermomechanical parameters to achieve a 
desirable strength-ductility synergy.
1. Introduction
High-entropy alloys (HEAs), comprising five or more principal ele -
ments with concentrations ranging from 5 at.% to 35 at.%, have 
garnered significant attention due to their exceptional properties. Since 
Yeh [ 1 ] and Cantor [ 2 ] introduced the concept of HEAs in 2004, these 
materials have become a focal point for both fundamental research and 
technological applications. HEAs exhibit superior mechanical, chemical, 
and physical properties compared to conventional metallic materials 
[ 3 – 7 ], spurring rapid advancements across diverse fields. The 
high-entropy effect promotes the formation of simple solid sol -
utions – such as body-centered cubic (bcc), face-centered cubic (fcc), and 
hexagonal close-packed (hcp) structures – while suppressing the devel -
opment of intermetallic compounds [ 8 – 10 ]. Among these, fcc-structured 
HEAs have received particular attention for their exceptional toughness, 
ductility, and corrosion resistance [ 11 – 13 ].
Although HEAs with fcc structures exhibit excellent ductility, their 
relatively low yield strength continues to limit their broader application 
in structural engineering and industrial settings [ 14 ]. To address this 
limitation, researchers have proposed several strengthening strategies 
[ 12 , 15 – 18 ], including alloying with non-metallic elements (e.g., car -
bon), designing dual-phase microstructures, and developing heteroge -
neous architectures to improve mechanical performance. Among these, 
thermomechanical processing has been identified as a simple and 
effective method to tailor the microstructure and mechanical properties 
of alloys without altering their original composition [ 19 – 22 ]. Thermo -
mechanical processing has been shown to induce the formation of 
annealed twins within the fcc phase, significantly refining the grain

high-entropy alloys [ 35 – 38 ]. For example, Yang et al. [ 39 ] studied the

and strain hardening. This behavior was attributed to the synergistic 
effects of phase boundaries (PBs), twin boundaries (TBs), and high-angle 
grain boundaries (HAGBs).
While the HEAs have garnered significant research attention 
[70 – 73] , a systematic understanding of thermomechanical processing 
effects on plastic deformation mechanisms in fcc HEAs, particularly 
those with heterogeneous microstructures, remains notably underde -
veloped. To clarified the mechanisms, this study employs in situ EBSD 
testing to dynamically track microstructural evolution and quantify 
defect evolution (including geometrically necessary dislocation density 
variations) during tensile deformation. We conducted systematic in -
vestigations into how thermomechanical treatment parameters influ -
ence the both microstructural characteristics and mechanical 
performance. The results elucidate the underlying strengthening 
mechanisms and microstructural evolution, offering a scientific foun -
dation for optimizing processing parameters and achieving a superior 
strength – ductility balance.

significantly affects the tensile properties of cold-rolled samples, 
consistent with findings on deformed and annealed CoCrFeMnNi HEA

high density of dislocations, leading to a slight decrease in strength and 
an improvement in elongation. As the annealing temperature increased, 
the yield and tensile strengths progressively declined, while elongation 
improved, reaching 45.3 % at 1273K. A similar trend of enhanced 
elongation with increased annealing temperature was observed in 
cold-rolled steel. Fig. 3 (b) illustrates the strain hardening behavior of the 
samples. The initial work-hardening rate of the cold-rolled samples was 
high and rapidly decreased with strain, indicating a high dislocation 
density. With increasing annealing temperature up to 1273K, the initial 
strain hardening rate declined progressively, and the rate of decrease 
stabilized. This behavior signifies complete recrystallization of the alloy, 
accompanied by the near-elimination of LAGBs.

4. Discussion
4.1. Effect of precipitate and grain boundary on strengthening behaviors
The microstructural evolution of the HEA samples following tensile 
deformation and annealing at 1073K and 1273K was analyzed using 
TEM, with the results shown in Fig. 13 . The CRA-1073K samples dis -
played a significant accumulation of dislocations, primarily concen -
trated around the Cr-rich phases. This suggests that the Cr-rich 
precipitates hindered dislocation motion during deformation, leading to 
local stress concentration and an increase in tensile strength. In contrast, 
the CRA-1273K sample exhibited a lower dislocation density, which can 
be attributed to its larger grain size and fewer precipitated phases. This 
reduction in dislocation density facilitates dislocation motion and pro -
motes twinning as the dominant deformation mechanism. Consequently, 
the CRA-1273K sample exhibited higher plasticity, while the CRA- 
1073K sample demonstrated higher work-hardening capacity and 
lower ductility due to dislocation accumulation.
As shown in Fig. 14 (a), the mechanical properties of CoCrFe -

state samples exhibit high yield strength (965 MPa) and tensile strength 
(1117 MPa), but their elongation is limited to 8.9 %. Cold rolling leads to 
grain fragmentation and elongation, while intense plastic deformation 
along the rolling direction promotes the formation of numerous sub- 
grains. As a result, the rolled-state samples are primarily composed of 
sub-grains, deformation twins, and high-density dislocations ( Figs. 6(a) 
and 7(a) ). At lower annealing temperatures (973K and 1073K), the 
deformed strip structure from cold rolling persists, with only a small 
number of grains beginning to recrystallize. The alloy remains pre -
dominantly composed of dislocations, sub-grains, and recrystallized 
grains, while maintaining elevated yield and tensile strengths. However, 
upon annealing at 1173K, the grains undergo complete recrystallization, 
leading to a significant reduction in yield and tensile strengths to 493 
MPa and 751 MPa, respectively, while elongation increases notably to 
44.27 %. Further annealing at 1273K results in the growth of recrys -
tallized grains and the formation of a substantial number of annealing 
twins. This causes a slight decrease in yield and tensile strength, 
accompanied by a modest increase in elongation.

5. Conclusion
This study systematically examined the effects of annealing tem -
perature (973 – 1273K) on the microstructure and mechanical properties 
of the CoCrFeNiMn
0.75
Cu
0.25 
HEA. EBSD and TEM analyses were used to 
clarify the toughening mechanisms. The key findings are as follows. 
1) As annealing temperature increased, the heterogeneous structures 
transfer to homogeneous structure due to the recrystallization and 
grain growth. Correspondingly, the grain boundary character shifted 
from predominantly LAGBs to HAGBs and TBs.
2) Annealing at 973 K initiated the precipitation of coherent Cr-rich 
precipitates with an initial spherical morphology. Subsequent tem -
perature elevation induced progressive precipitate coarsening. This 
microstructural evolution caused the enhancement of ductility (from 
16.49 % to 45.3 %).
3) The CRA-1073K alloy exhibited slip bands with dense dislocation 
pile-ups adjacent to Cr-rich precipitates, demonstrating dislocation 
glide as the dominant deformation mode. In comparison, the CRA- 
1273K sample predominantly underwent deformation twinning, a 
transition facilitated by its coarser grain structure, lower initial 
dislocation density, and improved microstructural homogeneity that 
collectively favored twin propagation over slip activity.
