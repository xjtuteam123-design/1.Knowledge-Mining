ABSTRACT
Refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) are renowned for their exceptional mechanical strength, however, the 
intrinsic brittleness of most RHEAs at room temperature severely limits their broader engineering application. To 
simultaneously achieve high strength and ductility while avoiding the formation of brittle phases, a

The as-cast alloy demonstrates a high yield strength (~1 GPa), exceptional tensile elongation (25.6 %), and a low 
elastic modulus (~54.6 GPa) without any processing treatments. The synergistic addition of Hf and Ta signifi -
cantly enhances the strength-ductility balance, where Hf promotes plasticity by reducing electron localization, 
whereas Ta strengthens the alloy by increasing lattice distortion through enhanced orbital overlap. Their com -
bined effect induces pronounced local chemical fluctuations, which promote dislocation pinning, stable strain 
hardening, and cross-slip activity. Additionally, edge dislocations — more prone to pinning than screw dis -
locations — generate local stress concentrations that drive the formation of kink bands. These kink bands formed 
by the < 111 > {110} and {112} edge dislocations can even mutually activate and transform, thereby relieving 
internal stress and extending the stage of uniform plastic deformation. While most (110) slip traces are impeded 
by kink bands, forming short edge dislocations, a minority traverse the bands and evolve into long, straight edge 
dislocations that facilitate further deformation. Unlike conventional RHEAs where screw dislocations dominate, 
plastic deformation in this alloy is primarily governed by edge dislocations. These insights provide a promising 
strategy to overcome the strength – ductility trade-off and address room-temperature brittleness in RHEAs.
1. Introduction
Refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) [ 1 – 3 ] that are generally of 
body-centered cubic (BCC) structure have garnered significant attention 
as promising candidates for next-generation structural materials over 
the past decade due to their exceptional mechanical strength [ 4 – 6 ]. A 
shortcoming limiting their practical application is insufficient plasticity 
[ 7 – 10 ]. This has resulted in their mechanical properties being assessed 
mostly through compressive tests [ 11 – 13 ]. Only a small subset — pri -
marily based on the Ti-Zr-Nb-Hf-Ta system — can demonstrate notable 
tensile ductility at room temperature [ 7 , 14 , 15 ]. Typically, enhancing 
room-temperature ductility compromises yield strength, exemplifying 
the well-established strength – ductility trade-off. To date, overcoming 
this trade-off has remained a central challenge in RHEA research [ 16 ,
17 ].
Several strategies have been proposed to enhance the room- 
temperature ductility of RHEAs. One effective approach involves 
introducing microstructural heterogeneity to alter the dislocation slip 
mode, promote work hardening, and facilitate strain delocalization to 
achieve concurrent strength and ductility enhancement [ 18 ]. For 
instance, Lei et al. [ 19 ] demonstrated that the addition of 2.0 at. % 
oxygen to the TiZrHfNb high-entropy alloy (HEA) transformed the 
dislocation shearing mode from planar slip to wavy slip by forming or -
dered interstitial complexes. This modification also promoted the for -
mation of Frank-Read sources during deformation, enhancing 
dislocation multiplication and significantly improving both yield 
strength (YS = 1.1 GPa) and fracture elongation (El = 27 %). Similarly, 
An et al. [ 20 ] designed a TiHfVNb alloy with YS of ~1.1 GPa and El of 28 
% that underwent spinodal decomposition, leading to the trans -
formation of the BCC ( β phase) into a β + β * phase mixture. This phase 
transformation generated nanoscale elastic strain fields, creating rugged 
energy landscapes that hindered dislocation motion and promoted

Recently, kink bands have emerged as a promising deformation 
mechanism for improving ductility [ 22 , 23 ]. These structures enable 
localized crystal bending in regions where neither slip nor twinning is 
favorable, thereby accommodating deformation [ 24 ]. However, 
research on the formation and evolution mechanism of kink bands re -
mains limited. Most previous studies have focused on kink band for -
mation through cold rolling and examining their effects on 
low-temperature impact toughness [ 23 , 25 – 27 ]. The quantitative role 
of key alloying elements in regulating kink band formation has yet to be 
systematically elucidated. Additionally, little attention has been given to 
the evolution of kink bands under tensile deformation, limiting their 
potential application in alloy design. Furthermore, conventional BCC 
alloys predominantly rely on screw dislocations for plastic deformation 
[ 16 , 28 ]. This mechanism, however, exhibits limitations in specific alloy 
systems and fails to explain the unique microstructural evolution and 
mechanical behavior observed under extreme deformation conditions. 
Therefore, a deeper understanding of the interactions between alloying 
elements, microstructure, and dislocation behavior is crucial for 
advancing the development of RHEAs.

dislocations. This work not only provides new insights into the plastic 
deformation mechanisms of RHEAs but also offers a promising strategy 
to mitigate room-temperature brittleness, paving the way for the 
development of next-generation biomedical and engineering alloys.

mechanism can be deduced. The designed RHEAs initially undergo 
uniform deformation mainly controlled by mixed and screw dislocation 
(plane slip and cross slip). At high strains during plastic deformation, 
coordinated slip of 〈 111 〉 edge dislocations lead to kinking of {110}, 
{112}, and {123} planes. Although mixed and screw dislocations 
interacting with themselves and LCFs can promote stable strain hard -
ening, the formation of kinks during plastic deformation mainly acts as a 
strain-softening mechanism, suppressing these interactions, resulting in 
lowered overall work hardening rates in the tensile curves of the RHEAs 
and extending the uniform strain. These kinks can continuously rotate 
the lattice to accommodate the exceptionally high local strain within the 
grains during plastic deformation, leading to stress relaxation and pre -
venting grain fracture. Moreover, these kinks ensure that the slip system 
maintain favorable orientations, effectively dispersing grain damage 
and enhancing the ductility of alloys.
