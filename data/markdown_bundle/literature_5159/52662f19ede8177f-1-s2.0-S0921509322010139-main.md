1. Introduction 
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) relate to a new group of multicomponent 
materials with unique physical, mechanical, and functional properties 
[ 1 – 3 ]. Equiatomic FeMnCrNiCo Cantor alloy with stable fcc crystal lat -
tice is among the most studied HEAs [ 2 ]. It is reputed due to extraor -
dinary low-temperature strength properties and ductility, but rather low 
yield strength at room and elevated temperatures limits its use as a 
structural material [ 2 , 4 – 6 ]. 
Interstitial alloying of Cantor alloy by carbon seems to be the 
promising way to improve strength characteristics due to activation of 
main hardening mechanisms such as carbon-assisted grain refinement, 
solid-solution and precipitation strengthening [ 7 – 16 ]. Formation of type

HEAs with the preservation of moderate plasticity [ 14 , 16 , 17 ]. At higher

the significant ductility loss [ 7 , 10 , 14 , 16 , 17 ]. The dissolution or refine -
ment of coarse particles and supersaturating of austenite by carbon 
could be achieved by different combinations of thermal and mechanical 
treatments, as it was previously used for conventional carbon-alloyed 
alloys [ 18 ]. Unfortunately, for Cantor alloy with carbon content 
higher than 1 at. %, conventional thermal-mechanical processing does 
not provide the complete dissolution of carbide particles [ 7 , 14 ]. In this

2. Materials and methods 
The 2-kg ingot of the high-entropy alloy with the actual composition 
19.9 Fe - 20.0 Mn - 20.0 Cr - 20.0 Ni - 15.0 Co - 5.1C, at. % (20.7 Fe, 20.3 
Mn, 19.4 Cr, 21.8 Ni, 16.5 Co, 1.2C, < 0.06 Si, < 0.002 S, < 0.001 P, <
0.01 Cu, < 0.018Al, < 0.001 Ti, < 0.06 N, mass. %, C-HEA) was pro -
duced by vacuum induction melting and casting in a pure argon atmo -
sphere. Melting was carried out in a periclase crucible, starting from the 
melting and degassing in a vacuum of inactive charge components (iron, 
nickel, cobalt). Then, carbon, chromium, and manganese were sequen -
tially introduced into the melt through a dispenser. The molten metal 
was casted in an argon atmosphere into a flat mold. Flat square-shaped 
(for microstructural analysis, 8 mm × 8 mm × 2 mm) and dumb-bell 
shaped specimens (for mechanical testing with a gauge section of 12 
mm × 2.7 mm × 1.3 mm) were cut from the cast material by electrical 
discharge machining.

3. Results and discussion 
In cast state, heavily carbon-alloyed C-HEA is a type of metal- 
ceramic composite material consisted of plastic austenitic matrix and 
rigid carbide inclusions ( Fig. 1 a). The total volume content of carbides, 
estimated using SEM images, is 11.5 ± 0.5%. According to the EDS 
analysis of the alloy, these inclusions are enriched by Cr and C but 
depleted by Co, Fe and Ni relative to the austenitic matrix ( Fig. 2 a).

Therefore, multiple thermal cycling provides the mechanical fragmen -

coefficients of austenite and carbides [ 20 ]. High stresses around carbide 
inclusions, specifically in the regions of their sharp corners, could 
stimulate both diffusion (dissolution of the carbides) during short-term 
heating of the specimens and its severe fragmentation during fast 
cooling.

treatment regimes (9.5 ± 0.5%), and the transverse size of the particles

All treated specimens show higher mechanical properties that the 
cast one ( Fig. 5 c and d, Table 1 ). Increase in their strength is associated 
with the increase in the effectivity of solid-solution (L-SST and Ms-SST) 
and precipitate hardening (all treatments). For Ms-HQ treatment, the 
mechanical fragmentation of carbides forces particle strengthening, but 
sharp edges of undissolved carbides act as stress concentrators and could 
assist cracks formation at low macroscopic stresses like in cast material. 
In L-SST and Ms-SST treatments, solid-solution and precipitation hard -
ening are dominating strengthening mechanisms but partial dissolution 
and smoothing of the carbides assist the increase of ductility of C-HEA 
( Fig. 5 c, Table 1 ). Notably, complex Ms-SST treatment increases 
ductility of C-HEA in additive way exceeding the individual effects of

Even though the complete dissolution of the carbides has not been 
obtained, both strength and, which is more important, ductility of the C- 
HEA alloy are improved using rather simple heat treatment (Ms-SST) 
( Table 1 ). The advantage of the proposed Ms-SST treatment is that it can 
be used repeatedly to processing the alloy without loss of material and 
restrictions on the number of heating and cooling cycles. It opens a 
perspective for further obtaining a supersaturated interstitial solid so -
lutions based on heavily carbon-alloyed high entropy alloys.
