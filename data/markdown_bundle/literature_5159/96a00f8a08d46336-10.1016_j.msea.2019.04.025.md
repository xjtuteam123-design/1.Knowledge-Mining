Regulating the strength and ductility of a cold rolled FeCrCoMnNi 
high-entropy alloy via annealing treatment 
 
Ji Gu, Song Ni, Yong Liu, Min Song* 
State Key Laboratory of Powder Metallurgy, Central South University, Changsha 410083, P.R. China 
 
Abstract 
An equiatomic FeCrCoMnNi high-entropy alloy was processed by cold rolling and then 
isochronous annealing treatments. The dependence of the tensile properties and microstructure of the 
alloy on the annealing temperature was investigated. Long-range ordered structure, Cr-rich precipitates, 
heterogeneous structure with different recrystallization degrees, and homogeneous structure with 
different grain sizes can be obtained via regulating the annealing temperature. It is operable to obtain 
optimal microstructure of the alloy and thus beneficial to the mechanical properties by varying the 
annealing temperature. This work provides a new concept to fabricating FeCrCoMnNi alloy with a 
combination of high strength and large ductility. 
 
Keywords: High-entropy alloy; strength; ductility; cold rolling; annealing

1. Introduction 
The single phase FeCrCoMnNi high-entropy alloy (HEA) with equiatomic ratio was one of the 
widely investigated face-centered cubic (FCC) HEAs [1]. The encouraging mechanical properties of 
this HEA, such as good ductility, excellent fracture resistance and good cryogenic properties, have 
attracted extensive attention in recent years [2-5]. 
Normally, single phase FCC structured HEAs [4-8] with a completely recrystallized 
microstructure exhibit excellent ductility but relatively low yield strength due to the intrinsic 
characteristics of the FCC structure [9]. Severe plastic deformation (SPD) or nanocrystallization can 
remarkably improve the yield strength but consume almost all the ductility [10,11], which is mainly 
due to the shear localization [12]. For single phase metals, some microstructural characteristics can be 
introduced to overcome the strength-ductility trade-off [13-19], such as bimodal grain size 
microstructure [13], high density nano-twins [14,15], gradient structure [16,17] and heterogeneous 
structure [18,19]. 
For multi-phase metals, the designed metastable phase [20] and the precipitation particles [21] 
might also efficiently conquer the strength-ductility trade-off. FeCrCoMnNi HEA is widely accepted to 
be a stable single phase structure, while recent investigations indicated that second phase precipitates 
can be formed after post-deformation annealing at a relative low temperature [11,22]. Our previous 
study also proposed that long-range ordered structure can be formed after annealing a cold rolled 
FeCrCoMnNi HEA at 773 K [23]. It would thus be a strategy to introducing long-range ordered 
structure or precipitation particles to regulating the ductility and strength of FeCrCoMnNi HEA. In this 
study, we systematically investigated the effects of annealing temperature on the mechanical properties 
and microstructures of a FeCrCoMnNi HEA after cold rolling. Advanced microstructural

2. Experimental 
The FeCrCoMnNi alloy ingot with equiatomic ratio was fabricated through vacuum induction 
melting high-purity Fe (99.95%), Cr (99.99%), Co (99.98%), Mn (99.5%) and Ni (99.995%) in 
high-purified argon atmosphere. The alloy ingot was homogenization annealed at a temperature of 
1273 K for 2 hours. Then cold rolling process was conducted on the homogenization annealed ingot 
with a thickness reduction of ~0.9. The plate after cold rolling was cut into “dog-bone shaped” samples 
with the size of 24 ×6×2 mm
3 (the gauge length: length ×width×thickness) and then sealed in 
vacuum tubes, followed by isochronous annealing at 773, 873, 923, 973, 1073 and 1173 K for 1 h, and 
then air cooled to the room temperature. Table 1 shows the detailed annealing temperatures and the 
corresponding specimen names.

Quanta FEG 250 field emission gun scanning electron microscope (SEM), equipped with EBSD

3. Results 
Fig. 1 reveals the engineering stress-engineering strain curves of the FeCrCoMnNi alloy before 
and after cold rolling and annealing treatments at various temperatures. It can be seen from Fig. 1 that 
the yield stress of the alloy increases rapidly from ~210 MPa to ~1150 MPa with a total sacrifice of the 
ductility after cold rolling treatment. The following annealing treatment at 773 K for 1 h results in 
additional increase of the yield stress to 1300 MPa and the alloy becomes completely brittle. With 
increasing the annealing temperature to 873 K, the yield stress decreases to 1050 MPa with partial 
recovery of the ductility to ~8%. The annealing treatments at 923, 973, 1073 and 1173 K lead to 
obvious ductility recovery with the yield stress decreases to 560 MPa, 520 MPa, 320 MPa and 280 
MPa, respectively. It can also be seen from Fig. 1 that the ductility of the specimens CR0-A T-1073-1 
and CR0-A T-1173-1 is better than that of the as-cast sample, and the yield stresses of all the samples 
are higher than that of the as-cast sample.

CR0-A T-973-1, CR0-A T-1073-1 and CR0-A T-1173-1, respectively. After annealed at 873 K and 923 K, 
the FeCrCoMnNi alloy was partially recrystallized, with the average grain size in the recrystallization 
regions being ~ 4 µm and ~ 8 µm, respectively. It can also be seen that the degree of recrystallization is

4. Discussion 
Annealing the cold rolled FeCrCoMnNi HEA at different temperatures leads to various 
microstructures, and the detailed microstructural evolution versus annealing temperature is shown in 
Fig. 6. With the increase of the annealing temperature, the microstructural evolution could be described 
as follows: long-range ordered structure →precipitation particles →partial recrystallization →complete 
recrystallization→grains coarsening. The critical annealing temperature to maintain a stable single 
phase structure and a completely recrystallized structure in this work are 923 K and 973 K, respectively. 
The microstructural variation with annealing temperature can provide feasibility to regulate the 
strength and ductility of FeCrCoMnNi HEA in a large scope. 
The precipitation phase and/or long-range ordered structure dispersely distributed inside the alloys 
can significantly enhance the strength, because these structures can severely block the dislocation 
motions [28]. However, a lot of metals with precipitation particles or long-range ordered structure are 
intrinsically brittle because of the stress localization in the interface and thus leading to early destroy 
subject to loading [21]. In this study, the deformed dislocation substructures in the sample 
CR0-A T-773-1 remains almost unchanged, accompanied by the formation of long-range ordered 
structure inside the grains, which results in additional hardening and strengthening of the alloy with a 
complete sacrifice of the ductility. The HEA alloy was partially recrystallized in the samples 
CR0-A T-873-1 and CR0-A T-923-1, and the ductility was partially recovered compared to the sample 
CR0, with the yield stress of the CR0-A T-873-1 being almost twice as much as that of the sample 
CR0-A T-923-1. The formation of Cr-rich precipitation phase in the sample CR0-A T-873-1 contributes 
to the high yield stress. When the annealing temperature exceeds 973 K, the cold-rolled FeCrCoMnNi

HEA was fully recrystallized (specimens CR0-A T-973-1, CR0-A T-1073-1 and CR0-A T-1173-1). 
Comparing the samples CR0-A T-923-1 and CR0-A T-973-1, the average grain size of the sample 
CR0-A T-973-1 is similar to that of the recrystallized regions in the sample CR0-A T-923-1 (as shown in 
Fig. 2). The sample CR0-A T-973-1 possesses a homogeneous structure, while the sample 
CR0-A T-923-1 possesses a heterogeneous structure, leading to a combination of higher strength and 
larger ductility. The heterogeneous structure can produce significant back-stress during deformation. 
The back-stress contributes to the strength of the materials and increases the work hardening ability 
simultaneously that also contributes to the ductility [29]. A previous work about Al0.1CoCrFeNi HEA 
also conquer the strength-ductility trade-off with heterogeneous structure [18]. The fully-recrystallized 
FeCrCoMnNi HEA possesses high strain hardening rate and good ductility. A high density of 
dislocation cells can be observed in the samples CR0-A T-923-1 and CR0-A T-1173-1 after tensile to 
failure (as shown in Fig. 5), and the average size of the dislocation cells is less than 100 nm. This 
would explain the high strain hardening rate and good ductility of the recrystallized samples. While in 
the specimens CR0-A T-773-1 and CR0-A T-873-1, shear bands and dislocation walls was observed (also 
as shown in Fig. 5). 
FeCrCoMnNi HEA was widely considered to have a stably single phased structure at total 
temperatures below its melting temperature [2,3]. For example, Otto et al. [26] showed that 
FeCrCoMnNi alloy remains a single phase structure after prolonged annealing at 1173 K. However, the 
FeCrCoMnNi HEA is unstable and nucleates second-phase precipitates after prolonged annealing at 
773 K and 973 K [26,27]. Previous study also predicted the temperature-time-transformation (TTT) 
diagram for FeCrCoMnNi alloy, and indicated that the second-phase precipitate formation temperature 
is in the range of ~873-1073 K [30]. It can be concluded that new structures can be formed in the

FeCrCoMnNi HEA at a low annealing temperature, while the single phase structure is stable at a high 
annealing temperature. In present study, the formation of substructure and defects during cold rolling 
can act as nucleation sites and fast diffusion pathways, leading to the precipitation phase and atomically 
ordered structure nucleation at a relatively low annealing temperature. The long-range ordered structure 
and Cr-rich precipitates were only detected at relatively low annealing temperatures of 773 K and 873 
K. High temperatures, for example 923 K, 973 K, 1073 K and 1173 K, lead to recrystallization and 
grain growth, without nucleating the precipitates phase and/or long-range ordered structure. The 
investigation showed that regulating the annealing temperature could control the dislocation evolution, 
the degree of recrystallization, and the nucleation of the long-range ordered structure and/or 
precipitation phases to obtain an optimal microstructure that benefits to the mechanical properties. This 
work provides a new concept to obtain a combination high strength and large ductility of FeCrCoMnNi 
HEA. 
 
5. Conclusion 
In summary, the influences of annealing temperature on the tensile properties and microstructures 
of a FeCrCoMnNi HEA subject to cold rolling was systematically investigated. Various annealing 
temperatures lead to the different microstructures of the FeCrCoMnNi HEA. Long-range ordered 
structure and Cr-rich precipitation phase were formed at 773 K and 873 K, while no second-phase 
particle or atomically ordered structure was formed at an annealing temperature higher than 923 K. As 
the annealing temperature is between 873~973 K, the annealed samples develop heterogeneous 
structure that comprise nanotwins in the recrystallized grains and a high density of the dislocations in 
the deformed regions. As the annealing temperature exceeds 973 K, the alloy exhibits fully
