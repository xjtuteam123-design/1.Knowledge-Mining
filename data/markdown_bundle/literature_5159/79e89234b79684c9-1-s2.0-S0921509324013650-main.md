ABSTRACT
The hot-end components of aero-engines and gas turbines not only require high-temperature alloys capable of 
withstanding extreme temperatures, but also demand welds with high-temperature-resistant properties. In this 
study, the CALPHAD method was employed, utilizing the thermodynamic theory of phase diagrams with 
Thermo-Calc software and the corresponding database, to design the interlayer composition for superalloy TLP 
diffusion connections. The optimization aimed to determine the interlayer material ’ s solidus-liquidus and 
compound phase content, resulting in the selection of a new nickel-based interlayer material containing B as 
MPD, with Co and W as strengthening elements. Using GH3230 alloy as the research subject, TLP diffusion 
bonding experiments were conducted at a welding temperature of 1200 
◦
C with a holding time of 4 h. The weld 
zone exhibited no defects, and the microstructure was identical to that of the GH3230 base metal, consisting 
entirely of a solid solution. High-temperature tensile tests revealed that fractures consistently occurred in the 
GH3230 base metal, indicating that the weld ’ s strength significantly exceeded that of the base metal. The 
average tensile strength of GH3230 high-temperature alloy bar tensile simulated specimens is 899 MPa at room 
temperature and 213 MPa at high temperature. In addition, the 90
◦
three-point bend test showed no cracking in 
the weld area, indicating adequate plasticity.
1. Introduction
As the thermal efficiency of aero-engines, gas turbines, and nuclear 
power engines continues to improve, their internal high-temperature 
components are subjected to prolonged high-temperature gas exposure 
and corrosion. These components require exceptional high-temperature 
mechanical properties [ 1 ]. This not only requires materials with excel -
lent high temperature and corrosion resistance, but also the organization 
and properties of the welds that are assembled and connected are 
equally demanding. Nickel-based alloys have become mainstream ma -
terials in the aerospace sector due to their excellent mechanical prop -
erties, long fatigue life, high-temperature oxidation resistance, and 
corrosion resistance [ 2 , 3 ]. In particular, GH3230 high-temperature 
alloy, as a representative nickel-based alloy, is widely used in the 
manufacturing of components such as combustion chambers, flame 
tubes, heat exchangers, and transition ducts for aircraft engines. Addi -
tionally, it is utilized in the production of thermocouple protective 
sheaths and conduits for combustion chamber components and injectors 
in gas turbines [ 4 – 6 ]. For the welding methods of GH3230, fusion welds 
are prone to severe thermal cracking and microstructural weakening, 
while brazed welds exhibit poor temperature resistance due to the low 
temperatures welding [ 7 ].
Transient Liquid-Phase (TLP) diffusion bonding is the most suitable 
method for achieving excellent high-temperature resistance in thin- 
walled structures of high-temperature components. This method al -
lows the weld to attain a microstructure similar or identical to that of the 
base metal, with comparable high-temperature performance and 
strength [ 8 ]. This method utilizes an alloy material (called an interlayer) 
that melts into a liquid phase at a welding temperature below the 
melting point of the base metal. During this process, the composition of 
the liquid phase in the weld is altered by the mutual diffusion of ele -
ments from the melted interlayer and the base metal [ 9 ]. This causes an 
increase in the melting point (solidus temperature) and leads to 
isothermal solidification of the entire weld. Consequently, this method 
achieves reliable welding without the need for significant pressure. The 
melting temperature of the weld microstructure approaches that of the 
base metal, ensuring that the weld microstructure and properties can 
withstand high-temperature conditions [ 10 ].

The interlayer used in TLP diffusion bonding must contain one or 
more melting point depressant (MPD) elements to realize the isothermal 
solidification process [ 11 ]. Besides these depressants, the interlayer also 
includes strengthening elements from the base metal or substitutes for 
these elements. There are two methods for designing the composition of 
interlayers for superalloy TLP diffusion bonding. The first method is to 
use simple ternary or quaternary alloys near the eutectic point based on 
experience, generally Ni-Cr-B, Ni-Cr-Si, or Ni-Cr-Si-B [ 12 ]; For most 
Ni-based high-temperature alloys, commonly used interlayers in both 
domestic and international TLP research are selected from standard 
brazing materials, including BNi-2, BNi-5, and MBF amorphous brazing 
fillers. BNi-2 has a lower melting temperature range (971 – 999 
◦
C) 
compared to general Ni-Cr-Si-B brazing material, which can realize the 
connection at a lower temperature reducing the interaction between the 
brazing material and the base metal [ 13 ]. The amorphous foil MBF 
brazing material is characterized by a uniform chemical composition, 
which can be spread uniformly on the surface of the base metal during 
joining, facilitating assembly and weld gap control [ 14 ]. Chen et al. [ 15 ] 
successfully brazed IN718 alloy using BNi-1 and BNi-2, showing suc -
cessful isothermal solidification after holding for 20 min, with no 
eutectic phases observed in the joint region. Chen et al. [ 16 ] used 
MBF-51 amorphous foil to braze the IN-625 alloy and found experi -
mentally that higher brazing temperatures and/or longer brazing times 
led to the disappearance of Nb6Ni16Si7 intermetallic compounds. 
Additionally, fine grain boundary borides were replaced by isolated 
coarse borides in the Ni/Cr/(Fe) -rich matrix. Using standard brazing 
fillers at higher temperatures and longer holding times can achieve 
relatively uniform weld joints, but there are some compositional dif -
ferences compared to the base metal.
The second method is to use an existing superalloy composition as 
the base component and then adding MPD elements to obtain an inter -
layer with the desired melting point. WAN Diet al. [ 17 ] proposed that 
standard nickel-based brazing fillers as interlayers for TLP diffusion 
bonding of nickel-based superalloys cannot meet the usage re -
quirements. In contrast, an amorphous interlayer based on the compo -
sition of the base metal can produce a joint with a composition similar to 
the base metal after welding. This is because the composition of the 
complex multi-component interlayer is closer to the base metal, 
reducing the diffusion time required for homogenization [ 18 ]. Addi -
tionally, the strengthening element such as W, Co, and Cr in the inter -
layer can effectively improve the high-temperature strength of the joint. 
Mingcan Liu et al. [ 19 ] successfully completed TLP diffusion bonding of 
Mar-M247 nickel-based superalloy using a NiCrCoWTaB interlayer at 
1150 
◦
C. After holding for 240 min, complete isothermal solidification 
was achieved, resulting in a more uniform microstructure of the joint, 
with the athermally solidified phase completely disappearing. The 
maximum tensile strength of 443 MPa was obtained after holding at 
1150 
◦
C for 240 min. Dong et al. [ 20 ] designed a novel Ni-Cr-Si-B 
brazing filler based on the cluster-plus-glue-atom model for vacuum 
brazing of GH4169 alloy. The weld consisted mainly of γ -Ni solid solu -
tion, with Nb, Ti-rich phases and Cr, Nb, Mo-rich borides distributed in 
the diffusion zone. Under the process conditions of 1240 
◦
C for 20 min, 
the joint achieved a maximum shear strength of 820 MPa. As a result, it 
is easier to achieve higher strength joints in a shorter period with 
complex multilayer interlayers that closely match the base material 
composition compared to simple ternary interlayers. Therefore, complex 
multilayer interlayers are the optimal choice for TLP joints in parts 
subjected to high stress levels.
Most interlayer development relies on experimental methods, where 
elements are added or subtracted based on empirical compositions 
derived from previous research or experience, and then welding ex -
periments are performed directly by the test welding method. This way 
is very laborious and the accuracy cannot be guaranteed. In the 1970s, 
Kaufman [ 21 ] suggested the CALPHAD (Computer Coupling of Phase 
Diagrams and Thermochemistry) technique, which has been widely used 
for the evaluation and calculation of phase diagrams in 
multi-component material systems [ 22 ]. Gao et al. [ 23 , 24 ] used the 
HEA1.0 database released by Thermo-Calc to calculate the phase dia -
gram of the FexCo20Ni20MnyCu20 system, obtaining phase diagrams 
for different Fe/Mn molar ratios. They found that Fe5Co20 -
Ni20Mn35Cu20 has a single FCC phase structure and a relatively low 
melting range (1090 – 1148 
◦
C). The simulation results differed from 
actual Differential Thermal Analysis (DTA) results by only about 10 
◦
C. 
They used Fe5Co20Ni20Mn35Cu20 to join Inconel 600, achieving a 
high shear strength of 530 MPa with brazing at 1200 
◦
C for 90 min. 
HARDWICK et al. [ 25 ] designed a novel NiCrFeGeB high-entropy 
alloy-type brazing filler using the SSOL4 database from Thermo-Calc. 
The liquidus temperature differed from the predicted value by 7 
◦
C. 
They vacuum brazed Inconel-718 with this filler, finding compound 
phases that matched predictions. At 1100 
◦
C for 60 min, they achieved 
fully isothermally solidified weld joints, also noting the limitations of 
the database in predicting compound phases. Therefore, CALPHAD is 
very reliable for high entropy alloy brazing material design with 
appropriate database selection.
This study focuses on the development of interlayers for TLP diffu -
sion bonding of GH3230 nickel-based superalloy. The Thermo-Calc 
thermodynamic software, matched with the TCHEA6 database, is used 
to design and optimize the interlayer composition using the solidus- 
liquidus temperature range and boride content as a reference. The 
melting temperature is verified through DSC (Differential Scanning 
Calorimetry) testing. TLP diffusion bonding experiments are conducted 
on GH3230 alloy, and the microstructure of the joint area and the room/ 
high-temperature tensile strength are examined. The research results 
have significant guiding implications for the development of interlayer 
materials for superalloy diffusion bonding and the manufacturing of 
aero engine components.
2. Preliminary alloy design study
The material used in this study is the GH3230 nickel-based super -
alloy, with its main elemental composition shown in Table 1 . This solid 
solution strengthened nickel-based superalloy is primarily reinforced 
with W and Mo. The combined content of W, Mo, and Cr exceeds 35 %, 
resulting in exceptional high-temperature strength, creep properties, 
fatigue resistance, corrosion resistance, and high-temperature oxidation 
resistance. It can withstand long-term temperatures up to 1150 
◦
C in an 
oxidizing atmosphere, making it an important material for high- 
temperature components in next-generation aircraft engines [ 26 ].
The microstructure of GH3230 primarily consists of a solid solution 
accompanied by granular carbides. To achieve strength similar to that of 
the base metal, the weld microstructure should closely resemble the 
solid solution. The main alloying elements include Cr, W, Mo, Co, Fe, Al, 
and Ti. Among these, Mo, Co, and Fe are present in smaller amounts, 
while Cr and W are more rich (Cr: 20 – 24 wt%, W: 13 – 15 wt%). W and Cr 
contribute to solid solution strengthening, and Cr also enhances corro -
sion and oxidation resistance. Since Al and Ti in GH3230 are easily 
oxidized and need to be removed.
The interlayer composition should be based on the main elements of 
the base metal. Given that Mo and W have similar strengthening effects 
and W is a primary element in the base metal, W is used instead of Mo to

5. Conclusion
This study utilized the CALPHAD method and Thermo-Calc software 
for the design of an interlayer suitable for TLP diffusion bonding of 
GH3230 alloy. The composition was optimized and validated using 
thermodynamic parameters from HEAs design experience. An amor -
phous foil was prepared for selected interlayer compositions. TLP 
diffusion bonding of GH3230 using the designed interlayer was con -
ducted at 1200 
◦
C with 4 h of holding time to explore the effect of 
interface microstructure and mechanical properties of the joint. The 
main conclusions are as follows. 
(1) The interlayer was primarily composed of GH3230 alloy ’ s main 
elements. B, chosen as a MPD due to its rapid diffusion in 
GH3230, aimed to achieve alloy compositions in the weld seam 
similar to GH3230 after B diffusion. Initial design of the inter -
layer showed that increasing Cr and W content raised the sys -
tem ’ s liquidus temperature, while Co had minimal impact. To 
ensure excellent high-temperature performance of the joint 
microstructure with a lower melting temperature of the inter -
layer, a Ni-Cr-Co-W-B quinary system with higher Co content was 
determined. Validation of existing interlayer data using the 
TCHEA6 database proposed a solidus-liquidus temperature range 
(1050 – 1130 
◦
C) and a standard of boride content lower than the 
solid solution content at high temperatures. Optimization of the 
quinary system led to the design of KNi-5, validated using HEAs- 
designed thermodynamic parameters, and amorphous foils were 
prepared. Predicted solidus-liquidus temperatures were in close 
agreement with DSC measurement results, with errors of 0.08 % 
and 0.44 %, respectively.
(2) The TLP diffusion weld seam of GH3230 alloy consisted of ISZ 
and DAZ regions. ISZ was similar to the base metal with a fully 
solid solution structure, devoid of defects and compound phases. 
After thorough diffusion, B elements in the TLP diffusion weld 
seam mainly distributed within the carbide phase of DAZ, with 
lower concentrations in solid solution grains and the base metal, 
and no borides in ISZ. Calculations using Thermo-Calc for the 
solidus temperature in the weld seam area indicated tempera -
tures lower than the base metal ’ s melting point, achieving at least 
the same level of high-temperature performance as the base 
metal.
(3) Using the KNi-5 interlayer under the conditions of welding at 
1200 
◦
C for 4 h, the tensile strength of the welded joint reached 
898.9 MPa at room temperature and 213 MPa at 950 
◦
C, with 
fracture occurring in the GH3230 base metal far from the weld 
seam center. The high-temperature elongation exceeded 52 %, 
indicating excellent high-temperature ductility. GH3230 alloy 
thin sheet weld joints showed no cracking after 90
◦
three-point 
bending, and microscopic observations revealed no defects such 
as cracks, demonstrating excellent room temperature ductility of 
the weld seam.
