ABSTRACT
This work focuses on a novel high-entropy alloy, ReMoTaW, with high density of 16.69 × 10 3 kg/m3. The high-
entropy alloy displays three disordered solid solution phases. The phase segregation is explained by binary alloy
phase diagrams and melting point temperature di ﬀerences during non-equilibrium solidi ﬁcation. The maximum
strength, failure strain and hardness are, respectively, 1451 MPa, 5.69% and 640 HV.
1. Introduction
High entropy alloys (HEAs) have opened up a wide range of novel
alloy system and they show a series of excellent properties, including
high hardness, high strength and other characteristics [ 1–3]. To date,
the mostly widely studied elements for HEAs are 3d transition metals:
Al, Co, Cr, Cu, Fe, Mn, Ni, Ti and V. These HEAs are mostly based on
CoCrFeNi, such as CoCrFeNiMn and AlCoCrCuFeNi [ 4]. Another widely
studied HEA family contains refractory metals: Cr, Hf, Mo, Nb, Ta, Ti, V,
W and Zr, plus Al, and most refractory HEAs are based on HfNbTaZr,
NbMoTaW, CrMoNbTa or CrNbVZr element groupings [ 4–11]. Some
HEAs such as NbMoTaW(V) and MoNbHfZrTi exhibited a single-phase
solid solution, while others indicated two or more phases such as
TiNbTaZrMo and AlMo0.5NbTa0.5TiZr.
Since metallic alloys with high density remain in high demand for
advanced science and technology, such as armour piercing projectile,
shaped charge liner in ordnance industry and density gradient materials
in dynamic high-pressure physics, it is a rationale for developing HEAs
consisting of components with high density. However, reviews of the
published papers about HEAs indicate that the NbMoTaW alloy had the
highest density of 13.75 × 10
3 kg/m3 and exhibited low ductility at
room temperature [ 5,6]. The reported HEAs are unable to meet the
requirement of high density (above 15 × 10 3 kg/m3) for high power
armor piercing projectile and some gradient materials. Other high
density elements such as Re, which has high density of 21.03 × 10
3 kg/
m3 and can enhance the ductility for several BCC elements such as W
and Mo [ 12,13], can o ﬀer new possibility.
In this letter, a novel ReMoTaW HEA with high density is produced
by replacing Nb with Re in the traditional NbMoTaW alloy. The
microstructure, density and mechanical properties are examined and
the strengthening mechanisms for this HEA are discussed.
2. Experimental procedures
The ReMoTaW alloy was prepared by vacuum arc melting of equi-
molar mixtures of the corresponding elements. Re, Mo, Ta and W were
in the form of metallic powders with purities of all above 99.95%. Arc
melting of the alloy was conducted under a protective argon atmo-
sphere on a water-cooled copper plate. To achieve a homogeneous
distribution of elements, the alloy was re-melted ﬁve times, ﬂipped for
each melt, and held in the liquid state for about 6 min during each
melting event. The mass of the arc-melted ingots was 20 g. The alloy
was prepared in the form of buttons of ~6 mm in thickness and
~15–20 mm in diameter.
The crystal structure was identi ﬁed with the use of a Bruker D8
Advance X-ray di ﬀractometer, Cu K α radiation, and the 2θ range of
20–140°. The microstructure was investigated by the scanning electron
microscope (SEM) equipped with energy dispersive spectrometry (EDS)
after etching using the hydro ﬂuoric acid, nitric acid and distilled water
(V
HF:V HNO3:V H2O = 1: 1: 2). Transmission electron microscope (TEM)
investigation was performed with EDS.
The density was measured by the Archimedes' method. Cylindrical
specimens with 4 mm height and 2 mm diameter were prepared for
compression tests. The compression testing was performed using an
Instron mechanical testing machine at a constant loading speed of
0.5 mm/min. Vickers hardness was measured on polished cross-section
surfaces under a 1 kg load applied for 15 s. Both the Vickers hardness
and strength values were measured for ﬁve times at room temperature

3. Results and discussions
Fig. 1 presents the XRD pattern and the SEM images of the Re-
MoTaW alloy. The XRD pattern along with the indexes of crystal planes
shows peaks of a single BCC and the lattice parameter is a = 0.3176 nm.
According to Guo et al. [ 14,15], the derived value of valence electron
concentration (VEC) for ReMoTaW alloy is determined as 6, lying
within the proposed range (VEC < 6.87 for BCC solid solution for-
mation). As shown in Fig. 1b and c, this alloy exhibits dark matrix and
bright particles. The matrix exhibits a coarse dendritic structure with
average grain size of ~182.76 μm. Multiple particles with average grain
size of ~1.93 μm are distributed in the grain interior of the matrix. The
composition analysis result obtained by EDS shows that element Ta is
rich in the particle phase and the content is about 86.45 at.%. Ac-
cording on these ﬁndings, a ﬁrst indication about a phase segregation
formation takes place.
The details of the microstructure were further studied using TEM.
Two regions with darker and brighter particles distinct from the matrix
are observed, as shown in Fig. 2. The selected di ﬀraction patterns of
diﬀerent regions are shown as insets in Fig. 2 while the average che-
mical composition is presented in Table 1. The selected di ﬀraction
pattern of the matrix (inset A) shows regular BCC crystal structure. The
element compositions are close to equiatomic. The darker Re-enriched
region (inset B), has HCP crystal structure and the lattice parameters
are a = 0.2503 nm, c = 0.4678 nm. The brighter region enriched Ta
(inset C), has a tetragonal structure and the lattice parameters are
a = 1.0201 nm, c = 0.5011 nm. Compared to β-Ta phase [ 16], the Ta-
rich phase with high content of Ta shows similar lattice parameter with
β-Ta phase. Thus, the Ta-rich phase can be identi ﬁed as solid solution
based on β-Ta phase (named as β-Ta-rich phase hereafter).
For many solid solution forming HEAs, it has been shown that the
phases in the as-cast state are quite stable and hence not far away from
the equilibrium state [ 6–8]. As the phase stability is still suspicious,
microstructure of this alloy in annealed condition will be studied in the
future.
As Senkov et al. proposed [ 5], the NbMoTaW alloy had a single BCC
solid solution structure. The processing history of the ReMoTaW alloy
and the NbMoTaW alloy is the same. However, the ReMoTaW alloy
displays three disordered solid solution phases. The critical di ﬀerence
of component elements between these two alloys is that Re is joined in
instead of Nb. Hence the properties between Re and other component
elements are the main reason of phase segregation. According to binary

Due to the large solubility of Re in other elements and the high entropy

solution of Ta in the matrix leads to serious lattice distortion and hin-

of such phase segregation phenomena is also widely criticized by many

The density of the ReMoTaW alloy is determined to be
ρ = 16.69 ± 0.01 × 10 3 kg/m3, which is 0.48% less than the calcu-
lated ρmix value (16.77 × 10 3 kg/m3, using the rule of mixtures),
probably due to porosity formed during melting. As the highest density
of the reported HEAs is 13.75 × 10 3 kg/m3, the density of the
ReMoTaW alloy (16.69 × 10 3 kg/m3) is signi ﬁcantly higher relative to
that of the other HEAs and meet the density requirement.
The compression tests and the hardness were performed to evaluate
the mechanical properties. Compressive engineering stress-strain curve
for the ReMoTaW alloy is illustrated in Fig. 3. This alloy shows a
maximum strength, σm, of 1451 MPa, and fails by splitting at
εp = 5.69%. Here εp is the plastic strain. The maximum strength and
strain to failure of the ReMoTaW alloy in the current study are ≈20%
and ≈170% higher relative to those of NbMoTaW alloy [ 5]. The Re-
MoTaW alloy shows the enhanced room-temperature plasticity, mainly
because this alloy has smaller lattice distortion due to the smaller
atomic size of Re than that of Nb [ 14]. In addition, as tetragonal β-Ta
phase exhibit the higher hardness [ 16], the precipitation of relatively
ﬁne and homogeneously distributed β-Ta-rich particles are likely to
impede the motion of dislocations in the BCC matrix and increase the
strength of the alloy in the form of precipitation strengthening [ 20].
SEM image of the fracture surface is also shown in Fig. 3(b), in which
quasi-cleavage fracture with evident characteristic such as river-pattern
markings and a large amount of ﬁne second phase particles are ob-
served. The hardness of the ReMoTaW alloy is determined to be
640 ± 23 HV, which is ≈44% higher than that of NbMoTaW alloy. For
metallic alloys, the cocktail e ﬀect indicates that the unexpected prop-
erties can be obtained after mixing many elements, which could not be
obtained from any one independent element, and the cocktail e ﬀect
implies that the alloy properties can be greatly adjusted by the com-
position change and alloying [ 21]. The enhanced hardness of the Re-
MoTaW alloy is caused by the cocktail e ﬀect, which is originated from
the high hardness of Re, and precipitation strengthening of relatively
ﬁne and homogeneously distributed β-Ta-rich particles.
4. Conclusions
In summary, a novel ReMoTaW high-entropy alloy with high den-
sity of 16.69 × 10
3 kg/m3 was synthesized by vacuum arc melting. The
high-entropy alloy displays three disordered solid solution phases. The
maximum strength (1451 MPa) and the failure strain (5.69%) of the
ReMoTaW alloy are ≈20% and ≈170% higher relative to those of the
NbMoTaW alloy, originating from Re having smaller atomic size and
the precipitation strengthening of relatively ﬁne and homogeneously
distributed tetragonal β-Ta-rich particles. The hardness is
640 ± 23 HV and increases by ≈44% than that of NbMoTaW alloy,
originating from the combination of cocktail e ﬀect and the precipita-
tion strengthening mechanism.
