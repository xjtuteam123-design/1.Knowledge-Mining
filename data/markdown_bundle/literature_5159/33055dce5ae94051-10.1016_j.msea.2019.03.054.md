1. Introduction 
For high-temperature applications, such as jet engi nes and turbines, in which 
traditional high-temperature alloys, such as Ni-bas ed superalloys are pushing close to 
their limits of performance and hardly to further r educe the density [1, 2]. Lighter and 
stronger refractory materials are highly needed to explore in order to further improve 
the efficiency. It is reported that refractory high -entropy alloys (RHEAs) (e.g. 
NbMoTaW [3] and AlMo 0.5 NbTa 0.5 TiZr [4]) exhibited superior high-temperature 
mechanical properties compared with traditional Nic kel-based superalloys (e.g. 
Inconel 718 and Mar-M247) and are considered as pro mising alternatives for 
high-temperature applications [3-7]. Nevertheless, these alloys show either inferior 
room-temperature ductility (<10%) or relatively hig h densities (>8.5g/cm 3), which 
substantially hinders their practical applications. 
To explore refractory high entropy alloys with low density and balanced mechanical 
properties, in this study, we fabricated a series o f novel Ti 50-xAl xV20 Nb 20 Mo 10 (x=10, 
15, 20 in molar percentage, denoted with Al10, Al15, Al20) alloys. Here, Al and Ti are 
added to reduce the density and improve the strength, Ti, V , Nb, and Mo are employed 
to guarantee high enough solidus and ensure high-te mperature performances. 
Concentrations of V , Nb, and Mo are kept constant t o ensure high melting point and 
high-temperature property, only Ti and Al are tuned simultaneously. Single BCC solid 
solution were predicted by the commonly suggested c riteria, i.e. the enthalpy of 
mixing -15 ≤∆Hmix ≤5kJ/mol, the atomic size difference δ≤6.6%, the entropy of mixing 
12 ≤ ∆Smix ≤ 17.5J/(K·mol), and valence electron concentration VEC <6.75[8, 9].

from reference [10].

3. Results and discussion 
The novel RHEAs exhibit significantly reduced densities of ~6 g/cm 3 (Table 2), which 
are much lower than those of materials for high tem perature applications (e.g. 
8.22g/cm 3 for Inconel 718 [11], 7.8 g/cm 3 for Mar-M247 [12], most RHEAs 
(>8.5g/cm 3) [7]). Significantly reduced densities of this novel RHEAs attribute to high 
concentrations of Al (2.7g/cm 3), V (6.11g/cm 3), and Ti (4.5g/cm 3) which are much 
lighter than those of the traditional refractory me tals, such as Nb, Mo, and W. XRD 
patterns (Figure 1 (a)) indicate that all these RHE As exhibit a single disordered BCC 
phase, which is well consistent with previous predi ctions. Microstructures of single 
solid solution phases in these alloys are also shown in the Supplementary Information. 
It is also noticeable that, as increasing the Al content, peaks shift slightly to higher 2θ, 
as typically shown by the enlarged (110) peaks on t he upper right corner of Figure 1 
(a). According to Bragg's law, this shifting corres ponds to the decrease of lattice 
parameters, which probably attributes to the smalle r atomic radius of Al (143 pm) 
compared with Ti (147 pm), i.e. increasing the Al c ontent reduces the lattice 
parameters. Based on XRD measurements, lattice para meters of disordered BCC 
phases in Al10, Al15 and Al20 were calculated as 0.3215nm, 0.3197nm and 0.3175nm 
respectively (Table 2). TEM images of Al10, Al15 an d Al20 are also shown in Fig.

increasing the Al content, and this trend is more e vident at lower temperatures than at 
1000°C. Comparing Figure 2(e) with (f), it is obvio us that, for A10 and A15, stresses

temperatures, stain hardening exhibits in both Al10 and Al20 at low plastic strains 
after yielding but it absents in Al20 when the true strain >6%; at high plastic strain, all 
alloys exhibit near zero stain hardening rates whic h implies that there was no 
softening occurred in these alloy during the steady plastic deformation. At 1000°C 
(Figure 2(i)), all alloys exhibit strain hardening at low plastic strains and no softening 
occurs at higher strains.

4. Conclusions 
Novel Ti 
50-xAl xVNbMo RHEAs exhibit combinations of high strength, good plasticity 
and significantly reduced density, which are superi or to the vast majority of reported 
RHEAs and are comparable with typical Nickel based superalloys. The 
comprehensive advantages of the novel RHEAs make th emselves competitive and 
promising candidates for high-temperature applicati ons, with great contributions on 
saving energy, improving efficiency, and ensuring good machinability and mechanical 
properties.
