Effect of Nb Concentration on Thermal Stability
and Glass-Forming Ability of Soft Magnetic
(Fe,Co)-Gd-Nb-B Glassy Alloys
WEI ZHANG, FEI JIA, XINGGUO ZHANG, GUOQIANG XIE, and AKIHISA INOUE
Addition of a small amount of Nb to the (Fe,Co)-Gd-B glassy alloy in (Fe 0.9Co0.1)71.5/C0x
NbxGd3.5B25 increased the stabilization of supercooled liquid. The largest supercooled liquid
region of 104 K was obtained for the x = 2 alloy. A distinct two-stage-like glass transition was
observed with further incresing Nb content. The nanoscale (Fe,Co) 23B6 phase precipitated in the
glassy matrix after annealing, while the two-stage-like glass transition disappeared, indicating that
the anomalous glass transition behavior originates from the exothermic reaction for the formation
of the (Fe,Co) 23B6 phase in the supercooled liquid region. The glass-forming ability (GFA) also
increased by addition of Nb, leading to formation of the bulk glass form for the Nb-doped alloys.
The best GFA with a diameter of over 3 mm was achieved for the x = 4 alloy. The (Fe,Co)-Gd-
Nb-B glassy alloys exhibited good magnetic properties, i.e., rather high saturation magnetization
of 0.81 to 1.22 T, low coercive force of 2.5 to 5.8 A/m, and low saturated magnetostriction of 9 to
19 9 10
/C06. In addition, the glassy alloys also possessed very high compressive fracture strength of
3842 to 3916 MPa and high Vickers hardness of 1025 to 1076.

I. INTRODUCTION
SINCE the syntheses of bulk glassy alloys (BGAs) in
multicomponent Ln- [1] (Ln: lanthanide metal) and
Mg-[2] based alloys by the copper mold casting method
in 1989, much eﬀort has been devoted to develop new
BGAs exhibiting high glass-forming ability (GFA) and
functional properties. As a result, a large number of
BGA systems were developed, and they exhibited unique
properties such as high strength, large elastic strain,
high hardness, good soft magnetic properties, excellent
corrosion resistance, and Newtonian viscosity in the
supercooled liquid temperature region.
[3–5] For ferro-
magnetic BGAs, since the synthesis of the Fe-(Al,Ga)-
(P,C,B,Si),
[6] the Fe- and Co-based BGAs have gained
considerable interest because they exhibit excellent soft
magnetic properties, high GFA, distinct supercooled
liquid region DT
x (deﬁned by the diﬀerence between
glass transition temperature Tg and crystallization
temperature Tx), and high fracture strength. [7] The
ferromagnetic BGAs can be generally classiﬁed into ﬁve
systems, i.e., (Fe, Co)-(Al, Ga)-(P, C, B, Si), [6,8] (Fe,
Co)-TM(= Zr, Hf, Mo, Hf, Ta, W)-B, [9–11] (Fe, Co)-Ln-
B,[12,13] (Fe, Co)-TM-(B, Si), [7,14,15] and Fe-(P, B, Si). [16]
The Fe62Co9.5Ln3.5B25 glassy alloys exhibited larger DTx
above 50 K and good soft magnetic properties with high
saturation magnetization, and the alloy with Ln = Gd
showed the largest DT
x of 63 K and best soft magnetic
properties.[17] We have noticed that the TM elements
have the intermediate atomic radius between Fe(Co) and
Ln.
[18] In addition, the TM-Fe, TM-Co, and TM-B pairs
have negative heats of mixing. [19] Thus, the (Fe,Co)-Ln-
TM-B alloy system with higher GFA and higher
stabilization of supercooled liquid can be expected.
[5]
We also conﬁrmed that the addition of a small amount
(2 at. pct) TM to Fe
62Co9.5Nd3Dy0.5B25 alloy increased
DTx and GFA. The largest DTx was 87 K for the alloy
with TM = Nb addition. [20] However, the GFA was
not so high, and the critical diameter ( dc) for formation
of a single glassy phase was below 1.5 mm. [21] In this
study, with the aim of developing a new ferromagnetic
BGA with high GFA and large DTx, we have investi-
gated the eﬀect of Nb concentration on the stabilization
of supercooled liquid, GFA, and magnetic properties of
a (Fe,Co)-Gd-B glassy alloy. In addition, the crystalline
behavior of the (Fe,Co)-Gd-Nb-B glassy alloy with a
two-stage-like glass transition was investigated.

III. RESULTS
Figure 1 shows DSC curves of the (Fe 0.9Co0.1)71.5/C0x-
NbxGd3.5B25 (x = 0 to 6) glassy alloys. As marked with
arrows, the Tg slightly increases with increasing Nb
content to 6 at. pct, while the Tx and DTx signiﬁcantly
increase from 904 to 966 K and 58 to 104 K, respec-
tively. However, an additional inﬂection point ( T
inf) can
be clearly observed in the supercooled liquid region of
the alloys with x = 3 to 6, which was initially inter-
preted as a two-stage-like glass transition. This ﬁnding
makes it diﬃcult to decide the DT
x value of the glassy
alloys.
In order to clarify the reason for the two-stage-like
glass transition of the alloys, we investigated the
crystalline behavior of the x = 4 alloy. Figures 2 and
3 show XRD patterns and DSC curves of the glassy
alloy annealed for 600 seconds at temperatures ( Ta)
ranging from 873 to 973 K, respectively. No crystalline
peaks were observed for the samples annealed at Ta
below 913 K. However, several diﬀraction peaks appear
on a broad halo pattern after annealing at 923 K,
indicating the precipitation of crystalline phases in
the glassy matrix. The diﬀraction peaks were identiﬁed
as a metastable (Fe,Co)
23B6 phase. Further annealing
resulted in the precipitation of (Fe,Co) 2Nb, (Fe,Co) 2B,
(Fe,Co)2GdB, and unknown phases at 973 K. A signif-
icant change in the glass transition behavior is also
observed upon annealing (Figure 3). With increasing T
a,
Tg remains unchanged, while Tx gradually decreases.

IV. DISCUSSION
A two-stage-like glass transition phenomenon can be
observed for the alloys with an addition of Nb of over 3
at. pct. Two-stage-like glass transition phenomena in
Fe-based glassy alloys have been reported or observed
for the (Fe,Co)-Nb-Ln-B (Ln = Nd, Dy, Tb)
[21,23] and
Fe-Nb-Y-B[24,25] alloys. However, the origin of the
anomalous glass transition behavior has not been
thoroughly investigated. Generally, two-stage glass
transition has been presumed to originate from the
phase separation into the two diﬀerent glassy phases in
the supercooled liquid, as similarly reported in the
Zr-Y-Ni-Al
[26,27] and Ti-Zr-Al-Co [28] glassy alloys.

However, the experimental results obtained from
detailed and careful XRD and HRTEM analyses
of the (Fe 0.9Co0.1)67.5Nb4Gd3.5B25 glassy alloys have
ruled out the possibility of the presence of two glassy
phases.
The DSC results (Figure 3) demonstrate that the
thermal history aﬀects the two-stage-like endothermic
reaction of the glassy alloy with x = 4. When T
a is
increased to 923 K, only one glass transition is observed.
From the XRD and TEM analyses (Figures 2 and 4),
the primary crystalline phase of the (Fe 0.9Co0.1)67.5-
Nb4Gd3.5B25 glassy alloy is identiﬁed as a phase of
Fe23B6 type, which has been generally recognized for Fe-
based glassy alloys containing Nb and B elements. [29,30]
The results suggest that the two-stage-like glass transi-
tion is attributed to the overlap of an exothermic
reaction for the formation of the nanocrystalline
(Fe,Co)
23B6 phase in the supercooled liquid region with
the endothermic reaction for the glass transition.
The appearance of the two-stage-like glass transition
phenomena may be due to the compositional ﬂuctuation
in the (Fe,Co)-Gd-Nb-B glassy alloy. There is a large
atomic-size diﬀerence and the negative heats of mixing
among the constituent elements are obtained in the
(Fe,Co)-Gd-B system, leading to the formation of the
glassy phases, which have dense random networks of
trigonal prismlike local units .
[31] The addition of Nb into
(Fe,Co)-Gd-B alloy causes the sequent change in
atomic sizes among the constituents (Gd 0.180 nm >
Nb 0.147 nm > Fe 0.127 nm, Co 0.125 nm > B
0.098 nm) [18] and the generation of new strong chemical
aﬃnities of Nb-B ( /C039 kJ /mol), Nb-Fe ( /C016 kJ /mol),
and Nb-Co ( /C030 kJ /mol) pairs, [19] which contribute to
the strengthening of the random networks of a trigonal
prismlike local unit, leading to enhancement of the
stabilization of the supercooled liquid. On the other
hand, a large positive heat of mixing between Nb and
Gd (30 kJ/mol)
[19] could also contribute to repulsive
chemical interactions, and may result in the local
compositional ﬂuctuation in the supercooled liquid. [32]
When the alloy is annealed, the nanoscale (Fe,Co) 23B6
phase separation can occur through the local composi-
tional ﬂuctuation.
The Nb-doped alloys exhibit a higher GFA (Table I).
Usually, the positive heats of mixing between the
constituent elements in the alloy systems are considered
to degrade the GFA or lead to limited GFA enhance-
ment.
[5] However, the present result is likely, because the
Nb-doped alloys have a lower Tl, resulting in a higher
Trg, which implies that the viscosity of the supercooled
liquid for the present alloy increases rapidly with
decreasing temperature, leading to easy solidiﬁcation
of supercooled liquid into a glassy solid state under the
suppression of crystallization reaction.
[5] In addition,
the primary precipitation phase is the metastable
(Fe,Co)23B6 phase for the Nb-doped alloys. The precip-
itation of the Fe 23C6-type phase having a complex
fcc structure from the networklike glassy structure
requires long-range atomic rearrangements of constitu-
ent elements and results in the enhancement of thermal
stability of the glassy phase against crystallization,
leading to the increase of GFA.
[31]
V. CONCLUSIONS
We investigated the thermal stability, crystalline
behavior, GFA, and magnetic and mechanical proper-
ties of the Fe-based (Fe 0.9Co0.1)71.5/C0xNbxGd3.5B25
(x = 0–6) glassy alloys. The results obtained are sum-
marized as follows.
1. The stabilization of supercooled liquid greatly
increased by the addition of a small amount of Nb.
The largest DTx of 104 K was obtained for the
x = 2 alloy . However, a distinct two-stage-like
glass transition phenomenon was observed with
further increasing Nb contents.
2. A metastable nanoscale (Fe,Co) 23B6 phase precipi-
tated in the glassy matrix after annealing, while the
two-stage-like glass transition disappeared, indicat-
ing that the two-stage-like glass transition originates
from the exothermic reaction for the formation of
the (Fe,Co)
23B6 phase in the supercooled liquid
region.
3. Addition of a small amount of Nb to (Fe 0.9Co0.1)71.5-
Gd3.5B25 alloy lowered the Tl and resulted in an
increase in Trg, leading to enhancement of the GFA.
The best GFA with a diameter of 3 mm was achieved
for the alloy x =4 .
4. The saturation magnetization ( I
s) values of the
glassy alloys decreased with increasing Nb content.
However, all the alloys exhibited good soft mag-
netic properties, i.e., rather high I
s of 0.81 to 1.22
T, low coercive force of 2.5 to 5.8 A/m, and low
saturated magnetostriction of 9 to 19 9 10/C06.
5. The Fe-based BGAs also possessed high compres-
sive fracture strength of 3842 to 3916 MPa, high
Young’s modulus of 185 to 192 GPa, and high
Vickers hardness of 1025 to 1076.
