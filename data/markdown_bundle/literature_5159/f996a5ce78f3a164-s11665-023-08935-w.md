REVIEW
A Review of High-Temperature Toughness Improvement
Strategies for Medium Entropy Alloys
Lei Wang, Haotian Zhou, Yazhou Chen, Yongfei Gao, Qingming Gan, Zhi Li, Yuntao Xi, Keren Zhang, Shanna Xu, Haitao Liu, Lei Wen,
Xinke Xiao, and Jiangtao Ji
Submitted: 1 March 2023 / Revised: 6 September 2023 / Accepted: 20 September 2023 / Published online: 13 November 2023
Since the medium entropy alloy (MEA) was proposed, it has become one of the key areas of material
research. Due to the multiple components in the MEA, it has a variety of combinations, and it usually shows
high strength, high plasticity and excellent fracture toughness at low temperature. However, at high tem-
perature, its comprehensive mechanical properties and toughness are signiﬁcantly reduced, which greatly
limits its application in engineering. This paper reviews the mechanical properties of MEAs, and discusses
the effects of transformation strengthening, solution strengthening and plastic deformation on the fracture
toughness of MEAs at high temperatures. It is found that the MEA has competitive impact fracture
toughness at low temperature, but with the increase in temperature, the comprehensive mechanical
properties of MEA materials will continue to decline, and the impact fracture toughness value will also
reduce. The mechanical properties of high/medium entropy alloy can be improved obviously by introducing
carbon, oxygen, nitrogen, boron and other interstitial elements. By introducing alloying elements into MEA
systems, additional strengthening effects such as solution strengthening, grain boundary strengthening and
precipitation strengthening can be achieved, thus improving the fracture toughness of materials at high
temperatures. The gradient twin structure constructed by plastic deformation will not change the stacking
fault energy (SFE) of MEAs, which is an effective method to improve the quasi-static and impact toughness
of MEA.
Keywords medium entropy alloy (MEA), strength, temperature,
toughness, twins
1. Introduction
Metal materials are an important material basis necessary for
the survival and development of human society, and play an
important role in promoting the development and progress of
human society (Ref 1-3). From the early ancient times, human
civilization has been continuously committed to developing
new materials. From the discovery of new metals to the
invention of new alloys, these alloys have played a key role for
thousands of years (Ref 4). According to mixing entropy ( D
Smix), alloys can be divided into high entropy alloys (HEA, D
Smix > 1.61R), medium entropy alloy (MEA, 0.69R £ D
Smix £ 1.61R) and low entropy alloy (LEA, D Smix < 0.69
R) three categories (Ref 5). Since the concept of HEA was put
forward by Professor Y e Junwei of Taiwan National Tsinghua
University in 2004 (Ref 6), it has been nearly 20 years and is
regarded as one of the major breakthroughs in the ﬁeld of metal
materials research in recent decades. HEA creates the concept
of multi-principal element design, breaks away from the
limitation of single principal element design of traditional
alloy and has advantages of high strength, hardness, wear and
corrosion resistance, as well as high-temperature thermal
stability. It not only has great research value in theoretical
research, but also has great application potential in the
industrial ﬁeld, and has been widely concerned by researchers
all over the world (Ref 6-13). MEA materials, developed from
HEAs, exhibit more excellent properties than HEA materials in

Extreme conditions widely exist in important ﬁelds such as

The emergence of MEA provides a huge exploration space
for the comprehensive properties of materials under complex
conditions. MEA materials have some mechanical properties
lacking in traditional alloys, such as high strength
(1.0 /C24 1.5 GPa), high plasticity ( > 50%). In addition, MEA
materials are characterized by excellent impact fracture tough-
ness (> 350 J) at low temperatures (Ref 19, 20). However, our
understanding of the mechanical properties of MEA at elevated
temperatures is still limited. According to the literature, almost
all MEAs will lose their outstanding strength and ductility at

development. It is precisely because the temperature of the
application environment of the MEA has an important inﬂuence
on its properties that more and more scholars have carried out
research on it.
The present work summarizes and analyzes the methods for
improving the room temperature and high-temperature tough-
ness of MEA through literature research, and discusses them
from three aspects: solid solution strengthening, phase trans-
formation strengthening and plastic deformation. Both of the
advantages and disadvantages of various strengthening meth-
ods are compared, and ﬁnally, the effect of adiabatic shear
localization under impact loading conditions is analyzed.
2. Effect of Phase Transformation Strengthening
on Toughness of MEA
In fact, all factors that affect the mechanical properties of
MEA and HEA at high temperatures have long been considered
in the development of traditional superalloys. Therefore, the
study of material strengthening strategies used in traditional
high-temperature materials can provide ideas for strengthening
MEA under high-temperature conditions. In order to improve
the mechanical properties of MEAs at high temperatures,
researchers have tried many methods. Adding alloy elements is
one of the most widely studied methods at present. By
introducing alloy elements into the MEA system, it can replace
the atoms in the lattice position of the alloy solid solution or ﬁll
in the gap position, resulting in lattice distortion of the system,
so as to achieve additional strengthening effects such as solid
solution strengthening, grain boundary strengthening and
precipitation strengthening. At the same time, alloyed elements
can introduce the second phase into the system. When the alloy
is stressed, dislocation movement bypassing the second phase
particles occurs dislocation strengthening or alloyed elements
as the additional second phase particles are ﬁne and dispersed
in the alloy matrix, forming a non-coherent relationship with
the alloy, and ﬁnally the mechanical properties and toughness
of the material can be improved (Ref 23).
Studies have shown that when appropriate elements are
added to FCC HEA, hard BCC intermetallic phases can be
formed in FCC soft matrix, thus effectively enhancing the
strong plastic synergy of HEA (Ref 15). The r phase is a well-
known intermetallic phase, which has excellent wear resistance
(Ref 24), high creep strength (Ref 25), and can maintain
acceptable strength even at high temperatures (Ref 26). In the
past decades, numerous scholars have made great efforts to try
to use r phase to enhance the toughness of MEAs. However,
the r phase formed in alloys will result in a signiﬁcant
reduction in its ductility, even if only a small amount r phase
precipitated can also lead to this phenomenon (Ref 27-29).
Wang et al. (Ref 30) found that when the volume fraction of
r phase in Fe-Cr-Ni stainless steel alloy reaches 5%, the tensile
strength can be increased by 10%, but the elongation decreases
from 0.6 to 0.5, and the impact toughness decreases from 275
to 65 J. In principle, the brittleness of r phase is due to its
topologically tightly arranged complex atomic structure (Ref
31). Therefore, in order to make up for this shortcoming, some
scholars have compared the secondary ductile phase with the r
combining to form a dual-phase metal composite material to
attempt toughening.
A newly designed V

and the yield strength increases from 352 to 587 MPa (Ref 32),
as shown in Fig. 2. This suggests that the multi-principal r
phase may not be as brittle as the traditional r phase. In other
words, by adjusting the composition of the multi-principal
alloy, a new generation of metal composites with excellent

3. Effect of Solution Strengthening on Toughness
of MEA
The principle of solution strengthening of MEA system is
similar to that of traditional alloys. By adding metal elements,
lattice friction is increased and mechanical properties can be
improved (Ref 39). On the basis of alloy design by Labush
model, 3% W element is added to CrCoNi alloy to produce
Solid solution strengthening effect and signiﬁcantly improve
the mechanical properties of the alloy (Ref 40). At the same
time, the addition of tungsten has an important impact on the
microstructure and mechanical properties of CrCoNi entropy
alloy, which has been experimentally conﬁrmed. As the
tungsten content increases, the recrystallization behavior of
the alloy is inhibited (Ref 41). The doping of W in CrCoNi
alloy inhibits the dislocation movement and grain boundary
migration during recrystallization. The addition of 3 at.% W
can increase the yield strength of CrCoNi alloy by 33%, while
maintaining high ductility and work hardening ability. Adding
Mo element to the MEAs can improve the strength and wear
resistance of FCC structured CrCoNi alloy (Ref 42). The
addition of Mo can cause serious lattice distortion of the alloy,
promote the formation of intermetallic compound phase and
signiﬁcantly improve the hardness and compressive yield
strength, but the generated intermetallic compounds have high
hardness and brittleness, and decrease the plasticity. The
fracture toughness of the alloy increases ﬁrst and then decreases
with the increase in Mo content due to the strengthening of
solution and the brittle competition of intermetallic compounds.

4. Effect of Plastic Deformation on Toughness
of MEA
FCC phase high entropy alloys typically exhibit high
fracture strength and good ductility due to their good plastic
deformation ability. Compared with FCC structure, the
medium/high entropy alloy of BCC structure has poor plastic
deformation ability, but its strength can reach up to 4.5 GPa,
surpassing most metal materials. The mechanical properties of
several typical MEAs/HEAs with FCC and BCC structure are
shown in Table 1. When the yield strength and fracture
elongation of these HEA/MEAs with FCC or BCC structures
are placed in the same ﬁgure (as shown in Fig. 4), it is not
difﬁcult to ﬁnd that the HEA/MEA with BCC structure (red
area) has signiﬁcantly higher strength, while the HEA/MEA
with FCC structure (black area) has better fracture elongation.
In the process of plastic deformation of materials, disloca-
tion slip and twinning deformation are two basic mechanisms
that coordinate material deformation. Dislocation slip refers to
the relative sliding of adjacent parts of a crystal along a certain
crystal plane and direction under the action of shear stress
during the loading process of a crystal material, which is
macroscopic manifested as plastic deformation of the crystal
material. Twin deformation refers to the deformation process in
which some crystals undergo shear along a certain crystal plane
(twin plane) and direction (twin direction) under the action of
shear stress when the movement of dislocations is obstructed
(Ref 65).
The twin deformation mechanism has always been consid-
ered as one of the important reasons why MEAs have excellent
mechanical properties and high toughness at low temperatures
(Ref 20, 66). The decrease in toughness of MEAs at room and
high temperatures is largely due to the high-temperature
environment is not conducive to the formation of twin
structures (Ref 67, 68). The twin interface can not only block
the movement of dislocations, but also react at the twin
interface to relieve the high stress state caused by congestion,
which can increase both strength and toughness (Ref 17, 69).
The twinning deformation during the deformation process of
medium/high entropy alloys can signiﬁcantly improve the yield
strength and strain hardening ability of the alloy. Bi et al. (Ref
70) demonstrated through their research on Cr
20Fe6Co34Ni34-
Mo6 alloy that twin boundaries can effectively hinder the
movement of dislocations.

rate of the material, thus exhibiting a strain rate effect in
mechanical properties. Under low-temperature impact loading

becomes the key to obtain excellent strength, plasticity and
toughness at room temperature and high temperature, and is
also the focus of researchers.
Improving the toughness of MEA at room temperature and
high temperature by phase transformation and solution
strengthening will change its SFE, and the twin deformation
mechanism cannot be used. The microstructure of the material
determines its macro-mechanical behavior. For MEAs, the
modiﬁcation of the microstructure through plastic deformation
will not change its chemical composition and SFE. The strength
and toughness can be comprehensively improved by using the
twin deformation mechanism.
As an important means of toughening metal materials,
gradient structure refers to that the size of structural units (such
as grain size or twin spacing) of materials changes in a gradient
in space, continuously increasing from nanoscale to macroscale
(Ref 69). The gradient change of twin interface is different from
the simple mixing or compounding of different sizes, which can
effectively avoid the performance mutation caused by the
sudden change of structural feature size, make twin structures
with different spacing coordinate with each other and optimize
as well as improve the overall performance and service
behavior of the material (Ref 82).
Through high-energy shot peening (HESP) treatment,
gradient nanocrystalline (GNG) structure was introduced into
FCC type CrCoNi MEA, and it has been found that gradient
nanocrystalline (GNG) CrCoNi MEA exhibited excellent
strength and ductility synergistic properties, with yield strength
and ultimate tensile strength reaching 1215 and 1524 MPa,
while maintaining approximately 23% ductility (Ref 75).
Ultimately, it has been proven that constructing a GNG
structure is a feasible and effective method to address the
trade-off between strength and ductility in FCC type MEAs/
HEAs and other low SFE materials. Wu Xiaolei et al. (Ref 20)
pointed out that non-uniform structure can better combine
strength and toughness, and conﬁrmed in their research (Ref
83) that the gradient distribution of stress/strain plays an
important role in the tensile deformation process. As the
incompatible deformation evolves along the gradient depth, the
applied uniaxial stress can be transformed into multiaxial stress,
thus promoting the accumulation and interaction of dislocations
and enhancing additional strain hardening and uniform elon-

5. Effect of Adiabatic Shear Localization
on Impact Toughness of MEA
Adiabatic shear localization (ASL) is a deformation mode of
materials under impact load (such as ballistic impact, collision).
This phenomenon usually occurs in extreme conditions such as
large shear strain ( > 1), high strain rate ( > 10
3 s-1), which
leads to strain localization and makes the strain occur in a width
of about 1 /C24 200 lm of the narrow area, the failure of
materials and structures is caused. At room temperature and
high temperature ( >298 K), the strain hardening ability of
MEA materials decreases signiﬁcantly. Under the high-temper-
ature high strain rate coupling loading conditions, ASL
phenomenon is one of the challenges faced by MEAs in
deformation (Ref 77), resulting in a signiﬁcant reduction in
shear toughness and strength, as shown in Fig. 9. The
occurrence of ASL phenomenon is the precursor of the failure
of MEAs and structures, and an important reason for the failure
of materials under impact load, which largely restricts the
application of MEAs in the extreme environment of high-
temperature and high strain rate. Therefore, a clear understand-
ing of the basic deformation mechanism of MEA materials
under high-temperature impact loads is essential for the design
of advanced impact-resistant structures. Restraining the adia-
batic shear localization of MEAs at high-temperature and high
strain rate is of guiding signiﬁcance for its engineering
application under extreme conditions, and is also one of the
key issues in the study of impact dynamics.

breakthroughs in the ﬁeld of metal materials research in recent
decades. Multi-principal components break through the tradi-
tional alloy design concept, making the alloy have high
strength, high plasticity, high hardness, high wear resistance

factors on their toughness under high temperature have been

(1) MEAs have excellent impact fracture toughness at low

(2) The mechanical properties of MEA can be improved

(3) Adding high melting point solute is the main choice to

reducing entropy.
(4) By introducing alloying elements into MEA systems,

mation strengthening, solid solution strengthening and
precipitation strengthening can be achieved, thus
improving the fracture toughness of materials at high
temperatures.

mation cannot change the SFE of MEA, which is an
effective method to improve the quasi-static and impact
toughness of MEAs.
7. Areas for further research
Considering the application prospects of MEAs in various
complex environments in the future, it is urgent to study their
strengthening and toughening under high-temperature condi-
tions. Future research should focus on the following aspects:
(1) Design of MEAs with different microstructures: V arious
deformation methods are supposed to use to form MEAs
with different microstructures, fully utilizing the twin-
ning deformation mechanism, and exploring the
strengthening and toughening mechanism from multiple
perspectives of gradient microstructure design, tempera-
ture effect and strain rate effect.
(2) Analyze the crack arrest and toughening of MEAs with
different microstructures, including setting precracks on
the samples at different twin density locations, studying
the crack initiation time and propagation speed at differ-
ent twin densities, the blocking effect of twin structures
with different densities on the uneven deformation at the
crack tip, the inﬂuence rule on fracture toughness and
the failure mode caused by cracks at different twin den-
sities as well as its relationship with deformation
strengthening mechanism.
(3) Research on the generation criteria of adiabatic shear
localization in MEAs with different microstructures un-
der high-temperature high strain rate loading conditions,
analyze the mechanism and characteristics of shear band
formation, improve the sensitivity model of adiabatic
shear localization and the generation criteria based on
critical strain.
(4) Suppressing the adiabatic shear localization phenomenon
of MEAs under high-temperature high strain rates: A
clear understanding of the basic deformation mechanism
of MEA materials under high-temperature impact loads
is crucial for designing advanced impact-resistant struc-
tures. The suppression of shear localization has impor-
tant engineering application signiﬁcance.
