ABSTRACT 
Co is a valuable γ solid solution strengthening element in nickel-based superalloys that improves their micro -
structure and creep resistance. However, the impact of Co additions on the kinetics of γ ′ precipitation remains 
controversial. In this study, we conducted CALPHAD-informed phase field simulations to quantitatively analyze 
the γ ′ precipitation process in Ni – Al – Co model superalloys and uncover the Co effect on the temporal evolution 
kinetics of the γ ′ phase. Our simulations revealed that the γ ′ coarsening rate initially decreases, then increases at 
1173 K with increasing Co content, a change that can be attributed to the competition between the γ / γ ′ lattice 
misfit and interdiffusion coefficients. Although Co is a γ -stabilized element, our calculations and previous ex -
periments show an anomalous increase in γ ′ volume fraction with increasing Co concentration, which is linked to 
variations in elemental partitioning behaviors between the γ matrix and γ ′ phase. Furthermore, the unique 
partitioning behavior of Ni suggests partial replacement of Al atoms at the corner site of γ ′ -Ni
3
(Al, Ni) sublattice 
by Ni atoms. Our research offers a new perspective on the effect of Co concentration on the kinetics of γ ′ pre -
cipitation in Ni – Al – Co model superalloys and outlines a guideline for designing novel multicomponent super -
alloys with superior microstructural stability. 
1. Introduction 
Nickel-based superalloys have been intensively used for turbine disk 
and turbine blades of aircraft engine because of their superior me -
chanical properties at high temperatures, which benefits from the 
microstructure with the L1
2
-ordered γ ′ precipitation coherently 
embedded in fcc γ matrix [ 1 , 2 ]. The morphology, size, volume fraction, 
and distribution of γ ′ phase influence the strength and creep properties 
of nickel-based superalloys [3] . The particle size of γ ′ could grow during 
long-term service at elevated temperatures, which can significantly 
affect the service life of nickel-based superalloys [4] . It was reported that 
the morphological evolution of γ ′ phase is dominated by the γ / γ ′ lattice 
misfit [ 5 , 6 ] and elemental diffusion [ 7 , 8 ], which can be controlled by 
alloying [ 9 , 10 ] and the elemental partitioning behavior between γ ma -
trix and γ ′ phase [11] . Hence, studying the alloying and elemental 
partition on the kinetic behavior of γ ′ precipitation in nickel-based 
superalloys is of particular importance for designing novel superalloys 
with superior microstructural stability. 
As the key γ ′ -Ni
3
Al formation element, Al is the major constituent 
element in nickel-based superalloys. The additional elements, such as 
Co, Mo, W, Re, etc., improve the mechanical properties of nickel-based 
superalloys [12 – 14] . Unfortunately, high levels of refractory elements 
Mo, W, and Re are prone to the precipitation of deleterious topologically 
close-packed (TCP) phase due to their limited solubility in γ matrix 
[15 – 17] . However, Co can inhibit the formation of TCP phases to 
improve the creep resistance of nickel-based superalloys [18 – 20] . Since 
Co and Ni possess similar electronegativity and atomic radius, they can 
form a continuous solid solution [ 21 , 22 ], making Ni – Al – Co alloys 
become a key sub-system of nickel-based superalloys [23] and even high 
entropy alloys (HEAs) [24] . Co has been reported to play a key role in 
enhancing the creep resistance of nickel-based superalloys by decreasing 
the stacking fault energy (SFE) of γ matrix [25 – 27] . Moreover, the Co

of the effect of Co concentration on the precipitation kinetics of γ ′ and 
offers insights to develop high-performance nickel-based superalloys.

parameter fields were used to simulate the compositional and structural 
evolution of γ ′ phase, respectively. For the disordering-ordering transi -
tion, the classical time-dependent Cahn – Hilliard and Ginzburg – Landau 
equations are introduced into the present work to govern the evolution 
process of the conserved concentration fields and non-conserved order 
parameter fields, respectively [38] .

The size of γ ′ precipitates is influenced by the kinetic process

Besides, the correlation between the γ / γ ′ lattice misfit and the γ ′ 
coarsening rate is an important aspect to consider in understanding and 
predicting the evolution of microstructures in nickel-based superalloys. 
By investigating this relationship, we gain insights into the factors 
influencing the coarsening kinetics and can guide the design and opti -
mization of these alloys. 
Experimental observations have indicated that Co could accelerate 
the diffusion rate of alloying elements and lead to a higher γ ′ coarsening 
rate [ 73 , 74 ]. Meanwhile, the γ / γ ′ lattice misfit has been reported to 
decrease with the increase of Co concentration, and the reduction of γ / γ ′ 
lattice misfit resulted in the decrease of γ / γ ′ interfacial energy and γ ′ 
coarsening rate [34] . Thus, with the increase of Co concentration, the 
γ / γ ′ lattice misfit decreases (see 3.1), but the interdiffusion coefficient of 
Al increases (see Table 1 ), resulting in the coarsening rate of γ ′ phase 
decreasing first and then increasing in Ni – Al – Co ternary model super -
alloys. Moreover, the competitive mechanisms have been confirmed by 
the reported experiment in multicomponent nickel-based superalloys 
[66] . The comprehensive role of the diffusion coefficient of the solute 
atoms and γ / γ ′ lattice misfit determines the γ ′ coarsening rate in 
Ni – Al – Co ternary model superalloys. 
The analysis above demonstrates that the γ ′ coarsening rate increases 
with increased γ / γ ′ lattice misfit and interdiffusion coefficients. To 
demonstrate further the interaction between γ / γ ′ lattice misfit and 
diffusion coefficients in Ni – Al – Co ternary model superalloys, we simu -
lated the γ / γ ′ lattice misfit and diffusion coefficients on the effect of γ ′ 
coarsening rates in Ni – 16 Al – 15 Co alloy as shown in Fig. 6 (a) and (b), 
respectively. It can be seen that the increased γ / γ ′ lattice misfit and 
diffusion coefficients increase the γ ′ coarsening rates, indicating the 
interaction between γ / γ ′ lattice misfit and diffusion coefficients in 
Ni – Al – Co alloys. To explore further the interaction between γ / γ ′ lattice 
misfit and diffusion coefficients in multicomponent nickel-based su -
peralloys, we plotted the linear fit of γ / γ ′ lattice misfit and diffusion 
coefficients versus γ ′ coarsening rates in multicomponent nickel-based 
superalloys as shown in Fig. 6 (c) and (d). The positive slope further 
reveals that the interplay between γ / γ ′ lattice misfit and diffusion co -
efficients persists in multicomponent nickel-based superalloys. Our 
findings have broad implications and underscore the need for balancing 
γ / γ ′ lattice misfit and diffusion coefficients when designing alloys to 
achieve the desired microstructural stability. Achieving this balance can 
be accomplished through adjustments to composition and heat- 
treatment histories during alloy design. 
To quantitatively investigate the influence of off-diagonal terms in 
the diffusivity matrix on the coarsening kinetics of the γ ′ phase, we 
conducted simulations using the Ni – 16 Al – 15 Co at.% alloy as a case 
study. This particular alloy was chosen due to its lowest observed γ ′ 
coarsening rate, as depicted in Fig. 4 (b). Our focus was specifically on 
examining the impact of both the diagonal and off-diagonal terms of the 
diffusivity on the coarsening kinetics, as illustrated in Fig. 7 . The results 
obtained clearly demonstrate that when the off-diagonal terms of the 
diffusivity are considered, the γ ′ coarsening rate decreases compared to 
simulations where these terms are neglected. This finding indicates that 
the absence of off-diagonal terms in the diffusivity leads to an over -
estimation of the γ ′ coarsening kinetics in Ni – Al – Co ternary model al -
loys. Furthermore, it is worth noting that previous studies [ 65 , 78 ] have 
reported that the incorporation of off-diagonal terms in the diffusivity 
aligns well with experimental data obtained from Atom Probe Tomog -
raphy (APT). This further emphasizes the significance of considering the 
off-diagonal terms in diffusivity for accurately quantifying the micro -
structural evolution. Therefore, it is crucial to include the off-diagonal 
terms of the diffusivity in quantitative simulations of microstructural 
changes to ensure the accuracy and reliability of the results.

thermodynamic calculations. 
CALPHAD-informed phase field simulations are indeed crucial for 
quantitatively understanding the dynamic process of microstructural 
evolution. However, the reliable acquisition of thermodynamic and ki -
netic information for multicomponent alloys often relies on commercial 
thermodynamic software such as Pandat and Thermo-Calc, among 
others. One limitation of these commercial software packages is lack of 
direct visibility into the underlying thermodynamic parameters of 
multicomponent alloys. Consequently, directly coupling CALPHAD 
thermodynamic parameters with phase field simulations becomes 
challenging. To address this challenge, further work is needed to 
establish a coupling between thermodynamic software and phase field 
simulations for microstructural simulations in multicomponent alloys. 
This would involve developing methodologies and algorithms that 
enable the extraction and integration of thermodynamic parameters 
from commercial software into phase field models [89 – 92] . 
4. Conclusion 
To summarize, our study employed CALPHAD-informed PF simula -
tions to investigate the influence of Co addition on the evolution kinetics 
of morphology and composition in Ni – Al – Co systems. Based on our 
simulations, we have reached the following conclusions: 
(1) The circle morphology of γ ′ precipitate phases in Ni – Al – Co alloys 
can be attributed to the small value of γ / γ ′ lattice misfit (less than 
0.002). The γ ′ coarsening behavior conforms to the classical LSW 
coarsening theory in Ni – Al – Co alloys, and exhibits diffusion- 
controlled coarsening. 
(2) The γ ′ coarsening rate constant initially decreases and then in -
creases with increasing Co concentration, due to the combined
