Abstract 
An extensive study of the composition distribution, bonding strength, hardness, and wear resistance of a 0Cr18Ni9 film deposited on a Ti811 
titanium alloy surface by ion beam enhanced deposition (IBED) is presented. Shot peening was introduced to post-treat the modified surface 
to synergistically improve the fretting fatigue resistance of the Ti811 alloy at 350°C. The results indicate that the 0Cr18Ni9 film with high 
density, small grain size, low void radio, and high bonding strength can be prepared using IBED. As a result, the hardness, wear resistance, 
and fretting fatigue resistance of the Ti811 alloy are increased to a remarkable extent. Compared with shot peening treatment o r IBED 
0Cr18Ni9 film alone, the Ti811 titanium alloy with an IBED 0Cr18Ni9 film combined with shot peening shows a higher fretting fatigue re-
sistance at 350°C. This is due to the synergistic effect of the high wear resistance of the film surface and the residual compressive stress in-
duced by shot peening. 
Keywords: titanium alloys; fretting fatigue; elevated temperature; shot peening; ion beam enhanced deposition 
 
 
1. Introduction 
Because of their low density, high Young’s modulus, ex-
cellent corrosion resistance, and good thermal stability, tita-
nium alloys have been widely used in manufacturing fans, 
compressor disks, and blades of aircraft engines. However, 
titanium alloys are very sensitive to fretting fatigue (FF) 
damage because of their low thermal conductivity and high 
coefficient of friction, which may result in FF failure in air-
craft engine compressor blade/disk dovetail joints [1-4]. 
Thus, studies of surface modification to improve the fretting 
fatigue resistance (FFR) of titanium alloys are vital for de-
veloping advanced aero-engines which have high reliability 
and long usable life. 
FF damage is closely related to the wear and fatigue re-
sistance of the surface of the material, and it has been recog-
nized that surface modification or coating can enhance the 
FFR of titanium alloys [5-8]. Shot peening (SP) is well 
known as a very effective method for improving FFR at 
room temperature [8-9]. However, the residual compressive 
stress induced by SP can be relaxed in a high temperature 
environment. Thus, the beneficial effect of SP on FFR will 
be reduced [10-11]. Combining shot peening with other sur-
face treatments appears to be a good method for improving 
the FFR of titanium alloys. Ion beam enhanced deposition 
(IBED) is a surface modification method which effectively 
combines ion implantation with physical vapour deposition. 
This method can improve the bonding strength between the 
film and the substrate. The wear resistance of the material 
surface can be improved without damaging the bulk proper-
ties of the substrate [12-13]. Furthermore, IBED films can 
outlive the post treatment of shot peening for obtaining good 
bonding strength, so IBED is suitable for combining with 
shot peening. Shot peening was combined with other surface 
treatments such as ion implantation and plasma thermal 
spraying in an attempt to improve the FFR of titanium alloys 
at room temperature [3, 9]. However, the FFR obtained after 
duplex treatment was lower than that after a single treatment 
of shot peening. The present authors have conducted re-
search on combining IBED with SP to improve the FFR of 
the Ti6Al4V titanium alloy at room temperature [14]. 
However, it was not known how a duplex surface modifica-
tion consisting of IBED and SP will affect the FFR at ele-
vated temperatures. 
The Ti811 titanium alloy is characterized by low density, 
high Young’s modulus, excellent damping capacity, good 
thermal stability, and fine welding and molding performance. 
In particular, its tensile strength/density ratio is excellent for 
applications in the aviation industry [15]. Thus, the Ti811 
alloy is an important material for the manufacture of rotary

2. Experimental 
2.1. Experimental material 
FF specimens and fretting pads were obtained from 
Ti811 titanium alloy bars (φ16 mm). The Ti811 alloy is al-
most all α -phase and contains 7.9 wt.% Al, 1.0 wt.% Mo, 
0.99 wt.% V, 0.05 wt.% Fe, 0.1 wt.% C, 0.01 wt.% N, 0.001 
wt.% H, 0.06 wt.% O, and balance Ti. The material was 
treated by double annealing (910°C for 1 h, cooled in air; 
580°C for 8 h, cooled in air). The resulting microstructure is 
an equiaxial α-phase and intergranular β-phase. The me-
chanical properties of the alloy are as follows: σ
b = 931 MPa, 
σ0.2 = 890 MPa, δ = 23% and Ψ = 46%. 
2.2. Preparation of IBED film 
IBED is a method which uses a high energy ion beam to 
bombard a film surface during vapour deposition so as to 
form a transient film in the interface between the deposited 
film and the substrate, so it can be used to prepare a film 
with high density, small grain size, low void radio, and high 
bonding strength. IBED can prepare films with a discretion-
ary elemental ratio and overcome the disadvantages of ion 
implantation producing only thin films and having a higher 
cost. 
An MIB-850 multifunction apparatus, including magne-
tron sputtering and ion beam enhancement, was used to 
prepare the IBED film. The magnetron sputtering target was 
a 0Cr18Ni9 alloy, which combined the properties of better 
oxidation resistance and good ductility. The specimen was 
carefully polished with 1000# waterproof abrasive paper and 
was ultrasonically cleaned with acetone. The ion current to-
wards the substrate was 200 mA, using argon ions, during 
0Cr18Ni9 deposition. The 0Cr18Ni9 deposition rate was 2.1 
nm⋅s
−1 at 4 keV. To produce good stitching at the interface, 
dynamic recoil mixing was applied at 20 keV. A 0Cr18Ni9 
film with a thickness of 22 μm was deposited. 
2.3. Film evaluation for characterization 
A Hitachi S-570 scanning electronic microscope (SEM) 
was used to record micrographs of the IBED film. A 
GDA750 glow discharge spectrometer was used to analyze 
the elemental distribution in a cross section of the film. An 
MSF-2M X-ray diffraction anal yzer was used to measure 
the residual compressive stress distribution in the SP layer. 
An HV-1000 microhardness instrument was used to meas-
ure the Knoop microhardness of the film with a load of 
0.245 N and a loading time of 20 s. The ductility and bond-
ing strength of the film were estimated by means of a 
multi-impact test with a total number of 3 × 10
4 impacts at a 
load of 800 N. The wear resistance of the film was estimated 
by means of a ball-disk wear test, with a rotated ball and a 
fixed disk. The material of the ball was Si
3N4. The diameter 
was 4.75 mm, the average surface coarseness was Ra = 0.05 
μm, and the hardness was HRC 80. The disk, made of the 
Ti811 titanium alloy, had a diameter of 40 mm, a thickness 
of 10 mm, and a surface polished to Ra = 0.05 μm. 
2.4. Shot-peening treatment 
Shot peening was carried out under the following condi-
tions: S110 shot, with an intensity of 0.17 Almen and a cov-
erage of 150%. 
2.5. Fretting fatigue test assembly 
A PLG-100C high-frequency fatigue machine was used 
to conduct the FF tests. A schematic drawing of the FF 
specimens and pad is available elsewhere [16]. The load was 
set in pull-pull. The contact state between the pad and 
specimen was flat to flat, with a rectangular contact area of 2 
mm × 6 mm. Relative slip between the specimen and pad 
was introduced by the difference in elastic deformation be-
tween them. The contact pressure of the pad was controlled 
at 85 MPa using a tensor mete r. The span length between 
the fretting bridge feet was 15 mm. The slip was approxi-
mately 36 μm for an axial loading force of 530 MPa in si-
nusoidal form at 110 Hz and a stress ratio of 0.1. A test 
temperature of 350°C was adopted to simulate the working 
conditions of aircraft engine compressors. An electrical re-
sistance furnace was used fo r heating and the temperature 
was controlled at 350 ± 1°C using a feedback temperature 
controller with a rectifier and a K-type thermocouple.

The reason for the FFL of the shot peened Ti811 titanium 
alloy being significantly improved at the elevated 
temperature was the residual compressive stress, which in-
hibited the early propagation of FF cracks [17]. The residual 
stress measurement indicated that a residual compressive 
stress in the sublayer (more than 100 μm from the surface) 
of the SP specimen still existed (about 450 MPa) after an-
nealing at 350°C for 100 h (Fig. 7). Cracks in the BM 
specimen quickly propagated when the cracks had just initi-
ated. The action of the residual compressive stress was to 
close up cracks at the surface and prevent their incipient 
propagation. Furthermore, the residual compressive stress 
reduced the effective tensile stresses induced by cyclic load 
and fretting contact; this would be expected to delay or pre-
vent crack initiation. The residual compressive stress turned 
the principal tensile stress in the sublayer to the direction 
normal to the surface, thus th e propagation direction of FF 
cracks in the early stage was affected by the compressive 
stress (Fig. 8). The fracture surface feature was characteristic, 
with sidesteps. This indicated that the crack propagation 
path was zigzag and the propagation resistance was high. 
Otherwise, the residual compressive stress induced by SP 
would not only reduce the effective driving force for fretting 
fatigue, but also decrease the effect of fretting wear [18]. 
Thus, the initiation of FF cracks was delayed by the com-
pressive stress. 
As shown in Figs. 9(a) and 9(b), because IBED can pre-
pare 0Cr18Ni9 films with high bonding strength and good 
ductility, the surface of the IBED film here could maintain 
its integrity after shot peening. The strengthening effect of 
shot peening on the IBED 0Cr 18Ni9 film, with its higher 
microhardness, was better than the effect on the Ti811 alloy 
substrate. As a result, the FFR of the Ti811 alloy at the ele-
vated temperature was improved by the synergistic role of 
the IBED 0Cr18Ni9 film and shot peening, and the FFL of 
the alloy was improved by a factor of 5.9 relative to the 
uncoated substrate at the elevated temperature. Because the 
hardness of the titanium alloy was low, intense plastic de-
formation could easily have occurred, and many craters ap-
peared on the surface of the shot peened titanium alloy. Mi-

4. Conclusions 
(1) By using ion beam enhanced deposition, a 0Cr18Ni9 
film was deposited on a Ti811 titanium alloy surface with 
high density, small grain size, low void radio, and high 
bonding strength.
 As a result, the hardness and wear resis-
tance of the Ti811 alloy were increased to a remarkable ex-
tent. 
(2) The FFR of the Ti811 titanium alloy was effectively 
improved at 350°C because of the moderate hardness and 
good ductility of the IBED 0Cr18Ni9 film.
