ABSTRACT 
The influence of annealing temperature on microstructure evolution and mechanical properties of CoCrFe -
NiMn
0.75
Cu
0.25 
high-entropy alloy (HEA) was systematically investigated. As the annealing temperature in -
creases, the grain becomes coarser, Cr-rich phase precipitates, and annealing twins appear in the matrix. The low 
angle grain boundary (LAGB) changes to high angle grain boundary (HAGB), which reduces the strength and 
increases the ductility of the studied HEA. The deformation bands, grain boundaries, and triple junctions of the 
grains are the preferred recrystallized nucleation sites. Based on the microstructural evolution, the contribution 
of the Cr-rich phase, LAGBs, HAGBs, and twin boundaries to the yield strength was analyzed by the modified 
Hall-Petch equation. These results may provide some guidance on tailoring the microstructures and mechanical 
properties for superior HEAs design. 
1. Introduction 
Since the first report by Yeh [ 1 ] and Cantor [ 2 ] in 2004, high-entropy 
alloys (HEAs) have attracted considerable fundamental and technolog -
ical interests because of their outstanding mechanical, chemical, and 
physical properties superior to conventional metallic materials [ 3 – 10 ]. 
These excellent properties enable them promising for structural appli -
cations. Owing to high-entropy of mixing, HEAs usually consist of a 
single solid solution with body-centered-cubic ( bcc ), face-centered-cubic 
( fcc ), or hexagonal close-packed ( hcp ) structures [ 11 – 13 ]. Among them, 
fcc -structured HEAs, such as CoCrFeMnNi, FeCuCrCuNi, Al
0.1
CoCrFeNi, 
CoCrFeNiMn

remarkable ductility [ 14 – 17 ]. However, these fcc -structured HEAs 
usually demonstrate low yield strength, which largely limits their wide 
applications in the future. 
Recently, there are plenty of strategies developed to overcome the 
strength-ductility trade-off of fcc HEAs, such as designing fine- and ultra- 
fine grained microstructure [ 18 ], obtaining high-density nano- -
precipitates [ 19 ] and making it dispersed in the matrix, fabricating 
various complex structural materials [ 20 , 21 ]. Sun et al. have prepared 
ultrafine-grained CoCrFeMnNi HEA sample with a superior 
strength-ductility synergy of a yield strength (YS) of 888 MPa, an ulti -
mate tensile strength (UTS) of 984 MPa, and a elongation (EL) of ~ 21% 
[ 22 ]. Especially, an ultra-fine-grained CoCrFeMnNi HEA shows 
extremely excellent comprehensive mechanical properties at a cryo -
genic temperature of 77 K with YS of 1.24 GPa and EL of 41% [ 23 ]. 
Stepanov et al. added a small amount of Al and C into the CoCrFeNiMn 
HEA, producing two kinds of second-phase precipitates - M

[ 26 ]. However, although the above-mentioned methods have improved

there are still some limitations in the large-scale production and appli -
cations. A thermo-mechanical treatment is one of the conventional and

3.4. Back-stress strain hardening in heterogeneous structures 
In order to investigate the contribution that the effect stress and the 
back stress have, the load-unload-reload (LUR) tensile tests were con -
ducted to explore the Bauchinger effect, as shown in Fig. 8 . It can be 
observed that both curves have a large hysteresis effect after each LUR 
cycle, indicating the existence of a strong Bauchinger effect [ 49 ]. For 
hysteresis loops under the same strain, 1173 K-annealed HEA has bigger 
hysteresis loops, compared with the 1373 K annealed ( Fig. 8 a and b). 
Fig. 8 c presents that the variation of the effective and back stresses with 
tensile strain for 1173 K and 1373 K annealed HEAs. Obviously, the back 
stresses in both the 1173 K and 1373 K annealed HEAs increase with the 
tensile strain. Near the yield point, the back stress of the 1173 K 
annealed HEA is 394 MPa, which is significantly higher than the 1373 K 
annealed HEA (235 MPa). The stress-partition coefficient caused by the 
back stress in the 1173K annealed HEA is 50.48% of the true stress near 
the yielding point ( Fig. 8 ), indicating that the high yield strength of the 
1173 K annealed HEA was primarily caused by its high back stress. The 
1173 K annealed HEA generated high back stress during the tension 
process, due to the plastic incompatibility caused by the heterogeneous 
microstructure (recrystallized grains, partial recrystallized, and 
non-recrystallized). The related work [ 31 , 50 ] indicated that the dislo -
cation density in partial recrystallized grains is much higher than 
recrystallized grains, even up to an order of magnitude. Therefore, the 
dislocation slip is easier in partial recrystallized grains because the re -
sidual dislocation after annealing can provide a large quantity of 
nucleation sites for its multiplication. In the present work, as shown in 
Fig. 2 b, large-sized partial recrystallized grains (or non-recrystallized 
grains) are surrounded by many ultra-fine-recrystallized grains. Under 
stress loading, this distribution of heterogeneous microstructures would 
restrict the deformation of partial recrystallized grains, leading to stress 
concentration. With the increase of the stress, partial recrystallized 
grains will preferentially reach the yield point, leading to the transfer of 
stress concentration to the adjacent recrystallized grains until plastic 
deformation occurs. Because the ultra-fine-recrystallized grains were 
attached to the surrounding partial recrystallized grains, which 
restrained the plastic deformation of partial recrystallized grains. This 
arrangement generates a long-range back stress that makes the dislo -
cation in partial recrystallized grains difficult to slip, thus improving the 
yield strength of this alloy [ 51 ].

4. Conclusion 
In summary, the effect of annealing temperature on the microstruc -
tures and mechanical properties of a cold-rolled CoCrFeNiMn
0.75
Cu
0.25 
HEA was discussed in details. The following conclusions can be drawn: 
1. Cold rolling induces the grains to be fragmented and elongated, fa -
voring the formation of a large number of sub-grains due to severe 
plastic deformation along the rolling direction. As the annealing 
temperature increases, the original sub-grain boundaries merge 
gradually into larger grains, resulting in a gradual evolution in the 
grain-boundary type from LAGB to HAGB + TB. The preferred 
recrystallized nucleation sites are deformation bands, grain bound -
aries, and triple junctions of the grains. 
2. The studied HEA sample was greatly strengthened by cold rolling, 
demonstrating a YS of up to 1366 MPa. An increase in the annealing 
temperature from 1073 K to 1373 K could cause a continuous soft -
ening, and thus result in a decrease in YS from 1123 MPa – 408 MPa 
and an increase in elongation from 7.1% to 27%. 
3. The improved Hall-Petch equation effectively predicts the YS con -
tributions of Cr-rich phase precipitates, LAGBs and HAGBs + TBs. 
The predicted strengths agree well with the experimental results.
