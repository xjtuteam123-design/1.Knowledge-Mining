ABSTRACT 
High-temperature alloy with good ambient ductility, high elevated-temperature strength and low density is 
desired for industrial application. Here, a novel lightweight TiZrVNbAl multicomponent intermetallic alloy with 
good combination of ambient ductility and high-temperature strength was developed. The designed alloy is 
composed of multicomponent B2 intermetallic matrix and dispersed micro-scale orthorhombic-phase (O-phase) 
precipitates. The multicomponent B2 intermetallic matrix can undergo plastic deformation through the mixture 
of planar and wavy slip of b = a < 111 > and b = a < 001 > dislocations, while the O-phase deforms by producing 
dislocation and stacking faults. Moreover, the O-phase can effectively contribute to strength by impeding 
dislocation motion at elevated temperatures (600 
◦
C – 800 
◦
C). Consequently, the TiZrVNbAl alloy exhibits a large 
elongation of ~14%, high specific tensile yield strength of ~171.2 MPa g/cm
3 
at room temperature and a high 
specific compressive strength of ~62.3 MPa g/cm
3 
at 800 
◦
C. The present results will give new insights into the 
development of lightweight ductile high-temperature alloys. 
1. Introduction 
The development of lightweight high-temperature alloys is of critical 
importance for the aerospace, high-speed transportation and marine 
industry, as light high-temperature alloys can greatly improve the fuel 
efficiency [ 1 – 3 ]. To date, numerous efforts have been made to develop 
the novel high-temperature alloys such as low-density titanium-alumi -
nides intermetallics (Ti – Al, Ti
3
Al) and intermetallic matrix composites 
[ 4 – 7 ]. Nevertheless, good ambient ductility is still one of the challenges 
to achieve in these emerging high-temperature alloys, and such a 
drawback restricts their application. Therefore, there is an urgent de -
mand to develop new lightweight high-temperature alloys. 
Recently proposed metallurgical design in high-entropy alloys 
(HEAs) also referred as multicomponent alloys system has revolution -
ized the traditional alloy-design strategy, using five or more principal 
components instead of single-principle element [ 8 , 9 ], which offers 
broader chemical composition space for exploring high performance 
alloys. On one hand, the existence of multi-principal elements can give 
rise to severe lattice distortion, impede dislocation motion and further 
enhance strength [ 7 , 10 ]. On the other hand, the high-entropy effect and 
sluggish diffusion kinetics in HEAs can enhance the high-temperature 
structural stability [ 11 ]. Based on the multicomponent design concept, 
the ordered BCC structured (B2) multicomponent alloys with 
high-specific strength containing relatively lightweight elements, e.g., 
Al, Ti, V, Zr, Nb et al., have been studied frequently. These ordered 
phases usually exhibit stronger bonding and closer packing between 
atoms, which restricts the atom mobility, slows diffusion processes, and 
gives them high-temperature strength and stability [ 12 – 17 ]. Recently, 
numerous investigations have focused on developing B2 system multi -
component alloys, such as, AlMo

high-temperature strength was achieved in the TiZrVNbAl MCIAs. The 
corresponding strengthening, deformation and fracture mechanisms 
were systematically investigated.

3.1. Microstructure after heat treatments 
The XRD results of the aging TiZrVNbAl MCIAs are shown in Fig. 1 . 
All specimens show a family of B2-peaks (the ordered B2 matrix can be 
identified by further TEM-SAED shown in Fig. S1 in Supplementary 
Material), and extra series of minor peaks identified as Ti
2
AlNb-type O- 
phase (Cmcm space group) were detected in Jade 6.0 software, indi -
cating the precipitation of secondary phases. The phase indexes were 
identified by using ICSD#105526 (O-phase) and ICSD#108633 (B2) in 
CrystalMaker software. The intensity of O-phase diffraction patterns 
gradually increases as decreasing the aging temperature, indicating an 
increased content of O-phase. The diffraction peaks position of B2-(110) 
tends to move towards lower value after aging treatment, suggesting a 
larger lattice constant, and the lattice parameter of B2 phase is deter -
mined to be ~0.33 nm of 800A specimens. The larger lattice constant 
indicates the increment in the elemental contents of Zr with large atomic 
size as substitutional atoms in the B2 crystalline lattice. The lattice pa -
rameters of O-phase were determined as a = 0.58 nm, b = 0.96 nm, and 
c = 0.47 nm by XRD results in Powdercell software. 
BSE images of the as-solution and aged TiZrVNbAl MCIAs are shown 
in Fig. 2 . Typical equiaxed grains can be found in 1000T specimens 
( Fig. 2 a), and the inset below Fig. 2 a shows homogeneous element dis -
tribution near grain boundaries, indicating a single-phase structure. 
While in as-aged specimens ( Fig. 2 b – d), profuse acicular precipitates 
distributed in the form of fine Widmanstatten structure can be observed 
[ 24 ]. Combined with XRD results ( Fig. 1 ), it can be deduced that these 
acicular precipitates are the O-phase structure, indicating the phase 
transformation of B2 → O + B2 occurred during aging. In addition, 
quantitative analysis of the O-phase size is shown in Fig. 2 e. The volume 
fraction of O-phase is estimated about ~28.6 ± 1.6%, ~41.1 ± 0.5%, 
and ~47.1 ± 0.5% in 800A, 780A, and 760A specimens by image 
analysis. The fraction increase with decreasing the aging temperature is 
consistent with the intensity of O-phase in the XRD pattern (see Fig. 1 ). 
The average transversal (width) and longitudinal sizes (length) of the 
O-phase in 800A are ~0.67 ± 0.15 μ m and ~5.68 ± 2.06 μ m, respec -
tively. The shape variation may be associated with different orientations 
with respect to the cross-section surface (so-called stereological effect). 
Decrease in aging temperature results in the significant reduction of 
O-phase size to 0.43 ± 0.11 μ m (width) and 3.54 ± 1.63 μ m (length) of 
760A specimen.

3.3. Fracture morphology 
In order to illustrate the fracture mechanism, fractography charac -
terized by SEM of the 800A MCIA was performed and shown in Fig. 5 . 
Obviously, a mixed inter-granular and trans -granular fracture can be 
found ( Fig. 5 a and d). The macro-cracks were defected at grain junc -
tions, indicating the blunt effect of B2 + O dual-phase structure for cracks 
( Fig. 5 a and b). Obviously, fine dimples with circular shape distributed 
near cracks can be found in Fig. 5 b, indicating a high ductile nature. 
Besides, slip compatibility between O and B2 phases was observed in 
Fig. 5 b and c, and less O/B2 cracks were noticed, indicating a good co- 
deformation capability of two phases. In addition, the detail frontal 
fracture morphologies are shown in Fig. 5 d – f. A mixed ductile dimples 
and cleavage facet can be found, the dimples were heterogeneously 
distributed in B2 matrix while the cleaved steps were distributed near O/ 
B2 phase interfaces ( Fig. 5 f). This B2 + O dual-phase microstructure 
exhibited a combination of the fracture characteristics: both cleaved O- 
phase and dimples within B2 regions, firmly verifying that the ductile 
nature of the multicomponent B2 matrix greatly contribute to the overall 
ductility of MCIAs. 
4. Discussion 
As illustrated above, the TiZrVNbAl MCIAs exhibit good deform -
ability and plastic accommodation. To clarify the deformation mecha -
nism, the detailed post-mortem SEM and TEM observations monitoring 
the dislocation behavior were carried out on 800A MCIAs during 
deformation. 
As shown in Fig. 6 , obvious slip lines can be manifest in the surface of 
the 800A MCIAs. At strain of ~2% ( Fig. 6 a), the slip lines across the O- 
phase with large spacing can be found, indicating that both the O and B2 
phase undergo plastic deformation at initial plastic deformation stage.

Further observations of deformation structure by TEM are shown in 
Fig. 6 b – d. Vast dislocations in the morphology of curved line in B2 
matrix and multiple dislocation interactions can be found, suggesting 
that the dislocations have nucleated and slipped. And obvious disloca -
tions tangled and pile-upped near interfaces, suggesting a hindering 
effect. Moreover, as shown in Fig. 6 c and d, many stacking faults (SFs) 
identified by TEM-SAED and IFFT image in HR-TEM can be found in O- 
phase, indicating that O-phase can deformed plastically by activating 
SFs. As shown in Fig. 6 c, the SF bundles have a thickness of ~4 nm with a 
varying spacing (below 140 nm). These SFs can further confine the di -
rection of the dislocation motion and promote dislocations accumula -
tion, making an extra contribution to the work hardening [ 28 , 29 ]. In 
addition, it was reported in many dual-phase alloys that the avalanche 
generation of dislocations appearance and realignment along a direction 
in a restricted region can form kink bands [ 30 ]. These kink-bands can 
not only alleviate the stress concentration near interface but also 
dynamically increase the dislocation resistance to promote strain hard -
ening, benefiting both the strength and ductility [ 30 , 31 ]. 
As strain increased to ~10% ( Fig. 6 e and f), massive parallel lines 
with small spacing and shorter length were observed, indicating strong 
dislocation slip interaction between O and B2 phases. It can be found 
that the slip traces in the B2 matrix apparently penetrated through the 
O-phase, which were expected to reduce stress build-up near interface. 
Obvious wavy slip appeared on the matrix, indicative of a kink or cross- 
slip mechanism, which is consistent with reported B2 intermetallic [ 32 , 
33 ]. Further TEM characterizations show many mutually parallel slip 
traces in Fig. 6 g and h, which may be attributed to the continuous planar 
slip of dislocations. Indeed, dislocations slip in ordered phase need to 
overcome the barriers of anti-phase boundary (APB) energy, which can 
be reduced by gliding of successive dislocations on the same plane [ 34 , 
35 ]. It is noteworthy that the interfaces exhibit specific orientation 
relationship, ie., ( 1 10 )

numerous intrinsic phase interfaces and strain induced kink-bands can 
also enable the further intrinsic kinematic hardening by enhancing 
dislocation motion resistance, improving the strength, uniform defor -
mation and tensile ductility. 
The two-beam TEM characterization was performed to further 
identify the dislocations. As shown in the high-angle-annular-dark-field 
(HAADF) TEM images in Fig. 7 a, the dislocations display as straight 
trace lines lying on the B2-(110) planes. The dislocations marked by red 
arrow are visible for g = [ 002 ] and g = [ 10 1 ] ( Fig. 7 b and c), while 
invisible for g = [ 1 10 ] ( Fig. 7 d). It has been reported that the common 
< 001 > slip mode is the dominate slip mode in B2-ordered intermetallic 
[ 5 ], hence it can be speculated that the b = a < 001 > dislocations are also 
existed in MCIAs ( Fig. 7 a – c). However, the < 111 > slip mode is rarely 
active in B2 intermetallic due to the high anti-phase boundary (APB) 
energy, which is key for plastic deformation [ 5 ]. In present MCIAs, many 
linear slip traces (shear-passing the O-phase) are visible (marked by blue 
arrow) for g = [ 002 ] in Fig. 7 b, while invisible for g = [ 10 1 ] and g =
[ 1 10 ] ( Fig. 7 c and d), indicating the existence of b = a < 111 > in B2. 
Therefore, it can be concluded that the plasticity is governed by the 
mixture of planar and wavy slip of b = a < 111 > and b = a < 001 > dis -
locations. The dislocations of B2 < 111 > can shear pass O-phase while 
the dislocations of B2 < 001 > were impeded by the O-phase. The above 
observations indicated that the plasticity originates from both sufficient 
slip activation in B2 and high transmission probability of O/B2 interface 
for dislocations.

5. Conclusions 
In summary, we developed a novel lightweight (orthorhombic- 
phase) O-phase strengthened TiZrVNbAl MCIA with good synergy of 
ambient ductility and high-temperature strength. The developed MCIA 
exhibits a high specific yield strength (~171.2 MPa g/cm
3
), consider -

strength (~62.3 MPa g/cm

enhance the dislocation motion resistance for hardening, but more 
importantly, it can generate stacking faults and provide high 
dislocation-slip transmission probability for plasticity. Moreover, the O- 
phase can also effectively impede dislocation motion and contribute to 
strengthening under high temperature compression. Consequently, the
