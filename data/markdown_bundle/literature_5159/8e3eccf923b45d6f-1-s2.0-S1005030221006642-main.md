The synergistic effect of Mo and Zr additions on microstructure evolution, room-temperature fracture 
toughness and microhardness of Nb-22Ti-15Si- x Mo- y Zr ( x = 4,8, y = 3,6 ) alloys manufactured by laser 
directed energy deposited (L-DED) have been investigated. The major phases in as-deposited 4Mo-3Zr 
alloy are Nb solid solution (Nbss), Nb 3 Si and γ-Nb 5 Si 3 . The Nb 3 Si phases disappear with increasing Mo 
content to 8 at.% or increasing Zr content to 6%. γ-Nb 5 Si 3 precipitates formed in the Nbss of as-deposited 
x Mo- y Zr alloys due to the cyclic re-heating, and the γ-Nb 5 Si 3 precipitates and Nbss matrix exhibits ori- 
entation relationship (OR) of [001] Nbss //[ ¯1 112] γ and (110) Nbss //(01 ¯1 0) γ in as-deposited 4Mo-6Zr alloy. The 
microstructure of x Mo- y Zr alloys became relative homogeneity and the Nbss matrix showed the better 
continuity after the heat treatment (1400 °C/30 h). The Nb 3 Si phase has transformed into Nbss and α- 
Nb 5 Si 3 with the OR have been determined as {100} Nbss //{100} α and{111} Nbss //{111} α in the heat-treated 
4Mo-3Zr alloy. When Mo content increase to 8at.%, a part of γ-Nb 5 Si 3 phase also transformed into the 
α-Nb 5 Si 3 phase with the OR have been determined as {11 ¯2 0} γ//{110} α and {10 ¯1 0} γ//{111} α after heat 
treatment. Among the as-deposited alloys, the 4Mo-6Zr alloy has the highest fracture toughness K Q with 
the lowest hardness. The average K Q of 4Mo-6Zr and 8Mo-6Zr alloys increased by 28% and 30% after the 
heat treatment, and reached 13.61 and 11.43 MPa ·m 1/2 , respectively. The hardness of Nb-22Ti-15Si- x Mo- 
y Zr alloys is signiﬁcantly decreased after the heat treatment.

1. Introduction 
Nb-Si-based alloys have been considered as a potential material 
for the high-section components of gas turbines engines, due to 
their outstanding physical properties: high melting point, relatively 
low density (about 6.6–7.2 g/cm 3 ), and attractive high temperature 
mechanical performance [ 1 , 2 ]. Nb-Si-based alloys are mainly com- 
posed of niobium solid solution (Nbss) and silicide phase. The Nbss 
matrix is in favor of the good room-temperature toughness, while 
the silicide phase offers the high strength and creep resistance at 
elevated temperature [3] . However, the Nb-Si-based alloys usually

hold the poor ductility and toughness at the room-temperature, 
which excludes them from industrial application. Thus, one of the 
most crucial issues for Nb-Si-based alloys has been to improve the 
toughness in the last decades. 
Extensive attempts have been made with the purpose of opti- 
mizing the Nb-Si-based alloy via the suitable alloying using Ti [4–
6] , Hf [7] , Ga [ 8 , 9 ], B [10] and V [11] etc. It is found that the Ti 
alloying is the most effective way to improve the fracture tough- 
ness of Nb-Si-based alloys, because of the decreasing of the Peierls- 
Nabarro (P-N) barriers energy with Ti addition, which can pro- 
mote the dislocation movement in Nbss matrix [ 5 , 12 ]. Addition- 
ally, the addition of Ti does not lead to the remarkable increase of 
the ductile-brittle transition temperature (DBTT) of Nbss in Nb-Si- 
based alloys [13] .

two-phase structure in the Nb-Si-based alloys [ 14 , 15 ]. Appropriate

Mo alloying could increase the continuity of Nbss matrix, which is 
of beneﬁt to improve the fracture toughness of Nb-Si-based alloys. 
Furthermore, Mo element in Nbss phase would lead to solid so- 
lution strengthening and its strengthening effect could last to the 
high temperature [16] . In recent years, the attentions were paid 
to Zr-modiﬁed Nb-Si-based alloys. Zr has been demonstrated to be 
an effective alloying element for improving the room-temperature 
fracture toughness of Nb-Si-based alloys. The improvement of frac- 
ture toughness is attributed to the increase in the volume frac- 
tion of the Nbss phase and the appearance of Nbss/Nb 5 Si 3 lamellar 
structure, both of which cause the bridging, capturing and deﬂec- 
tion during crack propagation of cracks [ 17 , 18 ]. It is also reported 
that Zr-modiﬁed Nb-Ti-Si-based alloys have better oxidation resis- 
tance than Zr-free Nb-Ti-Si-based alloy [19] . Therefore, the appro- 
priate alloying with Mo or Zr can modify the microstructure and 
properties of Nb-Ti-Si-based alloys. However, the synergistic effect 
of Mo and Zr on the microstructure and mechanical properties of 
Nb-Ti-Si-based alloys have not been explored and needed to be in- 
vestigated. 
Besides, more and more researches have demonstrated that the 
processing routes also showed a remarkable effect on the mi- 
crostructure and properties of Nb-Ti-Si-based alloys [20–22] . Main 
preparation routes explored so far to make Nb-Ti-Si-based al- 
loys are arc-melting [23] , directional solidiﬁcation [24] , and spark 
plasma sintering [25] . For instance, the microstructure of Nb-Ti- 
Si-based alloy prepared by directional solidiﬁcation showed the 
better crystallography orientation consistency and continuity along 
the growth direction than that prepared by arc-melting, which 
makes room-temperature fracture toughness of Nb-Ti-Si-based al- 
loys higher [20] . 
Recently, laser directed energy deposition (L-DED), has become 
an eﬃcient additive manufacturing method for the direct fabrica- 
tion of full-density near-net-shaped metal components [ 26 , 27 ]. It 
also allows the suﬃcient opportunities to tailor the microstructure 
and subsequent mechanical properties [28] . Especially, the use of 
L-DED with blended elemental powders offers two distinct advan- 
tages. One is that it allows the rapid change of the alloy compo- 
sition deposited by in situ changing the proportion of each pow- 
der in the mixture, which can realize the real-time control of 
the microstructure and mechanical properties in the alloy deposits 
[ 29 , 30 ]. The other is that L-DED allows a high-throughput design of 
new alloy through the deposition of continuously-graded composi- 
tion libraries of relative metallic alloys and rapid characterization 
of their mechanical behavior [ 31 , 32 ]. However, application of laser 
additive manufacturing technologies on Nb-Ti-Si-based alloys has 
not been widely explored, especially for prepared by L-DED. Our 
previous works have shown that the L-DED is a suitable for the 
preparation of Nb-Ti-Si-based alloys [ 18 , 33 ]. 
The motivation for this work was to investigate the synergis- 
tic effect of Zr and Mo on the microstructure evolution, room- 
temperature fracture toughness and microhardness of Nb-Ti-Si- 
based alloys prepared by L-DED. Meantime, the effect of heat treat- 
ment on phase stability and mechanical properties of (Mo + Zr)- 
modiﬁed Nb-Ti-Si-based alloys were also emphasized. 
2. Experimental 
2.1. Samples preparation 
Elemental powders of Nb, Ti, Si, Mo and Zr were used with pu- 
rities of 99.7 wt.%, 99.8 wt.%, 98.6 wt.%, 99.5 wt.%, 99.8 wt.%, re- 
spectively. The morphologies of the elemental powders are shown 
in Fig. 1 (a)–(e). Prior to L-DED, they were mixed with nominal 
composition by a planetary ball milling for 3 h at a rotation speed 
of 200 rpm, then dried in a vacuum oven at 100 °C for 3 h. 
Four simple straight walls (length (L) × width (W) × leight (H) 
mm, shown in Fig. 1 (h)) of Nb-Ti-Si-based alloys with the nomi- 
nal compositions of Nb-22Ti-15Si- x Mo- y Zr ( x Mo- y Zr: x = 4, 8; y = 3, 
6) alloys, and for simplicity, they are henceforth abbreviated in the 
forms of 4Mo-3Zr, 4Mo-6Zr, 8Mo-3Zr and 8Mo-6Zr alloys, respec- 
tively)(at.%), are shown in Fig. 1 (h). They were prepared in a clean 
deposition chamber under an argon atmosphere (the oxygen con- 
tent less than 100 ppm) using a 6kW continuous wave diode laser 
with a wavelength of 1060 nm directed energy deposition system. 
The schematic illustration of the L-DED process using blended ele- 
mental powders and the processing parameters used for the L-DED 
process are shown in Fig. 1 (f) and (g). A commercial pure Ti plate 
with dimensions of about 150 mm × 50 mm × 7 mm was used as 
the laser deposition substrate. 
The compositions at the middle of the simple straight walls 
were analyzed using bulk Energy Dispersive X-ray (EDX), as pre- 
sented in Table 1 , which present the good consistence with the 
nominal compositions. 
During heat treatment, when the pressure in the furnace cham- 
ber was lower than 1.0 × 10 −3 Pa, the furnace chamber was heated 
up and the temperature of the specimens was raised with rate of 
10 °C/min till it reached 1400 °C, and then the furnace chamber 
kept isothermally for 30 h. Finally, the specimens were furnace- 
cooled down to room-temperature. Sponge titanium were used to 
coat around the specimens in order to mitigate the surface oxida- 
tion. 
2.2. Analyzing methods 
Specimens used for the microstructural observations were cut 
from the middle of the simple straight walls by wire electro- 
discharge machining (EDM). Microstructural studies were carried 
out on metallographically polished surfaces of all the alloy de- 
posits. The microstructures were examined using scanning electron 
microscopes (SEM) (Helios G4 CX) equipped with an EDX micro- 
analyzer. Electron back scatter diffraction (EBSD) analysis was per- 
formed with a 0.5 μm step size using a Zeiss Gemini 500 Emis- 
sion SEM equipped with an HKL Channel 5 system. X-ray diffrac- 
tion (XRD) analysis was applied for the phase identiﬁcation with a 
diffractometer (Panalytical X’Pert PRO), operated with the assistant 
of Cu K α radiation. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) inves- 
tigation was employed on a Themis Z double Cs Corrector trans- 
mission electron microscope operating at 200 KV. The TEM spe- 
ciﬁc sample preparation of the precipitates inside of Nbss phase 
in as-deposited 4Mo-3Zr alloy is using the focused ion beam (FIB) 
milling. The distribution of Nb, Ti, Si, Mo and Zr elements were an- 
alyzed by SHIMADZU-1720 electron probe microanalysis technique 
(EPMA). The volume fraction of the constituent phases of (Mo + Zr)- 
modiﬁed Nb-Ti-Si-based alloys were measured by Image-Pro Plus 
6.0 software. At least ﬁve images ( × 500) of the microstructures 
from one alloy deposit were used for the measurement and the 
average value was calculated.

single-edge notched three-point bending using an Inston3382 uni- 
versal testing machine with a cross head speed of 0.05 mm/min 
and a span of 24 mm. An initial notch with the 3 mm depth on 
the specimen (without any fatigue precracking) was made by EDM 
machine according to the description in the ASTM Standard [34] ,

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Phase constitutions 
Fig. 2 (a) shows the XRD patterns of as-deposited Nb-22Ti-15Si- 
x Mo- y Zr alloys. There exist Nbss phase, Nb 3 Si phase and γ-Nb 5 Si 3 
phase in as-deposited 4Mo-3Zr alloy deposit. Ni 3 Si phase disap- 
pears when the Mo content further increases to 8at.% or Zr content 
further increases to 6at.% in as-deposited x Mo- y Zr alloys. Although 
previous researchers found that a certain amount addition of Mo 
element could promoted formation of β-Nb 5 Si 3 phase in Nb-Si- 
based alloys [ 14 , 15 ], β-Nb 5 Si 3 phase was not detected in the as- 
deposited x Mo- y Zr alloys. It also can be seen from Fig. 2 (a) that 
the higher content of Zr and Mo should be in favor of improving 
the stability of γ-Nb 5 Si 3 phase at the room temperature and sup- 
pressing the formation of Nb 3 Si phase. 
Fig. 2 (b) presented the XRD patterns of the Nb-22Ti-15Si- x Mo- 
y Zr alloys after heat treatment at 1400 °C for 30 h. The constituent 
phases of alloy deposits were not changed after heat treatment 
when Zr content is higher (6at.%, 4Mo-6Zr and 8Mo-6Zr). α-Nb 5 Si 3 
phase appeared in the alloy deposits after heat treatment when 
Zr content is lower (3at.%, 4Mo-6Zr and 8Mo-3Zr). Moreover, the 
metastable Nb 3 Si phase in the as-deposited 4Mo-3Zr alloy disap- 
peared after heat treatment, which indicated that the transforma- 
tion from Nb 3 Si to α-Nb 5 Si 3 phase occurred during the heat treat- 
ment.

observed in the interdendrite zone of primary eutectic dendrite in 
as-deposited 4Mo-3Zr alloy. It is inferred that the Nb 3 Si and γ- 
Nb 5 Si 3 phase should the product of eutectic reaction of L → Nbss + 
Nb 3 Si and L → Nbss + γ-Nb 5 Si 3 , respectively. The mechanisms of the 
formation of Nbss + γ-Nb 5 Si 3 lamellar microstructures is akin to 
the Zr modiﬁed as-deposited Nb-Ti-Si-based alloys, as we explored 
earlier [18] . Compared with Nb and Mo, Zr and Ti are low melting- 
point elements that will be rich ahead of solidiﬁcation interfaces, 
the segregation of Zr and Ti in residual liquid promoted the forma- 
tion of eutectic Nbss + γ-Nb 5 Si 3 phases. 
With increasing the content of Mo or Zr (4Mo-6Zr, 8Mo-3Zr and 
8Mo-6Zr) the Nb 3 Si phase disappeared and only the Nbss and γ- 
Nb 5 Si 3 phases existed. It has been reported that the Zr and Mo 
atoms can substitute the Nb atoms in Nb 3 Si phase, which make 
Nb 3 Si phase unstable. It can be seen from the XRD analysis in the 
Fig. 2 (a), that the high content of (Mo + Zr) in Nb-Ti-Si-based al- 
loys could contribute to the stable existence of γ-Nb 5 Si 3 phase at 
room-temperature. 
Thus, the solidiﬁcation path of 4Mo-3Zr alloy during the solidi- 
ﬁcation of molten pool can be summarized as follows: L → primary 
Nbss + L 1 → Nbss + Nb 3 Si + L 2 → Nbss + γ-Nb 5 Si 3 , While the solidiﬁca- 
tion path change to: L → primary Nbss + L 1 → Nbss + γ-Nb 5 Si 3 when 
the Mo content further increases to 8at.% or Zr content further in- 
creases to 6at.% in x Mo- y Zr alloys.

the γ-Nb 5 Si 3 precipitates from the Nbss phases in as-deposited

during the subsequent deposition. It has been also reported that 
the cyclic re-heating of the IHT could trigger precipitation in Dam- 
ascus steel in additive manufacturing [28] . 
According to the EDX analysis results listed in Table 2 , the dis- 
tribution of Ti in the as-deposited x Mo- y Zr alloys was not uni- 
form. The concentration of Ti in the Nbss phase was higher than in 
the γ-Nb 5 Si 3 phase. The Zr was mainly enriched in γ-Nb 5 Si 3 and 
Nb 3 Si phases, while Mo was mainly gathered in the Nbss phase. In 
addition, it can be shown that the increasing the content of Zr in 
(Mo + Zr)-modiﬁed Nb-Ti-Si-based alloys promote the increases of 
the Mo content in the Nbss phase.

the room-temperature fracture toughness in Nb-Ti-Si-based alloys.

as well. On comparison with as-deposited alloys, the heat treated 
x Mo- y Zr alloys possesses the larger size of Nbss. The Nbss in heat- 
treated alloys occupied a larger volume fraction on comparison 
with as-deposited alloy, therefore the hardness of heat-treated al- 
loys relatively lower than that as-deposited alloys. Zhang and Guo 
has shown that the hardness of Nbss and silicide phases in multi- 
component Nb-Ti-Si-based alloys decreased after heat treatment 
(1450 °C/50 h) [51] . 
4. Conclusions 
This work presents the synergistic effects of Mo and Zr on the 
microstructure evolution, mechanical properties, speciﬁcally the 
fracture toughness of Nb-22Ti-15Si- x Mo- y Zr alloys additively man- 
ufactured by laser directed energy deposition (L-DED). The effect 
of heat treatment on microstructure and mechanical properties 
of Nb-Ti-Si-based alloys has also been investigated. The following 
main conclusions can be drawn: 
(1) The microstructure of as-deposited 4Mo-3Zr alloy con- 
sists of Nbss, γ-Nb 5 Si 3 and Nb 3 Si phases. The crys- 
talline orientation relationship (OR) between Nbss and γ- 
Nb 5 Si 3 is{10 0} Nbss //{0 0 01} γ, while the OR between Nbss 
and Nb 3 Si are {100} Nbss //{100} Nb3Si and {110} Nbss //{111} Nb3Si . 
Nb 3 Si phases disappeared with increasing Mo content to 
8at.% or increasing Zr content to 6%. The microstructure of 
as-deposited 4Mo-6Zr, 8Mo-3Zr and 8Mo-6Zr alloys consist 
of Nbss and γ-Nb 5 Si 3 phases. The OR between Nbss and γ- 
Nb 5 Si 3 in as-deposited 4Mo-6Zr alloy is {110} Nbss //{10 ¯1 0} γ, 
while the OR between Nbss and γ-Nb 5 Si 3 in as-deposited 
8Mo-3Zr alloy are {0 01} Nbss //{0 0 01} γ and{111} Nbss //{10 ¯1 0} γ. 
The OR between Nbss and γ-Nb 5 Si 3 in as-deposited 8Mo- 
6Zr alloy {0 01} Nbss //{0 0 01} γ and {110} Nbss //{10 ¯1 0} γ. Addi- 
tionally, the γ-Nb 5 Si 3 precipitates are observed in Nbss 
phase in as-deposited x Mo- y Zr alloys. The γ-Nb 5 Si 3 pre- 
cipitates are nucleate along {110} Nb , and exhibits OR 
of [001] Nb //[ ¯1 112] γ and (110) Nb //(01 ¯1 0) γ between the γ- 
Nb 5 Si 3 precipitates and Nbss phase in as-deposited 4Mo-6Zr 
alloy. 
(2) After heat treatment at 1400 °C for 30 h, the microstruc- 
ture of x Mo- y Zr-HT alloys became homogeneous and the 
Nbss phases became more continuous. The Nb 3 Si phase 
in the 4Mo-3Zr alloy has transformed into Nbss and α- 
Nb 5 Si 3 . The OR between Nbss and α-Nb 5 Si 3 of 4Mo-3Zr- 
HT alloy are {100} Nbss //{100} α and{111} Nbss // {111} α. A part 
of γ-Nb 5 Si 3 phases in 8Mo-3Zr alloy transformed into the 
α-Nb 5 Si 3 phase during the heat treatment, and the OR 
between γ-Nb 5 Si 3 and α-Nb 5 Si 3 are {11 ¯2 0} γ//{110} α and 
{10 ¯1 0} γ//{111} α. 
(3) The average room-temperature fracture toughness K Q of as- 
deposited 4Mo-6Zr alloy was 10.61 MPa ·m 1/2 , which is the 
maximum among the as-deposited x Mo- y Zr alloys, while the 
average K Q value of as-deposited 8Mo-6Zr alloy was about 
8.93 MPa ·m 1/2 , which is the lowest among the as-deposited 
alloys. The average K Q of 4Mo-6Zr and 8Mo-6Zr alloy in- 
creased to 13.61 and 11.43 MPa ·m 1/2 approximately after 
heat treatment at 1400 °C for 30 h, increasing by 28% and 
30% from the K Q of the as-deposited alloys. 
(4) Among the as-deposited x Mo- y Zr alloys, the 8Mo-6Zr al- 
loy has the highest value of hardness, while 4Mo-6Zr alloy 
shows the lowest value of hardness. After heat treatment at 
1400 °C for 30 h, the hardness of x Mo- y Zr alloys signiﬁcantly 
decreased.
