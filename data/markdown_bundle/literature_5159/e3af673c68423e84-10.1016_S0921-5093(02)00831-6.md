Changes of microstructures and high temperature properties during
high temperature ser vice of Niobium added ferritic stainless steels
Nobuhiro Fujita *, Keiichi Ohmura, Akio Yamamoto
Steel Research Laboratories, Nippon Steel Corporation, 20-1 Shintomi Futtsu-city 293-8511, Japan

Abstract
To improve the fuel economy and clean the exhaust gas of automobiles, the temperature of exhaust gas is getting higher and
higher. Niobium added ferritic stainless steels are often being used in automoti ve exhaust systems, because of their excellent heat
resistant properties, especially thermal fatigue resistance, which is very important for materials of exhaust manifold. Howe ver,
coarse precipitates containing niobium, which cause degradation in high temperature strength and thermal fatigue resistance, are
unavoidable during high temperature ser vice. In this study, changes of microstructures and high temperature properties in high
temperature aging were in vestigated using se veral Nb added ferritic stainless steels. It has been found that the microstructure
stability of Nb /C1/Ti /C1/Mo alloyed steels in high temperature aging is superior to that of Nb added steels. The microstructure stability
leads to less degradation in high temperature strength during high temperature aging and to longer thermal fatigue li ves of Nb /C1/Ti /C1/
Mo alloyed steels than in Nb added steels.

1. Introduction
Recently, it is becoming extremely important to
consider global warming and air pollution. To protect
the environment, automobile manufacturers are making
great efforts both to impro ve fuel economy and to clean
exhaust gas. To achie ve these, it is necessary to increase
the exhaust gas temperature approximately to 900 8C
and to reduce the weight of parts. Exhaust manifolds are
the parts nearest to the engines so that the materials are
exposed to high temperature exhaust gas repeatedly.
Excellent heat resistant properties, especially thermal
fatigue resistance, are necessary for these parts. How-
ever, con ventional manifolds are made of cast iron,
which has poor heat resistance and is relati vely heavyi n
weight. Cast iron is therefore being replaced by other
materials, which ha ve good thermal fatigue resistance
and are light in weight.
To reduce weight of the parts, the structure of sheets
and pipes is more ad vantageous than the con ventional
cast structure in terms of thickness reduction. When the
parts made of sheets or pipes suffer thermal fatigue,
buckling by local strain concentration mainly causes
rupture [1]. In order to lengthen thermal fatigue li ves, it
is necessary to suppress the local strain concentration.
Materials should ha ve both high yield strength at high
temperature and low coefficient of thermal expansion
[1]. Ferritic steels ha ve a relati vely low coefficient of
thermal expansion so that a lot of efforts ha veb e e n
made to create new ferritic stainless steels with high yield
strength at ele vated temperatures [2 /C1

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Effect of alloying elements on the initial high
temperature strength
Fig. 2 shows the effects of Nb and Mo additions on
0.2% proof strength at 900 8C for 13Cr steels. The
addition of Nb does not ha ve much effect on the high
temperature strength when its content is less than 0.2
mass%. O ver 0.2 mass% addition of Nb impro ved the
high temperature strength effecti vely. The addition of
Mo also increases the high temperature strength ap-
proximately in proportion to the addition content. Fig.
3 shows the relationship between the addition content
and the amount in solid solution of Nb and Mo for the
13Cr steels. The amounts in solid solution of both
elements were obtained by chemical analysis of ex-
tracted residues. When the Nb addition content is less
than 0.2 mass%, Nb is not in solid solution but in
precipitates. When the Nb addition exceeds 0.2 mass%,
the amount of Nb in solid solution increases as the Nb
addition content increases. Because C and N remained
in the steels, niobium carbonitride easily formed in some
processing stages, e.g. in hot-rolling and annealing
processes. In this case, the total amounts of C and N
are 0.02 mass% so that approximately 0.15 mass% Nb
can be consumed if all of C and N are precipitated as
Nb(C, N). Since precipitates other than carbonitride
may take time to form, Nb can be supersaturated in the
matrix when the addition is more than stoichiometry for
Nb(C, N). The relationship between the Nb addition
content and the Nb amount in solid solution is in good
agreement with that between the Nb addition content
and the 0.2% proof strength at 900 8C. Regarding Mo
addition, precipitates containing Mo were not detected
in the extracted residues so that almost all of Mo
addition is in solid solution. The 0.2% proof strength
therefore increases in proportion to the Mo amount in
solid solution. These results suggest that the strengthen-
ing effects at high temperature by the Nb and Mo
additions should deri ve from solid solution strengthen-
ing.

4. Conclusions
(1) Nb in solid solution contributes to impro ving the
initial high temperature strength of ferritic stainless
steels effecti vely. Howe ver, it has been found that the
high temperature strength of Nb added steels decreases
during high temperature aging and that it is caused by
coarse Fe
3Nb3C formation in aging.
(2) Addition of Ti and Mo can control the precipita-
tion process in Nb alloyed ferritic stainless steels. It can
suppress coarse Fe 3Nb3C precipitation during high
temperature aging but Fe 2Nb forms instead. Because
of lower supersaturation and higher equilibrium con-
centration of Nb for Fe
2Nb in Nb/C1/Ti/C1/Mo alloyed steels
than those for Fe 3Nb3C in Nb added steels, nucleation
and growth of Fe 2Nb is assumed to be occur at slower
rates than those of Fe 3Nb3C in Nb added steels.
(3) The 14Cr /C1/0.3Nb /C1/0.1Ti /C1/0.5Mo steel showed less
degradation in high temperature strength and longer
thermal fatigue life than the 19Cr /C1/0.4Nb steel. This is
due to excellent microstructure stability during high
temperature aging. Alloy design of Nb, Ti and Mo
addition to ferritic stainless steel is therefore one of the
suitable methods for heat resistant materials in auto-
motive exhaust systems.
