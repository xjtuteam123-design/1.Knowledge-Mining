The superelastic behavior of an NiTiHfPd single-crystal shape memory alloy in compression along the [1 1 1] orientation was
investigated as a function of temperature. In the range of /C0 30 to 70 /C176C, a perfect superelastic loop with 4.2% transformation strain
was attained. In addition, this material exhibited an ultrahigh yield strength of more than 2500 MPa and an exceptional damping
capacity of 44 J cm /C0 3.

Keywords: Shape memory alloys (SMAs); Phase transformation; Damping; Compression test; High-strength shape memory alloys
Shape memory alloys (SMAs) possess the unique
ability to undergo a fully reversible, diﬀussionless, ther-
moelastic martensitic phase transformation that allows
them to recover very large deformations (up to 10%)
through stimulations by heat or stress [1]. Owing to their
superior mechanical properties, such as high ductility
and a consistent ability to recover large strains through
good shape memory behavior, near-equiatomic Ni–Ti
alloys have been the favored system for use in both
research and practical applications [1]. However, it is
also recognized that near-equiatomic NiTi has low
strength (<700 MPa), low transformation temperatures
(<100 /C176C) and low cyclic stability under high stresses
[1], severely limiting its potential applications.
It is well known that shape memory and superelastic
properties of NiTi alloys are highly composition depen-
dent [2]. In general, additions of Pd, Pt, Hf and Zr in-
crease the transformation temperatures of NiTi-based
alloys [2,3]. Based on cost, however, Hf and Zr are
considered the most practical alloying elements for
high-temperature applications because of the high raw
material costs associated with Pd and Pt additions. On
the other hand, NiTi(Hf,Zr) alloys have poor shape
memory behavior [3–5], are generally too brittle for gen-
eral use [4], and have low strength [3,6] and poor cyclic
stability [4]. In order to improve the shape memory
properties and ductility, Cu was added as a quaternary
addition to NiTiHf [4–6]. Although this addition was
found to improve the ductility, it also decreased the
strength (unpublished results). As an initial investigation
into the eﬀect of other quaternary additions on NiTiHf,
in this study we focus on the eﬀects of Pd addition on
the strength and superelastic behavior of NiTiHf SMAs
under compression.
It has been well documented that shape memory
properties of NiTi alloys are highly orientation depen-
dent [7–12]. The stress-induced martensitic phase trans-
formation in NiTi single crystals was originally
investigated by Takei et al. in 1983 [13], while Miyazaki
et al. studied the shape memory and superelastic behav-
iors of Ni
51Ti49 single crystals [7]. Since then, many as-
pects of NiTi single crystals, such as their cyclic
deformation behavior [8], tension–compression asym-
metry [9], orientation dependence of shape memory
properties [10] and aging eﬀects on shape memory
behavior [11], have been investigated. In NiTi single
crystals, yield strengths as high as 2000 MPa are possible
in the martensite phase for Ni
51.5Ti48.5 and Ni 50.8Ti49.2
with 3% transformation strain along the [1 1 1] direction
in compression [10,11]. Moreover, the mechanical
behavior of strongly textured polycrystalline materials
can mimic the behavior of single crystals [2].A sa n

High strength and high damping capacity are impor-
tant parameters for the consideration of SMAs in many
practical applications. Damping capacity is deﬁned as
the amount of energy that can be dissipated out of a sys-
tem that may result in relief. Hence, good damping
materials should have large mechanical hysteresis at
high stress levels. The stress hysteresis is about 200–
300 MPa in polycrystalline NiTi [14], increasing to
400–500 MPa in [1 1 1]-oriented Ni
51.5Ti48.5 and
Ni50.8Ti49.2 single crystals [10,11], while it is about
500 MPa in NiTiNb polycrystal SMAs and 950–
1000 MPa in NbTi/NiTi nanocomposite wires [15,16].
High-strength and wide-hysteresis SMAs are very
appealing for aerospace and biomedical applications
[17,18], as well as applications in civil and seismic struc-
tures [17,19]. They oﬀer several advantages over existing
systems, such as high-force accommodation, weight sav-
ing, portability and reduction in cost [17].
In this study, a Ni 45.3Ti29.7Hf20Pd5 single-crystal
SMA is investigated under compression along the
[1 1 1] direction for the ﬁrst time. Its superelastic behav-
ior as a function of temperature mechanical strength,
damping capacity and mechanical hysteresis are deter-
mined, revealing the potential of this ultrahigh-strength
NiTiHfPd single-crystal alloy for industrial applications
that require high-strength and high damping capacity.
NiTiHfPd single crystals were synthesized by the
Bridgman technique in an He atmosphere with a nomi-
nal composition of Ni
45.3Ti29.7Hf20Pd5 (at.%). The com-
position of the single crystals was determined by
inductively coupled plasma atomic emission spectros-
copy to be Ni
45.3Ti29.1Hf20Pd5.2 with 0.4% Zr (at.%).
Compression specimens, 8 mm /C2 4m m /C2 4 mm, were
cut by electric discharge machining from the as-grown
ingots with their loading axes oriented along the [1 1 1]
direction. Specimens were solution treated at 1050 /C176C
for 4 h in quartz tubes under vacuum to avoid oxidation
and then quenched in water. Transformation peaks were
not visible in diﬀerential scanning calorimetry measure-
ments even when the sample was cooled to /C0 150 /C176C.
The mechanical experiments were conducted with a
100 kN capable Landmark MTS servohydraulic test
frame using a strain rate of 8 /C2 10
/C0 4 mm s/C0 1. Specimens
were heated and cooled in situ by conduction through
compression grips with a ﬂuctuation of ±2 /C176C. Heating
and cooling rates were both ﬁxed at 10 /C176C min
/C0 1. Strain
was measured at the faces of the compression grips by
an MTS high-temperature extensometer with a gage
length of 12 mm. The contribution by the thermoelastic
deformation of the compression grips was taken into ac-
count during strain calculations.

solution-treated [1 1 1]-oriented Ni

At all test temperatures, the transformation strain was
fully reversible, meaning that there was a negligible
amount of plastic deformation (irrecoverable strain)
[10]. The superelastic window for the Ni
45.3Ti29.7Hf20Pd5
single-crystal alloy was at least 100 /C176C (between /C0 30 and
70 /C176C). The maximum transformation strain was 4.2% at
/C0 30 /C176C. The elastic moduli of the austenite and
martensite phases were 65–75 and 100–110 GPa,
respectively.
A remarkable feature observed in Figure 1 is the very
high strength of the Ni 45.3Ti29.7Hf20Pd5 single crystal,
which results in a perfect superelastic response with no
permanent plastic deformation under compressive loads
up to 2.5 GPa. Thus, it can be concluded that the stress
required for dislocation slip or irreversible deformation
twinning in the martensite phase is higher than
2.5 GPa for this material along the [1 1 1] orientation.
All of the superelastic loops in Figure 1 show harden-
ing even in the plateau regions. Moreover, as evident by
the increasing slopes of the plateaus, hardening increases
with increasing test temperature. The reason behind this
tendency could be the operation of additional martensite
variants activated by temperature, as observed in NiT-
iHf and CoNiGa single crystals [20,21]. Interactions
among these variants may hinder the transformation
or detwinning, and more stress may be needed to prop-
agate phase or twin boundaries [10,22] as the test tem-
perature in increased.

the yield stress of the martensite (and by inference the
austenite phase) is greater than 2500 MPa for the solu-

in over-aged and peak-aged conditions, respectively,
while the yield strength of the martensite phase was
1700 MPa for the over-aged and 2100 MPa for the

The maximum recoverable strain for the Ni 50.8Ti49.2
and Ni 51.5Ti48.5 single crystals along the [1 1 1] direction
was 3% in over-aged conditions in compression [10,11].
The maximum recoverable strain in the pseudoelastic
experiments for the Ni 45.3Ti29.7Hf20Pd5 single-crystal al-
loy is around 4.2%. Consequently, this alloy can recover
more strain compared to the aforementioned [1 1 1]-ori-
ented NiTi single crystals and it possesses a higher yield
strength even in the solution-treated condition. It should
be noted that in the current study no irrecoverable strain
was observed when the Ni
45.3Ti29.7Hf20Pd5 single-crystal
specimens were deformed at 70 /C176C up to a stress level of
2500 MPa.
Hysteresis in SMAs is a decisive parameter in the de-
sign and operation of many SMA-based components. It
is deﬁned as the non-reversible energy dissipation during
the thermoelastic martensitic phase transformation. The
mechanical or stress hysteresis was extracted from the
superelastic curves in Figure 1 and is summarized in Fig-
ure 2b as a function of the critical stress for the forward
transformation. The comparison between the results of
the current study and other NiTi-based alloys is de-
picted in Figure 3 . The [1 1 1]-oriented Ni
45.3-
Ti29.7Hf20Pd5 single-crystal alloy yields a mechanical
hysteresis of 1270 MPa at /C0 30 /C176C, which is the largest
mechanical hysteresis that the authors are aware of in
the SMA literature. In NiTi polycrystals, the mechanical
hysteresis is around 200–300 MPa [14], while it is around
400–500 MPa in NiTiNb alloys [15]. The stress hystere-
sis increases to 950–1000 MPa in NbTi/NiTi nanocom-
posite wires [16]. It is clear that [1 1 1]-oriented
NiTiHfPd alloys have signiﬁcantly higher stress hystere-
sis around room temperature than any other SMAs.
Thus, it is possible that textured NiTiHfPd alloys could
be a cost-eﬀective and promising candidate for biomed-
ical and damping applications.
It is well known that lattice friction and dislocation
generation increase both mechanical and thermal hyster-
esis in SMAs [23–25]. Since there is almost no irrecover-
able strain in the superelastic behavior of the material
and stress hysteresis is independent of transformation
strain, dislocation generation can be ruled out as a pro-
spective reason for the large hysteresis observed. Thus,
this large hysteresis can mainly be attributed to the lat-
tice incompatibility between the transforming phases,
the restricted mobility of phase front/twin boundaries
due to high frictional resistance or the relaxation of elas-
tic energy.
In thermoelastic phase transformations, elastic en-
ergy is stored during the forward transformation and,
in turn, promotes back transformation. Elastic strain
energy does not directly aﬀect thermal and mechanical
hysteresis [23]. However, if the stored elastic energy is re-
laxed subsequent to phase transformation, the potential
of the remaining elastic energy to promote back trans-
formation is lessened and thus the mechanical hysteresis
is expected to be larger [23,24].
Lattice friction is another dissipative mechanism that
increases hysteresis. Friction can be described as the
resistance of a phase front or variant motion in the
material [23]. Interactions between martensite variants
or among variants and phase front imperfections (i.e.

The close relationship between hysteresis and the lat-
tice compatibility of the transforming phases is well
known. Lattice compatibility is a function of the lattice
parameters of the transforming phases in SMAs. When
the interphase of the transforming phases is more com-
patible, a lower hysteresis is expected [26]. The large hys-
teresis observed in our Ni
45.3Ti29.7Hf20Pd5 single-crystal
alloy could be attributed to the incompatibility between
the austenite and martensite phases. It should be noted
that the mechanical hysteresis observed is about
1270 MPa at /C0 30 /C176C and 900 MPa at 70 /C176C for an ap-
plied strain of 6%. Such a change in hysteresis with tem-
perature could be due to the change in lattice parameters
of the transforming phases, which leads to a more com-
patible crystal structure at the interphase.
Mechanical hysteresis is closely related to the damp-
ing capacity of SMAs, which could be a useful ability
for various applications in the defense, construction
and transportation industries [17]. The damping capac-
ity can be calculated as the area between the loading
and unloading curves in superelasticity plots. For
[1 1 1]-oriented Ni
45.3Ti29.7Hf20Pd5 single crystals, the
damping capacity was approximately 44 J cm /C0 3, which
is smaller than the 54 J cm /C0 3 for NbTi/NiTi nanocom-
posites [16] and larger than the 38 J cm /C0 3 for NiTiNb
and the 16 J cm /C0 3 for Ni 50.6Ti49.4 alloys [19].
The ultrahigh strength, exceptional damping capacity
and practical ability to demonstrate superelastic behav-
ior at room temperature without irrecoverable strain
makes NiTiHfPd SMAs intriguing candidates for strate-
gically important applications in biomedical, aerospace,
oil–gas and construction applications. It should be
noted that, for biomedical applications, the presence
of Hf and Pd could also improve the visibility of NiT-
iHfPd components such as stents and prosthetics during
magnetic resonance imaging.
