a b s t r a c t 
In recent years, high-entropy alloys (HEAs) have become prominent metallic materials due to their unique 
design strategies and excellent mechanical properties. The HEAs-inherent high-entropy, lattice-distortion, 
sluggish-diffusion, and cocktail effects make HEAs maintain high strength, oxidation resistance, corrosion 
resistance, wear resistance, and other excellent comprehensive properties, showing stronger competitive- 
ness relative to traditional alloys. Refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) are considered as a new kind 
of high-temperature materials with great application prospects due to their excellent mechanical proper- 
ties and have the potential to replace nickel-based superalloy as the next generation of high-temperature 
materials. We reviewed the research status and preparation methods of RHEAs in recent years, including 
the metallurgical smelting, powder metallurgy, magnetron sputtering, and additive manufacturing tech- 
nologies. The microstructure and phase-transformation process of RHEAs were analyzed. The mechan- 
ical properties and main strengthening and toughening mechanisms of RHEAs, such as solid-solution 
strengthening, precipitation strengthening, and the transformation-induced plasticity (TRIP), were dis- 
cussed, and the deformation mechanism of RHEAs was revealed. The properties of RHEAs, including high 
strength, oxidation resistance, corrosion and wear resistance were reviewed. RHEAs will meet the huge 
market demand in the engineering materials ﬁeld, but there are still many challenges, such as the trade- 
off between high strength and high ductility, structural design, and performance optimization of RHEAs 
with brittle BCC structures. We believe that this combination of knowledge may shape the future of 
RHEAs and break through the mutually exclusive conundrum of high strength and high toughness for 
RHEAs.

1. Introduction 
Energy eﬃciency is affected by temperature. The higher the 
temperature, the higher eﬃciency. Therefore, all sorts of internal 
combustion engines (such as gas turbine) need to continuously in- 
crease the operating temperature. At present, most of the materi- 
als used in the high temperature ﬁeld are nickel-based superalloys, 
which still have excellent comprehensive properties at high tem- 
peratures up to 10 0 0 °C. However, when the working temperature 
exceeds 1300 °C, the application of nickel-based superalloys is lim- 
ited by its main components and the melting point of the precip- 
itation phase, which cannot meet the application needs of higher

In the traditional alloy design, a small amount of other ele-

In addition, different from traditional metals, HEAs have 
four unique effects, namely, high-entropy effect [ 17 , 18 ], sluggish- 
diffusion effect [19–21] , lattice-distortion effect [ 22 , 23 ], and “cock- 
tail” effect [24–27] , as shown in Fig. 2 . The high-entropy effect 
[ 28 , 29 ] makes the alloy always form a single solid-solution struc- 
ture, such as a body-centered cubic (BCC) or face-centered cubic 
(FCC) structure. The inherent lattice-distortion effect of HEAs refers 
to the formation of interstitial atoms, substitutional atoms, and 
precipitation phase/dispersion defects due to the addition of alloy- 
ing elements, leading to the strengthening and toughening of al- 
loys. The sluggish-diffusion effect results in many nanocrystalline 
and amorphous phases in HEAs, which can improve the strength 
and corrosion resistance of HEAs. In addition, the “cocktail” effect 
leads to the possibility of HEAs achieving unpredictably and excel- 
lent overall performance through the composition regulation [30–
33] . The four effects of HEAs are expected to solve the develop- 
ment of metal materials in the ﬁeld of high-temperature applica- 
tions. 
For the currently known HEAs, they can be divided into two 
categories according to the element to classiﬁcation. One is com- 
posed of transition metal elements, such as Co, Cr, Cu, Fe, and Ni 
of HEAs, and the other is based on the elements of refractory met- 
als, such as Mo, Ti, V, Nb, Hf, Ta, Cr, and W elements. Inspired by 
HEAs, Senkov et al. ﬁrst proposed the concept of RHEAs in 2010 
[35] . They introduced refractory elements into HEAs and studied 
NbMoTaW and VNbMoTaW [36] . These two RHEAs exhibit excellent 
properties at high temperatures and are much better than tradi- 
tional nickel-based superalloys in both the melting point and high- 
temperature mechanical properties. It shows that the RHEAs have 
the potential to replace the nickel-based superalloy as the high- 
temperature materials in the future. 
RHEAs have excellent mechanical properties, such as room and 
high temperature mechanical properties, elevated-temperature ox- 
idation resistance, corrosion resistance, etc., and have broad appli- 
cation prospects and research values [37] . In recent years, the re- 
search on RHEAs has become more and more extensive and in- 
depth. This paper reviews the research progress of RHEAs. In this 
paper, the preparation process, microstructure, strengthening, and 
toughening mechanisms and various properties of RHEAs are re- 
viewed. The room-temperature ductility of RHEAs is discussed. The 
last section brieﬂy summarizes the related problems of RHEAs, and 
gives an outlook on the future development and research direction 
of RHEAs.

2. Preparation technology of RHEAs 
In the preparation process of RHEAs, due to the high melt- 
ing point of refractory elements and the obvious differences be- 
tween them, it is easy to cause some defects in structures and per- 
formance [38] , which makes the preparation of RHEAs very diﬃ- 
cult and puts forward high requirements on processing equipment 
and process control. At present, the most commonly used prepara- 
tion method of RHEAs is casting, which mainly includes arc melt- 
ing [39] , induction melting, and electron-beam melting. Melt cast- 
ing is the ﬁrst method used to process HEAs, and it is also the 
most mature method to develop a new series of RHEAs. With the 
continuous development of new series of RHEAs, the preparation 
methods are gradually enriched. The preparation method of RHEAs 
by powder metallurgy (PM) has been developed. Researchers have 
prepared the NbMoTaWV RHEA in this way. The HEAs prepared 
in this way has the uniform composition and excellent compre- 
hensive performance. Hence, it has been gradually adopted by a 
large number of researchers. The additive-manufacturing technol- 
ogy, which has developed rapidly in recent years, is an advanced 
green intelligent manufacturing technology, which has been widely 
used in the aerospace, automobile mold, shipbuilding, and other 
ﬁelds. 
2.1. Smelting and casting 
Melting is the most widely used method for preparing RHEAs. 
Meanwhile, due to the poor oxidation resistance of refractory met- 
als at high temperatures, smelting needs to be carried out in vac- 
uum or high-purity inert atmosphere. Hence, most RHEAs are pre- 
pared by vacuum-arc smelting [39–42] , and its schematic diagram 
is shown in Fig. 3 (A). Vacuum-arc melting is to heat and melt var- 
ious high-purity metal element blocks or particles in a crucible 
according to a certain composition ratio in a vacuum arc furnace. 
Thus, the molten metal is solidiﬁed and cooled in the crucible. In 
order to avoid component segregation, multiple melting processes 
are required to ensure uniformity. Qiao et al. [43] successfully pre- 
pared the VTaTiMo RHEA with good mechanical properties through 
the method of vacuum arc melting. Guo et al. [44] prepared the 
MoNbHfZrTi RHEA by vacuum-arc melting, and studied the ther- 
mal deformation characteristics of its dynamic recrystallization. Yi 
et al. [45] prepared the CrHfNbTaTi RHEA by a vacuum-arc melt- 
ing method. The microstructures and mechanical properties of the 
alloy were analyzed. Meanwhile, its high-temperature mechanical 
properties were superior to those of nickel-base superalloys. 
2.2. Powder metallurgy 
With the development of preparation methods, a powder met- 
allurgy (PM) technique was developed to prepare RHEAs [46–48] . 
As shown in Fig. 3 (C1–C3), powder metallurgy consists of the mix- 
ing of metal powders as raw materials in a speciﬁc proportion, 
mechanical grinding in a high-energy ball mill, and then perform- 
ing spark-plasma sintering (SPS) consolidation to form products 
[ 47 , 49 ], and ﬁnally obtaining massive HEAs. Metal powder is the 
key to the preparation of refractory high-entropy alloys by pow- 
der metallurgy. The most important thing to pay attention to when 
preparing alloys by this method is that the powders cannot be con- 
taminated, which may negatively affect the mechanical properties 
of the alloys. 
Compared with the melting method, the preparation of RHEAs 
by the powder metallurgy method can effectively inhibit the com- 
position segregation of RHEAs, which makes HEAs smaller in grain 
size, more uniform in composition, and better in strength and 
plasticity [50] . It is reported that the compressive yield strength 
and compressive plasticity of the sintered TiNbTaZrAl RHEA are 
1740 MPa and 12% [51] , respectively, further indicating that the 
use of powder metallurgy can effectively improve the mechanical 
properties of RHEAs. The VNbMoTaW RHEA is prepared by powder- 
metallurgy process. The alloy has a superhard nanocrystalline BCC 
structure, and the hardness value reaches 16.3 GPa after sinter- 
ing [52] . Malek et al. prepared HfNbTaTiZr samples through pow- 
der metallurgy and obtained high hardness and ﬁne-grain struc- 
ture after high pressure torsional deformation [53] . Kang et al. pre- 
pared the Al 0.1 CrNbVMo RHEA by the mechanical-alloying (MA) 
and SPS process. The density of the alloy is lower than those of 
most nickel-based superalloys. The compressive strength of the al- 
loy is 2863 MPa at room temperature and exceeds 1400 MPa at 
10 0 0 °C. The speciﬁc yield strength of the alloy is 176 MPa cm 3 /g, 
which is superior to other RHEAs [54] . This trend indicates that the 
alloy is expected to be applied in the ﬁeld of lightweight RHEAs in 
the future. 
2.3. Magnetron sputtering 
In recent years, with the expansion of high-entropy alloys sys- 
tems, RHEAs coatings and ﬁlms have been widely used due to their 
high-temperature strength and stable thermal performance. Mag- 
netron sputtering is often used to prepare RHEAs ﬁlms [ 59 , 60 ], as 
shown in Fig. 3 (B). The principle is to use a magnetic ﬁeld to de- 
posit molten metal atoms on the surface of the target in a vacuum 
or special atmosphere to form a thin ﬁlm. The alloy ﬁlm prepared 
by this method can signiﬁcantly improve the mechanical proper- 
ties, such as the oxidation and corrosion resistance of the product, 
but the disadvantage is that the equipment is strictly required and 
the preparation cost is relatively high. RHEA is a good magnetron- 
sputtering material with excellent mechanical properties and good 
wettability. 
Bachani et al. [61] prepared the VNbMoTaWAl RHEA coating 
with ultra-high hardness by magnetron sputtering, which can ef- 
fectively improve the corrosion resistance of the 304 stainless steel 
matrix in an H 2 SO 4 solution. Alvi et al. [62] deposited a BCC- 
structured CuMoTaW RHEA ﬁlm on the 304 stainless steel by 
magnetron sputtering. The results show that the RHEA ﬁlm has 
nanocrystalline properties, the highest hardness is 21.3 GPa, the 
elastic modulus is 278.2 GPa, and it exhibits good wear resistance. 
This research will greatly promote the industrialization of HEAs 
ﬁlms. The RHEA thin ﬁlms prepared by magnetron sputtering usu- 
ally have higher hardness and wear resistance than as-cast and sin- 
tered RHEAs thin ﬁlms, and have broad application prospects in 
the ﬁeld of surface modiﬁcation. 
2.4. Additive manufacturing 
In recent years, the additive-manufacturing (AM) technology 
has attracted wide attention due to its advantages of fewer 
defects, high precision, high eﬃciency, and the ability to eas- 
ily manufacture complex-shaped parts. Therefore, the additive- 
manufacturing technology has been introduced to prepare HEAs. 
As shown in Fig. 4 (A), laser metal deposition (LMD) is an 
additive-manufacturing technology used in RHEAs to shape com- 
plex products. Dobbelstein et al. [63] successfully prepared RHEAs 
TiZrNbHfTa and TiZrNbTa by a laser metal deposition (LMD) 
method, overcoming the inﬂuence of huge melting point differ- 
ences among elements. 
Electron beam melting (EBM) is another additive-manufacturing 
process in which an electron-beam melts metal powder to obtain 
a speciﬁc part [64] . Fig. 4 (B) shows how it works. The single-phase 
TiVZrNbTa alloy with a BCC structure was prepared by the electron 
beam melting process, and the hydrogenation behavior of the syn- 
thesized material was studied [65] . Katz-demyanetz et al. [66] pre-

3. Microstructures and phases 
For each metal material, the microstructure and phase forma- 
tion have a great inﬂuence on its mechanical properties. Refer- 
ring to the existing literature, it can be found that most of the 
RHEAs systems currently studied and developed are single-phase 
BCC solid-solution structures. A few alloys have two or more phase 
structures, as listed in Table 1 . The RHEAs system can be roughly 
divided into three types: The ﬁrst type is the solid solution with 
a single-phase BCC structure, the second type is the precipitation 
of B2 and Laves phases-strengthening phases on the BCC matrix, 
and the third type is the precipitation of the HCP [72] , Al x Zr 5 [73] , 
M 5 Si 3 [74] , and other strengthening phases in the BCC matrix. 
The elements of RHEAs are mostly composed of transition 
group refractory metal elements, which themselves have a BCC lat- 
tice and good mutual solubility. Hence, they always form a BCC 
phase. In addition, subgroup IV metals of Ti, Zr, and Hf, which are 
often added to RHEA systems, are structurally stable BCC phases 
at high temperatures, and all have high solubility. ZrNbTiVHf is 
reported to form a BCC phase after high-temperature annealing 
[110] . The microstructure of the AlMo 0.5 NbTa 0.5 TiZr RHEA is re- 
ported [111] , as shown in Fig. 5 (A1–A3). The as-cast alloy has a 
typical dendritic structure ( Fig. 5 (A1)), the phase composition in- 
cludes the BCC/B2, and the Al-Zr rich phases is shown at high 
magniﬁcation ( Fig. 5 (A2)). Wu et al. [112] successfully prepared the 
HfNbTiZr RHEA. The RHEA remained a single BCC phase after ho- 
mogenized cooling at 1573 K for 6 h. Huang et al. [88] developed a 
BCC RHEA TaHfZrTi and achieved ductility induced by phase trans- 
formation. This ﬁnding provides an effective method to improve 
the HEAs strength and ductility combination of a brittle BCC struc- 
ture. 
In the multiphase alloy, the B2 phase is found as the ma- 
trix phase, and the B2/BCC mixed phase structure appeared, in 
which the BCC phase appears in the form of a precipitation 
phase. The structure of the modiﬁed B2/BCC nanophase is simi- 
lar to that of a superalloy, with good thermal stability and high- 
temperature strength retention. Chen et al. [113] prepared a single- 
phase ordered B2 structure of the AlNbTiZr RHEA. After the in-

A Laves phase (C14 or C15) is also a common phase in RHEAs,

cipitation, which usually leads to a sharp decline in the room- 
temperature ductility of RHEAs, but can effectively improve the 
high-temperature strength and oxidation resistance. Therefore, it 
is very important to understand the formation of the Laves phase 
[117] . The microstructures of TaMoCrTiAl and NbMoCrTiAl alloys 
are reported [116] , as exhibited in Fig. 5 (C1–C3, D1–D3). Both of

stroyed, forming two BCC and one cubic Laves phases. The exis- 
tence of the network structure (Laves phase) hinders the propaga- 
tion of cracks, and compensated for the decreased plasticity due to 
the introduction of the brittle-stiff Laves phase. 
4. Mechanical properties 
4.1. Strength and toughness at room temperature 
The crystal structure of the RHEAs matrix is usually a single- 
phase BCC structure. The strengths of RHEAs are higher than those 
of metals, but their plasticity is generally poor, and they often ex- 
hibit obvious brittleness. Researchers have tried many strategies to 
achieve a good combination of RHEAs strength and ductility. 
Adding or replacing elements in RHEAs can change the bal- 
ance between the strength and ductility. Yao et al. [119] added 
Ti and W elements to NbTaV alloy and carried out room temper- 
ature compression experiments. The yield strength of NbTaTiVW 
RHEAs reached 1420 MPa, the fracture strain was 20%, showing ex- 
cellent compression properties at room temperature. Zhang et al. 
[120] developed NbMoTa 0.5 Ni 0.5 HEAs with a compressive strength 
of 2297 MPa at room temperature and 651 MPa at high tempera- 
ture. On this basis, it is found that the addition of Ni and Ti can 
inhibit the formation of cracks, which has guiding signiﬁcance for 
the fabrication of RHEAs by additives.

The solid-solution strengthening and second-phase strengthen- 
ing caused by multiple-elements can also effectively im prove the 
room-temperature strength. The Al 2 O 3 ceramic particles are intro- 
duced into TaNbVTi, so that the TiO-precipitation phase forms a 
strengthening phase in situ , resulting in solid-solution strengthen- 
ing and precipitation strengthening, and achieving a benign com- 
bination of strength and plasticity [121] . Senkov et al. found that 
the strengths of multiphase refractory composite concentrated al- 
loys (RCCAs) are related to the melting temperature, Tm, and all 
of them decrease rapidly above the solidiﬁcation temperature, Ts, 
mainly due to the dissolution of the second phase [122] . Xiang 
et al. [123] set up a compositional gradient for the Al content to 
evaluate the mechanical properties of TaNbVTiAlx RHEAs. The re- 
sults show that all TaNbVTiAlx RHEAs have a single BCC solid solu- 
tion structure with good room-temperature strength and plasticity. 
These RHEAs have potential applications at 1200 °C. 
The valence electron concentration will affect the structures 
and properties of the material [124] , as shown in Fig. 6 (A). Ac- 
cording to the principle of the valence electron concentration 
(VEC), Chen et al. [125] designed four RHEAs [(ZrTiHfV 0.5 Nb 0.5 , 
Zr 2.0 TiHfVNb 2.0 , ZrTiHfNb 0.5 Mo 0.5 and ZrTiHfNb 0.5 Ta 0.5 ), VEC < 4.5]. 
The experimental results show that the plasticity of the al- 
loy is improved, the static compressive strain at room tempera- 
ture is greater than 50%, and a certain elongation is generated 
during the stretching process. In order to improve the room- 
temperature ductility of RHEAs, Sheikh et al. [126] designed an 
Hf 0.5 Nb 0.5 Ta 0.5 Ti 1.5 Zr RHEA based on the electronic theory, with the

4.2. High-temperature strength 
For superalloys, it has been focused more on high-temperature 
strengths than room-temperature strengths. Nickel-based superal- 
loys, as the most widely used typical superalloys, exhibited ex- 
cellent creep strength, oxidation resistance and fracture toughness 
[131–133] . However, they still cannot meet the strength and stabil- 
ity requirements at higher service temperature ( > 1100 °C) due to 
a fundamental limitation of melting points. Therefore, it is urgent 
to develop new RHEAs. Senkov et al. [ 35 , 36 ] discovered NbMoTaW 
and VNbMoTaW with a single-phase BCC structure composed of re- 
fractory elements for the ﬁrst time in 2010, and found that the 
yield strengths of these two RHEAs exceeded 400 MPa at 1600 °C, 
which is much higher than that of nickel-based superalloys, as 
shown in Fig. 9 (C). Therefore, researchers are very interested in the 
high-temperature strength of RHEAs. 
The stability of the RHEA system can be explained by the ther- 
modynamic formulation of the Gibbs free energy change of mixing. 
According to /Delta1G mix = /Delta1H mix - T /Delta1S mix , the higher the tempera- 
ture T m , the more chaotic the arrangement of atoms in the system, 
the higher the entropy value, and the minimum Gibbs free energy 
change /Delta1G mix , making the entire system stable. 
At present, the nickel-base superalloy Inconel 718 is widely 
used in the ﬁeld of superalloy. However, the temperature of this 
superalloy should not exceed 1300 °C. RHEAs show more at- 
tractive high-temperature performance. For example, VNbTiTaSi 
[134] RHEA precipitates nanoscale silicide during high temperature 
deformation. Its yield strength reaches 505 MPa at 10 0 0 °C, and 
it maintains good room temperature toughness with a room tem- 
perature ductility of 34%. Recently, Nie et al. [135] evaluated the 
mechanical properties of HfMoScTaZr RHEA with a single disor- 
dered BCC solid solution structure. The results show that the vick- 
ers microhardness and yield strength of the RHEA are 9400 MPa 
and 1778 MPa, respectively. The compressive yield strengths of the 
alloy at 10 0 0 °C and 120 0 °C are 963 MPa and 498 MPa, respec- 
tively. Xiang et al. [136] explored the high-temperature mechanical 
behavior of a series of TaNbVTiAl x RHEAs. The results show that all 
TaNbVTiAl x RHEAs have a single BCC solid solution structure with 
good high-temperature speciﬁc strength and reasonable plasticity 
as shown in Fig. 9 (A, B). These RHEAs also have application poten- 
tial at 1200 °C. 
In order to improve the high temperature strength of the alloy 
system and maintain reasonable room temperature toughness. Juan 
et al. [80] modiﬁed the composition of HfNbTaTizr and designed 
two new single-phase RHEAs HfMoTaTiZr and HfMoNbTaTiZr. The 
yield strength of the modiﬁed alloy is signiﬁcantly higher than that 
of HfNbTaTiZr alloy. At 1200 °C, the yield strength of HfMoNbTa- 
TiZr is more than 6 times that of HfNbTaTiZr. The alloy success- 
fully maintained good ductility at room temperature. NbTaTiV and 
CrMoNbV alloys [137] retain extremely attractive high-temperature 
strengths due to edge dislocations, as shown in Fig. 9 (D), un- 
like previous screw dislocations that control plastic ﬂow in 
BCC alloys. This study opens up new ideas for developing new 
RHEAs.

5.1. Oxidation resistance

temperature ﬁelds, such as aerospace engines, need to meet ex- 
tremely demanding conditions [158–160] . This material needs to 
have excellent high-temperature stability, speciﬁc strength, and 
strong high-temperature oxidation resistance. Usually in the pro-

5.3. Corrosion resistance 
Corrosion is one of the most common failure forms of metals, 
and it takes a huge toll on national resources. It is of great signiﬁ- 
cance to study the corrosion behavior of metals and develop good 
corrosion-resistant metals. Some metals exhibit inherently low cor- 
rosion rates due to passive ﬁlm formation, but once the passive 
ﬁlm is ruptured, localized pitting will lead to rapid metal failure. 
In recent years, researchers have found that RHEAs has good pas- 
sivation characteristics and can show excellent corrosion resistance 
[ 210 , 211 ]. This section reviews some advances in the corrosion re- 
search of RHEAs. 
Li et al. [42] found that the low-density TiCrVNb 0.5 Al 0.5 RHEA 
had low corrosion current density and high breakdown potential, 
and showed outstanding corrosion resistance in 3.5wt% NaCl and 
1 M HCL solution. Poulia et al. [212] studied the corrosion behav- 
ior of an MoTaNbVTi RHEA in the 3.5 wt% NaCl and Hank solu- 
tion. The results showed that no corrosion was found in the in- 
terdendritic region of the alloy. This trend indicates that MoTaN- 
bVTi is well responsive to these types of corrosive environments.

5.4. Bio-printing applications 
Compared to ceramics and polymers, metals are suitable as 
biomedical implant materials due to their high strength and plas- 
ticity. Metal biomaterials have been widely used in orthopedic 
implants such as hip replacements and artiﬁcial joints. Biomedi- 
cal metallic materials such as Ti and Ti-based alloys, 316 L stain- 
less steel, CoCrMo alloy and NiTi alloy have been used in the 
ﬁelds of hip joints, cardiovascular stents, bone plates, dental im- 
plants and surgical instruments [216] . Among them, Ti-based al- 
loys have become the ﬁrst choice for orthopedic and dental im- 
plant products because of their corrosion resistance and relatively 
low elastic modulus, good biocompatibility and mechanical prop- 
erties. However, titanium-based alloys exhibit unsatisfactory wear 
properties and “grain sickness” as implant materials. Amorphous 
titanium-based biomedical alloys have better resistance to dry fric- 
tion and wear than crystalline titanium alloys. However, titanium- 
based amorphous alloys suffer from pitting corrosion in physio- 
logical solutions, resulting in deterioration of frictional properties. 
Meanwhile, the size limitation and low plasticity of titanium-based 
amorphous alloys limit their clinical application as implant mate- 
rials. Therefore, the development of new biomedical implant al- 
loys with high corrosion and wear resistance has been the fo- 
cus of recent research on biomedical materials. The emergence 
of high-entropy alloys (HEAs) provides a new alloying strategy 
for its good mechanical properties and high corrosion resistance. 
Among them, TiZrHfNb [217] , TiZrNbTa [218] , TiZrHfNbMo [81] ,

6. Summary 
RHEAs have remarkable comprehensive characteristics and has 
considerable application prospects in aerospace, national defense, 
nuclear industry, medical devices, and other ﬁelds. For the past 
12 years, researchers have been designing new RHEAs and in- 
novating their manufacturing processes. In addition, researchers 
have carried out many studies on mechanical, oxidation, corro- 
sion, and wear behavior of RHEAs, and have made great progress. 
Many special RHEAs with excellent performance have been de- 
veloped for different harsh environments. In this last section, we 
summarize the current research status of RHEAs and propose 
some new ideas for the future development of RHEAs based on 
the latest developments in the ﬁeld of RHEAs in the existing 
literature.

With the continuous innovation and development of prepa- 
ration technologies, there are many preparation technologies for 
RHEAs, including smelting, powder metallurgy, magnetron sputter- 
ing, and additive manufacturing. Due to the relatively high melt- 
ing point of each element of RHEAs, large-size RHEAs cannot 
be prepared by the smelting process. Compared with the melt- 
ing method, RHEAs prepared by the powder-metallurgy method 
can avoid segregation and solidiﬁcation defects, eliminate pores 
and cracks, and has the advantages of uniform structure compo- 
sition and ﬁne grain size. These are all expected to play a role in 
preparing for RHEAs. Magnetron sputtering plays an important role 
in preparing RHEA ﬁlms. The rapid development of the additive- 
manufacturing technology provides more solutions for preparing 
high-performance special RHEAs. RHEAs with good mechanical 
properties were successfully prepared by laser cladding, LMD and 
SLM. Future research will focus on the need for researchers to fur- 
ther explore more eﬃcient and cheaper preparation technologies, 
so as to make the structures and performance of RHEAs control- 
lable, and realize the direct molding of RHEAs parts. 
The microstructure and phase formation of materials are very 
important and play a decisive role in the mechanical proper- 
ties of materials. RHEAs consist of three phases: BCC, B2, and 
Laves. The phase of RHEAs initially studied was mainly a single- 
phase BCC structure, and gradually developed into a dual-phase 
and multi-phase mixed structure. By changing the phase structure, 
researchers obtain more high-strength RHEAs, which is mainly 
caused by the introduction of strengthening phases (mainly Laves 
or B2) in the single-phase BCC matrix, but may also lead to re- 
duced plasticity of the alloy, which is often encountered in the de- 
velopment process of RHEAs. Future researchers should focus on 
how to maintain the toughness of RHEAs while strengthening it, 
and make further research progress on the combination of high 
strength and great ductility as possible. 
In recent years, researchers have carried out a large number 
of research experiments on improving the mechanical properties, 
oxidation resistance, corrosion and wear resistance of RHEAs. The 
addition of elements, such as Ti, W, Cr, Mo, V, Nb, Ta, and Zr, 
has a signiﬁcant impact on the oxidation resistance, corrosion, and 
wear resistance of basic properties. The phase structure of the al- 
loy can be controlled by selecting the proper preparation process 
and proper heat-treatment method, and the desired properties can 
be obtained. The realization of these good properties makes RHEAs 
promising to become ideal biomedical materials, which needs fur- 
ther exploration by researchers in the future. 
It is important to note that the best RHEAs architecture has 
not yet been found. HEAs have a wide range of components, and 
RHEAs have thousands of undeveloped ones, providing us with 
plenty of design opportunities. Researchers need to continue to ex- 
plore this compelling and vast ﬁeld of new materials boldly, using 
a combination of experiments, calculations, and advanced materi- 
als development tools, such as the Materials Genome Project.
