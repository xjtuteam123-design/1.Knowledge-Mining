ABSTRACT
The diffusion investigation of fcc Ni – Mo – Nb system is critical for establishing the kinetic database for the 
development of both Ni-based superalloys and cemented carbides, yet comprehensive diffusion studies remain 
absent. In this work, a total of twelve fcc Ni – Mo – Nb ternary diffusion couples were annealed at 1273, 1373, and 
1473 K for 504, 336 and 120 h, respectively. The compositional profiles of these diffusion couples were deter -
mined using Electron Probe MicroAnalysis. Utilizing available thermodynamic and kinetic databases for the sub- 
binary systems of the fcc Ni – Mo – Nb system and the experimentally determined composition profiles, the as -
sessments of interdiffusivities and atomic mobility parameters in fcc Ni – Mo – Nb alloys were simultaneously 
performed through a numerical inverse method incorporated in the CALTPP (CALculation of ThemoPhysical 
Properties) software. The good consistency between the simulated composition profiles by the numerical inverse 
method and the measured ones is further validated, demonstrating the reliability of the present assessments. 
Meanwhile, the model-predicted composition-dependent diffusivities indicate that the diffusion of Nb in fcc Ni is 
notably faster than that of Mo in fcc Ni. By utilizing the presently established atomic mobility parameters, the 
pre-frequency factor and activation energy were further predicted. It should be noted that the present diffusion 
investigation is pivotal for predicting microstructure evolutions and designing desired properties for advanced 
Ni-based superalloys and cemented carbides.
1. Introduction
Benefiting from the exceptional corrosion resistance, oxidation 
resistance, creep properties, and fatigue resistance at high temperatures, 
Ni-based superalloys are emerging as one of the ideal engineering ma -
terials for high-temperature applications such as modern turbine blades 
and nuclear reactors [ 1 – 3 ]. Moreover, Ni-based superalloys play an 
important role in many industrial applications (e.g., automotive, elec -
tronic communications, and aerospace) due to their high strength and 
remarkable weldability [ 4 – 6 ]. The comprehensive mechanical proper -
ties of Ni-based superalloys can be effectively improved by the addition 
of alloying elements. Studies have shown that the effective addition of 
Nb can promote the formation of γ ′ ′ precipitation phases and thus 
improve the tensile and creep properties as well as the high-temperature 
oxidation resistance of Ni-based superalloys [ 2 , 7 – 10 ]. Moreover, the 
additions of refractory elements such as Cr, Mo and W contribute to the 
maintenance of the structural stability of the γ and γ ′ phases for Ni-based 
alloys [ 2 , 11 , 12 ]. Among these refractory elements, Mo is important 
which can mitigate the coarsening kinetics of the γ ′ phase by controlling 
the lattice misfit of the γ and γ ′ phases in Ni based superalloys [ 13 – 15 ]. 
In addition, Nb and Mo are also useful additives for cemented carbides. 
Carbide and binder phases that have been sintered under an atmosphere 
or vacuum are named as cemented carbides [ 16 ]. Cemented carbide 
with Ni binder exhibits an excellent resistance to oxidation and corro -
sion, which can be utilized to manufacture wear mechanical parts for 
applications in oil extraction and water treatment [ 17 – 19 ]. However, 
compared to traditional Co binder, cemented carbides with Ni binder 
have a lower hardness and strength [ 20 ]. The addition of Nb and Mo 
carbides can effectively inhibit the grain growth of the WC phase, 
increasing the hardness and flexural strength of cemented carbides with 
Ni binder and improving the wettability between the WC phase and the 
binder Ni phase in cemented carbides [ 21 – 23 ]. The moderate addition 
of Nb and Mo can also enhance the corrosion resistance of cemented 
carbide [ 24 , 25 ]. Thus, Ni – Mo – Nb is a useful sub-system for both

Diffusion kinetics play a crucial component in the design and 
development of materials [ 26 , 27 ]. For Ni-based superalloys, diffusion is 
essential for predicting the microstructure evolution, creep resistance, 
and recrystallisation at high temperatures [ 28 ]. Furthermore, it can 
anticipate the grain size changes of cemented carbides around the sin -
tering process. It is thus crucial to investigate the diffusion behavior of 
the fcc Ni – Mo – Nb system to facilitate the design and development of 
novel Ni-based superalloys and cemented carbides with Ni binder pha -
ses. For the diffusion investigations in ternary alloys, Matano-Kirkaldy 
method is widely applied to determine the ternary diffusivities at the 
intersections of two diffusion paths [ 29 – 31 ]. And the software DICTRA 
(DIffusion Controlled TRAnsformation) [ 32 ] can be used to evaluate the 
atomic mobilities based on the determined diffusivities by 
Matano-Kirkaldy method in conjunction with the thermodynamic de -
scriptions within the CALPHAD (Calculation of PHAse Diagram) 
framework. This procedure to evaluate diffusivity and atomic mobility is 
the most widely utilized in diffusion investigations till now [ 33 – 36 ]. 
After the numerical inverse method being developed [ 37 , 38 ], we 
implemented this method through high-efficiency algorithms and 
incorporated it in CALTPP [ 37 , 39 ] software. By using this function, 
composition- and temperature-dependent diffusivities can be deter -
mined, and the atomic mobilities can be obtained simultaneously, which 
is of high accuracy and efficiency. Note that the reliability and efficiency 
of this function have been validated through considerable research work 
[ 40 – 43 ]. Thus in the present work, we use CALTPP software to evaluate 
diffusion behavior for fcc Ni – Mo – Nb system.
Using diffusion couple experiments and the numerical inverse 
method integrated with the CALTPP software, the principal objectives of 
the present work are as follows: (1) to quantitatively assess mobilities 
and interdiffusivities in fcc Ni – Mo – Nb alloys; (2) to validate the 
computational results produced by CALTPP via comparative analysis of 
predicted interdiffusivities, composition profiles, and diffusion paths 
with the experimental data; and (3) to calculate the activation energy, 
the pre-frequency factor, and the interdiffusivities throughout the whole 
range of composition and temperature studied using the currently 
determined atomic mobility parameters. The present work will advance 
the establishment of fundamental diffusion databases critical for alloy 
design, with a particular relevance to development of both next- 
generation Ni-based superalloy and cemented carbides.

4. Results and discussion
4.1. Experimental determination
Although the thermodynamic description of the Ni – Mo – Nb system is 
available [ 52 ], the thermodynamic description of the binary system 
adopted by Kaufman is inconsistent with the currently accepted Mo-Nb 
[ 53 ], Mo-Ni [ 54 ] and Nb – Ni [ 55 ] systems. Therefore, the thermody -
namic parameters of the Ni – Mo – Nb ternary system obtained by Kauf -
man do not accurately describe the phase equilibrium information of the 
Ni – Mo – Nb system. Referring to the available information on phase 
equilibria [ 52 ] and isothermal sections extrapolated by the binary sys -
tems, the compositions of all Ni – Mo – Nb alloys annealed at 1273, 1373 
and 1473 K for the present work were intended to be in the fcc single 
phase, which was verified by XRD ( Fig. 1 ). Table 1 lists the specific 
details of the constructed diffusion couples. Meanwhile, a representative 
backscattered electron image of the microstructure is shown in Fig. 2 , 
where it can be seen that there is only one phase in the samples. The 
dashed and solid lines in the figure are illustrated to show the Matano 
plane and the scanned path, respectively. Meanwhile, the arrow marks 
the direction of scanning in the EPMA measurement. The measured 
composition profiles for fcc Ni – Mo – Nb alloys at 1273, 1373 and 1473 K 
are represented by symbols in Figs. 3 – 5 , respectively. Moreover, the 
EPMA-measured composition profiles were fitted using Boltzmann 
functions, allowing the diffusivities at the intersections of each two 
diffusion paths to be calculated using the Matano-Kirkaldy method. 
Moreover, the fitted results were then used to determine the errors 
utilizing error propagation methods [ 46 ]. Note that the fitting results of 
composition profiles are attached in the supplementary file.

4.3. Verification of diffusivity and atomic mobility assessments
To critically validate that the current numerical calculation of dif -
fusivities and atomic mobilities for the fcc Ni – Mo – Nb system, Figs. 3 – 5
present a systematic comparison of CALTPP-simulated concentration 
profiles against experimentally measured profiles acquired at 1273 K, 
1373 K, and 1473 K. These plots show that, with the exception of minor 
variations in the composition profiles of Mo in the A1 diffusion couple, 
which might be due to EPMA experimental errors and solute segregation 
close to the interface that is not taken into account in the diffusion model 
[ 59 ], the predicted composition profiles (solid lines) and the experi -
mental results (symbols) are in good agreements. Moreover, Fig. 6
presents the diffusion paths of the presently prepared 12 diffusion
