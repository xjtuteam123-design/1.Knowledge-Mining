abstract
ZrC dispersion-strengthened FeCrAl alloys with enhanced high-temperature strength and good ductility
were fabricated through spark plasma sintering (SPS) and hot-forging. Nanosized ZrC particles with an
average particle size of 56 nm were homogeneously dispersed in the FeCrAl-ZrC alloys. The addition of a
small proportion of ZrC nanoparticles effectively re ﬁnes the grain size and improves the strength and
hardness of FeCrAl alloys. However, the ductility of FeCrAl alloys decreases gradually when the ZrC
content increases from 0 wt% to 2.0 wt%. The tensile strength of SPSed FeCrAl-1.0 wt% ZrC alloy at 800 /C14C
is 91 MPa, which is about 69% higher than that of pure FeCrAl, while the percentage elongation after
fracture of FeCrAl-1.0 wt% ZrC is 15.0%, which is lower than that of FeCrAl (48.2%). To improve the
ductility of FeCrAl-ZrC alloys, the SPSed samples were subjected to hot forging at 1000
/C14C. Through hot-
forging, signi ﬁcant enhancement in ductility of FeCrAl-ZrC alloys was achieved without reducing the
strength. The mechanical properties and microstructure of FeCrAl-1.0 wt% ZrC are stable after annealing
at 1000
/C14C for 5 h, exhibiting superior thermal stability as compared with FeCrAl.

1. Introduction
After decades of active research and development, zirconium
alloy is currently the most widely used cladding material in com-
mercial light water reactors (LWRs), owing to a combination of its
small neutron capture cross section, reasonable corrosion resis-
tance, and structural integrity under envisioned operating condi-
tions, compared with other clad options such as stainless steel,
beryllium, and aluminum [ 1,2]. However, the issues regarding
enhancing safety of nuclear plants during severe accidents became
a hot topic in many countries, following the Japanese Fukushima
Daiichi disaster in 201 1, which is caused by the failure in oxidation
resistance of zirconium alloy cladding materials during the loss of
coolant accident (LOCA) scenarios. One of the most attractive
concepts as the solution for improving safety is the accident
tolerant fuel (ATF), de ﬁned as the fuel-cladding system that can
maintain or improve the fuel performance for a considerable
duration compared with the standard UO 2-Zircaloy during loss of
active cooling in the core [ 2e4]. Considering that revolutionary
designs of a fuel-cladding system are dif ﬁcult to operate on current
serving reactors, it is more urgent to develop ATF claddings
compatible to current fuel pellets with enhanced high-temperature
performance [5].
One of the most promising candidate materials for ATF clad-
dings is Fe-Cr-Al (FeCrAl) alloys due to superior oxidation resistance
in comparison to zirconium-based alloys, and the FeCrAl alloy class
has been widely used successfully in many industries where high
temperature oxidation resistance is needed including water-vapor
containing environments in fossil fuel energy plants [ 6]. The FeC-
rAl class of ferritic alloys offer oxidation rates roughly two orders of
magnitude slower than Zr-based alloys over a wide temperature
range extending to near their melting point (~1500 /C14C) [7e10]. The
oxidation resistance strongly depends on the content of Cr and Al,
which can form protective scale on the alloy surface. In spite that

embrittlement causing reduction in ductility and fracture tough-
ness due to the formation of Cr-rich a0 precipitates [ 11e13]. The

welding and fabrication [ 6,14]. The results from several studies
showed that the alloy of Fe-13Cr-5Al can balance the detrimental
and bene ﬁcial effect of increasing Cr and Al content, and show
superior oxidation resistance at high temperature, and it was used
as the base reference material for Generation II alloy development
[6,15,16].
The raw FeCrAl alloys are well known for poor mechanical
strength at high temperature. Especially, FeCrAl is prone to defor-
mation under high temperature conditions of 800 e1000 /C14C due to
insufﬁcient high temperature strength, which poses a hidden
danger to the safety and reliability of fuel components, and cannot
improve the safety margin of a beyond design basis accident
(BDBA). At the same time, applying FeCrAl alloys to the cladding
requires reducing the wall thickness to compensate a neutron
penalty by FeCrAl whose thermal neutron absorption cross section
is about ten times that of zircaloy [ 17]. This requires increasing the
high temperature strength and plasticity of the alloy to compensate
its thickness loss.
To overcome the deterioration of strength, especially high
temperature strength, solid solution strengthening, grain boundary
strengthening and precipitation strengthening are commonly
employed. Nb distinguishes from other solid solution strength-
ening elements such as Mo and W, due to the larger atom radius. It
is also found that Nb tends to form Fe
2Nb-type Laves phase parti-
cles pinning the grain or subgrain boundaries, thus could effectively
preventing grain coarsening and improve high temperature
strength. However, at higher temperatures, the pinning effect of
Laves phase particles become weaker because of a lower volume
fraction and higher particle coarsening rate [ 18]. M.N. Gussev et al.
studied FeCrAl alloy with TiC addition and found that the addition
of 1.0 wt% TiC could effectively limit the grain growth and result in
reﬁned grains [ 16]. ZrC has a very high hardness and its melting
temperature is as high as 3540 /C14C, much higher than TiC (3160 /C14C).
ZrC was also used as strengthening phase in alloys [19]. Besides, the
thermal neutron absorption cross section of Zr is about thirty times
lower than that of Ti. These advantages make ZrC very attractive in
enhancing the high temperature mechanical properties of FeCrAl
alloys. In this work, ZrC nanoparticles were dispersed into FeCrAl
alloys to improve the high-temperature strength and to stabilize
the microstructure at high temperature. FeCrAl-(0, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0) wt%
ZrC alloys were fabricated by spark plasma sintering (SPS) method,
and the effects of ZrC addition on the microstructure and high
temperature mechanical properties of FeCrAl alloys were
investigated.
2. Experimental
FeCrAl-(0, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0) wt% ZrC samples for this investigation
were fabricated from nano-sized ZrC powders (average particle size
50 nm, purity >99.8%, purchased from Aladdin reagent Co., Ltd) and
atomized FeCrAl alloy powders (average particle size 10 mm, purity
>99%, purchased from Changsha Forever metal material Co., Ltd).
The chemical composition of FeCrAl alloy powders given by pro-
ducer catalog is listed in Table 1. The powders were ball-milled in a
planetary ball mill for 20 h in argon atmosphere (purity >99.999%)
with a ball-to-powder weight ratio of 12:1 and a rotation speed of
300 rpm. Stainless steel mill mortars and balls were used. The
diameter of the balls is in the range of 5 mm e10 mm.
The consolidation of the samples was carried out by using a SPS
equipment (FTC Group, SE-607, Germany). The as-prepared pow-
ders were loaded in graphite mold and then heated by pulse cur-
rent in a ﬂowing argon atmosphere (purity> 99.999%). The samples
were heated to 1 150 /C14C at a rate of 100 /C14C/min and held for 5 min
under a uniaxial pressure of 50 MPa, and then cooled to room
temperature (RT) at a rate of 100 /C14C/min. Three bulk samples with
30.0 mm diameter and about 3.0 mm thickness were fabricated for
each composition. During the SPS process, Fe 3C and Cr 23C6 layer
with a thickness of about 50 mm was formed on the sample surface
due to the reaction between FeCrAl and carbon from graphite
molds, which has been removed by wire-cut electrical discharge
machining. The as-cleaned samples were polished for further tests.
The density of the ﬁnal products was measured by using Archi-
medes principle. The theoretical density of the samples was
calculated from the fraction and density of each component. SPSed
FeCrAl sample was arc melted to obtain full dense sample and then
the density measured is 7.240 g/cm
3. The density of ZrC is 6.73 g/
cm3. After SPS, the FeCrAl and FeCrAl-(1.0 e2.0) wt% ZrC samples
were heated at 1000 /C14C and forged in air using an air power
hammer with a ﬁnal forging temperature of 800 /C14C. The thickness
reduction of forged samples is about 60%. In order to explore the
thermal stability of FeCrAl-ZrC alloys, the forged FeCrAl and FeCrAl-
1.0 wt% ZrC samples were annealed at 1000 /C14C in vacuum (10/C03 Pa)
for 5 h and 10 h.
Polished samples were subjected to low-force Vickers hardness
test at RT under an applied load of 200 gf and a dwell time of 15 s. A
minimum of ﬁve indentations were made for each sample, and the
average value was reported as the hardness value. Tensile testing
was carried out in ambient air at RT (20/C14C), 400 /C14C and 800 /C14Cw i t ha
strain rate of 10 /C03 s/C01 using an Instron-5967 machine. A type of
miniature tensile samples with a cross-section of 1.5 /C21.0 mm2 and
a gauge length of 5 mm have been used and at least three samples
were tested for each composition [ 19,20]. Tensile specimens were
held at the test temperature for 15 min prior to initializing each test
to ensure a homogeneous sample temperature during tensile testing.
The orientation and size distribution of FeCrAl-ZrC grains were
characterized by electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD). For EBSD
characterization, the samples were ground and polished using
emery papers of grades 400, 800, 1000 and 1500 grit size, and then
polished on polishing cloth using diamond polish suspension of 3
mm as abrasive. Finally, the samples were subjected to vibratory
polishing using an oxide polishing suspension to obtain high
quality surfaces. EBSD mappings were measured with an acceler-
ation voltage of 13 kV and collected using a CRYSTAL detector
(Oxford Instruments, Oxford, UK). Misorientation angle q > 10/C14was
used to distinguish the grain boundaries. The EBSD images are
slightly ﬁltered through the software (HKL Tango) to eliminate the
inﬂuences of second phase particles on FeCrAl grain observations.
The average grain size of FeCrAl and FeCrAl-ZrC alloys was
measured based on over 300 grains in EBSD graphs. The fracture
surfaces of the tensile-tested samples were characterized by using a
scanning electron microscope (SEM, Sirion200, FEI) and the accel-
erating voltage of electron beam is 10 kV. Transmission electron
microscope (TEM, JEM e2000FX) was used to study the micro-
structure of samples. Energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS,
INCA) analytical system installed on TEM was used for elemental
analysis. TEM samples were prepared by a Struers Tenupol-5 twin-
jet electropolisher.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Mechanical properties and microstructure of SPSed FeCrAl-ZrC
alloys
The density, grain size and hardness of SPSed FeCrAl and FeCrAl-
ZrC alloys is listed in Table 2. The relative density of all the samples
is higher than 98.8%, and the relative density of SPSed FeCrAl-ZrC
samples decreased slightly with the increasing ZrC content from
0% to 2.0%.
Fig. 1 shows the EBSD results of SPSed FeCrAl and FeCrAl-ZrC
samples. The SPSed FeCrAl samples without ZrC addition showed
several coarse equiaxed grain structure surrounded by some ﬁne
equiaxed grains, as shown in Fig. 1a. FeCrAl-ZrC samples exhibit the
similar structure, as shown in Fig. 1bed, but the average grain size
is much smaller. As listed in Table 2 , the average grain size of
FeCrAl-ZrC samples decreased from 0.94 mm to 0.50 mm when the
ZrC content increased from 0.5 wt% to 2.0 wt%, which is much
smaller than that of pure FeCrAl (1.87mm). This result indicated that
the addition of a small amount of ZrC nanoparticles could effec-
tively inhibit the grain growth during the high temperature sin-
tering process. It can be also found that a small fraction of grains are
relatively larger in both FeCrAl and FeCrAl-ZrC samples, as shown in
Fig. 1. This may originate from the inhomogeneity of FeCrAl parti-
cles size (1 e30
mm). The FeCrAl powders can be effectively re ﬁned
by ball-milling to micron or submicron scale. Nevertheless, some
FeCrAl particles remain relatively large in size after ball-milling at
300 rpm for 20 h, leading to inhomogeneous grain size distribution
in the SPSed FeCrAl. This inhomogeneous powder particle size also
reduces the homogeneity of ZrC particles distribution in the ball-
milled FeCrAl-ZrC mixture powders, which would inhibit the
pinning effects of ZrC on grain boundaries, and cause more signif-
icant grain growth in the zones with less ZrC distribution during
high temperature sintering process. The distribution of ZrC and the
grain size of FeCrAl-ZrC alloys should be more homogenous if the
ball-milling time was prolonged.
The hardness values of SPSed FeCrAl and FeCrAl-(0.5, 1.0, 2.0) wt
% ZrC are listed in Table 2. The hardness of SPSed FeCrAl is 243.8 HV.
As the ZrC content increased from 0.5 wt% to 2.0 wt%, the hardness
of FeCrAl-ZrC increased gradually from 275.6 HV to 298.3 HV. In
order to understand the hardness increase, the average grain size
and hardness of the FeCrAl with various ZrC contents is presented
in Fig. 2. It can be seen that the addition of 0.5 wt% ZrC signi ﬁcantly
reﬁnes the grains and increases the hardness of FeCrAl, and when
the content of ZrC increases from 1.0% to 2.0%, the grain re ﬁnement
effect and the increase of hardness tends to saturation. In poly-
crystalline metals, the grain boundaries impede the motion of
dislocations through materials. Reducing the grain size is a com-
mon method to improve the strength of materials, as described by
the Hall-Petch relationship. Besides, small particles dispersed in
metals can pin dislocations and thus strengthen the materials.
Therefore, the higher hardness of FeCrAl-ZrC samples comes
partially from the addition of nano-sized ZrC with high hardness
and partially from the smaller grains as compared with the SPSed
FeCrAl sample.

which could be observed in the engineering stress-strain curves in
Fig. 3a. The nanosized ZrC particles at grain boundaries can hinder

3.2. Effects of hot-forging on the microstructure and mechanical
properties of FeCrAl-ZrC alloys
The SPSed FeCrAl-ZrC showed high strength at RT and 800 /C14C,
however, its ductility is too low for ATF applications. In this work
therefore, hot-forging process after SPS was employed to improve
the ductility of FeCrAl-ZrC alloys, because high temperature plastic
deformation (e.g. forging, rolling, swaging) is very effective in
improving the relative density and the mechanical properties of
metals [ 23].
EBSD was conducted to show the microstructure of the forged
FeCrAl and FeCrAl-ZrC samples, and the inverse pole ﬁgures (IPF) in
Z-direction were plotted in Fig. 6 . The average grain size of forged
FeCrAl, FeCrAl-1.0 wt% ZrC and FeCrAl-2.0 wt% ZrC was 1.46 mm,
0.81 mm and 0.69 mm, respectively. The average grain size of FeCrAl
was reﬁned from 1.87 mm to 1.46 mm by forging, while the average
grain size of FeCrAl-ZrC grew up slightly, from 0.65 mm to 0.81 mm
for FeCrAl-1.0 wt% ZrC, and from 0.50 mm to 0.69 mm for FeCrAl-
2.0 wt% ZrC.
Fig. 7 shows the typical engineering strain-stress curves of the
hot-forged FeCrAl-(0, 1.0, 2.0) wt% ZrC tensile-tested at RT, 400 /C14C
and 800 /C14C. At RT, the forged FeCrAl showed higher tensile strength
(743 MPa) and comparable ductility with a percentage elongation
of 19.2% as compared with the SPSed one, whose UTS and per-
centage elongation is 690 MPa and 21.8%, respectively. For the
forged FeCrAl-ZrC samples, both the strength and ductility at RT
were improved and the enhancement in ductility is much more
signiﬁcant. For example, the UTS of the FeCrAl-1.0 wt% ZrC at RT
increases from 822 MPa to 835 MPa and the percentage elongation
increases from 12.2% to 15.3% after hot-forging. For the FeCrAl-
2.0 wt% ZrC samples, the enhancement in room-temperature me-
chanical properties is more remarkable, noting that the UTS in-
creases from 778 MPa to 858 MPa and the percentage elongation
increases from 4.1% to 9.2% after forging. When the temperature
increases to 400 /C14C, the strength of the forged FeCrAl and FeCrAl-
ZrC decreases while the ductility results are relatively stable
(Fig. 7b). At 800 /C14C, the UTS and percentage elongation of the forged
FeCrAl is 51 MPa and 68.5%, respectively. With the increasing ZrC
content to 1.0% and 2.0%, the tensile strength of forged FeCrAl-ZrC
samples at 800 /C14C increases to 91 MPa and 1 13 MPa, while the
ductility decreases gradually to 35.6% and 19.3%, respectively. After
hot-forging, the percentage elongation of FeCrAl at 800 /C14C increases
by about 42%, while those of the FeCrAl-1.0 wt% ZrC and FeCrAl-
2.0 wt% ZrC increase by over 84% ( Table 3). The forged FeCrAl-ZrC
samples exhibit superior ductility and comparable strength at
800 /C14C as compared with the SPSed ones. These results indicate that
the mechanical properties of the as-sintered FeCrAl-ZrC samples
have been effectively enhanced by hot-working.
The forged FeCrAl-1.0 wt% ZrC shows enhanced strength and
favorable ductility at both RT and 800 /C14C. Nevertheless, the high-
temperature strength of FeCrAl-ZrC is still lower than that of
some ODS-FeCrAl steels [ 24,25], which could be attributed to the
larger particle size of ZrC (56 nm). For example, the size of oxide
particles in ODS-FeCrAl fabricated by hot-extruding is only about
3n m[ 24]. The larger particle size leads to a lower number density
of particles and thus lower strength for the dispersion-
strengthened alloys with the same composition. More work is
needed to optimize the size and distribution of ZrC particles to
further improve the mechanical properties of FeCrAl-ZrC alloys.

tensile strength results of the forged FeCrAl-1.0 wt% ZrC and the

the strength. These results indicate that FeCrAl-1.0 wt% ZrC has

4. Conclusions
Fine-grained FeCrAl-ZrC alloys with enhanced strength and
favorable ductility were fabricated through SPS and hot-forging.
Effects of ZrC contents on the mechanical properties and micro-
structure of FeCrAl alloys were investigated. Nanosized ZrC parti-
cles with an average particle size of 56 nm were homogeneously
dispersed in FeCrAl-ZrC alloys. The average grain size of SPSed
FeCrAl-1.0 wt% ZrC is 0.65
mm, which is much smaller than that of
SPSed FeCrAl (1.87 mm). The addition of ZrC nanoparticles signi ﬁ-
cantly reﬁnes the grains and improves the strength and hardness of
FeCrAl-ZrC alloys, but leads to decreased ductility. The SPSed
FeCrAl-ZrC samples with high ZrC contents have ﬁne grains but
poor ductility. After hot-forging, signi ﬁcant enhancement in
ductility of the FeCrAl-ZrC alloys was achieved while the strength
was comparable or slightly increased as compared with the SPSed
ones. The forged FeCrAl-ZrC samples also exhibit excellent thermal
stability in microstructure and mechanical properties, which can be
attributed to the pinning effects of ZrC nanoparticles on disloca-
tions and grain boundaries. Nevertheless, more work is needed to
optimize the microstructure of the alloys including the size and
distributions of ZrC particles so as to further improve the me-
chanical properties.
