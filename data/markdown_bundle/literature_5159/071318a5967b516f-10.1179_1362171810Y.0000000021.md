Brazing C/SiC composites and Nb with TiNiNb
active filler metal
Y. Z. Liu, L. X. Zhang, C. B. Liu, Z. W. Yang, H. W. Li and J. C. Feng *
C/SiC composites and Nb were vacuum brazed with the Ti 39?4Ni39?4Nb21?2 alloy being the active
filler metal. The mechanical properties of the filler material, the microstructure and the strength of
brazing joints were investigated. The results showed that the filler TiNiNb alloy has a tensile
strength of 860 MPa, an elongation of 51% and an elastic modulus of 78 GPa. Both Ti and Nb
elements in the filler reacted with C/SiC during the brazing process, and a well bonded C/SiC–Nb
joint was obtained. The ductile filler metal released the thermal stress in the joint. When the
brazing was performed at 1220 uC for 20 min, the shear strength of brazed joints reached 149, 120
and 73 MPa at 20, 600 and 800 uC respectively.
Keywords: C/SiC composites, Brazing, TiNiNb active filler, Microstructure, Reaction, Mechanical properties
Introduction
Carbon ﬁbre reinforced SiC (C/SiC) ceramic matrix
composites (CMCs) have been considered as one of the
promising materials for various high temperature
applications, e.g. thrust chamber in rocket engines and
thermal protection system in re-entry spacecrafts, due to
their low density, high toughness and high oxidation
resistance.1,2 In many applications, the joining of C/SiC
to metals is necessary to fabricate complex structural
components.
Brazing,3–6 two-stage joining7 and joining via a pre-
ceramic polymer8,9 have been used to bond C/SiC with
metal/alloys. When using Ag–Cu–Ti as the active ﬁller, the
brazing joints exhibit high shear strength (180 and
140 MPa at room temperature and 500 uC respectively);3
however, they cannot serve at a temperature higher than
500uC. Joints that were brazed with Ni based metallic glass
alloys have intimate interfacial contact, but the strength of
joints is not clear yet.6 Two-stage joining was used to bond
C/SiC with the SiC coating.7 However, the pressure is too
high, which limits its application on complex structures.
With respect to preceramic polymer, it was only used to
join C/SiC itself.8,9 Therefore, similar to joining ceramics,
brazing is a promising method, 10–14 but the suitable ﬁller
metal for C/SiC requires further investigation.
Ti–Ni–Nb alloys have good mechanical properties15,16
and excellent oxidation resistance at high temperature.17,18
According to the research of Piaoet al.,19 the TiNi and Nb
rich (Nb,Ti) solid solution forms a pseudobinary eutectic
at 1150 ?7uC. The TiNi–(Nb,Ti) eutectic alloy has high
ductility.20,21 Moreover, both Ti and Nb are active
elements used for brazing ceramics. 10,22 These active
elements can react with ceramics, promote the wetting of
ﬁller metal on ceramics and obtain successful brazing
ceramic joints. Therefore, the TiNi–(Nb,Ti) eutectic alloy
is a promising ﬁller for brazing ceramics and CMC.
However, there is no report on brazing ceramic or CMC
with this ﬁller alloy yet.
The speciﬁc eutectic composition of the TiNiNb alloy
is not established. Both Ti
40Ni40Nb20 (at-%)16,19 and
Ti36Ni38Nb26 (at-%)23 were reported to be eutectic
alloys. In this study, Ti 37Ni37Nb20 (atomic ratio;
Ti39?4Ni39?4Nb21?2, at-%) alloy was developed and used
as the active ﬁller to braze C/SiC and Nb. Ti–Ni–Nb was
prepared by arc melting. The microstructure, mechan-
ical properties and melting point of the ﬁller were
studied. The effect of brazing temperature on the
microstructure of the C/SiC–Nb joints was investigated.
The shear strength of C/SiC–Nb joints at 20, 600 and
800uC was tested.
Experimental
Ti39?4Ni39?4Nb21?2 alloy was prepared by arc melting

30 min. The gage length of a tensile specimen was

Results and discussion
Microstructure and properties of
Ti39?4Ni39?4Nb21?2 alloy
The XRD pattern and microstructure of the
Ti39?4Ni39?4Nb21?2 alloy are shown in Fig. 1. The as cast
alloy consists of bcc-(Nb,Ti) solid solution and B2-TiNi
intermetallics. The white phase is (Nb,Ti), and the dark
phase is TiNi. These two phases form a ﬁne eutectic
structure.
The properties of the brazing ﬁller metal play a very
important role in the properties of the brazing joints. Before
brazing, the tensile strength of the Ti
39?4Ni39?4Nb21?2 alloy
(hot rolled and vacuum annealed) was tested at room
temperature. The typical stress–strain curve and fracture
surface morphology of the alloy are shown in Fig. 2.
Figure 2a shows that the alloy exhibits a superior tensile
strength of 860 MPa and a superior elongation of 51%. The
51% elongation corresponds to the dimple fracture surface.
The elastic modulus of the alloy is 78 GPa. The superior
ductility and the low modulus of the alloy indicate that
Ti
39?4Ni39?4Nb21?2 is a good choice to interconnect dissimilar
materials because the ductile interlayer in the dissimilar
material joints can alleviate the thermal stress in it.

Microstructure of C/SiC and Nb brazing joints
The microstructure of the C/SiC and Nb joint brazed with
Ti39?4Ni39?4Nb21?2 ﬁller metal at 1220 uC for 20 min is
shown in Fig. 4. It can be seen from Fig. 4 that the joint is
well bonded and contains TiNi–(Nb,Ti) eutectics.
At the interface of C/SiC and the ﬁller alloy, it can be
found that an interlocking reaction layer is formed,
which is believed to be beneﬁcial to the properties of the
joint. This layer is rich in Nb, Ti and C according to the
EDS result (Nb
15Ti24Ni1Si1C59, at-%). The XRD
patterns in Fig. 5 reveal that there is (Ti,Nb)C in the
joint. Therefore, this interfacial reaction layer could be
(Ti,Nb)C. The (Ti,Nb)C inserts into the matrix of the
C/SiC. The inserted (Ti,Nb)C embeds the carbon ﬁbres
of C/SiC, and this is beneﬁcial to improving the strength
of the brazing joint.
The formation of (Ti,Nb)C is related to the interfacial
reactions between ﬁller metal and C/SiC during brazing.
Both Ti and Nb are active elements and can react with
ceramics. The possible reactions between Ti,Nb and C/
SiC can be expressed as follows

In addition, reactions (3) and (4) release Si to the ﬁller

In the joint shown in Fig. 4 b, the grey phase near
(Ti,Nb)C mainly contains Nb, Ti, Ni and C

the Ti 2Ni structure as interstitial atoms [saturation,
(Ti,Nb)4Ni2(O,C)].25 It has been proven that C can
diffuse through the TiC layer when melting TiNi in the
graphite crucible26 and that (Ti,Nb)C has the same cubic
structure as, and similar crystal constant to, TiC.
Therefore, C from C/SiC diffuses through (Ti,Nb)C
layer into the ﬁller alloy.
There is a thin white grey layer between (Ti,Nb)C and
(Ti,Nb)2Ni, which is marked by the black double arrow
in Fig. 4 b. This layer is so thin that its composition
cannot be accurately determined by SEM/EDS, and its
structure cannot be analysed by XRD.
At the centre of the joint, some white grey lathes are
dispersed in the TiNi–(Nb,Ti) eutectics. The lath phase
contains some Si (Nb 34Ti23Ni38Si5, at-%). There is no
crystal structure information about this lath phase in the
XRD database. It is expressed as silicide in this study.
The formation of silicide in the joint can be attributed to
the Si concentration in the ﬁller alloys. It was released by
reactions (3) and (4).

The speed of reactions (1)–(4) increases when the
brazing temperature increases; the amount of reactants
[(Ti,Nb)C and Si] increases correspondingly. Once the
solid (Ti,Nb)C forms and grows into a continuous layer,
Ti and Nb cannot contact C and SiC directly; the
elements (including Ti, Nb, C and Si) have to diffuse
through the solid (Ti,Nb)C for further reaction, which
decreases the speed of reactions (1)–(4). The thicker the
(Ti,Nb)C layer grows to, the slower the reactions
become. Thus, although the brazing temperature
increases from 1180 to 1260 uC, the thickness of
(Ti,Nb)C just increases slightly. The amount of Si and
silicide does not change considerably.

Conclusions
The arc melted Ti 39?4Ni39?4Nb21?2 alloy exhibits a
homogeneous eutectic microstructure that contains
TiNi and (Nb,Ti). After being rolled at 900 uC and
annealed in vacuum at 1000 uC for 30 min, the tensile
strength and the elongation of the alloy are 860 MPa
and 51% respectively. The melting temperature of the
alloy is 1142 uC.
C/SiC and Nb can be successfully brazed at a wide
temperature range using the Ti39?4Ni39?4Nb21?2 alloy as the
active ﬁller. During brazing, both Ti and Nb react with C/
SiC and form (Ti,Nb)C, (Nb,Ti) 5Si3 and (Ti,Nb) 2Ni
phases at the (C/SiC)/ﬁller metal interface. When increas-
ing the brazing temperature from 1180 to 1260 uC, the
microstructure of the joints has no evident change. The
joint has the (C/SiC)/(Ti,Nb)C/(Nb,Ti)
5Si3/(Ti,Nb)2Ni/

eutectic/Nb structure. As a result of this, the room
temperature shear strength of joints brazed at 1180–
1260uC is higher than 113 MPa. In particular, the
maximum room temperature shear strength of joints
obtained at 1220 uC is 149 MPa. The shear strengths of
the brazing joints at 600 and 800 uC are 120 and 73 MPa
respectively.
