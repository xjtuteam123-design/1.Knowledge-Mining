Abstract 8 
Three Co-30Ni-xAl-(15-x)W-1Ta-4Ti-yCr septenary all oys were systematically 9 
studied with the aim of developing multiple-compone nt Co-Ni-base alloys with 10 
balanced properties. The microstructural stabilitie s were investigated at 1000 °C and 11 
1100 °C after long-term aging. The experimental res ults indicate that increasing the 12 
Cr content significantly reduces the microstructura l stability and promotes the 13 
precipitation of µ phase; while increasing Al/W ratio increases the γ′ volume fraction 14 
and the γ/γ′ lattice misfit. At the same time, increasing the C r content increases the 15 
hardness of γ′ phase and increasing Al/W ratio shows the opposite effect. The APT 16 
results reveal that such effects should be mainly a ttributed to the change of elemental 17 
partitioning behaviors between the γ and γ′ phases. Furthermore, increasing the Cr 18 
content and Al/W ratio predominantly enhances the o xidation resistance at 1000 °C, 19 
resulting in promising oxidation property which is comparable to commercial Ni-base 20 
superalloys Waspaloy and Udimet 720. Finally, the well balance of the microstructural 21 
stability, alloy density, γ′ phase hardness and oxidation resistance can be ach ieved. 22 
This study will be beneficial for the continuous ef forts on the development of 23 
multi-component Co-Ni-base superalloys for industrial applications. 24

1 Introduction 29 
Conventional carbide and solid solution strengthene d Co-base superalloys have 30 
higher melting temperature (by 50 - 100 °C), better fatigue resistance and weldability 31 
than Ni-base superalloys [1]. However, due to the a bsence of a stable γ′ strengthening 32 
phase, the application of Co-base superalloys at high temperature is more limited than 33 
that of γ′ strengthened Ni-base superalloys [1]. Co-Al-W allo ys with γ′ precipitation 34 
were first identified in 1971 by Lee [2], and were subsequently rediscovered and 35 
systematic studied in 2006 by Sato et al. [3], open ing up a new path for further 36 
development of Co-base superalloys. Since then, other γ′ strengthened Co-base alloys, 37 
such as Co-Ti-Cr-base [4], Co-Al-Mo-base [5-7] and Co-Al-V-base alloys [8], have 38 
been also developed by different research groups. It has been confirmed that the creep 39 
properties of Co-Al-W-base single crystal alloys ca n be comparable to that of 40 
commercial Ni-base single crystal superalloys [9, 1 0]. However, the oxidation 41 
resistance at high temperature of this new class of alloys is also essential for their 42 
engineering applications. Therefore, many previous studies aimed at elucidating the 43 
oxidation behavior and mechanism of Co-Al-W-base al loys [11-19]. Most of these 44 
studies focus on the oxidation behavior at 800 °C a nd 900 °C [11-17], with very 45 
limited data reported for 1000 °C [14, 18, 19]. The understanding of oxidation at 46 
800 °C and 900 °C is of importance for alloys inten ded for the use at intermediate 47 
temperatures, while temperatures above 1000 °C may be more important for 48 
accelerating the application of Co-Al-W-base single crystal alloys. 49 
Adding Cr to Co-Al-W-base alloys is one way to improve the oxidation 50 
resistance [11, 15, 16] by promoting the formation of a protective oxide layer, Cr 2O3, 51 
through a similar mechanism to Ni-base single cryst al superalloys [20]. However, as 52 
the γ/γ′ two-phase region of Co-Al-W-base alloys is very na rrow [3], the addition of 53 
Cr also promotes significant precipitation of britt le secondary phases [21, 22], which 54 
are deleterious for mechanical properties. To allev iate this problem, most of the 55 
reported Cr-containing Co-Al-W-base alloys utilize ~ 30 at.% Ni additions to improve 56 
their microstructural stability [14, 17, 23-26], as Ni has been shown to enlarge the γ/γ′ 57 
two-phase region [27]. Another issue is that the ad dition of Cr decreases the γ′ solvus 58 
temperature of Co-Al-W-base alloys, which is essent ial for the high temperature 59 
capability of superalloys. Thus, the addition of Cr to Co-Al-W-base alloys should be 60 
optimized carefully. 61 
Al is another element that can improve the oxidatio n resistance of metals by 62

forming a passive layer, and it typically has syner gistic effects with Cr on the 63 
formation of protective oxidation layers [28]. In N i-base superalloys, the Al and Cr 64 
contents have already been highly optimized, e.g., t he Al and Cr contents of the 65 
widely used 2 nd Ni-base single crystal superalloy CMSX-4 are 5.6 w t.% (12.6 at.%) 66

content to avoid deleterious phases. This indicates that the contents of Al and Cr in 69 
Co-Al-W-base alloys need to be further optimized fo r oxidation resistance and 70 
microstructural stability. However, the synergistic effects of Al and Cr in 71 
Co-Al-W-base alloys has been rarely considered and discussed by previous studies. 72 
Recently, Co-7Al-8W-1Ta-4Ti single crystal alloy with excellent creep properties 73 
at high temperature has been developed in our group , and its creep life is longer than 74 
1st generation single crystal superalloy CMSX-3 at 982 °C/248 MPa [9]. In order to 75 
promote the engineering application of this alloy, it is necessary to further improve the 76 
γ′ solvus temperature, microstructural stability and oxidation resistance, as well as 77 
reduce the alloy density. Our previous work indicat ed that the Al/W ratio is an 78 
essential parameter for Co-Ni-base alloys. For exam ple, increasing Al/W ratio (1:1 79 
substituting W with Al) could improve the γ′ solvus temperature, microstructural 80 
stability and reduce the alloy density in alloys with 30 at.% Ni additions [30]. 81 
On the basis of these results and with aim of impro ving the oxidation resistance 82 
without significantly reducing microstructural stab ility, three Co-Ni-Al-W-Ta-Ti-Cr 83 
alloys with different Cr contents and Al/W ratios w ere systematically investigated in 84 
the present work. The combined effects of Cr and Al /W ratio on the long-term 85 
microstructural stability and the elemental partiti oning behavior between γ/γ′ 86 
two-phases, as well as the oxidation behavior at 10 00 °C were revealed. In addition, 87 
nanoindentation was performed on the γ′ phase of these experimental alloys as a 88 
preliminary evaluation of mechanical properties, si nce the γ′ phase strengthening 89 
plays a critical role for the overall mechanical pr operties. This study will be helpful 90 
for the alloy design of multi-component Co-Ni-base superalloys to achieve a balanced 91 
property profile. 92

4.4 Oxidation resistance and mechanism 511 
Besides the microstructural stability, the oxidation resistance at high temperature 512 
is also an essential property for superalloys [20]. In general, Cr, Al, and Si can 513 
consistently result in the formation of protective scales for metals [28]. These 514 
elements could form the protective Cr 2O3, Al 2O3 and SiO 2 layers during the oxidation 515 
in air, respectively, but also have synergistic eff ects with each other. Nevertheless, 516 
Cr 2O3 is not stable at temperatures above 900 °C as it i s further oxidized to the 517 
gaseous CrO 3 [56]. Meanwhile, the addition of Si tends to embri ttle most alloys [28]. 518 
Since Al 2O3 is thermodynamically stable even at higher tempera tures above 1000 °C 519 
[28], a continuous Al 2O3 layer is the most desirable oxide protection for s uperalloys 520

[57]. 521 
The oxidation resistance of Co-Al-W-base alloys is commonly inferior without 522 
the additions of Cr or Si. For example, the mass ga in of alloy Co-30Ni-10Al-10W 523 
after isothermal oxidation at 1000 °C for up to 100 h in air is 43 mg cm -2
 [19]. The 524 
addition of Si could significantly reduce that mass gain value to 7.6 ~ 12.4 mg cm -2 525 
[18, 19] (Figure 6(b)); however, Si additions were not considered in the current alloys 526 
due to its embrittlement effect [28]. In the presen t study, alloy 5Cr-A formed a Cr 2O3 527 
layer and exhibited a mass gain of 7 mg cm -2 after the isothermal oxidation at 1000 °C 528 
for 100 h (Figure 7(a)), showing a distinctly bette r oxidation resistance compared to 529 
alloy Co-30Ni-10Al-10W, even though the Al concentr ation of alloy 5Cr-A is lower. 530 
Further addition of Cr in alloy 9Cr-A resulted in a similar but thinner oxide layer than 531 
that of alloy 5Cr-A (Figure 7(b)), indicating a fur ther improvement of oxidation 532 
performance at 1000 °C. The improved oxidation resi stance of the higher Cr 533 
containing alloy is due to the beneficial effect of Cr for the nucleation and growth of 534 
Cr 2O3 layer, which can reduce the growth rate of externa l oxides, such as CoO [28]. 535 
However, as Cr 2O3 has an evaporation effect above 900 °C [56], the C r 2O3 layers in 536

continuous Al 2O3 layer did not form in alloy 5Cr-A or alloy 9Cr-A a t 1000 °C, but 539 
forming discrete internal oxide Al 2O3, which is commonly undesirable. Therefore, 540 
these two alloys exhibited inferior oxidation resis tance compared to that of alloy 541 
5Cr-B (Figure 6). The higher Al concentration in al loy 5Cr-B allowed the formation 542 
of a dense continuous Al 2O3 layer (Figure 7(c)). This kind of Al 2O3 layer can provide 543 
an effective oxidation protection even at 1000 °C, resulting in a superior oxidation 544 
resistance of alloy 5Cr-B over that of alloy 5Cr-A and alloy 9Cr-A (Figure 6). 545
