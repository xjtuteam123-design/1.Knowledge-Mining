tungsten composites for high temperature applications

Abstract
Tungsten and tungsten alloys are widely used in high temperature environments where arc ablation or mechanical deformation
and damage are the main sources of materials failure. For high temperature critical applications in thermomechanical environments,
however, the low strength limits the use of tungsten and tungsten alloys. Hence, new tungsten based materials with good high
temperature thermomechanical properties need to be developed in order to extend the use of tungsten. TiC particle-reinforced
tungsten based composites (TiC
p/W) were fabricated by hot pressing at 2000/C176C, 20 MPa in a vacuum of 1:3 /C210/C03 Pa. The
composites were examined with respect to their thermophysical and mechanical properties at room temperature and at elevated
temperature. Vickers hardness and elastic modulus increased with increasing TiC content from 0 to 40 vol.%. The highest ﬂexural
strength, 843 MPa, and the highest toughness, 10.1 MPam1=2, of the composites at room temperature were all obtained when 20
vol.% TiC particle were added. As the test temperature rose, the ﬂexural strength of the TiCp/W composites ﬁrstly increased and
then decreased, except in the monolithic tungsten. The highest strength of 1155 MPa was measured at 1000/C176C in the composite
containing 30 vol.% TiC particles. The strengthening eﬀect of TiC particles on the tungsten matrix is more signiﬁcant at high
temperatures. With the addition of TiC particles, the thermal conduction of tungsten composites was drastically decreased from 153
Wm
/C01 K/C01 for monolithic W to 27.9 Wm/C01 K/C01 for 40 vol.% TiCp/W composites, and the thermal expansion was also increased.
The new composites are successfully used to make high temperature grips and moulds.

1. Introduction
The rapidly increasing demand for high performances
in materials in the last 20 years has placed no single
material in the position to fulﬁll the rising requirements
[1,2]. As a refractory metal with the highest melting
point, tungsten is technologically attractive and widely
used in various ﬁelds [3–5]. Especially, a small coeﬃcient
of thermal expansion and good high temperature me-
chanical properties are attractive characteristics for im-
portant structural applications at high temperatures.
But the strength of tungsten drops greatly as the tem-
perature rises. For example, the strength of tungsten
decreases 60–80% at 1000 /C176C compared with that at
room temperature [3]. In some facilities for testing high
temperature mechanical properties of materials, such as
Gleeble type equipments for dynamic thermal–mech-
anical testing of materials and physical simulation of
processes, the specimen grips are often made of tungsten
or tungsten alloy. If the high temperature strength of the
specimen is high, (that is higher than the tungsten grips),
the grips sometimes deform or can even be damaged due
to their relative lower high temperature strength [6]. The
grips cannot be made of ceramics because the grips must
possess good electronic conduction when the specimen is
heated by electric resistance heating. Furthermore, the
grips also should have good thermal conduction so as to
withstand the thermal shock during the frequently fast
heating orcooling processes. Therefore, it is necessaryto
develop a new high temperature grip material possessing
good electric conduction and good thermal shock
resistance.
Tungsten has received attention with a view of im-
proving the high temperature mechanical properties. As
dispersion strengtheners, refractory carbide, nitride and
oxide phases, such as TiC, ZrC, HfC, TiN, La

To date, unfortunately, research on tungsten matrix

phase has been rarely reported [5,16]. Titanium carbide,
with avery high melting temperature,/C243067 /C176C [17],has
excellent properties, such as high hardness, good high-

Additionally, no detrimental new phase will be formed
except that W diﬀuses into the TiC lattice to form
(Ti,W)C solid solution according to the TiC–W binary
alloy diagram [18]. Recently, TiC-dispersed Mo alloy
has exhibited a high toughness and good strength [19–
21]. ZrC-dispersed Mo alloy also shows much higher
strength than a recrystallized pure Mo at room tem-
perature and a large elongation of 180% at 1970 K [22].
In our previous study, high content ZrC particles (20
vol.%) were added to tungsten matrix to improve the
high temperature strength [16]. The crystal structure and
physical properties of TiC and ZrC are similar. In the
some cases, molybdenum is similar to tungsten on
physical and chemical properties. Thus, TiC is believed
to be a good reinforcement for tungsten.

new grips and moulds for high temperature creep test-

2. Experimental procedures
Tungsten powder (purity > 99 wt.%, Xiamen Jinlu
Tungsten Corp.) with an average size of3.5lm was used
to produce tungsten matrix composites. TiC particle
(purity > 98 wt.%, Central South University of Science
and Technology) with an average size of 2.5 lm was
employed as the reinforcement. W powders and TiC
particles (0, 10, 20, 30, 40 vol.%) were wet ball-milled in
ethyl alcohol for 24 h. The ball-milled powders were
dried in an oven at 70/C176C for 20 h and then 100/C176C for 10
h. After being dried, the mixtures were compacted in a
steel mold under 200 MPa at room temperature, and
then hot pressed in a graphite mold under 20 MPa at
2000 /C176C, under a vacuum of 1:3 /C210
/C03 Pa for 1 h.
The sintered billets were cut by electrical discharge
wire machining and polished with diamond disks to
prepare the specimens for testing. Specimens with
dimensions of 2 :5 /C24 /C230 mm were used for ﬂex-
ural strength measurement by the three-point bend-
ing method, with a 20 mm span at a 0.5mmmin
/C01
crosshead speed using an Instron universal testing
machine. The tensile surfaces of the specimens were
polished using diamond pastes and the edges were
chamfered. Room temperature measurement was con-
ducted in air, and elevated temperature measurement
was carried out in a ﬂowing argon atmosphere in an
electronic furnace. Fracture toughness was evaluated by
the single-edge notched beam test with a 16 mm span
and a cross head speed of 0.05mmmin
/C01 using test bars
of 2/C24 /C220 mm3, and a half-thickness notch was made
using a diamond wafering blade. Due to the brittle

bars were fabricated with the tensile surface perpendic-
ular to the hot-pressing direction. All the strength and
fracture toughness were calculated from the values of six
to eight specimens under each test condition. Elastic
modulus at room temperature was obtained by mea-
suring the stress–strain curves in the three-point bending
tests. Hardness was measured with a Vickers hardness
tester under a constant load of 196 N for 15s.
Both the polished surfaces and the fracture surfaces

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructures
The relative density values of the hot pressed TiC
p/W
composites were measured using a metallographic
method by measuring the pore size and content with a
software of Matrox Inspector 2.1 version. The relative
densities are shown in Fig. 1. It can be seen that the
presence of TiC particles slightly increases the relative
density, and the eﬀect is more pronounced with in-
creasing TiC particle content from 0 to 20 vol.%. But the
relative density decreases a little with further increasing
TiC content, and reaches 95.5% for the composite with
40 vol.% TiC.
It is diﬃcult to obtain a fully dense tungsten material
by hot pressing at 2000/C176C without any sintering aids or
sintering activators such as Pt, Ni, Pd, etc. [24]. The
eutectic temperatureof W–TiCis /C252750 /C176C according to
the W–TiC phase diagram, much lower than the melting
point of 3410/C176C for monolithic W [25]. Therefore, TiC
p/
W interface sintering should be much faster than that of
W grains at 2000/C176C. But as TiC content reached above
30 vol.%, the relative density decreased slightly. As the
TiC content increases, uniform dispersion of TiC par-
ticles in the W matrix becomes diﬃcult, and aggregation
of TiC particles at W grain boundaries occurs. There-
fore some of TiC particles cannot be in contact with the
W particles. Additionally, the sintering of TiC aggre-
gation is not easy at 2000/C176C due to its chemical bond
characteristics. Hence the densiﬁcation rate of TiC
p/W
composites with a high TiC content is decreased which
results in a lower relative density.

W interface; penetration of W into TiC at the sintering
temperature led to the formation of a solid solution

The ﬂexural strength of the composite tested at room

containing 30 vol.% TiC particles possessed the highest

/C176C. On the contrary, the strength of monolithic W al-

the application of the composites as structural materials
in high temperature environments.

4. Conclusion
TiC
p/W composites were prepared by vacuum hot
pressing at 2000/C176C under 20 MPa. The addition of TiC
particles to tungsten was found to have beneﬁcial eﬀects
on elastic modulus, Vickers hardness, fracture toughness
and ﬂexural strength of tungsten. Both the elastic
modulus and hardness of the composites are increased
with increasing TiCcontent from 0to 40vol.%.Atroom
temperature, ﬂexural strength in excess of 843 MPa and
fracture toughness in excess of 10.1 MPam
1=2 were
measured in the samples with 20 vol.% TiC particles,
Slight reductions in strength and fracture toughness
above 20 vol.% TiC particles are attributed to the in-
creased level of porosity. TiC particles inhibited W grain
growth during the sintering process. Fine W grains are
helpful to improve the strength and toughness of the
TiC
p/W composites. As the temperature rose, the ﬂex-
ural strength of the TiCp/W composites increased and
then decreased, whereas the strength of monolithic W
always decreased. The highest strength of 1155 MPa was
exhibited at 1000 /C176C for the composite with 30 vol.%
TiC particles.
With increasing TiC content, the mean thermal ex-
pansion coeﬃcient of TiC
p/W composites increased, and
thermal diﬀusivity and thermal conductivity decreased.
The mean thermal expansion coeﬃcient of the TiCp/W
composites exhibits a rise with temperature. As the
temperature rises from 30 to 800/C176C, the thermal con-
ductivities of the TiC
p/W composites slightly increased,
conversely, the conductivity of monolithic W decreased
rapidly.
The TiC
p/W composites possess excellent high tem-
perature strength and good thermophysical properties,
and they have been successfully used to prepare high
temperature grips and moulds.
