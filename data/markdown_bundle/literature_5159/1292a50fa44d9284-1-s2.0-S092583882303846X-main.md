ABSTRACT 
The metastable dual phase FeMnCoCr high entropy alloy (HEA) has attracted great attention since it was first 
proposed, as it can overcome the strength-ductility trade-off at room temperature. For materials in engineering 
applications, it is also important to achieve high corrosion resistance while maintaining good mechanical 
properties. In this study, we simultaneously improved the mechanical properties and corrosion resistance of a 
FeMnCoCr-based HEA via Cu alloying and by applying a simple processing route that combined cold rolling and 
partial recrystallization annealing. The alloy doped with 0.3 at.% Cu and processed by annealing at 650 
◦
C for 3 
min exhibited the best combination of strength and ductility, i.e., yield strength of 768 MPa, tensile strength of 
960 MPa and uniform elongation of 33.6%. The high yield strength majorly originated from high density dis -
locations and grain boundaries in the material, while the formation of deformation twins, martensite laths, 
stacking faults and Lomer Cottrell locks contributed to the continuous strain hardening and good ductility. The 
addition of Cu also enhanced corrosion resistance of the HEA in a NaCl solution by improving stability of the 
alloy matrix and elevating compactness of the passive film. The solid solution of Cu elevated equilibrium po -
tential of the matrix and promotes transformation of microstructure from the face-centered cubic (fcc) plus 
hexagonal close-packed (hcp) dual phases to the fcc single phase, effectively inhibiting the accelerated corrosion 
of micro galvanic couple between different phases and enhancing stability of the matrix. At the same time, the 
enrichment of CuO in the inner layer of the passive film reduced the defect density within the Fe-rich oxide layer, 
improving the protection of the passive film against the matrix. 
1. Introduction 
High entropy alloys (HEAs), also known as multi-principal elemental 
alloys (MPEAs) and compositionally complex alloys (CCAs) [1 – 4] , have 
attracted great attention in the field of materials science owing to their 
unique design concept and many excellent properties. For HEAs to be 
used as structural materials, the properties that have been focused 
mainly cover the following aspects: (i) strength and ductility at room 
temperature as well as deformability at high and cryogenic temperatures 
[5 – 7] , (ii) resistance to fatigue and wear [8 – 10] , and (iii) corrosion and 
radiation resistance [11 – 14] . The pioneering HEA, i.e., the equiatomic 
FeMnCoCrNi alloy (also known as Cantor alloy), is a stable solid solution 
with the fcc structure at room temperature [2] . However, yield strength 
of materials in which fcc is the dominating structure is generally low, 
which cannot meet the demand for high-performance structural mate -
rials in practical applications. For example, yield strength of the Cantor 
alloy with an average grain size of 4.4 µ m at room temperature is only 
355 MPa [15] . In 2012, Wang et al. [16] designed the Al

while deformation twins, deformation induced martensitic trans -

deformation mechanism in the alloy changes from martensitic trans -
formation to martensitic transformation plus mechanical twinning with 
increasing Cu alloying. The addition of Cu also enhances corrosion 
resistance of the alloy in a NaCl solution by improving both the stability 
of the alloy matrix and the compactness of the passive film. This study on 
Cu alloying in the FeMnCoCr-based HEAs has advanced the under -
standing of the effect of Cu on microstructures, tensile mechanical 
properties and corrosion resistance of alloys, which may pave a general 
pathway for developing a wide range of advanced alloys with low costs 
for practical applications.

For the Cu0.3 material after tensile deformation, in addition to the 
significant activation of martensite and twinning, defects such as SFs 
and L-C locks are also observed ( Fig. 5 b). The extensive twins, 
martensite laths and SFs intersect with each other, dividing one single 
crystal into many nano-scaled regions during tensile deformation. This 
leads to the dynamic refinement of microstructures and enhances strain 
hardening rates while increasing the flow stress [57,58] . In analogy to 
other interfaces, the SFs also act as obstacles to dislocation slip, thereby 
enhancing the strength of materials [59,60] . For the fcc materials with 
low SFE ( < 15 mJ ⋅ m

), one dislocation tends to be decomposed into 
two partial dislocations and a SF between them [61] . When two dislo -

plete dislocation (known as a L-C lock), which hinders the slip of other 
dislocations [62] . One L-C lock could generate and connect more fixed 
SFs, forming a "stacking fault network" and resulting in the higher 
structural stability [60,63] . In addition, L-C locks also serve as sources of 
Frank dislocations and continuously promote dislocation slip, resulting 
in the higher strain hardening [63] . This continuous hardening effect 
enables the Cu0.3 alloy own an ultimate tensile strength of 960 MPa and 
a uniform elongation of 33.6%.

the Cu-doped alloys, can be attributed to the fact that Cu enhances the 
stability of austenite, resulting in the lower content of thermal 
martensite prior to deformation. In addition, at the onset of plastic 
deformation the transformation from austenite to ε -martensite is 
delayed [64] , resulting in the lower work hardening rates during stage I.

4.3. Origin of high corrosion resistance in the Cu doped HEA 
The above electrochemical results prove that the addition of Cu 
effectively improves the corrosion resistance of FeMnCoCr HEA. All 
three Cu-containing alloys present passivation state under anodic po -
larization conditions compared to the activated dissolution of the Cu- 
free alloy. Moreover, with the increase of Cu content, the parameters 
including the OCP, the pitting potential of polarization curves and the 
low-frequency impedance modulus of EIS show a gradually increasing 
trend, while the passive current density of polarization curves and the 
carrier concentration of the passive film obtained from Mott-Schottky 
curves show a gradually decrease trend. 
The enhancement in corrosion resistance by Cu addition can be 
attributed to the following aspects: first, the addition of Cu affects the 
corrosion resistance of alloy matrix. As the substitutional solid solution 
atoms, Cu atoms with high stability replace the positions of active Fe 
atoms in the lattice. The doping of Cu increases the equilibrium potential 
of the matrix (from 0.47 V
SCE 
for Cu-free alloy to 0.38 V
SCE 
for Cu1 
alloy, as shown in Fig. 6 ), effectively improving the corrosion resistance 
of the matrix. It is important to emphasize here that the solid solution of 
Cu has the potential to impact the inherent self-potential of alloy matrix. 
Furthermore, the involvement of Cu in the formation of the passivation 
film can also have an impact on the surface potential following passiv -
ation. These two factors act jointly. Meanwhile, as an austenitic stabi -
lizing element, Cu addition results in Cu1 alloy with a single-phase 
austenite microstructure. Compared to other three alloys which have fcc 
plus hcp dual phases, the single-phase Cu1 alloy exhibits the better 
corrosion resistance due to the suppression of micro galvanic corrosion 
between phases. Secondly, Cu addition affects the protective perfor -
mance of passive film. Based on the Mott-Schottky curves and XPS re -
sults, it can be concluded that the passive film on the alloy surface can be 
divided into two layers. The outer layer is a p-type semiconductor, with 
a high content of Cr element, mainly consisting of Cr
2

and Cr(OH)

The inner layer is an n-type semiconductor mainly consisting of FeO and 
Fe

. The outer layer of Cr-rich oxide is more compact and exhibits the 
stronger pitting resistance, while the inner layer of Fe-rich oxide has a 
high defect density and the poor protective performance. Once the outer 
layer is damaged locally, aggressive ions such as chloride ions can easily 
penetrate into the matrix surface and initiate pitting corrosion. The role 
of Cu is precisely to improve its pitting corrosion resistance by 
increasing the compactness of the inner layer in passive film. According 
to the XPS results, Cu exists as the form of CuO in the inner layer of the 
passive film which effectively fills the defects in the Fe-rich oxide inner 
layer. This leads to a significant reduction in the carrier defect concen -
tration of the passive film and improves its protective performance. In 
summary, the addition of a small amount of solid solution Cu to the 
FeMnCoCr HEA effectively improves the corrosion resistance of the alloy 
in a 3.5 wt.% NaCl solution, through enhancing both stability of the 
alloy matrix and compactness of the surface passive film. Therefore, the 
designed FeMnCoCr HEA with solid solution Cu can aid in development 
of advanced alloys with the required strength, ductility and corrosion 
resistance. 
5. Conclusion 
In this study, the FeMnCoCr HEA was doped with a small amount of 
Cu, and a simple processing route that combines cold rolling and partial 
recrystallization annealing was employed to obtain materials with 
excellent combination of strength, tensile plasticity and corrosion 
resistance. The main conclusions obtained are as follows: 
1) The Cu0.3 alloy annealed at 650
◦
C for 3 min exhibits the best 
comprehensive mechanical properties, i.e., yield strength of 
768 MPa, tensile strength of 960 MPa and uniform elongation of 
33.6%. The high yield strength is mainly attributed to dislocation 
strengthening and grain boundary strengthening. The formation of 
deformation twins, ε -martensite, SFs and L-C locks collectively pro -
vide the alloy with continuous strain hardening. 
2) The SFE of the Cu1 alloy is 9.9 mJ ⋅ m
 2 
which is~3.4 mJ ⋅ m
 2 
higher 
than that of the Cu0 alloy. This causes a shift in the deformation 
mechanism from martensitic transformation to a combination of 
martensitic transformation and mechanical twinning. Consequently, 
uniform elongation increases from 33.6% of the Cu0.3 alloy to 37.7% 
of the Cu0.5 alloy, and then decreases to 31.1% of the Cu1 alloy. 
3) The addition of Cu significantly improves the corrosion resistance of 
the FeMnCoCr-based HEA in a 3.5 wt.% NaCl solution, which is 
manifested by the increased OCP and pitting potential as well as the 
decreased passivation current density and carrier concentration of 
the passive film. The solid solution Cu in the alloy raises the equi -
librium potential of the matrix, facilitating the transformation of the 
fcc plus hcp dual phases in the Cu0 alloy into the fcc single phase in 
the Cu1 alloy. This transformation prevents the accelerated corrosion 
due to micro galvanic couple between different phases. Moreover, 
the enrichment of CuO in the inner layer of the Fe-rich oxide within 
the passive film effectively reduces the defect density of the film, thus 
forming the more protective passive film. These synthetical effects of 
Cu on both the matrix and the passive film leads to a significant 
enhancement in the corrosion resistance for the HEA.
