abstract
The microstructure and compressive properties of AlCoCrCuFeNi high-entropy alloy aged at tempera-
tures ranging from 500 to 1000 /C14 C were investigated. The BCC and FCC phase structures remain
unchanged after aging the AlCoCrCuFeNi alloy at temperatures below 645 /C14 C. Aging the alloy at elevated
temperature causes the structure gradually to transform from stabilized BCC to FCC. Also, as the aging
temperature increases, the yield strength of the material decreases but plastic strain increases. When the
alloy was aged at 1000
/C14 C, the plastic strain even reaches 27%.

1. Introduction
High-entropy alloys (HEA) are composed of n major alloy
elements with n /C21 5 in equi-molar or near equi-molar ration which
much easily yield the formation of simple FCC or BCC solid solution
phase, nano-structure even amorphous in as-cast state [1,2].
Therefore, HEA have been referred to as the solid solution alloy
with higher strength, good thermal stability, and larger work-
hardening capacity, in combination with superior environmental
resistance [3,4]. Among the HEA investigated, AlCoCrCuFeNi is an
interesting system which has a wide range of microstructure and
properties [5,6], and the constituent addition or substitution on
microstructure and properties at room or elevated temperature
have been studied [3,7,8]. In this work, the microstructure and
compressive properties of AlCoCrCuFeNi after aging at different
temperatures are investigated. The phase transformation and the
relation between structure and properties are also discussed.
2. Experimental procedures
Alloy ingots with nominal composition of AlCoCrCuFeNi were
prepared by arc melting mixture of commercial-pure metals (purity
better than 99 wt.%) in a Ti-gettered high-purity argon atmosphere.
The ingots were then remelted at least three times to improve
homogeneity under high vacuum. After that, the ingots were
suction cast into a water-cooled copper mould to obtain cylindrical
rods of
43 /C2 70 mm.
The structures of as-cast sample and samples after heat-
treatment at temperatures range from 500 /C14 C to 1000 /C14 C for 5 h
were characterized by X-ray diffraction (XRD) using a Philips X’Pert
MPD. The morphology of alloys was examined with scanning
electron microscope (SEM, JEOL JSM-6360LV) equipped with
energy dispersive spectrometry (EDS). Compressive tests were
carried out on sample of
43 /C2 6 mm with INSTRON 5567 testing
machine at room temperature with a strain rate of 1 /C2 10/C0 4 s/C0 1.
3. Results and discussion
Fig. 1 shows the XRD patterns of AlCoCrCuFeNi alloy before and
after aging treatment for 5 h. The crystal structure of AlCoCrCuFeNi
alloy is identiﬁed to be simple solid-solution based on BCC as main
phase and some FCC structure, and the number of phase is not
affected by aging treatment. The BCC and FCC structures remain
unchanged for the aged AlCoCrCuFeNi alloy below 645
/C14 C.
However, with the increase of temperature above 645 /C14 C, the FCC
peak strength gradually approaches even exceeds the BCC at
2q ¼ 42–48/C14 which indicates that the volume fraction of FCC
increases while BCC decrease. Thus, the elevated aging temperature
makes the alloy gradually transform from stabilized BCC into FCC
and the initial phase transformation is about 645 /C14 C.
Fig. 2 shows the SEM microstructures of the AlCoCrCuFeNi alloys
aged for 5 h at different temperatures. Typical dendrite and inter-
dendrite structures are observed in the alloys aged at 600 /C14 C, which

4. Conclusions
The structure of AlCoCrCuFeNi alloys aged below 645 /C14 Cw a s
found to consist of mainly BCC phase with minor FCC phase.
However, the structure changes from a stabilized BCC to FCC when
the temperature is over 645 /C14 C. The mechanical properties of this
alloy have a strong correlation with the aging temperature. For the
alloys aged below 645 /C14 C, the yield strength and compressive
strength are about 1750 and 2100 MPa, respectively. Above 645 /C14 C,
the yield strength decreases but plastic strain increases with an
increasing temperature. For the AlCoCrCuFeNi alloy aged at
10 0 0/C14 C, the plastic strain is as high as 27%.
