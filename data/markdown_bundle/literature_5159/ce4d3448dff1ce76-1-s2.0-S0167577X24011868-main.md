1. Introduction
Thermal expansion, characterized by the coefficient of thermal 
expansion (CTE), is a common phenomenon in metals and alloys. At 
elevated temperatures, thermal expansion becomes more pronounced, 
leading to thermal stress and dimensional changes in assemblies. 
Consequently, achieving a low CTE is of paramount importance in in -
dustries such as aerospace and nuclear sectors [1,2] .
Traditional low thermal expansion superalloys are Fe-based super -
alloys, such as Incoloy 903 and 907. They exhibit low CTE below the 
Curie temperature (400 – 500

In recent years, machine learning has garnered significant attention 
in the field of materials science [4] . Numerous researchers have suc -
cessfully employed machine learning techniques in the study of 
superalloys [5 – 8] . However, there have been limited efforts to employ 
machine learning for the discovery of superalloys with low thermal 
expansion.
Hence, this study employs machine learning and experimental 
techniques to discover and fabricate novel Ni-based superalloys with 
low CTE at high temperatures. The primary advantage is accelerating 
the development of low-CTE superalloys, eliminating the traditional 
time-consuming and trial-and-error methods.
2. Methods
Ni-based superalloys data was gathered from the Superalloys 
Handbook [9] , MatWeb, and CINDAS, including 137 alloys and 1159 
CTE data points at different temperatures. In this work, the focus was on 
the effect of chemical composition on CTE, rather than the specific alloy 
manufacturing processes. This choice was justified by the fact that the 
CTE is predominantly influenced by composition and to a lesser extent 
by processing variables [8] .

An extensive composition space of Ni-based superalloys was built to 
explore low-CTE Ni-based superalloys. A pool of candidates with 
possible low thermal expansion were generated by the genetic algorithm 
(GA) in which the model was used as fitness function. Several selected 
candidates were prepared using a vacuum arc furnace (VAR), and CTE 
was measured using DIL 402 EXpedis.

4. Conclusion
In this study, we successfully developed an SVR model capable of 
predicting the CTE in Ni-based superalloys. Utilizing GA and the model, 
we identified and experimentally validated several promising candi -
dates with low CTE. The final alloy in Ni-Mo-W system demonstrates 
both low thermal expansion coefficient and superior mechanical prop -
erties at elevated temperatures, which has substantial potential for 
diverse applications.
