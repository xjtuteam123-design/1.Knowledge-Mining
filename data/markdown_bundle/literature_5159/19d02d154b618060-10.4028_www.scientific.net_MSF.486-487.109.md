Increased High Temperature Oxidation Resistance of 
Ni20Cr20Fe5Nb1Y 2O3 Alloy with Nano-Sized Grains 
 
Il-Ho Kim a and S. I. Kwun b 
Division of Materials Science and Engineering, Korea University, Seoul 136-701, Korea 
agsystem@hanmail.net, bsookkwun@korea.ac.kr 
Keywords: nano-sized grain, oxidation resistance, mechanical alloying, tensile strength 
Abstract. The oxidation and tensile properties of a Ni20Cr20Fe5Nb alloy and a Ni20Cr20Fe 
5Nb1Y 2O3 alloy with nano-sized grains were compared with those of the comercial IN718 alloy. The 
oxidation resistance of the Ni20Cr20Fe5Nb1Y 2O3 alloy was superior to that of the Ni20Cr20Fe5Nb 
and IN 718 alloys. This superior oxidation resistance was the result of both the formation of dense 
oxides on the surface of the alloy and the interruption of Cr migration in the alloy by the addition of 
Y2O3. Moreover, the tensile property of the Ni20Cr20Fe5Nb1Y 2O3 alloy at room temperature and 
400 oC was higher than that of the Ni20Cr20Fe5Nb and IN718 alloys by more than 300MPa (30%). 
This result can be attributed to the dispersion strengthening of Y 2O3. The relatively low tensile 
strength at 600 °C and 800 °C of the alloys fabricated by mechanical alloying was attribute d to grain 
refinement showing intergranular fracture at high temperatures. 
Introduction 
The IN 718 alloy has been used in high temperature structural applications such as parts of industrial 
gas turbines and the reactors in nuclear power plants[1]. In order to i ncrease the efficiency of their 
applications, it is essential to improve the oxidation resistance and strength. Therefore, many studies 
have been performed to improve the strength of IN 718 alloy such as therm omechanical treatments 
and direct aging[2,3]. However, the way to improve the oxidation resista nce of an IN 718 alloy has 
not been reported. It is well known that the alloys with dispersoids fabricated by mechanical alloying 
have a fine grain size, which increases not only the tensile stre ngth but also the high temperature 
oxidation resistance[4,5,6]. Therefore, if an alloy with a similar chemical composition to IN 718 alloy 
can be fabricated by a mechanical alloying method, it is expected that the alloy will have both 
excellent oxidation resistance and superior strength. In this study, a Ni20Cr20Fe5Nb1Y 2O3 alloy that 
has the added elements of Y 2O3 and Nb to NiCrFe, which is the main element of the IN 718 alloy, was 
fabricated using a mechanical alloying method. Its oxidation resistance and tensile property at room 
and high temperature were compared with those of commercial IN718 al loy and Ni20Cr20Fe5Nb 
alloy to evaluate the effects of addition of Y 2O3 and grain refinement on the oxidation resistance and 
mechanical properties. 
Experimental procedure 
The Ni20Cr20Fe5Nb1Y 2O3 and Ni20Cr20Fe5Nb alloys were fabricated by a mechanical alloying 
method and were consolidated by the SPS(spark plasma sintering) method. The nominal and chemical 
compositions of the specimens used in this study are shown in Table 1. The Ni20Cr20Fe5Nb1Y 2O3, 
Ni20Cr20Fe5Nb and commercial IN 718 alloys were oxidized at 1000 ℃ for up to 100 hours. The 
amount of weight gain was measured every 20 hours to compare the degree of oxidation in each alloy. 
In order to identify the type of oxides formed on the surface, XRD(x- ray diffraction) measurements 
were carried out on the oxide layer formed on the alloy surface, which was gradually polished off. The 
tensile tests for each of the three alloys were performed at room temperature, 400 °C, 600 °C and

Results and discussion 
The average grain sizes of the Ni20Cr20Fe5Nb and Ni20Cr20Fe5Nb1Y 2O3 alloy after consolidation 
were 54nm and 128nm, respectively, and that of commercial the IN 718 alloy was 87 µ m, showing 
fine grains in the alloys that were fabricated by mechanical a lloying(Fig. 1(a)(b)(c)). The smaller 
grain size in the alloy with Y 2O3 dispersoids compared with the alloy without Y 2O3 indicate the 
retardation of grain boundary movement by the dispersoids during consolidation, considering that the 
grain sizes of the two alloy powders were similar, 20nm and 22nm, res pectively. Fig.2 shows the 
results of the oxidation test of the three alloys at 1000 °C. The figure shows that the alloy with the 
Y2O3 dispersoids showed the best oxidation resistance.

They reported that the interfaces between the dispersoids and the ma trix in the alloy facilitate the 
formation of Cr oxide, which suppresses the successive penetration of ox ygen.
 Moreover, the

the heat treated alloy at room temperature was 1424MPa(Fig. 5), which was approximately 300MPa 
higher than that of the Ni20Cr20Fe5Nb alloy at room temperature. This suggests that the predominant 
strengthening mechanism in the Ni20Cr20Fe5Nb1Y 2O3 alloy is dispersion hardening rather than 
grain refinement strengthening. A simillar result has been observe d in Fe-Al+Y 2O3 alloy 
manufactured by the mechanical alloying method[8]. The Ni20Cr20Fe5Nb1Y 2O3 alloy showed a

Summary 
The grain sizes of the Ni20Cr20Fe5Nb and Ni20Cr20Fe5Nb1Y 2O3 alloys fabricated by mechanical 
alloying followed by consolidation by SPS were found to be 128nm and 54nm, respec tively. The 
smaller grain size of the alloy with Y 2O3 resulted from the addition of the Y 2O3 dispersoids 
suppressing grain growth during consolidation. The oxidation resistance of the Ni20Cr20Fe 
5Nb1Y 2O3 alloy with the dispersoids was superior to the Ni20Cr20Fe5Nb and IN 718 a lloys. The 
oxides formed on the alloy surface were Cr 2O3 and NiCr 2O4, and it was found that the NiCr 2O4 was 
formed on the upper surface of the oxide layer. The tensile strength of the Ni20Cr20Fe5Nb1Y 2O3 
alloy at room temperature and 400 °C was superior to that of the Ni20Cr20Fe5Nb and IN 718 alloys 
due to Y2O3 dispersoids. However, the strength of the alloy with small grains above 600  was lower 
than the IN 718 alloy with the larger grains. This appears to be due to a change in the deformation 
mechanism with temperatures in the alloys with nano sized grains fabricated by mechanical alloying.
