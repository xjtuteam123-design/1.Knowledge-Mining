ORIGINAL RESEARCH ARTICLE
Microstructure and High-Temperature Mechanical
Properties of TiAl Matrix Composites Reinforced
with Different Types of High-Entropy Alloy Particles
Na Wu, Xiaolei Song, Yuechen Cai, Xiaokai Li, Hongyang Yang, Jing Chen, Zhenxin Duan, and Huiliang Shao
Submitted: 16 May 2025 / Revised: 24 July 2025 / Accepted: 7 August 2025
TiAl alloys are extensively utilized in aerospace and automotive applications, attributed to their superior
properties. Nevertheless, attaining an optimal balance between strength and plasticity at elevated tem-
peratures remains a signiﬁcant challenge. This research successfully developed TiAl matrix composites
strengthened by high-entropy alloys (HEAs) using powder metallurgy techniques. The present study
probed the effects of particular high-entropy alloys, such as AlMnCrFeNi, CoMnCrFeNi, and Co-
MoCrFeNi, on the microstructural traits and high-temperature mechanical performance of composite
materials. The ﬁndings indicated that incorporating HEA particles substantially boosts the sintering pro-
cess of TiAl alloys, which in turn aids in creating dense composites. All three types of HEA particles were
evenly dispersed within the TiAl matrix and exhibited excellent interfacial bonding with the matrix. No-
tably, numerous ﬁne AlMnCrFeNi and CoMnCrFeNi particles generated in their corresponding compos-
ites. Moreover, the high-temperature tensile strength and ductility of all composites were improved relative
to the pure TiAl alloy, due to fewer defects, ﬁner grains, and synergistic deformation. Among these, the TiAl
composites reinforced with CoMoCrFeNi exhibited the most pronounced enhancement in mechanical
performance.
Keywords composites, high entropy alloys, high-temperature
mechanical properties, microstructure, TiAl alloys
1. Introduction
TiAl alloys combine the high strength and corrosion
resistance of titanium with the lightweight characteristics and
improved ductility of aluminum (Ref 1-3). This unique
combination makes them highly appealing for use in the
aerospace and automotive industries (Ref 4-8). These alloys
ﬁnd extensive application in critical components, including
low-pressure turbine blades in aeroengines (e.g., Boeing) and
turbocharger blades in automotive systems (e.g., Mitsubishi)
(Ref 9, 10), where their high strength-to-weight ratio and
elevated-temperature performance are crucial. Despite their
advantages, a key challenge remains in optimizing the balance
between high-temperature strength and plasticity (Ref 11-13),
which limits their broader application.
To address this limitation, TiAl-based composites reinforced
with secondary phases have been extensively explored (Ref 14-
17). Compared to monolithic TiAl alloys, such composites
exhibit improved strength, toughness, and thermal stability (Ref
18-21). Among various reinforcement approaches, including
ﬁber, lamellar, and particulate methods (Ref 22-25), particulate
reinforcement is particularly advantageous due to its processing
simplicity, microstructural uniformity, and isotropic properties
(Ref 26, 27). Particle reinforcements are typically classiﬁed into
ceramic and metallic types. Ceramic particles (e.g., TiB
2,
Al2O3) enhance high-temperature strength by inhibiting grain
boundary migration and dislocation movement (Ref 28-30).
However, their brittleness, processing challenges, and thermal
expansion mismatch with the TiAl matrix often degrade
ductility, offsetting their strengthening beneﬁts (Ref 31, 32).
In contrast, metallic particles (e.g., Nb, Mo) exhibit better
thermal compatibility with TiAl, promoting stronger interfacial
bonding and improved resistance to crack propagation (Ref 33-
35). Consequently, metallic reinforcements are more effective
in promoting a synergistic enhancement of strength and
plasticity at high temperatures.
Recent studies highlight high-entropy alloys (HEAs) as
promising reinforcements due to their exceptional mechanical
properties, including high strength, wear resistance, and
thermal stability (Ref 36-38). For instance, HEAs-reinforced
composites based on Cu (Ref 39), Ti6Al4V (Ref 40), and Mg
(Ref 41) exhibit superior hardness and ductility compared to
their unreinforced counterparts. Moreover, the application of
high-entropy alloys for enhancing TiAl-based composites has

This study investigates the effects of three high-entropy

powder metallurgy with a ﬁxed HEAs content of 4 wt%, and
their mechanical behavior was systematically evaluated to
identify the most effective reinforcement strategy.
2. Experimental Procedure
2.1 Raw Materials
The Ti-48Al-2Cr-2Nb pre-alloy powders (named Ti4822),
featuring a particle size of 45-150 lm and a purity of more than
99.9 wt%, was furnished by Xi’an Sailong Metal Materials Co.,
Ltd. Moreover, the HEA powders, such as AlMnCrFeNi,
CoMnCrFeNi, and CoMoCrFeNi, with a particle size range of
15-53 lm and a purity greater than 99.9 wt%, were provided by
Beijing Y anbang New Materials Technology Co., Ltd.
2.2 Sample Preparation
The Ti4822 and HEA powders were blended in a ball mill
(operated at 200 r/min for 1 hour with a ball-to-powder ratio of
10:1; the mill and balls were made of GCr15 steel) at a mass
ratio of 96:4. Subsequently, the blended powders were placed
into a graphite mold (40 mm in height and 20 mm in internal
diameter) and introduced into a vacuum hot-pressing sintering
furnace (model ZT-40-21Y , Chenhua, China). Figure 1(a)
illustrates the operational parameters for the sintering process.
The procedure was implemented at 1250 /C176 C, under a pressure
of 40 MPa, and held for 1 hour. The sintered samples were then
cooled to room temperature inside the furnace and subsequently
demolded. Figure 1(b) presents the optical photograph of the
samples obtained. They showed a cylindrical shape, a dense
structure and a metallic luster when observed macroscopically.
In the subsequent discussion, the TiAl matrix composites
reinforced with various HEAs were designated as AlMn-HEA/
Ti4822, CoMn-HEA/Ti4822, and CoMo-HEA/Ti4822, respec-
tively.
2.3 High-Temperature Tensile Testing
Wire electrical discharge machining was used to cut the
sintered samples into tensile specimens sized 10 mm 9 2
mm 9 2 mm. The specimens were then polished with 1200-grit
SiC abrasive paper to ensure surface ﬂatness prior to testing.
High-temperature tensile tests were carried out on a WDW-200
testing apparatus, with the temperature strictly maintained at
850 /C176 C in an air environment and a strain rate of 1 9 10
/C0 4 s/C0 1.
The overall elongations were calculated by comparing the
sample lengths before and after testing. The results reported
here represent the average of at least three tests conducted
under each condition.

spectrometer (EDS) coupled with the SEM. The relative

3. Results and Discussion
3.1 Raw Material Powders
Figure 2 illustrates the XRD patterns of the raw powders.
Figure 2(a) shows that the Ti4822 powder is largely composed
of the a2-Ti3Al and c-TiAl phases, with the a2-Ti3Al phase as
the primary component. Figure 2(b) demonstrates that the
AlMn-HEA powder exhibits a BCC solid solution structure. In
contrast, CoMn-HEA powder presents an FCC solid solution
structure as displayed in Fig. 2(c). The pattern in Fig. 2(d)
shows that the high degree of disorder results in the
microstructure of HEAs forming a simple face-centered cubic
(FCC) structure (PDF#47-1477) along with small amounts of
tetragonal crystal system r (PDF#09-0050) and r’ phases
(PDF#09-0052) and triclinic system l precipitation phases
(PDF#29-0489) (Ref 44-46).
Figure 3 presents the morphology and particle size distri-
bution of Ti4822 powder and three HEA powders. The Ti4822
powder shown in Fig. 3(a) exhibits an excellent spherical shape
and a dendritic surface texture, with no satellite particles
observed. As seen in Fig. 3(b-h), the particle size distribution
range of Ti4822 is 10-150 lm, with an average particle size of
73.47 lm. The particle size distribution ranges of the three
high-entropy alloy (HEA) powders are all within 15-53 lm,
with average particle sizes of 40.12 lm, 31.37 lm, and 34.78
lm, respectively. As shown in Fig. 3(c), the AlMn-HEA
powder exhibits relatively poor spheroidization, with rough
surfaces and numerous satellite particles adhering to the
surface. The CoMn-HEA powder (Fig. 3e) displays a mor-
phology similar to that of AlMn-HEA, but with smaller particle
size. In Fig. 3(g), the CoMo-HEA powder shows good
spheroidization and a smooth surface; however, it also contains
some irregular satellite particles, which may be attributed to its
high melting point and phase separation induced by rapid
cooling (Ref 40).

previous two composites, no ﬁne CoMo-HEA particles are

4. Conclusions
In this study, vacuum hot pressing was employed to prepare
TiAl matrix composites reinforced with AlMnCrFeNi,
CoMnCrFeNi, and CoMoCrFeNi high-entropy alloys. The
inﬂuences of different HEA types on the microstructural
features and mechanical behaviors at high temperatures for
the composites were investigated. The primary ﬁndings can be
summarized as follows:
(1) The addition of HEA particles facilitated the formation
of dense TiAl matrix composites. The three types of
HEA particles were evenly dispersed within the TiAl
matrix. A notable difference was that numerous ﬁne
AlMnCrFeNi and CoMnCrFeNi particles formed in the
AlMn-HEA/Ti4822 and CoMn-HEA/Ti4822 composites.
(2) All three types of HEA particles exhibited excellent
interfacial bonding with the TiAl matrix. Among them,
the CoMo-HEA/Ti4822 composites exhibited the largest
diffusion layer thickness due to the strong interfacial
reaction between CoMo-HEA powder and Ti4822 pow-
der during the sintering process.
(3) The incorporation of three types of HEA particles en-
hanced the high-temperature tensile strength and ductil-
ity of TiAl alloys by reducing defects, reﬁning grains,
and promoting synergistic deformation behavior. Among
these HEA types, CoMoCrFeNi demonstrated the most
signiﬁcant enhancement effects due to its ability to sup-
press crack propagation within the composites.
