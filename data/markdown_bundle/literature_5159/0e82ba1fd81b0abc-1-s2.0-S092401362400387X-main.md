ABSTRACT
The TiAl alloy is a novel lightweight high-temperature structural material that exhibits exceptional performance.
The brittleness and mechanical properties of the material can be enhanced by improving the microstructure via
rolling. The Ti-48Al-1Fe alloy with high density was produced using powder compaction and pressure-less
sintering. Subsequently, the TiAl alloy sheet was formed via hot pack rolling. This study examined the sheet
formability of PM Ti-48Al-1Fe alloy sheets at various temperatures, as well as the microstructure and mechanical
properties at varied levels of rolling deformations. The microstructure of the powder metallurgy (PM) TiAl alloy
sheet has a unique duplex structure, consisting of α
2
/ γ lamellar colonies and a composite structure. The rolling
deformation process generates spherical recrystallized grains, which effectively reduce stress concentration. The
enhanced composite structure is mostly localized at the interfaces between grains, creating a robust obstacle for
the movement of dislocations at high temperatures. This results in the desired outcome of reinforcing the me-
chanical properties of the material at high temperatures through grain boundary strengthening. This study
demonstrates that the ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of PM TiAl sheet tensile specimens in the rolling direction
at room temperature is 443 MPa with 1 % elongation, whereas at 800
◦
C, the UTS rises to 548 MPa with 2.5 %
elongation. This study proposes a novel process for the efficient production of Ti48Al1Fe sheets with good high-
temperature mechanical properties. This technique entails the hot rolling of high-density sintered powder
metallurgy billets, offering an innovative approach for the economical and swift production of TiAl alloy sheets
during practical manufacturing process.
1. Introduction
TiAl alloys are considered highly favourable for applications in the
aerospace and automotive industries because of their lightweight na-
ture, superior mechanical properties, and exceptional performance at
high temperatures [1,2] . TiAl sheets are suitable for applications in
aerospace vehicles that need high-temperature resistance, lightweight
construction, and thin-walled plate constructions. These applications
include skins, nozzles, and thermal protection systems (TPS) [3,4] .
Nevertheless, the limited ductility and deformability at room tempera-
ture restrict their widespread use [5] . The hot rolling of TiAl presents
greater challenges than hot forging and hot extrusion due to significant
tensile stresses, thermal losses, and heightened sensitivity to strain rate.
So, the manufacturing expense of γ -TiAl sheets is exceedingly elevated.
Hot pack-rolling is widely regarded as the most efficient technique for
producing TiAl alloy sheets. It efficiently mitigates fracture issues
resulting from oxidation and unequal deformation of the billet, without
requiring a heated roller, and may be performed on standard mills. Kim

TiAl based alloys are typically manufactured using two methods:
ingot metallurgical (IM) and powder metallurgical (PM) processes.
Zhang [7] and Appel [8] analyzed the microstructure and properties of
TiAl alloys prepared by ingot metallurgy and powder metallurgy,
respectively. The mechanical properties of cast TiAl alloys are typically
poor due to their coarse microstructure and composition segregation.
Prior to rolling, ingots are typically subjected to various processes in
order to achieve a uniform structure and refined organization. These
processes include multiple smelting, homogenizing heat treatment, and
forging, all of which are complex and expensive. The
Ti48Al2Cr2Nb2.5 C alloy with uniform composition was melted four
times by Fang [9] using vacuum arc melting. Shaaban [10] applies a
homogenizing heat treatment to the material to reduce segregation in
the as-cast microstructure, and Gaitzenauer [11] refines the micro-
structure of Ti-43.4Al-4.2Nb-1.1Mo-0.1B ingots by forging in the (a + β )
phase field region.
TiAl alloys produced through powder metallurgy with an uniform
microstructure and small grains are ideal raw materials for rolling, since
they reduce the occurrence of localized stress concentrations during
subsequent deformation. Zhang prepared a high-performance β -solidi-
fying Ti43A19V0.2Y sheet through powder metallurgy, revealing that
the PM sample contains many grain boundaries, which will significantly
promote the subsequent hot deformation process [12] . However, it is
difficult to achieve high density by pressure-less sintering of pre-alloyed
powder. Therefore, Shagiev [13] prepared high-density Ti47Al3Cr alloy
by hot isostatic pressing. Guo [14] studied the microstructure and
properties of Ti-48Al-2Cr-2Nb-xGd (x = 0, 0.15, 0.3) alloys by spark
plasma sintering. Jia [15] obtained high-density Ti22Al25Nb alloy by
hot pressing sintering.
To enhance the deformability of TiAl alloy during rolling, Zhang
incorporated β -stabilizing elements V [16] to facilitate dislocation slip
and mechanical twin formation, ultimately rolling the Ti-43Al-9V-0.2Y
sheet at 1200 ℃ . Nevertheless, You [17] have indicated that TiAl com-
ponents must operate at elevated temperatures, and an excessive
β -phase content may increase the risk of component softening. Shen [18]
found that high-Nb containing (6 – 10 at%) TiAl alloys exhibit excellent
high-temperature strength and oxidation resistance, but it is difficult to
be fabricated into sheet in comparison with the conventional TiAl alloys.
Shen obtained Tie45Al-8.5Nb-(W,B,Y) plate by rolling at a higher tem-
perature of 1280 ℃ . In recent years, the element Fe has garnered the
interest of researchers in the TiAl alloy sector. The studies by Yang [19]
and Nazarova [20] demonstrated that the incorporation of Fe into TiAl
alloy markedly influences microstructural refinement. Furthermore, this
will result in the creation of many Ti-Fe and Al-Fe metallic linkages,
which may improving the deformability of the TiAl alloy. However,
there is almost no in-depth research on Fe-containing TiAl alloy sheets.
Consequently, the resolution of the paradox between the facile form-
ability of TiAl sheets and their superior mechanical properties at high
temperatures remains elusive. The influence of the element Fe on the
formability of TiAl alloys warrants additional investigation, and the
mechanism of improved mechanical properties at high temperatures
also needs to be further elucidated.
This work presents a TiAl alloy sheet that is readily processable,
exhibits good high-temperature strength, and can be manufactured
swiftly and economically. An alloy with the composition of Ti48Al1Fe
was selected. The technique for fabricating Ti48Al1Fe alloy sheets
economically and expeditiously by powder metallurgy, pressure-less
sintering, and hot rolling holds notable relevance for different compo-
sition of TiAl sheets. The deformability of powder metallurgy Ti48Al1Fe
alloy at various rolling temperatures was examined, and the micro-
structure and mechanical properties under differing deformation situa-
tions were investigated. The microstructural changes and
crystallographic texture analysis of alloys were systematically investi-
gated. Furthermore, the process of composite structure formation and
the characteristics of tensile fracture are carefully investigated and
discussed, demonstrating that the spherical nano- τ
2
particles located at
the grain boundaries can hinder the migration of dislocations during
high-temperature deformation of the material, consequently enhancing
the high-temperature strength.

Fig. 6 a shows red grain boundaries representing LAGBs, and black
grain boundaries representing HAGBs. The percentage of LAGBs in ST is

proportion of LAGBs, accounting for 32.4 %, while the proportion of

The proportion of LAGBs for R3 is 12.1 %, while the proportion of
HAGBs is 87.9 %. As the rolling deformation intensifies, the quantity of
LAGBs experiences a notable increase, followed by a quick drop, and
ultimately a gradual increase. The variation in the quantity of HAGBs is
inversely proportional to the variation in the quantity of LAGBs. The
preceding research has demonstrated a close relationship between the
LAGBs and the dislocation network, twins, substructures, and subgrains.
As the quantity of LAGBs increases, the number of substructures also
increases. DRX and dynamic recovery (DRV) have the ability to include
dislocations or substructures inside the microstructure, resulting in a
large decrease in the quantity of LAGBs [24] .
Most of the grains in the ST samples exhibit a coarse texture and
significant phase disparities, resulting in a substantial number of
HAGBs. Upon increasing the observation magnification, it becomes
evident that a significant quantity of composite structures are localized
at the grain borders, as depicted in Fig. 8 a, and the proportion of HAGBs
amounts to 87.0 %. With an increase in rolling deformation, the initially
large grains undergo additional refinement, leading to an increase in the
number of dislocations and subgrains within the grains. Consequently,
there is a rapid rise in the number of LAGBs. Nevertheless, the amount of
energy generated by little deformation is insufficient to initiate DRX and
DRV. With increasing deformation, there is a greater acquisition of
external energy in R2 and R3. The dominance of DRX and DRV becomes
evident, resulting in a significant reduction in the number of dislocations
and subgrains due to the production of a large number of recrystallized
grains. Consequently, the number of LAGBs is ultimately reduced.

4.2. Mechanical properties and failure microstructure
To investigate the impact of rolling deformation on the characteris-
tics of PM Ti-48Al-1Fe alloy sheets, we conducted tensile tests on TiAl
alloy sheets with rolling deformations of 45 %, 60 %, and 70 % at both
room temperature and high temperature (800 ℃ ). The outcomes of
these tests are displayed in Fig.15 . Previously, the PM Ti48Al1Fe sin-
tered samples exhibited an UTS of 315 MPa at room temperature [27] .
Following the rolling process, the UTS of R1-RD, R2-RD, and R3-RD at
room temperature were measured to be 343 MPa, 413 MPa, and
443 MPa, respectively. Additionally, R3-RD exhibited 1 % elongation
after fracture. It is evident that as deformation increases, the grain size
decreases gradually, leading to a proportional improvement in me-
chanical properties. However, for the TD direction perpendicular to the
rolling direction, the UTS of R1-TD, R2-TD, and R3-TD at room tem-
perature were measured to be 324 MPa, 374 MPa, and 430 MPa,
respectively.
Despite the steady enhancement of mechanical characteristics with
increased deformation, the disparity between the RD and TD directions
is not getting smaller. The disparity in tensile characteristics of the R1
plate between the RD and TD directions is 19, the disparity for the R2
plate is 39, and the disparity for the R1 plate is 13. As deformation
grows, the performance disparity between the RD and TD directions of
the plate initially rises and subsequently declines, potentially linked to
the plate ’ s texture. The crystallographic texture analysis in the preced-
ing chapter indicates that the α
2
phase content in plates R1, R2, and R3
surpasses 80 %, thereby exerting a significant influence on the
anisotropy of the plates. Among these, the (0001) crystal plane exhibits
the highest texture strength; however, the texture strength of R2 in the
TD direction is the lowest when compared to R1 and R3. This elucidates
why the disparity in tensile characteristics between the TD direction and
the RD direction of the R2 plate is the most pronounced. The (001)
crystal plane in the γ phase has the highest texture strength compared to
the other three crystal planes. The R2 plate has the highest strength;
nonetheless, its texture is predominantly concentrated between the RD
and TD directions, in contrast to the R1 plate, whose texture is focused in
the TD and ND directions. Consequently, the γ phase in the R2 plate
exerts little influence on performance in the TD direction; The (100)
crystal plane in the τ
2
phase has the maximum texture strength, com-
parable to that of the γ phase. While the texture strength of the R2 plate
is the strongest, it is predominantly concentrated in the ND direction, in
contrast to the R1 and R3 plates, which exhibit high-strength texture
concentration in the TD direction. Furthermore, the τ
2
phase content
constitutes less than 3 %, indicating that the τ
2
phase exerts minimal
influence on performance in the TD direction.

5. Conclusions
This paper examines the deformability of powder metallurgy
Ti48Al1Fe alloy at various rolling temperatures, and the microstructure
and mechanical properties under differing deformation situations were
investigated. The microstructural changes and crystallographic texture
analysis of alloys were systematically investigated. The following are the
conclusions:

Ti48Al1Fe sheet. When the temperature reaches 1250

approach for the effective fabrication of TiAl plates in practical
