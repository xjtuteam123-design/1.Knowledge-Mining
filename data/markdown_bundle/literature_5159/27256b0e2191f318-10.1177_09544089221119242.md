Abstract
This study examines the high-temperature tensile properties of AZ31 and AZ31-1.0Ca-0.2Ce alloys in different rolling
directions. T ensile test results at 250 °C showed that the ductility of AZ31-1.0Ca-0.2Ce alloy increased threefold in the
90° perpendicular direction to rolling direction. Moreover , both strength and elongation values of AZ31-1.0Ca-0.2Ce
alloy were higher than those of AZ31. Macro-texture measurements revealed that AZ31 and AZ31-1.0Ca-0.2Ce alloys
showed strong and weakened basal texture in the rolling direction, respectively. The combined effect of secondary phases
and texture weakening increased the high-temperature ductility (49%) of AZ31-1.0Ca-0.2Ce. In the rolling direction, yield
(YS) and ultimate tensile strength of AZ31-1.0Ca-0.2Ce were obtained as 115 and 121 MPa, respectively. These values were
higher than AZ31 alloy due to the stronger texture of the non-basal planes. The fracture surface of AZ31 samples proved
that the rough and crack-dominated surface led to brittle fracture formation. However , ﬁne-sized dimples resulted in a

Introduction
Magnesium (Mg) alloys have remarkable properties such
as low density, excellent damping capability, and moder-
ate strength.1 Production of automobile components such
as oil pan, intake manifold, and valve covers with Mg
alloys is preferred to minimize fuel consumption by redu-
cing vehicle mass. 2 In addition, ﬁne-grained forged Mg
generally achieves higher mechanical properties than
as-cast ones. 3,4 However, their high-temperature tensile
properties are inferior due to their low melting tempera-
tures depending on compositions. The rare earth metals
(REMs) enhance the high-temperature strength of Mg
base alloys.
5–7 The use of Ca in Mg alloys came from
the bene ﬁcial effects on the biodegradable properties of
Mg base alloys. Ca is abundant in the human body and
is heavily stored by the bones. 8 Moreover, Ca addition
results in stable secondary phase forming, enabling high-
temperature oxidation performance in Mg alloys. 9 Song
et al.10 reported that the more reﬁned grains were obtained
by adding 1 wt.% Ca to AZ31 Mg alloy was developed
efﬁciently by combined effects of texture and secondary
phase strength mechanisms.
Ce addition with Ca contributes to the secondary
phase strength, which in ﬂuences the creep properties of
Mg–4Al–2Sn–1Ca (wt.%) alloy, mainly by forming the
CaMgSn and Al 11Ce3 phases.11 The 0.2, 0.5, and
1.0 wt.% Ce addition was studied to enhance the mechan-
ical properties of Mg-1.0Sn-0.6Ca alloy in extruded con-
ditions. The increase of Ce amount resulted in the
decreased grain size and altered the secondary phases of
CaMgSn, which was replaced by (Ca,Ce)MgSn and
Mg
12Ce. Ce-dependent texture weakening was observed
with the increased secondary phase density. The reason
has been connected to the restriction of dynamic recrystal-
lizations (DRXs) due to the high-volume fraction of sec-
ondary phases. The highest ductility of 27.6% was
acquired by 0.2 wt.% Ce addition.

obtained with different thickness reductions in the

430 mm/s). Elevated HSRR values result in higher twin
density and the consequent large fraction of DRX-
treated grains. However, they observed that a strain rate
of 2.9 with a thickness reduction of 8% led to rolling
without cracking.
The combining effect of Ca and Ce on the mechanical
properties of Mg alloys was studied by researchers who
considered the room temperature condition or one-way
direction. In addition, AZ31 Mg alloy, produced using
various rolling processes with different strain rate
values, was tested in only one direction at a high tempera-
ture. Moreover, the theoretical process maps developed
for Mg alloys demand further experimental studies to
evaluate the effects of hot rolling parameters on high-
temperature mechanical properties. However, the elevated
temperature tensile properties of hot rolled AZ31 Mg
alloys containing 1 wt.% Ca and 0.2 wt.% Ce is needed
for an investigation. Moreover, the direction-dependent
elevated temperature tensile properties of AZ31-Ca –Ce
alloys are still under discussion.
The aim of this study is to examine the high-
temperature tensile properties of hot rolled AZ31B and
AZ31B-1 wt.% Ca-0.2 wt.% Mg alloys tested in different
directions such as 0°, 45°, and 90° to the RD. The rela-
tionship between tensile properties and microstructure
has been investigated by macro-texture measurements
and secondary phase formation.
Experimental procedures
The studied alloys were produced using the gravity
casting method in an electric resistance furnace. Pure
Mg (99.9%), Al (99.9%), and Sn (99.9%) ingots were
added to the graphite ladle for melting at 750 ˚C under
argon gas. Mg base Mg –Mn (10%), Mg –Ca (25%), and
Mg–Ce (25%) master alloys were placed in the melt and
stirred for 15 min at 750 ˚C. The melted materials were
injected into the steel mold and then allowed to cool at
room temperature. Homogenization was carried out at
350 °C for 24 h to eliminate micro-segregation.
Total six passes were applied to 17 × 30 × 30 mm
plates during the hot rolling (400 °C) process, and
10 mm thick plates were obtained. Each pass had the

Conclusions
In this study, AZ31 and AZ31-1.0Ca-0.2Ce alloys were
successfully cast and rolled at 250 °C. The effect of hot
rolling process on microstructure, macro-texture and
mechanical properties at 0°, 45°, and 90° with respect to
RD was investigated. The following conclusions were
obtained from the study:
 After adding 1.0Ca-0.2Ce to the AZ31 alloy, small-
sized secondary phase particles were formed at the
grain boundaries.
 According to the tensile tests carried out at 250°, with
the addition of 1.0Ca-0.2Ce alloy, especially in the
45° and 90° directions to rolling, the strength increased
by 20% while the ductility increased more than twice.
 Due to the weakness of the macro-texture, the highest
ductility was obtained in the A1 and B1 samples,
which made an angle of 90° to the RD.
 In the tensile test at 45° to the RD, the B3 sample
showed more than three times ductility due to the
ﬁne-scaled (Al, Mg)
2Ca and Al11Ce3 secondary phases.
