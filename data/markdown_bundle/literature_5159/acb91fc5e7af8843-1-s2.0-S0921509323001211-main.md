ABSTRACT 
The conflict between strength and ductility of bimodal Ti – 5Al – 5V – 5Mo – 3Cr (Ti-5553) becomes more evident as 
yield strength attains 1400 MPa, which limits the further application of the alloy in the ultra-high-strength 
condition. In this paper, we found that the conventional bimodal Ti-5553 alloy with 1400 MPa yield strength 
processes four typical kinds of grain boundary microstructures. However, two kinds of grain boundaries with the 
short α side-plate are almost un-deformable in tension, contributing to the strength-versus-ductility conflict. 
Fortunately, short α side-plate might transform to α laminate that is parallel to the grain boundary of prior β grain 
after the dual-ageing consisting of the high-temperature ageing and the low-temperature ageing. It is found that α 
laminate is capable of shearing, which is beneficial to the ductility of bimodal Ti-5553 alloy. Base on the dual- 
ageing strategy, a novel bimodal Ti-5553 alloy with excellent combination of yield strength (1391 MPa), ultimate 
tensile strength (1484 MPa) and tensile elongation (12%) has been fabricated. The strength versus ductility data 
of the novel alloy might extend beyond the benchmark range established by the strongest and most ductile Ti- 
alloys known. The result of the present work is instructive for designing the grain-boundary microstructure of 
high-strength bimodal near- β titanium alloys. 
1. Introduction 
Owing to the high strength-to-weight ratio and the excellent corro -
sion resistance, near- β titanium alloy is of great importance to the 
weight control of advanced aircrafts [ 1 , 2 ]. The first generation of typical 
near- β titanium alloy is Ti – 10V – 2Fe – 3Al (Ti-1023) [ 3 ]. In the period 
from 1970s to the early twenty first century, melting, 
thermal-mechanical processing, microstructure evolution and mechan -
ical behavior of Ti-1023 alloy were being extensively investigated [ 4 – 7 ]. 
The concept for Ti – 5Al – 5V – 5Mo – 3Cr (Ti-5553) near- β titanium alloy 
was publicly introduced in 1997 as an improved version of the Russian 
alloy VT-22 [ 8 ]. Except for the high strength-to-weight ratio, which is 
the most common merit of near- β titanium alloys, Ti-5553 alloy pro -
cesses the following advantages compared to Ti-1023 alloy, including 
the better achievable compositional uniformity of the ingot, the wider 
thermal-mechanical processing window and the higher maximum 
quenching-diameter [ 8 ]. Thus, the relatively new Ti-5553 alloy has been 
used in thick section forgings for high strength airframe components, e. 
g. landing gear, flap tracks, ect [ 9 ]. 
Although Ti-5553 alloy exhibits the ultra-high strength, it is critical 
to balance the strength and the ductility of the alloy, which is the base 
requirement for aviation structural materials. As is pointed out by 
Ritchie, strength and ductility of metallic alloys are manually exclusive, 
and near- β Ti-5553 alloy also obeyed the rule [ 10 – 12 ]. The ultra-high 
strength of Ti-5553 alloy originates from the nano-scaled α precipita -
tion [ 13 ]. Much research has shown that the precipitation-strengthening 
effect of ultra-fine intragranular α ( α
Intra
) is proportional to the volume 
fraction of α

and is inversely proportional to the dimension of α

pends on the strain at which the crack becomes unstable and starts to 
propagate [ 12 ]. One feasible way to solve the conflict between strength 
and ductility of high-strength Ti-5553 alloy is to refine the grain size of 
prior β grain. As the grain size of high-strength Ti-5553 alloy is 
approximate 200 μ m (lamellar microstructure), the crack with the

The Φ 160mm × 420 mm Ti-5553 alloy ingot with the chemical

consumable electrode vacuum furnace. The β -transus temperature of the 
ingot is 845 
◦

processed to a Φ 75 mm bar. Extrusion of Ti-5553 alloy bar was carried 
out on 6 MN horizontal extruder. The forged bar was directly extruded 
to a Φ 25mm rod bar at 785 
◦
C. Rod bars with the dimension of Φ 6mm ×
45 mm were cut from the extruded bar for solution-ageing treatment. 
The heat-treatment of the bar was carried out in pre-heated Naber -

5553 alloy rod are listed in Table 1 . Electron backscatter diffraction

solution-treated alloy. Dimension of the EBSD specimen was Φ 6mm × 2

direction. The disk was first mechanically ground with SiC paper up to 
800 grids and was then electrolytically polished for 20 s at 10 
◦
C using 
a voltage of 40 V. The EBSD patterns were acquired under the following 
conditions: an acceleration voltage of 20 kV, a specimen tilt of 70
◦
, a 
scan step size of 0.5 μ m on the rectangular scan grid. ZEISS Supra-55 
scanning electron microscopy (SEM) was used to investigate the 
microstructure of the bimodal Ti-5553 alloys. 
Tensile test was carried out on Instron-5895 testing machine, 
following the GB/T 228.1 – 2010 standard. Fig. 1 shows the dimension of 
the tensile specimen. The gauge section of tensile specimen was Φ 3mm 
× 15 mm. The tensile strain rate was 5 × 10

3.9. Tensile fractography of bimodal Ti-5553 alloy 
Fig. 12 displays the tensile fractography of four kinds of bimodal Ti- 
5553 alloys. Fig. 12 (a), Fig. 12 (d), Fig. 12 (g) and Fig. 12 (j) show the 
marco-fractography of the alloys. It is evident that all the ruptured 
tensile specimens exhibit the cup-and-cone fracture surface, which 
consists of the flat fiber region and the shearing lip. Fig. 12 (b) shows the 
micro-fractography for the fiber region of the conventional bimodal Ti- 
5553 alloy, and the fractography of the shearing facet is shown in Fig. 12 
(c). Dimples and facets could be observed. It should be noted that the 
facet originates from the shearing crack that is caused by the intra -
granular shearing of prior β grain. Therefore, the conventional bimodal 
Ti-5553 alloy exhibits the mixed tensile fracture mode of the covalence 
of micro-void and the shearing. Fig. 12 (e) and Fig. 12 (f) show the 
micro-fractography for the fiber region of IA-5 bimodal Ti-5553 alloy. 
Dimples and facets could also be found. Since the intragranular shearing 
crack could also forms during the tensile plastic deformation of IA-5 
alloy ( Fig. 9 (c, d)), the facet should also be identified as the shearing 
crack. Consequently, the tensile fracture mechanism of IA-5 bimodal Ti- 
5553 alloy is the mixed of the covalence of micro-void and the shearing. 
As shown in Fig. 12 (h) and Fig. 12 (i), IA-10 bimodal alloy also exhibits 
the mixed tensile fracture mode. Fig. 12 (k) displays the micro- 
fractography in the fiber region of the IA-60 bimodal Ti-5553 alloy. It 
is evident that the tensile fracture surface is only composed of dimples. 
This finding suggests that the tensile fracture mechanism of the alloy is 
the covalence of micro-void. The transformation in the tensile fracture 
mechanism of IA-60 bimodal alloy agrees with the damage mechanism 
of IA-60 bimodal alloy.

4. Discussion 
4.1. Improving the ductility of high-strength bimodal Ti-5553 alloy by 
grain boundary shearing 
The aim of the work was to improve the ductility of the conventional 
bimodal alloy with yield strength of 1400 MPa by inducing the hybrid- α 
precipitation, and three kinds of novel bimodal Ti-5553 alloys with 
hybrid- α precipitation were designed and fabricated by different dual- 
ageing strategies. The novel IA-5 bimodal alloy, which processes the 
20% improved ductility and the comparable strength of the conven -
tional high-strength bimodal Ti-5553 allo, has been successfully 
designed and fabricated. Zhang et al. summarized the combination of 
strength and ductility of typical Ti-alloys, which is shown in Fig. 14 [ 17 ]. 
Tensile performance of IA-5 bimodal Ti-5553 is marked by the red star. 
As shown in this map, the strength-versus-ductility data for the novel 
IA-5 bimodal Ti-5553 alloy lies outside the benchmark established by 
the strongest and most ductile Ti-alloys known. 
Table 2 shows the microstructure comparisons between conventional 
bimodal Ti-5553 alloy and IA-5 bimodal Ti-5553 alloy. The short α side- 
plate of the conventional alloy is un-deformable in tension. In contrast, 
shearing of α laminate that is parallel to the grain boundary of prior β 
grain occurs in the grain boundary region of IA-5 bimodal alloy. Since 
the damage mechanism of the conventional bimodal alloy and the 
damage mechanism of the novel IA-5 bimodal are caused by the 
cracking of the elongated prior β grains, the shearing of α laminate could 
effectively delay the formation of the shearing crack. Therefore, we 
mainly attribute the improved ductility of the high-strength IA-5 
bimodal Ti-5553 alloy to the grain-boundary shearing. 
4.2. Applications and further work 
In the present work we proposed a feasible dual-ageing strategy for 
fabricating Ti-5553 alloy rod with the excellent combination of strength 
and ductility. As mentioned above, the dimension of the solution-treated 
rod is Φ 6mm × 45 mm. The rod was heated at high heating rate during 
the dual-ageing treatment, because it was heated in the pre-heated 
furnace. Therefore, the dual-ageing strategy presented here can be 
implemented in the high-strength bimodal Ti-5553 alloy component 
with the thin-section. For example, IA-5 bimodal alloy might be used as 
parent material of the hi-lock bolt with the diameter of 6 – 10 mm. Owing 
to the limited ductility of the conventional bimodal Ti-5553 alloy, the 
thread rolling of the conventional bimodal Ti-5553 alloy bolts with the 
UTS level of 1500 MPa might lead to the rupture of screw thread and the 
formation of micro-crack beneath the screw [ 18 ]. The improved 
ductility of IA-5 bimodal alloy should be beneficial to the thread rolling 
of Ti-5553 alloy hi-lock bolt. In further, we will investigate the fracture 
toughness (K
IC
) of IA-5 bimodal Ti-5553 alloy. 
5. Conclusion 
In this paper, we fabricated three kinds of novel bimodal Ti-5553 
alloys with hybrid- α precipitation by dual-ageing, which is based on 
the microstructural prototype of the conventional bimodal Ti-5553 alloy 
with fine equiaxial prior β grain. Tensile performance and the grain 
boundary deformation behavior of the novel alloys are compared with 
those of the conventional bimodal Ti-5553 alloy. The following con -
clusions may be drawn. 
1. The conflict between strength and ductility of the conventional 
bimodal Ti-5553 alloy with 1400 MPa yield strength might spring 
from the un-deformable short α side-plate. 
2. The prior β grain boundary with ultra-fine α precipitation and the 
prior β grain boundary with α laminate that is parallel to the grain 
boundary should be the ideal grain-boundary structures for ultra- 
high-strength bimodal Ti-5553 alloy, because they are capable of 
shearing. 
3. The coarse intragranular α that forms during the high-temperature 
ageing might lead to the decrease in strength of bimodal Ti-5553 
alloy.
