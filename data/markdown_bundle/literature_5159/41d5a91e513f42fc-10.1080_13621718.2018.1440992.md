ABSTRACT
Pressure welding (PW) is an advanced method for obtaining solid phase joints (SPJs). Using phys-
ical and finite element modelling (FEM) it is shown that PW under superplasticity conditions
provides SPJs between dissimilar cast single-crystal intermetallic and deformed polycrystalline
heat-resistant Ni-based superalloys. Special attention is paid to exclude the stagnant zones in
the solid phase joining region using surface relief. The results of physical modelling and FEM are
in qualitative agreement. It is revealed that the surface relief can decrease the stagnant zone and
increase localisation of deformation in the joining region, which helps to improve the joining
quality. A monotonous change in microhardness in the joint zone is shown.

The distribution of shear strain in the samples with
different surface reliefs was studied by FEM. Compar-
ison of the results confirms that the relief provides
redistribution of shear strains. From the experiments,
it was shown that for the reliefs with right angles the
physical contact was not achieved in the corners. For
the trapezoidal relief with obtuse angles, as well as
for wave-type relief and flat surface physical contact
was established along the entire surface. Estimation of
the porosity has shown that the samples are ordered
by the increase in porosity as follows: wave-type, flat,
trapezoidal,rectangularandtriangular.
A monotonous change in the microhardness in the
jointzonefromonematerialtoanotherhasbeenexper-
imentally revealed. From this, a smooth change in the
mechanical propertiesof the jointscan be expected.
