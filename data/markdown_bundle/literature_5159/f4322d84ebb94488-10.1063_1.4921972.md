Theoretical deformation response of hypothetical b-titanium alloys was investigated using ﬁrst-
principles calculation technique under periodic boundary conditions. Simulation was carried out on
hypothetical 54-atom supercell of Ti–X (X ¼ Cr, Mn, Fe, Zr, Nb, Mo, Al, and Sn) binary alloys.
The results showed that the strength of Ti increases by alloying, except for Cr. The most effective
alloying elements are Nb, Zr, and Mo in the current simulation. The mechanism of bond breaking
was revealed by studying the local structure around the alloying element atom with respect to vol-
ume change. Moreover, the effect of alloying elements on bulk modulus and admissible strain was
investigated. It was found that Zr, Nb, and Mo have a signiﬁcant effect to enhance the admissible
strain of Ti without change in bulk modulus.

I. INTRODUCTION
Titanium (Ti) and its alloys (Ti-alloys) are very impor-
tant structural materials especially in the ﬁelds of aerospace
and biomedical applications.
1–4 This is attributed to their
high strength, low density, excellent plasticity, and ductility.
Therefore, development of new b-Ti alloys with best combi-
nation of high strength and toughness has taken a consider-
able research interest recently. 5–10
When the material’s sample-wide stress reaches a signif-
icant fraction of its ideal strength, it is then called an
ultra-high strength material. 6–8,11 For application guided
research, the development of new alloys or processing tech-
niques to reach its ultra-high strength level is quite useful.
Therefore, to design new alloys that show ultra-high
strength, it is important ﬁrst to know the ideal solid solution
strengthening afﬁnity of the different alloying elements in
the alloy. It is important to mention that there are many fac-
tors affecting the ideal strength of the material such as the
dislocation interaction with the strain energy generated
around the solute atom as well as with each other. However,
the ideal solid solution strengthening produced from the lat-
tice strain affected by the interaction of the solute atoms will
be the major concern in the present study.
However, it is clear that the classical “trial and error”
way of obtaining a material with the desired properties is
relatively not effective and in most cases economically
unfeasible.
12 On the other hand, the development of material
modeling methods and simulations using the advance in
calculation-ability presents a simpler and somewhat faster
method to predict the desired property change. 13 The promis-
ing modeling methods for metal and alloys are ﬁrst-
principles and/or ab initio methods.13–15
For Ti and Ti-alloys simulation, Song et al . calculated
the theoretical strength of b-Ti alloys as a function of bind-
ing energy for different alloying elements. He used the
discrete variational cluster method applied on two cluster
models with 15 and 27 atoms, respectively.
16,17 Also, Raabe
et al. studied the effect of Nb and Mo alloying on the elastic
properties of b-Ti using ﬁrst-principles predictions based on
density functional theory (DFT). In Raabe’s work, the binary
systems used consisted of 2 /C2 2 /C2 2 elementary cubic or
hexagonal unit cells with a total of 16 atoms. 18,19 The effect
of dislocation interaction in Ti using Peierls-Nabarro model
was studied in many papers such as Refs. 20,21. Generally,
it is much better to use models under periodic boundary con-
dition compared with cluster models to analyze effects on
the alloy materials. The advantage of using periodic models
comes from both aspects of surrounding potentials and
geometry constraints closer to actual alloying circumstances.
In the present study, the strength of hypothetical b-Ti
alloy was investigated by the ﬁrst-principles method under
periodic boundary conditions with larger models containing
more number of atoms. The calculations were carried out on
models of pure-Ti and Ti-alloyed with manganese (Mn),
chromium (Cr), molybdenum (Mo), iron (Fe), aluminum
(Al), tin (Sn), niobium (Nb), and zirconium (Zr). These ele-
ments were selected for the purpose of scanning a wide range
of Ti alloying elements, with different powers in stabilizing
the (bcc) b-phase, including a-stabilizers (i.e., Al and Sn),
neutral (i.e., Zr), and b-stabilizers (i.e., Mn, Cr, Mo, Fe, and
Nb). A relationship between theoretical hydrostatic stress
and the volumetric strain was calculated for each model. The
mechanical properties such as strength, bulk modulus, and
admissible strain were evaluated from the theoretical hydro-
static stress-strain curves. For understanding the reason for
different effects of alloying elements on the mechanical
properties, study of atomic bond interaction and structural

II. CALCULATION METHOD
A. First-principles calculations
The ﬁrst-principles calculations on the periodic boundary
models of 54-atoms supercell originated from b-Ti structure
have been performed using VASP program package, 22,23
based on the DFT. 24 For the exchange-correlation interaction,
generalized-gradient approximation (GGA) method was used
with the PerdewWang (PW91) functional.
25,26 The ultrasoft
pseudopotentials prepared by Vanderbilt 27 was adopted. The
cutoff energy for wave functions of electrons with plane-
wave expansion was set at 30 Ry (408 eV) with 4 /C2 4 /C2 4
Monkhorst-Pack K-point sampling.
28
B. Ti-alloy models
In order to calculate the theoretical strength of pure Ti
and Ti-alloys, 54-atom supercell of hypothetical b-Ti was
proposed. All calculations were applied with the assumptions
that the material is homogeneous and isotropic. The pro-
posed supercell model was composed of 3 /C2 3 /C2 3 alignment
of cubic bcc unit cell representing hypothetical b-Ti, where
each unit cell includes two Ti atoms as displayed in Fig. 1.
The alloy models were devised by replacing a Ti atom
with another alloying element atom mentioned and discussed
in Sec. I. The model structure of hypothetical b-Ti was opti-
mized by the relaxation of lattice constant and internal coor-
dinates for no-stress condition. After that, external pressure
was calculated directly from the ﬁrst-principles calcula-
tions
29 by altering the lattice constant with relaxation of in-
ternal coordinates of atoms, and the hydrostatic stress can be
regarded as the absolute value of external pressure.

should be equal to vIdeal ¼ 0.2887 for the model with the
ideal symmetry of bcc structure. For pure Ti, it can be seen
that vðDVÞ stays constant even after the ideal symmetry in
the model begins to be broken. As for the higher vð0Þ values
such as Al, Zr, Sn, and Cr, the value of vðDVÞ increases to
much higher value as the volume of model increases. It
means that Ti–X bonds have relatively weak interaction in
the large volume model, and the elongation of bonds leading
to the break of the alloy model occurs in Ti-X bonds. On the
contrary, as for lower vð0Þ such as Fe, Mn, Mo, and Nb,
vðDVÞ decreases to a much lower value as the volume
increases. Namely, the Ti–X bonds keep relatively strong
interaction in the large volume model, and the break of
bonds occurs in Ti–Ti bond, particularly the neighboring
Ti–Ti bond to alloying element X.
The mechanism of breaking bonds was investigated
through studying the local structure in the vicinity of the
alloying element atom with respect to the volume change.
Ti-Cr and Ti-Fe alloy models were studied as typical exam-
ples, respectively. As discussed above, Ti–Cr bond lengths
are much longer than Ti–Ti ones due to the weakness of Ti-
Cr interaction. In the optimized structure of the Ti-Cr model,
the Cr atom is located on the exact center of the Ti cage
around the Cr atom, as shown in Fig. 4(a). As volume of the
system increases, the position of Cr atom deviates gradually
from the center of the Ti cage, as shown in Figs. 4(b) and
4(c). However, the Cr atom keeps the eight-coordination
state of bcc structure until the system reaches r
max, Fig. 4(c).
Finally, the local-structure transition can be observed under
the excess strain; namely, the local bcc structure around the
Cr atom has been completely broken and new Ti-Cr bonds
seem to be created, as shown in Fig. 4(d).
For the Ti-Fe alloy model, Ti–Fe bond lengths are much
shorter than Ti–Ti ones due to the high strength of Ti–Fe
interaction. The Ti–Ti interaction should be weakened due to
inﬂuence of the strong Ti–Fe interaction, as shown in
Fig. 5(a). Similarly as the Ti-Cr alloy model, the Fe atom is
located at the exact center of the Ti cage around the Fe atom
in the optimized structure. As the volume of the model
increases, the position of Fe atom deviates gradually from
the center of the Ti cage as shown in Figs. 5(b) and 5(c),
where the shortest Ti–Fe bond length is about 2.68 ˚A.
Simultaneously, the Ti–Ti interaction neighboring to Ti–Fe
bond is further weakened, leading to the break of the struc-
ture. Under the excess strain, the local bcc structure around
the Fe atom has been completely broken making the very
strong Ti-Fe bond as shown in Fig. 5(d), where the shortest
Ti–Fe bond length is 2.369 ˚A. At the same time, the very
strong Ti–Fe bond has also severely weakened another
Ti–Fe bond, where its bond length is 3.566 ˚A.

IV . CONCLUSIONS
Prediction of strength and bulk modulus for hypothetical
b-Ti and their alloys were performed using ﬁrst-principles
calculation under periodic boundary condition. Except for
Cr, all alloying elements show a positive effect to enhance
the strength of Ti under the hydrostatic stress condition.
Particularly, Nb, Zr, Mo, Fe, and Mn show a drastic
improvement in strength values, respectively. Strength of the
bond interaction between Ti and alloying elements plays an
important role in the bond breaking mechanism. Fe and Mn
signiﬁcantly increase the value of bulk modulus, and other
alloying elements have a small effect. As a result, Zr, Nb,
and Mo are effective to enhance the elastic admissible strain
for Ti-based engineering related applications.
