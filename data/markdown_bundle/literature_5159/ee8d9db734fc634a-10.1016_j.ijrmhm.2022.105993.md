ABSTRACT 
Breaking through the design concept of conventional high-temperature alloys, refractory high entropy alloys 
(RHEAs) exhibited increasingly alloy systems and excellent performance, which can be potentially used as 
turbine blades, nuclear materials, kinetic energy penetrators, catalysts for high-temperature service in space, 
nuclear reactor programs, electronic industries and military fields. Therefore, as novel promising candidates with 
great application potential beyond superalloys, RHEAs
′
research not only has cutting-edge academic value, but 
also has immeasurable economic benefits and strategic significance. Due to the explosively exponential growth of 
RHEAs in the years from 2019 to 2022 and based on the review of these newly published literature, the current 
development has been categorized into composition space extension, fabrication routes, post processing 
methods, performances, modeling and simulation section, as well as promising future possibilities. 
1. Introduction 
The demands for turbine engines, the need for high strength alloys 
for high-temperature service in “ space race ” , the requirement for high- 
temperature materials in nuclear reactor programs, and other desired 
applications drive the increasing development of refractory alloys. In -
dustry and academia have been chasing cost-effective, high-strength, 
lightweight and oxidation-resistance refractory alloys for stringent ap -
plications [ 1 ]. The concept of high entropy alloys (HEAs) aka multi- 
principal component alloys, metallic high-entropy materials, composi -
tionally complex alloys and baseless alloys, independently announced 
by Jien-Wei Yeh and Brian Cantor in open literature published in 2004 
[ 2 , 3 ], disruptively breakthroughs the alloying concepts and results in 
the rapid development of new materials due to the dramatical expansion 
of the material palette. The solid solution phase formation far less than 
the maximum predicted by the Gibbs phase rule corresponds to the alloy 
composition at the center of the multicomponent phase diagram, 
resulting in four core effects in HEAs [ 4 ]: high entropy effect for ther -
modynamics, severe lattice distortion effect for crystal structure, slug -
gish diffusion effect for kinetics and the resulted cocktail effect for 
performances, which are considered to be positive for material micro -
structure and properties. Many intriguing properties of HEAs originated 
from the local lattice distortions introduced by different element mixing 
with different atomic sizes and local electronic charge transfer. 
The introduction of refractory high entropy alloys (RHEAs) occurred 
in 2010 based on five refractory elements of Mo, Nb, Ta, V and W [ 5 ]. 
RHEAs are considered to offer a potentially disruptive and trans -
formative material solution for high-temperature capability with 
enhanced mechanical, irradiation and oxidation resistance [ 6 ]. Using 
“ refractory high entropy alloy ” , “ refractory multi-principal element 
alloy ” , “ refractory complex concentrated alloy ” and “ refractory 
compositionally complex alloy ” as the subject term with the defined 
period between 2019 and 2022, results in a visually statistical image 
Fig. 1 , which clearly shows the history and development in the field of 
RHEAs. As shown in Fig. 1 (a), The growth of RHEA articles can be 
expressed by the fitting equation of exponential rise. Due to the great 
potential in the application as turbine blades, nuclear materials, shaped 
charge liners and kinetic energy penetrators, the publication boom can 
be observed clearly in all economically developed regions, as shown in 
Fig. 1 (b). The top universities and research institutes focusing on RHEA 
work include United States Department of Energy, United States 
Department of Defense, United States Air Force Research Laboratory, 
Helmholtz Association of German Research Centers, League of European 
Research Universities, Centre National de la Recherche Scientifique, 
Belgorod State University, Karlsruhe Institute of Technology, Chinese

2. RHEA composition space extension in 2019-2022 
Composition is the most original aspect to determine the final 
microstructure and performance, and the composition of RHEAs is 
significantly different from those of traditional alloys. As the composi -
tion of a material determines its basic properties and different fabrica -
tion routes and post-processing methods can further modify the 
resultant microstructure and properties, therefore, new composition 
lays the foundation of the alloy development. As announced by D.B. 
Miracle and O.N. Senkov [ 7 ], a bold and expansive exploration of the 
immense composition space of HEAs is essential and meanwhile, it 
should be noted that the potential for discoveries has hardly been 
scratched [ 8 ]. The circumstance can be relieved by both experimental 
and high-throughput simulation prediction work which will be reviewed 
in the following simulation section. In the first studies on HEAs, in -
duction and arc melting followed by casting were used as the method of 
production [ 2 , 3 ]. Based on the RHEAs development in the latest years

3.2. Powder metallurgy 
Compared to the casting method suffering from the element segre -
gation, coarsened grains and hard melting process, powder metallurgy 
routes starting from pre-alloyed powders could obtain a more chemi -
cally homogenous and ultrafine structure. Mechanical alloying is a 
widely adopted method for synthesizing alloys from the starting ele -
ments of various melting points and vapor pressure with large discrep -
ancies. Combined mechanical alloying and the following spark plasma 
sintering could retain the mechanically alloyed ultrafine structure 
within a short sintering duration and obtain highly compacted samples 
with applied high pressure and temperature. 
Ultrafine-grained MoNbTaTiV was fabricated by mechanical alloy -
ing and spark plasma sintering [ 61 ]. The resultant RHEA with fcc pre -
cipitates uniformly dispersed in both boundaries and the interior of the 
bcc matrix grains exhibited high compressive strength of 3238 MPa with 
a compressive strain of 24.9%. The fcc precipitates were confirmed by 
SEAD and HAADF analysis to be compounds of TiO and TiN. The 
significantly enhanced mechanical properties compared to the coun -
terpart made by the vacuum arc melting method were attributed to the 
grain boundary strengthening and interstitial solid solution strength -
ening due to the powder metallurgy introduced O and N [ 61 ]. In another 
work, ultrafine-grained NbMoTaWVCr RHEA was fabricated by me -
chanical alloying and spark plasma sintering[ 62 ]. The precipitation of

3.3. Additive manufacturing 
Except for the material properties, fabricability was also a critical 
point for product manufacturing. Additive manufacturing, including 
selective laser melting, electron beam melting and fused extrusion, is 
considered a revolutionary technology to fabricate near-net-shape 
metallic components, which is especially more important when fabri -
cating expensive RHEAs. The highly effective and cost-saving material 
use, ability to produce complex structures and high flexibility make it 
more and more attractive in RHEAs. In the input type of laser energy, 
there are mainly two types including laser metal deposition (LMD) and 
selective laser melting (SLM) using in-situ alloying of the mixed 
elemental powder blends or pre-alloyed powders. By gradual substitu -

5. Performance 
5.1. Mechanical properties 
A major paradox for RHEAs is the balance between high-temperature 
high-strength and room-temperature ductility. The well-known Cantor 
alloy of FeCrMnNiCo exhibited rather high strength and ductility over a 
wide range of temperatures. For RHEAs however, the strength was high 
while the ductility was rather low. The deficient room temperature 
malleability also makes the RHEAs hard to be processed. To date, limited 
in-depth studies were reported on the underlying deformation mecha -
nisms and plastic properties during the deformation processes in RHEAs, 
which is essential to better understand the mechanical evolution under 
different service temperatures. The mechanisms of dislocation mobility 
and the mechanistic origin of the retained strength in RHEAs remain an 
open question to date, although some new findings from experimental 
and modeling viewpoints have been reported. The studies on as-cast 
HfNbTaTiZr carried out by J.P. Couzini ´e et al. and L. Lilensten et al. 
[ 124 , 125 ] argued that the deformation was controlled by the glide of 
screw dislocations with rectilinear shape and low mobility. Specifically, 
screw dislocations with Burgers vector parallel to the < 111 > directions 
were identified, along with dislocation debris such as loops and dipoles 
due to the solid solution pinning effect. The strengthening was 
associated with the compositional fluctuations which modify the screw 
dislocation core along the dislocation line, resulting in non-planar 
character of the screw dislocation core with non-Schmid effects exer -
ted high Peierls stresses [ 125 , 126 ]. According to the simulation work on 
the behavior of a/2[ 111 ] screw and edge dislocations by S.I. Rao et al. 
[ 50 , 127 ], the non-planar screw dislocations were also revealed. The 
strengthening was considered to arise from local variations in the screw 
dislocation core structure. However, in stark contrast to simple bcc 
metals, the critical resolved shear stress on edge dislocations in RHEAs is 
comparable to that for screw dislocations. Besides, the screw/edge 
anisotropy of strength in RHEAs was smaller. Therefore, it can be seen 
that both experimental and theoretical views support that the defor -
mation of many RHEAs is controlled by screw dislocations [ 128 ]. 
However, the underlying deformation mechanisms, which can be 
rather different from the above strategies, seem to be strongly correlated 
with the alloy composition of RHEAs. For example, edge dislocations are 
increasingly relevant in bcc structured RHEAs. Recent microscopic 
deformation mechanisms in bcc structured RHEA MoNbTi were carried 
out by both experiment and simulation, which revealed that the 
exceptional plastic deformability and strength were attributed to the 
non-screw character dislocation motion, i.e. beyond the usually assumed 
(110) < 111 > a/2 screw type, and numerous slip planes for dislocations

6. Simulation 
One critical challenge and pursuit of RHEAs is the accurate predic -
tion of phase formation and properties, due to their astronomically and 
exponentially immense composition space. Experimental methods are 
based on physical intuition or trial-and-error strategies. As the formation 
of RHEAs is controlled by thermodynamic and kinetic factors, according 
to which new alloys can be designed or predicted in a highly efficient 
and cost-effective way. Simulation and modeling with great significance 
in understanding the material, although on a small scale, have devel -
oped rapidly in the past three years, including machine learning (ML), 
density functional theory (DFT), molecular dynamics (MD) and calcu -
lation of phase diagrams (CALPHAD), which shed light on the devel -
opment of new RHEAs and the information on thermodynamic and 
kinetic factors. Various information obtained from these simulation 
work would provide another perspective including more detailed, more 
efficient or supplementary knowledge for experimental work. The data- 
driven method would play a more and more important role in the 
development of novel alloys due to the increasingly abundant materials 
data space with the aid of Materials Genome Initiative. 
6.1. Machine learning 
Machine learning (ML), as a subset of artificial intelligence (AI), can 
extract non-linear correlations between the input and output data, 
which include phase formation features and phase predictions. ML was 
found to be efficient in prompt phase predictions. ML is not expensive 
for calculation. A new strategy of accelerated designing RHEAs with the 
algorithms of machine learning was proposed, resulting in approxi -
mately 100 single-phase RHEAs in the large compositional space 
composed of W, Mo, Ta, Nb, V, Ti, Cr and Al, and exhibiting an accuracy 
of 96.41% when differentiating the single-phase solid solution and non- 
single-phase solid solution [174]. Generative deep learning with deep 
neural networks is a very innovative material design in RHEAs devel -
opment, which has proved that new RHEAs can be effectively designed 
based on a learned correlation between approximated mechanical 
properties and the latent code used by the generator [175]. Recently, 
using exact muffin-tin orbitals and coherent potential approximation 
method, a large data set covering fourteen elements space was gener -
ated, containing 7086 HEAs with 1911 complete elastic tensor [176]. 
However, the limitation of ML resulted from the database deficiency of 
many less-common phases. Besides, the inherent thermodynamic 
parameter of entropy has not been beneficially used in ML models, 
which needs to be emphasized in future work. 
6.2. Density function theory calculation 
Based on quantum-mechanical calculations of crystal and corre -
sponding electron structure according to Schrodinger equation, first- 
principal calculations according to density functional theories (DFT) 
provide information directly from the alloy basic physical properties 
without experimental nor empirical inputs. It turns out to be effective in 
phase and property predictions. For example, the effect of Cr and Ti on 
microstructure, elastic constants and elastic properties of VMoNbTaW 
RHEA was investigated based on DFT calculations, which revealed the

7. Critical challenges and future endeavors 
Under the guidance of not limiting design space unnecessarily, more 
novel alloy systems and compositions have been developed, including 
novel RHEAs with tetragonal structure, containing the uranium element, 
or bearing interstitial elements. Except for the alloy system or compo -
sition development, the progress in fabrication routes, post processing, 
performance tailoring, calculation and simulation result in more pro -
found materials and knowledge storage in RHEAs in the years from 2019 
to 2022, which are summarized as the followings with critical challenges 
and future endeavors. 
(1) Physical data including elastic constants, heat capacity, thermal 
conductivity, thermal expansion coefficient, diffusion coefficient, 
modulus, and so on are rather lacking, compared with the re -
ported deformation strength and strain values. As for the reported 
mechanical property data, it should be pointed out that the me -
chanical testing conditions were usually versatile and sometimes 
unknown, which should be taken with caution when making 
comparisons. Furthermore, the real situation tests under corro -
sive environments and other severe conditions are still to be 
executed to evaluate their performances. 
(2) Except for vacuum arc melting to fabricate bulk-sized RHEAs, SPS 
and additive manufacturing such as SLM, EBM and 3D ink- 
extrusion have drawn increasing attention. The highly effective 
and cost-saving material use, ability to produce complex struc -
tures and high flexibility make it more and more attractive in the 
development of RHEAs. Besides, the magnetron sputtering 
method are covering more fractions in manufacturing coatings, 
which would accelerate the application of RHEAs, as the coatings 
with the thickness of nanometer to micrometer sizes on various 
substrates could protect the substrate effectively to meet harsh 
environment requirements, greatly saving materials usage of the 
expensive materials. However, the high cost and low efficiency 
should be considered for magnetron sputtering and laser cladding 
methods. Except for the conventional powder synthesis, carbo -
thermal shock synthesis and other chemical syntheses of RHEA 
nanoparticles for energy and catalysis fields. As many routes use 
powders as initial materials, therefore, the synthesis of cost- 
effective and high-quality powders plays a remarkably impor -
tant role. 
(3) Pre-deformation by cold-worked or hot-worked deformation 
followed by annealing can accelerate the precipitation process in 
comparison with direct heat treatment, as the deformation could 
form various defects such as voids, dislocations and stacking 
faults to enhance the elemental diffusion process. Recent work on 
laser surface treatment and thermomechanical treatments such as 
hot forging, hot rolling and hot isostatic pressing to modify the 
microstructure and properties of RHEAs exhibit large and desir -
able tunning space. However, more experimental data and the -
ories are required for a more thorough understanding of the 
RHEAs. More optimization work on heat treatment and post- 
processing parameters needs to be conducted to obtain desired 
microstructure and properties. 
(4) A major paradox for RHEAs is the balance between high- 
temperature high-strength and room-temperature ductility. 
Except for the progress in compressive plasticity, tensile ductility 
is of more critical significance for the application and machin -
ability of RHEAs. Six typical strategies reported in the lasted three 
years to improve the tensile ductility have been summarized 
including natural-mixing guided design, doping of O or N 
element, decomposing via spinodal decomposition, simple hot 
rolling, decreasing the valence electron concentration, and 
annealing parameter tunning, which also show great tunning 
space in many processing parameters. Multiaxial nano- 
indentation creep experiment has been recently conducted to 
understand the creep behavior of RHEAs and more essential 
studies on fatigue and creep behaviors should be conducted more 
extensively for their real application, especially for additive 
manufactured components. The intriguing functional properties 
including irradiation-resistant properties, corrosion-resistant 
properties, electrical superconducting properties, hydrogen 
storage properties, and catalytic properties have triggered bur -
geoning studies in the field of RHEAs. 
(5) The development of computer technology has led to rapidly 
growing studies on material calculation and simulations, solving 
the problem of inefficient trial-and-error of traditional experi -
mental methods to a certain extent. The emergence and devel -
opment of artificial intelligence have prompted data-driven 
machine learning to attract more attention in the analysis and 
prediction of RHEAs; furthermore, machine learning combined 
with density functional theory calculation and thermodynamic 
database shows incomparable advantages in mining data and 
guiding experimental design to shorten the experimental period. 
The high-throughput experiments and computation will continue 
to expand, deepen, and increase the amount of data, making 
machine learning more precise and more important. However, it 
should be noted that the understanding of the underlying phys -
ical connotation and the construction of the theoretical system of 
systems science should be continuously deepened, and the rela -
tionship between parameters should not only be established. 
(6) Compared with the prediction of fcc structured RHEAs, the 
calculation of the phase diagram method is more accurate in the 
prediction of bcc phase. The preciseness of this approach lies in 
the level of the developed thermodynamic database. Thermody -
namic calculations were extrapolated from the binary system 
most of the time for compositional complex RHEAs, which should 
be treated with caution. Molecular dynamics simulations and 
first-principles calculations are widely adopted to reveal the 
mechanisms of deformation and toughening by studying the 
electron density and atomic distribution, defect growth and 
dislocation motion of RHEAs. Due to the limitation of computa -
tional cost, the modeling scales are still small, therefore the study 
of the properties and deformation mechanisms of high-entropy 
alloys needs to be extended to the mesoscopic scale and macro -
scopic deformation damage response; in addition, molecular
