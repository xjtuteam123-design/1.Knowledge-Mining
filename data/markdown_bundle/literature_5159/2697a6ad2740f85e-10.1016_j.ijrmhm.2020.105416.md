Abstract 
A new 3d transition metal high entropy alloy (3d TM HEA) CoFeNiTiV was prepared by vacuum arc-
melting. The phase component, microstructure and mechanical properties of CoFeNiTiV alloy were 
investigated thoroughly, with the comparison of CoFeNiTi -base alloys. The results show that both 
CoFeNiTiV and CoFeNiTiAl consists of a typical single bcc phase, while the CoFeNiTiCr and 
CoFeNiTiCu alloys are both composed of a single fcc phase although they are all CoFeNiTi based 
alloys. The effectiveness of VEC has been confirmed in the studied alloy system. From the 
perspective of the elemental role on microstructure, Ti is beneficial to the formation of compounds, 
while the elemental V, Cu, Al or Cr promote the formation of single -phase solid solution. Moreover, 
the yield strength reaches 2730 MPa and ultimate compressive strength of CoFeNiTiV alloy is up to 
3890 MPa, which is the highest of all CoFeNiTi based HEAs, even of all 3d TM HEAs up to date. 
Meanwhile, it exhibits a good ductili ty of 21.3 %. This excellent strength -ductility synergy of 
CoFeNiTiV alloy might stem from the majority constitution of bcc phase, solid-solution strengthening, 
the strong bonding among the principal elements and the inhabitation of cracking nucleation owi ng to 
the uniform solute distribution.

1. Introduction 
The emergence of high entropy alloys (HEAs), first proposed by Yeh et al. [1] and Cantor et al. [2], 
extensively expands the possibility of novel multi-component metallic materials[3-5] and brings new 
traction to materials discovery[6]. Originally, HEAs are defined as the alloys composed of 5 or more 
principal elements with concentration s ranging from 5 to 35 at.%. Up to now , a large number of 
multi-component alloys have been designed and reported [7-10]. Thereinto, the most widely studied 
alloy system was the 3d transition-metal (3d TM) HEAs, which contains at least 4 of the 9 following 
elements: Al, Co, Cr, Cu, Fe, Mn, Ni, Ti and V [1, 2, 4, 5, 11, 12] . Recently, Miracle et al. 
summarized that the amount of HEAs reported in literature wer e 408 in total up to early 2015, 
wherein the number of 3d TM HEAs reached 345[3]. Moreover, the se 3d TM alloys can be 
characterized into some branches by elemental grouping of the 9 elements. Accordingly, there are 126 
possible 4-element groupings. However, a significant majority of the alloys in this system (more than 
50%) actually contain the following five high -frequency 4-element groupings: AlCrFeNi, CoCrFeNi, 
AlCoFeNi, AlCoCrNi and AlCoCrFe. There are still much to be explored in the field of 3d TM HEAs 
and the vast number of HEAs have not been reported yet. 
Generally, single-phase solid solution is commonly considered to be beneficial to the acquisition of 
superior comprehensive mechanical and corrosion properties. Thus, in recent years, h igh entropy of 
random mixing of the princip al alloying elements has drawn much attention, due to its role in 
preventing the formation of ordered solid solution s and intermetallic phases [1, 13] . However, 
experimental results show that complex multiphase structures with a number of phases including 
ordered solid solutions and/or intermetallic phases usually exist in many HEAs[13-15]. In fact, single-
phase solid solution can be achieved only in a few 3d TM HEAs, such as CoCrFeNi, CoCrFeMnNi, 
CoFeNiTiAl and CoFeNiTiCu etc[13, 16-20]. In spite of only one principal element difference among 
CoFeNiTiAl, CoFeNiTiCu and CoFeNiTiCr, the phase microstructure of CoFeNiTiAl consists of a 
simple bcc solid solution, whereas that of CoFeNiTiCu and CoFeNiTiCr are made of a simple fcc 
solid solution [20, 21] . The foregoing statements indicate that the elemental composition of HEAs 
plays a critical role in the phase formation . Generally, in the synthesis of HEAs, Co, Cu, Ni, Mn and

Cr elements favor the generation of fcc solid-solution, while Al, W, V and Ti elements promote the 
formation of bcc phase[22-25]. However, this kind of understanding is not enough to guide the design 
of all HEAs. For instance, when adding the bcc-stabilizing element V into originally single-phase fcc

were observed rather than the expected bcc single -phase solid solution structure [13, 26, 27] . This 
indicates that the role of chemical composition on phase development in HEAs is worthwhile to 
investigate experimentally rather than empirically. 
Moreover, in terms of mechanical performance, V has a potential beneficial effect on increasing 
hardness and strength. For instance, in accordance with the Vickers hardness of the 
Al0.5CoCrCuFeNiVx alloy system reported by Yeh et al. [28], the maximum hardness of 640 HV, 
significantly larger than ~225 HV of Al 0.5CoCrCuFeNi, was acquired at x=1.0. Further, hardness is 
not only an initial screening of mechanical properties from the scientific view, but also general ly 
exhibits positive correlation with the resistance to wear from an engineering standpoint. 
Thus, with aim to exploit high performance HEAs and investigate the effect of V on phase formation, 
a new HEA CoFeNiTiV was designed by using bcc-stabilizing element V to substitute bcc-forming 
element Al, fcc-forming elements Cr and Cu in bcc CoFeNiTiAl, fcc CoFeNiTiCr and CoFeNiTiCu 
alloys, re spectively. The relationship among phase composition, microstructure and mechanical 
properties of as -cast CoFeNiTiV alloy were analyzed intensively. By comparison of phase 
components with the reported CoFeNiTi-(Al/Cr/Cu) alloys, the effect of alloying elements V, Al, Cr, 
Cu on phase formation was also explored in this work. 
2. Experimental procedures 
In the present work, the CoFeNiTiV alloy was prepared by vacuum arc melting of the equimolar 
mixtures of elemental Co, Fe, Ni, Ti and V which were in bulk form with a purity of Co 99.99%, Fe 
99.9%, Ni 99.95%, Ti 99.95% and V 99.9% (at. %), respectively. Arc melting of the alloy was 
conducted on a water -cooled copper crucible under a titanium gettered argon atmosphere. To ensure 
homogeneously compositional distribution, the alloy ingot was flipped and re-melted four times with

corresponds to an initial strain rate of 10-3 s−1.

In order to efficiently capture phase formation in HEAs that exhibit outstanding properties , several 
semi-empirical methods and parameters have been proposed to predict phase formation , such as 
configuration entropy, atomic size difference, electronegativity diﬀerence [3, 29] . Thereinto, the 
configurational entropy was widely recognized to be the predominant stabilizing parameter for a 
single-phase formation[16, 30-32]. However, according to the forgoing methods and/or parameters , 
only a small fraction of multi -component alloys are confirmed to form a single -phase solid solution, 
while the rest for m multi -phase structure[3, 33, 34] . Similarly, the validity of other methods or 
parameters are also limited to predict the phase form ation in HEAs[35, 36] . For instance, the 
MoNbHfZrTi alloy does not meet the constraints of Hume-Rothery rules to form a disordered solid 
solution phase[37], whereas the experimental results confirmed that a disordered single-phase solid 
solution indeed formed [3, 33, 34] . Consequently, the phase analysis in this work was conducted 
mainly based on XRD results. 
Figure 1 shows the X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of the as-cast CoFeNiTiV alloy of this work and 
the as -cast CoFeNiTiAl, CoFeNiTiCu, and CoFeNiTiCr alloys extracted from ref. [20] and [21] 
respectively. Hereby, both the as-cast CoFeNiTiAl and the CoFeNiTiV alloys consists of a typical bcc 
single-phase solid solution, as shown in Fig. 1(a, b), while the as-cast CoFeNiTiCu and CoFeNiTiCr 
alloys are distinctly composed of a fcc single-phase solid solution [see Fig. 1(c, d)]. According to the 
JCPDS cards, the XRD patterns of the experimental CoFeNiTiV alloy match well with the bcc-based 
B2-type ordered CoTi and FeTi phases. Further, the lattice parameter of the bcc phase was determined 
to be 293 pm. Rather than a typical single-phase solid solution, the quinary CoFeNiTi alloy possesses 
a main phase constitution of fcc phase and a bunch of unknown diffraction peaks[20]. In contrast, it is 
interesting to note that the phase constitution of ternary CoFeNi alloy is just composed of a single fcc 
solid solution. Therefore, the presence of the unknown phase in quinary CoFeNiTi alloy is bound to 
result from the incorporation of the active element Ti. Furthermore, with addition of V, Cu, Al or Cr 
into quinary CoFeNiTi alloy , the unknown peaks were completely inhibited. Accordingly, for the 
alloys with common principal elements Co, Fe and Ni, it is confirmed that the addition of Ti is related 
to the formation of unknown compounds, while all the elements V, Al, Cu and Cr play a role in

restraining the Ti -incorporated unknown compounds. This deduction is also proved by the 
undetectable unknown peaks in CoFeNiTiCuAl alloy with simultaneous addition of Cu and Al[20]. It 
is worthwhile to note that, for the aforementioned quinary CoFeNiTi alloy, the addition of V or Al 
apparently transfers the matrix phase from fcc into bcc, while the phase constitution for the alloys 
with Cu or Cr addition is still fcc, which is the same as CoFeNi alloy. 
As is well known, electron concentration plays a vital role in controlling the phase stability[35]. Two 
different parameters, e/a (the average number of itinerant electrons per atom ration) and VEC (the 
number of total electrons) were generally carr ied out to describe the electron co ncentration. In the 
present work, the studied HEAs are mainly composed of 3d TM elements. Considering the difficulty 
of defining the e/a values, VEC is thus a more straightforward evaluation parameter for them . 
According to ref. [34, 35], bcc phases can be stabilized at lower VEC (VEC<6.87), while fcc phases 
are stabilized at a higher VEC (VEC>8.0) in single-phase solid solution HEAs . In intermediate VEC 
(6.87<VEC<8), a mixture of fcc and bcc phases exist. Accordingly, the VEC for the studied alloys 
were calculated and summarized in Table 2. In contrast to CoFeNiTi with VEC of 7.75, the addition 
of Cu increases the VEC to 8.4, while the VEC decreases to 7.4, 7.2 and 6.8 with the addition of Cr, V 
and Al, respectively. Thus, the addition of Al with VEC<6.87 assist the alloy transforming from fcc 
into bcc, and the relatively low VEC with the V addition also favors the formation of a single bcc 
phase. Meanwhile, the Cu addition with VEC>8 helps maintain fcc phases, and the relatively high 
VEC with the Cr addition promote the formation of single fcc phase. The utilization of VEC seems to 
be an effective approach to predict the phase structure in CoFeNiTi-based alloy system.

in the dendrite, is 19.9, 19.9, 19.6, 19.6 and 21, respectively. To further verify the overall distribution

According to the results reported by Wang et al. [20], the morphology of the primary phase in 
CoFeNiTiCu can be identified as typical trunk dendrit es. While replacing Cu with Al, a majority of 
rosette dendrites with a little interdendritic regions rather than the trunk dendrites can be observed . In 
the present work, CoFeNiTiV alloy together with the aforementioned CoFeNiTiCu and CoFeNiTiAl 
alloys w ere fabricated by vacuum arc melting in a high -purity argon atmosphere, and directly 
solidified in water-cooled copper molds. SEM results show that CoFeNiTiV alloy possesses a typical 
columnar grain morphology, which radially filled the overall realm from the center to the margin of 
the rod specimen (Fig. 2a). 
As is known to us all , the solidification of alloys is mainly controlled by the solute diffusion and the 
formation of columnar crystals is att ributed to the fast growth of crystals with preferential 
crystallographic directions, such as <001> in bcc metals. The preferential crystallographic directions 
are commonly opposite to heat flow direction, which makes a faster growth rate of crystals and form 
the columnar crystals[38]. Thus, the crystallographic characterizations of the as-cast CoFeNiTiV was 
detected via EBSD analysis and illustrated in Fig. 4 . Figures 4a and 4b show the color -coded grain 
orientation map and corresponding pole figures. The relative position of the characterized region is 
marked in the schematic cross-section (Figure 4e) in the mapped sample (Figure 4f). Apparently, Fig. 
4a strongly supports the distributed features of the columnar grain structure in the CoFeNiTiV, which 
might stem from the solidification process. In accordance with their relative position, it can be 
spontaneously suggested that the columnar grains grows from the edge of the mold cavity to the 
center . Moreover, the grains show a preferred crystallographic orientation normal to the <001> and 
<101> direction, as confirmed by the standard stereographic triangle and the corresponding IPF 
(inverse pole figures) shown in Fig. 4c and 4d, which indicates that the grains prefer to grow along the 
<001> zone axis in bcc crystals . In addition, the volume fraction of the bcc phase was determined to 
be more than 90 %, which is in agreement with the phase constitution results identified by XRD 
analysis.

It is well known that the trace element s acting as interstitial atoms, such as O and N, play an 
important role on the mechanical properties, particularly in a Ti -containing alloy. In accordance with 
Table 1, the N content is very limited, which can be negligible without doubt. Additionally, compared 
with the previously reported Ti -23Nb-0.7Ta-2Zr (0.05 wt. %)[39], Ti-40Zr-6Al (0.3 wt. %)[40], Ti-
8Nb-2Fe-0.2O (0.2 wt. %) [41] Ti-containing alloys, the O con tent in the current studied alloy is 
considerably lower than that in them. Moreover, for those reported alloy, no extra fracture strength or 
hardness was reported, indicating that the influence of the O content on mechanical properties could 
be negligible in the current studied alloy. 
The engineering stress -strain curves of the as -cast CoFeNiTiV and CoFeNiTi-based HEAs [20, 21] 
obtained during compression testing at RT is shown in Fig. 5 a. To guarantee the reliability of 
compressive properties, the compressive test was carried out more than three times. Compressive 
properties of CoFeNiTi -based HEAs at room temperature such as yield strength , ultimate 
compressive strength , hardness and ductility ε are summarized in Table. 3. Regardless of 
preparation methods, the CoFeNiTiV HEA in this work possesses excellent mechanical properties , 
achieving the highest strength without sacrificing ductility. Take CoFeNiTiCr for example , through 
the substitution of Cr with V, the yielding strength is significantly increased from around 1500 MPa 
to 2730 MPa and the ductility of CoFeNiTiV alloy reaches up to 21.3 %, which is much larger than 
around 9% of the CoFeNiTiCr alloy. The ultimate compressive strength of 3890 MPa is achieved for 
the experimental CoFeNiTiV alloy, which is not only much higher than that of the reported 
CoFeNiTiCr alloy with the ultimate strength of 2020 MPa , but also larger than that of other 
CoFeNiTi-base HEAs (Fig. 5b) and almost all 3d TM HEAs. 
The solid solutions formed in HEAs are normally composed of single phase fcc, bcc or hcp solid 
solution or a mixture of these phases[35]. According to Peierls-Nabarro Model, Peierls stresses in bcc 
metals are usually higher than that fcc metals as the dislocation width s in bcc metals are narrower 
than that in fcc metals, and hence , bcc metals have higher yield stress. Therefore, single bcc-
structured HEAs are generally considered to acquire higher strength than that of fcc HEAs. Compared 
with CoFeNiTiCr alloy, the high strength of CoFeNiTiV alloy can be spontaneously attributed to the

primary bcc phase structure. In addition, the bcc matrix of CoFeNiTiV alloy are identified to be CoTi 
and FeNi based on XRD results . It is naturally considered that the solid solution strengthens of V 
atoms played a vital role in improved strength. Besides, unlike element segregation presented in 
CoFeNiTiCr, the elements in the CoFeNiTiV alloy are completely mixed and uniformly distributed in 
the matrix, and solid-solution strengthening is maximized. Moreover, the metallic bonding among the 
alloying elements was also considered to contribute to the remarkable high strength in spite of all the 
reported 3d TM HEAs. Despite the relatively low strength, fcc-structured HEAs usually possess 
attractive ductility owing to sufficient slip systems that can be activated. It is worthwhile to note that 
the ductility of CoFeNiTiV alloy is over twice that of CoFeNiTiCr alloy. This phenomenon might be 
closely related to the different morpholog ical configurations . For instance, all the CoFeNiTiCu, 
CoFeNiTiAl and CoFeNiTiCr alloys present a dendrite structure, which were readily identified as 
dendrite arm and the interdendrit ic region. This kind of structure is caused by compositional 
heterogeneity and inevitably increases the exist ing interfaces. In sharp contrast, the monotonous 
structure of CoFeNiTiV alloy confirmed either by SEM image or EBSD map dramatically indicates a 
more homogenous microstructure and a decrease in interfaces, which is beneficial to prohibit cracking 
nucleation and thereby increase the ductility. 
4. Conclusions 
In summary, a novel single-phase equiatomic CoFeNiTiV HEA was prepared by vacuum arc melting. 
The alloy consists of a typical single bcc phase that is the same as CoFeNiTiAl , while the 
CoFeNiTiCr and CoFeNiTiCu alloys are both composed of a single fcc phase although they are all 
CoFeNiTi based alloys. The effectiveness of VEC has been confirmed in the studied alloy system. 
From the view of the elemental role on microstructure, Ti is beneficial to the formation of compounds, 
while the elemental V, Cu, Al or Cr promote the formation of single-phase solid solution. The yield 
strength reaches 2730 MPa and ultimate compressive strength of CoFeNiTiV alloy is up to 3890 MPa, 
which is the highest of all CoFeNiTi based HEAs, even of all 3d TM HEAs up to date. Meanwhile, it 
exhibits a good ductility of 21.3 %. This excellent combination of strength and ductility in 
CoFeNiTiV alloy might stem from the majority constitution of bcc phase, solid-solution strengthening,
