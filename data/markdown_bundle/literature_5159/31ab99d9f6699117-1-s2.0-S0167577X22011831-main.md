ABSTRACT 
To prepare NiAl sheets with improved high-temperature strength, the V layers with different thicknesses were 
deposited on the single-side of Ni foils by magnetron sputtering and then the hot-pressing of laminated Ni(V)/Al 
foils were carried out. A NiAlV sheet consisting of V precipitates and NiAl matrix was successfully prepared using 
this reaction technique of laminated foils. The NiAlV alloy prepared by microalloying method can obtain better 
mechanical properties (166.4 MPa) and retain considerable fracture strain (54.5 %) at high temperature. 
1. Introduction 
NiAl alloy has been widely investigated as a lightweight high- 
temperature resistant material in the aerospace and automotive [1,2] . 
However, the actual applications of NiAl alloy are hindered due to the 
limited ductility at room temperature and low high-temperature 
strength [3,4] . 
The traditional method of preparing NiAl-based thin-walled com -
ponents with intrinsic brittleness is easy to crack or fracture during the 
cooling process. Poor thermal formability requires higher temperature 
(more than 900 
◦
C) and slower deformation rate (slower than 1 × 10
 2 
s
 1
) to produce NiAl sheets [5] . Thus, the manufacture of NiAl sheets/ 
thin shells is still a huge challenge. But for thin-walled parts, the foil- 
reaction technology of preparing NiAl/TiAl sheets have unique advan -
tage [6] . Fan et al. successfully prepared NiAl sheets by solid – liquid 
reaction, which could significantly reduce the diffusion time of Al foils 
[7] . Yuan at al. used a foil-reaction technology to prepare NiAl thin- 
walled shells [8] . The reaction annealing of alternately stacked Ni/Al 
foils is a novel technique for preparing NiAl sheets, which can avoid the 
direct deformation of brittle NiAl using conventional processes. How -
ever, the high-temperature performance of pure NiAl sheets/thin shells 
is low and cannot meet the performance requirement of practical ap -
plications [9] . Thus, it is proposed to improve the high-temperature 
performance of NiAl by sputtering different content of microalloying 
elements on foil precursors and subsequent reaction of laminated foils. V 
is the only element that can form both pseudo-binary eutectic and 
Heusler phase in NiAl alloy. And V has good mechanical property and 
low density, which is more suitable to be introduced into NiAl matrix as 
microalloying element [10] . 
In this work, NiAlV sheets were successfully prepared using the re -
action annealing of Ni(V)/Al foils, and the Ni(V) foils were obtained by 
magnetron sputtering. Then, different contents of V elements were 
introduced into NiAl alloy. This method can not only fabricate NiAl 
sheet but also improve mechanical properties of NiAl alloys by micro- 
alloying. 
2. Materials and methods 
Ni (60 μ m in thickness) and Al (100 μ m in thickness) foils were cut 
into 100 × 100 mm
2

side of Ni foils to obtain Ni(V) composite foils (Fig. (a1)) using ZXT-500 
equipment. The sputtering time was 1 – 4 h. Finally, alternating stacks of 
Ni(V) foils (14 pieces) and Al foils (13 pieces) were prepared as follows: 
(i) hot pressing at 620 
◦

◦

melting Al foils; and (iii) a further homogeneous reaction process at 
1250 
◦
C/1 h/40 MPa to obtain a homogeneous NiAlV sheet (Fig. (a3)).

3. Results and discussion 
NiAlV alloys with different V content (NiAl-0.27V, NiAl-0.57V, NiAl- 
0.75V and NiAl-1.01V) can be obtained by hot pressing Ni(V) foils and 
Al foils. The experimental details are provide in the Supplemental files . 
V layer greatly hinders the mutual diffusion between Ni and Al foils, so 
the diffusion channel is left on the side of the Ni foil to ensure the rapid 
reaction diffusion process of the Al layer. Fig. 2 shows the microstruc -
ture of the NiAl-1.01V sheet at different reaction stages. The micro -
structure of the prepared laminated Ni(V)/Al foils is shown in Fig. 2 (a). 
It can be seen that the Ni(V) foils and Al foils are closely connected 
through a reaction – diffusion process. There exists two different micro -
structures on both sides of the Ni foils due to the existence of the V layer.

It is documented that the solubility of V in NiAl is ~ 12 % at high 
temperature and the solubility decreases sharply with lowering the 
temperature. During the cooling process, the V element is excluded and 
precipitated from the NiAl matrix. One can see that the main precipitates 
in the NiAlV sheet are V phases and the V phases can improve the high- 
temperature properties of the NiAl matrix.

can also be noted that the improved high-temperature strength of NiAl- 
0.27V sheet with relatively fewer V content is mainly attributed to the 
solution strengthening effect of V, in comparison with pure NiAl. With 
the increasing of V content, the V phases are continuously precipitated 
from the supersaturated NiAl matrix, which significantly improves the 
high-temperature strength of NiAlV alloy with relatively more V content 
by precipitation strengthening.
