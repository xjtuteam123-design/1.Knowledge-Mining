2. Experimental procedures 
By non-consumable tungsten electrode water-cooled copper crucible 
arc melting, five alloy ingots with nominal compositions of 
Nb – 16Si – 20Ti (AC0), Nb – 16Si – 20Ti-0.1ZrC (AC1), Nb – 16Si – 20Ti – 1ZrC 
(AC2), Nb – 16Si – 20Ti – 3ZrC (AC3), and Nb – 16Si – 20Ti – 6ZrC (AC4) 
(atomic percentage, hereafter in at.%) were prepared. The Nb block 
( > 99.95 wt %), Si block ( > 99.99 wt %), Ti block ( > 99.4 wt %), and ZrC 
powder ( > 99.9 wt %) are the raw materials used throughout the 
experiment. The scale of ZrC powder is 300 mesh. Each prefabricated 
alloy ingot is 120 g, smelted six times to reduce segregation. The sample 
prepared by arc melting has a weight loss rate of less than 0.5% (above 
119.8 g), which satisfies the experimental criterion. The ingot size 
prepared by melting is 40 mm in diameter and 13 mm in maximum 
thickness. 
X-ray diffraction (XRD) was used to examine the phase structure and 
crystalline structures of each alloy sample using Cu- K α radiation, at a 
scanning rate of 8
◦

◦

microstructure and mechanical properties from the ingot. The micro -

(SEM) and electron probe micro-analyzer (EPMA, JXA-8230) with wave- 
dispersive spectroscopy (WDS). Table 1 shows the composition of the all 
ingot with wave-dispersive spectroscopy (EPMA, JXA-8230). The size of 
the measured region is 3 μ m × 3 μ m. The results indicate that all alloy 
ingots have essentially the same composition as the nominal composi -
tion. The area fraction (%) of continuous phases in alloy was calculated 
by Image-Pro Plus 6.0 software. Three SEM photos (500 × ) of each alloy 
are used for analysis. 
The size of room-temperature compressed sample is Ø 4 × 6 mm. 
Room-temperature compressive tests were conducted using the Instron 
5569 electronic testing machine with the loading rate of 0.5 mm/min.

(1) AC0 and AC1 alloys consist of Nbss, (Nb,X)
3

3

and Tiss phases. ZrC addition promotes the decomposition of (Nb,

5

1/2

(3) Adding ZrC produces a solid solution strengthening effect and 
increases the compressive strength. As ZrC continues to increase, 
the compressive strength decreases firstly and then increases. The 
compressive strength of AC1 alloy is the highest (2331 MPa), and 
that of AC3 alloy is the lowest (1995 MPa). The fracture strain is 
the opposite of the compressive strength, which increases first 
and then decreases. The fracture strain of AC2 alloy is the highest 
(11.4%).

which increased the room-temperature fracture toughness. The 
fracture mode of Nb–Si–Ti based alloys with ZrC addition is 
brittle quasi-cleavage fracture.
