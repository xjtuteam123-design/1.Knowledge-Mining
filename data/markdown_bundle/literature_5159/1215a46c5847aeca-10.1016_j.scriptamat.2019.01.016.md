medium-entropy alloy produced by high-pressure torsion and annealing

Ultraﬁne-grained materials with nanotwins are expected to produce a remarkable combination of strength and
ductility. In the present study, ultraﬁne-grained CoCrNi medium-entropy alloy with nanotwins is fabricated by
high-pressure torsion followed by annealing; and investigated for cryogenic tensile properties. The alloy exhibits
superior cryogenic tensile properties with a tensile strength of ~2 GPa and tensile strain of ~27%. The cryogenic
tensile strength of ultraﬁne-grained sample increased by 67% as compared to the cryogenic tensile strength of
coarse-grained sample due toﬁne grain size, annealing nanotwins, residual dislocation density, and strong tem-
perature dependence of yield strength.

High-entropy alloys (HEAs) and medium-entropy alloys (MEAs) are
class of advanced materials whose alloy designs are based on multi-
principal elements, and they possess remarkable mechanical properties
over conventional alloys [1,2]. These alloys have attracted wide atten-
tion from researchers due to their remarkable combination of high
strength and ductility, strength retention at a higher temperature, and
exceptional thermal stability [ 3–7]. Earlier studies have shown that
HEAs and MEAs with low stacking fault energy (SFE) exhibit superior
mechanical properties at cryogenic temperature due to the formation
of deformation twins [8–12]. As far as the authors knowledge, most of
these studies are focused on grain size larger than 1 μm and the cryo-
genic properties of ultraﬁne-grained (UFG) HEAs/MEAs have not yet re-
ceived much attention [ 13,14]. It is well known that the grain size
inﬂuences the strengthening behavior of materials based on the Hall-
Petch strengthening, and grain re ﬁnement is one of the effective
methods of strengthening by governing microstructure without altering
the chemical composition. The strengthening effect from grain bound-
aries is reported to be higher for alloys with low SFE [ 15]. Additionally,
annealing nanotwins can be easily formed during annealing of de-
formed alloys with low SFE. These annealing nanotwins can effectively
impede the dislocations movement and can retain the strain hardening
capability [16,17].
Among various HEAs and MEAs, CoCrNi MEA has low SFE
(~22 mJ/m
2) and is reported to have exceptional fracture toughness at
cryogenic temperature [8,18]. Recently, it is reported that the disloca-
tion storage capacity and resistance to plastic instability of CoCrNi
MEA is better than those of the conventional low and medium SFE steels
due to its high twinnability [19]. Thus, the fabrication of low SFE CoCrNi
MEA with ultra-ﬁne grain size (UFG) and annealing nanotwins can lead
to a good combination of strength and ductility at cryogenic
temperature.
Severe plastic deformation followed by annealing is one of the effec-
tive methods to produce a microstructure with UFG and annealing
nanotwins in materials with low SFE [ 17,20,21]. Accordingly, in the
present study, CoCrNi MEA with UFG and annealing nanotwins was fab-
ricated by high-pressure torsion (HPT) and annealing; and its cryogenic
tensile properties were investigated.
UFG CoCrNi MEA is fabricated by HPT at 6 GPa pressure and 5 turns,
followed by annealing at 600 °C for 60 min. The detailed fabrication pro-
cedure can be found in our earlier publication [22]. The miniature dog-
bone shaped tensile samples with dimensions of 1.5 mm gauge length
and 1 mm gauge width were cut from the HPT disc using electric dis-
charge machining. The cryogenic tensile test was performed at a strain
rate of 10
−3 s−1 using Instron equipment and custom-made liquid nitro-
gen chamber. The strain from the cross-head displacement was corrected
with a correction factor. To estimate the correction factor, room tempera-
ture tensile tests were carried out for a range of strain from 0.1 to 0.8. The

annealing nanotwins can be explained based on the Hall-Petch
strengthening, which indicates the strength increases when the disloca-
tion mean-free path decreases. High density of dislocations observed
near to the nanotwins in Fig. 3c clearly indicates the effective blocking
of dislocation motion by twin boundaries. The residual dislocation in
the initial microstructure can contribute to strengthening by acting as
a barrier to dislocation motion. Finally, strong temperature dependence
of yield strength is reported for FCC based HEAs/MEAs [34]. The ratio of
yield strength (YSR) at cryogenic temperature to room temperature can
represent the nature of strengthening and deformation mechanisms
[35,36]. High YSR of ~1.4 to 2.0 in CoCrNi MEA in the present study
(Table 1 ) indicates the strong temperature dependence of yield
strength. The yield strength at any given temperature can be expressed
as

The grain boundaries, twin boundaries, and dislocation-dislocation in-
teractions are long-range barriers contributing to theσath.T h ed e c r e a s e
in YSR with decreasing grain size in CoCrNi MEA (Table 1) indicates the
increased strengthening contribution from athermal barriers (grain
boundaries) to the dislocation motion [35,37]. It is to be noted that the
magnitude of increase in yield strength at cryogenic temperature as
compared to the room temperature is not signi ﬁcant with different
grain sizes, indicating the temperature dependence of yield strength is
not signiﬁcantly affected by grain size. A similar observation has been
reported for FCC HEAs/MEAs [ 34,38]. The temperature dependence of
yield stress is greatly determined by theσth. In general, pure FCC metals
exhibit weak temperature dependence of yield strength due to the neg-
ligible contribution from σth. However, the addition of solute atoms is
reported to increase the σth and show strong temperature dependence
of yield strength in FCC based binary alloys [ 34]. It is shown that the
Peierls potentials in FCC HEAs/MEAs are stronger than those in conven-
tional FCC metals leading to strong temperature dependence of yield
strength in HEAs/MEAs [ 38]. Recently, CoCrNi MEA is reported to ex-
hibit very high friction stress among various FCC conventional alloys
and HEAs due to the ﬂuctuation of Peierls potential for the dislocation
motion [26]. Thus, the high friction stress and its strong temperature de-
pendence of yield strength can result in higher yield strength at cryo-
genic temperature than at room temperature. In addition, the σth can
be greatly inﬂuenced by activation volume [36]. In pure nanocrystalline
(NC) FCC metals, ultra-high cryogenic strength is attributed to the very
large σth resulting from the low activation volume in the order of 10 b3
[36,39,40]. Interestingly, a very low activation volume of ~10–100 b3 is
reported for CG and NC HEAs/MEAs, unlike conventional FCC alloys
[35,41,42]. This activation volume can be much smaller for UFG HEAs/
MEAs at cryogenic temperature. This could be one of the reasons for
very high cryogenic strength observed in the present study. A more de-
tailed studies are required to understand the thermal activation process.
The reasonable strain to failure with high cryogenic strength in UFG
sample can be explained based on the initial microstructure and the for-
mation of deformation twins. The presence of annealing nano-twins in
the initial condition and the deformation twins can effectively prevent
the dislocation motion and dynamic recovery to some extent, leading
to a reasonable strain to failure [ 28]. In addition, the tensile curve of
UFG sample shows strain hardening at the initial stage of plastic defor-
mation followed by softening and then hardening to some extent. The
small strain hardening at lower strain indicates the generation of dislo-
cations followed by saturation. The dislocation saturation at very low
strain could be due to the ﬁne grain size and presence of residual dislo-
cation in the initial material. A similar phenomenon of softening
followed by hardening has been reported in multiscale architectured
steel with a gradient in grain size processed by HPT and annealing
[43]. It is reported that the dislocation activity gets saturated ﬁrst in
the ﬁne grain size and the dislocation activity continues to exhibit in
the coarser grains leading to a unique phenomenon of softening
followed by hardening. In another study, the deformation twins are re-
ported to occur at a very low strain in CoCrNi alloy with a grain size of 16
μm[ 18]. It is possible that the deformation twins occur at a lower strain
in the present study, contributing to a slight hardening after softening
behavior. Thus, the slight hardening observed after the softening could
be attributed to the formation of deformation twins or due to the pres-
ence of dislocations in ﬁner grains and dislocation free coarse grains.
The increased hardening could result in increased resistance to crack
propagation after softening and lead to an increase in strain-to-failure
[43].
In summary, the UFG CoCrNi MEA produced by HPT and annealing
shows extremely high cryogenic tensile strength with a reasonable
strain to failure of 27%. The high yield and tensile strength at cryogenic
temperature are attributed to the ﬁne grain size, annealing nanotwins,
residual dislocation density, and high friction stress. The presence of re-
sidual dislocation density leads to the earlier dislocation saturation and
limited uniform elongation. The formation of high density of stacking
faults/deformation twins due to low SFE of CoCrNi MEA increased the
resistance to crack propagation and thereby resulted in high strain to
failure.
