Abstract
Extrusion as a processing route for a multielement Nb–Ti–Al alloy is the basis for this study correlating the microstructure at
a subsequent processing step with a particular mechanical property. An 8 cm diameter extrusion was produced from an
industrial-scale ingot of this high strength, bcc-solid solution alloy. In the as extruded at 1100°C condition, the so-called van Gogh
sky (VGS) microstructure is obtained as a result of plastic instabilities and the accompanying segregation which occurred during
extrusion. The changes in its appearance, after a subsequent processing step (heat treatment) and the accompanying grain
structure and precipitate (identiﬁed as TiC) provide the microstructure that is linked to a particular mechanical property. These
are fatigue crack growth and fracture toughness, plane-strain fracture and tensile properties. Re-extruding the 8 cm diameter to
2.5 cm diameter and followed by additional processing to 1.6 cm diameter left only a small remnant of the VGS pattern in the
primary extrusion. A more uniform, recrystallized grain size occurs and the nature and distribution of the carbides are shown with
their identity conﬁrmed as cubic MC. This microstructure from the complete processing is correlated with tensile test results.

1. Introduction
There are bcc-base solid solution Nb–Ti alloys that
are ductile systems within the spectrum of advanced
intermetallic materials [1]. Our introductory paper [2]
on tensile property and fracture characteristics of a
44 Nb–35 Ti–6 Al–5 Cr–8V–IW–0.5 Mo-0.5 Hf
(at%) alloy produced the highest yield strength and
elongation up to 1000°C (in air) for any alloy in this
category. A sequel paper [3] on second-tier mechani-
cal properties revealed fatigue crack growth resistance
and plane-strain fracture toughness that will accom-
modate damage tolerance with respect to intrinsic or
service generated defects.
If any alloy within this spectrum of advanced inter-
metallic materials is to be considered for use as a
matrix material, a thorough understanding of the pro-
cessing-microstructure-property relationship will be re-
quired. Employing extrusion as the primary
processing route, this paper links the microstructure
at a selected processing step with a particular me-
chanical property. Thus, results of this three-link
chain for performance are shown for a ductile alloy
that was produced in the largest quantity (to date)
from commercial available materials and processing.
In large part, the paper is a compilation of results
contributed by individuals who included this alloy
within their own on-going study of a particular sub-
ject, and they are listed in the text and acknowledge-
ment.

1. A VGS structure is observed in the 8 cm diameter
extruded at 1100°C as a result of plastic ﬂow instabili-
ties which originate from the dendrite segregation and
local variations in chemical composition. The analyses
of the principal elements in the ingot that was extruded
to 8 cm diameter showed no macrosegregation and
microprobe analysis of their distribution were quite
acceptable.
2. The VGS structure appears as a series of dark and
light bands in a longitudinal section of the extrusion
held for 48 h at 1000°C. In the transverse direction,
subtle changes in VGS contrast occur after treatments
of 48 h at 800°C and 84 h at 1000°C. The dark areas
consist of precipitate particles and associated ﬁne grains
of 2–5 mm. Selected area diffraction patterns of the
precipitate after 48 h at 800°C identify it as typical
cubic TiC with a lattice parameter of 4.28 A ,.
3. A processing step of annealing the extrusion for 4
h at 1000°C produced a microstructure of unrecrystal-
lized grains and VGS pattern. This combination was
correlated with fatigue crack growth behavior, an im-
