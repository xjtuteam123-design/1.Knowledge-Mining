ABSTRACT 
Usually, Co-based superalloys contain a high fraction of W and/or Mo. The refractory elements stabilize the 
precipitate phase, but cause a high density. In this work, new L1
2
-phase hardened, low-density CoNiCr-based 
superalloys were developed with the assistance of CALPHAD calculations. Several alloys were studied experi -
mentally and their microstructures, elemental distributions, lattice parameters and the lattice misfit between the 
γ and γ
′
phases were evaluated using scanning electron microscopy, transmission electron microscopy, atom 
probe tomography and high energy X-ray diffraction. The alloys exhibit a very high misfit, good phase stability 
and excellent mechanical strength. It was found by hardness tests that a two-step heat treatment improves the 
strength of the new alloys even further. In comparison with conventional Co-based superalloys, Co – Al – W based 
superalloys and Co – Ti based superalloys, as well as Ni-based superalloys, i.e. Udimet 720Li, they have a low mass 
density and high yield stress. They also overcome the problem of a generally low lattice misfit of previous L1
2
- 
phase hardened CoNiCr-based superalloys with high Ni and Cr contents. As a result, they show cuboidal-shaped 
precipitates similar to Co – Al – W and Co – Al – Mo based superalloys. 
1. Introduction 
Since the presence of a L1
2
-phase in the ternary Co – Al – W system was 
reported by Sato et al. in 2006 [ 1 ], it has attracted a great deal of 
attention from the superalloys community even though Lee already re -
ported this phase in 1973 [ 2 ]. Novel γ / γ
′
Co-based superalloys based on 
the formation of this L1
2 
( γ
′
) phase, as a hardening constituent, exhibit 
excellent mechanical properties, similar to Ni-based superalloys that are 
used for high temperature gas turbine applications [ 3 , 4 ]. They also show 
a high solidus and a low solvus temperature of the hardening phase, 
which provides large processing windows for heat treatments and 
thermo-mechanical processing [ 5 ]. Many researchers have already 
taken great effort to increase solvus temperature of the γ
′
phase [ 6 – 8 ]. 
However, there are still several critical questions that need to be solved 
to qualify these alloys for actual applications, in particular their high 
density. Although the creep properties and yield stresses are already 
competitive to comparable Ni-based superalloys [ 2 ], there is still room 
for improvement. 
In the Co – Al – W system, the large amount of W required to stabilize γ
′
precipitates increases the density to unacceptably high values ( > 9.5 g/ 
cm
3
) compared to Ni-based superalloys [ 5 ]. Therefore, in order to obtain 
lower-density alloys, W-free L1
2
-hardened Co-based superalloys have 
been developed, i.e. Co – 10Al – 5Mo – 2Nb [ 9 , 10 ] ( > 8.36 g/cm
3
), which 
replace W with Mo and Nb. In addition, the Co – Ti based systems also 
came to people ’ s attention, as their properties can be improved by 
alloying with Cr and V [ 11 ]. In particular one alloy within the ternary 
Co – Ti – Cr system showed excellent yield stress and a low density (8.10 
g/cm
3
) [ 12 ], but its solvus temperature (1135 
◦
C) is very close to its 
solidus temperature (1153 
◦
C). The resulting narrow processing window 
limits its technical applicability. In general, no ternary alloy system has 
been found to date which exhibited a suitable set of properties so that it 
can be applied directly. Thus, it is necessary to investigate more complex 
alloy systems to obtain alloys with a balance of acceptable density, 
sufficient oxidation and fatigue resistance, high creep strength and yield 
stress. Lass et al. [ 13 ] recently tried to decrease the mass density while 
increasing the solvus temperature of Co-based γ / γ
′
alloys by adding Ni, 
Al, Ti, Ta, W, Mo and Nb. Cr additions to ternary alloys are beneficial for 
oxidation resistance and lower the misfit between the γ and γ
′
phase [ 14 ] 
but destabilize γ
′
[ 15 ]. Therefore, in order to overcome the trade-off 
between high oxidation resistance and a stable microstructure,

2. Experimental methods 
Alloys with nominal compositions Co – 15Cr – 5Al – 5Ti (at.%), 
Co – 15Ni – 15Cr – 5Al – 5Ti (at.%), Co – 30Ni – 30Cr – 5Al – 5Ti (at.%), 
Co – 30Ni – 15Cr – 5Al – 5Ti (Coalloy-5Ti, at.%) and Co – 30Ni – 15Cr 
– 5Al – 5Ti – 2Ta (Coalloy-2Ta, at.%) were arc melted at least 7 times under 
argon in the form of 70 g ingots on a water-cooled copper hearth using a 
laboratory-scale vacuum arc melting unit. The final compositions of 
these alloys as measured by SEM-EDS are given in Table 1 . The as-cast 
alloy Co – 30Ni – 15Cr – 5Al – 5Ti – 2Ta was homogenized at 1200 
◦
C for 
48 h and the other alloys at 1250

Subsequently, the homogenized alloys Co – 30Ni – 15Cr – 5Al – 5Ti (Coal -
loy-5Ti) and Co – 30Ni – 15Cr – 5Al – 5Ti – 2Ta (Coalloy-2Ta) were aged at 
900 
◦
C in air for 5 h, 30 h, 220 h, and 1080 h and subsequently air cooled 
to room temperature. 
The microstructures were characterized by backscattered electron 
(BSE) microscopy in a scanning electron microscope (FE-SEM Gemini, 
Zeiss, Germany). For a quantitative analysis of the microstructure the 
ImageJ software package [ 26 ] was used to binarise the SEM images to 
distinguish between the γ and γ
′
phases. The determined area fraction of 
the γ
′
phase is assumed to be equal to the volume fraction of the γ
′
phase. 
For TEM sample preparation, 3 mm disks from the heat-treated 
specimens were cut and mechanically polished to a thickness of 
around 75 μ m. The specimens were made electron transparent by using a 
twin jet electro polishing unit with a solution of methanol and 10 vol% 
perchloric acid at 10 
◦
C and 30 V. A Philips CM200 TEM operated at 
200 kV was used to investigate details of the microstructure. To accu -
rately resolve [001] lattice planes in later APT investigations, grains 
with [001] orientation were identified using electron backscatter 
diffraction (EBSD). Wedge-shaped bars were cut and lifted out from 
those grains using a dual beam focused-ion-beam (FIB) instrument 
(Helios NanoLab 600i). The bars were attached to commercial flat-top Si 
micro tips and subsequently ion-milled into a needle-shape for APT 
analysis. The specimens were cleaned by a final milling step at 5 kV 
acceleration voltage of the ion beam at a current of 41 pA to remove 
surface regions severely damaged by Ga implantation from the prior FIB 
milling steps. APT analyses were performed using a CAMECA LEAP ™ 
5000XS system in laser pulsed mode at a specimen base temperature of 
40 K. A laser pulse frequency of 200 kHz and a pulse energy of 40 pJ 
were applied. Data analysis was performed with the IVAS ™ software 
version 3.6.14. 
Lattice parameters in aged samples were determined by high energy 
X-ray diffraction (HEXRD, λ = 0.124 Å) at the synchrotron beamline 
HEMS run by Helmholtz-Zentrum Hereon at the PETRA III storage ring 
of the Deutsches Elektronen-Synchrotron (DESY, Hamburg, Germany). 
As Co-based superalloys specimens usually consisted of only few rather 
large grains, the specimens were rotated ± 40
◦
during the measure -
ments (i.e. integrating over a range of rocking angles). In order to ach -
ieve better statistics, measurements were performed at 3 different 
positions for each specimen. The {200}-reflections were fitted with 
three Pseudo-Voigt functions (a linear combination of Gauss and Lorentz 
profile) in MATLAB R2020a and lattice parameters of the γ and γ
′
phases 
were obtained after deconvolution of the overlapped functions. To 
consider the tetragonal distortion of the γ phase in the matrix channels, 
the γ phase was fitted with 2 sub-peaks [ 27 ]. 
The hardness of the samples was measured using a Vickers hardness 
tester with a load of 200 N at room temperature. Each hardness value 
reported is the average of five measurements and the area of the indent 
covered several hundred γ
′
precipitates. Compression tests to measure 
the 0.2% flow stress of the alloys were performed using a strain- 
controlled closed-loop machine MTS 810 (MTS System Cooperation) 
operated at a strain rate of 10
-4 
s
-1 
both at room temperature and at high 
temperatures, i.e. 600 
◦

◦
C, 750 
◦

◦

◦

Compression specimens had a gauge length of 7.5 mm and a diameter of 
5 mm and had been machined from heat treated material. The sample 
deformation was monitored and controlled by an extensometer.

strengthen an alloy at high temperature. Other alloys retain a certain

The newly developed alloys not only exhibit comparable yield 
strength as cast Udimet 720Li but also a similar density. It is noteworthy 
that an addition of B and C alloyed like in Udimet 720Li and a reduced 
grain size could further improve the mechanical properties. The good 
mechanical properties of the newly developed alloys are achieved by the 
adjustment of a high positive misfit by increasing the Ti/Al ratio. This 
allows for high contents of Cr, which is necessary for oxidation resis -
tance, while retaining a significant amount of lattice misfit. For example, 
the degree of misfit in alloys with reduced density but low Ti/Al ratio, 
Co – 30Ni – 10Al – 5Mo – 2Ta – 2Ti for example, decreased to values close to 
zero when higher amounts of Cr were alloyed for oxidation resistance 
[ 14 ]. Until now, there is no similar report on Co – Al – W- and 
Co – Al – Mo-based superalloys. 
5. Conclusions 
New low-density γ
′
strengthened CoNiCr-based superalloys were 
developed with the assistance of the CALPHAD method and transition 
temperatures, microstructures, lattice misfits and mechanical properties 
were characterized. The predictions from CALPHAD are almost consis -
tent with experimental results in terms of phase constitution and sta -
bility. In addition, the new alloys exhibit a lower mass density compared 
to Co – Al – W-based superalloys (8.03 g/cm
3 
for Coalloy-5Ti as compared 
to 9.4 g/cm
3 
for Co – 9Al – 9W). 
The new alloys have a high lattice misfit (around 1%) despite being 
alloyed with higher levels of Ni and Cr. This demonstrates that the usual 
trend of a decreasing lattice misfit in γ
′
-strengthened CoNiCr-based su -
peralloys if Ni and especially Cr are alloyed can be overcome by an 
overall balanced alloy composition. Furthermore, high coherency stress 
caused by high lattice misfit in the newly developed alloy with Ta 
addition seem to block dislocations from precipitate shearing during 
high temperature deformation and to contribute to a high strength.
