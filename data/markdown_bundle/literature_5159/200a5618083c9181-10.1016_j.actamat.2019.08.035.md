Abstract: The effect of crystal orientation on fretting fati gue induced crack initiation and 
dislocation distribution is studied by in-situ SEM observation and electron back-scattered 
diffraction (EBSD) in this paper. Cracks and slip lines are observed in the fretting contact area 
of Ni-based single-crystal (NBSX) superalloys. The in-situ SEM observation captures 
different crack and slip line behaviors under different crystal orientations. The EBSD analysis 
results show obvious misorientation and orientation deviation in the fretting contact area. For 
both crystal orientations, the geometrically necess ary dislocation (GND) density distributions 
in the contact area are obtained by using Hough-bas ed EBSD methods. The peak position of 
grain reference orientation deviation (GROD) and GN D density matches with the fretting 
fatigue crack formation position. EBSD analysis sho ws that the dislocation density 
distribution on each slip system is closely related to the crack initiation direction. The 
direction of slip system with the maximum dislocation density agrees with the crack initiation 
direction obtained by in-situ observation. 
Keywords: fretting fatigue; EBSD; crystal orientation; geome trically necessary dislocation; 
Ni-based single-crystal superalloys

1. Introduction 
Fretting refers to the phenomenon that micro-scale relative motion occurs between the 
contact surfaces when subjected to oscillatory load s [1-6]. Fretting contact can lead to high 
stress concentration at the edge of the contact zon e, which can cause the fatigue crack 
initiation under the reciprocating load [4, 7-10]. The whole process including crack initiation 
and propagation until the final fracture is defined as fretting fatigue [11]. In plain fatigue the 
specimen is subjected to a uniaxial tensile stress, and in indentation test the material is 
subjected to compressive force, while in fretting f atigue there are both normal and alternating 
tangential forces on the contact surface at the sam e time (Fig. 1(b)(c)(d)). So fretting fatigue 
is a typical multiaxial loading problem [12]. 
 
Fig. 1. A sketch map of (a) blade-disk joint in aero-engine, (b) fretting fatigue, (c) plain 
fatigue, and (d) indentation. 
Fretting fatigue is one of the important causes of structural damage in a wide range of 
industries [5, 13-15]. The dovetail joint between the turbine blade and disk in aero-engine is a 
typical fretting fatigue case (Fig. 1(a)) [16-18]. Fretting can cause the turbine blade to break at 
the contact area, which may lead to engine failure and even aviation accidents [19-22]. 
According to the statistics from the United States Air Force, fretting fatigue accounts for over

## Page 5

1/6 of fatigue-induced failures in aero-engines [19 , 23]. Therefore, the research on fretting 
fatigue performance of turbine blade has attracted more and more attentions in recent years. 
Nickel-based single-crystal (NBSX) superalloys are widely used in manufacturing of 
advanced turbine blades for their excellent mechani cal properties at high temperature. Due to 
the elimination of grain boundary which is an impor tant potential crack initiation source, 
NBSX superalloys have excellent resistance to fatig ue damage, while its fretting fatigue 
properties has become the major concern of various academic institutions and industries [24, 
25]. 
The experimental research on fretting fatigue was b egun by Ruiz [26], and developed by 
Hills [27], Nowell [28], Fouvry [3], Mutoh and Neu [29, 30] et al [18, 31-35]. The in-situ 
SEM experimental technique has been paid more and m ore attention because it can capture 
the realtime microstructure evolution on material s urface [36-38]. However, in-situ fretting 
fatigue device is difficult to design because two k inds of loads need to be applied in different 
directions within the SEM vacuum chamber [39]. Muto h and Nishida et al. improved the 
macroscopic bridge fretting fatigue experimental se tup to perform in-situ SEM observations 
[40, 41]. They studied the fretting wear properties of Ti-based alloys with coating and 
observed the wear extent of the materials. However, there is still a lack of research on the 
in-situ SEM observation on the microscopic plastic deformation mechanism of fretting fatigue 
in NBSX superalloys. 
Considering the anisotropy of NBSX superalloys, the crystal orientation has a significant 
influence on crystallographic slip behaviors and fr etting fatigue crack initiation properties 
[42-49], which is especially important for the indu strial production and application of turbine 
blades. Arakere et. al. reported that significant d ifference exists in the crack formation 
positions for NBSX blades with different crystal or ientations under the same fretting fatigue 
load [50]. In addition, Yue et. al. also found that the crystal orientation also has a significant 
effect on the fretting fatigue crack direction [51] . However, the mechanism of crystal 
orientation effect on fretting fatigue is still lac king, especially the analysis of microstructure 
and micromechanism is still in its infancy. 
The fretting fatigue crack initiation behavior is often accompanied by strong local plastic 
deformation, which will distort the original flat c rystal lattice and result in the orientation

## Page 6

deviation and intragranular misorientation [52-55] . In order to evaluate the misorientation, 
several common parameters such as the kernel averag ed misorientation (KAM) and grain 
reference orientation deviation (GROD) have been pr oposed [56-58]. Researchers have 
showed GROD and KAM are closely related to plastic deformation and dislocation density in 
both tension and creep cases [56, 58-62]. D. Kobaya shi conducted EBSD observation to 
obtain the GROD distribution after tension and cree p experiments of IN 738LC Ni-based 
superalloys [59-61]. Further, the GROD distribution has been widely used for the grain 
boundary micro-cracking analysis [63], fracture mod es identification [62], fatigue load 
prediction [56] and creep damage evaluation [58]. 
In the aspect of applying EBSD analysis to fretting fatigue, Huang et al. performed 
EBSD analysis on the contact zone in NBSX after the fretting fatigue experiment, and found 
that there was orientation deviation in the contact zone [64]. Swalla et al. obtained the pole 
figure (PF) of the contact zone, and the results al so showed that plastic deformation occurred 
in the contact area [65]. Wang et al. reported the inverse pole figure (IPF) result that the 
fretting process induced apparent orientation devia tion on the contact surface of Inconel 600 
alloy [66]. Although considerable effort has been m ade to study the orientation deviation of 
fretting fatigue, most of them are qualitative anal ysis, while the quantification of EBSD data 
and the effect of crystal orientation on the misorientation evolution need further research. 
On the other hand, the geometrically necessary disl ocation (GND, Fig. 2) density can be 
calculated from EBSD misorientation data with the h elp of continuum dislocation theory 
[67-69]. Kysar [67] and Demir [70] investigated the effects of indenter angle and size on the 
GND density distribution, and the results showed th at the GND density significantly 
increased when the indenter angle or size was large r. Littlewood and Wilkinson [71] studied 
the GND distribution evolution in Ti-6Al-4V in inte rrupted plain fatigue test, and a -type 
GNDs were found to decrease during continued cyclic loading. Mayama et. al. [72] simulated 
the GND density evolution during the cyclic loading. Zhao and Dunne et al. [73] obtained the 
GND development of Fe-based alloys in bend-testing, and found fine precipitates could 
promote the development of GNDs. Wallis [74] and Ru ggles [75] et al. have reported GND 
results by using HR EBSD which can provide a higher resolution (<0.01°). However, the

application of GND analysis on fretting fatigue is still lacked.

distribution, two contact orientations are chosen ( Fig.4(b)). The specimen with [110] contact

3.3. EBSD sample preparations and instruments 
After the fretting fatigue experiment, the specimen s need to be carefully polished for 
EBSD observation. Apart from the previous grinding by 600#, 800# and 1200# sandpapers for 
in-situ SEM observation, the polishing solution was used here to polish the surface for 30min 
on the Buehler AutoMet 250® polisher. The polishing solution contained 3um, 2um, 1um, 
0.5um diamond particles, respectively, that help to remove all small scratches. Finally, on a 
Buehler VibroMet 2® vibratory polisher, the specime ns were polished in an acidic polishing 
solution containing silica particles of 200 nm size for 2 hours. Thus, the residual stress 
generated during the surface polishing process coul d be released completely due to the 
mechanical-chemical joint effect. 
The EBSD observation instrument used in this paper is the TESCAN MIRA 3 LMH® 
SEM, equipped with a Nordly II® EBSD camera produce d by Oxford Instruments. Aztec® 
and EDAX/TSL-OIM Analysis 6.2® are used to obtain c rystal orientation data (in the format 
of Euler angles). 
 
4. Results and Discussions 
4.1. In-situ SEM observation results 
Fig. 6 shows the in-situ SEM observation results of orientation X and Y . In the aspect of 
crack formation position, for both orientation X an d Y the cracks initiate at the edge of 
fretting contact zone, this is related to the high stress concentration at the edge of the contact

## Page 14

zone [21, 49]. For orientation X, in addition to the main crack which initiates form the contact 
surface, there is a subsurface crack which has a ne ar 18 µm distance away from the contact 
surface (Fig. 6(a)). Slip lines are observed at the fretting contact area. For orientation Y , there 
is only one main crack, but crack branching occurs along the main crack. Slip lines distribute 
at the crack tip, and the crack propagates along th e direction of slip line, this indicates the 
crystallographic slip is the main deformation mechanism of fretting fatigue of NBSX [39, 92].

and propagate in a straight direction, which is almost perpendicular to the contact surface. But 
for orientation Y , the crack and slip line form and propagate in a 45° zigzag direction. This 
indicates the material crystal orientation has an i mportant effect on the crack behaviors in

5. Conclusion 
In this paper, the crystal orientation effect on fretting fatigue induced crack initiation and 
dislocation distribution of NBSX is studied by in-s itu SEM observation and EBSD analysis. 
The GROD, KAM and IPF results show there is obvious misorientation and orientation 
deviation in fretting contact area. This indicates fretting contact can induce significant plastic 
deformation and locally recrystallization. The dist ribution of GND density at the contact area 
is obtained. The peak value of GROD and GND occurs at the edge of contact area, which is in 
agreement with the crack formation position in in-situ observations. 
For orientation X and Y samples, cracks both form a nd propagate along the slip systems 
which have the maximum dislocation density. According to the dislocation density results, the 
dominate slip systems of orientation X and Y samples are different, which is closely related to 
the differences of crack initiation directions observed by in-situ SEM.
