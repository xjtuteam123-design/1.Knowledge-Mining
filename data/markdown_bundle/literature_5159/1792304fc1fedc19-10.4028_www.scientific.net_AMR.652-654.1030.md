Effects of Si, Cu and Mg on the high-temperature mechanical properties 
of Al-Si-Cu-Mg alloy 
Zhang Wenda 1,a , Yang Jing , Dang Jingzhi, Liu Yun, Xu Hong 
1School of Materials Science and Engineering, North University of China, Taiyuan 030051, China. 
azwdno@139.com 
Keywords: Al-Si-Cu-Mg alloy; orthogonal array; high-temperature mechanical property. 
Abstract. The regression equation of the relationship betwee n Si, Cu and Mg and the mechanical 
properties of Al-Si-Cu-Mg alloy was established acc ording to the orthogonal experimental results. 
The microstructure of the Al-Si-Cu-Mg alloy was ana lyzed with scanning electron microscopy and 
energy dispersive spectroscopy. The results show th at Si, Cu and Mg affected the mechanical 
properties (tensile strength and elongation rate) a t 250 ˚C most significantly, minimally and 
negatively, respectively. The interactions between Cu, Mg and Si greatly reduced the 
high-temperature tensile strength owing to the form ation of brittle and hard intermetallic 
Al 5Mg 8Cu 2Si 6 that behaved as the initial crack during stretching. 
Introduction 
With the growing awareness of environmental issues, cast Al-Si alloy has been widely applied in the 
automobile industry owing to the high specific stre ngth, excellent casting performance and low 
coefficient of thermal expansion. Particularly, veh icle parts, such as engine case and cylinder head, 
are subject to complicated mechanical and thermal l oads as well as severe working conditions. 
Therefore, their high-temperature mechanical properties should be improved. 
The casting of Al-Si-Cu-Mg alloy is affected by the contents of Cu, Mg and Si, heat treatment 
process, cooling rate, and etc. Many previous literatures have reported the influence of single variable 
on the microstructure and performance of cast Al-Si alloy [1-3]. The performance of Al-Si-Cu-Mg 
alloy is mainly influenced by the morphology, distribution and solidification of eutectic Si particles in 
the matrix as well as the Mg-rich and Cu-rich phases precipitated after solution aging [4]. The tensile 
strength of Al-Si-Cu-Mg alloy can be elevated from 152 MPa to 402 MPa by increasing the content of 
Cu from 1 wt.% to 6 wt.% [5]. Shabestari [6] found that the mechanical properties of Al-Si-Mg alloy 
were optimal in the presence of 1.5 wt.% Cu.Cu boosts the strength and fatigue performance of alloys, 
but excessive Cu expands the crystallization temperature range and worsens the casting performance, 
leading to defects (e.g. hot crack, shrinkage porosity) during sand casting [7]. However, Si reduces the 
solidus temperature, improves liquidity, enhances t he feeding capacity and prevents hot cracks. 
Besides, Mg strengthens alloys but reduces the plasticity of them. 
In this paper, nine groups of experiments were arra nged using the orthogonal design method, a 
quantitative relationship between various elements (Si, Cu, Mg) and high temperature mechanical 
properties has been established using the response surface regression analysis, which provides a 
candidate design method for high strength-toughness casting Al-Si-Cu-Mg alloys.

Analysis and discussion 
The high-temperature strength of the elements in Al alloy is related to the nature of solid solution a s 
well as the morphology, structure, quantity and thermal stability of the second phase. The GP zone in 
the solid solution dramatically elevates the room-temperature strength, whereas the strength of alloys 
is dominated by the second phase instead of the GP zone at high temperature (e.g. 250 ˚C). It has been 
previously reported that the high-temperature hardness and thermal stability of solid solutions and the 
second phase are affected by the self-diffusion coe fficients of alloying elements or their diffusion 
coefficients in Al, which determine the high-temperature strength eventually. 
A high-diffusion-coefficient element forms low-hard ness intermetallics at high temperatures, 
which declines rapidly as the loading time is extended. Mg is of distinct short-term high-temperature 
strength. Although the diffusion coefficient of Cu is lower, it is prone to forming thick Al 
2Cu 
intermetallic blocks and brittle AlSiCuMg phase wit h Si and Mg (Fig. 3), the corresponding 
components are summarized in Table 2. The softer ma trix at high temperature becomes the initial 
crack. Si in Al-Si binary alloy hardly dissolves in the matrix and exists as brittle eutectic or prima ry 
crystal Si, which weakens the strength of Al alloy. θ(A1 2Cu) phase rather than ternary compounds 
forms in Al-Si alloy upon the addition of Cu. A ternary reaction only occurs at 525 ˚C: L → α(A1) + Si 
+ θ(A1 2Cu) [8]. 
The lattice constants of Cu and Al differ significa ntly, but Cu dissolves in Al. Therefore, Cu 
distorts the lattice of Al in Al-Si-Mg alloy which is drastically strengthened as a result. With 
increasing Cu content, the strength and the elongation rate sharply increases and declines, respectively 
[9]. However, the elements interact in the multi-el ement alloy system, which directly impact the 
mechanical properties of Al alloy. Mg allows the formation of Cu-Mg intermetallic Al 5Mg 8Cu 2Si 6 by 
a complicated eutectic reaction in the final stage of solidification, yielding a low-melting-point 
eutectic structure. For instance, the reaction at 5 08 ˚C can be expressed as: 
Al+Al 2Cu+Si+Al 5Mg 8Cu 2Si 6 → Liq [9]. 
 
 
Conclusions 
1) In the test of short-term high-temperature mechanical performance, Mg, Cu and Si affected the 
tensile strength most significantly, minimally and negatively, respectively. Tensile strength 
deteriorated evidently due to the interaction between Cu and Mg. 
2) Mg influenced the elongation rate most significa ntly, whereas Si and Cu both influenced the 
elongation rate negatively. The interaction between Si, Cu and Mg produced brittle Al5Mg8Cu2Si6 
phase, which became the initial crack during high-temperature stretching.
