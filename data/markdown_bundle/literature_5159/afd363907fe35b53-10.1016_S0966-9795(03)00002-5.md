Microstructure and high temperature mechanical properties of
mechanically alloyed Nb3Al-based materials
A. Dolla´ra,*, S. Dymekb

Abstract
The microstructure and high temperature mechanical properties of mechanically alloyed Nb3Al-based materials have been
investigated. The results for four binary Nb–Al and three ternary Nb–Al–V materials are presented. The microstructure of the
present alloys consists of four phases: a disordered Nb solid solution, the Nb3Al and Nb2Al ordered phases and aluminum oxide
dispersoids. All alloys exhibit high strength at 800/C14C, but only ternary alloys retain the strength up to at least 1100/C14C. The pre-
sence of vanadium, with its atomic diameter signiﬁcantly smaller than those of Nb and Al, is postulated to lead to the solid solution
strengthening. In the ternary alloy Nb–15 at.% Al–20 at.% V consolidated at 1500
/C14C the creep rates are signiﬁcantly lower (one to
two orders of magnitude) than in all other alloys investigated in the present study because of the absence of the Nb2Al phase in its
microstructure. Creep rate dependence on temperature in the present alloys is controlled by the diﬀusivity of Nb and Al atoms (and
V atoms in the ternary alloys) in the ordered Nb
3Al phase and the creep mechanism is diﬀusion-assisted dislocation creep.

5. Discussion
5.1. Microstructure
Four phases were identiﬁed in the microstructure of
the binary alloys: Nb solid solution (Nb
ss), the Nb3Al
phase with the cubic A15 crystalline structure, /C27-type
Nb2Al tetragonal phase and aluminum oxides Al2O3.
The presence of the solid solution Nbss and the Nb3Al
phase was predicted based on phase stability studies of
Nb–Al binary system conducted by Suyama et al.[12]
and from the analysis of the Nb–Al phase diagram. The
dispersoids are a natural product of MA, and the
occurrence of the unexpected tetragonal phase can be
rationalized by the nature of mechanical alloying that
frequently produces far-from-equilibrium phases as a
result of large amounts of elements shifting phase
boundaries on the respective phase diagrams.

5.2. High temperature strength
The results presented inTable 3show that V-modiﬁed
Nb–Al alloys are stronger at high temperatures than
their binary counterparts. Also, what is even more
important if the materials are to ﬁnd high-temperature
structural applications, their strength does not dramati-
cally decreases with temperature as is the case in the
binary alloys, and their high strength is retained at least
up to 1100
/C14C. Among the three ternary alloys investi-
gated, Nb–18Al–24V is the strongest, followed by Nb–
15Al–20V consolidated at 1300 /C14C and Nb–15Al–20V
consolidated at 1500 /C14C. However, the diﬀerence in
strength between the alloy with 24 at.% V and Nb–
15Al–20V consolidated at 1500
/C14C is less than 10%.
The analysis of the chemical compositions of the solid
solution and the two ordered phases (Table 2) indicates
that dissolved V atoms occupy both Nb and Al sites in
all three phases. Since the atomic diameters of Nb and
Al are almost identical (2.860 and 2.864 A˚ , respectively)
and the atomic diameter of V is much less (2.621A˚ ),
thus the presence of vanadium in Nbss is postulated to
lead to the solid solution strengthening. It is also very
likely that V strengthens the ordered phases. Though no
study on the eﬀect of ternary additions on the strength
of monolithic Nb
3Al was reported, the solid solution
strengthening of ordered phases in Ni–Al system is well
documented [10,11]. Only marginally lower strength of
Nb–15Al–20V consolidated at 1500/C14C compared to the
strength of the same alloy consolidated at 1300/C14C indi-
cates that neither the absence of the tetragonal phase
nor the coarsening of the dispersoids during consolida-
tion have a signiﬁcant inﬂuence on the strength of the
MA Nb
3Al-based alloys.
The present ternary alloys exhibit higher—though still
limited (5–7%)—compressive strains to failure at 800/C14C
than the binary Nb-18Al alloy. Compressive ductility of
the ternary alloys at 1000 and 1100
/C14C exceeds 20%.
5.3. Creep behavior
5.3.1. Creep rates
In the present binary Nb–Al alloys, the steady-state
creep rates decrease with aluminum content increasing
from 15 to 18 to 20 at.% and then sharply increase
(Table 5). The diﬀerences in creep response among the
binary alloys can be rationalized when one assumes that
the amount of the niobium solid solution decreases and
the amount of the tetragonal phase increases with
increasing aluminum content. Neither TEM nor X-ray
studies allow us to determine, in a statistically sig-
niﬁcant way, the quantitative changes in the amounts of
the phases; however the assumption is consistent with
the changes in the alloys’ chemical composition. The
lower minimum creep rates of Nb–20Al compared to
Nb–18Al, and of Nb–18Al compared to Nb–15Al, are
postulated to be due to the decreasing amounts of the
disordered Nb
ss phase, in accordance with the results of
numerous studies proving that creep resistance is
enhanced by the presence of order[13]. On the other
hand, the higher creep rates in Nb–25Al compared to
Nb-20Al are postulated to result from the increased
presence of the apparently undesirable /C27-type Nb
2Al
tetragonal phase, reducing creep resistance of the Nb–
25Al alloy and more than ‘‘compensating’’ the further
decrease of the amount of Nb
ss in the microstructure.
Monolithic Nb3Al is known to have high-temperature
properties superior to those of Nb2Al, e.g., hardness of
Nb3Al is signiﬁcantly higher than that of Nb 2Al at
temperatures above 800 /C14C and the diﬀerence in hard-
ness increases with increasing temperature[14].
In the ternary alloys: Nb–15Al–20V consolidated at
1300 /C14C and Nb–18Al–24V the creep rates are compar-
able with those measured in the binary alloys (Table 5).
On the other hand, in the ternary alloy Nb–15Al–20V
consolidated at 1500/C14C the creep rates are signiﬁcantly
lower (one to two orders of magnitude) than in all other
alloys investigated in the present study (Table 5, Fig. 8).
This material is the only one in the present study that
does not exhibit the tetragonal phase. This observation,
combined with an apparently negative eﬀect the phase
has on the creep resistance of the present binary alloys,
allows us to postulate that the elimination of this phase
leadstoasigniﬁcantimprovementofthecreepresistance
inmechanicallyalloyedNb
3Al-basedalloys.Apparently,
the coarsening of oxide dispersoids during consolidation
of Nb–15Al–20V at 1500
/C14C has no negative impact on
the creep resistance of the material. The Nb–15Al–20V
alloy, consolidated at 1500
/C14C, has been identiﬁed for
further studies of its high temperature behavior at tem-
peratures in the range 1000–1300
/C14C.
The present study provides probably the ﬁrst evidence
that creep properties of Nb3Al are superior to those of
Nb2Al. It is postulated that the enhanced creep resis-

5.3.2. Activation energy and stress exponent
The dependence of creep rate on stress and tempera-
ture in Nb–20Al exempliﬁes the binary alloys’ response
to increasing stress at constant temperature and
increasing temperature at constant stress. The values of
the stress exponent (at 875
/C14C) and the activation energy
for creep (at 110 MPa) determined for Nb–20Al are 3.6
(Table 5) and 371kJ/mol [6], respectively. These values
are similar to those measured in a study on the single
phase Nb
3Al (2.86 and 348 kJ/mol) [16]. In turn, the
two values of the activation energy are very similar to
the value of the activation energy for chemical diﬀusion
in Nb3Al, calculated by Agafanow, quoted in[16], using
the chemical diﬀusion coeﬃcient equal to
D
ANB+DBNA, whereDi is the diﬀusivity of speciesi in
the compound and Ni the molar fraction of speciesi.
(The calculated value of the activation energy for che-
mical diﬀusion in Nb
3Al is 366 KJ/mol.) Thus, it is clear
that the dependence of creep rate on temperature in the
binary alloys is controlled by the diﬀusivity of Nb and
Al atoms in the ordered Nb3Al phase and the creep
mechanism is diﬀusion-assisted dislocation creep. (It is
well known, that when normalized stresses are in the
range 10
/C04–10/C02 G (where G is shear modulus)—as is
indeed the case in the present study—the prevailing
creep mechanism is diﬀusion-assisted dislocation creep
[17,18].) The present values of the stress exponent, in the
range 3.6–4.9, are consistent with the conclusion that
creep is controlled by the motion of dislocations, and
fall between n=2, which is characteristic for alloys in
which creep is controlled by jogged screw dislocation
glide and n=5, which is characteristic for alloys in
which creep is controlled by dislocation climb[17].
In the ternary alloys, with and without the tetragonal
phase, the values of the activation energy for creep are
lower than in the binary alloys ( Table 6), while the
values of the exponent n are within the range observed
in the binary alloys (Table 5). The lower values of the
activation energies indicate that the chemical diﬀusion
coeﬃcient in the V-modiﬁed Nb
3Al is smaller than in
the binary compound.
5.3.3. Role of vanadium
The present results lead to a conclusion that the pre-
sence ofvanadium dissolved inthesolidsolutionandthe
two ordered phases, which proved to cause signiﬁcant
strengthening, does not directly decrease creep rates in
mechanically alloyed Nb
3Al-based materials in the
temperature range 800–1000/C14C. Rather, as anticipated
based on the analysis of isothermal sections of the Nb–
Al–V ternary phase diagram [8], this element changes
phase composition, and, in the present study, under
some experimental conditions, eliminates the tetragonal
phase. On the other hand, the presence of vanadium
lowers the chemical diﬀusion coeﬃcient and, conse-
quently, the activation energy for creep, what allows us
to suggest that ternary alloys, even containing the tetra-
gonal phase, exhibit better potential for use at tempera-
tures above 1000
/C14C. The twofold role of vanadium is

5.3.4. MA Nb
3Al-base alloys: in situ composites or
ODSmaterials?
The MA Nb 3Al-base alloys, containing Nb ss, the
reinforcing phases Nb3Al and Nb2Al, and the oxide
dispersoids can be hypothetically considered as either in
situ composites or oxide dispersion strengthened (ODS)
materials. Typically, MA produces ODS alloys, as was
the case in the MA Ni–Al alloys investigated in our
laboratories [19]. The creep of ODS alloys is character-
ized by very high values of the stress exponent[20], the
activation energy for creep signiﬁcantly exceeding that
for lattice diﬀusion[20] and a threshold stress behavior
[21]. None of the above characteristics was observed in
the present study. Thus, the present alloys do not
behave as ODS materials, because of the limited num-
ber of oxide dispersoids in the microstructure, resulting
from reducing the oxygen level during mechanical
alloying to less than 10 ppm. On the other hand, the
activation energy for creep of in situ composites was
frequently found to be close to that of the reinforcing
phase [22], which is the case in the present study. Thus,
the creep behavior of the MA Nb
3Al-base alloys is
typical of that in in situ composites.

In the present study, aimed at optimizing high tem-
perature strength and creep resistance of mechanically
alloyed Nb
3Al-based materials, several binary Nb–Al
and ternary Nb–Al–V alloys were processed and char-
acterized.ThestudyprovedthatMAisaviableroutefor
processingofNb
3Al-based alloys,producingfullydense,
free from cracks materials exhibiting high strength at
room and high temperatures. Four phases: a Nbss solid
solution, the Nb3Al and Nb2Al ordered phases, and
aluminum oxide dispersoids were revealed in the alloys’
microstructure.
