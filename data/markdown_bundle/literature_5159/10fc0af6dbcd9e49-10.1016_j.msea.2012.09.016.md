abstract
It has recently been found that Mg97Zn1Y2 extruded alloy containing a long-period stacking ordered
(LPSO) phase has superior mechanical properties. In this study, the high-temperature deformation
mechanism of the Mg97Zn1Y2 extruded alloy was examined. Grain-boundary strengthening due to the
reﬁned Mg-matrix phase and ﬁber-like reinforcement due to the LPSO phase dominantly contribute to
the strengthening of the alloy at room temperature, and they were conﬁrmed to effectively act even at
200 1C. As a result, the extremely high strength of the alloy is maintained up to 200 1C, unlike other
conventional Mg alloys. At 300 1C, however, the yield stress of the Mg97Zn1Y2 alloy largely decreases,
and the orientation and the grain size dependence of the yield stress become weak. Increases in the
operation frequency of non-basal slip in the Mg-matrix grains weaken the grain-boundary strengthen-
ing effect. In addition, the effect of ﬁber-like reinforcement due to the LPSO phase is also weakened at
300 1C because the window of microstructure suitable for inducing this effect becomes signiﬁcantly
narrow at this temperature.

1. Introduction
Many types of Mg alloys have recently been developed
because they are light in weight and easy to recycle [1]. Their
use is expected to reduce the weight of transportation systems,
which will improve fuel efﬁciency and will reduce CO 2 emissions.
However, their useful temperature range is generally low. For the
typical Mg alloy AZ91, it is about 130 1C [1] since the strength of
the alloys decreases rapidly at that temperature. Improving the
high-temperature strength is of high interest to areas of engineer-
ing where low-weight Mg alloys can be applied. Rare-earth-
containing Mg alloys such as WE54 are a promising candidate
and large increases in high-temperature strength have indeed
been reported [2]. Another approach uses long-period stacking
ordered (LPSO) phase alloys [3–17]. In a recent representative
study, we examined the mechanical properties of the Mg97Zn1Y2
(at%) Mg/LPSO two-phase extruded alloy and conﬁrmed that
these properties are extremely superior at room temperature
(RT) [16]. Moreover, this alloy has signiﬁcant potential for use as a
high-temperature structural Mg alloy [15,17]. In the previous
study, we clariﬁed that the mechanical properties and the
strengthening mechanism of the alloy at RT vary, depending
largely on the microstructure [16]. In this study, the high-
temperature mechanical properties of Mg97Zn1Y2 were exam-
ined using the same experimental approach. The microstructure
in Mg97Zn1Y2 extruded alloy with a volume fraction in the LPSO
phase of /C24 24% was variously altered by long-term heat treatment
at high temperatures above 400 1C. Using the specimens, varia-
tions in high-temperature mechanical properties depending on
the microstructure were quantitatively clariﬁed by compression
tests. The plastic deformation behavior of the Mg99.2Zn0.2Y0.6
extruded alloy (whose alloy composition is nearly identical to
that of the Mg-matrix phase in the Mg97Zn1Y2 Mg/LPSO two-
phase alloy) was also examined. By comparing their mechanical
properties, the strengthening mechanisms operating in the
Mg/LPSO two-phase alloys at high temperatures were clariﬁed.

3. Results
The variations in microstructure due to heat-treatments were
reported in the previous paper in detail [16]. As an example of the
variation in the microstructure, Fig. 1 (a) shows the microstruc-
tures of as-extruded Mg/LPSO alloy, and Fig. 1 (b–e) shows those
annealed at 450 1C for various periods. In addition, Fig. 1(f) and (g)
shows the microstructure of the alloys annealed at 400 1C and
475 1C, respectively, for 168 h. The initial Mg grain size in the as-
cast ingots was at least more than 100 mm, but the grains were
extremely reﬁned due to their dynamic recrystallization during
the extrusion process. The average grain size in the as-extruded
alloy was /C24 8.3 mm in the Mg-ss alloy, while it was much ﬁner of
/C24 2.6 mm in the Mg/LPSO alloy. In the Mg-ss extruded alloy a
small amount of the LPSO phase appeared, but its volume fraction
(approximated by the area fraction measurement) was /C24 3 vol%.
In the Mg/LPSO alloy the LPSO phases coexisted with a relatively
large volume fraction of /C24 24 vol%. The LPSO phases showed plate-
like shapes and were aligned along the direction of extrusion.
The microstructures in the alloys were considerably stable and
almost no change appeared during the short-term annealing
below 300 1C. However, the grain size of the Mg matrix was
increased by long-term annealing at high temperatures above
400 1C. Even at the high temperatures, the growth rate of the
grain was considerably slow, especially in the Mg/LPSO two-
phase alloy as shown in Fig. 1 . Quantitatively, the average grain
sizes after annealing at 400, 450, and 475 1C for 168 h were 10.4,
81.6, and 270 mm in the Mg-ss alloys, but in the Mg/LPSO alloys
they were as small as 3.8, 9.5, and 13.8 mm respectively. The
microstructure of the LPSO phase was also relatively stable below
400 1C. Above 400 1C, however, the morphology of the plate-like
shape of LPSO phase grains gradually varied during the long-term
annealing. The lengths of the grains were gradually shortened
as shown in Fig. 1 (f). Above 450 1C, the morphology of the LPSO
phase was largely changed to short rod-like shapes as shown in
Fig. 1(e) and (g). The average grain lengths of the LPSO phase were
55, 34, 19, and 17 mm in the as-extruded specimen, and those
annealed at 400, 450, and 475 1C for 168 h, respectively.

the heat-treatments were examined by scanning electron micro-

diffractometer (XRD), and the results were reported in the

Fig. 6 in paper [16]), a very wide area was covered by tiny
recrystallized Mg-grains in the Mg/LPSO two-phase as-extruded
alloy, as is also seen in the OM image in Fig. 1 (a). Relatively large
worked grains with {10 10} texture along the direction of extru-
sion were locally remained, but its area fraction was as low
as approximately 10–15%. As a characteristic feature observed
in this Mg/LPSO two-phase alloy, the tiny recrystallized grains

tiny recrystallized Mg-grains with the randomized texture was
induced by the particle stimulated nucleation (PSN) mechanism
via the frequent occurrence of stress concentration around the
LPSO phase during the extrusion process [16]. In the Mg/LPSO
extruded alloy, the LPSO-phases exist with a volume fraction of
/C24 24%. Since the LPSO-phase exhibits strong plastic anisotropy
due to the limitation of the deformation mode [18,19], an
incompatibility between deformations frequently occurs at the
interface between Mg matrix grain and the LPSO-phase during
extrusion, which in turn induces a strong stress concentration.
This results in the formation of heavily concentrated deformation
zones in the Mg-grains along the LPSO phase. This must induce
the operation of many slip systems not only basal slip but also the
prismatic and pyramidal slips, which enhances the formation of
ﬁne recrystallized grains that have a considerably weakened basal
ﬁber texture [16]. This conclusion was further conﬁrmed by
Yamasaki et al. by using the Mg/LPSO alloy in which the micro-
structure was largely varied by controlling the casting speed of
the mother ingots [13]. In the Mg/LPSO specimen annealed at and
above 400 1C, the residual worked coarse grains almost disap-
peared, and the specimens were entirely covered by the tiny
recrystallized Mg-grains with a considerably randomized texture,
although in the specimens annealed at and above 450 1C the
amount of the Mg-recrystallized grains with {10 10} texture was
slightly increased [16].
In addition to the EBSD and XRD analyses, the TEM observation
was further conducted to examine the texture of LPSO phase
grains [13]. It was conﬁrmed that in the tiny recrystallized Mg-
grains with the randomized texture, many of LPSO phase grains
were embedded with the basal ﬁber texture, i.e. the basal plane in
the LPSO phase is aligned parallel to the extrusion direction in the
as-extruded specimen. In the previous study it was clariﬁed that
the interface of plate-like shape of LPSO phase grain is parallel to
(0001) [18]. Therefore, the aligned plate-like morphology of the
LPSO phase grains shown in Fig. 1 (a) also supports the develop-
ment of basal ﬁber texture in the as-extruded alloy. However, the
aligned morphology and the basal ﬁber texture of the LPSO phase
were gradually collapsed by long-term annealing above 450 1Ca s
shown in Fig. 1 (e) and (g).
The texture in Mg-ss alloys was also examined by X-ray pole
ﬁgure analysis and the results were shown in Fig. 7 in paper [16].
The ﬁber-like texture with the c-axes perpendicular to the direction
of extrusion was detected also in the Mg-ss as-extruded alloy due to
some amounts of residual worked grains, as similar to that in the
Mg/LPSO alloy. However, the basal ﬁber texture was fairly weakened
by long-term annealing at 400 1C, and it was almost eliminated in
specimens annealed above 450 1C.

Several mechanisms cooperatively strengthen the Mg/LPSO alloy.
Among them, we clariﬁed in the previous study that the short ﬁber
reinforcement due to the LPSO phase and the grain-boundary
strengthening due to the extremely reﬁned Mg-matrix grains
signiﬁcantly contribute to the strengthening of the alloy at RT
[16]. To quantitatively examine the temperature dependence of
strengthening due to these mechanisms, the temperature depen-
dence of the yield stress as a function of the Mg-matrix grain size
was evaluated. Fig. 4 (a), (b), and (c) shows the variations in yield
stress sy of the Mg/LPSO and Mg-ss alloys with the inverse of the
square root of Mg-matrix grain size d/C0 1/2, the so-called the Hall–
Petch plot, in deformation at RT, 200, and 300 1C, respectively. For
the Mg/LPSO two-phase alloy, in addition to the yield stress at
01-orientation, the yield stress of 45 1-oriented specimens was also
plotted with solid marks for comparison. In deformation at
01-orientation, the shear deformation always occurs across the LPSO
phase grains that have thin plate-like shapes; thus, the LPSO phase
grains must be deformed together with the Mg-matrix grains during
the compression test. At a 45 1-orientation, on the other hand, the
inﬂuence of the LPSO phase on the deformation of the specimen
would be minimized because macroscopic deformation can occur
only through the Mg-matrix phases.
At RT, the yield stress, sy, of the annealed Mg/LPSO alloy
specimens apparently showed a nearly linear relationship in
terms of d/C0 1/2 at both 0 1- and 45 1-loading orientations, although
the yield stress of the as-extruded specimens largely deviated
from linear. As shown in Fig. 4(b), a considerably similar relation-
ship versus that at RT is shown at 200 1C. These results are
recognized ‘as if’ the grain size of the Mg-matrix grains comple-
tely governed the yield stress of annealed specimens, in obedi-
ence to the Hall–Petch relation, not only in Mg-ss alloy but also in
Mg/LPSO two-phase alloy. However this must be incorrect,
because the strong orientation dependence of yield stress appears
in Mg/LPSO two-phase alloy, while such orientation dependence
of yield stress is little observed in Mg-ss alloy as shown in
Fig. 4 (a). In the Mg/LPSO alloy, tiny recrystallized Mg matrix
grains exhibit the fairly randomized texture as conﬁrmed in the
previous paper [16]. Nevertheless, a large difference between the
yield stress of specimens in the 0 1- and 45 1-orientations appears
in deformation at RT and 200 1C. This demonstrates that the yield
stress of the Mg/LPSO alloy is not only governed by the Mg-matrix
grains, but the LPSO-phase also signiﬁcantly affected the mechan-
ical properties of the alloy at the temperature region between RT
and 200 1C.

4. Discussion
This study demonstrated that the Mg97Zn1Y2 extruded alloy
maintains a superior strength even up to 200 1C in contrast to the
conventional Mg alloys. However, the strength largely reduces at
300 1C. In this section, the factors and mechanisms controlling the
deformation behavior at 200 and 300 1C are discussed.
As shown in Fig. 4 , the variation in yield stress depending on
the microstructure at 200 1C was similar to that at RT. This
indicates that the strengthening mechanism operating at RT was
still effective even at 200 1C and produced a high yield stress. As
previously clariﬁed [16], several mechanisms cooperatively con-
tribute to the strengthening of the Mg/LPSO alloy at RT. The short-
ﬁber-like reinforcement due to the LPSO phase and the grain-
boundary strengthening due to extremely reﬁned Mg grains
largely contribute to the increase in the yield stress of the alloy.
These are conﬁrmed to be also effective at 200 1C. The validity of
the short-ﬁber-like reinforcement due to the LPSO phase at 200 1C
has already been clariﬁed in previous section. The evidence that
the grain-boundary strengthening due to reﬁned Mg grains is also
highly effective at 200 1C is offered in the apparent Hall–Petch

5. Conclusion
In this study, the high-temperature strengthening mechanism
acting in Mg97Zn1Y2 Mg/LPSO two-phase extruded alloy was
examined by comparing the deformation features of a
Mg99.2Zn0.2Y0.6 Mg-solid-solution alloy. The obtained results
can be summarized as follows:
(1) High yield stress comparable to that at RT is maintained in the
Mg/LPSO two-phase extruded alloy even up to 200 1C. At
200 1C, grain-boundary strengthening due to the reﬁned
Mg-matrix phase and short ﬁber-like reinforcement due to
the LPSO phase both effectively contribute to the strengthen-
ing of the alloy.
(2) Yield stress of the Mg/LPSO two-phase extruded alloy largely
decreases at 300 1C. The orientation dependence and grain
size dependence of the yield stress become weak at 300 1C,
nevertheless they are signiﬁcant at low temperatures.
(3) Increases in the operation frequency of non-basal slip in the
Mg-matrix grains were conﬁrmed at 300 1C, which may
weaken the grain-boundary strengthening. In addition, the
ﬁber-like reinforcement due to the LPSO phase is also wea-
kened at 300 1C. These lead to a large decrease in the yield
stress at 300 1C.
