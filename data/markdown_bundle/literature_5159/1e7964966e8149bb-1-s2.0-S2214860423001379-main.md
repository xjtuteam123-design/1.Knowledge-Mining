ABSTRACT 
This paper presents a novel design concept for Al alloys with both sufficient laser powder bed fusion (L-PBF) 
processability and high-temperature mechanical performance using an Al – Fe – Mn ternary alloy with a refined 
α -Al/Al
6
M (M = Fe, Mn) two-phase microstructure. The near-eutectic (liquid → α -Al(fcc) + Al
6
M) composition of 
Al – 2.5Fe – 2Mn (mass%) was designed to avoid the formation of coarse primary intermetallics (having a detri -
mental effect on the PBF processability for densification) based on non-equilibrium liquidus projection ther -
modynamic calculations. The designed Al – 2.5Fe – 2Mn alloy powder exhibited sufficient L-PBF processability, and 
fully dense centimeter-sized samples (relative densities of above 99%) were successfully prepared. The L-PBF 
Al – 2.5Fe – 2Mn alloy samples exhibited a significantly refined solidification microstructure consisting of Al
6
M and 
α -Al phases in the melt-pool regions. Fe and Mn alloying elements contributed to the formation of nanoscale 
Al
6
M-phase particles and a high solid solution concentration in the α -Al matrix. The strength of the L-PBF 
Al – 2.5Fe – 2Mn alloy was slightly reduced at higher temperatures, but remained above 240 MPa at an elevated 
temperature of 300 
◦
C. This superior high-temperature mechanical performance was attributed to the high 
thermal stability of the refined α -Al/Al
6
M two-phase microstructure and the formation of nanosized Al
6
M-phase 
precipitates. 
1. Introduction 
Additive manufacturing enables the layer-by-layer fabrication of 
geometrically complex parts based on computer-aided design models [1, 
2] . Laser powder bed fusion (L-PBF) [3] is one of the most common 
additive manufacturing processes for metals and alloys. L-PBF uses 
scanning laser irradiation to fuse or melt a metal or alloy powder, 
whereby the rapid solidification of the melt pool produces unique mi -
crostructures [4,5] in materials such as lightweight aluminum (Al) al -
loys [6 – 8] . Notably, the solidification sequence promotes the formation 
of refined and metastable microstructures [9,10] , resulting in superior 
and anomalous mechanical properties [10 – 12] . Recently, L-PBF has 
been used to produce aluminum – iron (Al – Fe) binary alloys with high 
recycling efficiency [13 – 16] . Al – 2.5Fe (mass%) binary alloy powders 
exhibit good L-PBF processability, and the as-built components have a 
high relative density and refined microstructure with numerous dis -
persoids of the Al
6
Fe metastable phase (orthorhombic structure, Pearson 
symbol oC 28) [17] , resulting in a high tensile strength of approximately 
300 MPa [18] . The L-PBF Al – 1Fe – 1Zr (mass%) ternary alloy containing 
refined θ -Al
13
Fe
4 
phase (stable phase) exhibited high strength and 
adequate ductility [19] , and the strength was enhanced by post 
heat-treatments. In comparison, L-PBF Al – 15Fe (mass%) alloys have 
much higher strength, even at elevated temperatures of 300 
◦
C [20] . 
Notably, the high-temperature strength is considerably better than those 
of other L-PBF Al alloys [21,22] , as well as those of Al – Fe-based alloys 
processed by powder metallurgy techniques. In L-PBF Al – Fe binary al -
loys, the micromorphology of the refined Al
6
Fe phase is moderately 
stable, even after exposure to temperatures of 300 
◦
C for over 100 h [20, 
23] . These works demonstrated the feasibility of using the L-PBF Al – Fe 
alloys as high-temperature lightweight materials, such as 
geometrically-complex components used in automotive engines. 
Fe is one of the traditional alloy elements for improving the high- 
temperature strength of Al alloys [24] . Indeed, various heat-resistant 
Al alloys containing other transition metals, including Ce, Sc, Zr, Cr, 
Ni, and Er, have been recently proposed for manufacturing by L-PBF 
[24 – 33] . However, Al alloys with a high content of alloying elements 
often exhibit poor PBF processability (limited process window) with 
regard to densification [33 – 35] . For the L-PBF Al – 15Fe (mass%) alloy

Al – Fe – Mn ternary alloy based on thermodynamic calculations using the 
CALPHAD approach. The designed Al – Fe – Mn ternary alloy samples 
were manufactured by L-PBF with different process parameters (laser 
power P and scan speed v ) for identification of the optimum conditions 
with regard to densification. The microstructural features and me -
chanical performance were investigated. The experimental results were 
utilized to discuss the validity of the design concept for Al – Fe-based 
alloys with both high-temperature mechanical performance and suffi -
cient L-PBF processability. 
2. Thermodynamic calculations for design of Al – Fe – Mn ternary 
alloy 
The Al – Fe – Mn ternary alloy was designed based on thermodynamic 
calculations using the CALPHAD method with a commercially available 
thermodynamic database (PanAluminum) [40] . The database re -
produces the experimentally determined Al – Fe – Mn phase diagram [38] . 
The calculation results are summarized in Fig. 2 . The liquidus projection 
of the Al-rich corner of the Al – Fe – Mn ternary system was calculated 
under equilibrium solidification conditions. In the Al – Fe – Mn ternary 
system, the θ phase is stable, resulting in primary solidification of the θ 
phase over a wide composition range. To ensure the θ phase did not form 
during L-PBF, the Fe content was limited to 2.5 mass% in the designed 
alloy. (Hereafter, all compositions are given in mass percent, unless 
otherwise specified.) The θ phase is scarcely observed in L-PBF Al alloys 
with low Fe contents of below 3% [14 – 16] ; therefore, it can be assumed 
that θ -phase formation will be suppressed during L-PBF in alloys with 
less than 2.5% Fe, particularly given the high cooling rate (10
5
– 10
6

[41 – 43] ), which means that the alloy solidifies under 
non-equilibrium conditions. 
To simulate the solidification sequence during L-PBF, the liquidus 
projection was calculated using the thermodynamic database of the 
Al – Fe – Mn ternary system, excluding the θ phase. The calculated pro -
jection is shown in Fig. 2 (b). Under non-equilibrium solidification 
conditions (suppressing the formation of the θ stable phase), the calcu -
lated liquidus valley delineates the boundary between different primary 
solidification phases of Al
6
M (M = Fe, Mn) and α -Al. Al – Fe – Mn ternary 
alloys with the composition at the liquidus valley (eutectic line) undergo 
a two-phase eutectic reaction (liquid → Al
6
M + α -Al) during solidifica -
tion. Considering the refined Al
6
Fe/ α -Al eutectic structure in fully dense 
L-PBF Al – 2.5Fe alloys [14] , alloys with compositions close to the 
Al
6
M/ α -Al eutectic point should be favorable for producing fully dense 
Al – Fe – Mn ternary samples. Eutectic solidification is consistent with the 
design approach for Al alloys with high printability [44] (for suppress -
ing the hot cracking in solidification [45] ). 
To determine the optimal Mn content, the phase formation sequences 
were calculated for Al – 2.5Fe – x Mn alloys ( x = 1, 2, 3, and 4) under 
Scheil solidification conditions. The calculation results are shown in 
Fig. 2 (c). The Al
6
M phase is expected to form as a primary phase in al -
loys with Mn contents above 2%. Because high volume fractions of 
primary Al – Fe intermetallic phases have a detrimental effect on the 
densification of the sample [35] , the final composition of the experi -
mental alloy powder for L-PBF was set as Al – 2.5Fe – 2Mn. 
The Scheil solidification sequences were calculated for the 
Al – 2.5Fe – 2Mn alloy under non-equilibrium solidification conditions. 
The result is shown in Fig. 2 (d) in comparison to the result under 
equilibrium solidification conditions (i.e., at a low cooling rate). 
Although some primary Al
6
M phase forms at the beginning of the so -
lidification process under non-equilibrium conditions, the solidification 
then progresses by a two-phase eutectic reaction, forming a eutectic 
Al
6
M/ α -Al phase. In contrast, under equilibrium conditions, primary 
Al – Fe intermetallics (stable θ phase) are expected to form during 
solidification.

features have been observed for L-PBF Al – 2.5Fe binary alloys [14] , 
indicating that a refined two-phase (Al
6

Notably, Fe and Mn were enriched as Al

high concentration of solute Mn, equivalent to the solubility limit of Mn 
in Al – Mn binary systems [38] ( Fig. 11 ). The solute Mn and Fe contents 
were higher inside the melt pools than at the melt-pool boundaries. The 
location dependence of the solute element contents corresponds well to 
the results of L-PBF Al – Fe binary alloys [54] and Al – Si alloys [43,55] . 
The relatively low content of solute elements at the melt pool boundaries 
was hypothesized to be caused by the lower solidification and growth 
rate (relatively low cooling rate) of the α -Al phase in the grain boundary 
regions at the onset of solidification [43] . The dependence of the local 
cooling rate on the location (near the melt-pool boundaries or inside the 
melt pools) has been discussed elsewhere [43,52,53] . The result is in 
good agreement with lower partition coefficients of solid to liquid in 
lower solidification velocity (proposed by Smith and Aziz [57] ). 
Consequently, it can be considered that adding Mn to Al – Fe binary al -

5.3. Mechanical performance at elevated temperatures 
Tensile testing demonstrated that the Mn addition significantly 
strengthens the L-PBF Al – 2.5Fe – 2Mn alloy specimen without a loss of 
ductility ( Fig. 12 ). The yield strength (0.2% proof stress) of the 
Al – 2.5Fe – 2Mn alloy specimen was approximately 50 MPa higher than 
that of the Al – 2.5Fe alloy specimen over the entire range of testing 
temperatures ( Fig. 13 (a)). A similar result was obtained for the tensile 
strengths ( Fig. 13 (b)). Notably, the Al – 2.5Fe – 2Mn alloy specimen had 
reasonable ductility at room temperature, with 7% elongation to failure 
( Fig. 12 (c)). The strengthening effect of Mn is expected to be due to the 
enhanced formation of nanoscale Al
6
M (M = Fe, Mn) phases. The TEM 
observations ( Fig. 9 ) indicated that the L-PBF Al – 2.5Fe – 2Mn alloy 
samples had a higher volume fraction (and number density) of Al
6
M 
phase than the Al – 2.5Fe binary alloy samples [14] . It was difficult to 
quantify the volume fraction of Al
6
M phase from the present TEM im -
ages. Nevertheless, the enrichment of Mn in the Al
6
M phase ( Fig. 10 ) 
confirmed that the total fraction of the Al
6
M phase increased based on 
the partitioning of Mn atoms into the M sublattice sites ( Fig. 1 ). In 
addition, the solute Mn atoms in the α -Al matrix also contribute to 
solid-solution strengthening of the L-PBF specimen. Therefore, control -
ling the refined α -Al/Al
6
Fe two-phase microstructure by alloying could 
be an effective strategy for strengthening L-PBF Al – Fe-based alloys 
without a loss of ductility.

6. Conclusions 
The present study demonstrates the possibility of alloy design for 
achieving both sufficient L-PBF processability and superior high- 
temperature mechanical performance. The composition of the 
Al – Fe – Mn ternary alloy with a refined α -Al/Al
6
M (M = Fe, Mn) two- 
phase microstructure was designed based on the calculated liquidus 
projection in a non-equilibrium state. The optimum laser processing 
conditions for densification, microstructural features, and mechanical 
performance were investigated for the designed Al – Fe – Mn ternary alloy 
manufactured by L-PBF. The following conclusions were drawn from the 
experimental results: 
(1) Thermodynamic calculations based on the non-equilibrium liq -
uidus projection indicated that the near-eutectic (liquid → α Al +
Al
6
M (M = Fe, Mn)) composition of Al – 2.5Fe – 2Mn (mass%) 
would avoid the formation of primary intermetallic phases. The 
melt-pool structure of the L-PBF Al – 2.5Fe – 2Mn alloy samples 
(which was locally melted and rapidly solidified by scanning laser 
irradiation) contained a refined α -Al/Al
6
M two-phase micro -
structure. No coarsened intermetallic phases (including θ -Al
13
Fe
4 
phases, which form with the α -Al phase under equilibrium con -
ditions) were observed. The Fe and Mn atoms contributed to the 
formation of nanoscale Al
6
M phases and α -Al solid solutions with 
a high concentration of alloying elements. 
(2) The designed Al – 2.5Fe – 2Mn alloy powder had sufficient L-PBF 
processability for the manufacture of fully dense centimeter-sized 
samples. The deposited energy density model [47,48] provided 
an approximate critical value laser powder ( P ) and laser scan 
speed ( v ) for manufacturing dense samples with high relative 
densities of above 99%.
