Abstract
The high temperature tensile properties of a Cu–1.5 wt.% Ti alloy have been investigated in the temperature range of
100–550 °C. Substantial increase in yield and tensile strengths of solution treated alloy is observed with increasing temperature,
with a peak at 450 °C and decrease in strength beyond this temperature. In situ age hardening during testing at elevated
temperatures resulting in the precipitation of ordered, metastable and coherent Cu
4Ti ( /p981) phase is attributed to the increase in
strength up to 450 °C and coarsening of /p981 precipitate particles decreases the strength at temperatures greater than 450 °C. In
the peak-aged condition, however, the yield and tensile strengths are nearly constant up to 350 °C and decrease thereafter, up to
550 °C. The decrease in strength beyond 350 °C in the peak aged Cu–1.5Ti alloy is attributed to the coarsening of precipitate
particles and thermally activated cross-slip occurring at high temperatures, which makes the movement of the dislocations easy.
The high temperature tensile properties of Cu–1.5Ti alloy are comparable at 300 °C and even better at 425 °C than those of
Cu–1.5Ti–2.5Sn, Cu–1.5Ti–2.5Sn–0.5Cr and Cu–0.65Be–2.7Co alloys. Ductile mode of fracture is observed even at elevated
temperatures. © 2002 Elsevier Science B.V. All rights reserved.

1. Introduction
Cu–Ti alloys have been developed with the aim of
substituting them for the toxic and expensive Cu–Be
alloys. Earlier studies on Cu–Ti alloys have been lim-
ited to investigations on the mechanisms of spinodal
decomposition with little emphasis on mechanical prop-
erties [1–6]. Further, investigations on the effects of
prior cold deformation on ageing were conﬁned to
hardness measurements. The inﬂuence of ageing time,
temperature and prior cold work on tensile properties
and electrical conductivity has not been studied in
detail. Moreover, the alloy compositions studied were
also selective and not over a wide range. Therefore, a
project has been undertaken to investigate various as-
pects of Cu–Ti alloys of four compositions, viz. Cu–
1.5 wt.% Ti, Cu–2.7 wt.% Ti, Cu–4.5 wt.% Ti and
Cu–5.4 wt.% Ti. Hall–Petch relationship and high
cycle fatigue behaviour of the solution treated Cu–Ti
alloys have been studied in detail [7–12]. Recently, the
effects of prior cold work on hardness, tensile proper-
ties and electrical conductivity of four Cu–Ti alloys
have been investigated [13–18]. A more recent study
explains hot deformation of Cu–4.5Ti alloy [19,20].
However, little work has been done on the high temper-
ature tensile behaviour of Cu–Ti alloys. The present
paper, therefore, reports the results obtained on high
temperature tensile properties of a Cu–1.5 wt.% Ti
alloy in solution treated (ST) and peak aged (PA)
conditions.

found to be 1.5 wt.% Ti. The homogenised ingot was

3. Results and discussion
Fig. 1 shows the variation of yield and tensile
strengths with temperature in Cu –1.5 wt.% Ti alloy in
solution treated and peak aged conditions. In solution
treated condition, both yield and tensile strengths in-
creased with increasing temperature, reached a peak at
450 °C and decreased thereafter. The increase in
strength from room temperature to 250 °C is marginal
(YS from 110 to 115 MPa and TS from 290 to 300
MPa), while it is substantial beyond 250 °C (YS from
115 to 295 and TS from 300 to 425 MPa). A peak in
strength was found to occur at the temperature of
450 °C (425 MPa). The present observation that the
peak strength is obtained at 450 °C is in agreement
with our earlier results on age hardening studies in
Cu–Ti alloys [13,15,16]. The increase in both yield and
tensile strengths with increasing temperature up to
450 °C is explained on the basis of in situ age harden-
ing occurring in the solution treated Cu –1.5Ti alloy
with the formation of ordered, metastable and coherent
Cu
4Ti (/p981) precipitate [15,16]. The strength increases up
to 450 °C due to dislocations cutting through the ﬁne
particles of Cu 4Ti ( /p981) precipitate. However, beyond
450 °C, the precipitates grow and become coarse, in
which case, the dislocations take a path round the
precipitate particles, thus the strength decreases. The
increase in strength with temperature is reported for
solution treated Cu –Ni–Al alloy single crystals also
[21].
In the peak aged Cu –1.5Ti alloy, the yield as well as
tensile strength was found to be nearly independent of
temperature up to 350 °C and started decreasing there-
after. The yield strength was constant at 430 /p5710 MPa
up to 350 °C and decreased continuously beyond
350 °C to 240 MPa at 550 °C. Similarly, the tensile
strength was found to be constant at 590 /p5710 MPa up
to 350 °C and decreased drastically beyond 350 °Ct o
a value of 225 MPa at 550 °C. The specially marked
lines in Fig. 1 show the constancy in yield and tensile
strengths. The present observation that the strength is
independent of temperature up to 350 °Ci nC u –1.5Ti
alloy is in agreement with the reported behaviour in the
Al–Cu alloys containing coarse second phase particles
up to 373 K [22]. However, during testing at tempera-
tures beyond 350 °C, the ﬁne ordered, metastable and
coherent Cu
4Ti ( /p981) precipitates tend to grow larger in
size. Both yield and tensile strengths decrease beyond
350 °C due to these coarse precipitate particles as well
as thermally activated cross-slip occurring at high tem-
peratures, which would make the movement of the
dislocations easy. Dislocations avoid or take a path
round the precipitate particles instead of cutting
through them.

and tested at room temperature. The lower strength

at 450 °C is due to the short residence time ( /p21h )a t
450 °C during testing. Another observation to be noted
