Over-aging softening due to second-phase particle coarsening is a prevalent issue in age-hardenable cop- 
per alloys, limiting their performance optimization and high-temperature applications. We propose a 
dislocation-assisted heterogeneous co-precipitation strategy for high thermal stability in Cu-Cr-Hf alloys, 
leveraging the dislocation sensitivity of Hf phase nucleation as a key element of this approach. Through 
this, we explore their precipitation behavior and observe two novel co-precipitation modes. In solution- 
treated alloys, asynchronous co-precipitation occurs: Hf phases nucleate at transformation-induced in- 
terfacial dislocations (TIIDs) within Cr/Cu phase boundaries after initial Cr precipitation. In deformed 
alloys, synchronous co-precipitation is observed, where Hf and Cr phases nucleate simultaneously at 
deformation-induced dislocations. The resulting composite nanoparticles formed via co-precipitation ex- 
hibit reduced interfacial energy. This inhibits nano-precipitate coarsening, dislocation annihilation, and 
copper matrix recrystallization, ultimately suppressing the over-aging softening of the alloy. After being 
processed by a single-pass “solution-cold rolling-aging” process, Cu-Cr-Hf alloy shows tensile strength of 
597 MPa and electrical conductivity of 72 % international annealed copper standard, while maintaining 
high hardness after prolonged aging even at 500 °C, demonstrating an excellent strength-conductivity bal- 
ance and high thermal stability. We envisage that this innovative mechanism of enhancing nanoparticle 
thermal stability through multi-precipitate co-precipitation can provide a pathway based on conventional 
industrial operations for optimizing advanced high-strength, high-conductivity copper alloys and promote 
their high-temperature service.

1. Introduction 
Age-hardenable copper alloys with high strength and good elec- 
trical conductivity have long served as non-substitutable conductor 
materials across power systems, electronics, aerospace, and railway 
industries [ 1–3 ]. As technological advancements continue, the de- 
mand for copper alloys with both high strength and superior elec- 
trical properties has grown signiﬁcantly [ 4 , 5 ]. However, a major 
challenge common to these alloys is the insuﬃcient thermal stabil- 
ity of the nano-precipitates, which are inherently prone to coarsen- 
ing during the ageing process [ 6–8 ]. This coarsening occurs prema- 
turely, often, leading to softening of the alloy before it can achieve

ing. Thermo-mechanical treatment, through cold working, intro-

conﬁgurations. These features modulate precipitation driving forces 
and enrich nucleation sites. Consequently, nano-precipitate forma- 
tion eﬃciency is enhanced while high-temperature coarsening can 
be mitigated. Jia et al. [ 9 ] reported a high-temperature but short- 
time pre-aging process that balanced precipitation kinetics and nu-

operations, such as the single-pass process investigated herein. 
Microalloying modiﬁes second-phase growth and phase trans- 
formation behaviors of copper alloys by introducing trace elements 
to regulate the chemical environment of corresponding precipi- 
tates. “Interphase boundary segregation” represents a well-known 
microalloying mechanism proven to signiﬁcantly enhance second- 
phase thermal stability [ 18 , 19 ]. In commercial Cu-Cr-Zr alloys, trace 
additions of Zr reduce Cr nano-precipitate growth rates and en- 
hance their thermal stability, signiﬁcantly improving resistance to 
over-aging softening. Both 3D atom probe tomography (APT) mea- 
surements and ﬁrst-principles calculations conﬁrm that this en- 
hanced stability stems from Zr segregation at the Cr/Cu interphase 
boundaries, which reduces the interfacial energy [ 20–22 ]. Other 
microalloying elements, such as Mg [ 23 ], Ca, and Sr [ 24 ], have been 
reported to serve similar functions in Cu-Cr systems. Microalloying 
elements are typically added in small amounts due to their limited 
solubility in Cu [ 25 ] (e.g., Zr, Ca, and Sr) or their negative impact 
on electrical conductivity [ 26 ] (e.g., Mg). These constraints may re- 
strict microalloying effectiveness, still apt to apparent alloy soften- 
ing at a more elevated temperature (e.g., 500 °C) due to insuﬃcient 
precipitate thermal stability [ 27 ]. 
Recent studies highlight Cu-Cr-Hf alloys as a novel high- 
strength, high-conductivity alternative to the widely used Cu-Cr- 
Zr system. A key motivation stems from the potential for en- 
hanced performance and thermal stability. Indeed, a direct com- 
parative study by Rybalchenko et al. [ 28 ] on alloys processed by 
high-pressure torsion (HPT) demonstrated clear advantages for the 
Cu-Cr-Hf system. Their ﬁndings revealed that the HPT-processed 
Cu-Cr-Hf alloy achieved signiﬁcantly higher peak microhardness 
upon subsequent aging compared to its Cu-Cr-Zr counterpart pro- 
cessed under identical conditions. Furthermore, the Cu-Cr-Hf alloy 
exhibited superior resistance to softening at elevated temperatures, 
maintaining higher hardness levels during isochronal aging up to 
600 °C [ 28 ]. This enhanced performance is partly attributed to the 
considerably higher solubility of Hf in copper (about 1.1 wt.% or 
0.4 at.%) [ 29 ] compared to Zr (about 0.15 wt.% or 0.12 at.%) [ 30 ] 
near the solution treatment temperatures, offering greater poten- 
tial for precipitation strengthening without excessively compromis- 
ing electrical conductivity [ 31 ]. 
Beyond the magnitude of strengthening, the underlying mecha- 
nisms contributing to thermal stability may also differ. While Zr 
is well-known to enhance stability in Cu-Cr-Zr alloys primarily 
through segregation at Cr/Cu interfaces [ 21 ], which reduces interfa- 
cial energy and inhibits Cr precipitate coarsening, the role of Hf is 
less established. Although some studies suggest Hf might act simi- 
larly via segregation [ 32 ], others, including Rybalchenko et al. [ 28 ], 
observe the formation of distinct Hf-containing phases [ 31 ] (e.g., 
Cu5 Hf and Cu51 Hf14 ) alongside Cr precipitates. This suggests the 
possibility of different precipitate interactions and stability mech- 
anisms, such as the co-precipitation behavior investigated in the 
present work. Prior research using severe plastic deformation (SPD) 
like equal channel angular pressing (ECAP) also indicates complex 
precipitation sequences potentially inﬂuenced by deformation [ 33 ], 
and studies on binary Cu-Hf alloys have shown promising proper- 
ties [ 31 , 34 ]. These ﬁndings collectively underscore the potential of 
Cu-Cr-Hf alloys and motivate a deeper investigation into their pre- 
cipitation behavior and thermal stability, particularly under con- 
ventional thermomechanical processing conditions relevant to in- 
dustrial practice. However, there remains limited understanding 
of how Cu-Cr-Hf alloys respond to conventional thermomechani- 
cal processing rather than extreme deformation techniques such as 
ECAP or HPT. Moreover, the thermal stability of precipitates in Cu- 
Cr-Hf alloys during the aging process remains largely unexplored 
and not well understood. For instance, it remains unclear whether 
the coarsening of Hf-containing phases and Cr phases occurs inde- 
pendently or is coupled, particularly when the two phases possess 
independent microstructures. This may raise new patterns distinct 
from the conventional “interphase boundary segregation” concepts 
that interpret the mechanisms of thermal stability in alloys like 
Cu-Cr-Zr. 
In this study, we systematically investigated the aging behav- 
ior of a Cu-0.7Cr-0.9Hf alloy using a single-pass “solution-rolling- 
aging” process. Furthermore, the mechanical and electrical prop- 
erties of the alloy were optimized. The microstructural features of 
the alloy during aging were observed by means of multi-scale char- 
acterization. The performance characteristics, strengthening mech- 
anisms, multi-phase precipitation behavior, and heat resistance 
stability mechanisms of the alloy were discussed from the per- 
spective of the microstructure-property relationship. We aimed to 
clarify the following questions: (1) What are the performance re- 
sponse patterns and microstructure evolution of the Cu-Cr-Hf alloy 
under processing conditions based on conventional manufacturing 
steps? (2) How can we comprehend the multiple-phase precipita- 
tion behaviors and the interactions between the Cr phase and the 
Hf-containing phase? (3) What is the mechanism by which Cr-Hf 
precipitation enhances the alloy’s heat resistance? 
2. Materials and methods 
The nominal Cu-0.7Cr-0.9Hf (wt.%) alloy was melted in a 
medium-frequency induction furnace under nitrogen protection 
and cast into an iron mold. High puriﬁed electrolytic copper 
(99.95 wt.%), Cu-10Cr (wt.%) master alloy, and Cu-8Hf (wt.%) mas- 
ter alloy served as raw materials. The chemical composition of the 
as-cast alloys was ﬁrst veriﬁed using inductively coupled plasma 
optical emission spectrometry. After removing superﬁcial defects, 
the ingots were homogenized at 960 °C for 4 h, followed by im- 
mediate hot rolling with about a 70 % reduction in thickness. The 
hot-rolled strips were then solution-treated at 960 °C for 4 h and 
immediately water-quenched. The solution-treated samples were 
wire cut and grouped. After removing surface oxides, they under- 
went cold rolling with reductions of 0 %, 40 %, 60 %, 80 %, and 
90 %. This range of reductions was chosen to systematically study 
the effect of varying levels of stored deformation energy, with 0 % 
serving as the baseline. The 90 % upper limit represents a high 
yet practical level for conventional rolling, ensuring suﬃcient ex- 
perimental scope and signiﬁcant dislocation effects while mini- 
mizing the risk of edge cracking during processing. Subsequently, 
the samples were subjected to aging treatments at 400, 450, and 
500 °C for various durations. This aging temperature range (400–
500 °C) was selected based on established knowledge for Cu-Cr 
based alloys, ensuring effective precipitation kinetics for strength- 
ening while also allowing evaluation of thermal stability at a rela- 
tively high temperature (500 °C) where over-aging effects become 
more pronounced. 
Hardness and electrical conductivity curves as functions of ag- 
ing time were used to characterize the aging behavior of the al- 
loy. Vickers hardness was measured using an HV-5 hardness tester 
with a 2 kg load applied for 15 s. The hardness value of each sam- 
ple was represented as the average of 7 measurements. The electri-

It is worth noting that the Cu-Cr-Hf alloy exhibits excel- 
lent thermal stability during the aging process. Speciﬁcally, for 
solution-aged samples (with 0 % cold deformation), the hardness 
reaches a plateau after peaking, particularly at temperatures above 
450 °C ( Fig. 1 (b, c)). For alloys subjected to severe pre-deformation 
(90 % cold rolling), the resistance to softening remains remark- 
able. After extended aging for 720 min at 450 °C, the hardness 
decrease is only 13 % (214 HV to 185 HV). Even at the more de- 
manding temperature of 500 °C, a decrease in hardness of approx- 
imately 22 % (from a peak of 196 HV to 163 HV after 720 min) 
is observed ( Fig. 1 (c)). While this moderate decline indicates that 
softening mechanisms—likely including not only the slow coarsen- 
ing of precipitates but also thermally activated recovery processes 
such as dislocation annihilation—are naturally more pronounced 
than at 450 °C, the fact that the alloy retains substantial hardness 
(163 HV) under these prolonged, high-temperature conditions is 
signiﬁcant. This performance underscores the alloy’s inherent high 
thermal stability, which is attributed to the effective suppression 
of precipitate coarsening and the resulting stabilization of the mi- 
crostructure. The mechanisms are primarily investigated and evi- 
denced at the optimal aging temperature of 450 °C (as detailed in 
Sections 3.2 , 3.3 , and 4 ). A detailed quantitative analysis correlating 
the speciﬁc contributions to hardness loss at 500 °C is beyond the 
primary scope of this study. Importantly, this robustness at 500 °C 
highlights the effectiveness of the Cr-Hf co-precipitation strategy 
and suggests considerable potential for employing more advanced 
thermomechanical processing schedules in future optimization ef- 
forts without immediate concern for catastrophic thermal instabil- 
ity. Overall, these results demonstrate the superior resistance to 
over-aging softening conferred by the processing route and alloy 
composition.

dislocation density further decreases, discrete dislocations pinned 
by second phases remain prevalent (indicated by red arrows). Ex-

lizes, showing only slight coarsening of second phases, while nu-

4. Discussion 
This study systematically investigates the microstructural evo- 
lution and property responses of Cu-0.7Cr-0.9Hf alloy to thermo- 
mechanical processing. The key innovative ﬁndings are as follows: 
(1) The Cu-Cr-Hf alloy, processed through a single-pass ther- 
momechanical treatment, achieves an excellent balance of 
strength and electrical conductivity, demonstrating superior 
comprehensive performance and exceptional resistance to 
over-aging softening ( Figs. 1 and 2 ). 
(2) In Cu-Cr-Hf alloy, Cr and Hf-containing phases exhibit a 
novel co-precipitation pattern during aging: for solution- 
treated alloys, Cr precipitates prior to Hf-containing phases, 
with the latter nucleating heterogeneously at Cr/Cu matrix 
interfaces; in pre-deformed alloys, co-precipitation remains, 
but the desynchrony is no longer evident ( Figs. 3 , 4 , and 6 ). 
(3) After severe pre-deformation and prolonged aging, the nano- 
precipitates in the Cu-Cr-Hf alloy maintain their ﬁne size 
and highly dispersed distribution, proving excellent coars- 
ening resistance, signiﬁcantly inhibiting dislocation annihi- 
lation and the recrystallization process of the copper matrix 
( Figs. 5 and 7 ). 
Based on the above ﬁndings, we systematically discuss the mi- 
crostructural and performance characteristics of Cu-Cr-Hf alloy and 
its corresponding strengthening mechanisms to gain a deeper un- 
derstanding of its aging behaviors and impressive thermal stability. 
A novel mechanism for thermal stability is proposed based on the 
observed co-precipitation phenomenon.

4.2. Co-precipitation in Cu-Cr-Hf alloy and mechanisms for 
thermostability of nanoprecipitates 
The EDS analysis via TEM reveals a compelling observation: 
in the Cu-Cr-Hf alloy, the Cr phase appears to consistently co- 
precipitate with the Hf-containing phase ( Figs. 4 and 6 ), meaning 
both distinct precipitate types form during the same overall aging 
process. However, subtle differences exist between the solution- 
treated and pre-deformed aged alloys regarding the timing of their 
formation. Speciﬁcally, in the solution-treated alloy, Cr precipitates 
signiﬁcantly prior to the Hf-containing phase, with the latter sub- 
sequently nucleating heterogeneously at the Cr/Cu interfaces ( Figs. 
3 and 4 ). We term this behavior "asynchronous co-precipitation", 
deﬁned as the time-offset formation of distinct precipitate types 
(Cr and Hf-containing) during the same aging process. Crucially, 
in our system, this involves a mechanistic link where later Hf- 
phases nucleate heterogeneously on interfaces of the earlier Cr 
phase. This differs from "sequential precipitation", which often de- 
scribes the structural evolution of a single precipitate type. In con- 
trast, this clear asynchrony is less pronounced in the pre-deformed 
alloy, where synchronous co-precipitation at deformation-induced 
dislocations is observed ( Figs. 5 and 6 ). To validate this observa- 
tion, the precipitation kinetics of the Cu-Cr-Hf alloy were analyzed 
using the Johnson-Mehl-Avrami (JMA) kinetic model [ 52 , 53 ] (De- 
tailed calculation and ﬁtting procedures are provided in S2). Fig. 10 
depicts the ﬁtted lg ( 1 
1 − f ) vs. lg (t) curves based on the JMA ki- 
netic model, where t represents the aging time and f represents 
the degree of second phase precipitation completion. Notably, the 
kinetic process of the undeformed solution-treated Cu-Cr-Hf al- 
loy exhibits two discontinuous stages, particularly in the temper- 
ature range of 450 °C and above. It suggests the presence of at 
least two rate-determining steps or two distinct reaction processes 
[ 54 ]. In contrast, the ﬁtted kinetic curves for the pre-deformed al- 
loy show no distinct segmentation, and the entire aging process 
can be described by a continuous and uniﬁed function, supporting 
synchronous or near-synchronous co-precipitation. 
Previous studies on the aging behavior of Cu-Cr-Hf alloys un- 
der SPD conditions have yielded interesting, albeit somewhat con- 
ﬂicting, results regarding the precipitation sequence. Bochvar et al. 
[ 33 ], using ECAP, reported that deformation reversed the precip- 
itation sequence compared to the solution-treated state: Cu5 Hf 
phase precipitated ﬁrst (around 405 °C), followed by the Cr phase 
(around 465 °C). However, it is worth noting that their conclusion 
about this reversed sequence was based primarily on the inter- 
pretation of DSC curve peaks, without providing direct microstruc- 
tural characterization evidence (e.g., TEM/EDS) at the correspond- 
ing precipitation temperatures to deﬁnitively identify the phases 
responsible for speciﬁc exothermic events. Conversely, Rybalchenko 
et al. [ 28 ], employing HPT and combining DSC with TEM analysis, 
found that the precipitation sequence remained the same as in the 
solution-treated state (Cr phase ﬁrst, then Cu5 Hf phase), although 
HPT did inﬂuence the kinetics and peak temperatures. These differ- 
ing and potentially ambiguous results under SPD conditions high- 
light that the speciﬁc deformation method and its associated de- 
fect structure can signiﬁcantly alter the phase transformation path- 
ways, and relying solely on DSC data to determine precipitation se- 
quences in multi-phase alloys can be inconclusive. 
The key distinction and advancement of the present study lie in 
our systematic investigation of the Cu-Cr-Hf alloy’s response under 
conventional thermomechanical processing (single-pass “solution- 
rolling-aging”). This approach, utilizing industrially common tech- 
niques, contrasts with SPD methods that often face challenges in 
industrial scale-up due to cost and complexity, suggesting that 
the route investigated herein may be more amenable to potential 
industrial adaptation. Using a combination of precipitation kinet- 
ics analysis (JMA model) and detailed TEM/EDS characterization at 
various aging stages under these industrially relevant conditions, 
we observed that in the solution-treated state, Cr precipitates ﬁrst, 
followed by Hf-containing phases (asynchronous co-precipitation), 
consistent with the order reported in the solution-treated state 
by both Bochvar et al. [ 33 ] and Rybalchenko et al. [ 28 ]. How- 
ever, after conventional cold rolling, we observed a shift towards 
synchronous or near-synchronous co-precipitation of Cr and Hf- 
containing phases. This behavior is distinct from the solution- 
treated state and also differs from the speciﬁc sequences reported 
after either ECAP (Hf ﬁrst) or HPT (Cr ﬁrst) processing. More im- 
portantly, based on these observations under conventional process- 
ing, we propose a novel "dislocation-assisted heterogeneous co- 
precipitation" model ( Fig. 11 ). This model, predicated on the dislo- 
cation sensitivity of Hf-containing phase nucleation, differentiates 
the roles of TIIDs and deformation-induced dislocations (DIDs) in 
nucleating the Hf phase. This model provides a mechanistic frame- 
work to explain the transition from asynchronous to synchronous 
co-precipitation observed in our study under conventional process- 
ing conditions, thereby advancing the understanding of how defor- 
mation, particularly industrially relevant cold work, modulates the

Here, we elaborate on this new mode to understand the co- 
precipitation in Cu-Cr-Hf alloys. A well-established aspect of phase 
transformation is that the Cr phase transitions from a coherent to a 
semi-coherent or even incoherent interface during growth, reliev- 
ing transformation stress through the generation of TIIDs [ 55–59 ]. 
Furthermore, it is known that the primary difference between pre- 
deformed and solution-treated alloys is the introduction of a high 
density of DIDs into the copper matrix by cold deformation. It can 
be reasonably assumed that the nucleation of the Hf-containing 
phase is dislocation-sensitive, leading to the proposed dislocation- 
assisted co-precipitation model shown in Fig. 11 . 
In undeformed solution-treated Cu-Cr-Hf alloys (co- 
precipitation Pattern I, Fig. 11 (a–c)), the matrix dislocation 
density is low. The Cr phase preferentially nucleates and grows. As 
the Cr precipitate grows and the interface loses coherency, TIIDs 
form at the Cu/Cr interface to accommodate the lattice mismatch 
and reduce interfacial energy. Direct experimental evidence for 
such TIIDs is presented in Fig. S4. HRTEM imaging combined with 
Inverse Fast Fourier Transform processing clearly visualizes dislo- 
cations localized at the Cu/Cr interface (Fig. S4(c)), and Geometric 
Phase Analysis reveals signiﬁcant strain concentrations associated 
with these dislocations (Fig. S4(d, e)). 
It is important to acknowledge that TIIDs and DIDs gener- 
ally possess distinct crystallographic characteristics (e.g., Burgers 
vectors). TIIDs at FCC/BCC interfaces can be complex, potentially 
involving partials dependent on the interface structure [ 55–59 ], 
while DIDs in FCC Cu are typically perfect a /2 < 110 > dislocations. 
While Fig. S4 conﬁrms the presence of TIIDs and associated strain, 
determining their precise Burgers vectors at these nanoscale inter- 
faces is experimentally challenging and beyond the current scope. 
Crucially, despite potential crystallographic differences, we pro- 
pose that these TIIDs play a functionally similar role to DIDs in 
promoting Hf-containing phase precipitation. In Pattern I, these TI- 
IDs facilitate the nucleation of the Hf-containing phase, leading to 
the observed asynchronous co-precipitation. In pre-deformed Cu- 
Cr-Hf alloys (co-precipitation Pattern II, Fig. 11 (d–f)), the high den- 
sity of DIDs provides abundant nucleation sites. The Hf-containing 
phase gains nucleation conditions comparable to the Cr phase 
at these DIDs. Both TIIDs and DIDs act as potent heterogeneous 
nucleation sites and provide pathways for enhanced solute dif- 
fusion (pipe diffusion) due to their strain ﬁelds and core struc- 
tures. Therefore, Fig. 11 serves as a conceptual illustration of this 
dislocation-assisted mechanism, emphasizing the functional role of 
dislocations (both TIIDs and DIDs) in facilitating co-precipitation, 
rather than a precise crystallographic depiction differentiating 
them by Burgers vector. Consequently, in Pattern II, some Hf- 
containing phases co-nucleate and grow with the Cr phase at DIDs, 
while others may nucleate independently, consistent with observa- 
tions ( Fig. 6 ). 
The proposed dislocation-assisted co-precipitation model helps 
us understand the aging behavior of Cu-Cr-Hf alloys more deeply. 
For instance, in Pattern I, the presence of interfacial dislocations 
implies that the second phase has a relatively larger size and 
an incoherent interface morphology, resulting in a relatively weak 
Orowan strengthening effect ( Eq. (3) ). This explains the lower over- 
all strength of the solution-treated Cu-Cr-Hf alloy observed in 
Fig. 1 , and the signiﬁcant strength enhancement achieved after 
pre-deformation.

the kinetic pathways governing precipitate evolution, particularly

the observed anisotropic Hf-phases ( Figs. 4 and 6 ) and the sim-

potential difference ( /Delta1μ) across the particle population compared 
to a dispersion of smaller individual particles, further reducing the 
overall thermodynamic drive for coarsening. 
Beyond diminishing the thermodynamic driving force, the com- 
posite structure introduces signiﬁcant kinetic barriers that im- 
pede the rate at which coarsening can proceed. The complex in- 
terfaces inherent to the composite structure, such as Cr/Hf-phase 
boundaries and intricate Cr/Hf-phase/Cu triple junctions, may pos- 
sess inherently lower mobility compared to simpler Cr/Cu or Hf- 
phase/Cu interfaces. Factors like unique atomic bonding, speciﬁc 
segregation patterns, or structural complexity at these compos- 
ite interfaces could hinder the atomic attachment/detachment pro- 
cesses required for particle growth and dissolution. This lower in- 
terface mobility acts as a substantial kinetic impediment, slowing 
the coarsening process even when a thermodynamic driving force 
( /Delta1μ) exists. 
Therefore, the enhanced thermal stability stems from a syner- 
gistic effect: a reduction in the thermodynamic driving force for 
coarsening, principally through lowered interfacial energy, com- 
bined with increased kinetic resistance due to the lower mobility 
of the complex composite interfaces. These factors collectively di- 
minish the propensity for and rate of Ostwald ripening. This high 
stability of the composite nanoparticles, in turn, enhances their 
Zener pinning effect [ 51 ], resulting in higher stability of the dislo- 
cation structures within the copper matrix and strongly inhibiting 
matrix recrystallization. This allows for the sustained contribution 
of both precipitation ( /Delta1σp ) and dislocation ( /Delta1σdis ) strengthening 
( Eqs. (2 and 3 )), ultimately leading to the observed excellent re- 
sistance to over-aging softening and superior comprehensive alloy 
performance.

this study provides crucial insights into the alloy’s response to con- 
ventional processing. More signiﬁcantly, the proposed dislocation-

5. Conclusions 
(1) The studied Cu-0.7Cr-0.9Hf alloy, processed via a single- 
pass “solution-cold rolling-aging” route, achieves an excel- 
lent combination of tensile strength (597 MPa) and electri- 
cal conductivity (72 %IACS). Notably, the alloy demonstrates 
remarkable resistance to over-aging softening, maintaining 
high hardness even after prolonged aging at 500 °C in both 
solution-treated and pre-deformed states. 
(2) Novel co-precipitation behaviors involving Cr and Hf- 
containing phases are identiﬁed: asynchronous co- 
precipitation (Cr ﬁrst, then Hf at Cr/Cu interfaces) occurs 
under solution-treated conditions, while synchronous co- 
precipitation (Cr and Hf simultaneously at dislocations) 
dominates in pre-deformed states, supported by microstruc- 
tural observations and precipitation kinetics analysis. 
(3) A dislocation-assisted heterogeneous co-precipitation model 
is proposed, attributing the different co-precipitation modes 
to the distinct roles of TIIDs in solution-treated alloys and 
DIDs in pre-deformed alloys, leveraging the dislocation- 
sensitivity of Hf-containing phase nucleation. 
(4) The formation of composite nanoparticles via co- 
precipitation enhances thermal stability, likely by reducing 
the effective interfacial energy (as supported by our DFT cal- 
culations indicating Hf segregation lowers Cu/Cr interfacial 
energy, see S3) and thus diminishing the driving force for 
coarsening. This suppression of nanoprecipitate coarsening, 
coupled with inhibited dislocation annihilation and matrix 
recrystallization, sustains strengthening contributions and 
leads to the observed superior softening resistance. 
Looking forward, this work highlights promising avenues for 
future research centered on validating and exploiting the princi- 
ple of selective nucleation engineering via characteristic defects. 
Further investigations employing advanced characterization tech- 
niques, such as APT for atomic-scale compositional analysis and in- 
situ TEM for real-time observation of precipitation dynamics, are 
essential to directly verify the proposed interfacial segregation and 
defect-mediated nucleation mechanisms. Beyond validation, signif- 
icant potential lies in exploring the universality of this strategy by 
extending it to other multi-component alloy systems and in de- 
veloping reﬁned thermomechanical processing routes speciﬁcally 
designed to tailor the defect landscape, thereby enabling precise 
control over co-precipitation pathways for targeted optimization of 
material properties. 
The demonstrated combination of high strength, good conduc- 
tivity, and exceptional thermal stability makes the Cu-Cr-Hf al- 
loy processed via this route a promising candidate for demanding 
applications. These include high-performance electrical conductors 
and connectors operating under elevated temperatures or requir- 
ing superior stress relaxation resistance (e.g., in automotive en- 
gines and power generation/distribution systems), resistance weld- 
ing electrodes, lead frames and heat management components in 
high-power density electronics, and potentially structural compo- 
nents in aerospace where thermal stability and robust performance 
are paramount. The insights gained offer a pathway towards de- 
veloping next-generation copper alloys capable of meeting increas- 
ingly stringent service requirements.
