Improvement of the mechanical property of the near- b titanium alloy Ti-10V-
2Fe-3Al (TB6) fabricated by laser additive manufacturing can be achieved
through solution and aging treatment. In this paper, the changes in the
microstructures and mechanical properties of the deposited TB6 specimens
were tested by changing the solution temperature, aging temperature, and
aging time and the effects of the primary a phase (a
p) and secondary a phase
(as) on the mechanical properties were analyzed. The results show that the
solution temperatures mainly determine the volume fraction of ap and grain
boundary a phase ( aGB). Aging temperature and time have large impacts on
the morphology of as. The strength of the solution- and age-treated specimens
are controlled by the volume fraction and size of as. The higher volume frac-
tion, as, obtained by the higher solution temperature could lead to a higher
strength with balanced ductility. The coarser as obtained by the higher aging
temperature or longer aging time will contribute to higher ductility, but de-
crease the strength of the alloy. The fracture mode of tensile specimens is
mainly intergranular fracture due to the strain incompatibility between the
grains and the grain boundaries.
INTRODUCTION
Ti-10V-2Fe/C03Al (TB6),
1 with high speciﬁc
strength, fracture toughness, corrosion resistance,
and excellent hardenability, has been widely used in
aerospace applications such as in the Boeing 777,
2,3
F/A-22, Airbus 350, 3 and so on. 4,5 It is a
metastable b titanium alloy. Conventional
wrought-based processing techniques for TB6 alloys
always have a low material utilization ratio. For the
manufacturing of large-sized and complex-shaped
high-performance titanium alloy components, direc-
ted energy deposition (DED)
6–8 is a method with
higher design ﬂexibility and shorter processing time
compared to conventional methods
9 A large number
of studies 10 have conﬁrmed that, during DED,
materials undergo a complicated thermal history,
resulting in a complex and inhomogeneous struc-
ture of the components. Therefore, in order to make
the structure uniform and to improve the mechan-
ical properties, heat treatment is required.
11 As is
known, solution and aging treatment has been
found to be an effective way to obtain good compre-
hensive mechanical properties of TB6 titanium
alloy.
10,12,13 Terlinde et al.13 suggested that solution
and aging treatment for wrought TB6 alloy will
change the brittleness of the specimens and achieve
a reasonable ductility coupled with a high strength.
The treatment contained two steps: (1) heating to
700–780 /C176 C, holding for 1–3 h, and water-quenching
(WQ); (2) holding at 500–550 /C176 Cfor 6–8 h and air-
cooling (AC). The recommended solution and aging
treatment process was 760 /C176 C/2 h, WQ + 520 /C176 C/
8 h, AC.

A large number of studies15–17 have indicated that
the comprehensive mechanical properties of
wrought TB6 can be adjusted by controlling the
volume fraction, size, and morphology of the a
phase, the continuity of the grain boundary a phase,
and the size of the grains. Well-matched strength
and ductility can be achieved by reducing the
continuity of the a phase at the grain boundaries
and balancing the volume fraction of the primary a
phase (a
p) with the size and volume fraction of the
secondary a phase (as).15 The additive manufactur-
ing process undergoes a series of short-term solid-
state phase transformations, resulting in signiﬁcant
differences in microstructure and in its evolution
between additive manufacturing titanium alloy
components and wrought ones. Therefore, it is
worthwhile to study additive manufactured TB6
alloys, But, so far, there have been a few studies on
the heat treatment for additive manufacturing TB6
alloys, while a large number of studies have focused
on near-a, a + b and other near-b titanium alloys,
18
e.g., Ti6Al4V,19–23 Ti-6Al-2Zr-1Mo-1V,24–27 etc.
In the present study, the microstructures of the
alloy, including the ap and as, have been modiﬁed by
changing the solution temperature, aging tempera-
ture, and aging time. Moreover, the optimized heat
treatment protocol was introduced, and the rela-
tionship between microstructure changes and ten-
sile properties was established to gain insights into
the failure mechanism of the alloy.
EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURES
Spherical TB6 alloy powders used as feedstock in
this work, with a diameter of about 100 lm, were
prepared by the plasma rotation electrode method.
The laser additive manufacturing experiment was
conducted using a laser additive manufacturing
system equipped with a 10-kW ﬁber laser, a set of
four-axis CNC lathes, an argon gas protection
chamber (to ensure that the oxygen content in the
chamber is below 50 ppm during laser deposition),
and a coaxial feed device. The DED processing
parameters were as follows: laser power 5000 W,
beam size 6 mm, laser scanning speed 800–
1500 mm/min, and powder feed rate 15–25 g/min.
The process diagram is shown in Fig. 1a. The
dimensions of the specimen were approximately
300 mm 9 35 mm 9 300 mm (150 layers high),
with the deposition direction parallel to the z-axis.
Subsequent experimental positions were in the
stable deposition zone, as shown in Fig. 1b and c.
After deposition, the specimens were subjected to an
internal stress relief annealing heat treatment at
510 /C176 C for 2 h followed by air-cooling.
The chemical composition (in wt.%) of the powder
of TB6 is listed in Table I and complies with the
allowable speciﬁcation in GB/T 3020.1-2016.
The b-transus temperature of the laser additive
manufactured TB6 alloy is about 805 /C176 C (measured
by the metallographic method). Three groups of
solution and aging treatments were used, as shown
in Table II. According to the regular heat-treatment
process for TB6,
14 this research adopted the control
variable method to investigate the effects of solution
temperature at 730 /C176 C, 760 /C176 C, 790 /C176 C, and 820 /C176 C,
aging temperature at 520 /C176 C, 550 /C176 C, and 580 /C176 C,
and aging time of 2 h, 4 h, and 8 h.
Metallographic specimens were prepared by the
conventional mechanical polishing method. A mix-
ture of 1 ml HF, 6 ml HNO
3, and 100 ml H 2O was
used as the etching agent. The microstructures of
the specimens were characterized using optical
microscopy (OM; LECIA-DM4000M) and scanning
electron microscopy (SEM; JEOL-6010). The micro-
hardness of the specimens was tested by a
FUTURE-TECH DM-800. The parameters for the
microhardness tests were set as: load holding time
15 s, loading load 1000 g, and each sample was
tested at ten points. The average hardness was
obtained as the ﬁnal microhardness. Quantitative
measurements of the width, length, and aspect ratio
of a
p were conducted on at least ﬁve SEM micro-
graphs with a magniﬁcation of 9 2000 for each
specimen. The reason for selecting high magniﬁca-
tion images is that the size of ap is about 5 lm.
The dimensions of the heat-treatment specimens
for the tensile tests were 70 mm 9 35 mm 9 15
mm. After the heat treatments, three specimens
with a length of 70 mm and size U12 mm were cut
by electric discharge wire cutting from the center of
the specimens. Then, the specimens were machined
into the tensile specimens with U5 mm, as shown in
Fig. 1d. The approximately 0.2-mm oxide layer on
the heat-treated component was completely
removed, which did not affect the experimental
results. Room-temperature tensile properties were
tested according to the test standard of ISO 6892-
1:2019 (GB/T228.1-2021). The axial direction of the
specimens was parallel to the deposition direction.
The deformation strain was measured by an exten-
someter. Three tensile test specimens were tested
for each treatment to determine the mechanical
properties. The fracture surfaces and cross-sections
of the tensile test specimens were examined by SEM
and OM, respectively.

CONCLUSION
The effect of solution temperature, aging temper-
ature, and aging time on the microstructure and
tensile properties of the TB6 alloy have been
investigated. The main ﬁndings can be summarized
as follows:
1. The microstructure of the as-deposited TB6
alloy consisted of ultraﬁne a lamellar within
the grains, and the width of the a lamellar was
about 0.6 lm. The as-deposited alloy has good
ductility, but low strength. The ultimate tensile
strength (UTS) was about 919 MPa and yield
strength (YS) was about 894 MPa.
2. An increase of the solution temperature led to
the decrease of the volume fraction of a
p but the
increase of as, which improves the strength by
slightly sacriﬁcing the ductility of the alloy. By
increasing the solution temperature, 33% of
UTS and YS can be increased at the maximum.
The increase of aging temperature or aging
times can cause the coarsening of a
s, resulting in
the degradation of the strength of the alloy with
the increase of ductility.
3. Aging treatment plays a direct role in improving
the strength of the alloy, and the dispersion-
strengthening effect from the ultraﬁne nano-sized
a
s dispersed in theb matrix after aging treatment
can lead to the increase of the strength of the alloy.
