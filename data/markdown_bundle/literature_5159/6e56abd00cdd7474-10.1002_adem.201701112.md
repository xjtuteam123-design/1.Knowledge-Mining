Effects of Nb on Microstructure and Mechanical
Properties of Ti42Al2.6C Alloys
Hongze Fang, Ruirun Chen,* Xue Gong, Yanqing Su, Hongsheng Ding, Jingjie Guo,
and Hengzhi Fu
Ti42Al2.6C–xNb (x ¼ 0, 2, 4, 6, and 8 at%, similarly hereinafter) alloys are
synthesized by melting to investigate the effects of Nb on microstructure and
mechanical properties, especially the formation of B2 phase and high
temperature tensile properties. The results show that lamellar colony size
decreases from 33.3 to 13.2 mm and lamellar space decreases from 193.4 to
139.6 nm with increasing Nb from 0 to 8%. Lattice parameters of
γ and α2
phase change and B2 phase forms among lamellar colonies with the addition
of Nb. The volume fraction of B2 phases and content of Nb in Ti
2AlC phases
increase with increasing Nb. B2 phases form between lamellar colonies
because of Nb segregation. As high-melting-point element, Nb increases
heterogenous nucleation particles and impedes grain growing. Compressive
strength increases from 1568 to 1912 MPa with increasing Nb from 0 to 6%
and then decreases at 8% Nb. Tensile strength at 750
/C14 C can reach 465 MPa
and the strain is only 2.4% when the Nb content is 6%. The increased
strength is resulted from refined microstructure and solid solution of Nb.
And the decreased strength is because many B2 phases acts as crack source.
1. Introduction
Excellent TiAl-based alloys offer a great potential to replace
nickel-based super alloys as blade material in gas and aero
engines as well as in turbo chargers.
[1] First applications were
successfully realized for turbocharger wheels and low pressure
turbine blades.
[2,3] T o expand the applied range of TiAl alloys at
high temperature, additional phases need to be produced by
alloying during the casting, such as additions of Nb, W , Mo, Zr,
T a, Cr, or interstitial elements such as C, B, N, and O.
[4–8]
Among the alloying elements, carbon has an ef ﬁcient
hardening effect on TiAl intermetallic alloys if the ﬁne carbide
dispersion precipitates are obtained. [9,10] The carbon addition
can also improve the high temperature strength and creep
resistance of TiAl alloys effectively.
[11,12] The research has
indicated that TiAl matrix composites containing carbides show
excellent mechanical properties and oxidation resistance at
elevated temperature.
[13–15] As an intermediate product of the
Ti–Al–C reaction system, Ti 2AlC is formed
from TiC, formulated from melting TiAl 3
and C, and melting TiAl. [16] Ti2AlC ternary
carbide belongs to a special class of
materials known as layered ternary com-
pound.
[17] It has been reported that Ti 2AlC
has a hexagonal structure with a P63/mmc
space group, with the lattice parameters of
a ¼ 0.304 nm and c ¼ 1.360 nm. And it is
composed of carbide layers interleaved
with layers of element Al atoms by metallic
type.
[18,19] Due to its unique crystal struc-
ture, Ti 2AlC combines the merits of
ceramics and metals, which possesses high
electrical conductivity, excellent machin-
ability, high yield strength, and signi ﬁcant
plasticity at high temperature.
[20] In order
to prepare Ti2AlC ternary carbide, there are
two kinds of methods. One is the sintering
by using proper starting reactants, includ-
ing hot pressure (HP) sintering,
[21] hot
isostatic pressure (HIP) sintering, [22] and
spark plasma sintering (SPS). [23] The other
one is the combustion synthesis reaction by using mixed
reactant powders.
[24] According to the previous literatures there
are some problems in the preparation of composites by the
powder metallurgy technology, such as high requirement for raw
material, non-densi ﬁcation of microstructure and complicated
process. Hence, casting method was designed to prepare TiAl
matrix composites in this work because it method is convenient
and inexpensive.
Refractory metal of Nb is a strong
β-stabilizing alloying
element for TiAl alloys. When the β phase is retained with β-
stabilizing alloying element during the solidiﬁcation process, the
B2 phase will exist in the matrix. [25,26] In the TiAl alloys the
matrix is composed of γ (TiAl) and α2 (Ti3Al).[27] The γ phase is
the face-centered square structure of L10 with ordered super-
lattice structure and the
α2 phase is the hexagonal structure of
DO19 crystalline structure. The alloy can maintain a metastable
B2 phase with a ﬁne grained structure, and have favorable high-
temperature deformation behaviors. [28] In recent years, it was
reported[29,30] that B2 phase served as a grain-re ﬁning and
plastic-enhancing component in TiAl intermetallics at high
temperature. B2 phase has a body centered cubic lattice structure
and is softer than the
α2 and β phases at elevated temperature.

possessed quite promising improvements in both monotonic
fracture toughness and fatigue threshold value. Shu et al.

2. Experimental Section
Raw materials were titanium sponge (99.98% purity), aluminum
(99.98% purity), graphite powder (99.9% purity), and Al –Nb
master alloy (the content of Nb is 70 wt%). Ti42Al2.6C ingots
with different contents of Nb element, which were Ti42Al2.6C –
xNb ( x ¼ 0, 2, 4, 6, 8%), were prepared by the vacuum arc
melting. The mass of the ingots was the same and the weight was
80 g. The ingot melted completely (the molten metal can ﬂow
with arc force and there was no unmelted solid in it), and then
decreased current of 50 A every 15 s decreasing from 650 to 50 A.
Every ingot was melted ﬁve times to ensure the same solidi ﬁed
condition of ingots and make the microstructure uniformed. The
phase constituents of the ingots and the specimens were
examined by X-ray diffraction (XRD). The ingot was cut in half
longitudinally, and then polished and etched by corrosion liquid
of 5%HF þ 5%HNO
3 þ 90%H2O (volume fraction) for about
15 s. The microstructure of the ingots were examined by
Olympus GX71 Optical Microscope (OM) and Quanta 200F and
Japan TM3030 scanning electron microscopy (SEM) equipped
with Energy Dispersive Spectrometer (EDS). The cylindrical
specimens with the size of Ø4 /C2 6 mm were used for
compressive testing, which was operated on Electronic Universal
Material T esting Machine (AG-X plus250kN) with 0.5 mm min
/C0 1
loading rate. The size of the tensile specimens at 750 /C14 C was
18 mm /C2 3m m /C2 2 mm using the Instron 5569 Electronic
Universal Material T esting Machine with 0.3 mm min/C0 1 loading
rate. Every date must be achieved by testing three times and the
last date was average numerical value. These mechanical
properties were tested following Chinese National Standard
for test methods of mechanical properties of metals materials,
and the size of specimens and loading rate were all following this
standard.

high solubility in TiAl-based alloys and forms the stable β solid
solution at the high temperature. Through the order transfor-

With comprehensive analysis of microstructure and mechan-
ical, the mechanism of improving properties can be revealed.
The
α2 phase is of hcp structure and the slip system is limited
compared with γ phase. Iqbal et al. [46] found that the plastic
deformation can occur inside the γ phases, which is always
preceded by strong local elastic deformation using in situ
deformation in the Atomic Force Microscope. Therefore, the
mechanical properties is improved with the increase of the
volume fraction of
γ phase. Another reason shows that the effect
of solution strengthening of Nb and grain boundary strength-
ening are beneﬁcial to the mechanical properties. The reinforced
phases are the Ti 2AlC phases in the Ti42Al2.6C- xNb alloys. The
presence of dispersed Ti 2AlC particles leads to zigzag crack
propagation in the alloys. Carbide particles, including Ti 2AlC,
increases the fracture energy of TiAl matrix alloys. The content of
B2 phase is disadvantage to the mechanical properties, especially
the content of 8% Nb, which will reduce the mechanical
properties. It can be deduced that the B2 phases are usually
employed to act as crack sources during deformation process.
Other studies have drawn similar conclusions.
[47] As demon-
strated by Xu and Lin, [48] the deformation incompatibility of the
B2 phase and matrix induces microcracks between their
interfaces and reduces the ductility of the alloy. Lee et al.
[49]
suggested that the continuity of the lamellar microstructure was
disrupted by B2 phase, which is a main factor for the decline of
the properties.
In the future, the proper heat treatment or addition of alloying
element with low diffusion rate will be used into the
Ti42Al6Nb2.6C alloy to eliminate the segregation phenomena
of Nb and the formation of B2 phases. The solid solution
strengthening of Nb can increase the mechanical properties. T a
diffuses slower than Nb because of its higher position in the
periodic table.
[37] It is expected to slow down the growth of
lamellae. Further work is underway to investigate the effect of T a
on the ductility in alloys with low T a contents by grain-reﬁned.
[50]
At present, there is little research on the effect of low content of
T a by vacuum arc melting enhanced by Ti 2AlC phases in the
Ti42Al2.6C-xNb alloys.
4. Conclusions
The Ti42Al2.6C- xNb alloys reinforced by Ti 2AlC were synthe-
sized. Effects of Nb on phase composition, microstructure,
compression properties, tensile properties, and fracture behav-
iors were systematically investigated. The related mechanism
was revealed. Several major ﬁndings are summarized as follow.
1) With addition of Nb into Ti42Al2.6C alloys, lattice distortion
of
γ and α2 phases increases due to solid solution; B2 phase
forms when the content of Nb is above 4% due to the
segregation of Nb.
