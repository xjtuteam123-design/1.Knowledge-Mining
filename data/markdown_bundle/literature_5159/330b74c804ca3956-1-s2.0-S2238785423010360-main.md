abstract
This study involves investigation of metallurgical and mechanical properties of C-103
niobium alloy welded by electron beam. ICP, XRD and EDS method were used to determine
the composition and phase contents of the alloy. Electron beam welding was carried out on
specimens with various currents and transverse speeds. To examine macro- and micro-
structural characteristics of the welded samples, stereographic, optical, electron scan-
ning microscopy and EDS methods were used. Also, mechanical properties including
micro-hardness proﬁle and tensile strength were measured. It was observed that C-103
alloy has HfO
2 precipitates with two different morphologies of spherical coarse and ﬁne
particles. Within the weld zone, primary precipitates were dissolved and new nanometer-
sized ones were formed regularly throughout this zone. Morphology of the formed pre-
cipitates was mostly spherical and rarely ﬁlament-like. Due to greater number and smaller
size of precipitates in weld zone, hardness of weld zone was more than HAZ and base
metal zone. Tensile strength of welded sample was 317.5 MPa which is equal to 55% of
tensile strength of base metal. Fracture of all welded samples occurred within weld zone
which can be referred to enlargement of grains in this zone. The features presented on
fracture surface of unprocessed C-103 alloy, such as cross section reduction, formation of
dimples, opaque appearance of fracture surface indicate ductile nature of fracture. How-
ever, emerging of relatively smooth and sharp surface, abundant vein patterns and
cleavage planes obviously indicate brittle fracture. HfO
2 precipitates, as well as dissolved
oxygen in the matrix, signiﬁcantly affected metallurgical and mechanical properties of
welded C-103 alloy samples.

1. Introduction
C-103 niobium alloy contains alloying elements of 10%wt Hf
and 1%wt Ti. Hafnium (Hf) provides excellent solid solution
strengthening: strengthening extending to temperatures as
high as ~1400
/C14 C. Hf also acts as a sacriﬁcial element for any
internal oxidation. On the other hand, a small quantity of Ti
improves the overall oxidation resistance [ 1]. C-103 alloy is
malleable and machinable, but these properties diminish by
reaction with atmospheric gases during the manufacturing
process [ 2]. The alloy microstructure contains alloying-

element oxide particles for grain size control and dispersion
strengthening [3]. Compared to pure niobium, C-103 has more
high temperature strength and less sensitivity to recrystalli-
zation and softening. As a result of numerous efforts in
developing innovated alloys in space age of 1960s, this alloy as
a substitute for weak Nb-based alloys, such as Nb e1Zr, was
introduced, while it still remained the former alloys’ desired
formability and weldability [ 4]. C-103 is used in important
ﬁelds including thrust chambers, radiation nozzle extensions,

solid propellant expansion nozzle, solid-propellant rockets,
etc. [5,6]. Recently, this alloy is applied in heat pipes cooling at
stagnation regions, such as nose caps, wing and tail leading
edges hypersonic vehicles [ 7].
Comparing with pure niobium, Nb alloys have higher me-
chanical strength but less plasticity; hence, they are very
sensitive to atmospheric contaminations during welding and
heat treatment processes. These alloys also are very reactive
and react with all common gases i.e. nitrogen, oxygen,
hydrogen and carbon dioxide [ 8]. Electron beam welding
(EBW) and laser beam welding (LBW) can be used for these
alloys. Furthermore, resistance welding (RW) with a limited
welding time to 1 or 2 cycles and equipped with circulating
cooling water to suppress the generated heat fast, is a suitable
method [ 9]. Gas tungsten arc welding (GTAW) is also a rec-
ommended method for niobium welding [ 10].
According to increasingly demand for higher speed and
lower distortion during welding process, EBW is found as an
efﬁcient technique for coalescence of engineering parts [ 11].
The main advantages of this welding technique include high
weld depth to width ratio, defect-free welded parts, very small
HAZ and high efﬁciency [ 12,13]. Moreover, unlike other
welding methods, EBW remains the purity of base metal
within HAZ and weld zone [ 14]. Generating intense energy
density and eliminating destructive side factors through
making vacuum conditions, EBW has high capacities to
welding dissimilar metals [ 15e17]. The major applications of
this welding method are automobile, nuclear, electrical engi-
neering and aerospace industries [ 16].
Budkin [18] evaluated the physical and chemical properties
of EBW welded refractory metals. He studied weldability of
these metals under different conditions of welding process.
Budkin [19] also developed the process physical-mathematical
model for EBW of refractory metals to steel. Hajitabar and
Naffakh-Moosavy [20] investigated the effect of electron beam
welding current variations on the microstructure and me-
chanical properties of Nb e1Zr advanced alloy. It was found
that welds showed a cellular structure, and intercellular
dendrites in the central region of the weld have been caused
due to microsegregations created between the cells.
Bexiga et al. [ 21] researched the effect of heat input on the
microstructure and strength of the welded niobium specimen
by GTAW technique. It was found that increase of weld heat
input coarsens the microstructure in melted and heat affected
zones. The hardness and tensile strength of the weld also
increase due to the formation of precipitates due to the
introduction of oxygen and/or nitrogen through the electric
arc. Sankar et al. [ 22] studied the impact of internal oxidation
on the microstructure and mechanical properties of C-103 Nb-
based alloy and it was found that increase of the oxygen
content of alloy, decrease gradually the strength and ductility
of welded samples. Ashish Dhole et al. [1] investigated the role
of the metal-oxide Interface 's terminating layer on the selec-
tive cold cracking of a commercial Niobium eHafniumeTita-
nium (C-103) alloy. They observed that some high-angle grain
boundaries containing coarse HfO2 secondary phase pre-
cipitates suffered cold cracking due to cold rolling of the alloy.
It established that interface with one Nb atomic layer bonded
with oxygen (HfO
2) is weak and provides a site for easy
separation.
Reviewing the available publications in this ﬁeld, it is
realized that a few researches have been done regarding to
welding of Nb alloys, especially in regard to C-103 alloy ac-
cording to its wide important uses in advanced industries
such as aerospace, military and nuclear. In addition, there is
no published research about the effect of HfO
2 particles on
metallurgical and mechanical properties of welded C-103 alloy
via EBW. In this study, the inﬂuence of HfO 2 precipitates fea-
tures in metallurgical and mechanical properties of C-103
alloy welded by EBW method has been investigated.
2. Materials and methods
2.1. Base metal
In this study, cold rolled sheet niobium-based alloy C-103 with
2 mm thickness has been used. To determine precisely the
composition of this alloy, ICP technique (VARIAN 730-ES
model) was applied and the result is summarized in Table 1 .

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Characteristics of base metal
XRD patterns of C-103 alloy ( Fig. 2 ) exhibits that this alloy
consists of a single phase and alloying elements are presented
in the form solid solution. According to Fig. 2 , it can be seen
that the peaks of this graph shift slightly to the left and the
angle 2q decreases with respect to pure niobium. According to
the Bragg relation (Equation (1)), as the Bragg angle decreases,
the distance between the crystal planes and consequently the
lattice parameter increases. Therefore, the alloying elements
present in this alloy, which is often hafnium, are dissolved to
form a substitutional solid solution in the niobium phase, thus
increasing the size of the lattice parameter. Also, due to the
higher atomic radius of hafnium (159 pm) than niobium (146
pm) during solid solution formation, deﬂection occurs in the
niobium crystal lattice and as a result the lattice parameter
increases and according to the Bragg relation, the angle q
should decrease. This decrease is observed in the XRD anal-
ysis of Fig. 2.

During electron beam welding of C-103 alloy, with
increasing temperature and melting of the alloy, due to
complete dissolution of the initial HfO
2 precipitation in the
melt and insufﬁcient time for oxygen to exit, oxygen remains
dissolved in the melt. Due to the higher melting temperature
of HfO2 (2810 /C14 C) than the melting temperature of C-103 alloy
(2350 /C14 C), the ﬁrst solid phase created during solidiﬁcation of
melt pool is nanometer-sized HfO 2 precipitates, which is
formed as a result of in-situ oxidation of hafnium by dissolved
oxygen in melt pool (step 3 in Fig. 8 ). Of course, due to the
elevated rate of cooling in EBW, these precipitates did not ﬁnd
sufﬁcient opportunity to grow.
At the end of solidiﬁcation, the a-Nb matrix is frozen. It is
predicted that due to the high solidiﬁcation rate in electron
beam welding, all dissolved oxygen in the molten pool does
not react with hafnium and part of the oxygen remains solu-
ble in the a-Nb matrix. This dissolved oxygen reduces the
mechanical properties, which will be explained in the section
on the mechanical properties of welds.

electron beam welding was investigated. The main conclu-

formed as extended strips with non-uniform distribution
within the alloy grains.

during solidiﬁcation, white ﬁne HfO
2 precipitates were
formed with uniform distribution throughout the fusion
