ABSTRACT 
Due to the low recrystallization temperature and rapid growth of recrystallized grains in high-lithium content 
Mg-Li alloy. Nanoscale high-lithium Mg-Li alloys are difficult to obtain by traditional processing. This paper 
found that the homogenization treatment of the as-cast Mg-14Li-0.5Ni alloy causes the transformation of the bcc 
structure to an hcp structure, which can effectively improve the strength and plasticity. After further rolling at 
room temperature, a nanocrystalline strengthened β -phase Mg-Li alloy was successfully prepared, and its 
strength was 150% higher than that of the as-cast alloy. 
1. Introduction 
Magnesium alloys have attracted much attention as candidate metals 
for engineering and implant materials due to their light weight and good 
biocompatibility [1,2] . However, due to its inherent hcp structure, its 
plasticity is poor, which greatly limits its application. Li can reduce the 
value of c/a and activate < c + a > dislocation slip [3,4] . When the Li 
content exceeds 10.3 wt%, the β phase with the crystal structure of bcc 
appears, which greatly improves the plasticity. For instance, the elon -
gation of Mg-16Li alloys can reach more than 35% [5] . However, the Li 
atoms make the magnesium alloy soft. Researchers have done much 
work to improve the strength of β -phase Mg-Li alloys. 
Due to the large K value of magnesium alloys, grain refinement is one 
of the commonly used and effective methods for strengthening magne -
sium alloys [6,7] . Ultrafine or nanosized grains obtained through severe 
plastic deformation and other methods can greatly improve the strength 
[8,9] . However, the addition of Li reduces the recrystallization tem -
perature, and the dynamically recrystallized grains grow rapidly during 
deformation even at room temperature. Nanograined Mg-Li alloys usu -
ally need to be obtained under the cooling conditions of liquid nitrogen 
[10] , which increases production costs and difficulty. 
In this work, a large number of phase boundaries were introduced 
into the Mg-14Li-0.5Ni alloy by heat treatment, which creates condi -
tions for dynamic recrystallization (DRX). Subsequently, a nano -
crystalline strengthened magnesium-lithium alloy was obtained by room 
temperature rolling. 
2. Materials and methods 
Mg-14Li-0.5Ni (wt.%) was prepared by melting pure magnesium 
(99.95 wt%), lithium (99.90 wt%), and Mg-20 wt% Ni master alloy in an 
induction melting furnace under ambient argon gas. The melt was 
poured into a steel mold to obtain as-cast specimens. The density of the 
alloy measured by the Archimedes principle was 1.42 g/cm
3
. The as-cast 
alloy was homogenized at 200 
◦
C for 20 h and quenched. The homog -
enized and as-cast alloys were rolled at room temperature at a rolling 
speed of 500 cm/min, with a reduction of 0.2 mm per pass and a total 
reduction rate of 80%. The rolled specimen from the as-cast and the 
rolled alloy are named C-rolled and H-rolled, respectively. The tensile 
tests were conducted with dog-bone specimens with a gage length of 18 
mm and a diameter of 3 mm along the rolling direction (RD) on an 
INSTRON-5982 tester at a strain rate of 0.5 mm/min. The microstruc -
ture evolution was characterized by transmission electron microscopy 
(TEM, FEI Talos F200X G2). Thin foils for TEM were prepared by me -
chanical polishing (~40 μ m) and ion beam thinning (GATAN, PIPS II 
695) at 160 
◦
C.

Fig. 1 c shows the engineering tensile stress – strain curves of the as- 
cast and homogenized alloys. After homogenization, the ultimate ten -
sile strength is increased to 127 MPa. It is worth noting that the increase 
in strength does not sacrifice plasticity. The uniform elongation is 
increased from 9% to 15%, and the fracture elongation is increased from 
30% to 50%. The change in microstructure before and after homoge -
nization indicates that the increase in strength is mainly caused by the 
boundaries between α and β hindering the movement of dislocations. 
The α phase introduced by homogenization has a higher ability to pro -
duce dislocations than the matrix [11,12] , compared with the as-cast 
alloy, the homogenized alloy can produce more dislocations to adapt 
continuous deformation, thus, the uniform elongation is improved. On 
the other hand, the evenly distributed α phase disperses the stress of the 
matrix during plastic deformation, thereby avoiding the stress concen -
tration of the coarse grains and improving the ductility. In particular, it 
can be clearly seen in the Fig. 1 b that the layers of the α phase almost 
fully separate and surround the grains of the β phase. Further careful 
observation showed that the contact angle between the α phase and β 
phase is zero, which indicates that the grain boundary is completely 
wetting [13,14] . The β phase substitutes a GB separating both grains by a 
continuous layer. This is also conducive to the transfer of dislocations 
from the α phase to β phase or GB sliding, which is another reason for the 
improvement of the plasticity [15,16] . 
Fig. 2 a is the bright field TEM image of the C-rolled alloy. The alloy 
undergoes complete dynamic recrystallization after rolling at room 
temperature, and the average grain size is approximately 0.4 μ m. Fig. 2 b 
is the bright field TEM image of the H-rolled alloy, and Fig. 2 c is the 
corresponding dark field image. The recrystallized grain size is signifi -
cantly reduced to 80 nm, and the distribution of grain size is shown in 
the inset of Fig. 2 b. In addition, an α phase with a size of 50 nm was also 
detected, which is marked by a red arrow in Fig. 2 b. Fig. 2 d is an HRTEM 
image taken from the < 11 2 0 > zone axis of the α phase, and the inset is 
the corresponding FFT. This indicates that the α phase is also refined 
during the rolling process. The recrystallized grain size of the C-rolled 
alloy is 5 times that of the H-rolled alloy, which indicates that homog -
enization before rolling can effectively refine the recrystallized grain 
size.

DRX requires a critical dislocation density, and the higher microstruc -
ture heterogeneity increases the deformation strain during rolling, 
leading to an increase in dislocation density, providing a favorable 
condition for the occurrence of DRX. In addition, the boundaries be -

The engineering stress – strain curves of C-rolled and H-rolled alloys 
are shown in Fig. 3 a. The strength of the nanograined sample is 1.5 times 
that of the ultrafine-grained sample, which indicates that nanograins 
can effectively strengthen the β phase Mg-Li alloy. Fig. 3 b is the corre -
sponding true stress – strain curve, Fig. 3 c is the work hardening rate-true 
strain curve, and it can be seen that the work-hardening rate of the 
ultrafine-grained sample is greater than 0 after yielding. The nano -
structured sample lost the work hardening ability, and the work hard -
ening rate remained < 0 after yielding. This is mainly because both 
nucleation and motion of dislocations are suppressed in the nanograined 
sample [19] , and there are not enough dislocations to adapt to the 
subsequent strain, so the nanostructured sample exhibits higher strength 
and lower plasticity compared with the ultrafine-grained sample. On the 
other hand, in the rolled alloy, there is no continuous layer between α 
and β ( Fig. 2 d), the grain boundary becomes incomplete wetting, which 
indicates the transition from complete to incomplete wetting appeared 
during the rolling process. This transformation should be responsible for 
plastic loss. 
4. Conclusions 
The nanocrystalline strengthened Mg-14Li-0.5Ni alloy with a density 
of 1.42 g/cm
3 
is obtained by prehomogenization and room temperature 
rolling, its strength is increased by 150% compared with that of the as- 
cast Mg-14Li-0.5Ni alloy. Prehomogenization can induce the trans -
formation from the β phase to the α phase to improve the work hard -
ening ability. This not only improves the strength and plasticity but also 
creates conditions for the generation of nanocrystals in the subsequent 
rolling process. This article provides a new strategy for the manufacture 
of Mg-Li alloys strengthened by nanostructures.
