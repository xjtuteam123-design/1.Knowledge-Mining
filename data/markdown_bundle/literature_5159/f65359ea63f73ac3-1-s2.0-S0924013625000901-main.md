ABSTRACT
The introduction of alloying elements is an effective strategy to enhance the performance of titanium aluminides 
(TiAl). Wire arc directed energy deposition (DED) has cost advantages and high deposition rate, making it an 
emerging technology for fabricating TiAl alloys with promising application prospects. However, the efficient and 
flexible introduction of alloy elements for wire arc DED fabricated TiAl alloys is a key concern. In the present 
research, niobium (Nb) was selected as the alloying element, pure Ti, Al, and Nb wires were used as raw ma -
terials to introduce Nb into TiAl alloy via in-situ alloying using Triple-wire arc DED. This approach successfully 
fabricated the high Nb TiAl alloy Ti-45Al-8Nb and the feasibility of this method was confirmed. By comparing the 
phase composition, microstructure, and mechanical properties of Nb-containing Ti45Al8Nb and Nb-free Ti45Al 
fabricated by wire arc DED, the influence of Nb on the microstructure and strengthening mechanisms of TiAl 
alloys was revealed. The results indicate that Nb alloying significantly reduces internal defects, promotes the 
formation of the B2 phase, increases the content of γ phase and lamellar spacing, and refines the lamellar col -
onies. The solid solution strengthening, grain refinement strengthening, and dislocation strengthening effects 
induced by Nb alloying substantially enhanced the tensile strength and elongation of the TiAl alloy. The process 
for fabricating ternary TiAl alloys via wire arc DED proposed in this study utilizes three pure metal wires as raw 
materials without relying on the expensive and difficult-to-obtain pre-alloyed materials required in traditional 
processes. This approach demonstrates the capability of wire arc DED in fabricating multi-component TiAl alloys 
with controllable compositions.
1. Introduction
Mass reduction is an eternal theme in the development of aerospace 
weapons and equipment. The theoretical density of TiAl alloys is merely 
half that of nickel-based superalloys, and they can serve for extended 
periods in high-temperature oxidative environments exceeding 600
◦
C
[1,2] , making them an ideal material choice for mass reduction in 
aerospace engine design [3,4] . Research on TiAl alloys has been persis -
tent since the 1950s.
Traditional manufacturing techniques for TiAl alloy encompass 
precision casting and forging [5,6] . Additive manufacturing technology, 
with its significant advantages in manufacturing complex geometric 
shapes and the designability of composition and structure, is particularly 
suitable for processing TiAl alloys which are difficult to achieve with 
conventional manufacturing methods. In recent years, certain 
breakthroughs have been made in the preparation of TiAl alloys with 
additive manufacturing technology. L.E. Murr et al. [7] utilized Electron 
Beam Melting (EBM) technology to fabricate Ti47Al2Cr2Nb, which 
exhibited a microstructure with fine grains measuring 2 μ m, demon -
strating the capability of EBM technology in producing near-net-shaped 
and complex TiAl alloy components. Xue et al. [8] employed Laser Metal 
Deposition (LMD) technology to prepare high Nb TiAl alloy Ti48Al8Nb. 
The room-temperature tensile strength reached 880 MPa, which is 1.71 
times that of the cast alloy with the same composition, showcasing the 
superiority of additive manufacturing technology in terms of perfor -
mance. Additionally, Ma et al. [9,10] successfully fabricated binary TiAl 
alloys based on wire arc direct energy deposition (DED) technology by 
feeding Ti and Al wires into the same molten pool, overcoming the 
limitations of processing suitable TiAl wires due to the intrinsic brit -
tleness of TiAl alloys. Wang et al. [11,12] developed a dual-wire plasma

Binary TiAl alloys demonstrate superior high-temperature strength 
and creep resistance; however, the intrinsic brittleness and limited 
ductility at room temperature have restricted their broader application 
[14] . To augment the performance and workability of TiAl alloys, the 
incorporation of alloying elements is essential including grain-refining 
elements (B, Y, C, Si), elements that optimize mechanical properties 
(Nb, Mo, Cr, V, Mn), and elements that improve oxidation resistance (Ta, 
Mo, W) [15,16] . In terms of methods for introducing refractory alloying 
elements into additively manufactured TiAl alloys, for powder-based 
additive manufacturing techniques such as LMD and EBM, alloying 
can be achieved by preparing pre-alloyed powders containing the 
desired alloying elements or by directly mixing alloying element pow -
ders with TiAl base powders and melting them employing a heat source 
[17,18] .
In wire-based additive manufacturing, due to the high melting points 
of refractory alloying elements in TiAl alloys, these elements are typi -
cally pre-alloyed with Ti to produce wires, which reduces the raw ma -
terial melting point and promotes uniform distribution of the alloying 
elements. Subsequently, a dual-wire feeding approach is adopted and 
the alloying elements are introduced through in-situ alloying by melting 
all wires with the heat source. Zhou et al. [22] successfully achieved the 
fabrication of the second-generation TiAl alloy Ti48Al2Cr2Nb using 
customized Ti-xCr-yNb wire and pure Al wire, with performance com -
parable to traditional methods. Fu et al. [19] used pre-alloyed TiNb wire 
and pure Al wire as raw materials and employed wire arc DED with a 
large arc length transfer mode to eliminate composition segregation, 
successfully fabricating Ti2AlNb alloy with uniform composition and 
excellent properties. Li et al. [20,21] used electron beam as the heat 
source with similar raw materials to implement dual-wire additive 
manufacturing of Ti-22Al-25Nb alloys and developed a closed-loop 
control system aimed at controlling the droplet transfer distance, 
thereby enhancing elemental uniformity and morphology.
In contrast to the conventional approach of pre-alloying refractory 
elements with Ti to fabricate wire material feedstock, this study employs 
pure refractory metal wires as independent raw materials. Through 
Triple-wire arc DED, synchronous melting and in-situ alloying reactions 
between refractory metal wires, Ti wires, and Al wires are achieved, 
ultimately enabling the precise introduction of refractory elements into 
TiAl alloy. This study specifically selects plasma arc as the heat source, 
based on its advantageous characteristics of high energy density. The 
higher temperatures provided by the plasma arc, along with the con -
vection within the melt pool, are expected to sufficiently melt refractory 
metal wires and promote uniform distribution of elements. In this study, 
we will first validate the feasibility of the scheme; subsequently, we will 
delve into the distribution of refractory metal elements in TiAl alloys 
and their impact on the microstructure and mechanical properties of 
TiAl alloys.
The impetus driving the current work is twofold. First, taking the 
introduction of the refractory metal Nb as a case study, we use pure Ti, 
Al, and Nb wires as raw materials and employ plasma arc as the heat 
source to achieve in-situ alloying preparation of the third-generation 
TiAl alloy Ti45Al8Nb through the Triple-wire arc DED. This work 
aims to demonstrate a flexible and efficient in-situ alloying method for 
processing multi-component TiAl alloys. Second, we compare the 
microstructure and mechanical properties of Nb-containing Ti45Al8Nb 
samples with Nb-free Ti45Al samples to investigate the microstructural 
effects and strengthening mechanisms of Nb alloying. The findings 
provides essential experimental data for the design and fabrication of 
high-performance TiAl alloys by wire arc DED.

3. Results
3.1. Deposition process
High-speed photography was employed to observe the wire arc DED 
process fabricating TiAl alloys. During the deposition process of Ti45Al 
presented in Fig. 3 a, a typical dual-wire liquid bridge transition phe -
nomenon was observed. Specifically, the Ti and Al wires jointly formed a 
molten droplet, which transferred into the molten pool via a liquid 
bridge. This phenomenon indicates that the droplets from the two wires 
can stably transition under the action of the liquid bridge, ensuring the 
continuous stability of the molten pool. In the deposition process of 
Ti45Al8Nb shown in Fig. 3 b, Nb wire melts and mixes with the droplets 
formed by the Ti and Al wires, which are transferred via the liquid 
bridge, within the molten pool. Whether in the dual-wire arc DED of 
Ti45Al or the Triple-wire arc DED of Ti45Al8Nb, the wires can stably 
enter the molten pool without material loss. The above phenomena 
indicate, to some extent, that pure refractory metal wire (pure Nb wire 
in this study) is feasible as the raw material for manufacturing high Nb 
TiAl alloy via wire arc DED. To provide an intuitive understanding of the 
droplet transition process, the video links of the deposition processes for 
both samples are provided at the end page of this paper.

3.2. Chemical composition and phase identification
During the wire arc DED process, in-situ alloying reaction occurs 
between the Ti, Al, and Nb wires within the molten pool, necessitating 
the verification of chemical composition uniformity. Fig. 4 a,b illustrates 
the chemical compositions of the Ti45Al8Nb and Ti45Al samples 
determined via ICP-OES. The average chemical composition of the 
Ti45Al8Nb sample was found to be 47.04 ± 0.27 at% Ti, 44.84 
± 0.22 at% Al, and 8.12 ± 0.23 at% Nb. For the Ti45Al sample, the 
average chemical composition was 54.87 ± 0.33 at% Ti and 45.13 
± 0.33 at% Al. The element compositions of both samples fluctuate 
around the target content, indicating that under the action of plasma arc 
in-situ alloying, the chemical composition within the molten pool is 
relatively uniform. The uniformity of elements is closely related to the 
selection of the heat source, indicating that the plasma arc is conducive 
to element homogenization. The relevant element mixing mechanisms 
will be discussed in Section 4.1 .

3.3. Morphology
As demonstrated in the stereomicroscopy images of Ti45Al and 
Ti45Al8Nb specimens shown in Fig. 5 a,c, no significant macroscopic 
defects were observed in the stereoscope images of both alloys. Micro- 
CT was utilized to detect internal defects in two samples. Fig. 5 b,d pre -
sents the CT-3D reconstructed volume images of the Ti45Al and 
Ti45Al8Nb samples. A significant number of defects can be clearly 
observed in the Ti45Al sample, with a total defect volume of 
0.098 mm³ , which may considerably reduce the plasticity of material. 
In contrast, the defect volume in the Ti45Al8Nb sample was notably 
reduced to 0.019 mm³ , indicating that Nb alloying effectively improved 
the internal defect condition of the sample, contributing to enhanced 
strength and plasticity.
Through comparative analysis of the microstructure of Ti45Al8Nb 
and Ti45Al sample in Fig. 6 , several significant differences were 
observed. In the top region of Ti45Al sample as depicted in Fig. 6 a, the 
microstructure primarily consists of columnar grains with a small 
amount of dendrites. EDS compositional analysis of the dendritic region 
(Point 1) and the lamellar region (Point 2) revealed a higher Al content 
in the dendritic region. EDS mapping analysis of the top region of Ti45Al 
sample, as shown in Fig. 7 a, further confirmed the presence of Al 
segregation phenomenon. According to Fig. 6 b, the microstructure of 
the middle region of Ti45Al is predominantly equiaxed lamellar col -
onies, accompanied by intergranular cracks. The EDS point 3 – 4 and 
mapping results ( Fig. 6 b) for this region indicate no significant 
elemental segregation, suggesting that thermal cycling effectively mit -
igates elemental segregation in the dendritic region.
In the microstructure of Ti45Al8Nb sample, microcracks were hardly 
observed in either the top or middle regions of Ti45Al8Nb sample, 
indicating that the alloying of Nb significantly improved defect condi -

top and middle regions of Ti45Al8Nb are composed of equiaxed grains, 
with the lamellar colony size in the middle region being noticeably fine.

3.4. Mechanical properties
The differences in microstructure inevitably lead to variations in 
mechanical properties. Fig. 11 a illustrates the microhardness and 
compositional distribution along the deposition direction of the samples.

indents in the Nb-containing Ti45Al8Nb sample is smaller, suggesting 
that the alloying of Nb enhances microhardness.

4. Discussion
4.1. Feasibility analysis
The feasibility of preparing high Nb TiAl alloy Ti45Al8Nb via the 
Triple-wire arc DED was explored from the perspectives of materials, 
process, and experimental results, to validate the effectiveness of 
achieving flexible alloying.
The pure Ti, Al, and Nb wires utilized in this study are common in -
dustrial materials that do not require customization. These wires exhibit 
high purity, which ensures precise control over the alloy composition, 
and are widely available, offering excellent accessibility for large-scale 
experiments and industrial applications. Moreover, the stability and 
consistency of these wire materials further guarantee the controllability 
of compositional ratios during the experimental process, providing a 
solid foundation for the subsequent investigation of key material 
properties.

4.3. Strengthening mechanism of Nb alloying
The comparison of the mechanical properties between the Nb- 
containing Ti45Al8Nb sample and Nb-free Ti45Al sample indicates 
that Nb alloying significantly enhances the microhardness, tensile 
strength, and elongation of TiAl alloys. Therefore, it is essential to 
elucidate the strengthening mechanisms associated with Nb alloying in 
TiAl alloys during the wire arc DED. The related strengthening mecha -
nisms are as follows:
4.3.1. Solid solution strengthening
In the XRD results, it was initially observed that the diffraction peaks 
of the γ phase plane and the α ₂ phase plane in the Ti45Al8Nb sample 
shifted toward lower angles. Nb atoms typically occupy the Ti sublattice

the substitutional solid solution of Nb atoms replacing Ti atoms leads to 
an increase in lattice parameters. According to Bragg ’ s equation 
2 dsin θ = n λ , the increase in lattice spacing d causes the diffraction 
peaks of the γ phase to shift towards lower angles, thereby increasing the 
value of sin θ . This indicates that the alloying of Nb induces lattice 
distortion, which further increases the lattice parameters, demon -
strating that Nb alloying contributes to solid solution strengthening 
effects.

decreased defect population markedly suppresses premature failure

increasing crack susceptibility. At last, previous analyses have identified 
three strengthening mechanisms owing to Nb alloying: solid solution 
strengthening, grain refinement strengthening, and dislocation 
strengthening. These mechanisms synergistically enhance the strength 
and toughness of Nb-modified TiAl alloys, ultimately improving their 
crack arrest capability.

5. Conclusion
In the present research, the Triple-wire arc directed energy deposi -
tion (DED) technique enabled successful introduction of niobium (Nb) 
element into TiAl alloys and fabrication of the third-generation TiAl 
alloy Ti45Al8Nb for the first time. A systematic comparative analysis 
was conducted with Nb-free Ti45Al, providing investigation into the 
effects of Nb alloying on the microstructure and mechanical properties. 
The key findings of this work are summarized as follows:
1. The Triple-wire arc directed energy deposition process (DED) is 
able to introduce refractory metal elements into TiAl alloys using pure 
refractory metal wires as raw materials. This methodology demonstrates 
exceptional flexibility and efficiency in alloying element incorporation, 
showing significant potential for manufacturing multi-component TiAl 
alloys via wire arc DED. The technique exhibits broad applicability for 
other metallic systems, where adjustment of alloying elements and 
concentrations enables tailored material properties for diverse engi -
neering applications.
2. The as-fabricated Ti45Al8Nb samples achieved target composi -
tions with homogeneous elemental distribution. Compared to conven -
tional pre-alloyed Ti45Al8Nb powder feedstocks, this wire-based 
process utilizing commercial pure Ti, Al, and Nb wires reduced material 
costs by up to 66 %. Elimination of pre-alloying preparation time, 
combined with the inherent advantages of wire arc DED (high deposi -
tion rates and material utilization efficiency), significantly enhances the 
competitiveness of this approach for producing multi-element TiAl 
alloys.
3. 8 at% Nb addition induced substantial microstructural modifica -
tions and concomitant mechanical enhancement. Microstructural effects 
included significant crack reduction, B2 phase formation, α
2 
phase 
suppression, increased lamellar spacing, and refined lamellar colony 
size. The strengthening mechanisms encompassed solid solution 
strengthening, grain refinement, and dislocation strengthening. 
Compared with Ti45Al alloy, Ti45Al8Nb exhibited remarkable im -
provements in tensile strength and elongation: from 311.4 ± 14.5 MPa 
and 0.32 ± 0.04 % to 422.6 ± 17.3 MPa and 0.56 ± 0.05 %, 
respectively.
4. Future research directions include: (a) expanding the range of 
alloying elements and establishing a comprehensive database corre -
lating alloy composition with microstructure-property relationships for 
wire arc DED processed TiAl alloys; (b) optimizing process parameters to 
further enhance mechanical performance of Ti45Al8Nb specimens; (c) 
conducting high-temperature mechanical testing and fatigue charac -
terization to facilitate large-scale implementation of wire arc DED pro -
cessed high-performance TiAl alloys.
