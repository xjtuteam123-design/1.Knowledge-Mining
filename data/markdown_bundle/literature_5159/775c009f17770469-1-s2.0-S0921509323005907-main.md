ABSTRACT 
Novel titanium-based alloys are required to increase the efficiency of superplastic forming technology and 
decrease energy consumption. For this purpose, the superplastic deformation behavior, strain-induced micro -
structure evolution in a temperature range of 625 – 775 
◦
C, and post-deformation mechanical properties of 
Ti – 4Al – 1V – 1Fe – 1Ni-0.1B- x Mo alloys ( x = 1, 2.5, or 5 wt%) were investigated. The studied alloys demonstrated a 
stable flow with a high strain rate sensitivity coefficient m of 0.50 – 0.65 and an elongation-to-failure δ of 
700 – 1000% at temperatures of 700 – 775 
◦
C. Molybdenum insignificantly influenced superplastic deformation 
behavior at high temperatures due to a high fraction of the β phase of 22 – 62%. The Mo effect was significant at a 
low deformation temperature of 625 
◦
C. At this temperature, an increase in Mo content from 1 to 5% provided 
the β -phase fraction above the critical value of ~20% and increased the m- value from 0.4 to 0.5 and δ from ~200 
to ~700%. Increasing Mo from 1 to 5% enhanced the post-deformation yield strength at room temperature by 
180 MPa, the ultimate tensile strength by 170 MPa, and the ductility by 2.5%. Consequently, alloying 
Ti – 4Al – 1V – 1Fe – 1Ni-0.1B with 5% Mo provided an excellent combination of superplasticity at a low temperature 
of 625 
◦
C and post-forming tensile mechanical properties. 
1. Introduction 
Titanium alloys integrate excellent mechanical, physical, and 
chemical characteristics, i.e., a high strength-to-weight ratio, making 
them useful for a wide variety of industrial applications. Owing to their 
high strength, relatively low Young ’ s modulus and resulted strong 
springback effect, and high sensitivity to processing conditions, the 
formability of these alloys is a significant concern. Therefore, the pro -
duction of Ti complex-shape components by conventional techniques at 
comparatively low temperatures is a time- and energy-consuming pro -
cess [ 1 – 3 ]. The superplastic forming (SPF) method is a viable strategy 
for dealing with and ultimately overcoming these challenges [ 4 ]. A high 
forming temperature is the primary limitation of SPF for Ti-based alloys. 
Several conventional titanium-based alloys exhibit excellent super -
plasticity between 800 
◦
C and 1030 
◦
C [ 5 – 7 ]. Low-temperature forming 
reduces cycle time, energy consumption, die material costs and wear, 
extends die life, and prevents the development of an alpha-oxide layer 
on the surface of manufactured components [ 2 , 8 ]. Reducing the su -
perplastic forming temperature is a research challenge that has garnered 
significant interest from the scientific and industrial sectors. In general, 
there are several viable methods for decreasing the superplastic forming 
temperature of metallic materials, including refining the grains structure 
before superplastic forming [ 9 – 13 ] and modifying the chemical 
composition of the materials by alloying with high-diffusive elements 
[ 14 – 16 ]. 
The application of relatively simple thermomechanical processes is 
an effective technique for producing an ultrafine-grained structure and 
excellent superplasticity in two-phase titanium alloys [ 17 – 20 ]. Dutta 
and Birla [ 20 ] investigated the impact of different thermomechanical 
processing routes on the superplasticity of Ti-6.3Al-2.7Mo-1.7Zr (wt.%) 
alloy. At 900 
◦
C and 1 × 10
-3 
s
-1
, they achieved a high 
elongation-to-failure of 1700 – 1725%. The authors indicate high grain 
structure stability during the elevated deformation temperature of 
900 
◦
C [ 20 ]. Finer grains facilitate the grain boundary sliding during 
superplastic deformation, reduce flow stress and increase strain-rate 
sensitivity [ 10 , 12 , 21 ]. 
For two-phase alloys, including titanium-based, the optimum α / β 
phase ratio provided a good superplasticity is about 50/50% [ 22 , 23 ]. 
Alloying with β -stabilizers (i.e., Mo, V, Nb, Ni, Fe, Co, and Cr) is an 
efficient method for reducing SPF temperature due to obtaining the

This research contributes to the development of Ti-based alloys with 
superior superplasticity at lower temperatures while maintaining or 
enhancing their mechanical properties at room temperature [ 26 , 27 ]. 
The purpose of this research is to determine the influence of a 
slow-diffusive element, Mo, on the microstructure evolution, super -
plastic and room-temperature tensile properties of Ti – 4Al – 1V alloy 
modified with high-diffusive 1Fe and 1Ni (wt.%). 
2. Experimental procedure 
2.1. Material processing 
The studied alloys contained comparable concentrations of Al, V, 
Mo, Ni, Fe, and B, whereas the Mo concentration varied from 1 to 5% 
( Table 1 presented nominal and SEM-EDS data). All concentrations in 
this study are in wt.%. The chemical composition of the alloys was 
confirmed by SEM-EDS analysis via averaging of composition of above 
10 areas at a low magnification after different processing stages. The 
error bars in Table 1 are the standard deviation of a mean value. For this 
purpose, two samples were cut form different places of the ingot and hot 
rolled sheets, and the composition was also checked after annealing of 
the sheets. The deviation between nominal and experimental values for 
the Al, V, Fe, Ni, and Mo alloying elements was below 0.1 wt%. The 0.1 
wt% B was a nominal boron content. It is notable that the concentration 
of residual interstitial impurities (H,O,N) in the alloys was not 
determined. 
Technically pure Ti (99.99%), Al (99.99%), V (99.95%), Ni 
(99.95%), Fe (99.95%), and Ti-50% Mo master alloy were used to pre -
pare 100 g ingots with dimensions of 50 × 40 × 10 mm
3
. Melting and 
casting were processed in a vacuum arc melting cold crucible furnace 
Arc 200 with a copper water-cooling mold. The ingots were re-melted 
five times to obtain a homogeneous composition over the section. 
The heat treatment of the ingots included homogenization annealing 
at 800 
◦
C for 60 min, followed by annealing at 950 
◦
C, above β -transus, 
for 30 min and further water-quenching to form a fine martensitic 
structure. For sheet processing, the quenched ingot was subjected to hot 
rolling at 750 ± 10 
◦
C in the α + β -field with a total reduction of 90% 
(from 10 to 1 mm). After processing, the oxide layer on the samples was 
removed by chemical etching, by immersing for 60 min in a Kroll agent 
(3% vol. HF + 5% vol. HNO
3 
in water solution).

It is well known that the β phase has a significant influence on the 
superplastic behavior of ( α + β ) titanium alloys. Due to the b.c.c lattice, 
this phase exhibits a high diffusivity and ductility, and an increase in its 
fraction to a specific limit improves superplasticity [ 6 , 22 , 23 , 38 ]. Typi -
cally, the optimal phase ratio α / β is approximately 50/50, which pro -
vides good grain size stability for duplex structures [ 39 ]. A higher β 
phase fraction decreases superplastic properties due to a high tendency 
to the grain growth of the high-diffusive phase, whereas a smaller β 
fraction limits superplasticity owing to weakening diffusion-controlled 
deformation mechanisms. For Ti – 6Al – 2Sn – 4Zr – 2Mo-0.1Si alloy, 20% 
of β provides a good superplasticity, and 30% is required for 
Ti-2.5Al-1.8Mn alloy [ 6 , 38 ]. The superplastic behavior also depends on 
the diffusivity of alloying elements, and this effect is double-natured. 
First, alloying with high-diffusive Ni and Fe facilitates 
diffusion-dependent deformation mechanisms and reduces the temper -
ature of superplasticity [ 15 , 25 – 27 ]. In addition to accelerating diffu -
sion, these elements are β stabilizers and decrease the α / β = 50/50% 
temperature. Second, elements with a high diffusivity, such as Fe and Ni, 
may enhance dynamic grain growth and decrease superplastic elonga -
tions. Conversely, due to a significantly slower diffusion rate in β -Ti, 
β - stabilizing Mo should not facilitate grain growth but decreases 
β - transus and 50/50% temperature [ 25 ]. In the studied alloys contain -
ing Fe and Ni, it is reasonable that an increase in the Mo content 
increased the fraction of the β phase at low deformation temperatures 
and helped to control grain growth.

1946 [ 56 , 68 ], who suggests that active development of 
solution-precipitation processes helps to accommodate grain boundary 
sliding during superplastic flow.
