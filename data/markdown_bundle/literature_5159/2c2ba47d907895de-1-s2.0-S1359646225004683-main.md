ABSTRACT
Additive manufactured high-temperature titanium alloys typically exhibit poor plasticity and crack sensitivity. In 
this work, a novel strategy incorporating high-melting-point, low-diffusivity tungsten (W) as a microstructural 
modifier was designed for Ti-6.5Al-3.5Mo-1.5Zr-0.3Si alloy, with subsequent processing by laser powder bed 
fusion. At the mesoscale, the columnar prior β -grains of the alloys transformed to fine equiaxed-elongated 
morphology and formed a bimodal structure. Microscale characterization revealed that the α ’ phases were 
refined while the brittle α ’ / β interfaces were replaced with more ductile boundaries. Notably, the modified alloys 
achieved an outstanding tensile strength of 1717.6 MPa along with an improved elongation of 4.4%, which is 
attributed to the synergistic effects of grain structure and interface optimization. The present work proposes a 
promising approach for regulating the microstructure and mechanical properties of high-temperature titanium 
alloys by refractory elements.
High-temperature titanium alloys, predominantly developed within 
the Ti-Al-Sn-Zr-Mo-Si system, are pivotal engineering materials in 
aerospace[ 1 , 2 ]. Higher aluminum equivalent ( Al
eq
) enhances the 
elevated-temperature mechanical properties of the alloys, but reduces 
the intrinsic plasticity[ 3 – 5 ]. The poor ductility of high-temperature ti -
tanium alloys severely restricts their processability, particularly in ad -
ditive manufacturing (AM), where the rapid solidification exacerbates 
cracking susceptibility[ 6 , 7 ]. Coarse columnar β -grains at the mesoscale 
and metastable martensitic α ’ characteristics at the microscale are the 
dominating reasons that adversely affect the strength and ductility of the 
alloys[ 8 , 9 ]. Limited studies have focused on modifying AM 
high-temperature titanium alloys, and it is essential to address these 
dual challenges and facilitate their practical deployment.
Elements with a high constitutional supercooling parameter ( P ) will 
promote grain refinement and the columnar-to-equiaxed transition 
(CET)[ 10 ]. Alloying elements in the Ti-Al-Sn-Zr-Mo system exhibit 
relatively low P -values(0, 0.07, 0.34, and 0.4 K/wt.%, respectively)[ 10 ,
11 ], while the addition of high- P -value Si (62 K/wt.%) is strictly limited 
due to its detrimental effects on ductility[ 12 , 13 ]. Finally, the coarse 
columnar β -grains formed at the mesoscale. At the microscale, elevated 
Al
eq 
increases α ’ volume fraction in AM titanium alloys, especially in 
laser powder bed fusion (L-PBF) processed alloys subjected to extreme 
cooling rates (~10
7 
K/s)[ 14 , 15 ]. Additionally, cyclic thermal input from 
subsequent layers results in decomposition of α ’ and the formation of 
α ’ / β interfaces. The interface mismatch between α ’ and β -Ti promotes 
crack initiation, which decreases the ductility of the alloys[ 16 ].
The eutectoid elements with high P -value, such as Cu[ 17 ], Fe[ 18 ], Ni 
[ 19 ], etc., are always selected as modifiers to promote CET in AM tita -
nium alloys. However, these strong β -stabilizers would decrease the 
high-temperature strength of titanium alloys[ 5 ]. Excessive addition also 
leads to the precipitation of brittle intermetallics, so these elements are 
not appropriate for high-temperature titanium alloys[ 11 ]. Ceramic re -
inforcements, such as TiB, TiC, TiN, and rare-earth oxide provide an 
alternative CET effect that preserves high-temperature performance 
[ 20 – 22 ]. However, these phases react with titanium and lead to the 
oversaturation of interstitial elements, significantly reducing the plas -
ticity of materials[ 22 ]. Overall, a breakthrough in AM high-temperature 
titanium alloys requires multifunctional additives that can optimize the 
microstructure and preserve elevated temperature stability.

At the mesoscale, Fig. 1 (a- c ) show that the as-built 0W alloy exhibits 
coarse columnar prior β -grains along the building direction (BD). With 
the addition of 1 wt.% W, the columnar grains are refined; however, the 
prior β -grains in the 2W alloy have mixed equiaxed-elongated 
morphology. Fig. S4(a-c) show the fraction of equiaxed grains (aspect 
ratio < 2) in 2W alloy increased from 2.9 % (in 0W alloy) to 33.6%, while 
the proportion of grains with an aspect ratio exceeding 5 decreased from 
59.3 % to 11.9%, indicating a significant CET effect. Fig. S4(d – f) display 
the grain size distributions of the three alloys, indicating a significant 
refinement with increased W content. The 1W and 2W alloys exhibit a 
rising proportion of fine grains and a decreased maximum grain size. 
The 2W alloy exhibits a bimodal microstructure, consisting of 37.2 % 
fine grains ( < 20 μ m) and 27.2 % coarse grains ( > 40 μ m).
Distributions of low-angle grain boundaries (LAGBs) and high-angle 
grain boundaries (HAGBs) of the alloys were performed based on EBSD 
data, as presented in Fig. 1 ( d - F ). The proportion of HAGBs decreases

The morphologies and size parameters of α / α ’ phases in 0W and 2W 
alloys were characterized in Fig. S6 and Table 1 . The size and aspect 
ratio were measured by ImageJ software. In comparison, the martensites 
in the 2W alloy were significantly refined, with the average thickness 
decreasing by over 50%, the mean length diminishing by over 80%, and 
the aspect ratio declining by approximately 60%. With W addition, the 
morphology of α / α ’ phases also transformed from elongated laths to 
short acicular. This microstructural evolution can be attributed to the 
β -stabilizing effect of W, which provides solid solution strengthening 
and lowers the martensitic transformation temperature. The 
strengthened β -phase suppresses shear deformation during martensitic 
transformation, thereby promoting the formation of a refined martens -
itic structure[ 25 ].
The W particles remained partially unmelted during L-PBF, as shown 
in Fig. S5. Image-based quantitative analysis reveals that there are 0.55 
vol.% (about 0.023 wt.%) of residual W particles in 2W alloy, indicating 
that 98.9 % of the initial W content underwent complete melting or 
dissolution. Meanwhile, the unmelted W particles were dispersed by the 
molten pool stirring, resulting in a relatively uniform distribution. These 
unmelted W particles, all sub-0.5 μ m in size, formed diffusion-bonded 
interfaces with the Ti matrix. Moreover, as a strongly segregating 
element, W significantly elevates the P -value of the alloys. Assuming 
98.9 % W dissolution in the melt, the calculated P -value reaches 39.04 K, 
representing a twofold increase compared to the original Ti-6.5Al- 
3.5Mo-1.5Zr-0.3Si alloy ( P = 20.88 K).

Multiscale microstructure regulation leads to remarkable improve -
ments in the mechanical properties, as presented in Table 2 and Fig. 4 
(a) . 1W alloy shows comparable yield strength to 0W, but achieves a 166 
MPa increase in ultimate strength. Notably, 2W alloy achieves remark -
able strength-ductility synergy, not only exhibiting about 266 MPa en -
hancements in both yield strength (20.4 % increase) and ultimate 
strength (18.3 % increase) but also showing triple improvement in 
elongation compared to 0W. Fig. 4(b) reveals that the work hardening 
rate and uniform elongation of the alloys were both improved as the W 
content increased. As shown in Fig. 4(c) , this advancement represents an 
improvement over other alloying approaches and avoids the ductility 
compromise typically observed in ceramic-reinforced titanium matrix 
composites (TMCs). Contrary to traditional metallurgical understand -
ing, which considers W as a detrimental inclusion, our work demon -
strates that the addition of a controlled amount of W produces a 
synergistic enhancement in both strength and ductility.
At the mesoscale, the refinement of prior β -grains and the bimodal 
structure contribute to the enhancement of strength and ductility, 
following the Hall-Petch relationship. At the microscale, W effectively 
refined the α ’ phase and suppressed its decomposition, thereby effec -
tively tailoring the interfacial characteristics of the alloy. This primarily 
prevents the formation of brittle α ’ / β interfaces and enhances the 
capability of deformation. The increased proportion of LAGBs indicates 
that the dynamic recovery of titanium was suppressed during L-PBF, 
which is attributed to the low diffusivity of W. The strong solute-drag 
effect leads to higher work hardening rates of the alloys, and the 
atomic-scale effects of W require further investigation.
In summary, the addition of W into L-PBF Ti-6.5Al-3.5Mo-1.5Zr- 
0.3Si alloy provides effective microstructural optimization across mul -
tiple scales, resulting in the synergistic improvement of strength and 
ductility. At the mesoscale, W increased the nucleation rate of the alloy 
during solidification, achieving a refined β -grain size and bimodal 
structure. Both the size and morphology of α ’ were optimized owing to 
the strengthening and β -stabilizing effect of W. The thermal stability of 
α ’ was enhanced by W, and the formation of brittle α ’ / β interfaces was 
suppressed at the microscale. The 2 wt.% W-modified Ti-6.5Al-3.5Mo- 
1.5Zr-0.3Si alloy reaches 1.7 GPa strength with 4.4 % elongation, 
overcoming its original inherent brittleness. This work provides a novel 
strategy for designing AM high-performance titanium alloys, high -
lighting the potential of refractory elements.
