abstract
In order to obtain refractory alloys with both high strength and good ductility simultaneously, the novel
W0.4MoNbxTaTi high entropy alloys were developed, of which the crystal structure, microstructure, and
compression properties were investigated as well. The as-cast alloys had a single BCC phase structure
with dendritic substructure in coarse polygon crystal grains. The alloys exhibited outstanding mechanical
properties at testing temperatures. The optimal yield strength and fracture strain were 1334 MPa and
38.9% in W0.4MoNb1.3TaTi alloy at ambient temperature. Furthermore, its yield strength decreased to
1125, 905, and 425 MPa at 1073, 1273, and 1373 K, respectively. And it showed high speciﬁc yield
strength at elevated temperature. The compression performances of the W0.4MoNb1.3TaTi alloy were
superior in comparison with other refractory high entropy alloys.

1. Introduction
Recently, there has been a growing interest in HEAs owing to
their potential use in extremely high temperature applications
[1–6]. However, there is a severe limitation of refractory HEAs
applications for the poor coordination of favorable strength at high
temperature and good plasticity at ambient temperature [7–12].
For instance, NbCrMo0.5Ta0.5TiZr [8], AlMo0.5NbTa0.5TiZr [11]
and Al0.4Hf0.6NbTaTiZr [12] exhibit exceptional high-
temperature strength but limited room-temperature ductility,
while TaNbHfZrTi [7] and Ti40Al10V20Nb20Mo10 [9] possess
excellent plasticity at ambient temperature but relatively low
strength at elevated temperature. At present, it is a challenge for
refractory HEAs to achieve good matching of strength and plastic-
ity at room temperature (RT) while still maintaining high strength
at elevated temperatures. Generally, HEAs composed of refractory
elements (such as W, Mo, Nb, Ta, and Ti) have the potential for high
temperature applications due to their high melting point. For
example, the WNbMoTaTi with an excellent yield strength of
1455 Mpa holds potentials for applications as high temperature
materials, but its plasticity is only 11.5% at RT [13]. In order to
improve the ductility, according to the negative effect of W and
the positive effect of Nb on plasticity, measures had been taken
to reduce W content and increase Nb content [14,15]. Therefore,
novel W0.4MoNbxTaTi (x = 1.1, 1.3, 1.5) refractory HEAs demon-
strated excellent high-temperature strength and a good combina-
tion of distinguished strength and good ductility at RT were
designed. The phase composition, microstructure, as well as
mechanical properties of the alloys, are discussed.
2. Experimental procedures
The refractory HEAs with a nominal composition of W0.4MoN-
bxTaTi (x = 1.1, 1.3, and 1.5 in a molar ratio, denoted as Nb1.1,
Nb1.3, and Nb1.5, respectively) were prepared using vacuum arc
melting. The ingots were re-melted ﬁve times to improve the
chemical homogeneity. Crystal structures were measured via an
X-ray diffractometer. The microstructure and chemical composi-
tions of the resulted cast alloys were investigated by SEM and
EDS. An initial strain rate of 0.001 s /C01 was applied to the compres-
sive process. Cylindrical specimens ( U 4*6 mm) were tested on a
universal testing machine for room temperature compression test-
ing. For high temperature testing, the samples ( U 6*9 mm) were
performed on a thermal simulator at 1073, 1273, and 1373 K.
The heating rate was 300 K/min and the holding time was 300 s.
Density of the alloys was measured via hydrostatic weighting.
The hot-compressed specimens were cut parallel to the compres-
sion direction from the cylindrical samples after high temperature
compression. Microstructural observations of the hot-compressed
samples were analyzed by SEM.

3. Results and discussion
Fig. 1 illustrates information on structure of the as-cast
W0.4MoNbxTaTi alloys. The XRD patterns showing in Fig. 1 a
demonstrate that the alloys have a single phase with BCC lattice
according to Bragg’s equation. The position of the diffraction peak
of the solid solution deviates from the one of the elements, which
is caused by severe lattice distortion.
SEM images ( Fig. 1 b-d) manifest large grains with dendritic
structures inside. The results of EDS suggest that the dendrite
was enriched with W and Ta, while the interdendrite was enriched
with Ti, and Nb/Mo were evenly distributed. During cooling, the
high-temperature BCC phase rejected Ti into the interdendrite,
resulting in (W, Ta)-rich dendrite and Ti-rich interdendrite.
The true compression stress-strain curves of the W0.4MoN-
bxTaTi alloys at different temperatures are shown in Fig. 2 . The
resulting yield strength (
r0.2) and fracture strain ( ef) are summa-
rized in Table 1. The alloys showed good ductility at room temper-
ature as fracture strain ranging from 32% to 39%. The yield
strengths of the alloys were 1412 MPa (Nb1.1), 1334 MPa
(Nb1.3), and 1312 MPa (Nb1.5), respectively. Prominent work
hardening stages were observed after yielding. The yield stress
exhibited a pronounced decrease as the testing temperature
increased to 1073 K and 1273 K, which was attributed to the
energy provided by the high temperature. The higher energy would
promote the movement of dislocations and even grain boundaries,
leading to the reduction of yield strength.
Further increase of testing temperature to 1373 K resulted in a
further decrease in yield strength. However, the alloys still exhib-
ited high yield strength (585 MPa for Nb1.1, 425 MPa for Nb1.3,
and 503 MPa for Nb1.5) even if the testing temperature was
1373 K. After reaching the yield point, pronounced steady-state
ﬂow stages were observed on the true stress-strain curves. The
poor plasticity of Nb1.5 alloys at 1073 and 1273 K may be attribu-
ted to the fact that the higher Nb content in this temperature inter-
val leads to partial segregation of Nb and the formation of
precipitated phases of M-Nb at grain boundaries, which weakens
the high-temperature plasticity of the alloys [16].
Fig. 2 also demonstrates the hot-compressed microstructure
of the W0.4MoNbxTaTi alloys corresponding to different tem-
peratures. As the picture shows, the cracks were initiated at
the grain boundaries (GBs) and then propagated along the
GBs, meanwhile, some cracks expanded into the grains. As the
temperature elevated, the propagation of cracks was suppressed.
During the deformation process, stress concentration occurred,
which caused a sharp increase in local energy. When the stress
concentration reached a certain value, crack propagation began
to appear, releasing energy. As the testing temperature
increases, the atomic activity and dislocation migration rate rise
signiﬁcantly. Therefore, the local stress concentration could be
effectively alleviated, which would inhibit crack initiation and
propagation.
The produced refractory alloys, Nb1.1, Nb1.3 and Nb1.5, have
densities of
q = 10.7, 10.6 and 10.5 g/cm3, respectively. The com-
parison of mechanical properties and speciﬁc yield strength (SYS)
between the W0.4MoNbxTaTi alloys and previously reported HEAs
[1,4–6,8,9] are shown in Fig. 3. For the convenience of comparison,
all ef greater than 50% were represented by 50%. Fig. 3 a demon-
strates a comparison of mechanical properties at ambient temper-
ature. The reference HEAs can be classiﬁed into two categories: (i)
high strength but brittle alloys, having yield strength (YS)
>1400 MPa and ef < 12%; (ii) ductile but soft HEAs, possessing
YS < 1000 MPa and ductility of 50%. It could be found that there
is a typical tradeoff between strength and plasticity in these alloys.
Noticeably, the W0.4MoNbxTaTi alloys (marked with a red circle)
displayed high YS above 1300 MPa and good ductility with ef
greater than 32%, which perfectly combined the superiorities of
the aforementioned two types of HEAs.

Furthermore, the mechanical properties of the W0.4MoNbxTaTi
alloys at elevated temperatures were even more surprising ( Fig. 3-
b-c). The level of YS revealed by the studied alloys at 1073 K and
1273 K was superior to most reference alloys; only HfMoTaTiZr
and HfMoNbTaTiZr alloy were comparable to them. However,
these two reported alloys have poor room-temperature ductility.
Compared with the other alloys, Nb1.3 not only possessed the
highest strength but also had ﬁne compressive plasticity. Fig. 3 d
presents the SYS of the aforementioned alloys. Excluding the SYS
of soft AlNbTiV and brittle NbCrMo0.5Ta0.5TiZr at 1073 K, Nb1.3
has the highest SYS at 1073 and 1273 K among all alloys. Thus,
W0.4MoNb1.3TaTi has great potential in high-temperature appli-
cations over 1073 K.
The high-strength W0.4MoNbxTaTi HEAs beneﬁt from severe
lattice distortion caused by different elements randomly occupying
lattice positions. The shear stress of dislocation slip increases due
to the hindrance of lattice distortion, which reveals the high yield
stress of HEAs. The addition of Nb reduces the chance of mismatch,
alleviating the lattice distortion. Moreover, as the Nb content
increases, the number of strong bonds such as W-M and Mo-M
decreases. These illustrate the decrease in strength as Nb increas-
ing. Correspondingly, the plasticity of HEAs would be improved.
Furthermore, the increase in Nb content can reduce the degree of
element segregation, which also contributes to the high ductility
of W0.4MoNbxTaTi HEAs.
4. Conclusions
The microstructure and mechanical properties of W0.4MoN-
bxTaTi (x = 1.1, 1.3, 1.5) alloys were investigated thoroughly in pre-
sent paper. The as-cast alloys are composed of single BCC
structures with large polygon grains containing dendritic substruc-
ture. At room temperature, the alloys possess excellent yield
strengths exceeding 1312 MPa and high fracture strains above
32%. At elevated temperatures, the W0.4MoNbxTaTi alloys present
preeminent yield strengths as well. The compressive properties of
the W0.4MoNb1.3TaTi alloy are superior to previously reported
high-temperature HEAs at both room and higher temperatures,
and its speciﬁc yield strength at high temperature is excellent,
making it a promising candidate for high-temperature
applications.
