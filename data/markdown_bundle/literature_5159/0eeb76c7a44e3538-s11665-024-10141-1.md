Structural and Comprehensive Performance

L. Chen, S.C. Zhao, W. Liu, C.L. Liu, X. Jiang, and Y.X. Leng
Submitted: 20 December 2023 / Revised: 25 June 2024 / Accepted: 4 July 2024 / Published online: 30 September 2024
CoCrFeMnNi high-entropy alloy (HEA), as a typical FCC single-phase high-entropy alloy, is one of the
most maturely studied and most promising to be the ﬁrst to realize industrial applications. However, the
CoCrFeMnNi HEA prepared by different processes exhibits signiﬁcant differences in structure and
properties, there also have been few comprehensive evaluations of its overall service performance. In this
study, CoCrFeMnNi HEAs were prepared at different sintering temperatures via Fast Hot-Pressed Sin-
tering. The effects of sintering temperature on the microstructure, mechanical properties, tribological
properties, corrosion and hydrogen embrittlement resistance of CoCrFeMnNi HEA were comprehensively
and systematically studied. With the increase of sintering temperature, the density of the high-entropy alloy
increases. The sintering temperature of 1000 /C176C has the most outstanding mechanical properties, with
tensile strength and ﬁnal elongation of 678 MPa and 58% respectively. After the sintering temperature
reaches 900/C176C, the density of the high-entropy alloy reaches more than 95%, and the wear rate is around

1. Introduction
Compared with traditional alloys, high entropy alloys
exhibit signiﬁcant advantages due to their unique composition
and structure. Previous studies have shown that thermodynamic
high entropy effects contribute to the formation of stable solid
solutions, while lattice distortion effects can greatly improve
the mechanical properties of alloys (Ref 1-4).HEAs break the
design concept of traditional alloys and utilize rational
composition ratios and appropriate fabrication processes to
modify the microstructure, resulting in properties such as high
strength (Ref 5), good ductility (Ref 6], high wear resistance
(Ref 7), excellent corrosion resistance (Ref 8), and remarkable
hydrogen embrittlement resistance (Ref 9).
CoCrFeMnNi high-entropy alloy, also referred to as the
‘‘Cantor alloy’’ (Ref 10, 11). as a typical single-phase FCC
high-entropy alloy, has received considerable attention and is
one of the most mature and promising high-entropy alloys for
industrial applications. The deformation behavior of CoCr-
FeMnNi high-entropy alloy at room temperature and low
temperature has been extensively studied (Ref 12–14). At room
temperature, the material exhibits relatively low yield strength,
generally below 400 MPa, but excellent plasticity with fracture
elongation exceeding 50% (Ref 15, 16). Otto et al. (Ref 17)
studied the mechanical properties of this alloy at different
temperatures and found that at low temperatures (77 K), the
twinning deformation mechanism is active, resulting in signif-
icant enhancements in yield strength and elongation. As the
temperature increases from 293 to 1073 K, both yield strength
and plasticity exhibit a noticeable decrease. Previous studies on
the mechanical properties were mainly conducted on CoCr-
FeMnNi high-entropy alloy prepared by casting, which faces
difﬁculties due to large differences in melting points between
elements, severe element segregation, and element volatiliza-
tion (Ref 18-20). In recent years, studies have been scarce on
the tribological properties of CoCrFeMnNi HEA prepared by
the melting and casting process, and academic attention has
primarily focused on its corrosion resistance (Ref 21–23).
Zhang et al. (Ref 24) explored the effect of Mn addition on the
corrosion behaviour of equiatomic CoCrFeNi high entropy
alloys in 3.5 wt.% NaCl NaCl solution. Due to the narrow or
nonexistent passivation region in the passive ﬁlm of equiatomic

Although the combination of high-energy ball milling and
spark plasma sintering can efﬁciently prepare ultraﬁne-grained
CoCrFeMnNi high-entropy alloy, the mechanical alloying of
CoCrFeMnNi high-entropy alloy through high-energy ball
milling has disadvantages such as complicated milling process
and low powdering efﬁciency. Moreover, the combination of
mechanical alloying and spark plasma sintering will signiﬁ-
cantly sacriﬁce the high plasticity characteristics of CoCr-
FeMnNi high-entropy alloy (Ref 26). Fast hot pressing (FHP) is
a ﬁeld-assisted hot-pressing technique that uses a direct current
power supply instead of a pulse power supply, reducing
hardware costs and simplifying system complexity (Fig. 1). The
processing temperature in fast hot pressing is lower, and the
holding time is shorter, resulting in materials with smaller grain
size and lower porosity compared to traditional hot pressing
(Ref 35). Increasing the sintering temperature is one way to
improve the alloy /C213 s consolidation and achieve higher alloy
density. However, increasing the sintering temperature often
leads to signiﬁcant grain growth, which affects the mechanical
properties of the alloy. The study on achieving densiﬁcation
and inhibiting grain growth of CoCrFeMnNi high-entropy alloy
through fast hot pressing is of certain scientiﬁc value. The
comprehensive evaluation of the mechanical properties, tribo-
logical properties, corrosion resistance, and hydrogen embrit-
tlement resistance of CoCrFeMnNi high-entropy alloy prepared
by fast hot pressing is rarely reported, and such a comprehen-
sive evaluation is instrumental for facilitating the application of
this high-entropy alloy in critical components under complex
and harsh conditions.
In this work, fast hot pressing was used to achieve rapid
heating and densiﬁcation of CoCrFeMnNi high-entropy alloy,
replacing the spark plasma sintering using an expensive pulse
power supply. The resulting properties were comparable to
those of SPS-prepared alloy (Ref 29), and the high plasticity
characteristics of CoCrFeMnNi high-entropy alloy were also
retained. Detailed tests and analyses of the microstructural
characteristics and mechanical properties, frictional properties,
corrosion resistance, and hydrogen embrittlement resistance of
CoCrFeMnNi high-entropy alloy were conducted. In addition,
the inﬂuence of different densities and grain sizes on the
mechanical properties, frictional properties, and corrosion
resistance of CoCrFeMnNi high-entropy alloy was studied.
This research provides a more comprehensive experimental
basis for broadening the application prospects of CoCrFeMnNi
high-entropy alloy and offers a relatively economical and
efﬁcient method for preparing high-entropy alloys by powder
metallurgy.
2. Experimental
2.1 Sample Preparation
The raw material for CoCrFeMnNi HEAs is pre-alloyed
powder, with particle sizes ranging from 15 to 53 lm and an
equiatomic ratio.The raw material of CoCrFeNiMn HEAs is a
pre-alloyed spherical powder prepared by gas atomization, with
particle sizes ranging from 15 to 53 lm and nearly equal
atomic ratios (Fig. 2). The purity is greater than 99.9%.
CoCrFeMnNi pre-alloyed powder is loaded into a 40 mm inner
diameter graphite mold, and then sintered using Fast Hot-
Pressed Sintering (FHP-828, HaTeng, Shanghai) with prede-
termined sintering heating rate and pressure. The speciﬁc
sintering process is as follows: below 715 /C176 C, heating rate of
100/C176 C/min, pressure of 5 MPa; then, within 1 min, temperature
is increased from 715 to 750 /C176 C and pressure is increased from 5
to 50 MPa, followed by heating at a rate of 50 /C176 C/min to 800/C176 C,
900/C176 C, 1000/C176 C, and 1100 /C176 C, respectively (four sets of samples
are named G800, G900, G1000, and G1100); the dwell time is
10 min, quenching upon sintering completion, and the entire
sintering process is carried out under vacuum (less than 10 Pa).
2.2 Phase Composition and Microstructure Characterization
X-ray diffractometer (Philips X /C213 Pert Pro) was used to
characterize the phase composition of different CoCrFeMnNi
samples. The anode target was Cu ( k = 1.54 A˚ ), the x-ray tube
voltage was 40 kV , the current was 40 mA, and the detection
range was from 20 to 100 /C176 . Scanning electron microscopy
(SEM, JSM-7800 F-Japan) was used to observe the surface and
cross-sectional morphology of all samples. Electron backscatter
diffraction (EBSD) was used to measure the grain size of all
samples, the spot size for EBSD measurements was 0.5 lm.
The low angle grain boundary (LAGB) is deﬁned as 2-15 /C176 , the
high angle grain boundary (HAGB) as greater than 15 /C176 , and the
twin boundaries as having a 60 /C176 orientation. The number of
grains measured in each sample exceeds 200. Transmission
electron microscopy (TEM, FEI Tecnai F30) was used for
further analysis of the microstructure of all samples, and
selected area electron diffraction (SAED) was used to further
determine the phase composition.

After subsequent performance characterization, the G1000
sample with the best comprehensive performance was selected
for further analysis of phase composition and structure. The
phase composition of the G1000 sample was further charac-
terized by transmission electron microscopy (TEM). Fig 5a and
b show the main microstructures including the FCC matrix
phase, oxide precipitates, pronounced twins, and dislocations
(yellow markings in Fig 5a and b. Fig 5c and d display the
HRTEM images and selected area electron diffraction (SAED)
patterns of the matrix phase and twins. The HRTEM and SAED
conﬁrm that the matrix phase is an FCC phase in the (Ref 0-11)
direction and the twins are in the (Ref 110) direction of the
matrix phase. In the CrMnFeCoNi HEA prepared at 1000 /C176 C,
dislocations and twinning bands were observed in the
microstructure. These structural features arise as a consequence
of the thermal mismatch stress that occurs at the interfaces
during the alloy preparation process, stemming from the
dissimilar atomic volume changes of the constituent metal
atoms. When the magnitude of thermal mismatch stress exceeds
the yield strength of the alloy, stress is relieved through plastic
deformation, leading to the formation of dislocations (Ref 38).
The appearance of annealing twins, as observed in Fig. 5a,
results from the annealing process that follows the solidiﬁcation
and cooling of alloy. The thermal mismatch stress originating
from atomic volume differences during cooling leads to twin
boundaries, and their presence within the microstructure
contributes to a decrease in the mechanical strength of alloy
(Ref 37). Conversely, Fig 5b illustrates the accumulation of
dislocations within the microstructure, caused by the release of
thermal mismatch stress through plastic deformation. The
accumulation of dislocations leads to an increase in local stress
and promotes the formation of deformation twins, which
contribute to the alloy /C213 s enhanced mechanical properties in
terms of deformation capacity and work-hardening ability (Ref
39
). Deformation twins are a crucial mechanism for enhancing
the alloy /C213 s strength and ductility by accommodating the stress
and strain that result from external forces. Overall, the presence
of both annealing twins and deformation twins in the
CoCrFeMnNi HEA signiﬁcantly inﬂuences its mechanical
behavior. The balance between these twinning mechanisms
and the corresponding dislocation behavior plays a pivotal role
in determining the alloy /C213 s overall mechanical performance and
deformation characteristics. The oxide observed in Fig. 5bi s
mainly enriched in Cr and Mn. Due to the large difference in
electronegativity between Cr, Mn, and O, they readily form a
stable intermediate phase CrMn
1.5O4 (Ref 40). The enrichment
of dislocations and the interaction between dislocations and
twins promote strain hardening, enhancing the strength and
hardness of the material.

more metastable structures (Ref 43). Figure 5(a) and (b)

signiﬁcant lattice distortions and the formation of a large

alloy, speciﬁcally the high occurrence of twins and dislocations,
signiﬁcantly enhance its intrinsic yield strength ( r
0) compared
to cast alloys. Further analyses revealed that the Hall-Petch

press sintering than in casting. The dominant role of intra-grain

strengthening to the overall strength of the alloy, resulting in a
lower Hall-Petch coefﬁcient (k). This result suggests that intra-

critical role than grain boundary strengthening in high-entropy
alloys prepared by hot press sintering. In summary, it is difﬁcult
to accurately represent the true Hall-Petch relationship for this
alloy using sintered samples with lower densities and severe
lattice distortions.

3.4 Corrosion Resistance
Figure 13 presents the potentiodynamic polarization curves
of CoCrFeMnNi HEA samples. The corrosion potential (E corr)
and corrosion current density (I corr) are listed in Table 1.E corr
represents the corrosion tendency of the material, and the larger
its value, the more difﬁcult it is to corrode. The CoCrFeMnNi
HEA samples prepared by rapid hot pressing exhibited lower
corrosion tendencies compared to the literature-reported cast
CoCrFeMnNi HEA, with the G1000 sample demonstrating the
lowest corrosion tendency. I
corr represents the corrosion rate of
the samples, and a smaller value indicates better corrosion
resistance. It can be observed that, except for the G800 sample,
the I
corr of the rapidly hot-pressed CoCrFeMnNi HEA samples
was lower by one to two orders of magnitude compared to the
cast CoCrFeMnNi HEA (Ref 24). CoCrFeMnNi HEA contains
corrosion-resistant elements similar to standard AISI 304
austenitic stainless steel (72Fe-19Cr-9Ni), namely Cr and Ni.
Furthermore, they both possess a single-phase FCC structure
with similar lattice parameters (Ref 45). Hong Luo et al (Ref
21). Conducted an in-depth study on the corrosion behavior of
equiatomic CoCrFeMnNi high-entropy alloy compared to 304
stainless steel in acidic solutions, shedding light on the
composition and structure-related fundamental mechanisms of
passivation ﬁlms. It was found that equiatomic high-entropy
alloys and 304L SS both exhibit the capability to form
passivation ﬁlms effectively. The main constituents of the
passive ﬁlm on the equiatomic HEA are hydroxides of Cr, Fe
and Ni mixed with Mn, Co oxides, while the passivation ﬁlm
on 304L SS primarily consists of oxides of Cr and Fe. This
elucidates that the excellent corrosion resistance of CoCr-
FeMnNi HEA relies on the formation of a dense passivation
ﬁlm that acts as a protective barrier after corrosion. With the
increase in sintering temperature, the I
corr of the samples
initially decreases and then increases, with G1000 samples
exhibiting the lowest I
corr. On the one hand, as the sintering
temperature increases, the density of the samples continuously
improves, narrowing the pathways for the corrosive medium
and making it more challenging for the corrosive medium to
penetrate the interior of the samples, thereby enhancing their
corrosion resistance. On the other hand, with the increase in
sintering temperature, the grain size continually increases,
resulting in a reduced grain boundary area and fewer passiva-
tion oxide precipitates along the grain boundaries. Conse-
quently, large-grain CoCrFeMnNi HEA is more susceptible to
electrochemical corrosion. Considering the competition be-
tween these two different factors, the corrosion resistance
performance is optimal for the G1000 samples.

process of hydrogen-charged HEA samples, there may exist a
competition between beneﬁcial twin-induced toughening and
adverse hydrogen embrittlement mechanisms. HEAs prepared
by the hot press sintering method are less resistant to hydrogen
embrittlement compared to those prepared by the melt method.

sintering method makes it difﬁcult to completely eliminate
pores during the preparation process, resulting in the material
not achieving the desired fully dense state. The presence of
micropores in the material creates potential sites for hydrogen
atom accumulation, which weakens the resistance of high-
entropy alloys to hydrogen embrittlement. Second, under the
hot-press sintering conditions, the alloys tend to develop a ﬁner
grain structure. Although ﬁne grains can enhance certain
mechanical properties, they also increase in the number of grain
boundaries signiﬁcantly. Grain boundaries are regions where

4. Conclusion
The CoCrFeMnNi high-entropy alloys (HEA) with different
sintering temperatures were prepared by FHP sintering. The
effects of sintering temperature on the microstructure, mechan-
ical properties, tribology, corrosion resistance, and hydrogen
embrittlement resistance of high entropy alloys were studied.
The main conclusions are as follows:
• The CoCrFeMnNi HEA maintained a single-phase FCC
structure prepared at different sintering temperatures, and
the grain size increased gradually with the increase of sin-
tering temperature. The annealing twin and deformation
twin were observed inside The CoCrFeMnNi HEA.
• The CoCrFeMnNi HEA achieved high density and exhib-
ited good mechanical properties when the sintering tem-
perature exceeded 900 /C176 C. However, with the growth of
grain size, the hardness of the samples slightly decreased.
• The CoCrFeMnNi HEA demonstrated excellent tensile
properties, with both the tensile strength and elongation
increasing with the increase of sintering temperature.
However, excessively high sintering temperature (1100 /C176 C)
can lead to grain growth, which in turn leads to a decrease
in tensile properties.
• The CoCrFeMnNi HEA exhibited good wear resistance
during friction against Al
2O3 balls. The friction coefﬁcient
and wear rate of G1000 samples are 0.55 and
8.2 9 10/C0 4 mm3 N/C0 1 m-1, respectively. The wear mecha-
nism was primarily oxidative wear and abrasive wear.
• The rapid-hot pressing-sintered CoCrFeMnNi HEA exhib-
ited excellent corrosion resistance, with I
corr being lower
by two orders of magnitude compared to cast alloys and a
more positive corrosion potential. As the sintering temper-
ature increases, the corrosion resistance ﬁrst increases and
then decreases.
• After 72 h and 120 h of electrochemical hydrogen charg-
ing, the tensile strength of the CoCrFeMnNi HEA
(G1000) decreased by 11.0% and 11.7% respectively, and
the elongation also decreased by 22.4% and 45.3%
respectively.
• The G1000 sample exhibits the best comprehensive per-
formance. The FHP samples prepared from the same raw
powder have almost the same mechanical properties as the
SPS samples, but the FHP only needs a simple DC power
supply, which is conducive to reducing the production
cost. The corrosion resistance of the FHP samples is better
than that of the castings.
