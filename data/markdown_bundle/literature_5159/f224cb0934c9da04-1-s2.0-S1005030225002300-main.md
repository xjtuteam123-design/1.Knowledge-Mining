Near- α titanium (Ti) alloys are promising high-temperature (HT) structural materials for aerospace and 
automotive applications due to their superior speciﬁc HT strength and creep resistance. Nevertheless, 
improving the synergy between strength and ductility at HTs, and thereby expanding the operating tem- 
perature range, remains a key challenge in advancing the HT application potential of near- α Ti alloys. 
Herein, a novel in-situ silicide modulation strategy is proposed to achieve superior high-temperature 
strength and ductility synergy in near- α Ti alloys. Speciﬁcally, this strategy is realized through fabricating 
hypoeutectoid and hypereutectoid TA15 alloys (Ti-6.5Al-2Zr-1Mo-1V, wt.%) containing 0.5 wt.% and 1.0 
wt.% silicon (Si) via spark plasma sintering (SPS). Results indicate that Si alloying signiﬁcantly enhances 
the HT strength of the TA15 alloy without compromising its HT ductility. At 500 °C, TA15 alloy with 1.0 
wt.% Si achieves a tensile strength of 937.8 MPa with a break elongation of 17.5 %, showing superior 
strength-ductility synergy over most commercial near- α Ti alloys and Ti matrix composites. The superior 
HT strength-ductility synergy of the Si-containing alloys is attributed both to the silicides in-situ formed 
during SPS and to additional silicides in-situ formed during HT deformation. Additionally, adding 1.0 wt.% 
Si into TA15 alloy deteriorated the room-temperature ductility while having no adverse effect on the HT 
ductility, highlighting the temperature-dependent effects of intergranular silicides on mechanical proper- 
ties. Furthermore, quantitative analysis of HT strengthening mechanisms is performed, providing insights 
for designing near- α Ti alloys for HT structural applications.

1. Introduction 
In recent years, there has been growing interest in devel- 
oping high-performance, lightweight structural alloys [ 1–4 ]. Tita- 
nium (Ti) alloys are widely recognized as typical high-performance 
lightweight structural materials, commonly used in critical compo- 
nents such as airframe structures, gas turbine engines, and heat 
exchangers [ 5 ]. Among Ti alloys, near- α Ti alloys, primarily com- 
posed of alpha-stabilizers with small amounts of beta-stabilizers, 
are particularly valued for their outstanding speciﬁc strength and 
superior creep resistance at elevated temperatures [ 6 ]. The rela- 
tively low content of beta-stabilizers in near- α Ti alloys enhances 
their mechanical and phase stability and provides high resistance 
to fatigue and crack propagation, making them highly sought-after

for applications in high-temperature (HT) and high-stress environ- 
ments [ 7 , 8 ]. 
Expanding the operating temperature range of near- α Ti alloys 
is critical for meeting the increasing demand for high-performance 
structural materials in HT environments. To achieve this, both high 
strength and ductility at elevated temperatures are required. How- 
ever, achieving high strength and ductility synergy at HTs is still 
challenging for near- α Ti alloys due to heat softening caused by 
grain/phase boundaries sliding and migration [ 9–11 ]. Generally, 
fabricating Ti matrix composites (TMCs), such as by introducing ti- 
tanium boride (TiB) or titanium carbide (TiC) into Ti alloy, is con- 
sidered as an effective approach to inhibit grain/phase boundary 
sliding at HTs, thereby enhancing strength retention [ 10 , 12 ]. How- 
ever, the introduction of ceramic particles into the Ti alloy matrix 
also inevitably degrades ductility, as cracks tend to initiate from 
these sites during HT deformation due to the high hardness and 
high stiffness of these ceramic reinforcements [ 13 , 14 ]. As a result, 
achieving HT strength and ductility synergy by fabricating TMCs 
remains challenging.

In addition to fabricating TMCs, another commonly used strat- 
egy for enhancing HT mechanical strength is introducing minor ac- 
tive β-eutectoid alloying elements into Ti alloy. For instance, Si, 
as a typical active β-eutectoid element, is favorable for achieving 
superior HT performance in near- α Ti alloys [ 15 ], because it con- 
tributes to strengthening through the formation of silicides, such 
as TiSi2 and Ti5 Si3 . These silicides act as effective high-temperature 
strengthening phases by hindering sliding of grain/phase bound- 
aries and dislocation movement [ 16 , 17 ]. Nevertheless, the distri- 
bution, volume fraction, and morphology of silicides play crucial 
roles in achieving optimal HT performance. Besides, the signiﬁ- 
cant atomic size difference between Si and Ti creates a Cottrell 
atmosphere around the Ti (Si) solid solution, further impeding dis- 
location motion at elevated temperatures and thus improving HT 
strength [ 17 ]. Additionally, Si enhances HT oxidation resistance by 
forming a dense SiO2 protective ﬁlm on the surface, which pre- 
vents Ti4 + diffusion outward and inhibits inward oxygen intrusion 
[ 18 ]. 
Notably, the characteristics of silicides, including their volume 
fraction, morphology, and distribution, can be modulated during 
fabrication or deformation at HTs [ 15 , 19 ]. Our previous work in- 
dicated that Ti5 Si3 silicides formed during spark plasma sintering 
(SPS) of Ti-2Al-2Mo-2Nb- x Si ( x = 0, 0.3, 0.6, 0.9, and 1.2 wt%) el- 
emental powders when the Si content reached or exceeded 0.6 
wt% [ 5 ]. Experimental and simulation results showed that the vol- 
ume fraction, phase morphology, and even the distribution of the 
in-situ formed silicides are strongly affected by the Si content, 
and these characteristics affect room-temperature (RT) mechani- 
cal properties signiﬁcantly. Additionally, Yang et al. [ 19 ] reported 
that hot compression of a near- α Ti matrix composite (Ti-6Al- 
3.5Sn-9Zr-1Mo-0.7Nb-0.5Si with 5 vol% TiC) promotes in-situ sili- 
cide formation at α/ β phase boundaries, with the size and num- 
ber density of silicides determined by temperature, strain rate, and 
strain. The mechanism involves dislocations and grain boundaries 
acting as diffusion channels for Si and Zr, facilitating the nucleation 
and growth of silicides, which, in trun, enhance dynamic recrys- 
tallization by pinning dislocations. Together, these ﬁndings high- 
light that in-situ modulation of silicides during fabrication (e.g., 
SPS) and HT deformation provides new opportunities for fabricat- 
ing high-performance HT structural materials. However, the effects 
of in-situ silicide modulation in Si-containing near- α Ti alloys on 
HT mechanical properties, deformation behavior, and strengthen- 
ing mechanisms remain inadequately understood. 
Herein, the TA15 alloy (Ti-6.5Al-2Zr-1Mo-1V, wt.%) is selected 
as the matrix material for its reliable performance up to 500 °C 
[ 20 ]. Then, TA15 alloys with varying Si contents (0, 0.5, and 1 
wt%) were prepared using SPS to modulate silicide formation, aim- 
ing to enhance HT mechanical properties and investigating the ef- 
fects of in-situ silicide modulation on HT deformation behavior 
and fracture mechanisms. Furthermore, the mechanisms underly- 
ing strength and ductility, particularly at HTs, are explored through 
a combination of detailed characterization and theoretical calcula- 
tions. This approach offers valuable new insights for the design of 
high-temperature near- α Ti alloys. 
2. Thermodynamics and kinetics-guided alloy and process 
design 
A commercial near- α type, medium-strength TA15 alloy, Ti- 
6.5Al-2Zr-1Mo-1V (wt%), is selected as the model alloy system be- 
cause this alloy can operate continuously up to 30 0 0 h at temper- 
atures as high as 500 °C [ 21 ]. It is of great practical signiﬁcance 
to further increase its service temperature. In the TA15 alloy, Al 
provides signiﬁcant solid solution strengthening of the α phase, 
enhancing the alloy’s strength and thermal stability. Zr, as a neu- 
tral element, improves strength through solid solution strengthen- 
ing and promotes silicide precipitation by reducing the solubility of 
Si in the Ti matrix, thereby facilitating the nucleation and growth 
of silicide phases [ 22 ]. Mo and V act as β-phase stabilizers, pro- 
moting β phase retention and supporting high-temperature per- 
formance by enhancing the alloy’s strength and creep resistance 
at elevated temperatures. Si, a typical active β-eutectoid alloying 
element, is expected to induce the in-situ formation of silicides, 
thereby optimizing the HT mechanical performance of near- α Ti 
alloys. SPS is adopted to fabricate the Si-containing near- α Ti al- 
loys, as SPS provides unique advantages such as rapid densiﬁcation 
and ﬁne microstructural control, which are critical for the research 
and development of advanced materials [ 23 ]. 
The selection of optimized Si content and SPS sintering temper- 
ature is guided by thermodynamic predictions [ 24 ]. According to 
the binary Ti-Si phase diagram in Fig. S1 in Supporting Informa- 
tion, TA15–0.5Si and TA15–1Si alloys represent hypoeutectoid and 
hypereutectoid Ti alloys, respectively. As a result, these two Si con- 
tents (0.5 and 1.0 wt%) were selected as model systems to investi- 
gate the differences in silicide modulation and their effects on HT 
mechanical properties between hypoeutectoid and hypereutectoid 
Si-containing Ti alloys. The one-axis phase diagrams of the TA15 al- 
loys containing 0, 0.5, and 1.0 wt.% Si was calculated using JMatPro 
software, as depicted in Fig. 1 (a–c). In Fig. 1 (a–c), the formation of 
silicide is predicted by Eq. (1) [ 25 ]. As the Si content increases, 
the solvus temperature of silicides increases. For the TA15–1Si al- 
loy, the solvus temperature of the silicide is approximately 1189 °C 
( Fig. 1 (c)). To ensure the complete dissolution of silicide into the 
Ti matrix while avoiding excessive grain growth during sintering, 
the sintering temperature is set slightly above the solvus temper- 
ature (i.e., 1200 °C), as shown in Fig. 1 (d). Schematic diagrams il- 
lustrating the evolution of silicides and the Ti (Zr, Si) solid solu-

4. Results 
4.1. Microstructure characterisation of the TA15 alloys with Si 
additions 
Fig. 3 presents the microstructure of the SPS-fabricated TA15 al- 
loys with varying Si contents. All the samples consist of lamellar 
α+ β structure, massive α ( αm ) phase, and grain boundary α (GB- 
α) phase ( Fig. 3 (a–f)). Grain size statistics indicate that the average 
prior- β grain sizes of the SPS-fabricated 0Si, 0.5Si, and 1Si alloys

attributed to the in-situ formation of silicides, which act as pin-

shown in Figs. S2 and S3. This conﬁrms that 1100 °C is insuf- 
ﬁcient for complete silicide dissolution, validating the thermody- 
namic predictions in Fig. 1 for selecting the optimal sintering tem- 
perature.

4.2. RT and HT mechanical properties 
Engineering stress-strain curves and tensile properties of the 
SPS-fabricated alloys measured at RT, 500 °C, and 650 °C are ex- 
hibited in Fig. 6 and Table 1 , respectively. Overall, compared to the 
0Si base alloy, the 0.5Si and 1Si alloys exhibit signiﬁcantly higher 
yield strength (YS) and ultimate tensile strength (UTS) at RT. Un- 
der RT, the UTS and fracture strain ( εk ) of the 0.5Si alloy (as shown 
in Fig. 6 (a)) improve simultaneously compared to the 0Si base al- 
loy. The strength improvement of the 0.5Si alloy could be primarily 
from the strengthening effects induced by the nano-sized silicides 
[ 16 ]. The slight improvement in the RT ductility could be attributed 
to intragranular silicides acting as obstacles to dislocations, poten- 
tially promoting dislocation interactions and enhancing ductility 
[ 30 ]. However, increasing the Si content to 1 wt% signiﬁcantly re- 
duces the RT ductility, which could be related to the formation of 
primary silicide at the grain boundaries. Under RT, the presence of 
primary silicide could lead to stress concentration during the ten- 
sile test, thereby leading to premature failure and deteriorating the 
alloy’s ductility [ 31 ].

high temperatures (500 and 650 °C). The retention of strength (RS)

5. Discussion 
5.1. Effects of silicide formation on RT and HT deformation behavior 
Based on the RT and HT tensile test results, the Si alloying has 
been demonstrated to be an effective approach for enhancing the 
strength of near- α Ti alloys across RT and HTs. Notably, the 1Si al- 
loy has poor ductility at RT ( ∼3.5 %) while has great ductility at HT 
( ∼15.3 % at 650 °C), indicating the varying deformation behavior 
of the 1Si alloy at different temperatures. To analyse the fracture 
mechanism at RT and HT, the side surface of the tensile-fractured 
1Si sample (near the fracture region) is presented and compared 
with the 0Si alloy, as shown in Fig. 8 . Under RT, for both samples, 
cracks primarily initiate and propagate within the grains because 
under RT the strength of grain boundary region is higher than in- 
tragranular region [ 9 ]. As a result, the fracture modes of the 0Si 
and 1Si alloys under RT are both intragranular fractures, as pre- 
sented in Fig. 8 (a and d). Nevertheless, the 0Si alloy exhibits a ser- 
rated crack propagation path, whereas the 1Si alloy has a relatively 
smooth path, corresponding to the higher RT ductility of the 0Si 
alloy compared to the 1Si alloy. 
As the temperature increases, both GB and grain interior 
strengths decrease, but the strength of the GBs decreases more 
rapidly with temperature due to enhanced atomic diffusion and 
grain boundary sliding at HTs. This reduces the strength difference 
between the GBs and the grain interior. According to Li et al. [ 33 ], 
the equi-cohesive temperature of TA15 alloy prepared by powder 
metallurgy is between 550 and 600 °C, which corresponds well 
with our work. When the temperature exceeds the equi-cohesive 
temperature, the strength of the GBs becomes lower than that 
of the grain interior, resulting in intergranular fracture. Besides, 
lamellar bending is observed in the 1Si alloys at 500 °C, indicat- 
ing that the nano-sized silicides at α/ β phase boundaries can ex- 
ert a considerable interfacial strengthening effect, retaining the HT 
strength [ 9 ]. Under 650 °C, cracks are observed at the GB in both 
the 0Si and 1Si alloys, indicating crack initiation and propagation 
are prone to occur in the GBs at this temperature. Since the inter- 
granular silicides can prevent GB slip and crack propagation, cracks 
along the GBs in the 1Si alloy are discontinuous secondary cracks 
( Fig. 8 (f) and S7) whereas those in the 0Si base alloy are primary 
cracks. 
For investigating the effects of silicide modulation on RT and 
HT deformation behavior of the 1Si alloy, EBSD analysis is also per- 
formed on the tensile fractured 1Si samples ( Fig. 9 (a–c)). The grain 
sizes of the TA15 matrix in 1Si alloys have no signiﬁcant change af- 
ter tensile tests at varying temperatures. Under RT and 500 °C, the 
kernel average misorientation (KAM) maps of the tensile fractured 
samples in Fig. 9 (d) indicate the distribution of strain is relatively 
uniform. At 650 °C, however, there are evident strain concentra- 
tions at the GBs (the white arrows in Fig. 9 (f)), indicating that the

5.2. High-temperature strengthening mechanisms of Si-containing 
near- α Ti alloys 
5.2.1. Deformation behavior of the 1Si alloy at HTs 
To understand the superior HT strength retention of the Si- 
containing alloys, the strengthening mechanisms induced by the 
silicides will be discussed by TEM characterization and theoreti- 
cal calculations. As superior HT mechanical properties are achieved 
in the 1Si alloy, post-mortem TEM analysis is performed to char- 
acterize the micro-scale deformation behavior of silicides in the 
1Si alloy under RT and 650 °C ( Fig. 10 ). Fig. 10 (a and b) presents 
that β plate is broken into pieces after tensile fracture at both RT 
and 650 °C, indicating that deformation initiates in the β phase 
at both temperatures [ 50 ]. As deformation progresses, the α phase 
also plays a signiﬁcant role in strain accommodation. Interestingly, 
nano-sized silicides are observed within the α plates after de- 
formation at 650 °C, whereas such silicides are not observed in 
the RT tensile sample. Besides, Fig. 10 (c) indicates numerous dis- 
location tangles appear in the α and β plates of the fractured 
sample at 650 °C. In addition, silicides pinned at the α/ β phase 
boundary hinder dislocation sliding, leading to dislocation pile-up 
( Fig. 10 (d)). 
Fig. 10 (e) clearly illustrates the bi-modal distribution of sili- 
cides, and the silicide volume fraction in the 650 °C tensile frac- 
tured sample is much higher compared to the RT tensile fractured 
sample. The coarse silicides (indicated by yellow arrows) are lo- 
cated either at the α/ β phase boundaries or within the β plates 
and are mainly in-situ formed during the sintering process. In 
contrast, the ﬁne silicides (encircled by yellow dashed lines) are 
dispersed within the α plates and are attributed to deformation- 
induced diffusion during the HT tensile test. The inserted image in 
Fig. 10 (e) shows the SAED pattern at the white dashed box, indi- 
cating that the in-situ formed silicides are S1-type silicides, specif- 
ically (Ti, Zr)5 Si3 . Notably, the S2-type silicide is more commonly 
found in near α-Ti alloys when the cooling rate is not excessively 
high (above 103 K s−1 ), because the formation enthalpy of the S2 
phase is lower than that of the S1 phase [ 51 ]. The formation of 
HT deformation-induced silicides can be attributed to: (1) during 
the HT tensile test at 650 °C, the high-density dislocations gener- 
ated could act as a rapid diffusion pathway for Zr and Si atoms; (2) 
the elevated temperature provides the necessary driving force for 
atomic diffusion, accelerating the in-situ formation of silicides [ 52–
54 ]. Additionally, the thermomechanical process promotes elemen- 
tal re-partitioning and supplies further driving force, ultimately fa- 
cilitating the in-situ formation of the S1 silicide phase.

6. Conclusions 
In this work, a novel in-situ silicide modulation strategy is pro- 
posed to enhance the HT mechanical properties of near- α Ti al- 
loys. This strategy is implemented by fabricating TA15 alloys with 
0.5 and 1 wt% Si through SPS. The effects of in-situ silicide mod- 
ulation on microstructure characteristics, RT and HT mechanical 
properties, deformation behavior, and fracture mechanisms were 
systematically investigated. Additionally, the strengthening mech- 
anisms of silicides at HTs were analysed. The main conclusions are 
as follows: 
(1) Si alloying reﬁnes the grain size of the SPS-fabricated TA15 al- 
loy as a result of the pinning effect of silicides and reduced 
grain boundary mobility. The additional content of Si not only 
affects the volume fraction of silicides but also plays a criti- 
cal role in modulating their distribution by altering the phase 
transformation pathway. In the hypoeutectoid 0.5Si alloy, sili- 
cides are predominantly distributed within the grain interiors. 
In contrast, in the hypereutectoid 1Si alloy, silicides form not 
only within the grain interiors but also along the prior- β grain 
boundaries. 
(2) Si alloying signiﬁcantly enhances the tensile strength of the 
TA15 alloy at both RT and HTs, with strength retention at 
HT improving progressively as Si content increases. With the 
addition of 1 wt.% Si, the TA15 alloy demonstrates superior 
strength-ductility combination at 500 °C, achieving a supe- 
rior tensile strength (937.8 MPa) and break elongation (17.5 %). 
This outstanding HT strength-ductility synergy surpasses that of 
most commercial near- α Ti alloys and titanium matrix compos- 
ites. The superior HT mechanical properties highlight the effec- 
tiveness of the in-situ silicide modulation strategy. 
(3) Upon adding 1 wt.% Si, the RT ductility of the TA15 alloy de- 
creases signiﬁcantly, from 9.71 % to 3.5 %. However, the HT 
ductility of the 1Si alloy at 500 and 650 °C remains compa- 
rable to that of the base 0Si alloy, indicating the temperature- 
dependent effects of silicides on mechanical properties. Further 
investigation reveals that silicides located at grain boundaries 
serve as stress concentration sites at RT, leading to premature 
failure. In contrast, at HTs (500 and 650 °C), the strength of 
the grain boundaries is signiﬁcantly reduced, and additional slip 
systems are activated, thereby minimizing dislocation accumu- 
lation around the silicide particles. This reduction in dislocation 
accumulation prevents the grain boundary silicides from reduc- 
ing ductility at HTs. 
(4) The in-situ silicide modulation strategy not only induces the 
formation of silicides during SPS but also induces the forma- 
tion of the silicide during HT tensile tests. The in-situ formed 
silicides during SPS and HT deformation both exert a pinning 
effect on phase and grain boundaries and preserve the HT 
strength of the alloys. The strengthening mechanism analysis 
indicates that the primary contribution to the HT strength of Si- 
containing Ti alloys comes from the increased Hall-Petch coef- 
ﬁcient, driven by silicide-strengthened phase and grain bound- 
aries.
