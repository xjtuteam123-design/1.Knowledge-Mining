On The Superior High Temperature Hardness of
Precipitation Strengthened High Entropy Ni-Based
Alloys**
By Te-Kang Tsao, An-Chou Yeh,* Chen-Ming Kuo and Hideyuki Murakami
The microstructure and high temperature hardness of two face-centered cubic high entropy Ni-based
alloys with L1 2 g0 precipitates have been studied. Both alloys exhibit higher mixing entropy and with
the advantages in lower density and lower cost of raw materials than conventional Ni-based
superalloys. Their g
0 solvus are above 1 150 /C14 C, and the g–g0 microstructure can be thermodynamically
stable after isothermal ageing from 700 to 1 100 /C14 C for at least 500 h. By XRD peak deconvolution,
positive lattice mis ﬁts between g and g0 have been shown till elevated temperatures. The results from
nano-indentation test indicate that their highly alloyed g0 phase have rendered more signi ﬁcant
strengthening, and the underlying mechanism can be attributed to the higher anti-phase boundary
energy. Therefore, with minor refractory additions, the bulk hardness of present alloys can surpass that
of commercial superalloy from room to high temperature.
1. Introduction
By elevating alloying contents to an extreme level, the novel
high entropy alloys (HEA) offer a new approach to alloy design
and have shown ascendant and attractive properties.
[1–4]
Consequently , HEAs have been widely studied and reported
to provide possible applications as structural, electrical, wear-
resistant, corrosion, and oxidation-resistant components.
[5–8]
Our researches focus on developing high temperature applica-
ble HEAs. The materials should exhibit good mechanical
strength and surface stability at elevated temperatures. [9–12]
Therefore, in our previous study , face-centered cubic (FCC) high
entropy Ni-based alloys with L1 2 g0 precipitates were devel-
oped,[13] as the strengthening mechanism of superalloys. The
uniformly distributedg0 can be coherent with the FCCg matrix
and remain stable till high temperatures to impart strengthen-
ing.[14,15] According to references, L1 2 g0 precipitates have
also been reported in several HEA systems;[16–20] however, the
microstructures of the above mentioned HEAs still contain
extra phases except g and g0. These additional phases like d, s,
h, m, etc. would deplete the strengthening elements from g
matrix, and are often associated with the initiation of creep or
fatigue damage due to larger interfacial energy with g matrix
and their brittle characteristic.[21,22]
Our previous study has demonstrated the good surface
stability of present g0-bearing high entropy Ni-based alloys in
high temperature oxidizing and corrosive environments. [13]
As a result, in this study , we aim to investigate their
microstructure stability and softening resistance at high
temperatures. Furthermore, the signi ﬁcant precipitation
strengthening from highly alloyed g
0 would be revealed.

4. Discussion
In this research, two high entropy Ni-based
alloys with g
0 precipitates have been studied,
as the same strengthening manner of con-
ventional superalloys. Interestingly , present
HESA alloys contain quite high amount of Fe,
and their compositions are still able to
accommodate suf ﬁcient Al, Ti content for
g
0 phase to form in signi ﬁcant quantity . As
compared to the most similar Al, Ti-high
Ni-based superalloy IN100,[15] the Fe content
in HESAs is much higher. This can be
attributed to the entropy stabilized phase
stability in HESA. In Ni-based superalloys
with high Al, Ti content, Fe addition can easily
promote the formation of extra phases, such as
(Ni, Al)-rich b phase.
[33] By contrast, with also
high Co content in HESA, the solubility of
Co–Cr–Fe–Ni matrix to Al, Ti additions appears to be enlarged.
Therefore, the g–g0 microstructure in HESA with high Al, Fe, Ti
contents can still remain stable. HESA alloys with high Al
would be bene ﬁcial to oxidation resistance and high Ti can
lower the alloy density also strengthen the g
0 phase. By
Archimedes method, the densities of HESAs are below
8.0 g cm
/C0 3, which can be lower than most of the conventional
Ni-based superalloys (8.5 –9.0 g cm/C0 3).[34] In addition, high Fe
obviously lowers the alloy cost. By referring to the current metal
prices,
[35] HESA-2 can have 20% cost reduction in raw materials
comparing to that of CM247LC.
Furthermore, the strengthening of conventional Ni-based
superalloys has relied primarily on the growing addition of g
partitioning elements such as Mo, W, Re, and Ru, hence the
alloying solution in g gradually increase in later generation
ones, Table 2. Nevertheless, the strengthening to g0 phase has
been less considered through the development of Ni-based
superalloys. As for HESA, the g0 phases are more highly
alloyed, thus results in the stronger g0 strengthening,
Figure 8b. In addition to lattice distortion effect, high Ti
content in g0 can markedly enhance the anti-phase boundary
(APB) energy .[36] APB energy plays an important role for the
strengthening of g0. From the calculation of JMatPro software
(Ni alloys database), the g0 APB energies of HESA-1 and
HESA-2 can reach to about 0.25 and 0.23 J m /C0 2, respectively ,
while that of CM247LC is around 0.19 J m /C0 2. The higher APB
energy in HESA-1 can contribute to its higher hardness at
room temperature. However, the volume fraction and solvus
temperature of g
0 are both higher in the case of HESA-2 than

Although present alloys have shown the thermal stable
g–g0 microstructure, good surface stability in oxidizing and
corrosive environments[13] and good resistance against plastic
deformation till high temperatures, which implies the
potential for high temperature applications, there are still
concerns for these g0 strengthened high entropy Ni-based
alloys. First is that from nano-indentation result, the g matrix
in HESA alloys is weaker due to less g strengthening
refractory additions. During high-temperature creep, the
dislocation would mainly move and accumulate along the
g channels. Therefore, the less strengthening of g in HESA
might be drawback for creep performances. Second is that the
directional coarsening of g
0 precipitates known as “rafting” is
another factor for creep behavior. With negative mis ﬁt,
g0 can raft in direction perpendicular to the stress axis and
hinder the dislocation climbing, thus result in better creep
resistances.[37–39] Nevertheless, HESAs exhibit the positive
lattice misﬁt between g and g0, which might lead to less degree
of restriction against dislocation movement especially at
elevated temperatures.
5. Conclusions
The microstructure of present g0-bearing high entropy
Ni-based alloys can be stable after isothermal ageing from
inter-mediate to high temperatures for at least 500 h. Their g0
solvus are above 1 150 /C14 C, and positive lattice mis ﬁts between
g and g0 have been shown. Compare to conventional Ni-based
superalloys, present alloys exhibit the advantages in lower
density and cost of materials. Furthermore, the strengthening
of g
0 can be more pronounced due to higher anti-phase
boundary energy . Therefore, with less refractory additions,
the room to high temperature hardness of present alloys can
surpass that of commercial superalloys.
