1. Introduction 
Refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) have the superiority of high 
melting points, high hardness, high strength, excellent high temperature 
phase stability and high strength at elevated temperature [ 1 – 5 ]. In 
particular, the RHEAs show superior mechanical properties at elevated 
temperatures due to the severe solid solution strengthening effect [ 6 ] 
For example, the CrMoNbV RHEA can maintain ultrahigh yield strength 
above 1 GPa even in a high temperature environment of 1000 
◦
C [ 7 ]. 
Nevertheless, most RHEAs consist of high-density elements (such as W, 
Nb, Hf, Ta, Mo, Cr, V, etc.). Accordingly, the RHEAs usually have a large 
density ( > 6 g/cm
3
). In addition, these multi-principal heavy elements 
also induce poor plasticity. Thus the applications and developments of 
RHEAs in engineering and industry fields have been immensely 
restricted by these two factors [ 8 ]. To solve the aforementioned prob -
lems, the elements with low density and low valence electron concen -
tration (VEC) elements including Ti, Zr, Al, etc. are introduced into the 
RHEAs as principal elements or alloying elements to improve the plas -
ticity and reduce the density [ 9 – 13 ]. Especially the design of Ti-based 
RHEAs, such as V

However, due to the lack of a large amount of high melting point 
alloying elements, the strength and high temperature mechanical 
properties of Ti-based RHEAs are weakened. It is extremely urgent to 
find a way to improve the strength of Ti-based RHEAs without sacri -
ficing the plasticity significantly. Network architecture of TiBw re -
inforcements in-situ synthesized via low energy milling (LEM) and hot 
pressing sintering (HPS) have been proven to enhance strength-ductility 
synergy in titanium matrix composites (TMCs) [ 18 , 19 ]. Hence, it is 
prospective to improve the strength of Ti-based RHEAs at room tem -
perature and elevated temperatures by designing Ti-based RHEA matrix 
composites with network distributed boride whiskers.

4. Conclusions 
In summary, (Ti,Nb)Bw/Ti
1.5
ZrNbAl
0.3 
composite with a quasi- 
continuous network microstructure was successfully fabricated via low 
energy milling (LEM) and hot press sintering (HPS). The matrix 
exhibited a single BCC solid solution phase, while the in-situ (Ti,Nb)Bw 
reinforcements distributed at grain boundaries forming the special 
network microstructure which also refined the grains. Consequently, 
(Ti,Nb)Bw/Ti
1.5
ZrNbAl
0.3 
composite exhibited a superior strength- 
ductility synergy including the exceptional ultimate tensile strength of 
1046 MPa, yield tensile strength of 1015 MPa, and elongation of 17.5%, 
which are 10.7%, 11.5%, and 96.6% higher than those of the monolithic 
Ti
1.5
ZrNbAl
0.3 
alloy. The strengthening mechanisms are summarized as 
the grain refinement strengthening, the load-transfer strengthening, and 
the dispersion strengthening effects induced by the network distributed 
(Ti,Nb)Bw reinforcements.
