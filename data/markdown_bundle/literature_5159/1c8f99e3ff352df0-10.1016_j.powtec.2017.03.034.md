Influence of mechanical alloying on the microstructure and 
mechanical properties of powder metallurgy Ti2AlNb-based alloy

b Department of Material Engineering, Kimchaek University of Technology, Pyongyang 950003,

Abstract A fine grained Ti2AlNb-based alloy with a high strength was fabric ated from 
elemental powders by mechanical alloying (MA) and subsequent spark plasma sintering 
(SPS). The effects of MA parameters on the microstructure and mechanical properties of the 
mechanically alloyed powder and sintered compacts were investigated. The single phase of 
(Ti, Al, Nb) bcc solid solution with a normal composition (mole fraction, %) of 53 Ti, 22 Al 
and 25 Nb was produced by MA for 40 h / 400 rpm. The mechanically alloyed powder was 
subsequently synthesized by SPS at 1150 °C / 10 min / 35 MPa followed by furnace cooling. 
The microstructure of the sintered alloy consisted of fine grained B2-matrix, ultrafine 
acicular O-phase inside B2 -matrix, and ultrafine equiaxed α2-phases along the rim of 
B2-matrix. The yield strength and ultimate strength o f the alloy sintered from the 40 h 
mechanically alloyed powder were 1644 MPa and 2542 MPa in the compression test , 
respectively. Meanwhile, the strains to failure at room temperature and 650 °C were 31.3% 
and 55.3%, respectively. 
 
Keywords: Ti2AlNb-based alloy; Powder metallurgy; Mechanical alloying; 
 Spark plasma sintering

1. Introduction 
Ti2AlNb-based alloys, so-called ordered orthorhombic (O( Cmcm)) phase alloys, have a 
chemical composition of Ti ‒ (18 ‒ 30)Al ‒ (12.5 ‒ 30)Nb (mole fraction, %) [1]. S ince the 
discovery of orthorhombic (O) phase by Banerjee [2], Ti 2AlNb-based alloys have received 
special attention as a promising candidate for advanced aerospace and automotive application 
due to their high specific strength, excellent creep and oxidation resistance at the elevated 
temperature as well as good workability [3 ‒ 5]. Many researches on the fabrication of 
Ti2AlNb-based alloys by casting [6], rolling [7] and forging [8] have been conducted, in which 
the major focus was set on the elimination of disadvantages such as microstructure segregation 
and inhomogeneity . It i s because the thermodynamic properties of Ti, Al and Nb elements 
including a melting point, a density and a diffusion coefficient etc are much different. Recently, 
powder metallurgy (P/M) techniques have been applied to the fabrication of Ti 2AlNb-based 
alloys. P/M techniques provide the potential for solving many problems associated with the 
production of large ingots such as chemical components segregation and center -line porosity. 
Using P/M techniques, a high degree of composition and microstructure homogeneity with a fine 
or ultrafine grain size can be obtained from pre-alloyed powder or elemental powders [9‒ 12]. 
Until now, several researches on the fabrication of Ti2AlNb-based alloys by hot isostatic 
pressing (HIP) using pre -alloyed powder [1 3,14], and b y vacuum hot pressing (HP ) using 
pre-alloyed powder [15] and elemental powders [16,17] have been reported. These P/M methods, 
however, have a shortcoming that as -sintered compacts have still coarse grains due to the high 
sintering temperature and long sint ering time . Therefore, it is unfavorable for the following 
processes such as superplastic form ing. In general, the microstructure refinement leads to a 
significant improvement in strength and hardness as well as a noticeable reduction in the 
temperature for superplastic forming [18]. In order to obtain finer microstructure, Niu et al. [1 9] 
applied spark plasma sintering to fabricate Ti2AlNb-based alloys from pre-alloyed powder. Spark 
plasma sintering ( SPS) is a new rapid powder consolidating technique in which the rapid 
consolidation of powder was accomplished at relatively low sintering temperature. From the 
essential characteristics of SPS, when this technique was combined with mechanical alloying, the 
homogeneous microstructure with a fine or ultrafine gr ain size can be produced economically 
from cheap elemental powders and low process ing cost [20,21]. Mechanical alloying (MA) is a 
powder processing method, in which powder particles undergo a repeated process of cold

welding, fracturing and re -welding in a high energy ball mill . Using MA, it is possible to 
fabricate a homogeneous distrib ution of powder particles with finer grain size that otherwise 
would be difficult or even impossible to produce by typical molten techniques [ 22,23]. Some

with uniform and fine particles was fabricated by MA in a high-energy planetary ball mill 
[24,25]. Unfortunately, u ntil now, few researches on the fabrication of a fine grained 
Ti2AlNb-based alloy from elemental powder s by MA and SPS were conducted. In our previous 
work [21], a fine grained Ti2AlNb-based alloy was prepared from elemental powders by MA and 
subsequent SPS, and the effects of SPS parameters on the microstructure and mechanical 
properties of the sintered alloy compacts were discussed . In this paper, the effect s of MA 
parameters on the morphology and phase composition of the mechanically alloyed (MAed) 
powder and the sintered compacts were investigated. In addition, the effects of the MA time on 
the compression properties of the sintered compact s at room temperature (RT) and an elevated 
temperature were investigated. 
 
2. Experimental procedures 
P/M Ti2AlNb-based alloy was fabricated from elemental p owders by MA and subsequent 
SPS. Elemental powders of Ti (purity > 99.95%, < 325 mesh), Al (purity > 99.99%, < 400 mesh) 
and Nb (purity > 99.95%, < 325 mesh ) were used as starting materials. Fig. 1a ‒ 1c show the 
SEM micrographs of as -received elemental powders of Ti, Al and Nb . The Ti and Nb powder 
particles have a polyhedron -like shape with a size of less than 45 μm, and the Al powder 
particles have a sphere -like shape with a size of less than 38 μm. Fig. 2a shows the s chematic 
illustration of the procedures to fabricate P/M Ti2AlNb-based alloy. The parameters of MA 
process are listed in Table 1. Firstly, The Ti, Al and Nb powder s were mixed in a molar ratio of 
53 Ti, 22 Al and 25 Nb, and with a total weight of 75 g . The powder mixture put in a stainless 
steel vial together with bearing steel balls with diameters of 20, 10 and 6 mm, respectively . The 
weight ratio was 1 : 5 : 5. The ball to powder weight ratio was about 10 : 1. MA was carried out 
in a high-energy planetary ball mill (QM -3SP4) under a high purity argon atmosphere. The MA 
process consisted of the blending of elemental powders for 10 h at a rotation speed of 200 rpm 
and the high energy ball milling of the powde r mixture for different times (5, 10, 20 and 40 h) at 
a rotation speed of 300 and 400 rpm, respectively. The planetary ball mill was stopped for 15

min/h in order to minimize the sticking of powder to the vial and to the balls, and to avoid 
increasing the local temperature inside the vial. 
About 30 g of the MAed powder was directly loaded into a high strength graphite mold 
(Fig. 2b), and subsequently consolidated using a ZLY-60 SPS furnace at the 1150 °C for 10 min 
under an uniaxial pressure of 35 MPa. As shown in Fig. 2b, a quartz tube with a thickness of 2.5 
mm was inserted between the powder/punch arrangement and the sintering mold . Therefore, a 
high energy efficiency on heating (a topic that has not been paid significant attention in the 
published earlier SPS reference data ) was obtained. In the published earlier SPS literature, the 
powder/punch arrangement is in contact with a sintering mold. As a result, the current flows 
through two routes: punch→powder→punch and punch→sintering mold→punch. Generally, 
because the cross section of the sintering mold is much larger than that of the powder, most of 
the current through the route of punch→sintering mold→punch. It may reduce the energy 
efficiency. So, a quartz tube and a n insulating plate were inserted for removing the current flow 
route of punch→sintering mold→punch. In order to avoid overheating, the MAed powder was 
heated to 1050 °C with a heating rate of 100 °C/min. And then it was heated to 1100 °C with a 
heating rate of 50 °C/min, followed by heating to 1150 °C with a heating rate of 25 °C/min. After 
SPS, the sintered compact was cooled to room temperature in the furnace. Finally, a sintered 
compact, with a dimension of Ø 30 × (8 ‒ 9) mm, was obtained. 
The Archimedes method was used to measure the density of the sintered compacts. The 
morphology and microstructure of the MAed powder and spark plasma sintered (SPSed) 
compacts were characterized by X -ray diffraction (XRD) ( D/max-rB X -ray diffra ctometer), 
scanning electron microscopy (SEM) (Quanta 200FEG , FEI) and a 300 kV transmission electron 
microscope (TEM) (Talos F200x, FEI). The electrochemical dissolution of TEM foils was done 
in a twin -jet electropolishing unit using a solution of 60% methanol + 34% butanol + 6% 
perchloric acid. The crystallite size of the MAed powder was estimated using the Scherrer’s 
equation D = 0.9/ (Bcos) [26‒ 28], where  is the wavelength of radiation, , the Bragg angle, 
and B, the full width at half maximum (FWHM) of every peak. The phase volume fraction and 
grain size were determined by image analysis o n about 10 backscattered electron ( BSE) images 
using Image-Pro Plus software (Version 7.0). Microhardness was measured with a load of 1000 g 
for 10 s using a HVS -1000Z hardness tester. For the compression test, the sintered compact was 
cut into a cylindric al specimen with a dimension of 3 mm × 6 mm. C ompression tests were

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Morphology and microstructure of mechanically alloyed powder 
The XRD pattern of the powder mixture blended for 10 h at a rotation speed of 200 rpm is 
shown in Fig. 1d . The Ti, Al and Nb element peaks were observed as main peaks aft er blending 
for 10 h at 200 rpm . This indicates that no reaction and interdiffusion between Ti, Al and Nb 
elements happened in the blending process. Fig. 3 illustrates XRD patterns of the MAed powder 
for 5, 10, 20 and 40 h at a rotation speed of 300 and 400 rpm. The qualitative and quantitative 
analysis of the phase composition was performed by MDI Jade 7.0 software (XRD analysis 
software) using the RIR value (sample to Al 2O3 ratio for integrated intensity of the strongest 
peak). In General, with the increase of the MA time, the diffraction peaks of Ti, Al and Nb were 
decreased in intensity and broadened in width. On MA for 10 h at 400 rpm, the Al diffraction 
peaks at 2θ = 44.7, 65.1 and 78.2 almost disappeared (Fig. 3b). Meanwhile for the MA at 300 
rpm, the Al diffraction peak still existed at 2θ = 44.7 after MA for 20 h (Fig. 3a). During MA, the 
powder exhibits an extension of the equilibrium solid solubility limits. I n addition, it has also 
been possible to synthesize metastable supersaturated solid solutions by MA starting from 
blended elemental powders [29]. Therefore, after MA for 10 h at 400 rpm, most of the Al was 
dissolved into Ti and Nb lattices to form (Ti, Al) and (Nb, Al) solid solutions by interpenetration 
(Fig. 5). When increasing the MA time to 40 h at 400 rpm , the Ti diffraction peaks at 2θ = 35.1, 
40.2, 52.9, 62.9 and 76.2 disappeared, and only diffraction peaks of the (Ti, Al, Nb) bcc solid 
solution appeared with a broad width and a low intensity. During MA, the crystallites of 
constituent elements become very fine and are seriously disto rted. Once steady-state conditions 
are established, the lattice parameters of constituent elements merge and a homogeneous solid 
solution with the expected lattice parameter is formed [2 9]. After MA for 40 h at 400 rpm, the 
lattice parameters of the residu al Ti and (Nb, Al) solid solutions merged, and the lattice 
parameters of the residual Nb and (Ti, Al) solid solutions also merged. Finally, the homogeneous 
(Ti, Al, Nb) bcc solid solutions (about 96 mass%) with broader and lower diffraction peaks were 
formed. This indicates that after MA for 40 h at 400 rpm, the (Ti, Al, Nb) bcc solid solutions with 
nanocrystalline can be completely formed by the interdiffusion between Ti, Al and Nb elements.

The SEM micrographs and EDS maps of the MAed powder particles fabricated for 5, 10,

During the following MA for 10 h, the shape of the powder particles was gradually transformed 
to a sphere-like shape (Fig. 4c). After 40 h of MA , the powder particles (Fig. 4d) had a uniform 
and fine sphere-like shape. As shown in the BSE image s and EDS maps of Fig 4a – 4d, the area

After 10 h of MA, the light green areas of Al were hardly observed due to the formation of (Ti, 
Al) solid solutions (point B) and (Nb, Al) solid solutions (points D and E). Meanwhile, the light 
blue areas and pink areas corresponding to the residual Ti and Nb still existed after 20 h of M A. 
On mechanical alloying the powder mixture for 40 h, the Ti and Nb disappeared. Meanwhile, the 
Ti content in the ( Nb, Al) solid solut ion and the Nb content in the (Ti, Al ) solid solution were 
continuously increased. Finally, the powder particles had uniform and almost the same 
composition (Area 1) as the nominal composition ( Table 2). The SEM images and EDS analysis 
results agreed well with the XRD patterns of Fig. 3b. 
Fig. 6 shows the change of average particle size of the MAed powders with different 
milling times. The average size of the MAed powder particles produced for 40 h at 400 rpm was 
about 20 μm. During MA, the powder particle s are repeatedly flattened, cold welded, fractured 
and re -welded. The impact force between steel balls plastically deform s the powder particles , 
leading to work hardening and fracture. As a result, the crystallite size is continuously decreased 
with the increase of MA time. However, the new particle surfaces created enable the particles to 
weld together, and this leads to an increase in the particle size. Therefore, after MA for 40 h, the 
crystallite size was decreased to about 6.0 nm, while the particle (in fact, particle agglomerate) 
size retains about 20 μm. The chemical analysis of the MAed powder is shown in Table 3. It was 
found that although under a high purity argon atmosphere, MA caused some contamination of 
the powder by O, H, C and Fe . However, contamination gases such as oxygen are eliminated by

3.2. Phase composition and microstructure of spark plasma sintered compact 
Fig. 7 displays t he back-scattered SEM micrographs of the Ti2AlNb alloy compacts 
consolidated from the powder s produced for different MA times by SPS at 1150 °C (B2 phase 
region [30]) / 10 min / 35 MPa. The EDS results of points A ‒ E and volume fraction of phase in 
Fig. 7d are listed in Table 4. At the early stage of MA ( Fig. 7a and 7b), the microstructure of the 
sintered compacts was not uniform. Especially, the Nb -phase was observed in the compact 
sintered from the 10 h MAed powd er. Due to the very short reaction time during SPS and the 
poor diffusion capability, the residual Nb in the incompletely MAed powder could not finish the 
reactive diffusion, and it is retained as the Nb-phase. With the increase of the MA time, a notable 
change in the microstructure with second phases such as O-phase and α2-phase is presented. The 
EDS analysis results report that the light gray area is B2-matrix, the dark gray area is O-phase 
and the black equiaxed area is α2-phase. In the case of the compact sintered from 20 h MAed 
powder, although the Nb -phase caused by the residual Nb in the incompletely MAed powder 
disappeared, the second phase was very non -uniform in distribution and great in size. When 
increasing the MA time to 40 h, the second phases became more uniform and finer (Fig. 7c and 
7d). It is associated with the complete formation of the (Ti, Al, Nb) bcc solid solution at this MA 
time. The volume fractions of the B2-matrix, O -phase and α2-phase were 45.8%, 45.7% and 
8.5%, respectively. And the average grain sizes of the B2-matrix and α2-phase were 1.6 μm and 
0.4 μm, respectively. The grain size of the B2-matrix is about 9 ‒ 105 times finer than that of the 
Ti2AlNb-based alloy fabricated by casting and subsequent ring rolling, and other P/M 
Ti2AlNb-based alloys (Table 5). As shown in Fig. 7, no evident pores were observed in the BSE 
images of the sintered compacts. The average density of the Ti2AlNb alloy compacts 
consolidated from the 40 h MAed powder was 5.29 g/cm3. The relative density to the theoretical 
density (5.3 g/cm3) was 99.81 %, resulted in a completely dense compact. This indicates that the 
combination of MA and subsequent SPS is feasible for Ti2AlNb-based alloys with fine-grained 
matrix and ultrafine-grained second phases. 
Fig. 8 shows the XRD pa ttern of the Ti2AlNb alloy compact consolidated from the 40 h 
MAed powder by SPS at 1 150 °C / 10 min / 35 MPa. It was found that B2 and O-phases were 
observed as main phase, and a small amount of α2-phase was also present. This indicates that at

this SPS temperature, Ti, Al and Nb in the (Ti, Al, Nb) bcc solid solution reacted completely each

TEM micrograph (Fig. 9) reveals the subtle mo rphology of ultrafine O-phase precipitates in 
the B2 matrix interior after SPS at 1 150 °C / 10 min / 35 MPa. As shown in the EDS maps and 
SAED pattern of Fig. 9, the ultrafine O-phase precipitates could be divided into acicular O-phase 
(a-O) and rim O-phase (r-O). The ultrafine acicular O-phase was precipitated from the B2 matrix. 
Meanwhile, the rim O-phase formed at the periphery of the equiaxed α2-phase. In the heating and 
dwelling stage of SPS, the (Ti, Al, Nb) bcc solid solutions are transformed into the B2 -matrix by 
the reactive diffusion. During furnace cooling, the equiaxed α2-phase is firstly formed at the 
boundary of B2 matrix. Once the qu ite stable α2-phase formed, they could not be completely 
transformed into other phase due to the rapid cooling rate. When the temperature decreased to 
1000 °C, the ultrafine acicular O-phase and rim O-phase are precipitated from the α2-phase and 
the B2-matrix. The ultrafine acicular O -phase is precipitated in the B2 -matrix interior according 
to the phase transformation mechanism of B2 → O [32]. The rim O-phase is formed at the B2 / 
α2 interface by the peritectoid reaction of B2 + α2 → O. It acts as a diffusion barrier between the 
α2-phase (low Nb containing field) and the B2-matrix (high Nb containing field) [33], as a result, 
the equiaxed α2-phase can’t grow. 
 
3.3. Compression properties of spark plasma sintered compact 
Up to now, many investigations on th e tensile properties of P/M Ti2AlNb-based alloys 
have been reported. However, few researches on the compression properties of P/M 
Ti2AlNb-based alloys have conducted. In this work, the major focus was set on room temperature 
and high temperature compression properties. It is because the high temperature compression is a 
typical forming process for bulk materials such as superplastic forming. Fig. 10 provides the 
compression properties of the Ti2AlNb alloy compacts sintered from the MAed powder produced 
for 5, 10, 20 and 40 h at 400rpm , respectively. As shown in Fig. 10a, when the MA time 
increased from 5 h to 40 h, the RT compression yield strength and ultimate strength were 
increased from 1208 MPa and 1511 MPa to 1644 MPa and 2542 MPa, respectively. This 
strengthening is mainly caused by the refinement of the B2-matrix and the precipitation of 
ultrafine O-phase inside the B2-matrix. With the increase of the MA time, the matrix and second 
phases of the SPSed compacts became more uniform and finer. According t o the well known

Hall-Petch relationship, the grain refinement of material can improve the mechanical properties 
such as yield strength and hardness [ 34]. Moreover, the ultrafine acicular O -phase precipitate 
benefits the strengthening due to their good dislocation pinning ability [35‒ 37]. Meanwhile, the 
RT strain to failure had the same trend, it was increased from 5.1% to 31.3% with the increase of 
the MA time , respectively. This improvement of ductility relates to the elimination of the 
segregation such as Nb -phase and the uniform d istribution of the second phases such as the 
brittle α2-phase. The segregation such as Nb -phase is caused by the residual elements in the 
incompletely MAed powder. Some residual elements could not be diffused completely due to the 
short SPS time , resulted in the segregation . With the increase of the MA time, the amount of 
residual Ti and Nb was d ecreased, and the fraction of (Ti, Al, Nb ) bcc solid solution was 
increased. Therefore, the compact sintered from the completely MAed powder has the 
microstructure with uniform and fine matrix and second phases . The microhardness values of the 
Ti2AlNb alloy sintered from the powder produced by MA at 400 rpm / 40 h was about 540 HV . At 
the high temperature of 650 °C, the compression yield strength was decreased to 1 127 MPa, on 
the contrary, the compression strain to failure was increased to 5 5.3% for the compact sintered 
from the powder produced by MA at 400 rpm / 40 h (Fig. 10b). 
SEM images in Fig. 1 1 exhibit the compression fracture morphology of the specimen 
sintered from the powder prepared by MA at 400 rpm / 40 h. It was observed in Fig. 1 1a that the 
fracture was developed along a direction with the maximum shear stress, i.e., about 45 ° to the 
specimen axis. In addition, the fracture surface displayed mixed morphology with smear ed 
regions and dimple regions ( Fig. 11b). A lot of tiny dimples indicating a ductile fracture are 
embedded in the smeared regions. It is clear that the Ti2AlNb alloy compact fabricated by MA 
and SPS shows ductile predominant fracture behavior. 
 
4. Conclusion 
A fine grained Ti2AlNb-based alloy was fabricated from elemental powders by MA and 
subsequent SPS. The effects of MA parameters on the microstructure and mechanical properties 
of the MAed powder and sintered compacts were investigated. The significant conclusions are as 
follows. 
1) MA and subsequent SPS are feasible methods to fabricate a fine grained Ti2AlNb-based 
alloy from elemental powders.

2) The (Ti, Al, Nb) bcc s olid solution with an average crystallite size of 6 nm can be

3) The Ti2AlNb alloy compact with fine -grained B2-matrix, ultrafine e quiaxed α2-phases 
and ultrafine O-phases was fabricated from the 40 h MAed powder by SPS at 1150 °C 
/ 10 min / 35 MPa. With the increase of the MA time, the microstructure of the 
sintered alloy became more uniform and finer. 
4) The Ti2AlNb alloy compact exhibited the yield strength and ultimate strength of 1621

compression properties of the sintered alloy were improved.

Highlights 
 Mechanical alloying and spark plasma sintering are feasible for Ti2AlNb alloys. 
 Mechanical alloying at 40 h / 400 rpm produced (Ti, Al, Nb) bcc solid solution. 
 Sintered microstructure consisted of fine B2-matrix, ultrafine O and α2-phases. 
 Alloy sintered at 1150 °C / 10 min / 35 MPa exhibited good compression properties. 
 Microstructure and mechanical properties was improved with mechanical alloying time.
