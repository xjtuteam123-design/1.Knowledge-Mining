ABSTRACT
The present work describes a detailed investigation on the eﬀ ect of cold deformation and subsequent heat
treatment on a recently developed commercial β-type Ti-25Nb-25Zr (wt%) alloy. The β-type Ti-25Nb-25Zr alloy
exhibited excellent phase stability against severe plastic deformation as well as after subsequent heat-treatments
at elevated temperatures as primarily β-phase was retained. However, at low annealing temperatures, i.e. 673 K,
a small amounts of metastable HCP- α phase precipitated, which inhibited any recrystallization and grain growth.
Subsequent annealing at 873 K and 1073 K resulted in the gradual dissolution of HCP- α phase and recrystallized
randomly oriented ﬁne-grained equiaxed structure wherein average grain size increased with increasing tem-
perature. High annealing temperatures, i.e. at 1273 K, resulted in extremely coarse-grained structure with
preferential crystallographic orientation due to abnormal rapid grain growth. The average hardness, yield
strength, and ultimate tensile strength increased whereas elongation-to-fracture, i.e. ductility, decreased after
cold rolling due to strain hardening owing to large amounts of accumulated lattice defects. The retained de-
formed structure with α phase particles exhibited highest strength but lowest ductility. Increasing annealing
temperatures from 873 K to 1273 K exhibited continuously decreasing strength values and increasing ductility
due to the recrystallized structure and increasing grain size. The increasing grain size together with decreasing
amounts of HCP- α phase also demonstrated a higher uniform plastic deformation and shifting of plastic in-
stability point, i.e. UTS, towards higher strain values in the present beta Ti-25Nb-25Zr alloys.
1. Introduction
In recent decades, Titanium alloys have emerged as an important
class of engineering materials having applications in a wide range of
key engineering applications, such as aerospace, marine, biomaterials,
and chemical industries etc., owing to their attractive mechanical and
chemical properties such as high speci ﬁc strength, low Young's mod-
ulus, good corrosion resistance, and superior biocompatibility as com-
pared to their existing counterparts [1–5]. Especially, β-phase Ti-based
alloys with non-toxic and non-allergic biocompatible β-phase stabi-
lizing constituents, such as Nb, Zr, Ta, Mo, and Sn, have been found to
be suitable bio-structural materials for applications as body implants
due to their capability to possess a combination of excellent bio-
compatibility and good mechanical properties [1,4–9]. In particular, β-
phase Ti-Nb-(Zr,Sn) alloys have demonstrated the possibility to exhibit
low Young's modulus, comparable to that of the human bone, which
makes this class of alloys a suitable candidate for bone replacement as it
eﬀectively suppresses the problem of ‘stress shielding e ﬀect’, which
results into bone resorption over a long period of time, and enhances
the mechanical biocompatibility [1,4,5,7,10,11]. However, the com-
position and processing history remains the most important set of
parameters which control the resulting combination of properties as
these parameters have a critical e ﬀect on the ﬁnal microstructure.
The overall β-phase stability with respect to precipitation of second
phases and strain-induced phase transformation, nature and extent of
recovery and recrystallization, crystallographic texture, and deforma-
tion behavior are dependent on the combination of composition,
thermal treatment, and thermo-mechanical treatment involved during
processing [6,8,10,12–20]. It is possible that an increasing amounts of β
stabilizer alloying elements in a titanium alloy may enhance the for-
mation of certain phases, such as hexagonal α′, orthorhombic α″ and
hexagonal omega ( ω), depending upon subsequent heat treatment
conditions, leading to higher strength but reduced ductility and higher
Young's modulus. For example, in some of the given alloys with a

However, at the same time, the precipitation of α-phase also increases

Since the elastic modulus of single β-phase is insensitive to its grain

However, the nature of thermo-mechanical treatment also has a pro-

which could result in heterogeneous microstructure [19,26]. Therefore,

grains, grain boundary misorientation, and crystal orientations, are all
closely knitted together to a ﬀect the di ﬀerent microstructural evolution
mechanisms, and resulting microstructure and mechanical properties.
Recently, a new composition of a β-type Ti-25Nb-25Zr
(Ti25Nb25Zr) alloy with a low elastic modulus (approximately 70 GPa)
has been developed for bone implant applications [27,28]. Studies have
demonstrated that the β-type Ti25Nb25Zr alloy has a great potential for
the bone implant applications due to its excellent corrosion resistance
and biocompatibility in terms of non-toxicity and human bone marrow
mesenchymal stem cell responses. Recently, a couple of studies have
been reported with respect to the deformation behavior of β-type
Ti25Nb25Zr alloy under cyclic shear loadings [29,30]. However, there
is no detailed information available on the response of β-type
Ti25Nb25Zr alloy in terms of phase stability, microstructural evolution,
and mechanical properties with respect to various processing condi-
tions such as room/elevated temperature plastic deformation, thermal
treatments, and thermo-mechanical treatments. Obviously, such a
processing-structure-properties correlation is crucial for designing a
suitable processing strategy to manufacture components with desired
properties. Therefore, the present work is focused on the study of mi-
crostructural evolution and mechanical properties of β-type
Ti25Nb25Zr alloy after plastic deformation at room temperature as well
as after thermal treatment of plastically deformed specimens at dif-
ferent temperatures. The specimens were cold rolled to achieve plastic
deformation and, subsequently, heat-treated isothermally at di ﬀerent
temperatures for a ﬁxed period of time. The microstructural evolution
and mechanical properties at every stage of processing are presented
and discussed. Also, an attempt has been made to establish and eluci-
date the processing-structure-property correlation for the β-type
Ti25Nb25Zr alloy.
2. Experimental procedure
The Ti-25Nb-25Zr (wt%) ternary alloy was received in the form of
solution-treated (ST) rods with diameter approximately 6.0 mm. The
average chemical composition of the as received rod is presented in
Table 1 . The rod was cold rolled in multiple passes to prepare strips
with thickness approximately 1.3 mm, i.e. approximately 80% thickness
reduction was achieved. Subsequently, the cold rolled specimens were
annealed at 673 K, 873 K, 1073 K, and 1273 K, for 3.6 ks in a ultra-high
vacuum furnace. The microstructural characteristics of the as received,
cold rolled, and annealed specimens were investigated by X-ray
diﬀraction (XRD) technique using CuK α radiation and scanning elec-
tron microscopy (SEM) technique, including electron backscatter dif-
fraction (EBSD) method. The mechanical properties of the specimens
were evaluated via hardness measurements and tension tests at room
temperature. The Vickers micro-hardness was measured by making
indentations under a load of 4.903 N (HV0.5) for a dwelling time of
15 s. The indentations were made randomly and the distance between
the indents was kept atleast three times the size of the indent. An
average of 20 indentations was considered as the representative hard-
ness value of the specimen. Tensile properties were evaluated by car-
rying out tension tests at an initial strain rate of 5.6 × 10
−4 s−1 using
sub-size specimens with gauge size 3 mm × 1 mm × 1 mm.

3.2. Microstructure of cold-rolled Ti-Nb-Zr ternary alloy
Fig. 4 shows the XRD pattern of the cold rolled strips/specimens of
Ti-25Nb-25Zr alloy. The XRD pattern of the as-received solution treated

4. Discussion
The results related with the cold rolled specimens clearly demon-
strated that the cold rolling resulted in the X-ray peak broadening
without any observable phase changes. Moreover, the cold rolled spe-
cimens showed severely deformed microstructure and increased hard-
ness values, without any signi ﬁcant observable grain re ﬁning due to
severe plastic deformation. Therefore, the results clearly demonstrate
that the present alloy system is extremely stable against severe plastic
deformation via cold rolling as it did not induced any strain-induced
phase transformation. However, the cold rolling does result in sig-
niﬁcant strengthening due to strain hardening owing to the accumu-
lation of abundance of defects.
The results associated with the e ﬀect of heat-treatment/annealing
temperature clearly demonstrated that the present composition of alloy,
i.e. Ti-25Nb-25Zr, is extremely thermally stable in terms of stability of
beta phase, with a precipitation of HCP- α phase in plastically deformed
state at very low temperatures only, e.g. at 673 K. Moreover, such a low
temperature annealing does not seem to be su ﬃcient to initiate re-
crystallization in the specimens under the given deformation condi-
tions. Since HCP- α phase precipitates disappear after annealing at
temperatures higher than 673 K, it appears that the presence of large
amounts of deformation-induced defects are a crucial factor for the
precipitation of signi ﬁcant amounts of α phase at 673 K. Other re-
searches have indicated that α phase of Ti alloys always preferentially
nucleates at defects, such as dislocations and grain boundaries, in β
matrix at low temperature annealing [12–14,18,23,24,31,32]. Fur-
thermore, Guo et al. [33,34] have also reported such a precipitation of
α phase in 70% cold rolled β-type Ti-2524 alloys, containing de-
formation induced high dislocation density and large number of grain
boundaries, when aged at 673 K. It was demonstrated that ﬁne-grained
structure, with a large number of dislocations in the form of dislocation
tangles, and large number of grain boundaries were still retained after
aging at 673 K, which provide su ﬃcient energetic heterogeneous nu-
cleation sites for α phase precipitation. These dislocation tangles and
grain boundaries hinder the movement of dislocations, which is ne-
cessary for β to ω transition, leading to favorable conditions for α phase
precipitation.
An annealing at above 673 K exhibited the presence of recrystallized
structure with primarily β phase. The grain growth was gradual until
1073 K and a signi ﬁcant grain growth was observed at 1273 K, wherein
an equiaxed ﬁne-grained structure was obtained after annealing at
873
K whereas a relatively coarser equiaxed structure was obtained at
1073 K. Interestingly, randomly oriented grains were obtained in the
specimens annealed at 873 K and 1073 K, whereas grains had a pre-
ferred orientation in the specimens annealed at 1273 K. Such a micro-
structural evolution appears to be related with the gradual dissolution
of HCP- α phase with increasing annealing temperature. It can be ima-
gined that the dissolution of HCP- α phase particles starts while an-
nealing at 873 K. However, it can be expected that a signi ﬁcant amount
of HCP- α phase particles still remains present which may acts as a
growth inhibitor for the newly formed recrystallized grains, leading to
ﬁne-grained equiaxed structure. Similarly, a subsequent annealing at
1073 K would result in relatively lesser amounts of HCP- α phase par-
ticles due to further dissolution of particles, leading to relatively coarser
equiaxed grains. At 1273 K, all the HCP- α phase particles get dissolved,
leading to uncontrolled abnormal grain growth resulting in extremely
large sized grains.
The mechanical properties of the specimens can be directly related
with the microstructural evolution described above. As expected, the
hardness, yield strength, and ultimate tensile strength increased
whereas elongation-to-fracture, i.e. ductility, decreased after cold
rolling, i.e. plastic deformation, due to strain hardening owing to the
stored lattice defects. However, it was interesting to note that hardness
and strength values further increased whereas ductility decreased after
annealing at 673 K. Clearly, these changes in the mechanical properties
can be attributed to the precipitation of HCP- α phase particles with
retained deformed structure. A similar signi ﬁcant enhancement in the
strength of a β-Ti alloy due to precipitation of α phase has been re-
ported by other researchers [18,31–34]. Particularly, Guo et al. [33,34]
have reported the UTS value for β-type Ti-25Nb-2Mo-4Sn (wt%) alloys
approximately 1113 MPa whereas the ultimate tensile strength in the
present case was found to be approximately 1350 MPa, which is con-
siderably higher than that reported for Ti-25Nb-2Mo-4Sn alloys.
However, further annealing at higher temperatures resulted in
continuously decreasing strength values and increasing ductility, which
can be attributed to the recrystallized structure with increasing grain
size and decreasing amounts of HCP- α phase particles. It would be
worth reiterating that the increasing grain size together with decreasing
amounts of HCP- α phase results in higher uniform plastic deformation
and shifting of plastic instability point, i.e. UTS, towards higher strain
values in the present beta Ti-25Nb-25Zr alloys. Moreover, increasing

5. Conclusions
An investigation was carried out on the e ﬀect of cold deformation
and subsequent heat treatment on a recently developed commercial β-
type Ti-25Nb-25Zr (wt%) alloy. The severely cold-rolled specimens
consisted of deformed, elongated, and fragmented grains, together with
an abundance of shear bands. An absence of any strain-induced phase
transformation under severe plastic deformation, even after 80%
thickness reduction by cold rolling at room temperature, demonstrated
a high phase stability of the β-type Ti-25Nb-25Zr alloy to large plastic
strains. The cold-rolled specimens also exhibited high thermal phase
stability as primarily β phase was retained after heat-treatment at
various temperatures, i.e. 673 K, 873 K, 1073 K, and 1273 K. Annealing
at low temperatures, i.e. at 673 K, resulted in a precipitation of small
amounts of metastable HCP- α phase without any recrystallization. The
precipitation of α phase was attributed to the presence of abundance of
deformation-induced defects, i.e. dislocation tangles and grain bound-
aries, which not only act as nucleation sites for α phase precipitation
but also hinder the movement of dislocations to avoid β to ω transition,
leading to favorable conditions for α phase precipitation. Annealing at
intermediate temperatures, i.e. 873 K and 1073 K, led to gradual dis-
solution of α phase resulting in a recrystallized randomly oriented ﬁne-
grained equiaxed structure as retained α phase act as e ﬀective grain
growth inhibitor. Annealing at 1273 K resulted in extremely coarse-
grained structure with preferential crystallographic orientation due to
abnormal rapid grain growth owing to complete dissolution of α phase
in the β matrix. The average hardness, yield strength, and ultimate
tensile strength increased whereas elongation-to-fracture, i.e. ductility,
decreased after cold rolling due to strain hardening owing to large
amounts of accumulated lattice defects. After annealing at 673 K, a
further enhancement in strength and loss of ductility was observed,
which was attributed to the presence of precipitation of α phase par-
ticles with retained deformed structure. An annealing at higher tem-
peratures, i.e. 873 K, 1073 K, and 1273 K, exhibited continuously de-
creasing strength values and increasing ductility, which was attributed
to the recrystallized structure with increasing grain size and decreasing
amounts of HCP- α phase particles. The increasing grain size together
with decreasing amounts of HCP- α phase also demonstrated a higher
uniform plastic deformation and shifting of plastic instability point, i.e.
UTS, towards higher strain values in the present beta Ti-25Nb-25Zr
alloys.
