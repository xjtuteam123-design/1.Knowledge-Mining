Abstract
The outlook is good for the structure and mechanical properties of eutectic alloy Cr–Ti–C in the temperature range from 200 to
1200°C as high-temperature material has shown. The possibilities of increasing high-temperature strength of Cr–Ti–C alloy by
alloying with iron, scandium, lanthanum, molybdenum, and rhenium are discussed. Ó 1999 Elsevier Science Ltd. All rights re-

Keywords: High-temperature alloys; Heat-resistant alloys; Cr–Ti–C system
1. Introduction
The operating temperatures of classical high-tem-
perature alloys based on iron, nickel and cobalt do not
exceed 1000–1100°C [1]. These alloys cannot satisfy the
increasing requirements of aerospace technologies. The
operation of high-temperature alloys based on refrac-
tory metals is thought extremely promising. Their per-
formance temperatures are at the level of 0.4–0.5
melting point ( T
m) of alloy base [2].
A considerable increase in high temperature strength
of alloys based on refractory metals can be realized at
the expense of formation of disperse particles of the
interstitial phases in crystallization. Dissociation of an
over-saturated solid solution in the process of cooling
from high temperatures brings the same results [3]. It
should be noted that high dispersivity of the hardening
phase, its signiﬁcant volume content (to 30 vol % and
more) [4], as well as a high thermal stability of eutectic
structures formed in crystallization [5], are a great ad-
vantage of eutectic alloys based on refractory metals in
comparison with dispersion-hardened alloys.
2. The experimental review

transition metals of the VIA group and high resistance

diagrams chromium-d-transition metals–carbon [6] as
the foundation for the development of alloys on a
chromium base. Because of this work, a high-tempera-
ture alloy on the Cr–Ti–C system base was suggested [7].

Alloying leads to appreciable reﬁning of eutectic
structures. It is detected in the investigation of the in-
ﬂuence of alloying with iron (2.1 at.%), scandium
(0.5 at.%) and lanthanum (0.1 at.%). It is shown that
introduction of these elements results in a reﬁning of the
eutectic structure of the alloy (Fig. 5) as well as an ap-
preciable increase of hardness in the range of low and
middle temperatures (Fig. 6).
At alloying with lanthanum, hardness of the eutectic
alloy increases in all temperature intervals under inves-
tigation (20–1000°C), and at a temperature of 1000°C
the hardness increment is 600 MPa. The prospect for
strength increases at 1000°C to 400–450 MPa. It can be
expected that alloying by lanthanum must also exert a
positive e/C128ect on the plasticity and heat-resistance of
eutectic alloy.

It has been found that alloying with referred elements
did not inﬂuence, practically, the temperature of the
beginning of matrix softening ( 0:6 Tm)
With the aim of solid solution strengthening of the
matrix, molybdenum (3.8–11.2 at.%) and rhenium (5
and 10 at.%) were introduced [11]. Joint introduction of
molybdenum and rhenium was also carried out [11]. The
structural investigation testiﬁes to appreciable reﬁning
of the eutectic structure component after the introduc-
tion of 3.8–11.2 at.% of molybdenum. The analogous
picture is observed after introduction of rhenium (5–10
at.%) and after joint introduction of molybdenum and
rhenium. On the evidence of local X-ray and spectro-
scopic analysis, molybdenum and rhenium are pre-
dominantly dissolved in the metallic phase based on
chromium. They are distributed between metallic and
carbide phases in the relation (4/5):1.
The increase in eutectic dispersivity in four- and ﬁve-
component systems together with solid solution harden-
ing of metallic and carbide phases leads to an increase in
alloy high-temperature strength. Thus, the introduction
of 4–11 at.% of molybdenum increases high-temperature
hardness of this alloy at 1000°C to 1800–3000 MPa, such
increase of hardness was also observed after rhenium
introduction. Hot hardness of ﬁve-component alloys
(Cr, Mo, Re)–TiC is well beyond the analogous charac-
teristic for four-component alloys in the temperature
range under investigation. Maximum increase of hard-
ness is registered in the alloy containing 8 at.% Mo
and 20 at.% Re achieving 3500 MPa at 1000°C [10].
3. Conclusion
It is shown that eutectic alloy based on the Cr–Ti–C
system is promising as high-temperature material which
can provide strength at 1250°C at a 300 MPa level. The
high-temperature strength of this unique material may
be appreciably increased (to 500–1000 MPa at 1000°C),
as well as other service characteristics, by rational al-
loying.
