ABSTRACT
Refractory medium: and high-entropy alloys (RMEAs/RHEAs) hold great promise for extreme environments due to 
their high melting points and excellent high-temperature strength. However, their broader application is limited 
by the strength-ductility trade-off. To address this, a series of Ti
50
Zr
30
Nb
20- x
Al
x 
alloys with single-phase BCC 
structure and ambient ductility were designed and synthesized. The effects of Al content on structural stability 
and mechanical properties were studied by combining experimental techniques, including XRD, EBSD, TEM, cold 
rolling, tension and hardness testes, along with CALPHAD and first-principles calculations. The results reveal that 
all alloys retain a stable single-phase body-centered cubic (BCC) structure, and increasing Al content enhances 
both yield strength and hardness. Notably, the alloy containing 2.5 at.% Al exhibits the optimal 
strength – ductility synergy, achieving a yield strength of approximately 633 MPa while maintaining a tensile 
elongation of 22 %. However, when the Al content exceeds 5.0 at.%, the excessively negative mixing enthalpy 
leads to a pronounced deterioration in ductility. Cold-rolling experiments demonstrate that the BCC phase re -
mains stable under 80 % thickness reduction, during which the grains elongate along the rolling direction, 
forming fibrous structures and deformation bands, accompanied by a significant increase in dislocation density. 
The formation of rolling-induced kink bands promotes plastic deformation by promoting lattice rotation and 
dispersing local strain. First-principles calculations showed minimal impact of Al on charge density near the 
Fermi level, but ELF analysis revealed increased charge localization around Al atoms. Structural analyses 
confirmed that Al addition induces significant lattice distortion. These results demonstrate that fine-tuning Al 
content enables concurrent improvements in strength and ductility, offering valuable guidance for designing 
RMEAs.
1. Introduction
The rapid advancement of modern industry has led to increasingly 
stringent performance demands for materials, particularly in aerospace, 
nuclear energy, and precision machinery, where both high-temperature 
strength and ductility are crucial [ 1 , 2 ]. In recent years, refractory 
multi-principal element alloys (RMPEAs) have garnered significant 
attention due to their excellent high-temperature strength, structural 
stability, and irradiation resistance [ 3 , 4 ]. Current research on RMPEAs 
primarily focuses on two subclasses: refractory high-entropy alloys 
(RHEAs) and refractory medium-entropy alloys (RMEAs). RHEAs typi -
cally contain five or more refractory metal elements as principal com -

Zr) in similar compositions to stabilize predominantly BCC phases. They 
aim to achieve superior high-temperature performance, including high

Compared to RHEAs, RMEAs offer engineering advantages by using 
fewer constituent elements [ 8 ]. This simplification reduces material 
costs, minimizes compositional segregation, and facilitates recycling 
and reprocessing, making RMEAs a balanced choice for scalable 
applications.
One of the critical challenges in the design of RHEAs and RMEAs is 
how to simultaneously enhance their yield strength while maintaining 
sufficient ductility and workability at room temperature. Addressing this 
trade-off remains a pressing issue in the field of advanced materials 
design. In response, extensive and in-depth research efforts have been 
undertaken worldwide to explore viable strategies that balance strength 
and ductility in these complex systems. For instance, the HfNbTaTiZr 
alloy exhibits a compressive yield strength of 929 MPa while achieving a 
compressive plastic strain capability exceeding 50 % [ 6 ]. The HfNbTa -
TiV alloy demonstrates a tensile yield strength of 997 MPa and excellent 
room-temperature workability, achieving a rolling deformation of over 
84 % [ 9 ]. By increasing the V content in HfNbTaTiV system, local lattice 
distortion strengthening effect may be induced, and enhancing tensile 
yield strength while maintaining good ductility [ 10 ]. First-principles 
calculations reveal that adjusting the Hf content in HfNbTaTiV system 
can also modify the degree of lattice distortion and charge distribution 
uniformity, thereby enhancing the solid solution strengthening effect 
[ 11 ]. In NbTaTiV system, the addition of Ti weakens the solid solution 
strengthening effect but significantly reduces alloy density and increases 
lattice distortion [ 12 ]. The activation of a /2 〈 1 1 1 〉 dislocations and {1 1 
2} 〈 1 1 1 〉 twins in the NbTaTiV alloy contribute to excellent ductility and 
cold-rolling formability [ 12 ]. Adjusting the Ta content in NbTaTiV 
system enables an optimal balance between strength and plasticity [ 13 ]. 
Furthermore, the formation of ordered oxygen complexes in the 
HfNbTiZr alloy has been found to enhance both strength and ductility 
[ 14 ]. The HfNbTaTi alloys exhibit exceptional fracture resistance over a 
wide temperature range (77 K – 1473 K) [ 15 ]. At crack tips, numerous 
kink bands form, reorienting crystal slip planes along the high shear 
stress direction, and dispersing damage away from the crack tip through 
continuous nucleation and accommodation of local strain. The forma -
tion mechanism of these kink bands involves a /2 〈 1 1 1 〉 {1 1 0} and {1 1 
2} slip systems [ 15 ]. These findings indicate that precise composition 
design can synergistically optimize mechanical properties such as 
strength, plasticity, and elastic modulus, providing important guidance 
for the development of RMPEAs.

mismatch between Al and the transition metals induces local charge 
transfer and lattice distortions, generating strain fields that obstruct 
dislocation glide. In addition, the electronic characteristics of Al intro -

the material accommodates plastic strain through localized lattice 
bending, also known as kinking. This process involves lattice rotation, 
leading to the formation of localized shear bands where the lattice 
orientation changes abruptly, resulting in a distinct misorientation 
relative to the matrix [ 15 ]. The presence of kink bands can lead to a 
transition from low-angle-grain boundaries to high-angle-grain bound -

The lattice rotation induced by dislocation glide during kinking can 
be described by the Taylor rotation axis s T = γ b × n by using the 
intragranular misorientation axis (IGMA) analysis, where γ , b , and n 
representing the shear gradient, Burgers vector, and normal vector of the 
slip plane, respectively [ 40 ]. In BCC crystals, distinct Taylor axes 
correspond to various dislocation slip modes. The < 0 1 1 > , < 1 1 2 > , and 
< 5 4 1 > axes are associated with {1 1 2} < 1 1 1 > , {1 1 0} < 1 1 1 > , and 
{1 2 3} < 1 1 1 > slip systems, respectively [ 38 ]. Rotation within any of 
the {1 1 0}, {1 1 2}, or {5 4 1} crystal planes results in deformation 
bands categorized as kink bands [ 38 ]. Fig. 11 d presents pole figures for 
the {0 1 1}, {1 1 2}, and {5 4 1} orientations within the yellow rect -
angular region indicated in Fig. 11 a. A distinct pole focus is observed in 
the [ 41 ] pole figure, with all {1 1 2} and {5 4 1} poles rotating around 
this focus, confirming that the deformation bands in Fig. 11 are kink 
bands. Fig. 11 e illustrates the deformation of the kink bands. During 
plastic deformation, when slip and twinning are less feasible, dislocation 
accumulation promotes crystal rotation, leading to kink band formation. 
These bands either expand existing ones or form new ones with further 
deformation, aligning with the elongation and increase of kink bands at 
20 – 40 % rolling deformation, as depicted in Fig. 9 .

4.3. Negative mixing enthalpy effects
According to thermodynamic principles, the change in free energy of 
materials is determined by both the mixing enthalpy ( Δ H
mix
) and the 
mixing entropy ( Δ S
mix
), following the equation: Δ G
mix 
= Δ H
mix 
– T Δ S
mix
, where Δ G
mix 
is the Gibbs free energy, and T is the absolute 
temperature. Although mixing enthalpy and mixing entropy are inde -
pendent factors, they exert competing effects on phase stability. When 
Δ H
mix 
is close to zero, the chemical interactions between elements are 
weak, allowing mixing entropy to dominate. This leads to a random 
distribution of alloying elements, forming an ideal solid solution. 
However, when Δ H
mix 
is negative, variations in chemical affinity among 
elements promote preferential bonding between specific nearest- 
neighbor atoms [ 16 ]. This disrupts the solid solution structure, giving 
rise to complex microstructures such as local chemical fluctuations or 
ordering. Negative-enthalpy alloys not only exhibit significant lattice 
distortion due to high configurational entropy but also display local 
chemical segregation driven by negative enthalpy. By introducing ele -
ments such as Al, Pt, and Ru into multi-principal element alloys, the 
mixing enthalpy can be tuned to negative values, enabling a synergistic 
enhancement of strength and toughness. However, excessive addition of 
these elements may destabilize the solid solution structure, leading to 
the formation of brittle secondary phases that damage ductility. 
Therefore, optimizing alloy composition to achieve negative enthalpy 
while maintaining a stable solid solution remains a critical challenge.
The mechanical properties of negative enthalpy alloys are closely 
linked to the formation of heterogeneous structures, such as local 
chemical fluctuations (LCF) and short-range orders (SRO) [ 17 ]. To 
further advance negative-enthalpy alloys, precise characterization and 
description of these structures are essential. However, detecting LCF and 
SRO in negative enthalpy alloys is highly challenging, as their structural 
and compositional differences from the matrix are minimal. In partic -
ular, SRO requires a quantitative description of atomic occupancy and 
elemental distribution in RMEAs. Recent advancements in 
atomic-resolution transmission electron microscopy and elemental 
mapping techniques have enabled direct characterization of short-range 
ordered structures [ 45 ]. However, these techniques primarily provide 
two-dimensional information, lacking precise insights into 
three-dimensional morphology and distribution. Furthermore, their ac -
curacy is highly sensitive to atomic number differences among alloying 
elements and the stability of aberration-corrected TEM, making exper -
imental characterization extremely difficult. Additionally, the in -
teractions between heterogeneous structures in negative enthalpy alloys 
and dislocations remain largely unexplored [ 56 ]. In situ TEM techniques 
are necessary to dynamically characterize these interactions. Under -
standing how dislocations interact with LCF or SRO will provide insights 
into the work-hardening mechanisms of negative enthalpy alloys, 
thereby establishing a direct correlation between microstructure and 
mechanical properties.

5. Conclusions
This study systematically investigates the effects of substituting 
aluminum (Al) for niobium (Nb) on the microstructure and mechanical 
properties of Ti
50
Zr
30
Nb
20- x
Al
x 
( x = 0 to 10 at.%) RMEAs. The research 
follows a consistent experimental pathway encompassing alloy fabri -
cation via vacuum arc melting, cold rolling, recrystallization annealing, 
tensile and hardness testing, supported by first-principles calculations. 
Microstructural characterization from the as-cast to the post-annealed 
state confirms that all alloys maintain a stable single-phase BCC solid 
solution structure after Al addition. Experimental results indicate that 
increasing Al content significantly improves the yield strength and 
microhardness, while the fracture elongation first increases and then 
decreases, indicating a nonlinear coupling between yield strength and 
ductility. Notably, optimal strength and plasticity are achieved with a 
minimal Al addition ( x = 2.5). However, excessive addition of Al ( > 5.0 
at.%) leads to a reduction in their fracture elongation. During cold 
rolling, kink bands are activated at low strain levels. These bands, 
induced by dislocation slip, exhibit limited lattice rotation but effec -
tively subdivide grains and promote the accumulation of geometrically 
necessary dislocations. After annealing at 700 
◦
C for 60 min, the cold- 
rolled samples undergo recrystallization, with deformation textures 
transformed into fine equiaxed grains, thereby achieving strength and 
toughness enhancement without forming secondary phases. First- 
principles calculations show that Al addition does not significantly 
alter the electronic charge density near the Fermi level. However, 
electron localization function analysis reveals a higher degree of charge 
localization around Al atoms compared with transition metals. Further 
analyses, including bond length, bond angle distribution, and atomic 
displacement, collectively confirm the presence of substantial intrinsic 
lattice distortion in this alloy system.
