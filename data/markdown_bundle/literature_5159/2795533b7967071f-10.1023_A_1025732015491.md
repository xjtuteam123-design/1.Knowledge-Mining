FEATURES OF THE DEFORMATION AND FAILURE 
OF MOLYBDENUM-TUNGSTEN ALLOY 
WITH HIGH-TEMPERATURE REPEATED 
STATIC LOADING 
V. V. Bukhanovskii and V. S. Kravchenko 
UDC 539.4+548.53 
The low-cycle strength and cyclic creep, structural changes and the nature of failure for molybdenum-
tungsten alloy of the system Mo − 30% W − NbC − C prepared by powder metallurgy at 1770, 2020, and 
2270 K are studied. At these temperatures a single dependence for the steady-state cyclic creep rate on 
the ratio of the equivalent stress to the material yield point at the corresponding temperature and a 
generalized low-cycle strength curve for the alloy are obtained. It is shown that the cyclic nature of the 
applied load accelerates directional plastic deformation and failure of powder metallurgy alloy by a 
factor from 1.5 to 7 compared with prolonged static loading with retention of similarity for the cyclic 
and static creep curves. At these temperatures the alloy essentially fails as a result of the occurrence of a 
critical polygonization stage. The structural changes found are in complete conformity with previously 
predicted features of this mechanism. Under nonstationary loading conditions the incubation period of 
the critical polygonization stage is shorter, which results in acceleration of creep compared with testing 
under stationary conditions. 
Keywords: molybdenum-tungsten alloy, high-temperature low-cycle strength, cyclic creep, failure. 
Among the refractory materials used in aviation and space rocket technology the most suitable combination of 
physicomechanical, production, and operating properties is exhibited by molybdenum-tungsten alloys with composite 
strengthening prepared by powder metallurgy [1-3]. During operation articles and structural elements of a number of 
critical units and systems of flying equipment are subject to the action not only of high temperature and corrosive gas 
atmospheres, but also loads changing cyclically with time, and this makes it important to study their deformation and 
failure with nonstationary mechanical loading. The high-temperature low-cycle strength of refractory materials prepared 
by vacuum-arc melting has been studied. At the same time the low-cycle strength of metals and alloys prepared by 
powder metallurgy has hardly been studied [4-8]. 
Features of deformation and failure with isothermal low-cycle loading in the temperature range 1770-2270 K 
for alloy Mo − 30% W − NbC − C prepared by powder metallurgy has been studied in the present work. This material 
relates to the most heat-resistant highly-alloyed molybdenum material with composite strengthening: solid-solution as a 
result of tungsten (30 mass%) and dispersion as a result of refractory particles of niobium carbide (up to 0.5 vol.%). The 
average diameter of the strengthening particles was 0.03-0.05 µm. Carbon (up to 0.02 mass%) was introduced into the 
alloy as a stabilizing component in order to retain the carbide phase during material preparation. The objects studied 
were bars with a diameter of 52 mm and 65 mm in length prepared by standard powder metallurgy methods. Mechanical 
properties of the material were studied over a broad temperature range with active static tension with a relative strain 
rate of ∼1.5⋅10
−3 sec−1 (Table 1).

Thus, the generalized curves obtained that describe in the quasistatic region characteristics for high-temperature 
cyclic creep resistance and low-cycle strength of alloy Mo − 30% W − NbC − C are independent of the shape of the 
cycle for the change in load. The cyclic nature of the applied load under isothermal conditions at 1770, 2020, 2270 K

similarity of the cyclic and static creep curves. 
Features of failure were observed in a scanning electron microscope. Both the failure surface formed with high-
temperature testing and the surface of fractures of the same specimens were studied. The Laue x-ray method was used in 
order to monitor structural changes. A successive change was established in the nature of failure from viscous 
intragranular (according to the classification adopted in [17]) into viscous intergranular as the externally applied stress 
σ
max of the cycle decreased or the test temperature increased. Numerous macropores were observed along grain 
boundaries in the fractures of specimens with viscous intergranular failure. Their formation may point to the fact that a 
critical stage of polygonization is accomplished under the test conditions [18-20]. 
A critical stage of polygonization is disturbance of the elastic equilibrium state for the dislocation system 
followed by conservative redistribution and annihilation of dislocations, which is accompanied by improvement of the 
crystal structure and a change in material geometry. The elastic stresses (elastic and elastoplastic strains) that arise are 
caused by reaction of the surrounding matrix to the change in geometry in the area of improvement. Relaxation of the 
stresses that arise is accomplished by different methods: generation of recrystallization centers, microcracks, anomalous 
mass transfer, etc. 
The defining role of the critical stage of polygonization in forming the mechanical properties and high-
temperature failure of heat-resistant metallic materials has been demonstrated in [20-22]. Structural changes in test 
specimens do not conflict with the mechanism of the critical stage of polygonization and the effects accompanying this 
process. In fact, with the maximum values of externally applied σ
max of the cycle the critical stage is not realized as a 
result of which a viscous intragranular type of failure is formed. As there is a reduction in the externally applied stresses, 
i.e. as there is an increase in the dwell time for the material under high-temperature conditions, the critical 
polygonization stage becomes a defining stage in forming the failure surface. Stresses that arise in the critical 
polygonization stage are relaxed by forming microcracks along grain boundaries. In view of the fact that the e xternally

almost no areas of viscous failure typical for lower temperatures, but there are extensive areas with merged microcracks

Thus, according to the features of the critical polygonization stage given in [19, 20] elastic stresses do not arise 
at the specimen surface as a result of the fact that the change in geometry of the developing area in the surface layer is 
accompanied by material translation. Annihilation of dislocations in this case causes recrystallization in situ , i.e. 
improvement of a crystalline material structure without a change in its orientation. 
In deeper layers material translation is difficult, which determines the occurrence of elastic stresses, although 
the level of them does not reach failure stresses and therefore microcracks do not form at grain boundaries: there are no 
micropores in the fracture of the outer and single-crystal layers (Fig. 4). Stress relaxation at this level occurs apparently 
by anomalous mass transfer. As is well known, the phenomenon is observed with pulsed loading of metals with a 
deformation rate of 1 sec
−1 and above [23, 24]. According to experimental results in [25] elastic stresses that arise at this 
rate are comparable with the material yield strength. Anomalous mass transfer is apparently accomplished so that 
stresses are reduced at intergranular boundaries caused by anisotropy of material elastic constants, i.e. the process is 
directed towards a reduction in misorientation between grains as a result of which there is formation of a single-crystal 
(coarsely crystalline) layer. 
In the central part of specimens the effect of the surface is absent and stresses in the critical polygonization 
stage reach the level of failure stresses. As already noted, microcracks formed are transformed into micropores that 
merge and form a failure surface. Thus the results obtained are in total conformity with the previous predictions for the 
features of the critical stage of polygonization. 
The occurrence of the critical polygonization stage with creep governs not only the time to failure, but also 
apparently the deformation rate. In fact, under steady-state conditions an equilibrium (in a mechanical sense) dislocation 
system forms and disruption of equilibrium is determined by the time of dislocation ordering at the boundaries of cells 
by climb of the edge dislocation components into an equidistant position [18]. In contrast to steady-state conditions, 
with cyclic creep as a result of the periodic change in applied external stress there is formation of a nonoequilibrium 
dislocation system. Consequently, under nonsteady-state conditions the critical stage of polygonization is accomplished 
with a shorter incubation period, which determines the acceleration of creep.
