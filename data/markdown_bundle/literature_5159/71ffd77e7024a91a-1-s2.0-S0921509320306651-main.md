ABSTRACT 
We reported a nitrogen-containing CoCrFeMnNi high-entropy alloy (HEA) with ultrafine-grained structure. Ni -
trogen addition significantly enhanced the yield strength and retained high ductility, due to the increase of lattice 
frictional forces and the maintaining planar slip and twinning, providing an accessible way to ameliorate me -
chanical properties of HEAs. 
1. Introduction 
Since firstly proposed, high-entropy alloys (HEAs) have attracted 
broad attention due to their new alloy design concept and remarkable 
comprehensive properties [ 1 – 5 ]. Among various HEAs, CoCrFeMnNi 
with face-centered cubic (fcc) structure is one of the most promising and 
extensively studied HEA, which exhibits pronounced ductility and 
fracture toughness, especially at cryogenic temperature [ 6 – 9 ]. However, 
as a single-phase fcc HEA, the yield strength is relatively low [ 6 , 7 , 9 ]. 
Different methods are developed to overcome this disadvantage. For 
instance, grain refinement along with fully recrystallization is recently 
reported to effectively increase the amount of grain boundaries and thus 
the strength but at the expense of elongation in general [ 10 , 11 ]. 
Moreover, many attempts have been tried by utilizing the strengthening 
effects of interstitial carbon [ 12 – 15 ]. However, the formation of car -
bides leads to the deterioration of ductility [ 12 , 13 ]. Compared to car -
bide, the precipitation kinetics of nitride is much slower and nitrogen is 
more effective in improving yield strength than carbon [ 16 ]. Hence, 
interstitial nitrogen is an ideal substitute for carbon to improve the 
mechanical property. Recently, a few studies have been reported about 
nitrogen-containing CoCrFeMnNi [ 17 – 19 ]. However, few studies have 
focused on the effect of nitrogen on the microstructure evolution of this 
alloy during tensile deformation. 
In this work, we present a novel manufacturing route to fabricate 
nitrogen-containing CoCrFeMnNi, i.e., pressurized metallurgy. During 
the production, nitrogen-alloying was carried out by the increase of 
nitrogen pressure. Furthermore, nitrogen-containing CoCrFeMnNi with 
a fully recrystallized ultrafine-grained microstructure was fabricated by 
cold rolling and subsequent short-time annealing, which shows an 
impressive combination of strength and ductility, and the coordination 
deformation mechanism was discussed.

Fig. 2 presents tensile properties of 0 N and 0.52 N HEAs in the FG 
and UFG states. In contrast to the 0N-FG HEA, the 0.52N-FG HEA with a 
similar grain size (~8 μ m) shows an increase of about 100 MPa in yield 
strength at the expense of only 2.8% reduction in uniform elongation, as 
shown in Fig. 2 (b). In view of the similar grain size and no visible pre -
cipitates in 0.52N-FG HEA, the increase in strength could be attributed 
to the effect of dislocations drag caused by nitrogen addition. That is, as 
the dislocations move through the lattice, nitrogen atoms dissolved in 
the matrix could be obstacles for dislocation glide, which would result in 
dislocations dragging and hence the increased lattice frictional forces 
and strength [ 20 , 21 ]. We can also observe that the 0.52N-FG HEA shows 
superior mechanical response compared to the 0N-UFG HEA, i.e., higher 
ultimate tensile strength and total elongation, as shown in Fig. 2 (a). 
Upon grain refinement, 0.52N-UFG HEA possesses a better combination 
of strength and ductility with yield strength of 602 MPa, ultimate tensile 
strength of 831 MPa and uniform elongation of 32.2%, showing an in -
crease of 174 MPa in yield strength, while only 3.8% reduction in uni -
form elongation compared to the 0N-UFG HEA, and obvious yielding 
phenomenon is observed irrespective of nitrogen addition. It is worth 
noting that the increase in yield strength of 0.52 N HEA is higher than 
that of 0 N HEA under the premise of the similar degree of grain 
refinement, i.e., 237 MPa for 0.52 N HEA and 169 MPa for 0 N HEA. 
Compared to the undoped reference alloy, the addition of interstitial 
nitrogen atoms would lead to a higher strain field and larger elastic 
atomic size misfit caused by the atomic size difference between the 
interstitial atoms and the solvent atoms [ 22 ]. The elastic interaction 
between nitrogen atoms and dislocations could lead to severer lattice 
distortion of the alloy and higher stress level required for in-grain 
dislocation slipping and nucleation [ 22 – 24 ], which makes it more 
effective in improving yield strength with grain refinement for 0.52 N 
HEA. According to the above results, the strategy via nitrogen addition is 
valid in improving the strength-ductility balance. 
In order to investigate the strain-hardening behaviors of 0 N and 
0.52 N HEAs with different grain sizes, microstructural observations by 
TEM and ECCI were performed at true strains of 2% and 30%. Fig. 3 
characterizes the microstructure evolution of the 0 N HEA during tensile 
deformation. At a true strain of 2%, dislocation tangling can be observed 
in 0N-UFG HEA ( Fig. 3 (a)) and planar slip dominates the plastic defor -
mation for 0N-FG HEA ( Fig. 3 (c)), which could be attributed to the 
characteristic of low stacking fault energy of this alloy [ 6 , 7 ]. At the 
strain of 30%, deformation twins (DTs) can be frequently observed in 
0N-FG HEA, as shown in Fig. 3 (d). The plastic deformation is mainly 
dominate by the dislocation slip and deformation twins, and the inter -
action between dislocations and deformation twins could generate an 
accumulation of high density of sessile dislocations along the twin 
boundaries and thus result in the superior strain-hardening capacity [ 25 , 
26 ]. However, only high density dislocations but no deformation twin 
can be observed in the UFG specimen ( Fig. 3 (b)), which could be 
ascribed to the increased critical stress for twin nucleation due to grain 
refinement [ 27 ]. 
Fig. 4 shows the TEM and ECCI analysis of the deformation micro -
structures of the 0.52 N HEA. At the strain of 2%, dislocation slip 
dominates the plastic deformation, as shown in Fig. 4 (a) and (c). It is 
worth noting that dislocations can be stored efficiently even if the grain 
size is in the UFG regime, and dislocation tangles can be detected in the 
grain interior ( Fig. 4 (a)), which is beneficial to the strain-hardening 
capacity. Besides, planar slip dislocation configuration can also be 
observed (as shown in Fig. 4 (c)), even if the increased stacking fault 
energy by nitrogen addition has been reported [ 16 ], which can be 
attributed to the fact that nitrogen can increase the metallic component 
of interatomic bonds in austenitic alloys and assist the short range 
ordering [ 28 ]. During the process of plastic deformation, once the short 
range ordering is cut by a forward dislocation, a particular lattice plane 
in short range ordering would become a favorite path for the following 
dislocations, and thus assist the slip planarity of dislocations [ 29 ], which 
is considered to be an effective obstacle against the dislocation glide and 
conducive to improve the strain-hardening capacity [ 30 ]. As the tensile 
strain reaches 30%, dislocations still dominate the plastic deformation 
of 0.52N-UFG and 0.52N-FG HEAs, and obvious dislocation cell struc -
tures can be observed in 0.52N-FG HEA ( Fig. 4 (d)). Gavriljuk et al. [ 31 ] 
reported that the enthalpy of binding between nitrogen atoms and dis -
locations can be enhanced with increased nitrogen content, resulting in 
a stronger pinning of dislocations and therefore increasing the density of 
sessile dislocations. Furthermore, deformation twins have been found in 
a few grains of 0.52N-FG HEA after tensile test, and the pinning of dis -
locations by nitrogen can harden the boundaries of deformation twins 
[ 16 ], as shown in Fig. 4 (d1). Though the increased stacking fault energy

4. Conclusions 
In this study, CoCrFeMnNi-0.52%N (at. %) HEA was manufactured 
by pressurized metallurgy. Nitrogen atoms were fully dissolved in the 
matrix and no nitride was detected, which facilitates the successful 
exploration on the intrinsic effects of nitrogen alloying. The addition of 
nitrogen significantly increased the strength of CoCrFeMnNi without 
deteriorating the ductility by increasing lattice frictional forces and the 
maintaining planar slip and twinning, and the introduction of ultrafine- 
grained structure further ameliorated the strength-ductility balance.
