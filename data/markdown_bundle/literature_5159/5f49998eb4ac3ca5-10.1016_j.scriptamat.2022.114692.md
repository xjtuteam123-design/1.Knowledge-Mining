ABSTRACT 
A method combining internal oxidation and powder forging was used to fabricate Fe
40
Mn
40
Co
10
Cr
10 
high- 
entropy alloy (HEA), in which submicro-scaled oxide particles were in-situ formed and distributed uniformly 
in the matrix to improve the mechanical property. The prepared HEA achieved an excellent combination of yield 
strength 821 MPa, ultimate tensile strength 1009 MPa and fracture elongation 28.5% at room temperature and 
showed good thermal stability at high temperatures, exhibiting better strength-ductility synergy than those 
prepared by the conventional powder metallurgical methods. Compared to the non-internal oxidation alloy, the 
increased yield strength was mainly due to the higher density dislocation and finer grains which were both 
attributed to the presence of the oxide particles. Further analysis revealed the deformation mechanism of this 
HEA to be mainly controlled by dislocation slipping. The proposed method was applicable to various alloy 
systems to fabricate high-performance oxide-strengthened materials. 
Among the increasing number of high-entropy alloys (HEAs) in 
recent years, simple face-centered-cubic (FCC) structured HEAs exhibit 
excellent mechanical properties, in particular, the great tensile ductility 
at room temperature, superior fracture toughness at cryogenic temper -
atures, good phase stability and oxidation resistance at elevated tem -
peratures [1 – 3] , etc. Their yield strength (YS) is commonly low and 
many hardening methods have been adopted to overcome this draw -
back, such as the addition of interstitial atom [ 4 , 5 ], cold working [6] 
and formation of second phase [7] . While the strengthening effects of 
these methods will become weak or even vanish at elevated tempera -
tures, those of the oxide and ceramic particles can still be preserved at 
the increased temperature due to the high thermal stability [8 – 10] . 
Hadraba et al [11] . prepared the oxide-dispersion strengthened 
(ODS) CoCrFeNiMn HEA through mechanical alloying (MA) and spark 
plasma sintering. The YS of the ODS HEA was increased by 70% at 
800 
◦
C and the creep strain rate was three orders lower for 30 MPa stress 
level, both in comparison with the non-ODS HEA. Rogal et al [12] . found 
that the addition of 0.5 wt.% SiC nanoparticles could improve the 
compressive YS of the CoCrFeMnNi HEA from 1180 MPa to 1480 MPa. 
However, the plasticity of these particles-reinforced HEAs prepared by 
MA at room temperature is reduced significantly with the fact that the 
fracture elongation is always below 10% [11 – 13] . Since the plastic 
deformation capacity is crucial for the service safety of a structural 
material, a new method that can alleviate this problem is greatly 
desired. 
In this study, we proposed a new method to fabricate the in-situ 
oxide-reinforced HEAs with better mechanical performance by 
combining internal oxidation and powder forging (PF). As an emerging 
technology with the advantages of powder metallurgy and precision 
forging, PF has been successfully applied in aerospace and automotive 
manufacturing industries due to the low cost and energy consuming 
[14] . An FCC-based Fe

The EBSD and TEM analyses are conducted to investigate the 
deformation mechanism of PF-IO at room temperature, as presented in 
Fig. 3 . Only FCC phase (green colored area in the phase maps) is indexed 
in Fig. 3 a and b. The high angle grain boundaries (HAGBs) and (111) 
twin boundaries are marked in terms of black and red lines, respectively. 
Comparing the microstructure of PF-IO before tensile test with that near 
the fracture surface, the volume fraction of twins decreases from 10.4% 
to 6.8%. The twins existed before tensile test are mainly the annealing 
twins which can stretch across a whole grain and are formed during 
high-temperature hot rolling. With increasing strain, some of the initial 
annealing twins will be consumed and transformed into HAGBs by the 
reaction between dislocation and twin boundary [ 40 , 41 ]. The Kernel 
Average Misorientation (KAM) value is generally correlated with the 
density of geometrically necessary dislocations (GNDs) in deformed al -
loys. As shown in Fig. S4, the density of GNDs has the similar variation 
trend with comparison to the dislocation density obtained by Eq. (3) . 
The Fig. 3 c and d present that the PF-IO after the tensile test exhibits a 
significantly larger KAM value, which indicates the multiplication of the 
dislocations. From the TEM results in Fig. 3 e and f, a great increase in the 
dislocation density can be clearly observed and the dislocation has 
formed dense cell blocks neighboring the oxide particles. It reveals that 
the deformation mechanism of the present oxide particles-reinforced 
HEAs is primarily under control of dislocation slipping. It is noted that 
deformation twinning is activated as an additional mechanism at high 
strains ( > 10% true strain) for the as-cast Fe

However, the formation of deformation twins in PF-IO is inhibited by the 
fine grains [43] . 
An illustration to depict the underlying mechanisms in preparing the 
oxide particles-reinforced alloys with high mechanical performance is 
given in Fig. 4 . The pre-alloyed powder is primarily pressed into a blank 
with some intercrossing micro-channels remained between the powders 
for a better controlled oxidation later. The blank is then sintered in the 
oxygen atmosphere to form the in-situ oxides on the un-contacted sur -
faces of the powders. Although most of the oxides distribute along grain 
boundaries at this stage, some oxide particles can be located in the grain 
interior with the grain boundary movement and diffusion. The oxide 
particles are greatly refined by the accompanied normal and shear stress 
during hot forging in the closed die [44] and more of them enter into the 
grains along with the grain recrystallization and coarsening. Obviously, 
the oxides in grains cause less detriment to the plasticity than those on 
the grain boundaries. Further treatments can then be applied for a 
higher compactness and better mechanical property. 
By controlling the chemical compositions of the powder and the 
sintering conditions (oxygen partial pressure, temperature and time, 
etc.), the type, size and content of the oxide particles can be delicately 
manipulated. Unlike the conventional preparation process of the ODS 
HEAs, which generally consists of MA, thermal consolidation and me -
chanical processing to obtain a uniform distribution of the oxide parti -
cles [ 45 , 46 ], the present method is more cost and energy saving with 
higher production efficiency, and more importantly is applicable to 
other alloy systems. However, future work may still be needed to reduce 
the oxide particles size for better mechanical performance.
