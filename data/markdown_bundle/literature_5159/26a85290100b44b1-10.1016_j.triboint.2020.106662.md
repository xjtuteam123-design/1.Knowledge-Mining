1. Introduction 
Metallic materials with good strength, plasticity/ductility and wear- 
resistance are key for developing lightweight and high-efficiency com -
ponents in the automotive and aerospace industries [ 1 – 4 ]. Unfortu -
nately, the exclusion or imbalance of strength and plasticity/ductility is 
a long-term challenge for conventional alloy design strategies [ 5 , 6 ]. 
Recently, novel high entropy alloys (HEAs) with the multiple levels of 
heterogeneities are desirable for circumventing the strength-ductility 
imbalance. These heterogeneities will lead to a rugged energy land -
scape in topological space, and thus increase lattice friction and 
strain-hardening capability towards strength-ductility synergy [ 7 , 8 ]. 
Most recently, many researchers have successfully used the conven -
tional strengthening routes (solid-solution, interstitial, interface and 
precipitation strengthening) to design HEAs with heterogeneities. Ding 
et al. [ 9 ] have introduced nanoscale alternating strain fields and 
chemical short-range order in the CrMnFeCoNi HEA by dissolving 
palladium atoms with large electronegativity and atomic size, retaining 
high strength without sacrificing ductility. Lei et al. [ 10 ] have stated 
that ordered oxygen complexes in the interstitial positions of TiZrHfNb 
HEA not only severely distort the local lattice but also strongly interact 
with dislocations during deformation. This interstitial strengthening 
improves the tensile strength and plastic strain of TiZrHfNb HEA by 48% 
and 95%, respectively. Seol et al. [ 11 ] have found that boron element

Moreover, BCC phase in AlCoCrFeNi HEAs is nano-coupled hetero -
structure, which contains disordered A2 phase (Fe, Cr-rich) and ordered 
B2 (Ni, Al-rich) phase [ 14 – 16 ]. In the past decades, extensive studies on 
the regulation of FCC/BCC heterogeneous structure have been success -
fully conducted. The balance of strength and plasticity/ductility has 
been achieved in AlCoCrFeNi HEAs system by composition tailoring, 
processing routes and second phase precipitation. Chen et al. [ 17 ] found 
that increasing the content of Ni element is conducive to the phase 
evolution from BCC to FCC in AlCoCrFeNi HEA, balancing the strength 
and plasticity. Joseph et al. [ 18 ] appropriately increased the molar ratio 
of Al element that triggers the transition from single-phase structure 
(FCC) to mixed structure (FCC + BCC) in the AlCoCrFeNi HEA system, 
rendering its high yield strength of ~1500 MPa and fracture strain of 
~26%. Guo et al. [ 19 ] and Qin et al. [ 20 ] have found that both Fe and Co 
elements significantly increase the FCC phase volume fraction in 
AlCoCrFeNi HEA and thus optimize the plasticity. Gangireddy et al. [ 15 ] 
and Xiong et al. [ 8 ] introduced coherent nano-precipitates (disordered 
L1

) into the heterogeneous structure (FCC and BCC) of AlCoCrFeNi 
HEAs system via aging and annealing/aging treatment respectively, 
obtaining an attractive combination of strength and ductility. Unfortu -
nately, the combination of strength and plasticity achieved by regulating 
FCC/BCC heterogeneous structure is still unsatisfactory. It is reasonable 
to infer that, under the cooperative evolution of FCC, adjusting the 
A2/B2 nano-coupled heterostructure in BCC phase may realize the 
improvement of strength-plasticity synergy. So far there is no literature 
on this new alloy strategy that can clearly proof this speculation.

for the engineering materials in industrial applications [ 21 , 22 ]. Several

3.4. Worn surface analysis and wear mechanisms 
Fig. 10 shows the worn surface morphologies of the three tested

analysis, these dispersed debris are mainly various metal oxides. 
Compared to NAHEA, the worn surfaces of FCHEA and ERHEA at RT are 
covered by more oxide debris and therefore correspond to a higher wear 
rate and COF. At 400 
◦

large amount of oxide fragments accompanied by grooves on the FCHEA 
and ERHEA worn surfaces, illustrating the presence of the oxidative and 
abrasive wear. However, there are only a few oxide debris and slight 
grooves on the NAHEA worn surface, indicating the mild oxidative wear. 
It is noted that the increased oxidation wear and decreasing softening 
resistance at temperature from RT to 400 
◦
C are responsible for the 
deteriorated wear resistance for the three HEAs [ 29 , 40 ]. 
The comparison of the worn surfaces morphologies proved that the 
NAHEA presents preferable wear and oxidation resistance over FCHEA 
and ERHEA at low-to-moderated temperatures. This is because NAHEA 
is provided with the superior combinations of strength and plasticity by 
the nano-coupled island structure, which suppress the generation and 
further oxidation of abrasive particles on the worn surface. Therefore, 
the abrasive and oxidative wear on the worn surfaces are effectively 
suppressed by the island-like heterostructure.
