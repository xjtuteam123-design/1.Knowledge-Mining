Masumoto Institute for Materials Research, Tohoku University, Sendai 980, Japan Amorphous Mg-Cu-Y alloys with diameters (or thicknesses) ranging from I to 7 mm were formed in cylinder and sheet forms by using a high-pressure die casting process. The maximum diameter (or thickness) for formation of an amorphous phase was strongly dependent on composition and the largest value was obtained for Mg65CuZ5Y10. There is a tendency for the maximum diameter to increase with an increase in the supercooled liquid region. The glass transition temperature, crystallization temperature and heat of crystallization are independent of sample diameter in the range below 7 mm while the heat of relaxation decreases with increasing sample diameter, indicative of the formation of a more relaxed atomic configuration in the thicker samples. The tensile strength (Of) of the cast MgwCu10Y10 alloy is 630 MPa at room temperature (R T), remains almost unchanged in the range below 353 K and then decreases to 395 MPa at 423 K, accompanying the significant increase in elongation. The Of at RT is about 80% as high as that for the corre- sponding melt-spun ribbon and the difference is presumably due to the existence of pores which are evaluated to be about 17%. It is thus concluded that the application of the high-pressure die casting technique to the alloys with large glass-forming ability is useful for the formation of the bulk amorphous alloys with high tensile strength. Introduction It is known(l) that amorphization for most of metallic alloys by rapid solidification is usually achieved in the case of high cooling rates exceeding 104 K/ s. The necessi- ty of the high cooling rates causes the limitation to small values of sample thickness and diameter for the amor- phous alloys. Accordingly, the formation of bulk amor- phous alloys has been tried by various methods of warm and warm rolling(S) of amorphous alloy powders at the temperatures below the crystallization hydrostatic high- temperature, shock consolidation pressure sintering(8), etc. However, the tensile strength of the bulk amorphous alloys produced by these consolida- tion methods is rather low and little have been reported about the data that the tensile strength is nearly the same as that for the melt-spun amorphous ribbon. Recently, the present authors have found that new amorphous alloys with a wide supercooled liquid region in and based systems have large glass-forming ability and can be formed even by a metallic mold casting The cooling rate of the cylindrical samples achieved by the metallic mold casting method has been evaluated(16) to be of the order 400 K/ s for the diameter (d) of 1 mm and 120 K/ s for On leave from Toyo Machinery & Metal Co., Ltd., Futami-cho, Akashi 523-1, Japan. tt On leave from Research and Development, Teikoku Piston Ring Co., Ltd., Okaya 394, Japan. d=3 mm by the direct finite difference method (17). The low critical cooling rates for formation of an amorphous phase for the Mg-, La- and Zr-based alloys allow us to ex- pect that the application of a high-pressure die casting method causes the direct formation of bulk amorphous alloys with various net shapes. Furthermore, the develop- ment of the high-pressure die casting method as a new technique of producing an amorphous alloy seems to be important for further extension of practical fields in metallic amorphous alloys. This paper is intended to pre- sent the result that Mg-based bulk amorphous alloys with diameters ranging from 1 to 7 mm are formed by the high-pressure die casting method and exhibit high mechanical strengths and a wide supercooled liquid region. In addition, the cooling process is discussed on the basis of the data on the structural relaxation, glass transition and crystallization behavior for the Mg-based bulk amorphous alloys. II. When the high-pressure die casting process is regarded as a kind of rapid solidification processes leading to the formation of an amorphous phase, the process is thought to have the following features: (l) The casting of a molten alloy into a mold is com- plete within several milisecond, leading to achievements of a significant quenching effect and a high production rate. (2) The application of high-pressure causes a good contact between molten alloy and mold, leading to the in- crease in cooling rate through the increase in heat flow and heat transfer coefficient at the die-melt interface as well as the formation of a net shape alloy. (3) Casting defects such as shrink holes which generate by the shrinkage during solidification are reduc- ed. (4) Alloys with complicated shapes are produced di- rectly from a liquidus state even for a molten alloy with high viscosity. By taking advantage of these facts, it is expected that amorphous alloys with much larger shapes and dimen- sions than those obtained by the conventional melt spin- ning process can be formed in various alloy systems by the high pressure die casting process. III. Experimental Procedure Ternary Mg-Cu-Y alloys were used in the present study because their alloys had the widest supercooled liq- uid region in Mg-based alloy systems(16). Their ingots were prepared by induction-melting a mixture of pure Mg (99.99 mass%), cu (99.99 mass%) and Y (99.99 mass%) metals in a purified argon atmosphere. The com- positions are nominally expressed in atomic percent hereafter. From the master alloy ingots, cylindrical samples with lengths of 40 and 80 mm and different diameters of 1.0 to 9.0 mm and sheet samples with a length of 80 mm and different thicknesses of 0.5 to 9 mm were prepared by high-pressure die casting of the melt into copper molds with inner shapes of cylinder and sheet. The casting pressure, plunger velocity and holding time of applied pressure were 63 MPa, 1.7 m/ s and 5 s, respectively, and the copper mold was cooled with flow- ing water. A die lubricant was not used in the present casting operation because the strip of the cast samples from the mold was easy. For comparison, an amorphous ribbon with a cross-section of about 0.02 x I mm2 was also produced by the single roller melt spinning method in an argon atmosphere. The amorphicity of the as-cast samples in the cylinder and sheet forms was examined by X-ray diffractometry, transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and optical microscopy (OM) techniques. The specific heat (Cp) asso- ciated with structural relaxation, glass transition and crystallization was measured at a heating rate of 0.67 K/ s with a differential scanning calorimeter (DSC). The heating rate was chosen because of the appropriateness for distinct detection of the glass transition phenom- enon. The TEM samples were prepared by thinning elec- trolytically the cast sheet, which had previously thinned by mechanical polishing, in a solution of 20% nitric acid and 80% methanol at 273 K. Hardness was measured at room temperature by a Vickers microhardness tester with a 4.9 N load. The tensile strength of the as-cast sheet and melt-spun ribbon specimens and the compressive strength of the as-cast cylinder were measured in the tem- perature range of 293 to 473 K by an Instron-type tensile testing machine at a strain rate of 5.6 x 10-4 s -l for the tensile strength and 4.2 x 10-4 s -l for the compressive strength. The testing temperature was controlled by us- ing a heating medium of silicon oil. Tensile and com- pressive specimens of the as-cast alloy were machined from the cast sheets into sheets having a gauge dimension of 3 mm in width and 0.5 mm in thickness and from the cast cylinders into cylinders having the dimensions of 2 mm diameter and 4 mm height, respectively. Also, the melt-spun ribbon was cut into strips having a gauge dimension of 20 mm long. Subsequent to tensile testings, the fracture surface appearance was examined by scan- ning electron microscopy (SEM). The melting temperature of the as-cast ingots was measured from the heating and cooling curves obtained at a scanning rate of 0.33 K/ s with a differential thermal analyzer (DTA).

The feature of the stress-elongation curves can be divided into the following three types. That is, (1) high fracture stress and no appreciable elongation at 293 K, (2) high yield stress and small elongation in the temperature range of 353 to 393 K, (3) a clear yield phenomenon, followed by a significant decrease in nominal flow stress, accom- panying large elongation in the range above 423 K. On the basis of the nominal stress-elongation curves shown in Fig. 14, the fracture stress at 293 K, the proof stress at a fixed elongation of 0.2% and elongation are plotted as a function of temperature in Fig. 15. The fracture stress is 630 MPa at 293 K, remains almost unchanged at the temperatures up to 353 K and then decreases rapidly with increasing temperature. Thus, the high proof stress exceeding 400 MPa is kept in the temperature range below 373 K. On the other hand, the elongation is below 70/0 in the temperature range below 393 K, because of inhomogeneous deformation and fracture behavior without distinct work-hardenability, and increases rap- idly to about 37% in the temperature range above 400 K, because of the significant viscous flow.
