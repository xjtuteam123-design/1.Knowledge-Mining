ABSTRACT 
Balancing the strengthening effect and microstructure instability led by refractory elements is crucial for su -
peralloys. In this work, comparative study was conducted on two second-generation Ni-based single crystal 
superalloys which have similar components and total content of W + Mo except for different W/Mo ratios. It has 
been evidenced that μ phase precipitated in the high W/Mo ratio alloy after isothermal exposure for 1,500 h at 
950 
◦
C, whereas only sub-micron P phase particles formed in the low W/Mo ratio alloy even after 10,000 h of 
isothermal exposure. Striking increase in stress rupture life was achieved for the low W/Mo ratio alloy experi -
enced 10,000 h of isothermal exposure. The mutation in TCP phases and enhanced stress rupture life were 
ascribed to the dendrite segregation of W and Re, and partitioning behavior of Cr and Re in γ and γ
′
phases 
induced by W/Mo ratio. 
1. Introduction 
As the demand for thermal efficiency of gas turbines keeps rising, 
more refractory elements (Mo, W and Re, etc .) are added to nickel-based 
single crystal (SX) superalloys for higher temperature capability [1] . 
However, this could result in topologically close-packed (TCP) phase 
formation during long-term service at high temperature [2] , causing the 
depletion of solid solution strengthening (SSS) elements in γ matrix and 
destruction of coherent γ / γ
′
interface, which impair service performance 
and bring about premature failure [3,4] . Therefore, the optimization of 
refractory element addition is concerned for alloy design. 
W and Re segregate to the dendrite core during solidification and are 
difficult to homogenize due to low diffusivity [5] . Recently, the effect of 
replacing W with Mo in nickel-based superalloys was investigated. 
Fleischmann et al. [6] found that Mo provided stronger SSS effect than W 
of the same mass fraction in SX alloys. Increasing Mo/W ratio relieved 
the segregation of W and Re and resulted in more Re in γ phase in SX 
alloys [7,8] . However, Ma et al . [9] suggested Mo had the strongest 
potential on the formation of μ phase, followed by Re, W and Cr. Pan et 
al . [10] found that increasing Mo/W ratio reduced the activation energy 
of γ
′
precipitate coarsening and led to microstructure instability. 
Apparently, the effect of substituting Mo for W on TCP phase and 
microstructure stability of Ni-based superalloys remains uncertain. To 
elucidate this problem, comparative study was conducted on two nickel- 
based SX superalloys with different W/Mo ratios. The evolution of 
microstructure during long-term isothermal exposure was investigated, 
and the stress rupture life of isothermally exposed alloys were measured. 
This work is hopeful to provide a reference for the composition design of 
Ni-based SX superalloys. 
2. Experimental 
Two Ni-based SX superalloys denoted as NS6 and NS8 have similar 
nominal compositions (wt.%): balanced Ni, 8Co, 6Cr, 6Ta, 5.6Al, 3Re, 
minor Hf, B and C, but 1.5Mo, 6.5W for NS6 and 2.5Mo, 5.5W for NS8. 
They have equal content of W plus Mo, but the W/Mo ratio of NS8 is half 
of that in NS6. The SX bars were prepared by high-rate solidification 
(HRS) process and subjected to heat treatment (1290 
◦
C and 1280 
◦
C/4 h 
for NS6 and NS8, respectively; 1120 
◦
C/4 h and 900 
◦
C/16 h). Then the 
isothermal exposure was conducted at 950 
◦
C for 500 h, 1500 h, 5,000 h, 
and 10,000 h, respectively. 
Microstructure evolution was investigated by a scanning electron 
microscope (SEM). Electron probe microanalysis (EPMA) was conducted 
to measure compositions in the dendrite core and interdendritic region.

High angle annular dark field (HAADF)-EDS was used for composi -

stabilized P phase, which coincides with the current result. The P phase 
in NS8 remained sub-micro scale even after 10,000 h of isothermal

and only P phase nucleates after long-term isothermal exposure.

4. Conclusions 
The precipitation behavior of nickel-based SX superalloys was 
mutated by tuning W/Mo ratio. In NS6 alloy with high W/Mo ratio, 
needle-like μ phase precipitated after annealed for 1,500 h at 950 
◦
C. 
However, μ phase was completely suppressed in NS8 and only tiny P 
phase particles formed during isothermal exposure, which exhibited 
extraordinary kinetic stability. The mutation in TCP phase is closely 
related to the dendrite segregation and partitioning behavior of ele -
ments in γ and γ
′
phase. Among them, the dendrite segregation of W and 
Re, and partitioning behavior of Cr and Re are especially enhanced in 
NS6 alloys. After 10,000 h of thermal exposure, the stress rupture life of 
NS8 is increased by 80% compared with that of NS6, indicating the 
mutation in TCP phase remarkably improves the service performance of 
superalloys.
