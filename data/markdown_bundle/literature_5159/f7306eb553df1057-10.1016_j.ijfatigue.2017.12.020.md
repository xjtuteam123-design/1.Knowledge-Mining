Abstract 
Fatigue crack growth ( FCG) rate is an important material parameter in structure safety and reliability 
assessment. As temperature T has remarkable effects on FCG behaviors of superalloys, it is in urgent 
need to establish a n accurate FCG model which can predict FCG rates of superalloys at different 
temperatures. This work aims to model the temperature influence on FCG rate for superalloys. Firstly, 
systematic FCG experiments of a Ni -base superalloy at different temperatures are conducted to obtain 
the experimental data. Test results show that the FCG rate increases significantly with increas e of 
temperature. Then, based on the observed linear relationship between the ratio of FCG rate to T and the 
stress intensity factor (SIF) range △K, an empirical FCG model called temperature exponential model, 
in which there are four material parameters C1, C2, C3 and C4, is built up by means of fitting approach . 
Two different methods, which are based on the FCG rates data and Paris model parameters respectively, 
are developed to acquire the four material parameters. At last, the temperature exponential model is 
validated against a variety of superalloys, proving its high accuracy and wide applicability. 
 
Keywords: Fatigue crack growth rate; temperature influence; superalloy; temperature exponential model 
1. Introduction 
The fatigue crack growth (FCG) rate of material is an essential parameter to conduct residual life 
analysis or damage tolerance design of structures [1, 2]. According to previous research, the FCG 
behavior of superalloys is significantly affected by the factor of temperature [3-9]. Normally, the FCG 
rate of superalloys increases remarkably with the increase of temperature when other test conditions are 
kept identical [3-5]. High temperature structural components made of superalloys , such as turbine disk 
and shaft etc., usually go through a broad temperature range during system start-up and shut -down

operations. Even during the steady-state in operation, there still exists obvious temperature difference 
among different parts of the same component, and t he maximum temperature di fference in one 
component can reach more than 100℃ [10, 11]. Therefore, it is necessary to characterize precisely the 
FCG behavior of superalloys at different temperatures for structural design. 
The FCG behavior of superalloys is usually modelled by the relationship between FCG rate and its 
affecting variables. Previous developed models to predict FCG rates can be broadly classified into two 
categories: the physically-based ones that are derived from crack propagation mechanism [12, 13], and 
the empirical ones that are fitted from FCG test data [14, 15]. 
In the aspect of physically-based models describing temperature influence on FCG behavior, Jeglic 
et al. firstly proposed an Arrhenius-type FCG model, supposing that the FCG rate is dominantly 
controlled by a volume diffusive process [12]. Yokobori et al. established another FCG model based on 
dislocation dynamics [13]. To consider the effect of temperature influence on FCG rate, t he 
physically-based models are usually based on some assumptions, and introduce material parameters 
which are hardly to obtain in practical engineering, for example, the activation energy [12]. 
In comparison with the physically -based models, the empirical models get more applications .

Among the empirical models fitted from FCG test data, the Paris model is the most widely used one in 
engineering fields [14]. Actually, the Paris model itself, in which FCG rate is only the function of stress 
intensity factor (SIF) range, does not consider the effect of temperature influence . In order to describe 
the FCG behavior at different temperatures using Paris model, t he current practical way is determining 
the Paris model parameters at different temperatures, w hich is actually experimental, rather than 
modelling method to consider the temperature effects on FCG rate. 
This paper aim s to build a n empirical type of FCG model which can describe the temperature 
influence on FCG behavior of superalloys explicitly. Firstly, several groups of FCG experiments at 
different temperatures are designed and carried out . Based on the linear relationship between 
lg(da/dN)/T and lg(ΔK), an FCG empirical model called temperature exponential model is proposed. 
After that, the method to acquire material parameters in temperature exponential model is developed. At 
last, the accuracy and applicability of the temperature exponential model is validated against a variety 
of superalloys.

3. Temperature exponential model 
In this part, on the basis of above FCG experimental results, a new model is built to predict FCG 
rates of superalloys at different temperatures. In order to validate the proposed model, the methods to 
determine material parameters in the model are also developed. Afterwards, the accuracy and 
applicability of temperature model is validated by the investigated superalloy and four other kinds of 
superalloys.

4. Discussion 
According to the model validation results, the proposed temperature exponential model is able to 
predict FCG rate s of superalloys at different temperatures with high accuracy. In comparison with the 
physically-based models, for example the Arrhenius-type FCG model , the parameters in temperature 
exponential model are easier to determine. Once two groups of da/dN-ΔK data, or two groups of Paris 
model parameters C and m at different temperatures are provided, the parameters C1, C2, C3 and C4 can 
be calculated conveniently. In addition, the novel model covers a continuous range of temperature 
rather than a single temperature point, which is different from the traditional empirically-based models. 
Supposing the parameters C1, C2, C3 and C4 are derived from the data source at temperature s T1 
and T2 (T1 < T2), it is certain that the temperature exponential model can apply to the temperature range 
T1 ~ T2. Besides that, as the crack growth rate da/dN increases with the increase of stress intensity factor 
range ΔK generally, the slope of the linear FCG model in the double logarithm coordinate should be 
positive. As a result, the following relation can be established

5. Conclusion 
In order to model the temperature influence on the FCG behavior of supera lloys, a series of FCG 
tests of a Ni-base superalloy at different temperatures are designed and conducted. Based on the test 
results, a FCG model capable of describing the superalloys FCG behavior at different temperatures is 
proposed and validated. 
The FCG behavior of the Ni-base superalloy is tested at 300 ℃, 550℃ and 650℃. The FCG rates 
of the investigated superalloy are calculated based on the experimental results. It indicates that the FCG 
rates of the superalloy increase significantly with increase of temperature. In addition, the fractographic 
analysis shows that the single striation width increases with the rising temperature, further proving the 
acceleration effect of temperature on FCG rates. 
It is observed that there exists a linear relationship between the ratio lg(da/dN)/T and lg(ΔK) in this 
investigation, base on which, an empirical FCG law, called the temperature exponential model is 
proposed to describe the temperature influence. The model has an explicit and simple form, which 
includes two variables, i.e. the stress intensity factor range △K and temperature T, and four material 
parameters C1, C2, C3 and C4. In this paper, two approaches based on different available data a re 
introduced to determine the material parameters . According to the validation of the investigated 
material and four other kinds of superalloys, the minimum MAPE index is 9.55% while the maximum 
MAPE index is 23.96%, proving the model with high accuracy and wide applicability.
