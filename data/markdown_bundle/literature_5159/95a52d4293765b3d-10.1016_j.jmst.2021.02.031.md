Implementation of novel γ/ γ’ Co-based superalloys with higher strength and improved creep durabil- 
ity is a challenging task for researchers. The lack of atomic-level understanding of plastic deformation 
behavior has seriously limited the exploration of the full capacity of Co-based alloys. We put forward a 
comprehensive study of generalized stacking fault energies by ﬁrst principles to explore the effect of Ni 
and Al/W on the plastic deformation mechanism of γ’ precipitates in Co-based superalloys. It is found 
that alloying Ni and adjusting Al/W obviously change the dislocation glide and twinning nucleation in the 
γ’ precipitates by altering the stable fault energies and the unstable fault energy barriers. Excessive ad- 
dition of either Ni or W deteriorates the strength even the stability of alloys. The ratio of effective planar 
fault energy ( /Delta1E p ) bridges intrinsic energy barriers and various deformation mechanisms of superalloys 
at elevated temperatures. Except for alloying effects, the grain orientation also signiﬁcantly governs the 
operation of the plastic deformation of superalloys. Our theoretical results agree with the available mea- 
surements and well capture the observed phenomena in experiments.

1. Introduction 
Demands for high aero-engine eﬃciency require the increasing 
service temperature of superalloys. The development of Ni-based 
superalloys hits a bottleneck due to the limitation of the melting 
point of Ni. With the discovery of γ’-Co 3 (Al, W) [1] , a research 
fever has been raging in the development of advanced γ/ γ’ Co- 
based superalloys [2–12] , because they have higher melting tem- 
perature by 50–150 °C and greater high-temperature creep resis- 
tant than commercial Ni-based superalloys [ 1 , 13 ]. 
Recent researches [ 12 , 14 , 15 ] indicate that alloying Ni in the Co- 
Al-W system simult aneously widens the γ/ γ’ phase ﬁeld, stabi- 
lizes the γ’ precipitate, and enhances the γ’ solvus temperature. 
Particularly, the Ni-containing Co–Al–W alloys show signiﬁcant 
improvement in creep resistance with properties comparable to 
the 1st generation Ni-based superalloys [ 16 , 17 ]. The observations 
[ 7 , 9 , 18 , 19 ] imply that the impressive creep resistance is closely as- 
sociated with the unusually plasticity-induced stacking fault ribbon 
in the γ’ precipitate. Creep deformation of CoNi-based alloys re- 
sults in shearing of γ′ precipitates by the matrix dislocations, cre- 
ating 1/2 < 110 > antiphase boundaries (APBs) lying on {111} planes 
[3] . However, in contrast with the observation in Ni-based superal-

Al and W in the γ’ precipitates. This large variation of Al/W ratio 
induced by Ni may also tune the plastic deformation behaviors by 
adjusting the SFEs of planar defects in the γ’ precipitates [ 7 , 9 ].

commonly observed [ 2 , 3 , 9 , 23 ], the quantitative estimation of SFEs 
is particularly diﬃcult in experiments. Up to now, limited theoreti- 
cal research had been done to acquire the SFEs of γ’ phase by em-

analytical foundation of plasticity behavior for the rational design 
of novel CoNi-based superalloys with superior creep resistance.

2.2. Computational details 
All the calculations were carried out using the exact muﬃn-tin 
orbitals (EMTO) [ 28 , 29 ] method based on the DFT. The exchange- 
correlation functional was depicted by the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof 
(PBE). The Kohn-Sham equations were solved within the soft-core 
scheme and the scalar relativistic approximation. The paramag- 
netic state (PM) was simulated by the disordered local moments 
(DLM) model which treats spin-up and spin-down components for 
each magnetic element as randomly distributed [30] . A supercell 
composed of six-layers of {111} planes with and without stacking 
faults was constructed for the SFE calculations. 25 × 25 × 25 and 
6 × 11 × 4 k-mesh were adopted for the balance radius and the 
GSFE calculations after carefully testing. The chemical and mag- 
netic randomness was realized within the framework of coherent-

unstable GSFEs (i.e., γUSF / γUCISF , γUCESF / γUESF , γUAPB , and γUSISF )

cleation barrier of dislocation glide, were also identiﬁed. For the 
sake of convenience, all the stable and unstable GSFEs for the base 
phases are summarized in Table 2 . Overall, the agreement with the 
available data implies the reliability of the method utilized in the 
present study. It is worth noting that the stable faults of CISF, CESF, 
and APB for the L1 2 structure are not necessarily stable or are dis- 
placed away from the geometrically deﬁned position due to sym- 
metry change during the shear process [52] . Herein, the SFEs of 
CISF, CESF, and APB at the energy-minimum position were taken 
as the stable ones instead of those at the geometrically deﬁned 
position. 
3.2. Inﬂuence of Ni and Al/W on GSF surface of γ ’ Co 3 (Al, W) 
To explore the effect of solute Ni as well as Al/W ratio on 
the stacking faults, four typical phases were ﬁrst picked out for 
estimating their GSF surface, including Co 3 Al, Ni 3 Al, Co 3 (Al, W) 
(Co0Ni), and (Co 0.6 , Ni 0.4 ) 3 (Al, W) (Co30Ni). We identiﬁed all these 
phases to be in PM state with T C lower than 298 K based on 
Eq. (3) except Co 3 Al. The lattice parameters for Co 3 Al, Co 3 (Al, W) 
and (Co 0.6 , Ni 0.4 ) 3 (Al, W) are estimated to be 3.581 ˚A , 3.589 ˚A , and 
3.603 ˚A, respectively, which reproduces the available experimen- 
tal [ 14 , 53 ] and theoretical data [11] . The GSF surfaces are plotted 
in Fig. 3 . The limited measurement and calculation for Co 3 (Al, W) 
[ 10 , 24–26 , 54 , 55 ] are presented as well. The dispersion of measured 
GSFEs is due to the large uncertainty ascribed from the disloca- 
tion width, grain size, and residual stresses in the investigation of 
metastable morphology of Co 3 (Al, W) as well as the assumption 
made in the energy evaluation. The γAPB is evaluated as 155 ±20

by Guédou et al. [55] in experiments. The GSFEs from DFT cal- 
culations [ 10 , 24–26 ] are also scattered that may result from the 
different models adopted to handle the partial order conﬁguration 
of Co 3 (Al, W) with Al and W mixing in the B sublattice of A 3 B. 
By employing the CPA approach, our calculated stable GSFEs agree 
with very recently theoretical data [24] in the same order of mag- 
nitude.

3.3. Intrinsic stacking fault energy barrier 
As described in the GSFE curve in Fig. 1 , the intrinsic stacking 
fault energy barrier ( /Delta1γ) determines the diﬃculty of stacking fault 
formation when deforming a perfect lattice towards an arbitrary 
stacking fault. It is estimated based on stable and unstable GSFEs 
from Fig. 4 and Table S1. The γUCISF value sets the barrier for the 
complex stacking fault formation, and the γUCESF and γUAPB values 
relative to γCISF control the competition between CESF and APB 
models. The γUSISF value relative to γAPB sets the dislocation dis- 
sociated barrier from APB to SISF defects. Namely, the /Delta1γ values 
for CISF, CESF, APB and SISF are deﬁned as 
/Delta1γCISF = γUCISF , /Delta1γCESF = γUCESF − γCISF , /Delta1γAPB 
= γUAPB − γCISF , /Delta1γSISF = γUSISF − γAPB . (4)

4. Discussion 
4.1. Ratio of planar defect energy 
The high-temperature creep and mechanical properties of su- 
peralloys are believed to arise from the great resistance of matrix 
dislocation shearing cross the γ’ precipitates. The stable stacking 
energies of APB and SISF are inferred to determine the active de- 
formation model of γ’ in the CoNi-based system at high temper- 
atures [ 7 , 9 , 22 ]. Eggeler et al. [9] pointed out that the GSFEs dif- 
ference whereby γAPB should be larger than γSISF by 60 mJ/m 2 is 
the essential condition. Except the intrinsic GSFEs and γ’ particle 
shape, Feng et al. [22] considered that solute segregation at planar 
would facilitate the formation of APB/SISF/APB ribbons in the CoNi- 
based system. Based on our calculations shown in Fig. 4 , however, 
the inconspicuous variation of GSFEs of APB and SISF does not ex- 
plain the noticeable difference in the deformation models of the 
three systems. It is then believed that the coupling effect of APB 
and SISF energies may dominate the mechanism. The ratio of pla- 
nar defect energy, E p = γAPB / γSISF , was proposed here to explore 
the synergistic inﬂuence of APB and SISF on the plastic deforma- 
tion model. Fig. 7 presents the E p value for the three systems. It 
is unexpectedly found that the change of E p value with Ni/Co con- 
tents is negligible except few ones near either Co 3 Al or Ni 3 (Al, W). 
For instance, if focusing on the typical Co 3 (Al, W), (Co 0.6 , Ni 0.4 ) 3 (Al, 
W), and Ni 3 Al, one can ﬁnd that they show almost the same E p of 
2.03, 2.09, and 2.29, respectively. 
As aforementioned in Fig. 5 , besides the stable GSFEs, we con- 
cluded that the unstable fault energies are of equal importance 
for determining the formation of stacking fault in the plastic- 
ity. By considering the inﬂuence of GSFEs barrier, and thus the 
ratio of effective planar defect energy ( /Delta1E p ) was deﬁned as: 
/Delta1E p = /Delta1γAPB / /Delta1γSISF . The result is plotted in Fig. 7 as well. Sur- 
prisingly, the variation trend of /Delta1E p as composition change is dras- 
tic. The Co 3 (Al, W) shows a low /Delta1E p value of 4.54. Increasing Ni 
contents rapidly raises the /Delta1E p value, resulting in a large /Delta1E p 
value of 6.61 for the Co30Ni phase. For the Ni 3 Al, its /Delta1E p value 
reaches up to 10.28. Combined with the results in Fig. 5 , we ﬁnd 
that despite both fault energy barriers of APB and SISF fall, the in- 
crease in /Delta1E p value means the descending range of energy bar- 
rier of SISF is larger than that of APB. In other words, compared to 
the Co 3 (Al, W), the quite high /Delta1E p for Ni 3 Al implies that forming 
SISF by gliding of a 1/3 < 112 > leading partial dislocation across γ’ 
precipitates consumes a very small amount of shear stress to con- 
quer the defect energy barrier. And the adequately residual shear 
stress is applied to guarantee the smooth transformation from SISF 
to APB by merging a second 1/6 < 112 > trailing partial dislocation. 
The fair low /Delta1E p value for Co 3 (Al, W) means that the resolve shear 
stress is consumed to overcome the exhaustingly high energy bar- 
rier of SISF, and there is no enough residual stress to activate the 
APB-SISF transformation due to the even higher energy barrier of 
APB. Alloying Ni raises the /Delta1E p value of Co 3 (Al, W) to a moder- 
ate level by decreasing both /Delta1γAPB and /Delta1γSISF , which not only 
makes sure the formation of SISF but also guarantees enough resid- 
ual stress to overcome the energy barrier of APB to partially form 
the fault defect. These results well capture the phenomena in the 
previous experimental observations [ 2 , 7 , 9 ]. 
On the other hand, it is indicated in Fig. 5 that higher Ni in 
the Co-based system is beneﬁcial to the formation of APB by fa- 
cilitating the transformation from SISF to APB in the γ’ precipi- 
tates. Except the end-on Co 3 Al, despite the increase in /Delta1E p value 
of phases with increasing W concentration, it is predicted that the 
SISF is still the main deformation model of γ’ in the Co-based sys- 
tem with the relatively low /Delta1E p . For the Ni-based system, when y 
< 0.3 alloying W remarkably raises the /Delta1E p value and maintains 
the deformation model of γ’ as APB defect. In contrast, the addi- 
tion of high-concentration W sharply reduces the /Delta1E p value to a 
low level. Therefore, the SISF defect could be expected to be acti- 
vated in the γ’ of Ni-based system with high W contents.

5. Conclusions 
The disclosed atomistic model of plasticity is presented based 
on the generalized stacking fault energies obtained by ﬁrst princi- 
ples to understand the effect of Ni and Al/W ratio on the creep- 
induced shearing mechanism of γ’ precipitates in the CoNi-based 
superalloys. The reasonable agreement with the available measure- 
ments implies the reliability of the present work. Based on the re- 
sults, we have well captured several observed phenomena and suc- 
cessfully explained the relatively underlying mechanism. The main 
conclusions can be summarized as follows: 
(1) The unstable and stable GSFEs of CISF, CESF, APB, and SISF of 
Co-based superalloys change from enhancement at ﬁrst to sup- 
pression as the increase of Ni contents. The high-concentration 
addition of Ni would deteriorate the strength of Co-based alloys 
by signiﬁcantly reducing the GSF barriers of APB, CESF, and SISF 
to be convenient for the partial dislocation to glide through the 
γ/ γ’ interfaces during the shearing process. However, appropri- 
ately dilute Ni could enhance the strength by increasing glide 
resistance of 1/6 < 1 ̅1 ̅2 > CISF as few experiments observed. In 
addition, alloying Ni remarkably weakens the twinning ability 
of Co-based superalloys. 
(2) Alloying W in Co-based alloys linearly raises the entire GSFE 
surface and the GSF barriers of γ’ to stabilize the binary Co 3 Al 
and encounter severer resistance of shearing deformation. How- 
ever, alloying W in Ni-based alloys enhances the GSFE surface 
and GSF barriers at the beginning but then quickly declines 
them as W exceeds 7.5 at.%. Excessive W even deteriorates the 
stability of γ’ phase and signiﬁcantly reduces the strength of 
Ni-based alloys. Increasing W weakens the twinning ability of 
Co-based and Ni-based alloys. 
(3) The /Delta1E p value governs the deformation mechanism of super- 
alloys. The high /Delta1E p for Ni-based alloys guarantees the oc- 
currence of transformation from SISF to APB by costing quite 
low shear stress due to the small GSF barrier. The low /Delta1E p 
for Co-based superalloys suggests that the resolve shear stress 
is consumed to overcome the exhaustingly high energy barrier 
to form SISF, and no enough residual stress can be applied to 
activate the APB-SISF transformation by conquering the even 
higher energy barrier of APB. Alloying Ni raises the /Delta1E p to be 
a moderate level, which makes sure the formation of SISF but 
also ensures enough residual stress to overcome the energy bar- 
rier of APB to partially forming the fault defect in CoNi-based 
superalloys. 
(4) The decisive role of grain orientation in the operation of plastic 
deformation is unambiguously demonstrated. In the Ni-based 
system, two primary defects of CESF and SISF can be activated 
in the speciﬁc orientation angle at low temperatures. The ad- 
dition of W would alter the defect activated angles and change 
the main defect conﬁgurations from CESF/SISF to CESF/APB/SISF 
of Ni-based system. In contrast, there are three primary defects 
of CESF, APB, and SISF that can be activated in the Co-based 
system at low temperatures. The addition of Ni maintains the 
defect conﬁgurations but alters the activated angles in the Co- 
based system. The change of W contents in the Co-based sys- 
tem would result in the occurrence of three different defect 
conﬁgurations under speciﬁc orientation angles.
