Fabrication and Mechanical Properties of In Situ
Synthesized Ti 2AlN/TiAl Composite
Daqun Wang, Dongli Sun, Xiuli Han, Qing Wang, Ningbo Zhang, and Feixing Xu
(Submitted November 7, 2017; in revised form March 20, 2018)
TiAl matrix composites reinforced with Ti 2AlN were fabricated by in situ reactive method of powder
metallurgy. Microstructures, bending properties at room temperature and compressive properties at ele-
vated temperatures were investigated in contrast with those of TiAl alloy. The results reveal that the
microstructure of TiAl matrix is signiﬁcantly reﬁned due to the synthesis of Ti 2AlN. 15 vol.%Ti 2AlN/TiAl
exhibits excellent bending properties at room temperature, with bending strength of 753 MPa and fracture
strain of 0.74%, which are 24.3 and 19.7% higher than those of TiAl alloy. The compressive strength of
Ti2AlN/TiAl is markedly improved in comparison to that of TiAl alloy at elevated temperatures. Noticeably,
the compressive strength of 15 and 35 vol.%Ti 2AlN/TiAl increases by 67.4 and 58.7% at 1000 /C176C,
respectively. The remarkable enhancement of mechanical performance is mainly due to the noteworthy
reﬁnement of TiAl matrix and the efﬁcient obstruction of Ti 2AlN particles to crack propagation, as well as
the effective load-carrying role of Ti 2AlN with high elastic modulus.
Keywords bending property, compressive property, metallic
matrix composites, microstructure, powder metallurgy
1. Introduction
In recent years, taking into account the weight loss of high-
temperature structural materials, TiAl alloys have received
signiﬁcant attention as a potential candidate for applications in
aerospace and automotive industry due to their remarkable
properties, such as low density, high speciﬁc strength, high
melting point, excellent oxidation and creep resistance (Ref 1-
6). Nevertheless, it is well recognized that the poor ductility at
room temperature and insufﬁcient strength at elevated temper-
ature restrict their potential applications (Ref 7, 8). Over the
past decades, considerable efforts have been put into the
enhancement of their comprehensive mechanical properties
through heat treatment (Ref 9, 10), alloying (Ref 11, 12) and
composite technology (Ref 13-27).
Among these methods, composite technology is an effective
way which combines the properties of matrix and reinforced
phase, and a series of TiAl matrix composites have been
fabricated successfully. According to the reports on the
properties of TiAl matrix composites, the strength of the
externally formed composites fabricated by adding ﬁbers,
particles or whiskers into TiAl matrix is signiﬁcantly enhanced.
However, the interfacial problems, such as brittle layer and
wettability, limit the development of such composites (Ref 13).
Additionally, it is worth noting that less interfacial problems
exist in the endogenous composites which are prepared by the
in situ reaction to form the reinforced phases (Ref 14, 15, 28).
According to the investigations of in situ TiAl matrix
composites, ceramic phases, such as TiB 2 (Ref 16-18), Al 2O3
(Ref 19), Ti 2AlC (Ref 20-24) and Ti 2AlN (Ref 25-27), have
been regarded as thermochemically stable and compatible
reinforced phases for TiAl matrix. Particularly, Ti 2AlN and
Ti2AlC belong to a family of ternary layered compounds
abbreviated as M n+1AXn, where M represents an early transi-
tion metal, A represents the element of A-group (mainly IIIA
and IV A), and X represents nitrogen or carbon (Ref 29). MAX
phases are widely concerned due to the unique structure and
particular combination of both metallic and ceramic character-
istics, such as good machinability, high thermal conductivity,
high strength and modulus and good oxidation resistance (Ref
30-32). These excellent properties are derived from the
synergistic effect of the strong covalent bond of M-X and the
weak ionic bond of M-A (Ref 33, 34), suggesting MAX phases
display more advantages than those binary ceramics as
reinforced phases.
Recently, the researches on the fabrication process and
mechanical properties of M n+1AXn/TiAl composite have been
gradually developed. Ai et al. (Ref 20) prepared Ti 2AlC/TiAl
via reaction hot pressing process with Ti 3AlC2, Ti and Al
powder, reporting that the ﬂexural strength of 5 wt.%Ti 2AlC/
TiAl increased from 475 to 652 MPa in comparison to that of
TiAl alloy and the crack deﬂection was primarily responsible
for the observed toughening of the composite. Sun et al. (Ref
21) fabricated Ti 2AlC/TiAl composite through the method of
hot pressing sintering followed by isothermal forging, ﬁnding
that the elongation of the as-forged composite increased from
12.10 to 30.87%. Y ang et al. (Ref 22) synthesized Ti 2AlC/TiAl
composite by spark plasma sintering and reported that the
compressive yield strength of the sintered composite at 950 /C176C
reached 2058 MPa.
Remarkably, compared to Ti 2AlC, Ti 2AlN possesses more
excellent structural stability (Ref 35). Besides, the thermal
expansion coefﬁcient and density of Ti 2AlN (4.3 g/cm 3 and
8.8 9 10/C06 K/C01) are similar to those of TiAl alloy (4.0 g/cm 3
and 12 9 10/C06 K/C01) (Ref 3, 32), indicating that the combina-
tion of Ti 2AlN and TiAl matrix may avoid the generation of

and reported that 4 vol.%Ti 2AlN/TiAl showed excellent tensile

synthesis Ti2AlN/TiAl composite, ﬁnding that the compressive

composites with low volume fraction of the reinforced phase

composites has been paid less attention. Therefore, in this
paper, Ti

Ti2AlN were fabricated by the in situ method of vacuum hot
pressing sintering with Ti powder, Al powder and TiN powder.
The bending property at room temperature and the compressive
property at elevated temperature of Ti
2AlN/TiAl composites
were discussed. Moreover, the effects of Ti 2AlN on the
mechanical properties were also investigated in detail.
2. Material and Method
Ti2AlN/TiAl composites with 15 and 35 vol.%Ti 2AlN
(referred to TAN-15 and TAN-35 for brevity, respectively)
were fabricated by the in situ method of hot pressing sintering.
For the purpose of comparison, Ti-48Al alloy (referred to TAN-
0) was also prepared by the same process. Commercial powders
of Ti (99.5 purity, /C2425 lm), Al (99.7 purity, /C245 lm) and
TiN (99.7 purity, /C243 lm) were used as the raw materials. The
fabrication process was as follows. Firstly, the mass ratio of raw
materials was calculated according to the composition of the
material design and the result is shown in Table 1. Secondly,
the original powders were precisely weighted corresponding to
the mass ratio and mixed sufﬁciently for 10 h. Thirdly, the
mixed powders were compacted uniaxially in the graphite mold
with BN coated inside under 15 MPa for sintering. The in situ
reactive sintering was performed in a vacuum hot pressure
sintering furnace (ZYD-140-100L) at 1350 /C176C for 1.5 h under a
pressure of 20 MPa, with a heating rate of 10 /C176C/min.
The phase compositions of the as-sintered samples were
examined by x-ray diffraction (XRD, D/MAX-RB) with Cu K a
radiation. The scanning angle was from 20 /C176to 100 /C176, with a
scanning speed of 2 /C176/min. The microstructure observation and
composition analysis were performed by scanning electron
microscope (SEM, HITACHI S-570), transmission electron
microscopy (TEM, TALOS F200X) and energy-dispersive
spectroscope (EDS, OXFORD X-MAX 50), respectively.
Three-point bending tests at room temperature were conducted
through INSTRON-5569, and the specimens were 3 mm 9 4
mm 9 36 mm in size with a span of 30 mm. The crosshead
displacement rate of bending test was 0.5 mm/min. High-
temperature compressive tests of cylindrical specimens with a
size of U6m m 9 9 mm were carried out on GLEEBLE-1500D
thermal simulator at 800, 900, 1000 and 1100 /C176C with a strain
rate of 0.01 s
/C01. The fracture morphologies of as-sintered
samples under different performance tests were observed by
SEM (HITACHI S-570) and metallographic microscope
(OLYMPUS PME3), respectively.
3. Results and Discussion
3.1 Phase Composition and Microstructure of Ti 2AlN/TiAl
Composite
Figure 1 shows representative XRD patterns of the as-
sintered samples. It is evident from Fig. 1 that no diffraction
peak of Ti, Al or TiN exists in all the samples, suggesting that
the raw materials react completely through sintering process. In
addition, the XRD patterns of the composites indicate that there
are mainly Ti
2AlN phase besides c-TiAl and a2-Ti3Al phase,
while TAN-0 principally consists of c-TiAl and a2-Ti3Al phase,
implying that the reinforced phase has been synthesized during
the sintering process. Furthermore, the relative intensity of the
diffraction peak of Ti
2AlN phase increases gradually with the
increase in TiN content in original powders, which is in
accordance with the material design.

samples. Obviously, as illustrated in Fig. 2(a), TAN-0 displays
coarse lamellar structure composed of a2 and c phase with
uneven colony size. As observed from the microstructure of
TAN-15 shown in Fig. 2(b), particles with the average size of
approximately 5 lm uniformly scatter around the lamellar

3.2 Bending Properties of Ti 2AlN/TiAl Composite
Figure 4 shows the bending stress–strain curves and bend-
ing properties of the as-sintered samples at room temperature.
As observed from Fig. 4(a), no obvious plastic yielding occurs
during the bending process of all the as-sintered samples,
exhibiting the feature of brittle fracture. Besides, the bending
strength and fracture strain both increase ﬁrst and then reduce
along with the increased content of Ti
2AlN, and TAN-15
displays the highest bending strength of 753 MPa and fracture
strain of 0.74%, which are 24.3 and 19.7% higher than those of
TAN-0, suggesting that appropriate content of Ti
2AlN is
beneﬁcial to enhance the bending properties of TiAl matrix
composite. Furthermore, as evident in Fig. 4(b), the elastic
modulus increases monotonously with the incremental content
of Ti
2AlN, resulting from the higher elastic modulus of Ti 2AlN
(286GPa) than those of TiAl (160GPa) and Ti 3Al (145GPa)
(Ref 37, 38).
Based on the researches of TiAl matrix composite, the
mechanism of reinforced phase on enhancing the mechanical
properties can be divided into several aspects. The reﬁnement
of TiAl matrix shown in Fig. 2, attributed to the synthesis of
Ti
2AlN, is believed to be favorable to the improvement of the
strength and toughness of the composite (Ref 23, 39). Besides,
it is well known that the plastic deformation of material is
mainly achieved by the dislocation motion, such as slide,
climbing, multiplication and interaction of dislocation (Ref 21,
40). Ti 2AlN particle with higher hardness can effectively
impede the slide of dislocation, thereby leading to the
optimization of mechanical properties. Moreover, as reported

As a consequence, the fracture feature of TAN-0 is identiﬁed as

rough lamellar fracture steps are both noticeable on the fracture

3.3 High-Temperature Compressive Properties of Ti 2AlN/
TiAl Composite
Figure 7 shows the representative stress–strain curves of the
as-sintered samples under compressive deformation at a
temperature range from 800 to 1100 /C176C. As plotted in Fig. 7,
all the stress–strain curves present similar characteristics. In the
initial stage of deformation, the curve is approximated as a
straight line, suggesting that the true stress–strain is the
proportional relationship and elastic deformation occurs. After-
ward, the stress increases nonlinearly to the maximum, while
the rate of increment reduces gradually with the increase in the
strain, which illustrates the occurrence of work hardening. The
peak stress and the corresponding strain are deﬁned as the
compressive strength and the peak strain, respectively. Subse-
quently, the stress begins to fall down toward a stable state,
exhibiting signiﬁcant feature of ﬂow softening. According to
the investigations of compressive property at elevated temper-
atures, the work hardening primarily stems from the increased
quantity of dislocations, while the ﬂow softening is principally
caused by dynamic recovery and recrystallization (Ref 42).
Furthermore, in theory, the deformation behavior at high
temperature is the mutual result of work hardening and ﬂow
softening (Ref 43), and the ﬂow stress in steady state is
obtained as two factors stay in balance. Nevertheless, as
exhibited in Fig. 7(c), the ﬂow stress corresponding to elastic
limit is the maximum value at 1100 /C176C, indicating that work
hardening basically disappears at this temperature condition.
This phenomenon is probably attributed to the easy occurrence
of dislocation slip and climb at high temperature, which reduces
dislocation density, thus weakening the effect of work harden-
ing.
Figure 8 shows the compressive strength of the as-sintered
samples at different deformation temperatures. Obviously, the
compressive strength of the three samples presents a declining
trend as the deformation temperature increases from 800 to
1100 /C176C. This is potentially because the strength of grain
boundary decreases along with the increase in deformation
temperature, and the hindrance of grain boundary on deforma-

4. Conclusion
In current investigation, 15 vol.%Ti 2AlN/TiAl and
35 vol.%Ti 2AlN/TiAl composites have been successfully fab-
ricated by powder metallurgy with Ti, Al and TiN powder.
Three-point bending property at room temperature and com-
pressive property at elevated temperature of Ti
2AlN/TiAl
composite, as well as the effects of Ti 2AlN phase on these
mechanical performances, have been systematically discussed.
The main conclusions are as follows:
1. Ti 2AlN/TiAl composites exhibit a uniform and ﬁned-
grain structure. The morphology of Ti 2AlN changes from
granular state to blocky state as the volume fraction in-
creases from 15 to 35% and the microstructure of lamel-
lar TiAl matrix is reﬁned signiﬁcantly due to the
synthesis of Ti
2AlN.
2. 15 vol.%Ti 2AlN/TiAl displays the excellent bending
properties at room temperature, with bending strength of
753 MPa and fracture strain of 0.74%, which are 24.3
and 19.7% higher than those of TiAl alloy. The remark-
able enhancement of bending property is mainly due to
the efﬁcient obstruction of Ti
2AlN particles to crack
propagation and the noteworthy reﬁnement of TiAl ma-
trix.
3. The compressive strength of Ti
2AlN/TiAl is signiﬁcantly
improved in comparison to that of TiAl alloy at elevated
temperature, especially at 1000 and 1100 /C176C. For 15 and
35 vol.%Ti 2AlN/TiAl, the compressive strength is im-
proved by 67.4 and 58.7% at 1000 /C176C, as well as 33.5
and 51.8% at 1100 /C176C. The positive effect of reinforced
phase on compressive property is embodied in the effec-
tive load-carrying role of Ti 2AlN with high elastic modu-
lus and the reﬁnement of TiAl matrix.
