ABSTRACT
TiB whisker-reinforced Ti 2AlNb (TiBw/Ti 2AlNb) composites with a network
architecture were fabricated by the spark plasma sintering (SPS) technique at the
temperature ranging from 950 to 1250 /C176C with the pressure range of 15–50 MPa
for 5–30 min and followed by furnace cooling. The effects of SPS parameters and
the volume fraction of TiB
2 on the microstructure, densiﬁcation, phase compo-
sition and mechanical properties of the sintered composites were studied in
detail. The results revealed that the microstructure of TiBw/Ti
2AlNb composites
was related to the degree of in situ reaction, which was linked to sintering
temperature, time, pressure and the volume fraction of TiB
2. The TiBw/Ti 2-
AlNb composite exhibited the highest strength and plasticity at room temper-
ature with the sintering process of 1200 /C176C/15 min/50 MPa/2.5 vol. % TiB
2, and
its ultimate tensile strength was nearly 240 MPa higher than that of SPS sintered
Ti
2AlNb-based alloy. However, its high-temperature mechanical properties
were lower than those of SPS sintered Ti 2AlNb-based alloy, which is due to the
fact that the bonding strength between high-strength TiB whiskers and Ti 2AlNb
matrix was worse during hot deformation, the interface deboned before frac-
ture, and the strengthening effect of TiBw decreased. Compared with the tra-
ditional TiBw-titanium alloys system, this work systematically discussed the
inﬂuence of various parameters on the microstructure and mechanical proper-
ties of the composites under TiBw-Ti
2AlNb-based alloys system, which can
eventually enrich the application of network architecture in the ﬁeld of powder
metallurgy to improve the mechanical properties of materials and provide
guidance for the optimization of process parameters.

GRAPHICAL ABSTRACT
Introduction
Ti2AlNb-based alloys with orthorhombic structure
have high speciﬁc strength, excellent creep resistance,
oxidation resistance and fracture toughness and have
excellent application potential in aeroengine hot-end
components as high-temperature structural materials
[17, 28, 41, 45]. However, as long as the service tem-
perature exceeds 650 /C176C, the yield strength and creep
resistance of the Ti
2AlNb-based alloys decrease
sharply [ 15, 48]. Numerous studies have demon-
strated the feasibility of introducing reinforcements
into the Ti
2AlNb-based alloys to improve creep
resistance, tensile properties and fatigue properties
[35, 43, 44, 47]. TiB whiskers (TiBw) and/or TiC
particles (TiCp)-reinforced titanium matrix compos-
ites are considered the most promising composites
due to their low cost and isotropic properties [ 19]. Jia
et al. investigated the hybrid effect of TiBw and TiCp
in titanium matrix, indicating that TiBw had a dom-
inant contribution to the high strength of (TiCp ?
TiBw)/Ti composites in different strengthening fac-
tors [23]. Meanwhile, considering that TiBw has good
compatibility and similar thermal expansion coefﬁ-
cient with titanium alloys and Ti
2AlNb-based alloys
[8, 13, 37], it is necessary to carry out in-depth
research on TiBw/Ti 2AlNb-based composites to
provide theoretical guidance for the optimization of
preparation process.
Compared to strengthening treatment and hybrid
treatment of reinforced phases and selection of
forming processes, the distribution structure of rein-
forcement also plays an important role in improving
the mechanical properties of composites [ 4, 5]. Con-
ventional architecture at the expense of plasticity and
toughness seriously restricts the research and appli-
cation of titanium matrix composites [ 12, 18, 29, 36].
In recent years, Huang et al. proposed a novel net-
work architecture and applied it to TiBw/Ti6Al4V
composite by reaction hot pressing (HP) [ 18]. The
results showed that the strength and ductility were
signiﬁcantly improved in comparison with those of
the monolithic Ti6Al4V alloy. The novel network
architecture seeks the distribution of reinforcement
along the original particle boundary (OPB). Good
connectivity between matrix particulates can appro-
priately improve the plasticity. Moreover, adding a
proper amount of reinforced phases can overcome
the defect of grain boundary weakening of metal
materials at elevated temperature [ 20]. However, the

literature on the application of network architecture
in TiBw/Ti 2AlNb composites is still limited, and
systematic experimental studies are needed to ﬁll the
gap.
Powder metallurgy (P/M) technique can obtain
highly uniform ﬁne grain microstructure and avoid
large-scale composition segregations [ 24, 38, 39]. Jia
et al. prepared Ti-22Al-25Nb alloy by vacuum HP
sintering technique, which veriﬁed the applicability
of the PM process to the Ti
2AlNb-based alloy [ 22].
Emura et al. prepared Ti-22Al-27Nb alloy using hot
isostatic pressing (HIP) technology and investigated
the reﬁnement of prior B2 grains [ 7]. However, the
microstructure of Ti
2AlNb-based alloys solidiﬁed by
the sintering methods mentioned above was found to
be relatively coarse [ 31, 33]. Spark plasma sintering
(SPS) belongs to the ﬁeld-assisted sintering technique
(FAST), which can consolidate powders to high
densiﬁcation within a short time and gain ﬁne grain
microstructure based on the combination of plasma,
resistive heating and pressure [ 11, 14, 32]. Zhang
et al. prepared (La
2O3 ? TiB)/Ti2AlNb composites
by SPS technology [ 47]. The improvement of high-
temperature performance was realized, and the
inﬂuence of temperature parameters was studied.
Niu et al. carried out the preparation and
microstructure optimization of a Ti
2AlNb-based alloy
using the SPS technique and pointed out the superi-
ority of SPS in preparing ﬁne grain microstructures in
comparison with traditional powder sintering meth-
ods [ 31]. However, there are many difﬁculties in
preparing TiBw/Ti
2AlNb composites by SPS. On the
one hand, different from traditional titanium alloys,
the phase composition and microstructure evolution
of Ti
2AlNb-based alloys are extremely complex and
sensitive to process parameters [ 1, 9, 10, 19, 42]. On
the other hand, the formation of TiB whiskers is
highly dependent on the in situ synthesis technology.
The control of the in situ synthesis process parame-
ters has a crucial impact on the interfacial bonding
strength, which determines the load transfer, fracture
mode and the comprehensive properties of compos-
ites [6, 48].
Based on the above discussion, it is possible that
the mechanical strength of Ti
2AlNb-based alloys can
be improved in the form of the TiBw/Ti 2AlNb com-
posites with a network conﬁguration under appro-
priate process parameters. However, there is a lack of
systematic exploration of the SPS parameters and
TiB
2 volume fraction for fabricating TiBw/Ti 2AlNb
composites with network architecture. Consequently,
the microstructure evolution of the matrix and the
OPB of the TiBw/Ti
2AlNb composites under differ-
ent process parameters were ﬁrst investigated sys-
tematically. Furthermore, the correlation of
structure–mechanical properties was established on
the basis test results of porosity, tensile properties
and fracture surfaces. The results of this study would
be signiﬁcant for the SPS sintering technology and
preparation process of TiBw/Ti
2AlNb composites.
Materials and experimental procedure
Materials
The spherical Ti 2AlNb pre-alloyed powders fabri-
cated by plasma rotating electrode process (PREP)
were provided by Xi’an SAILONG Metal Materials
Co., Ltd. As shown in Fig. 1a and b, most of the Ti
2-
AlNb pre-alloyed powders exhibit a dendritic mor-
phology, which were formed during the preparation
of powders [ 34]. Figure 1c and d shows the particle
size distribution (with an average particle size of
107 lm) and mass/atom ratio of elements of the
Ti
2AlNb pre-alloyed powders, respectively. As
shown in Fig. 1e, the particulate size of the irregular
TiB2 powders prepared by mechanical crushing ran-
ges from 8 to 12 lm.
The difference between the manufacturing process
of composites with a network structure and tradi-
tional homogeneous composites lies in powder size
and powder mixing methods [ 21]. There are two
main steps in the preparation of network strength-
ened composites. First, the large Ti
2AlNb pre-alloyed
powders and small TiB2 powders are low-energy ball
milled to adhere the ﬁne TiB 2 powders onto the
surface of Ti2AlNb matrix, as shown in Fig. 1f. Then,
the SPS or other powder sintering approaches are
used to solidify the mixed powders.
Spark plasma sintering
The mixed powders were poured into a high-strength
graphite mold with a wall thickness of 30 mm and an
inner diameter of 60 mm. The detailed mold assem-
bly diagram is shown in Fig. 1g. To eliminate the
inﬂuence of the sampling position, the mold was
placed in the central position of the graphite disk.

Microstructure and properties
characterization
The metallographic samples were polished with
sandpapers and placed into the digital display con-
stant temperature magnetic agitator of model 85–2 for
electrolytic polishing. The ratio of electrolyte was 6
vol. % perchloric acid ? 34 vol. % butanol ? 60 vol.
% methanol. The voltage of electrolytic polishing was
30 V, the current was between 0.6 and 0.7 A, and the
polishing time was appropriate for 8–10 s. Kroll’s
reagent (84 vol. % H
2O ? 4 vol. % HF ? 12 vol. %
HNO3) was used to etch the polished samples for
about 15 s. Optical microscope (OM) and scanning
electron microscope (SEM) were adopted for
microstructure observation. In the scanning angle
range of 20 to 80 degrees, the phase identiﬁcation was
preliminarily explored by X-ray diffraction (XRD)
with Cu K
a radiation. Energy disperse spectroscopy
(EDS) was used to characterize the atomic fraction of
phases. The distribution of elements in some com-
posites was analyzed by surface scanning technology.
Image-Pro Plus software was adopted to measure the
porosity of the composites. To ensure the accuracy of
the porosity datum, ten OM images of the same
magniﬁcation were selected for analysis under each
parameter combination.
Both 25 /C176C and 650 /C176C tensile tests were performed
on a UTM5504X electronic universal testing machine
with a crosshead speed of 1.2 mm/min. The samples

Results
Phase identiﬁcation
Figure 2 shows the XRD patterns of the sintered
composites under different parameters. On the
whole, TiBw/Ti
2AlNb composites prepared by SPS
are mainly composed of O phase and B2 phase. It is
worth noting that (221)
O crystal plane and (110) B2
crystal plane have obvious difference in intensity of
diffraction peak under different parameters. With the
increase in temperature, time and pressure, the
intensity of its diffraction peak is almost increasing.
However, the effect of TiB
2 volume fraction on the
intensity of its diffraction peak increases ﬁrst and
then decreases. It also approximately demonstrates
the overall variation trend of O phase content.
Furthermore, due to the relatively large scanning
speed and too small phase content, neither a
2 nor TiB
phase was detected by XRD.
Figure 3a exhibits the OM image of the SPS sin-
tered composite at 1250 /C176C/15 min/50 MPa/2.5 vol.
% TiB 2. It can be seen that the TiBw/Ti 2AlNb com-
posite is divided into two regions, namely the Ti 2-
AlNb matrix region and the TiBw-rich region.
Compared with the conventional homogeneous
architecture, the reinforcement of composites with
network architecture presents a non-uniform and
quasi-continuous distribution. The matrix with rela-
tively good toughness can prevent crack propagation
and signiﬁcantly improve the damage tolerance.
Figure 3b-e shows the SEM images of composites
with typical characteristics at different magniﬁca-
tions. The atomic percentage in the dot positions and
micro-regions was obtained by EDS, and the results
are listed in Table 2. The O phase with more than 25%
Nb atoms mainly exhibits equiaxed/globular (point
B) morphology and ﬁne acicular (region C)/coarse
lamellar (point E) morphology. In contrast, the O
phase with less than 25% Nb atoms presents an
irregular aggregation state (point F).

Porosity and tensile properties
The relationship between the porosity of the com-
posites and different variables is shown in Fig. 12.
For T-950 and T-1000, unhealed holes in the OPB
region are clearly perceived due to insufﬁcient plastic
deformation [ 24]. With the temperature further
reaching 1050 /C176C, although voids are still visible, the
number of pores is greatly reduced. On the one hand,
higher temperature enhances the degree of plastic
deformation between powders. On the other hand,
the more sufﬁcient exothermic reaction between TiB
2
and Ti further softens the matrix and dramatically
reduces the deformation resistance. When the tem-
perature is greater than or equal to 1150 /C176C, the
porosity is close to 0%. When the temperature is kept
constant, the effect of holding time is relatively small,
and there are nearly no large-sized holes in the OPB
area. When the time reaches 15 min, the composite is
almost completely dense. Compared with the inﬂu-
ence of the time variable on porosity, the pressure of
30 MPa can also ensure the low porosity of compos-
ites. In addition, the porosity of composites with 0
vol. % and 2.5 vol. % TiB
2 is close to zero. However,
when the addition of TiB 2 is increased to 5 vol. % or
higher, the increased deformation resistance of the
OPB region leads to the rise of porosity again.

Discussion
As depicted in Figs. 12 and 13, the highest fracture
strength of the composites with low densiﬁcation is
only about 500 MPa. This is primarily due to the
abundant unhealed holes between powder particu-
lates. Additionally, Zhang et al. [ 16, 47] discussed the
relationship between tensile properties and thickness
of O phase under different temperatures by using the
extended Hall-Page formula based on Ti
2AlNb-based
alloys. The results revealed that the increase in tensile
strength caused by the thickness of internal O phase
was only 5.9 MPa. Therefore, it is more important to
study the relationship between the microstructure of
the OPB area and tensile properties.
The correlation between room temperature tensile
strength and microstructure of the OPB region was
discussed based on the results of microstructural
evolution, porosity and fracture surface. Figure 17
shows the schematic illustration of the microstruc-
tural evolution of SPS sintered TiBw/Ti
2AlNb com-
posites and Ti 2AlNb-based alloy. The red, blue,
purple and green arrows represent the increasing
trend of temperature, time, pressure and the vol-
ume fraction of TiB
2, respectively. In the ﬁrst sinter-
ing stage, the room temperature fracture strength of
composites under relatively low temperatures is
inferior (300–350 MPa) due to insufﬁcient densiﬁca-
tion and extremely low reaction at the interfaces. As

efﬁciently strengthening of TiB whiskers before the
matrix is torn. On the contrary, the existence of pull-

to play a sufﬁcient role in grain boundary strength-
ening. The interface debonding has already occurred
before fracturing, which is considered as ‘inefﬁciently
