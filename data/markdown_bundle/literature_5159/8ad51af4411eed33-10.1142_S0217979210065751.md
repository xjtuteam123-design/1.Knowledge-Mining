nearly the same static strength but different preci pitated particles, i.e. a peak aged condition (720 ℃ -
10h) and a double aged one (720 ℃ -8h, 620 ℃ -8h), in order to investigate the effect of precipi tated

suppressed by the carbide particles precipitated in a grain. 
Keywords : Fatigue; elevated temperature; rotating bending; alloy 718; aging condition. 
1. Introduction 
Ni-base super alloys are major materials for machin es and structures used under sever 
service conditions like a gas turbine, because the alloys have excellent properties on 
strengths in static and fatigue at high temperature and in corrosive environment. 
 
Therefore there are many studies on creep and fatig ue properties at high temperature 1-3. 
The alloys are hardened by the precipitation of int ermetallic compounds such as Ni 3Nb 
and Ni 3Al. That is, they are strengthened by particles pre cipitated not only at a grain

2. Material and Experimental Procedure 
The material used was a Ni-base superalloy Inconel 718 with a chemical composition (in 
wt. %) of 0.02C, 0.11Si, 0.12Mn, 0.009P, 0.001S, 18 .67Cr, 3.09Mo, 0.09Co, 0.01Cu, 
0.66Al, 0.90Ti, 18.67Fe, 0.004B, 5.12 Nb and Ta, an d balance Ni. The material was 
solution treated at 982 
℃ for 1h then water quenched. After solution treatme nt, the 
material was heat treated under two different aging conditions. That is, one is the typical 
aging condition which was aged at 720 ℃ for 8 h, then furnace cooled to 621 ℃ and aged 
at 621 ℃ for 8 h followed by air-cooling, a double-aged con dition, and another one is a 
peak-aged condition which was aged at 720 ℃ for 10h followed by air-cooling. 
The mean grain size of the materials was about 18 µm. Figure 1 shows precipitated 
particles of the double aged material observed by E DX. In both materials, white 
precipitated particles were observed at grain bound aries, and they were identified as 
Ni 3Nb. Moreover, MC-type carbide was also confirmed in grain of the double aged 
material (Fig.2). The carbide was not confirmed in the peak aged material. This is a 
significant difference in both materials. Table 1 s hows the mechanical properties. Static 
strengths of both materials are nearly the same at room temperature. However, the one of 
the double aged material is higher than that of the peak aged material at 500 ℃ .

3. Experimental Results and Discussion 
Figure 4 shows S-N curves for both materials at room temperature and 500 
℃ . In the 
figure, slash marks / mean that fracture occurred f rom an internal of specimen. Although 
most of fracture occurred from specimen surface, in ternal fractures occurred in the 
double aged material at 500 ℃ in long life region. Therefore the shape of S-N curve 
shows double staged one. Fatigue strength is higher in the double aged material than in 
the peak aged one at both temperatures, especially at 500 ℃ . Moreover in both materials, 
fatigue strength at 500 ℃ is higher than that at room temperature. That is, fatigue strength 
at 500 ℃ is very high in taking the softening at high temperature into account. 
Figure 5 shows crack growth curves at the same stre ss level at each temperature, as 
examples. At both temperatures, marked retardation of growth of a crack smaller than a 
few grain sizes is observed in the double aged mate rial, though a crack initiated at the 
early stage in the double aged material in comparis on with the peak aged one. This 
tendency is marked at 500 ℃ . As seen from the figure, most of fatigue life is occupied by 
the growth life of a crack smaller than ~1mm, espec ially the retardation period of a crack 
in the double aged material at 500 ℃ domains the fatigue life. Therefore, the main reasons 
for the differences in fatigue strength in both mat erials and at both temperatures can be 
explained by the difference in the growth behavior of a small crack. On the other hand, 
the relation between the logarithms of a crack leng th and the number of cycle can be 
approximated by a straight line at the growth proce ss of a crack over a few grain sizes. 
That is, the crack growth rate is proportional to the crack length.

From the results mentioned above, it is obvious tha t fatigue strength of the double 
aged material is higher than that of the peak aged material and it is marked at 500 ℃ in 
spite of decrease in the resistance to crack growth due to softening (Fig.7). This is mainly 
caused by their difference in growth resistances of a smaller crack of the materials. That 
is, a crack initiated in grain and propagated as a transgranular crack in both materials and 
at both temperatures. In the double aged material, many carbide particles precipitated in 
grain. A large carbide became a stress raiser and i nduced a crack initiation as shown in 
Figs. 5 and 8. On the other hand, small carbides pr ecipitated in grain contributed as 
obstacles to movement of dislocation, thus resistan ce to crack growth increased. 
Moreover, the reason for the larger blocking effect of carbide particles at 500 ℃ than at 
room temperature can be explained as follows. That is, the retardation of a crack growth 
with a few grain sizes was yielded by oxide induced crack closure effect 6 at 500 ℃ as 
shown in Fig. 5 (b) in both materials. This tendenc y was marked in the double aged 
material having two effects of the oxide induced cr ack closure effect in addition to the 
blocking effect due to carbide particles which was stable even at high temperature. 
Consequently, the retardation of a crack growth in the double aged material was larger 
than that in the peak aged one at 500 ℃ . 
4. Conclusion 
Fatigue strength was higher in the double aged mate rial than in the peak aged one at both 
temperatures. The main reason for high fatigue stre ngth of the double aged material was 
the retardation of a propagation of a small crack w ith a few grain sizes. Fatigue strength 
at 500 
℃ was higher than that at room temperature in both m aterials. At 500 ℃ , the 
propagation of a crack smaller than a few grain siz es was suppressed by oxide induced 
crack closure effect, though the growth resistance of a longer crack was decreased by 
softening. That is, the main reason for high fatigu e strength at elevated temperature was 
the suppression of propagation of a small crack.
