abstract
The ability of materials to display extensive plastic deformation at elevated temperature before perma-
nent deformation without necking is known as super plasticity. The optimization of superplastic forming
can be evaluated by application of gas pressure at elevated temperatures using hot superplastic forming
set up. The diffusion bonding process involves keeping together the required titanium alloy sheets or tita-
nium alloy with any other metal with the help of load at elevated temperatures and in an inert environ-
ment preferably in vacuum for the bonding process to complete. This method is used to eliminate many
bonding defects such as distortions and cracking which can occur in conventional joining methods such
as welding and brazing or riveting. The resulting micro structure at the interface can be evaluated using
scanning electron microscope (SEM) photography and also the bond strength can be determined using
micro hardness tester. Titanium based alloys are super- plastic in nature at elevated temperatures, and
they also display exceptional mechanical properties such as a great strength to weight ratio, good fatigue
life good corrosion resistance, high torsional rigidity and excellent bio compatibility. The components
bonded using both super plasticity and diffusion bonding possesses high tensile strength which is makes
it reliable for aerospace applications.

1. Introduction
Titanium (Ti) alloys display outstanding mechanical properties
such as a great strength to weight ratio, good fatigue life
unmatched corrosion resistance and exceptional bio compatibility
[1]. Over the last few decades, engineers, designers and scientists
have exploited Titanium’s natural capacity to be super plastically
formed and diffusion bonded at higher temperatures and low
strain rates to manufacture components and structures of optimal
speciﬁc strength and stiffness, at a reasonable cost. Superplastic
forming (SPF) is a unconventional manufacturing method for fabri-
cating very complex thin-sheet machineries [3]. A single SPF man-
ufacturing process can yield complex structural components
without the need for welding or brazing or riveting parts together
[4]. Titanium based alloys are a kind of natural superplastic mate-
rial. Here, ‘natural’ means that several commercial titanium alloys
possess unchanging equiaxed ﬁne grain micro structure and super
plasticity [5]. Numerous sandwich structured products are manu-
factured by this process with ﬁne grain structure of titanium
alloys; usually the Ti-6AI-4 V alloy [6]. Along with the superplastic
nature of titanium alloy there is also another huge manufacturing
capability, i.e. diffusion bonding. Typical uses include parts formed
and bonded into hollow sections with two or more titanium sheets
materials resulting in complex topological structures and sheets
formed with bonded details which are pre placed, replacing multi-
ple piece assemblies and machined parts [7]. Diffusion bonding can
be easily obtained due the presence of the ﬁne grain concentration.
Speciﬁc areas required to be bonded is frequently combined with
superplastic forming to manufacture various topological structures
with high torsional rigidity and high strength to weight ratio for
applications in aerospace [8]. The diffusion bonding process
involves keeping together the required titanium alloy sheets
together at elevated temperatures, preferably in vacuum for the
bonding process to complete.

3.3. Tensile test results
Equipment’s used to conduct the several tests were Computer
controlled, furnace attached UTM with load and strain rate control.
Titanium (OT4-1) is used to carry out the test. The machine which
was used to conduct the experiment is an advanced tension
machine which has a facility of heating the specimens and also
loading the specimens gradually which is done by the microchip
based controllers under different conditions of temperature and
the strain rate. The specimens used for testing usually thin whose
dimensions are made as per the ASME speciﬁcations. The test
results are tabulated and the graphs are plotted for showing the
tensile strength and other parameters. When the load is being
applied for the diffusion bonded tensile test specimen, it was
observed that there was a dislocation of the bonded area due to
the super elasticity of the material. The percentage elongation of
these tensile specimen at the gauge length varies because of the
different bonding strength at the interface of the material and
superplastic behaviour of the OT41 material under the conditions
of different strain rates and operating temperature. The super plas-
ticity of this material and its percentage elongation can be seen at
the elevated temperatures below the creep temperature at con-
stant strain rates. The Microstructural analysis indicates that plas-
tic ﬂow of the material is maximum at the constant strain rates and
at elevated temperatures.
