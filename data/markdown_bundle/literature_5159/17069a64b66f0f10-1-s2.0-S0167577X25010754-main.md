ABSTRACT
A dual-microalloying strategy is proposed to overcome the strength-ductility trade-off in laser-directed energy 
deposited (LDED) Ti-6Al-4V alloys. The Cu-Nb co-modified alloy, fabricated via LDED, achieves simultaneous 
α -lath refinement and reticulated structure due to intergranular Ti
2
Cu precipitation along β grain boundaries and 
the solute segregation effect. This unique microstructure configuration enables notable tensile properties (ulti -
mate tensile strength (UTS): 1182.3 MPa, elongation: 8.6 %), demonstrating an exceptional strength-ductility 
synergy with 21.2 % and 17.8 % enhancements, respectively. The dual-phase strengthening mechanism origi -
nating from Cu-induced intermetallic precipitation and Nb-mediated β -phase stabilization establishes a new 
paradigm for additive manufacturing (AM) of high-performance titanium alloys through compositionally- 
tailored, in-situ precipitation engineering.
1. Introduction
Titanium alloys, particularly Ti-6Al-4V, are widely used in aerospace 
[ 1 ] and industrial [ 2 ] applications due to their excellent strength-to- 
weight ratio. However, further improvements in mechanical proper -
ties, particularly strength and ductility, are necessary to meet the 
increasing demands of advanced engineering applications [ 3 ]. Alloying 
is a common strategy to tailor the microstructure and optimize me -
chanical performance [ 4 ]. Copper (Cu) and niobium (Nb) are two 
promising alloying elements that can significantly influence the micro -
structure and properties of Ti-6Al-4V. Li et al. demonstrated that 
introducing 1.0 wt% Cu into Ti-6Al-4V enhanced UTS by 30.9 %, while 
concurrently reducing ductility by 34.4 %. With the increase in Cu 
content, the strength of Ti-6Al-4V alloy gradually increases, whereas its 
ductility shows a pronounced decline [ 5 ]. Additionally, Sui et al. 
demonstrated that Nb alloying effectively enhances β -phase stabilization 
in Ti-6Al-4V, which subsequently improves ductility through β -phase 
mediated plastic deformation mechanisms [ 6 ]. However, owing to the 
high melting point of Nb (2468 ℃ ), excessive addition tends to induce 
incomplete fusion defects, thereby deteriorating the mechanical 
properties of titanium alloys. Therefore, the dual-microalloying of Ti- 
6Al-4V with Cu and Nb is expected to synergistically enhance both 
strength and ductility.
The study aims to elucidate the individual and synergistic effects of 
Cu and Nb additions on phase composition, grain morphology, and 
mechanical performance. The findings provide insights into the poten -
tial of Cu and Nb additions for optimizing Ti-6Al-4V, thereby resolving 
the characteristic strength-ductility paradox in AM-processed titanium 
alloys.
2. Experimental procedures
Four distinct Ti-6Al-4V variants were fabricated via LDED, including 
unmodified Ti-6Al-4V, 0.2 wt% Nb-modified, 1.0 wt% Cu-modified, and 
dual-modified (1.0 wt% Cu + 0.2 wt% Nb) systems. The deposited 
material consists of Ti-6Al-4V spherical powder (particle size range: 
45 – 105 μ m), pure Cu spherical powder (average particle size: 1 μ m), and 
pure Nb spherical powder (average particle size: 1 μ m). A high-energy 
ball mill (QM-3SP2) was used to achieve uniform mixing of Ti-6Al-4V 
with Cu, Nb, and Cu-Nb dual addition, as shown in Fig. 1 (a), with a

3. Results and Discussion
Fig. 1 (e) shows that the pure Ti-6Al-4V alloy primarily consists of the 
α phase with a needle-like martensitic structure. The incorporation of Nb 
into Ti-6Al-4V results in a higher density of nanoscale precipitates 
compared to the pure Ti-6Al-4V alloy as shown in Fig. 1 (f). These pre -
cipitates form a synergistic strengthening structure with the martensitic 
phase, thereby enhancing the material ’ s ductility [ 7 ]. Fig. 1 (g) shows 
that Ti-6Al-4V with Cu forms a spotted microstructure, which may be 
related to the formation of Ti-Cu intermetallic compounds, potentially 
enhancing strength while reducing ductility [ 8 ]. As illustrated in Fig. 1
(h), the Ti-6Al-4V alloy containing Cu and Nb retains a predominantly 
martensitic microstructure, characterized by a network-like texture 
surrounding the needle-like martensitic morphology. The EDS mapping 
indicates that the added elements are uniformly distributed within the 
material.

4. Conclusion
The Cu-Nb dual-microalloying synergistically improves both 
strength and ductility in Ti-6Al-4V alloy through integrated strength -
ening mechanisms involving α -lath refinement, dislocation density in -
crease, intergranular Ti
2
Cu precipitation, and network structure 
formation. The α -lath refinement (average width: 257 nm → 229 nm) 
and enhanced dislocation density (average KAM value: 0.38
◦
→ 0.45
◦
) 
collectively strengthen the matrix, leading to a 21.2 % improvement in 
UTS (975.1 MPa → 1182.3 MPa). Meanwhile, the increased β -phase 
content and formation of network structures contribute to a 17.8 % 
enhancement in elongation (7.3 % → 8.6 %). These synergistic effects 
effectively overcome the traditional strength-ductility trade-off.
