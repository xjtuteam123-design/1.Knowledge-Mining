Abstract
High-entropy alloys have shown great application prospects in the field of high-temperature brazing due to their excellent 
properties. However, due to their excessively high melting temperature and poor metallurgical compatibility, achieving 
high-temperature, highly reliable connections between ceramic matrix composites and refractory alloys still faces signifi -
cant challenges. This study proposes a semi-solid brazing strategy, which uses CoCrFeNiCu x high-entropy alloy filler to 
connect C/C-SiC composites with TZM (titanium-zirconium-molybdenum alloy). This filler exhibits a dual endothermic 
melting behavior, forming an adjustable semi-solid window, which not only ensures sufficient fluidity but also suppresses 
excessive interfacial reactions. During the brazing process, the Cu-rich liquid phase promotes the formation of the initial 
interface. On the alloy side, the face-centered cubic (FCC) phase replaces the Cu liquid phase, generating a continuous 
µ-Co7Mo6 layer. On the composite material side, the elements dissolved in the Cu-rich liquid phase react with the com -
posite material to form the M₇C₃ reaction layer, while Cu reacts with the SiC matrix to form the decomposition zone. As 
the temperature and holding time increase, the reaction intensity increases, and the M 7C3 reaction layer and decomposi -
tion zone thicken. The optimized joint (1180 °C, 15 min) achieved a high shear strength of 40.2 MPa and maintained its 
integrity at 800 °C, demonstrating an anomalous strengthening effect due to stress relaxation. The finite element simulation 
results indicate that joint fracture primarily occurs at the Cu(s, s)/M 7C3 boundary and is mainly influenced by the normal 
stress (S22), consistent with experimental observations. This study provides a mechanistic framework for the relationship 
between the evolution of semi-solid HEA phases, interface design, and strength retention, and offers new ideas for the 
development of connection technologies between ceramic matrix composites and refractory alloys.

To fully exploit their high-temperature capabilities while 
addressing poor machinability and brittleness, integration 
with metallic materials is a viable solution. In this case, 
TZM alloy has emerged as an ideal partner for bonding with 
C/C-SiC composites. It offers exceptional high-temperature 
strength, good oxidation resistance, and creep resistance. 
TZM alloy maintains strong mechanical properties at ele -
vated temperatures and is widely used in hot-end compo -
nents [6, 7]. Nevertheless, joining of C/C-SiC composites to 
TZM alloys still presents technical challenges. Traditional 
methods such as punching and bolting can facilitate joining. 
Yet, these methods often increase structural weight and may 
induce micro-cracks during the punching process, thereby 
compromising the composites’ mechanical properties [8, 9]. 
Consequently, brazing technology has become the predomi-
nant method for joining C/C-SiC composites with metallic 
materials [10–12]. It enables the formation of high-strength, 
damage-free joints through metallurgical bonding between 
the fillers and the materials.
Active metal brazing, as an effective method for joining 
composites to metals, has been widely used in aerospace 
and other high-temperature environments. By introducing 
elements with high chemical activity, such as Ti, Zr, Cr, V , 
etc., active brazing can significantly improve the wettabil -
ity and interface strength between filler and matrix material, 
thus achieving high-quality joints [13–15]. These active ele-
ments react with the matrix to form compounds or reaction 
layers, which effectively improve the metallurgical compat-
ibility between composites and metals. However, although 
active brazing can effectively address the problems of low-
temperature applications, the development of filler systems 
for high-temperature environments still faces numerous 
challenges, particularly in maintaining the strength, tough -
ness, and durability of brazed joints at elevated temperatures.
Currently, the most used filler systems comprise noble 
metal-based fillers, Ti-based fillers, Ni-based fillers, and 
Co-based fillers. Noble metal (AgCu, Au, Pd) based fill -
ers exhibit good wettability and low melting temperatures; 
however, they soften severely at high temperatures, which 
limits their application in long-term high-temperature envi-
ronments [16–18]. Ti-based (TiNi-Nb, TiNi-Co) fillers are 
widely used in the high-temperature brazing field due to 
their high melting point and excellent high-temperature per-
formance. However, the chemical reactivity between Ti and 
C/C-SiC composites is strong, and brittle compounds (such 
as TiC, Ti2Ni, Ti2Co, etc.) are easily formed, which affect 
the mechanical properties of the joint [ 19–21]. Ni and Co-
based fillers are common high-temperature fillers, which 
have certain advantages in high-temperature resistance, but 
their metallurgical compatibility with SiC is poor, and brit -
tle phases are easy to form in the brazing seam, which limits 
their application in the brazing of C/C-SiC composites [22–
24]. Therefore, although the filler systems mentioned above 
can be used for high-temperature brazing under certain con-
ditions, they generally have problems such as poor metal -
lurgical compatibility and brittle compound formation. It is 
urgent to develop new filler systems to meet more demand-
ing application requirements.
In recent years, high-entropy alloys, a novel type of multi-
element alloy system, have garnered extensive attention in 
academic circles due to their exceptional high-tempera -
ture stability, oxidation resistance, and robust mechanical 
properties [25, 26]. High-entropy alloys can maintain high 
mechanical properties and oxidation resistance in a high-
temperature environment through the diversity of elements 
and the increase of melting point, which makes them ideal 
candidate materials for high-temperature fillers. Notably, 
the CoCrFeNi series of high-entropy alloys, which exhibit 
excellent high strength and toughness, also display good 
high-temperature mechanical properties [27–29]. However, 
although single-phase CoCrFeNi high-entropy alloy and 
Nb0.74CoCrFeNi2.1 eutectic high-entropy alloy have higher 
melting points and stronger mechanical properties at high 
temperatures, their metallurgical compatibility with SiC and 
Mo alloys is poor, especially at high temperatures, where 
a large number of brittle phases (such as Cr 0.46Mo0.4Si0.14, 
(Fe, Cr)MoSi, Mo 3Si, etc.) are easy to form, thus affect -
ing the mechanical properties and stability of brazed joints 
[30–32]. It is worth noting that some current literature has 
reported that the use of CoCrFeNiCu high-entropy alloy fill-
ers can achieve good joints at lower temperatures [ 33, 34]. 
However, these studies have failed to explore the fundamen-
tal reasons why the filler system achieves brazing at low 
temperatures. Through further analysis of the existing lit -
erature, it is found that the CoCrFeNiCu high-entropy alloy 
filler exhibits two melting regions with a wide interval, indi-
cating semi-solid melting characteristics within a certain 
temperature range [ 35, 36]. It is preliminarily judged that 
this characteristic is closely related to the phase separation 
behavior between Cu(s, s) and FCC in CoCrFeNiCu high-
entropy alloy, and the two endothermic regions correspond 
to the melting process of the two phases. Furthermore, 
existing studies indicate that Cu-based fillers enable good 
bonding of SiC-related materials and exhibit good metal -
lurgical compatibility [37–39]. Based on the above analysis, 
a high-entropy alloy system based on CoCrFeNiCux has 
been developed. Relying on its phase separation behavior 
and semi-solid melting characteristics, it is expected to 
achieve high-quality bonding between C/C-SiC compos -
ites and TZM alloys at low temperatures, thereby solving 
the common problems of brittle phase formation and poor 
metallurgical compatibility in traditional high-temperature 
brazing systems.

In the semi-solid brazing process, the liquid phase con -
tent of the filler is a key factor in determining the connec -
tion quality, as it directly affects the fluidity of the filler, the 
interface connection quality, and the connection strength. 
To explore the influence of liquid phase content on brazed 
joints deeply, this study designed a series of high-entropy 
alloy fillers of CoCrFeNiCux (x = 1.0, 2.0, 3.0, 4.0) with dif-
ferent Cu contents, and systematically evaluated the effect 
of Cu content on the liquid phase ratio and joint quality. 
Based on this, the optimized composition was selected, 
and the evolution mechanisms of the interface microstruc -
ture and joint mechanical properties under various brazing 
process conditions (such as brazing temperature and hold -
ing time) were discussed. Finally, the formation mechanism 
of the interface was elaborated in detail.

the composite is characterized by carbon fibers interwoven

The CoCrFeNiCux (x = 1.0, 2.0, 3.0, 4.0) high-entropy alloy 
fillers were prepared using a DHL-400 high-vacuum arc melt-
ing and suction casting system equipped with electromagnetic 
stirring and suction casting (Fig. 1(e)). Raw material pellets 
were prepared according to the desired composition, and the 
mass of raw material per ingot was 120 g. To remove impuri-
ties and grease from the raw material surface, all raw material 
particles were washed three times in an ultrasonic cleaning 
solution for 5 min each time. During the melting process, 
after the vacuum degree reached 2.0 × 10− 3 Pa, high-purity 
argon (0.05 MPa) was introduced to form plasma by ioniza-
tion. First, the pure titanium ingot was repeatedly melted three 
times to eliminate the residual oxygen in the chamber. There-
after, the melting process of the high-entropy alloy filler was 
initiated. To ensure the uniformity of the alloy, all filler ingots 
were repeatedly melted under electromagnetic stirring for six 
times, while the flipping operation was performed simultane-
ously to effectively avoid uneven distribution of alloy com-
ponents. According to actual requirements, after melting, the 
filler was either directly removed or subjected to suction cast-
ing treatment to obtain button ingots or suction-cast rods.

the ternary alloy, and the chemical potential of elements in

form solid solutions or intermetallic compounds; there -

(Fig. 15(a)). In contrast, the interaction between Co and 
Mo is stronger, and many intermetallic compounds, such as 
Co2Mo3, Co7Mo6, Co3Mo, and Co9Mo2, can be formed, and 
the enthalpy of formation of the system is negative, indi -
cating that the formation of these compounds has a higher 
thermodynamic driving force (the relevant phase diagram is 
shown in Fig. S2). Further analysis shows that the µ-Co7Mo6 
phase has the lowest Gibbs free energy (-92.00 kJ/mol) 
among the above compounds, indicating that it is the most 
stable and the easiest to form (Figs. 15(b)(c)).

process, and this phenomenon intensifies gradually as the 
process parameters increase. The formation of the decom -

and Cu, resulting in the formation of Cu 3Si and graphite

phase lead to a significant decline in the properties of the 
decomposition zone, which in turn results in a deterioration 
of the shear strength of the joint.

4 Conclusions
CoCrFeNiCux (x = 1.0–4.0) high-entropy alloy fillers were 
developed for semi-solid brazing of C/C-SiC composites to 
TZM alloy. The influences of Cu content and process param-
eters on interfacial microstructure evolution, mechanical 
properties, and stress behavior were systematically elucidated 
through thermodynamic analysis and finite element modeling.
(1) CoCrFeNiCux alloys exhibited dual-phase (FCC + Cu(s, 
s)) microstructures with tunable semi-solid melting behav-
ior. The CoCrFeNiCu4.0 filler achieved superior wettabil-
ity and was selected for optimal joining performance.
(2) The typical interfacial structure of the optimized 
joint consists of: TZM / µ-Co 7Mo6 / (FCC + Cu(s, 
s)) / M 7C3 / C/C + decomposition zone (amorphous 
SiC + Cu3Si + graphite) / C/C-SiC. This multilayered 
configuration ensures metallurgical continuity but intro-
duces a weak region within the decomposition zone.
(3) Process parameters primarily influence reaction extent 
rather than phase type. Excessive parameters promote 
overgrowth of the M7C3 layer and decomposition zone, 
degrading joint strength. The maximum shear strength 
reached 40.2 MPa at 1180 °C / 15 min, increased anoma-
lously to 42.6 MPa and 53.1 MPa at 600 °C and 800 °C, 
respectively, and declined to 29.3 MPa at 1000 °C due 
to filler softening.
(4) The FCC phase diffuses preferentially toward the TZM 
side, forming the µ-Co 7Mo6 layer via chemical-poten -
tial-driven substitution. Cu infiltration into the SiC 
matrix induces decomposition to Cu 3Si and graphite, 
accompanied by volumetric shrinkage and void forma -
tion, leading to local weakening.
(5) Finite element analysis revealed stress concentration at 
the composite bottom and within the M7C3 layer. Resid-
ual stress relaxation between 600 and 800 °C accounts 
for high-temperature strengthening, while excessive 
softening of the Cu(s, s) phase above 1000 °C causes 
delamination and strength loss.
These findings establish a semi-solid brazing strategy using 
high-entropy alloy fillers to join ceramic matrix composites 
- refractory alloys, providing a new pathway for designing 
oxidation-resistant, high-temperature structural joints.
