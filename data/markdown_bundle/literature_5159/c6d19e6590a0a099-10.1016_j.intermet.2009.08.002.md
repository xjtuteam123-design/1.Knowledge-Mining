abstract
An eutectic multiphase alloy NiAl–28Cr–5.94Mo–0.05Hf–0.01Ho (at.%) was directionally solidiﬁed by
liquid metal cooling technique at withdrawal rates of 3, 8 and 10 mm min /C01 . The microstructure and
tensile properties of alloys at room temperature, 1253 K and 1373 K were investigated. With the increase
of withdrawal rate, the microstructure was reﬁned including the reﬁnement of eutectic cells and lamellae
between the NiAl and Cr(Mo) phases. At room temperature, the alloys fractured as cleavage mode. At
1253 K, yield behavior occurred which lead to higher of tensile strength and ductility compared to those
at room temperature, and the properties are independent of withdrawal rate. At 1373 K, tensile prop-
erties increase with the increase of withdrawal rate, and both the strength and elongation rate are lower
than those at 1253 K. The mechanisms responsible for these changes were discussed.

1. Introduction
NiAl intermetallic has been recognized as a promising candidate
for high temperature structural applications. It possesses some
attractive physical and chemical properties [1]. However, poor
damage tolerance at ambient temperatures and inadequate
strength at high temperatures have prevented its commercial
application [2]. In early 1970s, Cline found that directional solidi-
ﬁed in-situ NiAl–Cr eutectic alloy which introduced the refractory
metal phase as the toughening phase showed both improved
toughness and creep strength compared to single-phase NiAl [3].
Small additions of impurity element Mo will change the growth
direction from C10 0D to C111D for both phases, while changing the
microstructure from chromium rods to Cr(Mo) lamellar
morphology [4]. Some researches have shown that directional
solidiﬁed NiAl–Cr(Mo) eutectic alloy exhibits a promising room
temperature and high temperature properties [5]. Recently, Guo’s
group found that Hf-doped NiAl–Cr(Mo) eutectic alloy displays
better creep strength due to solid solution and precipitation
strengthening than Hf-free alloy [6], and rare earth elements can
improve both the mechanical properties and oxidation resistance
[7]. In the present work, a NiAl–Cr(Mo) eutectic alloy doped with
both Hf and rare earth element holmium (Ho) is fabricated by
directional solidiﬁcation in order to improve the mechanical
properties further. In addition, an advantage of the liquid metal Sn
cooling (LMC) system is that it can provide higher thermal gradi-
ents at the solid–liquid interface compared with the Bridgeman
technique [8]. The higher withdrawal rates which are usually used
in producing commercial superalloys are chosen in this work.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructural characterization
Typical transverse section eutectic microstructures of the DS3,
DS8 and DS10 samples are shown inFig. 1(a,c,e). The eutectic cells of
the two alloys are composed of three phases which are black NiAl
matrix, gray white lamellar Cr(Mo) phase and some white phase. The
white phase was identiﬁed by EPMA to be Hf solid solution, as shown
in Table 1. The Cr(Mo) eutectic lamellae in each cell are arranged in
a radial pattern normal to the cell boundary with the center of the
cell often composed of a few faceted rods. With TEM examination of
microstructures of DS10 alloy, as shown in Fig. 2 , very ﬁne Cr(Mo)
phase could be observed to precipitate in NiAl phase, or vice versa
which is similar to previous studies[6]. The preferential precipitation
of primary dendritic NiAl phase in the DS10 indicates that the
composition of the alloy falls out of the coupled zone under the more
rapid solidiﬁcation conditions. The amount of primary NiAl in DS10 is
about 2.3%.

As shown in Fig. 1(a) , several growth defects were also

to be responsible for adjusting the phase spacing to accommodate

these growth defects should be the major damage initiation sites
for the material under mechanical loading.
3.2. Tensile properties
The longitudinal tensile properties of the alloys tested at room
and elevated temperatures are listed in Table 2. At room tempera-
ture,
sb (tensile strength, which equals the fracture stress) of the
three alloys increase with the increase of withdrawal rate, but the
extent of variation is small. NiAl and Cr(Mo) phases in these alloys
are typically thought of as semibrittle materials prone to cleavage
fracture at room temperature, and both the two phases do not yield
during tension tests, and fail as cleavage fracture mode. As shown in
Fig. 3(a), the fractograph further conﬁrms that the alloys exhibited
a brittle failure behavior with a ﬂat fracture surface. Since the
cleavage stress of NiAl is lower than that of Cr(Mo), the cracks must
generate ﬁrst in NiAl phases during deformation, as shown in
Fig. 3(b) . The failure of the Cr(Mo) layer is caused by local over-
loading. The crack path trace is very straight on side fracture of
samples (see Fig. 3(b) ). Although the Cr(Mo) phase can provide
barriers during the process of cracks growth, fracture occurred
without any ductility.
Since NiAl/Cr(Mo) eutectic alloy exhibits ductile deformation
behavior at 1143 K [9], the alloys in our research show ductile
behavior during tensile process at 1253 K. As shown in Table 2,t h e
tensile properties at 1253 K are higher than those at room
temperature because yielding behavior appears at 1253 K and the
yield stress at 1253 K is higher than cleavage stress at room
temperature in those alloys. Both NiAl and Cr(Mo) phases fracture
in a ductile fashion. A noticeable feature is the presence of a large
amount of dimples (see Fig. 4(a)). From the Table 2,w ec a ns e et h a t
the tensile strength at 1253 K is independent of the withdrawal
rate. This is attributed to the competition of strengthening factors
and weakening factors. With the increase of withdrawal rate, the
reﬁnement of the microstructure results in the main strength-
ening. As the spacing between the lamellae decreases, the number
of lamellae must increase providing additional barriers to dislo-
cation motion [3]. The amount of solid solution elements is
increased (see Table 1 ), which lead to stronger solid solution
hardening and precipitation strengthening [10]. The weakening
factors include the increased cell boundaries and phase interfaces.
At 1253 K, the cell boundaries and phase interfaces are weak, as
shown in Fig. 4(b) . With the increasing of the withdrawal rate, the
total area of both cell boundary and phase interfaces increases
greatly. The weakening factor offsets the above strengthening
mechanism, thus makes the tensile properties almost the same
level.

mechanical behavior and microstructure (produced at different
withdrawal rate) at different temperature. This is the case in
present investigation, i.e., the tensile properties show the inde-
pendence and dependence of microstructures at 1253 K and
1373 K, respectively. The Cr(Mo) particles precipitated in NiAl

4. Summary
Eutectic multiphase alloy NiAl/Cr(Mo)–Hf–Ho ingots were
directionally solidiﬁed using the liquid metal cooling technique
with higher thermal gradient. With the increasing of withdrawal
rate, the microstructure was reﬁned including the reﬁnement of the
eutectic cells and the lamellae between the NiAl and Cr(Mo) phases.
At RT, the alloys fractured in the cleavage mode. For tensile tests at
