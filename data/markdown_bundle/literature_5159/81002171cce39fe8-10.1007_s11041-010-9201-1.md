ONE-STAGE PROCESS OF CHROMOALUMINIZING
OF GAS TURBINE BLADES BY THE METHOD OF CIRCULATION
V . N. Simonov,1 Yu. P. Shkretov,2 and M. V . Unchikova1
Translated from Metallovedenie i Termicheskaya Obrabotka Metallov, No. 10, pp. 43 – 46, October, 2009.
The conditions of simultaneous mass transfer of chromium and aluminum to the surface of high-temperature
alloy subjected to one-stage chromoaluminizing are determined. It is shown that the quality of such refractory
coatings can be improved. The methods preventing formation of oxide inclusions in the coatings are deter -
mined. Results of comparative high-temperature strength tests of specimens after circulation and powder
chromoaluminizing are presented.
Keywords: high-temperature alloys, chromoaluminizing, structure of coatings.
INTRODUCTION
The endurance of cooled running blades of gas turbine
engines (GTE) from nickel castable high-temperature alloys
is raised by depositing refractory multicomponent aluminum
coatings onto external and internal surfaces of the parts. Con-
siderable increase in the high-temperature strength and im -
provement of the physical and mechanical properties of
nickel aluminides is ensured by alloying them with chro -
mium. This explains the wide use of chromoaluminizing. It
has been shown that the required life of turbine blades under
conditions of oxidation and fatigue can be ensured by coat -
ings with a thickness of 25 – 40 /c109m bearing 15 – 20% Al and
4 – 10% Cr and having a two-phase /c98-NiAl – /c103/c162-Ni3Al struc-
ture. The coatings should not contain pores and brittle oxide
inclusions and should have a fine crystal structure.
Today such coatings are deposited by the powder method.
The process is conducted in a mixture of chromium and alu -
minum using ammonium chloride as an activator. However,
the powder method has some substantial disadvantages. For
example, the hygroscopic nature of the powder and the pre -
sence of moisture result in formation of aluminum oxide, the
particles of which grow into the coatings and cause its crack-
ing in operation. In addition, the interaction between chro -
mium and aluminum lowers the activity of the powder mix-
ture (especially after triple use), which lowers the stability of
the process. Another drawback of the powder method is the
use of manual labor.
The method of circulation is an advanced technology for
depositing protective coatings on external and internal sur-
faces of blades [1]. It is based on mass transfer of saturating
elements from the added alloy to the surface of the part due
to the drop of the partial pressures of the volatile halide com-
pounds. Today we are equipped with a two-stage method of
chromoaluminizing [2] with successive deposition of ele -
ments of the coating. A one-stage process developing under
conditions of joint mass transfer of aluminum and chromium
is more economical but its implementation requires optimi -
zation of the process factors of deposition of the coating.
The aim of the present work consisted in determining the
conditions of performance of single-stage chromoalumi -
nizing by the circulation method and optimizing the compo -
sition of the added alloy, which is the source of saturating
elements.
METHODS OF STUDY
Experimental deposition of chromoaluminized coatings
was performed in an UTsL-1 laboratory installation. The ob-
jects of study were cylindric specimens 10 mm in diameter
and 20 mm high produced from castable nickel refractory al-
loys ZhS6U, ZhS26U, and ZhS32.

The additions were specially melted chromium-alumi-
num alloys, the composition of which was chosen after ther-
modynamic computation of the content of halide compounds
of aluminum and chromium in the gas medium. It is known
that by varying the composition of the alloys and changing
the thermodynamic activity of the component chemical ele-
ments we can influence the equilibrium composition of the
gas medium and hence the intensity of mass transfer of the
elements of the coating to the surface of the part. The values
of the thermodynamic activity of components in chromium-
aluminum alloys have been determined in [3]. We chose
NiCl2 nickel chloride as an activator of the process [2]. The
computation was performed using the “Terra” software [4]
for the following proportions of chromium and aluminum in
the alloy: Al : Cr=1:1 ,2:3 ,3:7 ,a n d1:4 .
After charging the activator, the specimens, and the addi-
tion preliminarily broken into granules 10 – 20 mm in dia -
meter, the installation was evacuated to a residual pressure
no higher than 20 Pa. Then we performed chromoalumi -
nizing at 1000°C for 4 h. When the processes ended, the
specimens were cooled with the furnace to 200°C and with -
drawn from the installation. The chromoaluminized speci -
mens were subjected to thermovacuum treatment at 1210°C
for 1.5 h in order to raise the plasticity of the coating due to
decrease in the concentration of aluminum on the surface and
formation of a two-phase /c98-NiAl – /c103/c162-Ni3Al structure.
The structure of the chromoaluminized coatings was
studied by metallographic, x-ray diffraction, and microscopic
x-ray spectrum methods.
The metallographic study was performed under a
“METAM PB-21” microscope with /c180500 magnification.
The phase analysis was performed using a DRON-3 device
in cobalt K/c97radiation. The distribution of elements over the
diffusion layer was studied by the method of microscopic
x-ray spectrum microanalysis in a JEOL JSM-5600 device.
The porosity of the coatings was studied under a Leica light
microscope.
The quality of the coatings before testing for high-tem -
perature strength was controlled using a magnifying lens
with /c18015 magnification. In accordance with GOST 6130 the
presence of flaws in the form of corrosion traces, oxidation
tints, cracks, cleavages is impermissible.
We tested the coatings for heat resistance at a tempera-
ture of 1050 /c17710°C in still air. Chromoaluminized speci-
mens, preliminarily degreased and dried in air, were placed
into corundum crucibles heated to 1050°C. The structure of
the coatings was studied after 50, 100, 200, 300, 400, and
1500 h of testing. It was assumed that the protective proper-
ties of the specimens were exhausted if the data of the x-ray
spectrum analysis showed that the concentration of alumi-
num on the surface of the specimens decreased to 6 – 8%, or
if the results of the x-ray diffraction analysis showed that the
/c98-NiAl phase was absent on the surface.
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Chromoaluminizing can be performed in one stage if the
composition of the addition is optimized. Thermodynamic
computation of the equilibrium composition of the gas me -
dium above the source has shown that the mass transfer of
elements to the surface of a part is implemented by AlCl,
AlCl2, CrCl2, and CrCl 3 halides, simultaneous formation of
which is possible at any considered proportion of compo -
nents in the chromium-aluminum alloy. However, the CrCl 2
and CrCl3 compounds form less actively due to the lower (as
compared to aluminum) chemical affinity of chromium for
chlorine; therefore the required concentration of the element
in the coating can be obtained by using an added alloy con -
taining no less than 50% Cr. The specific composition of the
added alloy can be determined only by generalizing experi -
mental data on the structure and thickness of coatings be -
cause of the multi-factor nature of the process of formation
of diffusion layer.

Based on the experimental data, we chose a 50% Al –
50% Cr addition for the one-stage process of chromoalu -
minizing. In this case the coating has a two-layer structure
(Fig. 2a ). The external layer consists of a /c98-NiAl phase with
up to 34% Al and up to 5 – 5.5% Cr and the internal layer
consists of several phases. In addition to /c98-NiAl the layer
contains chromium, molybdenum, and tungsten carbides.
Specimens with such coatings were used for further study.
The data of metallographic and microscopic x-ray spec-
trum analyses show that the use of thermovacuum treatment
reduces the concentration of aluminum on the surface to
17 – 18.5% and causes formation of the required /c98-NiAl –
/c103/c162-Ni3Al two-phase structure in the external layer with indi-
vidual inclusions of dispersed particles of /c97-Cr (Fig. 2 b ).
The internal layer with 9.2% Al consists of a /c98-NiAl phase
and carbides.
The life of refractory coatings is largely determined by
their quality. In the first turn this concerns the absence of
pores that serve as stress concentrators under conditions of
thermal fatigue and accelerate the diffusion “resolution” of
the coating in service. The metallographic studies of the
structure show that the quality of the coatings obtained is
good. When estimating the content of defects we established
that, in contrast to the powder technology method, circula -
tion chromoaluminizing yields continuous poreless coatings
(Table 2).
In order to raise the operating characteristics of the coat -
ings by preventing formation of aluminum oxide in the diffu-
sion layer we performed a thermodynamic analysis of the ef-
fect of residual air pressure in the installation on the possibi -
lity of formation of this compound. Computations have
shown (Table 3) that decrease in the air pressure lowers the
content of Al2O3; at a pressure of 0.3 Pa it is about
10 –6 mole/c47kg. Further decrease in the air pressure virtually
does not affect the content of aluminum oxide. The working
gas medium can be cleaned from oxidizers more deeply by
using active oxygen getters, for example, yttrium. Computa -
tions show that yttrium in an amount of 0.01 mole /c47kg re -
moves O2 completely by bonding it in the Y 2O3 oxide. For
this reason we performed circulation chromoaluminizing us -
ing a nickel-yttrium alloy as a getter, which was fed into the
installation before starting every process.

CONCLUSIONS
1. We have used thermodynamic computation to show
that chromoaluminizing can be performed in one stage with
an optimum choice of added alloy that should ensure joint
presence of chlorides of the elements of the coating in the
gas medium in an amount sufficient for formation of the re -
quired structure on the surface of the part. One-stage process
of chromoaluminizing can be performed with added chro -
mium-aluminum alloy with Cr : A l=1:1 mass proportion
of the components.
2. In order to prevent the formation of aluminum oxide,
the particles of which lower the plasticity and hence the life
of protective aluminide coatings, it is expedient to reduce the
residual pressure of air in the installation and to use an oxy -
gen getter in the form of an yttrium-aluminum additive.
3. Chromoaluminized coatings deposited by circulation
and powder methods have the same high-temperature strength
and preserve protective properties after testing at 1050°C for
1500 h.
