a b s t r a c t 
NbMoTaW refractory high-entropy alloy (RHEA) shows outstanding mechanical properties at ultra-high tem- 
peratures, while room-temperature brittleness limits its application. In this study, carbon (C) microalloying was 
employed to improve the room-temperature brittleness resistance of NbMoTaW RHEA fabricated using laser pow- 
der bed fusion (LPBF). The addition of 0.5-at% C leads to good formability and remarkable microalloying eﬀects. 
Nano-NbC precipitates distributed at grain boundaries and dislocations eﬀectively reﬁned the grain size. The yield 
strength, ultimate compression strength, and ductility were successfully improved up to 1725 MPa, 1782 MPa, 
and 7.0%, respectively. Nano-NbC carbide precipitates in the form of diﬀusion transformation during multiple 
reheating was veriﬁed by thermodynamics, kinetics calculations, and microstructure characterization. C microal- 
loying endowed NbMoTaW RHEA with remarkable enhancements of strength and ductility, which suggested a 
strategy for designing strong and ductile RHEA through LPBF. 
1. Introduction 
RHEAs have shown great potentials for applications as new high- 
temperature metallic materials due to their superior thermal stability 
and mechanical properties at ultra-high temperatures [1] . NbMoTaW 
RHEA, ﬁrst reported by Senkov [1–3] , exhibits good phase stability 
and high-temperature strength ( ∼400 MPa at 1600°C), which is far be- 
yond the service temperature of Ni-based superalloys. However, sim- 
ilar to many body-centered cubic (BCC) structured metals and alloys, 
NbMoTaW RHEA suﬀers from intrinsic room-temperature brittleness 
and low ductility (usually no more than 2.1%), resulting in poor process- 
ability and formability [3] . Thus, the practical application of NbMoTaW 
RHEA is severely limited. 
For producing ductile NbMoTaW RHEA at room temperature, var- 
ious strategies have been studied [ 4 , 5 ]. By alloying with Ti [6] , 
Re [7] , Zr [8] , Hf [8] , C [9–11] , and B [10] to NbMoTaW RHEA, 
strength and ductility have been simultaneously enhanced. In particular, 
(NbMoTaW) 100-x C x RHEA (x = 0, 10 at%, 20 at% and 30 at%) have been 
synthesized by vacuum arc melting and the room-temperature compres- 
sive yield strength (YS) and ductility achieved 1285 MPa and 6.5%, re- 
spectively, due to a combination microstructures of BCC high entropy

most alloying strategies have focused on NbMoTaW RHEA prepared by 
arc melting and powder metallurgy. However, it remains diﬃcult to 
prepare components with complex shapes due to limited die shapes and 
poor processability [12] .

fabrication of components with complex conﬁguration by layer accumu- 
lation melting. Freedom of design and the ability to manufacture com- 
plex structures are the main beneﬁts of additive manufacturing (AM) 
or 3D printing [13] . In addition, the ultra-fast cooling rate (10 5 –10 8 
K/s) of LPBF can inhibit element segregation, improve phase stabil- 
ity, and obtain more reﬁned grains, which thus enhances mechanical

Motivated by the success of solving embrittlement of NbMoTaW 
RHEA with microalloying [10] , a feasible method was sought for re- 
alizing the fabrication of NbMoTaW RHEA via LPBF with C microal- 
loying. This study reported that (NbMoTaW) 99.5 C 0.5 RHEA with a high 
density of 99.6% has been successfully prepared by LPBF and the 
room-temperature yield, fracture strength, and ductility successfully im- 
proved up to 1725MPa, 1782 MPa, and 7.0%, respectively. The LPBFed 
(NbMoTaW) 99.5 C 0.5 was microstructurally more reﬁned by Nano-size 
carbide precipitates than previously reported NbMoTaW RHEA. The for- 
mation mechanism of unique microstructure and Nano-precipitates of 
LPBFed NbMoTaW RHEA was investigated using microstructural char- 
acterization. The strengthening and fracture mechanisms were analyzed 
to provide a strategy for designing strong and ductile NbMoTaW RHEA 
suitable for LPBF. 
2. Materials and methods 
NbMoTa and W powders of 15 to 53 𝜇m and WC pre-alloyed pow- 
ders of ∼500 nm with 99.9% purity were used here. The powders were 
mechanically mixed by planetary ball mill for 12 h. The eﬀects of C mi- 
croalloying on microstructure and mechanical properties were explored 
by mixing (NbMoTaW) 100-x C x RHEA (x = 0, 0.5 at%) for LPBF. An iSLM 
280 three-dimensional (3D) metal printer (Zhong Rui Technique, China) 
with the maximum power of 500 W was used for LPBF. The LPBF pro- 
cess parameters were: laser power of 400 W, scanning speed of 200 
mm/s, layer thickness of 30 𝜇m, and hatch spacing of 100 𝜇m. The rel- 
ative density was tested by computerized tomography (CT, Xradia 520 
Versa, Carl Zeiss AG, Oberkochen, Germany) analyses. The orientation 
map was determined by electron backscattering diﬀraction (EBSD) using 
a Quanta 200 FEG scanning electron microscope (SEM; FEI Co., Hills- 
boro, OR, USA). The phase composition was analyzed by X-ray diﬀrac- 
tion (XRD; D8 ADVANCE Da Vinci, Bruker Corp., Billerica, MA, USA). 
The diﬀraction proﬁle was measured at 40 kV, 40 mA, and a scanning 
rate of 1.5°/min. A transmission electron microscope (TEM; JEM-F200X, 
JEOL Ltd., Tokyo, Japan) was used for microstructural observations. 
TEM specimens were prepared using a Zeiss Aurigs focused ion beam 
miller (FIB; Carl Zeiss AG) operated at 100 kV. The JMatPro Database 
was used to calculate phase diagrams. Room-temperature compressive 
properties were carried out using a SANS CMT5105 machine (Sans Test- 
ing Machine Co., Ltd., Shenzhen, China) with video extensometer (LVE- 
MICRO30), the specimen size of 𝜑 2 mm ×4 mm and strain rate of 10 − 3 
S − 1 . 
3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Microstructural characterization 
Photos and SEM images of (NbMoTaW) 100-x C x RHEA (x = 0, 0.5 at%) 
were taken on a plane perpendicular to the print direction. It demon- 
strated that (NbMoTaW) 99.5 C 0.5 RHEA had good formability without ap- 
parent defects ( Fig. 1 a). X-ray microscope (3D-CT) results showed that 
the density of RHEA was measured to be 99.6%, with a few small-sized 
pores ( ≤ 23 μm, Fig. 1 b). Cracks were not observed both in SEM and 
3D CT results. In addition, XRD patterns of (NbMoTaW) 100-x C x RHEA 
(x = 0, 0.5 at%) indicated that the matrix consisted of BCC phase with a 
lattice constant of 3.216 Åin NbMoTaW and (NbMoTaW) 99.5 C 0.5 RHEA 
( Fig. 1 c). In addition, NbC phase existed in (NbMoTaW) 99.5 C 0.5 RHEA, 
with a lattice constant of 4.428 Å. 
Inverse pole ﬁgure (IPF) maps, kernel average misorien- 
tation (KAM) images, grain size distribution maps of LPBFed 
(NbMoTaW) 100-x C x RHEA (x = 0, 0.5 at%) showed that the NbMoTaW 
microstructure consisted of many blocky, elongated grains, and a 
few equiaxed grains ( Figs. 1d–1k , respectively). The grain size of 
the (NbMoTaW) 99.5 C 0.5 alloy is ﬁner with the addition of C atoms. 
(NbMoTaW) 99.5 C 0.5 and NbMoTaW have {100} direction texture in 
the Build Direction, the distribution is concentrated, and there is an 
obvious preference orientation. Nb, Mo, Ta, and W atoms were nearly 
homogeneously distributed in NbMoTaW RHEA, while a slight C ag- 
gregation was observed in (NbMoTaW) 99.5 C 0.5 RHEA. Generally, angle 
boundaries 2–15° are regarded as sub-boundaries and > 15° as grain 
boundaries. (NbMoTaW) 99.5 C 0.5 RHEA had a larger number of sub- 
boundaries in intragranular grains, accounting for ∼73.6% ( Fig. 1 h). 
The misorientation gradient of (NbMoTaW) 99.5 C 0.5 RHEA was sig- 
niﬁcantly higher than NbMoTaW ( Fig. 1 i), increasing along both 
the transverse and longitudinal directions, with the trend being 
more evident in the longitudinal direction. The average grain size of 
(NbMoTaW) 99.5 C 0.5 RHEA obtained by EBSD analysis was 10.6 ± 3 μm 
( Fig. 1 j). For LPBFed NbMoTaW RHEA, the content of sub-boundaries 
decreased to 54.8% and the average eﬀective grain size 18.4 ± 4 μm. 
These results showed that microalloying with C eﬀectively increased 
the proportion of sub-boundaries and reﬁned the grain size. 
In addition, the density of geometrically necessary dislocations 
(GND) increased with the KAM value. A large number of bright block 
areas were uniformly distributed in (NbMoTaW) 99.5 C 0.5 RHEA, which 
showed a higher GND density than NbMoTaW RHEA ( Figs. 1 f and 1 g). 
The detailed statistics of KAM are shown in Fig. 1 k. The average KAM 
value of (NbMoTaW) 99.5 C 0.5 RHEA was 2.3°, with the area fraction of 0–
1°meager, at ∼4.6%, and the proportion of 4–5°at ∼95.4%. For LPBFed 
NbMoTaW RHEA, the average KAM was 1.4°, area fraction between 0–
1° at ∼29.2%, and area fraction of 4–5° at ∼70.8%. It was concluded 
that C microalloying eﬀectively elevated GND density. 
3.2. Precipitation characterization 
Bright ﬁeld TEM images and diﬀraction patterns of LPBFed 
(NbMoTaW) 100-x C x RHEA (x = 0, 0.5 at%) showed that a 
large number of dispersed precipitates were only observed in 
(NbMoTaW) 99.5 C 0.5 RHEA, which were mainly distributed at grain 
boundaries and dislocations, while a few are distributed inside the 
grains ( Figs. 2 a and 2 b). Selected area electron diﬀraction (SAED) 
patterns of (NbMoTaW) 99.5 C 0.5 RHEA contained an additional set of 
diﬀraction spots. According to these patterns, the additional diﬀraction 
spots represented NbC precipitates and a [1–10] NbC ∥[110] BCC phase 
relationship from the [111] BCC zone axis. The phase composition 
measured by SAED patterns agreed well with XRD results. To reveal 
the precipitation characteristics of (NbMoTaW) 99.5 C 0.5 RHEA, detailed 
observations were made ( Figs. 2 c and 2 e). The precipitates at disloca- 
tions were analyzed by EDS and high-resolution TEM (HRTEM), which 
exhibit a polygonal shape with diameters of ∼58 nm ( Figs. 2 c–d). 
EDS results indicated that these precipitates mainly contained Nb 
and C ( Fig. 2 c). The atomic structures of precipitates were further 
characterized by HRTEM taken from the [001] BCC zone axis ( Fig. 2 d). 
Fast Fourier transform (FFT) patterns were taken from matrix areas 
(white-dotted line) and precipitated particles (red-dotted line) and it 
was found that the precipitated particles were NbC phase. The interpla- 
nar spacings of [1-10] BCC and [020] NbC were about 0.222 and 0.213 
nm, respectively. The phase relationship was [010] NbC //[1-10] BCC 
from the [100] BCC zone axis. Also, stripe-shaped NbC precipitates were 
distributed along grain boundaries with 90–110 nm in length and 
40–60 nm in width ( Fig. 2 e). The interplanar spacings of [110] BCC and

3.3. Mechanical properties 
Compressive stress-strain curves of LPBFed (NbMoTaW) 100-x C x 
RHEA (x = 0, 0.5 at%) showed that both strength and ductility of LPBFed 
NbMoTaW were dramatically enhanced by microalloying with 0.5-at% 
C at room temperature ( Fig. 3 ). Speciﬁcally, the compressive yield and 
fracture strengths of NbMoTaW RHEA were greatly enhanced from 1196 
± 15 and 1237 ± 20 MPa to 1725 ± 10 and 1782 ± 25 MPa, increased 
by ∼43% and 45%, respectively. Ductility was substantially increased 
from 4.6 ± 0.2 to 7.0 ± 0.5%. The elastic modulus of (NbMoTaW) 99.5 C 0.5 
RHEA and NbMoTaW RHEA were 334GPa and 318GPa, respectively. To 
unveil the fracture mechanism, fracture morphologies were observed, 
which showed that LPBFed NbMoTaW RHEA had a relatively smooth 
morphology with cleavage fractures. In comparison, the fractography 
of LPBFed (NbMoTaW) 99.5 C 0.5 RHEA showed sharp tearing edges with 
transgranular fractures. These results indicated that the fracture mech- 
anism of NbMoTaW RHEA changed from brittle mode to brittle-ductile 
mixed mode with 0.5-at% C addition. The strength and ductility of 
NbMoTaW RHEA had been substantially enhanced due to 0.5-at% C mi- 
croalloying.

mechanism of NbC precipitates under LPBF processing, the phase di- 
agram was calculated using the JMatPro Database, with the left ordi- 
nate axis representing the mole fraction of precipitates and matrix phase 
( Fig. 4 a). It was found that there was only the BCC matrix phase existing

0.5-at% C, (NbMoTaW) 99.5 C 0.5 RHEA contained BCC matrix phase and 
NbC carbides. These carbides precipitated in the temperature range of 
1400–2400°C and the calculated maximum volume fraction is 2.34%. 
This indicated that the precipitation temperature of NbC carbides was 
in the range of the BCC matrix phase, with the BCC matrix phase so- 
lidiﬁed ahead of the carbides. NbC precipitate nucleation is a kind of 
diﬀusion transformation, which relies on diﬀusion. The diﬀusion coeﬃ- 
cient reduces with decreasing temperature, but diﬀusion coeﬃcients of 
diﬀerent elements have diﬀerent sensitivity to temperature. At 1400°C, 
the diﬀusion coeﬃcients of Nb, Mo, Ta, W, and C atoms are 9.4 ×10 − 13 , 
2.4 × 10 − 17 , 5.9 × 10 − 17 , 3.2 × 10 − 17 , and 6 × 10 − 7 m 2 s − 1 , respec- 
tively [27–29] . The diﬀusion coeﬃcients of C and Nb atoms are several 
magnitudes higher than those of the other elements, so that Nb and C 
atoms more easily diﬀuse and form NbC precipitates at the relatively 
high thermal-cycle temperature of the LPBF process. However, as ob- 
served from scanning TEM (STEM) of (NbMoTaW) 99.5 C 0.5 RHEA taken 
from top surface of an LPBFed sample ( Fig. 4 b), there were no Nb and C 
enrichments and NbC precipitates. The diﬀerent NbC precipitation be- 
havior between top surface and other areas was mainly due to diﬀerent 
thermal histories during the LPBF process. The cooling rate of the single 
pass LPBF process was speedy and NbC carbides did not have suﬃcient 
time to precipitate [ 30 , 31 ]. In addition, the high entropy eﬀect also lim- 
ited the initial diﬀusion precipitation of NbC in the just-solidiﬁed layer.

Based on the aforementioned results, the mechanism of C microal- 
loying on microstructure and mechanical properties were discussed 
and elucidated here. Grain reﬁnement from C microalloying was at- 
tributed to the following reasons, with a schematic diagram shown 
in Fig. 5 . Firstly, the great undercooling of the LPBF process is prone 
to thermal deformation, resulting in a larger dislocation density. For 
(NbMoTaW) 99.5 C 0.5 , C element existed in the matrix as the interstitial 
solid solution at initial printing, which has a certain hindering eﬀect on 
the dislocation movement generated by thermal deformation [32] . In 
addition, the samples prepared by LPBF process have preferred orienta- 
tion ( Fig. 2 ). Therefore, the dislocation density of (NbMoTaW) 99.5 C 0.5 
is higher than NbMoTaW at initial printing. The diﬀerence of dislo- 
cation density within grains is more obvious ( Fig 1 i), and it contains 
more subgrain boundaries. The addition of C to NbMoTaW RHEA in- 
duced smaller grain formation during initial printing. Secondly, a large 
amount of Nano-NbC carbides precipitated, distributed mainly at grain 
boundaries and dislocations in (NbMoTaW) 99.5 C 0.5 RHEA after thermal 
cycle ( Figs. 2b-2f ). NbC particles precipitated at grain boundaries and 
dislocations led to a strong pinning eﬀect ( Fig. 2 e), which further lim- 
ited grain coarsening after the repeated heat input of the LPBF process 
( Fig. 5 ). The microstructural variation of NbMoTaW RHEA introduced 
by C microalloying consequently resulted in enhancement of mechan- 
ical properties. The strengthening mechanisms of C microalloying in 
LPBFed NbMoTaW RHEA were assumed to combinations of precipita- 
tion strengthening and grain reﬁnement. While according to the grain 
reﬁnement strengthening Equation 1 [12] , the strengthening eﬀect be- 
tween 10 μm and 18 μm is not signiﬁcant. However, the precipitation 
strengthening formula of Equation 2 shows that nanoscale NbC precip- 
itates can provide a signiﬁcant strengthening eﬀect [33] . Therefore, it 
can be considered that precipitation strengthening is the main mecha- 
nism for improving the strength of (NbMoTaW) 99.5 C 0.5 RHEA. Notably, 
the ductility of LPBFed NbMoTaW RHEA also increased after C microal- 
loying. This was because microalloying of C atoms suppresses the O seg- 
regation at grain boundaries which ensures stronger bonds of the host 
metals and suppresses intergranular crack [10] . In general, C microal- 
loying of LPBFed NbMoTaW RHEA achieved a good balance of strength 
and ductility enhancement by containing combinations of grain reﬁne- 
ment and precipitation strengthening.

4. Conclusions 
In summary, LPBFed NbMoTaW RHEA microalloyed with C 
(0.5 at%) exhibited good formability with 99.6% density and re- 
markable enhancement in strength and ductility properties. LPBFed 
(NbMoTaW) 99.5 C 0.5 RHEA possessed a reﬁned grain size comprising of 
BCC matrix and Nano-NbC precipitates with a [010] NbC //[1-10] BCC re- 
lationship. Most NbC precipitates were distributed along grain bound- 
aries and dislocations, as conﬁrmed by TEM and STEM. The yield and 
fracture strengths and ductility were simultaneously improved up to 
1725 MPa, 1782 MPa, and 7.0%, respectively. As veriﬁed by thermo- 
dynamics and kinetics calculations, Nano-NbC precipitation occurred in 
the form of diﬀusion transformation allowed by repeated heating during
