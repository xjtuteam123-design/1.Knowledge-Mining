ABSTRACT
Density functional theory is performed to comparatively examine how alloying elements (Sn, Fe, Cr, Cu, and Nb) 
modulate the grain boundary stability, cohesive strength, Young ’ s modulus, and fracture toughness for HCP Zr 
{ 10 11 } grain boundary. Thermodynamic analysis reveals that Cr, Fe, and Nb exhibit spontaneous GB segregation 
with negative segregation energies while simultaneously enhancing the stability and fracture toughness of the 
{ 10 11 } grain boundary. Notably, Cu and Sn additions substantially diminish cohesion strength, Young ’ s 
modulus, and fracture toughness. Co-alloying strategies with Cr/Nb effectively mitigate Sn-induced embrittle -
ment, restoring the fracture resistance of pristine ZrSn alloy. The derived results are discussed in terms of 
electronic structure and are in good agreement with experimental and theoretical results in the literature, which 
could provide a deep understanding of the intrinsic mechanism of the effects of alloying element on the fracture 
toughness of HCP Zr.
1. Introduction
Zirconium (Zr) alloys have been extensively utilized as structural 
components in nuclear reactor systems, particularly as fuel cladding and 
reactor pressure vessels, owing to their favorable characteristics 
including low neutron capture, superior corrosion resistance, high me -
chanical strength, and creep durability [ 1 – 8 ]. During operational con -
ditions, Zr-based components are subjected to significant 
thermomechanical stresses, aggressive steam corrosion, and high-flux 
neutron irradiation environments [ 4 , 9 ]. This operational reality neces -
sitates the development of advanced Zr alloys with optimized mechan -
ical characteristics, enhanced corrosion tolerance, and improved 
radiation resistance to ensure reactor integrity and long-term opera -
tional safety [ 10 – 12 ]. Typically, these zirconium alloys contain alloying 
elements such as Sn, Fe, Cr, Cu, and Nb, which are used to enhance the 
combined mechanical properties of the alloys.
In polycrystalline systems, grain boundary (GB) characteristics serve 
as critical determinants of material strength and failure mechanisms 
[ 13 , 14 ]. In this regard, there are already some experimental and theo -
retical investigations about the solute segregation and the effects of 
alloying elements on Zr grain boundary strength in the literatures 
[ 14 – 20 ]. Notably, intergranular fracture phenomena have been re -
ported as predominant failure modes in Zr alloy cladding applications 
within light water reactor (LWR) environments [ 14 ]. In addition, 
Christensen found that the strength of the Zr Σ 7 (0001) twist grain 
boundary is quite insensitive to most elements. This is partially due to 
the similar behavior of atoms in the grain boundary and at the free Zr 
surface [ 20 ]. And the alloying elements (Sn, Fe, Cr, and Nb) could 
enhance solid-solution strengthening of Zr { 10 12 } < 10 1 1 >
twin-boundary [ 19 ]. And Xue found that Cr, Mn, Fe, W and Mo atoms 
easily segregate at Zr {10 11} boundary grain and the segregation of Ag, 
Bi, Au and Sn presents a significant embrittling effect on the GB [ 15 ]. 
However, the tensile strength of Zr was significantly underestimated in 
the Rice-Wang model [ 15 ], i.e., the tensile strength along the < 101 >
direction was reported as 11.92 GPa, which is substantially lower than 
the theoretical value of ~70 Gpa [ 10 ]. Therefore, it is necessary to 
conduct in-depth research on the tensile strength of Zr grain boundaries 
and the influence of alloying elements on Zr grain boundary strength.

revealed the effects of swaging feed rate, annealing phenomena, and

Employing density functional theory (DFT) with highly accurate first 
principle calculations [ 31 , 32 ], present investigation establishes a com -
parison framework to systematically evaluate the influence of key 
alloying elements (Sn, Fe, Cr, Cu, Nb) on the grain boundary stability, 
cohesive strength, and fracture toughness of hexagonal close-packed 
(HCP) Zr { 10 11 } grain boundary. In addition, the cooperative effect of 
alloying elements (Cr, Nb, and Sn) on cohesion strength of Zr { 10 11 } GB 
has been studied. Accordingly, the Zr { 10 11 } GB is selected as a typical 
example in the present study, as it has lower grain boundary energy and 
was observed in the experimental investigations [ 30 , 33 ]. And Sn, Fe, Cr, 
Cu, and Nb are purposely chosen as they are the main alloying elements 
in the Zr alloys [ 4 , 10 – 12 ]. The calculated results will be discussed 
through comparison with similar observations in the literature, and will 
fundamentally reveal the intrinsic mechanism of the effect of alloying 
elements on the fracture toughness of HCP Zr.
2. Methods of calculations
In this study, the computational framework employs density func -
tional theory (DFT) implemented in the Vienna Ab Initio Simulation 
Package (VASP) [ 34 , 35 ]. Electron-ion interactions were modeled using 
the projector-augmented wave (PAW) formalism [ 36 , 37 ], with 
exchange-correlation effects treated through the 
Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) generalized gradient approximation 
[ 38 ]. A plane-wave basis set with a kinetic energy cutoff of 450 eV was 
systematically applied across all computational procedures. In addition, 
the temperature-smearing method of Methfessel-Paxton [ 39 ] and the 
tetrahedron method with Bl ¨ochl corrections [ 40 ] are performed for 
relaxation and static calculations, respectively.

3. Results and discussion
Extensive prior research has systematically characterized solute 
segregation phenomena and alloying element impacts on Zr GB strength 
through experimental and computational approaches [ 14 – 20 ]. Ther -
momechanical processing parameters and microstructural refinement 
strategies have been demonstrated to significantly influence fracture 
resistance in Zr-based systems, as documented in recent studies [ 21 – 30 ]. 
It is noteworthy that there are no reports on the mechanism of alloying 
effects on GB cohesion and fracture toughness in Zr systems. In present 
study, the investigation focuses on the selected alloying elements (Sn, 
Fe, Cr, Cu, Nb) to elucidate their effects on GB stability, cohesion 
strength, and fracture toughness characteristics in HCP Zr { 10 11 } GB.

sent study has quantitatively characterized grain boundary properties,

toughness for HCP Zr { 10 11 } GB systems with alloying elements (Sn, Fe,
