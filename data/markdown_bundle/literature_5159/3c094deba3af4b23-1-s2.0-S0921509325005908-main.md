ABSTRACT
In this study, the as-cast Ni-Fe-based superalloy GH4650T (HT650T) subjected to only two steps solution heat 
treatment shows good mechanical property at high temperature, and the yield strength increases abnormally 
with the increase of temperature. Correspondingly, the reasons for yield strength anomaly are explored in terms 
of precipitation behavior of strengthening phases, dislocation movement and evolution trend of grain boundary 
species. The experimental results indicated that the solution-treated GH4650T alloy can still maintain good 
mechanical property at high temperature, which is attributed to M
23
C
6 
carbides with fine particle morphology 
distributed along grain boundaries. In addition, the yield strength anomaly observed in solution-treated 
GH4650T alloy during high-temperature tension is primarily attributed to continuous dynamic recrystalliza -
tion. Dislocations interact each other through planar slips, forming string-like low angle grain boundaries 
(LAGBs) network inside the grain, the LAGBs network evolves to form sub-grains, and further plastic deformation 
prompts LAGBs in the sub-grains to transform into high-angle grain boundaries (HAGBs). The strong obstruction 
of HAGBs on dislocation movement eventually leads to the pile-ups of high-density dislocations at HAGBs, which 
eventually results in the increase of yield strength anomaly of GH4650T alloy.

4. Discussion
It is generally accepted that Kear-Wilsdorf (K-W) mechanism [ 27 ] 
could explain the yield strength anomaly of Ni-based superalloys. Aside 
from above mechanism, microstructural parameters such as volume 
fraction and size, antiphase boundary energy, grain boundary carbides 
and heat treatment, etc., also contribute to the appearance of yield 
strength anomaly. K-W locks are typically responsible for the yield 
strength anomaly of Ni-based superalloys when the volume percentage 
of large-size (micron-scale) γ ′ precipitates exceeds 40 %. The definition 
of K-W locks is that the cross-slip of dislocations from {111} to {001} 
planes with lower anti-phase boundary energy, leading to the formation 
of immobile dislocation segments that hinder deformation. The above 
mechanism is commonly utilized to elucidate the yield strength anomaly 
observed in Ni-based superalloys characterized by a high-volume frac -
tion of large cubic γ ′ phases. However, it should be noted that the 
occurrence of K-W locks often means that the alloy ’ s plastic deformation 
originated from γ ′ shearing caused by dislocations. Through the dislo -
cation configuration shown in Fig. 7 , it was found that the 
solution-treated GH4650T alloy deformed from RT to 750 
◦
C is always 
dominated by the planar slips of dislocations. Additionally, as illustrated 
in Figs. 2d and 7 , the solution-treated GH4650T alloy, having not un -
dergone aging heat treatment, lacks or even less precipitation of γ ′ 
phases. Some studies [ 28 , 29 ] have indicated that the likelihood of 
cross-slip events decreases with the reduction in γ ′ phase size. Conse -
quently, the yield strength anomaly observed in the solution-treated 
GH4650T alloy is unlikely to be associated with K-W locks. Similarly, 
the yield strength anomaly of the alloy cannot be explained by grain 
boundary carbides, despite the fact that fine granular M
23
C
6 
carbides 
discretely distributed at grain boundaries can effectively impede the 
movement of dislocations. This is because the size and morphology of 
M
23
C
6 
carbides in solution-treated GH4650T alloy have not changed 
significantly with an increase in tensile temperature, and the absence of 
obvious change in size of M
23
C
6 
carbides naturally did not cause 
abnormal fluctuation of yield strength. In summary, there might be a 
different dislocation blocking mechanism in solution-treated GH4650T 
alloy which causes the yield strength anomaly.

that Ʃ 3 and Ʃ CSL ≤ 29 grain boundaries are special high-angle grain

shown in Fig. 10 f. The recrystallization process is always accompanied 
by the intervolution of low- Ʃ CSL ( Ʃ ≤ 29) grain boundaries. For 
example, in the process of recrystallization, not only the grain boundary 
regeneration modes of Ʃ 3 + Ʃ 3 → Ʃ 9 and Ʃ 3 + Ʃ 9 → Ʃ 27 but also the 
grain boundary regeneration mode of Ʃ 27 + Ʃ 9 → Ʃ 3 exist, and the 
degree of occurrence of the growth mode of Ʃ 9 + Ʃ 27 → Ʃ 3 increases as 
the degree of recrystallization progresses [ 52 ]. This can also explain why 
the proportion of CSL grain boundaries in solution-treated GH4650T 
alloy shows a trend of increasing the proportion of Ʃ 3 grain boundaries 
and decreasing the proportion of low- Ʃ CSL ( Ʃ ≤ 29) grain boundaries 
with the increase of tensile temperature. Of course, the introduction of 
these low- Ʃ CSL ( Ʃ ≤ 29) grain boundaries can undoubtedly improve the 
mechanical property of solution-treated GH4650T alloy. The underlying 
reason is that the incoherent site lattice of HAGBs is widely recognized 
as an effective barrier to dislocation motion [ 53 , 54 ]. As a result, dislo -
cations accumulate and pile up at HAGBs, and such accumulation in -
tensifies with increasing loading cycles. In contrast, Ʃ 3 and Ʃ CSL ≤ 29 
grain boundaries, which possess a special coincidence site lattice, are 
less effective in obstructing the movement of dislocations compared to 
HAGBs. Therefore, when a crack tip encounters Ʃ 3 and Ʃ CSL ≤ 29 grain 
boundaries, it will bypass them and choose HAGBs to propagate thus 
consuming less energy.

In the current work, the microstructural evolution and deformation

have shown that the solution-treated GH4650T alloy can still maintain 
good mechanical property and its yield strength anomaly comes from 
continuous dynamic recrystallization mechanism. The main results can
