ABSTRACT 
Superior high temperature strength, better thermodynamic stability and good workability are some of the 
desirable attributes of structural materials for next generation nuclear reactors. In this regard, the present study 
involves the development of a low density, equiatomic ZrNbVTiAl High Entropy Alloy (HEA) for nuclear ap -
plications. The alloy was designed using a combination of thermodynamic simulations and first principle tech -
niques and later synthesized using levitation melting to ensure better purity and compositional homogeneity. The 
melted alloy possessed lower density (5.76 g/cm
3
) and higher hardness (5.7 GPa) in comparison to conventional 
structural materials like austenitic steel. Detailed characterization of the alloy showed the presence of bcc type 
phase along with small fractions of Al- Zr type intermetallics in as-solidified condition. The mechanical properties 
of the equiatomic ZrNbVTiAl alloy were studied using compression testing at room temperature and up to 
1000 
◦
C. The alloy exhibited superior specific yield strength as well as dynamic recrystallization at high tem -
peratures resulting in the breaking of cast structure, thereby reflecting its appreciable workability. The alloy also 
showed a satisfactory elastic modulus (128 GPa) when measured using ultrasonic technique. The experimentally 
measured values of elastic moduli of alloy were compared with the values obtained through Density Functional 
Theory (DFT) simulations and the results matched within acceptable range. Results from the present study in -
dicates that the equiatomic ZrNbVTiAl alloy could be a suitable candidate for light weight, high temperature and 
high strength structural applications. 
1. Introduction 
Unlike current light water based nuclear fission reactors, which 
operate at maximum temperature of 350 
◦

spectrum (25 meV), generation IV fission nuclear reactors will be sub -
jected to temperatures up to 1000

higher neutron fluence, and possibly, more corrosive environments than 
the current generation nuclear reactors [ 1 , 2 ]. In addition, proposed 
nuclear fusion reactors are expected to pose even more aggressive 
conditions in terms of higher stress, magnetic field and neutron energies 
(up to 14 MeV) [ 3 ]. Demands on the required properties of a structural 
material for such advanced reactors, in terms of enhanced dimensional 
stability under irradiation at temperatures ≧ 1000

irradiation creep and corrosion resistance in nuclear coolants like light 
and supercritical water, gases (CO

molten salts including superior workability, necessitates a paradigm 
shift in the approach of developing new structural materials [ 4 ]. In 
addition, reduction in density without compromising on temperature 
strength can provide an additional advantage of reducing the overall 
weight of the structural parts. 
In this regard, a new strategy in alloy design has been explored where 
five or more elements are combined in nearly equiatomic compositions 
allowing significant increase in the configurational entropy of the sys -
tem thereby leading to a much lower free energy; in other words, suf -
ficiently higher stability in comparison to conventional alloys [ 5 , 6 ].

Multicomponent “ High Entropy Alloys (HEAs) ” generally depict 
Body Centered Cubic (bcc) or Face Centered Cubic (fcc) based single 
phase crystal structures [ 3 ]. In addition, the multi-component approach 
has been reported to result in superior mechanical properties (even up to 
1000 
◦

making HEAs suitable candidates for high temperature structural ap -
plications [ 10 ]. Such outstanding properties generally link to their 
unique dislocation properties generated through the lattice distortion 
effect as well as the sluggish diffusion of alloying elements in multi- 
component HEAs [ 6 ]. The design methodology of HEAs is primarily 
based on satisfaction of various thermodynamic criteria including en -
tropy of mixing ( Δ S
mix
), enthalpy of mixing ( Δ H
mix
), valence electron 
configuration ( VEC ) and atomic size difference ( δ ) [ 11 ]. 
The possibility of the formation of a single phase HEA for a particular 
composition can be analytically predicted by calculating the values of 
these parameters (and matching with the statistical ranges) or by CAL -
PHAD simulations which could be used to predict the phase diagram for 
a composition over a range of temperature [ 12 ]. In addition, HEAs could 
also be designed using the first principle based Density Functional 
Theory (DFT) approach which predicts the stability of an alloy as well as 
the formation of single phase in HEA [ 13 ]. Mechanical properties of a 
system can be also studied through DFT calculations [ 14 ]. Nevertheless, 
the choice of individual elements to constitute a particular HEA also 
depends on the targeted application such that the combination of ele -
ments result in properties best suited for that applications. 
With regard to nuclear applications, a bcc phase is preferred in HEAs 
since the bcc structure is relatively more open and therefore offers 
greater resistance to irradiation swelling [ 9 ]. In this context, HEAs based 
on refractory elements have recently been developed showing primarily 
bcc phase, high melting temperatures (above 2873 K) and satisfactory 
mechanical response sometimes even up to 1400 
◦
C [ 15 ]. However, due 
to the presence of high melting elements like W, Ta and Mo, most re -
fractory HEAs generally show high densities ( > 10 g/cm
3
); which leads 
to low specific strength, difficulty in fabrication and associated heat 
treatments. Thus the need of the hour is to develop refractory based 
HEAs having bcc structure, high specific strengths and acceptable 
fabricability. 
In this regard, an equiatomic ZrNbVTiAl multicomponent alloy was 
developed at Bhabha Atomic Research Center (BARC) targeting appli -
cations in the nuclear industry. Selection of the aforementioned 
composition was mainly based on the following considerations: 
(i) Nb, Zr, V and Ti have very high melting points and mostly qualify 
to be in the class of refractory elements. All these elements have 
significant solubility in their binary combinations. These ele -
ments are thus expected to develop a high strength bcc based 
structure on alloying. In addition, Nb, Zr and Al have low neutron 
absorption cross section; a necessary requirement for their 
application as nuclear structural material [ 17 ]. 
(ii) The equiatomic NbVTiAl alloy has been reported to show a single 
bcc phase with satisfactory mechanical properties [ 18 ]. Addition 
of Zr to this alloy in equiatomic quantities is expected to improve 
its stability since Zr shows complete solubility with Ti while 
alloying of Zr and Al promotes bcc type phases [ 19 ]. 
(iii) Al being a light weight material is expected to decrease the 
overall density of the equiatomic ZrNbVTiAl alloy thereby 
improving specific strength of the HEA. Moreover, Al has similar 
atomic radii (143 pm) as those of Ti, Nb and V which is expected 
to promote formation of solid solutions in HEAs. 
The present paper reports on the investigations carried out on the 
aforesaid alloy composition, specifically, the alloy design, synthesis, 
microstructural characterization and evaluation of mechanical 
properties.

It may be noted that although HEAs have been historically defined to 
consist of one single phase, several research groups have also reported 
the formation of multiphase HEAs [ 30 , 31 ]. In many cases, these alloys 
are referred to as compositionally complex alloys although their properties 
are generally typical of those observed for conventional HEAs [ 32 ]. In 
certain cases, presence of an ordered 2nd phase in the matrix has led to a 
higher mechanical strength in comparison to the single phase ones [ 33 ]. 
For eg. microstructure of a CrMnFeCoNiAl
0.75 
high entropy alloy was 
found to consist of a mixture of FCC, BCC and B2 type phases and the 
alloy displayed an excellent compressive yield strength of 1194 MPa at 
room temperature [ 33 ]. This value was higher than that obtained for 
single FCC phase equiatomic high entropy alloy CrMnFeCoNi [ 34 ]. The 
present study is focused more on the development of a light weight and 
high strength material for advanced applications and thus the required 
analysis required for categorizing the alloy as HEA or CCA is beyond its 
scope. For the sake of simplicity, the present alloy is being termed as 
HEA since its thermodynamic properties satisfies the reported criterion.

having a primary bcc or fcc phase along with minor fractions of inter -
metallic phases have been reported in literature [ 32 , 33 , 42 ]. These alloys 
are expected to show a possibility of higher creep strength as a result of 
precipitation strengthening as well as irradiation resistance which could 
be provided by the pinning effect of the intermetallic compounds [ 42 ]. 
3.3. Hardness and elastic constants 
The hardness of the present alloy was found to be 576 HVN (or 5.7 
GPa) from microhardness tests which falls in the upper side of hardness 
range of most common refractory alloys [ 14 – 16 ]. HEAs containing 
aluminum have been previously found to display superior hardness 
values in literature reports. For e.g.: AlFeCoNiCu has a hardness of 463 
HVN [ 43 ] while the TiAlFeCoNi alloy before and after carbon doping 
can show harnesses over than 600 HVN [ 44 ] and 950 HVN [ 45 ] 
respectively. Addition of Nb to Al based HEAs alloy can raise the 
hardness value further as observed for TiAlFeCoNiNb (1000 HVN) [ 46 ] 
and AlCrFeCoNiNb (1030 HVN) [ 47 ] high entropy alloys, the later one 
falling in the ultra-high hardness range. The reason for such high 
hardness values is mainly attributed to the formation of hard interme -
tallic phases of various transition metals (Fe, Ni, Co, Zr, etc) with Al and 
strengthening of the matrix phase by Nb which results in an overall 
increase in hardness in theses alloys [ 43 – 47 ].

A thermodynamically stable, low density, high entropy alloy 
ZrNbVTiAl of equiatomic composition was designed for high tempera -

displayed around 90% of BCC type phase in as-cast condition along with

room temperature which in combination high hardness (5.7 GPa), low
