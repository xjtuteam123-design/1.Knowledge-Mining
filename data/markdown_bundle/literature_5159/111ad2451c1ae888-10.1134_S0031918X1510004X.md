INTRODUCTION
Because of its high corrosion resistance and high
temperature strength, high-alloyed iron-chromium-
nickel Inconel 718 alloy is widely used in the produc-
tion of heat-loaded parts for gas turbine jet engines,
energy-power plants, and products of space technol-
ogy . However, the high content of alloying elements is
responsible for the coarse grain structure in the alloy ,
which reduces its service properties. An increase in the
structure refinement significantly increases the yield
strength and tensile strength of nickel alloys while
increasing their service lives under cyclic loads [1–5].
One of the most effective ways of refining the struc-
ture of metal alloys is the use of modifiers in the form of
refractory nanoparticles [6–9]. These particles can lack
the orientation and dimensional compliance with the
crystalline structure of the alloy , but have the ability to
adsorb and retain metal films isomorphous with the
crystalline structure of the alloy on their surface. When
interacting with molten metal, stable chemical com-
pounds are formed on the surface of the nanoparticles,
which protect the nanoparticles from dissolution in the
melt and prevent their coagulation, while ensuring the
wettability of their surface with the melt. During the
cooling and crystallization of molten metal, the parti-
cles manifest themselves as crystallization centers that
form the grain structure of the alloy . At a fairly large num-
ber of nanoparticles of refractory chemical compounds,
an increased number of crystallization centers are formed
in the melt; hence, the number of grains increases and
the grain size decreases in the solidified alloy .
The primary disadvantage of this method of grain
structure refinement of the alloys via introducing
refractory nanoparticles into the melt is their dissolu-
tion in the melt. This is especially important in the
case of nickel alloys, the melts of which have high
chemical activity with respect to most of the known
chemical compounds. The high solubility of the parti-
cles of chemical compounds in nickel melts greatly
limits the choice of refractory compounds that are
prospective modifiers. On the other hand, the high
solubility predetermines the higher wettability of the
surface of modifying particles with a melt and, corre-
spondingly , their greater effectiveness as crystalliza-
tion centers in the melt. This contradiction may be
minimized by the use of composite powder particles
reinforced with nanoparticles of refractory chemical
compounds, e.g., based on an NiAl intermetallic com-
pound. NiAl coatings slow down the dissolution of
nanoparticles in a nickel melt, which increases the
probability of nucleation during the cooling of
the melt.

Abstract—The experimental results of the effect of pow der nanomodifiers of refractory compounds on the
strength properties, the macro- and microstructure of the high-temperature Inconel 718 alloy have been pre-
sented. It has been shown that the introduction of pow der modifiers into the melt leads to a decrease in the
average grain size by a factor of 1.5–2 in the alloy . The long-term tensile strength of the alloy at 650°C
increases 1.5–2 times, and the number of cycles at 482°C before fracture grows by more than three times. The
effect of nanoparticles on the grain structure and strength properties of the alloy is due to an increase in the
number of generated crystallization centers and the fo rmation of nanoparticle clusters of refractory com-
pounds at boundaries and junctions in the formed grain structure, which hinder the development of recrys-
tallization processes in the alloy .
Keywords: high-temperature alloy , nanopowders, interm etallic compounds, nanomodification, structure,
strength properties

Effect of Refractory Nanoparticles 
on the Fine Structure of Inconel 718 Alloy
In the initial state, the dislocation substructure of
the nickel alloy is characterized by the clusters of ran-
domly distributed dislocations (Fig. 4a). Scalar dislo-
cation density ρ = 2.5 × 1010 cm–2. The grain size is
very coarse (millimeters), since no grain boundaries
are observ ed on a foil. In some cases, inclusions of a
second phase about 50 nm in size and located in the
form of stitches are observed in the grain volume
(Fig. 4b). Indexing of electron diffraction patterns
(Fig. 4c) suggests that these particles have a composi-
tion of F eNi and hav e been formed as a result of the
dendritic segregation of iron.
The alloy modified with the (NiAl + 0.04% TiN)
composite powder has, as in the case of an alloy in the
initial state, a chaotic dislocation structure at the sca-
lar dislocation density ρ = 3.0 × 1010 cm–2. The grain
volume exhibits the inclusions of titanium nitride
(TiN) 50–150 nm in size, which are distributed mainly
along grain boundaries (Fig. 5).
In the case of the modification of the structure of
the Inconel 718 alloy with the NiAl + 0.06% (TiN +
TiC0.5N0.5) composite powder, submicrocrystalline
interlayers consisted of TiC0.5N0.5 particles (Fig. 6) are
formed at the grain boundaries in the alloy . The parti-
cle sizes in the interlayers are 10–20 nm, and the sizes
of particle clusters in the interlayers reach 50 nm.
The presented results of the examination of the fine
structure modified with NiAl-intermetallide-based
composite powders reinforced with titanium nitride
(TiN) and titanium carbonitride (TiC
0.5N0.5) nanopar-
ticles have demonstrated the following. The modifying
effect of refractory nanopowders on the grain size in
the Inconel 718 alloy consists of the formation of
microvolumes with a submicrocrystalline structure
and submicrocrystalline interlayers at the triple junc-
tions and boundaries that contain nitride and titanium
carbonitride nanoparticles. In the first case, the
refractory nanoparticles exhibit a nucleating effect on
the grain structure formation of the alloy (an increase
in the number of the crystallization nuclei in the melt).
In the second case, the refractory nanoparticles stop
the movement of grain boundaries during the forma-
tion of the grain structure of the alloy . The result of the
modifying effect of the nanoparticles of refractory
compounds is a decrease in the average grain size and
enhanced strength properties of the alloy .
