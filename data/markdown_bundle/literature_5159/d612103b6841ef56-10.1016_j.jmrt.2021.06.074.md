abstract
Novel Cr50Fe6Co7Mo14C15B6Y2 bulk metallic glass (BMG) with a critical diameter of about 1.5 mm
was developed using high-content solvent element Cr substitution for Fe from the precursor
Fe41Cr15Co7Mo14C15B6Y2 BMG. The alloy possesses integrated advantages of superior glass
transition temperature of 981 K, extraordinary high onset temperature of crystallization of
1018 K, dramatical high compressive fracture strength of 4.49 GPa and Vickers microhardness of
14.01 GPa, and prominent corrosion resistance of no detectable weight loss in 1 M HCl solution.
The structural origin for the decreased glass-forming ability of the present alloy in comparison
with its precursor Fe
41Cr15Co7Mo14C15B6Y2 BMG results from the lower fraction of icosahedral-
type structures (with bond pair indexes of 1551, 1541, and 1431) along with the higher fraction
of crystal-like structures (with bond pair indexes of 1421, 1422, 1441 and 1661), and the covalent-
like CreC and CreB bonds are responsible for the superior glass transition temperature, the onset
temperature of crystallization, fracture strength and hardness, based on theab-initiomolecular
dynamics simulation for the local structures. The present BMG can be a preferable alloy for in-
dustrial applications under the harsh environment of high temperature, wear and corrosion.

1. Introduction
As a remarkable kind of advanced alloys, bulk metallic glasses
(BMGs) have focused considerable attention on their superior
properties, such as high strength and hardness, excellent
wear resistance and prominent corrosion resistance, which
permits the alloys as promising materials for scientiﬁc in-
vestigations and engineering applications [ 1e10]. Although
plenty of BMGs have been synthesized, most of the alloys
possess moderate glass transition temperature ( T
g), the onset
temperature of crystallization ( Tx), strength, hardness, and
corrosion resistance, which obviously contracts the applica-
tions of the alloys in the severer environment of high tem-
perature, wear and corrosion. Meanwhile, it is preferred that
BMGs should be used at the temperatures below their T
x or Tg,
preventing the alloys from invalidation caused by crystalli-
zation. Correspondingly, BMGs with prominent T
g and Tx are
constantly expected, which can be employed more tolerantly.
Accordingly, successive efforts are considered to focus on
developing novel BMGs with integrated advantages of supe-
rior T
g and Tx, extremely high strength and hardness, and
amazing corrosion resistance, which can extend the prospects
for theoretical researches and engineering applications of the
alloys in harsher environments, i.e., earth excavating, drilling
and tunnel boring under the environment of high tempera-
ture, wear and corrosion.
As it is generally time-consuming work for developing new
BMGs, it is signiﬁcant to choose a useful method for the exploi-
tation of new BMGs. It has been reported that high-content
solvent element substitution is an effective approach or fabri-
cation process for developing new BMGs and tuning the glass-
forming ability (GFA) and properties [6,11e13]. Generally, high-
content solvent element substitution means high content sub-
stitution (normally no less than 15 at.%) of one solvent element
for another solvent element (both the two solvent elements are
generally neighboring elements in the periodic table of the ele-
ments, such as Fe and Co, La and Ce, Mn and Fe, Cr and Fe). For
examples, a (FeCo)eSieBeNb BMG with a critical diameter (d
c)o f
5 mm was developed by a high-content (36 at.%) solvent element
Co substitution for Fe in Fe eSieBeNb BMG as well as an
increased d
c, mechanical strength and hardness [11], a series of
(LaCe)eAleCo and La eAleCo BMGs were fabricated by a high-
content (26e65 at.%) solvent element La substitution for Ce in
CeeAleCo BMG together with a tuning dc, thermal properties
and density [12], several (MnFe)ePeBeC BMGs were produced by
a high-content (25e55 at.%) solvent element Mn substitution for
Fe in FeePeBeC alloy along with an enhancedTx,h a r d n e s sa n d
brittle behavior [13], Cr-rich BMGs were synthesized by a high-
content (29.4e44.1 at.%) solvent element Cr substitution for Fe
in (CrFe)eMoeCeBeY alloy as well as an elevated Tg, Tx,m e -
chanical strength and hardness [ 6]. Therefore, it can be a
considerable choice for developing new BMGs with integrated
advantages by high-content solvent element substitution.
Previously, two (CrFe)-based Cr 30Fe26Co7Mo14C15B6Y2 (at.%,
denoted as Cr30 in the following text) BMG and Cr 45Fe11Co7-
Mo14C15B6Y2 (at.%, denoted as Cr45 in the following text) BMG
with charming merits of high Tg, Tx, mechanical strength,
hardness and corrosion resistance were synthesized by the
high-content (15 and 30 at.%) solvent element Cr substitution for
Fe in the famous amorphous steel Fe
41Cr15Co7Mo14C15B6Y2 (at.%,
denoted as Cr15 in the following text) BMG [5]. Meanwhile, it has
been pointed out that the Tg, Tx, mechanical strength and
hardness of BMGs can be continuously enhanced by Cr alloying
[5,6]. Besides, BMGs with a certain content of Cr mainly possess
excellent corrosion resistance [ 1,2,5,6]. Accordingly, a higher
content of Cr-containing (CrFe)-based BMG with superiorT
g, Tx,
mechanical strength and hardness as well as excellent corrosion
resistance can be expected from the Cr15 BMG.
In this work, a novel Cr
50Fe6Co7Mo14C15B6Y2 (at.%, denoted
as Cr50 in the following text) BMG possessing integrated ad-
vantages of superior T
g, Tx, mechanical strength, hardness
and corrosion resistance was developed by high-content
(35 at.%) solvent element Cr substitution for Fe from the pre-
cursor Cr15 BMG. The glass formation, mechanical properties
and corrosion behaviors of the alloy were detailedly esti-
mated. Meanwhile, the structural origin for the prominent
thermal and mechanical properties as well as the effect of the
present high-content solvent element substitution on the GFA
for the BMGs were further revealed by ab-initio molecular dy-
namics (AIMD) simulation.

Additionally, bond pair (BP) analysis was employed to
further estimate the characteristics of the local structures for
these BMGs, which is believed as an available approach for
describing the features of the local structures of MGs [ 43]. In
this approach, four indexes of i, j, l and m are introduced to

Andersen's theory [ 43]. In details, i ¼ 1 denotes that there can
form a bond between atom A and atom B, i ¼ 2 denotes that
there can not form a bond between atom A and atom B. j is the
number of near-neighbor atoms, which can form bonds with
atom A and atom B. l represents the number of the bonds
forming among the shared neighbor atoms. m denotes the
additional index for further distinguishing the arrangement of
the bond, if the indexes of i, j and l are insufﬁcient for dis-

1431, 1421, 1422, 1441, 1661, 1311 and 1321 are mainly
employed, which denotes the perfect icosahedral ordering
structure, the distorted icosahedral ordering structure, the
distorted icosahedral ordering structure, the face-centered
cubic (FCC) ordering structure, the hexagonal closest packed
(HCP) ordering structure, the body-centered cubic (BCC)
ordering structure, the BCC ordering structure, the rhom-

symmetry ordering structure, respectively [ 43].

Interestingly, the present alloy possesses the integrated
advantages of superior Tg, Tx, sf and Hv. As is known that, the
thermal properties (such as Tg and Tx) and mechanical prop-
erties (such as sf and Hv) of MGs are closely related to their
bonding features among constituent elements. Consequently,
the electronic structures and the bonding features of the
present alloy were determined by AIMD simulation. Fig. 9
displays the charge density distribution of the present Cr50
BMG obtained by AIMD simulation. It can be seen that there is
a distinct overlap in charge density between metal element Cr
(light-steel-blue balls) and metalloid elements C (yellow balls)
and B (violet balls), and there are indistinctive overlaps in
charge density among metal elements Cr (light-steel-blue
balls), Fe (blue balls) and Mo (steel-blue balls), which means
the covalent character for Cr eC and Cr eB bonds and metallic
character for Cr eCr, Cr eFe, Cr eMo, Fe eFe, Fe eMo and
MoeMo bonds [ 13,42]. Generally, it is expected that covalent-
like bonds are the stronger bonds, which play a dominant
role in the superior properties of high T
g, Tx, strength and
hardness in MGs [ 1,2,4e6]. Accordingly, the present BMG
developed by high-content solvent element substitution pos-
sesses integrated advantages of superior Tg, Tx, sf and Hv.

3.3. Corrosion behaviors
Fig. 10 displays the potentiodynamic polarization curves for
the present Cr50 BMG and the counterpart SUS316 in 1 M HCl
solution at 298 K open to the air. It exhibits that the SUS316 is
actively dissolved at ﬁrst, then insigniﬁcantly passivated, and
ﬁnally transpassively dissolved. For comparison, the present
BMG depicts remarkable spontaneous passivation at ﬁrst,
then a followed wide passive region ( DE
pass) and the trans-
passive dissolution at last. Meanwhile, the present MG dis-
plays low passive current density ( I
pass), low corrosion current
density ( Icorr) and high corrosion potentials ( Ecorr). This in-
dicates that the present BMG developed by high-content sol-
vent element substitution possesses a more excellent
corrosion resistance in 1 M HCl solution. Besides, the corro-
sion rate in 1 M HCl solution for SUS316 is about
0.21 mm year
/C0 1, which was obtained by the weight loss after
immersion in 1 M HCl solution at 298 K open to the air with
168 h. However, the corresponding corrosion rate in 1 M HCl
solution for the present MG is lower than 0.0001 mm year /C0 1 as
no detectable weight loss, further indicating a more excellent
corrosion resistance for the present MG in comparison to
SUS316. The values for E
corr, Icorr, Ipass, DEpass and R of the Cr50
MG and the counterpart SUS316 are listed in Table 3.
It is well known that the corrosion resistance of MGs is
mainly dependent on the formed passive ﬁlm on the surface
and the glassy structure [ 1,2,5,6]. On one hand, it has been
reported that Cr and Mo are the preferable elements to form a
protective passive ﬁlm on the surfaces of MGs, which can
effectively inhibit the corrosion of the alloys in solution
caused by the harsh conditions (such as the penetration of
chloride ions) [ 5,6]. On the other hand, as is known that the
glassy structure of MGs possesses no crystal defects such as
grain boundaries which can promote the formation of corro-
sion [ 1,2]. For the present Cr50 BMG, it possesses an extraor-
dinarily high content of Cr and Mo (64 at.% in total), which is
beneﬁcial for the formation of a protective passive ﬁlm
enriched with Cr and Mo. Consequently, the present BMG
developed by high-content solvent element substitution
shows prominent corrosion resistance in 1 M HCl solution.
4. Conclusions
In this paper, a novel Cr 50Fe6Co7Mo14C15B6Y2 BMG was devel-
oped using high-content solvent element substitution by
conventional copper mold casting. The d
c of the BMG is about
1.5 mm. The alloy possesses the integrated advantages of
superior T
g of 981 K, extraordinary high Tx of 1018 K, dra-
matical high sf of 4.49 GPa and Hv of 14.01 GPa, which can be
attributed to the covalent-like bonds between metal element
Cr and metalloid elements C and B based on the AIMD simu-
lation. Meanwhile, the structural origin for the decreased GFA
of the present alloy in comparison with its precursor Fe
41-
Cr15Co7Mo14C15B6Y2 BMG is on account of the lower fraction of
icosahedral-type structures (with bond pair indexes of 1551,
1541 and 1431) along with the higher fraction of crystal-like
structures (with bond pair indexes of 1421, 1422, 1441 and
1661) based on the AIMD simulation. Additionally, the alloy
possesses a more prominent corrosion resistance than
SUS316 in 1 M HCl solution due to the characteristics of
spontaneous passivation, low passive current density, wide
passive region and no detectable weight loss after immersion
test. The present work would provide an amazing MG for in-
dustrial applications in the harsh environment of high tem-
perature, wear and corrosion.
