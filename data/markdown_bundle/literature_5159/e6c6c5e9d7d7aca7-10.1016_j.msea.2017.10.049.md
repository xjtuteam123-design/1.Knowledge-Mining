Abstract: Effect of Cr addition on the microstructure and mechanical properties of 
Al-12wt.%Si-3.5wt.%Cu-2wt.%Mn heat-resistant alloy were studied by OM , XRD and SEM 
microstructure observation and tension test at differen t temperature. Results indicate that Cr 
addition can obviously change the morphology of the primary Mn -rich phase in 
Al-12wt.%Si-3.5wt.Cu-2wt.%Mn, from slender rods to dendrites first and then to star -type 
particles. XRD and SEM -EDS results suggest that no new Cr -rich phase is formed in the studied 
alloy, and Cr is mainly dissolved in the Mn-rich phase, besides partially in the matrix. Addition of 
Cr can significantly increases st rength and elongation at room temperature and high temperature. 
When Cr addition level is at 1.0%, the strength at 350 oC reaches to the highest value of 106MPa, 
about 28% higher than that without addition of Cr. In the alloys with or without Cr addition, t he 
internal cracks originate from and propagate along the primary Mn -rich phase during tension at 
350oC. 
Key Words : Heat resistant aluminum alloy; Microstructure; High temperature strength; 
Chromium

1 Introduction 
In recent years, with the development of automobile industry, the requirements of energy -saving 
and emission-reduction become more and more rigorous. Al -Si system casting alloys have been 
widely used in automobile manufacturing because of its low density, good strength, and excellent 
casting performance, wear resistant and thermal conductivity. Various engines, as the heart of a car, 
are being almost completely made by Al-Si system casting alloys to reduce their weight [1-3]. 
Because engines are facing complicated and rigorous working environment, the materials to 
manufacture it should possess very excellent thermo-stability[4], namely high strength at high 
working temperature. Thus, Mg and Cu are usually added into Al -Si alloy to improve the high 
temperature performance by forming Mg 2Si, CuAl 2 and/or Q phase. However, those alloy s can 
only work under 200 oC, because at a higher temperature these strengthening phases are rapidly 
coarsened and hence the high temperature strength is decreased remarkably [5]. In order to further 
improve the thermos-stability of cast aluminum alloy, resear chers have been trying to find some 
new heat-resistant phases. 
As an alternative, transition elements, such as Ni and Zr, are often added to the Al-Si-Cu-Mg alloy 
to improve the high temperature strength. Hernandez-Sandov et al. [6] reported that addition of Ni 
and Zr could increase the high temperature strength of Al-Si-Cu-Mg alloy. When Ni and Zr were 
simultaneously added, the tensile strength of the alloy at 300 oC was 30% higher than that without 
addition, mainly due to the presence of the Ni -rich phase and the Al 3Zr phase. Adding Ni to the 
Al-Si-Cu-Mg alloy could make the high temperature strength at 350 oC increase to 62MPa, and 
combined with the adjustment of Cu content, the high temperature strength at 350 oC could reach 
to 93.5MPa [7-9]. It is though that the new formed Al 3CuNi phase in the alloy has good

Liao et al. [16] developed a high performance aluminum alloy for automotive engine blocks by 
adding inexpensive Mn on the basis of conventional Al -Si-Cu alloy. T he new formed Mn -rich 
phase can act as the main heat-resistant phase to improve the high temperature strength. When Mn

## Page 5

content was 1.2%, the best high temperature strength at 300 oC could reach to 125MPa. However, 
adding more amount of Mn does not increase the high temperature strength, and on the contrary, 
the strength is decreased, mainly due to the brittleness of the coarse Mn-rich phase dendrites. So , 
in order to further improve the high temperature strength, it is necessary and important to tailor the 
morphology and size of the Mn -rich phase. In this study, the effect of Cr addition on the 
microstructure and mechanical properties of Al-Si-Cu-Mn heat-resistant alloy was discussed. 
2 Experimental 
2.1 Alloy preparation 
In this work, SC0 , SC1, SC2 and SC3, four different Cr level alloys, were prepared by using 
Al-20wt.%Si, Al-20wt.%Cu, Al-10wt.%Mn and Al-10wt.%Cr master alloys and pure aluminum 
ingot as raw materials. After processing, the melt was poured at 720 oC into a plate-like iron mold 
(with a cavity of 170×100×20mm3, preheated at 250oC for 5h). The final chemical composition of 
the studied alloys was analyze d using MAXx LMF15 spark emission spectrometer, as listed in

2.2 Microstructure observation and tensile test 
Metallographic samples were cut from the castings, and were etched with a mixed acid reagent 
(20mL HCl + 20mL HNO 3 + 5mL HF + 55mL H 2O) for 15s. Microstructural observation was 
carried out using OLYMPUS BX60M metallographic microscope, scanning electron microscope

## Page 6

(SEM) equipped with EDS. The X -ray diffraction (XRD, D8 -Discover equipped with Cu -Kα 
radiation) was used to analyze phase co nstitutes with 2θ =20 90° with a scanning step of 
0.02o/step. Partial samples for microstructure observation were undergone a heat treatment of 
solutionizing at 510 oC for 5h + aging at 165 oC for 6h. The plate specimens for tension test were 
also cut from t he castings, with the gauge size of 18 33mm3. All tension specimens were 
undergone the same heat treatment as above. The tension test was carried out on CMT5105 with a 
rate of 1mm/min at room temperature, 150 oC, 250oC and 350oC, and the ultimate tension strength 
is taken as an average of 3 replicates. 
3 Results and discussion 
3.1 Microstructure 
 
Fig. 1 Optical microscopy photos of as-cast microstructure: (a) SC0, (b) SC1, (c) SC2, (d) SC3 
Fig.1 shows that addition of Cr has a significant influence on the morphology and size of primary

## Page 7

Mn-rich phase. In SC0 alloy, without Cr addition, the morphology of primary Mn -rich phase is 
long dendrites or slender rods (Fig. 1a). In SC1 alloy , with 0.5wt.%Cr addition, the formation of 
the slender rods is completely suppressed, and the size of the primary Mn -rich phase dendrites is 
reduced sharply (Fig. 1b). When Cr addition is 1.0wt%, the primary Mn -rich phase presents as 
star-like or particle -like (Fig.1c), and at the same time, the size of it become s smaller. However, 
further increasing Cr addition to 1.5wt%, the size of the primary Mn -rich phase become s larger 
(Fig.1d). With Cr addition, the volume fraction of the primary Mn -rich phase is also increased. It 
is interesting to note that no new Cr -rich phase is observed even in SC3 alloy with 1.5wt.% Cr 
addition. Cr and Mn belong to the same group element with very similar physical and chemical 
properties, so they can interchange in most intermetallic compounds to a certain extent [17-19]. 
Fig.2 Optical microscopy photos: (a) SC0 after T6 treatment, (b) SC2 after T6 treatment 
Fig.2 shows the microstructure of SC0 and SC2 after T6 treatment. The eutectic Si particles 
become spherical after solution treatment at 510 oC. The eutectic CuAl 2 phase, formed during 
solidification, is found to be partially dissolved into the Al matrix after solutionizing, as shown in 
the inserted photo at upper right corner of Fig. 2a. But, the morphology and size of primary 
Mn-rich phase did not change, indicating that the thermal stability of the primary Mn -rich phase is 
much better than CuAl2 phase and it can be used as the main heat-resistant strengthening phase.

From Fig. 4, it is seen that the peaks of Al, Si, CuAl 2 and Mn -rich phase s still exist after T6

with the result in Ref.16. This is mainly due to CuAl 2 phase partially dissolving into the Al matrix

Fig.7 SEM Mapping of Al, Si, Cu, Mn and Cr elements in the primary Mn-rich phase in SC2 alloy 
Fig.7 shows the SEM -Mapping of Al, Si, Cu, Mn and Cr elements in the primary Mn -rich 
phase in SC2 alloy. Al is in all zones of the alloy and its content in the matrix is the highest. Si is 
mainly distributed in the eutectic region and primary Mn -rich phase. Cu is mainly distributed in 
the final solidified eutectic region, and some Cu dissolves in the matrix. A great amount of Cr is 
distributed in the primary Mn -rich phase and small amount of Cr is dissolved in the matrix. Mn 
almost distributes in the Mn-rich phase. Where Mn exists, Cr does. This phenomenon is consistent 
with the relationship between Cr, Fe and Mn in Al -Si-Cu alloy reported by Fabrizi et al. [20]. The 
above results by XRD, OM, SEM -EDS and SEM -Mapping indicate that Cr addition in 
Al-Si-Cu-Mn alloy is mainly dissolved in to the Mn-rich phase to replace Mn and the other is in 
the matrix, which may be the reason for variation of the morphology of primary Mn -rich phase. It 
also indicates addition of Cr in the studied alloys does not form new Cr-rich phase.

4 Conclusions 
(1) Addition of Cr in Al -12wt.%Si-3.5wt.%Cu-2wt.%Mn alloy can remarkably change the 
morphology of the primary Mn -rich phase, from slender rods to dendrites first and then star -type 
particles, as well as increasing the volume fraction of Mn-rich phase. 
(2) Cr addition in the alloy is mainly dissolved into the primary Mn-rich phase and the other is in 
the matrix. No new Cr-rich phase is formed in the alloy with Cr addition. 
(3) Addition of Cr significantly improved the room temperature and high temperature strength and 
elongation of the alloy. When Cr addition level is at 1.0%, the alloy has the maximum strength at 
room temperature and high temperature, with UTS up to 279MPa and 106MPa at room 
temperature and 350oC respectively. This is mainly due to the morphology change of the primary 
Mn-rich phase and higher thermal stability of the primary Mn-rich phase induced by Cr addition. 
(4) In the alloys with or without Cr addition, the internal cracks originate from and propagate 
along the primary Mn-rich phase during tension at 350oC.
