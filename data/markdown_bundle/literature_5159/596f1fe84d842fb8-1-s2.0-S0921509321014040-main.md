1. Introduction 
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) and medium-entropy alloys (MEAs), 
particularly face-centered cubic (FCC) structures, have recently drawn 
considerable attention owing to their superior properties at cryogenic 
and room temperatures [ 1 – 3 ]. Numerous studies have focused on the 
CoCrFeNiMn, CoCrFeNi, and CoCrNi single-phase FCC alloys because of 
their excellent ductility, high work-hardening rates, and high fracture 
toughness [ 4 – 7 ]. However, low yield strength is an important limiting 
factor for engineering applications. Thus, improving the yield strength 
of FCC HEAs and MEAs is crucial. Among the aforementioned alloys, the 
CoCrNi MEA alloy possesses low stacking fault energy (~22 mJ/m
2 
[ 8 ]), 
leading to the formation of deformation twins at the early stage during 
plastic deformation [ 9 , 10 ]. Therefore, the CoCrNi MEA exhibits excel -
lent work-hardening ability and ductility. CoCrNi series MEAs are usu -
ally chosen as matrix alloys to improve their yield strength because of a 
large ductility reserve.

effective strengthening media because of their strong interaction with 
dislocations. The γ

3.2. Criterion for σ phase formation in HEAs/MEAs 
The σ phase is a TCP phase that lacks multiple-slip systems. It can 
improve the strength and hardness of alloys while significantly reducing 
their ductility. Therefore, the σ phases in superalloy and stainless steel 
are considered harmful. In the HEAs and MEAs with high Cr content, σ 
phase can form at annealing temperatures ranging from 500 
◦
C to 
1000 
◦
C [ 20 , 25 – 27 ]. However, nanoscale and submicron-scale σ parti -
cles have been shown to effectively enhance the strength of HEAs and 
MEAs without a substantial reduction in ductility. Therefore, σ particles 
can be chosen as an effective strengthening phase, and exploring the 
formation of the σ phase is valuable. 
The prediction of σ is of crucial importance to the design of HEAs/ 
MEAs. Although high Cr content is the key factor influencing the for -
mation of the σ phase, other factors have to be considered as well. The 
appearance of the σ phase is difficult to predict based on the content of 
constituent elements. Tsai et al. proposed that valence electron con -
centration (VEC) was directly related to the formation of the σ phase in 
Cr-containing HEAs [ 28 ]. They concluded that the HEAs with VEC be -
tween 6.88 and 7.84 tended to form σ phase in the as-cast state or in the 
annealed state. Moreover, Stepanov et al. [ 29 , 30 ] studied CoCrFe -

Mo (or W) and Cr are always enriched in TCP phases, suggesting that Mo 
and W are the strong TCP-forming elements. Nonetheless, in their 
studies, the test HEAs contained no Mo or W. The TCP-forming ability of 
the main constituent elements (Fe, Co, Cr, Ni, Mn, and Al) was weaker 
than that of Mo or W. Therefore, the range of the σ -forming VEC should 
be wider for HEAs containing Mo and/or W. Future research should 
focus on this point. Md, a d-electron parameter, is the d-orbital energy 
level of an alloying transition metal. It correlates with the charge 
transfer. When M atom and N atom combine to form a molecule, M atom 
with high energy level loses electrons (week electronegativity) and N 
atom with low energy level gets the electrons (strong electronegativity). 
It can be seen that Md is closely related to the electronegativity. 
Furthermore, Md is also affected by atom radius. Therefore, Md can be 
used to predict the phase stability. Md was successfully applied to 
distinguish the FCC SS (solid solution) and the FCC SS + TCP /GCP

selected mostly because of its successful application in forecasting the 
formation of the σ phase after the aging treatment in Ni-based or 
Co-based superalloys [ 33 ]. As superalloys always contain refractory el -

The nucleation sites of the σ phase can be summarized as follows: 
grain boundaries, twin boundaries, and grain interior in the recrystal -
lized grain region, {111} slip planes, and grain interior in the non- 
recrystallized grain region. The σ phase tends to choose defects as 
nucleation sites, rather than the FCC matrix with a perfect lattice 
structure, which selection is attributable to the low activation energy at 
the defects. In this study, the different defects, listed by activation en -
ergy in ascending order, were as follows: grain boundaries < twin 
boundaries < dislocations [ 40 ]. The σ phase could more easily precip -
itate at the grain and twin boundaries. Notably, the {111} planes were 
among the important positions for the nucleation of the σ phase. A 
similar phenomenon was observed in the FeCrNi alloy [41] and 
CoCrNi-based MEA [ 37 ], but the reason was unclear. Slone et al. [ 37 ] 
found that the {111} plane σ phase was only formed in alloys that un -
derwent direct aging after cold rolling. In fully recrystallized alloys, no 
{111} plane σ phase was found. The difference between the 
non-recrystallized and fully recrystallized allows was that the {111} 
planes in the former had high-density dislocations, whereas those in the 
latter had low-density ones. The reason of the formation of the {111} 
plane σ phase is as follows: After cold rolling, high-density dislocations 
formed on {111} planes. Dislocations can cause lattice distortion in a 
specific area on one side of slip planes. This distortion disappears when 
all dislocations slip to the alloy surface. However, the existence of 
high-density dislocations severely damages the perfect {111} plane 
structure in the non-recrystallized alloy. The {111} planes thus resemble 
coherent interfaces with high distortion energy, which can substantially 
reduce the activation energy. Meanwhile, solute atoms tend to accu -
mulate near dislocations. Therefore, a low activation energy and 
compositional fluctuation contribute to the formation of the σ phase on 
{111} planes. The formation mechanism of the intragranular σ phase 
measuring 10 – 20 nm in both regions is yet undetermined. Whether the 
{111} planes or dislocations, or both, are the sites of σ phase formation 
has not been concluded. No evidence of the presence of fine σ particles in 
non-recrystallized grains has been reported. Further research should 
focus on this point in the future. The precipitation behavior can help us 
to understand the distribution of the σ phase at different annealing 
temperatures. At 850 
◦
C, the σ phase preferentially formed and grew at 
the grain boundaries and twin boundaries. Inside the recrystallized 
grains, the σ phase was difficult to form and grow due to the weak 
diffusion capacity. It seems that there is a “ σ -free ” region (few σ partilces 
exist) inside the recrystallized grains. The improvement of diffusion 
capacity leads to the increase of the volume fraction and size of the σ 
phase at 850 
◦
C and 900 
◦

uniform.

4. Conclusion 
In this study, the evolution of the microstructure and mechanical 
properties of the Co
34.5
Cr
30
Ni
26.5
Al
5.4
W
3.6 
HEA was analyzed. The cri -
terion for σ formation was proposed. The mechanisms underlying σ 
precipitation and sluggish recrystallization were revealed. The key fac -
tors influencing mechanical properties were identified based on the 
strength mechanism. The main conclusions were as follows: 
(1) After cold rolling at 75%, partially recrystallized microstructures 
were formed by annealing at 700 
◦
C, 800 
◦
C, and 850 
◦
C for 1 h. 
Fully recrystallized microstructures were obtained after anneal -
ing at 900 
◦
C. σ particles were found at the {111} slip planes, 
grain boundaries, twin boundaries, and grain interior in partially 
recrystallized alloys. Md could well predict the formation of the σ 
phase, and the critical value ranged from 0.902 to 0.908. 
(2) Sluggish recrystallization is attributable to the σ phase and slow 
diffusion. On the one hand, σ particles hinder dislocation motion 
(recovery) and the migration of grain boundaries (recrystalliza -
tion). On the other hand, the slow diffusion caused by Al and W 
atoms can reduce the dislocation climb rate (recovery) and in -
crease the recrystallization resistance (recrystallization). 
(3) The yield strength decreases with an increase in annealing tem -
peratures. The volume fraction of non-recrystallized grains is the 
most important factor in the evaluation of the control of strength. 
The volume fraction and the size of σ particles are also important 
factors to consider. Partially recrystallized alloys have a lower 
work hardening rate, compared with fully recrystallized alloys 
because of the absence of deformation twins.
