ABSTRACT
We proposed an additively manufactured equiatomic CoNiV multicomponent alloy (MCA) using a 
conventional laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) method, and an exceptional strength-ductility 
synergy of the alloy was attained through a simple post-ageing treatment. Pronounced hierar -
chical microstructures were achieved in our printed alloys, including heterogeneous grain 
structures, and intragranular cellular structures composed of interior domain with limited dis -
locations and cell walls led by significant vanadium local segregation. Besides the outstanding 
mechanical properties at room temperature of 298 K, a giga-pascal yielding strength ( > 1.1 GP) 
and over 40% uniform elongation were attained in the aged specimen deformed at a cryogenic 
temperature of 77 K, predominating the mechanical properties of many alloys reported in pre -
vious works. Such exceptional performance of the aged alloy can be mainly ascribed to consid -
erable local chemical orders (LCOs), aggravated elemental fluctuation in the alloy matrix, and 
intensified vanadium segregation at walls of intragranular cellular structures which can strongly 
interact with dislocations. As a result, a planar slip array of dislocations with an extremely high 
density, namely large numbers of slip bands that can sustain and transfer high strains, dominates 
the deformation microstructures, thus efficiently strengthening and toughening the aged alloy, 
especially at a low temperature like 77 K. The above post-ageing strategy is readily and low-costly 
employed on additively manufactured MCAs with relatively high stacking fault energy (SFE) and 
proved as a feasible method to produce high-performance structural materials for extreme 
conditions.

1. Introduction
Multicomponent alloy (MCA, or called high/medium entropy alloy) with three or more primary elements, as a new class of metallic 
materials, has attracted lots of attention during the past ten years, due to their superior properties (e.g., high strength, desirable 
corrosion resistance, exceptional magnetic performance, etc.) over many traditional alloys with only one or two primary elements 
( Amar et al., 2023 ; Han et al., 2022 ; Huang et al., 2024b ; Wang et al., 2023 ; Wu et al., 2021a ; Zeng et al., 2022 ). In particular, many of 
the as-being MCA systems were reported to exhibit mechanical properties with excellent strength-ductility synergy at a wide tem -
perature regime, e.g., from room temperature to cryogenic temperature (like 77 K in a liquid nitrogenic environment) ( Cheng et al., 
2024 ; Gludovatz et al., 2014 ; Tong et al., 2019 ). For instance, a giga-pascal level of yield strength can be readily attained in a CoCrNi 
MCA at room and cryogenic temperature, while maintaining a significantly high fracture toughness over 275 MPa m
1/2

as dislocation slip, mechanical twinning, and displacive martensite transformation especially in the MCAs with relatively low stacking 
fault energy, as reported in many experimental and simulation studies ( Eleti et al., 2020 ; Gan et al., 2023 ; Niu et al., 2024 ; Zhang et al., 
2021 ). Moreover, special dislocation behaviors different from those in conventional alloys are also found in several MCAs, facilitating 
to attainment of desirable mechanical properties of such alloys ( Chen et al., 2023 ; Ma, 2020 ). Hence, it is readily deduced that the 
strength and ductility of MCAs are highly dependent upon their micro-/nano-structures ( Chen et al., 2021 ), and understanding their 
structure-property relations enables more rational strengthening-and-toughening design strategies via tuning the alloy 
microstructures.
Laser additive manufacturing (AM) is a recently emerging methodology to produce metallic parts used in aerospace and vehicle 
engineering ( Kurnsteiner et al., 2020 ; Zeng et al., 2020 ). The layer-by-layer building method using micro-sized pre-alloying powders 
via precise control of laser enables the metallic parts of geometric design of complicated configurations, as well as the low-cost but 
efficient processing ( Jiang et al., 2020 ; Niu et al., 2021 ). Among all AM techniques, the laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) AM technique 
exhibits overwhelming advantages over conventional manufacturing processes when producing metallic parts ( Kumar et al., 2023 ; Niu 
et al., 2024 ). Due to its rapid heating/cooling cycles, this technique possesses great potential in manufacturing complex-shaped parts 
with ultrafine grain sizes benefiting the improved strength for extreme service conditions ( Lee et al., 2022 ). Significantly, such rapid 
heating/cooling cycles during the layer-by-layer building are found to commonly cause a pronounced temperature gradient within the 
printed materials. As a result, hierarchical microstructures, involving multi-level grain structures, second-phase precipitates, and 
sub-grained dislocation structures, etc., are generated in the LPBF-produced metallic materials ( Liu et al., 2024 ; Wan et al., 2022 ). 
Accordingly, significant back stress is induced by geometrically necessary dislocations (GNDs) to accommodate the deformation 
heterogeneities of each component from such hierarchical microstructures, which can highly increase the strength of the printed 
materials ( Kok et al., 2018 ; Liu et al., 2018 ). For instance, Qin et al. introduced a high density of coherent nanoparticles into the 
multi-level grain structures of a Cu-based complex concentrated alloy produced using laser AM printing, which led to the yield strength 
being increased by over 300 MPa compared with the cast counterpart without significant ductility loss ( Qin et al., 2024 ). Moreover, 
heterogeneous grain structures with hierarchical precipitates composed of the B2, L1
2, 
and sigma phases were produced in an 
AM-printed face-centered cubic FeNiCoCrAl alloy, enabling a high yield strength of ~700 MPa at room temperature while ~1100 MPa 
in a liquid nitrogen environment ( Chu et al., 2024 ). Therefore, the LBPF-produced alloys usually exhibit much higher strength than 
their cast counterparts, as widely reported in lots of works ( Wang et al., 2022 ; Xiao et al., 2022 ; Zhang et al., 2024 ). Till now, the laser 
AM-printing method has been successfully used to produce lots of structural materials with high strength and good deformability, 
including Ni-based superalloy ( Zhu et al., 2023 ), steel ( Tan et al., 2021 ), as well as MCAs ( Kumar et al., 2023 ; Liu et al., 2023 ). 
Therefore, advanced AM techniques can help to realize more applications of MCAs in engineering.
We should notice that due to the metastability nature of the compositional complex solid solution, structural and/or chemical short- 
range orders (or called local chemical orders, LCOs) can be readily generated in the matrix of MCAs after long-time ageing treatment at 
medium temperatures or slow cooling followed by annealing ( Ding et al., 2018 ; Xu et al., 2024 ; Zhang et al., 2020 ). Such LCOs are of 
atomic size with the nanoscale, which can be hardly observed via normal electron microscopic characterization ( Hart et al., 2023 ; Wu, 
2023 ), while commonly affecting the mechanical performance of MCAs a lot ( Chen et al., 2021 ; Yin et al., 2020b ). For example, it is 
reported that the presence of LCOs can further enhance the strength and ductility of CoCuFeNiPd MCAs because such LCOs lead to a 
pseudo-composite microstructure which could enhance both the ultimate strength and ductility ( Chen et al., 2021 ). Additionally, 
prominent LCO strengthening was also found in a boron-doped non-equimolar FeMnCoCr MCA by introducing high lattice contraction 
at cryogenic temperatures, which significantly uprates the potential of MCAs for real applications ( Seol et al., 2020 ). Therefore, it can 
be deduced that the comprehensive mechanical properties of LPBF-printed MCAs can be improved when considerable LCOs are 
introduced to the alloy matrix by using rational post-ageing treatment at some elevated temperature. Besides, such an ageing can 
partly release the residual stress of the as-printed alloys, which benefits the quality of these metallic materials ( Niu et al., 2024 ). 
However, this attempt has been rarely made in LPBF-printed MCAs till now, and the theoretical correlation between their aged mi -
crostructures and final mechanical response is lacking, either (especially the effect of LCOs, rather than aged-induced nano-sized 
precipitates in the above-mentioned examples of AM-produced Cu-based complex alloy and FeNiCoCrAl MCA).
Motivated by the above discussion, we proposed a post-ageing treatment on an equiatomic CoNiV MCA produced using a typical 
LPBF-AM method in this work. The equiatomic CoNiV alloy fabricated via traditional casting and thermomechanical routes was found 
to exhibit high tensile strength combined with high ductility at wide temperature regimes, e.g., with high yield strength from over 
gigapascal at a cryogenic temperature of 77 K ( Xu et al., 2024 ) to ~930 MPa at ~1173 K ( An et al., 2023 ), as well as high anti-corrosion 
ability, rendering it a candidate alloy used for structural materials at extreme conditions ( Luo et al., 2020 ; Park et al., 2021 ; Xu et al., 
2024 ). Moreover, over giga-pascal strength has been attained in this CoNiV alloy produced by the laser melting deposition (LMD)

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructural features of the as-printed and aged MCA specimens
The initial microstructural features of the as-printed MCA specimens were first investigated using the SEM characterization 
method. Fig. 2 a shows the 3-D inverse pole figures (IPFs) of the as-printed specimen taken from the three planes. It can be seen that the

perpendicular to the building direction (BD), it is found that the larger-size equiaxed grains encircled by finer grains with random

Moreover, cellular structures are widely found in AM-produced alloys, and their origins are quite controversial but often thought of 
as related to the extreme thermal cycles and rapid solidification of AM. According to the solidification model of single-phase MCAs 
where alloyed powders first melt and then form a molten pool during laser scanning ( Liu et al., 2024 ), inevitable Marangoni effects 
triggered by remarkably sharp temperature gradient lead to complicated fluid convection in molten pools and cause an initial 
configuration of grain seeds ( Cai et al., 2019 ; Kok et al., 2018 ). Solid solution of the MCA nucleates at the bottom of the molten pools 
where the temperature reaches liquidus, motivated by supercooling and energetic fluctuations, and the solid interfaces of such nuclei 
experience a process of supercooling, migrating towards the melt pool, and the grains grow and cease upon contact with others ( Liu 
et al., 2024 ; Niu et al., 2021 ). Inside the solidified grains, the formation of dislocation-structured cellular structures is thought to come 
from the high residual stress caused by thermal expansion/shrinkage after solidification ( Kong et al., 2021 ; Li et al., 2021 ). If the 
residual stress that accommodates the localized expansion or contraction misfit is high enough to render plastic deformation, dislo -
cations could accumulate and self-assemble into cellular patterns with relatively low energy and might overlap with the pre-solidified 
dendrites, which can also interpret the case where dislocation cellular structures are rarely found in alloys with very high yield strength 
( Liu et al., 2018 ; Wang et al., 2020 ). Moreover, influenced by multiple and complicated thermal effects, further decomposition takes 
place within individual grains with subsequent elemental micro-segregation due to the different electronegativities and cohesions 
among primary elements ( Kong et al., 2021 ; Liu et al., 2024 ). Due to the lowest electronegativity of V which undergoes the greatest 
degree of electron loss ( Shi et al., 2024 ; Yin et al., 2020a ), the attraction of V atoms to the rest two atoms is comparatively weaker 
compared to atoms of other elements, therefore some of the V elements are accordingly excluded from the initial CoNiV matrix and 
local segregation forms around the previous region, which is quite similar to the case of Ti in the AM-produced TiNbTa-based MCA ( Liu 
et al., 2024 ). Additionally, such elemental segregation can be also promoted by in-being dislocations, similar to the so-called pipe 
diffusion mechanism ( Fan et al., 2022 ; Love, 1964 ), which facilitates the V-rich cell walls bundled with dislocation segments and 
would be aggravated via heat treatment at elevated temperatures. From the above results, it can be clarified that during the 
elevated-temperature treatment, the microstructures of the present MCA are significantly altered, i.e., the improved local atomic 
segregation promotes higher V concentration inside walls of cell structures, meanwhile distinct LCOs are generated in the alloy matrix.

3.3.3. Back stress from the hierarchical microstructures of the present MCA specimens
It is well-recognized that in deformed materials with hierarchical microstructures, strain gradient-induced geometrically necessary 
dislocations (GNDs) occur near the interphase boundaries including GBs, phase boundaries, and twin boundaries to accommodate the 
deformation incompatibility among soft and hard domains ( Gan et al., 2023 ; Lemoine et al., 2016 ; Wu et al., 2024 ). The local 
accumulation of such GNDs, e.g., dislocation pile-ups near GBs, can induce significant internal stress, i.e., the well-known back stress to 
hinder the further propagation and movement of trailing dislocations nearby, leading to the pronounced strengthening effect of hi -
erarchical microstructures ( Ding et al., 2023 , 2022 ; Evers, 2004 ). Different from the stress required locally for a dislocation to move 
(short-range interactions like lattice friction and forest hardening) ( Feaugas, 1999 ), back stress is elastic long-range internal stress 
produced by GNDs tending to locally accumulate at barriers or obstacles against mobile dislocations, accordingly obstructing further 
deformation and increasing the global flow stress ( Hansen et al., 2020 ). Back stress is usually very small in conventional homogeneous 
polycrystalline materials, and the observed mechanical properties in these materials can be adequately explained with the listed 
mechanisms from textbooks, involving GB hardening, solution hardening, dispersive-precipitation hardening, etc. ( Zhu and Wu, 
2019 ). Although the back stress in homogenous materials was indeed measured to be very low, it turns much more pronounced in 
materials with heterogeneous structures due to the existence of hard and soft domains where GNDs are readily generated in the 
interface regions between the domains with significant strain partitioning ( Hansen et al., 2020 ; Wu et al., 2020 ; Yang et al., 2016 ). As a 
consequence, the strength of the heterogenous materials is commonly enhanced compared with their homogeneous counterparts, due 
to the back stress strengthening, such as 316 L stainless steel ( Lei et al., 2021 ), copper ( Liu et al., 2016 ), and Al
0.1
CoCrFeNi MCA ( Pan

segments in the matrix, are suppressed when the alloy is subjected to cryogenic tensile deformation. Instead, planar slip turns to govern 
the dislocation behaviors in the aged specimens which exhibit much higher strength than the as-printed ones, especially when 
deformed at a low temperature of 77 K. Theoretically, the occurrence of thriving planar slip in face-centered cubic (FCC)-structured 
crystals can be mainly attributed to three key parameters:
(i) The SFE value. It is widely reported that a reduced SFE leads to more extended dislocations with a reduced ability of screw 
dislocations to cross-slip onto other slip planes ( Gerold and Karnthaler, 1989 ; Karnthaler and Schügerl, 1979 ; Rao et al., 2020 ; 
Shen et al., 2024 ). Significantly, the SFE value can be reduced with the lowered temperature, as supported by numerous 
experimental observations and theoretical calculations ( Chen et al., 2022 ; Dong et al., 2023 ; Fu et al., 2021 ; He et al., 2021b ). 
Therefore, it is seen that dislocation planar slip is highly activated in the MCA specimens deformed at cryogenic temperature 
(even in the as-printed one).
(ii) The friction stress original from solute atoms against dislocation movement ( Gerold and Karnthaler, 1989 ; Rao et al., 2020 ). It is 
proposed that solute atoms in the alloy matrix can efficiently affect the difficulty of cross slip, due to the dissociated partials of a 
screw dislocation containing edge components which are significantly influenced by solute atoms ( N ¨ohring and Curtin, 2018 ; 
Wang et al., 2018a ). Therefore, a cross-slip of such dislocation can hardly be activated since its dissociated partials must first 
join by overcoming the friction stress imposed by the solute atoms. For the aged specimen, the local EDS analysis (e.g., see 
Fig. 9 i) on different regions suggests the pronounced fluctuation of primary elements, which is said to improve the friction stress

(iii) The occurrence of LCOs (or short-range orders) in solid solutions or even that of ordered precipitates precipitation-hardened in 
alloys ( He et al., 2021a ; Pekin et al., 2018 ). Early, it was found that LCOs can significantly promote the planar slip of dislo -
cations, via a glide plane softening effect ( Welsch et al., 2016 ). In this case, the first dislocation moving through destroys the 
LCOs, leading to higher resistance against slip than all others, since such LCOs of a short-range can hardly store dislocations. 
Then others following this first dislocation push against it to overcome the high resistance, when a dislocation source is acti -

orientations in the aged specimen with considerable LCOs in the matrix, as shown in our TEM images.
Given the above issue, the strengthening and toughening effects of the aged MCA specimen can be summarized as follows 
(schematically shown in Fig. 11 ):
(i) Local elemental segregation is promoted via ageing treatment, causing the interface boundaries (IBs) between the cellular 
structures and alloy matrix, namely cell walls with a higher concentration of vanadium. Such altered chemical composition 
increases the chemical misfit between the cell walls and alloy matrix, strengthening the IBs and enhancing their capability of 
pinning dislocation on deformation ( He et al., 2023 ; Ma et al., 2024 ). As a result, the generation of dislocation pipe-ups is 
further facilitated at such cell walls, leading to a higher back stress level from higher-density GNDs against plastic flow and 
increasing the strength of the alloy ( Yang et al., 2016 ).
(ii) As mentioned above, severe local fluctuation of primary elements is attained in the aged specimen, which leads to higher 
friction stress against mobile dislocations, and accordingly increases the strength of the alloy ( Ding et al., 2019 ). This also 
suppresses the joint of partial dislocations and hinders the occurrence of dislocation cross-slip events, which may cause stress 
localization at places where dislocations are concentrated, namely local hardening of the deformed material ( Gan et al., 2023 ).
(iii) Considerable LCOs are produced in the aged alloy, which prominently promotes the planar slip of dislocations on typical (111) 
crystalline planes of FCC matrix (as identified by TEM diffraction pattern, see Fig. S9 of the Supplemental Materials). Especially

at the cryogenic temperature where the SFE value of the present MCA is further reduced, the cross-slip activities are highly 
suppressed, thus planer slip almost dominates the plastic deformation of the aged specimens. Unlike some previously reported 
MCAs with lower SFE values, where DTs or displacive martensite transformation are triggered to simultaneously enhance the 
strength and ductility of the alloys at low temperatures ( He et al., 2019 ; Wang et al., 2018b ), an ocean of multiple planar slips at 
different planes has been activated in the parent alloys to sustain the plastic flow, which produces dense dislocation networks 
composed of intersecting slip bands fully occupying the alloy matrix. A large number of sessile structures, e.g., tangles and jogs 
(which are much more in the aged specimen due to the denser slip bands with higher probability to intersect each other) are 
generated via dislocation reactions, leading to high short-range resistance against mobile dislocations and then increasing the 
flow stress ( Evers, 2004 ; Gan et al., 2023 ).
(iv) Moreover, it is found that the presence of LCOs can lower the energy barrier of dislocation nucleation at external stress but 
extend their nucleation period, which facilitates the generation of more dislocation sources for plastic deformation (namely 
benefiting the ductility of the specimen) and also increases the yield stress of the alloy ( Ma et al., 2022 ). Accordingly, the 
strength-ductility synergy of the aged MCA is improved.
At last, we should notice that the strengthening contribution from each microstructural component, e.g., GBs, cellular structures, 
dislocations, and SFs can be hardly given by using the classic relation composed of the lattice friction, Hall-Petch equation ( Hall, 1951 ), 
as well as Taylor dislocation hardening relation ( Taylor, 1934 ) widely employed in a lot of previous works ( Hu et al., 2023 ; Niu et al., 
2023 ). As illustrated in Fig. 10 , pronounced back-stress hardening occurs in the present MCA during tensile deformation, suggesting 
the presence of significant strain heterogeneity due to the nature of hierarchical microstructures in the LBPF-printed alloys ( Singh 
et al., 2022 ; Wang et al., 2022 ). The above-mentioned classic relations to depicting the strengthening from individual microstructural 
components are mainly original from the mean-field assumption, which is not available for the cases where the strain heterogeneities 
or strain gradients predominantly come into play since the configuration of dislocation arrays can significantly affect the strengthening 
( Gan et al., 2023 ; Hahner and Zaiser, 1999 ). Besides, a pronounced gap between the experimental result and theoretical prediction 
using the model combined with the above-mentioned constituent equations always exists, and some of them are said to originate from 
residual stresses caused by thermal cycles of laser printing ( Yang et al., 2022 ). Moreover, due to the special cell boundaries with 
significant V local segregation which can efficiently trap dislocations and facilitate the generation of pipe-ups, the contribution from 
such cellular structures should be considered when using the Hall-Petch relation to evaluate the grain/sub-grain size effects on 
strengthening. However, the essential parameters describing the strengthening value correlated with the average sub-grain size are 
lacking, which need to be further clarified using nano-micro experiments probed on single crystal only containing cellular structures in 
the future and cannot be simply replaced by the conventional ones previously used for normal grains. Therefore, a forced fitting via 
using the experimental data under the mean-field theoretical framework to estimate the strengthening contribution seems not rational 
here. We should also notice that till now multiscale modeling and simulation have been rapidly developed to accurately clarify the 
mechanical response of heterogeneous metals correlated with their compositionally and microstructurally complicated features, 
including these previous works conducted through experimentally validated crystal plasticity finite element method (CPFEM) 
( Chakrabarty et al., 2023 ; Geng and Harrison, 2020 ; Kapoor et al., 2018 ), continuous or discrete dislocation dynamics (CDD or DDD) 
simulation ( Sudmanns et al., 2023 ; Upadhyay, 2020 ; Zhang et al., 2022 ) and molecular dynamics (MD) simulation ( Babuska et al., 
2019 ; Peng et al., 2023 ; Zeng et al., 2021 ). However, due to the limited model sizes (i.e., the well-known size effect) of in-being 
simulation methods and unclear physical intrinsic of special microstructures (like LCOs and elemental segregation-dominant cell 
boundaries here) responsible for precise constituent relations of plasticity, the gaps between theoretical prediction and experimental 
data are unfortunately inevitable. Nevertheless, with unremitting endeavors in the community of material science, deformation 
mechanisms and strengthening contributions from each structural component of the present complex MCAs can be quantitively 
clarified by using a modified multiscale simulation framework in future works.
4. Conclusions
In this work, we proposed an additively manufactured equiatomic CoNiV multicomponent alloy, and significantly improved the 
strength and ductility of the alloy using a simple post-ageing treatment. A giga-pascal yielding strength and over 40% uniform 
elongation were attained in the aged specimen deformed at a cryogenic temperature of 77 K, predominating the mechanical properties 
of many alloys reported before. Such exceptional performance is mainly ascribed to the generation of local chemical orders, aggravated 
elemental fluctuation in the alloy matrix, and intensified vanadium segregation at walls of intragranular cellular structures. As a result, 
a planar slip array of dislocations with extremely high density dominates the deformation microstructures in the aged specimen, 
strengthening and toughening the alloy, especially at a low temperature. The above strategy is readily conducted on additively 
manufactured MCAs with relatively high stacking fault energy, which can provide an alternative method to produce high-performance 
structural materials serving at extreme conditions.
