Microstructure and room temperature
mechanical properties of hypereutectic Nb–Si
based alloy processed by directional
solidification
S. N. Yuan, L. N. Jia, L. M. Ma, H. Jiang and H. Zhang *
In this investigation, Nb–16Si–24Ti–4Cr–2Al–2Hf alloy was directionally solidified and then heat
treated, and the microstructure, fracture toughness KQ and tensile strength sb of the alloy were
studied. The directionally solidified microstructure consisted of primary Nb 5Si3 blocks and
NbSSzNb5Si3 eutectic cells. The cross section of eutectic cells changed from the square
morphology to the nearly round morphology with the increasing withdrawal rate. During heat
treatment, the boundaries of the round eutectic cells were eliminated as the Nb SS connected to be
a net-like structure. The values of KQ and sb increased with the increase of withdrawal rate. The
toughening from the crack deflection of continuous Nb ss network was significant and refinement
of the microstructure improved the tensile strength in heat treated samples.
Keywords: Nb–Si based alloy, Directional solidification, Heat treatment, Microstructure, Tensile strength, Fracture toughness
Introduction
With the development of aeronautic and astronautic
technologies, the ultra high temperature structural mate-
rials whose operating temperature exceeds that of conven-
tional nickel based superalloy are required. Niobium
silicides are currently used for ultra high temperature
applications, with the advantages of notably the high
melting point, moderate density, high stiffness, and good
strength at high temperatures.
1,2
Nb–Si based alloys contain the Nb solid solution phase
(NbSS) and brittle intermetallic phases (Nb 5Si3,N b 3Si,
Ti5Si3,T i3Si and NbCr2). Niobium has good ductility and
fracture toughness at room temperature, while the Nb
Silicides (Nb
5Si3) could provide high temperature creep
and oxidation resistance. Moreover, Nb–Si system alloys
have a wide Nb
5Si3 and Nb solid solution two-phase
region; therefore, eutectic and eutectoid reactions offer a
variation of microstructures to prepare Nb–Si based
alloys. As Chan et al.3 suggested, the fracture toughness
and creep strength (at 1200 uC) of Nb–Si based alloys
(Nbss/Nb5Si3) were computed as a function of volume
fraction of silicides. As the volume fraction increased, the
creep strength increased, and, on the contrary, the
fracture toughness decreased. In addition, the size, mor-
phology and orientation of individual phases also play an
important role to enhance the mechanical properties.
How to achieve a balance of the creep strength and the
fracture toughness has become of interest for high-
temperature structural applications.
In order to solve that problem, there are two types of
methods at present, which are alloying and processing
technologies.
4,5 Beneﬁcial alloying elements, such as Ti,
Cr, Al and Hf, were added. 6,7 Without changing the
phase constituents of the as cast alloy, the Ti (24 at-%)
and Hf (2 at-%) additions altered the morphologies by
generating a great amount of Nbss dendrites.
6 The
additions of Cr and Al could cause the direct precipita-
tion of Nb 5Si3 from the liquid alloy. 6,7 Compared with
the methods of vacuum non-consumable arc melting
(VCAM), induction skull melting (ISM) and power
metallurgy processing, the directional solidiﬁcation (DS)
could improve the room temperature toughness and
high temperature strength of Nb–Si based alloy.
2 And
the post heat treatment could eliminate the segregation
in component, optimise the DS microstructure and
improve the mechanical performance.
Based on that, a hypereutectic Nb–Si based alloy [Nb–
16Si–24Ti–4Cr–2Al–2Hf (at-%)] was adopted in this
investigation. The samples were manufactured by liquid-
metal-cooled directional solidiﬁcation method (LMC)
8,9
in self-contributed crucibles 10,11 at variable withdrawal
rates. The morphological characteristics and mechanical
properties with different processing factors were investi-
gated. The relationship between microstructure and
property was discussed to explore the ways of improving
the microstructures and mechanical properties.

Room temperature mechanical properties
The room temperature fracture toughness KQ and tensile
strength sb are shown in Fig. 7. The values of KQ and sb
increase with increasing withdrawal rate and the
maximums are 13?65 MPa m 1/2 and 389?16 MPa respec-
tively. Furthermore, the alloy exhibits no elongation at
room temperature.
As shown in Fig. 7, the fracture toughnessKQ increases
with the withdrawal rate. The previous research has
reported that a larger sized ductile particle can provide an
alloy with higher toughness increment, wherein the crack
bridging mechanism is operative.
15 However, in this
research, the fracture toughness of HT6 is the lowest
though the size of NbSS is the largest. Figure 8 shows the
near-tip regions in HT6 and HT36 samples. From Fig. 8,
we can identify the fracture process and follow the crack
path. In all the HT samples, the main cracks tend to
follow the silicide phases or Nb SS/Nb5Si3 interfaces
which are parallel to the crack path, sometimes extend
through a few Nb SS grains and cross Nb SS/Nb5Si3
interfaces which are perpendicular to the crack path. In
HT6, the main crack generates from the notch root and
propagates straightly. The occurrence of crack bridging
by Nb SS can be observed, as shown as the circles in
Fig. 8a. From Fig. 8 b, it can be seen that the crack is
deﬂected by the ﬁne Nb SS network and presents a zigzag
shape in HT36. Based on the results, it can be
demonstrated that the continuity of Nb SS is one of the
important parameters which improves the toughness of
the alloy. In this study, the toughening from the crack
deﬂection of continuous Nb
SS network is larger than that
from the crack bridging of larger sized ductile Nb SS
particles.

Conclusions
The microstructure and mechanical properties of the
Nb–16Si–24Ti–4Cr–2Al–2Hf alloy were studied in the
directionally solidiﬁed and heat treated conditions. The
conclusions are as follows.
1. The DS microstructure was hypereutectic and
consisted of primary Nb
5Si3 blocks and Nb SSzNb5Si3
eutectic cells. The cross-section of eutectic cells changed
from square morphology to nearly round morphology
with increasing withdrawal rate.
2. During heat treatment, the boundaries of the round
eutectic cells were eliminated as the Nb SS connected to
be a net-like structure and the Nb 5Si3 became discontin-
uous pieces.
3. The values of KQ and sb increased with the increase
in withdrawal rate and the maximums were 13 ?65
MPa m1/2 and 389?16 MPa respectively. The toughening
from the crack deflection of continuous NbSS network was
significant. Eliminating the primary Nb 5Si3 blocks with
large size and sharp edges and refinement of the
microstructure improved the tensile strength.
