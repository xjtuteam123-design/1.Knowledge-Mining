Materials Transactions, vol. 42, No. 3 (2001) pp. 484 to 490 @2001 The Japan Institute of Metals Effects of Titanium Addition on the Microstructure and Mechanical Behavior of Iron Aluminide Fe3Al Su-Ming Zhu, Kazushi Sakamoto, Makoto Tamura* and Kunihiko Iwasaki Japan Ultra-high Temperature Materials Research Institute, Ube 755-0001, Japan The microstructure, mechanical properties and deformation behavior of four Fe3Al-based alloys containing O, 5, IO or 15 at%Ti were investigated. With the addition of Ti, the coarse-grained microstructure of Fe3Al was refined and the degree of D03 ordering was decreased. Poor bending ductility and a change of fracture mode from cleavage to mixture of intergranular and cleavage were observed in Ti-added alloys. It was shown that the Ti addition decreased the room temperature yield strength, but increased the yield strength at elevated temperatures. Anomalous temperature dependence of yield strength was observed in all the alloys, and the peak strength was shifted to higher temperatures with increasing Ti content. The Ti addition was also revealed to decrease the strain rate sensitivity and activation energy for high temperature deformation. Finally, hot working conditions for the Ti-added Fe3Al alloys were proposed by taking the stress-strain response, strain rate sensitivity and deformation microstructure into account. Introduction Iron aluminides based on Fe3Al are excellent candidate for high temperature applications in view of their low cost, avail- ability of raw materials, conservation of strategic elements (Cr as an example), high specific strength, and remarkable corrosion resistance. 1-3) However, commercialization of these intermetallics has been very limited due to their low ductil- ity and impact resistance at ambient temperature as well as poor creep strength at elevated temperatures. Recent stud- ies by Hawk et al.4-6) have demonstrated that additions of Ti to Fe3Al have a positive influence on their tribological properties. For example, the alloy with 10 at%Ti substitut- ing for Fe in Fe3Al shows a 40% decrease in volume wear compared with Fe3Al. This inspiring result suggests that iron aluminides may find their application in some tribological cir- cumstances, especially where the oxidation or sulfidation is also a major concern. The phase structure and phase transition in Ti-added Fe3Al have been well studied by using transmission electron mi- croscopy (TEM), differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) and electrical resistance measurement. 7-11) These studies revealed that Ti addition had a pronounced effect in increasing the D03-B2 transition temperature as well as in altering the phase fields and thermal anti-phase boundary (APB) microstructure. Relatively fewer studies have been reported on the mechani- cal properties and deformation behavior of Ti-added Fe3Al. Diehm and Mikkola12) first investigated the effect of addition of Ti and/or Mo on the yield strength and work hardening rate of Fe3Al. They found that the Ti addition significantly improved the high temperature compressive yield strength. Agarwal et al.13) examined the corrosion and tensile proper- ties of Fe3Al alloys with addition ofTi. Their results showed that the addition of Ti improved room temperature ductility but led to poor yield strength. They attributed the improve- ment in ductility to the increase in passivity and the change in *Now at UBE Scientific Analysis Laboratory, Ube 755-8633, Japan. fracture mode. However, the low yield strength of Ti-added Fe3Al was not reasonably explained in their work. It is noted that the Ti additions in the above mentioned studies are less than IO at%. In the present work, the effects of Ti addition (up to 15 at%) on the mechanical properties and deformation behavior of Fe3Al were systematically investigated. This is a part of an extensive study which aims at developing Fe3Al-based alloys 14, 15) for tribological applications. 2. Experimental Fe��25 at%Al alloys containing 0, 5, 10 and 15 at%Ti (sim- ply termed as 0Ti, 5T1, 10Ti, and 15Ti alloys hereafter) were prepared by arc-melting 99.99 mass% elemental wires on a water-cooled Cu hearth under a purified Ar atmosphere. The ingots were remelted several times to ensure homogeneity. The weight of ingot was measured again after melting to check any losses. The weight change was negligible (less than 0.2%). Thus, compositions were assumed to be the same as the nominal ones. After melting, the ingots were further homogenized in vacuum at 1323 K for 14.4ks, followed by fumace cooling. Mechanical properties were evaluated by Vickers hardness, bending and compression tests. The Vickers hardness was measured under a load of 98 N. Bending specimens with dimensions of 3 x 4 x 40mm3 and compression specimens with those of 3 x 3 x 6mm3 were sectioned from the ingots by electro-discharge machining (EDM). All surfaces of the specimens were polished with 1000 grit emery paper. Four- point bending tests were conducted at room temperature with a crosshead speed of 0.5 mm/min with a Shimadzu high tem- perature materials testing system. Compression tests were conducted at a constant crosshead speed of 0.5 mm/min with an initial strain rate of 1.4 x 10��3 from room tempera- ture to 1273 K. All tests were carried out in air after 0.9 ks holding at the testing temperature. To minimize friction ef- fects, the specimen-die interface was lubricated with boron nitride powder. However, the IOTI alloy exhibits surface cracking, especially at temperatures be- low 1073 K. The cross sectional microstructures of the 0Ti and 10Ti alloys after high temperature deformation are shown in Figs. 10 and 11, respectively. For the OTI alloy, subgrain formation is observed at 873 K, and formation of new grains is obvious at temperatures above 1073 K. These newly formed grains are very fine, indicating the occurrence of dynamic re- crystallization. This implies the possibility of hot working for the 0Ti alloy. However, dynamic recrystallization is observed at temperatures above 1273 K in the case of 10Ti alloy, and grain boundary cavitation is observed in specimens deformed at 873 and 1073 K. Such cavitation occurs preferentially at grain boundaries, especially at their triple junctions, indicat- ing that grain boundary sliding is responsible for the defor- mation process. These results reveal that the 10Ti alloy has a relatively narrow processing window for deformation com- pared with the 0Ti alloy. The hot working temperature of the 10Ti alloy should be above 1273 K. 4. Discussion 4.1 Effect of Ti addition on room temperature properties The XRD analyses show that the lattice parameter of Fe3Al increases with increasing Tl content, from which a strong solid solution strengthening effect by Ti addition is expected. However, the hardness, bending and compression testing re- sults do not support this expectation: the hardness of Fe3Al is not improved by Ti addition while the compressive yield strength and bending strength decrease with Tl addition. To clarify this problem, the following two factors should be considered: the formation of second-phase particles and the change in ordering state associated with Tl addition. In view of the fact that second-phase particles generally strengthen the matrix by either Orowan looping or particle cutting mecha- nism, the change in ordering state by Ti addition is considered to be important. The Ti addition decreases the degree of 1)03 ordering (Fig. 2), thus lessening the ordering strengthening effect. As a result, low strength is observed in the Ti-added alloys. Our bending test results reveal that the Tl additions into Fe3Al change the fracture mode from cleavage to mixture of intergranular and cleavage. In general, there are two possi- ble explanations: either the fracture strength for cleavage is increased to that for intergranular fracture, or grain bound- ary strength is decreased due to some embrittling mechanism. For Fe3Al with additions of 2��6%Cr, the former mechanism is responsible for it.21) The fracture strength for cleavage in- creased with Cr addition to a comparable level with that for intergranular fracture. This enhancement in strength also re- sulted in an increase in overall ductility. In this study, the duc- tility decrease by Ti addition (Table 1) implies the dominance of the latter mechanism, i.e., the Ti addition decreases the grain boundary strength with a concurrent decrease in duc- tility. Solid solution strengthening by Ti addition is one important factor contlibuting to this increase. Another effect to be considered is ordering strengthening. The strength of Fe3Al-based alloys is well known to increase with temperature in an intermedi- ate temperature range and peak strength occurs near the D03- B2 transition temperature. The anomalous yield strength be- havior is generally attributed to the transformation from D03 to B2 ordered structure and its related change in dislocation mechanism.22) In this study, the Ti addition causes the shift of the anomalous yield strength peak to higher temperature. This is because the Ti addition increases the D03 to B2 transforma- tion temperature, thus enhancing the stability of D03 ordered structure. 7-11) The shift of the strength peak to higher temper- ature renders the Ti-added alloys to exhibit higher strength than the 0Ti alloy at high temperatures. Finally, the second- phase particles induced by Tl addition may also contribute to the high strength of Ti-added alloys at elevated temperatures. However, due to the lack of detailed microstructure analysis of these particles, the strengthening mechanism involved is not clear. Figure 12 plots strain rate against flow stress for the 0Ti and 10Ti alloys. It can be seen that the 10Ti alloy shows higher stress exponent than that of the 0Ti alloy. Moreover, under the same flow stresses, the 10Ti alloy shows significantly lower strain rate than that of the 0Ti alloy. These results imply that the Ti additions would improve the creep properties of Fe3Al, which will be studied in the future. 5. Conclusions Effects of Ti addition on the microstructure and mechanical properties of Fe3Al have been studied. The following conclu- sions are drawn: (1) The addition of Ti is effective in refining the coarse- grained microstructure of Fe3Al. (2) The addition of Ti decreases the room temperature yield strength and ductility ofFe3Al. The fracture mode shifts from cleavage fracture in Fe3Al to mixture of intergranular and cleavage fracture in the "El-added alloys. (3) The addition of Ti significantly improves the high temperature strength of Fe3Al and shifts the anomalous peak strength to higher temperatures. (4) The Ti-added alloys have a relatively nan-ower hot working range than Fe3Al. The hot working temperature of Ti-added alloys should be above 1273 K.
