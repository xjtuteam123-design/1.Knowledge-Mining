ABSTRACT
The effect of dispersion distribution of the precipitated phase ( γ’ phase) on strength and plasticity at atomic scale 
in Ni-based superalloys with 15 % volume fraction of γ’ phase and diameters of 10 and 30 nm at room- 
temperature were revealed by molecular dynamics simulation. The γ’ phase dispersion refinement can effec -
tively suppress crack propagation, and enhances its strength and plasticity. The increased number of phase 
boundaries for dislocation nucleation has improved its strength. The small-sized γ’ phase boundaries promote 
dislocation nucleation and emission to reduce the stress concentration. Additionally, the competitive mechanism 
ISF and phase transition can further enhance the plasticity.
1. Introduction
Nickel-based superalloys serve as crucial structural materials for 
turbine rotors in aero-engines, with their strength and toughness 
significantly influencing the operational and machining performance of 
turbine discs and blades. Enhancing the strength and toughness of these 
materials has long been a central challenge in materials research. 
Currently, several effective methods exist to enhance material strength, 
including the construction of gradient structures [1] , grain refinement 
[2] , dislocation density increase [3] , twin formation [4] , and sediment 
dispersion [5] . Each of these techniques improves material strength by 
introducing various defects that impede dislocation movement, thereby 
complicating the material ’ s ability to undergo plastic deformation. 
However, when lattice dislocations are obstructed and unable to 
accommodate plastic deformation, a corresponding decrease in material 
toughness occurs [6] . Consequently, the aforementioned methods aimed 
at strengthening the material may inadvertently lead to a reduction in 
toughness.
The second phase of dispersion strengthening has emerged as a pri -
mary method for enhancing the strength of nickel-based superalloys, as 
it effectively improves material strength without compromising tough -
ness. Experimental studies indicate that both the reduction in size and 
the increase in the content of the γ’ phase in nickel-based superalloys 
significantly contribute to improved yield strength [7 – 9] . However, 
investigating the mechanisms underlying the relative strength and 
toughness of the γ’ phase at the atomic scale presents challenges that 
cannot be addressed through experimental methods alone. Researchers 
have utilized molecular dynamics to examine the influence of the γ / γ’ 
interface on dislocation motion obstruction and the interplay between 
dislocations and sediment on material strength [10 – 15] . However, the 
existing research mainly focuses on the high-temperature mechanical 
behaviors and toughening mechanisms of nickel-based single crystal 
superalloys used for aero-turbine blades at service or near-service tem -
peratures. Nevertheless, aero-turbine blades are required to withstand 
impact loadings under room-temperature during the processes of 
turning, milling and broaching at room temperature, which imposes 
stringent requirements on their room-temperature mechanical strength 
and toughness. Additionally, their research mainly involves the in -
fluences of the volume fraction, morphology of the γ ′ phase and the γ / γ ′ 
interface on the mechanical strength and toughness of nickel-based 
single crystal superalloys. There is still a lack of in-depth understand -
ing of the influence mechanism at the atomic scale regarding the 
dispersion and refinement of the strengthening phase. Therefore, it is of 
great necessity to reveal the influence law and the action mechanism of 
the dispersion and refinement of the γ ′ phase on the room-temperature 
mechanical strength and toughness of nickel-based single crystal su -
peralloys from the atomic scale.
In this study, molecular dynamics models of nickel-based superalloys 
with distinct γ’ phases were established to elucidate the γ’ phase 
dispersion refinement effect under room-temperature. Dislocations,

2. Methodology
To investigate the γ’ phase dispersion distribution effect on strength 
and plasticity of Ni-based superalloys, the molecular dynamics models 
were illustrated in Fig. 1 . The nickel-based superalloy models were 
established by Atomsk software [16] . Additionally, molecular dynamics 
simulations were performed through LAMMPS [17] . The volume frac -
tion of the γ’ phase in the two MD models (Model I and Model II) is 15 % 
[18] with approximately 960,000 atoms. However, the Model I has 2 γ’ 
phases with a diameter of 30 nm, while Model II has 19 randomly 
distributed γ’ phases with a diameter of 10 nm (see Fig. 1 (a)). The size of 
the simulated box is 100 × 100 × 1 nm, with an edge crack measuring 
15 × 2 nm, as shown in Fig. 1 (b). The boundary conditions along the X 
and Y axes are Scaling Boundary Conditions, while the Z-axis boundary 
condition is a Periodic Boundary Condition.
The conjugate gradient method is performed to obtain the initial 
atomic configuration with the minimum energy. Then the initial 
configuration with minimized energy is relaxed under the NVT 
ensemble for 50 ps. The Nose-Hoover method [19] was employed to 
maintain the temperature of 300 and the pressure of 0.1 bar in the 
simulation system. After relaxation, a tensile load with a velocity of 7.7

The small-sized γ’ phases increase the phase boundary energy [25] and

dislocations transition into low-energy slidable Shockley dislocations. 
When two Shockley dislocations encounter each other at the junction of 
their respective slip surfaces, they can synthesize into an immobile Stair- 
rod dislocation ( Fig. 4 (e)). The diffuse refinement of the γ’ phase not 
only increases the density and number of dislocations but also promotes 
mutual obstruction and reactions among them, further contributing to 
the enhancement of strength in nickel-based superalloys.
