abstract
The present study describes the synthesis of nanocrystalline equiatomic CuNiCoZnAlTi high entropy alloy
by mechanical alloying and characterized by XRD, SEM and TEM. The CuNiCoZnAlTi high entropy alloy
is mainly composed of BCC solid solution with crystallite size less than 10 nm in as milled condition. The
alloy is thermally stable at elevated temperature about 800
◦ C as it retained its nanostructure. This alloy
powder was consolidated using vacuum hot press at 800 ◦ C with 30 MPa pressure to a density of 99.95%.
The hardness and compressive strength of the high entropy alloy are found to be 7.55 and 2.36 GPa,
respectively. Superior strength of high entropy alloy is attributed to solid solution strengthening and its
nanocrystalline nature.

1. Introduction
The usual approach for developing new alloys is always based
on microstructure modiﬁcation, alloying addition or/and surface
modiﬁcation on the existing alloys. These processes are mainly
based on one or two elements as major alloying elements, and
other minor elements used for the improvement of deﬁnite prop-
erties. Multicomponent alloys are expected to form more number
of intermetallic compounds with complex crystal structure and
microstructures. This would make the processing and analysis
of the alloy as difﬁcult task [1]. Recently, a new type of alloys
called “high entropy alloy” (HEA) have been developed devi-
ating from the conventional alloy design concepts. Yeh et al.
deﬁned high entropy alloys to have at least ﬁve principal ele-
ments with the concentration of each principal element being
between 35 and 5 at.% [2–4]. Solid solutions with multiprincipal
elements have been generally found to be more stable than inter-
metallic compounds at elevated temperatures due to their large
entropies of mixing, which lead to the sluggish diffusion of atoms
[3–7].
The conﬁgurational entropy at equiatomic compositions for
binary, ternary, quaternary, quinary and hexanary alloys is i.e. 5.8,
9.2, 11.6, 13.5, and 14.9 J/mol K, respectively, and with equiatomic
alloys with more than three elements, the conﬁgurational entropy

can be higher than the entropy of fusion (11–15 J/mol K) of most of
the common metals. The formation of amorphous phase, which can
be another competing phase in these multicomponent systems, can
be avoided by choosing elements carefully with small size factors
[8]. The promising properties of entropy HEAs offer the potential to
be used in many applications, such as tools, molds, dies, mechan-
ical parts and furnace parts which require high strength, thermal
stability and wear and oxidation resistance. In addition, coating
technology will further expand the application of HEAs to func-
tional ﬁlms, such as hard facing of golf heads and rollers, diffusion
barriers and soft magnetic ﬁlms [9–11]. Mechanical alloying (MA)
is a widely used solid state processing route for the synthesis of
nano materials with good homogeneity [12]. The present study is
taken up to synthesise nanocrystalline hexanary (CuNiCoZnAlTi)
high entropy alloy using MA.
2. Experimental details
Elemental powders Cu, Ni, Co, Zn, Al, Ti with diameter ≤45 /H9262m
and 99.7% purity with equiatomic compositions were used as start-
ing materials. The elemental powders were milled in a planetary
ball mill (Fritsch Pulverisette P-5) with tungsten carbide grind-
ing media in toluene. The ball-to-powder weight ratio is 10:1 and
300 rpm speed was used. The toluene was used as a process control-
ling agent in order to avoid excessive cold welding and also act as a
reducing medium to avoid oxidation of the alloy. In order to conﬁrm
the alloy formation during milling, powder samples were taken out
in every 5 h intervals (5, 10, 15 and 20 h). After 20 h of milling the

The milled samples were studied by X-ray diffraction (XRD)

microscope (TEM) was used to conﬁrm the nanocrystalline nature
and crystal structure of the CuNiCoZnAlTi alloy powders.
To conﬁrm the stability of the phases, the sample was annealed
at 800
◦C for 1 h and XRD analysis was carried out on the
heat treated sample. After the successful synthesis of equiatomic
nanocrystalline CuNiCoZnAlTi HEA powders, the consolidation was
carried out in 65 mm diameter graphite die with 250 ton vacuum
hot press (VHP). The typical VHP conditions are 800
◦C temperature,
30 MPa pressure with half hour holding followed by furnace cool-
ing. The walls of the graphite die were coated with boron nitride
coating to avoid the diffusion of the graphite into the compacted
sample. In order to ensure the absence of graphite layer, 1 mm of
metal surface is removed from the VHP sample. The EDM wire cut-
ting was used for sectioning the 65 mm diameter and 8 mm thick
disc shape VHP sample into 10 mm samples. The sectioned samples
have been further characterized by XRD, SEM–EDX and TEM anal-
ysis. For TEM studies the thin foils were prepared by ion milling at
room temperature. The 10 mm thick and 5 mm diameter VHP sam-
ples were polished up to diamond polish, to measure mechanical
properties like hardness using Vickers hardness tester with 3 kg
load and compressive strength using 1000 kN press.
3. Results and discussion
XRD pattern shown inFig. 1 reveals the phase formation in CuNi-
CoZnAlTi HEA with milling time. From the XRD results it is clear that
the alloy formation is completed after 10 h with the formation of
BCC solid solution with a lattice parameter of 0.290 nm. The crys-
tallite size, lattice strain and lattice parameter of the BCC phase
as a function of milling time are shown in Table 1 . The crystal-
lite size and lattice strain after 20 h of milling are 9 nm and 1.47%,
respectively. Thus, nanocrystalline single phase solid solution of
a simple structure such as BCC could be synthesised in this hex-
anary equiatomic alloy by MA. TEM dark ﬁeld image along with
SAD pattern, shown in Fig. 2, conﬁrm the nanocrystalline nature of
the mechanically alloyed equiatomic CuNiCoZnAlTi HEA with BCC
structure.

4. Conclusion
The nanostructured equiatomic CuNiCoZnAlTi high entropy
alloy has been successfully synthesised by MA and consolidated
by vacuum hot pressing at 800
◦C with 30 MPa pressure. The as
milled equiatomic CuNiCoZnAlTi alloy has mainly a BCC phase and
two minor FCC phases were observed on VHP. The very high hard-
ness of 7.55 GPa and the compressive strength of 2.36 GPa prove
the promising future of the alloy.
