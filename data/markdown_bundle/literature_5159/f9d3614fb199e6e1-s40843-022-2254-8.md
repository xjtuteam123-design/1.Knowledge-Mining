Phase dissolution strengthens and ductilizes a high-entropy alloy
Ruixin Wang1†, Li Li2,5†, Yu Tang1*, Zhifeng Lei3*, Jia Li2, Chao Ma3, Shun Li1, Yicong Ye1, Li'an Zhu1,
Yuanlin Ai1, Qihong Fang2, Shuxin Bai1* and Zhaoping Lu4
ABSTRACT Annealing is widely used in the thermo-
mechanical processing of metallic alloys. Usually, ductility
enhances while strength decreases because defects annihilate
with the increase in annealing temperature. Here, we report a
simultaneous improvement in strength and ductility of a
TiZrNbTa high-entropy alloy by promoting phase dissolution
with increasing annealing temperature. When annealing is
performed from 800 to 1250°C, the tensile yield strength in-
creases by 40% to 1003 ± 16 MPa, and the elongation is nearly
doubled to 16.79% ± 1.03%. The annealing-induced phase
dissolution promotes lattice distortion, contributing to en-
hanced lattice friction stress and yield strength. The corre-
spondingly decreased interface mismatch alleviates stress
concentration, and the stimulated formation of local chemical
orders encourages coplanar slip and dislocation multi-
plication. Both occurrences boost ductility. These results not
only expand our understanding of the phase dissolution and
annealing effect in metallic materials, but also provide an in-
sight into designing strong and ductile alloys.
Keywords: high-entropy alloy, phase dissolution, lattice distor-
tion, local chemical order, strengthening and ductilization
INTRODUCTION
Annealing is a heat treatment process that involves heating a
material at an elevated temperature for a specific period, fol-
lowed by cooling. As the annealing temperature increases, the
solubility and diffusion abilities of atoms increase[1]. Conse-
quently, the elemental segregation is weakened, high-stress
boundaries are reduced, and residual stress is alleviated[2]. Such
annihilation of obstacles reduces the critical stress for disloca-
tion motion and facilitates dislocation slips. Therefore, an
increase in ductility is often accompanied by a significant loss of
strength with increasing annealing temperature [3–6]. It is
worth noting that increasing the annealing temperature will also
promote phase dissolution and accelerate the disappearance of
multi-phase relaxation [7,8]. The solid solution of multiple
constituent atoms in a single lattice could enhance the lattice
distortion and consequently improve the strength. Intentionally,
phase dissolution is expected to avoid the strength-ductility
trade-off during annealing.
To demonstrate the synchronous enhancement in both
strength and ductility caused by phase dissolution, high-entropy
alloys (HEAs), which can maximize lattice distortion and the
solid solution strengthening effect, are selected as the model
material. HEAs follow the novel alloy design philosophy of
mixing four or more elements in equimolar or near-equimolar
ratios to achieve massive solid solutions, often in a single-phase
state [9,10]. Because of the random occupancy of various atoms
in a single solid solution namely chemical complexity, severe
distortion is seen as the core effect in HEAs[11]. The severely
distorted lattice provides a strong and long-range lattice friction
stress that impedes dislocation movement[12–14]. Thus, HEAs
often exhibit a stronger solid solution strengthening capability
and consequently much higher room- and high-temperature
strength than conventional metallic materials [15–18]. More-
over, local chemical orders exist predominantly in HEA matrices
[19–21]. Such unique microstructures can trigger strong fluc-
tuations in the local stacking-fault energy and change the dis-
location slip mode in HEAs [19–25]. Resultantly, both lattice
distortion and local chemical orders significantly affect the
mechanical properties of HEAs.
Here, we selected TiZrNbTa HEA as the model system due to
its potential structural application prospects[26–28], and found
simultaneous enhancement in its strength and ductility with
increasing annealing temperature. The tensile yield strength of
TiZrNbTa HEA increases from 716 ± 11 to 1003 ± 16 MPa after
annealing for 24 h corresponding to an increase in temperature
from 800 to 1250°C. Simultaneously, the elongation nearly
doubles, improving from 9.82% ± 0.96% for the 800°C-annealed
alloy to 16.79% ± 1.03% for the 1250°C-annealed variant. The
increase in strength originates from the annealing-induced
phase dissolution and the associated enhanced lattice distortion.
The improved ductility is due to the decreased interface mis-
match and the formation of local chemical orders upon
increasing the annealing temperature. On the one hand, the
exacerbated lattice distortion with the increase in annealing
temperature enhances the lattice friction stress and improves the
yield strength. On the other hand, the diminished interface
mismatch upon dissolving the secondary phase relieves the
stress concentration. Moreover, the annealing-stimulated for-
mation of local chemical orders provokes coplanar slips, facil-
itates dislocation multiplication, and ultimately improves the

EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
Materials preparation
TiZrNbTa alloys were prepared by arc melting a mixture of pure
metals (purity > 99.9 wt.%) in a Ti-gettered high-purity argon
atmosphere. Remelting was performed at least eight times to
ensure the chemical homogeneity of the ingots. The ingots were
eventually drop-casted into a water-cooled copper mold with
dimensions of 10 mm × 10 mm × 60 mm. The as-cast alloys
were sealed in quartz tubes filled with high-purity argon, sub-
jected to heat treatment at 800, 1000, and 1250°C for 24 h, and
finally quenched in water.
Microstructural characterization
X-ray diffractometer (XRD, RIGAKU SmartLab) was used to
determine the structure. The morphology of the polished sam-
ples was studied using a scanning electron microscope (SEM,
TESCAN MIRA3) equipped with energy dispersive spectroscopy
(EDS). The information on grain orientation and size was
obtained through a Hitachi S-3400N SEM equipped with an
electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) system (HKL Technol-
ogy Inc.). The nano- and atomic-scale structures were observed
via transmission electron microscopy (TEM, FEI Talos F200X)
and aberration-corrected scanning TEM (STEM, Thermo Sci-
entific Themis Z at the Analytical Instrumentation Center of
Hunan University), respectively. The TEM/STEM samples were
mechanically ground to a thickness of 50 μm and then prepared
by ion milling (Gatan 695), and were cleaned by plasma before
the test. Atomic strain maps were obtained using the geometric
phase analysis method[29]. We took at least five TEM images of
the same region and averaged the corresponding spot positions
to minimize the possible influence of vibration.
Mechanical and physical tests
Tensile tests were performed using an Instron 8801 tensile tester
at room temperature with a strain rate of 10
−3 s−1. The speci-
mens were cut from the annealed alloys by electrical discharge
machining and carefully polished to a particle size of 2000 grit.
The tensile tests were performed on dog-bone-shaped specimens
with a thickness of 1 mm and gauge length and width of 18 and
4 mm, respectively. The compression test was performed on
rectangular specimens with a diameter of 4 mm and length of
8 mm (aspect ratio of 2:1) using a universal testing machine
(Instron-3369). At least three measurements were obtained for
each sample to determine the average values of the mechanical
properties.
RESULTS
Tensile properties
For simplicity, the TiZrNbTa HEAs annealed at 800, 1000, and
1250°C for 24 h are hereafter denoted as TZNT-800, TZNT-
1000, and TZNT-1250 HEAs, respectively.Fig. 1ashows the true
tensile stress-strain curves of the annealed TiZrNbTa HEAs. The
tensile yield strength increases with increasing annealing tem-
perature, i.e., from 716 ± 11 MPa for TZNT-800 HEA to 1003 ±
16 MPa for TZNT-1250 HEA. The compressive yield strength
also exhibits a similar trend (Fig. S1). This phenomenon varies
from conventional wisdom on the softening effect induced by
increasing the annealing temperature[2–6]. Moreover, the ten-
sile ductility also increases with increase in the annealing tem-
perature from 9.82% ± 0.96% for TZNT-800 HEA to 16.79% ±
1.03% for TZNT-1250 variant. Fig. 1b illustrates the strain-
hardening behavior of the annealed TiZrNbTa HEAs. As the
annealing temperature increases, the strain-hardening rate cor-
respondingly increases. This observation suggests that increasing
the annealing temperature can effectively improve the work-
hardening capacity of the TiZrNbTa HEA. Furthermore, we
used the changing rate of property (Y) to quantify the changes in
strength and ductility with annealing temperature[30]. Here, the
rate of change in strength/ductility is defined as the ratio of the
variations in strength/ductility between high-temperature
annealing (HTA) and low-temperature annealing (LTA) pro-
cesses. Fig. 1c shows the anomalous annealing-induced
strengthening and ductilization behaviors of TiZrNbTa HEA
with respect to those of several established metals and alloys[3–
6,31–63]. The changing rates of strength are negative for the
reference metallic materials, demonstrating that increasing the
annealing temperature usually leads to softening. However, for
the current TiZrNbTa HEA, both the changing rates of strength
and ductility are positive, indicating a contrary trend upon
increasing the annealing temperature.

DISCUSSION
This study demonstrates that both the strength and ductility of
TiZrNbTa HEA improve with the increase in annealing tem-
perature from 800 to 1250°C along with phase dissolution. The
strengthening and ductilization mechanisms are illustrated in
Fig. 6. As presented above, the grain boundary strengthening,
precipitation strengthening and dislocation strengthening all
bring little effect on the total yield strength. The enhanced yield
strength primarily originates from the exacerbated lattice dis-
tortion and the resultant elevated lattice friction stress. Two BCC
phases, i.e., the Zr- and (Nb,Ta)-enriched phases, exist in the
HEAs that are annealed at 800 and 1000°C, whereas a single
BCC phase forms in the alloy annealed at 1250°C. Although the
Zr-enriched phases appear in the matrix of the dual-phase
TZNT-800 and TZNT-1000 HEAs, their contributions to the
yield strength are minimal (dark-cyan curves in Fig. 4a, b)
because of their large size (more than 200 nm). When the
annealing temperature is increased from 800 to 1000°C, the Zr-
enriched phase is partially dissolved (Fig. 2c2, d2) and element
segregation is weakened (inset tables in Fig. 2c1, d1). The
enhanced microstructural homogeneity and thus more diverse
atoms in lattices promote lattice distortion in the dual-phase
TiZrNbTa HEAs (Fig. 3). Therefore, TZNT-1000 HEA exhibits a
higher yield strength than TZNT-800 HEA with similar dual-
phase BCC structure. When the annealing temperature is further
increased to 1250°C, the Zr-enriched phase is completely dis-

fractured TZNT-1000 HEA, and the labyrinth structures
resulting from cross-slips emerge near the phase boundaries
(Fig. 5g, h). This is also confirmed by the transformation of the
fracture mode from cleavage fracture for TZNT-800 HEA
(Fig. S7a) to quasi-cleavage fracture for TZNT-1000 HEA
(Fig. S7b). Thus, the ductility improves when the annealing
temperature increases from 800 to 1000°C. However, a few
dipolar dislocation walls are observed in the fractured TZNT-
1000 HEA, indicating an undeveloped cross-slip. Moreover, the
confined dislocations at the phase boundaries still cause stress
concentration and microcracks. Consequently, the multi-
plication and propagation of dislocations are limited, leading to
modest ductility. In TZNT-1250 HEA, the secondary phase
disappears. This would reduce the obstacle for dislocation
motion and promote the unremitting dislocation motion, both
of which would weaken the strain hardening. However, due to
the existence of Zr- and (Nb,Ta)-enriched local chemical orders,
the distinct strain hardening still occurs in the TZNT-1250 HEA
(Fig. 2e4). Local chemical orders encourage planar slip because
of the glide plane softening effect [68–70]. During the initial
stage of plastic deformation, planar slips prevail along one
direction (Fig. 5i). Upon reaching a higher dislocation density,
and thus higher stress levels, dislocations start to cut through
both the Zr- and (Nb, Ta)-enriched local chemical orders.
Consequently, coplanar dislocation arrays occur (Fig. 5j). The
coplanar slip stimulates dislocation nucleation, facilitates dis-
location multiplication, and homogenizes the dislocation sub-
structure[19,22,70–72]. These features improve work hardening,
delay the onset of necking, and eventually lead to higher duc-
tility, which is also demonstrated by elongated dimples at the
fracture surface (Fig. S7c). Therefore, upon increasing the
annealing temperature from 800 to 1250°C, the phase dissolu-
tion alleviates the stress concentration and contributes to
enhanced ductility. More importantly, the stimulated formation

CONCLUSIONS
In summary, we reported synchronously enhanced strength and
ductility in the annealed TiZrNbTa HEA. The tensile yield
strength, ultimate tensile strength, and elongation increase by
40%, 37%, and 71%, respectively, when the annealing tempera-
ture is increased from 800 to 1250°C. The increase in the
annealing temperature incites the phase dissolution and aggra-
vates the inherent lattice distortion. The intensified lattice dis-
tortion enhances the lattice friction stress and yield strength.
When the annealing temperature is increased from 800 to
1000°C, the reduced interface mismatch alleviates the stress
concentration and enhances the ductility. When the annealing
temperature is further increased to 1250°C, Zr- and (Nb,Ta)-
enriched local chemical orders are formed in a homogeneous
matrix. The local chemical orders encourage the formation of
coplanar slips and accelerate dislocation multiplication, which
further improves the ductility. This work provides an effective
way to tailor the microstructure using phase dissolution and
annealing treatments to develop advanced metallic materials
with high strength and ductility.
