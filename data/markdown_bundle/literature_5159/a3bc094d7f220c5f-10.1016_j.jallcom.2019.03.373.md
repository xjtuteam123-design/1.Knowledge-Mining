abstract
In this study, CoCrFeMnNi (Cantor alloy) high-entropy alloy (HEA) with ultra- ﬁne grained (UFG) structure
were prepared using cryogenic (liquid nitrogen, 77 K) rolling followed by short time annealing. The
deformation behavior and strain rate sensitivity (SRS) of processed material were investigated by tensile
test at temperatures ranging from 77 K to 573 K. It was found that the critical twinning stress of the UFG
alloy decreased at 77 K, which was attributed to the decrease of stacking fault energy with the tem-
perature. In addition, the UFG alloy exhibited plastic instability and was prominently strengthened with
increasing testing temperature from 293 K to 473 K, which was related to obviously grain boundaries
relaxation in HEAs. The strain rate jump test revealed that the SRS change of UFG specimen with the
decrease of grain size was inversely proportional to the associated strength enhancement, and exhibited
a stronger temperature dependence than that of CG specimens especially from 293 K to 473 K. The
stronger temperature dependence of SRS of UFG Cantor alloys was due to the increased grain boundary
relaxation, which was a thermal activation process and depended on strain rate and temperature.

1. Introduction
High entropy alloys (HEAs) have caught much attention of sci-
entists due to their excellent properties [ 1e4]. Among various
alloying systems, Cantor alloy (CoCrFeNiMn HEA) with superior
ductility and cryogenic temperature damage tolerance gives them
potential advantages in structural and functional applications,
especially those alloys with ultraﬁne grained (UFG) structure [5e7].
According to Hall-Petch relationship, the strength of conventional
metals increases with the decrease of grain sizes. Therefore, the
alloy with UFG structure shows a higher mechanical strength
compared to their coarse-grained (CG) counterparts [ 6,8,9]. For
example, an ultimate tensile strength of ~1080 MPa was achieved
when the grain size of Cantor alloy was reﬁned to ~100 nm by equal
channel angular pressing (ECAP) followed by annealing [ 10].
Generally speaking, the deformation behavior of conventional
metals is dependent on test temperature and strain rate [ 11]. The
mechanical properties of the majority of UFG metals ﬂuctuates
more obviously than that of CG metals at different testing tem-
perature [ 12]. For example, the UFG austenitic Fe-14Cr-16Ni alloy
presents prominent plastic instability with the increase of testing
temperature from 293 K to 473 K, which are attributed to their
more obviously dynamic recovery at elevated temperature [ 13].
Similar results were also obtained in other UFG materials, including
pure metals [14] and alloys [15]. The multi-element effects of HEAs,
including high entropy, lattice distortion, low stacking fault energy
(SFE) and sluggish diffusion etc, result in more obvious temperature
dependence of mechanical properties than that of conventional
metals [ 16e19]. Previous research indicated that FCC-HEAs were
more favorable for the formation of deformation twins under
cryogenic conditions (liquid nitrogen, 77 K), which resulted in a
superior combination of strength and toughness [ 1,7,20]. In

should be noted that the previous researches on the SRS and effect
of temperature on deformation behavior concentrated on the HEAs
with coarse grain (gain size >1 mm). To date, the deformation
behavior and SRS of UFG HEAs especially at temperature ranging
from 77 to 573 K have been seldom reported.
In the present work, UFG structured Cantor alloy was produced
using cryogenic rolling followed by short time annealing. The effect
of temperature on deformation behavior and SRS of UFG Cantor
alloys was investigated by tensile and strain rate jump tensile test
(rate jumps from 1 /C2 10/C0 2 to 1 /C2 10/C0 5 s/C0 1) from 77 K to 573 K. The
corresponding intrinsic mechanism was analyzed in detail.
2. Experiment
Alloys with the equiatomic compositions of CoCrFeMnNi HEA
were produced by arc melting of pure elements (99.9% purity)
under argon atmosphere. Ingots were ﬂipped and completely re-
melted at least ﬁve times to ensure its chemical composition ho-
mogeneity. The rectangular specimens with a size of
100 mm /C2 20 mm /C2 10 mm were prepared by Cu molds drop cast-
ing method. The detailed information on the melting can be found
elsewhere [ 5]. The homogenization of the rectangular specimens
was carried out at 1473 K for 24 h under the vacuum argon atmo-
sphere, and then air-cooled to room temperature. After that,
cryogenic rolling was conducted [ 27,28]. To ensure cryo-
deformation between the rolling passes, the specimens were sub-
merged in liquid nitrogen at least 20min before each rolling pass. A
thickness reduction ~90% was ﬁnally performed after 20 rolling
passes, and followed by annealing at 923 K for 30 min and 1073 K
for 60 min for recrystallization, respectively.
The microstructures of the processed plates in the thickness
direction were characterized using a ﬁeld emission scanning elec-
tron microscope (SEM, Nova Nano SEM 230) and electron back-
scattering diffraction (EBSD) analysis. The specimens for SEM and
EBSD analysis were prepared by mechanical polishing, followed by
electro-polishing using a mixture solution of 10 vol % perchloric
acid þ methanol solution at about 273 K with the voltage of 20 V.
The step sizes of 1
mm and 0.08 mm were employed during the EBSD
scanning of CG and UFG specimens, respectively [ 29]. In addition,
each EBSD measurement was performed using the accelerating
voltage of 20 kV and the sample tilt of 70 deg. The phases were
identiﬁed by X-ray diffraction (XRD, D/MAX 2400 diffractometer)
with a Cu-Ka using a scanning angle of 20 e100 deg, a scanning rate
of 1 deg/min and a sampling pitch of 0.02 deg.
Dog-bone shape specimens with gauge sections of
10 mm /C2 3.0 mm /C2 1.0 mm (ASTM standard B 557 M-10) were cut
using electric discharge machine along to the rolling direction.
Uniaxial tensile tests (at strain rates of 10 /C0 3 s/C0 1) and the strain rate
jump tests (strain rate between 10/C0 2 and 10/C0 5 s/C0 1) were performed
using a SUNS CMT 5105 testing machine from 77 K to 593 K. Here,
the strain rate jump tests were performed during uniform elon-
gation. All the tests were repeated at least 3 times to obtain
reproducible tensile property.
To identify the microstructures evolution characteristic of the
processed materials during tensile, the transmission electron mi-
croscopy (TEM, JEOL JEM-200CX microscopy) and EBSD were car-
ried out. For this purpose, the interrupted tensile specimens were
prepared at a true strain of 5% and 15%, respectively. The specimens
for TEM examination were prepared by twin jet electro-polishing of
mechanically pre-thinned to 50 mm foils, in a mixture solution of
4 vol % HNO3þC2H6Oa t /C0 25 /C14 C with the voltage of 70 V.

4. Discussion
4.1. The effect of temperature on the deformation behavior of UFG
cantor alloy
The results of the mechanical properties of the present UFG
Cantor alloy exhibit a more pronounced temperature dependence
than that of CG counterparts ( Fig. 3 ). The superior combination of
the strength-ductility for the Cantor alloy at 77 K is attributed to the
deformation twinning. The deformation twin itself can greatly
accommodate the plasticity, while newly formed twin boundaries
ﬁrstly act as a coherent and stable interface strongly obstructing the
dislocation movement, secondly cause continuous grain fragmen-
tation, which reduces the mean free path of dislocation glide and
causes dynamic Hall-Petch effect [ 16,20]. The deformation micro-
structures of UFG specimens with tensile strain of 10% at 77 K are
characterized by TEM. The profuse deformation twins are detected
(Fig. 5 aec), and the kinked structures of twins are also observed.
According to report by Sun et al [34], the critical stress for twin
initiation in UFG Cantor alloy approaches 1800 MPa at room tem-
perature, which is much higher than the maximum ﬂow stress.
Therefore, the deformation twinning of Cantor alloy is restrained
when grain is re ﬁned to UFG regime. However, the present work
shows that the profuse deformation twins formed at true stress of
1 180 MPa (corresponding true strain of 10%). This implies that the
twinning evolution of UFG Cantor alloy at 77 K starts at an early
stage of deformation in this work. That is to say, the critical twin-
ning stress of UFG Cantor alloy is decreased at 77 K. This could be
related to the decrease of SFE with the temperature [ 35,36]. As
shown in Fig. 5d and e, the profuse stacking faults (SF) bundles are

In addition, it is important to note that the UFG cantor alloy also
appears to have extraordinary strengthening process across
293 Ke473 K. Hu et al . reported a similar hardening phenomenon
from 293 K to 873 K in nanograined pure Ni and Ni-Mo alloy, and
the maximum hardening increases with the decrease of grains [ 37].
Generally speaking, the high-angle grain boundaries present a
lower stability than that of the low-energy boundaries. Therefore,
the high fraction of high-angle grain boundaries decreases the
thermal and mechanical stability of materials, which results in a
ﬂuctuation of the mechanical properties through grain boundary
relaxation in a wide temperature range. The multi-element effects
of HEAs, including high entropy and serious lattice distortion, can
signiﬁcantly enhance its grain boundary energy, especially for the
HEAs with extremely ﬁne grains such as UFG [ 38]. This means that
the grain boundary stabilization of HEAs should be lower than that
of other traditional metals. Therefore, the grain boundary of HEAs
with a higher boundary fraction is easier to reduce system energy
through grain boundary relaxation compared with that of other
traditional metals. Fig. 8 shows the hardness values of UFG and CG
Cantor alloy annealed from 373 K to 573 K for 1 h. It can be seen that
the hardness of UFG samples exhibits a parabola shape distribution
in the present study. The peak hardness appears at 473 K, which is
in accordance with the change of strength. On the contrary, the
hardness of CG samples decreases weakly with the increase of
annealing temperature. Interestingly, anneal-induced hardening
was found in cold rolled Cantor alloy at elevated temperature
(773 K) [ 39]. Obviously, the lattice characteristics of Cantor alloy
easily induce grain boundary relaxation, and profoundly enhance
the critical grain size of boundary relaxation, as compared with that
of other metals. Consequently, a strengthening process of UFG
Cantor alloy at the temperature ranging from 273 K to 473 K can be
attributed to grain boundary stabilization through boundary
relaxation. Tailoring the stability of grain boundaries through grain
reﬁning followed by lower-temperature annealing might be a novel
strategy for the production of HEAs with superior properties.
4.2. Temperature dependent the plastic instability of UFG cantor
alloy from 293 K to 473 K
The strain-hardening rate (SHR) and SRS are considered as two
parameters for the evaluation of the deformation ability of metals
according to Hart's plastic instability criterion [ 40]. Enhanced SHR
and SRS under a given ﬂow stress, which maintains the homoge-
neous plastic deformation before necking and prevents the abrupt
fracture after necking, would restrain the high localization of strain.
Correspondingly, the ductility of metals is improved. Considering
the in ﬂuence of SRS and SHR on the deformation behaviors, the
obvious increase of plastic instability of UFG Cantor from 293 K to
473 K in the present study can be explained as follows. With the
increase of the temperature from 293 K to 473 K, SHR and SRS drop

The prominent temperature dependence of SHR and SRS at
293e473 K in the UFG Cantor alloy would be related to the grain
boundary relaxation. It was indicated that the plastic deformation
of metals correlated with the motion of dislocation serving as the
major carriers of plastic strain during deformation [ 5]. The fast
spatial nucleating rate of new dislocations and prominent propa-
gation interaction of previous dislocations lead to a continuous
strain-hardening, which contributes to the enhancement of plas-
ticity of metals [ 32]. However, with the re ﬁnement of grain size
down to the UFG regime, the activities of dislocation could be
restricted by a large concentration of grain boundaries because of
limited dislocation sources within grains [9]. In addition, it was also
proposed that the plastic strain of UFG materials can be facilitated
by dislocation climb, which requires suf ﬁcient spatial position
within grain boundary to accommodate dislocation climb [ 41,42].
Therefore, the contribution of grain boundaries must be considered
during the deformation process of UFG materials. It was suggested
that the deformation behavior of UFG materials was closely related
to their boundary stability, which was determined by the defect or
segregation density within grain boundaries [40]. Abundant defects
(such vacancies and interstitial atoms) within grain boundaries of
UFG assist in the climbing of dislocations and promote the inter-
action of dislocations. Thus, the UFG with a high initial defect
density within grain boundaries usually exhibits enhanced plas-
ticity. However, our results in Fig. 8 reveal that the grain boundary
relaxation of UFG Cantor is activated when the temperature in-
creases from 293 K to 473 K. The grain boundary relaxation reduces
the defect density within grain boundaries, which inhibits the
dislocation climb during deformation. As a result, plastic strain of
alloy is mainly accommodated by limited dislocations. As we can
see from Fig. 9 , under the same strain (5%), the magni ﬁed TEM
micrograph of the specimen tested at 473 K ( Fig. 9b) shows a lower
density of dislocations than the one tested at 293 K ( Fig. 9 a).
Consequently, the limited dislocations participate in the deforma-
tion of UFG Cantor alloy, resulting in a reduced strain-hardening
ability and an enhanced activation volume.
5. Conclusions
Cantor alloy with UFG microstructure was processed using
cryogenic rolling followed by short time annealing. Deformation
behavior and SRS of processed material were investigated by tensile
test and strain rate jump test operated from 77 K to 573 K. The
following conclusions can be summarized.
1. The critical twinning stress of UFG Cantor alloy decreases at
77 K, which attributes to the decrease of stacking fault energy
with the temperature.
2. With the increase of the testing temperature from 293 K to
473 K, an obvious strengthening with a plastic instability is
observed, which is due to the grain boundary stabilization
through grain boundary relaxation in UFG Cantor alloy.
3. Change of SRS with the decrease of grain size in UFG Cantor alloy
is inversely proportional to the associated strength enhance-
ment, and it exhibits a strong temperature dependence. The
value of m decreases with the increase of testing temperature
especially from 293 K to 473 K.
4. The lattice characteristics of Cantor alloy easily induces grain
boundary relaxation, and more profoundly enhances the critical
grain size of boundary relaxation, as compared with that of
other metals. Thus, tailoring the stability of grain boundaries
through grain re ﬁning followed by low-temperature annealing
might be a novel strategy for the production of HEAs with su-
perior properties.
