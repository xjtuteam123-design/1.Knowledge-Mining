abstract
The effects of Hf, Mo and W on the microstructure and hardness of as cast and heat treated (1500 /C14 C/
10 0 h) Nb e20Sie5Hfe5W (YG5), Nb-20Si e5Moe3W (YG6) and Nb e20Sie5Hfe5Moe3W (YG8) alloys
were studied. The macrosegregation of Si was strong in the as cast alloy YG6 and decreased in the order
of the synergy of alloying elements as follows (Mo þ W), (Hf þ W) and (Hf þ Mo þ W). All three alloys
were contaminated by oxygen. The phases present in the as cast alloys YG5, YG6 and YG8 were the Nb ss,
Nb5Si3 and HfO 2,N b ss,N b 3Si, and Nb 5Si3 and Nb ss,N b 5Si3 and HfO 2 respectively, with a lamellar
Nbss þ Nb5Si3 microstructure formed in all three alloys. In the as cast alloys YG5 and YG6 there were Nb ss
grains with no Si content. There was microsegregation of Hf and W in the Nb ss in the as cast alloy YG8
and microsegregation of Hf in Nb 5Si3 in the as cast and heat treated alloys YG5 and YG8. It is concluded
that the concentrations of Si and refractory metal (RM) in the Nb ss depend on the synergy of RMs with/
without Hf. The Nb ss with no Si and Nb 5Si3 were present in all three heat treated alloys and HfO 2 was
formed in the heat treated alloys YG5 and YG8 via the consumption of the Hf in the Nb ss. The stability of
the Nbss þ Nb5Si3 lamellar microstructure decreased in the order of the heat treated alloys YG5, YG8 and
YG6. In the presence of Hf the transformation of bNb5Si3 to aNb5Si3 was enhanced. It is concluded that
the equilibrium microstructures of the alloys consist of the Nb ss with no Si and aNb5Si3 phases. Alloying
with Hf caused reduction of the hardness of the alloys YG5 and YG8 after heat treatment.

1. Introduction
Creep resistance is a major requirement for Nb silicide based
alloys (or Nb silicide in situ composites, as these materials are also
known) that are currently receiving considerable attention as
potential replacements of Ni based superalloys in high temperature
structural applications in advanced gas turbine engines. The choice
of alloying elements is important regarding (a) the type and volume
fraction of the silicide(s) and other intermetallics in the micro-
structure [1e15], (b) the strengthening of the Nb ss [4e15] and (c)
the oxidation behaviour of the alloys [2,12,16e18]. Depending on
alloy chemistry, the microstructures of the developmental Nb sili-
cide based alloys contain the Nb ss and the Nb 3Si and/or Nb 5Si3
silicides with/without other intermetallics, for example the NbCr 2
Laves phase [ 1e15 and refs within]. The steady state creep rate of
Nb silicide based alloys is lower than that of the Nbss but higher that
that of silicides. Jackson and Bewlay [3] studied the steady state
creep of Nb silicide based alloys with additions of Hf, Mo and Ti at
1200
/C14 C and 140, 210 and 280 MPa and showed that the creep
properties of the alloys depended critically on the concentration of
Si (low creep rates for 16 at% < Si < 20 at%) and the Nb/(Ti þ Hf)
ratio in the alloy. For Nb e16Si based alloys creep rates of 10 /C0 7 s/C0 1
were reported for Nb/(Ti þ Hf) ¼ 2 at 1200 /C14 C and 210 and 280 MPa
[3]. Avoidance of the hexagonal gNb5Si3 in the microstructure of Nb
silicide based alloys is also desirable for creep resistance [1,2].

Nb and improve its high temperature strength. The addition of

Silicon in solid solution in Mo is believed to have an adverse
effect on the mechanical properties of Mo ss and MoeSieB alloys at
room temperature [24]. In Nb silicide based alloys the mechanical
properties of the Nb ss depend on the synergy of Si with other
alloying addition such as Al, Cr, Hf and Ti. For example, the fracture
toughness of Nb ss single-phase alloys and multiphase alloys
increased with decreasing (Si þ Al) content in Nb ss [25]. In a recent
publication it was reported that it is possible to form Nbss with no Si
in solid solution in the Nb e18Sie5Hfe3Tae2Mo alloy [5]. Silicon
gains electrons when alloyed with transition (TM)/refractory (RM)
metals [26e28], i.e., there is a charge transfer (charge redistribu-
tion) from the TM/RM to Si. On the other hand, when TM/RM are
alloyed together the charge redistribution is insigni ﬁcant [29,30].
The former would be expected to affect the strength of the Nb eSi
bond compared with the Nb eNb bond or Nb eTM/RM bond. A
recent study has shown that Nb eSi bonds are weaker than Nb eNb
bonds [28]. One motivation for the present work was to explore the
role of Mo and W on the chemistry of the Nb
ss in the presence/
absence of Hf and to compare with the effect of the synergy of Mo,
Ta and Hf.
The Nb5Si3 silicide forms with the D8 1,D 8m and D88 structures
known respectively as the a, b, and g forms of which the former two
have 32 atoms and the latter 16 atoms in the unit cells. The aNb5Si3
is the most stable phase (lowest formation energy) and the gNb5Si3
the least stable one. The b and g forms exhibit high anisotropy in
CTE (coef ﬁcient of thermal expansion) and moduli of elasticity E
(Young modulus) and G (shear modulus) and the a form exhibits
high strength at elevated temperatures and has nearly isotropic CTE
and E, G. The unalloyed Nb
5Si3 has very low fracture toughness
(<3 MPaOm [31]) that can be improved via alloying.
Directional bonding is a characteristic feature of silicides. In thea,
b and g forms of Nb5Si3 bonding is dominantly covalent. The unit cell
of aNb5Si3 consists of atomic layers some of which are more closely
packed and others less closely packed[28]. Substitution is possible in
Nb and Si atoms in either type of closely packed layers. Site substi-
tution of the alloying additions in Nb 5Si3 inﬂuences the electronic
structure and thus mechanical properties. In our group we have
shown that in Nb silicide based alloys the Nb
5Si3 can be alloyed with
TM/RM (sd metals) and metals that have valence sp electrons (sp
metals), with the former substituting Nb atoms and the latter Si
atoms [4e15,32]. The substitution of Nb atoms by Ti atoms in
aNb5Si3 weakens covalent bonding [33] and reduces the hardness
[10]. Tantalum, like Nb forms 5 e3 silicides with the D8 1 and D8 m
structures but Mo and W and Hf form 5e3 silicides of which those of
the former two metals are isomorphous with bNb5Si3 (D8m struc-
ture) and of the latter with gNb5Si3 (D88 structure). Another moti-
vation for the present study was to study the solid solubility of Hf,
Mo and W in Nb 5Si3 and the stability of the latter.
Control of the contamination by interstitial elements and of the
composition of Nb silicide based alloys is not easy, in particular the
content of Si and of low melting point alloying additions [6].
Furthermore, Nb silicide based alloys tend to exhibit macro-
segregation [13,32], the severity of which depends on the synergy
of alloying additions. A third motivation for the present study was
to explore alloy making and to ﬁnd out whether macrosegregation
is present in Nbe20Si silicide based alloys with TM and RM without
Ti, Cr and Al additions.
2. Experimental
The nominal compositions of the alloys of this study were
Nbe20Sie5Hfe5W, Nb e20Sie5Moe3W and Nb e20Sie5Hfe
5Moe3W (all compositions in this paper are given in at % unless
stated otherwise). The three alloys are designated YG5, YG6 and
YG8 respectively. The abbreviations AC and HT are used to indicate
the as cast and heat treated condition respectively. Ingots (w0.4 kg)
of the alloys were prepared in a water cooled copper crucible under
argon atmosphere using high purity elements (Nb 99.99 wt.%, Si
99.999 wt.%, Hf 99.99 wt.%, Mo 99.9 wt.%, and W 99.9 wt.%) and arc
melting with a non consumable tungsten electrode. Each alloy was
melted at least 6 times. The density of the alloys was measured
using the Archimedes principle and a Sartorious electronic preci-
sion balance equipped with a density determination kit. For heat
treatments cubic specimens (1.5 /C2 1.5 /C2 1.5 cm
3) were cut from the
centres of the ingots. These specimens were wrapped in Ta foil,
placed in an alumina crucible and heat treated at 1500
/C14 C for 100 h
in a tube furnace under argon ﬂow which was ﬁrst gettered over Ti
sponge that was placed in a separate alumina crucible.
Phase identi ﬁcation was based on two complimentary tech-
niques, namely X-ray diffraction (XRD) and Electron Probe Micro
Analysis (EPMA). A Philips X-ray diffractometer with a mono-
chromatic CuK a radiation was used and individual phases were
identiﬁed using JCPDS data. The X-ray diffractograms were recor-
ded from bulk specimens and thus texture effects were likely.
Secondary and backscatter electron imaging (SE and BSE), Energy
Dispersive X-ray Spectrometry (EDS) and Wavelength Dispersive X-
ray Spectrometry (WDS) were performed using a JEOL 8600 elec-
tron probe micro-analyser with Oxford Instruments INCA software.
EPMA used a 15 kV accelerating voltage, under which the spatial
resolution was about 1
mm and thus analysis was only performed on
phases of >5 mm diameter. High purity Nb, Si, Hf, Mo, and W
standards that had been polished to 1 mm ﬁnish were used and ZAF
iterative corrections were made to calculate the compositions from
measured X-ray intensities. The detection limits for Si were
w100 ppm (0.01 wt.%) by WDS and w1000 ppm (0.1 wt.%) by EDS.
Specimens from the top, centre and bottom of the ingot were
studied by EPMA. Each specimen was used for large area
(0.5 mm /C2 0.5 mm) analysis, point analysis and area analysis of
lamellar microstructures. Large area (0.5 mm /C2 0.5 mm) analyses
were taken from the top, centre and bottom of the ingot in order to
establish whether macrosegregation existed in the as cast alloys
and from the near the surface areas and the bulk of the heat treated
specimens. At least ten analyses for each area or phase were made
using EPMA. The data is presented by giving the minimum and
maximum and average analysis values and the standard deviation.
The minimum and maximum values are included as these provide
useful information regarding the segregation behaviour of alloying
elements [13,32]. The data for individual phases in the Tables is the
average of the point analysis data from phases present in all the
areas that were used for large area analyses. Selected areas of
lamellar microstructures in the as cast and heat treated alloys were
also analysed.
The Vickers hardness of the alloys was measured using a load of
10 kg on a Vickers Instruments hardness machine. Measurements
ensured that the typical microstructure was sampled by the
indenter. At least 10 measurements were taken for each alloy and
condition. The area fractions of Nb
ss were measured using images of
the microstructure taken in the EPMA and the Oxford Instruments
INCA software. The contrast of the phases was changed with the
aim of facilitating measurement. The area fractions were measured
from the same areas that were used for large area analyses in the
ingot and the bulk of the heat treated specimens.

4. Discussion
Macrosegregation of Si was stronger in the alloy YG6-AC
compared with the alloy YG5-AC and was not observed in the alloy
YG8-AC ( Tables 2, 4 and 5 ). Considering the data for the alloy
Nbe18Sie5Hfe3Tae2Mo (alloy YG4 in Ref. [5]), in which there was
strong macrosegregation of Si, it is concluded that the latter is affected
by the synergy of Hf with refractory metal additions in NbeSi based
melts and that the tendency for macrosegregation of Si in the as cast
alloys decreases in the order of the synergies (Hf þ Mo þ Ta), then
(Mo þ W), then (Hf þ W) and then (Hf þ Mo þ W).
Two types of Nb
ss were observed. One in which no Si was
detected by EPMA and another in which the Si concentration was in
the range reported for the Nb
ss in other silicide based alloys
[4e15,23,32,34,35]. This was also the case for the Nb ss in the alloy
Nbe18Sie5Hfe3Tae2Mo [5].M ae ta l . [23] have reported variation
in W content of Nb ss grains in the Nb e16Sie10Moe15W alloy, with
slightly higher concentration in the centre than in the outer parts of
Nbss. In the present work the aforementioned difference in Si
concentration in different Nbss grains was accompanied by different
segregation behaviour of W, which depended on the synergy of this
element with Hf or Mo individually (as in the alloys YG5, YG6) or
simultaneously (alloy YG8). Tungsten and Hf exhibited opposite
segregation behaviour in the Nb
ss in YG5-AC suggesting that an
increase in W concentration is accompanied with a decrease in Hf
and Si concentrations in the solid solution. The Mo concentrations in
the Nb
ss in the alloys YG6 and YG8 were higher than those reported
for the Nb e19Sie5Mo and Nb e13Sie4Mo alloys [35]. The concen-
tration of Mo in Nbss was not affected by the presence of W. However,
the W concentration increased in the solid solution with no Si in the
alloy YG6-AC. The segregation behaviour of W and Hf in Nb
ss
observed in YG5-AC and the segregation behaviour of Mo in Nb ss
observed in YG6-AC were observed also in the Nb ss in YG8-AC,
where, however, the synergy of W, Hf and Mo did not lead to the
formation of solid solution grains with no Si. Thus, it is concluded
that in Nb silicide based alloys, the concentrations of Si and refrac-
tory metal in the Nb
ss depend on the synergy of refractory metals in
the presence or absence of Hf.
After the heat treatment only the Nb ss with no Si was present in
the three alloys of this study unlike the Nb ss in the heat treated
alloy Nb e18Sie5Hfe3Tae2Mo [5], which would suggest that the
synergy of Ta with Mo and Hf cannot suppress the solid solubility of
Si in the Nb
ss. It should be noted that when Ta was in synergy with
Ti, Al and Cr in the alloys Nb e24Tie18Sie5Cre5Ale6Ta and
Nbe24Tie18Sie8Cre5Ae6Ta [7] the concentration of Si in the
(Nb,Ti)ss was higher than that in Nb silicide based alloys without
RM additions [6,10,1 1,13,15,32].
In the alloy YG5-HT the W and Hf concentrations in Nb ss were
reduced compared with the as cast alloy. In the alloy YG8-HT the
composition of the solid solution was close to that of the Nbss rich in
W and lean in Hf in the as cast alloy. Thus, in the alloy YG8 the Nb ss
lean in W and rich in Hf was metastable. The W concentrations in the
Nbss in the alloys YG5, YG6 and YG8 were in agreement with the data
for the Nbss in the alloy Nbe16Sie5W in [20]. The Mo concentrations
in the Nb ss in the alloys YG6 and YG8 were higher than those
reported for the Nb ss in Nb e16Sie5Mo [20]. The increase and
decrease respectively in the Mo and Si concentrations in the Nb ss in
the alloy YG6-HT compared with the as cast alloy is consistent with
Ref. [23]. The vol% of Nb ss in the alloy YG8-HT was the same to that

Won-Yong Kim et al. [42] reported that the total oxygen
concentration in Nb eMoeW alloys decreased with increasing Mo
content. Hafnium was effective in scavenging oxygen in the alloys
YG5 and YG8, but in the alloy YG6 the solubility of oxygen in the
near surface area was signi ﬁcantly higher than that reported in
Ref. [42]. Hafnia was formed via the consumption of Hf in the Nb ss.
The formation of HfO 2 and the segregation of Hf in the Nb 5Si3
silicide in the alloys YG5 and YG8 is in agreement with the data for
other Hf containing Nb silicide based alloys [4,5,9,12,17,18].
No nitride particles were observed in the heat treated alloys of
this study. Compared with the heat treated alloys Nb e18Sie
5Hfe5Cr [4] and Nb e18Sie5Hfe3Tae2Mo [5] in which nitride
particles were formed only in the former, it is concluded that the
synergies of W and Hf, W and Mo and W, Mo and Hf were effective
as those of Hf and Al (in the alloy Nbe18Sie5Hfe5Al [4]) and Mo, Hf
and Ta regarding the suppression of the formation of nitride
particles in the heat treated microstructures.
4.1. Hardness
The hardness data for the alloys YG5-AC and Nb e18Sie5Hfe
3Tae2Mo [5] would suggest (i) a stronger effect on hardness from
the synergy of Hf, Mo and Ta compared with that of Hf and W and
(ii) that the presence of Hf in the alloy causes reduction in hardness
after heat treatment. The latter is supported by the hardness data
for the alloys YG5 and YG8, and for the alloys Nb e18Sie5Hfe5Cr
and Nbe18Sie5Hfe5Al [4]. The important role played by Mo and by
the synergy of Mo and Hf in the hardness of the alloys was
conﬁrmed respectively by the data for the alloys YG6 and YG8, of
which the former exhibited remarkable stability of its hardness
after 100 h at 1500
/C14 C, and by the data of the as cast alloys
Nbe18Sie5Hfe3Tae2Mo [5] and YG8. Comparison of the hardness
data for the alloys Nb e18Sie5Hfe5Cr, Nb e18Sie5Hfe5Al [4] and
YG5 shows that W had a stronger effect on the hardness than Cr or
Al. The alloys YG5 and YG6 had similar volume fractions of Nb
ss in
the as cast and heat treated conditions. The lower hardness of the
alloy YG5-HT was attributed to the effect of Hf on the hardness of
Nb
5Si3 [4].
5. Conclusions
In this work we studied the effects of Hf, Mo and W on the
microstructure and hardness of the alloys Nb e20Sie5Hfe5W
(YG5), Nb e20Sie5Moe3W (YG6) and Nb e20Sie5Hfe5Moe3W
(YG8). The conclusions of this work are as follows:
- Macrosegregation of Si was strong in YG6-AC, was affected by
the synergy of Hf with refractory metal additions and
decreased in the order Mo þ W, then Hf þ W and then
Hf þ Mo þ W.
- The phases present in the as cast alloys YG5, YG6 and YG8 were
the Nb ss,N b 5Si3 and HfO 2, the Nb ss,N b 3Si and Nb 5Si3 and the
Nbss,N b5Si3 and HfO2, respectively.
- A lamellar Nb ss þ Nb5Si3 microstructure formed in all three
alloys.
- In the as cast alloys YG5 and YG6 there were Nb ss grains with
no Si content.
- In the as cast alloy YG8 there was strong segregation of Hf and
W in the Nb ss.
- There were Hf rich areas in Nb 5Si3 in the alloys YG5 and YG8.
- The Nb ss with no Si and Nb 5Si3 were present in all three heat
treated alloys and more HfO 2 was formed in the heat treated
alloys YG5 and YG8.
- The stability of the Nb ss þ Nb5Si3 lamellar microstructure
decreased in the order of the heat treated alloys YG5, YG8, and
YG6.
- In the presence of Hf the transformation of bNb5Si3 to aNb5Si3
was enhanced.
- The hardness of the alloys containing Hf was reduced after heat
treatment.
