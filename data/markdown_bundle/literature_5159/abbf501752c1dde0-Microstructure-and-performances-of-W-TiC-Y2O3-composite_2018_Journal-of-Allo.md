abstract
The mechano-chemical and wet-chemical methods were applied to prepare W-12 vol% TiC-4vol% Y 2O3
(hereinafter referred to as W eTiCeY2O3) precursors that were restored under 800 /C14 C of hydrogen for 1 h,
and the composite powders were sintered under different sintering technologies after being restored.
Finally, the sintering bodies prepared through the two technologies were subjected to laser thermal
impact and deuterium eion irradiation tests. According to the tests, the W eTiCeY
2O3 composite powders
prepared using the mechano-chemical method displayed better dispersity, whereas the W eTiCeY2O3
sintering bodies prepared using the mechano-chemical method displayed higher density, smaller grains,
higher hardness, and a more uniform distribution of the second phase and generated a more stable oxide
Y2(Ti2O7) and solid solution (W,Ti)C 1-x. Moreover, the W eTiCeY2O3 composite material prepared by the
mechano-chemical method displayed better thermal shock resistance and anti-irradiation performance.

1. Introduction
Tungsten and its alloy are considered the most promising ﬁrst
wall material facing with plasma for future nuclear fusion devices
(plasma-facing material, PFM) because of tungsten's high melting
point, high thermal conductivity, high high-temperature strength,
low sputtering yield and volume of tritium retention [1e5]. Tung-
sten materials shall be provided with better high-temperature
resistance and anti-radiation performance as PFMs, but the tung-
sten itself has low-temperature fragility, high ductile ebrittle
transition temperature (about 400 /C14 C), low recrystallization tem-
perature (about 1400 /C14 C) and irradiation fragility [6e8]. Therefore,
the key to applying tungsten materials in fusion reactors relies in
improving high-temperature performance (e.g., boundary local-
mode ELMs and transient thermal load environment) and anti-
irradiation performance (e.g., diplogen retention).
Many methods aim to improve the performance of tungsten
alloy; for example, by adding a second phase, such as nanometer
oxides (La 2O3 and Y 2O3) [9,10] and nanometer carbides (TiC and
Ta2C) [1 1,12]. Carbide particles (e.g., TiC) may hinder the develop-
ment of tungsten grains during sintering. When alloy materials
bear loads, such particles can deactivate or de ﬂect cracks that
hinder their expansion. The oxide (e.g., Y2O3) can absorb impurities,
purify the grain boundary, promote sintering, and improve
compactness. In addition, improving the preparation technology of
tungsten alloy can realize high compactness and a small grain size
for materials. In addition, a more balanced distribution of the sec-
ond phase can obviously improve the anti-irradiation performance
of materials. At present, common preparation technologies for
powders include chemical sedimentation, wet-chemical, and high-
energy ball milling methods [13e16]. The disadvantages of the wet-
chemical method include dif ﬁculty in introducing impurities and
small powder size, while the advantages of the mechanical method
include the apparent activation effect and easier densifying ability.
On the one hand, this test applies a mechano-chemical method
[1 7,18]consists of ball milling of metal oxide powders and a sub-
sequent hydrogen reduction to prepare composite powders, which
prevents the introduction of impurities for long-term high-energy

2. Experimental details
2.1. Preparation technology for W eTiCeY
2O3 composite
2.1.1. Preparation of W eTiCeY2O3 composite material by the wet-
chemical method
The NM TiC and Y 2O3 powders (produced by Shanghai Chao'er
Nano Technology Co., Ltd.) used in the test are provided with an
average grain size of 50 nm and purity of 99.9%, which is analyti-
cally pure for all chemical reagents (produced by Sinopharm
Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd.). Y(NO
3)3 $ 6H2O and (NH4)6H2W12O40 $
xH2O (AMT) with the necessary quality are weighed for test, dis-
solved by deionized water, and mixed under ultrasound conditions.
Then, necessary NM TiC powders are weighed into the mixed
liquid. A certain amount of C 2H2O4$2H2O is prepared as the pre-
cipitant, dissolved in water, and then slowly added into the mixed
liquid. Subsequently, the mixed liquid is added into an oil bath pan
at 180 /C14 C for evaporation and precipitation, and a magnetic stirrer is
used until dryness is achieved. Agate mortar is used to grind the
dried powder, which is added into a vacuum tube-type sintering
furnace for reduction. The restored W eTiCeY2O3 composite pow-
der is subjected to SPS sintering ( Fig. 1).
2.1.2. Preparation of W eTiCeY2O3 composite material by the
mechano-chemical method
First, (NH 4)6H2W12O40 $ xH2O (AMT) is heated to 400 /C14 Ci na
chamber electric furnace and oxidized into tungsten oxide. The
tungstenoxide, TiC, and Y2O3 powders with the required quality for
the test are weighed, added into the tungsten carbide-type ball-
milling pot, then put it in a full-dimensional ball mill (QM-QX,
Nanjing NanDa Instrument Plant) and set to a ball material ratio of
10:1, rotational ball mill speed of 300 r/min, and ball-milling time
of 20 h to obtain the complicated precursor. The precursor powders
are placed into a vacuum tube-type sintering furnace for reduction.
Then, SPS sintering is conducted for the composite powder after
being restored ﬁnally. The conditions of the reduction and sintering
technologies are the same with those of the W eTiCeY2O3 com-
posite material prepared by the wet-chemical method.
2.2. Powders and samples characterization
The microstructures of the prepared powders and the samples
were investigated using ﬁeld-emission scanning electron micro-
scopy (FESEM; SU-8020) and high-resolution transmission electron
(HRTEM) microscopy (JEM-2100F) analyses.
The surface of the W eTiCeY2O3 composite prepared using both
methods was implemented with precision polishing, and the laser
instrument (LSW-1000, Riton Laser Co., Ltd) was employed in the
laser thermal shock test. The test was conducted under the pro-
tective atmosphere of argon, with an argon ﬂow rate of 10 L/min;
Gaussian distribution for laser beam, with a laser scanning fre-
quency of 15 Hz; laser spot diameter D of 0.6 mm; the pulse width
of 1 ms; and laser current of 60 A. Through calculation, the density
of laser thermal impact energy density carried by the samples is
about 159 MW/m 2.
Linear cutting was applied to cut the W eTiCeY2O3 composite
prepared by both methods into round disks with a diameter of
3 mm. Then, the samples were ground into a 0.1 mm sheet and
polished precisely on both sides into mirror planes. The samples
were placed into a high-vacuum environment and irradiated under
5 KeV D2
þ. The irradiation dose is 1.0 /C2 1020 D2
þ/m2, and the samples
were irradiated in room temperature. After ion irradiation, the
samples were taken out and transferred to the gas thermal
desorption instrument (produced by Kyoto University Research
Reactor Institute) for the TDS test. When the D retention behavior of
the samples is measured using the TDS, the temperature scope is
from room temperature to 1050 K and the heating rate is 1 K/s.

3.2. Sintering performance of the W eTiCeY2O3 composites
prepared by different technologies
The density and hardness of the sintering body after the sin-
tering of the W eTiCeY2O3 composites prepared using the two
technologies are presented in Table 1. As shown in the table, the

pared using the mechano-chemical method are higher than those

Fig. 3 (a) and (b) show the appearance of the corrosive surface
after polishing and the local diagram of the W eTiCeY2O3 com-
posites prepared using the wet-chemical and mechano-chemical
methods. On the basis of the surface appearance shown in Fig. 3 ,
the size of the composite grain prepared using the wet-chemical
method is about 2 mm, whereas the size of the composite grain
prepared using the mechano-chemical method is approximately
1 mm. On the basis of the powder appearance shown in Fig. 2 and
the data in Table 1, the size of the powder prepared using the wet-
chemical method before reaching the temperature of sintering
densiﬁcation is in nanoscale, its surface energy is large, and tung-
sten powder tends to develop. However, no obvious development
of composite powder prepared using the mechano-chemical
method during sintering is observed. Before the sintering body
reaches the sintering densi ﬁcation temperature, the size of the
powder grain prepared by the wet-chemical method varies, and the
diffusion path of the powder particles is large during sintering,
which is not bene ﬁcial to sintering densi ﬁcation. However, the
powder prepared by the mechano-chemical method has a small
size, a large contact area, and has a short diffusion path during
sintering, resulting in easy sintering densi ﬁcation. In addition, the
microhardness of the W eTiCeY2O3 composite prepared by such
method is higher than the one by the wet-chemical method
because the small size and strong ﬁne-crystal strengthening effect
of the materials prepared by the mechano-chemical method.
According to the partially enlarged detail and the energy spec-
trum of the second phase, which are indicated by the arrows shown
in Fig. 3 (a) and (b), the second phase of composites materials
prepared by the above two methods are distributed in the grain
boundary and interior, indicating that both methods can prepare
composite materials with a uniformly distributed second phase.

3.3. Microstructure and composition analysis of the W eTiCeY2O3
composite prepared by the mechano-chemical method
From the above analysis, the second phase prepared by the
above two methods contains Ti and Y. Thus, studying the micro-
cosmic composition of the second phase is necessary. The
mechano-chemical method is selected to prepare the WeTiCeY2O3
composite for the analysis of the microstructure and composite
under the transmission electron microscopy (TEM). Fig. 4 shows the
TEM low-power appearance of the W eTiCeY2O3 body prepared

the grain boundary, as shown in “see b,” and in the grain interior, as
shown in “see a. ”
As shown in Fig. 5 (a), the “see a” region in Fig. 4 is enlarged. As
shown in Fig. 5 (b), (c), and (d), the HRTEM of the diffraction pattern
and matrix in the region of Fig. 5 (a) and the second phase are
observed to conduct calibration analysis. According to the calibra-
tion results, the matrix of the composite material is W and the
second phase in the region is Y2O3 that mainly grows in the internal
W grain.
As shown in Fig. 6 (a), the “see b” region in Fig. 4 is enlarged. The
HRTEM at the boundary between the second phase and the matrix

calibration results show that the second phase in the region is TiO 2,
where TiC absorbs oxygen from the grain boundary and the other
interfaces during sintering purifying the grain boundary.
Fig. 7 shows the TEM appearance of a certain region in the
samples. The whole grain has clearly different morphologies,
indicating the presence of various elements in the interior of the

The electronic incident angle of the TEM is adjusted to obtain

3.5. Retention behavior of the W eTiCeY2O3 composites prepared
by different technologies
Fig. 12 shows the TDS data map of the samples prepared under
different technologies. For deuterium, the defects in W, such as
dislocation loops, vacancies, and precipitates, played a role in the
trapping. In TDS experiments, the deuterium trapped by the
different defects is released at different temperatures, with the
number of defects affecting the release of deuterium. If the peak
position is similar, the same type of defects may exist. As shown in
the ﬁgure, at the desorption temperature of 400 K, two samples
have desorption peaks of D 2, indicating that the corresponding
traps are of the same kind [25]. However, the retention curve of the
sample prepared by the mechano-chemical method is generally
lower than the retention curve of the wet-chemical method. Hence,
the sample prepared by the mechano-chemical method has a better
resistance of deuterium retention.
Based on the curve integral shown in Fig. 12, the total volume of
the D retention is obtained. In the previous work [26,27], we also
carried out the same deuterium retention experiment of pure W
and W-Nb alloys, and the results showed that the total D retention
volume of pure W was 6.3 /C2 1016 in such conditions. As shown in
Table 2, the total D retention volume of the samples prepared by the
two methods are both lower than pure W and W-Nb alloys. How-
ever, the total D retention volume of the sample prepared by the
mechano-chemical method is apparently lower than that of the
wet-chemical method, further indicating the better anti-radiation
performance of the sample prepared by the thermo-mechanical
method.
3.6. Discussions on the better performance of the sample prepared
by mechano-chemical method
Compared with pure mechanical and chemical methods,
mechano-chemical combines the advantages of mechanical and
chemical methods. In fact, in many works referred to fabrication of
WeCu composite, the researchers have used this method in a
mature way [17,18,28]. These works also prove that mechano-
chemical method can prepare nearly fully dense composites with
homogeneous microstructure. The same method used in a second-
phase doped tungsten composite material, which should have a
better effect than pure chemical method or mechanical method.
And this work shows that samples prepared by the mechano-
chemical method can show better performance than that of wet-
chemical method.
The composite blocks prepared by mechano-chemical method
have better performance, mainly because they have higher density
and ﬁner grain. The re ﬁnement of grain makes the materials
prepared by mechano-chemical method have higher hardness. At
the same time, the grain boundary density increases. In thermal
shock experiments, the sample prepared by wet-chemical has
lower density, so the materials have more internal porosity and
defects, which leads to the formation of cracks under the action of
heat and crack extension become easier, eventually form more
crack and crack width is wider. The formation and motion of the
crack will become more dif ﬁcult in the samples prepared by
mechano-chemical method due to higher density and greater
grain boundary density. Therefore, the samples prepared by
mechano-chemical method have better thermal shock resistance.
In the deuterium retention experiment, there are the natural de-
fects in the samples [20], such as the pore and grain boundary. The
density of the samples prepared by the wet-chemical method is
lower and have more defects, resulting in a higher D retention
peak with a lower temperature than the mechano-chemical
method and more pores and defects make the total D retention
volume higher. And that's why the samples prepared by the
mechano-chemical method have better anti-radiation perfor-
mance. Moreover, mechano-chemical method has its unique ad-
vantages so that it might be a new method for preparation of
ultraﬁne tungsten materials with high density and homogeneous
microstructure.

4. Conclusions
1) The W eTiCeY2O3 composite powder was prepared successfully
using the mechano-chemical method. The powders prepared by
the wet-chemical method were in nanoscale, but were easily
gathered. The size of the powder prepared by the mechano-
chemical method was about 1 mm, which has better dispersity.
2) Compared with the wet-chemical method, the W eTiCeY2O3
composite prepared using the mechano-chemical method was
featured with higher density and hardness, smaller grains, and
stronger material strength.
3) During the process of preparing the W eTiCeY2O3 composite
using the mechano-chemical method, TiC and Y 2O3 diffused
mutually and generated a more stable oxide Y 2(Ti2O7). More-
over, W and TiC generated a solid solution (W,Ti)C 1-x.
4) After the laser thermal shock test relative to the wet-chemical
method, the W eTiCeY2O3 composite prepared by the
mechano-chemical method had no apparent erosion on the
surface and had a small number of cracks with small widths,
indicating a better thermal shock resistance performance.
5) After the D

WeTiCeY2O3 composite prepared by the mechano-chemical
method and its total volume is also lower, indicating the bet-
ter anti-radiation performance of the samples prepared by the
mechano-chemical method.
