Abstract
Ni-based single crystal superalloys play a vital role in critical areas such as aerospace and gas turbines due to their superior 
high-temperature strength. However, accurately predicting the creep rupture life of these alloys has been a challenge. In this 
study, an artificial neural network-based prediction model was developed to effectively improve the accuracy of creep life 
prediction for Ni-based single crystal superalloys by incorporating 15 new descriptors. The R2 for the test set was 0.8595. 
Further, the SHAP value results guided the design of new low-cost, high-performance alloys, among which the new designed 
alloy (5.91 Cr, 6.21 Co, 1.62 Mo, 6.37 W, 5.64 Al, 7.22 Ta, 1.45 Re, 0.52 Ti and Ni balance, wt%) showed a higher creep life 
than the existing alloy CMSX-4, while having a Re content < 1.5 wt%. The results not only provide new tools for superalloy 
design, but also confirm the practical value of machine learning in materials science.
Keywords Ni-based single crystal superalloys · Creep life · ANN model · Alloy design
1 Introduction
Superalloys, renowned for their exceptional strength at 
elevated temperatures, play crucial roles in industries such 
as aerospace and gas turbines [1 –3]. Ni-based single crys-
tal superalloys, distinguished by their unique single-crystal 
structure, exhibit superior mechanical properties and creep 
resistance at high temperatures [4 , 5]. However, creep life 
prediction of these alloys has been a challenge in the field 
of materials science, where traditional experimental meth-
ods are time-consuming and costly. Therefore, research-
ers have attempted to make creep life predictions. In the 
past, creep life prediction methods were mainly based on 
the time–temperature-parameter (TTP) method [6 ] and the 
establishment of creep constitutive models (CCM) [2 ]. The 
time–temperature parametric method, represented by the 
Larson-Miller criterion, can predict creep life over a range 
of temperatures and stresses similar to actual conditions. 
Creep intrinsic modeling, on the other hand, is applicable 
to alloys with the same creep mechanism. All these methods 
have major limitations and are difficult to predict creep life 
for unknown alloys or experimental conditions.
Recently, with the significant increase in computing 
ability and the development of big data technologies, 
machine learning (ML) has become a powerful tool for 
solving complex scientific problems [7 , 8]. The ability of 
machine learning models to learn and extract laws from 
large amounts of data opens new possibilities for material 
performance prediction. In particular, the neural network 
model, by constructing a multi-layer neural network, is 
able to automatically extract the complex features of the 
data, significantly improving the accuracy of the predic-
tion. This data-driven approach has been successfully 
applied to the design and development of materials such as 
high-entropy alloys [9] and chalcogenides [10]. In the field 
of superalloys, our team has prepared high-throughput 
experimental samples by diffusion multiple (a conglom-
erate composed of three or more distinct metallic blocks

the predictive capabilities of machine learning models. To 
more accurately predict the creep life of Ni-based single 
crystal superalloys, 15 new descriptors, which are closely 
related to the chemical composition and microstructure, 
are introduced with the aim of providing more compre-
hensive information on material properties. The results 
show that by adding these new descriptors, the R 2 of the 
ANN neural network model in predicting the creep life of 
Ni-based single crystal superalloys increases. Then, SHAP 
values were calculated for each input feature to explain 
the effect of these descriptors on the creep life. In addi-
tion, a novel low-cost and creep-resistant Ni-based single 
crystal superalloy (Re content < 1.5 wt%) was successfully 
designed with the assistance of this model, which has a 
creep rupture life (982 °C, 248 MPa) higher than that of 
the 2nd generation single crystal alloy CMSX-4. It not 
only validates the effectiveness of the new descriptors, 
but also demonstrates the potential of machine learning 
applications in materials science. This study provides new 
perspectives on the design and application of superalloys 
and offers an important reference for the development of 
future models for predicting material performances.
2 Methods
2.1 New Descriptors
Figure 1 documents the relationship between creep rupture 
life [19] and some compositional, physical parameter and 
other features of 1st–5th generation single crystal superal-
loys at 1100 °C, 137 MPa. In terms of chemical composi-
tion, the creep life of single crystal alloys grows sequentially 
from low to high generations accompanied by an increase 
in Re content. The Re element exists mainly in the form of 
solid solution, slowing down the diffusion rate of the alloy-
ing elements, thus reducing the creep rate and improving 
the creep resistance of the alloy, and these laws have been 
widely reported [20, 21]. Figure  1b–d describes several 
composition-dependent thermodynamic parameters which 
show a visible correlation with the creep life. For example, 
the ΔSconf values of superalloys are decreasing with increas-
ing generations, and the creep life has a negative correla-
tion with it. Likewise, Ω (a geometric parameter) used to 
predict solid solution formation, shows a similar trend. Ω 
is the ratio of the entropy changes ΔSconf due to mixing to 
the heat change ΔH mix during mixing at the melting point 
temperature Tm is described. It can provide information 
about the thermodynamic properties of the mixing process. 
ΔV∕V is often used to describe volume changes in an alloy 
or solution due to mixing of different components. ΔV∕V 
(the parameters of a novel two-dimensional structure map) 
exhibited a positive correlation with creep life. Based on 
these findings, an attempt was made to establish an intrin-
sic relationship with the creep life of the alloy composition 
using the new descriptors. Table 1 shows the 15 descriptors 
used in this paper, ΔSconf , ΔH mix , δ , etc., respectively. These 
thermodynamic parameters and their physical significance 
are described in detail in Table  1. These descriptors are all 
closely related to the composition of the alloy and may be 
able to provide more comprehensive information for the pre-
diction of creep life, the exact values were calculated by a 
self-written Python script.

3 Results and Discussion
3.1 Selection of Input Features and Model 
Hyperparameters
Figure 5 describes in detail the selection process of input 
features and hyperparameters of the models. The composi-
tion of the alloy, the test temperature and the stresses must 
be considered during the selection of the input features, as it 
contains the most essential information. In addition, the 15 
descriptors described in Table 1 are calculated based on the 
basic information described above and do not require addi-
tional testing. To find the optimal number of input features, 
the SHAP-feature importance values of each input feature in 
the ANN model were calculated, as shown in Fig. 5a. Next, 
based on the 13 features containing composition, tempera-
ture and stress, the input features were added one by one 
based on the magnitude of the feature importance value until 
all the new descriptors were imported completely. Figure 5b 
demonstrates how the number of input features affects the 
predictive ability of the ANN model. To reduce the risk of 
model overfitting, this paper divides the dataset into train-
ing set and test set according to the ratio of 9:1, and then 
performs tenfold cross-validation on the training set, so the 
results shown in Fig.  5b contain the average of the results 
of the validation set in tenfold training and the results of the 
final test set. The results show that the predictive ability of 
the ANN model fluctuates with the number of input features, 
both in terms of MSE and R2. However, with the number 
of input features being 28, the model exhibited the highest

and test sets. Figure  5c shows the difference between the 
prediction results of the validation set in the tenfold cross-
validation and the test set of the ANN model. If the differ -

overfitting. The results show that the model performs best 
when the number of input features is 28. In summary, in this 
paper, 28 input features are selected as the input features of 
the ANN model. Moreover, the ANN model structure with 
different numbers of hidden layers and neurons are consid-

For example, features such as “Stress”, “ ΔHmix ”, “ Λ ”, and 
“ /u1D6FF ” have high positive SHAP means, suggesting that they 
contribute positively to model predictions in most cases. It 
should be noted that these SHAP values are the results in 
the case of that dataset and are only somewhat interpretable 
for results within that data range. Generally, an increase in 
Stress decreases the creep rupture life of a material, but in 
the 920 data, high Stress tends to correspond to low test 
temperatures and hence exhibits a higher creep rupture life, 
which can explain why the mean of the SHAP values for 
the Stress feature is positive. In addition, ΔHmix , the mix-
ing enthalpy, is a quantity that describes the energy change 
when different components are mixed, which is related to 
the compatibility and interfacial stability of the material and 
may indirectly affect the creep performance. As a physi-
cal parameter, “ Λ ” is difficult to explain the mechanism 
of its action on creep life from the perspective of material 
science, and it is only assumed that it has a certain posi-
tive contribution under a specific data set and ANN model. 
The Misfit feature indicates the degree of lattice mismatch, 
which affects the stability and distribution of the γ′ phase 
and hence the creep properties of the material. In addition, 
the features “T”, “Re”, “Mo” and “W” show negative SHAP 
mean values. It is not difficult to understand that the effect 
of feature “T” on creep life is negative, but the negative 
SHAP values of refractory elements such as “Re”, “Mo” and 
“W” need to be discussed. In fact, this is closely related to 
the compositional characteristics and service conditions of 
different generations of single crystal alloys. Lower-gener -
ation single-crystal alloys have lower content of refractory 
elements and lower service temperatures, showing longer 
creep rupture life. However, higher-generation single-crystal 
alloys exhibit higher service requirements, and despite the 
raised content of these refractory elements, they may have 
lower creep rupture life in response to high temperatures and 
high stresses. This also explains why the content of refrac-
tory elements is negatively correlated to the ANN model. 
Another descriptor worth mentioning is Deff, in materials 
science knowledge, an increase in the diffusion coefficient 
may indeed be detrimental to the creep rupture life as it may 
promote elemental diffusion at the γ/γ′ interface, accelerat-
ing the coarsening of the γ′ phase and thus reducing the 
creep resistance and life of the alloy. However, this study is 
a mathematical fitting problem to explore the effect of each 
characteristic parameter on creep life from a computational 
point of view. The results show (Fig. 9d) that there is a weak 
positive correlation between the creep life and the effec-
tive diffusion coefficient (Deff) in the analyzed dataset. This 
finding, although contrary to conventional understanding in 
materials science, does reflect the actual situation in the data 
set. It not only accurately maps the specific phenomena in 
the dataset, but also profoundly reveals the need for comple-
mentarity between computer science and materials science.

volatility of the feature’s effect. A low standard deviation

precipitation strengthening effect, thus enhancing their high-

is usually closely related to the stability and variation of 
their microstructure, especially the γ′ phase (Gamma prime).

accelerates the process of material design and optimization 
because it reduces the reliance on experimental iterations 
and allows the designer to be guided by ML model for more 
purposeful material development.

5 Conclusions
In this study, an artificial neural network (ANN)-based pre-
diction model incorporating 15 new descriptors was success-
fully developed to improve the accuracy of creep life predic-
tion for Ni-based single crystal superalloys. The model was 
tested for generalization performance with new alloy data 
and applied to composition design. Specific conclusions are 
presented below:
1. Through tenfold cross-validation, the ANN model 
combined with the new 15 descriptors showed the best 
predictive ability under several evaluation metrics, 
including mean square error (MSE) and coefficient of 
determination (R2 of training set is 0.8697, that of test 
set is 0.8595).

4. A new alloy T1 with low cost (Re < 1.5 wt%) and high
