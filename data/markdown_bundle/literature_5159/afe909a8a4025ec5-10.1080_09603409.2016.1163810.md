Mechanistic modelling of time-dependent 
fatigue crack growth in Ni-based superalloys
Kwai S. Chan
Advanced Ni-based superalloys for aero-engine disk applications can experience multiple damage 
mechanisms that include oxidation, creep and stress corrosion in additional to cycle-dependent 
fatigue crack initiation and growth. Interactions of these various damage mechanisms can lead to the 
occurrence of frequency, temperature, heat dwell and environmental effects on the fatigue crack growth 
response and service life of Ni-based superalloys. In this overview paper, the development of a set of 
generic microstructure-based fatigue crack growth models for treating concurrent time-dependent and 
cycle-dependent damage mechanisms at the microstructural levels is summarised. Key features of the 
mechanistic models are highlighted and utilised to assess the variability of time-dependent fatigue crack 
growth response in the threshold and power-law regimes due to variations in microstructure such as 
grain size, and γ′ size. This methodology provides a pathway for evaluating microstructural effects on 
multiple damage modes and extending the service lives of hot-section components.
Keywords: Time-dependent crack growth, Environmental degradation, Microstructural effects, Life prediction, Crack growth threshold, Ni alloys, Dwell 
fatigue, Fatigue crack growth
Introduction
Hot-section components in advanced turbo-propulsion sys-
tems, which are typically made of Ni-based superalloys, are 
expected to experience long duration dwell at high temper -
atures. Under high heat dwell conditions, Ni-based super -
alloys may be susceptible to the occurrence of concurrent 
time-dependent damage modes such as oxidation, creep and 
stress corrosion in addition to cycle-dependent fatigue crack 
initiation and growth. 1–9 This coupling of cycle-dependent 
and time-dependent fracture mechanisms can result in syn-
ergetic interaction effects and enhanced crack growth rates 
that require additional considerations in life-prediction meth-
odologies and computational codes such as DARWIN ®.10 
To account for synergetic interactions of multiple damage 
modes on component life reliability, a set of mechanistic 
time-dependent fatigue crack growth models
11–17 has been 
developed recently for treating multiple damage modes in 
advanced Ni-based alloys for operations with long duration at 
high temperatures where time-dependent degradation mech-
anisms such as creep, oxidation, corrosion and stress rupture 
may compete with time-independent fatigue crack growth as 
the component life-limiting mechanism.
In the literature, the coupling of time-dependent and 
cycle-dependent crack growth has sometimes been referred 
to as creep–fatigue interactions. However, extensive work in 
the literature has shown that many aspects of the time depend-
ency associated with fatigue crack growth at elevated tem -
peratures actually originates from environmentally induced 
fracture processes which involve the interactions of oxygen 
in air with a crack tip in metallic alloys. The interaction of 
oxygen with metallic alloys such as iron-,
18 nickel-6,19,20 and 
cobalt-based superalloys21 at elevated temperatures can lead 
to two closely related environmental degradation mecha -
nisms, which have been coined as: (1) oxygen6,18 or dynamic 
embrittlement,22–25 and (2) stress accelerated grain boundary 
oxidation and fracture of the oxidised grain boundaries. 6,18 
Both degradation mechanisms are time-dependent fracture 
processes that proceed along grain boundaries at elevated 
temperatures (≥650 °C) where intake of oxygen by grain 
boundary diffusion can occur at relatively short times. A 
recent study has revealed that dynamic embrittlement in 
Ni-based superalloys can be mitigated and suppressed by the 
formation of common oxides such as Cr
2O3 and Al2O3 that 
exhibit large transformation strains and induced large com-
pressive stresses ahead of the crack tip or in the crack wake.15 
As a result, time-dependent crack growth in Ni-based and 
Co-based superalloys is strongly affected by the formation 
and fracture of oxide layers along grain boundaries located 
ahead of the crack tip.
14,15
Besides oxidation, time-dependent deformation in 
Ni-based superalloys can lead to creep crack growth as 
the consequence of the formation, growth and coalescence 
of creep cavities along grain boundaries.
3,26–29 Oxidation-
induced crack growth originates from grain boundaries of 
surface grains that are exposed to air or oxygen at elevated 
temperatures, while creep cavitation and creep crack growth 
typically occur in vacuum, inert environments or in interior 
grain boundaries which are not exposed to air or lie outside 
the oxygen-affected zone. At sufficiently high temperatures, 
certain Ni-based alloys may exhibit concurrent creep- and 
oxidation-induced crack growth, which may lead to enhanced 
crack growth rates compared to individual crack growth 
mechanism acting alone.
30 The role of small-scale creep at 
the crack tip also plays an important role in affecting the 
time-dependent crack growth response in a manner that is 
contrary to traditional creep crack growth. In particular,

Synergistic interaction of degradation 
mechanisms
The presence of time-dependent degradation mechanisms 
such as oxygen (dynamic) embrittlement, oxidation, stress 
relaxation and creep can contribute to the time-dependent 
fatigue crack growth response of Ni-based superalloys. 
Synergistic interaction of these time-dependent degradation 
mechanisms can influence the time-dependent crack growth 
response in complex manners. In particular, concurrent deg-

Summary
This overview paper summarises a set of mechanistic models 
that has been developed for treating the effects of competing 
time-dependent damage modes including oxidation, creep 
and stress relaxation in the presence of concurrent cycle-de-
pendent (fatigue) crack growth on long-term performance and 
reliability of engine disks made from Ni-based superalloys. 
These microstructure-based time-dependent crack growth 
models have been implemented into a probabilistic life-pre-
diction code, DARWIN, to provide a platform for assessing 
the risk of fracture in gas turbine disks that could exhibit 
location-specific microstructures, microstructural variability 
or evolution of microstructures after thermal exposures at 
elevated temperatures for extended time periods. Using the 
enhanced risk analysis tool and material constants calibrated 
to ME3 data, the effect of time-dependent crack growth on 
the risk of fracture in a turbo-engine component was demon-
strated for a generic (fictitious) rotor design and a realistic 
mission profile. The enhanced DARWIN code was utilised 
in this benchmark problem to demonstrate the potential use 
of such an analysis tool for assessing grain size variability 
on the part life and reliability of a turbo-engine subjected to 
aggressive mission profiles that contain long durations at high 
peak and dwell temperatures, where time-dependent damage 
modes such as oxidation-induced crack growth, creep and 
stress corrosion or rupture can compete with cycle-dependent 
damage modes. DARWIN simulations indicate that the initial 
disk risk is controlled by time-controlled crack growth (da/
dt), while the later disk risk is controlled by cycle-dependent 
crack growth (da/dN). Furthermore, controlling grain size or 
gamma prime size can be an effective means for mitigating 
time-dependent crack growth and fracture risk in turbo-engine 
components under dwell fatigue conditions at high service 
temperatures.
