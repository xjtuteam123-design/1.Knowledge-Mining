abstract
Compressive strengths of twelve Ir-1at%X binary alloys were evaluated at 2223 K to understand the
strengthening effects of alloying elements. The alloying elements were selected from group 4 to group 10
in the periodic table. The maximum strength of about 65 MPa was obtained in Ir eHf and IreZr alloys. The
effect of adding multiple alloying elements on strength was also investigated. Isothermal oxidation
behavior was investigated at 1373 K for Ir alloys including Nb and/or Hf. The weight of all the tested
alloys decreased linearly during the oxidation test, indicating that Ir oxide evaporated during oxidation.

1. Introduction
Iridium, which melts at 2739 K, is used in crucibles for growing
optical materials or in the tips of car engine spark plugs to prolong
plug life as well as provide high strength at high temperature and
good spark characteristics. In such applications, Ir or Ir alloys are
used at around 2273 K and thus require high strength at high
temperature and good oxidation resistance. To design high tem-
perature structural materials, the strengthening effects of alloying
elements are very important. However, the dif ﬁculty of conducting
strength tests over 1473 K has hindered investigations of these
metals at ultra-high temperature.
The high temperature strength and creep behavior of Ir were
investigated by Lupton and Fischer et al. in the late 1990s to early
2000s [1e3]. Ir alloys with the fcc and L1 2 two-phase coherent
structure were also investigated as ultra-high temperature mate-
rials in the late 1990s to early 2000s [4e8]. However, since then,
there have been few studies on the high temperature strength of Ir
or Ir alloys.
In our previous study, we investigated the effect of alloying ele-
ments on the strength of Ir alloys at 2223 K[9]. Pt, Rh, Hf and Zr were
chosen as alloying elements. Pt and Rh were fully soluble in Ir, but Hf
and Zr had limited solubility. We found that Hf and Zr, which have
limited solubility, were more effective for strengthening Ir than Pt
and Rh which are fully soluble in Ir. In the present study, we focused
on other alloying elements that have greater solubility than Hf and
Zr but less solubility than Pt and Rh, and systematically investigated
the strengthening effect in Ir at 2223 K using twelve alloys. The effect
of adding multiple alloying elements on strength and the oxidation
behavior of Ir alloys were also investigated to understand the po-
tential of Ir alloys as ultra-high temperature materials.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructure
Typical microstructures of Ir solid solution hardened alloys are
shown in Fig. 1. The fcc single phase without any precipitates was
observed in all samples. The grain sizes of the investigated alloys
are summarized in Table 2. The grain size of pure Ir is about 2 mm,
and the size did not change signi ﬁcantly in most of the alloys upon
adding the second element; a reduction in grain size to 500 mmw a s
observed only in the Ir e1Hf and Ir e1Zr alloys. In the Ir e1Nb and
Ire1V alloys, small grains of several hundred mm in size were also
observed partially together with large grains of about 2 mm in size.
It is well known that strength is proportional to the inverse
square root of grain size; that is, high strength is obtained in alloys
with smaller grain size, which is known as the Hall ePetch relation.
The Hall ePetch relation applies when the grain size is between
1 mm and 10 mm. In this study, the grain size of all tested alloys
exceeded 500 mm, and so the grain size effect was considered to be
negligible.

caused lattice expansion and lattice distortion.

3.3. Mechanical properties
The 0.2% ﬂow stresses at 2223 K are summarized in Table 2.N o
precipitates were observed in all the tested alloys, so the
improvement of strength was considered to be due to solid solution
hardening. It is well known that solid solution hardening is corre-
lated with a combined parameter of atomic size mis ﬁt and shear
modulus mis ﬁt as shown by Fleischer [12]. Since the study by
Fleischer, there have been a number of studies on modifying the
model. Mishima applied Fleischer ’s model to Ni solid solution alloy
and found that the solid solution hardening of Ni can not be
interpreted by the combined parameter related to the elastic
interaction between solute atoms and screw dislocations suggested
by Fleischer [13]. Instead, the modi ﬁed combined parameter
related to elastic interaction between solute atoms and edge dis-
locations was shown to be correlated with the solid solution
hardening effect [13]. It was also indicated that the combined
parameter involving edge dislocations was caused by mainly the
atomic size mis ﬁt, and that the modulus mis ﬁt has minimal effect.
Therefore, in this study, we focused on only atomic size mis ﬁtt o
analyze the solid solution hardening of Ir alloys.
Fig. 2 plots the 0.2% ﬂow stress at 2233 K against atomic size
misﬁt listed in Table 1. The strength of pure Ir is represented by a
solid circle. Different symbols are used for the solute atoms in the
different groups of the periodic table. The 0.2% ﬂow stress tended to
increase almost linearly with increasing atomic size misﬁt. The ﬂow
stress of Ir was 19.7 MPa and the highest stress was found to be
about 65 MPa, three times that of Ir, in the Ir e1Hf and Ire1Zr alloys
with large atomic size mis ﬁt. Ru, V and Re whose atomic sizes are
close to that of Ir did not exhibit any obvious solid solution hard-
ening; the effect became clear when the size mis ﬁt was above 0.02.
Our results were consistent with those of a previous study on Ni eX
and NieCoeX alloys obtained by Mishima ’s group [14,15].
Mishima’s group also found a correlation between solid solution
hardening and solid solubility in binary and ternary Ni alloys and
binary Cu and Au alloys [14e17], that is, an alloying element with
small solubility to Ni has large solid solution hardening. The solu-
bility limit was related to the absolute value of the heat of forma-
tion of intermetallic compounds formed in Ni eX binary alloys
[15,16]. The heat of formation of intermetallic compounds is pro-
portional to the interaction parameter between solute atom and Ni.
When the intermetallic phase is more stable than the solid solution
phase, the heat of formation becomes negatively large due to strong
interaction between solute atom and Ni, causing the small solubi-
lity of the solute atom to Ni. Their results indicated that the solu-
bility limit is a simple parameter which can be used to predict solid
solution hardening.
Next, 0.2% ﬂow stresses of Ir alloys were plotted as a function of
solubility limit, that is, the reciprocal of the maximum solid solu-
bility, 1/Cmax, at 2223 K in Fig. 3(a). The trend that alloying elements
with small solubility limit have large solid solution hardening was
observed and was consistent with the case of Ni alloys [14e17].
Fig. 3(b) plots the correlation between the atomic size mis ﬁt and 1/
Cmax. From the ﬁgure, it is clear that the alloying elements with
large atomic size mis ﬁt had a small solubility limit, in accordance
with the well known Hume-Rothery ’s rule. From Fig. 3 ,i tw a s
concluded that there is a correlation between solubility limit and
solution hardening in Ir binary alloys at 2223 K as well as in Ni, Co

were formed, a more accurate, detailed investigation is necessary.
The cross section microstructure of Ire5Hf and Ire5Nbe3Hf was
investigated to characterize the oxide layers, as shown in Fig. 1 1.
The surface layers in both alloys consisted of small particles of
oxides that were not dense. The thickness of the oxide layer was
about 50 mm for Ir e5Hf and 80 mm for Ir e5Nbe3Hf. The formed
oxide layer of Ir e5Hf was thinner than that of Ir e5Nbe3Hf, indi-
cating the greater oxidation resistance of Ir e5Hf. The average
composition of the oxide layer investigated by EDS analysis is
shown in Table 4. Although the compositions derived from the EDS
analyses did not agree with the stoichiometric compositions of
HfO2 and Nb2O5 and the oxygen contents were always higher than
the expected values, combining with X-ray diffraction analysis, the
formation of HfO 2 and Nb 2O5 could be con ﬁrmed. In the Ir e5Nbe
3Hf alloy, the bright phase was observed in the oxide layer.
Although the possible phase expected for Nb 2O5 was not identiﬁed
from the X-ray diffraction patterns, EDS analysis indicated large
amounts of Ir and oxygen in the bright phase. It is considered that
the bright phase was the remaining Ir or IrO 2.
Generally, it is well known that IrO2 forms during oxidation, and
that IrO 2 changes to IrO 3 and vaporizes above 1365 K [21]. Under
the oxidation test condition of 1373 K in this study, IrO 3 started to
be vaporized. Since the major oxide phase was HfO2 and Nb2O5, not
Ir oxide, it is inferred that the decrease of weight during the
oxidation test was mainly caused by the vaporization of Ir oxide.
The porous structure of the oxide layer also supported the vapor-
ization of Ir oxide during the simultaneous formation of HfO 2,
Nb2O5 and IrO 2. The difference in rate of weight loss among the
alloys was related to the formation rate, vaporization and spallation
of the oxide layer. It is considered that the formation of HfO 2
delayed the formation of Ir oxide in Ir e5Hf. In Ir e5Nb alloy, it is
considered that spallation of Nb 2O5 occurred together with
vaporization of IrO 2, causing the largest weight loss during the
isothermal oxidation test.
4. Conclusions
Compressive strength was investigated in Ir-1at%X binary alloys
and ternary Ir alloys at 2223 K to understand the strengthening
effects of the alloying elements. The added elements were selected
from group 4 to group 10 in the periodic table. The strength was
drastically improved from 20 MPa for pure Ir to 65 MPa for Ir eHf
and Ir eZr alloys. Solid solution hardening was closely correlated
with atomic size misﬁt. The addition of elements with larger atomic
size mis ﬁt tended to produce larger solid solution hardening. The
atomic size misﬁt was also correlated with the solubility limit of the
solute element. A solute element with a small solubility limit had a
large solid solution hardening effect. It was also found that the
solubility limit could be predicted from the periodic table. In
ternary alloys, the combination of Hf, an element with large atomic
size mis ﬁt, and Nb, an element with large solubility limit, effec-
tively improved the solid solution hardening. Isothermal oxidation
behavior was investigated at 1373 K for Ir alloys including Nb and/
or Hf. Visible weight loss was observed for all the tested alloys
during the oxidation test, indicating separation of Nb or Hf oxide
and volatilization of Ir oxide.
