ABSTRACT
Chromium (Cr) is widely used in superalloys to improve oxidation resistance and creep strength. Still, excessive 
additions can promote the formation of detrimental topologically close-packed (TCP) phases, compromising 
microstructural stability. Therefore, controlling the Cr content to achieve excellent overall performance is a 
critical aspect in the design of superalloys. By employing the CALPHAD approach and the Co-based superalloy 
thermodynamic database developed by our research team, in conjunction with investigations into the γ / γ ′ 
microstructure, phase transformation temperatures, and lattice misfit, this study optimized a Co-40Ni-14.5Al- 
4Nb alloy. Based on this alloy, the Cr content was systematically incorporated and controlled through CAL -
PHAD calculations combined with experimental characterization. This led to the development of an optimized 
Co-40Ni-14.5Al-4Nb-12Cr alloy, featuring a high γ ′ solvus temperature (1172 
◦
C), a well-aligned γ / γ ′ micro -
structure, and a reduced lattice misfit. Moreover, this alloy exhibited a compressive yield strength of 745 MPa at 
800 
◦
C, a low density of 7.73 g/cm³ , and excellent specific yield strength. Notably, the alloy maintained a high 
compressive yield strength with minimal variation over the 500 – 800 
◦

interaction between lattice misfit and dislocation behavior. After 200 h of oxidtion, a protective oxide layer 
composed of Al ₂ O ₃ , Nb ₂ O ₅ , and (Al,Cr) ₂ O ₃ formed, markedly enhancing oxidation resistance. These findings 
highlight the effectiveness of Cr content optimization in achieving a balanced enhancement of mechanical and 
oxidation performance, offering valuable insights for next-generation superalloy design.
1. Introduction
In recent decades, significant progress has been made in the devel -
opment of superalloys, which have become essential components in 
manufacturing advanced jet engines, gas turbines, and rocket parts 
[1 – 5] . Compared to Fe-based alloys, Ni-based and Co-based superalloys 
have garnered widespread attention due to their unique microstruc -
tures. Ni-based superalloys, consisting of the ordered Ni ₃ Al phase (L1
2 
structure) and γ -Ni phase (Fcc structure), offer high strength and 
exceptional creep resistance, making them the preferred choice for 
practical applications [2,6] . However, their relatively low melting point 
(approximately 1455 ℃ ) compared to Co limits their use in 
ultra-high-temperature environments [6,7] . In 2006, Sato et al. discov -
ered the metastable γ’ -Co ₃ (Al, W) phase in the Co-Al-W system, which 
opened new directions for developing novel Co-based superalloys [8] . 
However, the high alloy density resulting from the inclusion of W

In Co-based superalloys, Ni functions as a γ ′ -forming element; its 
addition facilitates the formation of a stable γ ′ phase with a more regular 
cubic morphology, which is advantageous for improving high- 
temperature strength and creep resistance [8,18] . In Ni-based superal -
loys, Co can replace Ni atoms within the γ matrix, enhancing 
solid-solution strengthening and improving high-temperature strength 
and creep performance [19] . Furthermore, Co reduces the stacking fault 
energy of the alloy, which promotes the formation of nanotwins and 
contributes to the simultaneous enhancement of strength and ductility at 
elevated temperatures [20,21] . However, an excessive amount of Co 
may result in the precipitation of brittle phases such as the σ phase, 
degrading the high-temperature mechanical properties and micro -
structural stability of the alloy [22,23] . Consequently, the development 
of various Co-Ni-based superalloys has emerged, which integrate the 
high phase stability of the Ni ₃ Al phase characteristic of Ni-based su -
peralloys with the high melting point of Co [24 – 27] . This combination 
enables Co-Ni-based superalloys to achieve superior mechanical prop -
erties at elevated temperatures, thereby extending their service tem -
perature range and making them promising candidates for applications 
in more extreme thermal environments. Recently, Cao et al. experi -
mentally identified a lightweight Co-10Al-3Nb alloy with a γ / γ ′ 
two-phase microstructure [28] . They observed that the addition of Ni 
stabilized the γ ′ -Co ₃ (Al, Nb) phase and prevented its transformation into 
the competing B2 and D0
19 
phases. However, the γ / γ ′ lattice misfit of 
this alloy is relatively high at 1.64 %. Despite further alloying with Ni, 
Ti, and Ta, the potential for optimizing the lattice misfit remains. A 
lower misfit would enhance the alloy ’ s structural stability and improve 
its creep resistance [29,30] . Moreover, the appropriate addition of Nb in 
superalloys facilitates the formation of dense oxide scales such as Cr ₂ O ₃ , 
thereby improving the continuity and adhesion of the protective layer 
[31] . However, the formation of Nb ₂ O ₅ at elevated temperature can 
introduce internal stress within the oxide scale, which may lead to 
cracking and spallation [32] . Consequently, the oxidation behavior of 
Nb-containing superalloys warrants detailed investigation.
The addition of Cr to Co-based superalloys improves their oxidation 
resistance. Although Cr reduces the stability of the γ / γ ′ phases, it 
simultaneously provides oxidation resistance comparable to commercial 
Ni-based superalloys [33 – 37] . Numerous studies have aimed to deter -
mine the optimal Cr concentration required to enhance the alloy ’ s 
overall performance. Moreover, Cr incorporation decreases the γ / γ ′ 
lattice misfit and lowers the γ ′ phase solvus temperature [38] . Recently, 
Wang et al. [37] developed the Co-30Ni-10Al-6Ta-10Cr alloy, which 
achieves a balance between superior mechanical properties, structural 
stability, and oxidation resistance. In conclusion, these studies show that 
while Cr significantly enhances oxidation resistance and structural sta -
bility, it poses challenges in maintaining the stability of the γ / γ ′ 
two-phase microstructure.
Recent studies have emphasized the effectiveness of the CALPHAD 
method in thermodynamic calculations, which aids in the efficient 
design of alloy compositions and performance optimization for high- 
temperature applications [25,27,39,40] . In this study, phase diagram 
calculations were conducted for the quaternary Co-Ni-Al-Nb and qui -
nary Co-Ni-Al-Nb-Cr alloy systems using the Co-based superalloy ther -
modynamic database developed by our research team [10,11,26,41,42] , 
to predict their γ + γ ′ two-phase regions and phase transformation tem -
peratures. Subsequently, Cr was incorporated into the alloy to examine 
its impact on the γ / γ ′ microstructure, γ ′ solvus temperature, γ / γ ′ lattice 
misfit, mechanical properties, and oxidation resistance. This study aims 
to develop a low-density high-temperature alloy with excellent overall 
performance and promising application potential.

(99.9 wt%). The alloy compositions are given throughout the paper in at

2.5. CALPHAD and first-principal calculations
In this study, we utilized calculated phase diagrams to identify the 
appropriate composition within the γ / γ ′ phase region and to predict the 
phase fraction. Our group developed a Co-based superalloy thermody -
namic database based on previous experimental and thermodynamic 
calculations. Here, the phase diagram and phase fraction calculation for 
the Co-Ni-Al-Nb-Cr system alloy were performed using Pandat and our 
thermodynamic dataset.
Ab initio molecular dynamics (AIMD) simulations, encompassing 
both geometric relaxation and energy calculations, were conducted 
using the Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP) [43] . The 
exchange – correlation effects were treated within the framework of the 
Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) functional under the generalized 
gradient approximation (GGA) [44] . Core-valence electron interactions 
were represented through the projector augmented-wave (PAW) method
[45,46] . A plane-wave kinetic energy cutoff of 520 eV was employed, 
and reciprocal space integration was carried out using a γ -centered 
k-point mesh with a resolution of 0.04 (2 π /Å). Electronic 
self-consistency was achieved when the total energy change between 
iterations fell below 1 × 10 ⁻⁵ eV, with a Methfessel-Paxton smearing of 
second order and a width of 0.05 eV applied to aid convergence. 
Structural optimizations proceeded until the residual forces on all atoms 
were reduced to below 0.02 eV/Å. To account for long-range dispersion 
interactions, the DFT-D3 correction with Becke-Johnson damping was 
included [47] . All AIMD simulations were performed within the canon -
ical (NVT) ensemble, using a Nos thermostat to regulate temperature. 
The atomic trajectories were propagated using the velocity Verlet inte -
gration scheme with a time increment of 1 fs (1 fs = 10 ⁻ ¹ ⁵ s).

3.2. Development of Co-40Ni-14.5Al-4Nb-xCr alloy

relatively high lattice misfit (0.98 %), indicating significant potential for 
further optimization. This alloy composition resides in a broad γ + γ ′

can reduce the lattice misfit between the γ ′ and γ phases, thereby

4. Conclusions
This study used the CALPHAD method to design and produce an 
innovative Co-40Ni-14.5Al-4Nb (4Nb). In addition, the influences of Cr 
on microstructure, phase transition temperature, γ / γ ′ lattice misfit, 
density, mechanical properties, and oxidation resistance were discussed 
in detail. The key findings are summarized as follows:
(1) The Calculated Co-40Ni-14.5Al-xNb phase diagram exhibits wide 
γ + γ ′ phase region, suggesting significant alloying potential. Based on 
this, Co-40Ni-14.5Al-2Nb (2Nb), Co-40Ni-14.5Al-4Nb (4Nb), and Co- 
40Ni-14.5Al-6Nb (6Nb) alloys were prepared. Among them, the 4Nb 
alloy exhibited a relatively high γ ′ phase solvus temperature (1172 ℃ ), 
but its relatively high γ / γ ′ lattice misfit still requires further 
optimization.
(2) The calculated phase diagram of Co-40Ni-14.5Al-4Nb-xCr alloys 
reveals a wide γ + γ ′ phase region. According to the diagram, we pre -
pared Co-40Ni-14.5Al-4Nb-8Cr (4Nb8Cr) and Co-40Ni-14.5Al-4Nb- 
12Cr (4Nb12Cr) alloys. The 4Nb12Cr alloy demonstrated the best 
comprehensive performance including excellent structural stability, a 
high γ ′ phase solvus temperature (1116 ℃ ), and superior high- 
temperature compressive yield strength (745 MPa at 800 ℃ ), and low 
density (7.73 g/cm³).
(3) The dislocation motion experiences a transition from misfit- 
dominated to temperature-controlled behavior with increasing tem -
perature. As the temperature increases, the elevated lattice misfit of the 
γ / γ ′ phases leads to a progressively stronger γ / γ ′ coherency strength -
ening, which impedes dislocation motion through the γ ′ phase, and 
enhances the yield strength. When the temperature increases to 900 ℃ , 
the temperature plays a dominant role in controlling dislocations mo -
tion, allowing them to shear the γ ′ phase, thus reducing the yield 
strength.
(4) Adding Cr significantly improves oxidation resistance, with the 
4Nb12Cr alloy showing the best performance, achieving a lower mass 
gain (1.58 g/cm²) compared to the commercial U720Li superalloy. The 
oxidation process of the 4Nb12Cr alloy progresses through three stages,
