Effects of lamellar spacing, volume fraction and grain size on
creep strength of fully lamellar TiAl alloys
K. Maruyama a,*, R. Yamamoto a, H. Nakakuki a, N. Fujitsuna b

Abstract
Lamellar spacing, volume fraction of constituent phases and grain size are the major structural factors to be controlled in alloy
design of fully lamellar TiAl alloys. Creep tests were carried out on binary TiAl alloys with fully lamellar structure, to elucidate
effects of the three structural factors on their creep strength. Lamellar alloys give better creep strength than the constituent single
phases at higher stresses, due to their higher yield stress. Creep rate of lamellar alloys decreases with decreasing lamellar spacing,
suggesting that a ﬁne lamellar structure is effective in improving creep strength. The decrease is remarkable at high stresses. At
low stresses however, the difference in creep rate diminishes, due to the increasing contribution of dynamic recrystallization and
interface sliding. Creep rate is insensitive to volume fraction of the constituent phases and aluminum concentration is free in alloy
design of TiAl alloys as far as creep strength is concerned. Creep rate is also independent of grain size d when d\100 mm. Because
of easy dynamic recrystallization in the lamellar alloys, creep rate may increase when dB100 mm. If grain size B100 mmi s
necessary for adequate ductility, dynamic recrystallization should be prevented by some means. © 1997 Elsevier Science S.A.

1. Introduction
Ordered intermetallic compounds usually give higher
creep strength than disordered alloys, due to their lower
diffusivity [1,2]. In addition to the high creep strength,
titanium aluminides have low speciﬁc gravity and the
consequent high speciﬁc creep strength [3]. Because of
this attractive mechanical property, titanium aluminides
are a candidate for aerospace and engine applications
[4]. Titanium–aluminum systems provide solid solution
alloys, intermetallic compounds and dual phase materi-
als. High temperature structural materials should have
enough ductility and toughness at room temperature, as
well as high creep strength. High ductility may be
achieved by microstructural control and dual phase
materials are required for the microstructural control.
This is why the dual phase TiAl alloys, consisting of
gTiAl and a
2Ti3Al phases, draw the attention of many
researchers [4,5].
TiAl alloys take several types of microstructures de-
pending on their heat treatment [4]. They are, near
gamma, duplex, near lamellar and fully lamellar struc-
tures. Duplex structure usually shows better ductility at
room temperature, whereas fully lamellar structure
gives superior creep strength at elevated temperatures
[4]. For practical application of TiAl alloys, their ductil-
ity, toughness and creep strength have to be optimized
by means of appropriate microstructural design. The
microstructural design requires detailed knowledge of
the relationship between microstructure and creep
strength. The present paper aims at studying how creep
strength of TiAl alloys are affected by their microstruc-
tures.
Section 2 provides an overview of the state of the art
on creep strength of TiAl alloys. It will be discussed,
which is better in terms of creep strength among the
four types of microstructures and how creep strength of
TiAl alloys has been improved so far. A fully lamellar
structure with ﬁne grain size gives a good combination
of room temperature properties and high temperature
creep strength [5]. The rest of the present paper, there-
fore, is focused on the fully lamellar TiAl alloys. Lamel-

2. Creep strength of dual phase TiAl alloys
2.1. Selection of an appropriate microstructure
Dual phase TiAl alloys generally consist of g and
lamellar grains. Volume fraction of lamellar grains
increases in the following order: near gBduplexBnear
lamellarBfully lamellar. The fully lamellar structure
has 100% of lamellar grains. Grain size is also different
among the four microstructures. Fully lamellar struc-
ture has a grain size usually larger than 100 mm, since a
high annealing temperature is necessary for obtaining
the prior a single phase. TiAl alloys are annealed in the
ag two phase regime, thus having duplex structure.
Because of the lower annealing temperature and the
presence of the two phases, the duplex structure has a
small grain size, usually B50 mm.
Creep rates of a duplex TiAl alloy are plotted against
volume fraction of lamellar grains in Fig. 1a [6]. The
creep rates take a maximum value at 40% lamellar
grains. They decrease with the increasing volume frac-
tion and fully lamellar structure gives the lowest creep
rate [4,6–10]. A comparison is made in Fig. 2 [7]
between creep rates of duplex and fully lamellar struc-
tures. The decrease in creep rate of the fully lamellar
structure is more signiﬁcant at low stresses under which
the materials are to be used. The superiority of fully
lamellar structure has been conﬁrmed in various litera-
ture [4,5,7–12]. Near g and near lamellar structures
have small a
2 or g grains along their grain boundaries.

5. Conclusions
Creep of dual phase TiAl alloys with fully lamellar
structure was investigated to elucidate effects of lamel-
lar spacing, volume fraction of constituent phases and
grain size on creep strength.
gTiAl and a
2Ti3Al single phase materials loose their
creep resistance above their yield stress. A ﬁne lamellar
structure raises yield stress and the loss of creep resis-
tance does not occur up to high stresses in lamellar
materials. Because of this beneﬁt, creep resistance of
lamellar TiAl alloys is apparently better than single
phase materials above the yield stress of single phase
materials.
Creep rate of lamellar TiAl alloys decreases with
decreasing lamellar spacing at high stresses, suggesting
that reduction of lamellar spacing is useful for improv-
ing creep strength. The difference in creep rate however,
diminishes at low stresses due to increasing contribu-
tion of dynamic recrystallization and interface sliding.
The dynamic recrystallization and the interface sliding
have to be prevented in order to utilize the strengthen-
ing by g:a
2 interfaces at low stresses.
Creep rate of lamellar TiAl alloys is insensitive to
volume fraction of the constituent phases, in other
words, aluminum concentration, when average lamellar
spacing and grain size are similar. One can choose any
alloy composition in alloy design of TiAl alloys, as far
as creep strength is concerned.
Creep rate of fully lamellar TiAl alloys is indepen-
dent of grain size when grain size is larger than 100 mm.
Dynamic recrystallization however, readily takes place
