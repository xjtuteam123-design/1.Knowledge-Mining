ABSTRACT 
In present work, the effect of Y
2
O
3 
on tensile properties and fracture behavior of Ti-48Al-2Cr-2Nb alloy at 
elevated temperature was investigated. The alloys were successively fabricated by induction skull melting. The 
results revealed that tensile strength and elongation of the Y
2
O
3
-bearing TiAl alloy both improved evidently at 
elevated temperature. The TiAl-Y
2
O
3 
alloy still exhibited considerable mechanical properties with ultimate 
tensile strength of 587 MPa and elongation of 23.0% at 850 
◦
C. Simultaneously, the brittle-ductile transition 
temperature of the TiAl-Y
2
O
3 
alloy reduced about 50 
◦
C, which displayed the toughness fracture feature with 
dimples firstly at 800 
◦
C. In addition, Y
2
O
3 
could effectively prevent the movement of dislocation and promote 
the nucleation of deformation twins during tensile testing. The excellent properties could be attributed to the 
compounding effect of fine lamellar phase, increasing content of γ phase and dispersive Y
2
O
3 
particles. 
1. Introduction 
TiAl alloys are considered as promising and potential structural 
materials in aerospace and automobile engines due to their low density, 
high specific strength, and good creep resistance [ 1 – 3 ]. However, the 
insufficient room temperature ductility and service temperature limit 
their applications in a wide range [ 2 , 4 ]. Numerous researchers took 
great interest in the influence of alloying elements on high temperature 
mechanical properties of TiAl alloys. Given the solid solution strength -
ening, the alloying with Nb, Cr, V, Mn and Mo could greatly improve 
elevated temperature performance [ 5 – 8 ]. Besides, TiAl alloys reinforced 
by second phase particles also have attracted plenty of attention and 
several ceramics, such as TiB
2

2
AlC, Ti
2
AlN and Al
2
O

regarded as effective reinforcements [ 9 – 11 ]. 
Recently, many investigations suggested the rare earth element Y 
played a substantial role in optimizing elevated temperature properties 
of TiAl alloys [ 12 , 13 ]. The systematical studies by Wu [ 14 , 15 ] on 
Ti-46.6Al-1.4Mn-2Mo-0.3C alloys prepared by elemental powder met -
allurgy showed the addition of Y significantly enhanced the mechanical 
properties and promoted the activity of 1/2 < 110] type ordinary 
dislocations by forming Y
2
O

through internal oxidation. Similarly, 
Zhang indicated the strength of Y-bearing Ti-43Al-9V alloys produced 
by powder metallurgy technology remained at a high level up to 700 
◦

2

precipitates [ 16 ]. Wu and 
Zhang both argued that there existed a good bonding strength between 
Y

complex Y-containing precipitates (such as Al

Hence, adding Y

microstructure and optimize performance. Moreover, Kenel revealed 
Ti-45Al-3Nb alloys with Y

the amount of Y

3.4. Fracture behavior and strengthening mechanism 
The schematic diagram showing the fracture behavior of the two 
alloys during tensile tests at elevated temperatures is illustrated in Fig. 9 .

especially at the trigeminal grain boundaries. Moreover, the high 
temperature softening of massive γ phase contributes to the growth of 
the cavities. In addition, some lamellar phases will change the orienta -
tion by bending themselves to adapt to the plastic deformation, which 
induces the voids to initiate at lamellar interfaces. However, the 
development of interlaminar cracks still plays a crucial role in the fail -
ure. At 850 
◦

fracture with partial dimple fracture.
