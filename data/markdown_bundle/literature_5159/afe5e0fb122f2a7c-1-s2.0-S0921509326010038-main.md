ABSTRACT
Refractory high-entropy alloys are promising candidates for high-temperature applications, but their practical 
use is restricted by the strength-ductility trade-off and oxygen-induced intergranular embrittlement. This study 
addresses these challenges by developing single-phase body-centered cubic HfMoNbTaTiVZr-based alloys 
through tuning the ratios of Group IV – VI elements and boron microalloying (0.1 – 0.3 at.%) to enhance grain 
boundary cohesion. At room temperature, boron doping suppresses brittle intergranular fracture and promotes 
intrinsic transgranular failure, achieving a yield strength of 1074 MPa and 10.1% elongation. Such exceptional 
performance is retained at 1000
◦
C, with a yield strength of approximately 150 MPa and tensile ductility reaching 
up to 95.6%. Under sustained tensile loading, creep tests at 900 – 1000
◦
C reveal a solute-drag-controlled mech -
anism, with Mo addition significantly improving creep resistance. Collectively, these findings highlight the 
synergy between matrix tuning and boron doping for achieving robust high-temperature tensile and creep 
performance in refractory high-entropy alloys.
1. Introduction
Structural materials capable of withstanding higher stresses at 
elevated temperatures are essential for applications in the aerospace and 
energy sectors, such as in more energy-efficient turbine engines, hy -
personic vehicles, and nuclear reactor components [ 1 , 2 ]. Body-centered 
cubic (BCC) refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs), a subset of 
high-entropy alloys (HEAs), have shown exceptional high-temperature 
mechanical properties, demonstrating their potential for applications 
in extreme environments [ 3 – 6 ]. The classic WTaMoNb alloy retains a 
yield strength of 405 MPa even at 1600
◦
C and maintains a strength of 
approximately 850 MPa in the temperature range of 600 – 1000
◦
C, 
demonstrating exceptional high-temperature strength and softening 
resistance [ 7 ]. Such strengthening is attributed to the rugged potential 
energy landscape arising from local chemical fluctuations. This pro -
motes the intersection of kink pairs at energy valleys through the for -
mation of immobile cross kinks, which in turn improves the strength of 
RHEAs [ 8 – 12 ]. Furthermore, the reduced mobility of edge dislocations 
and pronounced dislocation interactions on high-order slip planes 
contribute to the superior high-temperature resistance to thermal 
softening [ 13 – 16 ].
However, tensile properties and creep behavior, which are critical 
for high-temperature industrial applications, have been scarcely re -
ported in RHEAs, primarily due to their intrinsic brittleness and the high 
oxygen sensitivity of constituent elements [ 17 – 19 ]. Most RHEAs face a 
pronounced strength-ductility trade-off, typically manifested as severe 
room-temperature brittleness due to a high ductile-to-brittle transition 
temperature (DBTT) that suppresses dislocation activity [ 20 ]. In addi -
tion to intrinsic compositional factors, the brittleness of RHEAs is further 
exacerbated by oxygen contamination. Due to the low solubility of ox -
ygen in refractory elements, oxygen preferentially segregates at grain 
boundaries, leading to a severe reduction in grain boundary cohesion 
and facilitating crack nucleation [ 21 – 27 ]. This susceptibility is partic -
ularly detrimental under tensile loading, during which stress concen -
trations at the crack tip accelerate catastrophic crack propagation and 
result in brittle intergranular fracture [ 28 ]. Consequently, many RHEAs, 
including the strong WTaMoNb alloy, lack tensile ductility and fail 
elastically at both ambient temperature and 1200
◦
C with fracture 
strengths below 70 MPa [ 29 ]. Conversely, the more ductile HfNbTaTiZr 
alloy suffers from insufficient high-temperature strength, exhibiting a

Our previous research and other relevant studies have demonstrated

insufficient to achieve robust tensile capability. Hence, in this study, we 
designed single-phase BCC HfMoNbTaTiVZr-based alloys using a syn -
ergistic approach by optimizing the matrix composition (Group IV – VI 
ratios) to balance strength and plasticity, while adjusting the boron 
content to further strengthen grain boundaries. This design strategy 
provided the mechanical foundation for achieving enhanced tensile 
ductility and creep resistance, which were systematically investigated at 
ambient and elevated temperatures.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructure and basic properties of alloys
Fig. 1 summarizes the crystal structures, lattice constants, Vickers 
hardness values, and microstructural features of the RHEA and RHEB 
series. Regarding chemical homogeneity, the elemental mappings in 
Supplemental Fig. S1 reveal a uniform distribution of all constituent 
elements across alloys RHEA1 through RHEA8. Furthermore, as sum -
marized in Supplemental Table S3 , the measured average compositions 
align closely with the designed nominal values. The XRD patterns in 
Fig. 1 (a) confirm that all alloys consist of a single-phase BCC solid so -
lution. A distinct shift of the diffraction peaks toward lower angles is 
observed from RHEA1 to RHEA8, corresponding to a steady increase in 
the average lattice constant, as plotted in Fig. 1 (b) . This trend is 
attributed to the progressive increase in the concentration of elements 
with larger atomic radii (Hf, Zr) and the concurrent reduction of the 
smaller-radius element (V). In contrast, Fig. 1 (c) reveals a descending 
trend in Vickers hardness, dropping from 431 HV for RHEA1 to 329 HV 
for RHEA8. The higher hardness in alloys with compositions closer to 
RHEA1 is ascribed to their higher Mo content, which enhances inter -
atomic bonding and consequently increases resistance to dislocation 
slip. As the Mo content decreases toward RHEA8, dislocation motion 
becomes easier, resulting in lower hardness [ 45 ]. Notably, the discon -
tinuity in hardness observed between RHEA5 and RHEA6 can be 
attributed to the alloys crossing a valence electron concentration (VEC) 
threshold. As reported in the literature, BCC RHEAs with a VEC below 
approximately 4.5 often exhibit intrinsic ductility, correlating with the 
observed softening [ 46 ]. Specifically, the VEC decreases from 4.53 for 
RHEA5 to 4.48 for RHEA6, crossing this critical threshold.
Fig. 1 (d) demonstrates that alloys doped with both low (0.1 at.%) 
and high (0.3 at.%) concentrations of boron retain a single-phase BCC 
structure. No boride precipitates were detected by XRD, likely due to the 
high interstitial solubility facilitated by the severe lattice distortion 
inherent in HEAs [ 42 , 47 ]. It should be noted that the confirmation of the 
single-phase BCC structure is constrained by the inherent detection limit 
of conventional laboratory XRD, which typically cannot resolve trace 
secondary phases below 2 – 5 vol%. While our macroscopic character -
izations did not reveal micro-scale secondary phases, the presence of 
ultra-fine, low-density nanoscale borides or clusters (such as (Ti, Zr)B
2
) 
below the detection threshold cannot be entirely ruled out. Neverthe -
less, even if such trace nanoscale features exist, their ultra-fine size 
dictates that they would typically undergo direct dislocation shearing 
rather than activating strong Orowan bypassing mechanisms [ 48 ]. 
Furthermore, given their critically low volume fraction, these potential 
precipitates would not significantly impede macroscopic dislocation slip 
or alter the core deformation behavior. The EBSD IPF maps in Fig. 1 (e)
display equiaxed grains with randomly distributed orientations across 
all alloys, and reveal significant grain refinement in the boron-doped 
alloys. This refinement is hypothesized to arise from inhibited grain 
growth, as enhanced grain boundary cohesive energy reduces the energy 
difference between the grain interior and the boundary and increases the 
activation energy for grain growth [ 27 ]. As quantified in Fig. 1 (f) , the 
average grain size of the base alloys (RHEA3, RHEA5, and RHEA7) 
ranges from 200 to 280 μ m. The addition of 0.1 at.% boron (RHEBX01) 
significantly reduces the grain size to 70 – 100 μ m. However, increasing 
the boron content further to 0.3 at.% (RHEBX03) results in a less pro -
nounced reduction. This plateau suggests that the initial 0.1 at.% boron 
addition substantially lowers the driving force for grain growth; conse -
quently, the additional grain refinement achieved by further increasing 
the boron concentration is limited [ 42 ].

RHEB501 and RHEB503 exhibit strengthened grain boundaries and 
sufficient grain deformability. RHEB501 fails in a mixed-mode fracture 
characterized by transgranular features with small, shallow dimples, 
whereas the higher boron content in RHEB503 further enhances yield

dislocation-interstitial interactions, leading to greater plastic stability

ductility or a pronounced strength-ductility trade-off (blue-shaded re -
gion) [ 29 , 30 , 49 – 55 ]. In contrast, the RHEB503 and RHEB703 alloys 
show an improved balance of strength and ductility, enabled by 
boron-induced grain boundary strengthening with optimized matrix 
compositions. At the atomic scale, this effect arises from boron miti -
gating oxygen-induced grain boundary embrittlement. Highly electro -
negative oxygen contaminants at grain boundaries attract charges from 
adjacent metallic elements, forming a charge-depleted region between 
the oxygen atoms and the host lattice. The resulting charge depletion 
weakens interatomic bonds and degrades grain boundary cohesion [ 59 ]. 
This degradation mechanism has been explicitly confirmed by recent 
density functional theory (DFT) calculations and atom probe tomogra -
phy (APT) investigations, which reveal that interstitial oxygen severely 
segregates to boundaries in RHEAs, inducing premature brittle failure 
[ 22 , 27 ]. In contrast, highly diffusive boron preferentially replaces oxy -
gen at grain boundaries, further preventing oxygen segregation. Its 
relatively low electronegativity preserves or even enhances the local 
charge density around surrounding metal atoms, leading to increased 
grain boundary cohesion [ 21 ]. Furthermore, under extreme environ -
ments, these boron-enriched boundaries act as barriers that suppress the 
rapid high-temperature inward diffusion and localized accumulation of 
oxygen [ 60 ]. This beneficial interfacial modification is widely observed 
across various alloy systems, ranging from enhanced phase boundary 
stability [ 41 ] to the formation of highly compatible gradient-ordering 
boundaries that facilitate stress transfer [ 61 ]. Moreover, our previous 
studies have experimentally verified this boron-induced enhancement of 
grain boundary cohesive energy across multiple length scales, ranging 
from microscopic to macroscopic levels [ 38 ]. Consistent with these re -
ported studies, the competitive segregation of boron within our Group 
IV-VI matrix effectively suppresses oxygen embrittlement and signifi -
cantly enhances interfacial cohesion. Consequently, this fundamentally 
shifts the failure mode away from catastrophic intergranular fracture, 
ultimately achieving exceptional ductility.
It is worth noting that the influence of boron on the mechanical 
properties of refractory alloys can vary significantly depending on the 
specific matrix and doping level. While recent literature indicates that 
trace boron addition (e.g., 0.003 at.%) can induce specific elemental 
segregation to grain boundaries, leading to localized grain boundary 
softening in certain equiatomic systems [ 62 ], the present HfMoNbTa -
TiVZr alloys exhibit enhanced mechanical performance upon higher 
boron doping (0.1 – 0.3 at.%). This difference in behavior likely stems 
from synergistic effects related to doping concentration, lattice distor -
tion, and intrinsic oxygen sensitivity. Specifically, the higher boron 
concentration is expected to provide sufficient interstitial occupancy to 
facilitate strong covalent-like B-metal interactions at the grain bound -
aries and generate localized strain fields within the matrix, thereby 
potentially contributing to concurrent grain boundary and interstitial 
strengthening [ 27 , 47 ]. Furthermore, the substantial lattice distortion 
( δ = 5.46 – 6.15%) induced by Mo and V incorporation is anticipated to 
retard long-range elemental diffusion, which might mitigate severe 
grain boundary chemical fluctuations [ 63 , 64 ]. Finally, as established 
above, for these oxygen-sensitive matrices, boron actively replaces the 
embrittling oxygen atoms segregated at the grain boundaries. This 
dominant mechanism successfully changes the failure mode from 
intergranular fracture to ductile transgranular fracture. Consequently, 
the combination of these mechanisms allows the overall mechanical 
properties to be continuously enhanced by boron addition.

high oxygen sensitivity of the matrix, resulting in intergranular failure, 
as evidenced by the corresponding fractography in Supplemental

boundaries and, coupled with high-temperature grain softening, pro -
motes plastic deformation and a transition to transgranular fracture.

3.5. Creep deformation mechanism analysis
To elucidate the microstructural evolution during creep, EBSD 
analysis was performed within the representative deformed gauge sec -
tions. Fig. 5 presents the inverse pole figure (IPF), constituent phase, and 
kernel average misorientation (KAM) maps of RHEB503 ( Fig. 5 (a) ) and 
RHEB703 ( Fig. 5 (b) ) under various testing conditions. To maximize the 
observable contrast in microstructural evolution, the characterizations 
in Fig. 5 focus on the extreme boundary conditions of the tested stress 
regimes for each alloy (i.e., 60 and 100 MPa for RHEB503; 40 and 
80 MPa for RHEB703), effectively bracketing the fundamental transi -
tions in their dominant deformation mechanisms. At the lowest tem -
perature (900
◦
C) and applied stress, voids formed in both alloys, 
primarily at stress concentration sites such as triple junctions, where 
stress could not be sufficiently relaxed by diffusional flow or accom -
modation slip. However, their plastic strain distributions differed 
significantly. In RHEB503, KAM signals were strongly localized along 
grain boundaries, indicating boundary-dominated deformation. In 
contrast, RHEB703 exhibited a more homogeneous strain distribution 
across both grain interiors and boundaries. With increasing applied 
stress at 900
◦
C, the localized voids grew and linked together, progres -

Concurrently, both RHEB503 and RHEB703 developed markedly higher 
dislocation densities, as evidenced by elevated and more uniform KAM 
values throughout the grain interiors and boundaries. Upon increasing 
the temperature to 1000
◦
C, significant crack propagation became 
evident. The enhanced thermal activation facilitated rapid void growth 
and linkage, leading to the formation of continuous grain boundary 
cracks [ 92 ]. The KAM intensity further increased within both grain in -
teriors and boundaries, with a slight tendency toward grain boundary 
localization, reflecting substantial dislocation accumulation prior to 
fracture. Under the most severe conditions (highest temperature and 
highest stress), the enhanced diffusion and local plasticity facilitated the 
extensive coalescence of intergranular defects, ultimately causing both 
alloys to fail by intergranular fracture.
