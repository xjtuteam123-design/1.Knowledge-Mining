Single crystalline Al 2O3 fibres (sapphire), coated with the NiAl alloy IP75 by
physical vapour deposition (PVD), were assembled to fabricate composites by
means of diffusion bonding. The microstructure and chemistry of both as-coated
fibre and as-diffusion bonded composites were investigated by electron
microscopy and microanalysis. The interface shear stress for complete debonding
was measured by fibre push-out tests at room temperature, and the composite
tensile strength was measured at 900
/C14 C and 1100 /C14 C. An amorphous layer with a
thickness of about 400 nm formed between the fibre and the matrix during the
PVD process and was maintained during diffusion bonding. A Laves phase
precipitated along NiAl grain boundaries in the IP75 matrix. This caused a lower
tensile strength of the IP75/Al
2O3 composite at high temperatures compared to
as-cast monolithic IP75 and rendered the composite useless for structural
applications.
1. Introduction
The intermetallic alloy NiAl has been extensively investigated with respect to its high
temperature mechanical properties because it is considered a candidate material for
structural applications owing to its high melting point, low density, good thermal
conductivity and outstanding oxidation resistance [1, 2]. However, NiAl suffers from
its low strength at high temperature and poor toughness at room temperature.
To overcome such deficiencies, continuous ceramic fibres were incorporated into the
NiAl matrix [3]. Single crystal aluminium oxide fibres (sapphire) are of particular
interest because of their high modulus and strength, low density and their excellent
chemical compatibility with NiAl at high temperatures.
A higher strength at elevated temperatures with tolerable ductility at low
temperatures can also be achieved by adding hard second phases to the NiAl matrix.
Laves phases show outstanding high strength at high temperatures and may be used

4.2. The precipitation mechanism of Laves phases and Cr-rich phase in
as-diffusion bonded IP75/Al 2O3 composite and as-cast IP75 and their
effect on tensile strength
The chemical analysis showed that the IP75 matrix is mainly composed of B2 NiAl,
hexagonal C14 and cubic C15 Laves phases Ta(Cr, Ni, Al) 2, a Cr-rich phase and
(Ni, Al)-oxides. The binary Laves phase TaCr 2 has a large homogeneity range to
both sides of the stoichiometric composition [14, 15]. They can dissolve comparably
large amounts of third elements without changing their crystallographic structure
type. Hence, Ta(Cr, Ni, Al)
2 corresponds to the binary Laves phase TaCr 2, where
Ni and Al primarily substitute for Cr, i.e. they occupy the same kind of sublattice
sites in this Laves phase. The Ni/Al ratio can be varied within broad limits according
to the ternary phase diagram [16, 17]. The NiAl phase in NiAl-Ta alloys can dissolve
up to about 3 at.% Cr [6]. However, the Cr content in IP75 is 7.5 at.%. The surplus
Cr, therefore, is precipitated in a disordered Cr-rich phase. Its low strength can be
used for improving the mechanical behaviour, especially the ductility and toughness,
of NiAl alloys.
In the current study, columnar NiAl grains formed during PVD. During diffusion
bonding, the hexagonal C14 and the cubic C15 Laves phases were produced by:

Comparing the microstructure of IP75 in both the cast and the deposited state, it is
obvious that the eutectic hexagonal Laves phase formed in the as-cast alloy will hinder
deformation more effectively than the small globular hexagonal and cubic Laves
phase precipitates in the grain boundaries of the deposited and diffusion bonded state.
Hence, the microstructure of the IP75 matrix in the as-diffusion bonded IP75/Al 2O3
composite causes a low strength of the IP75 matrix and consequently a low strength
of the composite. Even the reinforcement by Al
2O3 fibres cannot compensate the
loss of strength so that IP75/Al 2O3 composites have a lower tensile strength than
as-cast IP75. In essence, IP75 fabricated by a PVD process is not suitable as matrix
material for fibre-reinforced composites with high strength and toughness.
4.3. Interfacial properties and debonding mechanisms
The shear stress for complete debonding in Al 2O3 fibre-reinforced IP75 and NiAl
composites, fabricated by a fibre coating process, was determined to be 217 /C6 59 and
250 /C6 42 MPa, respectively. These values are of the same order as reported for
sapphire/NiAl composites fabricated by binder-free powder metallurgy methods
(283 /C6 53 MPa) [23]. The high debonding shear stress was attributed to the adherence
of Al2O3 fibres to NiAl and the clamping pressure from the matrix on the fibre which
builds up during cooling after diffusion bonding owing to the different CTEs of fibre
and matrix [8]. The lower interfacial shear strength in IP75-composites compared to
NiAl-composites [8] is apparently due to the amorphous layer between fibre and
matrix, which affects the debonding mechanism. In NiAl/Al 2O3 composites,
interface debonding took place at the boundary between the NiAl matrix and the
amorphous layer [8]. By contrast, in IP75/Al 2O3 composites every grain boundary
groove on the NiAl surface is complemented by a corresponding ridge of the
amorphous layer on the fibre surface (figure 6). During the fibre push-out test, the
IP75/Al2O3 interface is subjected to an interfacial shear stress, and a stress
concentration is generated at the grain boundary grooves, which causes crack
initiation and propagation in the amorphous layer (figure 8). After the push-out test,
the about 400 nm thick amorphous layer has been split: one part sticks to the fibre
surface in the form of ridges; the counterpart forms the inner surface of the matrix.
In essence, interface debonding occurred in the amorphous layer along NiAl grain

5. Conclusions
The microstructure and mechanical properties of both as-coated and as-diffusion
bonded IP75/Al 2O3 composites were investigated. An amorphous interlayer with a
thickness of about 400 nm was formed between fibre and matrix after the PVD
process and maintained after diffusion bonding. The IP75 matrix produced by PVD
and diffusion bonding had a less favourable microstructure than as-cast IP75 with
respect to mechanical properties, and therefore, is not suitable for high temperature
composites.
The shear stress for complete debonding of IP75/Al
2O3 composites was found to
decrease to 217 /C6 59 MPa compared to 250 /C6 42 MPa for NiAl/Al 2O3 composites.
Interface debonding occurred in the thick amorphous layer, and is different from the
debonding mechanism in NiAl/Al 2O3 composites.
