Very small-angle neutron scattering (VSANS) was used to develop
a nondestructive inspecting technique for predicting the residual life
time of turbine blades made from single crystal Ni-base superalloy.
The VSANS curves, obtained from single crystalline CMSX-4 samples
with the various degrees of damage, clearly show the ﬁrst order peak
of the lamellar structure created by a sequence of /AD and /AD ’ phases in
this material. The peak shifts in the direction of lower scattering vector
magnitudes when the creep damage increases, which reﬂects a corre-
sponding increase of the period of the lamellar structure. According to
the VSANS data, the interphase distance in the samples increases grad-
ually (but does not grow rapidly) with increasing creep damage when
the latter is /BO 54% of the creep leading to rupture. Thus, the interphase
distance is a sensitive parameter which can be used to describe the
morphological changes in Ni-base superalloys related to creep damage.
The results are consistent with those obtained from scanning electron
microscopy.
1. Introduction
Single crystal Ni-base superalloys were developed to be employed in
blades of gas turbines which can be operated at higher temperature to
achieve higher efﬁciency. A prototype turbine blade made from a sin-
gle crystal Ni-base superalloy (TMS-12) was characterized by neutron
diffraction topography (Tomimitsu, Iijima, Aizawa & Yoshinari, 1995).
The excellent mechanical properties of these materials at high temper-
ature arise from their morphology (Giamei, Pearson & Anton, 1985).
After a standard heat treatment, the/AD ’ phase, which is an ordered phase
with L12 structure, regularly precipitates with cuboidal shape in the /AD
matrix which is a disordered phase with f.c.c structure. Because the
morphology is related to the strength of this materials, the characteri-
zation of morphology of superalloys is very important.
A number of applications of small-angle scattering using neutrons
or X-rays to investigate precipitation phenomena in single crystal Ni-
base superalloys have been performed (Bellet et al., 1992; F¨ahrmann,
Fratzl, Paris, F¨ahrmann & Johnson, 1995; Sequeira, Kostorz & Peder-
sen, 1997; Strunz, Wiedenmann, Zrn´ik & Luk´aˇs, 1997). These studies
mainly investigated the evolution of the /AD ’ precipitates during thermal
aging.
On the other hand, the creep properties of lower stress and temper-
ature combination and higher stress and temperature combination in
a nimonic alloy were studied by using small-angle neutron scattering
(SANS) (Miller, Messoloras, Stewart & Kostorz, 1978). Among the
techniques used to investigate the morphology of precipitates in single
crystalline Ni-base superalloys, SANS can obtain bulk average infor-
mation. Its use can be very interesting from the practical point of view
of the nondestructive testing of operated blades made from Ni-base
superalloys. The feasibility of nondestructive microstructure analysis
of operated turbine blades made from Ni-base superalloy UDIMET
720 was demonstrated by using very small-angle neutron scattering
(VSANS) (Bianchi et al., 1988) and the positional dependence of mor-
phology of the single crystal superalloy CMSX-2 with creep damage
was investigated by VSANS technique (Strunz et al., 1994). Therefore,
in order to develop a nondestructive inspecting technique for predicting
the residual life time of operated blades, we have applied particularly
VSANS experiments with creep damage to the CMSX-4 superalloy as
a model case.
2. Experimental
2.1. Sample
A CMSX-4 single crystal grown by the mold withdrawal method,
with growth axis parallel to the [001] crystallographic direction, was
used in this study. The chemical composition of CMSX-4 is : Cr 8.0,
Co 10.0, W 2.1, Re 1.0, Mo 0.4, Ta 2.2, Al 13.0, Ti 1.3, Hf 0.03, Ni
to balance in at% (Fullagar et al., 1996). After a standard solution heat
treatment, a primary aging was performed at 1353 K for 4 h and subse-
quently the samples were quenched in water. Then, a secondary aging
was carried out at 1143 K for 20 h and the samples were cooled in air.
To introduce the creep damage, the samples were loaded to a tensile
stress of 137 MPa at 1313 K. The stress direction was parallel to the
[001] crystallographic direction. The creep rupture time was 562.6 h.
We prepared samples with 10, 30, 54, 80 and 85% creep damage lead-
ing to rupture. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM), which is a de-
structive technique, for as heat-treated and 10% creep damaged sam-
ples, reveals the microstructures shown in Fig. 1. In the as heat-treated
sample, the
/AD ’ phase with cuboidal shape precipitates regularly in the
/AD matrix, while in the creep damaged sample, the morphology changes
to a lamellar structure. The normal axis is parallel to the stress direc-
tion. When increasing loading time, the average period of the lamellar
structure increased.

as heat-treated sample and the 30% creep damaged one. Incident neu-

detector distance of 10 m and a wavelength of 0.66 nm were used. The

To observe clearly the evolution of morphological changes for creep
damage, it is adequate to detect the change of the ﬁrst order peak of
the lamellar structure. The VSANS intensities obtained from uncoated
samples, as heat-treated and with damage between 10 and 85%, are
shown in Fig. 6(a) together with ﬁtted curves, which are explained later.
No slit height correction was performed in this study. The correlation
length of the as heat-treated sample, obtained from the position of the
interparticle interference peak, was 0.35 /AM m. In the case of creep dam-
aged samples, the VSANS curves clearly show the ﬁrst order peak of
the lamellar structure. The peak position shifts to lower q values in-
creasing with creep damage, which reﬂects the corresponding increase
of the period of the lamellar structure. The VSANS intensities obtained
from coated samples are shown in Fig. 6(b). Due to the large particles
of the coating materials, additional intensity is observed in the low q-

The interphase distance in the samples increases gradually with in-
creasing creep damage when the latter is/BO 54% of the creep at rupture
time. The agreement for the periods of the lamellar structure for un-
coated and coated samples is good. This means that the inﬂuence of
the coating for the evaluation of the period may be neglected because
the smearing by additional scattering has no major inﬂuence on the po-
sition of the interparticle interference maximum. Further, the result is
consistent with that obtained via SEM. We emphasize that the variation
of d is similar to that of the strain. This means that the evolution of the
lamellar structure is correlated with the creep strain. The dependence
