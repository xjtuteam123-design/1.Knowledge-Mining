abstract
The compositions of novel Ti eCueNieNb alloys were designed by the proportional mixing of the Ti 2Cu
Laves phase and Ni 60Nb40 eutectic unit. The Ti 28Cu14Ni34.8Nb23.2 alloy is composed of Ti 2Ni, NiNb, NbNi 3
phases, and a small amount of amorphous structure; the Ti 30Cu15Ni33Nb22 alloy is composed of Ti 2Ni and
NiNb phases; the NiNb phase can form the twin structure with the different twin planes in the two
alloys. The Ti
32Cu16Ni31.2Nb20.8 alloy is composed of the Ti 2Ni, NiNb and Nb phases. The high strength of
Ti28Cu14Ni34.8Nb23.2 and Ti 30Cu15Ni33Nb22 alloys are attributed to grain re ﬁnement and twin strength-
ening; in addition, the existence of NiNb twins improves the strength and hardness of the two alloys,
compared with that of Ti
32Cu16Ni31.2Nb20.8 alloy. In this study, the proportional mixing of the Laves phase
and eutectic unit is an effective method for the composition design of high entropy alloys with high
strength.

1. Introduction
The ﬁrst report of high entropy alloys (HEAs) was in 2014 [ 1e3].
In the ﬁeld of alloy research, HEA is a new branch; compared with
the components of traditional alloy, the components of HEAs are a
new design [ 1e3]. The traditional alloy takes one element as the
main element and the rest as the minor added element, while the
component of HEAs is that the percentage of all elements is equal or
approximate equal atomic ratio [ 4e6]. The early requirement of
high entropy alloy composition is that the alloy has more than ﬁve
elements, and the atomic percentage of each element is between
5% and 35% [ 4e6]. The typical HEAs reported are single phase and
simple structure, such as CoCrFeMnNi alloys with face-centered
cubic (FCC) [ 2], MoNbTaVW alloys with body-centered cubic
(BCC) [7], HfNbTiZrTa alloys with BCC structure [8], and HoDyYGdTa
alloys with close-packed hexagonal (HCP) [9]. Recently, researchers
care more about the multiphase alloys, called complex concen-
trated alloys (CCAs) [ 6,14,16,15,17] or compositionally complex al-
loys (CCAs) [10,12,13]. The mechanical properties of these alloys are
superior to single phase HEAs, like higher strength and hardness
and more potential in high temperature conditions [ 18e22], due to
the strengthening effect from multiphase structure [ 6,10,13,16,17].
In this paper, the compositions of novel multiphase Ti eCueNieNb
CCAs with high strength and hardness are designed based on the
composition design of HEAs and bulk metallic glasses.
When the alloy is in eutectic composition, the glass forming
ability of alloys will be improved [ 18e20]. For multicomponent
alloys, the composition of metallic glass is composed of several
binary eutectic units according to different ratios [23e25]. In fact, in
the composition design of metallic glass, the chemical composition
of binary eutectic clusters is called binary eutectic unit for short
[23]. During the cooling process, the melt of alloy composed of
eutectic units has higher stability than that of the alloy composed of
non eutectic units. When the cooling rate is higher than the critical
cooling rate, the alloy melt is easy to form amorphous alloy; when
the cooling rate of alloy melt is about lower than the critical cooling
rate, the nanocrystalline is distributed on the amorphous matrix
[26]. The mechanical properties of alloy can be improved by the
nanocrystalline [ 26]. Therefore, in the design of CCAs alloy
composition, eutectic unit is chosen as the design condition of alloy
component. When the particle size of alloy is smaller, the strength
of alloy is higher. In the stable eutectic solution, the crystal phase
which is easy to nucleate is introduced. To restrain the rapid growth
of crystal phase, the particle size of crystal will be smaller when
several crystal phases restrain each other. Based on the Hall ePetch* Corresponding author.

To improve the strength of high entropy alloy, solution
strengthening or second phase strengthening are used to improve
the strength of alloys [ 28e33]. In the study of second phase
strengthening, in a large number of HEAs, Laves phase has been
found [28e33]. In binary alloys, Laves phase is a stable intermetallic
compound. When Laves phase is introduced into the eutectic melt,
Laves phase will nucleate ﬁrst; to suppress the particle size of Laves
phase, clusters of other competing crystal phases are needed in the
alloy melt. When the competing crystal phases inhibit the rapid
growth of each other, the alloy with small particle size can be
prepared. Therefore, the composition design of the new alloys re-
quires not only eutectic units, but also Laves phase. In this paper,
the compositions of Ni 60Nb40 eutectic unit and Ti 2Cu Laves phase
were chosen to design TieCueNieNb CCAs. The microstructure and
mechanical properties of Ti eCueNieNb alloys were investigated.
2. Experimental procedure
Ingots of Ti eCueNieNb alloys with nominal compositions
were prepared by arc-melting a mixture of pure metal elements
(purity of >99.9% or higher) in a titanium-gettered argon atmo-
sphere. To achieve chemical homogeneity, all ingots were re-
melted at least four times, and then suction-cast into copper
molds to form rod-shaped samples with the diameter of 3 mm and
a length of 50 mm. The surfaces of the alloys were prepared using
standard metallographic procedures, consisting of grinding up to
2000 grit with a SiC paper, polishing with a colloidal silica sus-
pension. For observing the microstructures, the surfaces of pol-
ished samples were etched. The etched solution is a mixed
solution of HF, HNO 3,a n dH 2O, and the ratio of the corresponding
volumes is 1:4.5:4.5. The microstructures of the alloys were
observed using optical microscope (OM). The phases of the sam-
ples were characterized by X-ray diffraction (XRD), using a Philips
X-pert system with Cu K a radiation at 30 kV. The densities of
samples ( r) were measured by Archimedes method using ZMD
electronic density meter. The lower layer of the double tray is
immersed in deionized water at room temperature. The sample is
ﬁrst placed on the upper tray to measure the real mass, and then
transferred to the lower tray to ensure that the samples are
completely immersed in deionized water. Measure the mass at
this time, and ﬁnally calculate the densities of samples through
the formula set by the instrument system.
The mechanical properties of the samples were tested by quasi-
static compression at room temperature. According to the standard
compressed testing process (GB/T 7314 e2005 standard of China),
the ratio of length ( L) to diameter ( d) is in the range of 1 e2. The
cylindrical Ti eCueNieNb samples with diameter of 3.0 mm and

were carried out on a JEM e2100F microscope operating at 200 kV.

3. Designing composition of Ti eCueNieNb alloys
In multiphase alloys, when the multiple competing crystalline
phases inhibit each other in the growth process, the particle size of
multiple crystalline phases will become smaller, and the smaller
grain size can improve the strength of alloys. According to the
phase diagram of binary alloy, the precipitated phase can be pre-
dicted in quaternary alloy; to facilitate the design of alloy compo-
nents, some binary alloys are required to be in ﬁnitely soluble or
form solid solutions, i.e., the heat of mixing ( DH) of some binary
alloys is zero and a small positive or negative value. Alloys that can
form solid solutions have little in ﬂuence on the formation of
competitive crystalline phases in other binary alloys. In the qua-
ternary Ti eCueNieNb alloys, as the DH values of Ti eNb and
CueNi(Nb) atomic pairs are the small positive values (see Table 1)
[37], the interaction force of the atomic clusters containing Ti eNb
and Cu eNi(Nb) atomic pairs is weak, indicating that solid

component does not meet the requirements of composition design.
Accordingly, the (Ti 2Cu)eNieNb alloy component can be consid-
ered based on the “proportional mixing of Ti 2Cu Laves phase and
eutectic unit containing Ni and Nb elements ”. As liquid tempera-
ture ( Tl)o fN i 59.5Nb40.5 eutectic (1 175 /C14 C) is lower than that of
Ni84Nb16 eutectic (1282 /C14 C), the solution stability of the former is
higher than that of the latter. The stability of (Ti 2Cu)e(Ni59.5Nb40.5)
alloy solution is stronger than that of (Ti 2Cu)e(Ni84Nb16) alloy so-
lution. So, in the design of (Ti 2Cu)eNieNb alloy composition, the
Ni59.5Nb40.5 eutectic unit can meet the design requirements. To the
simple calculation, the Ni 60Nb40 eutectic unit is selected to replace
the Ni 59.5Nb40.5 eutectic unit. Therefore, the expression of the
designed alloy components ( Ccry) is,
Ccry ¼ (Ti2/3Cu1/3)100-x þ (Ni0.6Nb0.4)x. (3)
To obtain a more complex competition crystal phase, the design
of alloy composition needs to consider the atomic ratio of Ti and Ni.
In the binary Ti eNi phase diagram in Fig. 1b, when the Ni content
locates in 50%e75%, the TiNi and TiNi3 phases can precipitate in the
cooling process of TiNi alloy melt. The stability of TiNi 3 interme-
tallic compound is higher than that of the TiNi crystal phase, to
avoid the precipitation of a large number of TiNi 3 phases, the
atomic ratio of Ti and Ni should be less than 40:60. When the
atomic percentage of Ti and Ni locates between 50:50 and 40:60,
the corresponding Ti content reduces and the Ni content increases;
in the cooling process of Ti eCueNieNb alloy solution, the
complexity of the competing crystal phase increases, the mutual
inhibition of competing crystal phase is further strengthened, and
the stability of alloy solution will be enhanced, resulting in that the
particle size of crystal phase will be reduced. When the atomic ratio
of Ti and Ni is 50:50, in Eq. (3), x is equal to 52.38 at.%. For the
simple calculation, the selected x values are equal to 52, 55 and
58 at.%, respectively; the corresponding compositions are
Ti32Cu16Ni31.2Nb20.8 (x ¼ 52 at.%), Ti 30Cu15Ni33Nb22 (x ¼ 55 at.%),
and Ti28Cu14Ni34.8Nb23.2 (x ¼ 58 at.%), respectively.

Fig. 13 shows the compression fracture morphology of
TieCueNieNb alloys. For the Ti 28Cu14Ni34.8Nb23.2 alloy, Fig. 13 a

NiNb strip grains show transgranular fracture under the action of
large stress; in Fig. 13b, the parallel strip grains show intergranular
fracture feature. Therefore, the Ti 28Cu14Ni34.8Nb23.2 alloy is a macro
brittle fracture mechanism with quasi cleavage fracture as the main
fracture and intergranular fracture as the auxiliary. For the
Ti30Cu15Ni33Nb22 alloy, Fig. 13c shows a large area of cleavage sur-
face, but from Fig. 13d, the alloy still shows a tearing ridge of quasi
cleavage fracture with a small area of ductile fracture. During the
formation of ductile fracture zone, a certain amount of plastic work
can be absorbed, so that the sample has a small macro plastic
deformation, which is consistent with the small plastic deforma-
tion of the alloy. Because of the small particle size of the alloy, the
dimple size of the intergranular fracture is smaller, and the plastic
work absorbed is correspondingly smaller, which can result in the
small plastic deformation of the alloy. Therefore, the fracture
mechanism of Ti
30Cu15Ni33Nb22 alloy is mainly quasi cleavage
fracture, supplemented by ductile fracture and intergranular frac-
ture. For the Ti 32Cu16Ni31.2Nb20.8 alloy, Fig. 13e shows ductile frac-
ture, while Fig. 13f shows the tearing ridge of ductile fracture and a
small number of dimples characterized by ductile fracture; in
addition, a small number of intergranular fracture dimples are also
found. When the ductile tearing ridge of dimple is formed, a large
amount of plastic work can be absorbed, resulting in a large plastic
deformation of the alloy, which is consistent with the larger plastic
deformation in Fig. 12. The Nb phase with simple cubic structure is
prone to plastic deformation under stress, thus forming stress
concentration near the second phase. When the concentration
stress is larger than the combining force between the second phase
and the matrix Nb, the second phase and the matrix Nb fall off, thus
forming a dimple of ductile fracture. Therefore, the fracture
mechanism of Ti 32Cu16Ni31.2Nb20.8 alloy is mainly ductile fracture,
and intergranular fracture is secondary.
