Abstract 
The development of an effective strategy to improve the elastic performance of 
non-phase-transformation high -entropy alloys (HEAs), as w ell as the investigation on the effect of 
metastability on HEAs, is of great significance both practically and theoretically. The structure, 
morphology and mechanical properties of the HfZrTiTa (HZTT) alloys quenched from 873 K -1573 K 
were studied systemat ically. Results revealed that the HZTT HEAs composed of two coherent BCC 
structures was in a metastable state. Dispersed nano -lamellar modulated structure inside the equiaxed 
grains was derived from spinodal decomposition. The dependence of the width of th e stripe on 
quenching temperature is controlled by the uphill diffusion of the constituent elements, while the 
variation of the lattice distortion is in accordance with Logistic model. The mechanical properties of 
the HZTT HEAs, including hardness, yield s trength, elastic modulus and elastic limit strain, are 
regulated by metastability from the perspectives of lattice distortion and bonding energy. The yield 
strength and the elastic limit strain of the HZTT HEAs can reach 1.78 GPa and 3.1%, respectively, by 
increasing the quenching temperature and improving the metastability of the alloys. The as -prepared 
alloys are promising candidates in the application field of high-strength damping bearings. 
 
Keywords: high -entropy alloy, metastability, elastic deformat ion, spinodal decomposition, 
non-phase-transformation, high-strength damping bearings

1 Introduction 
For thousands of years, materials researchers always focus on improving performance and 
developing new systems for applications. Nowadays, a novel metallic material family, high-entropy 
alloys (HEAs), has become a research hotspot in the field of materials science [1-3]. Unlike traditional 
alloys, HEA s consisting of as least four elements in a solid solution exhibit some intrinsic features 
such as hi gh-entropy effect and disordered atom occupation, leading to the formation of single or 
dual-phase solid solution with lattice distortion [4 -6]. The u nique structure of this novel family of 
metallic materials results in some extraordinary properties such a s high fracture -resistant at cryogenic 
temperature and self-healing under irradiation [1,7-10]. 
Previous researches on HEAs are mostly concentrated on overcoming the trade -off between 
strength and ductility, which has long been a constraint in the design o f high-performance HEAs until 
the emergence of metastability engineering in 2016 [2]. Since then, several metastable HEAs with both 
high strength and high ductility have been reported [11,12]. With the help of metastability engineering, 
the strength and th e ductility of HEAs can both be improved by tailoring the alloys ’ 
thermodynamically metastability, or thermodynamically nonequilibrium state . In most application 
situations, the performance of a material before plastic deformation is more crucial. For example, high 
yield strength and high elastic limit strain are both necessarily required in high -strength damping 
bearings. Although the original intention of metastability engineering is to overcome the 
strength-ductility trade-off, the recent researches on the metastability of HEAs are actually focused on 
the stimulation of strain-induced transformation by tailoring the alloys’ metastability, and then 
improving the properties by transformation -induced plasticity (TRIP) effect. This conception of 
tailoring metastability turns out to be an effective way of controlling and improving HEAs ’ yield 
strength and elastic limit strain. However, all reported strategies for tailoring the phase stability of 
HEAs are only to alter the alloys ’ composition, which encounte rs the difficulty of mixing different 
elements that possess various chemical and physical properties and limits the obtainability of more 
kinds of HEAs. In fact, some regular strategies for tailoring the phase stability of metallic materials, 
such as quenc hing from different temperatures, may lend insight into the development of 
high-performance HEAs. For HEAs, on one hand, quenching may intensify the lattice distortion and 
resultantly stabilize the high -temperature structure by elevating dynamic barrier; o n the other hand, it 
may control the metastability of HEAs without affecting the structure obviously. Hence, it is of interest

to investigate the influence of metastability on the elastic deformation of HEAs, especially 
high-strength and brittle BCC HEAs, via the strategy of controlling quenching temperature. 
Moreover, an in-depth exploitation on the new mechanism of metastability is still lack to some 
extent in previous studies. A series of reported high -entropy alloys including TRIP HEAs [2,12], 3d 
HEAs [13-15], refractory HEAs [12,16,17] and eutectic HEAs [18] were fabricated by quenching in 
the final step. While the effects of high entropy and high temperature result in the formation of 
single-phase solid solution at high temperature, the dynamics barri er caused by lattice distortion effect 
and quenching renders the HEAs to exist in dynamic stable and thermodynamically metastable state at 
room temperature. However, although quenching has been widely used in the fabrication of HEAs, the 
effects of quenchi ng temperature, which can result in differen t metastability states, on the 
microstructure and the mechanical behaviors of HEAs are rarely reported. In other words, except TRIP 
effect, the effects of metastability on the mechanical behavior s of HEAs are not adequately 
investigated. The influence of metastability on non -phase-transformation HEAs and the possibility of 
improving mechanical properties by controlling HEAs’ metastability have not yet been revealed. 
Herein, a non-phase-transformation BCC HEA , HfZrTiTa (HZTT), was proposed as an example 
to verify our strategy and it also turns out to be a promising candidate for high -performance 
applications. It should be noted that non -phase-transformation alloys comprise the majority of the 
HEAs family and exhibit some particular characteristics that are desired in the application fields where 
TRIP HEAs cannot meet the requirements. Therefore, it is necessary and of great significance to 
investigate the effect of metastability on non -phase-transformation HEAs, especially brittle BCC 
HEAs, via the manipulation of quenching temperature. Particularly, quaternary HZTT HEAs are 
expected to exist in a metastable state by controlling quenching temperature on the grounds that the 
HZTT0.4-0.6 systems are typical metastable TRIP HEAs [11]. 
In this work, the lattice structure, lattice distortion and modulation structure of HZTT prepared 
from different quenching temperature s were investigated in detail . T he relationship between the 
microstructure and the metastability of a s-prepared HZTT was revealed both experimentally and 
theoretically. The effects of metastability on the mechanical properties of the non-phase-transformation 
HEAs were analyzed semi -quantitatively. On this basis, an effective strategy to improve the 
performance of non-phase-transformation HEAs by controlling metastability was developed.

2 Experimental 
The HZTT alloy s were prepared by vacuum arc melting (DHL -1250, Sky Technology 
Development) from raw metals with a purity of > 99.9% in a high -purity argon atmosphere. Residual 
gases were absorbed by Ti getter. The alloy s were prepared in the shape of a button with 10 mm in 
thickness and 40 mm in diameter. The as -prepared alloy s were re-melted six times under magnetic 
stirring in order to achieve a homogeneous distribution of the constituent elements. During each cycle, 
the sample was flipped and kept in liquid state for ~5 min. A part of as -cast alloys, which were 
electrical-discharge wire-cut into the size of 20 mm × 5 mm × 5 mm, were sealed in vacuum quartz 
tube by DR6130 ablation instrument. These samples were put in a molybdenum tube furnace 
(SX-12-16, Sky Technology Development) with the temperature of 873 K-1573 K for 2 hours and then 
water-quenched to room temperature. The line diagram for the preparation of the HZTT alloy s is

However, the presence of SSS is mainly dependent on thermodynamic stability, which relies on 
its Gibbs free energy G. The lower G is, the more stable the system will be. Compar ed with quinary 
HEAs, the mixing entropy ( ∆𝑆𝑚𝑖𝑥 = 𝑅𝑙𝑛𝑁) of HfZrTiTa system, which is mainly dependent on the

high strain energy in HZTT system result in the formation of SSS with BCC structure , and such HEAs 
with severe lattice distortion may be in a metastable state due to the relative low mixing entropy and 
large atomic-size difference.

Moreover, t he metastable state in the alloys generally leads to the presence of some special 
morphology, such as basket-weave fabric shown in Fig. 4(b) and (c). Basket-weave fabric is the typical 
morphology of spinodal decomposition, which is a mechanism for the rapid unmixing of a mixture 
from one thermodynamic phase to form two coexisting phases [41,42]. Spinodal decomposition can be 
illustrated on a phase diagram exhibiting a miscibility gap as shown in Fig. 6. Considering a mixture 
which forms a single thermodynamic phase at high temperature, spinodal decomposition occurs when 
it is suddenly cooled to the unstable region where the curvature of the free -energy curve is negative. 
The primary cause for spinodal decomposition is that there is no thermodynamic barrier for the 
nucleation and the growth of the new phases , or precisely speaking, the spontaneous diffusion of the 
elements and the formation of spinodal decomposition structure, during the cooling process, which is 
decided by the interaction energy of binary constituent elements. According to regular solution mod el 
[43], only positive interaction energy can result in the miscibility gap. It means that when the 
interaction energy of the constituent elements of the single thermodynamic phase is higher than those 
of two or more new phases, the barrier for the nucleation of the new phases disappears, giving rise to 
the generation of driving force for spinodal decomposition. The binary phase diagrams of Hf, Zr, Ti 
and Ta are shown in Fig. S 1 in the Supplementary Material. As can be seen, the liquid -solid phase 
regions of Hf-Ta, Hf-Ti, Zr-Ta and Zr-Ti diagrams are cupped and there exists a minimum point where 
the interaction energy of liquid solution is lower than that of solid solution, while the solid -solution 
regions of Hf -Ta and Zr -Ta systems manifest a typical miscib ility gap. Furthermore, the mixing 
enthalpy of any two of Hf, Zr, Ti and Ta is positive [ 44], which accounts for a higher interaction 
energy. Therefore, spinodal decomposition will occur in these binary systems and the consequent 
HZTT systems as well . As shown in Fig. 3 and 4, the SSS is actually composed of two phases with 
coherent relationship, which means they have the identical lattice structure and lattice parameters but 
the different compositions. According to the EDS results shown in Table 1, while the lighter lamellas 
is relatively rich in Ti element, the gray/dark lamellas exhibits clear enrichment in Ta element.

As mentioned above, spinodal decomposition is sensitive to environment temperature or 
quenching temperature. When the alloys were heat-treated in unstable region, such as annealed at T1 as 
depicted in Fig. 6, different elements start ed to diffusion and cluster ed to form two coexisting phases 
with a-point and b-point composition. In contrast, at higher temperature above TS, the elements mix to 
form a single identical phase. As a consequence, phase separation is unavoidable when the quenching 
temperature falls into the spinodal region, which is exactly the case as observed in the alloys quenched 
from 873-1073 K (Fig. 3). Moreover, in spinodal region, the free energy of the equilibrium phase 
composition is a function of composition. As demonstrated in Fig. 6, as T increases, the composition 
difference between a -point and b -point phase decreases. The variation of the composition generally 
results in the change of lattice. Therefore, as quenching temperature increases, the decrease in 
composition difference leads to a decrease in lattice difference and lattice distortion, reflected by the 
low-angle shift of the peak position or the increment in the lattice constant [45,46], as shown in Fig. 3. 
As a matter of fact , the decrease in lattice difference suggests the increase in lattice distortion 
energy, which can be reflected by the full width at half maximum (FWHM) of the diffraction peaks. 
Thus, the reciprocal of the position difference (1/ Dangle) and the average FWHM of the diffraction 
peaks as a function of quenching temp erature T display the same trend during the quenching 
temperature region of 873-1073 K as shown in Fig. 7(a). In addition, the FWHM curve obeys Logistic

independent variable. In material s science, the logistic function is generally related to the diffusion of

Besides hardness, the lattice distortion and the spinodal decomposition also affect the yield 
strength of the HZTT HEA s. The co -contribution of solid solution strengthening and spinodal 
decomposition strengthening facilitates the value of y of quenched HZTT HEA s to increase as the 
quenching temperature increases as shown in Fig. 8(c). 
Moreover, although the effect of the binding energy on the hardness can be neglected in HZTT 
HEAs due to the presence of lattice distortion and spinodal decomposition , the effect of the binding 
energy, which changes with the metastability state of the lattice, can still be reflected by elasti c 
modulus, E. As is known, elastic modulus is generally considered as the intrinsic property of a material 
on the grounds that it is less sensitive to structure and defects. That is to say, the binding energy will 
play a more critical role in determining the elastic modulus of HZTT. The lower the binding energy is, 
the lower the elastic modulus will be. In this scenario , the binding energy that decreases with 
increasing T can neutralize the contributions of the lattice distortion and the spinodal decomposition 
strengthening, which increase with increasing T conversely, and then leads to a stable E value of the 
HZTT HEAs quenched from different temperatures. 
An increased yield strength and stable elastic modulus inevitably lead to the increase of el astic 
limit strain e, whose value is equal to y/E. Under such circumstances, the phenomenon that the e of 
quenched HZTT HEA s increases from 2.4% to 3.1% as the quenching temperature increases may be 
attributed to the change of metastability, which directly affect s the lattice distortion, the spinodal 
decomposition and the binding energy of the alloys. It is known that the lattice distortion that is related 
to metastability is actually the representation of the degree that the constituent atoms deviate from the 
lattice sites. Higher lattice distortion means more atoms deviat e from lattice site, and thus more space 
for the atoms to move, which exhibits as higher elastic limit strain. It is noteworthy that the elastic 
limit strain of the HZTT HEA s quenched from 1573 K is larger than the common value of metal s 
(~2%), whose structure is stable, and closed to the value of metallic glasses (~5%) [4 8]. As is known, 
the main characteristic s of metallic glasses are the completely disorder ed amorphous structure and 
metastability. As discussed above, the HZTT HEAs were well crystallized and in a metastability state. 
Therefore, the similarity between the mechanical behaviors of metastable HZTT HEA s and metallic 
glasses may be an evidence of the fact that the high elastic limit strain of the HZTT HEAs is the result 
of metastable state.

In light of these results , it can be concluded that the metastable state that can be controlled by 
quenching temperature gives rise to high hardness, high yield strength and high elastic limit strain of 
HZTT HEA s. All these parameters are the critical properties for the ir applications in the field of 
high-strength damping bearing s etc. However, it should be noted that although controlling 
metastability has been proved to be an effective and efficient way to improve the performance of 
HEAs, this method still has a limitation. When the metastability reaches a certain threshold, the st rong 
lattice distortion and thermodynamic driving force will result in the transition of lattice structure or 
production of various intermetallic compounds, which may promote the release of lattice distortion 
energy and the appearance of some unwanted defects, and thus the rapid deterioration of the properties.

4. Conclusion 
In this work, HZTT quaternary HEA s were prepared by vacuum arc melting successfully. Low 
mixing enthalpy, suitable electro -negativity difference, valence electron concentration and high strain 
energy jointly contribute to the formation of single -phase solid solution with BCC structure. In the 
meantime, low mixing entropy and large atomic -size difference facilitate the quaternary alloys to be 
able to exist in metastable state and resultantly produce spinodal decomposition and lattice distortion. 
As quenching temperature increases, metastability gets improved following the prediction of Logistic 
model, leading to the dependence of lattice distortion and spinodal decomposition on temperature. 
The metastability of the HEAs was semi-quantitatively characteriz ed by valuing spinodal 
decomposition for the first time, and thereafter a bridge connecti ng the metastability and the 
mechanical properties of the HEAs was established. With metastability and microstructure being the 
predominant controlling factors, t he increase in the amplitude and the wavelength of the modulation 
neutralize the effect of spi nodal decomposition strengthening, the hardness and the yield strength of 
HZTT. Moreover, the binding energy that is directly determined by the metastability of the alloys 
gives rise to an elastic modulus independent of quenching temperature and a high elastic limit strain. 
Conclusively, the effect of metastability on the elastic deformation of non -phase-transformation 
HEAs was revealed for the first time, and a novel and effective strategy to improve the performance of 
HEAs was proposed and demonstrated . A metastable HEA system, HZTT, possessing high yield 
strength (1.78 GPa), high elastic limit strain (3.1%) and showing excellent application potential as 
high-strength damping bearing s was also presented. T he findings o f this work may open up 
opportunities in the design and preparation of novel structural materials.
