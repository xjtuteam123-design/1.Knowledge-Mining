ABSTRACT 
A family of novel Os – Co – Ta – B high-temperature bulk metallic glasses (HTBMGs) was fabricated by introducing 
high-modulus refractory metal Os (50 wt% at least) and strong-covalent metalloid B (up to 40 at%). These OsCo- 
based HTBMGs exhibit high glass transition temperature up to 1028 K and high Vickers microhardness up to 
17.6 GPa. The compressive strength can reach up to 6.0 GPa at room temperature and even over 4 GPa at 873 K 
(a new record for strength of metallic materials at this high temperature so far). The impressive strength is 
attributed to the strong bond characteristics especially for Ta – Os and B – B bonds. By combining experimental and 
theoretical analysis for the relationships among alloy composition, atomic structure and the related properties, a 
promising route was provided to design HTBMGs with desirable properties. The castable OsCo-based HTBMGs 
with superior mechanical properties and high thermal stabilities are significant for high-temperature applications 
as advanced structural materials. 
In past three decades, considerable interests have been focused on 
bulk metallic glasses (BMGs) due to the availability of bulk specimens 
produced at a relatively slow critical cooling rate (below 1000 K/s) and 
novel mechanical properties of high strength, high hardness, large 
elastic strain, and superior wear resistance compared with their crys -
talline counterparts [1] . These alloys are promoted to play a key role as 
advanced structural materials in aerospace, mechanical manufacture, 
and consumer electronics fields [2 – 9] . However, these distinguished 
properties of BMGs are critically dependent on their thermal stability. 
They would lose the mechanical properties because of the softening and 
crystallization at a relative low temperature near the glass transition

the enhanced thermal stability and high-temperature mechanical 
properties. Among all of metal elements, osmium (Os) has the highest 
bulk modulus, the highest Vickers hardness, the highest density, and the
