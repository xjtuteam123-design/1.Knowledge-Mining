Abstract 
Details of strengthening by cold drawing of Fe-Ni-Cr-Si-C alloy wires produced by the in-rotating-water spinning 
method have been presented. The formation of a strain-induced a' phase and the work-hardening of y and a' phases 
cause a significant increase in the tensile strength of the rapidly solidified wires. The tensile strength reaches as high as 
3500 MPa for the Fe-10Ni-12Cr-4Si-0.25C alloy wire. Subsequent low temperature annealing results in a further 
increase in the tensile strength to 4050 MPa. The corrosion resistance of the cold-drawn wires was found to be 
comparable with that of high strength SUS304 wires. It was found that the work hardening behavior in cold drawing 
strongly depends on the chemical composition, especially in alloys with high C contents. The optimum chemical composi- 
tion for high strength in the Fe-Ni-Cr-Si-C alloy wires has been proposed by using the Ni-equivalent content. 
1. Introduction 
It has been found that Fe-based crystalline wires can 
be produced by melt spinning in rotating water and 
their tensile fracture strengths reach 2500-4000 MPa 
after cold drawing [1-4]. Their strengthening is con- 
sidered to arise from the formation of a strain-induced 
martensite and a remarkable work-hardening ability of 
the austenite (y) and martensite (a') phases. However, 
the crystalline Fe-based wires examined to date have 
been limited, and little information has been obtained 
on strengthening in rapidly solidified crystalline wires. 
The purpose of this paper is to investigate the strength- 
ening of Fe-Ni-Cr-Si-C wires by cold drawing, and to 
present an optimum chemical composition of the wire 
for high strength and good corrosion resistance.

5. Conclusions 
The 7+ a' and V structured wires of Fe-Ni-12Cr- 
4Si-C alloys were produced by the in-rotating-water 
spinning method. The phase field in the as-quenched 
state and the work-hardening behavior caused by cold 
drawing of the wires were investigated. The formation 
of a strain-induced a' phase, and the work hardening 
of V and a' phases cause a significant increase in the 
tensile strength of the rapidly solidified wires. The 
corrosion resistance of the cold-drawn 
Fe-Ni-Cr-Si-C wires was found to be comparable 
with that of high strength SUS304 wire. It was 
recognized that the work-hardening behavior in cold 
drawing strongly depends on the chemical com- 
positions of the wires. The optimum chemical com- 
positions for high strength in Fe-Ni-Cr-Si-C alloy 
wires have been proposed using the Ni~q value.
