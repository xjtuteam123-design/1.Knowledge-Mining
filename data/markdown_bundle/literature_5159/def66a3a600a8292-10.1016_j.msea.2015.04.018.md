abstract
The microstructure and tensile behaviour of Mo-doped Co –9Al–(9 /C0 x)W–2Ta–0.02B–xMo alloys (where
x¼ 0, 2, and 4 at% is hereafter referred to as 0Mo, 2Mo, and 4Mo alloys, respectively) at room and high
temperatures have been investigated. Aged at 800 1C for 100 h, all alloys consist of a Co solid-solution
γ-CoSS matrix and nano-scale cuboidal γ0-Co3(Al, W) intermetallic precipitates. The γ matrix is coherent
with the γ0-Co3(Al, W) precipitates. With a coherent γ/γ0-Co3(Al, W) microstructure, the aged alloys
show tensile yield strength anomalies at temperatures above 700 1C, and the 0Mo alloy always exhibits
the highest strength at 600 –800 1C. It is interesting that the alloys also possess anomalously low
elongation of 0.4 –3.3% at 600 –800 1C, which is signi ﬁcantly below the /C24 10% elongation at room
temperature. Intergranular cleavage is found to be the dominant failure mode of the γ/γ0-Co3(Al, W)
microstructure at high temperatures, resulting in tensile plasticity anomalies. At room temperature,
however, slip bends are observed on the aligned γ and γ0 phases, and the γ/γ0-Co3(Al, W) coherent
microstructure fails in a dimple mode with excellent elongation.

1. Introduction
Since the discovery of a γ0-Co3(Al, W) phase in the Co –Al–W
ternary [1], signi ﬁcant interest in the phase stabilisation, micro-
structural evolution, and mechanical and oxidation behaviours of
the aforementioned ternary by multi-alloying has developed [2–
8]. Because the γ0 phase precipitates coherently from the γ matrix,
the Co –Al–W ternary material exhibits a microstructure similar to
that typical of Ni-based alloys [9,10]. This introduces the coherent
interface strengthening mechanism and enhances the high-
temperature strength to a level considerably above that of tradi-
tional Co-based alloys, strengthened only by solid solution ele-
ments and carbides [1 1,12].
Frequently, alloying elements such as Ta, Cr and/or Re, Mo
[5,7,8,13] have been added to the Co –Al–W ternary to increase
strength at room and high temperatures. Systemic research under
compressive loading has revealed that with Ta, Cr and/or Re, and
Mo additions, Co –Al–W alloys exhibit excellent ductility and ﬂow
stress anomalies above 600 1C, whereas the anomalous peak
strength values appear at 700 –800 1C. Ta is found to be the most
efﬁcient strengthening element for a Co –Al–W ternary alloy [2].
For example, with the addition of 2 at% Ta, the solvus temperature
of the γ0 phase in the Co –9Al–9W alloy increases from 1000 1Cu p
to 1079 1C, and the compressive strength at 800 1C increases from
360 to 580 MPa [2,3,7], for conventional Co-based alloys, this value
only /C24 300 MPa [3,12]. In addition, strengthening of the grain
boundaries by precipitates of borides or intermetallic phases has
been found to be critical to the mechanical properties of the γ0-
strengthened Co-base superalloys [14,15], which exhibited even
better creep strength that was comparable to the Ni-base alloy
IN100. The microstructural evolution of the high-temperature
compressed samples has revealed that a slip of paired 1/2
o110 4 superpartial dislocations on the {0 0 1} planes in addi-
tion to the {1 1 1} planes in the γ0 precipitates [2,3,16] results in
ﬂow stress anomalies at 700 –800 1C. This dislocation movement
mode is the same as that of the stress anomalies mechanism found
in the γ0-Ni3Al compound [17,18].
Recently, the tensile behaviour of the Co –Al–W ternary-based
alloys at room temperature was investigated [7,19]. An elongation
larger than 1 1.5% was obtained in Co –9.2Al–9.5W alloys [7] and
Co–9Al–(5–9)W–2Ta–(0–4)Mo [19] when doped with 0.02 at% B.
This mechanism of ductility enhancement is explained by the
suppression of any intergranular fractures with B, which have
been discovered in Ni 3Al single-phase polycrystalline alloys with
brittle behaviour and grain boundary failure [18]. In this study,
Co–9Al–9W–2Ta–0.02B is used as the base composition, and 2 at%
and 4 at% Mo are added to replace the same amount of W. A
detailed investigation with regard to room- and high-temperature

2. Experimental procedures
Button ingots of the 0Mo, 2Mo and 4Mo alloys, each with a
mass of 150 g were prepared by arc melting through ﬁve or six
cycles in an argon atmosphere. The purity of all raw elements was
99.99% by mass or higher. The ingots were solution-treated at
1350 1C for 8 h in a vacuum, followed by furnace cooling, and were
then sealed in an evacuated quartz capsule that was back- ﬁlled
with argon and aged at 800 1C for 100 h before cooling in air. X-ray
diffraction (XRD) analysis was performed on the bulk samples to
identify the phase constitutions using Cu K α (λ¼ 0.15405 nm)
radiation at 40 kV and 40 mA (Rigaku D/Max 2500PC). Back-
scattered electron (BSE) images were taken to investigate the
microstructures of the as-cast samples and the aged samples, and
the fracture surfaces of the tested samples using an SEM (JEOL
JXA-8100). The grain sizes were measured by the crystal orienta-
tion map using the electron backscattering diffraction (EBSD)
function of the SEM at the scanning step sizes of 1 –3 μm. Phase
compositions were determined using energy-dispersion X-ray
spectroscopy (EDS) (EDX, Oxford Instruments, UK). The phase area
fraction was statistically determined by quantitative metallo-
graphic analysis of the microstructure images taken in the BSE
mode. The electron diffraction patterns and the energy-dispersive
X-ray spectroscopy spectra (EDX) of selected areas were obtained
in an FEI Tecnai G 2 F20 transmission electron microscope (TEM) to
conﬁrm the phase crystal structure and composition of the aged
samples, respectively. TEM samples with a thickness of 0.5 mm
were cut and then mechanically grinded/thinned to 50 μm. Foils
3 mm in diameter were obtained from the mechanically thinned
samples by punching, and a ﬁnal thinning was achieved using a
GL-696F ion-milled instrument at an angle of 20 1 with an applied
voltage of 6 kV and a current of 0.15 mA. Finally, the perforated
foils were ion-milled again for 30 –40 min at an angle of 15 1 to
remove residual contamination and/or to improve the differential
thinning effects.
Differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) was used to measure
the phase transition temperatures under high-purity argon gas
with a heating rate of 10 K/min. Single pre-notched dog-bone
plates were used for the in-situ tensile behaviour observation
under SEM at room temperature. The parallel parts of the dog-
bone samples are 15 mm in length, 1 mm in width and 0.5 mm in
thickness. A notch with an a/w of 0.15 where a is the notch depth
and w is the specimen width, was machined into the middle of the
sample by electrical discharge machining with a Cu wire 0.2 mm
in diameter. The notch tip is likely the concentrated deformation
zone under tension, which allowed monitoring of the deformation
characteristics. The in-situ tensile tests were conducted on a
Quanta200F SEM with a tensile loading device, in which the
crosshead displacement rate can be manually controlled, allowing
us to stop loading at any time when plastic deformations of
interest occur. Dog-bone plate specimens were also used to
measure the high-temperature tensile behaviours of these materi-
als. Tensile tests were conducted at 600 1C, 700 1C and 800 1C in air
on an Instron 8810 machine at a strain rate of 3 /C2 10
/C0 4 s/C0 1. The
dimensions of the parallel parts of the dog-bone plates were
3 /C2 1.8 mm in cross section and 16 mm in length. The specimens
were held for 5 min at the testing temperature prior to testing to
homogenise the temperature. To obtain the average strength,
three samples of each alloy were taken for measurements of the
tensile stress –strain curves at each testing temperature. All speci-
mens for the tensile tests were mechanically polished using 1000-
grit SiC paper with water before testing.

Fig. 4 shows the DSC curves of the aged alloys. It is shown that
the solvus temperature of the γ0 phase in the 0Mo alloy is near
1091 1C; however, the solvus temperature of the γ0 phase
decreases with the addition of Mo: 1065 1C for the 2Mo alloy

displaced by Mo, the solvus temperature (i.e., the stabilisation of
the γ0 phase) decreases, which may affect the high-temperature
mechanical behaviour of the aged alloys. Similar trend between
the Mo additions and the γ0 solvus temperature has also been
shown elsewhere [13].

3.4. Fractographic observations on the fracture surfaces at high
temperatures
To understand the mechanism of why the coherent γ/γ0
microstructure shows such a low tensile ductility at high tem-
peratures, a fractographic observation of the fracture surface was
performed. Fig. 7 shows the deformation and damage morpholo-
gies of the γ/γ0 microstructure in the position away from the
fracture surface for 0.2 –0.3 mm along the tensile axis direction.
The aligned cuboidal γ0-Co3(Al, W) precipitates have been altered
(see Fig. 7 a), the arrangement of the γ and γ0 phases is not as
regular as that in the samples in the aged state ( Fig. 3 ). However,
the cuboidal shape of the γ0-Co3(Al, W) precipitates remains, and
no slip lines are found in the γ/γ0 microstructure. In addition, small
voids can be observed occasionally in the γ phase, as identi ﬁed
with the arrows A in Fig. 7 a and b, which show the locations of
preferential damage at small deformations (i.e., the channel of the
γ phase). It is evident that the deformation and damage of the
coherent γ/γ0 microstructure is not severe before fracture because
the total elongation at 600 –800 1C is as small as of 0.4 –3.3%.
Typical morphologies of the fractured surfaces in Fig. 8a–c suggest
that at temperatures of 600 –800 1C, the coherent γ/γ0 microstruc-
ture fractures completely along the grain boundaries. The enlarged
SEM images of the separated grain boundaries shown in Fig. 8a0–c0
demonstrate smooth surfaces with slight river lines, revealing the
dominant fracture mode to be the intergranular cleavage. In
addition, a small amount of dimples is found to scatter on the
cleavage surface and is believed to result in the /C24 3.3% plastic
elongation observed. It is concluded that the anomalous tempera-
ture dependence of the tensile ductility corresponds to the
fracture mechanism changing from dimple mode (see Fig. 5 e and
f) at room temperature to the intergranular cleavage mode at high
temperatures.
4. Discussion
A series of works [3,7] have conﬁrmed that under compressive
conditions, the Co–Al–W ternaries and those alloys with the B and
Ta additions show ﬂow stress anomalies beginning at temperatures
of approximately 650–700 1C and that the peak ﬂow stress appears
at 750 –850 1C. These results are consistent with those measured
under tension in the Co –Al–W–Ta–B–Mo alloys studied here (see
Fig. 6(d)). This implies that the ﬂow stress anomalies of the Co –Al–
W based alloys are independent of the loading mode, whether
compression or tension, and indirectly reveals that there are similar
dislocation mechanisms for the ﬂow stress anomalies at high
temperatures. The 0.2% compressive ﬂow stress of the referred
Co–8.8Al–9.8W–2Ta alloy at 800 1C [3] is found to be nearly

5. Conclusions
This study investigates the room- and high temperature
tensile behaviours of Co –Al–W–Ta–B–Mo alloys with a
coherent γ/γ0 microstructure, and the following conclusions
are drawn.
1) The microstructure of the as-cast 0 –4Mo alloys consists of the
γ-CoSS matrix and the Co 3(Ta, Me) chins, and exhibits a
dendritic-like morphology. After aging, the Co 3(Ta, Me) phase
disappears, and the microstructure is composed of the γ-CoSS
matrix and the nano-scale cuboidal γ0-Co3(Al, W) precipitates,
while the γ-CoSS and γ0-Co3(Al, W) phases cohere with
each other;
2) Tensile yield strength anomalies are observed when the tem-
perature exceeds 700 1C. Additionally, an increase in the
nominal amount of W replaced by Mo decreases the tensile
yield strength at 600 –800 1C.
3) The dimple failure mode at room temperature and the inter-
granular cleavage fracture at high temperatures are typical
failure modes for polycrystalline Co –Al–W–Ta–B–Mo alloys
with the coherent γ/γ0 microstructure. With the synergistic
slip property, the γ/γ0 microstructure exhibits an outstanding
plastic elongation of approximately 9% at room temperature.
4) Due to the change in the failure mode from the dimple mode to
intergranular cleavage as the temperature increases, the γ/γ0
coherent microstructure shows an anomalously low plastic
elongation of 0.4 –3.3% at 600 –800 1C.
