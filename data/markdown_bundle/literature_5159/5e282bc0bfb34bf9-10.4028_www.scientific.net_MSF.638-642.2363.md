Keywords: Precipitation, Ni-base superalloys, INCONEL718, INCONEL740 ® 
Abstract. The second phase precipitation strengthening effect of γ′, γ′′+γ′, γ′+γ′′ phases in 
INCONEL718 type and newly developed INCONEL740 ® Ni-base superalloys and their structure 
stability have been studied. Experimental results show that careful control of Nb, Ti and Al for alloy 
modification can not only raise their strengths but also improve structure stability at high 
temperatures. These modified newly developed alloys are suggested to be used at higher 
temperatures. 
Introduction 
Rapid development of industry especially aero-engin es and power engineering requires high-
temperature materials such as Ni-base superalloys to be used at higher service temperatures. 
Most of Ni-base superalloys with Al and Ti are stre ngthened by Ni 
3(Al,Ti) type γ′ phase 
precipitation. In addition of Nb to these Ni-base a lloys, γ′′-Ni 3(Nb, Ti, Al) can form for achieving 
higher strengths, such as INCONEL 718 alloy contain ing with about 5%Nb, 1%Ti and 0.5%Al. 
INCONEL 718 is wildly used in today’s aero-engine a nd other industries. However, γ′′-
Ni 3(Nb,Ti,Al) is a meta-stable phase, which can transform to stable plate-like phase δ-Ni 3Nb at high 
temperatures. Therefore INCONEL 718 alloy can be used at temperatures below 650ºC only. 
For higher thermal efficiency and environmental pro tection the development of advanced fossil 
power plants require higher steam temperatures. Eur opean THERMIE AD700 project has set the 
steam temperature at 700°C for very long time servi ce (several decade years). US Department of 
Energy has intended to raise the steam temperature up to 760°C. Because of long term service at 
high temperatures and severe corrosion and oxidati on environmental condition, today existed 
commercial superalloys can not meet these strict re quirements. Recent newly developed Ni-base 
superalloy INCONEL740 ® containing with second phase strengthening elemen ts about 2%Nb, 
1.8%Ti and 0.9%Al has been planned to be used for t he ultra super-critical (USC) boiler super-
heater and re-heater materials. 
In order to meet above mentioned requirements the precipitation strengthening effect of γ′, γ′′+γ′, 
γ′+γ′′ and also their phase stability from the view point of alloy design and development have been 
studied in detail. Several new modified alloys are suggested to be used at higher temperatures.

The variation of Al, Ti and Nb contents result in changes of phases not only in grains but also at 
grain boundaries. With the variation of Al, Ti and Nb contents, the phases at grain boundaries 
evolve from needle-like δ-Ni 3Nb to short rod or granular morphology(see Fig.3). The latest 
experimental results indicate that the associated precipitation of γ′+γ″ in the grains and the granular 
phase formed at grain boundaries make Alloy C (see Fig.4) characterized with high structure 
stability and superior mechanical properties after long time aging at higher temperatures [4].

Conclusions 
1. For promoting strengthening effect and structure st ability improvement of Ni-base superalloys 
to be used at higher temperatures, one of the effec tive methods is to control 
γ′ and γ′′ phase 
precipitation behaviour by means of careful adjustment of Nb, Ti and Al. 
2. A modified INCONEL 718 type alloy (50-55%Ni, 17-21% Cr, 2.8-3.3%Mo, 5.2-5.5%Nb, 0.8-
1.3%Al, 0.7-1.15%Ti, 0-1%W, 0.012-0.015%P, 0.005-0. 01%B and Fe in balance) with the 
associated precipitation of γ′+γ″ in grains and globular phase precipitation at grain boundaries is 
suggested to be used beyond the INCONEL 718 ceiling temperature 650 ℃ up to 680-700 ℃. 
3. INCONEL 740 ® keeps a good microstructure stability at 725 ℃. Its thermal instabilities include 
γ′ coarsening, η formation and certain fraction of G phase existenc e at grain boundaries after 
long time aging above 750 ℃. Modified 740 alloys by adjustment of Al, Ti and/o r Si level 
exhibit stable structure at 750 ℃ even up to 800 ℃. New INCONEL740 type alloys are 
suggested to be used at 750-800 ℃.
