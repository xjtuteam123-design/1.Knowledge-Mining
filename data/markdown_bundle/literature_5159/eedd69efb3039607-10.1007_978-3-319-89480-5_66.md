A. Ota, N. Ueshima, K. Oikawa and S. Imano
Abstract For high performance air craft engine disks, high-performance Ni-based
wrought superalloys (e.g. U720Li TM, AD730 TM) with over 40vol% of the γ′ phase
at 600 –700 °C have been developed. Applying them to gas turbines is expected to
improve the efﬁciency. However, due to their low workability, it is dif ﬁcult to make
large size components. We have developed a new innovative process (the MH
process) to improve the workability of high-performance Ni-based wrought
superalloys. The strengthening mechanism of these γ′ precipitation-typed superal-
loys is widely known to be enhanced by the coherent interface between the γ phase
and γ′ phase. We focused on the incoherent γ′ phase that precipitated on the γ phase
grain boundary during forging and it was found that the incoherent boundary shows
no strengthening effect. Formation of γ/incoherent γ′ two-phase microstructure
would dramatically improve their workability. Cold working was achieved by
applying the MH process to the U720-type alloy AD730. The MH process made it
possible to fabricate cold rolled sheets, cold drawn wires and the forged turbine
blades. It also exhibited excellent ductility at high temperature; we obtained 99%
reduction of area at over 920 °C and 500% elongation at 950 °C by applying the
MH process. It was demonstrated that U720Li can be processed by die forging at
low temperature and high strain rate without using superplastic forming. In this
study, we investigated how the heat treatment condition affects the formation of
unique microstructures with good hot and cold workability.

Introduction
Along with the technological innovations in alloy and processing,shown in Fig. 1,
Ni-based wrought superalloys with higher strength than conventional forged
materials, such as IN 718, have been developed for aircraft turbine disks in recent
years and are being promoted for practical use [ 1–5]. These high-performance
Ni-based wrought superalloys have the same level of creep strength are at worst the
same and at best more than 1.5 times higher than precision casting alloys for the gas
turbine blades. Therefore, applying the new alloys to gas turbine blades or disks
makes it possible to expand the blade length, reduce the amount of cooling air
needed, and reduce the thickness. These changes contribute to improved gas turbine
efﬁciency. In addition, since they have higher strength than conventional forged
materials, applying them to a combustor is expected to improve heat resistance at
the operating temperature and thereby extend life. However, these alloys don ’t have
good workability and their use is limited to the production of components such as
aircraft disks.
Generally, as shown in the left side of Fig. 2, in the Ni-based superalloys of γ′
phase precipitation strengthening type, the γ′ phase coherently precipitates in the γ
phase of the matrix, and the interface between the γ phase and the γ′ phase con-
tributes to strengthening (this type of γ′ phase is de ﬁned as “coherent γ′ phase”).
However, we noticed that the γ′ phase precipitated on the grain boundaries of the γ
phase during hot forging at temperatures below that of the γ′ phase solvus does not
achieve lattice matching with the γ phase (this type of γ′ phase is de ﬁned as
“incoherent γ′ phase”). Therefore the incoherent γ′ phase has no strengthening
effect. We found that by applying heat treatment after hot forging, as shown on the
right side of Fig. 2, the workability could be greatly improved by forming a
two-phase structure consisting of a γ phase and an incoherent γ′ phase. Alloys that
have such a two-phase structure can be worked more easily into the desired shape.

Heat Treatment and Evaluation Method
The MH material softening heat treatment process is explained as follows. As
mentioned above, the materials are heated to the forging temperature and the
coherent γ′ phase is dissolved. In this example the AD730 to a forging temperature
of 1070 °C and maintained it at that temperature for 1 h. The material was then
gradually cooled at a controlled rate to the set temperatures shown in Fig. 5 and
then water quenching (WQ).

Results and Discussion
Relationship Between Formation of Two-Phase Structure
of γ/γ′ich and Workability
It was interpreted that the dynamic recrystallized γ phase precipitates to cover the
boundary of the coherent γ′ phase, so that the interface between the coherent γ′
phase and the newly formed γ phase becomes incoherent. As shown in Fig. 3, with
AD730 it is possible to precipitate an incoherent γ′ phase of about 10% by forging
at 1050 to 1070 °C. In the MH process, this incoherent γ′ phase is grown by
controlled cooling shown in Fig. 5 to form a two-phase structure consisting of an
incoherent γ′ phase and a γ phase.
The microstructures from selected AD730 heat treated specimens were evaluated
by SEM microscopy. Figure 7a shows SEM image of AD730 specimen after
1070 °C/1 h/WQ. Figure 7b, c show AD730 specimens cooled from 1070 °C to
1020 °C and 400 °C respectively at a controlled cooling rate. Figure 7d shows
AD730 after receiving a 1070 °C/1 h/FC (FC: Furnace Cooling) heat treatment.
The cooling rates are described below.
Figure 7a (WQ) > Fig. 7d (FC) > Fig. 7b, c (Controlled Cooling). In the case
of WQ and FC, incoherent γ′ phases and coherent γ′ phases were observed on the
grain boundary and in the grain. Compared with WQ sample, the fraction of
incoherent γ′ phase was increased by FC, but it is not noticeable. Coherent γ′
phases were precipitated during cooling from 1070 °C. In the case of controlled
cooling, where the cooling rate was slower than that of FC, the precipitation of
coherent γ′ phase was not observed in the sample cooled to 1020 °C and it was
conﬁrmed that incoherent γ′ phase increased. It is considered that incoherent γ′
phase tends to increase because the crystal grain size is ﬁne and the precipitation
distance of incoherent γ′ phase is suf ﬁciently short compared to the diffusion dis-
tance of the γ′ phase former.

Conclusions
We investigated the effect of the lattice matching between the γ phase and γ′ phases
on the workability of γ′ phase precipitation strengthened high-performance
Ni-based wrought AD730 and obtained the following conclusions.
(1) We found that the incoherent γ′ phase increased by controlling cooling from the
forging temperature after hot forging of AD730 under the γ′ phase solvus.
(2) We con ﬁrmed that the incoherent γ′ phase did not contribute to strengthening
because the hardness was not increased as the incoherent γ′ phase was
increased.
(3) The results of a high temperature tensile test of AD730 with the MH-process
showed a reduction area of over 50% at 800 °C or higher and nearly 100% at
920 °C. At 950 °C, the elongation of nearly 500% was obtained. In addition,
the 0.2% yield strength at high temperature was lower than that of IN718, and
this can be expected to produce a large forged products.
(4) MH processed AD730 demonstrated improvement in cold, machining and also
hot workability were obtained.
• Rolled thin sheet (Thickness 1.8 mm) and drawn wire ( ϕ5 mm) of AD730 were

• A turbine blade machining from forged bar shows the machinability is equiv-

• Die forging is demonstrated at low temperature and high strain rate outside
