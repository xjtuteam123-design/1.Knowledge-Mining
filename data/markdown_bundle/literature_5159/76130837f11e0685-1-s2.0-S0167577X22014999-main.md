1. Introduction 
High entropy alloy, which is different from the traditional alloy 
design concept, has attracted extensive attention from scholars since its 
emergence. The emergence of refractory high entropy alloys (RHEAs) 
greatly expanded research in the field of high-temperature materials [1] . 
Many RHEAs have excellent properties [1 – 4] but contain Ta, W, Hf, and 
other elements with high density. Due to the lightweight design concept, 
a new RHEAs system is developed by replacing some elements in the 
original RHEAs, thereby greatly reducing the density of the alloys [5 – 9] . 
These new alloys are not only lightweight, but also have excellent 
properties. For example, AlNbTiVZr alloy with a density of 5.79 g/cm
3 
still possesses compressive yield strength of 550 MPa at 1073 K [6] . 
Similarly, the CrNbTiVZr alloy with a density of 6.57 g/cm
3 
has a 
compressive yield strength of 615 MPa at 1073 K [7] . By proper alloy 
design, high hardness and wear-resisting tools such as cutting tools and 
abrasives can be obtained, and they can also be used as turbine blades, 
welding materials and refractory materials for high temperature fur -
naces. However, the research and application on lightweight RHEAs is 
still in its infancy. Therefore, in the current work, a lightweight

alloy with excellent room temperature and high- 
temperature properties was reported.

4. Conclusions 
Microstructure and properties of a lightweight Al
0.8
Nb
0.5
Ti
2
V
2
Zr
0.5 
refractory high entropy alloy with high specific yield strength were 
investigated. The matrix phase is identified to possess a BCC structure. 
The C14-Laves phase, as a second phase, is distributed in a network at 
the grain boundary of the matrix. The density and hardness of the alloy 
are 5.35 g/cm
3 
and 5.86 GPa, respectively. The high concentration of 
matrix atoms, the hard and brittle C14-Laves phase, and the formation of 
strong chemical bonds give a high strength to Al
0.8
Nb
0.5
Ti
2
V
2
Zr
0.5 
alloy. 
In addition, it also exhibits excellent specific yield strengths at different 
temperatures compared with most of the other hitherto reported alloys.
