ABSTRACT
Laser Powder Bed Fusion (LPBF) additive manufacturing technology offers a route for developing high- 
performance Al alloys. This study utilized LPBF to fabricate Al-Mn-Mg-Sc-Zr alloys, focusing on the high- 
temperature mechanical properties and fracture behavior. Results indicate that the alloy with bimodal struc -
ture exhibits an excellent strength-ductility balance from room temperature to 250 
◦
C, with a yield strength of 
512 MPa and an elongation of 12.3 % at room temperature, and a yield strength of 370 MPa and an elongation of 
12.0 % at 250 
◦
C. Even at 300 
◦
C, this alloy retains a satisfactory yield strength of 269 MPa. The exceptional 
high-temperature performance results from the Al
3
Sc and Al
6
Mn dual-nanoprecipitation strengthening. How -
ever, high temperature ductility dip occurs at temperatures above 300 
◦
C due to the coarsening of Al
6
Mn pre -
cipitates in the fine-equiaxed grain regions. This study provides valuable insights for designing the 
microstructure of heat-resistant Al alloys used in additive manufacturing.
1. Introduction
Aluminum alloys are widely used in various industries due to their 
lightweight, excellent mechanical properties, and corrosion resistance 
[ 1 ]. Traditionally, these alloys are shaped through methods such as 
casting, extrusion, or forging and are extensively applied in aerospace, 
automotive, and construction sectors. With the advent of additive 
manufacturing, there is now an opportunity to create complex geome -
tries and lightweight structures with high precision [ 2 , 3 ]. This tech -
nology enhances design flexibility and material efficiency while also 
enhancing the mechanical properties and surface quality of Al alloys [ 4 ]. 
Laser Powder Bed Fusion (LPBF), as one of the metal additive 
manufacturing technologies, has been widely used in aluminum alloys. 
As the demand for lightweight components grows, particularly in the 
aerospace and automotive industries, there is an increasing need for Al 
alloys that can perform reliably at temperatures above 200 
◦
C. To 
address this need, it is essential to develop Al alloys that offer excellent 
high-temperature performance and durability for LPBF additive 
manufacturing.
The unique high cooling rates associated with LPBF additive 
manufacturing allow for fine microstructures and even the formation of 
heterogeneous structures, achieving a balance between strength and 
ductility at room temperature [ 5 , 6 ]. However, at high temperatures, 
grain boundary softening and the rapid coarsening of secondary phases 
present substantial challenges for the high-temperature performance of 
LPBF Al alloys [ 7 – 9 ]. For example, in LPBF AlSi10Mg alloys, the rapid 
coarsening of the Si phase makes it difficult for this alloy to maintain 
safe service above 200 
◦
C for prolonged periods [ 10 , 11 ]. To enhance the 
high-temperature performance of LPBF Al alloys, incorporating nano -
scale precipitates with good resistance to coarsening can significantly 
improve the high-temperature strength [ 12 , 13 ]. Recent studies have 
demonstrated a strong interest among researchers in the potential ap -
plications of eutectic alloys in the field of heat-resistant Al alloys 
fabricated by LPBF, investigating systems such as Al-Cu-Ce, Al-Ni, and 
Al-Ni-Sc-Zr alloys. Eutectic alloy systems exhibit excellent printing 
performance and high-temperature microstructure stability, utilizing 
ultrafine eutectic microstructures can significantly enhance the me -
chanical properties of alloys. Baul et al. [ 14 ] reported that the Al-Cu-Ce

development of high-performance heat-resistant alloys through additive 
manufacturing.

melt pools with very few defects. Further EBSD analysis indicates that 
the melt pools exhibit a typical bi-modal initial microstructure, con -

fine-equiaxed grain (FG) regions in the melt pool boundary, as shown in

1.08 ± 0.27 μ m while the CG region has an average grain size of 3.77 ±

5. Conclusion
This study provides a comprehensive analysis of the high- 
temperature performance, microstructure, and fracture mechanisms of 
LPBF Al-Mn-Mg-Sc-Zr alloys. The following conclusions can be drawn: 
(1) The as-built sample exhibited a typical bi-modal microstructure, 
which consisted of coarse-columnar grain and fine-equiaxed 
grain regions. No significant grain growth occurred after aging 
at 300 
◦
C for 6h.
(2) The heat-treated alloy showed good high temperature micro -
structure stability at RT-350 
◦
C and an excellent balance between 
strength and ductility from room temperature to 250 
◦
C. Despite 
showing high temperature ductility dip at 300 
◦
C, the alloy 
retained a notable yield strength of 269 MPa.
(3) The exceptional high-temperature yield strength resulted from 
dual-nanoprecipitation strengthening, where Al
6
Mn and Al
3
Sc 
phases offered resistance to coarsening and inhibited dislocation 
movement, ensuring the alloy maintains high strength at both 
room and elevated temperatures.
(4) The high temperature ductility dip was attributed to the slow 
coarsening of Al
6
Mn precipitates within fine-equiaxed grain re -
gions, which act as crack initiation sites, leading to a sharp 
decrease in the ductility.
