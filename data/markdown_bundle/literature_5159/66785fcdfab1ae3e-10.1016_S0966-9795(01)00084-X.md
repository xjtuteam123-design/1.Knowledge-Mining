Abstract
Eﬀects of alloying on mechanical properties of solidiﬁcation processed polycrystalline MoSi2, ternary (Mo, 2.5 at.% Re)Si2,
Mo(2 at.% Al, Si)2, (Mo, 1 at.% Nb)Si2, and quaternary (Mo, 1 at.% Re)( 2 at.% Al, Si)2 alloys were evaluated by microhardness
testing at room temperature and compression testing at elevated temperatures. Alloying with 2.5 at.% Re resulted in an anom-
alously high solid solution hardening in MoSi2, while alloying with 2 at.% Al lowered the brittle-to-ductile transition temperature,
in compression, of polycrystalline MoSi2 from 900oCt o 425 oC. Similar to the eﬀects observed in the quaternary (Mo, 1 at.%
Re)(2 at.% Al, Si)2 alloy, 1 at.% Nb containing samples exhibited concurrent enhanced ambient temperature ductility and higher
elevatedtemperature strengthcomparedtopureMoSi 2.Theeﬀects ofalloyingMoSi 2 with2.5at.%Re,1at.%Re+2at.%Al,and
1 at.% Nb on hardness and yield strength are discussed.# 2001 Elsevier Science Ltd. All rights reserved.

1. Introduction
Despite excellent oxidation resistance, high melting
point, and low density, the potentials of molybdenum
disilicide (MoSi
2) as a high temperature structural
material have not been utilized due to its brittleness at
low temperatures (<900
oC) and low strength at high
temperatures ( >1200 /C14C) [1]. For example, below
900 /C14C, the fracture toughness of MoSi2 is in the range
of2–4 MPa ﬃﬃﬃﬃmp [1],andthe 0.2% oﬀsetyieldstrengthof
MoSi2 at 1600 oC is about 20 MPa [2,3]. Alloying or
reinforcing with a second phase may lower the brittle-
to-ductile transition temperature (BDTT) of MoSi
2.
However, ductile-phase toughening with metallic phases
has limited applicability in MoSi
2 due to the chemical
reaction with silicon to form silicides, and reinforcing
with ceramic second phases such as SiC and ZrO2 has
only a modest eﬀect on enhancing plastic ﬂow and
increasing toughness [4].
First principles calculations indicate that alloying of
MoSi
2 while maintaining its body-centered tetragonal
(C11b) structure may result in improved mechanical
properties [5]. For example, Al and Nb may enhance
ductilityandRemayincreasestrength[5].Improvements
in both low and high temperature mechanical properties
of MoSi
2 have been reported by alloying MoSi2 with
small amounts of Al, Nb, and Re (<2 at.%) [2,3,6–8].
During alloying, below the solubility limits of alloying
elements in the C11
b structure of MoSi2, Al substitutes
for Si, whereas Re and Nb substitute for Mo [9]. The
solubility limits of Re, Nb and Al in MoSi
2 have been
reported as /C252.5, 1.3 and 2.7 at.%, respectively [2,9].
Although improvements in the ambient temperature
toughness have been reported by alloying of MoSi2
beyond the solubility limits with Nb and Al [10,11], the
rates of improvement per fraction of solute are not as
considerable as those observed in single phase alloys.
Furthermore, the presence of secondary phases with a
lower high temperature strength than the matrix alloy
would degrade the mechanical properties at high tem-
perature (>1500
oC) for which applications MoSi2 is an
excellent candidate [12,13].
The aim of this investigation was to explore the pos-
sibility of obtaining concurrently enhanced room tem-
perature ductility and high temperature strength in
single-phase MoSi
2 by combining the high temperature
hardening and the low temperature softening eﬀects of
Re, Al, and Nb. Hardness testing at room temperature
and compression testing at 1600
oC are conducted on
unalloyed and alloyed MoSi2 samples in order to study
both low and high temperature eﬀects of each alloying
composition on the mechanical properties of MoSi
2.

3. Results
Arc melting is a very suitable method of obtaining
homogeneous samples of MoSi
2 due to its very high
melting temperature. However, Si has a relatively high
vapor pressure and some Si is vaporized during arc
melting. Forx=2.00, 2.01, and 2.02, arc melting resul-
ted in a second phase with Si deﬁciency (Mo
5Si3), stoi-
chiometric, and excess Si, respectively, in the as-melted
samples. The single-phase samples with C11b structure
obtained in melts with starting compositions ofx=2.01
were used for mechanical testing to ensure that the
alloying addition did not partition to any second phase
that may form due to deviations from stoichiometry or
excess alloying.

4. Discussions
4.1. Solidsolutionhardening
Preliminary investigations on unalloyed MoSi2 indi-
cated regular sub-grain formation consistant with a
climb-controlled dislocation mechanism with primarily
100] slip above 900
oC [3]. Alloying with Re was found
to change the deformation mechanism to a viscous
glide-controlled dislocation motion with primarily 100]
and some 1/2111] slip. The hardening eﬀect of Re on
MoSi
2 was found to be much greater than those pre-
dicted by the classical theories based on atomic size and
modulus misﬁts [3]. A hypothesis was presented to
interpret this anomalous hardening. Since rhenium
‘‘disilicide’’ has a Si-deﬁcient stoichiometry of ReSi
1.75,
addition of four Re atoms results in a Si vacancy [14].
Re substitutionals may pair with Si vacancy to create
these defect complexes. The elliptical distortions around
these point defect complexes are expected to result in a
strongerinteractionbetweenthedefectsanddislocations,

hardening by substitutional solute with spherically

Both 2 at.% Al and 1 at.% Nb individually resulted
in low-temperature (<600

and high-temperature (>1300 oC) solid solution hard-
eningofMoSi 2.However, when MoSi2 was alloyedwith
both elements, their beneﬁcial eﬀects both at low and
high temperatures were nulliﬁed by the presence of each
other. Two phenomena may explain these observations.
First, Al and Nb may react during arc melting resulting
in an Al–Nb intermetallic phase, thus, eliminating the
beneﬁcial eﬀects of either element alone. No evidence of
an Al–Nb phase was found during EDS investigations.
However, more detailed structural characterization with
X-ray diﬀraction was not attempted. Second, the eﬀects
of alloying elements may be nulliﬁed if the size misﬁt
eﬀects on the matrix due to one element are opposite to
those of the other element. Similar eﬀects have been
observed by alloying Al with solutes of diﬀerent atomic
radii [15].
Alloying with 2 at.% Al and 1% Nb increased the
yield strength of MoSi
2 at 1600oC by factors of 4 and
10, respectively. The hardening eﬀects observed in these
samples are greater than those which could be attrib-
uted to solid solution hardening in metals due to size
misﬁt and elastic moduli mismatch alone as discussed
previously for the eﬀects of Re in MoSi
2 [3]. Deforma-
tion of MoSi2+1 at.% Nb at 1200oC resulted in dis-
locations with predominantly 100] Burgers vectors [16].
High density of dipole loops and isolated 110] disloca-
tion segments were also observed. 110] type sessile dis-
locations were formed from the [100]+[010]=[110]
reaction.Theregularsub-graindislocationstructureindi-
cative of climb-controlled dislocation creep, observed in
unalloyed MoSi
2 after deformation at high tempera-
tures, was not present in Nb-alloyed samples. Rather,
the dislocation substructures were consistent with the
increased glide resistance due to Nb alloying.
The high temperature solution hardening eﬀects of 1
at.% Nb are similar to those of 2.5 at.% Re. However,
Re forms Si-deﬁcient ReSi
1.75 and Nb forms stoichio-
metric NbSi2 with Si. Therefore, alloying MoSi2 with
Nb may not result in Si vacancy as was the case with Re
alloying. The point defect structures in (Mo,Nb)Si 2
alloys need to be studied in more detail to interpret the
high hardening rate at elevated temperatures.

V, Ta, etc., which change the crystal structure of MoSi

ted region. This change in the stacking fault energy
