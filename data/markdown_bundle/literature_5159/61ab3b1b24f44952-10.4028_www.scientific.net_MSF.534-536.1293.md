The Effects of Molybdenum Con tent on the Dynamic Properties of 
Tungsten-based Heavy Alloys 
Woei-Shyan Leea and Tien-Yin Chanb 
Department of Mechanical Engineering, National Cheng Kung University, 
Tainan 701, Taiwan 
awslee@mail.ncku.edu.tw, btyc59@ms41.hinet.net 
Keywords: molybdenum, dynamic properties, tungsten heavy alloy, Hopkinson bar 
Abstract. The effects of the content of molybdenum on the dynamic propertie s of tungsten heavy 
alloys were investigated.Incre ase in mechanical properties wa s observed in molybdenum-added 
tungsten heavy alloys, due to the refined microstr ucture. On the other hand, decrease in mechanical 
properties was also observed in the alloys with high molybdenum concentrations, due to the de-
creased strength of the matrix pha se and the precipitation of an in termetallic phase. Hopkinson bar 
dynamic test under strain rates ranging from 2000 s -1 to 8000 s -1 at room temperature revealed that 
the flow stress of tungsten heavy alloys depended st rongly on the strain, strain rate, and the content 
of molybdenum. The variation of flow stresses was caused by the competition between work hard-
ening and heat softening in the materials at differ ent strain rates. The high temperature strength of 
the matrix phase was increased by the addition of molybdenum, which enhanced the strength of the 
tungsten heavy alloys in high strain rate test. 
Introduction 
The major applications of tungste n heavy alloys have been in th e industries of aerospace and na-
tional defense. Ever since the war of Persian Gulf, it has been recognized that traditional depleted 
uranium alloys such as U-Ti, U-M, and U-Nb, either in us e or in stock, have caused seriously envi-
ronmental contaminations hence sequelae, the tungsten heavy alloys with high penetration, becomes 
the vintage alternative. Previous efforts were made to enhance the mechanical properties of 
W-Ni-Fe heavy alloys by adding elements including Mo, Ta, Re, Nb, Al, or Ti; nevertheless, not all 
the additives were satisfactory [1-2]. Among them , Mo was found to significantly promote the me-
chanical properties of the W-Ni-Fe alloys under qua si-static test. The BCC-latticed Mo element not 
only decreases the solubility of the liquid-phase si ntered W atom, retards the growth of W grains 
and refines the W grain size, but also provides solution strengthening to the matrix. In addition, the 
Mo element has significant influences on the micros tructures of W heavy all oys such as contiguity, 
grain shape, interfacial phase, etc. [3-7]. Current studies on the effects of Mo on the mechanical 
properties of W-Mo-Ni-Fe alloys are based on quasi -static tests and analys es. In this study, the 
split-Hopkinson bar apparatus is employed and dynamics properties tests are conducted to study the 
effects of varying the Mo-content on the characters of tungsten heavy alloys subjected to high strain 
rates. The optimal addition of Mo additive is determined. 
Experimental procedure 
The compositions of five samples were designed to have an equivalent volume ratio of W and 
Mo to Ni and Fe, but with different ratios of W to Mo, in the powder state, and fixed weight ratio of 
Ni to Fe (7/3) . Table 1 lists the designed compos ition in this study.The specimens were sintered 
then machined to a geometry of 6 mm in diameter and a height-to-diameter ratio of 1. Quasi-static 
compression testing is performed using Saginom iya 100 metal forming testing machine at 
0.1s
-1strain rate. Dynamic compression test is perf ormed using split-Hopkinson bar at strain rates 
ranging from about 2000 to 8000 s -1. Quantitative elemental analysis of the matrix was carried out 
by electron probe microanalysis (EPMA), with an excitation voltage of 20 kV .

Results and discussion 
 Fig.1 shows the evolution of microstructure s of alloy 1 and allo y 5 sintered at 1520 oC and iso-
thermally held for 60 minutes. It can be seen that the grain size differs evidently as the Mo additive 
is added in the tungsten alloy, the averaged grain size is 32.5μm before Mo is added and 17μm after 
a 13.44wt% of Mo is added. The gain size is refined a bout in half. It is also observed that the grains 
are spherical after sintering instead of bei ng contiguously jagged. This phenomenon is also ob-
served on other alloys in this study.

An EPMA measurement is performed next for th e variation of the com positions of the matrix. 
The results are shown in Fig.2. From the phase diag rams of Ni-Mo and Ni-W, it is evident that the
solubility of Mo and W in Ni matrix is 27 at.% and 17.5 at.%, re spectively. Thus, Mo atoms have 
higher tendency than W atoms to di ssolve in (Ni, Fe)-based matrix phase. With 3.08wt% of Mo ad-
dition, the W content in the matrix of Alloy 2 is reduced to 0.9 at.% compared with that of without 
Mo addition. Meanwhile, Mo increases by an amount of 1.7 at.%, and the total amount of W and 
Mo solute atoms is increased. The increase of Mo c ontent in the matrix results in an increase of the 
combined solubility of W and Mo but a decrease of Ni content, while the content of Fe has no sig-
nificant change. This transition co ntinues until th e total amount of Mo is over 9.79wt% when the 
contents of the four compositions in the matrix becomes leveled off. At this stage, the su-
per-saturated Mo atoms apparently precipitate in the form of intermetallic phase [6]. The above ob-
servations are considered important factors af fecting the properties of the tungsten alloys. A 
quasi-static test is carried out herein to study the mechanical properties of the alloys. Test specimens 
with various Mo content are subjected to compression with 0.1 s
-1 strain rate. The stress-strain 
curves show that while excessive Mo content is not beneficial , moderate Mo content (9.78wt%) 
improves the strength of tungsten alloy. This is because the added Mo elements in liquid phase pro-
vide solution strength to the matrix, and, more importantly, refine the grain size and thus strengthen 
the mechanical properties [4]. Alloy 5 shows the least mechanical strength due to the excessive Mo 
content that allows intermetallic phase to form around the W-Mo grains. In addition, the ultimate 
strength measured on Alloy 4 (1296 MPa) is greater than that w ithout Mo addition (1185 MPa) by 
almost 10%.

Conclusions 
Under high strain rate, the ultima te strength of the tungsten-based alloys added with a moderate 
Mo content (9.79wt% ) can be raised by 278% (524MPa ), compared with that of the alloy without 
the Mo addition. The improvement in strength is about 10% based on th e quasi static test. It is in-
appropriate to apply the quasi static test results to characterize the behavior of the alloys subjected 
to high strain rates. A moderate Mo addition in tungsten-based alloy not only provides grain refine-
ment but also increases the solubility (about 3.2 at.%) of W and Mo so lute in the matrix. One of the 
features of the Mo addition is to maintain the st rength of the alloy at hi gh temperatures; thus, the 
impact strength of the alloy at high strain rates can be effectively improved. The tungsten-based al-
loy is of potential as an alternat ive to depleted uranium alloys in defense application provided that 
the Mo addition is moderately control and precipitation of intermetallic phase is prevented.
