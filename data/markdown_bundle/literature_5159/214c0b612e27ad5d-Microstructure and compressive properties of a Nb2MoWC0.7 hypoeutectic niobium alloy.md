ABSTRACT 
Refractory niobium alloys with low density are widely studied as high-temperature structural components, but 
they suffer from high-temperature softening or room-temperature brittleness. This paper provides a Nb
2
MoWC
0.7 
hypoeutectic alloy with excellent strength and plasticity with a high engineering yield strength of 1.47 GPa, 
compressive strength of 1.95 GPa, and plastic strain of 10.6%. A large amount of primary soft BCC improve the 
room-temperature plasticity, while eutectic structures with semi-coherent BCC/NbC interface enhance strength 
mainly by interfacial-dependent strengthening. 
1. Introduction 
Niobium alloys have been widely used in aerospace because of their 
excellent properties, including low density, high melting point, and 
good weldability. However, niobium alloys lost high-temperature 
strength above 1000 ℃ . Alloying elements and a few second phases 
were usually added to improve their high-temperature strength through 
solution strengthening and second-phase strengthening [1,2] . However, 
the improvement of high-temperature strength is limited due to limited 
additions. Furthermore, Sha et al. have increased the strength of Nb- 
10Mo-15 W-10Ti-18Si niobium alloy to 0.65 GPa at 1773 K by numerous 
silicides. However, the room-temperature plastic strain is less than 1 % 
[3,4] , making the room-temperature processing difficult. 
Refractory eutectic high-entropy alloys composed of high-entropy 
alloys and carbide exhibit high-temperature strength through solution 
strengthening and second-phase strengthening, while the fine-grain 
strengthening and well-bonded interface improve the room- 
temperature plasticity simultaneously [5,6] . Bases on this design 
concept, Zhang et al. added W, Mo, and C into Nb to form a eutectic 
carbide reinforced Nb
2
MoWC
0.96 
niobium alloy [7] . This eutectic alloy 
shows a high yield strength of 808 MPa at 1673 K. Unfortunately, the 
eutectic alloy containing high content of brittle carbide theoretically has 
poor plasticity [3 – 4,7] . 
In this work, a new Nb
2
MoWC
0.7 
hypoeutectic niobium alloy with 
excellent strength and plasticity was prepared through composition 
adjustment. This alloy with a large amount of BCC shows good plastic 
deformation ability. The deformation structure is studied and the 
deformation mechanism is revealed. The strengthening mechanism of 
the heterogeneous interface was systematically analyzed. 
2. Material and methods 
High-purity metal and graphite powders (above 99.99 %) were used 
to prepare a Nb
2
MoWC
0.7 
alloy by arc melting. The powders were 
weighed and then melted for more than five times to obtain the samples 
with a size of about 23 mm × 10 mm. 
X-ray diffraction (XRD) was used to identify the phase structure with 
a Cu target at a speed of 2
◦
/min. Scanning electron microscope (SEM) 
and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) were used to characterize 
the microstructure. EBSD was performed and analyzed by Channel 5. 
Compressive properties were tested on a Instron 5966 testing machine 
with a strain rate of 0.5 mm/min. Nanoindentation measurements were 
carried out to obtain elastic moduli using the Oliver-Pharr method under 
a maximum load of 10 mN for holding 5 s.

EBSD was used to clarify the room-temperature deformation mech -
anism ( Fig. 3 ). The local misorientation values ( see kernel average 
misorientation (KAM) map) of the as-cast alloy are relatively low, 
indicating low dislocation densities in the BCC and NbC phases. These

dislocation slips in BCC. The pole figure results reveal that the deformed 
BCC and NbC phases maintain the nearly K-S relationship. Thus, the 
eutectic structure possesses high mechanical stability.

4. Conclusions 
In this study, a Nb
2
MoWC
0.7 
hypoeutectic alloy was synthesized by 
arc melting, which is composed of primary soft BCC and numerous 
eutectic structures with a semi-coherent BCC/NbC interface. This alloy 
has a high engineering yield strength of 1.47 GPa, compressive strength 
of 1.95 GPa, and plastic strain of 10.6 %, showing a good comprehensive 
performance of strength and plasticity than other traditional niobium 
alloys. The BCC/NbC interface presents a strong dislocation hindrance, 
and interface-dependent strengthening modes dominate the total 
interface strengthening. The room-temperature plasticity is attributable 
to the dislocation motions in the soft BCC matrix.
