1. Introduction 
A new alloy development concept, high-entropy alloys (HEAs), has 
been studied since 2004 when the term was coined by Yeh [1,2] . 
Exploring HEAs for high temperatures applications, Senkov et al. [3 – 6] 
designed refractory high entropy alloys (RHEAs) and refractory com -
plex, concentrated alloys (RCCA). Later Senkov et al. [7] denominated 
the alloys with microstructures similar to those of conventional super -
alloys as ‘refractory superalloys ’ (RSA). Independent of nomenclature, 
these alloys have demonstrated potential for structural applications at 
room temperature (RT) as well as at elevated temperatures. 
In the context of this work these alloys are simply named multi- 
principal element alloys (MPEA). The objective of this work was to 
develop a MPEA to use in structural engine airplane components with a 
work temperature of about 700 
◦
C, with superior specific yield strength 
relative to conventional superalloys. For this, the elements Al (2.70 g. 
cm
 3
), Nb (8.57 g.cm
 3
), Ti (4.51 g.cm
 3
), V (6.11 g.cm
 3
), and Zr 
(6.51 g.cm
 3
), were chosen, aiming to produce a low density single- 
phase alloy, based on the extensive mutual solubility among Nb, Ti, V 
and Zr as well as the proved high solubility of Al in both Al-Nb-Ti and Al- 
Ti-V systems.

3. Results and discussion 
The XRD diffractograms obtained from the homogenized and ho -
mogenized + heat treated alloys are shown in Fig. 1 , both presenting 
reflections essentially from BCC lattice. 
The microstructures of both are essentially a BCC matrix containing 
small fraction of particles of Zr-rich phase (86.8% Zr and 77.5% Zr, 
respectively), as shown in Fig. 2 . In addition, the homogenized + heat 
treated alloy have a high volume fraction of nanometer-sized particles 
finely precipitated inside the grains, revealed at higher magnifications, 
that chemical analysis could not be evaluated by EDS because of their 
small size. 
According to the TEM investigations of homogenized + heat treated 
alloy, Fig. 3 , the matrix possesses an A2 disordered BCC structure, and 
the nanometer-sized particles correspond to the Al
3

phases, both with hexagonal lattice. The micrometric light gray particles 
Zr-rich illustrated in Fig. 2 could not be characterized by TEM because 
such regions could not be located in the samples.
