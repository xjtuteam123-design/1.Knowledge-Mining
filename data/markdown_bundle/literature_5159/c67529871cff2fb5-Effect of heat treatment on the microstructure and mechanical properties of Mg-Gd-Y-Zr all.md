ABSTRACT 
Wire arc additive manufacturing (WAAM) technology is expected to be used for the fabrication of 
large and complex magnesium alloy components. In this work, Mg-9.54Gd-1.82Y-0.44Zr (wt.%, 
GW92) alloy thin-walled components were fabricated by cold metal transfer (CMT) arc in CMT- 
Advance mode. The microstructure of the WAAMed GW92 alloy consists of fine equiaxed ɑ-Mg 
grains with an average size of ∼17 μm, eutectic phases, β&quot; phase, cubic phases, and Zr 
particles. The short-term high-temperature solution treatment at 525 ℃ for 25 minutes 
significantly reduced the content of the eutectic phase, resulting in a significant increase in 
elongation (EL) (13.5 ± 0.3%). The peak aging time of the solution-treated GW92 alloy at 225 ℃ 
for 12 h. The WAAM-T6 sample showed a significant increase in strength while maintaining 
good ductility, with a yield strength (YS) of 235 ± 3 MPa, ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of 360 
± 7 MPa, and elongation of 10.8 ± 1.7%. The synergistic strength and ductility properties of Mg- 
Gd-Y-Zr alloys fabricated by the WAAM process were significantly impacted by the short-term 
high-temperature solution + aging treatments to preserve the fine grain and high-density.

1. Introduction
Magnesium alloy is one of the lightest metal structural 
materials with high specific strength and stiffness [ 1 ], 
making it suitable for lightweight structures in aero -
space and transportation [ 2 ], and it has received a lot 
of attention as a replacement for aluminum alloy [ 3 ]. 
At present, the application of magnesium alloys is still 
far less extensive than aluminum alloys. In addition to 
the high chemical activity of magnesium, easy oxidation, 
and poor corrosion resistance [ 4 ], the low strength, 
especially the poor mechanical properties at high temp -
eratures is one of the main constraints [ 5 ]. To improve 
the mechanical properties of magnesium alloys, many 
new magnesium rare earth (Mg-RE) alloys have been 
developed, such as Mg-Gd-Y-based and Mg-Nd-based 
alloys [ 6 ]. The Mg-Gd-Y-based alloys are particularly 
attractive for aerospace, military, and automotive appli -
cations due to their excellent specific strength and high- 
temperature resistance [ 7 ].
Conventional magnesium alloy machining tech -
niques have constraints such as complex processes, 
limited material usage, and high manufacturing costs, 
limiting their broad application [ 3 ]. Additive manufactur -
ing (AM) offers the possibility of directly fabricating net- 
shape components [ 2 , 8 ], providing new opportunities 
for using high-strength Mg-RE alloys for lightweight 
and large monolithic components in aerospace appli -
cations. The wire arc additive manufacturing (WAAM) 
process, which has a high deposition rate and process 
flexibility, is expected to be used for manufacturing 
large and complex magnesium alloy components, 
improving manufacturing efficiency and reducing costs.
Research on the WAAMed Mg alloys has mainly 
focused on Mg-Al-based alloys, with less attention 
given to Mg-RE alloys. Studies have reported on the 
process characteristics, macroscopic microstructure, 
and mechanical properties of high-performance Mg- 
RE alloys formed by WAAM technology, including Mg- 
Y-Nd-Zr alloys [ 9 , 10 ]; Mg-Gd-Y-Zr alloys [ 11 , 12 ], and 
Mg-Gd-Y-Zn-Zr alloys [ 11 , 13 , 14 ]. Due to the short 
melting and solidification process in WAAM, the large 
addition of RE elements can result in segregation at

UTS of 392 MPa and EL of 3.3%. The WAAMed Mg- 
Gd-Y-Zr alloys that have been reported so far have 
achieved high strength but poor ductility.
During the WAAM process, several thermal cycles 
and a high cooling rate generate high residual stres -
ses, resulting in frequent dislocation pile-ups [ 16 ]. 
Most dislocations are preserved after the short-term 
high-temperature solution treatment, and in addition 
to grain refinement, the primary cause of the Mg- 
Gd-Y-Zr alloys’ ultra-high strength is the highly pro -
moted precipitation behaviour caused by dislocation 
pile-ups during the aging treatment [ 15 ]. In order to 
achieve good strength-ductility synergies, in-depth 
studies are needed for the dissolution and precipi -
tation behaviour of the second phase in WAAMed 
Mg-Gd-Y-Zr alloys and to maintain fine-grained and 
high-density precipitation using high-temperature 
and short-term heat-treatment programmes.
Based on a previous study [ 17 ], the CMT-advanced 
mode of CMT-WAAM was used to form Mg-9.54Gd- 
1.82Y-0.44Zr (wt.%, GW92) alloy components, which 
was capable of obtaining a finer microstructure. The 
macroscopic morphology and microstructural of the 
as-built samples were researched. A short-time high- 
temperature solution treatment was applied to dissolve 
the eutectic phases, followed by a peak aging treatment 
to obtain a high density of strengthening phases. The 
dissolution behaviour of the eutectic phase and the pre -
cipitation behaviour of new phases were analysed. The 
effect of heat treatment on the microstructure and 
mechanical properties of the WAAMed GW92 alloy was 
investigated, and its strengthening mechanism was 
elucidated.
2. Materials and methods
2.1. Materials and WAAM deposition
The thin-walled components were fabricated by CMT- 
WAAM using GW92 wire with a diameter of 1.6 mm, 
and the chemical composition of the wire and deposit 
are presented in Table 1 . The CMT-WAAM equipment 
used in the experiment is mainly composed of a 
Fronius CMT 4000R welding machine and a robot. The 
single-pass multilayer samples of GW92 alloy were 
formed in the Fronius – 1.6 mm – AlSi 5 database in 
CMT-Advance mode, and the samples were formed 
with a scanning speed of 10 mm/s, a preset feed 
speed of 4 m/min, a layer height of 2.3 mm, a gas flow 
rate of 25 L/min, and an interlayer dwell time of 60 s 
between two layers. The manufactured specimen size 
was 200 mm × 10 mm × 70 mm (length × width × height), 
as can be seen in Figure 1 a, and 30 layers were deposited 
adopting the same two-way scanning forming strategy for 
each layer in total.
The GW92 alloy samples used for metallographic 
analysis were prepared to use standard metallographic 
procedures, after which they were immediately etched 
with solution (4 ml nitric acid and 96 ml ethanol) for 50 
∼ 60 s. The microstructures were observed using an 
optical microscope (OM, VHX-2000E, Keyence, Japan), 
scanning electron microscope (SEM, Gemini Supra, Carl 
Zeiss, Germany), field emission high-resolution trans -
mission electron microscopy (TEM, Talos F200X, FEI, 
Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Microhardness distri -
bution was measured by applying a Vickers hardness 
tester with a loading force of 100 gf and a holding 
time of 15 s. Tensile tests were performed on the elec -
tronic universal testing machine (CMT5150, China) at a 
displacement rate of 1 mm/min. The tensile specimen 
is a scale specimen designed according to ISO 6892-1: 
2016, as shown in Figure 1 d.

5. Conclusion
In this paper, single-pass multilayer GW92 alloy 
samples were formed by the CMT-WAAM process. 
The macroscopic morphology, microstructure, and 
mechanical properties of the WAAMed GW92 alloy 
were investigated. A short-term high-temperature sol -
ution treatment and peak-aging treatment were 
carried out to analyse the dissolution behaviour of 
the eutectic phase and the precipitation behaviour of 
the nanoprecipitates. The main conclusions are as 
follows: 
(1) The microstructure of the as-built GW92 alloy con -
sists of fine equiaxial α-Mg grains with an average 
size of ∼17 μm, eutectic phases, β
′′
phase, cubic 
phases, and Zr particles. The YS, UTS, and EL of the 
as-built samples were 146 ± 3 MPa, 253 ± 2 MPa, 
and 8.7 ± 2.1%, respectively.
