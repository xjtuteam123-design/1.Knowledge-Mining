1. Introduction
Magnesium alloys are promising structural materials used in
the automobile and aerospace industries for weight-sensitive
applications. However, the relatively poor ductility and low
strength greatly limit their applications. Following the Hall–
Petch relationship, the mechanical properties of Mg alloys can
be signiﬁcantly improved via grain reﬁnement processes, such as
severe plastic deformation (SPD) [1,2] and powder metallurgy
(PM) [3,4]. The advantages of PM stem from rapidly solidiﬁed (RS)
powders with remarkable reﬁnement of grains and precipitates,
assuring ultrahigh strength and near-net shape production for Mg
alloys. However, UFG PM Mg alloys do not always yield better
ductility. Our previous work [5] showed that the ductility of the
PM AZ91 alloy was maintained only at 6–8% because of
the incompatibility between the brittle Mg 17Al12 particle and
the matrix. Ayman et al. [6] suggested that 17% elongation was
realized in the PM Mg-7Al-1Zn-1Ca-1.5La alloy because of
dynamic precipitation of the ﬁne Al 11La3 and Al 2Ca particles.
Hence, the selection of a suitable alloying element or secondary
particles into the PM Mg alloy is also essential. Considering that
the extruded ZK60 alloy containing hard MgZn 2 phase particles
usually exhibit good ductility ( 415%) [7], fabricating an ultra-
high-strength and high-ductility ZK60 alloy via PM processing is
possible. Besides, the correlation between the microstructure and
tensile behavior in the PM ZK60 alloy was discussed brieﬂy.
2. Experimental procedures
Argon-gas atomized ZK60 (Mg-5.20Zn-0.33Zr, wt%) powders sup-
plied by Tangshan Weihao Magnesium Powder Co., Ltd., China were
used in the present study. The sie ved powders with mean particle
diameter of 102 mm were compacted at 200 1C under vacuum by
slowly increasing the pressure up to 360 MPa in a steel die. The
resulting compacts of 48 mm in diameter were preheated for 10 min
in die and then extruded at 200, 250, 300 and 350 1Ce m p l o y i n ga n
extrusion ratio of 25:1, where the deformed temperature deviation
from the preset temperature was within 72 1C. The extruded
samples were machined into tensile specimens with 5 mm gauge
diameter and 25 mm gauge length. Tensile tests were performed at
room temperature with an initial strain rate of 3.5 /C2 10/C0 4 s/C0 1.T h e
average value of three test results was used as the ﬁnal value.
Deformed microstructures were examined at sections parallel to the
extrusion direction using optical microscopy (OM) and transmission
electron microscopy (TEM, JEOL-2000EX). The average grain size was
measured using the mean lineal intercept method according to ASTM
standard E 112-88. Metallographic observations consisted of mechan-
ical polishing and etching using a mixture of 1 g oxalic acid, 1 ml
nitric acid, 1 ml acetic acid and 100 ml water. TEM foils were
prepared by a twin-jet electropolisher using a solution of 2%
perchloric acid and 98% methanol at /C0 20 1C.

grained Mg ( d4 1 mm) and the associated strain hardening rate is
recovered. Hence, the tensile deformation behavior at room

Firstly, a distinct difference in the strain hardening behavior of
alloys fabricated at differentT values is observed. When the alloys are

4. Conclusions
The present work reveals the effect of the microstructure on
the tensile deformation behavior of PM ZK60 alloys. The following
conclusions can be drawn:
1. At a critical T of approximately 2501C, full DRX occurs throughout
the material. Signiﬁcant grain growth and dynamic precipitation
of the b particle occur whenT further increases to 3001C.
2. Strain hardening introduced by the particle-dislocation inter-
action is evident in the relatively coarse-grained Mg alloys but
almost absent in the UFG materials mainly because of dynamic
recovery.
