Enhanced Mechanical and Corrosion-Resistant

YAN JIANG, QIAN YANG, JIAHUI GAO, and XINGYU ZHANG
This study addresses the challenges of lightweight medium-entropy alloys (MEAs) for
mechanical properties across a wide temperature range and lightweight design. By employing
a strategy of interstitial oxygen doping, a body-centered cubic (BCC) structured TiVNb–O
lightweight medium-entropy alloy was designed and fabricated. This alloy exhibits exceptional
comprehensive properties, with a density below 6.4 g/cm
3 and remarkable strengthening eﬀects
within the oxygen doping range of 0 to 1 at. pct. Even at an elevated temperature of 800 /C176 C, the
TiVNb–O1.0 alloy retains outstanding strength characteristics, achieving tensile and compressive
yield strengths of 502 and 630 MPa, respectively. The tensile strain of the alloys demonstrates
pronounced temperature and oxygen content dependence. As the temperature increases and the
oxygen content rises, the tensile strain signiﬁcantly decreases, exhibiting a distinct ‘‘duc-
tile-to-brittle transition’’. Through in-depth analysis of oxygen’ role in the tensile failure
mechanisms of TiVNb–O alloy, the interaction mechanisms between environmental oxygen,
interstitial oxygen and the alloy were elucidated. Speciﬁcally, the reaction of vanadium with
environmental oxygen reduces surface oxidation resistance, while the binding of interstitial
oxygen with vanadium induces localized stress concentration. These two factors synergistically
exacerbate the tendency for tensile embrittlement at medium to high temperatures. Further-
more, electrochemical test results reveal that the TiVNb–O alloy possesses excellent corrosion
resistance, with a corrosion potential higher than 0 mV and a corrosion current density on the
order of 10
/C07 A/cm2.

I. INTRODUCTION
HIGH-ENTROPY alloys (HEAs), as a novel class of
high-performance material systems primarily governed
by multi-principal element solid solution strengthening
mechanisms, exhibit substantial potential for applica-
tion in advanced industrial materials.
[1–3] Traditional
HEA design adheres to the principle of ﬁve or more
elements in equimolar ratios, with performance opti-
mization primarily focusing on alloys based on Al, Co,
Cu, Fe, Ni.
[4–6] With the growing demand for high-tem-
perature applications, studies have shown that the
incorporation of refractory elements ( e.g., Nb, Zr, Mo,
W) can signiﬁcantly enhance the high-temperature
strength of HEAs.
[7–9] This advancement has led to the
development of refractory high-entropy alloys, expand-
ing the potential of this material class. The refractory
elements improve high-temperature performance but
substantially increase alloy density (typically exceeding
8 g/cm
3). This conﬂicts with the urgent need for
lightweight materials in high-temperature applications.
To overcome this, researchers have developed light-
weight refractory high-entropy alloy systems with den-
sities of 5 to 7 g/cm
3.[10–14] By optimizing compositions
with lightweight elements such as Ti, Al, and V,
equimolar alloy like AlNbTiVY, non-equimolar alloys
like Ti
3V2NbNi0.5 and Ti 3V2NbAl0.5Ni0.5 have been
designed.[11,14] Most of the alloys exhibit excellent
room-temperature mechanical properties, but their
high-temperature strength and ductility deteriorate sig-
niﬁcantly, falling short of practical service requirements.
Consequently, one of the critical scientiﬁc challenges in
this ﬁeld is to enhance high-temperature performance
without compromising lightweight advantages.
Recent studies have demonstrated that doping light-
weight refractory high-entropy alloys with non-metallic
interstitial atoms such as oxygen oﬀers an eﬀective

II. EXPERIMENTAL METHODS
A. Sample Preparation
High-purity titanium (Ti), vanadium (V), and nio-
bium (Nb) metal blocks (purity ‡ 99.9 wt pct) and
titanium dioxide (TiO
2) particles were used as raw
materials. The Ti, V, and Nb atomic ratio was set to
1:1:1 for ingredient preparation. By varying the TiO 2
addition, TiVNb–O x alloys with oxygen contents of 0.5,
1.0 and 1.5 at. pct were prepared using a vacuum arc
melting furnace equipped with magnetic stirring. To
ensure compositional homogeneity, the melting process
was repeated six times.
B. Microstructural Characterization
The preliminary phase analysis of the alloys was
conducted using X-ray diﬀraction (XRD; X’Pert 3
Powder, Cu K a, k = 0.154 nm). Multiscale microstruc-
ture observation and further phase identiﬁcation were
performed using both electron backscatter diﬀraction
(EBSD; Oxford Instruments, UK) and transmission
electron microscope (TEM, FEI Titan Cube 80-300).
C. Mechanical Property Testing and Analysis
The hardness of the alloys was measured using a
Vickers microhardness tester (HV-1000 STA, Shanghai)
under a load of 0.2 kg with a dwell time of 15 seconds.
At least ﬁve valid measurements were taken for each
sample. Uniaxial tensile tests were conducted using a
tensile machine (INSTRON 5967) at room temperature
(RT), 600 /C176 C, and 800 /C176 C, with a tensile rate of 0.3 mm/
min. Tensile specimens were machined into dog-bone
shapes, with a gauge length of 5 ± 0.1 mm and a
cross-sectional area of (1.2 ± 0.2) 9 (1.5 ± 0.2) mm
2.
The surfaces were progressively polished with sandpaper
up to 2000 grit. The fracture morphology of the tensile
specimens was observed using a scanning electron
microscope (SEM; Zeiss Evo 18, Germany) to analyze
the fracture mechanisms. Compression tests were per-
formed at RT and 800 /C176 C with a strain rate of 0.3 mm/
min, employing an extensometer for strain measure-
ment. In compliance with GB/T 7314-2017 (Chinese
national standard), rectangular specimens were
machined to dimensions of b = 6 mm and L = 9 mm
(aspect ratio L/b = 1.5), satisfying the standard’s
requirement of L = (1–2) b. High-temperature com-
pression tests were conducted using a Gleeble-3500
thermal–mechanical simulator. Detailed sample prepa-
ration methods and testing parameters followed the
procedures as described in Reference [ 23].
D. Electrochemical Testing
The electrochemical behavior was evaluated using a
conventional three-electrode system (CORRTEST,
Wuhan Corrtest Instruments), with a 3 mm-thick alloy
disk as the working electrode, a saturated calomel
electrode (SCE) as the reference electrode, and a
graphite rod as the counter electrode. The tests were
performed in a 3.5 wt pct NaCl solution prepared from
analytical-grade reagents, with a ﬁxed working electrode
exposure area of 1 cm
2. Electrochemical impedance
spectroscopy (EIS) was conducted over a frequency
sweep from 10
5 to 10 /C02 Hz with an AC amplitude of
10 mV. The polarization curves and EIS spectra of the
alloys were obtained to assess their corrosion resistance.

IV. DISCUSSION
A. Effect of Lamellar Precipitates on the Mechanical
Properties of TiVNb–O x Alloys
As demonstrated in previous sections, oxygen pri-
marily exists in solid solution and serves as the domi-
nant strengthening mechanism in TiVNb–O x alloys.
However, the role of lamellar oxide precipitates in
governing mechanical properties remains unclear.
Directly isolating their contribution is experimentally
challenging due to concurrent microstructural variables.
To address this, a solution treatment (1200 /C176 C for
12 hours in a ﬂowing Ar atmosphere) was employed to
dissolve these precipitates, followed by comparative
mechanical characterization of the alloys in their pre-
cipitate-containing and precipitate-free states. This
enables evaluation of the precipitates’ eﬀects, whether
beneﬁcial or detrimental, on alloy performance.
The TiVNb–O
0.5 alloy was selected as a representative
for detailed microstructural analysis before and after
heat treatment (Figures 8(a) and (b)). Compared to the
as-cast state, the solution-treated alloy displayed notice-
able grain coarsening with only trace amounts of
retained lamellar precipitates. SAED analysis conﬁrmed
the presence of a single BCC solid-solution phase. Such
metastable lamellar structures are commonly observed
in b-dominant Ti and Ti–Nb alloys, typically manifest-
ing as a¢ (hcp), a¢¢ (orthorhombic) or x (hcp)
phases.
[29,30] The martensitic a¢ and a¢¢ phases form
under rapid cooling, and are more prone to develop
when either the concentration of b-stabilizing elements
is insuﬃcient
[31] or a-stabilizers such as oxygen are
present.[32] These metastable phases, however, dissolve
upon high temperatures. [33] In the b TiVNb–Ox system,
the observed lamellar precipitates show structural sim-
ilarities to those found in b-dominant Ti and Ti–Nb
alloys. During the rapid cooling process of arc smelting,

B. Effect of Interstitial Oxygen Content on the Failure
Modes of Alloys
The marked reduction in alloys tensile strain with
elevated temperatures and oxygen content requires
detailed investigation. Fractographic analysis was per-
formed on TiVNb–O
0.5 and TiVNb–O 1.0 alloys that
exhibited relatively large tensile strains. At RT, the
TiVNb–O0.5 alloy exhibited typical ductile fracture mor-
phology, primarily manifested as dimples and shear
characteristics (Figure 9(a)). When tested at 600 /C176 C, the
alloy remained its ductile fracture but displayed emerging
transgranular features. The fracture surface revealed a
high density of incipient microvoids (early-stage dimples)
with surface oxidation products (Figure 9(b1)). Further
increase in temperature to 800 /C176 C resulted in a primarily
transgranular fracture mode with secondary intergranu-
lar and ductile fracture components (Figure 9(c)).
The TiVNb–O
1.0 alloy, in contrast, showed a trans-
granular-dominated fracture at RT (Figure 10(a)), with
the fracture surface containing numerous microvoids
resembling those in Figure 9(b). At 600 /C176 C, distinct slip
traces and oxidized microvoids were evident
(Figure 10(b)), while testing at 800 /C176 C produced cleav-
age facets with river patterns (Figure 10(c)). These
observations indicate that while oxygen solid solution
substantially enhance mechanical properties, the TiVNb
medium-entropy alloy system shows high sensitivity to
both interstitial oxygen concentration and environmen-
tal oxygen exposure.
Interstitial atoms play a crucial role in inﬂuencing the
mechanical properties of high-entropy alloys, primarily
through atomic radius mismatch and local elastic
distortion ﬁelds. These eﬀects collectively modify dislo-
cation motion and slip resistance. As the interstitial
concentration increases, the resulting lattice distortion
intensiﬁes, leading to substantial strengthening, a
well-established interstitial strengthening mechanism.
[20]
However, the resulting ductility behavior shows remark-
able system composition-dependent variability. For
instance, in the TiZrHfNb high-entropy alloy system,
oxygen content up to 2 at. pct could produce good
ductility. This ductility arises from the formation of
ordered interstitial oxygen complexes, a distinct struc-
ture state between oxide ceramic nanoparticles and
disordered interstitial atoms. These complexes facilitate
ductility enhancement by altering the dislocation slip
modes.
[15] Conversely, in ZrTiHfNb0.5Ta0.5Ox (x = 0.05
to 0.2) high-entropy alloy, oxygen atoms were fully
dissolved in the matrix without precipitation. When
oxygen content exceeds 0.2 at. pct, the alloy’s compres-
sive strain began to decrease markedly.
[34] These con-
trasting behaviors indicate that interstitial atoms aﬀect
the ductility through diverse mechanisms that are highly

V. CONCLUSION
This study investigates the strengthening eﬀects of
oxygen doping on TiVNb lightweight medium-entropy
alloys, with a focus on its inﬂuence on microstructural
evolution, mechanical properties and electrochemical
corrosion behavior. Key results demonstrate that oxy-
gen dissolves into the BCC matrix while forming limited
lamellar (V, Ti, Nb)–O precipitates, whose phase struc-
ture are closely related to the oxygen concentration.
After homogenization annealing (1200 /C176 C, 12 h), these
precipitates dissolve into the matrix. Oxygen doping
