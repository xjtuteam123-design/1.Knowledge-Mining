Effects of Cooling Rate on the Microstructure and Room Temperature 
Tensile Properties of Orthorhombic Ti-22Al-21Nb-1Ta-1W Alloy 
Dong Luo 1, a, Yuyou Cui 1, b and Rui Yang 1, c 
1Institute of Metal Research, Chinese Academy of Sciences, 
72 Wenhua Road, Shenyang 110016, China 
adluo@imr.ac.cn, byycui@imr.ac.cn, cryang@imr.ac.cn 
Keywords: Ti 2AlNb, Microstructure, Mechanical Property, Cooling Rate 
Abstract. In order to improve high temperature mechanical properties of Ti 2AlNb based alloys, 
alloying with a combination of Ta and W was studied in the on-going work. The effects of cooling 
rate after solution heat treatment on the room temperature mechanical properties have been reported 
in this paper. All samples were solid solution treated at near β transus temperature, cooled at 
different cooling rates, and then aged at subtransus temperature. E xperimental results showed that, 
with increasing cooling rate, room temperature yield strength decreased sharply to a minimum value, 
and then increased. Change of elongation exhibits a trend opposite to yiel d strength. Microstructure 
of the alloy varied from near lamellar to lamellar plus Widma nstätten, and then to full 
Widmanstätten structure with the increase of cooling rate, and the faster the cooling rate, the finer 
the laths of the O phase. The samples with near lamellar micros tructure obtained at the cooling rate 
of 6 oC/min possess the best ductility but relatively low yield strength. 
Introduction 
Since the orthorhombic (O) phase in Ti-25Al-12.5Nb (at.%) was discovere d by Banerjee et al. 
in 1988 [1], this kind of titanium aluminides (based on Ti 2AlNb) have attracted much attention as 
potential materials for high temperature structural applications , primarily due to their higher 
specific strength and stiffness than nickel-base superalloy a s well as a good balance of strength and 
room-temperature ductility [2]. Ti 2AlNb-based alloy generally consists of β (B2 structure) and O 
phases, and some alloys also contain a small amount of α2 phase (D0 19 structure). Like commercial 
titanium alloys, the mechanical properties of Ti 2AlNb based alloys depend strongly upon the 
microstructure and therefore processing method. Recent investigations have centered on the 
relationship between the processing, microstructure and mechanical properties of ternary Ti-Al-Nb 
system [2-5]. In order to improve high temperature strength of Ti 2AlNb-based alloys, alloying with 
W (Ti-22Al-20Nb-2W) [6] or Ta (Ti-22Al-20Nb-7Ta) [7] were investigate d. The former alloy with 
W has good tensile strength but poorer room-temperature ductility tha n Ti-Al-Nb alloys. The latter 
alloy was reported to have good balance of strength and ductility. How ever, the use of a significant 
amount of the expensive alloying element Ta will not proper for en gineering application because of 
the high cost.

combination of Ta+W, and Ti-22Al-21Nb-1Ta-1W alloy was prepared in or der to obtaining good 
balance of mechanical properties. In this paper, the effect of coolin g rate on the microstructure and

Experimental 
The alloy with nominal composition of Ti-22Al-21Nb-1Ta-1W (at.%) was prepared by melting 
in a consumable vacuum arc furnace and remelting in a cold-wall va cuum furnace to obtain the 
homogenous of ingot. The cast ingot was broken down in B2 phase field, and the n recrystallization 
annealed at 850 oC for 1 h followed by air cooling to achieve fine grain sizes. Fina lly, the 
recrystallized ingot was free forged into rods with a diamet er of about 30 mm in α2+B2 phase field 
in order to obtain fine α2 precipitates in the B2 matrix. 
All specimens were electro-discharge machined from the as-for ged rods. Tensile specimens 
were 4.0 mm in diameter and 20 mm in gauge length. Tensile tests w ere performed at room 
temperature in air. The data of tensile properties were the a verage values of three specimens. 
Microstructure was observed by a scanning electron microscope (SEM , Leo 1450) in the 
back-scattered electron (BSE) imaging mode. 
Results and Discussion 
The β-transus temperature of Ti-22Al-21Nb-1Ta-1W was determined to be 1110±10 oC by, firstly, 
heating samples of the alloy at a series of temperatures for 1 h, and then examining the 
microstructure with an optical microscope. 
A previous study by Emura et al. [8] showed that hot working in the α2+B2 phase field led to a 
dispersion of α2 precipitates in the B2 matrix, which can be used to control grain growth and to 
obtain fine B2 grains during subsequent heat treatments. In our investigation, the alloy was also free 
forged in the α2+B2 phase field, and the as-forged microstructure was shown in Fig.1. D ue to the 
effect of W, the distribution of α2 particles is not as homogeneous as expected. 
In order to obtain good balance of mechanical properties, all specime ns of Ti-22Al-21Nb-1Ta- 
1W alloy were solution treated at 1100 oC for 1 h, and then cooled at different cooling rates followed 
by ageing at 900 oC for 2 h. During the cooling, the O/ α2 phase precipitated from the matrix of 
primary B2 phase. The typical microstructure was shown in Fig.2, in which the fine black particles 
are α2 phase precipitated during forging in the two-phase field. The inset at the upper right corner 
shows that coarse lamellar laths grew from a grain boundary to g rain interior, and Widmanstätten 
laths formed within the grains. The microstructure of samples coole d at different rates exhibits 
distinct features as shown in Figs.2 and 3. With the increase of cool ing rate, it can be seen from 
Figs.2 and 3 that the portion of the Widmanstätten structure increases at the expense of lamellar lath.

Conclusion 
The microstructure and mechanical properties of Ti-22Al-21Nb-1Ta-1W a lloy depend on the 
cooling rate after solution treatment significantly. The micros tructure of the alloy changed from 
near lamellar to lamellar plus Widmanstätten, and then to full Wi dmanstätten with increasing 
cooling rate; the faster the cooling rate, the finer the laths. As a result, yield strength decreases 
sharply with increasing cooling rate from 1 oC/min, and reaches a minimum at 6 oC/min. When the 
cooling rate is higher than 6 oC/min, yield strength increases. The change of elongation shows a 
trend opposite to that of yield strength.
