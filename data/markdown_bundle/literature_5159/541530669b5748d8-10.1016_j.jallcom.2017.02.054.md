abstract
The effect of gallium addition on the solidi ﬁcation microstructure and micromechanical properties of
constituents was investigated in two sets of Nb e16Si(1Ga) (in at.%) and Nb e16Sie22Tie2Cre2-
Ale2Hf(5Ga) (all in at.%) alloys. The addition of gallium was found to promote the formation of Nb 5Si3
and (Nb,Ti) 5Si3 phases in Nb e16Sie1Ga and Nb e16Sie22Tie2Cre2Al-2Hfe5Ga alloys, respectively.
Evaluation of micromechanical properties of constituents were done by nanoindentation and micropillar
compression testing. Nanoindentation showed that gallium addition reduces hardness and Young's
modulus for both Nb
ss and silicide phases (Nb 3Si and (Nb,Ti)5Si3); while it enhances fracture toughness of
silicide phases. However, micropillar compressions showed that the addition of gallium decreases the
compressive strength of the Nb ss phase while increasing the strength of the silicides.

1. Introduction
NbeSi based alloys are promising materials for ultra-high
temperature structural applications, such as gas turbine and
aircraft engines. This is due to their higher melting point (>1750
/C14 C)
and lower density (6.6 e7.2 g/cm3) compared to the Ni based su-
peralloys (9.2 g/cm 3), as well as high temperature strength, and
good oxidation resistance [1e3]. The high strength of these alloys is
attributed to the presence of silicide phases (Nb 5Si3 or Nb 3Si) [1].
However, due to relatively low room temperature fracture tough-
ness and ductility, they have, to-date, had limited applications in
service. In recent years, continuous efforts have been made to
enhance both strength and fracture toughness by modifying the
alloy compositions [3e11 ], or processing parameters [12e15].I n
those strategies, control of the volume fraction and morphology of
the Nb solid solution (Nbss) and eutectics (Nbss/silicide) is the key to
obtaining desirable properties.
Binary Nb eSi alloys show high strength but are too brittle for
practical applications. Thus, various alloying elements have been
added to enhance the fracture toughness, forming multicomponent
NbeSi based alloys. Among those multicomponent alloys, Nb e
SieTieAleCreHf alloy is one of the most promising alloys with
excellent comprehensive mechanical properties [12,16]. Studies
have shown that the addition of Ti in the Nb eSi alloy improves the
fracture toughness and ductility of the Nb
ss phase at room tem-
perature, as well as improving oxidation resistance [7,11,17,18].C r
addition was found to increase the room temperature strength of
Nbss due to solid solution strengthening [19]. The addition of FCC
elements, such as Al, in the Nb eSi alloys has been shown to reduce
the density and resulted in an improvement in mechanical and
oxidation properties of Nb eSieTi alloys [19,20]. In addition, Hf has
been shown to improve fracture toughness of the bulk material by
reﬁnement of the microstructure [21]. A recent report on the effect
of Ga addition in a ternary Nb eSieGa alloy with hypereutectic
composition showed that Ga can alter the solidi ﬁcation path and
reduce the eutectic spacing, thus improving both the compression
properties and fracture toughness of the bulk material signi ﬁcantly

Understanding the mechanical properties of individual phase is
very helpful in understanding the mechanical behavior of the bulk
material [27e32]. The successful application of Nb eSi based alloys
strongly relies on understanding the effect of the various alloying
additions on mechanical properties. Currently, most of the studies
have concentrated on the effect of these additions on the micro-
structure and resulting bulk mechanical properties. Very few
studies have been performed to investigate the effect of these
alloying elements on the mechanical properties of each constituent
phase present in the microstructure. In the present work, we
attempt to explore the effect of gallium alloying on microstructure
evolution and mechanical properties of constituents in two sets of
NbeSi based alloys: Hypoeutectic binary Nb e16Si and multicom-
ponent Nbe16Sie22Tie2Ale2Cre2Hf alloy. Mechanical properties,
such as hardness, Young's modulus and fracture toughness, were
evaluated for each constituent phase using nanoindentation.
Compressive deformation behavior of each constituent phase was
obtained and compared using the micropillar compression
technique.
2. Experimental procedure
Two sets of master alloy ingots with nominal compositions of
Nbe16Si (in at.%) and Nb e16Sie22Tie2Ale2Cre2Hf (in at.%) were
prepared by arc melting high purity alloying elements. The alloys
were remelted four times, under puri ﬁed Ar gas protection, for
chemical homogenization before it was further suction cast in a
water cooled copper crucible. In order to study the effect of Ga
addition, Nb e16Sie1Ga and Nb e16Sie22Tie2Ale2Cr-2Hfe5Ga
alloys were also prepared for comparison. A small sample of
~8 /C2 8 /C2 3m m3 was machined from the central region of the cast
ingot. The sample was ﬁrst mechanically grounded with silicon
carbide paper and then polished to a 1 mm diamond ﬁnish. Finally, a
suspension of 0.05 mm colloidal silica was used as a ﬁnal polish to
remove possible surface damage caused by previous polishing
steps. Chemical compositions of each phase were determined by
energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) analysis. X-ray diffraction
(XRD) analysis was performed to determine the phases present in
the alloy. The XRD measurements were conducted at a step size of
0.02/C14 for a range of 20e90/C14 with an accelerator detector using CuKa
radiation (0.15418 Å) (D/max-RB, Rigaku Co., Tokyo, Japan).
Micropillars were fabricated using a dual-beam focused ion
beam (FIB) in a scanning electron microscope (Nova 200 NanoLab
FEGSEM/FIB, FEI Co, Oregon, U.S) with Ga þ ion source operated at
30 keV. Annular milling approach was used to fabricate the
micropillars [33]. The ion beam currents were decreased from
20 nA to 100 pA to obtain pillars with top diameters of ~1.7 mm for
both Nb ss and silicide phase (Nb 3Si or (Nb,Ti) 5Si3). In addition,
(Nb,Ti)5Si3 pillars with a top diameter of ~5.5 mm were also fabri-
cated in Nbe16Sie22Tie2Cre2Al-2Hfe5Ga alloy to ensure that the
pillars tested were representative of the microstructural phase
being evaluated By the annular milling approach pillars with a
typical aspect ratio (length/diameter) of 3 e3.5 and taper less than
3/C14 were obtained. After the fabrication of the pillars, EDS was
performed at the base of each pillar to verify the composition of the
individual phase.
Nanoindentation and micropillar compression were utilized to
evaluate the micromechanical properties of each constituent in the
alloys. Nanoindentation tests were carried out in a commercial
nanoindenter XP system (Keysight Technologies, Santa Clara, CA)
after the calibration of indenter tip on a standard fused silica
sample. Continuous stiffness measurement (CSM) technique was
used to measure the hardness and Young's modulus with depth of
indentation. Before starting the actual experiment, the sample was
allowed to thermally equilibrate with the instrument until the drift
rate was measured to be below 0.05 nm s /C0 1. The experiments were
conducted to a maximum indentation depth of 1000 nm at a strain
rate of ~0.05/s. In order to evaluate the indentation depth on the
deformation behavior, tests with a maximum depth of 500 nm and
300 nm were also run on (Nb,Ti) 5Si3 phase of Nb e16Sie22Tie2-
Ale2Cre2Hf(5Ga) alloys. At least 15 indentations were made in
each phase to obtain reasonable statistics. After testing, the in-
dentations were examined in a scanning electron microscope (XL30
Environmental FEG, FEI Co, Oregon). Micropillar compressions
were carried out using a nanoindenter XP equipped with a ﬂat
Berkovich diamond indenter tip (three-sided pyramid, 10 mm long
for each side of the cross section). The displacement rates were
changed according to the height of the pillars to maintain a con-
stant initial strain rate of ~10 /C0 3 s/C0 1. The actual displacement was
corrected after subtracting the displacement due to the compliance
of indenter and the substrate [34].

3.1. Microstructural characterization and phase identi ﬁcation
Fig. 1 shows typical backscattered electron (BSE) micrographs of
the as-cast microstructures for the four alloys. Phases in the as-cast
alloys were identi ﬁed by x-ray diffraction technique, as shown in
Fig. 2 . EDS was also performed to obtain the composition of each
constituent phase, as listed in Table 1 . The microstructure and
phase/composition analyses show that the as-cast microstructure
of hypoeutectic Nb e16Si alloy is comprised of Nb
ss (white phase)
and Nb 3Si (gray phase). However, the ternary alloy with 1a t.% Ga
has an additional phase of Nb 5Si3 (dark gray phase in Fig. 1(b)). It is
interesting to note that the addition of even a small concentration
of Ga in the Nb e16Si alloy can promote the formation of Nb 5Si3.
This is because the solidi ﬁcation path of the binary alloy was
modiﬁed/altered with the addition of Ga, following the non-
equilibrium metastable phase diagram [9].
In the complex multicomponent alloys, the results show that
the as-cast microstructure of Nb e16Sie22Tie2Ale2Cre2Hf alloy
consists of primary blocky (Nb,Ti) 5Si3 (light gray color in Fig. 1(c))
and two different types of eutectic microstructures of Nb ss and
(Nb,Ti)5Si3. We believe that the Ti enriched eutectic (Nb,Ti) 5Si3
(dark gray color) is a direct solidi ﬁcation product under the cooling
condition imposed by the water cooled copper crucible. It should be
pointed out that the composition of the eutectic (Nb,Ti) 5Si3 phase
(light gray color) is quite similar to that of the primary (Nb,Ti) 5Si3
phase. It is evident that the addition of multiple alloying elements
changed the solidi ﬁcation path of binary hypoeutectic Nb e16Si
where the formation of primary Nb
ss is expected. Although Ti has
been reported to stabilize Nb 3Si phase, Cr, Hf and Al have been
reported to promote the direction formation of Nb 5Si3 phase
[10,23,35e37]. A similar alloy solidi ﬁcation microstructure

alloy, microstructure is composed of primary large blocky

Nbss and (Nb,Ti)5Si3. The EDS analysis in Table 1 shows that Cr and

3.2. Micromechanical properties of nanoindentation
Nanoindentation was performed on Nb ss phase and silicide
phases with relatively large grain size. In the case of Nb e16-
Sie22Tie2Ale2Cr-2Hfe5Ga alloy, nanoindentation was only per-
formed on (Nb,Ti) 5Si3 phase and not on Nb ss phase due to very
small size of the latter phase. Young's modulus (E) and hardness (H)
values for each indentation were measured as an average value over
a displacement of 75 e150 nm into the specimen. The selection of
this depth was based on the observation that some of the curves
increased or decreased at higher depth of indentation indicating
that there was effect from the surrounding and/or other phases. In
addition, cracks that occurred at the higher load, i.e., at higher
depth of indentation, in the Nb 3Si or (Nb,Ti) 5Si3 phase would also
have introduced some errors in the measurement of both the
hardness and Young's modulus, at higher depths. The selection of
this depth for measurement was done to minimize the above ef-
fects. Results in Table 2 clearly show that Young's modulus and
hardness of the silicide phase are signi ﬁcantly higher than those of
the Nb ss counterpart. More interestingly, the addition of Ga was
observed to reduce both the Young's modulus and hardness of each
constituent. In the case of Nb e16Sie1Ga alloy, the hardness and
Young's modulus values are decreased by 1 1.9% and 4.6%, respec-
tively, for the Nb ss. However, a slight decrease in Young's modulus
and hardness values (2.3% and 5.5%, respectively) was observed in
the case of Nb 3Si. The addition of 5 at.% Ga in the multicomponent
alloy resulted in reduction of hardness from 22.5 ± 1.4 GPa to
18.8 ± 0.6 GPa and Young's modulus values from 373.7± 16.8 and to
255.9 ± 8.3 GPa for the (Nb,Ti) 5Si3 phase. The values are 16.4% and
31.5% lower than those in the alloy before Ga alloying. The hardness
of various phases depends largely on composition and crystal
structure. In this study, Ga should substitute as an alloying element
of Si [39]. In our samples, due to the addition of gallium, the Si
content in each phase was reduced ( Table 1 ), resulting in lower
hardness values of silicide phases due to smaller strengthening
effect of gallium. This is also supported by the fact that the reduc-
tion in hardness is greater in the sample containing 5 at.% Ga than
the 1 at.% Ga. A similar effect of Ga on the reduction of harness of
niobium silicide phase has also been reported due to the presence
of Ga in the phases [9,40].
Fig. 3 shows typical deformation of indentations made on the
Nbss and silicide phases in the four alloys for the depth of 1000 nm.
It can be seen that the silicide phase showed indentation-induced
cracks at the corners of the indentations, indicating a brittle char-
acteristic of the silicide phases. The absence of any cracks around
the indentations made on Nb ss suggest that it was able to release
energy by plastic deformation, and therefore is ductile in nature.
One may also notice that in the case of indentations made on
(Nb,Ti)5Si3 phase of Nb e16Sie22Tie2Ale2Cr-2Hfe5Ga alloy pro-
duced slightly smaller cracks than those in Nb e16Sie22Tie2-
Ale2Cre2Hf alloy. Moreover, some (5 out of 15) indentations on
(Nb,Ti)5Si3 phase in Nb e16Sie22Tie2Ale2Cr-2Hfe5Ga alloy
exhibited no cracks, while all indentations on (Nb,Ti) 5Si3 phase
showed cracks in Nb e16Sie22Tie2Ale2Cre2Hf alloy. This in-
dicates that the addition of Ga in the alloy, and therefore in the
silicide phase, increased fracture toughness, which will be further
discussed in the following section.
In order to study the effect of different indentation depths on
the indentation behavior, nanoindentations at depths of 500 nm
and 300 nm were also performed on the (Nb,Ti) 5Si3 phase of
Nbe16Sie22Tie2Ale2Cre2Hf(5Ga) alloys, respectively, and the
results are shown in Fig. 4 . Interestingly, no cracks around the
(Nb,Ti)5Si3 indentations in Nb e16Sie22Tie2Ale2Cre2Hf alloy
were detected in some of 500 nm ( Fig. 4(b)) and 300 nm ( Fig. 4(d))
indentations. It is noticed that none of 500 nm ( Fig. 4 (e)) and
300 nm ( Fig. 4 (f)) indentations on (Nb,Ti) 5Si3 phase of Nb e16-
Sie22Tie2Ale2Cr-2Hfe5Ga alloy show cracks. Thus, the enhanced
fracture resistance of (Nb,Ti) 5Si3 phase due to the presence of Ga is
evident. However, we need to point out that due to the complex
tetragonal structure of (Nb,Ti) 5Si3 phase, the crystallographic
orientation of the grain on which the indentation was performed
may also affect the deformation properties.

fabricated by annular milling, as reported recently by Hutsch et al.
[53]. The effect of friction between the indenter tip and the top
surface of the micropillar on the deformation is usually minimized

friction is very difﬁcult to remove in the micropillar compression as
lubrication cannot be applied between ﬂat punch and the pillar top.
Zhang et al. [49] showed that the presence of friction can result in
the increase of ﬂow stress during compression and the effect in-
creases with increase in aspect ratio of sample. They showed that
the effect of friction in increasing ﬂow stress is only after about
9e10% strain, which is higher than the values in this study, when
the aspect ratio is ~3 (which is our case). Rabbe et al. [54] also found
that effect of friction on ﬂow stress only takes place at higher strain
values. Moreover, friction has been shown to be helpful in pre-
venting buckling of the micropillar [49,54,55]. Therefore, the effect
of friction on ﬂow stress values of Nb
ss should not be considered to
be signi ﬁcant.

4. Conclusions
In this work we report on the effect of Ga on both microstruc-
tural evolution and micromechanical properties of constituents in
hypoeutectic Nb e16Si and multi-component Nb e16Sie22Tie2-
Ale2Cre2Hf alloys. Based on the results observed in this work, the
following conclusions can be made:
(1) Ga addition promotes the formation of Nb 5Si3/(Nb,Ti)5Si3
silicides in Nb eSi based alloys.
(2) Both nanoindentation and micropillar compression show a
much lower hardness, Young's modulus, and compressive
strength values for Nb ss than the silicide counterparts.
Micromechanical tests show that the addition of Ga reduces
the hardness and Young's modulus values of both Nb ss and
silicide phases, e.g. Nb 3Si and (Nb,Ti) 5Si3; while it enhances
fracture toughness and compressive strength of silicide
phases.
(3) Fracture toughness of Nb 3Si and (Nb,Ti) 5Si3 were evaluated
by nanoindentations and they are measured to be
~2.1e2.8 MPa-m 1/2 and ~2.0 e5.4 MPa-m 1/2, respectively, by
Laugier's model.
(4) Compression of ~1.7 mmN b ss micropillars reveals a strength
of ~1.0e1.3 GPa, while the compressive strength of Nb3Si and
(Nb,Ti)5Si3 silicide are measured to be 6.6 e8.3 GPa and
6.7e8.8 GPa, respectively.
