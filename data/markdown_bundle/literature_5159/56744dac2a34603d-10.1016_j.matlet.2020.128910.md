abstract
The microstructure, phase transformation temperature, c/c0 lattice misﬁt, and mechanical properties of
Co-4Ti-10V-2W-2Ta (at. %, referred to as 2Ta) quinary alloy were investigated in detail. In addition,
the results show that Ti, W, and Ta exhibit a strong partitioning tendency to the c0 phase, resulting in
a high c0 solvus temperature ( ~1131 /C176C) in the studied alloy. The c/c0 lattice misﬁt of the 2Ta alloy
was found to be ~+0.33%, which is much lower than those of Co-Ti binary and Co-Ti-Mo/W/Re/Cr ternary
alloys. Moreover, the designed alloy also exhibits excellent yield strength (880 MPa at 750 /C176C), which is
comparable to Co-12Ti-4Mo alloy.

1. Introduction
The recent detection of ternary c0 phases in Co-Al-W/Mo/Ta [1–
3] and Co-V-Nb/Ta [4] alloy systems have made the development
of c0-strengthened Co-based superalloys more attractive. However,
the applications of this class of alloys are hampered by their low c0
phase stability with a low solvus temperature. Co-Ti-based super-
alloys strengthened by the stable
c0 -Co3Ti phase are generally
regarded as a promising candidates for the development of c0 -
strengthened Co-based superalloys despite their extremely high
c/c0 lattice misﬁt and low microstructural stability [5]. Experimen-
tal trials have been made to explore the low c/c0 lattice misﬁt in
Co-Ti-Cr/Mo/W/Re ternary alloys; however, either their solidus
temperature or high-temperature strength are low when com-
pared with other Co-based alloys [6–9]. Recently, our group
reported that V-modiﬁed Co-Ti-V alloys with irregular cuboidal
c0 precipitates might exhibit a lower lattice misﬁt [6]. Even then,
the yield strength of Co-Ti-V alloys is still on the low side [6].
While Al, Re, Mo, Cr, Nb, W, and Ta are regarded to be important
elements in the development of Co-Ti-based alloys, Nb, W, and
Ta as the accepted strong c0 -former elements can signiﬁcantly
improve the c0 solvus temperature and strength, but Nb will reduce
the melting temperature in Co-based superalloys [6–9]. Therefore,
it is possible to replace V by W and Ta to design a multicomponent
Co-Ti-V-W-Ta alloy with the combination of low lattice misﬁt and
high strength.
2. Experimental procedures
The alloy Co-4Ti-10V-2W-2Ta (at. %) deﬁned as 2Ta alloy, were
melted many times. The purity of all raw materials used in this
study exceeded 99.9%. The specimens for heat-treatment were
sealed in a vacuum quartz tube. After homogenizing at 1200 /C176C
for 2.5 h and then annealing at 800 /C176C for 100 h (the standard heat
treatment), the ingots were cut into columns of U 4m m /C2 6m mt o
conduct compression tests (1.0 /C2 10/C0 4 s/C0 1 strain rate). The phase
transformation temperatures were examined using differential
scanning calorimetry (DSC) with a heating rate of 20 /C176C/min and
the mass densities were determined by the Archimedes law. A
transmission electron microscope (TEM) was utilized to determine
the crystal structure of c0 precipitates and the composition of the
c/c0 phases were determined by STEM-EDX. X-ray diffraction
(XRD) was used to measure the lattice constants of phases.

3. Results and discussions
3.1. Microstructure and lattice misﬁt
The TEM images and the composition of 2Ta alloy are shown in
Fig. 1(a-c). Fine c/c0 two-phase microstructures are detected in 2Ta
alloys, and Fig. 1 b is the diffraction pattern along the [001] zone
axis and reveals that the c0 precipitates are L1 2-ordered structures.
The composition of c/c0 and the partition coefﬁcient are displayed
in Table 1 . Usually, the partitioning tendency of alloying elements
between the c and c0 phases can be ascertained by the partition
coefﬁcient K X [10], where K X > 1 indicates that X is partitioned to
the c0 phase, while K X < 1 means that X distributes to the c phase.
Thus, it is clear that the Ti, V, W, and Ta partition to the c0 phase
and the Co partitions to the c phase, which accords with the
reported Co-based superalloys [6–8,11]. In addition, the DSC
curves ( Fig. S1 in Supplementary material ) shows the c0 solvus
and the solidus temperature of 2Ta are 1131 /C176C, indicating that
the addition of W and Ta signiﬁcantly improved the c0 solvus tem-
perature of the 2Ta alloy compared to the Co-5Ti-15V alloy
(1023 /C176C) [6]. Moreover, the lattice constants of c/c0 phases of
the 2Ta alloy are measured to be ~0.3595 nm and ~0.3607 nm
(Fig. 1 d), respectively, and thus, the lattice misﬁt of the 2Ta alloy
is calculated to be ~+0.33% by d ¼ 2ð

3.2. Lattice misﬁt, solvus temperature, and density comparison
Fig. 2 shows the lattice misﬁt, c0 solvus temperature, and den-
sity comparisons of 2Ta, Co-Ti-Mo/Cr/V/W/Re-based [6–9,12], Co-
Al-W/V/Mo-based [2,10,11], and Co-V-Ta-based [13] alloys. It is
found that the lattice misﬁt of binary or other multicomponent
Co-Ti-based (0.35–1.17%) alloys are higher than that of 2Ta
(0.33%) alloys because of the excessive Ti content. Moreover, Co-
9Al-9W and Co-V-Ta-based alloys without Ti exhibit a lower lattice
misﬁt (0.32–0.53%). Therefore, it can be reasonably speculated that
a higher Ti content usually leads to a higher value of misﬁt, which
is consistent with the law in Co-Al-W-based alloys [14] whose lat-
tice misﬁt increases continuously with the rise in Ti content. In
addition, the c0 solvus temperature of the 2Ta alloy is higher than
that of Co-Ti-Mo/Cr/Re-based, Co-Al-W/V/Mo-based, and Co-V-Ta
alloys because of the higher proportion of the stronger c0-former
element content in the 2Ta alloy. However, the c0 solvus tempera-
ture of the 2Ta alloy is close to that of the Co-Ti-W-based alloy due
to the comparable proportion of the stronger c0-former element
content in these alloys. The density of 2Ta is signiﬁcantly higher
than that of Co-Ti-Mo/Cr-based and Co-Al-V/Mo-based alloy, but
is comparable to Co-Ti-W/Re-based, Co-Al-W-based,
Co-V-Ta-based alloys because of the addition of high density ele-
ment (W, Ta) in 2Ta alloy. Thus, to design an alloy with excellent
comprehensive performance requires a compositional trade-off.
3.3. Mechanical properties
Fig. 3 presents the temperature dependence of the yield
strength of 2Ta, Co-Ti/V/ Mo/Re/Cr [6,8,9,16], Co-9Al-9W-based
[17], and the traditional Co-based (MAR-M509 [18]) superalloys.
There are three stages of the change in yield strength. Firstly, the
yield strength shows a normal decrease with the rise in tempera-
ture. In the second stage, that is, at 600 /C176C to 750 /C176C, an abnormal
temperature dependence of strength is detected and the maximum
value ( ~880 MPa) appears at 750 /C176C. Finally, the yield strength
decreases with the increase in temperature. This abnormality has
been frequently observed in other L1 2-order c0 phases [7,8,10,11].
It is widely accepted that the start of a large amount of 1/3 h112i
partial dislocations at low temperature and the transformation in
the dislocation mechanism from shearing to bypassing at high
temperature might be responsible for the anomaly in yield
strength [17]. It is found that the strength of the Co-12Ti-4Mo alloy
is comparable to that of 2Ta alloy due to its ultra-high lattice misﬁt
[16] (see Section 3.2 ). Nevertheless, the excessive lattice misﬁt
might lead to instability of the microstructure as in the observed
coarsened c/c0 microstructure in Co-12Ti-4Mo alloy [7]. Addition-
ally, the yield strength of 2Ta alloy is also superior to other novel or
traditional Co-based alloys.
4. Conclusions
We successfully designed a potential Co-4Ti-10V-2W-2Ta quin-
ary alloy that exhibits a low c/c0 lattice misﬁt (+0.33%) lower than
those of other Co-Ti-based superalloys, a higher c0 solvus temper-
ature (1131 /C176C) compared with the partial Co-Ti-based, Co-Al-W/V/
Mo-based, and Co-V-Ta-based superalloys, a moderate density
(8.77 g/cm 3), and an attractive yield strength that reaches
1025 MPa and 880 MPa at 25 /C176C and 750 /C176C, respectively.
