ABSTRACT 
In most γ "-strengthened superalloys, the γ " →δ stabilization is generally considered as an unfavorable trans -
formation process due to the incapability of δ phase on conferring high-temperature strengthening. While recent 
studies in multi-component alloys indicate that another stabilized γ " phase ( ε phase with D0
19 
structure) has been 
found and proven to be beneficial to mechanical properties. This work shows that the precipitation of ε phase 
could be tailored in such a way, that it is accompanied by the formation of nano-twins in low stacking fault 
energy NiCoCr-based multi-component alloys, establishing a unique matrix/(nano-twin/ ε /nano-twin)/matrix 
double-stacked-sandwich heterogeneous nanostructure. This novel ε -plate/nano-twin structure has been found to 
provide satisfying high temperature strength-ductility synergy and structural stability after aging, thereby 
leading to a yield-strength enhancement of ~120% at 25 
◦
C and ~98% at 700 
◦
C, respectively, while still 
maintaining sound ductilities. This unique self-organized, heterogeneous, lamellar nanostructure opens up new 
avenues for further development of hierarchically-nanostructured, high-temperature structural materials. 
1. Introduction 
Ni-based superalloys are extensively applied in advanced applica -
tions in aerospace, land-based power-generation, nuclear and petro -
chemical industries [ 1 , 2 ]. The precipitation-strengthened Ni-based 
superalloys are most widely used, and many of them are strengthened by 
metastable, ordered γ "-Ni
3
Nb phase (tetragonal D0
22 
structure) which is 
coherent with the γ matrix. It has been shown that the γ " phase can 
significantly improve the room- and medium-temperature strengths of 
Ni-based superalloys on account of high anti-phase boundary energy and 
high lattice misfit (about~2.9%) [ 3 ]. This group of alloys, such as 
IN625, IN725 and IN718, have a good combination of strength, 
ductility, weldability and good resistance to high temperature corrosion 
during prolonged exposure to aggressive environments [ 4 – 6 ]. Currently, 
multi-component alloys, also known as high-entropy alloys (HEAs) or 
medium-entropy alloys (MEAs), have gained increasing research in -
terests due to their unique compositions and excellent properties [ 7 – 9 ]. 
Research on γ "-strengthened multi-component alloys has also received 
interests [ 10 – 13 ], and in our recently published results, ref. [ 14 , 15 ], the 
precipitation of γ " can greatly improve the room- and 
medium-temperature specific strengths of NiCoCr-based multi-compo -
nent alloys in a similar fashion as in superalloys. 
However, the γ " phase is a metastable form of Ni
3
Nb which tends to 
quickly destabilize and transform into the D0
a 
structured δ phase (in Ni- 
based superalloys) [ 16 ] or the D0
19 
structured ε phase (in some HEAs) 
[ 17 , 18 ]. This γ " →δ transformation in Ni-based superalloys usually oc -
curs during service or thermal/mechanical aging over 650 
◦
C [ 16 , 19 , 
20 ], and is accompanied by a sharp decrease in strength [ 21 ]. This 
strength decrease is due to the incapability of δ phase to confer similar 
level of high-temperature strength, since it is invariably incoherent with 
γ matrix [ 16 , 22 ]. While the coherent ε phase has been proven to be 
beneficial for high temperature strengths of some HEAs [ 17 ], but it still 
cannot be comparable with γ’’ . The poor stability of γ’’ phase and lack of 
high temperature strengthening from stabilized phases ( ε and δ ) severely 
limit the development of γ "-strengthened alloys and restrict their ap -
plications at medium temperatures.

In order to further investigate γ’’ -strengthened alloys, plenty of re -
searchers are focusing on how to increase the stability of γ’’ phase, 
aiming to improve the high temperature service performance [ 11 , 23 , 
24 ]. Previously, efforts are made in by Pan et al. in improving the phase 
stability of γ’’ in NiCoCr-based multi-component alloys via alloying with 
γ’’ -forming elements [ 14 , 15 ]. Even so, the γ " stabilization trans -
formation is still inevitable. To break through this design bottleneck, an 
alternative approach would be addressing the long-standing dilemma in 
the lack of high temperature strength of the more thermodynamically 
stable δ / ε phases. In alloy systems containing high Co content, the γ " 
phase exhibits a greater propensity to transform into stable ε phase [ 18 ]. 
However, there are few reports on the efforts to optimize the ε phase 
strengthening of superalloys or multi-component alloys at present. 
Materials with heterogeneous structure has become a popular 
research field due to its superior performance and great application 
potentials [ 25 ]. Nano-lamellar structure or nano-twin structure con -
taining high-density of interfaces are also a kind of heterogeneous 
structure [ 25 ]. Alloys with lamellar nanostructures have been proved to 
be promising structural materials because of their unusual 
interface-driven properties, such as extremely high strengths [ 26 – 28 ]. 
The effects of nano-twin boundaries are also significant in improving 
mechanical properties by impeding dislocation slip, while they can also 
provide room for dislocation accumulations and storage, whereas 
high-angle grain boundaries cannot [ 29 – 31 ]. 
In this paper, we found that by tailoring the nano-sized aligned ε 
plates, high temperature strength can be drastically improved in NiCoCr 
multi-component alloy system, which is different from the typically 
reported microstructure of ε phase with Widmanst ¨atten pattern in multi- 
component alloys [ 17 , 18 ]. A novel matrix/(na -
no-twin/ ε /nano-twin)/matrix double-stacked-sandwich hetero- -
nanostructure has been established out of the low stacking fault energy 
(SFE) in NiCoCr alloy system [ 32 ]. The morphology and formation of 
this heterogeneous nanostructure have been thoroughly investigated 
and discussed in terms of strengthening mechanism and enhanced high 
temperature mechanical properties.

4. Discussions 
4.1. Formation mechanism of nano-sized double-stacked-sandwich 
heterostructure 
A nanoscale double-stacked-sandwich heterostructure is formed in 
the presented NVT800 alloy, leading to an obvious improvement of the 
mechanical properties at room and high temperature. As shown in Fig. 6 , 
the formation process of this nanostructure was carefully investigated by 
TEM analyses in order to further understand this unique high strength 
and stable nanostructure. 
Fig. 6 (a) shows that ε nucleation occurs at γ grain boundary, then 
grow into lamella. The TEM BF image of the 5 h aged alloy exhibits a 
typical nano-lamellar structure, which is the same as that observed for 
the 100 h aged alloy, as displayed in Fig. 6 (b). However, the SAD pat -
terns ( Fig. 6 (b1)) show that twinning reflections are not presented in the 
5 h aged alloy, indicating that the precipitation of ε phase occurs before 
the nano-twin formation. HR-TEM investigation was carried out on 
these short-term aged alloys as shown in Fig. 6 (c), and a higher 
magnification image of area 1 in Fig. 6 (c) is shown in Fig. 6 (c1), which 
shows that there are stacking faults adjacent to the γ / ε interface. 
According to Ref. [ 45 ], the ε phases in Ni-based superalloys grow in a 
“ double cell ” manner, exhibiting a Widmanst ¨atten morphology. There 
are few reports regarding on the nano-sized ε plates. It has been reported 
that the precipitation of δ phase in γ
′

by continuous precipitation or discontinuous precipitation [ 46 ]. The 
continuous precipitation often occurs at temperatures above 800 
◦
C, and 
results in homogeneously distributed and isolated platelet-like δ phase 
throughout the microstructure, where different variants would intersect 
[ 46 ]. Meanwhile at temperatures ≤ 800 
◦
C, the primary precipitation 
mode of δ phase is discontinuous precipitation, which leads to an 
organized lamellar morphology of δ phase [ 46 ]. Observation as shown 
in Fig. 6 (a) implies that the precipitation mode of ε plates in NVT800 at 
800 
◦
C is similar to δ phase via a possible discontinuous precipitation, as 
is controlled by grain boundary diffusion [ 47 ]. In the case of NVT800,

(2) the small ε precipitate has a semi-coherent (low-energy) interface

interface to balance the interfacial energy between two sides of the

Nevertheless, it is worth mentioning that another alternative ε for -

shows that the γ " phase firstly precipitates from γ matrix then transforms 
to ε phase, indicating a possible eutectoid reaction. However, in present 
work, no γ " phase was found in NVT800 alloys at this moment. As a 
result, further studies are still needed in the future for the determination 
of formation mechanism of ε plates in this alloy system. 
As shown in Fig. 6 (c, c1), plenty of SFs accumulate at the γ / ε inter -
face, which is important to understand the formation mechanism of the 
nano-twin. Tadmor et al. [ 49 ] pointed out that the twinnability of FCC 
alloys strongly depends on their SFEs, and NiCoCr-based multi-compo -
nent alloys have been reported to have relatively low SFEs [ 50 , 51 ]. The 
low SFE contributes to the formation of SFs in the matrix, which can 
subsequently act as twin nuclei. In addition, the growth of Co and 
Cr-poor ε phase (TEM-EDS results as shown in Fig. 3 (f)) would lead to an 
enrichment of Co and Cr at the γ / ε interface as these elements are 
rejected, reducing the SFE locally [ 52 ], resulting in favorable locations 
for the an accumulation of SFs at/near the γ / ε interface ( Fig. 7 (a) Step 
II). Moreover, it has also been reported that Co and Cr atoms tend to 
segregate along the dissociated dislocations and crystal defects upon 
aging [ 53 , 54 ], which can further reduce the local SFE at SFs, and pro -
mote shearing on consecutive planes, leading to the formation of twins. 
Therefore, the low SFE and SFs at γ / ε interface promotes a SF to 
nano-twin transformation [ 55 ], resulting in the formation of this unique 
double-stacked-sandwich nanostructure ( Fig. 7 (a) Step III).

5. Conclusions 
In present work, a new NiCoCr-based multi-component alloy 
strengthened by nanoscale self-organized double-stacked-sandwich 
heterostructure was fabricated. The ε /nano-twin/ γ lamella in this alloy 
exhibit outstanding strengthening effects. The microstructure evolution 
and formation mechanism of nanostructure have been carefully inves -
tigated, as well as its strengthening mechanism at high temperatures. 
The following conclusions can be made: 
(1) Ta has been shown to be a strong ε -forming element, and Ta 
addition can reduce lamellar spacing and increase the volume 
fraction of ε phase, leading to a significant improvement in 
hardness and tensile strength. 
(2) The nano-sized ε plates is still maintained upon long-term (1000 
h) aging, due to the low interfacial energies of semi-coherent γ / ε 
interface and γ /nano-twin boundary, exhibiting outstanding 
microstructural stability. 
(3) The self-organized and nano-structured ε phase in tandem with 
nano-twins has been found to provide satisfying high tempera -
ture strengthening effects in NiCoCr-based multi-component al -
loys, where the mechanical properties from 25 
◦
C to 700 
◦
C are 
significantly improved, resulting in a yield-strength enhancement 
of ~120% at 25 
◦
C and ~98% at 700 
◦
C. Besides, sound ductil -
ities (elongations of 17% – 25%) can also be maintained in 
NiCoCr-based alloys at temperatures from 600 
◦
C to 800 
◦
C. 
In summary, this work proposes a solution to the problem of de -
teriorations of the mechanical properties in γ "-strengthenedalloys due to 
the γ " stabilization transformation at elevated temperatures. Through 
the fabrication of double-stacked-sandwich hetero-nanostructure, 
improved high-temperature strength can be achieved, providing solid 
foundations for further development of hierarchically-nanostructured 
materials for elevated temperatures.
