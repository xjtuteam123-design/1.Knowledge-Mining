Abstract 
The substantial reduction in strength has limited t he widespread usage of Co-Cr 
based alloys at elevated temperatures. Here, we des igned and fabricated an 
ultrafine-grained Co-28Cr-3Ta alloy which could ret ain over 80% of its room 
temperature yield strength at 300 ˚C, two times that of its equivalent without addition 
of Ta. Such high thermal stability was achieved via a processing route which 
precipitated nanoscale Laves phases. The results demonstrate that Orowan 
strengthening operates as the dominant mechanism in the alloy. The dislocation loops 
formed from interactions between dislocations and p recipitates could effectively 
prevent the dislocation gliding at modest temperature. 
Keywords: Micropillar compression; Ultrafine-grained microstr ucture; Laves phases; 
High-temperature deformation; Spark plasma sintering

1. Introduction 
As an important class of high-temperature alloys, C o-Cr based alloys show 
excellent hot corrosion, wear and oxidation resistance. However, the high temperature 
strengths of conventional Co-Cr alloys are commonly inferior due to lack of effective 
precipitation strengthening by intermetallic compou nds compared with the Ni-base 
superalloys [1, 2]. In particular, upon the grain s ize down to submicron or nanometer 
scale, a large volume fraction of grain boundaries (GBs) can serve as barriers to 
prevent dislocation motion and consequently offer h igh strength at room temperature 
(RT), but grain coarsening at elevated temperatures will diminish the material strength. 
Hence, developing ultrafine-grained (UFG) or nanost ructured Co-Cr alloys that retain 
their high strength over a wide temperature range i s highly desirable for both 
scientific interests and demanding applications [3] . Previous studies have 
demonstrated that precipitation of Laves phases can enhance high-temperature 
strength in many alloy systems [4-6]. In determinin g the phase diagram of the 
Co-Cr-Ta ternary system, a binary phase region of L aves phase ((Co, Cr) 2Ta) and a 
hcp ε-Co matrix was found thermally stable at 800-900˚C [7]. Therefore, introducing 
Laves phases into Co-Cr alloy matrix could be a pro mising strategy to strengthen the 
alloy at elevated temperatures. 
In this context, we dedicatedly designed a Co-28Cr- 3Ta (at.%) alloy system with 
nanoscale precipitates of Laves phases uniformly di spersed in the UFG hcp ε-Co 
matrix. Then, we investigated the effect of Laves p hases on the strength of UFG 
Co-Cr alloys at modest temperature by micropillar c ompression technique. With this

2. Experimental procedure 
The bulk UFG Co-28Cr-3Ta alloy was fabricated by a combination of 
mechanical alloying and spark plasma sintering (SPS ). Commercially pure Co (Alfa 
Aesar, 1.6 µm, 99.8%), Cr (Alfa Aesar, ＜10 µm, 99.5%) and Ta (Alfa Aesar, 325 
mesh, 99.9%) powders were mixed with the nominal co mposition and subjected to 
high energy ball milling using a SPEX 8000D mixer i n an argon glove box for 12 h at 
RT (25˚C). The milled powders were subsequently packed into a graphite die with an 
inner diameter of 10 mm and spark plasma sintered ( SPS-211Lx, Fujidempa Kogyo. 
Co., ltd) at 900 ˚C under 40 MPa for 5 min in vacuu m. To illustrate the enhancement 
of the Laves phases, a Co-30Cr alloy of similar gra in size but in the absence of Laves 
phases was also fabricated as control. The phases o f the sintered alloys were analyzed 
by X-ray diffraction (XRD, Smartlab, Rigaku) and th e microstructures were analyzed 
by high angle annular dark field scanning transmiss ion electron microscopy 
(HAADF-STEM), bright field TEM (BF-TEM) and high re solution TEM (HRTEM) 
(Tecnai F30, FEI, USA). Grain sizes of matrix and p recipitates were statistically 
determined from BF-TEM and HAADF-STEM images, respe ctively. V olume

and calculating the area fraction. These images wer e analyzed using the ImageJ

The mechanical property at small length scale of th e alloys were measured using

hydrogen atmosphere. At least five independent micr opillars per alloy was tested at 
per testing temperature. The average yield strength s with standard deviations were 
provided. The micropillars before and after compres sion were imaged using 
field-emission scanning electron microscopy (SEM) mode of the FIB system.

3.2 Mechanical behavior of the micropillars at elevated temperatures 
To investigate the strengthening effect of the Laves phase at modest temperature, 
we then fabricated micropillars from the two spark- plasma-sintered bulk alloys and 
performed uniaxial compression tests at RT, 100 ˚C and 300 ˚C. The representative 
compressive engineering stress-strain curves of the two alloys at the three 
temperatures are presented in Fig. 4a-b, respective ly. Due to the constraint of the 
maximum load of the triboindenter, only the Co-30Cr pillars tested at 300 ˚C reached 
the fracture strength but all pillars yielded after compression. Thus, herein, we mainly 
focused on the yield strength. With the increasing temperature, the Co-28Cr-3Ta 
pillars have only slight drop in yield strength, fr om 2.99 (
± 0.01) GPa at RT, to 2.97 
(± 0.13) GPa at 100 ˚C and 2.56 ( ± 0.19) GPa at 300 ˚C. In contrast, the yield 
strength of Co-30Cr pillars dramatically decreased from 2.81 ( ±0.26) GPa at RT to 
2.14 ( ± 0.13) GPa at 100 ˚C and 1.22 ( ± 0.01) GPa at 300 ˚C (Fig. 4c). The 
percentage of strength reduction at elevated temper ature indicates the strength 
softening behavior of a material due to temperature increase [3]. At RT, the two alloys 
have similar yield strengths. However, at 300 ˚C, t he Co-30Cr shows a dramatic 
reduction in yield strength up to ~ 57%; while the strength reduction of Co-28Cr-3Ta 
is only ~ 17%. Even at 100 ˚C, the Co-30Cr alloy sh ows a 24% reduction in yield 
strength, while the Co-28Cr-3Ta alloy has almost no change in yield strength.

4. Discussion 
The primary findings of the present work are two-fo lds: (1) the addition of a 
small amount of Ta in the conventional Co-Cr alloy fabricated using a combination of 
mechanical alloying and SPS could induce the formation of nanoscale Laves phase; (2) 
the introduction of nanoscale Laves phase can effec tively suppress of strength 
reduction of UFG Co-Cr alloys at elevated temperatu res. We thus discuss the 
formation of the particular microstructure and the strengthening mechanisms of the 
fabricated Co-Cr-Ta alloys. 
 
4.1 Formation of the microstructure 
In equilibrium phase diagram of Co-Cr-Ta, the Co-28 Cr-3Ta alloy is composed 
of ε-Co and Co-rich (Co, Cr) 2Ta intermetallic compound, and the Co-30Cr alloy is a 
single ε-Co phase [7]. However, here, σ phase is present in the as-sintered Co-30Cr 
alloy, consistent with our previous observation [9] , and the Cr partitioned in both 
matrix and Laves phase in the form of metastable Cr -rich (Co, Cr) 2Ta in the 
Co-28Cr-3Ta alloy. This should be attributed to the high-energy ball milling used in 
this work, which is a typical non-equilibrium proce ss. The present results clearly 
show that the addition of 3 at.% Ta could effective ly suppress the formation of the 
metastable σ phase and lead to the precipitation of the Laves phases. 
In order to obtain a similar grain size of the ε-phase matrix, the Co-28Cr-3Ta 
alloy was sintered at 900 ˚C, which is higher than that of the Co-30Cr alloy at 800 ˚C. 
Initially, we also sintered the Co-28Cr-3Ta at 800 ˚C, but we found that the grain size

was only of ~ 87 nm. This indicates that, compared with the σ-phase, the Laves 
phases could suppress the grain coarsening more eff ectively. The nanoscale 
precipitates in both alloys offer pinning effect to suppress the grain growth. However, 
apart from the pinning effect from Cr 2Ta, the Co-28Cr-3Ta alloy shows a dual phase 
microstructure of matrix phase and Co 2Ta phase, which also helps to suppress the 
grain growth [10]. Thus, the combination of pinning effect and dual phase 
microstructure makes the suppression of grain growt h in the Co-28Cr-3Ta alloy more 
evident.

At modest temperature, cross-slip and climb of disl ocations become easy and the 
GBs become weak, leading to softening of the alloys . In addition, grain rotation 
serves as an important mechanism for deformation at elevated temperatures. The 
micropillar compression results indicate that the C o-30Cr shows a ~ 57% reduction in 
yield strength. It has been reported that successiv e cutting of precipitates by 
dislocations reduces the barrier strength to about half its original value [23, 24]. Thus, 
it is reasonable to hypothesize that the precipitat e strengthening mechanism changes 
from Orowan dislocation bypass to dislocation shear ing in the Co-30Cr alloy. Since 
the σ-phase is coherent or semi-coherent with the slip p lane of (10 11) and (0002) of 
the matrix phase, dislocations could cut through σ-precipitates upon deformation 
because of the alignment between slip planes in mat rix and precipitates. Subsequently, 
dislocations may move continuously across the grain /phase boundaries (Fig. 6d). 
Compared with the Co-28Cr-3Ta pillar with multiple slip planes, the Co-30Cr pillar 
compressed at 300 ˚C shows a dominant slip plane fo rming that traverses the entire

pillar and leads to simple shear. (Fig. 5f). Howeve r, in the Co-28Cr-3Ta alloy, Orowan 
mechanism still operates as the dominant mechanism and dislocation loops formed, 
due to the presence of the incoherent Laves precipi tates. With increasing strain of the 
pillar, denser dislocation networks forms. The newl y formed dislocation networks 
could effectively prevent the dislocation gliding a nd thus strengthen the alloy. In 
addition, motion of dislocation could be either pre vented by the phase boundaries of 
the Co 2Ta and matrix (Fig. 6c) due to complex crystal stru cture and relatively coarser 
grain size of the Co 2Ta precipitates. That is why the Co-28Cr-3Ta pillar s show only a 
slight reduction in yield strength compared with th at at RT, and has more uniform 
deformation. 
Finally, it is worth mentioning that the present mi cropillar compression tests 
were performed only in the temperature range of RT to 300 ˚C. Despite that it has 
clearly demonstrated the significant role of Laves phases in suppressing the softening 
of UFG Co-Cr alloy at elevated temperature, more me chanical tests at much higher 
temperature should be performed in future for high- temperature applications. In 
addition, future work will also be directed to in s itu TEM nanomechanical testings at 
elevated temperature which may provide direct evide nce of Orowan looping and 
particle shear. 
 
5. Conclusion 
In summary, we fabricated an UFG Co-28Cr-3Ta alloy by a combination of 
mechanical alloying and SPS. Microstructure and mic roscale mechanical property at

## Page 19

17 
 
modest temperature were investigated. We find that addition of 3 at.% Ta prevents the 
formation of σ-phase but induces the precipitation of two types o f Laves phases of 14 
nm and 86 nm, respectively. Such nanoscale Laves ph ase precipitates could 
effectively maintain the strength of Co-Cr alloys a t elevated temperatures through 
preventing the dislocation gliding. Our processing approach should enable the 
development of microstructurally stable Co-Cr alloy s with high strength for elevated 
temperature applications.
