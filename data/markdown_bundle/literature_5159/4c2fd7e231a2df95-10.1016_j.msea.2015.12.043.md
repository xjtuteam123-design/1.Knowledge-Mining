abstract
The microstructure and elevated temperature tensile property of Fe-doped NiAl –Cr(Mo)–(Hf,Dy) eutectic
alloy before and after heat treatment were investigated by SEM, TEM and elevated temperature tensile
test. The result shows that the as-cast alloy has higher elevated temperature strength. The heat treatment
leads to the coarsening and local dissolution of lamellar structure, and thus results in the weak decrease
of elevated temperature strength and the increase of the ductility. Moreover, the observation of side
surface reveals that the micropores in the heat-treated alloys, hindering the facture of tensile specimen,
are less than those in the as-cast alloy. Meanwhile, more dimples and lamellar neckings are observed in
the fracture surface of heat-treated alloys, even the dislocation substructure occurs in Cr(Mo) phase. This
demonstrates that the heat treatment results in the decrease of elevated temperature strength and the
increase of ductility.

1. Introduction
Intermetallics have been taken as the next generation of high
temperature structural materials due to its excellent properties,
such as NiAl, TiAl, Ni 3Al and FeAl. Among these intermetallics, NiAl
and NiAl-based eutectic alloy possess a high melting point, good
thermal conductivity and excellent oxidation resistance [1–3].
Especially, NiAl –Cr(Mo) eutectic alloy possesses a better compre-
hensive property, and thus may be a potential candidate of turbine
blade of aeroengine. In order to adapt to the application of ad-
vanced aeroengine, it is necessary to further improve the elevated
temperature strength. Previous investigation shows that the ad-
dition of Hf can effectively enhance the elevated temperature
strength of NiAl –Cr(Mo) eutectic alloy [4,5]. Unfortunately, the
fracture toughness decreases obviously because of the existence of
Heusler phase (Ni
2AlHf) or Hf solid solution at the NiAl/Cr(Mo)
interface ( ±6. 24 0. 3 MPa m [6]). Some investigations show that
the decrease of Hf amount or the addition of rare earth element Dy
and Ho can improve the fracture toughness to a certain extent by
reducing Hf-containing phase or the re ﬁning and purifying effect
[7–11 ]of rare earth element, and the fracture toughness ascends
to about
10. 0MPa m [12,13].
Previous investigations reported that the addition of Fe can
improve the ductility [14,15], and thus may enhance the fracture
toughness. Our recent investigation [16] has demonstrated that
the addition of Fe effectively improves the fracture toughness, and
the heat treatment enhances the fracture toughness further. The
toughness is almost close to that of NiAl –Cr(Mo) eutectic alloy
(
21. 6MPa m [17])). Meanwhile, the potential use of the alloy will
be in the environment of high temperature, thus making clear the
change of elevated temperature tensile strength of the alloy before
or after the heat treatment is important to the future application.
Previous investigations only reported the effect of heat treatment
on the microhardness and compressive property of NiAl –Cr(Mo)-
based eutectic alloy [18,19], and the heat treatment leads to the
decrease of microhardness and compressive strength. Moreover,
previous investigation also reported the effect of hot isostatic
pressed (HIP) processing on the compressive and tensile proper-
ties of NiAl –Cr(Mo)-based eutectic alloy, and HIP improves the
compressive and tensile strength [4]. However, the effect of heat
treatment on elevated temperature tensile property of the alloy is
rarely investigated. Thus, the paper investigates the effect of heat
treatment on the microstructure and elevated temperature tensile
property of Fe-doped NiAl –Cr(Mo)–(Hf,Dy) eutectic alloy.

2. Experimental details
The master alloy of Ni –30.95Al–28Cr–6Mo–0.05Hf (at%) –0.1Dy
(wt%)–4Fe (at%) alloy (NiAl –Cr(Mo)–(Hf,Dy)–4Fe for short) was
prepared by vacuum induction melting. The Ø 7 mm rods were cut
from the homogenized cast ingot by electro-discharge machining
(EDM). The rod was put in a high purity alumna crucible which
was 7.1 mm in inner diameter and 1 15 mm in length for directional
solidiﬁcation in Bridgeman apparatus. The melt temperature was
16607 10 °C, and the temperature gradient at the solid –liquid
interface was about 200 K/cm. The temperature in the specimen
was measured with 0.5 mm diameter insulated W-Re thermo-
couples inserted into the dual-hole alumina ﬁxed within the
sample. Two W-Re thermocouples were connected to measure-
ment unit which consists of data-logger and computer. The tem-
perature gradient ( G¼ΔT/ΔX) in the liquid phase was determined
by the values of ΔT and ΔX. The specimens were pulled down at
the withdrawal rate of 60 μm/s. The pulling length of directional
solidiﬁcation specimen was 80 mm. Some samples were heat-
treated at 1250 °C/48 h/furnace cooling (HT-1) and at 1250 °C/
48 h/water quenching and then aged at 1050 °C/24 h/furnace
cooling in evacuated quartz tube (HT-2).
The corrodent of mild etch is a solution of 80% HCl þ20% HNO3
by volume, and the corrodent of deep etch is a solution of 100 ml
H2O, 20 ml HCl and 5 g of CrO 3 to remove NiAl matrix. The mi-
crostructures were observed by scanning electron microscopy
(SEM) and transmission electron microscope (TEM). Tensile spe-
cimens having a gauge section of 3 /C2 3 /C2 17 mm3 were cut from
the DS alloy along the growth direction by EDM, and all the sur-
faces of specimens were ground with 2000-grit SiC abrasive.
Tensile tests were performed at 1253 K and 1323 K and at a strain
rate of 1 /C2 10/C0 4 s/C0 1 on an ultra-temperature tensile testing
machine.
3. Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructure
Fig. 1 shows the transverse microstructures of as-cast, HT-1 and
HT-2 alloys, and the microstructure is composed of eutectic cells.
Each cell consists of alternating NiAl (black) and Cr(Mo) lamellas
(gray white), which exhibits a radial emanating pattern from the
cell interior outward to the cell boundary, as shown in Fig. 1 (a).
After the heat treatment of 1250 °C/48 h (HT-1), the coarsening of
lamellar structure occurs, and Cr(Mo) lamellas are pinched off at
random, as displayed in Fig. 1(b). After the further aging (HT-2), no
signiﬁcant change occurs in the lamellar structure, that is, the
microstructure is similar to that of HT-1 alloy, as shown in Fig. 1(c).
In order to make clear the change of lamellar morphology, we have
also done the deep etch of the microstructures of as-cast, HT-1 and
HT-2 alloys. For the as-cast alloy, the growth defects are obviously
observed, such as the termination and mismatch, as displayed in
Fig. 1(d). It can be seen from Fig. 1(b and c) that the termination
and mismatch decrease obviously after heat treatment, which in-
dicates that the instability of lamellar structure preferentially oc-
curs at the growth defects. For the HT-1 and HT-2 alloys, the deep
etch demonstrates that Cr(Mo) lamellas are not completely pin-
ched off, and only the local dissolution occurs in Cr(Mo) lamellas,
as illustrated in Fig. 1(e and f).
In our recent investigation, we have discussed that the high
temperature instability of lamellar structure may be attributed to
the addition of 4 at% Fe, since Fe dissolves into NiAl and Cr(Mo)
phases and thus ascends the diffusion coef ﬁcient by reducing the
melting point of two phases [16,20–22]. Previous bulk reports
showed that NiAl and Cr(Mo) phases maintain stable at the range
of 1027 –130 0°C for NiAl –Cr(Mo) eutectic system alloy with the
larger interlamellar spacing ( 4 1 μm) [5,17,23,24]. Only several
investigations reported that the coarsening of NiAl and Cr(Mo)
phases occurs in NiAl –Cr(Mo) eutectic system alloy with the ex-
tremely ﬁne lamellar spacing [19,25]. It is deduced that the la-
mellar spacing should have some potential effect on the high
temperature stability of lamellar structure. Therefore, both the ﬁne
interlamellar spacing ( o1 μm) and the addition of Fe may result
in the instability of NiAl and Cr(Mo) phases in the present in-
vestigation. In the future, it may be a good investigation to make
clear the speci ﬁc effect of interlamellar spacing or Fe on the sta-
bility of NiAl and Cr(Mo) phases by consecutively changing the
interlamellar spacing using directional solidi ﬁcation or by chan-
ging the additional amount of Fe, respectively.
Moreover, the distribution of white precipitate is also observed
before and after the heat treatment, and the white phase is
identiﬁed as Hf solid solution (Hf
ss) by SEM-EDS. The result is also
consistent with previous investigations that Hf ss phase usually
appears in NiAl –Cr(Mo)–xHf ( xr 0.2 at%) eutectic alloys
[6,8,12,13,16,26], and Heusler phase commonly appears in NiAl –Cr

3.2. Elevated temperature tensile property
3.2.1. Elevated temperature tensile property at 1253 K
The longitudinal tensile properties of NiAl –Cr(Mo)–(Hf,Dy)–4Fe
eutectic alloys before and after the heat treatment tested at 1253 K
are given in Table 1. For the comparison, the elevated temperature
tensile properties of NiAl –Cr(Mo)-0.2Hf/0.5Hf [4,5,26] eutectic al-
loys are given in Table 1 . The result shows that the elevated
temperature strength of as-cast NiAl-Cr(Mo) –(Hf,Dy)–4Fe eutectic
alloy (308 MPa) is relatively higher than that of NiAl –Cr(Mo)-0.5Hf
eutectic alloy (297 MPa) [4,5]. After the heat treatment of HT-1, the
elevated temperature strength decreases weakly. After the heat
treatment of HT-2, the elevated temperature strength decreases
weakly further, but it is still higher than that of NiAl /C0 Cr(Mo)–
0.2Hf eutectic alloy. Inversely, the heat treatment leads to the in-
crease of ductility.
In order to better understand the fracture behavior, the side
surfaces near the fracture surface are observed, as shown in Fig. 3.
Fig. 3 (a–c) show the relatively macroscopical side surfaces of as-
cast, HT-1 and HT-2 alloys, respectively, and an indication of de-
formation by slip is seen in the longitudinally sectioned tensile
specimens. Generally, the slip bands lie in the angle of 45 deg to
the tensile axis. Fig. 3 (d–f) show the more microscopic side sur-
faces of as-cast, HT-1 and HT-2 alloys, respectively. For the as-cast
alloy, many micropores appear at NiAl/Cr(Mo) phase interface and
tend to form in NiAl phase, as displayed in Fig. 3 (d). Previous in-
vestigations reported that the strength of NiAl phase is lower than
that of Cr(Mo) phase [16,23]. Therefore, a larger deformation oc-
curs in NiAl phase, and inversely a smaller deformation occurs in
Cr(Mo) phase. This discordance of deformation of NiAl and Cr(Mo)
phase leads to the formation of micropore, and thus results in the
occurrence of interface debonding. The interface debonding is an
effective strengthening mechanism [24]. Namely, the formation of
micropore brings some resistances to the fracture of tensile spe-
cimen. Compared to the as-cast alloy, the micropores at NiAl/Cr
(Mo) phase interface decrease obviously in the HT-1 and HT-2
alloys, as shown in Fig. 3 (e and f). Consequently, the elevated
temperature strength of as-cast alloy is higher than that of the HT-
1 and HT-2 alloys.
Meanwhile, we also observe the macroscopic fracture surfaces
of as-cast, HT-1 and HT-2 alloys. The large majority in the fracture
surface seems to be lack of the dimples, as shown in Fig. 4 (a–c).
The relatively obvious plastic deformation only occurs in the local
zone (round in Fig. 4 (a–c)). Moreover, the magni ﬁed images of
local zones of as-cast, HT-1 and HT-2 alloys are also shown in Fig. 4
(d–f), respectively. For the as-cast alloy, blocky lamellas are ob-
served in the fracture surfaces, as displayed in Fig. 4(d). Obviously,
the size of blocky lamella is larger than the thickness of lamella
shown in Fig. 1(a and d). Thus, it is not dif ﬁcult to imagine that the
interface debonding of NiAl and Cr(Mo) lamellas occurs during the
tensile process, and the occurence of interface debonding is rela-
tively common. Meanwhile, some micropores and lamellar neck-
ings are observed, as illustrated in Fig. 4(d). For the HT-1 and HT-2
alloys, besides the micropores and lamellar neckings, the dimples
are observed, as displayed in Fig. 4(e and f). Especially, the lamellar
neckings and the dimples are more common in the HT-2 alloy,
which indicates that the HT-2 alloy has a better ductility. In a
word, the microscopic observation of fracture surface indicates
that the ductility of heat-treated alloy is better that that of as-cast
alloy.
3.2.2. Elevated temperature tensile property at 1323 K
Besides 1253 K, we also tested the longitudinal tensile prop-
erties of NiAl –Cr(Mo)–(Hf,Dy)–4Fe eutectic alloys before and after

elevated temperature strength at 1323 K is lower than that at

3.4. Weakening mechanism caused by the heat treatment
The heat treatment leads to the weak decrease of elevated
temperature strength and the increase of ductility. The decrease of
elevated temperature strength should be attributed to the coar-
sening of lamellar structure and the local dissolution of re-
inforcement Cr(Mo) phase shown in Fig. 1 . Inversely, the pre-
cipitation of ﬁne Hf ss phase at NiAl/Cr(Mo) interface may improve
the elevated temperature strength, which offsets the part decrease
of elevated temperature strength. Therefore, the elevated tem-
perature strength decreases weakly after the heat treatment.
Moreover, some extrinsic factors also demonstrate the weak de-
crease of strength, like the change of the quantity of micropores
and dimples. From the observation of side surface (See Fig. 3 ), the
micropores, providing some resistances to the fracture of tensile
specimen, decreases gradually after the heat treatment, and thus
leading to the decrease of elevated temperature strength. In ad-
dition, the observation of fracture surface (See Figs. 4 and 6) in-
dicates that the heat treatment results in the increase of ductility
due to the increase of dimples.
Furthermore, we have also done a more microscopic observa-
tion of a region just below the fracture surface of HT-2 alloy in the
elevated temperature tensile test at 1323 K by TEM. The result
shows that the dislocation substructure occurs in NiAl phase, as
shown in Fig. 8(a). This indicates that a plastic deformation occurs
in NiAl phase. In addition, the dislocation substructure also occurs
in Cr(Mo) phase, as shown Fig. 8 (b). That is, a plastic deformation
also occurs in Cr(Mo) phase. In previous investigation [16,23],i ti s
not common that a plastic deformation occurs in NiAl and Cr(Mo)
phases simultaneously, since the yield strength of NiAl phase is
lower than that of Cr(Mo) phase. In the present study, the dis-
location substructure existing in NiAl and Cr(Mo) phases not only
indicates that the yield strength of Cr(Mo) phase decreases to
some extent after the heat treatment, but also demonstrates that
the HT-2 alloy has a better ductility.
4. Conclusion

of NiAl –Cr(Mo)–(Hf,Dy)–4Fe eutectic alloy before and after the
heat treatment have been investigated. From the investigations,
the following important conclusions can be drawn:

(1) The thermal stability of lamellar structure of NiAl –Cr(Mo)–(Hf,

structure and the redistribution of Hf ss phase.
(2) Compared to the traditionally high-strength NiAl –Cr(Mo–
0.5Hf eutectic alloy, the as-cast NiAl –Cr(Mo)–(Hf,Dy)–4Fe eu-

The heat treatment leads to the decrease of elevated tem-
perature strength, which is attributed to the coarsening and
local dissolution of lamellar structure.
(3) The heat treatment leads to the increase of ductility. The ob-
servation of fracture surface indicates that the heat-treated
alloy possesses a good ductility because of the occurrence of
more dimples and lamellar neckings, and the formation of
dislocation substructure in NiAl and Cr(Mo) phases also de-
monstrates the conclusion.
