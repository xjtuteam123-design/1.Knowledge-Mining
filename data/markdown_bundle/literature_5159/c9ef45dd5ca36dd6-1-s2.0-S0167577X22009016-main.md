1. Introduction 
Over the last decades, high-entropy alloys (HEAs) have received 
much attention due to their attractive mechanical and functional char -
acteristics, especially the novel design concept with vast composition 
space [1] . In this new family of multi-principal component alloys 
(MPCAs), refractory HEAs (RHEAs) composed of high melting point 
elements (Ti, V, Cr, Zr, Nb, Mo, Hf, Ta, W) are of considerable interest 
[2 – 5] . Senkov et al. proposed and fabricated a series of RHEAs with a 
single BCC phase that possesses the yield strength of more than 500 MPa 
at 1600 
◦
C, which are superior to many advanced Ni-based superalloys 
[6] . However, most of the reported RHEAs exhibit limited tensile 
ductility and relatively high densities ( > 8.5 g/cm
3
), which significantly 
restricts their widespread practical applications. Great works have been 
devoted to improving the ductility of RHEAs. The introducing of phase 
transformation and spinodal modulations are all commonly used 
methods to induce ductility in RHEAs [7,8] . Nevertheless, the vast 
majority of studies on the mechanical properties of RHEAs have focused 
on quasi-static loading conditions. In order to make a more compre -
hensive assessment of their potential for engineering application, the 
completion of examining the damage toughness under relatively high 
strain rates is also very important. 
In this work, we developed a ductile Ti
3
Zr
1.5
NbVAl
0.25 
RHEA uti -
lizing V, Nb, Zr and Ti with intrinsic ductility and relatively low density. 
In addition, a small amount of Al was added to provide solid solution 
strengthening. After hot forging and subsequent annealing, the pre -
sented RHEA shows excellent room temperature ductility, and the 
combination of high tensile strength and Charpy impact toughness is 
presented for the first time in RHEA. The deformation and fracture 
mechanism for the superior mechanical properties of this RHEA are also 
discussed.

With the aim of deciphering plastic mechanisms of the 
Ti
3
Zr
1.5
NbVAl
0.25 
alloy, detailed TEM observations have been made to 
monitor the dislocation behavior at different strains. Fig. 4a shows the 
TEM image after 1.5% strain. Coplanar slip of dislocations dominates 
plastic deformation in this early stage with occasional cross-slipped 
dislocations. At middle strain (10%), the massive straight planar slip 
bands (PSBs) were observed (see Fig. 4b), and around them, the dislo -
cations are arranged in loops and bundles, as shown in Fig. 4c, indicating 
the frequent dislocation cross-slip, which often prevails in BCC metals 
due to the close-packed {1 1 0} and {1 1 2} slip planes [20]. With further 
straining to fracture, wavy slip dominates the plastic flow, and the 
abundant cross-slip results in the formation of high dense dislocation 
walls (HDDWs), as shown in Fig. 4d, which effectively contribute to the 
durability of plastic deformation. Additionally, deformation twins have 
not been detected after tension to a high strain, analogues to previously 
reported RHEAs [2,4]. Based on the above results, the plastic deforma -
tion in current RHEA appears to be accommodated mainly by disloca -
tion activity, and the multiple dislocation interactions via dislocation 
multiplication are considered to be the dominant mechanism for the 
large plasticity of this RHEA. 
4. Conclusions 
In this work, a forged Ti
3
Zr
1.5
NbVAl
0.25 
RHEA with a single BCC 
phase has been prepared. The hot forging followed by annealing at 
elevated temperature leads to a homogeneous microstructure with 
coarse equiaxed grain. Owing to the solution strengthening effect and 
intense dislocation activity, the obtained alloy exhibits an excellent 
combination of high yield strength (899 MPa), large plasticity (24.50%), 
and high impact toughness (119.5 J/cm
2
), which is far superior to most 
traditional alloys and HEAs.
