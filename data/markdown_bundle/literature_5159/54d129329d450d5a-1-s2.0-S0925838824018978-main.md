1. Introduction 
Refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) composed of refractory el -
ements such as Re, W, Ta, Hf, Mo, Nb, V, Zr, Cr and Ti have great 
application potential under ultra-high temperature service conditions 
because of its excellent high-temperature thermal stability, remarkable 
high-temperature strength and prominent high-temperature wear 
resistance. Therefore, the research and development of RHEAs has 
attracted extensive attention [1 – 4] . By optimizing the preparation pro -
cess and reasonable composition design, RHEAs with high strength, high 
hardness, wear resistance and corrosion resistance can be prepared 
[5 – 8] . However, it is not easy to prepare RHEAs because of the high 
melting point of each component element of refractory high entropy 
alloy and the great difference between each component element. 
The most common preparation method of RHEAs is arc smelting. 
Pandey et al. [9] prepared TiVZrMoW RHEA by melting method and 
studied the phase evolution and stability of the alloy. The cast TiVZr -
MoW RHEA showed the presence of two BCC phases, that is, major BCC1 
(Mo and W rich), and minor BCC2 (Ti and Zr rich) along with a minor 
fraction of ordered B2 structures and C15 Laves phase Zr(Mo, W)

However, in the process of arc melting, the temperature is generally 
high, the elements with higher melting point diffuse slowly and are 
difficult to melt, and the elements with lower melting point are easy to 
volatilize. Therefore, RHEAs prepared by arc melting generally have 
defects such as element segregation, porosity and residual stress [3, 
12 – 15] . Different from arc melting method, powder metallurgy method 
can realize low-temperature sintering, effectively inhibit composition 
segregation and prevent dendrite formation, and finally obtain RHEAs 
with uniform phase structure [16 – 18] . In addition, powder metallurgy 
has obvious advantages in manufacturing irregular components, which 
can significantly improve the utilization ratio of materials and reduce 
costs. Therefore, powder metallurgy technology has great potential and 
application prospects in the preparation of RHEAs. 
Up to now, scholars have done a lot of research work on the prepa -
ration of RHEAs by powder metallurgy. Kang et al. [19] prepared 
WNbMoTaV RHEAs with a simple BCC structure by MA and SPS, and

RHEAs not only have a series of excellent comprehensive properties, 
but also inevitably inherits the inherent defects of refractory alloys, such 
as low plasticity, high brittleness and high density at room temperature. 
Therefore, the research goal of this project is to design and develop a 
new type of alloy with low density, high strength, and good room 
temperature plasticity. In this study, the non-equiatomic composition of 
Mo-Nb-Ta-V-Cr was explored, reducing the content of expensive Ta el -

2. Experimental procedures 
The nominal composition of MoNbVTa
0.5
Cr RHEA (at%) was pre -
pared by blending 99.9 % pure elemental Mo (1 – 3 μ m), Nb (10 – 20 μ m), 
Ta (10 – 20 μ m), V (10 – 15 μ m), and Cr (1 – 3 μ m). The mixed powder and 
cemented carbide ball (WC) were placed in a stainless-steel bottle at a 
mass ratio of 6:1, and high-energy ball milling was performed using a 
planetary ball mill (QM-3SP2, China). The ball milling speed was set to 
350 rpm, and Ar gas was used as a protective gas during the ball milling. 
After high-energy ball milling for 60 h, the mixed powder was sintered 
by spark plasma sintering system (SPS-20 T-10, China). The sintering 
temperatures for SPS were 1500 ℃ , 1600 ℃ , and 1700 ℃ , respectively. 
The powders were heated to the desired sintering temperature at a 
heating rate of 100

of the samples sintered at 1500 ℃ is obviously higher than that of the 
samples sintered at 1600 ℃ and 1700 ℃ . This is because the grain size of 
the 1500 ℃ sintered sample alloy is the smallest, and the small grains 
size increases the resistance that dislocation movement needs to over -
come, making it difficult to accumulate dislocation. In addition, the 
interaction between refined grains is stronger, which can coordinate 
deformation better and avoid premature fracture caused by excessive 
local stress [19,23] . Moreover, there are many exfoliated small particles 
on the fracture surface, which are determined to be oxide precipitates by 
surface scanning analysis ( Fig. 12 c). It is generally believed that oxide 
particles dispersed within coarse grains are beneficial for crack propa -
gation, leading to a transition from intergranular fracture to trans 
granular fracture. In a word, the size of the sintered sample alloy and the 
content, size and distribution of precipitates have a significant influence 
on the fracture mode of the alloy under compression.
