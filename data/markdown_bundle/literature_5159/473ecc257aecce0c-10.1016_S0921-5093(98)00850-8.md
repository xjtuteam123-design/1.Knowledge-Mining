Comparison on the microstructure and mechanical strength of a
Ni-based alloy with and without 3wt.% CeF 3 addition

Abstract
The need for high temperature self-lubricating composites with excellent tribological properties as well as mechanical strengths
leads to the survey for new types of high temperature solid lubricant material. The addition of CeF 3 to a Ni-based alloy showed
promise for providing good mechanical strength as well as tribological properties. In the paper, based on the authors’ previous
work, comparison on the microstructure of a Ni-based alloy with and without 3 wt.% (4.3 vol.%) CeF
3 addition was made.
Finally, the correlation between the microstructure and mechanical strength is discussed. © 1998 Elsevier Science S.A. All rights
reserved.
Keywords: Microstructure; Mechanical strength; CeF 3; Ni-based alloy
1. Introduction
There is an ongoing need for developing high temper-
ature self-lubricating materials with excellent tribologi-
cal properties plus high mechanical strengths [1,2]. The
currently used high temperature self-lubricating com-
posites can be classiﬁed into two groups according to
the lubricating components. The ﬁrst group contains
solid lubricants, such as CaF
2, PbO etc. [3–5]. Since
these solid lubricants are effective in reducing friction
and wear, good tribological properties can be obtained.
The latter contains active elements, such as sulfur [6].
Since sulfur can form metal sulﬁdes in the process or in
sliding, the tribological properties can be improved
accordingly. Unfortunately however, a reduction in the
mechanical strengths of the composites due to the
addition of these solid lubricants or active elements is
quite common [6,7]. As such, the balance between the
tribological properties and mechanical strengths of the
composites should be taken into consideration when
both speciﬁc tribological properties and mechanical
strength are required. It is not surprising to note that a
speciﬁc kind of solid lubricant or active element can
have a beneﬁcial effect on both tribological properties
and mechanical strength alike. In the authors’ labora-
tory, much attention is paid to the rare earth ﬂuorides.
Rare earth ﬂuorides, typically LaF
3 and CeF3, are good
high-temperature solid lubricants, based on the work of
NASA [8] and the authors [9–11]. The friction coefﬁ-
cient was 0.16 to 0.18 from 400 to 700°C. Preferential
orientation, degree of crystallization and oxidation are
the factors related to the high temperature lubricating
mechanism. In addition, there is a synergistic effect on
reducing friction at 700°C. In the authors’ previous
research, the effects of the addition of an amount of
CeF
3 on the tribological and mechanical properties of a
Ni-based alloy have been investigated [12]. Findings
indicated that by adding 3 wt.% (4.3 vol.%) CeF
3 not
only the tribological properties, but also the impact
toughness, at room temperature and compressive
strengths at high temperature of the Ni-based alloy can
be improved. At room temperature the friction coefﬁ-
cient and wear rates of the frictional couple were re-
duced, especially for the wear rates of their
counterparts (high speed steel), as low as half of the

2. Experimental details
2.1. Preparation of the alloy
The Ni-based–CeF 3 self-lubricating material was
comprised of Hastelloy C, a Ni–Cr based alloy and :or
3 wt.% (4.3 vol.%) CeF 3. The materials were prepared
using powder metallurgy methods in an intermediate
frequency (4000 Hz) induced heating furnace. The main
steps were powder blending, mechanically cold pressing
and hot pressing in sequence. The ﬁrst step was to
blend the components into the required composition.
The next step was to make a material with relatively
more than 80% of the theoretical density. The ﬁnal step
was to make the material of nearly full density. The
experimental details are described elsewhere [12]. The
sintering temperature, pressure and time of sintering
were 1130°C, 12 MPa and 15 min, respectively.
2.2. E6aluation method for the mechanical strengths
By using the weight–volume method, the density of
the material was measured. The hardness of the mate-
rial was tested on an A-200 type hardness tester made
in Italy. An impact toughness sample was prepared
without a notch with size of ¥451010 mm
3.A
constant-strain-rate-compression-technique was used to
investigate the compressive strength of the material.
The experimental details to conduct the mechanical
property test are described elsewhere [12].
2.3. Characterization of the microstructure
Samples for the evaluation of the microstructure
were prepared. The microstructures of the alloys were
characterized by electron probe microanalysis (EPMA).
3. Results and discussion
3.1. Mechanical strengths
As shown in Table 1, the density of the alloy de-
creased slightly with the addition of CeF 3, while the
porosity of the alloy declined considerably due to the
addition of CeF
3. It can be deduced that the alloy
compacted with the addition of CeF 3. As for hardness,
it is easy to see that the addition of relatively soft CeF 3
lead to a reduction in hardness.
The results listed in Table 2 show that by adding 3
wt.% (4.3 vol.%) CeF 3, the impact toughness of the
alloy was increased by 25.5%. It is suggested that the
alloy can be toughened with 3 wt.% (4.3 vol.%) CeF
3
addition. As for the compressive strength, an increment
can be seen at 700°C, whilst giving a very slight de-
crease at room temperature. The strengthening role of
adding CeF
3 to the alloy seems to take effect only at
high temperatures. Despite all of this, it is a very good
result since the composite is intended for use more
frequently at high temperatures.
All these results suggest that the addition of CeF
3 to
the alloy have a beneﬁcial effect on the mechanical
strengths.

3.2. Microstructure
Fig. 1(a) shows the microstructure of a Ni-based
alloy without CeF 3 addition. It is clear that three
regions can be observed. The continuous phase (gray
region) indicates the Ni–Cr matrix with Mo solution
according to Fig. 1(b–d). The bright region indicates
second phase Mo which separated out from the Ni–Cr
matrix (Fig. 1(d)). Since Mo has a high melting point,
the dispersed Mo phase may have some strengthening
effects upon the alloy. The dark area is porosity. As
shown in Fig. 1(a), the dark area is not well distributed.
As is known for powder metallurgy materials, porosity
has a negative or catastrophic effect on the mechanical
strength. As such, the existence of porosity may ad-
versely affect the mechanical strength of the alloy.

3.3. Mechanism
It is clear that by adding 3 wt.% (4.3 vol.%) CeF 3 the
mechanical strength of a Ni-based alloy can be pro-
moted as a result of the small and well distributed
porosity. Moreover, the distribution of Ni and Cr (the
matrix) of a Ni-based alloy with 3 wt.% (4.3 vol.%)
CeF
3 addition are more uniform compared to the alloy
without CeF3 (Fig. 1(b–c), Fig. 2(b–c)). This may have
made some contribution to the improved mechanical
strengths. Thirdly, the XRD results indicate that CeF
3
transformed to CeO2 after the process (Fig. 3). Fig. 3(a)
shows the XRD spectrum of CeF 3 powder as received.
The existence of a small amount of CeO 2 was due to
the preparation of the raw material. After heating at
800°C for 1 h, entire transformation of CeF 3 to CeO 2
was observed (Fig. 3(b)). The XRD spectrum of the
prepared material could not be used to determine the
existence of CeO
2 because it is difﬁcult to distinguish
the peak from the background noise. It can be inferred
that CeO
2 has some disperse strengthening effects. To
summarize, it is the former two aspects together that
lead to the improvement in the mechanical strength of
the Ni-based alloy.
