Abstract 
The Monte Carlo method was applied to the simulation of temporal evolutions of atomic arrangement in Ni-base superalloys. 
The ordering kinetics was controlled by direct exchange of a randomly selected single atom with one of its neighbouring atoms. 
The formation of 7' phase y'/~ equilibrium and substitutional behaviours of alloying elements were predicted by the model. The 
reductions of total energy normalized by initial energy by ordering are about 3% for superalloys CMSX-4 and about 2% for 
TMS63. The formation and coarsening rates of 7' phase in CMSX-4 are higher than those in TMS63. The size distribution profiles 
for CMSX-4 and TMS63 approach time-invariant profiles. The variations of calculated phase composition of }" and }, phases of 
CMSX-4 and TMS63 with time are not significant. The enrichrnent of A1, Ti and Ta in 7' phase and Co and y phase is observed. 
Cr is partitioning into both y' and 7 phases. This qualitatively agrees with the phase analysis of Ni-A1-Ti ternary alloys by cluster 
variation method. The variations of calculated concentration of alloying element in Ni and AI sublattices of 7' phase in CMSX-4 
and TMS63 with time are not significant. The preferential substitutions of Ti, Mo and Ta in A1 sublattice and Co in Ni sublattice 
are predicted. Cr is entering into both Ni and AI sublattices. The tendency of site occupation predicted by the simulation is 
consistent with the classification given by Enomoto and Harada. The Monte Carlo simulation results on site occupancies of 
alioying elements of CMSX-4 and TMS63 are in good agreement with those observed by atom probe FIM analysis and then 
calculated by the cluster variation method except for Ni concentration in the A1 sublattice. ,D t997 Elsevier Science S.A.

1. Introduction 
Ni-base single crystal superalloys with superior high 
temperature performance have been designed for tur- 
bine blades in aeroengines. There are strong demands 
for further improvement of high temperature properties 
of superalloys in order to enhance the efficiency of the 
engine. The superior properties of Ni-base superalloys 
at high temperature can be attributed to the fine disper- 
sion of coherent 7' (L12) in the ductile 7 (fcc) matrix. 
The evaluation of the effects of multiple alloying addi- 
tion on the characteristics of 7'/7 phase equilibrium by 
a reliable model is indispensable for the efficient alloy 
design of Ni-base superalloys. 
Harada et al. [1-3] have established the alloy design 
program based on multiple regression analysis to pre-

A thermodynamic model is preferred for avoiding a

was reported that compositions of 7' and ), phases, site 
occupation of alloying element in the 7' phase and 7'/)' 
lattice misfit are in good agreement with those obtained 
by the atom probe FIM analyses and high temperature 
X-ray diffraction. 
The next step of the alloy design of Ni-base superal- 
loys will be to predict temporal and spatial evolutions 
of the atomic arrangement in Ni base superalloys. This 
paper describes research into the kinetics of ordering of 
Ni-base superalloys by the Monte Carlo simulation 
with Lennard-Jones potential.

performed by the direct the exchange of a randomly 
selected single atom with one of its neighbouring atoms 
(Kawasaki dynamics [7]). The exchange trail is accepted 
with probability, W

The values of the gennard-Jones potential parame-

be significant. Table 2 shows the composition of the 
Ni-base superalloys studied. The simulations were per- 
formed on the fcc lattice of size of 323 (131 072 atoms). 
Periodic boundary condition was used throughout. 
Temperature of the system was set to 1323 K. 
With the use of the cluster analysis method, the

On the basis of the cluster variation analysis of 
Ni-AI-X ternary alloys, Enomoto and Harada [6] 
classified the substitutional behaviours of alloying ele- 
ments into three groups: Type I elements (Co and Cu) 
which preferentially substitute for Ni; Type II elements 
(Ti, Nb, Mo, Ta and W) which almost substitute for 
AI: and Type III elements (Cr, Mn and Fe) which enter 
both subtattices of T'. The tendency of site occupation 
predicted by the simulation is consistent with the clas- 
sification mentioned above. However, the site occu- 
pancy fraction of Co and Cr is not so remarkable as is 
expected by the cluster variation analysis of Ni-AI-X 
ternary alloys. The site competition between alloying 
elements may have occurred because of the limitation 
of the number of sublattice sites. 
Figs. 14 and 15 show comparison of Monte Carlo 
simulation results with those observed by atom probe 
FIM analysis and those calculated by the cluster varia- 
tion method [9]. The present Monte Carlo simulation 
result is in good agreement with those by atom probe 
FIM analysis and cluster variation method. However, 
some discrepancies between the Monte Carlo simula- 
tion and the cluster variation analysis are observed. The 
Ni concentration in the A1 sublattice is negligible in the 
cluster variation analysis. On the other hand the Monte 
Carlo simulation results shows the considerable occu- 
pancy fraction of Ni in the A1 site, which is coincident 
with atom probe FIM analysis. This may be attributed 
to the consideration of a higher order interaction in the 
Monte Carlo simulation than that in cluster variation. 
However, the detail will still remain unknown. The 
reliability of interatomic potential for higher order 
temps must be checked.
