ABSTRACT
Influences of addition of niobium carbide (NbC) in the fusion zone (FZ) on room-temperature and high- 
temperature mechanical properties of laser-welded joints of molybdenum (Mo) alloys were studied. Results 
show that after adding NbC powder, the average Vickers microhardness in the upper part of the FZ increases from 
183.0 HV to 712.3 HV; the room-temperature tensile strength grows from 33.9 MPa to 323.1 MPa, reaching 45.4 
% that of base metal (BM). In addition, the fracture mode of joints turns from intergranular fractures into 
transgranular fracture; at 1100 
◦
C, the high-temperature tensile strength of joints added with NbC is 141.6 MPa, 
which is 65.9 % that of BM. Energy dispersive spectrometer (EDS) and electron backscattered diffraction (EBSD) 
results show that after adding NbC powder, the average grain size in the FZ diminishes from 40.3 μ m to 32.9 μ m, 
where the number of low-angle grain boundaries (LAGBs) increases; the FZ not only contains NbC phase but also 
a large quantity of Nb
2
O
5 
and Mo
2
C phases dispersed on grain boundaries (GBs), and the number of MoO
2 
phase 
on GBs decreases apparently. Therefore, physical mechanisms underlying significant improvement of room- 
temperature and high-temperature tensile strengths of laser-welded joints of Mo alloys added with NbC in the 
FZ mainly include fine-grain strengthening, GB purification, and GB strengthening.
1. Introduction
Molybdenum (Mo) alloys, characterized by a high melting point, 
favorable high-temperature mechanical properties, small coefficient of 
linear expansion, and good thermal conductivity, are important candi -
date materials for producing ultra-high-temperature heat pipes of space 
reactors [ 1 – 4 ]. The outer shell of heat pipes made of Mo alloys is 
composed of a Mo pipe and end plugs at both ends. The Mo pipe and end 
plugs need to be connected by welding. Mo alloys with poor weld ability 
need to be welded using a method of low thermal input. Although the 
heating temperature of brazing is low, the high-temperature mechanical 
properties of brazed joints are generally low. As a solid-phase welding 
method, friction welding can avoid generation of pores and brittle 
phases in the fusion zone (FZ). However, heat pipes are thin-walled 
slender members that cannot bear the axial load in the friction weld -
ing process. Laser welding and vacuum electron beam welding (VEBW), 
featuring a high energy density, low heat input, and small heating zone, 
are suitable for welding of Mo alloys [ 5 – 11 ]. Compared with VEBW, 
laser welding does not need a vacuum chamber and has advantages 
including the low equipment cost and high processing efficiency 
[ 12 , 13 ].
Despite the excellent mechanical properties, Mo alloys face problems 
including cracks, pores, embrittlement, and softening after being molten 
and recrystallized, and the mechanical properties of welded joints are 
significantly deteriorated [ 14 ]. Zhang [ 15 , 16 ] investigated improve -
ment effects and mechanisms of preheating and post-welding laser 
remelting on defects and mechanical properties of joints. Cheng [ 17 ] 
significantly improved the room-temperature tensile strength of laser- 
welded joints of Mo alloys by adding titanium (Ti) in the FZ and 
analyzed the solid-solution strengthening and deoxygenation effects of 
Ti. However, because of the low melting point of Ti, strengthening 
schemes that add Ti in the FZ are not ideal solutions to joints used in 
environments hotter than 1000 
◦
C. In comparison, adding second-phase 
particles with a high melting point in the FZ is more conducive to 
improving high-temperature properties of Mo joints.

2.2. Methods
The laser welding system used in the research included an IPG YLS- 
8000 laser and an ABB robot with six degrees of freedom. The maximum 
output power, focal spot diameter, laser wavelength, and focal length of 
the laser were 8 kW, 200 μ m, 1068 nm, and 200 mm, respectively. 
Before welding, absolute ethyl alcohol was mixed with NbC powder and 
the mixture was stirred sufficiently to prepare NbC suspension, which 
was uniformly spread to a thickness of 0.3 mm on the surface of Mo 
sheets using a powder spreader. Self-fusion welding was performed after 
complete evaporation of absolute ethyl alcohol. The scheme in Fig. 2
was used in the welding experiments, thus obtaining joints without and 
with NbC, which are separately labeled as Joint-without and Joint-with.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. The results of Single-factor experiment and orthogonal experiment
The results of the single factor test are shown in Fig. 4 , and it is 
reasonable to choose the level of laser power p of 3200 W ~ 4000 W 
according to the penetration depth of the metallographic of the weld 
cross-section. Among them, the weld seam cross-sectional shape of (a3) 
is better, and single-factor experiments were conducted on the defo -
cusing amount f and welding speed v based on (a3). It was found that 
when the defocusing amount f was 2 mm, a depression appeared on the 
weld surface; when f was 2 mm, the cross-sectional surface was not 
penetrated, and the defocusing amount f level range was determined to 
be 2 mm ~ 2 mm. Similarly, the welding speed v level was determined 
to be 0.5 m/min ~ 1.5 m/min. Laser welding experiments were con -
ducted on Mo sheets following the three-factor three-level orthogonal 
experimental design scheme in Table 2 , thus obtaining experimental 
results in Fig. 5 . The cross section of weld in Fig. 5 (e) is well formed, 
while defects including through-going cracks, lack of penetration, or 
surface depression are observed in other welds. Welding parameters 
corresponding to the weld in Fig. 5 (e) were adopted in subsequent tests, 
including laser power p of 3600 W, defocusing amount f of 0 mm, and 
welding speed v of 1 m/min.

Figures 6 and 7 show cross-sectional microstructures of Joint-

4. Conclusions
The influences and influencing mechanisms of addition of NbC in the 
FZ on the room-temperature and high-temperature mechanical proper -
ties of laser-welded joints of Mo alloys were mainly studied. The 
following main conclusions were drawn:
1) The room-temperature tensile strengths of Joint-Without and Joint- 
With are 33.9 and 323.1 MPa, respectively. Adding NbC in the FZ 
improves the room-temperature tensile strength of laser-welded 
joints of Mo alloys by 289.2 MPa, and changes the fracture mode 
of joints from intergranular fracture into transgranular fracture. 
Moreover, the joint strength reaches 45.4 % that of BM.
