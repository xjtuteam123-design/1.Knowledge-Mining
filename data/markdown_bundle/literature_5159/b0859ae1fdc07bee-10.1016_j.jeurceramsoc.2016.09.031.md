Reliable brazing of carbon ﬁber reinforced SiC (Cf /SiC) composite to Nb-1Zr alloy was achieved by adopting
a
 novel Ti45Co45Nb10 (at.%) ﬁller alloy. The effects of brazing temperature (1270–1320 ◦ C) and holding
time
 (5–30 min) on the microstructure and mechanical properties of the joints were investigated. The
results
 show that a continuous reaction layer (Ti,Nb)C was formed at the Cf /SiC/braze interface. A TiCo
and
 Nb(s,s) eutectic structure was observed in the brazing seam, in which some CoNb4 Si phases were dis-
tributed.
 By increasing the brazing temperature or extending the holding time, the reaction layer became
thicker
 and the amount of the CoNb4 Si increased. The optimized average shear strength of 242 MPa was
obtained
 when the joints were brazed at 1280 ◦ C for 10 min. The high temperature shear strength of the
joints
 reached 202 MPa and 135 MPa at 800 ◦ C and 1000 ◦ C, respectively.

1. Introduction
Cf/SiC has been considered as a desirable high temperature
structural material in aviation turbine engine and other thermal
structural components due to its high strength, high modulus and
excellent oxidation resistance at a high temperature [1–3]. How-
ever, compared with common metallic structural materials, Cf/SiC
composite, with its brittleness and high strength, is difﬁcult to
be manufactured into large size or complex shape. Usually, Cf/SiC
composite needs to be joined to itself or high temperature alloys,
especially to Nb alloys that are widely used in aerospace ﬁeld [4,5].
Therefore, the joining of Cf/SiC composite and Nb alloy is critical to
widen their utilization in the thermal structural components ﬁeld.
Among various joining techniques, such as mechanical connec-
tion [6], diffusion bonding [7,8], transient liquid phase bonding
[9,10], spark plasma sintering [11] and brazing [12], active braz-
ing has been attracting more and more attention in the joining of
Cf/SiC due to its simplicity, good reproducibility as well as perfect
adaptability of joint size and shape. So far, Ag-Cu eutectic alloys
containing Ti have been widely used to join Cf/SiC composite to
metals [13–15]. However, the joining of Cf/SiC composite with
Ag-Cu-Ti based ﬁller alloy will obtain a relative low mechanical
property at high temperature. And it is difﬁcult to ﬁnd an effective

ﬁller alloy to join the Cf/SiC composite that maintains the strength

Ti-based [16–19] ﬁller alloys are used to join Cf/SiC composite
due to their good wettability and relatively high melting point. Yang
et al. [18] used (TiH2-66Ni)1 − xBx (x = 3 wt.%) ﬁller alloy to braze
Cf/SiC composite and TiAl alloy. It was found that there were many
intermetallic compounds in the joints because of the large afﬁnity
between Ti and Ni or Al, and the shear strength of the joints at 800 ◦C
sharply decreased to less than 30 MPa. Some other researchers
designed Cu-based brazing alloys containing noble metal Au and
Pd for Cf/SiC joining, such as Cu-Au-Pd-V alloy [20]. And the result
shows that the three-point bend strengths of the Cf/SiC-Cf/SiC
joints at room temperature were about 128–135 MPa, and the joint
strength could be maintained up to 800 ◦C. However, when the test
temperature further increased to 900 ◦C, the joint strength showed
an extremely low value of 27 MPa. Therefore, searching for new
high temperature brazes for Cf/SiC composite joining to obtain
heat-resistance joints should be of great signiﬁcance.
As described previously, it has been demonstrated that the
active element Ti in ﬁller alloys could react with Cf/SiC compos-
ite and suitable reactions have been occurred at the interfaces
[15–17,19]. Additionally, Co possesses good thermal and oxidation
resistance, and Co-based ﬁller alloys have been successfully used
to join ceramic and metal [21,22]. Meanwhile, Nb is also an active
element and has high melting point. Therefore, it can be deduced
that Ti-Co-Nb ternary alloys should be good high temperature ﬁller
candidates for Cf/SiC joining.

In
 this research, a novel Ti45Co45Nb10 (at.%) ﬁller alloy was
used to join Cf/SiC composite and Nb-1Zr alloy. The microstructure
and interfacial evolution mechanism of Cf/SiC composite and Nb-
1Zr alloy brazed joints were investigated. The effect of the brazing
temperature and holding time on the microstructure and mechan-
ical properties of the joints were discussed in detail.
2. Materials and experimental procedures
Two-dimension Cf/SiC composite prepared by precursor inﬁltra-
tion pyrolysis process was offered by Northwestern Polytechnical
University. The composite is reinforced with T-300 carbon ﬁbers,
and low pressure chemical vapor inﬁltration (CVI) was employed
to deposit a thin pyrolytic carbon layer and the silicon carbide
matrix. The carbon ﬁbers are distributed in the composite in the
form of bundles. The volume fraction of carbon ﬁber is 40% in
Cf/SiC composite, and the density of the composite is 1.98 g/cm3.
The porosity of the composite is 21.5% measured by Archimedes
method. Cf/SiC composite was cut into the samples with the
dimension of 4 mm × 4 mm × 4 mm. Niobium alloy with nominal
composition of Nb-1Zr (at.%) was prepared by arc melting of high
purity Nb and Zr in a water-cooled copper crucible. And the Nb-
1Zr specimens were sliced into 5 mm × 4 mm × 4 mm pieces for
microstructure observation and 10 mm × 5 mm × 4 mm for shear
test. The bonding surfaces (4 × 4 mm2) of the samples were ground
by SiC sandpapers up to grit 800.
The Ti45Co45Nb10 brazing alloy was prepared by arc melt-
ing the mixture of sponge Ti, electrolytic Co and smelt Nb in a
water-cooled copper crucible under argon atmosphere. The purity
of the Ti, Co and Nb are 99.7, 99.95 and 99.9 (wt.%), respectively.
The melting process was repeated ﬁve times to get a composition
homogenous alloy. Then the alloy was sliced into 4 mm × 4 mm
slices, and ground to a thickness of 0.6 mm with 800 grade SiC
sandpapers.
Before assembling, all samples were degreased ultrasonically
in acetone for 15 min, cleaned by ethanol and then dried. After
that, the samples were assembled in Cf/SiC/Ti-Co-Nb foil/Nb-1Zr
order by using organic glue and then placed in a graphite jig.
The brazing process was carried out at different temperatures
(1270–1320◦C) for different holding times (5–30 min) in a vacuum
of (1.0–3.0) × 10−3 Pa. At the beginning, the assembly was heated
to 300 ◦C at a rate of 10 ◦C/min and hold for 30 min to volatilize the
organic glue and thereby ensure a clean brazed surface. Then, the
temperature was increased to the designed brazing temperature
at a rate of 10 ◦C/min and kept steady for designed holding time.
Finally, the brazing samples were cooled down at a rate of 5 ◦C/min
to 300 ◦C and then cooled in the vacuum furnace without power.
The shear strength of the joints was determined by a shear test in
air at room temperature and at high temperatures using a specially
designed jig, as illustrated in Fig. 1. The specimens were loaded by
the testing machine at a constant speed of 0.5 mm/min. At least
three specimens were tested for each joining condition. Differen-
tial thermal analysis (DTA, TGA/SDTA851e) was used to measure
the melting temperature of the ﬁller alloy. The microstructure and
composition of the ﬁller alloy and the joints were investigated by
scanning electron microscope (SEM, FEI Quanta 200FEG) equipped
with an energy dispersive spectroscope (EDS). The phases in the
ﬁller alloy and joints were identiﬁed using an X-ray diffraction
(XRD, D/max-rb) with Cu-K/H9251 radiation.

3.2. Microstructure characterization of the brazed joints
Fig. 3 displays the typical microstructure and EDS composi-
tional maps of the Cf/SiC/Nb-1Zr joint brazed with Ti-Co-Nb alloy
at 1300 ◦C for 10 min. As shown in Fig. 3(a), a defect-free joint is
obtained, which revealed that good wetting and intimate contact
are formed between the substrates and the ﬁller alloy. The brazing
seam can be divided into two regions named zone I and II sepa-
rately (Fig. 3(b)). Zone I is made up of a continuous reaction layer A
and a discontinuous layer B close to the Cf/SiC composite. Zone II,
the central part of the joint, is mainly composed of dark gray phase
(marked by C), white phase (marked by D) and light gray phase
(marked by E).
EDS compositional maps were obtained during SEM (Fig. 3(c–f))
to determine the elemental spatial distribution. It can be clearly
found that a continous Ti-rich layer is formed adjacent to Cf/SiC
composite. And it can also be observed that element Ti diffuses
into the Nb-1Zr alloy at the brazing seam/Nb-1Zr interface. Addi-
tionally, Dark gray phase C is rich in Ti and Co and white phase D is
mainly composed of Nb. These two phases form the eutectics as the
main matrix of the brazing seam. Moreover, Fig. 3(f) shows that the
element Si in the SiC matrix diffuses into the brazing seam, existed
in the light gray phase E with Nb and Co.
Fig. 4. XRD pattern of the joint brazed at 1300 ◦ C for 10 min.
Further analysis of the phases in the joint was carried out using
the EDS compositional analysis and XRD method. The elemental
contents listed in Table 1 shows that reaction layer A in Fig. 3(b)
mainly contains elements Ti, Nb and C, indicating that phase A is
(Ti,Nb)C combining with the XRD result in Fig. 4. Layer B is rich
in elements Ti, Co and Nb. Considering the complete mutual sol-
ubility of Ti and Nb, and the atomic ratio of (Ti + Nb) and Co is
approximately equal to 2:1, layer B can be considered as (Ti,Co)2Co
[23]. In the middle of the brazing seam, the dark gray phase C is
mainly composed of Ti and Co, and the two elements have almost
1:1 atomic ratio. Combining with the XRD analysis result of the
brazing seam region, phase C can be determined to be TiCo inter-
metallic compound with some dissolved Nb. Meanwhile, phase D
can be identiﬁed as Nb(s,s). Additionally, light gray phase E is com-
posed of Nb, Ti, Co and Si elements. Considering Ti can completely
dissolved into Nb, the atomic ratio of the (Nb + Ti), Co and Si in phase
E is about 4:1:1. Moreover, the XRD result in Fig. 4 shows the exis-
tence of CoNb4Si, so we can conclude that the phase E is CoNb4Si
compound.

3.3. Effect of brazing temperature and holding time on the
microstructure of the joints
Fig. 5 displays backscattered electron images of the Cf/SiC/Nb-
1Zr joints brazed by Ti-Co-Nb ﬁller alloy at different temperatures
for 10 min. It can be observed that the thickness of the brazing seam
decreases, whereas the amount and the size of the CoNb4Si phase
increase with elevating the brazing temperature. In addition, the
reaction layer at Cf/SiC side becomes thick. At the lower brazing
temperature (1270 ◦C, Fig. 5(a)), the thickness of the brazing seam is
about 450 /H9262m. And the amount of Nb-1Zr alloy meltd into the ﬁller
alloy is limited at 1270 ◦C. Therefore, the microstructure of brazing
seam is similar to that of the ﬁller alloy itself shown in Fig. 2(a).
Additionally, it can also be noted that the Cf/SiC substrate is not
joined to the brazing alloy efﬁciently and crack can be detected
along the whole Cf/SiC-ﬁller interface. This is mainly because the
brazing temperature is so low that the ﬁller alloy cannot react with
Cf/SiC composite completely to form a continuous reaction layer.
As the brazing temperature increases up to 1280 ◦C (Fig. 5(b)), the
thickness of the brazing seam declines to 116 /H9262m, and a continuous
reaction layer can be observed. Meanwhile, the amount of Nb-1Zr
alloy meltd into the ﬁller alloy increases. As a result, more white
phase Nb(s,s) can be detected in the brazing seam. Besides, some
light gray phase CoNb4Si can be found close to the (Ti,Nb)C reac-
tion layer. Further increasing the brazing temperature to 1300 ◦C,
as shown in Fig. 5(c), the thickness of the brazing seam decreases to
84 /H9262m, and more light gray phase CoNb4Si appeare in the brazing
seam. While the brazing temperature reaches 1320 ◦C (Fig. 5(d)), an
only 46 /H9262m thick brazing seam can be detected, and the CoNb4Si
compounds grow larger.
Fig. 6 shows SEM micrographs of the joints brazed at 1280 ◦C
for different holding times. It can be clearly seen that all the joints
are well-bonded. As the holding time increased, the thickness of
the brazing seam, as well as the microstructure of the brazing
seam, display the same changing trend as the brazing temperature
elevated. The amount of large-sized TiCo intermetallic compound
reduces and the eutectics increase with lengthening the holding
time. Simultaneously, the CoNb4Si compound became more and
larger. At the shorter holding time (5 min, Fig. 6(a)), there is only
a discontinuous reaction layer at the interface of Cf/SiC/ﬁller alloy.
When prolonging the holding time, as shown in Fig. 6(b–d), the
reaction layer (Ti,Nb)C becomes continuous and thicker. It is inter-
esting to note that when the holding time reaches 30 min, as shown
in Fig. 6(d), (Ti,Nb)2Co layer appeares close to the (Ti,Nb)C reaction
layer.
From the microstructure of the joints discussed above, we can
draw a brazing mechanism diagram as illustrated in Fig. 7. Firstly,
the ﬁller alloy is sandwiched between Cf/SiC and Nb-1Zr with a
physical contact during the heating process from the room tem-
perature to solidus curve of the ﬁller alloy. Subsequently, when the
temperature is up to the solidus curve of the ﬁller alloy, it begins to
melt and inﬁltrates into the voids of the Cf/SiC composite. Mean-
while, some Nb atoms of the Nb-1Zr alloy (Zr atom is neglected due
to its small percentage in Nb-1Zr alloy) and dark gray phase TiCo
compounds in the ﬁller alloy dissolve into the liquid ﬁller alloy dur-
ing brazing process. And because of the good afﬁnity between Cf/SiC
and active elements Ti and Nb, Ti and Nb atoms diffuse to the inter-
face of Cf/SiC/ﬁller alloy as shown in Fig. 7(b). Subsequently, Ti and
Nb react with Cf/SiC, forming the reaction layer (Ti,Nb)C, and some
Si atoms are released into the liquid ﬁller alloy as shown in Fig. 7(c).
With further elevating the temperature or prolonging the holding
time as illustrated in Fig. 7(d,e), Ti(Nb) elements further react with
and Cf/SiC composite, leading to a continuous and thicker reac-
tion layer (Ti,Nb)C. Meanwhile, more Nb-1Zr alloy dissolved into

3.4. Mechanical properties of the joints
Shear test was used to evaluate the bonding properties of
Cf/SiC and Nb-1Zr joints brazed with Ti45Co45Nb10 active ﬁller
alloy. Fig. 8(a) shows the shear strength of the joints brazed at
1270–1320◦C for 10 min. It can be seen that the shear strength
of the brazed joints sharply increased to 242 MPa as the brazing
temperature increases from 1270 ◦C to 1280 ◦C. Above 1280 ◦C,
the joint strength decreases gradually as the brazing tempera-
ture goes up. According to the observations of the microstructure
above (Fig. 5(a)), we know that when brazed at 1270 ◦C, there is
a crack between Cf/SiC and ﬁller alloy, resulting in a week bond.
When brazed at higher temperatures (1280–1320◦C), sound joints
obtained, while CoNb4Si compounds become more and larger in the
brazing seam with increasing the brazing temperature (Fig. 5(b–d)).
As CoNb4Si is a brittle compound, it is easy to produce stress
concentration between CoNb4Si compound and the surrounding
phases during the cooling process or under external force. There-
fore, the joint strength decreases gradually to 104 MPa when
increasing the brazing temperature from 1280 ◦C to 1320 ◦C.
Fig. 8(b) illustrates the compressive shear strength of the joints
brazed at 1280 ◦C for different holding times. It shows the same
change trend of the joint strength with that by increasing braz-
ing temperature and the joint strength ﬁrstly increases and then
decreases with extending the holding time.
Due to
 its excellent high temperature properties, Cf/SiC com-
posite usually serves in high temperature working environment,
and it is often
 needed to withstand as high as about 800 ◦C. So it
is
 necessary to study the high temperature strength of the braz-
ing joints.
 Fig. 9 shows the shear strength of the joints testing at
different temperatures. The average shear strength of the brazed
joints (1280 ◦C for 10 min) reached 242 MPa, 202 MPa and 135 MPa
at room temperature, 800 ◦C and 1000 ◦C, respectively. Therefore,
the Cf/SiC-Nb-1Zr joints brazed with Ti-Co-Nb ﬁller alloy can prob-
ably withstand 1000 ◦C, which is higher than most Cf/SiC brazed
joints [16,19,24]. This indicates that Ti-Co-Nb ﬁller alloy is a promis-
ing high temperature brazing alloy, and we can also try to use it to
join other high temperature materials.
4. Conclusions
1. The Ti-Co-Nb brazing alloy manufactured by arc-melting is
mainly comprised of TiCo and Nb(s,s) phases. The melting tem-
perature of the brazing alloy is about 1258 ◦C. Cf/SiC and Nb-1Zr
were successfully brazed with this brazing alloy.
2. The typical structure of the Cf/SiC/Nb-1Zr joint is: Cf/SiC compos-
ite/(Ti,Nb)C reaction layer/(Ti,Nb)2Co layer/Nb(s,s) + (Ti,Nb)Co
eutectics with some CoNb4Si compounds/Nb-1Zr.
3. With increasing the brazing temperature or prolonging the hold-
ing time, the thickness of the brazing seam decreases, while
the volume fraction of CoNb4Si compounds in the brazing seam
increases. Besides, the shear strength of the joint also presents
the trend of ﬁrst increasing then decreasing.
4. The optimum brazing process parameter for attaining the high-
est shear strength (242 MPa) is 1280 ◦C for 10 min. And the shear
strength of the joint reached 202 MPa and 135 MPa at 800 ◦C and
1000◦C, respectively.
