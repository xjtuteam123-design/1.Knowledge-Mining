ABSTRACT
The eutectic Nb 30Ti35Co35 and hypoeutectic Nb 15Ti42.5Co42.5 ﬁller alloys are used
to join bcc Nb–Ti solid solution alloy. It is found that the composition of ﬁller
alloy affects the dissolution of the Nb–Ti matrix into the ﬁller alloy, and con-
sequently inﬂuences the interfacial morphology of the brazing seam. The
Nb
30Ti35Co35/Nb–Ti joint at elevated temperature is mainly composed of the
Ti2Co phase and the Ti–rich phase, while the Nb 15Ti42.5Co42.5/Nb–Ti joint
mainly consists of the TiCo phase. With the increase in brazing temperature, the
TiCo or Ti
2Co phases in the Nb–Ti–Co/Nb–Ti joints increase. The shear strength
of the joints is closely related to the number of TiCo and Ti 2Co phases because of
their high shear modulus. As the brazing temperature further increases, how-
ever, the Ti–rich phase precipitates in the Ti 2Co phase, leading to the increases
of interal stress and the decrease in shear strength. The maximum shear strength
of Nb
30Ti35Co35/Nb–Ti and Nb 15Ti42.5Co42.5/Nb–Ti joints is 598.9 MPa and
573.1 MPa, respectively.
Introduction
Body centered cubic Nb–Ti solid solution alloy is an
attractive candidate for many applications, particu-
larly in high temperature structure application of
aerospace, due to its high strength (1000–1200 MPa at
room temperature), high melting point, moderate
fracture toughness and good room temperature
plasticity [ 1]. Besides, by adding the Ti elements, the
density of Nb–Ti alloy can be adjusted between 6.2
and 6.9 g/cm

can also improve its oxidation resistance [ 3–5]. Most
of previous researches about Nb–Ti alloy revolve
around its production techniques and less have been
carried out about its post-processing techniques such
as joining. It is therefore of great signiﬁcance to
develop reliable joining techniques for its practical
application.
Welding, diffusion bonding and brazing are three
joining techniques which have been commonly
applied in the ﬁeld of high temperature structure
material joining [ 6]. Brazing, due to its relatively low
joining temperature, the possibility of complex
structure joining, high precision, dissimilar material
joining, simplicity and low-cost investment, has been
widely used in the ﬁeld of high temperature structure
material joining [ 7].
As far as brazing concerned, selection of a suit-
able ﬁller alloy plays a key role in obtaining high-
properties joints that can apply in special serving
conditions. It is well known that the operational
temperature of Al, Ag or Au-based brazing joints is
usually below 500 /C176C, which is not sufﬁcient for the
high temperature application [ 8–10]. In contrast, Co-
based ﬁller alloys showed excellent high temperature
behavior in brazing ceramics and metals. The Co–Ni–
Nb–V [ 11], Co–Ti–Zr–Ta–C [ 12] and Ti–Co–Nb
[13, 14] brazing alloys were reported to braze Ni-
based alloys, ceramics and C
f/SiC composites mate-
rials, and their brazing temperature can reach up to
1350 /C176C. Wang et al. [ 15] used Co-based active ﬁller
metals to braze cubic boron nitride (cBN). It is found
that the formation of TiB
2, TiB and TiN reaction layer
on the surface of cBN grains enhanced the bonding
strength. However, the excessively thick reaction
layer deteriorated the bonding strength. Moreover,
Zhang et al. [ 14] used Nb
10Ti45Co45 (at.%) ﬁller alloy
to braze C f/SiC–Nb and Nb–1Zr joints. The brazing
temperature plays an important role in the joint
strength. The thickness of the interfacial reaction
layer and the amount of brittle compound (CoNb
4Si)
are closely related to the brazing temperature. How-
ever, limited investigations have been reported on
using Co-based brazing alloy to braze Nb–Ti alloy.
In order to take full advantage of the high tem-
perature properties of Nb–Ti superalloy, Ti, Nb and
Co elements are selected as the main elements of
brazing alloy in this paper, in which Ti and Nb ele-
ments can ensure the chemical and physical incom-
patibility between the brazing alloys and the base
metal, and Co can adjust the melting point. Besides,
the dissolution enthalpy of Co in liquid Ti is -
115 kJ/mol, indicating the high afﬁnity of the Co
with Ti, and kinds of intermetallic compounds such
as TiCo, Ti
2Co, TiCo 3,C o 2Nb, Co 7Nb2,C o 6Nb7 and
(Nb, Ti)Co could form according to the Ti–Co and
Co–Nb binary alloy phase diagrams [ 16–18]. In
addition, it is reported by Hashi K et al. [ 19] that the
microstructure of Nb30Co35Ti35 ﬁller alloy is com-
pletely composed of eutectic structure {(Nb, Ti) ?
TiCo}. Xue et al. [ 20] have demonstrated that the
eutectic structure {(Nb, Ti) ? TiCo} has excellent
plasticity.
In this research, two compositions of Nb–Ti–Co
ﬁller alloys, namely eutectic Nb
30Ti35Co35 (at.%) and
hypoeutectic Nb 15Ti42.5Co42.5 ﬁller alloys (at.%) were
used to braze Nb–Ti alloy. The effects of the brazing
temperature and the alloy composition of Nb–Ti–Co
ﬁller alloys on the microstructure evolution,
mechanical properties and failure mechanism of the
joints were investigated.
Materials and experimental procedures
The Nb–Ti alloy with nominal composition of 50Nb–
30Ti–6Al–10V–4Cr (at.%) was received in the form of
a cast rod with diameter of 80 mm, and it was pro-
vided by China Aerospace Science and Technology
Corporation. The eutectic Nb
30Ti35Co35 (at.%) and
hypoeutectic Nb 15Ti42.5Co42.5 (at.%) ﬁller alloys were
fabricated by arc melting high purity Nb (99.7 wt. %),
Ti (99.7 wt.%), Co (99.97 wt.%) under vacuum con-
dition. The ingots were remelted ﬁve times to ensure
homogeneity, and the ﬁnal weight loss of the brazing
alloy was less than 0.1 wt.%. The Nb–Ti–Co brazing
ﬁller foils were cut into 8 9 8 9 0.2 mm
3 using the
wire electrical discharge machine, and the Nb–Ti
alloys were cut into 5 9 5 9 2m m

cessively. Prior to brazing, the Nb–Ti–Co ﬁller alloy

sonically in acetone for 10 min and dry by air blow-
ing. Afterward, the Nb–Ti–Co foil was sandwiched

different at elevated temperature. As the Nb–Ti
matrix contains about 50% Nb, in the Nb

continuous dissolution of Nb in the matrix into the
liquid ﬁller alloy, resulting in the decrease in grain
size and the formation of connected micro-channels
between the grains. Meanwhile, under the driven of
capillary force, the liquid ﬁller alloy penetrated deep
inside the Nb–Ti matrix through these micro-chan-
nels. The diffusion zone was more than 300 lmi n
width after brazing at 1330 /C176C for 15 min.
For the Nb

1380 /C176C for 15 min, lower content of Nb in the Nb 15-

with the higher brazing temperature, the diffusion rate

gradual precipitation, aggregation and growth of the

result, the (Nb, Ti) solid solution prevented the pene-

less than 100 lm in width after brazing at 1380 /C176C.

For the Nb 30Ti35Co35/Nb–Ti joints, the shear
strength increased ﬁrstly and then decreased with the
increasing brazing temperature, which is mainly
caused by the following two reasons. Firstly, with the
increase in brazing temperature, the eutectic struc-
ture and the TiCo phase gradually decreased, while
the amount of Ti
2Co phase increased. It is reported
that the shear modulus of the TiCo and Ti 2Co phases
is 61 GPa and 94 GPa, respectively [ 25]. Thus, the
shear strength increased with the increase amount of
Ti
2Co phase. Secondly, further elevating the brazing
temperature to 1310 /C176C, the Ti–rich phase precipi-
tated in the Ti 2Co phase. Due to the brittleness of Ti–
rich phase, an internal stress was generated between
the Ti
2Co and the Ti–rich phases [ 26]. Therefore, the
shear strength gradually decreased, and cracks initi-
ated and propagated inside the Ti
2Co phase and at
the Ti 2Co/Ti–rich phase interface.
Figure 11 shows the cross section of Nb–Ti alloy
joint brazed with Nb 15Ti42.5Co42.5 ﬁller alloy at
different temperature after shear test. It can be seen
that when the brazing temperature was 1260 /C176C, the
failures of the joint occurred in the brazing seam and
the cracks were detected mainly in the (Nb, Ti) and
TiCo phases. With the increase in brazing tempera-
ture (from 1290 to 1380 /C176C), the fracture cracks
mainly located in the TiCo phase.
For the Nb
15Ti42.5Co42.5/Nb–Ti joints, when the
brazing temperature reached 1260 /C176C, the brazing
temperature was relatively low so that the dissolu-
tion of the ﬁller alloy was insufﬁcient, resulting in a
low shear strength. When the brazing temperature
increased to 1320 /C176C, the ﬁller alloy reacted with the
Nb–Ti matrix completely, the amount of TiCo phase
gradually increased, leading to the increase in shear
strength, and reached the maximum shear strength of
573.1 MPa at 1320 /C176C. It can also be seen from Fig. 9

30Ti35-
Co35/Nb–Ti joint is 25.8 Mpa higher than the Nb 15-
Ti42.5Co42.5/Nb–Ti joint, which is mainly attributed to
the shear modulus of Ti 2Co is 33 GPa higher than the
TiCo. As the brazing temperature further increased,
the TiCo phases presented a network-like distribu-
tion around the (Nb, Ti) solid solution, which
decreased the plastic deformation ability of the TiCo
phase, and thus deteriorated the shear strength
[27, 28]. Therefore, the shear strength gradually
decreased, and cracks initiated and propagated in the
TiCo phase.
