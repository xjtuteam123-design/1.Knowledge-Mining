Abstract 
The phase stability of γ′ precipitates in single crystal Ni-based superalloys at the elevated temperature 
is inherently associated with creep performance. During high temperature in-service exposure, the coarsening 
of γ′ precipitates typically occurs and further results in coalescing that is regarded as an indicator of 
degradation of creep resistance. In this research, we design three single crystal Ni-based superalloys by means 
of solely tuning Mo:W ratio to investigate their coarsening behaviors upon different temperature s (950-
1050 °C) and exposure times (0-500 h). The experimental results show ed that the coarsening rate of γ′ 
precipitates increases with increasing Mo:W ratio. The coarsening kinetics are adequately characterised by 
the classical LSEM model. The experimental results can be used to optimize the design of alloy chemistry . 
 
Keywords: Metals and alloys, Ni-based single crystal superalloy , Microstructure, Mo:W ratio, γ′ precipitates, 
coarsening rate

1. Introduction 
Ni-based single crystal (SC) superalloys are widely used in high pressure turbine blade and vane due to 
their outstanding tensile strength, creep strength and corrosion resistance at the elevated temperature [1–3]. 
These attractive mechanical and corrosion properties are primarily attributed to a combination of 
strengthening effects arising from γ′ precipitates and solid solution strengthening. In past decades, the total 
content of refractory elements such as Re, Mo, W and Ta are increasingly added from 14 wt.% to 20 wt.% in 
first-generation and third-generation Ni-based SC superalloys to improve high temperature properties. As the 
most effective strengthening element, Re has been added up to 6 wt.% in the third-generation Ni-based SC 
superalloys [2,4,5]. However, recently developing low-Re and Re-free alloy is becoming a hot topic because 
of its high price and the increasing precipitation propensity of brittle topological close-packed (TCP) phases 
[4]. Unfornately , decreasing the percentage of Re inevitably suffers from the loss of mechanical performance, 
and thus other refractory elements, in particular Mo and W, are intentionally increased to balance it. Previous 
literatures [6,7] have shown that Mo offers benefits to relieve the segregation degree of Re and W, which is 
advantageous to heat treatment process and alloy chemistry. In addition, a higher negative lattice misfit can 
be obtained with increasing Mo content because Mo prefers to segregate to γ phase and leads to larger lattice 
distortion [8]. The potential of Mo to improve creep strength in Ni -based SC superalloys have been 
experimentally ascribed to finer interfacial misfit dislocation network [8]. In this research, we designed and 
picked up three Ni-based SC superalloys via solely changing Mo:W ratio while the total content of Mo+W 
remains constant. The experimental results in these three alloys show that increasing Mo:W ratio corresponds 
to higher coarsening rates of γ′ precipitates, which can be used for alloy design. 
 
2. Experimental materials and procedures 
The nominal composition (at. %) of three SC superalloys (LR1, LR2, LR3) are given as following: Al: 
13.00%; Cr: 7.00%; Co: 6.50%; Re: 0.50%; Ti: 0.60%; Ta: 2.50%; Hf: 0.03%; a total amount of Mo+W is 
3.10% and Ni as balance, in between, the ratios of Mo:W are 1.00: 2.10, 1.75:1.35 and 2.50:0.60 for LR1, 
LR2 and LR3, respectively . The SC superalloy bars were prepared by means of crystal selection method in a 
directional solidification vacuum furnace under a high thermal gradient. The withdrawal rate of the mold was 
3.0 mm/min.

The SC superalloys bars were heat treated by two steps of solutions and aging treatments using the 
procedures as 1300°C/4h+AC→1310°C/2h+AC→1080°C/4h+AC→870°C/16h+AC. The samples for long-
term exposure were sectioned by spark cutting and the (001) planes were parallel to the specimen faces. 
After long-term exposure, the samples were metallographically ground, polished and chemically etched 
with 33% HNO3-33% acetic acid -33% H 2O-1% HF . Subsequently , the microstructural evolution was 
characterized by FEI Quanta 650 field emission scanning electron microscope (SEM). The mean sizes of γ′ 
were measured by Image-Pro Plus software and at least 500 γ′ particles were measured for every state. The 
average particles size was defined as a=√area. And the average particles size has been determined was in 
dendritic regions. 
 
3. Results and discussion 
Fig. 1 illustrates the evolution process of γ′ precipitates at different exposure temperature and time for 
three alloys. Prior to exposure treatment, the morphologies of γ′ precipitates are cuboidal and their average 
sizes are 0.305, 0.311 and 0.313 μm, for LR1, LR2 and LR3, respectively . After exposure, the experimental 
observations show that increasing exposure temperature is in correspondence with faster coarsening rate of 
γ′ precipitates for three alloys. At 1050 °C and time over 200 h, a fraction of γ′ precipitates coalesce into 
larger blocks and in whole the coarsening rate further increases. The measurement results show that 
increasing Mo:W ratio can increase the growth rate of γ′ precipitates. It should be noted that the size of γ′ 
precipitates are not measurable at 1050 °C, 500 h due to severe coalescence. 
The driving forces for γ′ precipitates growth arise from the reduction of interface energy, lattice 
mismatch strain and modulus misfit at the γ/γ′ interface [9]. The coarsening process can be explained by the 
mechanism controlled by solute diffusion through the matrix, i.e., the LSEM model, which describes the 
dependence of coarsening process of the γ′ precipitates on time by the following equation [10]:

Conclusions 
1. The coarsening kinetics of γ′ precipitates in three single crystal superalloys follows a cube rate law behavior 
with exposure time. 
2. The activation energy during the coarsening process of γ′ precipitates in three single crystal superalloys 
was determined as 274.8, 270.2 and 266.4  kJ/mol, respectively , which indicates that the coarsening rates of 
the γ′ precipitates is accelerated with increasing Mo:W ratio.
