ABSTRACT 
The tensile strength and ductility of face-centered cubic (FCC) multi-principal element alloys (MPEAs) typically 
decline monotonically with increasing temperature. In this work, we conducted tensile tests at different tem -
peratures and characterized deformation microstructures for a nonequiatomic CoCrNi alloy with low stacking 
fault energy (SFE). Exceptional ductility is obtained at medium temperature, breaking the general sense of 
monotonic change with temperature. The effect of deformation mechanisms of dislocations, stacking faults and 
phase transformation on the ductility was discussed. This work reveals the unique temperature effect on the 
mechanical properties of metals with very low SFE. 
1. Introduction 
In recent years, the concept of MPEAs has provided a broad 
compositional design space for the development of new alloys [ 1 , 2 ]. 
These alloys can exhibit a range of desirable characteristics, such as high 
strength, hardness, wear resistance, corrosion resistance and thermal 
stability [ 3 – 8 ]. 
Considerable attention has been directed towards single-phase FCC 
MPEAs due to their comprehensive mechanical properties. Researchers 
have developed a large number of FCC MPEAs, such as CoCrNi, CoCr -
FeNi, CoCrFeMn etc. The CoCrNi alloy possesses the best combination of 
strength and ductility among the equiatomic binary, ternary, and qua -
ternary subsets of CoCrFeMnNi [ 9 ] in the temperature range of 77 to 
673 K. Therefore, CoCrNi alloys have been systematically studied. 
Gludovatz et al. [ 10 ] found that deformation-induced nanotwins 
contributed to continuous and stable strain hardening, enabling the 
alloy to achieve high tensile strength of 1.3 GPa and excellent ductility 
of 70 % at 77 K. Through theoretical calculations and experimental 
validation, it was found that the SFE of CoCrNi alloy system varied by 
changing the chemical composition [ 11 ]. Accordingly, nonequiatomic 
CoCrNi alloy with very low SFE was designed by changing the compo -
sition [ 12 ]. Due to the phase transformation-induced plasticity (TRIP) 
effect, the comprehensive strength and ductility of the alloy are even 
better than those of the equiatomic CoCrNi alloy [ 12 ]. 
The temperature can impact significantly on the deformation 
mechanism for FCC MPEAs [ 13 ]. CoCrNi alloy has a fracture toughness 
of up to 459 MPa m ½ at 20 K due to the progressive synergy of dislo -
cation slip, stacking fault, nanotwin and phase transformation [ 14 ]. Sun 
et al. [ 15 ] constructed a fully recrystallized ultrafine-grained CoCr -
FeMnNi alloy and compared the mechanical properties at 293 K and 77 
K. The alloy has better strength and ductility at 77 K, which is attributed 
to the synergistic effect of multiple deformation mechanisms such as 
dislocation slip, stacking fault and nanotwin. They also studied the 
tensile properties of the equiatomic CoCrFeMnNi alloy in a wide tem -
perature range from 77 K to 873 K [ 16 ], and found that the strength and 
ductility of the alloy increased simultaneously with the decrease of 
temperature. In addition, by carrying out tensile tests at 77-673 K for 
CoCrFeMnNi, CoFeMnNi, CoCrMn, CoFeNi, CoCrNi, CoMnNi and other 
single-phase FCC MPEAs [ 9 ], researchers found that the strength and 
ductility of these alloys decreased synchronously with the increase of 
temperature. In summary, it has been generally accepted that the 
strength and ductility of MPEAs are optimized with the decrease of 
temperature, which is mainly due to the transition of dislocation slip 
modes and the enhancement of twinning and phase transformation 
capability at low temperatures. Based on these insights, the tensile 
properties of nonequiatomic CoCrNi alloy with extremely low SFE were

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Microstructure and tensile properties 
The annealed 23Ni alloy was characterized by XRD and ECCI, and 
the XRD profile and micrograph were shown in Fig. 1 . The XRD pattern 
confirms that single-phase structure was obtained, which is in accor -
dance with the well-studied equiatomic CoCrNi alloy. The annealed 
23Ni has been fully recrystallized, with profuse annealing twins in the 
grain interior, indicating the low SFE of the alloy. The average grain size 
of the annealed sample was measured by the line intercept method 
including and excluding twin boundaries (TBs), respectively, which 
were d = 17 ± 2.5 μ m ( d = 60.2 ± 3.2 μ m). In this case, tensile tests were 
carried out at different temperatures. 
The stress-strain curves of 23Ni alloy at different temperatures are 
shown in Fig. 2 a. As the temperature increases from 293 K to 873 K, the 
strength of the alloy decreases monotonously. The yield strength and 
ultimate tensile strength decrease from 375 ± 3 MPa and 758 ± 13 MPa 
to 153 ± 5 MPa and 457 ± 6 MPa, respectively. The decrease of yield 
strength is due to the thermally activated dislocation slips, which can 
overcome the lattice friction barrier and the pinning effect of solid atoms 
[ 17 ]. Notably, unlike other single-phase FCC MPEAs [ 9 , 13 – 16 , 18 ], the 
ductility of 23Ni did not diminish with increasing temperature. It 
reached the highest value at the medium temperature of 573 K (uniform

images in Fig. 2 a depict the length of tensile fractured specimens, which 
corresponds well with the stress-strain curves. In order to explore the 
origin of the ductility change of 23Ni alloy, the strain-hardening curves 
of 23Ni alloy were plotted at different deformation temperatures, as 
shown in Fig. 2 b. These strain-hardening curves can be roughly divided 
in two parts, and the rapid decline part is attributed to dynamic recovery

stronger strain-hardening sustainability at 573 K. There are obvious 
plateau regions at 293 K and 573 K, which may be related to the gen -

gion disappears when the temperature rises to 873 K. In addition, 
serrated stress-strain curve was observed at 873 K, which was supposed 
to be induced by the dynamic strain aging (DSA) effect [ 19 ]. 
3.2. Microstructures after tensile tests 
The anomalous variation of ductility of 23Ni alloy at different tem -
peratures is probably attributed to the transition of deformation mech -
anisms. Previous studies have demonstrated that 23Ni alloy undergoes 
hexagonal close packed (HCP) phase transformation during the tensile 
tests at room temperature [ 12 ]. The change of tensile deformation 
temperature will influence the SFE of the alloy, which may lead to 
changes in the phase transformation behavior of the alloy. To further 
reveal the HCP phase fraction of 23Ni at different temperatures, XRD 
and EBSD characterization were performed for the tensile specimens, as 
shown in Fig. 3 . When the tensile strain is 0.05, there is no HCP 
diffraction peak in the samples tensile tested at the three temperatures, 
indicating that the HCP phase transformation was not activated at low 
tensile strains. In contrast, FCC peaks were found in all fractured sam -
ples, and HCP peaks were detected only in the tensile fractured samples 
at 293 K and 573 K. Further, the volume fraction of the HCP phase of the 
fractured specimens were measured based on the XRD profiles. The 
volume fraction of the HCP phase at 293 K was 15 %, while it was 5 % at 
573 K. No HCP diffraction pattern was found at 873 K, indicating that 
phase transformation was inhibited. 
In order to further explore the deformation mechanism of the 23Ni 
alloy, EBSD and ECCI characterization was performed for the samples 
after tensile tests, as shown in Figs. 4 and 5 . When the tensile strain is 
0.05, stacking faults prevail and intersect in different directions at 293 
K, forming the stacking faults network, as shown in Fig. 4 a and d. In 
contrast, deformation patterns of line traces are not well defined at 573 
K and 873 K. This might be induced by the low flow stress at high 
temperatures, even though, planar deformation patterns can be 
captured. According to Figs. 3a and 4 , it is clear that the plastic defor -
mation was dominated by stacking faults and/or dislocations at the 
three temperatures, but HCP phase was not activated and did not 
contribute to the strain-hardening behavior when the tensile strain is 
0.05. In contrast, the deformation patterns became quite different when 
the plastic strain proceeded until fracture, as shown in Fig. 5 . 
EBSD patterns for the tensile fractured samples are illustrated in 
Fig. 5 a – c. The fraction of HCP phase is 12.6 % at 293 K, and it drops to 5 
% at 573 K, which match well with the calculated results based on the 
XRD profiles. No HCP phase was detected at 873 K, probably due to the 
increased SFE of 23Ni alloy with temperature [ 20 , 21 ]. The high SFE can

3.3. Effect of temperature on the mechanical properties of various MPEAs 
Fig. 2 indicates that the mechanical responses of the 23Ni alloy 
presents unusual change with deformation temperature. In this case, the 
mechanical properties of the 23Ni alloy are compared with those of 
other FCC MPEAs at different temperatures [ 9 , 18 ], as shown in Fig. 7 . 
Under the premise of similar grain sizes, the yield strength and ultimate 
tensile strength of all alloys including 23Ni alloy decline with increasing 
the temperature. In terms of ductility, most FCC MPEAs gradually 
decrease as the temperature rises, with a few alloys exhibiting slight 
fluctuations in the high-temperature range. However, the ductility of the 
23Ni alloy changes dramatically with temperature, reaching its peak at 
the medium temperature. This unique variation in ductility, differing 
apparently from other alloys, can be attributed to the extremely low SFE 
of the material and the corresponding deformation mechanisms at 
different temperatures, which will be discussed in detail as below. 
The SFE plays a significant role in determining the plastic deforma -
tion mechanism of FCC metals. The SFE of 23Ni alloy can rise with the 
temperature, leading to a transition from stacking faults to perfect 
dislocation slip and a suppression of HCP phase transformation. A 
schematic illustration on the deformation mechanisms is shown in 
Fig. 8 . At 293 K, the 23Ni alloy exhibits an extremely low SFE, and the 
plastic deformation is primarily governed by stacking faults and 12.6 % 
HCP phase transformation. Perfect dislocations appear at 573 K, which 
dominate the plastic deformation together with stacking faults. In

When the temperature rises to 873 K, numerous perfect dislocations and

The absence of the TRIP effect and the enhanced dislocations recovery at 
873 K cause the decreased strain-hardening rate compared to 293 K and 
573 K. Furthermore, the stress-strain curve at 873 K exhibits serrated 
behavior, indicating the presence of DSA. It was reported that the solute 
atoms segregation can pin dislocations and increase the dislocation 
density within the Portevin-Le Chatelier (PLC) bands as strain increases. 
This leads to the formation of stress concentrations and a reduction in 
material ductility [ 30 ]. Hence, the decrease in ductility at 873 K can be 
attributed to the increased SFE and the DSA effect. 
4. Conclusions 
In summary, we designed 23Ni alloy with extremely low SFE and 
conducted tensile tests at different temperatures. As the tensile defor -
mation temperature increases, the strength of the alloy decreases 
monotonously, while the highest ductility is achieved at medium tem -
perature. Deformation mechanisms change apparently with increasing 
temperature. Profuse stacking faults dominate the plastic deformation 
and phase transformation is activated at 293 K, in contrast, stacking 
faults and full dislocations dominate the plastic deformation at 873 K. At 
the medium temperature of 573 K, the 23Ni alloy exhibits the highest 
uniform elongation (~64.8 %) due to the coupled effect of stacking 
faults, dislocation slip and a small amount of HCP phase transformation. 
This work explores the influence of tensile deformation temperature on 
the mechanical properties and deformation mechanism of extremely low 
SFE alloys, breaking the conventional sense that the ductility of FCC 
MPEAs elevates monotonously with decreasing temperature.
