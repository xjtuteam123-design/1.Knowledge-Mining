Abstract: Ti-based bulk metallic glasses (BMGs) alloy of (Ti40Zr25Cu9Ni8Be18)100-xNbx with x=0, 1, 3, 4 and 5 at% were prepared by 
copper mould casting. Cylindrical rods of more than 6 mm in diameter consisting of fully glassy single phase were obtained. The 
effects of Nb addition on the glass-forming ability (GFA) and thermal stability of the (Ti40Zr25Cu9Ni8Be18)100-xNbx alloy were studied 
by X-ray diffraction (XRD), transmission electron microscope (TEM) and differential scanning calorimeter (DSC). Mechanical 
properties were measured on a MTS810-type machine at room temperature under a compressive mode with a strain rate of 2 × 10 -4 
s-1. It is found that the GFA is not influenced and the thermal stability is increased by Nb addition. With the increase of Nb conte nt, 
the alloys exhibit high fracture strength. When Nb content x=4, the (Ti 40Zr25Cu9Ni8Be18)96Nb4 rod of fully glassy single phase 
exhibits the highest compressive strength of 1945 MPa and plastic elongation of about 7.0%, showing significant plasticity 
improvement. The large ductility of (Ti40Zr25Cu9Ni8Be18)96Nb4 is due to the development of the large density shear bands proved by 
scanning electron micrographs (SEM). 
 
Key words: Ti-based bulk metallic glasses; mechanical properties; thermal stability; Nb addition; glass-forming ability 
 
 
 
 
During the past twenty years, multi-component bulk 
metallic glasses have attracted great interest due to their 
unique properties such as high yield strength, large elastic 
limit, corrosion and wear resistance and lower elastic 
modulus
[1]. Since the early 1990s, a number of amorphous 
alloys have been successfully synthesized in multi-component 
systems, such as Zr-, Pd-, Fe-, Ni-, Co-, Cu-, and Ti- based 
alloy systems [2,3]. Ti-based amorphous alloys have shown an 
encouraging potential as structural materials due to high 
specific strength and low density [4,5]. It was reported that 
several Ti-based alloys, such as Ti-Be-Zr [4], Ti-Ni-Cu [6], and 
Ti-Ni-Cu-Al[7], show a widely supercooled liquid region 
before crystallization. However, a cooling rate higher than 10 4 
K/s for these alloys is required to obtain glass. So a full 
amorphous structure is only available by fabricating ribbon or 
powder. Therefore, it is necessary to develop new Ti-based 
amorphous alloys, which exhibit very good glass-forming 
ability and require only low cooling rates less than 100 K/s to 
obtain glass state by copper mould casting. Recently, Zhang et 
al. 
[8] reported that a full amorphous rod of a 
Ti50Cu20Ni24Sn3B1Si2 alloy with a diameter of 1 mm was 
fabricated by injection casting. Moreover, Kim et al. reported 
that fully amorphous rods of a Ti
50Cu25Ni15Sn3Be7 alloy with 
up to a diameter of 3 mm [9] and a Ti 40Zr25Cu9Ni8Be18 alloy 
with up to a diameter of 8 mm [10] were successfully fabricated 
by injection casting. 
Unfortunately, most of Ti-based BMGs reported so far 
exhibit only a plastic strain of about 2% when deformed under 
compressive mode. Therefore, the very low level of plasticity 
for the Ti-based BMGs restricts their application as structural 
components. Many attempts, therefore, have been made to 
prepare plasticity-reinforced BMGs by particle

1 Experiment
The master alloy ingots with a nominal composition of 
(Ti40Zr25Cu9Ni8Be18)100-xNbx with x=0, 1, 3, 4 and 5 at% were 
prepared by arc melting the mixture of pure ( 99.9%) Ti, Zr, 
Ni, Cu, Be and Nb metals under a high purity argon 
atmosphere. Each ingot was re-melted at least three times in 
order to guarantee chemical homogeneity. Fully glassy 
cylindrical rods with a diameter of 6 mm and the composition 
of (Ti
40Zr25Cu9Ni8Be18)100-xNbx ( x=0, 1, 3, 4, 5 at%) were 
prepared by copper mould casting. The amorphous structure 
was characterized by XRD using a D/max-2500/PC X-ray 
diffractometer with Cu K Į radiation and a JEM-2010 TEM. 
The thermal properties associated with glass transition 
temperature (T
g), the onset crystallization temperature (Tx) and 
liquidus temperature (Tl) of the cast rods were investigated by 
a STA449c DSC. DSC traces were monitored during 
continuous heating from 350 K to 1200 K at a constant 
heating rate of 0.30 K/s. The experimental error occurring 
during the thermal measurement is about f2 K. Cylindrical 
specimens with a diameter of 3 mm and height of 6 mm were 
cut from the as-cast rods, and mechanical properties were 
measured on a MTS810-type machine at room temperature 
under a compressive mode at a strain rate of 2×10
-4 s -1. The 
specimens were deformed until fracture occurred. The 
deformed fracture surface after failure was investigated by 
scanning electron microscopy (SEM KYKY-2800 operated at 
25 kV).

With the increase of Nb content, the fracture strength 
increases from 1870 MPa for x=3 to 1945 and 1936 MPa for 
x=4 and 5, respectively. On the other hand, the values of the 
room temperature plastic strain are 4%, 7% and 3% for the 
BMGs with x=3, 4 and 5, respectively. This indicates when Nb 
content x=4, the alloy exhibits the best mechanical property. 
The increase in the fracture strength and plastic strain of the 
alloy for x=4 is believed to be due to the addition of Nb. 
The dissimilar deformation behavior of the alloy with Nb 
content x=4 indicates that there may be a different 
deformation mechanism. To further investigate the reason why 
the alloy exhibits the best mechanical property when Nb 
content x=4, the deformed fracture surface of 
(Ti
40Zr25Cu9Ni8Be18)96Nb4 was observed by SEM as shown in 
Fig.5. Fig.5 shows the shear bands on the deformed fracture 
surface of (Ti
40Zr25Cu9Ni8Be18)96Nb4 with vein-like pattern, 
which is typical for the BMGs with good toughness [21]. The

3 Conclusions
1) The (Ti 40Zr25Cu9Ni8Be18)100-xNbx ( x=0, 1, 3, 4, 5 at%) 
bulk glassy alloys with strong GFA can be obtained by copper 
mould casting. 
2) Nb addition can increase the thermal stability but does 
not influence the GFA. 
3) The bulk glassy alloys with Nb addition have higher 
fracture strength than the base alloy Ti 40Zr25Cu9Ni8Be18. The 
optimum addition of Nb (content=4 at%) can lead to the 
highest fracture strength (1945 MPa) and plastic strain (7%).
