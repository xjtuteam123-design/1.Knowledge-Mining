ABSTRACT
The effects of minor additions of B on microstructure, ambient tensile strength, fracture toughness and high-
temperature oxidation resistance of Nb –Si based alloys were investigated. The added contents were designed
as 0.05, 0.10 and 0.20 at.%. The results show constituent phases in 0.05B and 0.20B alloys were Nbss, α-Nb5Si3;
while only 0.10B alloy consisted of γ-Nb5Si3. Minor B was prioritized into Nbss and redundant one dissolved into
silicide enhanced volume fraction of the silicide. With increasing concentrations of boron, the microstructure was
reﬁned at ﬁrst and then coarsened, while the tensile strength was enhanced remarkably. Compared with other
two alloys, the 0.10B alloy containing γ-Nb5Si3 shows both the best fracture toughness and oxidation resistance.
The important roles of γ-Nb5Si3 in balancing overall properties are emphasized.
1. Introduction
Due to the limits of higher-temperature application of nickel based
alloys, the Nb –Si based alloys have drawn extensive attentions owing to
its high melting point, low density and high-temperature strength [ 1,2].
As structure materials served in the ultrahigh-temperature environment,
the Nb –Si based alloys have to possess good comprehensive properties,
such as ductility and toughness at ambient temperature,
high-temperature strength and creep resistance, oxidation resistance and
so on [ 1,3–5]. One of the most important ways to balance these prop-
erties of Nb –Si based alloys is alloying.
After several decades of study, the alloys have been developed from
Nb–Si binary alloys to Nb, Si, Ti, Cr, Al, Hf multicomponent alloys to
achieve overall performance [2,6]. On the basis of Nb –Si multicomponent
alloys, the effects of Fe, Ta, W, Ge, B, Mo, Ge, Ta, W, Fe, Sn has been further
investigated [ 7–13]. Among the alloying elements, Ti was found to
improve oxidation resistance and toughness by the formation of tough-
ening α-Nb5Si3 [14]. However, the addition of some elements, e.g. Fe, Mo,
W, might even decrease the toughness. Tending to form laves phase Cr2Nb,
Cr addition was bene ﬁcial to oxidation resistance and strength but
decreased the toughness [ 15]. Al and Hf could enhance oxidation resis-
tance via the selective oxidation. Ge and Sn eliminated the pest oxidation
and promoted the generation of continuous oxidation layer [ 9,16].
Unfortunately, the aforementioned elements were mainly investi-
gated on either a single property or multiple properties in different
alloying systems with varied components, which is not in favor of in-
dustrial applications of Nb –Si based alloys. It has been reported that the
addition of element B increases the content of silicide which is the
strengthening phase. Behrani et al. presented that boron-containing
silicide (T 2 and D8 8) showed better oxidation resistance than not-
alloying Nb 5Si3 [17]. Besides, the addition of B was found to re ﬁne the
microstructure [ 18], and the toughness was enhanced from 7.85 to
11.98 MPa m 1/2 with 4.5 at.% addition of B [ 19]. Thus, it is worth
expecting that the strength, fracture toughness and oxidation resistance
could be improved with B addition.

3. Results
3.1. Microstructure analysis
The constituent phases of three experimental alloys are shown in
Fig. 1. It was found that there are two phases of Nbss and α-Nb5Si3 (D81-
type tetragonal structure) in 0.05B and 0.20B alloys. However, another
kind of silicide, γ-Nb5Si3 (D88, prototype Ti 5Si3) appeared in the 0.10B
alloy [ 21]. Unlike the preferred <100> orientation of both silicide and
Nbss in Sc-containing Nb –Si alloys [ 22], the XRD patterns of these three
alloys showed no preferred orientation, according to the observation of
the different diffraction peaks.
Fig. 2 shows both transverse and longitudinal microstructure of these
three alloys. Only two contrasts are contained in Fig. 2 (a) and (c), in
which the bright phase is Nbss and the grey one represents α-Nb5Si3.
However, for the 0.10B alloy, another phase with dark contrast existed
and it was determined as γ-Nb5Si3 according to the XRD results ( Fig. 1).
All of the above features of microstructure are in agreement with the XRD
results. As for the longitudinal section the silicide and Nbss arranged
abreast along the solidi ﬁcation direction. The region with the dark
contrast in 0.05B and 0.20B alloys should be attributed to the segregation
of Ti in α-Nb5Si3. In general, it is widely considered that the γ-Nb5Si3
could be stabilized by high (Ti þ Hf) concentrations. The concentration
of Ti or (Ti þ Hf) in γ-Nb5Si3 is over 20 at.% at least, while that in
α-Nb5Si3 is about 12 at.% [ 23]. In the dark-contrast silicide in 0.05B and
0.20B alloys, the content of Ti is slightly higher than that in α-Nb5Si3 but
still much less than γ-Nb5Si3. Thus, the regions with darker contrast in
0.05B and 0.20B alloys may result from the segregations of Ti. By
comparing the phase distributions in Fig. S1 (in Supporting Information)
with that in Fig. 2 (b), it can be concluded that the phases with bright,
grey and dark contrasts in the 0.10B alloy are Nbss, α-Nb5Si3 and
γ-Nb5Si3, respectively. Therefore, it is reasonable to use the different
contrasts (in Fig. 2) to represent different phases. Based on the different
contrasts of the different phases, the average area and area fraction of the
silicides in three alloys were measured to represent silicide size and
contents of the silicide. The total contents of silicides in the three alloys
are 33.2%, 35.7% and 36.6%, respectively, while the sizes of the silicides
in the three alloys are 13.6, 12.1 and 15.0
μm2, respectively. It is worth
noting that the total contents of the silicide increase with higher addition
of B, because B could substitute Si atom in α-Nb5Si3 or exits in γ-Nb5Si3
interstitially [9,21]. Among total 35.7% silicide in the 0.10B alloy, there
is 5.6% γ-Nb5Si3. According to the size distribution of the silicides, the
silicide grain in the 0.10B alloy is super ﬁne than other two alloys.
3.2. Tensile strength and fracture toughness at room temperature
Fig. 3 (a) shows the ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of the investigated
alloys. The tensile strengths of 0.05B, 0.10B and 0.02B alloys are 198,
468 and 810 MPa, respectively, indicating that the UTS could be
remarkably enhanced with trace addition of boron. The UTS of the 0.20B
alloy was even four times higher than that of the 0.05B alloy. The stress-
strain curves of three alloys (Fig. 3 (b)) show that the elongations of three
alloys were 0.15%, 0.32% and 0.51%, respectively. The plastic defor-
mation was not observed, which was common for brittle Nb –Si based
alloys. Whereas, the fracture toughness did not exhibit the same ten-
dencies with the UTS. As displayed in Fig. 3 (c), the toughness of the
0.20B alloy was slightly higher than that of the 0.05B alloy, from 7.9 to
8.4 MPa m 1/2. However, the 0.10B alloy exhibited much higher tough-
ness, which was up to 14.0 MPa m 1/2. The fracture surfaces of the three
alloys after tensile testing are shown in Fig. 4. The 0.05B alloy ruptured
with a large-area ﬂat surface, showing the obvious brittle fracture. A
number of cleavage planes were linked together by tortuous-edge
cleavage steps. The surfaces of the 0.10B and the 0.20B alloys tended
to be more undulated and showed more tearing ridges belonging to Nbss.
Furthermore, some apparent interphase separation of Nbss/Nb 5Si3 and
microcracks could be observed on the surface of 0.20B alloy whose
tensile property was the best.

4. Discussion
4.1. Effects of B on microstructure
For a long period, the researches on B-alloyed Nb –Si based alloys
were mainly focused on the concentration more than 1 at.% and revealed
that the content of B in silicides was higher than that in Nbss [ 19,21,25,
27,30]. However, in this study, both area and line scanning ( Fig. 6) show
that the added minor B mainly existed in Nbss. According to the phase
diagrams of Nb–Si–B and Nb–B, the B can be dissolved in Nbss though in
a limited solubility [ 31,32]. When solidi ﬁed at a relatively faster rate in
the process of directional solidi ﬁcation, the solid solubility limitation
could be enhanced [ 33,34]. Thus, in consideration of previous results, it
is reasonable to speculate that the added B in Nb –Si based alloys was
dissolved in Nbss at ﬁrst thermodynamically or kinetically, and then
redundant B exceeding the solubility limitation would be soluble into
silicide. Zhang et al. ‘s investigation on the effect of 2, 5 and 10 at.% B on
Nb–22Ti–16Si–5Cr-4Hf-3Al, similar composition with this work, showed
that the whole section was nearly full of silicide and re ﬁned eutectic
microstructure with increased concentration of B, indicating that the
content of the silicide could be notably increased and re ﬁned with a bit
addition of B [ 25]. In the solidi ﬁcation process, trace B tends to be
rejected from melt and builds a solute-rich layer ahead of the solidi ﬁ-
cation front because of limited solubility and results in constitutional
supercooling which would cause faster nucleation [ 35–37], like the be-
haviors of small-radius hydrogen [ 38]. In addition, the addition of B has
been found to lower diffusion coefﬁcient of Si [39]. Thus, the diffusion of
Si could be inhibited and the Si content is enough for nucleation of sili-
cide in the local area. Nevertheless, the average composition is not

0.20B alloy due to the microstructure re ﬁning. The microstructure
reﬁning, indicating more interfaces exist in the 0.20 alloy, provides more
diffusion paths and thus accelerated heat-treatment process. The above
discussions are conﬁrmed by the longer-time heat-treatment of the 0.20B
alloy ( Fig. S5 in Supporting Information). After heat treatment for 18 h
and 24 h, the γ-Nb5Si3 gradually transformed into α-Nb5Si3. When sub-
ject to heat treatment for 12 h, the α-Nb5Si3 originating from phase
transformation of γ-Nb5Si3 and the original α-Nb5Si3 in the 0.20B alloy
were merged together, while the γ-Nb5Si3 in 0.10B alloy did not
completely transform into α-Nb5Si3, and some γ-Nb5Si3 were retained.
4.2. Effects of B on tensile strength and fracture toughness
As the in-situ composites, the properties of Nb–Si based alloys depend
on the volume fraction of Nbss and silicides phases. Usually, the brittle
Nb5Si3 provides creep resistance and strength at room and high tem-
perature, whereas the fracture toughness is supplied by ductile Nbss [ 2].
It is worth noting that the Nb –Si based alloys can be strengthened as the
fraction of silicide increases, which was also veri ﬁed using an arti ﬁcial
neural network [ 41]. Besides, the degree of lattice distortion caused by
the solute B in Nbss increases with more B additions. Solution strength-
ening is one of the most typical methods for strengthening materials.
The experimental results showed that the toughness of the alloys
didn't increase with higher additions of B, while the 0.10B alloy showed
the best toughness and other alloys exhibited similar toughness. It should
be notable that a signiﬁcant difference between the 0.10B alloy and other
two alloys is that γ-Nb5Si3 formed only in the 0.10B alloy. Thus, it's not
surprising to consider the toughness of α-Nb5Si3 and γ-Nb5Si3. The value

Apart from the types of silicides in the Nb –Si based alloys, the
microstructure is another in ﬂuencing factor to toughness. The high
plastic constraint caused by high-strength phases in metal-matrix com-
posites unfavorably in ﬂuences the fracture toughness, which is affected
by the shape, size and distributions of the hard phase, i.e. Nb 5Si3 [46]. As
displayed in Fig. 2 , the homogeneous and re ﬁned silicide reduces the
constraint to Nbss and makes Nbss more continuous, which leads to
inevitable crack propagating in Nbss and larger plastic deformation
generated [47,48]. The γ-Nb5Si3 shows greater roundness ( Fig. 2), i.e. its
cross-sectional shape is closer to the globularity, which is in favor of
reduction of the plastic constraint, based on the ﬁnite-element method
analysis [49]. These can explain why the toughness of the 0.10B alloy is
higher than that of other two alloys.
4.3. Oxidation resistance affected by B additions
The oxidation resistance of Nb –Si based alloys is restricted by the
poor anti-oxidation of Nbss, while the silicides show excellent oxidation
resistance owing to the low diffusion rate of oxygen in silicides. The oxide
from silicides, the SiO 2, prevents Nb5Si3 from being further oxidized [ 8].
Besides, the intervals between oxides tend to be ﬁlled with ﬂowing SiO2
at high temperature, which could heal the voids and cracks generated in
the oxidation process and could reduce the oxygen diffusion rate through
oxidation layer [ 29]. Thus, it is not surprising the adhesion of oxidation
scales of the 0.10B alloy and the 0.20B alloy are better than that of the
0.05B alloy, as shown in Figs. 8 and 11. In addition, the more uniform
elemental distributions of the outmost oxidation layer and ﬂatter
oxidized surfaces facilitate to improve oxidation resistance ( Fig. 10 ).
Resultantly, oxidation behaviors of alloys are dependent on volume
fractions of silicide. Higher contents of silicides, higher the oxidation
resistance. However, the 0.10B alloy with fewer silicide than 0.20B
shows the best oxidation resistance, which is attributed to the re ﬁned
microstructure and formation of γ-Nb5Si3 in the 0.10B alloy.

5. Conclusion
1. Minor B addition of 0.05 –0.20 at.% in Nb –Si based alloys is dissolved
in Nbss phase preferentially with a limited solubility and the exces-
sive B goes into the silicide. The content of the silicide increased with
increasing B. The microstructure of the alloys is re ﬁned at ﬁrst and
then coarsened with the increase of B addition.
2. The strength of Nb –Si based alloys increases from 198 MPa to 810
MPa with increasing content of B from 0.05 to 0.20 at.%. The 0.10B
alloy containing γ-Nb5Si3 shows higher fracture toughness than only
α-Nb5Si3-containing alloys while γ-Nb5Si3 doesn't lower the tensile
strength of the alloys.
3. In the process of oxidation, the γ-Nb5Si3 facilitates the formation of a
compact and the lower-PBR transitional oxidation layer, suppresses
the inward the diffusion of oxygen and the movement forward of the
oxidation front, and thus improves the oxidation resistance.
As a consequence, the γ-Nb5Si3 is considered as a bene ﬁcial phase to
balance the strength, fracture toughness and oxidation resistance of the B
contained Nb–16Si–23Ti–4Cr–2Al-2Hf system alloys.
