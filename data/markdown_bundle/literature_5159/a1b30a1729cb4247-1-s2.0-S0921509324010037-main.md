ABSTRACT
The γ ′ phase promotes dynamic recrystallization (DRX) nucleation is closely related with the dislocation- γ ′ phase
interactions. Due to the morphological instability of octets γ ′ phase, dislocations are easier to pill-up at the
concave or interior of γ ′ phase. Moreover, numerous dislocations accumulated around the octets γ ′ phase acti-
vates the γ - γ ′ deformation twin. When γ - γ twin encounters the γ - γ ′ interface, shearing by a/6 [11 – 2] superlattice
shockley partial dislocations result in the formation of γ ′ - γ ′ pseudo twin, and then γ - γ ′ deformation twin formed
between octets γ ′ phase and new γ grain during further deformation.
1. Introduction
Ni-based superalloys have excellent mechanical properties,
depending on the precipitation strengthening effect of coherent and
ordered L1
2
γ ′ phase, and are widely applied for manufacturing turbine
discs [ 1 – 4 ]. The morphology, size and distribution of the γ ′ phase have
great significance to improve the high temperature resistance and me-
chanical properties (tensile, creep, etc), and also influence the hot
compression behaviors of the Ni-based superalloys [ 5 – 7 ]. Previous re-
searches mostly focused on the effect of the presence or absence of γ ′
phase on the microstructure evolution and DRX mechanisms [ 8 – 10 ],
then hot compression tests at sub-solvus and super-solvus temperatures
were conducted [ 11 , 12 ]. However, the effect of γ ′ phase morphology
(size) on deformed microstructure and DRX behaviors during hot
compression have not yet been fully understood. Therefore, the
γ ′ -morphology (size) effect on microstructure evolution, including the
dislocation- γ ′ phase interactions and DRX mechanisms need thoroughly
explored and discussed.
To further reveal the influence of ordered L1
2
γ ′ phase on high
temperature mechanical properties, many investigations were con-
ducted for the interactions between γ ′ phase and dislocations such as
precipitate cutting and Orowan mechanisms [ 13 – 16 ]. The dislocation
cutting through the precipitate usually occurred when the fine γ ′ phases
are distributed in Ni-based superalloy. The presence of fine γ ′ phase
promotes heterogeneous slip, which enhances the resistance to fatigue
damage [ 17 ]. With increasing of temperature, Orowan mechanisms play
a dominant role in coarsen γ ′ phase structure, and different dislocation- γ ′
phase interactions affect deformation behavior and the corresponding
microstructue evolution process [ 18 , 19 ]. In addition, a particular
mechanism, deformation twin, has been taken more attention recently
[ 20 – 24 ]. It has been reported under various deformation conditions
[ 25 – 27 ] and Knowles et al. [ 28 ] have provided that the formation of
deformation twin is closely related with superlattice extrinsic stacking
faults (SFESFs). High resolution TEM images have indicated that the a/6
[11 – 2] Shockley partial dislocations shearing both the γ matrix and γ ′
phase on adjacent {111} crystal planes during deformation twin for-
mation [ 29 ]. However, the effect of γ ′ phase morphology (size) on the
dislocation- γ ′ phase interactions in Ni – Co – W type superalloys is still not
clear, which is the main focus of this work.
In this paper, the relationship between γ ′ phase morphology (size)
and dislocation- γ ′ phase interactions was identified. DRX mechanisms
associated with near-sphere and octets γ ′ phase were thoroughly
explored and discussed, which provides a new perspective for control-
ling the morphology and size of γ ′ phase to regulate the microstructure,
and then affecting the mechanical properties of Ni-based superalloy.

dislocation movement in the γ ′ phase needs a much more lattice
distortion energy (about 4 times as much as in the γ matrix) [ 33 ].
Therefore, the γ ′ phase is more difficult to cut by dislocations, and the
Orowan mechanism became the main deformation mechanism during
hot deformation. As shown in Fig. 3 (a) – (c), a large amount of smaller
near-sphere γ ′ phases are distributed within deformation microstructure
and typical Orowan process for dislocations bypassing near-sphere γ ′
phase could be observed. The dislocations movement is impeded when it
meets near-sphere γ ′ phase, leads to the dislocation line bends around γ ′
phase. Finally, Orowan loop formed around near-sphere γ ′ phases during
further deformation.

For Octets, due to the irregular and larger octets γ ′ phase with
concave feature are unstable in terms of energy, it will preferentially
dissolve along concave direction during hot deformation, as shown in
Fig. 3 (d). This process could be explained by the capillary effect, the γ / γ ′
interface in concave position usually caused high strain energy, then
leads to the dissolution of octets γ ′ phase. Subsequently, the subgrain
formed within octets γ ′ phase, as shown in Fig. 3 (f). In addition, low-
magnification TEM maps present the interaction between octets γ ′
phase and dislocations. Firstly, the movement of dislocations also could
be impeded by octets γ ′ phase, and Orowan loop formed around γ ′ phase
during further deformation. Besides, it also can be observed that
numerous dislocations pill-ups at concave or interior of octets γ ′ phase,
leads to form the high dislocation density deformation zone. Therefore,
there is a higher average GND density in Octets, and then the critical size
of the recrystallization nucleus is smaller, which benefits for the DRX
nucleation.
The interaction between twin and different morphology γ ′ phase also
has great influence on the formation of new DRX grains. For Near-
Sphere, Fig. 4 (a) shows amplified microstructure marked by zone 1#
within Fig. 2 (d). EBSD maps present the pre-existed twin boundary
assisting DRX nucleation mechanism. IPF map reveals that subgrain
formed around the twin boundaries, and it can be evolved into DRX
grain trough absorbing the surrounding dislocations during further
deformation ( Fig. S12 ). Further analysis reveals that the higher dislo-
cation density in the twin is closely related to the exist of near-sphere γ ′
phase. Low-magnification TEM maps reveal near-sphere γ ′ phase located
on twin boundaries, as marked by red arrows in Fig. 4 (b). Due to the
twin boundary ( Fig. S13 presents the bright-field and dark-field TEM
images of twins) and γ ′ phase can effectively impede the movement of
dislocations, leads to high dislocation density zone formed around the
twin boundary, and then the subgrain or incomplete DRX grain formed.
Fig. 4 (e) shows the high-magnification TEM image of the twin boundary,
and near-sphere γ ′ phase is existed on the boundary. The selected area
electron diffraction (SAED) is shown in Fig. 4 (d), from which typical
feature of twin reflections (as marked with a ‘T ’ subscript) can be
observed along the [011] zone axis. The high-resolution TEM (HRTEM)
image taken from the purple square area within Fig. 4 (e) is shown in
Fig. 4 (f), from which the Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) of γ matrix and γ ′
phase viewed along the [011] zone axis. In addition, it can be observed
that the ordered γ ′ phase has obviously superlattice reflections feature.
The Inverse Fast-Fourier Transform (IFFT) image indicated that signif-
icant lattice distortion is induced by the γ ′ phase, which leads to
numerous dislocations formed around twin boundaries and then pro-
motes the DRX process.
For Octets, it can be observed form low-magnification TEM maps

4. Conclusions
In conclusion, the effect of γ ′ phase morphology and size on defor-
mation microstructure and dislocation- γ ′ phase interactions during hot
compression were investigated. Orowan mechanism is the mainly
deformation mechanism in both the near-sphere and octets γ ′ phase. Due
to the morphological instability of octets γ ′ phase, dislocations are easier
to pill-up at the concave or interior of γ ′ phase, leads to higher disloca-
tion density zone formed within deformed microstructure and then
promotes DRX nucleation. Near-sphere γ ′ phase located on pre-existed
twin boundaries to promote DRX grain formed around twin boundary.
While, for octets γ ′ phase, when γ - γ twin encounters the γ - γ ′ interface,
shearing by a/6 [11 – 2] superlattice shockley partial dislocations result
in the formation of γ ′ - γ ′ pseudo twin, and a γ - γ ′ deformation twin formed
between octets γ ′ phase and new γ grain during further deformation,
which promotes DRX.
