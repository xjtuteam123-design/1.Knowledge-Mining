Abstract 
In present work, a hybrid-reinforced titanium alloy has been β forged and heat treated 
successively. The microstructure evolution, deforma tion mechanism and fracture behavior were 
investigated to evaluate the contribution of HT on strength. The fine zone was formed after HT at 
the prior β grain boundary, and the (TiB+TiC+Y 2O3) reinforcement could significantly enlarged 
the area of fine zone and homogenized the microstructure. Silicide precipitation was also observed 
in intra lamellar and α/β boundary after HT, thereby increasing the strength via Orowan 
strengthening mechanism. In addition, the deformati on mechanism analysis indicated that the 
grain boundary slip contributed a great effort to t he high temperature deformation during tensile 
testing. Due to the formation of fine zone and sili cide precipitation, the grain boundary strength 
and intragranular strengths both improved. However, the equal-strength temperature improved 
more thus leaded to a high yield strength in (TiB+TiC+Y 2O3)/ α-Ti composite after HT. This result 
also had a substantial influence on fracture morpho logy, which achieved considerable strength in 
the matrix and composite at elevated temperature. 
 
Key words : Titanium alloy composite; heat treatment; tensile properties; deformation behavior; 
fracture behavior. 
1. Introduction 
It was reported that the discontinuously reinforced titanium matrix composites (DRTMCs) 
reinforced with ceramic particles or whiskers are extensively applied in aerospace and remarkably 
increase service temperature [1-4] . In addition, the thermo-mechanical processing (TM P), as an 
effective way, could provide simultaneous high stre ngth and outstanding ductility. Zhang [3] 
reported a new (TiB+Y 2O3)/ α-Ti composite produced by multi-directional forging (MDF) in near 
β phase region that achieved an exceptionally high ultimate tensile strength of 1.47 GPa and 7.2% 
elongation at room temperature. Ma [5] investigated various TMPs of TiB/Ti1100 composite that 
also achieved high strength and ductility at 650 . Gaisin [6] and Imayev [7] systematically studied 
the microstructure evolution and mechanical propert ies of TiB/VT18U and (TiB+TiC)/VT18U

## Page 4

subjected to various hot forging deformations. And found that the composite depicted excellent 
high-temperature strength, competitive elongation and an appreciably creep resistance. 
Specifically, heat treatment (HT) after TMP also ha s an incomparable advantage on 
improving strength and creep resistance owing to si licide precipitation [8] . It’s reported that the 
silicide are incoherent with respect to β and α phase and precipitated at the α/β lamellar boundaries 
that effectively hindered the movement of dislocati ons. Zhang [9] investigated the relationship 
between β heat treatment & microstructure evolution and mech anical properties of 
(TiB+La 2O3)/near α titanium alloy. Results confirmed that the composites treated at 10  above β 
transus obtained fine grain microstructures and sup erior mechanical properties compared with 
those treated at 20  above β transus. Wang [10] studied β and ( α+β) HT both on fully lamellar and 
equiaxed microstructures of (TiB+TiC+La 2O3)/7715D titanium alloy, and found that the lamellar 
microstructures displayed a better elevated tempera ture strength and poor ductility than the 
equiaxed one. 
As we all know, many studies [3, 5-7] merely concerned about the microstructure control and 
mechanical property optimization. To some extent, t hey indeed achieved high mechanical 
properties at the sacrifice of trial and error. How ever, the deformation behavior and strength 
mechanism must also be studied in detain to further guide engineering applications. Notably, 
during tensile test deformation, two important frac ture processes, i.e., crack nucleation and crack 
propagation, are associated with grain boundary sli p and α/β phase slip in titanium alloy [11-15] . In 
general, crack nucleation is caused by the stress c oncentration that originates from uncoordinated 
deformation and involves β grain to β grain, α colony to α colony, and even α/β laths. Bridier [12, 16] 
revealed that the local crystallographic orientatio n has a significant impact on fatigue behavior. 
The basal and prismatic slip are dominant during de formation and cracks formed from prismatic 
and basal slip planes. Besides, the grain boundarie s were also prone to form the microcracks 
owing to its weakest strength during high-temperatu re deformation, and its fracture may directly 
cause microcrack initiation and propagation [17] . Therefore, further knowledge on fracture behavior 
would help in understanding the micro-deformation mechanism. 
In this work, forging deformation and HT was conduc ted on hybrid-reinforced titanium 
matrix composite. Then the microstructure evolution and 650 °C and 700 °C tensile tests were 
investigated. Particularly, Deformation behavior an d fracture model were analyzed based on 
scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) microstructure 
to evaluate the effect of HT on strength increment.

3.3 Deformation behavior during elevated tensile test after HT 
It was well know that plastic deformation of polycr ystalline alloys is mainly achieved 
through intragranular deformation and grain boundary slip [11, 27, 28] . The intragranular deformation 
is associated with α lamellar slip and α/β phase slip, and grain boundary slip is associated with β 
grain boundary slip and α colony boundary slip. And there were some useful s uggestion to 
mechanical properties in understanding the deformat ion behavior and mechanism during elevated 
tensile test. 
3.3.1 Slip of α lamellar 
In general, the deformation behavior is closely rel ated to α and β lamellar slips. Due to high 
volume fraction of α phase in near α titanium alloy, this work focused on the slip of α phase. The 
slip of dislocation in α phase in the HT condition matrix and (TiB+TiC+Y 
2O3)/ α-Ti composite 
after testing at 700  was showed in Fig. 5 . 
After deformation, the most of dislocations in matr ix were parallel arranged inside the α 
lamellar, which was perpendicular to the long side of the α lamella. By the means of selected area 
diffraction (SAD) ( Fig. 5 c ), the slip plan was determined to be (0001) α plane. Based on the work 
of Joseph [29] and Tan [30] , the type of dislocation was identified as basal< a>/prism < a> slip (as 
pointed with red arrow in Figs. 5a and b). However, except basal< a>/prism < a> slip, the 
pyramidal < a> slips were also observed in the (TiB+TiC+Y 2O3)/ α-Ti composite ( Fig. 5 c ).

rises. In titanium alloy, the α and β phase maintain Burgers orientation relationship (B OR, i.e. 
{0001} α//{110} β and <11-20> α//<1-11> β). Because the mainly slip plane in α ({0001} α) and β

slip deformation between α and β was so harmonious that their morphologies maintain an

in matrix was depicted in Fig. 6 . Results revealed that the α and β phase still maintained BOR

the atom arrangement of the α and β boundary revealed a small mismatch between them. 
Furthermore, an additional atom face in the β phase that resulted from the IFFT of region A was

3.3.3 Slip of grain boundary 
The deformation of polycrystalline alloys is mainly achieved by intragranular deformation 
and grain boundary slip [11, 27, 28] . When deformed at room temperature, the grain boun dary 
strength is high enough to resist slip deformation, thus, the deformation is mainly achieved by 
intragranular deformation. When deformed temperatur e raised, both the grain boundary strength 
and intragranular strength decrease, but the grain boundary strength decreases more sharply [28] . 
Until it reaches a certain temperature, the grain b oundary strength and the intragranular strength 
are equal. Therefore, the grain boundary slip and t he intragranular deformation contribute equally 
to deformation. This temperature is so-called equal-strength temperature . If the deformation 
temperature is further increased, intragranular str ength becomes higher than grain boundary 
strength. Hence, the grain boundary is the weakest location of the polycrystalline alloys. So the 
deformation is mainly achieved by grain boundary slip. 
Owing to the difficulty in directly investigating t he slip of grain boundary, we chose to 
evaluate the microstructure near the fracture, putt ing emphasis on the crack nucleation and crack 
propagation, which indirectly revealed the slip beh avior of grain boundary. The slip between 
adjacent β grains and adjacent α colonies is highly dependent on its orientation an d is thereby 
prone to crack nucleation [32, 34] . The microstructure near the fracture, particularl y the crack 
morphology after tensile deformation were showed in Fig. 7 . cracks were mainly along β grain 
boundaries and α colonies boundaries in matrix ( Fig. 7 a-c). It was worth noting that the crack was 
also along the boundary of fine zone, which was an evidence of uncoordinated deformation 
between fine zone and prior coarse grain ( Fig. 7a ). However, in the (TiB+TiC+Y 2O3)/ α-Ti 
composite, besides the crack along β grain boundaries and α colonies boundaries, there were 
cracks along the inter-lamellar( Fig. 7f ). According to the above intragranular and grain b oundary 
strength theory, the intragranular and grain bounda ry strength both improved because of silicide 
precipitation and fine zone formation after HT. To some extent, cracks along the inter-lamellar 
region revealed that the improvement degrees of gra in boundary strength in the 
(TiB+TiC+Y 2O3)/ α-Ti composite was much large than that in the matri x. In other words, the 
equal-strength temperature of the matrix was relatively lower than that of the composite, resulting 
in a large percentage of grain boundary slip.

the matrix at 650 °C. What is noteworthy is that th e (TiB+TiC+Y 2O3)/ α-Ti composite had similar 
YS increment at such high temperature after HT. Thi s consumingly demonstrated that the fine 
zone microstructure may be another key factor that contributes to high YS increment. 
3.4.2 Contribution of fine zone 
During high temperature deformation, the grain boun dary slip participated more in 
deformation as the deformation temperature increase d. Interestingly, the fine zone formed after 
HT happened to be located in the prior β grain boundary. That is to say, dynamic recrystallization 
during deformation and phase transformation during HT were performed successively at such 
region that finally formed fine zone. Therefore, th e whole fine zone can be referred to as a newly 
grain boundary zone and the prior coarse zone (prior intragranular β grain, i.e., long acicular 
lamellar region) as the newly intragranular zone. A schematic diagram of the fine zone is clearly 
illustrated in Fig. 8 . 
Due to the formation of fine zone and precipitation of silicide, the grain boundary and 
intragranular strengths both increased after HT. Ho wever, the grain boundary strength of the 
(TiB+TiC+Y 2O3)/ α-Ti composite improved more than that of the matrix . In other words, the 
equal-strength temperature of the (TiB+TiC+Y 2O3)/ α-Ti composite was more enhanced than that 
of the matrix after HT. Particularly, the presence of cracks along the inter-lamellar region 
identified this argument as shown in Fig. 7f . The improved grain boundary and intragranular 
strengths resulted in the ultimately high YS after HT at last. In addition, armed with 
(TiB+TiC+Y 2O3) reinforcements, the composite showed larger fine zone, thus leading to higher 
grain boundary strength. This finding also showed t hat the (TiB+TiC+Y 2O3) reinforcements in 
turn depicted more YS increment than the matrix. Th is model was similar to that of Huang [37, 38] 
who designed a novel network reinforcement distribu ted at grain boundary, which achieved 
significantly higher tensile strength than that of unreinforced matrix alloy.

3.5 Fracture of (TiB+TiC+Y 2O3)/ α-Ti composite after HT 
In general, the fracture type can be classified int o two types: transgranular fracture and 
intergranular fracture. In present work, the fractu re of (TiB+TiC+Y 2O3)/ α-Ti composite showed 
both of them, and the schematic of two type fractur es are illustrated in Fig. 9 . The microcrack 
prefers to nucleate at the locations such as β grain boundary, α colony boundary, and α/β lamellar 
boundary (particularly the site where silicide is s eparated out) due to uncoordinated deformation 
during elevated temperature tensile deformation. In one case, when the grain boundary strength 
was stronger than the intragranular strength (such as deformation temperature below the 
equal-strength temperature), the deformation was ma inly concentrated on the intragranular zone. 
Therefore, most of microcracks nucleated at the int ragranular zone such as the site where silicide 
was separated out ( Fig. 9b ). As a result, the specimen eventually failed due to transgranular 
fracture (type 1 in Fig. 9 ). In another case, when the grain boundary strength was equal or weaker 
than the intragranular strength (such as deformatio n temperature higher than the equal-strength 
temperature), the grain boundary slip was the main deformation mode. Therefore, the microcrack 
nucleated at the β grain and α colony boundaries. As the deformation continued, crack propagation 
occurred along the β grain and α colony boundaries through merging of micro-cracks. Once a 
huge crack was formed along several grain boundarie s, the specimen eventually failed due to 
intergranular fracture (type 2 in Fig. 9 ).

4. Conclusions 
In this study, the hybrid-reinforced titanium alloy has been β forged and heat treated 
successively. The difference of microstructure and high temperature tensile properties were 
investigated. The main conclusions were as follow: 
(1) The fine zone was formed after HT at the prior β grain boundary, and the (TiB+TiC+Y 2O3) 
reinforcement could significantly enlarged the area of fine zone and homogenized the 
microstructure. 
(2) The precipitation of silicide was observed in intra lamellar and α/β boundary after HT, 
thereby increased the yield strength via Orowan strengthening mechanism.

## Page 19

(3) Not only the plain < a> slip of α lamellar participated in the deformation, but also the grain 
boundary slip contributed a great effort to the def ormation during tensile testing. Moreover, 
the α colony morphology keep complete in matrix while cr acked (TiB+TiC+Y 2O3)/ α-Ti 
composite after tensile test 
(4) The strength of the composite after HT increased significantly at 650 °C and 700 °C. 
(5) The grain boundary and intragranular strengths obviously improved because of the formation 
of fine zone and silicide precipitation. Therefore, high YS after HT has a significant influence 
on fracture morphology.
