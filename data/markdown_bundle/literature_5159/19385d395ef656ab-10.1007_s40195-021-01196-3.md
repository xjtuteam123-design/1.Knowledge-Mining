Effects of Homogenized Treatment on Microstructure and Mechanical 
Properties of  AlCoCrFeNi2.2 Near‑Eutectic High‑Entropy Alloy
Xuan Huang1 · Yong Dong1 · Shaomu Lu1 · Chuanqiang Li1 · Zhengrong Zhang1
Received: 29 September 2020 / Revised: 9 December 2020 / Accepted: 14 December 2020 
© The Chinese Society for Metals (CSM) and Springer-Verlag GmbH Germany, part of Springer Nature 2021
Abstract
A 4 kg AlCoCrFeNi2.2 near-eutectic high-entropy alloy ingot was prepared by vacuum medium frequency induction melting. 
The effects of homogenized treatment on microstructure and mechanical properties of AlCoCrFeNi2.2 were studied. The 
results showed that all the alloys consisted of the primary FCC phases and eutectic FCC/B2 phases. After homogenized treat-
ment, lots of precipitated phases appeared in the primary phase. The hardness of the as-cast alloy was HV296. The hardness 
values of samples were decreased and were around HV250 after homogenized treatment. The tensile fracture strength of the 
as-cast alloy reached 900 MPa, while the elongation was 18%. After homogenized treatment at 900 °C, the alloy showed the 
most excellent mechanical properties with the fracture strength 880 MPa and the elongation was 29%, respectively. All the 
alloys displayed a mixture fracture mechanism, including ductile fracture in primary FCC phases and eutectic FCC phases, 
and brittle quasi-cleavage fracture in eutectic B2 phases. Through a simple heat treatment method, the strength of the alloy 
was not reduced but the plasticity was greatly enhanced, which was more conducive to the industrial application prospects.
Keywords High-entropy alloys · Eutectic alloys · Microstructure · Tensile strength · Microhardness
1 Introduction
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) are generally defined as 
alloys consisting of five or more major elements with 
near-equiatomic ratios [1 ]. In the previous research work, 
high-entropy alloys have been proven that they usually 
have unique microstructures and excellent properties, 
such as excellent mechanical properties [2 –6], corrosion 
resistance [7 , 8], thermal stability [9 , 10], and radiation 
resistance [11, 12]. At present, one of the major research 
fields about HEAs is the mechanical properties. The typi-
cal face-centered cubic (FCC) single-phase HEAs usually 
have high strength and good plasticity. For example, the 
tensile strength of CrMnFeCoNi HEA exceeds 1 GPa, 
while the failure strains exceed 70% at room temperature. 
Even the properties are significantly improved at liquid 
nitrogen temperature [2 , 3]. The typical body-centered 
cubic (BCC) single-phase HEAs usually show excellent 
high temperature strength and extraordinary microhard -
ness, such as AINbTiV, TaNbHfZrTi, MoNbRe 0.5TaW, 
W–Nb–Mo–Ta, and W–Nb–Mo–Ta–V alloys [5 , 9, 13, 
14]. The single-phase hexagonal close-packed (HCP) 
HEAs are rarely seen, but they still have high hardness and 
strength in the existing findings [15, 16]. Although HEAs 
have exhibited some excellent properties compared to 
ordinary alloys, there are still some defects in the single-
phase HEAs. For example, the FCC single-phase HEAs 
in the as-cast state usually have low yield and fracture 
strength, the BCC single-phase HEAs and HCP single-
phase HEAs show poor plasticity in tensile test. Based on 
the nature of mechanical properties of single-phase HEAs, 
many researchers tried to find ways to improve the short-
comings of single-phase high-entropy alloys. Salishchev 
et al. achieved solid solution strengthening of CoCrFeNi 
system HEA by alloying approach [17]; Wu et al. obtained 
equiaxed microstructures with comparable grain sizes by 
thermomechanical treatment and successfully strength-
ened the FCC single-phase HEA [18]. Wang and Chen 
et al. strengthened FCC single-phase HEAs with inter -
stitial atom to improve the plasticity of HEAs [19, 20].

HEAs by adding particles such as SiC and Al2O3 [21, 22]. 
Tong et al. tried to make the FCC matrix phase of HEA

improve the mechanical properties of the alloys, such as

[30]. In addition to the trade-off of the strength and plastic-
ity that needs to be resolved, the single-phase HEAs have 
a poor casting fluidity, which severely limit the industrial 
application of HEAs.
In order to solve the above problems, Lu et al. developed 
the concept of eutectic HEAs (EHEAs) with fine lamel-
lar microstructures comprising FCC and B2 phase. The 
alloys achieved a good balance of strength and ductility 
and had a good casting performance [31]. The yield and 
fracture stresses of AlCoCrFeNi 2.1 EHEA were 545 MPa 
and 1100 MPa, while the elongation exceeded 20% [32]. 
Wani et al. used heavy cold-rolling and annealing to fur -
ther increase the strength of AlCoCrFeNi 2.1 EHEA [33]. 
Bhattacharjee et al. found that the strength and ductility of 
 AlCoCrFeNi2.1 EHEA could be increased due to the hierar-
chical microstructure after cryo-rolled and annealed [34, 35]. 
Xiong et al. introduced nanoprecipitates into each constitu-
ent phase of AlCoCrFeNi2.1 EHEA and generated additional 
strengthening effect on the material without sacrifice ductil-
ity [36]. Gao et al. using the classical casting technique to 
prepare the AlCoCrFeNi2.1 EHEA with (FCC) (L12)/(BCC) 
(B2) modulated lamellar structures to obtain the ultimate 
tensile strength (1351 MPa) and ductility (15.4%) [37]. Shi 
et al. prepared AlCoCrFeNi2.1 EHEA with ultrafine-grained 
duplex microstructure by tailored thermomechanical pro-
cessing, and attributed the mechanical response during the 
deformation process to both a two-hierarchical constraint 
effect and a self-generated microcrack-arresting mechanism 
[38].
Although a lot of works about AlCoCrFeNi2.1 alloy were 
reported, it has been found that in the as-cast condition the 
mechanical properties of AlCoCrFeNiNi 2.2 hypoeutectic 
HEA were superior to those of AlCoCrFeNi2.1 alloy [32]. 
In AlCoCrFeNiNi2.2 alloy, the primary FCC phases provided 
more ductility and the second precipitate in primary phases 
guaranteed the strength. By now, few works on hypoeutectic 
 AlCoCrFeNi2.2 were reported. Considering that EHEAs are 
attractive due to their superior work-ability and mechanical 
properties, so that they may be processed by conventional 
processing and need to be processed by severe plastic defor-
mation and other exotic processes [39, 40]. Thus, it is neces-
sary to study the microstructural evolution and the second 
precipitate of as-cast and as-homogenized AlCoCrFeNi2.2 
alloys to further optimize the mechanical properties and pre-
pare for the deformation process.
2 Experimental
The AlCoCrFeNi 2.2 alloy (Marked as Ni2.2 alloy) was 
prepared by vacuum medium frequency induction melt-
ing with pure element materials (aluminum, cobalt, 
nickel: 99.9 wt%; chromium, iron: 99.5–99.6 wt%). The 
raw materials were cleaned in an ultrasonic vibrometer 
containing absolute ethanol and dried in air. After the 
vacuum reached 5 Pa, the raw materials were melted 
under a vacuum of 0.05 MPa by backfilling pure argon 
(Ar). The liquid metal was held at 1500 °C for 15 min 
and then poured into a high purity graphite crucible. An 
ingot with a weight of 4 kg, a diameter of Φ 55 mm, and 
a height of 160 mm was obtained. Four samples of diam-
eter 10 mm and length 10 mm were cut from the ingot. 
Three samples were homogenized at the temperatures of 
900, 1000, 1100 °C for 4 h, and then quenched in water, 
respectively. The as-cast and homogenized samples were 
marked as AC, H-900, H-1000, and H-1100, respectively. 
The crystal structures were identified with a Ultima IV 
multi-function X-ray diffractometer using a Cu target, with 
the scanning rate of 8°/min and the 2θ scanning range of 
20°–100°. The as-cast and as-homogenized metallographic 
samples were mechanically polished and etched with aqua 
regia. The microstructure observation and internal features 
were observed by scanning electron microscopy (SEM, 
S-3400 N) with energy disperse spectrometer (EDS). The 
hardness test was performed on a micro-Vickers hard-
ness tester (HVS-1000Z) under a load of 1000 g for 15 s. 
Five measurements were made on each sample to obtain 
the averaged experimental data. Bar-shaped tensile test 
samples with an average gauge length of 20 mm, width 
of 5 mm, and thickness of 1.3 mm were prepared. The 
tensile tests were performed in an AG-S Plus Shimadzu 
electronic universal tensile testing machine with an initial 
strain rate of 10–3 s−1. When analyzing tensile test results, 
the yield strength was taken as the stress value producing 
0.2% residual deformation.

negative mixing enthalpy) and the solid solution group

exhibited a B2 structure from room temperature to melt-

calculated from the strongest (111) FCC peak and (110) B2

were similar with the parameters of NiAl (2.8870 Å) and 
CoCrFeNi (3.574 Å) [ 48, 49], which suggested that the 
experiment results were well in agreement with the predic-
tion of the group theory.
Figure  2 shows the SEM microstructures of as-cast 
 Ni2.2 alloy. The alloy consisted of the primary phases 
and eutectic phases. The ratio of the two-phase in as-cast 
 AlCoCrFeNi2.2 was counted with Image-Pro Plus software 
and the volume fraction of the FCC and B2 phases were 
about 69.19% and 30.81%, respectively. The Co and Ni ele-

some phases enriched Al elements but lacked Cr and Fe 
elements, as seen in Fig. 3. Table 2 lists the distributions of 
different constituent elements in the microstructure deter -
mined by SEM–EDS; the location of the EDS scan on the 
sample is shown in Fig.  2b. Based on the results of XRD 
and SEM–EDS, it can be concluded that the primary phase 
was the CoCrFeNi-rich FCC solid solution and the lamel -
lar eutectic structure was a mixture of CoCrFeNi-rich FCC 
and NiAl-rich B2 solid solution phases. These results were 
also in agreement with the results in precedent publications 
[32, 37, 50]. Besides, the Al content in FCC primary phases 
was much higher than that in eutectic FCC phases, which 
implied that the FCC primary phases were in an unstable 
state compared with the eutectic FCC phases.

4 Conclusion
In the present work, the effect of homogenized treatment 
temperature on the microstructure and mechanical prop-
erties of AlCoCrFeNi 2.2 near-eutectic high-entropy alloy 
was investigated. The as-cast Ni2.2 alloy consisted of the 
primary FCC phases and eutectic FCC/B2 phases. After 
homogenized treatment, lots of precipitated phases clearly 
appeared in the primary phase. Moreover, the size of the 
precipitates increased significantly with the increase in the 
homogenizing temperature. The hardness of the as-cast Ni2.2 
alloy was HV296. The hardness decreased and was around 
HV250 after homogenized. The tensile fracture strength of
