CRYSTALLOGRAPHY AND PHASE TRANSITIONS IN THE Ta-Ru AND Nb-Ru 
SHAPE MEMORY ALLOYS 
R. W. Fonda and R. A. Vandermeer 
Naval Research Laboratory, Code 6324, Washington DC 20375 
A new class of shape memory alloys, based on near-equiatomic compositions of Ta-Ru and Nb-Ru, 
was recently discovered at the Naval Research Laboratory. These alloys exhibit transformation 
temperatures ranging up to 1400 °C for Ta-Ru and 1100 °C for Nb-Ru, making them the highest 
transition temperature shape memory alloys yet discovered. Other shape memory alloys typically 
have transition temperatures within a couple hundred degrees of room temperature.
1 These two alloy 
systems are quite similar in their transformation behavior. Near the equiatomic composition, the high 
temperature p phase of both systems has a B2-ordered cubic crystal structure.2-3 During cooling, the 
cubic lattice of p undergoes a slight tetragonal distortion to form the p1 phase. Continued cooling of 
the tetragonal p' (within specific composition ranges) causes a transformation to p", which is reported 
to have an orthorhombic crystal structure. 4 The effect of alloy composition on the temperatures, 
hysteresis, and strains of these transformations was determined by dilatometry. 
Although x-ray diffraction of equiatomic TaRu corresponded well with the previously reported 
orthorhombic crystal structure, electron microdiffraction of the low temperature P" phase revealed 
additional diffraction reflections (Figure 1). These superlattice reflections reduced the symmetry to 
the monoclinic 2/m space group. These superlattice reflections were used to infer a unit cell for this 
P" phase, shown in Figure 2, with dimensions of a=0.801 nm, b=0.437 nm, c=0.551 nm, and 
p=96.5°. 
The microstructure of the equiatomic TaRu and NbRu alloys is similar to that observed in other shape 
memory alloys (Figure 3). This characteristic microstructure consists of coarse twins a couple 
microns wide containing fine twins a couple tenths of microns wide. Underlying this twinned 
microstructure are fine boundaries with the appearance of antiphase domains (Figure 4). Electron 
microdiffraction across these boundaries reveals them to be 8-boundaries. 
The p" to p' transformation was observed in-situ by high temperature TEM. The room temperature 
microstructure undergoes a nearly instantaneous transformation during heating which removes the 5-
boundaries yet leaves the twinned microstructure intact (Figure 5). The change in electron 
microdiffraction patterns, however, occurs much more gradually. The disappearance of the 
superlattice reflections occurs over many tens of degrees C, leaving the reflections from the ordered 
tetragonal P' phase (Figure 6). Thus, both the 8-boundaries and the 3- and 6-fold superlattice 
reflections are due to the low-temperature monoclinic p" phase. The effect of composition on both the 
room temperature microstructure and the phase transformation behavior of these alloys will also be 
discussed.
