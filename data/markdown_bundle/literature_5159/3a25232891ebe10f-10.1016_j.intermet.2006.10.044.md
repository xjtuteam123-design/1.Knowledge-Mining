Effects of alloying elements on phase stability in
NbeSi system intermetallics materials
Shiyu Qu *, Yafang Han, Liguo Song
Beijing Institute of Aeronautical Materials, P.O. Box 81-1, Beijing 100095, PR China
Available online 7 February 2007
Abstract
The effects of alloying elements, such as Ti, Cr, Al and Hf on phase stability in Nb eSi system intermetallics materials have been investigated
by scanning electron microscopy (SEM), X-ray energy dispersive spectrum (EDS) and X-ray diffraction (XRD). The binary, ternary and mul-
ticomponent alloys have been fabricated by vacuum non-consumable arc melting method. The results showed that Ti and Hf tend to stabilize
Nb
3Si phase to lower temperatures, while Cr and Al promote the direct formation of Nb 5Si3 phase. The coordinate effects of alloying elements
on microstructures of Nb eSi system intermetallics materials have also been examined.

1. Introduction
Advanced aeronautical engines require materials with high
temperature capability and reasonable balance of properties
such as low temperature toughness, high temperature strength,
creep resistance and oxidation resistance. Nb/Nb 5Si3 in situ
composites [1e4] may have the potential of meeting the high
temperature requirements of advanced turbine engines. The
potential of increased temperature capability of Nb/Nb
5Si3 in
situ composites is estimated as nearly 200 /C14 C for both bulk
average temperature and maximum surface temperature as
compared to the current single-crystal superalloys [5,6]. The
demonstrated rupture performance of the Nb/Nb
5Si3 in situ
composites compares well to the second- and third-generation
single-crystal superalloys. However, the use of the Nb/Nb
5Si3
in situ composites at surface temperatures much higher than
1370
/C14 C [7,8] will require further substantial increase in oxida-
tion resistance for both the Nb/Nb 5Si3 in situ composites and
protective coatings. The density target for the Nb/Nb 5Si3 in
situ composites is <6.8 g/cm3, and a density of 7.2 g/cm 3 has
already been demonstrated for tough, strong Nb/Nb 5Si3 in
situ composites [9]. This target is at least 25% lower than den-
sities of third-generation single-crystal superalloys. In this
paper, the process, microstructure and mechanical properties
of refractory Nb-silicide-based composites have been studied.
2. Experimental procedure
The highly pure starting raw metals of Nb, Ti, Cr, Al and Si
were selected to prepare the binary, ternary and multicompo-
nent alloys. The chemical compositions of the alloys in the
present investigation are listed in Table 1 , in which, alloys
2e5# are used to examine the effects of Cr, Al, Hf, Ti on phase
stability in Nb eSi system alloys, compared with 1#. While al-
loys 6e11# are used to examine coordinate effects of alloying
elements on the microstructures of Nb eSi system alloys.
Small alloy button ingots were prepared by arc melting un-
der an argon atmosphere on a water-cooled copper hearth, us-
ing a non-consumable tungsten electrode. The alloy buttons
were melted at least 3 times to assure the chemical homogene-
ity of constituent elements.
Specimens for microstructural observations were prepared
by electro-discharge machining (EDM). Microstructural im-
ages were taken using back-scattered electron image (BEI)

3.2. The coordinate effects of alloying elements
on microstructures of Nb eSi alloys
In order to examine the coordinate effects of alloying ele-
ments on microstructures of Nb eSi alloys, the microstructures
of Nb eCreAleSi, Nb eTieAleSi, Nb eTieCreSi, Nb eCre
HfeSi, Nb eAleHfeSi and Nb eTieCreAleHfeSi alloys
have been investigated. Fig. 3 shows the microstructures of
the multicomponent Nb eSi alloys. The results of EDS and
XRD analyses showed that the microstructures of the multi-
component alloys essentially maintained the feature of the ter-
nary alloys. As all of the alloys contained Al and/or Cr,
Nb
5Si3 phases formed directly. It must be noticed that for
NbeTieCreSi alloy (alloy 8#, Fig. 3 (c)), Nb 3Si is still a pri-
mary component phase, only a small amount of Nb 5Si3 phase
occurs, as indicated by the circle.
4. Conclusions
1. Ti and Hf tend to stabilize Nb 3Si phase to lower tempera-
tures, while Cr and Al promote the direct formation of
Nb
5Si3 phase.
2. The microstructures of the multicomponent Nb eSi system
alloys essentially maintained the feature of the ternary al-
loys. Nb 5Si3 phases formed directly.
