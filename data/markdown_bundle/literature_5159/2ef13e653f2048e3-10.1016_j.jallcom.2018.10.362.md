Abstract 
Using the hot-pack rolling method, β-solidifying Ti-43Al-9V-0.2Y alloy sheets 
were rolled (thickness reduction: 85%) within the α(α2) + β + γ ternary-phase region at 
temperatures ranging from 1100 °C to 1200 °C. The m icrostructure and texture 
evolution occurring during dynamic recrystallizatio n of the γ phase and their effect on 
the mechanical properties were systematically inves tigated. After hot-rolling, residual 
lamellar structures of the as-forged TiAl alloy wer e completely broken down and the 
fraction of dynamically recrystallized grains incre ased significantly with increasing 
rolling temperature. Lower-temperature and higher-t emperature rolling were conducive 
for dynamic-recrystallization nucleation and growth , respectively, of the γ grains. At 
1100 °C, the precipitation of numerous fine (thickn ess: ~200 nm) γ laths from the 
β-phase ribbon was accompanied by the emergence of γ twins in the new grains. The

1. Introduction 
Intermetallic TiAl alloys have, in recent years, at tracted significant attention as a promising 
light-weight high-temperature structural material. Compared with conventional aerial materials, TiAl 
alloys satisfy the continuous demand for weight red uction and increased engine efficiency in 
aerospace and automobile industries and are, hence, expected to replace the nickel-based alloys [1-3]. 
However, these alloys suffer from various drawbacks including a narrow hot-processing window and 
poor room-temperature mechanical properties, which hinder their widespread use in industrial 
applications [4, 5]. To overcome the limited deform ability, a β-γ TiAl alloy combined with suitable 
thermo-mechanical processing methods (isothermal forging [6], canned extrusion [7], and hot-rolling 
[8, 9]) is developed through extensive alloying wit h β-stabilizing elements, including Nb, Mo, Cr, 
and V [3, 5]. The hot-pack rolling method constitut es an effective means of (i) homogenizing the

chemical composition and microstructure and (ii) ob taining additional complex components with 
improved mechanical properties [10-12]. Therefore, this method, for the β-solidifying TiAl alloys, 
may become a long-term topic of academic research a nd has significant potential for application to 
TiAl alloy sheets. 
Preparation of TiAl alloy sheets via conventional h ot-pack rolling is challenging, owing to the 
heat-loss, large stress, and high strain-rate sensi tivity [13]. Choosing appropriate rolling process 
parameters (such as rolling temperature, rolling sp eed and pass reduction) is crucial to obtaining 
high-quality alloy sheets with desirable microstruc ture and mechanical properties [14]. Various 
solid-state phase transformations, which are induced by cooling from the single-phase or multi-phase 
regions, can yield different microstructures and textures via multiple-temperature thermo-mechanical 
deformation [15-17]. Grain refinement is more prono unced in a specimen deformed at lower 
temperature than that in its counterpart deformed a t higher temperature [15]. Moreover, the high 
rolling temperature generates a coarser microstruct ure than that of the initial material, resulting in 
degradation of the performance [9]. The rolling par ameters also have a significant influence on the 
dynamic recrystallization (DRX) behavior and textur e evolution [18, 19]. High temperatures can 
promote the breakdown of lamellar structures and the occurrence of DRX [20]. Schillinger et al. [21] 
evaluated the texture evolution of the γ phase at different hot-rolling steps and proposed that the 
crystal orientation depends on recrystallization nu cleation and grain growth. Petra et al. [19] 
proposed four mechanisms that influence the texture of TNM alloy sheets hot-rolled at different 
phase-region temperatures. However, to date, γ-grain microstructural characteristics, 
recrystallization behavior, texture development, an d especially their effect on the mechanical 
properties of TiAl alloy sheets rolled at different temperatures in a single-phase region, remain only

Based on our previous study, the volume fraction of the β phase in Ti-43Al-9V-0.2Y alloy can

Furthermore, microstructure evolution (such as the formation of α2/γ structures) induced by cooling 
from the two-phase (α + γ) region can be prevented at these temperatures within α(α2) + β + γ region. 
The aim of the work is to evaluate the effect of ro lling temperature on the microstructure, texture, 
and mechanical properties of TiAl sheets. The under lying relationship between recrystallization 
nucleation-growth and hot-rolling temperature is also assessed. Existing traditional theories, together 
with data from microstructural, texture and mechani cal property characterization were used to 
determine the respective magnitudes of individual contributions. The presented findings are expected 
to further improve the design of high-performance TiAl alloy sheet manufacturing routes.

B. Dynamic-recrystallization behavior of the γ phase with increasing rolling temperature 
Owing to the high stacking fault energy of the diso rdered bcc β-phase and consequent rapid 
dynamic recovery at high temperature, a low defect density is maintained during deformation [15, 
28]. Therefore, this phase can provide a sufficient number of independent slip systems that 
accommodate significant plastic strain, and directl y act as a ductilizing constituent during

hot-deformation [29]. The SEM images show that the morphologies of the β phase (which is mainly 
distributed at γ grain boundaries) change only slightly after hot-r olling, as reported in previous 
studies [9, 18]. In contrast, the γ-grain morphology of the alloy sheets varies signif icantly with the 
rolling temperature. Therefore, the dynamic recryst allization of the γ phase at different rolling 
temperatures is analyzed in further detail. 
The GOS, which is based on the premise that the ori entation spread of dynamically 
recrystallized grains is lower than that of deforme d grains, has been successfully applied to the 
investigation of dynamic-recrystallization behavior [30]. Figure 5 shows the GOS distribution map 
of the γ phase in TiAl alloy sheets processed at different rolling temperatures. A multimodal 
distribution corresponding to partly recrystallized specimens is obtained in all three cases. The firs t 
prominent peak represents grains with a very small GOS value, which are considered dynamically 
recrystallized. Moreover, the number fraction value of the first peak increases with increasing rolling 
temperature, indicative of the rolling-temperature dependence of dynamic recrystallization (DRX).

 Application of this “cut-off” yields the area frac tions of recrystallized grains and deformed 
grains (see Fig. 6), where the blue regions in the maps represent grains with GOS values lower than

rolling temperature. At 1100 °C, low-GOS γ grains constitute only 16.8% of the microstructure and 
the dynamically recrystallized grains nucleate pred ominantly at the grain boundaries (see Fig. 6a).

corresponding values of most β-γ TiAl alloys [9, 18, 47]. The YS values decrease to 728 MPa 
(1150 °C) and 640 MPa (1200 °C) when the rolling te mperature is increased. In general, a 
fine-grained microstructure can lead to significant improvements in the strength of an alloy, owing to 
the increase in the fraction of grain or sub-grain boundaries [48, 49]. These boundaries render slip 
deformation (under the action of a shear stress) di fficult, resulting in the high strength of the allo ys 
(i.e., grain boundary strengthening) [48]. The SEM and EBSD results (Figs. 2 and 6) show that the 
number of new γ-nucleated nano-scaled laths and LAGBs increase markedly with decreasing rolling 
temperature. This increase indicates that the numbe r of grain and sub-grain boundaries have 
increased. Therefore, the fine microstructure formed during low-temperature rolling is beneficial for 
obtaining high-strength TiAl sheets [50]. In additi on, the TEM images show that a high-density of 
mechanical twins and dislocations are generated in the room-temperature tensile specimen (see Fig. 
9), indicating that dislocation motion and twinning are induced by a large stress. The twin 
boundaries can impede dislocation motion effectively, as shown in Fig. 9b and c. Therefore, the twin 
boundaries in original newly nucleated γ laths, which can impede dislocation motion [51], m ay also 
contribute to the high tensile strength of the alloy sheet rolled at 1100 °C.

B. Tensile properties at elevated temperature 
The tensile properties of the as-forged and hot-rol led Ti-43Al-9V-0.2Y alloys tested at 750 °C 
and a strain rate of 5×10 - 4 s- 1 are shown in Fig. 11. The tensile strength at elev ated temperature, 
associated with different temperature hot-rolling, is significantly higher than that of the as-forged 
alloy. Similar to the strength enhancement at room temperature, the high-temperature strength also 
increases significantly with decreasing rolling tem peratures. The alloy sheet rolled at lower 
temperature (1100 °C) exhibits higher strength (UTS: 550 MPa, YS: 480 MPa) than the sheets rolled 
at higher temperatures (1150 °C, UTS: 533MPa, YS: 4 34 MPa; 1200 °C, UTS: 480MPa, YS:

decreasing rolling temperature (from 1200 °C to 110 0 °C, see Fig. 11). This indicates that the 
elevated-temperature deformation mechanism of the a lloy sheets differs completely from the 
room-temperature mechanism. 
The TEM images of the high-temperature tensile spec imen, which is cut from the alloy sheet

4. Conclusions 
β-solidifying TiAl alloys with a nominal composition of Ti-43Al-9V-0.2Y are hot-rolled within 
α(α2) + β + γ region at temperatures ranging from 1100 °C to 120 0 °C. The microstructure and 
texture evolution occurring during DRX of the γ phase and their effect on the mechanical propertie s 
of the alloy sheets are investigated. Based on the obtained results, the following conclusions are 
reached: 
(1) The hot-pack rolling method results in complete fragmentation of the residual β/γ lamellar 
colonies, yielding finer microstructures than that of the as-forged alloy. During the 
processing of the β-γ TiAl alloy, massive nano-scaled γ laths (with twin structures) 
nucleate from the β-phase ribbon rolled at low temperature (1100 °C). These fine laths, 
which are generated (with a certain OR) via the β → γ phase transformation, coarsen with 
increasing rolling temperature. 
(2) Owing to the high density of dislocations and sub-s tructures, many nucleation sites for 
DRX of the γ phase are generated at 1100 °C. Although grain gro wth is favored at rolling 
temperatures higher than 1100 °C, the fraction of D RXed γ grains increases continuously 
with increasing rolling temperature. 
(3) The texture of the γ phase evolves significantly with the rolling tempe rature. Sharp 
recrystallization texture components, including the Cube and Goss where the 
crystallographic <001> directions are parallel to t he RD, dominate the texture formed at 
1100 °C. With increasing temperature, the texture i s gradually transformed into random 
deformation components (including orientations with <100>, <01

Research highlights 
1. Ti-43Al-9V-0.2Y alloy sheets are obtained via hot-pack rolling at various temperatures. 
2. At low rolling temperatures, massive nano-scaled γ laths nucleate from the β-phase. 
3. The fraction of dynamically recrystallized γ grains increases significantly with 
increasing rolling temperature. 
4. Texture evolution is strongly dependent on the hot-rolling temperature. 
5. Low-temperature rolling yields strength enhancement and excellent high-temperature 
ductility.
