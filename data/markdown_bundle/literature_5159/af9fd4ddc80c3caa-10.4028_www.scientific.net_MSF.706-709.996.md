Abstract. The authors have developed a new friction-stir welding (FSW) tool that enables to weld 
high-softening-temperature materials (HSTMs), such as steels, titanium and zirconium alloys. The 
new tool is made of a Co-based heat-resistant alloy strengthened by precipitating intermetallics, 
Co 3(Al,W), with a L1 2 structure at high temperatures. The Co-based alloy tool exhibits yield 
strengths higher than 500 MPa at 1000 deg C, so it might have a great potential as a tool material 
for FSW of HSTMs. In this study, the feasibility of using the Co-based alloy tool with various 
HSTMs was examined. Changes in the tool shape during FSW and the weld appearances produced 
with the Co-based alloy tool will be briefly shown. 
 
Introduction 
The tool material is critical in friction stir welding (FSW) of high-softening-temperature materials 
(HSTMs). The tool must maintain sufficient strength to constrain the material to be welded at the 
softening temperatures, and also be resistant to fatigue, fracture, mechanical wear, and chemical 
reactions with both the atmosphere and the weld material [1]. To date, two classes of materials have 
been found that meet these significant requirements: superabrasive tools and refractory-metal tools 
[1]. 
A superabrasive tool made of polycrystalline cubic boron nitride (PCBN) is one tool of proven 
applicability to the FSW of HSTMs. PCBN superabrasive tools usually exhibit little wear during 
FSW of mild steels. However, the repeated heat cycle accompanying the rapid heating and cooling 
during FSW often causes unexpected cracking on the tool surface due to the low fracture toughness 
of the superabrasive material, which eventually leads to chipping of the cracked region. Therefore, 
it is very difficult to predict the durability of the PCBN tool. 
The W-Re tool is well known as a representative refractory-metal tool. It enables the welding of 
Ti/Ti alloys and relatively thick steel plates. A W-Re tool exhibits good fracture toughness 
compared with superabrasive tools. However, it often shows a tendency to wear rapidly. To make up 
for the disadvantages of the two classes of tool materials, a composite tool material consisting of

PCBN and W-Re powders has also been recently developed. This composite material has relatively 
good wear resistance and fracture toughness [2]. However, the composite tool is too costly to apply 
to most consumer industries, so its usage might be limited to a few applications such as in defense 
industries. 
The authors have focused on the research and development of a new FSW tool suitable for HSTMs, 
considering the combination of properties at high temperature and manufacturing methods while 
being cost conscious. The properties and cost of the tool depend on both the composition elements 
and the manufacturing method. 
The authors have developed a tool made of a new Co-based alloy, which can be obtained at a 
relatively moderate price compared with Re. This alloy is strengthened by precipitating 
intermetallics Co 3(Al,W) with a L1 2 structure, which exhibits a yield strength higher than 500 MPa 
at 1000˚C. Since the new Co-based alloy can be designed as a cast alloy, this tool can be 
manufactured at a low cost through a simple production method consisting of casting, 
heat-treatment, and then machining. In this paper, a feasibility study was conducted for several 
HSTMs using the new Co-based alloy tool. Changes in the tool shape during FSW of HSTMs and 
the weld configuration are briefly introduced. 
 
Co-based heat-resistance alloy 
Co and Ni are metallurgically similar. In general, however, it is known that Co-based alloys show 
lower high-temperature strength than Ni-based superalloys, because the ordered intermetallic 
phases with the L1 
2 structure effective for high-temperature strengthening are believed to be stable 
only in Ni. 
However, a ternary phase Co 3(Al,W) with the L1 2 structure, designated as γ’, was found in the 
Co-Al-W ternary system by the co-authors in 2006 [3]. The coherent γ/γ’ microstructure of 
Co-Al-W alloy is morphologically identical to that of a Ni-based superalloy [3,4]. The new 
Co-based alloy exhibits higher high-temperature strengths than a Ni-based superalloy, i.e., higher 
than 500 MPa at 1000˚C, which is probably due to the higher strength of the Co 3(Al,W) γ’ phase at 
high temperatures compared to Ni 3Al γ’ phase [5]. Additionally, the Co-based alloys are generally 
used in wear-resistant components at room and high temperatures. The high strength and good wear 
resistance suggest that the new Co-based alloy with the γ/γ’ microstructure might be suitable as a 
FSW tool for HSTMs. Moreover, a near net-shape tool can be manufactured through a simple 
method consisting of a lost-wax process, suited to mass production of the tool. 
 
Experimental procedures 
A FSW tool was made of the Co-based alloy strengthened by γ’ Co 
3(Al,W) with the L1 2 structure. 
28 rods of the new Co-based alloy were produced by a lost-wax process (Fig. 1(a)) and then 
heat-treated to obtain the γ’ Co 3(Al,W) precipitates in the γ matrix. The tool was shaped from the 
new Co-based alloy by a conventional machining process (Fig. 1(b)). The tool used was composed 
of a shoulder with a 15 mm diameter and an unthreaded probe with a length of 1.7 mm. The probe 
was tapered from 6 mm at the shoulder to 3.5 mm at the probe tip. FSW was conducted with 3 kinds

Results and discussion 
Mild steel. The weld appearance of mild steel is presented in Fig. 2(a). A smooth and shiny surface 
on mild steel weld was obtained using the new Co-based alloy tool. This preliminary trial revealed 
that the tool exhibited a great potential in mild steel. In order to assess performance as an FSW tool, 
the durability of the Co-based alloy tool was examined. The appearance of the tool tip before and 
after several weld lengths is shown in Fig. 2(b). The tool shape hardly changes after 0.7 m FSW. No 
large variation of the tool shape was seen at a weld length less than 10 m. The edge of the shoulder 
and the probe showed slight wear and deformation after 13.3 m of welding, beyond which both the 
shoulder wear and the probe deformation became prominent, as shown in the tool appearance after 
45.7 m of FSW. 
A cross-section of the tool after 45.7 m of FSW is shown in Fig. 3(a). The peripheries of the probe 
and shoulder were obviously worn during FSW. Optical micrographs of the shoulder periphery and 
the probe tip are shown in Fig. 3(b) and (c), respectively. The as-cast microstructure was deformed 
near the surface where the mild steel contacted the probe during FSW, which implies that the tool 
wear occurred mechanically. Moreover, cracking is found at the root of the probe. It is found that 
the cracking propagates along the solidification cell boundaries. Even though the cracking occurs,

Summary 
In this study, the feasibility of a cost-efficient Co-based alloy tool with a γ/γ’ microstructure was 
briefly shown for some HSTMs. The new Co-based alloy does not contain any expensive alloying 
elements, and a tool can be manufactured from it through a lost-wax process suited to mass 
production. The preliminary FSW trials established that the tool successfully produced welds in 
some HSTMs without large variations of tool shape. Currently, a FSW trial of thick steel plates and 
optimization of the chemical composition of the Co-based alloy are ongoing. Those results will be 
reported in fut ure papers.
