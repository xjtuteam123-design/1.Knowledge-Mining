Keywords: Nb-Ti-Cr-Al alloy, as cast structure, low-density super alloy, Nb based alloy 
Abstract. Due to low-density, higher elevated temperature st rength and good oxidation resistance; 
the Nb-Ti-Cr-Al alloy was estimated to become a new kind of material used at elevated temperature 
of next generation. Therefore, it was widely studied by western developed country, but there are still 
many questions unsolved in this area. In this article, several kinds of Nb-Ti-Cr-Al alloys of different 
compositions were prepared by arc melting, and analyzed by metallographic method, XRD, SEM and 
EDS. The results indicated, in Nb-40Ti-15Al alloy, the solidified Structure was typical small 
equiaxed grains, columnar crystals and common equiaxed crystals. And in Nb-40Ti-10Cr alloy, there 
is a serious coring segregation of Cr element; it c an induce intergranular fracture of ingot. As the 
content of Cr element increased, constitutional sup ercooling occurred in solidification process, and 
the microstruture changed from straight crystal boundary to dendrite, such as Nb-40Ti-20Cr alloy. As 
for Nb-40Ti-10Cr-10Al alloy, the addition of Al and Cr lead to a great number of sub boundary and 
precipitated phase, and the microsegregation in alloy are also reduced to a certain degree. To sum up, 
the content level of Cr and Al is essential to both the microstructure and mechanical performances of 
Nb-Ti based alloys. 
Introduction 
As the development of the aviation and aerospace in dustry, a great deal of academic research 
and effort were invested in materials research by the desire to produce advanced aerospace structures 
and propulsion systems, especially the gas turbine engine. In general, advanced turbine must operate 
at higher temperatures and using lighter materials at the same temperature to achieve a greater 
thrust-to-weight ratio. But the dominated Ni-based superalloys used in hot section of aerospace 
turbine have reached the upper temperature limit of possible application. Refractory metals and 
notably Chromium, Molybdenum, Tungsten, Niobium and Tantalum have very high melting 
temperatures and low coefficients of thermal expans ion, so alloys and intermetallic compounds of 
refractory metals are the most possible candidates to replace Ni-based alloy, especially Niobium [1]. 
With relatively low density, high melting temperatu res of 2468 ℃ and excellent ductility, the 
applications of Nb in area of super alloy were paid a great attention from the middle of the last 
century, a great deal of Nb based alloys were inve nted and used in aviation and aerospace industry, 
such as Nb-10Hf and Nb-1Zr alloys etc[2].. In this kind of alloys, Nb521 (matrix is Nb and the W，
Mo and Zr as elements of solid solution) is the m ost successful one and it can be used at 
ultra-temperature near 1600 ℃. However, the poor oxidation resistance at elevate d temperature 
handicap the development of Nb based alloy greatly, almost all Nb based alloys need a surface 
coating to avoid atmospheric attack. Besides, the additions of W, Mo and Hf increase the weight and 
the cost of alloys. In 90`s, Nb-Ti based alloy attract great attentions of material engineers because Nb 
and Ti can form solid solution in any proportions and the potential low density and good mechanical 
performance. But when environmental temperature is higher than 1000 ℃, the creep resistance of 
Nb-Ti based alloy drops greatly and this kind of p henomenon is called super plastic[3]. By adding 
elements of solution reinforcement mainly Al , Cr a nd Si, the Nb-Ti based alloy can be used at 
1000 ℃-1400 ℃[4,5]. However, this kind of alloys usually exhibit ed a very limited ductility at 
ambient temperature. And the ductility is relying heavily on the amount of additions of Al, Cr and Si 
in alloys [6, 7, 8, and 9]. So relations between ductility and contents of Cr, Al and Si are important to

Material and experimental procedures 
Raw material of 99.9 weight percent (wt %) Nb powder, 99.9 wt. % Ti powder and 99.9 wt. % Ti 
powder were used in arc melting and casting process . Mix these powder together according 
nominated chemical compositions. The melting process conducted in a water-cooled copper crucible 
in an arc melting chamber backfilled with argon at 0.5 MPa. The buttons (cast) were remelted 2-3 
times to ensure homogeneity, invert the casting each time before remelting. 
Four typical Nb-Ti-Cr-Al alloys were studied in thi s article. The nominated chemical 
compositions of these alloys are respectively Nb-40 Ti-15Al, Nb-40Ti-10Cr, Nb-40Ti-20Cr and 
Nb-40Ti-10Cr-10Al (compositions in atom percent). 
An OLYMPUS PMG3 optical microscope and a Rigaku D/M ARX2200PC X-ray diffraction 
analyzer were used to observe microstructure and analyze the aberrance of the lattice. On a Shimadzu 
SSX-550 scanning electron microscope, the experimen ts of SEM and EDS were conducted and the 
micro segregations of alloys were studied. Metallog raphic samples for OM are polished and etched 
with a 25 vol pct HNO

The pictures of four typical Nb-Ti-Cr-Al cast alloy s are showed in Fig.1. Obviously, the 
Nb-40Ti-15Al cast alloy is a representative single- phase equiaxed crystal. At triple junctions of 
adjacent grains, the angles of two grain boundaries are almost at 120°.It indicates that the neighbor 
grains own the same interface energy, hence a homog eneous single-phase alloy. Following the 
ASTM standard E112, the grain size was calculated to be 250 µm-300 µm. Because large grains lead 
to poor yield strength and high DBTT (ductile-britt le transition temperature) according the 
Hall-Petch relationship, this kind of solidification structures leaves much to be desired. As for the cast 
microstructure of Nb-40Ti-10Cr alloy, it is also a single-phase equiaxed crystal. The difference is the 
clearly and densely sub-grain boundaries. In Nb-40Ti-20Cr alloy ，a microstructure consisted of bulky 
dendrites can be distinguished. It can be attribute d to constitutional supercooling. Because 
undercooling increases with the content increase of Cr and Al, a structure of directional columnar 
crystals is obtained in this alloy. But the Nb-40Ti -10Cr-10Al alloy is still straight crystal boundary 
and densely sub-grains boundaries because the critical undercooling was not reached.

The Fig.3 is SEM (scanning electron microscopy) pho tograph of cast Nb-40Ti-15Al alloy. In 
Fig.3, the whole sample is homogenous and sprinkled with some small blow holes and some bright 
protuberances. These blow holes can be explained du e to porous samples of pressed from mixed 
metals powder and it formed the blow holes in proce ss of casting. The photograph of EDS (energy 
dispersive spectrometer, surface scanning) at one o f these protuberances is showed in the Fig.4. By 
analysis the element concentration profiles of Al, Ti, Nb and O, we found that the sample is enriched 
in Al but depleted in Nb and Ti at the bright protu berances. So we can identified it is globular 
segregation of Al. It is two major minting errors i n Nb-Ti-Al alloy. The origin of this kind of 
segregation can be attributed to the huge deference of melting points between Al and NbTi alloy. 
These defects can be eliminated by multiple melting processes, but the evaporation loss of Al is also 
increased with the times of melting.

Summary 
1) As the increase of content of Cr in Nb-Ti-Cr-Al alloy, the microstructure changed from 
equiaxed crystal to dendrite because of constitutional supercooling. 
2) The lattice parameters of Nb-Ti-Cr-Al alloys are mainly determined by Al. And Al has stronger 
solution reinforcement effect than Cr. But as the content of Al increased, the ductility of Nb-Ti-Cr-Al 
alloys at ambient temperature decreased. 
3) In Nb-Ti-Cr-Al alloys, the element of Cr is enri ched in grain boundary. This kind of 
grain-boundary segregation has a severe effect on mechanical performances of material. 
4) For Nb-Ti-Cr-Al alloys, the contents of Cr and A l are an essential factor to material 
performance. The contents of Cr+Al shouldn’t exceed 20% atom percent in materials design of this 
kind, or else the alloy change to be brittle and casting defects increase greatly.
