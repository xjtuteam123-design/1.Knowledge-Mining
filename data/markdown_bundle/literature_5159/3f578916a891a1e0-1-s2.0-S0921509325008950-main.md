ABSTRACT
The synergistic effects of rhenium (Re) addition on the microstructure and mechanical properties of a 
precipitation-strengthened Co-Ni-based superalloy was systematically investigated. Current Co-Ni-based super -
alloys face challenges in balancing strength and ductility, with excessive Re often inducing detrimental topo -
logically close-packed (TCP) phases. To address this, alloys with Re contents ranging from 0 to 3 wt% were 
designed, synthesized via vacuum arc melting, and subjected to thermomechanical processing and aging. Results 
demonstrate that 2 wt% Re optimizes strength-ductility synergy, achieving an ultimate tensile strength of 1250 
MPa and elongation of 24 %. Mechanistic analysis reveals that Re enhances solid solution strengthening by 
amplifying γ / γ
ʹ 
lattice mismatch (from 0.26 % to 0.78 %), promoting interfacial dislocation networks. Concur -
rently, Re indirectly drives solute redistribution to grain boundaries, forming cohesive solute-rich layers that 
suppress intergranular cracking. Furthermore, Re lowers stacking fault energy, facilitating dislocation dissoci -
ation and Suzuki segregation to enhance plasticity. However, excessive Re (i.e. 3 wt%) triggers μ -phase (TCP) 
precipitation at grain boundaries, degrading strength (1100 MPa) and ductility (20 %). This study clarifies Re ’ s 
dual role in Co-Ni-based superalloys, establishes a critical Re threshold (~2 wt%), and provides guidelines for 
designing advanced alloys with balanced high-temperature performance and microstructural stability.
1. Introduction
Superalloys have been widely applied in aerospace and gas turbine 
industries due to their exceptional high-temperature capabilities, 
including outstanding thermal resistance, ductility, fatigue resistance, 
and oxidation resistance under elevated temperature conditions [ 1 – 5 ].
Over recent decades, researchers have persistently pursued the 
strategic incorporation of elevated concentrations of refractory alloying 
elements (e.g., Mo, W, Ta, Re) [ 6 – 10 ] into superalloys, aiming to 
enhance creep resistance and phase stability. Yao et al. [ 11 ] proposed a 
γ -matrix strengthening mechanism in single-crystal superalloy via Ru 
and Re co-alloying. Wu et al. [ 12 ] demonstrated that Re addition 
doubled the creep rupture life in second-generation Ni-based 
single-crystal superalloys, revealed Re enrichment at partial dislocations 
and crystallographic defects during creep deformation based on the 
atom probe tomography analysis. Pandey et al. [ 50 ] demonstrated that 
Re addition significantly alters the partitioning behavior of Mo in 
Co-Ni-Al-Mo-Nb alloys. 2 wt% Re reduced γ / γ
ʹ 
lattice misfit by around 
40 %, driving a morphological transition of γ
ʹ 
precipitates from spherical 
to rounded cuboidal shapes, while 6 wt% Re extended the creep rupture 
life from 250 h to 2500 h. However, excessive Re introduction risks 
promoting detrimental topologically close-packed (TCP) phase forma -
tion [ 13 ], thereby accelerating alloy degradation. Therefore, the rein -
forcing effect of Re exhibits a strong dependence paradox on its addition 
level. Since Giamei ’ s seminal discovery of the “ Re effect ” [ 14 ], extensive 
investigations [ 15 – 18 ] have focused on resolving Re ’ s atomic-scale 
partitioning behavior and its mechanistic interplay with mechanical 
properties. Nevertheless, the underlying mechanisms governing Re ’ s 
dual role remain debated within the superalloy community.

3.3. Element distribution at grain boundaries
The nanoscale elemental distribution in the alloy was characterized 
via scanning transmission electron microscopy (STEM). The elemental 
segregation behavior at grain boundaries (GBs) was observed to inves -
tigate the effect of Re addition on atomic migration at interfacial re -
gions. The STEM-EDS elemental maps of GBs of 0Re and 2Re alloys were 
compared ( Figs. 5 and 6 ).
It is evident that no significant elemental segregation occurs at grain 
boundaries (GBs) in the 0Re alloy. In contrast, the 2Re alloy exhibits 
significant segregation of Co (2nd line in Fig. 6 , purple), Cr (3rd line in 
Fig. 6 , red), Mo (5th line in Fig. 6 , yellow), and Nb (6th line in Fig. 6 , 
green) at the grain boundaries (GBs), while Re segregation is less pro -
nounced (as shown in the inset of Fig. 6 illustrating segregation in -
tensity, represented by the last line, blue). This segregation does not 
result in precipitated secondary phases but instead forms a solute- 
enriched layer that acts as a “ glue, ” pinning GBs and suppressing GB 
sliding. Such solute segregation strengthens GBs and mitigates weak- 
boundary failure. Notably, Re itself does not heavily segregate to GBs, 
consistent with its high lattice solubility in the γ matrix (attributed to 
strong covalent interactions between Re ’ s d-orbitals and Ni ’ s d-orbitals, 
which stabilize Re ’ s solid solution in the γ phase [ 30 , 31 ]). The addition 
of Re drives the redistribution of other solute elements toward GBs.

4. Discussion
The “ Re Effect ” in precipitation-strengthened Co-Ni-based alloys is a 
multifaceted phenomenon rooted in Re ’ s unique atomic characteristics 
and its interactions with the alloy ’ s microstructure. Below, we analyze 
its mechanisms and implications based on experimental observations 
and theoretical insights in Co-35Ni-17.5Cr-4Mo-3Nb-2Al-1.6Fe-0.8Ti- 
Re alloys.
4.1. Solid solution strengthening and lattice mismatch
The addition of Re induces pronounced solid solution strengthening 
primarily within the γ matrix, as evidenced by XRD-derived lattice 
constant evolution ( Fig. 3 a). Re atoms, with their large atomic radius 
(1.97 Å, much larger than the atomic radius of Ni 1.24 Å and Al 1.43 Å), 
generate substantial lattice distortion in the γ phase, impeding disloca -
tion glide. Notably, since Re preferentially occupies Al sites rather than 
Ni in the γ
ʹ 
phase, this behavior exacerbates the solubility contrast of Re 
between the γ matrix and γ
ʹ 
phase. Such partitioning drives a significant 
increase in the γ / γ
ʹ 
lattice misfit ( δ ) from 0.26 % (0Re) to 0.78 % (3Re) 
( Fig. 3 b), resulting in dense interfacial dislocation networks [ 33 ]. 
Concurrently, Ding et al. [ 34 ] demonstrated that Re segregation near 
interfacial dislocation networks (particularly at dislocation cores) cre -
ates a glue-like effect, hindering crack nucleation during phase shearing 
and thereby enhancing matrix ductility. Additionally, trace Re incor -
poration into the γ
ʹ 
phase elevates the antiphase boundary energy, 
increasing resistance to dislocation shear [ 35 ], which further contrib -
utes to solid solution strengthening.
4.2. Grain boundary segregation and stability
In crystalline solids, solute atoms preferentially segregate to 
extended defects such as dislocations and grain boundaries (GBs) 
through chemical and mechanical interactions, a phenomenon critical to 
the stability and performance of γ / γ
ʹ 
microstructures in superalloys. 
While Re itself does not segregate to grain boundaries apparently ( Figs. 5 
and 6 ), it indirectly modulates solute redistribution. Neumeier et al. [ 36 ] 
investigated the diffusion behavior of transition group metals in fcc Co 
using first principles and experimental validation, which provided 
important evidence for elemental migration in Co-Ni-based alloys. Re is 
the slowest diffusing element in fcc Co among the investigated elements. 
The electronic structure calculations indicate that this is caused by 
strong directional bonds between Re and neighboring Co atoms. The 
interdiffusion coefficients of the elements with fcc-Co calculated from 
first principles (at their respective content concentrations) Nb > Ti > Al 
> Fe > Cr > Mo > Re, which also indicate that the diffusion of gam -
ma ’ -forming elements (Nb, Ti, Al) is more frequent than that of 
solid-solution strengthening elements (Fe, Cr, Mo) in fcc-Co. However, 
in this experimental study, the addition of Re has little effect on the 
morphology and content of the γ
ʹ 
phase (gamma prime phase), and no 
significant diffusion behavior of the γ
ʹ 
phase-forming elements (e.g., Al, 
Ti, etc.) is observed.
The lattice distortion induced by Re generates severe localized stress 
fields, which suppress the diffusion of other elements within the matrix 
[ 10 , 37 , 38 ]. This forces solute atoms to reduce the system energy pri -
marily through segregation near grain boundaries. These solute-rich 
zones may drive the transformation of grain boundaries from a disor -
dered state to semi-coherent interfaces. Concurrently, the solute atom 
layers further pin the grain boundaries, enhancing their strength. 
Moreover, the presence of solute-enriched grain boundary regions re -
duces local chemical potential gradients, thereby diminishing the ten -
dency for harmful phases (e.g., TCP phases) to precipitate [ 39 ]. This 
mechanism effectively avoids ductility loss caused by brittle intergran -
ular phases.
4.3. Segregation at band of stacking fault
Co-Ni-based superalloys inherently exhibit low stacking fault energy 
(SFE), leading to abundant stacking fault (SF) formation. The addition of 
Re further reduces the SFE of the γ matrix [ 40 , 41 ]. In 2Re alloy, distinct 
SF features are observed: intersecting “ linear ” striations ( Fig. 12 a), 
indicative of intrinsic/extrinsic stacking faults. A Lomer-Cottrell lock 
structure is also identified ( Fig. 12 b), with HRTEM confirming its as -
sociation with SFs. Elemental mapping across SF regions ( Fig. 12 d and e) 
reveals pronounced Suzuki segregation of Re (green, 2nd line), Mo

through controlled SF shearing. The synergy between SF shearing and 
segregation-assisted strengthening enhances alloy strength while 
maintaining ductility.
4.4. Mechanical properties
The influence of Re addition on the mechanical properties of alloys is 
a combined result of solid solution strengthening, grain boundary 
segregation, and stacking fault segregation. Under room-temperature 
tensile conditions, these synergistic effects initially enhance plasticity 
with increasing Re content ( Fig. 10 a), as evidenced by the fracture 
morphology variations observed at different Re levels ( Fig. 11 ). Within 
the limits of its solubility, Re atoms primarily participate in the redis -
tribution of other solutes at grain boundaries, thereby mitigating the

limited. At low Re concentrations, the improvement in intragranular 
strength is limited, but the alloy plasticity increases significantly. This 
phenomenon arises from the combined effects of lattice distortion- 
induced intragranular strengthening (due to Re atom incorporation) 
and the weakening of solid solution strengthening caused by solute 
segregation at grain boundaries. These opposing contributions to 
strength cancel each other, while solute segregation at grain boundaries 
significantly enhances the alloy ’ s plasticity, resulting in unchanged 
strength but improved ductility. Conversely, excessive Re addition ( > 2 
wt%) leads to a substantial reduction in strength. This phenomenon can 
be attributed to the elevated tendency of Re to promote the precipitation 
of topologically close-packed (TCP) phases (e.g., μ -phase and σ -phase). 
Continuous precipitation of TCP phases at grain boundaries disrupts 
matrix continuity and initiates crack formation. Additionally, recent 
first-principles studies by Zhu et al. [ 46 ]. revealed that when Re content 
exceeds 2 %, Re segregation at γ / γ
ʹ 
interfaces facilitates the formation of 
localized Ni4Re or Ni8Re-type structures ( Fig. 4 d, d1, 4d2). The prolif -
eration of these structures depletes Re atoms from the solid solution 
matrix, thereby diminishing the efficacy of solid solution strengthening 
and negatively impacting the performance of superalloys. This mecha -
nism provides a clear explanation for the performance degradation 
observed in 3Re alloys.
This Re-induced enhancement of strength and plasticity has also 
been validated under high-temperature conditions ( Fig. 10 b). The low 
diffusion coefficient of Re significantly inhibits the diffusion of solid 
solution strengthening elements, thereby maintaining microstructural 
stability. Simultaneously, Re segregation at stacking faults enhances 
high-temperature creep resistance by pinning dislocations, reducing 
dynamic recovery, and prolonging the plastic deformation stage. How -
ever, the strength decline under high-temperature conditions can be 
further mitigated by adding Ru. The “ reverse partitioning effect ” of Ru 
suppresses TCP phase formation at elevated temperatures, making solid 
solution strengthening more pronounced. Even under low-Re condi -
tions, Ru addition can inhibit γ
ʹ 
phase coarsening, further optimizing 
high-temperature microstructural stability [ 11 ].
In summary, the addition of Re enables the Co-35Ni-17.5Cr-4Mo- 
3Nb-2Al-1.6Fe-0.8Ti alloy to exhibit significantly enhanced mechani -
cal properties, effectively mitigating its inherent plasticity deficiency. 
We propose that this performance improvement arises from the syner -
gistic effects of solid-solution strengthening, grain boundary segrega -
tion, and stacking fault segregation — mechanisms schematically 
summarized in Fig. 13 .
5. Conclusion
This study systematically investigates the influence of Re on the 
microstructure and mechanical properties of a novel Co-Ni-based su -
peralloy. Experimental and analytical results demonstrate that Re 
addition synergistically enhances both strength and ductility, with 
optimal performance achieved at 2 % Re (ultimate tensile strength: 
1260 MPa, elongation: 24 %). Key conclusions are summarized as 
follows: 
I Re predominantly resides in the γ matrix, while trace amounts 
partition into the γ
ʹ 
phase.
II Strengthening arises from the combined effects of solid solution 
hardening, GB segregation, and stacking fault segregation,
III Re preferentially enhancing GB plasticity through solute 
redistribution.
IV Excessive Re ( > 2 %) promotes μ -phase (TCP) precipitation at 
GBs, which acts as crack nucleation sites, degrading mechanical 
properties.
