2Hf- x V- y Zr (( x , y ) = (0, 0), (3, 0), (0, 4) and (3, 4)) (at.%) were prepared by vacuum non-consumable arc

◦
C of the 
alloys have been investigated. The results show that the 0V – 0Zr alloy is eutectic structure of Nb
SS

5

5

5

1. Introduction 
To develop a new type of environmentally friendly materials is a key 
way to solve most environmental problems, and thus attracts wide -
spread attentions of researchers [ 1 ]. It is well known that the global 
warming, one of the most serious environmental problems in the world, 
is partly caused by CO
2 
gas emissions of aircraft engines. Among 
numerous ways to reduce these CO
2 
gas emissions, improving the 
thermal efficiency of aircraft engines has received widespread concern, 
since it can simultaneously improve the aircraft performance [ 1 – 4 ]. This 
generally requires the hot section materials of aircraft engines can serve 
at higher temperatures as well as possess lower density [ 1 – 4 ]. Among 
the high temperature structure materials, Nb – Si based in situ composites 
which possess relatively lower density (6.6 – 7.2 g/cm
3
) and outstanding 
mechanical properties at 1200 – 1400 
◦
C (it has exceeded the limited 
working temperatures of third-generation nickel-based superalloys used 
widely at present, 1150 
◦
C) have attracted much attentions as one of 
potential alternatives to be applied in the aeroengines [ 2 , 3 , 5 , 6 ]. How -
ever, their low room temperature fracture toughness and poor oxidation 
resistance restrict the industrial application of Nb – Si based alloys [ 2 , 3 , 5 , 
6 ]. Alloying is an effective way to improve and balance the compre -
hensive performances of Nb – Si based ultrahigh temperature alloys, 
because the contents, morphologies and distribution characteristics of 
ductile Nb
SS 
(Niobium Solid Solution) and brittle silicides in the alloys 
are sensitive to the types and contents of element additions, which in 
turn affects the comprehensive properties of Nb – Si based alloys [ 7 ]. In 
our previous works [ 8 – 14 ], the effects of Ti, Cr, Al and Hf on Nb – Si 
based alloys have been investigated and a multi-elemental 
Nb – Ti – Si – Cr – Al-Hf system with well-balanced comprehensive perfor -
mances has been established [ 8 ]. Nevertheless, their properties are still 
far away from the requirement of industrial applications, thus it is 
necessary to continuously optimize Nb – Si based alloy ’ s compositions by 
further alloying. 
In recent years, the additions of V and Zr have been concerned by 
researchers due to the positive effects on both mechanical properties and 
oxidation resistance of Nb – Si based alloys [ 4 , 15 – 22 ]. For instance, Guo 
et al. [ 16 ] reported that adding 1 at.% V in the alloys of 
Nb – 15Si – 24Ti – 4Cr – 2Al-2Hf improved both the room temperature

2. Experimental procedures 
Based on our previous works and the published reports [ 15 , 18 , 
23 – 26 ], the promising addition content of V was around 3 at.%, while 
the optimum content of Zr was around 4 at.% in Nb – Si based alloy 
systems in consideration of both oxidation resistance and mechanical 
properties. Therefore, in the present work, four Nb – Si based alloys with 
compositions of Nb – 22Ti – 15Si – 5Cr – 3Al-2Hf- x V- y Zr (( x , y ) = (0, 0), (3, 
0), (0, 4) and (3, 4)) (at.%) were prepared by vacuum non-consumable 
arc melting under an argon atmosphere in a water cooled copper cru -
cible. For convenience, the four alloys are referred to 0V – 0Zr, 3V – 0Zr, 
0V – 4Zr and 3V – 4Zr here after in this paper, respectively. Four button 
ingots were melted at least 4 times. In order to ensure the same external 
conditions during solidification, the similar key parameters of furnace 
(argon pressure of furnace chamber and circulating water pressure in the 
copper crucible is 0.03 MPa and 0.25 MPa, respectively) were used 
during each melting process. The diameters, thickness and mass of in -
gots were about 66 mm, 23 mm and 200 g, respectively. 
Three cubic samples with dimensions of 7 × 7 × 7 mm
3 
were cut 
from each ingot by electro discharge machining (EDM). One of them was 
polished for microstructure analysis. Two others were placed in alumina 
crucibles and then put in a open-ended tube electric furnace for oxida -
tion tests at 1250 
◦
C for 1 and 20 h, respectively. In order to carryout the 
oxidation test, the specimen was initially heated to 450 
◦
C with a heating 
rate of 10 K/min. Further, the heating was continued till 1250 
◦
C at a 
rate of 27 K/min. At 1250 
◦
C, the specimen was kept for 1 or 20 h fol -
lowed by cooling to room temperature at the rate of 5 K/min. The di -
mensions of samples were measured by a vernier caliper with an 
accuracy of 0.02 mm, the mass gains of the alloys were measured by an 
electronic balance with an accuracy of 0.0001 g. 
An electric universal mechanical testing machine (Instron-3382) was 
used for single-edge notched bending test to obtain the room tempera -
ture fracture toughness of the alloys. The single-edge notched bending 
samples with dimensions of 30 × 5 × 2.5 mm
3 
were cut by EDM and the 
depth of notch was 2.5 ± 0.1 mm. The cross-head speed was 0.2 mm/ 
min in the tests. The K
Q 
value of room temperature fracture toughness 
was calculated by a formula mentioned in Ref. [ 27 ] and were the 
average of three samples. HXP-1000TM hardness machine was applied 
to obtain microhardness of constituent phases in the alloys. Because of 
the different sizes of Nb
SS 
and primary silicide blocks, two indenter loads 
of 0.98 N and 0.25 N for holding 15s are applied in the microhardness 
test of primary silicides and Nb
SS
, respectively. The microhardness 
values of phases were average of at least 5 test points. Compressive yield 
strength at 1250 
◦
C of the alloys was measured by Gleeble 3500 testing 
machine. The sample was a cylinder with dimensions of 8 mm in 
diameter and 12 mm in length. The strain rate was 0.005 s
 1 
and the 
height reduction was 30%. In order to obtain accurate data, the samples 
for above tests were taken from the same position in each ingot and then 
grounded up to 2000 grit by SiC paper for removing both surface 
metamorphic layers of EDM and roughness effects of surfaces. 
The constituent phases of alloys and oxidation products in the scales 
were identified by X-ray diffraction analysis (XRD, Panalytical X ’ Pert 
Pro, Cu K α ), and their chemical compositions were measured by energy 
dispersive spectroscopy (EDS, Inca X-sight). The microstructures of al -
loys were observed by scanning electron microscopy (SEM, Tescan Vega 
3 LMU). Quantitative metallographic analysis and size of constituent 
phases in the alloys were measured by high resolution imaging analysis 
software of Image-Pro Plus version 6.0, at least 5 SEM images were 
explored.

Si

5
Si

Eutectic II in multi-elemental Nb – Si based alloys.
