ABSTRACT
The improvement of overall performance of high-temperature materials is still a big challenge. By means of
ﬁrst-principles approach, in this work, we systematically investigate the e ﬀect of transition metals (TM=Mo and
Re) on the strength and high-temperature thermodynamic properties of NbSi 2 with C 40 structure. The phonon
dispersions, Debye temperature and heat capacity of NbSi 2 with alloying elements are calculated in detail. We
predict that alloying elements of Mo and Re are thermodynamically and dynamically stable. These alloying
elements prefer to occupy the Si site in comparison to Nb site. We further ﬁnd that the compression strength of
NbSi2 with alloying element of Re is up to 15.3% in comparison to perfect NbSi 2. Importantly, alloying elements
at Si site result in brittle-to-ductile transition for NbSi 2. The heat capacity of NbSi 2 with alloying elements is
slightly larger than that of NbSi 2.
1. Introduction
Among all the high-temperature ceramic materials, Nb-based
silicides have received great attention because of their high melting-
point ( > 2023 K), low density, high-temperature strength and excellent
oxidation resistance etc. [1–8]. The microstructure and mechanical
properties of Nb-based silicides have been widely investigated over the
last years [9–11]. However, the mechanical properties of Nb-based
silicides are mainly in ﬂuenced by Si– Si covalent bond and Nb –Si bond
[12–14]. Therefore, Si concentration plays an important role in
improving the mechanical properties. Recently, we have reported that
the Young ′s modulus and Vickers hardness of NbSi
2 are 339 GPa and
27.5 GPa [15], respectively, which are larger than the corresponding
elastic modulus for other Nb-based silicides. In addition, Nakano et al.
[16] have investigated the deformation behavior of NbSi 2 with C 40
structure. In particular, NbSi 2 exhibits high melting-point, excellent
oxidation resistance and suitable electrical conductivity etc [17–20].
These results indicate that NbSi 2 is a potential ultrahigh-temperature
ceramic candidate for the application of gas turbines and turbine
blades.
However, the strength and thermal stability of high-temperature
materials play a crucial role in the high-temperature environment.
Importantly, keeping the balance of mechanical properties and
thermal stability is still a big cha l l e n g e .T h e r e f o r e ,i ti sn e c e s s a r y
to explore new method to improve the overall performance of high-
temperature material. As we known, alloying design is an eﬀ ective
approach to enhance the overall performance. For example, alloying
additions of W, Mo and Si can e ﬀectively improve the high-
temperature strength of Nb-based silicides [21,22] .C re l e m e n t
improves the oxidati on resistance of Nb-based silicides [23,24] .
Although the mechanical properties and oxidation resistance of
NbSi
2 are studied [25], it is important to further improve the
mechanical properties of NbSi 2. On the other hand, the high-
temperature thermodynamic properties of NbSi 2 are unknown. As
a result, the e ﬀect of alloying elements on thermodynamic properties
of NbSi 2 is not well understood.
In the present paper, we investigate the in ﬂuence of alloying
elements on the structural stability, elastic properties and thermo-
dynamic properties of NbSi 2. Considering the d-orbit e ﬀect, we select
the alloying elements of 4 d- Mo and 5 d- Re. To ascertain the alloying
mechanism, we consider two di ﬀerent substitutional sites: Nb site and
Si site. The phonon dispersion curves of these compounds are
calculated in order to examine the structural stability. Additionally,
the Debye temperature and heat capacity of NbSi
2 with alloying
elements are calculated based on the phonon and Debye models. Our
works predict that alloying element of Re can markedly improve the
compression strength and slightly enhance the heat capacity for NbSi
2.
We can conclude that alloying elements result in brittle-to-ductile
transition for NbSi
2.

2. Theoretical methods
2.1. Model
NbSi2 is known as a hexagonal C 40 structure (space group: P6222,
No. 180), in a unit cell within lattice parameters a=4.797 Å and
c=6.592 Å, respectively. In this structure, Nb atom and Si atom occupy
the Wycko ﬀ 3d(0.5000, 0, 0.5000) and 6 j(0.1593, 0.3185, 0.5000)
sites, respectively [26]. We can see that the alternative stacking of Nb
and Si layers can be viewed along the crystallographic c-axis. The
calculated bond lengths of Si– Si covalent bond and Nb –Si bond are
2.571 Å and 2.620 Å, respectively. According to the structural feature
and atomic symmetry, alloying elements may occupy two possible sites:
Nb site and Si site. The corresponding substitutional sites are denoted
by TM (TM=Re and Mo) -Nb site and TM -Si site , respectively. The
structural model of NbSi 2 with substitutional site is shown in Fig. 1 .
2.2. Computational details
All calculations in this paper were carried out by using a ﬁrst-
principles with DFT method, as implemented in the CASTEP code [27].
The interactions between electrons and ions were described by using a
ultrasoft pseudopentioals [28]. The exchange correlation functional
was used by the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) with PBE
[29]. The valence shells and electronic con ﬁgurations were 4 p
64d45s1,
3s23p2,4 p64d45s1 and 5 p65d56s2 for Nb, Si, Mo and Re atoms,
respectively. A plane-wave basis set for electron wave function with
cutoﬀ energy of 400 eV was used. The Brillouin zone was sampled with
14×14×9 mesh grids for integrations. During the process of structural
optimization, all atomic positions, lattice parameters and internal
coordinates in a system were fully relaxed.
To estimate the dynamically stable, the phonon frequency of NbSi
2
with alloying elements were calculated by using the PHONOPY package
[30]. As we known, the elastic properties such as bulk modulus ( B),
shear modulus ( G) and Young ′s modulus ( E) were calculated by the
elastic constants ( Cij). Thus, elastic constant plays an important role in
mechanical properties. Here, the elastic constant of NbSi 2 was calcu-
lated by using the strain vs stress method. In this paper, the bulk
modulus and shear modulus were calculated by the Voigt-Reuss-Hill
(VRH) approximation method [31]. To predict the ﬁnite temperature
thermodynamic properties, we used the quasi-harmonic Debye model
based on the Gibbs code [32,33].

4. Conclusions
In conclusion, we use the ﬁrst-principles calculations to investigate
the lattice dynamics, thermodynamically stable, phonon dispersion,
elastic properties, brittle or ductile behavior and chemical bonding of
NbSi
2 with alloying elements of 4 d-Mo and 5 d-Re. Two substitutional
sites such as Nb site and Si site are considered in detail. The e ﬀect of
alloying elements on thermodynamic properties of NbSi 2 is studied,
and the Debye temperature and heat capacity of NbSi 2 with alloying
elements are predicted.
NbSi2 with alloying elements are thermodynamically stable at the
ground state. Alloying elements prefer to occupy the Si site in
comparison to Nb site, and the alloying of Re is more thermodynami-
cally stable than that of Mo. The calculated phonon dispersion shows
that NbSi 2 and NbSi 2 with alloying elements are dynamically stable.
Although the bulk modulus of NbSi 2 is lower than that of Nb 5Si3, the
shear modulus and Young ′s modulus of the former are larger than the
latter. The bulk modulus of alloying elements of Re and Mo at Nb site is
15.3% and 8.2% larger than that of NbSi
2. The increasing of bulk
modulus is derived from the high valence electron density In addition,
alloying elements of Mo and Re result in brittle to ductile transition for
NbSi
2. The calculated elastic constants and elastic modulus of TM -Nb
site are larger than that of TM -Si site. We further ﬁnd that the heat
capacity of NbSi 2 with alloying elements is slightly bigger than that of
NbSi2. The Debye temperature of TM -Nb site is larger than that of TM -Si
site. On the contrary, the heat capacity of TM -Nb site is smaller than
that of TM -Si site. Finally, we conclude that alloying element of Re and
Mo are bene ﬁcial to improving the overall performances of NbSi 2.
