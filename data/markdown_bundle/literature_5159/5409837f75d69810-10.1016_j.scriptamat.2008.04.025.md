Anisotropy of secondary reaction zone formation in aluminized
Ni-based single-crystal superalloys
Hideyuki Murakami a,* and Takashi Sakai b
aCoating Materials Group, Composites and Coatings Center, National Institute for Materials Science (NIMS),
1-2-1 Sengen, Tsukuba, Ibaraki 305-0047, Japan
bTakasago Machinery Works, Mitsubishi Heavy Industries Ltd., 2-1-1 Niihama, Araimachi, Takasago, Hyogo 676-8686, Japan

Distribution of the secondary reaction zone (SRZ) on the (1 0 0) plane was investigated in the aluminized Ni-based single-crystal
superalloy TMS-75. Preferential formation of the SRZ was observed along the {0 0 1} h110 i directions, whereas the SRZ was not
formed along the {0 0 1} h100 i directions. This result can be explained by the anisotropy of recrystallization in the single-crystalline
substrate.

Keywords: Aluminizing; Ni-based superalloy; Secondary reaction zone (SRZ); Crystal orientation; Electron back-scattered diﬀraction
Aluminizing is a cost-eﬀective coating technique fre-
quently used for the protection of metal surfaces against
oxidation, via the formation of an a-Al2O3 surface layer.
This technique has been applied to protect Ni-based sin-
gle-crystal superalloys which are used as turbine compo-
nents. However, when aluminized superalloys are
exposed to high-temperature environments, interdiﬀu-
sion of elements between the superalloy substrate and
the coating layer takes place, resulting in a change in
the microstructure of the substrates. For instance, out-
ward diﬀusion of Ni from the substrate leads to the
enrichment of refractory elements and promotes the for-
mation of topologically close-packed (TCP) phases that
seriously deteriorate the mechanical properties of sub-
strates. A secondary reaction zone (SRZ), in which
TCP precipitation takes place as a result of interdiﬀu-
sion, is formed in this manner. It is thus important to
fully understand the formation kinetics of SRZs in order
to control and minimize the microstructural changes of
aluminized coatings.
On the other hand, it is well established that the ther-
momechanical properties of Ni-based single-crystal
superalloys have a strong crystal orientation depen-
dence. For instance, a change in tensile orientation dras-
tically aﬀects the creep rupture life [1,2]. An orientation
relationship between TCP precipitates and matrix super-
alloys has also been reported [3]. It is thus expected that
aluminized Ni-based superalloys should exhibit some
orientation dependence in terms of microstructure. How-
ever, while there are several reports focusing on the
microstructural morphologies of aluminized [4],a n d
Pt- [5–7] or Ir- [8–10] modiﬁed aluminized superalloys,
not so much attention has been paid to the relationship
between crystal orientation and microstructural changes.
This study investigates the eﬀect of crystal orientation
on microstructural changes of aluminized Ni-based sin-
gle-crystal superalloys. As an example, anisotropy of the
SRZ on the (0 0 1) plane was introduced and its forma-
tion mechanism was discussed.
A so-called third-generatio n Ni-based single-crystal
superalloy TMS-75, developed by NIMS[11],w a su s e di n
this study. The nominal composition of TMS-75 is Ni–
12.0Co–3.0Cr–2.0Mo–6.0W–5.0Re–6.0Al–6.0Ta–0.1Hf in
wt.%. The alloy was solution heat treated at 1573 K for
1 h followed by 1593 K for 5 h in an argon atmosphere,
and a subsequent two-step aging treatment was con-
ducted, i.e., 1423 K for 4 h and 1143 K for 20 h, both
followed by air cooling to room temperature. The heat-
treated alloy was supplied as a single-crystal bar approx-
imately 10.5 mm in diameter. The crystal orientation of
the bar was further checked by the X-ray Laue reﬂection
method, and a [1 0 0]-oriented cylindrical bar 7 mm in
diameter and 50 mm in length was prepared by electric
discharge machining.

by means of a machining center followed by #1200 ﬁn-

was not used at all during the course of this study.
The specimen was then embedded in an Al

24.5 wt.% Fe and 2 wt.% NH 4Cl powders and heated
at 1273 K for 5 h in ﬂowing argon in order to carry
out the pack-cementation aluminizing treatment. The
aluminized sample was cut along the (0 0 1) plane for
cross-sectional observation. The microstructure and
crystal orientation of the sample were investigated by
a Philips XL 30 scanning electron microscope and a
JEOL JSM-7000F ﬁeld emission scanning electron
microscope equipped with a TSL-OIM electron back-

Figure 1 presents the typical cross-sectional backscat-
tered electron micrograph on the (0 0 1) surface of the as-
aluminized specimen. Note that in this ﬁgure, the sur-
face layer enriched in Al is not clearly visible due to its
dark contrast. These ﬁgures demonstrate well the inho-
mogeneous distribution of a SRZ that was observed just
beneath the brightly contrasting interdiﬀusion layer. For
instance, a SRZ was clearly observed in the region indi-
cated by the arrow ‘‘(i) ” as shown in Figure 1 b, while a
SRZ was not observed in the region indicated by the ar-
row ‘‘(ii) ” (Fig. 2 c). It should also be noted that modu-
lation of the contrast in the substrate in Figure 1 ai s
attributed to the remaining segregation of solute ele-
ments by the casting process, where enrichment of
refractory elements such as Re, W, Mo in the dendrite
core leads to the brighter contrast in the backscattered
images.
EBSD was used to elucidate the relationship between
crystal orientation and SRZ formation. Figure 2a shows
the cross-sectional SEM image where a SRZ was not ob-
served, and Figure 2b and c are the corresponding orien-
tation maps assigned as face-centered cubic (fcc) and
body-centered cubic (bcc) structures, respectively. The
image quality map (IQ) corresponding to Figure 2 a–c
is also shown as Figure 2 d. In these ﬁgures, the lower
part of the images is the single-crystal substrate and ori-
entation maps are displayed from the reference direction
(RD), which is normal to the substrate/coating inter-
face. Therefore, the set of images was taken where the
substrate is oriented along the (0 0 1)[1 0 0] direction. It
is clear from these ﬁgures that the surface aluminized
layer consists of bcc polycrystalline grains while the
interdiﬀusion zone (IDZ) consists of bcc and fcc phases.
Most importantly, a SRZ was not observed along the
(0 0 1)[1 0 0] direction. On the other hand, Figure 3 was
taken where the surface is oriented along the
(0 0 1)[1 1 0] direction. Although signiﬁcant diﬀerences
in microstructures of the substrate region, such as size
and volume fraction of c
0 phases, were not observed be-
tween the (0 0 1)[1 0 0] and (0 0 1)[1 1 0] directions, the
existence of a SRZ that is accompanied by the local
recrystallization of the single-crystal superalloy sub-
strate was clearly observed along the (0 0 1)[1 1 0] direc-
tion. The thickness of the SRZ as a function of crystal
orientation is plotted in Figure 4 , showing the clear
four-fold symmetry, i.e., no SRZ formation along the
{0 0 1}h100 i directions, and greatly enhanced SRZ for-
mation along the {0 0 1} h110 i directions. Indeed, a sim-
ilar orientation dependence of SRZ formation is
observed in a CoNiCrAlY-coated Ni-based single-crys-
tal superalloy fabricated by low-pressure plasma spray-
ing [12,13].
There are several possible reasons to explain this
anisotropic formation of the SRZ. Firstly, some TCP
phases exhibit preferred precipitation orientation in
relation to single-crystal substrates. For instance, Rae
et al. showed that the r phase, one of the TCP phases,
formed coherently on {1 1 1} planes in platinum-modi-
ﬁed aluminized Ni-based single-crystal substrates [7].
However, the precipitates in the SRZ observed in this
study have a rather rounded shape and did not show
such an orientation relationship. It can also be assumed
that irregularity of solute element distribution would
lead to a preferred formation of the SRZ as such local
enrichment of refractory elements would promote the
precipitation of TCP phases. These refractory elements
are enriched in dendrite cores which tend to spread
along the h100 i directions, as shown in Figure 1 .I ti s
concluded that although local concentration modulation
might aﬀect the formation kinetics of the SRZ, this
hypothesis cannot apply to this study, because the
‘‘SRZ-free” region in Figure 1 a included brightly con-
trasting dendrite cores. In addition, Figure 1 a indicates
that in the vicinity of the coating layer, the interdendritic
regions and dendrite core regions show up in turn. Nev-
ertheless, this local concentration change does not seem
to aﬀect the thickness of the SRZ region.
Finally, anisotropy of recrystallization is considered.
Internal stress should have been introduced during sur-
face polishing and subsequent aluminizing, which pro-
motes the recrystallization of the substrate in the
vicinity of a substrate/coating interface. It is well known
that the dominant slip systems for Ni-base superalloys
are {1 1 1}h110 i and {1 1 1}h112 i [14]. Stress-induced lo-
cal deformation of substrates could occur along these
slip systems, as indicated by the arrows in Figures 2 d
and 3d. While the cross-slip should operate to retard
the local deformation along the h100 i directions, orien-
tations such as h110 i along which the cross-slip system

and re-heat treatment resulted in the formation of cellu-
lar structure accompanied by recrystallization [17].
These reports support our hypothesis.

tance of secondary orientation normal to the component
surface when designing the single-crystalline turbine
blades. Localized damages in the blades may not only
be caused by the irregular stress and temperature distri-
bution applied to the blade, but also be aﬀected by the
secondary orientation. However, as our previous study
