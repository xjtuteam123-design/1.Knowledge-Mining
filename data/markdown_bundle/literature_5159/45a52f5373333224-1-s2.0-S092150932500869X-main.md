ABSTRACT
To develop Co-based superalloys with reduced density and exceptional high-temperature performance, this study 
designed a novel TiC- γ ′ dual-phase reinforced Co-based composite with excellent mechanical properties based on 
the in-situ TiC formation mechanism of Ti
2
AlC. The optimal sintering process was determined via differential 
scanning calorimetry (DSC), clarifying the decomposition pathways of Ti
2
AlC in the matrix (Al/Ti, Ti/C). The 
decomposition of Ti
2
AlC not only generates TiC particles in situ but also releases Al and Ti atoms, promoting the 
formation of the γ ′ phase in the matrix. The introduction of Ni element based on CALPHAD calculations expands 
the γ - γ ′ two-phase region and inhibits the formation of brittle phases, thereby further enhancing the performance 
of the composite. TEM and SEM-EDS analyses revealed semi-coherent TiC/ γ ′ interfaces and coherent γ / γ ′ 
boundaries, contributing to exceptional high-temperature stability. Experiments indicate that the 25 wt% 
Ti
2
AlC/CoNi
40 
composite showed an enhanced specific 0.2 % yield strength (124.5 MPa/g ⋅ cm
 3 
at 800 
◦
C and 
74.8 MPa/g ⋅ cm
 3 
at 900 
◦
C) due to its extremely low density of 6.66 g/cm
3
, surpassing Co-based superalloys and 
conventional Ni-based superalloys. The dislocation strengthening mechanism of coordinated deformation and 
the nucleation growth of TiC- γ ′ dual-phases was analyzed. This study offers a novel strategy for developing high- 
performance Co-based superalloys with superior high-temperature performance.
1. Introduction
New Co-based superalloys containing the L1

Co-based superalloys are considered promising candidates for 
next-generation gas turbine blade and disk applications [ 3 , 4 ]. However, 
γ ′ -Co

the density [ 5 , 6 ]. In contrast, the thermodynamically stable phase

compared to both the γ ′ -Ni

However, traditional Co-Ti alloy has inherent limitations that hin -
ders its further development. These include a narrow γ - γ ′ two-phase 
compositional region and a relatively low γ ′ solidus temperature 
(~1000 
◦
C), which compromises phase stability and mechanical per -
formance under high-temperature service conditions [ 8 ]. Therefore, 
enhancing the high-temperature mechanical properties of Co-Ti alloys 
remains a key challenge, particularly in expanding their phase stability 
and maintaining strength.
To overcome these limitations, the introduction of in-situ ceramic 
reinforcements has emerged as a promising strategy. The addition of 
ceramic particles can reduce density and improve mechanical perfor -
mance. In particular, recent studies have focused on the in-situ forma -
tion of fine MX particles derived from MAX phases within various metal 
matrices (Cu [ 9 , 10 ], Fe [ 11 , 12 ], Ni [ 13 , 14 ]).Titanium aluminum car -
bide (Ti
n + 1
AlC

combines the characteristics of metals and ceramics, offering excellent 
mechanical properties, thermal stability, and chemical resistance [ 15 ,
16 ]. Hu et al. prepared a dual-phase (TiC- γ ′ ) strengthened Ni-based su -
peralloy through hot isostatic pressing using Ti
2
AlC [ 17 – 21 ] or Ti
3
AlC
2 
[ 22 – 24 ] as a precursor. Ti

in the precipitation of the L1

ever, research on in-situ ceramic reinforcement of Co-based superalloys 
remains limited, primarily due to the narrow compositional range 
within Co-based systems.

4.1. Reaction mechanism
The reaction mechanism of the composites prepared in this work can 
be summarized into three steps: (1) The Decomposition of Ti
2

4.1.1. The decomposition of Ti
2
AlC
The DSC results ( Fig. 2 ) show the A-site element (Al) is released from 
the Ti
2

the A-site element and the M-X layers is weak, especially under elevated 
temperatures. As a result, the Ti
2
AlC decomposes into sub- 
stoichiometric TiC
x 
and releases Al vapor through endothermic 
decomposition. The decomposition of Ti

At higher temperatures, Ti

decomposition increases as the temperature rises.

Based on previous studies [ 38 ], the nucleation of TiC in this work is

The Al-Ti released from the decomposition of Ti

superalloys and is the key to improving the mechanical properties of 
composites, as the coherent γ ʹ - γ two-phase structure significantly en -
hances the high-temperature mechanical properties.

4.2. Strengthening mechanism
4.2.1. Theoretical strengthening mechanism
The composites exhibit excellent mechanical properties due to the 
combined effects of in-situ TiC and γ ′ phase. Based on the continuum 
model and micromechanics model, the strengthening mechanisms 
include: solid solution strengthening, dislocation strengthening, and 
dispersion strengthening.
Solid solution strengthening occurs through the incorporation of Al- 
Ti into the Co-Ni lattice, where their larger atomic radii (Al: 0.143 nm, 
Ti: 0.147 nm) induce compressive stress in the Co-Ni lattice (Co: 0.125 
nm, Ni: 0.124 nm), causing lattice distortion. This distortion greatly 
increases the resistance to dislocation motion, making slip deformation 
in the Co-Ni matrix more challenging, thereby enhancing the alloy ’ s 
strength and hardness. The elevated yield strength in a single disordered 
FCC- γ phase is attributed to solute atoms acting as frictional barriers to 
dislocation propagation.
The increase in yield strength in the disordered FCC- γ phase is pri -
marily attributed to the presence of solute atoms, which act as obstacles 
to dislocation slip thereby hindering plastic deformation. The enhanced 
yield strength of the composite with increasing Ni content can be 
explained through solid solution strengthening and dispersion 
strengthening.
In solid solution strengthening, Ni atoms preferentially substitute Co 
atoms within the γ matrix, altering the lattice structure and introducing 
strain fields that impede dislocation movement. Concurrently, precipi -
tation strengthening occurs as Ni atoms contribute to the γ’ -(Co,Ni)
3
(Al, 
Ti) precipitates, which are characterized by ordered structure and high 
hardness, further strengthening the composite by pinning dislocations 
and preventing motion. The increase in composite yield strength with 
higher Ni content can be explained by both solid solution hardening and 
precipitation strengthening. Ni atoms preferentially replace Co atoms in 
the γ matrix and to form γ’ -(Co,Ni)
