ABSTRACT 
The size and distribution of second phase particles have an important influence on the mechanical properties of 
Mg alloys and further determine their application. In this study, extreme plastic deformation was employed to 
control the microstructure and tune the corresponding mechanical properties of Mg – Zn – Ca alloy. The uniformly 
dispersed nanoparticles (average diameter of ~14.3 nm) could be successfully introduced into the Mg – Zn – Ca 
alloy wire after cold drawing with an area reduction of ~99.8%, and the high yield strength of ~285 MPa was 
obtained. When compared to the as-extruded alloy with yield strength of ~171 MPa, the exceptional 
improvement in yield strength (~114 MPa) for the Mg – Zn – Ca alloy wire is mainly attributed to the formation of 
nanoparticles. Moreover, the Mg wire also exhibited a good ductility of ~11.8%. The results indicate that 
extreme plastic deformation can enable a refined microstructure to enhance the mechanical properties of 
Mg – Zn – Ca alloys. 
1. Introduction 
Magnesium and its alloys have been attracted increasing attention 
for use in biodegradable implants such as bone screws, staples, and 
stents owing to their excellent biocompatibility and complete biode -
gradability, and are expected to become substitutes for traditional per -
manent implant materials [ 1 – 7 ]. In particular, Mg – Zn – Ca alloy is 
considered to be suitable for biomedical application due to its total 
nutrient elements composition with benign biocompatibility and good 
corrosion resistance [ 8 , 9 ]. Zn is an abundant essential element in the 
human body, and Ca has been proved to effectively accelerate human 
bone healing [ 10 ]. Despite these advantages, the large-scale application 
of Mg – Zn – Ca alloys is limited by their low absolute strength and poor 
formability caused by their hexagonal close-packed crystal structures 
[ 11 , 12 ]. To be effective as implant materials, Mg – Zn – Ca alloys need to 
have excellent yield strength and ductility to provide sufficient me -
chanical support after implantation to avoid operation failure. 
Investigations indicate that grain refinement can increase the yield 
strength of Mg alloys [ 13 – 16 ]. However, achieving further grain 
refinement in Mg – Zn – Ca alloys when grains had already been refined to 
some extent is a challenge. Furthermore, stress concentration is prone to 
occur because of the limited capacity of storing dislocation for 
fine-grained structures during plastic deformation [ 16 – 18 ]. This results 
in a significant reduction in ductility. Hence, the yield strength of 
Mg – Zn – Ca alloys without sacrificing their ductility is particularly 
important. Recently, the increase in the strength of Mg alloys through 
the dislocation pinning effect caused by considerable quantities of 
nanoparticles has been widely studied [ 19 , 20 ]. More importantly, 
introducing nanoparticles is an effective approach for achieving high 
strength without significant loss in ductility [ 21 ]. In general, dispersed 
nanoparticles are usually acquired by solid solution and aging treatment 
in Mg alloys, which have a certain solid solubility for solute atoms [ 22 ]. 
Based on Fick ’ s law, the diffusion driving force decreases with the solid 
solution treatment. To generate uniformly distributed nanoparticles 
during subsequent aging treatment and achieve a better precipitation 
strengthening effect, a long solid solution treatment should be con -
ducted. However, this may cause an increase in the grain size and a 
sharp decrease in the grain boundary (GB) strengthening effect. There -
fore, developing a novel method to obtain nanoparticles in Mg alloys 
while maintaining the fine grain size is required.

fragments during the hot-rolled deformation process (with a thickness

locations can also provide driving force for atomic migration [ 39 ], 
resulting in solute atoms segregating close to the dislocation cores.

4. Conclusion 
In this study, the microstructures and tensile mechanical properties 
of as-extruded and cold-drawn Mg-2.46Zn-0.69Ca alloys were investi -
gated. The main conclusions are drawn as follows: 
(1) The high yield strength (~285 MPa) and high ductility (~11.8%) 
Mg-2.46Zn-0.69Ca alloy wires with a diameter of 0.28 mm were 
prepared by ~99.8% area reduction cold drawing deformation. 
The increase in tensile yield strength for the CD-At-220-30 wire 
compared to that of the as-extruded alloy was ~114 MPa, and no 
evident differences in ductility were observed. 
(2) The uniformly distributed nanoparticles with semi-coherent 
Ca
2
Mg
6
Zn
3 
and coherent MgZn
2 
phases with an average diam -
eter of ~14.3 nm were formed in the CD-At-220-30 wire, which 
can be attributed to the effect of mechanical crushing and dy -
namic precipitation. 
(3) The high yield strength of the CD-At-220-30 sample was mainly 
related to GB, Orowan, and dislocation strengthening mecha -
nisms, with calculated contribution values of ~122 MPa, ~107 
MPa, and ~73 MPa, respectively. More importantly, the sub -
stantial enhancement of yield strength of CD-At-220-30 wire 
compared with that of as-extruded alloy was mainly due to the 
Orowan strengthening caused by nanoparticles. The good 
ductility is mainly related to the fine grain structure and uni -
formly distributed nanoparticles. 
(4) The fabrication of the staples and anastomosis test results indi -
cated that the CD-At-220-30 wire has excellent formability and 
potential application prospects.
