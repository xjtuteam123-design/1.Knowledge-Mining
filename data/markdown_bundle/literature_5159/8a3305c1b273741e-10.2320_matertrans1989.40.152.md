Materials Transactions, JIM, Vol. 40, No. 2 (1999), pp. 152 to 158 Phase Stability and Mechanical Properties of Nb-rich Nb-Mo-Al Ternary System at Elevated Temperatures tt Keizo Hashimotot and Toshihiro Hanamura Advanced Technology Research Laboratories, Nippon Steel Corp., Kawasaki 211-0035, JAPAN Nb3Al base intermetallic compounds have become attractive as structural materials for ultra high temperature use. To improve high temperature strength, molybdenum (Mo) has been selected as the third element. Phase stability and fun- damental mechanical properties of Nb-AI-Mo system have been investigated. Based on the phase and the microstructure observations in the various alloys, ternary phase diagrams have been calculated, applying a modified Thermo-calc data- base. Mo addition stabilizes both the bcc and A 15 phases in the all composition range. The total solid solution of both bcc and A15 (M03AI-Nb3AI) phases exists in the ternary phase diagram. The solution hardening effect of Mo atoms on A15 structure is clarified. The high temperature strength of bcc-Nb alloys and Nb3Al intermetallic compounds is closely related to the phase stability of the Nb-Al-Mo ternary system. The Mo doped A15 structure exhibits the highest tensile strength in the Nb-Al-Mo ternary system at 1873 K. TEM observations revealed the role of dynamic re-crystallization on the high temperature deformation process. Introduction There is a strong demand for the structural materials, which perform for a long period over 1373 K, since a small improvement on the energy emciency of jet engines and power generators largely contributes to the solution of the global energy problems. Among the high tempera- ture materials, Ni-base superalloys are commonly ap- plied in the severe environments. However, the alloy de- velopments of Ni-base superalloys are almost saturating with their heat resistance capability because of the melt- ing point of Ni (1726 K). If a new type of the high temper- ature materials which can withstand at the ultra high tem- peratures such as 1873 K could be developed, the maxi- mum operation temperature of the jet engines and the power generators could be designed more higher, and the turbine blades could be produced with no cooling sys- tem. Experimental Procedure Twenty Nb-Al binary ingots whose compositions range are from Nb-13at%Al to Nb-35.3at%Al were pre- pared by a plasma arc melting (PAM) and a non con- sumable arc melting (AM). Ingots were homogenized at 1873 K for 86.4 ks in a vacuum (10-3 Pa). In order to study phase stability at the high temperatures, levitation and quenching experiments were carried out. As quen- ched specimens were examined by optical microscopy (OM) and X-ray diffraction (XRD). Nb-Mo-Al ternary phase diagrams Molybdenum (Mo) belongs to the refractory metal group and its melting point is 2883 K. According to the bi- nary phase diagram of the Mo-Al system, M03Al (A15) intermetallic compound is stable; therefore, the A15 phase makes total solid solution from Nb3Al to M03Al in the Nb-Al-Mo ternary system(12). Figure 4 shows typical microstructures of the Nb-Al-Mo specimens which were quenched from 2073 K by introducing helium gas into the chamber. When the microstructures of Nb-20.7at0/o Al-4.5at%Mo in Fig. 4 are compared with those of 1837 K, the A15 single phase at 1873 K changes to the bcc+A15 two phases at 2073 K (Fig. 4). Nb-20.7at%A1- 4.5at%Mo in Fig. 4 shows that the A15 phase precipitates at the vicinity of the bcc grain boundaries. Specimens having 23.7 to 26. lat%Al show the A 15 single phase and the microstructure of Nb-28.8at%Al-4.4at% Mo shows the A 15+0 two phases. In Fig. 4, specimens having relatively high Mo concentrations exhibit the bcc+A15 two phases up to 22.4at%Al, the A15 single phase appears at 22.9at%Al and the A15 + a two phases appear at 27.6at%Al, respectively. Table 1 summarizes the observed phases and the analyzed chemical composi- tions of the Nb-Al-Mo samples based on the microstruc- ture observations and XRD. By comparing the quenched microstructures between 1873 and 2073 K, it can be con- cluded that the bcc phase region expands to the higher Al concentration region, while the c phase shift to lower the Al concentration. Thermo-calc program has been utilized to calculate an equilibrium phase diagram. At first, Nb-Al, Mo-Al and Nb-Mo binary phase diagrams have been established by the modified databases and then they have been expand- ed to the Nb-Al-Mo ternary system. Figure 5 shows the calculated isothermal cross section of the Nb-Mo-Al ter- nary phase diagram at 1873 K. In Fig. 5, the Nb-Al and the Al-Mo binary phase diagrams are also presented. Databases of binary systems have modified to the ternary system, by introducing many additional interaction parameters. In the calculation, both the Nb3Al-M03Al (A15) and the Nb2Al (a) have two sub-lattices and not only the primary interaction but also the secondary inter- action parameters have been closely examined. The mar- kers in Fig. 5 are indicating the results of the experimen- tal observations. Although the database of the Nb-Al- Mo ternary system has been modified based on the ex- perimental observations, the calculated phase boundaries have not coincided with the experimental observations around 25at%Mo additions. The features of the Nb-Al- Mo ternary phase diagram are the total solid solution of both bcc and the A15 phases, since both Nb and Mo have the bcc structure in the pure state and have many similar physical and chemical properties. Conclusion The phase stability and the fundamental mechanical properties of the Nb-Al-Mo system have been investigat- ed. Based on the phase and the microstructure observa- tions in the various alloys, the ternary phase diagrams have been calculated using the modified Thermo-calc database. Mo additions stabilize both the bcc and the A15 phases in the all composition range. The solution hardening effect of Mo atoms in the A15 structure is clarified. High temperature strength of the bcc-Nb alloys Fig. 9 TEM observation of CW234 specimen tensile tested at 2073 K. and the Nb3Al intermetallic compounds is closely related with the phase stability of the Nb-Al binary system. Mo doped A15 structure shows the highest tensile strength among the Nb-Al-Mo ternary specimens at 1873 K. The mechanical properties of the designed materials have been evaluated at the elevated temperatures. It can be concluded that the Mo doped Nb3Al base intermetallic compounds have the superior mechanical properties at the temperature range from 1573 to 1973 K but these properties disappear beyond 2073 K.
