abstract
The effect of homogenization treatments on the microstructure and tensile properties at
room temperature and 650
/C14 C of Thermo-Span alloy are systematically investigated. Laves
phases with different sizes and morphologies have been obtained by using three types of
homogenization treatments. The results show that the precipitation of small granular
secondary Laves phases and g' phases are inhibited effectively by large blocky primary
Laves phases, resulting in high plasticity and low strength of the alloy. The precipitations
of secondary Laves phases and g' phases increase with the dissolution of primary Laves
phases, leading to low plasticity and high strength of the alloy. The secondary Laves phases
continue to increase and g' phases slightly decrease with the almost elimination of primary
Laves phases, resulting in high plasticity and strength of the alloy. Hence, fewer large
blocky primary Laves phases are more favorable for the best match between strength and
ductility. The factors affecting the yield strength at room temperature and 650
/C14 C tensile
are discussed in detail. Moreover, it is ﬁrst found the deformation mechanism of Laves
phases i.e. the stacking faults shearing in Thermo-Span alloy. In terms of ductility, small
granular secondary Laves phases are more favorable than large blocky primary Laves
phases.

1. Introduction
Laves phases are intermetallic phases with topologically
close-packed structures and extreme brittleness and poor
fracture toughness due to them being less than 5 independent
slip systems. Meanwhile, Laves phases can break and become
nucleation points for micro-cracks due to high-stress con-
centration caused by their high resistance to dislocations
motions, and thus have a detrimental effect on the strength
and plasticity of alloys [ 1e5]. However, recent researches
show that Laves phases are beneﬁcial to mechanical

meters) and granular. Not only does effectively hinder
dislocations movement and prevent grain boundaries (GBs)
sliding, but also has good coordinate deformation ability,
resulting in high strength and ductility of alloys [ 6e10]. In

only improve the limited strength of the alloy, though their
resistance to dislocation is quite high [ 11e13]. Therefore,
reasonably governing the sizes and morphologies of Laves
phases can effectively improve the comprehensive mechani-
cal properties of low expansion superalloys.
Thermo-Span alloy is an oxidation-resistance type of low
expansion superalloys. Compared with the early developed
low expansion superalloys, such as IN903, IN907, IN909,
Thermo-Span alloy possess good low expansion performance
and excellent oxidation and corrosion resistance due to the
addition of 5.5wt% Cr. Therefore, it can be served for a long
time at 650
/C14 C without a protective layer and has been applied
in aero-engine rings which served at 650 /C14 C or below 750 /C14 C.
Laves phases are largely formed during solidiﬁcation and
post-treatment in Thermo-Span alloy. Primary Laves phases
(precipitated in the solidiﬁcation process) are in form of large
(micron-scale) blocky shapes. Secondary Laves phases are in
small (nanometer-scale) granular shapes, formed in post-
treatment. However, how to control two Laves phases
morphology and their inﬂuence on mechanical properties
remain unclear in Thermo-Span alloy.
Post-treatments are generally used to control the quanti-
ties, morphologies and sizes of precipitations to strengthen
superalloys. Nevertheless, serious grain growth problem oc-
curs after post-treatment due to the high solve temperature
(>1093
/C14 C) of primary Laves phases, resulting in tensile and
fatigue properties decrease in Thermo-Span alloy [ 14,15].
Thus, pretreatment i.e. homogeneous heat treatment is per-
formed to obtain Laves phases with different morphologies
and sizes in the study. The deformation behavior of Laves
phases and its effect on tensile properties would be system-
atically investigated. It is an effective method to improve the
mechanical properties of Thermo-Span alloy by controlling
the precipitation.

4.2. The crack formation modes of laves phases and
their effect on tensile ductility
The crack formation modes of Laves phases and their effect
on tensile ductility depend on their sizes, morphologies, and
tensile temperature. The crack formation modes of Laves
phases can be divided into two cases: self-fragmentation and
interface decohesion. During the tensile process at room
temperature, large blocky primary Laves phases are difﬁcult to
deform with the g matrix, unless they are broken up into
smaller ones ( Fig. 9 g). On the contrary, small granular sec-
ondary Laves phases are easy to be separated from the g
matrix and subsequently deform with the matrix ( Fig. 9 i),
similar to Goods ' [30] and Sui 's[ 3] researches. On the other
hand, slip lines can propagate through secondary Laves pha-
ses by cross slip, but they are unable to propagate through
primary Laves phases. These conﬁrm that secondary Laves
phases are more beneﬁcial for plastic deformation. During the
tensile process at 650
/C14 C, primary Laves phases are easier to
move with the matrix ( Fig. 10 d, g), suggesting that they are
more favorable for plastic deformation compared with the
room temperature tensile test. Slip lines still propagate
through secondary Laves phases by cross slip, but do not
propagate through primary Laves phases ( Fig. 10g, i). Notably,
the elongation of Sample 3 with the most secondary Laves

Moreover, Sui [ 16] found the fragmentation of Laves phase
was dependent on the applied stress amplitude i.e. Laves
phase cracked under high-stress amplitude (750 MPa), while
kept intact under low-stress amplitude (630 MPa). Generally,
the matrix strength of superalloys decreases in high-
temperature. Thus, high shearing stresses by the yielding
matrix at the room temperature tensile test also contribute to
primary Laves phases fragmentation in the study. This can be
proved by dislocation networks cut into primary Laves phases
at room temperature tensile test ( Fig. 12a).
4.3. The effect of phases on the location of crack
initiation and propagation
According to the observation for fracture surfaces and longi-
tudinal sections of tensile tests ( Figs. 7e10), the GBs of Sample
2 is beneﬁcial for crack initiation and propagation. On the
contrary, the intragranular Laves phases of the other two
Samples provide crack initiation sites and propagation paths.
The difference depends on the distribution and sizes of Laves
phases and the precipitation of g' phases. Generally, GBs
preferentially provide crack initiation and propagation sites,
due to large strain/stress concentration caused by accommo-
dating deformation between adjacent grains and impeding
dislocations movement [36,37]. This is the case with Sample 2.
However, more large-size blocky primary Laves phases can be
observed in the grain interior of Sample 1 compared with
Sample 2 ( Fig. 3 b, Table 2 ), providing more large-size crack
nucleation positions ( Fig. 9a, b, g, h, 10(a, b, g, h) ), resulting in
crack initiating in the grain interior, consistent with Du 's
research [ 38]. It was found amounts of the carbides distrib-
uting in the grain interior caused the crack propagating in the
matrix, otherwise, the crack propagated along the GBs.
Moreover, the matrix hardness of Sample 1 is less than that of
Sample 2 ( Fig. 4 a-c), resulting in crack propagation along slip
lines in the grain interior ( Figs. 9d and 10 d). For Sample 3, the
volume fraction of grain-interior Laves phases is an equiva-
lence with that of Sample 2 (Fig. 3e, h, Table 2), suggesting that
the numbers of their positions for micro-cracks formation are
similar and Laves phases have little effect on the crack
propagation path. Interestingly, the obvious g' phases deple-
tion zone- g matrix can be observed in the vicinity of GBs of
Sample 3, while g' phases uniformly distribute in GBs of
Sample 2 (Fig. 5c, d). Cai 's[ 39] and Li's[ 40] research found that
g
00 phases depletion zone- g matrix could act as micro-plastic-
zone to inhibit crack initiation and propagation. Therefore,
GBs of Sample 3 can better accommodate deformation than
that of Sample 2, difﬁcult to crack.
5. Conclusion
1. The sample with the most granular secondary Laves pha-
ses, shows the best strength and ductility, while the sam-
ple with the most blocky primary Laves phases shows the
worst strength and high ductility. The sample with me-
dium primary and secondary Laves phases possess high
strength and worst ductility.
2. The yield strength is inﬂuenced by g' phases and Laves
phases. The g' phases are the main factor affecting yield
strength at room temperature, while secondary granular
Laves phases are the main factor affecting yield strength at
650
/C14 C.
3. The precipitations of g' phases are inﬂuenced by Laves
phases due to competition for element Nb. The pre-
cipitations of g' phases increase with the dissolution of
primary Laves phases releasing element Nb to the matrix.
The precipitations of g' phases decrease with the pre-
cipitations of secondary Laves phases consuming Nb from
the matrix.
4. Laves phases possess a certain accommodate plastic
deformation ability due to stacking faults produced and the
ability depends on the sizes and morphologies of Laves
phases and tensile temperature. Small granular secondary
Laves phases are more beneﬁcial for the plastic deforma-
tion of Thermo-Span alloy than large blocky primary Laves
phases.
