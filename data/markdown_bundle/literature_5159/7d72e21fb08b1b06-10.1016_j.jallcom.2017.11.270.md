abstract
The redistribution of the second phases in Ti-Ni-Hf alloy was achieved by the hot-pressed sintering of the
rapid solidi ﬁed alloy powders. The second phases distributed mainly at the surface of the original alloy
powder other than the grain boundaries, forming a network structure. The second phase was (Ti,Hf) 2Ni
phase, when the sintering temperatures were not more than 1 100 /C14 C. The (Ti,Hf) 2Ni and Hf 2Ni phases
coexisted at the sintering temperature of 1 150 /C14 C. Such a change of the second phases in type and
distribution led to the variation of the martensitic transformation temperatures. The martensitic
transformation temperatures increased slightly when the sintering temperatures increased from 1000
/C14 C
to 1 100/C14 C and then decreased when the sintering temperature was 1 150 /C14 C.The sintered Ti-Ni-Hf alloys
with a network structure formed by the second phases showed the higher compressive strength and the
larger recoverable strain, without signi ﬁcantly sacri ﬁcing the compressive strain.

1. Introduction
Ti-Ni-Hf high temperature shape memory alloys (HTSMAs) have
received much more attentions owing to high transformation
temperatures, high strength, high work outputs and two-way
shape memory effect as well as relatively lower cost than others
[1e6]. Such an excellent combination of numerous advantages
promotes them as the most potential candidate materials for a
range of high temperature applications such as automotive, aero-
space and nuclear industry and so on [6]. However, the Ti-Ni-Hf
based bulk alloys show the shape memory effect of about 3%,
which is far lower than that of binary Ti-Ni alloys (>6%) and hinders
their extensive applications to some extent [6e9]. Such a poor
shape memory property is attributed to the simultaneous occur-
rence of the formation of (001) compound twin and the slip of a/2
on the (001) plane during deformation owing to the lower matrix
strength [10,1 1]. In order to enhance the strength of the matrix,
several methods including the thermo-mechanical treatment,
adding the quaternary element or the precipitation hardening, and
the combination of them have been adopted [12e19]. The equal
channel angular extruded (ECAE) Ti-Ni-Hf alloy followed by the
lower temperature annealing shows the larger transformation
strain and the stable two way shape memory effect (TWSME) strain
of 1.5%, which is attributed to the microstructural re ﬁnement and
increased dislocations density [12]. Nevertheless, the shape mem-
ory properties would deteriorate due to the reduction of disloca-
tions density in the higher temperature applications. In addition,
the mechanical and shape memory properties are enhanced as a
result of the addition of Pd, Nb and Al, but the transformation
temperatures decrease or even less than 100
/C14 C [13e15]. However,
the quaternary Ti-Ni-Hf based alloys maintain the relatively higher
transformation temperatures and show the slight improvement in
the shape memory properties due to the Cu, Y and Zr addition
[16e18]. Similarly, the improvement of yield strength and shape
memory properties in the aged (Ti þHf)-rich Ti-Ni-Hf alloy is not
signiﬁcant [19]. As a matter of fact, the hard and brittle (Ti,Hf) 2Ni
phase distributing at the grain boundaries is one of the main rea-
sons for the poor mechanical and shape memory properties in Ti-
Ni-Hf alloys [20,21].
In our previous study, the rapid solidi ﬁed Ti-Ni-Hf-Cu amor-
phous ribbon is prepared by the melt spinning and followed the
annealing. In the annealed Ti-Ni-Hf-Cu alloy ribbon, the (Ti,Hf) 2Ni
phases distribute homogeneously in the grain interior other than
the grain boundaries, which signi ﬁcantly strengthens the matrix
and improves the shape memory properties and two way shape* Corresponding author.

2. Experimental procedure
In the present study, the as-received rapid solidiﬁed Ti36Ni49Hf15
(at.%) alloy powders were prepared by plasma rotating electrode
process (PREP). And the alloy powders with the particle size of
45e75 mm were used as the starting materials. The morphologies of
the as-received alloy powders were shown in Fig. 1. The Ti-Ni-Hf
alloy powders showed mainly regular spherical. The alloy pow-
ders were poured into a graphite die with a diameter of 25 mm and
then sintered by the hot-pressed sintering (HPS) at the different
sintering temperatures in the vacuum condition. The degree of the
vacuum is as high as ~1 /C2 10
/C0 3 Pa during the hot-pressed sintering
process. The details of the sintering process were shown in Fig. 2 .
The pressure was maintained at 60 MPa throughout the whole
process.
The specimens were prepared by the electro-discharge machine
from the casted and sintered alloys and polished for the subsequent
measurement. X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurements were carried
out using Cu K a radiation at room temperature. Microstructural
observation was performed using a FEI Quanta 200FEG scanning
electron microscope (SEM) equipped with energy dispersive
spectroscopy (EDS) and Transmission electron microscopy (TEM)
investigations on the sintered samples at different sintering tem-
peratures were conducted using a Talos F200x microscope with an
accelerating voltage of 200 kV at room temperature. Martensitic
transformation temperatures were determined by a Perkin-Elmer
Diamond differential scanning calorimeter (DSC) with a heating/
cooling rate of 20 /C14 C/min. Hardness was evaluated by the Vickers
micro-hardness with the following test parameters: 200 g loading
and holding for 20 s at room temperature. The values of hardness
were the average of at least 10 measurements for each sample. The
mechanical properties were measured under the compressive
mode at a displacement speed of 0.2 mm/min using an Instron-
5569 testing system at room temperature. The dimensions of the
compressive samples were small cylinder with 43m m /C2 5 mm.

3.2. Martensitic transformation behaviors
Fig. 6(a) shows the DSC curves of the Ti-Ni-Hf alloys sintered at
the various temperatures. Only one endothermic and exothermic
peak appears in the heating and cooling DSC curves respectively,
indicating that all sintered samples undergo the only one-step
martensitic transformation from cubic parent phase (B2) to
monoclinic martensite phase (B19 0). The characteristic trans-
formation temperatures of martensite peak (M p) and austenite
peak (Ap) are extracted from DSC curves. The results show that the
transformation temperatures of the sintered sample at 1 100 /C14 Ca r e
the maximum value with the M p ¼ 185.78 /C14 C and Ap ¼ 239.42 /C14 C. It
is well known that the martensitic transformation temperatures
increase with the (TiþHf)/Ni ratio of the matrix increasing for Ti-Ni-
Hf based alloy [33]. The ratio of (Ti þHf)/Ni in the matrix is almost
equal for the sintered samples regardless of the sintering temper-
atures, which indicates that the (TiþHf)/Ni ratio in the matrix is not
the decisive factor in the variation of transformation temperatures.
Several crystallographic defects such as dislocation and re ﬁned
grains introduced by rapid solidi ﬁcation can't be eliminated under
the inadequate sintering temperature of 1000 /C14 C [34], which should
be responsible for the slight lower transformation temperatures. It
has been reported that there is an abrupt increase of M p by almost
20 /C14 C/at.% Hf in Ni-poor Ni-Ti-Hf alloys when the Hf content is
beyond 10 at.% [6]. Moreover, compared with the sintered samples
at 1000 /C14 C and 1 100 /C14 C, the content of Hf element in the matrix
decreases by about 2 at.% due to the coexistence of Hf 2Ni phase and
(Ti,Hf)2Ni phase in the Ti-Ni-Hf alloy sintered at 1 150 /C14 C, which is
well consistent with the reduction of martensitic transformation
temperatures. Thus, it can be concluded that the lower Hf content

4. Conclusions
The hot-pressed sintering of the rapid solidi ﬁed Ti-Ni-Hf alloy
powders was performed to adjust the distribution of the second
phases and obtain the bulk Ti-Ni-Hf alloy with the network struc-
ture second phases. Moreover, the microstructure, martensitic
transformation behaviors, mechanical and shape recovery proper-
ties of the sintered Ti-Ni-Hf alloys were studied. The results were as
follows:
(1) The second phases mainly distributed at the surface of the
original alloy powder and constructed non-continuous
network structure for all sintered samples. The higher den-
sity can be achieved by the higher sintering temperature.
When the sintering temperatures were not more than
110 0
/C14 C, the second phase was (Ti,Hf) 2Ni phase, while the
coexistence of (Ti,Hf) 2Ni phase and Hf 2Ni phase was
observed at the sintering temperature of 1 150 /C14 C.
(2) All sintered samples were characterized by the one-step
B19’!B2 martensitic transformation. The improvement of
the matrix strength in the sintered Ti-Ni-Hf alloy with the
network structure second phases should be responsible for
