ABSTRACT 
The computational materials design is performed to develop novel single crystal superalloys with balance of 
multi-objective properties. The entire design process is carried out by materials computation from systems se -
lection to composition determination. The design rules are proposed to optimize compositions of Ni-based single 
crystal superalloys for materials characteristics, using first-principles calculations, CALPHAD calculations, 
theoretical models, and machine learning. By first-principles calculations, the effect of alloying elements on 
structural, elastic, electronic properties of Ni
3
Al are investigated and the Ni-Al-V-Cr-Nb-Mo-Ta-W-Re systems are 
determ5ined. Application of theoretical models and CALPHAD calculations allows the large materials exploring 
space to become narrow, based on materials design criterion: microstructure, density, castability, and process -
ability. The creep resistance of remained alloys is estimated by using machine learning and creep merit index. 
There are 12 alloys selected from 779,625 composition combinations, reaching the balance of multiple design 
properties. With comparing to commercial superalloys, the selected alloy has excellent performance in trade-offs 
between different factors, specifically prominent in the aspect of Cr-Al space of oxidation resistance. This design 
procedure is expected to reduce excessive consumption of cost and time in the process of trial-and-error testing, 
providing a guide on developing potential Ni-based superalloys systems.

Historically, the only way to develop new materials is through 
repeated experiment testing and improvement [14] . This situation 
brings about commercial superalloys developed based on empirical 
rules, which may be unable to meet the balance of multi-objective 
properties [15] . For example, more than 8 kinds of alloying elements 
are added to the Ni-based superalloys than the original heat resisting 
alloy 80Ni20Cr, which is bound to make the relationship between the 
composition, phase and properties more complex [16] . The “ trial and 
error ” methods are hardly performed in a large composition space (the 
combinations of 6 alloying elements with 1 wt% resolution are on the 
order of 10
9 
[13] ). To overcome these difficulties, the computational 
materials design was developed to empower researchers to optimize 
superalloys using theoretical analysis and computer modelling tools 
[17,18] . Before experiment test, researchers primarily optimize com -
positions through theoretical models, CALPHAD calculations, and ma -
chine learning models, which greatly improve the efficiency of 
designing novel superalloys [13,15,19 – 21] . Modeling the mechanical 
properties to optimize the composition, processing and microstructure 
can be used to design novel superalloys with the best target properties 
[22 – 26] . However, the past design cases have a premise that alloying 
elements still rely on empirical research. The alloying element selected 
by computation to assist superalloys theoretical design has not been 
proposed. How to determine a beneficial element combination of creep 
resistance by computation is a key point. The high-throughput experi -
ments and calculations have been draw into Materials Genome Initiative 
[27] , which are effective ways to accelerate development novel super -
alloys [16,28] . Using high-throughput first-principles calculations can 
finish calculative search to compare alloying effect in γ
′
precipitate to 
select alloying elements, which paves the way for the subsequent 
computational design model. Additionally, the combination of theoret -
ical models, CALPHAD calculations, and machine learning models to 
design single crystal superalloys may reach aims of balance of multi- 
objective properties. 
In this work, we performed a computational design of single crystal 
superalloys to achieve balance of multi-objective properties to guide 
novel single crystal superalloys development. Through this method, the 
quantitative relationship between alloy composition and properties can 
be established. The superalloys with excellent theoretical properties 
were obtained by composition optimization, using creep resistance, TCP 
phase, γ
′
content, lattice misfit, density, castability, and processability. 
The first-principles calculations, CALPHAD calculations, theoretical 
models, and machine learning models were applied in this design pro -
cess. Fig. 1 shows the workflow of computational design. In Section 2 , 
the alloying elements are selected through first-principles calculations. 
In Section 3 , an overview of materials design criterion is presented. In 
Section 4 , an analyzing approach of the creep resistance is performed. 
The results and discussion of selection are provided in Section 5 . Finally 
in Section 6 , the concluding remarks are presented.

5.2. Exploring space 
The exploration space of superalloy is not randomly generated. Re 
significantly improves creep resistance of single crystal superalloy, 
called “ Re effect ” [62] . The 1st generation single crystal Ni-based su -
peralloys is Re-free and the 2nd generation has the concentration of Re is 
< 1 at. % (Re in DD406 is 0.7 at. % [63] ). The Re concentration of the 
3rd, 4th and 5th generation are 1 – 2 at. %, 1.6 – 2.2 at. %, and 1.6 – 2.4 at. 
% respectively [64] . To satisfy high temperature oxidation resistance, Al 
and Cr are added to form Al

corrosion resistance; low Al/Cr improves environment suitability and 
loses strength; Sulzer therefore proposed a design area that 8.5 – 12 at. % 
Al and 13.2 – 18 at. % Cr [64] . Note that V is hardly added into single 
crystal because of increasing TCP and decreasing hot corrosion resis -
tance, but it has been found that about 1.2 at. % V can improve creep life 
[66,67] . Counting 13 Re-containing commercial single crystal superal -
loys (Supplementary Table S3), the concentration range of Nb, Mo, Ta 
and W are 0 – 1.1 at. %, 0.3 – 2.6 at. %, 1.4 – 3.1 at. %, and 1.6 – 2.9 at. %.

from 8 to 12 at. %, the V from 1 to 2 at. %, the Cr from 13 to 18 at. %, the 
Nb from 1 to 2 at. %, the Mo from 1 to 3 at. %, the Ta from 1 to 4 at. %, 
the W from 1 to 3 at. %, and the Re from 1 to 3 at. %. Because the

6. Conclusion 
The work is to prove that application of computational design can 
accelerate search of novel Ni-based single crystal superalloys. The 
combination of first-principles calculations, theoretical models, CAL -
PHAD calculations, and machine learning achieves the exploration of 
balance of multi-objective properties, in various alloying elements space 
and the huge composition space. Application of the computational 
design procedures enables potential Ni-based single crystal superalloys 
to be identified, beneficial for continuing to improve the application 
ability of aero engines. The strict screening index is the key to optimize 
the composition, which leads 99.9985% alloys to be rejected. For 
selected alloy, the excellent performance in trade-offs between different 
factors is the advantage, comparing with other commercial superalloys. 
The application accuracy of the design approach largely depends on the 
accuracy of the computational software, and the selected alloys need to 
be tested in future experiments; nevertheless, the design concept pro -
vides an effective way to accelerate the design of Ni-based single crystal 
superalloys. The design procedure is expected to reduce excessive con -
sumption of cost and time in the process of trial-and-error testing and to 
guide further development of potential Ni-based superalloys.
