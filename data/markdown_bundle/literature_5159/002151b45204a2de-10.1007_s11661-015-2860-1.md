A Grain Boundary Fracture Model for Predicting Dynamic
Embrittlement and Oxidation-Induced Cracking in Superalloys
KWAI S. CHAN
Nickel-based superalloys are sometimes susceptible to oxygen embrittlement in the form of
dynamic embrittlement or oxidation-induced grain boundary cracking during services at
elevated temperatures. Dynamic embrittlement is a fracture process that involves the ingress and
diﬀusion of atomic oxygen to induce time-dependent decohesion of grain boundaries. A related
fracture process, also a time-dependent process, is stress-accelerated grain boundary oxidation
and oxide-induced cracking along grain boundaries. In this paper, a micromechanical model is
developed to treat both dynamic embrittlement and oxidation-induced crack growth in Ni-
based superalloys. The model is utilized to assess: (1) the conditions where dynamic embrit-
tlement are dominant, (2) the conditions where oxidation-induced crack growth are dominant,
and (3) the role of oxidation in suppressing dynamic embrittlement in Ni-based superalloys. For
illustration, the grain boundary fracture model is applied to predict the onset of dynamic
embrittlement and oxidation-induced crack growth in superalloys such as IN 718.

I. INTRODUCTION
IT is well-established that the interaction of oxygen
with metallic alloys such as iron-, [1] nickel-,[2–4] and
cobalt-based superalloys [5] at elevated temperatures can
lead to two closely related environmental degradation
mechanisms, which have been coined as (1) oxygen [1,4]
or dynamic embrittlement, [6–9] and (2) stress-accelerated
grain boundary oxidation (SAGBO) and fracture of the
oxidized grain boundaries. [1,4] Both degradation me-
chanisms are time-dependent fracture processes that
proceed along grain boundaries at elevated temperatures
[‡923 K (650 /C176 C)] where intake of oxygen by grain
boundary diﬀusion can occur at relatively short times.
The pioneering work by Bricknell and Woodford
[1–5] has
ﬁrmly established that oxygen embrittlement, [1,4] also
referred to as dynamic embrittlement, [6–9] is likely the
operative degradation mechanism in iron-based super-
alloy IN 903A, cobalt-based superalloy, pure Ni, and
Ni-based superalloys.
[4]
Dynamic embrittlement was ﬁrst used to describe the
loss of tensile ductility and fracture toughness in Ni 3Al
due to oxygen-induced intergranular fracture at both
ambient and elevated temperatures. [6] Under sustained
loading in air, the fracture process proceeds as a time-
dependent grain boundary decohesion process instigated
by the ingress of oxygen from the external surface to the
interior grain boundaries in polycrystalline Ni
3Al. Dy-
namic embrittlement of Ni 3Al can be reduced by the
microalloying of boron to strengthen the grain bound-
aries[6] and by Cr addition to promote the formation of
a protective oxide layer to suppress or delay the ingress
of oxygen to interior grain boundaries. [10,11] As pointed
out by several investigators, [7,9,12,13] dynamic embrittle-
ment of Ni alloys due to the segregation of oxygen to
grain boundaries is similar to temper-embrittlement
induced as the result of segregation of trace elements
such as S, P, and Sn to grain boundaries.
[14,15] These
impurities lower the grain boundary cohesion, promote
intergranular fracture, and reduce both tensile ductility
and fracture resistance.
The time-dependent crack growth response of IN 718
polycrystalline material and bicrystals under sustained
loading at 923 K (650 /C176 C) were reported by Pfaendtaner
and McMahon
[7] and by Krupp. [8,9] Rapid crack growth
rates, d a/dt, were observed as a function of the stress
intensity factor, K, once a growth threshold was exceeded.
The fracture surfaces appeared to show an absence of
oxides on grain boundary facets and the embrittlement
mechanism was identiﬁed to be oxygen-induced inter-
granular cracking or dynamic embrittlement.
[7–9] On the
other hand, the crack growth threshold, Kth, of IN 718
was relatively high in the dynamic embrittlement regime.

The objective of this article is to report the results of an

damage states ranging from oxygen enrichment to oxide

II. DEVELOPMENT OF INTERGRANULAR
FRACTURE MODEL
Time-dependent crack growth in Ni-based alloys
starts with the ingression of oxygen into the metal
surface through grain boundary diﬀusion. In the lit-
erature,
[7–9] it has been reported that ingression of
oxygen into the grain boundaries of Ni-based alloys
such as IN 718 can cause dynamic embrittlement, which
is manifested as time-dependent crack growth similar to
that associated with oxide formation and fracture at
grain boundaries. To model both dynamic embrittle-
ment due to oxygen enrichment at grain boundaries and
oxidation-induced intergranular fracture, oxygen mole-
cules are assumed to ingress and segregate to a grain
boundary located ahead of a crack tip subjected to a
sustained tensile load, as shown in Figure 2(a). The
oxygen-enriched grain boundary may or may not
decohere under the applied tensile stress, depending on
the fracture resistance of the segregated grain boundary.
If brittle decohesion does not occur under continual
oxygen enrichment, the local oxygen content at the
crack tip may be suﬃciently high to cause oxide
formation along the grain boundary, as depicted in
Figure 2(b). Both fracture processes are considered in
the intergranular fracture model to derive the critical
conditions for the onset of crack growth.

V. DISCUSSION
The grain boundary fracture model proposed in this
study is motivated by previous ﬁrst-principles compu-
tations which showed that the nonlinear stress–strain
response predicted by stretching of a unit cell can be
represented in terms of Eq. [ 1]. The advantage of using
Eq. [ 1] to describe the decohesion of grain boundaries
with segregated oxygen is that the degradation response
can be related directly to the oxygen content and the
unknown material parameter, b, can be evaluated from
experimental data such as tensile ductility, as shown in
Figure 4. According to this model, segregation of
oxygen to grain boundaries can weaken the bonding
between Ni and Ni bonds in a manner such that the
elastic modulus and the cohesion stress are both reduced

phenomena in Ni-based superalloys, is that dynamic
embrittlement can occur prior to oxide formation along
grain boundaries when the oxygen-embrittled layer

that of the oxide). Oxide formation along grain bound-

cations forming the oxides. Stress relaxation by creep at

VI. CONCLUSIONS
The conclusions reached in this investigation are as
follows:
1. Grain boundary fracture can be modeled on the ba-
sis of a nonlinear elastic stress–strain relation that
simulates the decrease of elastic modulus and cohe-
sive strength due to stretching of bonds under ten-
sile loading. The grain boundary fracture model
predicts correctly the decrease of tensile ductility of
Ni-based superalloys with increasing oxygen con-
tents and temperatures.
2. The fracture resistance of oxygen-segregated grain
boundary can be as low as the oxide layer that may
form along the grain boundary so that dynamic em-
brittlement can occur prior to oxide formation.
Subsequent oxide formation on the fracture surfaces
in the crack wake can lead to crack-tip shielding
and an increase in the static stress intensity thresh-
old for time-dependent crack growth if the oxide
formation process results in a volume expansion
with a positive transformation strain.
3. The activation energy for dynamic embrittlement
appears to be the heat of oxygen solution in Ni.
4. Dynamic embrittlement predominates when subse-
quent oxide formation on the fracture surfaces re-
sults in little or no transformation toughening so
that crack growth can continue without hindrances.
Oxides that fall into this category include Nb
2O5

and gaseous oxides such as CO, CO 2,S O 2, and
SO3.
5. Oxidation-induced crack growth predominates when
oxide formation results in residual compressive
stresses and transformation toughening so that
crack growth cannot occur until the stress intensity
factor exceeds the static crack growth threshold,
K
th. Oxides that fall into this category include NiO,
CrO, TiO 2, CoO, Cr 2O3, and among others, Al 2O3.
6. The oxygen penetration rate and the oxide penetra-
tion rate in Ni-based superalloys at a given tem-
perature are essentially the same for identical
diﬀusion path. The most important rate-controlling
parameter for the kinetic processes is the oxygen
eﬀective diﬀusivity.
7. For IN 718 at K greater than K
th, time-dependent
crack growth is governed mostly by oxygen diffu-
sivity along oxide interface or grain boundary. The
presence of microcracks in the oxides can lead to
significantly increases in oxygen diffusivity and
time-dependent crack growth rate.
8. A striated oxide structure can lead to variability of
time-dependent crack growth rates at K > K
th
because of a wide range of oxygen diffusivity along
oxide interface.
