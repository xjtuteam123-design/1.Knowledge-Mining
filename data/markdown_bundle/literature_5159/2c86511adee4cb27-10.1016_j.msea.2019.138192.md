ABSTRACT 
Designing an alloy with both high strength and high ductility is a daunting challenge for 
structural materials. CoCrFeMnNi high-entropy alloy with FCC structure has been proven to be 
an ultra-ductile but low-strength alloy. Precipitation hardening is an effective way to solve this 
problem. However, the precipitation mechanisms are generally complex during the solidification 
process because of the composition with five or more elements in high-entropy alloys. We 
reported a designing concept that the liquid phase separation was used to simplify the mechanism 
of precipitates formation. The doped Nd separated from the matrix and precipitated with Ni and 
Mn because of the negative mixing enthalpy. Precipitation occurred randomly at grain 
boundaries and in the interior of grains. Compared to the single-phase FCC matrix, the hardness 
of HCP precipitates was three times of the matrix. The alloys were strengthened with good 
ductility and it was demonstrated by uniaxial tension tests. 
 
Keywords: High-entropy alloys; Mechanical properties; Microstructure; Nanoindentation

1. Introduction 
The equal-atomic high-entropy alloys (HEAs) have been extensively investigated and have 
attracted much attention over the past decade [1-6] due to their superior and unique properties, 
such as excellent ductility [7, 8], high cryogenic fracture toughness [9, 10], impressive 
irradiation stability [11, 12], promising corrosion [13, 14] and wear resistances [15, 16]. The 
well-known CoCrFeMnNi HEA; i.e., Cantor alloy, with face-centered cubic (FCC) single phase 
structure has been proven to be an ultra-ductile alloy; however, its low strength has limited its 
engineering applications [9, 17, 18]. Designing an alloy with both high strength and high 
ductility is a daunting challenge for researchers to overcome the strength-ductility trade-off. 
Precipitation hardening is an effective way to solve this problem, including coherent [12, 19-23] 
and incoherent precipitates [24-26]. However, the precipitation mechanisms are generally 
complex during the solidification process especially when there are five or more elements in 
HEAs. 
Liquid phase separation is an effective way to introduce the second phase in the base alloy 
[27, 28]. The significant parameter, mixing enthalpy ( ∆ ), should be considered to study 
whether the elements are miscible in the liquid phase. The elements with positive mixing 
enthalpy would undergo liquid phase separation in the miscibility gap when the liquid reaches a 
critical temperature during cooling. The liquid would decompose into two immiscible liquid 
phases and maintain until solidification. This method can help to predict and introduce second 
phase in HEAs with complex compositions. 
The purpose of the present study was to investigate the liquid phase separation-induced 
precipitates in CoCrFeMnNi HEA and its microstructures and mechanical properties for strength 
enhancements. To achieve this, different amounts of rare-earth element Nd were doped in 
CoCrFeMnNi HEA using arc melting. The microstructure and the crystal structure were 
examined, respectively, by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and X-ray diffraction (XRD). 
The element distributions and grain orientations were studied using energy dispersive X-ray 
spectroscopy (EDS) and electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD), respectively. The mechanical

2. Materials and Methods 
2.1. Processing of (CoCrFeMnNi) 
100-xNd x HEAs 
Alloy ingots with nominal compositions of (CoCrFeMnNi) 100-xNd x (x = 0, 0.1, 0.5 and 1.0 
named Nd-0, Nd-0.1, Nd-0.5 and Nd-1.0 accordingly) were fabricated by arc melting in the pure 
Ar atmosphere. The purity of each constituent raw metal was at least 99.9 wt.%. Because of high 
vapor pressure of Mn [29], additional 7 wt.% Mn was added to keep equal-atomic in 
CoCrFeMnNi. Before the alloys were melted, a pure Ti ingot was melted to gather residual 
oxygen and nitrogen in the chamber. Each ingot was re-melted at least five times to ensure 
homogeneous mixing. The ingots were then annealed in the Ar atmosphere with the pressure of 3 
Pa at 1000 ºC for 100 h and quenched in water for homogenization. The ingots were 
subsequently cold rolled in the same direction ten times with the thickness reduction of ~8% for 
each rolling to prevent cracking. In the present case, the starting and the final thicknesses of 
ingots were ~16.7 and ~3.3 mm, respectively, with the total thickness reduction of ~80%. The 
cold-rolled sheets were then annealed at 900 ºC for 1 h in the Ar atmosphere and quenched in 
water to obtain fully recrystallized microstructure with reduced grain sizes for strength 
enhancement. 
 
2.2. Characterizations 
The metallographic phase was examined by optical microscopy (OM, Zeiss Axio Lab A1) 
and scanning electron microscopy (SEM, JEOL JSF-7800F) equipped with EBSD and EDS 
detectors. The samples observed by OM were polished and etched using aqua fortis. The surface 
of the sample for EBSD was cleaned by ion miller (Hitachi, IM4000). The lattice constants of the 
matrix and precipitates were calculated based on the X-ray diffraction (XRD, Rigaku TTRAX 3) 
using Cu-Kα radiation with 2 θ from 20º to 80º. The surface of measured section for XRD was 
polished to remove the surface stress. The microstructure of the alloys was observed by transition

3.4. Mechanical properties 
To investigate the difference in mechanical properties between the FCC matrix and HCP 
precipitates, the hardness and the reduced modulus were measured by nanoindentation with a 
Berkovich tip and loading of 5000 µN. The hardness of matrix and precipitates in Nd-1.0 were 
3.88±0.16 GPa and 10.60±0.14 GPa, respectively, while the reduced moduli were 
192.7±1.6 GPa and 178.6±1.0 GPa, respectively, and similar results were obtain ed for 
Nd-0.5. The scanning probe microscopy (SPM) image of Nd-1.0 after indentation is shown in 
Fig. 6. The difference in hardness between the precipitate and the matrix could be visualized 
from the areas of residual marks after indentation. The alloys were strengthened as the hardness 
of precipitates was about three times of the matrix.

σ y, of Nd-0, 
Nd-0.1, Nd-0.5 and Nd-1.0 were 226, 219, 270 and 322 MPa, respectively. Compared to Nd-0,

the plastic deformation. The continuously decreasing strain-hardening rate demonstrated that the 
plastic deformation of the samples at room temperature was dominated by the dislocations slip. 
In addition, as the slipping dislocations annihilated at the interface between the matrix and 
precipitates in Nd-0.5 and Nd-1.0, the strain-hardening rate decreased much faster than that of 
Nd-0.

4. Conclusions 
In summary, the mixing enthalpy can be used to evaluate the liquid phase 
separation-induced precipitates in HEAs, and it is fruitful in designing HEAs with precipitation 
hardening. By doping Nd in CoCrFeMnNi HEA, Nd-Ni-Mn with HCP structure segregated from 
the matrix and precipitated randomly at grain boundaries and in the interior of grains. The 
precipitates with the hardness of three times of the matrix could strengthen the alloy effectively,
