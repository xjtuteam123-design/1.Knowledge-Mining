ABSTRACT 
Conventional pressing and sintering methods provide difficulties in achieving high density for PM TiAl alloys. 
The Ti-48Al-1Fe alloy was prepared via vacuum pressure-less sintering, and the microstructure evolution and 
mechanical properties during the sintering densification process of the mixed powder system were investigated. 
The results shows that the density of the mixed powder reached 98.3 ± 0.14% after pressure-less sintering for 
3 hours under high vacuum conditions at 1300
◦
C. The microstructure of the as-sintered alloy is a fine duplex 
structure composed of α
2
/ γ lamellar colonies and the composite structure. The ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of 
the as-sintered alloy at room temperature is 315 MPa. The density of the as-sintered alloy reach more than 99.9% 
after non-capsule hot isostatic pressing (HIP). The as-HIPed alloy exhibits an UTS of 332 MPa at room tem -
perature. At high temperatures, it maintains high strength, with an UTS of 408 MPa and yield strength of 
386 MPa at 900
◦
C, which is comparable to that of forged TiAl alloy. Furthermore, the elongation of the sample at 
900
◦
C is 20.0%. This process offers a novel method for sintering PM TiAl with high density and mechanical 
properties at a low cost and near-net shape. 
1. Introduction 
TiAl-based alloys are increasingly being considered as lightweight, 
high-strength structural materials in the aerospace and automotive in -
dustries due to their superior high-temperature strength and oxidation 
resistance. Their density is less than half that of nickel-based alloys [1, 
2] . Recent findings indicate that γ -TiAl is being utilised as a substitute 
for Inconel 718 in the manufacturing of turbine blades and compressor 
blades [3] . Nevertheless, the processing and manufacturing of TiAl al -
loys pose challenges because to their limited ductility, hot workability, 
and fracture toughness. To attain high mechanical properties in TiAl 
alloys, accurate control of the cooling rate during the smelting process, 
repeated billet forging, and cyclic heat treatment procedures are 
necessary in ingot metallurgy (IM) technologies [4 – 6] . Powder metal -
lurgy (PM) offers an attractive near-net shape technology that could 
produce alloys with a uniform microstructure and fine grains [7 – 10] . 
Cold isostatic pressing (CIP) and pressure-less sintering is an economical 
and attractive approach for fabricating TiAl alloys [11] . 
Two typically employed powders for sintering PM TiAl are pre- 
alloyed (PA) powder, which is produced by gas atomization and 
rotating electrode atomization, and blend element (BE) powder. The PA 
powder has a mostly spherical shape and possesses limited sintering 
capability. The primary cause of this is mostly attributed to the poor self- 
diffusion coefficient and inadequate sintering driving force. In order to 
obtain high densities, TiAl alloys often need sintering at temperatures 
near the solidus. However, this process unavoidably results in grain 
coarsening, leading to a decrease in mechanical properties. Xia et al. 
[12] calculated that the diffusion coefficient of TiAl is approximately

The Kirkendall effect is a key issue that limits high density in the

also a potential risk of pollution from the tank. Although there are dif -
ficulties encountered in BE powder sintering, it continues to be a pri -

comparison to PA powder.

a high level of density.

5. Conclusions 
In this work, a high-density Ti-48Al-1Fe alloy was prepared via 
vacuum pressure-less sintering of low-cost mixed powders, and a 
microstructure with a composite structure was developed. The tensile 
properties of samples were tested at RT, 800 ℃ and 900 ℃ , the un -
derlying mechanisms for the improved high-temperature mechanical 
properties of the composite structure were revealed. The following 
conclusions can be drawn: 
(1) TiAl

2 
and Fe powders are mixed with D

(3) The Ti-48Al-1Fe alloy described in this paper has a simple 
composition and exhibits high short-term properties at high 
temperatures. The sintered sample has an UTS of 315 MPa and an
