ABSTRACT 
The practical application of refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) has been limited by their brittleness at room 
temperature. This study prepared fine-grained HfMoNbTaTi refractory high-entropy alloys using a powder 
metallurgy process combining mechanical alloying (MA) and spark plasma sintering (SPS). This method effec -
tively addresses the issue of room temperature brittleness and achieves a favorable balance between strength and 
plasticity. The resulting sintered alloy comprises two body-centered cubic (BCC) solid solution matrices and a 
range of nanoprecipitates, including the γ -phase, σ -phase, and α -phase. With increasing sintering temperature, 
the average grain size increased from 0.45 μ m (at 1400 
◦
C) to 4.56 μ m (at 1700 
◦
C). Simultaneously, the content 
of the γ -phase gradually decreases due to the greater influence of mixing entropy. Under quasi-static loading, the 
fine-grained multiphase structure not only benefits from grain boundary strengthening and precipitation 
strengthening effects, but also enhances the deformation uniformity, ultimately improving both the strength and 
plasticity. At 1450 
◦
C, the sintered alloy exhibited a compressive yield strength of 2325 ± 8.40 MPa and a plastic 
strain of 26.44 ± 1.17 %. These values represent a 35.73 % increase in yield strength and a 120.33 % increase in 
plastic strain compared to those of the as-cast alloy. The deformation process at room temperature is mainly 
governed by the multiplication of screw dislocations and cross-slips. In the middle and late stages of deformation, 
the grains exhibit a preferred orientation, further enhancing the plastic strain. In summary, this study presents a 
practical approach for overcoming the issue of room temperature embrittlement in RHEAs. 
1. Introduction 
The development and application of new ultra-high temperature 
resistant structural materials hold immense promise in curbing emis -
sions and enhancing energy efficiency [ 1 , 2 ]. Yet, the prevalent 
nickel-based superalloys, despite their widespread use in 
high-temperature environments, face a critical limitation with a melting 
point of approximately 1300 
◦
C, constraining their operational ceiling 
[ 3 , 4 ]. Furthermore, while ceramic materials tailored for 
high-temperature conditions exist, their deficiency in metal-like damage 
tolerance poses a significant hurdle in extreme settings marked by 
elevated temperatures and pressures [ 5 ]. Refractory high-entropy alloys 
(RHEAs), characterized by their composition of various refractory metal 
elements, have excellent room/high-temperature strength, as well as 
strong resistance to oxidation, wear, and corrosion. These alloys provide 
a promising avenue for the development of 
ultrahigh-temperature-resistant structural materials [ 6 – 8 ]. Unfortu -
nately, predominantly possessing body-centered cubic (BCC) structures 
with a sole deformation mechanism, RHEAs face limitations in their 
strain-hardening capabilities and frequently exhibit brittleness at room 
temperature [ 6 , 9 , 10 ]. These factors restrict the load-bearing reliability 
of RHEAs as structural materials and their forming performance. 
With the in-depth exploration of the material space, researchers have 
uncovered the remarkable deformation capabilities of the HfNbTaTiZr 
alloy. Specifically, it demonstrates an impressive 50 % compressive 
strain resistance without fracturing at room temperature. Regrettably, 
its compressive yield strength at room temperature stands at a mere 929 
MPa [ 11 ]. By introducing the Mo element, the compressive yield 
strength of the HfMoNbTaTiZr alloy increases to 1512 MPa, while the 
compressive strain decreases to 12 % [ 12 ]. Subsequent removal of the Zr

At present, the powder metallurgy process (P/M) has emerged as an 
optimal method for tailoring high-performance RHEAs on demand, 
effectively addressing common casting defects [ 14 , 15 ]. Firstly, nano -
crystalline particles with uniform composition are synthesized at room 
temperature by mechanical alloying (MA) process [ 16 , 17 ]. Subse -
quently, the preparation of bulk RHEAs is achieved through various 
sintering techniques, such as hot pressing sintering (HPS) [ 18 – 20 ] and 
spark plasma sintering (SPS) [ 10 , 14 , 21 ]. SPS, in particular, can realize 
the solidification and sintering process of alloy powders in a short time, 
while maintaining a uniform composition and avoiding grain coarsening 
to the greatest extent [ 15 , 22 ]. The RHEAs prepared by MA and subse -
quent SPS generally exhibit excellent room temperature properties, 
owing to enhanced strength and plasticity facilitated by grain refine -
ment. It should be noted that impurity elements introduced during alloy 
preparation, such as O, C, and Fe, significantly influence the alloy ’ s 
microstructure and properties [ 23 ]. The impact of this effect varies 
depending on the impurity element content. Relevant studies have 
shown that the addition of trace elements such as O, Al, and Ti to HEAs 
promotes the formation of the spinodal structures or nano-precipitates, 
thereby enhancing strength and plasticity [ 24 – 27 ]. However, excessive 
trace element content can damage the alloy ’ s plasticity. For example, in 
TiZrHfNb alloy, plasticity gradually diminishes when O content exceeds 
2 at.%, and in TiZrNbTa, when Al content surpasses 1.5 at.% [ 24 , 26 ]. 
Therefore, it is significant to study the control and mechanism of im -
purity elements in the P/M process for regulating the microstructure and 
mechanical properties of RHEAs. 
In this paper, fine-grained HfMoNbTaTi RHEAs are prepared by P/M 
(MA + SPS) process. The microstructures obtained consist of two BCC 
solid solution matrices and various nanoscale precipitated phases due to 
the introduction of O and Fe elements during the preparation process. 
The study investigates the mechanism of formation of these micro -
structures and their roles in the plastic deformation process. The room 
temperature compressive yield strength and plastic strain of HfMoNb -
TaTi RHEA are increased by 35.73 % and 120.33 %, respectively, due to 
the synergistic effect of solid solution strengthening, fine grain 
strengthening, and precipitation strengthening. The deformation 
mechanisms of HfMoNbTaTi RHEA are revealed, and the strategy and 
effect of the multiphase fine-grain structure obtained by the MA + SPS 
process on the mechanical properties of the alloy are demonstrated.

3.2. Microstructure of as-sintered alloys 
3.2.1. Phase composition of as-sintered alloys 
Fig. 3 (a – b) displays the XRD patterns of the HfMoNbTaTi alloys 
sintered at various temperatures. XRD analysis of the pre-alloyed pow -
ders revealed a single-phase BCC structure, consistent with the TEM 
results ( Fig. 2 (c)). The lattice constant of the pre-alloyed powders is 
calculated to be 3.3018 Å, which is slightly smaller than that of the as-

ternal defects in the pre-alloyed powders and grain growth. However, 
the shape of the (100) peak remains constant, while the other diffraction 
peaks split. Particularly at high angles ( Fig. 3 (b)), the peaks accompa -
nied by sidebands indicate the presence of two BCC phases (labeled 
BCC1 and BCC2) within the as-sintered alloys. The lattice constants of 
the two phases exhibit some variation, with a difference of approxi -
mately 0.02 Å at different sintering temperatures ( Fig. 3 (c)). Further -
more, faint diffraction peaks are detected near the (110) peak, 
particularly at lower angles, indicating the formation of 
nano-precipitated phases.

Due to variations in orientation between adjacent grains, disloca -

boundaries. Consequently, grain boundaries impede the displacement of 
dislocations, thereby enhancing the material strength. The extent of this

limited assistance in facilitating the crossing of dislocations across the 
grain boundary. As a result, a higher external stress must be applied to

To summarize, the deformation mechanism of the as-sintered 
HfMoNbTaTi alloy at room temperature was revealed through EBSD 
and TEM observations. Fig. 11 shows a schematic diagram of the 
deformation mechanism under quasi-static loading at room tempera -
ture. These observations indicate that plastic deformation initiates with 
the plane slip of dislocations. However, when dislocation motion is 
impeded by lattice distortions caused by nanoprecipitated phases and 
impurity elements such as C, and O, cross-slip occurs, leading to the 
formation of dislocation dipoles and bending dislocations. As the plastic 
strain increases, the dislocation density within the dislocation dipoles 
increases, leading to the formation of a GND band. A further increase in 
the dislocation density causes lattice deflection on both sides of the GND 
bands, leading to the formation of LAGBs within the GND band. Addi -
tionally, a significant number of screw dislocations multiply and ho -
mogenize inside the subgrains. Concurrently, the grains undergo 
preferential orientation, forming a < 001 > //CD texture ( Fig. 9 (b3)). 
With continued plastic strain, dislocations become entangled within the 
subgrains. Furthermore, the grains undergo further rotation, resulting in 
the existence of < 001 > //CD and < 111 > //CD textures ( Fig. 10 (c1) and 
9(c3)). 
As shown in Fig. 7 (a – b), the compressive yield strength of the as- 
sintered HfMoNbTaTi alloy gradually decreased as the sintering tem -
perature increased, while the plastic strain gradually increased. The 
microstructure analysis revealed that an increase in the sintering tem -
perature led to both grain growth and a decrease in the γ -phase. As a 
result, the decrease in yield strength can be attributed to the weakening 
of the grain boundary strengthening effect and the precipitation 
strengthening effect. However, it should be noted that larger sizes result 
in higher dislocation densities stored within the grains. The rapid 
saturation of dislocation accumulation and subsequent high stress 
concentration are considered significant factors contributing to the 
premature failure of BCC RHEAs [ 75 ]. Moreover, the high dislocation 
density in the GND bands can cause local stress concentration, further 
promoting damage [ 24 ]. Therefore, reducing the γ -phase is beneficial 
for reducing the GND band density and facilitating a uniform distribu -
tion of dislocations. In addition, the alloy ’ s density gradually increases 
with increasing sintering temperature, thereby reducing the number of 
internal defects such as sintering pores. These combined aspects result in 
increased plastic strain.

5. Conclusions 
In this study, fine-grained HfMoNbTaTi alloys with various nano- 
precipitates are prepared by the powder metallurgy process (MA +
SPS), exhibiting an excellent strength-ductility trade-off. The effects of 
sintering temperature (1400 
◦
C – 1700 
◦
C) on the microstructure and 
mechanical properties of HfMoNbTaTi alloy are systematically studied. 
Based on the theory of material thermodynamics, the mechanism of 
impurity elements such as O and Fe in the formation of precipitated 
phases is revealed. Additionally, the strengthening and deformation 
mechanisms of the HfMoNbTaTi alloy are discussed. 
(1) Following 50 h of milling, the pre-alloyed powders with nano- 
crystalline and single BCC structures are obtained. The ele -
ments within the pre-alloyed powders are uniformly distributed. 
However, impurity elements (such as O and Fe) are inevitably 
introduced during the MA process, which has a significant impact 
on the microstructure and mechanical properties of the sintered 
body. 
(2) After SPS sintering, the HfMoNbTaTi alloy forms a multiphase 
fine-grained structure comprising two BCC solid solutions as the 
matrix and a variety of nano-scale precipitated phases. The pre -
cipitates are primarily composed of the γ -phase with an FCC 
structure (rich in Hf and Fe), the σ -phase with an orthorhombic 
structure (rich in Hf and O), and the α -phase with an FCC struc -
ture (rich in Hf). With the increase in sintering temperature, the 
content of the γ -phase decreases gradually and nearly disappears 
at 1650 
◦
C. Simultaneously, the average grain size exhibited a 
notable increase, from 0.45 μ m (1400 
◦
C) to 4.56 μ m (1700 
◦
C). 
(3) Based on the thermodynamic theory of materials and the reported 
alloy system, the mechanism of precipitate formation is dis -
cussed. O and Hf have a higher affinity, so they combine to form 
the σ -phase, which avoids the formation of other types of harmful 
oxides. The influence of mixing enthalpy during low-temperature 
sintering leads to the formation of γ -phase between Hf and Fe. As 
the sintering temperature increases, the content of the γ -phase 
decreases gradually due to the offset of the influence of mixing 
entropy by mixing enthalpy. The α -phase is mainly derived from 
the precipitation of the Hf element during furnace cooling. 
(4) The multi-phase fine-grained structure, consisting of nanoscale 
precipitates ( γ -phases, σ -phases, and α -phases), improves both 
strength and plasticity under quasi-static loading. The pre -
cipitates provide a strengthening effect, while also transforming 
plane slip into wavy slip, resulting in more uniform plastic 
deformation. In addition, this study fully exploits the role of grain 
refinement in improving strength and plasticity, thereby 
achieving the common improvement in strength and plasticity 
observed in the HfMoNbTaTi alloy. The alloy sintered at 1450 
◦
C 
exhibits compressive yield strength of 2325 ± 8.40 MPa and 
plastic strain of 26.44 ± 1.17 %, demonstrating an excellent 
balance between strength and plasticity. 
(5) The excellent strength exhibited by the as-sintered HfMoNbTaTi 
alloys at room temperature stems from the synergistic contribu -
tion of several strengthening mechanisms. Substitutional solid 
solution strengthening is identified as the dominant contributor. 
Compared to the as-cast alloy, the presence of trace amounts of Fe 
elements has been observed to enhance the substitutional solid 
solution strengthening effect by about 190 MPa. The calculations 
indicate that the nano-precipitated phases increase the strength 
mainly through the Orowan dislocation bypassing mechanism. In 
addition, grain refinement and lattice distortion caused by the C 
and O elements contribute to the improvement of the room 
temperature strength.
