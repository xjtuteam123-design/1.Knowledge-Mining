a b s t r a c t 
Commercial wrought high-strength Cu-Cr-Zr alloys face limited high-temperature properties due to the 
rapid coarsening or dissolution of Cr precipitates. Here, we report a laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) 
fabricated Cu-0.84Cr-0.42Zr (wt.%) alloy with exceptional heat resistance after aging. Primary Cr@Cu5 Zr 
phase ( ∼39.8 nm) with core-shell structure and a high density of heat-stable dislocations were intro- 
duced from the rapid solidiﬁcation of LPBF and enabled the alloy to gain signiﬁcant improvement in 
high-temperature properties. After aging treatment, secondary Cr and Cu51 Zr14 phases ( ∼3.4 nm) were 
precipitated, in which Zr solute was segregated at one side of the Cr phase, enhancing the thermal sta- 
bility of Cr phase. The excellent combinations of strength and thermal conductivity were achieved at 
or above 400 °C. Particularly at 600 °C, the aged sample not only exhibited a high tensile strength of 
∼196 MPa, which signiﬁcantly surpassed that of wrought Cu-Cr-Zr alloys, but also possessed a thermal 
conductivity of ∼349 W/(m K) comparable to that of pure copper.

1. Introduction 
Cu-Cr-Zr alloys are widely employed as heat sink materials in 
nuclear fusion and aerospace ﬁelds owing to their high strength, 
excellent thermal conductivity as well as good radiation and wear 
resistance [ 1–3 ]. Due to their exceptional combinations of ther- 
mal conductivity and mechanical properties below ∼300 °C, Cu- 
Cr-Zr alloys have been widely used to fabricate components for 
high-temperature applications, including liquid rocket engines [ 4 ]. 
However, improving the thrust-to-weight ratio of liquid rocket en- 
gines requires higher operating temperatures and current commer- 
cial wrought Cu-Cr-Zr alloys cannot meet the future demands of 
high-temperature strength and thermal conductivity for later gen- 
erations of liquid rocket engines [ 5 , 6 ]. When considering high- 
temperature conditions from 400 °C to 600 °C, previous reports 
have indicated that the inferior properties are mainly attributed to 
the coarsening (Ostwald ripening) or dissolution of Cr nanoprecip- 
itates [ 7–9 ].

At present, literature reports have conﬁrmed that two ap- 
proaches can improve the high-temperature properties of tradi- 
tional fabricated Cu-Cr-Zr alloys: (1) reducing the interfacial en- 
ergy between the Cr nanoprecipitates and α-Cu matrix by interfa- 
cial solute segregation to enhance the thermal stability of Cr nano- 
precipitates [ 10–12 ]; (2) increasing dislocation density through se- 
vere plastic deformation to improve high-temperature mechani- 
cal properties, although the recovery and annihilation of disloca- 
tions are inevitable in high-temperature environment [ 13 , 14 ]. For 
the ﬁrst approach, Qu et al. [ 12 ] demonstrated that the addition of 
0.05 wt.% Si signiﬁcantly improved the softening resistance of the 
wrought Cu-0.8Cr-0.12Zr (wt.%) alloy at 600 °C owning to the Si so- 
lute segregated at the interface between the Cr phase and Cu ma- 
trix. For the second approach, Vinogradov et al. [ 14 ] investigated 
the structure, thermal stability, and properties of the Cu-0.44Zr- 
0.2Zr (wt.%) alloy fabricated by multi-pass equal-channel angu- 
lar pressing (ECAP), and found that ECAP gave rise to substantial 
strengthening and enabled the alloy to exhibit good thermal sta- 
bility. 
As one of the most promising methods in additive manufac- 
turing, laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) exhibits rapid solidiﬁca- 
tion characteristics, which is beneﬁcial to achieve the nanosized 
precipitates segregated by interfacial solute [ 15–18 ]. Yang et al.

cipitates with a core-shell structure and high-density dislocations 
with good thermal stability were achieved in LPBF-fabricated Cu- 
0.84Cr-0.42Zr (wt.%) alloy, which means that the alloy is expected 
to achieve improved high-temperature properties. 
Therefore, fully dense Cu-0.84Cr-0.42Zr (wt.%) alloy was fab- 
ricated by LPBF and then directly aged in this work. The mi- 
crostructural characteristics, high-temperature properties, and frac- 
ture mechanisms of the as-fabricated and direct aging (DA)-treated 
samples were systematically studied. In addition, the formation 
mechanism and coarsening resistance of the primary and sec- 
ondary precipitates were discussed in detail to further determine 
the mechanism of enhanced strength at high temperatures. 
2. Experimental procedure 
Tailored gas-atomized Cu-Cr-Zr alloy powder was used as raw 
material in this study. The detailed chemical compositions of the 
powder and as-fabricated sample determined by inductively cou- 
pled plasma optical emission spectrometry (ICP-OES 730, America) 
are listed in Table 1 . As shown in Fig. 1 (a, b), the powder exhib- 
ited a near-spherical shape with a few satellites, thereby enhanc- 
ing the loose packing density and ensuring its ﬂuidity simulta- 
neously. The particle diameter distribution of the powder is pre- 
sented in Fig. 1 (c), wherein D10 , D50 , and D90 values of 18.7, 33.2, 
and 51.8 μm, respectively, while the average diameter was mea- 
sured as 39.4 μm. The cross-section morphology and element dis- 
tribution of the powder reveals that the Cu matrix is composed of 
ﬁne equiaxed grains, while Cr and Zr elements are uniformly dis- 
persed in the matrix, as depicted in Fig. 1 (d). 
The sample was fabricated by a Lim-X260 device (Tianjin Ra- 
dium Laser Technology Co., Ltd., China) ﬁlled with high-purity 
(99.99 %) argon gas. The schematic and operating principle of the 
device is shown in Fig. 1 (e). During the LPBF process, the build- 
ing substrate temperature was maintained at 80 °C. As illustrated 
in Fig. 2 (a), a rotation scanning strategy of 67 ° between layers 
was implemented to mitigate the residual thermal stress in the as- 
fabricated sample [ 21 ]. The LPBF-fabricated bulk samples are illus- 
trated in Fig. 2 (b), with dimensions of 60 mm × 15 mm × 2.5 mm. 
The speciﬁc process parameters can be found in our previous work 
[ 20 ]. Subsequently, the as-fabricated sample was treated by DA at 
450 °C for 2 h to further enhance the precipitation strengthening 
and thermal conductivity [ 22 ]. 
The microstructure was examined using a scanning electron mi- 
croscope (SEM, Zeiss MERLIN Compact, Germany). The grain char- 
acteristics and geometrically necessary dislocations (GNDs) were 
analyzed utilizing an electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) sys- 
tem with a scan step of 2 μm. The grain sizes for the as-fabricated 
and DA-treated samples were determined using the equivalent cir- 
cle diameter law [ 23 ]. Prior to SEM and EBSD detection, the sam- 
ples needed to be electrochemically polished in a 70 % aqueous 
phosphoric acid solution at 2 V for 150 s. The precipitates were 
identiﬁed utilizing a ﬁeld emission transmission electron micro- 
scope (TEM, FEI Themis Z, America) at an accelerating voltage of 
300 kV. The TEM was equipped with an energy dispersive spec- 
trometer (EDS) to determine the chemical composition of the pre- 
cipitates. 
The thermal conductivity was measured using a laser thermal 
conductivity meter (LFA 467 HyperFlash, Germany) at temperatures 
of 40 0, 50 0 and 60 0 °C. The samples for measuring thermal con- 
ductivity were cylindrical in shape with a diameter of 12.6 mm 
and a thickness of 2 mm. Tensile tests were carried out using a 
double-column electronic universal testing machine (Instron 5582, 
America) with a constant rate of 0.6 mm/min at temperatures of 
40 0, 50 0 and 60 0 °C. According to the GB/T 228.2–2015 standard, 
the dimensions of tensile samples are illustrated in Fig. 2 (c). The 
tensile test loading direction was perpendicular to the building di- 
rection (BD) of samples. To ensure thermal homogeneity, the sam- 
ples were maintained at the test temperature for 20 min prior 
to conducting tensile testing. The thermal conductivity and tensile 
properties were measured three times and the average values were 
calculated. Fracture surface and cross-section near fracture surface 
were examined using SEM and EBSD to determine the tensile de- 
formation characteristics, respectively. The evolution of precipitates 
after tensile was investigated using TEM.

3.2. High-temperature properties 
Fig. 7 (a, b) shows the engineering stress-engineering strain 
curves at high temperatures for the as-fabricated and DA-treated 
samples, respectively. The high-temperature tensile curves exhib- 
ited a similar behavior: the stress increased slowly following elas- 
tic deformation and then decreased after the peak stress was 
reached. However, the as-fabricated sample displayed serrated ﬂow 
characteristics during plastic deformation at 400 °C, which was a 
typical Portevin-Le Chatelier effect [ 31 ]. This can be attributed to 
dynamic strain aging, which arises from the dynamic pinning and 
unpinning interactions between dislocations as well as Cr and Zr 
solutes [ 32 , 33 ]. The corresponding values for yield strength ( σy ), 
ultimate tensile strength ( σu ), and elongation ( εel ) are provided 
in Table 2 . The σu of the as-fabricated sample was 250 ± 2 MPa, 
293 ± 3 MPa and 195 ± 2 MPa at 400 °C, 500 °C and 600 °C, re- 
spectively. After DA treatment, the σu was 374 ± 3 MPa, 305 ± 1 
MPa and 196 ± 5 MPa at 400 °C, 500 °C and 600 °C, respec- 
tively. For the DA-treated sample, the σu decreases with the ten- 
sile temperature increases, which can be attributed to that the ac- 
tivation of the multi-slip system under high-temperature condition 
reduces the resistance to dislocation motion [ 34 ]. In comparison, 
an anomalous increase in σu at 500 °C was observed in the as- 
fabricated sample, which was attributed to that the supersaturated 
solutes of Cr and Zr in the α-Cu matrix caused by rapid cooling 
of LPBF can precipitate from the Cu matrix and provide precipita- 
tion strengthening contribution during the high-temperature ten- 
sile process [ 35 ]. Table 2 illustrates the thermal conductivity at

3.3. Fracture characterization 
Fig. 8 shows the tensile fracture surface morphology of the DA- 
treated samples at high temperatures of 400 °C, 500 °C and 600 °C. 
As shown in Fig. 8 (a), a rough fracture surface accompanied by 
numerous shallow dimples was presented at 400 °C, indicating a 
typical ductile fracture pattern. At 500 °C, the fracture surface be- 
came ﬂat with reduced and shallower ductile dimples, as well as 
the emergence of cleavage steps, exhibiting a mixed brittle-ductile 
fracture pattern, as depicted in Fig. 8 (b). At 600 °C, the fracture 
surface exhibited a complete cleavage steps feature, which was 
a typical brittle fracture pattern, as shown in Fig. 8 (c). The brit- 
tle fracture at 600 °C may result from the detrimental impact of 
oxygen-induced embrittlement, in which oxygen is prone to diffuse 
along the grain boundaries during the testing process to reduce the 
cohesive strength of the alloy [ 48 ].

During the solid-state phase transformation of Cu-Cr-Zr alloys, 
it has been conﬁrmed that the secondary Cr phase is precipitated 
from α-Cu matrix ﬁrst, then the secondary Cux Zry phase [ 14 , 60 , 61 ]. 
Generally, the Zr solute in Cu-Zr alloys preferentially segregates 
and precipitates near grain boundaries and dislocations due to the 
presence of strain ﬁelds [ 62 , 63 ]. In fact, the pre-precipitated Cr 
phase can also result in a non-uniform elastic strain at the Cr/Cu 
interface [ 64 ]. Zhang et al. [ 65 ] proved through ﬁrst-principle cal- 
culations that the Zr solute exhibited a pronounced interfacial seg- 
regation energy to segregate toward the Cr/Cu interface, thereby 
facilitating the relaxation of strain ﬁeld and reducing interfacial 
energy at the Cr/Cu interface. Therefore, besides dislocations and 
grain boundaries, the secondary Cu51 Zr14 phase is prone to precip- 
itate around the secondary Cr precipitates. It should be noted that 
the interfacial segregation energy of Zr solute decreases to zero 
when the coverage of Zr solute at the Cr/Cu interface reaches ∼1/3 
[ 65 ]. Thus, the Cu51 Zr14 phase precipitated from α-Cu matrix was 
enriched only at one side of the Cr phase, as illustrated in Figs. 6 (b) 
and 12 (b).

5. Conclusions 
In this study, the microstructural evolution as well as the ten- 
sile properties and thermal conductivity at high temperatures of 
LPBF-fabricated Cu-Cr-Zr alloy were analyzed. The key ﬁndings are 
summarized as follows: 
(1) The primary Cr@Cu5 Zr phase ( ∼39.8 nm) with core-shell struc- 
ture is obtained in the as-fabricated sample. After DA treat- 
ment, secondary Cr and Cu51 Zr14 phases ( ∼3.4 nm) are precip- 
itated, in which Zr solute is segregated at one side of the Cr 
particle, enhancing the thermal stability of the Cr precipitates. 
The heat-stable dual-nanoprecipitates contribute to achieving 
enhanced strength and thermal conductivity at high tempera- 
tures. 
(2) The as-fabricated sample has a dislocation density of 5.1 
× 1013 m-2 . After DA treatment, the dislocations are effectively 
retained. The dislocations derived from the liquid-solid tran- 
sient transition of LPBF process exhibit excellent thermal stabil- 
ity and can mitigate the deterioration of dislocation strengthen- 
ing contribution at high temperatures. 
(3) The DA-treated sample exhibits an excellent combination of 
σu and thermal conductivity at temperatures of 40 0, 50 0 and 
600 °C. Particularly at 600 °C, the DA-treated sample not only
