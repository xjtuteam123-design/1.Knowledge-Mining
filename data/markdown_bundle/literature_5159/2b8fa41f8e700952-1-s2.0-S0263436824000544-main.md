ABSTRACT 
Microstructural characteristics and mechanical properties of as-cast Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr- x V ( x = 2, 5, 10, 15 
and 20 at.%) and Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr- y Sn ( y = 1, 3, 5 and 7 at.%) alloys are systematically investigated in this 
study. The individual addition of V or Sn obviously promotes the eutectoid decomposition of metastable phase 
(Nb
3
Si). All alloys consist of the primary Nb
5
Si
3 
and the eutectic Nbss/Nb
5
Si
3 
structures. For the V-added alloys, 
the low content of added V (2 at.%) cannot change the constituent phases (Nbss and α -Nb
5
Si
3
) of Nb-16Si-22Ti- 
2Al-2Cr alloy. However, the other V-added alloys consist of the primary β -Nb
5
Si
3 
and the eutectic structures of 
Nbss/ β -Nb
5
Si
3
. Similarly, addition of 1 at.% Sn also cannot affect the constituent phases of Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr 
alloy, while the β -Nb
5
Si
3 
and Nb
3
Sn are formed with the increase in Sn content. Moreover, the addition of V or Sn 
promotes the formation of chaotic eutectics and suppresses the appearance of reticulate and cellular eutectics. 
The fracture toughness of the alloys is continuously elevated by the V addition, while it gets weakened when the 
content of added Sn exceeds 1 at.%. The high-temperature compressive strength of the V-added alloys increases 
when the V content is below 10 at.%, and then it decreases with increasing V content. The Sn-added alloys, 
except 3Sn alloy, exhibit higher compressive strength than Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr alloy, due to the increase in 
volume fractions of brittle phases ( β -Nb
5
Si
3 
and Nb
3
Sn). Moreover, the crystallographic orientation relationships 
among the constituent phases are also explored before and after high-temperature compression. 
1. Introduction 
The Nb
–
Si based alloys are the composite materials that consist of 
brittle silicide phases (Nb
3
Si, Nb
5
Si
3
) and ductile niobium-based solid 
solution (Nbss) phase [ 1 ]. The silicides afford strength, creep and 
oxidation resistance at high temperature, and the Nbss provides the 
fracture toughness at room temperature. Moreover, the Nb
–
Si based 
alloys have the high melting temperature ( > 1700 
◦
C) and low density 
(6.6 – 7.2 g/cm
3
) [ 2 ]. These characteristics enable the alloys to replace 
the nickel-based superalloys in the application of aerospace turbine 
blades. 
However, the Nb
–
Si binary alloys consisting of Nbss and Nb
3
Si, 
exhibit inferior room-temperature fracture toughness and high- 
temperature oxidation resistance [ 3 – 5 ], which severely limit their in -
dustrial application. To eliminate these deficiencies, many methods 
have been used to improve the mechanical properties of Nb
–
Si based 
alloys. The improvement measures can be classified into the following 
two types: preparation and alloying methods. Compared with the 
traditional casting methods, the advanced preparation methods, such as 
directional solidification [ 6 , 7 ], spark plasma sintering (SPS) [ 8 ] and 
selective laser melting (SLM) [ 9 ], can significantly ameliorate the 
properties of Nb
–
Si based alloys. Nonetheless, these advanced prepa -
ration methods involve huge consumption of energy and financial re -
sources. In contrast, the alloying methods are effective and convenient 
to improve the properties [ 10 , 11 ]. Moreover, minor additions of alloy -
ing elements can effectively influence the constituent phases and 
microstructure, thereby altering mechanical properties of Nb
–
Si based 
alloys. The alloying elements include Ti, Al, Cr, Hf, Mo, W, Zr, Ge, V, Sn, 
etc. [ 12 , 13 ]. Titanium (Ti) element is favorable to improve the tough -
ness through reducing the Peierls – Nabarro barrier energy [ 14 ]. More -
over, the high-temperature oxidation resistance of Nb
–
Si based alloys 
gets significantly improved with the addition of Ti [ 15 , 16 ]. However, 
the additive amount of Ti usually remains at around 14 – 24 at.% to avoid 
the reduction in strength. Furthermore, the addition of a small amount

Based on our previous study [ 22 ], the Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr alloy 
was selected as the matrix alloy and then V and Sn were added to the 
matrix alloy in this study. To date, the investigations on the addition of V 
and Sn in the Nb
–
Si based alloys predominantly focus on a narrow 
range of contents. Therefore, the added contents of V and Sn were in the 
wide ranges to systematically investigate the effects of individual 
addition of V and Sn on the microstructural characteristics and me -
chanical properties of Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr alloy. The purpose of this 
study is to achieve a balance between toughness and compression 
strength. 
2. Experimental procedures 
The nominal compositions of the V-added and Sn-added alloys have 
been listed in Table 1 . For the convenience of narration, the V-added and 
Sn-added alloys were named as 2 V, 5 V, 10 V, 15 V, 20 V, 1Sn, 3Sn, 5Sn 
and 7Sn, respectively. These nine alloy button ingots (100 g) were 
prepared through the non-consumable vacuum arc furnace under an 
argon atmosphere in a water-cooled copper crucible. Each button ingot 
was remelted at least five times to ensure uniform distribution of 
alloying elements. High-purity ( > 99.4 wt%) raw materials of each 
element were used in this study. The cubic samples with dimensions of 6 
× 6 × 3 mm
3 
were used for analyses of microstructure and constituent 
phases, which were cut from the center of these button ingots by electro- 
discharge machining (EDM). The cubic samples were sanded and pol -
ished to no scratches. The constituent phases of the alloys were detected 
by X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis. The field-emission scanning elec -
tron microscopy (SEM, Quanta 200FEG) was used to characterize the 
microstructural morphologies of the alloys. Further, the crystallographic 
orientation relationships between Nbss and Nb
5
Si
3 
phases were explored 
by electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) method. The field-emission 
transmission electron microscopy (TEM) was used for observing the 
phase orientation relationships and dislocations. 
The three-point bending test was conducted on an electronic uni -
versal testing machine (5 kN/20 kN) to acquire the room-temperature 
fracture toughness ( K
Q
). The single-edge U-notched three-point 
bending specimens with dimensions of 20 × 4 × 2 mm
3 
and the notch 
of 2.0 mm, were cut from the center position of each ingot. The notch 
root radius of the sample was approximately equivalent to the radius of 
the metal wire used in EDM. Every alloy was cut to three bending 
specimens to acquire the value of K
Q 
and then their average value was 
obtained to represent the toughness of the alloys. The detailed calcula -
tion formulas of K
Q 
were proposed in our previous study [ 22 ]. The 
fracture surfaces of bending specimens were observed by SEM to explore 
the room-temperature fracture mechanism. 
The high-temperature compressive test was performed on cylindrical 
specimens with the height of 9 mm and diameter of 6 mm. A dynamic 
thermal simulation testing machine (Gleeble1500D) was used to mea -
sure the compressive strength at 1200 
◦
C. The specimens for measuring 
the compressive strength were first heated to 1200 
◦
C at the rate of 
20 
◦
C/s, then the temperature was maintained for 3 min to ensure 
uniform heating. Finally, the specimens were compressed under the 
strain of 20% at a rate of 0.001 s
 1
. During the hot compression, high 
purity argon gas was filled into the working chamber as a protective gas 
after environmental pressure evacuated to below 5 × 10
 2 
Torr. For 
observing the high-temperature compressive microstructure, the cylin -
drical specimens were immediately water-cooled after compression. 
3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Microstructural analysis 
The analysis results of XRD patterns of as-cast Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr- 
x V ( x = 2, 5, 10, 15 and 20 at.%) and Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr- y Sn ( y = 1, 
3, 5 and 7 at.%) alloys are presented in Fig. 1 . The results indicate that in 
the V-added alloys ( Fig. 1 (a)), only Nbss and α -Nb
5
Si
3 
phases exist when 
the V content is up to 2 at.%, while the α -Nb
5
Si
3 
gets completely 
replaced with β -Nb
5

3 
with the increase in the V content beyond 2 at.%. 
This phenomenon reveals that V can promote the formation of β -Nb
5
Si
3 
in the Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr alloy. Notably, the highest peak of Nbss 
clearly shifts toward right with the increase in the V content, as shown in 
Fig. 1 (a ’ ). According to the Bragg eq. 2 d ⋅ sin θ = n λ , the interplanar dis -
tance ( d ) and angle of diffraction ( θ ) exhibit an inverse proportional 
relationship, when the wavelength of X-rays ( λ ) is a constant. Thus, 
increasing value of θ indicates that the d of Nbss lattice decreases. This 
result indicates that the addition of V leads to the shrinkage of Nbss 
lattice, which is attributed to the difference in the atomic radii of V 
(1.35 Å) and Nb (1.47 Å). Kim et al. [ 19 ] also reported that the lattice 
parameters of Nbss diminished with V addition. 
The constituent phases include Nbss and α -Nb
5
Si
3 
in the as-cast 1Sn 
alloy, as presented in Fig. 1 (b). However, α -Nb
5
Si
3 
undergoes complete 
transformation transforms into β -Nb
5
Si
3
, when the contents of Sn exceed 
1 at.%. Further, a new phase (Nb
3
Sn) exists, other than Nbss and 
β -Nb
5
Si
3
, in the as-cast 5Sn and 7Sn alloys. The results indicate that Sn 
not only promotes the formation of β -Nb
5
Si
3
, but also prompts the 
production of Nb
3
Sn. The highest peak of Nbss slightly shifts toward left 
with the addition of Sn, as shown in Fig. 1 (b ’ ). The atomic radius of Sn is 
1.57 Å, which is larger than that of Nb atom [ 23 ]. Thus, the solid so -
lution of Sn in Nbss leads to the expansion of Nbss lattice, which is 
opposite to the effect of V on Nbss lattice. Interestingly, the constituent 
phases of Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr alloy included Nbss, α -Nb
5
Si
3 
and few 
Nb
3
Si in our previous study [ 22 ]. However, no Nb
3
Si is observed in the 
V-added and Sn-added alloys, which manifests that the addition of V or 
Sn contributes to the decomposition of the metastable Nb
3
Si phase.

4. Conclusions 
The effects of individual addition of V or Sn on microstructural 
characteristics and mechanical properties of Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr alloy 
are systematically investigated in the present study. Based on the results, 
the following conclusions can be drawn as follows: 
(1) The low contents of added V (2 at.%) or Sn (1 at.%) do not affect 
the constituent phases of Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr alloy. However, 
their high contents obviously promote the formation of β -Nb
5
Si
3
. 
Moreover, the addition of V or Sn is conducive to the formation of 
chaotic eutectics but not for the reticulate and cellular eutectics. 
(2) The fracture toughness of Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr alloy gets 
elevated with the increase in the V content, because V promotes 
the formation of chaotic eutectics and changes the fracture mode 
of Nbss from cleavage fracture to ductile fracture. The 1Sn alloy 
exhibits the toughness higher than that of the Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al- 
2Cr alloy owing to the existence of chaotic eutectics. However, 
the toughness of other Sn-added alloys decreases with increasing 
Sn content because of the appearance of brittle Nb
3
Sn. 
(3) The high-temperature compressive strength of V-added alloys 
enhances for the V content of below 10 at. %; however, it 
weakens with the increase in the V content. In contrast, the 
strength of Nb-16Si-22Ti-2Al-2Cr alloy improves with the addi -
tion of Sn. In addition, the crystallographic orientation relation -
ships among the constituent phases change after hot 
compression, owing to the flow of Nbss.
