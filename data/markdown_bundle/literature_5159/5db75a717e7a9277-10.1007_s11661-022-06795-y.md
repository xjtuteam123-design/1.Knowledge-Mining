TOPICAL COLLECTION: PROCESSING AND APPLICATIONS OF SUPERALLOYS
High-Temperature CoNi-Based Superalloys
Strengthened by c¢-(Ni,Co)3(Cr,Al,Ti,X): The Effect
of Refractory Elements
ZHIDA LIANG, JONATHAN D.H. PAUL, ANDREAS STARK, ANDREAS BEZOLD,
STEFFEN NEUMEIER, MATHIAS GO¨KEN, and FLORIAN PYCZAK
Recent research on Co-based and CoNi-based alloys revealed that the Co–Al–W-system
provides interesting properties, however, the high content of W addition triggers high mass
density of alloys which limits its industrial application. Therefore, new high temperature
superalloys based on the Co–Ni–Al–Ti-system with high content Cr and strengthened by
c¢-(Ni,Co)
3(Cr,Al,Ti) precipitates, have been developed, and the eﬀect of diﬀerent refractory
element additions was investigated. STEM-EDS and HEXRD were employed to determine the
elemental partitioning behavior and the lattice misﬁt between the c and c¢ phases. Ta and Nb
strongly concentrate within the c¢ phase, whereas Mo weakly partitions to the c phase. W
distributes equally between the c and c¢ phases. These new superalloys have an unexpectedly
high positive misﬁt compared with some conventional Ni-based superalloys and Co-based
superalloys. Nb and Ta additions increase the lattice misﬁt further, while Mo and W decrease
the lattice misﬁt. The eﬀect of refractory elements alloying on the yield stress at room
temperature was evaluated by analyzing the contributions of diﬀerent strengthening mecha-
nisms. Alloying with Nb or Ta signiﬁcantly improves precipitation strengthening by increasing
the antiphase boundary energy. Mo has the highest solid solution strengthening eﬀect in the c
phase, followed by W. Compared with some conventional Ni-based superalloys, the investigated
new CoNi-based superalloys exhibited better mechanical properties at high temperature, which
indicates that these compositionally complex alloys are possible candidates for high temperature
applications.

I. INTRODUCTION
TURBINE disc materials, which usually operate at
maximum service temperatures of 650–750 /C176 C, require a
combination of a high yield strength, microstructural
stability, creep resistance, fatigue strength and oxidation
resistance as well as a low density and low costs of raw
materials.
[1] In order to attain these requirements, it is
necessary to add numerous alloying elements. Sta-
te-of-the-art Ni-based superalloys usually contain up
to ten alloying elements
[1–3] for optimized mechanical
properties through a favorable combination of c¢ vol-
ume fraction, size, strength and phase stability, c/c¢
lattice misﬁt, oxidation resistance, solid-solution
strengthening of both the c and c¢ phases[4] and
grain-boundary strengthening. [5,6]
Over the last two decades many researchers have
started to focus on Co-based superalloys, [7–15] since a
new family of c¢-strengthened Co–Al–W-based superal-
loys was reported in 2006. [16] These new superalloys,
however, have a comparably high density due to high
contents of W. Therefore, there are so many researchers
have took great eﬀort on reducing W content and
density of Co-based and CoNi-based alloys.
[17–20]
In addition, many researchers started to combine the
‘‘high-entropy alloy’’ concept with the precipitation
strengthening of superalloys by L1 2 ordered precipitates.
The addition of Al and Ti to high-entropy alloys
(HEAs)
[16,21–23] and medium entropy alloys (MEAs)[24,25]
has been studied and high tensile strengths were obtained
at room temperature. However, these alloys typically had
a small volume fraction of the L1 2 phase ( ~ 20 pct), [26]
which is only stable at low-temperatures, and thus
unsuitable for many practical applications at temperature
over 700 /C176 C. Some scientists tried to develop refractory
HEAs and exploit their high temperature application.
However, refractory HEAs [27] based on W–Nb–Mo–Ta
and W–Nb–Mo–Ta–V are hampered by the exceptional
high density and high cost of raw materials.
The work presented in this paper tries to provide new
insights into the design of L1
2-strengthened alloys,
which are related to CoNi-based superalloys with high
content of Cr, with the aim towards high-temperature
applications, such as turbine discs. In order to increase
the service temperature to use such L1
2 hardened
superalloys, it is, therefore, necessary to increase the c¢
volume fraction and thus the creep life. In addition,
refractory elements usually have low diﬀusion rates in
Co or Ni alloys which is beneﬁcial for improving creep
life.
[1,28,29] Therefore, compositionally complex CoNi-
based superalloys, such as Co–35Ni–15Cr–5Al–5Ti and
its derivative alloys containing small amounts of the
refractory elements Nb, Ta, Mo and W are investigated
in this work. The study had several speciﬁc objectives:
(1) to systematically characterize the c/c¢ microstructure
after aging at 900 /C176 C; (2) to determine the partitioning
coeﬃcient of the elements between the c/c¢ phases; (3) to
determine the yield stress at high temperature and assess
their potential for high temperature applications.
II. EXPERIMENTS
A. Materials and Specimen Preparation
A series of alloys was produced, whose compositions
(all given in atomic pct) are given in Table I. The
baseline composition was Co–35Ni–15Cr–5Al–5Ti–0.1B

Ta (CoNi–2Ta), 2 at.pct Nb (CoNi–2Nb), 3 at.pct of W
(CoNi–3W) and a combination of 2 at.pct Mo
and 1 at.pct W (CoNi–2Mo1W). Based on the alloy
CoNi–2Mo1W, more complex alloy compositions,
Co–35Ni–15Cr–5Al–7Ti–2Mo–1W–0.1B (CoNi–2Mo1W2Ti)
and Co–35Ni–15Cr–5Al–5Ti–2Mo–1W–1Ta–0.1B
(CoNi–2Mo1W1Ta), were also investigated to study the
combined eﬀects of diﬀerent transition elements. For
comparison, ingots of established Ni-based superalloys,
Udimet 720Li (developed by Special Metals Corporation,
Huntington, WV) and TWM-4M3 (developed by NIMS,
Tsukuba, Japan),
[2,31,32] were produced. It is noteworthy
that these alloys are usually employed in a wrought
processing state. In this work, they were produced by the
same arc melting method as the series of CoNi-superal-
loys for a better comparability. To order to make fair
comparison, both of C and Zr addition were omitted and
only 0.1 at.pct B was added to Udimet 720Li and
TWM-4M3 similar to the other alloys. In order to
indicate that both diﬀer from their commercial parent
alloys in terms of composition and processing, they are
named U720Li-EXP and TWM-EXP, hereafter. All the
alloys were produced as 70 g ingots that had been melted
at least 7 times on a water-cooled copper hearth using a
laboratory-scale argon arc melting unit. The as-cast alloys

48 hour (all treatments were done under vacuum). The
homogenized alloys were aged at 900 /C176 C in air for
220 hour, and subsequently air cooled to room
temperature.
The SEM samples were prepared by vibrational
polishing after cutting and grinding. For TEM sample

F. Implications for Future Improvements of the New
CoNi-Based Superalloys
The developed CoNi-based superalloys provide very
promising properties. However, their microstructure
and thus their mechanical properties can be even further
optimized by appropriate processing and heat treatment
procedures, e.g. forging and multi-step heat treatments.
The grain as well as the precipitate sizes of the
investigated samples are quite large. If a grain size D
of about 20 lm is realized a further increase in yield
strength of about 168 MPa can be expected from grain
boundary strengthening. Similarly, if the aging heat
treatment is adjusted such that the size of the c¢
precipitates is much smaller and the c¢ precipitates are
only sheared by strong-pair coupled dislocations, the
yield strength will be also further enhanced (see
Figure 10). However, the size of the precipitates c¢ (r
s)
should be tailored to give optimal fatigue and creep
properties and a combined distribution of primary,
secondary and ternary c¢ precipitates is beneﬁcial for
ductility, fatigue and creep properties.
[62] In any case,
the developed CoNi-based superalloys have a great
potential in terms of mechanical properties which
should be investigated further in future studies.
V. CONCLUSION
High-temperature CoNi-based superalloys with high
content of Cr, strengthened by a L1
2-ordered
c¢-(Ni,Co)3(Cr,Al,Ti) phase in a c matrix with refractory
element additions (Nb, Ta, Mo and W), were investi-
gated in the present study. The main ﬁndings are:
1. Nb and Ta concentrate preferentially in the c¢
phase, Mo in the c matrix and W shows a balanced
distribution between the c and c¢ phases. Accord-
ingly, Nb and Ta increase the c¢ volume fraction
stronger than Mo or W. The c¢ solvus temperature
significantly increases by the addition of Nb, Ta or
by an increased Ti content, followed by Mo and W
additions. However, Nb or Ta additions or a higher
Ti content narrow the solution heat treatment
window.
2. The lattice misfit between the c and c¢ phases in the
alloy CoNi-baseline is higher than that of conven-
tional Ni-based superalloys and other Co-based
superalloys. Nb or Ta additions or a higher Ti
content can further increase the lattice misfit, while
Mo and W additions have the opposite effect.
3. Vickers hardness and the compressive yield stress
indicate that Ta, Nb and W additions give rise to
significant strengthening. Mo addition results in
