Introduction Reduced-activation ferritic steels containing 7��9% chrom- ium are one of candidate alloys for structural materials in fu- Sion energy system, because of their phase stability and irra- diation embrittlement resistance. 1'2) The steels have some dis- advantages in low tensile strength at higher temperatures than 5000C, in corrosion resistance, and ferro-magnetism. Increas- ing chromium content in these steels improves their strength and corrosion resistance, and it degrades the ductility. Re- cently, Abiko et al. showed that the ductility of Fe��Cr al- loys with relatively high chromium content (18% and 50%) was improved by purification, especially by the reduction of interstitial impurities such as carbon and nitrogen atoms. The highly purified Fe��50Cr alloy possessed a high strength at higher temperatures4) and corrosion resistance,5) and no a-precipitate formation was observed in matrix and even at grain boundaries during the heat treatment in the Fe��50Cr alloys aged at 7000C for 5 x 103 h.6) Kako and Isozaki et al. investigated the effect of tungsten atoms in the Fe��50Cr alloys on the tensile properties.7'8) The tensile strength in- creased with tungsten content, and the ductility of the Fe�� 50Cr��8W alloy was better than that in the Fe��50Cr alloy at above 4000 C. Under the irradiation with high-energy particles, the puri- fied high-chromium Fe��Cr alloys may be expected to have a high strength, good ductility, and excellent corrosion resis- tance. However, it is known that phase transformation under irradiation occurs often at different temperatures and differ- ent solute contents as compared with the ordinary phase di- agrams 9' 10) Therefore, it is very important for the applica- tion to nuclear materials such as the structural materials of fusion nuclear system to examine the phase stability in the alloy during irradiation. It is suggested that to improve the irradiation resistance of high- chromium Fe��Cr alloy, the suppression of a'-precipitate for- mation is effective. The radiation-induced or/and enhanced at-precipitate during the irradiation is related to chromium segregation through point defect flow to the defect sinks. To reduce the a-precipitate formation, it needs to develop or/and improve the alloys as described by the following methods: (1) to suppress the chromium segregation, (2) to reduce the mobility for chromium atoms, doping some elements, (3) to make many internal trapping sites of chromium atoms, such as, the high number density of carbides or radiation-induced carbides, (4) to form the other type of precipitates including chromium atoms. As the other choice, it may be better to use the alloys at higher temperatures than 6000C, in order to suppress the a'-precipitate formation, and (5) to increase chromium content up to 70��90% in Fe��Cr alloys, in order to reduce the formation of the brittle precipitates such as d- phase. 5. Conclusions Neutron-in-adiation effects on tensile propenies of high- purity Fe��(9��50)Cr and Fe��50Cr��(0��8)W alloys were inves- tigated. The tensile specimens of SS-3 type were irradiated at 400, 500, and 6000C to 0.4 dpa in the JRR-3M (Modified Japan Research Reactor-3). The tests were performed at the same temperature as the irradiation ones at a strain rate of 4.4 x 10��4 s��l after the il-radiation. The results obtained are as follows: (1) The yield and ultimate strengths increased by the ir- radiation, and the increment of the strength tended to increase with increasing chromium and tungsten contents. (2) The tensile properties of the irradiated alloys were ductile, except the Fe��50Cr and Fe��50Cr��W alloys in-adiated at lower temperatures than 5000C. (3) The tensile properties in the Fe��(9��50)Cr alloys can divide three regions, i.e., ductile, brittle, and the intermedi- ate regions, in the brittle-ductile diagram obtained from the propenies of the tensile curves which depend on chromium content and irradiation temperature. On the other hand, the tensile properties of the Fe��50Cr��(0��8)W alloys can divide two regions, i.e., ductile and brittle, depending on the ilTadia- tion temperature. (4) These behaviors of tensile properties in the Fe��Cr and Fe��Cr��W alloys may be closely related to the at-precipitates and dislocation loops formed by the irradiation.
