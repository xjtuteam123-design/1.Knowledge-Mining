1. Introduction
Nickel based superalloys can be categorized into two types
according to their strengthening mechanisms, namely solid-
solution strengthened alloys such as alloy 617 and precipitation
strengthened alloys such as alloy 738. Among these materials, pre-
cipitation strengthened superalloys have been generally accepted
for gas turbine components such as blades and vanes, since the
alloys have better properties than solid-solution strengthened
alloys. /H9253′ of Ni3Al(Ti,Ta) and /H9253′′of Ni3Nb are mainly used for
strengthening these precipitation strengthened superalloys [1–9].
Previous studies reported that components made of precipita-
tion strengthened superalloys suffered from a variety of damage
such as wear and cracking in spite of their superior properties dur-
ing operation. It is well-known that the temperature of the gas used
to spin the turbines exceeds 1000 ◦C and these temperatures are
constantly increasing to improve the efﬁciency of the power plants
[9,10].
Repair techniques for these components are being developed
due to the recent high price of the components globally. Welding
and transient liquid phase bonding (TLP) bonding are considered
to be the candidate techniques for repair. Additionally, welding is

Meanwhile, many studies on welding Ni based superalloys have
been conducted and these have revealed that welding superalloys
is known to be very difﬁcult, because alloying elements such as Cr,
Ti and Al, added to enhance the material properties, cause harm-
ful precipitation and cracking in the welds. There is, however, still
a lack of study on TLP bonding of /H9253′-precipitation strengthened
superalloys for gas turbines.
In this study, the focus was on investigating TLP bonding charac-
teristics of two types of /H9253′-precipitation strengthened superalloys
named GTD111 and Udimet520 under various bonding conditions.
GTD111 is produced by the directional solidiﬁcation (DS) process,
while Udimet520 is a wrought alloy. TLP bonding was carried out
with these materials, and the microstructural characterization was
investigated. The difference in TLP bonding behaviors and effects of
the microstructures and melting points of the base metals on these
behaviors are discussed thoroughly.

Particularly, GTD111 specimens solidiﬁed directionally were

based on the liquidus temperature of the ﬁller metal.
TLP bonding operation was carried out at 1140, 1170 and 1190 ◦C
for 60, 90 and 120 min under vacuum of 1×10−2 Pa, followed by
furnace cooling to 25 ◦C after the bonding ﬁnished.
For microstructural characterization, the TLP bonded specimens
were sectioned perpendicular to the bond and polished up to 1 /H9262m
surface ﬁnish. The polished specimens were etched using Mables

of joints were examined by optical microscopy (OM) and a ﬁeld
emission scanning electron microscope (FE-SEM). Chemical anal-
yses of various phases formed in the joints were conducted using
JOEL JXA-8530F electron probe micro-analysis (EPMA).
JmatPro simulation program was used to calculate melting
points and precipitaion behaviors of GTD111 and Udimet520.
3. Results and discussion
Fig. 2 compares the microstructures of the base metals. Direc-
tional solidiﬁcation occurred following the direction of the arrow
in Fig. 1 and only one grain boundary (indicated by the dotted line)
was observed in GTD111. The bright area (indicated as “A” in Fig. 2)
is the dendrite core and the dark area (B) is the dendrite boundary
in this material. Udimet520, on the other hand, has a polycrystalline
structure with polygonal grains. A dendritic structure, observed in
GTD111, is not observed in Udimet520.
OM microstructures around the joint of GTD111 are shown in
Fig. 3. In the specimen bonded at 1140 ◦C for 60 min, the joint can be
distinguished clearly from the base metal. An area with a lot of ﬁne
particles called the diffusion affected zone (DAZ) is also observed
between the joint and the base metal. Black phases (indicated as
“A”) are observed in the center of the joint bonded at 1140 ◦C for
60 min. If holding time is not enough for isothermal solidiﬁcation
to be completed, residual melted ﬁller metal will be solidiﬁed via
athermal solidiﬁcation and produce brittle phases with melting
point depressing elements such B and Si in the joint [11]. The joint
bonded at 1140 ◦C for 60 min with such phases indicates that a
holding time of 60 min is not enough for completion of isothermal
solidiﬁcation at 1140 ◦C.
In the microstructure of the joint bonded at 1140 ◦C for 90 min,
there are no phases verifying that isothermal solidiﬁcation could be
completed in joints bonded for holding times longer than 90 min at
1140◦C. DAZ is still observed in the areas between the joints and
the base metals.
Meanwhile, through the OM examination of the specimens
bonded at 1170 ◦C and 1190 ◦C, it is revealed that the microstruc-
tural behavior of the specimens seems to be distinctly different
from that of the joints bonded at 1140 ◦C. In other words, the
interface between the joint and the base metal is not clearly
distinguished and there is no DAZ in the vicinity of the joints. Addi-
tionally, changes in the microstructure of the specimens bonded at
temperatures higher than 1170 ◦C according to the bonding condi-
tions could not be determined by OM observation.

bonded at various conditions. The microstructure of the specimen
bonded at 1140 ◦C for 60 min has a phase-free joint and also DAZ
between the joint and the base metal. From this result, it could
be concluded that isothermal solidiﬁcation was completed in this

It is well-known that isothermal solidiﬁcation in TLP joints is
controlled by the diffusion rate of melting point depressing ele-
ments such as B from the joints into the base metals. EPMA analysis
was performed around the joint and DAZ of GTD111 made at
1140 ◦C for 90 min for further examination of the joints solidi-
ﬁed isothermally and the results of this analysis are shown in
Figs. 5 and 6.
As revealed in Fig. 3, no phases were detected in the joint,
and in Fig. 5, it is clearly conﬁrmed that the joint was solidiﬁed
isothermally at the bonding conditions. Differences in concentra-
tions of alloying elements between the joint and the base metal are
observed.
The concentration of Cr, Ti and Al in the joint is lower than that
in the base metal. In contrast, the joint has higher Si and Ni con-
centration than the base metal. These concentration differences of
the elements are induced by lower diffusion rate of these elements.
In the case of boron, with a high diffusion rate, the concentration
difference is nearly undetectable between the joint and the base
metal, but a number of precipitates with B are detected in DAZ of
the specimen. The EPMA result on the DAZ shows that these precip-
itates are ﬁne bulky Cr carbo-boride particles and larger needle-like
Cr carbo-borides (in Fig. 6). The precipitation of the Cr carbo-borides
is caused by boron diffusion into the base metals during the bond-
ing process. And TiC (indicated as “A”) in the DAZ is the carbide
precipitated during the superalloy production process.
The joint has lower Ti and Al concentration than the base metal
and the effect of this on /H9253′-precipitation behavior in the joint was
examined. The SEM imagery veriﬁes that /H9253′precipitates were not
formed throughout the joint, although very ﬁne /H9253′ precipitates
were found in the narrow region indicated by the dotted line neigh-
boring the DAZ in Fig. 7. The reason for this is thought to be the low
Ti and Al concentration in the joint to precipitate /H9253′due to their low
diffusion rates. Because of this, previous studies reported that post
bonding heat treatment for homogenization of these elements was
an essential step to precipitate /H9253′in the TLP joints after bonding has
been completed [16,17].
EPMA analyses were carried out around the joints of Udimet520
bonded at 1140 ◦C for 60 min and the results are given in
Figs. 8 and 9.The concentration difference of alloy elements
between the joint and the base metal is observed here as well. Fine
Cr-borides and needle-like Cr-borides, which were identiﬁed in the
DAZ of the GTD111 specimen, were ﬁrst detected in the grains
and also along the grain boundaries in the DAZ. No phases were
observed in the joint and this means that isothermal solidiﬁcation
was completed in this joint.
From the experimental results described above, it could be
inferred that isothermal solidiﬁcation generally develops more
quickly in Udimet520, the wrought alloy, than in GTD111, the direc-
tional solidiﬁed alloy, under the same bonding conditions.

It might be expected that isothermal solidiﬁcation by boron diffu-
sion develops more quickly at 1170 ◦C and 1190 ◦C than at 1140 ◦C,
because the diffusion rate of boron depends on temperature. How-
ever, these phases found in this joint suggest that the joint did
not solidify isothermally, although the specimens were bonded at
higher temperatures than 1140 ◦C, and previous studies explained
this phenomenon as follows: [16,18,19]
If the specimens are bonded at 1170 ◦C or higher temperatures,
more dissolution and more diffusion of the base metal alloying ele-
ments, particularly Ti, from the base metal into the joint ﬁlled with
the melted ﬁller metal could be expected in comparison to lower
bonding temperatures. In this joint with a high concentration of Ti,
isothermal solidiﬁcation is controlled not by boron but by titanium.
However, the diffusion rate of Ti in Ni alloys is much lower than that
of B, because the element is a substitutional solute in Ni alloys. Con-
sequently, the isothermal solidiﬁcation rate of the joint is depressed
by Ti with low diffusion rate and the isothermal solidiﬁcation could
not be completed during the bonding time. Residual melted metal
ends up solidifying non-isothermally and forming various phases
such as Ni3Si and eutectic-like phases containing Ti from the base
metal during cooling in the joint. In addition, these phases degrade
the mechanical properties of the joint [18–20].

In this article, TLP bonding behaviors of /H9253′-precipitation
strengthened superalloys, GTD111 and Udimet520 were inves-
tigated carefully. The analysis results proved that the bonding
temperature should be optimized to achieve joints solidiﬁed
isothermally for these superalloys. Isothermal solidiﬁcation could
be accomplished completely in the joints at 1140 ◦C. But increasing
the bonding temperature depressed the rate of isothermal solidiﬁ-
cation in the joints. The joints of GTD111 and Udimet520 showed
different bonding behaviors and these were thought to be caused
by the differences in microstructure and melting point of GTD111
and Udimet520 superalloys.
4. Conclusions
According to the results of this study, the following conclusions
can be drawn:
(1)
 In the analysis result on the joint made at 1140 ◦C for var-
ious holding times, isothermal solidiﬁcation was completed
in GTD111 after 90 min and in Udimet520 after 60 min. The
isothermal solidiﬁcation generally develops more quickly in
Udimet520, the wrought alloy, than in GTD111, the directional
solidiﬁed alloy. This is thought to be caused by the num-
ber of grain boundaries in these materials, because the grain
boundaries are important paths for diffusion of melting point
elements into the base metals.
(2) There was no precipitation of /H9253′particles in the joint zone when
isothermal solidiﬁcation completed, because of the low con-
centration of Ti and Al, and ﬁne Cr carbo-borides were detected
in the diffusion affected zone along the joints. Additional heat
treatment is necessary to precipitate /H9253′in the joints after bond-
ing is ﬁnished.
(3) Various phases such as Si-Ti-Ni compounds and TiC carbides
resulting from residual melted ﬁller metals were observed in
the joints bonded at 1170 ◦C and 1190 ◦C. This meant that the
joint did not solidify isothermally and increasing the bond-
ing temperature depressed the rate of isothermal solidiﬁcation
in the joints. This solidiﬁcation behavior was thought to be
induced by elements with low diffusion rate brought from the
base metal into the joint at temperatures higher than 1170 ◦C.
