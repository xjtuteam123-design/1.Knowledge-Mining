ABSTRACT
Random high-angle grain boundaries are the preferential sites for hydrogen-induced cracking (HIC) in 
precipitation-hardened superalloys, while the stronger or weaker effect of Σ 3 twin boundaries ( Σ 3s) to resistant 
HIC remains ambiguous. Here we systematically investigated the different roles of Σ 3s on HIC in modified A286 
and IN718 using uniaxial tensile tests and multiscale microstructural characterization. Our results reveal that 
strengthening precipitates with a mirror-symmetric structure at Σ 3s is a universal phenomenon in superalloys. 
Atomistic analyses furtherly reveal that the sandwich-like γ ′ / γ ″ / γ ′ co-precipitates and segregation of Ni, Nb and Ti 
at the Σ 3s in IN718 take the responsible for potential sites for strain localization and subsequently HIC along the 
Σ 3s under the combination of hydrogen and mechanical loading. While spherical γ ′ precipitates and segments 
without γ ′ at Σ 3 s in modified A286 exhibit a strong resistance to crack initiation and propagation.
1. Introduction
Superalloy A286 and IN718 are austenitic precipitation-hardened 
alloys, widely used for harsh environments due to their high strength 
and superior corrosion resistance [1 – 3] . Since the low diffusivity and 
high solubility of hydrogen [4 – 6] , these alloys have also attracted 
immense attention in high-pressure hydrogen environments that require 
high-strength alloys. For precipitation-strengthened alloys, the high 
strength is typically achieved by face-centered cubic γ ′ (Ni

coherent relation. However, these alloys exhibit higher hydrogen 
embrittlement susceptibility compared with single-phase austenitic al -
loys due to their higher strength [7] . High-energy or so-called random 
grain boundaries (RGBs) in polycrystalline alloys play a key role on the 
diffusion and trapping of hydrogen, which is found to be the preferred 
sites of hydrogen-induced cracking (HIC) cracks for material failure 
[8 – 12] .
Σ 3 twin boundaries, as typical coincidence site lattice boundaries 
preferred under the framework of grain boundary engineering, are 
thought to be inherently resistant to grain boundary degradation such as 
creep [13,14] , fatigue [15,16] , and stress-corrosion cracking [17,18] . 
Recent advancements in developing single-phase and 
precipitation-strengthened HE-resistant alloys have focused on Σ 3s due 
to their high surface separation energy and low hydrogen solubility [10, 
19 – 21] . It has been shown that the high HE resistance of a commercially 
pure nickel alloy Ni-201 can be obtained by introducing a high pro -
portion of Σ 3s [22] , similar results were also reported in 304 [23] and 
TWIP [24] steels. In contrast, Σ 3s are found to be the vulnerable sites of 
strain/stress localization and HIC cracks in γ ″ -strengthened nickel-based 
945X [25] . In addition, Matteo Seita et al. [26] discovered that Σ 3s are 
susceptible to hydrogen-induced crack initiation but resistant to prop -
agation because of different physical mechanisms in Inconel 725. 
Differently, distinct roles of Σ 3s are found in γ ′ -strengthened Fe-Ni based 
alloys, which both exhibit high resistance to HIC crack initiation and 
propagation [27] . This is a puzzling and interesting result that the role of 
Σ 3s on HIC is so different for the single-phase alloys, γ ″ - and/or 
γ ′ -strengthened superalloys.
Based on the strengthening mechanisms, γ ″ and/or γ ′ - precipitates 
offer much greater resistance to glide dislocations movement due to 
their ordered structure, which can render the alloys high strength. The 
dislocation slip will be further increased with the presence of hydrogen, 
which can result in strain localization and even cracking along Σ 3s. 
While strain localization associated with Σ 3s was reported in numerous 
studies is generally attributed to the Schmid factor of Σ 3s and elastic 
anisotropy across Σ 3s [25,28,29] , the physical mechanisms are still

4. Discussions
4.1. Strain localization associated with Σ 3s
Further analyses show that high KAM values at RGBs are observed 
both in MA286 and IN718 deformed to a strain of 2 %, reflecting a 
significant strain localization associated with RGBs ( Fig. 7 a and b). Such 
high strain localization suggests that RGBs have very limited capability 
to accommodate strains, and thus, exhibit poor resistance to the initia -
tion and propagation of hydrogen-induced intergranular cracks. It does 
not obviously engender strain localization at Σ 3s in MA286. However, 
strain localization is found at Σ 3s or at the intersection sites between Σ 3s 
and RGBs in IN718 (indicated by white arrows in Fig. 7 b). Due to the 
disordered structure at RGBs, there is a certain lattice misfit on both 
sides of the grain boundary, which has a strong retarding effect on 
dislocation movement. Dislocations tend to accumulate and form strain 
localization at the RGBs. Hydrogen carried by the dislocations can be 
dumped at the RGBs. The local plastic enhancement caused by the 
interaction between hydrogen and dislocations will make the RGBs the

4.2. Mechanisms for precipitation related hydrogen-induced Σ 3 cracking
It is known that certain γ ″ and γ ′ precipitates are considered as 
hydrogen reversible traps [38,39] , which can increase the HE suscep -
tibility of alloys. However, Σ 3 s are often thought to be more resistant to 
the HIC crack initiation and propagation due to their lower binding 
energy for hydrogen trapping compared to RGBs. As mentioned earlier,

precipitates-decorated Σ 3 s are important factors in determining the 
propensity for HIC of MA286 and IN718. Firstly, the unit cell and atomic 
arrangements on the close-packed (111) plane of both γ ″ and γ ′ pre -

its original crystal symmetry [40] . The other two directions must 
contain four matrix dislocations due to the lower crystal symmetry 
( Fig. 8 c). In contrast, considering the symmetry of γ ′ precipitates, all 
three slip directions are equivalent in the γ ′ ( Fig. 8 d). The shearing of γ ″ 
precipitates in the matrix is more difficult than that of γ ′ precipitates due 
to that the number of available slip systems in γ ″ precipitates is less than 
that in γ ′ precipitates. Besides, it has been proved that the interplay 
between γ ″ and γ ′ precipitates in the γ matrix results in complex defor -
mation pathways of coprecipitates in IN718 [41,42] . The most impor -
tant thing to note is that the deformation pathway of γ ′ / γ ″ / γ ′ 
co-precipitates is different from that of monoliths of γ ″ and γ ′ pre -
cipitates, as the presence of the respective other phase constrains the 
number of energetically favorable mechanisms [41] . This is generally 
associated with an increased shear resistance to sliding dislocations. 
When moving dislocations in DSBs encounter Σ 3s and cut through 
further in IN718, they can be blocked by the γ ′ / γ ″ / γ ′ co-precipitate itself 
and even Σ 3s within the coprecipitates, which accounting for premature 
dislocation activities and pronounced strain localization associated with 
Σ 3s. Additionally, the lattice misfits of the γ ″ / γ ′ , γ ″ / γ , and γ / γ ′ interfaces 
at the Σ 3s in MA286 and IN718 are calculated based on the lattice 
parameters ( Fig. 3 and Fig. 4 ). Compared to the γ ′ / γ phase interface at 
the Σ 3s (where the lattice misfit is 0.6 % in MA286 and 0.8 % in IN718), 
greater lattice misfits of the γ ″ / γ ′ and γ ″ / γ interfaces are 2.9 % and 3.8 %, 
respectively. The interfacial misfit dislocations are introduced as a result 
of accommodating the strain gradient of the phase interfaces when 
subjected to tensile deformation, especially at the γ ′ ′ / γ and γ ′ ′ / γ ′ in -
terfaces of the Σ 3s, which further leads to a larger strain field around the 
γ ″ phases and higher strain localization at Σ 3s, as reported in our pre -
vious work [43] . Besides, the misfit dislocations are the most effective 
hydrogen trapping sites in the γ ′ ′ / γ and γ ′ ′ / γ ′ phase interfaces along Σ 3s. 
Hydrogen carried by the dislocations may be dumped to the Σ 3s, 
resulting in weakening of interatomic bonds and a decrease in the 
boundary plane cohesive strength, which facilitates micro-voids for -
mation. However, moving dislocations are more easily transferred 
across only γ ′ -decorated Σ 3s in alloy MA286. The segregation of Ni, Nb 
and Ti along the Σ 3 boundary plane without precipitates can also lead to 
increased lattice distortion at the interface in IN718, which will result in 
a higher tendency for strain localization and hinder the interaction be -
tween dislocations and the Σ 3 boundary interface. It has been docu -
mented that the presence of Nb and Ti atoms at Σ 3s in Ni-based alloys 
may inhibit hydrogen accumulation, but they can considerably reduce 
the formation energies of vacancies and vacancy-hydrogen clusters at 
Σ 3s [44] . Thus, the increase in the number of vacancies and 
vacancy-hydrogen clusters during plastic deformation subsequently fa -
cilitates nanovoid nucleation, leading to eventual crack initiation at Σ 3s.

5. Conclusions
In conclusion, our study provides new insights into the local micro -
structures at Σ 3s and their different roles in HIC in MA286 and IN718. It 
proves that γ ′ and/or γ ″ precipitates with a mirror-symmetric structure at 
Σ 3s is a universal phenomenon in superalloys. The sandwich-like γ ′ / γ ″ / γ ′ 
co-precipitates and segregation of Ni, Nb and Ti at the Σ 3s in IN718 can 
result in a higher tendency for strain localization, which is responsible 
for premature hydrogen-induced micro-crack initiation along Σ 3s under 
the combination of hydrogen and mechanical loading. On the contrary, 
Σ 3s with only γ ′ precipitates exhibit a strong hindering effect on the 
initiation and propagation of HIC cracks in MA286, which has a fully 
coherent γ ′ / γ interface along the Σ 3s, as well as in the perfect atom 
structure without precipitates. This work also has important implica -
tions for predicting the performance of Σ 3s and opens up opportunities 
for further improvement of HE resistance and other critical applications 
related to grain boundaries, such as stress corrosion cracking.
