NIOBIUM-BASE REFRACTORY ALLOYS WITH SILICIDE AND CARBIDE
HARDENING CURRENT STATUS AND PROSPECTS
M. I. Karpov 1, 2
Translated from Metallovedenie i T ermicheskaya Obrabotka Metallov, No. 1, pp. 9 – 14, January, 2018.
Achievements in the field of development of a new generation of refractory alloys based on the Nb – Si and
Nb – C systems in the leading producing countries are reviewed. Results obtained for specimens of a patented
domestic alloy with composition 45Nb – 16Si – 13Ti – 4Zr – 4Hf – 3.5Cr – 3.5Al – 9Mo – 2Y (at.%) are pre -
sented. Data on the structure and strength properties of the alloys based on the N b – C system are discussed.
Key words: high-temperature alloys, refractory metals, intermetallic and carbide hardening, melt -
ing technologies, alloys of the Nb – Nb 3Si and Nb – Nb 2C systems.
INTRODUCTION
The analytical review in [4] presents chronological data
on the gas temperature at turbine inlets of military and civil
aircraft engines of five generations. According to these data,
the evolution of the gas temperature at turbine inlets was as
follows:
1000 – 1100 K (727 – 827°C) for the first generation
(1950 – 1970);
1150 – 1250 K (877 – 977°C) for the second generation
(1970 – 1985);
1300 – 1450 K (1027 – 1177°C) for the third generation
(1985 – 1990);
1500 – 1650 K (1227 – 1377°C) for the fourth genera -
tion (1990 – 1995);
1850 – 1900 K (1577 – 1627°C) for the fifth generation
(1995-nowadays).
In the later monograph [2], the requirements on the gas
temperature at the turbine inlet for the fourth and fifth engine
generations are stricter, i.e., 1600 – 1700 K (1327 – 1427°C)
and 1800 – 1900 K (1527 – 1627°C), respectively. The ex -
pected condition for the fifth generation is > 2000 K
(1727°C). Growth in the gas temperature at the turbine inlet
is very important from the standpoints of technology and
ecology. This is connected with the fact that the higher the
gas temperature in the engine the higher the thermodynamic
efficiency of its operation and the lower the contamination of
the atmosphere with carbon oxides and nitrides.
The blades of the turbines of engines of the 1st and 2nd
generations were not cooled. The gas temperature in the en-
gines of the 2nd generation was raised due to the use of new
materials, i.e., superalloys based on the Ni – Ni
3Al system
with melting temperature about 1400°C for turbine blades in-
stead of the refractory nickel alloy with precipitation harden-
ing. Further increase in the operating temperature of turbines
has been connected with advancement of superalloys and of
the system of cooling of the blades. However, in the unani-
mous opinion of specialists, these possibilities have been vir -
tually exhausted. This became clear already in the late 1980s.
Therefore, creation of principally novel high-temperature
and refractory alloys for gas turbine engines has become a
very important task of the science of materials.
In the present work, we shall analyze the developments
in the leading countries in the field of creation of new-ge -
neration refractory alloys based on the Nb – Si and Nb – C
systems.

In Russia, alloys of the Nb – Si system were studied on
specimens fabricated by the method of directional crystalli -
zation [23 – 25]. The initial alloys were induction-melted in
a suspended state in an argon atmosphere. It was discovered
that the ratio of the concentration of molybdenum to that of
titanium at their constant total content in the alloy affects the
structure and the creep rate at 1300°C [24]. It can be seen
from Fig. 4 that growth in the content of titanium in the alloy
makes its structure less homogeneous; solid-solution den -
drites based on the Nb 5Si3 intermetallic appear in the center
of the eutectic colonies. By the data of [23], the creep rate of
specimens containing 13% Ti and 8.5% Mo is almost two or -
ders of magnitude lower than in the specimens with a lower
content of molybdenum and higher content of titanium.
By the data of [25] (see Table 1), the speed of drawing
under directional crystallization at 1300°C affects the short-
term and long-term strength of an alloy with 8.5% Mo. It is
interesting that the mean values of the short-term strength in-
creased from 925 to 1223 MPa in the bending tests with
drawing speed of 1.25 and 10 mm /c47min, respectively. Simul-
taneously, the mean 100-h strength decreased from 258 to
203 MPa, respectively. The increase in the short-term
strength may be associated with decrease in the diameter of
the eutectic colonies (by about a factor of 5) upon increase in
the drawing speed from 1.25 to 10 mm /c47min, whereas the de-
crease in the 100-h strength is associated by the authors with
the more stable structure formed at the low crystallization
rate.

The results presented allow us to infer that the strong ef -
fort made in most industrially developed countries has re -
sulted in creation of novel refractory alloys based on the
N b–N b 3Si system and of processes of production of gas tur -
bine engine parts from them. According to some data, these
alloys are already applied in the production of engine parts.
However, the recent level of properties permits their opera -
tion at a temperature not higher than 1200 – 1300°C. This is
much lower than the expected 1500°C. Therefore, the re -
search aimed at advancing the compositions and the produc -
tion processes of these alloys is continued.

mechanical properties of refractory alloys of the Nb – Nb 2C
system with melting temperature of the eutectic 2340°C. The
pioneer works [27, 28] appeared in 1974. Specimens with a
eutectic composition were fabricated by electron-beam zone
melting from an alloy obtained by solid-phase carbidization.

CONCLUSIONS
Niobium-based alloys with silicide and carbide rein -
forcement attract much attention as promising high-tempera -
ture materials for gas turbine engines. Eutectic alloys based
on the Nb – Nb
3Si system obtained by arc melting followed
by directional crystallization or fabricated from granules ob -
tained by gas atomization and then subjected to selective la -
ser melting or gas pressing have been studied the most.
Alloys of the Nb – Nb 2C system have been studied
poorly and not systematically. The effect of the alloying on
their structure and properties has not been studied exhaus -
tively. Data on their long-term high-temperature strength
have not been reported. The low interest in these alloys
seems to be explainable by the difficulties with fabrication of
specimens even for laboratory studies due to their high melt -
ing temperatures.
