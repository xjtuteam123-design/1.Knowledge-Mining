ABSTRACT 
Interstitial oxygen plays an important role in the microstructure and mechanical properties of recently developed 
refractory high-entropy or complex concentrated alloys. However, the mechanical properties of the oxygen- 
doped complex concentrated refractory alloy over a wide temperature range remain unclear. In this work, the 
effect of interstitial oxygen on the mechanical behavior of TiZrNb medium-entropy alloys (MEAs) has been 
investigated. At room temperature (RT), the interstitial oxygen improves the yield strength and tensile ductility 
owing to interstitial solid-solution strengthening and strain-hardening enhancement. However, simultaneous 
improvement of the RT strength-ductility by adding interstitial oxygen comes at the expense of increasing the 
ductile-brittle transition temperature (DBTT). Furthermore, the high-temperature yield strength of TiZrNb alloy 
can be improved up to 800 
◦
C with the addition of 2 at% oxygen. The results above provide valuable insight into 
the mechanical properties of TiZrNb MEA, which would contribute to optimizing the high-performance re -
fractory complex concentrated alloys in various applications. 
1. Introduction 
The recently emerged “ high-entropy alloys ” , “ medium-entropy al -
loys ” or “ complex concentrated alloys ” alloy design strategies greatly 
accelerate the development of new and unexpected alloys in complex 
composition spaces with high performance [1 – 4] . For these multicom -
ponent alloys, the substitutional solid solution mechanism plays a crit -
ical role in achieving excellent mechanical properties [5 – 8] . However, 
the increase in the yield strength often sacrifices ductility for refractory 
HEAs [9 – 11] , i.e., a notable decrease in the uniform elongation under 
uniaxial tension owing to the weak strain hardening ability [12,13] . 
Unlike substitutional solid solution elements, interstitial atoms 
added intentionally or incorporated during processes impose significant 
impacts on the mechanical properties of titanium [14] and refractory 
alloys [15,16] . The interstitial hardening by oxygen is used in 
commercially pure titanium (CP-Ti) with the HCP phase. For example, 
CP-Ti Grade 4 exhibits extra high strength mainly attributed to its high 
oxygen content (0.4 wt%) [17,18] . Recently, it has been documented 
that interstitial oxygen translates the dislocation arrangement from 
wavy-to-planar slip and suppresses the deformation twinning, which 
causes embrittlement of α -titanium (also known as oxygen sensitivity) 
[19] . By co-alloying with O and Al, the recovery of dislocation cross-slip 
and wavy slip provide extra strain-hardening ability, thereby, mitigating 
the strong oxygen sensitivity of α -titanium [20] . The well-known 
“ Gum-Metal ” β -titanium alloys (TNTZ-O) are synthesized by adding 
oxygen to the metastable Ti-Nb-Ta-Zr base alloy [21 – 23] . The interstitial 
solute strengthening of oxygen can stabilize the BCC phase and promote 
multiple dislocations slips with a higher frequency of cross slip [22,23] , 
which is the key to achieving the high strength-ductility combination 
while maintaining a low Young ’ s modulus of these alloys. For refractory 
metals such as Nb [16] and V [15] , it has been proven that appropriate 
interstitial O addition (0.5 – 1 at%) can improve the yield strength while 
retaining high uniform elongation due to the increased propensity of 
screw dislocations cross slip.

MEAs exhibit low sensitivity to interstitial constituents, offering

Although the proper addition of interstitial oxygen can evade the 
strength-ductility trade-off of refractory metals and RHEAs, the 
composition windows for oxygen incorporation are considerably narrow 
[26,31] . Due to the high affinity of refractory metals within groups 
IVB-VIB for oxygen, the tight control of interstitial oxygen poses a 
challenge. Once the oxygen content exceeds a certain threshold, a brittle 
fracture may occur. For refractory alloys, cold brittleness is closely 
related to the interstitial impurities including oxygen [32] . To date, 
however, our understanding of the effects of interstitial oxygen on the 
mechanical properties of RHEAs over a wide temperature range is 
insufficient. Interstitial elements, including oxygen, play an important 
role in the ductile-brittle transition tendency of refractory alloys [33, 
34] . In addition, for aerospace applications, e.g., rocket engine thrust 
and satellite, exist concurrently extreme high-temperature and cryo -

cold brittleness tendency of RHEAs. In this study, an equiatomic TiZrNb 
medium-entropy alloy was selected to investigate the impact of inter -
stitial oxygen on mechanical behavior, including strength-ductility 
properties, inherent deformation mechanisms, and susceptibility to 
cold brittleness across a wide temperature range.

As shown in Fig. 3 (a), sharp yielding followed by yield-drop are 
presented in oxygen-added TiZrNb alloys, contrasting with the gradual 
yielding of the base material. This distinct yield phenomenon widely 
existed in low-purity body-centered cubic metals [36] , interstitial 
oxygen-modified metastable β titanium alloys [22,37,38] , and recently

non-equiatomic Ti-Zr-Nb [26] multicomponent alloys. The fundamental 
mechanisms responsible for this unique yielding are related to the 
locking of grown-in dislocations by interstitial oxygen atoms or OOCs. 
Therefore, dislocation multiplication can only be achieved under high 
stress [36] . 
According to the Consid `ere criterion, necking occurs when the value 
of the strain hardening rate equals the true stress during tension. In 
Fig. 3 (b), the strain hardening rate of TiZrNb base alloy decreases 
monotonically with increasing tensile strain throughout the entire 
plastic deformation stage. After the intersection point (hollow circle), 
the sample undergoes a prolonged stage of strain-localized necking 
deformation. For the TiZrNb-2.0 O alloy, as shown in Fig. 3 (d), after

maintains uniform elongation up to 7% (true strain). For the TiZrNb-

curves do not intersect after sharp yielding in Fig. 3 (c), which further 
indicates the poor strain-hardening ability despite alloying with 1.5 at% 
oxygen. This might be due to the precision of the data processing of 
strain-hardening rate curves. 
The necking strain is quite sensitive to the gauge length of the 
sample. The shorter the gauge length, the greater the measured necking 
strain [39] . Hence, it is not rigorous to directly determine the ultimate 
tensile strength and elongation directionally from the peak of the true 
stress-strain curves for TiZrNb-xO alloys in this work.

4. Conclusions 
In this study, the effects of interstitial oxygen on the mechanical 
properties of TiZrNb medium-entropy alloy have been investigated 
across a wide temperature range. It was found that interstitial oxygen 
can effectively strengthen the TiZrNb alloy from room temperature to 
800 
◦
C. However, this strengthening comes at the expense of increasing 
the ductile-brittle transition temperature of the alloys. The main find -
ings are outlined below: 
1. The interstitial oxygen simultaneously improves the tensile strength 
and ductility of TiZrNb alloy at room temperature. 
2. The addition of 1.5 at% and 2.0 at% oxygen directly causes the cold 
brittleness of TiZrNb alloy at 196 
◦
C, indicating that the interstitial 
oxygen inevitably raises the ductile-brittle transition temperature of 
the TiZrNb alloy. 
3. The addition of 2.0 at% oxygen demonstrates a remarkable 
strengthening effect at 600 
◦
C and 800 
◦
C compared to the TiZrNb 
alloy. However, this strengthening effect decreased significantly 
above 1000 
◦
