1. Introduction
Zr alloy is widely used in the nuclear field because of its excellent 
neutron radiation resistance, good corrosion resistance, outstanding 
mechanical properties and low thermal neutron absorption cross section 
[ 1 – 3 ]. However, Zr alloy will react with high-temperature steam to 
produce hydrogen in Loss of Coolant Accidents (LOCA), and Zr alloy 
cladding failure will lead to great safety risks [ 4 – 7 ]. In addition to high- 
temperature steam, the ingress of air into the core during a LOCA can 
also induce severe oxidation within the core. Research indicates that the 
chemical heat generated by oxidation of the nuclear core exceeds that 
generated by high-temperature steam, thereby increasing the likelihood 
of accident escalation [ 8 – 10 ]. Currently, the preparation of protective 
layer on the surface of Zr alloy to improve the oxidation resistance of Zr 
alloy at high temperature is an important development direction to 
improve the safety of nuclear reactors.
High entropy carbide (HEC) ceramic has been widely concerned 
because of their superior high temperature performance, good oxidation 
resistance and remarkable radiation resistance [ 11 – 14 ]. Harrington 
et al. [ 15 ] explored the phase stability and mechanical properties of 12 
kinds of five-metal carbides, and HEC showed enhanced hardness 
compared to the constituent binary carbides. Wang et al. [ 16 ] compared 
the oxidation resistance of (Hf-Ta-Zr-Nb)C materials, monocarbides and 
binary carbides. The (Hf-Ta-Zr-Nb)C exhibited a high oxidation onset 
temperature and a superior oxidation resistance. Wang et al. [ 17 ] re -
ported that the (Zr

2. Materials and method
2.1. Experiments
The (TiZrTaNbCr)C ceramic was fabricated by spark plasma sinter -
ing technology reported in our previous work [ 24 ]. The Zr-4 alloy was 
purchased from Shenzhen AVIC Special Alloy Co., Ltd., China. The 
element compositions and physical properties of Zr-4 alloy were 
described in Table 1 and Table 2 . The (TiZrTaNbCr)C ceramic and Zr-4 
alloy were cut into blocks with a dimension of 5 × 5 × 5 and 10 × 10 × 5 
(mm), respectively. Before brazing, these blocks were polished to 
remove the oxide layer from the surface, then cleaned and dried.
The commercial BNi-2 filler was employed to bond (TiZrTaNbCr)C 
ceramic and Zr-4 alloy in this work. The element composition of BNi-2 
was composed of Ni-3.4B-3.6Fe-4.7Si-6.4Cr (wt%). The BNi-2 filler 
was placed in the middle of the (TiZrTaNbCr)C ceramic and Zr-4 alloy, 
and the assembled samples were transferred into vacuum furnace. The 
brazing process was performed at 1050 – 1200 
◦
C for 10 min. The shear 
strength of brazed joints was tested by standard testing machine with a 
loading speed of 0.5 mm/min [ 25 ]. The high temperature shear strength 
of the joint was tested at a loading speed of 0.5 mm/min using a standard 
testing machine equipped with a high temperature heating furnace. The 
high-temperature shear strength of brazed joints was tested at 800 
◦
C. To 
explore the oxidation resistance, the brazed joints were oxidized at 
900 
◦
C for 2 – 8 h, and then the shear strength was tested.
2.2. Materials characterization
The microstructure and phase composition of (TiZrTaNbCr)C/Zr-4 
joint was investigated by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and en -
ergy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS). The wavelength dispersive 
spectroscopy (WDS) was used to explore the element distribution of 
brazed joint. To analysis the phase composition, the joint was polished 
to the interface reaction layer and then corroded to remove the metal 
filler. The X-ray diffraction (XRD) technology was employed to analysis 
phase composition. Furthermore, the sample of interface reaction layer 
was extracted by focused ion beam technology (FIB). The interface 
reaction layer was analyzed by transmission electron microscopy (TEM).
