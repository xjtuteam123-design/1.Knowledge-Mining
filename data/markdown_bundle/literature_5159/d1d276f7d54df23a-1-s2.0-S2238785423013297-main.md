abstract
Metallic glass matrix composites (MGMCs) have received intensive attention due to their
higher strength at room temperature compared with traditional metallic glasses. However,
they suffer from lose of strength and microstructural instability at elevated temperatures
due to softening and crystallization of the glass matrix. Here, a novel Ti-Zr-Hf-Nb-Ta-Cu-Be
high-entropy MGMCs with improved high-temperature mechanical properties was devel-
oped through the multi-principle element alloying strategy of refractory elements, which
effectively retards the degradation of high-temperature mechanical properties compared
with traditional MGMCs. The results provide an effective route to design high-performance
high-entropy MGMCs for the potential application in a wide temperature range.

1. Introduction
Amorphous microstructure and variable composition endow
bulk metallic glasses (BMGs) with unique mechanical prop-
erties, which undoubtedly make them potential structural
materials for various load-bearing applications [1,2]. However,
almost all the BMGs exhibit poor ductility due to their sus-
ceptibility to shear localization through shear bands, and
moreover their mechanical properties would deteriorate
rapidly caused by softening and crystallization closed to the
glass transition temperature ( T
g) and the crystallization tem-
perature ( Tx), both of which severely limit the practical
application of BMGs in a wide temperature range. Metallic
glass matrix composites (MGMCs), as the derivatives of BMGs,
was expected to solve the brittleness of BMGs at room
temperature [ 3e5]. The introduction of second crystalline
phases promotes the proliferation of shear bands and prevent
their rapid propagation [ 6,7]. Previous reports showed that b-
Ti dendrite-reinforced in-situ MGMCs with high speciﬁcal
strength can manifest signiﬁcant ductile deformation and
undergo strain hardening at room temperature (RT) and
lower-temperature [ 8e11]. However, the high-temperature
mechanical properties and the related deformation mecha-
nism of MGMCs are rarely reported in the literature. Modern
load-bearing applications such as those in aerospace engi-
neering require advanced structural materials to serve in
broad temperature ranges. Indeed, T
g and Tx of the glassy
matrix determine the thermal stability and the upper limit of
service temperature of MGMCs [ 12e16]. Among the reported
conventional Zr- and Ti-based MGMCs,they all have a low T
g
below 750 K [ 17]. When the ambient temperature elevated

The thermal softening of the metallic glass matrix causes a
dramatic decrease in its strength. In addition, according to the
mixing rule, the strengthening effect of dendritic phases is
bound to improve the total strength of MGMCs. However,
previous studies mainly focused on the modulation of plastic
deformation ability of the secondary phase to enhance the
total ductility of MGMCs at room temperature and low tem-
perature by the activation of multiple deformation mecha-
nisms such as dislocation slip, transformation-induced
plasticity (TRIP) and twinning-induced plasticity (TWIP)
[20e22]. Therefore, how to enhance the thermal stability (i.e.
T

of dendritic phase plays a key role to solve the problem of the
low strength of MGMCs at high temperature, and it is partic-
ularly helpful for the development of novel MGMCs with su-
perior mechanical behavior in the improved upper
temperature limit.
The concept of high-entropy alloys (HEAs) (or called multi-
principal element alloys) has created a huge compositional
space for alloy design of metallic materials [ 23]. Initially, HEAs
are deﬁned as solid solution alloys with ﬁve or more principal
elements in near-equimolar ratios [ 24,25]. To date, the
concept has been expanded to include non-equiatomic vari-
ants containing four or more principal elements [ 26]. Based on
the design concept of constituent element diversiﬁcation, the
development of refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) and
even high-entropy bulk metallic glasses (HE-BMGs) have
evoked unprecedented fervors [ 27,28]. The severe lattice
distortion with multi-component solid-solution strength-
ening is considered to be the main reason for the high strength
of HEAs, especially bcc-structured RHEAs [ 29,30]. In addition,
the sluggish diffusion effect is beneﬁcial for the formation of
supersaturated solid solution in HEAs. The slow diffusion rate
makes RHEAs less susceptible to adverse effects such as grain
coarsening and recrystallization at high temperatures. Thus,
RHEAs have better thermal stability and strength compared to
conventional HEAs [ 23,31].
On the other hand, it is also recognized that the enhance-
ment of conﬁgurational entropy and crystallization
complexity through multi-component alloying with similar
elements, i.e. the constituent diversiﬁcation through alloying
two or more elements with similar atomic sizes and valence
electronic structures (normally they can form continuous
solid solution), is an effective strategy to improve the glass-
forming ability (GFA) [ 32,33]. It has been reported that the
abundant introduction of refractory metals through co-
alloying similar elements can signiﬁcantly increase thermal
stability and GFA of metallic glasses with a higher T
g and Tx
compared to their “lower entropy ” counterparts [ 34]. More-
over, the high conformational entropy promotes a random
distribution of atoms in BMGs, resulting in difﬁculty in moving
the atoms during heating or compression, and thus improves
thermal stability and strength of the HE-BMGs [ 35e39]. In
traditional MGMC systems, two constituent phases, i.e.
metallic glass matrix and crystalline secondary phase, dis-
played distinguished alloy compositions, which should be
designed and controlled respectively so as to possess conso-
nant mechanical properties [ 3]. For example, the alloy
composition of crystalline phase in the Zr-based MGMCs is in
the range of Zr
40e44Ti42e45Nb11e14Cu1e3, while the different
composition of the glassy matrix is in the range of Zr 31e34-
Ti17e22Nb1e2Cu9e13Be31e38 to satisfy the glass forming ability
[4]. Therefore, the high-entropy MGMCs can be designed
through entropy-enhancing strategy to introduce various
similar elements into both the secondary phase to strengthen
the crystalline solid solution and the metallic glass matrix to
increase the thermal stability, which could break through the
bottleneck of microstructural instability and strength loss at
high temperature for traditional MGMCs.
In this study, we attempt to design high-entropy MGMCs
using similar element co-alloying strategy. Two entropy-
enhancing alloys of Ti
40Zr10Hf10Ta10Nb10Cu5Be15,T i 16Zr16-
Hf16Ta16Nb16Cu5Be15 were designed through the abundant
introduction of Hf and Ta refractory metals. The constitutes of
Ti, Zr, and Nb in their parent alloy of Ti
48Zr20Nb12Cu5Be15 (a
typical Ti-based MGMC) is partly substituted by the similar
elements of Ta and Hf with higher melting temperature. The
effects of the entropy enhancement by multi-component
coexistence of similar elements (i.e. Ti, Zr, Nb, Hf, and Ta)
on phase stability, microstructure, and mechanical properties
(RT to 800 K) were evaluated. The phase forming regulation
and strengthening mechanisms in these newly designed high
entropy metallic glass matrix composite were discussed.
2. Material and methods
Alloy ingots with nominal compositions of Ti 48Zr20Nb12Cu5-
Be15,T i 40Zr10Hf10Ta10Nb10Cu5Be15 and Ti 16Zr16Hf16Ta16Nb16-
Cu5Be15 (named as Ti48, Ti40, and Ti16 in the following
context, respectively) were prepared from mixtures of high-
purity Ti, Zr, Hf, Ta, Nb, Cu, and Be metals (purities over
99.9 wt%). For each sample, the button ingot with a weight of
about 30 g, was directly obtained by arc-melting of the mix-
tures on a water-cooled copper hearth under an argon atmo-
sphere. A pure titanium-getter was melted prior to the arc-
melting process to deoxidize the chamber. Each ingot was
overturned and remelted at least six times to ensure chemical
uniformity. Meanwhile, all samples in this study were pro-
duced by arc-melting on the surface of water-cooled copper
plates at a relatively slow cooling rate of approximately
100 K s

compared to that of Ti48, even less than that of Ti40.
Meanwhile, the excessive introduction of the refractory
metals can be an adverse factor for the improvement of GFA,
proofed by the undetectable glass matrix in Ti16. The GFA of a
multicomponent alloy relies on the stability of the super-
cooled molten liquid, which is highly dependent on the alloy
composition [ 44]. The abundant introduction of the solution-
strengthening refractory metal elements into the glassy ma-
trix would uncontrollably increase the melting temperature of
the interdendritic phase, and result in the composition devi-
ation far away from eutectic point (which is the composition
of maximized GFA). The current results indicate that the
appropriate introduction of the similar elements is an effec-
tive way to improve conﬁgurational entropy for both crystal-
line and amorphous phases in dual- or multi-phase composite
systems.

phase in MGMCs with large plastic deformation ability at
room temperature plays a key role to promote the branching
of multiple shear bands, and suppress the propagation and
destabilization of the main shear bands in the amorphous
matrix. Thus, the matching secondary phase and glassy ma-
trix can promote overall room-temperature plasticity of
MGMCs [ 4]. However, the pronounced solid-solution
strengthening in the dendritic phase for Ti40 causes the loss

shear bands in the matrix, which eventually leads to brittle
fracture of the samples as observed in Fig. 3 (a). Also, the for-
mation of brittle intermetallic to replace the amorphous phase
in the matrix during solidiﬁcation of Ti16 alloy leads to pre-
mature fracture.

the strong solid solution hardening due to high entropy alloying

4. Conclusion
A high-entropy MGMCs with the improved high-temperature
performance was developed by co-alloying of similar re-
fractory elements. The increase of the conﬁgurational entropy
through the introduction of multiple refractory metal elements
can effectively suppress the degradation of high-temperature
strength than traditional MGMCs. Our study develops a strat-
egy to develop novel high-entropy MGMCs for potential high-
temperature applications. Further improvements will
concentrate on balancing the trade-off between room tem-
perature ductility and high temperature strength of the mate-
rial, which requires delicate alloy design for both phases.
