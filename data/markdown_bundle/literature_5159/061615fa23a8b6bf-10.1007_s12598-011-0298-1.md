Microstructural evolution of unidirectionally solidified NbSS-Nb5Si3 eutectic alloy 
 
LU Yuanyuan, ZHANG Jin, TIAN Lixi, LI Yulong, and MA Chaoli 
School of Material Science and Engineering, Beijing University of Aeronautics and Astronautics, Beijing 100191, China 
Received 29 September 2010; received in revised form 29 December 2010; accepted 10 January 2011 
© The Nonferrous Metals Society of China and Springer-Verlag Berlin Heidelberg 2011 
 
Abstract 
The microstructure of NbSS-Nb5Si3 eutectic alloys unidirectionally solidified at various growth rates and the phase boundary characteristics 
between niobium solid solution (NbSS) and Nb5Si3 phases in these alloys were studied, by using electron probe microanalysis (EPMA) and 
electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) method. Lower growth rate is beneficial to form lamellar eutectic structure. When the growth rate 
decreased to 3 mm/h, large-scale regular lamellas could be detected in alternative distribution. Three kinds of crystallographic orientation 
patterns, i.e. {111}Nb//{011}Nb5Si3, {110}Nb//{111}Nb5Si3 and {112}Nb//{100}Nb5Si3 were identified in lamellar and non-lamellar eutec-
tic structures with different growth rate conditions. Associated with the X-ray diffraction (XRD) results about investigated alloys, two-phase 
eutectic growth shows strong preferential orientation when growth rate is low. The influence of growth rate on microstructural evolution in 
unidirectionally solidified NbSS-Nb5Si3 eutectic alloy had been discussed in this paper. 
Keywords: NbSS-Nb5Si3 eutectic alloy; unidirectional solidification; lamellar structure; phase boundary; EBSD 
 
 
1. Introduction 
For the increasing desire for next generation aircraft en-
gines with better thrust weight ratio and fuel consumption 
efficiency, people keep attempting to develop new high tem-
perature structure materials to replace traditional Ni-base 
superalloys for turbine blades [1-5]. Since niobium (7.16 
g/cm
3) has lower density than nickel (9.2 g/cm 3) while its 
melting point is 1000 ºC higher, Nb-base alloys become sub-
stantial promising candidate for use at ultrahigh temperature. 
Among those Nb-base alloys, NbSS-Nb5Si3 two-phase alloy 
has attracted great attentions because of the combination of 
impressive ductility and fracture toughness from Nbss with 
excellent stiffness and strength retention from Nb5Si3 [6-10]. 
Moreover, in wide composition range this material pos-
sesses the capability of maintaining two phases chemically 
stable up to very high temperature which provides possibil-
ity of microstructure control and mechanical properties de-
velopment through doping proper alloying elements or 
adopting suitable processing methods. 
Previous study found the existence of eutectic reaction: 
L→β-Nb
5Si3 + Nb SS in Nb-Si-Mo ternary phase diagram 
rather than Nb-Si binary alloy system, which is considerably 
helpful to develop well-organized lamellar structure of NbSS 
and Nb5Si3 while avoiding the presence of metastable Nb3Si 
meanwhile [11]. Even though Nb5Si3 is brittle, this eutectic 
reaction could offer the availability to achieve a good bal-
ance of high temperature stre ngth and room temperature 
toughness in the mean of fully exerting the function of duc-
tile Nb
SS. Improvement of poor room temperature toughness 
of NbSS-Nb5Si3 eutectic alloy will definitely facilitate its po-
tential economical applications [12-14]. 
To ameliorate the cooperation of co-existing eutectic 
phases in NbSS-Nb5Si3 eutectic alloy, it is necessary to get 
thorough understanding of its microstructural evolution, es-
pecially the phase boundary ch aracteristics under different 
processing conditions. During eutectic growth process, Nb
SS 
and Nb5Si3 phases have to maintain some certain crystallo-
graphic orientation relationships so as to reduce the interfa-
cial energy of eutectic phase boundary. This paper aims to 
investigate the eutectic microstructure characteristic and 
crystallographic orientation relationship between Nb
SS and 
Nb5Si3 phases in Nb-17Si-10Mo-3Al alloy prepared by uni-
directional solidification at different growth rates. Al has 
been incorporated as the fourth solution element in our study 
because of its positive effect on the generation of lamellar 
eutectic microstructure and improvement of oxidation resis-
tance in Nb
SS-Nb5Si3 eutectic alloy to some extent [15-16]. 
2. Experimental 
Pure Nb (99.9 wt.%), Si (99.9 wt.%), Mo (99.9 wt.%) and

Al (99.99 wt.%) were used to prepare the preliminary ingot 
by arc-melting in a water-cooled copper crucible and argon 
atmosphere, with strict accordance to the element proportion 
of Nb-17Si-10Mo-3Al (at.%) alloy. The ingots were remelt 
for four times to ensure the chemical homogeneity. The uni-
directional solidification experiment was performed in an 
optical floating zone melting furnace under a high purity 
argon atmosphere. The specimens were cut from unidirec-
tional solidified ingot parallel to the solidification direction 
and polished with emery papers carefully to guarantee sur-
face smooth enough. 
Microstructure observations were conducted by electron 
probe microanalysis (EPMA) while constituent phases were 
identified by X-ray diffraction (XRD). Electron backscatter 
diffraction (EBSD) analysis were performed to identify the 
crystallographic orientation relationships between Nb
SS and 
Nb5Si3 phases at distinctive microstructure regions in the 
unidirectionally solidified eutectic alloys prepared at four 
growth velocities (V0 = 3, 5, 10, 18 mm/h). 
3. Results and discussion 
Figs. 1 (a)-(d) show the typical microstructures of unidi-
rectionally solidified Nb-17Si-10Mo-3Al alloy obtained at 
growth rates of 3, 5, 10 and 18 mm/h respectively. Their 
corresponding XRD patterns are presented in Figs. 2 (a)-(d). 
There is no emergency of metastable Nb3Si phase observed 
in each alloy which could prov e the complete reaction of 
L→β-Nb5Si3 + Nb SS. The microstructure of these studied 
eutectic alloys consists of bright phase, identified to be nio-
bium solid solution (Nb
SS), and the dark phase which is 
β-Nb5Si3 with body-centered tetragonal structure. It is evi-
dent that at lower growth rate, as shown in Fig. 1 (a) and (b), 
the eutectic structure displays well-aligned lamellas com-
posed of bright Nb
SS and dark Nb 5Si3. Surrounding the la-
mellar eutectic regions, it is observed the minor existence of 
another eutectic microstructure (called non-lamellar eutectic 
region below) characterized by rod-like Nb particles em-
bedded in Nb
5Si3 matrix with larger thickness than the la-
mella. With increasing growth rate, continuous NbSS phase 
in regular lamellar structure seems to break into finer seg-
ments or be distorted into entanglements as shown in Fig. 1 
(c). When growth rate reaches 18mm/h, the lamellar micro-
structure becomes unconspicuous, almost fully replaced by 
the coarse non-lamellar eutectic microstructure in Fig. 1 (d). 
In this paper, we choose alloy composition near eutectic 
point in Nb-Si binary system. Low growth rate condition in 
unidirectional solidification can provide a relatively stable 
environment for Nb
SS-Nb5Si3 eutectic growth. However, this 
kind of equilibrium would be interrupted by the increasing 
growth rate. That is a reasona ble factor contributing to the 
irregular morphology in Fig.1 (c) and (d).

shown in Fig. 2 where the red dash line is the peak of pure

alloys slightly shift to right from the expected pure niobium 
peak position. This deviation from standard peak position 
suggests that Nb
SS has a contracted lattice parameter after 
alloying Mo and Al. Combining EBSD and XRD results of 
crystallography, alloying effects on phase boundary charac-
teristics in unidirectionally solidified Nb SS-Nb5Si3 eutectic 
alloys is apparent.

graphic orientation relationship between Nb SS and Nb 5Si3 
tends to be simple when the growth rate was down. When 
the growth rate decreased to 3 mm/h, only {111}Nb// 
{011}Nb5Si3 orientation relationship has been detected. In 
addition, the tendency of crys tallographic orientation rela-
tionship becomes simple accompanied with growth rate re-
duction is in consistence with the XRD data obtained. 
Comparing four X-ray diffractograms in Fig. 2, from 18 to 
3 mm/h, it is evident that Nb
SS and Nb 5Si3 show stronger 
eutectic preferred orientation as the growth rate becomes 
lower. Thus, besides the composition of NbSS-Nb5Si3 eutec-
tic alloy, growth rate also plays a crucial role on phase 
boundary crystallography. As an important processing pa-
rameter in directional solidification process, growth rate al-
most decides solid-liquid interface stability during unidirec-
tional solidification process thus has great impacts on eutec-
tic microstructure and mechanical properties of Nbss-Nb
5Si3

4. Conclusion 
For the Mo, Al doped Nb SS-Nb5Si3 eutectic alloy fabri-
cated by unidirectional solidification at various growth rates 
from 3 mm/h to 18 mm/h, it is found the lower growth rate 
during unidirectional solidification is beneficial for alloy to 
form regular lamellar eutectic microstructure. As for the 
important phase boundary characteristics study, there are 
three basic crystallographic orientation relationships patterns 
identified at phase boundary, i.e. {111}Nb//{011}Nb
5Si3, 
{110}Nb//{111}Nb5Si3 and {112}Nb//{100}Nb5Si3 both in 
lamellar and non-lamellar eutectic regions. Low growth rate 
below 5 mm/h is not only beneficial to form large area la-
mellar eutectic microstructure during unidirectional solidifi-
cation but also more likely to create relatively simple crys-
tallographic orientation relationships between eutectic 
phases. During unidirectional solidification, low growth rate 
also encourages eutectic phases to grow in preferential 
growth direction.
