Microstructural Observations on Oxidized Ni-Based Superalloy
TMS-75 and PGM-Doped Derivatives
Karin Pruessner a,b,c,*,z and Hiroshi Harada d,e
aSchool of Physics, bDST/NRF Centre of Excellence in Strong Materials, and cMaterials Physics Research

dHigh-Temperature Materials Center and eRolls-Royce Centre of Excellence for Aerospace Materials,

The isothermal oxidation behavior of experimental third and fourth generation Ni-based superalloys was studied at temperatures
between 900 and 1100°C. It was found that the lower Cr concentration in third and fourth generation alloys compared to second
generation alloys negatively affects the ability of the alloy to form and maintain a protective alumina scale especially at lower
temperatures /H20849900°C/H20850. An extensive internal reaction zone comprised of oxide and nitride phases is observed underneath a
nonprotective external oxide scale. At higher temperatures, a continuous alumina scale forms underneath /H20849Ni,Co/H20850O and spinel
layers. Due to the low level of reactive element /H20849RE/H20850additions in these experimental alloys, scale adhesion is poor. An increase
of Cr content is necessary to ensure formation of a protective alumina scale at lower temperatures. Addition of Y and/or Zr is
recommended for improved oxide adhesion, resulting in better overall oxidation performance.

Ni-based superalloys are state-of-the-art materials used in the hot
section of gas turbines. Higher operating temperatures improve the
efﬁciency of the turbine while also reducing harmful greenhouse
emissions. Substantial economic and environmental beneﬁts can
therefore be achieved if turbine operating temperatures are in-
creased. Because currently used alloys have reached their tempera-
ture limit, new alloys with better high temperature capabilities need
to be developed.
1-4 Critical alloy properties for these applications are
mechanical properties at high temperatures /H20849creep and fatigue /H20850and
environmental stability at high temperatures /H20849oxidation resistance
and hot corrosion resistance /H20850.
The platinum-group metals /H20849PGMs/H20850Ru, Rh, Pd, Ir, and Pt offer
potential for high temperature applications because their melting
points are higher than that of pure nickel /H20849Ni: 1453°C, Ru: 2334°C,
Rh: 1960°C, Pd: 1555°C, Ir: 2443°C, Pt: 1769°C /H20850. Therefore,
PGM-based intermetallics5-7 and superalloys8-13 are under develop-
ment in several laboratories and their oxidation behavior under high
temperature conditions has recently found increased interest.
14-20
Unfortunately, PGM alloys have substantially higher densities than
Ni-based alloys and are rather expensive. Their use will therefore
likely be limited to specialty applications. Starting around 1980,
Ni-based alloys with PGM additions have therefore been developed
as a lower cost and lower weight alternative.
21-24 Platinum and /H20849to a
lesser extent /H20850Pd are also being used in bond coats, where these
elements improve the oxidation resistance of alumina-forming
alloys.
25-27 Whereas the mechanism of this effect is not fully under-
stood, the potentially beneﬁcial effects of other PGMs on the oxida-
tion behavior of Ni-based superalloys need to be explored.

SEM studies on oxidized metal surfaces in plan view revealed
that for lower oxidation temperatures /H20849900°C/H20850, the alloy surface is
completely covered with an external oxide scale exhibiting a variety
of surface structures. The adhesion of the scale is good at this tem-
perature. Spallation of the scale is not typically observed. At higher
temperatures /H208491100°C/H20850, the external oxide scale spalled almost com-
pletely upon cooling of the sample. The alloy composition of the
TMS-series alloys did not have a marked effect on the overall oxi-
dation performance as judged by the percentage of oxide surface
coverage after cooling. Second generation alloy René N5, however,
exhibits excellent oxidation resistance at 1100°C due to the forma-
tion of a dense, slow-growing and well-adherent alumina scale on
the alloy surface.
32
Further SEM studies on polished cross sections through the ther-
mally grown oxide scales showed that at 900°C, the external oxide
scale consists entirely of a mixed oxide with composition /H20849Ni,Co/H20850O,
which is nonprotective, thereby allowing diffusion of reactive gas
species such as oxygen and nitrogen from the air into the alloy.
Underneath this external scale, an extensive zone of internal reaction
products consisting mostly of discrete particles was found /H20849Fig. 2a/H20850.
The penetration depth of this reaction zone as well as its phase
composition is very nonuniform and seems to be affected by surface
roughness. Phases identiﬁed in this layer include alumina, chromia,
mixed oxide phases as well as nitrides. The internal reaction zone
forms when oxygen and nitrogen from the atmosphere diffuse
through the nonprotective external scale and the local solubility
limit in the alloy is exceeded. The nucleation and growth of these
oxide and nitride particles leads to internal stresses in the metal.
Underneath the internal precipitation zone, the strengthening
/H9253//H9253
/H11032-microstructure of the alloy is lost due to depletion of elements
during the oxidation reaction. Both the precipitation of oxides and
nitrides within the alloy and the loss of the strengthening
/H9253//H9253
/H11032-microstructure result in a mechanically weakened region un-
derneath the metal/oxide interface that extends several tens of mi-
crometers into the alloy after 100 h of oxidation at 900°C.
The formation of a protective external alumina scale was found
to improve at higher oxidation temperatures for all TMS-series al-
loys. At 1100°C, the external oxide scale consists of a continuous
alumina layer underneath a layer of /H20849Ni,Co/H20850O and a layer of mixed
spinel /H20849Ni,Co/H20850/H20849Al,Cr/H208502O4. Within the scale, accumulations of ox-
ides containing heavy elements such as Ta, W, and Re can be found
/H20849Fig. 2b/H20850. Unfortunately, adhesion of this protective external oxide
scale to the alloy is poor and most of the oxide spalls during cooling
of the alloy. A higher concentration of effective reactive elements
/H20849REs/H20850and/or a combination of more than one reactive elements /H20849RE/H20850

and therefore improve the overall oxidation performance.

Phase composition, microstructure, and thickness of the internal
reaction layer often exhibit large variation in a single sample. Dis-
tinct regions include /H20849i/H20850a gray ﬁne-grained internal oxide layer close
to the interface /H20849Fig. 3b/H20850, /H20849ii/H20850an extended internal reaction zone

/H20849iii/H20850regions rich in particles with bright BSE contrast, indicating
accumulations of heavy element such as Ta, W, Re, and PGMs /H20849not
shown here /H20850. The origin of these local variations in the reaction
layers is not clear at this point, but it could be an indication that
small local variations in chemical composition have a marked effect
on the oxidation process. The thickness of both the external and
internal reaction layers is determined by the presence or absence of
a continuous alumina layer within the internal reaction zone. If in-
ternal precipitates of alumina grow large enough and connect, the
diffusion of reactant species is effectively stopped and both the in-
ternal reaction layer and the external scale stay thin. If the formation
of such a continuous layer does not happen, the internal and external
reaction continues to proceed.

The striking difference in oxidation performance between the
second generation alloy René N5 and the third and fourth generation
alloys of the TMS series can be understood in terms of their com-
positional differences. Alloys of the third and fourth generations
were developed mostly with mechanical properties at high tempera-
tures in mind. The addition of high amounts of Re resulted in the
desired creep properties, but the alloys were prone to TCP forma-
tion. As a result, Cr concentrations were reduced. Such alloys be-
come borderline in terms of oxidation resistance because Cr is re-
quired to ensure the formation of a protective alumina scale. At
lower temperatures, this protective scale cannot be formed and re-
active species from the gas phase diffuse into the alloy, resulting in
an internal reaction zone. In addition, the nitrogen solubility of Ni-
based superalloys strongly depends on the Cr concentration.
43 At
lower Cr concentrations, the solubility drops dramatically, resulting
in precipitation of nitrides. An increase of Cr concentration would
improve the oxidation behavior by promoting the formation of a
protective scale, but the stability of the alloy against TCP formation
would possibly suffer. At higher temperatures, an external alumina
scale can be formed and internal oxidation and nitridation are there-
fore prevented. Adhesion of this external scale is poor though. Spal-
lation could possibly be prevented if another reactive elements /H20849RE/H20850
was added to the alloy.
34
The overall oxidation behavior of Ni-based superalloys of the
TMS series seems to be largely dominated by these two effects.
Minor differences in the oxidation behavior due to the presence of
different PGMs were not easily obvious at this stage. Further, more
detailed studies to elucidate the effects of Ru, Rh, Pd, Ir, and Pt on
the oxidation performance of these alloys are currently under way.
Conclusions
The alloy composition, namely the low Cr content and the low
concentration of REs compared to second generation alloy René N5,
dominate the high temperature reactions of third and fourth genera-
tion Ni-based superalloys of the TMS series. The low Cr content
results in extensive internal oxidation/nitridation at lower tempera-
tures, while the low concentration of REs is not sufﬁcient to prevent
spallation of the protective alumina scale at higher temperatures. An
increase of Cr content and RE content should improve the overall
oxidation performance of these alloys.
