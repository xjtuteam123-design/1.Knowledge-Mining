ABSTRACT
The combination of tensile strength and ductility of equiatomic CrMnFeCoNi high-entropy alloy (HEA) is a 
critical issue in achieving intended mechanical properties for cryogenic applications. Here laser powder bed 
fusion (LPBF) technology is used to prepare TiB
2 
particle reinforced CrMnFeCoNi high-entropy composite, which 
exhibits ~1350 MPa ultra-high tensile strength (UTS) and ~ 19 % fractured elongation at cryogenic temperature, 
almost twice that of room temperature. The underlying mechanisms were unraveled, in which the formation of 
stacking faults (SFs), deformation twins (DTs) in the matrix and hard σ phase particles, TiB
2 
particles, and their 
generated dislocation networks were found to synergistically promote the substantial improvement of strength 
and elongation during cryogenic temperature deformation.
1. Introduction
High-entropy alloys contain multi-principal elements in equimolar 
or near-equimolar proportions, which have been intensively explored 
due to their immense potential in advanced structural applications [ 1 ]. 
It is now known that HEAs exhibit incomparable advantages over 
traditional alloys, such as superior fracture toughness, corrosion resis -
tance, and excellent combination of tensile strength and ductility [ 2 – 6 ]. 
The HEAs have become potential candidates for cryogenic applications 
due to their remarkable mechanical responses [ 7 ]. As one of HEAs, 
CoCrFeMnNi (so-called Cantor alloy) which typically forms a single- 
phase face-centered cubic (FCC) solid solution has excellent plasticity 
but does not have a satisfactory strength. While the cryogenic temper -
ature (CT) environment requests higher requirements for the mechani -
cal properties of Cantor alloy. To further optimize the strength-ductility 
combination, new ways must be developed to obtain high-performance 
of FCC HEA materials, including manufacturing methods, composition 
modifications, and their combination. Currently, additive 
manufacturing (AM) technologies as novel processing methods are uti -
lized to manufacture CoCrFeMnNi HEA, owing simple and efficient 
merits. LPBF is representatively one of the metal-based AM technologies, 
which provides higher geometrical freedom, reduces otherwise long 
supply chain, and shortens manufacturing time [ 8 ]. During the AM 
process, the rapid solidification rate and high-temperature gradients of 
melting pool can result in homogeneous solid solution microstructure 
and grain refinement, which is an excellent strategy to improve the 
mechanical properties of HEAs [ 9 ]. In addition, the addition of nano- 
ceramic particles into high-entropy alloys to develop new ex-situ HEA- 
based nanocomposites (HEABNCs) is also an effective method to 
improve their performance [ 10 ], which is a new approach of material 
design. For example, Lu et al. [ 11 ] found that the 2 wt% TiC/CoCr -
FeMnNi composite promotes a remarkable combination of strength and 
ductility at both room and cryogenic temperatures, in which cryogenic 
tensile strength reaches ~1506 MPa and cryogenic ductility increases by 
10.3 % compared with those of room temperature. However, Kim et al. 
[ 2 ] evidenced that in-situ nano-oxide reinforced CoCrFeMnNi HEA 
matrix nanocomposite fabricated by additive manufacturing had an 
ultra-high tensile strength of 1.45 GPa at cryogenic temperature, 
whereas the elongation decreased. Therefore, to address the above 
divergence, the cryogenic deformation mechanism of HEAs needs to be
