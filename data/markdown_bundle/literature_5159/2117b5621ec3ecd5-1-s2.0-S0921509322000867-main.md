1. Introduction 
In the 21st century, facing the gradual lack of energy, nuclear fusion 
energy is likely to become the main energy to solve the energy dilemma 
[ 1 – 3 ]. Plasma facing materials (PFMs) are the very important candidates 
in the nuclear fusion reactor, where these materials need to undergo the 
bombardment damage of plasma [ 4 ]. Among them, tungsten and 
tungsten-based alloys have been identified as the best candidate mate -
rial for plasma because of their advantages, such as high plasma erosion 
resistance, high melting point, and low sputtering yield [ 5 – 7 ]. However, 
in order to eliminate the intrinsic brittleness of tungsten alloys caused by 
low temperature, recrystallization and irradiation [ 8 ], it is necessary to 
improve the properties of tungsten alloy by changing the preparation 
method and material composition design to meet the needs of the rapid 
development for the society [ 9 ].

boundaries, resulting in grain refinement in the sintering process. Grain 
refinement means that the numbers of W grains per unit volume in -
creases, leading to more grain boundaries. The finer the grains, the more 
grain boundaries participate in hindering the dislocation slip, resulting 
in the improvement of strength. Furthermore, the more grain boundaries 
also help to prevent crack propagation and thus postpone the failure
