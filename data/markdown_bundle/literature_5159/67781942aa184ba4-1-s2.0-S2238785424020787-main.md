ABSTRACT
As a typical age-strengthened alloy, Cu – Cr – Zr alloys have high strength, good electrical conductivity, and 
excellent wear resistance. In this work, the Cu – Cr – Zr – Nb alloys were in different aging states after the aging 
process. After aging at 460 
◦
C for 4 h, the alloy was peak-aging. The common effect of the existence of the FCC Cr 
particle, which was coherent with the Cu matrix, and the increase in hardness improved the wear property of the 
alloy. The results of the wear test showed that the optimal wear parameter was a speed of 300 rpm and a load of 
5 N. Under these wear parameters, the alloy had the lowest mean coefficient of friction (COF) value and the least 
mass loss, having the best wear resistance. Furthermore, the wear mechanism of the alloy was mainly adhesive 
wear and abrasive wear.
1. Introduction
With its high strength and good electrical conductivity, the Cu – Cr – Zr 
alloy is widely used in functional and structural fields [ 1 – 3 ]. Among the 
above fields, there are some typical applications, such as circuit contact 
lines and resistance welding electrodes, requiring Cu – Cr – Zr alloys to be 
used in harsh working condition. Therefore, the alloy is required to have 
excellent mechanical and wear properties [ 4 ].
Nowadays, some researchers have found that the improved me -
chanical and wear properties of Cu alloys are due to the common effects 
of fine grain strengthening and precipitation strengthening after large 
deformation and subsequent aging treatment [ 5 , 6 ]. For example, Purcek 
et al. [ 7 ] prepared the ultrafine-grained Cu – Cr – Zr alloy by equal channel 
angle extrusion. The tensile strength and vickers hardness of the alloy 
reached 741 MPa and 210 MPa, respectively, after being aged at 470 
◦
C 
for 5 h. The improvement of wear properties was closely associated with 
an increase in hardness. On this basis, Purcek et al. [ 4 ] chose 
high-pressure torsion for the extruded Cu – Cr – Zr alloy, which improved 
its wear resistance. After being aged at 450 
◦
C for 1 h, the coefficient of 
friction (COF) value and mass loss were reduced, which illustrated that 
the wear properties were further improved. Zhiyaev and his co-worker 
[ 8 ] studied the influence of equal channel angle extrusion times on 
the wear properties of the Cu-0.3Cr-0.5Zr alloy. The results showed that 
the wear rate was strongly related to the wear subsurface, and the wear 
mechanism was the primary factor determining its wear properties. 
Sagolam et al. [ 9 ] found that the wear rate reached the lowest of 5 ×
10
 7 
mg/m when the Cu-0.65Cr-0.07Zr alloy aged at 500 
◦
C for 2 h. 
When the aging temperature rose to 530 
◦
C, the alloy was over-aging, 
and the wear rate increased sharply to 1.5 × 10
 6 
mg/m, correspond -
ing to a decrease in hardness.
The main reasons for choosing to add Nb element among many 
alloying elements are as follows: First, Nb has good thermal stability 
[ 10 , 11 ]. Second, Nb will form a stable compound with Cr [ 12 , 13 ]. 
Finally, Nb will not cause too much electron scattering in the Cu matrix 
[ 14 ]. Therefore, the Nb element can improve the properties of the 
Cu – Cr – Zr alloy.
In this work, the optimal wear parameters of the Cu – Cr – Zr – Nb alloy 
under different aging processes were determined. And the relationship 
between the phase structure, hardness, and wear properties of the alloy 
was systematically established. On this basis, the wear mechanism of the 
alloy under different aging processes was further explored.

2.3. Performance testing
The Vickers hardness test was conducted on Cu – Cr – Zr – Nb alloy 
using a Zwick/Roell tester with a load of 100 gf and a dwell time of 15 s. 
And the hardness was measured five times and averaged to ensure 
accuracy.
A HT-1000 wear test machine was used to test the wear properties of 
the Cu – Cr – Zr – Nb alloy with a friction pair on the ball-disk. A 45
# 
stainless steel ball with a 4 mm diameter was selected as the friction pair 
with a grinding radius of 4 mm. The schematic diagram is shown in 
Fig. 1 , and the parameters of the wear test are shown in Table 2 .
The YHM-2003 electronic balance was used to measure the mass loss 
of the alloy during the wear test with an accuracy of 0.001 g. In this 
work, the mass loss of the Cu – Cr – Zr – Nb alloy was considered, whereas 
the mass loss of the ball was ignored. On the one hand, a new ball was 
selected for each wear test, which ensured the same test condition. On 
the other hand, the mass loss of the ball was very small compared to the 
alloy sample.
The wear surface was characterized by laser scanning confocal mi -
croscope (OLS4000). A nanoindentation instrument (Hysitron TI950) 
was used to test the subsurface microhardness of the wear track with a 
Berkovich indenter. The load, holding time, and loading/unloading rate 
were 5 mN, 10 s, and 1 m/s, respectively. The microhardness was 
measured five times to minimize measurement error.
