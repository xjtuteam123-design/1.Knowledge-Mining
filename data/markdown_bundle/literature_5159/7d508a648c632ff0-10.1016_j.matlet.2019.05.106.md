Abstract The effect of Ca content on the microstructure and tensile property of AE42 alloy was studied. 
By increasing Ca content, the microstructure changed from dendrite crystal to equiaxed grain, and the grains 
were refined continuously. The Al2Ca phase which has higher heat resistance was formed, replacing the 
original Al 11Sm3 phase, and the formation of Mg 17Al12 phase was effectively inhibited. Moreover, t he 
addition of Ca significantly improved the yield strength of AE42 alloy at both room and elevated 
temperatures. Furthermore, the tensile strength of AE42 alloy at elevated temperature was improved 
significantly . 
Keywords: AE42 alloy; Calcium content; Microstructure; Phase transformation; Tensile property; 
1. Introduction 
Nowadays, magnesium alloy s have a good development prospect in automobile, aerospace and 
electronic industry [1]. However, the poor elevated temperature performance restricts their further 
application [2]. Therefore, how to improve the elevated temperature performance of magnesium alloys has 
been the focus of research. The successful development of AE42 alloy has greatly expanded the application 
range of magnesium alloys [3]. In the AE42 alloy , Rare Earth (RE) and Al elements will form the dispersion 
distribution intermetallic compounds, such as Al2RE and Al11RE3 phases. However, there is Mg17Al12 phase

which will soften when the temperature is higher than 120℃ in the AE42 alloy [4]. Furthermore, when the 
temperature is higher than 150℃, the Al11RE3 phase will decompose [5]. This greatly limits the application 
of AE42 alloy when the temperature is above 150℃. Wang et al. [6] found that Ca element could combine 
with Al elements to generate Al 2Ca phase which has high thermostability in Mg-Al alloys, and it could 
improve the heat resistance obviously. Then, can the addition of Ca to AE42 alloy also improve the heat 
resistance? 
Hence, the aim of the research is to study the effect of Ca addition on the microstructure and tensile 
property of AE42 alloy . Analyzing the varying pattern of microstructure and tensile property under the 
change of Ca content. 
2. Experimental 
In the study , 0.5wt%, 1wt%, 2wt% and 3wt% Ca were added into AE42 alloy . All the tested alloys 
were melt in graphite crucible by using an electric resistant furnace. The pure magnesium and aluminum 
were melted at 730 ℃, then at 760 ℃ added Mg-Sm and Mg-Ca master alloys in the melt. When all the 
master alloys were melted, raising the temperature to 780 ℃ for static settlement 30 minutes and then 
poured the molten alloys into a permanent mould ( 150×80×25mm). The samples for microstructure 
observation were ground by using abrasive papers and etched in a solution of 2ml nitric acid and 98ml ethyl 
alcohol. The microstructures of all samples were examined by light microscope (GX71, OLYMPUS, Tokyo, 
Japan). The precipitated phases were examined by transmission electron microscope ( JEM-2100, 200kV , 
Tokyo, Japan) and scanning electron microscope ( Quanta 200, FEI, Eindhoven, The Netherlands) which 
equipped with an energy -dispersive X -ray spectrometer. The universal tensile testing machine which 
equipped with an electronic furnace was used to test the tensile property of all samples. The tensile property 
was tested with a strain rate 0.5mm/min at 25℃ (room-temperature), 150℃, 175℃ and 225℃. The size

3. Results and discussion 
Fig. 1 shows light microscope micrograph and grain size of these alloys. As seen in this figure, the 
morphology of the α-Mg phase in AE42 alloy is dendrite crystal. However, by increasing Ca content, the 
α-Mg phase tends to change to equiaxed grain. Furthermore, the grain size is also affected by Ca content. 
The higher the Ca content, the smaller the grain size . This is because with the Ca addition , bone-like 
intermetallic phase formed in the grain boundary . The bone -like intermetallic phase can hinder the 
continuous growth of grains [7]. By increasing the Ca content, the amount of bone-like intermetallic phase 
increases. 
In order to understand the precipitated phases in tested alloys, SEM together with EDS analysis were 
performed, as shown in Fig.2. As seen in this figure, there are particle-like Al2Sm, needle-like Al11Sm3 and 
reticular-like Mg17Al12 phases in AE42 alloy . When adding 0.5wt%Ca in AE42 alloy , the amount of 
Al11Sm3 and Mg17Al12 phases decreases, and bone-like Al2Ca phase is formed. When the content of Ca 
increased to 1wt%, the Mg17Al12 phases disappeared, the amount of Al11Sm3 phase continued to decrease. 
As continued to increase the Ca content, Al11Sm3 phase disappeared and lamella-like (Mg, Al)2Ca phase is 
formed. The reason for this phenomenon is the affinity of Al with Ca is larger than that with Mg. When Ca 
element was added, Al element combined with Ca element to form Al2Ca phase, which inhibited the 
formation of Mg17Al12 phase. As continued to add Ca element, the Al content was not enough to form Al2Ca 
phase with Ca element. At this point, the Ca will combine with Mg and Al to form (Mg, Al)2Ca phase. 
To verify further the precipitated phases, TEM together with electron diffraction were performed, as 
shown in Fig. 3. By calculating the interplanar spacing and angle in the diffraction patterns, it was 
determined that particle-like phase is Al2Sm, needle-like phase is Al11Sm3, reticular-like phase is Mg17Al12,

bone-like phase is Al2Ca, and lamella-like phase is (Mg, Al)2Ca. The results of TEM analysis is identical 
with SEM observations and EDS analysis. 
Fig. 4 shows tensile strength, yield strength and elongation of these tested alloys. When Ca is not 
added, the yield strength of AE42 alloy under room temperature is lower than the literature data. This 
phenomenon is due to the difference in rare earth elements . In this study , the Sm which is low cost was 
selected as the raw materials for rare earth. Comparing with Y , Gd, Nd and other rare earth elements, the 
solid solubility and the strength of the precipitated phase of Sm elements in Mg -Al alloys are lower. 
Therefore, the property of AE42 alloy with Sm as rare earth element is lower than that of traditional AE42 
alloy . In addition, when the experimental temperature was room temperature, by increasing the Ca content, 
the tensile strength and elongation decrease d, while the yield strength increase d. This is because by 
increasing Ca content, the amount of non-equilibrium eutectic phase increases. The Al2Ca and (Mg, Al)2Ca 
phases belong to brittle phase [8]. This leads to a decrease in tensile strength and elongation. Furthermore, 
with the increase of Ca content, the microstructure is effectively refined. The grain refinement results in 
higher yield strength of the alloys [9]. As keep raising the experimental temperature, the tensile strength of 
AE42 alloy which is without Ca addition decreases obviously. Compared with room temperature, when the 
experimental temperature is 200℃, the tensile strength of AE42 alloy decreased by 94MPa. However, the 
tensile strength of AE42 +3wt%Ca alloy decreased by only 7MPa . Moreover, when the experimental 
temperature was 200℃, the tensile strength of AE42+3wt%Ca alloy was higher than that of AE42 alloy 
with 38MPa. This is due to the different precipitated phas es in these alloys. At room temperature, the 
Al11Sm3 phase is stable, and plays the role of grain boundary strengthening. However, as the experimental 
temperature increases, the Al11Sm3 phase decomposes and cannot strengthen the grain boundary. Compared 
with the Al11Sm3 phase, the Al2Ca phase is more stable and would not decompose at 200℃ experimental

elevated temperatures, the yield strength increases with the Ca content. It is different than the theory at

performed at elevated temperatures, the Al2Ca phase plays a important role to inhibit grain boundary gliding

4. Conclusion 
(1) The addition of Ca element to AE42 alloy causes significant refinement of microstructure, and the 
Al2Ca phase which has higher heat resistance was formed, replacing the original Al11Sm3 phase. 
(2) The addition of Ca significantly improved the yield strength at both room and elevated 
temperatures. The tensile strength at elevated temperature was improved significantly .
