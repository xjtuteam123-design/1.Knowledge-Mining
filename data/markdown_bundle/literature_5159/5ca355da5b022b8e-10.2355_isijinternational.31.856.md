Mechanical properties were studied by tensile test of a Ti-15V-3Cr-3Sn-3Al alloy obtained by
duplex-aging after cold-swaging, i.e. aging at high temperatures followed by re-aging at a lower temperature.
Combinations of short-time aging at 823, 873 and 923Kand re-aging at 673Khave greatly improved the
strength-ducti]ity balance of this alloy, realizing high strength of about I .8 GPawith tensile elongation of
50/0. This duplex-aging process further diminishes the influence of the amountof reduction by cold-swaging
on the mechanical properties after aging.
KEYWORDS:beta titanium alloy; Ti-15V-3Cr-3Sn-3Al; cold-working; aging; mechanical properties;
duplex-aging.
1. Introduction
The heat-treatment method of aging after cold-
working is known to remarkably promote the aging
response and fine precipitation of alpha phase in
commercial beta titanium alloys.1) It wasreported that,
compared with the method of aging without cold-
working after solution-treatment, this heat-treatment
method improved the balance between strength and
ductility of a commercial beta titanium alloy, Ti-13V-
l ICr-3Al.2) The features of the beta titanium alloy in-
clude its superior cold-formability in the solution-treated
state and its increased strength after undergoing sub-
sequent aging-treatment. Consequently, the method of
aging after cold-forming is a valuable heat-treatment
methodwhich utilizes the features of the beta titanium
alloy. However, it
was considered that this heat-treat-
ment methodreduced ductility while aiming to achieve
high strength and that the strength-ductility balance
could not be improved for the commercial beta tita-
nium alloy, Ti-15V-3Cr-3Sn-3A1 (hereafter abbreviat-
ed as Til 53).3 ~6)
This research investigates the mechanical properties
achieved through the aging of Ti-15-3 after solution
treatment followed by cold-working, focusing particu-
larly on highlow temperature duplex aging.

5. Conclusion
This research investigated the mechanical properties
of Ti-15-3 that underwent aging after cold-working,
with particular emphasis on highlow temperature du-
plex aging. Themain findings can be summarizedas fol-
lows:
(1) Although high-temperature agings performed at
823 and 873Kafter cold-working produced only a slight
increase in strength, a temporary increase in ductility was
observed as aging progressed. This increase in strength
was at most in the samelevel as the increase produced
by aging after solution-treatment, but superior ductility
was realized if a proper aging time was selected.
(2) Strength wassignificantly increased by the second
aging at 673K in addition to the previous aging.
However, only moderate reduction of ductility was
observed as the aging progressed.
(3) By combining the first aging that wasperformed
at a high temperature for a short period of time and the
secondaging that wasperformed at 673K, it waspossible
to improve the balance betweenthe strength andductility
of this alloy. For example, while maintaining 5o/o total

(4) By this duplex aging method, the influence of
