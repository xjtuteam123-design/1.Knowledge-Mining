ORIGINAL RESEARCH ARTICLE
Inﬂuence Mechanism of c/C213Phase Dissolution
on Microstructural Characteristics and Creep Properties
of Ni-Based Single-Crystal Superalloys at 1200 /C176 C
Liang Luo, Longlong Di, Hao Fu, Yuebiao Yang, Xuehai Qian, Zheyu He, Yimin Li, and Fenghua Luo
Submitted: 1 May 2024 / Revised: 6 September 2024 / Accepted: 14 September 2024 / Published online: 8 March 2025
Advanced aero-engines with thrust-to-weight ratios of 15–20 require turbine blade materials (single-crystal
superalloys) that can operate at temperatures of up to 1200 /C176C. However, the microstructural evolution of
such superalloys is dominated by c/C213phase dissolution at 1200 /C176C. Here, the effect of c/C213phase dissolution on
the microstructural characteristics and creep properties of superalloys at 1200 /C176C was investigated. Nine
experimental Ni-based superalloys containing different c/C213-forming elements, including Ta and Al, were
prepared. The study showed that c/C213phase dissolution decreased the strength and elastic modulus of the c
matrix and increased its effective diffusion coefﬁcient. The yield strength increment resulted from increased
resistance to dislocation shear in the c/C213phase, which was enriched by Ta and Al. Moreover, the effect of c/C213
phase dissolution on the strength of the c/C213phase was negligible. The formation of rattan-shaped structures
and topological inversion of the c–c/C213phases in Al- and Ta-rich alloys were instrumental in weakening the
creep properties at 1200 /C176C. Finally, a creep property–microstructure control model that considers c/C213phase
dissolution and excessive Ta and Al contents at 1200 /C176C was established; the values calculated using this
model were consistent with the experimental observations. The ﬁndings of this study promote the design or
selection of superalloys suitable for ultra-high-temperature applications.
Keywords creep behavior, dissolution, microstructural
characteristic, superalloy, ultra-high temperature
1. Introduction
Aero-engines comprise numerous components, among
which high-pressure turbine guide blades and rotor blades are
the core components, requiring high temperature and load-
bearing capacities and strongly affecting engine performance.
The high-pressure turbine blades are made of Ni-based single-
crystal (SC) superalloys. The operation temperature limit of
superalloys has gradually increased from 1020 to 1120 /C176 C (Ref
1-3], while the target temperature for next-generation superal-
loys is in the 1150–1200 /C176 C range (Ref 4, 5). Future advanced
aero-engines with thrust-to-weight ratios of 15–20 will likely
require turbine blade materials (SC superalloys) with an
operation temperature limit of 1200 /C176 C (Ref 6).
The temperature-bearing capacity of SC superalloys is
closely related to their high-temperature creep properties, which
are directly affected by microstructural evolution. At £
1100 /C176 C, the microstructural evolution of these alloys is
dominated by c/C213coarsening, such as c/C213non-directional
coarsening, c/C213-rafting, and c-c/C213topological inversion. The
effects of these processes on the creep properties of SC
superalloys have been investigated (Ref 7-12). However,
microstructural evolution at > 1100 /C176 C is dominated by c/C213
phase dissolution (Ref 13-16). This process is completed within
a short period (Ref 17-19) and presents in the creep deceler-
ation stage or the initial stage of a steady creep. Therefore, c/C213
phase dissolution has a strong effect on the creep behavior of
SC superalloys. This process reduces the fraction of the c/C213
phase, weakens the precipitation strengthening effect, decreases
the concentration of c-strengthening elements, and weakens
solid-solution strengthening because the c matrix forming
elements Re, Mo were ‘‘diluted.’’ In addition, c/C213phase
dissolution promotes the reduction of the interface misﬁt (Ref
10, 17, 18). Therefore, compared with c/C213phase coarsening, the
effect of c/C213phase dissolution on creep properties is more
signiﬁcant at ultra-high temperatures.
Thus far, few studies on Ni-based superalloys have focused
on the correlation among c/C213phase dissolution, microstructural
characteristics, and ultra-high-temperature creep properties.
Moreover, a quantitative relationship between c/C213phase disso-
lution and the ultra-high-temperature creep properties of
superalloys has not been established.

vacuum induction melting furnace. SC rods were prepared
using a DZG-0.025 directional solidiﬁcation furnace.

3. Results and Discussion
3.1 c/C213Phase Dissolution at 1200 /C176C
The nine superalloys were quenched after aging for different
periods at 1200 /C176 C. The microstructural evolution of the ﬁve
typical superalloys is illustrated in Fig. 1. After aging for 1 h,
the corner of the cubic c/C213phase disappeared and the c channel
widened. Despite partial dissolution, the c/C213phase exhibited
directional coalescence in the SC9 superalloy. After aging for
2 h, the c/C213phases were coarsened and further dissolved in
several superalloys. However, after aging for 10 h, the adjacent
c/C213phases were merged and interconnected. Using the SEM
images, the V
c/C213values of several superalloys were measured
(Fig. 2). The V c/C213of each superalloy was observed to decrease
with increasing aging time and stabilize after 2 h.
Considering the results presented in Fig. 2, the dissolution
coefﬁcient ( k) that describes the degree of c/C213phase dissolution
is deﬁned as k =( 1 /C0 f0)/(1 /C0 fT), where f0 is Vc/C213at 25 /C176 C and
fT is V c/C213in the thermal equilibrium state at temperature T. The

Fig. 3. The k values were observed to decrease with increasing

3.5 Creep Properties of SC Superalloys at 1200 /C176C
The creep curves of the nine superalloys at 1200 /C176 C/80 MPa
are illustrated in Fig. 7a. The creep lives of the SC3, SC4, SC5,
and SC6 superalloys at 1200 /C176 C/80 MPa exceeded 100 h,
particularly for the SC5 superalloy, which exhibited a good
ultra-high-temperature creep life. The creep lives of the other
alloys were lower than that of the SC5 superalloy (Fig. 7b).
Notably, the addition of Ta or Al increased the fraction,
strength, and c/C213phase thermal stability within a speciﬁc range
(Ref 5). Therefore, the creep lives of the SC1–SC4 alloys were
lower than that of the SC5 superalloy with higher Ta and Al
contents. However, upon the further addition of Ta or Al atoms,
the creep lives of superalloys, such as the SC9 superalloy, were
not further improved and were even lower than that of the SC5
superalloy.
The creep lives of superalloys are directly affected by their
microstructure before service operations. Some studies have
shown that the c/C213volume fraction is the key parameter
controlling the strain rate at ultra-high temperatures (Ref 40–
42). In our previous publications (Ref 43, 44), we reported
that rattan-shaped structures with irregular arrangements and
random distributions were easily formed in Al- and Ta-rich
alloys (Fig. 8a) from the irregularly arranged c/C213phases
(Fig. 8b). In fact, rattan-shaped structures are actually
composed of a series of c/C213connected together; therefore, the
volume fraction is related to the content of c/C213phase-forming
elements such as Ta and Al, i.e., the higher the Ta or Al
content, the more rattan-shaped structures will form. In the
studied creep fracture samples, approximately 68% of the
micropores and cracks tended to initiate and expand in or near
the rattan-shaped structures (Fig. 8c). These structures also
obstructed c/C213rafting and formed a bow-shaped interface at
their junctions with cubic c/C213rafts. Notably, dislocations were
easily stacked at the interface. As dislocations continuously
accumulated, these dislocations caused a stress concentration
at the interface and facilitated the initiation of microcracks

4. Conclusions
Herein, the inﬂuence mechanism of c/C213phase dissolution on
the microstructural characteristics and creep properties of Ni-
based SC superalloys at 1200 /C176 C was investigated. From the
obtained results, the following conclusions have been drawn.
1. c/C213phase dissolution was inhibited by increasing the Ta
and Al contents. This decreased the strength and elastic
modulus of the c matrix and increased the diffusion coefﬁcient.
2. 4r
p caused by dislocation shear in the c/C213phase increased
with increasing Ta and Al contents. c/C213phase dissolution
resulted in the redistribution of the c and c/C213phase compositions,
which had negligible effects on the c/C213phase strength.
3. A creep property–microstructure control model was
established at 1200 /C176 C that considers c/C213phase dissolution and
excessive Ta and Al contents. The obtained experimental
results were in good agreement with the values calculated using
this model.
