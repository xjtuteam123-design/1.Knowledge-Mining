Effect of Microstructure on High-Cycle Fatigue Properties 
of Ti-6Al-4V Alloy Forging at Cryogenic Temperatures 
 
T. Yuria, Y. Onoa, T. Ogataa, and H. Sunakawab 
aNational Institute for Materials Science, Tsukuba, 305-0047, JAPAN 
bJapan Aerospace Exploration Agency, Tsukuba, 305-8505, JAPAN 
 
Abstract. The effect of microstructure on the high-cycle fatigue properties of Ti-6Al-4V alloy forging at cryogenic 
temperature was investigated using four materials with different microstructure. One of them was α+β annealed and has 
bimodal microstructure which consists of equiaxed- α and α+β lamellar mi crostructure. Three other materials were 
water quenched (WQ), air cooled (AC) or furnace cooled (FC) after β -annealing. The β -annealed materials have 
lamellar microstructure and α lamellar width increase in the order of WQ, AC and FC materials. The fatigue test was 
done with uni-axial load-controlled and a stress ratio of R = σmin/σmax = 0.01 at 20 K (in gaseous helium), 77 K (i n 
liquid nitrogen) and 293 K (in laboratory air). The 107-cycles fatigue strengths at 20 K decrease in the order of WQ, 
AC, FC and the α+β annealed material. Namely, the β-annealed Ti-6Al-4V alloys, which has lamellar α microstructures 
shows higher fatigue strengths than bimodal microstructure. 
 
Keywords: Ti-6Al-4V alloy, β-annealed, high-cycle fatigue properties, cryogenic temperatures 
PACS: 96.30.nd; 81.40.Np; 81.40.Gh 
 
INTRODUCTION 
 
 
Titanium alloys have high specific strength and many advantageous physical properties, such as low 
conductivity and high electric resistivity when used as cryogenic structural components. For example, titanium 
alloys are used as the material for the Japanese rocket en gines that serve as high-performance propellant system 
using liquid hydrogen and liquid oxygen [1]. The engine parts of titanium alloy are exposed to liquid hydrogen and 
used under tension-tension loading condition during oper ation. Therefore, it is very important to evaluate the 
fatigue performance for titanium alloys to be used for the engine parts. 
Ti-6Al-4V alloy is one of the most popular α+β titanium alloy and used in varieties of applications. It is well 
known that the mechanical properties of α+β titanium alloys are very sensitive to the microstructure and that of 
α+β titanium alloys strongly depends on both the processing history and the heat treatment [2-6]. 
When titanium alloy is used for structural components, it is generally used for titanium alloy which has the 
equiaxed or the bimodal microstructure. On the other hand, it was reported that the large-size Ti-6Al-4V alloy with 
the lamellar microstructure is more advantageous for high cycle fatigue strength than equiaxed microstructure 
[7,8] because the turbo pump is made from the forged large-size titanium alloy of about φ180×L150 by cutting [9]. 
In this study, we obtained the high-cycle fatigue properties of the bimodal and lamellar α microstructures for 
the Ti-6Al-4V alloy at cryogenic temperatures and discussed the effect of the microstructure on high-cycle fatigue 
properties at cryogenic temperatures.

EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURE 
 
 
Material and Specimens, and Testing 
 
The material used in this study was the Ti-6Al-4V alloy forging, which is co mmercial titanium alloy in 
conforming to AMS 4928 E (forging ratio : 2.4, φ350×L200); (α+β) annealed material were heated for 7.2ks at 
1213 K followed by water-quenching and for 10.2ks air-cooled at 978 K. The chemical compositions of the present 
alloy are listed in Table 1. 
Heat treatment conditions of each β-annealed material are shown in Figure 1. The β-annealed materials were 
heated for 3.6ks at 1323 K (above β-transus temperature), and then furnace-cooled (FC), air-cooled (AC) and 
water-quenched (WQ). Finally, FC, AC and WQ materials were followed by 7.2ks furnace-cooled at 993 K. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
The tensile properties of (α+β) annealed and β-annealed Ti-6Al-4V alloy at each test temperature are listed in 
Table 2. The 0.2% proof stress and the tensile strength (TS) of FC and AC materials at each temperature were 
slightly lower than those of ( α+β)-annealed material. However, those of WQ material were higher than 
(α+β)-annealed material. The elongation and the reduction of area for the each β-annealed material at each 
temperature were lower than those of (α+β)-annealed material. 
The fatigue test specimens were the hourglass-type with a minimum waist diameter of 4.5 mm. The specimen 
surfaces were polished down to #600 using SiC emery paper.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION 
 
 
Microstructures 
 
Figure 2 represents the optical micrographs of each microstructure. The microstructure of the ( α+β) annealed 
material (Figure 1(a)) has bimodal microstructure which consists of equiaxed-α (diameter ~20µm) and α+β lamellar 
microstructure. On the other hand, the β-annealed materials, FC (Figure 1(b)), AC (Figure 1(c)) and WQ (FIGURE 
1(d)) has lamellar microstructure. The width of α lamellar in FC material is from 4 to 12µm. and that of AC 
material from 2 to 3µm. The WQ material shows the finer α lamellar structure. The prior- β grain structure was 
observed in FC, AC and WQ materials and its size in each material was roughly about 900~2000µm, 700~1800 
µm and 600~1500µm, respectively. The prior-β grain structure is consists of several colonies.

SUMMARY 
 
The effect of microstructure on the high-cycle fatigue properties of Ti-6Al-4V alloy forging at cryogenic 
temperature were investigated using four materials with different microstructure. One of them was α+β annealed 
and has bimodal microstructure which consists of equiaxed- α and α+β lamellar microstructure. Three other 
materials were water quenched (WQ), air cooled (AC) or furnace cooled (FC) after β -annealing. The β-annealed 
materials have lamellar microstructure and α lamellar width in crease in the order of WQ, AC and FC materials. 
The 107-cycles fatigue strengths at 20 K decrease in the order of WQ, AC, FC and the α+β annealed material. This 
result means that the high- cycle fatigue strengths of β -annealed materials are much higher than that of α+β 
annealed one in the present forging. However, the opposite results were reported in the pastmany studies. This is 
considered to be the rolling or the forging procedure affecting texture. Further investigations are clearly needed to 
analyze the crystallographic orientation of a facet formed at a fatigue crack initiation sites in a specimen fatigue 
tested by electron backscatter diffraction (EDSD) in a SEM.
