ABSTRACT 
Accurate prediction of the creep life is important during the alloy design and optimization of nickel-based single 
crystal superalloys, especially for those with expensive alloying elements such as Re and Ru. In this study, a two- 
model linkage method was developed to predict the creep life of nickel-based single crystal superalloys through a 
data-driven machine learning approach, based on a small dataset collected from literatures. Owing to the small 
data amount, the precision of the model which was constructed with the alloy composition, creep temperature, 
and creep stress as inputs and used to predict the creep life is low, but the precision of the model which was 
constructed with the alloy composition, creep temperature, and creep life as inputs and used to predict the creep 
stress is relatively high. The high-precision model was applied to judge and adjust the prediction results of the 
low-precision model, thus effectively improving the accuracy of the overall creep life prediction. Moreover, the 
well-trained model-linkage was used to analyze, as a showcase, the interactive effects of alloying elements on the 
creep life of nickel-based single crystal superalloys. The prediction results provided a clear mapping of syner -
gistic effects in the investigated compositional space, which was very consistent with previous, experimental 
results. This consistency thus confirms the effectiveness of the two-model linkage method in studying synergistic 
effects of multiple alloying elements. This study will benefit the compositional design and optimization of next- 
generation nickel-based single crystal superalloys. 
1. Introduction 
Nickel-based single crystal (SX) superalloys have excellent compre -
hensive properties at high temperature, hence they have been exten -
sively used for the manufacture of hot-section in aero-engine and gas 
turbine components [1,2] . To further improve the performance, effi -
ciency and reliability of engines, the development of SX superalloys with 
improved creep performance at even higher temperatures is very 
important. Although the creep life can be determined experimentally, 
the use of a non-minute amounts of various refractory elements in these 
alloys, especially of the expensive Re and Ru [3,4] , leads to prohibitively 
high costs associated with creep tests. Thus, establishing a quantitative 
relationship between the composition and creep life is critical for 
guiding alloy design and optimization of next-generation nickel-based 
SX superalloys [5] . 
Traditionally, the Larson-Miller parametric method and the θ pro -
jection method are widely used to predict creep life. The Larson-Miller 
parametric method [6] expresses the relationship between tempera -
ture and creep life as a function of stress, and extrapolates to long-term 
life of alloys by fitting the data in the short term creep life region. The θ 
projection method [7] and its modified model [8,9] are based on the 
assumption that the creep process is composed of creep strengthening 
and softening. The description of the complete creep curve is achieved 
by describing the creep strain versus time in these two stages separately 
via an exponential form. Both methods are based on a significant 
amount of experimental results for a given alloy (and processing), and 
cannot be applied to another alloy. To address this problem, Yokokawa 
et al. [10] established creep life prediction equations of nickel-based SX 
superalloys for different creep conditions, using multiple regression 
analysis. This method uses the γ’ phase composition, γ’ volume fraction 
and lattice misfit as explanatory variables, which necessitates that the 
initial dataset contain large amounts of experimental results for the SX 
superalloys. However, the coefficients of the equations have not been 
fully disclosed, making it difficult to easily apply these equations to

In recent years, with the emergence of materials genome engineering 
(MGE) and data mining methods [11 – 13] , the successful use of machine 
learning (ML) to predict the performance of materials [14,15] and to 
design materials [16 – 18] has gradually attracted widespread attention. 
For instance, Guo et al. [19] used ML to establish a relationship between 
composition, heat treatment and mechanical properties of a nickel- 
based polycrystalline superalloy K44 with a large amount of mechani -
cal property data accumulated over an extended period of time. Conduit 
et al. [20] trained several artificial neural network (ANN) models on a 
big dataset to predict the mechanical properties of nickel-based poly -
crystalline superalloys, e.g. fatigue life, yield strength and fracture 
stress. The well-trained models were then applied to design a novel alloy 
with excellent comprehensive properties. Similarly, Suzuki et al. [21] 
made use of a large amount of historical data for nickel-based SX su -
peralloys accumulated by General Electric (GE) over the years and 
adopted different ML algorithms to construct models of physical prop -
erty, mechanical property, manufacturing property and environmental 
resistance of the alloys, thus realizing an efficient development and 
design of nickel-based superalloy. 
However, the publicly reported data for nickel-based SX superalloys 
are not enough, in comparison with other types of nickel-based super -
alloys. Therefore, it is crucial to address problems related to the limited 
data when considering complex mechanisms, and how to improve the 
predictive capability of a given ML model. In order to accelerate the 
modeling of the creep life of nickel-based SX superalloys using a small 
database, Liu et al. [22] developed a divide-and-conquer self-adaptive 
(DCSA) learning method incorporating multiple material descriptors, 
including stacking fault energy, lattice parameters, mole fractions of the 
γ’ phase, diffusion coefficients and shear moduli, as calculated by the 
CALPHAD (CALculation of PHAse Diagrams) method and various basic 
materials structure – property relationships. They divided the 266 alloy 
samples obtained from the published patents into different clusters by 
clustering analysis and assumed that the superalloys in each cluster 
followed the same creep mechanism, and then constructed an optimal 
model for each cluster. This greatly improved the predictive ability of 
their ML model. Even when the eight newly acquired alloy samples 
whose compositions varied significantly with different test conditions 
from the 266 instances were added to the original dataset, the prediction 
accuracy of DSCA model still reached 0.8268. Such works should 
encourage the use of methods emerging from the field of artificial in -
telligence to be adapted to the characteristics of materials data. 
In the present study, we propose a two-model linkage method which 
uses ML to predict the creep life of nickel-based SX superalloys from a 
small dataset. Such a model linkage effectively improves the prediction 
accuracy by simply using the publicly reported data of alloy composi -
tion, test temperature, test stress and creep lives. In addition, the 
interaction of key alloying elements, such as Co, Cr, Mo, and Ru, on the 
creep life is further explored using this method and compared with 
experimental data. This study provides stepping stones for the modeling 
of small datasets in materials science. A good understanding for the 
characteristics of data in a dataset, not limited to constructing ML model 
for the main predicted target, establishing a combination of a main 
model and an additional model for error correction may serve as an 
attempt to model small datasets. 
2. Methodology 
Fig. 1 shows the framework for predicting the creep life of nickel- 
based SX superalloys used in this study. It consists of three main parts: 
(I) building a small dataset by collecting creep life data of nickel-based 
SX superalloys from the published literature; (II) establishing a life 
model, Model L, and a stress model, Model σ , by comparing the appli -
cability of seven ML regression algorithms; (III) linking the two models 
through computer programming for a more accurate creep life 
prediction.

2.2. Modeling methods by ML 
Several ML algorithms can be used for the modeling, and each of 
them has different parameters. The performance of the trained model 
often differs significantly depending on the algorithm and how the 
different parameters for the same algorithm are configured. Therefore, it 
is crucial to determine the most suitable algorithm and parameters for a 
particular work. In this paper, seven regression algorithms were selected 
for modeling, viz. Ridge Regression (Ridge) [23] , Lasso Regression 
(Lasso) [24] , K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) [25] , Random Forest Regres -
sion (RFR) [26] , Gradient Boosting Regression (GBR) [27] , Support 
Vector Regression (SVR) [28] and Back Propagation Neural Network 
(BPNN) [29] . 
Fig. 3 shows a schematic diagram of the modeling based on the ML 
approach. The modeling process is divided into 4 steps. Step I, using the 
MinMaxScaler feature in the Scikit-learn toolkit [30] to preprocess the 
dataset, the principle of which is to convert all data feature values to 
between 0 and 1, making the model training faster and more accurate. 
Step II, using the preprocessed dataset to get optimized models of each 
algorithm through parameter tuning. Step III, comparing the optimized 
models of these algorithms and selecting the model with the best per -
formance as the final model. Step IV, comparing the predicted and actual 
values of the final model. 
In Step II, a grid search method with 10-fold cross-validation is used 
to tune the parameters of the algorithm, which can be directly called via 
the GridSearchCV program in the Scikit-learn toolkit. GridSearchCV 
considers all parameter combinations by grid-search and evaluates 
model performance by cross-validation, which is helpful to avoid over -
fitting. This method contains the following two steps: first, setting 
several different values of each important parameter in the algorithm; 
second, splitting the dataset into 10 parts. For each combination of pa -
rameters, 9 parts are used as the training dataset for model training and 
the remaining 1 part is used as testing dataset for model testing. The 
average value of the testing goodness-of-fit (R
2
) of all 10 iterations is 
used as the metric for evaluating each group of parameters. The best 
model corresponding to each algorithm will be obtained after

3.3. Evaluation of the method reliability 
To further evaluate the reliability of the two-model linkage method 
proposed in this study, two aspects were tested: Interpolation and 
Extrapolation. 
Eight new samples whose composition, temperature and stress are all 
in the range of the database were selected to test the interpolation 
capability. These new data included alloy CMSX-4 (tested at 1100 ℃ / 
137 MPa and 1000 ℃ /245 MPa), alloy CMSX-10 (tested at 1100 ℃ /137 
MPa), alloy TMS-173 (tested at 1100 ℃ /137 MPa) and alloy TMS-238 
(tested at 1000 ℃ /245 MPa) [31,32] . The prediction results of the 
two-model linkage method are listed in Table 1 . Although there is still 
room for improving the predictive accuracy, the two-model linkage 
method developed in this study demonstrates considerable interpolation 
capability, considering the sparsity of the database (small amount of 
data, unevenly distributed, etc.) and the error and dispersion of the 
experimental process itself. 
Two alloys with creep conditions outside of the database range were 
used to test the extrapolation capability, namely, alloy CMSX-4 and alloy 
TMS-173 tested at 900 ℃ /392 MPa and 800 ℃ /735 MPa, respectively. 
The results are also listed in Table 1 . It seems that the accuracy of the 
two-model linkage method proposed in this paper is limited when pre -
dicting data beyond the coverage of the database. The main source of 
error stems from the difference in creep deformation mechanisms that 
are operative at different test condition [33,34] . Since most of the data 
in our database belongs to the high temperature/low stress conditions, 
the predictive capability of the ML model for data that has significantly 
different testing conditions, i.e. potentially very different operative 
creep-deformation mechanisms, becomes more limited. This showcases 
the limitations of trying to extrapolate into conditions that have physi -
cally different deformation mechanisms during creep. That is to say, it is 
not trivial to build a universal model for all creep problems in materials 
science. 
4. Discussion 
4.1. Reasons of not including microstructural parameters in our model 
The present work does not use specific microstructural parameters, 
such as volume fraction, size and morphology of γ’ phase as input fea -
tures when modeling. However, it does not mean that the microstructure 
features are not important for the creep properties of superalloys 
[35 – 37] . The microstructural parameters can be subdivided into two 
aspects, including the primary microstructure after the standard heat 
treatment [38] and its evolution during creeping [39,40] . For the former 
aspect, there are two reasons why it wasn ’ t introduced in the modeling. 
Firstly, most of the published studies only report the test conditions and 
creep life of the alloy, but do not give statistically quantitative results of 
the primary microstructural parameters. Secondly, according to the 
analysis of the collected primary microstructural parameters, the γ’ 
volume fraction of nickel-based single crystal superalloys after the 
standard heat treatment was mainly distributed in the range of 
62.8 – 69.1%, and the size of the γ’ phase was mainly distributed in the 
range of 0.32 – 0.45 μ m. These are close to the optimal microstructural 
parameters of high-performance single crystal superalloys [38,41] . 
Thus, we assume that the alloys whose creep lives have been reported in 
the literatures have optimal or close to optimal primary microstructure. 
Menou et al. [42] also used similar assumptions when establishing a 
model for the creep fracture stress of nickel-based single crystal super -
alloys and achieved good results. As for the latter aspect, the micro -
structural evolution of nickel-based single crystal superalloys during 
creep mainly depends on the composition and creep condition. There -
fore, the microstructural evolution has been considered indirectly.

time was set as the output target, the prediction error of the model was 
large. In contrast, the prediction error of the model was small when 
temperature was set as the output target based on the same database. 
The authors speculated that the discrepancy was caused by the large 
range of the time. In our study, although the input and output data were 
normalized by data preprocessing before modeling, the normalization

and hidden rules more easily when the creep stress is taken as the output 
target. It is the accuracy of Model σ that enables us to realize success 
with the two-model linkage method.

some purposes, but it is unrealistic to pursue a perfect model, especially 
given that the experimental behavior also has a certain level of uncer -
tainty and probabilistic nature. We believe that the current accuracy of 
this method is fully capable for capturing the trend of creep life and can 
quickly evaluate the creep performance of a large number unexplored 
alloys. This method has a broad prospect for the rapid design and 
optimization of the composition of nickel-based single crystal superal -
loys, because it is convenient in that it only requires alloy composition 
and creep conditions as input parameters. 
4.3. Studying the synergistic effects of alloying elements by two-model 
linkage method 
Superalloys typically contain 10 – 15 elements (sometimes even 
more) to confer the desired comprehensive properties [44,45] . More -
over, the contributions made by individual alloying element to the 
microstructure and properties are not independent of each other. In 
order to take full advantage of the beneficial effects of alloying elements, 
exploring the influence of complex multi-element interactions on the 
creep properties is one of the most challenging issues in the alloy design 
and optimization of nickel-based SX superalloys. 
As one of the applications of the creep life prediction method 
established in this study, a composition space was constructed by (7 – 15) 
Co-(3.5 – 6)Cr-(1 – 2.5)Mo-(2.5 – 4)Ru-6Al-4Re-8Ta-5W-Bal.Ni (wt.%), the 
contents of Co, Cr, Mo and Ru were changed with a step size of 0.5 wt%. 
The creep lives of all alloys in the composition space under the creep 
condition of 1100 ℃ /140 MPa were predicted by the two-model linkage 
method. Combined with the creep lives of similar alloys measured by Shi 
et al [46,47] , the interactive effects of alloying elements Co, Cr, Mo and 
Ru on the creep life of nickel-based SX superalloys were analyzed. 
Table 2 shows the experimental results and the predicted results. Fig. 6 
shows the interactive effects of Co, Cr, Mo and Ru on the creep life 
obtained by the predictive method. The black dots indicate the experi -
mental results. Some interesting points are discussed as follows: 
As shown in Fig. 6 (a1), under the condition of low Ru and Mo 
contents, the increase of Cr content can reduce the creep life of the al -
loys. Although Cr is beneficial to the increase of γ / γ ΄ lattice misfit ab -
solute value and decrease the average interfacial dislocation spacing, the 
detrimental effect of promoting the precipitation of TCP phases is 
responsible for the reduced creep life of alloy SC1211 [46,47] . While, 
the creep life of the alloys firstly increases and then decreases with the 
increase of Co content, which is slightly different from the conclusion of 
Shi et al. [46,47] . Their experimental results show that the increase of 
Co content decreases the creep life of alloy SC2111. The difference may 
be attributed to that Co often poses both beneficial and detrimental ef -
fects on the creep properties of the superalloys [46] - the positive side 
being that the increase of Co content suppresses the TCP phase and the 
negative side being that the increase of Co content decreases the lattice 
misfit. However, Shi et al. [46,47] only had low Co (7 wt%) alloys and 
high Co (15 wt%) alloys, which maybe the reason they did not capture 
the inflection point. 
Comparing Fig. 6 (a1), 6 (b1), 6 (c1) and 6 (d1) shows that the in -
crease of Ru content can improve the creep lives of the alloys, which is 
consistent with the experimental results obtained by Shi et al [46,47] . 
They found that increasing Ru content can decrease the average inter -
facial dislocation spacing and suppress the formation of harmful TCP 
phases, thus improve the creep life of SC1112 compared to SC1111.

4.4. How to make the two-model linkage method more effective 
In our research, although the two-model linkage method provided a 
promising approach for improving the performance for property pre -
diction in small datasets, it also encountered some common issues dis -
cussed by Liu et al. [48] , such as the contradictions between high 
dimension and small sample data, the complexity and accuracy of the 
ML model, as well as the results and domain expert knowledge. 
Therefore, some corresponding measures can be taken to make the 
two-model linkage method more useful and accurate. First, although 
adding additional samples to the small dataset is an important approach 
for improve the performance of the model, it is often difficult to sup -
plement data with a large number of experiments due to cost and time 
resources. To address this, generating virtual data, as also proposed by 
Liu et al. [48] , may be very beneficial in making models more robust. 
Second, in order to improve the accuracy of the model, we adopted a 
more complex BPNN algorithm, which consequentially made the model 
less interpretable. Hence, some model interpretation methods (such as 
SHAP [49] ) can be adopted to explain how features affect the model 
prediction and which features have a greater impact on the model 
performance, so as to increase the understanding of the ML model. 
Third, introducing domain knowledge into the model may be an effec -
tive method to improve the accuracy and interpretability, but it is usu -
ally difficult to select appropriate scientific descriptors. Recently, Liu 
et al. [50] proposed a data-driven muti-layer feature selection method 
incorporating domain expert knowledge. This novel approach will 
accelerate the application of domain knowledge in ML. Overall, we 
believe that the above three measures will make the two-model linkage 
method more efficient in creep life predicting. 
5. Conclusions 
(1) A two-model linkage method based on ML was proposed to pre -
dict the creep life of nickel-based single crystal superalloys with a 
small dataset. In this method, a model predicting the applied 
stress with high prediction accuracy was creatively used to 
compensate for the inaccuracy of the model predicting the creep 
life. In doing so, the accuracy of the final linkage model was 
significantly improved. 
(2) The influence of elemental interaction on the creep life was 
predicted by the two-model linkage method, and in comparison, 
matched well with experimental observations. Therefore, it is 
evident that this method can predict the properties of composi -
tions in the coverage of the dataset, help to capture the optimal 
points of elemental interactions, and it can be used to do a high- 
throughput evaluation of the alloying effects during alloy design.
