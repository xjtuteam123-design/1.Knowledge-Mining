ABSTRACT 
In this paper, the thermomechanical fatigue (TMF) properties of Ni-based single crystal superalloys are studied 
by molecular dynamics simulations. Two different cyclic deformation mechanisms of superalloys are found under 
TMF loadings. The sample has a higher cyclic stress range, plastic strain energy density and a shorter fatigue life 
under Out-of-phase TMF loading than those under In-phase TMF loading. Moreover, the low-temperature tension 
half-cycle is more favorable for dislocations and stacking faults to cut into the γ ʹ precipitate phase, causing an 
earlier failure of superalloys under Out-of-phase TMF loading, which attracts more attention in actual operation. 
1. Introduction 
Ni-based single crystal superalloys are used as indispensable mate -
rials for turbine blades in aero-engines because of their excellent creep, 
fatigue and corrosion resistance [1,2] . Turbine blades are subjected to a 
large transient load and rapid change of temperature during take-off, 
landing, acceleration and deceleration of the aircraft, this cyclic 
changes in temperature and mechanical loading ultimately leads to se -
vere thermomechanical fatigue (TMF) of turbine blades [3,4] . More -
over, the large temperature gradient changes occur on the surface of 
turbine blades during the operation of cooling system, which leads to 
high thermal stresses and ultimately TMF failure of turbine blades [5,6] . 
In order to improve the overall performance of Ni-based single 
crystal superalloys, a lot of research has been done on the mechanical 
properties of superalloys under different loading conditions, such as 
uniaxial tension [7 – 9] , creep [10,11] , and fatigue [12 – 14] . Due to the 
limitations of experimental conditions and equipment, studies on fatigue 
mechanical properties of superalloys were mainly focused on isothermal 
fatigue (IF) [12 – 15] . However, it was found that the TMF damage was 
more complex than IF, and the fatigue life of superalloys was also 
different under IF and TMF loadings [16,17] . When the superalloys 
suffered from TMF loadings, there were more cracks in the superalloys, 
and the cracks initiated and extended more readily and faster than IF 
loadings [18,19] , causing the damage of the superalloys produced by 
TMF loadings more severe than IF loadings. This results in the predicted 
life of the superalloys based on IF is higher than the actual life under 
TMF loadings obtained in experiments [19 – 23] . However, there is still 
less information about the mechanical properties and especially for the 
detailed microstructure evolution of Ni-based single crystal superalloys 
under TMF loadings. Therefore, this work focuses on the mechanical 
properties and microstructure evolution of Ni-based single crystal su -
peralloys under TMF loadings and analyzes the similarities and differ -
ences of the superalloys under IF and TMF loadings. 
In TMF tests, the phases of mechanical loading and temperature can 
vary arbitrarily, but the most representative tests are In-phase TMF (IP- 
TMF) and Out-of-phase TMF (OP-TMF), respectively [18,24,25] . Previ -
ous studies found that the mechanical properties and damage mecha -
nisms of the superalloys were different under IP-TMF or OP-TMF 
loadings [21,25] . In IP-TMF test, creep was the main damage mecha -
nism, while in OP-TMF test, oxidation was considered to be the main 
damage mechanism [21,25] . Meanwhile, the superalloys generally 
produced positive mean stress under IP-TMF loading, while negative 
mean stress under OP-TMF loading [25] . The different mean stress be -
haviors were related to the nucleation and propagation of cracks, thus 
affecting fatigue life of the superalloys under TMF loadings [21] . In 
addition, it was also found that the superalloys under IP-TMF and OP- 
TMF loadings had a different fatigue life in strain-controlled fatigue 
tests. Some researchers found that the superalloys had longer life under

The microstructure evolution characteristics play an important role 
in the mechanical properties of superalloys [30] . However, it is difficult 
to observe the whole process of microstructure evolution through in-situ 
TMF tests [31,32] . Molecular dynamics (MD) simulation not only can 
simulate effectively the mechanical properties under different loading 
conditions but also to observe systematically the whole process of 
microstructure evolution of superalloys [33 – 37] . In our previous work, 
MD simulation had been well implemented to investigate the mechan -
ical properties and microstructure evolution of Ni-based single-crystal 
superalloys [37 – 40] . However, the microstructural evolution of super -
alloys under TMF loadings has not been studied by MD simulation at the 
atomic scale, which makes the microstructure evolution characteristics 
and failure mechanisms of superalloys under TMF loadings still unclear. 
Therefore, MD simulations are carried out in this work to study the TMF 
properties and microstructure evolution of superalloys, and the simi -
larities and differences of fatigue mechanical properties and micro -
structure evolution under TMF and IF loadings are compared and 
analyzed. The results will provide important guidance for comprehen -
sive performance evaluation and damage life design of turbine blades.

the lowest possible cycling temperature closer to the ambient temper -

and the melting point of Ni-based single crystal superalloys is much 
higher than 1200 K [50,51] . Furthermore, in our previous work [38,39] , 
the mechanical properties of superalloys under IF loadings at 300 K and

selected as the lowest cycling temperature, and 1200 K is selected as the 
highest cycling temperature in the present work. NPT ensemble is used 
to control the change of the temperature during the simulation. Firstly, 
the model is warmed up without load applied. In IF test, the temperature 
gradually increases from 1 K to the loading temperature (300 K or 1200 
K in this work) and then holds constant. In TMF test, the temperature 
ranges from T = 300 K to 1200 K, thus the temperature gradually in -
creases from 1 K to 750 K without applying strain. After that, the tem -
perature and load are changed simultaneously. In IP-TMF test, the 
temperature increases from 750 K to 1200 K as the strain increases from 
0 % to 3 %, then the temperature decreases from 1200 K to 300 K as the 
strain changes from 3 % to 3%, and the temperature increases from 
300 K to 750 K as the strain changes from 3% to 0 %. In OP-TMF test, 
the process is exactly opposite with IP-TMF, temperature decreases from 
1200 K to 300 K as strain increases from 3% to 3 % and increases from 
300 K to 1200 K as strain changes from 3 % to 3%, as shown in Fig. 2 . 
Finally, the atomic-scale microstructure evolutions and deformation 
mechanisms are analyzed through Dislocation analysis (DXA) [52] and 
Common neighbor analysis (CNA) [53] with the cooperation of visual -
ization program-OVITO software [54] .

3.1.4. Comparison of mechanical properties of superalloys under IF and 
TMF loadings 
Fig. 6 shows the comparison of the stress – strain curves of samples 
after entering the cyclic stable stage under IF and TMF loadings. It can be 
seen that the stress – strain curves of the sample under TMF loadings 
exhibit significant asymmetry compared to the sample under IF load -
ings. This is because TMF is non-isothermal fatigue, this asymmetry 
indicates that the cycling of temperature has significant effect on the 
mechanical properties of superalloys. The main reasons for the different 
mechanical properties of the sample under TMF loadings are the 
following two aspects: 
(1) IF is a form of fatigue with only mechanical load variation, while 
TMF is a form of fatigue with simultaneous variations of me -
chanical load and thermal load. Young ’ s modulus is a quantity 
independent of the mechanical load, but varies with temperature. 
In our previous work [57] , it had been found that Young ’ s 
modulus decreases slowly with the temperature increasing in the 
temperature range from 1 K to 1500 K. Therefore, Young ’ s 
modulus at the low-temperature half-cycle is higher than that of 
the high-temperature half-cycle, resulting in a higher stress 
occurring at the low-temperature half-cycle under the same strain 
amplitude. In IP-TMF test, the low-temperature half-cycle is in 
compression state, while in OP-TMF test, the low-temperature 
half-cycle is in tension state. Therefore, this results in a nega -
tive mean stress under IP-TMF loading and a positive mean stress 
under OP-TMF loading [21] . 
(2) Another reason is that the different dislocation motion forms in 
different half-cycles can lead to different mechanical properties 
[25] . And the different plastic deformations of γ’ precipitate 
phase of superalloys under IP-TMF and OP-TMF loadings also 
leads to the difference of mechanical properties of superalloys. In 
the next section, the microstructure evolution of superalloys 
under TMF loadings is analyzed in detail. 
In the four cyclic loading cases, the superalloys have the minimum 
cyclic stress range, stress – strain hysteresis loop area, and the lowest 
plastic strain energy density under IP-TMF loading. In contrast, the 
maximum cyclic stress range, stress – strain hysteresis loop area and the 
highest plastic strain energy density occur under OP-TMF loading. That 
is, Ni-based single crystal superalloys fail earlier under OP-TMF loading 
than other loading cases, and may have the worst mechanical properties. 
3.2. Microstructure evolution of superalloys under TMF loadings 
3.2.1. Dislocation analysis 
Fig. 7 shows the variations of dislocation density and the proportion 
of stacking faults (SFs) of samples under IF and TMF loadings. Here, the 
dislocation density is calculated by the same method as our previous 
work [39] , which is the total length of dislocations per unit volume in 
the whole sample. From Fig. 7 (a), it can be seen that the samples have a 
higher dislocation density at the initial stage both in IF and TMF tests. As 
the loading continues, dislocation density gradually decreases and en -
ters a dynamic equilibrium stage. In IF tests, in the initial stage, the 
sample has a higher initial dislocation density due to the activation of 
more slip systems at high temperatures [58] under IF-1200 K loading. As 
the cycle progresses, the sample enters a cyclic stable stage, and the 
dislocation density of the sample under IF-300 K loading is higher than 
that of the sample under IF-1200 K loading. This is because the occur -
rence of dislocation cross-slip at high temperatures leads to the 
neutralization of dislocations in different directions, thus resulting in a 
decrease in dislocation density [59] . This cross-slip mechanism of dis -
locations at high temperatures has been well demonstrated in our pre -
vious work [38] . Compared with the dislocation density under IF and 
TMF loadings, it can be seen that the dislocation density of the samples 
under TMF loadings has a strong fluctuation. From the enlarged view in 
Fig. 7 , it is found that whether in IP-TMF test or OP-TMF test, a higher 
dislocation density occurs in the high-temperature half-cycle in each 
cycle, which indicates that the high-temperature half-cycle is more 
conducive to the increase in dislocation density. This is because the 
dislocation proliferation and annihilation are influenced by the chang -
ing temperature under TMF loadings. In the high-temperature half- 
cycle, the higher temperature promotes the activation of more slip sys -
tems, leading to the increase in dislocation density [58] . While in the 
low-temperature half-cycle, it will cause the decrease in dislocation 
density. In TMF tests, due to the cyclic change of temperature, it is 
difficult to achieve a balance between dislocation proliferation and 
annihilation in a single cycle by cross-slip mechanism at high temper -
atures [38,59] , thus leading to the dislocation density fluctuates more 
drastically in each cycle when the superalloys under TMF loadings. 
Therefore, the dislocation density shows a dynamic equilibrium under 
TMF loadings. 
Fig. 7 (b) shows the variation of SFs, it can be seen that the pro -
portion of SFs under the four loading conditions shows dynamic equi -
librium during the whole loading process. The highest proportion of SFs 
occurs at the lowest temperature whether in IP-TMF test or OP-TMF test, 
which indicates that low temperature is more conducive to the sample 
generating SFs under TMF loadings.

4. Conclusions 
In this paper, the mechanical properties and microstructure evolu -
tion of Ni-based single crystal superalloys under TMF loadings are 
analyzed through MD simulation and compared with the fatigue 
behavior and microstructure characteristics of the superalloys under IF 
loadings, the main conclusions are summarized as follows: 
(1) In TMF tests, the superalloys generate a tensile mean stress under 
IP-TMF loading but a compressive mean stress under OP-TMF 
loading. And superalloys have a higher cyclic stress range, plas -
tic strain energy density, and a shorter fatigue life under OP-TMF 
loading than those under IP-TMF loading. 
(2) During TMF loadings, high temperature is more favorable to 
promote dislocation nucleation and emission, leading to an in -
crease in dislocation density. While low temperature is more 
favorable to the formation of SFs. This leads to a higher disloca -
tion density in the high-temperature half-cycle and more SFs in 
the low-temperature half-cycle. 
(3) The microstructure evolutions of superalloys under TMF loadings 
are different from those under IF loadings. In IF tests, the dislo -
cation motion forms are almost identical in the tension and 
compression half-cycles. While in TMF tests, In the high- 
temperature half-cycle, dislocations tend to form dislocation 
loops and concentrate in the γ matrix channels and the γ / γ
′
interface, while in the low-temperature half-cycle, dislocations 
and SFs tend to cut into the γ
′
precipitate phase through the form 
of extended dislocations. 
(4) Tensile strain is more favorable to promote dislocation and SFs 
cutting into the γ
′
precipitate phase, leading to more severe 
plastic deformation. And the dislocation shearing γ
′
precipitate 
phase mainly occurs in the low-temperature half-cycle. There -
fore, the superalloys will suffer the most severe damage in the 
low-temperature tension half-cycle, resulting in the shortest fa -
tigue life under OP-TMF loading compared with IP-TMF loading 
and IF loadings.
