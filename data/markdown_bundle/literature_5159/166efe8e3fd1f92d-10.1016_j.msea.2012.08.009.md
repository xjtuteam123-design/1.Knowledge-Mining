abstract
A Heusler Ni 50Mn30Ga1Cu4 alloy with the highest compressive strength has been obtained by
substituting 4 at% Cu for Ga in a ternary Ni 50Mn30Ga20 ferromagnetic shape memory alloy. It is shown
that the highest compressive strength of 3178.9 MPa with a fracture strain up to 13.6% can be achieved
at room temperature. To date, this is the highest compressive strength reported in the Ni–Mn–Ga alloy
system. The martensitic structure changes from 7M with 0 at% Cu to non-modulated T martensite with
4 at% Cu . In addition, the Cu doping markedly increases the martensitic transformation temperature
from 82.1 1C to 136.6 1C. At the same time, the Curie temperature and saturation magnetization for the
Ni
50Mn30Ga16Cu4 alloy slowly decrease due to the 4 at% Cu addition.

1. Introduction
During the past few years, considerable attention has been
devoted to the Ni–Mn–Ga alloy system due to its large magnetic-
ﬁeld-induced strain and high response frequency, which make it a
potential candidate material for new magnetic actuators [1–5].
Unfortunately, the brittleness, low strength and poor processability
of Ni–Mn–Ga alloys limit their application greatly. This has initiated
the search for new alloy compositions. Consequently, a lot of
attention is currently being paid on the search for alloys possessing
magnetoplastic behavior with increased transformation temperatures
and ductility. Recently, several rare earth elements [6–11]a n dF e
have been added to ternary Ni–Mn–Ga alloys to improve their
mechanical properties. Cu-alloying has been studied recently from
the viewpoint of magnetocaloric properties[12,13], with the greatest
magnetocaloric effect being found near room temperature. In parti-
cular, a 4.1% magnetic ﬁeld induced shear strain at 330 K was
achieved with a coupling of magnetic and structural transitions in
single crystalline Ni–Mn–Ga–Cu alloys for Cu¼4 at% by Glavatskyy
et al. [14]. In addition, a single-phase wide-hysteresis shape memory
alloy Ni50Mn25Ga17Cu8 with a shape memory ef fect of 6.2%, com-
pressive plasticity of 22%, and compressive strength of 878 MPa has
been reported by Wang and Jiang[15]. But the authors did not clarify
the ferromagnetic property of Ni
50Mn25Ga17Cu8 alloy at room tem-
perature. This is of technological and scientiﬁc interest, since, Cu
alloying may ﬁrstly increase ductility, giving better possibility for the
machining; and then it might reduce the cost of the actuators to be
built on the base of magnetic materials.
Thus, the current study concentrates on the search for an appro-
priate composition able to produce magnetic-ﬁeld-induced strain and
excellent mechanical properties with single-phase. In addition, little
information about the addition of Cu to Ni–Mn–Ga alloys on the
crystal structure and mechanical behavior is available to date.
Previously, we reported a high compressive strength of 1403 MPa
obtained in a polycrystalline Ni53Mn23.5Ga18.5Ti5 alloy aged at 873 K/
3h [16,17]. To date, this is the highest compressive strength reported
in the Ni–Mn–Ga alloy system. In this paper, the highest compressive
strength of 3178.9 MPa is obtained in the Ni50Mn30Ga16Cu4 alloy. At

3. Results and discussion
Fig. 1(a) and (b) illustrates the optical images of Ni 50Mn30Ga20
and Ni 50Mn30Ga16Cu4 alloys at room temperature. A single
martensitic phase is observed, which is characterized by typical
lamellar twin substructures. As shown in Fig. 1(a) and (b), straight
plate twinned martensitic variants are identical in both alloys. In
addition, it is found that the size of the grain decreases by Cu
doping. The martensitic variants exhibit conﬁgurations with self-
accommodation arrangement, when the Cu content increases to
4 at%, although there is no obvious change in the martensitic
variants.

The high phase transformation temperatures indicate that this
material may have great promise as a high temperature shape
memory alloy. The reason for the effect of Cu doping on marten-
sitic transformation has been explained in the following. Based on
the Hume–Rothery mechanism, the valence electron concentra-
tion is considered as an important parameter in inﬂuencing the
transformation temperatures. It has been reported that higher
electron concentration could increase the martensitic transforma-
tion temperatures. In the present work, for the Ni 50Mn30Ga16Cu4
alloy, the increase of the electron concentration by substituting
Cu (3d
104s1) for Ga (4s 24p1) results in the increase of the
transformation temperatures. The changes of enthalpy and
entropy increase with the increase of the phase transformation
temperatures. With doped Cu, the degree of order decreases,
leading to an increase in both the internal energy and the latent
heat released during phase transformation. It is seen that the
thermal hysteresis increases with increasing content of Cu in
Table 1. Wang and Jiang [15] reported that the thermal hysteresis
in the Ni–Mn–Ga ternary shape memory alloys originates from
the friction of phase boundary motion. Thus, it is speculated that
the Cu-doping increases the friction losses of phase boundary
motion in the Ni
50Mn30Ga16Cu4 alloy.
In order to gain a better insight into the mechanical properties,
compression tests were conducted at room temperature with
both Ni 50Mn30Ga20 and Ni 50Mn30Ga16Cu4 alloys. All the samples
were compressed to fracture. The result in Fig. 3 shows that the
Ni50Mn30Ga20 sample achieves a maximum compressive strength
of 258.22 MPa with a fracture strain of 5.05%. In comparison, the
mechanical improvement is remarkable due to the Cu-alloying. A
maximum compressive strength of 3178.9 MPa with a fracture
strain of 13.6% is obtained in the Ni 50Mn30Ga16Cu4 samples,
which is about 2900 MPa higher than that of the Ni 50Mn30Ga20
alloy. Therefore, the mechanical strength and the plasticity of the
single phase Ni
50Mn30Ga16Cu4 alloy are higher than those of
single-phase and dual-phase Ni–Mn–Ga alloys, and comparable
to those of the alloys with other element-alloying. It is well
known that Ni–Mn–Ga is too brittle. Especially, the polycrystal-
line grain boundary brittleness has been a bottleneck for Ni–Mn–
Ga. The r phase introduced by doping Ni, Fe or Co [19,20]
elements and rare earth elements was found to improve the
mechanical properties of Ni–Mn–Ga alloys. Here, Cu substituting
for Ga does not introduce a second phase; nevertheless this
substitution not only increases the yield strength but also
strengthens the grain boundaries, leading to the improvement
in plasticity of the Ni
50Mn30Ga16Cu4 alloy. Here, partial addition
of Cu effectively improves the compressive strength, which is
related to strengthening of grain boundaries. The fracture surface
of Ni–Mn–Ga alloys generally exhibits an intergranular crack
pattern. By Cu partially substituting for Ga, the intergranular
pattern may become weaker. On the other hand, it is known that
the mechanical property of the non-modulated T martensite is
better than that of the 5M and 7M martensites. Thus, it is
considered that the change of crystal structure also contributes
to the improved mechanical properties of the present alloys.
To clarify the fracture mechanism and investigate the nuclea-
tion and propagation of cracks in the Ni 50Mn30Ga20 and Ni50Mn30-
Ga16Cu4 alloys, SEM observations of the fracture surfaces were
performed, as shown in Fig. 4 . It can be seen that the fracture
surface of Ni 50Mn30Ga20 alloy exhibits typical brittle character-
istics with an intergranular pattern. The fracture surface of
Ni50Mn30Ga16Cu4 alloy exhibits characteristics of ductile fracture,
as evidenced by the formation of some tearing edges observed, as
shown in Fig. 4(b). A lot of tearing edges provide the evidence that
many courses were completed and a lot of energy was consumed
before fracture. The mechanism of improvement of ductility is
partly provided by the Cu-alloying due to strengthening of the
intergranular and intracrystalline structures in the Ni 50Mn30-
Ga16Cu4 alloy. In addition, when deformed, the microcracks
generate and propagate along the intergranular boundaries. At
the same time, the effects will certainly increase the energy for
the crack propagation, thus giving rise to the increase of ductility.

Fig. 5 (a) shows the Curie temperature dependence for

interesting subject for further studies. Fig. 5 (b) shows the satura-

butable to the decrease of Ga content. In the Ni–Mn–Ga alloy, the
magnetic coupling of Mn–Mn is completed through the itinerant
electron of Ni and Ga. With increasing content of Cu, the decrease
of the Ni content in the matrix causes the decrease of the
conduction electron-reduced pairs of Mn atoms, weakening the
exchange effect of Mn–Mn atoms, resulting in the decrease of
saturation magnetization.

4. Conclusions
In conclusion, the microstructure, martensitic transformation
and mechanical properties of Ni 50Mn30Ga20 and Ni50Mn30Ga16Cu4
alloys were investigated. The applied alloying had a strong effect
on the transition temperatures, compressive strength and mag-
netic properties. The following results were obtained.
The microstructure of the Ni
50Mn30Ga20 alloy is notably
altered by the addition of Cu. The addition of Cu results in a
change in the martensitic structure. At room temperature, a
seven-layered martensite is found in the Ni 50Mn30Ga20 alloy,
whereas the Ni 50Mn30Ga16Cu4 alloy exhibits a non-modulated
tetragonal T martensitic structure. One-step thermoelastic mar-
tensitic transformation occurs in this quaternary alloy. Cu-
alloying has a signiﬁcant inﬂuence on martensitic transformation
temperatures in Ni 50Mn30Ga20 alloy. It can be seen that the
martensitic and reverse martensitic transformation temperatures
of the Ni 50Mn30Ga16Cu4 alloy increase compared to that of the
Ni50Mn30Ga20 alloy. It is found that Cu addition can signiﬁcantly
enhance the compressive strength and improve the ductility of
Ni50Mn30Ga20 alloy. A maximum compressive strength of
3178.9 MPa with a fracture strain of 13.6% is obtained in the
Ni50Mn30Ga16Cu4 samples, which is about 2920.7 MPa higher
than that of the Ni 50Mn30Ga20 alloy. The highest compressive
strength in the Ni–Mn–Ga alloy system to date was reported
compared with that of the alloys with other element-alloying. The
Curie temperature and saturation magnetization for the
Ni50Mn30Ga16Cu4 alloy slowly decrease due to 4 at% Cu addition.
