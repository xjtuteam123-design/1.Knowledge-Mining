of Al-Si cast alloys

Keywords: Al-Si cast alloy, Nickel, high-temperature strength, connectivity, contiguity. 
Abstract. In order to investigate the effect of Ni on the hi gh-temperature strength of Al-Si cast 
alloys, tensile properties of hypoeutectic and eute ctic alloys were determined at 250 °C after long-
term annealing at test temperature. LOM- and SEM-analysis revealed the existence of Al 3Ni-phases 
in close contact to eutectic Si. It was shown that the strength can be increased by the addition of Ni , 
though just to a certain level, depending on the fr action of eutectic phase in the alloy. The alloys 
were considered as a coarse two-phase system where a hardening effect is caused by load transfer to 
the harder phase, which requires a certain connecti vity/contiguity of the latter. The paper describes 
the extent of contiguity of the eutectic as well as the hard silicon and Al 3Ni-phases within the 
eutectic, and discusses their contribution to an en hanced strength of Al-Si alloys at elevated 
temperatures. 
Introduction 
As weight reduction and the request to reduce exhau st emissions have become a key issue for 
automotive manufacturers, there is an increasing de mand for Al-Si cast alloys with better 
performance concerning yield and tensile strength a t elevated temperatures up to 250 °C [1, 2]. In 
previous work Ni was identified to significantly enhance the high-temperature performance of Al-Si 
piston alloys [1-3]. Since the prize of Ni (28.000 USD/t; February 2011) is nearly three times higher 
than that of Cu, both the alloy producers and the c ustomers in the automotive industry are 
economically interested to keep the Ni concentratio ns as low as possible without cutting back the 
materials’ high-temperature performance. 
The intention of the present paper was to verify i) the ability of Ni to improve the elevated-
temperature strength of Al-Si cast alloys, ii) to analyze the operating strengthening mechanisms and 
iii) to evaluate the required Ni content. 
Experimental 
Four alloys based on the system AlSi7 and three eut ectic alloys (12% Si) with varying Ni 
concentration were fabricated by the AMAG testing l aboratory. The composition of the samples is 
shown in Table 1. All materials were melted in a 10 0 kg induction furnace and cast into a steel 
mould to gain tensile test bars. The mould was preh eated to a temperature of 320 
 5 °C and 
coated before casting using boron nitride. Melt tem perature was held constant at 750 5 °C over 
the whole casting process. The alloys were solution treated at 495 °C for 8 h and subsequently 
quenched into water at room temperature.

Results 
Tensile Testing. Fig. 1a shows the results of tensile tests carried out at 250 °C on the alloys of 
group I (7% Si). By adding 1% Ni to AlSi7, the yiel d strength is enhanced by approximately 
30 MPa (+70%). An extra 0.5% Ni has no further considerable effect on the strength of the samples. 
The mechanical properties of the alloys in group II (12% Si) are shown in Fig. 1b. Like in AlSi7 
alloys the yield and tensile strength of AlSi12 are significantly increased by adding Ni. However, in 
contrast to AlSi7 an increase of the Ni content to values >1 wt% leads to further increase of the 
yield and tensile strength. While Fig. 1 represents the values for ‘technical yield strength’ at 0.2% 
plastic deformation, yield strength for strains of 0.01% and 0.05% was supplementary determined 
by analyzing the particular stress-strain curves (d ata not shown here). A hardening effect of Ni is 
already visible at very low plastic strains.

Microstructure Analysis. Using LOM and SEM various phases could be identifie d in the 
alloys, namely eutectic Si, Ni-aluminides (Al 3Ni) and Fe-containing intermetallics (see Fig. 2). 
With increasing Ni content the volume fraction of t hese phases increases. Since the content of 
eutectic Si and Fe-containing phases is independent of the Ni concentration the increase in volume 
fraction can be attributed to the formation of Al 3Ni. 
Discussion 
The Al-Si system is of type ‘simple eutectic’, for which the amount of α-solid solution in the 
solidified material can be evaluated to be 54% (all oys of Group I) and 9% (alloys of Group II), 
respectively (for example see insert in Fig. 3 for alloy 3). When Ni is added to the Al-Si system the 
eutectic transformation is characterized by a simul taneous formation of eutectic Si and Al 3Ni, as 
illustrated in Fig. 3. Consequently, eutectic Si an d Al 3Ni form a geometrically entangled system, as 
can be clearly seen in Fig. 2.

Considering the whole microstructure as a coarse tw o-phase system, with the phases ‘primary 
Al solid solution (α prim )’ and ‘eutectic (E)’ and assuming E to be the stronger/harder phase, the latter 
will significantly contribute to the overall streng th only if it is subjected to load transfer. Load 
transfer, however, requires a continuous network of the respective phase [5]. Such contiguity of the 
harder phase E undoubtely exists for the microstructures of the alloys investigated. 
The eutectic phase E consists of more or less soft ‘eutectic Al (α E)’ and hard ‘eutectic Si and 
Al 3Ni’. Therefore, E can be treated as a composite wit h the same requirements as discussed above 
for the α prim + E-compound. In order to form a strong/hard E-pha se the hard ‘eutectic Si and Al 3Ni’ 
needs to be 3-dimensionally connected. 
During a solution treatment the eutectic Si platele ts spheroidize and their aspect ratio decreases, 
which might result in a loss of interconnectivity o f the eutectic phases [3, 6]. However, in the 
presence of Ni-aluminides the loss of interconnecti vity of eutectic Si is significantly reduced. The 
Ni-phases in combination with eutectic Si form a st rongly interconnected 3D-structure, whose 
contiguity is highly preserved even after a solutio n treatment for 24 h [3]. It is further observed th at 
Ni additions improve not only the 0.2% yield streng th but also the 0.01% flow stress (data not 
shown here). Therefore, work hardening caused by th e formation of geometrically-necessary 
dislocations cannot explain the hardening effect ca used by the Ni-addition [7]. The hardening 
contribution can only be related to the enhanced lo ad transfer due to the increased contiguity of the 
hard phases ‘eutectic Si and Al 3Ni’. 
Contiguity of the Si-Phase within the Eutectic Phase E. According to Uggowitzer et al. [8] the 
contiguity (C R) of a rigid phase (R) in a soft matrix (M) is depending on its volume fraction V R. The 
authors propose a minimal volume fraction V R
L1 , beneath that contiguity is not given any more 
(C R ≈ 0). At a volume fraction of V R
L2 contiguity is fully maintained and increasing V R has only 
little further influence on the connectivity of the material. V R
L1 depends strongly on the shape and 
distribution of the hard phases, e.g. the eutectic Si and Al 3Ni. 
The amount of Si in the eutectic is constant with 1 1.7%, which seems enough to achieve 
contiguity in the as-cast condition. However, spher oidization in the course of a heat-treatment 
changes the shape of the Si-platelets from acicular to round, whereby V R
L1 as a minimum volume 
fraction to achieve contiguity is switched towards higher values. In the presence of Al 3Ni however, 
two features are of importance: i) the volume conte nt of rigid phases increases, and ii) the 
spheroidization of eutectic Si is probably reduced. Thus the system is shifted towards higher 
contiguity. 
Depending on the amount of eutectic phase, more or less Ni is required to restore the 
connectivity of the eutectic Si-network. Pandat cal culations [4] show that the amount of Al 3Ni in 
the eutectic is linearly increased with increasing Ni concentration. However, the absolute values 
differ strongly with changing eutectic fraction. 1% Ni in AlSi7 equates an Al 3Ni fraction of 4.04%, 
whereas the same concentration of Ni in AlSi12 gene rates an aluminide fraction of 2.07%. Adding 
2% Ni to AlSi12 results in an Al 3Ni fraction of 4.17% within the eutectic phase. 
Turning towards the idea of an interconnected netwo rk of rigid phases the values above have to 
be added to the 11.7% Si in the eutectic phase, which results in above V R.

Contiguity of the Eutectic Phase E. The minimum Si concentration to achieve a continuou s 
network of the eutectic phase in as-cast materials is supposed to lie in the range of 7 % [3, 6]. 
However, when AlSi7 and AlSi12 are compared yield a nd tensile strength are significantly higher 
for the latter alloy. This is not because of differ ent grades of contiguity but varying volume 
fractions of eutectic material. Uggowitzer et al. [8] presented a modified version of the linear rule of 
mixture for metal matrix composites. The authors de fined a volume fracture of contiguity carrying 
the stress σRC , which leads to the formula

with σ M as effective yield stress of the soft matrix and σ RC as effective stress of the contiguity

volume fraction of eutectic phase in the alloy, whi ch affects strength. In AlSi12 the latter is nearly 
two times higher than in AlSi7 (91% and 46%, respec tively), which explains why the level of 
strength of AlSi12 alloys lies significantly above the hypo-eutectic material. 
Summary 
Ni can enhance the high-temperature performance of Al-Si foundry alloys by stabilizing the 
contiguity of the eutectic network. This happens by increasing the volume fraction of rigid phases 
VR in the eutectic phase. Exceeding a certain limit o f volume fraction (V R
L2 ) does not result in a 
further improvement of interconnectivity and strengthening stagnates. 
In the case of AlSi7 only 1 wt% Ni seems to be enou gh to restore the eutectic’s contiguity, 
whereas in AlSi12 a linear increase of strength up to a Ni content of 2% can be observed. 
At a given value for contiguity C R, depending on annealing time and temperature, it i s the volume 
fracture of eutectic in the alloy, which affects st rength. Therefore the level of strength of eutectic 
AlSi12 lies significantly above that of the hypoeutectic AlSi7 alloy.
