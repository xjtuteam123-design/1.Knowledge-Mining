Effects of P and Nb on mechanical properties
of GH4169 alloy
J.-H. Du, X.-D. Lu *, C. Tang and Z. N. Bi
GH4169 alloy has good corrosion resistance, high mechanical properties and excellent
weldability. It is employed in gas turbines, turbine blades and in extrusion dies and containers.
The development of advanced aero-engine design demand requires that the hot components
have higher strength and longer service life. Many researches try to improve design of
composition of GH4169 alloy. The effects of different P (0 ?006–0?019%), Nb (5 ?2%–5?6%) content
on the alloy phase precipitation patterns and distribution state were studied systematically in this
paper. Room temperature and 650 uC tensile, stress rupture at 650 uC/690 MPa were tested. The
effect mechanism of Nb and P was also discussed through structure observation, fractograph
analysis and thermodynamic calculation. The results show that as Nb content increases from 5 ?2
to 5 ?4%, the ultimate tensile strength ( s
b) and yield strength ( s0?2) of direct aging (DA) samples
increase at room temperature and 650 uC, whereas the strength decreases as Nb content from
5?4t o5 ?6%. Increase rate of strength for standard heat treatment (SHT) samples is also slower
than DA samples but without a descending tendency. The increase in P level (0 ?006–0?019%)
improves the stress rupture life of both DA and SHT samples at 650 uC/690MPa significantly.
Keywords: GH4169 alloy, Tensile, Stress rupture, Microstructure, Thermodynamic
Introduction
GH4169 superalloy has good corrosion resistance, high
mechanical properties and excellent weldability. It is
employed in gas turbines, turbine blades, and in
extrusion dies and containers.
1–4 GH4169 is a precipita-
tion hardenable alloy, containing signiﬁcant contents of
Fe, Nb and Mo. Minor contents of Al and Ti also
present. Ni and Cr improve the corrosion resistance of
this material. They crystallise as c phase (face centred
cubic). Nb is added to form hardening precipitates c0
(a metastable intermetallic compound Ni
3Nb, centred
tetragonal crystal). Al and Ti are added to accelerate the
formation of intermetallic c’ [Ni
3(Al,Ti), simple cubic
crystal]. The hardening effect of the c’ phase is lower
than the c0 phase. C is also added to accelerate the
precipitation of MC carbides (M5Ti or Nb). In this case
the content of C must be low enough to allow Nb and Ti
to precipitate in the form of c0 and c’ particles. Mo is
also frequent in GH4169 superalloy in order to increase
the mechanical resistance by solid solution hardening. In
addition, d phase (intermetallic Ni
3Nb) can also appear.
It is an equilibrium particle with orthorhombic struc-
ture.
5–9 The d phase can precipitate along the grain
boundaries of the c matrix and increase the intergra-
nular ﬂow resistance of the present alloy. 10–14
In this paper, effect of different Nb (5 ?2–5?6%) and P
(0?006–0?019%) content on the room temperature and
650uC tensile, stress rupture at 650 uC/690 MPa proper-
ties was investigated as the direct aging (DA) and
standard heat treatment (SHT) samples. Results show
that the strength of SHT samples increases from 5 ?2
to 5 ?6%, strength of DA samples ﬁrst increases and
then decreases. The increase in P level (0 ?006–0?019%)
improves the stress rupture life of both DA and SHT
samples at 650 uC/690MPa signiﬁcantly.
Experimental methods
The ingots were vacuum induction melted with each
weight of 50 kg. The chemistry of these blanks is
listed in Tables 1 and 2. Nb content is in the range of
5?2t o5 ?6 (wt-%) and is the only apparent variable
among compositions. P content is in the range of 0 ?006
to 0 ?019% and is the only apparent variable among
compositions.
All test alloys were homogenised and hot rolled to
18 mm diameter bars. Specimen blanks for tensile and
stress rupture tests were cut from the bars and then given
the following different heat treatment. One method is
SHT: solutionised at 960 uC for 1 h, air cooled and then
aged for 8 h at 720 uC, furnace cooled to 620 uC, held at
620uC for 8 h, air cooled. The other method is DA heat
treatment: aged for 8 h at 720 uC, furnace cooled to
620uC, held at 620 uC for 8 h, air cooled.
These test samples with different heat treatments were
then electrolytically etched in 80 mL HCl z13 mL

Delta phase (Ni 3Nb) is not strengthening phase of
alloy, but has an important role in improving the grain
boundary strength. The uniform distribution of the
spherical and rod-like d phase helps to reﬁne the grain,
and needle-like d phase will affect the persistence
properties and fracture toughness of alloys. Delta phase
precipitation of grain boundary in the appropriate will
consume part of the c0 phase forming elements (Nb),
reduce the strength of the material, while release the
stress concentration around grain boundaries, improve
the plasticity and toughness of materials, but the pre-
cipitate excessive or needle-like precipitate will on plastic
adverse.
15–17 d phase and c0 are rich Nb phase, therefore
the Nb content may change precipitation behaviour of the
both phases. Effect of Nb content on mechanical proper-
ties is achieved by the inﬂuence of precipitation behavior
for second phases.

exists in stress rupture properties, as shown in Fig. 4. As
a whole, as DA samples have a higher stress rupture life

samples is far more susceptible to P level than SHT

0?019% P, which is over three times more than the

Other researchers consider the effect of P may come
from its direct action to grain boundary.
18,19 The seg-
regation of P to grain boundary increases the grain
boundary cohesion by impeding dislocation motion,
resulting in the improvement of stress rupture life. As
P-dislocation interaction increases, stress rupture life rises
as well. Considering the similar size of original grains, the
microvoid may be formed by creep at grain boundaries.
Hence, increase in P content could signify higher grain
boundary cohesion and longer stress rupture life.
Under the same test conditions, stress rupture life of
DA samples, as a whole, has a higher value than SHT
samples. This is mainly determined by the differences of
strength and morphology. Moreover, the stress rupture
life of DA alloys without d phase grows more steeply
and faster than SHT alloys with d phase. It means that
d phase may affect the mechanism of P action. Viskari
and Stiller
20 observed alloy 718 (i.e. GH4169) and
Allvac 718Plus in atomic scale with atom probe tomo-
graphy, and revealed that P segregated at c/c grain
boundaries of the DA samples and c/d phase boundaries
of the SHT samples. In fact, the view that adding P will
change the morphology of d phase also proves the
enrichment of P at c/d phase boundaries.15,16
Thus, it can be considered that the P to some extent
enriches ond phase interface of SHT samples, the quantity
of P segregating at grain boundaries will decrease accord-
ingly. However, P gathers primarily at grain boundaries in
DA samples. In other words, when compared in the same P
content, P of DA samples has more effective than SHT
samples, resulting in a more obvious increase in stress
rupture life. This opinion agrees well with that adding
proper P will enhance grain boundary cohesion.
Conclusions
To sum up, the tensile strength of GH4169 alloy can be
effectively improved by Nb, P alloying, and the stress
rupture life of the alloy can be obviously improved.
Conclusions are as follows.
1. At room temperature and 650 uC tensile test
condition, sb and s0?2 of DA samples of GH4169 alloy
increase with the increasing Nb content from 5 ?2t o
5?4%, but decrease when the Nb content reach to 5 ?6%.
The strength of SHT samples increases with the in-
creasing Nb content from 5 ?2t o5 ?6%.
2. Adding P (0 ?006–0?019%) improves stress rupture
life of GH4169 alloy with both DA and SHT samples at
650uC/690 MPa significantly. As P content increases,
growth rate of stress rupture life for DA samples is
higher than SHT samples. This may be related to the
enrichment of P at d phase interfaces.
