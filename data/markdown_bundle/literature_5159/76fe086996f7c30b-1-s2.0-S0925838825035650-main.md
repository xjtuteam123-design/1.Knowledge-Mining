ABSTRACT
This study presents a non-destructive methodology for predicting the yield strength of refractory high-entropy 
alloys (RHEAs) through measurements of electrical resistivity ( ρ ) and its temperature coefficient (TCR). This 
study systematically analyzed a series of RHEAs (W, WTa, WTaV, WTaVTi, WTaVTiCr) with maximized solid 
solution strengthening to evaluate how the characteristics of individual alloying elements influence the me -
chanical properties of the material. As solid solution strengthening is strongly correlated with lattice distortion, 
which simultaneously influences the electrical properties of RHEAs, the relationship between TCR and yield 
strength ( σ
y
) was also investigated. A linear correlation was identified between 1/TCR and the σ
y 
of RHEAs, 
demonstrating the feasibility of predicting mechanical strength based on TCR measurements. This methodology 
provides a practical, non-invasive tool for material scientists, enabling the prediction of strength without 
destructive testing. The findings highlight TCR ’ s potential as a robust indicator of mechanical strength and offer 
valuable guidelines for the development and application of RHEAs in structural materials. Furthermore, this 
study underscores the importance of integrating theoretical and experimental approaches to advance material 
design and evaluation.

However, it has also been reported that in cases with complex lattice 
distortion, the solid solution strengthening effect cannot be accurately 
understood merely by theoretical differences in metallic radius [15] . For 
example, despite having nearly identical theoretical metallic radii 
among constituent elements, FCC HEAs exhibit substantial solid solution 
strengthening due to differences in electronegativity ( χ ), which deter -
mine the effective atomic size rather than metallic radius. Thus, for FCC 
HEAs with minimal atomic radius differences, a model considering Δ χ 
might predict solid solution strengthening more accurately. However, 
the atomic volume difference induced by atomic-level pressure remains 
negligible (within 5 %) in RHEAs, which this study focuses on. Even 
considering this, RHEAs are anticipated to have more than twice the 
solid solution strengthening effect compared to FCC HEAs, explaining 
their high strength from a similar perspective [13] . (The difference in χ 
affecting the effective atomic radius and its relation to strength is 
compared in Supplementary Figure S1 .)
Despite the significant solid solution strengthening in HEAs, the 
actual degree varies depending on the constituent elements, composi -
tion ratios, and other variables. Given the vast diversity in HEA com -
positions, verifying each combination through trial and error is 
impractical. Therefore, establishing a non-destructive method to predict 
strength (or solid solution strengthening effects) offers significant sav -
ings in both time and cost. Various methods to predict σ
y 
based on 
composition have been proposed for conventional alloys, often using 
indirect methods such as hardness tests [16 – 19] . Similar efforts are 
underway for HEAs, with recent advancements in data-driven regression 
techniques aiming to predict strength using big data [20 – 22] . Recent 
developments in material property prediction methods now allow 
measurement of atomic-level defects, with techniques like electrical 
resistivity ( ρ ), which is significantly influenced by lattice distortion, 
being developed for prediction. Particularly, voids at the macro scale 
affect both ρ and strength, enabling the use of parameters related to ρ to 
predict the strength of materials like rock and concrete [23] . Addition -
ally, studies on changes in ρ with temperature (TCR, Temperature Co -
efficient of Resistivity) concerning lattice distortion are underway. TCR 
is highly sensitive to minimal doping or lattice defects, making it widely 
used in temperature sensors [24] . A recent study by Tanimoto et al. 
reported measuring Short range order formation based on TCR differ -
ences in HEAs [25] , demonstrating that atomic-level defects without 
interfaces can be predicted through changes in ρ .
This study aims to propose a model for predicting the σ
y 
of RHEAs by 
utilizing TCR, a parameter closely correlated with lattice distortion (or 
solid solution strengthening). To this end, we utilized a series of RHEAs 
with varying degrees of lattice distortion to measure their σ
y 
and 
correlate it with the TCR of each alloy. Notably, variables that could 
influence strength beyond solid solution strengthening — such as grain 
size — were carefully controlled. This allowed us to demonstrate that σ
y 
could be reliably predicted by simply measuring TCR of different 
RHEAs.
In details, we measured the ρ of the materials at various tempera -
tures, expressing the response as TCR to estimate the extent of lattice 
distortion. Since electrical conduction in metals relates to the mean free 
path of free electrons to atomic jumps, TCR can serve as a measure of 
lattice distortion, closely related to solid solution strengthening effect in 
HEAs. This study systematically analyzed the equiatomic RHEA series 
developed by Kim et al., including W (unary), WTa (binary), WTaTi 
(ternary), WTaTiV (quaternary), and WTaTiVCr (quinary) [26] . This 
RHEA series, designed to maximize lattice distortion by incorporating 
elements with large atomic number differences, serves as a suitable 
model system for this analysis. This approach highlights the potential of 
using TCR alone to predict σ
y 
in HEA systems.
2. Results and discussion
2.1. Alloy design of RHEA series with high solid solution strengthening 
effect
To systematically measure the variation in strength due to solid so -
lution strengthening, we utilized a RHEA series designed to maximize 
lattice distortion. Initially, we alloyed pure W with various alloying el -
ements to form RHEAs including up to five elements. W was chosen as 
the base element for these RHEAs due to its thermodynamic stability in 
the BCC structure and its exceptionally low ρ , surpassed only by Cu, Au, 
and Ag among metallic materials. These properties make W an ideal 
model system for this study, as its resistance is highly sensitive to the 
addition of alloying elements [27] . Additional alloying elements were 
selected from transition metals in Periods IV to VI and Groups IV to VI, 
which readily form single-phase BCC RHEAs when alloyed with W. El -
ements with widely varying atomic numbers were strategically chosen 
to enhance the solid solution strengthening effect. As a result, the RHEA 
series developed in this study comprises five equiatomic compositions: 
unary W, WTa (binary), WTaTi (ternary), WTaTiV (quaternary), and 
WTaTiVCr (quinary).

more intricate thermodynamic interactions among their constituent el -

2.4. Correlation between the yield strength and TCR of RHEAs
As discussed above, a decrease in TCR with an increasing number of 
alloying elements is a well-recognized trend in metallic materials. This 
effect is particularly pronounced in HEAs due to their complex lattice 
structures, which typically result in lower TCR as well as higher ρ . In 
dilute alloys, significant changes in ρ with temperature arise from 
enhanced electron-phonon coupling, caused by increased lattice vibra -
tions, resulting in relatively large TCR values [48] . In contrast, in HEAs, 
the lattice is already significantly distorted by high solute contents, 
resulting in extremely low electron conduction even at room tempera -
ture. Consequently, the change in ρ , represented by TCR, also becomes 
minimal. In other words, electron jumps are restricted even at low 
temperatures due to the pre-existing lattice distortion in HEAs with high 
lattice distortion. Therefore, the λ does not change significantly, 
resulting in minimal changes in ρ even as temperature increases. Thus, 
lattice distortion not only increases ρ by reducing λ but also contributes 
to a decrease in TCR. As illustrated by the colored spheres in Fig. 5 , 
increasing the number of alloying elements results in higher ρ and a 
corresponding decrease in TCR, reflecting an inverse relationship.
Moreover, the increase in ρ with changing constituent elements not 
only reflects changes in electrical properties but can also be associated 
with an overall increase in lattice distortion, which directly relates to 
solid solution strengthening. A study by Zhao et al. has demonstrated a 
proportional relationship between lattice distortion and normalized in -
ternal stress, a relationship observed in both Ni-based FCC HEAs and Nb- 
based BCC HEAs [42,49] . As mentioned above, TCR can serve as an 
indicator for predicting the solid solution strengthening of alloys, as 
strength increases with lattice distortion while TCR decreases, indi -
cating an inverse relationship between the two variables. Consequently,

3. Conclusion
This study successfully developed and analyzed a series of RHEAs 
exhibiting significant solid solution strengthening effects. Additionally, 
a predictive methodology was introduced for estimating the σ
y 
of 
WTaVTiCr RHEAs through TCR measurements. By integrating experi -
mental data with insights from existing literature, this work highlights 
the practicality of non-destructive techniques for evaluating RHEAs, 
offering substantial time and cost savings. The key findings are as 
follows:
(1) A series of RHEAs with maximized lattice distortion was designed 
by alloying W with Ta, V, Ti, and Cr. To precisely analyze the rela -
tionship between lattice distortion and solid solution strengthening, 
other factors such as precipitation hardening and grain refinement were 
intentionally excluded. This approach confirmed a strong linear corre -
lation between the M-factor, derived from compositional data, and the 
material ’ s σ
y
.
(2) The electrical properties of the RHEAs, including ρ and TCR, were 
systematically measured. The inverse relationship between ρ and TCR, 
observed in pure elements, was also confirmed in the alloy systems. This 
result highlights the strong correlation between TCR and lattice distor -
tion, demonstrating that TCR alone can reliably predict the degree of 
lattice distortion in alloys.
(3) It was demonstrated that 1/TCR is directly proportional to the M- 
factor, representing solid solution strengthening, and ultimately to the 
material ’ s σ
y
. This proportionality was consistent across different alloy 
systems and crystal structures, including FCC and BCC. For the RHEAs in 
this study, σ y was estimated using the equation 0.49/TCR + 836, while 
FCC alloys (e.g., CrMnFeCoNi) exhibited a lower slope of 0.23/TCR 
+ 68. These results emphasize the intrinsic advantages of BCC struc -
tures, including higher baseline strength and greater sensitivity to lattice 
distortion-induced strengthening.
(4) This study redefines TCR as a dual-purpose property that con -
nects electrical behavior with mechanical performance. The proposed 
methodology provides a versatile, non-destructive tool for assessing the 
mechanical properties of RHEAs, paving the way for their broader 
adoption in structural applications. These findings advance predictive 
modeling in alloy development, streamlining the design of high- 
performance materials for engineering applications.
Experimental
RHEA ingot fabrication
The RHEA ingots were fabricated through the arc-melting technique, 
utilizing high purity elements with a purity level of 99.999 %. The arc- 
melting process was performed under a Ti-gettered Ar atmosphere. It is 
worth noting that the boiling points of Ti (3560 K), V (3680 K), and Cr 
(2944 K) are comparatively lower than the melting point of W (3683 K). 
To address this, a mixture of Ta and W was initially prepared to decrease 
the overall melting point. Subsequently, V, Ti, and Cr were sequentially 
incorporated into the W-Ta alloys to minimize concentration errors 
resulting from the evaporation of these elements. To ensure a homoge -
neous distribution of the constituent elements, the buttons obtained 
from the initial melting process were remelted more than five times and 
flipped during each melting cycle. The final ingots were then rapidly 
solidified on the water-quenched copper hearth.

Thermodynamic evaluation
The characteristic temperatures and thermodynamic stability of each 
alloy were assessed based on Calphad simulation. Particularly, pseudo- 
binary phase diagrams were generated using Thermo-calc software 
(TCHEA 3 database) to provide insights into the alloy ’ s behavior and 
composition.
Microstructural evaluation
To investigate the phases and microstructures of the as-cast alloys, 
various microstructural evaluation techniques were utilized. For OM 
analysis, the specimens were mounted in an epoxy resin, followed by 
grinding with a series of silicon carbide (SiC) papers up to 2000 grit. 
Final polishing was performed using a 6, 1 and 0.05 μ m diamond sus -
pension to obtain a mirror-like surface. The microstructures were 
observed using a Nikon Eclipse LV150 optical microscope.
The crystal structures of the alloys were confirmed by XRD analysis 
using a Bruker D8 Advance diffractometer. To ensure accurate peak 
positions for lattice parameter calculation, samples were mounted on a 
precisely height-adjusted holder to minimize sample displacement error. 
For the analysis, the specimen was rotated at 15 rpm and scanned with 
an exposure time of 1 s per 0.015
◦
step. The collected XRD raw data 
were then processed to computationally remove the contribution from 
the Cu K α ₂ wavelength, yielding accurate and symmetric peak profiles.
Quantitative chemical composition and elemental distribution of the 
alloys were evaluated using a Field-Emission Electron Probe Micro- 
Analyzer (FE-EPMA; JXA-8530F, JEOL). The analysis was conducted 
under conditions of a 15 kV accelerating voltage and a 50 nA probe 
current.
Compressive test
Compression tests were conducted on fabricated alloys using an 
Instron 5967 universal mechanical testing machine. The tests were 
carried out at room temperature, applying a strain rate of 10
 3

 1
. The 
testing process was terminated once the compressive elongation excee -

× 2 mm × 4 mm, were prepared by cutting from the ingots using an 
electrical discharge machine (SPW-10C, SEEPEL). Prior to testing, the 
specimens were meticulously polished to eliminate any oxidized surface, 
ensuring accurate results. To establish reproducibility, a minimum of 
three samples for each material were subjected to compressive tests.
Electrical property measurements
The electrical properties of the alloys were evaluated using a stan -
dard four-point probe method. Measurements were performed within a 
commercial physical property measurement system (PPMS, Quantum 
Design) across a temperature range of 150 – 350 K. This range was 
selected to assess the alloys ’ electrical behavior under varying thermal 
conditions.
