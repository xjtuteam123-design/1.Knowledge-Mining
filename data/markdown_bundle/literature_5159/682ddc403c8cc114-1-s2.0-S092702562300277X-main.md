ABSTRACT 
Data-driven research mode plays an increasingly important role in scientific research. In this study, a dimen -
sionality reduction strategy coupling with physical metallurgy models and CALPHAD method was proposed to 
established a machine learning model for Ni-based single crystal creep life prediction. SHAP analysis was applied 
to explain the internal mechanisms and the final results of the model. The results showed that the model was of 
good prediction accuracy and its prediction results could be reasonably explained. Thus, the model can be 
applied to predict the creep lives of engineering-applied superalloys and to search for the relationship between 
microstructures and creep lives of superalloys, which is expected to be applied to the design of new alloy. 
1. Introduction 
Nickel-based single crystal superalloys are essential materials for 
aircraft engines and industrial gas turbines because of their excellent 
high-temperature properties including yield strength, oxidation and 
corrosion resistance, fatigue and creep properties [1] . For Ni-based 
single crystal superalloys, creep life is an important indicator to 
appraise the creep property. However, superalloys are complex alloy 
systems containing a variety of elements, and the relationship between 
alloy compositions and creep life has not been clear. Thus, predicting 
creep life of Ni-based single crystal superalloys remains a challenging 
work. Over the past few decades, a few methods had been developed to 
predict creep life including iso-thermal/iso-stress extrapolation methods 
[2,3] , time-temperature parameter methods [4] and Larson-Miller 
parameter method [5,6] . These methods are some kinds of empirical 
formulas summarized by experts with some uncertain constants needed 
to be confirmed via a few experimental data. These methods can predict 
creep lives of superalloys to some extent, but the correlation between 
creep life and creep characteristics is hard to be described by these 
formulas comprehensively, which results in undesirable prediction 
accuracy. 
With the development of computer science and artificial intelligence, 
data-driven research mode gradually becomes a new way to solve the 
problems that are insolvable in traditional methods [7 – 9] . Machine 
learning (ML), a widely applied artificial intelligence method, has been 
used to predict the creep lives of alloys. Liu et al. [10] established a 
divide-and-conquer self-adaptive ML model for Ni-based single crystal 
superalloys creep life prediction. Within the model, the result of unsu -
pervised learning corresponded to some known or unknown creep 
mechanisms. The model error which showed with coefficient of deter -
mination (R
2
), root-mean-square error (RMSE), and mean-absolute- 
percentage error (MAPE) as 0.9176, 0.3839 and 0.0003 was accept -
able. Han et al. [11] proposed a two-model linkage machine learning 
method to predict superalloy creep life. It fitted the experimental results 
well and successfully analyzed the interaction of elements. As can be 
seen that ML methods have been used in prediction of creep life of 
superalloys. 
At present, ML methods have been applied in the field of superalloys. 
However, the internal mechanism of ML model is difficult to be 
explained clearly. For a simple ML model (such as linear regression), 
when given an output of it, the contribution of each input feature can be 
accurately determined. However, as the model becomes more complex, 
it will become more difficult to figure out the contribution of input 
features. Therefore, most machine learning methods are “ black boxes ” , 
from which we can get accurate predictions, but cannot clearly explain 
the mechanisms of these predictions. This leads to doubts about the 
rationality and reliability of the prediction results, which limits the 
application of ML. In recent years, there is a new concept called IML

(Interpretable machine learning) in the field of machine learning [12] . It 
studies the causal relationship between the input features and the output 
results of the ML model. Developed by Lundberg and Lee in 2017 [13] , 
SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) analysis is a method to explain 
predictions of ML models. SHAP analysis constructs an additive inter -
pretation model based on the game theoretically optimal Shapley values 
and all the features are regarded as contributors. It calculates the SHAP 
values of the features in each sample, which represents the contribution 
of the features to the output of the model. SHAP analysis has been 
applied to explain the result of ML prediction in superalloys. Qin et al. 
[14] established a phase prediction model of Ni-based superalloys 
through high-throughput experiments and machine learning. They 
explained the model by SHAP analysis method and obtained the influ -
encing factors of harmful phase (TCP or GCP) precipitation. 
When ML was applied in designing of superalloys, the composition- 
performance mapping relationship is widely adopted [15 – 17] , which 
will lead to the problems that the dimensions of input features are 
excessive and the models are complex. In addition, the datasets used in 
material science are always small. Multi-dimension features and little 
quantity of data make ML models easy to overfit and be inaccurate. In 
the field of machine learning, traditional dimensionality reduction only 
by algorithms like principal component analysis(PCA) has been applied 
to reduce feature dimensions in the process of model training [18] . It 
could help remove redundant information in data, simplify model and 
avoid dimensional disasters [19] . However, although these 
dimensionality reduction methods can remove redundant information in 
data and improve the accuracy ability of model, some important features 
would lose their physical meaning after dimensionality reduction and 
the model will be unexplainable as well. Therefore, a creep life predic -
tion model based on ML method was needed to be constructed in order 
to simplify the key features and improve the interpretability of this 
model. 
In this study, a dimensionality reduction strategy coupling with 
physical metallurgy (PM) models and CALPHAD method was proposed 
to transform test temperature and 14 composition features into 8 
microstructural features so as to improve the prediction accuracy of the 
creep life model. Besides, the internal mechanisms among the key fea -
tures and prediction results were explained by SHAP analysis method. 
2. Method 
2.1. Dataset 
Training ML model needs a great amount of data, it is unrealistic to 
get these data by creep experiments that take plenty of time and money. 
Consequently, collecting data from published works is common in ma -
chine learning research [15,20] . Herein, the Ni-based single crystal su -
peralloys creep data used for training ML models were collected and 
extracted from published literatures [10,21] and patents [22 – 29] , which 
were considered credible. These data contain alloy compositions (14 
elements), test conditions (temperature and stress) and corresponding 
creep lives. The feature space formed by the data is shown in Table 1 . 
2.2. Dimensionality reduction 
As shown in Table 1 , the original dataset contains 14 compositions 
and testing condition features, which might contain redundant features. 
These redundant information might lead to a complicated model which 
is easy to overfit and get poor accuracy [18] . Dimensionality reduction is 
a good way to solve the problem. As shown in Fig. 1 , three ML models 
were established according to different dimensionality reduction stra -
tegies. The first model was a not-dimensionality-reduced (Not DR) 
model which used composition and test condition features as inputs to 
train ML model. The second model was a dimensionality-reduced model 
by Principal Component Analysis (PCA DR) and was trained by the 
features gotten from PCA. The number of principal components (PCs) of 
PCA was optimized and selected through R
2
, and finally 9 PCs were 
applied to the model. This method will make the model fit better, but it 
will lead to the loss of the input features ’ physical meaning and the lack 
of the model ’ s interpretability. In order to improve the accuracy of the 
model while retaining its interpretability, physical metallurgy was 
applied to guide the training of ML model. Thus, a dimension-reduced 
model guided by traditional physical metallurgy models and CAL -
PHAD method (PM DR) was designed. Through the special dimension -
ality reduction, test temperature and 14 composition features were 
transformed into 8 structural features which would be taken as input 
when training ML model. In this way, the number of features could be 
reduced and the meaning of microstructural features could be retained 
as well, which made the model more accurate and explainable 
concurrently.

2.4. Data preprocessing 
As shown in Table 2 , there are apparently numerical and dimen -
sional differences among those features. For example, lattice misfit 
varies from 0.38% to 0.74%, while test stress varies from 70 MPa to 
345 MPa. When training ML model, these undesirable data would cause 
a singular matrix and mislead ML model training like increasing training 
time and decreasing prediction capacity [18] . Thus, max-min normali -
zation is applied to narrow the input features into (0,1), which would 
enhance the comparability of features. Additionally, creep life data were 
transformed into logarithmic form to solve the non-normal distribution 
problem of data and improve prediction performance of ML model. 
2.5. Machine learning algorithm and model training 
As a bagging integrated algorithm based on decision trees, Random 
Forest algorithm [35] was employed to fit the creep data here. It selects 
several features and samples from dataset to train decision trees 
randomly, and integrates all results of decision trees as the result of 
Random Forest subsequently. As an integrated algorithm, Random 
Forest is of better accuracy than the other algorithms on account of the 
random features and samples selected for ML model training. Moreover, 
when training Random Forest model, it is faster than other algorithms 
because training decision trees are mutually independent and easy to 
parallelly compute [36,37] . 
In order to get an ML model with good prediction and generalization 
ability, the creep life dataset was divided into training set and test set in 
4:1 ratio. Grid Search method in Scikit-Learn toolkit was applied to 
adjust the hyperparameters of Random Forest algorithm. Additionally, 
ten-fold cross validation strategy was adopted to get the best hyper -
parameter combination. Then, the three ML models as shown in Fig. 1 
were trained. 
2.6. Evaluation of models 
In this paper, R
2
, RMSE, and MAE (mean-absolute error) were 
employed to evaluate the precision of ML models. They were calculated 
on the log-transformed creep rupture lives. As for the interpretability of 
ML model, the questions in superalloy need to answer “ which charac -
teristics have the greatest impact on properties ” and “ what is the rela -
tionship between properties and compositions or microstructures ” . In 
this paper, SHAP analysis was applied to the interpretability of ML 
model. It evaluates the interpretability with Shapley values which 
represent the impact of features on the prediction results. 
3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Correlation analysis 
For training ML models, different input features are expected to be 
possibly irrelevant for the reason that two highly relevant features 
contain little information but become redundant data [38] . In order to 
figure out correlation of the selected microstructural features, correla -
tion analysis has been processed and Pearson correlation coefficients is 
obtained. 
As shown in Fig. 2 , the absolute values of the Pearson correlation 
coefficients between all microstructural features are less than 0.7, 
indicating that there is no strong correlationship among these features. 
That is, these features have good independence and will not produce 
multicollinearity. Therefore, these features can be directly used as the 
feature input of ML model. Thus, it ’ s reasonable to select these micro -
structural features as inputs for training ML model. Through the corre -
lation analysis of the features, the independence of the features is 
ensured, the noisy data in the feature screening stage is avoided, and the 
data redundancy is reduced. This will make the ML model have faster 
training speed and better fitting degree.

5. Conclusions 
In this paper, a dimensionality reduction strategy based on the 
physical metallurgy was proposed and a creep life prediction ML model 
based on 8 key microstructural features was established. Moreover, 
SHAP analysis was applied to explained the internal mechanisms among 
the key features and prediction results of the physical metallurgy guided 
ML model. The main conclusions of the study are as follows: 
(1) The ML model guided by physical metallurgy constructed in this 
paper had the best prediction accuracy compared with the others. 
After the physical metallurgy guided dimensionality reduction, 
the original composition features were successfully reduced into 
limited key microstructural features. These microstructural fea -
tures guided well the training process of creep life prediction 
model and made it the most accurate.
