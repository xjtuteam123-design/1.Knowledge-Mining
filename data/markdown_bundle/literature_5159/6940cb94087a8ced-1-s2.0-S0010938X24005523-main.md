ABSTRACT
Understanding the transformation behavior of the oxide layer on FGH96 powders during sintering should be in
terms of oxides formation, decomposition and the diffusion of oxygen. This study investigates the early sintering
stages of FGH96 superalloys, revealing the transformation of amorphous oxide layer into stable (Al
2
O
3
, ZrO
2
)
and unstable (NiO, CoO, Cr
2
O
3
, TiO
2
) crystalline particles. Oxygen ions, generated by the decomposition of
unstable oxides, preferentially integrate into γ matrix due to lower formation energy, overshadowing γ ′ phase
diffusion. The low propensity for internal oxidation, attributed to high energy barriers and oxygen partial
pressure, leads to oxygen dissolution in the matrix.
1. Introduction
Powder metallurgy (PM) Ni-based superalloys are widely used in the
manufacture of aeroengine turbine disk because of the excellent high
temperature mechanical properties [1 – 3] . However, the appearance of
the oxides at prior particle boundaries (PPBs) during hot isostatic
pressing (HIP) consolidation easily causes stress concentration and
become the crack source [4,5] . Extensive researches have been dedi-
cated to mitigate the adverse effects of oxides on high temperature
mechanical properties of the alloys, such as hot isothermal forging and
heat treatment [5,6] . Therefore, understanding the formation and evo-
lution of oxides at PPBs plays a crucial guiding role in the rational
control of oxide particles.
The PPBs in the formed alloy are closely related to the surface
characteristics of the original powder and the preparation process of the
alloy. Previous studies have provided comprehensive insights into the
oxide films on the powder surfaces of Ni-based superalloys, as well as the
characterization of oxide phase at PPBs after HIP sintering. Generally, a
uniform oxide film composed of various oxides such as Al
2
O
3
, TiO
2
,
Cr
2
O
3
, and NiO covers the powder surface [7,8] . This is almost un-
avoidable because the preparation, storage, transportation, and use of
powders are difficult to completely isolate from oxygen. One of the
difficulties in studying this oxide film lies in the extremely complex
composition of Ni-based superalloys, where each component exhibits
unique oxidation kinetics [9,10] . Nevertheless, it is widely acknowl-
edged that primary oxide particles predominantly consist of Al
2
O
3
and
ZrO
2

et al. [11] revealed the presence of exclusively Al

particles at the PPB interface by utilized Transmission Electron Micro-
scopy (TEM) and Electron Energy Loss Spectroscopy. Similar phenom-
ena on FGH96 superalloys were also obtained from literatures [12,13] .
Another important issue that needs to be addressed is how the oxide film
on the powder surface evolves into oxides at PPBs in the formed alloy.
Maximenko and Olevsky [14] suggested that the solid-state sintering of
spherical powder particles is primarily a process of element diffusion
along grain boundaries or volume diffusion. Similarly, Tan [15] believed
that the prolonged holding time under high pressure during HIP enables
sufficient diffusion of elements from the powder interior into PPBs. This

Furthermore, the uneven diffusion mechanism of oxygen (O) in high-
temperature alloys exacerbates the difficulty of studying oxide evolu-
tion. Most research efforts have been focused on elucidating the oxida-
tion mechanism of metals or alloys within high-concentration O
2
atmospheres on a macroscopic level [16 – 18] . In such scenarios, defects
such as grain boundaries or phase boundaries harbor numerous va-
cancies stemming from lattice distortions, thereby facilitating the
diffusion of oxygen in these regions [19,20] . However, it has been also
suggested that O diffusion in the system itself may not be affected by
these factors [21,22] . Konrad et al. [21] have discerned a network of
oxides, primarily comprising ZrO
2
particles formed at the oxidation
front, serving as a pathway for the inward diffusion of O in Ni-Zr-Y alloy.
Weiser et al. [22] argue that the significant accumulation of vacancies in
the alloy matrix significantly enhances O diffusion, which in turn trig-
gers lattice expansion near the O transport pathway, ultimately leading
to the formation of oxides. Therefore, it is evident that the potential for
O to diffuse through the phase itself in the oxidation behavior of alloys
could not be disregarded. The applicability of this possibility to Ni-based
superalloys and the specific conditions under which it occurs remain to
be further investigated. Given the high vacuum conditions maintained
during sintering, the powder ’ s intrinsic O content remains extremely
low, unaffected by exogenous O sources. Consequently, conclusions
drawn from studies conducted under high O content environments may
not be directly applicable. Notably, in the intricate alloy systems of su-
peralloys, the diffusion of O can pose challenges related to selective
oxidation, which could further complicate the oxidation behavior and its
underlying mechanisms [18,23] . In Ni-based superalloys, γ ′ particles
contain higher content of Al atoms than γ matrix, which suggests a
greater susceptibility to oxidation However, in many experiments, the
oxidation reaction occurs preferentially in γ matrix, and the mechanism
is confused [10,19,20,24] . Therefore, it is worth considering whether
different diffusion pathway exist due to varying oxidation rates in the γ
matrix and γ ′ phase in Ni-based superalloys.
In this study, interrupt sintering was conducted using Spark Plasma
Sintering (SPS) under rapid vacuum conditions to facilitate the early
stage of sintering necks in FGH96 Ni-based superalloys. By using TEM
and three-dimensional atomic probe (3D-AP) characterizations, the
structure of oxide layer on the original powder surface and its trans-
formation, including the oxides formation, as well as the dissolution and
diffusion behavior of atomic and ionic O during the initial sintering are
analyzed from both structural and elemental distribution perspectives.
The potential diffusion mechanism of O was discussed through first-
principles calculations. These findings provide new perspectives for
understanding the progressive formation and evolution of oxides during
the early stages of sintering in FGH96 Ni-based superalloys.

2. Experimental
2.1. Materials and experiment
The original FGH96 alloy powders used in this work was provided by
AECC Beijing Institute of Aeronautical Materials. The powders were
fabricated by argon atomization, and the normalized composition was
shown in Table 1 . The obtained atomized powders was packed in vac-
uums prevent further oxidation in the air. To simulate the powder
contact with partial consolidation, SPS was employed to achieve the
interrupt sintering and obtain the sintering neck interface. 10 g FGH96
alloy powder were firstly weighed and loaded into a graphite mold with
a diameter of 10 mm for SPS. The sintering parameters were set at a
temperature of 1000 ℃ under a vacuum condition of 10
 3
Pa for a
duration of 10 min, with a radial pressure of 10 MPa. To simulate the
powder surface without consolidation after sintering, the heat treatment
for alloy powders was performed by microwave sintering at Ar atmo-
sphere due to its rapid heating advantage. The heat treatment temper-
ature was 1000 ℃ for 10 min.

The morphology of the neck interface after sintering was charac-

energy dispersive spectroscope (EDS) equipped by TEM was used to
analyze the elements distribution in oxide layer of original powder and
the neck interface. 3D-AP analysis was performed on LEAP 5000 XR
(CAMECA, France) with a laser pulse energy at an ambient pressure of
p < 10
 10

 200 ℃ to characterize the atomic/ionic of O on the surface of the

(Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). The initial stage of the FIB process in-

material removal. After the roughing process, the voltage is reduced to
less than 5 kV for fine processing. This lower energy is used to refine the
sample area and remove residual Ga ions on TEM and 3D-AP samples.

Generally, the diversity of alloying elements indicates the complexity
of oxides on the surface of alloy powders, and the diffusion behavior of
elements dominates the formation of oxides at powder surface during
initial sintering stage [14] . Herein, the diffusion behavior of the ele-

thickness of the oxide layer increased from 30 nm to 50 nm after initial

element diffusion mechanism for oxide growth [9,34] , and explains the

3.4. The dissolution of the oxides and diffusion behavior of O during
sintering
It is widely acknowledged that O diffusion resembles the process of
matrix oxidation [35] . Additionally, the mutual diffusion of elements
within the alloy primarily occurs through the migration of ions or
electrons [38] . The diffusion of O and the internal oxidation is governed
by the formation of oxides [21] . Such harsh conditions make it difficult
to capture internal oxidation behavior. Consequently, we have extracted
data pertaining to complex ions at the oxide-matrix interface. This
analysis aims to elucidate the distribution and speciation of alloy and O
elements across the interface, thereby shedding light on the diffusion
process of O. Fig. 5 (a) illustrates the interface between the oxide and
matrix, visualized by a 6.0 at% Cr iso-concentration contour (grey
interface). As marked with distinct colors in Fig. 5 (b) and (c), three
regions: the oxide area, the diffusion area, and the matrix are divided in
the composition profile. An enlarged and clearer depiction of the
interface is presented in Fig. 5 (d). It can be seen that a segregation of Ti
and Al atoms at the Cr
2
O
3
area ( Fig. 5 (b) and (d2), indicating that Ti and
Al elements may partly exist as solid solution atoms in the oxide parti-
cles. Meanwhile, the rich Ti-O and Al-O ions compare with other
metal-O ions (Ni-O, Co-O and W-O complex ions) in Fig. 5 (c) proved that
the competitive relationship among Al, Ti and Cr elements in the initial
oxidation process, as observed in Figs. 4 (b) and 5 (d1). Notably, despite
the significant presence of Ni in the oxide particles, its higher Gibbs free
energy requirement for oxidation compared to Al, Ti, and Cr results in Ni
being predominantly present in an atomic rather than ionic state ( Figs. 5
(b) and (d4)), a pattern also observed for Co, W, and Mo elements.

summary, the diffusion of O is concomitant with oxide formation,
consistent with the internal oxidation behavior proposed by Konrad

terminants in the dissolution of oxides and the progression of internal
oxidation.

In general, the primary diffusion mechanisms of O in Ni-based su-
peralloys are grain boundary diffusion or phase boundary diffusion.
Chen et al. [20] investigated the influence of rhenium (Re) on the
oxidation behavior of a Ni-based single-crystal superalloy via in situ
environmental TEM. The study revealed that oxidation initially occurred
at the γ / γ ′ interface, subsequently involving the γ channels, and ulti-
mately leading to the oxidation of the entire alloy matrix. In such case,
the abundance of vacancies and defects localized at grain or phase
boundaries promotes the enrichment and diffusion of O. This facilitates
an oxidative advancement that spans across boundaries. Concurrently,
the oxides exhibit a growth pattern that extends in both directions along
the γ / γ ′ interface [19,45] . The corresponding diffusion mechanism of O
and subsequent internal oxidation direction are shown in Fig. 10 (a).
Actually, in situ oxidation experiments of superalloys replicate the
oxidation behavior experienced by components in service more accu-
rately, which is predominantly induced by an excess supply of O from
the environment. However, in contrast to extrinsic sources of O, which
may arise from the environment or external factors, the diffusion of O
during sintering is primarily sourced intrinsically from the powder
surface (shown in Fig. 10 (b)). This type of O, originating from within the
material itself, can be termed ’ intrinsic O ’ . The minimal O content on the
powder surface prompts the diffusion of O to overlook the ease of
diffusion offered by grain or phase boundaries ( Figs. 3 (c) and 6 (c)).
Nonetheless, these interfaces could offer greater convenience when O
atoms traverse these areas for their passage. Despite this, O can spon-
taneously dissolve within the γ matrix and γ ′ phase, enabling intrinsic O
to undergo interstitial diffusion through these phases. The variance in
the free energy of O dissolution between the γ matrix and γ ′ phase
further directs the diffusion of intrinsic O preferentially through the γ
channels.

4. Conclusion
In conclusion, the evolution of the amorphous oxide layer of FGH96
superalloy powder during sintering was investigated. The trans-
formation behavior of the oxides should be divided into two parts ac-
cording to the formation of the sintering neck.
Prior to the sintering necks formation, the amorphous oxide layer
composed of various oxide particles of the original powder surface firstly
grow up to form stable (Al
2
O
3
and ZrO
2
) or unstable (NiO, CoO, Cr
2
O
3
and TiO
2
) crystalline oxide particles. At the same time, the edge of oxide
particles in contact with the inside of the powder is decomposed under
the reduction of O partial pressure. Until forming the sintering neck, the
unstable oxides decomposed completely, leaving only stable oxide par-
ticles at PPBs. In the process of internal diffusion of O ions provided by
the decomposition of oxides, O requires the lowest formation energy to
solid into the Oc interstices of the γ matrix (-8.06*10
 19
J) compared
with γ ′ particles (-7.90*10
 19
J), which dominates the diffusion
behavior of O in γ matrix. The highest formation energy required for O
replacing Ni atoms and extremely low partial pressure of O restrict the
occurrence of the internal oxidation behavior. Consequently, the
sequence of preferential reactions involving the dissolved O from the
oxide layer within the matrix is ranked as: solid solution formation in the
Oc sites of γ > solid solution formation in the Te sites of γ > oxidation to
form oxides. Finally, O atoms should be dissolved in the matrix as solid
solution atoms.
In summary, the evolution of amorphous oxide layer in sintering
process of FGH96 superalloy powders has been comprehensively studied
in this study. A detailed analysis of the transition behavior of the oxides
and the subsequent formation of the sintering neck has shed new light on
the complex kinetic processes. This study provides a new perspective for
the evolution of oxide film in sintering process of other PM alloy
powders.
