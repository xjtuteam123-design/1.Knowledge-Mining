ABSTRACT
Spark plasma sintering (SPS), followed by hot extrusion technique is an effective powder metallurgy strategy for 
preparing high-performance Al-Ni based alloys by forming large numbers of Al-Ni phases. In order to obtain 
optimum strength and plasticity, one feasible method is to control the size and shape of Al-Ni phases by changing 
the sintering temperature of SPS. Thus, this study investigates the impact of SPS temperature on the micro -
structure and mechanical properties of Al – Ni – Sc – Zr alloy. The results indicate that the strength decreases 
gradually while the plasticity gradually increases with rising SPS temperature. The decrease in strength is mainly 
due to the coarsening of Al-Ni phases, resulting in the reduced particle strengthening. While, this coarsening 
transforms the shape of Al-Ni phases from rod-shape to spherical-shape, thus eliminates the strain heterogeneity 
around the Al-Ni phases during deformation. Additionally, a higher SPS temperature suppresses the appearance 
of Ni-free region, further mitigating the heterogeneous distribution of plasticity during deformation. These 
combined effects alleviate strain localization and microcracks initiation, thereby promoting the plasticity. These 
results highlight the significant role of SPS temperature in strength and plasticity, which should be considered 
carefully for preparing high-performance eutectic Al alloys processed by powder metallurgy.
1. Introduction
Al-Ni based eutectic alloys have attracted extensive attention due to 
their good mechanical properties [ 1 – 4 ]. Such good mechanical prop -
erties are closely attributed to the fine Al-Ni phases in microstructure (i. 
e. Al
3
Ni and Al
9
Ni
2 
phases) [ 5 – 7 ], which contribute significantly to 
strengthening. In order to prepare the Al-Ni based alloy with a higher 
strength, a microstructure with fine Al-Ni phases of higher number 
density is highly desirable [ 8 , 9 ].
Several strategies have been proposed to obtain the fine Al-Ni phases. 
One common method is alloying design, where specific elements are 
introduced into the Al-Ni alloy to suppress the coarsening of Al-Ni 
phases, such as Mn [ 10 ], Mg [ 11 ], Ti [ 12 , 13 ], V [ 13 ], Fe [ 13 ], Sc and 
Zr [ 13 – 16 ]. Among these elements, Sc and Zr elements are particularly 
effective in suppressing the coarsening of Al-Ni phases [ 17 – 20 ]. This is 
attributed to the segregation of Sc and/or Zr at α -Al/Al – Ni phases 
interface, which restricts the transport of Ni solute through the 
aluminum matrix [ 17 – 20 ]. Besides, the addition of Sc and Zr can also 
improve the mechanical properties by forming nanosized Al
3
(Sc, Zr) 
particles [ 15 , 21 – 24 ]. Therefore, Al – Ni – Sc – Zr alloys are expected to 
exhibit superior mechanical properties compared to binary Al-Ni alloy.
On the other hand, additive manufacturing (AM) and powder met -
allurgy (PM) techniques can help to obtain fine Al-Ni phases with unique 
phase morphology, as the powders used for AM and PM undergo the 
rapid solidification (RS) during the preparation. Many studies have 
demonstrated the advantage of RS techniques over conventional ingot 
casting in aluminum alloys [ 25 , 26 ]. In an as-cast Al-Ni eutectic alloy, the 
Al
3
Ni phases typically form as large fibrous-shape or large lamellar- 
shape due to the low cooling rate [ 17 , 27 , 28 ]. In contrast, the 
extremely high cooling rates and significant undercooling achieved by 
RS technology create an unstable growth interface, leading to the for -
mation of unique phase morphologies [ 29 – 31 ]. In addition, a higher

spherical Al-Ni phases can be obtained by the RS techniques [ 33 ]. It is

Among the techniques combined with RS, spark plasma sintering 
(SPS) is widely applied in engineering applications due to its efficiency 
in rapidly densifying materials. The powder is compacted, necked, and 
finally sintered during the sintering process [ 34 – 38 ]. The sintering 
temperature is a critical parameter which can significantly influence the 
microstructure and mechanical properties of Al-Ni based alloy [ 39 ]. On 
the one hand, a higher sintering temperature enhance grain boundary 
migration and solute diffusion during SPS, leading to coarser grains and 
phases, which reduces strength [ 40 – 43 ]. On the other hand, a higher 
sintering temperature improve powder compaction and reduce the de -
fects (e.g. pores, cracks), leading to a more homogeneous microstructure 
[ 44 , 45 ]. This would be beneficial for a higher strength and a higher 
plasticity. The conflicted two aspects need a detailed investigation on 
the sintering temperature for the optimization of strength-plasticity in 
Al-Ni based alloy, which is still not clear.
In summary, the present work firstly attempts to prepare the high- 
performance Al-Ni based alloy through microalloying design and SPS 
process. Considering the important influence of Sc and Zr elements on 
suppressing the coarsening of Al-Ni phases, an Al – Ni – Sc – Zr alloy is used 
as a model alloy in the present work. Due to the RS process of powder 
preparation, Sc and Zr can reach supersaturation in the aluminum ma -
trix, leading to a higher strength. Thus, a high Sc content is usually used 
for obtaining a higher strength in Al alloys processed by RS. The 
microstructure and mechanical properties of Al – Ni – Sc – Zr alloys through 
different sintering temperatures are studied and the underlying physical 
mechanisms controlling strength and plasticity are discussed. This study 
might inspire subsequent design and optimization of Al-Ni based alloys 
by RS technique.
2. Experimental and methods

3.3. Mechanical properties
Fig. 5 (a) shows the engineering stress-strain curves of the AE430, 
AE455 and AE480 samples. The yield strength (YS), ultimate tensile 
strength (UTS) and total elongation of the AE430 are about 324 ± 5 
MPa, 374 ± 6 MPa and 8 ± 1 %, respectively. For AE455 sample, the YS, 
UTS and total elongation are about 301 ± 3 MPa, 358 ± 8 MPa and 12 
± 1 %, respectively. The YS, UTS and total elongation of AE480 sample 
are about 298 ± 2 MPa, 350 ± 3 MPa and 15 ± 1 %, respectively. As the 
sintering temperature increases, the YS and UTS of the samples gradu -
ally decrease, while the total elongation gradually increases. The com -
parison of the product of strength and plasticity shows that AE480 
sample has the best strength-plasticity synergy ( Fig. 5 (b)). This indicates 
that a higher SPS temperature can lead to a high strength-plasticity in 
terms of the product of strength and plasticity. Compared with the 
conventional Al-Ni based alloys prepared by PM process reported in 
other studies [ 26 , 55 , 56 ], our SPS processed Al – Ni – Sc – Zr alloy has a 
good combination of strength and plasticity ( Fig. 5 (c)).

5. Conclusions
In this study, the influence of sintering temperature on the micro -
structure and mechanical properties of Al – Ni – Sc – Zr alloys fabricated by 
spark plasma sintering (SPS) and hot deformation process is investi -
gated. The underlying physical mechanisms controlling the strength and 
plasticity are discussed. The main conclusions are summarized as 
follows: 
1. The Al – Ni – Sc – Zr alloy processed by SPS and hot extrusion exhibits 
an improved strength and plasticity (yield strength ~297.4 MPa to 
324.5 MPa, plasticity ~8.6 % to 15.7 %), which are higher than that 
of the conventional Al-Ni alloy. It is suggested that the SPS is an 
effective strategy for preparing the high-performance Al-Ni based 
alloys.
2. The SPS temperature has a significant influence on the strength and 
plasticity. The strength decreases gradually with the increase of SPS 
temperature, while the plasticity increases gradually with the in -
crease of SPS temperature. A higher SPS temperature can lead to a 
higher product of strength and plasticity.
3. The higher SPS temperature leads to the decrease in strength, which 
is mainly due to the decrease of particle strengthening from the 
coarsening of both Al
3
Ni and Al
3
(Sc, Zr) particles, as well as the 
decrease of strengthening effect from grain boundary strengthening.
4. The higher SPS temperature leads to the increase of plasticity, as the 
strain heterogeneity caused by Al
3
Ni phases evolution (from rod- 
shape to spherical-shape) and NFR is suppressed. The crack nucle -
ation associated with such strain heterogeneity is thus suppressed, 
which leads to a higher plasticity.
