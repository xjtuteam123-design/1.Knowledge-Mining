ABSTRACT 
Single-phase medium-entropy alloys (MEAs) with face-centered cubic (fcc) structure exhibit high elongation and 
toughness but suffer from insufficient strength. In this work, two novel (CrCoNi)Al
4 
and (CrCoNi)Al
4
Nb
2 
MEAs 
were designed with the aim of introducing precipitation hardening effect and thus improve strength of the alloys. 
It was revealed that addition of Al alone, only slightly increased the strength, but the elongation decreased 
severely. Conversely, the combination of Al and Nb can significantly increase strength and maintain acceptable 
elongation. The Al addition promotes the formation of a disperse distribution of BCC/B2 composite precipitates, 
however, the large size and incoherent nature of these composite precipitates produce minor hardening response. 
Except for the BCC/B2 composite precipitates, a high density of the nanoscale γ ′ phase with L1
2 
structure and 
stoichiometry of (Ni, Co, Cr)
3
(Al, Nb) is produced in the Al
4
Nb
2 
MEA, which significantly increases the strength 
of the alloy. After the addition of Al and Nb, the deformation mechanism of the alloys is changed from nanotwins 
to stacking fault (SF) dominated plasticity. Such phenomenon mainly relates to the increasing critical twinning 
stress affected by the channel width of the matrix. This study provides new insight for the composition design of 
precipitation-hardening MEAs. 
1. Introduction 
High entropy alloys (HEAs) or medium entropy alloys (MEAs) hold 
great promise for engineering applications due to their excellent com -
bination of properties, including high strength, excellent ductility, and 
fracture toughness [1 – 4] . Among various HEA/MEAs, the 
three-component CoCrNi MEA is currently receiving extensive attention 
due to their superior mechanical properties at room and cryogenic 
temperatures, even when compared to four or five-component HEAs [2, 
4] . The formation of nanotwins and some hexagonal close-packed (HCP) 
phases during deformation is responsible for the improved 
work-hardening rate of the CoCrNi alloy through a “ dynamic Hall- -
Petch ” effect [5 – 8] . For engineering applications, the ductility of these 
single-FCC MEAs is surplus, while their yield strength is insufficient for 
their application in structural products. Therefore, it is necessary to 
enhance yield strength of the CrCoNi alloy while maintaining suitable 
ductility. 
A doping strategy by dilute addition of metallic elements to form 
nano-sized precipitates is a feasible strategy for improving mechanical 
properties of the HEA/MEAs by acting as barriers for dislocation motion 
[9 – 11] . It has been reported that when Al [12] , Ti [13] , Cu [11] or Mo 
[14] elements are introduced, the L1
2
, ordered BCC (B2), σ and μ 
intermetallic compounds can be formed within the FCC matrix, leading 
to outstanding mechanical properties of the alloys. Lee et al. [12] re -
ported that Al addition in CrCoNi alloy leads to the formation of a 
NiAl-rich B2-phase, which substantially increases the yield strength by a 
combination of several strengthening mechanisms, such as substitu -
tional solid solution, grain refinement, and the B2 precipitation hard -
ening. The B2 phase also changes the deformation mechanism of the 
alloy from deformation twinning to dislocation slip. Feng et al. [15] 
reported that the Al
0.5
CoCrFeNi alloy exhibits excellent 
fatigue-crack-initiation resistance due to the high elasticity, plastic 
deformability, and martensitic transformation of the B2 phase. Zhang 
et al. [16] also reported that the addition of Al and Ta in a CrCoNi alloy 
results in an outstanding strength-ductility synergy at 77 K via the 
synergy of multiple mechanisms, in particular the B2-driven twinning at

3.1. Mechanical properties 
Representative engineering stress-strain curves and work-hardening 
rate curves of the three alloys are shown in Fig. 1 . It can be seen that the 
base CoCrNi MEA has a yield strength (YS) of 350 MPa, ultimate tensile

4

plotted along with the true stress-strain curves as shown in Fig. 1 (b). 
Initially, the work hardening rates of the three MEAs sharply decreased 
as the strain increases, exhibiting a linear relationship with different

The Al

the early stage of deformation with a moderate decrease at the plastic

mechanical properties of the CrCoNi MEA, while a combination of Al 
and Nb can significantly increase the strength of the alloy while main -

properties of the base CrCoNi MEA is lower than that reported in pre -

mation during cold rolling. This difference does not affect the properties 
comparison among the three alloys.

3.3. Plastic deformation mechanism 
To understand the strengthening mechanism of the three MEAs, the 
deformation microstructure evolution at different plastic strain levels 
were characterized using EBSD and TEM techniques. Fig. 8 shows in -

nano-twins is the dominant deformation mode for the CrCoNi MEA,

4. Discussion 
4.1. Effect of Al and Nb additions on precipitation phases 
It has been known that the Al addition in CrCoNi MEA acts as the 
secondary-phase former and thus improve the mechanical properties by 
precipitation hardening. The microstructure and constitution of the 
secondary phases are very sensitive to the Al content and can be varied 
from single-phase FCC (for x ≈ 0 – 0.4), to dual-phase FCC + BCC/B2 (for x 
≈ 0.4 – 0.9) to single-phase BCC/B2 (for x ≈ 0.9 – 2.0). Lee et al. [26] 
reported that Al
7.7
CrCoNi MEA produces a heterogeneous precipitation 
of the NiAl-rich B2 phase at grain boundaries and grain interior, and a 
refined grain size during annealing. Sathiyamoorthi et al. [27] also 
reported that the formation of a heterogeneous microstructure and B2 
precipitates gives a remarkable combination of cryogenic yield strength 
and elongation in an Al
0.3
CoCrNi MEA. For the Al
4

present work, a uniform distribution of B2/BCC composite precipitates 
are formed, which is different from the heterogeneous B2 precipitates 
formed in Al

5. Conclusion 
In this study, the effect of Al and Nb on the precipitation hardening 
and deformation mechanism of CrCoNi MEAs was systematically studied 
by a combination of different characterization techniques. The main 
conclusions are as follows: 
1. Compared with the single-phase CrCoNi MEA, the addition of Al in 
the CoCrNi MEA can only slight increase the strength, and the elonga -
tion is obviously reduced, which is not favorable for the properties of the 
alloy. In contrast, the combination of Al and Nb can significantly in -
crease the strength of the alloy, although the elongation is still 
decreased. 
2. For both of the Al
4 
and Al
4
Nb
2 
MEAs, a dispersed distribution of 
B2/BCC composite precipitates is formed in the grain interior and grain 
boundaries. The formation of these composite precipitates is mainly due 
to the disorder-order transformation of the BCC to B2. Owing to the low 
density and incoherent interface of the composite precipitates, only 
slight increase of strength is achieved for the Al
4 
MEAs. Besides, a high 
density of the nanoscale γ ′ phase with L1
2 
structure and composition of 
(Ni, Co, Cr)
3
(Al, Nb) is formed in the Al
4
Nb
2 
MEA, which is responsible 
for the enhanced strength of this alloy. 
3. The deformation mechanism of the CrCoNi MEA is changed after 
the addition of Al and Nb. Compared with the nanotwins and small 
fraction of HCP lamella formed in the base CrCoNi MEA, a high density 
of stacking faults dominated the whole deformation of the Al
4 
and 
Al
4
Nb
2 
MEAs. Besides, a high density of dislocation cells, and disloca -
tion tangles around particles are also formed. The absence of nanotwins 
in Al
4
Nb
2 
MEA is mainly related to the increasing critical twinning stress 
affected by the channel width of the matrix.
