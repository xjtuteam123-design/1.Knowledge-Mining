Abstract 
The high-temperature mechanical properties of 95W-3.5Ni-1.5Fe and 95W-4.5Ni-0.5Co alloys were investigated in the temperature range of 
room temperature to1100°C. The yield strength and tensile strengths declined gradually, and the ductility of both alloys increased as the test-
ing temperature was increased to 300°C. All the three properties reached a plateau at temperatures between 300 and 500°C in the case of 
95W-3.5Ni-1.5Fe and at temperatures between 350 and 700°C in the case of 95W-4.5Ni-0.5Co. Thereafter, the ductility as well as yield and 
tensile strengths decreased considerably. 
Keywords: liquid phase sintering; tungsten alloys; tensile testing; mechanical properties 
 
 
1. Introduction 
Tungsten heavy alloys are metal-matrix composites of 
W-Ni-Fe, W-Ni-Cu, or W-Ni-Co and are produced by liquid 
phase sintering. Their high density, strength, and ductility 
have been studied extensively [1-10]. This makes them use-
ful for applications, such as chatter-free boring bars, radia-
tion shields, sporting goods, balance weights in gyroscopes, 
and kinetic energy penetrators. Although polycrystalline 
tungsten is normally brittle at room temperature, the matrix 
imparts ductility to the composite [11-12]. Considering the 
large number of parameters associated with this material, the 
variability in mechanical properties is not surprising. Gener-
ally, the factors influencing mechanical properties can be 
divided into three categories: factors that produce different 
results between studies, such as composition, sintering tem-
perature, test geometry, sintering atmosphere, and heat 
treatment; factors that account for the differing properties of 
similarly processed alloys, such as density, pore size, impu-
rities, and particle size; factors that contribute to property 
variations to alloys sintered at the same temperature, such as 
thermal and gravitational gradients [13]. 
The aim of the research reported in this paper was to as-
sess the influence of temperature on the strength and ductil-
ity of high tungsten content 95W-3.5Ni-1.5Fe and 
95W-4.5Ni-0.5Co alloys. 
 
2. Experimental 
Elemental powders of tungsten, nickel, cobalt, and iron 
were mixed to produce tungsten heavy alloys with composi-
tion 95W-3.5Ni-1.5Fe and 95W-4.5Ni-0.5Co. The powders 
were consolidated into green compacts of tensile specimens 
with 55%-65 % of theoretical density by die pressing. Dur-
ing the heating cycle, there was a dwell at 1100°C for 1 h for 
oxide reduction and then at 1350°C for 1 h in hydrogen at-
mosphere for solid-state sintering. Solid-state sintered tung-
sten heavy alloys were subsequently sintered at 1500°C for 
30 min in hydrogen atmosphere. The sintering cycle fol-
lowed for all the experiments was 10 °C/min up to 800 °C 
and 5°C/min thereafter. During the cooling cycle, at 1400°C, 
the atmosphere changed from hydrogen to argon. In order to 
reduce the content of absorbed hydrogen and prevent em-
brittlement, the sintered samples of 95W-3.5Ni-1.5Fe were 
subsequently annealed in a vacuum at 1100 °C for 5 h and 
then in argon for 3 h, and those of 95W-4.5Ni-0.5Co were 
annealed in a vacuum at 1200 °C for 5 h and then in argon 
for 3 h. They were then water quenched to circumvent the 
impurity segregation problem. The densities of the sintered 
specimens were measured by the Archimedes water immer-
sion method. Tensile tests were conducted at a strain rate of 
0.5 mm/min in argon atmosphere. The temperature range for 
tensile testing was room temperature to 1100 °C, and the 
heating was accomplished by electrical resistance and

3. Results and discussion 
Fig. 2 shows the variation of yield and tensile strengths of 
95W-3.5Ni-1.5Fe and 95W-4.5Ni-0.5Co alloys at various 
temperatures. As can be seen that, up to 275 °C, both yield 
and tensile strengths decrease as the temperature increases. 
There is then a plateau between 275 and 500 °C for 
95W-3.5Ni-1.5Fe alloys and between 300 and 700 °C for 
95W-4.5Ni-0.5Co alloys, thereafter a gradual decrease up to 
700°C for 95W-3.5Ni-1.5Fe alloys and up to 800 °C for 
95W-4.5Ni-0.5Co alloys, and, finally, a sharp decrease be-
yond those temperatures. It is readily apparent from the fig-
ure that 95W-4.5Ni-0.5Co alloys have slightly improved 
quasi-static mechanical properties as compared to those of 
95W-3.5Ni-1.5Fe alloys. The plateau in yield and tensile 
strengths in 95W-4.5Ni-0.5Co alloys and especially the very 
slight rise after 500°C can be explained by considering the 
effect of the formation of precipitates at or above 500°C to 
700°C as these alloys are susceptible to intermetallic phase 
formation and also to the eff ect of dynamic strain ageing 
(DSA), as reported earlier [11-12, 14]. From the figure, it is 
readily apparent that intermetallic phase formation has no 
marked effect on the properties of these alloys. 
Variations in elongation of 95W-3.5Ni-1.5Fe and 95W- 
4.5Ni-0.5Co alloys in relation to temperature are shown in 
Fig. 3. It can be seen that the percentage elongation shows a 
linear increase with temperature in both types of alloys, 
from 11% at room temperature to 19% for 95W-3.5Ni-1.5Fe 
alloys and 21% for 95W-4.5Ni-0.5Co alloys at 300°C. The 
strength of the tungsten grains relative to the matrix strength 
steadily increases, and because the tungsten-matrix interfa-
cial strength is not changed, the homogeneity of strains and 
stresses also increases in the composite, resulting in an in-
crease in ductility of the composites. Then, between 300 and 
500°C, an anomalous behavior occurs in 95W-3.5Ni-1.5Fe

Microhardness indentation tests for both types of alloys 
were performed at different temperatures in order to find the 
contribution of W grains and matrix phase to deformation at 
various temperatures. Fig. 4 shows the hardness of tungsten 
grains and matrix phase at 100 N load for a dwell of 10 s. As 
can be seen, there is no significant change in hardness until 
300°C, and there is then a slight increase, followed by sig-
nificant increases after 425 °C for 95W-3.5Ni-1.5Fe alloys 
and after 500°C for 95W-4.5Ni-0.5Co alloys. The changes 
in microhardness at these temperatures support the conclu-
sion that dynamic strain ageing occurs in these alloys, as 
discussed above and reported by Refs. [11-12, 14], and as it 
is well known, these alloys also display static strain age 
hardening at about the same temperature interval. It is im-
portant to note that the binder hardness does not signifi-
cantly vary in these ranges, es pecially in 95W-3.5Ni-1.5Fe 
alloys. In 95W-4.5Ni-0.5Co alloys, the matrix shows slight 
change. One possible reason for this is the formation of pre-
cipitation. Another is because the bonding strength of the 
matrix with W grains is very good, which might therefore be 
deformed to a greater extent. 
Fig. 5 shows the scanning electron micrographs (SEM) of 
the fracture surfaces of specimens, which were tested at dif-
ferent temperatures. The fracture morphology shows that, as 
the temperature increases from room temperature to 50 °C, 
failure occurs by the mixed mode in both types of alloys, but 
mainly as a result of fractured tungsten grains because the 
intrinsic nature of tungsten is never changed in different ma-
trixes. From 50 to 150 °C, failure still occurs by the mixed 
mode, but now, the intergranular and transgranular failures 
are almost equal in this range because the strength difference 
between tungsten and matrix phase decreases as the hard 
tungsten phase starts becoming soft. As the test temperature 
increases, the failure modes transfer to intergranular failure, 
and they are almost exclusively through intergranular failure 
between 275 and 500 °C, which is contrary to previous re-
sults [15-16].

at a test temperature of 350°C in 95W-3.5Ni-1.5Fe alloy. As 
can be seen, it consists of highly elongated tungsten grains 
with large voids that have formed between the tungsten and 
the matrix, and as the stress increases, transgranular failure 
may never be achieved, and failure only occurs by initiation 
and growth of intergranular voids. Fewer voids were ob-
served at similar test temperatures, from which it can be in-
ferred that the higher bonding strength of 95W-4.5Ni-0.5Co 
results in a slightly higher ductility in these alloys. In the

4. Conclusion 
As the temperature increases up to 300°C, the yield and 
tensile strengths decrease, and the ductility of these alloys 
increases. Both types of alloys then reach a plateau between 
300 and 500°C in the case of 95W-3.5Ni-1.5Fe and between 
300 and 700 °C in the case of 95W-4.5Ni-0.5Co. In this 
temperature range, the strength difference between tungsten 
and matrix phase decreases significantly as the tungsten 
softens, resulting in the formation of voids, and failure oc-
curs almost exclusively through the initiation and growth of 
voids. At a higher temperature range, failure occurs as a re-
sult of greater brittleness, which is a result of a dramatic loss 
in bonding strength between tungsten grains and matrix 
phase.
