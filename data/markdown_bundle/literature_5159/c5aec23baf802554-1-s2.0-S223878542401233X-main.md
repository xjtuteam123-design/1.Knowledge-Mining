1. Introduction 
Copper alloys have long been the cornerstone of materials engi -
neering in marine environments due to their inherent resistance to 
biofouling, which is an inevitable challenge in aquatic applications [ 1 , 
2 ]. However, the evolving demands of the marine industry, have 
necessitated the development of copper alloys with enhanced mechan -
ical properties and superior resistance to corrosion in seawater. Tradi -
tional copper alloys are encountering limitations in meeting these 
evolving requirements. The concept of high-entropy alloys (HEAs) [ 3 , 4 ] 
has emerged as a promising avenue for advancing materials science and 
engineering. By incorporating multiple principal components, HEAs 
produce a high mixing entropy effect [ 4 ], which stabilizes the formation 
of solid solution phases and reduces the likelihood of intermetallic 
compound phase precipitation [ 5 ]. The tendency to form single-phase or 
multiphase simple solid solution structures gives them exceptional me -
chanical properties [ 6 , 7 ] and corrosion resistance [ 8 ]. Despite some 
research efforts on Cu-containing HEAs have been done [ 9 – 11 ], two 
main issues persist: 1) most HEAs have a low copper content, implying 
that the capacity for copper ion release might not be sufficient; 2) HEAs 
with a high copper content often exhibit poor mechanical properties, 
particularly in terms of ductility, scarce works of Cu-containing HEAs 
with moderate tensile ductility have been reported. Achieving a balance 
between copper content and mechanical properties has become a diffi -
cult task in the design of Cu-containing HEAs. 
Our previous work [ 1 ] reported a cast Cu

strengthening mechanisms, aside from solid solution strengthening 
mechanism. This prevented the YS of the HEA from ever exceeding 500 
MPa. Hence it is necessary to introduce additional mechanisms to 
further enhance the alloy. The nucleation-growth and spinodal decom -
position are two major processes of compositional separation in HEAs. 
The nucleation-growth process has been extensively investigated 
experimentally and theoretically by multiple methods [ 12 – 16 ], while 
the number of studies on the spinodal decomposition of HEAs is much 
fewer than the nucleation-growth process. For rolled alloys with high

From a synthesis perspective, previous research indicates that many 
high-entropy alloys exhibit sluggish diffusion kinetics [ 36 ], which may 
impede the onset of spinodal decomposition during rapid cooling pro -
cesses. For instance, Fe – Co – Ni – Mn – Cu HEAs are known to decompose 
prematurely, resulting in the formation of bands enriched in individual 
elements with minimal volume and volume fraction [ 24 ]. In contrast, 
spinodal decomposition is more likely to occur at lower temperatures 
where the driving force for decomposition is higher [ 37 , 38 ]. This is 
consistent with the finding that spinodal decomposition was not 
observed in the AN sample but was present in the air-cooled sample after 
6 h of low-temperature aging.

BCC phases, which contribute to its strengthening. Moreover, nanoscale 
interwoven structures formed through spinodal decomposition within 
the matrix create numerous phase interfaces that impede dislocation 
movement, thereby reinforcing the alloy.
