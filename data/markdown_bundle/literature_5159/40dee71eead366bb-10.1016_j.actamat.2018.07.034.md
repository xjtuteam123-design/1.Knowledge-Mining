abstract
This paper develops statistically equivalent RVEs or SERVEs for intragranular microstructures of Ni-based
superalloys, characterized by g-g0 phase distribution. The SERVE represents an optimal computational
domain to be used for micromechanical simulations for effective properties or response variables in the
microstructure. The SERVE is further classi ﬁed as a microstructure-based SERVE or M-SERVE or property-
based SERVE or P-SERVE, depending on whether the statistics of morphological characteristics or
convergence of chosen material properties are its determinants. Starting from FIB-SEM data for the
superalloy Ren /C19e 88DT, the paper systematically develops a host of algorithms for generating validated
statistically equivalent virtual microstructures, from which the M-SERVE is estimated from convergence
of selected morphological and spatial distributions. Subsequently the P-SERVEs are established for global
properties like yield strength and hardening rate, and local variables including dislocation density and
the maximum resolved shear stress. Spatially-averaged quantities are found to converge quicker than the
local distributions for both M-SERVE and P-SERVE.

1. Introduction
The superior thermo-mechanical properties of Ni-based super-
alloys, e.g. high strength and thermal resistance, is often attributed
to a g-g0 phase distribution in the underlying intragranular
microstructure, shown in Fig. 1. Superalloys are one of the most
highly engineered classes of materials with typically six to ten
different alloying elements present. The complex chemical
composition promotes phase stability, solid-solution strength-
ening, oxidation and corrosion resistance, grain boundary struc-
ture, and control of carbide formation. Two primary phases are
resultant of the alloying process and heat treatment: (i) a Ni solid-
solution g matrix phase with FCC structure and (ii) an ordered
intermetallic Ni3Al secondary g0 phase with L1 2 structure forming
as dispersed precipitates. Their mechanical behavior is strongly
dependent on the g /C0 g0 phase morphology [ 1e3], primarily
because of the g0 precipitates acting as obstacles to dislocation
motion in the matrix. Effective image-based micromechanical
models can provide good insights into the in ﬂuence of morpho-
logical descriptors like size, shape, orientation, volume fraction,
and spatial correlations on the material response and properties at
higher scales, e.g. the single crystal or polycrystalline scales. Two
important factors govern the development of these micro-
mechanical models. The ﬁrst is the creation of 3D statistically
equivalent virtual microstructures (SEVMs). The statistics of
morphological descriptors in SEVMs must be equivalent to those
derived from high ﬁdelity data-sets obtained from experiments
involving electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) [ 4,5], scanning
electron microscopy (SEM) or computed tomography (CT). Deter-
ministic models e.g. in Ref. [ 6] are not well-suited for microstruc-
tures with signi ﬁcant spatial variations. The second factor is that
the SEVMs used in micromechanical analysis for predicting overall
material statistics, response and properties must be optimally
deﬁned with respect to accuracy and ef ﬁciency. This paper is
motivated by these goals.
Various methods of generating polycrystalline or polyphase
microstructures have been proposed in the literature e.g. in
Refs. [ 7e13]. These methods account for spatial variations in

These methods focus on the modeling and subsequent statistics of

particles have been examined in this paper, the following tech-
niques can be used to describe more complex heterogeneous mi-
crostructures with minor alterations.
The second factor arises from the consideration that direct nu-
merical simulations (DNS) of large microstructural regions can be
computationally prohibitive. Representative computational do-
mains must therefore be optimally de ﬁned for evaluating effective
properties without having to solve large microstructural regions.
This has led to the concept of the representative volume element or
RVE [ 17], as a microstructural subdomain that represents the
averaged structure-property relations of the entire microstructure.
The RVE concept has been further clariﬁed in Ref. [18] with separate
convergence criteria for the microstructure volume element (MVE),
property volume element (PVE) and design volume element (DVE)
respectively. However, it is dif ﬁcult to quantify a RVE for micro-
structures with nonuniformly dispersed heterogeneities as shown
in Fig. 1 a, due to the lack of uniformity or periodicity [ 19]. The
statistically equivalent RVE or SERVE has been introduced in
Refs. [20,21] to overcome many of these challenges. It is designated
as the smallest microstructural domain, for which statistical dis-
tribution functions of morphological parameters, as well as mate-
rial properties converge to those for the entire microstructure.
Exterior statistics based boundary conditions have been designed
for these SERVEs in Ref. [ 22].
This paper focuses on the development of the SERVE for intra-
granular microstructures of the Ni-based superalloy Ren /C19e 88DT
[23]. In alignment with the discussion in Ref. [ 18], the SERVE is sub-
classiﬁed into a microstructure-based SERVE or M-SERVE and a
property-based SERVE or P-SERVE. Statistics of morphological
characteristics of the microstructure are determinants of the M-
SERVE, while the P-SERVE is determined from convergence of
chosen material properties. Prior to the determination of M-SERVEs
and P-SERVEs, statistically equivalent SEVMs are generated from
experimentally obtained intragranular g /C0 g0 microstructures of a
Ni-based superalloy. A sequence of tasks, involving serial
sectioning, image processing, feature extraction and statistical
characterization are implemented for the generation of SEVMs. This
is followed by micromechanical analysis of the SEVMs. Conver-
gence tests are conducted for establishing the M-SERVE and P-
SERVE.
2. Experimental data acquisition and image processing
2.1. Data acquisition using a FIB-SEM system
The ﬁrst step in the microstructure building process is the
acquisition of high-ﬁdelity micro and nano-scale 3D data of the g /C0
g0 microstructure using an automated, high throughput focused ion
beam (FIB), coupled with a high-resolution scanning electron mi-
croscope (SEM) in a backscattered electron (BSE) con ﬁguration that
exists within a larger Tri-Beam system [ 24]. The FIB process serially
sections layers of a Ren /C19e 88DT specimen, ablating approximately
20nm of material in each pass. Individual section images are sub-
sequently obtained using the SEM using a backscatter electron
detector. The process generated 182 gray-scale images for a 3D
specimen of dimensions 3 mm /C2 4mm /C2 5mm, with each section
image containing 1996 /C2 1596 pixels. This yields a resolution of
2:5nm between pixels and 20 nm between slices. In the SEM pro-
cess, the interaction volume of the material with the incident
electron beam can cause blurring when a backscatter electron de-
tector is used for imaging. This method also has the effect of sam-
pling the material below the surface, causing inclusions below the
surface to be registered on the surface [ 25]. Image processing with
data cleanup and phase segmentation are needed to generate the
reference 3D voxelized microstructure.
2.2. Image segmentation

an automated image processing pipeline to ef ﬁciently segment the
images into g and g0 domains. The segmentation methodology

7.5 nm to reduce noise. The image stack is then sharpened by image
sharpening function (IMsharpen) in MATLAB with r ¼ 20, with the
multiplier set to 10. Subsequently, it is segmented with a minimum
entropy threshold [26], and all connected sections containing fewer
than 700 pixels are removed with a despeckling ﬁlter. The result is

3. 3D parametric function mapping of the precipitate domain
To avoid surface pro ﬁle delineation with large data-sets, the
precipitate morphology is represented through mapping functions
with a ﬁnite number of parameters. Such parametrization enables
direct incorporation of the morphological parameters in para-
metrically homogenized constitutive models [ 28,29]. These
constitutive models can then predict the sensitivity of various
response ﬁelds with respect to these morphological parameters.
For each precipitate, coef ﬁcients of the parametrized mapping
function are calibrated via an orthogonal distance minimization
(ODM) algorithm [ 30], in which the 3D voxelization is reduced to a
list of ðx; y; zÞ coordinates for surface voxels. A parametric mapping
function for a generalized superellipsoid is selected to geometri-
cally represent the g0 precipitate morphology. This parametrized
shape is capable of describing precipitates of varying size, orien-
tation, aspect ratio, and roundness (ellipsoidal to cuboidal shapes).
A limitation of the generalized superellipsoid description is that it
smooths rough surface undulations, i.e. regions of signi ﬁcant phase
boundary roughness. However, these undulations are not observed
to be signi ﬁcant for this material, and incorporating the additional
necessary parameters to capture the surface ﬂuctuations is not
efﬁcient in the development of a parametrically homogenized
constitutive law for higher scale simulations. The function repre-

6. Determining property-based statistically equivalent RVE
(P-SERVE)
The mechanical response of Ni-based superalloys is highly
dependent on the nonuniform intragranular g /C0 g0 microstructure.
Microstructural simulations for evolving variables and overall
response ﬁelds must incorporate large enough computational RVEs
to account for variabilities in evolving ﬁelds caused by spatial and
morphological heterogeneities. The property-based statistically
equivalent representative volume element (P-SERVE) corresponds
to the minimum simulation volume needed for convergence of
mechanical properties and response functions to within a pre-
scribed level of accuracy. Such properties may be classiﬁed into two
categories, viz. spatially-averaged and local. Spatially-averaged
properties are mean- ﬁeld values over the entire SERVE, such as
overall yield strength or hardening rate. Local properties on the
other hand are described in terms of the spatial distribution or the
extreme values of a response variable. Both classes are important
when performing a P-SERVE analysis. In this study, the P-SERVE is
determined from convergence of evolving ﬁelds and properties in
the microstructure that are evaluated from dislocation density-
based crystal plasticity ﬁnite element (DD-CPFE) simulations.
While methods like discrete dislocation dynamics maybe be more
appropriate at the nm scales of
g channels, their current capabilities
are not yet adequate for simulating the large heterogeneous do-
mains required for this problem.
A comprehensive sequence of steps is executed to generate
microstructure realizations and perform FE simulations with
output in the form of properties and ﬁelds determined from
dislocation density-based crystal plasticity ﬁnite element (CPFE)
simulations, as summarized below.
/C15 Microstructure Generation: The SEVMs and M-SERVEs of the g /C0
g0 microstructure are generated following steps described in the
previous sections.
/C15 Precipitate Smoothing and Meshing: The voxelized precipitates
are smoothed using the Simmetrix code [39] to remove spurious
artifacts. The cubic SERVE is then meshed with 4-noded tetra-
hedral (TET4) elements, capturing the precipitate geometries.
/C15 Pre-processing: FE input ﬁles are generated for the microstruc-
ture and loading conditions.
/C15 FE Simulation: Dislocation density-based crystal plasticity FE
simulations with special locking-free TET4 elements are per-
formed for multiple SEVMs.
/C15 Output Extraction: The averaged and local ﬁelds are extracted
from the CPFE simulation results for use in analyzing properties.

7. Conclusions and summary
This paper develops statistically equivalent RVEs or SERVEs for
intragranular microstructures of Ni-based superalloys, character-
ized by g-g0 phase distribution. The SERVE represents an optimal
computational domain that should be used for micromechanical
simulations leading to the evaluation of effective properties or
response variables in the microstructure. Furthermore, the SERVE
can be classi ﬁed as a microstructure-based SERVE ( M-SERVE), or a
property-based SERVE ( P-SERVE), depending on whether the sta-
tistics of morphological characteristics or convergence of chosen
material properties are its determinants. Steps to the development
of the M-SERVE and P-SERVE include the development of statisti-
cally equivalent virtual microstructures or SEVMs, for which sta-
tistical distribution functions are equivalent to those from
experimental data. The main difference between the M-SERVE and
the SEVMs is that the M-SERVE is the optimal domain with respect
to a chosen microstructural feature.

Subsequently, P-SERVEs are established with respect to a few
global and local material properties from evolving variables in
dislocation density-based crystal plasticity FE (DD-CPFE) simula-
tions. While other methods like discrete dislocation dynamics may
be more suited for modeling at the lower scales, the current ca-
pabilities of the DD-CPFEM make them the best candidates for
modeling the large heterogeneous g /C0 g0 domains. The global
properties correspond to the yield strength and hardening rate,
while the local variables include the effective plastic strain, mobile
dislocation density and the maximum resolved shear stress. It is
observed that the convergence with respect to the global properties
occurs in the vicinity of z 10e20 precipitates for relative error
bounds of 5%. When local variables are considered, a higher num-
ber of precipitates with Npz150 /C0 200 becomes necessary for
convergence. Spatially-averaged quantities converge quicker than
the local distributions, independent of whether the quantity is
microstructure or property-based. Reasonable agreement is seen
between averaged morphological and response variables that are
obtained by the M-SERVE and P-SERVE process and from the
literature.
The convergence characteristics of the M-SERVE and P-SERVE
can generally be used to delineate the relation between morpho-
logical characteristics and certain response functions in the
microstructure. The SERVE and parametric representations in this
paper are useful for developing parametrically homogenized crys-
tal plasticity constitutive relations that will be discussed in future
publications.
