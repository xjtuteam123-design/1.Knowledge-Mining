ABSTRACT 
For a refractory Nb-Ti-Zr-Cr alloy system, we show that the effect of increasing chemical complexity, which is 
accepted as the main source for unique properties of high-entropy alloys, on the strength and oxidation resistance 
is sensitive to the particular elements added. A transition from pure Nb to NbTi and then to NbTiZr alloys that 
retained a single-phase body-centred cubic (bcc) structure improved the ambient-to-high-temperature strength 
and oxidation resistance at 1000

After decades of stagnation in elaborating refractory alloys for high- 
temperature structural applications [1] , the interest in these materials 
was rejuvenated in 2010, when refractory complex concentrated alloys 
(RCCAs) were introduced [2] . Two first compositions with a 
single-phase body-centred cubic (bcc) structure, namely, NbMoTaW and 
VNbMoTaW, which demonstrated extraordinary strength at 1600 
◦
C, 
claimed a great promise of this new alloying paradigm to design alloys 
capable to replace commercial nickel-based superalloys [3] . 
Many works highlight that the unique properties of RCCAs originate 
from their chemical complexity [4 – 7] . For instance, a transition from 
pure Nb to quinary HfNbTaTiZr alloy is accompanied by an approxi -
mately tenfold increase in the room-temperature yield strength due to 
solid solution strengthening [7] . Besides, most Cr- and/or Al-containing 
RCCAs are more oxidation-resistant than commercial refractory alloys 
due to the formation of complex oxides [ 5 , 8 ]. However, some works also 
show that maximising the number of constitutive elements in RCCAs 
does not always provide the best performance [9 – 14] . Specifically, 
ternary HfNbTa alloy appears to be ~ 25 % stronger than quaternary 
HfNbTaTi alloy [12] , while single-phase bcc NbTiZr alloy achieves 
higher 1000 
◦
C-strength than dual-phase NbTiZrV, NbTiZrVTa, and 
NbTiZrVAl
0.24 
counterparts [11] . Possibly, the most surprising findings 
that make controversial the strive to increase the chemical complexity 
can be found in the Laves phase-containing R СС As. Their strength and 
oxidation resistance degrade abruptly when transitioning from binary 
CrNb to ternary CrNbTi and further to quaternary CrNbTaTi alloys [ 9 , 
10 ]. 
Such diverse data on the structure-property relations of RCCAs, 
especially those containing Laves phases, the most abundant in -
termetallics, with different levels of chemical complexity rise the need 
for more detailed and systematic studies on different alloys. Thus, the 
aim of the current study was to advance the understanding of the effect 
of chemical composition on the overall performance of Nb-Ti-Zr-Cr 
RCCAs through systematic investigation of the structure, mechanical, 
and oxidation responses of pure Nb, NbTi, and NbTiZr alloys upon ad -
ditions of Cr, which can (i) induce the formation of a Laves phase [ 11 , 
15 – 19 ], (ii) improve the strength [ 11 , 16 , 17 , 19 , 20 ], and (iii) protect a 
material against oxygen attack [21] .

To conclude, our work highlights that, for Nb-Ti-Zr-Cr RCCAs, 
maximising the number of elements is a promising strategy to design of 
high-strength and oxidation-resistant yet only single-phase solid solu -

NbTiZr alloys (or more complex counterparts (Fig. S6)), that preserves 
the single-phase bcc structure, increases the room-to-high-temperature 
strength and provides a fair protection against oxygen attack. Howev -
