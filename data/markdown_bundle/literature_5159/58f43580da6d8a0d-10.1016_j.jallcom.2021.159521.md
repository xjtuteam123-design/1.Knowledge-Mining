abstract 
Duplex phase Al 0.25FeMnNiCrCu0.5 high entropy alloy (HEA) was fabricated successfully from elemental 
powder blends through a Laser Melting Deposition (LMD) process. The structural characteristics and tensile 
properties of the alloy have been studied by XRD, optical microscope (OM), scanning electron microscope 
(SEM), transmission electron microscope (TEM), and tensile test. The alloy consists of (FCC1 + FCC2 + BCC) 
phases with lattice parameters of 3.62 Å, 3.58 Å and 2.85 Å, respectively. The yield strength, tensile strength 
and elongation of LAM Al 0.25FeMnNiCrCu0.5 at room temperature are 636 MPa, 1052 MPa and 24.5%, 
respectively. The LMD Al 0.25FeMnNiCrCu0.5 alloy presents an outstanding combination of high tensile 
strength and good ductility.

1. Introduction 
In recent years, high entropy alloy (HEA), also known as multi- 
element alloy [1,2], has attracted interest in research and application 
because of its outstanding properties such as excellent mechanical 
properties [3], high wear - corrosion resistance [4,5], and excellent 
magnetic properties [6]. Arc-melting and casting are the most popular 
method for preparing HEA [7]. However, this conventional method has 
disadvantages that it is challenging to produce a component with 
complex geometries, and the low cooling rate easily leads to the che
-
mical compositional segregation in HEAs. Currently, the advanced Ad-
ditive Manufacturing (AM) technique has been used in manufacturing 
HEAs [8,9]. AM technique used in the field of 3D printing builds objects 
by adding materials layer by layer. AM technique has many advantages 
compared to traditional manufacturing methods, including a rapid so
-
lidification cooling rate to avoid the phenomenon of segregation, and 
the ability to produce complex parts. Laser melting deposition (LMD) is 
a method widely used in the AM technique to manufacture components 
from metal powders. During the LMD process, the metal powders are 
transported to the laser beam focus area by the powder feeding system, 
then melted and rapidly solidified to form a layer of material on the 
substrate [10,11]. The next material layer is deposited with a laser head 
movement that follows the pre-designed 3D geometry, which finally 
forms the 3D components. As a result, the LMD method is an effective 
approach in the 3D printing field due to the ability to fabricate complex 
structural parts and flexible powder feeding [12]. 
The HEA system manufactured quite popularly by AM technology is 
a single-phase face-centered cubic (FCC) - CoCrFeMnNi alloy system 
(also known as Cantor alloy) [13–16]. Xiang et al. [16] used the LMD 
technique to make a CrMnFeCoNi HEA. The laser power affects the 
mechanical properties of the sample, with 1400 W getting the best 
mechanical properties. The yield strength, ultimate tensile strength, and 
elongation at room temperature are 290 MPa, 535 MPa, 55%, respec
-
tively. Besides, there have been studies on other alloy systems. For ex-
ample, Huang et al. [17] built the AlxCoCrFeNi HEAs by LMD method, 
and the results show that with Al addition the LMD-AlxCoCrFeNi HEAs 
transformed from a single FCC phase to duplex FCC/BCC phase, and then 
to BCC phase. The yield strength, tensile strength, plasticity of duplex 
phase Al
0.7CoCrFeNi alloy are 771 MPa, 1171 MPa, and 11%, respectively. 
Luo et al. [18] used the selective laser melting (SLM) technique to make 
(BCC + B2) phase AlCrCuFeNi alloy system, resulting in a compression 
strength that can reach 2052.8 MPa, and a fracture strain of 6.8%. Thus 
in terms of mechanical properties, FCC phase HEAs show good ductility, 
however the strength values are not enough, while BCC phase HEAs 
have high strength but low ductility. For the HEAs to achieve high

The majority of research fabricating HEAs by the AM process uses 
pre-alloyed powders like starting feedstock due to an improved 
homogeneity of parts. However, the ability to make HEAs simply by 
elemental powder blends may create the potential benefits for re

searches to manufacture powder quickly and fabricate HEAs in situ 
in a cost-efficient way. Recently bulk samples of HEA systems have 
been produced in situ by the use of elemental powder blends. For 
instance, TiZrNbTa [24], CoCrCuFeNiAl [25], Al
xCoCrFeNi [26,27], 
Al0.5CrMoNbTa0.5 [28], CoCrFeMnNi [13]. In this study, we first used 
the LMD technique to fabricate thin wall Al 0.25FeMnNiCrCu0.5 HEA

characteristics, phase identification, and tensile properties. The 
present work aims to examine the feasibility of LMD deposited 
(FCC+BCC) duplex phase HEA by simple elemental powder blends. 
The resultant properties of the component created using powder 
mixture are still not fully understood. However, an interesting result 
is obtained that the LMD Al
0.25FeMnNiCrCu0.5 HEA has good manu-
facturable and excellent comprehensive mechanical properties. 
2. Experiments 
The high purity metal powders Al, Cu, Cr, Fe, Mn, Ni (> 99.95%) 
with particle size 50–150 mm were used as raw materials in the LMD 
process. The metal powder weighed following the molar ratio of 
Al0.25FeMnNiCrCu0.5 alloy. Subsequently, the powder mixture was 
blended uniformly in a planetary milling machine with a speed of 
300 rpm, and the mixing time is 24 h. The morphology of the six 
metal powders and the powder mixture shown in Fig. 1. The AISI 304 
stainless steel plates with dimensions of 150 mm × 50 mm × 10 mm 
were used as substrate materials, which were removed surface 
oxides by SiC paper and cleaned by acetone. 
The Al
0.25FeMnNiCrCu0.5 HEA samples were produced by a 
YLR-1000-MM-WC fiber laser system with a wavelength of 1070 nm, 
and a YASKAWA DX200 6-axis robot. The powder feeding system 
used to feed powders, and the powder mixture provided using four 
coaxial nozzles with the shielding of argon gas. Thin wall LMD 
specimens with a length of 60 mm, a height of 20 mm, were de
-
posited by multi-layer scanning (schematic in Fig. 2a). After a series 
of optimization experiments, the main parameters of LMD processes 
chosen as follows: laser power 800 W, scanning speed 2 mm/s, laser 
beam diameter 3 mm, powder feeding rate 3.5 g/min. The height of 
the laser head has risen to 0.5 mm after a single laser scan. 
The phase component of samples was examined by X-ray dif
-
fraction (XRD 6100-Japan) with Cu - Ka, scanning angle of 250-850 at 
a scan rate of 2 0/min. The XRD analysis was carried out on the side 
surfaces of the thin wall LMD sample. The microstructure was ob -
served under Zeiss AxioVert A1 Optical Microscope (OM) and 
Scanning Electron Microscope (SEM - FEI Quanta 250 F) with X-ray 
Energy Dispersive Spectrometer (EDS). For microstructural ob -
servation, the samples were ground with SiC paper to 1200, me -
chanically polished and then etched in a mixture of HNO 3 - HCl - 
CH3COOH - H2O solution. 
Transmission Electron Microscope (TEM- FEI Technai 20) was 
also used to further identify the phase of LMD samples. The TEM 
samples were mechanically ground to a thickness of about 50 µm, 
pierced into circular disks of 3 diameter mm, and then the electro
-
lytic thinning carried out by a twin-jet electronic polishing with a

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Phase identification 
Fig. 3 shows the XRD analysis result of the LMD Al
0.25FeMnNiCrCu0.5 
HEA. It can be seen that the LMD sample has a duplex FCC/BCC phase. 
There are typical diffraction peaks of the FCC phase at the 2θ angle 
positions of approximately 43.50, 50.50, and 74.60 [11]. On the other 
hand, at those positions, there are two adjacent peaks. This phenom-
enon may indicate that there are two types of FCC phases appearing in 
the alloys. The diffraction peaks at the 2θ angle positions of approxi-
mately 45.160, 65.420, and 82.650 are known as the BCC phase [29,30]. 
Thus, through the XRD profiles, we can make a preliminary conclusion 
that the phase component of this alloy comprises (FCC1 + FCC2 + BCC) 
phase. This result was also recorded on Al0.3CrCuFeMnNi HEA in pub-
lished research [31]. The lattice parameters of the FCC1, FCC2, BBC 
phases of the LMD sample are 3.62 Å, 3.58 Å, 2.85 Å, respectively. 
3.2. Microstructure characterization 
Fig. 2b shows the macro image of a thin wall Al0.25FeMnNiCrCu0.5 
sample produced by LMD. It can be observed that the deposited LMD 
sample is without macro-defects. However, on the sample's side 
surfaces, there are small spherical powders adhered to their surface. 
This phenomenon is due to the high laser power's influence, which 
makes the temperature of the sides of the deposited sample still 
high enough after the laser beam scanning. For that reason, the 
powders dropping on the sides get sintered.

%), which is identified to be the BCC phase. 
In addition, EDS results show that the element Mn and Al content 
is lower than the nominal design. As shown in Fig. 1, the Mn powder 
exists some particles bigger in size than other powders and has a 
polygon morphology. During the LMD process, large particles of Mn 
powder can get clogged up at the powder feeding system and noz
-
zles, leading to the uneven chemical composition of the powder 
mixture at the melting pool. This probably reduced the fraction of 
Mn in the deposited sample and higher content of other elements 
such as Fe, Ni, and Cr. In the powder mixture, Al and Mn powders 
with lower evaporation temperature easily evaporate under the 
impact of a high-energy laser beam, resulting in a decrease in Al and 
Mn contents. 
In general, combining the results of XRD, SEM-EDS and TEM 
analysis, it can be concluded that LMD Al
0.25FeMnNiCrCu0.5 HEA has 
a two-phase FCC + one phase BCC structure. The results achieved can 
confirm that the use of elemental powder blends with the in-situ 
processing to produce Al
0.25FeMnNiCrCu0.5 HEA via the LMD tech -
nique is entirely feasible.

value of 24.5%. Compare with duplex phase SLM AlCrCuFeNi3.0 HEA [34] 
and as-cast eutectic HEA (EHEA) [21,35], the tensile properties of the 
duplex phase LMD Al0.25FeMnNiCrCu0.5 HEA reveals superiority. 
As mentioned above, the microstructure of LMD Al0.25FeMnNiCrCu0.5 
mostly comprises a mixture of (FCC1 + FCC2) phases with the BCC phase 
distributed on the FCC matrix phase. Thus, the alloy's tensile properties 
present an impressive combination between ductility (elongation ap-
prox 24.5%) and ultimate tensile strength (UTS approx 1052 MPa), which 
can be explained through structural characteristics. With a large amount 
of soft, ductile FCC phase contained in the alloy will ensure a high degree 
of ductility at high strength, and nano-scale hard BCC particles pre -
cipitated inside the FCC matrix can enhance the alloy's strength by 
preventing the movement of dislocation lines. Previous research results 
for eutectic HEAs, for example, Fe
20Co20Ni41Al19 alloy [35], AlCoCrFeNi2.1 
alloy [36] have also shown that the primary mechanism for the high 
ductility and high strength of these alloys is the pairing between the 
hard BCC phase and soft FCC phase. 
In addition, the as-built Al
0.25FeMnNiCrCu0.5 HEA exhibits 
notably heterogeneous structures with a mixture of (FCC+BCC) 
phase microstructure. Thus, the back stress strengthening me
-
chanism may be significantly contributing to improving mechanical 
properties. This mechanism has been widely reported in hetero -
geneous materials [34,37,38]. In present work, the existence of de -
formation behavior difference between the soft FCC phase and hard 
BCC nano-scale phase can cause the inhomogeneous deformation. 
During plastic deformation, the inhomogeneous deformation surely 
occurs at the domain boundaries, leads to formation strain gradient 
across boundaries, and thus appears a back stress. The back stress 
effect will prevent necking during tensile testing, thus improving the 
strength and ductility [38]. In conclusion, the LMD fabricated 
Al
0.25FeMnNiCrCu0.5 HEA exhibits heterogeneous structures con -
sisting of soft FCC and hard BCC phases, leading to superior combi -
nations of the strength and ductility.

4. Conclusion 
The thin wall Al0.25FeMnNiCrCu0.5 HEA was prepared by the LMD 
technique with blended elemental powder. The phase composition 
of the alloy is composed of two FCC structure and one BCC structure 
phases. The LMD Al 0.25FeMnNiCrCu0.5 HEA sample has excellent 
tensile properties with the yield strength, ultimate tensile strength, 
and elongation of 636 MPa, 1052 MPa, and 24.5%, respectively. The 
remarkable in this research is that the use of elemental powder 
blends can successfully produce low-cost Al
0.25FeMnNiCrCu0.5 HEA 
with a reasonably good balance of ductility and tensile strength. This 
opens up the potential of using the elemental powder blends for 
manufacturing HEA using AM technology. The as-built 
Al0.25FeMnNiCrCu0.5 HEA has a promising potential to be used for 
structural materials for engineering applications.
