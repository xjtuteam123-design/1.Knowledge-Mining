a b s t r a c t 
Normal strengthening methods through precipitations and deformation obviously enhance the strength 
of metallic materials while resulting in the sacriﬁce of ductility, and synergistic improvement of strength 
and ductility is currently an urgent requirement. Herein we developed a cryogenic deformation combined 
with an annealing method to fabricate CoCrNiMo 0.2 medium entropy alloy, which achieved an ultrahigh 
strength of 1.8 GPa with synergistic improvement in strength and ductility. Microstructure, mechanical 
performance, and strengthening mechanisms of the developed alloys were investigated compared with 
that prepared by the regular room temperature deformation method. It was found that high-density nan- 
otwins were produced in CoCrNiMo 0.2 MEA via cryogenic deformation. Fine grains, hard precipitations, 
and high volume fraction of nanotwins greatly strengthened the alloy, obtaining a yield and ultimate 
tensile strength of 1400 MPa and 1800 MPa. Ductility improvement of the developed alloy was mainly 
attributed to the production of deformation nanotwins due to the lower stacking fault energy, which 
greatly increases the dislocation storage ability, and thus, the ductility of the alloy was enhanced.

1. Introduction 
The development of structural materials combined high 
strength with excellent ductility and toughness is a perpetual 
challenge. The strength of pure metals is insuﬃcient, despite their 
excellent plasticity, which is diﬃcult to fulﬁll for engineering 
applications. Normally, the hard precipitated particles and nan- 
otwins are two popular strengthening methods, which have been 
widely adopted to strengthen pure metal structure materials and 
have received popular attention [1–6] . The former can effectively 
hinder the plastic deformation to produce high-density disloca- 
tions (HDDs), and signiﬁcantly improve the strength, whereas the 
ductility will be seriously sacriﬁced [ 2 , 3 , 7 ]. However, the latter can 
not only enhance the strength by blocking the movement of dislo- 
cations but also promote the development of plastic deformation 
through the shear force of nanotwins and thus improve the duc-

the mechanical properties of materials [ 2 , 8 ]. Currently, this com-

Therefore, producing high-density nanotwins in face-centered 
cubic (FCC) structured H/MEA that enriched hard precipitated 
particles is uniquely suited to exceptionally enhance strength, 
ductility, and toughness. This developed and proposed idea is an 
ideal strengthening method to achieve synergistic enhancement of 
strength and ductility. 
Differ from traditional alloys consisting of 1–2 primary ele- 
ments, a recently developed novel concept, named high/medium 
entropy alloys (H/MEAs), composed of at least 3 dominant ele- 
ments in an equal or near equal atomic ratio, which is considered 
to be a promising material due to many excellent properties, 
such as high strength, excellent ductility and toughness, high 
work hardening ability, and so on [ 2 , 7 , 11 , 12 ]. Surprisingly, H/MEAs 
usually tend to form a simple crystalline structure due to the high

conﬁgurational entropy rather than multiple phases or complex 
intermetallic compounds, such as FCC, body-centered cubic (BCC), 
hexagonal close-packed (HCP) microstructures [13–16] . Among 
the various M/HEAs, the FCC-structured H/MEAs have garnered 
considerable attention in recent years due to their interesting 
structures and high mechanical properties. However, the strength 
of FCC-structured H/MEAs is insuﬃcient for practical structural 
application, although its ductility is quite outstanding [ 15 , 17 , 18 ]. 
Precipitation strengthening has been attempted to enhance 
strength, but this approach is accompanied by a severe sacriﬁce 
of ductility. Many alloying elements, such as Mo [ 3 , 7 , 19–22 ], W 
[ 23 , 24 ], Ta [ 2 , 25 , 26 ], Nb [ 27 , 28 ], etc., have been introduced into 
FCC-structured H/MEAs producing varies hard precipitation phase 
to improve their strength, while their plasticity is severely reduced. 
Fortunately, the production of high-density nanotwins can pro- 
vide greater development space for plastic deformation. It is worth 
noting that the FCC-structured H/MEAs have lower stacking fault 
energy (SFE) in comparison to traditional alloys due to the in- 
creased conﬁgurational entropy [ 6 , 29 ]. For example, the SFE of the 
CoCrFeMnNi and CoCrFeNi HEAs is 27–30 mJ/m 2 [29] . The SFE of 
CoCrNi MEA is only 18–22 mJ/m 2 [12] . Meanwhile, it has demon- 
strated that deformation mechanisms of HEAs vary with decreasing 
SFE from only dislocation slip to dislocation slip plus mechanical 
twinning. Thus, SFE reduction provides more convenient conditions 
for the production of nanotwins and plays an important role in im- 
proving properties. Moreover, the SFE of FCC-structured H/MEAs 
has a powerful dependence on deformation temperature and the 
components. On the one hand, the SFE is intrinsically related to 
temperature-sensitive deformation behavior [ 6 , 30 ]. Recent reports 
for an equiatomic CoCrNi MEA have revealed that the SFE drops 
as temperature decreases [ 12 , 31 ]. At lower deformation tempera- 
tures, the reduced SFE further promotes the tendency of twinning 
and nanotwins are signiﬁcantly activated compared to room tem- 
perature [ 6 , 32 , 33 ]. On the other hand, the SFE can be effectively 
tailored via appropriate adjusting of the chemical composition of 
the alloys caused by deformation temperature [34] . Previous re- 
searches have shown that Cr elements not only can reduce SFE 
but also promote the formation of the hard second phase [ 35 , 36 ]. 
The content of Cr in the precipitated phase is unchanged. Thus, 
the content change of Cr element in FCC phase is determined by 
the content of the precipitated phase. The higher the content of 
the precipitated phase, the more Cr element is used, which would 
result in the content reduction of Cr element in FCC phase, and 
thus, the SFE would be reduced signiﬁcantly. Consequently, adjust- 
ing the modulation ratio of Cr element in the precipitated and FCC 
phases through a change in deformation temperature may change 
the deformation mechanism of the HEAs, thereby inﬂuencing their 
mechanical properties. 
Motivated by the above concerns, the high-density nanotwins 
with different volume fractions were tailored in hard nanopre- 
cipitation strengthened CoCrNiMo 0.2 MEA via different deforma- 
tion temperatures and the modulation ratio of Cr element, which 
achieved the strength and ductility trade-off. Meanwhile, the ef- 
fect of high-density nanotwins on the mechanical properties and 
strengthening mechanism of developed CoCrNiMo 0.2 MEA enriched 
with hard nanoprecipitated particles was investigated through a 
comparative investigation. 
2. Experimental methods 
Two as-cast CoCrNiMo 0.2 MEAs were fabricated via arc melting 
in the high-purity argon (Ar) atmosphere. The purity of raw mate- 
rials (Co, Cr, Ni, and Mo, about 3–5 mm particles) was more than 
99.95 wt.%. In order to ensure the uniformity of chemical compo- 
sition, the CoCrNiMo 0.2 MEAs were melted 5 times repeatedly and 
turned over before each melted. Next, they were homogenized at 
1200 °C for 3 h in the Ar atmosphere, followed by water quenching 
to room temperature. Further, the two MEAs were rolled at dif- 
ferent temperatures, respectively. The one was deformed at room 
temperature, which was marked as M 0.2 -KR MEA. About 90% total 
thickness reduction of M 0.2 -KR MEA was performed and per thick- 
ness reduction was controlled at about 0.2–0.3 mm/time. For an- 
other one, it was deformed in cryogenic temperature and marked 
as M 0.2 -YDR MEA. For M 0.2 -YDR MEA, its deformation tempera- 
ture was controlled at about –196 °C. Before the ﬁrst rolling de- 
formation, the alloy was completely submerged in liquid nitrogen 
for 10 min in order to ensure –196 °C was reached from the out- 
side to the heart, after which, the completely liquid nitrided al- 
loy was rolled two times. After the ﬁrst rolling deformation, the 
later submerged time was controlled for 5 min every time. After 
each submerges, two times rolling deformation was carried out. 
The per thickness reduction was set as 0.1–0.3 mm/time, and about 
90% total thickness reduction was performed, which was the same 
as M 0.2 -KR MEA. (Noted, the alloy has the more resistant ability 
for deformation due to a harder cryogenic environment. There- 
fore, even if the ﬁxed deformation was controlled, the actual de- 
formation was smaller than that at room temperature under the 
same conditions. Thus, more times should be carried out at cryo- 
genic deformation compared with room temperature deformation.) 
Finally, the deformed two sheets were encapsulated in Ar atmo- 
sphere and heated to 800 °C with a rate of 9 °C/min and were 
annealed at 800 °C for 4 h cooling with the furnace to 400 °C. 
In order to deﬁne the crystal structure, X-ray diffraction (XRD) 
was employed for two developed MEAs in a Bruker D8 Advance 
system with Cu K α1 radiation. The scan range was from 20 ° to 90 °
and the scan rate was 2 °/min. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM, 
Hitachi S-480 0, 10 0 kV) was carried out for the detailed micro- 
morphology of two developed MEAs and their chemical compo- 
nents were conﬁrmed by an energy dispersive spectrometer (EDS). 
Transmission electron microscopy (TEM, TECNAI G2 F30) obser- 
vations were performed with an accelerating voltage of 300 kV. 
The chemical components of the FCC phase were tested via scan- 
ning transmission electron microscopy (STEM-EDS) and 5 points 
were tested to ensure accuracy. /Phi13 mm TEM specimens were 
prepared by grinding polished down to a thickness of 0.1 mm. 
Then, they were electro-polished to an electron-transparent thick- 
ness via a twin jet electro-polishing system in the electrolyte of 
90% CH 3 CH 2 OH and 10% HClO 4 at 25 °C (room temperature). In or- 
der to quantify the volume fraction of the nanotwins, the electron 
back-scattered diffraction (EBSD, Hitachi S-4800) was performed 
with a beam size of 20 nm and a step size of 0.1 nm. Before 
the performance, the surfaces of the two developed MEAs were 
electro-polished for 5 s at 20 V/25 °C (room temperature) with 90% 
CH 3 CH 2 OH and 10% HClO 4 as electrolytes. 
The plate-like specimens with a gauge dimension of 13 
mm × 2.0 mm × 1.2 mm for tensile tests were performed at room 
temperature (universal tensile testing machine, SUST CMT5105GL) 
with length direction parallel to the rolling direction without ex- 
tensometer due to sampling size limitation. The strain rate was set 
to 5 × 10 –4 /s. Three tensile specimens were measured to obtain 
reliable results. After tensile tests, the TEM of M 0.2 -YDR MEA was 
carried out near the fracture for the detailed deformation behavior.

3.2. Mechanical properties of M 0.2 -KR and M 0.2 -YDR MEAs 
In order to explore the mechanical properties of two devel- 
oped MEAs deformed under different deformation temperatures, 
the representative tensile engineering stress–strain curves of two 
MEAs are shown in Fig. 5 (a). As seen, the yield strength (YS), ulti- 
mate tensile strength (UTS), and fracture elongation (FE) of M 0.2 - 
KR MEA were about 1270 MPa, 1544 MPa, and ∼11.5%, respec- 
tively. Compared with M 0.2 -KR MEA, M 0.2 -YDR MEA not only ex- 
hibits higher YS and UTS values of 1402 MPa and 1775 MPa but 
also a larger FE of ∼13.75%. On the whole, M 0.2 -YDR MEA exhibits 
better strength and ductility than M 0.2 -KR MEA. Furthermore, in 
order to better understand the work-hardening effect of two MEAs 
during the tensile deformation, the strain-hardening rate (SHR) is 
the response in Fig. 5 (b). The SHR curves suggested intuitively that 
the M 0.2 -YDR MEA contained the higher SHR, which was due to the 
increments of solid-solution strengthening, dislocation strengthen- 
ing, and second phase strengthening according to Ref. [21] . Note 
that there are several strain-hardening rate platforms in the M 0.2 - 
KR MEA, which implies that a high strain-hardening rate has been 
maintained at a certain strain range [7] . According to that the 
strain hardening rate is proportional to the increased speed dur- 
ing straining, in the precipitation-hardened HEAs, on the one hand, 
precipitates pin the dislocations and promote the increase of dis- 
location density. On the other hand, potentially act as a disloca- 
tion emission source and again promote the dislocation density in- 
crease. Therefore, the work hardening rate of the M 0.2 -YDR HEA 
is higher than that of the M 0.2 -KR HEA at the uniform defor- 
mations stage [37] . In addition, the fracture morphologies of two 
MEAs illustrated a large number of dimples ( Fig. 5 (b, c)), indicat- 
ing a typical ductile fracture mechanism. M 0.2 -YDR MEA exhibited 
larger dimples, which corresponds to better FE [37] . Fig. 5 (d) il- 
lustrates the fracture elongation-ultimate tensile strength compar- 
ison of different CoCrNi-based M/HEAs [ 11 , 12 , 21 , 24 , 38–40 ]. It can 
be seen that the CoCrNiMo 0.2 MEA in this work outperforms other 
CoCrNi-based M/HEAs. Nevertheless, structural materials that com- 
bine high strength and excellent ductility still need to be further 
explored.

nucleation and containment dislocations, which increases the ca- 
pacity of the TBs to absorb containment dislocations, resulting 
in increased plasticity. Dislocation can also react with nanotwins,

4. Conclusions 
In summary, as a new method, the high-density nan- 
otwins were introduced into hard nanoprecipitation strengthened 
CoCrNiMo 0.2 MEA by cryogenic deformation and composition ad- 
justment. A synergistic increase in strength and ductility was 
achieved via this new method. The strength enhancement by the 
hard precipitated particles and nanotwins, and the ductility en- 
hancement mechanism dominated by deformation nanotwins were 
investigated. The main conclusions are as follows. 
(1) High-density nanotwins were produced due to the cryogenic 
deformation and composition adjustment to signiﬁcantly re- 
duce the stacking fault energy. The volume fraction of nan- 
otwins in M 0.2 -YDR MEA reached 43.5%, while the volume frac- 
tion of nanotwins in M 0.2 -KR MEA was only 31%. 
(2) Compared with room temperature deformation, the strength 
and fracture elongation of the alloy deformed at cryogenic tem- 
peratures were signiﬁcantly improved. The yield strength and 
ultimate tensile strength were signiﬁcantly increased from 1270 
MPa to 1405 MPa, and 1544 MPa to 1775 MPa. The fracture 
elongation was increased from 19.5% to 23%. 
(3) The enhancement of strength is mainly attributed to the hard 
precipitated particles and high volume fraction of annealing 
nanotwins. The improvement in ductility can be contributed to 
the severe deformation of the nanowires themselves and the 
generation of deformation nanotwins.
