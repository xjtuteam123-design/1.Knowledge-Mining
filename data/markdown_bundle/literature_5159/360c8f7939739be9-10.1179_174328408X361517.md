Oxidation resistant Ru containing Ni base
single crystal superalloys
K. Kawagishi 1, A. Sato 1,2, H. Harada *1, A.-C. Yeh 1, Y. Koizumi 1 and
T. Kobayashi 1
Oxidation resistant fourth and fifth generation Ni base superalloys were developed. The fourth
generation superalloys TMS-138A were modified by increasing the content of Al and Cr and
deceasing Mo. Designed alloys were cast as single crystal and examined in cyclic and isothermal
exposures at 1100 uC and creep rupture test. Under high temperature/low stress condition,
0?5 wt-% of Al addition was effective to improve the oxidation resistance but degrade creep
property. Cr addition and Mo and W reduction were effective to improve the oxidation resistance
and display creep property almost equal to the original TMS-138A. The fourth generation
superalloy TMS-138 and fifth generation superalloy TMS-173 were also modified. By adding Cr
and removing Mo and W, their oxidation resistances were dramatically improved. Thus it was
found that it is possible to improve the oxidation resistance of Ru containing SC superalloys with
keeping the high temperature strength by the increase in Cr and decrease in Mo and W.
Keywords: Oxidation resistance, Ni base superalloy, Single crystal, Fourth generation, Fifth generation
Dedicated to the memory of Professor Malcolm McLean
Introduction
Ru addition to Ni base superalloys improves the
stability of microstructure 1,2 and results in excellent
high temperature strength. 3–5 On the other hand, these
alloys are likely to have lower oxidation resistance than
the previous generations of superalloys due to the nature
of the alloying elements, such as Mo, Re and Ru. 6,7
Figure 1 shows the oxidation resistance of from the ﬁrst
to ﬁfth generation Ni base superalloys. The weight
decrease due to the spallation of the scale is getting
larger as the generation advances. The improvement of
high temperature oxidation resistance is one of the
important issues for practical use of these superalloys.
There are some articles about the oxidation behaviour
of conventionally cast superalloys,
8–11 ﬁrst generation
single crystal superalloys, 12,13 second generation single
crystal superalloys 14–16 and third generation single
crystal superalloys.16 However, only few attempts have
so far been made at Re and Ru containing fourth and
ﬁfth generation Ni base superalloys. 6,7 In the authors
previous research, the oxidation behaviour of Ru
containing Ni base superalloys was examined
17,18 and
it has been clariﬁed that Re and Ru deteriorate the
oxidation resistance and addition of Hf and Ta are
effective to improve the oxidation resistance. Hf affects
to the adhesiveness of the scale at the interface of the
oxide and the substrate, and Ta affects to stabilise
ruthenium and rhenium oxide in the scale as tantalum
oxide, which is actually in the form of nickel tantalum
oxide, existing in the oxide layer of Ru containing Ni
base superalloys. Nevertheless, for the practical use of
advanced generation superalloys, it goes without saying
that suppression of NiO and the spinel formation during
the oxidation is necessary.
In the present study, the development of the fourth
generation superalloy which has high oxidation resis-
tance equivalent to second generation superalloy has
been attempted. Content of Al, Cr, Mo and other
elements were drastically changed to optimise the
composition for the formation of protective oxide with
keeping the creep strength of fourth and ﬁfth generation
superalloy.

Results and discussion
Figure 2 shows the cyclic oxidation properties of speci-
mens in Table 1 at 1100 uC. TMS-138A is found to have
largest mass decrease due to the spallation in these alloys
and TMS-138A zAl shows better oxidation resistance
due to higher Al content. With increasing Cr content
and decreasing Mo content, oxidation resistances of
TMS-198 and TMS-199 are improved and its behaviour
is almost comparable with second generation superalloy
CMSX-4. Cross-sections of the oxide formed after 1 h
isothermal oxidation at 1100 uC were observed and
backscattered electrons images are displayed in Fig. 3.
TMS-138A and TMS-138A zAl form thick NiO on the
surface and internal Al
2O3 columnar oxides at the
interface. Between NiO and internal Al 2O3 layer,
NiAl2O4 spinel and composite oxide of other elements
are formed. Thickness of NiO and internal Al 2O3 of
TMS-138AzAl are thinner than TMS-138A because of
its high Al content. However, the Al content of TMS-
138AzAl is found to be insufﬁcient to form a protective
Al2O3 layer. On the other hand, TMS-198 shows
obviously different oxide from others. Al 2O3 at the
interface forms a thin layer and this protective layer may
affect to suppress NiO formation. Thus, NiO of TMS-
198 was thinner than others and spallation of NiO and
spinel in the cyclic oxidation examination was smaller
than TMS-138A and TMS-138A zAl.

Conclusions
Al, Cr and Mo modiﬁed alloys were designed for the
fourth generation Ni base superalloys TMS-138A/TMS-
138 and the ﬁfth generation Ni base superalloys TMS-
173. Alloys were examined in cyclic and isothermal
exposures at 1100 uC and creep rupture test and the
following were clariﬁed.
1. Al addition of 0 ?5 wt-% was effective to improve
the oxidation resistance but degrade creep property
under high temperature/low stress condition.
2. Cr addition and Mo and W reduction were
effective to improve the oxidation resistance and keep
creep property almost equal to original fourth and ﬁfth
generation superalloys.
