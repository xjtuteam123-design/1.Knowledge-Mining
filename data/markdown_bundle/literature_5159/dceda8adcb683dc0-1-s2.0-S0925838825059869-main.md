1. Introduction
High-entropy alloys (HEAs), composed of multiple principal ele -
ments in near-equiatomic ratios, have emerged as a revolutionary class 
of materials that offer exceptional mechanical, thermal, and chemical 
properties [1 – 5] . Among various HEA systems studied, the equiatomic 
CoCrFeMnNi alloy, also known as the Cantor alloy, has garnered sig -
nificant attention due to its single-phase face-centered cubic (FCC) 
structure and excellent ductility, fracture toughness, and corrosion 
resistance. However, despite these favorable properties, a critical limi -
tation of FCC HEAs lies in their relatively low yield strength, particularly 
in the coarse-grained, as-cast condition [6,7] . For example, as-cast 
equiatomic CoCrFeMnNi alloys typically exhibit yield strengths below 
300 MPa, which severely constrains their application in structural 
components requiring both strength and ductility [8] . Consequently, 
extensive research has focused on exploring various strengthening 
mechanisms to overcome this intrinsic limitation.
Multiple approaches have been employed to enhance the mechanical 
strength of FCC HEAs. These include grain refinement via thermo -
mechanical processing [9,10] , solid-solution strengthening through the 
introduction of substitutional solutes [11 – 15] , precipitation hardening 
via secondary phase formation [16 – 20] , and interstitial strengthening 
by the addition of light elements such as carbon or nitrogen [21,22] . 
Among these, interstitial alloying has shown promising potential due to 
its profound effect on lattice distortion, stacking fault energy (SFE), and 
phase stability. Carbon, in particular, has been widely investigated as a

matrix, and contribute significantly to precipitation hardening [26 – 28] .

subgrain structures during annealing and prevent excessive grain 
growth, thereby enhancing the Hall-Petch strengthening effect. Recent 
studies show that Nb also contributes to the refinement of lamellar 
structures during aging, leading to an improved balance of strength and 
ductility even in cryogenic environments [29] .
The aging process plays a critical role in controlling the nature, size, 
and distribution of carbides in carbon- and Nb-doped HEAs. Aging 
treatments at intermediate temperatures promote the nucleation and 
growth of M

precipitates without causing embrittlement or excessive grain coars -

increases thermal stability by anchoring dislocations and retarding grain 
boundary movement under stress or thermal cycling [29 – 32] .
The dislocation substructure evolution during deformation also dif -
fers significantly between undoped and carbon-doped HEAs. While 
wavy slip and dislocation cell formation are predominant in undoped 
alloys, carbon-doped alloys exhibit planar slip, stacking faults, and 
eventually microband formation at high strains. The synergy between 
interstitial strengthening, grain refinement, and precipitation hardening 
enables the design of alloys with high yield strengths while retaining 
adequate ductility [1,2,20,22,33,34] .

diffusing back into the solidifying dendrites. Once segregated, carbon 
exhibits strong interactions with carbide-forming elements such as Cr,

for 20 h. The formation of annealing twin boundaries (ATBs) in FCC 
HEAs, particularly carbon-containing systems, is a thermally activated 
process that occurs during the recrystallization stage following cold or 
cryogenic deformation. ATBs are typically characterized by a coherent 
Σ 3 {111} twin relationship, which has lower grain boundary energy 
compared to random HABs. This energetic advantage promotes the 
nucleation and growth of ATBs during annealing, contributing to the 
development of refined and thermally stable grain structures. Several 
mechanisms have been proposed to explain the formation of annealing 
twins. Among them, three are widely accepted: (i) growth faults, (ii) 
overlapping stacking faults (SFs), and (iii) boundary dissociation. 
Growth faults arise from local disruptions in the stacking sequence 
during grain growth, leading to mirror symmetry across the faulted re -
gion. Overlapping stacking faults form when Shockley partial disloca -
tions glide successively on adjacent {111} planes, generating coherent 
twin lamellae. This is the most commonly accepted atomic-scale 
mechanism in FCC systems. Boundary dissociation refers to the local 
transformation of high-angle grain boundaries into twin boundaries, 
particularly when grain boundary segments encounter favorable crys -
tallographic orientations or stress fields. The motion of Shockley partial 
dislocations is central to twin formation. These dislocations can be 
activated by shear stress during recovery or recrystallization and prop -
agate across grains along {111} planes. When sequentially emitted from 
a grain boundary ledge, they result in the formation of a coherent twin. 
Importantly, the twinning process does not require long-range atomic 
rearrangements but is governed by the localized movement of partials, 
making it particularly favorable in low to moderate stacking fault energy 
(SFE) alloys [62] . The addition of interstitial elements such as carbon 
has been shown to enhance annealing twin formation. Segregation of 
carbon atoms at partial dislocations and twin boundaries enhances their 
thermal stability by impeding detwinning processes [1,2] . Recent 
studies have shown that increasing carbon content in CoCrFeMnNi HEAs 
leads to a simultaneous reduction in grain size and an increase in 
annealing twin boundary density. Carbide precipitation, especially of

This study investigates the effects of Nb addition and subsequent 
aging on the microstructure and mechanical properties of FeMn

Co

1. Nb-free alloy (FeMn

a small volume fraction of nanoscale spherical Cr-rich carbides, as 
confirmed by TEM and EDS. These finely dispersed precipitates 
contribute to enhance mechanical performance through the precip -
itation strengthening mechanism.
2. After aging, the YS increases from 300 MPa to 370 MPa, and the UTS 
improves from 600 MPa to 800 MPa, while maintaining good elon -
gation (~30 %), highlighting a balanced strength – ductility synergy.
3. The Nb-containing alloy formed eutectic Nb
2

ing aging transformed into well-defined, aligned lamellar structures, 
substantially increasing the carbide volume fraction from ~14 % to 
~19 % and eliminating the ε -phase, resulting in a cleaner matrix 
structure.
4. Mechanical testing confirmed that Nb addition markedly improved 
yield strength (568 MPa) and ultimate tensile strength (870 MPa), 
while maintaining high ductility (~51 %), primarily due to the 
tailored carbide morphology and refined microstructure.
In summary, controlled precipitation behavior, nanoscale spherical 
Cr-carbides in the Nb-free alloy vs. coarsened lamellar Nb ₂ C carbides in 
the Nb-containing alloy, offers distinct pathways to strengthen FeMn -
CoCrC HEAs. While the Nb-free alloy favors ductility through fine 
dispersion strengthening, the Nb-containing variant achieves higher 
strength via lamellar carbide coarsening and ε -phase elimination. These 
insights provide design guidelines for tailoring mechanical performance 
in carbon-containing HEAs via carbide engineering and thermal 
processing .
