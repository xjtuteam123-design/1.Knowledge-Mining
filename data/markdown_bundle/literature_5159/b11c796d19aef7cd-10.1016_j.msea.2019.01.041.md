Abstract 
Dual-phase Al 0.5CoCrFeMnNi high-entropy alloy consisted of face -centered cubic (FCC) and body -
centered cubic (BCC) phases alloy exhibited enhancement of both strength and strain hardening 
ability at 77 K. It resulted from back stress hardening in high work hardening due to large strength 
difference of two constituent phases with decreasing temperature. 
 
Keywords: High-entropy alloy, Dual-phase, Strain partitioning, Cryogenic temperature 
 
 
1. INTRODUCTION

## Page 3

2 
 
In the past decade, high-entropy alloys (HEAs) has been attracting significant attention all over the 
world, which is composed of multiple principal elements forming simple phase due to their high 
configurational entropy [1,2]. In particular, single face–centered cubic (FCC) structured HEAs (e.g., 
CoCrFeMnNi HEA) have been intensively investigated due to their excellent tensile ductility [3], 
corrosion resistance [4], and outstanding fracture toughness [5]. However, the yield strength of these 
HEAs is generally insufficient for engineering applications although their ductility is often 
superfluous. Therefore, there have been several attempts to attain not only high strength but also 
favorable ductility of the HEAs using hard secondary phases to broaden their applications [6]. 
Recent studies [7-9] reported that Al addition to FCC structured Co -Cr-Fe-Mn-Ni HEA system has 
a strong effect on the formation of AlNi -rich ordered body-centered cubic (BCC) phase and changed 
crystalline structure from single FCC to FCC + BCC structure . This type of alloys showed sharp 
increases in yield strength while sacrificing their ductility with increasing volume fraction of hard 
BCC phase similar to conventional composite behavior [8,9]. Furthermore, our previous studies 
showed that the balance of stren gth and ductility of the dual -phase HEAs could be successfully 
improved with controlling phase fractions and morphologies by thermo-mechanical process at specific 
composition [10,11]. 
Meanwhile, low temperature performance of the FCC structured HEAs has b een one of the 
fascinating properties [5,12]. However, there are limited work s on low temperature behaviors of the 
dual-phase (FCC + BCC) HEAs in detail [13,14]. For this reason, we focused on tensile behavior of 
the dual-phase Al0.5CoCrFeMnNi HEA at low temperature. The tensile tests at room temperature (RT, 
298 K) and liquid-nitrogen temperature (LNT, 77 K) were performed to present HEA with 
microstructural analysis in parallel. 
 
2. EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURE 
The Al0.5CoCrFeMnNi alloy was produced by vacuum induction melting using pure element pellets 
(purity above 99.9%). To dissolve the secondary phase for improving workability of the ingots,

## Page 4

3 
 
homogenization heat treatment was conducted for 6 h at 1200 °C [10], followed by cold rolling from 
7.0 to 1. 5 mm in thickness reduction of 78.6% . To produce the dual -phase Al 0.5CoCrFeMnNi HEA 
having uniform microstructure, annealing was performed at 1000 °C for 2 h with subsequent water 
quenching [11]. 
 X-ray diffraction (XRD) was performed with Cu Kα radiation using RIGAKU D/MAX -2500 to 
identify phase of initial samples . For the m icrostructural characterization, electron backscatter 
diffraction (EBSD) analysis was carried out using a field-emission scanning electron microscope (FE-
SEM, XL-30S FEG) with orientation imaging microscopy (OIM) software. All the EBSD specimens 
were mechanically polished up to 1 µm level with colloidal silica treatment. 
For the mechanical testing, tensile specimens having dog bone shape (gauge length of 5.0 mm × 
width of 2.5 mm × thickness of 1.5 mm ) were prepared from the as -annealed samples. The tensile 
tests were performed at a strain rate of 10 -3 s-1, and repeated at least three times at RT and LNT for 
reproducibility. A liquid-nitrogen chamber with a size of 50 × 40 × 38 cm3 was set up on the tensile 
testing machine (Instron 1361) for the cryogenic tensile tests. All the specimens were marked by 
micro-hardness trace at the end regions of the gauge length using a Vickers hardness tester (FM-700) 
to measure pre cise strains after testing. All the tensile tests were repeated at least three times for 
reproducibility. In addition, to explore microstructural evolution of the alloy during RT- and LNT-
deformations, the tensile tests were interrupted at the true strain levels of ~7%, ~12%, and ~18%, 
followed by EBSD analysis on the middle of gauge regions in the tensile specimens. After the tensile 
tests, fracture surfaces of the specimens were observed using a FE -SEM equipped with energy -
dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) detector.

The results of the tensile tests at RT and LNT are indicated in Fig. 1(c). The alloy at RT condition 
indicates yield strength (YS) of 537 MPa, tensile strength (TS) of 874 MPa, and a total elongation (T.

the YS of 796 MPa and the facture strength of 1329 MPa, whereas T. EL (30.5%) was almost retained 
as compared with the RT sample. Noticeably, the alloys were fr actured on the region of uniform 
deformation. The uniform elongation in RT-tensile curve is determined as 25.7%, which implies that 
the necking was delayed for the present HEA under the LNT -deformation. As shown in Fig. 1(d), the 
work hardening rate ( d𝜎/d𝜀, WHR) of the alloy under the LNT -deformation is much higher than that 
of the RT condition in overall deformation. Furthermore, the WHR of the alloy at the LNT nearly kept 
its value after elastic -plastic transition [15] up to the fracture while it gradua lly decreased in RT -
deformation.

From the references [14,16], the stress partitioning in both phases has been proposed as a main 
strengthening mechanism of the Al -containing dual-phase HEAs; the hard BCC phase carried higher 
stresses compared to the soft FCC phase during the plastic deformation. Therefore, the strength of the 
two phase material follows the simple rule of mixtures of soft FCC and hard BCC phases as reported 
in recent publications [8,11]. On the other hand, Abuzaid et al. [14] investigated the partitioning 
behavior of the plastic strain on the dual-phase Al13(CoCrFeNi)87 (at %) HEA. They demonstrated that 
the alloys consisting of soft FCC matrix and hard BCC phase (over the volume fraction of ~25%) 
displayed heterogeneous deformation behavior w ith significant strain accumulation in FCC grains in 
micro-scale. Furthermore, strain partitioning behavior was further enhanced by increasing volume 
fraction of hard BCC phase and decreasing temperature [14].

Figure 3 indicates the evolution of geometrical necessary dislocation (GND) density with true strain 
for each phase under RT - and LNT- deformations. Noticeably, the GND densities of the both phases 
were evolved with similar increments in parallel during the RT -deformation. On the contrary, during 
the LNT- deformation, the average GND dens ity in the BCC phase were lower than that under RT 
condition mostly. Furthermore, the GND density in the FCC phase exhibited much more active 
evolution than that of RT-deformation, and it became higher than that of the BCC phase. 
The Al-Ni rich BCC phase of the HEAs reportedly exhibits exceptionally high friction stress and 
the ex panded strain-field due to the strongly ordered structure [17]. The friction stress is generally 
associated with the temperature dependence of materials [18]; BCC metals have much stronger 
temperature dependence of the flow stress due to the higher friction stress than FCC metals. It could 
lead to severe mismatch of the strength level with high plastic incompatibility in two constituent 
phases at low temperatur e. Therefore, under the LNT-deformation, the strain gradient is much more

8 
 
severe to maintain the physical continuity, and it generates larger amount of GNDs along the phase 
boundaries in the FCC phase at the same deformation level of RT condition. 
Several studies [19-21] reported that profuse GND evolution contributes to hardening due to the 
obstacles to slip and generating a long -range back stress. It is also reported that the back stress 
hardening could provide enhancing both strain hardening capability and uniform elongation of the 
materials [20,21]. For the present results, dense GNDs by severe dislocation pile -ups near the 
FCC/BCC interfaces under the LNT-deformation can be clearly observed. Therefore, it can be inferred 
that the back stress induced hardening acts as the additional strengthening mechanism in LNT -
deformation, which leads to improved WHR and uniform deformation of the current HEA.

Figure 4 showed the fracture characteristics of the samples deformed at RT and LNT. It clearly 
shows that a number of voids were initiated after the RT -deformation. In most cases, the hard 
secondary phase acts as a precursor of mi cro-void formation in a soft matrix during tensile 
deformation [22]. As shown in Fig. 4(c), a ductile fracture mode predominates in the RT-deformed 
sample, which is attributed to formation of dimples by secondary BCC particles. On the contrary, for 
the LNT-tensile fractured specimen, intergranular fracture mode could be observed with cracking

## Page 10

9 
 
behavior of the BCC phase at the fracture surface as shown in Figs. 4(b) and (d). On the intergranular 
fracture surface, the sharply broken particles were observed as marked by the yellow boxes. From the 
EDS mapping results, these broken particles are the AlNi-rich BCC phase. In the cryogenic conditions, 
the micro-void formation and growth could be delayed by high stain hardening behavior of the alloy 
with extended uniform deformation. However, the BCC phase became significantly hard and brittle 
than that under RT conditions by decreasing temperature [17,18]. Therefore, at the last part of the 
LNT-deformation, the brittle BCC phase could not withstand its stra in due to high stress level of the 
matrix, and it was finally broken by cleavage fractures. This micro -crack within the BCC phases 
rapidly propagated to grain boundaries or FCC/BCC interfaces, which led to sudden fracture of the 
alloy when it uniformly deformed at LNT.

CONCLUSIONS 
In present study, the tensile behaviour of the dual-phase Al 0.5CoCrFeMnNi HEA at cryogenic 
temperature was investigated. The alloy showed that substantial improvement of strength at LNT with 
similar amount of total elongation at RT. It can be suggested that the significant difference of the 
strength between soft FCC matrix and hard BCC phase led to severe strain partitioning effect with 
back stress hardening of the alloy in decreasing tempera ture. However, the brittle BCC phase at the 
grain boundaries became hard to withstand the strain and finally broken at the high stress level under 
the LNT -deformation, which resulted in intergranular fracture of the alloy when it uniformly 
deformed. Nevert heless, at the LNT sample, the alloy kept the total elongation with high strain 
hardening ability as compared with those of RT condition. Our results imply that the dual-phase HEA 
has potential in cryogenic applications due to its high strength and favorab le elongation at low 
temperature.
