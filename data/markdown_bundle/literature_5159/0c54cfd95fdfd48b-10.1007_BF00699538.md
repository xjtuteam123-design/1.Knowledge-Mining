PREDICTING THE INFLUENCE OF STRUCTURE FACTORS ON THE MECHANICAL 
PROPERTIES OF HIGH-TEMPERATURE STRENGTH ALLOYS 
A. V. Logunov, N. V° Petrushin, E. A. Kuleshova, 
and Yu. M. Dolzhanskii 
UDC 669.017.3:669.14.018.44 
Strength, life at high temperatures, and other important service properties are deter- 
mined to a significant degree by the structure of alloys and its thermal stability. However, 
at present a functional relationship between the characteristics of the structure and the 
properties, which must be known for a sufficiently reliable evaluation of the properties of 
alloys, has not been established. 
At present the cybernetic approach is being used widely for predicting chemical compounds 
and new alloys with previously specified properties [1-6], such as methodsof recognitionof pat- 
terns with study [1,219 a formal statistical approach [4], and mathematical experiment planning [3- 
5]. In particular, the use of the cybernetic approach to nickel high-temperature strength 
alloys has made it possible to develop the high-temperature strength alloys TRW-NASA-VIA, 
Rene 125, and Ford-406 [3, 4, 6]. 
In relation to setting up the problem of investigation of the variable factors, use is 
made of various characteristics of chemical elements and phases, the chemical composition 
of alloys, and also physical theories and semiempirical rules. 
The purpose of this article is to establish the mathematical relationship of certain 
mechanical properties of type MAR-M200 high-temperature strength alloys to structural fac- 
tors. 
Depending upon alloying, melting method, and heat treatment the following phases may be 
formed in nickel high-temperature strength alloys: i) a nickel-base solid solution (y-phase); 
2) an Ni3AI base intermetallide phase (y'-phase); 3) carbides; 4) type o, ~, and Ni~Cr-type 
intermetallide phases; 5) boride phases; 6) phases with a bcc lattice based on solid solu- 
tions of chromium, tungsten, and molybdenum [7, 8]. 
The influence of these phases on the characteristics of strength and high-temperature 
strength is ambiguous [7]. It is known that y'-phase has a very great influence on the pro- 
perties of nickel alloys. 
The high-temperature strength of alloys strengthened by precipitates of ordered y'- 
phase coherent with the matrix depends upon the composition of this phase (degree of disper- 
sion of the particles and their volume share, form, and distribution) and the kinetics of 
change in structure and to a significant degree is determined by the thermodynamic stability 
of the (y+y') system all the way up to temperatures equal to 0.8Tmelt. 
At higher temperatures there is intense coagulation and solution of y'-phase particles 
in the y-solid solution, which leads to loss of strength of the alloys and a reduction in 
their high-temperature strength. In connection with this, an important parameter charac- 
terizing the integral thermal stability and, consequently, the effectiveness of nickel alloys 
is the temperature of solution of the y-phase, which limits the temperature area of existence 
of the heterophase structure of the alloy. 
We haveestablished [9] a general rule for nickel alloys: together with the maximum quanti- 
ty of strengthening y'-phase the most heat-resistant alloys have the maximum temperature of 
complete solution of it. In addition, the mechanism of influence of alloying elements, in 
particular chromium and cobalt, on the one hand, and tungsten, on the other, on the solubility 
of y'-phase in an alloy is different [5].

A decrease in chromium and cobalt content and an increase in tungsten content in the 
alloys leads to an increase in the temperature of complete solution of y'-phase in the solid 
solution, which corresponds to modern tendencies in alloying of high-temperature strength 
alloys [8, i0]. 
An increase in the aluminum and tungsten content and additional alloying withniobium, 
hafnium, tantalum, vanadium, and rhenium also make it possible to increase the operating 
temperature [i0]. At the same time, the increased complexity of the alloying leads to a 
change in the phase composition and microstructure of nickel alloys including the appearance 
in solidification of excess phases such as (F--~') eutectic, (Ni, Co)a(W, Cr)3C base M6C car- 
bides, and tungsten solid solution base phases [7, 8, ii]. The influence of these phases 
on the high-temperature strength of the alloys is complex and at present there are no esta- 
blished concepts on the character of this influence and the optimum quantity of excess phases 
in the alloys from the point of view of life and other properties [8]. 
We have made systematic investigations and have determined the temperature of complete 
solution of y'-phase and precipitates of the excess phases, (~--y') eutectic, Ni3WsC-type car- 
bides, and (m--W) tungsten solid solution base phases, in type MAR-M200 nickel high-tempera- 
ture strength alloys [12] melted by the method of equiaxial crystallizati~n and having dif- 
ferent chemical compositions (Table i). Also established were relationships between the 
character and degree of alloying and the phase composition of the alloys. The content of 
the alloying elements (aluminum, titanium, and niobium) was varied in accordance with the 
matrix of a type 23 full factoral experiment [13] for three variables with levels of varia- 
tion of 1.0 and 2.0% by weight for titanium, 5.8 and 6.4% by weight for aluminum, and 1.2 
and 2.4% by weight for niobium. In the investigated alloys, particles of excess phases en- 
riched with tungsten, NiaW3C and s--W, precipitate out. This may be explained by the fact 
that an increase in the content of the stronger y'-forming elements than tungsten (aluminum, 
niobium, and especially titanium) in the alloy leads to replacement of tungsten in the com- 
position of the y'-phase and precipitation of it in the form of separate phases [ii]. As a 
result the (y--y') eutectic, located in the interaxial spaces, is depleted of tungsten.

It should be noted that in the investigated alloys the precipitation of (y--y') eutectic 
is accompanied by the simultaneous precipitation of phases enriched with tungsten. This 
must be taken into consideration in analyzing Eqs. (6)-(8). 
Such an analysis of the relationships obtained makes it possible to conclude that al- 
loying leading to simultaneous precipitation of a large quantity of excess phases [~ 26%(y--y') 
and ~3% Z(~-W, Ni3W3C)] causes a decrease in life at II00°C and ductility at 20°C and an 
increase in strength at 20°C. It is known [15] that with an increase in the volume share 
of particles of a second phase, the strength of an alloy increases as a result of a decrease 
in the length of free travel of dislocations. 
The decrease in life of alloys in alloying causing simultaneous precipitation of dif- 
ferent excess phases is apparently related to the fact that the (y--y') eutectic-matrix boun- 
dary possesses an increased diffusion permeability in comparison with the alloy matrix it- 
self [17]. With a large quantity of eutectic, a significant portion of the interaxial 
spaces are occupied by a phase with increased diffusion permeability. In addition, phases 
enriched with tungsten, precipitating in the interaxial spaces, deplete their composition 
of tungsten [ii], which also must lead to a reduction in high-temperature strength of the 
alloys. 
However, it should be noted that the (7-7') eutectic--matrix interphase boundary is 
less diffusion permeable than the grain boundaries [17]. Therefore, the small quantity of 
eutectic available in the grain boundaries in the interaxial spaces "heals" the grain boun- 
daries in which failure of nickel alloys occurs under creep conditions [16]. 
The models of the influence of structure factors on the characteristics of strength and 
high-temperature strength of nickel alloys obtained in this work make it possible to predict 
their properties. In particular, for a nickel high-temperature strength alloy of the type 
of TRW-NASA-VIA a prediction was made of the characteristics of high-temperature strength 
and the temperature range of existence of y'-phase. 
The use of mathematical relationships and the computer significantly decreases the time 
for development of materials with specified properties and makes it possible to make purpose- 
ful experiments taking into consideration the influence of structure factors.

DENDRITIC SEGREGATION IN HEAT-RESISTANT NICKEL ALLOYS 
E. A. Kuleshova, E. R. Cherkasova, 
and A. V. Logunov 
UDC 669.245:620.186.2 
One of the significant characteristics of ingots is the extent of dendritic segregation, 
which in many cases determines the final properties of machine parts and their service life. 
Determining the extent of dendritic segregation requires precise quantitative analysis within 
the limits of dendritic cells. Direct quantitative determination of the extent of chemical 
heterogeneity is difficult because of the small size of the dendritic cells. 
The purpose of this work was to determine the dendritic and phase heterogeneity in 
castings of heat-resistant nickel alloys. 
The study was conducted with the Kamebaks microprobe analyzer. To determine the quanti- 
tative characteristics we used standards consisting ofsingle-phase homogenized (at 1200°C for 
48 h) ternary intermetallic compounds: Ni3(AI, Ti); Ni3(AI, Nb); Ni3(AI, Hf); Ni3(AI, Mo); 
Ni~(AI, W). Alloying of Ni3AI with chromium, cobalt, and vanadium leads, within the concen- 
trations investigated, to a biphase y + y' structure, as the result of which it is necessary 
to use such ternary systems as standards. For this reason, spectrally pure (100%) chro- 
mium, cobalt, and vanadium were used for the standards. Using standards with compositions 
and properties very close to those of the objects reduces the difference in the intensities 
of analytical lines from the sample and the standard. In this case the chemical shift of 
the lines does not affect the results. Is is necessary to introduce corrections for the 
differences resulting from the conditions of excitation and absorption of analytical lines 
in the sample and the standard that approach unity. 
The composition of the ternary standard was determined by means of chemical analysis 
and also by microprobe analysis (with 100% pure elements) by introducing correction factors 
in the computer program. The difference in the concentration of alloying elements did not 
exceed 1% by weight in this case. 
For the analysis we used analytical lines of K~Ni, AI, Ti, Co, Cr, and V and L~W, Nb, 
Mo, and Hf. The accelerating voltage in determining all elements was constant -- 20 kV. 
The probe current was 2 × 10 -7 A. The results of the measurements were averaged over 9-11 
points. The data on the relative intensities were treated for 95% probability, for which 
we compared the program of statistical treatment of the data for the MIR-I computer. In this 
case the largest standard deviation was 0.1-0.2% by weight. Corrections for atomic number, 
absorption, and fluorescence were introduced by means of the KAPAT program [i] in the Minsk- 
32 computer.
