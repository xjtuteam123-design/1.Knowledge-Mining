ABSTRACT
An unprecedented high- γ’ content Co-Al-W-based superalloy ZGH688 has been proposed for laser-directed en -
ergy deposition (L-DED), which exhibits excellent crack-resistant and mechanical properties. In this alloy, the 
volume fraction of γ’ phase is 77.5 ± 5.5 %, surpassing other typical Ni/Co superalloys fabricated by additive 
manufacturing technique. Meanwhile, the γ’ precipitate shows an excellent cubic degree with a 47.7 ± 7.6 nm 
side length. A series of crack evaluation criteria show that the composition of ZGH688 alloy falls into the crack- 
free zone. Additionally, this work validates that the modeling approach to defect optimization in Ni-based su -
peralloys is suitable for Co-Al-W-based alloys. Finally, the newly AMed ZGH688 alloy demonstrates superior 
mechanical behavior under both room and high temperatures compared with other as-built Ni/Co-based su -
peralloys. This work demonstrates the enormous potential of the freshly Co-Al-W-based superalloys in additive 
manufacturing for high-temperature applications.
Additive manufacturing (AM) has garnered significant attention due 
to its distinct advantages, including greater precision, reduced material 
waste, and enhanced design freedom, particularly for fabricating intri -
cate structural components such as turbine blades [ 1 – 3 ]. The intense 
and periodic exposure to high-energy laser beams throughout the 
printing process results in rapid solidification and shrinkage within the 
moving molten pool. This dynamic thermal cycle induces short-term 
non-equilibrium solid-state phase transitions within the fabricated 
part, accumulating residual stress throughout the forming process and 
increasing the likelihood of defect formation, such as cracks and holes 
[ 4 ]. Consequently, crack susceptibility has emerged as the primary 
factor hindering the promotion and widespread application of AMed 
metal materials.
Currently, nickel-based research dominates the field of additive 
manufacturing for superalloys, as it ’ s the preferred choice for structural 
components like turbine blades in the hot section of aircraft engines [ 5 ]. 
The decent service performance in high temperatures is attributed to 
high γ’ phase content and resultant strengthening effect. However, on 
the one hand, the sufficient content of γ’ phase-forming elements such as 
Al and Ti tends to form a low melting point eutectic γ / γ’ , leading to a 
deterioration in the strength of alloys [ 6 ]. On the other hand, the rapid 
precipitation of the γ’ phase impedes thermal stress relaxation after 
solidification, resulting in strain-age cracking and ductility-dip cracking 
[ 7 ]. Currently, the reported Ni-based superalloys with good printability 
for additive manufacturing have a γ’ phase content of no > 40 %, such as 
ABD-850AM, AM80, and Ren ’ e80 [ 8 , 9 ] . Consequently, these alloys 
exhibit inferior performance at high temperatures and fail to meet the 
requirements of turbine blades. Therefore, overcoming the trade-off 
between a high γ’ content and low crack susceptibility has been a 
longstanding challenge in AMed superalloys.

compared to nickel-based alloys [ 10 ], decreasing susceptibility to

In summary, this paper introduces a new crack-resistant Co-Al-W- 
based superalloy, ZGH688, with excellent mechanical properties for the 
l-DED. The volume fraction of the γ’ -Co
3
(Al, W) phase in ZGH688 is 
approximately 77.5 ± 5.5 %, nearly twice that of commonly Ni-based 
alloys. Additionally, the γ’ phase exhibits excellent cubic morphology 
and a normal size distribution, with a mean diameter of 47.5 ± 7.6 nm. 
Despite the high γ’ content, the reasonable ratio of Co, Ta, Al, W and Ni 
elements enables the Co-Al-W-based alloy system to have a small M
SAC
, 
SAC and SCI value. After careful inspection, there was no hot cracking or 
strain-age cracking inside the as-built sample. As a result, the tensile and 
stress-rupture properties of ZGH688 are superior to those of represen -
tative AMed Co-based, Co-Ni-based, and Ni-based alloys. These findings 
demonstrate the potential of the newly developed Co-Al-W-based su -
peralloys as additive manufacturing materials, offering ideal printability 
and superior mechanical properties.
