ABSTRACT
Nb-1Zr alloy with high strength-ductility synergistic effect was prepared by the friction stir processing (FSP). The 
temperature field, microstructure evolution, and mechanical properties of the stir zone (SZ) were systematically 
studied. The grain refinement mechanism of Nb-1Zr alloy was attributed to the combined effects of geometric 
dynamic recrystallization (GDRX), continuous dynamic recrystallization (CDRX), and discontinuous dynamic 
recrystallization (DDRX). After FSP, the Nb-1Zr alloy with fine-grained structure achieved bimodal particle size 
distribution characteristics, resulting in a significant improvement in yield strength and tensile strength of the 
Nb-1Zr alloy at the expense of sacrificing a small amount of elongation. The highest yield strength and tensile 
strength of FSPed Nb-1Zr sample are 295 MPa and 414 MPa, which are 80 % and 53 % higher than BM, while the 
elongation loss is only 9.4 %. In the present study, a strong orientation dependence of grain boundary 
strengthening was reported for Nb-1Zr alloy, much lower k

Introduction
With low density (8.58 g/cm
3
), high melting point (2472 ℃ ), good 
corrosion resistance, and excellent high-temperature performance, 
niobium (Nb) alloys have been applied in the biomedical field [1,2] and 
traditional nuclear energy field [3 – 5] . However, its relatively low 
strength is a limiting factor.
Severe plastic deformation (SPD) has been a subject of great interest 
due to its potential in enhancing mechanical properties via grain 
refinement. Recently, SPD has helped in refractory metals/alloys such as 
Nb [6 – 9] , Ta [10 – 14] , Mo [15] , V [16] , etc. These studies on SPD of Nb 
and its alloys mainly use high-pressure torsion (HPT), accumulative roll 
bonding (ARB), or equal channel angular pressing (ECAP) technique. 
Other than the mentioned techniques, friction stir processing (FSP) is a 
high-temperature SPD technique developed by Mishra et al. [17] .
The intrinsic properties of FSP usually include ultra-high strain, high 
temperature, and high strain rate, as well as its dependence on pro -
cessing parameters plays a major role in refining the grains. These 
properties provide an opportunity to adjust the microstructure, leading 
to dynamic recrystallization (DRX) and microstructure evolution [18] . 
Therefore, the microstructure of the material can be significantly refined 
in the processed zone (so-called “ stir zone ” ). Abbreviations of technical 
terms used in the present paper are listed in Table 1 .
Researchers have done various work on the mechanisms of grain 
refinement and texture evolution during FSP for aluminum, copper, 
magnesium, titanium alloys, and various steel [18] . However, the FSP 
technology has not been applied to Nb alloys until recently [19] , while 
the research on the microstructural evolution and mechanical properties 
of Nb alloys fabricated by FSP is a blank in the field.
Unlike the strain state of simple shear applied in ECAP and HPT, the 
strain and strain rate applied in FSP are highly non-uniform and vary 
with multiple parameters such as the distance between rotating pin and 
shoulder, the rotational and traverse speeds and thermal conductivity, 
etc [18] . Therefore, the evolution mechanism of microstructure during 
FSP is different from that during ECAP and HPT.

Materials and experimental methods
FSP experiment
The material used for FSP experiments in this study was 3mm-thick 
Nb-1Zr alloy rolled plates. Nb-1Zr alloy was processed with twice of 
electron beam melting to achieve ingots, then forge to become slab with 
resistance furnace heating at 1050 ℃ for 2 hours. The slab then un -
derwent 1050 ℃ annealing for 90 min, and rolled to fabricate plates 
with 3 mm thickness, and finally annealed at 1050 ℃ /90 min as the final 
product. The processing direction (PD) of the FSP is in parallel with the 
corresponded rolling direction (RD). Real-time measurement of the 
thermal history of materials during FSP was conducted using external 
thermocouples under the board. The material of the stir tool was W-Re 
alloy. A cylindrical stir tool was used, and its shoulder diameter, pin 
diameter, and length were 12 mm, 5 mm, and 1.4 mm, respectively. The 
processing parameters are shown in Table 2 . To prevent surface oxida -
tion of the material during the processing, argon gas protection was 
used.

Results
Macrostructure
During FSP, the combination of severe plastic deformation and 
thermal effect creates different regions in the processing area, resulting 
in unique microstructural features. The FSP processing zone can usually 
be divided into four zones based on its thermal mechanical properties, 
namely the SZ, the thermal-mechanical affected zone (TMAZ), the heat- 
affected zone (HAZ), and the BM [20] . Fig. 2 shows the macroscopic 
morphology of FSPed Nb-1Zr samples at different processing parame -
ters, with SZ resembling a bowl shape. When the spindle rate ( ω ) 
maintains a constant speed of 1600 rpm, the area of SZ decreases as the 
feed rate (v) increases. Due to the low strength and high melting point of 
niobium alloy, unlike the traditional FSP processing zone, no HAZ 
characterized by coarse microstructure was observed between TMAZ 
and BM.

Tensile properties
At room temperature, processed samples and BM samples showed 
different tensile properties ( Fig. 8 ), it can be seen that the processed 
samples significantly improved the yield strength and ultimate tensile 
strength at the expense of a small amount of elongation. In the best 
conditions, the yield strength and ultimate tensile strength were 
significantly improved with almost no decrease in elongation, 
(1600 – 150-PD, 1600 – 200-PD, 1600 – 400-PD). This result is similar to T 
Niendorf et al. [6] reported mechanical properties after strengthening 
Nb-1Zr alloy using ECAP. Meanwhile, FSPed Nb-1Zr samples exhibit 
strong mechanical anisotropy. When tension along TD, the yield 
strength and ultimate tensile strength of FSPed samples were signifi -
cantly higher than those tension along PD, while when tension along TD, 
the elongation of FSPed samples was significantly lower than that along 
PD.
Fig. 9 shows a comparison of the tensile properties of fine-grained 
and ultrafine-grained Nb-1Zr alloys prepared by FSP and other SPD 
techniques [6,9] . The results indicate that FSP is an effective method to 
improve the strength of Nb-1Zr alloys while maintaining good ductility.
