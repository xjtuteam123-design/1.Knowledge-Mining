Abstract. The addition of refractory elements is effective i n improving the comprehensive 
performance of Ni-based superalloys, and meanwhile, the precipitation trend of TCP phases 
increases with the elements content due to the segr egation of refractory elements during high 
temperature service. The precipitation of TCP phase s obviously decreased the creep properties and 
creep rupture life of superalloys. The toughness an d plastic of superalloys also dramatically 
deteriorated. The addition of Ruthenium (Ru) is sho wn to suppress the formation of TCP phases in 
Ni-based superalloys, resulting in much improved th e creep resistance and the microstructural 
stability. The studies on TCP phases in Ni-based su peralloys including crystallography and 
precipitation of TCP phases and the effect of alloy composition on TCP phases are reviewed. 
Introduction 
The superior high temperature properties of Ni-base single crystal superalloys make them the best 
candidates for use in jet engines and the blades in aerospace turbine engines. It is necessary to 
develop Ni-based single crystal superalloys with fa vorable high temperature properties and other 
multiple properties, especially with mechanical pro perties and microstructural stability in order to 
satisfy the demands of the sustainable development of high-performance aeroengine[1,2]. Additions 
of some refractory elements, such as Re and W, have played a key role in improving the high 
temperature properties of this class of material. T CP phases are principally composed of the 
elements Ni, Cr, Co, Mo, W, Re, and unfortunately t his list contains the elements which are most 
effective at solution strengthening. Thus the forma tion of TCP phases depletes the surrounding 
matrix of refractory solid-solution strengthening e lements, and reduces their solid strengthening 
effect. In addition, the precipitation of TCP phase s during prolonged exposure at high temperatures 
is frequently associated with the formation of void s which may potentially act as initiation sites for 
fracture. As these brittle intermetallic TCP phases precipitate, they can initiate or accelerate crack 
propagation, lead to the reduction of the stress ru pture life and the deterioration of the ductility a nd 
toughness, which have been shown to cause deleterio us effects on the high temperature strength of 
superalloys[3-7]. TCP phases are prone to nucleatio n and growth rapidly in the hot section turbine 
components during exposure to conditions of high te mperature and stress, which seriously threaten 
the aircraft engine and gas turbine security. There fore, proventing the precipitation of TCP phases 
can improve the toughness and plasticity of superal loys, and further increase creep rupture life. 
Recently, TCP phases have restricted the developmen t of superalloys. To obtain better 
microstructural stability and mechanical properties , the addition of the platinum group metal 
ruthenium (Ru) has been reported to suppress the formation of TCP phases during high-temperature 
exposures, significantly improve the high-temperatu re creep resistance[8,9]. In order to provide 
some concept of alloy design and further know more about TCP phases, this paper summarizes the 
type, morphology and composition of TCP phase, the influence of alloy composition on TCP phases 
as well as the precipitation of TCP phases in superalloys are also analyzed respectively.

σ phase is a kind of electron compound with body-ce ntered tetragonal lattice. The unit cell contains 
30 atoms with coordination number as high as 15, wh ich structure is familiar with that of M 23 C6. 
The σ phase forms in all alloys as thin sheets (30µm thick) lying parallel to the {111} planes of γ/γ′. 
The structure of the σ phase depends on the mismatch between the γ matrix and the σ phase and it is 
suggested that alloys which show good lattice misfi t suffer accelerated TCP precipitation[11]. The 
platelet σ precipitates align with the γ′ particles , which is the typical characteristic of σ precipit ates 
in Ni-based superalloys[12]. Most go on to form eit her the µ or P phase, depending on the alloy 
composition[11]. The disappearance of σ phase at hi gher temperature mainly results from the 
formation of M 23 C6 and M 6C carbides, because they can largely combine with C r which is the σ 
forming element[13]. Due to its inherent brittleness and plate-like morphology, σ plate acts as many 
crack initiation sites. It has been reported that high temperature creep fracture often occurs along the 
interface between σ plate and γ/γ′ matrix, which de trimental to alloy properties[14,15]. P phase is a 
new ternary phase in Cr-Co-Mo and Cr-Ni-Mo systems, having structural similarities to σ phase. 
Some researches[11,16] have confirmed that the comp ositions of P and σ are very similar and 
characterised by high Re. 
The structure of µ phase belongs to rhombohedral sy stem. The unit cell contains 39 atoms with 
complicated structure. The precipitation of µ phase is principally composed of W, Mo, Cr and Co, 
and also contains a small amount of Re[11,17]. µ ph ase was shown as having needle-shaped, 
rod-like, globular or blocky morphology [11,18,19]. Behaviour of the µ phase particles was shown 
dependent on their morphology[19]. The µ phase play ed an insignificant role in the reduction of 
high temperature stress rupture lives and room temperature tensile ductility[20-22]. 
Effect of alloying component on TCP phases 
TCP phases are composed mainly of the elements Ni, Cr, Co, Mo, W and Re[11]. Normally, Ni, Cr 
and Fe distribute in σ phase[11,14], W, Mo and Co condense in µ phase[19,24]. Minor additions led 
to the faster formation of TCP phases in the modifi ed RR2086 than in the base material[25]. 
Numerous studies[25,26] have indicated that Re and W addition promote the precipitation of TCP 
phases. In order to enhance microstructural stabili ty during the alloy design process, it is necessary 
to consider the addition of solution strengthening elements, provided make certain the influence of 
alloy compositions on TCP phases[15]. 
TCP phases are formed preferentially at the dendrit e cores in single crystal superalloys without 
carbon or having trace carbon, whereas are limited to interdendritic regions for RR2072 single 
cryastal superalloy with higher content of carbon[1 1,14,19,25,28-33]. The difference of 
precipitation sites is mainly attributed to the eff ect of carbon, the reason is that carbon is shown t o 
decrease the segregation of W, Co, Re and Cr at the dendrite cores[25,31], and increase the contents 
of the above elements in interdendritic regions. Ho wever, the effect of carbides on the formation of

TCP phases is controversial. On one hand, carbides combine with the metal elements and decrease 
the segregation at the dendrite cores. On the other hand, the larger metal atoms are released during 
the transformation among carbides, which provides t he TCP phases forming elements[25,31,33]. 
Carbon additions of 0.1 wt.% were found to suppress the formation of TCP phases and enhance the 
phase stability of the as-cast alloys[31]. The addi tion of carbon results in the reduction of 
segregation degree of refractory elements, such as W[8,34]. 
In the alloys RR2072 and RR2073, the increase in th e W content while decreasing the content of 
molybdenum result in the considerable increase of t he σ volume fraction and the decrease of the 
number of P phase precipitates. Therefore, Mo appea rs to stabilise P phase. In the alloys RR2075, 
RR2072 and RR2074, the TCP volume fraction accordingly increases with the content of Cr. This is 
accord with the high content of Cr in TCP phases in general but particularly in σ phase. The 
elements W and Mo partition strongly to the µ phase[11]. 
The content of Co is higher in µ phase than that in σ phase, and a higher Co alloy would favour the 
formation of µ phase over σ phase[11]. In addition, increasing the level of Co could eliminate the σ 
phase completely, but may be prone to the formation of fewer and larger µ precipitates after 
prolonged high temperature exposure[32]. C. M. F. R ae et al[11] have made some explanation for 
the conclusion that Co additions reduce the volume fraction of TCP phases. Firstly, the addition of 
Co decreases the thermodynamic stability of σ phase relative to other phases; secondly, high Co 
“poisons” the nucleation of σ phase and thirdly, Co reduces the γ′ volume fraction. 
With the development of superalloys, the addition of the platinum group metal Ru in Ni-base single 
crystal superalloys has been reported to increase m icrostructural stability during high temperature 
exposures and suppress the formation of TCP phases, thus improving the oxidation resistance, the 
creep resistance and the corrosion resistance to en able superalloys obtain good mechanical 
properties and microstructural stability[35-41]. 
Precipitation of TCP phases 
Duing to the low diffusivities of rhenium (Re) in n ickel in the third generation superalloys at 
elevated temperatures, solidification and segregation behaviors lead to the formation of TCP phases 
in the dendrite core[35,36]. There has been some re searches claimed that the TCP precipitates first 
formed at regions associated with the dendrite core and eventually spread into interdendritic regions 
and covered the entire microstructure[6]. 
The factors that influence the precipitation of TCP phases mainly include the diffusion behaviors 
and the contents of elements and aging temperature and time, etc. The diffusion behaviors of 
elements have a close relation with the formation o f TCP phases. Under the condition of low 
diffusion temperature and a short period of diffusi on time, the tendency for Re, Cr, Mo, W to 
segregate are not significant, resulting in γ phase existing as solid solution and no TCP phases 
precipitates in the matrix. Thus, diffusion behavio rs of elements increase the tendency of 
precipitating TCP phases during exposure to the con ditions of high temperature and long time. 
However, this does not apply to all alloys under th e above conditions. It is because the solubilities 
of alloying elements increase in the matrix at high temperature, and the elements may be distributed 
evenly in the matrix associated with the increasing diffusion rate, which make γ phase in the state of 
subsaturation and no TCP phases precipitate. Based on the alloys with high levels of Re, W, Mo, 
these slowly diffusing alloying elements may not ev enly distribute in the matrix even during 
exposure to the conditions of high temperature and long time, and subsequently provide the primary 
driving force for the precipitation reactions[42]. 
TCP phases mainly form in interdendritic regions du e to the region provides a rapid diffusion path 
for the TCP phase forming elements. when the γ matr ix becomes supersaturated with refractory 
elements, σ phase is prone to precipitate from the γ matrix[15]. σ phase generally precipitates from 
γ phase at solid state and seriously degrades mecha nical properties of superalloys due to its 
plate-like morphology and high refractory elements containing[12]. Previous studies have shown 
that σ phase nucleates at dendritic cores and near MC carbide particles. σ phase appear as needles or 
thin sheets first at M

The precipitation tendency of TCP phases in the sin gle crystal Ni-based superalloys increases with 
the additive amount of W, Re and Mo. During aging t reatment for 6%W and 4.5% Re alloys, TCP 
phase is identified as µ phase, and precipitates al ong <110> orientation on {111} planes in the 
shape of the slice-like structure[48]. M. P. Simone tti et al[49] have confirmed that µ phase mainly 
precipitates in dendritic core regions, and appears as needles or globular. The difference of 
morphology demonstrates there are different orientation relationship between µ phase and matrix. 
Initially sheets of the σ phase form in the dendrit e core regions, Fig. 1(a); the µ phase forms as 
needles embedded in σ phase, and the amount of σ ph ase decreases as the µ grows, Fig. 1(b); The 
observation confirmed that σ phase is metastable wi th respect to µ phase. Due to the 
microsegregation of Re to the cores, the density of the precipitation decreases from the dendrite 
cores to the interdendritic regions even after the homogenisation heat treatment[50]. In addition, the 
TCP precipitation varies with the local changes in composition: σ and µ phases are observed in 
Re-rich dendritic core regions, however, P phase and R phase become dominant, Fig. 1(c). The only 
phase can be seen is needle-shaped P phase at 1050°C after annealing for 200 h[11]. 
Precipitation mechanisms of TCP phases 
Recent trends in the development of superalloys and continued demand for higher temperature 
properties have focused on the precipitation mechan isms of TCP phases. Most previous 
publications[11,14,19,25,28-31] have found that the development of TCP phase is not confined to 
regions within the primary and secondary arms where as mainly occur at the areas around carbides 
in interdendritic regions. There are three proposed explanations. Firstly, the reaction from MC into 
M
6C and M 23 C6 carbides results in the concentration of Cr, W, Mo and Co; secondly, the dendritic 
boundary provides a rapid diffusion path for the TC P phase forming elements and thirdly, the 
carbon decreases the segregation of W in dendritic region by forming carbides. 
Both nodular σ phase and platelet η phase were obse rved in the as-cast microstructure of Ni-based 
superalloy. During the heat treatment process, σ ph ase decomposed into γ phase leaving serrated 
transformation interfaces between σ and γ. The form ation mechanisms of σ phase and η phase were

studied by analyzing the orientation relationship of σ/γ and η/γ . It was found that σ phase nucleated 
and grew from the liquid during solidification in t he interdendritic region. The η phase had regular 
platelet morphology and definite orientation relati onship with γ phase, which indicated that it

Due to the complexity of σ crystal structure, the f ormation of σ phase from the liquid cannot be 
analyzed by the conventional nucleation and growth mechanism of crystals. It has been reported 
that σ phase has close structural relationship with some quasicrystals. H. Chen et al[19] have 
reported the intimate intergrowth of σ phase and a quasicrystal which is of 12-fold rotational 
symmetry. So the growth mechanisms of quasicrystals can be used to explain how σ phase grows 
from liquid. If the σ phase has nucleated from the γ matrix and grown into the liquid, certain 
definite orientation relationship will exist betwee n σ phase and γ matrix. If no orientation 
relationship is observed, the nucleation mechanism of nodular σ phase should be direct nucleation 
from the remaining liquid in a random way[15]. 
Ru is proven to be an effective element that can su ppress the formation of TCP phases in 
superalloys. The fundamental mechanisms by which Ru additions are able to improve the 
high-temperature phase stability and creep resistan ce of Ni-base superalloys and significantly 
surppress the precipitation of TCP phases are still not well understood [8,35-39,52]. Atsushi Sato et 
al[8] draw the conclusion that Ru additions increas ed the solubility limits of solid solution 
strengthening elements, particularly for Re and W, reduced the supersaturation in γ phase and 
suppressed the precipitation of TCP phases. A.C. Ye h et al[35] presented that Ru additions 
decreased the diffusion rate of refractory elements between the TCP and matrix interfaces and 
changed the kinetics of TCP precipitation. R.A. Hob bs et al[53] obtained the same conclusion that 
Ru additions reduced the solid solubility of Cr, Mo , W and Re in γ phase, thus decreased the 
precipitation kinetics of TCP phases. The presence of Ru in the γ phase and the resulting change in 
lattice misfit between the γ phase and TCP phases m ay potentially influence the ability of the TCP 
phases to nucleate. The addition of Ru enhances pha se stability can be related to the degree of 
segregation during unidirectional solidification. T he driving force for precipitation of TCP phases 
can be minimized by controlling the degree of W and Re segregation and preventing the dendrite 
cores from becoming supersaturated with refractory elements[35]. 
Summary 
Addition of refractory elements effectively improve d the excellent high temperature properties of 
superalloys. However, the segregation of refractory elements promote the precipitation of TCP 
phases. TCP phase precipitates act as barriers to moving dislocations which pile up at the precipitate 
interface, leading to interfacial decohesion and cr ack initiation. The precipitation of TCP phases 
depletes the surrounding matrix of refractory solid -solution strengthening elements, and reduces 
their solid strengthening effect which obviously de creases the creep properties of superalloys. 
Although many researchers from different aspects an alyse the mechanisms of Ru suppress TCP 
precipitation, which provide the ideas and methods for reference, this hot issue still need further to 
explore. However, there is no correlational researc h and report on the fracture mechanism of single 
crystal superalloys containing TCP phases due to th e difficulty of characterization test methods, a 
detailed understanding of the fracture mechanism as well as the effect of Ru additions on the 
fracture mechanism is required.
