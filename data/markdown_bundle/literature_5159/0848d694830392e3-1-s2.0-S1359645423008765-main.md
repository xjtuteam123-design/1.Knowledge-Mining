ABSTRACT 
The wider application of copper alloys is challenged due to their relatively low strength and thermal stability. 
Here we report a nano-reinforcement dispersion strategy to achieved an excellent combination of ultrahigh 
strength, good ductility, high thermal stability and high thermal conductivity in a hierarchical nanostructured 
copper-tungsten (Cu-W) immiscible alloy. Our strategy relied on a uniform dispersion of nanoscale W particles 
(average size ~ 7.6 nm) in ultrafine-grained Cu matrix (~ 0.48 μ m), by employing a molecular-level sol-gel 
synthesis and followed two-step reduction at low-temperature. The nanostructured Cu-W alloys exhibit a high 
tensile strength of 709 MPa, a total elongation of 20 % and a high thermal conductivity of 370 Wm

annealing at 800

potential to offer superior irradiation resistance. This work provides an effective strategy for constructing high- 
performance nanostructured immiscible alloys. 
1. Introduction 
Copper (Cu) and its alloys have spread applications in many indus -
trial fields for their high thermal conductivity and electrical conduc -
tivity, good ductility, and machinability. However, their relatively low 
strength and inadequate thermal stability greatly limits their further 
practical applications due to the growing demand for durability at 
higher temperatures [ 1 , 2 ]. Specifically, aiming at fusion energy appli -
cations, Cu alloys should simultaneously possess excellent 
high-temperature strength, high thermal conductivity, high thermal 
stability, compatibility with tungsten (W) -based plasma-facing mate -
rials and radiation tolerance as a primary candidate for heat-sink ma -
terials [ 3 , 4 ]. 
Over the recent decades, many efforts have been made to develop Cu 
alloys with high strength, good ductility, and good electrical/thermal 
conductivity by grain refinement [5] , solid-solution and precipitation 
strengthening [ 6 , 7 ], and dispersion strengthening of non-soluble 
ceramic or metallic particles [8 – 10] . The strength-ductility trade-off 
limit of Cu alloys has been greatly pushed by microstructure design and 
regulation [ 11 , 12 ]. However, the excellent combination of high 
strength, thermal stability and high thermal conductivity rarely coexists 
in Cu materials. Refining grains to obtain nanocrystalline metals can 
remarkably enhance the room-temperature strength, and improve the 
radiation resistance due to the high fraction of grain boundaries that act 
as sinks of irradiation-induced defects, but nanograined metals usually 
have poor thermal stability and their grains tend to coarsen at moder -
ately low temperatures [ 1 , 2 , 13 ]. Solid solution elements can improve 
the strength of Cu alloys, but lead to a large decrease in thermal/ -
electrical conductivity. Precipitation-strengthened Cu alloys, such as 
CuCrZr alloy, have high strength and good thermal conductivity at room 
temperature, yet their strength will deteriorate when the temperature is 
above the aging temperature due to the coarsening or dissolution of the 
precipitates [ 4 , 14 , 15 ]. The inadequate thermal stability hinders their 
applications at elevated temperatures. As for the 
dispersion-strengthened (DS) Cu alloys, the homogeneous dispersion of 
stable second-phase particles (such as Al

Theoretically, Cu alloys with improved comprehensive performance 
could be achieved by uniformly dispersing small amounts of thermally 
stable second-phase particles with high thermal conductivity in 
ultrafine-grained matrix owing to the following reasons. Firstly, ac -
cording to Orowan strengthening mechanism, the finer and more uni -
formly dispersed the particles, the stronger the alloy. Secondly, well- 
dispersed nanoparticles can impose a strong resistance to grain bound -
aries (GBs) migration and thus enhanced thermal stability by the Zener 
mechanism [ 18 , 26 , 27 ]. Thirdly, a plenty of heterophase interfaces act as 
efficient sinks to irradiation-induced defects and suppress helium bubble 
swelling, and thus improve the irradiation resistance [28 – 31] . Based on 
the above consideration, W seems to be an optimal choice for 
strengthening particles owing to its relatively high thermal conductivity

Besides, compared to Ta, W also has unique advantages of low tritium

high strength, high stability, and maintain high thermal (or electrical) 
conductivity by dispersing low-volume fraction of nano-sized W parti -
cles in fine-grained Cu. However, conventional bulk Cu-W alloys by 
mechanical alloying exhibit either low strength or low thermal con -

tend to aggregate and coarsen. Recently, through ball-milling of Cu-W

dispersion-strengthened alloys [ 27 , 35 – 37 ]. As such, it is still a challenge 
to prepare sub-10 nm W particles uniformly dispersed in bulk 
ultrafine-grained Cu alloys. 
In this work, we report a nano-reinforcement dispersion strategy to 
achieve a bulk nanostructured (NS) Cu-W immiscible alloy with sub-10 
nm W dispersoids, rendering an excellent combination of high tensile 
strength, good ductility, high thermal and electrical conductivity, and 
microstructural stability. The key of our strategy is to employ the W@Cu 
core-shell nanopowders as precursor by a molecular-level sol-gel syn -
thesis and subsequent two-step low temperature hydrogen reduction. As 
a result, nanosized W particles (average size ~7.6 nm) are uniformly 
distributed in ultrafine-grained (average grain size ~0.48 μ m) Cu ma -
trix. Additionally, the NS Cu-W immiscible alloy with abundant in -
terfaces shows potential in improving irradiation resistance.

3.2. Hierarchical nanostructure and microstructural stability 
To determine the size and distribution of W particles in bulk NS Cu-W 
alloy, we characterized the microstructure of as-sintered Cu-1.8at%W 
alloy by using TEM, high-resolution TEM and HAADF-STEM, as shown 
in Fig. 2a – d. Fig. 2a shows the W particles both in ultrafine Cu grains and 
at GBs, while Fig. 2b highlights the fine W particles within grain. It can 
be seen that nanoscale near-spherical W particles with an average 
diameter of 7.6 nm were uniformly distributed in ultrafine-grained Cu 
matrix, and nanoscale twin boundaries (TBs) were also found in many 
Cu grains. Most of the W particles are less than 10 nm in size, although 
some larger W particles of about 20 – 50 nm in size were also found. Due 
to the numerous W nanoparticles interacting with GBs, the average grain 
size of NS Cu-1.8at% W is only 0.48 μ m. During sintering, the Cu grains 
grow through Ostwald ripening. The nanoscale W particles hinder the 
GB migration through the Zener pinning effect, resulting in a final grain 
size of submicron scale. The high-resolution TEM image showed that the 
measured interplanar distance of the particle and matrix is 0.224 nm and 
0.183 nm respectively, corresponding to the values of W (110) and Cu 
(200) planes ( Fig. 2c ). The HAADF-STEM images ( Fig. 2d , e) of the Cu- 
1.8at%W alloy further reveals the homogeneous distribution of W par -
ticles in ultrafine Cu grains, at nanoscale TBs and GBs.
