a b s t r a c t 
To improve the yield strength of metallic materials at low temperatures, a strategy of combining the cal- 
culation of phase diagrams (CALPHAD) technique with the overall valence electron concentration (OVEC) 
principle is applied, and a Ni 2 CoCrNb 0.2 medium-entropy alloy (MEA) with D0 22 superlattice (noted as 
the γ′ ′ phase) is designed. Bulk MEA samples without defects were successfully fabricated using laser 
additive manufacturing (AM), followed by solution treatment at 1200 °C for 1 h and then aging at 650 
°C for 120 h. The nanoscale γ′ ′ phase precipitated. The tensile results indicated that the MEA had supe- 
rior yield strengths of ∼1180 MPa and ∼1320 MPa and tensile strengths of ∼1335 MPa and ∼1552 MPa 
at 293 K and 77 K, respectively. The yield strength obtained was superior to that of currently re- 
ported medium/high-entropy alloys and typical advanced cryogenic steel. The mechanical properties of 
the Ni 2 CoCrNb 0.2 MEA demonstrated a strong temperature dependence, and the increased yield strength 
was mainly attributed to the increase in lattice friction stress at low temperatures. This research provides 
a new strategy for producing materials with ultrastrong cryogenic yield strengths by AM.

1. Introduction 
Metallic materials with outstanding mechanical properties at 
room and cryogenic temperatures are highly desirable for ap- 
plication under extreme service conditions, especially in Antarc- 
tic scientiﬁc research, aerospace, marine shipbuilding, and petro- 
chemical industries [1] . Recently, extensive research has been per- 
formed to develop cryogenic metallic materials, including typical 
advanced cryogenic steel and cryogenic medium/high-entropy al- 
loys (MEAs/HEAs). Advanced cryogenic steel shows great cryogenic 
temperature performance; its yield strength can reach ∼800 MPa, 
and its elongation can reach ∼70% [2–6] . Regarding MEAs/HEAs, 
face-centered cubic (FCC) structural alloys [7–18] have been ac- 
tively studied, and the results indicated excellent mechanical per- 
formance at cryogenic temperatures. An equiatomic ternary NiC- 
oCr MEA demonstrated a maximum yield strength of ∼700 MPa 
and an elongation of over 70% [19] . To improve the cryogenic 
yield strength of MEAs/HEAs, researchers applied second-phase

cation under extreme conditions. Therefore, it is necessary to pro- 
vide a feasible approach for improving the cryogenic- temperature 
yield strength of MEAs/HEAs. Unfortunately, there are only a few 
studies on improving the cryogenic-temperature yield strength of 
MEAs/HEAs by incorporating the γ′ ′ phase with the D0 22 struc- 
ture, and there are no reports in the ﬁeld. 
Inspired by previous studies, second phase strengthening (L1 2 
phase [ 1 , 2 ], oxide particles [ 3 , 4 ], and ceramic particles [5] ) can 
signiﬁcantly improve the cryogenic yield strength of metal ma- 
terials. For MEAs/HEAs, the nanoscale γ′ ′ phase usually has a 
higher lattice mismatch and antiphase boundary energy, leading 
to high reinforcement ability ( ∼14% volume fraction of γ′ ′ phase 
contributes ∼727 MPa strength enhancement) and an excellent

proposed to design the γ′ ′ phase based on combining the overall 
valence electron concentration (OVEC) principle and the calculation 
of phase diagrams (CALPHAD) technique, and at room temperature, 
the yield strength of the resulting Ni 2.1 CoCrFe 0.5 Nb 0.2 HEA was 
∼1005 MPa, and the elongation was ∼20%, indicating that the in- 
troduction of Nb plays an important role in precipitation strength-

HEA has exhibited excellent room- and cryogenic-mechanical prop- 
erties [23] . However, G. Laplanche et al. proved that the cryogenic 
performance of the CrCoNi MEA was better than that of the CoCr-

feasible pathway for introducing a rich Nb γ′ ′ phase with high 
strengthening ability into the CoCrNi MEA, because the CoCrNi 
MEA with an extremely high cryogenic twinning ability could in- 
crease the yield strength of cryogenic materials. Nevertheless, the 
composition design and fabrication methods of MEAs/HEAs with 
the γ′ ′ phase still have signiﬁcant challenges when it comes to im- 
proving the cryogenic yield strength. 
The preparation process for cryogenic MEAs/HEAs mainly in- 
cludes electromagnetic suspension casting and vacuum arc melting 
[ 1 , 7-10 , 25 ]. Only a few studies have reported the use of the addi- 
tive manufacturing (AM) process to prepare cryogenic MEAs/HEAs 
[ 21-23 , 26-29 ]. According to the yield strength of a sample re- 
ported in the literature, AM technology is superior to the tradi- 
tional process, which proves the superiority of the AM process. AM 
technology has seven typical processes, including material jetting, 
vat polymerization, sheet lamination, binder jetting, powder bed 
fusion (PBF), material extrusion and directed energy deposition 
(DED) [ 30 , 31 ]. Among them, laser DED and PBF are the most com- 
mon processes for fabricating MEAs/HEAs using powder. Due to the 
advantages of net-shape manufacturing and design freedom, it is 
easy to obtain complex products with internal structures directly 
from computer-aided design (CAD) models [32] . Coaxial powder 
feeding laser DED is a convenient process for preparing HEA prod- 
ucts. It not only uses in-situ synthesized HEA powder but also sup- 
plies different element powders through multiple hoppers at the 
same time. Although the extreme thermal environment of laser 
DED results in excellent cryogenic performance, there are still great 
challenges for the composition design of printable powders and 
optimization of the preparation process parameters. 
Motivated by the challenges mentioned above, in the present 
work, the design of a coherent γ′ ′ phase-precipitated Ni 2 CoCrNb 0.2 
MEA is achieved by the combination of the CALPHAD technique 
with the OVEC principle. MEA samples without defects were suc- 
cessfully fabricated using laser DED. The effects of the γ′ ′ phase 
on the mechanical properties of the Ni 2 CoCrNb 0.2 MEAs at 293 K 
and 77 K are explored. The Ni 2 CoCrNb 0.2 MEA is also studied after 
applying different heat treatment processes. At 77 K, excellent me- 
chanical properties with a yield strength of ∼1320 MPa and a ten- 
sile elongation of ∼25% were obtained, which is superior to that of 
cryogenic metallic materials reported in the literature. 
2. Design of an MEA with a D0 22 precipitation-hardened 
superlattice 
In this work, our design strategy includes selecting CoCrNi MEA 
as the matrix and introducing Nb to form the γ′ ′ phase. To elimi- 
nate the holes and cracks that often occur in the extreme thermal 
environment of AM and introduce second-phase strengthening, the 
purpose of our project is to ensure that the high-temperature area 
of the alloy phase diagram indicates a single-phase FCC structure, 
and the low-temperature area indicates the precipitation of the γ′ ′ 
phase. Moreover, it is easier to form the γ′ ′ phase with D0 22 when 
the OVEC of the alloy is higher than 8.4 [33] . In a previous study, 
a Ni 2.1 CoCrFe 0.5 Nb 0.2 HEA with outstanding room-temperature me- 
chanical properties was designed [24] . Therefore, to ensure that the 
OVEC of the alloy is higher than 8.4, we select the Ni element with 
the highest valence electron concentration as the variate, and the 
Ni x CoCrNb 0.2 MEA is designed. The phase compositions of MEAs 
with different Ni contents are analyzed using the CALPHAD tech- 
nique, as shown in Fig. 1 (a). The analysis shows that the Laves 
and Sigma phases are easy to form when the Ni content is low, 
which is unfavorable for the design of this MEA. However, with 
increasing Ni content and OVEC, the Laves and Sigma phases grad- 
ually disappear. When the atomic ratio of Ni is 47.5%, the phase of 
the Ni x CoCrNb 0.2 MEA is mainly composed of FCC and γ′ ′ phases 
with a D0 22 structure, and the phase area of the γ′ ′ phase is the 
widest. Therefore, a Ni 2 CoCrNb 0.2 MEA with an OVEC of 8.57 is de- 
signed by applying the CALPHAD technique to a large number of 
speciﬁc components. Fig. 1 (b) shows the phase ﬁeld calculation re- 
sults of the Ni 2 CoCrNb 0.2 MEA. The results show that the alloy has 
a single-phase FCC structure, as indicated by the high-temperature 
area of the phase diagram, and the γ′ ′ phase can be precipitated, 
as indicated by the low-temperature area, which is a very ideal 
composition for AM. Moreover, the element composition of the γ′ ′ 
phase is analyzed, as shown in Fig. 1 (c). The ﬁgure shows that the 
γ′ ′ phase is mainly composed of Ni, Nb, Co and a small amount of 
Cr. 
3. Experimental details 
The bulk Ni 2 CoCrNb 0.2 MEA samples were produced with a 
laser DED system equipped with a 6 kW power continuous wave 
ﬁber laser unit, and the process ﬂow is shown in Fig. 2 (a). The AM 
system was equipped with a robotic arm, cooler, atmosphere pro- 
tection box, powder feeder, etc. To protect the molten pool from 
oxidation, the bulk MEA was prepared in an argon-ﬁlled treatment 
chamber, and the oxygen content was controlled below 50 ppm. 
Atomized spherical metal powder was used as the raw material, 
including high-pure purity Ni, Co, Cr and Nb with particle sizes 
of 45–105 μm. The prepared alloy powder was fully mixed in a 
variable speed ball mill for 4 h, and then dried to prevent oxida- 
tion. An AISI 304 stainless-steel plate with a polished and clean 
surface was used as the printing substrate. After a series of prelim- 
inary experiments, the optimal laser DED parameters (laser power: 
1.2 kW, laser spot diameter: 3 mm, powder feeding rate: 2 r/min, 
scanning speed: 15 mm/s, layer thickness: 0.35 mm) were deter- 
mined. The ﬁnal printed bulk Ni 2 CoCrNb 0.2 MEA had dimensions 
of 70 × 3 × 50 mm 3 , and the sample morphology is shown in 
Fig. 2 (b). The red arrow in the ﬁgure indicates the scanning direc- 
tion (SD) and building direction (BD). Subsequently, according to 
the shown in Fig. 1 (b) and a large number of experiments, the heat 
treatment process was determined. The bulk Ni 2 CoCrNb 0.2 MEA 
was solid-solution treated at 1200 °C for 1 h, followed by water 
quenching to obtain a homogeneous microstructure, and then aged 
at 650 °C for different times to analyze the precipitation strength- 
ening mechanism of the γ′ ′ phase. 
The phase of the MEA samples was identiﬁed using an X-ray 
diffractometer (XRD) equipped with a Cu target at testing an- 
gles ranging from 30 ° to 110 ° and a testing speed of 2 °/min. 
The microstructure was characterized using electron-backscattered 
diffraction (EBSD, Oxford Nordlys Max3) and scanning electron mi- 
croscopy (SEM, Zeiss Sigma500). The EBSD test results were pro- 
cessed through Channel-5 software. The hardness was tested using 
a microhardness tester (JMHV-10 0 0AT) at a loading force of 1.98 N 
for 10 s at 20 different locations along the BD. Specimens before 
and after deformation were prepared for transmission electron mi- 
croscopy (TEM, JEOL 2100 F) observation. The area fraction and size 
of the γ′ ′ phase were calculated from the micrographs using com- 
mercial software (Image-Pro-Plus). Tensile specimens in a rectan- 
gular dog-bone shape with dimensions of 13 × 3 × 1 mm 3 were

4. Results 
4.1. Microstructures and elemental distribution 
The XRD results of the three Ni 2 CoCrNb 0.2 MEA states (the as- 
deposited state, solid-solution state and aged state) are shown in 
Fig. 3 . The diffraction pattern clearly shows that the three states 
have an FCC structure. Although the CALPHAD results showed that 
the γ′ ′ phase precipitated after aging, the XRD results demon- 
strated that only the FCC structure was identiﬁed. This is because 
the lattice parameters of γ matrix and nanoscale γ′ ′ phases are 
close, so it is very diﬃcult to distinguish them using XRD, and 
some adjacent diffraction peaks may partially overlap [34] . 
The inverse pole ﬁgure (IPF) diagram, pole diagram and grain 
size of the microstructure were analyzed using EBSD. Fig. 4 ((a 1 - 
a 3 ) - (c 1 -c 3 )) shows the results for the as-deposited state, solid- 
solution state (1200 °C for 1 h), and aged state (650 °C for 
120 h), respectively. Fig. 4 (a 1 ) shows that the microstructure of the 
Ni 2 CoCrNb 0.2 MEA was mainly composed of columnar crystals, and 
the growth direction was along the (100) direction [35] , as shown 
in Fig. 4 (a 2 ). Fig. 3 (a 3 ) shows the grain size distribution, and the 
statistical average grain size was 44.99 μm. The microstructure of 
the MEA usually grew outward in the direction of the maximum 
temperature gradient, and as the height of the building direction 
(BD) increased layer by layer, the microstructure exhibited nonuni- 
form grain evolution [21] . However, from the as-deposited state to 
the solid-solution state, the columnar crystal was transformed into 
an equiaxed crystal, the crystal had no obvious preferential orien- 
tation, and the average grain size grew to 87.38 μm, as shown in 
Fig. 4 (b 1 -b 3 ). From the solid-solution state to the aged state, the 
grains did not change signiﬁcantly, as shown in Fig. 4 (c 1 -c 3 ).

4.2. Characterization of γ′ ′ phase with the D0 22 structure 
The dark ﬁeld (DF) image of the Ni 2 CoCrNb 0.2 MEA aged at 
650 °C for 120 h is shown in Fig. 6 (a), indicating that disk-shaped 
precipitates with different crystal orientations were uniformly dis- 
tributed in the matrix. According to statistics on the DF images, 
the proportion of disk-shaped precipitate in the matrix was 16%, 
which is agreement with the above CALPHAD results. The length 
and width of the disk-shaped precipitate were 10 nm and 3 nm, 
respectively. Fig. 6 (b–d) shows diffraction pattern (DP) images for 
the [001], [011] and [112] zone axes, where the weak spot with 
the red symbol is the γ′ ′ phase and the bright spot with the 
blue symbol is the γ matrix. There were three variants of the γ′ ′ 
precipitated particles, which are indicated by patterns of differ- 
ent shapes, as shown in Fig. 6 (b). The crystal orientation relation- 
ship between the γ′ ′ phase and matrix was {100} m //{100} γ′ ′ and 
< 0 01 > m //[0 01] γ′ ′ [36] . The lattice parameters of the γ′ ′ phase and 
matrix were a γ′ ′ = 3.695 ˚A and c γ′ ′ = 7.29 ˚A, a m = 3.615 ˚A, re- 
spectively. The lattice misﬁt between the matrix and γ′ ′ phase was 
calculated to be 1.2%. 
The high-resolution TEM images of the three variants are dis- 
played in Fig. 7 (a), which are marked with three ellipses of dif- 
ferent colors. The morphology of the nanoscale γ′ ′ precipitated 
particles indicated by these images is in agreement with that in- 
dicated by the above DF images, and the disk-shaped γ′ ′ pre- 
cipitated particles are located in the {100} m plane of the γ ma- 
trix. The nanoscale γ′ ′ phase precipitated in the Ni 2 CoCrNb 0.2 MEA 
showed a typical D0 22 superlattice structure, which is exactly con- 
sistent with the crystal structure of Ni 3 Nb [37] . The D0 22 superlat- 
tice structure is equivalent to two FCC unit cells superposed along 
the [001] axis direction, as shown in Fig. 7 (b). The γ′ ′ phase has 
three variants because the [001] axis of the D0 22 crystal struc- 
ture can be parallel to the ⟨ 100 ⟩ of the FCC crystal structure, as 
shown in Fig. 7 (c). Fig. 7 (d–g) shows the corresponding inverse fast 
Fourier transform (IFFT). Fig. 7 (d) shows the IFFT maps of the three 
variants, which is consistent with the results in Fig. 6 (b). Fig. 7 (e) 
shows the IFFT image of variant 1, in which the crystallographic re- 
lationship was [10 0] γ′ ′ //[0 01] m . The superlattice diffraction points 
appeared at the (210) and (1 1 
2 0) crystal plane locations, demon- 
strating that Nb tended to occupy the central and corner locations 
in the D0 22 superlattice structure [38] . The IFFT maps of variant 2 
are shown in Fig. 7 (f). The γ′ ′ phase diffraction points appeared 
at the (120) and ( 1 
2 1 0) crystal plane locations, and the crystallo- 
graphic relationship was [010] γ′ ′ //[001] m . Fig. 7 (g) shows the IFFT 
image of variant 3, in which the crystallographic relationship was 
[0 01] γ′ ′ //[0 01] m . The large antiphase boundary energy and lattice 
distortion energy of γ′ ′ phase contributed to the improvements in 
the mechanical properties. Moreover, there was a completely co- 
herent interface between the γ′ ′ phase and matrix, which did not 
change the direction of dislocation movement during deformation, 
thus improving the plasticity of the material.

4.3. Characterization of the γ′ ′ phase with APT 
To obtain a more accurate compositional analysis of the γ′ ′ 
phase, nanosized needle tips were cut off from the MEA aged 
at 650 °C for 120 h and then further analyzed by APT. Fig. 8 (a) 
shows the typical atomic maps of the Ni, Co, Cr and Nb elements, 
presenting a similar elemental distribution to that analyzed using 
TEM-EDX. The γ′ ′ phase particles were reconstructed from the iso- 
concentration surface of 8 at.% Nb. The average size of the γ′ ′ pre- 
cipitates is consistent with the above TEM observation ( Fig. 6 ). The 
volume fraction of the γ′ ′ phase was 16%. The chemical compo- 
sitions of the matrix and precipitate were quantitatively analyzed 
through a mean proximity histogram calculated from an 8 at.% Nb 
isoconcentration surface, as shown in Fig. 8 (b), and the detailed re- 
sults are summarized in Table 2 . The results showed that the γ′ ′ 
phase was rich in Nb and Ni but poor in Co and Cr. 
To further clarify the three-dimensional atomic distribution, 
stoichiometry, morphology and spatial occupation of the γ′ ′ phase 
at the atomic scale, the APT results were analyzed in detail, as 
demonstrated in Fig. 9 . The 3D reconstruction map of an APT nee- 
dle tip of the γ′ ′ phase is displayed in Fig. 9 (a). The two adjacent 
γ′ ′ precipitates were analyzed with a distance of 20 nm (position b 
in Fig. 9 (a)), and the distributions of different elements are shown 
in Fig. 9 (b 1 ). It can be seen in detail that the composition changed 
from matrix to precipitates. Moreover, it can be seen that Ni and 
Nb diffused from the matrix into precipitates, while Co and Cr did 
just the opposite. 
In the region of two adjacent precipitates, a box of 
8 nm × 8 nm × 8 nm was extracted to completely cover two 
precipitates (position b in Fig. 9 (a)). The 2D-concentration map 
in Fig. 9 (b 2 ) displays the relative distributions of four elements 
by using a thermal color gradient. The high-deﬁnition Ni, Co, Cr 
and Nb atomic distribution map is shown in the box [39] . Since 
the Ni and Nb in the Ni 2 CoCrNb 0.2 MEA had a high SFE when the 
two elements were enriched in the γ′ ′ phase, the SFE of the FCC 
matrix was signiﬁcantly decreased [40] . This was very beneﬁcial 
for nanotwin formation during low-temperature tensile testing; 
thus, the cryogenic tensile properties were improved. According 
to the above CALPHAD technique, it was concluded that the D0 22 
structure of the γ′ ′ phase was similar to the Ni 3 Nb structure. 
Moreover, previous studies demonstrated that Cr and Co prefer to 
occupy Ni sites in a Ni 3 Nb phase [41] . Therefore, by combining the 
APT analysis results with the CALPHAD results, it was concluded 
that this γ′ ′ phase precipitate had a stoichiometry of (Ni, Co, 
Cr) 3 Nb.

4.4. Mechanical properties 
The hardness of the Ni 2 CoCrNb 0.2 MEA aged at 650 °C for dif- 
ferent times is shown in Fig. 10 (a). The hardness gradually in- 
creased with increasing aging time, reaching 628 ± 16 HV after 
aging for 120 h. This showed that the strengthening phase grad- 
ually grew with aging time. The engineering stress-strain curves 
of the MEAs in various states are shown in Fig. 10 (b). After so- 
lution treatment, the strength decreased slightly, and the plastic- 
ity increased slightly. Moreover, the postprinting aging treatment 
conspicuously improved the tensile strength of the MEA at 293 K, 
and the yield strength increased from ∼439 MPa (as-deposited) to 
∼1180 MPa (650 °C-120 h), with an increment of 169%, but the 
ductility decreased from ∼50% to ∼19%. It is worth noting that ex- 
cellent tensile properties were obtained at 77 K, the yield strength 
was increased to ∼1320 MPa, the tensile strength was increased to 
∼1552 MPa, and the fracture elongation was increased to ∼25%. In 
conclusion, the Ni 2 CoCrNb 0.2 MEA prepared by AM had excellent 
room-temperature and cryogenic-properties. 
Typical ductile dimples were observed on the fracture sur- 
face of the Ni 2 CoCrNb 0.2 MEAs in various states, as shown in 
Fig. 11 . Fig. 11 (a) shows the fracture surface of the MEA in the as- 
deposited state. Due to the heterogeneous elemental distribution, 
a hybrid fracture with a combination of raised edges and dimples 
was observed. Fig. 11 (b) shows the fracture surface of the MEA in 
the solid-solution state. A large number of deep dimples were ob- 
served on the fracture surface, because the elements were homo- 
geneously distributed after solution treatment. Fig. 11 (c) shows the 
fracture surface after aging at 650 °C for 120 h under tensile tests 
at 293 K. The existence of the γ′ ′ phase resulted in an increase 
in the probability of microcracks and a decrease in the fracture 
area, promoting a reduction in dimple size. Moreover, some nano- 
sized particles located at the bottom of the fracture dimples were 
observed. However, the fracture surface of the MEA is displayed 
in Fig. 11 (d) under the tensile tests at 77 K. It was observed that 
the size of dimples decreased and the dimples themselves were 
deeper, as marked with arrows and circles, respectively, which al- 
lowed the Ni 2 CoCrNb 0.2 MEAs to have a stronger deformation abil- 
ity at 77 K [20] . 
Fig. 12 (a) illustrates the strain-hardening behavior of the 
Ni 2 CoCrNb 0.2 MEAs after aging at 650 °C for 120 h as a func- 
tion of the true strain at 293 and 77 K. It can be seen that the 
strain-hardening rate (SHR) of the sample at 293 K showed only 
two monotonically decreasing stages. In contrast, three SHR stages 
were demonstrated at 77 K, namely, stage I, stage II and stage III. 
In stage I, the SHRs of the two samples were almost equal, and the 
SHRs dropped sharply in the elastic-plastic dominated deformation 
transition [25] . In stage II (8% ≤ ε ≤ 14%), the slopes of the strain-

5.2. Temperature-dependent uniform elongation 
The Ni 2 CoCrNb 0.2 MEA demonstrated an unexpected strain- 
hardening capacity at 77 K, which originated from the poten- 
tial microstructure, including hierarchical SFs, nanotwins, L -C locks 
and ordinary dislocations, and more importantly, the interactions 
among them. However, the superiority of uniform elongation at 
low-temperature deformation was closely related to the strain- 
hardening ability [ 55 , 56 ]. A higher density of hierarchical SFs and 
small amount of microtwins existed in low-temperature deforma- 
tion specimens, which not only played an important role in strain 
strengthening but also functioned as important plastic carriers to 
provide outstanding plasticity. Moreover, these twin boundaries 
provided a storage space for the aggregation of dislocations and 
acted as pathways to promote the movement of dislocations along 
twin boundaries [ 57 , 58 ]. Therefore, the stress concentration was 
reduced, and the elongation of the material was increased. More- 
over, previous studies have shown that the interaction mechanism 
between dislocations and twin boundaries in alloys is sensitive to 
temperature [59] . That is, at low temperatures, dislocations are 
transported to the twin thin layer by penetrating through twin 
boundaries, while at room temperature, dislocations are immersed 
in the cross-slip process of twin boundaries [ 25 , 59 ]. In conclusion, 
at 77 K, the interaction between small amounts of microtwins and 
higher density SFs during plastic deformation enabled the MEA to 
have outstanding uniform elongation. 
5.3. Temperature-dependent stacking fault energy 
As mentioned above, a large number of twins emerged in the 
solid-solution sample ( Fig. 12 (a 1 , a 2 )), while the aged sample only 
exhibited a small number of microtwins at 77 K ( Fig. 13 (c, d)). In 
general, the formation of deformed twins is mainly affected by SFE. 
Deformation twins are activated when the SFE is low, which leads 
to an increase in the dislocation storage capacity, strain-hardening 
rate and ductility [60] . The SFE in the alloy was closely related to 
the strain energy of the matrix. Therefore, geometric phase analy- 
sis (GPA) was further employed to understand the SFEs of the three 
different states (solid solution at 293 K, aged for 120 h at 77 K, and 
aged for 120 h at 293 K), as shown in Fig. 16 . Fig. 16 (a–c) shows 
that horizontal positive strain ( εxx ), shear strain ( εxy ), and vertical 
positive strain ( εyy ) were exhibited [61] . The spots marked with 
white circles are the sites of GPA analysis, and the scaleplate of 
the right side presents strong and weak strain energy of the ma- 
trix, where the tensile strain is positive, the compressive strain is 
negative, and the positive and negative values alternate [62] . The 
strain energy of the solid-solution MEA matrix was the lowest, fol- 
lowed by aging the MEA aged for 120 h at 77 K, and the highest 
strain energy was observed for the MEA aged for 120 h at 293 K. 
It was further observed that the γ′ ′ phase increased the SFE of the 
MEA, and the SFE decreased with decreasing ambient temperature. 
In conclusion, the formation of deformed twins was strongly tem- 
perature dependent in terms of the SFE. 
Apart from the temperature, with the precipitation of nanoscale 
γ′ ′ phase, the change in alloy composition also affects the SFE. Ac- 
cording to the APT analysis results, Cr and Co were enriched in the 
FCC matrix, while Ni and Nb with high SFE were enriched in the 
γ′ ′ phase, resulting in the SFE of the FCC matrix was signiﬁcantly 
decreased [40] . This was very beneﬁcial for nanotwins formation 
during low-temperature tensile testing; thus, the cryogenic tensile 
properties were improved. Furthermore, due to the presence of γ′ ′ 
phase, the width of FCC matrix channel became narrowed and the 
SFE of the FCC matrix was signiﬁcantly decreased at 77 K, leading 
to a greatly increased stress level to initiate deformation nanotwins 
(see in Fig. 14 (d)) [ 1 , 63 ]. Meanwhile, the SF networks divided the 
space into nanoscale microstrips, resulting in a dynamic Hall-Patch

6. Conclusions 
In this work, a novel γ′ ′ phase precipitation-hardened 
Ni 2 CoCrNb 0.2 MEA is designed by combining CALPHAD technology 
with the OVEC strategy. The tensile tests at 293 K and 77 K of 
different state specimens indicate that the mechanical properties 
show a strong temperature dependence. The following conclusions 
can be drawn: 
(1) A bulk MEA without defects was successfully fabricated 
using AM. Its yield strength ( ∼439 MPa), tensile strength 
( ∼860 MPa), and elongation-to-failure ( ∼50%) were studied. 
After aging at 650 °C for 120 h, the γ′ ′ phase with a vol- 
ume fraction of 16% causes the yield strength to increase 
from ∼439 MPa (as-deposited) to ∼1180 MPa (650 °C-120 h), 
with an increment of ∼169%, but the elongation-to-failure 
decreases from ∼50% to ∼19%. The γ′ ′ phase precipitation 
strengthening is the main strengthening mechanism, and its 
contribution to the yield strength is 741 MPa. 
(2) The mechanical properties of the Ni 2 CoCrNb 0.2 MEA demon- 
strate a strong temperature dependence. Excellent tensile 
properties are obtained at 77 K, the yield strength is in- 
creased to ∼1320 MPa, the tensile strength is increased to 
∼1552 MPa, and the elongation-to-failure is increased to 
∼25%. The γ′ ′ phase precipitation strengthening effect is 
still maintained, and the increase in lattice friction stress im- 
proves the yield strength. 
(3) The deformation mechanism of the Ni 2 CoCrNb 0.2 MEA ex- 
hibits a strong temperature dependence. At 293 K, defor- 
mation occurs dominantly by ordinary dislocation plasticity, 
while at 77 K, includes dislocation, SF, l -C locks and defor- 
mation microtwins, as well as their extensive interactions. 
In particular, the dislocation and twin boundary interaction 
promotes an improvement of ductility.
