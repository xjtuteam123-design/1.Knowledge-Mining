and the stability of low-intensity jets is poor during jet

Cu–Ta alloys are two-phase composite metal materials
with high density, high plasticity, high strength, and good
thermal stability. Te presence of Ta particles signifcantly
improves the thermal stability of Cu–Ta alloys, making them
still have high strength at high temperatures, resulting in
Cu–Ta alloys being highly valuable materials for the shaped-
charge liner of shaped-charge jets [15–18]. Cu and Ta have
diferent crystalline structures (FCC and BCC, respectively)
and negligible mutual solubility in the solid state [19]. High-
energy mechanical alloying is capable of forcing a signifcant
amount of Ta into a metastable FCC solid solution with Cu
[20]. During subsequent thermal processing, Ta precipitates
in a variety of structural forms ranging from atomic-level
coherent clusters to incoherent particles reaching a few
nanometers and larger in size. Purja Pun et al. [21] pointed
out that the formation of Ta clusters is considered to be the
reason for the extraordinary properties of mechanically
alloyed Cu–Ta alloys. Such clusters tend to form at grain
boundaries(GBs),preventinggraingrowth.Infact,thesizeof
the Ta clusters is independent of the Ta content of the
samples. On the contrary, the increase of Ta content shows
