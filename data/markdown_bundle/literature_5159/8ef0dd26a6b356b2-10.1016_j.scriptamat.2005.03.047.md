Boundary liquation and interface cracking characterization
in laser deposition of Inconel 738 on directionally solidiﬁed
Ni-based superalloy
Minlin Zhong *, Hongqing Sun, Wenjin Liu, Xiaofeng Zhu, Jinjiang He
Laser Processing Research Centre, Department of Mechanical Engineering, Tsinghua University, Beijing 100084, China
Received 25 January 2005; received in revised form 18 March 2005; accepted 30 March 2005
Available online 25 April 2005
Abstract
Laser deposition of Inconel 738 on directionally solidiﬁed (DS) Ni-based superalloy demonstrates a strong susceptibility to crack-
ing. Cracks originate from the liquation of low melting point eutectic on the DS grain boundary. Five boundary liquation and inter-
face cracking styles were identiﬁed. Sound Inconel 738 deposition layers were achieved free of cracks.

Keywords: Nickel alloys; Laser deposition; Liquation cracking; Superalloys; Directional solidiﬁcation
1. Introduction
Directionally solidiﬁed (DS) Ni-based superalloys
have better elevated temperature strength and hot
corrosion resistance than the conventional Ni-based
superalloys due to the elimination of transverse grain
boundaries perpendicular to the applied stress. They
are widely used to produce gas turbine and jet engine
blades nowadays [1]. Defects such as cracks sometimes
occur after service or during the investment casting of
the DS Ni-based superalloy blades. When non-fatal de-
fects appear in critical positions such as the hub area on
the leading edge of the blade, the defects dramatically
reduce the lifetime of the expensive blades in high pres-
sure, high temperature working conditions [2]. These de-
fects must be scraped and a technique is therefore of
great economic interest to repair the scraped blade por-
tions without cracking. Unfortunately, the as-cast DS
superalloys are extremely diﬃcult to weld by conven-
tional methods since their coarse microstructures, their
serious segregation of alloying elements and their high
amount of precipitation-strengthened constituents leave
them highly sensitive to cracking [3,4]. In addition, the
intense heat input during conventional welding may
destroy the DS characteristic of the turbine blades and
decrease the operating life by recrystallization. To
maintain the superior properties of the DS superalloys,
Inconel 738 is considered an appropriate repair material
candidate. Inconel 738 is strengthened primarily by pre-
cipitation of ordered L1
2 intermetallic Ni 3(Al,Ti) c0
phase. Containing substantial amounts of Al and Ti
(>6 wt.%), it is generally considered very diﬃcult to
weld due to its high susceptibility to cracking in the heat
aﬀected zone during welding [5,6]. Therefore, welding
the very crack-sensitive Inconel 738 to the very crack-
sensitive DS superalloy substrate is a great challenge.
Due to its lower heat input and partial heating, laser
deposition has reportedly eﬀectively repaired Ni-based
superalloy blades [7–10]. With an extra high tempera-
ture gradient and cooling rate during the deposition pro-
cess, laser deposition is able to produce directional
solidiﬁcation structure and to control the microstructure

superalloys. Laser deposition results in high quality

reported research on this topic to date. This paper fo-
cuses on research on interface liquation and cracking
characterization in laser deposition of Inconel 738 on
directionally solidiﬁed Ni-based superalloy.
2. Experimental procedures
The deposition experiments were carried out in a
laser system consisting of a PRC 3000 3 kW continuous
wave fast axial ﬂow CO
2 laser and a CNC controlled
working table. The substrate used was a cast DS
Ni-based superalloy plate with dimensions of 80 mm ·
60 mm · 20 mm. Commercial Inconel 738 powder of
150–250 mesh size was used as the deposition material.
Its composition is also shown in Table 1 . A preplaced
deposition technique was used in this research in order
to decrease and control the heat input to the substrate. A
thin Inconel 738 coating with thickness of 0.2–0.6 mm
was pre-deposited on the (1 0 0) preferential plane of
the DS Ni-based superalloy substrate with organic adhe-
sive. A laser beam scanned the coating to melt it
completely and heat the substrate to form the deposition
layer. The laser processing parameters were: laser power
of 0.4–1.2 kW, scanning speed of 0.1–0.4 m/min, beam
diameter of 2–4 mm, powder feeding rate of 2–6 g/min
and overlap rate of 30–50%. Argon was used as a shield-
ing gas for protection of the melt pool.
The analysis specimens were cut from the cross sec-
tion of the deposition passes, ground mechanically, pol-
ished with ﬁne diamond paste and etched with CuSO
4/
HCl/H2O (80 g/20 l/100 l) solution. The microstructures
were observed using a CSM-950 scanning electron
microscope (SEM) and JSM6310F ﬁeld emission SEM
equipped with Link ISIS300 energy dispersive spectrom-
etry (EDS) for composition analysis. A D/max-rB high
power multi-crystal X-ray diﬀractometer was used for
phase identiﬁcation. Vickers microhardness measure-
ments were made using a 200 g load and a holding time
of 15 s.
3. Experimental results and discussion
3.1. Boundary liquation and interface cracking
characterization
Fig. 1 shows the morphology of the Inconel 738 pow-
der ( Fig. 1 a) and the microstructure of the DS super-
alloy substrate ( Fig. 1 b). The DS superalloy exhibits a
typical directional solidiﬁcation columnar structure with
a very coarse columnar diameter averaging 0.4 mm. The
DS superalloy contains elements like Ni, Cr, Co, Ti, Al,
Ta, W, and Mo, etc. Its microstructure is mainly com-
posed of c
0 phase in addition to c phase, (c + c0) eutectic
and MC-type carbides. Severe composition segregation
exists in the coarse DS columnar structure. The carbides
and the low melting point eutectics are densely distrib-
uted along the parallel line-shaped grain boundaries,
indicating that the grain boundaries in the DS super-
alloy are much more vulnerable to thermal strain or
solidiﬁcation shrinkage stress than the normal grain
boundaries of a non-DS structure.
Experimental results on laser deposition indicated
that when the laser energy was high enough to cause
substantial melting of the DS superalloy substrate sur-
face, severe cracks occurred in the interface area be-
tween the DS superalloy substrate and the Inconel 738
deposition layer as shown in Fig. 2. The evidence clearly
shows that the cracks originate at the grain boundaries
of the DS superalloy substrate, propagate along the
grain boundaries and spread across the interface into
the deposition layers.
Fig. 3 a shows the morphology around the cracks,
containing some particles along the crack (marked A
and B in Fig. 3 a) and light-colored ﬁlms within the
cracks (marked C in Fig. 3 a). Their chemical composi-
tions were determined by EDS analysis, as shown in
Table 2 . Compared with the composition and phase
structure of the DS superalloy substrate, it is apparent
that the particles are MC type carbides with high
amounts of Ti and Ta. It can be deduced that the
light-colored ﬁlms distributed within the cracks, con-
taining high amount of Al, Cr, Co and Ni, are the
re-solidiﬁed ( c + c
0) eutectics. Therefore, it can be

Liquation occurs either by non-equilibrium interface
melting below an alloy /C213s solidus or by equilibrium super-
solidus melting. Laser deposition is a typical rapid heat-
ing/rapid cooling non-equilibrium process. It is well
proven that rapid heating will increase the liquidus of
an alloy to several tens of degrees or even over a hun-
dred degrees. Therefore when the laser energy/heat input
is high enough to melt the surface layer of the DS super-
alloy substrate, the temperature will be much higher
than its equilibrium liquidus temperature. This will
either cause severe melting of the low temperature eutec-
tics in grain boundaries or dissolution of the carbides in
the boundaries. The parallel line-shaped grain bound-
aries promote a more rapid and deeper penetration melt-
ing of the eutectics. Simultaneously, rapid cooling
induces great thermal strain and rapid solidiﬁcation
accompanies high shrinkage stress by rapid precipitation
of phase like c
0. These factors deﬁnitely aggravate liqua-
tion and cracking in the DS superalloy substrate.
Cracks, as shown in Fig. 2 , are almost inevitable. Even
if the surface of the DS superalloy substrate is not sub-
stantially melted, liquation may also occur due to sub-
solidus melting of the ( c + c
0) eutectic during heating.
Once initiated, liquation may easily propagate along
the parallel line-shaped grain boundaries. Therefore,
the DS superalloy substrate is extremely susceptible to
liquation cracking and is very sensitive to heat input
via laser deposition parameters.
Heat input can be determined by power density q ¼

pD2 and line energy k ¼ P

D is the beam diameter and v is the laser scanning speed.

relationships between the number and/or size of cracks
in the interface area and the heat input. An obvious de-

300 kJ/m. With decreased heat input parameters q =
2.5 · 105 kW/m2 and k = 124 kJ/m, fewer and/or smaller
cracks were observed as in Fig. 4 .

It is generally understood that cracking results from
competition between the mechanical driving force for
cracking (stress/strain) and the material /C213s intrinsic resis-
tance to cracking. The processing parameters in laser
deposition can be accurately modulated to precisely con-
trol the heat input, which is not comparable to con-
ventional welding methods. The preplaced deposition
method used in this research allows the laser energy to
completely melt the coating material ﬁrst, after which
the heat propagates into the substrate. Thus it is possible
to strictly control the heat input into the DS substrate
and to decrease the liquation of the DS superalloy sub-
strate. In addition, the rapid cooling/solidiﬁcation
accompanying the high temperature gradient results in
a very ﬁne directional columnar structure (for example,
68 ﬁne columnar crystals in the Inconel 738 deposition
layer grow from one columnar crystal in the DS super-
alloy substrate in the interface), and a more homoge-
neous composition distribution and less segregation,
which are beneﬁcial to inhibiting boundary liquation
and cracking. Therefore, the above liquation and crack-
ing phenomena were attributed to the comprehensive ef-
fects of various beneﬁcial and detrimental factors which
are dependent on the processing parameters and local
composition/structure conditions. When the interface
temperature is lower than the liquidus temperature of
the DS superalloy but higher than the melting tempera-
ture of the ( c + c
0) eutectic, liquation in the DS super-
alloy substrate grain boundary eutectics occurs ( Fig.
3a and b). The liquation may initiate cracks ( Fig. 4 b)
in the deposition layer driven by the induced stress, or
the liquation may penetrate into the deposition layer
and introduce cracking in local favorable composition/
structure conditions ( Fig. 4 a). When the interface tem-
perature is slightly lower than the eutectic temperature,
liquation in the DS superalloy substrate may not occur.
While the stress induced may still initiate cracks at the
interface, cracks may ( Fig. 4 c) or may not ( Fig. 4 d)
propagate into the deposition layer depending on the
material/C213s intrinsic resistance. At high temperature, the
material/C213s ability to withstand stress is normally very
weak. Even if there is no liquation or cracking in the
DS superalloy substrate, the heated grain boundary will
still be a weak point, which may initiate cracking in the
deposition layer ( Fig. 4e). Fig. 4a–e show the high liqua-
tion and cracking susceptibility in the DS superalloy
substrate and the deposited Inconel 738 layer. There-

3.2. Microstructure characterization of deposited layer
free of boundary liquation and interface cracking
In order to ensure the deposited Inconel 738 layers
have suﬃcient high temperature performance, the Inco-
nel 738 powder must be melted completely with enough
laser energy/heat input to produce high density and a
good metallurgical bond with the DS superalloy sub-
strate. An appropriate temperature gradient and cooling
rate are also required to maintain good DS characteris-
tics in the deposited layers. Therefore the optimization
of process parameters is crucial to good deposition
results. Optimal parameters, obtained through a series
of experiments, are: laser power P = 500 W, scanning
speed v = 0.25 m/min, laser beam diameter D = 2 mm,
where q /C25 1.6 · 10
5 kW/m2 and k = 120 kJ/m. With
these parameters, sound deposition layers with good
metallurgical bond were successfully achieved. No
boundary liquation occurred in the DS superalloy sub-
strate and no interface cracking appeared on the inter-
face. As well, deposition layers were free of cracks and
pores ( Fig. 5 a and b).
Typical cross section microstructure of the deposited
Inconel 738 layers is exhibited in Figs. 5b and 6 a. Due to
the ultra-high temperature gradient on the solid–liquid
interface of the melted pool during solidiﬁcation, Inco-
nel 738 laser deposited layers show strong directional
solidiﬁcation characteristics with epitaxial growth from
the DS superalloy substrate surface with h100 i orienta-
tion. The very ﬁne columnar structure array follows the
preferential crystallography h100 i orientation, with pri-
mary spacing of the column about 5 lm, much smaller
than that of the original DS superalloy substrate, which
averaged about 300–400 lm. Fig. 7 shows the X-ray dif-
fraction result of the Inconel 738 deposition layer, indi-
cating that the layer was mainly composed of c
0 phase
and TiC particles. Fig. 6 b shows the cross section mor-
phology of the directional solidiﬁcation columnar crys-
tals. Many cubic particles are distributed uniformly in
the microstructure, which is the typical morphology of
the c0 phase in the superalloy. The average size of the
c0 phase is about 2.5 lm. White particles (shown both
in Fig. 6 a and b) were distributed along the grain
boundaries as well as the matrix; these are MC (1) type
carbides rich in elements Ti and Ta as determined by
EDS analysis.

4. Conclusions
(1) Laser deposition of Inconel 738 on directionally
solidiﬁed Ni-based superalloy demonstrates strong
susceptibility to boundary liquation and interface
cracking. Cracks originate from the liquation of
the low melting point eutectics in the DS superal-
loy grain boundaries, propagate along the grain
boundaries and spread across the interface into
the deposition layer.
(2) Five boundary liquation and interface cracking
styles were identiﬁed, which depend on the heat
input and the local composition/structure con-
ditions.
(3) By optimizing the processing parameters to
decrease the heat input, sound Inconel 738 deposi-
tion layers were achieved on the DS superalloy
substrate. Deposition layers were free of boundary
liquation and interface cracking and showed
strong directional solidiﬁcation columnar struc-
ture, composed of c
0 phase and TiC particles.
The microhardness of the deposited layers was
about HV
0.2410 on average, which is similar to
that of the DS superalloy substrate.
