Abstract 
As a new class of materials, High Entropy Alloys (HEAs), have been recently used for various 
usages such as high -temperature applications. High entropy alloys have such unique properties 
as high strength, high -temperature phase stability, good fracture resistance, and excellent 
softening resistance at high temperatures. Studies on the oxidation behavior of as -cast high 
entropy alloys are , however, currently limited. The oxidation behavior of Al0.5CrCoFeNi HEA 
was investigated at three different temperatures of 800, 900, and 1000 °C, and in the air. The 
results showed that the oxidation kinetics of this alloy followed the parabolic rate law. However, 
at 1000 °C, two -step oxidation kinetics was found: in the first step, it followed the almost linear 
oxidation rate law ( n=0.97); in the second one, the predominant mechanism followed the 
parabolic rate law ( n=0.56). Analysis of the morphology and phase composition of the oxide 
layers was done using the Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM) and X -ray diffraction. At all 
temperatures, the results showed that the oxide layers consist ing of Cr 2O3/Al2O3 and spinel of 
Ni, Co, and Fe were formed in the Al depleted zone.

Introduction 
To increase the efficiency of turbines engines, the pressure to weight ratio and fuel efficiency 
must be increased. The fundamental principles of jet propulsion include fuel consumption, the 
engine thrust, thermal efficiency , and engine weight. Increasing the turbine inlet temperature 
leads to improving the output power of the high-pressure turbine engine [1]. 
Many gas turbine engine components are made of Ni -based superalloys, which must be highly 
resistant to high temperatures in order to improve engine efficiency. As a result, the addition of 
refractory materials leads to some increases in the thermal resistance of nickel-based superalloys 
[2,3,4,5,6]. Researchers have designed alloys base d on the "high entropy" by combining two 
sluggish diffusion effects with high lattice distortion. High entropy alloys belong to new types of 
advanced materials that can be used in different industries , such as aviation and turbine 
manufacturing [7,8]. Engineering alloys used in different industries usually consist of a principal 
component. However, high entropy alloys have no main component and generally consist of five 
or more metallic components at equiatomic or nonequiatomic concentrations [5]. These alloys 
are heat-resistant and lighter than steel, aluminum alloys, and other classical materials [5]. 
Due to unique and simple phase compositions, the high entropy alloy has such desirable 
mechanical properties as high strength and hardness [9,10,11,12], outstanding fatigue resistance 
[13,14], good fracture toughness [15], excellent resistance against softening at elevated 
temperatures [16], and remarkable corrosion and oxidation resistance [17,18,19,20,21]. For 
example, the sluggish diffusion behavior in the FeCoNiCrMn high entropy alloy is much lower 
than that in the commercial stainless steel and Fe -Cr alloys [22]. Further, the hardness values of 
FeCoNiCrAlx HEA (where x is changed from 0 to 2) are justifiably raised with increasing the Al 
content; this value is approximately 110 and 130 Hv for FeCoNiCr and FeCoNiCrAl 0.375, 
respectively, while it is 484 and 509 Hv for FeCoNiCrAl1.25 and FeCoNiCrAl2, respectively. 
Guo et al. [23] found that the average valence electron concentration (VEC) of all of its elements 
and concentrations was an essential parameter in predicting the BCC and FCC crystal structures 
in HEA. For example, the FCC phase was formed for alloys that had a VEC less than 8 and the 
BCC phase for alloys that had a VEC higher than 6.87 [24]. Therefore, it was expected that 
Al0.5CoCrFeNi alloy with VEC 7.87 could have a mixed phase of BCC and FCC. The valence

Two significantly important factors for high-temperature applications are commonly the 
oxidation resistance and thermal stability of oxygen affine alloy systems. However, very few 
studies have addressed the formation mechanisms and oxidation behaviors in HEAs [10,11,19]. 
It should be, however, noted that oxidation resistance is one of the most important properties for 
materials used at high temperatures. In general, t here are two principal modes for the oxid ation 
of metal alloys [ 25,26,27]. The first condition occurs when oxygen is in direct contact with the 
surface, and the mechanism of rate control is the oxidation of the gas -metal interface reaction. In 
this case, according to the oxidation growth law:

The second one occurs when a dense and passive layer is form ed on the surface of the alloy, 
protecting the alloy from direct contact with gaseous oxygen ; the diffusion by the oxide layer 
becomes the rate-controlling oxidation. Therefore, the rate of increase in the mass per unit area is 
reduced by oxidation over time, in this case, the surface oxide volume formed is equal to or 
higher than the volume of the alloy participating in the reaction, and the oxide has high adhesion. 
In this case, the oxidation growth law (Equation 2) is parabolic (n = 0.5) [27,28].

Bulter et al. [ 29] investigated the oxidation of two series of high entropy alloys, including 
Al10Co22.5Cr22.5Ni22.5Fe22.5 and Al 20Co25Cr25Ni25Si5 at 1050 °C. The oxidation kinetics in both 
alloys followed the parabolic rate law after 1000 hours. The oxidatio n rates for the lower and 
upper aluminum alloys were 1.1–1.5 ×10 -11 and 5.1 –5.8 ×10 −15 (g2/cm4 s), respectively. They 
concluded that the oxide layers formed for the low Al alloy contained a discontinuous Cr 2O3 
outer layer and an Al 2O3 substrate with AlN precipitates. However, for high Al alloys, there was 
a continuous Al2O3 layer without an inner oxide layer. 
The results , therefore, have shown that HEAs are selectively oxidized like other engineering 
alloys, and Al and Cr can have a significant influence on the oxidation behavior of HEAs [30]. In 
this regard, extensive research has been reported on the microstructure and composition of the 
AlCoCrCuFeNi phase, confirming that the growth rate of Cr and Al is prolonged and the 
parabolic constants at 1000 °C are 2×10 -11 and 7×10 -12 (mg2cm−4s−1) for both elements 
respectively. Besides, increasing the amount of Al could improve the oxidation resistance of 
HEA by forming the Al2O3 oxide layers. Concerning the recent systemic studies related to the 
investigation of the oxidation behavior, there are , however, no studies regarding Al 0.5CoCrFeNi 
HEAs at temperature s of T=800, 900, and 1000 °C. This study , therefore, investigated the 
oxidation behavior of CoCrFeNi-based HEA with Al0.5 at several distinct temperatures. 
 
Experimental: 
The Al0.5CoCrCuFeNi high entropy alloy was synthesized into a button of 350g by arc melting in 
a mixture of high purity argon atmosphere (99.99%) , by using the mixture of highly pure Al 
(99.99 wt%), Co (99.98 wt%), Cr (99.99 wt%), Fe (99.95 wt %) and Ni (99.95 wt%). The alloy 
homogeneity was assured by flipping over the button and re -melting it at least four times. The 
cast alloy was homogenized in a discharged quartz tube for 11 h at 1100 °C. The specimens were 
machined for oxidation testing by an electrical discharge machine of 6 * 11 * 11 mm. The 
samples were initially ground using the 1000-grit SiC paper, and this was followed by washing 
through distilled water. Then the specimens were cleaned ultrasonically in acetone to remove any 
grease or dirt and dried immediately before the tests. The specimens were placed in a 3 cm long 
ceramic container inside a laboratory oven.

## Page 6

The isothermal oxidation test was conducted at T=800, 900, and 1000 °C for 2, 4, 6, 24, 48 , and 
72 h under ambient air in a LAC -PKE furnace. The specimens were systematically removed 
from the furnace at regular intervals to measure mass with an electronic weight balance; then, the 
weight of each specimen was measured, and the weight gain was continued to be estimated by 
subtracting the weight of the initial sample from the final weight of the oxidized sample. The 
characterization of the alloys and oxide scale was initially done using X -ray diffraction with 
monochromatic Cu-Kα radiation under the operation condition ; this consisted of the accelerating 
voltage and current of 45 kV and 40 mA, respectively. Also, the surface area and surface 
morphology of the samples were evaluated by scanning electron microscopy (SEM Philips Xl-
30) equipped with a backscattered electron (BSE) and wave -dispersive X -ray spectroscopy 
(WDS) detector.

Oxidation microstructure 
Figure 2 illustrates the X-ray diffraction patterns of the high entropy alloy after oxidation tests at 
1000 °C after 72 h. There were three types of oxide on the surface of HEA: Al 2O3, Cr2O3, and 
spinel ( the molecular formula as (Co, Ni)) (Al, Cr, Fe) 2O4. The type of spinel oxides was 
comparable to M -Cr-Al alloys ( with M = Ni, Co, or Fe) [ 33]. Because the Al0.5CoCrFeNi 
oxidated layer at 800 and 900 °C was very thin, X -ray diffraction could not be correctly 
performed. Therefore, X -ray diffraction was conducted on the specimens oxidized at 1000 °C 
after 72 hours . In the X -ray diffraction pattern, the Al2O3 peaks appear ed due to Al 0.5. 
Nonetheless, due to the lack of the Al element , enough Al2O3 layers could not be created. Wang 
et al. [34] investigated the microstructure of the AlxCrFeCoNi high entropy alloy with different 
amounts of Al. They found that by increasing the amount of Al, the structure was changed from 
FCC to BCC. Throughout the oxidation process, Al was oxidized on the surface, and the Al 
content in the region was declined; then, the structure was changed from BCC to FCC [ 34]. As 
the exfoliation phenomenon occurs continuously during the oxidation process, the Cr in the alloy 
begins to form Cr 2O3, and the diffraction peak of Cr 2O3 and spinel oxides could be gradually 
enhanced. According to figure 3 (a), in the initial step (2 hours) of oxidation at 1000 °C , it was 
only associated with the formation of Al2O3 oxides. The Cr2O3 oxide layers to be formed in three 
stages. In the first stage, the discontinuous scale between Al2O3 precipitates was formed. In the 
second stage, continuous oxide layers above the first stage oxide layers (discontinuous scale) 
were formed (after the oxidation for 6 hours, as shown in figure 3 b). Moreover, in the final stage 
(Figure 3 c), after 72 hours of oxidation, the external spinels of NiCr2O4 and Fe(Cr, Al)2O4 were 
formed above the Cr2O3 continuous oxide layer. 
Figure 4 exhibits the cross-section of the HEA oxide layers to understand better the formation of 
the oxide layers at different times and high temperatures. The Al 2O3 oxide layer started to grow 
at the beginning of oxidation (Figure 4 a). With the increase of the oxidation time and the loss of 
aluminum content at the surface, Cr 2O3 oxide layers were formed (Figure 4b). The oxide layer 
thickness grew with increasing the oxidation time, leading to the formation of NiCr2O4 spinel 
oxide layers. Figure 4 (c) demonstrates that the Cr 2O3 layers formed approximately above the 
Al2O3 layer with the increase of the oxidation time in the Al0.5CoCrFeNi high entropy alloy.

The Al and Cr elements, due to the lower Gibbs free energy, as compared to Ni, Co, and Fe, have 
a higher tendency to react with oxygen ions. The Al2O3 growth rate is higher than that of Cr2O3; 
so, firstly, Al was oxidized, forming an inner oxide layer ; Al on the surface was decreased, 
forming the Al depletion-zone, while Cr and other spinel oxide formed the external oxide.

elements. Therefore, the formation type of oxide layer and oxidation step process could be

energy of formation for some oxide s was calculated , and the standard Gibbs functio n of

Regarding the previous section oxidation kinetics and oxidation behavior, formation of the oxide 
layer by means of Al and Si element s prevents the penetration of the oxygen from the surface 
and; as a result, has an outstanding improvement in oxidation resistance of the alloy. The oxide 
layer of Al, as a diffusion barrier against O2 penetrate into the substrate, enhances the alloy 
performance against high-temperature oxidation where the rate of penetration is high. Parabolic 
kinetic equation coefficient or Kp demonstrates the oxidation rate and because of higher Gibbs 
free energy of Al and Si elements, alloys that contain these elements have a lower oxidation rate. 
On the other hand, alloys which do not contain Al element in their chemical composition have a

 Conclusion 
The oxidation behavior of the Al0.5CoCrFeNi high entropy alloy was investigated at three 
temperatures of 800, 900 , and 1000 ° C in vitro . The Al 0.5CoCrFeNi high entropy alloy was 
composed of FCC and BCC phases. The rate of weight gain per unit area during oxidation at the 
temperatures of 800 and 900 °C followed the parabolic rate law. These values varied at 1000 °C, 
following the paralinear (parabolic-linear) rate law. At 800 and 900 ° C, the oxide layer was very 
thin, and XRD could not decompose it ; this low thickness of the oxide layer reflected the slow 
diffusion of these elements during oxidation and their resistance to oxidation at these 
temperatures. The high content of various substances in the high entropy alloy led to the 
formation of complex oxide s. However, the high Cr activity of high entropy alloys could 
enhance the formation of such protective layers as Cr2O3 on the surface. At the end of the test 
(72 h), the surface complex oxide layers consisted of Cr 2O3, NiCr2O4, Fe(Cr, Al)2O4, Al2O3, and 
AlN were formed at 1000 C. In general, the Al0.5CoCrFeNi HEA oxidation process with time 
could be explained as follows. Al is first oxidized because of the Gibbs energy lower. As the 
oxidation time was increased, Al was reduced at the surface , and the Al depleted -zone was 
formed; then, Cr began to oxidize. The Cr oxide layers are formed above the Al oxide layers . 
With oxidation, most other elements (Co, Fe, Ni) begin to oxidize , forming spinel oxide layers 
above the chromium oxide layer.
