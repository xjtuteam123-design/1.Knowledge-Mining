ABSTRACT 
This work systematically investigated the effect of Ni additions of 10, 20 and 30 at% on the oxidation behavior of 
oxide dispersion-strengthened (ODS) Co-20Cr-15Al (at%) superalloys at 900 
◦
C in air. Alumina scale with a 
duplex structure consisting of outer θ -alumina and inner α -alumina was formed on all tested Co-based ODS 
superalloys. Ni addition reduced the oxidation rate by inhibiting the growth of the outer θ -alumina during initial 
oxidation and the inner α -alumina during prolonged exposure. Cr
2
O
3 
formation between the interface of the θ - 
and α -alumina layers was confirmed with the increase of Ni content. 
1. Introduction 
Co-based superalloys have excellent corrosion resistance and high- 
temperature strength, making it suitable for high-temperature applica -
tions such as aircraft jet engines and gas turbine components [1,2] . To 
further increase the service temperature of cobalt alloys, alumina for -
mation is essential to ensure oxidation resistance at high temperatures, 
however this seems to be difficult to achieve for conventional carbide or 
γ’ precipitation strengthened Co-based alloys, as Al usually leads to a 
loss of strength [3 – 5] . A promising strategy for improving the oxidation 
resistance of Co-base model alloys with γ / γ’ -microstructures was pro -
posed by Hagen et al., who demonstrated that the formation of protec -
tive alumina scales can be achieved using shot-peening treatment prior 
to oxidation exposure [6,7] . Co-based ODS superalloys have recently 
been proposed as one of the potential materials to overcome the 
trade-off between alumina formation and strength, as it has been shown 
to possess excellent strength despite the addition of a high Al content 
[8 – 10] . The developed Co-based ODS superalloys contain a Co solid 
solution phase with face-centered cubic (fcc) structure and a CoAl phase 
with body-centered cubic (B2) structure. With increasing Al content, 
fine Y
2
Hf
2

in the both phases, which are expected to serve as reliable reinforcing 
precipitates due to their superior stability up to 1200 
◦
C [11] . Besides, it 
was demonstrated that increasing Al content from 10 to 20 at% enabled 
the formation of alumina scale instead of a multilayer scale consisting of 
CoCr

Co-based ODS superalloy with the fine Y

because of its excellent oxidation resistance and favorable strength. 
Nevertheless, a point of concern is that with the addition of a large 
amount of Al, the volume fraction of the brittle B2 phase is increased, 
causing the brittleness of the Co-based superalloys to become an issue 
[8] . In order to prevent the ductility loss of alumina-forming Co-based 
ODS alloys, it is important to strictly control the added Al content to 
obtain the least amount of the brittle B2 (CoAl) phase. Based on the 
results of previous studies on the effect of Al content on the 
alumina-formation, the addition of 15Al at% was chosen in the current 
study. In addition to adjusting the Al content to limit the brittle CoAl 
phase, the introduction of Ni alloying is also proposed, since Ni is a 
well-known fcc stabilizer and has a strong affinity for Al, which may play 
a role in inhibiting the formation of the CoAl phase [13] . It has been 
demonstrated that the Ni addition in CoCrFeAl high entropy alloys 
(HEA) could accelerate the formation of plastic fcc phase and result in a 
noticeable increment in ductility [14] . Mukherji et al. investigated the 
Ni addition on mechanical properties of Co-based alloys and found that 
the addition of 15 at% Ni to the Co-17Re-23Cr alloy significantly 
improved ductility without loss of strength [15,16] . Therefore, the 
improvement of ductility by Ni alloying in the alumina-forming 
Co-based ODS superalloys is expected. The effect of alloyed Ni con -
tents on the oxidation behavior of the alumina-forming Co-based ODS 
superalloys is still unknown and has been the main impetus for con -
ducting this study.

2. Experimental 
Four grades of Co-based ODS superalloys of nominal chemical 
composition Co-20Cr-15Al-xNi-0.4Y
2
O
3
-0.6Hf (at%) with different Ni 
content of x = 0, 10, 20 and 30 at%, were prepared by mechanical 
alloying (MA) and spark plasma sintering (SPS) at 1100 
◦
C with 50 MPa. 
The consolidated block materials were subjected to a hot rolling process 
and a final annealing treatment at 1100 
◦
C. Detailed fabrication process 
has been interpreted elsewhere [11] . Depending on the various contents 
of Ni added, the abbreviations 0Ni, 10Ni, 20Ni and 30Ni are used in this 
work to define the materials. The samples being used for oxidation 
testing were cut into a rectangular shape of 15 mm × 8 mm × 1 mm. 
Prior to the oxidation test, all surfaces of these samples were wet ground 
with SiC papers up to 2400-grit and then polished with diamond paste 
until 1 µ m . After confirming that there were no residual scratches on the 
surface, the samples were ultrasonically cleaned in alcohol and dried in 
hot air. Oxidation tests were performed in a muffle furnace in air at 
900 
◦
C. After different exposure times (1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 49, 64, 81, 100, 
225 h), the samples were simultaneously removed from the furnace and 
weight changes were measured after cooling to room temperature by a 
Sartorius precision electronic balance (MC 5) with a resolution of 1 μ g. 
The samples were subjected to multiple oxidation and cooling processes 
in order to measure the mass gain during each exposure time. 
X-ray diffraction (XRD, RIGAKU Ultima IV/SG operated at 40 kV/40 
mA with Cu-K α ) was used to identify the crystalline structure of the 
oxide scales formed on the surface of all tested samples. Field-emission 
scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM) observation was carried out on 
the surface morphology and cross-sections of the oxidized samples, 
respectively. Electron probe micro-analyzer (EPMA, JEOL JXA-8530F) 
characterization, including elemental mapping and quantitative point 
analysis, was conducted to understand the element distribution in the 
oxide scales. In order to know more detailed microstructural informa -
tion, especially the distribution of nanoscale oxide particles within the 
oxide scales, transmission electron microscopy (TEM, JEOL ARM-200F) 
characterization was performed using scanning transmission electron 
microscopy (STEM) images and X-ray energy dispersive spectroscopy 
(EDS) elemental maps. Focused ion beam (FIB, HITACHI, FB2200) was 
used to prepare the TEM samples, in which carbon and tungsten coatings 
were successively overlaid on the oxidized surface to maintain the 
integrity of the oxide scales during the FIB milling process. Thermo-Calc 
software along with thermodynamic database SSOL5 [17] was used to 
perform the binary phase diagram calculations.

4. Discussion 
4.1. Ni addition on the formation of alumina scale 
Based on the above results, it can be affirmed that the newly designed 
Co-20Cr-15Al- (0, 10, 20, 30)Ni (at%) ODS superalloys are capable of 
forming the alumina scale consisting of outer θ -alumina and inter 
α -alumina at 900
◦
C in air. Increasing the Ni content contributes to the 
oxidation resistance of the Co-based ODS superalloys via suppressing the 
growth rate of the alumina scale, as shown with the k
p 
values in Table 1 . 
The XRD results in Fig. 3 demonstrated that θ -alumina was rapidly 
generated at the early stage of oxidation, while α -alumina grew and 
dominated at the late stage of the oxidation at 900
◦
C. It has been 
claimed that the alumina-forming alloys preferentially nucleates and 
grows θ -alumina, which subsequently nucleates α -alumina at the inter -
face between the θ -alumina and the substrate [20,21] . Since α -alumina 
is much denser compared to θ -alumina, once a continuous α -alumina 
layer exists at the oxide/alloy interface, the further oxidation of the alloy 
would be dominated by the growth of the α -alumina layer [22] . The 
addition of Ni did not change the alumina growth pattern, as all tested 
samples with different Ni contents possessed a similar alumina growth 
sequence in Fig. 3 . 
However, it is certain that the Ni introduction suppressed the growth 
of the outer θ -alumina, as shown with a thinner outer θ -alumina layer 
found in the 30Ni sample in Fig. 7 . Besides, compared to the thin and not 
yet continuous inner α -alumina presented in the 0Ni sample after 64 h 
oxidation, a thick and continuous inner α -alumina existed in the 30Ni 
sample. Although the TEM observation of the most initial stage of the 
inner α -alumina formation was not performed, it is reasonable to assume 
that the Ni addition facilitated the formation of the inner α -alumina 
during the early oxidation process, allowing the preferential growth of 
the inner α -alumina in 30Ni into a continuous layer. The thin θ -alumina 
in the 30Ni sample is closely related to its ability to form inner α -alumina 
rapidly, since the growth of the θ -alumina is governed by the outward 
diffusion of cations [23 – 25] , which would be effectively inhibited by the 
formation of the inner α -alumina. The preferential formation of the inner 
α -alumina layer is associated with a rapid nucleation of the α -alumina. 
Based on the TEM images in Fig. 10 (a and e), it can be confirmed that 
the grain size of the inner α -alumina formed on 30Ni within 64 h is much 
smaller than that in the 0Ni sample, which implies the presence of more 
α -alumina nuclei in the 30Ni sample. It is verified that Cr
2
O

initial stage of oxidation is able to act as nucleation sites for α -alumina,

30Ni, an enrichment of Cr was detected in the interface between the θ - 
and α -alumina layers, which was supposed to be a thin Cr
