Life Prediction Method of CC and
DS Ni Base Superalloys Under

Polycrystalline conventional casting (CC) and directionally solidiﬁed (DS) Ni base su-
peralloys are widely used as gas turbine blade materials. It was reported that the surface
of a gas turbine blade is subjected to a biaxial tensile-compressive fatigue loading during
a start-stop operation, based on ﬁnite element stress analysis results. It is necessary to
establish the life prediction method of these superalloys under biaxial fatigue loading for
reliable operations. In this study, the in-plane biaxial fatigue tests with different phases of
x and y directional strain cycles were conducted on both CC and DS Ni base superalloys
(IN738LC and GTD111DS) at high temperatures. The strain ratio
/H9278was deﬁned as the
ratio between the x and y directional strains at 1/4 cycle and was varied from 1 to /H110021.

superalloy. On the other hand, the main cracks of the DS superalloy propagated only in
the x direction, indicating that the failure resistance in the solidiﬁed direction is weaker
than that in the direction normal to the solidiﬁed direction. Although the biaxial fatigue
life of the CC superalloy was correlated with the conventional Mises equivalent strain
range, that of the DS superalloy depended on
/H9278. The new biaxial fatigue life criterion,
equivalent normal strain range for the DS superalloy was derived from the iso-fatigue life

blades, which are subjected to biaxial fatigue loading during operation.

1 Introduction
About 40% of electricity generated by thermal power plants is
provided by the steam and gas combined cycle plants in Japan.
Currently, most of the combined cycle power plants are composed
by 1100°C and 1300°C inlet temperature class gas combustion
turbines, in which polycrystalline and directionally solidiﬁed Ni
base superalloys are used as blade materials. So far, the authors
/H208511/H20852clariﬁed the temperature and stress distribution within the ﬁrst
blade rows of these gas turbines by conducting temperature and
stress analysis using their own developed ﬁnite element code.
From the analysis results, it was found that some parts of the
blade surface are subjected to normal stress in the longitudinal
direction, and shear stress in the radial direction, and the other
part of the surface is subjected to normal tensile and compressive
stress, both in the longitudinal and radial directions, depending on
the thermal gradient between the inner and outer surfaces of the
blade during start-stop operations. The authors /H208512–5/H20852performed
thermomechanical fatigue tests under tensile-compressive and tor-
sional strain loading, in which surface strain and temperature
cycling conditions of the blade were simulated, on IN738LC and
GTD 111DS superalloys, and the thermomechanical fatigue life
prediction method was proposed, based on the experimental re-
sults.
On the other hand, no study of high temperature fatigue life
property under biaxial tension-compression condition on the Ni
base superalloys has been reported due to difﬁculty of in-plane
biaxial fatigue testing on superalloys at high temperatures. In or-
der to predict crack initiation life on the blade surface under the
biaxial tension-compression fatigue loading, as mentioned above,
it is necessary to obtain the in-plane biaxial fatigue life property at
high temperature, and to develop a biaxial fatigue life evaluation
method for the Ni base superalloys. Establishment of the biaxial
fatigue life evaluation method is necessary for the maintenance of
gas turbine blades. Additionally, it is interesting from an academic
point of view to clarify the effect of grain orientation or solidiﬁed
direction on the fatigue life of the superalloys, as well as the effect
of the biaxial stress on the fatigue life.
In this study, the in-plane biaxial fatigue tests were conducted
on both a polycrystalline conventional casting /H20849CC/H20850and a direc-
tionally solidiﬁed /H20849DS/H20850Ni base superalloys at high temperatures.
Deformation behavior and feature of failure appearance under the
biaxial fatigue loading were clariﬁed, and a biaxial fatigue life
criterion for the directionally solidiﬁed Ni base superalloy was
proposed, based on the experimental results.

2 Test Procedure
2.1 Materials and Specimen Geometry. Materials used in
this study are a polycrystalline Ni base superalloy, IN738LC, and
a directionally solidiﬁed Ni base superalloy, equivalent to
GTD111DS. Chemical compositions of these materials are shown

3 /H20849length,

The geometry of a cruciform specimen used in this study is
shown in Fig. 1. The total length of the specimen is 95 mm, and a
diameter of the central portion of the specimen is 11 mm with
0.75 mm thickness. Radius of the gage length is 10 mm. The
cruciform specimen was machined from the plate using an electric
discharge machining technique, and the surface of the specimen
was ﬁnished with a buff. The x axis of the GTD111DS specimen
corresponds to the solidiﬁed direction of the material.
2.2 In-Plane Biaxial Fatigue Testing Machine. An in-plane
biaxial fatigue testing machine is used in this study. Two sets of
hydraulic pressure driven actuators, so called x and y directions,
are installed in the main frame with a 45 deg inclination from the
horizontal direction. The maximum load is 25 kN and the maxi-
mum stroke is 10 mm. The load ratio and phase difference be-
tween the x and y directions are controlled arbitrarily by a com-
puter. Not only a LVDT, a load cell and an extensometer output,
but also a differentiation of the LVDT output between the x and y
directions is fed back to maintain the center position of the cruci-
form specimen. As a result, the movement of the specimen center
was minimized within 1.48
/H9262m.
The specimen is heated by an induction heating coil from one
side. The temperature is measured by a thermocouple attached on
the specimen and controlled by a digital PID system. The tem-
perature is controlled within /H110065°C within the gage length at the
maximum test temperature. Normal strains in the x and y direc-
tions are measured by using a high temperature biaxial extensom-
eter, which was developed for the cruciform specimen, indepen-
dently and simultaneously.
2.3 Test Conditions. Test temperature for the IN738LC was
850°C, and that for the GTD111DS was 870°C, considering the
operating temperature of both materials /H208511/H20852. Strain control tests
were performed with a triangular strain waveform. The strain ratio
/H9278was deﬁned as the ratio between the x and y directional strains
at 1/4 cycle, where the strain reaches the peak absolute values.
Adopted test conditions were /H9278=1, where the x and y directional
strains are cycled with the same phase, /H9278=0, where either the x or
y directional strain is equal to 0, and /H9278=−1, where the x and y
directional strains are applied with 180 deg phase difference. For
the GTD111DS, 6 min strain hold time tests at either the maxi-
mum compressive or the maximum tensile strain was conducted
under
/H9278=1. Failure life was deﬁned as cycles where either the x
or y directional stress reduced to 75% of its maximum value.
