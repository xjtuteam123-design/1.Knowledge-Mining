ABSTRACT 
The influences of nitrogen alloying on the microstructural evolution and tensile properties of CoCrFeMnNi high- 
entropy alloys (HEAs), which were subjected to cold rolling and subsequent annealing at 773 – 1173 K, were 
systematically investigated. The results show cold rolling-induced microbands in the nitrogen-alloyed HEAs 
instead of the deformation twins and shear bands found in nitrogen - free HEAs. During annealing at 773 – 873 K, 
the cold - rolled nitrogen - free HEAs experience a partial annihilation of the deformation twins, while the cold- 
rolled nitrogen - alloyed HEAs exhibited higher microstructural stability. When the annealing temperature ex -
ceeds 973 K, a large number of Cr
2
N precipitates form in the recrystallized regions of the nitrogen-alloyed HEAs, 
and the tensile strength of the cold-rolled nitrogen - free HEAs decreases with increasing annealing temperature. 
However, the tensile strength of the nitrogen-alloyed HEAs annealed at 773 and 873 K experiences an abnormal 
increase compared to that of the cold-rolled sample. The best combination of strength and ductility are achieved 
in the nitrogen-alloyed HEAs treated by cold-rolling and annealing at 973 K. When the annealing temperature 
increases to 1073 – 1173 K, the nitrogen-alloyed and -free HEAs exhibit completely recrystallized structures with 
relatively low strength and excellent ductility. The influences of nitrogen alloying on the microstructural evo -
lution are related to the interaction between nitrogen atoms and dislocations, and the multi-mechanism 
(including nitrogen solid-solution strengthening, precipitation strengthening, and grain refinement strength -
ening) accounts for the improved tensile properties of the nitrogen-alloyed HEAs treated by cold-rolling and 
subsequent annealing at 973 K. 
1. Introduction 
Single-phase CoCrFeMnNi high-entropy alloy (HEA) with equia -
tomic ratio is one of the most promising and well-studied HEAs [ 1 – 3 ]. It 
has a single face-centered cubic (FCC) and stable phase structure [ 4 , 5 ]; 
nevertheless, several researchers have reported that the precipitation of 
secondary phases can occur after annealing at intermediate tempera -
tures [ 6 , 7 ]. These alloys exhibit a remarkable work hardening capacity 
at room and cryogenic temperatures, which leads to high ductility and 
excellent fracture toughness [ 8 – 10 ]. However, the relatively lower 
strength limits the wide application of CoCrFeMnNi HEAs [ 2 ]. 
The addition of substitutional atoms [ 11 – 17 ] or interstitial atoms 
[ 18 – 22 ] can improve the mechanical properties of HEAs through 
solid-solution and precipitation strengthening. Regarding substitutional 
atom strengthening, Agustianingrum et al. [ 14 ] showed that the addi -
tion of Al evidently increases the yield strength (YS) of Al
x
CoCrNi 
medium-entropy alloys (MEAs). Moreover, significant strengthening 
due to the formation of a precipitation phase was discovered in 
Ti-containing FeCoNiCr HEAs [ 17 ]. Compared to substitutional atoms, 
interstitial atoms can induce greater lattice distortions, which result in 
greater solid-solution strengthening [ 21 ]. For example, Chen et al. [ 22 ] 
discovered that the addition of 0.83 at. % carbon can improve the me -
chanical properties of as-cast equiatomic CoCrFeMnNi HEAs, and Peng 
et al. [ 20 ] observed that carbides in annealed CoCrFeMnNi – 1.3C HEAs

2. Experiment

4. Discussions 
4.1. Effects of nitrogen alloying on microstructural evolution during cold 
rolling 
As previously mentioned, the differences in the cold-rolled micro -
structures of the two HEA groups indicate an inherent influence of ni -
trogen alloying. It has been reported that the operating sequence of the 
deformation mechanism in CoCrFeMnNi HEAs treated without nitrogen 
alloying during cold rolling includes dislocation slipping, deformation 
twinning, and SB formation [ 42 – 44 ]. The present N0-CR HEAs exhibit 
similar cold-rolled microstructural features. However, the cold-rolled 
deformations in the N1-CR HEAs are dominated by dislocation slip -
ping, which is characterized by uniformly distributed MBs ( Fig. 4 c). In 
addition, the dislocation density of the N1-CR HEAs is slightly higher 
than that of the N0-CR HEAs after the cold-rolling deformation ( Fig. 9 a). 
This proves that the N1-CR HEA experiences a stronger dislocation slip 
deformation than the N0-CR HEAs. It is already well known that the 
deformation mechanism of an alloy at a given temperature depends on 
its stacking fault energy (SFE) [ 45 ]. In the case of a low SFE, the 
deformation mechanism exhibits deformation-induced twinning for an 
increased resistance to dislocation slipping [ 46 ]. For example, the 
twinning-induced plasticity (TWIP) effect plays an important role in the 
cold deformation of austenitic TWIP steel when the SFE is in the range of 
20 – 40 mJ/m
2 
[ 46 ]. The reported SFE range of CoCrFeMnNi HEAs at 
room temperature is approximately 18.3 – 27.3 mJ/m
2 
[ 47 , 48 ]. Thus, the 
occurrence of deformation twins in the N0-CR HEAs is reasonable. 
Furthermore, SBs are frequently observed as the result of local defor -
mation heterogeneity in severely cold-rolled HEAs [ 31 , 49 ]. Stepanov 
et al. [ 49 ] discovered SBs formed in cold-rolled CoCrFeMnNi HEAs at the 
late deformation stage with a strain of 80% at 293 K and 40% at 77 K 
owing to saturated twin deformation at a high rolling strain, which leads 
to the formation of SBs for deformation coordination. Furthermore, ni -
trogen alloying can increase the SFE remarkably when the nitrogen 
concentration does not exceed 1.5 at. % in Fe – 18Cr – 16Ni – 10Mn 
austenitic steels [ 50 ]. Owing to the similar concentrations of CoCr -
FeMnNi HEAs and Fe – 18Cr – 16Ni – 10Mn steels, it seems reasonable that 
the SFE of the N1-CR HEAs (0.9 at. % nitrogen) is higher than that of the 
N0-CR HEAs. Indeed, Moravcik et al. [ 30 ] discovered that nitrogen 
alloying reduces deformation twinning in CoCrNi MEAs, and Guo et al. 
[ 38 ] found a large number of MBs and no twins in cold-rolled 
CoCrFeMnNi – C1.0 HEAs. Similarly, N1-CR HEAs prefer to form MBs 
rather than deformation twins during cold rolling for an increased SFE 
by nitrogen alloying. 
4.2. Effects of nitrogen alloying on microstructural evolution during 
annealing 
For the N0-CR-773 and N0-CR-873 HEAs, the recovery of disloca -
tions and annihilation of deformation twins is the main characteristic 
during the annealed-microstructural evolution ( Fig. 7a and b, and 9 a). 
The annihilation of deformation twins is due to two aspects: first, the 
deformation twins are gradually swallowed by adjacent sub-grains by a 
bulge mechanism [ 51 ]; second, the density of twin bands decreases, and 
the width of twin bands increases by a consolidation mechanism; 
eventually, the interfaces of twins with widened lamellae disappear 
owing to the dislocation activity [ 51 ]. In addition, no sigma phase can be 
found in N0-CR-773 and N0-CR-873 HEAs ( Fig. 7 a and b). Nevertheless, 
many researchers have reported the phase decomposition and sigma 
phase formation in CoCrFeMnNi HEAs during low temperature anneal -
ing (773 – 873 K) [ 6 , 7 , 52 , 53 ]. This is due to the fact that the formation of 
a sigma phase at 773 – 873 K requires recrystallization [ 54 ] or prolonged 
annealing [ 6 , 7 ]. In this case, it may be impossible to form sigma phases 
in N0-CR-773 and N0-CR-873 HEAs. By contrast, the N1-CR-773 and 
N1-CR-873 HEAs exhibit higher microstructural stability ( Fig. 7 c and d). 
It is well known that interstitial atoms (i.e., carbon or nitrogen atoms) 
prefer segregating in regions with high density dislocations during 
annealing or aging [ 55 ]. Indeed, Moravcik et al. [ 56 ] investigated car -
bon segregation at recrystallized grain boundaries of CoCrNi MEAs using 
atom probe tomography, which effectively hindered the grain boundary 
migration. Therefore, the influences of nitrogen alloying on the micro -
structural evolution of annealed N1-CR HEAs can be associated with the 
dragging effect on the dislocation movement due to nitrogen atoms. 
Although no concrete proof of nitrogen segregation are presented here, 
the dragging effect of the nitrogen atoms on the climbing and slipping of 
dislocations and recovery and recrystallization of N1-CR HEAs during 
low temperature annealing is reasonable. When the annealing temper -
ature increases to 973 K, the two groups of cold-rolled HEAs experience 
recrystallization. However, the average size in the N1-CR-973 HEAs 
decreases to 1.85 μ m, which is evidently smaller than the 6.82 μ m of the 
N0-CR-973 HEAs ( Fig. 6 ). There are two possible reasons to explain this 
result: first, nitrogen atoms can impede the motion of dislocations and 
lead to a considerable increase in the deformation storage energy during 
cold rolling. Therefore, the N1-CR HEAs have a higher recrystallization

4.3. Effects of nitrogen alloying on tensile properties 
As shown in Fig. 11 a and b, the tensile strengths of the N1-CR HEAs 
are 18% higher than those of the N0-CR HEAs under cold-rolling con -
ditions. This can be attributed to the significant solid-solution 
strengthening effect of the nitrogen in CoCrFeMnNi HEAs [ 28 ]. How -
ever, both the N0-CR and N1-CR HEAs exhibit poor ductility ( Fig. 11 c) 
and weak strain hardening capability ( Fig. 10 c and d). This is because 
the high-density tangled dislocations in the N0-CR and N1-CR HEAs are 
difficult to move during tension deformation ( Fig. 9 a). After annealing 
at 773 – 873 K, the N0-CR HEAs undergo a decomposition of the 
deformed sub-structures owing to the recovery of dislocations ( Fig. 9 a) 
and the annihilation of deformation twins ( Fig. 7 a and b). Consequently, 
the YS and UTS of the N0-CR-773 and N0-CR-873 HEAs gradually 
decrease. In contrary, the N1-CR HEAs show an abnormal increase in the 
tensile strength under the same annealing conditions. This abnormal 
increase in the tensile strength has been observed for CoCrFeMnNi HEAs 
after cold rolling and annealing at 773 – 873 K [ 39 , 59 – 61 ]. Gu et al. [ 60 , 
61 ] ascribed it to the formation of long-range ordered structures in the 
HEA matrix. Moreover, Li et al. [ 59 ] and Klimova et al. [ 39 ] explained 
that the Cr-rich phases and sigma particles formed during annealing 
promote the precipitation hardening. However, no long-range ordered 
structure or Cr-rich particles can be observed in the deformed sub -
structures in the N1-CR HEAs annealed at 773 – 873 K ( Fig. 7 c and d). In 
addition, Li et al. [ 62 ] demonstrated that dislocations in nitrogen-doped 
silicon move more slowly and shorter distances than those in 
nitrogen-free silicon, which is owing to the pinning effect of segregated 
nitrogen atoms on dislocations. Similarly, it can be inferred that the 
segregation of nitrogen atoms in the dislocation regions may be the 
reason for the abnormal increase in the tensile strength. 
When the annealing temperature increases to 973 K, the usual 
variation in the trade-off of the tensile behavior occurs in two annealed 
HEA groups. The N1-CR-973 HEAs exhibit a better combination of 
strength and ductility, which is due to the multi-mechanism (including 
nitrogen solid-solution strengthening, precipitation strengthening, and 
grain refinement strengthening) resulting from nitrogen alloying. First, 
the grain size in the recrystallized region of the N1-CR-973 HEAs is 
evidently smaller than that of the N0-CR-973 HEAs ( Fig. 6 ). In addition, 
a large number of Cr
2

the MBs in the deformation regions improve the tensile strength and 
strain hardening capability.

5. Conclusions 
In this study, the influences of nitrogen alloying on the microstruc -
ture and mechanical properties of CoCrFeMnNi HEAs treated by cold 
rolling and subsequent annealing at 773 – 1173 K were systematically 
investigated. The following conclusions can be drawn: 
1 The microstructural evolution of the N0-CR HEAs was dominated by 
deformation twinning and SBs; whereas the microstructural evolu -
tion of the N1-CR HEAs exhibited uniformly distributed MBs. This is 
because nitrogen alloying increases the SFE of CoCrFeMnNi HEAs 
and thus promotes dislocation slipping during cold rolling. 
2 When annealed at 773 – 873 K, the N0-CR HEAs experienced the 
partial annihilation of deformation twins, while the N1-CR HEAs 
exhibited higher microstructural stability. With further increasing 
annealing temperatures, the N0-CR and N1-CR HEAs experienced 
recrystallization. The dragging effect of nitrogen atoms played an 
important role in the grain refinement of the annealed N1-CR HEAs. 
In addition, a large number of Cr
2
N precipitates formed in the 
recrystallized regions of the nitrogen-alloyed HEAs. 
3 The tensile strength of the N0-CR HEAs decreased with increasing 
annealing temperatures. However, the tensile strength of the N1-CR- 
773 and N1-CR-873 HEAs exhibited an abnormal increase compared 
to that of the cold-rolled samples. This was due to the segregation of 
nitrogen atoms in the regions with high densities of dislocations and 
the consequent dragging effect on dislocations. 
4 The N1-CR-973 HEAs exhibited the best combination of tensile 
strength and ductility under the presented processing conditions was 
achieved in the N1-CR-973 HEAs. The YS and UTS reached to 872 
MPa, 1025 MPa, respectively, together with the UE of 9.57%. The 
multi-mechanism in terms of nitrogen solid-solution strengthening, 
precipitation strengthening and refine grain strengthening resulted 
from the nitrogen-alloying can account for it.
