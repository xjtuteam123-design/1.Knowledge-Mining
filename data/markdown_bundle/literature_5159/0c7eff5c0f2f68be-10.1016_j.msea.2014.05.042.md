abstract
The high quality Mg –xYmm ( x ¼6, 9, 12) –4Zn (Ymm ¼Y-rich misch metal, wt%) alloy ingots with
diameter of 90 –95 mm and length of 600 mm were successfully fabricated by water-cooled mold
casting, and the microstructure and tensile properties have been investigated. The results demonstrated
that the alloys contained four secondary phases: a plenty of 18R LPSO and 14H LPSO phases and a few of
Mg
3Zn3RE2 and Mg 24Y5-type compounds. The ultimate tensile strength of Mg –12Ymm–4Zn alloy could
keep above 130 MPa until 400 1C. The excellent high-temperature strength was mainly attributed to
dense LPSO lamellae strengthening Mg matrix and network-like LPSO phase reinforcing grain
boundaries.

1. Introduction
Recently, the research of lightweight magnesium (Mg) alloys
has become prosperous due to their potential applications in
aerospace, automobile and 3C industries. However, the inherently
weak properties of Mg alloys such as low absolute strength and
poor heat-resistance seriously hinder their wide applications.
It has been well known that rare earths (RE) have a favorable
effect on Mg alloys, and the Mg –Gd [1,2],M g –Y[3,4],M g –Ho[5],
Mg–Dy [6] based alloys exhibited superior strength and heat-
resistance compared with common Mg alloys. In recent years,
researchers paid much more attentions to Mg –RE–Zn alloys
(RE¼ Y, Gd, Ho, Er, Dy, Tm, Tb) strengthened by the novel long
period stacking ordered (LPSO) phase [7,8]. However, very little
reports refer to the performance of as-cast alloys strengthened
mainly by LPSO phase.
Both the high performance and low cost of Mg alloys are the
main factors that accelerate their widespread application. How-
ever, in fact, the price of most pure RE metals is expensive,
especially the heavy RE metals. In this work, the Yttrium-rich
misch metal (Ymm) was selected as the main alloying elements,
because its price is much lower than that of pure heavy RE metals.
Table 1 lists the main compositions of Ymm. Besides, the water-
cooled mold casting was employed in order to achieve high
cooling rate to obtain high quality ingot. Finally, three Mg –xYmm
(x¼6, 9, 12) –4Zn (wt%) alloy ingots were fabricated, and their
microstructure and tensile properties at room and elevated tem-
peratures were investigated.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructure
Fig. 2 shows optical micrographs of the as-cast Mg –xYmm
(x¼6, 9, 12) –4Zn (wt%) alloys. It reveals that the as-cast alloys are
mainly composed of α-Mg matrix and intermetallic phases mainly
distributed at the grain boundaries and in the interdendritic
regions. With the increasing Ymm content, the dentritic structure
is more developed, and the average grain sizes reduce gradually
and they are about 108 μm, 97 μm and 76 μm, respectively, which
is according to the linear intercept method and the consistent
orientation of lamellae in a same α-Mg grain. At the same time, the
amount of intermetallics compounds increases obviously, and their
morphologies change gradually from semi-continuous network
to continuous network. Besides, two types of secondary phases are
observed at the grain boundaries an d in the interdendritic regions,
as shown in Fig. 2(a) and (b), i.e. a few of eutectic compounds with
black contrast and a plenty of block-like compounds with gray
contrast. While with the increase of Ymm content, the eutectic
compounds are reduced drastically. Another remarkable feature of
the microstructure is ﬁne-lamellar compounds which possess
speciﬁc orientation in a same α-Mg grain and occupy almost all
the α-Mg matrix. Both of the block-like phase and the ﬁne-lamellar
phase have a special LPSO structure and their con ﬁrmation is
discussed in the following section.
Fig. 3 shows the magni ﬁed SEM images and EDS results to
reveal the secondary phases of Mg –Ymm–Zn alloy. Fig. 4 reveals
the XRD analysis and Fig. 5 further presents the TEM observations
and the selected area electron diffraction (SAED) patterns. Four
secondary phases are found in the alloy under SEM observation,
which are marked as A, B, C, D in Fig. 3 (b), and the corresponding
EDS results are revealed in Fig. 3 (c)–(f), respectively. The phase
marked as A is the gray and block-like one. Especially, under the

The SAED pattern ( Fig. 5 (b)) further con ﬁrms the 18R-type LPSO
structure, in which additional spots occur at positions n/6 (where
n is an integer) of the (0002) Mg diffraction. This is the evidence
commonly used in previous studies [9] to prove the existence of
18R LPSO structure. The ﬁne lamellae with certain orientation in a
α-Mg grain are marked with B in Fig. 3 (b). Under observation of
TEM image ( Fig. 5 (c)), the spacing between the dense lamellae is
just from several nanometers to several tens of nanometers. SAED
analysis ( Fig. 5 (d)) con ﬁrms that they are 14H LPSO structure,
which is different with that of block-like phase.
The typical eutectic compounds are marked as C, and their EDS
results reveal that the atomic ratio of Zn and RE (RE contains Y, Er,
Ho and others) is about 3:2. Combined with the XRD analysis, they
are identi ﬁed to be Mg 3Zn3RE2 compounds, i.e. W phase men-
tioned in previous reports [10]. Also, they are further con ﬁrmed
as Mg 3Zn3RE2 with fcc structure by TEM and SAED analysis
(Fig. 5 (e) and (f)). The fcc structure W phase is not considered to
be an effective strengthening phase in Mg alloys [10]. While the
formation amount of W phase is small, especially in high Ymm
content alloys, so it is believed that the W phase has little negative
impact on the performance of the alloy. In addition, very few of
cubic shaped phase (marked as D) are observed through SEM
observation. Based on the EDS results, the cubic shaped particles
are identi ﬁed as Mg 24Y5-type compounds, which are commonly
observed in Mg-Y based alloys [1 1]. However this phase is not
detected by XRD and TEM due to its trace amounts. Although
Mg24Y5 phase is treated as a strengthening phase in Mg-Y based
alloys [12], in view of trace amounts, it is believed that this phase
has little effect on the performance of these alloys.

3.2. Mechanical properties
Fig. 6 shows the tensile properties of the as-cast Mg –xYmm
(x¼6, 9, 12) –4Zn (wt%) alloys tested from room temperature (RT)
to 400 1C. The three alloys exhibit excellent high temperature
strength. As for Mg –6Ymm–4Zn alloy, its ultimate tensile strength
(UTS) is 192 MPa at RT, which is the highest value among that of
the three alloys. The UTS decreases with increasing temperature,
but the decrease rate of the UTS is much smaller than that of the
commercial AZ91 alloy [13], as shown in Fig. 6 (a). The as-cast
Mg–6Ymm–4Zn alloy exhibits same UTS at RT compared with the
as-cast AZ91 alloy, while the UTS is upto 138 MPa at 300 1C and
much higher than 50 MPa, i.e. the UTS of AZ91 alloy.
More notable, the alloys exhibit better high temperature
strength with the increase of Ymm content, though their RT
strength is lower than that of Mg –6Ymm–4Zn alloy. Especially
for Mg–12Ymm–4Zn alloy, the UTS tested at elevated temperature
upto 350 1C is still higher than RT strength. This phenomenon
of higher strength at high-temperatures was found in very few
Mg-RE based alloys with high RE content which were strength-
ened by β0 nano precipitate phase [14]. Even at 400 1C, the UTS is
still upto 137 MPa, indicating that the retention of strength is upto
90% at 400 1C compared with strength of 151 MPa at RT. Both the
relative low RT strength and the excellent high-temperature
strength are mainly related to the large volume fraction of LPSO
phase. It was acknowledged that the formation of the hard, ductile
and thermally stable LPSO phase in Mg alloys is bene ﬁcial to both
strength and ductility [15,16], however it also should be noted
the great in ﬂuence of its morphology and distribution. At room
temperature, the dislocation motion would be inhibited severely at
grain boundaries due to the existence of the continuous and
network-like LPSO phase; subsequently the localized stress con-
centration causes the microcracks to nucleate easily at the inter-
face of LPSO phase and Mg matrix. Consequently, the premature
fracture leads to relatively low strength and also low ductility as
shown in Fig. 6 (b). The large area fraction of cleavage planes are
observed from the SEM micrograph of the fracture surface after
tensile tests at RT, as shown in Fig. 7 (a), which conforms well to
the tensile results. While at elevated temperatures, more deforma-
tion mechanisms are activated and localized stress concentration
is effectively released. Moreover, it has reported that the LPSO
structure phase can kink or bend itself to afford certain plastic
deformation and coordinate overall deformation of the material,
especially at high temperatures [16], which is very different
from the common brittle intermetallics and is important for the
ductility of the alloy. And then the premature brittle fracture is
relieved, consequently the ductility can be brought into full play.
Compared with Fig. 7(a), it can be seen from Fig. 7(b) that the area
fraction of cleavage planes decreases obvious and some dimples
are formed on the fracture surface of the sample tested at 250 1C,
which is in accordance with the analyses and test results of tensile
elongation. On the other hand, when tested at high temperatures,
the 18R LPSO block-like phase distributed at the grain boundaries
can pin the grain boundary sliding and strengthen the boundaries
effectively. Moreover, the ﬁne, dense and uniformly dispersed 14H
LPSO lamellae within α-Mg grains can reinforce the Mg matrix.
Matsuda et al. [17] has suggested that the formation of LPSO
structure could increase the critical resolved shear stress of the
basal plane so as to hinder the basal slip to contribute to
strengthening. Also, it was reported the dislocations must travel

4. Conclusion
In the present study, Mg –xYmm ( x¼ 6, 9, 12) –4Zn (wt%) alloy
ingots were successfully prepared by water-cooled mold casting
method. Four secondary phases are observed in microstructure, i.e.
a large volume fraction of 18R LPSO and 14H LPSO phases and
a few of Mg 3Zn3RE2 and Mg 24Y5-type compounds. The UTS of
Mg–12Ymm–4Zn alloy can remain above 130 MPa till 400 1C. The
excellent high-temperature tensile properties are mainly asso-
ciated with network-like LPSO phase reinforcing grain boundaries
and dense LPSO lamellae strengthening matrix.
