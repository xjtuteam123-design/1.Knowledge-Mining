ABSTRACT
This study focuses on the effect of the main antioxidation element Al on the high-temperature oxidation resis-
tance of Ni-Cr-Co based superalloys. A novel “ 3D printing-high temperature diffusion ” high-throughput method
was used to accurately determine the critical Al content to form continuous Al
2
O
3
film. Results show that Al
2
O
3
film significantly affects the element diffusion process during oxidation, and the critical Al content to form
continuous Al
2
O
3
film is 0.8 wt%. This conclusion was further confirmed by the corresponding discrete samples.
It was also found that Ni-Cr-Co based superalloys exhibit “ oxidation weight loss ” and “ K
p
-antioxidation positive
correlation ” phenomena in high humidity condition.
1. Introduction
Ni-Cr-Co based superalloys, which can work at 760 – 870
◦
C for a long
time, are currently the most widely used and most mature engine su-
peralloy materials, and have been used in turbine disks, turbine blades
and other engine components [ 1 , 2 ]. High-temperature oxidation can
lead to catastrophic failure and is one of the main failure modes of su-
peralloy components [ 3 , 4 ]. With the increase of thrust-weight ratio and
bypass ratio of aero-engine, the service temperature of engine materials
is getting higher, so the requirement for high-temperature oxidation
resistance of Ni-Cr-Co based superalloys is gradually increasing [ 5 , 6 ].
Therefore, it is necessary to conduct systematic and precise research on
the oxidation behavior and oxidation mechanism of this alloy in order to
provide a basis for its composition optimization and thus improve its
high-temperature service capability and stability. In this article, we have
explored the proper method that can optimize the composition of the
alloy and do deep study on the optimization of Al element.
At present, the widely used 3rd generation Ni-based superalloys have
excellent comprehensive properties and can work long below 800
◦
C. In
order to meet the increasing working temperature and harsh working
environment need, 4th and 5th generation Ni-based superalloys with the
addition of refractory elements like Re and Ru are being developed
[ 7 , 8 ]. However, even though their mechanical properties are enhanced,
the oxidation resistance of them are reduced especially when the tem-
perature is over 800
◦
C [ 9 , 10 ]. Besides, high temperature can lead to
high oxygen partial pressure, and calls for better antioxidation perfor-
mance [ 5 , 11 ]. Therefore, it is important to understand the oxidation
mechanism of Ni-based superalloys and optimize the antioxidation el-
ements like Al and Cr of the alloys.
The oxidation process of Ni-Cr-Co based superalloys is the competi-
tive oxidation of Al, Cr, Ti and other elements with O, among which Al is
the primary antioxidation element. The oxidation resistance of this alloy
depends on whether a dense oxide film, which is closely related to the
structure of Al, Cr and Ti oxides, especially Al oxide, can be formed
during oxidation.
Wang [ 12 ] studied the oxidation behavior of cast GH738 in static air
at 800 – 1000
◦
C. When Al content is 1.4 % (all compositions in this work
are in wt%, unless otherwise stated), the internal oxide film is composed
entirely of Al
2
O
3
. At higher oxidation temperatures, the size of the in-
ternal oxidized aluminum becomes finer, leading to increased continuity
of internal oxidation film. Ma [ 13 ] studied the influence of grain size and
twin density on the high-temperature oxidation behavior of cast Ni-
based superalloy GH738. When Al content is 1.46 %, the finer the
grains, the denser the Al
2
O
3
film, and the better the oxidation resistance
of the alloy. Huang [ 14 ] studied the effect of Al content (16 at.%,18 at.%
and 20 at.%) on the oxidation behavior of cast Ni-based alloy at 1100
◦
C.

As an efficient method for composition optimization and new ma-
terial design, high-throughput methods have been used for the research
of process and composition optimization of Ni-Cr-Co based superalloys.
However, there are no reports on the use of these methods to study the
oxidation resistance of superalloys.
Qin [ 18 ] established a γ’ phase precipitation database using high-
throughput experiments and deep learning method. Based on this
database, they constructed a model which can predict the volume
fraction, average size and size distribution of the γ’ phase in Ni-based
superalloys. Zhu [ 19 ] studied the effect of Nb and Ta on the phase sta-
bility, microstructure and properties of a Ni
–

high-throughput preparation and analysis tools. Yang [ 20 ] proposed a
deep learning method to identify the microstructure of Ni-based super-
alloys with different compositions and heat treatment regimes during
high-throughput characterization. Wang [ 21 ] constructed a “ composi-
tion-hardness ” model for Ni-based superalloys using high-throughput
experiments and machine learning and predicted the optimal composi-
tion for high-hardness superalloys. Xu [ 22 ] studied the microstructural
evolution of Ni-based single crystal superalloys during high-temperature
creep process through high-throughput experiments and deep learning.
This study established the quantitative relationship between micro-
structural evolution and creep conditions.
Based on above analysis, this study designed a “ 3D printing-high
temperature diffusion ” high-throughput preparation method and ob-
tained Ni-Cr-Co based superalloy samples with continuously varying Al
content. With these high-throughput samples, this study characterized
the high-temperature oxidation behavior and oxidation mechanism of
Ni-Cr-Co based superalloy at 870
◦
C, which is the current maximum
service temperature of the alloy, and accurately determined the critical
Al content that can form continuous Al
2
O
3
film. Moreover, to verify the

also analyzed the oxidation behavior of discrete samples with different
Al contents. It is proved that the “ 3D printing-high temperature diffu-
sion ” high-throughput method of this study is suitable to characterize
the effect of single element or the coupling of multiple elements on the
oxidation resistance of superalloys.
2. Material and methods
2.1. Material
Ni-Cr-Co based superalloy powder (IMR, China), Al powder (99.9 %
purity, Qinhuangdao ENO High-Tech Material Development Co., China)
and Ni powder (99.9 % purity, Qinhuangdao ENO High-Tech Material
Development Co., China) prepared by gas atomization method are used
in this study. The microstructure and size distribution of the powders are
shown in Fig. 1 , and the main compositions of Ni-Cr-Co powder are
listed in Table 1 . The compositions of the Ni-Cr-Co based superalloys in
this study are based on GH4738 superalloy.
2.2. Preparation of high-throughput sample
2.2.1. 3D printing
An Al element single-gradient sample was printed using a four-
channel coaxial powder feeding laser cladding system (Nanjing Huirui
Photoelectric technology Co., iLAM511C). After dried at 80
◦
C for 12 h
in a vacuum drying oven, Ni-Cr-Co, Al and Ni powder were separately
added to three channels of the system. By controlling the feeding speed
of each feeding hoppers, a Ni-Cr-Co based superalloy sample with 0 % Al
at the lower layers and 2.5 % Al at the upper layers were prepared on a
304 stainless steel substrate. During the process, the addition of Ni and
Al powders was controlled to ensure that, apart from Ni and Al, the
content of other elements remained consistent. The printed sample with
a size around 15*15*20 mm is shown in Fig. 2 a.
The printing parameters were as follows: laser scanning speed of 10
mm/s, powder feed rate of 5 g/min, single pass width of 1.3 mm, overlap
rate of 23 %, single layer lift of the laser head at 0.5 mm, and substrate
made of 304 stainless steel. To reduce the burning loss of Al, the laser
power was set to 550 W and 450 W separately during the print of the
lower layer with 0 % Al and the upper layers with 2.5 % Al.
The sample was cut from the substrate by a DK77 series wire-cut
machine. Thus, the as-printed sample was obtained.
2.2.2. High-temperature diffusion
The diffusion characteristics of Al in the Ni-Cr-Co based superalloys
were calculated using the Diffusion Module of Thermo-Calc Software
(TC, TCNI9 and MOBNI5 database) at a diffusion temperature of 1150
◦
C
(above the γ’ solvus temperature) and diffusion time of 12 h, 24 h and
48 h [ 23 , 24 ]. As shown in Fig. 2 c, when the diffusion time is 24 h, the
diffusion distance of Al is about 500 μ m, which can meet the charac-
terization requirements.
What's more, to confirm whether Al element would diffuse signifi-
cantly during the following oxidation process, the diffusion behavior of
Al at 870
◦
C/100 h was also calculated using TC. As shown in Fig. 2 d, Al
element hardly occurs transverse diffuse during oxidation.
Based on TC calculation results, after ground, cleaned and dried, the
as-printed sample was sealed in a vacuum tube and then treated at 1150
◦
C for 24 h in a GSL-1400 × tubular furnace. Finally, the sample was
removed from the furnace and air cooled to room temperature. Thus, the
diffusion-treated sample was obtained.
2.2.3. Heat treatment
To eliminate the residual stresses in the diffusion-treated sample and
try to maintain the same microstructure as the engineering Ni-Cr-Co
based superalloy, the sample was aging treated [ 25 ]. After grinded
and cleaned, the sample was sealed in a vacuum tube, then treated at
750
◦
C for 24 h in a tubular furnace, and finally air cooled to room
temperature.
Finally, a high-throughput sample with Al gradient was obtained.

The oxidation processes of different regions are discussed in detail.
The high-temperature oxidation of Ni-Cr-Co based superalloys is a
process of the selective oxidation of solute compositions (mainly Cr, Al,
and Ti). When sufficient Al and Cr are contained in the alloy, dense oxide
film which can block further infiltration of O
2

obtained, and thereby shows an antioxidation effect. The oxidation rules
of the solute compositions can be analyzed by the free energy changes
during the oxidation process.
According to the metal-O

peralloys is Al > Ti > Cr > Ni. Because the - Δ G value for Cr (the major
alloying element of the alloy) is higher than Ni (the matrix element), Cr
is preferentially oxidized by O

are oxidized simultaneously, which is the main “ transient oxidation ”
process [ 28 ].

During the transient oxidation stage, the alloying elements react
quickly with O

stage. Therefore, the duration of the transient oxidation process is
relatively short.
What's more, during the transient oxidation process, Al is hardly
involved, resulting in the similar oxidation behavior in SI and SIII re-
gions. However, in the “ steady-state oxidation ” stage, due to the for-
mation of Al oxidation film, significantly oxidation behavior differences
arise in SI and SIII regions.

Ni-Cr-Co based superalloys. The outer and internal oxide films formed
by them can prevent further infiltration of O
2
into the matrix and slow
down the oxidation of the alloy.
The steady-state oxidation process of SIII region is illustrated in
Fig. 7 d. Since no Al exists, the antioxidation of this region can only rely
on the outer oxide film. However, as discussed above, the density of the
outer oxide film is poor, so the inward diffusion of oxygen and outward
diffusion of Cr, Ti and Ni continue, in other words, the alloy is contin-
uously oxidized. Moreover, the NiO phases which formed in steady-state
oxidation process further react with the Cr
2
O
3
in the outer oxide film
and generate a large amount of NiCr
2
O
4
spinel phases, leading to a
continuous increase in the thickness of the outer oxide film.
During the steady-state oxidation stage, the formation of the outer
oxide film inhibits the outward diffusion of alloying elements and the
inward diffusion of oxygen, so diffusion process becomes the rate-
controlling step. Therefore, the duration of the steady-state oxidation
process is longer than the transient oxidation stage.
In summary, the differences in the microstructure and size of the
oxide films in SI region (with enough Al) and SIII region (without Al) lie
in whether a dense Al

three-layer structure consist of outer oxide film, sub-outer oxide film,
and internal oxide film is formed, and the outer oxide film of this
structure is thin. As contrast, in SIII region, a two-layer structure consist
of outer oxide film and sub-outer oxide film is formed, and the outer
oxide film of this structure is thick.
The wrinkling, cracking and peeling of the oxide film observed in
Fig. 5 a can be attributed to the generation of growth and thermal stress
during oxidation [ 32 ]. Thermal stress arises from the thermal expansion
coefficients difference between the matrix and the oxide film, and
generates during the heating and cooling of the sample. Growth stress
results from the volume differences between the matrix and the oxide
film, and its stress level is related to the Pilling-Bedworth ratio (PBR)
[ 33 ]. When PBR > 1, the oxide intrudes into the metal, acting as a linear
expansion center, and creates short-range tensile stress within the metal.
Conversely, when PBR < 1, short-range compressive stress is generated
within the oxide. The PBR values for Al

O
3

and Cr

1.70, and 2.07, respectively, while the PBR value for NiCr

greater than that of Cr

[ 34 ]. Consequently, the growth stress in the
outer oxide film composed of Cr

During the oxidation of the Ni-Cr-Co based superalloys, the types of

outer oxide layer.

oxide layer. The higher the diffusion coefficient, the closer of their
oxides to the surface [ 35 ].

4. Conclusions
This study focuses on the influence of Al content on the oxidation
resistance of Ni-Cr-Co based superalloys at 870
◦
C/100 h using the “ 3D
printing-high temperature diffusion ” high-throughput method. The
following conclusions can be drawn:
1. High-throughput sample with continuous gradient Al can be ob-
tained using the “ 3D printing-high temperature diffusion ”
method. With this sample, the effect of Al on the oxidation
resistance of Ni-Cr-Co based superalloys can be studied quickly
and efficiently.
2. The oxidation of Ni-Cr-Co based superalloys can be divided into
two stages, “ transient oxidation ” and “ steady-state oxidation ” .
During the steady-state oxidation stage, the formation of the in-
ternal Al
2
O
3
film significantly affects the elemental diffusion in
the alloy, thereby determines the oxidation behavior of the alloy.
3. The ability to form a continuous Al
2
O
3
film is crucial for assessing
the oxidation resistance of Ni-Cr-Co based superalloy.
4. The critical Al content to form a continuous Al
2
O
3
film in the Ni-
Cr-Co based superalloys of this study is 0.8 wt%. Therefore, based
on this study, in order to maintain a good antioxidation proper-
ties, Al content in Al-Cr-Co should be higher than 0.8 wt%.
(1) When Al < 0.8 wt%: The oxide film is thick and the interface
between the film and the matrix is rough; The external oxide film
which is mainly composed of Cr
