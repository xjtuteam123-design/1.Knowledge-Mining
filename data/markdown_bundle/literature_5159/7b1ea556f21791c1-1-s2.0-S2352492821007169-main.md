1. Introduction 
In recent years, Ni – Ti – Al alloys have been widely used in structural 
metallic applications because of their high strength, high temperature 
resistance, and good oxidation resistance [1 – 4] . Therefore, several 
studies have focused on the physical and mechanical properties of the 
Ni – Ti – Al binary and ternary systems [5 – 7] . Among this system, the 
nickel – titanium (NiTi) material is well known as one of the best shape 
memory alloys (SMAs) because of its high shape recovery property, 
superelastic behavior, and favorable biocompatibility [8 – 10] . Because 
of the unique properties, NiTi has been widely used in numerous fields, 
such as aerospace [8,11] , clinical applications [8,12 – 20] , electrical de -
vices [13] , and endodontic instruments [21] . However, NiTi SMAs 
generate significant recovery stress if restrictions exist during heating, 
which may reach up to 800 MPa [22 – 24] . Moreover, because of their 
high melting point, high strength and corrosion resistance, and excellent 
creep resistance, titanium – aluminum (TiAl) alloys are known as 
high-temperature materials [25 – 29] . However, TiAl alloys provide low 
ductility at room temperature, which severely limits the applications of 
this material [30,31] . 
Many studies have shown that the Ni – Ti – Al ternary system has better 
mechanical properties than the binary system. Koizumi et al. showed 
that with Al atom addition, the compression strength of NiTi – base alloys 
is drastically improved [5] . Zhang et al. reported that TiAl alloys with a 
small amount of Ni addition will improve the steady-state flow behavior, 
sensitivity of the strain rate, and thermal deformability [32] . Strutt et al. 
demonstrated that the high-temperature creep strength of β -NiAl can be 
improved by Ti atom introduction [33] . Consequently, the above studies 
attracted our interest in ternary Ni-Ti-Al alloy.

4. Conclusion 
In summary, we recorded the deformation mechanism under various 
conditions of Ni – Ti – Al nanowires, by changing the grain size, temper -
ature, and Al content in the tensile process. We concluded that for small 
grains (grain size < 80 Å), the stress concentration on the grain 
boundaries. Moreover, for large grains, dislocation defects appeared 
inside the grains. Besides, the temperature affected the mechanical be -
haviors of the alloy as well. The UTS values decreased with an increase 
in the temperature. Furthermore, with Al atoms addition, the ductility of 
a NiTiAl material is increased. There are stacking fault defects that exist 
in the gradient Al content specimens. However, the stacking fault defect 
did not significantly affect the destructive behavior of the materials. 
Consequently, it can be demonstrated that the size of nanograins and 
temperature were the main factors that directly affected the material ’ s 
destructive mechanisms. The critical grain size of the Ni

microstructure design or applications of the NiTiAl ternary alloys.
