CONDENSED-STATE PHYSICS 
TRIBOTECHNICAL AND MECHANICAL PROPERTIES OF 
COARSE-GRAINED AND SUBMICROCRYSTALLINE Ti–Al–V 
(VT6) ALLOY WITH A Ti–C–Mo–S MULTI-COMPONENT 
ANTIFRICTION COATING 
A. I. Potekaev,1 V. M. Savostikov,1 A. N. Tabachenko,1 UDC 621.73 
E. F. Dudarev,1 and I. A. Shulepov2 
The results of a comparative study of the effect of a nanophase Ti–C–Mo–S coating on tribotechnical and 
mechanical properties of a VT6 ( α+β)-titanium alloy in its coarse-grained and submicrocrystalline states are 
presented. To form the coating of a Ti–C–Mo–S chemical composition, use is made of a low-temperature 
magnetron-plasma deposition process at the substrate temperature not higher than 300° С. With a 1–1.3µm 
multi-component nanophase coating, the friction coefficient in a friction pair of the like grain structure 
(submicrocrystalline or coarse-grained) is shown to reduce by a few factors, while the wear resistance appears 
to increase by two orders of magnitude. The high strength achieved in the alloy due to the formation of the 
submicrocrystalline structure is maintained. 
Keywords: titanium alloy, submicrocrysta lline structure, strength, multi- component coating, magnetron-
plasma synthesis, friction coefficient, wear resistance. 
INTRODUCTION 
An urgent problem for titanium alloys has been a concurrent improvement of mechanical and tribotechnical 
properties. By now a good deal of experimental data has been accumulate d, which have demonstrated how to 
significantly increase strength of commercially pure titanium and its alloys while preserving a satisfactory plasticity via 
formation of ultrafine-grained structure (submicrocrystalline and nanocrystalline) using severe plastic deformation [1–
4]. On the other hand, this has not completely solved the problem of improving tribotechnical properties of these 
materials while maintaining their submicrocrystalline and na nocrystalline structure [5, 6]. The issue is to improve 
tribotechnical properties of a friction pair made from the same alloy of the like grain-subgrain structure. 
In the case of submicrocrystalline stru cture, conventional processes of m odification of titanium alloy surfaces 
by oxidizing, nitriding, borating, etc., cannot be used. This is due to the high temperatures of the process of coating 
synthesis (~800–1000ºС), at which their submicrocrystalline structure degrades and strength decreases. In view of this 
circumstance, a comparatively low temperature is required for coating formation on submicrocrystalline alloys, at 
which the submicrocrystalline structure is still thermally stable. A met hod for magnetron-plasma deposition of a multi-
component coating under such conditions was proposed in [7]. It has been applied to synthesize nanocomposite 
tribotechnical coatings on several coarse -grained and submicrocrystalline titanium alloys only. With this coating, all 
these alloys demonstrated significantly improved tribotechni cal properties practically without deterioration of their

EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURE 
The experimental material used in this study was a VT6 medium-strength ( α+β)-titanium alloy containing 
~6% Al and ~4.5% V (wt.%) [10]. To form submicrocrystalline structure in the alloy by severe plastic deformation, use 
was made of an аbс-pressing process at elevated te mperatures. A temperature-deformation scenario of pressing was 
used, which allowed a higher strength (~ 1000 MPa) compared to th e coarse-grained state to be formed, with a relative 
elongation to failure being no less than 10%. For tribotechnical tests the specimens were prepared both from coarse-
grained (VT6cg) and submicrocrystalline (VT6 ns) alloy. Multi-component Ti–C–Mo–S co atings on the su bstrates from 
coarse-grained and submicrocrystalline alloy were deposited in a Kompozit-3 facility containing a duo-magnetron with 
two disk-shaped sputtering cathode targets of the same chemical composition (Ti–C–Mo–S) inside the processing 
chamber [11]. When forming an antifricti on coating, the substrate surface was in all cases pre-processed by ion-plasma 
doping [7]. The regimes of coating deposition ensu red the substrate temperature not higher than 300 С. The deposition 
time was 60 minutes for the same current-voltage characteristics of the magnetron discharge at the substrate 
accelerating voltage 200 V. The coa ting thickness was within 1–1.3 µm . Mechanical properties of the 
submicrocrystalline VT6ns alloy prior to and after coating deposition were measured at room temperature by tensile 
loading of flat test pieces shaped as dumb-bells. The flow curves were calculated from the re sulting load -versus-
elongation diagrams. When calculating the flow stress, use wa s made of the initial cross s ection area of the specimen, 
i.e., calculating the so-called technical tensile diagrams. Hardness was measured by the method of nanoindentation in 
an NHT-S-AX-000X hardness tester at the load 0.01 N, and friction coefficients were measured in a series of friction 
pairs with a counter body (indenter) made from a titanium alloy in a THT-S-AX000 tribom eter (CSM) using a pin-on-
disk procedure. The disk diameter a nd thickness were 30 and 4 mm, respectively. The disk surface was pre-ground and 
polished down to Ra ≤ 0.1 µm. The indenters were shaped as cylindrical rods measuring 3 mm in diameter and having 
a conical top with the curvature radius 1.5 mm. The indenter load in the frictio n tests was the same 10 N. The linear 
velocity of counter-body motion was 2 cm/s. The wear rate of the surface layer of disk-s haped test pieces and coated 
specimens was measured following an equal number of disk revolutions (~3000) using a Micro Measure 3D Station 
(STIL) by calculating the cross section area averaged after ~150 m of the path length from the averaged diameter of the 
wear spot measured in an IMC toolmaker’s optical micros cope. Moreover, additional comparative measurements were 
made of the friction coefficient in a Tsiklometr device id entifying the time of testing, using the above-mentioned pin-
on-disk setting, but with a stainless steel ball (Russian grade ШХ-15) hardened to HRC62 used as an indenter, whose 
diameter was 3.2 mm, with the indenter load 1.0 N and the relative counter-body displacement velocity increased up to 
100 cm/s. 
EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS AND DISCUSSION 
The role of magnetron-plasma coating deposition in the deformation behavior, strength and plasticity of a VT6 
alloy were investigated in its coarse-grained (VT6 cg) and submicrocrystalline (VT6 ns) specimens at room temperature. 
The investigations demonstrated that practically no changes occurred in the shape of the σ – ε flow curves, strength and 
plasticity in the alloy of either grain structures following coating deposition (Table 1).

SUMMARY 
The results of comparative investigations suggest a conc lusion on a high efficiency and a good promise of 
application of a multi-component Ti–C–Mo–S coating deposite d to improve tribotechnical characteristics of titanium 
alloys. This coating, containing both strengthening and relatively soft components (sulfides) with low shear strength, 
offers a several-fold decrease in the friction coefficient and a considerable increase in wear resistance of the surfaces of 
friction pairs made from titanium alloys. Under the opera ting conditions used and low-temperature magnetron-plasma 
modes of deposition of a Ti–C–Mo–S coating the strength and ductility of submicrocrystalline titanium alloys undergo 
no changes.
