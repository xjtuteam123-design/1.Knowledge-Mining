ABSTRACT 
Tantalum-tungsten alloys are considered as potential materials used in high temperature applications due to their 
exceptional mechanical properties. To evaluate the mechanical properties of tantalum-tungsten alloys at elevated 
temperatures, a binary tantalum alloy containing 4 wt% tungsten (Ta – 4%W) was chosen and their mechanical 
properties were tested at 1400 – 2000 
◦
C, respectively, with the aid of a homemade coupled thermal-mechanical 
material testing system. The results show that the ultimate tensile stress, the yield stress and the uniform 
elongation gradually decrease, while the total tensile elongation keeps constant as the test temperature increases. 
Ta – 4%W alloy have continuous work hardening capability at ultrahigh temperature even up to 2000 
◦
C. The 
microstructural observations show that grain size increase and dislocation density decrease rapidly when the test 
temperature was increased to 1800 
◦
C and 2000 
◦
C. Furthermore, the dislocation substructure and dislocation 
types at elevated temperatures were analyzed and found to be distinctly different from that at room temperature. 
Base on the model between quantitative microstructural parameters and strength, the flow stress was calculated 
utilizing additive law from the microstructures with the structural morphology and related quantitative pa -
rameters. Mechanical strength loss of Ta – 4%W at elevated temperature was evaluated after comparing the 
calculated and experimentally measured values. 
1. Introduction 
As an important member of the high melting point refractory metals, 
tantalum metal has good comprehensive mechanical properties, espe -
cially high temperature mechanical properties [ 1 – 3 ]. These properties 
are expected to make tantalum metals as candidates for materials of 
rocket nozzles, turbines, combustion chambers, nuclear reactor radia -
tion shields and other high temperature components. For example, 
tantalum has recently been considered as a candidate for plasma-facing 
materials for future fusion reactors due to its strong resistance to high 
temperature radiation [ 4 ]. However, when plasma-facing materials are 
subjected to plasma bombardment, they will be heated to temperatures 
as high as 2000 
◦
C. The high-temperature mechanical properties of 
plasma-facing materials have thus garnered significant attention. 
Considering the low strength of pure tantalum, the application of 
tantalum has limitations, so it is common practice to add tungsten 
element to tantalum metal to form single-phase substitutional solid so -
lution tantalum-tungsten alloys. Tungsten additions are typically kept 
below 10 wt% in order to maintain good plasticity. The addition of 
tungsten improves the mechanical properties of the material, and also 
improves the high temperature corrosion and radiation resistance of the 
material [ 2 , 4 – 7 ]. 
The mechanical properties of tantalum-tungsten alloys have been 
extensively studied since the 1960s. The investigations mainly focuses 
on the work hardening properties of single crystalline and the effect of 
tungsten addition on the mechanical properties of tantalum-tungsten 
alloys. Researches on the deformation mechanism of tantalum- 
tungsten alloys mainly was based on considering the interaction be -
tween individual dislocations and tungsten solutes [ 8 – 12 ]. Since the 
1990s [ 13 – 16 ], there has been extensive development of 
tantalum-tungsten alloys with typical chemical compositions such as 
Ta-2.5 wt%W and Ta-10 wt%W. The effect of temperature, strain rate,

have complex effects on its mechanical properties. The presence of 
tungsten atoms can interact with screw dislocations and provide a 
pinning effect, impeding their movement and increasing the strength of 
the alloy. At the same time, single tungsten atoms can create additional 
slip planes, allowing for more dislocation motion. This increased 
mobility of dislocations can help to accommodate plastic deformation 
without significantly reducing the elongation. Therefore, the addition of 
tungsten to a tantalum alloy can result in a combination of increased 
strength due to the friction stress and enhanced mobility of screw dis -
locations, which can contribute to maintaining a reasonable level of 
elongation [ 10 , 20 , 21 ].

The microstructural evolution can be affected by a change in deforma -
tion temperature. The three-dimensional mobility of dislocations by 
cross-slip will be activated when plastic deformation occurs at elevated 
temperature, make the deformed microstructure towards an more or less 
well-formed low energy dislocation structures [ 30 , 31 ]. The long-range 
stress of low energy dislocation structures is either nil or finite up to 
an infinite distance, which is supported by the observations of alter -
nating rotations between neighbouring points and the finite accumula -
tive misorientation angles at different tensile deformation temperatures. 
The accumulative misorientation angles decrease illustrated that 
long-range stresses were heavily screened in the high temperature 
deformed microstructure [ 20 , 32 ], which mean that the deformation 
microstructure evolves toward an more ideal low energy dislocation 
structures. The microstructural evolution can be affected by the process 
parameters and alloying element. In this study, the three-dimensional 
mobility of dislocations by cross-slip can be promoted by the single 
tungsten atoms in tantalum and also by the deformation when the 
temperature increases. The deformation microstructure of Ta – 4%W 
evolves towards more ideal low-energy dislocation structures due to two 
influencing factors: the tungsten alloying element and the test temper -
ature [ 32 ].

The wavy glide pattern, which is associated with screw dislocations, 
refers to the movement of dislocations in a wave-like path within the 
lattice. It occurs during deformation under high stress or high temper -
ature conditions. In contrast, the planar glide pattern, commonly 
observed in edge dislocations, refers to dislocations moving in a straight 
or planar path within the lattice. The planar glide pattern is typically 
seen when dislocations are under relatively low stress or low tempera -
ture deformation. Despite the prevalent wavy glide pattern in the cur -
rent deformation microstructure, there are still instances of long and 
straight screw dislocation morphology present in some regions as shown 
in the inset of Fig. 10 c. Such nascent deformation structure is mainly due 
to the constraint of extended screw dislocation at small strain. From the 
inverse pole figure maps, it is seen that dynamic recrystallization occurs

recrystallized grains occur and grow progressively. Some of the new 
grains appeared at the final stage of deformation and thus went through 
the non-recovery state without the rearrangement and annihilation of 
dislocations by cross-slip of screw dislocations (the inset of Fig. 10 c). 
The distribution of tungsten element detected by energy spectrum 
equipped on transmission electron microscope show that there is no 
obvious segregation of tungsten element, whether it is on the dislocation 
substructure or the boundaries ( Fig. 10 d). The tungsten element in 
Ta – 4%W is well distributed and avoids the solute banding common to a 
Ta – 10%W alloy [ 20 , 24 ]. The well-distributed tungsten elements facili -
tate the three-dimensional mobility of dislocations through cross-slip, 
thereby promoting the evolution of the deformation microstructure of 
Ta – 4%W towards more ideal low-energy dislocation structures.
