Abstract
Many applications of shape memory alloys (SMAs) require the development of alloys with high martensitic transformation (MT) temperatures.
Among the different systems for high temperature SMAs, equiatomic RuNb alloys demonstrate both shape memory effect (SME) and MT tempera-
tures above 800
◦C. This work investigates Ru50−xNb50+x (at.%) alloys and shows that Nb content signiﬁcantly affects the MT behavior. Alloys near
the equiatomic composition (x = 0, 2, 4) undergo two displacive transformations on cooling:/H9252(B2) → /H9252′(body centered tetragonal)→ /H9252′′(mono-
clinic). The Ru45Nb55 alloy exhibits a single transition from cubic to tetragonal on cooling. This MT gives rise to a highly twinned microstructure
with a (0 1 1) compound-twinning mode and is considered to be responsible for the SME in both types of alloys. The reorientation of martensite
variants during deformation has been conﬁrmed through scanning electron microscopy of compression specimens. A promising shape memory
behavior is obtained through three-point bend tests performed both in the /H9252
′and /H9252′′phases.

1. Introduction
Shape memory alloys (SMAs) have the ability to perform
both sensing and actuating functions[1]. The operating temper-
atures of most SMAs depend on their martensitic transformation
(MT) temperature. As commercial functional materials, Ti–Ni-
and Cu-based SMAs have been applied in mechanical, biomed-
ical and electronic areas but are limited to low-temperature
applications (20–250
◦C). The introduction of this category of
materials in aeroengines requires the development of SMAs with
higher MT temperatures typically above 400
◦C. Among the dif-
ferent SMAs, only some might be used as high-temperature
SMAs (HTSMAs): these are Ti–Ni(Pt,Pd), NiAl, RuTa and
RuNb alloys[2]. Ti–Ni(Pd–Pt) alloys demonstrate high MT tem-
perature but are reported to present a poor shape memory effect
(SME) at high-temperature, which may be due to a decrease
of the critical stress for slip towards a level characteristic for
the martensite detwinning process [1]. NiAl alloys transforma-
tion temperatures may be raised up to 1200 K depending on
the composition but the brittle room temperature martensite is
not an equilibrium phase and decomposes into NiAl + Ni

2. Experimental procedures
A set of four Ru
50−xNb50+x alloys with x = 0, 2, 4, 5 at.% was
prepared from elements of commercial purity by arc-melting
under argon. All alloys were remelted several times to ensure
homogeneity. Final compositions were determined by the atomic
absorption spectrometry (Table 1) within an experimental pre-
cision of 0.5 at.% and proved to be very close to the intended
compositions.

4. Conclusion
(i) MT temperatures in Ru 50−xNb50+x alloys strongly depend
on Nb content. A good homogeneity of the alloy compo-
sition is thus required. However, the varying Nb content
would enable to adjust the MT temperatures for a precise
high-temperature application. MT temperatures demon-
strate both high stability and low hysteresis. All these MT
characteristics make Ru
50−xNb50+x alloys a good candidate
materials for HTSMAs.
(ii) The revised RuNb phase diagram based on our experimen-
tal results shows that the /H9252′-phase domain of existence is
actually far smaller than expected according to the literature
phase diagram [4].
(iii) The lattice invariant shear of the /H9252→ /H9252
′transformation is
determined to be (0 1 1) compound twinning. The associ-
ated low twinning shear ( s = 0.1) results in high damping
capacities associated with an easy motion of twin bound-
aries.
(iv) SEM observations and high shape memory recovery indi-
cate that deformation is mainly achieved through martensite
variants reorientation contrary to other HTSMAs for which
dislocation slip predominates at high-temperatures. In situ
TEM experiments should conﬁrm the low activity of dislo-
cation slip mechanism.
