HIGH TEMPERATURE CYCLIC DEFORMATION OF A

Abstract-The high temperature low cycle fatigue behaviour of a directionally solidified Ni-base superalloy 
hardened by about 65% volume fraction of y’-precipitates was investigated in order to determine the 
fatigue life parameters for longitudinal (L) and longitudinal transverse (LT) grain orientations. The 
fatigue resistance was compared with that of two oxide dispersion strengthened (ODS) Ni-base superalloys 
with a similar elongated grain structure. 
The fatigue life of the alloy can be adequately predicted by Basquin and Cof6n-Manson empirical 
relationships and the fatigue ductility parameters in these relationships show a similar trend with the 
tensile ductility properties. 
The studied alloy exhibits a fairly stable cyclic stress response, with only a slight stress softening. 
Fatigue crack initiation occurs mainly at shrinkage pores on the surface or sub-surface of the specimens. 
The crack growth direction is predominantly perpendicular to the applied load. The fracture mode in 
the LTdirection is transgranular and fatigue life is shorter by a factor of about six compared to the 
L-direction. The fatigue life of the alloy is longer than that of the ODS Ni-base superalloys with which 
it is compared. 
Keywords-Directionally solidified Ni-base superalloy; Low-cycle fatigue; ODS superalloys; Elongated 
grain structures; Surface pores

INTRODUCTION 
Ni-base superalloys are used in gas turbine vanes and blades and other high temperature 
structural components because of their excellent mechanical strength which is derived from y’ 
(Ni,Al) hardening precipitates or some other relatively stable hardening phases, such as inert 
oxide dispersoids in ODS superalloys. The improvement in the high temperature mechanical 
performance of these alloys has evolved over a long period from the conventionally cast superalloys 
up to the modem directionally solidified and single crystal alloys. 
The high temperature mechanical properties of these alloys are of primary concern to design 
engineers who have to predict the service lives of components made of these materials.

The aim of the present work is to examine the low cycle fatigue (LCF) behaviour of the 
CM247LC DS directionally solidified Ni-base superalloy, hardened by 65% volume fraction of y' 
precipitates. Attempts have been made to correlate the parameters defining the alloy fatigue life 
with the tensile properties although the deformation mechanisms under the two modes of loading 
have been reported to be different. While a massive y'-particle shearing occurs mainly in monotonic 
loading, TEM observations of specimens after LCF reveal little or no particle shearing by dislo- 
cations or a dislocation rearrangement so as to form a network around the y'-precipitates [ 1-41. 
It has been pointed out that the deformation mechanisms and the morphology of the particulate 
could only influence the crack tip plasticity [5] and crack propagation, but not the fatigue crack 
initiation stage of Ni-base superalloys [6]. 
The origin of failure under low cycle fatigue is mainly related to the geometrical discontinuities 
on the specimen surface. These include the presence of shrinkage pores [ 5,7], crevices created by 
oxidation effects [8-111, fracture of carbides located at the specimen surface [ 121 or indentations 
arising from sources like machining marks [ 13,141. These flaws on the specimen surface act as 
sites of fatigue crack initiation. 
It is still a question of debate if crack initiation starts very early in the specimen life or towards 
the end of specimen life [ 15,161, although the work of Fleury and RCmy [9] on a single crystal 
Ni-base superalloy seems to indicate that crack initiation occurs early in the specimen life and 
then progressively propagates. 
The contribution of all these factors to the low cycle fatigue behaviour of directionally solidified 
CM247LC DS is also examined in this paper and the fatigue resistance of the alloy is finally 
compared with that of ODS superalloys showing a similar elongated grain structure. 
MATERIAL AND EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURE 
The material studied in this investigation is CM247LC DS, a directionally solidified Ni-base 
superalloy containing about 67% volume fraction of y'-hardening phase. The alloy microstructure 
consists of columnar grains with boundaries parallel to the (001) growth direction. Figure 1 shows 
the microstructure (a) in the longitudinal and (b) in the longitudinal-transverse direction. The alloy 
chemical composition in wt% is as follows: 0.07 C, 5.55 Al, 9.5 Co, 8.1 Cr, 0.5 Mo, 3.2 Ta, 0.69 Ti, 
9.5 W, 1.4 Hf, <0.05 Nb, and the balance nickel. The alloy, supplied in the form of slabs, was 
homogenised at different temperatures slightly lower than the solidus temperature, then aged for 
4 h at 1080°C followed by a further 20 h at 870°C.

The low cycle fatigue tests were performed on a closed loop electro-hydraulic system using 
cylindrical specimens having an 8 mm gauge diameter and a 12 mm gauge length. The specimens 
were heated to test temperatures of 850°C and 950°C by an induction coil that was controlled by 
a radio frequency generator. At 950"C, both the longitudinal (L) and longitudinal transverse (LT) 
grain orientations of the alloy were studied. 
LCF air tests were conducted under longitudinal strain controlled conditions at a constant 
strain rate of 3 x s-'. A fully reversed triangular wave form was used to achieve the various 
strain amplitudes. During the fatigue tests the stress response and the stress-strain hysteresis loops 
were recorded at regular intervals. The fracture surfaces of the specimens were examined by 
scanning electron microscopy (SEM). 
RESULTS AND DISCUSSION 
Cyclic stress response 
The cyclic stress response of a material can provide important information concerning the 
deformation mechanisms as well as its overall mechanical stability. 
In the strain controlled LCF tests the cyclic stress response curves represent the variation of the 
stress amplitude versus the progressive number of cycles. The stress range levels were almost 
constant through the test with only approximately 3% softening until close to failure. The levels 
of stress at the first cycle for all the experimental conditions are plotted in Fig. 2. 
A comparison of the cyclic stress response of L and LT specimens shows that the stress amplitude 
is higher in the latter, for the same strain range imposed. The results can be explained by a higher 
Young modulus, E, for the material in the LT-direction (Table 1) and taking into account that in 
all the tests the elastic component A&e of the total strain range is much larger than the plastic 
component A$.

mechanisms of dislocation interaction with the precipitates and the evolution of the particulate

Low cycle fatigue curves 
The fatigue results are usually plotted as total strain range versus the number of cycles to failure. 
The number of cycles to failure, N, has been defined as the point where a rapid variation of the 
stress level in the Aa-n curve occurs. This variation corresponds to a crack length from approx. 
0.5 to 1 mm depending on the total strain imposed. Figure 3(a) shows the influence of test 
temperature and specimen grain orientation on the fatigue life of the CM247LC DS alloy. A 
fatigue life decrease is observed when temperature increases and when the material is tested in the 
LT-direction. For the same imposed strain range, the fatigue life in the L-direction is from 6 to 7 
times longer than in the LT-direction. 
The fatigue damage of the alloy is also evaluated by measuring the plastic and the elastic strain 
components of the total strain range at half life. Figure 3(b) shows the elastic and plastic strain 
components versus the number of cycles to failure (N) at 850°C and 950°C. 
On the basis of the elastic strain-life data, the fatigue resistance at 850°C is superior to that of 
950°C, whereas the reverse seems to be the case when comparing the plastic strain-life curves. 
However, the plastic strain-life curve presents some scatters at the lower imposed strain ranges.

Fatigue fracture process 
The fatigue failure mechanisms in Ni-base superalloys involve crack initiation at near-surface 
flaws. Attempts were therefore made to detect some of the features that are often associated with 
fatigue failure. The SEM micrograph in Fig. 4(a) shows the fracture surface of a specimen tested 
at 850°C with a total imposed strain range of 1.2%. The micrograph shows a micropore at the 
specimen surface which could act as a zone of stress concentration and eventually as the site of 
fatigue crack initiation. The pores that are situated within the specimen could also act as an origin 
for fatigue crack initiation, which seems to be indicated by the SEM micrograph in Fig. 4( b) for 
the L specimen tested in LCF at 850°C under an imposed total strain range of 1.6%. No slip off- 
sets were discernible on the specimen surface. 
Fatigue striations are occasionally observed and secondary cracks are frequently found in 
L-direction specimens as shown by SEM micrograph in Fig. 4(c). Also in LT-direction specimens 
transgranular crack propagation and secondary cracking are observed. The fracture aspect and 
the occurrence of secondary cracks depend on the imposed strain range. At a low imposed strain 
range (AE~ = 0.5%) no secondary cracks are observed and the fracture surface is macroscopically 
flat. At higher strain ranges, fatigue striations are also observed with numerous secondary cracks. 
The fatigue crack initiation phase consists of cracking along the crystallographic direction 
inclined to the applied load (i.e. a stage I type) and a typical example of an initiation zone is 
shown in Fig.4(d); the subsequent crack growth direction is perpendicular to the applied load 
(stage I1 type). 
The fatigue failure mechanism can thus be envisaged as follows: a crack initiates along the slip 
bands that are probably generated from defects (pores) on the specimen surface; although the 
precise role of oxygen can not be specified, the diffusion of this element along the deformed zone 
could also facilitate shear decohesion while the pore induces a local stress concentration. Once the 
crack is formed, its subsequent growth direction is perpendicular to the applied load.

CONCLUSIONS 
The low cycle fatigue behaviour of a directionally solidified Ni-base superalloy (CM247LC DS) 
has been investigated at 850°C and 950°C. 
The alloy exhibits a fairly stable cyclic stress response, with only a slight stress softening. The 
fatigue life of the alloy can be satisfactorily predicted by the empirical relationships of 
Coffin-Manson and Basquin with the latter giving a better correlation. 
The fatigue ductility parameters obtained from these relationships give a reasonable correlation 
with the tensile properties of the alloy. 
Fatigue crack initiation is mainly due to the shrinkage pores located at the surface or sub- 
surface of the specimen. 
The alloy exhibits a superior fatigue resistance when compared with oxide dispersion strength- 
ened Ni-base superalloys.
