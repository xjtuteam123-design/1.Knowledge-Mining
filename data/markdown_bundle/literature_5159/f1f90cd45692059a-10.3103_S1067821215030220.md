INTRODUCTION
When selecting a hard-alloy brand for the produc-
tion of picks of rolling-cutter drilling bits, it is neces-
sary to take into account the character of loads sub-
jected by a hard-alloy pick during drilling. The rock
destruction mechanism during pick drilling is based
on the principle of its crushing with certain sliding,
i.e., with the attrition. Th erefore, the alloy demands
increased requirements in regards to compression
strength, abrasive resistance, and crack resistance for
pick production.
Hard tungsten–cobalt alloys, for example,
VK10KS and VK11-VK, are used in the domestic
industry as drilling alloys. These are medium-grained
alloys which are fabricated with the application of nor-
malizing sintering and having a grain size of 4.0–
5.5
µm [1].
VK10, VK13, and VK16 alloys were used at OAO
V olgaburmash (further OAO VBM) for the pick pro-
duction of main peripheral series, and VK9 alloy was
used for the production of picks of the reverse rolling-
cutter cone. However, cases of bit failure, primarily
due to cleavages, as well as wear of the hard-alloy cut-
ting structure, forced us to start developing new alloys
with improved plasticity and crack resistance.

An increase in the crack resistance of the hard alloy
is mainly determined by the plasticity of a cobalt
binder, while the latter, in turn, is determined by the
degree of alloying with tungsten. It is known that the
tungsten content in a cobalt binder is in an inverse
dependence on the carbon fraction in the alloy , and an
increase in the former (W) rapidly lowers the alloy
crack resistance [1].
Based on the investigations into the cyclic stability
performed at the OAO VBM, the optimal range of the
carbon content in the alloy in the limits of the two-
phase region of 6.08–6.13 wt % is established. Enter-
ing this region yields high plasticity and, consequently ,
high operational resistance of hard-alloy picks. Maxi-
mal indices of physicomechanical properties of alloys
are attained at such carbon fraction [3].
The magnetic saturation of the hard alloy also
depends on the degree of alloying the cobalt binder
with tungsten. This dependence has the same linear
character as the dependence of the amount of dis-
solved tungsten on the carbon content. Correspond-
ingly , the larger the magnetic saturation is, the higher
the crack resistance of alloy will be. Investigations into

Abstract—The influence of strength properties of a cobalt binder and tungsten carbide grains on the opera-
tional characteristics of hard-alloy picks of drilling bits is investigated. Results are used to develop new brands
of hard alloys based on high-temperature tungsten carbides such as VK6S, VK10S, VK15S, and VK15K. It is
established that the application of these alloys in the pick production gives the opportunity to increase the
penetration speed due to the use of picks with a more aggressive design. It is shown that new alloy brands also
make it possible to increase the resource of the instrumental accessories made of them.
Keywords: drilling bits, cobalt binder, carbon balance, magnetic saturation, high-temperature tungsten car-
bides, impact cyclic resistance, high-pressure sintering, microstructure

DEVELOPMENT 
OF NEW HARD ALLOY BRANDS
It was already mentioned that in order to equip
main series of rolling cutters, alloys VK10, VK13 (with
the TaC additive), and preferentially VK16 were
applied, i.e., developers were forced to use high-cobalt
alloy in order to increase plasticity . However, this in
turn led to a decrease in hardness; wear resistance of
the cutting structure; and, consequently , hole making.
After finding the above-presented results of investi-
gations of hard alloys based on high-temperature
tungsten carbides, the decision was accepted to
develop new brands of hard alloys with a lower cobalt
content but based on high-temperature tungsten car-
bides. Since there is no possibility at the OAO VBM to
produce high-temperature tungsten carbides, the
requirements for carbide powder were formulated and
technical specifications were agreed with several pro-
ducers—powder suppliers.

Brands of medium-grained alloys VK10S and
VK15S with a grain size of 2.5–3.5 mm were intro-
duced for picks of the main series (T able 2). Both the
crack resistance and wear resistance were increased for
this alloys compared with the previously applied alloys
with the conservation of the size of the carbide phase
due to the more uniform alloy structure and use of
high-temperature tungsten carbide. Picks made of
these alloys successfully passed the tests for cyclic
resistance at the test stand and showed better results
than picks made of old alloys under the tests in field
conditions. For example, according to the results of
comparative tests performed at the Poltava mining
combine in 2008, an increase in hole making of R745
bits by almost 60% was acquired only due to new hard-
alloy picks [5].
When developing an alloy for equipping the inverse
cone of rolling cutters for plane-shaped picks, it was
decided to increase the wear resistance at the expense
of a certain decrease in crack resistance. T wo manu-
facturing variants were used in this case: (i) a decrease
in the size of the initial tungsten carbide grain and
(ii) a decrease in the cobalt content in the alloy . This
measure, correspondingly , would increase the coercive
force and hardness of the new alloy . The higher the
coercive force is, the finer the WC grain size is. It is
noteworthy that the grain size determines the wear
resistance of the alloy with the same cobalt content
[1, 6]. Thus, controlling the coercive force of hard-
alloy picks, we can control the granularity and wear
resistance of the alloy . In such a way , a new hard alloy

Compared with the previously used alloys, VK6S
and VK15S have a lower cobalt content. This always
leads to the formation of a more porous structure in
the hard alloy . Therefore, the vacuum sintering tech-
nology with the additional high-pressure supply was
introduced into the production. The residual porosity
was almost completely excluded with the application
of this type of sintering (see Table 2).
Based on the performed variations of the produc-
tion process, the enterprise standard for technical
requirements to hard-alloy wares is renewed with the
introduction of new alloy brands based on high-tem-
perature tungsten carbides.
Developed new hard alloy brands possess elevated
plasticity and crack resistance, which is especially
important for the fabrication of hard-alloy picks of
rolling-cutter bits. It is noteworthy that their wear
resistance is no worse than that of previously used
alloys and even surpasses them in the case of VK6S
alloy .
The good set of properties of VK6S alloy (see Table 2)
allowed us to assume that it can be used in the fabrica-
tion of wear-resistant tool accessories where the wares
made of VK9 alloy previously showed bad results
because of low wear resistance. Cutters for the fabrica-
tion of welding electrodes, which previously often
failed before the established time because of crushing
the cutting edges, became an example of such a tool.
T o prolong the service life of cutters, the decision was
accepted to apply VK6S drill alloy as their material.
The samples of VK6S alloy , possessing the homoge-
neous structure, high density , and sufficient crack
resistance, hold large impact loads, while fine carbide
grains having high hardness withstand the attrition
effects. These facts were confirmed by the results of
comparative tests in industrial conditions: the service

A new brand of coarse-grain alloy VK15K was
comparatively recently (in 2010) introduced into the
enterprise standard of the OAO VBM. The order for
the development of this alloy in combination with the
variation in the geometric design of hard-alloy picks
was received from consumers. It was required to equip
a series of bits with hard-alloy picks of a more “aggres-
sive” design (with a complicated shape and an increase
in the height of the working part of a pick), which can
considerably increase the penetration speed of
medium rocks without cleavages and fractures. For
this purpose, it was necessary to use hard alloy of
increased strength (crack resistance) with the conser-
vation of the sufficient wear resistance for the fabrica-
tion of the pick material. T o fabricate the VK15S alloy ,
high-temperature tungsten carbide WC11.5 with a
granularity of 10–12 
µm was applied for the first time
at OAO VBM. The production process included oper-
ations of wet refining–mixing the charge in attritors,
making it possible to attain a homogeneous structure
and sintering in vacuum-compression furnaces pro-
duced by ALD (Germany). Microstructures of the
sample of alloys VK15K and VK15S are presented in
Fig. 3.
It is seen in presented images that the grains in
VK15K alloy are coarser than in VK15S alloy , and the
distribution of cobalt binder is homogeneous, which
excludes crack propagation; i.e., two factors take place
here—high strength of the grains themselves and high
alloy plasticity caused by an increase in the thickness
of cobalt interlayers.

cutter body (working part of the pick) and the magni-

The development of a new VK15K alloy brand with
an increased crack resistance allowed us to create the
more aggressive design of picks, which allowed us to
increase the penetration speed of medium rocks with-
out cleavages and fractures of the hard-alloy cutting
structure.
CONCLUSIONS
(i) The determining role of characteristics of plas-
ticity and crack resistance of components of hard
alloys of the VK group in providing the high opera-
tional stability of hard-alloy picks of drilling rolling-
cutter bits is discussed.
(ii) It is shown that the carbon content in the alloy
affects the crack resistance of hard alloys. The optimal
carbon content for drilling hard alloys is presented.
(iii) The value of applying the controlling procedure
of magnetic saturation of hard alloy is considered.
(iv) The strength and plastic properties, as well as
the structure of the grains of tungsten carbides fabri-
cated at low and high carbonization temperatures, are
compared, and the advantage of high-temperature
tungsten carbides is shown. Based on these results,
new brands of drilling alloys based on high-tempera-
ture tungsten carbides are developed and introduced
into the production, notably , VK6S, VK10S, VK15S,
and VK15K alloys with high physicomechanical prop-
erties.
(v) It is established that the application of these
alloys in pick production offers the opportunity to
increase the penetration speed due to the application
of picks with a more aggressive design.
(vi) It is shown that the use of new alloy brands also
promotes an increase in the resource of the tool acces-
sories.
