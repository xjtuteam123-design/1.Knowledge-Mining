abstract
Designed Co eCreFeeNi-Hf bimodal eutectic high-entropy alloy (BEHEA) exhibits the
largest yield strength (2123 ± 38 MPa) at 600 /C14 C reported in the literature due to hard
BCCþLaves eutectics and achieves a decent ductility owing to the surrounding soft
FCCþLaves eutectics. The BEHEA provides a new strategy to design EHEAs.

High-entropy alloys (HEAs) have attracted great attention due
to interesting and unusual properties since 2004. Particularly,
refractory HEAs (RHEAs) exhibit large yield strength at
elevated temperatures due to high melting points [ 1,2].
Whereas, a higher melting point leads to worse ﬂuidity and
more defects during casting [ 2,3]. Some components in tur-
bine engine have complicated shapes, which are fabricated by
casting [ 4]. Therefore, it is urgent to develop alloys simulta-
neously with excellent high-temperature yield strength and
good castability.
Eutectic HEAs (EHEAs) not only have good castability but
also have excellent combination of strength and plasticity due
to in-situ composite structure. This structure consists of
alternative lamella, such as FCC þBCC [ 5], FCC þLaves [ 6e9]
and BCC þLaves [ 10]. Among them, BCC þLaves EHEAs can
achieve high-temperature yield strength but has a limited
ductility. For example, Al
28Cr20Nb15Ti27Zr10 is fractured at
1435 MPa before yielding when deformed at 600 /C14 C[ 10].

EHEAs (BEHEAs) in Co eCreFeeNi-Hf system. They consist of

As indicated by XRD patterns ( Fig. 1 (k)), all of alloys pre-

Laves phases (Fig. 2(b)). Particularly, a small fraction of s phase
is not only observed inside the FCC phase in regular eutectics
(Fig. 2 (b)), but also inside the BCC phase in cellular eutectics

Through EDS analysis ( Fig. 2 (ced)), BCC phase is enriched
with Cr; Laves phase is enriched with Ni and Hf; FCC phase is
enriched with Cr, Fe and Ni; s phase is enriched with Cr and
Fe. Their chemical compositions are summarized in
Supplementary Table 1, which are similar to those reported in
Refs. [ 6,12,14e16]. Alloy A contains only cellular eutectics,
which consists of BCC and Laves phases. With increasing Ni
content and decreasing Cr content in Alloy B and Alloy C, the
volume fraction of regular eutectics increases, which consists
of FCC and Laves phase. It suggests that Ni favors the forma-
tion of FCC þLaves eutectics, while Cr favors the formation of
BCCþLaves eutectics. According to the phase diagrams of
NieHf and Cr eHf, FCC þLaves eutectics forms at 1190
/C14 C and
BCCþLaves eutectics forms at 1665 /C14 C. Therefore, as illustra-
tion in Fig. 3 , BCC þLaves eutectics forms earlier during
solidiﬁcation and following this, FCC þLaves eutectics forms
due to the Cr depletion and Ni enrichment in remaining liquid.
During cooling between 1000 and 1200 /C14 C, s phase is formed
from both BCC and FCC phases, as demonstrated by small
peaks indicated by black arrows in Fig. 1(c, f, j). It is because of
too low Cr content in BCC phase [16] and too high Cr content in
FCC phase [ 15], making them unstable.
When uniaxially compressed at room temperature, all the
alloys exhibit brittle fracture ( Fig. 4(a)). From fracture surface,
cellular eutectics can be clearly seen, indicating hardly
plastic deformation ( Fig. 4 (d)). In contrast, tearing ridges are
observed in regular eutectics, which originate from FCC
phase ( Fig. 4 (e)). These tearing ridges have been reported in
eutectic AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 consisting of soft FCC phase and hard
B2 phase. The tearing ridges suggest plastic deformation and
can delay brittle fracture. This leads to an increased plastic

In conclusion, the designed Co eCreFeeNi-Hf BEHEAs
consist of BCC þLaves eutectics surrounded by FCC þLaves
eutectics. The hard BCC þLaves eutectics ensures high
strength while the soft FCC þLaves eutectics provides
ductility. As-cast Co 0.60Cr1.17Fe0.31Ni0.88Hf0.41 BEHEA not only
exhibits the largest compressive yield strength (2123 ± 38 MPa)
at 600 /C14 C among reported HEAs, but also has a decent ductility.
The BEHEA opens a new avenue to design EHEAs.
