2.DevelopmentHistoryofTiAlAlloys
TiAl alloys combine the ductility of metals with the high-
temperature strength of ceramics, making them the only
lightweight high-temperature metallic materials capable of sus-
tained use in oxidizing environments above 600 °C. Since
the 1950s, a signiﬁcant wave of research on TiAl alloys has
emerged globally. After nearly a century of investigations, re-
searchers have developed three generations of typical TiAl al-
loys. Below, we provide a brief overview of the evolution of TiAl
alloys.
As early as the 1980s, Pratt & Whitney spearheaded a series
ofstudiesfocusedonTiAlalloys,exploringvariousalloycompo-
sitions with diﬀerent mechanical properties. These eﬀorts cul-
minated in the development of the Ti-48Al-1V-0.3C (at.%) al-
loy, a notable example representing the ﬁrst generation of TiAl
alloys.[29] Thisalloyexhibitsexcellentcastabilityandmachinabil-
ity, along with high fracture toughness.[30] However, its room-
temperatureductilityandhigh-temperaturestrengtharetoolow
tomeettherigorousrequirementsofaerospaceengines.Conse-
quently, a concerted eﬀort was made to optimize alloy composi-
tionsandmanufacturingprocesses, leadingtotheadventofthe
second generation of TiAl alloys with improved service temper-

of the second-generation TiAl alloys include the cast 4822 alloy
developed by General Electric (GE),[31] and the 45XD alloy (Ti-
45Al-2Nb-2Mn-0.8 vol.% TiB2).[32] In 2006, GE announced the
useofthe4822alloytoreplaceNi-basedalloysinmanufacturing
