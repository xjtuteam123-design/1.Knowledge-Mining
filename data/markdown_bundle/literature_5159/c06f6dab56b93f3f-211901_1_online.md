ABSTRACT
To reveal if universal rules may exist in face centered cubic (FCC) concentrated solid solution (CSS) alloys for strength and plastic
deformation at room and cryogenic temperatures, we select a FCC CSS superalloy Haynes 188 to demonstrate the deformation mechanisms
from atomic to micrometer scales. In FCC CSS alloys, the yield strength (YS) is intrinsically determined by atomic/modulus mismatches and
affected extrinsically by grain size; the tensile strength and elongation to fracture (EF) are governed by the plastic process where low stacking
fault energy is beneﬁcial. Moreover, almost all CSS alloys show that YS and EF increase simultaneously with the decrease in the temperature.
Our ﬁndings may expand alloy application and can be used as a design strategy for stronger and tougher alloys.

The similarity of mechanical behaviors among M/HEAs and
Ni-based SS superalloys indicates that universality of strength and
plastic deformation might exist in FCC CSS alloys. In general, for SS
alloy, the YS is determined intrinsically by atomic size and modulus
mismatches
19,20 and extrinsically by dislocation obstacles, e.g., disloca-
tions, twin/phase/grain boundaries,21 whereas UTS is further deter-
mined by work hardening during plastic deformation, which is closely
related to the stacking fault energies of FCC SS alloys.
22,23 Low stack-
ing fault energy (SFE) is required to trigger transformation induced
phase or twin plasticity (TRIP or TWIP).
24,25 To shed light on the pos-
sibility of that the strength and plastic deformation in FCC CSS alloys
exist universal rules, we carefully select a model alloy from the current
Ni/Co FCC CSS alloy database and explore the deformation mecha-
nisms from atomic to micrometer scale, with the help of advanced
microscopy.

In summary, based on the current experiment and data collected
from M/HEAs, SS superalloys, and TWIP/TRIP steels, we demonstrate
the universality of strength and plastic deformation in FCC CSS alloys
at room and cryogenic temperatures, which might provide an alloy
design strategy for stronger and tougher SS alloys for applications
under extreme conditions. The YS of SS alloys is dominated intrinsi-
cally by atomic/modulus mismatches and meanwhile affected extrinsi-
cally by their grain sizes. The UTS and EF mainly are governed by the
plastic deformation processes, including Taylor hardening, TWIP,
and TRIP effects, where low SFE is beneﬁcial. FCC CSS alloys
normally show that the YS and EF simultaneously increase with the
decreasing temperature, which expands the service temperature of SS
high temperature superalloys to extraordinary low temperatures.
