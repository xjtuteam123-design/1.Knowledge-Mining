A high-temperature shape-memory alloy Ni 54Mn25Ga21
Huibin Xu,a) Yunqing Ma, and Chengbao Jiang
Department of Materials Science and Engineering, Beijing University of Aeronautics and Astronautics,
Beijing 100083, People’ s Republic of China
~Received 21 November 2002; accepted 12 March 2003 !
A high-temperature shape-memory alloy, Ni 54Mn25Ga21 , was developed with a shape-memory
effect of 6.1% and a martensitic transformation temperature higher than 250 °C for single crystals.
The measured compressive strength and strain were 845 MPa and 20.5%, respectively, with a
compressive axis along the growth direction of the rods at room temperature. One thousand thermal
cycles were performed on the Ni
54Mn25Ga21 without obvious changes of the martensitic structure,
transformation behavior, and shape-memory effect, indicating an excellent thermal stability for the
present alloy. © 2003 American Institute of Physics. @DOI: 10.1063/1.1572540#
Recently, Ni
2MnGa Heusler alloys have attracted much
attention as candidates for actuator materials. 1–5 Several
papers6–8 revealed that a high martensitic transformation
temperature ~above 250 °C! exists in NiMnGa alloys with Ni
content higher than the stoichiometric Ni 2MnGa. However,
such alloys exhibit low Curie temperatures and saturation
magnetizations, and therefore show little potential as mag-
netic shape-memory alloys.
The need for high-temperature shape-memory alloys
~HTSMAs! is constantly increasing,
9,10 and current HTSMAs
systems have experienced various problems. Cu-based and
NiAl HTSMAs are considered unstable,11,12 with equilibrium
phases precipitated at high temperature destroying their
shape-memory effect ~SME!. NiTiZr and NiTiHf are too
brittle for practical use.
13 As for the NiTiPd alloys, although
its full recovery strain has been increased to 5.5% by proper
thermomechanical treatment,
14 the high cost of precious
metal Pd hinders its application. The studies to explore
lower-cost HTSMAs are continuing. In the present study, a
HTSMA was developed, and a SME of 6.1% was observed
in Ni
54Mn25Ga21 single crystal with martensitic transforma-
tion temperature higher than 250 °C, and this SME remains
almost unchanged even after 1000 thermal cycles. All these
properties make it a good candidate for applications as a
HTSMA.
Ni
54Mn25Ga21 was selected off the stoichiometric
Ni2MnGa to raise the martensitic transformation temperature
much higher than room temperature. High-purity nickel,
manganese, and gallium were arc-melted four times into but-
tons under argon atmosphere and then drop-cast into a
chilled copper mold to obtain master rods with a diameter of
7.2 mm and a length of 110 mm. The master rods were used
to prepared single crystals by zone-melting unidirectional so-
lidiﬁcation in a XLL 500 furnace with a pulling rate of 10
mm/h. The furnace chamber was ﬁrst pumped to 2
310
23 Pa, followed by backﬁlling high-purity argon gas to
0.53105 Pa. Single-crystal samples of 3 3335m m3 for
uniaxial compression experiments were cut by spark-cutting,
with the long edge parallel to the growth direction.
The phase structure was identiﬁed at room temperature
by a Rigaku D/Max 2200 PC diffractometer with Cu K
a ra-
diation. The phase transformation temperatures of the alloy
were determined by differential scanning calorimetry ~DSC!
~NETZSCH STA449 !, with cooling and heating rates of
10 °C/min. The uniaxial compression experiments were car-
ried out at room temperature on the MTS 880 materials test-
ing system with the compressive direction parallel to the
growth direction of single crystal. The deformation rate was
0.05 mm/min. In order to examine the SME, samples were
precompressed to a speciﬁc strain at room temperature and
then heated to 400 °C for recovery. Stabilities of phase trans-
formation and SME were examined by cycling between
350 °C and room temperature up to 1000 times in a thermal-
cycle furnace, model Sk2-6-12, staying at 350 °C for 10 min,
and then wind-cooling to room temperature in 5 min. DSC
and SME measurements were performed after different ther-
mal cycles, and the microstructure before initial thermal
cycle and after 1000 thermal cycles were observed by TEM
~Hitachi H800 operating at 200 kV !.
The powder x-ray diffraction ~XRD! pattern of
Ni
54Mn25Ga21 at room temperature indicates a nonmodulated
martensitic structure with the crystal parameters a5b
50.7671 nm, and c50.6689 nm. DSC measurements dem-
onstrate that the martensitic transformation start temperature
Ms , the martensitic transformation ﬁnish temperature M f ,
the austenitic transformation start temperature As , and aus-
tenitic transformation ﬁnish temperature Af are 260.2, 237.8,
262.5, and 287.8 °C, respectively. The martensitic state at
room temperature was conﬁrmed by the optical microstruc-
ture observation and TEM observation, and the reversible
martensitic transformation was checked by high-temperature
XRD analysis around these critical temperatures.

The thermal stability of the Ni 54Mn25Ga21 alloy was ex-
amined by cycling between 350 °C and room temperature.
Figure 2 shows its powder XRD patterns at the original state
and after 1000 thermal cycles, and the inset shows the cor-
responding DSC curves. In the original state, the M
p ~the
peak temperature of martensitic transformation ! and Ap ~the
peak temperature of the reverse transformation! temperatures
were 250.9 and 274.1 °C, respectively. The latent heats of
martensitic and reverse transformations were 9.5 and 8.4 J/g,
respectively. After 1000 thermal cycles, the M
p and Ap tem-
peratures were 248.4 and 267.4 °C, respectively, and that la-
tent heats were 9.6 and 8.7 J/g, respectively. Martensitic
transformation behavior shows little difference before and
after 1000 thermal cycles. As for the XRD patterns shown in
Fig. 2, although the intensities of several peaks show a slight
difference, their positions are identical before and after 1000
thermal cycles, indicating good stability of its microstruc-
ture. Compared with the Cu-based HTSMA decomposing
into equilibrium phases after several thermal cycles, and the
NiAl HTSMA precipitating the Ni
5Al3 phase upon heating
runs, the Ni 54Mn25Ga21 alloy is quite advantageous in this
respect. Its good thermal stability is thought to be attributed
to its single-phase nature and to the high degree of self-
accommodation of the martensitic twins.

The stability of the SME was also checked after thermal
cycles. All the samples were ﬁrst precompressed to 8% total
strain, then heated to 400 °C for recovery. Table II summa-
rizes the shape-memory behavior of Ni
54Mn25Ga21 alloy af-
ter different thermal cycles. In the original state, the perma-
nent strain after unloading, the residual strain after heating,
the shape recovery strain due to SME, and the recovery rate
were 6.5%, 0.4%, 6.1%, and 93.8%, respectively. After 250
thermal cycles, these changed to 6.6%, 0.4%, 6.2%, and
93.9%, respectively, and after 1000 thermal cycles, they were
6.7%, 0.6%, 6.1%, and 91.0%, respectively. Only the perma-
nent strain and the residual strain showed minor increases
during thermal cycles, leading to a slight decrease in the
recovery rate from 93.8% to 91.0%. The SME of the
Ni
54Mn25Ga21 alloy stays almost unchanged during 1000
thermal cycles. This is due to the excellent stability of the
reversible martensitic transformation.
