ABSTRACT 
Local chemical order (LCO) is energetically favorable in multi-principal element alloys (MPEAs) due to the 
chemical affinity difference among constituent species. Here we show that the increase of LCO is often accom -
panied by a decrease of lattice distortion in body-centered-cubic (BCC) MPEAs. This leads to a trade-off between 
LCO and lattice distortion in their effects on retarding the glide of edge and screw dislocations. With a random 
solid solution without LCO as a reference, the emergence of LCO at the expense of lattice distortion could weaken 
the local trapping force for dislocation glide and promote dislocation mobility. However, when the LCO becomes 
sufficiently large and robust, it acts to hinder dislocation glide. We found that the mobility of edge dislocation is 
more affected by local lattice distortion, while the kink-pair based screw dislocation motion is more governed by 
LCO. We further quantitatively evaluate the contribution of LCO and lattice distortion to the overall critical shear 
stress for dislocation glide using analytical models. For the present equiatomic NbTiZr MPEAs, the critical shear 
stress is dominated by lattice distortion when the LCO is still limited to chemical short-range order (CSRO). 
However, LCO could even surpass lattice distortion to become a major contributor to the critical shear stress once 
it grows to a sufficiently large size. Our work indicates that increasing the degree and extent of LCO may provide 
an effective means to regulate dislocation motion, shedding light on how to use LCOs to improve the strength of 
BCC MPEAs. 
1. Introduction 
The recently emerging multi-principal element alloys (MPEAs) with 
body-centered-cubic (BCC) structure have attracted extensive attention 
due to their promising mechanical properties at elevated temperatures 
[ 1 , 2 ]. For example, CrMoNbV and NbTaTiV MPEAs have a compressive 
yield strength of 1000 MPa and 620 MPa at 1173 K, respectively [3] . The 
NbMoTaW MPEA shows an impressive compression yield strength of 
around 400 MPa at a temperature as high as 1900 K [4] . 
Many studies have been carried out to probe the origin of excellent 
mechanical properties in BCC MPEAs. Pronounced lattice distortion can 
be induced by atomic size and elastic modulus mismatch in MPEAs [5] . 
The interaction between lattice distortion and dislocations significantly 
influences the mechanical behavior of BCC MPEAs [6 – 8] . For BCC 
metals, edge dislocation moves very fast under low stresses, e.g., 0.7 
km/s under 80 MPa at room temperature in α -Fe [9] . However, the 
width of edge dislocation core becomes narrower as lattice distortion 
becomes larger in BCC MPEAs, leading to a higher critical shear stress 
for dislocation motion [ 10 , 11 ]. Lee et al. observed that edge dislocation 
has a wavy configuration and moves via nanoscale segment detrapping 
in NbTaTiV MPEA [12] . Our previous work has shown that the relative 
speed of edge-to-screw dislocation depends on the magnitude of lattice 
distortion [13] . In a BCC MPEA with large lattice distortion, the edge 
dislocation has a comparable mobility with that of screw dislocation [9] . 
As such, edge dislocation makes a considerable contribution to the 
strength in BCC MPEAs. The core structure of screw dislocation in BCC 
MPEAs could spread into three equivalent {110} planes with an asym -
metrical configuration due to local lattice distortion, making it varied 
along the screw dislocation line [14 – 16] . This could promote the 
spontaneous nucleation of kink-pairs along the screw dislocation line, 
yet inhibit the propagation of kink-pair with an appreciable activation 
barrier [9] . The kinked screw dislocation provides a new strengthening 
mechanism in BCC MPEAs [17] . 
Besides lattice distortion, local chemical order (LCO) is intrinsically 
formed in MPEAs due to the complex enthalpic interactions and atomic 
size discrepancy between the constituent species [18] . LCO starts from 
the sub-nanometer range as chemical short-range order (CSRO) but can 
grow to domains with several nanometers in dimension through aging

is still unclear in BCC MPEAs. 
In this work, we study the influence of LCO on dislocation mobility 
and its correlation with mechanical strength in the equiatomic NbTiZr 
MPEAs with BCC structure. We observed that there is a trade-off be -
tween LCO and lattice distortion that increasing LCO is accompanied by 
a decrease of lattice distortion. For both edge and screw dislocations, the 
gliding speed is elevated when LCO is still very weak, due to a weak -
ening of the trapping ability by local lattice distortion. However, as LCO 
becomes stronger, it acts as a dominant factor to impede dislocation, 
leading to a decrease of dislocation mobility. We further quantitatively 
evaluate the contributions of LCO and lattice distortion to the overall 
critical shear stress for dislocation motion via analytical models. The 
predicted values match well with those obtained in our atomistic sim -
ulations. We demonstrate that LCO can only make a minor contribution 
to the overall shear stress when it has a small size (like in its early stage, 
the case of CSRO), but its contribution could surpass that induced by 
lattice distortion once it grows to a few nanometers. These results could 
help to understand whether the LCO can provide a key mechanism in 
strengthening MPEAs. If the answer is positive, how to tune LCO to make 
it effective becomes an important issue in designing BCC MPEAs with 
good mechanical properties. 
2. Simulation methods 
2.1. Introduction of LCO in NbTiZr MPEAs using a hybrid MD/MC 
simulation 
We used the empirical embedded atom method (EAM) potential 
developed by Zhou et al. to describe the interatomic interaction of 
NbTiZr alloy [ 28 , 29 ]. It gives a relatively good description of various 
properties, such as elastic constants, shear modulus, unstable stacking 
fault energy and dislocation core structure, consistent with that obtained 
by first-principles calculations [14] . To understand the effect of LCO on 
dislocation mobility, a variety of different degrees of LCO were intro -
duced into the NbTiZr MPEAs. Specifically, we used a hybrid molecular 
dynamics/Monte Carlo (MD/MC) simulation to anneal the random solid 
solution at different temperatures ranging from 600 K to 1300 K. The 
variance constrained semi-grand canonical ensemble is used to conduct 
the swap of atoms in MC steps during MD/MC simulation. The pressure 
was kept zero with an isothermal-isobaric ensemble during MD/MC 
simulations. For every MC simulation cycle, the swap was accepted with 
the swap probability conforming to the Metropolis criterion [30] . The 
parameters of chemical potential differences in MC simulation are set to 
be 1.03 eV for Nb-Zr and 2.72 eV for Nb-Ti. The variance constraint 
parameter κ is 1000. These parameters for MC operation could guar -
antee that all samples have a close-to-equiatomic composition. After 
every 20 MD steps, a MC cycle was performed to obtain the equilibrium 
configuration at the given annealing temperature. The final equilibrium 
configuration with LCO structure was obtained after 5000 MC cycles.

2.4. Measuring dislocation mobility 
The simulation box has dimensions of 24 nm × 36 nm × 24 nm. It is 
oriented as x -[ 110], y -[11 2], z -[111] for a 1/2 < 111 > screw dislocation 
and x -[1 21], y -[ 101], z -[111] for a 1/2 < 111 > /{110} edge dislocation. 
Periodic boundary condition was imposed along [111] direction. The 
surfaces along the [ 110] for screw dislocation and [1 21] for edge 
dislocation were maintained traction-free. Figure S1 shows the sche -
matic illustration of our simulation model with dislocation residing on 
the slip plane (see Supplementary Note 1). The dislocation line has a 
length of 24 nm in the direction along z -axis. A 0.6-nm-thick layer was 
fixed at the top and bottom surfaces of the simulation box in the x di -
rection for screw dislocation and y direction for edge dislocation, 
respectively. To drive the dislocation to move, the constant shear stress 
was obtained by adding the external force in the rigid layers. The whole 
gliding distance is around 20 nm, less than one periodic length along its 
glide direction. For the annealed samples, this dislocation sweep cuts 
through the equilibrium LCO only once (see Supplementary Note 2).

dislocation velocity and the applied force are balanced with τ b = Bv ,

dislocation velocity and B is the drag coefficient. We characterized the

3.6. Predicting the critical shear stress for dislocation motion 
In this section, in order to explore the effect of the trade-off between 
LCO and lattice distortion on the strength in a quantitative way, we use 
two separate strengthening models to evaluate the separate contribution 
of LCO and lattice distortion to the critical shear stress for dislocation 
motion in our NbTiZr MPEAs. Early studies have revealed that edge 
dislocation could make an almost equal contribution to strength as that 
of screw dislocation in BCC MPEAs with a large lattice distortion [ 13 , 
46 ]. Baruffi et al. have made an extensive comparison between the 
prediction of strengthening caused by edge and screw dislocations [47] . 
The results showed that strengthening caused by edge dislocation could 
become comparable to or even exceed the screw counterpart in BCC 
MPEAs when the misfit parameter δ is larger than 0.035. Here for the 
present NbTiZr MPEAs, the misfit parameter δ can reach 0.04, sug -
gesting that edge dislocation should have a non-negligible effect on 
strength. To present a quantitative analysis, we found that the critical 
shear stress for edge dislocation motion is 125 MPa at 300 K in this 
NbTiZr random solid solution, around one half of 282 MPa for screw 
dislocation. Compared with the large (orders of magnitude) difference in 
critical shear stress between edge and screw dislocations in pure BCC 
metals [48] , the much smaller disparity implies that edge dislocation 
could now make a significant contribution to the overall strength in 
NbTiZr MPEAs. Moreover, the close mobility for the two types of 
dislocation in our simulations also confirms that the edge dislocation 
behaves more like the screw dislocation ( Fig. 4 ). Thus, although some 
models are developed to predict strength in BCC MPEAs with respect to 
screw dislocation [ 49 , 50 ], we focus on developing strengthening models 
based on edge dislocation, as shown in the following sub-sections. 
There are several models available for predicting the critical shear 
stress for edge dislocation in MPEAs. Maresca and Varvenne in Curtin ’ s 
group developed a strengthening model that can be applied to BCC and

3.7. Implications for experimental observations 
Our present work demonstrates that the trade-off between LCO and 
lattice distortion could result in a two-stage behavior of dislocation 
mobility ( Fig. 4c - d ). With the random solid solution as a reference state, 
the degree of LCO is enhanced as the annealing temperature decreases in 
our NbTiZr MPEAs. In the sample annealed at high temperature, the 
formation of LCO could help to weaken the trapping effect induced by 
local lattice distortion, hence promoting dislocation mobility to some 
extent. This is consistent with the observations by Antillon et al. that the 
1473 K-annealed NbZrTi MPEA with a weak LCO has a lower critical 
shear stress in comparison to the random solid solution [27] . At this 
stage, the dominant factor governing dislocation motion is the magni -
tude of lattice distortion. However, as the LCO degree increases upon 
annealing at a lower temperature, the lattice distortion is reduced 
significantly. LCO then could become the main obstacle hindering 
dislocation glide. The pinning effect induced by LCO could even rival 
that by lattice distortion, leading to the decrease of dislocation mobility. 
At the second stage, LCO replaces the lattice distortion to become the 
dominant mechanism in controlling dislocation motion ( Fig. 12 ). In 
addition, we found that the mobility of edge dislocation is enhanced 
pronouncedly as lattice distortion becomes weaker during the first stage, 
consistent with the previous works [ 17 , 62 ]. The speed of screw dislo -
cation reduces more abruptly with the increase of LCO at the second 
stage. Therefore, it shows that the mobility of edge dislocation is more 
determined by lattice distortion, while the screw dislocation motion is 
more controlled by LCO. This difference demonstrates the complex 
interplay of LCO and lattice distortion in affecting the mobility of 
dislocation with different characters. 
Some experimental studies have shown that the low-degree LCO like 
CSRO formed at a high annealing temperature might have a negligible 
effect on improving strength in MPEAs [26] . Here based on our results, 
this observation could be explained as follows. First, the incipient LCOs 
themselves, with only partial chemical order over a small distance, 
contribute little to retarding dislocations. Second, most of these weak 
LCOs get destructed after some dislocation slip, so the macroscopic yield 
basically happens at the same stress level as a random solution. Third, 
even when the LCO contributes a bit of strength, the increment is 
compromised by the reduction in dislocation trapping from lattice 
distortion, which decreases with the emergence of LCO. As shown in 
Figs. 10 and 11 , the strengthening caused by LCO is offset by the 
decrease of lattice distortion in samples after annealing at high tem -
peratures, resulting in the reduction of the overall strength. However, 
this balance is titled in the direction favoring LCO as the major 
contributor to strength, once it grows to a large size exceeding a few 
nanometers after annealing at lower temperatures. This is verified by 
some experimental results that a relatively high degree of LCO could 
interact with dislocations strongly to improve the strength of MPEAs 
[63] . Another example is the Ti

(1) We observed an interplay between LCO and lattice distortion in 
annealed NbTiZr MPEAs. As the annealing temperature de -
creases, the degree of LCO increases, while the lattice distortion 
decreases in the meantime. This happens because LCO formation 
preferentially establishes close contact between chemically dis -
similar species. These species also tend to be dissimilar in atomic 
size, such that their intimate bonding via LCO offsets the local 
size mismatch and reduces elastic strain. 
(2) The mobility of both edge and screw dislocations is controlled by 
a compromising trade-off between LCO and lattice distortion. 
With the random solid solution as the reference, the dislocation 
mobility is elevated with emerging LCO. At this stage, the for -
mation of LCO reduces the lattice distortion, leading to the 
weakening of the resistance to dislocation motion. However, 
when LCO develops to become robust and persistent, it could act 
as a relatively strong obstacle to impede dislocation glide, making 
dislocation mobility decrease despite of the LCO-lowered lattice 
distortion. 
(3) The sensitivity of edge and screw dislocations to the degree of 
LCO is quite different. The mobility of edge dislocation drops 
more quickly with the increase of LCO in comparison to that of 
screw dislocation in the first stage (see caption of Fig. 12 ). 
However, the speed of screw dislocation reduces more abruptly 
with the increase of LCO in the second stage. This reveals that the 
mobility of edge dislocation is more affected by the magnitude of 
lattice distortion, while screw dislocation motion is more 
controlled by LCO. 
(4) We quantitatively evaluated the influence of LCO and lattice 
distortion on the critical shear stress for dislocation motion using 
analytical models. The predicted values match well with those 
obtained by atomistic simulations. We found that the strength 
elevation induced by LCO becomes larger with the increase of 
LCO size. Its contribution to the total stress could even exceed 
that of lattice distortion when LCO grows to a few nanometers. As 
such, larger and persistent LCOs are recommended in lieu of 
CSRO if strengthening is the goal.
