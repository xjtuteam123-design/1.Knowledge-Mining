The effect of quaternary additions on the microstructures and
mechanical properties of orthorhombic Ti 2AlNb-based alloys
Feng Tang *, Shizuo Nakazawa, Masuo Hagiwara
National Institute for Materials Science , 1-2-1 Sengen, Tsukuba, Ibaraki 305-0047, Japan
Abstract
In an attempt to enhance the mechanical properties of the orthorhombic Ti 2AlNb-based (O-based) alloy, six testing alloys were
created by adding the quaternary elements to the O-based alloy. The microstructure, creep behavior and tensile properties of these
alloys were investigated. The phase constitution was found to be either B2 +O, B2 + /p97
2 +Oo rB 2 + /p972, depending on the Nb
content and aging temperature. The results of the mechanical test showed that V was effective in improving room temperature
ductility, while W was effective in enhancing high temperature tensile strength and creep resistance. Among the six alloys,
Ti–22Al–20Nb–2W exhibited the best balance with its high-temperature 0.2% proof stress, room temperature ductility, and 1%
creep-strain lifetime. © 2002 Elsevier Science B.V. All rights reserved.

1. Introduction
In an earlier study of the effects of Nb on Ti 3Al(/p972)-
based alloys, it was found that, at high niobium levels,
the /p97
2 phase evolved into a new ordered orthorhombic
structure that was based on the composition of
Ti
2AlNb. The Ti2AlNb phase (O phase) was stable over
a large range of Nb content. A single O phase was
obtained in the alloys with chemical compositions from
Ti–25Al–12Nb to Ti–25Al–30Nb after aging at
800 °C [1].
The microstructure of an O-based alloy often con-
tains an O phase and a B2 (ordered bcc) phase. The ﬁne
two-phase structure of O and B2 results in signiﬁcant
strengthening of the alloy and in better room-tempera-
ture ductility and fracture resistance than single-phase
O alloys exhibit. The B2 +O alloys exhibit better
strength and room temperature elongation than Ti
3Al-
based alloys, and show higher speciﬁc strength than the
nickel-based supper alloy IN718 [2,3].
The O phase can dissolve a great fraction of /p98(bcc
phase) stabilizers into solid solution. So it is possible to
improve the mechanical properties of the O alloy with
solid-solution effects. In this paper, in an attempt to
enhance the alloy’s high-temperature mechanical prop-
erties, quaternary elements such as Mo, W and V were
added to an O-based alloy. The effects of these quater-
nary additions on the microstructure, tensile properties
and creep behavior were investigated and discussed.
2. Experimental
Among the O-based alloys, a two-phase B2 +O alloy
such as Ti–22Al–27Nb is said to have a good balance
of tensile, creep and fracture toughness properties [4].
Our research involves modifying the composition of
this alloy by adding the quaternary elements Mo, W
and V.
In order to obtain a balance between the high-tem-
perature strength and room-temperature elongation, it
is very important to retain a proper amount of B2
phase when creating a new O-based alloy. According to
the literature [5], the /p98-phase stabilizing abilities of Mo,
W and V are stronger than that of Nb. A one atomic
percent addition of Mo, W or V corresponds to the
replacement of 4.25, 3.36 or 1.58 atomic percent of Nb,
respectively. In order to get a proper B2 volume as in

ranges from 32 to 40% when the microstructure is B2+O

VB2 is large and ranges from 49 to 55%. This fact can be
explained qualitatively by a phase diagram in reference
[8]. An experimental result has shown that, in a B2 + /p97
2
(or B2 + /p972 +O) alloy, the Nb content in the /p972 phase
is lower than that in the O and B2 phases [8].
The BC2(0.3)A-treated Ti–22Al–20Nb–2W showed a
metastable B2 + /p972 +O structure. This result can be
explained as follows. When an alloy is cooled down from
a temperature over the B2 transus point, according to the
phase diagram [8], it goes through a B2 +/p97
2 two-phase
area at temperatures just below the B2 transus point.
When the alloy has been in this area for a long enough
time, as occurred in BC2(0.3)A-treated Ti–22Al–20Nb–
2W (Fig. 1c), the coarse /p97
2 plates precipitate. These /p972
plates are rather hard to transform to the O phase in the
B2 +O two-phase area at lower temperatures, and so the
alloy shows a metastable B2 +/p972 +O three-phase struc-
ture. This phenomena has also been reported for a
Ti–23Al–27Nb alloy [8]. No /p97
2 was retained in the
BC2(3)A- and BC2(30)A-treated Ti –22Al–20Nb–2W
alloy due to the relatively high cooling rate.
4.2. Tensile properties
At temperatures from 750 to 850 °C, the EL showed
a ‘valley’ (Fig. 2), which is thought to be due to the
so-called intermediate temperature embrittlement, be-
cause the grain boundary fracture has been observed in
the 750 and 850 °C tension samples. Intermediate tem-
perature embrittlement is well-known in many alloys
such as Al–Mg alloys and is thought to be related to the
grain-boundary fracture induced by cavitation on the
grain boundaries [9,10]. It has been suggested that the
nucleation of cavities is related to the activation of
grain-boundary sliding accompanied with the stress con-
centration in an uneven grain boundary.
The sharp drop in PS at temperatures over 650 or
750 °C as shown in Fig. 2, which was accompanied with
the drop in EL, is also likely to be related to the activation
of grain boundary sliding.
At temperatures higher than 850 °C, the recrystalliza-
tion could be activated, so no stress concentration would
occur during the sliding of grain boundaries. Therefore,
no cavity occurred. This is the reason for the recovery
of ductility at temperatures over 850 °C [10].

process, the W, which is thought to have low diffusion

5. Conclusions
Six test alloys were created by substituting Mo, W and
V for a part of Nb in Ti –22Al–27Nb. The microstruc-
ture, tensile properties and creep behavior were investi-
gated. The results are summarized as follows:
1. The phase constitution was found to be either
B2 +O, B2 + /p97
2 +Oo rB 2 + /p972, depending on the Nb
content and aging temperature.
2. The results of the tensile test show that V is effective
in improving the room temperature ductility, while W is
effective in enhancing the high-temperature 0.2% proof
strength.
3. The transient creep strain shows no clear depen-
dency on the quaternary addition, but decreases with a
decreasing B2 volume fraction.
4. W is effective in lowering the steady-state creep rate
by enhancing the apparent activation energy of the creep.
5. Ti –22Al–20Nb–2W exhibited a good balance be-
tween the high-temperature 0.2% proof stress, the room-
temperature ductility, and the creep behavior.
