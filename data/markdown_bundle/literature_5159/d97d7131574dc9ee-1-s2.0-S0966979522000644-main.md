alloys. However, it is difficult to combine high strength and high 
ductility in single-phase HEA. For example, the FeMnCoCrNi HEA with

EHEAs are usually prepared by melting. However, the as-cast HEAs 
have problems with composition segregation, porosity, and other cast -
ing defects [ 13 , 14 ]. In contrast, powder metallurgy can produce bulk 
HEAs with nanoscale grains, uniform microstructure, and homogeneous 
composition, which can significantly improve the mechanical properties 
of HEAs. Near-final formation of metal parts can also be realized by 
powder metallurgy which can improve the utilization of materials. In 
addition, powder metallurgy is convenient for adding second phase 
particles to obtain metal matrix composites. However, most HEAs 
powders for powder metallurgy were prepared by mechanical alloying, 
which inevitably caused the contamination by process control agents 
and grinding balls. Rahul [ 15 ] et al. prepared AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 
eutectic 
high-entropy alloy by MA and SPS methods. The strength of the alloy 
was greatly improved, but the ductility was only 1%. Moreover, me -
chanical alloying usually requires more than 10 h [ 16 , 17 ], which is 
inefficient. Gas-atomization is an efficient and rapid method to prepare 
HEAs powders. It can produce tens or even hundreds of kilograms of 
powder within a few hours, and the composition is quite uniform [ 18 ]. 
Meanwhile, some literatures [ 19 , 20 ] showed that HEAs prepared by 
gas-atomization powder have comparable or even better mechanical 
properties than HEAs prepared via the melting method. 
As the most typical EHEA, the AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 
alloy possessed excel -
lent properties, such as high strength and ductility [ 9 , 21 ], good oxida -
tion resistance at high temperature [ 22 ] and wear resistance [ 23 ], etc. 
However, there are fewer investigations about eutectic high-entropy 
alloy prepared by powder metallurgy. Taking advantage of the 
gas-atomization and SPS, we prepared a series of AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 
bulk 
EHEA in this work. The effect of sintering temperature on microstruc -
ture, mechanical properties, and wear resistance of high strength 
AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 
bulk alloy were systematically investigated. An evolution 
of microstructure from lamellar eutectic to block eutectic was observed, 
and its effect on the mechanical properties was discussed.

Fig. 5 is a schematic diagram of the evolution of the microstructure 
from lamellar eutectic microstructure to massive eutectic microstruc -
ture. This was similar to the transition from lamellar pearlite to granular 
pearlite in steel [ 28 , 29 ]. The lamellar eutectic microstructure had high 
surface energy, which is an unstable microstructure. When it was 
maintained at a high temperature for a period of time, it spontaneously 
transformed into a structure with lower surface energy. As shown in 
Fig. 9 (g), there were many dislocations in the lamellar BCC phase, and it 
formed a sub-grain boundary with a lamellar FCC phase. Therefore, pits 
were formed at the contact points between the FCC and BCC phases. The 
FCC phases on both sides of the pits had a small radius of curvature. This 
caused the diffusion of Cr elements, which precipitated in the form of the 
FCC phase on the nearby plane of the FCC phase. To maintain balance, 
the sharp angle of the FCC phase on both sides of the pits would grad -
ually dissolve, increasing the radius of curvature. To maintain this 
equilibrium, the pits deepened as the FCC phase continued to dissolve. 
As this occurred, the FCC lamellar phase dissolved and fractured. Then 
the sharp angles dissolved, and the plane grew larger and fractured. The 
fine FCC phase kept coarsening, and finally, the large granular massive 
FCC phase was obtained. After that it gradually grew and coarsened. 
Moreover, the grains of the FCC phase gradually grew into larger grains, 
finally forming a massive eutectic microstructure.

3.3.3. Tensile properties 
Generally, there are unavoidable pores in powder metallurgical al -
loys, which result in poor tensile ductility. The tensile test can better 
reflect the ductility of the material. To further verify the density, 
strength, and ductility of the AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 
bulk EHEA, tensile tests 
were carried out on the alloys. The engineering stress-strain curves are 
shown in Fig. 12 . The strength and ductility of the 10T alloy were better 
than the 11T alloy. The yield and fracture strength reached 538.13 MPa 
and 963.55 MPa, respectively, while the tensile ductility reached 
13.30%. This far exceeds the findings of the literature [ 15 ]. The strength 
and ductility of 11T alloy decreased compared to 10T alloy. Grain 
growth and the reduced amount of lamellar eutectic microstructures 
may have been the main reasons for decreasing strength and ductility. 
Lamellar eutectic microstructure would have high strength than massive 
lamellar eutectic microstructure. The first reason is that there exist 
semi-coherent interfaces in the lamellar eutectic microstructure. The 
semi-coherent interface between the FCC and BCC phases [ 33 , 34 , 39 ] 
and the difference in shear modulus on both sides of the interface [ 37 ] 
lead to an enhanced dislocation accumulation capacity which could 
enhance the strength of the alloy. And the lamellar microstructure co -
ordinate with the soft FCC phase and hard BCC phase, which restrain the 
crack propagation more effectively than massive eutectic microstructure 
[ 34 ]. Thus, the 10T alloy has higher strength. Secondly, the lamellar 
microstructure can store higher dislocation density, form the vertical 
distribution of slip bands, and show a higher work hardening rate, which 
enabled it to have better ductility [ 40 ].

the fracture surface of 11T alloy, revealing the feature of brittle fracture.

friction coefficient curve typically suddenly increased to the maximum 
value in the initial running-in stage, then gradually decreased after 
entering the stable stage, and finally reached a stable value. The trend of 
the friction coefficient curves in Fig. 16 was essentially the same as that 
described in the literature [ 55 – 58 ]. At the beginning of the test, the 
interface was very rough and the contact area with the grinding ball was 
small. When the grinding ball was worn, a cold-welding effect occurred, 
requiring a large shear force to cut it, which caused the friction coeffi -
cient to increase rapidly [ 58 ]. With the increase of wear time, the con -
tact area became larger, and the interface became a smooth friction 
layer, which made the friction coefficient gradually stabilize. Compared 
with other alloys, the 8T and 9T alloys show more obvious curve 
fluctuations. This is mainly caused by the periodic accumulation and 
elimination of wear debris from the wear surface [ 56 , 57 ]. The coeffi -
cient of friction was increased when debris on the worn surface accu -
mulated. However, the friction coefficient decreased when the wear 
debris was separated from the worn surface or filled in the crack. In the 
alloy with hard matrix, the periodic local fracture is the main contrib -
utor to the curve fluctuation [ 55 , 59 ]. The local fracture was also caused 
by pores in 8T and 9T alloys. Therefore, the local fracture caused by the 
pores was another reason for the large fluctuation of the friction coef -
ficient [ 55 – 57 ].
