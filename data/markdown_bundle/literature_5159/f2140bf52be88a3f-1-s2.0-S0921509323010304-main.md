ABSTRACT 
This short communication elucidates the developed as-cast CoCrNi-based eutectic high entropy alloy by prefixing 
valence electron concentration to balance strength and ductility. The developed EHEA includes a face-centered 
cubic (FCC) phase, ordered B2 phase, and precipitates. The dislocation pile-up at the FCC/B2 interface balances 
the strength and ductility in a wide temperature range. 
1. Introduction 
The development of a novel high entropy alloys (HEAs) system 
combining exceptional strength and ductility is a challenging task [ 1 – 3 ]. 
HEAs contain either a single face-centered cubic (FCC) or body-centered 
cubic (BCC) phase, with FCC providing greater ductility with moderate 
strength and BCC providing higher strength with insufficient ductility 
[ 4 – 6 ]. Meanwhile, the presence of 5 – 6 elements with higher concen -
tration can weaken the fluidity, resulting in a final product with limited 
strength. To overcome this problem, the eutectic high entropy alloys 
(EHEAs) concept was proposed by Lu et al. [ 7 ] AlCoCrFeNi2.1 had 
controlled microstructure characteristics (hard B2 phases in the soft FCC 
phase) with superior mechanical properties. Up to now, EHEAs with the 
different phase compositions of BCC + B2, FCC + B2, and FCC + Laves 
have been studied. Among them, the EHEA with the FCC + B2 phase has 
shown superior load-bearing properties due to the plasticity provided by 
the FCC phase and the strength provided by the B2 phase. Guo et al. [ 8 ] 
recommended that the VEC of the system should be ≥ 6.87 and ≤ 8 to 
obtain a stable two-phase microstructure. On the basis of this, VEC 
criterion, the selected CoCrNi based EHEA is designed with a VEC = 7.7. 
The present work focuses on developing novel EHEA and investigating 
the phases, microstructural features, and tensile properties over a wide 
range of temperatures.

FCC phase is softer than the B2 phase due to the availability of the slip 
system [ 29 ]. During tensile, when the dislocations pass through the FCC 
phase, the hard B2 phase and B2 precipitates hinder the dislocation 
movement resulting in improving the strain hardening behavior and 
mechanical strength [ 30 ]. Baker et al. [ 4 ] reported that the Ni – Al rich B2 
phase had high hardness (1122 HV). The lattice friction stress and strain 
fields around the B2 phase contribute to this exceptional hardness [ 25 ]. 
Nevertheless, the B2 precipitates act as void initiation sites by stress 
localization at their grain boundaries, leading to early fracture of the 
alloy [ 31 ]. However, the developed EHEA exhibits a balanced ratio of 
strength and ductility at 298 K. The elongation is reduced with 
decreasing temperature, i.e., at 77 K. The YS and UTS are improved 
compared to room temperature properties. At cryogenic temperature, 
dislocation motion is difficult due to the absence of thermal energy 
needed to cross the Peierls-Nabarro barriers [ 32 ]. In addition, disloca -
tions accumulate inside the crystal before moving out to accommodate 
the plastic strain which reduces the ductility. These findings are 
compatible with Lu et al. [ 33 ]. At high temperature, the decrease in YS 
from RT is very minimal at 473 K, whereas at 673 K there is a large 
decrease with good strain. In general, dynamic recovery occurs when 
deforming at elevated temperature [ 34 ]. During the dynamic recovery 
process, the B2 phase and the precipitate in the FCC matrix inhibit the 
recovery process by exerting strengthening effect [ 35 , 36 ].

hetero-deformation-induced (HDI) strengthening arises from the defor -
mation incompatibility between soft and hard regions. The strain 
accumulation in the FCC phase is comparatively higher than that in the 
BCC phase, leading to the generation of back stress within the FCC 
phase, and causing stress concentration at the interface [ 37 ]. Conse -
quently, stress accumulation at the interface increases the KAM value. 
During deformation, the softer FCC phase can accommodate more strain 
produced than the B2 phase, which is called strain partitioning. Notably, 
Fig. S2 presents the results of the loading-unloading-reloading (LUR) 
test, underscoring the effective contribution of HDI strengthening to the 
mechanical properties a room temperature. The higher KAM value at 77 
K is an indication of the severe strain gradient during plastic deforma -
tion. Therefore, a large number of dislocations will be generated at the 
FCC/B2 interface at sub-zero temperature compared to the ambient 
condition [ 38 ]. However, it is crucial to note that the inherent difficulty 
in dislocation motion at cryogenic temperatures can result in dislocation 
persistence, potentially leading to premature material degradation. 
Conversely, the lower KAM value at high temperatures is attributed to 
the strain softening during dynamic recovery in the FCC followed by the 
BCC phase [ 39 ]. As a result, the process of dislocation recovery serves to 
dilute the HDI strengthening effect.

designed by analytical calculations and validated by Thermo-Calc. The 
identified constituent phases were FCC and ordered B2 phase together 
with the ordered B2 precipitates. Both phases are found to exhibit a K – S 
OR. Mechanical strength-ductility combination is achieved at 298 K,

Originality statement 
A novel CoCrNi-based eutectic high entropy alloy (EHEA) has been 
developed by prefixing valence electron concentration (VEC), based on 
the requirement to balance strength and ductility in high entropy alloys 
(HEAs). The developed EHEA includes two-phase structures: the FCC 
phase and the ordered B2 phase as well as the ordered B2 precipitates. 
The developed HEA showed excellent mechanical properties in a wide 
range of temperatures (77 K, 298 K, 473 K, and 673 K). The strength and 
ductility become compromised at 473 K, i.e., yield strength 365 MPa, 
ultimate strength 826 MPa with 60% ductility. The dislocation motion in 
the soft FCC phase and their piling up at the FCC/B2 interface balanced 
the strength and ductility.
