Thermal aging effects on the mechanical properties of
as-cast Ni3Al-based alloy
Dongyun Lee a,b,∗, M.L. Santella a,b

Received 23 October 2005; accepted 1 May 2006
Abstract
A series of tensile tests were conducted at room and elevated temperatures on specimens of the alloy known as IC221M (Ni–8.2Al–
7.6Cr–1.5Mo–2.1Zr, wt.%). Specimens were tested in the as-cast condition or after aging in either air or Ar for up to 1000 h at 900–1100◦C. Room
temperature yield strength decreased continuously with aging time at 900◦C from the as-cast value of 530–320 MPa after 1000 h. A similar trend
was found for hardness. The strength reductions with aging time at 900◦C were similar for aging in either air or Ar. For aging at 1050 and 1100◦C,
aging in air caused signiﬁcant strength loss that was attributed to aggressive oxidation. The room temperature yield strength of specimens aged in
Ar increased for aging at 1050 and 1100
◦C. Microhardness testing also showed that specimens aged at 1100 ◦C had higher hardness than those
aged at 900 ◦C. Analysis using equilibrium thermodynamics suggested that the increases of yield strength and hardness resulted from increasing
the amount of ﬁne /H9253′precipitation in the alloy matrix. For testing at elevated temperatures, the as-cast alloy had room temperature yield strength
of 530 MPa that increased continuously up to a value of 650 MPa at 700 ◦C. Aging in Ar for 1000 h at 900 ◦C resulted in a room temperature
yield strength of 320 MPa that increased to 560 MPa at 700 ◦C and decrease slightly to 550 MPa at 900 ◦C. Aging in Ar for 1000 h at 1100 ◦C
produced room temperature yield strength of 435 MPa that increased to 550 MPa at 500◦C and decreased continuously at high test temperatures.
The behavior of these specimens was also rationalized using equilibrium thermodynamics to estimate the fractions of coarse and ﬁne /H9253′particles
in the microstructures.

2. Experimental procedures
The analyzed chemical composition of the IC221M used
for this work was: Ni–8.21Al–7.61Cr–1.52Mo–2.09Zr, wt.%.
The mechanical properties were evaluated by tensile test-
ing and Vickers microhardness testing. Tensile specimens
with gage dimensions of 3.175 mm width × 0.762 mm thick-
ness × 38.1 mm length were cut from a centrifugal casting hav-
ing dimensions of 350 mm diameter × 20 mm wall. The spec-
imens were oriented to avoid complications presented by the
outer diameter surface that contacted the refractory mold coat-
ing. All of the tensile tests were done using an Instron
® model
4507 tensile testing machine (1127 lb load cell) at a cross head
speed 1 × 10
−3/s. Elevated temperature tensile tests were done
in air using about 30 min to equilibrate the specimens at temper-
ature and complete the test. The Vickers hardness measurements
were done using an indenter load of 300 gf.
To assess the effects of aging and oxidation on mechanical
properties two types of thermal treatments were used. In one
case, specimens were held in air for up to 1000 h at 900–1100
◦C.
In the second case, similar heat treatments were done in Ar. The
specimens aged in Ar were encapsulated in quartz tubes to pre-
vent oxidation during annealing treatments done to understand
how the intrinsic properties of IC221M were affected by thermal
aging. The results represent the average of three tests in each
case. The fracture surfaces and transverse sections of tensile
specimens were observed by optical microscopy and scanning
electron microscopy (SEM) equipped with energy dispersive X-
ray spectroscopy (EDX).
3. Results and discussion
3.1. Mechanical properties at room temperature
3.1.1. Effects of aging time in air at 900
◦C
The effects of increased aging time in air at 900 ◦C on the
room temperature tensile behavior of the IC221M alloy are
shown in Fig. 1 . The yield strength of the as-cast alloy was
about 530 MPa and the plastic tensile elongation was about 9%.
Aging signiﬁcantly decreased the yield strength with the major-
ity of the change, a reduction of about 28%, occurring after only
50 h of aging. The tensile ductilities decreased simultaneous to
the increases in yield strength. The ultimate tensile strength was
essentially unaffected by aging. Since this behavior also occurs
for aging in Ar, it is not an oxidation effect but related to other
microstructural changes discussed later.

3.1.2. Effects of aging temperatures: specimens aged in air
and argon
Results for specimens aged for 1000 h either in air or Ar at
900–1100 ◦C are presented in Fig. 5 where the dependencies
on aging temperature of the room temperature yield strength,
ultimate tensile strength, and plastic strain are shown. After
1000 h at 900
◦C, the yield strengths of specimens aged in both
air and Ar decreased by about 40% from that of the as-cast
alloy. This is not caused by oxidation since the same phe-
nomenon was observed for aging in both air and Ar. The aging
atmosphere did not have a signiﬁcant effect on the room temper-
ature yield strength until about 1000
◦C. At temperatures greater
than 1050 ◦C, the yield strength increased for aging in Ar but
decreased quite dramatically for aging in air. This difference
is due to development of oxides on the surfaces of specimens
aged in air. The behavior of the ultimate tensile strength and
plastic strain are shown in Fig. 5b and c. Aging in Ar had little
effect on the ultimate tensile strength and it did not degrade room
temperature ductility. Aging in air decreased both the ultimate
tensile strength and plastic strain, particularly above 1000
◦C.
This degradation of tensile properties coincided with aggres-
sive oxidation of the alloy as described in previous work [30].
Oxide spikes penetrate the surfaces of IC221M to depths of about
0.7 mm after aging for 1000 h in air at 1100
◦C. The specimen
thickness of 0.762 mm used for the tensile testing was selected,
in part, to accentuate the effects of oxidation on the tensile prop-
erties. Thus, the room temperature tensile property degradations
illustrated in Fig. 5 for aging in air are related to the sample size
and may not be a signiﬁcant concern for much thicker specimens
or components.
Fig. 6 shows the variations of fracture mode after aging in Ar
and indicates that fracture was caused by intergranular failure
along the cast dendritic microstructure atT ≤ 1000
◦C. A cleav-
age fracture mode dominated for aging at 1050 ◦C and above
although the fracture mode was still mixed as illustrated by the
higher magniﬁcation micrograph in Fig. 6f.
Collectively, these results show that thermal aging of as-cast
IC221M in Ar in the temperature range 900–1000
◦C actu-
ally improved the room temperature strength properties while
decreasing ductility. As discussed relative to the hardness mea-
surements, possible reasons for the increase in yield strength
of specimens aged in Ar are strengthening by solution of Zr
from the eutectic colonies into the base alloy and reprecipitation
of /H9253
′during cooling from the aging temperature. Zirconium is
one of the most effective solid solution strengtheners in Ni 3Al
alloys [31]. According to the earlier results [16,17], while the
volume fraction of the /H9253+N i5Zr eutectic colonies remains at
about 3.5 vol.% after aging 1000 h at 900◦C, at 1100◦C most of
the eutectic is dissolved into the base alloy (below 0.5 vol.%).
Therefore, aging at successively higher temperatures increases
the potential strengthening due to dissolved Zr. In addition,
the supersaturation at low temperatures relative to /H9253

tation increases at an increasing rate as the aging temperature is
increased, Fig. 4. This means that the amount of ﬁne, strength-

3.2. Mechanical properties at elevated temperature
3.2.1. Effect of testing temperatures: aging in Ar at 900 and
1100 ◦C
Tensile test results for the as-cast material and specimens
aged for 1000 h at 900 and 1100 ◦C in Ar are shown in Fig. 7.
Fig. 7a shows the variation of yield strength with testing temper-
ature. The yield strength of the as-cast material increased to a
peak of about 650 MPa at a temperature in the 600–800
◦C range.
This is consistent with the previous work [20,27,32] on this
material and illustrates the anomalous yield strength increase.
The behavior of the specimens aged in Ar at 900
◦C was gen-
erally similar to that of the as-cast alloy with the exception of
a lower yield strength. The yield strength of specimens aged
at 1100
◦C was intermediate to those of the as-cast and 900 ◦C
aged specimens up to 500 ◦C, but it decreased more rapidly at
higher temperatures. Fig. 7 b and c show the ultimate tensile
strengths and plastic strains, respectively. The tensile strength
for all three aging conditions was nearly the same for the room
temperature and 300

those aged at 900 ◦C maintained their tensile strengths up to
700 ◦C while those of the specimens aged at 1100 ◦C began to
decrease above 300◦C. The elongations of the specimens aged
at 900 and 1100 ◦C were much higher than those of the as-cast
alloy up to 300 ◦C, but beyond this testing temperature, the as-
cast material had higher tensile ductility.

4. Conclusions
A series of tensile tests were conducted at room and elevated
temperatures on specimens of the Ni–Al–Cr alloy, IC221M.
Specimens were tested in the as-cast condition or after aging
in either air or Ar for up to 1000 h at 900–1100
◦C. The objec-
tive of the testing was to measure the intrinsic properties of the
alloy and to assess the effects of oxidation on strength.
Room temperature tensile testing showed that the as-cast
IC221M alloy had yield strength of 530 MPa with plastic elon-

gation of about 9%. After 50 h of aging at 900 ◦C the room
temperature yield strength decreased by 28%. This reduction of
strength was observed for aging in either air or Ar, indicating
that oxidation did not inﬂuence this result. Vickers microhard-
ness measurements showed a similar effect of short time aging
at 900
◦C.
Room temperature tensile testing was also done on speci-
mens that were aged in air or Ar for 1000 h at 900, 950, 1000,
1050, and 1100
◦C After aging for 1000 h at 900 ◦C, the room
temperature strength experienced a reduction of about 40% rel-
ative to the as-cast condition. This result was the same for aging
in air or Ar. For aging at 1050 and 1100
◦C, aging in air caused
signiﬁcant strength loss that was attributed to aggressive oxida-
tion. The room temperature yield strength of specimens aged
in Ar increased for aging at 1050 and 1100
◦C. Microhard-
ness testing also showed that specimens aged at 1100 ◦C had
higher hardness than those aged at 900◦C. Analysis using equi-
librium thermodynamics suggested that this behavior was the
result of increasing the amount of ﬁne/H9253
′precipitation in the alloy
matrix.
Specimens of the as-cast metal and those that were aged in
Ar for 1000 h at 900 and 1100 ◦C were also tested at elevated
temperatures of 300, 500, 700, 900, and 1100◦C. Yield strength
peaks were observed for all three material conditions. The as-
cast alloy had room temperature yield strengths of 530 MPa
that increased continuously up to a value of 650 MPa at 700
◦C.
Aging at 900 ◦C resulted in a room temperature yield strength
of 320 MPa that increased to 560 MPa at 700 ◦C and decrease
slightly to 550 MPa at 900◦C. Aging at 1100◦C produced room
temperature yield strength of 435 MPa that increased to 550 MPa
at 500
◦C and decreased continuously at high test temperatures.
The behavior of these specimens was also rationalized using
equilibrium thermodynamics to estimate microstructures of the
aged specimens.
