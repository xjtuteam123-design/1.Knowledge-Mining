STRUCTURE AND PROPERTIES OF DEFORMABLE NICKEL 
ALLOYS FOR GAS TURBINES 
M. A. Filatova and V. S. Sudakov UDC 669.14.018.44:620.17 
The construction of high power gas turbine installations necessitates the development and use of new high- 
strength heat-resistant nickel alloys for large forged turbine blades. The blade material must possess not only 
superior properties (high temperature strength, ductility, fatigue and thermal fatigue strength, stability in the 
fi~el combustion products) but also the required level of metallurgical and manufacturing producibility. The 
results of a study of the deformable nickel alloys KhN65KVMYuTB (EK78), KhN65KMVYuB (EP800), and 
KhN6OKVYuMB (EP957), which have substantially better properties than the alloy now used for gas turbine 
blades (KhN65VMTYu) (E1893), are presented in this article. 
These alloys were developed by the combined efforts of the Scientific-Production Organization "TsNIITMASh,: 1 
"TsNIIChERMET," the "t~lektrostal'" works, and the Scientific-Production Organization "TsKTI" [1-7] for the manufacture 
of blades for gas turbine installations in which the working temperature of the metal is up to 810°C. 
The most heat resistant of the alloys is t~P957 (Fig. 1). All of the developed alloys possess heat resistance equal to the 
best foreign alloys Ud700 and Ud720, and are substantially better than the domestic alloy KhN65VMTYu (I~1893), which was 
developed more than 25 years ago and is still in use [8]. 
All of the alloys are strengthened mainly by the precipitation of 3"-phase (Ni, Co, Cr, W, Mo)3(A1, Nb) (in alloys 
I~P957 and t~PS00) or (Ni, Co, Cr)3(Ti, AI, Nb) (in alloy I~K78). 
All alloys (Table 1) were prepared by vacuum-arc melting. Their properties were determined primarily by the amount 
of 3,'-phase (volume fraction); the larger the amount of strengthening 3,'-phase, the greater the high-temperature strength of 
the alloy. 
Differences in the amount of 3"-phase in the alloys are obtained by alloying and heat treatment. The structure of alloy 
1~K78 after heat treatment contains a homogeneous precipitate of rounded 3"-particles (19-23 %), uniformly distributed within 
the austenite grains. The total amount of carbide precipitates [M23C6; Nb(C, N); (Hf, Nb)(N, C)], and M3B2-type borides, 
is about 0.2% located principally at the grain boundaries. The particles of (Hf, Nb)(N, C) are approximately 4/~m, of M3B 2 
-- 1.0/xm, and of M23C 6 -- 10 Izm in size. 
The particle size of the strengthening 3,'-phase in alloy 1~K78 remains unchanged after aging at 550-600°C for 5000 
h. The composition and total number of 3" particles are changed, but not appreciably. Increase in the amount of ,),'-phase 
amounts to -3% (from - 19 to -22%) due to an increase in the concentrations of Ni, Co, and A1, and a slight decrease in 
the Cr and Nb concentrations (Table 2). Increasing the aging temperature to 750-800°C does not lead to an increase in the 
amount of strengthening 3"-phase in the alloys, but is accompanied by an increase in the size of the rounded 7'-particles -- 
by about a factor of three, compared to the particle size in the unaged alloy, after aging at 750°C, and by almost an order of 
magnitude after aging at 800°C. The aforementioned structural changes in alloy 1~K78 upon prolonged aging do not have a 
substantial effect on its properties (Table 3).

Let us consider the influence of/z-phase on the properties of alloy I~P957, whose presence, according to the data of 
others, is undesirable in nickel alloys. Our investigations [1, 5, 7] did not reveal a negative effect. Attention is drawn to the 
fact that decrease of the stress-rupture strength in the presence of/z-phase is not the same for all nickel alloys [9]. 
The negative effect of/z-phase on the stress-rupture strength is explained as a consequence of weakening of zones 
adjacent to the grain boundaries due to depletion of the solid solution by the precipitation of this phase. In nickel alloys the 
/z-phase forms with a high concentration of molybdenum and tungsten. It has been shown [1-7] that a moderate amount of/z- 
phase helps in maintaining a satisfactorily high level of ductility in alloys I~P800 and I~P957 at working temperatures. There 
is practically no information on the effect of/z-phase on the change in all properties of nickel alloys during prolonged use at 
high temperatures.

gas turbine installations, is small (it was less than the amounts of binary carbide and aluminum nitride found by anodic

In spite of the altered structure of an alloy which has been aged for a long time (up to 10,000 h) at high temperatures 
(700, 750, and 800°C), the short term strength properties (% and o0.2) are not changed appreciably, and the ductility remains 
at a level corresponding to that of a structural material (6 _> 5%) (Table 3). 
The principal changes in the properties of alloy I~P957 during aging at various temperatures occur in the first one 
thousand hours. Stress-rupture tests at 650-850°C showed that the hot strength of alloy t~P957 is not substantially reduced. 
It should be noted that alloy t~P957 not only has a high endurance limit a R = 290-340 N/mm 2 (tests at T = 800°C 
on the basis of 1" 108 cycles, asymmetrical cycle, %s = 150 N/mm2), but also retains this property at practically the same level 
in the presence of g-phase after prolonged aging (up to 10,000 h) in the range 700-800°C. 
Prolonged aging of alloy I~P957 at 700-800°C did not appreciably change the high-temperature low-cycle fatigue 
strength in the deformation range Axe = 0.8-1.0. At Axe = 0.4-0.6 a tendency for this property to decrease is observed -- 
approximately 15% on the basis of 10,000 cycles (T = 800°C). 
Thus, investigation of the complex high-strength heat-resistant deformable alloy t~P957 revealed that it contains - 50% 
~/-phase and -7% /z-phase after prolonged aging, which is not in agreement with the idea that the presence of ~-phase is 
undesirable in alloys of this type. 
The basic properties of the alloys under consideration, relative to their use as the load-bearing blades of stationary gas 
turbines, are given in Table 4. It is evident that alloys I~K78, I~P800, and I~P957 have substantially better working properties 
than alloy t~I893, which was put into use some time ago. Alloy 1~K78 possesses substantially higher corrosion resistance than 
I~I983 in a medium of natural gas containing additions of NaCI, Na2SO 4, and various oxides [I I]. 
The stress-rupture strength and other properties of the alloys (short-time strength, high-cycle fatigue strength) can be 
substantially improved by adjusting the chemical composition within the designated limits -- melting at the upper limit of 
alloying elements, in particular aluminum and titanium, which enter into formation of the principal strengthening phase 7'. 
Tests of t~K78 (Fig. 2) indicate that the alloy with higher concentrations of aluminum and titanium (-2.5% of each) has a 
substantially higher stress-rupture strength at 600-750°C than the alloy with average concentrations (- 1.7% of each) [10]. 
It should be noted that the ductility of the alloys under consideration in stress-rupture tests at 600-800°C is adequate 
for this class of materials; in 10,000 h tests ~i = 8%. 
Large forged blades of alloys t~P800 and I~P975 have been fabricated and put into use in the industrial gas turbines 
GTl~-45 and GTt~-150 (T = 950°C); blades of alloy 1~K75 are in the stage of forging for use in the gas turbine GTt~-M5.1 
Thus, the nickel alloys I~K78, I~P800, and I~P957 which have been developed and brought into commercial production 
have substantially better working properties than the nickel alloy t~I983 used up to the present time.
