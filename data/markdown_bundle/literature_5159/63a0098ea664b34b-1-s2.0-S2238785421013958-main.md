abstract
In this paper, molecular dynamics simulations are performed to study the dynamic me-
chanical response and microstructural evolution of Ni-based single crystal superalloys
under different shock velocities. The results show that when the shock velocity
(U
p /C20 0.75 km/s), the interfacial dislocation network composed of the Stair-rod dislocation
will prevent the Shockley dislocation from shearing the g0 phase, the deformation of the
microstructure is dominated by the slipping and dragging of dislocations. When the shock
velocity ( U
p /C21 1 km/s), the interfacial dislocation network is destroyed under the shock
loading, the dislocations basically disappear, the atomic structure becomes disordered and
undergoes structural phase transformation. In addition, the increase of the shock velocity
leads to the regular increases of the shock pressure, internal energy and normal stress of
the superalloys. However, the shear stress increases sharply once the shock velocity is
greater than 1 km/s due to the change of the microstructural deformation mechanism. This
work has an important guiding signiﬁcance for in-depth understanding of the failure
mechanism of Ni-based single crystal superalloys under shock loading.

1. Introduction
Ni-based single crystal superalloys are the key material for the
manufacturing the turbine blades used in the advanced aero-
engine. During the operation of an aero-engine, turbine blades
as the hot end component of the engine will be repeatedly
subjected to the shock of high temperature and high pressure
gas, resulting in severe damage or even fracture, the service
life of turbine blades largely determines the survivability of
the aircraft [ 1e4]. However, a large number of studies on
mechanical properties of turbine blades are based on quasi-
static loading [ 5e9], few researches under shock loading. As
the preferred material of advanced aero-engine turbine
blades, Ni-based single crystal superalloys have excellent high
temperature creep, fatigue and corrosion resistance, and their

dynamic mechanical response and deformation characteris-
tics of Ni-based single crystal superalloys under shock loading
deserve extensive attention.
To the best of our knowledge, most researchers focus on
the study of the laser shock strengthening [ 13e15] and the
mechanical properties such as fatigue and creep of superal-

haviors of Ni-based superalloys under shock wave loading.
Part of the existing researches have combined the results of
experiments and numerical simulations to propose new the-
ories model to predict the failure mode and resistance char-
acteristics of superalloys under shock loading [ 21,22]. In terms
of experimental researches, relevant result on shock loading
of Ni-based superalloys shows that the mechanical properties
reﬂect non-monotonic temperature dependence at high strain
rate of shock loading, which may be the result of the rear-
rangement of the structure at different temperatures [ 23]. In
addition, the work by Lee et al. revealed that under high strain
rate of shock loading, the work hardening rate increases, the
deformation twins are more active, and the ﬂow stress is
related to the evolution characteristics of dislocations and
twins [ 24]. The above results reveal some aspects of the
properties of superalloys under shock loading, and show that
the dynamic mechanical properties are closely related to the
evolution characteristics of microstructure. However, the
above results mainly focus on the characteristics before and
after the deformation process, as well as the static micro-
scopic characteristics of the material at a certain time, which
are lacking of observation on the evolution process of atomic
microstructural interaction during deformation. The dy-
namics mechanical behaviors and microstructural evolution
of superalloys under shock loading are poorly understand, an
effective research method is still needed to characterize the
dynamics mechanical behavior and microstructural evolution
of superalloys under shock loading.
Molecular dynamics (MD) simulation can provide details of
atomic-scale mechanisms that cannot be directly observed in
experiments, which is a very effective tool to study the
chemical reactions, physical phenomena and mechanical
properties of metals and amorphous materials under shock
loading [ 25e32], and has become an important research
method for studying the dynamic behavior of materials in
recent years [ 33e35].
Therefore, in order to effectively explore the dynamic
behavior and microstructural evolution of Ni-based single
crystal superalloys under shock loading, and reveal its defor-
mation mechanism. MD simulations have been carried out to
study the shock loading tests on the Ni-based single crystal
superalloys, and analyze the stress response, internal energy
change, particle velocity and microstructural evolution char-
acteristics of the superalloys. The results reveal the dynamic
mechanical behavior and deformation mechanism of the su-
peralloys under the shock loading, and provide important
information for understanding the physical parameters and
microstructural characteristics of superalloys under shock
loading. This work has important guiding signiﬁcance for the
in-depth understanding of the failure mechanism of super-
alloys under shock loading and the development of shock
wave propagation theory for superalloys.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Veriﬁcation of Hugoniot relationship
In order to verify the correctness of the simulation and the
interaction potential function, the shock simulations of single
crystal nickel and aluminum are carried out by using the
simulation method and the potential function described in the
Section 2. The physical meaning of Hugoniot relationship
curve is the connection of the points of the ﬁnal state after the
shock waves with different velocities propagating in the same
medium with the same initial state. The comparison of
Hugoniot relationship between simulation data and experi-
mental data is shown in Fig. 2. The results of shock pressure-
speciﬁc volume relationship ðP /C24 V =V
0Þ and shock pressure-
particle velocity relationship ðP /C24 UpÞ are consistent with the
experimental results [ 50,51]. According to the comparison
results, it can be judged that the simulation and potential
function used in the present work are suitable for the shock
loading simulation of superalloys.
Figure 3 shows the spatial distribution of particle velocities
along the shock direction when the shock velocity is 0.5 and
1.5 km/s, respectively, which accurately depicts the propaga-
tion process of shock loading. It can be seen from the Fig. 3
that the shock wave generated by the piston gradually prop-
agates along the Y direction. Moreover, only elastic waves
appear in Fig. 3 a, because the generated shock pressure is
lower than the elastic limit at U
p ¼ 0.5 km/s. The effect of
elastic waves causes the particle velocity behind the wave-
front to ﬂuctuate basically at 0.5 km/s. At U
p ¼ 1.5 km/s, the
shock pressure exceeds the Hugoniot elastic limit of the ma-
terial, and the particle velocity proﬁle will have a double-wave
structure in which the elastic wave and the plastic wave are
separated. It can be seen from Fig. 3b that the particle velocity
ﬁrst increases sharply to about 1.25 km/s at the elastic wave
front, and then gradually rises to 1.5 km/s under the action of
the plastic wave. This is because the propagation velocity of
elastic waves is greater than that of plastic waves, the parti-
cles near the wavefront are only affected by the elastic wave at
ﬁrst, and the particle velocity can only reach about 1.25 km/s.
After the subsequent propagation of the plastic wave, particle
velocity reaches 1.5 km/s under combined action of the elastic
and plastic double waves. The double-wave structure is a
typical feature under the action of one-dimensional elasto-
plastic shock waves [ 34,52,53].

loading velocities, Fig. 5 shows the microstructural char-

the shock loading is small, the superalloys can effectively
resist the shock loading due to the dragging effect of
interfacial dislocation, and maintain a stable interface
microstructure [ 54,55]. When the shock velocity increases
to 0.75 km/s, the Shockley dislocation will gradually shear
the g

the process of shock due to the dragging action of Stair-rod
dislocation on the interface, resulting in the failure to
continue cutting the g
0 phase after slipping to a certain
extent as show in Fig. 5 b. And multiple Lomer-Cottrell locks
are formed in the subsequent equilibrium stage, which
basically maintains the stable microstructural morphology
of superalloys. As the shock velocity continues to rise,
Fig. 5 c shows that when the shock velocity is 1 km/s, the
atoms in the area swept by the wavefront appear disorderly
arrangement, and the Stair-rod dislocation on the g/g
0
phase interface also generate fracture under shock loading.
The destruction of the interface dislocation network makes
the superalloys unable to resist the action of the shock
loading. In the subsequent equilibrium stage, a large
number of disordered atomic structures will appear in the
superalloys, and the dislocations line almost disappear. The
microstructural evolution characteristics at the shock ve-
locities of 1.25 and 1.5 km/s are shown in Fig. 5 da n de ,
respectively. The atomic structure of the area swept by the
wavefront changes from an ordered to a disordered state,
and in the subsequent equilibrium stage, a large number of
disordered atomic structures appear in the superalloys and
no dislocations are observed.

4. Conclusions
In this paper, molecular dynamics simulations are per-
formed to study the dynamics mechanical response and
microstructural evolution of superalloys under shock
loading. The evolutions of dislocations and interfacial
dislocation network, dynamics mechanical behavior and
changes of related physical parameters under different
shock velocities are discussed. The major conclusions are as
follows:
(1) When Ni-based single crystal superalloys are sub-
jected to a lower shock loading, only elastic waves
appear in the sample. When the shock pressure is
greater than the Hugoniot elastic limit, an elasto-
plastic double-wave structure appears, which is
consistent with the shock wave propagation theory of
metallic.
(2) When the shock velocity ( U
p /C20 0.75 km/s), the defor-
mation mechanism under shock loading is that
Shockley dislocations shear into the g
0 phase, but
only to a certain extent under the hindrance of the
interfacial dislocation network, which is mainly
dominated by dislocation slipping and dragging.
When the shock velocity ( U
p /C21 1 km/s), the shock
loading directly causes the damage or even disap-
pearance of the dislocation network, and the atomic
structure gradually become disordered, the micro-
structural deformation is mainly the structural phase
transition.
(3) With the shock velocity increases, the shock pressure,
internal energy and normal stress of the superalloys
increase regularly. But the shear stress increases
sharply after the shock velocity is greater than 1 km/s,
because the deformation mechanism changes from the
dislocation slipping and dragging to the structural
phase transition mechanism in this case.
