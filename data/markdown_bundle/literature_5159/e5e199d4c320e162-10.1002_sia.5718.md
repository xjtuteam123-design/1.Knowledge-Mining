Ni-based superalloys with niobium (Nb) or/and yttrium (Y) were prepared by vacuum melting. The oxidation kinetics of these al-
loys was studied by thermogravimetry at 800 °C for 100 h in static air. Morphology of oxides was studied using SEM, and the com-
position was analyzed by X-ray diffraction. Energy-dispersive X-ray spectrometer was employed to examine the linear element
distribution of the cross section of the oxidation films. Results showed that the oxidation kinetics all followed a parabolic law
at different stages. The oxide films were mainly comprised of Cr2O3, NiCr2O4,A l2O3 and TiO2. All the oxide films exhibited layered
structure owing to different oxidation stages. With the addition of Nb or Y, the high-temperature oxidation resistance of the su-
peralloy was improved significantly and the surface morphology of the oxidation film was ameliorated. The comprehensive effect
of Nb and Y was remarkable in improving the high-temperature oxidation resistance of Ni-based alloys. Copyright © 2014 John

Introduction
Ni-based superalloys have been widely used in aerospace, energy,
metallurgy and chemical industries owing to its superior mechani-
cal performance, high-temperature oxidation resistance and corro-
sion resistance.
[1–4] In order to obtain better strength and oxidation
resistance at high temperature, different elements have been intro-
duced in Ni-based superalloys. For example, Cr, Mo and W can dis-
solve in Ni, producing γ–Ni solid solution. Except for a solution
strengthening effect, the added elements would affect the diffu-
sion process in Ni-based alloys. And then, the process such as solid-
ification, homogenization and γ′ precipitation would be affected.
[5]
Al, Ti and Nb in a Ni-based superalloy would form intermetallic
compound strengthening phases γ′–Ni3(Al, Ti), η–Ni3Ti, δ–Ni3Nb
and η–Ni6AlNb, which always exist as grain boundary precipitation
and are propitious to the mechanical properties such as strength,
ductility and resistance to intergranular fracture of the alloys. [6–9]
Cr2O3, always existing as a dense and compact film, is protective
for the Ni-based alloys and conventional stainless steels with appro-
priate amount of Cr addition.
[10,11] Besides, Cr can cause a third-
element effect in the oxidation of Ni –xCr–7Al alloys, preventing
the internal oxidation of aluminum and inducing the formation of
external Al
2O3 scales.[12] Except for Cr2O3, a compact Al 2O3 layer is
beneficial to the protection of metals or alloys. The substrate will
be isolated from the oxygen or other aggressive media by the pro-
tective layer, and hence, further oxidation or corrosion is
restrained.
[13,14]
The high-temperature oxidation resistance has been a hot issue
in developing Ni-based superalloys apart from their high-
temperature strength. And numerous studies have been performed
regarding this issue. Except for Cr and Al, the effects of some certain
special elements or compounds, such as C,
[15] Si,[16] Fe, Co,[17] Ta,
Cb,[18] La,[19] Y,[20] Zr[21] and Y2O3,[22,23] on high-temperature oxida-
tion resistance of Ni-based superalloys have been studied.
Besides, Nb was also introduced into Ni-based superalloys or
other alloys in the interest of enhancing high-temperature oxida-
tion resistance.
[24–31] According to Niu et. al. [27], the oxidation
properties of two Ni–Nb alloys containing 15 and 30 wt% Nb were
studied at 600 –800 °C in pure oxygen. It was concluded that nio-
bium was moderately deleterious for the corrosion resistance to ox-
ygen because of the formation of the duplex oxidation scales with
an outermost layer of NiO and an inner layer composed of a mixture
of NiO and NiNb
2O6, which was attributed to the low solubility of
niobium. However, Nb doping in TiO 2 was proven more effective
in improving the oxidation resistance of Ti –Ni–Al–Nb alloys be-
cause of a decrease in oxygen vacancies and the suppressed inward
diffusion of oxygen. In addition, the effect of Nb was relevant to its
existence, either in precipitates or in a solid solution.
[30]
Rare earth has been used widely in material modification be-
cause of its excellent properties.[32–37] Rare earth element was also
introduced in Ni-based superalloys, such as Y. It has been proven
that the isothermal oxidation rate of Ni–30Cr–0.5Y alloy was lower

In this paper, a series of new Ni-based alloys were fabricated with
the addition of Cr, W, Mo, Ti and Al. Moreover, the effects of Nb, Y
and both of them combined on the high-temperature oxidation re-
sistance of Ni-based alloys were studied at 800 °C. The oxidation ki-
netics, oxidation scale morphology and the phase composition
were discussed in depth.
Experiment
A vacuum arc melting furnace was employed in our study. By vac-
uum melting appropriate amounts of high-purity elements with in-
duction stir, the Ni-based superalloys were prepared. The
composition of the alloys is shown in Table 1. For the sake of ho-
mogenization, the cast ingots underwent normalizing for 4 h at
1200 °C and aging for 10 h at 800 °C, respectively. Specimens, with
a size of 10 mm × 10 mm × 3 mm, were cut from the ingots by con-
ventional wire-electrode cutting. Each specimen was mechanically
abraded on successively finer abrasive papers up to 800#, cleaned
ultrasonically with alcohol and acetone, respectively, and then
dried immediately before oxidation testing.
As far as we know, the initial surface conditions of the substrates
do have an effect on the oxidation or corrosion behavior of the
materials.
[45–48] First, a higher residual stress was obtained in the ox-
idation scales with a larger initial surface roughness of the sub-
strate, increasing the spalling tendency of the scales.
[45] Second,
too high surface roughness was harmful to the adhesion of the ox-
idation scale with the substrate.
[46] Third, the surface roughness
could also influence the integrity of the oxidation scales. [47] How-
ever, the surface treatment of all the specimens before the oxida-
tion test was identical in this paper as mentioned earlier. Hence,
the effect of substrate roughness was neglected. And we will prob-
ably carry on some relevant study in our future study.
The uniformity of the cast structure of the alloys was checked by
the metallographic observation by SEM. Microstructures of the alloys
before oxidation testing are uniform, as shown in Fig. 1. In contrast,
microstructure of the alloy with the addition of Nb and Y is finer.
The high-temperature oxidation test in this paper was conducted
by referring to the Chinese national aviation standard (HB 5258-
2000, test method for determination of oxidation resistance of steel
and superalloy). Oxidation testing was carried out in static air at
800 °C for 100 h in a chamber electric furnace. An air hole with a di-
ameter of 15 mm was set on the furnace door to guarantee the ox-
idizing atmosphere in the chamber of the furnace. The relative
humidity of the air in the laboratory was 30 –40%, and the average
room temperature was 25 °C during the oxidation test process,
tested by a KTH-A1 thermohygrometer (Tianjin Kailongda
instrument Co., Ltd, China). The air was mainly comprised of O
2
(21 vol%) and N2 (78 vol%).
In our study, the designed Ni-based superalloys will be employed
in the fabrication of spark plug electrodes. Spark plug is an essential
part of engine for cars and motorcycles. The electrode on spark
plug is subject to high-temperature oxidation and hot corrosion
owing to the combustion of fuels. The best operating temperature
of the electrode is 500–900 °C. A temperature below 500 °C will result
in carbon deposit. And too high temperature will accelerate the oxida-
tion of the electrode. So, we choose a middle temperature (800 °C) as
the test temperature during the high-temperature oxidation test.
The oxidation kinetics was measured by thermogravimetry. The
mass changes of the specimens were discontinuously recorded
by an electronic balance (accuracy of 10
/C0 4 g) at 1 h, 5 h, 10 h, 20 h,
50 h and 100 h, respectively. The phase constitution was analyzed
by a D/Max-γB X-ray diffraction (XRD) instrument (Rigaku Corpora-
tion, Tokyo, Japan) using CuK α radiation with the scanning speed
of 4°/min. The microstructures of the oxide scales were examined
by a JSM-6380LA SEM (JEOL Ltd, Tokyo, Japan) with an energy-
dispersive X-ray spectrometer. The specimens for transversal sur-
face observation were inlaid in Bakelite to protect the oxide films
from dropping and prepared by a traditional mechanical polishing
process.

In general, the high-temperature oxidation of metals and alloys is
a complicated process. First, oxygen is absorbed on the surface of
the substrate and oxides will nucleate from the reaction between
the metallic elements and oxygen. Second, oxides will grow along
the surface of the substrate, forming a thin, continuous oxidation
film. Third, the formed oxidation layer will thicken in pace with
the oxidation process.
The oxidation rate is high at the initial stage; during which, the
specimens are exposed in high-temperature air and oxidation pro-
ceeds quickly. Therefore, the weight increases quickly in the begin-
ning of the oxidation. The oxidation process is controlled by the
chemical reaction rate between the metal atoms and the oxygen.
Oxidation films will take shape on the surface of the alloys with
the process of oxidation. Taking Cr
2O3 for instance, the diffusion
of oxygen inward and the metallic ions outward will be depressed
as soon as the continuous dense Cr
2O3 film forms.[50] In this case,
the oxidation rate will be slowed to some extent. In the final stage,

The surface morphologies of the four alloys oxidized for 100 h at
800 °C are shown in Fig. 5. The oxidation scales for specimen A is
nonuniform, and there are many raised oxides in striping morphol-
ogy (Fig. 5a) along the polishing scratch left behind in the process
of grinding before oxidation testing. Defects exist in this area, and
oxides easily nucleate here. Numerous particle-like phases assem-
ble on the surface, and they are most likely TiO
2 in reference to
the previous XRD analysis and the following energy-dispersive X-
ray spectrometer results. TiO
2 tends to be agminate on the surface
in irregular shapes, and micro-cracks are observed on the rough
surface, as Fig. 5b shows. Except for the white phases, the oxidation
scale mainly consists of little gray grains, a mixture of Cr
2O3 and a
few NiCr2O4 phases, as shown in Fig. 5b (the gray area).
The surface morphology of the oxidation scale is improved dis-
tinctly with the addition of 2.0 wt% Nb or 0.4 wt% Y. A relatively
even and smooth surface is obtained after high-temperature oxida-
tion for 100 h, as Fig. 5c and 5e shows. Comparatively speaking, the
surface of specimen C is more uniform except for some dispersed
large TiO2 aggregates. TiO2 particles in irregular shape are also ob-
served in specimen B (Fig. 5d), but with a smaller size compared
with that in specimen A. Compared with Nb, the effect of Y is more
significant, as shown in Fig. 5d and 5f.
The addition of trace Y is proven effective in improving the sur-
face of the oxidation film. As shown in Fig. 5f, a superior surface is
obtained with massive small white particles distributing on it, and
the average size of the particles is 0.5 –0.7 μm. The oxides are
refined notably owing to the effect of Y. As a surface active element,
Y tends to distribute along the grain boundary or other crystal de-
fects such as dislocation.
[52] The interfacial energy or other physical
or chemical properties of the boundary will change because of the
interplay between Y and the defects or other elements; as a result
of which, the growth of the crystals is restricted.
In addition, Y is more active than Cr and Y
2O3 can be obtained
easier during the oxidation. And the first generated Y 2O3 will act
as the nucleating center of Cr2O3, promoting the generation of pro-
tective oxidation film and refining the oxide scales.[53] According to
the calculation of theΔG in Table 3, the Gibbs free energy of forma-
tion of Y2O3 is lower than that of Cr 2O3. Thus, Y probably takes ef-
fect in the form of Y 2O3 in this study. However, no Y 2O3 was
detected in the XRD results probably because of its trace amount.
As soon as the protective oxidation film forms, further oxidation
will be difficult. What’s more, the REE of rare earth element plays a
vital role in enhancing the high-temperature oxidation resistance of
the alloys. The residual stress in virtue of the growth process or the
temperature change can be released because of REE.
[20] Hence, ox-
idation scale with better adhesion with the substrate is acquired,
which is beneficial to the oxidation resistance of the alloy.
As a result of the comprehensive effect of Nb and Y, the surface
morphology is improved prominently. There is scarcely any
agminate oxide, and compact oxide particles distribute on the sur-
face almost without any defects, as Fig. 5g and 5h shows. The out-
standing surface morphology is beneficial to the excellent high-
temperature oxidation resistance. More details of the surface mor-
phology are obtained by SEM (Fig. 6a –d), and the effects of Nb or
Y and their comprehensive effect are remarkable. In contrast, spec-
imen D with the addition of Nb and Y exhibits an even and dense
surface after oxidation test, as Fig. 6d shows.

In contrast, specimen D possesses the thinnest oxidation film of
1.0 μm. The thicknesses of specimens B and C are 2.6 and 1.2 μm,
which can be obtained by the scale on the photograph. Thus, it
can be seen that the effect of Nb and Y on the oxidation process
of Ni-based alloys is significant. As Fig. 7b and 7d shows, a certain
amount of Nb is detected in the oxidation film (the ring). The mech-
anism of Nb can be summarized as follows. The Nb-rich layer
beneath the oxidation scale impedes the diffusion of oxygen and
metallic element, suppressing further oxidation.
[55] Besides, Nb
has been proven effective in the formation of a compact and pro-
tective oxidation scale.
[56]
In this study, the effect of Nb is notable in improving the high-
temperature resistance of the Ni-based superalloys, as Fig. 2 shows.
Besides, the surface morphology of the oxidation scale is improved
more with the addition of Nb (Fig. 5d). After introducing Y into Ni-
based alloy subsequently, the oxidation film turns out to be finer
and closer (Fig. 5g), restraining further oxidation and contributing
to the thinnest oxidation film on specimen D.
Although a certain amount of W and Mo is detected in the oxida-
tion film, no oxides containing W or Mo are picked out in XRD. It
was proven that W and Mo mainly exist in γ–Ni with the effect of
solid-solution strengthening. What’s more, W and Mo will weaken
the diffusion process of Al, Ti and Cr at high temperature, enhanc-
ing the high-temperature strength of Ni-based superalloys.
[57,58]
However, the effect of W and Mo on the high-temperature oxida-
tion properties of Ni-based alloys needs more intensive study.
In summary, the effect of Nb and Y on the high-temperature re-
sistance of the Ni-based superalloys is notable. This study lays ex-
perimental and theoretical foundations for the application of the
designed Ni-based superalloys in industries, especially in the fabri-
cation of spark plug electrodes.
Conclusions
Four Ni-based superalloys with or without niobium (Nb) or/and yt-
trium (Y) were prepared by vacuum melting in this study. After ox-
idation for 100 h at 800 °C, the Ni–Cr–Nb–Y–M Ni-based superalloy
exhibits the best high-temperature oxidation resistance. The oxida-
tion scales mainly consist of Cr
2O3,N i C r2O4,A l2O3 and TiO2.N ba n d
Y are proven effective in enhancing the oxidation properties of Ni-
based superalloys. The comprehensive effect of Nb and Y is remark-
able. Both the oxidation kinetics and the surface morphology of the
oxidation scales are improved. Besides, the alloy with the addition
of Nb and Y shows the thinnest and most dense oxidation scale,
impeditive for further oxidation.
