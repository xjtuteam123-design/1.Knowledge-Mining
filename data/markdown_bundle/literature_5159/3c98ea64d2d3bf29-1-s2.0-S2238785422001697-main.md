abstract
To improve the effect of solid solution strengthening of Nb-Si based alloys, the inﬂuence of
Cr addition on microstructure evolution and mechanical properties, such as the
compressive strength and the room-temperature fracture toughness of Nb-16Si- xCr alloys
are studied by using vacuum non-consumable melting combined with ultrasonic vibration.
Results show that with the Cr content increase, the silicide becomes more continuous,
which causes easy crack propagation. The NbCr
2 Laves phase is precipitated in Cr-rich
region by addition of 4 at.% Cr. The element Cr is mainly dissolved in the Nb solid solu-
tion (Nbss) phase, the fracture toughness and compressive strength increase with the
increasing of Cr content, which is attributed to the solid solution strengthening by Cr
addition. However, the precipitated NbCr
2 phase results in the signiﬁcant decrease in
mechanical performance. In order to eliminate NbCr 2 phase and reﬁne the coarse silicide,
ultrasonic vibration is introduced into the Nb-Si-Cr melt. With the ultrasonic treatment for
60s, silicide has been reﬁned and the NbCr
2 phase disappear, the fracture toughness is
9.33 MPa m 1/2 and the compressive strength is 2363 MPa, which are 39.5% and 14.8% higher
than the as-cast Nb-16Si-6Cr alloy, respectively.

1. Introduction
Nb-Si based alloys have been widely studied as a new kind of
potential elevated temperature structural material for the
aero industry due to the more excellent elevated temperature
mechanical properties and the lower density compared with
the nickel based superalloys [ 1]. As the requirements for the
thrust-to-weight ratio (TWR) of aero engines are getting
higher and higher, there are two methods for improve the
TWR, one is lighten the aircraft, the other is increase the gas
temperature at the front of the engine blades [2,3]. Nb-Si based
alloys show the excellent prospects for replacing Ni based

adequate room-temperature fracture toughness is essential to
the security of manufacturing and assembling. We hope that
the appropriate Cr content and the ultrasonic vibration
treatment (UST) could reﬁne the coarse primary silicide and
improve the mechanical properties for further processing.
In this paper, the microstructural evolution and room
temperature mechanical performance of Nb-16Si- xCr (all
chemical compositions are given in atomic percentage) alloys
has been systematically investigated, and the reasons for va-
riety in mechanical properties caused by change of the
microstructure of Nbss and silicide has been analyzed. We
hope that the ultrasonic treatment could be a novel process-
ing application method for microstructure modiﬁcation of Nb-
Si based alloys.
2. Experimental procedures
In this study, as-cast ingots with the nominal compositions of
Nb-16Si-xCr (x ¼ 0,2,4,6,8 at.%) were prepared by vacuum non-
consumable melting under argon atmosphere inside the
water-cooled copper crucible, and all alloys were melted ﬁve
times for the uniformity of elements. The purity of all the raw
material used in this paper is 99.95% or higher (in mass, based
on the supplier speciﬁcations). The commercial ultrasonic
generator with titanium amplitude ampliﬁer horn and radi-
ator were used. The ingots were put in the water-cooled
copper crucible and directly melted by arc heating. When
the ingots melted, the ultrasonic vibration was immediately
introduced to the liquid Nb-16Si- xCr alloy melt; the melt was
cooled in the vacuum and continuous cooling solidiﬁed. The
ultrasonic vibration was sustained for 60s with the ultrasonic
power of 2000 W and vibration frequency of 20 kHz and the
maximum available ultrasound amplitude at the radiating
face is 20 mm.

phase and different crystalline intermetallics, such as a-

3.3.3. Fracture morphology
Fig. 6 shows the fracture surface of Nb-16Si- xCr (x ¼ 0, 2, 4, 6,
8) alloys, which subjected to bending tests on notched
specimens. The fractography of the Cr free alloy Nb-16Si is
composed of the ductile phase Nbss and the brittle silicide
phase, which exhibits typical tearing edges of Nbss and
cleavage plane of silicide phase in Fig. 6(a). With the increase
of Cr content, the microstructure of Nb-16Si- xCr has been
reﬁned signiﬁcantly as shown above, which expresses in
Fig. 6 as the decrease in the area of cleavage planes. There are
some secondary cracks can be observed in Fig. 6 (b), which
can explain the reason of Nb-16Si-2Cr alloy exhibits better
toughness than the Nb-16Si alloy. When generate secondary
cracks, the crack tip stress distribution changes and absorbs
more energy [ 22]. Fig. 6 (c) shows there is a large amount of
cleavage planes on the fracture surface of Nb-16Si-4Cr alloy.
And the continuity of silicide, which has shown and dis-
cussed in Fig. 3 leads to the connection of cleavage planes
into cleavage steps during fracture. In this case, the main
crack can expand rapidly and show a low level fracture
toughness. With the increase of Cr content, some pre-
cipitates can be observed in Fig. 6 (d) and (e). In order to
determine the composition of the granular precipitate, EDS
analysis has been used. And it shows the composition is Nb
35.77%, Si 9.61 at.% and Cr 54.62 at.%, which can be regarded
as NbCr
2 phase. Therefore, the addition of 6 at.% induces the
formation of NbCr 2 phase and it is extremely harmful to
room temperature fracture toughness, because of the
appearance of precipitate reduces the bonding strength of
the Nbss/silicide interface.
In order to improve the solid solution strengthening effect
of Cr element, eliminate precipitates and reﬁne the micro-
structure of Nb-16Si-xCr alloys, ultrasonic treatment has been
introduced into the Nb-Si based alloys. The large area of
cleavage plane disappears as shown in Fig. 6 (f), and the
fracture morphology is the typical river pattern. As discussed
above, ultrasonic treatment for 60s can reﬁne the micro-
structure effectively, and make Nb-16Si-6Cr alloy exhibit the
nearly eutectic structure, which is beneﬁcial to mechanical
properties.
