Glass-forming ability and mechanical properties of Cu50Zr50−xTix alloys
H. Men∗, S.J. Pang, T. Zhang
Department of Materials Science and Engineering, Beijing University of Aeronautics and Astronautics, Beijing 100083, PR China
Accepted 29 August 2005
Abstract
Glass-forming ability (GFA) of Cu50Zr50−xTix (x = 0–50 at.%) alloys was investigated by melt-spinning and copper mould casting methods. Bulk
metallic glasses can be formed in the composition range of 0–15 and 35–45 at.% Ti. The x = 7.5 and 40 alloys show the best GFA in the local
compositional vicinity, and critical diameters for glass formation is 5 and 3 mm, respectively. The GFA of thex = 20–30 alloys was degraded due to
appearance of a Mg2Zn-type Laves phase in the center portion of Cu–Zr–Ti diagram. The supercooled liquid temperature region and reduced glass
transition temperature are 40 K, 0.587, respectively, for Cu50Zr42.5Ti7.5, and 44 K, 0.556, respectively, for Cu50Zr10Ti40. These glassy alloys exhibit
the high compressive fracture strength. The fracture strength and plastic strain before failure are 1798 MPa, 1.0%, respectively, for Cu50Zr42.5Ti7.5,
and 1810 MPa, 1.5%, respectively, for Cu50Zr10Ti40.

1. Introduction
In the past decades, a great number of bulk metallic glasses
(BMG) have been developed in the multicomponent systems,
such as La- [1],Z r -[2–4] or Pd-based [5] alloys, which exhibit
excellent glass-forming ability with a large supercooled liq-
uid temperature region before crystallization. Recently, the
BMGs with ordinary metals (Fe [6–8],T i [9,10],C u [11–14]
or Ni [15,16]) as main components have been drawing increas-
ing interest due to higher strength and lower material cost,
which are preferable for application as engineering materi-
als. Since the synthesis of Cu
60Zr30Ti10 BMG with a diame-
ter of 4 mm [11], many Cu-based bulk glassy alloys, such as
Cu60Hf40−xTix (Cu0.6Zr0.3Ti0.1)98Y2, (Cu 0.6Zr0.3Ti0.1)98Be2,
(Cu0.6Zr0.3Ti0.1)99Sn1,C u50Zr45Al5,C u46Zr42Al7Y5 and so on
had been developed [12,17–22]. The Cu-based BMGs exhibit
high compressive fracture strength and large plastic strain before
failure [11,19]. Very recently, it was reported that binary Cu–Zr
system has a surprising glass-forming ability[23–26], and 2 mm
diameter rods can be formed for Cu
64Zr36,C u50Zr50 alloys. It is
expected that the glass-forming ability (GFA) can be improved
with partially substituting Ti for Zr in Cu
50Zr50. On the other
hand, the appearance of a MgZn2-type Laves phase in the center

2. Experimental procedure
Cu50Zr50−xTix (x = 0–50) alloy ingots were prepared by arc
melting the mixtures of pure Cu, Zr and Ti under an argon
atmosphere. Cylindrical rods with diameters of 1–6 mm were
prepared by a copper mould casting method. In addition, ribbon
samples with a cross section of 0.02 mm× 2 mm were prepared
by the melt-spinning technique. Glassy structure was examined
by X-ray diffraction (XRD) using Cu K /H9251radiation. Thermal
stability associated with the glass transition and crystalliza-
tion was examined by differential scanning calorimeter (DSC)
at a heating rate of 0.67 K/s. Melting behaviors were exam-
ined by differential thermal analysis (DTA) at a heating rate of
0.67 K/s. Cylindrical rods (φ 2m m× 4 mm) were used to mea-
sure compressive mechanical properties at room temperature on
an Instron-type testing machine at a strain rate of 4× 10
−4 s−1.
The Young’s modulus was measured using a strain-gauge
meter.

4. Discussion
There are large negative heats of mixing of −22 kJ/mol
for Cu and Zr and −9 kJ/mol for Cu and Ti, suggesting the
intensive attractiveness between Cu and Zr or Ti atoms in the
melts. Otherwise the heat of mixing between Zr and Ti is zero,
and so Zr and Ti can be considered interchangeable in the
Cu–Zr–Ti system. As such, the liquidus temperature is signiﬁ-
cantly reduced with partially substituting Ti for Zr in Cu
50Zr50
and vice versa. It is indicated that the ternary Cu 50Zr50−xTix
alloys has a more stable undercooled liquid, compared to the
binary alloys. However, the liquidus temperature keeps nearly
constant from 15 to 35 at.% Ti, as shown inFig. 4. It is attributed
to the appearance of a MgZn
2-type Laves phase in the center
portion of ternary Cu–Zr–Ti system (as shown in Fig. 7 ). In
this case, the Laves phase has a relatively low and ﬂat liquidus
curves.
In binary Cu–Zr and Cu–Ti equilibrium phase diagrams,
CuZr phase is a thread compound and CuTi phase has a homo-
geneity range of 48–52 at.% Cu. The melting temperatures of
CuZr and CuTi phases are the highest in the local compositional
vicinity for the respective binary system. It is assumed that CuZr
or CuTi phase is the competing crystalline phase against glass
formation upon cooling of the corresponding alloy melt. The
Goldschmidt atomic radii of Cu, Ti and Zr are 0.128, 0.147 and
0.16 nm, respectively. Therefore, CuZr or CuTi phase may be
destabilized with the addition of Ti or Zr, owing to the large

5. Summary
Bulk metallic glasses can be formed from x = 0–15 and
x = 35–45 for the Cu50Zr50−xTix alloys by a copper mould cast-
ing method. The best glass former is located at x = 1 5i nt h e
Zr-rich range and x = 40 in the Ti-rich, which critical diame-
ters are 5 and 3 mm, respectively. Tg, /Delta1Tx and Trg are 677, 40,
0.587 K, respectively, for Cu50Zr42.5Ti7.5, and 652, 44, 0.556 K,
respectively, for Cu50Zr10Ti40. E, σf, εp are 88 GPa, 1798 MPa,
1.0%, respectively, for Cu50Zr42.5Ti7.5, and 86 GPa, 1810 MPa,
1.5%, respectively, for Cu50Zr10Ti40. The degrading GFA in the
composition range from 15 to 35 at.% Ti is attributed to the
appearance of the Cu2ZrTi Laves phase.
