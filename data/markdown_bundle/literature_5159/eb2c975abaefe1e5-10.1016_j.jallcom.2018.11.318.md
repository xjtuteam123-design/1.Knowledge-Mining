abstract
Mechanically alloyed (MA) NbMoTaWVCr refractory high-entropy alloy (HEA) powders were sintered by
spark plasma sintering (SPS) at temperatures of 1400 e170 0/C14 C. The microstructural evolution and me-
chanical properties of sintered HEAs were subsequently investigated. During the MA process, only a
supersaturated body-centered cubic (BCC) structured solid solution was formed. However, C15 Laves
phase (Cr,V)
2(Ta,Nb) and Ta 2VO6 particles were precipitated from the disordered BCC phase during the
sintering process at temperatures /C20 150 0/C14 C. When the sintering temperature increased to 1600 /C14 C, the
Laves phase was transferred to C14 structure and its volume fraction was dramatically reduced. The
plasticity of the refractory HEA was strongly affected by the fraction, size and distribution of Laves phase
and oxide particles. The NbMoTaWVCr alloy sintered at 1500
/C14 C obtained an excellent combination of
yield strength (3416 MPa) and failure plasticity (5.3%) at room temperature. The extraordinary high
strength of this HEA could be dominantly attributed to the grain boundary strengthening from the
micron-sized BCC phase (1.24
mm), interstitial solid solution strengthening from O in the matrix and the
inherent solid solution strengthening in the multi-principal element NbMoTaWVCr alloy.

1. Introduction
Ever since the concept of high-entropy alloy (HEA) was ﬁrst
proposed by Yeh in 2004, it has experienced a great development
during the past few years. HEAs consist of at least ﬁve principle
elements with equal or near equal atomic ratios, with the con-
centration of each element ranging from 5% to 35% [ 1e3]. Some
HEAs exhibit promising performance in mechanical strength [ 4,5],
oxidation resistance [ 6], corrosion resistance [ 7,8]and anti-wear
properties [ 9] in comparison with traditional alloys. Obviously,
this designing concept based on multi-principle element alloy
signiﬁcantly accelerated the exploration and development of new
alloy systems.
A new bunch of refractory high-entropy alloys has been re-
ported by Senkov et al. by extending the HEA design to refractory
alloys [10e12]. It was found that refractory HEAs such as NbMoTaW
and NbMoTaWV with high melting points exhibited higher me-
chanical strength at elevated temperatures compared with tradi-
tional super-alloys such as Haynes 230 and Inconel 718 [ 12].
Recently, other classes of refractory HEAs such as TaNbHfZrTi [ 11],
TiZrNbTaMo [ 13], AlNbTiVZr x [14] and CrNbTiVZr [ 15] have been
also widely investigated. In general, those refractory HEAs with BCC
structure show higher strength and hardness than HEAs with FCC
structure. Some of refractory HEAs might possess simple BCC
crystal structures because of their large mixing entropy. However,
intermetallic compounds were occasionally observed in refractory
HEAs in some cases. For example, in NbCrMoTaTiZr, CrNbTiZr, and
CrNbTiVZr alloy systems, Laves phase formed in BCC matrix due to
the smaller atomic radius of Cr compared with the other alloy el-
ements in those refractory HEAs [ 15e17]. The mechanical proper-
ties can be affected by the microstructure, especially the
precipitation of Laves phases in refractory HEAs. A considerable
improvement of strength at elevated temperature was observed in
those Cr-containing refractory HEAs, meanwhile, the room-
temperature plasticity of HEA was obviously reduced [ 16,17].

Here, we introduced the element of Cr into NbMoTaWV HEA in
order to further enhance its mechanical properties. Mechanically
alloying (MA) followed by spark plasma sintering (SPS) has been
applied to fabricate NbMoTaWVCr HEA bulk material. The MA
process can produce nanocrystalline alloying powders with good
homogeneity from elemental powders, while SPS can consolidate
powders to high density by applying pressure and electric pulse
current within a short time. Compared with vacuum arc-melting
method, MA-SPS could fabricate HEAs with ﬁner grain size and
more homogeneous microstructure [ 18e21].
In the present work, a ﬁne-grained NbMoTaWVCr alloy pos-
sessing ultra-high strength was reported. Microstructural evolution
of the refractory HEA during the sintering process and the effect of
microstructure characteristics on its mechanical properties were
investigated. The phase stability and strengthening mechanisms in
the sintered HEA were subsequently discussed.
2. Experimental procedures
Nb, Mo, Ta, and W elemental powders with the purity of 99.5%
and V, Cr elemental powders with the purity of 99.9% were mixed
in an equiatomic ratio. Particle sizes of those elemental powders
were all less than 45
mm( /C0 325 mesh). The mixed powders and
stainless-steel balls were put into stainless-steel vials at a ball to
powder ratio of 12:1. The MA process was performed in a high-
energy planetary ball mill under Ar as the protecting atmosphere.
The ball milled powders were taken out for analysis after ball
milling for 5, 10, 20, 30 and 40 h, respectively. The powders ball
milled for 40 h were subsequently consolidated by a SPS machine
(Sumitomo Coal Mining Co. Ltd., Japan) at 1400 e170 0/C14 Ci na
graphite die. After being hold at a predetermined sintering tem-
perature for 10 min in vacuum under a mechanical pressure of
30 MPa, the sintered samples were cooled at a rate of 300 /C14 C/min to
800 /C14 C and then slowly cooled down to ambient temperature. The
fabricated bulky HEA samples were cylinders with the diameter of
20 mm and height of 10 mm.
The phase analysis of milled powders and sintered bulks were
conducted by a Bruker D8 ADVANCE X-ray diffractometer (XRD)
with a Cu-K a radiation. Microstructures of sintered samples were
observed by Scanning Electron Microscope (SEM, Nava NaNo SEM
430, USA) equipped with an Energy Dispersive Spectrometer (EDS)
for chemical composition analysis. Specimens for SEM observation
were grinded and subsequently polished. The phase composition
and microstructure of bulk alloys were analyzed by Transmission
Electron Microscope (TEM, TECHNAL G2 F20 S-TWIN, USA) with an
EDS. Samples for TEM tests were thinned by mechanical polishing
and then via ion beam thinning. The thermal analysis was per-
formed in a Differential Scanning Calorimeter (DSC, Netzsch Sta
449C) at a heating rate of 10 K/min under ﬂowing high purity argon
atmosphere. The gas contamination of O in the sintered compacts
were determined by TC6000 Nitrogen/Oxygen Determinator (LECO
Co., US).
Densities of consolidated HEA samples were measured by
Archimedes principle method. The hardness test was carried out by
a digital Micro Hardness (Tester HVS-1000 Vickers hardness
apparatus) at a load of 500 gf. The compressive properties at room
temperature were measured by a testing system (AG-100 KNx,
Shimadzu Corporation, Japan) at a strain rate of 1 /C2 10
/C0 3 s/C0 1 using
cylinder specimens with the size of F3m m /C2 6 mm.
3. Results
3.1. Microstructure, phase formation and phase composition
Fig. 1a shows the XRD patterns of NbMoTaWVCr powders ball
milled for different times. Diffraction peaks of all the elemental
powders could be seen in the initial blend. As the milling time
increased to 5 h, peaks of Cr, Mo and V were found to disappear in
the XRD pattern. After ball milling for 10 h, only peaks of W could
be clearly observed while peaks of Nb and Ta were dramatically
weakened. When the ball milling time increased to 40 h, a super-
saturated NbMoTaWVCr solid solution phase was formed in the ball
milled powders. Noting that these six alloying elements occupy the
same or neighboring columns in the periodic table, which re ﬂects
similar aspects in their electronic structure, therefore some of these
components can be completely solid soluble in each other. It is
indicated that the solid solution sequence of the mechanically
alloyed elements was basically related with their melting points.
The elements with lower melting points generally dissolved more
quickly than the elements with higher melting points [ 22]. The
solid solution in the 40 h-milled powders was indexed to be a single
body-centered cubic (BCC) phase with a lattice parameter of
3.162 Å. The average grain size and lattice strain of these mechan-
ically alloyed powders were estimated to be 9.7 nm and 0.42%,
respectively, from the evident peak broadening according to the
Scherrer's formula. Fig. 1b demonstrated the morphology of 40 h-
milled HEA powders. After mechanically alloying, the diameter of
milled powders was decreased to less than 7
mm.
Fig. 1c illustrates the XRD patterns of bulky NbMoTaWVCr high-
entropy alloys fabricated by SPS at different sintering temperatures
from 40 h-milled powders. It was found that the samples sintered
at 1400 e150 0/C14 C consisted of a BCC phase, a kind of tetragonal
phase corresponding to Ta2VO6, and a C15 Laves phase whose peaks
were very close to those of Cr 2Ta. However, only the disorder BCC
matrix phase and Ta 2VO6 phase could be clearly detected at the
samples sintered at 1600 e170 0/C14 C. The XRD results suggested that
a Laves phase and an oxide phase were precipitated from the BCC
solid solution during SPS process, and what's more, the precipita-
tion of Laves phase could be hindered by the increase of temper-
ature. Fig. 1d illustrates the DSC image of 40 h-milled powders. Two
exothermic peaks were clearly seen at temperatures of 918.3 and
1035.2 /C14 C, corresponding to the precipitation of oxide and Laves
phase, respectively. As a non-equilibrium process, mechanically
alloying facilitates the formation of metastable phases and super-
saturated solid solutions [ 23,24]. When the temperature increased,
decomposition would take place and then new phases were
precipitated from the BCC solid solution.
Fig. 2 illustrates the SEM backscattered electron images of
NbMoTaWVCr HEAs sintered at different temperatures. The
chemical composition of the sintered HEAs and constituent phases
are measured by EDS/SEM and EDS/TEM, as listed in Table 1.A sw e
could see from Fig. 2 a-c, three different phases were clearly iden-
tiﬁed in the sintered samples. The matrix BCC phase in light color
had a chemical composition close to the nominal composition of
NbMoTaWVCr alloy. The grey regions distributed at the grain
boundaries were enriched in Cr, Nb, Ta and V, suggesting the

introduction of oxygen could be primarily attributed to the long-
term mechanical alloying process of the HEA powders.
Microstructure of the sintered HEA was found to gradually
change with the increasing sintering temperature. The volume

analysis. There were 24.0% precipitated phases distributed at the

sample sintered at 1400 /C14 C, as shown in Fig. 2a. When the sintering
temperature increased to 1500 /C14 C, fraction of Laves phase was
obviously reduced, and the volume fractions of Laves phases and

4.2. Strengthening mechanisms
As shown in Table 4, the NbMoTaWVCr HEA sintered at 1500 /C14 C
possessed excellent yield strength (3416 MPa) and Vickers hard-
ness (9908 MPa) which are quite higher than those of the cast
NbMoTaWV alloy (1246 MPa, 5250 MPa). Obviously, the bulk
NbMoTaWVCr HEA in the present work showed a microstructure
different from those HEAs prepared by arc-melting. Compared with
the coarse dendrite grains in cast HEAs, the NbMoTaWVCr HEA
fabricated by MA and SPS exhibited much ﬁner equiaxed grains
with the average grain size decreasing from several tens of microns
to 1.24 mm, which provided strong grain boundary strengthening
effect in the BCC matrix. Additionally, Cr has the smallest atom
radius among elements of the senary alloy, thus the dissolving of Cr
into the NbMoTaWV alloy is expected to enhance the atomic size
misﬁt and therefore increases the strength of BCC matrix.
Furthermore, the samples in the present work were processed by
powder metallurgy, oxygen was inevitably introduced into the
mechanically alloyed powders. Even most of O atoms were com-
bined with Ta and V, it was suggested that the rest of O contami-
nation was dissolved into the matrix and acted as interstitial solid
solution atoms. Interstitial elements such as O, C and N could
produce strong solid solution strengthening effect in refractory
HEA. It was previously reported that the strength of NbMoTaWV
HEA dramatically increased while the plasticity decreased simul-
taneously with the addition of interstitial C in BCC matrix [ 26].
Therefore, the interstitial solid solution strengthening from O could
also contribute to the improvement of strength in the sintered
NbMoTaWVCr HEA. Besides, the oxide particles and Laves phase
dispersed in the matrix acting as barriers of dislocation movements
might also increase the hardness and strength. These multiple
strengthening mechanisms are expected to be responsible for the
high strength of the HEA. The yield strength of sintered NbMo-

5. Conclusions
A high-strength equiatomic NbMoTaWVCr HEA was successfully
fabricated by MA and SPS in the present study. A single BCC su-
persaturated solid solution phase was formed in the ball milled
powders. After SPS, (Cr,V)
2(Ta,Nb) Laves phase and Ta2VO6 particles
were precipitated from the BCC matrix.
When the sintering temperature increased, the average grain
size of BCC matrix and particle size of precipitated phases gradually
increased, and the volume fraction of Laves phase obviously
decreased. The strength and hardness of the NbMoTaWVCr HEA
were found to be reduced with the coarsening of microstructure,
while the plasticity of the sintered alloy was mainly in ﬂuenced by
the volume fraction, particle size and distribution of Laves phase
and oxides.
The NbMoTaWVCr sample sintered at 1500 /C14 C exhibited a multi-
phase microstructure with 7.7% Laves phase and 6.2% Ta 2VO6 par-
ticles distributed in equiaxed BCC matrix with the average grain
size of 1.24 mm. The ﬁne grain size and homogeneous dispersion of
tiny particles enabled the sintered HEA to possess high strength
and fair plasticity at the same time. The yield strength ( sy), plastic
strain ( εp) and Vickers hardness (H v) of this HEA arrived at
3416 MPa, 5.3% and 9908 MPa, respectively. This ultra-high
strength and hardness dramatically exceeded other cast or sin-
tered refractory HEAs in the literature. The main strengthening
mechanisms in the sintered NbMoTaWVCr are interstitial solid
solution strengthening, grain boundary strengthening and inherent
solid solution strengthening.
