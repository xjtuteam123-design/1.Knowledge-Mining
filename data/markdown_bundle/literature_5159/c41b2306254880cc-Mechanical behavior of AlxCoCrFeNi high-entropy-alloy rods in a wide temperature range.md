1. Introduction
The pursuit of materials with outstanding mechanical properties for 
specialized applications, such as aerospace, naval vessels, and polar 
exploration equipment, continues to be a significant area of scientific 
and industrial interest [ 1 – 3 ]. High-entropy alloys (HEAs), composed of 
multiple principal elements in near-equiatomic proportions, have 
emerged as a revolutionary class of materials due to their exceptional 
mechanical properties and structural stability [ 4 – 9 ]. Among these al -
loys, the Al
x
CoCrFeNi (x < 0.5) system has garnered significant atten -
tion for its remarkable combination of strength, ductility, and resistance 
to extreme environments [ 10 – 13 ]. This system exemplifies a 
single-phase FCC structure [ 14 – 17 ], which is especially notable for its 
exceptional mechanical properties, including good tensile strength 
[ 18 – 21 ] and fracture toughness [ 22 – 26 ].
HEAs like Al
x
CoCrFeNi are characterized by a high concentration of 
multiple principal elements [ 27 – 29 ], leading to significant 
solid-solution strengthening and enhanced mechanical performance 
compared to traditional alloys [ 30 – 35 ]. The impressive tensile strength 
and ductility are primarily attributed to mechanisms, such as 
twinning-induced plasticity (TWIP) [ 36 – 38 ] and the influence of 
stacking fault energy (SFE) on dislocation behavior [ 39 – 41 ]. The acti -
vation of twinning requires substantial stress, and the unique properties 
of HEAs facilitate twinning deformation, thereby enhancing ductility 
and work-hardening capacity. Additionally, the SFE in HEAs plays a 
crucial role in determining the ease of dislocation movement and twin 
formation, further contributing to their superior mechanical properties 
[ 42 – 44 ]. Increasing the Al content alters the phase stability signifi -
cantly, driving the microstructure toward a BCC or duplex BCC-FCC 
structure [ 45 , 46 ]. In single-phase FCC Al ₓ CoCrFeNi (x < 0.5), DRX 
(dynamic recrystallization) is often governed by discontinuous DRX 
(DDRX), aided by low SFE and twinning-induced recrystallization. In 
duplex Al ₀ . ₆ CoCrFeNi, DDRX is prominent around BCC-FCC interfaces. 
While in Al ₀ . ₉ CoCrFeNi (BCC phase), continuous DRX (CDRX) occurs via 
progressive subgrain rotation. Such phase-dependent DRX mechanisms 
not only illustrate that FCC regions favor DDRX whereas BCC regions 
exhibit CDRX with mixed edge/screw dislocations, but also lead to 
heterogeneous strain partitioning [ 47 ].
Despite extensive research on bulk HEAs, a notable gap exists in 
understanding the microstructural evolution and mechanical properties 
of HEAs in a rod form, particularly with small diameters, such as 6 mm.

2. Experimental methods
2.1. Alloy preparation
Al ₓ CoCrFeNi (x = 0.1 and 0.3) HEAs were synthesized using a vac -
uum induction furnace with pressure of ~10 ⁻⁻ ³ Pa . High-purity argon 
was used as a protective atmosphere during melting to minimize 
oxidation and contamination. Elemental metals with purities exceeding 
99.9 wt % were accurately weighed according to the desired atomic 
ratios and thoroughly mixed to ensure homogeneity. Each alloy was 
remelted at least five times to enhance chemical uniformity. The as-cast 
ingots were then hot rotary forged at 1100 
◦
C into rods with a diameter 
of 6 mm ( Fig. 1 ) and subsequently air-cooled to room temperature. 
Sections of 200 mm length were cut from the central portion of the rods 
to avoid any end effects resulting from the forging process.
The surfaces were cleaned using aqua regia to remove surface oxides 
and any contaminants formed during processing. Subsequently, the 
specimens were ground with silicon-carbide papers up to 2000 grit to 
achieve a smooth surface and then electrolytically polished in a solution 
of 5 vol % perchloric acid and ethanol at room temperature with a direct 
current voltage of 29 V. This polishing method effectively removed any 
mechanically induced deformation layers, producing a mirror-like finish 
suitable for microstructural analysis.

3. Results
3.1. Microstructure of forged Al ₓ CoCrFeNi rods
The XRD patterns of the forged Al ₓ CoCrFeNi rods (x = 0.1 and 0.3) 
are presented in Fig. 3 . The diffraction patterns indicate that both alloys 
possess a single-phase FCC structure. No secondary phases or interme -
tallic compounds were detected. The peaks corresponding to the FCC 
phase are slightly shifted with increasing the Al content, suggesting 
lattice-parameter changes due to the substitutional addition of Al atoms.
The SEM images and EDS elemental maps of the forged rods are 
shown in Figs. 4 and 5 . The microstructures revealed homogeneous 
grains without noticeable segregation or secondary phases. EDS 
confirmed uniform elemental distributions of Co, Cr, Fe, Ni, and Al 
throughout the matrix. The measured compositions were close to 
nominal values, with slight deviations in the Al content, likely due to its 
lower melting point and potential loss during high-temperature pro -
cessing (see Table 1 ).

multiple slip-system activations, contributing to the ductility by

Fig. 7 reveals a significantly higher density of low-angle grain 
boundaries (LAGBs) in the Al ₀ . ₁ CoCrFeNi alloy compared to the 
Al ₀ . ₃ CoCrFeNi alloy. This disparity primarily stems from fundamental 
differences in SFE, deformation mechanisms, and DRX behavior be -
tween the two alloys.
The lower SFE of the Al ₀ . ₁ CoCrFeNi alloy facilitates dislocation pile- 
ups and inhibits dynamic recovery processes, thereby promoting the 
formation of LAGBs during thermomechanical processing. In contrast, 
the elevated SFE in the Al ₀ . ₃ CoCrFeNi alloy enhances dislocation cross- 
slip and promotes dynamic recovery, leading to dislocation rearrange -
ment and subsequent evolution of LAGBs into high-angle grain bound -
aries (HAGBs).
This microstructural divergence directly correlates with the alloys ’ 
mechanical performance. The Al ₀ . ₁ CoCrFeNi alloy exhibits superior 
ductility and strain hardening capacity, attributable to its high density of 
LAGBs that impede dislocation motion while accommodating plastic 
strain. Conversely, the Al ₀ . ₃ CoCrFeNi alloy demonstrates enhanced 
strength and thermal stability, resulting from its refined HAGB network 
that provides effective barriers to dislocation propagation through 
combined boundary strengthening and Hall-Petch mechanisms.

4.1. Fracture morphology
The fracture morphologies of Al

CoCrFeNi are 
similar. The SEM analysis of the Al

significant variations in fracture morphologies with increasing temper -

fracture morphology, where the pronounced necking is clearly observ -
able, indicating substantial localized deformation prior to fracture. At - 
50 
◦
C [ Fig. 11 (b)], the fracture surface exhibits relatively few and 
shallow dimples, suggesting limited ductility under low-temperature 
conditions. As the temperature rises to 25 
◦
C [ Fig. 11 (c)], an increase 
in both the number and uniform distribution of dimples is evident, 
reflecting enhanced ductility and better accommodation of plastic 
deformation at room temperature. Further elevation to 600 
◦
C [ Fig. 11
(d)] results in a noticeable augmentation in the dimple density and 
depth, corroborating improved elongation and mechanical performance 
at intermediate temperatures. At 800 
◦
C [ Fig. 11 (e)], the fracture sur -
face begins to show the presence of molten globules alongside the 
deepened and more abundant dimples, indicating the onset of partial 
melting and a transition towards a more ductile failure mode. This trend 
becomes even more pronounced at 1000 
◦
C [ Fig. 11 (f)], where extensive 
molten globules are present, and the dimple structure is markedly 
intensified, signifying maximal ductility. The correlation between the 
increasing dimple characteristics and the elongation behavior un -
derscores the plastic deformation with temperature. However, the 
emergence and growth of molten globules at elevated temperatures 
suggest a shift in the fracture mechanism, where thermal effects begin to 
dominate, potentially compromising the material ’ s structural integrity 
despite the increased ductility. The fracture morphology evolves from

5. Conclusion
This study characterized the microstructure and mechanical prop -
erties of 6-mm-diameter Al ₓ CoCrFeNi (x = 0.1 and 0.3) HEA rods across 
a temperature range from 50 
◦
C to 1000 
◦
C. Both alloy compositions 
exhibited a single-phase FCC structure with finely refined grains aver -
aging 4 – 5 μ m and prominent (001) and (111) fiber textures, which 
contributed to their enhanced tensile strength and ductility. Mechanical 
testing revealed that tensile strength decreased with increasing tem -
perature, while elongation improved, indicating a typical strength- 
ductility trade-off. Notably, the Al
0.3
CoCrFeNi alloy demonstrated su -
perior strength at lower temperatures, achieving a tensile strength of 
976 MPa at - 50 
◦
C, and maintained higher strength compared to 
Al
0.1
CoCrFeNi up to 600 
◦
C. However, both alloys experienced signifi -
cant softening beyond 800 
◦
C. Fractography confirmed a transition from 
limited ductility at low temperatures to extensive ductile deformation at 
high temperatures. When compared to other reported HEA rods, the 
Al ₓ CoCrFeNi rods showcased a favorable balance of strength and 
ductility, especially given their small diameter. These findings highlight 
the potential of Al ₓ CoCrFeNi HEAs for applications demanding high 
strength and reliable performance across a wide temperature range. 
Future work may focus on optimizing the aluminum content further and 
exploring long-term thermal stability to enhance their applicability in 
extreme environments.
