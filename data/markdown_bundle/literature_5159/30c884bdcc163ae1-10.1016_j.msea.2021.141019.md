ABSTRACT 
This paper investigated the effects of Gd and Y solutes on aging behaviour and corresponding mechanical 
properties of the extruded Mg – Gd(-Y)-Zn-Mn alloys at room and elevated temperatures. The results show that 
aging treatment provided significant improvement of ~100 MPa in strength by forming ellipsoidal β
′
nanophases 
in the as-extruded alloys. Partially substituting Y for Gd in the as-extruded Mg-Gd-Zn-Mn alloys can delay age- 
hardening response, but improve the strength increment after aging treatment. As the Y/Gd atomic ratio changed 
from 0 to 1, the Mg-1.75Gd-0.75Y-0.5Zn – Mn(at.%) alloy with a Y/Gd atomic ratio of 0.4 obtained the higher 
peak-hardness and mechanical properties. Enhanced age-hardening response and better mechanical properties 
were detected after separate additions of Y and Gd. The extruded-T5 Mg-2.5Gd-0.75Y-0.5Zn-0.3Mn alloy 
exhibited superior ultimate tensile strengths of 520 MPa at room temperature, 344 MPa at 250 
◦
C, and 225 MPa 
at 300 
◦
C. Fracture behaviours reveal that a change in predominant deformation mechanism from one based on 
dislocations to one mediated by grain boundary (GB) processes was found as the tensile temperatures arise from 
250 
◦
C to 300 
◦
C. The activation of GB sliding of the fine grains partially resulted in the decrease of tensile 
strength at 300 
◦
C. 
1. Introduction 
Magnesium alloys containing rare earth (RE) elements have received 
much attention due to their low density, high specific strength and 
stiffness [ 1 , 2 ]. Especially, in recent years wrought Mg-RE-Zn alloys 
containing long-period stacking ordered (LPSO) phase exhibit high 
mechanical properties at room temperature (RT), which are comparable 
to 2xxx and 7xxx aluminum alloys [ 3 – 5 ]. The lower density of Mg alloys 
makes it have large potential applications in the aerospace and rail 
transportation fields [ 6 ]. For their wider application demands of Mg 
alloys, many efforts are being made on developing high-temperature 
properties of wrought Mg-RE-Zn alloys. 
In general, micro alloying is the feasible and important approach to 
modify the high-temperature mechanical properties in Mg-RE system 
alloys. For example, the additions of Y have been suggested in the 
literature on Mg-Gd-Y-Zr alloys [ 7 ], and the results showed that 
increasing Y contents can significantly enhance the strength of the alloys 
at room and elevated temperatures. The adding of Y can refine grains 
and promote the precipitation of dense β
′
phases in the matrix, which 
would resist dislocations movement and subsequently improve the 
high-temperature stability of the microstructure. Effects of Gd contents 
on the peak-aged Mg-(3 – 12) Gd-3Y-0.5Zr (wt%) alloys were investi -
gated by Jafari Nodooshan et al. [ 8 ], and they attributed the superior 
high-temperature tensile strength to solution strengthening and pre -
cipitation hardness. After adding Zn solute to Mg – Gd(-Y) series alloys, 
LPSO phase, with higher elastic module and strength than the α -Mg 
matrix, would form in the alloys. However, regarding Mg-RE-Zn series 
alloys with LPSO phase, previous studies mainly focused on the influ -
ence of alloying on the mechanical properties at RT, and only a few 
studies have reported the high-temperature performance of Mg-RE-Zn 
alloys containing LPSO phase [ 9 – 15 ]. E.g. Wang et al. [ 16 ] studied the 
effects of Zn additions on the microstructure of hot extruded

2. Experimental procedure 
The as-extruded Mg-2.5Gd-0.5Zn-0.3Mn, Mg-1.75Gd-0.75Y-0.5Zn- 
0.3Mn, Mg-1.25Gd-1.25Y-0.5Zn-0.3Mn (at.%) alloys with different Y/ 
Gd atomic ratios were fabricated through semi-continuous casting, 
solid-solution treatment, and hot extrusion processes. The cast ingots 
were solution-treated at 763 K for 2 h, 788 K for 14 h and cooled in the 
air to RT [ 20 ]. Before hot extrusion, the heat-treated samples with 100 
mm in diameter and 120 mm in length were preheated at ~763 K for 1.5 
h for homogenizing the temperature of the samples. The extruded rods 
were produced with an extrusion ratio of 6.25, a mold temperature of 
593 K and an extrusion ram speed of 0.5 mm/s. Finally, the as-extruded 
Mg – Gd(-Y)-Zn-Mn alloys were aged at 200 
◦
C (T5 treatment). The 
studied alloys were defined as 2.5Gd, 1.75Gd + 0.75Y and 1.25Gd +
1.25Y, respectively ( Table 1 ). A 2.5Gd + 0.75Y alloy with a chemical 
component of Mg-2.5Gd-0.75Y-0.5Zn-0.3Mn (at.%) was prepared for 
studying the influence of separated additions of Y or Gd solutes. The 
actual compositions of the as-cast samples were measured using an 
inductively coupled plasma (ICP) analyzer, as shown in Table 1 . 
The tensile specimens have a gauge length of 15 mm, thickness 2

Z100 TEW test machine with a strain rate of 10

The microstructure of the samples was observed by a scanning

microscopy (TEM, JEOL-2100HC). The samples for SEM observation

alloy [ 21 ]. Before tensile, the tensile samples of the extruded-T5 alloys 
were polished and etched. Samples for TEM observation were prepared 
by mechanically polishing and precision ion milling technique (GATAN,

3. Results 
3.1. Microstructures and mechanical properties of the as-extruded and 
extruded-T5 Mg – Gd(-Y)-Zn-Mn alloys 
Fig. 1 shows the SEM micrographs of the as-extruded Mg – Gd(-Y)-Zn- 
Mn alloys. Bimodal grain structures with coarse deformed grains and 
fine recrystallized (DRXed) grains were observed in the four as-extruded 
alloys. As reported in our previous study [ 22 ], the coarse deformed 
grains usually have strong basal textures and the DRXed grains exhibited 
random textures in the as-extruded Mg-RE alloys. The block-shaped 
phase with a white contrast is LPSO phase [ 16 ], which was arranged 
along the extrusion direction. Inside the matrix of the deformed grains, 
lamellar structures are also observed. Those lamellar structures consist 
of LPSO structure and RE/Zn segregated stacking faults (SFs) as reported 
in Ref. [ 23 ]. The morphology and corresponding selected area electron 
diffraction (SAED) patterns of the RE/Zn segregated SFs and LPSO phase 
are shown in Fig. 2 a-d. 
The DRXed fraction, unDRXed fraction and the fraction of block- 
shaped LPSO phase in the as-extruded alloys were summarized in 
Table 2 . The DRXed fraction of the as-extruded alloys increased by 
decreasing Y/Gd atomic ratios or adding 0.75 at.%Y in the 2.5Gd alloy. 
The average DRXed grain sizes in the as-extruded 2.5Gd, 1.75Gd +
0.75Y, 1.25Gd + 1.25Y and 2.5Gd + 0.75Y alloys were about 3.3 μ m, 
2.0 μ m, 2.2 μ m and 2.7 μ m, respectively. The result indicated that 
mutual additions of Gd and Y can decrease the DRXed grain size com -
pares with the alloys only containing Gd atoms. The fraction of LPSO 
phase was also calculated based on their obvious contrast compares to 
Mg matrix, and the result showed that both Y additions and the increase 
in Y/Gd atomic ratios would increase the fraction of LPSO phase. 
As shown in the insert of Fig. 1 , the DRXed grains were surrounded 
by fine white particles (as marked by red arrows) in the as-extruded 
2.5Gd and 2.5Gd + 0.75Y alloys. According to the SAED and EDS re -
sults in Fig. 2 e and f, the nanoparticles are considered to be β -Mg
5
RE 
phase (fcc, a = 2.23 nm [ 22 ]). These nano β phases are attributed to 
dynamical precipitation during hot extrusion. Compared with the 
as-extruded 2.5Gd (or 1.75Gd + 0.75Y) alloy, the amount of the nano -
particles increased in the as-extruded 2.5Gd + 0.75Y alloy, showing that 
RE (Y or Gd) addition can cause the formation of nano β particles. 
However, unlike the 2.5Gd alloy, those fine particles were not visible in 
the as-extruded 1.75Gd + 0.7Y and 1.25Gd + 1.25Y alloys. This in -
dicates that Gd element is more effective than the Y element in the 
formation of dynamic precipitation. Partially replacing Gd with Y 
(increasing Y/Gd atomic ratio) would impede the formation of β phase.

4. Discussion 
4.1. Alloying effects on age-hardening response and aging strengthening 
This work investigated the separate and joint additions of Gd and Y 
on the age-hardening response and mechanical properties of Mg – Gd(-Y)- 
Zn-Mn alloys at RT, 250 
◦
C and 300 
◦
C. Separate additions of Gd or Y 
solutes can enhance the age-hardening response in the as-extruded 
Mg – Gd(-Y)-Zn-Mn alloys, which is in good agreement with the 
solution-treated Mg-10Gd-xY-0.4Zr(x = 1,3,5 wt%) alloys [ 7 ]. The high 
RE solute concentrations in the matrix of the as-extruded 2.5Gd + 0.75Y 
alloys contributed to the high aging precipitation kinetic. Increasing 
Y/Gd atomic ratios slowed down the ageing precipitation. The differ -
ences in age-hardening response caused by replacing Gd with Y in the 
Mg-Gd-Y-Zn-Mn alloys may be attributed to the following reasons: (1) 
Since Gd and Y have similar atomic sizes (180.4 p.m. for Gd and 180 p. 
m. for Y), Y can easily substitute Gd to form the nano-precipitations 
during aging treatment. But the formation energy of precipitates is 
different. Issa et al. [ 32 ] assessed the formation energy of β
′′
and β
′
precipitates in the binary Mg – Gd/Mg – Y alloys by using first-principles 
calculations. They found that the formation energies of the β

5. Conclusions 
In the present work, four Mg – Gd(-Y)-Zn-Mn alloys with different Gd 
and Y contents, namely Mg-2.5Gd-0.5Zn-0.3Mn, Mg-1.75Gd-0.75Y- 
0.5Zn-0.3Mn, Mg-1.25Gd-1.25Y-0.5Zn-0.3Mn, Mg-2.5Gd-0.75Y-0.5Zn- 
0.3Mn(at.%) were prepared by hot extrusion and aging treatment. The 
age-hardening response and mechanical properties of the extruded-T5 
Mg – Gd(-Y)-Zn-Mn alloys at room and elevated temperatures were 
investigated. Some conclusions can be drawn as follows: 
(1) Separate additions of Gd or Y enhanced the age-hardening re -
sponses for the Mg – Gd(-Y)-Zn-Mn alloys. Fixing the G + Y atomic 
content (2.5 at.%), partially substituting Gd with Y slowed down 
the age-hardening responses in the as-extruded alloys. 
(2) As the Y/Gd atomic ratio increases from 0 to 1, Y presented better 
aging strengthening efficiency than Gd for the as-extruded Mg- 
Gd-Y-Zn-Mn series alloys. The strength was further improved by 
adding 0.75 at.% Gd or Y. The peak-aged Mg-2.5Gd-0.75Y-0.5Zn- 
0.3Mn alloy obtained maximum ultimate tensile strength of 520 
MPa at RT, 344 MPa at 250 
◦
C, and 225 MPa at 300 
◦
C. 
(3) In the peak-aged Mg – Gd(-Y)-Zn-Mn alloys with bimodal grain 
structure, Gd and Y elements play a positive role in improvement 
of mechanical properties of the wrought alloys at high tempera -
tures. Dislocation slips and fine-grain boundary sliding are 
dominant at 250 
◦
C- and 300 
◦
C-tensile. 
(4) After the tensile temperatures increased from 
RT~250 
◦
C – 300 
◦
C, dramatically drops in strength was noticed in 
the extruded-T5 Mg – Gd(-Y)-Zn-Mn alloys. Fine-grain of the Mg- 
RE alloys is considered to the key factor that the studied alloys 
no longer have the advantage of strength than that of T6-treated 
Mg-RE alloys.
