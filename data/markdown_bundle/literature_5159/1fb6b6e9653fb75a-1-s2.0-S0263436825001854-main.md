ABSTRACT
Niobium alloy has attracted widespread attention in the aerospace industry due to its high strength, excellent 
weldability and formability. It is capable of producing structural thin plates and complex shaped parts by laser 
directed energy deposition (LDED). In this work, single-track Nb-37Ti-5Al samples were fabricated by LDED to 
optimize of processing parameters, then multi-track Nb-37Ti-5Al was prepared. The as cast Nb-37Ti-5Al was 
prepared to compare its mechanical properties with LDEDed alloy, the effect of oxygen content on the fracture 
behavior was revealed. LDEDed alloy has high strength and low ductility, which is mainly due to the high oxygen 
content in powder feedstocks. The stability, elastic properties, electronic structure of pure niobium (Nb), niobium 
with dissolved oxygen (Nb(O)), Nb-37Ti-5Al and Nb-37Ti-5Al with dissolved oxygen (Nb-37Ti-5Al(O)) were 
investigated using first principles calculation. Interstitial oxygen atom causes electron rearrangement of niobium, 
titanium and aluminum, which promotes the formation of ionic bonds and covalency bonds. This work provides 
new insight regarding the correlation between the interaction of oxygen and metallic elements at atomic-level 
and mechanical behavior.
1. Introduction
Niobium alloys possess low density, high specific strength at elevated 
temperatures, excellent weldability and formability. They can be made 
into thin plates and complex parts, which could be used as thrust 
augmenter flaps in jet engines and rocket nozzles [ 1 , 2 ]. Among various 
niobium alloys, low-density Nb-Ti-Al alloys could achieve the balance of 
strength and plasticity through compositional design, moreover they 
could meet the strict temperature and density requirements of aerospace 
industry [ 3 – 7 ]. Bai et al. successfully developed low-density Nb-35Ti- 
5Al-5V-0.7Zr. Vanadium and zirconium are added for solid solution 
strengthening, and a small amount of carbon is added for precipitation 
strengthening. The density of this alloy is lower than 6.0 g/cm
3
, the 
tensile strength at room temperature is 1020 MPa, the elongation is 
greater than 17 % [ 8 ].
Laser additive manufacturing is a feasible and attractive technique 
for refractory metals and alloys, and very high temperatures can be 
reached in the molten pool [ 9 ]. Awasthi et al. successfully fabricated 
crack-free C103 alloy with a relative density greater than 99 % via laser 
powder bed fusion (LPBF) [ 10 ]. Its tensile strength is 595 MPa at room 
temperature, exceeding the 481 MPa of forged part. This is due to the 
formation of high dislocation density and dislocation cell structure 
caused by the rapid cooling rate in LPBF process. Guo et al. fabricated 
continuous single track Nb-37Ti-13Cr-2Al-1Si (at.%) samples by LPBF. 
Both the width of the single track sample and its penetration depth into 
the substrate increase with the linear laser beam energy density [ 11 ]. 
Chen et al. fabricated the Nb521(Nb-5W-2Mo-1Zr) by LPBF, and hot 
isostatic pressing (HIP) was conducted to improve densification. Nano -
scale ZrO
2 
precipitates are found in the LPBFed parts and those after 
HIP. ZrO
2 
precipitates enable the dislocation pinning and TRIP effect 
during tensile deformation. The LPBFed sample after HIP achieves 
promising ultimate strength of 678.7 ± 1.1 MPa and fracture elongation 
of 5.91 ± 0.32 % [ 12 ]. For niobium alloys prepared by laser additive 
manufacturing, the microstructure can be controlled by adjusting pro -
cess parameters such as laser power and scanning speed. However, 
systematic research on achieving desirable equiaxed grains, refining 
grain size, and optimizing the balance between equiaxed and columnar 
grains remains limited. Additionally, powder characteristics 
(morphology, size, purity) critically affect formability and defect for -
mation. The effect of oxygen impurity in powder feedstocks on the 
performance of niobium alloys prepared by laser additive 
manufacturing has received little attention. Related studies have shown

2. Experiment and computational procedures
2.1. Experimental procedure
According to Nb-Ti-Al ternary phase diagram, single phase Nb-37Ti- 
5Al was designed. Unless otherwise stated, all elemental compositions 
refer to atomic percentage. For single track and overlapped samples, 
nonspherical niobium powders (75 μ m – 150 μ m, 0.23 wt% oxygen), 
nonspherical titanium powders (75 μ m – 150 μ m, 0.30 wt% oxygen) and 
nonspherical aluminum powders (75 μ m – 150 μ m) were chosen. They 
were thoroughly mixed, and a thick Ti-6Al-4V plate was used as the 
substrate. For Single track decomposition was performed using laser 
powers of 1 kW, 1.5 kW and 1.8 kW and scanning speeds of 3 mm/s, 5 
mm/s, 8 mm/s and 10 mm/s. The laser spot diameter was fixed at 2.5 
mm. The LDED process was carried out in an inert atmosphere protective 
chamber filled with high-purity argon, which was also used as powder- 
carrying gas during the LDED process to avoid oxidation. Fig. 1 (a) shows 
LDEDed single-track samples. For multi-track alloy, laser power was 
increased to 1.9 kW to avoid the unmelt niobium powders observed in 
single track samples. The scanning speed was selected as 3 mm/s. The 
overlapping ratio of 40 % was adopted, and thus multi-track alloy was 
fabricated, as shown in Fig. 1 (b). The LDEDed samples were cut by wire 
electrical discharge machining, and their microstructural characteriza -
tion were performed along the laser scanning direction.
Niobium buttons, titanium sponges and aluminum buttons with the 
purity of 99.99 wt% were melted in a vacuum induction furnace. The 
maximum power in the melting process was 120 kW, and as-cast ingot of 
1.25 kg was finally obtained by 5 times melting, as shown in Fig. 1 (c).

structure method in Alloy Theory Automated Toolkit(ATAT) software.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Optimization of processing parameters for LDEDed Nb-37Ti-5Al
When laser powder is 1 kW and 1.5 kW, the scanning speed is 3 mm/ 
s, the surfaces of these samples appear rough. While the samples become 
smooth and compact when laser powder is increased to 1.8 kW, even at 
the scanning speeds of 3 mm/s, 5 mm/s, 8 mm/s and 10 mm/s. More -
over, no obvious cracks and pores are observed, as shown in Fig. 1 (a). 
When the laser power was 1.8 kW, both the width of the sample and the 
forming efficiency gradually decrease as the scanning speed increases 
from 3 mm/s to 10 mm/s.
Fig. 3 shows the morphology of the samples with the laser power of 
1.8 kW and different scanning speeds. It can be seen that some black 
holes appeared, which are formed during the laser deposition process. 
When the scanning speed increased, some unmelted niobium powders 
appeared as white spherical objects. Under the same laser power, larger 
scanning speed produces less laser energy and lower temperature in the 
molten pool, so unmelted niobium powders appear. In LDED processes, 
each new layer is melted and fused onto the previously solidified layer, 
so each layer has a unique microstructure. In order to determine the 
influence of process parameters on the forming efficiency, OM images 
were used to measure average height of the deposition layer, vernier 
caliper was used to measure average width of the sample. The corre -
sponding results were collected in Fig. 4 . When the scanning rate is 3 
mm/s, the height of the deposited layer increases from 282 μ m to 538 
μ m along with laser power increases from 1 kW, 1.5 kW to 1.8 kW, and 
the width increases from 4.20 mm to 6.23 mm. It can be seen that the 
increase of laser power is beneficial to improving deposition efficiency. 
When laser power is 1.8 kW, the average height decreases from 538 μ m 
to 150 μ m along with the scanning speed increases from 3 mm/s to 10 
mm/s, and the width decreases from 6.23 mm to 4.54 mm. This in -
dicates that the increase in scanning speed would lead the decrease of 
single-layer deposition efficiency. In LDED process, the height of the 
cladding layer is crucial for the vertical manufacturing accuracy. 
Therefore, the scanning speed must be limited to a certain range so that 
the height of each cladding layer can match the lifting height.

significantly improving strength and uniform deformation. Moreover, 
the cellular structure facilitates the redistribution of dislocations during 
later deformation to avoid strain concentrations within the grains.

3.3. Mechanical properties of LDEDed and as-cast Nb-37Ti-5Al
3.3.1. Tensile behavior and strength comparisons
To clarify the effect of oxygen content on the LDED alloy and as-cast 
alloy, their mechanical properties tests were compared. Fig. 11 shows 
their room temperature tensile curves. The UTS value, YS value and El 
value of LDEDed Nb-37Ti-5Al are 842 MPa, 586 MPa and 2.55 %, 
respectively. While those of as-cast alloy are 599 MPa, 580 MPa and 
8.34 %, respectively. The LDED sample exhibits higher tensile strength 
but lower elongation than the as-cast alloy.
Fig. 11 shows the fracture morphology of LDEDed multi-track Nb- 
37Ti-5Al and as-cast Nb-37Ti-5Al. For LDEDed alloy, it is shown that the 
tensile fracture is typical brittle intergranular fracture, where the grain 
boundary strength is less than the yield strength, and the grain boundary 
has no plastic deformation. The intergranular fracture process includes 
the formation and propagation of cracks. When the material is deformed 
by force, the dislocation movement inside the grain is blocked by the 
grain boundary, resulting in stress concentration. When the stress con -
centration reaches the grain boundary strength, the grain boundary will 
crack. For as-cast Nb-37Ti-5Al, there are obvious dimples and some 
micropores in Fig. 12 (d), which is a typical ductile fracture. Under 
external force, some tiny holes are generated in the region with apparent 
deformation due to the accumulation of strong slip dislocations. Under 
the action of load, the holes continue to grow as dimples, accumulate 
and form cracks until they are finally separated, and their morphology is 
mainly affected by the stress state.
3.3.2. Impact of microstructure on mechanical properties
The key factors affecting the strength and fracture mechanism of Nb 
alloys include grain size, grain morphology, grain orientation, second 
phase and other factors. For LDED Nb alloy, it is composed of equiaxed 
grains and columnar grains, the average size is about several micro -
meters. For as cast Nb alloy, it is mainly composed of equiaxed grains, 
and some grains have the size of a few millimeters. According to Hall- 
Petch equation, LDED Nb alloy should has good strength and ductility, 
while it has brittle fracture mode and poor ductility. The presence of 
some columnar crystals in the alloy and high oxygen content from 
powder feedstocks have led to this result. Columnar crystals could be 
reduced through the optimizing processing parameters and promoting 
CET transition. Additionally, oxygen is easy to enter niobium alloy as 
impurity. This will reduce the ductility and fracture toughness of 
niobium alloy. The study has found that dramatically hardening and 
embrittlement can be caused by only 1 at.% oxygen [ 26 ]. Niobium with 
solute oxygen exhibits typical transgranular quasi-cleavage fracture 
morphology, which consists of deformation induced ridges, localized 
deformation band and deformation localization induced microcracks. In 
Klaus K. Schulze ’ s article, it is stated that the concentration of dissolved 
oxygen in niobium reach value of 3 at.% between 800 and 1800 
◦
C [ 27 ]. 
This solid solubility of oxygen is higher than the oxygen content in these 
two alloys, which indicates that the formation of metal oxides (NbO and 
NbO) has not yet formed.

3.4.1. Lattice distortion and elastic properties
For Nb, Nb(O), Nb-37Ti-5Al and Nb-37Ti-5Al(O), average valence 
electron concentration (VEC), atomic size difference ( δ ) and electro -
negativity difference ( Δ χ ) were calculated, as listed in Table 1 . VEC can 
be used to predict the phase structure of the alloy. When VEC > 8.0, the 
alloy is easy to form single FCC phase. When VEC < 6.87, the alloy is 
easy to form single BCC phase. When VEC is greater than 6.87 and less 
than 8, the alloy is generally composed of BCC and FCC [ 28 ]. Their VEC 
values are all less than 6.87, which indicated that they were all 
composed of the single BCC phase. δ reflects the degree of atomic misfit 
and lattice distortion, and it can be used to predict the solid solution 
stability of the alloy. When δ < 6.6 %, the BCC single-phase solid so -
lution structure is formed. The δ values of Nb(O), Nb-37Ti-5Al and Nb- 
37Ti-5Al(O) are all less than 6.6 %. The δ value of Nb-37Ti-5Al(O) is 
larger than that of Nb-37Ti-5Al, which indicates that there are signifi -
cant atomic misfit and lattice distortion. Electronegativity refers to an 
atom ’ s ability to attract and lose electrons. The higher the electroneg -
ativity of an element, the stronger it attracts electrons. Conversely, lower 
electronegativity is associated with greater plasticity and metallic 
property. The Δ χ values of Nb(O) and Nb-37Ti-5Al(O) are high, and this 
indicated that interstitial oxygen reduces the plasticity of the alloy. 
Nelson et al. found the VEC of Nb-24Ti-18Si-5Hf-5Al is reduced due to 
aluminum addition. Compared with Nb-18Si-5Hf-5Al, it is also found 
that the VEC of Nb-24Ti-18Si-5Hf-5Al is decreased, and δ and Δ χ are 
increased [ 29 ]. This is consistent with the calculated results of the 
decrease of VEC and the increase of δ and Δ χ due to titanium and 
aluminum addition.

4. Conclusion
In the present work, Nb-37Ti-5Al was successfully fabricated by laser 
directed energy deposition (LDED), and the microstructure and me -
chanical properties of the LDEDed Nb-37Ti-5Al were compared with as- 
cast Nb-37Ti-5Al. The stability, elastic properties, electronic structure of 
pure Nb, Nb(O), Nb-37Ti-5Al, and Nb-37Ti-5Al(O) were investigated 
using first principles calculation method. 
(1) Each cladding layer of LDEDed Nb-37Ti-5Al is composed of 
columnar grains at the bottom and equiaxed grains at the top. 
Obvious crystal texture in the region of columnar grains is 
observed. As-cast Nb-37Ti-5Al is composed of larger equiaxed 
grains. The UTS value, YS value and El value of LDEDed Nb-37Ti- 
5Al are 842 MPa, 586 MPa and 2.55 %, respectively. While those 
of as-cast alloy are 599 MPa, 580 MPa and 8.34 %, respectively. 
This difference is mainly caused by high oxygen from powder 
feedstocks.
(2) The results of VEC, δ and Δ χ show that both Nb-37Ti-5Al and Nb- 
37Ti-5Al(O) have single BCC phase, which are consistent with 
XRD results. After the addition of oxygen, the lattice parameter 
increases from 0.3276 nm of Nb-37Ti-5Al to 0.3288 nm of Nb- 
37Ti-5Al(O). Compared with Nb-37Ti-5Al, E and G of Nb-37Ti- 
5Al(O) are increased, indicating that the interstitial oxygen 
atom improves the strength of the alloy. Meanwhile, the decrease 
of B/G and ν indicates that the plasticity of the alloy has 
decreased.
(3) The electronic structure analysis explains the above changes in 
elastic properties and mechanical properties. Interstitial oxygen 
atom causes electron rearrangement of niobium, titanium and 
aluminum. For Nb-37Ti-5Al(O), both DOS and CDD results show 
that the covalency between oxygen and niobium/titanium 
formed, which improves the alloy strength. The electrons gather 
around oxygen atoms, which reduce the metallic bond in the 
matrix and the alloy plasticity.
