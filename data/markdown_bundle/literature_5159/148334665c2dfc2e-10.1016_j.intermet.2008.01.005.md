Abstract
An enhancement of plasticity in high strength ultraﬁne eutectic Fe eNb composites has been accomplished by introducing heterogeneities in
length-scale of the microstructure. Addition of Al acting as a stabilizer for the a-Fe phase in the Fe eNb composites results in the formation of
a larger volume fraction of primary a-Fe phase and an ultraﬁne eutectic structure with reduced lamellar spacing. As a result, Fe eNbeAl com-
posites exhibit a good combination of fracture strength and plasticity. Due to the presence of the solution hardened a-Fe phase, the Fe eNbeAl
composites exhibit signiﬁcantly enhanced work hardening behavior at room temperature.

1. Introduction
Nano- (or ultraﬁne) grained materials or bulk metallic
glasses exhibit a remarkable improvement in strength when
compared with conventional coarse-grained alloys [1e10].
However, these materials generally suffer from insufﬁcient
plastic strain and reduced toughness during deformation. Since
the deformation proceeds without effectively activating dislo-
cations, the applied load is concentrated in a few number of
shear bands which leads to high strength, but eventually results
in catastrophic failure [11e15]. Moreover, most nanostruc-
tured materials do not exhibit clear work hardening behavior
due to operation of shear bands during deformation [16e19].
Besides nano-grained materials, bulk metallic glasses or
bulk metallic glass matrix composites, ultraﬁne eutectic
structured materials can give similar properties when a proper
selection of composition combined with a controlled solidiﬁ-
cation process is made [20e33]. Recently, multicomponent
Ti- [34,35] and Zr- [36,37] alloys have been developed that ex-
hibit a higher level of strength and plasticity (compressive
fracture strength: w1.75 GPa, compressive plastic strain:
w10.3%) by introducing a micrometer-scale primary phase
in an ultraﬁne (or nanometer) scale eutectic matrix. Even bi-
nary eutectic Ti eFe has been reported to show high strength
and plasticity (compressive fracture strength: w2.2 GPa, com-
pressive plastic strain: w6.7%) [38]. More recently, it has
been shown that in binary eutectic Fe eZr a good combination
of properties (compressive fracture strength: w1.8 GPa, com-
pressive plastic strain: w12%) can be obtained [39]. In partic-
ular, enhanced mechanical properties have been obtained
when the propagation of shear bands is limited in the area be-
tween the coarse primary dendrites embedded in the ﬁne-scale
eutectic matrix, preventing catastrophic failure [40e42]. Dis-
locations are generated inside the coarse primary dendrites

In this study, we report on in situ formation of primary den-

binary Fe eNb eutectic alloys. In order to improve the work
hardening response, i.e. to control the volume fraction of the
primary a-Fe phase, Al is added as an a-Fe stabilizer. This sig-
niﬁcantly enhances the mechanical properties of the Fe eNbe
Al composites compared to binary Fe eNb.
2. Experimental
The alloys were prepared by arc melting high purity ele-
mental constituents under an Ar atmosphere. Each master
alloy ingot was remelted at least three times to ensure compo-
sitional homogeneity. Bulk samples were produced by suction
casting into a cylindrical cavity of 2 mm diameter and 50 mm
length or a rectangular cavity of 3 mm /C2 3 mm width and
50 mm length. Microstructural analysis of the samples was
done by optical and scanning electron microscopy (SEM,
Hitachi, S-2700). Phase analysis was performed using an
energy dispersive X-ray spectrometer (EDS, Kevex superdry)
attached to the SEM. X-ray diffraction (XRD; Rigaku
CN2301, monochromatic Cu K a radiation) was employed
for structural characterization. Mechanical testing was done
by compression tests carried out in an Instron-type machine
at a strain rate of 1 /C2 10
/C0 4 s/C0 1 at room temperature. Vickers
hardness was measured with a microhardness tester with
a load of 50 gf.

pressing the grain growth, thus resulting in an improvement
of the strength. The reduction of the eutectic lamellar spacing

high level strength as for polycrystalline material with nanome-
ter-scale grain size or bulk metallic glass [48]. In the present
investigation, in situ formed bulk ultraﬁne eutectic Fe eNbe
(Al) alloys have been produced through suction casting at a
relatively higher cooling rate ( w10
2 K/s) than conventional
casting techniques (10 0e101 K/s). As revealed from the
detailed microstructural investigations, the average lamellar
spacing in the present eutectic structures decreases down to

ductile dendrites plastically deform with formation of slip

develops at the dendrite eultraﬁne eutectic matrix interface

through the ultraﬁne eutectic matrix until they are obstructed
by the dendrites. The ductile dendrite phase can serve as
a site for initiating shear bands as well as it can retard or hin-
der the propagation of shear bands. With further increasing
strain, multiple shear bands are developed to accommodate
the strain through the whole sample volume, i.e. primary shear
bands are branched and/or secondary shear bands are nucle-
ated by interaction of the coarse dendrites and the ﬁne-scale
eutectic. Accordingly, the distinct length-scale inhomogeneity
in the dendrite eultraﬁne eutectic composites obviously con-

5. Conclusions
In situ bulk ultraﬁne eutectic Fe eNbe(Al) composites
were successfully synthesized using suction casting. The
eutectic alloy Fe
90Nb10 composed of alternating ﬁne lamellar
layers shows high yield ( sy ¼ 1.1 GPa) and fracture strength
(smax ¼ 1.8 GPa), but limited plastic strain. In contrast, eutec-
tic composites with micrometer-scale primary dendritic phase
(a-Fe or Fe
2Nb) in an ultraﬁne-scale eutectic matrix exhibit
high fracture strength (1.4 e1.8 GPa) as well as reasonably
good plasticity (6.5 e13%). Consequently, composite micro-
structures containing coarse dendritic phases are suitable to
enhance the plasticity of the high strength nanostructured
alloys.
The addition of Al in the Fe e10Nb eutectic alloy improves
the mechanical properties, i.e. fracture strength and plastic
strain of 2.2 GPa and 12%, respectively, can be reached in
Fee60Nbe40Al. The enhanced properties are mainly attrib-
uted to: (1) presence of a large volume fraction of a solid
solution hardened primary phase [ a-Fe(Al)] and (2) reduction
of the interlamellar spacing. The supersaturated a-Fe(Al)
phase allows for signiﬁcantly enhanced strain hardening
behavior. The present study shows that the synthesis of
nano/ultraﬁne eutectic composites containing phase constitu-
ents with different length-scales can yield a simultaneous
enhancement of strength and plasticity by controlling the plas-
