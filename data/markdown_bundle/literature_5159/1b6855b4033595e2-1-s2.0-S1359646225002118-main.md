ABSTRACT
Body-centered-cubic (BCC)-base superalloys primarily containing refractory elements have great potential as one 
kind of ‘beyond Ni-base superalloy ’ materials, owing to their increased melting points and analogous coherent 
microstructures composed of BCC solid solution and its ordered B2 or L2
1 
phases. The mechanical behavior of 
BCC superalloys (including ferritic and refractory superalloys) is a key topic that has been progressively inves -
tigated. However, major challenges remain in terms of room-temperature brittleness and high-temperature creep 
resistance, as well as mechanical property degradation induced by the coarsening of BCC-B2/L2
1 
coherent 
microstructure or/and brittle phase precipitation. This viewpoint paper concentrates on the capability and issues 
regarding the mechanical behavior (strength, ductility, and creep) of BCC superalloys across diverse systems, in 
which the crucial influence factors are highlighted. Then the ductility challenges of these high-strength super -
alloys will be discussed. And possible approaches to improve mechanical properties will also be explored for 
developing novel BCC superalloys.
Introduction
The exceptional mechanical properties of superalloys for high- 
temperature (HT) applications are primarily attributed to their specific 
coherent microstructure consisting of ordered nm-scale precipitates 
within a disordered solid-solution matrix, as exemplified by the widely- 
used Ni-base superalloys in aerospace and energy generation [ 1 – 4 ]. The 
coherent precipitation of ordered B2 phase (NiAl-type) into disordered 
body-centered-cubic (BCC) solid solution has given rise to ferritic su -
peralloys in Fe-Cr-Ni-Al-based systems, which have been broadly 
applied in HT oxidation environments, such as heating furnaces, nuclear 
reactors, petroleum refinery and automotive exhaust systems [ 5 – 9 ]. 
However, it is more challenging to further elevate their service tem -
perature due to the modest melting point ( < 1811 K) of the base ele -
ments of Ni and Fe. By contrast, several BCC alloys primarily consisting 
of early transition metals (ETMs = Cr, Mo, W, V, Nb, Ta, etc.) have great 
potential to be applied in higher temperature environments, as exam -
pled by W-, Nb-, Ta-, and Mo-base refractory alloys due to their much 
higher melting points of base elements ( > 2700 K) [ 10 ]. Unfortunately, 
it is tricky to simultaneously realize high strength and sufficient ductility 
over a broad temperature range in these refractory alloys, because the 
second-precipitated phases are generally incoherent with the BCC ma -
trix, e.g., the hexagonal Nb

BCC-base superalloys containing Al and ETMs to form BCC-B2 coherent

raises the concept of refractory superalloys (RSAs) [ 13 – 24 ]. Besides, 
there also exist several simple RSAs constituted of ETMs and late tran -

Creep behavior of BCC-superalloys
The prominent creep resistance is dependent on the high thermal 
stability of coherent microstructure and high strength at HTs. Generally, 
a large lattice misfit between coherent phases produces a high yield 
strength, but accelerates the coarsening of coherent precipitates, leading 
to a reduction in microstructural stability [ 41 , 61 ]. Thus, an appropriate 
lattice misfit and particle size, as well as a high-volume fraction of 
precipitates are necessary to guarantee the creep resistance of superal -
loys [ 40 , 51 , 58 , 63 ].

Summary and outlook
To summarize, the formation of BCC-B2/L2
1 
coherent microstruc -
ture and its thermal stability are of great importance to the mechanical 
properties of BCC superalloys at both RT and HTs. The added amount of 
B2/L2
1
-forming elements and their partitioning between coherent 
phases determine the lattice misfit δ between them, as well as the size 
and volume fraction of precipitates, which greatly affects the mechani -
cal strength of superalloys. Moreover, the grain size of matrix, the par -
ticle size, and amount of precipitates are also crucial to the RT ductility 
and HT creep resistance of BCC superalloys. Their relationship between 
the coherent microstructure and mechanical properties is still chal -
lenging and needs to be further clarified. In addition, the thermal sta -
bility of coherent microstructure is also key against the coarsening of 
coherent precipitates, the destabilization of the matrix and coherent 
phases, as well as the precipitation of brittle compounds during the 
aging at HTs, which leads to a degradation in mechanical properties. 
Moreover, the kinetic diffusion of elements also affects the partition in 
coherent phases and then the lattice misfit, where the presence of 
chemical short-range ordering among constituted elements due to strong 
interactions may effectively retard elemental diffusion (i.e., the sluggish 
effect [ 76 ]), slowing down the coarsening of coherent precipitates and 
improving the microstructural stability at HTs. These factors are closely 
related to the chemical compositions of superalloys. Thus, a careful 
composition design and predictive approach (e.g., Integrated Compu -
tational Materials Engineering (ICME) [ 90 , 101 – 103 ]) should be

Most BCC superalloys, especially the RSAs, are brittle at RT due to 
the limited slip systems for dislocation motion, which need to be 
improved. Traditional ways to refine the matrix grains and to enhance 
the grain-boundary strength by adding a minor amount of elements (like 
C, B) are still effective. Besides, in light of the interaction of local 
chemical orders (LCOs) with dislocations [ 88 , 90 ], we can deliberately 
tailor alloy composition not only to enhance the LCOs in disordered BCC 
solid solution for the generation of more planar-slip bands, but also to 
reduce the LCOs of B2 compound for an easier dislocation glide. 
Meanwhile, the coherent interfaces between BCC-B2 will be soft, thus, 
dislocations can cut through the precipitates to realize a uniform 
deformation in coherent microstructure. Actually, a lower APB energy 
can facilitate the dislocations cutting through hard particles more easily 
under the condition of an applied stress, thereby improving the defor -
mation compatibility of coherent phases [ 88 ]. And a lower stacking fault 
energy is conducive to the formation of twins, resulting in the 
twinning-induced-plasticity (TWIP) [ 104 , 105 ]. Such case is expected to 
appear in BCC-base superalloys containing Ti and Zr elements, since the 
TWIP effect always exists in single-phase BCC TiZr-base alloys [ 106 ]. 
The formation of anti-phase domains and twins is closely related to the 
local atomic distribution of constituted elements. An intentional regu -
lation of local atomic distributions, such as elemental segregation to 
slightly weaken the LCO, would be desired to produce anti-phase do -
mains and twins for improving ductility of BCC superalloys.
