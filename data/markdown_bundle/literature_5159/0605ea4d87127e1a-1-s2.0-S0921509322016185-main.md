ABSTRACT 
The compressive properties of sintered tungsten alloys with different m -ZrO
2 
weight percentage (0, 0.5, 1.0%) 
were tested at room temperature and high temperature, and the hot deformation behaviors of the three metals 
were investigated under different temperature (1300 – 1600 
◦
C) and strain rate (0.005 – 1 s
 1
). The room tem -
perature strengthening model of zirconia particle reinforced tungsten alloy was established, and the hot defor -
mation activation energy and constitutive equation of the three metals were calculated. Characterized by field 
emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM) and the electron back scattering diffraction (EBSD) technique, 
the results show that zirconia doping can significantly refine the tungsten grain size, improve sintering densities, 
and uniformly disperse in the boundaries and inside of tungsten grain. The room temperature compression re -
sults show that the ultimate compressive strength and strain-to-failure of W-1.0 m -ZrO
2 
increase by 51.94% and 
96.69%, respectively compared with pure tungsten. The main contribution to the room temperature strength of 
tungsten alloys is the fine grain strengthening caused by zirconia doping, about 60% for W-1.0 m -ZrO
2
， while 
the strengthening of the secondary phase particles accounts only for about 5%. High-temperature compression 
results show that zirconia doping increases the dislocation density of the alloy and significantly improve the 
ultimate compressive strength under high-temperature compression, but the influence of zirconia content on the 
strength will be weakened at high temperature. The doping of zirconia reduces the hot deformation activation of 
tungsten alloys and improves the hot processing behavior of tungsten alloy. 
1. Introduction 
As a refractory metal material with the highest melting point 
(3410 
◦
C), tungsten has the characteristics of high density, good high 
temperature strength, excellent thermal conductivity, low thermal 
expansion coefficient and strong radiation resistance. It is a national 
strategic resource, widely used as a key material in aerospace, national 
defense industry, nuclear energy, microelectronics and other fields, and 
plays an important role in safeguarding national defense and economic 
security [ 1 – 5 ]. However, pure tungsten also suffers from 
low-temperature brittleness, recrystallization embrittlement, and irra -
diation embrittlement, which make it difficult to meet the needs of 
high-tech fields such as fusion reactors [ 6 , 7 ]. Therefore, it is necessary to 
improve the performance of tungsten alloys through material prepara -
tion methods, sintering processes and plastic deformation processes. 
From the point of view of designing the composition of tungsten alloys, 
the current research mainly focuses on strengthening by solid solution, 
among which the representative alloys are W – Re [ 8 ], etc.; strengthening 
tungsten alloys by adding a secondary phase, the representative alloys 
are W – Y
2
O
3
, W – ZrC [ 9 , 10 ], etc.; there are also some designs of high 
specific gravity tungsten alloys such as W – Ni – Fe [ 11 , 12 ], etc. The 
doping of the secondary phase can significantly refine the tungsten 
grain, increase the recrystallization temperature of tungsten alloys and 
improve the mechanical properties of tungsten alloys, which is an 
effective way of strengthening. The commonly used secondary phases 
studied by scholars are mainly divided into tungsten carbide and oxide 
strengthening, where the oxides are mainly rare earth oxides, such as 
Y
2

, La
2

scarce, costly, and belong to national strategic resources. 
In recent years, our team has developed a series of tungsten and 
molybdenum alloys using ceramic oxide reinforcement (Al

, ZrO
2
).

higher than that of PW at room temperature, because the strength of

of transgranular fracture ratio enhances the ability of tungsten alloy to 
resist failure.

4. Conclusions 
1. The doping of ZrO
2 
can significantly refine the grain size of tungsten 
alloy, improve the sintering density, and uniformly disperse in the 
grain boundary and grain interior of tungsten alloy. 
2. The ultimate compressive strength and fracture strain of W-1.0 m - 
ZrO
2 
increased by 51.94% and 96.69% respectively compared with 
PW. The proportion of transgranular fracture can be increased with 
the increase of ZrO
2 
doping content. 
3. The main contribution to the room temperature strength of tungsten 
alloys is the fine grain strengthening caused by zirconia doping, 
about 60% for W-1.0 m -ZrO
2
， while the strengthening of the sec -
ondary phase particles accounts only for about 5%. 
4. The doping of ZrO
2 
can significantly improve the ultimate 
compressive strength under high temperature compression, but the 
influence of ZrO
2 
content on the strength will be weakened at high 
temperature. 
5. ZrO
2 
doping improves the hot processing behavior of tungsten alloy 
and reduces the hot deformation activation energy of tungsten alloy. 
6. The compressive texture orientations of the three alloys are similar, 
but ZrO
2 
can hinder the transformation of deformed grain texture 
from < 111 > to < 100 > . In addition, the ZrO
2 
can increase the 
dislocation density and improve the high temperature compression 
property, and ZrO
2 
can improve the recrystallization rate in tungsten 
alloy.
