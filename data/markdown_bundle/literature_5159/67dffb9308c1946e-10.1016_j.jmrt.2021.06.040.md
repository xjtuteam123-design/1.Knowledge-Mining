abstract
A high entropy alloy (HEA) with a composition of Ti 2ZrMo0.5Nb0.5 was prepared by vacuum
induction melting technology. The mechanical properties at room temperature and
elevated temperature (900
/C14 Ce1150 /C14 C), microstructure evolution and hot deformation
behavior during hot deformation are studied. The as-cast alloy is composed of equiaxed
grains with the yield strength, the apparent plastic strain of 1306 MPa and 44%, respec-
tively. By TEM analysis, the ZrTi
2 Laves C15 phase is found corresponding the precipitation
strengthening effect accompanied with the solid solution strengthening as a ramiﬁcation
of the high-entropy alloy. Then the hot compression tests were carried out. The deformed
alloy still maintains the same single BCC structure as the as-cast state. It is noted that
when the temperature is 900
/C14 C, the structure of the alloy mainly exhibits dynamic re-
covery (DRV) characteristics, and when the temperature reaches 1000 /C14 C, there are clear
dynamic recrystallization (DRX) characteristics in the structure, which are analyzed as the
features of continuous dynamic recrystallization (CDRX). Keeping the temperature at
1150
/C14 C, when the strain rate is 0.1 s /C0 1, the characteristics of discontinuous dynamic
recrystallization (DDRX) appear, and as the strain rate decreases, CDRX becomes the main
recrystallization mechanism again.

1. Introduction
In recent years, as a new type of alloy, high-entropy alloys
(HEAs) have been widely studied in the world because of their
unique excellent properties. It refers to mixing 4 or more ele-
ments in an equimolar ratio or near equimolar ratio to obtain
alloys with a simple solid solution structure [1,2]. Based on this
basic and unique design concept, different types of alloys are
gradually derived from high-entropy alloys, such as the re-
fractory high-entropy alloys [ 2,3] (RHEAs), the high-entropy
ceramics [ 4,5] (HECs), and high-entropy ﬁlms [ 6] (HEFs). The
alloys have many unique characteristics due to the high mixing

Due to the relatively high mixing entropy, the crystal struc-
ture of HEAs is generally relatively simple, mainly including face-
centered cubic (FCC) and body-centered (BCC) crystal structure.
Nevertheless, the simplicity of the structure cannot avoid the
drawbacks that easily cause failure in performance. To meet the
requirement of commercial applications, the alloy must have
proper response to mechanically controlled processing under
high temperatures. During hot deformation, grain reﬁnement
always plays an important role in the modiﬁcation of mechanical
properties, which are regarded as the consequence of dynamic
recrystallization (DRX). In addition, many studies also showed
that hot deformation is an effective means to modify the prop-
erties of high-entropy alloys [11]. Therefore, it is of great signiﬁ-
cance to investigate the hot deformation behavior especially
DRX mechanism for expansions in commercial application. For
example, Guo et al. [1] found continuous dynamic recrystalliza-

Among the common high-entropy alloy elements, Ti, Zr,
Mo, Nb, etc. are the most commonly used composition to
prepare high entropy alloys with great performances [ 1,3]. In
the current work, we report a new alloy with a composition
of Ti

deformed microstructure was observed by XRD, OM, SEM and
TEM. And the room temperature compression test and hot
compression tests were carried out. At the same time, the
EBSD analysis was carried out on the samples obtained after
hot deformation to analyze the hot deformation mechanism
and microstructure evolution. The research results are going
to provide this kind of high-entropy alloy with basic theo-
retical guidance for the future applications.

3.2. Flow curves
In order to study the microstructure and properties of Ti 2-
ZrMo0.5Nb0.5 high-entropy alloy after hot deformation, the hot
compression tests were conducted. It should be noted that all
samples were compressed to 50% deformation, and none of
them broke. The stress estrain curves are shown in Fig. 4 .
Analysis of the curves shows that the stress estrain curves all
show approximately the same trend under the different
experimental parameters, i.e. as the strain increases, the
stress of the alloy ﬁrst rises to a certain peak and then it slowly
decreases with gradual stabilization. The appearance of this
stable value indicates the dynamic competition between the
“work hardening” and “dynamic softening” of the alloy during
the hot deformation. In addition, as the temperature in-
creases, the peak strength of the alloy gradually decreases. It
can be attributed to the decrease of the critical slip shear
stress caused by the increase of temperature, which is bene-
ﬁcial to the movement of dislocations and thermal diffusion.
And the phenomenon that the peak stress is not obvious is an
important feature of DRX [ 15,16]. The detailed analysis of the
deformation mechanism will be carried out in Section 4.3.

4. Discussions
4.1. Room temperature compressive properties
From Fig. 3 , it can be found that the alloy has relatively high
yield strength (1306 MPa) and excellent plasticity with plastic
strain of 44% at room temperature. In the present study, the
high strength can be attributed to the following aspects: (I)
serious lattice distortion effect of high-entropy alloys; (II)
precipitation strengthening effect caused by precipitation
phases. The four elements that make up the alloy are com-
bined in a nearly equiatomic ratio, which means each element
can be regarded as a solid solution atom and produce severe
lattice distortion. The hindrance to dislocations movement
increases, causing the increase of strength [ 17,18]. In addition,
it has been found that there are precipitates in grain bound-
aries and grains. Fig. 9 shows the TEM images of the room-
temperature microstructures. From Fig. 9 (a), the grain
boundary precipitate presents a bar shape with an aspect ratio
of 5:1, which can be regarded as strong barriers to dislocation
movement. The strengthening effect can be identiﬁed by the
dislocation wall and dislocation tangle formed around the
precipitates which have been pointed out by arrows.
Fig. 9 (bef) shows the EDS mapping of the elements and the
SAED pattern. Zr is still enriched in the precipitation phase,
which is consistent with the EDS analysis in Table .1 .I n
addition to the BCC matrix, it can be found from Fig. 9(f) that
the precipitates on the grain boundary also maintain a BCC
structure, which can be regarded as BCC2 phases. However,
due to its low content, it is hardly observed by XRD. Besides,
the studies [ 19,20] reveal that the precipitates on the grain
boundary will cause the pinning effect, which can inhibit the
growth of the grains. According to the Hall ePetch relation-
ship, the small grains will cause the increase of strength. So at
the present study, the high compressive strength is regarded
as the result of the combination of both precipitation and
grain reﬁnement effect.
From Fig. 2 , there are also intragranular precipitates
pointed out by point 3. The TEM analysis was also performed
as shown in Fig. 10 . The enrichment of Zr also exists in

5. Conclusions
Through the room temperature and hot compression tests of
Ti
2ZrMo0.5Nb0.5 high-entropy alloy, the microstructure, me-
chanical properties and hot deformation mechanism of the
alloy are studied, and the following conclusions are drawn:
(1) The alloy is composed of equiaxed grains with an average
size of 36 mm in the as-cast state and maintains a single
BCC structure. After room temperature compression, the
yield strength of the alloy reaches 1306 MPa, the rapture
strength reaches 2690 MPa, and the apparent plastic
strain reaches about 44%. The high strength is deter-
mined by both solid solution strengthening and precipi-
tation strengthening effect by the BCC2 precipitates and
nanometer-size ZrTi
2 Laves C15 phase. And the good
plasticity can be attributed to the decrease of the effective
slip length of cracks caused by the BCC2 precipitates.
(2) After the hot compression tests, the alloy still keeps a
single BCC structure which indicates good thermal sta-
bility in the present study. And it is found that with the
increase of temperatures and strain rates, the deforma-
tion becomes more and more severe. According to the
stressestrain curves, the peak stress decreases with
increasing temperatures and decreasing strain rates. In
addition, the discontinuous yielding phenomenon (DYP)
happens during the hot compression process, which is
related to the proliferation and migration of dislocations.
(3) EBSD analysis was conducted to analyze the deformation
behavior. Keeping the strain rate at 0.01 s
