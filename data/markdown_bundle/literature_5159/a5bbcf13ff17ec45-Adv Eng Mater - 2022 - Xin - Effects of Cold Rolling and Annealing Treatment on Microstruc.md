1. Introduction
Traditional alloys are mainly based on an alloying element, and
their properties are relatively simple, which makes it dif ﬁcult to
meet the development of the current diversi ﬁed society.
Therefore, people have begun to look for new metals and alloys
to meet the needs of high performance in industry. The multi-
component alloys proposed by Yeh [1] and Cantor[2] in 2004 were
designed with ﬁve or more elements as the
main elements, and a new type of alloy
(high-entropy alloys (HEAs)) was proposed
by this method. The solid solution struc-
ture of HEAs is simple and has distinctive
effects: high-entropy effect, delayed diffu-
sion effect, lattice distortion effect, and
“cocktail” effect. Due to their many unique
and interesting properties, the possible
use of HEAs in advanced structural appli-
cations has attracted unprecedented
attention.[3– 6] There is no primary and sec-
ondary distinction between the elements in
HEAs, most of which are simple solid solu-
tion structures, and the structure is more
simpliﬁed: FCC, BCC, FCC þ BCC or
HCP structures, [7– 10] and the existence of
intermetallic compounds less. FeCoNi-
based HEAs systems with suitable alloying
(such as Cr, Mn, Ti, V, Cu, Al, Mo, Nb, and
Ta) elements have been extensively studied
by scholars.[11] The most widely studied are
CoCrFeNiMn[12,13] and Al0.1CoCrFeNi [14]
HEAs with an FCC phase structure.
These HEAs with an FCC matrix structure
are highly ductile, but their yield strength is relatively low.
Improving the strength of FCC-HEAs is a key issue to expand
the practical application range of this series of HEAs.
Reﬁning the grain size can signi ﬁcantly improve the strength
of HEAs. Nanocrystalline (NG) and ultra ﬁne-grained (UFG)
materials prepared by large plastic deformation (SPD) have excel-
lent mechanical properties. [15– 17] There is considerable interest
in the SPD of HEAs for grain reﬁnement, which is also an impor-
tant prerequisite for preparing superplastic materials. [18] There
are several methods to realize SPD technology, such as equal
channel angular pressing (ECAP), [19– 21] high-pressure torsion
(HPT),[22] severe cold rolling (SCR), etc. [23] Schuh et al. [24]
reported that nanocrystalline CoCrFeNiMn HEAs prepared by
HPT exhibited good tensile strength at break and hardness
values of 1950 MPa and 520 HV, respectively. However, using
the aforementioned method will decrease the plasticity of the
material. A treatment process that combines plastic deformation
and heat treatment can obtain tailored microstructures and
balanced mechanical properties. [25]
The rolling process is the most common and ef ﬁcient method
for producing large pieces of ﬁne-structured material. Compared

Herein, a combination of cold rolling (CR) and annealing treatment is used to
investigate the evolution of the microstructure and mechanical properties of
CoFeNiMnV high-entropy alloys (HEAs) fabricated by powder plasma arc additive
manufacturing (PPA-AM). The deposited CoFeNiMnV HEAs exhibit a face-cen-
tered cubic (FCC) structure with a small amount of σ phase precipitation. After
50% CR, the microstructure of CoFeNiMnV HEAs is severely deformed along the
rolling direction, and the grain size is obviously re ﬁned. Therefore, the hardness
and strength of the alloy are signi ﬁcantly improved. The rolled CoFeNiMnV HEAs
are annealed at 500, 700 and 900 °C, for 60 min. The results show that annealing
at 500 °C has little effect on the microstructure and dislocation density of the
alloy, and the alloy still maintains high strength and hardness. With increasing of
annealing temperature to 700 and 900 °C, the dislocation density of the
CoFeNiMnV HEAs is signi ﬁcantly reduced. Meanwhile the σ phase is gradually
dissolved, and the recrystallized grains and annealing twins are signi ﬁcantly
coarsened. The analysis suggests that it decreases the strength of the
CoFeNiMnV HEAs, but increases the elongation. CoFeNiMnV HEAs annealed
at 700 °C for 60 min after CR exhibit an excellent high strength –toughness
combination.

with high basic strength are selected, a CR process with moderate
rolling quantity is adopted, and short-term annealing treatment

At present, the most commonly used methods for the
preparation of HEAs are casting, mechanical alloying, and laser
cladding. Segregation of components occurs during the melting
and casting process, resulting in uneven composition and poor
performance. Mechanical alloying to prepare HEAs has a high
oxidation tendency due to the high-speed ball milling of the alloys
in a ball-milling tank. The equipment for laser cladding is too
expensive and relatively low energy. In addition, other prepara-
tion methods also have certain limitations. In contrast, powder
plasma arc-only additive manufacturing (PPA-AM) technology
can produce HEAs with high efﬁciency, low production cost, uni-
form structure, and stable performance. [34]
The FCC phase CoFeNiMn HEAs have excellent ductility, and
the addition of adjacent elements V and Cr can promote the
strength enhancement of CoFeNiMn-based HEAs. [35]
However, the addition of Cr did not signi ﬁcantly improve the
strength of the HEAs. Due to the poor compatibility of V with
other elements, precipitates from in the solid solution of the
alloy, thereby promoting a signi ﬁcant improvement in the
strength of the alloy. [36] Therefore, this article selects PPA-AM
deposition to prepare CoFeNiMnV HEAs, and adopts an innova-
tive method combining PPA-AM, CR, and annealing to study the
microstructure evolution and mechanical property differences of
CoFeNiMnV HEAs in the deposition and rolled state (50%) and
short-term (60 min) annealing at 500, 700, and 900 °C after rolling.
2. Experimental Section
In the experiment, pure metal powders of Co, Fe, Ni, Mn, and V
(≥99.5% purity, particle size ranging from 150 to 250 mesh)
were used as experimental raw materials. To unify the particle
size of the powder and improve the uniformity of the material
structure, a high-energy ball mill (Hunan MITR Co., Ltd.
YXQM-12L) was used to mix mineral powder. The metal powder
and alumina (metal powder and grinding ball mass ratio of 1:2)
grinding ball were put into the high-energy ball mill for 6 h of
grinding experiment. When grinding, enough argon gas should
be ﬁlled into the grinding tank as grinding protective gas so that
the metal powder will not be oxidized during grinding. After
grinding, the metal powder was wrapped in tinfoil and placed
in a vacuum-drying oven to dry for 3 h. In the experiment,
304 stainless steel plate (150 /C2 80 /C2 10 mm) was used as the
deposition substrate of CoFeNiMnV HEAs. Before the deposi-
tion experiment, the surface of the substrate was cleaned with
absolute ethanol to remove surface contaminants, and the sur-
face of the substrate was further polished with a grinding wheel.
Finally, the substrate was preheated for 3– 5 min to avoid cracks at
the joint between the deposition material and the substrate,
resulting in a less dense joint. High-purity argon (99.9% purity)
was used as protective gas, ion gas, and powder feed gas during
the deposition process. During the PPA-AM process, each layer
of the deposited material was surface treated with a grinding
wheel to ensure good bonding between the layers. The experi-
mental deposition parameters are shown in Table 1 .
Figure 1 a shows a schematic diagram of the preparation of
CoFeNiMnV HEAs by PPA-AM, as well as the CR and annealing
process. Samples ready for rolling were prepared using wire cut-
ting to remove bulks of 55 mm /C2 15 mm /C2 20 mm (shown in
Figure 1b) from the deposited CoFeNiMnV HEAs. The
CoFeNiMnV HEAs samples were subjected to multipass rolling

rolling mill) at room temperature. The rolling amount was 50%,
and the ﬁnal thickness after CR was 10 mm. Then the cold-rolled
samples were annealed at 500, 700, and 900 °C for 60 min. The
deposited, cold rolled, and annealed CoFeNiMnV HEAs samples
were de ﬁned as HEA-depositing, CR-HEA, CR-HEA þ 500,
CR-HEA þ 700, and CR-HEA þ 900, respectively.

of CR-HEA specimens are signi ﬁcantly widened, which is
attributed to the microstructure re ﬁnement, residual stress
enhancement, and dislocation density increase after plastic

ously enhanced, but the intensity of the diffraction peaks returns

Similar characteristics have been found in references, [31,50,51]
indicating that rolling and annealing treatments have a signi ﬁ-
cant effect on the microstructure of the alloys after plastic
deformation.
3.2. Microstructure Characteristics
The microstructure of the deposited and treated samples was
characterized by SEM and the local chemical composition was
analyzed by energy dispersive spectrometry (EDS). Figure 3a – e
shows the microstructures of different samples of CoFeNiMnV
HEAs. Figure 4 shows the element distribution diagram of the σ
phase in the CoFeNiMnV HEAs deposition sample measured by
SEM-EDS. Table 3 presents the compositional results of the
SEM-EDS spot sweep area of the deposited, cold rolled, and
annealed CoFeNiMnV HEAs. The grains of HEA-Depositing
are relatively coarse (Figure 3a), and there are σ phases (gray-
black area) dispersed in the gray FCC matrix and between the
grain boundaries. From the element map determined by
SEM– EDS in Figure 4, it can be seen that the σ phase is rich
in two elements, Mn and V. Figure 3 shows that the proportion
of the σ phase gradually decreases with increasing annealing
temperature, and it can be determined that a certain amount
of sigma is dissolved during the annealing process. After 50%
CR deformation, the grains of CR-HEA are deformed along
the RD direction, and the grains are re ﬁned. During annealing
at 500, 700, and 900 °C for 60 min, the CoFeNiMnV HEA under-
goes different degrees of recrystallization. The experimental
results showed that no obvious grain boundaries were observed
in the SEM image after annealing at 500 °C (Figure 3c). At an
annealing temperature of 700 °C, partial recrystallization grains
occur in the CR-HEA þ 700 sample (Figure 3d), and the recrys-
tallized grains are small in size. Upon further increasing the
annealing temperature, when the annealing temperature reaches
900 °C, the deformed microstructure of the CR-HEA þ 900 sam-
ple (Figure 3e) is fully recrystallized and accompanied by grain
growth, at which time the recrystallized grains are mostly equi-
axed grains.
Figure 5 shows the EBSD inverse pole ﬁgure (IPF) and grain

HEA-depositing sample exhibits elongated and coarse grain char-

in the crystal of the CR-HEA sample (Figure 5b), and the grains

3.3. Mechanical Properties
Figure 8 shows the hardness data of PPA-AM in the as-deposited
and as-rolled states and after annealing at different temperatures
after rolling. Figure 9 shows the engineering stress – strain curve
obtained from tensile tests under different treatment conditions.
The yield stress (YS, σy ¼ σ0:2, stress under 0.2% plastic strain),
ultimate tensile strength (UTS) and uniform strain (US) are sum-
marized. The hardness of CoFeNiMnV HEAs deposited directly
by PPA-AM is relatively uniform from top to bottom, with an
average hardness of 182.1 HV, which may be attributed to the
relatively uniform microstructure of CoFeNiMnV HEAs pre-
pared by PPA-AM. The yield strength of the directly deposited
sample is 406 MPa, the ultimate tensile strength reaches
702 MPa, and the uniform elongation is 32.7% (Figure 9b).
Compared with HEAs [32,56– 59] without any subsequent treat-
ment, PPA-AM deposited CoFeNiMnV has relatively higher
hardness, yield, and tensile strength. This may be because
CoFeNiMnV HEAs deposited with PPA-AM have a more homo-
geneous microstructure and precipitate many ﬁne σ phases in
the FCC matrix (as shown in Figure 3a). The presence of these
σ phases plays a role in precipitation strengthening.

3.4. Strengthening Mechanisms
The strength and other mechanical properties of the alloy can be
designed by lattice defects in the crystal. These defects affecting
the material properties are mainly dislocations, solute atoms,
precipitates, and grain boundaries. These defects act as obstacles
to slip motion during deformation, resulting in an increase in
the strength of the material. The strengthening mechanism of
polycrystalline materials can be summarized as solid solution
strengthening, grain boundary strengthening, dislocation
strengthening, and precipitation strengthening. Kamiokawa
et al.[60] related the strengthening mechanism and yield strength
and proposed Equation (6)
σ0:2 ¼ σ0 þ σss þ σgb þ σdis þ σppt (6)
where σ0 is the lattice friction stress and σss, σgb, σdis, and σppt are
the strengthening contributions of the solid solution, grain
boundary, dislocation, and precipitation, respectively. Since there
is no distinction between solvent atoms and solute atoms in the
HEAs composition, it is impossible to describe an element as a
solute, so it does not make sense to separate σ0 and σss, and usu-
ally σ0 contains σss.[61] The lattice friction stress σ0 of
CoFeNiMnV HEAs is considered to be 165.77 MPa (based on
Figure 10 ). Therefore, the yield strength equation can be
simpliﬁed to
σ0:2 ¼ σ0 þ σgb þ σdis þ σppt (7)

4. Conclusions
In this study, bulk CoFeNiMnV HEAs were prepared by PPA-
AM. The combined treatment process of CR and annealing
was used to study the microstructure and mechanical properties
of the deposited, cold rolled, and annealed samples. Based
on the aforementioned, the following conclusions are drawn:
1) CoFeNiMnV HEAs after PPA-AM deposition, rolling, and
annealing all have FCC and σ phases, and the σ phases gradu-
ally decrease with increasing annealing temperature; 2) 50%
rolling resulted in a large number of deformed grains in
CoFeNiMnV HEAs, which were subdivided by deformed bands.
The deformation makes the slip dislocations pile up, and the
microstructure of CR-HEA is mainly composed of subgrains,
of which LAGBs account for the main part ( /C25 92.83%), and
the subgrains store high levels of deformation energy and a
high density of dislocations; 3) After 50% rolling, the micro-
hardness of CoFeNiMnV HEAs increases nearly 2 times, and
it has excellent yield strength ( /C25 920 MPa) and tensile strength
(/C25 1016.7 MPa). The signi ﬁcant increase in the strength of
CoFeNiMnV after rolling can be attributed to grain boundary
strengthening due to dislocation strengthening and grain
reﬁnement; 4) No recrystallized grains are observed in the
samples annealed at 500 °C because the recrystallization
temperature of CoFeNiMnV HEAs is not reached. Since the
microstructure of the alloy does not change much, the alloy still
maintains high strength and hardness; 5) When the annealing
temperature reaches 700 °C, both recrystallized grains and non-
recrystallized grains exist in the CoFeNiMnV HEAs structure,
and a small amount of annealing twins also appear. The alloy in
this state has the best combination of yield strength (669 MPa),
tensile strength (890 MPa), and uniform elongation (16.7%);
6) At the 900 °C annealing temperature, most of the deformed
ﬂat grains are transformed into equiaxed grains. During this
process, the alloy has almost completely recrystallized, and
the grain boundaries of the alloy are mainly composed of
HAGBs and twins. The proportion of annealing twins increased
to 31.17%. The hardness and strength of CoFeNiMnV HEAs are
signiﬁcantly reduced, and the uniform elongation is effectively
increased (37.5%).
