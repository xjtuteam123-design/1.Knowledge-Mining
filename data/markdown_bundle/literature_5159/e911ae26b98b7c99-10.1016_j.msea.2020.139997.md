Abstract: TiAl-based alloys with high melting point become th e promising high 2 
temperature structural materials, but the inherent brittleness drawback suppresses their 3 
application. Here, we proposed a controllable strat egy to concurrently achieve high 4 
strength and high plasticity in TiAl/Nb composites that were prepared by introducing 5 
fine plastic Nb particulates to disperse around mat rix TiAl, followed by powder 6 
metallurgy reaction at 1200 oC/50MPa/1h. A complex atomic diffusion reaction (ADR) 7 
at the interfaces between matrix TiAl and plasticiz er Nb was clarified. The results of 8 
uniaxial compression of TiAl/Nb composites at room temperature (RT) show the high 9 
strength and excellent plasticity, due to the opera tion of multiple deformation 10 
mechanisms of involved phases in the novel structur e. Definitely, the prominent 11 
deformation mechanism of matrix TiAl was deformation twinning, 12 
assisted by dislocation cross-slip. The plasticizer , such as fresh Nb-based 13 
intermetallics (Nb im ) and remained Nb (Nb r), coordinated the overall migration of 14 
matrix particulates in the form of compatible defor mation. The strengthening phase 15 
O/B2 belonged to the dislocation-mediated deformati on and was responsible to 16 
propagate stress from the plasticizer to the matrix . Predominantly, the tailoring 17 
microstructure that Nb im /Nb r was interspersed in the fresh γn and O/B2 was conducive 18 
to the successive straining of the composites, whic h in turn facilitated the intersection 19 
or entanglement of the proliferating dislocations and thereby resulted in high strength. 20

1. Introduction 2 
In recent decades, TiAl-based alloys have been paid tremendous attentions for 3 
high temperature structural applications, which are essentially due to their low density, 4 
excellent elevated-temperature strength, superior o xidation resistance and favorable 5 
creep performances [1-3]. As a result, TiAl-based a lloys are deemed to be the most 6 
promising structural materials concerning the substitution of Ni-based superalloys and 7 
used for saving weight so as to improve fuel effici ency in turbine-engine applications 8 
[4]. Despite the outstanding characteristics that T iAl-based alloys possess, up to now, 9 
the applications are still limited owing to the inh erent poor toughness and formability 10 
at RT [5-7]. Accordingly, processive enhancement of the strength while maintaining 11 
appropriate plasticity of lightweight metallic mate rials becomes enduring pursuit of 12 
scientific researchers [8,9]. However, the increase of strength invariably results in the 13 
deterioration of plasticity, which is known as strength-plasticity trade-off [10,11]. 14 
With the purpose of evading the strength-plasticity trade-off dilemma in structural 15 
materials, many researchers have been made great ef forts and achieved remarkable 16 
progress. The effective methods available to concur rently enhance the strength and 17 
plasticity in metallic materials at RT primarily cover four strategies: (i) introduction of 18 
high-density nanotwins [8,12,13], (ii) construction of gradient or hierarchical 19 
nanostructures [10,14-16], (iii) design of ultrafin e-grained structures [17-20] and (iv) 20 
severe plastic deformation [21-24]. The former thre e ways currently require large 21 
deformation intervention, occasionally, need electr odeposition and annealing, to 22 
produce nanotwins, gradient or hierarchical nanostr uctures as well as ultrafine grains 23 
(reverse Hall-Petch relationships) [14-20]. However , the above methods haven’t been 24 
widely spread in the current industry, which is mai nly attributed to the high cost, low 25 
efficiency and immature technology of these production processes. The fourth method 26 
with plenty of dislocations can induce recrystalliz ation by subsequent annealing [23] 27 
in order to weaken basal texture [22], which is com monly applied in amelioration of 28 
magnesium and titanium alloys. Moreover, the plasti city is extremely susceptible to 29 
dislocations which in turn deteriorate compatible d eformation once multi-junctions 30

Powder metallurgy (PM) is an economical and control lable fabrication process, 5 
which can not only achieve near net forming but als o ensure that the composition and 6 
microstructure of materials can be controlled, thus becoming an indispensable method 7 
in the field of industrial manufacturing. Hitherto, this process has been utilized to 8 
prepare TiAl-based alloys and composites based on t he pre-alloyed powders and/or 9 
element powders [26,27]. Liu et al. [28] prepared T i-44Al-3Nb-1Mo-1V-0.2Y alloy 10 
by spark plasma sintering (SPS) of pre-alloyed powd ers, and the RT compression 11 
strength and final strain of the alloy are signific antly higher than those of the 12 
conventional casting alloy. Park et al. [29] synthe sized Ti-46.6Al-1.4Mn-2Mo alloy 13 
processed by PM of blended elemental powders, and t he high-temperature tensile 14 
properties and the creep performances of the alloy are remarkably improved. Cui et al. 15 
[30] fabricated carbon fibers/graphene reinforced T iAl-based composite by PM and 16 
the composite exhibits the compression strength of 2.3GPa and the fracture strain of 17 
26.3%, which are much greater than those of TiAl-ba sed alloy. Ai et al. [31] prepared 18 
Ti 2AlC/TiAl composite by hot-pressing sintering (HPS) through the reaction system 19 
of the pre-alloyed powders Ti 3AlC 2 and TiAl, and the flexural strength and fracture 20 
toughness of the composite are 993.9MPa and 6.3MPa m 1/2 , respectively. Accordingly, 21 
PM can be a promising direction to strengthening an d plasticizing TiAl-based alloys 22 
or composites. Note that the enhancement of the mec hanical properties of the above 23 
alloys or composites is mainly realized by tailorin g and controlling the element 24 
composition. 25 
In this study, we proposed a novel route, i.e., the tailoring of microstructure, to get 26 
better the performances of TiAl-based composites. I nspired by the WC/Co 27 
architecture model [32], a new architecture regardi ng TiAl-based composites were 28 
successfully designed and synthesized by PM techniq ue. Parsimoniously, the refined 29 
Nb particles were introduced into TiAl matrix to pl ay the role of bridging the matrix. 30

Highlights 
 
1. The TiAl/Nb composites with high strength and high plasticity were prepared by
