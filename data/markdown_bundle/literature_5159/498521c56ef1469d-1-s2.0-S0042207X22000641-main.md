ABSTRACT 
A NbMoTaWV high entropy alloy (HEA) was fabricated by mechanical alloying (MA) and spark plasma sintering 
(SPS). Characterization of milled powders indicated that only a single body-centered cubic (BCC) solid solution 
phase was generated in the MA process. After SPS, a tetragonal (Ta,V)O
2 
oxide phase precipitated from the 
primary BCC phase. The NbMoTaWV alloy exhibited a compressive yielding strength ( σ
y
) of 2785 MPa, 
compressive strength ( σ
max
) of 3661 MPa and strain to failure ( ε
f
) of 16.2% at room temperature. The 
compressive yielding strength of the sintered alloy was dramatically higher compared with most previously 
reported HEAs. The compressive strength of NbMoTaWV alloy at 1000 
◦
C arrived at 1978 MPa. The excellent 
strength at room and elevated temperatures are mostly ascribed to the fine (Ta,V)O
2 
particles dispersed in the 
BCC matrix. 
In recent years, high entropy alloys (HEAs) have attracted consid -
erable attention due to their encouraging properties [ 1 – 6 ]. Earlier ef -
forts of the HEAs development were mostly focused on obtaining the 
structure of single solid solution phase [ 3 – 5 ]. However, it was found that 
a two-phase structure may also maintain a high performance due to 
strengthening by the second phases [ 6 – 9 ]. For example, the second 
silicide phase that formed in HfMo
0.5
NbTiV
0.5
Si
0.7 
alloy greatly 
improved the yield strength at 1200 
◦
C by the addition of Si into the 
single body-centered cubic (BCC) solid solution phase [ 8 ]. 
Refractory HEAs which consist of refractory elements (e.g., Nb, V, 
Mo, Ta, W, Ti, Hf and Zr) tend to form a simple BCC phase structure. A 
lot of refractory HEAs exhibit high strength at room and elevated tem -
peratures [ 3 , 7 ], and so they are motivated by high temperature structure 
applications such as advanced gas turbines and airplane engines [ 1 , 2 ], 
further more they are devised to compete with nickel superalloys [ 3 ]. 
For example, Senkov et al. used vacuum arc melting to synthesize 
VNbMoTaW HEA with a simple BCC structure, and the alloy exhibited 
high strength at both room and elevated temperatures [ 3 ]. However, 
further improvement of the strength relies on other mechanisms such as 
precipitation strengthening [ 10 – 12 ] and grain boundary strengthening 
[ 11 , 13 ]. Mechanical alloying (MA) followed by spark plasma sintering 
(SPS) could fabricate HEAs with finer grain size and more homogeneous 
microstructure [ 11 , 13 ], thereby providing sufficient strengthening. For 
instance, Fu et al. fabricated a high-performance two-phase Al
0.6
CoNi -
FeTi
0.4 
HEA with ultrafine microstructure using MA and SPS [ 6 ]. We 
used MA and SPS to fabricate ultra-high strength NbMoTaWVCr re -
fractory HEA with fine-grained microstructure [ 13 ]. 
In the present work, we succeeded in fabricating a two-phase re -
fractory NbMoTaWV HEA with excellent strength at room and elevated 
temperatures by MA and SPS, the microstructure and mechanical 
properties of the sintered alloy were reported. 
Elemental powders of Nb, Mo, Ta, W and V with high purity ( > 99.9 
wt%) and particle sizes ≤ 48 μ m were used as starting materials for bulk 
NbMoTaWV HEA. The powders in equiatomic composition (Nb:Mo:Ta: 
W:V = 1:1:1:1:1) was ball milled in a stainless steel vial together with 
stainless steel balls. At milling times of 10, 20, 30 and 40h, a portion of 
ball milled powders were taken out for analysis. The procedures were 
carried out inside a glove box to decrease oxidation of HEA powders. 
When the total milling time reached 40 h, the milled powders were 
removed from the vial. 
After milling, the milled powders were loaded into a graphite die and 
compacted by a Dr. Sinter 825 apparatus, which is shown in Fig. 1 (a).

XRD patterns of NbMoTaWV HEA MA powders with different milling 
times are shown in Fig. 2 (a). It was found that peaks of V disappeared 
after milling for 10 h, suggesting that V element exhibited the highest 
alloying rate. As the milling time increase to 30 h, peaks of most phases 
of single element disappeared, and a single solid solution phase with a 
BCC structure was obtained. To gain a more homogeneous solid solu -
tion, ball milling was continued to 40 h. Based on Williamson-Hall ’ s 
formula, the crystal size for the BCC phase of the 40 h-milled powders 
were estimated to be 10.8 nm. 
Fig. 2 (b) depicts XRD patterns of NbMoTaWV equiatomic HEA 
samples obtained by SPS, and the pattern of the 40 h mechanically 
alloyed powders is also included for comparison. The sintered HEAs 
were mainly composed of a BCC solid solution phase, plus a minor 
tetragonal phase which was characterized by several weak diffraction 
peaks. This suggests that a tetragonal phase was precipitated from the 
BCC matrix during the SPS process. 
Fig. 3 (a) displays the SEM micro graph of the sintered sample. Bulk 
HEA alloy with a density of 11.6 g/cm
3 
was obtained. As can be seen, the 
HEA exhibited a typical two-phase structure: the tetragonal phase (dark- 
colored) homogeneously dispersed in the BCC matrix (light-colored). 
Moreover, the volume fraction of the tetragonal phase was approxi -
mately 13.8% with the average particle size of approximately 0.5 μ m. 
The TEM bright field images and corresponding selected-area elec -
tron diffraction patterns of sintered samples are presented in Fig. 3 (b – d). 
Two phases with different lattice structures could be detected (as shown 
in region ‘1 ’ and ‘2 ’ in Fig. 3 (b)). The average grain diameter of the BCC 
matrix phase was approximately 2.7 μ m. According to the corresponding 
[111] zone axis diffraction pattern ( Fig. 3 (c)), region ‘1 ’ was identified 
to be a BCC structure phase, with the lattice parameter being calculated 
as 3.316 Å. The corresponding [ 1-1-1 ] zone axis diffraction pattern 
( Fig. 3 (d)) of region ‘2 ’ confirmed a tetragonal structure phase, with the 
lattice parameter a = 4.709 Å and c = 3.067 Å. From the results of EDS, 
the average concentrations of Nb, Mo, Ta, W and V elements in region ‘1 ’ 
are ~25.38, ~22.55, ~15.26, ~19.64 and ~18.63 at.%, respectively, 
which are close to the nominal composition. In addition, the region ‘2 ’ is 
enriched in Ta, V and O, while hardly had any Nb, Mo, W. The average 
concentrations of Ta, V and O in region ‘2 ’ were ~22.3, ~14.95 and 
~60.32 at.%, respectively. Combining with the XRD results, diffraction 
peaks of (Ta,V)O
2 
phase are close to those of TaO
2
, but part of Ta atom is 
replaced by V. The sum of content of Ta and V is about half of the content 
of O, so a conclusion can be drawn that the tetragonal phase is (Ta,V)O
2 
phase.

It is reasonable to conclude that the (Ta,V)O
2 
phase was precipitated 
from the initial BCC phase during the SPS heating process. Oxygen might 
be introduced during powders preparation and SPS process. It is well 
known that Ta and V exhibit considerably higher oxygen affinity than 
other three elements [ 14 ], hence the (Ta,V)O
2 
phase formed. 
Fig. 4 (a) displays compressive engineering stress-strain curves of the 
NbMoTaWV alloy at different temperatures. It is obviously that the re -
fractory NbMoTaWV HEA exhibited a combination of high strength and 
good ductility at room temperature. As can be seen, the alloy exhibited 
yield strength ( σ
y
) of ~2785 MPa, compressive strength ( σ
max
) of 
~3661 MPa, strain to failure ( ε
f
) of ~16.2% and Vickers hardness of 
~774 HV, as listed in Table 1 . Compared with the corresponding alloy 
produced by vacuum arc melting [ 3 ], the σ
y
, σ
max
, ε
f 
and Vickers 
hardness of the alloy increased by 123.5%, 188.3%, 864.7% and 47.4%, 
respectively. The notable improvements of strength could result from 
the precipitation of relatively fine (Ta,V)O
2 
phase, which acted as an 
obstacle to dislocation motion in the BCC matrix. On the other hand, the 
average grain size of this NbMoTaWV HEA (2.7 μ m) were much smaller 
than that of the as-cast one in previous work [ 3 ], which was about 80 
μ m. Therefore, the grain boundary strengthening was considered as 
another strengthening mechanism. 
At elevated temperatures dislocations are easy to move due to the 
effect of thermal activation. Therefore, the strength decreased with the 
increasing temperature [ 15 ]. However, the compressive strength at 
1000 
◦
C was even high at 1978 MPa, and decreased to 1433 MPa at 
1200 
◦
C, which are much higher than those in previous work ( σ
max 
of 
~1454 MPa at 1000 
◦
C and σ
max 
of ~943 MPa at 1200 
◦
C) [ 3 ]. The 
excellent strength of NbMoTaWV alloy at elevated temperatures is 
mostly ascribed to the presence of the second phase which hinders the 
grain boundary sliding. Nevertheless, the alloy became less ductile at 
elevated temperature, which was probably attributed to the presence of 
the second phase activating cracks along grain boundaries.

Fig. 4 (b) presents the compressive yield strength versus strain at 
room temperature for this alloy and other HEAs in previous works [ 3 – 9 ]. 
Table 1 provides a summary of the mechanical properties of all the 
related HEAs in Fig. 4 (b). It is evident that the compressive yield 
strength and compressive strength of this NbMoTaWV alloy were 
dramatically higher than those of most reported HEAs. 
A refractory NbMoTaWV equiatomic HEA with prominent 
compressive properties was obtained by MA and SPS in this work. The 
sintered samples showed a two-phase microstructure with a tetragonal 
(Ta,V)O
2 
phase (0.5 μ m) dispersed in the BCC matrix with the average 
grain size of 2.7 μ m. At room temperature the sintered NbMoTaWV HEA 
exhibit superior compressive strength and ductility compared with the 
corresponding HEA through cast method. The notable improvements in 
strength are attributed to the precipitation of (Ta,V)O
2 
phase and grain 
boundary strengthening of fine BCC matrix. We have shown a successful 
exploration to benefit the optimization of both strength and ductility in 
refractory HEAs.
