abstract
The effects of Re on low-cycle fatigue (LCF) behaviors of single-crystal superalloys can be summarized
mainly in the following aspects. On one hand, the addition of Re strongly retards the coarsening process
of γ0-Ni3Al phase and maintains the uniformity of the phase structure. On the other hand, the Re addition
can promote the segregation, which usually increases the lattice mismatch of the superalloy and further
impedes any individual dislocation to move in the γ-Ni matrix freely and induces the activation of more
dislocation movement by a combined glide-climb process. All these factors have improved the LCF
properties of single-crystal superalloys at 900 1C, for instance, the deformation becomes more homo-
geneous, the strengthening of γ
0 phase is more effective and therefore the fatigue life becomes longer due
to the addition of Re.

3. Experimental results
3.1. Cyclic hardening and LCF behaviors
Fig. 1 presents various LCF curves of alloy 0Re and alloy 3Re at
900 1C, in which the cyclic stress response curves of the two alloys
display the similar change and certain cyclic softening phenomena
with increasing strain amplitudes. By comparing the two alloys, it
can be found that their cyclic stress response curves are very similar.
With increasing the cyclic number, the alloys initially showed fast
cyclic softening to some extent and then slowly softened. Finally the
stress value decreased rapidly due to the rapid propagation of the
crack [1 7]. With increasing the strain amplitude, the cyclic softening
became more obvious and the fatigue life gradually decreased. The
softening behavior can be attributed to the rapid formation of
dislocation networks at the γ/γ0 interfaces [18], which is the primary
cause resulting in the initial softening. The subsequent slow softening
phenomenon is attributed to th e appearance of time-dependent
coarsening of γ0 phase [19].
As shown in Fig. 2 (a)–(c), comparing the cyclic stress response
curves between both alloys at the strain amplitude of 1.3%, 1.6%
and 2.1%, it can be seen that Re-containing alloy exhibits higher
cyclic stress amplitude and longer fatigue life than Re-free alloy.
For example, at Δεt ¼ 1:3% the fatigue life of the alloy 3Re is
one third longer than that of the alloy 0Re and the cyclic stress
amplitude is about 25 MPa higher. Erickson [20] pointed out
that in the temperature range of 800 –900 1C, as compared with
CMSX-4 containing 3 wt% Re, CMSX-6 with 6 wt% Re displayed
2.5 times longer fatigue life and 15% higher fatigue strength. Fig. 2
(d) summarizes the LCF lives of two alloys at different strain
amplitudes, it can be concluded that the alloy 3Re has a higher
fatigue life than the alloy 0Re at any strain amplitude, indicating a
positive effect of Re on improving fatigue properties. In the

4.1. Effect of Re on size of γ0 phase
Fig. 6 (a) and (b) shows the initial γ0 morphologies of the two
alloys on the (001) observed plane after standard heat treatment.
It can be found that both alloys consist of similar two-phase
structure, in which ordered γ
0 precipitates with different original
sizes are embedded in a matrix with a face-centered cubic
structure. Fig. 6 (c) and (d) describes the original sizes of γ0 phases
in the two alloys. The Gaussian ﬁts to the distribution are plotted
here and the corresponding full width at half maximum (FWHM)
and the average sizes a are also presented. With increasing strain
amplitudes, the sizes of γ0 phases are statistically analyzed and
summarized in Fig. 7 . Before LCF tests, both size and FWHM of γ0
phases in alloy 3Re are much smaller than those in alloy 0Re, as
shown in Fig. 6. This indicates that Re would retard the coarsening
process of the γ0 precipitates and equalize the distribution pattern
of γ0 phase during the heat treatments, which is similar to the
reports by Giamei et al. [3] and Jin et al. [24].
After LCF tests at different strain amplitudes, the coarsening
extent of γ0 phase in the alloy 0Re becomes higher, as shown in
Fig. 7 . In addition, with increasing the strain amplitude, the
coarsening trend becomes more obvious. At the total strain
amplitude of 2.1%, however, the coarsening extent falls back to
the same as that at 1.0%, which may be due to the shorter testing
time. Compared with the alloy 0Re, the coarsening of γ0 precipi-
tates in the alloy 3Re is not distinct after LCF test, but the trend is
the same, as shown in Fig. 7(g). Moreover, the γ0 phase in the alloy
3Re seems to be more uniform by comparison in either Figs. 6
and 7, which suggests that the coarsening of the γ0 precipitates has
been well restrained and the more uniform phase structure
ultimately appears in the alloy 3Re. In a word, it is suggested that
Re can retard the γ0 coarsening and make the phase structure
uniform not only during the heat treatment, but also during the
high-temperature LCF tests, which is bene ﬁcial to the LCF proper-
ties of superalloys at high temperature.

4.2. Effect of Re on dislocation movement and distribution
At 900 1C the deformation of the alloy 0Re becomes very
homogeneous. As shown in Fig. 8 (a), most dislocations are
distributed in the γ matrix and the whole specimen can be divided
into the deformed zone and un-deformed zone, among which the
deformation zone consists of the by-passing dislocation regions
with high dislocation density and the un-deformed zone is
composed of the low dislocation density areas. Both matrix
dislocation and interface dislocation can be found in Fig. 8 (b). At
this temperature, the lattice mismatch at the γ/γ0 interface has
been high enough. Under cyclic loading, a plenty of mis ﬁt
dislocations form at the interface. Also due to high testing
temperature, high stacking fault energy (SFE) brings about the
easy cross-slip of screw dislocations and high activation energy

Compared with the alloy 0Re, the dislocation distribution in the

deformed zone and un-deformed zone disappeared and the whole
specimen consists of uniformly distributed dislocations, as shown

dislocation loops (see Fig. 8 (d)), which indicate that the deforma-
tion mechanism of the alloy 3Re is similar to the alloy 0Re. The
difference is that the addition of Re prevents the γ0 phase from
growing and effectively decreases the size of γ0 phase. On the other
hand, the element Re itself is easy to increase the segregation of
other element, which will enhance the lattice mismatch of the
superalloy and cause a number of the mis ﬁt dislocations. These
misﬁt dislocations hinder the activation and movement of sub-
sequent dislocation to strengthen the superalloy. Therefore, the
cyclic stress amplitude of the alloy 3Re is obviously higher than
that of the alloy 0Re.
In a word, at 900 1C, the dislocation movement in two Ni-based
SC superalloys gives priority to cr oss-slip of screw dislocation and
climbing of edge dislocation due to the increase of SFE and activation
energy. The deformation tends to be homogenized by Orowan by-
passing at this temperature. The addition of the element Re inhibits
the growth of γ0 phase to reduce the width of the γ matrix and
further induces the activation of more dislocation movement by a
combined glide-climb process so that the deformation homogeniza-
tion becomes higher. From this it is naturally clear that the Re-
containing Ni-based SC superalloys obtain higher cyclic stress
response and longer fatigue life at 900 1C.
