ABSTRACT 
In this study, a novel magnetic CoCuFeMnNi high entropy alloy (HEA) was synthesized using mechanical 
alloying (MA) and spark plasma sintering (SPS). Thermodynamic analysis showed that the solid solution state of 
the CoCuFeMnNi alloy system has the highest negative Gibbs free energy ( 59.34 kJ/mol), meaning that the 
solid solution state is very stable. Energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) and X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis 
confirmed the formation of an FCC solid solution after 50 h mechanical alloying. The vibrant sample magne -
tometer (VSM) test illustrated a soft magnetic behavior with 48 emu/g magnetic saturation (M
S
) and 15 Oe 
coercivity field (H
C
) in 1 T (T) applied magnetic field. The maximum of densification and microhardness were 
attained at 850 
◦
C, and the ultimate values of shear strength and tensile strength were 320 MPa and 576 MPa, 
respectively. The CoCuFeMnNi HEA was subjected to a pin-on-disc dry sliding wear test at various temperatures 
from room temperature to 600 
◦
C. A detailed microscopic characterization of the worn surfaces revealed a 
transition in the predominant wear mechanism from delamination wear at room temperature to oxidative and 
delamination wear at elevated temperatures. The formation of an oxidized tribo-layer at 600 
◦
C on the worn 
surfaces led to a low wear rate and low coefficient of friction, enabling the CoCuFeMnNi HEA for high- 
temperature applications. 
1. Introduction 
High entropy alloys (HEAs) belong to a novel class of metallic ma -
terials containing more than five principal elements in an equimolar or 
near equimolar ratio, with a slight difference in atomic radius ( < 15 %), 
and the concentration of each constituent element ranges from 5 to 35 
at.% [1 – 5] . Due to the strong solid solution effect, HEAs exhibit 
appealing physical, mechanical, and chemical properties, including high 
hardness, excellent corrosion and oxidation resistance, high thermal 
stability, high strength at room and high temperatures, superior fatigue 
resistance, excellent tribological behavior [6 – 8] and desirable soft 
magnetic properties [9 – 12] . The combination of these features in HEAs 
has drawn considerable attention for using them in industries such as 
hydrogen storage materials, diffusion barriers, electromagnetic shield -
ing materials, thermoelectric materials, functional coatings, anti- 
bacterial materials, and high-temperature applications [13] . 
One of the most prominent challenges in magnetic industries is 
related to the lack of soft magnetic materials with desirable mechanical 
properties. In fact, conventional soft magnetic materials suffer from low 
strength or weak wear resistance, especially in electromagnetic sensors, 
magnetic shielding, magnetic springs, and highspeed magnetic rotors 
which need high soft magnetic properties and high hardness and wear 
resistance [14,15] . This challenge can be addressed by developing new 
metallic materials. Research has shown that HEAs can simultaneously 
boost both soft magnetic properties and mechanical behavior. This 
ability stems from the presence of some ferromagnetic elements such as 
Fe, Co, and Ni resulting in high magnetic saturation (M
S
) and strong 
solid solution effects that improve the strength, hardness, and tribo -
logical behavior [16 – 20] . 
HEAs can be synthesized by different fabrication routes, mostly by 
the liquid-phase synthesis methods, such as arc melting, induction 
melting, and Bridgeman solidification [21] . Due to restrictions in final 
product shape, the compositional segregation of high melting point el -
ements, inhomogeneous microstructure, and evaporation of low melting 
point elements, these fabrication methods are inappropriate for indus -
trial manufacturing [22 – 24] . Therefore, during the last decade, re -
searchers have been attracted to the production of HEAs by solid-state 
processes such as the mechanical alloying (MA) method followed by

2. Materials and methods 
Thermodynamic prediction of fabrication of CoCuFeMnNi HEA was 
carried out by the Extended Miedema model [28] . Elemental powders 
with a diameter of ≤ 45 μ m and purities of > 99 % with an equal molar 
ratio were used as starting materials. Table 1 shows the quantities of 
elemental powders expressed. The milling process was performed in a 
hardened chromium steel vial associated with hardened high ‑ carbon 
steel balls. The volume of the vial was 500 ml, and the diameters of the 
balls were 20,16 and 10 mm. The high-energy planetary ball mill 
(Retsch: PM-100, Germany) with a rotational speed of 250 rpm and a 
ball to powder weight ratio of 10:1 was used. To avoid oxidation and 
nitration, the milling process was performed under the argon atmo -

powders from cold welding and agglomeration, Stearic acid was

energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) detector was used for elemental 
analysis and examination of the dissolution of elements for milled 
powder and sintered alloys.

sintering (KPF) in a 40 mm-inner-diameter graphite die. The amount of 
powder was chosen in such a way that the bulk sample had a height of 3

tion, graphite foils were sited between powder and die walls. The con -

3.1. Thermodynamic analysis 
The values of Gibbs free energy change ( Δ G) and mixing enthalpy 
change ( Δ H) of the amorphous state, solid solution, and possible binary 
intermetallic compounds in the CoCuFeMnNi alloy system are calcu -
lated using Miedema's semi-empirical model for binary systems [31] and 
Extended Miedema's model [28] for the multicomponent systems. 
Values of Δ G and Δ H are calculated and presented in Table 3 . In fact, 
these values thermodynamically predict the formation driving force of 
each possible phase. Detail explanation of Miedema's semi-empirical 
model and Extended Miedema's model has been written elsewhere 
[32 – 35] . Based on the values of Table 3 , among all probable interme -
tallic compounds, amorphous phase, and solid solution, the solid solu -
tion of CoCuFeMnNi has the most negative value of Δ G in the 
CoCuFeMnNi alloy system, which will be the most favorable phase after 
mechanical alloying.

3.2. Synthesis by mechanical alloying 
3.2.1. X-ray diffraction analysis 
Fig. 2 indicates the X-ray diffraction patterns of the mechanically 
alloyed powders prepared under different ball milling durations. The 
pattern obtained from the 0 h powder mixture (Co, Cu, Fe, Mn, and Ni) is 
also shown for comparison. After 10 h milling, the diffraction peaks of 
the respective elements can still be observed. As the milling time 
increased, the intensity of the elemental peaks decreased. High energy 
ball milling caused the high stored energy in the grain boundaries, high 
mixing entropy, and expansion of the solid solubility range, all of which 
contribute to the formation of a single-phase FCC solid solution after 30 
h ball milling. Given the close size of the atomic radius of the constituent 
elements, at this stage, it makes sense to form a solid solution of 
randomly distributed elements with an ordered compact structure. As 
the milling time reached up to 50 h, the diffraction peaks indicated no 
alteration except for a slight broadening. Longer milling time can lead to 
the formation of a more homogeneous solid solution. Throughout the 
milling process, the reduction in intensity and peak broadening in the 
diffraction peaks can be attributed to high lattice strain and the for -
mation of nanocrystalline structure induced by mechanical deformation 
[22,39] . 
The crystallite size, lattice strain, and lattice parameter of CoCu -
FeMnNi alloy system powders were calculated from the X-ray peak 
broadening after different milling times using the Scherrer and Bragg 
equations [22] . As shown in Table 5 , the crystallite size in the FCC phase 
considerably reduced as the milling duration increased and reached to 
21 nm after 50 h milling. There was no significant alteration in crys -
tallite size as the milling time increased from 30 h to 50 h. The lattice 
strain of the CoCuFeMnNi HEA powders increased gradually as the 
milling continued. The 50 h milled alloy powder exhibited a lattice 
strain as high as 0.43 %. Fundamentally, the increase in lattice strain is 
ascribed to several factors, including the size mismatch effect between 
constituent elements, an increase in grain boundary fraction because of 
the ongoing decrease in the grain size, and the increasing dislocation 
density caused by severe plastic deformation during the mechanical 
alloying process [40] .

significant impact on the sample surface, causing more surface defor -
mation and leading to more weight losses [59] . In the normal load of 5

400

◦

delamination fracture results in the falling out of the debris from the 
worn surface. These observations indicate that the main wear mecha -
nism is delamination wear. By increasing the testing temperature to 
400

the CoCuFeMnNi HEA is softened, and more wear occurs than ambient 
temperature. Fundamentally, the hardness of the material and the 
testing temperature are the most crucial parameters affecting the 
tribological behavior of materials. As the temperature rises, the hardness 
and subsequently the abrasive wear resistance decreases due to soft -
ening [60] . In the sample tested at the temperature of 400 
◦

grinding ball's micro-asperities [38,65 – 67] . By increasing the testing 
temperature to 600 
◦

the surface damage has reduced, and the wear surfaces have become 
smoother, indicating improvement in the CoCuFeMnNi HEA's wear 
resistance. Regarding the effect of testing temperature on the tribolog -

searchers [58,60] . Considering the effect of the applied normal load, it 
can be seen that the worn surfaces in the normal load of 5 N are 
smoother compared to those in the normal load of 10 N. These results 
are consistent with weight loss and wear depth results.

tribological behavior of the CoCuFeMnNi HEA. As can be seen, the

resulting in a decrease in friction coefficient [59] . A decrease in the 
coefficient of friction by increasing the applied normal load has also 
been noticed in the tribological behavior of other HEAs and metallic 
materials, reported in various references [58,59,74] . 
4. Conclusions 
This paper describes the synthesis of magnetic CoCuFeMnNi HEA 
using mechanical alloying method and spark plasma sintering process. 
Thermodynamic prediction, magnetic and mechanical properties, and 
tribological behavior of the CoCuFeMnNi HEA were studied. The 
following conclusions can be derived: 
• Thermodynamic analysis showed that the solid solution state of the 
CoCuFeMnNi alloy system is the most stable state of this alloy system 
due to the highest negative Gibbs free energy ( 59.34 kJ/mol) in the 
solid solution state, in comparison to the amorphous state ( + 21.34 
kJ/mol), and all probable binary intermetallic compounds. 
• The CoCuFeMnNi HEA powder with FCC solid solution structure and 
average crystallite size of about 21 nm was successfully synthesized 
after 50 h mechanical alloying of elemental powders. 
• The CoCuFeMnNi HEA powder illustrates a soft magnetic behavior 
with 48 emu/g magnetic saturation (M
S
) and 15 Oe coercivity field 
(H
C
) in 1 T (T) applied magnetic field. 
• The CoCuFeMnNi HEA bulk sample with the highest relative density 
of 97 % was fabricated by SPS at the temperature of 850 
◦
C for 8 min 
under an applied pressure of 40 MPa. 
• The CoCuFeMnNi HEA, SPSed at 850 
◦
C, had the highest average 
microhardness of about 340 ± 5 HV due to its denser microstructure. 
Moreover, the shear punch test for this sample showed a shear 
strength of 320 ± 18 MPa. 
• The wear rate of the CoCuFeMnNi HEA increased from room tem -

◦

CoCuFeMnNi HEA at 600

ture applications. 
• SEM micrographs of worn surfaces revealed a transition in the pre -
dominant wear mechanism from delamination wear at room tem -
perature to oxidative and delamination wear at elevated 
temperatures.
