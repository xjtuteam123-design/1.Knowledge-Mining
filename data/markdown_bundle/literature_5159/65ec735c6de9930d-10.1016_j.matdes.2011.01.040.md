abstract
The effects of 2 wt.% rare earth element addition on the microstructure evolution, thermal stability and
shear strength of AZ91 alloy were investigated in the as-cast and annealed conditions. The as-cast struc-
ture of AZ91 consists of
a-Mg matrix and the b-Mg17Al12 intermetallic phase. Due to the low thermal sta-
bility of this phase, the strength of AZ91 signiﬁcantly decreased as the temperature increased. The
addition of rare earth elements reﬁned the microstructure and improved both thermal stability and
high-temperature mechanical properties of AZ91. This was documented by the retention of the initial ﬁne
microstructure and ultimate shear strength (USS) of the rare earth elements-containing material after
long-term annealing at 420 /C176C. The improved stability and strength are attributed to the reduction in
the volume fraction of b-Mg
17Al12 and retention of the thermally stable Al 11RE3 intermetallic particles
which can hinder grain growth during the annealing process. This behavior is in contrast to that of the
base material which developed a coarse grain structure with decreased strength caused by the dissolu-
tion of b-Mg
17Al12 after exposure to high temperature.

1. Introduction
Great attention has been paid to Mg–Al alloys for their excellent
combination of superior castability, good corrosion resistance and
acceptable mechanical properties. The AZ91 alloy containing
9 wt.% Al and 1 wt.% Zn is the most commonly used cast magne-
sium alloy for low-temperature applications. The high aluminum
content of this alloy results in the massive formation of b-Mg17Al12
precipitates along grain boundaries [1,2]. Due to their low melting
point, these precipitates are thermally unstable and thus they can
accelerate grain boundary diffusion, resulting in considerable
instability of the microstructure in regions adjacent to grain
boundaries [3,4]. This has been recognized as the main cause for
deterioration of high-temperature mechanical properties, making
the AZ91 magnesium alloy unsuitable for applications involving
high temperature and load [5].
Most of the attempts for improving the high-temperature struc-
tural stability of this alloy have concentrated on suppressing
discontinuous precipitation of Mg 17Al12 and creating thermally
stable intermetallics in the magnesium matrix. Due to the high
afﬁnity of aluminum atoms for elements such as Ca, Ti, Zr, and rare
earth (RE), it is possible to reduce the amount of the Mg
17Al12 by
consuming the aluminum content of the alloy to form Al-contain-
ing intermetallic compounds with relatively high melting points
[6–9]. The Mg–Al–RE alloys contain a mixture of RE elements, pro-
ducing thermally stable Al 11RE3 precipitates. The formation of
these branch-shape compounds together with the low diffusion
rate of RE elements in Mg matrix can explain the excellent
mechanical resistance of the RE-containing Mg–Al alloys at ele-
vated temperatures [10,11].
Pettersen et al. [10] studied the effect of 1 wt.% of RE on the
microstructure of Mg–4Al. Two different kinds of particles, Al 11RE3
and Al 10RE2Mn7, were identiﬁed at grain boundaries. Combining
aluminum with RE atoms lowered the level of solute aluminum in
Mg matrix in as-cast condition and consequently reduced the driv-
ing force for discontinuous precipitation of Mg 17Al12 during creep.
Addition of 1 and 3 wt.% RE to AZ91–0.3Ca has shown that creep
resistance at 473 K had an ascending trend with increasing RE per-
cent, the best high temperature properties being obtained for
3 wt.% RE elements [11]. Similar results have been obtained for
high-temperature mechanical properties of RE-containing AM60,
AZ91, and AE42 alloys [12–15]. Improvement in high temperature
strength has been ascribed to the formation of the high melting
point Al11RE3 particles as strong barriers to grain boundary sliding.
However, coarsening of these particles due to increasing RE content
has been introduced as the major cause for reduction of ultimate
tensile strength [11–13]. Nami et al. [16] studied simultaneous
addition of 1 wt.% La-rich misch-metal and 1 wt.% Ca to AZ91. They
reported superior creep resistance of the RE- and Ca-containing al-
loy with respect to the base alloy. Effects of adding different types of
RE elements (Ce, Nd and Y) on the room temperature mechanical

The aim of this study was to investigate the effects of 2 wt.% RE

tures. It was also intended to examine the microstructural

by using the shear punch testing (SPT) technique, which has been
recently attempted for some cast [7,18,19] and wrought [20] mag-

ing strength data which are well correlated with those found by
the conventional tensile tests [21,22]. Moreover, this method ben-
eﬁts from simpler sample preparation and signiﬁcant material sav-
ing owing to small sample sizes, as compared to the conventional
tensile test.
2. Experimental procedure
2.1. Materials and processing
The materials used were AZ91 alloy with an actual chemical
composition of Mg–8.9 wt.% Al–0.78 wt.% Zn–0.32 wt.% Mn, and
the same material with 2.1 wt.% RE addition. High purity Mg
(99.90 wt.%), Al (99.90 wt.%), Zn (99.95 wt.%), Mn (99.95 wt.%),
and a misch-metal mainly composed of 71.5 wt.% La, 10.2 wt.%
Ce, 6.5 wt.% Nd, and 11.8 wt.% Pr were used to prepare the alloys.
Melting was carried out in an electrical furnace under the Foseco
MAGREX 36 covering ﬂux to protect molten magnesium from oxi-
dation. The melt was held at 750 /C176C for 20 min and mechanically
stirred for 2 min using a stainless steel rod to ensure a homogenous
composition. The melt was then poured into a mild steel mould
preheated to 150 /C176C, using a tilt-casting system to minimize the
melt turbulence. The cast bars had dimensions of 13 mm /C2 30
mm /C2 200 mm, from which 1 mm /C2 13 mm /C2 30 mm slices were
cut by an electrodischarge wire-cut machine for shear punch test-
ing (SPT) and structural characterization.
2.2. Microstructural characterization
Some of the cast specimens were annealed at 420 /C176C for 4 and
24 h to study the thermal stability of the alloys. After annealing,
the specimens were cooled in air. Microstructure of the alloys in
as-cast and annealed conditions was studied by optical microscopy
to reveal the effect of RE addition on the microstructure and its
thermal stability. The cast samples were also studied by scanning
electron microscopy to examine the as-cast microstructure as well
as to identify the phases present in the microstructure. The speci-
mens were polished with 0.3- lm alumina powder and distilled
water. Etching was carried out using an acetic picric etchant
(10 ml acetic acid, 10 ml distilled H 2O, 4.2 g picric acid and 70 ml
ethanol) for 3–5 s at room temperature. X-ray diffraction (XRD)
analysis was carried out on selected specimens to identify the
existing phases. In these tests, polished samples were exposed to
Cu K a radiation ( k = 1.54056 Å) using a scanning speed of 2 /C176/min.
The energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) analysis was performed
to reveal the concentration of alloying elements in selected areas
of the microstructure.
2.3. Shear strength measurements
In order to study the effects of RE addition and annealing treat-
ments on the mechanical properties of the alloys, shear strength
was studied by SPT. The 1-mm thick slices of the materials an-
nealed for 0, 4, and 24 h were ground to a thickness of 0.7 mm,
from which disks of 10 mm in diameter were punched for the
SPT. A shear punch ﬁxture with a 3.175 mm diameter ﬂat cylindri-
cal punch and 3.225 mm diameter receiving-hole was used for this
experiment. Shear punch tests were performed in the temperature
range 25–250 /C176C using a screw driven SANTAM material testing
system with a load cell of 20 kN capacity and a crosshead speed
of 0.25 mm/min. After application of the load, the applied load P
was measured automatically as a function of punch displacement;
the data were acquired by a computer so as to determine the shear
stress of the tested materials using the relationship [23].

of the more thermally stable Al 11RE3 phase with a high melting

after the addition of 1 wt.% RE to AZ91 has shown about 60% reduc-

the thermally stable Al 11RE3 particles are distributed inside the
grains as well as in the grain boundary areas. Therefore, they
strengthen both grains and grain boundaries and oppose both
recovery and recrystallization processes during deformation at ele-
vated temperatures, increasing the shear strength of the materials.
This is achieved by obstructing the dislocation annihilation and
thus restricting the slip inside the grains, and also by inhibiting
the grain boundary migration or grain boundary sliding during
deformation.
Further examination of the effect of RE addition on the thermal
stability can be made through the study of its impact on shear
strength of the materials under different processing conditions,
as depicted in Fig. 9 a–c. The results of variation in USS with tem-
perature for the as-cast condition, presented in Fig. 9 a, indicate
that although both materials have similar strengths at room tem-
perature, the strength of AZ91 drops at a higher rate as the temper-
ature increases. The same is true for the alloys annealed for 4 h, as
shown in Fig. 9 b. The only difference is that due to the lower vol-
ume fraction of the Mg 17Al12 phase in the RE-containing alloy, this
material can reasonably maintain its strength up to 250 /C176C. For the
24-h annealed materials in Fig. 9c, there is a wide gap between the
strength curves of the AZ91 and AZ91 + 2RE alloys at all tempera-
tures. In these conditions, there is almost no Mg 17Al12 phase pres-
ent in either of the alloys, and thus both materials behave in a
similar way up to the high temperature of 200 /C176C. At higher tem-
peratures, however, there is a steep drop in the strength of AZ91,
which might have been caused by excessive grain growth. The
RE-containing alloy, however, can better maintain its strength by
resisting against softening at high temperature.
A comparison of the results obtained in the present work with
those reported in the literature indicates that the addition of RE
elements, both in pure form and as misch-metal, improves
mechanical properties at elevated temperatures. There is an opti-
mum level for the added RE elements, for which the highest
strength is achieved. Above this optimum concentration, the
Al11RE3 particles coarsen [13] and lose their effectiveness in
enhancing mechanical properties. The optimum content of the RE
depends on the type of added elements. For example, for pure Ce
this has been reported to be 1 wt.% [27] and 1.5 wt.% [17], while
for the Ce-rich and La-rich misch-metals the best combination of
mechanical and creep properties have been obtained for 2 wt.%
addition [8,15]. Irrespective of the type of the RE elements, sup-
pression of Mg 17Al12 formation, reﬁnement of the residues of this
phase, and formation of Al 11RE3 particles are known mechanisms
of enhancement in high-temperature mechanical properties of
the cast Mg–Al alloys.
4. Conclusions
(1) The as-cast microstructure of AZ91 consists of Mg 17Al12
intermetallic phase in the Mg matrix, which experienced
extensive grain coarsening during exposure to high temper-
ature. Addition of 2 wt.% RE elements reﬁned the dendritic
as-cast microstructure, suppressed the formation of Mg17Al12
phase, and encouraged the formation of the Al 11RE3 phase.
(2) The higher thermal stability of AZ91 + 2RE alloy can be
attributed to the formation of the acicular branch-shape
Al11RE3 particles, which are thermally stable barriers oppos-
ing grain coarsening during high temperature annealing.
This behavior is in sharp contrast with that of the AZ91 base
alloy which underwent a drastic grain growth after the same
annealing regime.
(3) The effect of microstructural stability on the mechanical
properties of the alloys at high temperatures and after expo-
sure to long-term annealing was investigated by SPT. The
results showed that addition of RE elements to the AZ91
alloy considerably increases the strength of the alloy in all
conditions. Reﬁnement of the microstructure, hardening by
the Al 11RE3 particles, and the high thermal stability of this
phase, which can resist softening and dissolution during
