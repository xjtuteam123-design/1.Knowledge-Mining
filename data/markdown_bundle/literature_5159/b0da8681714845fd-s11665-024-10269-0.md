ORIGINAL RESEARCH ARTICLE
The Evolution of Tensile Mechanical Properties
and Deformation Mechanism of GH4720Li Nickel-Based
Alloy with Temperature
Xiaocan Wen, Xinyu Meng, Shaomin Lyu, Xingfei Xie, Jinglong Qu, and Jinhui Du
Submitted: 28 March 2024 / Revised: 24 June 2024 / Accepted: 15 July 2024 / Published online: 25 October 2024
GH4720Li nickel-based superalloy is widely used in the manufacture of key components such as aeroengine
turbine disks and blades for their stable high-temperature mechanical properties, good fatigue and creep
resistance, as well as excellent corrosion and oxidation resistance. However, the research on the evolution of
plastic deformation mechanism with temperature is lacking. In this paper, the evolution of tensile
mechanical properties of GH4720Li alloy at four different temperatures was studied, and the plastic
deformation mechanism was analyzed in detail. It is found that the yield strength and tensile strength of
GH4720Li alloy decrease slowly with increasing temperature below 600 /C176C. When the temperature rises
above 600 /C176C, the yield strength and tensile strength of the alloy decrease signiﬁcantly, which is mainly
related to the strengthening effect of the c’ strengthening phase that increases ﬁrst and then decreases with
the increase in temperature, while the strength of the c matrix decreases with the increase in temperature.
Further deformation mechanism analysis shows that the plastic deformation mechanism of the alloy
gradually changes from dislocation cross-slip to stacking fault and twin with the increase in temperature,
and the transformation of the plastic deformation mechanism may be related to the change of stacking fault
energy with temperature.
Keywords GH4720Li superalloy, grain size, high-temperature
tensile property, microstructure
1. Introduction
GH4720Li is a nickel-base superalloy with high strength,
corrosion resistance and high-temperature endurance which
was initially employed for land-based turbine blades and then
used in turbine disks in the temperature range of 650–750 /C176 C
for long-time service and at 900 /C176 C for short time use (Ref 1-9).
As a precipitation strengthened alloy, it contains high volume
fractions of L12 ordered c¢ (Ni
3Al, Ti) precipitates as high as
40-50%(Ref 10-17). Trimodal distributions of c¢ phases with
irregular primary c¢ phases, butterﬂy-like or square-like sec-
ondary c¢ phases and spherical tertiary c¢ phases also contribute
high strength (Ref 1, 18-22).
Researchers have carried out a lot of research work on
GH4720Li nickel-based superalloy at different temperatures.
Liu et al. studied the thermal deformation behavior GH4720Li
alloy with coarse, ﬁne and mixed grain size in the temperature
range of 1040-1190 /C176 C by thermal compression experiment.
The results showed that in the single-phase region, dynamic
recrystallization mainly occurred along the grain boundaries.
The decrease in grain size accelerates the dynamic recrystal-
lization kinetics, but in the dual-phase region, the dynamic
recrystallization at the grain boundaries of coarse crystals is
limited (Ref 3). Y u et al. studied the thermal compression
mechanical behavior of GH4720Li alloy in the temperature
range of 1070 /C176 C-1190 /C176 C and established the relationship
between dynamic recrystallization and c’ precipitated phase. It
is found that for coarse crystals, the nucleation of dynamic
recrystallized grains is affected by the spacing of c’ precipitates
(Ref 23). Monajati et al. studied the high-temperature defor-
mation behavior of Udimet 720 superalloy in the temperature
range of 1000 /C176 C–1175 /C176 C and in the strain rate range of 10
/C03–
1s /C01 through thermal compression experiments, ﬁnding that a
uniform deformation was observed when testing between
1100 /C176 C and 1150 /C176 C with dynamic recrystallization as the
major ﬂow softening mechanism above 1125 /C176 C (Ref 21).
At present, the mechanical properties of GH4720Li alloy
mainly focus on the high-temperature stage, and the compres-
sion behavior is the main performance. The research on the
tensile mechanical properties at medium–low-temperature stage
and related deformation mechanism is few. Considering that the
main service temperature of GH4720Li alloy is below 750 /C176 C,
it is of great signiﬁcance to study the tensile mechanical
behavior and the evolution of the deformation mechanism of
GH4720Li alloy in the medium–low-temperature stage.

In this work, the tensile mechanical properties and the
plastic deformation mechanism of GH4720Li alloy at four
temperatures (23 /C176 C, 400 /C176 C, 600 /C176 C and 800 /C176 C) were studied
by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and transmission
electron microscopy (TEM). This study can not only provide an
in-depth understanding of the tensile mechanical behavior of
GH4720Li alloy in the medium–low-temperature range, but
also provide a reference for further optimizing the mechanical
behavior of GH4720Li alloy by regulation the deformation
mechanism in the future.
2. Materials and Methods
GH4720Li alloy ingot was made by vacuum induction
melting, electroslag remelting and vacuum consumable melt-
ing. Then, the ingot casting undergoes homogenization treat-
ment at 1180 /C176 C for 4 h and hammer cogging process. The
solution treatment at 1100 /C176 C for 4 h and aging treatment at 650
/C176 C for 24 h + 760 /C176 C for 16 h were carried out on the forged
structure. The main chemical composition of GH4720Li alloy
is shown in Table 1. The tensile properties of GH4720Li alloy
at four different temperatures were tested. The size of the
tensile test sample is shown in Fig. 1. For each specimen, at
least 3 measurements were carried out to ensure the accuracy of
the data.
Microstructures under various deformation conditions were
examined using optical microscopy (OM), scanning electron
microscopy (SEM), the electron backscattered diffraction
(EBSD) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM). OM
and SEM were used for the grain microstructural and c¢ phases
observation. TEM was used for the further detailed microstruc-
tural observation.
The optical microscope (OM) sample was prepared by
mechanical polishing method. After mechanical grinding and
polishing, the sample was subjected to chemical corrosion in
3 g CuCl
2 +2 0m LH C l+3 0m LC 2H5OH mixed solution for
10–15 s. The scanning electron microscope (SEM) samples
were prepared by electrolytic polishing with polishing reagent
of 20 mL HCl + 80 mL CH
3OH, polishing voltage of 15–
20 V , polishing time of 3 /C245 s and then electrolytic corrosion
with corrosion reagent of 170 mL H 3PO4 +1 0m LH 2SO4 +
15 g CrO 3, corrosion voltage of 3–5 V , corrosion time of 1–
2 s. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) was performed
using a FEI-Talos F200X microscope coupled with a EDS
detector operating at a voltage of 200 KV . Electron transparent
disks for TEM analysis were prepared using a twin-jet electro-
polisher. The electrolyte containing a mixture of 90% methanol
and 10% perchloric acid (vol%) was used at /C030 /C176 C and at a
voltage of 20 V .
In addition, the sampling locations for initial OM and SEM
microstructures observations at the test specimen only undergo
heat treatment process, while the sampling locations for both
EBSD and TEM deformed microstructural observations were
near the fractography of the tensile sample after tensile tests.
3. Results
The initial microstructures of GH4720Li superalloy before
tensile test are presented in Fig. 2. The average grain size of
was determined to be about 13 lm, as shown in Fig. 2a. This is
attributed to the grain boundary pinning effect originating from
the formation of primary c¢ precipitates at c grain boundaries,
as shown in Fig. 2b. Secondary c¢ precipitates and ternary c¢
precipitates were precipitated inside c grains, as shown in
Fig. 2c–d. While the tertiary c¢ precipitates are spherical,
secondary c¢ precipitates are irregularly shaped but with
rounded edges. The size, morphology and distribution of all
microstructural features observed in the material under study
agreed well with those reported earlier (Ref 23–30).
Figure 3(a) shows the tensile engineering stress–strain
curves of GH4720Li alloy at different temperatures. The
variation of tensile strength, yield strength and elongation with
temperature is shown in Fig. 3(b–d). The speciﬁc mechanical
properties value is shown in Table 2. It can be seen that with the
temperature increase from 23 /C176 C to 600 /C176 C, the tensile strength
and yield strength of GH4720Li alloy decrease slowly from
1577 MPa to 1510 MPa and from 1188 MPa to 1109 MPa,
respectively, while with the temperature further increase to
800/C176 C, the tensile strength and yield strength of alloy decrease
signiﬁcantly to 910 MPa and 804 MPa, respectively. In
addition, as the temperature increases from room temperature
to 800 /C176 C, the elongation of the alloy gradually increases from
21% to 27%.
Figure 4 shows the EBSD inverse pole ﬁgure (IPF) maps of
GH4720Li alloy samples deformed to fracture at different
temperatures. It can be seen that the specimens deformed at
four different temperatures have ﬁne grains with uniform
orientation distribution, and no obvious texture was observed.
However, the internal orientation difference of the grain is
obvious, which indicates that plastic deformation occurs inside
the grain. Compared with the microstructure before tensile test
(Fig. 2), the grain size of the deformed sample at different
temperatures remained unchanged, which could be attributed to
that the grain size of GH4720Li alloy is mainly controlled by
the c’ phase pinning at the grain boundary. In the test
temperature range of this study, the highest temperature is
800/C176 C, which is far lower than the remelting temperature of the
c’ phase. Therefore, deformation in the temperature range of
23–800/C176 C had no effect on the volume fraction of c’ phase, and

Figure 5 shows the EBSD kernel average misorientation
(KAM) diagram of GH4720Li alloy before tensile deformation
and tensile to fracture at different temperatures. The average
KAM value, as a signature of a local misorientation, reveals the
density of geometrically necessary dislocations (GNDs), which
are generally generated to accommodate the strain gradient
upon heterogeneous deformation (Ref 32, 33). It can be seen
that there is geometric dislocation density, that is, the accumu-
lation of strain in GH4720Li alloy samples before tensile test
(Fig. 5a), which may be related to the accumulation of internal
stress during the preparation of the alloy. Figure 5 b-d shows
the KAM diagram of GH4720Li alloy deformed to failure at
different temperatures. It can be seen that the local misorien-
tation in the deformed samples increases with the increase in
temperature, indicating that the density of geometrically
necessary dislocations gradually increases, which is consistent
with the increasing trend of tensile plasticity of the alloys with
the increase in temperature (Fig. 3d). Further analysis reveals
that the local misorientation preferentially forms near grain
boundaries (GBs), implying the pileup of GNDs at these sites.

Figure 8a–c shows the microstructure of GH4720Li alloy

4. Discussion
It has been reported that the yield stress of c’ strengthened
phase in superalloy increases ﬁrst and then decreases with
increasing temperature, and the yield stress of c matrix
decreases as the temperature increases (Ref 31). Therefore,
the tensile strength and yield strength of GH4720Li alloy
decrease slightly with the temperature increases from room
temperature to 600 /C176 C. However, when the temperature further
increases above 600 /C176 C, the strengthening effect of c’ precip-
itated phase is weakened, and the strength of c matrix is further
reduced, resulting in a signiﬁcant decrease in the yield strength
and tensile strength of the alloy. In addition, with the increase in
temperature, the dislocations motion was intenser and the

In a general way, the plastic deformation mechanism such as
dislocation slip and twins is generally related to the phase
stability or stacking fault energy of the material. With the
decrease in phase stability or stacking fault energy, the
deformation mechanism often transforms from dislocation slip
to twin and other mechanisms (Ref 34-36). In this study, the
deformation mechanism of GH4720Li alloy changes from
dislocation cross-slip at room temperature to dislocation planar
slip at 400 /C176 C, and then to nanotwin and stacking fault
mechanism at 600 /C176 C, which may be related to the decrease in
the stacking fault energy of the alloy with the increase in
temperature.
5. Conclusion
(1) The yield stress of c’ strengthened phase in GH4720Li
alloy increases and the strength of c matrix decreases
with the temperature increase from 23 /C176 C to 600 /C176 C.

(3) TEM microstructure analysis shows that the plastic
deformation mechanism of the alloy gradually changes
from dislocation cross-slip to nanotwins and stacking
fault with the increase in temperature. The transition of
the deformation mechanism with temperature in
GH4720Li alloy may be related to the evolution of the
stacking fault energy.
