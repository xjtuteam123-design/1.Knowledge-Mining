1. Introduction 
Overcoming the strength-ductility trade-off in high-entropy alloys 
(HEAs) is challenging [ 1 – 3 ]. Besides well-studied methods such as 
microstructural refinement [ 4 – 6 ], solid-solution strengthening [ 7 ], and 
precipitation hardening [ 8 – 10 ], novel strategies including tuning the 
short-range order [ 8 , 11 ], tailoring the stacking fault energy (SFE) to -
ward triggering phase transformation/deformation twinning [ 12 – 14 ], 
and introducing a heterogeneous structure [ 15 , 16 ] including gradient 
hierarchical structure [ 17 , 18 ] have been utilized to develop 
high-performance HEAs [ 2 ]. In this regard, benefiting from meta -
stability by phase transformation strengthening and 
transformation-induced plasticity (TRIP) effect leads to high strength 
and remarkable ductility [ 19 – 21 ]. 
By introducing the HEA concept, design strategies toward 
metastability engineering have been widely extended [ 22 ]. There are 
numerous studies and developed alloy systems about the occurrence of 
considerable TRIP effect by phase transformation of FCC ( γ -austenite) to 
HCP ( ε -martensite) at room temperature [ 23 , 24 ]. Notably, the FCC 
( γ -austenite) to BCC ( α ΄ -martensite) phase transformation, which is 
called the BCC-TRIP effect in this work, is more beneficial for enhancing 
work-hardening capacity [ 23 , 25 ]. The BCC-TRIP effect typically occurs 
in the Fe-rich HEAs, particularly at cryogenic deformation temperatures 
[ 26 – 29 ]. Nonetheless, this subject is in its infancy and there are a few 
recent publications about room-temperature BCC-TRIP effect in specific 
HEA systems [ 23 – 25 , 30 – 33 ]. According to the successful experience of 
metastable austenitic stainless steels, the classical HEA design strategy 
benefits from decreasing phase stability and SFE at room temperature by 
adjusting the contents of FCC stabilizers such as Mn and Ni [ 31 , 32 ]. In 
this regard, Yang et al. [ 31 ] developed a Fe

5. Summary 
Metastability engineering was used to fine-tune the values of Δ G 
FCC → HCP
, Δ G 
FCC → BCC
, and SFE by replacing Mn with Ni and adjusting the 
Si content in the BCC-TRIP FeCoCrVMnSi system for designing a single- 
phase HEA with a remarkable BCC-TRIP effect up to high strains, leading 
to the brake-up of the strength-ductility trade-off. It was found that a 
high amount of Si (~6 at%) is desirable to not only promote the BCC- 
TRIP effect but also strengthen the BCC crystal structure; while the 
presence of Ni is required to retain the initially fully austenitic structure 
and control the rate of the TRIP effect to maintain it up to high strains. 
The results were supported by the thermodynamics calculations, XRD, 
and microstructural analyses based on EBSD and TEM. In summary,
