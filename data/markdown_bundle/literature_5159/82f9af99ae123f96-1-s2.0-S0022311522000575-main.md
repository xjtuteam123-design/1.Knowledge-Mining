FeCrAl alloy has great application potential to replace traditional zirconium alloy as an accident-tolerant 
fuel (ATF) candidate material. In this study, Fe-13Cr-4Al-2Mo-0.65Nb-0.4Ta alloy was prepared into a thin- 
walled tube with a diameter of 9.5 mm and a wall thickness of 0.35 mm by cold rolling. The effects of 
different annealing temperatures on the microstructure morphology, texture growth direction, and me- 
chanical properties of FeCrAl alloy were studied., alloy underwent recovery and recrystallization with 
increasing annealing temperature, and the grain size increased. The theoretical calculation showed that 
the pinning action of Fe2Mo4C and Fe2Nb increased the dislocation density, and the interaction with 
the dislocation increased the room temperature strength. The addition of Ta led to the formation of more 
second-phase precipitates at the grain boundary, which hindered the growth and reﬁned grains. The high- 
temperature stable second phase provided suﬃcient temperature strength for the alloy.

1. Introduction 
FeCrAl ferrite alloy is originally used as heat-resistant materi- 
als in high-temperature environments. Examples of these materials 
include steam heat transfer tube [1] and high-temperature electric 
furnace wire [2] due to the excellent oxidation resistance of Fe- 
CrAl (Cr 2 O 3 is formed at temperatures below 1273 K and α -Al 2 O 3 
is formed at temperatures above 1273 K [3–6] ). After the concept 
of accident-tolerant fuel was proposed [7] , the microstructure and 
processing properties of FeCrAl alloys are obtained by adjusting the 
composition of 10–13%wt to meet the ATF concept of the cladding 
tube performance (when Cr content is higher than 13% wt, α phase 
will precipitate, resulting in brittleness at 475 ◦C) and 4–6%wt Al 
[8–10] . The addition of Y and other reactive elements results in 
better adhesion of the α-Al 2 O 3 oxidation protective layer [11–13] ; 
moreover, Mo, Nb, and Ta confer the Fe-bcc matrix with solid so- 
lution strengthening and microstructure optimization. 
As a cladding tube, FeCrAl alloy must have excellent machin- 
ability, and the presence of Al makes the alloys easy to crack dur- 
ing casting and to roll [ 9 , 13 , 14 ], so a set of mature process routes 
is one of the keys to producing a seamless ﬁnished thin-walled

2. Experimental materials and method 
FeCrAl alloy with Ta was investigated, and the theoretical mass 
fraction of each element is listed in Table 1 . Rolling process all the 
alloy powder was vacuum-melted and then cast into ingots. After 
homogenization and solution treatment, the ingot was forged and 
rolled ﬁve times. The ingot is annealed at 800 °C for 30 min after 
each forging to reduce stress and defects and ensure that the tem- 
perature of the ingot during the forging process is not less than 
600 °C. Finally, the ingot was prepared by a drawing process into 
a full-size thin-walled tube with a length of about 40 0 0 mm, a di- 
ameter of 9.5 mm, and a wall thickness of about 0.35 mm. 
After passing the ultrasonic detection, the full-size tube was 
cut into a short tube of 10 0 0 mm for the experiment. Specimens 
were divided into two groups and annealed at 700 ◦C (to eliminate 
stress) and 800 ◦C (to complete recrystallization) for 1 h. The sam- 
ples were then placed in the air and cooled to 25 ◦C. The specimen 
of N1 untreated is deﬁned as S1, the sample of N1 annealed at 
700 ◦C is deﬁned as S2, and the sample of N1 annealed at 800 ◦C 
is deﬁned as S3. The specimens of S2 and S3 were aged at 400 ◦C 
from 1 to 240 h to verify the abnormal mechanical properties. 
Metallographic specimens were cut by wire cut electric dis- 
charge with a length of 10 mm and then cut into a semicircu- 
lar tube along the RD direction. The metallography specimen was 
ﬁxed in epoxy resin, ground to 20 0 0# with sandpaper, and pol- 
ished with Alpha-202 double-disk and double-control grinder. The 
polishing paste was W1 and W0.5 diamond polishing paste. The 
metallographic corrosion solution was FeCl 3 : HCl: H 2 O = 50 g: 
30 mL: 70 ml. The specimen was electrolytically polished in 
8%HClO 4 –C 2 H 6 O solution to determine laves-phase particles. A fo- 
cused ion beam (FIB) was used for TEM preparation. Tissue mor-

(SEM), DX-2700B X-ray diffraction (XRD), electron backscattered

3.1. Microstructure and morphology 
The microscopic ultra-depth images show the FeCrAl alloy’s 
metallographic state along with the RD and TD directions. As 
shown in Fig. 2 (d), the cold-rolled tube S1 exhibits typical stream- 
lined grains, which are ﬂat and have a large aspect ratio. S2 ap- 
pears as recovered grains after stress-relief annealing, although it is 
still streamlined grains and the aspect ratio decreases [ Fig. 2 (b,e)]. 
As the annealing temperature increases [ Fig. 2 (c,f)], the stream- 
lined grains in the S3 sample disappear completely and are re- 
placed by recrystallized grains appearing as equiaxed crystals. 
The EBSD results are shown in Fig. 3 , and the IPF maps of S1–
S3 are presented. As shown in Fig. 3 (a), the microstructure of the 
rolled alloy is composed of thin and streamlined grains along the 
RD direction. The strong texture of α-Fe characterizes the grain 
orientation of the cold-rolled FeCrAl thin-walled tube ( < 110 > //RD) 
and weak texture of γ-ﬁber ( < 111 > /ND) and θ-ﬁber ( < 100 > //ND), 
similar to most of the bcc alloys. However, the difference is that 
the proportion of γ-ﬁber in the plate seems to be higher than that 
in the tube, and the proportion of α-ﬁber in the plate is lower than 
that in the tube. With increasing annealing temperature and the 
progress of recrystallization, the partial annealing texture changes 
from α-ﬁber to γ-ﬁber and θ-ﬁber compared with the cold-rolled 
texture. For example, the area fractions of α-ﬁber and γ-ﬁber of 
the cold-rolled microstructure are 78.3 and 4.18%. After annealing 
at 700 ◦C for 1 h, the area fraction becomes 61.6% and 9.4%, re- 
spectively. As the annealing temperature increases to 800 ◦C, the 
fractions of the grains with ⟨ 110 ⟩ and ⟨ 111 ⟩ become 65.8 and 6.4%.

FeCrAl alloy matrix. Compared with the original rolled state, the 
annealing treatment allows the growth of laves-phase particles. 
As shown in Fig. 3 (b), the average particle size of laves phase is 
larger than that of the rolled state. Smaller precipitation phases 
are dispersed in the recrystallized grains. The size of laves-phase 
particles at the boundary of the recrystallized grains is generally 
larger, indicating that the laves phase pinning action of larger par- 
ticles hinders the migration of grain boundary during recrystalliza- 
tion. At the same time, the alloying elements precipitate within 
the grain to form a new Laves phase, which gradually grows and 
merges into larger particles under the action of high temperature 
and grain boundary migration. However, as the recrystallization 
process slows down, the migration of driving force is reduced, and 
the laves-phase particles are dispersed in the grain. The structures 
of precipitated particles were further analyzed. TEM analysis was 
performed on S3. The high-angle annular dark-ﬁeld (HAADF) im- 
ages of Fe, Cr, Al, Nb, Mo, and Ta and the corresponding compo- 
sitions diagrams are shown in Fig. 5 (i). The contents of Nb, Mo, 
Ta, and Al in the precipitated phase particles are higher than those 
in the matrix. The contents of Fe and Cr are lower. The composi- 
tion of the precipitated particles was analyzed by EDS point scan- 
ning. The elements of the 1# point are Fe:54.73, Cr:9.36, Al:10.80, 
Nb:14.07, Mo:4.46, and Ta:4.48 (at.%), and the elements of 2# point

Fe:(Nb + Mo + Ta) = 2.37 and 2.11, respectively. This result is close 
to the atomic ratio of the Fe 2 Nb-type laves phase ( Fig. 6 ).

research in future explorations. Therefore, this study is only a brief

specimen of 25 ◦C. As shown in Fig. 7 (c) is the shear modulus of 
the alloy, it can be seen that the shear modulus of the alloy before

even at 900 ◦C, the yield strength of the specimens is higher than 
20 MPa, which shows that the FeCrAl alloy can maintain stabil- 
ity for a certain period even in the event of water loss. The post- 
fracture elongation of all specimens is higher than 15%.

4.2. Tensile properties 
The main factors affecting material strength are crystal (solute 
atoms, precipitation, and grain boundaries) in the material. These

4.3. High-temperature thermal stability 
The second-phase particle strengthening of microalloying- 
reinforced low-carbon steel is the main component of its high- 
temperature strengthening mechanism, and the working tem- 
perature of the light-water nuclear reactor is about 400 ◦C. 
Fig. 15 shows the changes in particle direct and area fraction af- 
ter aging from 1 to 240 h. The diameter of the particle is between 
0.35 and 0.5 μm. After aging for 240 h, the average diameter of 
the particles tends to be 0.38 μm, and the area fraction of the 
particles tends to be stable (4.7% for S2 and 3.6% for S3). This ﬁnd- 
ing indicates that the second-phase particles have good stability 
at 400 ◦C, and the precipitation strengthening mechanism can ef- 
fectively strengthen the sample. According to the calculation, the 
strength provided by the second-phase particles at 400 ◦C accounts 
for about 6.5 and 12.59% of the total yield strength, and the pro- 
portion increases to 80% when the temperature rises to 800 ◦C. 
The high-temperature mechanical properties of micro-alloyed 
mild steel mainly depend on the second-phase particles in the al- 
loy. Fig. 16 (a,b) shows the laves-phase particle morphology of S2 
and S3 after the tensile test at 900 ◦C. In high-temperature ten- 
sile holding, the second-phase particles grow up (compared with 
the samples before tensile holding; the average particle sizes of S2 
and S3 increase by 17% and 7.5%, respectively; and the maximum 
particle diameters are 1.62 and 1.65 μm, respectively), and more 
second-phase particles are precipitated. The pinging effect of the 
second-phase particles inhibits the elongation and growth of the 
grains (no obvious grain growth compared with that before ten- 
sile) and the growth and consolidation of cracks during the ten- 
sile process, thereby increasing the high-temperature strength of 
the alloy. The yield strength of FeCrAl at 900 ◦C (about 25 MPa) 
is higher than the actual working stress. As shown in Fig 16 (c–e), 
after the alloy was annealed at 800 °C for 10 h, a laves-free phase 
region appeared in the grain, and more laves phases were gathered 
at the grain boundary, and an elongated rod-like lavas phase was 
observed at the grain boundary. There are a large number of rod- 
like lavas phases at the grain boundary after annealing at 900 °C 
for 1 h, and the number of leaves phases in the grain is reduced 
after the annealing time is increased to 3 h, and there are still a 
large number of rod-like laws phases at the grain boundary. This 
phenomenon is due to the grain boundary migration so that the 
lavas phase particles migrate and merge to form a large particle 
of the lavas phase, resulting in a decrease in the number of sub- 
marine lavas phase particles, the formation of a non-precipitation 
phase region within the crystalline, while the precipitation at the 
grain boundary to form a new rod-like laves hinders the migration 
of the grain boundary. Ta has a similar effect with Zr, which can

5. Conclusions 
This study investigated the microstructure and mechanical 
properties of as-rolled and annealed FeCrAl alloy thin-walled tubes 
with Nb and Ta. The results showed that these parameters could be 
regulated by annealing temperature to balance strength and tough- 
ness and thus realize its expected application as a potential alter- 
native material for Zr alloy in light–water nuclear reactors. The fol- 
lowing are the research conclusions of this paper: 
(1) The typical deformed texture of low-carbon steel was ob- 
served in FeCrAl alloy thin-walled tubes. The main textures in- 
cluded α-ﬁber ( < 110 > //RD), γ-ﬁber ( < 111 > /ND), and θ-ﬁber 
( < 100 > //ND). Unlike most of the investigated plates of FeCrAl 
with Nb and Ta with strong γ-ﬁber ( < 111 > /ND) texture ori- 
entation, the tube of FeCrAl alloy showed a stronger α-ﬁber 
( < 110 > //RD) texture orientation (cold-rolled up to 78.3%). With 
increasing annealing temperature ( ≤800 ◦C), the texture of α- 
ﬁber was transformed to γ-ﬁber. 
(2) The mass distribution of Fe 2 Nb laves-phase particles and 
Fe 2 Mo 4 C precipitation phase-stabilized the microstructure of 
the FeCrAl alloy thin-walled tube. When annealed below 
800 ◦C, the laves-phase particles had high thermal stability and 
a stronger nailing effect. Meanwhile, Ta made the laves phase 
precipitate more easily at the grain boundary, which impeded 
grain growth. As a result, the thin-walled tube of FeCrAl alloy 
obtained higher plasticity while maintaining good strength. 
(3) The thin-walled tubes had excellent mechanical properties at 
room temperature. The yield strength of the cold-rolled alloys 
was as high as 766.7 MPa, and the speciﬁc elongation was 
higher than 15%. Meanwhile, appropriate annealing tempera- 
ture and time could be used to optimize the tensile proper- 
ties of the FeCrAl thin-walled tube at room temperature. At 
room temperature, recovery and recrystallization signiﬁcantly 
increased the plasticity. The speciﬁc elongation increased from 
15.3 to 27.2% with increasing annealing temperature. At 400 ◦C, 
the plasticity of the alloy after recovery and recrystallization 
decreased to a certain extent. 
(4) The dispersive Fe 2 (Mo, Nb, Ta) type second-phase particles can 
effectively prevent grain growth and improve the performance 
of the alloy. The remelting temperature was about 10 0 0 ◦C, and 
the second phase particles almost completely remelted after 
30 min of annealing; as such, the time margin provided by the 
particles was not more than 30 min.
