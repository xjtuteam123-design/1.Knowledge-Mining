Improvement of Mechanical Properties of Tantalum–
Tungsten Alloy by Multi-pass Low-Pressure
Reciprocating Cold-Rolling Process Combined with
Annealing Treatment
Yulong Yang, Yanping Zeng, Leijun Zhan, Zengyang Huang, Zhongchen Xu, Zhaobin Gong, Shaolei Liu, and Zhiyong Zhao
Submitted: 26 November 2024 / Revised: 3 January 2025 / Accepted: 24 January 2025 / Published online: 31 March 2025
Under the annealing treatment of 900 /C24 1150 °C, the multi-pass low-pressure reciprocating cold-rolling
(MLR-CR) process and less-pass high-pressure unidirectional cold-rolling (LHU-CR) process of tantalum –
tungsten alloy were studied. The microstructure and mechanical properties under the two conditions were
characterized and analyzed. The results show that the mechanical properties of MLR-CR process were
obviously improved at the same annealing treatment conditions. Even under the annealing condition of
1150 °C, the yield strength of MLR-CR process still reached over /C24 370 MPa. The microhardness was
maintained at 220-240HV , and the elongation was close to 48%. Compared with LHU-CR process, the
comprehensive mechanical properties of tantalum –tungsten alloy was improved under the condition of
MLR-CR process, which was mainly attributed to the ﬁne microstructure of MLR-CR process. Under the
same annealing conditions, the dislocation density increased and recrystallization phenomenon was delayed
in MLR-CR process compared with LHU-CR process. The mechanical properties of MLR-CR process are
improved by dislocation strengthening and grain re ﬁnement synergistically.
Keywords annealing, cold rolling, low-pressure, mechanical
properties, multi-pass, recrystallization, tantalum –
tungsten alloy
1. Introduction
Tantalum is a rare metal with a body-centered cubic (BCC)
structure, which has many excellent characteristics such as high
density, high melting point, strong corrosion resistance and
good room temperature ductility (Ref 1-4). Considering the low
strength of pure tantalum metal, tantalum alloy is usually
prepared in industry in order to obtain more stable mechanical
properties, and tantalum –tungsten alloy has been concerned
and is widely used in electric power, medical, nuclear energy
and military ﬁelds (Ref 4-7).
In order to make better use of tantalum metals and alloys in
different ﬁelds, many studies are devoted to adjusting the
microstructure of pure tantalum and tantalum alloys through
different cold-plastic deformation processes, so as to improve
their mechanical properties (Ref 8-10). Ma et al. studied the
microstructure and mechanical properties of tantalum –tungsten
alloy (Ta-4W) after cold-rolling from small strain to large strain
and pointed out the microstructure evolution and strengthen-
ing–toughening mechanism of tantalum –tungsten alloy under
different strain conditions (Ref 11). Wang et al. also pointed out
that microbands do not form until the reduction reaches 20%,
and shear bands appear when the reduction reaches 40% after
studying the microstructure evolution of coarse crystal Ta-2.5%
W alloy during the deformation of low-strain cold-rolling
process, and a model is used to explain why new small grains
are developed in shear bands (Ref 1).
It can be inferred that the in ﬂuence of strain conditions on
microstructure and mechanical properties during cold-rolling
deformation has many uncertainties. However, the above
studies are based on the strain as a variable and ignore the
effect of rolling pass and annealing recrystallization. The strong
hardening phenomenon produced by the cold-plastic deforma-
tion under high-pressure strain will seriously affect the
migration and recrystallization behavior of the dislocation
during subsequent annealing treatment (Ref 2, 3, 12-16).
It is well known that for metal materials, effective regulation
of dislocation and recrystallization behavior is conducive to the
improvement of mechanical properties (Ref 17-22). The high
dislocation density is bene ﬁcial to recrystallization nucleation,
while the fault energy of the slip system is effected by the
orientation distribution of the substructure, thus effecting the
subsequent recrystallization behavior (Ref 3, 16, 23). However,

with post-annealing treatment. Therefore, this study can
provide theoretical basis and process support for related ﬁelds.
2. Materials and Methods
The experimental material used is an annealed commercial
tantalum–tungsten alloy sheet produced by a company in
China. The chemical composition and mechanical properties of
raw materials are shown in Tables 1 and 2.
The tantalum–tungsten alloy sheet was ﬁrst homogenized at
1350 °C for 2 h and then air-cooled to room temperature for
use. Wire cutting equipment was used to cut and sample for
observation and further experimental study. The designed
multi-pass low-pressure reciprocating cold-rolling process
(MLR-CR) and conventional less-pass high-pressure unidirec-
tional cold-rolling (LHU-CR) were used to deform the plates by
a straight-drawn cold-rolling machine (roll diameter: φ450 mm;
roll width: 450 mm). The initial width and thickness of sheet
before rolling were 150 mm and 5 mm, respectively. The
rolling direction was carried out along the length of the plate.
The ﬁnal thickness of the plate after rolling was 1 mm, and the
width of the plate was free to unfold. The rolling speed of both
processes remained the same at 15 m/min. No lubrication was
required except during the biting phase. The cold-rolled
samples were annealed at 900 °C, 1050 °C and 1150 °C with
holding for 1 h, respectively, and then cooled to room
temperature. The speci ﬁc rolling process and heat treatment
route are shown in Fig. 1.
According to the tensile test standard GB/T228-2002, the
samples were sampled by wire cutting equipment and tested by
CMT5105 universal testing machine. The main technical
parameters of the tensile test are test force: 100KN, strain rate:
6 mm/min. The size of tensile sample and location of
metallographic samples are shown in Fig. 2(a) and (b).
After grinding and polishing, the metallographic samples
were etched with a mixed solution of sulfuric acid/nitric acid/
hydroﬂuoric acid=5:2:2 (volume ratio), and the etching time
was 15-20 s. The microstructure of the samples under different
conditions was analyzed by optical microscope (OM). The
microhardness was measured by FM-700 microhardness tester
at 200gf load conditions, and more than 10 points were
measured equidistant along the rolling direction for each
sample. To obtain the suitable EBSD samples, electrolytic
polishing was used to etch the as-received and annealing
samples after chemical polishing process. The mixture of
H
2S04 and HF=9:1 (volume ratio) was used as the electrolyte,
the current was controlled at 0.1 A/cm 2, and the etching time
was about 7 /C24 9 min. Field emission scanning electron micro-
scope (FE-SEM ZEISS Ultra-55) was used for EBSD analysis.
The step was set to 1.5 μm. The HKL Channel 5 software was
adopted for data processing. The substructure was observed by
high-resolution ﬁeld emission transmission electron micro-
scopy (FETEM) with an output voltage of 200 keV . The sample
was thinned to about 50um by mechanical methods, and then,
the disk with a diameter of 3 mm was blanked, and ﬁnally, the
electrolytic double spray instrument was used for perforation.
The electrolyte was a mixture of methanol and sulfuric acid
with a volume ratio of 9:1. The electrolytic voltage was 20 KV ,
and the temperature was −20 °C.

4. Discussion
4.1 Microstructure
The commercial tantalum –tungsten alloy used in the
experiment inevitably retains local residual stress (Ref 2).
After the homogenization condition of 1350 °C/2 h, the residual
stress can be released to different degrees, which provides
sufﬁcient power for the growth of some grains (Ref 24).
Therefore, individual grains grow abnormally, as shown in Fig.
3, but the overall grains are uniform, which does not affect the
subsequent experiments.
In addition, it is obvious that the grains under the MLR-CR
process show a tendency to be ﬁne at the same annealing
temperature, as shown in Fig. 5. The reason is that compared
with LHU-CR process, MLR-CR process inhibits the grain
growth process after annealing and retains most of the ﬁne
substructure, which has a positive effect on grain re ﬁnement.
The result of multi-pass reciprocating rolling under low
pressure is the accumulation of a large amount of micro-strain
(Ref 1, 4, 11). Therefore, it is easy to accumulate a large
number of dislocations with different orientations after each
cold-rolling pass, and the slip system is impeded by a large
number of dislocation clusters and entanglement within the
substructure (as shown in Fig. 14a, b) as well as the movement

4.3 Recrystallization
As can be seen from Fig. 5, the recrystallization under LHU-
CR process and MLR-CR process increases with the increase in
annealing temperature, which is mainly due to the fact that
higher annealing temperature provides greater driving force for
recrystallization (Ref 34). The essence of recrystallization is the
process of re-nucleation and growth of grains (Ref 12, 13, 35).
Under the same cold-plastic deformation conditions, higher
annealing temperature will help to promote the dislocation to
the grain-boundary movement, promote the formation of high-
angle grain boundaries, and promote the development of
recrystallization (Ref 3, 24). According to Johnson –Mehl
equation, there exists a relationship among recrystallized grain
size d, nucleation rate N and growth rate G (Ref 16, 24).

5. Conclusions
In this study, the mechanical properties of tantalum –
tungsten alloy are improved by MLR-CR process with suitable
annealing procedure, compared with the traditional cold-rolling
process. In addition, the microstructure and mechanical prop-
erties under different processes were characterized, and the
essential reasons for the better mechanical properties were
revealed. The following conclusions are drawn:
1. Under the MLR-CR process, the strengthening index de-
creased with the annealing temperature increasing from
900 to 1150 °C and the tensile strength and yield
strength decreased from 545 and 420 to 505 and
375 MPa, respectively. Microhardness reduced from 245
to 220HV . However, the elongation rose from 42 to 47%.
Compared with LHU-CR process, MLR-CR process with
reasonable heat treatment interval improved the compre-
hensive mechanical properties of tantalum –tungsten alloy.
2. Compared with LHU-CR process, the elastic accumula-
tion of MLR-CR process produced dynamic strain aging
phenomenon, resulting in dislocations with different ori-
entations interlocking. In the annealing stage, the move-
ment of the slip system within the substructure was
hindered by the entangled interlocking dislocations as
well as the movement of the grain boundaries. The effect
of dislocation strengthening played a positive role in
improving the comprehensive mechanical properties.
3. Under the same cold-plastic deformation conditions, the
higher annealing temperature helped the dislocation move
to the grain boundaries, promoting the formation of high-
angle grain boundaries, then promoted the development
