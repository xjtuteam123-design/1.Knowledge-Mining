Experimental determination of the Ni–Ni5Zr eutectic point for binary 
Ni–Zr alloy phase diagram
Q. Wang1 · H. P . Wang1  · D. L. Geng1 · B. Wei1
Received: 22 February 2020 / Accepted: 18 April 2020 
© Springer-Verlag GmbH Germany, part of Springer Nature 2020
Abstract
The Ni–Ni5Zr eutectic alloy becomes a promising structural material owing to its high strength and desirable plasticity. 
However, those nominal eutectic alloys with previously assumed compositions of Ni-8.8 at.% Zr and Ni-9.0 at.% Zr were 
found to involve a considerable amount of primary (Ni) phase. Hereby, an experimental study was conducted by differential 
scanning calorimetry and careful electron microscopy to clarify the situation. A total of 13 alloys in the composition range 
of 8.8 ~ 11.0 at.% Zr were explored with respect to their phase transition temperatures and microstructure evolution charac-
teristics. The Ni-9.9 at.% Zr alloy exhibited primary (Ni) phase, whereas the Ni-10.1 at.% Zr alloy displayed primary Ni
5Zr 
phase. Finally, the eutectic point was determined as Ni-10.0 at.% Zr alloy with a transition temperature of 1467 K, and the 
relevant part of Ni–Zr phase diagram was revised accordingly.
Keywords Phase diagram · Differential scanning calorimetry · Ni–Zr alloy · Eutectic alloy · Metallic systems
1 Introduction
Eutectic alloy comprising of cooperative growth phases 
exhibits extraordinary properties and has attracted consid-
erable attentions [1 –3]. On the one hand, it is particularly 
appealing because of the novel growth mechanism under 
large undercooling [4 –6]. On the other hand, the lamel-
lar/rod eutectic phase provides a unique approach to tune 
microstructure and further enhances its functional properties 
[7–9]. The Ni–Zr eutectic alloys with high strength, large 
plasticity and good glass formation ability have aroused sus-
tained attentions in the fields of metallurgical engineering, 
thermodynamics and condensed matter physics, et al. [10, 
11] Besides, the mechanical properties of Ni–Zr eutectic 
alloys are highly dependent on their microstructure, espe-
cially for in situ composites tuned by phase orientation [12, 
13]. However, the primary phase caused by segregation, 
cooling rate or deviation from eutectic composition breaks 
the regular eutectic pattern of Ni–Zr alloys [13– 15]. Thus, 
the precise eutectic composition is the basis of Ni–Zr eutec-
tic researches. Unfortunately, despite the well-documented 
thermophysical data of Ni–Zr alloys, the Ni–Ni
5Zr eutectic 
composition is continuously assessed in last decades.
According to Park et al. [12], eutectic-dendrite compos-
ite prepared by Ni-8.0 at.% Zr alloy comprised of ultrafine 
eutectic microstructure and primary (Ni) dendrites. The 
alloy exhibited high strength combined with large plastic 
deformability. Okamoto et al. [16] reported that the eutectic 
reaction takes place at 1443 K, where liquid Ni-8.8 at.% Zr 
alloy solidifies to fcc (Ni) and compound Ni
5Zr coopera-
tively. This eutectic composition was supported by Toku-
naga et al. [17], who implemented CALPHAD calculation 
on Nb–Ni–Zr ternary system. Wang et al. [18] argued that 
the eutectic composition between (Ni) and Ni
5Zr compounds 
is at 9.0% Zr content by their calculation. Recently, anterior 
researches on Ni–Zr system were reviewed by Bochvar et al. 
[19] Their report maintained the view that Ni-9.0 at.% Zr 
is the eutectic composition between (Ni) and Ni
5Zr. Previ-
ous studies have witnessed that the researches’ cognition of 
Ni–Ni
5Zr eutectic point shifts to higher Zr content, from 8.8 
to 9.0%. However, according to our recent electromagnetic 
levitation study [14] and the differential scanning calorim-
etry (DSC) experiments (see Fig.  1), neither 8.8% Zr con-
tent nor the latter modified 9.0% Zr content is the accurate 
eutectic composition. Both of them solidify as primary (Ni) 
embedded in eutectic microstructure.

The purpose of this work is to confirm the precise eutec-
tic composition between (Ni) and Ni5Zr compound by DSC 
experiments. The microstructures and DSC curves on a 
series of Ni–Zr alloys around eutectic composition were ana-
lyzed. In order to further verify the revised eutectic compo-
sition under different undercoolings and sample masses, an 
arc-melting experiment and DSC measurements with lower 
cooling rates were conducted. Finally, the precise eutectic 
composition and modified phase diagram around Ni–Ni
5Zr 
eutectic point are presented.
2 Experimental methods
In the experiments, Ni–Zr alloys around eutectic compo-
sition were prepared by a SPI SP300 fiber laser under Ar 
atmosphere from bulk Ni and Zr with 99.99% and 99.95% 
purity, respectively. In order to avoid the segregation, alloy 
samples were weighted about 80 mg to fit the DSC (Netzsch 
STA 449F3 and Netzsch DSC 404C) crucibles. Samples 
experienced a constant heating rate of 20 K/min from 300 to 
1573 K and then cooled to 300 K with a cooling rate of 20 K/
min (labeled as procedure A). The solidified microstructure 
analysis was performed by a field-emission scanning elec-
tron microscope (SEM, Phenom Pro). In rapid solidification, 
alloys deviating from eutectic composition may solidify as 
complete eutectic microstructure due to the undercooling. In 
order to avoid the interference of undercooling, experiments 
with higher and lower cooling rate were carried out. In the 
first experiment, eutectic alloy of mass about 0.5 g was pre-
pared in an arc-furnace with water-cooled copper crucible. 
The second is the solidification of preliminarily determined 
eutectic composition with cooling rate of 10 and 0.5 K/min.
3 Results and Discussion
3.1 Microstructures of previously assumed eutectic 
alloy
Near-equilibrium solidification on previously reported 
eutectic compositions Ni-8.8 at.% Zr and Ni-9.0 at.% Zr 
alloys was performed through DSC procedure A. Micro-
structures of solidified Ni-8.8 at.% Zr and Ni-9.0 at.% 
Zr are illustrated in Fig.  1a, b. Obviously, dark primary 
(Ni) dendrites [14] homogeneously disperse in a brighter 
matrix in Fig. 1a. A closer inspection of primary (Ni) den-
drite of Ni-8.8 at.% Zr and the brighter surrounding reveals 
that the brighter matrix is typical rod eutectic microstruc-
ture, which comprises of (Ni) and Ni
5Zr phases. Figure  1b 
depicts the microstructure of Ni-9.0 at.% Zr alloy, which 
is similar to Ni-8.8 at.% Zr. Coarse primary (Ni) dendrites 
embedded in eutectic matrix are identified.

10.5 and 11.0) alloys were chosen to experience the DSC 
procedure A. The heating DSC curves are illustrated in 
Fig. 2a. Except for main peaks, shoulders are observed for 
Ni-x at.% Zr (x = 8.8, 9.5, 10.4, 10.5 and 11.0) alloys. This 
indicates primary phase starts to melt after the melting of 
eutectic phase. For the sake of clarification, only enlarge-

3.3 Verification of eutectic composition
Cooperative growth of eutectic phase in equilibrium solidi-
fication forms eutectic microstructure. However, in non-
equilibrium conditions, alloys near the eutectic composition 
may solidify as complete eutectic microstructure, so-called 
pseudoeutectic alloys. It is believed that the pseudoeutec-
tic microstructure is caused by different growth rate of two 
phases under different undercooling. However, the cooling 
rate of practical DSC measurements cannot be reduced to 
zero. This may generate undercooling during solidification. 
From this perspective, it is necessary to confirm the eutec-
tic composition under different cooling rates. Here, we per-
formed experiments with higher and lower cooling rate on 
Ni-10.0 at.% Zr alloy.
In the first experiment, Ni-10.0 at.% Zr sample with 
mass of 0.5 g was prepared by an arc-melting furnace, 
where water-cooled copper crucible was applied. Its cool-
ing rate is far larger than 20 K/min and is supposed to 
achieve higher undercooling. To be clarify, the top, mid-
dle and bottom region of sample are depicted. In Fig.  4, 
only Ni
5Zr phase remained after sample is etched by acid 
solution (1 mL HF, 3 mL HNO3, 3 mL H2O2 and 16 mL 
 H2O). Lamellar Ni5Zr phase is observed in Fig.  4a, b. Due 
to different orientations of eutectic lamella, there exists 
a large luminance difference between region A and B in 
Fig. 4a, i.e., eutectic layers in dark region are perpendicu-
lar to observed surface. As for sample bottom, the cooling 
rate and under cooling deviate from other region. Primary 
 Ni
5Zr solidified paralleling to substrate. Notice that the 
magnification of Fig. 4c is not small. An estimation of vol-
ume fraction shows that primary Ni5Zr is less than 0.03% 
in Ni-10.0 at.% Zr sample.

4 Conclusion
In conclusion, primary (Ni) dendrites are identified in previ-
ously documented eutectic alloys with compositions of 8.8 
and 9.0 at.% Zr alloys. DSC experiments with cooling rate 
of 20 K/min indicate that Ni-x at.% Zr (x = 9.6, 9.7, 9.8, 
9.9, 10.0, 10.1, 10.2 and 10.3) alloys only exhibit a single 
melting peak, whereas both melting peak and shoulder are 
observed for Ni-x at.% Zr (x = 8.8, 9.5, 10.4, 10.5 and 11.0) 
alloys. Meanwhile, the microstructures of Ni-9.9 at.% Zr and 
Ni-10.1 at.% Zr contain either primary (Ni) or Ni
5Zr phase, 
respectively. Ni-10.0 at.% Zr comprises complete eutectic 
microstructure. This eutectic composition is further verified 
by DSC measurements with cooling rates of 10 and 0.5 K/
