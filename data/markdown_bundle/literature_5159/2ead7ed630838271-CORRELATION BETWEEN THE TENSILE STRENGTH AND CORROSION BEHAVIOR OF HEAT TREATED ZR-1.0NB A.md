INTRODUCTION Metallic fuels can be utilized as nuclear fuel for advanced light water reactors due to their excellent thermal conductivity [l -4]. As a fabrication process of these metallic fuels, a billet composed of a metallic fuel and cladding material can be co-extruded by using their superior workability properties [5-7]. The plastically deformed metallic fuel rods should be heat treated to recover their mechanical properties degraded from the working. Thus, the heat treatment conditions involved have become a subject of interest. The Zr-Nb alloys as a cladding material for the metallic fuels have been known to have excellent mechanical properties at high temperatures along with superior corrosion resistance [8-15]. The excellent mechanical properties of these alloys have been mainly attributed to the Nb-containing precipitates [8,9], while the superior corrosion resistance has been ascribed to the ?-Nb precipitates [10-12] and an equilibrium Nb concentration in the a-Zr matrix [13-15]. From these research results, it is thought that reliable mechanical and corrosion properties can be obtained when these alloys consist of not only the a-Zr matrix with an equilibrium Nb concentration but also ?-Nb precipitates. In a Zr-Nb binary system, the ?-Zr (��20 wt% Nb) and the ?-Nb (��80 wt% Nb) phases are precipitated above and below 6100C, respectively. It is well known that the ?-Zr NUCLEAR ENGINEERING AND TECHNOLOGY. The Zr-l.0wt%Nb alloy, which contains a Nb concentration above its solubility (��0.6 wt%), should be heat treated below 61 OOC in order to form the ?-Nb precipitates. As a preliminary experimental result, a heat treatment at 5800C of an extruded Zr-Nb alloy induced improved corrosion resistance, but revealed a relative low tensile strength [16], It is thus necessary to reduce the heat treatment temperature to less than 5800C in order to obtain an improved tensile strength along with a reliable corrosion resistance. In this study, an extruded Zr- 1.0wt%Nb alloy is heat treated at 4800 C, and its microstructure, tensile strength, and corrosion behavior are evaluated. Based on these results, a correlation between the tensile strength and corrosion resistance is discussed. 2. CONCLUSION A Zr-l .0wt%Nb alloy was extruded at 7000C and then heat treated at 4800C for 3, 8, 16, and 32 hours. Most of the precipitates in the extruded sample were analyzed to be the ?-Zr phase with a crystal sffucture of bcc (a=0.3545 nm). These precipitates were continuously transformed to enriched 13-Nb precipitates during the heat treatment. The heat treatment also induced a grain growth as well as a decreased area fraction of the precipitates. The tensile strength at 4000C continuously decreased with an increasing heat treatment time, mainly due to grain growth and a reduction in the amount of precipitates. However, the corrosion resistance in an aqueous ammonia solution at 3600C was enhanced, mainly due to the formation of ??Nb precipitates. It is concluded that a longer heat treatment time at 4800C provides a better corrosion resistance while degrading the tensile strength.
