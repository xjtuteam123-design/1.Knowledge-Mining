ABSTRACT
An Nb-Silicide in situ composite with a nominal composition of Nb-16Si-10Ti-10Mo-5Hf (at. %) was fabricated
by mechanical alloying followed by hot-pressing sintering. The microstructure consisted of an Nb solid solution,
Nb5Si3 and a small amount of Nb 3Si. This in-situ composite exhibited good balance of strength between ambient
temperature and high temperatures; the ultimate tensile strength was 413 and 496 MPa at room temperature and
1200 °C, respectively. The tensile fracture behavior was dominated by cleavage of the Nbss and Nb
5Si3 at
1200 °C and lower temperatures. However, the fracture behavior was governed by ductile rupture of Nbss at
1300 °C and higher temperature, which was ascribed to both the increased ductility of Nbss and the decreased
interface strength. At 1400 °C and higher temperature, the material exhibited extensive plasticity or super-
plasticity; the dominant deformation mechanism was grain boundary sliding at 1400 °C and higher temperature.
1. Introduction
The Nb 5Si3 is currently considered as potential replacements of Ni
based superalloys in certain applications in gas turbine engines owing
to its low densities, high melting point and high temperature strength
[1]. However, the intrinsic brittleness of Nb 5Si3 at ambient temperature
inhibits its practical applications. To overcome this critical issue, duc-
tile Nbss (Nb solid solution) in equilibrium with Nb
5Si3 up to very high
temperatures has been incorporated into an Nb 5Si3 matrix in an in situ
manner to produce composites based on the concept of ductile-phase
toughening of brittle compounds [2,3]. The typical examples were re-
ported by Mendiratta et al. [4] and Yu et al. [5] using hot-extruded in
situ composites consisting of Nbss and Nb 5Si3. They show much higher
room temperature fracture toughness exceeding 20MPam 1/2, as com-
pared with monolithic Nb 5Si3. Accordingly, Nb-Silicide in situ compo-
sites rather than monolithic Nb 5Si3 are promising for such applications.
However, the binary alloys exhibited poor oxidation resistance, and,
in general, any alloying addition to improve the high temperature
oxidation leaded to deterioration of the high temperature mechanical
properties of these materials [6]. Thus, a multicomponent approach
was necessary to counterbalance the negative e ﬀects of one alloying by
the other [7]. The results of previous studies [8] indicated that partial
substitution of Nb by Ti leaded to appreciable improvement in the
oxidation resistance of these composites. The Ti also improved fracture
toughness, but on the other hand reduced the melting point and the
high temperature strength. Therefore, to counteract the negative e ﬀect
of Ti, element such W, Mo, Hf and Ta et al. were added, as these pro-
vided strong solid solution strengthening of the Nb and silicide phase
[9,10]. The Nb-Silicide in situ composites strengthened by Mo and W
solution elements exhibited extremely high compressive strength at
temperature ranging from room temperature to 1500 °C [11,12]. Fur-
thermore, the balance between high temperature compressive strength
[13,14] and room temperature fracture toughness [15,16] of Nb-Sili-
cide in situ composites have been also achieved.
However, only a few reports are currently available on the tensile
properties and fracture behavior of Nb-Silicide in situ composites at
elevated temperature. Therefore, understanding of the tensile proper-
ties and fracture behavior are essential. Jin-Hak Kim et al. [17] reported
that the tensile properties of the Nb-18Si-5Mo-5Hf-2C (at. %) in com-
posite fabricated by arc melting method. Excellent tensile strength of
460 MPa was obtained at 1470 K. However, this composite did not
exhibit good plasticity even at elevated temperature; the tensile elon-
gation reached only about 0.8% at 1470 K. Here we introduced a new
metallurgical manufacturing route, i.e. mechanical alloying (MA) fol-
lowed by hot-pressing sintering which fabricated a ﬁne-grained Nb-Si-
licide in situ composite. Mechanical alloying (MA) have been used to
synthesize supersaturated solid solution, nano-crystalline, metastable
compounds and amorphous solids [18,19]. Due to the reduced di ﬀusion

2. Experiments
The raw materials used in the present study were Nb (+200 mesh),
Si (+325 mesh), Ti (+200 mesh), Hf (- 200 mesh) and Mo (+130
mesh) powders with purity of 99.9%, 99.9%, 99.9%, 99.9% and 99.9%
(wt. %), respectively. The Nb-silicide in situ composite was prepared by
a powder metallurgy (PM) technique using elemental powders. The
powders with the nominal composition of Nb-16Si-10Ti-10Mo-5Hf (at.
%) were ball milled with ball-to-powder volume ratio of 6:1 in argon
atmosphere for 50 h until a powder with a nanostructured micro-
structure was obtained. In order to avoid cold welding, 1 wt % stearic
acid was added as a surfactant. The powders after MA were sintered at
1500 °C and 40 MPa for 1 h by hot-pressing sintering in the argon at-
mosphere.
To identify the constituent phases, X-ray di ﬀraction (XRD) mea-
surements using a Cu-K α line were carried out, scanning from 20° to
150°. The specimens for microstructure observations were machined,
mechanically polished to a mirror surface using water proofed SiC pa-
pers, and then used diamond particles with distilled water on platens.
Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) studies using backscattered elec-
tron image were conducted to reveal the distribution and size of con-
stitution phases.
The tensile specimens with 3 mm × 1 mm cross-section and
10.5 mm gauge length were cut by an electro-discharge machining
(EDM) from the as-sintered materials. Then, the surfaces of the speci-
mens were polished so that microstructure investigations of tested
specimens were enabled. The tensile tests were carried out in an
Instron-type material-testing machine. The test at ambient temperature
was carried out with an initial strain rates 2.31 × 10
−4s−1 in air. The
tensile tests were performed at temperature ranging from 1200 °C to
1500 °C under a constant crosshead speed with an initial strain rate
2.31 × 10
−4s−1 in vacuum (of less than 5 × 10 −2MPa). Finally, SEM
fractography was carried out to characterize the fracture modes.
3. Results
3.1. Material characterization
A typical backscattered electron image of Nb-16Si-10Ti-10Mo-5Hf
in situ composite was presented in Fig. 1. The microstructure consisted
of an Nb solid solution, β-Nb5Si3 and a small amount of Nb 3Si, and
appeared as a (nearly) continuous Nbss matrix with uniformly dispersed
intermetallic phases. In Fig. 1, the bright phase was Nbss; the gray
phase was Nb 3Si and the dark phase was the Nb 5Si3 phases. This
composite had a ﬁne microstructure, and the grain shape was nearly
equiaxed. The EDX analyses revealed the chemical composition of the
constitute phases, which were summarized in Table 1 .
Fig. 2 showed a XRD pro ﬁle of the as-sintered composite. The
Nb
5Si3 was found to be a beta phase. The lattice parameters, a and c,
were 0.6581 and 1.1874 nm, respectively, which were smaller than
those of binary Nb
5Si3 (a = 0.65685 nm and c = 1.18815 nm) [20],
and the volumetric shrinking due to the Ti and Mo alloying. The lattice
parameter of Nbss 0.3284 nm is about 0.6% smaller than that of pure
Nb (0.3304 nm) [20].

The strengthening mechanism was not clearly understood at pre-
sent, but there were some possible explanations for the strengthening.
The ﬁrst was the solid solution hardening of Nbss by Mo. As shown in
Table 1, Mo was preferentially solved in Nbss, which decreased the
lattice parameters. The crystal lattice distortion caused by alloying
elements dissolution in Nbss may greatly increase the resistance to
deformation. Moreover, di ﬀusion controlled high temperature de-
formation of silicide may be suppressed by Mo and Hf addition. Ac-
cording to P. R. Subramanian et al. [1], creep deformation in Nb-Si
binary alloy was controlled by bulk deformation of Nb in Nb
5Si3. Al-
though their deformation conditions were di ﬀerent from ours, it would
be reasonable to assume that high temperature deformation in this
study was controlled by bulk di ﬀusion of Nb. The atomic bonding
strength may greatly increase by Mo and Hf addition, which would
retard the bulk di ﬀusion of Nb at high temperature, thereby increasing
the high temperature strength of silicides. The strength of the present
research composite was comparable to that of some ultra-high tem-
perature tungsten alloys. Notwithstanding, the yield strength at 1200 °C
was lower than that at 1300 °C. These results imply that the micro-crack
generates at an initial stage of testing. Strain of 0.2%, where the stress
was deﬁned as yield strength, was due to local deformation of the Nbss,
subsequent failed at an only 1.2% and with highest UTS.
At elevated temperature over 1200 °C, the UTS continuously de-
creased with the increase of the testing temperature, meanwhile the
elongation markedly increased from 1.2% at 1200 °C to 27% at 1300 °C.
Thus, at temperature 1300 °C and higher, the Nbss should possess an
excellent capability to arrest the crack growth inside Nbss. The good
deformability of the Nbss can relieve stress concentration at interface or
grain boundary by increased dislocation activity, and consequently,
large plasticity should be attainable up to the rupture of the Nbss. In
fact, a tensile elongation of 123% was measured from the sample tested
at 1400 °C and an initial strain rate of 2.31 × 10
−4s−1. The specimen
after tensile test at 1500 °C and an initial strain rate of 2.31 × 10 −4s−1
was shown in Fig. 4, in which the upper specimen was the untested one
for comparison. At 1500 °C the composite showed superplasticity, and
the tensile elongation reaches 213%. It was apparent from Fig. 4 that
the specimen exhibited high tensile ductility without visible macro-
scopic necking.
4. Discussion
From the results of the tensile tests, shown in Table 2, it can be
stated that the present Nb-Silicide in situ composite exhibited an overall
brittle fracture from ambient temperature to 1200 °C, and ductile
rupture at above 1200 °C. The details were revealed by fractographic
investigations through scanning electron microscopy (SEM). Typical
SEM micrographs of local fracture surfaces at various temperatures
were shown in Fig. 5. At room temperature, as shown in Fig. 5 (a), most
of the Nbss and Nb
5Si3 fractured in a cleavage manner, shown hair river
lines; the overall appearance was rough and composed of two kinds of
large and small facets, as noted by black arrows C and B. The Nbss
showed cleavage steps from an Nb 5Si3 to another Nb 5Si3 over the Nbss
itself, indicating cleavage crack linkage. Since catastrophic failure
happened before macroscopic yielding, the contribution of the in-
corporated Nbss seemed to be sparse. However, local stretching of Nbss
was partly observed as noted by the black arrows A in Fig. 5 (a). This
implied possible plastic deformation of Nbss.
The local stretching of Nbss (noted by the black arrows A) fre-
quently occured at 1200 °C, as seen in Fig. 5 (b). It should be noted that
the Nbss necks enough to be aculeate like a sharp edge (noted by the
black arrows B) and shallow interface separation (noted by the black
arrows C) occured. The necked Nbss suggested that the Nbss exhibited
evident ductility. However, the actual elongation of the composite was
only 1.2%, which was attributed to shallow interface separation. The
interface separation can release the stress concentration at interface/
grain boundary and forms free surface, which was e ﬀective to blunt the
crack tip. If the interface debonding goes on deeply, larger elongation
should be obtained. Thus, both the interface separation and the
stretching of Nbss contributed to improve elongation at 1200 °C.
The fracture at 1300 °C was apparently transitioned into the ductile
mode due to the better ductility of Nbss. At 1300 °C, the reticular Nbss
elongated along the loading axis and a number of dimples (noted by the
black arrows A) are observed, as shown in Fig. 5 (c). Since interface
separation and ductile rupture of the Nbss were observed at 1200 °C,
those mechanisms of fracture becomes more active by temperature
increment, as noted by the black arrows B in Fig. 5 (c). Accordingly,
cleavage fracture of the Nb
5Si3 should be impeded. Furthermore, the
weakened Nbss/Nb 5Si3 interface, along with the improved ductility of
Nbss, must contribute to the high elongation 27%.

Typical SEM micrograph of fracture surfaces at 1500 °C were shown
in Fig. 5 (d). At 1500 °C, fracture behavior was obviously di ﬀerent from
those at 1200 and 1300 °C, and a similar fracture appearance was ob-
served at 1400 °C. The dimples were more obviously, as shown by white
arrows A in Fig. 5 (d). Many dimples and deep holes (noted by white
arrows) were produced. These holes were the result of Nb 5Si3 pullout
from the Nbss matrix. After the Nb 5Si3 pullout, the adequately de-
formable Nbss would elongate along the loading axis and rupture.
These observations suggested that the interface strength remarkably
decreased. If the main deformation mechanism of the present composite
was intragranular slip during the temperature range from 1300 to
1500 °C, the corresponding elongation should be slowly increased with
increasing temperature. Actually, the tensile elongation of 27%, 123%,
213% was measured from the sample tested at 1300, 1400 and 1500 °C,
respectively. Thus, there was a jump at the temperature of 1400 °C,
which suggested the main deformation mechanism changes. As we all
known, it has an equicohesive temperature for polycrystalline materials
during plasticity deformation process. Below this temperature inter-
face/grain boundary has higher strength than grain, and intragranular
slip is the main deformation mechanism. Above such a temperature
grain strength is higher than that of interface/grain boundary. At
1500 °C grain strength is higher than that of interface/grain boundary.
Although the interface debonding occurred, grain boundary sliding is
dominant deformation mechanism during plastic deformation process.
Therefore, it was reasonable to consider that for the present Nb-silicide
composite grain boundary sliding process dominated during plastic
deformation at 1400 °C and higher temperature. After plastic de-
formation at 1500 °C, as shown in Fig. 5 (d), the ﬁne-grained micro-
structure was still retained, illustrating its high thermal stability. Grains
were generally round and smooth as a result of extensive surface dif-
fusion at high temperature. Fracture surface exhibited an intergranular
fracture mode. These observations suggested that the large plasticity
was primarily a result of intergranular sliding caused by the ﬁne grains,
not a result of intragranular deformation. These results further con-
ﬁrmed that deformation mechanism of the present study composite at
1400 °C and higher temperature was grain boundary sliding.
During grain boundary sliding, the sliding strain must be properly
accommodated. Otherwise, high local stresses at grain tripe point were
expected to be developed and lead to cavitation failure. In the present
Nb-Silicide composite, the microstructures consisted of ﬁne-grained
Nbss and intermetallic phases. The Nbss phase was soft and readily
deformable at 1400 °C and higher temperature. In comparison, the in-
termetallic phases were much less deformable. This composite ex-
hibited extensive plasticity at 1400 and 1500 °C, which was attributed
to the fact that it contained a continuous Nbss. Strain accommodation
at grain triple junctions can be readily achieved by soft Nbss phase.
Since deformation of Nbss phase was easier than other phases, the
presence of Nbss phase was obviously bene ﬁcial in reducing the stress
at grain triple junctions and, thus, delaying cavitation and fracture. This
explained that the extensive tensile elongation of 123% at 1400 °C was
obtained in the composite. With temperature increasing the grain
boundary strength further droped, which resulted in increase of the
movable grain boundary number. In addition, the Nbss phase at higher
temperature was more soft and readily deformation. Since deformation
of the Nbss and the grain boundary sliding played an important role
during plastic deformation and contributed to plastic extending, this
explained why the tensile elongation of the present Nb-Silicide com-
posite increased rapidly with increasing test temperature, shown in
Table 2. The Nb-16Si-10Ti-10Mo-5Hf composite exhibited extensive
plasticity or superplasticity at 1500 °C. While Nb-Silicide composites
had superior characteristics, such as high melting point, high hardness
and good strength retention at high temperature, one of the major
drawbacks was poor formability even at elevated temperature for
manufacturing products with complicated shapes and minute sizes.
Superplasticity, however, had the advantage of precise forming to the
ﬁnal net shapes. If the superplastic forming of Nb-Silicide composites
can replace the conventional technologies, it would bring signi ﬁcant
advance to the shape-forming of Nb-Silicide composites.
5. Conclusions
An Nb-Silicide in situ composite with a nominal composition of Nb-
16Si-10Ti-10Mo-5Hf was fabricated by powder metallurgy method.
Mechanical properties of this composite at ambient temperature and
elevated temperature were evaluated in tension. Excellent tensile
strength was attained. The solid solution hardening and uniformly
dispersed silicides hardening were two important strengthening me-
chanisms at elevated temperature.

The fracture was due mainly to the cleavage of silicide phases and
Nbss at temperature up to 1200 °C. Further increases in temperature
promoted the interface separation, as well as the plastic deformation of
Nbss, so that the fracture mode was dominated by the ductile rupture at
1300 °C. At 1400 °C and higher temperature, the dominant deformation
mechanism of the composite may vary from intragranular slip to grain
boundary sliding, which resulted in extensive plasticity or super-
plasticity. During grain boundary sliding, the Nbss grains accom-
modated the sliding strain and reduced the propensity for cavitation at
grain triple junctions, thus, delayed the fracture process. It was also
pointed out that the superplasticity of the Nb-16Si-10Ti-10Mo-5Hf
composite can provide solution for the poor formability of Nb-Silicide
composites and promoted them for applications as structural materials
resistant to elevated temperatures.
