25 Ni-based single crystal (SC) superalloys are currently the preferred
26 material for aero-engine turbine blades due to their excellent
27 mechanical properties in high temperature environments. As the
28 core component of the aero-engine, the turbine blade’s service
29 states directly affect the work performance of the airplane. F atigue
30 fracture caused by fatigue damage is the major source of failure in
31 turbine blades.
[1,2]
The anisotropic fatigue failure analysis of Ni-
1based SC superalloy has thus attracted the
2attention of researchers and has become the
3focus of research on SC turbine blades.
[3,4]
4The material performance of Ni-based SC
5superalloy is varies with crystal orientation.
6Based on crystal anisotropy characteristics,
7researchers have carried out a large number
8of tests including creep, fatigue and creep-
9fatigue interaction, to examine the effects of
10temperature, life model and micro-structure
11damage.
[5–7]
Previous studies have shown
12that crystal anisotropy significantly affects
13the fatigue life of an SC turbine blade,
[8]
and
14the turbine blade’s life could increase by
15approximately 20% through crystal orienta-
16tion optimization design.
[9]
Currently , the
17understanding of crystal anisotropy has
18advanced to include the microscopic level.
19In terms of preparation technology , research
20has mainly considered crystal orientation
21and orientation deviation effect factors, such
22as withdrawal rate and starter block size.
[10]
23M aterials with crystal anisotropy perform
24well under creep and fatigue loading. While
25the crystal orientation seriously affects the
26response of impression creep, the creep
27parameters of various orientations differs
28greatly .
[11]
The face-centered cubic SC struc-
29ture exhibits particularly strong material
30property dependence on the orientation and loading direction.
31However , the influence of the secondary orientation cannot be
32ignored.
[12]
Ad ditionally , including temperature, dwell time, and
33critical slip surface
[13,14]
in the study of the effect of crystal
34orientation on fatigue behavior has shown that the effect exhibits
35cyclic softening and cyclic hardening during test process.
36F urthermore, crystal plastic theory and damage theory were
37established to describe material damage and fatigue life predic-
38tion.
[15,16]
F atigue cracking behaviors, such as crack initiation and
39crack propagation, were also affected by crystal orientation. F atigue
40crack propagation tests were carried out to analyze the effects of
41temperature and crystal orientation and the effect of crystal
42orientation on fatigue crack propagation was studied by measuring
43the shear crack propagation rate.
[17]
The major factor determining
44crack propagation was the crack driving force.
[18,19]
