ABSTRACT
TZM alloys are widely used in high-temperature structural applications. However, the degradation of mechanical 
properties in TZM alloys after heat treatment remains a critical issue. In this study, we introduced the hetero -
geneous lamellar (HL) structures into TZM via isothermal rolling. By optimizing the annealing process, the room- 
temperature ductility and high-temperature strength are significantly enhanced with minimal loss of room- 
temperature strength. The HL structures in as-rolled TZM alloy present α -fiber and γ -fiber textures, which are 
alternately composed of bundled grains and deformation bands (DBs). The texture composition is changed after 
annealing at 1000 
◦
C due to high-temperature recovery. After 3 h annealing, entangled dislocations generate 
plentiful subgrain boundaries and polygonal dislocation walls (PDWs), while the subgrain coarsening occurs after 
annealing for 5 h. The as-rolled alloy exhibits an ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of 935 MPa at room temperature 
and 454 MPa at 1000 
◦
C with a total elongation of 21.5 %. After annealing at 1000 
◦
C for 3 h and 5 h, the UTS at 
1000 
◦
C is increased to 572 MPa and 549 MPa, with elongation rising to 25 % and 32.3 %, respectively. Notably, 
the room-temperature strength is decreased by only 48 MPa compared to the as-rolled state. However, annealing 
above 1200 
◦
C reduces the strength-ductility due to recrystallization-induced embrittlement. During high- 
temperature deformation, DBs restrict the deformation of adjacent grains while supporting grains rotate to 
accommodate plastic strain. Furthermore, subgrain boundaries and PDWs emit and store dislocations, syner -
gistically driving dynamic grain refinement and enhancing hetero-deformation induced (HDI) hardening, 
thereby achieving an abnormal increase in high-temperature performances. This study provides new insights into 
designing TZM alloys with superior balanced mechanical properties.
1. Introduction
The rapid advancement of high-temperature processing technologies 
has intensified the demand for structural materials that maintain supe -
rior mechanical properties under extreme thermal exposure. The TZM 
alloy (Mo-0.5Ti-0.1Zr-0.06C), characterized by its high melting point 
and outstanding high-temperature strength, has emerged as a prime 
candidate material for critical applications including hot-working dies 
and aerospace propulsion components [ 1 – 3 ]. However, TZM alloys face 
several challenges in service under high temperature environments 
(above 1000 
◦
C), including insufficient resistance to high temperature 
oxidation, thermal fatigue damage, and limited high temperature creep 
resistance due to inadequate high temperature strength. In addition, two 
intrinsic limitations hinder the broader implementation of TZM alloys: 
significant low-temperature brittleness and high ductile-to-brittle tran -
sition temperature (DBTT), both of which significantly escalate the risk 
of catastrophic failure without prior plastic deformation warning. The 
high stacking fault energy (SFE) intrinsic to the molybdenum matrix 
severely restricts the activation of toughening mechanisms like 
twinning-induced plasticity (TWIP) and transformation-induced plas -
ticity (TRIP) even at elevated temperatures [ 4 ]. Additionally, incoherent 
micron-scale intergranular secondary particles induce localized stress 
concentration during tensile deformation, promoting premature micro -
crack nucleation and propagation, which combined account for the 
inadequate ductility [ 5 – 7 ]. While annealing can partially mitigate the 
brittleness of TZM alloys [ 8 ], which inevitably compromises the strength 
at room/high-temperature, leading to an inherent dilemma between 
strength and ductility for TZM alloys.

The limited ductility of TZM alloys fundamentally stems from the 
constrained dislocation slip during plastic deformation. Previous 
research has predominantly focused on traditional methods such as 
grain boundary engineering [ 9 , 10 ] and second-phase strengthening 
[ 11 ] to optimize the mechanical properties of molybdenum alloys. 
However, these strategies exhibit limited efficacy in property enhance -
ment. Hu conducted a comparative study of rolling versus rotary forging 
in TZM alloy processing under equivalent deformation [ 12 ], which 
revealed that the rolled alloy achieved a high room-temperature 
strength of 650 MPa, whereas it exhibited insufficient elongation 
(~15 %) alongside a 40 % reduction in high-temperature strength 
compared to forged sample. Similarly, Li developed LaB
6
-doped Mo al -
loys through low-temperature sintering, achieving an UTS of 980 MPa in 
sintered billets [ 13 ]. However, the plastic deformation was suppressed 
by ultrafine-grains and lead to brittle fracture. Critically, current ap -
proaches fail to fundamentally modify deformation mechanisms or 
adequately activate dislocation slip in Mo alloys, resulting in an inverted 
relationship between strength and plasticity.
Recent advances in HDI strengthening which pioneered by Zhu [ 14 ] 
and Wu [ 15 ] have demonstrated a viable method for achieving the 
synergy of strength-ductility. This mechanism fundamentally relies on 
microstructural heterogeneity, including dual-phase structure [ 16 ], 
gradient structure [ 17 ], harmonic structure [ 18 ], and bimodal grain 
structure [ 19 ]. During deformation, significant mechanical property 
disparities at heterogeneous interfaces can induce the generation of HDI 
stresses, promoting geometrically necessary dislocations (GNDs) for -
mation and thereby enhancing strain-hardening capacity. Yang devel -
oped heterostructure high-entropy alloys (HEAs) through severe 
deformation rolling followed by prolonged annealing [ 20 ]. The syner -
gistic effects of hierarchical precipitates, TRIP, and HDI strengthening 
achieved a 12 % ductility enhancement without strength compromise. 
Gu engineered harmonic structure in pure Cu through powder metal -
lurgy, enabling adjustable strength-ductility synergy via ultrafine- 
grained (UFG) and coarse-grained (CG) ratio modulation [ 21 ]. 
Notably, DBs are demonstrated to simultaneously improve both strength 
and ductility in heterogeneous FCC/BCC-structured refractory high- 
entropy alloys (RHEAs) [ 22 , 23 ], in which DBs simultaneously hinder 
dislocation and promote the HDI effect, significantly improving work- 
hardening capabilities. Although introducing DBs to create heteroge -
neous structures represents a feasible strategy for activating HDI 
strengthening and enhancing dislocation mobilization, this approach 
remains poorly explored in TZM alloys.
Inspired by preceding research, in this work, we propose a strategy in 
which employing isothermal rolling induces DBs into TZM alloy to 
construct HL structures, combined with annealing to enhance the me -
chanical properties. The HL structures alternately are composed of 
bundled grains and deformation bands (DBs). The synergistic of HDI 
strengthening and subgrain strengthening improves the strain hardening 
capacity and high temperature strength, thereby achieving an optimal 
balance between the high temperature strength and room temperature 
ductility. Furthermore, we elucidate the influence mechanism of 
annealing on microstructures and mechanical properties, providing 
novel design principles for achieving high-performances hetero -
structured TZM alloys.

fect [ 22 , 42 ]. During the high temperature tensile deformation, < 110 >

4. Conclusions
(1) The as-rolled TZM alloy exhibits HL structures, including α -fiber 
and γ -fiber textures. DBs are formed by shearing and kinking in 
the microstructures.
(2) Annealing at 1000 
◦
C induces the transformation of entangled 
dislocations into PDWs, which subsequently form subgrain 
boundaries. Prolonged annealing initially increases the γ -fiber 
content (peaking at annealing for 3 h) and then declines, and the 
subgrains gradually merge. Complete SRX occurs at 1400 
◦
C, 
resulting in bundled grains evolving into rod-shaped grains.
(3) The ZT sample exhibits room-temperature UTS and ductility of 
935 MPa and 21.5 %, respectively, with a high-temperature UTS 
of 454 MPa. After annealing for 3 h, the 1000 – 3 sample demon -
strates optimal mechanical properties due to the synergistic 
strengthening effect of γ -fiber, PDWs and subgrain boundaries. 
The total elongation and high temperature UTS are enhanced to 
25 % and 572 MPa, respectively, while the high-temperature 
strength is decreased to 549 MPa owing to subgrain coarsening 
after annealing for 5 h.
(4) The synergistic effect of high γ -fiber contents and substructures 
promotes the dynamic grain refinement and HDI stress, thereby 
enhancing the UTS at elevated temperatures.
