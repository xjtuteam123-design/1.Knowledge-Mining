1. Introduction
Multi-principal element alloys (MPEAs), also known as high-entropy 
alloys [1] , are composed of numerous elements with nearly equal atomic 
ratios. These alloys exhibit excellent physical, chemical, and mechanical 
properties attributed to their unique composition. The emergence of 
MPEAs challenges principles of traditional alloy design, which typically 
focuses on alloys with a dominant principal element. By adopting a 
multi-element, equi-atomic ratio approach, the new paradigm has 
significantly broadened the horizons for alloy design and application. 
NiCoCr MPEAs have been widely used in aerospace, energy production, 
and petrochemical industries due to their excellent resistance to 
high-temperature oxidation, fatigue, corrosion, along with their favor -
able mechanical characteristics [2 – 5] . They are particularly suited for 
use as critical structural components in extreme environments, 
including gas turbine blades and nuclear reactor fuel cladding. The 
intricate microstructures of NiCoCr MPEAs may consist of Face-Center 
Cubic (FCC), Body-Center Cubic (BCC) phases or a mixture of the two, 
as well as abundant defect structures like dislocations and twins. These 
complex microstructural features have an important influence on the 
properties of the alloys.
Cracking phenomena may occur in MPEAs during long-term service, 
particularly under complex stress and environmental conditions. The 
initiation and propagation of cracks are directly related to the life and 
safety of alloy components. Crack propagation is a key factor influencing 
the mechanical properties of materials, especially critical in assessing 
the fatigue and fracture susceptibility of engineering structures [6] . 
MPEAs often exhibit distinct fatigue properties compared to conven -
tional single-phase or two-phase alloys. Consequently, NiCoCr MPEAs 
may display unique crack propagation behavior due to their specific 
chemical composition and microstructure. The investigation of the 
relationship between the crackgrowth rates and various loading condi -
tions, temperature, corrosive media and other factors in NiCoCr MPEAs 
can contribute to enhancing the mechanical properties of these alloys.
In the field of mechanical property research on NiCoCr multi- 
principal element alloys, the study by Wu et al. [7] in 2014 marked a 
significant advancement. The research revealed that within the tem -
perature range of 77 K to 673 K, the yield strength, tensile strength, and 
uniform elongation of the alloy increased significantly with decreasing 
temperature, reaching their optimal values at 77 K. These findings not 
only enriched the understanding of the performance of NiCoCr alloys 
under various temperature conditions but also provided valuable data

also particularly important for the selection or design of alloys with

In actual physical experiments, the effects of temperature and strain 
rate on the mechanical properties of Multi-Principal Element Alloys 
have been partially explored in some tensile and compressive tests. 
However, the strain rates were relatively low, and in some cases, the 
temperature ranges considered were narrow. With the continuous 
improvement of computational power and the refinement of simulation 
algorithms, numerical simulation has become an indispensable compo -
nent of modern materials science research. Due to the unique chemical 
composition and complex microstructure of MPEAs, traditional experi -
mental methods often struggle to fully elucidate their intrinsic structure- 
property relationships and their dynamic evolution. Consequently, the 
use of Molecular Dynamics (MD) simulations to investigate the defor -
mation mechanisms, strengthening mechanisms, and fracture behaviors 
of MPEAs has emerged as a significant research trend [12] . MD simu -
lations serve as a powerful tool for investigating plastic deformation, 
mechanical properties, and solidification process in MPEAs, supple -
menting existing experimental techniques and theoretical frameworks. 
Through MD simulations, the formation and propagation of cracks can 
be observed at the atomic level, thereby revealing the microscopic 
mechanisms that govern crack extension. This insight can guide the 
microstructural design of alloys to optimize their mechanical properties. 
Numerous studies have utilized MD simulations to explore the defor -
mation mechanism of MPEAs [13 – 15] .
Despite extensive research, the microstructural evolution and crack 
extension mechanism of NiCoCr MPEAs at low, medium, and high 
temperatures, as well as under varying strain rates, remain poorly un -
derstood. MD simulations serve as a powerful complement to experi -
mental methods, offering the capability to dynamically monitor 
microstructural changes that are challenging to capture experimentally. 
This approach provides an effective way to explore the mechanical 
properties and microdeformation mechanism of MPEAs at the nano -
scale. Therefore, in this study, an MD simulation approach is utilized to 
investigate the crack propagation behavior in NiCoCr MPEAs under 
tensile loading at different temperatures and strain rates. By exploring 
the microstructure of NiCoCr during the initiation and progression of 
internal cracks, and by revealing the physical mechanisms and rules 
governing crack propagation, we aim to identify the factors that inhibit 
crack initiation and growth. We anticipate that this work will provide 
strong theoretical support for the design and application of the new 
generation of high-performance alloys.
2. Simulation method and details
The core of MD simulation is potential function (or force field), 
which defines the interaction forces between particles in the alloy sys -
tem. In the simulation, the force between atoms is derived from the 
potential function, which will affect the evolution of the entire system, 
so the selection of the potential function is very important for the ac -
curacy of the simulation results. In this study, the Embedded Atom 
Method (EAM) developed by MA [16] et al. was used to describe the 
atomic interaction in Ni-Co-Cr system, this function is obtained by 
first-principles calculation (such as plane energy, stacking fault energy, 
etc.) and calibration of experimental results. It can accurately describe 
the mechanical properties of NiCoCr such as structure, dislocation 
nucleation [13 – 15,17 – 19] .
In this paper, a NiCoCr MPEAs model is selected to explore the effects 
of different temperatures and tensile strain rates on the mechanical and 
microstructural aspects of the model during tensile stretching. Fig. 2
constructs a sample of rectangular NiCoCr MPEAs with a completely 
random atomic distribution pre-existing edge crack embedding. The 
initial length of the crack is 89 Å (about one-fourth of the x[010] di -
rection), and the lattice constant is chosen to be 3.58 Å. The cell size 
along the x[100], y[010] and z[001] directions is 358 Å × 179 Å 
× 358 Å, respectively, and contains a total of 2002,650 atoms. The 
leading edge of the crack is in the x-direction, and unidirectional 
stretching along the z-direction is simulated. To ensure the dimensional 
reliability of the doping models, two larger-scale atomic models were 
constructed in this study. Model A extends the side length in the y -di -
rection and contains 100, 100, and 100 layers of atoms along the x -, y -, 
and z -axes, respectively; while model B extends the side length in the x - 
and z -directions and contains 200, 100, and 200 layers of atoms along 
the x -, y -, and z -axes, respectively. In Table 1 , we add the data for these 
two large models at different yield stresses and strains, while in Fig. 1 , 
we add the plots of atomic structure changes for model A at different 
temperatures. The results of the analyses show that the evolutionary 
trend of the atomic configurations is essentially independent of the size 
of the system, and therefore the smaller model was chosen in this study 
for further analyses. To enhance the reliability of the computational 
results, three independent stochastic distribution models were con -
structed and employed in the simulations. Some of the results that will 
be mentioned in the subsequent parts of this paper are derived based on 
the average of these three independent models.

It is well known that lattice distortion effects are closely related to 
the structural and mechanical properties of multi-principal element al -
loys, and it is common practice to use Mean Square Displacement (MSD) 
to detect lattice distortions in NiCoCr, as well as other reported MPEAs 
[25] . The resistance to dislocation motion depends entirely on the dis -
torted lattice, and thus the trend of yield strength can be predicted by 
analysing the results of a suitable MSD, which is an important scalar 
parameter for predicting yield strength [26] . The variation of MSD 
versus time at five temperatures, 77 K, 298 K, 800 K and 1100 K is 
plotted as shown in Fig. 5 (a). It can be seen from the figure that the MSD 
value of the alloy system increases with increasing temperature. The 
Radial Distribution Function (RDF) versus distance is also plotted for 
each temperature as shown in Fig. 5 (b), the height of the peaks in the 
RDF plot corresponds to the probability of finding an atom at a partic -
ular interatomic distance. It can be observed that the height of the peak 
decreases with increasing temperature from 77 K to 1100 K. The prob -
ability of finding an atom at a given distance decreases with increasing 
interatomic distance. This means that as the temperature increases, the 
atoms gain kinetic energy and begin to move vibrating faster, and the 
resulting thermal motion of the atoms causes the atoms to occupy a 
larger volume, the spacing between the nearest neighboring atoms in -
creases, and the alloy system becomes more chaotic, with higher free 
energies and lower stability. In addition, the thermal motion of the 
atoms also weakens the bonds between the atoms, this is because the 
gravitational force between the atoms decreases with increasing dis -
tance, which leads to a decrease in the potential energy, the atomic 
bonds are easily broken, and the MPEAs lattice is more likely to be 
deformed at high temperatures, which is in agreement with the results 
obtained by the previous researchers [27] .

The dislocation motion at the crack tip is crucial for the plastic 
deformation process of the alloy. Fig. 10 shows an enlarged view of the 
crack tip portion of NiCoCr MPEAs under uniaxial stretching at a tem -
perature of 298 K illustrating the microstructural evolution of the crack 
tip portion of NiCoCr MPEAs, In this figure, the grey debris-like structure 
is the dislocation lattice due to stretching. It is observed that during 
crack extension, dislocations tend to be emitted in the crack tip region 
due to the susceptibility of stress concentration in the crack tip portion. 
Since the emission of dislocations affects the stress distribution, the 
primary cracking mechanism changes from fully brittle cracking to 
plastic behaviour of dislocation nucleation when the loading stress 
surpasses the critical stress associated with crack extension. When the 
strain from defects at the crack tip (point defects, dislocations and twins) 
and severe lattice distortions exceeds the maximum elastic energy 
within the crystal, amorphous nucleation occurs due to elastic instability 
at high deformation rates according to the Born criterion [31] . As the 
strain increases, dislocations continue to be generated and interact with 
small amorphous phases, causing the lattice to collapse, leading to the 
expansion of the amorphous zone.

cation density ( ρ ) is a key parameter for measuring the plastic defor -
mation properties of a material, which is defined as the ratio of the total 
length of the dislocation lines ( L ) to the volume of the material ( V ), viz:

system at different temperatures and strain rates were studied. The re -
sults indicate that the yield stress of NiCoCr MPEAs exhibits a decreasing 
trend with increasing temperature. Analysis of the Radial Distribution 
Function and the Atomic Mean Square Displacement confirmed that the 
high temperatures impart greater kinetic energy to the atoms, leading to 
increased system disorder, elevated free energy, and reduced stability, 
which in turn causes a decrease in stress. And the high temperature will 
lead to the emergence of vacancy structure inside the alloy, destroying 
the FCC system of the alloy itself and reducing the strength of the alloy.
The tensile strength and plasticity of NiCoCr multi-principal element 
alloys exhibit a pronounced dependence on the strain rate. Specifically, 
the tensile strength of NiCoCr MPEAs enhances with increasing strain 
rate; the faster the tensile velocity, the greater the alloy ’ s resistance to 
deformation. This phenomenon is attributed to heightened dislocation 
densities and the formation of dislocation locks within the alloys under 
conditions of high strain rates. Dislocation nucleation and propagation 
are intimately related to plastic deformation processes. The 
1 / 6 〈 112 〉( Shockly ) dislocations are the main cause of laminar disloca -
tions in the crystal structure, which are critical to the deformation 
behaviour. Higher dislocation densities lead to entangled intersection of 
dislocations within the alloy, which hinders plastic deformation of the 
alloy. Additionally, the presence of Lomer-Cottrell (L-C) locks can like -
wise elevate internal stresses within the alloy and inhibit the decrease in 
the stress-strain curve, which can effectively enhance both the ultimate 
strength and the work-hardening capacity of the alloy.
During crack propagation, dislocations are initially emitted from the 
crack tip due to its susceptibility to stress concentration. The presence of 
these dislocations helps to relieve the concentrated stresses at the crack 
tip. Since the dislocation emission alters the stress distribution, when the 
loading stress exceeds the critical level for crack extension, the primary 
cracking mechanism transitions from fully brittle fracture to a more 
plastic behaviour characterized by dislocation nucleation.
This study provides new insights into the performance variations of 
NiCoCr multi-principal element alloys under different temperatures and 
strain rates. To further advance research in this field, future researchers 
should consider improving computational accuracy, particularly in the 
refinement of the potential function, as well as delving into the impact of 
microstructure on the alloy ’ s tensile properties. These measures will 
provide a more solid foundation for the design and performance opti -
mization of the alloys.
