ABSTRACT 
A novel approach based on rapid thermal annealing and advanced electron microscopy is used to elucidate the 
differences in early stages of high-temperature oxidation of Ni- and Co-base superalloys at 900 
◦
C. STEM-EDX 
results obtained for two model superalloys with exactly the same alloying additions demonstrate the individ -
ual role of the γ and γ’ phase during initial oxidation. Observed differences in Al
2
O
3 
formation can be directly 
correlated to changes in the partitioning behavior and the type of defects introduced during oxidation, depending 
on the base element. Explanations for the worse oxidation resistance of γ’ -strengthened Co-base superalloys are 
proposed. 
1. Introduction 
Ni-base single-crystal (SX) superalloys exhibit excellent high- 
temperature properties owing to their characteristic two-phase γ / γ’ 
microstructure. Nevertheless, these conventional alloy systems are ex -
pected to reach fundamental limits in the near future. They are not 
capable to withstand further increase of the operating temperatures 
(reaching today 1400 
◦
C when additional coatings are applied), which 
would be one opportunity to further increase the efficiency of modern jet 
engines. With imperative demands to reduce CO
2

multicomponent γ’ -strengthened Co-base SX superalloys, like ERBOCo-1 
with nominal composition 44.5Co-32Ni-8Al-5W-8Cr-2.5Ti-1.5Ta-0.4Si- 
0.1Hf [ 1 ] may play an important role in prospective applications. This 
arise from the similar microstructure to γ’ -strengthened Ni-base SX su -
peralloys and 40 
◦
C higher melting point of Co compared to Ni [ 2 ]. 
Co-base SX superalloys have proven remarkable mechanical properties, 
including high creep resistance [ 3 ] and fatigue strength [ 4 ]. However, 
poor oxidation resistance of this new class of superalloys at 900 
◦
C and 
above, so far significantly limits the range of possible applications. In 
contrast, the high-temperature oxidation resistance of uncoated Ni-base 
superalloys is ensured by the formation of a continuous layer of ther -

Ni-base superalloys [ 6 ]. For the hottest sections of the turbine, where 
additional coatings are used, this helps also to reduce the negative

Though extensive research on high-temperature oxidation of 
γ’ -strengthened Ni-base SX superalloys is available in the literature, 
there are clear indications that the oxidation mechanism of 
γ’ -strengthened Co-base SX superalloys significantly differs from that of 
Ni-base superalloys. It should be emphasized that the direct comparison 
is difficult due to different compositions of the alloying elements typi -
cally added to Ni- and Co superalloys, which also contributes to changes 
in the oxidation mechanism. In two recent studies [ 11 , 12 ], a model SX 
superalloy series with exactly the same concentration of alloying ele -
ments and change of base element from Ni to Co, was investigated 
regarding to the oxidation behavior. Long-term oxidation of SX 
Co-9Al-8W-8Cr (at.%) led to more extensive Al and Cr depletion in 
sub-scale regions and more severe dissolution of the original γ / γ’ 
microstructure in comparison to SX Ni-9Al-8W-8Cr (at.%). In addition, 
Ni-9Al-8W-8Cr (at.%) alloy demonstrated the growth of a continuous 
and diffusion-limiting Ni-Cr-spinel, which was not the case for its 
Co-base counterpart. Moreover, in a variety of Co-base SX superalloys, 
formation of the brittle Co
3
W phase (detrimental for mechanical prop -
erties) is observed during thermal exposure [ 11 – 13 ]. In Ni-base super -
alloys, formation of similar phases caused by Al depletion is not a critical 
issue, even when W concentration is high [ 11 , 12 ]. To understand the 
reported changes in the internal oxidation zone (IOZ), especially after 
initiation of considerable oxide growth, fundamental differences be -
tween these two types of superalloys need to be further investigated. 
During long-term exposure, the focus is on steady-state reaction kinetics. 
Consequently, oxygen transport through the unaltered two-phase 
microstructure is difficult to address. Due to experimental challenges, 
extensive elucidations of processes right after the initiation of oxidation, 
when morphology and composition of the two-phase microstructure still 
dominate the material transport, are rarely reported in the literature. 
Such investigations require advanced microscopy techniques that offer 
high-resolution quantification of elemental composition in order to 
reveal the mechanisms governing oxidation at very early stages [ 14 , 15 ]. 
In the present study, we take advantage of the unique features of a 
Rapid Thermal Annealing (RTA) furnace, typically used for semi -
conductor processing [ 16 ], to realize ultrashort oxidation experiments 
at 900 
◦
C down to oxidation times of seconds. To demonstrate the dif -
ferences between oxidation mechanisms of γ’ -strengthened Ni- and 
Co-base SX superalloys, we examine two model alloys developed and 
characterized by Zenk et al. at [ 8 , 17 ] with the nominal same amount of 
Al, Cr and W: Ni-9Al-8W-8Cr (at.%) and Co-9Al-8W-8Cr (at.%). To 
enable dynamic observation of microstructural evolution during 
oxidation, Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM) samples were 
oxidized at times ranging from 2 s to 512 s. Scanning transmission 
electron microscopy (STEM) and Energy-dispersive X-ray (EDX) spec -
troscopy techniques were used to characterize the morphology and 
chemical composition of the oxide phases as well as the depletion of 
elements in the γ / γ’ microstructure beneath. The temporal evolution of 
the oxide formation was characterized in plan-view using identical 
location STEM-EDX complemented by cross-section investigations on 
focused ion beam (FIB) prepared TEM lamellae. The mechanisms of 
oxide nucleation and growth is compared for the mentioned model 
Ni-base and Co-base SX superalloy and underlying reasons for the 
different oxidation behavior commonly observed between those two 
classes of superalloys are discussed. 
2. Material and methods 
Two single-crystal model alloys Ni-9Al-8W-8Cr (at.%) and Co-9Al- 
8W-8Cr (at.%), further referred as Ni-SX and Co-SX (see Table 1 ), 
were cast using the Bridgman process by the Institute of Metal Science 
and Technology (FAU, Germany). A detailed consideration of the 
complete model alloy system can be found elsewhere [ 8 , 17 ]. After so -
lution heat treatment at 1250 
◦
C for 24 h, both alloys were aged at 900 
◦
C for 100 h to facilitate γ’ precipitation. The original microstructure 
prior to oxidation was characterized using STEM and EDX. 
To enable comprehensive characterization of the samples after 
oxidation during early stages, a dedicated work-flow, presented in Fig. 1 
was developed. TEM samples with [001] foil normal were prepared by 
electropolishing to expose the γ / γ’ microstructure. Subsequent Ar + ion 
beam polishing using a precision ion polishing system (PIPS) guaranteed 
a high quality of the sample surfaces. The superalloy TEM samples were 
oxidized using a RTA furnace. Rapid Thermal Annealing is a processing 
step in semiconductor technology designed for short-duration and high- 
temperature annealing in controlled atmospheres. The heating is real -
ized by halogen lamps located behind a quartz window. This allows for 
ultrafast and precise heating and cooling thus enabling short annealing 
times typically ranging from seconds to minutes. Moreover, different gas 
mixtures and a wide range of gas pressures can be applied. This together 
makes the RTA a suitable tool for high-temperature oxidation experi -
ments that particularly address early stages. Model superalloys were 
oxidized at 900 
◦
C for different times, ranging from 2 s to 512 s in a dry 
air atmosphere. Prior and after oxidation, the chamber was purged with 
pure argon. Fast heating and cooling rates of 20 
◦
C/s were applied for 
accurate inspection of diffusion-controlled phenomena. 
Identical-location STEM-EDX technique, interrupted with RTA 
oxidation has been applied to follow microstructural and compositional 
changes from the initial stage and during following oxidation times of 2, 
8, 16 and 32 s. A dual-beam FIB-SEM Helios NanoLab 660 was used to 
extract cross section lamellae from the TEM samples oxidized at 8, 32, 
64, 128, 256 and 512 s. For this preparation, samples were cut 
perpendicular to the edges of the γ’ cubes. To eliminate additional fac -
tors that could influence the significance of the conducted analysis, all 
TEM samples were cut from dendritic regions. It should be emphasized 
that cross-sections were prepared from thick regions of the TEM sam -
ples, representing bulk behavior. This approach offers the unique pos -
sibility to combine high-resolution studies of the nucleation of new 
phases on the surface with detailed insights into internal oxide growth 
during early stages of oxidation. Microstructural and chemical compo -
sition analyses were performed at 300 kV with a double-corrected FEI 
Titan Themis TEM equipped with a SuperX detector. Combined plan- 
view and cross-section analysis enabled to obtain 3D information on 
the microscopic oxidation process including the distinct behavior of the 
γ and γ’ phase. Complementary to this, the surface was studied by 
scanning electron microscopy to gain a more detailed insight in the 
external oxidation mechanism.

3.4. Oxygen transport paths 
Despite decades of research on the oxidation behavior of 
γ’ -strengthened Ni-base superalloys, little is known about the oxygen 
diffusion path in the alloy [ 15 ]. For the more recently developed 
γ’ -strengthened Co-base superalloys, even fewer reports that consider 
elemental transport are available. It is commonly accepted that prefer -
ential oxygen transport during oxidation of polycrystalline superalloys 
takes place along grain boundaries (GB) of both the oxide and alloy 
substrate serving as fast-diffusion paths [ 29 , 30 ]. Some theories consider 
also metal/Al
2
O

diffusion in diluted Ni-Al alloys [ 31 ]. For SX superalloys, oxygen diffuses 
to a large extent along GB of the external oxide. Tracer diffusion ex -

have demonstrated that in principal the same transport mechanisms 
occur in Ni-SX and Co-SX during steady-state growth [ 12 ]. Nevertheless, 
oxygen diffusion in the IOZ or during early stages of oxidation, when the 
outer oxide is not yet developed, remains unclear. Therefore, in this 
section a comprehensive review of oxygen diffusion in Ni- and Co-base 
superalloys through the internal oxidation zone will be presented. 
Considering the different properties of γ and γ’ , the results obtained 
in this study as well as recent reports [ 14 , 15 , 33 ], the two-phase 
microstructure plays an important role during oxidation, in particular 
during early stages. Taking into account that the γ / γ’ interface is 
approximately 2 nm wide [ 34 , 35 ], it was proposed that atomistic mis -
matching of the matrix and precipitate can serve as preferential diffu -
sion path for oxygen [ 14 , 15 , 33 , 36 ]. Edmonds et al. [ 36 ] observed 
selective γ’ oxidation in a fourth generation Ni-base superalloy. The 
study furthermore revealed that γ channels in the investigated alloy are 
covered by porous surface oxides and therefore act as access paths for O. 
On the other hand, Weiser et al. showed preferential γ oxidation with 
simultaneous γ’ dissolution in the Co-Al-W model SX superalloys [ 14 ]. In 
[ 15 ], in situ TEM studies on a Ni-base SX superalloy revealed preferential 
oxygen diffusion along γ / γ’ interface. The same results were also 
demonstrated during recent in situ exposures under mechanical load 
[ 33 ], where Al
2
O
3 
formation was detected at the γ / γ’ interface. These 
findings are in agreement with our observations for Ni-SX. Fig. 5 a and b 
show that the IOF propagates along the γ / γ’ interface and at the same 
depth from the surface oxidation is limited to γ’ without affecting γ (see 
blue arrow on the O EDX map). We did not observe such behavior for 
Co-SX, which suggests the existence of another, more energetically 
favorable way of oxygen transport. 
Other commonly known fast-diffusion paths, however mainly 
considered for alloying elements, are crystal defects, like dislocations or 
stacking faults [ 37 – 40 ]. It was proven that intentionally introduced 
deformation in near-surface regions leads to a significant increase in 
internal oxidation kinetics and can trigger faster Al
2
O

for Ni- [ 30 , 33 ] and Co-base superalloys [ 13 ], due the development of 
fast-diffusion paths for the light alloying element (Al). Nonetheless, 
defects may arise not only under (external) loading conditions but also 
as result of stress associated with the volume expansion of the growing 
oxide precipitates in the IOZ [ 41 – 44 ]. Oxide growth generates stresses in 
the superalloy directly beneath the propagating alloy/oxide interface. 
During the early stages of oxidation, crystal defects locally form to 
release these stresses, while in the long term, the consequence may be 
the spallation of large segments of the oxide scale [ 5 ]. In our study, we 
observed defect formation both for Ni-SX and for Co-SX. We propose that 
those defects not only serve as fast-diffusion paths for oxygen but also 
have a direct impact on the oxidation mechanism. The concluded model 
predictions are described in more detail in the next section.

where supersaturation of oxygen occurs, providing conditions for oxide 
growth with regular morphology, terminated by {111} planes.

Development of new generation γ’ -strengthened Co-base SX super -

alloying elements on the oxidation mechanism. However, typical alloy 
development practices base on the simultaneous change of several 
alloying elements to maintain alloy microstructural stability [ 5 ], which 
makes it difficult to determine the effect of each single element. 
Therefore, modelling of the oxidation behavior based on the alloy 
composition remains a challenge [ 55 – 57 ], also due to lack of reliable 
thermodynamic and kinetic data for mixed oxides, which concurrently 
form with Al
2

fluence of the two-phase microstructure on the early stages of oxidation 
behavior is critical. Promising results were reported for predictions 
based on DICTRA [ 58 ], which can be applied to multicomponent envi -
ronments. Yet, as the accuracy of simulations depends on the available 
experimental data, a better understanding of the mechanisms that are 
responsible for the differences between oxidation resistances of Ni- and 
Co-base superalloys is necessary for reliable modelling. 
Although model superalloys used in this study were not desgined to 
provide good oxidation resistance, they allow to eliminate misinter -
pretation of the obtained results that could be attributed to different 
concentrations of the alloying elements, typically present in Ni- and Co- 
base superalloys. Moreover, our insights can be directly correlated to the 
differnces in oxidation mechanisms commonly reported for the 
γ’ -strengthened Ni- and Co-base SX superalloys. Our investigations of 
the early stages of oxidation of model Ni-SX and Co-SX superalloys help 
also to better understand the behavior of these alloys during long 
oxidation exposure (up to 100 h) that we investigated in earlier studies 
[ 8 , 11 , 12 ]. During longer exposure times however, the two-phase 
microstructure and thus the demonstrated differences in segregation 
behavior of key elements, play a minor role. Nevertheless, it was pro -
posed that insufficient oxidation resistance of Co-base superalloys can be 
correlated to lower diffusivity of Al and Cr in Co-base superalloys in 
comparison to Ni-base superalloys [ 11 , 59 ]. Avoiding TCP phases is one

stages of oxidation (up to 512 s) in the present study we did not observe

porosity and enhanced oxygen penetration. In the following, the influ -
ence of the two-phase microstructure on early stages of oxidation, 
depending on the base element (Ni or Co), will be addressed to com -

4. Conclusions 
Our approach provided a unique opportunity to explain the differ -
ences in oxidation mechanism of Ni- and Co-base superalloys that are 
frequently reported in literature. Our findings show that optimization of 
the alloy composition of Co-base superalloys with sufficient oxidation 
resistance needs to include considerations regarding partitioning of el -
ements to the γ and γ’ phases together with prediction of defect for -
mation mechanisms of the alloy. During the discussion of results, it 
became apparent that the investigated early stages influence the 
oxidation behavior during longer durations of exposure that was in the 
center of previous studies. In more detail, our dedicated analysis allowed 
us to draw the following conclusions: 
• Al
2
O
3 
formation is dependent on the Al availability and composition 
of the γ’ phase. For Ni-SX, alumina preferentially grows in γ’ 
(following partitioning behavior), whereas for Co-SX nucleation in γ 
(against partitioning behavior) prevails. 
• W enrichment in the γ’ phase slows down Al diffusion and limits 
Al
2
O
3 
formation. 
• Internal oxidation introduces mechanical stresses that result in for -
mation of crystal defects, which are distinctively different for Ni-SX 
(perfect dislocations) and Co-SX (stacking faults) and nicely re -
sembles defects observed in creep experiments at similar tempera -
ture. The defects have direct impact on the morphology of the oxide 
precipitates and on the oxidation mechanism. 
• During early stages of oxidation, Ni-SX samples exhibit a uniform IOF 
development with oxygen diffusion along the γ / γ’ interface and 
along dislocations resulting from oxide growth stresses. In Co-SX, the 
IOF is much less uniform with strong local variations. Stacking faults
