Eﬀect of the alloying element on the temperature-
dependent ideal shear strength of g0-Ni3Al
Xiaoxia Wu ab and Chongyu Wang *ac
The temperature-dependent ideal shear strength ( sIS)o f g0-Ni3Al and the e ﬀects of alloying (Re, Ru, Mo, Cr,
Co, W, and Ta) were studied in terms of ﬁrst-principles calculations within the {111} slip plane along the
[11/C0 2] and [ /C0 110] directions. Based on the predicted volume versus temperature relations, the
temperature dependence of sIS was obtained using the quasistatic approach, where it was found that sIS
gradually decreased with increasing temperature. For the {111}[11 /C0 2] slip systems, all of the alloying
elements except for Co were found to improve the sIS of g0-Ni3Al at the considered temperature. Among
these elements, Re was the most e ﬀective element, and doping a small amount of Re ( /C24 3 at%) was
found to increase sIS by /C24 23.5% at 0 K. Next, W and Mo increased the sIS by 20.2 and 18.1%, respectively.
For the {111}[ /C0 110] slip systems, only Re increased the sIS of g0-Ni3Al while the other dopants decreased
the ideal shear strength of the system at 0 K. Interestingly, a high-temperature relative strengthening
via W alloying was observed for the g0-Ni3Al phase within the {111}[ /C0 110] slip system at temperatures
T > 800 K. However, the sequence of the sIS of the g0-Ni3Al phase doped with other alloying elements
was not altered by increasing temperature. According to these calculations, Re and W at the Al sites
can e ﬀectively improve the shear resistance of the g
0-Ni3Al phase, and especially at high temperature.
Furthermore, the e ﬀect of alloying upon the shear strength is understood by the electronic
structure analysis.
1. Introduction
The Ni-based single crystal superalloy composed of the g-Ni
matrix and the g0-Ni3Al precipitate phases is an ideal structural
material at high operating temperatures and its stability is very
important for material applications.
1–3 The ideal shear strength
(ISS, sIS) is the minimum stress needed to plastically deform
a perfect crystal, and can be used to characterize the stability of
the material. The ISS is related to the stress necessary for the
nucleation of a dislocation and the formation of a stacking
fault.
4 The g0-Ni3Al, with its anomalous temperature depen-
dence of the yield stress, 5 is largely responsible for the strength
of superalloys and their resistance to deformation at high
temperatures. Therefore, a study of the mechanical behaviors of
the g
0 phase should partially re ect the global properties of Ni-
based superalloys. With the addition of platinum metals and
refractory elements such as Co, Cr, Mo, Re, Ru, Ta and W, the
overall performances of superalloys have largely been improved,
and especially the high-temperature creep rupture strength,
thermal fatigue resistance, and oxidation resistance.

Al site, so an investigation into the shear behaviors of Ni 3Al, and
especially the e ﬀect of alloying, is very necessary.
Measuring the ideal strength of a material is di ﬃcult using

2. Computational methodology
To estimate the nite-temperature thermodynamic properties
of g0-Ni3Al, the Helmholtz free energy F(V,T) at constant volume
V and temperature T were calculated by the quasi-harmonic
approximation rst-principles approach25,27 with the formalism
F(V,T) ¼ E0(V)+ Fel(V,T)+ Fvib(V,T), (1)
where Fel(V,T) is the thermal electronic contribution to the
Helmholtz free energy, which is very important for metal
because of the non-zero electronic density at the Fermi level.
The Fvib parameter is the vibrational free energy of the lattice
atoms described in the present work by phonon calculations,
and E
0(V) is the static energy at 0 K from the rst-principles
calculation. Using eqn (1), the Helmholtz free energies were
calculated at 11 volume points every 50 K from 0 to 1600 K,
which is close to the melting point of pure Ni
3Al (1663 K), and
tted to the Murnaghan equation of state 28 to determine the
equilibrium volume ( V0) and bulk modulus ( BT)a t nite
temperatures.
Subsequently, based on the predicted relation of equilibrium
volume versus temperature (V0(T)) of the g0-Ni3Al with the alloying
elements, we adopted a quasistatic approach recently proposed25
to predict the temperature-dependent ISS, sIS(T). Here, we
assumed that the temperature dependence of the ideal shear
strength was mainly caused by volume expansion, while anhar-
monic eﬀects, kinetic energy contributions anductuation of the
microscopic stress tensors were not considered. In the present
work, the shear deformation of the close-packed {111} plane
along the [11/C0 2] and [ /C0 110] directions of g

To understand the electronic basis of the shear strengthening of
the g0-Ni3Al phase via alloying, we analyzed the charge density
diﬀerence (CDD) and the total electronic density of states
(TDOS) of the g0-Ni3Al phase. The CDD of Ni 24Al7X( X ¼ Re, W,
Mo, Cr, Ta, Ru, Co, and Al) on the (100) plane at zero strain are
shown in Fig. 4, where an obvious directional distribution
between X (Re, W, Mo, Cr and Ta) and their rst nearest

Re (W, Mo, Cr and Ta) form covalent-like bonding with the 1NN
Ni atoms, resulting in larger shear strength. The Co atom
appears to form a typical metallic-like bonding with the 1NN Ni
atom because the charge accumulation around Co is nearly
spherically symmetric. As a result, shear so ening is observed
in the Ni

Ru and 1NN Ni atom almost spherical symmetric and a weak
directional distribution is observed. In line with those dis-
cussed above, a small shear strengthening via Ru doping is

4. Conclusion
Using the rst-principles approach in combination with the
quasi-harmonic phonon approximation and the quasi-static
approximation, we systematically investigate the in uence of
the alloying elements X (X ¼ Re, Ru, Ta, W, Mo, Cr, and Co)
upon the temperature-dependent ideal shear strength (ISS) sIS
of the g0-Ni3Al phases for the two typical slip systems of {111}
[11/C0 2] and{111}[ /C0 110]. The results demonstrate that the ISS
gradually decreases with increasing temperature and the alloy-
ing does not alter the temperature dependence of the ISS.
Alloying considerably a ﬀects the ISS of the g
0-Ni3Al phase at
nite temperatures, however. For the {111}[11 /C0 2] slip systems,
almost all of the alloying elements except for Co improve the ISS
of the system at 0 K. Also, the corresponding critical shear strain
g
c is 0.18 with the addition of Re, Ru, Mo, Cr, and Co, which is
identical to the gc of the pure Ni3Al, but the gc value increases to
0.20 with the addition of W and Ta. Among the alloying
elements, Re, W, and Mo are the most eﬀective; but for the {111}
[/C0 110] slip systems, the addition of Re increases the ISS of Ni
3Al
consistent with the previous studies, and the other alloying
elements decrease the ISS of the system at 0 K. With increasing
temperature, W strengthens the g
0-Ni3Al phase at high
temperature ( T > 800 K), and the decreasing rate of the ISS as
a function of temperature for the g0-Ni3Al phase doped with Mo
gradually becomes small and thus the ISS of the Mo-doped
g
0-Ni3Al phase will be very close to the ISS of the W-doped and
undoped g0-Ni3Al phase at high temperature. In comparison,
the inuences of the other alloying elements are identical with
the strength at 0 K. The critical shear strain gc of {111}[ /C0 110]
slip systems reduces from 0.30 to 0.24 at 0 K for almost all
alloying elements except W, and g
c of Ni 3(Al, W) is 0.26.
Furthermore, analysis of the DOS of the Ni3(Al, X) (X ¼ Al, Re, W,
and Co) systems during the process of shear deformation is also
provided.
