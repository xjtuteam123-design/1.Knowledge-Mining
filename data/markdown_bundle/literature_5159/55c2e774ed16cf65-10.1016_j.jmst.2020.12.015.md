1. Introduction
Refractory high-entropy alloys (HEAs), containing multicom-
ponent metallic elements, have been considered as promising
candidates for high-temperature application [1–4]. They possess
the most compelling properties, especially high strength and good
high-temperature softening resistance. Among the reported refrac-
tory HEAs [5–7], MoNbTaW HEA with a good single BCC phase
possesses the highest softening resistance over a wide tempera-
ture range from 600−1600 ◦C [8–10]. However, MoNbTaW HEA
exhibits brittle fracture at 25 ◦C (failure strain of ∼2.1 %) which is
a major challenge for manufacturing work-pieces with this alloy,
limiting its industrial applications. It is also suggested that single-
phase HEAs suffer from insufﬁcient strength or ductility [11,12],
and poor castability [13].
In recent years, substantial research on dual-phase HEA materi-
als, with unique microstructure, has been conducted to achieve the
strength-ductility trade-off [3,14–22]. For example, AlCoCrFeNi2.1
and CoCrFeNiNbx HEAs with lamellar eutectic structures com-
posed of soft HEA and hard reinforced phases [3,14,15], and

consists of BCC refractory HEA and face-centered cubic (FCC) car-
bide with good bonding [17]. The BCC HEA is a solid solution of
Re, Mo, Nb, Ta, and W, and the ﬁve element compositions are all
between 10 at% and 35 at%. The FCC-type carbide is a hard multi-
component carbide (Ta, Nb, Mo, W)C containing many Ta and Nb,
which can be referred to as FCC-MC. Since this composite is com-
posed of refractory metals and TaC, it is expected to have good
high-temperature softening resistance. As reported in other stud-

◦C) phases [17] give Tr = 1950 ◦C and Tr = 2102 ◦C, respectively.

2. Materials and methods
2.1. Sample preparation and compression test
Composite ingots having a Re0.5MoNbW(TaC)0.5 (at%) composi-
tion were synthesized by arc-melting a mixture of corresponding
metals and TaC (with high-purity > 99.9 %) in an argon protec-
tive atmosphere. Each composite ingot was remelted ﬁve times
to guarantee a uniformly distributed composition. The ingots had
dimensions of ˚ 20−25 mm × 8 mm. To study the phase sta-
bility, the as-cast composite was sealed in a Quartz tube ﬁlled
with high-purity argon, placed in an electric furnace, and then
annealed at 1300 ◦C for 72 h (h) and 168 h, respectively, subse-
quently furnace-cooled to 20 ◦C. Equilibrium phase composition
of the composite was calculated using Thermo-Calc 2017a soft-
ware with TCNI8 database. The thermal analysis was carried out
by a STA449F3 simultaneous thermal analyzer (DSC-TG) at a heat-
ing rate of 10 ◦C/min up to 1400 ◦C in ﬂowing high-purity argon
atmosphere. Compression tests of cylindrical specimens with ˚ 4
mm × 6 mm were conducted at 1000 ◦C and 1200 ◦C in argon atmo-
sphere using a Gleeble-2000 thermal simulator with a strain rate
of 1 × 10−3 s-1. The test specimens were compressed to fracture or
to a 35 % height reduction, whichever occurred ﬁrst.
2.2. Microstructural test
The crystal structure was measured using a X’Pert MPD X-ray
diffraction (XRD) at a speed of 0.3◦/min with Cu K/H9251 radiation. The
microstructural characterization was done by a Quanta FEG250
scanning electron microscope (SEM) with back-scattered electron
(BSE), energy-dispersive spectrometry (EDS), and HKL-Max elec-
tron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) detectors. The phase fractions of
the as-cast sample were determined by numerous EBSD maps. The
EBSD data were analyzed using a TSL-OIM 5.0 data acquisition soft-
ware. The average lamellar spacing of the eutectic structures was
measured based on numerous SEM images by Image-Pro Plus 6.0
software. Detailed microstructure was tested using a Talos-F200S
transmission electron microscope (TEM) with ultra-high resolution
high angle annular dark ﬁeld (HAADF) detectors, and energy disper-
sive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) system. The samples for TEM were
thinned using a Gatan691 ion polishing system.
3. Results
3.1. Microstructure of the as-cast composite
The typical morphology of the as-cast Re0.5MoNbW(TaC)0.5
composite is shown in the EBSD phase map (Fig. 1(a)). The compos-
ite consisted of dual-phase comprising of BCC HEA matrix phase
and FCC-type carbide MC. The FCC-MC and BCC phases formed a
eutectic structure which was distributed in the grain boundary of
dendritic BCC primary phase. The statistical volume fractions of
BCC and FCC-MC phases were ∼ 80 % and ∼ 20 %, respectively. The
microstructure of BCC and FCC-MC was further clariﬁed by TEM
(Fig. 1(b)). The BCC phase had a lattice constant of 3.193 Å, while
that of FCC-MC was 4.416 Å. A small amount of dislocations with
curved morphologies were presented in the two phases. Fig. 1(c)
displays the high resolution TEM (HRTEM) image of BCC/FCC-MC
phase interface, showing that the two phases are well combined
with very low density of dislocations at the interface.

chemical compositions of BCC and FCC-MC phases changed little
after annealing. All these results showed that the annealing treat-

the microstructure of the Re0.5MoNbW(TaC)0.5 composite. To iden-

3.4. Deformation mechanisms via TEM characterizations
The microstructure of the as-cast composite after compression
at 1200 ◦C was examined using TEM, to identify the dislocation
activity at the phase interface and inside the lamellae interior,
understand its deformation mechanism, and clarify the origin of
high strength. Fig. 5(a–b) show representative HAADF-STEM micro-
graph and the corresponding TEM bright-ﬁeld micrograph of the
compression tested Re0.5MoNbW(TaC)0.5 composite. BCC and FCC-
MC dual phases with eutectic structure were observed, but no
cracks and new phase were seen. High density of dislocations was
introduced in the BCC and FCC-MC phases upon severe compres-
sion. The corresponding EDX maps indicate that the BCC HEA has
more Re, Mo, and W, whereas the FCC-MC carbide includes more
Nb, Ta, and C, which is consistent with the compositions of as-cast
Re0.5MoNbW(TaC)0.5 composite reported before [17].

3.4.2. Deformation of BCC HEA and FCC-MC carbide
Fig. 7(a) shows the TEM bright-ﬁeld micrograph of BCC HEA after
plastic deformation. The dislocation density increased dramatically
compared to the as-cast microstructure shown in Fig. 1(b). Severe
dislocation entanglement and dipolar dislocation walls were pre-
sented in the BCC HEA. Dislocation dipoles generated by dislocation
cross-slip and distinguishable pinning effect were clearly observed
in the deformed BCC phase, as shown in Fig. 7(b). In the BCC phase,
the substructures of dipolar dislocation walls originated from the
cross-slip of some screw dislocations, indicating the plastic defor-
mation of BCC HEA is predominated by wavy slip.
Fig. 8 shows the representative TEM bright-ﬁeld micrographs of
deformed FCC-MC carbide. The TEM images in Fig. 8(a–b) shows
that coplanar dislocation arrays produced by planar slip and stack-
ing faults were formed in FCC-MC phase after deformation. The
insets of the HRTEM image and FFT in Fig. 8(b) prove that defects
were stacking faults further. Deformation twins were also detected
in the deformed FCC-MC phase, as delineated in Fig. 8(c–d). Through
analysis, the deformation twin was identiﬁed by mirror symmetry.
The twin boundaries were recognized to be parallel to the {111}
planes. Meanwhile, multiple narrow twins with {111} type twin-
ning planes resided in and near the large twin boundaries, as shown
in Fig. 8(e). Stacking faults and dislocations can also be observed
here, indicating the large twin boundaries are strong obstacles for
dislocation movement.

4.2. Microstructural evolution
Fig. 9 shows the microstructural evolution schematic of the
Re0.5MoNbW(TaC)0.5 composite. The as-cast composite consisted
of BCC primary phase and lamellar eutectic structures. There exhib-
ited a small amount of dislocations in the two phases and a few
stack faults in the FCC-MC phase [17]. After compression at 1200
◦C, no new phases occurred. BCC and FCC-MC dual phases with
lamellar eutectic structures were maintained. High density dislo-
cations were formed in the BCC HEA with dipolar dislocation walls,
and lots of planer slip, stack faults and deformation twins were
activated in the FCC-MC carbide. It is analyzed that the BCC and
FCC-MC phases deformed primarily by dislocation slip in the ini-
tial stage of deformation. The numerous slip lines in the FCC-MC
carbide (Fig. 8(c)) substantiate this. At higher degrees of defor-
mation, dipolar dislocation walls formed in the BCC HEA, which
were commonly observed in BCC metals and the formation of wavy
slip patterns was due to frequent cross-slip caused by the sim-
ilarly close-packed {110} and {112} slip planes [35,36]. For the
FCC-MC carbide, high dislocation density could locally achieve the
high-resolution shear stress to activate mechanically induced twins
and serve as a precursor for the nucleation of deformation twins
(Fig. 8(c)). At room temperature, the stacking fault energy (SFE)
of the transition-metal carbides was generally high [37]. However,
brittle-ductile transition occurred at approximately one-third of
the melting temperature, Tm, for NbC, TaC and WC under compres-
sion [38,39]. It is reasonable to speculate that the FCC-MC carbide
would exhibit ductility above 1168 ◦C (one-third of Tm = 3504 ◦C for
the FCC-MC phase). Therefore, the FCC-MC phase exhibited signiﬁ-
cant plastic behavior and a lower SFE at 1200◦C. The FCC-structured
materials with lower SFE were reported to lead to formation of pla-
nar slip for deformation during loading [40] and have better stack
faults and deformation twinning ability [41]. Herein, lots of planer
slip, stack faults and deformation twins were formed in the FCC-MC
carbide after compression at 1200 ◦C.
When compression at 1000 ◦C, the ductility of the
Re0.5MoNbW(TaC)0.5 composite was limited. As reported before,
the brittle-ductile transition temperature of the MoNbTaW HEA
was located below 600 ◦C [9]. Re could increase the ductility
and decrease the ductile-brittle transition temperature of W and
Mo alloys [42]. Herein, the BCC HEA in the Re0.5MoNbW(TaC)0.5

4.3. Strengthening mechanism
The mechanical properties of composites depend on their inter-
nal structure. The Re0.5MoNbW(TaC)0.5 composite at 1200 ◦C had
excellent strength and ductility, which can be attributed to the
multiple plastic deformation mechanisms, such as dislocation slip,
stacking faults, and twinning activities. High density defects offer
signiﬁcant reinforcement by restricting the dislocation glide. The
defects may contribute collectively to the notable strength in
three aspects. First, the BCC HEA and FCC-MC carbide interface
is semi-coherent and the pinning effect is clearly observed in
Fig. 6. The semi-coherent phase interface favors the accumula-
tion of dislocations and the occurrence of back-stress effect. During
the compression test, the pinning effect from the phase interfaces
enhanced the capability of dislocation accumulation, improved
work hardening, and thus, contributed to the high strength of
the composite. The composite, with ﬁne eutectic microstructure,
has many phase interfaces, which increases the back-stresses and
thereby affecting the dislocation motion effectively. Second, severe
dislocation entanglement and dislocation cell structures in the
BCC HEA can be observed in Fig. 7. The interaction of cross-slip
and pinning signiﬁcantly impedes dislocation from slip. The dipo-
lar dislocation
 walls are formed, as the plastic strain increased
promoted work hardening [43,44]. Third, restricted dynamic inter-
action between coplanar dislocation, stacking faults, and twins in
FCC-MC phase is restricted during the compression test, which
leads to high density of dislocation accumulation, thereby requiring
higher stress to promote further plastic movements. As shown in
Fig. 8(e), multiple narrow twins and stacking faults accumulated at
the twin boundaries and dislocation could not move freely. There-
fore, the strength of this composite can mainly be considered as
the summation of the strength contributions from phase interface,
dislocations entanglement, and twin boundaries.
The ductility of materials depends on the dislocation multiplica-
tion capacity during deformation. The BCC HEA plastic deformation
is attributed to facilitated cross-slip along with the related promo-
tion of dislocation nucleation and propagation. The deformation
of FCC-MC carbide contributed to the dislocation slip in planar
mode, formation of stacking faults and deformation twins. The dis-
location cell structures in BCC and interaction between the three
defects in FCC-MC would indicate a certain work hardening capa-
bility, which is beneﬁcial in resisting strain localization, delaying
the occurrence of necking instability, and therefore, improving the
ductility [43,45–48].
Higher density dislocations, especially near the BCC/FCC-MC
phase interface, were seen in BCC HEA as compared to FCC-MC
carbide (Fig. 5). Therefore, the easy dislocation motion in the
relatively soft BCC HEA is responsible for the high ductility of
the Re0.5MoNbW(TaC)0.5 composite. The blockade of dislocations
at the semi-coherent interface becomes dominant in providing
high strength to the composite. As a result, the combination of
hard and soft phases with many eutectic lamellar structures of
Re0.5MoNbW(TaC)0.5 composite creates the ideal combination of
ultra-high strength and high ductility at high temperatures.
5. Conclusion
In
 this paper, the phase stability, mechanical properties and
strengthening mechanisms of dual-phase Re0.5MoNbW(TaC)0.5
refractory HEA matrix composite at high temperatures were sys-
tematically investigated.
(1)
 The as-cast composite was composed of BCC refractory HEA and
FCC-MC carbide with many ﬁne eutectic structures. The phases
and lamellar eutectic structures remained stable after anneal-
ing at 1300 ◦C for 168 h. The Re0.5MoNbW(TaC)0.5 composite
had high phase and structural stability.
(2) The composite exhibited high engineering yield strength (937
MPa at 1000 ◦C and 901 MPa at 1200 ◦C) and true ultimate com-
pressive strength (1235 MPa at 1000 ◦C and 1186 MPa at 1200
◦C), which are larger than those of other reported refractory
HEAs and composites. After compression at 1200 ◦C, disloca-
tion cell structures were formed in the BCC HEA, while coplanar
dislocation arrays, stacking faults, and deformation twins were
formed in FCC-MC carbide, resulting in work hardenability and
good ductility. Their semi-coherent interface combined well
with an orientation relationship as [111]BCC // [011]FCC-MC,
(101)BCC // (200)FCC-MC, and offered dominant strengthening.
(3) Re0.5MoNbW(TaC)0.5 composite with dual-phase structure cre-
ates the perfect balance between ultra-high strength and high
ductility at high temperatures. This ﬁnding could be an impor-
tant contribution to theoretical research and applications in the
high-temperature anti-softening ﬁeld.
