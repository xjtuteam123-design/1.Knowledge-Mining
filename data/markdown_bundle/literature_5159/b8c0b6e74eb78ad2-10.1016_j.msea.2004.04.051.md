Abstract
The deformation microstructures of a fourth-generation single-crystal (SC) Ni-base superalloy TMS-138 after low-cycle fatigue (LCF)
have been studied. For comparison, a typical third-generation (TMS-75), and a second-generation (CMSX-4) Ni-base SC superalloy were
also examined. The TMS-138 exhibited an excellent LCF behavior under a condition of R ratio of 0 at temperatures of 1073 K and 1173 K.
The addition of refractory elements resulted in a remarkable improvement of LCF properties due to the different microstructure developed
in TMS-138 as compared to the reference superalloys. The formation of a large number of stacking faults (SFs) in the matrix at 1073 K and
the more completed rafts and smaller interfacial dislocation spacing at 1373 K were observed and discussed in relation to the performance of
TMS-138 under a speciﬁc condition of R = 0. This work suggests that a fourth-generation SC superalloy TMS-138 is a promising candidate
for applications in aeroengines.

1. Introduction
The mechanical properties of Ni-base superalloys used in
gas turbine and jet engines have been extensively studied in
the past decades. TMS-75, a typical third-generation Ni-base
single-crystal (SC) superalloy with a content of 5.0 wt.%
of Re [1–3], has a remarkable improvement in terms of
creep strength over CMSX-4, which is a second-generation
Ni-base SC superalloy containing 3.0 wt.% of Re [4],H o w -

closed packed (TCP) phases with a high content of Re. More-

It is necessary to increase the lattice misﬁt between the/H9253and

SC superalloy was developed at NIMS with the addition of
Mo for increasing the lattice misﬁt and Ru for stabilizing the

4. Discussion
Although there is no report on the E
SF for the /H9253phase in
a two phase SC superalloy in the literature, one may refer to
the E
SF measured for a Ni-base alloy with a single /H9253phase
[20]. Where, the stacking fault energy is estimated by mea-
suring the splitting of dislocations formed by deformation
at temperatures up to 1323 K. For a Ru-containing alloy, the
E
SF is 28 ± 6 mJ/m2. Future research is required to obtain
clariﬁcation of the effects of the ESF of both /H9253and /H9253′phases
on the deformation of superalloys.
It has been observed that the alloying elements in Ni-base
superalloys partition preferentially into the matrix and lower
the stacking fault energy [21,22]. As shown in Fig. 9, dense
stacking faults were observed in the /H9253channels, indicating
a substantial reduction of the stacking fault energy in the
TMS-138 superalloy due to addition of Mo and Ru compared
with that of TMS-75. Like Mo and Ru, Re is a strengthen-
ing element mainly to the /H9253rather than the /H9253
′phase [1,18].
Therefore, TMS-138 is even stronger in terms of the strength
of the /H9253phase in comparison with CMSX-4. Thus, the dif-
ference in the rupture lives of the three superalloys tested
at 1073 K is understandable in part. Since, the /H9253
′ precipi-
tates in the TMS-138 was not sheared at 1073 K, the longest
rupture life of this alloy among the three investigated alloys
can be attributed to the reduced mobility of matrix dislo-
cations due to the dissociation of matrix dislocations in the
TMS-138. One may even speculate that such a dissociation
of matrix dislocation could retard crack propagation along
the horizontal /H9253channels.
The misﬁts of TMS-75 and TMS-138 were measured
to be −0.17% and −0.33% at 1373 K, respectively. It has
been reported that the high creep strength of TMS-138 at
1373 K could be attributed, on one hand, to the denser inter-
facial dislocation networks; on the other hand, the addition
of Mo and Ru has a solution-strengthening effect. Never-
theless, the misﬁt plays a more important role in forming
the denser dislocation networks and, hence, a higher creep
strength [2]. In the present case, the LCF tests under the
R ratio of 0, and, hence, the superalloy was subject to ten-
sion stress. This is quite similar to the case of creep; there-
fore, it is not surprising that directional coarsening was also
achieved. It is well known that dense interfacing disloca-
tion spacing is beneﬁcial for high-temperature creep strength
[2,6,17]. Note that the average interfacing dislocation spac-
ing for TMS-138 is smaller than that of TMS-75 in the fa-
tigued specimens, which is in coincidence with those mea-

Our previous and the current work shows that the perfor-
mance of TMS-138 in terms of creep and LCF properties
is the best among all the Ni-base superalloys studied [1–3].
It is believed that the excellent performance of TMS-138
at 1073 K is due to the reduced mobility of matrix disloca-
tions, and due to the more complete rafts and ﬁner disloca-
tion networks at 1373 K. Though the addition of refractory
elements increases the expense of producing this superal-
loy, TMS-138 is still an attractive superalloy to be applied
to aeroengines as blade and vane materials.
5. Conclusions
The microstructure of a fourth-generation Ni-base SC
superalloy TMS-138 has been studied in comparison with
those of reference superalloys after LCF. The LCF property
of a fourth-generation Ni-base SC superalloy TMS-138 un-
der a speciﬁc condition of R = 0 was associated with its
developed microstructures. At 1073 K, shearing of the /H9253

The most striking defect structure in the TMS-138 was the
stacking faults generated in the /H9253matrix. The better per-
formance of the TMS-138 than the reference superalloys at
1073 K can be attributed to reduced mobility of matrix dislo-
cations due to the addition of refractory elements decreasing
stacking fault energy. At 1373 K, the better performance of
TMS-138 was correlated with the more complete rafts and
ﬁner dislocation networks.
By comparison with the reference superalloys, this work
indicates that TMS-138 is an outstanding superalloy with
high LCF strengths both at intermediate and high tem-
peratures. This superalloy is an attractive candidate as a
high-temperature material for applications to aeroengines.
