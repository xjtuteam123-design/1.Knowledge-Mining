ABSTRACT
Nb-18Si-24Ti-2Cr-2Al-2Hf (at.%) alloy was fabricated by selective laser melting (SLM). Results showed that an
ultra-ﬁned microstructure was achieved by SLM. After oxidation at 1250 °C for 50 h, the weight gains of Nb-Si
based alloys via SLM and vacuum induction melting (VIM) were 96.48 mg/cm 2 and 198.91 mg/cm 2, respec-
tively. Compared with the VIM alloy, the SLM alloy exhibited a slower oxidation kinetic rate, featured by the
formation of a more compact oxide scale without spallation. The improvement in the oxidation resistance was
attributed to the microstructure re ﬁnement.
1. Introduction
As aerospace turbine blades materials, Nb-Si based ultrahigh tem-
perature alloys are considered as alternative materials to Ni base su-
peralloys, due to their high melting temperature, low densities and high
temperature strength [1–5]. The last thirty years have witnessed the
development of chemical composition from binary Nb-Si to Nb-Si-Ti-Cr-
Al-Hf based multicomponent system [4,6,7]. The improvements in
mechanical and environmental properties have been bene ﬁted from the
alloying element additions and the development of processing techni-
ques [3,6–9]. However, the poor oxidation resistance is still one of the
current bottlenecks that restrict the application of Nb-Si based alloys
[10–12].
Up to now, two possible approaches, i.e. elements alloying and
protective coatings, have been employed to enhance the oxidation re-
sistance of Nb-Si based alloys [3,7,13–15]. These alloying elements
include Ti, Hf, Cr, Al, Zr, Ge, B etc [3,4,7,13,16,17]. Cr addition ben-
eﬁts the formation of the Cr
2Nb Laves phases, which greatly enhanced
the oxidation resistance of Nb-Si based alloys [13,18]. Su et al. [4]
presented that the presence of alloyed B and Ge improved the oxidation
resistance by producing a continuous and dense oxide scale, which
contained the protective SiO
2 phase. Unfortunately, excessive addition
of those elements may lose the mechanical properties [3,19], and the
balance has not achieved yet by the alloying method. Meanwhile, the
oxidation resistant coatings, mainly silicide coatings, have been de-
veloped to protect Nb-Si based alloy from oxidation [11,14,20]. Though
striking progress has been achieved, the oxidation resistance of the bare
alloy still starves for further improvement in case of the spalling of
coatings.
Grain re ﬁnement has emerged as an e ﬀective method for strength-
ening metals without losing toughness, as well as the improving the
oxidation resistance [21–25]. As reviewed by Wang [24] and Mahesh
[22], nanocrystallization or microcrystallization provides two im-
portant bene ﬁcial e ﬀects on the oxidation resistance of alloys, i.e.,
promoting selective oxidation of reactive alloying elements and en-
hancing scale adhesion. Wang et al. [26] presented that laser-remelting
signiﬁcantly reduced the grain size of Mo-10Si –14 B alloy and im-
proved the oxidation at 1000 °C and 1200 °C, by accelerating the
transportation of Si and O through grain boundaries. However, the
oxidation behaviour of ﬁne-grained Nb-Si based alloy has not been
investigated in detail.
Selective laser melting (SLM) is one of the powder bed-based ad-
ditive manufacturing (AM) techniques, using laser beam scans to se-
lectively melt the power bed directly from a computer-aided design
model. Diﬀerent with the traditional processing technologies, the state-
of-the-art process of SLM has the superiority of potential cost reduction,
few steps in the production process and design-freedom [27–31]. Apart
from the processing superiorities, microstructure re ﬁnement and re-
sultant improved properties, arising from rapid solidi ﬁcation proces-
sing, make SLM su ﬃciently attractive [27]. The SLM + HT (Heat
Treated) IN 718 alloys were at least equal to those wrought IN718,
owing to grain re ﬁnement and the good metallurgical bonding with
minimal defects [28]. Thijs et al. [29] presented that AlSi10Mg parts
processed by SLM had an extremely ﬁne microstructure with sub-
micron-sized cells and hence a high hardness.

2. Materials and methods
2.1. SLM process
The nominal composition of the pre-alloyed powders for SLM was
shown in Table 1. The pre-alloyed powders were produced via the jet
milling process [32]. SLM experiments were performed using an in-
house developed SLM system, the diagram of which was displayed in
the previous work [32]. It was equipped with an IPG YLR-500 Yb:YAG
ﬁber laser, which could project a laser beam with a wave length of
1060 nm and reach a maximum power of 500 W in the continuous
mode. Titanium substrates were used for SLM experiments, as Ti has
unlimited solid solubility in Nb [3]. Prior to SLM processing, each ti-
tanium substrate was preheated by the rapid scanning of laser beam for
four times. The parameters for preheating were as follows: laser
power = 150 W, scanning speed = 1500 mm/s and hatching distance
(h) = 0.12 mm. The printing process of SLM was shown in Fig. 1(a).
Driven by the computer, the laser beam was scanned selectively across
the powder bed. After the melting of the present layer, the building
platform was lowered down and another new layer was subsequently
raked. Powder spreading and melting were repeated to prepare an
additively layered rectangular part. The manufacturing process was
protected by Ar atmosphere. The SLM parameters were as follows: laser
power = 375 W, scanning speed = 1100 mm/s, hatching distance (h)
= 0.10 mm and layer thickness = 0.05 mm. For comparison, the Nb-Si
based alloys were also prepared by VIM.
2.2. Oxidation testing
Oxidation experiments were performed in a tube furnace at 1250 °C.
The diagram of the experimental setup was displayed in Fig. 1(b). The
tube in the furnace was made of high purity alumina ﬁber. All the
specimens were gradually ground with SiC papers up to 800 grit, fol-
lowing by being cleaned in acetone. Previous to oxidation, each spe-
cimen was placed in a separate alumina crucible. All the alumina cru-
cibles had been preheated for more than 2 h at 1250 °C to remove any
moisture, until no mass gain was observed. Both ends of the tube in-
stalled in the furnace were protected by isolators in order to avoid
humidity from outside atmosphere. The specimens were directly re-
moved out from the tube furnace, cooled down to room temperature in
air and weighed together with the supporting crucible after a certain
exposure time. Then the specimens were reinserted to the tube furnace
and then the oxidising and weighing processes were repeated until the
end of the oxidation experiment. The selected exposure times were
5 min, 3 h, 7 h, 11 h, 23 h, 35 h and 50 h. The total mass gains (sample
+ spalled oxide scales) were measured using a precision analytical
balance (Model CPA225D, Germany) with an accuracy of 0.00001 g.
The weights of Nb-Si based alloys fabricated by SLM and VIM were
measured in the same way, and the spallation of oxide scales was not
particularly measured.
2.3. Analysing methods
The main phases of Nb-Si based alloys and oxidation products were
identiﬁed by X-ray di ﬀraction (XRD, D/MAX-2500), using Cu K α ra-
diation at 40 kV and 40 mA with a 2θ scanning rate of 6 °/min. Top
surface of the as-built part via SLM was observed by laser scanning
confocal microscopy (LSCM, Olympus Lext OLS4000). Surface
morphologies of the oxidised specimens were analysed using a ﬁeld-
emission scanning electron microscopy (SEM, JSM7500).
Microstructural analysis was conducted on polished specimens, using a
SEM and an electron probe micro-analyzer (EPMA, JXA-8230)
equipped with a wave-dispersive spectroscopy (WDS). For cross-sec-
tional investigation, samples were mounted by nonconductive epoxy
resin, ground gradually using silicon carbide papers, mechanically po-
lished on Fe
2O3/CrO3-impregnated cloths and deposited by Au layer
eventually. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM, JEM-2100) ob-
servations were conducted, using thin foils prepared by a conventional
ion-milling method. Copper rings were used to support thin sample
foils. The compositions of main oxides were obtained by energy-dis-
persive spectroscopy (EDS) with the exclusion of copper.

4. Discussion
The enhancement of the oxidation resistance of Nb-Si based alloy
via SLM is supposed to be due to the ultra-re ﬁned microstructure.
Mainly three bene ﬁcial e ﬀects are to be discussed in the present work:
(1) lowering the oxygen partial pressure in the alloy; (2) improving
scale adhesion with lower stress level; (3) facilitating the formation of
the more continuous outer (Ti, Cr)-enriched oxide layer and inner SiO
2
layer, as well as larger volume of glassy SiO 2 phase.
During oxidation at 1250 °C, the inward di ﬀusion of oxygen is ty-
pically through Nbss phases, due to its high oxygen solubility [34]. The
oxidation of Nbss takes place, while silicides tend to maintain a low
oxygen partial pressure at their edges, against the movement of the
oxidation front [4,18]. It is known that a substantial reduction of the
size of silicides leads to a signi ﬁcant increase of their speci ﬁc surface
area. Resultantly, the oxygen partial pressure is lowered at metal-oxide
interfaces, owing to the dispersedly distributed silicides. The oxidation
rate of Nb-Si based alloy is thus reduced. Apart from silicides, the grain
reﬁnement of Nb matrix also leads to an improvement in oxidation
resistance [17]. As testi ﬁed in Fig. 10 (c), the Nbss phases are oxidised
more slightly.
For Nb-Si based alloys, scale spallation as well as cracking occurs
frequently, resulting from the thermal and growth stress generated
during oxidation [4,10,35,36]. During the oxidation experiments, dis-
continuous exposures combine the e ﬀects of accumulated stress,
leading to the cracking of the oxidation scales on both VIM and SLM
alloy ( Figs. 6 and 7). Meanwhile, severe scale spallation is observed on
the VIM alloy during oxidation, but not displayed on the SLM alloy,
indicating the oxide scale on VIM alloy is less compact and resultantly
less protective. Accordingly, it can be inferred that lower stress is
generated in the oxide scale on SLM alloy during oxidation. Micro-
structure re ﬁnement is supposed to be the main reason. At the initial
oxidation stage, the re ﬁned Nbss and silicides lead to the formation of
the microcrystallized outmost oxides ( Fig. 7(b)). It is more favourable
to the reduction of volume expansion and the relieving of growth stress
via plastic deformation [25,37]. Scale adhesion is improved resultantly.
As shown in Figs. 11 and 12, two continuous oxide layers are ob-
served on the SLM alloy, but not on the VIM alloy. The outer one is
enriched in Ti and Cr, primarily a mixture of TiO
2 and CrNbO 4 oxides.
The inner one is enriched in Si, mainly SiO 2 phases. The formation of an
external continuous SiO 2, TiO 2 and CrNbO 4 oxide layer typically re-
quires a critical volume fraction of internal precipitates. Based on the
classical Wagner theory, the minimum Si, Ti and Cr concentrations for
the formation of an external SiO

Vm is the molar volume of alloy, DSi/Ti/Cr is the di ﬀusion coe ﬃcient of
Si/Ti/Cr, and VOX is the molar volume of the oxide. Accordingly, the
critical Si/Ti/Cr concentration required for the formation of external
oxide layer decreases with the increase of DSi/Ti/Cr. Owing to the ultra-
reﬁned microstructure of Nb-Si based alloy via SLM, more short-circuit
diﬀusion channels are generated, including phase interfaces and grain
boundaries [38]. Thus DSi/Ti/Cr is drastically increased. At the initial
stage, the di ﬀusion of Si, Ti and Cr is accelerated, facilitating the se-
lective formation of SiO 2, TiO 2 and Cr 2O3. Besides, Wang et al. [39]
argued that for two-phase alloys, the homogeneously distributed ﬁne
particles were more bene ﬁcial to the exclusive formation of protective
oxides, concluded form a nonsteady-state di ﬀusional analysis.
On one side, the formation of a successive SiO 2 layer creates a
concentration gradient at the interface of the outer oxide layer and
transitional oxide layer. The SiO 2 phase is determined to be amorphous
(Fig. 8 (g) and (h)), which could e ﬀectively decrease the oxygen di ﬀu-
sion rate through oxide scale and reduce the scale growth rate [4,11].
Noteworthily, in comparison with the VIM alloy, more dispersive glassy
SiO2 phases are observed in the outer oxide layer on the SLM alloy
(Fig. 11), which tend to connect other oxides. The protective SiO 2
phases generate a more ﬂuid glass, being able to heal cracks and holes
during oxidation [11,20]. Resultantly, the di ﬀusion of oxygen into
substrate alloy is decelerated and the scale adhesion is improved. On
the other hand, Cr 2O3 oxides are formed on the outmost surface, since
the chromium ion di ﬀuses outward faster than niobium ions [40]. Then
Cr2O3 typically reacts with Nb 2O5, resulting in the formation of external
CrNbO4 oxide layer [41]. The continuous CrNbO 4 oxide layer has been
reported to be bene ﬁcial to the oxidation resistance of Nb-Si based alloy
[4,12,16]. Furthermore, for Nb-Si-Cr based alloys, Yao et al. [16] re-
ported that the decrease of particle size and the increase of volume
fraction of NbCr 2 facilitated the exclusive formation of the protective
CrNbO4 oxides. Besides, the continuous TiO 2 oxide layer tends to be
more compact with lower stress, in comparison with Nb 2O5. The reason
is that the Pilling-Bedworth ratio (PBR) of TiO 2 is 1.70 –1.78, much
smaller than that of Nb 2O5, which is 2.68 [42]. A more compact TiO 2
oxide layer would restrain the inward di ﬀusion of oxygen and lower the
oxidation rate of Nb-Si based alloy resultantly.

5. Conclusions
The main ﬁndings of this work are concluded in the following
points:
(1) Nbss, Nb 5Si3 and Nb3Si were the main phases of Nb-Si based alloys
fabricated by either SLM or VIM. Compared with the VIM alloy, the
SLM alloy was distinguished by an ultra-re ﬁned microstructure.
(2) Compared with the VIM alloy, a more compact oxide scale without
spallation were found on the SLM alloy. Besides, an outer con-
tinuous Ti- and Cr-enriched oxide layer was found on the SLM alloy,
together with an inner successive SiO 2 layer between the outer
oxide layer and transitional oxide layer. The two continuous layers
beneﬁtted the inward di ﬀusion of oxygen and improved the scale
adhesion.
(3) The Nb-Si based alloy via SLM exhibited a much slower parabolic
oxidation kinetic rate. After oxidation at 1250 °C for 50 h, the
weight gains of Nb-Si based alloys via SLM and VIM were
96.48 mg/cm
2 and 198.91 mg/cm 2, respectively The improvement
of oxidation resistance was attributed to the microstructure re-
ﬁnement.
