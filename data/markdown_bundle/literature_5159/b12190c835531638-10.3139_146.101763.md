Microstructure and room temperature
mechanical properties
of Hf and Sn-doped Nb-20Ti-5Cr-3Al-18Si alloy
In this paper, the effects of Sn and Hf additions on micro-
structures and room temperature mechanical properties of
Nb-20Ti-5Cr-3Al-18Si alloy were investigated. The Sn
addition has no significant effect on the microstructure
of the alloy. Sn is preferentially partitioned in (Nb, Ti)ss
rather than (Nb, Ti) 5Si3. The addition of Sn decreases
the solubility of Cr in (Nb, Ti)ss. The addition of Hf
causes coarsening of the eutectic structure of (Nb, Ti)ss
and (Nb, Ti) 5Si3 and the formation of the Hf-rich
c(Nb, Ti)5Si3 along phase boundaries. With the addition
of Sn the strength of the alloy decreases, while its duc-
tility increases slightly. The Hf addition simultaneously
improves strength and ductility of the alloy. The relation-
ships between composition, microstructure and properties
are discussed.
Keywords: Intermetallics; Microstructure; Mechanical
properties
1. Introduction
As a promising potential structural material for high-tem-
perature applications, the intermetallic compound Nb 5Si3
has the characteristics of high melting point, low density,
excellent strength and creep resistance at elevated tem-
peratures [1 – 4]. However, its shortcomings such as low
ductility and toughness at room temperature, have hin-
dered its industrial application [3, 5 – 7]. In order to solve
the problem, in-situ composites consisting of a ductile
Nb-based solid solution phase (Nbss) and a silicide phase
have been developed [8 – 15]. However, in general, the
fracture toughness and oxidation resistance of these bin-
ary alloys are inadequate for high-temperature structural
applications. According to the report of Bewlay et al.
[16], the Hf addition may reduce the pesting susceptibil-
ity of Nb-silicide based in-situ composites, while the Sn
addition ( *1.5 at.%) can eliminate this problem in the
temperature range from 750 8C to 950 8C. However, very
little work has been done on the effects of the Hf and
Sn additions on the mechanical properties of Nb silicide
based in-situ composites. Therefore, the purpose of this
paper is to investigate the effects of Hf and Sn additions
on the microstructure and room temperature mechanical
properties of in-situ composites based on the Nb – Ti –
Si – Cr – Al system.
2. Experimental procedures
The purities of starting raw materials were 99.9 at.% Nb,
99.99 at.% Si, 99.9 at.% Ti, 99.9 at.% Al, 99.99 at.% Cr,
99.9 at.% Hf and 99.9 at.% Sn. Small buttons of 70 g of
the alloy were prepared by arc melting on a water-cooled
copper hearth under an argon gas protective atmosphere
using a non-consumable tungsten electrode. The button in-
gots were re-melted five times in order to achieve chemical
homogeneity. Since weight losses were generally less than
0.5 mass.%, the compositions of the alloys were considered
to be equal to their nominal compositions. The nominal
compositions of the investigated alloys are listed in Table 1.
X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis was conducted to monitor
the structure of the phases using a Rigaku D/Max-r A
X-ray diffractometer through Cu-K
a radiation. The samples
for scanning electron microscopy (SEM) observation were
prepared by conventional metallographic techniques. The
microstructures were observed under SEM and the chemi-
cal compositions of the constituent phases were analyzed
by electron probe microanalysis (EPMA). Transmission
electron microscopy (TEM) was used to characterize the
microstructure and determine the coexisting phases in
greater detail. Slices of about 0.5 mm in thickness were cut
from the alloy buttons by electro-discharge machining
(EDM). Thinning was performed by mechanical polishing
to 50 lm in thickness, then shaped into 3 mm in diameter
followed by ion milling to the final thickness. Vickers hard-
ness tests for different phases in the alloys were carried out
using an LM247AT micro-hardness tester under an applied
hold of 10 g. The hardness values were the average of at
least 5 indentations for each load. For the purpose of better
observing indentation features, a hold of 50 g was applied.
Rectangular compression specimens with the dimensions
of 4 · 4 · 6m m
3 were cut from the buttons by EDM and
the major surfaces were mechanically ground with 600-grit
SiC abrasive prior to compression testing. The compression
tests were conducted in air using a Gleeble 1500 testing ma-
chine with an initial strain rate of 2 · 10
–3 s –1 .

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructure
The three alloy buttons were re-melted alternately five
times on a water-cooled copper hearth with three crucibles
under an argon gas atmosphere using a non-consumable
tungsten electrode. Therefore, the cooling conditions for
the three alloy buttons are considered to be similar, i. e.,
the microstructures of the three alloys are obtained under
the same cooling conditions.
The XRD patterns of the studied alloys are presented in
Fig. 1. The XRD results indicate that the microstructures
of the alloys consist of Nbss and Nb
5Si3. In alloys A and B
Nb5Si3 is found to be tetragonal bNb5Si3, while in alloy C
it is found to be either tetragonal bNb5Si3 or hexagonal
cNb5Si3. Typical SEM backscattered electron images of
the three alloys are shown in Fig. 2. With consideration of
the XRD results, the EPMA analysis (see Table 2) reveals
that the gray-white, gray, and black contrasted regions in
the figures can be identified as (Nb, Ti)ss, (Nb, Ti) 5Si3,
and Ti-rich (Nb, Ti) 5Si3, respectively, as indicated by the
arrows in Fig. 2. The typical microstructures in the alloys
consist of primary (Nb, Ti)
5Si3 and eutectic of (Nb, Ti)ss
and (Nb, Ti) 5Si3. It can be observed from Fig. 2 that the Sn
addition causes no obvious changes in the microstructures.

The EPMA analysis results reveal that Sn in alloys A and
B is preferentially solved in (Nb, Ti)ss rather than in
(Nb, Ti)
5Si3. At the same time the content of Cr in
(Nb, Ti)ss decreases with the addition of Sn, which means
that the addition of Sn decreases the solubility limit of Cr
in (Nb, Ti)ss. However, the Ti, Al and Si contents in
(Nb, Ti)ss are not affected by the presence of Sn in the al-
loys. In alloy C the Hf content in the Hf-rich c(Nb, Ti)
5Si3
phase is about three times as high as that in the
b(Nb, Ti)5Si3 phase. The Ti content in the Hf-rich
c(Nb, Ti)5Si3 phase is also higher than that in the
b(Nb, Ti) 5Si3 phase, suggesting that the Hf addition pro-
motes the segregation in Ti into the Hf-rich c(Nb, Ti)5Si3
phase. As a result, this leads to a decrease in Ti content in
b(Nb, Ti)5Si3. However, the contents of other elements in
(Nb, Ti)ss and b(Nb, Ti)5Si3 do not change significantly
after the Hf addition.
3.2. Room temperature mechanical properties
Table 3 shows the mechanical properties of the studied al-
loys at room temperature. Vickers hardness measurements
were conducted on the constituent phases of the alloys.
However, the microhardness of the c(Nb, Ti)5Si3 phase
was not tested because it was too small. At least 5 points
were measured for each case and the average results are
summarized in Table 3. It is evident from Table 3 that the
Vickers hardness of (Nb, Ti)ss decreases with the addition
of Sn, then recovers and is slightly beyond that in alloy A
with the addition of Hf. The Vickers hardness of
b(Nb, Ti)
5Si3 is not so sensitive to the Sn addition, while it
is significantly increased by the Hf addition. It could be
concluded that the Sn addition weakens the (Nb, Ti)ss,
while the Hf addition strengthens the (Nb, Ti)ss and
b(Nb, Ti)5Si3 at room temperature.
Compression tests were carried out to examine the Sn
and Hf effect on the compressive properties using various
samples at room temperature with an initial strain rate of
2 · 10 –3 s –1 . The 0.2 % yield strength, fracture strength
and compressive ductility are also summarized in Table 3.
It is noted that alloy A fails before yielding, i. e., it has zero
compressive ductility. Alloy B doped with 1.5 at.% Sn ex-
hibits about 2.1 % compressive ductility, while its fracture
strength decreases by 126 MPa, compared to that of alloy
A. With Hf addition, the yield strength and fracture strength
of alloy C increase respectively by 73 MPa and 166 MPa,
and an increase of 4.2 % in ductility is observed (see Ta-
ble 3), compared with those of alloy B. The decrease in
fracture strength for alloy B at room temperature can be
mainly attributed to the decrease of Cr content in
(Nb, Ti)ss, as shown in Table 2, with almost no apparent
change in microstructure between alloy B and alloy A, as
seen in Fig. 2. The decrease of Cr content in (Nb, Ti)ss af-
fects adversely the solid solution strengthening and in-

propagation can be effectively retarded by thick (Nb, Ti)ss
[19, 20], as shown in Fig. 4 [20], the increase in thickness
of (Nb, Ti)ss is favorable to the enhancement in ductility
of alloy C. Secondly, the addition of Hf can improve the
ductility of Nbss. Zheng’s investigation showed that Hf is
beneficial to the ductility of Nbss [21]. Chan pointed out
that Hf and Ti are the only ductilizing/toughening ele-
ments for Nbss [22]. They claimed that Hf, like Ti, lowers
the Peierls – Nabarro barrier energy, which increases the
fracture toughness and ductility of Nbss. Finally, the
formation of Hf-rich c(Nb, Ti)
5Si3 phase instead of
b(Nb, Ti)5Si3 is also found favorable to plastic deforma-
tion because only a small amount of cracks are observed
in the Hf-rich c(Nb, Ti)5Si3 phase, and some cracks
emitted from fractured b(Nb, Ti)5Si3 are even arrested in
the Hf-rich c(Nb, Ti)5Si3 phase, as clearly seen in Fig. 5.
This is consistent with Chan’s study [23] which has
showed that Ti and Hf additions can enhance the fracture
toughness of Nb 5Si3 from 2 *3t o8 *10 MPa · m 1/2. The
toughness enhancement may be attributed to the slip of
[001] partial dislocations, which results in an increased
fracture toughness with decreasing bond order by increas-
ing Ti and Hf content.
For the purpose of analyzing the compressive damage
mechanism, SEM fractographic observations on the surface
of the tested samples were carried out. SEM images taken
from surfaces of the as-cast samples compressed at room
temperature are presented in Fig. 5. A lot of microcracks in-
itiate at independent polygonal b(Nb, Ti)5Si3. Few micro-
cracks are found at the (Nb, Ti)ss/(Nb, Ti) 5Si3 interface
and no obvious plastic deformation appears within the duc-
tile (Nb, Ti)ss. The microcracks are broadened and pene-
trate through whole width of polygonal b(Nb, Ti)5Si3.
Some cracks are arrested when encountering (Nb, Ti)ss or
c(Nb, Ti)5Si3. Others propagate from a b(Nb, Ti)5Si3 parti-
cle to another one beyond (Nb, Ti)ss. It is suggested that
the compressive damage process mainly obeys crack arrest-
ing and bridging mechanisms.

hold of 50 g in alloy C. The (Nb, Ti)ss and (Nb, Ti)
5Si3
show different behaviors during penetration of the hardness
indenter. When the indentation tip locates on the
b(Nb, Ti)
5Si3 phase, as shown in Fig. 6a, cracks initiate
and propagate within the b(Nb, Ti)5Si3 phase (arrows A
in Fig. 6a). However, the cracks are arrested in the
c(Nb, Ti)5Si3 or (Nb, Ti)ss phases (arrows B or C in
Fig. 6a) during propagation. When the indentation wholly
locates within the (Nb, Ti)ss, as seen in Fig. 6b, no crack
appears within the (Nb, Ti)ss phase area. Some evident de-
formation traces such as slip lines (arrows D in Fig. 6b)
are found around the indentation. This indicates that the
b(Nb, Ti)
5Si3 phase is brittle, while the (Nb, Ti)ss deforms
plastically and the c(Nb, Ti)5Si3 also shows a few plastic
features at room temperature, which is in agreement with
the results of fractographic observations on the surface of
the tested samples.

4. Summary and conclusions
1. The addition of Sn causes no obvious change in micro-
structure. Sn is preferentially partitioned in (Nb, Ti)ss
rather than (Nb, Ti) 5Si3. The addition of Sn decreases
the solubility of Cr in (Nb, Ti)ss. The addition of Hf
causes coarsening of the eutectic structure of Nbss and
Nb5Si3 and the formation of an Hf-rich c(Nb, Ti)5Si3
thin layer phase along phase boundaries.
2. With the addition of Sn the strength of the alloy de-
creases, whereas the ductility increases slightly. This is
attributed to the decrease in Cr content of (Nb, Ti)ss.
With the addition of Hf the strength and ductility of the
alloy increases at the same time. The increase in the
strength is attributed to solid solution strengthening of
(Nb, Ti)ss by Hf addition. The coarsening of the eutectic
structure and the formation of the Hf-rich c(Nb, Ti)5Si3
phase may be responsible for the increased ductility.
Therefore, Hf addition is an effective way of improving
the strength and ductility of Nb-silicide based alloys.
