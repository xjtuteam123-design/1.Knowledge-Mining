Abstract--High temperature (823-923 K) brittle intergranular cracking behavior has been studied in ~' 
strengthening nickel alloys doped with and without S, B and/or Zr. The growth rate of brittle intergranular 
cracks was found to be described by da/dt = ao(K/Ko) n where K 0 and 40 represent a transition from the 
crack nucleation to growth controlling process. The alloy composition and temperature produced quite 
different effects on K0 and n. In undoped and S-doped alloys, high temperature cracking occurred at the 
lowest K0 which strongly depends on the temperature. However, the undoped and S-doped alloys exhibited 
the lowest and highest values of n, respectively, almost independent of the temperature. The addition of 
B increased K 0 and decreased n more greatly than that of Zr in alloys with high S. The alloy with high 
S and Zr showed the strongest temperature dependence of n in all the alloys. It has been shown by 
estimating the activation energy from the Arrhenius plot of 40 that the brittle intergranular cracking 
behavior is controlled by S diffusion at grain boundaries and on crack surfaces. 
1. INTRODUCTION 
It is well recognized that high temperature (HT) 
fracture predominantly occurs along grain bound- 
aries. There exist two types of HT intergranular 
cracking. Materials with low to medium strength 
generally exhibit cavitated intergranular fracture sur- 
faces. Many research groups [1, 2] have extensively 
studied the mechanisms of grain boundary cavitation. 
The nucleation of cavities is induced by the conden- 
sation of vacancies at particle/grain boundary inter- 
faces and grain boundary sliding near the triple 
junction. The cavity growth process is controlled by 
self diffusion at grain boundaries, on cavity surfaces 
and/or power law creep deformation. 
In high strength ferritic steels and nickel alloys, on 
the other hand, HT intergranular fracture surfaces 
often show a brittle feature without extensive cavita- 
tion [3-6]. It has been demonstrated in ferritic alloy 
steels [7-10] that HT brittle intergranular cracking is 
attributed to the dynamic segregation of embrittling 
solute such as S near the crack tip. Several mechan- 
isms for S enrichment near the HT crack tip and the 
growth of brittle intergranular cracks have been 
proposed. Hippsley et al. [7, 8, 11] have presented 
that S enrichment on crack surfaces and at grain 
boundaries is achieved via lattice S fluxes driven by 
the stress gradient. Shin and McMahon [9] have 
proposed that S is dissolved from sulfides on HT 
crack surfaces and is accumulated near the crack tip 
by surface diffusion. It is suggested that segregated S 
near the HT crack tip induces brittle cracking by 
facilitating intergranular decohesion [7-9,12,13] 
and surface self diffusion/plasticity-controlled crack 
growth [9, 14, 15]. 
It is also known in nickel base alloys [16] that the 
HT fracture properties are degraded by the presence 
of residual impurities like S. The effect of segregated 
S and/or precipitated sulfides on grain boundary 
cavitation has been clearly demonstrated [16, 17]. 
Moreover, the addition of Zr and/or B produced a 
beneficial effect on the creep ductility in nickel base 
superalloys [6, 18]. Since the nickel base superalloys 
show mixed modes of intergranular cavitation and 
brittle cracking, however, the effect of alloying and 
impurity elements on the HT brittle intergranular 
cracking behavior has not been fully understood. 
Therefore, it is necessary to investigate systematically 
the role of S, Zr and/or B in inducing HT brittle 
intergranular cracking using model nickel base alloys 
with high strength. 
The present paper is the first part of a study on the 
mechanism of HT (823-923 K) brittle intergranular 
cracking in 2,' strengthening nickel alloys undoped 
and doped with S, Zr and/or B. The characteristics 
of HT brittle intergranular cracking in the various 
nickel alloys were examined under vacuum by 
measuring the crack growth rate, da/dt, as a function 
of the applied stress intensity, K. The composition 
dependence of the stress relaxation behavior due to 
the transient creep deformation was investigated in 
order to clarify mechanical factors controlling HT 
brittle cracking. The rate process controlling HT 
brittle intergranular cracking is discussed. In part II 
[19], solute segregation at grain boundaries and on 
HT fracture surfaces is examined using scanning

2. EXPERIMENTAL 
The materials used in this study were five nickel 
base alloys containing Ti, A1, S, Zr and/or B. The 
chemical composition of the alloys is shown in 
Table 1. The alloys with contents of low S and A1, low 
S, high S, high S and Zr, and high S and B were 
designated as LSA, LS, HS, HSZ and HSB alloys, 
respectively. Note that the LSA and LS alloys had 
high purity, and the HS, HSZ and HSB alloys 
were doped with S. All the alloys but the LSA alloy 
contained similar contents of AI and Ti. Rod shaped 
ingots of the alloys were prepared using a vacuum 
drop casting method. Disc shaped alloy samples cut 
from the ingot were initially cold pressed to 10% and 
then recrystallized at various temperatures ranging 
from 1390 to 1530 K for 2 h. The grain size of the 
alloys varied from 300 to 400/~m. Then all the alloy 
specimens were aged at 973 K for 10 h to produce the 
same hardness of HRC 38 (see the age hardening 
behavior in Fig. 1). Disc shaped compact tension 
(CT) specimens with a width of 20.3 mm, a thickness 
of 8 mm and 1 mm deep side grooves and compres- 
sive specimens with the dimension of 4.5 x 4.5 x 
9.8 mm used for stress relaxation tests were machined 
from the heat treated alloys. The CT specimens were 
precracked in the range of AK from 10 to 15 MPax/m 
in 300 K air. 
Crack growth and stress relaxation experiments 
were performed under vacuum of 1.4 x 10 -5 Pa using 
a servo-hydraulic testing machine in the temperature 
range from 823 to 923 K. Under the present vacuum 
condition, it was found that residual gaseous el- 
ements are mostly water vapor and the oxygen partial 
pressure is less than 6 x 10 -7 Pa. The CT and com- 
pressive specimens were heated by an induction coil 
in a stainless vacuum chamber mounted to the testing 
machine. The test temperature was monitored and 
controlled by a thermocouple attached to the CT 
specimen or a compressive loading jig and a digital 
optical pyrometer. After the test temperature had 
been maintained for 1 h, a constant load was applied 
to the CT specimen and da/dt was measured using a 
potential method. A constant direct current (10 A) 
was applied to the top and bottom sides of the CT 
specimens. In order to eliminate the effect of tempera- 
ture fluctuation on potential changes, the current 
polarization was instantaneously changed using a 
computer controlled switch and the average potential 
across the crack mouth was recorded via a multimeter 
by a computer. Using a calibration relation deter- 
mined by fatigue cracking experiments, the crack 
length was estimated from the recorded potential 
change. By measuring directly the crack length in 
load-interrupted specimens, it was confirmed that the 
potential method provides a reasonable estimate of 
the average crack length through the specimen thick- 
ness. The relationship between the da/dt and applied 
K was determined from the variation of crack length 
with the loading time. The applied load was occasion- 
ally increased during the crack growth experiment. 
The da/dt was found to increase without an appreci- 
able retardation as the load was slightly increased. 
The stress relaxation behavior was examined in com- 
pression under a constant displacement condition 
to minimize the effect of microcrack formation. HT 
fracture surfaces and crack profiles of the various 
alloy specimens were examined by scanning electron 
microscopy. 
3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION 
As shown in Fig. 1, the age hardening behavior 
widely varied depending on the alloy composition. 
Under the unaged condition, the HSZ and HSB 
alloys exhibited the highest hardness, and the 
HS alloy did the lowest one. The hardness in the 
LSA and HS alloys more rapidly increased with 
increasing aging time than in the HSZ and HSB 
alloys. Then all the alloy specimens reached the 
same value of HRC 38 after 10 h aging. Softening 
due to overaging occurred most readily in the HS 
alloy. In the LSA alloy, double peaks of the hardness 
were observed during the aging. Figure 2 shows 
the morphologies of ~,' precipitates and grain bound- 
aries in the various nickel alloys. All the alloys 
but the LSA alloy exhibited wavy or zigzag grain 
boundaries because of the formation of coarse 7' 
precipitates. 
It has been shown [6, 20-22] that HT cracking in 
high strength alloys can be characterized in terms of 
the applied K rather than the nominal applied stress 
or the path independent integral because the stress 
relaxation behavior resulting from the creep defor- 
mation is greatly limited. The logarithmic relation- 
ships between the da/dt and applied K for the various 
alloys tested at several temperatures are shown in 
Fig. 3. The HT cracking behavior strongly depends 
on the alloy composition and temperature. However, 
the content of A1 did not affect the HT cracking 
behavior in undoped alloys.

The occurrence of HT brittle intergranular fracture 
is significantly influenced by solute segregation and 
precipitation. As shown in the part II [19], the present 
nickel alloys exhibited S segregation at grain bound- 
aries, near stressed grain boundary sulfides and on 
HT crack surfaces. In addition, the grain boundaries 
did not contain carbide precipitates which become 
nuclei of cavities and C segregation giving rise to 
toughening effects. Because of the presence of segre- 
gated S and sulfides, and the absence of segregated C, 
the HT brittle intergranular cracking mode domi- 
nates in the high strength nickel alloys. On the 
contrary, nickel base superalloys with contents of 
low S, and high C tend to fracture in mixed modes 
of intergranular cavitation and brittle cracking at 
HT [6, 181. 
HT brittle intergranular cracking is most likely to 
be controlled by stress while intergranular cavitation 
is driven by strain. From a mechanical point of view, 
alloys with high creep deformation resistance are 
prone to fracture in a brittle manner because the local 
stress relaxation ahead of the HT crack tip is limited. 
Therefore, the effects of alloy composition and tem- 
perature on the stress relaxation behavior which 
might affect HT brittle intergranular cracking were 
investigated. Figure 10 shows the variation of com- 
pressive stress normalized by initial applied stress, a 0, 
(950-1000 MPa) with the relaxation time at 873 and 
923 K in the various alloys. The stress relaxation 
behavior occurred more readily as follows: the LSA 
alloy> the LS alloy > the HS alloy> the HSB 
alloy > HSZ alloy. The composition dependence of 
stress relaxation became stronger with increasing test 
temperature. The addition of Zr or B in S-doped 
alloys mitigated the stress relaxation behavior. This 
result is in agreement with in the nickel base super- 
alloys [6, 18]. Increasing the bulk S content also 
increased the stress relaxation resistance despite par- 
tial occurrence of intergranular cracking, which 
might promote stress relaxation, in the HS alloy. It 
is apparent from Figs 5 and 10 that the LSA or LS 
and HS alloys with low stress relaxation resistance 
show high susceptibility to HT brittle intergranular 
cracking. Thus the composition dependence of HT 
brittle intergranular cracking controlled by stress can 
not be explained by that of stress relaxation. This 
finding might be attributed to the creep deformation

The characteristics of HT (823-923 K) brittle inter- 
granular cracking in high strength nickel alloys 
undoped and doped with S, Zr and/or B have been 
investigated in terms of the da/dt vs applied K 
relationship and the stress relaxation behavior. The 
results obtained in this study are summarized as 
follows: 
1. The HT crack growth rate (stage II) was found 
to be characterized by da/dt = do(K/Ko)" where K0 
and ~i 0 represent a transition from the crack nucle- 
ation to growth controlling process, and n indicates 
the stress dependence of da/dt. 
2. In undoped and S-doped alloys, HT cracking 
occurred at the lowest K 0 which strongly depends on 
the temperature. However, the undoped and S-doped 
alloys showed the lowest and highest values of n, 
respectively. 
3. The addition of B increased K0 and decreased n 
more greatly than that of Zr in S-doped alloys. The 
alloy with high S and Zr exhibited the strongest 
temperature dependence of n. 
4. HT fracture surfaces of all the alloys predomi- 
nantly showed a brittle intergranular mode although 
intergranular cavitation was only partly observed in 
the undoped alloy. 
5. The stress relaxation rate decreased in the fol- 
lowing order: the alloys with low S and AI, low S, 
high S, high S and B, and high S and Zr. The 
composition dependence of brittle intergranular 
cracking controlled by stress is not highly correlated 
with that of stress relaxation. 
6. The activation energies estimated from the 
Arrhenius plot of do in the various alloys are closely 
correlated with those for S diffusion at grain bound- 
aries and on crack surfaces.
