ABSTRACT
In the present study, Mg-6Zn-1Zr (ZK60) alloy with a homogeneous ﬁne-grained structure (~5 µm) was
produced by hot extrusion. The in ﬂuence of temperature on tensile properties anisotropy and the associated
change in deformation mechanisms in the range of 25 –275 °C of the ﬁne-grained ZK60 alloy were investigated.
Under room temperature, the ﬁne-grained ZK60 alloy exhibited a high tensile strength of ~269 –327 MPa and
high ductility of ~15 –39% when tested at three di ﬀerent directions, indicating a high anisotropy. With
increasing testing temperature to 150 °C and further to 275 °C, the alloy showed a gradual decrease in
anisotropy. Especially, the ﬁne-grained ZK60 alloy exhibited superplasticity of ~376 –434% when tested at
275 °C, which should be due to the increasing role of grain boundary sliding (GBS), as the strong basal texture
associated with room temperature deformation became almost random at elevated temperatures. Moreover, the
abrupt decrease in tensile anisotropy and associated change in texture with increasing temperature correlates
closely to the transformation of deformation mode from a dislocation-dominated behavior to a GBS-mediated
one.
1. Introduction
As the lightest materials among construction alloys, magnesium
(Mg) alloys are expected to be widely-used in transportation and
aerospace industries [1]. For high-performance structural applications
it is desirable to develop wrought magnesium products, such as
extruded pro ﬁles, rolled sheets and forgings [2]. However, Mg has a
hexagonal closed-packed (HCP) crystal structure with limited number
of available slip systems, which results in the formation of a strong
crystallographic texture upon thermo-mechanical processes [3,4].
Wrought Mg ZK60 alloy usually has wide spread applications owing
to its excellent ductility [4]. Still, the existing commercial ZK60 alloys
generally have a strong deformation texture, exhibiting poor form-
ability and strong anisotropy in mechanical behavior at temperatures
below 200 °C, which limits their wider application [3].
Exploring and hence minimizing or eliminating the anisotropy eﬀect is
an important research topic in recent decades. Signi ﬁcant improvements
in tensile strength and ductility were reported for Mg alloys after cyclic
extrusion [5] and equal channel angular extrusion (ECAE) [4]. However,
most of the strength tests carried out of wrought Mg alloys were focused
on samples with tensile direction paralleling to the extrusion direction
(ED) [4,5].Y ie ta l . [6] tested Mg –2.8Al–0.8Zn–0.2Mn (AZ31) and
Mg–1.3Zn–0.2Ce–0.1La (ZE10) alloy sheets in three selected loading
directions (RD (rolling direction), 45D (45° to RD) and TD (transverse
direction)), where both alloys exhibited in-plane anisotropy in the yield
strength, while the ultimate tensile strength (UTS) was not signi ﬁcantly
aﬀected by the sample orientation. Also, Zhang et al. [7] reported that
there was signi ﬁcant anisotropy in mechanical properties of an ECAP-
processed Mg–9Al–1Zn (AZ91) Mg alloy, where the strength along TD was
the largest while that along ND was the smallest, which is attributed to the
basal texture in the deformed AZ91 alloy.
The deformation characteristic along certain direction is strongly
dependent on the type of active deformation modes and their Schmid
factors [3]. At ambient temperature, plastic deformation of magnesium
is limited mainly to basal {0001} < 11
20 > slip and {10 12} < 1011>
mechanical twinning [2], while non-basal slip mechanisms are respon-
sible for improved formability of Mg at elevated temperatures
(> 180 °C) [4]. Moreover, an enhanced formability at warm/high
temperatures is often exhibited in ﬁne-grained Mg alloys due to
superplastic behavior arising from grain boundary sliding (GBS) [8].
It has been proposed [9,10] that grain boundary (GB) mediated
plasticity (e.g. GBS and/or grain rotation) could substitute for conven-

However, either an enhanced activity of pyramidal < c + a > slip [16]

moderate to high temperatures still remains a controversial issue.
The objective of the present work is to investigate the in ﬂuence of
temperature on tensile properties anisotropy and the associated change
in deformation mechanisms in a ﬁne-grained extruded ZK60 alloy. We
found that the deformation texture was weakened greatly and hence the
anisotropy in mechanical properties decreased signi ﬁcantly once the
deformation mechanism changed from dislocation-dominated to GBS-
mediated.
2. Experimental procedure
The material used in this study was as-extruded ZK60
(Mg–5.50Zn–0.55Zr, in wt%) Mg alloy sheet with 40 mm width and
5 mm thickness (extrusion ratio R=26) followed by homogenization at
320 °C for 3 h. Tensile samples with a gauge dimension of
30 mm×10 mm×1.5 mm were cut from the sheet, with tensile direction
parallel to the extrusion direction (ED), transverse direction (TD) and 45°
along the extrusion direction (45ED), respectively. For convenience,
samples with tension directions along the extrusion direction, transverse
direction and 45° rotated from ED of the as-extruded ZK60 alloy were
labeled as ED-, TD- and 45D sample, respectively. Tensile tests for ED, TD
and 45D samples were performed on INSTRON 5869 testing (INSTRON,
Boston, USA) machine at temperatures of 25 °C, 150 °C and 275 °C by
using a strain rate of 1.0×10
−3 s−1. Specimen temperature was measured
in the three-zone furnace after a 10 min soak at the testing temperature.
Width and thickness measurements were made after tensile tests in order
to evaluate the normal anisotropy r-values.
The metallographic samples were prepared via grinding and
polishing, and then were chemically etched in acetic picric solution
(5 g picric acid, 5 ml acetic acid, 10 ml distilled water, 80 ml ethanol)
for about 20 s. The microstructure and fracture surfaces were char-
acterized using SEM (EVO18, Carl Zeiss, Germany). For each condition,
at least eight micrograph images were included to analyze the grain size
and its distribution by using the software Nano Measurer System 1.2.
Texture and phase analysis was carried out by means of X-ray
diﬀractometer (XRD, D/Max 2500PC, Rigaku, Japan) with Cu K α

The distinct deformation behavior between the ED and TD samples
under room temperature tension is related to the operation of di ﬀerent
deformation mode. The image quality map superimposed with twin
boundaries of longitudinal section of ED- sample after tension at 25 °C
is shown in Fig. 4b. It reveals that in addition to a small volume fraction
of {10
12} extension and {10 11}-{1012} secondary twin, a considerable
volume fraction of {10 11} contraction twins are observed in the ED-
sample subjected to tension at 25 °C. When tension deformed along ED,
shear stress is almost in parallel with {0002} basal plane, which makes
it very di ﬃcult to operate basal slip. As most of the grains having c-axis
normal to the tension direction (as revealed in Fig. 1c), they favor both
the prismatic < a > slip and {10
11} contraction twinning. The CRSS of
the prismatic < a > slip is far less than that of the {10 11} contraction
twinning, so the yielding should be predominated by the prismatic
< a > slip, while {10 11} contraction twinning operates as the ﬂow
stress further increases. It is in accordance with the fact that a
considerable volume fraction of {10 11} contraction twins are observed
in the ED sample ( Fig. 4a –d). Prismatic < a > slip and {10 11} contrac-
tion twinning are considered to be hard deformation mechanisms,
leading to a high yield stress and low fracture elongation ( Fig. 2a). In
contrast, in addition to the hard deformation of prismatic < a > slip
and {1011} contraction twinning, the {10 12} extension twinning can be
easily activated by tensile stress along the c-axis [25]. Therefore, when
tension deformed along TD, grains with c-axis close to the TD (as
revealed in Fig. 1c) are more favorably oriented for tensile twinning
and basal slip. According to Ref. [26], for tension parallel to the c-axis,
the early deformation to ~ 3% is dominated by slips while twinning
becomes a predominant deformation mode in the strain range 3 –9%,
which then induces a random distribution of the c-axis of twin texture
in the RD(ED)-TD plane. The crystallographic reorientation that occurs
during tensile twinning generally places grains in a more di ﬃcult
orientation with respect to continued slip or twinning, which results in
a subsequently increased macroscopic harden, promoting desirable
plastic stability against necking [27], and hence a simultaneous high
strength and high ductility. When tension deformed along 45D, there is
an angle between the shear stress and basal plane of the grains with c-
axis close to TD, which is favorable for the basal slip, and hence a lower
yield strength but higher ductility.

signiﬁcantly enhanced activities of GBS, which is known to be e ﬀective
in reducing r -values and the anisotropy at elevated deformation
temperature (above 200 °C) [35,36].
5. Conclusions
In summary, a ﬁne-grained (~5 µm) ZK60 magnesium alloy was
prepared by hot extrusion in the present work. Moreover, tensile
deformation modes and anisotropic behavior of the ﬁne-grained ZK60
alloy during tensile deformation at 25, 150 and 275 °C were investi-
gated. At room temperature, the ﬁne-grained ZK60 alloy exhibits a high
tensile strength of ~269 –327 MPa and high ductility of ~15– 39% when
loaded at ED, TD and 45D directions, indicating a high anisotropy due
to the relatively strong basal texture. Under tension at 150 °C, a
decrease in anisotropy is observed in the as-extruded ZK60 alloy, which
can be attributed to the relatively balanced activity of basal and non-
basal systems during straining. As tensile temperature further increases
from 150 °C to 275 °C, a transition from climb-controlled dislocation
creep to grain boundary-mediated GBS takes place, leading to high
superplasticity, i.e. fracture strains of ~376– 434%. Meanwhile, the
enhanced activities of GBS at 275 °C results in a nearly random texture,
which is responsible for the abrupt decrease in tensile anisotropy.
