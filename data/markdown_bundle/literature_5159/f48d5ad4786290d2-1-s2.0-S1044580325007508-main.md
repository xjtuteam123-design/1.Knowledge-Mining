1. Introduction
Nickel-based superalloys, comprising a face-centered cubic- γ matrix 
strengthened with L1
2
- γ’ precipitates, are used extensively in the aero -
space and power industries as they display excellent mechanical per -
formance at high operating temperatures over years of use with good 
coarsening and oxidation resistance [ 1 – 3 ]. Iron and cobalt, which 
partition to the γ matrix, can be added in minor quantities to Ni-base 
superalloys to decrease cost and increase corrosion resistance, respec -
tively [ 2 – 4 ]. Cobalt-based superalloys can offer superior high- 
temperature mechanical properties and melting temperatures, as 
compared to conventional Ni-superalloys [ 2 ]. However, L1
2
-strength -
ened Co-based superalloys typically utilize the Co
3
(Al,W) γ’ phase, with 
numerous studies following their development [ 5 – 12 ]. W-containing 
Co-based superalloys thus display relatively high density and cost, 
leading to various attempts to reduce or even remove tungsten from 
these alloys, replacing it with lightweight γ’ -formers [ 13 – 23 ]. Addi -
tionally, recently there has been a push to further replace Co with other 
matrix elements such as Ni and especially Fe to lower costs [ 21 – 27 ].
While Ni-, Co-, Ni-Co- and Ni-Fe-based superalloys are well studied, 
much less is known about alloys where all three principal elements — Co, 
Fe, and Ni — are used in near-equiatomic concentrations with the aim to 
improve cost and density-specific strength in extreme environments. 
These high-entropy superalloys (HESAs) favor the stabilization of face- 
centered cubic (FCC) or body-centered cubic (BCC) structures over 
more embrittling intermetallic phases [ 24 , 26 – 31 ]. In addition to their 
multi-principal-element CoFeNi matrix, HESAs contain alloying ele -
ments which encourage the formation and stability of the strengthening 
γ’ phase, such as Al, Nb, Ti, V and W [ 25 , 26 , 32 – 37 ]. Additional re -
fractory metals like Mo, Nb, and Ta typically improve the solvus tem -
perature [ 16 , 32 , 36 , 38 ] and creep resistance, while Cr is crucial for 
oxidation resistance [ 4 , 9 , 13 , 31 ].

based HESAs. Their compositions are linked to two studies by Ohl

their toughness and ductility ( Table 2 ). All remaining alloys contain 
~25 – 50 vol% of γ’ phase, as estimated from post-etched secondary 
electron imaging. Further analysis of lattice mismatch and precise vol -

tion, synchrotron X-ray diffraction, transmission electron microscopy, or 
atom probe tomography in future work.

with medium- or high-volume fractions of non- γ’ intermetallics were

not chosen due to poor expected oxidation resistance. The two modified 
HEA-based alloys, HEA-4Cr and HEA-8Cr , were thus chosen. However, 
their lack of refractory elements can be expected to lower their solvus 
temperatures and creep resistance, and future studies to enhance the γ - γ’ 
solvus transition temperature beyond 950

4. Conclusions
Twelve high-entropy superalloys were investigated — with nine 
comprising an equiatomic CoFeNi γ -matrix — alloyed with lightweight γ’ 
formers (Al, Ti, V), additions of refractory elements (Nb, Ta, Mo), and 
Cr. With the aim of improving high-temperature γ - γ’ phase stability and 
mechanical properties, these alloys were developed based on three 
previous alloys [ 12 , 14 ]. The following main conclusions are reached: 
1. Transformation Temperatures : As expected, the additions of re -
fractory elements increase solvus temperature for most alloys, except 
for HEA-22Cr , which shows very high levels of Cr-rich intermetallic 
phases in the microstructure. A higher Ti:Al ratio significantly in -
creases the solvus temperature from HEA-Al to HEA-Ti .
2. Microstructure : 
- All alloys exhibit a dual phase γ - γ’ microstructure, with various 
amounts of non- γ’ phases. All except HEA-22Cr and 0-Ni contain 
25 – 50 vol% of γ’ precipitates.
- All HEA-based alloys (except for HEA-22Cr and HEA + ) show a low 
volume fraction of round Al/Ti-rich non- γ’ precipitates, similar to 
the original HEA composition.
- Cr additions in the HEA-xCr series can be tolerated up to 8 at. % Cr 
without formation of additional non- γ’ phases.
- Refractory metal additions in all alloys result in the formation of 
refractory-rich non- γ’ precipitates. However, these are less preva -
lent in CCA-NbTa and CCA + as compared to the CCA or 18Fe8Cr 
base compositions.
- Fe-base (with Fe/(Ni + Co) = 1) shows promise as a precipitate- 
strengthened Fe-Co-Ni-based superalloy; future efforts in 
lowering the refractory content may reduce non- γ’ precipitates.
3. Oxidation : The addition of 4 and 8 at. % Cr in HEA-xCr results in 
slightly thinner oxide layers than HEA-B , upon oxidation at 850 
◦
C. 
The oxidation growth rate shows a small improvement with 
increasing Cr content. Most notably, the addition of Fe up to equia -
tomic CoFeNi (26 – 29 at. % Fe) provides similar oxidation resistance 
compared to previously studied medium-Fe variants 12Fe4Cr , 
14Fe4Cr , and 18Fe4Cr (12 – 18 at. % Fe). Further Cr additions may be 
needed to achieve significant improvements in oxidation resistance, 
but alloys with high Cr and Fe content show high amounts of non- γ’ 
phases, as demonstrated by both EqFeCr and HEA-22Cr . Aluminum 
additions in line with CCA-derivative compositions may instead 
achieve improved oxidation resistance.
