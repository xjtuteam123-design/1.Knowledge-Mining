ABSTRACT 
This paper presents an innovative investigation on γ / γ’ -strengthened multi-component Co-Ni-Ti-V-based su -
peralloys, which has led to the successful development of high-strength Co-Ni-Ti-based superalloys. This research 
holds immense importance for the advancement of Co-Ti-based superalloys, offering valuable insights and 
support for their future progress. We present a design process that utilizes CALPHAD to develop the Co-Ni-Ti-V- 
Al quinary alloy from Co-Ni-Ti-V quaternary superalloy in this paper. The optimized composition of Co-20Ni- 
10Ti-4V was determined in the Co-Ni-Ti-V quaternary system, which exhibited high γ’ solvus temperature, 
low coarsening rate of γ’ precipitates, and high yield strength (after 60 h of annealing at 750 
◦
C, the maximum 
value at room temperature can reach 1718 MPa and the maximum value at 900 
◦
C reaches 600 MPa). The 
addition of Al as a microalloying element in Co-20Ni-10Ti-4V reduces lattice mismatch and density, and im -
proves the thermal stability of the γ’ phase, thereby further enhancing the alloy ’ s high-temperature performance. 
The study also revealed that the distribution behavior of Al shifted with an increase in its content, which may be 
attributed to the complex interactions among Al, Ti, and V atoms. Moreover, the developed Co-20Ni-10Ti-4V-1Al 
alloy demonstrated excellent performance with high γ’ solvus temperature, low mass density, low γ / γ’ lattice 
mismatch, and exceptional γ’ phase thermal stability. This alloy also exhibited superior specific yield strengths 
compared to W and Ta-free Co-Ti-based lightweight superalloys, thus positioning it as a promising contender for 
high-temperature applications. 
1. Introduction 
Superalloys have made remarkable strides over the past few decades, 
becoming an essential component in the creation of innovative jet en -
gines, gas turbine engines, and rocket components [ 1 – 4 ]. Nickel-based 
superalloys are widely used in comparison to cobalt-based and 
iron-based alloys due to their unique microstructure. The presence of 
ordered Ni
3
Al phase (L1
2 
structure) and γ -Ni phase (Fcc structure) 
provides nickel-based alloys with high strength and creep resistance [ 5 ], 
making them a preferred choice for various applications. However, 
nickel-based superalloys have a major drawback: their melting point 
(about 1455
◦
C) is lower than that of cobalt, which hinders their ability 
to perform in ultra-high temperature environments [ 6 – 8 ]. As a result, 
cobalt-based superalloys have been the subject of research interest since 
the first γ / γ’ two-phase Co-Al-W alloys were reported in 2006 [ 9 ].C 
Cobalt-based alloys boast a higher melting point of roughly 1495
◦
C and 
excellent mechanical properties, to the point where some researchers 
predict they could supplant nickel-based superalloys as the next gener -
ation of high-temperature structural materials [ 6 , 9 – 13 ]. 
When it comes to strengthening superalloys, the Co-Ti binary system 
is unique in its ability to form stable γ’ (L1
2 
Co
3

phases. This feature makes Co-Ti-based alloys particularly appealing to 
researchers searching for potential candidates for γ / γ’ -strengthened Co- 
based superalloys [ 14 – 16 ]. One remarkable aspect of Co

yield strength increases with temperature, peaking near 750 
◦

(anomalous yield strength of Ni

When evaluating the value of these alloys, it is imperative to consider

In addition, over the past century, researchers have developed su -
peralloys with exceptional properties through a lengthy process of trial 
and error. However, this approach is time-consuming, expensive, and 
may not yield the desired outcome. To improve superalloy design effi -
ciency, reduce resource waste, and increase success rates, advanced 
technologies such as computer-aided materials design simulation have 
become necessary. These technologies provide comprehensive material 
simulation and prediction in a relatively short period of time, allowing 
scientists to quickly identify potential superalloy components, process -
ing methods, and performance parameters [ 34 – 37 ]. This rapid devel -
opment and optimization of superalloys can lead to significant 
advancements in the field. 
The purpose of this study is to explore the potential of Co-Ni-Ti-based 
lightweight superalloys without heavy, high-valence elements such as W 
and Ta. The objective is to combine Co-Ti-based and Co-Ni-based su -
peralloys to create a high-performance Co-Ni-Ti-based superalloy, in 
order to fill a gap in the relevant field. First, to facilitate the rapid design 
of Co-Ni-Ti-V quaternary alloy with phenomenal properties, we have 
utilized the Co-Ni-Ti-V calculated phase diagram according to the 
thermodynamic database of Co-based superalloys established by our 
group. In addition, we have also investigated the impact of the Al 
element on the morphology, solvus temperature of the γ’ phase, and the 
coarsening rate of γ’ precipitates in Co-Ni-Ti-V quaternary alloys. 
2. Materials and methods 
2.1. CALPHAD and first-principles calculations 
2.1.1. Alloy design method 
We performed thermodynamic calculations for the Co-Ni-Ti-V alloy 
system using Pandat and the Co-based superalloy thermodynamic 
database established by our group. 
2.1.2. First-principles calculations 
In the present work, a 3 × 3 × 3 supercell for L1
2 
structure was 
adopted to construct the model of these γ’ phases. The Special Quasi- 
Random Structure (SQS) approach can obtain either a disordered or 
partially ordered L1
2 
structure. All of the calculations were periodic DFT 
[ 38 ] calculations using the Vienna Ab initial Simulation Package (VASP) 
[ 39 , 40 ]. The interaction between core electrons and valence electrons 
was treated by the projector augmented wave (PAW) [ 41 ]. The 
exchange-correlation interaction between valence electrons was 
described by the revised Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof functional with the 
generalized gradient approximation (GGA) [ 42 ]. A 450 eV kinetic en -
ergy cutoff of wave function was used. The Brillouin zone sampling was 
performed with a Г -centered 5 × 5 × 5 k -point mesh for L1
2 
structure. 
Reciprocal space integration was performed by means of the 
Methfessel-Paxton [ 43 , 44 ] technique with a smearing width of 0.1 eV. 
The atomic coordinates were optimized to get an accurate geometry, and 
the convergence criteria for structure optimizations were based as fol -
lows: the convergence thresholds of total energy and the maximum force 
on the atoms were set to 10
 5 
eV and 10
 2 
eV/Å, respectively.

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Co-Ni-Ti-V quaternary alloy design 
The discovery of a stable ordered L1
2 
phase in the Co-Ti binary 
system has stimulated significant interest from the superalloy research 
community. However, the Co-12Ti alloy has not yet achieved its inten -
ded performance due to defects such as the tendency of L1
2 
ordered 
phase to precipitate discontinuously in the cellular form and the inferior 
stability of the L1
2 
phase at elevated temperatures [ 19 , 45 ]. To mitigate 
these issues, researchers have examined various strategies, with the 
primary approach involving the introduction of a third element, such as 
Mo, V, or Cr, to the alloy and reducing the Ti content [ 23 , 45 – 47 ]. These 
measures are intended to enhance the microstructural stability of the 
L1
2 
phase, with the aim of optimizing the properties of Co-Ti alloys for 
high-performance applications. In addition, this study aims to introduce 
Ni element into Co-Ti-based alloy to form Co-Ni-Ti-based superalloys. 
However, the introduction of high levels of Ni elements into Co-Ti-based 
alloys easily leads to the formation of the Ni
3
Ti phase with a D0
24 
structure [ 48 ]. For the Co-Ni-Ti-V quaternary alloy design, an annealing 
temperature of 750 
◦
C, a Ti content of 10 at.% and a Ni content of 10 – 20 
at.% have been identified as optimal within the parameters of the spe -
cific research purposes and conditions. It is important to note that these 
settings and parameters may not be universally applicable or optimal for 
other purposes or contexts. 
Fig. 1 (a)-(c) present the isopleth sections of Co-10Ni-10Ti-xV, Co- 
15Ni-10Ti-xV, and Co-20Ni-10Ti-xV alloys. It can be observed that the γ’ 
solvus temperature increases with increasing V content as well as the 
existence of a significant γ / γ’ two-phase region. Furthermore, with 
increasing V content, the γ phase melting point temperature decreases, 
indicating that the difference between the γ phase melting point tem -
perature and the γ’ solvus temperature gradually decreases. Therefore, it 
is necessary to provide a hot appropriate processing windows that would 
maintain the operability of the alloy.

In terms of the coarsening behavior of γ’ precipitates, it can be 
justified through the LSW coarsening theory. This mechanism is initi -

phase. In this regard, diffusion across an ordered interface potentially 
constitutes the rate-limiting step in the coarsening of the precipitated 
phase [ 58 ]. Consequently, the evolution of average precipitate radius

Systems that follow a volume diffusion-controlled coarsening 
mechanism exhibit a dependence of the coarsening rate on the volume 
fraction of the precipitated phase. An increase in the γ’ volume fraction 
(V γ’ ) leads to an increase in the coarsening rate [ 67 – 69 ]. As the γ’ 
volume fraction (V γ’ ) increases with the addition of aluminum (Al), the 
coarsening rate (K) increases accordingly. However, due to the increase 
in γ’ volume fraction, the reduction in γ channels and the decrease in γ’ 
precipitate spacing will result in the overlapping of the diffusion field 
[ 70 ]. As a result, the LSW model may no longer be valid. In this study, 
the average size of γ’ precipitates conforms to the growth law of the LSW 
model. In addition, once coalescence occurs, it implies the overlapping 
of diffusion fields. Particles usually undergo a rapid transformation to an 
equilibrium shape by particle/matrix surface diffusion, which means 
that the LSW model is no longer valid [ 70 ]. In order to prevent such 
situations from occurring, the aging time of the corresponding alloy has 
been shortened. 
It is worth noting that the diffusion rate has a significant impact on 
the coarsening rate. The diffusion rate of aluminum (Al) into the cobalt 
(Co) matrix is similar to that of titanium (Ti) into the cobalt matrix, but it 
is much faster compared to vanadium (V) in the cobalt (Co) matrix [ 71 , 
72 ]. This observation suggests that the interdiffusion process is accel -
erated. Curiously, in this particular system, lattice mismatch does not 
have a noteworthy influence on the coarsening rate. The research 
findings presented here, which concluded that the diffusion rate of 
alloying elements, followed by lattice mismatch, is the primary factor 
affecting coarsening rate, are similar to the conclusions of He et al. [ 73 ].

In the study, the bulk moduli of the γ’ strengthening phase and γ 
matrix of 0Al, 1Al, and 6Al alloys were calculated based on density 
function theory, and their temperature dependence was plotted in 
Fig. 14 (a). As can be seen, the γ’ strengthening phase show higher bulk 
moduli then the γ matrix for 0Al, 1Al, and 6Al alloys in the studied 
temperature range, although both of them display a decreasing trend 
with rising temperature. These results provide the intrinsic mechanical 
properties of the matrix and strengthening phases. However, additional 
factors must be considered to gain a better understanding of the actual 
strength differences between the 0Al, 1Al, and 6Al alloys. 
The mismatch degree is always a crucial factor to consider when 
studying superalloys. Therefore, we utilized first-principle calculations 
to determine the change in volumetric thermal expansion coefficients of 
the γ’ strengthening phase and γ matrix with temperature in 0Al, 1Al, 
and 6Al alloys, as depicted in Fig. 14 (b). As temperature increased, we 
observed a gradual increase in the difference between the volumetric 
thermal expansion coefficients of the γ matrix and the γ’ strengthening 
phase. This suggests that the lattice mismatch between these two phases 
decreased with increasing temperature, which is consistent with pat -
terns previously reported in superalloys [ 87 – 89 ]. Notably, the reduced 
difference in the volumetric thermal expansion coefficients between the 
two phases in the 1Al and 6Al alloys, relative to the 0Al alloy, suggests 
that the addition of Al can enhance the lattice match between the γ’ 
strengthening phase and γ matrix, thereby improving interfacial 
stability.

4. Conclusion 
This study demonstrates the efficiency of using the CALPHAD 
method to design high performance superalloys and the feasibility of 
designing novel lightweight high temperature structural materials in the 
Co-Ni-Ti-V system. The results of the current study can be summarized 
as follows: 
1. Utilizing CALPHAD method, a Co-20Ni-10Ti-4V quaternary super -
alloy with excellent properties has been designed, exhibiting high 
value of yield strength (after 48 h of annealing at 750 
◦
C, 1358 Mpa 
at room temperature & 512 Mpa at 900 
◦
C). 
2. With increasing Ni and V contents, the γ’ solvus temperature 
increased and the γ / γ’ lattice mismatch decreased in the Co-xNi- 
10Ti-yV (x = 10, 15 and 20; y = 2 and 4) alloys. Thus, the Co- 
20Ni-10Ti-4V alloy exhibits both high γ’ solvus temperature 
(~1156 
◦
C) and low lattice mismatch (0.70%). 
3. The partition tendency of Al elements varied with Al content. In 1Al 
alloys, Al showed a preference for distribution into the γ phase, 
whereas in 3Al and 6Al alloys, it tended to be distributed in the γ’ 
phase, which may be attributed to the complex interactions between 
Ti, V and Al atoms. 
4. The Co-20Ni-10Ti-4V-1Al alloy developed in this study has high γ’ 
solvus temperature (~1220.7 
◦
