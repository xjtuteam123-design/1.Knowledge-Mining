Mechanical Properties of Alloys of IrNb and 
Other High-Temperature Intermetallic Compounds 
R.L. FLEISCHER, R.D. FIELD, K.K. DENIKE, and R.J. ZABALA 
Starting from the results of earlier screening tests of binary high-temperature intermetallic 
compounds, alloys based on A13Nb, A18Vs, IrNb, Si2V, and Si3Y 5 have been studied by micro- 
hardness, elastic modulus, impact strength, and, in some cases, compressional stress-strain be- 
havior. Significant improvements were observed only in the IrNb-based alloys, several of which, 
like IrNb itself, have good impact resistance and some plasticity at room temperature. 
I. INTRODUCTION 
INTERMETALLIC compounds that are structurally useful 
at high temperature are being tested for potential aero- 
space and engine applications. The recent renewed in- 
terest in these compounds I1'2'3] is based on the prospects 
of high specific strength and specific modulus at ele- 
vated temperatures and the fact that there are hundreds 
of mechanically untested binary compounds. [4] In partic- 
ular, diverse binary high-temperature materials have re- 
ceived intensive attention in a recent survey.t5] 
The stiffnesses of intermetallics are often greater than 
those of the metals from which they are formed t61 and 
they frequently maintain their strength better with in- 
creasing temperature than do disordered alloys, t7,81 The 
low specific gravities of many of them produce high spe- 
cific stiffness and specific strength, two quantities that 
are critical to most uses in aircraft and rotating parts. 
This paper reports results of alloy variations on a few of 
the ordered intermetallics that were screened tSj as part of 
a search for future high-temperature materials. 
II. SELECTION OF ALLOYS 
The primary focus in this work is on alloys of the Llo

temperature and tough at room temperature, tSj The iri-

III. PROCEDURE 
A. Preparation of Samples 
Samples were arc melted into disk-shaped ingots using 
as high-purity components as were reasonably available. 
Although occasionally portions of the arc-melted ingots 
contained porosity that was visible on polished sections, 
the least porous fractions were selected for hardness and 
modulus measurements. It is believed that neither type 
of measurement was appreciably degraded by porosity. 
For the five binary compounds studied, the observed 
specific gravities were compared to the values that are 
calculated from the lattice parameters. They agreed within 
0.03 pct on the average, and the average magnitude of 
the discrepancy was - 1 pct. Examples of the purities are 
Ir: 99.95 pct; Cr, Nb, Ti, and V: 99.99 pct; Co and Ni: 
99.998 pct; and AI: 99.9999 pct. Samples were an- 
nealed in Ar-filled SiO2 envelopes along with a small 
piece of Y to take up oxygen. All samples were electro- 
machined and polished to 1.0  0.5  0.5 cm, then 
subjected to mechanical tests. 
B. Mechanical Tests 
Three types of mechanical tests are used for all of the 
samples. 
Ultrasonic acoustic wave reflections gave the moduli, 
of which three of the values for binary compounds have 
been reported previously.j6] Young's moduli are given in 
Tables I and II.

broken with light tap of light hammer; level 1, required 
repeated sharp blows for fracture with a light hammer 
(160 g); level 2, required repeated sharp blows with a 
heavy hammer (729 g); and level 3, not fractured even 
with the heavy hammer. On this scale, we reported [1~

A. Microhardness 
Results are given in the form of smooth curves that 
were drawn through the data in the manner illustrated by 
Figure 2 of Reference 10. In order to minimize confu-

V. DISCUSSION 
A. Miscellaneous Alloys 
The alloys listed in Table I did not provide encour- 
aging results. Proceeding in sequence, the D022 struc- 
tures (Figure 1) showed weakening on alloying, no 
improvement in Tbd, and little change in room-temperature 
toughness. The AIsVs(cI52) alloys with boron were mar- 
ginally stronger (Figure 2) but had much higher Tb~'s and 
no improvement in toughness. However, one alloy im- 
proved by 28 pct in Young's modulus. Si2V(hP9) alloys 
similarly had no improvement in Tbd but experienced a 
slight increase in chisel toughness. The parabolic oxi- 
dation rate in air tl21 for Si67V23Cr10, expected to be the 
best of the three, was indeed good: 2- 10 -12 g cm -4 s -1 
at 1000 ~ i.e., slower than the oxidation of Cr to Cr203 
and similar to the rate of oxidation of Si to SIO2. The 
Si3Ys(D8s)-based alloys were stronger than Si3Y5 at all 
temperatures (Figure 4), except for Si3sY52Cq0 + 1 pct B. 
That exception, curiously, is the only one of the five to 
have a nonzero chisel toughness; it also had the lowest 
volume fraction of the major phase. The Tbd values ranged 
widely but did not correlate with room-temperature 
toughness (since both strain rate sensitivity and the pres- 
ence of second phases could affect the two quantities 
differently).

B. Discussion 
The observed dislocation structure within the IrNb 
specimens does not appear to explain the relatively high 
ductility and toughness displayed by this material. The 
number of slip systems is clearly limited for both phases, 
and the dislocations transcending twin boundaries in the 
L10 phase do not appear to be mobile. However, the highly 
twinned nature of the L10 phase and high density of 
stacking faults in the Ir11Nb9 phase, coupled with the twin 
relationship between the two crystal structures, point to 
the possibility that a stress-induced martensitic transfor- 
mation exists between the two phases. This trans- 
formation may be able to accommodate the imposed 
strains. In situ cold-stage experiments were conducted in 
the transmission electron microscope to determine if a 
martensite reaction could be thermally induced. Al- 
though the specimen was cooled from room temperature 
to -180 ~ no transformations between the two phases 
were observed. As a final observation, it is interesting 
to note that a 1964 reference to a phase in the Ta-Rh 
system, which has the same orthorhombic structure as 
IrllNb9, states that "thin rods can be bent easily. "II31 
Plasticity in the Ir25Ni25Vs0 specimen is clearly limited 
to the L10 phase, since no dislocations could be found 
within the A15 grains. The fact that the L10 grains are 
actually fcc with precipitates of the three tetragonal vari- 
ants probably facilitates slip in this material, even though 
the actual volume fraction of the cubic phase appears to 
be quite small. Deformation occurs by slip of 1/2(110) 
dislocations, with the tetragonal axis defined by the 
dominant variant in the grain. The stacking faults are 
likely to be caused by 1/6(112) twinning dislocations, 
as seen in other L10 compounds. I151 A higher density of 
these is observed in the vicinity of grain boundaries, in 
order to accommodate the increased need for arbitrary 
shape changes in this region. 
VII. NOTES AND CONCLUSIONS 
A. Potential Applications of IrNb-Based Alloys 
Although these alloys are of high density, they could 
still be useful in jet engines, first, if they can be made 
less costly by replacing Ir, and second, if they allow in- 
creased engine operating temperatures (see the discus- 
sion in Reference 4). A second application is in 
radioisotope thermoelectric generators, using IrNb or de- 
rivatives to replace Ir. Better toughness at lower cost and 
lower density would be attractions of such a substitution. 
These preliminary measurements, however, are not suf- 
ficient to establish whether such a substitution would sat- 
isfy all of the needs. For example, a temperature must 
be identified at which useful formability of IrNb is 
available. 
B. Prospects 
The most promising of the alloys are those with the 
highest room-temperature toughness, the four shown in 
Figure 8 and COl0Ira0Nbs0 . Of these, only the latter has 
a high-temperature "strength" (microhardness) that ex- 
ceeds that of the base compound IrNb. 
C. Conclusions 
Among the various alloys reported here, IrNb has shown 
the best mechanical properties of the intermetallics that 
we have examined. Microhardness is still high at 1100 ~ 
(3.7 GPa), no cracks were seen at indentations (even at 
room temperature), and the material survived the chisel- 
and-hammer tests. Several alloys based on IrNb show 
similar properties and have the merit of containing less 
iridium.
