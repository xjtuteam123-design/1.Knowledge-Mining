Abstract 
This study developed a series of refractory Al xHfNbTaTiZr high-entropy alloys 
(HEAs) with an aim to improve strength, and reduce density of the very ductile base 
alloy HfNbTaTiZr. Despite the diversity of crystal structures among the constituent 
elements, all the HEAs are single solid solution phase with body-centered cubic (BCC) 
structure. The addition of Al significantly improve s the strength but reduces the 
ductility due to large solution hardening. The line ar relation between yield strength 
and atomic percentage of Al suggests that the strengthening effect of a certain element 
in a single-phase HEA alloy can be explained based on quasi-binary alloy concept. 
Crack formations in deformed AlHfNbTaTiZr alloy with the lowest fracture strain are 
mainly along the boundaries between dendrite and in terdendrite. This agrees with its 
large deviation of Al content and thus strength between dendrite and interdendrite. 
 
Keywords: High-entropy alloys; Refractory alloys; Mechanical property; 
Solution hardening

1. Introduction 
Materials for high temperature applications, such a s those in aerospace and 
petrochemical industries, are in high demand. Due t o the incipient melting point at 
around 1300 oC, Ni-based superalloys have their applicability only up to temperatures 
between 1160 and 1277 oC [1, 2]. Various alloys based on refractory metal elements 
having melting point higher than 1650 oC, such as niobium and tantalum alloys, might 
provide a practical solution to this problem [3-6]. It is because alloy softening does 
not generally occur until temperature exceeds 0.5 t o 0.6 T m [7], at which the material 
becomes susceptible to thermally activated deformat ion. Unfortunately, these two 
refractory alloy systems show high densities, poor oxidation resistance, and poor 
ductility at elevated temperatures. In considering the benefits of high-entropy and 
sluggish diffusion effects [8-12] which respectivel y enhance the formation of solid 
solution phase, and slow down the diffusion rate an d thus creep rate, HEAs with 
suitable composition are considered as a good candi date to high-temperature 
applications. In recent years, Senkov et al. [13, 14] proposed a refractory metal design 
concept to develop new alloys aimed at overcoming t he temperature limitation of 
superalloys [15, 16], which are named as refractory HEAs. The first two researches 
revealed that NbMoTaW and VNbMoTaW refractory HEAs possess a simple 
body-centered cubic (BCC) microstructure and pronou nced solution hardening [13, 
14]. Both alloys still have yield strengths of 405 and 477 MPa at 1600 °C, 
respectively. However, their drawbacks include much higher density than 9.0 g/cm 3 
and room-temperature brittleness. Later, they published the BCC alloy of HfNbTaTiZr 
[17, 18] with slightly lower strength than the firs t two refractory HEAs but having 
excellent ductility exceeding 50 %. However, this a lloy still has a density higher than 
9.0 g/cm3. Apparently, this alloy is unique in its large room-temperature ductility since 
BCC-HEAs are seldom ductile. In addition, this allo y also has quite high strength at

elevated temperatures and thus is promising for hig h temperature applications. This 
finding has led researchers to investigate microstr ucture and properties of other 
refractory HEAs with various molar ratios of their constituent elements [19-25]. 
Among these, Senkov et al. reported four high-hardn ess and low-density refractory 
HEAs of the Cr-Nb-Ti-V-Zr system [23] and two Al-co ntaining refractory HEAs: 
AlMo0.5NbTa0.5TiZr and Al0.4Hf0.6NbTaTiZr [25]. It was noted that the addition of Al 
could stabilize the bcc crystal structure and help to reduce the density of the refractory 
HEAs. The partial substitution of Hf with Al in the HfNbTaTiZr alloy was shown to 
reduce the density of the alloys by ∼9%, increase the RT hardness by 29% and 
enhance yield strength by 98%. On the other hand, c omplete substitution of Cr with 
Al in the CrMo0.5NbTa0.5TiZr alloy reduced the alloy density by 10.1%, incr eased RT 
hardness and yield strength by 12%, noticeably impr oved RT ductility and 
considerably increased, by more than 50%, high-temp erature strength in the 
temperature range from1073 K to 1473 K. Therefore, it is realized that Al addition 
could bring positive effects on the microstructure and properties of such refractory 
HEAs. 
Based on the above consideration, this study select ed Al for incorporation into 
HfNbTaTiZr for various kinds of benefits including lower density, and higher strength 
with adequate ductility. The evolution of microstru cture, mechanical properties and 
fracture behavior of refractory Al xHfNbTaTiZr HEAs with the variation of x from 0 
to 1 were investigated and discussed. Our aim has t wo more purposes: (1) to verify 
the ability of producing a single phase refractory solid solution with the addition of Al 
and (2) to optimize the Al content with best proper ties and provide a reference for 
further tuning the composition.

2. Experimental procedures 
Bulk AlxHfNbTaTiZr refractory HEAs with various quantities of Al ( x = 0, 0.3, 
0.5, 0.75, and 1.0), also simply denoted by Al 0, Al 0.3, Al 0.5, Al 0.75, and Al 1.0, 
respectively which were prepared in a vacuum arc melting furnace under a high-purity 
argon atmosphere. Ingots were obtained in a copper mould. The purity of all raw 
elemental materials was better than 99.99 wt%. Each sample was reversed and 
re-melted four times in order to assure chemical ho mogeneity . The final samples were 
button-shaped, approximately 8 mm thick, with a shi ny surface. The microstructure 
and chemical composition of the alloys were analyze d by scanning electron 
microscope (SEM, JEOL JEOL JSM-5410) and energy dispersive spectrometer (EDS). 
The crystal structures of the as-cast specimens wer e identified by X-ray diffraction 
(XRD, D/Max2500 V) using Cu K α radiation. Grain size was measured by the linear 
intercept method from micrographs taken by an optic al microscope (OM, Olympus 
BX51M). Vickers hardness measurements were conducte d using a MA TSUZAWA 
SEIKI MV -1 hardness tester under a load of 10 kgf w ith the duration of 15 s. Seven 
measurements on each specimen were done to obtain a n average. Room temperature 
compression tests were performed using an Instron 4 468 machine under a strain rate 
of 10 -3/s. In this test, we employed at least two cylindri cal specimens 3 mm in 
diameter and 5 mm in length for each alloy, and put Teflon interface layers in-between 
specimen and platen for minimizing the friction and barrel effect.

surprising when considering the tendency of Al to f orm strong BCC structure. It is 
well-known that although Al is an FCC element, it interacts with transition metal with 
hybrid pd-orbital and enhances the directional bonding for B CC structure [26-29]. 
The p-orbitals (filled) of Al as well as their high electron density and high Fermi level 
tend to promote the transfer of electrons to transi tion metals (TMs) with d-orbitals 
(partially filled), such as Ti, Zr, Nb and Hf. This results in the formation of covalent 
bonds in which Al-TM bonds are significantly shorte r than the sum of the

3.3 Microstructure and composition analysis 
As-cast specimens of Al xHfNbTaTiZr alloys have been observed under optical 
microscope to evaluate the grain size by linear int ercept method. The Al 0, Al0.3, Al0.5 
Al0.75, and Al1.0 specimens have grain sizes of 120 ± 23 µm, 105 ± 12 µm, 86 ± 9 µm, 
89 ± 13 µm, 92 ± 13 µm, respectively. This indicates that an increase in Al content 
reduces grain size at first but has no obvious reduction for Al0.5 Al0.75, and Al1.0 alloys. 
Fig. 3 presents backscattered electron images (BEI) of the Al xHfNbTaTiZr 
as-cast specimens, which show that each microstruct ure contains dendrite and 
interdendritic phase regions. Table 3 lists the act ual overall compositions of the 
various specimens, as determined by large-area EDS analysis. It shows that the actual 
compositions are very close to the designed composi tions of Al xHfNbTaTiZr alloys, 
respectively. At x = 0, the bright dendrite region is enriched with Nb and Ta, while the 
dark and gray interdendrite region is enriched with Hf, Ti, and Zr, as shown in Fig. 3 
(a). This segregation trend is similar to that anal yzed for the same alloy [17, 20]. At x 
= 0.3 to 1.0, Al segregates to interdendrite region and intensifies with increased Al 
addition. In contrast, Ta and Nb concentrations in dendrite region are increased 
relative to interdendrite region correspondingly. I t is interesting to note that Ti shows 
less segregation between dendrite and interdendrite region as compared with other 
elements and becomes lesser segregation with increa sed Al addition. The observed 
partitioning of elements in the present alloys coul d be attributed to two main factors: 
differences in the melting point (see Table 1) and the driving of bonding energy (see 
Table 2). Ta, Nb-rich dendrite first forms simply because these two elements were the 
highest two in melting points. This was also used t o explain the segregation of 
highest-melting-point elements in the dendrite of refractory alloys [15, 20]. According 
to phase diagram, dendrite forms from liquid phase to solid-liquid phase field.

Subsequent, solidification would complete from soli d-liquid region into solid phase 
region. Therefore, the solid-liquid region of the p resent alloys would be between Nb, 
Ta-rich solid phase and Nb, Ta-depleted liquid phas e. On the other hand, Al has the 
lowest melting point and stronger bonding with Hf, Ti, and Zr, both factors enhance

The extra diffraction peaks existing in XRD pattern of AlHfNbTaTiZr could be 
explained now. As aluminum content in the dendrite of this alloy is 6.8 at.% and that 
in the interdendrite is 19.6 at.% (see Table 3), th eir theoretical lattice constants based

large enough to be resolved in XRD pattern. Further more, this reversed deviation 
again confirms the strong bonding effect between Al and other constituent elements 
since higher Al content significantly enhances the reduction of lattice constant. This is 
reasonable because stronger bond strength could result in shorter bond length and thus 
smaller lattice constant [26-29, 33].

4. Conclusions 
Al has been selected to improve the strength and re duce the density of very 
ductile BCC HfNbTaTiZr alloy . The resulting AlxHfNbTaTiZr alloys (x = 0, 0.3, 0.5, 
0.75, and 1.0) maintain a single BCC solid solution with typical dendritic structures in 
its as-cast state. The addition of Al increased the compressive yield strength from 
1073 ± 6 MPa to 1489 ± 7 MPa; however, ductility wa s reduced from > 50 to 21 %. 
The increase in yield strength can be attributed to solid solution strengthening 
resulting from increased lattice distortion and str onger bonding between Al and 
constituent transition metals. The solution hardeni ng shows a linear relation between 
yield strength and atomic percentage of Al, which s uggests that the explanation of 
strengthening effect of a certain element in a sing le-phase HEA alloy can use 
quasi-binary alloy concept and the effect is linear as long as no second phase forms.
