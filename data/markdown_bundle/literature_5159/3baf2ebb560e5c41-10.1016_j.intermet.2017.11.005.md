ABSTRACT
Phase stability information regarding the formation of the topologically close packed (TCP) phases is critical for
the design of advanced Ni-based and Co-based superalloys that contain high concentrations of refractory ele-
ments. Isothermal sections of the Co-Ni-W, Co-Ni-Mo, Co-Ni-Nb and Co-Ni-Ta systems at both 900 °C and 800 °C
were constructed for the ﬁrst time from electron probe microanalysis (EPMA) and electron backscatter dif-
fraction (EBSD) measurements on two Co-CoAl-Cr-Mo-Ni-Nb-Ta-W di ﬀusion multiples. The new data will serve
as experimental input to future thermodynamic assessments of the Co-Ni-X ternary systems in order to improve
the thermodynamic databases for the design and development of advanced Ni-based and Co-based superalloys.
1. Introduction
Nickel-based superalloys [1] with an exceptional combination of
high temperature strength, toughness, and excellent oxidation re-
sistance have been widely used as critical structural components in
aircraft, marine, and industrial gas turbines as well as other challenging
devices [2]. The remarkable high temperature properties are attributed
to the ordered L1
2 structured γ′-Ni3Al phase precipitated from the
disordered γ matrix. Refractory alloying elements W and Mo partition
preferentially to the γ matrix and thereby enhance the solid solution
strengthening [1,3]; whereas Nb and Ta promote the formation and
strengthening of the γ′ phase, as well as the γ′′ phase in the Ni-Fe-based
superalloys such as Inconel 718 [4,5]. Due to the increasing demand for
improved high temperature strength and higher creep resistance, an
increasing amount of refractory alloying elements has been added to
advanced Ni-based superalloys. However, superalloys with high con-
centration of these refractory alloying additions are susceptible to
precipitation of deleterious TCP phases, such as the δ, μ, σ and Laves
phases, during long-term exposure at elevated temperatures and
stresses [6–8].
Thermodynamic calculation via the CALPHAD (CALculation of
PHAse Diagram) approach has been widely used for designing multi-
component superalloys. It is very useful to predict the solubility limits
of these refractory elements (i.e., the exact phase boundaries of the
γ + γ′ two-phase region) when the related databases are available.
Unfortunately, predicting the stability of the TCP phases for the multi-
component systems is still a challenge even using the state-of-the-art
thermodynamic databases for Ni-based superalloys [9]. Therefore,
more experimental phase diagrams of the very basic ternary systems
that contain the TCP phases are urgently needed to improve the current
thermodynamic databases, and thus to improve the thermodynamic
description of the TCP phases. This is especially true for phase diagrams
at intermediate temperatures such as 900 °C and 800 °C where the
superalloys are usually more prone to the formation of the TCP phases.
A systematic experimental study of the phase equilibria of the Co-
Ni-X (X = W, Mo, Nb, Ta) ternary systems at both 900 °C and 800 °C
was thus carried out in the present study to collect more experimental
data as input to future thermodynamic assessments of the Co-Ni-X
ternary systems to re ﬁne the current thermodynamic databases for
more accurate predictions of the TCP phase stability, and eventually for
more reliable design of advanced Ni-based superalloys. The phase sta-
bility information of the Co-Ni-X systems can also be very useful for
designing Co-based superalloys, since additional elements such as W,
Mo, Nb, Ni, Ta are needed for alloying to increase and balance various
properties of the alloys [10–23].
Phase relations in the constituent binary subsystems (Co-Ni, Co-W,
Co-Mo, Co-Nb, Co-Ta, Ni-W, Ni-Mo, Ni-Nb and Ni-Ta) have been well
investigated and assessed in the literature [24]. The crystal structures of
the phases in the Co-Ni-X ternary systems within the present in-
vestigated temperature range are listed in Table 1 [25 –33]. A review of

2. Experimental methods
Ad i ﬀusion multiple [51–54] is an assembly of several pieces of
metals or alloys with intimate interface contact in a designed geometry
to contain several di ﬀusion couples and triples in one sample. After
diﬀusion annealing at an elevated temperature for an extended dura-
tion of time, solid solutions and intermetallic compounds form by
thermal interdi ﬀusion. Local equilibrium at the phase interfaces [55]
evaluated using compositional analysis from EPMA data is employed to
obtain the tie-line information and thus to build the equilibrium phase
diagrams. In the present study, the di ﬀusion-multiple approach was
employed to determine the isothermal sections of the Co-Ni-X (X = W,
Mo, Nb, Ta) ternary systems at both 900 °C and 800 °C.
Two Co-CoAl-Cr-Mo-Nb-Ni-Ta-W di ﬀusion multiples were made
with the same geometry, as shown schematically in Fig. 1(a). These
same di ﬀusion multiples were employed to determine the phase dia-
grams of the Co-Al-X (X = W, Mo, Nb, Ni, Ta) ternary systems and the
results were reported previously [56]. The procedure to make the dif-
fusion multiples was also described in detail already [56]. All the re-
sults reported in this article came from the Co-Ni-X (X = W, Mo, Nb,
Ta) di ﬀusion triples marked with rectangles in Fig. 1(a). The di ﬀusion
multiples were then encapsulated individually into quartz tubes for
annealing heat treatments at 900 °C and 800 °C for 1010 h and 2200 h,
respectively. A few pieces of Ta foils were placed inside each quartz
tube to act as oxygen getters to minimize the oxidation of the sample
from the residual oxygen or any oxygen that may di ﬀuse into the quartz
tube during the long-term heat treatment. Upon completing the di ﬀu-
sion annealing, the quartz tubes containing the di ﬀusion multiples were
quickly taken out of the furnaces and quenched into water by breaking
the tubes.
All the di ﬀusion multiples were sectioned using electrical discharge
machining (EDM), mounted, and ground/polished using standard me-
tallographic sample preparation techniques. SEM backscatter electron
(BSE) images were collected to analyze the phases that have formed
during the annealing heat treatment. Composition pro ﬁles were ob-
tained from quantitative EPMA across the phase interfaces on a JEOL
JXA-8530F microprobe with an accelerating voltage of 15 kV and a
probe current of 20 nA. The step size of each EPMA line-scan is 1 μm.
EBSD equipped in SEM is utilized for the phase veri ﬁcation of the in-
termetallic
phases in the Co-Ni-X ternary systems.

the formation of equilibrium phases by interdi ﬀusion processes.

They were identi ﬁed as the Co
3W and μ-Co7W6 phases based on the

Three phases were observed between pure Ni and pure W at the bottom

4W, NiW and NiW 2 phases re-
spectively according to the previously accepted Ni-W binary phase

4W is the

previously reported the intermetallic phases NiW and NiW
2 are actually

6W6C and Ni 2W4C carbides

is presented as open circles and the tie-lines are plotted as dotted lines.

Ni
4W and bcc (W) solid solution are the carbon-stabilized Ni 6W6C and
Ni2W4C phases rather than the NiW and NiW 2 phases; these two phases
were not included in the Co-Ni-W phase diagram.

3.2. The Co-Ni-Mo system
Fig. 4(a) is an SEM BSE image of the Co-Ni-Mo tri-junction region
marked by one of the small rectangles in the di ﬀusion multiple shown
in Fig. 1(a). Two layers between pure Ni and pure Mo were observed at
the bottom part of Fig. 4(a), but the layer adjacent to pure Mo is not an
intermetallic compound, but the bcc (Mo) solid solution that grew in a
columnar fashion. The phase layer to the right of this bcc (Mo) solid
solution layer was con ﬁrmed as the NiMo phase based on the con-
sistency of its compositions with the binary Ni-Mo phase diagram. Si-
milarly, the bcc (Mo) solid solution layer was observed at the left-hand
side of Fig. 4(a) where the Co-Mo di ﬀusion couple resides. The two
additional layers are the Co
3Mo and μ intermetallic compounds. The
sharp contract between the Mo solid solution and pure Mo in the SEM
image, as shown in Fig. 4(a), is due to the steep composition change as a
result of the extremely low di ﬀusivity of Co in Mo at 900 °C. The Ni-Mo
binary phase diagram [24] shows the Ni 3Mo intermetallic compound is
a stable phase at temperatures below 910 °C. However, Ni 3Mo was
absent in the SEM image. Although missing phases in di ﬀusion couples
are known from the literature [41,60], the exact reasons for their ab-
sence under certain conditions are still not well understood. It is pos-
sible that the Ni 3Mo phase layer is too thin (much less than 1 μm) to be
observable under SEM even after long-term heat treatment due to its
extremely sluggish di ﬀusion kinetics at 900 °C. Another more likely
possibility is that some kinetic barriers such as nucleation di ﬃculties
prevented the formation of this phase at 900 °C which is very low
comparing with the melting point of Mo. Fortunately the absence of the
Ni
3Mo phase does not signi ﬁcantly alter the topology of the isothermal
section of the Co-Ni-Mo system at 900 °C since other phase regions
dominate.

One can clearly see that a very large amount of reliable phase
equilibrium data was obtained from two Co-CoAl-Cr-Mo-Nb-Ni-Ta-W
diﬀusion multiples that were annealed at 900 °C and 800 °C, respec-
tively. It would take hundreds of equilibrated alloys to obtain similar
amount of data to establish the phase diagrams of four Co-Ni-X (X = W,
Mo, Nb, Ta) and ﬁve Co-Al-X (X = W, Mo, Nb, Ni, Ta) [56] ternary
systems. Despite its e ﬀectiveness, the di ﬀusion-multiple approach relies
on and is at the mercy of di ﬀusion kinetics. Occasionally phase equi-
librium information in certain regions of phase diagrams cannot be
obtained in situations such as: 1) missing phases which rarely occur at
high temperatures but sometimes at intermediate and low tempera-
tures, 2) phases that are too thin to be reliably measured by EPMA or
SEM, and 3) cracks and voids in the di ﬀusion multiple samples. For
instance, the phase boundary of the μ phase in all the four Co-Ni-X
systems was not well determined. In such a situation, it is highly de-
sirable to make a few alloys and use the equilibrated alloy method
(melting or powder metallurgy sintering of alloys followed by heat
treatments and analysis of phase equilibria) to obtain the missing phase
equilibrium information. Even though the equilibrated alloy method
has long been treated as the ultimate “gold-standard” in equilibrium
phase diagram determination, one needs to heat treat the alloys long
enough to ensure that they reach equilibrium conditions as well as grow
the equilibrium phases large enough to allow accurate EPMA mea-
surements or XRD phase identi ﬁcation or transmission electron micro-
scopy (TEM) characterization.
4. Summary
Two Co-CoAl-Cr-Mo-Nb-Ni-Ta-W di ﬀusion multiples were fabri-
cated and annealed at 900 °C and 800 °C for 1010 h and 2200 h, re-
spectively. EPMA line-scans across the phase interfaces in the Co-Ni-X
(X = W, Mo, Nb, Ta) tri-junctions in the di ﬀusion multiples provided
the local equilibrium information to extract the tie-lines. Both the
900 °C and 800 °C isothermal sections of the Co-Ni-W, Co-Ni-Mo, Co-Ni-
Nb, and Co-Ni-Ta ternary phase diagrams were established based on a
large amount of tie-lines obtained from just two di ﬀusion multiples. All
these isothermal sections of the four ternary systems are the ﬁrst such
phase diagrams experimentally established at both temperatures. The
data will be critical input to future CALPHAD modeling to improve the
TCP phase thermodynamic parameters in order to improve the ﬁdelity
of thermodynamic databases for Ni-based and Co-based superalloys.
Accurate predictions of the upper limits of refractory element additions
while avoiding the formation of the TCP phases can signi ﬁcantly reduce
the composition iterations during the design of future advanced su-
peralloys.
