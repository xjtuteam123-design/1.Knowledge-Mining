Abstract 
Alloy design of a Co -Ni-Al-W-Ta-Ti senary alloy was performed through a 
CALPHAD (CALculation of PHAse Diagrams) approach coupled with experimental 
investigation, with aim to attain high γ′ solvus temperature, microstructural stability, 
as well as low density . The results of the thermodynamic calculations indicated that 
increasing the Ni content would broaden the γ+γ′ two -phase region, meanwhile 
simultaneously increasing the Ni content and the Al/W ratio would lead to high γ′ 
solvus temperature and microstructura l stability. The experimental results indicated 
that a high Al/W ratio with a high Ni content of more than 20 at.% was beneficial not 
only for obtaining a high γ′ solvus temperature, but also for realizing high 
microstructural stability and low density in these Co -Al-W-base superalloys. 
Increasing the Ni content enhances the hardness of both the γ and γ′ phases, while 
increasing the Al/W ratio has an opposite effect. The optimized alloy, 
Co-30Ni-11Al-4W-1Ta-4Ti (at.%), demonstrated γ′ solvus temperature of above 
1200 °C, low density of 8.76 g/cm3, and excellent microstructural stability after aging 
at 1150 °C for 1000h. This study will be helpful for continuous efforts on the 
development of Co -Al-W-base superalloys for industrial application, as well as 
populating thermodynamic databases. 
 
Keywords 
Co-Al-W-base superalloys ; CAHPHAD; γ′ solvus temperature ; Al / W ratio ; 
Microstructural stability.

At present, the primary restrictions for the development of Co -Al-W-base 
superalloys are the lower γ′ solvus temperature ( Tγ′-solvus), weaker microstructural 
stability at high temperature, as well as higher alloy density in comparison with 
commercial Ni-base superalloys. Recent researches have focused on improving these 
shortcomings, and some of them suggest that Ni containing Co-Al-W-base alloys are 
suitable for further development due to their higher Tγ′-solvus and better microstructural 
stability than their Ni -free counterparts [5-7]. Recently, Lass [5] reported alloy 
Co-30Ni-10.5Al-4Ti-7W-2.5Ta, which possessed the highest Tγ′-solvus (1269 °C) 
among the Co -base alloys in the existing literature . However, its relatively high W 
and Ta contents would result in a high alloy density, limiting its engineering 
application to some extent. Therefore, substituting W with lighter elements, such as Al, 
without reducing Tγ′-solvus is desirable; however, knowledge on the effect of the Al / W 
ratio on the high temperature capability of Co -Al-W-base superalloys is still limited. 
In the current research, the Al / W ratio was increased while ma intaining a constant 
(Al + W) content, with a goal of reducing alloy density without significantly 
decreasing Tγ′-solvus. 
The conventional method of alloy design for Co-Al-W-base superalloys involves 
repeated compositional adjustment and experimental test ing, which is an inefficient 
way to design new alloys in comparison with a CALPHAD approach [8]. On the other 
hand, few studies on Co -Al-W-base alloys employ the CALPHAD method as the 
thermodynamic databases are still rather limited. In this study, alloy 
Co-7Al-8W-1Ta-4Ti [2] was selected as the base alloy to be further improved. A 
combination of CALPHAD and experimental investigation was used to design a 
Co-Ni-Al-W-Ta-Ti senary alloy, with high Tγ′-solvus and good microstructural stability, 
as well as low alloy density. The effects of the Ni content and Al / W ratio on the 
Tγ′-solvus and long -term microstructural stability at 1100 °C and 1150 °C were

2 Experimental 
In this study, the thermodynamic calculations for the Co-Ni-Al-W-Ta-Ti alloy 
system were performed using Pandat TM with the commercial Co-base thermodynamic 
database PanCo2018 (latest version at the time of manuscript preparation ). The 
experimental alloys were prepared by arc melting under argon atmosphere. Before 
heat treatments, the samples were sealed in quartz tubes, which were back -filled with 
Ar gas. Solution heat treatment was conducted at 1270 °C for 24 h, and long -term 
aging treatments was subsequently conducted at 1100 °C and 1150 °C for 1000 h, 
followed by water quenching. Alloy densities were experimentally measured using 
water displacement based on the Archimedes principle. The Tγ′-solvus was determined 
using NETZSCH S TA 449C differential scanning calorimeter (DSC) with a heating 
rate of 10 °C min -1 between 600 °C and 1300 °C. Microstructural observation was 
performed using a ZEISS SUPRA 55 field -emission scanning electron microscope 
(FE-SEM). Alloy and phase compositions were measured using a JEOL JXA -8230 
electron probe microanalyzer (EPMA). The volume fraction of γ′ precipitates was 
measured through the standard point count method. 
The samples aged at 1100 °C for 1000 h were selected as the bulk specimens for 
nanoindentation, as the microstructure consisted of coarsened γ' precipitates - 
enabling separate indentation of both the γ and γ' phases. The samples were first 
mechanically polished and then further polished with an electrolytic solution of 80% 
CH3OH and 20% H 2SO4 (by volume) at 30 V for 20 s, in order to ensure complete 
removal of any plastically deformed layers. Nanoindentation was performed with an 
Agilent Nano Indenter G200 using the continuous stiffness method (CSM) [9]. A 
strain rate and Poisson's ratio of 0.05 s -1 and 0.3, respectively , were used . The 
hardness of the γ and γ' phases was determined by analyzing the load -displacement 
curves using the Oliver-Pharr method [10].

3 Computational Design using CALPHAD Approach 
W is an essential γ′ stabilizing element in Co-Al-W-base alloys; however, high W 
content could lead to a higher alloy density, which restricts the applications where a 
good strength to weight ratio is crucial. Therefore, substituting W with light elements 
such as Al, to reduce alloy density without decreasing Tγ′-solvus, is pursued for further 
development of the base alloy, i.e., alloy Co-7Al-8W-1Ta-4Ti. 
In order to predict the influence of the nominal Ni concentration and the Al / W 
ratio on the Tγ′-solvus and phase equilibrium, isopleth sections of the 
Co-xAl-(15-x)W-1Ta-4Ti-yNi (at.%) systems were calculated for different Ni 
contents (1:1 substitution with Co), as shown in Figure 1. For each isopleth, the sum 
of Al and W contents was invariably 15 at.% in order to maintain the total 
concentration of γ′ formers constant; in other words, only varying the Al / W ratio. 
The right sides of the isopleths are the Al -rich regions, while the left sides are the 
W-rich regions. Figure 1 illustrates that adding Ni significantly increased th e area of 
the γ + γ′ two -phase region (shaded in yellow). Furthermore, the γ + γ′ region shifted 
towards the Al -rich side with increasing Ni content . Shinagawa et al. [6] reported 
similar observations in the experimentally measured isothermal sections of t he 
Co-xNi-Al-W systems. Therefore, with increasing Ni content, the Al / W ratio should 
also be increased to avoid the precipitation of μ and χ phases, which are detrimental 
W-rich phases. On the other hand, in the systems containing 0 or 10 at.% Ni, 
increasing the Al / W ratio causes a significant decrease in the Tγ′-solvus, as indicated by 
the bold green lines in Figure 1(a) - (b). To achieve the highest γ′ solvus temperature 
(Tγ′-solvus(MAX)) for these systems, the Al / W ratio should be reduced to extremely low 
values, as indicated in Table 1. However, low Al / W ratio would result in a 
significant increase in the alloy density. Fortunately, the trend is reversed when the Ni 
content is increased to 20 or 30 at.%. In these systems, the T γ′-solvus increased with 
increasing Al / W ratio (Figure 1(c) - (d)). As shown in Table 1, both the T γ′-solvus(MAX) 
and its corresponding Al / W ratio increased significantly with increasing Ni content. 
It should be noted that substituting Ni for Co should not appreciably affect the alloy 
density as the densities of the two elements are nearly the same. 
The above CALPHAD results suggest that the Ni content should be above 20 at.% 
and the Al / W ratio should be high. This adjustment for alloy composition is 
beneficial for enhancing the Tγ′-solvus and reducing the alloy density of the base alloy 
Co-7Al-8W-1Ta-4Ti. In order to validate the calculated results, four experimental

4 Results 
4.1 γ′ solvus temperature 
Figure 2(a) shows the DSC heating curves, where clear endothermic peaks 
corresponding to the γ′ solvus temperature (Tγ′-solvus) - taken as the temperature before 
the change in heat flow on the heating curve in this study - can be observed. Figure 
2(b) illustrates the T γ′-solvus of the experimental alloys, including the experimental 
results (black columns) and calculated results (grey columns). These two sets of 
results show a similar trend where the T γ′-solvus significantly increased with increasing 
Ni cont ent. With increasing Al / W ratio, the T γ′-solvus was slightly reduced in the 
alloys containing 20 at.% Ni, but increased in the alloys containing 30 at.% Ni. 
However, a reasonable deviation exists between the predicted and experimental 
results (maximum di fference of 33 °C). It should be noted that the temperature limit 
of the DSC apparatus was reached before complete melting, hence the solidus and 
liquidus temperatures are not reported, however, the measured solidus temperatures 
were close to the predicted ones (maximum difference of 16 ºC) . The experimental 
Tγ′-solvus of the present alloys are all above 1160 °C, and the highest one therein is 
above 1200 °C (alloy 30Ni -B), which is an appreciable increase over that of base 
alloy Co-7Al-8W-1Ta-4Ti (1131 °C) and most other typical Co -base superalloys 
(Table 3) [1, 4, 11 -16]. The significant increase of T γ′-solvus in these alloys is mainly 
due to the increase of the Ni content and Al / W ratio compared with the base alloy.

4.2 Microstructural stability 
Figure 3 shows the typical microstructures of the experimental alloys after aging 
at 1100 °C and 1150 °C for 1000 h. All alloys exhibited a γ + γ′ two -phase 
microstructure with no precipitation of other secondary phases, except for alloy 
20Ni-B, where an additional black contrast phase, identified as β phase, was found. At 
1100 °C, t he morphologies of the γ′ precipitates changed from nearly cubic to 
coalesced with increasing Al / W ratio (Figure 3(a1) - (a4)). With increasing aging 
temperature to 1150 °C, t he morphologies of γ′ precipitates in all alloys were 
distinctly changed. They were spherical in the low Al / W ratio alloys, while nearly 
cubic in the high Al / W ratio alloy (Figure 3 (b1), (b3) and (b4)). 
Increasing the Ni content from 20% to 30% didn’ t affect the morphologies of the 
γ′ precipitates obviously, however, it resulted in higher γ′ volume fractions (Table 4). 
At 1100 °C, except for alloy 20Ni -B, the γ′ volume fractions of the other three alloys 
were higher than 55% (experimental results). Wh en the aging temperature was 
increased to 1150 °C, the γ′ volume fractions of all alloys were distinctly reduced. 
However, it is noteworthy that the γ′ volume fraction in alloy 30Ni -B was nearly 50% 
after aging at 1150 °C for 1000 h and no other secondary phases were observed. 
Reasonable deviations were found between the calculated and experimental 
results for equilibrium phases, as well as γ′ volume fractions (Table 4). These are 
likely due to the majority of previous studies being focused on the microstructural 
stability under 1050 °C (Table 3), resulting in less data above 1100 °C for the 
thermodynamic database. Therefore, the experimental phase equilibriums at 1100 °C 
and 1150 °C in this study will help to make up this blank compositional space.

4.4 Hardness of γ′ and γ phases 
Figure 5(a) shows an inset SEM image of a 5 × 6 point -grid of nanoindents and 
typical load -displacement curves for indentation of the γ and γ′ phases in alloy 
30Ni-B. It should be noted that only points that lay completely within the γ or γ′ phase 
were con sidered. All load -displacement curves for both phases show pop -ins [21], 
which are caused by the transition from elastic to plastic deformation, indicating a 
proper sample preparation and test. Figure 5(b) shows the average hardness of the γ 
and γ′ phases in the experimental alloys after heat treatment at 1100 °C for 1000 h, in 
comparison with the data of a 1 st generation Ni -base single crystal superalloy, 
CMSX-6, under the same max -load condition with this study [22]. It should be noted 
that unlike single crystal alloys, the crystal orientation of the current experimental 
alloys is difficult to control as they are polycrystal line alloys. The hardness of the γ′ 
phase is higher than that of the γ phases in all experimental alloys, in agreement with 
the previous studies [7, 20, 22, 23] . It is clear that increasing Ni content led to higher 
hardness of both the γ and γ′ phases when maintaining a constant Al / W ratio, 
consistent with the results in reference [7]. Increasing the Al / W ratio led to a 
softening of both the γ and γ′ phases in the alloy systems containing either 20 at.% or 
30 at.% Ni content. Finally, the hardness of both phases in the experimental alloys are 
lower than those of Ni -base superalloy CMSX -6, suggesting that further alloy 
improvement is still needed.

5.2 Effects of Ni and Al / W ratio on microstructural stability 
5.2.1 Phase equilibrium 
Experimental data for the phase equilibrium above 1100 °C is very useful for the 
development of Co -Al-W-base superalloys and improvement of the thermodynamic 
database, however, these data are very limited so far (Table 3). In the present study, 
the higher Al / W ratio in alloy 20Ni -B compared to alloy 20Ni -A, resulted in the 
precipitation of deleterious β phase at 1100 °C ( Figure 3(a1) - (a2)). The 
stoichiometry of β phase is (Co, Ni)Al, and it is generally formed by Co or Ni atoms 
with the oversaturated Al atoms in γ matrix. The composition al results for the 
different phases indicate that the solubility limit of Al in the γ phase is nearly 10.9 at.% 
at 1100 °C (Table 5). Increasing the Ni content from 20 to 30 at.% likely increased 
the solubility limit of Al , hence avoiding the formation of β phase (Figure 3(a4)). In 
other words, the γ + γ′ two -phase region was expanded towa rds the Al-rich side with 
increasing the Ni content, which allows for higher Al / W ratios. This is in agreement 
with the calculated isopleths in Figure 1, illustrating the effectiveness of CALPHAD 
for guiding compositional optimization . On the other hand, the experimental phase 
equilibrium at 1150 °C of alloy 20Ni -B was γ + β, which was not consistent with the 
calculated result of γ + γ′ + β. This indicates that the calculated boundary of phase 
regions between γ + γ′ + β and γ′ + β should be closer to the W-rich side. This also 
shows the importance of quality experiments to populate databases for effective 
CALPHAD. 
 
5.2.2 γ′ volume fraction 
It is well -known that the γ′ volume fraction plays a critical role for the creep 
resistance of superalloys [17]. For Ni-base single crystal superalloys, the optimal 
volume fraction of γ′ phase is in the range of 65% to 70% [17]. Although the optimal 
value for Co -Al-W-base superalloys has not been reported yet, the available data 
suggest that alloys with a γ′ volume fract ion higher than 60% performed better 
mechanically, compared to those with lower γ′ volume fraction [3]. In the present 
study, the γ′ volume fraction in alloy 30Ni -B was nearly 65% after aging at 1100 °C

5.2.3 Morphology of γ′ precipitates 
The morphology of the γ′ precipitates has a close relationship with the lattice 
misfit between the γ and γ′ phases , δ, which significantly affects the creep resistance 
of single crystal superalloys [17]. The γ′ precipitates are typically spherical when δ is 
close to 0, while |δ| > 0 leads to the cuboidal morphology [27]. Thus, the morphology 
of γ′ precipitates provides some insight into the magnitude of δ, which is commonly 
positive in Co -Al-W-base superalloys. The δ depends on two factors: (i) the 
partitioning of a solute i between γ and γ′ and (ii) the influence of solute i on the 
lattice parameters based on its atomic radius [17]. 
Shinagawa et al. [6] found that the morphology of the γ′ precipitates transformed 
from cuboidal to spherical by increasing the Ni content from 0 to 60 at.% in the 
Co-xNi-10Al-7.5W system. The main reason for that change is the distinct variations 
of 𝐾𝛾′/𝛾

coherency strengthening effects. This weakening would facilitate the cutting of γ′ 
precipitates by channel di slocations and deteriorate the creep resistance [25]. 
Therefore, the Ni content should be carefully controlled in Co -Ni-Al-W-base alloys,

coefficient of the γ matrix over that of the γ′ phase [29].

and the δ value of the experimental alloys still need to be evaluated experimentally. 
 
5.3 Effects of Ni content and Al / W ratio on the hardness of the γ′ and γ phases 
Other than the γ′ phase volume fraction , another factor strongly influencing the 
creep resistance of superalloys is the strength of the γ′ precipitates, i.e., its resistance

against shearing. Nanoind entation of superalloys was first demonstrated by Göken et 
al. [22], and then widely utilized in other studies to measure the mechanical properties 
of the γ and γ′ phases [7, 20, 23, 30] . However, studies reporting nanoindentation 
results for novel Co -base superalloys are still limited. All previous studies 
demonstrate that the γ′ phase is undoubtedly harder than the γ phase in both Ni -base 
and Co-base superalloys. 
In line with the previous studies, the hardness of the γ′ phase in all the present 
alloys were higher than those of the γ phase (Figure 5(b)). This is because γ′ phase is 
an ordered phase, which is generally more difficult to deform than the disordered γ 
phase, due to the formation of anti -phase boundaries . Another reason is the 
enrichment of solid solution elements such as W, Ti and Ta in the γ′ phase (Figure 4), 
as these elements can cause significant strengthening [20, 31]. Increasing the Al / W 
ratio obviously softened both the γ and γ′ phases by distinctly decreasing the contents 
of solid solution elements in both phases (Table 5). For example, the sum contents of 
W, Ti and Ta of the γ′ phase in alloy 20Ni-A and 30Ni-A are 11.6 at.% and 11.3 at.%, 
respectively, obviously higher than that in alloy 20Ni -B and 30Ni -B, which are 10.3 
at.% and 9.6 at.%, respectively. A similar case can be observed for the γ phase. The 
fact that the hardness of the γ′ phase increased with increasing Ni content is somewhat 
surprising as the first-principles studies estimate that Co3(Al, W) has a higher intrinsic 
strength than Ni3Al [32]. Further work is needed to evaluate the exact reason for this, 
as Ni additions influence many parameters, such as the stacking fault energy [17] and 
the anti-phase boundary energy [33]. 
Although a good T γ′-solvus, microstructural stability and alloy density were 
obtained for alloy 30Ni -B, the increase of the Al / W ratio resulted in softer γ and γ′ 
phases compared with alloys 20Ni-A and 30Ni-A. This could result in a weaker alloy 
strength, and shows the importance of carefully balancing multiple properties during 
alloying design. On the other hand, the oxidation resistance of the current 
experimental alloys may not be good due to the absence of environmental resistance 
elements. Cr is a known element to enhance the oxidation resistance by improving 
stabilizing a passive oxide layer, however , such additions also promote the formation 
of undesirable secondary phases [7]. Therefore, our future work of alloy development 
will be focus on the optimization of Cr-containing alloy systems.

6 Conclusions 
Based on the results and observations presented in this study, the following 
conclusions can be drawn: 
1. A combined CALPHAD and experimental approach was used to design a senary 
alloy Co -30Ni-11Al-4W-1Ta-4Ti with high T γ′-solvus (1202 °C), excellent γ + γ′ 
microstructural stability at 1100 - 1150 °C, as well as low density (8.74 g cm-3). 
2. The Al / W ratio was identified as a key optimization factor in the Ni -containing 
Co-Al-W-base alloy system, as it not only affects the T γ′-solvus and alloy density, 
but also γ′ morphology, and the hardness of γ and γ′ phases. 
3. In the alloy system with low Ni content, increasing the Al / W ratio resulted in a 
decrease of the Tγ′-solvus due to the precipitation of β phase, while simultaneously 
increasing the Ni content and the Al / W ratio led to high Tγ′-solvus and 
microstructural stability.
