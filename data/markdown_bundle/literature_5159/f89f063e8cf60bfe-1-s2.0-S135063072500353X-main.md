ABSTRACT
The performance and durability of modern high-performance aero engines depend on the service 
life of hot-end turbine blades, which often dictate the engine ’ s overall lifespan. Ni-based single 
crystal superalloys are preferred materials for these components due to their superior fatigue, 
creep, oxidation and corrosion resistance. However, surface defects that occur during operation 
compromise their mechanical properties and reliability. Therefore, effective repair techniques are 
essential for restoring these defects and improving service performance. Although various repair 
methods have been developed, those capable of effectively addressing surface defects in Ni-based 
single crystal superalloys without damaging material properties remain limited. This review ex -
amines the potential of high-energy beam repair technologies as an innovative solution for sur -
face defect restoration. We assess the existing repair techniques, identify their limitations and 
demonstrate how high-energy beams can restore material integrity while minimizing thermal 
damage. Key factors such as beam parameters, surface quality and defect recovery efficiency are 
also discussed. High-energy beam repair methods offer a promising alternative to conventional 
techniques, significantly enhancing the longevity and performance of repaired components. This 
review contributes to the optimization of repair strategies, improving the reliability and service 
life of turbine blades and other critical aero-engine components. Furthermore, it provides insights 
into the future potential of high-energy beams in advancing material repair technologies, 
particularly in the aerospace industry.

The main damages generated by Ni-based single crystal superalloy turbine blades after service include pitting, porosity and cracks,

when gases cannot be expelled or the liquid phase does not receive effective compensation, cast porosity is formed. During the solution 
treatment process, solution porosity is produced. The formation of solution voids is mainly related to the diffusion imbalance during

single crystal superalloy turbine blades also develop a fatal damage cracks, during service. Cracks in the Ni-based single crystal 
superalloy can initiate as numerous fine micro-cracks in areas of high surface stress concentration, and these micro-cracks can continue 
to expand, eventually forming larger cracks [ 78 ]. In addition, the propagation direction of thermal fatigue cracks is at a 45

volume fraction of the γ’ phase near the oxide layer, which in turn decreases the strength of the alloy around the pores, leading to crack

Zhao Y et al . [ 81 – 83 ] conducted research classifying the failure of turbine blades into categories such as fatigue failure, creep 
failure, overheating failure, foreign object damage failure and thermal corrosion failure. He also believes that overheating failure has 
become one of the main modes of blade failure. The main cause of this type of failure is over-burning, which leads to fracturing under 
the influence of external forces after the material has begun to melt. For wear and corrosion damage on Ni-based single crystal turbine 
blades that cover a small area, repairs are generally completed through brazing or similar processes. For cracks that occur, laser or 
electron beam techniques are typically used to repair through additive manufacturing methods. As for broken turbine blades, due to 
the higher safety risks associated with repair, it is common practice to replace the blades directly.

3.1. Laser metal deposition technology
LMD technology [ 112 – 116 ] is a typical manufacturing process that organically combines the principles of layered superposition 
manufacturing and laser cladding, also known as Laser Directed Energy Deposition [ 92 ]. The principle is shown in Fig. 5 . Firstly, Ni- 
based alloy powder is conveyed to the laser action area by a powder feeding device. A high-energy – density laser beam is focused on the 
repair area of the blade, causing the powder to melt instantly and form a metallurgical bond with the surface of the base metal. As the 
laser beam and powder feeding continue, the molten pool deposits layer by layer, gradually filling in the damaged part of the blade, 
ultimately achieving the repair of the blade [ 117 ]. The development of LMD technology originated in the 1990s when it was first 
proposed by the Sandia National Laboratories in the United States and subsequently it has been developed in many parts of the world. 
Additionally, LMD technology includes various forms such as Laser Powder Deposition [ 118 – 120 ] and Direct Metal Laser Deposition 
[ 121 ].
The repair area of LMD technology, due to the rapid cooling rate, has a significantly smaller primary dendrite spacing in the 
deposition area compared to the substrate area. At the same time, the carbides in the deposited sample are also smaller, and the 
formation of ( γ + γ’ ) eutectic in the initial layer of the repaired single crystal can be suppressed during the repair process, which can 
reduce metallurgical defects. This is crucial for maintaining the single crystal nature and comprehensive performance of the material. 
Therefore, LMD technology is used to repair surface micro-cracks and other damages in Ni-based single crystal superalloy turbine 
blades. Cardoso JASB et al. [ 123 ] conducted laser deposition experiments of the Rene N4 superalloy on the Rene N5 Ni-based single 
crystal superalloy components and found that the deposited material continued the single crystal structure and orientation of the 
original crystal. The chemical composition and microstructure of the deposited Rene N4 alloy also remained unchanged across all 
deposition layers, indicating that it is feasible even on a heterogeneous substrate. Finally, the microstructure of the deposited material 
can be optimized through proper heat treatment to meet the performance requirements at high temperatures. Guo Y et al . [ 96 ] 
conducted the repair of DD32 Ni-based single crystal superalloy via laser-directed energy deposition technology and a newly 
customized heat treatment process was implemented on the repaired superalloy. It was found that the high-temperature stress rupture 
life of the standard heat-treated specimen lasted only 8 h, while the lifespan of the specimen treated with the customized process 
reached 19.09 h.
3.2. Laser cladding
Laser cladding [ 124 , 125 ] involves placing the selected Ni-based alloy coating material on the surface of the turbine blade that 
requires repair, either in the form of pre-placed powder or synchronous powder feeding, and then irradiating it with a high-ener -
gy – density laser beam. The energy from the laser melts a thin layer of the coating material and the surface of the blade substrate 
simultaneously, which then rapidly solidifies to form a surface coating that is metallurgically bonded to the substrate, thereby 
achieving the purpose of repairing the blade [ 126 – 129 ]. Based on the different powder feeding methods, laser cladding technology can 
be divided into coaxial powder systems and pre-placed powder systems. During the repair process, laser cladding not only allows for 
precise control of the microstructure and properties of the cladding layer, enhancing the corrosion resistance, wear resistance and 
hardness of the blades, but also maintains the integrity of the single crystal structure, preventing the formation of polycrystalline grains 
[ 130 – 132 ], ensuring the reliability of the blades under high temperature and high-pressure conditions. By optimizing process

3.3. Electron beam powder bed fusion
Electron beam powder bed fusion technology involves an electron beam gun emitting a high-energy electron beam in a vacuum 
environment. The electron beam is focused on a powder bed covered with Ni-based alloy powder, where the energy of the electrons is 
converted into heat, causing the powder to melt rapidly. As the electron beam moves along a pre-set path on the powder bed, the 
molten pool also moves, solidifying into a dense metal structure after cooling. By melting and solidifying the powder layer by layer, the 
repaired turbine blade or the damaged part of the blade is ultimately manufactured [ 138 – 143 ]. The EB-PBF technology was first 
introduced by the Swedish company Arcam AB in 2002. Tsinghua University in China began the development of its own independent 
technology and equipment in 2004 and launched a commercial product, QBeamLab, in 2018. This development made China the 
second country capable of independently developing this technology. This technology uses metal powder with an average particle size 
of 30 – 100 μ m as raw material for part layer-by-layer formation. Compared to metal deposition and other wire materials as raw 
materials, the powder bed fusion technology can form more delicate internal structures. Additionally, since the electron beam has a 
high energy absorption rate for different materials, scanning the powder bed with an electron beam can preheat it up to 1000 
◦
C, which 
can greatly reduce the thermal stress during the melting process. Therefore, EB-PBF technology has a high energy utilization rate and 
forming efficiency.

4.1. Effect of process parameters on microstructure during laser repair
During the laser repair process of Ni-based single crystal superalloy turbine blades, different process parameters, such as scanning 
path, laser power, scanning speed, and heat treatment, significantly affect the microstructure and properties [ 153 ]. For instance, the 
microstructure of the epitaxial growth layer begins at the bottom with continuous columnar dendrites, and as the dendrites grow to a 
certain height, they transition into equiaxed crystals. The columnar crystal structure is very stable, and even after post-heat treatment, 
the coaxial crystal structure may coarsen, leading to a reduction in hardness, but subsequent aging treatment can increase hardness. 
Additionally, laser power and scanning speed also have a significant impact on the single-pass morphology, crystal orientation and 
microstructure [ 154 ]. At the appropriate laser power and scanning speed, the melt track is continuous and smooth, the melt pool is flat 
and regular, which is conducive to the stable growth of single crystal organizations. Crystals within the melt pool can continue the 
orientation of the substrate and grow epitaxially along the < 001 > direction. However, convection within the melt pool may cause 
small angular deviations in the crystal growth direction, leading to a transition from columnar to equiaxed crystals at the top of the 
melt pool, resulting in polycrystalline defects.
Laser power greatly impacts the microstructure and mechanical properties of repaired Ni-based single crystal superalloys. Xu J et al . 
[ 153 ] conducted research on the effects of process parameters during laser metal deposition on the microstructure and hardness of 
repaired Ni-based single crystal superalloys made from CSU-B2 powder. They found that as laser power increased, the height and width

4.2. Effect of process parameters on microstructure during electron beam repair

weld penetration and depth, reduces welding stress and deformation and improves the mechanical properties and high-temperature 
performance of the repair. This approach enhances the reliability and service life of the repaired turbine blades.

choosing process parameters wisely. During high energy beam repair of Ni-based single crystal superalloys, compositional

compositional segregation.

However, the ( γ + γ’ ) eutectic and porosity can degrade the high-temperature performance of the Ni-based single crystal superalloys, 
leading to a decline in performance at elevated temperatures. Samples that have undergone solution treatment and aging exhibit 
higher hardness due to the precipitation strengthening of the phase, compared to those treated only with solution treatment. Laser 
scanning speed and power affect the microstructure and mechanical properties of Ni-based single crystal superalloys, while heat 
treatment can reduce the hardness and tensile strength of the samples. The presence of a series of closely spaced or clustered voids 
along the columnar grain boundaries results in better ductility and strength of the samples along the deposition direction compared to 
the perpendicular direction. Due to a series of closely spaced or clustered voids along the boundaries of columnar grains, the ductility 
and strength of the samples along the deposition direction are superior to those perpendicular to the deposition direction.

1. Maintenance and control of single crystal structure: during the high-energy beam repair process, it is necessary to control the

2. Precise control and optimization of process parameters: In the repair process of turbine blades, the accurate adjustment of key

the single crystal structure but also be capable of restoring or optimizing the microstructure, such as the distribution and size of the
