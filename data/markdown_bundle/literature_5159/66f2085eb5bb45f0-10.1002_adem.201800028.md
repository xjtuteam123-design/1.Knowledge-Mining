Room-Temperature Mechanical Properties of
V20Nb20Mo20Ta20W20 High-Entropy Alloy
Shao Jie Wu, Xiao Di Wang, Jin Tao Lu, Rui Tao Qu,* and Zhe Feng Zhang*
Refractory high-entropy alloys (HEAs) have shown promising high tempera-
ture strengths, while their mechanical behaviors at room temperature are
rarely reported. In this work, the room-temperature mechanical properties of
V
20Nb20Mo20Ta20W20 refractory HEA under various different loading modes
including tension, compression, bending, shear loading, and microhardness
are investigated. The results show that this alloy exhibits very high
compressive strength but quite low strengths under tension, bending, and
shear loading, similar to the conventional brittle materials. However,
pronounced plasticity and slip bands are observed in compression samples,
and no indentation cracking is observed in low-load microhardness tests,
which indicate the potential ability of plastic deformation in this refractory
HEA. The present work suggests that the microstructure or composition of
this HEA should be carefully tailored before its practical usage to suppress
its large tendency for cracking and eventually improve its ductility and
strength under tension.
1. Introduction
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) have many potential applications
due to their promising mechanical properties such as high
hardness, superior strength and excellent wear resistance.
[1–6]
Especially for refractory HEAs, which are mainly composed of
refractory elements such as Cr, Hf, Mo, T a, V , W , and Zr with
body-centered cubic (BCC) major phase, they have been
considered as promising structural materials in aerospace
industry due to their attractive mechanical properties at elevated
temperatures.
[7–17] Because the diffusion
rate and resistance to thermal softening of
metallic materials are directly related to the
melting point of the material, it can be
expected that the refractory HEAs should
have a great combination of elevated
temperature mechanical properties. It is
reported that the high speci ﬁc yield
strengths of the NbMoTaW and VNbMo-
T aW HEA can be sustained over to 1800 K,
which are even better than some super-
alloys such as Inconel 718 and Haynes
230.
[11]
In the as-solidi ﬁed condition, the refrac-
tory HEAs usually have dendritic, equiaxed
grain microstructure consisting entirely of
single-phase BCC crystal structure with
lattice parameters and alloy densities equal
to the average of the same parameters in
the elemental constituents.
[7,11] The unique
microstructures render the refractory
HEAs superior strength and resistance to
thermal softening.
[9–11,18–20] However, in order to apply
refractory HEAs to engineering industries, some issues are
still required to be further investigated. Firstly, refractory HEAs
usually contain heavier elements and thus show high densities;
hence the lightweight design of refractory HEAs is quite
urgent.
[21,22] Secondly, the mechanical properties of refractory
HEAs at room temperature are de ﬁnitely of great signi ﬁcant.[23]
Although the strength and creep resistance of the refractory
HEAs are excellent at high temperature, the research results
about the mechanical properties and deformation behaviors at
room temperature are very rare. In this work, a series of
mechanical tests with different loading modes (including
tension, compression, bending, shear loading, and microhard-
ness) were conducted to systematically investigate the room-
temperature mechanical properties and behaviors of
V
20Nb20Mo20Ta20W20 refractory HEA. The room-temperature
deformation and fracture mechanisms were also carefully
studied by observing the deformation and fracture morphologies
and conducting an interrupted compression experiments.
2. Experimental Section
A refractory V 20Nb20Mo20Ta20W20 HEA with excellent elevated
temperature properties[11] was selected for the experiments. This
HEA was prepared by vacuum arc melting of equimolar
mixtures of the corresponding elements. The alloy was prepared
in the form of button of /C25 6 mm thick and /C25 30 mm in diameter.

3. Results
3.1. Microstructure Characterization of
V20Nb20Mo20Ta20W20 HEA
The microstructure features of V 20Nb20Mo20Ta20W20 HEA are
shown in Figure 1. The SEM backscatter electron image reveals a
dendritic structure in this HEA (Figure 1a), in good accordance
with the previous study.
[11] As shown in Figure 1d, the four major
diffraction peaks on this X-ray pattern have been identi ﬁed as
single BCC phase rather than dual BCC phase. Furthermore, the
typical inverse pole ﬁgure map of this HEA (Figure 1b) and the
maximum pole density of 1.725 (Figure 1c), suggest that the
HEA consists mainly of equiaxed grains. Figure 1e and f display
the distributions of misorientation angle and grain size,
respectively. The grain size distributes in a narrow range,
indicating that a homogeneous and coarse-grain microstructure
was obtained. The EDS element maps of the ﬁve principal
elements reveal good chemical homogeneity ( Figure 2 ), which
implies that the segregation between dendrites is not obvious.
The above results indicate the prepared refractory HEA exhibits a
relatively homogeneous microstructure with equiaxed and
coarse grains, as well as a high fraction of low-angle grain
boundaries (Figure 1e), which may be critical for the mechanical
properties.

3.4. Deformation Evolution Under
Compression
Even though this HEA exhibits such low
strengths under tension, bending and shear, it
still exhibits high compressive fracture
strength and prominent plasticity. Why does
this HEA show such excellent compressive
mechanical properties? To explore the answers
for this question, we conducted an interrupted
compression experiments. Figure 8 presents
the engineering stress –strain curves under
compression at different stages of deforma-
tion, and the corresponding observations on
the deformation and cracking features are
shown in Figure 8. Figure 1b and f have
already suggested that this HEA is composed
entirely of equiaxed coarse grain microstruc-
ture so that the slip bands should be formed
easily. Slip bands were thus observed after the
unloading at the strain ( e) of 0.79%, as shown
in Figure 9 a–c. In two samples that were
unloaded at the larger strains of 1.04% and
2.57%, splitting cracks appeared on the
surface, as shown in Figure 9d –e. When the
compressive strain increases to 9.38%, much
more splitting cracks were observed, as shown
Figure 9f –g.
By comparing the cracking emergences of
compressive samples at four different strains,
we can see that not only much more cracks
appear, but the cracks also become longer and
wider with increasing the strain. Therefore, it
can be concluded that the propagation of crack
is gradual rather than rapid, suggesting that
the crack propagation is suppressed under
compression compared with that under ten-
sion. Besides the emergences of cracks, it can
also be found that more and more pile-ups were generated with
increasing strain, as shown Figure 9d –f. Observing the pile-ups
carefully, we found evident slip bands on the surfaces of pile-ups
(Figure 9h). The results indicate that plasticity of the
V
20Nb20Mo20Ta20W20 HEA is dominated by slip bands although
the HEA is relatively brittle. Accordingly, it can be deduced that
the high fracture strength and appreciable plasticity of
V
20Nb20Mo20Ta20W20 HEA under compression is mainly caused
by the combination of suppression of cracking propagation and
promotion of slip deformation.

Cleavage is the main fracture mode in the V 20Nb20Mo20-
Ta20W20 HEA and thus the fracture strengths under tension,
bending and shear are relatively low. However, this HEA still
exhibits high compressive fracture strength and prominent
plasticity. It should be worth noting that although the failure in
tension and compression is both dominated by cleavage fracture,
the detailed fracture mechanism is different between tension
and compression. Figure 6 suggests that the intra-dendritic
cracking and inter-dendritic cracking are the main fracture
features under compression and tension, respectively. Usually,
the mechanical strength of a brittle materials is sensitive to
tensile stress. When a tensile stress is applied perpendicular to a
deep, thin crack, the stress at the tip of the crack is greatly
magniﬁed above the applied stress.
[26,32] Simultaneously, the
inter-dendritic structure is weak under tensile stress. Therefore,
the inter-dendritic crack is easier to open and thus propagate
rapidly under tension so that the tensile strength is low. By
contrast, the intra-dendritic cracking occurs under compression
instead of inter-dendritic cracking due to the absence of tensile
stress. Moreover, compressive stress makes the crack to be
closed, and thus the intra-dendritic crack propagation under
compression is suppressed, which can be con ﬁrmed as Figure 9.
The retarded crack propagation under compression renders this
HEA high compressive fracture strength. Meanwhile, such high
stress before fracture could motivate the dislocation and form
slip bands, as shown in Figure 9b, g, and h. Hence, the plasticity
under compression is appreciable.
Nevertheless, although the as-solidi ﬁed V
20Nb20Mo20Ta20W20
HEA exhibits superior strength and plasticity under compres-
sion, this microstructure or composition of HEA should be
carefully tailored to improve its ductility and strength under
tension before its practical usage. T o suppress the crack
propagation under tension is the key to enhance its ductility
and plasticity, and thus according to the process of strengthening
and toughening in metals and ceramics,
[26] the addition of ﬁbers
or phases and the grain re ﬁnement may be the practical ways
which could render the propagation of a crack more dif ﬁcult and
thus improve its tensile plasticity and strength.
5. Conclusions
In summary, the overall mechanical properties of the
V
20Nb20Mo20Ta20W20 HEA at room temperature were studied
by performing tension, compression, bending, shear, and
microhardness experiments. It was found that the V
20Nb20Mo20-
Ta20W20 HEA exhibits quite high compressive strength versus
very low tensile strength. The large strength difference can also
be con ﬁrmed by the low fracture strengths under bending and
shear. Through observing the fracture features, it can be found
