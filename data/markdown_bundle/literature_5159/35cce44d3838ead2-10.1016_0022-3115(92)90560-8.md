1. Introduction 
Recently, attention has been paid to Fe-Cr-Mn 
austenitic steels as candidate materials for the first wall 
of a fusion reactor due to their low-radtoactwity char- 
acteristics. Much effort is underway to explore the 
neutron irradiation damage [1]. Meanwhile, before ir- 
radiation the structural material should posses good 
microstructurai stability and high temperature proper- 
ties prior to neutron irradiation. 
In our former work, the microstructural behavior 
and sigma-phase formation in solution treated Fe-Cr- 
Mn alloys over a wide compositional range were inves- 
tigated and a preferable material composition of Fe- 
12%Cr-15%Mn was proposed as a candidate base 
metal [2]. Carbon and nitrogen are one of the most 
important alloying elements, because they stabilize the 
austenite phase and improve strength through solid 
solution and precipitation strengthening. The vahdity 
of these alloying elements on phase stability, preven- 
tion of sigma-phase formation and high temperature 
strength improvement has previously been reported

2. Experimental procedure 
The chemical composition of the material tested is 
given in table 1. The alloying elements were varied 
from 0.1-0.2%C, 0.1-0.2%N and 0-3.2%W and the 
effect of sulphur on hot cracking susceptibility was also 
investigated. The material CI and W1-W4 were used 
for mechanical testing and D1-D7, DW1 and DW2 
were used for welding tests. Steel ingots 10-15 kg in 
weight were prepared by vacuum melting in a high- 
frequency induction furnace. The steels were homoge- 
razed at 1423-1473 K for 3.6 ks, subsequently, the 
ingot was hot-rolled to 15 mm thickness and after that 
solution treated at 1373 K for 3.6 ks. A round-bar 
tensile specimen of diameter 6 mm and gauge length of 
30 mm parallel to the rolling direction were prepared 
for high temperature tests. The tensile and creep test- 
ing were carried out at 873 K at a strain rate of 10 -4 
s -I, and 873 K at an applied stress of 216 MPa 
respectwely The toughness of the material was evalu- 
ated by hal~-size (5 × 10 × 55 ram) V-notch specimens. 
The notch was aligned m the transverse direction and 
specimens were aged at 773, 873 K and 973 K for 3600 
ks. Instrumented Charpy impact tests were conducted 
at a test temperature of 273 K, and part of the heat 
treated materials were tested to obtain a ductile-brittle 
transition curve from room temperature to 77 K. 
The wcldability of each alloy was determined using 
the Varestraint test. The sample dimensions were 320 
x 40 × 3 or 4 mm. A welding current of 80 A and 
travel speed of 8 cm/min were used. An augmented 
strain of 2.4-5% was employed. The test specimen 
dtmensions for the weld mechanical properties were 
400 × 90 × 12 mm. The welds were done using high 
frequency pulsed TIG. The welding conditmns were: 
welding current of 400 A; base current of 210 A; pulse 
frequency of 15 kHz and travel speed of 11)0 mm/mm. 
3. Results and discussion 
3.1. High temperature strength 
F~g. 1 shows the effect of carbon, nitrogen and 
tungsten on the high temperature tensile properties of 
aged and solution treated alloys. An increase of 0.2% 
proof strength and ultimate tensile strength on the 
three alloys containing tungsten comparable to the 
0.2%C, 0.1%N alloy is observed while there is no 
remarkable change on elongation. On the other side, 
the alloy containing 0.2%C, 0.2%N and 2%W compar- 
atively shows a remarkable increase of strength. This is 
possibly due to the effect of nitrogen on solution 
hardening. It is observed that there is no significant 
difference in high temperature tensile properties be- 
tween the aged and solution treated conditions. 
Table 2 shows the effect of tungsten on the creep 
behavior. The time to rupture increases with increasing 
tungsten concentration while the steady-state creep 
rate decreases. It was also observed that with the 
increase of tungsten content, creep-ductility decreases 
slightly.

4. Conclusion 
The effect of alloying elements of tungsten, carbon 
and nitrogen to the base metal (Fe-12%Cr-15%Mn) 
on high temperature strength, toughness and weldabfl- 
ity was investigated. The experimental results are sum- 
marized as follows. 
(1) In the case of Fe-12%Cr-15%Mn-0.2%C- 
0.1%N, the high temperature strength at 873 K in- 
creases with the addition of 2-3%W without affecting 
ductility. 
(2) Tungsten additions improve creep strength with- 
out affecting creep ductility. 
(3) Tungsten additions do not affect toughness, while 
carbon affects toughness. The alloy with 0.2%C-0.2%N 
shows a minimum absorbed energy value. However, the 
transition temperature of the alloy studied Is lower 
than room temperature after aging at 873 K for 3600 
ks. 
(4) The degradation of toughness after aging may be 
related to the precipitation of M23C 6 on gram bound- 
aries. 
(5) Hot cracking susceptibility of the weld metal is 
reduced by lowering the sulphur content. Tungsten- 
containing welds are more registant to hot cracking 
than commercial austenitic stainless steel AISI 316L.
