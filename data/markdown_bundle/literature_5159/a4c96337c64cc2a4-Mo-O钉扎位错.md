ABSTRACT 
To investigate the interstitial-substitutional interaction in dislocations, the 
effect of 0 on the temperature, frequency and amplitude dependence of the 
internal friction Q-' in Nb-0, Nb-20mol% Ta-0 and Nb-20mol% Mo-0 
single crystals has been studied (f = 1.55-8.2 Hz) in the temperature range from 
298 to 1473 K. In our previous study, 
Nb-Mo 
and Nb-Ta single crystals were 
found to be strengthened by solute 
0. 
It was also suggested that the 
interstitial- 
substitutional interaction in dislocations contributes to the increase in their 
critical resolved shear stress (CRSS). In this study, Snoek-type relaxation peaks 
due to 0 are observed between 500 and 700 K in all the single crystals. The Snoek 
peak of 
Nb-20mol% 
Mo-0 
consisting of several peaks is analysed. The 
activation energy of the Snoek peak in 
Nb-20mol% 
Mo-0 
is higher than that 
of 
Nb-0. 
These results are attributable to the existence of the interstitial- 
substitutional complexes. The amplitude dependence of Q-' at intermediate 
and high temperatures decreases as the 0 content increases. Moreover, the 
breakaway stress of 
Nb-20mol% 
Mo-0 
still has a high value at 
1200K 
and 
does not decrease much even at 1473 K. This suggests that the formation of 
Mo-0 
complexes reduces the dislocation mobility at high temperatures. From 
the results, the effect of the interstitial-substitutional interaction on the CRSS was 
discussed.

5 1. INTRODUCTION 
Bcc 
metals and alloys are highly reactive with interstitials such as 
0, 
C and N 
and are strengthened as a result (Sheely 1962, Lavi and Gibala 1970, English 1984). 
In addition, it is known that the interstitial-substitutional (i-s) interaction affects the 
strength of the alloys (Monma et al. 1965, Monma and Sudo 1966, Motoyoshi et al. 
1993, Miura 1999, Miura et al. 1999). For instance, several studies on the creep of 
Fe-Mo and Fe-W alloys revealed that they are strengthened by the addition of 
N, and thus it is attributed to the fact that the interstitial-substitutional interaction 
affects the dislocation mobility or climb in the alloys (Motoyoshi et al. 1993). 
In our previous studies (Miura 1999, Miura et al. 1999, 2001, 2002), Nb-Ta and 
Nb-Mo single crystals were found to exhibit remarkable solid-solution strengthening 
on the addition of 0 in a wide temperature range. Their temperature dependences 
of yield stress can be divided into three (low, intermediate and high) ranges. The 
temperatures at which the 0 solid-solution strengthening occurs are between room 
temperature and 1100 K (low to intermediate temperatures) for the Nb-Ta single 
crystal, and up to 1473 K (low to high temperatures) for the Nb-Mo single crystal. 
0 solid-solution strengthening in Nb and Nb-Ta alloy increases with decreasing 
temperature, and the effect of 0 addition in Nb-Mo alloy is the strongest at inter- 
mediate temperatures (500 K-1000 K). In addition, it was found that the increment in 
the critical resolved shear stress (CRSS) due to 0 addition in Nb-Ta alloy is larger 
than that in Nb. Therefore, the 0 solid-solution strengthening in Nb-Ta and Nb-Mo 
alloys is strongly connected with the substitutional elements, namely Ta or Mo. 
From transmission electron microscopy (TEM) observation of Nb-Mo single 
crystals compressed to approximately 4% strain (Miura et al. 2002), it was suggested 
that the 0 solute atoms affect the deformation microstructures. At 298 K, screw 
dislocations are predominant in all alloys. At 973 K (in the intermediate temperature 
range), in Nb-20 
mol% 
Mo and Nb-40 
mol% 
Mo single crystals with a low 0 con- 
tent, the dislocation microstructure contains the same amount of edge dislocations 
and small edge dipoles that tends to aggregate into clusters. In Nb-20 
mol% 
Mo with 
a high 0 content, however, the arrangement consists of long glide loops parallel to 
the screw direction. At 1473 K (in the high temperature range), edge dislocations are 
dominant in all alloys. It was also suggested that 0 solute atoms impede screw 
dislocation motion in a Nb-Ta single crystal deformed at room and intermediate 
temperatures (Miura 1999). 
Furthermore, the effect of 0 addition on the orientation dependence of the CRSS 
of Nb-Ta deformed at 298K and Nb-Mo single crystals at 298 and 973 K was 
investigated (Miura et al. 1999). From the result of the TEM observation and the 
orientation dependence, the following conclusions are drawn. 
(i) 0 atoms impede screw dislocation motion at low and intermediate tempera- 
tures, and edge dislocation motion at high temperatures. 
(ii) 0 atoms take part in the anisotropy change in the core of screw dislocations. 
(iii) In the Nb-Mo alloy, the influence of 0 atoms on the screw segment is 
significantly strong; thus striking effects of 0 on mechanical properties 
were observed at intermediate temperatures. 
(iv) In addition, in the Nb-Mo alloy, 0 atoms impede edge dislocation motion 
even at high temperatures. 
These conclusions implied that an 
0-M 
(M = Mo or Ta) atmosphere is formed 
around dislocations during deformation. That is, the interstitial-substitutional

It has been ascertained that the interstitial-substitutional interactions affects 
diffusivity and/or thermodynamic activity of interstitial atoms through studies of 
Nb-0

et al. 1995). The mechanism of the interstitial-substitutional interaction in refractory 
metal-based alloys has been studied using internal-friction measurement (Szkopiak 
and Smith 1975, Klam et al. 1979, Blanter and Fradkov 1987, Carlson et al. 1987, 
Indrawirawan et al. 1987, Kushnareva et al. 1993, Numakura et al. 1995) or a solid 
electrolytic cell (Lauf and Altstetter 1979, Albert et al. 1983). 
Internal-friction measurements have been widely recognized as a very useful 
technique to detect many physical properties. Various types of information can be 
obtained by changing the parameters, for example, the shear modulus, the diffusivity 
of point defects and the short-range dislocation mobility. In general, a Snoek-type 
relaxation peak in 
bcc 
metals appeared owing to the presence of interstitial solute 
atoms, and 0 in Nb is observed as a Snoek peak (Nowich and Berry 1972). 
In addition, it has been reported that the Snoek peak is markedly affected by the 
substitutional solute atoms (Kushnareva et al. 1993, Numakura and Koiwa 1996). 
This effect on the internal friction originates from the interstitial-substitutional 
interaction. The relaxation strength and the activation energy of the peak give 
some information about the properties of 0 in Nb alloys. In addition, the amplitude 
dependence of the internal friction is related to short-range dislocation motion in 
metals. Therefore, this method is very effective for investigating the influence of the 
interstitial-substitutional effect on mechanical properties. 
This study has been performed in order to clarify the effect of 0 addition on the 
diffusivity and dislocation mobility in Nb-20 
mol% 
Ta and Nb-20 
mol% Mo single 
crystals. In this study, the anomalous behaviour of 0 atoms interacting with substi- 
tutional atoms (Ta or Mo) in Nb-based alloys was investigated using single crystals 
through internal-friction measurements. The temperature, frequency and amplitude 
dependences of the internal friction were examined. The obtained results will be 
discussed in terms of the interstitial-substitutional (interaction) effect on the defor- 
mation behaviour of Nb-based alloys.

4.5. Cause of the interstitial-substitutional interaction in Nb-based alloys 
Many results of internal-friction measurements have indicated that the inter- 
stitial-substitutional effect on diffusion is mainly caused by the elastic interaction 
(size factor), modulus factor and chemical or electrical affinity between substitu- 
tional and interstitial atoms (Szkopiak and Smith 1975, Lauf and Alstetter 1979, 
Albert et al. 1983, Gridnev and Kushnareva 1987, Indrawirawan et al. 1987, 
Kushnareva et al. 1993, Numakura et al. 1995). From our previous results on 
Nb-Mo and Nb-Ta (Miura et al. 1999, 
2001), 
it was strongly suggested that the 
interstitial-substitutional effect on mechanical properties in Nb-based alloys was 
caused by the size misfit and/or chemical affinity between substitutional solute 
atoms and Nb. The present results indicate that the interstitial-substitutional 
interaction plays the role of dislocation anchorage, and it appears more signifi- 
cantly with Mo addition than with Ta addition, as shown in figures 5 and 6. It is 
known that Mo addition strengthens Nb, while Ta addition does not, because of 
the size misfit. The difference in the size misfit of Mo and Ta versus Nb should 
influence interstitial-substitutional interaction behaviour. The elastic interaction 
caused by the size misfit of Mo and Nb exists in any temperature range, not 
only in the region where 
Nb-20mol% 
Mo exhibits weak negative temperature 
dependence of the CRSS (T > 0.5 T,) (Miura 1999). This induces the attractive 
force between Mo and 0 atoms in a wide temperature range, which accommo- 
dates the elastic strain. As a result, a 
Mo-0 
complex is supposed to be formed 
preferentially. This would be the reason why the Nb-Mo alloy strongly exhibits 
the interstitial-substitutional interaction at elevated temperatures.
