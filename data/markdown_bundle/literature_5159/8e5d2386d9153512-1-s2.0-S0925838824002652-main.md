ABSTRACT 
The high cracking susceptibility is the major obstacle to the wide application of high γ ′ -phase nickel-based su -
peralloys in additive manufacturing. It is especially important to explore the effects of alloying elements during 
the nickel-based superalloys and establish a new alloy design criterion. In this work, a new nickel-based su -
peralloys with crack-free and low porosity was fabricated by adjusting the content of solid-solution and 
precipitating elements of Inconel 738LC (IN738LC) alloy utilizing laser directed energy deposition (LDED) 
technology, achieving an increase in strength and ductility simultaneously. The newly developed nickel-based 
superalloys maintain a high content of γ ′ -phase and display substantial fine bulk MC carbides, resulting in 
higher strength (UTS: 1461 MPa, YS: 1105 MPa) and elongation (EL: 12.9%). After heat treatment, extensive 
long-stripes of M
23
C
6 
carbides were detected in the IN738LC sample, which led to poor tensile properties at high 
temperature (900 
◦
C). However, due to the finer γ ′ phase uniformly distributed in the matrix, the developed new 
nickel-based superalloys exhibits optimal tensile properties (UTS: 593 MPa, YS: 475 MPa) and ductility (9.4%) at 
900 
◦
C. This approach can provide guidance for the design of high γ ′ -phase nickel-based superalloys for additive 
manufacturing starting from elemental powders. 
1. Introduction 
Nickel-based superalloys have a wide variety of application in the 
aerospace industry (e.g., engine turbine blades, guide vanes and turbine 
disks components), owing to their excellent high-temperature strength, 
creep resistance, fatigue properties, as well as outstanding antioxidation 
and corrosion resistance [1,2] . Howbeit, as the traditional 
manufacturing process involve highly time, energy and material 
consuming processing steps, along with the challenges in shaping and 
repairing large and complicated components, which have limited the 
further application of nickel-based superalloys [3,4] . In recent years, the 
rapid development of additive manufacturing (AM) technology has 
provided a way to address this problem [5,6] . In particular, laser 
directed energy deposition (LDED) offers low cost, high deposition ra -
tios, and high laser power, making the LDEDed nickel-based superalloys 
components particularly suitable for aerospace applications [7] . 
IN738LC is a typical precipitation-strengthened nickel-based super -
alloy characterized by a high volume fraction of intermetallic γ ′ pre -
cipitates, a basic chemical composition of Ni
3
(Al, Ti) phase, and a crystal 
structure of L1
2 
[1] . The crucial feature is that its complicated 
composition, which usually consists of various alloying elements, 
including Co, Cr and Mo serving as the γ phase forming elements for 
solid-solution strengthening, Al and Ti as the γ ′ -Ni
3
(Al, Ti) phase form -

grain-boundary strengthening elements [8] . However, because of its 
special composition (Al + Ti > 6%) and extremely high content of 
γ ′ -Ni
3
(Al, Ti) phase, IN738LC alloy with high cracking susceptibility is 
very prone to cracking during LDED process, which seriously impacts 
the mechanical properties of nickel-based superalloy [6,9] . This would 
destroy the structural integrity of the laser additive manufactured 
IN738LC material, which is even inferior to the properties of the alloy 
fabricated by casting process [10] . This impedes the widespread appli -
cation of the LDEDed IN738LC alloys in aerospace [11] . 
At present, substantial studies have focused on adjusting the content 
of grain-boundary strengthening elements in IN738LC alloy to mitigate 
or eliminate cracks for enhancing the mechanical properties. For 
example, Guo et al. [12] fabricated IN738LC alloys with different B 
content (0.005%, 0.02%) by laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) technology, 
and observed that the liquid film of borides were formed in the samples 
with a higher B content, resulting in cracking under the effect of thermal

Considerable researches have demonstrated that solid-solution 
strengthening and precipitation strengthening elements play an impor -
tant role in enhancing the mechanical properties of nickel-based su -
peralloy. Su et al. [14] revealed that Co element would facilitate the 
reduction of Ti and Al solubility in the γ phase, promoting the formation 
of ordered γ ′ -Ni
3
(Co, Al, Ti) phase, which enhances the volume fraction 
of the γ ′ phase. In addition, the presence of Co element improves phase 
stability and reduces the stacking fault energy, and thus enhancing the 
mechanical properties [15] . The sublattice sites of Al and Ti atoms in the 
γ ′ -Ni
3
(Al, Ti) phase can be occupied by Nb atoms to form γ ′ -Ni
3
(Al, Ti, 
Nb) phase. And the substituted atoms of the Al and Ti atoms can be used 
to form a new γ ′ phase again. Also, the element Nb contributes to the 
reduction of Al and Ti solubility in the γ phase and increases the volume 
fraction of the γ ′ phase in the matrix, leading to a strong inhibition on 
dislocation motion that increases the transient tensile strength and creep 
rupture strength [16] . At the same time, the Mo element, as a slowly 
diffusing refractory element, is beneficial for improving creep strength 
at high temperatures and low stress creep conditions [17] . However, the 
high viscosity (8 × 10
 3 
Pa • s), high melting point, poor fluidity and fast 
solidification rate of W elements will introduce microstructural defects 
during additive manufacturing process [18] . Moreover, Ta element can 
effectively increases the volume fraction of γ ′ phase and refines the size 
of γ ′ phase. Therefore, the precipitation strengthening effect of Ni-based 
superalloys can be improved by the addition of Ta element [19] . How -
ever, Ta element, as an expensive strategic element, has the strongest of 
segregation degree in the interdendritic region compared to Al and Ni 
elements [20] . Additionally, the addition of Ti and Al elements with 
lower content and solid-solution strengthening elements with higher 
content will lead to the alloys being transformed into solid-solution 
strengthening dominated or with a low content γ ′ phase, which impos -
sible to satisfy the applications of hot section components such as gas 
turbines, jet engines or nuclear reactors [8,21] . However, excessive Ti 
and Al of the IN738LC alloy contents are prone to cracking during the 
laser additive manufacturing process, reducing the mechanical proper -
ties of the alloy [6,9] . Therefore, the optimal effect of coupling effect of 
the solid-solution strengthening and precipitation strengthening can be 
achieved by reasonable regulation of solid-solution strengthening and 
precipitation strengthening elements, and thus obtaining a significantly 
increase in the mechanical properties of IN738LC alloy. 
Nevertheless, to the best of authors ’ knowledge, the improvement of 
mechanical properties of laser additive manufactured IN738LC alloy by 
adjusting the solid solution strengthening and precipitation strength -
ening elements has scarcely been investigated to date. In this work, three 
new nickel-based superalloys were successfully fabricated by LDED 
technology and adjusting the content of solid-solution strengthening and 
precipitation strengthening elements based on traditional IN738LC 
alloy. Compared with the conventional IN738LC alloy, the newly 
developed nickel-based superalloys increases the content of Co, Nb and 
Mo elements and decreases the content of W and Ta elements, together 
with adjusts the Ti and Al content according to a gradient. As a critical 
material for hot section components, nickel-based superalloys required 
sufficient high-temperature strength [22,23] . Thus, this work

2. Experiment 
2.1. Materials 
Prior to the LDED deposition, the 304SS substrate measuring 80 mm 
× 50 mm × 30 mm (length, width, thickness) was sequentially ground 
with SiC paper of grit scale 200 – 2000 and cleaned using absolute 
ethanol. The powders with compositions presented in Table 1 were used 
as the depositing material. The four alloys powders of IN738LC, 1#, 2# 
and 3# with a mean particle diameter of 75 µ m , were deposited on the 
substrate by the LDED technology. To achieve a uniform distribution, 
they were processed using the QM-3SP04L ball mill at a speed of 120 r/ 
min for two hours. Afterward, they were dried in a drying oven at 60 
◦
C 
for one hour and screened through a 180 mesh before being subjected to

2.2. LDED process 
Fig. 1 a depicts the schematic view of the LDED process. To prepare 
the sample with an FL-1500 1.5 kW fiber laser was employed along with 
a synchronous powder-feeding and water-cooling system. To ensure the 
samples were free from defects such as pores and microcracks, the 
processing parameters was optimized as follows: a laser power of 650 W, 
spot diameter of 1.2 mm, traverse speed of 550 mm/min, energy density

2

min. High-purity argon (99.999% Argon) was used as both a carrier and 
shielding gas at a flow rate of 10 L/min.

4. Discussion 
As well-known, the tensile properties of LDEDed nickel-based su -
peralloys at room and high temperatures have a close relationship with 
defects, grain structure and precipitation phase. Therefore, the evolution 
of microstructure in the LDEDed IN738LC and the three novel alloys 
developed in this work have been discussed in detail, and the relation -
ship between microstructure and tensile properties has been established. 
4.1. The effects of microstructure on tensile properties at room 
temperature 
The cracks and pores can be observed in the LDEDed IN738LC and 
3# specimens as shown in Fig. 2 . Generally, the generation of stress 
concentration can be caused by pores and cracks, triggering the 
expansion of cracks and reducing the strength together with ductility of 
the material [29] . Therefore, IN738LC and 3# specimens display poor 
tensile properties. In difference, a large amount of γ - γ ′ eutectic structure 
at the grain boundaries of the 3# specimen, which can promote the 
generation of the continuous liquid film. The liquid film has been 
emphasized as a key factor leading to solidification and liquation 
cracking, and thus the initiation of crack can be promoted by the 
continuous liquid film [13] . In addition, the presence of γ - γ ′ eutectic 
structure tends to result in the weakness of grain boundary and 
increasing the local stress concentration [45] , which seriously affects the 
strength and ductility of the 3# sample.
