abstract
Excellent high-temperature mechanical properties and high recrystallization temperature are crucial for
molybdenum (Mo) alloys in practical applications. The microstructure and high-temperature mechanical
properties of Mo-0.8%La 2O3-2%ZrC alloys prepared by traditional powder metallurgy technology asso-
ciated with cross rolling post treatment were investigated. Results showed that the Mo1950-rolled alloys
had a ﬁne grain size of about 1.65 mm due to hot cross rolling and high tensile strengths of 988 MPa and
189 MPa at room temperature and 1400 /C14 C, respectively. SEM and TEM examinations indicated that
although most of the large La 2O3 and ZrC particles were distributed along the grain boundaries, some
nano-sized particles were well dispersed in intragranular zones. Particularly, dislocations were found to
intercepted by the second phase particles in tensile samples, which could effectively enhance both the
tensile strength and recrystallization temperature. Dislocation, grain re ﬁning, and second phase particles
were all found to play important roles in strengthening the alloys below the recrystallization temper-
ature, while second phase particle strengthening was the only strengthening mechanisms above the
recrystallization temperature.

1. Introduction
Refractory metal molybdenum has a relatively high melting
temperature (2620 /C14 C), a low thermal expansion coef ﬁcient, satis-
factory electrical and thermal conductivities, and an excellent
corrosion resistance. Thus, it has been extensively used in various
industries, including the metallurgy, machinery, oil, and chemical
industries [ 1e4]. However, further applications of molybdenum
have been inhibited by its disadvantages, e.g. a low room-
temperature toughness, a low recrystallization temperature, an
inadequate high-temperature strength, and a high ductile-to-
brittle transition temperature (DBTT). In particular, its low
recrystallization temperature results in grain coarsening and thus
deteriorates its high-temperature mechanical properties. Accord-
ingly, alloying was expected to be a promising route to resolve
these problems, which lead to the development of titanium-
zirconium-molybdenum alloys (TZM), aluminium-potassium-
silicon-doped molybdenum alloys (AKS) [ 5,6], rhenium-doped
molybdenum alloys (Mo-Re) [ 7], carbide-strengthened molybde-
num alloys [ 8], and rare earth oxide dispersion strengthened mo-
lybdenum alloys (ODS) [ 9]. Speci ﬁcally, Cockeram [ 10] found that
the room temperature tensile strength of TZM plates produced by
arc melting was 808 MPa, but it deteriorated rapidly to 414 MPa and
172 MPa at 1200
/C14 C and 1400 /C14 C, respectively. Wang et al. [ 11] re-
ported that the tensile strength of AKS alloys produced using
powder metallurgy declined abruptly to 369 MPa and 261 MPa at
800 /C14 C and 1350 /C14 C, respectively. As for the addition of Re, re-
searchers found that increasing the content of Re could promote
the tensile strength, yield strength of the Mo-Re alloys and

of grain size and uniform distribution of nano-sized particles. The
Mo alloy had high strength (800 e900 MPa) and extraordinary

that larger La2O3 particles were located at grain boundaries or sub-

grain boundary from moving and sliding during annealing or
deformation, which was found to be bene ﬁcial to high temperature

Besides rare earth oxides, carbides with ultrahigh melting
temperature were the other strengthening candidates for molyb-
denum alloys. The addition of carbide particles such as TiC, ZrC, and
HfC gave the alloys a uniform ﬁne grain structure, a high room-
temperature strength, a low DBTT, a high recrystallization tem-
perature, and an excellent high temperature strength and tough-
ness. Generally, Dispersion strengthening was the main
strengthening mechanism for carbide-strengthened molybdenum
alloys. Moreover, some carbides such as ZrC, TiC, and molybdenum
might dissolve in each other at high temperature and then the
interface strength between the two phases can also be improved by
the formation of a solid solution [ 18]. In addition, Kurishita et al. [ 8]
veriﬁed that a carbon concentration at the grain boundary can
effectively improve the grain boundary strength of molybdenum.
So, in this work, to further improve the high-temperature me-
chanical properties of molybdenum alloys, we prepared Mo-La 2O3-
ZrC alloys by adding nano-sized ZrC and La 2O3 particles via tradi-
tional powder metallurgy technology. Nevertheless, it is well
known that even though experiencing high temperature sintering
at high temperatures, some closed pores still remain in the sintered
samples which limits further increase of the bulk density and more
seriously, the crystal grains would get excessively coarsened during
sintering process. So in this study, cross rolling post treatment was
introduced to eliminate the remnant pores and break the coarsened
grains simultaneously. Although the effects of deformation
strengthening by cross rolling might not work at the temperature
beyond recrystallization [ 19,20], enhanced mechanical properties
could be anticipated in this work because of the synergistic
strengthening mechanisms of grain re ﬁning, oxides dispersion,
carbides and deformation strengthening. Particularly, the grain
orientation and the interaction between the dispersed second
phase particles and the dislocations were carefully examined to
determine the roles of different strengthening mechanisms at
different temperatures.
2. Experimental
2.1. Preparation of Mo-La 2O3-ZrC alloys
2.1.1. Fabrication by powder metallurgy technology
Mo powder (2.8 mm ＜ d ＜ 3.2 mm) with a purity of 99.9% and
La2O3 (d ＜ 40 nm) and ZrC (d ＜ 50 nm) with purities of 99.9% were
mixed at a weight ratio of 97.2:0.8:2 to prepare Mo-0.8%La 2O3-2%
ZrC alloys. The powder mixtures were loaded into the stainless
steel V-type vial of a V-type mixer and mechanically milled for
14 h at a rotation speed of 500 rpm. The vial was ﬁlled with puriﬁed
nitrogen gas to avoid contamination and oxidation from air during
the milling. Then, the prepared powder mixtures were pressed into
plate compacts by cold isostatic pressing at 160 MPa for 5 min. The
as-prepared compacts were sintered in a hydrogen atmosphere at
1950 /C14 C, 1980 /C14 C, or 2010 /C14 C for 6 h.
2.1.2. Cross rolling post treatment
After sintering, the plates were subjected to ten passes of cross
rolling. During the rolling process, the plates were rotated by 90/C14 on
the rolling plane and annealed after each pass (see Fig. 1 ). This
conﬁguration was selected to minimise the risk of cracking due to
an excessive accumulation of tensile stresses in the rolling direction
and to reduce the anisotropy of the mechanical properties due to a
possible excessive elongation of the grains in the rolling direction.
The cross rolling schedule is outlined in Table 1. After cross rolling,
the alloys were annealed at 1200 /C14 C for 40 min to relieve the re-
sidual stress.

Microstructure of the samples were examined using a Zeiss ﬁeld

back-scattered diffraction (EBSD), and a transmission electron mi-

ethanol. Selected tensile testing samples were used for metallo-

alloys at different temperatures. Metallographic structures of cross
section and rolling plane of the alloys were observed (see Fig. 3 ).
The samples were prepared by electro-discharge machining, and
then polished using metallographic techniques and etched with a
potassium ferricyanide and sodium hydroxide composite erosive
liquid. The grain size was measured using computer image analysis
software. A sample for EBSD observations was ﬁrst prepared using
metallographic techniques, and then was polished for 4 h using
vibration polishing to remove the distortion layer on the surface. In
the samples prepared for TEM observations, slices were ﬁrst cut

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Room temperature mechanical properties of Mo-0.8%La 2O3-2%
ZrC alloys

988 MPa and an elongation of 10%. This could be because the

and the crystal grains would be smaller accordingly. The UTS of
Mo1950-rolled alloys is 51% higher than pure Mo and 22% higher
than TZM alloys [ 10].

The metallographic structures of the rolling plane and cross
plane of the Mo1950-rolled alloys at different testing temperatures
are shown in Fig. 7 . It can be seen that the grains grow with an
increase in the testing temperature and change from a ﬁbrous
shape ( Fig. 7 b, d, and f) to an equiaxed shape ( Fig. 7 h and j) when
the testing temperature is beyond 1200 /C14 C. The grains sizes were
measured according to Chinese National Standard GB/T 6394-2002.
Fig. 8 shows the grains size histograms and average grain sizes at

would decrease correspondingly. As a result, the tensile strength
and yield strength decreased and the elongation rapidly increased,
as reported in Table 3 . The recrystallization temperature of the
Mo1950-rolled alloys was higher than 1200 /C14 C but lower than
1400 /C14 C, whereas this temperature for pure Mo is approximately
110 0/C14 C[ 15]. La 2O3 and ZrC particles were distributed at the grain
boundaries and grain interior. Thus, the particles effectively
impeded the motion of the dislocations and grain boundaries,
which improved the recrystallization temperature and high-
temperature strength.
The elongation of Mo1950-rolled alloys at room temperature
was about 10% and the fracture mode was basically ductile fracture.
It was reported that the DBTT of pure molybdenum is approxi-
mately 25 /C14 C[ 10], it is reasonable to deduce that the DBTT of
Mo1950-rolled alloys is lower than 25 /C14 C due to the addition of
La2O3 and ZrC particles and consequent ﬁner grains. The curves of
“Load-Displacement” in Fig. 9 indicates that all the samples tested
at elevated temperatures fractured in a similar ductile fracture
mode. This is in accordance with the morphology of the fracture
surface and previous work [ 21e24]. It can be discerned in Fig. 10
that with testing temperature increase, the dimples got enlarged
and tearing ridges got lengthened. On the one hand, the amount of
dislocations would decrease due to increased climbing and anni-
hilation and thus the elongation decreased at ﬁrst. On the other
hand, once the temperature exceeded 1200 /C14 C, the slide of grain
boundary became easier and thus the growth of dimples got rather
smoother. As a result, at higher temperature above 1200 /C14 C, the
grain boundary sliding played the dominant role and hence the
plasticity of the alloys was enhanced.
It was interesting that both tensile strength and elongation of
Mo1950-rolled alloys were larger than those at 800 /C14 C. For gener-
ally, the strength of deformed metal materials would decrease
while the plasticity would increase because of recovery or recrys-
tallization occurred at high temperature. In this study, dynamic
recovery would occur during the tensile tests at 800 /C14 C owing to
distortion energy stored during cross rolling [25], so it is reasonable
to get a lower tensile strength at 800 /C14 C for the decreased dislo-
cation density and consequent weakened dislocation strengthening
mechanism [26]. However, the decreased elongation at 800 /C14 Cw a s
likely attributed to the ductile laminate failure mechanism or
weakening of thin sheet toughening mechanism [ 10]. More spe-
ciﬁcally, for Mo alloys with sheet-like or pancake-shaped crystal
structure ( Fig. 7 b), during tensile testing, the initial constraint
condition was triaxial stress state, this kind of stress tended to
induce the generation of microcracks in interlaminar boundaries
between ﬂake-like grains. Once the initial microcracks propagated
along the grain boundaries, the grains were separated ultimately,
and then the triaxial stress state would change into plane stress
state for the sheet-like grains. Consequently, the ﬂake-like grains
were then pulled to failure with a high degree of necking and
brought about an increased elongation for the alloys. This might be
the main mechanism of thin sheet toughening [ 10,21]. In principle,
a higher resistance to necking can bring about a higher elongation
or enhanced thin sheet toughening effects. Then in this study,
during the tensile testing at 800 /C14 C, the working-hardening rate for
the Mo alloys deceased due to the in-situ merging or annihilation of
dislocations because of dynamic recovery [27], hence the resistance
to necking was weakened at 800 /C14 C compared with that at room
temperature. Accordingly, the elongation at high testing tempera-
ture got lower than that obtained at room temperature [ 22].

grain re ﬁning strengthening and second phase particle strength-

materials. In this work, during cross rolling, not only a large number
of dislocations were introduced, but also the crystal grains were
reﬁned because of compression. So once the load was exerted on
the sample at room temperature, these dislocations acted as ob-
stacles to each other by piling up, intertwining and intercepting.

4. Conclusions
Mo-0.8%La2O3-2%ZrC alloys with a ﬁne grain size of 1.65 mm, in
which La2O3 and ZrC particles are located at the grain boundaries or
within the grains, were successfully prepared by powder metal-
lurgy process associated with subsequent cross rolling post treat-
ment. Mechanical testing shows a good combination of strength
and ductility at room temperature, with an ultimate tensile
strength of 988 MPa and a total elongation of 10.0%. The dispersion
of La 2O3 and ZrC particles were found to effectively increase the
strength and recrystallization temperature of the Mo-0.8%La2O3-2%
ZrC alloys, which is bene ﬁcial for their high-temperature me-
chanical properties. Dislocation strengthening, grain re ﬁning
strengthening, and second phase particle strengthening were all
found to play important roles below the recrystallization temper-
ature, while second phase particle strengthening was the only
strengthening mechanism above the recrystallization temperature.
