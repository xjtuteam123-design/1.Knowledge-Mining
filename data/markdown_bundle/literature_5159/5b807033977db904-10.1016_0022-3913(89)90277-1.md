Conflicting views have been presented regarding the 
influence of high-temperature soldering on the strength of 
base metal alloys. Furthermore, in a clinical situation, bend- 
ing is the most frequent type of stress applied to partial 
denture frameworks. Therefore, this study was conducted to 
determine the flexural strength of a Co-Cr alloy that had 
been soldered with two commercially available high-melting 
solders and to compare it with the flexural strength of intact 
specimens of the same alloy. 
MATERIAL AND METHODS 
The partial denture framework alloy tested was Vitallium 
cobalt-chromium alloy (Howmedica Inc., Dental Division, 
Chicago, Ill., batch No. 3393). In all, 34 cylindrical rods ap- 
proximately 25 mm long and with a diameter of approxi- 
mately 2 mm were produced. The castings were done by a 
commercial dental laboratory according to a procedure de- 
scribed by the manufacturer of the Eiectromatic III Univer- 
sal (Howmedica, Inc.) high frequency casting machine. 
The cast rods were randomly assigned to three test groups. 
One group of rods (n = 14) was tested for bending strength 
without prior soldering and the specimens in the two other 
groups (10 rods in each group) were tested for bending 
strength after soldering. Before soldering, these rods were 
embedded in soldering investment for high-precision sol- 
dering (Fig. 1). At a distance of 12 mm from one end, the rods 
were cut by a diamond circular saw (Discoplan TS, Struers, 
Denmark) under water spray. The distance between the 
segments after cutting was 0.5 f 0.05 mm. 
After cutting, the segments were retained in the invest- 
ment and returned to the laboratory for soldering. Ten of the 
cut specimens were soldered by Bego Co-Cr solder (order No. 
52520, Bego Goldschlligerei Wilh. Herbst GmbH & Co., Bre- 
men, West Germany, batch No. 6303) (soldering tempera- 
ture 1180’ C). The other 10 cut specimens were soldered by 
Vitallium welding rods (Item No. 0412-01, Howmedica, Inc., 
batch No. 06413) (soldering temperature 1289’ C). X-ray 
microanalysis (energy dispersive, EDAX) revealed that in 
addition to the main elements of Co and Cr in both solders, 
the Bego solder contained silicium and the Vitallium solder 
tungsten. 
After soldering, the specimens were retrieved from the in- 
vestment and by the use of a special grinding device the rods 
were ground by a diamond wheel under water spray to a near 
exact cylindrical shape (approximate diameter 1.7 mm) in a 
length of 8 mm, starting 11 mm from one end of the rod and 
including the soldered part. The reduced segments were 
projected on a screen at x30 magnification and measured 
with a precision of 1 pm. For each cylindrical part a 
maximum variation of 20 wrn in diameter was tolerated. On 
the nonsoldered specimens similar nearly exact cylindrical 
portions were produced in the same manner. 
For testing, one end of the specimen was mounted in a jig

This study compared the flexural strength of a high-tem-

The cast specimens were randomly assigned to one control

specimen rods in the test groups were cut by a diamond cir-

strength (MPa) between the groups.
