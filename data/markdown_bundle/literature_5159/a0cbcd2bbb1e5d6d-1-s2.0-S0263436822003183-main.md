ABSTRACT 
Electron beam melting (EBM) is a promising powder bed fusion additive manufacturing (AM) technology for the 
preparation of complex refractory components due to the significant advantages of vacuum environment and 
high level in energy utilization, scanning speed and temperature in the build chamber. However, the fabrication 
of crack-free and refined W-based alloy remains a challenge. This study aims to address this problem by intro -
ducing 0.7 wt% carbon in EBM W-3.5Nb alloy. Results revealed that the as built W-3.5Nb alloy had a typical 
columnar structure with obvious solidification cracks along the grain boundary. Doping 0.7 wt% carbon 
generated refined microstructure, which was composed of the initial dendrite of tungsten and the eutectic phase 
in the dendrites gap. Due to the low distribution coefficient of carbon, the nucleation at the bottom of the molten 
pool was caused by constitutional supercooling in the front of liquid solidification in a single molten pool, and 
velocity of the solidification front was reduced by carbon concentration leading increased thermal supercooling. 
Supercooling (thermal supercooling together with constitutional supercooling) refined the microstructure of W- 
3.5Nb alloy effectively. Meanwhile, due to the formation of eutectic phase with low melting point, the fluidity of 
liquid phase at the terminal solidification was improved and the solidification cracks along the grain boundary 
were reduced. The carbon addition also played a significant role in alloy strengthening. The microhardness of the 
alloy increased from 382 HV to 696 HV, and the compressive strength increased from 962.89 MPa to 1892.56 
MPa. These findings offer a new perspective on the AM fabrication of high-quality W-based alloys. 
1. Introduction 
As a typical refractory metal, Tungsten is used in a variety of critical 
applications benefiting from its attractive properties such as high den -
sity, high melting point, high thermal conductivity, low expansion co -
efficient and low vapor pressure. Especially, the use of tungsten as 
plasma facing components in fusion reactors has been discussed and 
studied for decades considering its good erosion resistance against 
plasma sputtering [ 1 , 2 ]. Due to the extremely high melting point, the 
main routes of manufacturing tungsten and W-based alloys are tradi -
tional powder metallurgy (PM) followed by hot working [ 3 , 4 ], spark 
plasma sintering (SPS) [ 5 , 6 ], chemical vapor deposition (CVD) [ 7 ], etc., 
which represent limiting in the manufacture of complex parts. In addi -
tion, the extreme hardness and brittleness of tungsten at room temper -
ature make it vulnerable to post-machining, which further limits the 
application of W-based materials. Additive manufacturing (AM), also 
known as 3D printing, is a technique for fabricating complex structures 
based on 3D model data. For the advantage of manufacturing 
customization and free form fabrication, metal AM has drawn lots of 
attentions in a variety of research fields [ 8 ]. In recent years, great pro -
gresses have been reported in fabricating refractory metals such as 
tungsten and molybdenum, which are very similar in properties, using 
AM technique. However, Cracking and coarse columnar microstructure 
are still main problem in literatures [ 9 – 12 ]. Many studies on charac -
terization and inhibition of cracking have been conducted. Generally 
speaking. Causes for cracking can be generally summarized as follows: 
1) Tungsten and molybdenum possess quite high ductile-to-brittle 
transition temperatures (DBTT), which means low plasticity and 
high susceptibility to cracking at room temperature. Cracks occurred 
in the solid state when the sample was below DBTT as a result of 
significant inelastic deformation, which attributed to the develop -
ment of significant residual stresses in AM. AM is a process involving 
the overlapping of tiny molten pool, and there is a significant thermal 
gradient between the molten pool and the surrounding environment. 
Meanwhile, AM is a layer by layer accumulation process, the heat

Recently, some progress has been made in additive manufacturing of

[ 21 , 22 ]. At the same time, the addition of tantalum can also form sub -

Some scanning strategies, such as the chessboard scanning strategy, the 
zigzag scanning strategy and the remelting scanning strategy, were also 
conducted, and the cracks seemed to be inevitable [ 24 ]. 
As an important powder bed fusion additive manufacturing tech -
nology, electron beam melting (EBM) has many advantages in fabri -
cating tungsten materials. Vacuum condition and electron beam with 
extremely high scan speeds up to 8000 m/s are available in EBM, which 
is very advantageous in the case of tungsten-related fabrication. High 
scan speed allows each powder layer or deposited layer to be heated by 
rapid scan of high power electron beam to keep the sample at elevated 
temperature all through the build process. Previous studies have proved 
its effectiveness in controlling residual stress and cracking [ 25 , 26 ]. 
Although some progress has been made in the control of solid state 
cracks, the high melting point and thermal conductivity of tungsten 
generate the remarkable heat conduction along the forming direction, 
resulting in coarse columnar structure under the action of epitaxial 
growth., up to date, the production of fully dense and crack free tungsten 
parts with refined microstructure by AM is still an unsolved problem. 
Based on the model for grain size prediction for less nuclei [ 27 ], the 
grain refining requires an alloying element with a high growth restric -
tion factor and a low diffusion rate within the melt. So, carbon was 
chosen as a potential grain refining element in AM. The reports showed 
that carbon has played a significant role in the production of crack-free 
and fine microstructure molybdenum and refractory high entropy alloys

2. Material and method 
Pre-alloyed W-3.5Nb and W-3.5Nb-0.7C alloy spherical powder 
prepared by plasma spheroidization were used as feedstock in this work 
(Stardust Technology (Guangdong), Co., Ltd., China). The particle size 
of the powder lies between 15 and 53 μ m. Their morphologies, particle 
size distribution and chemical compositions are given in Fig. 1 and 
Table 1 . 
Samples of W-3.5Nb and W-3.5Nb-0.7C alloys with a cubic dimen -
sion of 10 mm × 10 mm × 10 mm were fabricated in a EBM system 
(Sailong S2, Xi ’ an Sailong Metal Materials Co., Ltd., China). The EBM 
process was conducted under a vacuum atmosphere with a vacuum 
pressure controlled to be < 10
 2 
Pa. First of all, a 3D model was sliced 
into layers with a thickness of 0.05 mm. The EBM process started with a 
preheating of the build platform to 600 
◦
C with electron beam, then a 
layer of feed stock was applicated on the build platform. Then, an 
electron beam scanned and melted the powder layer based on the layer 
information of the 3D model with a beam current of 19 – 21 mA and a 
scanning speed of 1100 mm/s. Subsequently, the entire platform was 
lowered by the thickness of one layer, and the spreading and melting 
processes were repeated till the whole sample was built. The scan 
strategy is shown in Fig. 2 , where the scan direction is rotated by 90
◦
between layers. 
The as built samples were polished using standard metallographic 
technique and then etched with reagent (HF: HNO
3 
= 8: 1). The 
microstructure of the alloy was investigated by optical microscopy (OM, 
Axio Vert A1 Stemi) and scanning electron microscopy (SEM, JSM- 
IT500LA). Phase characterization was carried out using X-ray diffrac -

◦

detailed information on grain structure and crystal orientation distri -
bution. Microhardness tester was employed to measure the Vickers 
hardness. The load and dwell time were 50 g and 10 s, respectively. 
Compressive test at ambient temperature was performed on an Instron 
machine (Instron 5985) at the strain rate of 0.001/s with cylindrical 
samples of φ 5 mm × 5 mm. Afterwards the fracture surfaces were 
characterized via SEM.

Tungsten and niobium form a complete solid solution during the 
melting process. Because of the excellent thermal conductivity of 
tungsten and layer by layer thermal accumulation during the EBM 
process, there is a distinct heat conduction along the building direction 
Z. As a result, the solidification process of W-3.5Nb solid solution is 
similar to that of pure tungsten, which is carried out by forced epitaxial 
growth and the grains grow opposite to the heat flow direction to form a 
coarse columnar structure (as shown in Fig. 9 (a)). 
Doped with carbon, the solidification, nucleation and grain growth 
of the W-3.5Nb alloy change significantly. The low distribution coeffi -
cient of carbon in tungsten (the ratio of solute concentration in the solid 
to that in the liquid) is 0.0017 [ 32 ], causing a distinct concentration 
gradient at the solid/liquid interface during the solidification process. It 
may establish the enrichment of carbon in liquid regions ahead of an 
advancing solid front where the actual temperature is lower than the 
liquidus temperature. In certain conditions, this constitutional super -
cooling can trigger nucleation on the contact surface between the bot -
tom of the tiny molten pool and the deposited part. Owing to the action 
of the nucleation particles, tiny equiaxed grains forms at the bottom of 
the molten pool (as shown in Fig. 9 (b)). Part of tiny equiaxed grains 
grow to be tiny columnar grains under the effect of epitaxial growth. In 
addition, it was also reported on AM molybdenum, which is very similar 
to tungsten in physical and chemical properties, that grain refinement 
can also be ascribed to thermal supercooling as a result of the lag in the 
growth rate of the Mo and Mo
2
C solid phases relative to the solidus 
temperature. This lag owes to the accumulation of a carbon ahead of the 
solidification front, which reduces the solidification front velocity [ 33 ]. 
So, We attributed grain refinement via carbon to increasing thermal 
supercooling together with constitutional supercooling. 
The solidification front interface becomes unstable enough to break 
due to the disturbance of the solute so that the interface changes from 
planar to cellular. In a single molten pool, columnar dendrites are 
formed along the conduction direction of the overall heat flow. With the 
further decrease of temperature, there is an obvious eutectic reaction L 
→ W
2
C + W in the W
–
C binary system. Hence, W-3.5Nb-0.7C alloy 
exhibits a mixed structure consisting of dendritic phase and interden -
dritic eutectic phase. 
3.2. Cracking of W-3.5Nb alloy and the inhibition effect of carbon on 
cracks along the grain boundary

coarse columnar grain boundaries propagate across multiple deposited 
layers in W-3.5Nb alloy. Two main potential mechanisms for cracking 
initiation in AM process have been proposed: (i) Solid-state cracking: 
Cracking occurs during cooling after solidification of liquid once the 
residual thermal stress exceeds the ultimate tensile strength of the solid 
material at a given temperature. In this case, cracks usually possess clean

terminal stage of solidification when the liquid feeding becomes diffi -

cracking, which mostly due to the joint action of residual stress and 
grain boundary oxygen accumulation. Our previously research on EBM 
pure tungsten show that [ 25 ]: Due to the characteristic of preheating 
treatment in EBM, the temperature within the built chamber reaches the 
annealing temperature of W-based material to in-situ release the stress 
effectively and reduce solid-state cracking obviously. Meanwhile, oxy -
gen content of the spherical powder used in the previous research was 
0.017 wt%, which was similar with this study of 0.023 wt%. The results 
showed that oxygen has no significant effect on cracking in EBM tung -
sten. Therefore, the influence of residual stress and oxygen on cracking 
is not deeply analyzed here. In this study, the cracks in W-3.5Nb alloy 
mainly exist along grain boundaries and show rough surface with some 
discontinuous holes, which conforms well to the morphological char -
acteristics of solidification cracks. Compared with pure tungsten, W- 
3.5Nb alloy has a broaden solidification range with a region of semisolid 
(called mushy) at the end of the solidification process. The shrinkage 
during solidification (inside the mushy zone) and thermal contraction 
(both inside and outside the mushy zone) cause intergranular cracking 
[ 34 ]. The addition of 0.7 wt% carbon to W-3.5Nb alloy results in a 
refined grain structure with little cracks and pores along grain boundary. 
Micro-segregation of carbon is a possible condition for reducing solidi -
fication cracking, which promotes the formation of low-melting eutectic 
phase, increasing the flowability of liquid to fill the gap caused by 
shrinkage between dendrites in time. Another reason for crack reducing 
is the smaller grain size (~ 30 μ m) compared with that of the W-3.5Nb 
samples, suggesting that the small grain size is effective in suppressing 
the cracking issue. It has been well-accepted that grain refinement im -
proves the cracking resistance during AM mainly by accommodating the 
local strain. Furthermore, grain boundary density could affect the 
cracking susceptibility by adjusting the thermal stress/strain level. 
3.3. Mechanical properties of W-3.5Nb and W-3.5Nb-0.7C alloys 
Mechanical properties were evaluated based on Vickers hardness and 
compression tests. The Vickers hardness of W-3.5Nb-0.7C sample rea -
ches 696 HV which is 82% higher than that of the W-3.5Nb sample as 
382 HV (as shown in Table 2 ). The reason for the high hardness is the 
present of W
2
C in a closed network structure. Fig. 11 shows the 
Compressive mechanical property of the as-fabricated W-3.5Nb and C- 
doped W-3.5Nb at ambient temperature. Similar to the hardness test 
results, the compression property of W-3.5Nb sample reached 962.89 
MP. Alloying with 0.7 wt% carbon leads to an increase of the 
compression strength to 1892.56 MPa. Fig. 12 and Fig. 13 show the 
fractural morphologies of the W-3.5Nb and carbon-doped specimens, 
respectively. The fractural surfaces in both alloys are smooth without 
any deformation marks, indicating the brittleness and typical inter -
granular fracture mode. 
4. Conclusion 
1. W-3.5Nb and W-3.5Nb-0.7C alloy were fabricated by EBM. Doping 
0.7 wt% carbon transforms coarse columnar grains of W-3.5Nb alloy 
into fine dendrites with W phase being surrounded by a closed 
network of eutectic phase due to thermal supercooling together with 
constitutional supercooling. 
2. Compared with W-3.5Nb alloy, the microhardness of the W-3.5Nb- 
0.7C alloy increased from 382 HV to 696 HV, and the compressive 
strength increased from 962.89 MPa to 1892.56 MPa. 
3. The addition of carbon generates eutectic phase with low melting 
point and refines the microstructure, effectively reducing the solid -
ification crack along the grain boundary.
