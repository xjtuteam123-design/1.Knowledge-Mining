Abstract 
Ti,Al-based alloys have excellent high-temperature strength, but their poor oxidation resistance restricts the applications of 
these alloys at high temperature. In this study the microstructure, oxidation resistance, and mechanical properties of pack 
cementation Al, Si, and Cr coatings on Ti,Al-based alloys, i.e. Ti-25Al-lONb and Ti-25Al-l3Nb, were investigated and 
discussed. The morpholoa of aluminized coating is a single layer of the TiA1, phase. The siliconized coating shows a multilayer 
structure with the composition of TiSi,, TiSi, TiA1, and TiAl from the outer surface to the substrate. The chromized coating is a 
Cr-rich beta phase. The high-temperature oxidation resistance of Al and Si coatings is quite superior, and their temperature limit 
of oxidation resistance is up to 1100°C. In contrast to A1 and Si coatings, the oxidation resistance of the chromized coating is 
poor, since it is unable to improve the oxidation resistance of the Ti-25Al-lONb alloy. The high-temperature tensile test of 
Ti-25A1-13Nb with A1 or Si coatings shows that the hard coating layer may reduce the ductility of the base alloy. The alloy with 
the A1 or Si coating is suitable for applications which operate at high temperatures but low stress levels. 0 2000 Elsevier Science

Several reasons to explain the improvement of the high 
temperature oxidation resistance of Ti,Al-based alloys 
by the addition of Nb have already been published 
[10,13,16,17], among others: (1) Nb raises the activity of 
Al, and favors the Al,O, formation; (2) Nb makes the 
oxide layer denser by promoting dissolution of Al,O, 
in TiO,; and (3) Nb,O, reduces the concentration of 
vacancy of oxygen ions and interstitials of titanium ions 
in TiO,, and consequently lowers the flux of oxygen 
and titanium ions through the oxide scale. Although 
the addition of alloying elements, such as Nb, improves 
the oxidation resistance of Ti,Al-based alloys, a contin- 
uous protective Al,O, layer is difficult to form on the 
alloy surface after high-temperature air exposure 
[lo-171. Therefore, an effective coating process is a 
necessary procedure prior to the practical applications 
of Ti,Al-based alloys at high temperatures. 
A surface modification technique is commonly ac- 
complished by applying protective layers on compo- 
nents so that the expected high-temperature oxidation 
resistance may be achieved. Generally, by the forma- 
tion of a dense oxide layer on the coating surface, the 
coating and the oxide layer become a diffusion barrier 
for oxygen ions and, as a consequence, it lowers the 
oxidation rate significantly. The formation of a continu- 
ous protective Al,O,, Cr,O, or SiO, layer is the most 
effective way for providing an excellent diffusion bar- 
rier to withstand oxidation at high temperatures. Among 
the various techniques of applying surface coatings, the 
pack cementation process is an effective and inexpen- 
sive method to modify the surface of Ti,Al-based al- 
loys. This technique has been used to form an Al-, Cr- 
or Si-rich layer. The coating is expected to form a 
continuous protective Al,O,, Cr,O, or SiO, layer af- 
ter air exposure at elevated temperatures. Only a few 
studies on the pack cementation technique in relation 
to Ti,Al-based alloys have been reported 118-231. Ex- 
cept two studies on siliconization of Ti,Al-based alloys 
[22,23], the major part focused on aluminization of the 
same alloys [18-211. It also reveals that the pack ce- 
mentation process with Ti, Al-based alloys still needs 
more investigation. For practical applications the me- 
chanical properties of the coating layers must be stud- 
ied. There are no satisfactory reports discussing the 
high-temperature mechanical properties of the coated 
Ti, Al-based alloys. 
The present research aims to modify the surface of 
Ti, Al-Nb alloys by using the pack cementation tech- 
nique forming a protective Al,O,, Cr,O, or SiO, coat- 
ing layer at elevated temperatures. The structure and 
properties of the coating layers were investigated. Fi- 
nally, the mechanical properties of the coated Ti, Al- 
based alloys were also evaluated. 
2. Experimental procedures 
The alloys, including Ti-25Al-lONb for kinetics and 
oxidation resistance tests, and Ti-25Al-13Nb for me- 
chanical property test, were prepared by vacuum arc 
remelting. These alloys have a similar microstructure 
consisting of a dual phase, 01, + p, but the high-tem- 
perature mechanical properties of the Ti-25Al-13Nb 
alloy is superior to that of the Ti-25Al-lONb alloy [24]. 
To achieve a homogeneous composition, the 
Ti-25Al-lONb alloy was heat-treated in a vacuum fur- 
nace at 1050°C and lop5 torr for 120 h. To avoid 
microsegregation, Ti-25Al-13Nb was heat-treated at 
1100°C for 12 h in a vacuum. Subsequently, it was 
thermomechanically treated by hot-rolling to obtain a 
sheet with fine grain microstructure, which was pre- 
pared for mechanical testing. The procedure of ther- 
momechanical treatment is shown in Fig. 1. Alloy sam- 
ples were cut into small pieces (10 x 10 x 1 mm) with a 
diamond saw, and then polished before the experi- 
ments. 
The components for the pack cementation process, 
comprising a master alloy (Al, Cr or Si powder), halide 
activator (i.e. AIF, or NH,Cl), and inert filler (i.e. 
Al,O, or SiO,) were weighed and mixed. The packed 
components for aluminization, chromization and sili- 
conization pack cementation processes are listed in 
Table 1. Fig. 2 shows a schematic diagram of the pack 
cementation equipment used in this experiment. The 
specimens were embedded in the pack mixture in an 
alumina crucible and heated in a furnace under Ar 
atmosphere for various times. The optimum process 
time was determined by measuring the minimum value 
of weight gain of specimens of various coating condi- 
tions followed by oxidation at 1000°C in air. After the 
optimum pack cementation treatment, the coated 
specimens were oxidized in air at elevated tempera- 
tures to evaluate their oxidation resistance. The op- 
timized process of pack cementation is suitable for 
both Ti-25Al-lONb and Ti-25Al-13Nb alloys.

3.4. Optimum parameters of the pack cementation process 
I I I 
From a practical point of view, the thickness of the 
coating layer is of important concern. A coating layer, 
which is too thin, will lead to reduction of the protec- 
tion ability of the coated alloys. If the coating is too 
thick, owing to the different thermal expansions 
between the coating and the substrate, it will introduce 
many cracks in the coating layer and will result in 
severe local oxidation or corrosion attack. So the opti- 
mum thickness of the coating layer has to be found to 
obtain the optimum protection. 
The optimum process time of the pack cementation 
process was obtained by high-temperature oxidation 
experiments in which the coated specimens, treated for 
various coating times, were oxidized at 1000°C for a 
fixed time. The pack cementation process by which the 
coated specimen owns the lowest weight gain after the 
high-temperature oxidation test has been considered as 
the optimum process time. Fig. 12 indicates the results 
of the weight change of the oxidation test of the 
aluminized, chromized and siliconized specimens 
processed for various coating times. The experimental 
results reveal that the lowest weight gain in the oxida- 
tion test may be obtained for the aluminization time of 
4 h (Fig. 12a), and for the siliconization time of 3 h 
(Fig. 12c). In the optimum test of chromized specimens, 
the specimens pack-chromized for less than 1 h may 
cause spallation of the coating layer after high-temper- 
ature exposure. The spallation may be due to the weak 
bonding between the deposited layer and the substrate 
because the chromization time is too short for the Cr 
atoms to diffuse deeply into the substrate. The opti- 
mum process time of the pack chromization is approxi- 
mately 1.5 h.

3.6. Mechanical property of the coating layer 
For practical applications, the influence of the coat- 
ing layer on the mechanical properties of the substrate 
alloy must also be concerned. The microstructure and 
oxidation behavior of the coating of either aluminiza- 
tion or siliconization on the Ti-25Al-13Nb alloy are 
the same as the Ti-25Al-lONb alloy [24], and will not 
be repeated here. Table 2 shows the results of the 
tensile test at 975°C for uncoated, aluminized and 
siliconized Ti-25Al-13Nb alloys. The test results re- 
veal that the coated alloys lose their ductility signifi- 
cantly, since the coating layers have an impact on the 
mechanical properties of the alloy. The elongation data 
show that the specimen with a siliconized coating is 
much more brittle than that with an aluminized coat- 
ing. In other words, the alloy with the aluminized 
coating of a single phase layer (TiAI,) has a better 
ductility than that with the multilayered siliconized 
coating. Results from micro-hardness measurements of 
the substrate alloy and the coatings are given in Table 
3. The hardness of either the aluminized or the sili- 
conized coating is much higher than that of the subs- 
trate alloy. It can also be found that the hardness of 
the siliconized coating is higher than that of the 
aluminized coating. The reduction of ductility of the 
coated alloy is apparently due to the hard and brittle 
coating layer. The Ti,Al-Nb alloy with Al or Si coating 
is suitable to be used at high temperature under low 
tension. If the alloy is to be applied at high tempera- 
tures under high tension, an improvement of the ductil- 
ity of the coating layer is still required.

2. The optimum process parameters of the pack- 
aluminization, chromization and siliconization of 
the Ti-25Al-lONb alloy were determined. The 
high-temperature oxidation resistance of the Al 
and Si coatings is quite superior, and their temper- 
ature limit of oxidation resistance is 1100°C. The 
oxidation resistance of chromized coating is poor, 
and the Cr coating is unable to improve the oxida- 
tion resistance of the Ti-25Al-lONb alloy. 
3. The results of the high-temperature tensile test of 
the Ti-25Al-13Nb alloy with Al or Si coating show 
that the hard coating layer lowers the ductility of 
the alloy substrate. The alloy with the Al or Si

tures under low tension.
