ABSTRACT
This work reports on the unprecedented high tensile strength and ductility of single-phase VCoNi 
medium-entropy alloys (MEAs) at liquid helium temperature (4.2 K) and reveals the effects of 
grain size on strength, strain-hardening capability, and discontinuous plastic flow (DPF) behavior 
at 4.2 K. The fine-grained VCoNi MEA with an average grain size of 2.2 μ m exhibits exceptional 
yield strength of 1386 MPa and tensile stress of 1845 MPa at a high elongation of 43%. The 
Hall – Petch (H – P) relationship at 4.2 K for the VCoNi MEA was established for the first time, 
demonstrating that the yield strength enhancement with decreasing temperature primarily 
originates from a reduction in dislocation width, leading to an exceptionally high solid-solution 
strengthening contribution of 782 MPa. In addition to planar slip, nano-twinning and stacking 
faulting were activated at 4.2 K upon plastic deformation, contributing to a sustained strain- 
hardening capability. The restricted mobility of screw dislocations at 4.2 K suppresses dynamic 
recovery, facilitating dislocation proliferation and further enhancing strain hardening. In terms of 
DPF behavior, characterized by abrupt serrations in the stress-strain curves, fine-grained speci -
mens exhibit more pronounced DPF due to the rapid accumulation of geometrically necessary 
dislocations. However, the maximum stress drop prior to plastic instability remains similar across 
all grain sizes, suggesting that dislocation density is the primary factor governing DPF behavior. 
These findings provide important insights into the development and mechanistic understanding 
of ultrastrong and ductile alloys for applications at extremely low temperatures.

Nevertheless, the evaluation of load-bearing performance at cryogenic temperatures remains largely constrained to liquid nitrogen 
temperature (77 K) due to experimental limitations. Besides, as the temperature approaches absolute zero, many metallic materials 
exhibit discontinuous plastic flow (DPF) in contrast to the liquid nitrogen environment, a phenomenon characterized by inhomoge -
neous and localized deformation ( Kim et al., 2024 ; Skocze ´n et al., 2014 ; Tabin, 2021 ; Tabin et al., 2016 ; Tirunilai et al., 2020b ). Given 
that DPF-induced stress drops can undermine mechanical reliability, achieving both superior mechanical performance and suppressed 
DPF is essential for ensuring the structural integrity of materials operating at extreme cryogenic conditions.
Among potential cryogenic materials, austenitic stainless steels with a face-centered cubic (FCC) structure have been considered for 
ultra-low temperature applications due to their high strength ( Kim et al., 2024 ; Zheng et al., 2022 ). However, these alloys exhibit 
vigorous DPF behavior at temperatures near 0 K, introducing a serious risk of localized deformation. Kim et al. ( Kim et al., 2025 ) 
demonstrate that localized deformation bands induced by intense DPF in 316 L stainless steel trigger a brittle-fracture mode. Addi -
tionally, Son et al. ( Son et al., 2025 ) reported a significant reduction in ductility at 4.2 K compared to 77 K in 304 L stainless steel 
fabricated via laser powder bed fusion. This vigorous DPF behavior in austenitic stainless steels has been attributed to 
deformation-induced martensitic transformation ( Tabin et al., 2016 ). However, the underlying mechanism remains controversial: DPF 
may be governed either by the α ʹ martensite phase itself or by the high dislocation density introduced during the γ→ α ʹ phase trans -
formation. Additionally, austenitic stainless steels contain interstitial carbon or nitrogen, which contributes to the Portevin – Le Cha -
telier (PLC) effect ( Hwang et al., 2024 ; Lee et al., 2021 ; Wei et al., 2023 ), further complicating the interpretation of DPF origins. These 
complex interactions make it challenging to isolate and identify the effect of individual microstructural parameters on mechanical 
instability at ultra-low temperatures. Consequently, there remains a gap in understanding from the metallurgy perspective regarding 
how microstructural parameters influence the mechanical response at extremely low temperatures.
While austenitic stainless steels face critical limitations at ultra-low temperatures due to DPF and phase instability, FCC-structured 
high-/medium-entropy alloys (H/MEAs) have emerged as promising alternatives for cryogenic applications owing to their remarkable 
cryogenic properties ( Chu et al., 2024 ; Gao et al., 2023a ; Haftlang et al., 2023 ; Xie et al., 2023 ). To understand the cryogenic response 
of H/MEAs, extensive studies have investigated the effect of cryogenic environment on key intrinsic factors, such as stacking fault 
energy (SFE) ( Wagner and Laplanche, 2023b ), solid-solution strengthening ( Fan et al., 2023 ; Li et al., 2025 ), and grain size ( Lavakumar 
et al., 2023 ; Wagner and Laplanche, 2023a ). However, at extremely low temperatures below the liquid nitrogen temperature, little 
attention has been paid to such key properties of H/MEAs. Although some representative alloys such as CrCoNi and CrFeMnCoNi 
exhibit relatively mild DPF behavior and sustained strain-hardening capabilities at extremely low temperatures near 0 K ( Gao et al., 
2023b ; Kim et al., 2024 ; Naeem et al., 2020 ; Yang et al., 2019 ), these studies have primarily focused on their deformation mechanisms. 
Therefore, a comprehensive understanding of how fundamental microstructural features, particularly solid-solution strengthening and 
grain size, govern the mechanical response, including flow stress, deformation mechanism, and DPF at ultra-low temperatures, re -
mains necessary.
To address these knowledge gaps, we investigate the ultra-low temperature mechanical responses of a single-phase VCoNi MEA 
with three distinct grain sizes, which allows systematic evaluation of solid-solution and grain-boundary strengthening at 4.2 K. The 
VCoNi alloy maintains an FCC solid solution characterized by high solid-solution strengthening ( Sohn et al., 2019 ) and high SFE 
( Chung et al., 2022 ), making it a promising FCC matrix for achieving ultra-high strength ( An et al., 2023 ; Deng et al., 2024 ; Deng et al., 
2025 ; He et al., 2024 ; Huo et al., 2025 ; Xu et al., 2024a ). This alloy offers key advantages for understanding the effect of individual 
strengthening mechanisms on mechanical responses and DPF behavior at extremely low temperatures: (1) inherently high yield 
strength derived from strong solid-solution and grain-boundary strengthening, facilitating a clear evaluation of these strengthening 
mechanisms; (2) stable FCC phase that avoids deformation-induced martensitic transformation; and (3) absence of interstitial elements 
such as C or N, eliminating PLC effects by interstitials. Tensile tests reveal that the fine-grained VCoNi alloy exhibits a yield strength of 
1386 MPa at 4.2 K, accompanied by exceptional strain-hardening behavior. For the first time, this study provides a systematic 
approach to understanding the effect of solid-solution strengthening and grain size on the deformation mechanism and DPF at the 
liquid helium temperature. Especially, the Hall – Petch (H – P) relationship is established in a MEA at 4.2 K, enabling quantitative 
deconvolution of strengthening contributions. The mechanisms underlying deformation structure and DPF are critically examined, 
offering insights into how grain boundaries govern mechanical instability at ultralow temperatures. This study thus advances the 
understanding of cryogenic deformation physics and offers a robust pathway for designing next-generation cryogenic alloys with high 
strength and DPF resistance.

3.1. Microstructure
Fig. 1 shows the single FCC-structured polycrystalline microstructure of the annealed VCoNi alloys, exhibiting equiaxed grains and 
abundant annealing twins. The average grain sizes of D20, D4, and D2, measured via EBSD, were 20.3 μ m ( Fig. 1 a), 3.7 μ m ( Fig. 1 b), 
and 2.2 μ m ( Fig. 1 c), respectively. The KAM maps of the annealed specimens ( Fig. 1 d – f) display grains with low misorientation, 
indicating a fully recrystallized structure with low dislocation density. Fig. 1 g – i demonstrate that the recrystallized microstructure 
exhibits a nearly random texture, with the maximum multiples of random distribution measured to be 1.25, 1.09, and 1.52 for D20, D4, 
and D2, respectively, indicating a nearly untextured state ( Schneider et al., 2020 ; Wagner and Laplanche, 2023a ).

3.3. Discontinuous plastic flow behavior
Fig. 5 demonstrates the DPF behavior of the annealed VCoNi alloys at 4.2 K. The stress drop between the local maximum and 
minimum in the true stress-strain curve is plotted as a function of true strain ( Fig. 5 a) and true stress ( Fig. 5 b). The vertical lines 
indicate the onset of plastic instability. In all specimens, the magnitude of stress drop increases as true strain increases. A decrease in 
grain size amplifies the stress drop intensity, with finer-grained specimens exhibiting higher stress drops from the early stages of plastic 
deformation. In contrast, as shown in Fig. 5 b, the variation in stress drop magnitude with respect to true stress remains minimal across 
different grain sizes. The maximum stress drops prior to plastic instability are comparable, measured at 120 MPa, 135 MPa, and 136 
MPa for D20, D4, and D2, respectively. Notably, these values are significantly lower than those reported for austenitic stainless steels, 
which typically exhibit stress drops exceeding 300 MPa ( Zhang et al., 2020 ).
Fig. 5 c demonstrates the stress drop magnitude as a function of the strain burst, defined as the strain difference during a stress drop 
event. The proportionality between strain burst and stress drop follows a consistent trend, regardless of grain size, indicating that more 
significant stress drops correspond to more pronounced localized deformation at 4.2 K.

4.1.2. Strain hardening behavior
Despite the increased flow stress of the VCoNi alloy at 4.2 K due to enhanced solid-solution strengthening, the alloy exhibits 
improved strain-hardening capability and a superior strength – ductility combination ( Fig. 4 ). The strain-hardening capability of the 
VCoNi alloy is generally understood to originate from planar slip behavior facilitated by the glide of perfect dislocations. Similarly, in 
the present study, no evidence of deformation twinning was observed at room temperature (Supplementary Fig. 1 and Supplementary 
Fig. 3). The high friction stress in the VCoNi alloy hinders the conjugation of partial dislocations and restricts cross-slip, leading to a 
pronounced planar slip behavior ( Chung et al., 2022 ). This planar slip characteristic suppresses dynamic recovery, thereby promoting 
effective dislocation accumulation and, consequently, significant strain-hardening capability.

plastic deformation at 4.2 K. The activation of these mechanisms is typically linked to SFE, which governs the separation of partial

grain boundaries accelerates dynamic recovery due to dislocation absorption at grain boundaries ( Liu et al., 2023 ), which reduces the 
strain-hardening rate. In contrast, D20, with lower grain boundary density and reduced grain constraint, exhibits a relatively lower 
initial strain-hardening rate but maintains a high strain-hardening rate at larger strains.
The sustained strain-hardening capacity in the coarse-grained specimen is further influenced by deformation twinning and stacking 
faults. As shown in Fig. 9 b,c, the activation of stacking faults and deformation twinning refines the deformation substructures, 
reducing the mean free path of dislocations and introducing a dynamic H – P effect, which is strongly dependent on the interspacing 
between deformation twinning ( Schneider and Laplanche, 2021 ; Laplanche et al., 2017 ). This reduction in free path enhances effective 
dislocation accumulation and contributes to an extended strain-hardening regime at 4.2 K. Active deformation twinning in D20 acts as 
a secondary and synergistic mechanism that refines substructures and reduces the mean free path, thereby imparting a dynamic 
Hall – Petch contribution. This finding is in line with previous literature ( Laplanche et al., 2017 ) in CrCoNi and CrMnFeCoNi alloys, 
which demonstrated that early and active deformation twinning in CrCoNi promotes higher strain-hardening capability compared to 
that of CrMnFeCoNi. Accordingly, the more active twinning behavior in D20 compared to D4 and D2 is considered to play a key role in 
sustainable strain hardening.
In addition to deformation twinning, another key factor contributing to enhanced strain-hardening capability at cryogenic tem -
peratures is the suppression of dynamic recovery. Strain-hardening behavior is governed by the balance between dislocation gener -
ation and annihilation. At cryogenic temperature, due to low thermal fluctuations, dislocation annihilation through dynamic recovery 
is restricted, thereby leading to higher dislocation accumulation ( Tang et al., 2025 ).
In particular, at ultra-low temperatures approaching 0 K, the dynamics of screw dislocations also play a critical role in dynamic 
recovery. The glide of jogged screw dislocations requires the formation and subsequent annihilation of point defects such as vacancies 
( Tirunilai et al., 2020b ). However, the reduced lattice vibration and negligible diffusion at such temperatures hinder this defect 
compensation, thereby suppressing the mobility of screw dislocations. For a jogged screw dislocation to glide, the formation of point 
defects, such as vacancies, should accompany its motion. As a result, cross-slip events and dynamic recovery are substantially 
inhibited. Consequently, the VCoNi alloy deformed at 4.2 K exhibits a more pronounced dislocation accumulation and a significantly 
higher dislocation density compared to that deformed at 293 K ( Table 1 ).
Consequently, the strain-hardening behavior of the VCoNi alloy at 4.2 K is primarily governed by limited dynamic recovery, grain- 
size effects, activation of stacking faults and deformation twinning, and the suppression of screw dislocation glide. These factors 
collectively contribute to the exceptionally high strain-hardening capability of the VCoNi alloy at cryogenic temperatures, enhancing 
both strength and ductility.

4.2. Discontinuous plastic deformation
The occurrence of DPF at cryogenic temperatures is a critical concern for structural applications, as it is characterized by abrupt 
stress drops and localized plastic deformation. To elucidate the origins of DPF, two primary models have been proposed: the thermal 
instability model and the mechanical instability model.
The thermal instability model attributes DPF to localized softening due to heat dissipation during deformation ( Basinski and 
Hume-Rothery, 1957 ). At temperatures near 0 K, both heat capacity and thermal conductivity of metals decrease significantly. 
Consequently, the heat generated by deformation is not efficiently dissipated and instead accumulates within the material, leading to 
localized temperature rises that promote softening and trigger DPF. However, while experimental studies have observed localized 
temperature rises at extremely low temperatures, many investigations report that stress drop signals appear before any measurable 
temperature increase ( Naeem et al., 2021 ; Tabin et al., 2016 ). This suggests that the thermal instability effect alone cannot fully 
explain the onset of DPF.
In contrast, the mechanical instability model, as initially proposed by Seeger ( Seeger, 1955 ), regards DPF as a consequence of 
dislocation mechanics. This concept suggests that the dominant edge dislocation character at extremely low temperatures induces DPF. 
Experimental evidence for this mechanism was provided in austenitic stainless steels, where DPF was absent during martensitic 
transformation, which was accompanied by screw dislocation activity, but reappeared when such transformation was suppressed ( Obst 
and Nyilas, 1991 ). For H/MEAs, based on the mechanical instability model, Tirunilai et al. ( Tirunilai et al., 2020b ; Tirunilai et al., 
2022 ) proposed a dislocation-based framework to explain DPF and serrated behavior at temperatures approaching absolute zero. As 
discussed in Section 4.1.2 , the lack of thermal fluctuations at extremely low temperatures significantly reduces the mobility of jogged 
screw dislocations and suppresses their cross-slip propensity. At a critical temperature, where the stress required for cross-slip exceeds 
the activation threshold, piled-up dislocations overcome the lattice barriers through cooperative motion, known as avalanche slip. This 
transition from individual cross-slip to cooperative avalanche slip is the key factor that shifts from continuous to serrated plastic 
deformation, i.e., DPF. According to the dislocation-based model, increasing dislocation density leads to a reduction in the interspacing 
of jogs on screw dislocations, thereby increasing the stress required to activate cross-slip. Therefore, the frequency and magnitude of 
avalanche slip events increase with dislocation density, provoking a greater magnitude of stress drops. In the current VCoNi alloys, the 
magnitude of stress drops increases progressively with plastic strain ( Fig. 5 a), consistent with this dislocation-based framework and 
supporting a strong correlation between dislocation density and the severity of DPF.
The grain size dependence of DPF behavior of the current VCoNi alloys further supports this mechanism. In this study, specimens 
with smaller grain sizes exhibit greater stress drops at the early stages of plastic deformation and show a stronger sensitivity of stress 
drop magnitude to true strain ( Fig. 5 a). This trend can be attributed to the higher density of GNDs in fine-grained structures, as 
observed in Fig. 8 . As discussed in Section 4.1.2 , it is well established that GNDs in fine-grained structures accumulate actively from the 
onset of plastic deformation due to strain incompatibility across grain boundaries ( Cordero et al., 2016 ), which also cause discon -
tinuous yield behavior in the fine-grained D4 and D2 ( Fig. 2 ) ( Sinclair et al., 2006 ). Furthermore, the higher strain-hardening rates 
observed in the fine-grained VCoNi alloys during the initial plastic regime ( Fig. 12 d) are a consequence of this increased GND 
accumulation. From the perspective of DPF, such vigorous proliferation of GNDs leads to a substantial increase in total dislocation 
density, which can amplify the sensitivity of stress drop magnitude to plastic strain in the fine-grained specimens ( Fig. 5 a). Therefore, 
at extremely low temperatures, grain boundaries may play a critical role in facilitating the effective proliferation of GNDs and 
dislocation accumulation, thereby intensifying DPF behavior.
However, it should be noted that when the stress drop is plotted as a function of true stress ( Fig. 5 b), the variation among different 
grain sizes is relatively minor compared to that observed with true strain ( Fig. 5 a). Moreover, the maximum stress drop values 
measured prior to ultimate tensile strength are comparable across all specimens, with values of 120, 135, and 136 MPa for D20, D4, 
and D2, respectively. Given that the uniform elongation regions of the fractured D4 and D20 specimens exhibit similar dislocation 
density ( Table 1 ), these observations suggest that dislocation density primarily governs the magnitude of stress drop in single-phase 
VCoNi alloys rather than grain size effects alone.
Furthermore, although twinning activity varies with grain size during plastic deformation at 4.2 K and may indirectly influence 
DPF by contributing to dislocation density evolution, the present results suggest that its direct impact is limited, as evidenced by the 
comparable stress drop magnitudes and dislocation density observed just prior to the ultimate tensile strength of the D20 and D4. 
Notably, as dislocation density increases, the severity of DPF not only manifests as larger strain drops but also results in enhanced 
strain bursts during DPF events ( Fig. 5 c). This correlation highlights the role of dislocation dynamics in amplifying localized

5. Conclusion
In this study, the mechanical response of single-phase VCoNi alloys with different grain sizes was investigated at liquid helium 
temperature. The dependence of mechanical behavior on temperature and grain size, including DPF behavior at extremely low 
temperatures, was systematically elucidated. The key findings are summarized as follows: 
(1) At 293 K, the VCoNi alloy exhibits continuous plastic flow, whereas at 4.2 K, plastic deformation is accompanied by serrated 
stress-strain curve stress drops, indicative of DPF. Compared to 293 K, the yield strength at 4.2 K is significantly enhanced while 
maintaining ductility, and the strain-hardening rate is considerably improved, independent of grain size.
(2) The H – P relationship of the VCoNi alloys at 4.2 K was established, elucidating the contributions of solid-solution and grain- 
boundary strengthening to yield strength. The observed increase in yield strength is primarily attributed to the reduction in 
dislocation width with decreasing temperature, arising from limited thermal fluctuation. This effect enhances solid-solution 
strengthening, coupled with the inherent severe lattice distortion of the VCoNi alloy, leading to an exceptional solid-solution 
strengthening of 782 MPa at 4.2 K.
(3) At 4.2 K, the VCoNi alloy exhibits multiple deformation mechanisms in addition to dislocation glide, including nano-twinning 
and stacking faults, which are activated due to the enhanced flow stress. The coarse-grained VCoNi alloy shows a pronounced 
twinning activity, contributing to the sustained strain-hardening capability at 4.2 K. Furthermore, the restricted mobility of 
screw dislocations at extremely low temperatures suppressed dynamic recovery, promoting effective dislocation accumulation. 
This distinct deformation mechanism at 4.2 K leads to a higher dislocation density and an enhanced strain-hardening capability 
compared to 293 K.
(4) As plastic deformation progresses at 4.2 K, the magnitude of stress drops and strain bursts increase, indicating intensified DPF 
and localized deformation. The increase in stress drop magnitude with plastic strain is more pronounced in finer-grained 
specimens. The active generation of GNDs accelerates dislocation density evolution and induces intensified stress drops. 
However, the maximum stress drop at the plastic instability point remains similar across different grain sizes, suggesting that 
DPF behavior is primarily governed by dislocation density rather than grain size alone.
