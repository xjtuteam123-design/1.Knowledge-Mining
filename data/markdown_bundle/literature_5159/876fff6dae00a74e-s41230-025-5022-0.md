Abstract: The improvement of the morphology and distribution of ceramic particles is a crucial aspect in 
enhancing the high-temperature strength and service temperature of TiAl-based composites. In this study, 
the Er element was successfully used to modify the morphology of the Ti 2AlC reinforcing phase and improve 
room-temperature and high-temperature mechanical properties of the alloy. Results show that as the Er content 
increases from 0.04at.% to 0.08at.%, in-situ Er
2O3 particles are distributed at the surface of Ti2AlC reinforcing phase 
and within the lamellar colony. Both the lamellar colony and Ti2AlC reinforcing phase are significantly refined. The 
orientation relationship between Er 2O3 and Ti 2AlC is {111}Er2O3//{0001}Ti2AlC and {110}Er2O3//{1121}Ti2AlC. The higher 
surface energy of Ti2AlC and the favorable orientation relationship allow the Er 2O3 particles to nucleate on the 
Ti2AlC reinforcing surface. As a result, the growth of Ti 2AlC is restricted by the fine Er 2O3 particles, preventing 
its uncontrolled expansion and ultimately leading to the formation of fine, short rod-like structures. The fine 
Er
2O3 and Ti 2AlC act as nucleation sites for β grains, which is fundamental for obtaining fine lamellar colony. 
The Ti42Al6Nb2.6C0.08Er alloy exhibits superior room-temperature compressive properties with a compressive 
strength of 2,106 MPa and a compressive strain of 26.8%. The high-temperature compressive properties also 
demonstrate a great balance between strength and plasticity. The improvement in compressive properties are 
attributed to significant changes in microstructures, including the notable refinement of the lamellar colony, 
precipitation of Er
2O3 particles, load transfer effect of the refined Ti 2AlC reinforcing phase, and solid solution 
strengthening of Er solutes.

2 Experimental materials and methods
Sponge titanium (99.99%), aluminum particles (99.99%), 
aluminum niobium master alloy (75wt.% Nb), carbon 
powder, and high-purity erbium particles (>99.99%) were 
utilized as the raw materials of the Ti42Al6Nb2.6CxEr alloys 
(x=0.04, 0.06, 0.08, 0.1, 0.4, 0.8, in at.%, hereinafter referred 
to as 0.04Er, 0.06Er, 0.08Er, 0.1Er, 0.4Er, 0.8Er alloys). The 
vacuum non-consumable electric arc furnace was used to melt 
button ingots. Each button ingot weighed 80 g and solidified in 
a copper crucible chilled with water. The vacuum degree was 
maintained below 10
-3
 Pa until the completion of melting to 
prevent oxidation.
Each button ingot was remelted five times in an argon 
environment to guarantee composition consistency. To evaluate 
the microstructure, the button ingot was ground, polished, 
and chemically etched with Kroll reagent [5 HF+10 HNO
3+
85 H 2O (vol.%)] for 5 -7 s. An Olympus GX71 optical 
microscope (OM) was used to analyze the microstructural 
characteristics, and the Quanta 200F scanning electron 
microscopy (SEM) with an energy dispersive spectrometer 
(EDS) was utilized to examine microstructure evolution and 
element distribution. An X-ray diffraction examination was 
carried out to qualitatively examine the phase constitution, and 
the K
α spectrum of copper target was chosen, with a diffraction 
angle of 20°-90°. The interface characteristics of in-situ Er 2O3 
and Ti2AlC particles were further observed through Talos F200X 
transmission electron microscopy (TEM) analysis, including 
bright field (BF) image, high angle annular dark field (HAADF) 
image, select area electron diffraction (SAED) pattern, and 
scanning transmission electron microscopy (STEM).
Mechanical characteristics in this study included room-
temperature and high-temperature compressive properties. On 
the Instron 5569, a cylinder with a diameter of 6 mm and a 
height of 9 mm was deformed at a loading rate of 0.5 mm·min

800 °C, 900 °C, and 1,000 °C, and the loading rate was the

samples were measured for all the TiAl samples with different

3.4 Compressive properties at different 
temperatures of Ti42Al6Nb2.6CxEr alloys
The room-temperature compressive properties of the TiAl 
alloys with varying Er contents and the high-temperature 
compressive properties of the TiAl alloy with 0.08at.% Er are 
shown in Fig. 7. As the Er content increases from 0.04at.% 
to 0.08at.%, the room-temperature compressive strength

strength is associated with the continuous precipitation of

the optimal compressive properties are achieved when the

Therefore, the alloy exhibits a great balance between strength 
and plasticity at 800 °C. Excessive compression temperature 
not only accelerates dislocation motion but also accelerates 
oxidation of the alloy. As a result, the compressive properties

4 Discussion
4.1 Effect of Er content on microstructures of 
Ti42Al6Nb2.6CxEr alloys
As addition of Er element ranges from 0.04at.% to 0.8at.%, 
and the oxygen content is strictly controlled during the alloy 
preparation process, the content of Er
2O3 particles is low. 
According to previous study
[32]
, Er element has little effect on 
the nucleation of Ti 2AlC reinforcing phase. Based on the Al 
content in the alloy, the solidification path of the TiAl alloy 
follows the β-solidification mode. During the phase transition 
from liquid to solid, Ti
2AlC reinforcing phase preferentially 
nucleates from the liquid phase. As the process consumes Al 
elements, an Al-enriched dark gray region forms around the 
Ti
2AlC reinforcing phase. From the analysis of Figs. 5 and 6, 
it can be observed that nanoscale Er 2O3 particles are adsorbed 
onto the surface of Ti 2AlC reinforcing phase, where they 
subsequently grow. The Ti 2AlC reinforcing phases adsorbed 
by Er 2O3 particles are unable to grow further. Therefore, 
after the addition of Er element, the aspect ratio of Ti 2AlC 
reinforcing phases gradually decreases, and their morphology 
changes from elongated rod-like shapes to short rod-like 
shapes. Subsequently, β grains nucleate from the liquid phase 
at the sites of nanoscale Er
2O3 particles and refined Ti 2AlC 
reinforcing phases. The nucleation rate of β grains significantly 
increases, and the size of the β grains is greatly refined. When 
the temperature decreases to the β→α transformation region, 
α grains are also refined, which lays the foundation for the 
formation of small-sized lamellar colonies
[33]
.
As the temperature further decreases, the α→α 2+γ phase 
transformation involves the diffusion of Al elements via a 
stepwise mechanism, leading to the enrichment of Al elements 
at the boundaries of the lamellar colonies. As the Er content 
increases to 0.08at.% or above, the content of Er
2O3 particles 
no longer increases, and the remaining Er elements will 
continue to react with the Al elements at the lamellar colony 
boundaries to form the ErAl
3 phase. As a result, the relative 
content of γ lamellae decreases, and the relative content of α 2 
lamellae increases due to the consumption of Al. In addition, 
the doping of rare earth elements has been reported to delay 
the α→α
2+γ phase transformation, thus preserving a higher 
fraction of ordered α 2 phase
[34]
. This is because the atomic 
radius of Er is much larger than that of Ti and Al, and as a 
solute atom, it occupies lattice gaps or substitution sites, 
causing local lattice distortion. Lattice distortion forms an 
elastic strain field that hinders the long-range diffusion of 
Al atoms, thereby delaying the compositional segregation 
required for the α→α
2+γ phase transition. Er element reacts 
with oxygen to generate Er 2O3, pinning the α/α 2 interface 
and inhibiting the migration of the γ phase boundary. During 
the solidification process, the pinning effect and diffusion 
inhibition work together to preserve a more ordered structure 
of the α
2 phase under non-equilibrium condition. These 
factors promote the presence of more α 2 lamellae rather than γ 
lamellae within the lamellar colonies. The sharp-edged, brittle 
ErAl
3 phase, under the interface stress, weakens the bonding 
strength at the lamellar colony boundaries and ultimately 
becomes a stress concentration region, aggravating the 
accumulation and propagation of microcracks.

during the melting process, the temperature at which Er 2O3 
particles precipitate from the liquid phase is lower than 1,600 °C,
which is below the temperature at which Ti
2AlC reinforcing 
phase precipitates. Therefore, Er2O3 particles have no effect on 
the nucleation process of Ti 2AlC reinforcing phase. Compared 
to the liquid phase, the surface of the stable precipitated Ti2AlC 
reinforcing phase has a higher surface energy, making it an 
ideal site for the nucleation of Er
2O3 particles. Thus, Er 2O3 
particles tend to move to the surface of Ti 2AlC. These Er 2O3 
particles preferentially adhere to the growth active sites of 
Ti
2AlC through chemical adsorption, forming a physical 
barrier. This covering effect directly hinders the diffusion 
and adhesion of atoms Ti, Al, and C to the crystal surface of 
Ti
2AlC. Due to limited diffusion pathways, Ti2AlC grains cannot 
grow sufficiently, thus forming fine and more uniform structure. 
Figure 5(e) shows that there is an interface orientation 
relationship between Er
2O3 particles and Ti 2AlC reinforcing 
phase, with {111}Er2O3//{0001}Ti2AlC and {110}Er2O3//{1121}Ti2AlC, 
which also facilitates the development of Er 2O3 particles on 
the surface of Ti 2AlC reinforcing phase. In addition, Er 2O3 
particles, as highly thermal-stable phase, alter the local heat 
conduction, leading to uneven temperature fields at the growth 
front of Ti
2AlC reinforcing phase and inhibiting its coarsening. 
These three factors lead to the constraint of Ti2AlC reinforcing 
phase by fine Er 2O3 particles, preventing its free growth, 
ultimately resulting in the formation of finer short rods in the 
liquid region. As the temperature decreases to the solid-state 
phase transition stage, the refined Ti
2AlC reinforcing phase and 
fine Er 2O3 particles act as heterogeneous nucleation sites for 
the primary β phase. This increases the number of nucleation 
sites for β grains, thereby increasing the number of β grains 
and reducing the size of β grains. Therefore, the in-situ Er
2O3 
particles have a different refinement mechanism for Ti 2AlC 
particles compared to lamellar colonies. Essentially, in-situ 
Er
2O3 particles inhibit the growth of Ti 2AlC reinforcing phase 
while enhancing the nucleation rate of β grains.
4.3 Strengthening mechanisms of TiAl alloys 
dopped with Er
The improvement in compressive performance is primarily 
attributed to the significant changes in the microstructure: 
(1) significant refinement of lamellar colonies; (2) precipitation 
of nano-sized Er
2O3 particles; (3) load transfer effect of the 
refined Ti 2AlC reinforcing phase; and (4) solid-solution 
strengthening by Er atoms.
As heterogeneous nucleating particles, Er, Er 2O3 particles, 
and the Ti 2AlC reinforcing phase increase the undercooling 
of the TiAl melt, which promotes the nucleation rate of β 
grains. When atomic diffusion is suppressed with decreasing 
temperature, grain growth becomes constrained, and further 
grain coarsening ceases. The movement of grain boundaries 
requires atomic diffusion. When diffusion slows down, grain 
boundary migration becomes difficult, and grains are unable 
to grow by consuming smaller neighboring grains. Further 
considering the driving force and resistance to grain growth, 
it can be noted that the driving force for grain growth is 
the reduction of grain boundary energy. In other words, the 
system tends to lower its total energy by reducing the total 
grain boundary area. The migration of grain boundaries relies 
on atomic diffusion. When the diffusion rate decreases, the 
speed of grain boundary migration decreases, leading to 
a slowdown or even termination of grain growth process. 
In this way, during the solidification process of the TiAl 
alloy, the grains do not have enough time or driving force 
to continue growing, thus maintaining a smaller size. This 
results in a significant refinement of lamellar colonies at room 
temperature, increasing the boundary density of these lamellar 
colonies. Under the elastic interactions of dislocations and the 
short-range stress fields at the grain boundaries, dislocations 
are prevented from entering or passing through the boundaries 
and instead accumulate at the boundaries
[35]
. The increased 
grain boundary density allows for more dislocations to be 
accommodated, which helps to shorten the dislocation pile-up
length without reducing compressive strain
[37, 38]
. Prior to 
fracture, the stress required for dislocation motion must exceed 
the stress at the grain boundaries to sustain plastic deformation, 
thus significantly increasing the compressive strength of the 
TiAl alloys. Additionally, a greater number of lamellar colony 
boundaries reduce the stress concentration within individual 
lamellae, facilitating more uniform deformation of the 
lamellar colonies, thereby promoting stress redistribution. The 
refinement of lamellar colonies contributes to the improvement 
in both compressive strength and compressive strain.
It is known that Er dissolves as solute atoms in the TiAl 
matrix. Due to the difference in atomic radii among Er, Ti, 
and Al, lattice distortion occurs, which increases both the 
chaos degree and the internal energy of the system
[39, 40]
. The 
interaction between Er atoms and the stress field generated 
by the moving dislocations increases the resistance to 
dislocation motion, thereby raising the stress required for 
dislocation sliding. The excellent compressive strength of 
the TiAl alloy is closely related to the precipitation of Er
2O3 
particles. Er2O3 particles, which possess high elastic modulus 
and hardness, effectively hinder dislocation motion. Although 
some dislocations may escape from the pinning effect of Er
2O3 
particles, the stress generated by bending deformation weakens 
dislocation propagation
[41]
. Therefore, continuous dislocation 
slip requires higher stress, and the macroscopic plastic 
deformation of alloy demands higher external force, which in 
turn enhances the compressive strength.
The increase in compressive strength is also associated with 
the refined Ti
2AlC reinforcing phase. As one of the MAX-type 
ceramics, Ti 2AlC combines the excellent properties of both 
metals and ceramics. When Ti 2AlC is incorporated into the 
TiAl alloy, its aspect ratio directly influences the size of the β 
phase. Ti
2AlC with a small aspect ratio promotes the formation 
of fine β phase. Since Ti 2AlC has higher hardness than the

After adding Er, the distribution of Ti 2AlC reinforcing 
phases becomes more uniform, reducing the degree of stress 
concentration within the microstructure. As shown in Fig. 8, 
Ti
2AlC reinforcing phase exhibits bridging and pull-out 
effects. When cracks encounter Ti 2AlC, these reinforcements 
act like bridges, connecting the two sides of the crack, thereby 
preventing further crack propagation. When the interface 
bonding strength between Ti
2AlC reinforcing phase and 
lamellar colony is high enough, the refined Ti 2AlC reinforcing 
phase can effectively bridge cracks and improve the crack 
resistance of the alloy
[46]
. Furthermore, when cracks propagate 
and bypass Ti 2AlC reinforcing phases, these reinforcements 
are pulled out, consuming energy and slowing down the crack 
propagation rate. In addition, when cracks encounter Ti
2AlC 
reinforcements, multiple deflections occur, which further 
enhances the compressive performance.
Figure 9 shows the microstructure morphology of 
Ti42Al6Nb2.6C0.08Er alloy after compression deformation 
at 800 °C. As shown in Fig. 9(a), high-density nanotwins are 
generated in the γ lamellae, and there are abundant dislocations 
distributed between these nano-twins. Chen et al.
[47]
 reported
that after tensile deformation, three types of dislocations 
are formed in polysynthetic twinned TiAl single crystals, 
among which the super dislocation can be decomposed into 
two dislocations, accompanied by the formation of twins. 
Twinning is initiated through dislocation decomposition, 
at the same time, act to hinder dislocation movement. This 
mechanism mitigates severe work hardening and helps 
preserve high-temperature plasticity. In addition, it can be 
observed from Fig. 9(b) that the Er
2O3 particles distributed in 
the Ti2AlC reinforcing phase and TiAl matrix act as obstacles 
to dislocation slip, helping to maintain the high-temperature
strength. At the same time, the Ti
2AlC reinforcing phase 
undergoes morphological changes, as evidenced by the 
following features: (1) its short axis is about 200 nm, 
representing a significant reduction in size compared to the 
as-cast state; (2) its long axis exhibits a consistent orientation; 
(3) its morphology of Ti
2AlC is no longer regular rod-like, but 
instead displays a tortuous boundary. These characteristics 
indicate that during the high-temperature compression process, 
the Ti
2AlC reinforcing phase adapts to plastic deformation 
through self-fragmentation, refinement, and consistent 
orientation. Dislocations are emitted at the interface between 
Ti
2AlC reinforcing phase and γ phase. Most dislocations 
reduce interfacial stress concentration by sliding towards the 
γ phase, while a few dislocations can also slip into the interior 
of the Ti
2AlC reinforcing phase. This indicates that at this 
temperature, the slip system in Ti 2AlC phase is also activated, 
which is beneficial for enhancing the coordinated deformation 
ability of different phases. Therefore, the interaction between 
the second phases of Er
2O3 and Ti 2AlC and dislocations, as 
well as the nano twinning of γ lamellae, are the main reasons 
for the excellent strength-plasticity balance exhibited by 
Ti42Al6Nb2.6C0.08Er alloy at 800 °C.
It is worth noting that when the Er content exceeds 0.08at.%, 
the compressive properties at room temperature no longer 
increases, but instead decreases, which can be attributed to 
three aspects. One is the stress concentration effect of brittle 
ErAl
3 phase. The elastic modulus and fracture toughness of ErAl3 
phase are significantly different from those of the TiAl matrix. 
Under compressive load, stress concentration is prone to occur at 
the interface between ErAl
3 phase and matrix, which becomes a 
source of crack initiation. The second aspect is the weakening of 
the lamellae interface bonding and the obstruction of dislocation 
movement. During compression process, the interface between 
ErAl
3 and the lamellar colony is prone to delamination. ErAl 3 
phase impedes dislocation slip and climb, leading to dislocation 
accumulation at the phase boundary, causing local strain 
concentration and accelerating the nucleation of micropores. 
Thirdly, the integrity of the lamellar structure is compromised, 
with ErAl
3 phase occupying the lamellar colony boundaries, 
disrupting the continuity of the original α2/γ lamellae structure. 
The strengthening effect of the lamellar structure is weakened, 
resulting in a decrease in yield strength.

5 Conclusions
The addition of Er significantly alters the microstructure of 
the Ti42Al6Nb2.6C alloy. The in-situ formed nanoscale Er 2O3 
particles notably refine the lamellar colonies and Ti 2AlC 
reinforcing phase. The phase constitution and solidification 
path were analyzed using SEM, XRD, and TEM. The 
room-temperature and high-temperature compressive properties 
were tested, and the refinement mechanisms of the microstructure 
by Er were clarified. The improvement mechanisms of the 
refined Ti
2AlC and in-situ Er2O3 particles on the microstructure, 
as well as the strengthening mechanisms of the Ti42Al6Nb2.6C 
alloy, were ultimately revealed. The main conclusions are 
summarized as follows:
(1) As Er content increases from 0.04at.% to 0.08at.%, the 
average size of lamellar colonies decreases from 57.9 to 25.6 μm.
The decrease in aspect ratio of Ti
2AlC reinforcing phase 
leads to its refinement and more uniform distribution, thereby 
promoting overall microstructural refinement. 
(2) The high surface energy of Ti
2AlC reinforcing phase, 
along with the favorable orientation relationship between 
Er
2O3 and Ti 2AlC, facilitate the nucleation of Er 2O3 particles 
on the surface of Ti 2AlC. Therefore, the growth of Ti 2AlC is 
constrained by Er2O3, leading to the formation of finer, shorter 
rod-like structures. Both Er2O3 particles and refined Ti2AlC act 
as nucleation sites for β phase, which is the fundamental for 
obtaining finer lamellar colonies.
(3) Ti42Al6Nb2.6C0.08Er alloy exhibits superior compressive 
properties, with a compressive strength of 2,106 MPa and a 
strain of 26.8%. Its high-temperature compressive properties 
show great strength-plasticity balance, especially at 800 °C, the 
compressive strength reaches 1,587 MPa, and the compressive 
strain increases to 66.9%.
(4) The improvement in compressive properties is attributed to 
the significant changes in the microstructures, including significant 
refinement of the lamellar colonies, the precipitation of nanoscale 
Er
2O3 particles, the load-transfer role of the refined Ti 2AlC 
reinforcing phase, and the solid-solution strengthening of Er atoms.
