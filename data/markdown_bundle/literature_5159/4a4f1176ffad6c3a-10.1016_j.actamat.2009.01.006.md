A new phase-ﬁeld method for simulating c0 precipitation
in multicomponent nickel-base superalloys
T. Kitashima *, H. Harada
High Temperature Materials Center, National Institute for Materials Science, 1-2-1, Sengen, Tsukuba, Ibaraki 305-0047, Japan
Received 18 November 2008; received in revised form 18 January 2009; accepted 18 January 2009
Available online 14 February 2009
Abstract
The phase-ﬁeld method is used to develop a new technique to simulate c0 precipitation in multicomponent Ni-base superalloys. This
technique generates the Gibbs free energy for both the c and c0 phases from the single function formalism of both phases in the CALP-
HAD method without using conventional CALPHAD software. The Gibbs free energy surfaces for eight-element multicomponent sys-
tems are used to conﬁrm that there is an accurate coupling with the CALPHAD method, because they compare favorably with calculated
results from Thermo-Calc. In addition, c0 precipitation in a third-generation Ni-base superalloy containing eight components is simu-
lated in one dimension with anti-phase eﬀects of the c0 phase on the precipitate interactions. The simulation results reveal late-stage
enrichments of Re and W in the c phase during coalescence of the c0 precipitates.

1. Introduction
Modeling microstructure evolution is important for eﬃ-
cient alloy design as well as for understanding underlying
phenomena in phase transitions. The phase-ﬁeld method
has been used to predict c
0 precipitation from a c matrix
in Ni-base superalloys [1–8]. These calculated results were
in good agreement with experimental data. However, such
previously reported calculations were focused on binary
Ni–Al [1–6] or ternary systems, e.g. Ni–Al–Mo [7] and
Ni–Al–Cr [8], even though commercial Ni-base superalloys
often incorporate as many as 10 elements, such as Co, Cr,
Mo, Re and Ru. Therefore, there have been few examples
of the phase-ﬁeld method being used to design commercial
multicomponent Ni-base superalloys. In order to design
such multicomponent superalloys eﬃciently, it is essential
to understand the eﬀects of the content of each element
on microstructure evolution, such as the temporal change
of the morphology and size distribution of c
0 precipitates,
in addition to the phase instability attributed to the exces-
sive alloying (which possibly degrades creep strength).
One reason for the diﬃculty with multicomponents
associated with the phase-ﬁeld method is the complexity
of deriving a chemical free-energy function that depends
on the element content in a multicomponent system. There
have been some simulations coupling with the CALPHAD
(calculation of phase diagrams) method to provide the
chemical free energy for the phase-ﬁeld method in the mul-
ticomponent order/disorder solid-state phase transitions
[7–13]. In the CALPHAD method, the Gibbs energy func-
tion of an ordered phase (such as the c
0 phase) is usually
described using a sublattice model with element site frac-
tions. This site fraction is not used as a variable in the
phase-ﬁeld method. Therefore, the degree of freedom of
the element site fraction was reduced to pass thermody-
namic information obtained from the CALPHAD method
to the phase-ﬁeld method. This was achieved as follows: (i)
linking to a dataﬁle that contains the Gibbs energy and
equilibrium conditions calculated from the CALPHAD

Although each of these three methods has advantages

systems. To the authors’ knowledge, previously simulated
c

to binary and ternary systems [1–8].
The authors have previously used a Gibbs energy single
formalism for c/c0 phases with a four-sublattice model for
ternary Ni–Al–Cr [14] and quaternary Ni–Al–Re–Ta [15]
systems. In these simulations, the order parameter of each
element corresponded to long-range ordering in the c
0
phase, because the order parameter was expressed using
the element site fractions of a four-sublattice model in
the CALPHAD method. This approach was based on a
report describing a binary Ni–Al system [2]. Nevertheless,
there was still a gap in the number of elements between
these model alloys and the typical multicomponent superal-
loys that are of commercial interest. This paper develops a
new technique for simulating c
0 precipitation in eight-ele-
ment Ni-base superalloy systems without using conven-
tional CALPHAD software.
There have been only a few reports on the c
0 precipitate
interaction with anti-phase eﬀects in commercial multicom-
ponent systems. Even with careful experiments, it is diﬃ-
cult to observe such a time series of precipitate
interactions by tracking one precipitate. There have been
many atom probe analyses focused on the local equilibrium
at c and c
0 interfaces, and the morphology and size of the c0
precipitates, e.g. Refs. [16–21]. However, the temporal
change in concentration during precipitate interactions
was not previously reported. In the current study, the tem-
poral evolution of element concentration during coales-
cence of the c
0 precipitates in a third-generation Ni-base
superalloy system (i.e. TMS-75 [22]) is simulated and
discussed.

tendency of Ta to occupy an Al site. Indeed, Ta showed a
signiﬁcant occupation of Al sites even at, and adjacent to,
the inter-phase interface in the c phase, as shown by the
curve labeled A in Fig. 4 . While the order parameter of
Ta resulted in the preferential occupation of an Al site near
the interface even in the c phase, the average concentration
of the lattice sites was the same as that of the bulk of the c
phase at these sites. This observation indicates the diﬀer-
ence in the interface width and position between the chem-
ical and structural proﬁles. In contrast, Mo, Cr and W
showed a wide inter-phase interface in the c

example, this result is shown for W by the curve labeled
Bi n Fig. 4 . These observations suggest that the ordering
degrees for Mo, Cr and W in the c

interface are not as large as in the bulk of the c0 phase,
although the average concentration of lattice sites is the
same as the bulk. These proﬁles are characterized by the
Gibbs free energy surface obtained by the CALPHAD
method.

4. Summary
A new multicomponent phase-ﬁeld method was devel-
oped which is useful for simulating c0 precipitation from
a c matrix in multicomponent Ni-base superalloys. The
Gibbs energy of the c and c0 phases in eight-element sys-
tems was successfully obtained by employing the CALP-
HAD method without using conventional CALPHAD
software. The order parameter of each element in the alloy
was expressed using its element site fraction in a four-sub-
lattice model. It was therefore possible to distinguish four
diﬀerent domains of the c
0 phase in a multicomponent
microstructure. This capability also allowed anti-phase
eﬀects to be accounted for. The multicomponent phase-
ﬁeld method was further used to simulate c
0 precipitation
in TMS-75, a third-generation Ni-base superalloy. This
method demonstrated both diﬀusion-controlled c0 growth
under local equilibrium at the c/c0 interfaces and precipita-
tion interactions (such as coalescence). The simulated tem-
poral evolution of element concentrations during c0
coalescence suggested that Re- and W-enriched c channels
could be formed during late-stage microstructure
evolution.
