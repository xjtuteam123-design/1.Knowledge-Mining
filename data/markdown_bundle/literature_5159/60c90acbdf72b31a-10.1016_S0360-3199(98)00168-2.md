Abstract
This paper presents research of the hydrogen gas e/C128ects on physical and mechanical properties of the
precipitation-hardened nickel alloys EP741 and EP99 at pressures up to 65 MPa and temperatures up to 840 8C. It
has been shown that conventional yield strength, tubular-shaped sample fracture stress and long-term strength are
not practically a/C128ected by hydrogen. The characteristics of alloy ductility are more susceptible to hydrogen e/C128ect.
EP741 alloy is especially susceptible to the hydrogen environment e/C128ect at temperature of 460 8C, and EP99—at
temperatures of 20 8C and 460 8C. Hydrogen permeability relationships for alloys in a wide range of temperature and

1. Introduction
Due to their high temperature-strength, processabil-
ity and rather high level of mechanical properties,
nickel-based alloys are considered to be preferable
structural materials (SM) for energetic units operating
on hydrogen fuel.
The mechanical properties of structural materials are
a/C128ected signiﬁcantly in hydrogen gas. This leads to the
ductility reduction, ‘hydrogen embrittlement’ [1], which
can result in an early sudden fracture of structures
exposed to hydrogen gas environment.
This study presents both the results showing hydro-
gen gas e/C128ect on mechanical properties of high-tem-
perature Ni-alloys EP741 and EP99, and the pressure
(up to 65 MPa) and temperature (up to 840 8C) depen-
dence of hydrogen permeability in these alloys.

Cylindrical specimens (after short-term tensile tests)
and tubular-shaped specimens (tested for carrying ca-
pacity) were subjected to metallographical analysis.
Examination of the cylindrical specimens after the ten-

cracks were considered to be initiation sites of the spe-

i.e. when a higher degree of HE was observed. After
high-temperature tests carried out in the range of 700

the region of break-up.

4. Conclusion
Modes of HE for EP741 and EP99 are typical for
steels and alloys of high nickel concentration (see [1,4–
7]). More complete review of various types and mech-
anisms of HE of metals has been presented in the
monograph by Kolachev [1]. According to this study,
the HE of EP741 and EP99 can be characterized as an
embrittlement caused by the hydrogen environment
(the 7th mode of embrittlement). For this type of HE,
the following mechanisms, that were also observed in
the study of the high-pressure hydrogen e/C128ect on
EP741 and EP99 alloys properties are typical:
. HE is observed in a deﬁnite temperature range. At
rather high temperatures HE is not displayed;
. in tests on smooth specimens, the reduction of area
and elongation decrease with the increase of hydro-
gen pressure;
. when a notch is done, the susceptibility of steels and
alloys to hydrogen gas e/C128ect increases drastically
(EP741’s insensitivity to notch can be probably
explained by the fact that the concentration of stres-
ses is not critical for this alloy);
. the higher hydrogen pressure, the higher the HE;
. harmful e/C128ect of hydrogen is observed in plastically
deformed zones of the specimens only.
Some HE theories have been proposed to describe the
mechanisms given above. Nowadays, the preferable
one is considered to be the dislocation theory of re-
versible HE. According to this theory, hydrogen atoms
produce Cottrell atmospheres on the dislocations. At
higher stresses, these dislocations begin their motion
by absorbing hydrogen atoms. In the regions, where
the dislocations nucleate, i.e. on the grain boundary or
near other obstacles, the hydrogen concentration
reaches maximum for the cracks to be initiated and
propagated.This theory helps to explain the tempera-
ture range of HE e/C128ect for the tested alloys. In ad-
dition, transport of hydrogen atmospheres by
dislocations is possible at velocities signiﬁcantly higher
than the lattice di/C128usion (0100-fold increase for auste-
nitic steels, nickel and its alloys), that is considered to
be the reason for HE in EP741 and EP99 during
short-term tensile tests.
