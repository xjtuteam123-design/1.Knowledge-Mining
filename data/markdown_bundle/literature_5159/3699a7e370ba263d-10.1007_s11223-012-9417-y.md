HIGH-TEMPERATURE STRENGTH OF REFRACTORY METALS,
ALLOYS AND COMPOSITE MATERIALS BASED ON THEM.
PART 2. MOLYBDENUM AND NIOBIUM ALLOYS
V. K. Kharchenko and V. V. Bukhanovskii UDC 539.4
The results of investigations of the mechanical characteristics of molybdenum and niobium alloys
and their welded joints, which are used in space-rocket hardware, have been generalized. The
investigations were carried out under short-time, sustained static and low-cycle loading conditions
on small time bases in a temperature range of 290–2270 K.
Keywords: molybdenum and niobium alloys, short-term strength, long-term strength, low-cycle strength, second
creep, plasticity, high temperatures, correlation.
Introduction. Molybdenum and niobium alloys, which possess a unique set of physicomechanical,
technological and operational characteristics, are widely used in aircraft equipment and space-rocket hardware (SRH)
in the manufacture of parts and assemblies which are acted upon by high loads and temperatures, aggressive gaseous
media and other unfavorable factors. The basic advantages of modern molybdenum and niobium alloys, in
comparison with tungsten, are higher plasticity and weldability that allows one to create welded structures of intricate
shapes. Moreover, they possess high reliability and cost-effectiveness. In early 1980s, considerable achievements in
the field of metallurgy and metal science of refractory materials have been attained. Due to development of vacuum
electrometallurgy, molybdenum and niobium alloys with quite low content of interstitial impurities have been
produced. On the other hand, successful solution of technological problems in welding of refractory metals by the
scientists of the Paton Institute of Electric Welding of the National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine opened wide
prospects of their application for manufacturing of high-duty welded structures for SRH [3].
This article is devoted to generalization of results of investigations of the mechanical characteristics of
molybdenum and niobium alloys and their welded joints, which are used in SRH, under short-time, sustained static
and low-cycle loading conditions on small time bases in a wide temperature range (up to 2270 K) performed at the
Pisarenko Institute of Problems of Strength, National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine (further – IPS).
Mechanical Characteristics of Alloys under Short-Term Static Tension and High-Cycle Loading. In
1980s, the USSR industry started to produce weldable molybdenum low alloys TsM-6, TsM-10, TsM-12, MI-5, 4604
and 4605 of Mo–Zr–B, Mo–Al–B, Mo–Zr–Hf–B, Mo–Re, Mo–V–C, and Mo–Ti–C systems, respectively, as well as
refractory niobium alloys 5VMTs and 10VMTs of Nb–W–Mo–Zr system and their modifications with additional
alloying by tantalum, vanadium, rhenium, carbon and boron in the form of rolled sheets, forged bars, tubes and other
profiles. These alloys were produced by vacuum-arc and electron beam fusion with the subsequent high-temperature
forming of sheets or bars with intermediate high-temperature annealings and stabilizing thermal treatment at the
technological process final stage.
At the same time, the IPS started to conduct studies of mechanical characteristics of such materials using
plane, cylindrical and tubular specimens cut out from the base metal, metal of weld and metal of welded joints in the
temperature range 290 to 2270 K (Figs. 1–4).

The effect of structural condition of molybdenum and niobium alloys, as well as technological factors
controlling this condition (high-temperature treatment, welding technique and welding regime parameters) on
characteristics of short-term strength and plasticity of base metal, metal of weld and welded joints has been
investigated. It has been revealed that welds of niobium alloys, due to formation of intrinsic cellular structures in
them, manifest high plasticity and have the same strength as the base metal within the total investigated temperature
range. For molybdenum alloys the equal strength of welded joints and base metal occurs only at temperatures above
1770 K, which is attributed to brittleness of weld zone cast metal at room and elevated temperatures [4–17]. Based
on this finding, recommendations for the choice of optimum regimes of high-temperature treatment and welding of
the specified alloys have been made.
Fatigue resistance characteristics of thin-sheet molybdenum and niobium alloys and their welded joints in
various structural conditions have been also investigated on long-term bases up to 11 0 6/c215 cycles, which has been
necessitated by the request of maintenance of integrity of welded SRH structures during their railroad transportation
[18–20]. Based on the test results, correlation relationships between characteristics of short-term strength, endurance
and grain sizes of TsM-6 and TsM-10 molybdenum alloys, as well as 5VMTs and 10VMTs niobium alloys, have
been established, which made it possible to provide essential reduction of expenses for conducting further research
studies.

The analysis of experimental results on cyclic creep and long-term strength (Figs. 5–7) of rolled sheet metal
of 4604, TsM-6, and TsM-10 molybdenum alloys shows that low-cycle fatigue curves and critical cyclic creep rate
diagrams can be adequately described using the data on static creep and long-term strength at the same temperatures,
taking advantage of the equivalent stress concept and the linear hypothesis of damage accumulation [21, 23, 28].
Considering that a number of high-duty structural components of SRH operate under conditions of exposure
to high-temperature oxidative and aggressive gas media, research studies have been conducted on estimation of the
effect of rocket propellant combustion products on structure and mechanical characteristics of 10VMTs niobium
alloy, and characteristics of high-temperature strength, plasticity and creep of 5VMTs niobium alloy with silicide
ceramic coatings in vacuum, inert medium and in air have been determined [30–33].
The analysis of the experimental data obtained made it possible to establish correlation relationships
between characteristics of short- and long-term static strength, low-cycle strength and creep of the investigated
molybdenum and niobium alloys within certain thermal-mechanical loading ranges corresponding to (0.5–0.8) Tmelt ,
where plastic deformation and material fracture are controlled by the same prevailing physical processes and
mechanisms. Analytical dependences have been proposed for the description of these correlations, and it has been
shown that in some cases they have a common pattern, can be described by a unified curve and are irrelevant of the
material chemical composition and structural condition [34–36].

Strength in a Complex Stress State. The performed studies of the strain-hardening laws and critical
plasticity of molybdenum alloys in a complex stress state have revealed that the stress state type essentially affects
strength and plasticity of an alloy [37–41], especially within low-temperature range (160 to 290 K). Superposition of
the second tensile component at temperatures lower than 200 K results in the reduction of both strength and plasticity
of alloys by approximately an order of magnitude. Within high-temperature range, the effect of stress state type is
less considerable. Thus, in contrast to low-temperature range, the hypothesis of the existence of a unified
deformation curve in “stress intensity–strain intensity” coordinates has been proved to be valid at temperatures 290
to 1520 K (Figs. 8 and 9).
The limiting condition of fracture in high-temperature area is adequately described by the maximum tangential
stress criterion, while none of the available criteria of strength has been found to be applicable for calculation of
limiting conditions within low-temperature range. Therefore, bearing capacity of structural components from
molybdenum alloys should be assessed on the basis of the experimental data obtained under particular conditions of
thermal and mechanical loading. The analysis of experimentally obtained curves of fracture of molybdenum and its
alloys has shown that temperature increase results in the change of location and pattrern of the limiting curves,
which suggests phase transformations in metals, due the effect of both temperature, and stress state type.
A strength criterion controlling the limiting condition of anisotropic molybdenum-basedd alloys within a
wide temperature range has been proposed [39]. It has been established that anisotropy of pre-strained molybdenum
alloys, which is observed at room temperature, remains within the total investigated temperature range, whereas the
anisotropy exhibits a sharp increase within the interval of average homological temperatures (0.35–0.45) Tmelt .
