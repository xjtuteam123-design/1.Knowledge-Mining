ABSTRACT
The effects of Y addition on microstructure and mechanical property of Nb-1Zr-0.1C alloy were investigated. 
Electron backscattered diffraction results show that no recrystallization was observed in the warm-rolled and 
annealed Nb-1Zr-0.1C alloys with different Y additions. In the cold-rolled and annealed Nb-1Zr-0.1C alloy with 
0.1 % Y addition, Y
2
O
3 
was continuously distributed along the grain boundaries of the Nb matrix that had 
undergone complete recrystallization. While as the Y addition increased to 0.4 %, Y
2
O
3 
restored a granular 
distribution in the cold-rolled and annealed Nb-1Zr-0.1C alloy. The addition of Y element to the Nb-1Zr-0.1C 
alloy could enhance both the strength and plasticity to a certain extent. Compared with the as-cast and warm- 
rolled and annealed samples, the Nb-1Zr-0.1C alloy with different Y additions after cold-rolling and annealing 
exhibited a higher work hardening rate.
1. Introduction
Refractory metal Nb and its alloys are promising candidates for 
structural applications in high-temperature nuclear reactors due to their 
exceptional high-temperature strength, long-term thermal creep 
strength, ease of fabrication, and relatively low neutron absorption 
cross-section [1,2] . Nb-1Zr alloy which is created by introducing 
0.8 – 1.2 % zirconium into Nb has been used in space power systems 
[3 – 6] . The addition of Zr enhances the material strength through both 
solid solution strengthening and precipitation strengthening while 
maintaining the plasticity of the Nb matrix [7,8] . However, compared to 
Nb-W, Nb-Mo, and Nb-Ta alloys, the Nb-1Zr alloy may not meet the 
service requirements for high-power space reactors due to the lower 
room temperature strength and higher temperature creep strength 
[9,10] .
Due to strong binding energy with O, the rare-earth element Y can be 
added into Nb alloys to form the body-centered cubic (BCC) structure

thereby enhancing the room temperature strength and high-temperature 
creep resistance [11] . Thence, in this study, we added 0.1 % and 0.4 % Y 
to the Nb-1Zr-0.1C alloy to create a new Nb-1Zr-0.1C + Y alloy and the 
effects of Y addition on the microstructure and mechanical properties of 
Nb-1Zr- 0.1C were in-depth investigated.

size distribution displayed in Fig. 2 (e) indicate that a typical completely 
recrystallized microstructure with an average grain size of about 50 μ m 
was observed in the cold-rolled and annealed Nb-1Zr-0.1C alloy with 
0.1Y addition.

4. Conclusion
In this study, effects of Y addition on the microstructure and me -
chanical properties of Nb-1Zr-0.1C alloy were investigated. Experi -
mental results show that the Nb-1Zr-0.1C alloy with 0Y, 0.1Y and 0.4Y 
additions possess typical as-cast microstructure with an average grain 
size of about 500 µ m . No recrystallization was observed in WR & A Nb- 
1Zr-0.1C alloys with different Y additions. After CR & A treatment, 
Y
2
O
3 
phase was continuously distributed along the grain boundaries in 
the Nb-1Zr-0.1C alloy with 0.1Y addition, contributing to the recrys -
tallization of the Nb matrix during the annealing process. The addition 
of Y to the Nb-1Zr-0.1C alloy could enhance both the strength and 
plasticity to a certain extent. Additionally, the Nb-1Zr-0.1C alloy with 
different Y additions exhibited a higher work hardening rate after cold- 
rolling and annealing.
