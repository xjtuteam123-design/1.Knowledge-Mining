The strength-ductility (or thermal conductivity) trade-off is unavoidable in most conventional alloys due 
to the limited composition space. Herein, we propose a mechanism-based design strategy for concurrent 
optimization of high temperature (HT) strength, room temperature (RT) ductility, and HT thermal con- 
ductivity by considering atomic size misﬁt for solid-solution hardening and lattice distortion, and valence 
electron concentration for shear instability. As a test case, we extend the composition space of binary W- 
Ta alloys to W-Ta-V-Ti-Cr refractory high entropy alloys (RHEAs), and the present RHEAs (e.g. WTaVTiCr) 
exhibit superior HT strength (1210 ±43 MPa at 1073 K) and RT ductility (23.4 ±5.7%). Further, we show 
that the thermal conductivities of the present RHEAs increase with increasing temperature, and theoret- 
ically can reach to ~40% of that of pure W at 20 0 0 K. This work thus provides a general design rule of 
RHEAs that enables effective utilization of compositional complexity for potential applications.

With high melting points and superior wear resistance, re- 
fractory metals (group IVB, VB, VIB elements) have been widely 
used as machining materials and for metalworking applications 
[1] . Their high melting points (1941–3683 K) provide an advan- 
tage as potential high temperature (HT) structural materials, as the 
operation temperature of Ni-based superalloys is limited to about 
1400 K owing to the low melting point of Ni (1728 K) [2] . Yield 
strengths of the pure refractory elements, however, dramatically 
decrease with increasing temperature (Fig. S1) [3] while those of 
Ni-based superalloys can retain at high temperatures (e.g. Inconel 
718: above 10 0 0 MPa until 80 0 K [2] ). This rapid softening can 
be compensated by introducing additional strengthening mecha- 
nisms such as solid-solution hardening or precipitation hardening. 
Preceding research has shown that refractory high entropy alloy 
(RHEA) with the body-centered cubic ( BCC ) structure can main- 
tain high σy (60 0–80 0 MPa) until 1700 K, which is much higher 
than that of Ni-based superalloy [4] . It is thus important to de- 
velop refractory metal-based alloys with HT strength and RT duc- 
tility for the application of refractory metals. In this paper, HT in- 
dicates the temperature range of 0.2 to 0.5 times melting temper- 
ature (e.g., 50 0–130 0 K for WTaVTiCr). Adding small amount of el-

This work is inspired by two recently proposed beneﬁts of con- 
centrated refractory alloys for HT strength and RT ductility, which 
are: 1) increasing HT strength by introducing the edge dislocation- 
mediated solid-solution hardening mechanism that is insensitive at 
HT [ 7 , 8 ], and 2) increasing RT ductility by increasing the shear in- 
stability of the lattice of group VIB refractory metals [9] . The latter 
mechanism for ductility requires suﬃcient movement of the Fermi 
energy away from the minimum of density of state, and hence re- 
quires signiﬁcant amount of alloying elements (e.g. 30 at.% of Ta 
for W) [ 10 , 11 ]. Therefore, both HT strength and RT ductility can be 
improved simultaneously in concentrated refractory alloys when 
the key variables of each mechanism are properly considered. 
This paper introduces a design concept of multicomponent re- 
fractory metal-based alloys—so-called RHEAs—for simultaneously 
increasing HT strength and RT ductility. The key variables of the 
aforementioned mechanisms—large atomic size misﬁt parameter 
( ε a ) for high solid-solution hardening and low valence electron 
concentration (VEC) [12] for high shear instability—are discussed 
in terms of their effects on strength and ductility. Thermal con- 
ductivity ( κ) is additionally considered, as it is often a topic of in-

The model alloys of the present study are WTa-based RHEAs 
with Ti, V, and Cr as additional elements. W and Ta have the high- 
est melting points among the refractory elements, and they form 
complete solid solution with each other. The WTa-based RHEA sys- 
tem, thus, is one of the best candidates to potentially create a com- 
position that would have the highest melting point among the fu- 
ture RHEAs. Ti, V, and Cr have the lowest melting points among 
the refractory elements. WTa-based HEAs with the three elements 
thus can provide the rock bottom of the high-temperature proper- 
ties of WTa-based RHEAs; WTa-based RHEAs with other refractory 
elements such as Zr, Nb, Mo, and Hf can have better HT properties 
than the present RHEAs. The alloy ingots from pure W to WTa- 
based RHEAs were fabricated by arc-melting equipment. Vickers 
micro-hardness was measured on the ﬁnal polished sample under 
1 kg load. Compressive tests were conducted using an Instron-type 
testing machine at a strain rate of 10 −3 s −1 from 77 K to 1073 
K. The tests were stopped when the compressive strain exceeded 
30%. Rectangular specimens with dimensions of 2 mm ⅹ 2 mm ⅹ 4 
mm were used for the tests. κ was calculated as a product of ther- 
mal diffusivity ( α), speciﬁc heat (C p ), and density ( ρ) [14] . See sup- 
plementary information for detailed experimental methods. 
We start to establish the design criteria from the W-Ta binary 
alloy system. In a binary alloy system, the solid-solution hardening 
effect is expected to be the highest at the center region, since it is 
proportional to the difference between the two elements (atomic 
size difference and modulus difference). On the other hand, the 
ductility is expected to monotonically increase by adding the so- 
lute element with low VEC, as shear instability has inverse rela- 
tionship with VEC in refractory alloys due to the Jahn-Teller dis- 
tortion effect [9] . Therefore, one may expect that the simultane- 
ous increase of strength and ductility may be achieved in the W 
(r = 139 pm, VEC = 6)-rich compositions, where the VEC decreases 
and the atomic size misﬁt parameter increases with adding Ta 
(r = 146 pm, VEC = 5). Fig. 1 a shows the hardness of the W-Ta bi- 
nary alloys depending on the W concentration. Additional hard- 
ness, /Delta1H is estimated by the subtraction of the measured hard- 
ness from the rule of mixture of the hardness values of pure Ta 
and W. The center region (40–60 at.% W) has higher /Delta1H owing to 
the solid-solution hardening effect. Com pressive σy also shows the 
similar trend ( Fig. 1 b). On the other hand, deviating from the VEC 
prediction (the higher Ta, the higher ductility), compressive strain 
is minimal at the center region; the W-rich region, where the VEC 
is between 5.6–6, shows decrease of ductility with decreasing VEC. 
Previous DFT studies showed that atomic-row displacement energy 
(which relates to the stiffness for moving an atomic row in the dis- 
location core) [15-18] and resultant Peierls stress which impedes 
the motion of dislocations (and thereby reduces ductility) [18-21] , 
exhibit maxima at ~25 at.% Ta [21] . The mismatch between the VEC 
and the compressive strain in center region is thus expected to be 
caused by additional effects of deformation defects (i.e. dislocation 
core) with high Peierls stress. These effects become pronounced for 
alloys with high VEC close to group VIB (VEC = 6).

from Fig. 1 . First, increase of both strength and ductility can- 
not be successfully achieved in the W-Ta binary alloy system. 
The design space has to be extended to RHEAs to increase the 
degree-of-freedom of choosing elements. Second, using the VEC

Two indications are drawn from Fig. 2 b and c. First, although 
a rough correlation, σy increases with increasing ε a ( Fig. 2 b). In 
particular, addition of Cr (which is smallest among the alloying el- 
ements in this study) signiﬁcantly increases ε a and contributes to 
the highest σy of WTaVTiCr. Screw dislocation has been known to 
govern the solid-solution hardening mechanism in dilute BCC al- 
loys due to its low mobility. However, recent studies suggested that 
large atomic size difference in BCC HEAs can decrease the mobil- 
ity of edge dislocations, while kink nucleation of screw dislocation 
may be facilitated due to local chemical heterogeneity [27-29] . As a 
result, solid-solution hardening in some BCC HEAs (especially HEAs 
with large atomic size misﬁt) can be mediated by edge dislocation, 
where atomic size misﬁt parameter becomes an important factor. 
The correlation between σy and ε a in Fig. 2 b supports the recent 
proposal of edge-dislocation mediated solid-solution hardening of 
RHEAs [27] . 
Second, compressive strain shows a good inverse correlation 
with VEC in the given VEC range (5–5.6, Fig. 2 c). In particular, the 
addition of Ti (which exhibit smallest VEC (~4) among the alloying 
elements in this study) decreases the total VEC of the alloy and 
signiﬁcantly increases compressive strain of WTaVTiCr. It should be 
noted that WTaVTiCr RHEA simultaneously exhibit highest σy and 
largest RT ductility among the alloys in the present study, over- 
coming strength-ductility trade-off. We expect that simultaneous 
addition of both Ti (largest element) and Cr (smallest element) in- 
creases the strength by increasing ε a , and low VEC of Ti contributes 
to the large compressive strain by decreasing VEC of the alloy. 
There may be additional effects from microstructure (i.e., Hall- 
Petch strengthening, dendritic microstructure) on strength and 
ductility. Due to the large grain sizes (Fig. S2), however, the Hall- 
Petch strengthening is expected to be less than few tens MPa; Ac- 
cording to [30] , increasing the grain size from 38 μm to 128 μm of 
HfNbTaTiZr RHEA decreases the yield strength only ~20 MPa (from 
960 to 940 MPa). In addition, the large fraction of both DR and 
ID in quaternary and quinary RHEAs (Table S1) may render the 
overall properties (strength, ductility) the average between DR and 
ID, as the deformation cannot be localized into a speciﬁc region. 
This argument is supported by the previous report that the as- 
cast (dendritic microstructure) and homogenized (equiaxed grain) 
NbTaTiV RHEA samples exhibit almost identical deformation be- 
haviors during the compression test [31] . Therefore, although the 
detailed strain localization behaviors can be affected by the mi- 
crostructural effects (see Supplementary Materials for the detailed 
discussion), here we focus only on the average compositional ef- 
fects and the resultant intrinsic properties.

Fig. 3 e summarizes the measured σy of the selected alloys as a 
function of temperature. σy of pure W abruptly decreases down to 
~100 ±10 MPa with increasing temperature until 673 K. However, 
WTaVTi and WTaVTiCr exhibit a much-delayed reduction behav- 
ior, which means that they have substantial resistance to the soft- 
ening at high temperatures. It should be noted that WTaVTi and 
WTaVTiCr RHEAs have lower melting points and lower VECs than 
those of W or W 60 Ta 40 alloy, which has an effect in the direction 
of lowering the relatively HT strength. Thus, it can be understood 
that the exceptional resistance of softening of present RHEAs at 
HT originates from their large ε a values, which generates the edge 
dislocation mediated strengthening in RHEAs. In the edge dislo- 
cation mediated strengthening model [27] , strength exponentially 
decrease with increasing temperature. Therefore, the yield strength 
can be less sensitive to the temperature in the present HT range in 
Fig. 3 e (0.2 to 0.5 times melting temperature).

above 0.5 times melting temperature. Jog and dipole strengthening, 
which hinders the motion of screw dislocations and dominates the 
screw-dislocation mediated solid-solution hardening, is weakened 
at HT due to the formation of thermal vacancies. However, the pin- 
ning of edge dislocation comes from the atomic-level complexity 
(here, atomic size difference, large ε a ) of RHEAs, which is not sig-

Fig. 3 f shows the HT σy (1073 K) and RT ductility (293 K) of 
various RHEAs [ 23 , 26 , 39-43 ] (Inconel 718 is added for compari- 
son [44] ); ε a , VEC, and calculated melting temperature are listed 
in Table S4. Since the studies of mechanical properties at HT in 
previously reported RHEAs are few, multiphase RHEAs are also in- 
cluded. Generally, HT strength and RT ductility exhibit a severe 
trade-off relationship, which may be attributed to the low Peierls 
stress of RHEAs with low VEC; for example, NbTiV 2 Zr and NbTiVZr 
[41] have low HT strength due to the combination of their low VEC 
values and low melting temperatures. On the other hand, alloys 
having moderate ductility (compressive strain of ~10–20 %), such 
as MoNbHfZrTi [43] , HfMoNbTaTiZr [42] , and the present RHEAs 
do not show an apparent correlation between HT strengthand RT 
ductility. Indeed, the superior HT strength over 1210 ±43 MPa (at 
1073 K) and RT ductility over 23.4 ±5.7% of present WTaVTiCr indi- 
cates the potential of the present design strategy for overcoming 
strength and ductility trade-off. Therefore, further investigations 
are required to independently control the strength and ductility of 
RHEAs at various temperatures, by properly considering ε a , VEC, 
and melting temeprature. 
Fig. 4 a shows the measured κ of the developed RHEAs as a 
function of temperature. Pure W, W-Ta binary alloys, and some 
conventional superalloys are added for comparison [45-47] . RHEAs 
have smaller κ ( < 50 Wm −1 K −1 ) compared to pure W (178 
Wm −1 K −1 ) at RT. In particular, WTaVTiCr RHEAs exhibit the low- 
est κ ~13.8 Wm −1 K −1 among the alloys in present study, which 
is similar value of Cantor HEA ~13.6 Wm −1 K −1 . Thus, WTaVTiCr 
RHEAs exhibit the highest σy / κ ratio (~115) reported up to date, 
which exceeds that of the Cantor high-entropy alloy [14] by nearly 
a factor of four. 
Fig. 4 b shows κ and σy at RT and 1073 K as /Delta1S mix of the 
alloys in the present study. The /Delta1S mix value of each alloy was 
listed in Table 1 . The smaller κ of RHEAs at RT are attributed 
to their severe lattice distortion, and hence to the large atomic 
size misﬁt. The decrease of κ is thus unavoidable when design- 
ing RHEAs with high strength as shown in Fig. 4 b. An interesting 
observation in Fig. 4 is that the κ of RHEAs (as well as W-Ta bi- 
nary alloys) increase with increasing temperature (positive slope of 
/Delta1κ/ /Delta1T), while the κ of pure W continuously decreases (negative 
slope of /Delta1κ/ /Delta1T). From RT to 1123 K, κ of pure W continuously 
decreases from 178 Wm −1 K −1 to 117 Wm −1 K −1 ( Fig. 4 b). However, 
κ of alloys with high solute contents increases; W 60 Ta 40 : 56 → 80 
W/mK, WTaV: 25 → 47 Wm −1 K −1 , WTaVTi: 15 → 33 Wm −1 K −1 , 
WTaVTiCr: 13.8 → 30 Wm −1 K −1 ( Fig. 4 b), which is identical to that 
in 3d transition metal-based HEAs. The ratio between the κ of pure 
W and other RHEAs thus increases with increasing temperature; 
for example, the ratio between the κ of pure W and WTaVTiCr 
increases from ~8% at RT to 26% at 1073 K. This increasing trend 
persists, for example, 40% at 20 0 0 K and, in theory, creates an in- 
tersection where the comparative advantage of the κ value in pure 
W is reversed, which can be predicted using a rough extrapola- 
tion (cubic b-spline method). We thus expect that one may de- 
velop new RHEAs with combined exceptional properties such as 
both high σy and high enough κ via proper optimization of the 
compositional complexity, which will merit further investigation. 
In summary, this study proposes a design strategy of refrac- 
tory alloys to simultaneously optimize the conﬂicting properties by 
extension of the composition space from binary alloys to RHEAs. 
Unlike W-Ta binary alloys, the present RHEAs (e.g. WTaVTiCr) ex- 
hibit superior HT strength (1210 ±43 MPa at 1073 K) and RT duc- 
tility (23.4 ±5.7%), which can be obtained via optimization of VEC 
(5–5.6) and large atomic size misﬁt parameter (0.035–0.045). Al- 
though WTaVTiCr RHEA exhibits low κ, it increases with increasing 
temperature (positive slope of /Delta1κ/ /Delta1T). Thus, the κ of WTaVTiCr 
RHEA (~13.8 Wm −1 K −1 at RT) may reach to the 40% of the κ of 
pure W at 20 0 0 K. This work thus provides general design rules of 
RHEAs that enable effective utilization of atomic level information 
to design RHEAs with tailored properties.
