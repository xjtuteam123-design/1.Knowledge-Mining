High‑temperature compression behaviour and phase analysis 
of additively manufactured Ti45V45Al5Cr5 medium‑entropy alloy
Pradyumn Kumar Arya1 · Neelesh Kumar Jain1  · Dan Sathiaraj1 · Ajit Kumar2
Received: 20 April 2024 / Accepted: 29 October 2024 / Published online: 11 November 2024 
© The Author(s), under exclusive licence to Springer Nature Switzerland AG 2024
Abstract
This manuscript describes study of solidification behaviour, high-temperature compression behaviour, phase evolution, and 
microstructure of novel lightweight Ti45V45Al5Cr5 medium-entropy alloy (MEA) developed by micro-plasma powder 
additive manufacturing (µ-PPAM) process. Solidification behaviour of Ti45V45Al5Cr5 MEA predicted by Thermo-Calc 
simulation indicated complete solidification of BCC and HCP phases at temperatures up to 2000 K and 900 K, respectively. 
Previously identified optimum parameters of µ-PPAM process were used for multi-layer deposition of the MEA. The devel-
oped MEA showed 590 MPa compressive strength at 900 °C which is enabled by solid solution strengthening, formation of 
the precipitates, reduced grain growth, and phase decomposition at the elevated temperatures. Its microstructural analysis 
revealed the presence of two distinct phases with overall BCC crystal structure of Ti–rich and V-rich phases. Its elemental 
colour mapping indicated that V-rich phase is enriched with Cr and Ti–rich phase is enriched with Al. The developed MEA 
will be useful in aerospace applications requiring high-temperature strength.
Keywords High-temperature compression · Titanium; Medium entropy alloy · Additive manufacturing
1 Introduction
The ever-growing emphasis on addressing the issue of cli-
mate change, particularly by reducing energy consump-
tion and carbon dioxide emissions, is a significant driving 
force behind the development of lightweight, high-strength, 
and environmentally friendly materials. It has made devel-
opment of lightweight materials possessing exceptional 
high-temperature strength as a pivotal research area in the 
aerospace, automotive, manufacturing, and energy storage 
industries due to potential of these materials to significantly 
improve performance, efficiency, and safety in these indus-
tries [1, 2]. Several lightweight alloys based on aluminium, 
titanium, and magnesium have gained widespread adoption 
across numerous industries due to their higher strength-
to-weight ratio [3 –6]. However, strength of these alloys 
reduces significantly for the operating temperature beyond 
600°C, thus considerably reducing their use in the high-
temperature working environment. Therefore, researchers 
are constantly exploring design and development of novel 
lightweight materials which are capable of withstanding 
high-temperature working environment. Recently, develop-
ment of lightweight high-entropy alloys (HEA) and medium-
entropy alloys (MEA) have gained significant attention due 
to their exceptional mechanical properties and versatility. 
Some developed lightweight MEA exhibited low densities 
(2.7 to 4 g/cm3) but experienced reduction in their strength 
at elevated temperatures [7 , 8], whereas some developed 
lightweight MEA and HEA exhibited excellent mechanical 
strength at high temperatures but possessed higher densities, 
i.e., above 5.8 g/cm3 [9, 10]. Design and development MEA 
involving Ti and V with carefully controlled amount of Al 
and Cr such that formation of brittle intermetallic phases is 
avoided [11] will have very good strength-to-weight ratio, 
corrosion resistance, and good biocompatibility thus mak -
ing it well suited for various demanding applications. It can 
also be observed from the past works [7–10] that vacuum arc 
melting, powder metallurgy, and laser-based additive manu-
facturing process have been used to develop the lightweight 
HEA and MEA. These processes have some limitations, such 
as poor mechanical properties of the developed material,

2 Materials and methods
Titanium and vanadium have been selected as the matrix 
for the lightweight Ti45V45Al5Cr5 MEA due to their same 
crystal structure, and good solubility to form a single solid 
solution phase and 5 at.% of Al and Cr each was selected 
to benefit from their solid solution strengthening effect and 
at the same time mitigating the risk of formation of brittle 
intermetallic phases.
2.1 Solidification behaviour and preparation 
of powders of Ti45V45Al5Cr5 MEA
Solidification behaviour of Ti45V45Al5Cr5 MEA was pre-
dicted by the Thermo-Calc simulation. Figure  1a depicts 
Scheil solidification of Ti45V45Al5Cr5 MEA showing vari-
ation in mole fraction of its solidified phases with decrease 
in temperature, whereas Fig.  1b presents its equilibrium 
solidification depicting change in volume fraction of its 
different phases formed as a function of its temperature. 
Powders of titanium, vanadium, chromium, and aluminium 
were acquired from Loba Chemie Pvt Ltd Mumbai. These 
powders were used in a planetary ball milling machine to 
prepare the powder of Ti45V45Al5Cr5 MEA. A ratio of 
2:1 of tungsten carbide ball to an alloy powder and 200 rpm 
speed was used in the planetary ball milling machine. The 
moisture content of the prepared powder of each alloy 
was removed by heating it in a muffle furnace at 80 °C for 
25 min. Figure  1c presents scanning electron microscopic 
(SEM) images showing morphology of the prepared powder 
particles of Ti45V45Al5Cr5 MEA.
It can be seen from the Scheil solidification diagram 
(Fig. 1a) that entire liquid phase of Ti45V45Al5Cr5 MEA 
solidifies into the body centred cubic (BCC) phase at 
1580 K. The equilibrium solidification diagram (Fig.  1b) 
reveals that the formation of the BCC phase initiates at 
2000 K temperature and continues up to 500 K with the 
mole fraction attaining a value of 0.5, and that formation 
of HCP phase starts at 800 K temperature and continues 
up to 500 K with the mole fraction value of 0.55. Table  1 
summarizes values of the thermodynamic and Hume–Roth-
ery parameters for Ti45V45Al5Cr5 MEA. Negative value 
of enthalpy of mixing ‘ΔHmix’, positive value of entropy 
of mixing ‘ΔSmix’, smaller value of atomic size difference 
‘δ’ (≤ 6.6%), and high value of thermodynamic parameter1 
‘Ω’ (> 1) support the formation of a solid solution phase in 
Ti45V45Al5Cr5 MEA [12]. Value of valence electron con-
centration (VEC) being less than or equal to 6.87 indicates 
formation of BCC phase [13].

2.2 Multi‑layer deposition and sample preparation 
of Ti45V45Al5Cr5 MEA
Powder of Ti45V45Al5Cr5 MEA was prepared in a plan-
etary ball milling machine by mixing the commercially 
available powders of Ti, V, Al, and Cr procured from Loba 
Chemie Pvt Ltd, Mumbai (India). The prepared powder was 
dried in a furnace at 90 °C for 30 min to remove its mois-
ture content. In-house developed 5-axis computer numerical 
controlled (CNC) machine for µ-PPAM process was used for 
fabricating multi-layer deposition of Ti45V45Al5Cr5 MEA 
using the optimum parameters identified by Arya et al. for 
Ti6Al4V5Cr alloy [14]. Values of these parameters are: 418 
W powder for µ-plasma arc formation, 51 mm/min as travel 
speed of μ-plasma torch, and 2.9 g/min as gravimetric pow-
der flow rate of Ti45V45Al5Cr5 MEA. Optimum values of 
other parameters identified by Sawant and Jain for Ti6Al4V 
alloy [15], namely, 8 mm as stand-off distance, 0.3 Normal 
litre per minute (Nl/min) as argon gas flow rate for μ-plasma 
arc formation; and 5.0 Nl/min as argon gas flow rate for 
melt pool protection were used. A detailed description of 
the CNC machine for µ-PPAM process can be found in [14, 
15]. Figure 2 shows photograph of the fabricated multi-layer 
deposition of Ti45V45Al5Cr5 MEA. It consists of 8 deposi-
tion layers achieving thus total deposition height of 24 mm. 
It can be observed from Fig.  2 that though the multi-layer 
deposition has good bonding between the adjacent layers, 
but it has poor surface finish and some waviness implying 
that additional finishing processes will be needed to achieve 
the desired surface finish and geometrical accuracy.
A cuboid sample of 10 mm × 10 mm × 1 mm size was 
cut from the multi-layer deposition of Ti45V45Al5Cr5 
MEA at the middle of its deposition height by wire electric 
discharge machining (WEDM) process to study its phase 
evolution, microstructure, and elemental mapping. The cut 
sample was polished with SiC emery papers up to a grit 
size of 2500, followed by final polishing with a 1–1.5 μm 
fine alumina solution on velvet cloth. Evolution of different 
phases was analyzed using an X-ray diffractometer (XRD) 
(model: PANalytical from Empyrean, The Netherlands) at 
room temperature using λ radiation having 1.54 Å wave-
length, value of 2θ angle in a range from 20° to 100°. The 
peaks corresponding to various phases and their crystal-
lographic planes were identified using the JCPDS dataset. 
Kroll’s etchant (containing 6 ml HNO3 + 2 ml HF + 92 ml 
DI water) was used to etch the polished sample to reveal its 
microstructure using scanning electron microscope (mode: 
JSM-7610FPlus from JEOL, Japan) and their elemental 
colour mapping was obtained using the energy dispersive 
X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) analysis. A cylindrical sample 
of 2 mm diameter and 3 mm length was cut from the multi-
layer deposition of Ti45V45Al5Cr5 MEA by WEDM pro-
cess for its high-temperature compression tests performed 
at 900 °C, 1000 °C, and 1100 °C using strain rate of 0.001 
per second on the Gleeble 3800 thermo-mechanical simula-
tor. Each sample was heated to the required test temperature

3 Results and discussion
3.1 Evolution of phases
Figure  3 shows the XRD graphs of the Ti45V45Al5Cr5 
MEA revealing a single BCC solid solution phase. This is 
evidenced by the presence of four distinct diffraction peaks 
corresponding to the (011), (002), (112), and (200) crys -
tal planes without detection of any secondary peaks. High 
value mixing entropy promotes this single-phase structure 
[9] which is also supported by Scheil solidification diagram 
(Fig. 1a).
3.2 Microstructure analysis
Figure  4a presents the SEM images of Ti45V45Al5Cr5 
MEA showing its microstructure and Fig.  4b depicts its 
elemental mapping obtained by EDX.
Microstructure of Ti45V45Al5Cr5 MEA (Fig. 4a) reveals 
that it comprises of binary phase morphology having black-
colored Ti–rich phase and a grey-colored V-rich phase. This 
two-phase microstructure can be a cause of inhomogene-
ous distribution of constituent elements of Ti45V45Al5Cr5 
MEA. The elemental mapping of Ti45V45Al5Cr5 MEA 
(Fig.  4b) also shows that distinct Ti–rich phase (green 
color areas) and V-rich phase (red color areas) form pri-
mary matrix of this alloy. Chromium (purple color) and 
aluminum (blue color) are distributed throughout these 
phases indicating their incorporation into both Ti–rich and 
V-rich regions. Though Cr is slightly enriched in the V-rich 
phase and Al is slightly enriched in the Ti–rich phase. This 
elemental segregation is due to the differences in the solu-
bility of these elements in their respective phases which is 
driven by their similar crystal structures (body-centered 
cubic for Cr and V, hexagonal close packed for Al and Ti). 
Additionally, thermodynamic and kinetic factors during 
solidification contribute to such this elemental segregation. 
It enhances the high-temperature compressive strength of 
Ti45V45Al5Cr5 MEA by promoting solid solution strength-
ening and improving thermal stability. The distribution of 
Cr and Al in Ti45V45Al5Cr5 MEA suggests that these ele-
ments might form fine precipitates which help to enhance 
mechanical properties of Ti45V45Al5Cr5 MEA through 
precipitation hardening. It is supported by the findings of 
Duan et al. [20] who have reported that presence of Al and 
Cr precipitates within the Ti–rich and V-rich phases sig-
nificantly enhanced mechanical properties of AlCoCrFeNi2.1 
alloy by impeding movement of dislocations thus providing 
precipitation hardening.
3.3 High‑temperature compression behaviour
Figure  5a shows compressive stress–strain curves for the 
Ti45V45Al5Cr5 MEA at a temperature of 900 °C, 1000 °C, 
and 1100 °C obtained using strain rate of 0.001 per second 
and Table 2 presents their average values of the compressive 
strength at these temperatures. It can be observed in Fig. 5a 
that maximum compressive strength of Ti45V45Al5Cr5 
MEA is obtained at 900 °C as 590 MPa, but it decreases 
to 296 MPa and 97 MPa at 1000 °C and 1100 °C, respec -
tively. Compressive strength in a single-phase alloy is pri-
marily governed by two mechanisms, namely, constituent 
element strengthening and solid solution strengthening. 
Higher compressive strength of Ti45V45Al5Cr5 MEA at 
the elevated temperature can be explained by the following 
factors: (a) The incorporation of vanadium atoms into the 
titanium lattice and vice versa, along with chromium and 
aluminium atoms which induce lattice strain. This lattice 
strain impedes movement of dislocations thereby increas-
ing compressive strength of the MEA at elevated tempera-
tures. Similar observation has been made by Kang et al. [16] 
and Qiu et al. [17] that incorporation of various alloying 
elements induces lattice strain in alloys, thereby hinder -
ing movement of dislocations and enhancing compressive 
strength at high temperatures, and (b) presence of multiple 
elements in Ti45V45Al5Cr5 MEA enhances its thermal sta-
bility through its phase decomposition and reducing its grain

4 Conclusions
This work was aimed to develop novel lightweight 
Ti45V45Al5Cr5 MEA for high-temperature application 
in aerospace industries. Maximum compressive strength 
of 590 MPa is achieved at 900 °C due to solid solution 
strengthening, formation of precipitates, reduced grain 
growth, and phase decomposition at elevated temperatures. 
Developed MEA has a single BCC solid solution phase and 
microstructural analysis indicates binary phase morphology 
consisting of two distinct phases, namely, Ti–rich phase and 
V-rich phase. Ti–rich phase is enriched with Al due to their 
similar HCP crystal structure which promotes solid solu-
bility of Al in Ti–rich phase and V-rich phase is enriched 
with Cr due to their similar BCC crystal structure which 
promotes solid solubility of Cr in V-rich phase. Such a dis-
tribution of Cr and Al in these phases will help in improving 
the high-temperature compressive strength Ti45V45Al5Cr5 
MEA as compared to other Ti-based alloys, thus making it 
suitable high-temperature applications in aerospace, auto-
mobile, and manufacturing industries.
