ABSTRACT 
Multi-principal element alloys (MPEAs) exhibit different degrees of intrinsic lattice distortion, which varies with 
constituent species. Using atomistic simulations, here we found the mobilities of both edge and screw disloca -
tions are dependent on lattice distortion in body-centered-cubic (BCC) MPEAs. Lattice distortion influences not 
only the activation of kink nucleation and propagation governing screw dislocation motion, but also the strength 
of local pinning for edge dislocations. As a consequence, the disparity between the velocities of screw and edge 
dislocations decreases with increasing lattice distortion. We further demonstrate that lattice distortion can be 
taken as an indicator to correlate with the reported brittleness of BCC MPEAs. This implies that maximizing 
lattice distortion could be a plausible strategy to improve the ductility for brittle BCC MPEAs. 
Refractory multi-principal element alloys (MPEAs) with body- 
centered cubic (BCC) structure have attracted considerable attention 
due to their superior mechanical properties at elevated temperatures [ 1 , 
2 ]. For example, the compressive yield stress could exceed 1 GPa at 
1200 K in CrMoNbV, far beyond the traditional Ni-based superalloys [3] . 
However, BCC MPEAs usually suffer from low ductility at room or low 
temperatures, which significantly degrades their formability for appli -
cations. For the case of the well-known NbMoTaW alloy, it encounters 
severe brittleness with a fracture strain less than 3% at room tempera -
ture, although its compressive strength can reach 405 MPa at around 
1900 K [4] . 
In comparison to traditional metals, there exists intrinsic distortion 
that varies lattice by lattice in MPEAs due to the mismatch of atomic size 
and elastic modulus. The interaction of lattice distortion with disloca -
tions could dramatically alter mechanical properties [ 5 – 9 ]. Local 
distortion has been reported to produce strengthening, and promote 
kink nucleation in screw dislocation for BCC MPEAs [ 9 , 10 ]. The glide of 
edge dislocation could be pinned by the local strain pronouncedly [11] . 
Several studies have revealed the contribution of edge dislocation to 
strength, aside from the conventional dominant role played by screw 
dislocations [12] . In addition, a HfNbTaTiV alloy with relatively severe 
lattice distortion [13] was reported to exhibit a good ductility in com -
bination with a high strength. This indicates lattice distortion might 
affect ductility as well. 
Earlier studies have demonstrated that the dislocation dynamics 
show distinct difference in BCC MPEAs in comparison to conventional 
BCC metals [ 14 – 17 ]. For example, screw dislocations are observed in 
experiments to be severely pinned and curved upon sliding due to the 
large lattice distortion in NbTiZrHf alloy [18] . In addition, the 
non-screw type dislocations are widely observed during plastic defor -
mation in NbTiMo alloy [12] . The local stress fields could even drive 
edge dislocation to glide on high-order slip planes due to the strong local 
pinning force [12] . As such, with the intrinsic lattice distortion, the 
contribution of edge and screw dislocations to plastic deformation 
should be reassessed in BCC MPEAs. This could affect their ductility 
consequently, considering that the relative mobility of screw to edge 
dislocation influences the ductile-to-brittle transition in BCC metals 
[19] . More interestingly, there is a high degree of freedom to tune lattice 
distortion in the vast composition hyperspace of MPEAs. Therefore, 
there is a pressing need to establish a general picture to correlate 
dislocation dynamics with the magnitude of the intrinsic lattice 
distortion. 
Here we show that the intrinsic lattice distortion imposes a sub -
stantial effect on both edge and screw dislocation mobilities in BCC 
MPEAs. Weak lattice distortion induces a large disparity between the 
mobilities of edge and screw dislocations, reminiscent of the scenario in 
conventional BCC metals and alloys. By contrast, their mobilities 
become comparable in severely lattice-distorted alloys. We further

The movement of screw dislocation is based on the kink-pair 
mechanism (see supplementary Fig. S7). Local distortion could pro -
mote kink nucleation to minimize elastic energy. The kinks are trapped 
at the energetically favorable sites. The density of spontaneously 
nucleated kink increases as intrinsic lattice distortion becomes stronger 
(see supplementary Note 5), i.e. a large number of kinks are generated in 
TaNbHfZr alloy in comparison to NbMoTaW and NbTiZr alloys. Starting 
from the initial configuration (grey lines), Fig. 2 e – g compare the 
movement of screw dislocation at a given time under the low (blue lines) 
and high (red lines) stresses for three alloys at 300 K. The motion of 
kinked dislocation requires to overcome local pinning sites, generating 
debris left behind upon sliding. Moreover, differently oriented kinks 
could meet to form cross-kinks, which act as the pinning obstacle for 
dislocation motion [30] . The depinning of cross-kinks also generates 
debris, in particular point defects. The severely pinned dislocation seg -
ments limit the dislocation mobility. With a higher distortion, the den -
sity of debris increases pronouncedly ( Fig. 2 g). Here we use the density 
of debris atoms, i.e. the total number of atoms in debris per slide area, to 
characterize the pinning effect caused by lattice distortion on dislocation 
motion. We found that the density goes up as lattice distortion becomes 
larger, as shown in Fig. 2 h. This confirms that lattice distortion could 
significantly alter the mobility of screw dislocation.

example, a high density of dislocations was observed in NbTaTiV alloy 
during plastic deformation experimentally [8] . With our simulation re -
sults above showing that the speeds of screw and edge dislocations can 
be regulated by lattice distortion, there should exist a correlation be -

To sum up, our present work shows that the dislocation mobility is 
significantly influenced by the degree of intrinsic local lattice distortion 
in BCC MPEAs. The large disparity of edge and screw dislocation mo -
bilities known for conventional BCC metals is reduced with increasing 
lattice distortion in the BCC MPEAs. By correlating lattice distortion 
with experimental room-temperature ductility, we found large lattice 
distortion usually indicates a good ductility for BCC (equiatomic) 
MPEAs. This general trend could help guide the search for desired me -
chanical properties, by judicious design in the vast compositional space 
available for BCC MPEAs.
