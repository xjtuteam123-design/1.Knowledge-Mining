All investigated alloys exhibit remarkable age hardening by precipitation of phase with D019 crystal structure and phase with bco crystal structure even at aging temperatures higher than 2000C. Introduction Effective utilization of the high specific strength of mag- nesium alloys in the automobile industry entails the devel- opment of alloys that retain high strength at high temper- atures. Yttrium and heavy rare earth elements have been found to raise the heat resistance of magnesium by precip- itation of metastable phases that remain stable even at tem- peratures higher than 2000C.l-5) Furthermore, magnesium al- loys containing gadolinium and yttrium have higher strength at high temperatures than conventional aluminum and mag- nesium alloys 6-8) These alloys also possess high corrosion resistance.9'10) The suitability of the alloys for practical use has been investigated by forging the alloys into cup and pis- ton shapes, 11' 12) and the high temperature tensile properties of the hot-forged pistons have also been evaluated. The forged pistons exhibit properties similar to those of cast samples and they still posses tensile properties superior to those of the conventional heat resistant WE54A alloy at elevated temper- atures. 13) However, the particular contributions of each ele- ment to various properties of the alloys have not been clearly understood. This is necessary in order to determine the opti- mum composition for specific applications. Therefore, in this research, Mg��Gd��Y��Zr alloys containing different Gd:Y mole ratios of 1:0, 3:1, 1: l, 1:3 and 0:1 with a constant Y+Gd content of 2.75 mol% were prepared and the dependencies of aging characteristics and tensile properties on the content of each element were investigated from room temperature to 3000C. Cast and hot rolled samples of the al- loys were used. 2. Experimental Procedure Mg��Gd, Mg��Gd��Y and Mg��Y master alloys were first melted using magnesium, gadolinium and yttrium of 99.9% purity. Melting was conducted in high frequency induction furnace under argon atmosphere, and the melt was poured into a mold held at room temperature. In all in- vestigated alloys tested at room temperature and 2000C, there are many deformation twins in the grains. Cracks, which orig- inate at the twins and propagate along the grain boundaries, are also observed. However, such twins are not observed in specimens tested at 3000C. This implies that elongation of the specimens also depends on the easiness of twin forma- tion. On the other hand, grains of specimens tested at 3000C are extended to the tensile direction and large cavities are also formed at the grain boundaries. This suggests that the coars- ening of precipitates and easy gliding on non-basal planes at high temperatures facilitate the deformation of grains, result- ing in intergranular fracture and an increase in elongation. 4. Conclusions In this paper, the effect of Y:Gd ratio on age hardening characteristics and tensile properties of cast and hot-rolled Mg��Gd��Y��Zr alloys having different Gd:Y ratios of 1 3: 1, 1: l, 1:3, and with a constant Gd+Y content of 2.75 mol% has been investigated in order to identify the specific roles of gadolinium and yttrium in improving the high temperature strength of the alloys. The results obtained are summarized as follows: (1) Mg��Gd��Y��Zr alloys indicate remarkable age harden- ing by precipitation of F" phase with D019 crystal structure and F' phase with bco crystal structure. (2) The precipitates contributing to age hardening are finer and their amount increases as gadolinium content in- creases, resulting in increased peak hardness, tensile strength and 0.2% proof stress. On the other hand, high yttrium con- tent results in high elongation. (3) Hot rolling refines the grain size and leads to fine pre- cipitates contributing to age hardening, thereby, improving the tensile properties. (4) The tensile strength and 0.2% proof stress of rolled- T5 specimens of G17K, GY122K, and GY94K alloys at all test temperatures are higher than those of WE54A alloy, which is the most heat resistant magnesium alloy already in existence. However, the tensile strength and 0.2% proof stress of GY56K and YIOK alloys are only higher than those of WE54A at temperatures above 2000 C.
