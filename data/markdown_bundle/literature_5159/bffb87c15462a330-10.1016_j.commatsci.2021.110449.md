ABSTRACT 
γ / γ’ phase interfaces are hidden troubles that threaten the service safety of Ni-based single crystal superalloys 
due to their thermodynamic instabilities and lower mechanical strengths. To gain insights into the role of hy -
drogenated vacancy in the stability and cohesion of γ / γ’ interfaces, the formation mechanism of hydrogenated 
vacancy at (0 0 1) γ -Ni/ γ’ -Ni
3
Al interface and its influences on interfacial stability and fracture strength were 
systematically studied by using first-principles calculations. Our results show that the short-range interplay 
between monovacancy and single hydrogen atom at (0 0 1) γ -Ni/ γ’ -Ni
3
Al interface causes the formation of Vac-H 
complex structure (i.e., hydrogenated vacancy) as separated by a certain distance rather than a direct recom -
bination. The hydrogen atom in the first nearest-neighbor interstices of Ni vacancy (site 6) deviates from the 
interstitial centers to the vacancy, owing to the interattraction between monovacancy and hydrogen atom. The 
formation of hydrogenated vacancy at (0 0 1) γ -Ni/ γ’ -Ni
3
Al interface can promote the interfacial thermodynamic 
stability as compared with the vacancy-containing γ / γ’ interface, but weaken the interfacial fracture strength and 
change the preferable fracture place of γ / γ’ interface. The underlying mechanisms of stabilizing and weakening 
effects on the γ / γ’ interface were revealed by using spin-polarized density of states and differential charge 
densities. 
1. Introduction 
Ni-based single crystal superalloys are widely used to produce the 
turbine blades of advanced aero-engines due to their excellent high- 
temperature strengths and creep resistances [1 – 3] . To meet the higher 
performance requirements for advanced aero-engines, the volume of 
γ’ -Ni
3
Al (L1
2
) precipitates in many high-performance Ni-based single 
crystal superalloys usually account for up to 70%, resulting in tens- 
nanometers-wide γ -Ni phase channels and a considerable amount of 
γ -Ni/ γ’ -Ni
3
Al phase interfaces formed in the superalloys [4] . Besides the 
great yield strength of γ’ -Ni
3
Al precipitates, which can prevent dislo -
cations from directly cutting through γ’ phases, the γ / γ’ phase interfaces 
also play an essential role in the creep deformation process of superal -
loys, because they can absorb parts of dislocations and have a certain 
pinning effect on the motion of dislocations [5] . 
The γ / γ’ interfaces are thermodynamically unstable due to the 
presence of lattice mismatch and chemical composition mutation in 
interfacial regions. The instability of γ / γ’ interfaces makes the growth or 
even directional coarsening (i.e., rafting) of γ’ phases possible, and thus 
unstabilizes the microstructures and mechanical properties of superal -
loys [6 – 8] . Moreover, the γ / γ’ interfaces are generally the weakest 
mechanical regions in Ni-based single crystal superalloys. They may 
deteriorate the overall performance of superalloys [9,10] . Therefore, the 
γ / γ’ interfaces are hidden troubles that threaten the service safety of 
superalloys, due to their thermodynamic instabilities and relatively 
lower mechanical strengths. 
As a common trace impurity element, hydrogen (H) atoms 
unavoidably present in various metallic structural materials such as 
iron, steel, Ni-based superalloys and so on, and they always reside in the 
interstices of host materials [11,12] . The interactions between hydrogen 
atoms and host crystal lattice can affect the microstructure trans -
formation of metallic materials, increasing the brittleness of host ma -
terials [11] . Some mechanisms have already been proposed to explain 
the hydrogen embrittlement phenomenon, including the hydrogen- 
enhanced decohesion (HEDE), hydrogen-enhanced local plasticity 
(HELP) and so on [13 – 17] . Considering the probable hydrogen-rich

environment in aero-engines and the possibility of using hydrogen as 
fuel for aero-engines, it is necessary to study the effect of hydrogen on 
the microstructures and properties of Ni-based single crystal superalloys 
[18] . Early in 1980s and 1990s, it was found that hydrogen can lead to a 
sharp drop in the fracture strain of PWA1480 and CMSX-2 single crystal 
superalloys, and their yield strengths was reduced by about 10% along 
the [0 0 1] crystal orientation [19,20] . In 1998, Brass et al. reviewed lots 
of experimental studies and concluded that both the local plasticity and 
the embrittlement of nickel-based single crystal superalloys are 
increased by hydrogen. Moreover, they attributed the ‘arborescent ’ 
character of hydrogen induced brittle zone towards the bulk of the su -
peralloy specimen and the reduced plasticity of the superalloy to a strain 
induced enhancement of the hydrogen transport in the alloy [21] . In 
addition, Wu et al. found that the hydrogen-induced embrittlement at 
the Ni/Ni

3
Al-based alloys than in Ni- 
based alloys [22] . From these previous studies, it is obvious that 
hydrogen embrittlement occurs in the nickel-based single crystal 
superalloys. 
Belonging to a kind of common point defects, vacancies (Vac) also 
inevitably exist in metallic materials [23 – 27] . Hence, the vacancy 
assistance mechanism has also been used to clarify the hydrogen 
embrittlement [28] . Li et al. found that the interactions between 
hydrogen atoms and a vacancy in α -Fe lead to the formation of Vac-H 
complex defect structure, i.e., hydrogenated vacancy, which can 
significantly reduce the diffusion rate of hydrogen atoms and vacancies 
in α -Fe. Moreover, the hydrogenated vacancy has a pinning effect on the 
motion of dislocations [12] . Because of the difference in lattice constants 
between γ and γ’ phases, misfit dislocations which could be considered 
as arrays of vacancies, inevitably appear at the γ / γ’ interfaces [5] . It was 
also observed that a large number of dislocations, vacancies and other 
defects can be generated at the γ / γ’ interfaces in the process of creep 
deformation of superalloys [28,29] . In addition, the climb of disloca -
tions at the γ / γ’ interfaces, which depends on the diffusion of vacancies, 
is an important mechanism for the creep deformation of Ni-based single 
crystal superalloys [5] . Zhao et al. have studied the influence of alloying 
elements on the self-migration of a single Ni vacancy at the γ / γ’ interface 
[27] . As a result, it is quite possible for vacancies to exist at the γ / γ’ 
interfaces. 
Some studies shown that hydrogen atom will be trapped by grain 
boundaries or vacancies during the diffusion of hydrogen atoms [30,31] . 
From in situ observation of X-ray diffraction, Fukai et al. attributed the 
lattice contraction of FCC Ni specimen under high hydrogen pressure 
and temperature to the copious formation of vacancies. The copious 
formation of vacancies was rationalized by considering hydrogen- 
vacancy complexes, because a single vacancy in Ni is known to trap 
up six hydrogen atoms, i.e., 6H-vacancy complex [32] . Sanyal et al. 
found that hydrogen atoms have natural tendencies to segregate at the 
γ / γ’ interface [33] . Moreover, we have calculated the segregation en -
ergies of hydrogen atom at the vacancy-containing γ / γ’ interface. The 
negative segregation energies of hydrogen atom indicate that hydrogen 
atom can segregate to the first nearest neighbor octahedral interstices of 
the vacancy, demonstrating that hydrogen and vacancy can co-exist at 
the phase interface (see Table S1 in Supporting Information for details). 
However, both the interaction between vacancy and hydrogen atom and 
the tendency to form a hydrogenated vacancy at the γ / γ’ interface are 
far from clear. And the potential effects of hydrogenated vacancy on the 
mechanical strength and thermodynamic stability of γ / γ’ interface still 
need to be elucidated. Therefore, it should be reasonable and also 
rewarding to explore the formation mechanism of hydrogen-vacancy 
complex at the interface as well as the possible effects of hydrogenat -
ed vacancy on the properties of γ / γ’ interface. 
Experimentally, the atomic-scale hydrogen atoms, vacancies and 
hydrogen-vacancy (Vac-H) complexes at the γ / γ’ interfaces are not easy 
to be observed without the help of top instrumental equipment. By 
contrast, several theoretical methods including first-principles calcula -
tions, molecular dynamic simulations and cluster dynamics, etc., have 
been successfully applied to interfaces, point defects and hydrogenated 
vacancy related studies on the atomistic and electronic levels, due to 
their powerful prediction abilities and high accuracies 
[5,11,12,23,25,27,28] . For example, using first-principles calculations 
based on density functional theory (DFT), Zhao et al. studied the site 
preference of alloying elements and vacancies at the γ / γ’ interface as 
well as the effects of vacancy on the site preference of alloying elements. 
The strengthening mechanism mediated by vacancy for the γ / γ’ inter -
face was also evaluated [27] . Hayward et al. investigated the behavior of 
hydrogen and vacancies and their interactions in BCC iron using ab initio 
and classical molecular dynamics methods [11] . Comprehensive first- 
principles calculations and kinetic Monte Carlo simulations were car -
ried out to elucidate the role of grain boundaries in affecting the 
hydrogen diffusion in FCC Ni [28] . Hence, first-principles total-energy 
calculations based on DFT were employed in this work to investigate the 
interaction between hydrogen atom and monovacancy at the γ / γ’ 
interface and its effects on the thermodynamic stability and fracture 
strength of the interface. Our work would give significant information 
on the formation mechanism of hydrogenated vacancy at the γ / γ’ 
interface, and also would help to better understand the role of hydro -
genated vacancy in the stability and cohesiveness of γ / γ’ phase 
interface.

2.2. First-principles total-energy calculations 
In present work, all first-principles calculations were performed by 
using the Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP) software devel -
oped by University of Vienna under the framework of density functional 
theory (DFT) [36,37] . The Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) exchan -
ge – correlation functional in generalized gradient approximation (GGA) 
was employed [38,39] . A plane wave cut-off energy of 350 eV was 
chosen after convergence tests. The convergence criteria for electronic 
self-consistency and ionic relaxation loops were set to a change in energy 
of less than 1 × 10
-5 
eV and the force for each ion of less than 0.05 eV/Å, 
respectively, to yield a reasonable convergence for total energies and 
forces acting on atoms. Brillouin-zone integrations were performed on 
Г -centered symmetry-reduced Monkhorst-Pack k -point meshes, using a 
Gaussian-smearing width of 0.2 eV and the method of Methfessel-Paxton 
order 1 for all structural relaxations and total energy calculations. The 
division of k grid is 6 × 6 × 3 for the interface model [40] . Spin po -
larization was considered in all calculations.

hydrogen atom at the γ / γ’ interface, a hydrogen atom was directly

hydrogen atom seemingly eliminates the point defects at the γ / γ’ 
interface, this direct recombination at the interface is unstable actually. 
After the relaxation of atomic positions in γ / γ’ interface, it can be easily 
found that the hydrogen atom deviates from its initial position (site 6) 
and migrates into the 1

4. Conclusions 
Comprehensive studies on the site preference of monovacancy and 
single hydrogen atom and their interplay at (0 0 1) γ -Ni/ γ’ -Ni
3
Al phase 
interface of Ni-based single crystal superalloys, tendency to form hy -
drogenated vacancy and potential effects of hydrogenated vacancy on 
the thermodynamic stability and Griffith fracture work of γ / γ’ phase 
interface have been performed in this work from first-principles calcu -
lations, to gain more insights into the role of hydrogenated vacancy at 
the γ / γ’ phase interface. The main results are summarized as follows. 
(I) The corner point site at (0 0 1) γ -Ni atomic plane of the γ / γ’ phase 
interface is the most preferable position for the generation of 
monovacancy due to the lowest vacancy formation energy. Single 
hydrogen atom prefers occupying the octahedral interstices over 
the tetrahedral interstices in the vicinity of (0 0 2) coherent layer 
of perfect γ / γ’ phase interface, and this behavior was successfully 
interpreted by using the ratio of maximum radius of solute atom 
that can be placed into tetrahedral and octahedral interstices of 
FCC structure to the radius of solvent atom. 
(II) The interaction between monovacancy and single hydrogen atom 
at the γ / γ’ interface leads to the formation of Vac-H complex 
structure (i.e., hydrogenated vacancy) as separated by a certain 
distance rather than direct recombination. First nearest-neighbor 
(1
st
-NN) octahedral interstices of Ni vacancy are more preferable 
for hydrogen atom to occupy in comparison with 1
st
-NN tetra -
hedral interstices and 2
nd
-NN interstices. The deviations of the 
hydrogen atom from the center of 1
st
-NN interstices to the va -
cancy can be mainly attributed to the short-range interattraction 
between monovacancy and single hydrogen atom. 
(III) The presence of monovacancy or single hydrogen atom at the γ / γ’ 
phase interface lowers the interfacial stability as compared with 
the perfect γ / γ’ phase interface, while the formation of hydro -
genated vacancy increases the interfacial stability as compared 
with the vacancy-containing γ / γ’ phase interface, due to the 
lower and broadened density-of-states peak at the Fermi level of 
γ / γ’ phase interface containing a stable hydrogenated vacancy. 
(IV) The formation of hydrogenated vacancy at the γ / γ’ phase inter -
face is deleterious to the interfacial fracture strength and can 
change the preferable fracture place of γ / γ’ phase interface, 
which can be attributed to the charge depletion (or accumula -
tion) in the region between the (0 0 2) γ / γ’ coherent atomic layer 
and its adjacent (0 0 1) atomic layer of γ (or γ’ ) phase.
