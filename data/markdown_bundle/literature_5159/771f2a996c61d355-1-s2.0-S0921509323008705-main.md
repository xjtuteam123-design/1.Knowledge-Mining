ABSTRACT 
Improving the strength of CoCrFeNi high entropy alloy with a face-centered cubic structure has been a subject of 
significant research in recent years. However, most strengthening mechanisms are limited by the strength- 
ductility trade-off. In this study, we proposed an entirely new strategy of multi-scale microstructure strength -
ening for high entropy alloys that significantly enhances the yield strength and ultimate tensile strength to 751 
MPa and 1100 MPa, respectively, while maintaining high elongation of 15.1%. Through annealing at different 
temperatures, we tailored the microstructure of CoCrFeNiNb
0.1
Mo
0.3 
alloy, in which a multi-scale microstructure 
was achieved through inducing the micro-scale Laves phase, nano-scale precipitate and atomic-scale short-range 
order structure. Through Orowan bypass mechanism and shearing mechanism, the synergistic interaction effect 
of multi-scale reinforcements can realize a breakthrough in mechanical properties to further overcome strength- 
ductility trade off, and thus demonstrate a promising approach to improve engineering performance of high 
entropy alloys. 
1. Introduction 
High entropy alloys (HEAs) have been the subject of prosperous 
research for several years since their inception in 2004 [ 1 , 2 ]. The HEAs 
exhibit a wide range of exceptional characteristics, including remark -
able strength [ 3 , 4 ], superb mechanical performance in 
high-temperature environments [ 5 ], outstanding resistance to fatigue 
[ 6 ], and excellent protection against corrosion and oxidation [ 7 , 8 ]. 
Among them, the face-centered cubic (FCC) structure HEA has garnered 
particular interest due to their high ductility and work hardening ability 
[ 9 ]. The CoCrFeMnNi HEA, also known as Cantor alloy is an example 
which has generated significant interest [ 10 ]. Most recently, He [ 11 ] 
et al. noticed that Mn element is so active that is easy to form oxides, 
which is not conducive to the stability of the microstructure. Moreover, 
Mn element is easy to volatilize during smelting process. George [ 12 ] 
et al. found that CoCrFeNi quaternary alloy has the same single FCC 
solid solution structure as CoCrFeMnNi, and the two alloys show the 
same tensile mechanical behavior in the temperature range of 
73 – 1273K. The CoCrFeNi HEA with an FCC structure has high ductility 
but relatively low yield strength and is thus seen as a good base for 
further reinforcement. As a result, improving the strength of FCC HEAs 
has been a major focus of research in recent years. 
Various strengthening mechanisms such as fine grains [ 13 , 14 ], pre -
cipitate strengthening [ 15 , 16 ], and short-range ordered (SRO) struc -
tures [ 17 , 18 ] have been adopted to enhance their strength. Otto et al. 
[ 13 ] conducted research aimed at strengthening the FCC HEA by 
focusing on grain refinement. The results showed that when the grain 
size was refined from 144 to 4.4 μ m the yield strength (YS) would be 
enhanced from 200 to 350 MPa at ambient temperature. Although this 
suggests the occurrence of grain boundary hardening in the FCC HEA,

Inducing precipitate into the FCC matrix has attracted significant 
attention due to superior mechanical properties and interesting defor -
mation behaviors [ 15 , 16 ]. Implementing precipitation hardening would 
require the chemical composition modification of the alloy. As an 
example, the addition of Al [ 19 , 20 ] to CoCrFeNi HEA can result in the 
formation of a body-centered cubic (BCC) phase within the FCC matrix. 
As the content of Al increasing, the crystal structure of the HEA un -
dergoes a transition from FCC to BCC. This transformation enhances the 
strength of the HEA but comes at the expense of reduced ductility. 
Furthermore, the incorporation of Ti [ 21 , 22 ] into CoCrFeNiTi
x 
alloy 
leads to the phase evolution of the material, progressing from FCC, FCC 
+ R phase + σ phase, to FCC + R phase + σ phase + Laves phase as the 
value of x increases from 0 to 0.5. As a result, the compressive strength of 
the alloy rises from 871 MPa to 1502 MPa, but at the expense of reduced 
fracture strain, which decreases from 75% to 20%. Likewise, the addi -
tion of Nb [ 23 , 24 ] and Mo [ 25 ] to CoCrFeNi HEA brings about an 
enhancement in strength but concurrently leads to a reduction in its 
plasticity. 
Additionally, some research suggest that the chemical complexity in 
HEAs motivates the formation of SRO structures, and thus increase the 
energy barrier dominating the effective frictional resistance to disloca -
tion movement, leading to an increase in strength [ 26 – 28 ]. However, 
these metallurgical mechanisms for increasing strength can result in a 
single-scale homogenized microstructure, leading to a ductility loss, 
known as the strength-ductility trade-off. To overcome this, hetero -
structural materials containing both fine- and coarse-domains have been 
reported to have a superior combination of strength and ductility [ 29 , 
30 ]. 
Moreover, in most investigations of HEAs, precipitation and SRO 
strengthening mechanisms are considered as separate strengthening 
strategies rather than being combined as a synergistic strengthening 
mechanism. Thus, we consider whether we can combine these 
strengthening mechanisms and adopt the strategy of configuration 
tailoring the microstructure to achieve a multi-scale microstructure. If 
we can construct multi-scale microstructure through inducing micron- 
scale, nano-scale precipitates and atom-scale SRO structures into the 
FCC matrix, it is expected to overcome the strength-ductility trade-off in 
HEAs. 
To implement this strategy, the CoCrFeNiNb

while the elongation remained almost unchanged. This investigation 
provides insights into the strengthening mechanism of combined pre -
cipitates and SRO structures and offers a new idea for tailoring the mi -
crostructures of HEAs to achieve exclusive mechanical performance.

4. Discussion 
4.1. Microstructure evolution 
The microstructure of the HEAs studied was engineered to be multi- 
scale through annealing treatment. The evolution of the microstructure 
is illustrated in Fig. 8 (a). In the as-cast alloy, the Laves phase exists as a 
eutectic lamellar structure. In NM600, the majority of the lamellar 
eutectic structure disappears and the remaining Laves phase is with 
irregular lath shape. In addition, SRO structures are observable in the 
matrix. In NM700, more of the lamellar eutectic Laves phase dissolves, 
and a significant number of nano-scale precipitates appear in the matrix. 
Moreover, the extent of SROs is reduced. In NM800, the lamellar Laves 
phase almost entirely vanishes. The remaining Laves phase merges and 
grows in size, while the nano-scale precipitates tend to spheroidize. The 
SROs continue to decrease in size and quantity. 
The statistics of the volume fraction and average size of the pre -
cipitates and SROs are presented in Fig. 8 (b) – (c). By adjusting the 
microstructure, a multi-scale Laves phase, precipitate, and SRO struc -
ture were obtained. The micron-scale Laves phases enclose the pre -
cipitates intermittently to form a reinforced frame that serves as a basis 
for alloy enhancement. Furthermore, the high density of nano-scale 
particle precipitates and atomic-scale SRO fill the matrix, forming a 
multi-scale structure. The fine precipitates and SRO structures improve 
the matrix minimal loss of plasticity. By combining precipitation and 
SRO strengthening mechanisms through a tailored micron-scale, nano- 
scale precipitates and atomic-scale SRO structure microstructure, we 
achieved remarkable tensile properties in the current HEA system. The 
multi-scale microstructure strengthening strategy overcomes the 
strength-ductility trade-off, as expected.

4.3. Relevance of microstructure to ductility 
The work hardening rate as well as the ductility of HEAs are related 
to dislocation accumulation. The higher work hardening rate means that 
the dislocation accumulation rate is faster [ 58 ]. The investigated ma -
terials exhibit a various deformation behavior as shown in work hard -
ening curves in Fig. 9 . The initial hardening rate of the alloy increases 
with the increase of annealing temperature. The reason is that the size of 
Laves phase and nano-scale precipitates increased with the increase of 
annealing temperature because the Ostwald ripening mechanism 
driving the coarsening of Laves phase and precipitates [ 59 ], which 
would make dislocations accumulate faster during deformation. There -
fore, the initial work hardening rate is higher in the HEA with higher 
annealing temperature. When the precipitates are numerous and suffi -
cient fine, a part of the dislocations can be bypassed, the accumulation of 
dislocations is not easy to exceed the critical value to form crack sources. 
So the hardening rate of NM700 decreases slowly and the decrease of 
ductility is not obvious. In NM800, the precipitates are coarsened and 
less in number compared with NM700, which leading to more disloca -
tions cannot pass through when dislocations accumulate to a certain 
extent. Dislocations accumulate fast and tangle to form crack sources, so 
the work hardening rate decreases rapidly, which lead to the reduction 
of ductility. The phenomenon is consistent with the research of Li [ 60 ] 
and Yang [ 61 ]. Furthermore, in FCC solid solutions, increase of the SRO 
degree leads to the “ glide plane softening ” effect [ 62 ], i.e., the disloca -
tion movement restricts the plastic deformation to one or several 
neighboring glide planes, thus making cross slip more difficult and then 
promoting planar slip leading to the destruction of SRO structures [ 63 , 
64 ]. Consequently, the SRO strengthening would not significantly 
reduce the ductility. Through tailoring multi-scale microstructure, we 
strengthen the HEA while minimizing the weakening of ductility.
