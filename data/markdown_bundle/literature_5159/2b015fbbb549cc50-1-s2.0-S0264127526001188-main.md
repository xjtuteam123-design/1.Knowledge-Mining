ABSTRACT
Refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) show great promise for extreme environments, but their development is 
hindered by the vast compositional space and the challenge of balancing multiple properties. This study presents 
an integrated machine learning (ML) framework for the efficient design of RHEAs for achieving both high 
hardness and excellent corrosion resistance. A comprehensive dataset was constructed, and multitask learning 
with tree-based ensemble algorithms was employed to develop predictive models for hardness, corrosion po -
tential (E
corr

corr

compositional space. Data from 36 publications were processed, with E

corr

electronegativity, average melting point, and mixing entropy for hardness and the difference in the atomic size

1. Introduction
High-entropy alloys (HEAs), which consist of five or more principal 
elements, each constituting between 5 and 35 at.%, were initially 
introduced by Yeh et al. in 2004 [1] . Owing to their simple, solid- 
solution microstructures, exceptional mechanical properties, and 
strong resistance to corrosion and oxidation, they have garnered wide -
spread research attention [2 – 5] . In particular, refractory high-entropy 
alloys (RHEAs) composed of refractory transition metals (including Zr, 
Mo, Nb, Hf, Ta, V, W, and Re) have been increasingly studied over the 
past decade. They are characterized by their high hardness, elevated 
strength, enhanced corrosion resistance, and outstanding high- 
temperature stability [6 – 8] . Notably, in 2010, Senkov et al., 2010 [9]
synthesized two representative RHEAs (W – Nb – Mo – Ta and 
W – Nb – Mo – Ta – V) that exhibited superior high-temperature mechanical 
performance compared with traditional nickel-based superalloys. 
Consequently, RHEAs are considered highly promising for use in 
extreme environments involving high temperatures and aggressive 
conditions, such as gas turbines, nuclear reactor components, and 
chemical processing systems [10 – 12] .
In recent years, extensive research has focused on enhancing the 
specific properties of RHEAs through compositional design. For 
instance, to achieve high strength and hardness, researchers have 
significantly improved the room-temperature and high-temperature

years, machine learning (ML) approaches have garnered significant 
attention for addressing complex problems in materials science [16 – 19] . 
In particular, ML-based techniques for extracting informative features 
from raw data have been widely adopted and have demonstrated strong 
potential in guiding the design of material compositions, with their 
effectiveness being thoroughly validated [20] . He et al. [21] developed a 
dual-task ML model capable of simultaneously predicting yield strength 
and fracture strain in Nb – Ta – Ti – V – W alloys, enabling rapid screening 
for ultrahigh yield strength. Experimental validation revealed that two 
of the designed alloys exhibit exceptional yield strength (1915 – 1983

counterparts), whereas two others demonstrated unprecedented 
ductility (achieving fracture strains exceeding 16.6 % without failure

phases. Thus, ML approaches have demonstrated accurate predictions of 
the phase formation, hardness, and yield strength of high-entropy alloys.
ML not only provides powerful tools for predicting material prop -
erties, but its interpretability techniques (such as SHAP and LIME) have 
become keys to revealing hidden physical mechanisms in materials 
science. In alloy design, these methods have been successfully applied to 
identify critical characteristics affecting phase transition temperatures, 
strength, or plasticity in traditional alloys [23 – 25] . SHAP analysis has 
helped clarify the nonlinear relationships between complex stainless 
steel compositions and pitting resistance in high-throughput computa -
tional databases [25] . However, existing research mostly focuses on 
interpreting relatively simple systems or single performance metrics. 
The deep application of interpretable machine learning methods like 
SHAP to analyze multi-objective performance trade-offs (such as hard -
ness versus corrosion resistance) in complex multi-component systems 
like RHEAs, and to guide compositional reverse engineering, remains 
underreported. The design of alloys for practical extreme environments 
necessitates a holistic approach that simultaneously optimizes multiple 
critical properties. In particular, the combination of high hardness and 
superior corrosion resistance is paramount for applications in corrosive 
and mechanically demanding settings, such as pumps and valves in 
nuclear power cooling circuits, cutting tools in corrosive coolants, and 
components in deep-sea engineering [26 – 29] . In the research of RHEA 
multi-objective optimization, important progress has been made in the 
study of the synergistic optimization of strength and plasticity [30] , the 
combination study of hardness and corrosion resistance [31] , and the 
correlation study of hardness and yield strength [32] . Nonetheless, the 
interplay between these properties in RHEAs is complex and often in -
volves trade-offs; for instance, elements that enhance solid-solution 
strengthening might compromise the stability of the passive film, 
thereby reducing corrosion resistance [25,33] . This multiobjective 
optimization challenge, compounded by the vast compositional space, 
remains inadequately addressed by conventional methods or single- 
property ML models.
To bridge this gap, this study presents an integrated ML framework 
for efficiently designing RHEAs with high hardness and excellent 
corrosion resistance. We constructed a comprehensive dataset from the 
literature and experiments, ensemble fusion for hardness and indepen -
dent modeling of corrosion metrics (e.g., corrosion potential and 
corrosion current density). Furthermore, SHAP-based feature impor -
tance analysis is employed to uncover and quantify the pivotal role of 
specific elemental constituents and thermodynamic/electronic param -
eters in governing these properties. This interpretability provides 
fundamental insights into the physical metallurgy principles that dictate 
the performance of RHEAs. The proposed approach not only accelerates 
the screening of promising compositions but also provides insights into 
the key factors governing these properties through interpretable ML. 
This methodology offers a viable strategy for multiobjective material 
design in complex compositional spaces.
2. Methodology
2.1. Design framework
Fig. 1 schematically outlines the integrated ML framework employed 
in this study to design RHEAs with superior hardness and corrosion 
resistance. The methodology follows a structured sequence, 
commencing with (a) database preparation, followed by (b) model 
optimization, (c) feature selection, and culminating in (d) composition 
optimization. Finally, the top-performing alloy compositions identified 
using ML were experimentally synthesized and characterized to validate 
the model predictions.
2.2. Database establishment
The database for this study was established with the primary crite -
rion being fabrication using arc melting and characterization in the as- 
cast state, thereby mitigating the influence of the processing history 
on the properties. The investigated compositional space was confined to 
common HEA elements, including 3d transition metals (Co, Cr, Cu, Fe, 
Mn, Ni, Ti, and V), refractory metals (Mo, Nb, Ta, and W), and Al. 
Specific exclusions included noble-metal-containing alloys (due to 
limited data), interstitial-strengthened systems with B, C, N, or O 
(beyond the scope), and lightweight alloys with Be, Mg, or Li to avoid 
complexities associated with complex phase formation. Based on the 
above principles, we integrated data on the hardness, corrosion current 
density (I
corr
), and corrosion potential (E
corr
) of HEAs from 36 literature 
sources.
The initial dataset was rigorously curated to ensure data integrity. 
Entries with anomalous property values were pruned, and compositions 
exhibiting significant discrepancies in reported properties across 
different studies were excluded. To simplify the model, the average 
value of a property was adopted for a given composition if it showed 
minimal variation across the literature. The final, cleaned dataset 
comprised 192 entries for hardness, 304 for corrosion current density 
(I
corr

corr

datasets comprised 157, 187, and 93 nominal compositions for hard -

respectively. It should be noted that the sample size of the valid E
corr

have made every effort to integrate data, the small sample size remains 
an inherent challenge in machine learning modeling, which may affect 
the statistical robustness and generalization ability of the model.

Hyperparameter tuning was performed using a Bayesian optimiza -
tion framework. This method employs a Gaussian process as a surrogate 
model to approximate the objective function, while an acquisition 
function — such as expected improvement (EI) — guides the efficient 
search for optimal hyperparameters. A five-fold cross-validation strat -
egy was integrated into the optimization loop on the training set to 
guard against overfitting and enhance the generalizability of the 
selected model configurations. The hyperparameter search space 
encompassed key parameters for each algorithm, including the number 
of estimators, maximum tree depth, learning rate, and data subsampling 
ratios.
Given the distinct physical nature of the two target proper -
ties — corrosion current (a kinetic parameter) and corrosion potential (a 
thermodynamic parameter) — separate models were constructed for 
each. This specialized approach allows the modeling framework to 
accommodate the unique underlying mechanisms and data character -
istics associated with each corrosion metric.

optimal model is obtained. The element distribution of the dataset is

distribution of hardness values in the screened dataset is shown in Fig. 2

The analysis of feature importance derived from ML models eluci -
dates the critical role and underlying physical mechanisms of four 
consensus descriptors in determining the hardness of high-entropy al -
loys. The d-electron concentration, as the primary electronic structure 
descriptor, directly affects the strength and directionality of metallic 
bonds in transition metal systems [39] . It significantly contributes to 
improving the hardness by enhancing the interatomic bonding force, 
increasing the dislocation motion resistance, and increasing the elastic 
modulus [40] . Moreover, the average electronegativity regulates the 
charge distribution and bonding properties of multicomponent alloys; 
its moderate difference is conducive to the formation of synergistic 
strengthening effects of metallic, covalent, and ionic bonds, thereby 
optimizing the overall mechanical properties [41,42] . The average 
melting point, as an intuitive indicator of the thermal stability of 
interatomic bonding, is directly related to the intrinsic strength of ma -
terials. The introduction of high-melting-point elements such as W and 
Mo significantly enhances the deformation resistance of alloys at high 
temperatures [43,44] . The mixing entropy reflects the unique configu -
rational entropy effect of high-entropy alloys, which promotes the for -
mation of single-phase solid solutions, introduces lattice distortion 
strengthening, and inhibits the precipitation of brittle phases to main -
tain microstructural stability. These features collectively constitute a 
multiscale strengthening mechanism spanning from the electronic level, 
chemical bonding, and thermodynamic stability to the crystal structure, 
providing a key theoretical basis and design dimensions for the rational 
design of high-performance high-entropy alloys.
The exceptional performance of the RF model can be attributed to its 
inherent algorithmic characteristics, which are particularly well-suited 
for the challenges associated with materials science data [45,46] . Its 
ensemble approach, which aggregates predictions from a multitude of 
decorrelated decision trees, effectively mitigates overfitting and en -
hances generalization to unseen compositions. This strength is clearly 
demonstrated in Fig. 5 , which contrasts the performance of all four 
models. The minimal discrepancy observed between the training set (R
2 
= 0.9171) and test set (R
2 
= 0.9137) performance for the RF model is a 
primary indicator of its strong generalization capability, confirming that 
the model has learned underlying physical patterns rather than merely 
memorizing noise in the training data. In contrast, other models, such as 
XGBoost, often exhibited a more significant decrease in performance 
from training to testing, which is a classic sign of overfitting. Further -
more, the absolute prediction error, quantified by an RMSE value of 
55.74 HV, represents only 8.3 % of the average hardness value (669.19 
HV) across the dataset. This level of precision is not merely a statistical 
achievement but holds substantial practical significance. In the iterative 
process of alloy design, such a model provides a highly reliable tool for 
the virtual screening of vast compositional spaces. It enables researchers 
to rapidly identify promising candidate compositions with high pre -
dicted hardness, thereby prioritizing them for costly and time- 
consuming experimental synthesis and validation. This outcome signif -
icantly accelerates the discovery cycle and enhances the efficiency of 
developing novel, high-performance RHEAs.

corrosion behavior of traditional alloys and will not be able to accurately

behavior, such as Cu and Mn, are introduced into the original data. Due

4. Discussions
In this study, an ML model for predicting the hardness and corrosion 
resistance of refractory high-entropy alloys was established. Through 
feature filtering, we obtained the relevant feature variables. In the ML- 
hardness model, the characteristics include electronic structures and 
thermodynamic descriptors such as the electron concentration, average 
electronegativity, average melting point, and mixed entropy. In the ML- 
E
corr 
and ML-I
corr 
dual-target driving models, the characteristics include 
geometric chemical parameters such as atomic size difference, electro -
negativity difference, and key component characteristics. The models 
are trained for a narrowly defined Nb-Mo-Ta-W-V compositional space.
As shown in Fig. 13 , this study uses the SHAP method to evaluate the 
impact of features on the prediction results. The x-axis represents SHAP 
values, and the y-axis corresponds to different features, with each dot 
representing a sample. The SHAP values of each feature and their 
importance rankings in the ML-hardness model and the corrosion model 
are shown in Fig. 13(a) and 13(b) , respectively. In the SHAP analysis, the 
d-electron concentration and average electronegativity are identified as 
core electronic structure descriptors that control the hardness. High d- 
electron concentrations significantly increase dislocation motion resis -
tance by strengthening metallic bonds and increasing the electron 
density of states near the Fermi level, whereas moderate average elec -
tronegativity increases interatomic interactions by optimizing the 
charge distribution and bonding hybridization [54 – 56] . In terms of 
corrosion resistance, atomic size difference ( δ r) and electronegativity 
difference ( Δ χ ) are confirmed as key geometric – chemical parameters 
affecting corrosion potential. Smaller δ r and Δ χ values promote the 
formation of a uniform and stable passivation film by reducing lattice 
distortion [57 – 59] . Notably, although the solid solution stabilization 
effect of the mixing entropy ( Δ S
mix
) in the high-entropy range of 12 ～ 16 
J/(mol ⋅ K) helps maintain a tough matrix, the mixing enthalpy ( Δ H
mix
) 
exhibits a more complex strengthening mechanism within the range 
of — 24 to 3 kJ/mol — moderate negative values promote solid solution 
strengthening, whereas excessively low negative values may induce the 
precipitation of intermetallic compounds [60 – 62] . This trend explains 
the nonlinear correlation between the mixing enthalpy and hardness 
observed in the SHAP analysis.
Figs. 14, 16, and 18 show the average deviation of the three models 
relative to their expected prediction values, whereas Fig. 15 , Fig. 17 , and 
Fig. 19 present the corresponding SHAP consensus analysez. An analysis 
of the average deviation from the model ’ s expected prediction values 
reveals that the baseline value for the hardness model is 669.19 HV, and 
the SHAP values of the three optimized alloys are offset by 24.61, 
 150.69, and 162.49 HV, respectively. This trend quantitatively ex -
plains the inherent reason why Alloy A1 achieves the highest hardness. 
According to the researches of Jia et al [63] , a rational element ratio not 
only purifies grain boundaries by reducing the harmful phases but also 
customizes the deformation dislocation configuration. Combined with 
the rational design of microstructure, it enables a significant break -
through in the mechanical properties of RHEA [64,65] . In the SHAP 
consensus analysis, four features — d-electron concentration, average 
electronegativity, average melting point, and mixing entropy — are 
ranked among the top 10 in importance across the four independent 
models: RF, XGBoost, LightGBM, and CatBoost. This cross-algorithm 
consistency fully verifies the universality of the influence mechanism 
of the electronic structure and thermodynamic parameters on the 
hardness. For the corrosion performance model, the baseline value for 
E
corr 
is 0.4137 V, and the SHAP analysis reveals that the atomic size 
difference ( δ r) and electronegativity difference ( Δ χ ) are jointly identi -
fied as key features in multiple models. The Pearson correlation coeffi -
cient between δ r and E

It should be explicitly noted that the ML model predictions provided 
in this study are deterministic point estimates, without systematic 
quantification of uncertainty. The uncertainty in these predictions may 
primarily stem from the following aspects: measurement or computa -
tional errors inherent in the training data, performance variability 
caused by variations in molding processes for the same material 
composition; generalization errors resulting from model architecture 
selection, hyperparameter settings, and limited training data volume. 
Although cross-validation was employed, the model's confidence level in 
predicting new material properties still requires evaluation. The selected 
material descriptors may not fully capture all physicochemical mecha -
nisms underlying target properties, leading to information gaps. These 
uncertainties may potentially influence alloy screening decisions based 
on this study in the following ways: candidate alloys with similar pre -
dicted performance values may have their true performance rankings 
reversed due to errors. Therefore, rankings should not be regarded as 
absolute criteria. Greater attention should be paid to the performance 
trends revealed by predicted values, with decisions based on relative 
comparisons and cross-judgment incorporating physical metallurgy 
knowledge. We recommend using the study's predictions as a powerful 
tool to narrow experimental exploration scope and guide initial exper -
