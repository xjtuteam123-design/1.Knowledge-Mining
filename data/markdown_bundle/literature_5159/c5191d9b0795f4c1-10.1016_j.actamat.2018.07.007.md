Abstract: 
Precipitation pathways in many nickel-based superal loys are highly complex and in some cases 
proceed very slowly. This behavior has hampered the ir introduction into some novel application 
spaces ranging from microelectromechanical systems (MEMS) to protective coatings, wherein 
their high temperature strength and oxidation resis tance would otherwise be highly desirable. In 
this investigation, the microstructure of Ni-25Mo-8 Cr superalloy thin films prepared via direct 
current magnetron sputtering (DCMS) is shown to be key to manipulating and understanding 
phase transformations. Specifically, the columnar, highly textured, and nanotwinned grains are 
shown to facilitate precipitation of the equilibriu m DO 
a (Ni 3Mo) phase during a mild heat 
treatment. Conversely, elimination of the nanotwin s through a two-step heat treatment leads to 
precipitation of metastable Ni 2(Mo,Cr) (isomorphous with Pt 2Mo and Ni 2Cr) precipitates. The 
straightforward capacity to select one of several possible phase transformation pathways presents 
an opportunity to create precisely tuned microstruc tures specific to the desired application, while 
simultaneously providing unprecedented insight on these complex phase transformations. 
Keywords: Nickel-based superalloys, Precipitates, Nanotwins, Sputter Deposition, Aging

1. Introduction 
Thin metallic films or foils, which are used in app lications ranging from microelectronics to 
surface coatings, can be fabricated via numerous ro utes. Such methods include mechanical 
thinning [1], electrodeposition [2,3], or direct current magnetron sputtering (DCMS) [4–6]. Each 
of these routes impart unique microstructural defec ts to the metallic films that may dominate the 
mechanical response. Sputtered metallic films in t he as-deposited state are typically 
characterized by very fine, highly textured microst ructures and may also include a high density 
of point and planar defects [7,8]. The intragranul ar planar defects may be twins or stacking 
faults, resulting in a microstructure that is effec tively randomly close-packed, depending on the 
density of the planar faults. More often than not, these randomly close-packed structures are 
simply referred to as nanotwinned. There now exist s a large body of literature exploring the 
interaction of dislocations with nanotwinned struct ures and the subsequent strengthening 
behavior [9,10]. To date, in depth experimental c haracterization of such plasticity mechanisms 
in nanotwinned structures have been largely limited to elemental metals, e.g. [2,11], and only a 
handful of simple alloys, e.g. [6,12,13], have been fabricated with similar planar defect densities. 
As a result, the influence of the high density of p oint and planar defects on precipitation kinetics 
and phase evolution have largely been overlooked. 
In this investigation, a commercial Ni-Mo-Cr-based alloy, Haynes® 242™, was selected 
in order to examine the effect of sputter depositio n on aging in alloys containing multiple 
competing phase transformations. Haynes® 242™ exhi bits high tensile strength at elevated 
temperatures due to precipitation of coherent, meta stable Ni 
2(Mo,Cr) (Pt 2Mo-type) precipitates 
[14–16]. These precipitates are stabilized by the p resence of chromium and are characterized by 
segregation of molybdenum to every third {420} face centered cubic (FCC) plane [17].

Precipitation of Ni 2(Mo,Cr) at moderate temperatures typically occurs v ia continuous ordering 
from a short range ordered FCC structure [17]. Pre vious examinations of Haynes® 242™ have 
determined that the Ni 2(Mo,Cr) precipitates in conventionally processed ma terial are remarkably 
stable and persist for thousands of hours [18,19]. However, when the material is severely 
plastically deformed prior to aging for thousands o f hours, the Ni 2(Mo,Cr) precipitates 
decompose into the fully stable DO a phase. The typically coarser, plate-like DO a precipitates are 
associated with severely diminished ductility [20]. 
More broadly, in nickel-based superalloys with appr oximate 3Ni:Mo stoichiometry, the 
stable DO a phase is not typically observed under normal aging conditions. Instead, these 
superalloys form coherent, metastable precipitates based on the FCC matrix such as D1 a 
(Ni 4Mo), DO22 (Ni 3Mo), or Ni 2(Mo,Cr) superstructures [21]. These superstructures are uniquely 
identified by ordering of molybdenum on every fifth {420}, fourth {420}, and third {420} FCC 
plane, respectively [22]. It should be noted that of these possible metastable phases, only the 
Ni 2(Mo,Cr) superstructure has ever been observed for the alloy composition specific to Haynes® 
242™ [17]. The Ni 2(Mo,Cr) structure is stabilized by the relatively h igh Cr concentration [29, 
40]. On the other hand, the DO a phase is an orthorhombic superstructure based on h exagonal 
close packed (HCP) ordering. Precipitation of the D O a phase requires the presence of a stacking 
fault on the {111} FCC plane of the matrix phase. However, formation of t his stacking fault and 
subsequent precipitation is not straightforward [24 ]. The phase transformation pathway leading 
to the DO a phase is complex — it involves (i) precipitation o f metastable precipitates, (ii) 
subsequent decomposition of the FCC matrix and said metastable precipitates, (iii) 
transformation from FCC to HCP ordering via shear t ransformation, and finally (iv) continuous 
ordering of the DO a precipitate phase [24,25]. The DO a phase has registry with the FCC phase on

In the present work, Haynes® 242™ was quenched from the vapor phase via direct 
current magnetron sputtering. The resulting microst ructure was composed of nanocrystalline, 
columnar grains rich in planar faults perpendicular to the film growth direction. An aging 
treatment of 48 hours at 650 °C resulted in precipitation of the stable DO a phase and complete 
absence of the metastable Ni 2(Mo,Cr) phase. When the planar faults were removed via a solution 
anneal, subsequent aging led to precipitation of th e metastable Ni 2(Mo,Cr) phase. This study 
aims to establish that the local HCP ordering creat ed by the high density of planar faults in the 
as-deposited microstructure were key to disrupting the formation of the metastable phase, and 
instead facilitated transformation of the equilibri um phase. This mechanism is proposed by 
leveraging what is known about the role of stacking faults in the phase transformation pathway 
for the DO a phase against the native planar defects of the spu tter-deposited films, thereby 
illuminating an alternative route for the study of complex phase transformation in advanced 
alloys.

4. Discussion 
The cumulative evidence from the complementary TEM and XRD characterization 
highlights several critical points that merit furth er discussion, namely: (i) the nature and 
concentration of planar defects in the as-deposited microstructure; (ii) the role of these defects in 
the accelerated transformation of the DO a phase; and (iii) the apparent recovery of the 
conventionally observed phase evolution pathway via the metastable Ni 2(Mo,Cr) phase.

4.1 As-deposited DCMS Haynes® 242™ 
As reported in Section 3.1, TEM observations of the as-deposited Haynes® 242™ film revealed 
a strong {111} FCC texture in the growth direction, with nanotwinning perpendicular to the growth 
direction. Transmission XRD corroborated the presen ce of both hexagonal and face-centered 
cubic close packed structures, at a ratio of approximately 1:1 FCC:HCP. The principal difference 
between the two structures simply falls to the sequ ential ordering of the closest packed planes. 
FCC structures follow ABCABC planar ordering, where as HCP structures follow ABABAB 
ordering. In this examination, the coexistence of t he two close packing motifs were 
accommodated by dense nanotwinning and intrinsic st acking faults as revealed via HRSTEM in 
Figure 4(b), such that the film grew via subsequent layers of closest packed {111} FCC // 
{0001} HCP planes, cf discussion in Section 3.1 and Figure 4. 
Additional confirmation of the co-existence of thes e close-packed structures was found 
upon closer inspection of the SAED presented in Figure 4(a). This diffraction pattern was clearly 
indexed as the [1120 ]HCP zone axis with significant streaking between {0001 } HCP reflections. 
The evident HCP ordering collected by SAED was init ially unexpected, because the matrix 
structure of nickel superalloys is typically FCC. S imilar investigations of DCMS superalloys 
have found that plan-view nanotwinning can typicall y be indexed in terms of the ⟨011 ⟩FCC zone

lowered stacking fault energy further sensitized th e material to nanotwinning [38]. Additionally, 
the deposition parameters strongly affected the mat erial texture and ordering: the high sputtering

4.2 Early evolution of DO a-type phase from one-step heat treatment in DCMS Ha ynes® 
242™ 
The microstructural observations presented in this study corroborate previously reported 
aging behavior of a different DCMS-deposited superalloy, INCONEL® 718 [30]. While the DO a 
structure in INCONEL® 718 is formed by δ-Ni 3Nb, not Ni 3Mo, it is prudent to draw parallels 
between these isomorphic precipitates. Burns et al. [30] postulated that rapid aging kinetics, 
facilitated by the defect-rich, nanograined microst ructure, lead to rapid stabilization of the 
equilibrium DO a-type phase. While that assessment captures part of the overall process, the 
additional microstructural evidence presented here is critical for advancing the understanding of 
phase transformation behavior in DCMS superalloys. 
As discussed in Section 4.1, examination of the mic rostructure in the side view of the as-
deposited DCMS Haynes® 242™ film, presented in Figu res 2 and 4, revealed dense planar

While there is a preponderance of microstructural e vidence supporting enhanced, defect-
assisted species mobility in these DCMS films, thes e features cannot completely account for the 
early, overwhelming presence of the DO a precipitate phase. This conclusion is drawn is due to 
the marked complexity of the established transforma tion pathway preceding DO a precipitation in 
typical Ni-based superalloys [24,39]. In convention ally processed INCONEL® 718 and 
Haynes® 242™, precipitation of the DO a phase proceeds via decomposition of prior metastab le 
DO 22 or Ni 2(Mo,Cr) phases, respectively. This indirect transf ormation pathway is necessary 
because precipitation of the DO a phase requires formation of a stacking fault on {1 11} FCC planes 
to transform the local ordering from FCC-like to HC P-like. Formation of this stacking fault 
occurs via a shear transformation following decompo sition of prior metastable precipitates 
[24,39]. After formation of the geometrically necessary stacking fault, the DO a phase precipitates 
coherently on (010) DOa //{111} FCC planes. 
If accelerated transport kinetics alone were respon sible for the premature presence of the 
DO a phase in DCMS Haynes® 242™, it would require that the material precipitated the 
metastable Ni 2(Mo,Cr) phase which then rapidly decomposed, formed the geometrically 
necessary stacking faults, and then precipitated th e DO a phase in only 48 hours at moderate 
temperature. Likewise, in the case of DCMS INCONEL® 718, a similarly complex process 
(involving prior metastable DO 22 rather than Ni 2(Mo,Cr)) would have happened in only 16 
hours.

deposited material. Such an absence is difficult to rationalize on the basis of solute redistribution 
during precipitation of the DO a phase. Ni 2(Mo,Cr) is stable over a range of Mo and Cr 
compositions, but it is stabilized by increasing Cr concentrations. Conversely, Mo is more 
soluble than Cr in DO a [23]. If there were significant compositional diffe rence between regions 
of DO a ordering and the surrounding microstructure, it wo uld follow that the surrounding phases 
would be enriched in Cr. This increase in the nomin al Cr:Mo ratio would not suppress but rather 
would enhance stability of the Ni 2(Mo,Cr) phase. It can be concluded that the Ni 2(Mo,Cr) phase
