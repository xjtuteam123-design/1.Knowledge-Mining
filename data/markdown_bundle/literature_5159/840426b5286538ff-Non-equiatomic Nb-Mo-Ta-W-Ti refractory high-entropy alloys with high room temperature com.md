ABSTRACT
A series of non-equiatomic Nb-Mo-Ta-W-Ti refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs) with single-phase BCC 
structure were developed. The alloys exhibit a combination of excellent high-temperature strength and superior 
room temperature (RT) compressive performance. All alloys exhibited compressive plastic strains exceeding 28 
% at RT, significantly superior to those of equiatomic Nb-Mo-Ta-W-Ti RHEAs. Meanwhile, the alloys exhibit yield 
strengths exceeding 450 MPa at 1100 
◦
C and 350 MPa at 1200 
◦
C. Strength predicted using inter-dendritic 
compositions aligns more closely with the experimental measurements. Under high-temperature conditions, 
the RHEAs exhibited strain softening behavior, mainly attributed to the dynamic recrystallization at grain 
boundaries.
1. Introduction
Refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs), composed of high-melting- 
point elements, exhibit excellent resistance to high-temperature soft -
ening, making them strong candidates for ultra-high-temperature 
structural applications. For example, H.T. He et al. reported that high- 
entropy alloys composed of Ta, W, Hf, and Re exhibit exceptional 
high-temperature strength, with yield strengths exceeding 700 MPa at 
1400 ℃ [ 12 ]and still maintaining an impressive value above 350 MPa 
at 1750 ℃ [ 11 , 13 ].What is more, the equiatomic Nb-Ta-Mo-W RHEA 
designed by Senkov maintains a compressive yield strength exceeding 
400 MPa at 1600 ℃ [ 6 ]. However, the intrinsic brittleness of refractory 
elements limits their compressive plastic deformability. The Senkov 
alloy, for instance, exhibits only 2 % compressive ductility at RT [ 6 ], 
which is insufficient for processing and forming operations. Some 
studies have reported that adding various proportions of Ti to equia -
tomic Nb-Ta-Mo-W alloys can enhance both strength and compressive 
plasticity at RT. When 20 at.% Ti is added, the compressive strain to 
failure reaches 12 % [ 1 ], though this improvement remains inadequate 
for practical applications.
Most existing studies on the Nb-Ta-Mo-W-Ti system have focused on 
equiatomic compositions or the adjustment of a single elemental con -
tent. However, as the definition of high-entropy alloys continues to 
evolve, non-equiatomic HEAs have increasingly attracted research in -
terest [ 9 , 10 ]. By appropriately adjusting the elemental ratios, for 
instance, reducing the brittle Mo and W contents while increasing the 
ductile Nb and Ti fractions, it is possible to simultaneously retain high 
yield strength and improve RT plasticity, thereby achieving superior 
mechanical performance. Nonetheless, systematic studies in this direc -
tion remain limited. In this work, non-equiatomic Nb-Ta-Mo-W-Ti 
RHEAs with high Nb content were designed to investigate the effects of 
varying Nb and Ti contents on both RT and high-temperature mechan -
ical properties, aiming to provide experimental insights for the further 
optimization of RHEAs in terms of strength and compressive ductility 
across a broad temperature range.

3. Results and discussion
Fig. 1 a – c present the Backscattered electron images and the Energy 
Dispersive Spectrometer of the as-cast RHEAs microstructure. All three 
alloys exhibit typical dendritic morphologies with pronounced dendritic 
segregation. Ti, Nb, and Mo are enriched in the inter-dendritic regions, 
while Ta and W are concentrated in the dendritic regions. The degree of 
segregation becomes more evident with increasing Ti content. The 
specific elemental compositions of dendritic and inter-dendritic regions 
are listed in Table S2 . The XRD patterns shown in Fig. 1 d indicate that all 
three alloys exhibit single-phase BCC structures.
Fig. 2 a – c shows the EBSD results of the Ti10, Ti15, and Ti20 alloys, 
respectively. Fig. 2 d – f present the grain size distribution histograms of 
corresponding alloys, the average grain sizes of the three alloys are 490 
± 172 μ m, 279 ± 102 μ m, and 244 ± 89 μ m, respectively. With 
increasing Ti content, a clear grain refinement trend is observed. This 
trend is plausibly due to the reduced melting point, which promotes 
higher undercooling and nucleation rates during solidification while 
shortening the solidification time and limiting grain growth.
The RT compressive properties of the three alloys are shown in 
Fig. 3 a. The yield strengths (YS) of the Ti10, Ti15, and Ti20 alloys are 
1144 ± 24 MPa, 1066 ± 35 MPa, and 1059 ± 53 MPa, respectively, with 
corresponding compressive plastic strains ( ε
p
) of 29.2 %, 33.4 %, and 
28.5 %. As shown in Fig. 3 b, the non-equiatomic RHEAs developed in 
this study exhibit significantly better RT compressive plasticity than 
other single-phase BCC RHEAs with similar compositions [ 4 ], while 
maintaining high specific yield strength. The enhancement in 
compressive deformability is driven by compositional adjustments, 
increasing ductile Nb and decreasing brittle Mo and W. Dendritic 
segregation within grains results in inhomogeneous solid solution 
strengthening (SSS), the dendritic exhibit stronger strengthening than 
the inter-dendritic regions.

strengthening follows the classical Hall-Petch relationship, with a 
strengthening coefficient of 270 MPa [ 2 ]. Based on the grain sizes ob -
