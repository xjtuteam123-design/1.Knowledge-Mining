RECONDITIONING HEAT TREATMENT OF TURBINE BLADES
FROM REFRACTORY NICKEL ALLOY
A. M. Smyslov, 1 V . V . Sedov,2 A. A. Bybin, 1 and R. R. Nev’yantseva 1
Translated from Metallovedenie i Termicheskaya Obrabotka Metallov, No. 2, pp. 44 – 48, February, 2009.
The structure and mechanical properties of nickel alloy IN738LC with a level of high-temperature strength
and corrosion resistance similar to that of domestic alloy 3MI-3U are studied after long-term operation and re-
conditioning heat treatment, i.e., homogenizing at different temperatures.
INTRODUCTION
In the process of long-term operation turbine blades pro-
duced from refractory nickel alloys undergo structural
changes under the action of high temperatures and stresses,
and the operating capacity of the parts deteriorates. It has
been shown in [1, 2] that the structure of nickel alloys after
service changes considerably. The change manifests itself
primarily in coagulation of particles of the hardening
/c103/c162-phase and formation of micropores in grain-boundary bor-
ders. Operation of turbine blades with such defects is imper-
missible, which makes it necessary to recondition the blades.
Today the undesirable changes in the structure of blades,
which are detected after a certain period of operation, are
commonly removed with the help of reconditioning heat
treatment (RHT) [1, 3]. In accordance with the data of [2, 4]
RHT performed in vacuum includes the processes of homog-
enizing and aging. The homogenizing causes full dissolution
of secondary segregations of /c103/c162-phase, formation of a super -
saturated solid solution, and partial segregation of /c103/c162-phase in
subsequent cooling. The aging ensures formation of an opti -
mum structural state of the /c103/c162-phase in the matrix. Homoge -
nizing is also accompanied by annihilation of dislocations
accumulated during service as a result of plastic strain, which
leads to restoration of the structure and properties of the ma -
terial of the blades [5]. However, it is stated in [6] that RHT
does not ensure complete recovery of the structure of the ma-
terial due to incomplete removal of the creep-induced
micropores. At the same time, it is shown in [4] that after a
standard heat treatment of nickel alloy ChS88U the endu -
rance of the metal in tests for long-term strength increases by
a factor of 1.8 with respect to the cast state despite some
growth in the microporosity. The efficiency of the use of
RHT is also confirmed by the data of [1], where it is shown
that heat treatment of blades from alloy ZhS6U that have
served for 6000 h promotes recovery of the structure of the
material of the feather. The treatment has raised the endu-
rance limit at a test temperature of 900°C by 20 MPa as com-
pared to the initial value. We may infer that despite the pro-
bability of incomplete removal of micropores due to recondi-
tioning heat treatment the growth in the mechanical proper-
ties to a certain level can prolong the service life of parts af-
ter long-term operation.
The aim of the present work consisted in studying the
structure and mechanical properties of alloy IN738LC (the
domestic counterpart is alloy ZMI-3U) after operation and
reconditioning heat treatment.
METHODS OF STUDY
We studied moving blades of HPT after service for about
60,000 h. The blades had been cast into investment patterns
from nickel alloy IN738LC. The chemical composition of
the alloy in accordance with AMS 2268 is as follows:
15.7 – 16.3% Cr, 8.0 – 9.0% Co, 3.2 – 3.7% Ti, 3.2 – 3.7%
Al, 1.5 – 3.5% Mo, 2.4 – 2.8% W, 1.5 – 2.0% Ta, 0.6 – 1.1%
Nb, 0.09 – 0.13% C, 0.007 – 0.012% B, 0.03 – 0.08% Zr. Al-
loy IN738LC is widely used by foreign gas turbine makers
for fabricating blades; its refractoriness and corrosion resis -
tance correspond to those of domestic alloy 3MI-3U (TU
1-801-279–91).
Before being put to operation the blades are subjected to
standard heat treatment that consists of 2-h homogenizing at
1120°C and 24-h aging at 840°C [7]. In our work the recon -
ditioning heat treatment was performed in an SÉV-5.5 va -
cuum furnace at residual pressure in the chamber not exceed-

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
The results of metallographic analysis of the alloy in the
root and feather zones of the blade in the state after operation
are presented in Fig. 1. In accordance with the data of [8] the
microstructure of a root coincides with the initial (before op-
eration) microstructure due to the low operating temperature.
Our study of the material of the root (Fig. 1a ) has shown that
the microstructure of the alloy is represented by a matrix
with fcc lattice containing a coherent intermetallic/c103/c162-phase, a
/c103+ /c103/c162eutectic, and Me23C6 and MeC carbides with equiaxial
morphology. Nickel alloy IN738LC in the root part of the
blade is characterized by quite high density of the metal. The
content of segregations of /c103/c162-phase in the root part is
43 wt.%; in accordance with the data of [9] this is typical for
the initial state of the metal after standard heat treatment. The
hardening /c103/c162-phase with cubic morphology has a mean parti -
cle size of 0.45 – 0.55 /c109m. By the data of [10] this size of
particles of /c103/c162-phase is optimum from the standpoint of
high-temperature creep.
In contrast to the root, the structure of the metal in the
feather part of the blade underwent segregation of a fine dis -
persed /c103/c162-phase and coagulation of primary particles of
/c103/c162-phase (Fig. 1b ). In accordance with the data of [8] opera -
tion can also cause segregation of Me 23C6 carbide, the parti-
cles of which fill the grain particles more densely and occur
in greater number around MeC carbides, of a primary
/c103/c162-phase, and of a eutectic. We established that in the direc -
tion from the root to the end section of the feather the mean
size of particles of the hardening /c103/c162-phase increased from 0.6
to 1.9 /c109m and its content grew from 47 to 52%. In the feather
part, which was more heated in operation, the content of the
/c103/c162-phase was higher than in the root part. Growth in the con -
tent of /c103/c162-phase in operation has also been detected by the
authors of [8] in their study of alloy ZMI-3U after 22,000 h
of service.
Metallographic analysis of microporosity of specimens
cut from feather sections at different heights was performed
using a light microscope at magnification of /c180100 – 500. It
turned out that in the end section of the feather micropores
with a mean size of up to 5 /c109m were present over grain
boundaries. In the middle and root sections of the feather and
in the root zone micropores were not detected. Such distribu-
tion of micropores over the height of the feather was also
confirmed by the results of measurements of the density of
the alloy by the hydrostatic method (Table 1). It can be seen
from Table 1 that the density of the alloy in the end section of
the feather is 0.07% lower than in the root part. In accor -
dance with the data of [2] this can indicate formation of
micropores in the structure of the alloy in operation. The va -
lues of the mean density of the alloy in the specimens of the
root part and of the feather in the middle and root sections
differ inconsiderably.

The difference in the kinds of structural change in the al-
loy, which is connected with nonuniform distribution of the
temperature field over the height of the blade in operation,
affects the mechanical properties. The results of tensile and
hardness mechanical tests of specimens cut from the end part
of the feather and from the root zone are presented in Ta -
ble 2. For comparison, we also present in the table the stan -
dardized characteristics for alloy IN738LC after standard
heat treatment given in specification No. 481981.6.00009 for
HPT blades. It follows from the data of Table 2 that the me-
chanical properties of the alloy in the feather part and in the
root zone differ. In operation the ultimate rupture strength in
the middle section of the feather has decreased by a factor of
1.2 on the average, the elongation has decreased by a factor
of 1.7, and the time before failure in the endurance test has
decreased by a factor of 1.5. The results of the tests for hard-
ness of the metal in different zones of the blade also reflect
lowering of the mechanical characteristics of the alloy. For
example, the hardness of the material in the feather part is
228 HB and that in the root part is 302 HB.
When choosing the regime for RHT we took into account
the necessity for homogenizing at the temperature of com -
plete dissolution of /c103/c162-phase [11]. For nickel alloy IN738LC
the temperature of complete dissolution of /c103/c162-phase is about
1120°C [9]. However, in accordance with the data of [12]
operation causes layering of the /c103/c162-phase into two fractions
one of which is enriched with Ti, Al, and Nb. The tempera -
ture of complete dissolution of /c103/c162-phase is higher than in the
alloy in the initial state (before operation). As a result, the
homogenizing temperature of 1120°C can be insufficient for
recovery of the structure. However, it is noted in [13] that re-
covery of structure in a nickel alloy can be attained at this
temperature but at the expense of some increase in the hold
time. Having analyzed the available data we chose the fol -
lowing modes for performing RHT: ( 1 ) homogenizing at
1120°C with cooling to 100°C + double aging at 1050°C for
4 h + 845°C for 24 h and ( 2 ) homogenizing at 1140°C for
2 h with cooling to 100°C + aging at 1050°C for 4 h + 845°C
for 24 h.
Figure 2 presents the microstructure of alloy IN738LC in
the end part of the feather after RHT by regimes 1 and 2.W e
established that the hold at 1120°C does not cause complete
homogenizing of the structure of the nickel alloy; the metal
contains segregations of different-size particles of /c103/c162-phase
and remnants of fine dispersed /c103/c162-phase (Fig. 2 a ). The con -
tent of segregations of the /c103/c162-phase is about 49 wt.% and the
size of the particles fluctuates from 0.55 to 3.0 /c109m. At ho -
mogenizing temperature of 1140°C the /c103/c162-phase dissolves
virtually fully; in subsequent cooling and two-stage aging the
/c103/c162-phase segregates in the form of cubic particles 0.5 –
0.6 /c109m in size (Fig. 2 b ). The content of the /c103/c162-phase
segregations in this case is 45 wt.%, which virtually coin-
cides with the data obtained for the metal of the root part.
Metallographic analysis of the microporosity of laps has
shown that in the case of homogenizing temperature of
1120°C an inconsiderable amount of pores with a mean size
of about 4 /c109m remains on grain boundaries (Fig. 2 c ). After
homogenizing at 1140°C the structure of the alloy is dense;
grain boundaries are virtually indiscernible even under mag -
nification of /c180500 (Fig. 2d ). The results of the measurement
of the density of specimens after RHT show (Table 3) that
the heat treatment ensures some growth in the density of the
alloy and, respectively, some decrease in the level of
microporosity over grain boundaries. The density of the ma -
terial increases more after homogenizing at 1140°C.

CONCLUSIONS
1. After long-term operation the feather part of a blade
produced from alloy IN738LC has undergone structural
changes that indicate a loss in the operating capacity of HPT
blades and require conduction of reconditioning heat treat -
ment.
2. The alloy in the root part after operation has not under-
gone structural changes and its mechanical properties exhibit
a satisfactory level of operating capacity of the material.
3. Heat treatment of blades after long-term service,
which includes homogenizing at 1140°C for 2 h and two-
stage aging, ensures recovery of the structure of the alloy in
the feather zone and matching of this structure to that of the
root part of the blade. The formed particles of the hardening
/c103/c162-phase have a cubic morphology and optimum sizes, the
microporosity decreases, and the operating characteristics of
the alloy recover to the initial level.
