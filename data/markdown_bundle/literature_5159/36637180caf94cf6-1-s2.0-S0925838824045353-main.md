ABSTRACT
The Ti element was designed to optimize the Cu/Nb interface structure. The Cu-Nb and Cu-Nb-Ti alloys were
successfully prepared by laser directed energy deposition. Results show that the introduction of Ti can change the
incoherent Cu/Nb interface into the semi-coherent Cu/ β -Cu
3
Ti/Nb interface. The Cu/Nb interfaces are easily
sheared, producing an attractive force on the dislocations, and the dislocations pile up at the incoherent Cu/Nb
interfaces. As a consequence, the Cu-Nb deposited alloy exhibits a relatively low total elongation of 7.7 ± 1.1 %.
Instead, semi-coherent Cu/ β -Cu
3
Ti/Nb interfaces change the movement of dislocations, making it easier for
dislocations to transmit across the interface, thus relieving the stress concentration at the interface, which en-
dows the Cu-Nb-Ti deposited alloy the higher total elongation of 13.4 ± 0.2 %. In addition, due to the precipi-
tation strengthening and solid solution strengthening, the Cu-Nb-Ti deposited alloy exhibits higher strength of
795 ± 7 MPa.
1. Introduction
Copper alloys have been widely applied in various applications, such
as the electricity, metallurgy, nuclear power and aerospace industries
due to their excellent electrical and thermal conductivity [1 – 4] . How-
ever, low strength and poor ablative resistance are the main drawbacks
for copper alloys [5,6] , which limit their further applications such as
high-voltage contact materials. Preparing copper alloys with addition of
refractory metals such as W and Mo is an effective way to improve their
ablative resistance [7,8] . However, the phase immiscibility of W or Mo
and Cu would result in the weak interface, thereby leading to low me-
chanical properties, especially in elongation [7,9] . When used as
high-voltage contact material, they are easy to crack under the condition
of repeated mechanical force and high-temperature arc erosion [10] .
Therefore, it is significantly important to improve the mechanical
property and anti-ablative performance.
Laser directed energy deposition (LDED) is one of additive
manufacturing technologies that the deposited materials are melted by a
laser beam, achieving the fraction of complex components [11,12] .
There are some researches about depositing anti-ablation material on
the surface of copper. Wang et al. [13] utilized LDED to obtain W-Cu
composites on the surface of copper alloy to improve the arc erosion
resistance and investigated the tensile property, but the strength and
elongation were relatively low.
Nb, as one of the refractory metals, is of excellent plasticity and
toughness. Compared with W and Mo, the elastic modulus of Nb is closer
to that of Cu (Ecu = 145 GPa [9] , E

difference at the Cu/Nb interface is less than that at the Cu/W or Cu/Mo
interface, so the cracks are more difficult to generate at the Cu/Nb
interface. Therefore, Nb was designed to add into the copper alloy to
improve the anti-ablative performance.
In recent years, researches have confirmed that interface plays an
important role in improving the mechanical properties of the composites
[15,16] . It can be predicted that the elongation of Cu-Nb deposited alloy
would be low due to the mutual immiscibility in Cu-Nb system. While a
semi-coherent/coherent interface can decrease the lattice misfit and
relieve the stress concentration during the deformation of the compos-
ites [15 – 17] . Therefore, the key point to improve the elongation of the
Cu-Nb alloy is how to introduce an interphase which is

In this study, the Cu-Nb and Cu-Nb-Ti deposited alloys were suc-

of the deposited alloys were characterized and the mechanical proper-
ties were studied. We hope that the results will be helpful for the further
development of copper alloys with addition of the refractory metals.
2. Experiment
A copper alloy blocked with a dimension of 200 mm × 100 mm
× 80 mm was selected as the substrate, grit blasted before the experi-
ment to increase the absorption of laser. As shown in Fig. 1 , Nb and
CuCrZr or CuTiCrZr with a specified particle size distribution ranging
from 5 to 25 μ m and 38 – 75 μ m were used as the raw materials,
respectively. Their chemical composition is shown in Table 1 .
The LDED system consists of a 20 kW continuous-wave fiber laser
combined with a 6-axis robot. The laser beam is focused on the substrate
surface in a laser spot size of approximately 6 mm × 8 mm. The pro-
cessing parameters for samples were laser power in the range of
8 – 14 kW, a laser scanning speed of 1500 mm/min, a Nb powder feed
rate of 8 g/min and a CuCrZr or CuTiCrZr powder feed rate of 12 g/min.
As the laser beam scanned, the 4 layers of the deposited alloys were
manufactured with the dimension of 120 mm × 80 mm × 3.4 mm to
obtain the samples.
The samples were cut by wire electrical discharge machining (EDM).
The metallographic samples were cut from the cross-section of the
deposited alloys, mechanically ground and polished to be analyzed by
scanning electron microscopy (SEM) in back scattered-electron (BSE)
mode. For transmission electron microscopy (TEM) characterization, the
substructures of the deposited alloys were characterized by using an FEI
Tecnai G2 F30. The tensile samples with gauge length of 25 mm, width
of 6.0 mm and thickness of 2.0 mm were investigated with a video
extensometer on a Shimadzu AG-100 kN tester at room temperature
with a 1 mm/min loading speed, and each type of samples were
repeated three times.

3.2. Mechanical properties
Fig. 7 (a) and (b) present the engineering stress-strain curves and
mechanical property testing results of the Cu-Nb and Cu-Nb-Ti deposited
alloys. The ultimate tensile strength (UTS) and yield strength (YS) of Cu-
Nb deposited alloy reach 535 ± 9 MPa and 305 ± 7 MPa, respectively,
with total elongation (TE) value of 7.7 ± 1.1 %. Compared with the Cu-
Nb deposited alloy, the UTS and YS of the Cu-Nb-Ti deposited alloy
exhibit a much higher performance of 795 ± 7 MPa and 558 ± 6 MPa,
respectively. In addition, it should be noted that the TE of Cu-Nb-Ti
deposited alloy reaches 13.4 ± 0.2 %, which is about 1.7 times higher
than that of the Cu-Nb deposited alloy.
Compared with the Cu-Nb deposited alloy, the strengthening in-
crements in Cu-Nb-Ti deposited alloy are attributed to precipitation
strengthening and solid solution strengthening. For precipitation
strengthening, β ′ -Cu
4
Ti which is fully coherent with the Cu matrix is the
precipitated phase at the nanoscale observed in the Cu-Nb-Ti deposited
alloy, as shown in Fig. 8 (a-b), which impedes the dislocation motion
[19] . When Ti was added into the Cu-Nb-Ti deposited alloy, the Ti can
solubilize in Cu and Nb and the solubilized Ti can distort the crystal
lattice of Cu and Nb. The lattice distortion can block the motion of the
dislocations [20] . Therefore, the strength of the Cu-Nb-Ti deposited
alloy was improved.
It has been demonstrated that the Cu/Nb interface is an incoherent
interface [21] . The interfaces are able to act as the barrier to dislocations
glide. It is relatively difficult for the dislocations to pass through the
incoherent Cu/Nb interface [21 – 23] . During the deformation of the
sample, the Cu/Nb interfaces are easily sheared under the stress field of
dislocations owing to the low interfacial shear resistance [21] . More-
over, the sheared Cu/Nb interfaces produce an attractive force on the
dislocations, thus the dislocations pile up at the incoherent Cu/Nb in-
terfaces, which further hinder dislocations motion [21] . As a result, the
Cu-Nb deposited alloy exhibits a relatively low TE.
As mentioned above, both the β -Cu

interface [22,23] , which endows the Cu-Nb-Ti deposited alloy the higher
TE.
4. Conclusions
In summary, the Cu-Nb and Cu-Nb-Ti deposited alloys were suc-
cessfully prepared on the surface of the copper alloy by LDED. After the
introduction of Ti, the Cu-Nb-Ti deposited alloy exhibits a higher
strength due to the precipitation strengthening and solid solution
strengthening. The Cu/Nb interface is incoherent that the dislocations
can not pass through the Cu/Nb interface and then pile up at the inco-
herent Cu/Nb interface during the deformation of the Cu-Nb deposited
alloy. After Ti addition, the semi-coherent Cu/ β -Cu
