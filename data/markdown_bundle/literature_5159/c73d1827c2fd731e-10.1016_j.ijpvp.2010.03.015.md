abstract
The carbon and nitrogen free new alloys which were composed of supersaturated martensitic micro-
structure with high dislocation density before the creep test have been investigated systematically. These
alloys were produced from the new approach which raised creep strength by the utilization of the
reverse transformed austenite phase as a matrix and intermetallic compounds such as Laves phase and
mu-phase as precipitates during heating before the creep test. It is important that these alloys are
independent of any carbides and nitrides as strengthening factors. The high temperature creep test over
700
/C14 C exceeds 50,000 h, and the test is continuous. Creep behavior of the alloys is found to be different
from that of the conventional high-Cr ferritic steels. The addition of boron to the alloy pulled the
recrystallization temperature up in the high temperature, and it became a creep test in the un-recrys-
tallization condition, and the creep property of high temperature over 700 /C14 C was drastically improved.
The minimum creep rates of Fe eNi alloys at 700 /C14 C are found to be much lower than those of the
conventional high Cr ferritic heat resistant steels, which is due to ﬁne dispersion strengthening useful
even at 700 /C14 C in these alloys. As a result it became clear that the value for 100,000 h was exceeded at
700 /C14 C and 100 MPa calculated from the Larson-Miller parameter at C ¼ 20.

1. Introduction
In the large diameter tube like main steam pipe and header for
the USC power plant boiler, the increase of using steam tempera-
ture and pressure requires creep strength in high temperature over
600 /C14 C or improved heat resistant material. The goal steam
condition rises to 30 MPa at 630 /C14 C at present [1], and it is necessary
to develop the new heat resistant steel which is more excellent in
creep strength and waterproof steam oxidation characteristic than
existing P91 high Cr ferritic heat resistant steels.
In Europe development of boilers and steam turbines for plant
operating at temperatures in excess of 700 /C14 C is being carried out
within the EU supported AD700 project [2]. The collaborative
involves all of the major European power plant manufacturers
supported by utilities and research institutes. The ﬁrst phase of the
project began in 1998 and runs until the end of 2003. Second phase
began in 2002 and runs until the end of 2005. The objective is the
development of all technology necessary for the construction and
operation of such plant.
Various carbon and nitrogen free steels were produced based on
the new approach that the improvement has been applicable for
the application which required long time creep properties at high
temperatures were attempted [3e8]. They have made the maraging
steels which have originally combined high strength and excellent
toughness at room temperature as the starting material before the
creep test. A
c1 temperature of them locates below 650 /C14 C, since Ni is
abundantly contained, and the reverted transformation to austenite
occurs before the creep test.
We have melted about 40 heats in order to achieve the superior
heat resistant steels to the existing austenitic steels and super
alloys at 700
/C14 C or more. In this study, we selected three kinds of
steels with the highest creep strength of all, and effect of micro-
structure on the transformation behavior, creep strength and the
oxidation characteristic has been investigated.

3.2. Creep properties at 650 and 700 /C14 C
Fig. 2 shows relationship between creep rate and time curves of
the specimen crept at 650 /C14 C and 100 and 120 MPa. Creep rates of
three steels similarly have been decreasing with increasing time
even after 50,000 h. Among them creep rate of X73 indicates the
lowest value even in the higher applied stress of 120 MPa than
those of P7 and P8 in 100 MPa.
Fig. 3 shows relationship between creep rate and time curves of
the specimen crept at 700 /C14 C and 100 MPa. Creep rate of all three
steels decreases with increasing time monotonously. The testing
time of P8 passed 21,000h. The similar time of P7 was 65,000h. And
the time of X73 was 55,000h. The creep test of them is continuing
to now in the transition creep deformation region close to the
minimum creep rate.
Fig. 4 shows relationship between stress and time to rupture of
the specimen crept at 700
/C14 C in three. P7 and X73 exhibited similar
tendency with decreasing the applied stress. Time to rupture of two
steels gradually elongated with decreasing the stress. Ruptured
stress of both steels in 100,000 h expected at about 150 MPa from
the extrapolated line into the ﬁgure. While the slope of P8 steeply
decreased from 300 to 80 MPa. As clearly shown in this ﬁgure
ruptured stress was about 80 MPa at 100,000 h.

4. Conclusion
We have melted about 40 heats in order to achieve the superior
heat resistant steels to the existing austenitic steels and super alloys
at 700 /C14 C or more. In this study, we selected three kinds of steels with
the highest creep strength of all, and effect of microstructure on the
transformation behavior, creep strength and the oxidation charac-
teristic has been investigated. 12Ni e9Coe10We9Cre0.005B (P8)
steel was an austenitic steel but the coefﬁcient of thermal expansion
was considerably smaller than that of Type 316. Creep strength at
700
/C14 C and 100, 000 h of P8 was estimated about 80 MPa. Steam
oxidation resistance of P8 at 700 and 750 /C14 C was remarkable lower
than that of Type 316. 12Ni e9Coe10We5Cre0.005B (P7) and
12Nie5Cre10Moe0.005B (X73) steels were martensitic steels. They
were easy to be occurred the reverted austenite transformation
during the heating process before the creep test. Creep strength at
700 /C14 C and 100,000 h of P7 and X73 steels were estimated about
150 MPa. A further research is necessary for an improvement of
oxidation resistance though it is in the situation to be able to reach
the objective for the creep strength.
