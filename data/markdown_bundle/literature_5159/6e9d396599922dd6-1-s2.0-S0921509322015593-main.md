ABSTRACT 
The microscopic mechanism of time-dependent crack growth (TDCG) in single crystal (SC) Ni-based superalloys 
under a relatively high-temperature condition (900 
◦
C) is critically investigated. Two alloy types are compared: 
alloy A containing 3% rhenium (Re) and alloy B containing no Re. With an initial stress intensity ( K ) value of 40 
MPam
1/2
, both alloys show similar two step TDCG, i.e. a steady stage followed by an acceleration stage. The total 
life to failure ( t
f
) of alloy B is, however, an order of magnitude shorter than alloy A. In addition to the con -
ventional fractography, cross-sectional analyses (EBSD and EDS) are also conducted for an interrupted crack tip. 
Two distinct fracture modes are then identified in both alloys: {111} < 112 > twin-induced shear fracture and 
non-crystallographic fracture along dendrite boundaries. The twin systems observed are the secondary ones 
having a relatively smaller Schmid factor compared to the primary ones that remain inactive under the < 100 >
tension. In the case of alloy A, the twin induces local phase transformation involving TCP precipitation, 
decomposition of γ (Ni)/ γ’ (Ni
3
Al) structure and recrystallization, which eventually enhances local ductility and 
dominates the crack growth rate. In the case of alloy B, the macroscopic appearances of these fracture modes are 
much the same, but they are significantly facilitated by micro-voids formed exclusively in γ channel whose 
stability is much lower than that in alloy A. The apparently brittle TDCG nature of alloy B compared to alloy A 
can be interpreted as an enhanced localization of plasticity at the crack tip. 
1. Introduction 
Owing to the growing demand for cost/carbon effective power 
generation, ensuring long term durability of gas turbine generators has 
gained much more attention. Since recent high efficiency gas turbines 
rely their high temperature operation on the usage of single crystal (SC) 
nickel (Ni)-based superalloys as blade materials, their mechanical 
characteristics under various temperature/stress conditions are vital 
information. Typically, power switching (including start/shutdown) and 
steady running between each switching may respectively cause blade 
failure through cycle-dependent (fatigue) crack growth mode and time- 
dependent (creep) crack growth mode. The mechanisms of these failure 
modes in SC superalloys therefore must be carefully examined for the 
accurate life estimation of the blades as well as for the proposal of less 
damaging turbine operation. 
As far as SC superalloys are concerned, studies focusing on the 
fundamental properties of fatigue crack growth (FCG) are in fact larger 
in number compared to those independently treating creep crack growth 
(CCG) or, more generally, time-dependent crack growth (TDCG). This 
trend has an inevitable connection to the nature of SC alloys, i.e. the 
absence of grain boundaries (GBs) where creep damage accumulation 
and hence TDCG are most likely to prevail [ 1 – 4 ]. In addition, evaluation 
of fatigue (including dwell fatigue) properties at elevated temperatures 
inherently contains both cycle-/time-dependent mechanisms [ 5 ], which 
gives higher motivation from a practical (design oriented) standpoint. 
With such backgrounds, the authors have made our original 
electron-microscopic analyses on the high-temperature FCG in failed SC 
turbine blades and simulated lab specimens [ 6 ]. We then found that the 
FCG rate (d a /d N ) under a relatively harsh condition (in 900

subsequent brittle cracking of ultrathin oxide film along γ (Ni matrix)/ γ’

Hints on TDCG mechanisms of SC superalloys involving the crack tip

nologically picked up [ 13 – 24 ], basically employ unnotched specimens 
suitable for the observation of damage accumulation and/or evolution 
within relatively large volume subjected to uniform stress. Sharply 
notched or pre-cracked specimens are necessary for the evaluation of 
pure TDCG whose process zone size around the crack tip in SC alloys will 
be fairly limited perhaps to less than several microns. 
Another interesting topic that should be pursued is the contribution 
of rhenium (Re) on TDCG mechanism. The addition of Re in Ni-based SC 
superalloys is known to alter their high-temperature durability, such as 
elongated creep/fatigue failure life or improved Larson-Miller (LM) 
parameter [ 25 – 30 ]. The kinetic aspect of the “ Re effect ” is quantitatively 
(and most symbolically) expressed e.g. in a systematic work by 
Fleischmann et al. [ 31 ]: the addition of Re more effectively lowers 
steady creep rate than other refractory elements i.e. molybdenum (Mo) 
and tungsten (W). The underlying atomistic mechanism of such phe -
nomena has drawn significant attention (see Ref. [ 32 ] for example), and 
critical findings on Re-defects interaction [ 33 , 34 ] are now being facili -
tated e.g. by atom probe technique. Despite the above situation, no 
studies have ever explicitly treated how the added Re affects the local 
deformation/fracture behavior around a growing crack tip, which re -
quires a detailed characterization conducted with suitable techniques 
under suitable magnification. 
The aim of this study is firstly to investigate the TDCG properties of 
Ni-based SC superalloys. Particular attention is then paid to the micro -
scopic characterization of a life controlling crack tip via scanning elec -
tron microscopy (SEM), electron backscattered diffraction (EBSD) 
analysis and energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS). The micro -
scopic mechanism of Re effect on the crack growth properties is then 
discussed.

Fig. 5 shows the fracture surface micrographs of alloys A and B. The 
crack takes two distinctly different fracture paths soon after it initiates

4. Discussion 
The macro/microscopic aspects of time-dependent crack growth in 
two alloy types (A, B) has been investigated with various methods. The 
morphological appearance of the post-mortem specimens, at a first 
glance, has analogous features, see Figs. 4 (a) and Fig. 5 . This can be 
related to the similarity of crack growth behavior in terms of t / t
f 
shown 
in Fig. 4 (c): the mixing of different fracture modes (i.e. shear and 
interdendritic) at any moment is basically dependent on the same me -
chanical condition. The distinct difference of crack growth behavior in 
terms of the real time ( Fig. 4 (b)), however, suggests that the microscopic 
process at the crack tip is essentially time-dependent, whose rate is 
affected strongly by the difference of chemical composition. The ana -
lyses further suggest that the shear fracture is probably the main rate- 
limiting process in the current sigmoidal crack growth. Conversely, 
the interdendritic fracture that preferably appears at the mid-thickness 
area or during the final acceleration stage is a relatively brittle, fast 
propagation mode. These speculations at first sight go against the 
existing literatures reporting the worse effect of shear fracture encoun -
tered in smooth bar specimens [ 40 – 44 ]. Recall that the fracture of a 
plate specimen generally advances more rapidly in the thickness center 
than at the surface due to the plain strain conditions prevailing in the 
center and the lesser plane strain toughness [ 1 ]. In the present cases, 
shear fracture at the specimen center is effectively restrained due to the 
crack tip stress triaxiality and hence interdendritic fracture is facilitated 
instead. The competition of these modes and its influence on the char -
acteristic life properties ( Fig. 4 ) explicitly indicate the importance of 
stress state on fracture prediction. 
The crack tip micro-analyses ( Figs. 6 – 9 ) clearly indicate that the 
shear fracture is preceded by the formation of twin bands. Of the twelve 
twin systems subjected to [100] tension, TW1: (11-1)[112] and TW2:

The drastically lower crack growth resistance of alloy B assisted by 
micro-void formation can be rationally understood by the absence of the 
Re effect. The essential mechanism of Re effect has been generally linked 
to the low diffusivity of solute Re in Ni matrix [ 32 ]. Since Re has 
significantly higher partitioning preference to γ phase than other ele -
ments (e.g. W, Mo) [ 31 ], it is expected to effectively hinder the activity 
of vacancies and/or dislocations necessary for creep void formation [ 4 ] 
in γ channel. Recent studies have further shown that solute Re in γ phase 
can enter γ
′

non-negligible effect on the creep deformation rate at an intermediate 
temperature (750

resistance achieved by alloy A can therefore be attributable to the better 
γ

thereafter together with the basic stability of γ phase. 
The apparently “ brittle ” crack growth behavior encountered in alloy 
B can be also discussed from another viewpoint. Fig. 12 shows the 
relation between load point displacement, δ , and crack length, a . Here, δ 
is used as an alternative measure for crack opening displacement (COD). 
Although δ contains both macroscopic elastic deflection and near-tip 
plasticity, the latter can be effectively compared provided that the 
elastic rigidities of both alloys can be regarded as the same. Both alloys 
show almost linear relation before reaching the final acceleration stage. 
Alloy B generally shows larger a at the same value of δ , indicating that 
alloy B is macroscopically brittle than alloy A. Or, at the same value of a , 
alloy A shows larger (about twice as much) ductility than alloy B. The 
extremely localized plasticity (i.e. small scale creep void formation) and 
its strong contribution to the crack tip advancement then reasonably 
explains the apparently “ brittle ” behavior of alloy B. Such an interpre -
tation has an essential affinity to, e.g., the localized plasticity theory of 
hydrogen embrittlement (so-called HELP theory [ 53 , 54 ]) where solute 
hydrogen atoms collected around a crack tip promote dislocation ac -
tivity and enhance local ductility .

mechanism owing essentially to the smaller number of initial disloca -
tions remained in γ channels. The contribution of oxidation to the latter 
mechanism is, however, quite complex since various defects (twin 
boundaries, boundaries of recrystallized micro-grains, creep voids and 
dislocations), all of which may act as possible diffusion paths for 
incoming oxygen atoms, are dynamically formed in the crack tip 
deformation bands. In other words, the effect of oxide formation may be 
inseparably contained in the degradation process. The authors therefore 
should carefully use the general term “ time-dependent ” instead of “ creep ” 
for describing the present crack growth phenomenon at this stage of 
study. Nevertheless, it is worth noting that the observed crack growth 
rate of alloy A (6.4 × 10
 10 
m/s) is orders of magnitude smaller than 
that of a conventional polycrystalline (PC) Ni-based superalloy (IN718) 
measured under the same K level and temperature [ 3 ]. The strong effect 
of oxygen embrittlement due to fast diffusion along grain boundaries (i. 
e. approx. one micrometer per second in 900 
◦
C air) of Ni and related 
superalloys is also indicated [ 55 ]. The effect of oxygen attack on the 
present crack tip may thus be of little significance compared to the 
thermally activated creep processes triggered by twin formation. 
5. Summary and conclusions 
The time-dependent fracture mechanism of single crystal (SC) Ni- 
based superalloys subjected to a relatively high temperature condition 
(900 
◦
C) was critically investigated by focusing on the life-controlling 
crack introduced in a CT specimen and its microscopic features. Two 
alloy types (alloy A with 3%Re, alloy B with no Re) were compared 
under the same testing condition to further facilitate discussion. The 
main results obtained are summarized as follows. 
(1) With an initial K value of 40 MPam
1/2
, both alloys showed similar 
two step time-dependent crack growth (TDCG), i.e. a steady stage 
followed by an acceleration stage. The total life to failure ( t
f
) of 
alloy B was, however, an order of magnitude shorter than that of 
alloy A. 
(2) Two distinct fracture modes were identified in both alloys: 
{111} < 112 > twin-induced shear fracture and non- 
crystallographic fracture along dendrite boundaries. Both of 
these fracture modes macroscopically appeared in the same 
fashion. 
(3) The twin systems observed were secondary ones having a rela -
tively smaller Schmid factor. The primary ones having a larger 
value but anti-twin in nature were inactive in the present crack 
growth under the [100] tension. 
(4) In the case of alloy A, the crack tip twin deformation triggered the 
subsequent local phase transformation: precipitation of TCP, 
decomposition of the original γ / γ
′
structure and recrystallization. 
Such a transformation was postulated to enhance local ductility 
and dominate the crack growth rate.

(5) In the case of alloy B, both of the above fracture modes were

The present study clearly points out that the mechanism of TDCG in 
Ni-based SC superalloys can be a complex issue when different fracture 
modes coexist (compete) depending on the stress state. Studies on the 
effect of specimen thickness are therefore important. Temperature is 
also expected to have a significant effect (particularly on twinning) that 
may directly change the fracture modes. The effect of Re should also be 
examined in combination with these conditions. These topics will be 
pursued elsewhere.
