1. Introduction 
Recently, high-entropy alloys (HEAs) with multiple elements have 
attracted massive concerns in the metallic material community [1 – 5] . 
Enlarged compositional possibilities in HEA systems provide substantial 
space for designing alloys with the desired strength-ductility combina -
tions [6 – 11] . Among them, eutectic HEAs (EHEAs), especially those 
consisting of face-centered cubic (FCC) and body-centered cubic 
(BCC)/ordered BCC (B2) structures [12 – 15] , have exhibited unique 
properties such as excellent castability [ 16 , 17 ], particular damage 
behavior [18] , and mechanical properties at ambient temperature 
[19 – 23] . For this class of materials, the FCC/BCC phase boundary (PB) is 
a key factor in determining their mechanical properties [24] . 
The effects of the interface on the deformation of EHEAs at ambient 
temperature have been widely investigated in terms of interface strength 
and strain transfer. Wang and Misra [25] summarized the interaction 
between interfaces and lattice dislocations on the interfacial shear 
strength, particularly highlighted the high interfacial shear strengths 
observed in semi-coherent FCC/BCC interfaces with a Kurdjumov-Sachs 
orientation relationship. Basu et al. [26] established a correlation be -
tween nanoindentation-induced plasticity and local residual stresses, 
revealing that the FCC/BCC boundary in the Al
0.7
CoCrFeNi HEA 
demonstrates approximately four times higher total interfacial 
strengthening stress compared to conventional FCC/BCC boundaries. 
The susceptibility to fracture of the FCC/BCC boundary was also evi -
denced quantitatively by the nano-indentation results. 
Although the positive effects of the FCC/BCC PB on room- 
temperature strengthening have been confirmed [ 24 , 26 ], the situation 
will be different at elevated temperatures. At elevated temperatures, the 
FCC/B2 type EHEAs exhibit considerable flow stress [ 27 , 28 ], 
outstanding thermal stability [29 – 31] , and excellent oxidation resis -
tance [ 32 , 33 ], making them promising candidates for severe working 
conditions. Nevertheless, the softening and cracking of PB significantly 
limit the high-temperature mechanical properties of FCC/B2-type 
EHEAs. On the one hand, boundary softening often leads to reduced 
high-temperature strength by promoting grain sliding and boundary 
decohesion [34] . On the other hand, due to the premature failure 
induced by PB decohesion, the deformation ability of the alloys cannot 
be fully released, leading to reduced ductility at both ambient and high 
temperatures. 
Generally, minor boron doping could significantly improve the high- 
temperature mechanical properties of various alloys [35 – 42] , and there 
are several theories proposed for its beneficial effects. Firstly, the most 
popular theory indicates an increase in boundary strength and cohesion

3. Results 
3.1. Characterization of initial microstructures 
Back-scattered electron (BSE) images showing the initial micro -
structures of boron-free, 0.03B, 0.30B, and 1.00B alloys in the PSRA 
state are given in Fig. 1 . Previous studies have shown that the bright and 
dark regions correspond to the FCC and B2 phases, respectively [16] . 
The B2 phase appears as strip-shaped structures in the four alloys, with a 
volume fraction of approximately 40 %, an average length of 10 to 60 
μ m and a thickness of less than 10 μ m. There are needle-shaped FCC 
precipitates in the B2 phase with a length of around 0.2 μ m. Generally, 
the eutectic lamellar microstructure of as-cast EHEAs formed within a 
single grain [16] . However, recrystallization occurs during the 
PSR/PSRA processes, the interconnected FCC phase is composed of 
multiple FCC grains, while each individual B2 phase forms a separate 
grain [45] . The microstructures of the four alloys in Fig. 1 are quite 
similar. No significant precipitates can be observed at boundaries in the 
micron scale. Therefore, the addition of a small amount of boron has no 
significant effect on the microstructure of the alloy in the PSRA state. 
Nevertheless, based on previous studies on boron ’ s effect in superalloys 
[ 40 , 44 , 46 , 47 ], it is anticipated that doping boron will substantially 
enhance the mechanical properties of the EHEAs, despite its slight 
impact on the initial microstructures.

Compared with the boron-free alloy, the 800 
◦

ultimate tensile strength after doping boron. The high-temperature 
strengthening effect of boron element can be attributed to three 
possible factors: interstitial solid solution strengthening, boride precip -

First, we discuss the possible interstitial solid solution strengthening 
effect of boron in the EHEAs. Previous studies have revealed that boron 
doping in NiAl-type B2 intermetallic compounds occupies the smaller 
octahedral interstitial site in the B2 structure, resulting in significant 
lattice distortion and large misfit strains [52 – 54] . George and Liu [55] 
found that boron has a significant strengthening effect on the yield 
strength of NiAl and Ni

primarily focused on the strengthening effect at ambient temperature, 
providing limited insights in their high-temperature effects. In order to

phase in the EHEAs studied in this research, we compared the boron-free 
and boron-doped B2 and FCC phases, respectively. The chemical 
composition of B2 and FCC phases in the boron-free alloy is determined

doped in both the two compositions to simulate the boron-doped B2 and

lower boundary strength than the grain interior, rendering it more 
susceptible to crack propagation along the boundary. Conversely, the 
boundary strength of the boron-containing alloys is much higher than 
that of the grain interior, resulting in cracks propagated inside the 
grains. Theoretically, grain sliding and cracking can reduce yield 
strength at high temperatures [57] . Therefore, the boron-doped alloys 
can be strengthened by improving the boundary cohesion strength.

segregation of boron at the PBs. Secondly, microcracks in the 0B alloy

C are the FCC/B2 PBs. For the

strengthening effect on PBs is more significant than on GBs. The 
improved boundary cohesion strength coordinates boundary sliding 
during high-temperature deformation, allowing the grains to accom -

for the attainment of ultra-high elongation in the 0.30B alloy. During the 
high-temperature deformation of boron-free alloy, the premature failure 
of the boundary hinders the release of its deformation capacity. In 
contrast, the boundary of the 0.30B alloy is strengthened by boron, 
facilitating a sustained dynamic recovery process, which effectively 
stimulates the exceptional high-temperature deformation capability of 
the alloy.
