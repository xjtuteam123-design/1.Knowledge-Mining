ABSTRACT 
The significance of segregation processes during high temperature deformation in superalloys has become 
increasingly evident in recent years. Here, the importance of additionally tailoring the active deformation 
mechanisms is shown by correlating the strain-rate dependent high temperature strength of two slightly different 
polycrystalline CoNi-base superalloys with their underlying defect structures. Transmission electron microscopy 
investigations revealed that at high strain-rates shearing by APB-coupled dislocation pairs occurs in both alloys. 
With decreasing strain-rate, the deformation mechanism in one alloy transitions to stacking fault shearing and at 
very low strain-rates also to microtwinning, while APB-based shearing remains dominant in the other alloy. In 
comparison, stacking fault shearing induced a relative increase in strength of 200 MPa, which is diminished to 
100 MPa as microtwinning becomes more prevalent implying that the high temperature strength of this alloy 
system can be significantly increased by tailoring the acting deformation mechanism. 
Polycrystalline superalloys are widely used as structural material for 
gas turbine discs due to their excellent property profile consisting of 
outstanding high temperature strength and resistance against oxidation 
and corrosion. In order to increase the operating temperature to improve 
gas turbine efficiency and reduce CO
2 
emissions, significant efforts are 
being made to increase the temperature capability of this alloy system. 
While commercially only Ni-base superalloys are used, advanced poly -
crystalline CoNi-base superalloys have been developed in recent years, 
which have excellent high temperature strength, oxidation resistance 
and a wide processing window for cast & wrought processing [1 – 5] . 
Previous investigations on the high temperature deformation mecha -
nisms in polycrystalline CoNi-base superalloys revealed the occurrence 
of stacking fault shearing and microtwinning during tensile creep 
similar to Ni-base superalloys [ 6 , 7 ]. 
In general, the deformation behavior of Ni-, CoNi- and Co-base su -
peralloys transitions from athermal mechanisms, such as shearing of the 
L1
2
-ordered γ’ precipitate phase by anti-phase boundary (APB)-coupled 
dislocations pairs or looping via the Orowan mechanism, to segregation- 
assisted deformation mechanisms with increasing temperature [8 – 12] . 
A similar transition also occurs with decreasing stress or strain-rate as 
long as the temperature is high enough [9] . Numerous 
segregated-assisted mechanisms, i.e., shearing under superlattice 
intrinsic or extrinsic stacking fault (SISF/SESF) formation, 
microtwinning, shearing under the formation of an APB-SISF-APB 
configuration or segregation-assisted shearing under APB formation by 
either single dislocations or pairs, have been observed as a function of 
temperature, stress, grain orientation and alloy composition [ 8 , 9 , 
13 – 17 ]. 
Depending on the alloy composition and the type of planar defect, 
different alloying elements segregate in various magnitudes to or away 
from the defects [ 14 , 18 – 23 ]. Atomic-scale transmission electron mi -
croscopy (TEM) investigations by Smith et al. [24 – 26] revealed that 
segregation to stacking faults can also induce a local phase trans -
formation along the stacking faults to either the D0
19
-ordered χ , 
D0
24
-ordered η or the unordered fcc γ matrix phase. The former two 
transformations are advantageous as they impede detrimental defor -
mation mechanisms such as SF ribbons or microtwinning and thus lead 
to an improved high temperature strength. Nevertheless, segregation 
processes in general are considered to decrease the high temperature 
strength of these alloys by lowering planar fault energies and increasing 
the dislocation propagation velocity [ 18 , 19 , 27 ]. Recently, Barba et al. 
determined the propagation velocities of various deformation mecha -
nisms and concluded that shearing by APB-coupled dislocation pairs is 
desirable from an alloy design perspective as the propagation velocity is 
slower in comparison with SISF/SESF shearing and microtwinning [19] .

In this work, we investigated the strain-rate dependency of two 
complex, polycrystalline CoNi-base superalloys over four orders of 
magnitude using constant strain-rate and creep experiments. These al -
loys were designed to vary only slightly in their chemistry to induce 
different deformation mechanisms while exhibiting comparable micro -
structures. By correlating the mechanical properties and the defect 
structures of both alloys, the effect of these different deformation 
mechanisms on strain-rate dependence and high temperature strength is 
evaluated. The aim of this study is to find the superior deformation 
mechanism for enhanced creep strength of polycrystalline γ’ strength -
ened superalloys.

Early alloy development in Co-base and CoNi-base superalloys 
focused on optimizing the γ’ volume fraction, increasing the γ’ solvus 
temperature and improving the thermal stability of the γ / γ’ micro -
structure [29 – 33] . Extensive characterization on the resulting alloys 
revealed that a variety of different deformation mechanisms can be 
observed in this alloy system at intermediate temperatures [ 6 , 13 – 16 , 
22 ]. By varying the strain-rate, the transition in the deformation 
behavior of PHESA1 and PHESA2 was systematically investigated in the 
present study revealing different mechanisms in both alloys at low 
strain-rates despite only small compositional differences regarding the 
Al and Ta + Ti content. While Ta and Ti strongly partition to the γ’ pre -
cipitates [ 32 , 34 ], these elements are neither substantially enriched nor 
depleted along planar defects in comparison to other alloying elements 
in similar CoNi-base superalloys [ 12 , 18 , 20 ]. However, Ta and Ti are 
known to significantly impact planar fault energies [ 35 , 36 ], which has a 
dramatic effect on the deformation behavior as simulated by Feng et al. 
[37] . As shown by numerous studies [38 – 41] , Ta and Ti strongly in -
crease the APB energy and also the SISF and CISF energies in Ni
3
(Al,X).

Since stacking fault shearing is a segregation-assisted mechanism 
[ 18 , 20 , 24 , 42 ], the amount and type of segregating alloying elements 
critically influence the alloy performance. As shown by Smith et al. 
[24 – 26] , segregation of matrix-stabilizing elements to stacking faults 
lead to detrimental deformation mechanisms such as stacking fault 
ribbon shearing and microtwinning, which also led to a relative reduc -
tion in strength of about 100 MPa in this study. However, these mech -
anisms can be inhibited if segregation to stacking faults and microtwin 
boundaries is tailored to induce a local phase transformation to the or -
dered χ or η phase instead of the disordered γ phase [ 25 , 26 , 43 ]. 
Furthermore, segregation significantly influences the propagation ve -
locity of the stacking faults [ 19 , 27 ]. Hence, in addition to tailoring the 
deformation mechanism to stacking fault shearing, it is also necessary to 
optimize the segregation behavior of the alloying elements in order to 
increase the high temperature capability of this alloy system.
