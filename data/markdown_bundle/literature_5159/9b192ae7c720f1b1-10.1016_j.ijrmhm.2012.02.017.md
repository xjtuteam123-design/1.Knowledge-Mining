Fine-grained tungsten heavy alloys (WHAs) were successfully produced using the high-energy ball milling
assisted spark plasma sintering (SPS) method. The effects of increasing sintering temperatures on the micro-
structure and mechanical properties of the alloy were studied in detail. The hardness of the alloy was found
to continuously decrease from 79.3 to 63.8 HRA. In contrast, the bending strength continuously increased
from 353.6 to 954.5 MPa. W grain size increased with increased sintering temperature. The temperature
ranges from 1000 to 1100 °C and 1150 to 1200 °C were a period of rapid growth of W grain. According to
the color change in the scanning electron microscope (SEM) image, the W alloy microstructure were
classiﬁed into white W grains, off-white W-rich particles, dark grey matrix γ-(Ni, Fe, W), as well as
pitch-black W- and O-rich particles. The bending fracture of the alloy mainly displays the features of intergra-
nular fracture. The microporosity of different sizes was distributed on the bending fracture, and grew with
increased sintering temperature.

1. Introduction
Tungsten heavy alloys (WHAs) have excellent properties such as
high density, hardness, strength, and ductility. Hence, these alloys
are widely used in the defense, aviation, and civil engineering
industries. With the scienti ﬁc and technological development, there
is an urgent need for improving the properties of WHAs. Using ﬁne-
powder raw materials and/or novel techniques to prepare ﬁne- or
ultraﬁne-grained WHAs are the current trends. Fine- or ultra ﬁne-
grained WHAs are usually prepared via sintering ultra ﬁne/nano W
composite powders and controlling the growth of the W grains.
Nanocrystalline W alloy powders are mainly prepared by mechanical
alloying [1–6] and spray drying [7,8]. The mechanical alloying
method has the advantages of simple equipment and process and
easy operation that make it suitable for mass production. Hence,
ﬁne-grained WHAs are primarily prepared in this way. The grain
growth of nanocrystalline W alloy powders during sintering is pres-
ently controlled by solid-phase sintering and two-step sintering. In
the solid-phase sintering, the W grain size of the alloys can be con-
trolled to 5 μm or less [9,10]. However, the W/W connectivity is
large, and the ductility of the alloy is low [9]. After the solid-phase
sintering, if the second step (liquid-phase sintering) is introduced,
the microstructural uniformity and elongation of the WHAs will be
improved to a certain extent. The alloys produced by two-step sinter-
ing have large W grains [11,12], which negatively affect the perfor-
mance of WHAs. Recently, a newly developed sintering technique
called spark plasma sintering (SPS) is used in the preparation of
WHAs [13,14]. For example, Li et al prepared the WHAs by SPS tech-
nique and investigated the effects of heating rates on the densi ﬁca-
tion behavior and the microstructure evolution of WHAs [13]. The

2. Experimental
The raw materials included W, Ni, and Fe powders. The W powder
had particle sizes of 2.3 –2.7 μm, and the Ni and Fe powders had
particle sizes of 1 –3 μm. The purity of all powders was higher than
99.5%. The powders were mixed at a mass ratio of 90:7:3 (W:Ni:Fe).
First, the W alloy composite powders were placed in a QM-2SP20
planetary high-energy ball mill. The weight ratio of ball-to-powder
was 5:1. The ball milling speed was 266 rpm, the milling time was
40 h, and the ball mill atmosphere was Ar gas. Second, the powders
were sintered in a SPS-825 spark plasma sintering system for 8 min
at 50 MPa pressure, 1000 –1250 °C temperature, and 100 °C/min heat-
ing rate. The powders were then cooled to room temperature, and the
ﬁne-grained WHAs were successfully obtained.

3.2. Effects of sintering temperatures on the phases and microstructures
of WHAs
Fig. 2 shows the XRD patterns of the WHAs prepared by HEBM
assisted SPS method. The strongest diffraction peak in all of samples
is that for W phase, which is expected related to the high W
proportion of raw powders (90 wt.%). Weak peaks of γ-(Ni, Fe)
solid solution are observed in the alloy phases. A small amount of
NiW phase also appears. The formation of NiW intermetallic
compound is possibly related to the impurities produced by the
high-energy ball milling process. During the sintering process, the
existence of NiW phase becomes the obstacles of atomic diffusion
and migration, and makes the γ phase brittle. This causes the decline
of the mechanical properties of WHAs.
Fig. 3 shows the scanning electron microscopy images of the
WHAs. The small black particles in the ﬁgure represent the
γ-(Ni, Fe, W) binding phase, whereas the white ones are the W
grains. The microstructure of the alloy varied with the sintering tem-
perature. With increased temperature, the W grains grow larger.
From 1000 to 1100 °C and 1150 to 1200 °C, the most signi ﬁcant
grain growth of W occurs. Although W grain growth is obvious, the
W/W grain connectivity gradually decreases. The binding phase
increases and its distribution become more uniform with increasing
the sintering temperature. These changes in the microstructure help
improve the mechanical properties of WHAs.
To study the changes in the alloy microstructure and composition,
the sample sintered at 1200 °C was analyzed by SEM and energy
disperse spectroscopy (EDS). The results are presented in Fig. 4 and
Table 1 .I n Fig. 4 a, the color changes in the microstructure can be
classiﬁed into white, off-white, dark grey, and pitch-black. The corre-
sponding feature points are shown in 1 –4, respectively. White is the
W grain, small off-white particle is W-rich phase, and dark grey is
the matrix γ-(Ni, Fe, W) binding phase. The dissolved W content in
the binding phase is higher than that in conventional liquid-phase
sintering. Meanwhile, a little O is also dissolved in the binding
phase. For the pitch-black particles, the W and O contents are very
high. O may mainly result from the oxidation of Fe of raw powders.
The high oxygen content can lead to decreased mechanical properties
of the WHAs.

4. Conclusions
Using the HEBM assisted SPS method to produce successfully ﬁne-
grained ( b5 μm) WHAs at different sintering temperatures
(1000–1200 °C). With increased sintering temperature, the relative
density and hardness of the alloy were both found to decrease. In con-
trast, the bending strength continuously increased. Microstructures
showed that the W grains gradually grew with increased sintering
temperature. The microstructures of WHAs could be classi ﬁed as
white W grains, off-white W-rich particles, dark grey matrix γ-(Ni,
Fe, W), as well as pitch-black W- and O-rich particles. The intergranu-
lar fracture of the W grains was in the absolute superiority. With
increasing the sintering temperature, the microporosity distributed
on the bending fracture grew gradually.
