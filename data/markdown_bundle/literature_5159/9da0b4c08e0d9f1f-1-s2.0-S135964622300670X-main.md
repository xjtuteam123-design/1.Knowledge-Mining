ABSTRACT 
Nanostructured ferritic alloys (NFAs) have demonstrated exceptional mechanical properties (strength and creep 
resistance) and radiation tolerance. However, the high strength (typically above 1.5 GPa) always comes with a 
significantly limited ductility. Here, we report a novel heterostructural nanolamellar ferritic alloy (NLFA) with 
microcrystalline (MC) domains (~ 35 % of volume fraction) dispersed in an ultrafine-grained (UFG) matrix (~ 
500 nm grain size), enabling the alloy to achieve ultrahigh yield strength of 2 GPa and an excellent tensile 
elongation of ~ 13 %. The nanolamellar-shaped grains remarkably strengthen the alloy via grain-boundary 
strengthening. The soft MC domains can effectively suppress the microcrack initiation and propagation along 
the oxide-rich preliminary powder boundaries (PPBs) via local plastic deformation. Moreover, linear oxide bands 
can induce numerous interlayer cracking and trigger delamination toughening, collaboratively ensuring the alloy 
a large ductility. The present study opens a new frontier toward high tensile ductility for the most cutting-edge 
ultra-strong NFAs. 
Nanostructured ferritic alloys (NFAs) are considered as competitive 
candidate materials in nuclear reactors because of their superior me -
chanical properties, such as high strength and creep resistance, and 
higher radiation resistance compared to conventional oxide dispersion 
strengthened (ODS) steels [1 – 8] . Their superior properties are generally 
ascribed to the thermally stable nanoprecipitates (NPs) and 
high-fraction grain boundaries (GBs) which can act as strong barriers for 
dislocation movements as well as sinks for trapping radiation-generated 
defects [ 4 , 9 , 10 ]. However, such improvement in mechanical strength is 
achieved at the expense of ductility and fracture toughness [ 11 , 12 ]. It 
has been found that the room-temperature (RT) total elongation of 
high-strength NFAs (with the yield strength approaching approx. 1.5 
GPa) is invariably lower than 5 %. For example, the most advanced 
ultra-strong 14YWT NFA (with yield strength of ~ 1.6 GPa) developed 
by the Oak Ridge National Laboratory shows an extremely limited total 
elongation of less than 2.5 % from RT to 500 
◦
C [3] . The ductility 
improvement of these advanced high-strength NFAs is vitally essential 
for their practical applications as core structural components in nuclear 
reactors. 
Recently, some fundamental understanding concerning the defor -
mation and fracture mechanisms of high-strength NFAs have been 
achieved [ 3 , 5 , 13 ]. Since the vast majority of ODS steels, including NFAs, 
are generally fabricated by powder metallurgy (PM) process, PM defects 
such as micropores, powder (grain aggregates) boundaries and coarse 
oxides, are believed to be an important external source of the low 
ductility [14] . The poor ductility of the 14YWT NFA was ascribed to the 
preferential initiation and propagation of microcracks at the 
above-mentioned PM defects [13] . The intrinsic factor of poor ductility 
is the limited strain hardening capacity of the overhardened UFG matrix, 
where high-fraction GBs and high-density non-shearable NPs drastically 
reduce the mean free paths of dislocation motion, deteriorating the 
ductility of high-strength NFAs [3] . 
The heterostructure design has proved to be very promising in 
addressing the strength-ductility trade-off dilemma [ 15 , 16 ]. The 
incompatible deformation between the soft (coarse-grained) regions and 
the hard (fine-grained) regions in heterostructural materials can 
generate an interfacial strain gradient, activating the so-called hetero- -
deformation-induced hardening to enhance strain hardening and

optimal in designing ductile ODS steels. On the one hand, the significant 
mechanical contrast between the soft dispersion-free coarse-grained

harsh conditions such as high-dose irradiation and high-temperature 
stress, especially for the alloys with high-fraction soft domains. 
Here, we develop a novel heterostructural nanolamellar ferritic alloy 
(NLFA) with ultra-high yield strength of 2 GPa and an excellent ductility 
of ~ 13 % by mechanical alloying followed by a two-step consolidation 
process, i.e., high-pressure pre-sintering and the subsequent hot rolling. 
The MC domains also contain high-density NPs, similar to the distribu -
tion of NPs in the UFG matrix. The reduced mechanical contrast enables 
the relatively soft MC domains to blunt microcracks via local plastic 
deformation without interfacial decohesion. Interestingly, we also find 
that the oxide bands with nanoscale debonding corners can induce 
numerous interlayer cracking under the ultrahigh flow stress, triggering 
a delamination toughening process.

The XRD patterns ( Fig. 1 (a)) show the phase evolution of the het -
erostructural alloy. The as-milled powders show a single-phase ferrite 
structure which remains stable during the subsequent two-step consol -
idation. The electron backscatter diffraction - inverse pole figure (EBSD- 
IPF) of the HPed alloy ( Fig. 1 (b)) reveals a heterogeneous microstructure 
with MC domains embedded in the UFG/nanocrystalline (NC) matrix, 
which is also confirmed by the scanning TEM-bright field (STEM-BF) 
image ( Fig. 1 (c)). Besides, many sub-boundaries are observed primarily 
inside the micrometer-sized grains ( Fig. 1 (b)). It is believed that the 
heterogeneous microstructure of the HPed alloy is inherited from the 
ball-milled powders, wherein the MC domains of the HPed alloy corre -
spond to the interior regions of the ball-milled powders that have un -
dergone a relatively limited plastic strain during mechanical milling 
[19] . Meanwhile, high-density NPs are uniformly dispersed in the alloy 
(Fig. S1) with no obvious difference in NPs distribution between the MC 
and the UFG/NC regions. The average diameter and the number density

Fig. 1 (d) and (e) display the three-dimensional (3D) EBSD-IPF maps 
and the corresponding phase maps of the HRed NLFA, respectively. The 
alloy shows a distinctive heterogeneous single-phase structure with 
some micrometer-sized lamellar-shaped ferrite grains (with a volume 
fraction of ~ 35 %) dispersed in the UFG ferrite matrix. These large 
lamellar-shaped grains are extensively elongated along the rolling di -
rection with an average equivalent diameter of ~ 1.5 ± 0.6 μ m on the 
rolling direction (RD) - transverse direction (TD) plane ( Fig. 1 (f)) and 
significantly thinned in the normal direction (ND) with an average 
thickness of 380 ± 90 nm. Most of the ultrafine matrix grains are also 
elongated along the RD, yielding an average equivalent diameter of 500 
± 100 nm. The grain thickness of the UFG matrix (340 ± 80 nm) is 
slightly smaller than that of the large lamellar-shaped grains. The STEM- 
BF image ( Fig. 1 (g)) confirms the laminated grain morphology with a 
mean thickness of 67 ± 17 nm. The relatively large discrepancy between 
the EBSD and TEM characterization can be mainly attributed to the 
limited resolution of the EBSD analysis, especially for lamellar with 
thickness as low as 100 nm. Compared with the heterostructural NLFA, 
the homogeneous alloy (Fig. S2) shows a relatively uniform grain size 
distribution on the RD-TD plane (500 ± 200 nm) and a similar grain 
thickness (330 ± 70 nm) on the RD-ND plane, but with few grains 
elongated extensively along the RD. 
The OM image of the HPed heterostructural alloy (Fig. S3(a)) clearly 
shows the preliminary powder boundaries (PPBs), which are decorated 
by many coarse particles (~ 100 nm in diameter, Fig. S3(b)). These 
particles are identified as Mn-Ti-O-enriched oxide particles by further 
TEM characterization (Fig. S4). Such a chain-like oxide distribution is 
often observed in ODS alloys [ 10 , 14 ], mainly resulting from the surface 
oxidation during the powder processing. The introduction of Mn in the 
present alloy can be mainly attributed to the wear of Mn-containing 
milling balls during the mechanical alloying [10] . After hot rolling, 
the PPBs are elongated in the RD (Fig. S3(c)). The 3D forward scattering 
diffraction (FSD)-SEM images of the HRed NLFA ( Fig. 1 (h)) shows a 
stretched chain-like distribution feature of these coarse oxides in the 
RD-TD plane, in accordance with the OM result (Fig. S3(c)). By com -
parison, both the RD-ND and TD-ND planes reveal a near linear align -
ment of oxides, forming oxide bands along the extended PPBs. The 
spacing of oxide bands is in the range of several micrometers.

The EBSD-IPF map ( Fig. 3 (a)) shows a typical heterogeneous struc -
ture of the HRed NLFA. The corresponding Kernel average misorienta -
tion (KAM) map ( Fig. 3 (b)) reveals an inhomogeneous global 
distribution of the local misorientation, wherein high KAM values are 
mainly confined to the MC domains, indicating a higher density of 
geometrically necessary dislocations (GNDs) and higher dislocation 
storage ability. Moreover, the GNDs are highly localized, forming 
prevalent sub-boundaries inside these micrometer-sized grains, mainly 
due to the dynamic recovery of high-density dislocations during hot 
rolling. 
After tensile testing, some microcracks ( Fig. 3 (c)) and micropores 
(Fig. S5(a)) are occasionally observed near the final fracture surface. 
However, these microscale fracture defects disappear in the necked re -
gions away from the fracture surface, where nanopores are clearly 
detected (Fig. S5(a)). This result is consistent with the strain-field evo -
lution, where no highly localized strain is observed after necking ( Fig. 2 
(b)). The fracture behaviors of the heterostructural NLFA are quite 
different from those of previous reported high-strength NFAs, where 
numerous micropores and microcracks were uniformly distributed over 
a wide region near the fracture surface [ 3 , 13 ]. The ductility of 
high-strength NFAs such as 14YWT alloy is believed to depend mainly 
on the local strain level where the microcracking initiates, and micro -
cracks often nucleate at prevalent coarse oxides and/or residual 
gas-trapped micropores. The numerous microcracks can readily propa -
gate and connect with each other, resulting in a premature fracture and a 
very small elongation [13] . By comparison, the two-step consolidation 
in the present study results in a nearly fully dense NLFA with no obvious 
residual gas-trapped pores. The pores near the fracture surface always

The fracture surface of the heterostructural NLFA ( Fig. 4 (a)) shows 
multiple delamination cracks with different lengths. A further exami -
nation reveals a well-developed dimpled microstructure within the 
delamination bands ( Fig. 4 (b)). However, the dimples are rather 
shallow, and their size is similar to the grain size. It is believed that these 
nanoscale dimples are formed by decohesion of nanograins and/or their 
conglomerates involving only a shallow plasticity layer which contrib -
utes rather little to the macroscopic ductility. The distribution of the 
delamination cracks in the through-thickness sections normal to the 
fracture surface was also examined ( Fig. 4 (c)), wherein numerous 
delamination cracks are observed. By comparison, the homogeneous 
alloy shows much less delamination cracks emitted from the fracture 
surface (Fig. S6(a)). 
The detailed microstructures of a long delamination crack were 
further characterized to clarify the delamination cracking mechanism 
( Fig. 4 (d)). The crack with a stepped pattern penetrates into the matrix, 
and the step height is in the range of several micrometers, in good 
agreement with the spacing of the oxide bands. Meanwhile, the steps are 
frequently observed to directly connect to a section of oxide bands. The 
magnified view at the crack tip ( Fig. 4 (e)) further confirms the pre -
dominant propagation path of delamination crack along the oxide 
bands. Moreover, secondary microcracks can also be observed near the 
steps ( Fig. 4 (d)) and the tips of the main delamination cracks (Fig. S7), 
which are also connected to the oxide bands.

However, these Mn-Ti-O-rich particles maintain a spherical 
morphology, and angular debonding zones with tens of nanometers in 
size are identified around these particles, indicating that the hard oxides 
can hardly deform coordinately with the soft matrix during hot rolling. 
Thus, local stress concentrations at the debonding corners are expected, 
and the oxide bands are supposed to serve as the “ relatively weak in -
terfaces ” and preferential sites for the initiation and propagation of 
delamination microcracks under a sufficiently high flow stress (above 2 
GPa). In the case of the homogeneous alloy, however, the thinner oxide 
bands with sparse particles (Fig. S8) would result in a much lower ten -

also the preferential nucleation sites for main cracks (Fig. S5). Excessive 
oxides at PPBs may induce very high stress concentration, promoting the 
rapid nucleation and propagation of the main cracks, and reducing the 
ductility. Thus, it is suggested that the surface oxidation of powders and 
other defects such as gas-trapped pores should be properly controlled 
during the mechanical alloying and subsequent thermo-mechanical 
treatments. 
In summary, we have demonstrated a novel heterostructural NLFA 
that achieves ultrahigh yield strength of 2 GPa and an excellent tensile 
elongation of ~ 13 %. The extremely thin nano-lamellar structure 
remarkably strengthens the alloy. The soft microcrystalline domains can 
effectively suppress the initiation and propagation of microcracks along 
the oxide-rich PPBs via local plastic deformation. Moreover, the oxide 
bands with nanoscale debonding corners can induce numerous inter -
layer cracking, trigger a delamination toughening process, which 
collaboratively ensure the alloy a large ductility. This work paves the 
way for a novel breakthrough in achieving enhanced tensile ductility in 
the most advanced ultra-strong NFAs.
