Inﬂuence of Sintering Temperature on Mechanical
Properties of Ti-Nb-Zr-Fe Alloys Prepared by Spark
Plasma Sintering
Qiang Li , Hao Sun, Junjie Li, Xufeng Yuan, Masaaki Nakai, Mitsuo Niinomi, and Takayoshi Nakano
Submitted: 6 October 2020 / Revised: 11 March 2021 / Accepted: 3 April 2021
Ti-15Nb-25Zr-(0, 4, 8)Fe (mol.%) alloys were prepared via spark plasma sintering (SPS) for 10 min at 800,
1000, and 1200 /C176C to study the effect of sintering temperatures and Fe content on the mechanical properties
of alloys. The main phase of the alloys sintered at 800 /C176Ci s a phase. When the temperature is increased to
1000 and 1200 /C176C, the alloys are mainly b phase. Raising the sintering temperature can promote the
diffusion of elements. Therefore, high strength and high plasticity are obtained in the alloys sintered at high
temperature. The compressive strength of Ti-15Nb-25Zr sintered at 1200 /C176C is 1604 MPa, and the fracture
strain is around 35%. The strength and plasticity are inﬂuenced by the complex effects of elemental
diffusion, grain boundary and solid-solution strengthening. Adding Fe can improve the diffusion of ele-
ments, promote the formation of b grains and supply solid-solution strengthening effect. As the sintering
temperature is increased, the compressive strength of Ti-15Nb-25Zr-4Fe shows an increasing trend and the
plasticity is almost not changed. The Fe content is excessive in Ti-15Nb-25Zr-8Fe, which leads to some
disadvantages including intermetallic compounds formation and grain boundary coarsening. The
mechanical properties of Ti-15Nb-25Zr-8Fe are not always better than those of Ti-15Nb-25Zr-4Fe. The
compressive strength of Ti-15Nb-25Zr-8Fe shows an increasing trend and the plasticity ﬁrstly increases and
then decreases when the temperature is raised from 800 up to 1200 /C176C. Although the sintering time of SPS
is short, Ti-15Nb-25Zr-(4, 8)Fe sintered at 1000 /C176C and Ti-15Nb-25Zr-(0, 4)Fe sintered at 1200 /C176C show
good combination of strength and plasticity.
Keywords mechanical properties, microstructure, spark plasma
sintering, b-type titanium alloy
1. Introduction
Ti and Ti alloys are suitable biomedical materials owing to
the good mechanical properties, high corrosion resistance,
excellent biocompatibility and low Y oung’s modulus (Ref 1, 2).
Ti-6Al-4V ELI (mass%) alloy is often applied for clinical
implant (Ref 3), however, some problems are still unresolved,
such as the stress shielding effect caused by the high Y oung’s
modulus of Ti-6Al-4V ELI ( /C24110 GPa) and safety problems
caused by the Al and V ion releasing (Ref 4-6). Recently, b-Ti
alloys have attracted much attentions owing to the lower
Y oung’s modulus and higher biocompatibility. Non-toxic
elements, such as Nb, Mo, Ta, Sn, and Zr, have been added
to Ti alloys to obtain desirable mechanical properties (Ref 7-9).
Nb is a widely used b stabilizer, which can reduce the b transus
temperature and a‘‘ martensite transformation temperature. The
Ti-Nb-based alloys usually show low Y oung’s modulus around
60 GPa (Ref 8, 10, 11). Mo is a stronger b stabilizer than Nb,
but Ti-Mo-based alloy usually show higher Y oung’s modulus.
The b stable ability of Ta is a little weaker than Nb. Sn and Zr
are a so-called neutral elements of the same family as Ti. They
are usually viewed as weak b stabilizer because they can inhibit
x phase transformation and a’’ martensite transformation (Ref
12). Ta, Sn, and Zr can supply solid-solution strengthening
effect, thus they are usually used to adjust the b stability and
improve the strength (Ref 13-15). A series of Ti-Nb-based
alloys with Mo, Ta, and Zr addition have been developed for
biomedical applications, such as Ti-13Zr-13Nb (mass%), Ti-
35Nb-7Zr-5Ta (mass%, TNZT), Ti-29Nb-13Ta-4.6Zr (TNTZ),
Ti-35Nb-2Ta-3Zr (mass%), and Ti-24Nb-4Zr-7.9Sn (mass%)
(Ref 16-20). Moreover, Cr and Fe are strong b stabilizer with

The melting and powder metallurgy are two usually used
methods to prepare Ti alloys. However, the reduction process-
ing after melting leads to a waste of materials and ﬁnally results
in high cost of products. The Ti alloys prepared by traditional
powder metallurgy are usually subjected to long-time high-
temperature sintering process, during which the oxidization and
grain growth easily occur. Spark plasma sintering (SPS) is a
rapid sintering technology to prepare high temperature refrac-
tory metals and alloys and shows the advantages of simple
operation and convenient temperature control (Ref 26, 27).
Almost full-dense metallic materials can be prepared by SPS
under a low temperature for a short sintering time (Ref 28).
Many Ti alloys have been prepared by SPS technology, such as
Ti-35Nb-7Zr-5Ta, Ti-22Al-25Nb (mol.%), Ti-34Nb-25Zr
(mass%), and they show much higher micro-Vickers hardness
and strength than those of conventional vacuum-sintered or
melted alloys (Ref 29-31). In previous study, Ti-15Nb-25Zr-(0,
2, 4, 8)Fe (mol.%) were designed via using Zr and Fe to partly
instead of Nb for reducing the excessive segregations of Nb
(Ref 32). The alloys were prepared via SPS at 1000 /C176C for
10 min and then heat-treated at 900 /C176C to improve the
composition uniformity. The as-sintered Ti-15Nb-25Zr-4Fe,
as well as the Ti-15Nb-25Zr-(4, 8)Fe after heat treatment,
showed high compressive strength, hardness and plasticity. In
order to reduce heating process, Ti-15Nb-25Zr-(0, 4, 8)Fe are
sintered at 800, 1000 and 1200 /C176C, respectively, to investigate
the effect of different sintering temperature on the microstruc-
tures, micro-Vickers hardness and compressive properties of
the alloys.
2. Experimental Process
The Ti-15Nb-25Zr-(0, 4, 8)Fe (mol.%) powders were
obtained by weighing pure titanium, niobium, zirconium and
iron powder, and then mixed in V-type mixing machine for
12 hours at a speed of 120 r/min. They were prepressed into a
graphite mold (diameter: 20 mm) using graphite papers
between the powder and the graphite mold for the easy
removal of the sintered specimens. Then, the mold was placed
in the SPS equipment followed by three times air pumping and
Ar ﬁlling to avoid oxidation. The specimens were heated at a
speed of 100 /C176C/min in the SPS equipment until reaching the
ﬁnal sintering temperature (800, 1000 and 1200 /C176C), kept at the
ﬁnal sintering temperature for 10 min and then cooled in the
equipment to room temperature. Throughout the sintering
process, the applied axial pressure was always 50 MPa. The
sizes of ﬁnally obtained samples are all around 20 mm in
diameters and 3 mm in thicknesses. Wire electrical discharge
machine was used to cut the samples for the following tests. For
convenient expression, sintering temperature was used as a
sufﬁx following the alloy composition to represent the sample
sintered at the temperature. For example, the Ti-15Nb-25Zr
sintered at 800 /C176C was referred as Ti-15Nb-25Zr-800.
The specimens were ground by 200-1500 # sandpapers and
subjected to chemical composition analysis, which was carried
out using a ﬁeld-emission scanning electron microscope (SEM)
equipped with an energy-dispersive x-ray spectroscope (EDS).
The microstructures were observed via optical microscopy. The
phases were studied via x-ray diffraction (XRD) with Cu K a
radiation at a voltage of 40 kV , current of 40 mA, and scanning
speed of 6 /C176/min. The load for the micro-Vickers hardness was
set as 19.61 N (2000 gf) and maintained for 10 s, and 10 points
were randomly selected on the specimen surface to measure.
The 3 9 3 9 6m m 3 specimens were used for compression
test, three repeated tests for each specimen, and cross-head
speed was set as 1 mm/min. It should be pointed out that the
data of the Ti-15Nb-25Zr-(0, 4, 8)Fe sintered at 1000 /C176C were
from the already published results in Ref 32 for comparison.
3. Result and Discussion
3.1 EDS Results and Microstructures
The EDS results of Ti-15Nb-25Zr-4Fe sintered at different
temperatures are shown in Figs. 1, 2 and 3. Ti, Nb, Zr, and Fe
are not uniformly distributed and the segregations of Nb, Zr and
Fe are found in Ti-15Nb-25Zr-4Fe-800 (Fig. 1). It is because
the alloying elements cannot diffuse sufﬁciently at low
sintering temperature for a short time. Additionally, Fe is a b-
stabilizing element and shows lower solubility in a phase than
that of the b phase (Ref 33), thus 4% Fe is not evenly dispersed.
When specimens are sintered at 1000 /C176C, the segregations of
Nb is still observed, but the distributions of Ti, Zr and Fe
become more uniform (Fig. 2). The segregation of Nb almost
disappears and all the elements are nearly uniformly distributed
in Ti-15Nb-25Zr-4Fe-1200 (Fig. 3). The EDS results indicate
that the elemental diffusion occurs during the sintering process,
although the sintering time is only 10 min. It is well known that
increasing temperature can improve the diffusion of elements,
thus high sintering temperature is beneﬁt for getting uniformly
distributed samples (Ref 34).
No signiﬁcant pores are observed in the microstructures of
Ti-15Nb-25Zr-(0, 4, 8)Fe (Fig. 4) indicating that the raw
powders are well combined with each other. Black, light gray,
and bright white regions are observed in the microstructures.
The bright white regions are viewed as the segregation of Nb in
previous researches (Ref 32, 34). The boundaries between the
three regions are very clear, and the bright white region show
irregular polygon shape in the Ti-15Nb-25Zr-800 (Fig. 4a). It
indicates that original shape of the raw powders is maintained,
thus the diffusion between the powders is very limited. The
boundaries between the three regions is fuzzy in the Ti-15Nb-
25Zr-1000 (Fig. 4b), but the bright white regions are also
observed. When the temperature is 1200 /C176C (Fig. 4c), more b
grains are observed with segregation of Nb still existing. Ti-
15Nb-25Zr-(4, 8)Fe shows similar changes with the tempera-
ture raised from 800 to 1200 /C176C.
The black regions are gradually decreased by Fe addition in
the Ti-15Nb-25Zr-(0, 4, 8)Fe sintered at 800 /C176C (Fig. 4a, d and
g). When the temperature is increased to 1000 /C176C, grain
boundaries appear and become more obvious with addition of
Fe. The formation of b grain is promoted and the grain size is
reﬁned via adding Fe to the Ti-15Nb-25Zr-(0, 4, 8)Fe sintered
at 1000 /C176C (Fig. 4b, e and h) and 1200 /C176C (Fig. 4c, f and i).
Moreover, the grain size become larger with the increase of
sintering temperature in Ti-15Nb-25Zr-(4, 8)Fe, respectively.

3.2 Phase Constitution
The XRD results of the Ti-15Nb-25Zr-(0, 4, 8)Fe sintered at
800, 1000 and 1200 /C176C are shown in Fig. 5. The a phase, a‘‘
phase, b phase and a-Zr phase show diffraction peaks in the
alloys sintered at 800 /C176C. The allotropic transformation tem-
peratures of pure Ti, pure Zr and pure Fe are around 882, 863,
and 912 /C176C, respectively (Ref 1, 35, 36). Limited element
diffusion occurs among a-Ti (HCP), a-Zr (HCP), a-Fe (BCC),
and Nb (BCC) when the temperature is 800 /C176C. Therefore, the
alloys mainly consist of a phase. Since the mole fraction of Zr
is up to 25% in alloys, some peaks corresponding to a-Zr phase
can be found in the XRD patterns. The diffraction peak of Nb
basically coincides with b-Ti in XRD patterns (Ref 34). The
diffusion between Nb and Ti make Ti form some metastable b
phase, thus, a’’ phase and b phase can be detected. When the
sintering temperature is 1000 /C176C, strong diffraction peaks of b
phase and weak diffraction peaks of a‘‘ phase in the alloys,
indicating that Nb, Zr and Fe have been well diffused in Ti. Due
to the local segregation of Fe, the diffraction peaks corre-
sponding to intermetallic compounds Fe 2Zr and FeTi appear in
Ti-15Nb-25Zr-8Fe-1000. The diffraction peaks corresponding
to b phase is stronger and narrower in the alloys sintered at
1200 /C176C, indicating that the b phase is dominant and the grain
becomes bigger. The diffraction peak of intermetallic com-
pound is not observed in Ti-15Nb-25Zr-8Fe-1200. The XRD
patterns indicate that the formation of b phase in the alloys is
mainly determined by the sintering temperature.
3.3 Micro-Vickers Hardness
Figure 6 presents the micro-Vickers hardness of Ti-15Nb-
25Zr-(0, 4, 8)Fe sintered at 800, 1000 and 1200 /C176C. As
mentioned above, the elemental diffusion is limited in the
alloys sintered at 800 /C176C, thus the alloying elements just
provide weak solid-solution strengthening effect. The elemental
diffusion is promoted by the increase of sintering temperature,
thus the micro-Vickers hardness shows an increasing trend in
Ti-15Nb-25Zr as the sintering temperature raised from 800 up
to 1200 /C176C. It has been pointed that the addition of Fe can
provide solid-solution strengthening effect and promote the
elemental diffusion (Ref 32), thus the micro-Vickers hardness is
increased in the 800 /C176C sintered alloys with the content of Fe
increased from 0 to 8 mol.%. The increasing trend is more
remarkable in Ti-15Nb-25Zr-(4, 8)Fe when the sintering
temperature is increased from 800 up to 1200 /C176C. The Ti-
15Nb-25Zr-(4, 8)Fe-1000 show similar micro-Vickers hardness
values owing to the complex effect of intermetallic compound
precipitations which has been pointed in previous studies (Ref
32, 37). When the alloys are sintered at 1200 /C176C, the micro-
Vickers hardness is increased only a little with the content of Fe
increased from 4 to 8 mol.%. The changes of micro-Vickers
hardness of the Ti-15Nb-25Zr-(0, 4, 8)Fe sintered at different
temperatures indicate that raising sintering temperature and

3.4 Compressive Properties
Figure 7 shows the compressive stress strain curves of Ti-
15Nb-25Zr-(0, 4, 8)Fe sintered at different temperatures. The
compressive strength ( rb) and 0.2% proof stress ( r0.2) obtained
by compressive test are shown in Fig. 8.
Ti-15Nb-25Zr-800 shows a r0.2 of 1202 MPa and a rb of
1472 MPa. When the sintering temperature is increased to
1000 /C176C, the r0.2 is increased to around 1292 MPa but the rb is
still around 1471 MPa. When the sintering temperature is
further increased to 1200 /C176C, the r0.2 is maintained at around
1281 MPa but the rb is increased to around 1604 MPa. Based
on the compressive stress–strain curves, the section from the
yielding strain (the strain corresponding to 0.2% proof stress) to
the fracture strain in the compressive stress–strain curve can be
deﬁned as stress plateau, which reﬂects the plasticity of the
sample and is mainly determined by the fracture strain. As
shown in Fig. 7a, Ti-15Nb-25Zr-800 fractures just after
yielding and shows small stress plateau and fractures at the
strain around 11%, indicating low plasticity. The plasticity is
increased with the increase of sintering temperature. Ti-15Nb-
25Zr-1200 shows a large fracture strain of 35%. The diffusion
between the raw powders is limited with the boundaries of the
raw powders existing in Ti-15Nb-25Zr-800. The boundaries
can strengthen the materials, although solid-solution strength-
ening effect is weak. However, the binding between the
powders is weak resulting in a low plasticity. The increase of
sintering temperature promotes the elemental diffusion and
combination of powder, thus the compressive strength and
plasticity are both increased.
With the sintering temperature raised from 800 up to
1000 /C176C, the r0.2 and rb of Ti-15Nb-25Zr-(4, 8)Fe show an
increasing trend owing to the enhanced solid-solution strength-
ening effect. The stress plateau is almost not changed in Ti-
15Nb-25Zr-4Fe, but it ﬁrstly increases and then decreases in Ti-
15Nb-25Zr-8Fe. The change of stress plateau in Ti-15Nb-25Zr-
4Fe can be attributed to the complex inﬂuence of solution solid-
solution strengthening and b grain formation. The complex
inﬂuence makes the plasticity in an equilibrium status, thus the
Ti-15Nb-25Zr-4Fe sintered at 800, 1000, and 1200 /C176C all show
fracture strains around 18%. The formation of ﬁne grains and
improved elemental diffusion lead to higher plasticity in Ti-
15Nb-25Zr-8Fe-1000 compared with that of Ti-15Nb-25Zr-
8Fe-800. When the sintering temperature is further raised from
1000 up to 1200 /C176C, grain boundary becomes clear and
coarsening, thus the plasticity is decreased and fracture strain is
decreased from 20% in Ti-15Nb-25Zr-8Fe-1000 to 13% in Ti-
15Nb-25Zr-8Fe-1200.
For the alloys sintered at 800 /C176C, the r0.2 and rb are both
increased with the increase of Fe content. Ti-15Nb-25Zr-(4,
8)Fe-800 show similar stress plateau and the fracture strains are
18 and 15%, respectively, which are larger than that of Ti-
15Nb-25Zr-800. The increase of strength and plasticity can be

4. Discussion
Spark plasma sintering is a feasible technique to prepare
bulk Ti alloys. The sintering temperature is a key parameter that
can inﬂuence the structures and mechanical properties (Ref 40).
The high temperature makes the elements diffuse more fully.
Moreover, when the heating rate and cooling conditions are the
same, the heating and cooling time of the samples sintered at
higher sintering temperature is longer, which means that the
time of samples staying in high temperature is longer and the
diffusion time is prolonged, so the diffusion is more sufﬁcient.
In this study, the heating speed is 100 /C176C/min, thus the sintering
time of the alloys sintered at 1200 /C176C is 4 min more than that of
the alloys sintered at 800 /C176C in the heating process. Although it
is difﬁcult to obtain a uniform distribution of alloying elements
via SPS during a relatively short sintering time, high temper-

5. Conclusions
The microstructures and mechanical properties of the Ti-
15Nb-25Zr-(0, 4, 8)Fe alloys prepared by SPS at 800, 1000,
and 1200 /C176C were studied. The following conclusions were
obtained based on the above results and discussions:
(1) Raising sintering temperature can improve the diffusion
of alloying elements. When the alloys are sintered at
1200 /C176C, the segregation of Nb is still observed in the
alloys. The alloys mainly consist of a phase when the
sintering temperature is below the b transus temperature
of Ti. As the sintering temperature is raised above the b
transus temperature, the main phase in the alloys is b
phase.
