ABSTRACT
Rhenium (Re) is known to enhance the microstructural stability of superalloys, enabling components such as 
turbine blades to withstand extreme thermal stresses. In this study, we investigate the influence of Re additions 
(0 – 6 at.%) on the microstructure, lattice misfit, thermophysical properties, and high temperature mechanical 
properties of a low density Co-30Ni-7Al-12Cr-4Ti-2Nb γ / γ ′ alloy. Alloys with up to 3 at.% Re exhibit refined γ ′ 
precipitate size, morphological transitioning from cuboidal to rounded cuboids, while additions beyond 4 at.% 
promote the formation of detrimental topologically close-packed (TCP) phases. HR-XRD reveals that Re reduces 
γ / γ ′ lattice misfit and causes a misfit sign reversal from positive to negative as temperature increases from 900 
◦
C 
to 1000 
◦
C. APT confirms partitioning of Re in the γ -matrix without interfacial segregation. Differential scanning 
calorimetry (DSC) was used to construct a quasi-binary Co-Re phase diagram, highlighting key phase transition 
temperatures. Re addition also causes atomic volumetric shrinkage, increasing the alloy density beyond the 
predictions of the rule of mixtures. Notably, the 3 at.% Re alloy displays enhanced yield strength at both room 
and high temperatures, exhibiting a yield strength anomaly.
1. Introduction
The global shift toward decarbonization and energy efficiency has 
created a pressing demand for advanced materials that can perform in 
extreme conditions, especially in high performance systems like gas 
turbine engines [ 1 ]. Ni-based superalloys, indispensable in the hot sec -
tions of these engines, offer an exceptional balance of strength, creep 
resistance, and oxidation resistance at elevated temperatures, all while 
maintaining a favorable performance-to-cost ratio [ 2 , 3 ]. Their superior 
mechanical properties stem from the unique γ / γ ′ microstructure, where 
coherent L1
2
-ordered γ ′ precipitates interact with dislocations within an 
FCC γ matrix [ 4 – 6 ]. However, the future development of Ni-based su -
peralloys faces significant challenges. Gas temperatures now exceed 
1550 
◦

◦
C 
– approaching the melting point of Ni-based superalloys, which is 
around 1300 
◦

materials capable of withstanding such extreme environments.
The discovery of the γ / γ ′ microstructure in Co-Al alloys with tungsten 
(W) has opened new opportunities for material innovation in high 
temperature applications [ 9 ]. Cobalt with a higher melting point and 
better sulfidation resistance than Ni will enable gas turbine engines to 
operate at even higher efficiencies [ 10 , 11 ]. Early Co-Al-W alloys showed 
significant promise [ 12 – 15 ], but challenges such as high mass density 
and the formation of Co
3
W phase during long-term exposure limited 
widespread adoption [ 13 , 16 ]. Recent developments have shifted the 
focus toward W-free, low-density Co-based alloys, with base composi -
tions of Co-Ti [ 17 ], Co-Al-Mo-Nb/Ta [ 18 – 20 ], Co-Ti-Cr [ 21 ], Co-Al-V 
[ 22 , 23 ], Co-Ti-V [ 24 ], Co-Ta-V and Co-Nb-V [ 25 ] systems. These al -
loys have undergone a remarkable evolution in composition, incorpo -
rating more elements tailored to enhance their high-temperature 
stability and creep properties. Additions of Ni and Ti have been shown to 
strengthen the γ ′ phase [ 24 , 26 , 27 ], while refractory elements such as 
Mo, W, Re, and Ru contribute to solid solution strengthening of the γ 
matrix [ 28 – 30 ]. Grain boundary strengthening was achieved through 
small additions of B [ 31 ], and Cr has been optimized for oxidation and 
corrosion resistance under operational conditions [ 32 , 33 ]. The distri -
bution of these solutes across the γ / γ ′ interfaces also influences the local 
lattice misfit, which directly affects creep properties at elevated

Re (3 – 6 wt%), used extensively in second, third, and fourth gener -
ation single-crystal Ni-based superalloys, significantly improves creep 
resistance by stabilizing the microstructure and slowing γ ′ precipitate 
coarsening [ 8 ]. However, excess addition of Re can lead to precipitation 
of brittle topological close-packed (TCP) phases that seriously degrade 
the mechanical properties of the alloy [ 37 ]. Despite its high cost and 
density, Re contributes significantly to the creep resistance of Ni-based 
superalloys, the so-called “ Re-effect ” has justified its usage. Huang 
et al. [ 38 ] highlighted the importance of the coupling between Re 
segregation and the γ / γ ′ interfacial dislocation for improving the creep 
properties of DD6 single-crystal superalloy containing 2 wt% Re. Wu 
et al. [ 39 ] showed that adding 3 wt% Re to a second-generation sin -
gle-crystal superalloy doubled its creep life by segregating to partial 
dislocations and imposing a drag effect during high-temperature creep.
In Co-based superalloys, however, the effects of Re addition is 
underexplored. Intriguingly, Neumeier et al. [ 40 ] demonstrated through 
experimental and first-principles kinetic Monte Carlo simulations that 
Re is the slowest diffusing element among all transition metals in 
face-centered cubic (fcc) Co. This sluggish diffusion is attributed to Co ’ s 
higher vacancy formation energy, resulting in a greater diffusion acti -
vation energy barrier for Re compared to nickel (Ni) [ 40 , 41 ]. Despite 
these characteristics, Kolb et al. [ 42 ] and Volz et al. [ 43 ] reported that 
incorporating Re into single-crystal Co-Ni-Al-W-based alloys yields only 
marginal or negligible improvements in creep properties, falling short of 
the significant role Re plays in second- and third-generation Ni-Al-based 
superalloys. This diminished impact stems from the low partitioning of 
Re into the γ matrix. Consequently, Re is not considered essential for 
Co-Al-W-based superalloys — however, Pandey et al. [ 28 ] showed that 
adding 2 at% Re to the Co-30Ni-10Al-5Mo-2Nb alloy influenced γ ′ pre -
cipitate coarsening by reducing the γ / γ ′ lattice misfit by up to 40 % (from 
0.32 to 0.19), and inducing a rounding of the γ ′ precipitate. In contrast to 
Co-Al-W base alloys, Re strongly partitions into the γ matrix and seg -
regates at the γ / γ ′ interface, thereby enhancing the high-temperature 
microstructural stability. Li et al. [ 44 ] reported that adding up to 5 at. 
% Re to a Co-12Ti alloy increased the γ ′ -solvus from 1022

binary alloy to 1085 
◦

ume fraction of 53 %. Additionally, Re reduces the γ / γ ′ lattice misfit to 
0.5 %, decreases the γ ′ coarsening rate, with increases the alloy density 
to 8.92 g/cc.
A recently developed alloy composition, Co-30Ni-12Cr-7Al-4Ti-2Nb, 
focuses on achieving low density and successfully demonstrates an 
optimized balance between stacking fault energy (SFE) and stable γ ՛ 
phase, which enhances room-temperature mechanical properties, 
including strength and ductility [ 45 ]. Given its low density, alloying 
with Re and understanding its microstructural evolution could be ad -
vantageous for improving high-temperature properties. This study aims 
to improve the alloy through Re addition, which particularly offers a 
concomitant effect on improving multiple properties, including me -
chanical strength, microstructural stability, and precise tuning of lattice 
misfit.

2.1. Alloy preparation and heat treatment
Alloy ingots weighing 50 g were prepared using a vacuum-arc 
melting unit equipped with a water-cooled copper hearth and a non-

avoid the formation of deleterious TCP phase during aging.

As Re content increases, the size of γ ′ precipitates decreases from 
~146 nm for 0Re, ~125 nm for 1.5Re, and ~109 nm for 3Re, which is 
attributed to the sluggish diffusion of Re in the matrix. A gradual change 
in the precipitate shape is also observed, transitioning from sharp 
cuboidal to round-cornered cuboids. This change is quantified using the 
shape factor (c/a), which is the ratio of the diagonal to edge length of a 
cube. As the c/a ratio approaches to 1 indicates more spherical 
(rounded) precipitates, as shown in Fig. 5 b. The alignment of pre -
cipitates is in a cubic fashion up to 3Re and random for the 4Re and 6Re 
alloy, which indicates the reduction in elastic anisotropy of the alloys 
with higher Re concentration [ 62 ]. Fig. 5 c illustrates the microhardness

attributed to the solid solution strengthening effect of Re.
In superalloys, the shape of γ ′ precipitates is governed by the balance 
between interfacial energy and elastic strain energy. The elastic strain 
energy arises from the lattice misfit between the γ matrix and the γ ′ 
precipitates, and is influenced by factors such as precipitate size, elastic 
stiffness, and the degree of anisotropy in the matrix. Jokisaari et al. [ 35 ] 
reported that lattice misfit strain plays a dominant role in determining 
elastic energy in cobalt-based superalloys. During the early stages of 
nucleation (post-solutionizing and quenching), the precipitates are 
spherical due to near-zero misfit and the drive to minimize interfacial 
energy, which scales with r
2 
(precipitate radius). As the precipitates 
grow, the elastic strain energy, which scales with r
3
, becomes more 
dominant. In an elastically anisotropic matrix like γ , this drives the 
evolution from spherical to cuboidal precipitates, particularly favoring 
alignment along the elastically soft [100] crystallographic directions. 
The addition of Re significantly influences this morphological evolution 
due to its impact on both lattice misfit and solute partitioning between γ 
and γ ′ phases [ 28 , 52 , 63 ].
In the current study, the observed transition from cuboidal to 
rounded cuboid precipitates is attributed to this interplay between 
elemental partitioning and lattice misfit. Therefore, detailed in -
vestigations using HR-XRD and APT were conducted, with correlations 
discussed in the following sections.

4. Conclusions
This study demonstrates that Re effectively enhances the micro -
structural and mechanical properties of Co-30Ni-7Al-4Ti-12Cr-2Nb al -
loys. The key findings are as follows. 
1. Up to 3 at.% Re addition provides a beneficial balance of properties: 
Re promotes solid-solution strengthening, refine the γ ′ precipitate 
size, and adjusts the matrix/precipitate lattice misfit toward 
enhanced high-temperature yield strength.
2. The lattice misfit ( δ ), due to changes in chemical composition, shifts 
from positive at 900 
◦
C to negative at 1000 
◦
C. Additionally, γ -matrix 
tetragonality is observed at 1000 
◦
C from XRD peaks. The reduction 
in | δ | between 900 and 1000 
◦
C is more pronounced in Re-containing 
alloys, indicating a strong influence of Re on the thermodynamic 
stability of the γ phase.
3. APT confirms that Re strongly partitions to the γ matrix, which is 
very much required to improve the coarsening and creep resistance 
of the alloy due to its sluggish diffusion and strengthening effect.
4. A quasi-binary Co – Re phase diagram indicates that increasing Re 
content significantly broadens the freezing range. This promotes 
microsegregation during solidification, which adversely affects 
castability and requires careful compositional control.
5. Alloys with Re content above 3 at.% exhibits sluggish homogeniza -
tion kinetics and are prone to the formation of topologically close- 
packed (TCP) phases. These factors underscore the necessity for 
stringent control of Re content to maintain microstructural stability 
and processability.
6. The refractory and chemical nature of Re causes its addition to the 
alloy to increase the density more than estimated by the ideal rule of 
mixtures, likely due to atomic volume shrinkage.
7. The 3 at.% Re alloy exhibits a clear yield strength anomaly (YSA) in 
the temperature range of 670 – 870 
◦
C. It retains a high yield strength, 
exceeding 689 MPa at 870 
◦
C, demonstrating the alloy ’ s potential for 
high-temperature structural applications.
