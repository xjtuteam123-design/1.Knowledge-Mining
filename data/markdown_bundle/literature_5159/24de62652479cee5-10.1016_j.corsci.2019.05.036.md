Abstract High temperature oxidation behaviours of γ′ -strengthened Co -based superalloys with 
various Ni additions were investigated at both 800 °C and 900 °C. The results show that the 
superalloys show excellent oxidation resistance due to the formation of compact spinel oxide 
scales. With the increasing of Ni addition, the concentration of Cr in scale/alloy interface will be 
increased but that of Co will be decreased. And the growth of Cr2O3 will be promoted, while CoO 
nucleation will be hindered. Therefore, increasing Ni addition helps t he formation of protective 
spinel oxide scales and delays nodular oxidation, which favours to be better oxidation resistance. 
 
Keywords: High temperature oxidation, γ′, Co-based superalloy, Ni addition, Oxide layer 
 
1 Introduction 
The need to increase both inlet temperature and pressure of turbine engines lead to an 
increasing demand for superalloys to withstand the load under high stress level at high 
temperatures [1]. In Ni -based superalloy systems, Ni 3Al-γ′ phase in γ -Ni matrix can hinder the 
dislocation moving, which allows N i-based superalloys to keep high temperature strength [2]. 
Therefore, Ni -based superalloys are wildly used as hot -part materials in many fields such as 
aeronautics, astronautics as well as power and chemical industries. Compared with Ni -based 
superalloys, conventional Co -based superalloys show lower high temperature strength due to 
lacking of the possibility of stable γ′. In 2006, a stable Co3(Al, W)-γ′ phase was found in Co-Al-W 
alloy [3]. This report provides a new pathway for the development of Co -based superalloys with 
higher strength under the service temperature. 
As oxidation is one of the important factors for superalloy service life, it is necessary to study 
the oxidation behaviour and how it might be influenced by alloying elements [4 -12]. When 
exposed to air at high temperatures, external Cr 2O3 and Al 2O3 layers can protect superalloys 
against the further oxidation [8 , 9]. Wright et al. reported that the oxidation resistance of Co -Cr 
alloys (containing 20 -30% Cr) was increased by increasing Cr conte nt [10]. The formation of 
Cr2O3 may facilitate the resistance to oxygen ion or corrosive medium intrusion, so that adding of 
Cr to superalloys improves high temperature oxidation resistance [7]. But as a γ-stabilized element, 
Cr can decrease γ solvus temperature as well [12]. Thus, it is meaningful to find a low 
Cr-containing Co-based superalloy with excellent antioxidant properties. 
In the previous studies, it was found that the solvus temperature of γ could be increased by 
adding of Ni [13 , 14]. Thu s, it is possible to increase the service temperature of Co -based 
superalloys by adding of Ni. It has been reported that Ni has negligible effects on oxidation 
properties of Co -Ni-Al-W and Co -Ni-Al-W-B alloys [4 , 5]. In the preliminary research, it has 
been found that Ni can promote the diffusion of beneficial elements like Al [ 15, 16], which helps 
to increase the oxidation resistance indirectly. But effect of Ni addition on the distribution 
behaviour of Cr in the oxide layer is unknown. So in this paper, h igh temperature oxidation tests 
were carried out on Cr -containing Co-based superalloys with different Ni content. The aim is to 
clarify the effect of Ni addition on oxidation properties of Co -based superalloys, and provide a

2 Experiments 
New type Co -based superalloys were prepared by double vacuum melting methods into Ф11 
mm cast bar. Table 1 lists the nominal compositions of the superalloys used in this study. Alloying 
elements like Ti, Ta and Mo are added into the superalloys to increase γ solvus temperature. B 
and Zr are added to strengthen the grain boundary. Co-based superalloys were homogenized at 
1200 °C for 12 h and then aged at 900 °C for 100 h to get fine γ′ phase. Superalloys were loaded 
into quartz tubes with high purity argon during heat treatment. 
For high temperature oxidation tests, samples with a dimension of 10 mm 10 mm 3 mm 
(lengthwidthheight) were used . During tests, the samples were placed in the alumina crucibles 
which were heated to constant weight. All the samples were ground by SiC sand papers from 240 
grit to 1500 grit and cleaned by CH 3CH2OH in ultrasonic cleaner ( JP-040ST). Cyclic oxidation 
tests were carried out at both 800 °C and 900 °C in air . Oxidation tests were carried out in a 
muffle furnace (SX-10-13). The interval of the first 10 cycles was 2 h and the next cycles was 23 h; 
the total oxidation t ime was 503 h. Each cycle consisted of exposing the superalloys inside the 
furnace for the certain oxidation time and then 25 min air -cooling outside the furnace. After tests, 
the samples were cooled in air to room temperature. W eight gains (both the samples and the 
crucibles) were measured by analytical balance (Sartorius BS 224S) with the accuracy of 0.0001 g. 
The oxidation products were examined via X -ray diffraction (XRD, Smart Lab) with 
Cu-Kα
 The morpholog y of the oxidation products was characterized by scanning 
electron microscopy (SEM, SSX 550 and Ultra Plus) ; the accelerating voltage is 10 kV . 
Qualitative and quantitative chemical compositions were determined by energy dispersive 
spectroscopy (EDS, X -maxN) and electron probe micro -analyser (EPMA, J XA-8530F), 
respectively. The accelerating voltages of EDS and EPMA are 15 kV. A least 5 selected zones 
(800 °C: Ф1 μm; 900 °C: Ф3 μm ) covering the spinel oxide scale were used to certain the 
component concentrations by EPMA. 
3 Results 
Fig. 1 shows the oxidation kinetic curves of Co -based superalloys at 800 °C and 900°C , 
respectively. The thermogravimetric results of different Ni -containing superalloys oxidized at 
800 °C indicate a transient oxidation and a steady -state period [ 17]. The average mass gain rates 
of the superalloys are 1.0×10 -2-1.1×10-2 g/(m2 h1) at 800 °C and 3.1×10 -2-3.7×10-2 g/(m2 h1) at 
900 °C. Compared with the results of Yan et al. [12], the average mass gain rates are relatively 
lower. 
Fig. 2 shows t he XRD results of the oxidized superalloys. After oxidation at 800 °C for 503h, 
the oxidation products are mainly Al2O3, Cr 2O3, TiTaO4, CoWO 4 and spinel -structure Co2CrO4 
and CoCr 2O4. Moreover, Co 3W also have formed due to the removal of Al [4]. Increasing 
oxidation temperature leads to CoO formed at 900 °C. As the XRD spectrum shows, the products 
of the testing superalloys with different Ni addition are similar, which indicates increasing Ni 
addition may not change the types of oxidation products. 
Fig. 3 shows the cross -sectional morphology of the oxidized superalloys (800 °C for 503 h in 
air). According to EDS, t he products in the oxide layer can be identified as an outer oxide scale 
rich in Co and Cr , a thinner scale of TiTaO4 as well as an inner Al2O3 oxide scale (plus some 
CoWO4). The outer oxide scale is mainly composed of the spinel oxides and Cr2O3. There is also

an oxidation -effect zone (OEZ) between the oxides and γ/γ′ matrix [4, 6]. Combined with the 
XRD results shown in Fig. 2(a), the main phases in OEZ are Co 3W and γ. The mean depths of 
oxide layer and the thickness of OEZ are shown in Table 2. Both the oxide layer depth and OEZ

Table 3 shows the mean thickness and Cr 3+ concentration of the spinel oxide scales. It is found 
that Cr 3+ concentration in the spinel oxide layer is 8.8 at.% in 16Ni superalloy. But in 24Ni 
superalloy, it is 29.1 at.%. The superalloy with relatively more Ni content shows more Cr 
concentration in the spinel oxide layer. Cr 2O3 and the coalescence of Co -Cr spinel oxides could 
help to hinder O diffusion [18, 19]. Thus, increasing Ni addition leads to more Cr -rich oxides 
formed in the outer oxide layer and helps to protect superalloys from further oxidation. 
At 900 °C oxidation, there are two kinds of oxide products formed on the surface. As shown in 
Fig. 4, oxides in region 1 are compact. According to EDS results, the oxides are spinel oxides. In 
region 2, block y CoO with obvious pores has formed on the top of spinel oxides . The 
cross-section morphology of the two regions are shown in Fig. 5. It is clear that the cross-section 
morphology of the two regions is quite different. In region 1, the oxide layers formed at 900 °C 
have the similar microstructure to that formed at 800 °C. Besides the external oxides, particle-like 
and strip-like nitrides rich in Ti disperse beneath Al2O3 scales. EPMA (Table 3) results show that 
Cr tends to be enriched in spinel oxide scales as well. Cr concentration in spinel oxide scale is in 
this order: 16Ni<20Ni< 24Ni. With the increasing of Ni addition, more Cr can be observed in 
spinel oxide scales, which helps to form protective spinel oxide scales. 
Different from the uniform oxide layer in region 1, nodular oxidation occurs in region 2. As 
shown in Fig. 5(d), (e) and (f), porous CoO as well as blocky Co 2CrO4 have formed on the top of 
spinel oxides. Simultaneously, the superalloys undergo the severe internal oxidation. According to 
EDS, the oxides beneath spinel oxide scale are mostly wolframite-structure CoWO4 (white 
contrast) and spinel CoAl2O4 (grey contrast). No continuous Al 2O3 scale can be found underlying 
the nodular oxides. As shown in Table 2, the nodular oxide depth is much deeper than the oxide 
layer depth in region 1 . Thus, i t is nodular oxidation that causes the corresponding increase in 
mass gain as shown in Fig. 1. The corresponding increase time in 16Ni, 20Ni and 24Ni 
superalloys are 204 h, 296 h and 342 h, respectively. It helps to distinguish the sequence of 
nodular oxidation in different Ni-containing superalloys. So t he later corresponding increase time 
indicates that increasing Ni addition delays the occurrence of nodular oxidation . Therefore, the 
higher Ni-containing superalloys show the shallower nodular oxide layer depths (Table 2). 
4 Discussions 
4.1 Oxidation process 
In order to analyse the oxidation process, oxidation characteristics of the testing superalloys 
oxidized at 900 °C for 60 h were also studied. Fig. 5 shows the XRD pattern of the oxidation 
products in 20Ni superalloy. The main oxides are Cr 2O3 and TiTaO4, which is different from the 
oxide composition of the superalloy oxidized at 900 °C for 503 h. The cross-sectional morphology 
of 20Ni superalloy oxidized at 900 °C for 60 h is shown in Fig. 6. After oxidatio n for 60 h, the 
oxides are ma inly Cr2O3, TiTaO4 and internal Al 2O3. Combined with the images in Fig. 5(a), (b) 
and (c), the outer oxide s will change from Cr 2O3 to spinel oxides. A fter long -term oxidation, 
protective Al2O3 scale will form, which helps to increase the oxidation resistance.

The oxidation process of γ′-strengthened Co-base superalloys can be described by the schematic 
diagram in Fig. 7. Internal oxidation occurs at initial stage. Since Al has a strong affinity for 
oxygen in Co-base superalloys [20], the internal oxide is mainly Al 2O3 by EDS. Consequently, the 
oxygen pressure is decreased. The lower oxygen pressure allows the diffusion of Cr through the 
internal oxidation zone to the reaction front [21, 22]. So external Cr2O3 scale forms. Moreover, 
rutile-structure TiTaO4 can also form in the oxide layer. With the increasing of oxidation time, 
spinel oxides form by the reactions:

4.2 Nodular oxidation 
In nodular oxidation, there are two features: (i) blocky CoO with pores forms on the spinel 
oxides, and (ii) no continuous oxide scales can be found except for spinel oxide scales. It has been 
reported that the formation of nodular oxidation is associated with either cracks or loca l depletion 
or enrichment of solute [29-31]. But no cracks can be found in protective spinel oxide scales in the 
present research . During oxidation , Co 3W is easy to form and causes the inhomogeneous 
distribution of Al (Fig. 7). It is hard to form continuous Al2O3 scales. Combined with the features, 
the major factors of nodular oxidation are the discontinuity of Al 2O3 and the formation of CoO. 
During oxidation, the diffusion flux of cation s is larger in region 2 than that in region 1 due to the 
discontinuous Al 2O3. So it is easy for CoO to nucleate as a result of Cr depletion [19, 21]. The 
growth of CoO is fast, which leads to more vacancy injected in S /A interface due to the bulk diffusion 
in spinel oxides [32-34]. Thus, the vacancy gradient is established, and Co diffusion is promoted. It has

been reported that O can be trapped by vacancy to form vacancy -oxygen complexes [2 6-28, 35]. The 
interaction between Co diffusion and vacancy-oxygen complexes causes the increasing of the solubility 
of oxygen, which accelerates the internal oxidation. 
The process of nodular oxidation can be illustrated in Fig. 8e and f. CoO forms in the region 
where Al2O3 scale is discontinuous. The rapid growth of CoO leads to more vacancy generated. The 
diffusion of O and Co is promoted under the interaction with the vacancy. Thus, the rates of both 
internal oxidation and the growth of CoO are increased. Moreover, the oxygen pressure in S /A 
interface is also increased, which leads to the transform f rom Al2O3 to CoAl2O4 and the formation of 
CoWO4. The reactions are: 
[Co] + Al2O3 + [O] = CoAl2O4 (4) 
[Co] + [W] + 4[O] = CoWO4 (5) 
When further Cr is supplied, Co 2CrO4 forms on the top of CoO by reaction (2). But the Co 2CrO4 
loses its original protective effect because O can easily diffuse through the pores in CoO. 
4.3 Ni effect on formation of Cr-rich oxide scales 
The evaluation data in Table 2 indicate that high temperature oxidation resistance could be 
increased by increasing Ni addition in Co -based superalloys. In 2016, Zenk et al. reported the 
oxidation properties of Ni -xCo-9Al-8W-8Cr-0.04B alloys [36]. The results show that, in Co -rich 
alloys, the oxidation resistance can be increased by increasing Ni addition. Not only that, 
Maslenkov et al. studied the oxidation behaviour of Co-20Cr-9.3Ni (8#), Co-20Cr-17.5Ni (7#) and 
Co-20Cr-27.3Ni (6#) at 1200 °C [37]. The mass gains of the alloys are in the order: 8 #>7#>6#. 
The results illustrate that Ni does have beneficial effect on high temperature oxidation of Co-based 
superalloys. It is found that Cr is added into the testing Co -based superalloys in these studies. 
Accordingly, in Cr-containing Co-base superalloys, increasing Ni addition contributes to improve 
the oxidation resistance.

increasing Ni addition (Table 3). These results show that Ni addition effect on high temperature

4.4 Ni effect on nodular oxidation 
As the discussed above, the Cr3+ flux will be increased, while the Co2+ flux will be decreased by 
increasing Ni addition. The nucleation of CoO will be hindered in higher Ni-containing superalloys 
under the same cycle duration . Therefore, nodular oxidation is delayed. Not only that, the 
generation of protective Al2O3 is enhanced by increasing Ni addition due to the increased Al 
diffusion [16], which helps to hinder the occurrence of nodular oxidation. 
Even though nodular oxidation occurs, the presence of spinel oxide scale helps to hinder the 
diffusion of the cations and anions. Both the growth of the blocky oxides and internal oxidation 
rate will be decreased. So the average mass gain rates of the testing superalloys are relatively low 
[17], which are 3.5×10-2-4.8×10-2 g/(m2 h1). 
 
5 Conclusions 
1. The γ′-strengthened new type Co-base superalloys show excellent oxidation resistance at 
800 °C, which attributes to the protective spinel oxide and Al 2O3 scales. At 900 °C, n odular 
oxidation occurs, but the average mass gain rates are relatively low compared to that of other 
Co-Al-W-base superalloys due to the formation of spinel oxide scales. 
2. Increasing Ni addition increases the Cr3+ flux while decreases the Co2+ flux to the reaction 
front, which promotes the growth of Cr 2O3. Therefore, the concentration of Cr3+ in spinel oxide 
scales is increased by increasing Ni addition in Co-base superalloys. 
3. Nodular oxidation is related to the discontinuity of Al 2O3 and the nucleation of CoO. It is 
easy for CoO to nucleate in the region where Al2O3 scale is discontinuous. Once CoO forms, t he 
rapid nodular oxidation rate leads to the corresponding increase in the mass gains. However, the 
nucleation of CoO is hindered by increasing Ni addition due to the lower Co concentration in 
scale/alloy interface. And nodular oxidation will be consequently delayed in higher Ni-containing 
superalloys.
