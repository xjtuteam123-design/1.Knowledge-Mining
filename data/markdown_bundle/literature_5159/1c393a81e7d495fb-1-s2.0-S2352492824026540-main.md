ABSTRACT
This paper investigated the grain structures and mechanical properties of GH3625 alloy fabricated by Selective 
Laser Melting （ SLM ） technology at different heat treatment temperatures. GH3625 alloy samples were fabri -
cated using SLM and subjected to heat treatment at different temperatures. Microstructural characterization was 
conducted using Optical Microscopy (OM), Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM), and Electron Backscatter 
Diffraction (EBSD). Tensile tests were also performed to evaluate the mechanical properties. It was found that the 
heat treatment temperature significantly affects the microstructure of SLM-fabricated GH3625 alloy. As the 
temperature increases from 950
◦
C to 1100
◦
C, the grains gradually become uniform equiaxed morphologies with 
weakened grain preferred orientation, increased grain size, reduced average misorientation, and increased 
proportion of high-angle grain boundaries. The transformation of microstructure leads to a decrease in tensile 
strength and an increase in plasticity of the alloy. Samples treated at 950
◦
C have higher tensile strength,while at 
1100
◦
C exhibits higher plasticity. The study shows that by controlling the heat treatment temperature, the grain 
size, grain boundary characteristics, and dislocation density of SLM GH3625 alloy can be adjusted, thereby 
affecting the mechanical properties of the alloy, providing important technical support for its application in high- 
performance fields.
1. Introduction
Ni-based superalloy GH3625 is widely used in the manufacturing of 
high-temperature components in aerospace, nuclear power generation, 
and chemical equipment due to its excellent high-temperature strength, 
oxidation resistance, and corrosion resistance [1,2] .
As additive manufacturing technology advances, Selective Laser 
Melting (SLM) technology has provided a new way for fabricating 
GH3625 alloy ， with its advantages in manufacturing complex-shaped 
parts [3] . However, the rapid melting and solidification process in 
SLM may lead to coarse and uneven grains, which affects mechanical 
properties [4] .
Heat treatment, as a post-processing technique, has been proven to 
significantly improve the microstructure and mechanical properties of 
materials manufactured by SLM [5 – 7] . For GH3625 alloy, appropriate 
heat treatment can not only refine grains but also enhance its strength 
and toughness through precipitation strengthening mechanisms [8] . 
Therefore, studying the heat treatment of SLM GH3625 alloy is of great 
significance for optimizing its microstructure and mechanical 
properties.
Although there have been studies on the microstructure and me -
chanical properties of GH3625 alloy manufactured by SLM, systematic 
research on the impact of different heat treatment processes on its grain 
structures and mechanical properties is still limited [9,10] . This study 
aims to provide a scientific basis for the processing and application of 
this alloy by investigating the effects of different heat treatment tem -
peratures on the grain structures and mechanical properties of SLM 
GH3625 alloy.

2. Materials and methods
2.1. Materials
The material used in the experiment is GH3625 alloy powder, pro -
duced by Avimetal AM Tech Co.(Beijing, China), Ltd through gas at -
omization method, with the detailed chemical composition shown in 
Table 1 . The powder particle size ranges from 15 μ m to 53 μ m, meeting 
the specific requirements for powder particle size of GH3625 alloy 
during laser selective melting and forming process.
2.2. Forming and heat treatment
SLM forming experiments were conducted on the LIM-X260A 
equipment (Tianjin Lim Laser Technology Co., Ltd) ， which equipped 
with an Nd: YAG laser with a maximum power of 500 W, a wavelength of 
1070 nm ± 10 nm, and a spot diameter of 80 – 100 μ m. Argon gas is used 
as the protective gas inside the forming chamber, and the oxygen con -
tent is controlled to be below 300 ppm. The optimized forming param -
eters include a laser power of 285 W, a scanning velocity of 960 mm/s, a 
powder layer thickness of 40 μ m, a scanning interval of 0.12 mm, and a 
67
◦
rotation between adjacent scanning layers.
After the SLM forming was completed, wire cutting was used to 
remove the samples from the build platform, followed by heat treatment 
at 950
◦
C, 1000
◦
C, and 1100
◦
C for 1 hour, respectively ， water cooling.
2.3. Sample preparation
The preparation of GH3625 alloy samples for microstructural ex -
amination commenced with the grinding and mechanical polishing of 
both as-built specimens and those subjected to heat treatment at varying 
temperatures. Following a ultrasonic cleaning in an alcohol bath for a 
duration of 10 min, the samples were delicately etched using a mixed 
solution of hydrochloric and nitric acids in a 3:1 ratio.
Microscopic organization observation of the sample scanning surface 
(i.e. XOY surface) was conducted on a optical microscopy (OM) and 
scanning electron microscope (SEM). Electron backscatter diffraction 
(EBSD) was used to analyze the scanning surface (XOY) of the samples to 
study the grain structures during the heat treatment process.
The preparation method of EBSD specimen is to cut the threaded end 
of the tensile specimen with a diameter of 14 × 5 mm by wire, and after 
polishing, use electrolytic polishing to eliminate the mechanical strain 
layer on the surface of the specimen.
For Tensile sample, SLM-fabricated cylindrical samples of Φ 14 ×
76 mm were mechanically processed and the tensile sample specifica -
tions are shown in Fig. 1 .
2.4. Characterization and testing
The surface microstructure of the sample was examined through a 
Leica optical microscope (OM) and a Thermo Scientific Apreo2C HiVac 
field emission scanning electron microscope (SEM), respectively.
The sample ’ s scanning surface was subjected to electron backscatter 
diffraction (EBSD) analysis using a SEM equipped with an EDAX Ve -
locity Super detector. The acquired data was then analyzed and pro -
cessed with the HKL Channel 5 software to investigate grain properties.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Grain structure
Fig. 2 shows the microstructure of the SLM GH3625 alloy samples of 
as-built and heat treatment. The as-built samples formed by SLM with 
the parameters used in this study had a dense microstructure, with no 
obvious visible cracks or porosity defects, and a density is above 
99.85 %. The microstructure morphology of the scanning surface 
showed as Fig. 2 (a), the melting channels were continuous, and the 
adjacent melting channels had an angle of 67
◦
between them, corre -
sponding to the scanning strategy.
After heat treatment at 950
◦
C, the as-built microstructure gradually 
degraded, nucleated, and formed new fine-grained structures, but some 
of the original microstructure was still visible ( Fig. 2 b), suggesting that 
while the heat treatment fostered the recrystallization of the grains, it 
was not sufficient to erase the microstructural traits that were estab -
lished during the SLM fabrication process [11] . The presence of 
columnar subgrains and cellular subgrains can be observed under the 
scanning electron microscope ( Fig. 2 c).
When the heat treatment temperature was raised to 1000
◦
C, the 
microstructure of the alloy underwent significant changes. The original 
columnar grains and residual scanning traces of the as-built state almost 
completely disappeared, replaced by a uniformly distributed equiaxed 
mixed grain microstructure ( Fig. 2 d), indicating that the heat treatment 
temperature of 1000
◦
C was sufficient to promote complete recrystalli -
zation of the alloy, resulting in a more uniform and finer grain structure.
Further increasing the heat treatment temperature to 1100
◦
C, the 
microstructure of the alloy changed to fully recrystallized equiaxed 
grains ( Fig. 2 e), accompanied by the formation of lath-shaped twin 
structures. The formation of twins is closely related to the grain growth 
mechanism during the heat treatment process. During the migration of 
grain boundaries and the rearrangement of atomic layers, the stacking 
order at the grain boundary corners on the (111) plane is accidentally 
stacked incorrectly, leading to the formation of twin boundaries, and 
annealing twins subsequently formed at the grain boundary corners 
[12] . With the increase of heat treatment temperature, the lath-shaped 
twins also gradually grew larger. In the thickness direction, due to the 
merging mechanism, the number of twin plates gradually decreased, and 
the plates gradually widened. In the length direction, twins gradually 
grew larger with the migration of grain boundaries. The growth of twins 
was accompanied by the movement of dislocations, leading to the 
disappearance of some twin interfaces and a gradual decrease in the 
number of twins. This phenomenon may be related to the reduction of 
twin boundary energy and the interaction of dislocations [13] .
During the heat treatment process, these microstructural changes 
have a direct impact on the macroscopic mechanical properties. The 
formation of fine equiaxed grains and twin structures helps to improve

alloy ’ s plastic deformation ability. At a lower heat treatment tempera -

ability may be limited. Conversely, at an elevated heat treatment tem -

dislocations within the grains, potentially enhancing the alloy ’ s plastic 
deformation ability [19] .
3.2. Mechanical properties
3.2.1. Room temperature tensile performance
Table 2 shows the room temperature tensile properties of the SLM 
GH3625 alloy, subjected to three heat treatment temperatures. Within 
each temperature category, a set of three samples was tested, the 
tabulated data reveals that the tensile performance of the samples shows 
good stability after heat treatment.
The tensile strength of the SLM GH3625 alloy shows a decreasing 
trend with the increase of heat treatment temperature ( Fig. 6 ). After heat 
treatment at 950
◦
C, the strength reaches the maximum value, with an 
average tensile strength of 943 MPa and yield strength of 611.3 MPa. 
When the temperature is increased from 950
◦
C to 1000
◦
C, the tensile 
strength is significantly reduced. The average tensile strength drops to 
874 MPa, and the yield strength drops to 411.7 MPa, a decrease of 7.3 % 
and 32.7 %, respectively.
This reduction in strength can be attributed to the following aspects: 
First, as mentioned earlier, with the increase of heat treatment tem -
perature, the dislocation density decreases significantly. The reduction 
of dislocation density means that there are fewer obstacles within the 
alloy to hinder the movement of dislocations, and the ability to resist 
deformation is also reduced; Second, with the increase of heat treatment 
temperature, the grain size increases significantly, and the grain 
boundary strengthening effect is weakened; At the same time, it may 
also be related to the dissolution of carbides. As the temperature rises, 
carbides begin to dissolve into the matrix, reducing the number of 
strengthening phases, thereby reducing the strength of the material 
[20] .
When the heat treatment temperature is further increased to 1100
◦
C, 
the strength remains essentially stable. This stability of strength may be 
related to the residual stress and the segregation of alloy elements in the 
GH3625 alloy [21] which requires further research.
In summary, the heat treatment temperature significantly affects the 
tensile performance of the SLM GH3625 alloy. By optimizing the heat 
treatment process, the microstructure of the alloy can be controlled to 
achieve the best mechanical properties.
3.2.2. Fracture analysis
Fig. 7 presents the room temperature tensile fracture morphologies 
of the SLM GH3625 alloy at different heat treatment temperatures. 
Fracture morphology analysis is an essential means of understanding the 
alloy ’ s fracture mechanism. In this study, the fracture of the GH3625 
alloy after treatment at different solution temperatures all exhibited 
distinct dimple features, indicating that the fracture mechanism of the 
alloy is primarily ductile fracture.
Fig. 7 (a) to (c) show the fracture morphology of the samples treated 
at 950
◦
C. It can be observed that the dimples are small in size and 
shallow in depth, suggesting that at the lower heat treatment tempera -
ture, the alloy ’ s capacity for plastic deformation is limited. When the 
heat treatment temperature increases to 1000
◦
C and 1100
◦
C, the dim -
ples gradually become larger and deeper, reflecting enhanced plasticity 
of the alloy. This trend aligns with the data presented in Table 1 , which 
shows how plasticity values vary with temperature.
4. Conclusions
This paper investigates the grain structures and mechanical proper -
ties of GH3625 alloy prepared by SLM technology at different heat 
treatment temperatures, the findings are summarized as follows: 
(1) The heat treatment temperature significantly affects the micro -
structure of the SLM GH3625 alloy, including grain size and grain 
boundary characteristics. As the temperature increases from 
950
◦
C to 1100
◦
C, the grains gradually transform into uniform 
equiaxed morphologies, the preferred grain orientation weakens, 
the grain size increases, the average misorientation of grain 
boundaries decreases, and the proportion of high-angle grain 
boundaries increases.
(2) With the increase of the heat treatment temperature, the tensile 
strength of the alloy shows a decreasing trend, while the plas -
ticity is enhanced. The alloy heat-treated at 950
◦
C exhibits higher 
tensile strength due to its higher dislocation density; the alloy
