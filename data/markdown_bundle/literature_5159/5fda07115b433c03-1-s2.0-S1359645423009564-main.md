ABSTRACT 
Refractory multi-principal element alloys (RMPEAs) could provide next-generation high temperature alloys, but 
their ductility and high temperature strength need significant improvement. Emulating superalloy γ - γ’ micro -
structures, RMPEAs combining a ductile BCC matrix with embedded B2 precipitates for strengthening could meet 
this goal. Two-phase BCC-B2 RMPEAs have recently been demonstrated, but the B2 phase typically exhibits 
insufficient thermodynamic stability for operating temperatures ≥ 1300 
◦
C. 
Using high-throughput CALPHAD predictions, we screen across 3,500 potential BCC-B2 systems. Promising 
compositions are predicted for alloys combining Ru-based B2s with refractory BCC elements. A total of 20 such 
compositions were arc-melted to characterize their as-cast and heat-treated microstructures. In these alloys, the 
RuHf B2 exhibits exceptional stability beyond 1900 
◦
C but cannot be solutionized. By contrast, RuTi does sol -
utionize and reprecipitate between 1300 and 1900 
◦
C, providing a robust thermal processing pathway. RuAl can 
be solutionized but also tends to form competing intermetallic phases. Altogether, Ru-B2 RMPEAs offer great 
design flexibility and surpass the stability and thermal processability of previously studied BCC-B2 refractory 
alloys. 
1. Introduction 
High temperature structural alloys are widely utilized across energy, 
aerospace, and defense applications, for example in components for 
turbines and jet engines. Due to their ability to operate at high fractions 
of their melting point, Ni-based superalloys are a leading class of high 
temperature alloys [1 – 3] . Their γ - γ’ microstructures successfully bal -
ance ductility provided by the γ -FCC Ni matrix with high temperature 
strength provided by the coherent γ’ -L1

performance of superalloys has been continuously enhanced over the 
decades, operating temperature limits have recently plateaued around 
1100

stable at first, and then the alloy melts. 
In pursuit of higher temperature limits, alloys composed of the re -
fractory elements (Ti,Zr,Hf,V,Nb,Ta,Cr,Mo,W) offer a natural advantage 
in terms of melting temperatures. Traditionally, such refractory alloys 
were designed based on a single principal element, offering a limited 
range of materials and properties [4 – 6] . More recently, refractory 
multi-principal element alloys (RMPEAs) have departed from this 
paradigm: They contain three or more principal elements, each at sig -
nificant concentrations ( ≥ 15at.%), opening a vast new design space 
[7 – 10] . 
Initial efforts in RMPEA design focused on single-phase alloys, which 
have demonstrated some promising properties. Alloys in the 
TiZrHfNbTa system, for example, have received most attention due to 
their ductility, cold-workability, and moderate density [11 – 14] . Around 
1200 
◦
C, however, their yield strength drops significantly [15] . While 
additions of Mo and W can increase high temperature strength, they also 
impair ductility and manufacturability [ 5 , 16 , 17 ]. Additions of Cr and Al 
can reduce the density and improve oxidation resistance, but they also 
promote the formation of brittle intermetallic phases [18 – 20] . Alto -
gether, single-phase RMPEAs have not yet been able to meet the 
balanced set of properties needed for high temperature applications. 
By contrast, microstructures composed of two or more phases can 
offer greater design flexibility and additional mechanisms to engineer a 
competitive balance of properties [20 – 22] . In particular, precipitation 
and dispersion strengthening are proven strategies to achieving both 
ductility and high temperature strength. Further, coherent precipitates

3. CALPHAD assessment of the potential BCC-B2 RMPEA space 
3.1. Design goals 
The vast RMPEA composition space combined with the suite of 
available processing strategies offers an extensive toolbox to design 
BCC-B2 RMPEAs. The design requirements, however, are many, ranging 
from thermal stability, phase fraction, and morphology of the B2 pre -
cipitates, to the mechanical properties of both the BCC and B2 phase as a 
function of temperature, to additional requirements such as oxidation 
resistance, low density, cost, and manufacturability. In this study, we 
define three initial goals ranked by their importance: 
1: Phase stability: Target microstructures should be comprised of a 
primary BCC matrix phase, combined with significant fractions of 
embedded secondary B2 phase stable at least up to 1300 
◦
C. 
2: Thermal processability: Alloys preferably exhibit a B2 solvus 
transition above 1300 
◦
C. The resulting dissolution-precipitation 
pathway (BCC + B 2 ↔
T
solvus
BCC) enables microstructure control and reju -
venation by thermal processing. 
3: Precipitate morphology: Emulating typical superalloy γ - γ’ mi -
crostructures, the B2 phase ideally forms coherent, cuboidal, sub-micron 
particles. This morphology is best suited to harness matrix phase 
ductility, while retaining strength at elevated temperatures by forcing 
dislocations to bypass the more shear-resistant precipitates [41] . For 
coherence, lattice misfits within ± 1 % are typically needed [ 2 , 22 ]. 
3.2. Design strategy: pseudobinary BCC-B2 alloy systems 
In the first step of searching for suitable BCC-B2 RMPEAs, all binary 
alloy systems containing a refractory BCC element (Cr, Nb, Ta, V, Mo, 
W) and exhibiting B2 phase formation were examined. Only few such 
binaries can be found [42 – 45] and, based on the available equilibrium 
phase diagrams which are in some cases only partially developed, those 
binaries are of limited usefulness (see supplementary section 2.3). For 
example, in V-Ru, Nb-Ru, and Ta-Ru the B2 phase exhibits attractive 
thermal stabilities ≥ 1800 
◦
C, but no clear two-phase BCC + B2 field is 
observed. Instead, a continuous BCC to B2 ordering transition occurs 
along the compositional axis [46 – 48] . Other binaries are of limited use 
due to a low B2 thermal stability or neighboring intermetallic phases 
preventing BCC + B2 equilibrium. 
Beyond the refractory BCC elements, the refractory HCP elements 
(Ti, Zr, Hf) undergo allotropic transformations to BCC at elevated tem -
peratures. As such, they can exhibit BCC + B2 two-phase fields with 
conveniently sloped solvus lines (e.g. Hf-Ru [49 – 51] ). Nonetheless, their 
inclination to form HCP phases at lower temperatures limits their 
usefulness.

design strategy: First, two elements are selected for their ability to form 
a stable B2 phase. Then, this B2 forming pair is combined with one or 
more additional elements, selected to form a coexistent BCC phase. For

3.4. High-throughput CALPHAD screening of the potential BCC-B2 space 
The pseudobinary composition space can be defined as a two- 
dimensional combinatorial grid, where the first axis represents the B2 
formers and the second axis represents combinations of one or more 
matrix elements. Each grid field then corresponds to a potential pseu -
dobinary system.

are 279 experimentally observed B2 phases (see supplementary material

stability (e.g. Rh, Hg, Li, Tc). Excluding such constituents, only 24

Turning towards suitable elements for a high-melting BCC matrix,

the best foundation. Other non-BCC elements (e.g., Ni, Cu, Al) cannot 
provide a suitable basis for a BCC matrix, but minor additions could in 
principle be accommodated. In the interest of comprehensively 
screening the potential composition space, we choose to include the nine 
refractory elements as well as all other elements already represented in 
the list of practical B2s ( Fig. 2 a). From these 17 base elements (high -
lighted in Fig. 2 b), 153 unique combinations of one or two elements are 
obtained as potential matrix element combinations.

5. Discussion 
5.1. Agreement between predictions and experiments 
Across the grid space, the CALPHAD predictions ( Fig. 3 a) show 
surprisingly good agreement with the experimentally observed BCC-B2 
behavior ( Fig. 3 b). Indeed, when screening for promising BCC-B2 can -
didates ( Fig. 2 c), CALPHAD is expected to provide an effective tool to 
filter out those alloy systems that are likely to form unwanted competing 
phases, if the underlying binary and ternary systems have been critically 
assessed. Of the remaining alloy systems, many may be accurately 
labeled as potential BCC-B2 formers. However, many of these may also 
form unexpected competing phases, especially if the underlying binaries 
and ternaries have not been fully assessed. For the RuHf and RuTi-based 
RMPEAs studied here, it appears that, by favorable circumstances, no 
unexpected competing phases are formed, leading to the good agree -
ment between experiments and predictions. 
Meanwhile, CALPHAD is typically less accurate in predicting phase 
fractions, phase compositions, solvus temperatures and melting tem -
peratures in multicomponent alloys, which are critical parameters for 
the targeted design of two-phase microstructures. Experimental obser -
vations are invaluable to address this gap. Comparing the experimen -
tally observed phase formation behavior ( Figs. 3 – 5 ), the thermal 
stability, phase compositions and phase fractions ( Fig. 6 ), and the lattice 
constants and misfits ( Fig. 7 ) across the B2 and matrix elements can 
reveal overarching trends and design rules.

tioning of elements between the BCC and B2 phase could be utilized as 
additional design tool to tune phase properties including the lattice 
constants and misfit.
