ABSTRACT
As a material extensively utilized in aerospace applications, the mechanical properties of nickel-based single 
crystal (NBSC) superalloys are susceptible to anisotropic characteristics. These anisotropic features significantly 
impact surface wear in multi-axis stress environments, highlighting the importance of wear management for 
extending the lifespan of components. In this paper, the effect of anisotropy on the wear resistance of different 
crystal planes of NBSC superalloys at macroscale and room temperature was investigated. The results showed 
that the wear depths of (100), (110), and (111) crystal planes exhibit periodicities of 90
◦
, 180
◦
and 60
◦
, 
respectively. It was confirmed that while these anisotropic effects persist at high temperatures, they are 
diminished due to the formation of a protective glaze layer. Nanoscale tests further revealed that the anisotropic 
influence on surface wear depended on the angle between the slip system and the sliding direction. The smaller 
the angle, the more prone the material is to wear. These findings are not only beneficial for enhancing the cross- 
scale analysis techniques, but also for optimizing the selection of grain orientations to maximize the lifetime of 
NBSC superalloys.
1. Introduction
Nickel-based single crystal (NBSC) superalloys are extensively used 
in aviation and aerospace, particularly in aero-engine turbine blades, 
due to their exceptional creep, high-temperature strength, toughness, 
and tensile properties [ 1 – 6 ]. These turbine blades are exposed to prac -
tically high temperatures and huge centrifugal forces, resulting in a 
complex stress system with multi-axial loading [ 7 , 8 ]. The constantly 
fluctuating stresses and vibrations encountered during operation can 
lead to severe wear on NBSC surfaces [ 9 ]. A severely worn surface will 
exhibit numerous surface flakes that begin to peel off, each acting as a 
potential crack [ 10 ], which leads to fatigue failure [ 11 ]. Kapoor et al. 
suggested that controlling the increase in wear offers the possibility of 
extending the fatigue life of components [ 10 ]. It is also observed that at 
temperatures above 85 
◦
C, the wear rate and coefficient of friction (COF) 
decrease [ 12 ]. Moreover, the anisotropic characteristics of NBSC su -
peralloys have an essential influence on their mechanical properties, 
fracture resistance, creep, and fatigue life [ 13 – 18 ]. Therefore, investi -
gating the impact of anisotropy on surface wear of NBSC superalloys, 
particularly at elevated temperatures, is essential for enhancing the 
lifespan of aero-engine turbine blades.
The anisotropic characteristics and surface wear behaviors of NBSC 
superalloys have attracted considerable attention from researchers. Su 
et al. conducted fatigue tests at 600 
◦
C and 700 
◦
C on two secondary 
orientations, [110] and [010], and found that [110] has a superior 
micromotion fatigue life compare to [010] [ 19 ]. Lou et al. demonstrated 
that dislocation slip in NBSC superalloys is anisotropic depending on the 
crystal orientation, with plastic deformation closely related to the di -
rection of dislocation slip [ 20 ]. Stoyanov et al. observed that the coef -
ficient of friction (COF) differs between crystal planes, with the COF

Although the anisotropic characteristics and surface wear properties 
of NBSC superalloys have been extensively studied, the underlying 
mechanisms by which anisotropy influences surface wear are not yet 
fully understood. This paper employs a combined microscopic and 
macroscopic approach to investigate the surface wear across different 
orientations on three typical crystal planes — (100), (110) and (111) of 
NBSC superalloys. Additionally, the anisotropic effect of (100) crystal 
plane on surface wear is analyzed at temperatures up to 600 
◦
C. Mech -
anisms by which anisotropy affects surface wear are explored in 
conjunction with microscale wear experiments. These findings not only 
enhance the current understanding of the anisotropy-related wear 
behavior of NBSC superalloys but also provide a foundational basis for 
designing high-performance aero-engine components and improving the 
wear resistance of engineering materials.

2.1. Material and characterizations
The second-generation NBSC superalloys DD6 (the composition is 
shown in Table 1 ) were grown in accordance with the (100) crystallo -

China) using chemical-mechanical polishing (CMP). All samples were 
sonicated in ethanol for 5 min, then rinsed with deionized water and 
finally dried with nitrogen gas.

4. Conclusion
In summary, this paper investigated the mechanism of crystal 
anisotropy on the surface wear of NBSC superalloys, and revealed that 
the cross-scale anisotropic damage mechanism is primarily driven by 
differences in wear resistance due to the orientation of slip systems along 
specific crystal directions and surfaces. On the same crystal plane, the 
depth of wear tracks is more pronounced in the direction that aligns with 
the slip system. Furthermore, the mechanical properties of different 
crystal planes vary due to differences in atomic arrangement and density 
induced by the slip systems, which in turn affect the extent of material 
damage. This research offers a robust foundation and theoretical guid -
ance for enhancing life prediction models of NBSC superalloys.
