ABSTRACT
Superalloys have been used successfully to resist high temperature corrosion; however, they still lack
resistance to corrosion during longer periods of use. Inhibitors and fuel additives have been used to
reduce corrosion rates with varying degrees of success. In the present investigation, high temperature
corrosion studies were conducted on two Ni-base superalloys, namely Superni 718 and Superni 601,
in a simulated boiler environment (Na
2SO4 – 60%V2O5), with and without the presence of a CeO 2
coating as an inhibitor. Accelerated corrosion testing was carried out in a silicon carbide tube furnace
under cyclic conditions at 900
/C14 C for 50 cycles. Weight change measurements were made after each
cycle. The corroded samples were characterised by SEM, XRD and EPMA techniques. Superni 601
was found to be more corrosion resistant than Superni 718 in the Na
2SO4 – 60%V2O5 environment.
The presence of CeO 2 in the environment reduced the rate of corrosion of both the alloys, which was
attributed to the presence of unreacted CeO 2 on the surfaces of the samples. The presence of a Cr 2O3
layer in the oxide scale and a dense, thin Cr 2O3 layer at the substrate – scale interface may also be a
contributing factor for the protection of these alloys.
Keywords: high temperature corrosion, superalloys, inhibitors, oxide additives, oxide scales
INTRODUCTION
Metals and alloys sometimes experience accelerated oxida-
tion when their surfaces are covered with a thin ﬁlm of fused
salt in an oxidising atmosphere at elevated temperatures.
This mode of attack is known as high temperature corrosion,
which is also popularly known as ‘‘Hot Corrosion’’. Hot
corrosion can be deﬁned as deposit modiﬁed gas-induced
degradation of materials at high temperature [1]. Many
important engineering systems operating at high tempera-
tures (650 – 1100
/C14 C) involve contact of metallic or ceramic
materials with combustion gases containing inorganic impu-
rities, such as gas turbines, steam generators, incinerators and
numerous petrochemical process vessels. As the gases are
cooled, fused salt ﬁlms may condense on the hardware to
generate a highly corrosive condition analogous to aqueous
atmospheric corrosion resulting in accelerated degradation
known as hot corrosion [2]. A prime example of hot
corrosion is the attack of Ni-, Co- or Fe-base alloys by
Na
2SO4, which has served as a model for degradation
process in gas turbines and other fossil-fueled devices.
Nickel-based superalloys are the commercial alloys
commonly used for the manufacture of components used in
aggressive environments of gas turbines, steam boilers, etc.,
where these alloys are vulnerable to the hot corrosion
degradation. Several countermeasures have been suggested
and investigated by the researchers in the recent past. Effects
of surface-applied reactive element oxide ﬁlms and inhibitors
on the oxidation of alloys have also attracted considerable
attention. As early as 1945, a patent claimed that in the case
of chromia – forming alloys, the reactive element effect
(REE) could be achieved by a surface deposit of a reactive
element oxide [3]. There are numerous inhibitors commer-
cially available that are intended to reduce the severity of oil
ash corrosion (hot corrosion) such as Mg- and Mn-based
additives. CaO, MnO
2,A l2O3, ZnO, BaO, PbO, SiO 2, BaO,
Ba(OH)2, Ca(OH)2, CaCO3, as well as oil soluble Ni, Al, Fe
and other compounds. However, in commercial practice Mg-
and Mn-based inhibitors have proven to be effective [4]. In
this paper, the effect of an oxide additive, namely CeO
2,o n
the high temperature corrosion performance of two Ni-based
superalloys, viz Superni 718 and Superni 601, is investigated
in a simulated boiler environment, comprising Na 2SO4 –
60%V2O5, by accelerated corrosion testing.

RESULTS
Visual examination
The samples were critically assessed after each cycle for
change in colour, luster, and presence yabsence of the salt on
the surface of sample as well as scaling and spalling
tendency. During hot corrosion in the Na
2SO4 – 60%V2O5
environment, the colour of the Superni 718 sample changed
from reddish yellow to black, and the oxide scale formed was
loose and had a tendency to spall. The scale formed on the
Superni 601 sample, subjected to the same environment, was
dark black and was adherent in nature, with only marginal
spallation of the scale noticed in the form of black particles.
When exposed to the Na
2SO4 – 60%V2O5 þ CeO2 environ-
ment, the oxide scales for both the alloys were dull and
compact, with the presence of some whitish and grey areas
along with black patches indicating the existence of
unreacted thick salt ﬁlm on the surfaces of the samples.
However, the amount of unreacted salt present on the surface
of alloy Superni 718 was comparatively less when compared
to that on the alloy Superni 601. In both the cases, only
marginal spallation of the oxide scales was observed.

DISCUSSION
High temperature corrosion in Na
2SO4 – 60%V2O5
environment
In this environment, Superni 718 showed more overall
weight gain in comparison with Superni 601. Therefore, it
is evident that Superni 718 has a greater corrosion rate.
Furthermore, the alloys have shown relatively higher corro-
sion rates during the initial cycle of the study. Otereo et al .
[7] tried to explain the increase in corrosion rate in the initial
stages through the higher ﬂuidity of molten salt mixture
during their study on hot corrosion of IN-657. They
concluded that the molten salt acts as an oxygen carrier,
which leads to both increase in diffusion and transport
phenomenon. The protective scale is destroyed or eliminated
by molten salts and consequently the metal surface is
exposed to direct action by aggressive environments. The
molten salt also provides rapid diffusion paths for reactant
species.
In the case of the Superni 718, there is a gradual increase
in weight and the maximum weight gain was approximately
8.6 mg cm
/C0 2. A thin NiO layer had formed just above the
substrate. Above this thin NiO layer, there is a continuous
thin layer of Cr

portion of scale, containing oxides of V, Cr, Fe and Ni.
NiCr
2O4 and Ni(VO3)2 spinels were also present in the scale
as has been identiﬁed by the XRD analysis. The higher
corrosion rate in this case may partly be attributed to the

High temperature corrosion studies in
Na
2SO4 – 60%V2O5 þ CeO2 environment
In the presence of CeO 2, it has been observed that the oxide
scale thickness was reduced for both the alloys. The rate of
corrosion was reduced for both the alloys, as the weight gain
had decreased by 28% and 33% for Superni 718 and Superni
601 respectively. Thus it can be inferred that ceria is
effective in reducing the corrosion rate.
After exposure, a more intact layer of salt was present on
the Superni 601 when compared with the Superni 718 alloy.
In both cases, a thick continuous layer of Cr
2O3 was detected
by EPMA, which could have contributed to the decreased
corrosion rates. The presence of both Ce and V at discrete
locations would suggest the formation of CeVO
4, CeO2 was
detected using XRD, within the scale formed on Superni
601. The formation of CeVO 4 was also supported by the
EPMA micrographs. The presence of CeO 2 on the surface
and formation of CeVO 4 may be contributing to the reduc-
tion in corrosive attack for both alloys. Reidy and Jones [12]
proposed that ceria (CeO
2) is chemically inert in the presence
of the molten NaVO3 –S O3 system at 1073 K for SO3 partial
pressure up to 3 610 /C0 6 atm. But at higher SO 3 partial
pressure CeO 2 and V2O5 components within the melt react
to form CeVO 4. Further, Ecer et al . [13] observed that the
rates of oxidation for both Ni- and Fe-base alloys were
markedly reduced by the superﬁcial application of CeO
2
powder on the alloy surface and it also improved the scale
adherence and resulted in marked changes in the oxidation
morphology. Seal et al . [14,15] have also reported that
superﬁcially applied CeO
2 not only reduced the rates of
scale growth for all the three varieties of steel but also
imparted improved scale adhesion to the respective alloy
substrates. Similar ﬁndings have also been reported by Mitra
et al . [16] in their studies on superﬁcially applied CeO
2
coating on 304 stainless steel. They proposed that such
improvement could be due to the change in mechanism of
scale growth from the scale – gas interface to the alloy – scale
interface and faster attainment of a steady state Cr
2O3-rich
scale along with complex spinel formation.
CONCLUSIONS
The presence of a thin surface coating of CeO 2 inhibitor in a
Na2SO4 – 60%V2O5 environment led to a decrease in overall
weight gains for Superni 718 and Superni 601 superalloys
during high temperature oxidation at 900
/C14 C.
1. On the basis of the total weight gain measurements
(around 30 cycles), the following conclusions can be
drawn: (a) In Na 2SO4 – 60%V2O5 environment, the
corrosion rate for Superni 601 was lower than that for
Superni 718. (b)The effectiveness of CeO
2 was nearly
similar for both the superalloys.
2. The effectiveness of CeO 2 in reducing the high tem-
perature corrosion may be attributed to the presence of
unreacted CeO2 on the surfaces of the alloys.
3. The presence of a Cr 2O3 layer within the scale and dense
Cr2O3 thin layer at the substrate–scale interface may also
be a contributing factor for the protection of these alloys.
