temperature properties of Pt base alloys

Abstract
Pt–Al alloys attain high creep strength by precipitation hardening of the face-centred cubic matrix phase by the L1 2 ordered intermetallic γ′
phase Pt3Al (analogous to Ni base superalloys). Along with superior environmental resistance these alloys have a potential as high-temperature
materials. In this investigation the effect of Ta on strength, phase composition, lattice misﬁt andγ′volume fraction are assessed. Starting from the
composition with 12 at.% Al, 5 at.% Cr and Pt balance, precipitation hardened Pt–Al–Cr–Ta alloys were analysed. Ta contents higher than 9 at.%
lead to a decrease of γ
′volume fraction. Ta increases the lattice parameters of both phases, but decreases the lattice misﬁt. By substitution of Al
by Ta more Al partitions to the γ′phase, while Ta shows an inverse tendency.

1. Introduction
At present Ni base superalloys are the most advanced alloys
for use in high-temperature applications up to about 1100 ◦C.
These alloys exhibit the best combination of high-temperature
mechanical properties and corrosion resistance in comparison
with other materials. These two-phase alloys consist of a matrix
or γ phase (Ni solid solution) and a γ
′phase (L12 ordered with
a composition close to Ni 3Al). They contain up to 10 alloying
additions and γ′volume fractions of up to 70% [1]. At this vol-
ume fraction the highest creep strength is observed[2]. However,
the application of Ni base superalloys is limited by the melting
point of Ni at 1455
◦C. Considerably higher service tempera-
tures require the development of materials based on elements
with higher melting points.
One of the promising base elements is Pt due to its high
melting temperature of 1770
◦C. Alloys with Pt as base element
are used for example in bushings for glass ﬁbre fabrication [3].
In the glass industry, Pt alloys are generally used for their out-

standing corrosion and oxidation resistance combined with good
mechanical strength at ultra-high temperatures.
Precipitation hardened Pt base superalloys are analogous to
the Ni base superalloys [4–9] in terms of a face-centred cubic
(fcc) matrix strengthened by coherent precipitations of L1 2
ordered γ′ particles. In Pt base superalloys the γ′ phase has
a composition close to Pt 3Al. The microstructure of Ni base
superalloys is regarded as a model for the development of Pt
base superalloys.
In order to achieve high creep strength the following param-
eters have to be considered: size, form and arrangement of γ
′
particles, high γ′volume fraction, slow coarsening of the pre-
cipitates and optimised misﬁt between matrix and γ′particles.
Precipitation strengthened alloys of the system Pt–Al–Cr–Ni
was studied in previous work [6–10]. The effects of Al, Cr and
Ni additions and various heat treatments on the properties of
Pt–Al–Cr–Ni alloys have been described in detail. V orberg et
al. [9] analysed the microstructure of Pt alloys by means of
transmission electron microscopy (TEM). The authors observed
γ
′precipitates of irregular, cubical and round shape. Dislocation
networks at the γ/γ′ interface in some samples were visible.
The shape of γ′particles and the dislocations structures in the
investigated alloys are correlated with the misﬁt stresses.

In the present work Pt alloys with the alloying elements Al,
Ta and Cr are examined. Particularly the inﬂuence of Ta on
the above-mentioned parameters was analysed. For microstruc-
ture analysis, scanning electron microscopy (SEM) was applied.
Similar shapes ofγ
′precipitations as in TEM observations were
detected, which again, can be related to misﬁt values.
In all examined alloys Al serves as γ′ forming element.
Numerous X-ray diffraction (XRD) experiments on ternary
Pt–Al–X alloys from Hill et al. [4,5] have proven, that Cr
stabilises the L1
2 structure of theγ′phase down to room temper-
ature, moreover it has a positive effect on the high-temperature
strength and phase stability. In this investigation, Al is substi-
tuted gradually by Ta.
2. Experimental
Compositions of Pt

3.1. Microstructure after homogenisation (1520

Fig. 1 shows the microstructures of the ﬁve alloys after

3.5. Slow compression tests
The compression tests were carried out at the National Insti-
tute for Material Science (NIMS), Japan. The acting load and
the plunger position were measured. The resulting stress and
strain were calculated and the maximum compression stress was
determined. The maximum stresses against the alloy composi-
tions are plotted in Fig. 7 . Ta additions up to 9 at.% lead to
signiﬁcant enhancement of high-temperature strength, whereas
at both investigated temperatures a decrease in maximum com-
pression stress was observed for Ta12. At the same time Ta
leads to a decrease in ductility. On the surfaces of samples
with higher Ta content, considerable more ruptures traces were
observed.
4. Discussion
Ta has a positive inﬂuence on the high-temperature strength of
Pt base materials. Compression tests from Hill et al.[19,20] with
ternary Pt base alloys demonstrate that additions of Ta compared
with other elements such a Ti, Ru, Ir and Cr lead to the highest
increase of yield strength at 800, 1100 and 1300
◦C. On the other
hand Ta induces poor oxidation properties at high temperatures.
Douglas [21] assumes that solid solution hardening of the
γ matrix is decisive for the strengthening of two-phase Pt base
superalloys. In the present work a tendency to an increase of
the maximum stress by increasing Ta content was observed
too, which can be related to solid solution hardening. But there
are other effects that need to be considered as well: size and
morphology of γ
′particles, γ/γ′misﬁt and the distribution of
alloying additions onto the phases.

In all alloys, except Ta12, a second phase with L1 2 lattice
structure was observed. The Pt amount of the γ′phase at each
alloy composition was 77–78 at.%, similar to Pt3Al phase of the
binary system Pt–Al [12,22]. According to Hill et al. [19,20],
it can be assumed that Ta and Cr substitute Al atoms on their
lattice sites in the L1
2 structure. EDX analysis reveals that Al
is enriched in the γ′ phase. With substitution of Al by Ta a
larger part of the remaining total Al content partitions to the γ′
phase, while Ta shows an inverse tendency (see Fig. 3). It can
be explained with a lower solubility of Al in Pt compared with
Ta in Pt (see Pt–Al and Pt–Ta phase diagrams[22]).
With an increase in temperature the solubility of Al rises,
while the solubility of Ta remains unchanged. This explains
why a reduction of Al distribution coefﬁcient at higher tem-
peratures is observed. A decrease of the γ
′volume fraction can
be explained with the solubility of Al in Pt as well, because Al
plays a major role for the formation of γ
′phase. Although Ta
atoms substitute Al atoms on their sites in the L1 2 structure,
the γ′volume fraction at higher Ta contents is reduced. How-
ever, Ta partitions strongly to theγ′phase at low concentrations,
i.e. Ta acts as aγ′forming element. At the same time the lattice
constant of both phases increases. In the matrix phase the depen-
dence of the lattice parameter from Ta content is linear. However
the absoluteγ/γ
′misﬁt becomes lower, which enhances the long
time phase stability and results in cuboidal particles for Ta9.
A better mechanical strength of Ta containing alloys is based
both on the solid solution hardening effect of the matrix and on
the ﬁner γ
′precipitates, the more homogeneous dispersion and
low absolute lattice misﬁt ensuring coherency. The dependencies
of all parameters determined in this work after heat treatment for
24 h at 1200
◦C are displayed together with the evolution of the
microstructures with rising Ta content in Fig. 8. For higher Ta
contents theγ′precipitates are smaller. At 9% Ta the distribution
of precipitations is monodispers: particles of about the same size
are arranged periodically and oriented. They are cuboidal and
their size varies between 50 and 200 nm. Theγ/γ
′microstructure
in this alloy is close to a Ni base superalloy microstructure, which
is due mainly to an optimized misﬁt. Obviously misﬁt and small
γ
′particles in the alloy Ta9 are more important for a strengthen-
ing effect than the reduction in volume fraction from 20 to 14%.
Also ﬁner particles and lower misﬁt values lead to an increase
in mechanical strength. The solid solution strengthening of the
Ta containing alloys is presumably higher because of a larger
diameter of Ta (1.47 ˚A) in comparison with Al (1.43 ˚A) [18].
The alloy Ta12 has a single-phase structure. Its high-temperature
strength is much lower than that of Ta9. But in comparison with
the two-phase system Ta0 the maximum compression stress is
still higher. In this case the solid solution strengthening by Ta
provides a higher strength increase than by γ
′precipitate hard-
ening with a volume fraction of 19% but with large and irregular
shaped precipitations.
5. Conclusions
1. Ta additions to Pt base superalloys increase high-temperature
strength and phase stability, but decrease ductility and pro-
mote oxidation.
2. Because of reduction of Al solubility in the matrix phase
through Ta, more Al partitions to theγ
′phase and affects the
γ′volume fraction.
3. Ta increases the lattice constant of both phases and decreases
the absolute value of the misﬁt.
4. An optimum misﬁt value can be set up in order to reach
a desirable shape and distribution of γ′ particles, which
enhance high temperature material properties.
