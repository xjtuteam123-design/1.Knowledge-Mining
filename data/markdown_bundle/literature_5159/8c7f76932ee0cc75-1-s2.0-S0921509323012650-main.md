ABSTRACT 
Refractory multiple-principal element alloys (RMEAs) systems have drawn particular attentions in recent years 
due to their great potential for high-temperature application. However, most of RMEAs exhibit tensile brittleness, 
oxidation, and phase decomposition, which limit their potential applications. Hence, developing new RMEAs 
with excellent ductility has become one of the urgent tasks for RMEAs ’ applications. Here, a ductile VNbTa 
equiatomic RMEA has been fabricated by vacuum arc melting. The VNbTa alloy exhibits high tension strength

periments and theoretical calculations, but also provides more helpful information to design RMEAs with su -
perior ductility at room temperature.

high-purity V, Nb, and Ta raw metals (99.95 wt%) by non-consumable

2.2. Computational methods 
Potential phases in the equilibrium and non-equilibrium solidifica -
tion of VNbTa alloy were predicated by CALPHD calculations using the 
TCHEA5 thermodynamic database of ThermoCalc software. The elastic 
constants, electronic and thermodynamic properties of VNbTa alloy 
were investigated by performing first-principles calculations. Several 
special-quasirandom-structures (SQS) supercells containing 128 atoms 
in each supercell were constructed by Monte Carlo searching optimi -
zation algorithm [ 11 , 12 ]. The SQS model with the lowest energy was 
selected as the major supercell for calculating material properties. Fig. 1 
presents one typical SQS supercell of VNbTa alloy. The SQS supercells 
were fully optimized by relaxing their cell shape, volume, and atomic 
positions. After geometric optimization, the elastic constants of the alloy 
were calculated according to the Hook ’ s law, i.e., σ = C ε , where σ , C and 
ε are the matrix of stress, elastic constants and strain, respectively. For 
each deformation freedom, seven finite strains ( ε = 0.000, ± 0.005, 
± 0.010, ± 0.015) were performed to determine the relationship between 
stress ( σ ) and strain ( ε ). Other elastic properties, e.g., bulk modulus ( B ), 
shear modulus ( G ), Young ’ s modulus ( E ) and Poisson ’ s ratio ( ν ), were 
predicated by the Voigt-Reuss-Hill (VRH) approximation [ 13 – 15 ]. The 
electronic and lattice-distortion properties of the alloy were also eval -
uated based on the electronic density of states (DOS), electron locali -
zation function ( ELF ), and chemical bonding analysis. All first-principles 
calculations were implemented in the Vienna Ab-initio Simulation 
Package (VASP) within the density functional theory (DFT) framework 
[ 16 ]. The ion-electron interactions were described by plane-wave pro -
jector augmented wave (PAW) pseudopotential [ 17 ]. The 
exchange-correlation functional of electrons was expressed using a

The lattice rotation axes of kink bands can be identified by the 
intragranular misorientation axis (IGMA) analysis. According to the 
IGMA method [ 26 ], the lattice rotation axis ( T ) during kinking forma -
tion can be described by T = γ b × n , where γ , b and n are the plastic 
shear gradient, burgers vector and slip plane normal vector of slip sys -
tem. As a single-phase BCC alloy, a /2 < 1 1 1 > dislocation may glide on 
{1 1 0}, {1 1 2}, and {1 2 3} planes. The three slipping types correspond 
to < 1 1 2 > , < 0 1 1 > , and < 5 4 1 > lattice rotation axis, respectively. If 
the observed microbands are kink bands, the invariant crystal plane 
during deformation should belong to one of {1 1 2}, {0 1 1}, or {5 4 1} 
poles [ 27 ]. Fig. 7 d presents the {0 1 1}, {1 1 2} and {5 4 1} pole figures 
of the selected areas (dashed box) in Fig. 7 a. An apparent pole focus is 
observed in the {0 1 1} figure, all {1 1 2} and {5 4 1} poles rotated 
around this focused pole. The pole focus in the {0 1 1} figure means that 
the deformation bands in Fig. 7 a are proved to be kink bands. The < 0 1 
1 > rotation axis corresponds to the arrangement of dislocations with 
a /2 < 1 1 1 > Burgers vector on {1 1 2} planes, implying that a /2 < 1 1 1 >
{1 1 2} slipping systems were activated [ 25 ]. This conclusion is 
consistent with the dislocation mechanisms of kink bands formation in 
MoNbTaVW [ 27 ] and TiZrHfNb
0.7 
alloys [ 28 ]. 
It is generally accepted that kink bands is generated by local crystal 
reorientation induced from intensive dislocation activity [ 29 ]. For 
example, the kink bands in TiZrHfNbTa alloy are resulted from the 
increasingly aligned < 1 1 1 > {1 1 2} dislocations [ 30 ]. The formation of 
kink bands can relieve the stress concentration during plastic deforma -
tion, and benefit to improve the plastic workability of alloys. Thus, kink 
bands is recognized as a strain-softening characteristic [ 31 ]. In addition, 
there are apparent boundaries observed between the kink bands and 
adjacent regions ( Fig. 7 a). As shown in Fig. 7 b, the KAM values near the 
kink boundaries are larger than that inner kink bands, suggesting a 
higher local plastic strain around the kink boundaries. The local plastic 
strain and crystal misorientation may impede dislocation pass through 
kink boundary, similar to the strain-hindering effect of twin boundary 
[ 29 ]. It is well known that the formation of twining bands not only 
provides excellent plastic deformation capability but also contributes to 
enhance the strength of materials [ 32 ]. Unlike twinning bands, despite 
numerous studies conducted on kink bands, the fundamental mecha -
nisms of their formation and their effect on the mechanical properties of 
alloys remain unclear. More in-depth works are necessary for clarifying 
this issue in the future [ 29 ].

4. Conclusion 
To summarize, an equiatomic ternary VNbTa refractory medium- 
entropy alloy was prepared by vacuum arc melting. In the melting 
process, the VNbTa alloy melts solidify into a BCC single-phase dendritic 
microstructure. Composition segregation occurs between dendritic and 
interdendritic regions. The segregation of V is more significant than that 
of Ta and Nb due to the relative low melting point of V. The BCC phase is 
stable in a wide temperature range (318 
◦
C – 2145 
◦
C), and no solid-state 
phase transition occurs in the cold rolling and annealing processes. The 
alloy exhibits a good combination of strength ( σ
0.2 
= 1033 MPa), 
excellent tensile ductility ( ε
f 
> 19 %), super rolling formability ( > 80 % 
thickness reduction), and work-hardening effect at RT. The excellent 
ductility of VNbTa alloy originates from the mobility of a /2 < 1 1 1 > - 
type dislocations and the formation of kink bands. The elastic and 
thermodynamic properties of VNbTa alloy are also clarified. Solid so -
lution strengthening is the major strengthening mechanism of VNbTa 
alloy, which is associated with the local lattice distortion and inhomo -
geneous charge distribution induced by multiple elements. However, the 
dislocation and twinning mechanisms of VNbTa alloy need to be further 
characterized in future. Future work should pay attention to the 
oxidation and creep resistances of VNbTa alloy at high temperature, 
which are very important for its engineering applications.
