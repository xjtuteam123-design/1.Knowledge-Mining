processes of nickel-based superalloys

Abstract
Nickel-based superalloys are most commonly used in aerospace and marine applications owing to their superior mechan-
ical properties. They show great resistance against creep and stress rupture at high temperatures. However , they are con-
sidered dif ﬁcult to machine by conventional machining (CM) techniques owing to their low thermal conductivity, work
hardening ability, presence of hard carbide particles, and chemical af ﬁnity toward various tool materials. Hybrid-assisted
machining techniques can be an alternative to CM to improve the machinability of these alloys. Therefore, the present
article discusses the effect of heat-assisted machining techniques like laser-assisted machining (LAM), ﬂame-assisted
machining (FAM), plasma-assisted machining (PAM), and induction-assisted machining (IAM) of Ni-based superalloys com-
pared to CM. Additionally, cryogenic machining, ultrasonic vibration-assisted machining (VAM), and a combination of
hybrid-assisted machining techniques on the machinability of Ni-based superalloys have also been discussed.
Keywords
Laser-assisted machining, ﬂame-assisted machining, plasma-assisted machining, hybrid machining, Inconel 718

Introduction
Nickel-based superalloys are used for aero engine compo-
nents due to their high tensile strength and hardness over a
wide range of temperatures and possess better corrosion
resistance. They show superior strain hardening behavior
at low and high strain rates. This is due to the presence
of coherent carbide precipitates, in the austenite matrix
and at the grain boundaries, which hinder the dislocation
movement during plastic deformation. In high-strain rate
processes like machining, most of the heat will be taken
away through the chip. In the case of materials with poor
thermal conductivity, such as nickel base superalloys, a
steep temperature gradient develops at the tool chip inter-
face. Thus, the conventional machining (CM) of nickel-
based superalloy is dif ﬁcult due to high cutting forces,
excessive tool wear, and poor surface ﬁnish.
1,2
In recent years, hybrid machining processes seem to be
promising in improving the machinability of nickel-based
superalloys. They are broadly classi ﬁed into two groups,
the ﬁrst is a category of hybrid-assisted machining pro-
cesses and the second is the combination of hybrid
machining processes (Figure 1). 3 The material removal
takes place only by one process in the former while it
occurs due to multiple processes in the latter.
In heat-assisted machining process, several heating
sources (laser beam, 4 plasma arc, 5 induction coil, 6 gas
ﬂame,7 etc., as shown in Figure 2(a) –(c)) are used to
preheat the surface of the workpiece before machining.
Figure 2(a) shows the laser-assisted milling (LAML)
process in which the laser is used to preheat the material
in the temperature range of 750 –880°C to decrease the
yield strength of Inconel 718. This causes thermal soften-
ing of the workpiece material which improves the machin-
ability of the material. The advantages and limitations of
each heat source are presented in Figure 2(d).
The maximum surface temperature attained during
the heat-assisted machining process can be predicted
using infrared pyrometer and ﬁnite element analysis
(FEA).

on Inconel 718 without machining to determine the
heat-affected depth. They observed a maximum surface
temperature of 1080°C and three distinct microstructural
zones, that is, melted zone (top layer), martensitic zone,

Media-assisted machining techniques (Figure 4(a) –(c))
involve the supply of cutting ﬂuid in liquid/gaseous form
into the machining zone to reduce the temperature of tool
and/or workpiece. Based on the type of cutting ﬂuid and
volume of cutting ﬂuid supplied, media-assisted techniques
are classiﬁed into cryogenic machining,15 minimum quantity
lubrication (MQL),ﬂood cooling, etc. Incryogenic machin-
ing, the operational temperatures of cryogenic ﬂuids like
liquid nitrogen (LN 2) and liquid carbon dioxide (LCO 2)
are −196°C and −76°C, respectively, which are much
lower than the conventional cutting ﬂuids (room tempera-
ture). The cryogenic ﬂuids also act as a heat sink when
they come in contact with the cutting tool. MQL uses a
very low volume of straight/oil/water-based cuttingﬂuid in
comparison to cryogenic ﬂuids and a pressure of 2 to 6
bar. The cutting ﬂuid has delivered in the form of mist (a
mixture of compressed air and cuttingﬂuid) and can effect-
ively penetrate into a contact zone. The current review is
conﬁned to cryogenic machining and comparison among
the media-assisted machining techniques.
In 1D vibration-assisted machining (VAM), ultrasonic
generator uses either piezoelectric or magnetostrictive
actuator, which converts high-frequency electric signal
into reciprocating harmonic motion of high frequency
(15 kHz) and low amplitude (1 –15 µm). Sonotrode
(a combination of acoustic wave guide and horn) further
ampliﬁes the vibrations generated by the ultrasonic gener-
ator. These vibrations, in turn transfer to the cutting tool
placed at the horn ’s end (Figure 4(d)). Based on the
vibration pattern imposed on tool/workpiece, these are
classiﬁed into 1D VAM, 2D VAM, and 3D VAM.

The operating wavelength of lasers greatly affects the
absorptivity of metallic surfaces. The absorptivity of CO 2
laser (10.6 µm) on Inconel 718 surface can be improved
by applying suitable coatings.23,24 The use of shorter wave-

tivity and eliminates the need for additional coatings.
The surface temperature (preheat temperature) of
material depends on laser power and laser scan speed. It
increases with an increase in laser power and decrease
in laser scan speed.

Cutting forces. Cutting forces, in general, are reduced in
LAM in comparison to CM. This is primarily due to the
thermal softening of material, which results in a yield
strength reduction.
2,26 In the case of Inconel 718, the
deformation is heterogeneous at room temperature due
to the presence of precipitates. At higher temperatures,
once the stability of the precipitates have reached, disloca-
tions will be uniformly distributed in material, which
results in homogenous deformation.
27 In LAT and
LAML, within the effective depth of cut, cutting forces
decrease with an increase in depth of cut. 25,28 At low
cutting speeds, the percentage reduction in cutting
forces increases with an increase in cutting speed.
Beyond the optimum cutting speed and feed, the percent-
age reduction in cutting forces decreases. This is due to
the lesser interaction time between the laser beam and
workpiece.
29,30

notch wear. Abrasion wear and edge chipping were
lowered in LAM, resulting in improved surface ﬁnish.25

was improved by two to three folds, and ceramic tool

in surface roughness and a 50% reduction in tool chip-
ping.

a reduction in ﬂank wear in LAML with heat shield in
comparison to CM and LAML without heat shield. 33

Comparison of cryogenic machining of
Ni-based alloys with dry machining
In cryogenic drilling of Inconel 718, the cutting tempera-
tures are found lower than dry machining. The increase
in ﬂow strength of material results in higher cutting
forces.15,52 Further, it improves hole accuracy in terms
of geometrical tolerance parameters like cylindricity and
circularity.15 It produces better surface integrity due to
lower severe plastic deformation region and microhard-
ness in comparison to wet and dry machining.
52
Moreover, in cryogenic milling, the cutting forces are
observed lower for chosen machining parameters. This
is attributed to the improved lubrication capability of
cryogenic ﬂuid between chip and tool interface.

machining.

In 1D VAM, vibration to the cutting tool can be applied

periodic interaction with the workpiece. To ensure peri-

Conclusions and future scope
In the current review, the machinability studies of Ni-based
superalloys were reviewed using various assisted machin-
ing techniques. In LAM, beyond the optimum values of
cutting speed and feed, the percentage reduction in
cutting forces decreases with the increase in cutting vel-
ocity and feed. Further, the tool wear depends on laser
power and offset distance between the laser spot and
cutting edge of cutting tool. Use of heat shield between
the laser spot and cutting tool can effectively reduce the
thermal damage of tool. Furthermore, the residual stresses
magnitude and direction on machined surface mainly
depend on laser power. LAML of 3D surfaces using con-
touring approach provides better results in comparison to
CM. PAM and IAM provide lower cutting forces and
better tool life compared to CM. The cryogenic machining
results in lower tensile residual stresses on the surface and
higher compressive residual stress depth, better tool life
compared to CM. In VAM, a lower tool-to-work contact

Most of the researchers performed individual machinabil-

processes result in a synergistic effect while machining
Ni-based superalloys. It is further to iterate that the
heat-assisted milling techniques are not yet commercialized
due to its limitation in achieving uniform heating zones
ahead of cutting tool while machining 3D surfaces.
Machinability studies of 3D surfaces were performed
mostly in LAM. So there is ample scope for the researchers
to explore machinability studies on 3D surfaces using other
heat-assisted machining techniques.
