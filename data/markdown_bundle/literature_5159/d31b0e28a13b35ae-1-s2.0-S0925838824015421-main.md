ABSTRACT 
The room temperature strength and hardness of TaW alloys limit their further development for engineering 
applications. In order to improve the room temperature mechanical properties of TaW alloys, Hf elements was 
selected to strengthen the traditional TaW alloys. The elastic constants and charge densities of Hf-doped TaW 
alloys were calculated by first-principles to reveal the strengthening effect. It was found that the Hf alloying 
obviously enhanced the hardness of TaW alloys, which was attributed to the formation of metallic and ionic 
bonds in the alloys. In order to verify the calculated results, hardness and compression tests were carried out for 
TaWHf alloys. The hardness and yield strength significantly increased with the increased of Hf elements, which 
was in good agreement with the first-principles calculation. Among them, yield strength of Ta-10.17 W-19.83Hf 
alloy was as high as 1220 ± 8.4 MPa, twice larger than that of the normal Ta-10 W alloy without obviously 
sacrifice in plasticity. It was indicated that Hf alloying can effectively strengthen TaW alloys, which provided a 
guidance for the development of high-performance TaW alloys. 
1. Introduction 
Refractory alloys are those alloys that consist primarily of refractory 
metallic elements. They are widely used in many industrial fields due to 
their high melting points and excellent high-temperature mechanical 
properties [1,2] . Among them, Ta and its alloys were considered as 
excellent candidates for high temperature applications owing to their 
high melting points, high density, and superior machinability [3,4] . 
However, their insufficient strength and hardness at the room temper -
ature limited their further development in engineering applications [5, 
6] . Studies on TaW alloys have shown that the addition of W can in -
crease the yield strength and work hardening rate of Ta. It was found 
that the increase in yield strength was normally attributed to the solid 
solution hardening effects leading to the higher dislocation friction 
stresses. The increase in work hardening rate was related to dislocation 
density and alignment, reactivation barriers and dislocation capture 
rate [7,8] . This suggested that the addition of W had an important effect 
on the dislocation motion and the deformation behavior of the Ta alloys. 
This also implied that the deformation weave and microstructure can 
also be influenced by the addition of W [9,10] . It was reported that the 
mechanical strength of the Ta alloys was obviously enhanced due to the 
W addition, such as Ta-2.5 W, Ta-10 W and Ta-12.5 W alloys, while the 
brittleness of the alloys increases significantly with the increase of the W 
content [11 – 13] . 
Alloying was an effectively method to enhance the mechanical per -
formance of metals. Some studies had shown that the doping of re -
fractory elements can have greater improvement in the mechanical 
properties of TaW alloys. Cui et al. studied the effect of V addition on 
TaW alloys and found that doped V can enhance the metallicity of the 
alloys and affect the elastic modulus and stability of the alloys [14] . It 
had also been found that the doping of Mo improved the hardness of 
TaW alloys and it had been hypothesized that Mo doped also enhanced 
the wear resistance of TaW alloys [15] . Zhang et al. on the other hand 
showed that Nb doped can also strengthen TaW alloys. At the same time, 
they doped TaWNb alloys with Hf elements and found that the corrosion 
resistance of the alloys was greatly improved, but the hardness 
decreased [16] . Numerous studies have demonstrated that the doping of 
refractory elements can indeed play a role in strengthening TaW alloys,

Among refractory elements, the melting point of Hf elements is high, 
implying that alloying Hf into the Ta alloys will not sacrifice much to the 
high-temperature performance of TaW alloys. Additionally, the large 
atomic radius of the Hf atoms can enhance the lattice distortion effect of 
the alloying system and thus obviously strengthen the alloys [17] . At the 
same time, the Hf elements can promote the formation of oxide layer at 
high temperature and enhance the high temperature oxidation resis -
tance and corrosion resistance of the alloys [18,19] . However, there are 
few investigations on Hf-doped TaW alloys and whether alloying Hf can 
significantly strengthen TaW alloys is still not clear. Herein, Hf was 
selected as alloying elements to investigate whether it can strengthen 
TaW alloys. TaWHf alloys were modelled and their elastic constants, 
modulus and charge densities were calculated based on first-principles 
calculation, prediction of the effect of the presence of Hf on the 
strength and hardness of TaW alloys from computational results. And 
then a batch of TaWHf alloy ingots were prepared and experimentally 
tested based on the calculated alloys compositions to further validated 
the results obtained from the calculations. 
2. Methods 
2.1. Calculations 
Based on first-principles calculation, the CASTEP module of Mate -
rials Studio software was used to construct the models. By using the SQS 
method, 1 × 1 × 5 supercell models of TaW alloys were firstly built, the 
replacement doping of Hf atoms was carried out based on the optimized 
structurally stable TaW supercell model with full consideration of the 
substitution positions of Hf atoms. In particularly, we investigated the 
effect of doping with Hf elements and their doping content on the me -
chanical performance of TaW alloys. It was finally decided to use single 
Hf atom and two Hf atoms for the replacement doping of TaW alloys due 
to the size of the models. In the calculation, the electron-electron ex -
change correlation interaction was described by the Perdew Burke 
Ernzerhof (PBE) generalized function under the Generalized Gradient 
Approximation (GGA) [20] . Norm conserving pseudopotential for all the 
elements in reciprocal space were applied. The Schr ¨odinger ’ s equation 
was solved via Broyden-Flecher-Goldfarb-Shanno (BFGS) algorithm 
[21] . During structural optimization, the cells and atoms were fully 
relaxed until the SCF iterative convergence conditions were met: energy 
< 1 × 10
-5 
eV/atom, maximum force < 0.03 eV/Å, maximum pressure <
0.05 GPa, and maximum displacement < 0.001 Å. 
2.2. Experimental 
TaWHf alloys ingots were produced by using vacuum arc melting 
furnace and the ingots composition was metered according to the 
calculated material composition, using 99.99 per cent pure Ta blocks, W 
particles and Hf blocks as raw materials. In order to ensure the unifor -
mity of the alloy ingots composition, each alloy ingot was repeatedly 
melted 7 times. Based on the prepared ingots, samples for compression 
and hardness tests were prepared by electrical discharge machining. The 
size of the compression tests samples was Φ 4 × 6 mm cylinders, and three 
samples of each composition were prepared to ensure accuracy and 
consistency of the compression tests. The samples for hardness tests 
were 3 mm thick blocks, each sample was polished by sandpaper and 
polishing machine to ensure the surface of the specimen was smooth and 
even, and the tests were carried out used a semi-automatic Vickers 
hardness tester with a constant pressure of 0.1Kg and a holding time of 
15 s. 
3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Prediction of mechanical properties 
The supercell models of TaWHf alloys had been developed using the 
SQS method based on first-principles calculation. To investigate the law 
of influence of Hf content on the mechanical performance of TaW alloys, 
substitution doping was carried out used one Hf atom and two Hf atoms, 
respectively, with full consideration of all substitution doping positions. 
Fig. 1 (a) showed three models with one Hf atom doped, and Fig. 1 (b) 
showed six models with two Hf atoms doped. The structure of each 
model was optimized and its mechanical parameters, such as elastic 
constants, modulus and hardness were calculated, and the results were 
showed in Fig. 2 .

3.2. Electronic structure 
It is well known that the charge density and differential charge 
density reflect the degree of electron interaction between atoms and the 
type of bonding, which is important to determining the plasticity/brittle 
of materials [26 – 28] . In order to further investigate the effect pattern of 
alloying Hf on TaW alloys, we calculated the charge density and dif -
ferential charge density for each alloy model, with a view to analyzed 
the mechanism of the effect on atomic scale. The results of the charge 
density and differential charge density can be analyzed to predict the 
bonding within the alloys system and hence the associated property 
changes. 
Fig. 4 showed the charge density (a) and differential charge density 
(b) for three models of TaW alloys doped with single Hf atom. From the 
charge density diagrams, we can see that when the Hf atom doped, the 
electron cloud of the Hf atom had no obvious overlap with that of the 
surrounding atoms, and its electron cloud had no obvious directionality. 
This was thought to be metallic bond with the surrounding atoms. The 
electron clouds of the Ta and W atoms had obvious overlap and direc -
tionality, indicating that they formed covalent bonds. From the differ -
ential charge density plots, it can be seen there was a significant charge 
transfer throughout the alloys system, and the stronger the charge 
transfer strength, the stronger the bond energy of the metal bond 
formed. Observing the color states of each model under a uniform scale 
showed that the charge transfer intensity was different for each model, 
the bond energy strength of the metal bond formed was also different.

Fig. 5 showed the charge density (a) and differential charge density 
(b) plots of the TaW alloys doped with two Hf atoms. It can be seen in the 
charge density diagrams that with the increased of the content of Hf 
atoms, the electron cloud of Hf atoms did not change significantly. There 
was no obvious overlap with the electron cloud of the surrounding atoms 
and no obvious directionality, which was the same as that in the case of 
replacement doped of single Hf atom, and the formation of a metallic 
bond was presumed. The differential charge density diagrams showed 
that charge transfer still existed in the alloys system when the content of 
Hf atoms increased. The charge transfer was stronger in some of the 
models than in TaW alloys doped with single Hf atom, indicated that the 
formation of metallic bonds was also stronger. In general, the stronger 
the metal bonds, the harder the alloys would be, which was consistent 
with the results predicted by the previous changes in the elastic 
constants. 
3.3. Experimental mechanical properties 
In order to further verify the results predicted by the previous cal -
culations, we prepared a batch of specimens based on the calculated 
alloys composition, and compressive mechanical property tests and 
hardness tests were carried out on this batch of specimens. 
Fig. 6 showed the compressive stress-strain curves of the alloys, from 
which we can clearly see that the Ta-10 W alloy itself was plastic, and its 
maximum compressive strength and yield strength were about 
1000 MPa and 600 MPa, respectively. While with the addition of Hf 
elements, there were significant increase in compressive strength and 
yield strength. As improving the content of Hf elements, the compressive 
strength and yield strength were further strengthened without sacri -
ficing much plasticity. Among them, yield strength of Ta-10.17 W- 
19.83Hf alloy was as high as 1220 ± 8.4 MPa, it ’ s more than twice as 
high as Ta-10 W, the strengthening effect was quite significant. 
Fig. 7 showed the hardness of the experimental tests and the average 
hardness obtained by calculations, the hardness of Ta-10 W alloy was 
only 251.8 ± 6.2 HV. While with the doping of Hf elements, its hardness 
had been significantly improved and increased with the increased of the 
content of Hf elements, among which the hardness of Ta-10.17 W- 
19.83Hf was the highest, which was about 427.5 ± 5.3 HV, with a sig -
nificant strengthening effect. 
Combining the computationally predicted results with the experi -
mental results, it can be seen when Hf elements were doped in TaW 
alloys, the results of the experimental tests coincided with the compu -
tationally predicted influence law. 
4. Conclusions 
The influence of Hf addition on the mechanical performance of TaW 
alloys was investigated in this study. 
(1) Calculated results showed that the doping of Hf elements would 
lead to the formation of metallic and ionic bonds within the alloy 
system, and at the same time, the hardness of the alloys would be 
enhanced. It was predicted that Hf element doping could enhance 
the mechanical properties of Ta-W alloys. 
(2) The results of the compressive performance tests showed that 
alloying Hf improved the compressive strength and yield strength 
of TaW alloys and increased as the Hf content increased. Among 
them, yield strength of Ta-10.17 W-19.83Hf alloy was as high as 
1220 ± 8.4 MPa, it ’ s more than twice as high as Ta-10 W, the 
strengthening effect was quite significant. 
(3) The results of the hardness tests showed that alloying Hf 
improved the hardness of TaW alloys and that the hardness 
strengthens further as the percentage of Hf increases, which was 
consistent with the trend predicted by the calculations. Among 
them, the hardness of Ta-10.17 W-19.83Hf reached about 427.5 
± 5.3 HV.
