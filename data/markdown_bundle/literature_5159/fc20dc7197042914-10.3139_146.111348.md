Mechanical properties of the novel B2-type
binary Zr – Co alloys containing the B33 phase
In binary Zr – Co alloys, the B2 parent phase to B33 phase
ratio can be defined by defining the atomic percent ratio of
Zr to Co. The B2-type alloys Zr 48Co52,Z r 46Co54,Z r 44Co56
and Zr42Co58 were fabricated by rapid solidification techni-
ques, with the B33 phase being present at room tempera-
ture. The experimental results showed that the presence of
the B33 phase yielded higher strength, elongation and
toughness in the B2-type Z 50Co50 alloy. Consequently, the
appropriate combination of the B2 and B33 phases en-
hances both strength and ductility. This combination of
high strength and ductility of the novel B2-type binary
Zr – Co alloys containing the B33 phase means they may
be considered as novel engineering materials.
Keywords: Zr – Co alloys; Mechanical properties; Brittle-
ness and ductility; Intermetallics
1. Introduction
One of the basic criteria for the selection of engineering
materials is the combination of both high strength and in-
creased ductility. The materials that demonstrate higher/
lower strength but lower/higher elongation values cannot
be trusted as engineering supporting materials. However,
materials that combine both higher strength and ductility
can be used as engineering materials in a variety of applica-
tions, ensuring a high safety factor during utilization.
Recent studies have shown an increased interest in the
Zr – Co-based alloys, which can be attributed to their me-
chanical [1, 2], physical [3, 4], chemical and biological
properties [5]. A polycrystalline B2-type Zr 50Co50 alloy
was reported to reach elongation values as high as 7 % [6].
Also, the Zr 50Co50 alloy can be compressed into thin pel-
lets, at room temperature, without mechanical failure [7].
The B2-type Zr 50Co50 alloy cannot be used as an engineer-
ing material, as it demonstrates lower strength values.
Hence, it is critical to improve the strength and maintain
the good ductility, in the Zr – Co-based alloys, in order for
them to be able to be employed as engineering materials.
As reported by Tan et al. [7], the B33 phases of Zr 50Co50
and Zr 49Co49Al2 alloys can be demonstrated by measuring
the compressive stress. Also, the B2 to B33 phase transfor-
mation in binary Zr – Co alloy system can occur through
minor additions of Ni or Pd [8]. The strength and ductility
of the Zr – Co – Ni(Pd) alloys can be improved when the
secondary B33 phase is added into an alloy that primarily
consists of the B2 phase [8]. Thus, it is shown that the
strength of such alloys increases by the appropriate combi-
nation of the relatively ductile B2 and moderately brittle
B33 phases [8 – 10]. In the present study, novel B2-type
binary Zr 48Co52,Z r 46Co54,Z r 44Co56 and Zr 42Co58 alloys,
containing the B33 phase, were designed by adjusting the
ratio of Zr to Co. The strength and toughness values of the
resulting alloys were examined.
2. Experimental procedure
Various combinations of pure Zr and Co (purity of 99.5 %
or higher) were employed to constitute ingots of five
Zr – Co alloys, with nominal compositions (Zr 50Co50,
Zr48Co52,Z r 46Co54,Z r 44Co56 and Zr 42Co58) were prepared
in a high vacuum (3 · 10–3 Pa), magnetic controlled tung-
sten arc melting furnace, developed by Shenyang scientific
instrument of China, in an argon protective atmosphere.
All ingots were re-melted at least four times to achieve
chemical homogeneity and then suction cast into copper
molds to form rod-like shape samples, with diameters of 2,
3 and 4 mm. Using an operating voltage of 30 kV, the mi-
crostructure of the alloys was characterized by means of
X-ray diffraction (XRD) employing Cu-K a radiation. Ex-
amination of the samples was performed on the cross-sec-
tion of the cylindrical samples, which were 4.0 mm in dia-
meter. Considering that the samples usually have smaller
dimensions, and the usual testing standards, such as ASTM
or ISO standards, are not suitable for the current specimens,
a special test procedure is used to suit the size of the
samples. Also, one cylindrical sample with a diameter of
2.0 mm and a height of approximately 4.0 mm was pre-
pared from the as-cast rods and was subjected to uniaxial
compression testing. The compression testing was con-
ducted at room temperature, in a Shanghai CMT5105 elec-
tronic universal testing machine, with a strain rate of
2.5 · 10–4 s–1. Three measurements were taken for each
specimen and the results were averaged, as shown in Ta-
ble 1.

4. Conclusions
In binary Zr – Co alloys, the composition of the B2-type
Zr48Co52,Z r 46Co54,Z r 44Co56 and Zr 42Co58 alloys, which
contain the B33 phase, can be designed by adjusting the
atomic percent ratio of Zr to Co. The highest strength and
toughness values of the B2-type Zr – Co alloys, which con-
tain the B33 phase, were found at room temperature. The
optimum combination of both the B2 and B33 phases is a
critical factor that can determine enhanced strength and
ductility values. It is suggested that the Zr 46Co54 and
Zr44Co56 alloys, which demonstrate higher yield strength,
good plasticity and higher A values may be employed as
novel engineering materials.
