A new insight into the microstructural stability was proposed in Ni-based single crystal superalloys 
with Ru addition, and the element segregation behavior at γ/ γ′ interface was investigated by three- 
dimensional atom probe technology (3D-APT). After standard heat treatment, it was found that Ru addi- 
tion barely altered the element partitioning coeﬃcient between γ matrix and γ′ phase, and no element- 
segregation layer was observed at γ/ γ′ interface. During the heat exposure at 1100 °C, Ru addition ob- 
viously promoted the rafting of the γ′ precipitates and inhibited the precipitation of topological close- 
packed (TCP) phases. It was more important that an element-segregation layer containing Re, Co, and 
Cr was formed in the γ matrix close to the γ/ γ′ interface due to an “uphill diffusion” effect, and its 
concentration was obviously reduced after Ru addition. Finally, the microstructural stability based on the 
element segregation behavior at γ/ γ′ interface was discussed. This element-segregation layer increased 
the γ/ γ′ interfacial energy by increasing the absolute value of the lattice misﬁt of γ/ γ′ interface to 
promote the rafting of the γ′ precipitates after Ru addition. On the other hand, the decrease of the seg- 
regation concentration of Re, Co, and Cr elements as TCP phase-forming elements near the γ/ γ′ interface 
due to a “reverse partitioning” effect inhibits the precipitation of TCP phases in Ni-based single crystal 
superalloys after Ru addition.

1. Introduction 
Ni-based single crystal superalloys possess excellent high- 
temperature strength, fatigue resistance, and corrosion resistance, 
which are extensively applied in aero-engine turbine blades [1–4] . 
With the improving requirements for the temperature-bearing ca- 
pacity of turbine blades, the content of refractory elements such as 
Re, W, and Ru is gradually increased in advanced Ni-based single 
crystal superalloys [ 5 , 6 ]. Among these alloying elements, Ru ele- 
ment plays an important role in improving high-temperature mi- 
crostructural stability. 
It is well known that the coarsening and rafting of γ′ precipi- 
tates, as well as the precipitation of TCP phases seriously endanger 
the microstructural stability of Ni-based single crystal superalloys 
during high-temperature heat exposure. As for the coarsening and 
rafting of the γ′ precipitates, Han et al. [7] showed that the addi- 
tion of Ru element promoted the coarsening and rafting of the γ′

“reverse partitioning” effect by changing the partition behavior of 
other alloying elements between γ and γ ʹ phases, thus reducing 
the supersaturation of γ phases to inhibit the precipitation of TCP

tion did not alter the partition behavior of other alloying elements 
but reduced the nucleation rate of TCP phases. Furthermore, Peng 
et al. [13] proposed that the Ru addition hindered the nucleation 
of the TCP phase by increasing the TCP/ γ lattice misﬁt. It was clear 
that the mechanism of Ru addition in the microstructural stability 
of Ni-based single crystal superalloys still remained controversial.

behavior at γ/ γ′ interface and the microstructural stability of Ni-

regation behavior at γ/ γ′ interface, γ′ rafting behavior, and TCP 
phases precipitation behavior in Ni-based single crystal superalloys 
after Ru addition was deeply investigated, and a new mechanism 
about the microstructural stability based on the element segrega- 
tion behavior at γ/ γ′ interface might be proposed in Ni-based sin- 
gle crystal superalloys with Ru addition. 
2. Experimental procedures 
Two experimental superalloys were used in this work, and their 
nominal compositions were listed in Table 1 (abbreviated as 0Ru 
alloy and 3Ru alloy). Single crystal bars with orientation [001] 
were prepared by liquid metal cooling directional solidiﬁcation fur- 
nace. All the alloy rods used in this experiment deviated from 
[001] orientation within 10 °. The incipient melting points of the 
two experimental alloys were within the scope of 1325–1330 °C, 
due to the initial melting temperatures of the two alloys being 
very close, the same standard heat treatment process was adopted 
as follows: 1315 °C/16 h + 1325 °C/16 h/air cooling (AC); 1150 °C 
/4 h/AC; 870 °C/24 h/AC. The heat-exposure experiment was car- 
ried out at 1100 °C after standard heat treatment. 
The microstructures were further examined by a scanning elec- 
tron microscope (SEM) of ZEISS SUPRA. FEI Talos F200X transmis- 
sion electron microscopy (TEM) was used to study the precipita- 
tion behavior of TCP phases, and specimens were prepared by ion 
milling. The chemical composition of the TCP phase was identiﬁed 
by a Super-X energy dispersive X-ray spectrometry (EDS) detec- 
tor equipped with an FEI Talos F200X TEM. Specimens for 3D-APT 
analysis were thin bars of 0.5 mm × 0.5 mm × 20 mm, prepared 
by standard two-step electro-polishing procedures. 3D-APT analy- 
sis was performed in a LEAP 40 0 0 HR, under voltage pulsing, at a 
specimen temperature of 50 K, a pulse repetition rate of 200 kHz, 
and a pulse voltage fraction of 15%. The standing voltage on the 
needle specimen was varied automatically to maintain an evapora- 
tion rate of 5 ions every 10 0 0 pulses. The background vacuum level 
of the analysis chamber was kept at less than 10 –8 Pa. The detec- 
tion eﬃciency of the instrument was 36%. The reconstruction and 
quantitative analysis of 3D-APT data were performed using IVAS 
3.6.12 software.

4. Discussion

and Cr presented a typical uphill diffusion characteristic as shown 
in Fig. 5(b) , causing an element-segregation layer in γ matrix near

that the “uphill diffusion” occurred for the element in this region.

Moreover, Ni-based single crystal superalloys are subject to 
coarsening and rafting of γ′ precipitates during long-term heat ex- 
posure at high temperatures under unloaded conditions. This is a 
kind of microstructural instability that is affected by the co-lattice 
stress, diffusion, and surface energy generated by the lattice mis- 
ﬁt of the γ/ γ′ interface. The coherency stress of γ/ γ′ lattice misﬁt 
can act as the driving force for directional coarsening and rafting of 
γ′ precipitates at high temperatures, and the high lattice misﬁt of 
γ/ γ′ interface increases the driving force of γ′ precipitates rafting 
[27] . Combined with the results of Section 3.2 , during long-time 
heat exposure at 1100 °C, the addition of Ru elements increased 
the lattice misﬁt of the γ/ γ′ interface, thus increasing the inter- 
facial energy of the γ/ γ′ phases, which coincided with the calcu- 
lation results of the interfacial energy of two alloys in Section 4.1 . 
Ultimately, the addition of Ru elements achieved the effect of pro- 
moting the rafting of the γ′ precipitates. 
4.3. TCP phases precipitation 
To further explore the preferential nucleation site of the TCP 
phase, the precipitation behavior of the TCP phase in 0Ru alloy was 
further obtained by TEM, and the result was displayed in Fig. 10 . 
It was seen that some tiny TCP phases had been precipitated in 
the γ matrix after heat exposure at 1100 °C for only 2 h in 0Ru 
alloy as shown in Fig. 10(a) . It should be noted that the number 
of TCP phases near the γ/ γ′ interface was more than that in γ
matrix. Therefore, it could be inferred that the TCP phases were 
likely to preferentially be precipitated at γ/ γ′ interface near γ ma- 
trix. Furthermore, the composition of γ, γ′ , and TCP phases in 0Ru 
alloy after heat exposure at 1100 °C for 5 h was analyzed to ob- 
tain the constituent elements of the TCP phase more accurately. 
Fig. 10(b) showed the high-angle annular dark-ﬁeld (HAADF) image 
of a TCP phase in γ matrix and the corresponding EDS elemental 
distribution maps. It was clear that the TCP phase contained a large 
number of Re, Co, Cr, Mo, and W atoms, and most of them were 
just the segregation elements in the γ matrix near γ/ γ′ interfaces. 
Furthermore, the present work demonstrated that the Ru ad- 
dition inhibited the precipitation of TCP phases (see Fig. 4 ), and 
some TCP phase forming elements, such as Re, Co, Cr, and Mo, 
were also the segregation elements near the γ/ γ′ interface (see 
Fig. 10(b) ). Meanwhile, the Ru addition reduced the segregation 
degree of Re, Co, Cr, and Mo in γ matrix near the γ/ γ′ interface 
after heat exposure at 1100 °C for 100 h. As the result shown in 
Table 2 , the “reverse partitioning” effect caused by Ru addition 
led to the reduction of element segregation degree in γ matrix 
near the γ/ γ′ interface. Simultaneously, it was found that TCP 
phases were preferentially precipitated near the γ/ γ′ interface (see 
Fig. 10(a) ). Therefore, it could be deduced that the mechanism of 
Ru addition on the precipitation of TCP phases in Ni-based single 
crystal superalloys might be that the Ru addition reduced the seg- 
regation degree of TCP phases forming elements (such as Re, Co, 
Cr, Mo) in γ matrix near γ/ γ′ interface to reduce their precipita- 
tion rate, thus improving the microstructure stability of Ni-based 
single crystal superalloys. Finally, A schematic diagram of Ru addi- 
tion in inhibiting TCP phases precipitation process was shown in 
Fig. 11 . 
5. Conclusions 
In this work, the rafting behavior of γ′ precipitates and precip- 
itation behavior of TCP phases were systematically studied in Ni- 
based single crystal superalloys to propose a new insight into the 
mechanism of Ru addition on microstructural stability. The follow- 
ing conclusions could be obtained: 
(1) After standard heat treatment, Ru addition hardly changed par- 
titioning coeﬃcients between γ and γ′ phases of other alloy- 
ing elements, and no element-segregation layer was observed 
at the γ/ γ′ interface. 
(2) After heat exposure at 1100 °C for 100 h, the segregation of 
Re, Co, and Cr appeared near the γ/ γ ʹ interface due to an “up- 
hill diffusion” effect, which was going to decrease the interfacial 
free energy. It was noteworthy that the element concentration 
of this segregation layer was obviously reduced after Ru addi- 
tion. 
(3) Ru addition increased the γ/ γ′ interfacial energy by increasing 
the absolute value of the lattice misﬁt of γ/ γ′ interface, which 
further promoted the rafting of the γ′ precipitates. 
(4) Ru addition had a “reverse partitioning” effect on Re, Cr, and 
Cr, which reduced the segregation degree of Re, Co, Cr, and Mo 
at the γ/ γ ʹ interface, thus inhibiting the precipitation of TCP 
phases.
