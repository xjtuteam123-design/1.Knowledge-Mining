abstract
High-pressure torsion (HPT) processing currently receives much attention mainly because
it can signiﬁcantly improve the strength of alloys compare to other sever plastic defor-
mation. However, a common problem for these HPT processed alloys is that they suffer
from low plasticity. Here we proposed an optimized post-aging treatment that achieves
HPT processed 6016 alloy with tensile elongation as large as ~36% and ultimate tensile
strength over 500 MPa. Through systematically comparing the effects of high and low
temperature aging treatments on the microstructure and properties of alloys, it was found
that low-temperature long-time aging treatment can make HPT processed 6016 alloy
exhibit higher plasticity while maintaining higher strength. The higher strength mainly
originates from ﬁner grains and higher strengthening effect of L precipitates, while the
higher plasticity is attributed to the formation of nanoscale Si particles with smaller size
and distributed in the grain interior after low-temperature long-time aging treatment. In
addition, thermo-kinetic calculation demonstrates that the low-temperature long-time
aging treatment provides higher thermodynamic driving force, higher kinetic activation
energy and higher generalized stability for L phase precipitation, which is conducive to
achieve a good strength eplasticity combination of HPT processed alloys.

1. Introduction
Improving the strength and plasticity of alloys is the central
topics in material science and technology, which has been
investigated for many years. In recent years, severe plastic
deformation (SPD) processing receives much attention [ 1],
which is an effective way to modify the microstructures and
enhance the properties of alloys. More and more desired ﬁne
and hierarchical microstructure of alloys have been obtained
by equal channel angular pressing (ECAP) [ 2], cryorolling [ 3]
and high-pressure torsion (HPT) [ 4]. Due to the simultaneous
introduction of multiple strengthening mechanisms, such as
grain reﬁnement strengthening, dislocation strengthening,

strength of alloys can be increased signiﬁcantly through SPD
processing. Unfortunately, a lot of work which usually only
increases the strength of alloys, but disappointingly shows a
low plasticity [ 5e7], which seriously hampers their commer-
cial applications at ambient temperature.
To improve the plasticity of SPD processed alloys, huge
research has recently been carried out. Based on the fact that
inter-pass annealing can improve deformation ability of
AleMg alloy with a high Mg concentration, Min Zha et al. [ 8]
achieved a high plasticity in SPD deformed Al e7Mg alloy by
utilizing a combination of ECAP and inter-pass annealing.
They pointed out that the high plasticity was attributed to an
enhanced work hardening originated from the pronounced
dynamic strain aging effect and a bimodal grain structure.
Ting Yuan et al. [9] proposed a warm multi-pass ECAP process,
and high deformation temperature led to a relatively low
dislocation density and high fraction of high-angle grain
boundaries which was also beneﬁcial to improve the work
hardening rate, thereby obtaining an excellent plasticity in
ECAPed Al-5.5Mg-1.6Li-0.1Zr alloy. Engineering of grain-
boundary composition is a new strategy to enhance the
plasticity of SPD processed alloy. K. Edalati et al. showed that
room-temperature superplasticity in HPT processed Al eZn
alloy was realized by engineering the Zn segregation along
the Al/Al boundaries [ 10]. Another more commonly used
approach is applying post-aging treatments to SPD deformed
alloys, which is also one of the most promising to be used in
industry. By weighing the softening caused by grain coars-
ening and dislocation annihilation, and the hardening caused
by phase precipitation during post-aging treatments, the post-
aging treatments can be roughly divided into two categories:
high-temperature short-time (HS) aging treatment and low-
temperature long-time (LL) ageing treatment. For instance,
the plasticity of cryorolled 2024 alloy [ 11] was signiﬁcantly
improved by the LL aging treatments (aging at 100
/C14 C for 100 h),
which was mainly due to the dramatic decrease in dislocation
density and the precipitation of a high density of nano-sized
S
0-phase precipitates during aging, both of which can in-
crease the work-hardening rate, thereby improving the plas-
ticity. On the other hand, the HS aging treatment (aging at
200
/C14 C for 3 min) was also applied to cryorolled Cu [ 12], which
promoted the formation of inhomogeneous microstructure.
The strain hardening mechanism induced by the inhomoge-
neous microstructure was conducive to stabilize the tensile
deformation and successfully improved the plasticity of cry-
orolled Cu. In order to simultaneously improve the strength
and plasticity of equal-channel angular extrusion deformed
6060 Al alloy, M. Hockauf et al. [ 13] performed a similar HS
aging treatment (ageing at 170
/C14 C for 18 min). They revealed
that the decrease of both grain size and defect density during
HS aging treatment was responsible for the high plasticity in
extruded 6060 Al alloy.
Despite many successful cases in improving the plasticity of
SPD processed alloys by the HS or LL aging treatment, there are
few published studies that provide a direct comparison of the
effects of HS and LL aging treatments on the plasticity of SPD
processed alloys at the same time. In the present work, the
heat-treatable 6016 Al alloy, which is widely adopted as struc-
tural material in auto-mobile industry, is taken as an example.
Among the above-mentioned SPD techniques, HPT is chosen
speciﬁcally, because most studies show a poor plasticity of HPT
processed alloys. A systematic investigation into the effect of
HS and LL aging treatments on the microstructure and prop-
erties of HPT processed 6016 alloy is performed. Special atten-
tion is given to the investigation of precipitation phenomena in
HPT processed samples under two aging treatments.
2. Materials and experimental procedures
2.1. Material processing
The 5 mm thick hot-rolled plates of 6016 alloy, with the
chemical composition (in wt %) 0.4 Mg-1.0 Si-0.2 Fe-0.1 Cu-0.15
Ti-balanced Al were used in this work. These hot rolled plates
were cut into disk samples with a diameter of 10 mm and a
thickness of 1.5 mm by wire cutting. The disk samples were
solution heat treated at 540
/C14 C for 1 h, water-quenched to
room temperature, and then HPT deformation was performed
immediately. The HPT anvils are machined from high
strength tool steel with a diameter of 6 cm and a height of
5 cm. The semi constrained cavity at the top of the anvil has a
depth of 4 mm and a diameter of 10 mm. Under a hydraulic
pressure of 6 GPa, disc samples were continuously twisted for
5 revolutions at a ram rotation speed of 1 revolution per
minute. According to the study of K. Edalati [ 14], under the
high imposed pressure of 6 GPa, there is very little slippage
between the 6016 alloy and the anvil during HPT processing.
The temperature rises of Al alloy samples during HPT pro-
cessing are not signiﬁcant, as proved by ﬁnite element simu-
lations and experimental measurements [ 15,16]. Then, the LL
and HS aging treatments were designed and conducted to
improve the plasticity of HPT processed samples.
2.2. Properties testing
Microhardness measurements were performed in a SHI-
MADZU HMV-2 microhardness tester using a load of 9.807 N
and a holding time of 15 s. The average hardness value was
determined from 8 points of each condition. It is important to
note that the Vickers microhardness measurement and
microstructure observation were performed in an annular
area 1.5 e2.5 mm from the center point of the disk specimen,
since the heterogeneity of microstructure in different regions
of disk specimen. The dog-bone shaped tensile specimens
have a gauge length of 2 mm, a width of 1 mm and a thickness
of 1 mm, as shown in Fig. 1. The center point of gauge region of
tensile specimen is about 1.76 mm from the center point of the
disk specimen. Considering that the use of miniature speci-
mens may result in higher test results of elongation to failure
[17], both deformed and undeformed alloys were tested using
small-sized tensile specimen in order to make reliable com-
parisons. Tensile tests were carried out in an Instron 5967
universal tensile test machine at an initial strain rate of
5 /C2 10
/C0 4s/C0 1. The video extensometer adopted in this study is
based on digital image correlation (DIC) technique, which can
accurately measure the total strain of small-sized tensile
specimens [18,19]. At least four tensile specimens were tested
for each condition to ensure the reliability of the test results.

2.4. Thermodynamic calculations
To elucidate the observed precipitation phase in the current

3. Results
3.1. Mechanical behavior
Firstly, comparison of representative tensile stress estrain
curves between HPT processed and undeformed 6016 sam-
ples are shown in Fig. 1 , and corresponding tensile property
date are listed in Table 1 . HPT deformation signiﬁcantly im-
proves the strength of 6016 alloy, and its ultimate tensile
strength (UTS) is close to 600 MPa, which is comparable to that
of 7xx high-strength aluminum alloys. However, compared
with the undeformed samples, the total elongation to failure
(ETF) of HPT processed samples is very low, and only about 3%,
which cannot meet the requirements for structural engi-
neering applications.
In order to improve the plasticity of HPT processed 6016
alloy, subsequent LL and HS aging treatments were designed
and applied. At ﬁrst, 70
/C14 C and 180 /C14 C were selected as the
aging temperatures of LL and HS, respectively. To further
determine the aging time, the hardness curves of HPT pro-
cessed alloy with time under two aging temperatures were
obtained through hardness test, as shown in Fig. 2 , with the
error bars indicating the deviations of individual measure-
ments. The result indicates that no matter aging at low-
temperature (70
/C14 C) or high-temperature (180 /C14 C), the
hardness-time curves of HPT processed samples exhibit a
quick softening at the initial stage. This is because the pre-
cipitation hardening was overcompensated by the signiﬁcant
decrease of dislocation density and grain coarsening upon
aging [23]. With more details, for the low-temperature aging at
70
/C14 C, the hardness of HPT processed samples can still be
maintained at 135 HV even when the aging time lasted up to
490 h ( Fig. 2 a); whereas, for the high-temperature aging at
180
/C14 C, the hardness of HPT processed samples rapidly
decreased to 125 HV after a short time aging, only about
10 min, and then decreased slowly with the aging proceeding
(Fig. 2b). Based on the above hardness test results, the LL aging
treatment parameters were ﬁnally determined as: aging at
70
/C14 C for 490 h, and the HS aging treatment parameters were:
aging at 180 /C14 C for 10 min.
Finally, tensile tests were carried out on HPT processed
samples after LL aging treatment (designated as HPT þ LL) and
HS aging treatment (designated as HPT þ HS), respectively.
The engineering stress-strain curves of these two aged sam-
ples are compared in Fig. 3 , and corresponding tensile prop-
erty date are listed in Table 1. Obviously, the plasticity of HPT

Furthermore, the relatively good plasticity in two aged
samples can also be reﬂected by the fracture surface of tensile

cleavage plane with river pattern presents on the fracture

compared the dimple morphology of these two aged samples,
the sample HPT þ LL shows larger and deeper dimples than
that of sample HPT þ HS, which is indicative of a higher
plasticity of alloy [ 27].
To sum up, these results indicate that the plasticity of HPT
processed 6016 alloy can be improved by subsequent LL and
HS aging treatments. More importantly, compared with the
HS aging treatment, LL aging treatment is more effective in
improving the plasticity of HPT processed alloys, while
maintaining relatively high strength.
3.2. Microstructure
3.2.1. Grain size
Fig. 6 displays representative morphologies of grains and
histograms of grain size distribution in HPT processed and
two aged samples. It is evident that the average grain size of
HPT processed 6016 alloy is close to the nanocrystalline scale,
and 40% of the grains have a diameter of less than 100 nm (see
Fig. 6a and d). After LL and HS aging treatments, the grain size
is slightly coarsened, as observed in Fig. 6 b and c. In com-
parison, sample HPT þ HS exhibits slightly coarser grains
ranging from 100 to 450 nm, with an average grain size of
225 nm; whereas the coarsening degree of grain in sample
HPT þ LL is lower, and the size distribution ranges from
100 nm to 350 nm, with an average grain size of 197 nm.

60 MPa higher than that of sample HPTþ HS (see Table 1). Thus,

matrix are different. As shown in Fig. 16 a and b, small-sized

large-sized particles tend to be distributed at the grain

which is an important reason for its poor plasticity. In addi-

with lager-sized particles, small-sized nanoscale particles are
prone to serve as dislocation sources at high stress, leading to
a sustainable deformation mechanism for improving plas-
ticity, which may be another factor for the higher plasticity in
sample HPT þ LL.

5. Conclusions
In this work, the HPT processed 6016 alloy possesses an ultra-
high strength (~600 MPa), but a low elongation of less than 3%,
whereas the HS and LL aging treatments successfully
improved its plasticity without sacriﬁcing much of the
strength. The corresponding mechanical properties and
microstructural features under two aging treatments were
systematically characterized and compared. The strength-
ening mechanisms and factors affecting plasticity of aged al-
loys were also analyzed, and the principal conclusions are
summarized as follows.
(1) Compared with sample HPT þ HS, sample HPT þ LL
exhibits higher strength (YS: 458 MPa, UTS: 523 MPa)
and higher plasticity, and its total elongation to failure
(~36%) is even higher than that of undeformed sample
in T6 temper.
