Research on the thermal stability of a near α titanium alloy before and
after laser shock peening
Weiju Jia , Hengzhang Zhao, Quan Hong, Lei Li, Xiaonan Mao
Northwesten Institute for Nonferrous Metal Research, Xi'an 710016, China
abstractarticle info

The room-temperature tensile properties and thermal stability of Ti834 alloy before and after laser shock peening
(LSP) were investigated. Micro-hardness measurement of the untreated and LSP treated specimens were carried
out by using a Vickers indenter. The characteristic of microstructures were analyzed by TEM. The results show
that both of the strength and ductility decrease for the 600 °C/100 h exposed Ti834 alloy, especially the decrease
of ductility is more pronounced than that of strength. The strength and micro-hardness of Ti834 alloy can be
improved by LSP, which is attributed to the high-density dislocation and dislocation-cell formation in the near-
surface of the specimen. LSP treatment has a detrimental effect on the thermal stability of Ti834 alloy because
of the formation of high density defects in the surface of the alloy.

Microstructures1. Introduction
Titanium alloys are widely used in the aerospace applications be-
cause of their light weight, high strength and excellent intermediate
temperature performance [1,2]. However, the airplane components,
such as the compressor blades and discs, always suffer the fatigue load-
ing during ﬂight, and the maximal tensile stress always locates at the
surface of the components, which will lead to the initiation of the fatigue
crack and early failure. In order to solve this problem, many mechanical
surface treatments, such as deep rolling, shot peening and laser shock
peening have been studied widely in the metallic materials [3–5].
Laser shock peening (LSP) is a novel surface treatment technique
which has already been employed successfully to several metal
materials. Compared with other treatments, LSP treatment is capable
of introducing deeper compressive residual stress and cold work in
the near-surface layers without compromising the surface roughness
[6,7]. Therefore, many studies have been undertaken to understand
the mechanisms that controlling wave propagation and their effect on
the mechanical properties of the alloy. Fang et al. [8] found that the
shocked effect was improved obviously by comparing the stress
nephrograms in shocked area along the direction of blade basin and
depth. The maximum value of equivalent stress was 256 MPa, and the
maximum compressive residual stress was not in the center of laser
spot size but at a distance of about 1.1 mm from the center along x
axis, and the maximum value of shock depth was 0.2 mm. Ruschau
et al. [9] found the fatigue performance of titanium alloy processed by
LSP can be improved. Zhang et al. [10] also found that the high micro-
hardness and compressive residual stress were introduced at the
surface of Ti–6Al–4V alloy after LSP, and the fatigue life increased to a
maximum and then decreased with increasing the number of over-
lapped laser spots. King et al. [11] investigated the effect of LSP on the
fatigue strength and damage tolerance of the root of the blade
manufactured by Ti–6Al–4V alloy. Results showed that LSP can cause a
local plastic deformation layer which can reach a depth of 2.0 mm.
Nalla et al. [6] also compared the fatigue improvement techniques by
cold rolling versus LSP. Through these efforts, LSP was proven as a viable
alternative to conventional shot peening for inducing deep compressive
residual stress in the subsurface of a material. As a result, the fatigue and
wear properties of the alloy can be improved largely. In addition, the
thermal stability of the alloy after exposure is another important prop-
erty for the titanium alloys. Thomas et al. [4] investigated the effect of
shot peening on the microstructure and properties of a near-alpha
titanium alloy following high temperature exposure. The results suggest
that shot peening may be deleterious to the mechanical integrity of
titanium alloy components that are subject to high temperature expo-
sure in oxidizing environments. The research ﬁndings have directive
function for the development and application of shot peening. However,
there is little research work related to the in ﬂuence of laser shock
peening on the thermal stability of the materials, especially for the
high-temperature titanium alloys.
The aim of this paper was to investigate the effect of the LSP impacts
on the thermal stability of Ti834 near- α titanium alloy. The room-
temperature tensile properties of the alloy were investigated before
and after LSP. Micro-hardness measurements of the untreated and LSP

2. Experimental
The experimental material used in the present work was produced
by the Northwest Institute for Nonferrous Metal Research. After multi-
ple forging, a bar with a diameter of 150 mm was obtained. The initial
microstructure consisted of approximately 30% equiaxedα phase with-
in a ﬁne transformedβ matrix, as shown inFig. 1. The specimens for the
tensile and thermal stability testing were machined from the bar with a
gauge length of 25 mm and a diameter of 5 mm. The specimens for ther-
mal stability testing were divided into two groups. One group was
exposed at 600 °C in laboratory air for 100 h directly, and the other
group was LSP treated ﬁrstly and then exposed under the same condi-
tion. The results of tensile properties were the mean values obtained
from three tested specimens. Prior to the LSP treatment, all the surface
of specimens were polished with SiC paper with different grades of
roughness, followed by cleaning in deionized water. Ultrasound in
acetone was used to degrease the specimen surfaces.
The LSP experiments were performed using a YAG laser operating at
a repetition frequency of 0.5 Hz with a wave length of 1064 nm and
pulse duration of 20 ns. The spot diameter was 2.6 mm and the power
density of laser was around 5GW/cm
2. For all specimens, the overlap-
ping rate used in LSP was kept 50% between two adjacent spots. The
water with a thickness of 2 mm was used as the transparent con ﬁning
layer and the black tape with a thickness of 0.1 mm was used as the
absorbing layer to protect the specimen surface from thermal ablation.
Micro-hardness measurements of the specimens were carried out by
using a Vickers indenter with 200 g load and 30s hold time. TEM speci-
mens with a thickness of 0.3 mm were prepared by cutting in the treat-
ed specimens and mechanically thinned to 40–50 μm in thickness. After
jet polishing by a double-jet polisher at − 30 °C, specimens were ob-
served in HITACHI H-800 transmission electron microscope at 175 kV.
3. Results and discussion
3.1. Thermal stability
The room-temperature (R.T.) tensile properties and thermal stability
of Ti834 alloy before and after LSP are listed in Table 1. Compared with
the R.T. tensile properties, both of the strength and ductility decrease
after 600 °C thermal exposure, especially the decrease of ductility is
more pronounced than that of strength. Although the decrease of
strength is slightly after exposure, all the three tested values of strength
were lower than that of the initial specimens. Similar trend were
observed in Ti60 alloy [12]. This phenomenon may be attributed to
the surface contamination during the exposure. Donlon et al. [13] also
found that the ductility of α + β processed IMI834 alloy was constant
in the range of 6 to 8%. It is suggested that the thermal exposure of
Ti834 alloy is a strengthening process which maybe include the changes
of the internal microstructures and the surface condition of the speci-
mens during the high-temperature exposure. For the LSP specimen,
the R.T. strength increases but the ductility has nearly no change
compared with the initial specimens. However, after 600 °C exposure,
the strength increases and the ductility decreases further. The results
indicate that the LSP treatment has a detrimental effect on the thermal
stability of the alloy.

Generally, there are three possible mechanisms of hardening according
to the previous reports. The ﬁrst is mainly ascribed to the increase
in the dislocation density [15]. The second mechanism is the
nanocrystallization or grain reﬁnement during the severe plastic defor-
mation [16,17]. The last one is that the shock waves can induce new
phase formation in the surface of the materials which results in the
hardening [18]. The hardening mechanism of Ti834 alloy in this work
will be explained further at a later section with microstructural
evidence.

The TEM images of Ti834 alloy with LSP are shown in Fig. 4 .
Compared with the specimen without LSP, highly tangled dislocation
arrangements and dislocation-cell structures can be observed in the
lamella α and equiaxed α phase. No nanostructure was observed
in the near surface of the alloy. Similar results were obtained by
Altenberger in the AISI304 [21]. However, Zhou et al. [22] observed
the nanostructure in TC6 titanium alloy after ﬁve laser shock impacts.
It maybe indicate that the laser power or the plastic deformation were
not enough to induce the formation of the nanocrystallization in the
present work. The XRD patterns of Ti834 alloy before and after LSP is
shown in Fig. 5. It can be seen that the spectral peak of X-ray diffraction
and its position nearly no change after LSP treatment, which indicates
that no new phase was generated in the surface of the alloy. Therefore,
it can be concluded that the increment in hardness of Ti834 alloy is
caused by the increasing dislocation density and the formation of
dislocation-cell during the LSP.
Fig. 6 shows the near-surface TEM images of laser shock peened
Ti834 alloy after thermal exposure at 600 °C for 100 h. Compared with
the LSP specimens, it is clear that the dislocation density decreases
largely, and amount of dislocation-cell can be observed in the α phase,
as shown in Fig. 6a. Dislocations are prior to pile-up at the α/β bound-
aries. Meanwhile, superlattice diffraction indicates that the α

has also precipitated from the alloy, as shown in Fig. 6 b. It is well
known that titanium alloys readily absorbs oxygen when exposed to at-
mospheric conditions at high temperatures, resulting in the formation
of the α-case near the surface of the alloy. Several investigations have
shown that this brittle α-case can decrease the ductility of the alloy
[23,24].L e y e n se ta l .[25]investigated the inﬂuences of long-term expo-
sure on tensile properties and fracture of Ti-1100 alloy. They found that
the ultimate tensile stress of the alloy increases slightly at least up to
100 h and decreases considerably after 1000 h of exposure. The thick-
ness of α-case of the specimens with lamellar microstructure is smaller
compared to the bimodal microstructure. The results indicate that the
oxygen diffusion is faster in bimodal microstructures than in lamellar
microstructure because higher portions of diffusion paths can be offered
in the former. Thomas[4] tested the oxygen content of Ti-834 alloy with
different surface conditions after high temperature exposure. The re-
sults showed that shot peening leads to an increase in the measured
levels of subsurface oxygen when compared with the wire electro-
discharge machined (EDM) condition, and increasing the shot peening
coverage to 1200% further promotes the oxygen ingress of the alloy
during thermal exposure. It is clear that the diffusion rate of oxygen ac-
celerated within the heavily shot peened surface. The authors pointed
out that the increased sub-surface oxygen content in shot-peened
material can be attributed to rapid diffusion along the mechanical
twin boundaries and dislocation slip bands within the plastically
deformed layer. For the laser shock peened Ti834 alloy, defects such as
the dislocations were formed near the surface of specimens during
the plastic deformation. These defects can serve as the passage way
for the diffusion of oxygen during the exposure, thus more α-case

4. Conclusion
The thermal stability of Ti834 alloy before and after LSP was investi-
gated. Micro-hardness of the untreated and LSP treated specimens were
measured, and the characteristic of microstructures were analyzed by
TEM. The obtained results are as follows:
(1) Both of the strength and ductility decrease for Ti834 alloy after
600 °C/100 h thermal exposure, especially the decrease of ductil-
ity is more pronounced than that of strength. The formation ofα
2
phase in the Ti834 alloy is the main reason that leads to the
decreases of ductility after thermal exposure.
(2) The strength of Ti834 alloy increases after laser shock peening
which is attributed to the high-density dislocation and
dislocation-cell formation in the near-surface of the specimen.
(3) LSP treatment has a detrimental effect on the thermal stability of
Ti834 alloy because of the formation of high density defects on
the surface of the alloy.
