abstract
Discontinuous yielding in Ni-base superalloy during high-speed compression has been investigated.
Flow stress curves of GH4049 can be divided into two types: the ﬁrst type curves display common ﬂow
behavior revealing work hardening, stable, softening and steady stages; the second type curves present
abnormal ﬂow behavior revealing discontinuous yielding feature, which were characterized by a sharp
peak stress (
σP), obvious upper yield point ( σU) and a lower yield point. Apparent activation energies
for peak values and upper values were calculated to be QP ¼1 162 kJ mol/C0 1 and QU ¼1 106 kJ mol/C0 1,
respectively. Constitutive equations represent peak stress and upper stress as functions of strain rate and
deformation temperature are described. First type curves present common work hardening behavior;
however, second type curves present spiral hardening behavior since discontinuous softening during
high-speed deformation. When GH4049 superalloys present ﬁrst type ﬂow behavior, volume fraction of
dynamic recrystallization ( X
DRX) can be described in terms of normal S-curves revealing slow –rapid–
slow property. However, when alloys present second type ﬂow behavior, XDRX can be described in terms
of double S-curves exhibiting sudden –steady–rapid–slow property.

1. Introduction
Nickel-base superalloys are of great interest to aerospace
industries for high temperature structural applications [1,2], such
as turbine engine components, because of their unusual ability to
retain excellent combinations of mechanical properties and corro-
sion resistance at high temperatures comparing with other mate-
rials [3,4]. Nickel-base superalloys mainly consist of γ0 precipitate
(L12) and γ matrix (fcc) two-phase structure, which is important
for them to keep excellent high temperature properties [5,6].
GH4049 is a γ0 phase strengthened wrought nickel-base superalloy
with good oxidation resistance and strength at elevated tempera-
tures [7]. This superalloy is currently used for rotor vanes, turbine
blades and other high temperature load bearing components
servicing at temperatures below 900 1C [8].
Turbine components operate in high temperature and high
pressure gas surroundings. Their working reliability largely affects
ﬂight security. With the raising of thrust –weight ratio and gas
temperature before turbine, creep, fatigue and creep –fatigue have
become three main failure modes for hot section components
in aeroengine [9]. To meet present requirements of favorable
mechanical properties, better control of forging processes for
GH4049 is rather important. However, GH4049 superalloy is
rather difﬁcult to deform because of its poor workability especially
when strain rate is above 6 s /C0 1. For GH4049, the available
processing data are very limited. These are far from the need for
the optimization of processing parameters.
In this work, isothermal compression tests were conducted on
GH4049 superalloys with strain rates ranging 0.1 –50 s /C0 1. Consti-
tutive model associating discontinuous softening feature has been
proposed. Microstructure observation for hardening and softening
mechanisms has been investigated. Discontinuous yielding in
GH4049 superalloy during high-speed compression has also been
investigated. All these studies are foundations for application of
GH4049 superalloys.

3. Experimental results and discussion
3.1. Abnormal ﬂow behavior at high strain rates of 10 and 50 s /C0 1
Flow curves obtained under various compression conditions
are shown in Fig. 1 . It can be found that ﬂow stress increases
signiﬁcantly with increasing strain rate ( _ε) and decreasing tem-
perature ( Td). It is also demonstrated the sensitivity of ﬂow stress
on the variation of strain rate and temperature. Summarized, ﬂow
curves of GH4049 superalloys can be divided into two types. The
ﬁrst type curves display a severe work hardening to a single stress
peak (σP), and a gradually decreased ﬂow stress. As shown in Fig. 1,
red curves show four different stage, viz., work hardening stage,
stable stage, softening stage and steady stage. These ﬂow curves
indicate that the softening mechanism, such as DRX, is suf ﬁcient to
cancel out the work hardening [10]. For further analysis, micro-
structure observation should be conducted.
The second type curves are characterized by a sharp peak stress
at a lower strain with an obvious upper yield point and a lower
yield point, which is usually called discontinuous softening. Pre-
vious research reported that discontinuous softening always
occurred at higher temperatures during hot processing with high
speed strain rates [1 1–14]. In this work, discontinuous softening is
observed for GH4049 superalloys processing with high strain rates
of 10 and 50 s /C0 1 at all deformation temperatures except 1060 1C.
As blue curves shown in Fig. 1 , four stages are exhibited for
discontinuous softening curves. Firstly, the ﬂow stress increases
severely to a peak value ( σP). And then, a sharp drop of ﬂow stress
occurs until reaching the lower value ( σL). After the lower stress,
the ﬂow stress increases slowly to an upper value ( σU), and then
followed by a continuous decrease. The severe increase of ﬂow
stress is due to the rapid proliferation of movable dislocations. The
critical density of dislocations is obtained in local deformed region,
and local dynamic recrystallization results in the sharp drop of
ﬂow stress. The slow increase of ﬂow stress is mainly associated
with the interaction of strain hardening and softening mechanism.
As the strain increases, the second peak value, named upper value
(σU), is reached, and the softening mechanism plays a dominant
role. The microstructure observations indicate that the softening
mechanisms for GH4049 superalloys present discontinuous soft-
ening are considered as discontinuous dynamic recrystallization
(DRX). Peak value ( σP), upper value ( σU) and lower value ( σL) can
also be seen in Fig. 2 , which also gives processing conditions
associated with discontinuous DRX. Based on ﬂow behavior
analysis and microstructure observation, the second ﬂow behavior
associated with discontinuous DRX occurred at deformation tem-
peratures of 1090 –118 01C with high strain rates of 10 –50 s /C0 1.

3.3. Discontinuous softening mechanism and discontinuous dynamic
recrystallization
Typical hardening rate ( θ¼dσ/dε) versus stress ( σ) is plotted on
the basis of second type ﬂow curves, which is shown in Fig. 4 .A s
discontinuous softening during high-speed deformation, the hard-
ening rates can be described in terms of spiral curves. Five stages
are discernible from Fig. 4 b. In the ﬁrst stage, the hardening rate
drops until reaching an in ﬂection point of the θ/C0 σ curve. In the
second stage, the hardening decreases slowly with a lower slop
towards the peak stress ( σP), at which θ¼0. In the third stage, the
hardening begins from peak stress ( σP) to the lower stress ( σL,
which corresponds to θ¼0), and the value of θ changes from
positive to negative owing to 1st DRX softening. The stress for the
maximum softening rate can also be determined in this stage
when θ reaches to the negative peak. Similarly, in the fourth stage,
the working hardening begins from the lower stress ( σL) to the
upper stress ( σU, which corresponds to θ¼0), and the value of θ
changes from negative to positive owing to 2nd hardening. In the
ﬁfth stage, the working hardening begins from upper stress ( σU)t o
the steady-state stress ( σU, which corresponds to θ¼0), and the
value of θ changes from positive to negative again owing to
2nd DRX.
The positive θ value is associated with work hardening. And
the negative θ value is associated with softening mechanism
induced by DRX and DRV. Sometimes, DRV could be disregarded
since severe dislocation motion leads to DRX nucleation during
high-speed deformation. By applying the hardening rate analysis
method, the θ/C0 σ curves under different conditions associated
with DRX softening mechanism are plotted, as shown in Fig. 5 ,
which obviously reveals that the ﬁrst ﬂow behavior presents
common hardening (red curves) and the second ﬂow behavior
presents spiral hardening (blue curves).
Typical microstructures of GH4049 superalloys compressed at
115 01C/10 s/C0 1 and 1 1501C/50 s /C0 1 with slight height reduction of
10% are shown in Fig. 6 , which revealing local DRX. In the ﬁgures,
the imaginary lines highlight non-recrystallized grains. From
microstructure observation, it can be found that local DRX is
intermediate microstructure. During hot compression, dislocation
stimulates the occurrence of DRX at original grain boundaries.
When original grains are entirely covered with recrystallized
grains, 1st local DRX is formed. Then dislocations stimulate next
DRX. The volume fraction of DRX increases with increasing true
strain.
A series of ﬂow curves of GH4049 superalloy compressed at
115 01C/10 s/C0 1 with true strains of 0.36, 0.60 and 0.92 are shown
in Fig. 7. The ﬁgure reveals an identical ﬂow behavior. The effect of
true strain on DRX microstructure is explained in the following. On
the basis of microstructure observation, it is found that the DRX
preferably occurs at original grain boundaries and develops
inwards. A local DRX microstructure is obtained upon the iso-
thermal compression with the strain of 0.36. With an increase of
strain, the fraction of the DRX grains is increased as well. The
global DRX occurs when true strain reaches 0.60.

4. Conclusions
GH4049 is generally regarded as one of the most important
Nickel-base superalloys; however, this alloy is rather dif ﬁcult to
deform because of its poor workability especially when strain
rate is above 6 s /C0 1. In order to optimize forging process, iso-
thermal compression tests were conducted with strain rates
ranging 0.1 –50 s /C0 1. The following conclusions are drawn from
this investigation.
