Abstract 
Microstructure and yield strength at room temperature and 300°C in Al-5wt%Y 
2O3 
and Al-5wt%La 2O3 fabricated by mechanical alloying (MA) have been investigated by 
transmission electron microscopy and compression test. Al-5wt%Y 2O3 and

## Page 3



Al-5wt%La 2O3 were fabricated by MA with powders Al, Y 2O3 and La 2O3. The alloy 
powders were consolidated by hot pressing at 550°C under 400MPa. Yield strengths 
in the consolidated Al-5wt%La 
2O3 at room temperature and 300°C were higher than 
those in the consolidated Al-5wt%Y 2O3. Vickers microhardness at room temperature 
in Al-5wt%La 2O3 after the consolidation increased as compared with that before the 
consolidation. In contrast, Vickers microhardness at room temperature in 
Al-5wt%Y 
2O3 after the consolidation was almost the same as that before the 
consolidation. X-ray diffraction analyses revealed that in Al-5wt%La 2O3, an 
intermetallic compound Al 11 La 3 was formed during the consolidation. Al 11 La 3 was 
finely dispersed in the matrix after the consolidation. In Al-5wt%Y 2O3, however, no 
intermetallic compound was formed after the consolidation. It is noted that higher 
yield strength in Al-5wt%La 
2O3 than Al-5wt%Y 2O3 at room temperature and 300°C is 
derived from grain boundary strengthening and dispersion strengthening due to pinning 
of Al grain growth and retarding of dislocation motion by fine numerous dispersoids. 
The key factor is the large number density of dispersoids in Al-5wt%La 
2O3, which is 
due to the in-situ formation of intermetallic precipitates Al 11 La 3 during the 
consolidation.

Introduction 
Aluminum alloys are widely used as aerospace materials, automotive materials, 
and so on due to low density and high strength [1-3]. For high temperature application, 
relevant properties at elevated temperature are widely investigated [4-6]. Most 
aluminum alloys decrease their strength above approximately 300ºC [7-9]. In order to 
improve high temperature strength, dispersion and/or precipitation strengthening are 
known to be effective [10,11]. It is desirable that dispersoids are dispersed finely in a 
matrix. Mechanical alloying (MA) is effective for that purpose [12,13]. For 
improving high temperature properties, rare earth elements are often utilized [14-16]. 
But pure rare earth elements are so costly. In the present study, rare earth oxides were 
used because oxides are much less expensive than pure elements. In the present study, 
Al-Y
2O3 and Al-La 2O3 were fabricated by MA, and their mechanical properties at room 
and elevated temperatures were evaluated.

Experimental procedure 
 Powders of Al, La 
2O3 and Y 2O3 were used as raw materials. The powders were 
weighed to be of the composition of Al-5wt%La 2O3 and Al-5wt%Y 2O3 in a glove box 
filled with Ar gas. Al-5wt%La 2O3 and Al-5wt%Y 2O3 are hereafter named as 
Al-5La 2O3 and Al-5Y 2O3, respectively. The weighed powders were set into a 50ml jar 
with 10mm balls in diameter. The mass ratio of ball and powder were 10:1. The jar 
and balls were made of SUS304. These powders were subjected to MA with 2wt% 
stearic acid as a process control agent. The ball mill used were a planetary ball mill 
named Super Labo mill Model PM-100 (CMT co., ltd.). MA was conducted for 100 
hours at revolution and rotation speed of 300 and 700 rpm, respectively. After MA, 
the powders were consolidated by hot pressing in a SUS304 stainless steel die at 550ºC 
under a uniaxial load of 400MPa for 1 hour in air. The as-hot pressed sample was a 
cylinder, whose diameter is 4 mm and height is 15 mm. 
 Constituent phases were analyzed by X-ray diffraction (XRD) at a sca nning speed 
of 0.1deg/min with CuKα 1 radiation generated at 30kV at 15mA. Microstructure was 
observed by transmission electron microscope (JEM-2010, JEOL) operated at 200kV . 
Elemental analysis was carried out by energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) 
equipped with TEM. TEM specimen was made by twin-jet electropolishing

(Tenupol-3, Struers) or focused ion beam (JEM-9310FIB, JEOL). In the case of 
twin-jet electropolishing, electrolyte used was 20% perchloric acid and 80% ethanol. 
The twin-jet polishing was performed at the voltage of 30V . As for FIB, TEM 
specimens were picked up from the alloy ingot and micromanipulated onto a 
carbon-coated copper mesh TEM grid. 
 Mechanical properties were evaluated by Vickers microhardness (MVK-E, Akashi) 
at room temperature, and compression test (AG-10kNI, Shimadzu) at room and 300ºC 
at a strain rate of 5 x 10 
-4 s -1. The specimens for the compression tests were 
rectangular parallelepipeds, whose dimensions were 2 mm in width, 2 mm in depth and 
3 mm in height. 
 
Results 
 Figure 1 shows results of XRD for Al-5Y 
2O3. The raw material shows peaks of 
Al and cubic Y 2O3 as shown in Fig.1(a). After MA and the consolidation (Fig. 1(b) 
and (c)), the peaks of Al and Y 2O3 were still observed and almost no significant change 
can be seen. In contrast, Fig.2 shows results of Al-5La 2O3. The raw material shows 
peaks of Al and La 2O3 as shown in Fig.2(a). The La 2O3 is a mixture of different 
structures with space groups of Ia3 and Im3m. After MA (Fig. 2(b)), the peaks of Al

## Page 7



and La 2O3 are still observed without significant change. But after the consolidation 
(Fig.2(c)), peaks of an intermetallic compound of Al 11 La 3 can be seen. It is likely that 
La 2O3 is dissociated into La and O during MA in Al-5La 2O3, and the intermetallic 
compound Al 11 La 3 are produced after the consolidation by combining Al and La, 
whereas Y2O3 is not dissociated and no intermetallic compound is formed in Al-5Y 2O3. 
 Standard free energies of formation of Y 2O3 and La 2O3 at 300K are -1811 and 
-1702 kJ/mol, respectively [17]. The standard free energy of formation of Y 2O3 is 
lower than that of La 2O3. This suggests that Y 2O3 is more stable oxide than La 2O3. 
That could be why Y 2O3 does not dissociate into Y and O and remains after MA. As 
for the formation of the intermetallic compound, partial enthalpies of mixing of Y in 
Al-Y system and La in Al-La system are -144.1 and -190.1 kJ/mol, respectively [18 ]. 
Absolute value of partial enthalpy of mixing of La in Al-La system is larger than that of 
Y in Al-Y system. This means formation of Al-La intermetallic compound tends to be 
easier than that of Al-Y intermetallic compound. This fact could result in the 
formation of Al 
11 La 3 in Al-5La 2O3, but no formation of intermetallic compound in 
Al-5Y 2O3.

It should be noted that La atoms being dissociated from La 2O3 during MA most likely 
dissolve into the Al matrix and combine with Al atoms to precipitate as Al 11 La 3 during 
the consolidation. This is consistent with the result of XRD profile shown in Fig.2. 
 The detail of microstructure of Al-5Y 2O3 and Al-5La 2O3 is summarized as follows. 
Al-5Y 2O3 includes one kind of dispersoid of Y 2O3 in Al matrix, whereas Al-5La 2O3 
includes two kinds of dispersoid: the larger one is La 2O3 and the smaller one is Al 11 La 3. 
The sizes of dispersoids are listed in Table 1 as well as number densities of dispersoids. 
The number densities were evaluated from TEM micrographs, although thickness of the 
specimen is assumed to be 100nm. The sum of the number density of dispersoids is 
larger in Al-5La 
2O3 than that in Al-5Y 2O3. This is because La 2O3 is dissociated during 
MA of Al-5La 2O3, and Al 11 La 3 is precipitated finely after the consolidation. The 
formation of fine precipitate of Al 11 La 3 results in the large number density of 
dispersoids in Al-5La 2O3. 
 Figure 8 shows Vickers microhardness of Al-5Y 2O3 and Al-5La 2O3. In the case 
of Al-5Y 2O3, hardness before the consolidation is almost the same as that after the 
consolidation. In contrast, Al-5La 2O3 shows a significant increase in hardness after the 
consolidation. This is because Al-5La 2O3 has the large number density of dispersoids 
due to the fine precipitate of Al 11 La 3 after the consolidation, whereas Al-5Y 2O3 has no

 Figure 9 shows stress-strain curves of Al-5Y 
2O3 and Al-5La 2O3 at room 
temperature and 300ºC. The stress and the strain are engineering stress and 
engineering strain, respectively. Yield strength (0.2% proof strength in the present 
study) of Al-5La

Al-5La 2O3 shows larger yield strength than Al-5Y 2O3 at room temperature, which is 
consistent with the result of Vickers microhardness in Fig.8. A fracture strain of 
Al-5La 
2O3 at room temperature is larger than that of Al-5Y 2O3. The reason for the 
difference of the fracture strain is not clear now. The fracture surface was not able to 
be observed because the fracturesurfaces of these specimens were crushed and flattened 
due to the compressing shock when the specimens fractured. The relative densities of 
Al-5La 
2O3 and Al-5Y 2O3 are almost the same, 86 and 85%, respectively, which were 
evaluated by Archimedes method. Thus, the porosity is not likely to be a main reason 
for the difference of the fracture strain. It is speculated that fine and numerous 
dispersoids in Al-5La 
2O3 leads to suppression of local accumulation of dislocation at 
dispersoids, resulting in suppression of crack initiation [19]. That could be why a large 
fracture strain is achieved in Al-5La 
2O3. At 300ºC, yield strength of Al-5La 2O3 is 245

Conclusions 
Al-5Y 2O3 and Al-5La 2O3 were fabricated by MA. Effect of Y 2O3 and La 2O3 on 
mechanical properties and microstructures were investigated. The results are 
concluded as follows. 
(1) Al-5Y 
2O3 has one kind of dispersoid of Y 2O3, whereas Al-5La 2O3 has two kinds: 
La 2O3 and Al 11 La 3. It is likely that La 2O3 remains unmilled due to insufficient MA 
and Al 11 La 3 forms during consolidation, whereas Al-Y intermetallic compound does 
not form. The formation of Al 11 La 3 during the consolidation is due to a large 
negative value of partial enthalpy of mixing of La in Al-La system. 
(2) Vickers microhardness and yield strength at room temperature of Al-5La 2O3 are 
larger than those of Al-5Y 2O3. Yield strength at 300ºC of Al-5La 2O3 are also larger 
than that of Al-5Y 2O3. The large yield strength in Al-5La 2O3 results from finer Al 
grains and large number density of dispersoids. The number density of dispersoids 
is large in Al-5La 
2O3 because fine precipitations of intermetallic compound of 
Al 11 La 3 are formed during consolidation. These fine numerous dispersoids could 
keep Al grains in Al-5La 2O3 fine at high temperature, resulting in large yield 
strength in Al-5La 2O3 through grain boundary strengthening and dispersion 
strengthening as demonstrated by Orowan mechanism.
