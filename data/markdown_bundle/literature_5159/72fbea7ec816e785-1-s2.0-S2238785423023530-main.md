abstract
Precipitation behavior and dynamic recrystallization (DRX) behavior of Mg eZneY alloys
with different Zn/Y atomic ratios under different extrsuion temperatures were systemat-
ically investigated in this work. The results shows that the types of precipitated phases in
the casted alloys are changed with the increase of Zn/Y atomic ratio. After extrsuion, the
brittle W-phases in Mg
98.7Zn1Y0.3 and Mg98Zn1Y1 alloys are broken into ﬁner particles along
the extrusion direction, but the resistance of W-phases to grain growth is weak at higher
extrusion temperature. However, Mg 97.5Zn1Y1.5 alloy exhibits relatively stable grain size at
different extrusion temperatures, due to the signiﬁcant inhibitory effect of LPSO phases on
grain growth at high temperatures. The banded LPSO phases with wide phase spacing can
promote DRX behavior via particle stimulated nucleation (PSN) resulting in highest DRX
fraction. Nevertheless, the lamellar LPSO phases could effectively hinder the grain
boundary migration and dislocation motion, which is against the nucleation and growth of
DRX grains. It is precisely due to the inﬂuence of LSPO phases on the DRX behavior and its
own kinking effect that Mg
97.5Zn1Y1.5 alloy has better heat resistance. Mg 97.5Zn1Y1.5 alloy
exhibits excellent tensile strength and ductility, with ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of
413 MPa, yield strength (YS) of 330 MPa and elongation (EL) of 12.1% after extrusion at 573 K.
The good ductility is mainly due to the coordinated deformation ability of the LPSO phase
and the activated non-basal slip effect. The synergistic effect of lamellar LPSO phase and
kinking deformation effectively reﬁnes the microstructure of the alloy and improves the
strength.

1. Introduction
As a kind of the lightest metal structural materials, magne-
sium alloy has attracted more and more attention due to its
high speciﬁc strength, good damping and low density [ 1].
However, its low absolute strength and poor plasticity limit its
wide application as structural parts. In recent years,
MgeZneY magnesium alloys have received more and more
research due to their unique microstructure and excellent
mechanical properties [ 2]. It has been reported that the
strength of Mg eZneY alloys prepared by rapid solidiﬁcation
can exceed 600 MPa [ 3]. The excellent mechanical properties
of the MgeYeZn alloy are owning to the grain reﬁnement and
a long-period stacking ordered (LPSO) phase.
Precipitation behavior is one of the main factors affecting
the deformation microstructure and mechanism properties of
magnesium alloy. Mg eZneY series alloys have relatively
complex phase transformation behavior, and different kinds
of precipitates can be obtained with different Zn/Y mola ratio.
There are three kinds of ternary equilibrium phases in
MgeZneY alloys, known as X-phase (Mg
12Zn1Y1, LPSO struc-
ture), W-phase (Mg 3Zn3Y2, cubic structure), and I-phase
(Mg3Zn6Y1, icosahedral quasicrystal structure) [ 4e6]. It was
reported that the phase composition can be produced by
adjusting the Zn/Y mola ratio [ 7,8]. Among them, X-phase
(LPSO phase) is considered as an excellent strengthening
phase due to its unique crystal structure, high strength, high
hardness and high temperature thermal stability [ 9]. More-
over, it is found that the strength of Mg eZneY alloy increases
with the increase of X-phase content [ 9].
DRX behavior is another important aspect to control the
deformation microstructure and properties of magnesium
alloys. It is generally believed that DRX can effectively reﬁne
the as-cast grain structure and improve the strength. For
MgeZneY alloy, the structure and distribution of LPSO phase
could affect the nucleation and growth of DRX grains [ 10,11],
but it is still controversial whether LPSO phase can inhibit or
promote the effect of DRX. Lv [ 12,13] believed that LPSO phase
in magnesium matrix would strongly hinder the growth of
DRX grains, which is because stacking faults and twisted LPSO
phase restrict the movement of dislocations. However, Tong
[14] had a different view and they believed that the coarse
LPSO phase can accelerate DRX through “particle deformation
zones, PDZs ”.X u[ 15] believed that the high-density lamellar
LPSO phase in the magnesium matrix limite DRX. Therefore, it
is necessary to study the inﬂuence of LPSO phase structure
and LPSO-Mg interface on dislocation accumulation and the
nucleation and growth of DRX grains. Meanwhile, the volume
fraction of the second phase also has a signiﬁcant effect on the
DRX behavior of Mg eZneY alloy [ 16].
Both DRX behavior and precipitation behavior of MgeZneY
alloys are greatly affected by deformation temperature. It is
generally believed that the deformation temperature affects
the nucleation and growth of DRX grains by changing the
cumulative rate of dislocation density [ 17]. With the increase
of temperature, the critical shear stress of deformation
decrease [ 8]. Therefore, DRX of magnesium alloy can be pro-
moted by increasing deformation temperature. The variation
of deformation temperature can cause non-equilibrium
segregation of solute atoms, which leads to the formation
and rearrangement of precipitated phases [ 17]. Choisez [ 18]
found that the precipitates in the recrystallization region ﬁrst
dissolve and then precipitate again of Ti-12mo alloy during
the deformation. The change of precipitated phases also affect
DRX behavior.
Therefore, the purpose of this paper is to investigate the
effects of extrusion temperature on the precipitation behavior
and DRX behavior of Mg eZneY alloys containing different
types of precipitated phases, so as to reveal the reasons for the
changes in microstructure and mechanical properties caused
by deformation temperature, and ultimately elucidate the
strengthening mechanism of Mg eZneY alloys.

accumulation of strain gradients during the deformation
process, which is necessary for the PSN mechanism. At the
interfaces between LPSO phases and Mg matrix, local stress
concentration is more likely to be induced by dislocation pile-
ups and rearrangement [ 27]. The high KAM near the banded

kink behavior of the banded LPSO phases (as shown in Fig. 13)
can provide nucleation sites for the occurrence of DRX. Ac-
cording to Yamasaki et al. [ 28], DRX grains are preferentially
formed in kink boundaries with high orientation angles. This
is attributed to the high energy stored in the kink boundaries
as the nucleation site for DRX. Therefore, it can be indicated
that the banded LPSO phases can promote DRX and effectively
hinder grain growth, presenting obvious CDRX process. It was
widely reported that the size of the deformation zone is
related to the size of the banded LPSO phases during the
thermal deformation process [29,30]. According to Humphreys
[29], the deformation region increased with the size of LPSO
phase increases. As mentioned earlier, an increase in extru-
sion temperature is beneﬁcial for the uniform distribution of
LPSO phases. This can explain the reason why the grain size of
alloys containing LPSO phases is relatively stable during
extrusion at high temperatures.
The lamellar LPSO phases also have a signiﬁcant effect on
the DRX behavior of the alloys during hot deformation. As
shown in Fig. 13 , no obvious DRX grains are found near the
lamellar LPSO phases in Mg
97.5Zn1Y1.5 alloy. This is related to
the width and spacing of the phases. From Fig. 13(b) and (d), it
can be seen that the wide spacing of the lamellar phases is
only 0.5e0.7 nm, making it difﬁcult for such narrow phases to
accumulate dislocations and form DRX grains. Usually, the tip
of the kinks in the lamellar LPSO phases accumulate disloca-
tions, providing favorable location for the nucleation of DRX
grains [31]. As shown in Fig. 14(e) and (f), grain boundaries also
play a certain hindrance role in the lamellar LPSO phases.
Lamellar LPSO phases could effectively hinder the grain
boundary migration and dislocation motion, which is against
the nucleation and growth of DRX grains.
The spacing between lamellar LPSO phases is also an
important factor in the formation of DRX grains. As shown in
Fig. 14 (c) and (d), the spacing between the lamellar phases is
too narrow, only 0.5 e4.1 nm. Chun et al. showed that when
the spacing between lamellar LPSO phases is large enough
(2 mme3 mm), DRX grains can be formed at the gap between
two lamellar LPSO phases [ 32]. When the interlayer spacing of
the lamellar LPSO phases is too narrow, it cannot meet the

4.2. Effect of LPSO phase on mechanical properties of the
alloys with different Zn/Y atomic ratios at different
extrusion temperatures
As can be seen from Fig. 10 and Table 2 , the mechanical
properties of three alloys with different Zn/Y molar ratios
present the following rules after extrusion at 573 e673 K. (1)
When the extrusion temperature is low, the elongation of
Mg
97.5Zn1Y1.5 alloy containing only LPSO phases is 12.1%,
which is much higher than the other two alloys (3.1% and
4.9%). (2) LPSO phases have obvious strength enhancement
effect relative to W-phases. (3) LPSO phases can improve the
stability of mechanical property. Below, we will conduct a
detailed discussion and analysis of the above results.
Mg
97.5Zn1Y1.5 alloy has good plasticity is mainly due to the
coordinated deformation ability and texture weakening effect
of LPSO phases. Both Mg
98.7Zn1Y0.3 and Mg 98Zn1Y1 alloys
contain brittle W-phases, while Mg 97.5Zn1Y1.5 alloy contains
more ductile LPSO phases. Hagihara et al. [ 26] compared the
differences between the ductile LPSO phases and the brittle
W-phases on the microstructure and mechanical properties of
extruded alloys, and analyzed the inﬂuence of the second
phases on the extrusion deformation behavior. The research
indicated that the degree of fragmentation of the ductile
phases during the extrusion process is lower than that of the
brittle phases, resulting in the grain size of the alloy con-
taining the ductile phases being larger than that of the alloy
containing the brittle phases. However, due to its good
toughness, the ductile phases alloy have higher elongation.
Meanwhile, LPSO phases have the ability to accommodate
plastic deformation and coordinate deformation, which has a
positive impact on the improvement of the toughness. The
blocky LPSO phases participate in plastic deformation during
extrusion deformation, and as the degree of deformation in-
creases, its deformation substructure gradually evolves from
high-density deformation kinking and subgrain to DRX grains.
In addition, the formation of LPSO phases is closely related to
stacking faults mechanism. The precipitation of LPSO phases
is accompanied by the generation of stacking faults [ 33]. LPSO
phases and stacking faults precipitate in the same direction in
the matrix, forming on the (0001) basal plane and growing and
extending along the [10
1 0] direction of the Mg matrix, which
has a positive impact on the improvement of the plasticity
[34,35]. In addition, LPSO phases can weaken the basal texture
and enhance the internal shear stress of the magnesium
matrix during deformation, activating non-basal dislocation
slip in the magnesium matrix [ 36]. As seen in Fig. 15 , TEM
observation results show that a large number of dislocations
arrange in parallel to form a dislocation wall, and the

Undoubtedly, according to the Hall-Petch formula [ 37,38],

seen that the interface between LPSO phases and matrix is
coherent, and this interface relationship is stable and difﬁcult
to break. The LPSO phases are embedded in the matrix,
forming ﬁber reinforced phase, increasing the interface area
between the matrix and LPSO phases in the ED-ND plane,
thereby strengthening the alloy. Meanwhile, the ultimate
stress of the alloy can also be improved by transferring stress
from the matrix to the LPSO phases [ 39]. In addition, the kink
of the LPSO phases can be used to strengthen the alloy,
resulting in the formation of ﬁne grains. It is found that the
essence of deformation kink interface is LAGBs. Fig. 9 shows
that the substructure and LAGBs of Mg
97.5Zn1Y1.5 alloy in-
crease as the fraction of LPSO phase increases. The LAGBs is
formed by the cumulative rotation of the crystal lattice, which
is caused by the rearrangement and accumulation of the
dislocations in the base slip plane [ 40]. The LPSO phases with
LAGBs undergoes serious plastic deformation during the hot
extrusion process. The formation of substructures within the
LPSO phases is the result of further continuous rotation of the
LAGBs, and the continuous grain rotation is the result of
further accumulation of basal dislocation. At the same time,
the kink of LPSO phases will cause considerable plastic
deformation of the alloy, which is also very important for the
ductility [ 41]. The synergistic effect of lamellar LPSO phase
and kinking deformation effectively reﬁnes the microstruc-
ture of the alloy and improves the strength and plasticity.
The relatively stable extrusion performance of Mg
97.5Zn1Y1.5
alloy can be attributed to the following points: (1) The pro-
moting effect of LPSO phases on grain reﬁnement. The LPSO
phases ave very stable and do not undergo coarsening at high
temperatures, hindering the grain growth phenomenon
accompanying the increase in extrusion temperature, making
the grain size relatively stable. (2) LPSO phases also have good
coordinated deformation ability at low extrusion temperatures.
On the one hand, the LPSO phases have good coherent rela-
tionship with the matrix, and stress can be transmitted effec-
tively; On the other hand, the kinking ability of LPSO phase and
its promoting effect on non-basal slip also promote plastic
deformation effectively.
