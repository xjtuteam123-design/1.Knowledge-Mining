Internal Friction and Mechanical Strength of Hydrogenated Ti-rich 
Multicomponent Glassy Alloys 
 
M. Hasegawa a, M. Takeuchi, D. Nagata, T. Wada, 
H. Kato, Y. Yamaura and A. Inoue 
 
Institute for Materials Research, Tohoku University 
2-1-1 Katahira, Aoba-ku, Sendai, 980-8577 Japan. 
amasa@imr.tohoku.ac.jp 
Keywords: Internal friction, Glassy alloy, hydrogen, mechanical property, damping material. 
 
Abstract. The hydrogen-induced internal friction and mechanical strength of the Ti-rich 
Ti 
34 Zr 11 Cu 47 Ni 8 and (Ti 34 Zr 11 Cu 47 Ni 8)98 Si 2 hydrogenated glassy alloys have been investigated. It is 
found that the tensile strength is more than 0.8 GPa at room temperature when the hydrogen content is 
below about 20 at% for both alloys. The frequency dependence of peak temperature of the 
hydrogen-induced internal friction of (Ti 34 Zr 11 Cu 47 Ni 8)98 Si 2-17.3 at%H hydrogenated glassy alloys 
has been clarified. Activation energy and pre-exponential factor are estimated to be 0.35 eV and 
1.3x10 -12 , respectively. Compared with these values with those of Zr 40 Cu 49 Al 10 Si 1 hydrogenated 
glassy alloys which show an internal friction peak around 300 K at about 300 Hz, it is found that the 
activation energy is much smaller than that of the latter although the pre-exponential factor is almost 
the same. Considering their similar composition and different component (Al), it is suggested that the 
component Al of the latter glassy alloys is effective for the higher activation energy which results in 
the increase of the peak temperature. 
 
Introduction 
 
Recently, damping properties of materials attract a great deal of attention because of various 
new reasons such as finer machinery and demands in the electronic industry. Accordingly, new type 
of damping materials are expected to be fablicated. Both high mechanical strength and high damping 
ability are important properties for good damping materials. One of such kinds of materials is a 
glassy alloy. They can be prepared in a bulk form such as rod, plate, button and so on [1,2], which is 
different from conventional amorphous alloys. They have high tensile fracture strength more than 1 
GPa [1,2]. These characteristics of glassy alloys indicate that bulk glassy alloys have high ability of 
new damping materials if we can use internal friction due to re-orderings of interstitial atoms such as 
hydroegn and nitrogen. Several groups including ours have already reported studies on internal 
friction of hydrogenated glassy alloys, especially Zr-based ones, from this point of view [3-17]. 
Besides, we have reported on the Ti-rich multicomponent glassy alloys which is adavantageous for 
engineering appications because they are lighter in weight and less expensive than Zr-based ones 
[14-17]. 
One of the important required properites for damping materials is high mechanical strength. 
Hasegawa et al. have reported mechanical strength and internal friction behavior of 
(Ti 
34 Zr 11 Cu 47 Ni 8)98 Si 2 hydrogenated glassy alloys [15]. It was found that they had high tensile 
strength more than 0.8 GPa when the hydrogen content was less than 20 at%. In this study, tensile 
stregth of Ti 34 Zr 11 Cu 47 Ni 8 hydrogenated glassy alloys is clarified because their internal fricrion 
behavior has been already reported [14]. Then, the results were compared to those of 
(Ti 34 Zr 11 Cu 47 Ni 8)98 Si 2 hydrogenated glassy alloys [15].

Another important properites for damping materials are, of course, damping properties : peak 
internal friction and peak temperature. Although a lot of studies to improve damping properties have 
been reported, most of the prepared hydrogenated ones show peak temperature of internal friction 
below room temperature (< 300 K) when the peak internal friction is higher. This is one of the serious 
problems when they are used as damping materials for engineering. However, Mizubayashi et al. 
reported Zr 40 Cu 49 Al 10 Si 1 hydrogenated glassy alloys which show an internal friction peak around 300 
K at ~300 Hz [8,10]. The hydrogen-induced internal friction is originated from the relaxation of 
hydrogen. Accordingly, the peak temperature depends on the activation energy, pre-exponential 
factor and oscillation frequency. In this study the activation energy and pre-exponential factor of 
hydrogenated (Ti 34 Zr 11 Cu 47 Ni 8)98 Si 2-17.3 at%H glassy alloys, which show high internal friction as 
well as Zr 40Cu 49 Al 10 Si 1 but low peak temperature (~200K) at ~300 Hz frequency, have been 
estimated from the experimental results of the frequency dependence of the peak temperature. Then, 
the results are compared with those of the Zr 40 Cu 49 Al 10 Si 1 hydrogenated glassy alloys. 
 
Experimental Procedure 
 
The alloy ingots were prepared in an arc-melting furnace in a purified argon atmosphere. Glassy 
alloys were prepared by a conventional single-roll spinning method in a purified argon atmosphere. 
The as-prepared ribbons were approximately 1 mm wide and 20 µm thick. The glassy phase was 
certified with X-ray diffraction using Cu-Kα radiation and a Ni filter. Thermal properties were 
investigated using differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) with a heating rate of ~0.67 K/s under a 
purified argon atmosphere. Hydrogen was absorbed by an electrochemical method using a 1N H 2SO 4 
solution. The hydrogen content of the samples was determined using an inert gas carrier melting 
thermal conductimetric method. 
Fracture strength was measured using an Instron-type tensile testing machine at room 
temperature. The strain rate was 8.3 x 10 
-4
 /s. Five samples were used for measurement, and the 
averaged fracture strength was used as a final value. The internal friction measurements were carried 
out using a reed method over the temperature range of ~90-380 K in vacuum (~ 10 -2 Pa). One end of 
the ribbon sample was clamped tightly while an electrostatic force was used for oscillation excitation 
of the sample. The electrical capacity of the sample was measured to detect the oscillation of the 
sample. The internal friction was measured using a resonance oscillation method at a frequency of 
100~300 Hz. The strain ampliitude was ~ 1.0 x 10 -6. The details of the instrumental setup and 
measurement procedure have been described elsewhere [13].

content region than the latter. This indicates that only 2 at%Si is effective to decrease mechanical 
strength although it is effective to increase peak internal friction and peak temperature reported 
before [15]. Accordingly, the former hydrogenated glassy alloys which do not contain Si (metalloid 
element) are advantageous for damping materials if they have good damping properties. Hasegawa et 
al. reported the effects of Pd addition on the hydrogen-induced internal friction of Ti 34 Zr 11 Cu 47 Ni 8

(Ti 34 Zr 11 Cu 47 Ni 8)98 Si 2 ( i.e. (Ti 33.3 ,Zr 10.8 )Cu 46.1 Ni 7.8 Si 2.0 ) one, it is interesting to see its activation 
energy and the pre-exponential factor of these glassy alloys. Those of the Zr 40 Cu 49 Al 10 Si 1 
hydrogenated glassy alloys were reported to be 0.75 eV and 1.5 x 10 -12 for Zr 40 Cu 49 Al 10 Si 1-6.3at%H

energy of the (Ti 34 Zr 11 Cu 47 Ni 8)98 Si 2 hydrogenated glassy alloy is much smaller than that of the 
Zr 40 Cu 49 Al 10 Si 1 although its pre-exponential factor is almost the same. Accordingly, the lower peak 
temperature of the former is attributable to its smaller activation energy. As mentioned above, their 
components and compositions are similar. However, it should be noted that there is one clear 
difference. Although both glassy alloys contain a small amount of Si which is effective to increase a 
peak internal friction [8,10,15], the latter glassy alloys contain Al. Effects of the Al addition on the

Conclusion 
 
The mechanical strength of the Ti-rich Ti 
34 Zr 11 Cu 47 Ni 8 hydrogenated glassy alloys and 
hydrogen-induced internal friction of (Ti 34 Zr 11 Cu 47 Ni 8)98 Si 2 hydrogenated glassy alloys have been 
investigated. It is found that the tensile strength of the Ti 34 Zr 11 Cu 47 Ni 8 ones is more than 1.0 GPa at 
room temperature when the hydrogen content is below about 20 at%. The frequency dependence of 
peak temperature of the hydrogen-induced internal friction of (Ti 34 Zr 11 Cu 47 Ni 8)98 Si 2-17.3 at%H 
hydrogenated glassy alloys has been clarified. The peak temperature increases with increasing 
oscillation frequency. Activation energy and pre-exponential factor are estimated to be 0.35 eV and 
1.3x10 -12 , respectively. Compared with these values with those of Zr 40 Cu 49 Al 10 Si1 hydrogenated 
glassy alloys which show an internal friction peak around 300 K at about 300 Hz, it is found that the 
activation energy of the former is much smaller than that of the latter (0.47~0.75 eV) although the 
pre-exponential factor is almost the same. Since their alloy compositions are similar but components 
are different, especially Al, it is suggested that the component Al of the latter glassy alloys is effective 
for the higher activation energy which results in the increase of the peak temperature.
