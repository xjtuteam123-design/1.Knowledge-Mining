Formation of nanograins in Ni-Co based superalloys compressed
quasistatically at high temperature

In this study, the performance of hot compressive deformation was investigated in two newly developed Ni-Co
based superalloys having different stacking fault energy (SFE). It is interesting to discover that nanograins (NGs)
can be produced in superalloy deformed at high temperature and relatively low strain rate. With the decrease of
SFE and strain rate, the formation of NGs will be promoted obviously, leading to the negative strain rate sensitiv-
ity of compressive stress in superalloys. The NGs, generated through the fragmentation of subgrains by
microtwins, may become a candidate choice for dynamically strengthening superalloys at high temperature.

Ni-base superalloys are widely used in the turbine blades and discs
of aircraft engines and industrial gas turbines for their superior combi-
nation of mechanical properties, such as good high-temperature
strength, excellent fatigue properties, high oxidation resistance at ser-
vice temperatures [1]. Grain size plays an important role in achieving
the desirable mechanical properties and optimizing hot-working pro-
cess in superalloys. It has been well documented that dislocation-relat-
ed grain reﬁning down to nanoscale could take place in pure Fe and Ni
with high SFE when processed at extremely high strain rate [2,3]. For
the medium-SFE Cu and Al, the grain size can be re ﬁned to submicron-
scale at low strain rate [4,5].G e n e r a l l y ,g r a i nr eﬁnement via dislocation
activities arises from the strain induced evolution of dislocation conﬁgu-
ration, from dense dislocation tangles, walls or cells to the subboundaries,
and then to highly misoriented grain boundaries (GBs)[2].
In pure Cu, deformation at high strain rate will introduce abundant
nanoscale twin/matrix lamellae as the prerequisite for grain reﬁnement,
and then nano-sized blocks are produced with the fragmentation of
these lamellae by the well-developed dislocation walls, which can
evolve forward into randomly oriented NGs ﬁnally [4,6]. Further de-
creasing the SFE in Cu alloys induced by alloying of Al or Zn will lead
the twin fragmentation to be the dominated grain re ﬁnement mecha-
nisms, the equilibrium grain size also decreases on account of the
improved twinning capability and reduced twin spacing[7,8].I nm a t e -
rials with highly twinned structure characterized by thin T/M lamellae,
shear bands may develop to accommodate strain, which results in the
transformation of the T/M lamellar structure to NGs[9]. It is interesting
to ﬁnd that when deformed at extremely high strain rate and low tem-
perature, Inconel 600 superalloy can also be re ﬁned to nanoscale with
the interaction of mechanical microtwins (MTs) and dislocations [10].
However, practical application of this NG in the mechanical property
may be restricted by its instability and coarsening at high temperature.
It is meaningful if we can ﬁnd the way producing NGs that is thermally
stable and available for strengthening superalloys at high temperatures.
This study will give an introduction for the formation of thermally stable
NGs and its effect on compressive stress in newly developed
superalloys.

In order to ﬁgure out the underlying formation mechanism of NGs,
lots of TEM observations were carried out to capture the evolution pro-
cedure of NGs. An original subgrain containing several nano-sized
blocks is shown in Fig. 4a, in which four blocks with small misorienta-
tion (named as a–d) and two blocks with high misorientation (named
as e, f) can be clearly distinguished. Further observation reveals that
these blocks can be produced by the intersection and encirclement of
SFs and MTs ( Fig. 4b). As the plastic strain increases, these blocks may
gradually transform into nanoscale subgrains or NGs because twin
boundaries can transfer into the GBs of the block by remnant disloca-
tions from the de-twinning of thin MTs as reported by Cao et al. [17].
The polycrystalline diffraction pattern is presented in the inset, along
with some imperfect diffraction spots of MTs due to the strain induced
lattice distortion. These grains border upon each other with polygonal
boundaries, meanwhile the MT or SF shears across the whole grain
(Fig. 4c). For a NG, the intersection of MT or SF can further re ﬁne the
grain size, meanwhile the generation of numerous MT/SF also plays a
complementary role in accommodating plastic strain (Fig. 4d).
Considering the above results, it can be inferred that the deformation
mechanisms of two superalloys are dependent on the strain rate and
SFE. Decreasing SFE and strain rate can both promote the formation of
MT and NG since the variation of SFE will greatly affect the dissociation
of dislocations inγ matrix. It is convinced that decreasing the SFE of two
superalloys by adding Co element can hinder the cross-slip of disloca-
tions, promote the decorrelation of a/2 〈110〉 matrix dislocations
into Shockley partials, and facilitate the initiation of SFs and MTs in
the matrix [18]. For instance, in superalloys with relatively low SFE,
abundant MTs can be introduced under the plastic deformation at
high temperature and low strain rate [19,20]. It has been veri ﬁed that
deformation twinning in the precipitation strengthening superalloys

22]. Pseudo twins will form after the overlapping of four consecutive
complex SF (CSF) on {111} planes and transform into a true MT after
the atom reordering. This process is thermally activated that needs

The important role played by the MT in the re ﬁnement of subgrain
into NG reﬂects that the grain reﬁnement is controlled by the formation
of MT, which can be facilitated by decreasing the SFE of tested alloys. It
can be inferred that the formation of NG depends on whether MT exist
in deformed microstructure, meanwhile the proportion of NGs is also
correlated to the content of MT in two alloys. For instance, NGs can
only be observed after compression at low strain rate forming MTs,
moreover the quantity of NGs increases with twinnability induced by
decreasing SFE. Dislocation-mediated slip is also important for NG for-
mation because it serves as the precursor for nucleating the subgrains
that reﬁne original coarse grains and supply Shockley partials for twin-
ning [23]. The dynamic introduction of NGs during compressive defor-
mation probably acts as strong obstacles for dislocation motion,
resulting in dispersion strengthening in materials. For 13Co alloy with
higher SFE, only small amount of NGs is introduced at 10 −2 s−1, the
strengthening effect is mostly offset by the drawback of strain rate.

superalloys having controllable SFE and certain plastic deformation ca-
pacity, which needs further study in the future.
In summary, the present results lead us to propose a formation
mechanism of NGs that operates through the re ﬁnement of subgrains
by MTs. It is interesting to ﬁnd that abundant NGs can be introduced
in the low-SFE superalloys by compressive deformation at high temper-
ature and low strain rate, which will increase theﬂow stress in superal-
loys. NGs are produced following the sequence of 1) formation of
subgrains from dislocation tangles and cells, 2) fragmentation of
subgrains down to nanoscale blocks by MTs, 3) development of these
blocks into NGs. The formation of NGs is related to the formation of
MTs, which is an atom diffusion controlled thermal activation process.
Decreasing the SFE and strain rate can promote the formation of MTs,
which therefore leads to the increase of the proportion of NGs ﬁnally.
