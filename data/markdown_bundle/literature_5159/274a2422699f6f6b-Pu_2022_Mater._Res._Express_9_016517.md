Abstract
This paper presents a study on plastic deformation behavior of Cu– 50Ta alloy at temperatures of
286– 473 K and strain-rate of 0.01– 6200 s−1. The effects of temperature, strain-rate, and strain on the
yield strength, ﬂow stress, and strain-rate sensitivity coefﬁcient were determined. A phenomenological
model was established to predict variation of the strain-rate sensitivity coefﬁcient for Cu– 50Ta alloy
under dynamic compression. A Johnson– Cook constitutive model was established to predict the
equivalent stress– equivalent plastic strain relationship under extreme deformation(high temperature
and strain-rate). The results showed that the plastic deformation behavior of Cu– 50Ta alloy was
affected by temperature, strain-rate, and strain. The material exhibited obvious strain-rate
strengthening and thermal softening. As the strain-rate increased, the yield strength logarithmically
increased. At a temperature of 286 K, the strain-rate increased from 0.01 s
−1 to 6200 s−1, and the yield
strength increased from 543.75 MPa to 881.13 MPa. In addition, the yield strength linearly decreased
as the deformation temperature increased. Under conditions of dynamic deformation, the variation
of strain-rate sensitivity coefﬁcient could be expressed as a function of strain-rate and strain. The
phenomenological model accurately described the variation of the strain-rate sensitivity coefﬁcient of
Cu– 50Ta under dynamic deformation conditions. The Johnson– Cook constitutive parameters,
calibrated by experimental data, described the plastic deformation behavior of the alloy under high-
velocity impact.
1. Introduction
Cu and its alloys have the advantages of high density, high speed of sound, and excellent dynamic mechanical
properties, which make it an ideal metal for explosive forming. It is widely used in military applications for the
fabrication of traditional shaped charges[1, 2]. One of the most commonly used is the shaped charge jet(SCJ)
warhead. The huge energy generated by explosive detonation causes the conical-shaped charge liner to collapse
at high strain rate, converging on its axis to form a rod-shaped metal jet with a head velocity of 10
4 ms −1. The
huge kinetic energy carried by an SCJ can lead to great damage of an armored target. Research has shown that
increasing the jet densityρj and length l can effectively improve the penetration ability of the jet[3]. To further
improve the damage ability of an SCJ warhead to an armored target, researchers[4– 10] tried to prepare a W– Cu
material to replace the existing pure copper material and increase the jet density; however, Lee[11] found that
the contribution of W to the total deformation of a W– Cu jet was very limited. The W– W boundary of W– Cu
alloys was prone to fracture during tensile deformation, which limited penetration performance of a W– Cu jet at
large stand-off. Compared with the W phase in a W– Cu alloy, Ta has lower strength and better dynamic
plasticity, and is also a good explosive-forming material[12, 13]. Darling [14– 20] published a series of research
results on a nano-structral Cu– Ta alloy, which revealed the effect of Ta particles on the plastic deformation
behavior and micromechanism of nanometer crystalline Cu. The results[20] showed that the presence of Ta

2. Materials and experiments
2.1. Material
Cu– Ta composite material with a mass ratio of 1:1 was prepared by a liquid-phase sintering process. The
parameters of the Cu and Ta powders are shown in table1. To improve the material density, the sintered samples
were forged at room temperature. The density of the Cu– 50Ta alloy was 11.528 g cm−3, which was 99.5% of the
theoretical value. Figure1 shows a microstructure of the Cu– 50Ta alloy. Near-spherical Ta particles(light-
colored structures) were embedded in the Cu matrix phase(dark color). The average size of the Ta particles was
about 20 μm. The Ta particles exhibited slight deformation and aggregation after forging, but the overall
distribution was uniform and there were no obvious cavities in the structure.
To study the thermal– mechanical properties of the material, the melting point and speciﬁc heat capacity
(50 °C– 600 °C) of the Cu– 50Ta alloy were measured at standard atmospheric pressure according to ASTM
E773– 2006 (2018). As shown inﬁgures 2 and 3, the melting point of the Cu– 50Ta prepared in this study was
1093.5 °C under standard air pressure. As the ambient temperature increased, the speciﬁc heat capacity of
Cu– 50Ta gradually increased. In the temperature range of 50°C– 600 °C, the speciﬁc heat capacity of the
material ﬂuctuated between 0.345– 0.482 J gK−1.

increases and exceeds 40% of the melting point(Tm) of the material(0.4Tm was considered as the critical

onset and rapid completion of dynamic recrystallization, which can result in rapid grain reﬁnement to less than
10 μm, so the material has high dynamic plastic deformation ability. According to the research of Gang[33], the
grain-reﬁnement process of the liner material is completed during the initial stage as the liner material is crushed
to its axis.

3.4. Constitutive model
As illustrated above, the dynamic compression plastic deformation response of Cu– 50Ta at temperatures of
286– 473 K and strain-rate of 0.01– 6200 s−1 was systematically studied. According to the relationship between
equivalent stress and equivalent plastic strain of the Cu– 50Ta alloy, the evolution of the strain-rate sensitivity
coefﬁcient m under dynamic compression deformation was studied under different strain-rate at 286 K.

deformation, it was necessary to establish a dynamic constitutive model suitable for Cu– 50Ta.

[35] studied the ﬂow characteristics and microstructure evolution of aging nickel-based superalloys. A uniﬁed
model based on dislocation density was established. The model can accurately predict theﬂow behavior of alloys

4. Conclusions
In this study, Cu– 50Ta alloy with high strength and density was prepared by liquid-phase sintering and forging.
The plastic deformation behavior of the alloy at high temperatures and high strain-rate was studied. The effects
of strain, temperature, and strain-rate on yield strength,ﬂow stress, and strain-rate sensitivity coefﬁcient were
analyzed according to the relationship between equivalent stress and equivalent plastic strain.
(1) The Cu – 50Ta alloy prepared in this study had obvious strain-rate strengthening and thermal-softening
properties. Under quasi-static deformation, there was no obvious strain-hardening phenomenon. As the
strain-rate increased, the yield strength of the material signiﬁcantly increased and theﬂow stress decreased
with increasing strain.
