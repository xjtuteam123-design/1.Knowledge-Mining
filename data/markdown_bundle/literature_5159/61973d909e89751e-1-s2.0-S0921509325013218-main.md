1. Introduction
Aerospace engineering is one of the most important tools to assess 
the comprehensive technological level of each country. The next gen -
eration of aeroengines have been put forward new requirements, which 
need the higher thrust-to-weight ratio and operating-temperature [ 1 , 2 ]. 
Nb-Si superalloys are known as the candidate materials to replace the 
Nickel-based superalloys because of their lower density (7.3 – 8.6 g/cm
3
) 
and higher melting point (above 1700 
◦
C) [ 3 , 4 ]. The niobium solid so -
lution (Nbss) and silicide work together to guarantee the comprehensive 
properties. However, the poor room-temperature (RT) fracture tough -
ness limits their industrial application [ 5 , 6 ]. And the content of Si 
usually exists in the hypoeutectic region to avoid the silicide phases [ 7 ].
Up to now, lots of ways have been attempted to improve the overall 
performance of Nb-Si alloys, such as alloying [ 8 , 9 ], directional solidi -
fication [ 10 , 11 ], ultrasonic assisted solidification [ 12 , 13 ] and so on. 
Element Ti is commonly used to increase the fracture toughness, which 
can decline the P-N barrier energy [ 14 , 15 ]. However, element Ti doping 
is harmful for the melting-point of system alloys. Moreover, the Tiss 
precipitation easily separates in the phase boundary. Some studies 
report that the congener element Zr with Ti can also improve the for -
mation of eutectic to increase the K
Q 
values of Nb-Si alloys [ 16 , 17 ]. 
Moreover, Wang et al. [ 18 ] reported that high content of Zr is still 
beneficial for the fracture toughness. Hence, element Zr with the higher 
melting-point have been studies to replace Ti in Nb-Si based superalloys. 
However, the RT fracture toughness of Nb-Si-Zr can ’ t still meet the 
practical application. Recently, interstitial element B has been reported 
that can improve the fracture toughness of Nb-Si alloys [ 19 – 21 ]. Tsa -
kiropoulos et al. [ 19 ] showed that B doping could accelerate the 
decomposition of (Nb, X)
3
Si and form the Nbss/ α (Nb, X)
5
Si
3 
eutectic. 
Ma et al. [ 20 ] found that the eutectic microstructure occurred the 
coarsening, and the lattice parameter of α (Nb, X)
5
Si
3 
declined as doping 
of B. Sun et al. [ 21 ] showed that element B mainly dissolved into Nbss 
phases, and the tensile strength signally increased with the increase of B.

2. Experimental methods
The nominal composition of Nb-16Si-24Zr- x B ( x = 1, 2, 4, 8) is 
prepared via the vacuum arc-melting, and five remelting cycles are 
performed to assure the compositional homogeneity. The same samples 
(5*5*5 mm) are cut for the microstructure observation and phase 
analysis using the Quanta 200F and the X-ray diffractometer (XRD). 
Moreover, the element distribution is observed by wave-dispersive 
spectroscopy (WDS) on electron probe microanalyzer (EPMA, 
JXA8230). Three-point bending texts (20*4*2 mm) are measured under 
the AGX-plus with the 0.2 mm min
 1 
speed. And the precracks of 
samples are processed via the Mo wire of 0.2 mm by electrical discharge 
machining. The nanoindentation experiments (5*5*5 mm) are tested on 
the G200 nanoindenter and the compressive properties ( φ 4*6 mm) are 
prepared with AGX-plus under a speed of 0.36 mm min
 1
. Furthermore, 
the dislocation behaviors are observed using the FEI Talos F200X.
