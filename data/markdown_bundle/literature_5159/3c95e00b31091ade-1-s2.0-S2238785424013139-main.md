ABSTRACT 
This work investigates the effect of microstructural degradation on the overtemperature rupture performance in 
Ru-containing Ni-based single crystal superalloys. Unlike the initial samples, the rupture lives in the degraded 
sample are largely lost, and adding Ru in the degraded sample increases the rupture life at 1120 
◦
C/100 MPa and 
1180 
◦
C/100 MPa. Based on the difference in the microstructure and microstructural evolution in the initial and 
degraded samples. Their influence on the rupture performance can be derived. The enlarged width of the γ 
matrix is the major reason for the reduction of the rupture life and increase in the strain for the degraded sample 
compared to the initial samples. The existence of the dislocation network and the inhibition of the precipitation 
of the TCP phase are the major reasons for the lower rupture life reduction in the 5Ru degraded sample than in 
the 3Ru degraded sample. The mobility of the dislocation increases with the temperature, weakening the benefit 
effect caused by Ru.

4.1. The microstructural degradation after the thermal exposure 
After thermal exposure at 1100 
◦

structure degradation will proceed, i.e., the formation of the dislocation 
network at the γ ′ - γ interface, the evolution of the volume fraction and 
morphology of the γ ′ phase, the precipitation and growth of the TCP 
phase.

of the dislocations, which leads to the formation of the dislocation 
network at the γ ′ - γ interface to relax the misfit [ 36 , 37 ]. The higher the 
misfit is, the denser the dislocation network will produce [ 14 ]. Thus, the

The heat treatment and the compositions influence the volume 
fraction of the γ ′ phase. According to the temperature of the final aging 
treatment is lower than the service temperature, which increases the 
solubility of the γ matrix, i.e., decreases the volume fraction of the γ ′ 
phase in the degraded microstructure. In addition, the coarsening of the 
γ ′ phase will also proceed along the < 001 > directions, leading to the 
morphology evolving from cubic to labyrinth structure. At the same 
time, the width of the γ channel also enlarged. Adding Ru can further 
decrease the volume fraction as it promotes the dissociation of the γ ′ 
phase [ 38 ]. Correspondingly, the width of the γ phase in 5Ru alloy is 
higher than that in 3Ru alloy, as shown in Fig. 7 . 
The precipitation and growth of the TCP phase is an uphill diffusion 
process. Even though the volume fraction of the γ matrix is enlarged 
during the thermal exposure process, i.e., the solid solubility of the TCP 
phase forming elements is decreased, the TCP phase still will precipitate. 
The addition of Ru can inhibit the precipitation of the TCP phase, as seen 
in Fig. 2 . The behavior should relate to the mechanisms in the literature. 
Firstly, Ru improves the solid solubility of refractory elements [ 26 , 39 ]. 
Secondly, Ru reduces the segregation coefficient of refractory elements 
between the γ and γ ʹ phases [ 40 ]. Thirdly, Ru increases the lattice strain 
energy of the TCP/ γ phase interface [ 41 ]. Lastly, Ru decreases the vol -
ume fraction of the γ ʹ phases and thus increases the solid solubility of 
refractory elements in the γ matrix [ 42 ].

4.3. Comprehensive analysis of rupture performance 
The rupture performance is influenced by the mobility of the dislo -
cations, i.e., the higher mobility decreases the rupture life but increases 
the strain. The dislocation network in the degraded sample provides 
back stresses for the dislocations, thus reducing the dislocation mobility. 
On the contrary, the lower volume fraction of the γ ′ phase (higher width 
of the γ matrix) in the degraded sample increases the dislocation 
mobility by decreasing the Orowan resistance, as shown in equation (2) . 
The existence of the TCP phases in the 3Ru samples decreases both the 
rupture life and strain as it is a brittle phase, which easily causes stress 
concentration and promotes nucleation of the dislocations.

parison between the 3Ru and 5Ru in the degraded samples, the differ -
ence between the width of the γ matrix is only 1.1 times. Although it also 
causes a higher strain in the 5Ru degraded sample than the 3Ru 
degraded sample, the higher density of the dislocations and almost zero 
TCP phase is the major reason for the higher rupture life in the 5Ru 
sample. On the contrary, the detrimental effect of increasing the creep 
cavities caused by adding Ru is not the major reason. For the comparison 
between the degraded samples rupture at 1120 
◦
C and 1180 
◦
C, the 
benefit of adding Ru on the rupture life is decreased. This is due to the 
mobility of the dislocations increasing with temperature, thus weak -
ening the strengthening effect provided by the dislocation network and 
not having a TCP phase. 
5. Conclusion 
This study investigates the effect of microstructural degradation on 
the overtemperature rupture performance in Ru-containing Ni-based 
single crystal superalloys. The experimental evidence and the discus -
sions presented above enable the following main conclusions: 
1) After thermal exposure, the microstructural degradation contains the 
formation of the dislocation network, the dissociation of γ ′ phase, 
and the precipitation of the TCP phase. Adding Ru increases the 
dislocation density, promotes the dissociation of the γ ′ phase and 
inhibits the precipitation of the TCP phase. 
2) After the rupture test, the density of the dislocation and the disso -
ciation of the dislocations both increase in the initial and degraded 
samples, whereas the content of the TCP in the degraded samples 
remains almost unchanged. 
3) The enlarged width of the γ matrix is the major reason for the 
reduction of the rupture life and increase in the strain for the 
degraded sample compared to the initial samples. 
4) The existence of the dislocation network and the inhibition of the 
precipitation of the TCP phase are the major reasons for the lower 
rupture life reduction in the 5Ru degraded sample than in the 3Ru 
degraded sample. 
5) The mobility of the dislocation increases with the temperature, 
weakening the benefit effect caused by Ru.
