ABSTRACT 
Recent research has focused on the development of disk superalloys with better temperature capability and 
mechanical properties via a new alloying concept that uses high levels of Co and γ
′
formers of Al, Ti, Ta and Nb to 
produce composite precipitation strengthening. In this work, alloying effects of Nb and Ta on phase stability, 
microstructure and property of a novel Ni-Co-based superalloy were systematically investigated using the high- 
throughput multi-component diffusion multiples (MCDMs) in combination with various high-throughput mea -
surement tools. A deep learning-based image recognition method was developed to efficiently extract the γ
′
morphological features, including area fraction, particle size, number density. The rapid characterizations of 
alloy compositions, microstructure and Vickers hardness (HV) enable us to establish the composition-dependent 
microstructural characteristics and mechanical property as well as their correlation with the phase stability, 
which can be directly applied for designing Ni-Co-based superalloys with superior microstructure stability and 
strength. 
Modern Ni-based superalloys are remarkable for their resistance 
against mechanical and chemical degradation at elevated temperatures 
and extremely oxidative and corrosive conditions, and hence have 
emerged as the material of choice for critical structural parts in aero -
engines and industrial gas turbines [1 – 3] . These superior properties of 
superalloys are mainly attributed to solute strengthening in the γ matrix 
and hardening effect of the L1
2 
γ
′
precipitates and carbides, which are 
achieved by addition of various alloying elements and their composing 
microstructures [4 – 6] . Driven by the increasing requirements of 
high-performance engines to enhance fuel efficiency and reduce envi -
ronmental and acoustic emissions, Ni-based superalloys with increased 
properties and temperature capability have been intensively explored 
for the key components, including disks and blades, to tolerate elevated 
engine operating temperatures. 
For applications in turbine disks at temperatures beyond 700 º C, a 
new kind of Ni-Co-based superalloys, designated as TMW alloys were 
designed and exhibit superior microstructure stability and mechanical 
properties at elevated temperatures that are comparable with the 
advanced powder metallurgy (P/M) RR1000 and ME3 alloys [7 – 9] . In 
these alloys, apparently high levels of Co and Ti in the range of 21.8 to 
31.1 wt.% and 5.4 to 7.4 wt.%, respectively, were added to produce 
composite precipitation strengthening of γ
′
-Ni
3
Al and γ
′
-Co
3
Ti phases. 
The multi-beneficial effects of high Co level have been well documented, 
typically, for improving the high-temperature phase stability [10 – 12] , 
creating a wider processing temperature window by decreasing the γ
′
solvus temperature [ 8 , 9 , 13 ], and enhancing the creep resistance by 
decreasing the stacking fault energy (SFE) [14 – 16] . Ti is a strong 
γ
′
-forming element that serves to increase the volume fraction of γ
′
phase 
and enhance precipitation strengthening [ 17 , 18 ]. Besides Al and Ti, Nb 
and Ta are also two major γ
′
-forming elements that are essential to

More recently, considerable attention has been paid to understand 
the functions of Nb and Ta additions in Ni-Co-based superalloys. 
Christofidou et al. [22] investigated the influence of Nb on the micro -
structure and properties of two novel P/M Ni-Co-based superalloys. 
With 1 wt.% addition of Nb, the alloy (Alloy B in Ref. [22] ) exhibited 
enhanced tensile and creep properties, and better oxidation resistance 
than the Nb-free alloy (Alloy A in Ref. [22] ). Zhou et al. [23] found the 
Ni-Co-based PM superalloy containing 1 wt.% Nb (1Nb) owned a higher 
γ
′

Nb during long-term thermal exposure at 800 º C. In addition, a slightly 
larger γ

coarsening rate was observed in the 1Nb alloy due to the 
increased diffusion coefficients of Al and Nb in the γ matrix. Zhu et al. 
[ 24 , 25 ] confirmed the Ta addition, in the range from 0 to 4 wt.%, 
increased the γ
′
volume fraction and meanwhile promoted the precipi -
tation of (Ti,Ta)-rich MC carbide, resulting in obvious improvement of 
the creep resistance and tensile strength. However, in the alloy with a 
higher Ta concentration of 4 wt.%, η precipitates displaying rod-like and 
lamella-like morphology were observed and believed to be responsible 
for the decrease of creep resistance in comparison with the 2Ta alloy. 
The subsequent work form Bai et al. [26 – 29] revealed an increased 
volume fraction of γ
′
phase and enhancement of the tensile strength, 
ductility and creep properties with increasing Ta. However, a high 
volume fraction of the plate-like σ phase was observed in the 4.8Ta alloy 
after long-term thermal exposure at 750 º C.

precipitation from the supersaturated γ matrix, allowing rapid and 
reliable study of microstructure and mechanical strength across wide 
compositions.

To further investigate the mechanical properties of different micro- 
alloys with wide composition variations, micro-hardness testing was 
performed at room temperature using a Qness Q10A automatic micro- 
indenter with a load of 500 g and a dwell time of 10 s. As indicated in 
Fig. 1 (d), a series of 121 Vickers hardness (HV) values were obtained 
across the entire 2000 μ m × 2000 μ m region using a step size of 200 μ m 
that is much larger than the minimum recommended spacing (i.e., 2.5 
times the Vickers diagonal of the indentation) [40] . The results are 
plotted as color-coded contour maps, Fig. 5 , to intuitively show the 
Vickers hardness distribution across the entire K0-K6-K7 diffusion re -
gion. It is obvious that the Vickers hardness increases from the bottom 
side (baseline alloy) to the top side (Ta- and Nb-rich alloys) of Fig. 5 (a) 
and reaches a maximum value of 518 at the upper right corner. The 
composition dependence of Vickers hardness is constructed in Fig. 5 (b), 
exhibiting a continuously increasing tendency with respect to increasing 
Nb and Ta concentrations. In Ni-based superalloys, precipitation 
strengthening and solid solution strengthening are the two primary 
contributors to their strength. With the increase in Ta and Nb contents, 
higher alloy hardness and strength are achieved by providing higher 
volume fraction and APB energy of γ
′
precipitates, as well as the 
enhanced solid solution strengthening from the γ matrix. Furthermore, 
the Nb-rich alloys possess superior hardness compared with that of the 
Ta-rich alloys, indicating a more effective strengthening effect is ach -
ieved by the addition of Nb. Considering the very similar γ
′
area fraction 
and particle size in both Nb- and Ta-rich alloys, Fig. 4 , we speculate that 
the APB energy of γ
′
precipitates plays a dominant role and Nb is more 
significant in increasing the APB energy [41] . This phenomenon will be 
a subject of our future detailed studies using individual bulk samples 
with various Nb and Ta contents. 
In recent years, considerable efforts have been continued to develop 
next generation Ni-based superalloys for disk applications by adjustment 
of the overall alloy chemistry and in particular, elevated levels of Ta and 
Nb as well as relatively high levels of Co and Ti, such as Alloys A and B 
[22] and ME5 series alloys [42] . Appropriate alloying addition of Ta and 
Nb is known to constantly confer exceptional strength, creep and 
oxidation benefits to superalloys [22 – 29] . However, excessive addition 
of Ta and/or Nb can develop a tendency to precipitate η and/or δ phases 
that generally have detrimental effects on the alloy microstructure and 
properties. In our previous work [33] , the composition criterion 
regarding the η stability was evaluated by the use of two key parameters, 
Al/(Nb + Ta + Ti) ratio and Co content, perfectly categorizing the 
well-established commercial and experimental Ni-based superalloys 
into γ + γ
′
and γ + γ
′
+ η dominant regions. The zero-phase-fraction 
(ZPF) line of η based on the presently studied baseline alloy K0 was 
superimposed onto Figs. 4 and 5 , providing significant guidance for 
designing better alloys by adding high levels of Nb and Ta to achieve the 
most effective strengthening effect while avoiding the formation of 
undesirable η phase. 
It is also worth noting that the effect of η phase on the mechanical 
properties of superalloys is closely related to its morphology, quantity 
and distribution such that small amounts of η phase existing as fine 
blocks or individual platelets can be beneficial for advantageous me -
chanical properties [43 – 45] . This provides an alternative pathway to -
wards the design of future Ni-based superalloys by taking advantage of 
the composite precipitate phase strengthening of γ
′
and η . The formation 
and morphology of η precipitates are believed to be particularly sensi -
tive to the contents of Al, Co, Ta, Nb and Ti as well as some relative ratios 
among them, for instance, the precipitate size of η may be effectively 
controlled by adjusting the Ta/Ti ratio [45] . Accordingly, the compo -
sition map consisting of the correlation among Al, Ti, Nb, Ta and Co, 
Fig. 5 (d) in our previous work [33] , together with the presently 
measured composition-dependent microstructure and hardness, can also 
serve as guidance for identifying novel γ + γ
′
+ η superalloys with 
improved high temperature capacity and properties.

diffusion multiple (MCDM) approach to systematically investigate the 
effects of Nb and Ta additions on the microstructure and property of a 
novel Ni-Co-based superalloy. The composition-dependent microstruc -
ture and Vickers hardness as well as their correlation with the phase 
stability were established in a much rapid and efficient manner. The γ
′
