HIGHLIGHTS
• The effects of Co and temperature on mechanical properties of nickel-based superalloys were studied by molecular dynamics.
• The combined effect of Co and low temperature results in more stationary Stair-rod dislocations, resulting in increased flow stress.
• Co leads to more HCP and stacking faults in γ’ phase, hinders the formation of dislocation loops and decreases tensile strength at 300 K-600 K.

ABSTRACT
Ni(Nickel)-based superalloys are used as indispensable materials for turbine blades in aero-engines because of
their excellent creep, fatigue and corrosion resistance. Co(Cobalt), as one of the new key elements in Ni-based
superalloys, has attracted extensive attention in Ni-based superalloys field. However, the effect mechanism of
Co on Ni-based superalloys is still controversial. To solve this problem, tensile properties and microstructure of
Ni-based superalloys doped with or without Co (5 %) have been investigated through molecular dynamics
simulation, and the joint impact of temperature and Co on the superalloy mechanical properties has also been
analyzed. The findings indicate that 5 % Co doping reduces the yield stress, yield strain, and elastic modulus of
the structure, while increasing the flow stress at 300 K – 600 K; however, the advantage decreases at 900 K and
disappears completely at 1200 K. This can be explained that Co doping leads to more slippage of stacking faults
in the elastic stage and hinders the loop formation of dislocations at 300 K – 600 K, while more Stair-rod dislo-
cations occur in the plastic stage. These effects disappear at 900 K – 1200 K.
1. Introduction
Ni(Nickel)-based superalloys are widely used as turbine blade ma-
terials for aircraft engines [ 1 – 3 ], due to their excellent high-temperature
mechanical properties. As is well known that Ni-based superalloys are
composed of two-phase, namely ordered L1
2

FCC structure with γ -Ni phase [ 3 ]. Ni-based superalloys are much
stronger than pure γ -Ni and γ ′ -Ni

proven that various factors can influence the mechanical properties of
Ni-based superalloys, such as crack propagation, fatigue deformation,
chemical constituents, dislocation evolution and microtwinning
formation [ 4 – 6 ], etc. The fabrication of Ni-based superalloys will also
affect their properties. The fabrication of traditional cast Ni-based su-

will occur during the processing [ 8 , 9 ], serious chemical
micro-segregation, and serious waste of post-treatment [ 10 ]. The addi-

lenges encountered in traditional manufacturing processes, and only the
AM process is capable of designing alloys with high volume fraction γ ′
phase [ 11 ] that match the dimensions of the models in this simulation.

significantly improved by the addition of alloying elements, such as W,
Ta, Re, Ru and Co. For example, W and Ta, is usually a main

stable stress in the plastic stage of the two structures also decreases
gradually with the increase of temperature, because the plastic defor-
mation of the alloy is related to the dislocation movement. At low
temperatures, the movement of the dislocation is quite difficult due to
the large frictional stress generated by Peierl ’ s valley. However,
increasing the temperature can overcome this frictional stress [ 37 , 38 ].
Therefore, as the temperature increases, the flow stress will decrease.
In order to further study the effect of Co on the mechanical properties
of the structure, Fig. 4 shows the variation of the mechanical properties
of the model with temperature and Co. Fig. 4 (a) shows that the flow
stress of the structure decreases significantly with the increase of tem-
perature [ 4 , 40 ]. Taking the 0 % Co structure as an example, its flow
stress decreases from 4.62Gpa at 300 K to 3.80Gpa at 1200 K. The flow
stress response of the 5 % Co structure is different from the 0 % Co
structure with the change of temperature, compared with the 0 % Co
structure, the flow stress of the 5 % Co structure increases most obvi-
ously at 300 K – 600 K, and the advantage gradually weakens with the
increase of temperature. Finally, at 1200 K, the flow stress of the 5 % Co
structure is lower than that of the 0 % Co structure. The overall elastic
modulus shows a decreasing trend with the increase of temperature in
Fig. 4 (a), mainly because the increase of temperature, the increase of
atomic kinetic energy, the intensification of thermal motion, and the
increase of atomic spacing lead to the decrease of elastic modulus
[ 40 – 42 ]. The addition of Co slows down the decrease rate of structural
elastic modulus with the increase of temperature. In terms of yield stress
and yield strain, as shown in Fig. 4 (b), it is intuitively found that
whether the structure has Co or not, the yield stress and yield strain
decrease steadily with the increase of temperature. After adding Co, the
yield stress and yield strain of the 5 % Co structure are lower than that of
the 0 % Co structure.
The above results show that the mechanical response of different
structures at different temperatures is different. On the whole, regard-
less of whether there is Co, the elastic modulus of the structure at the
elastic deformation stage in the tensile process decreases with the in-
crease of temperature, and the increase of temperature leads to the
decrease of the flow stress at the plastic deformation stage. The addition
of Co will also enhance the flow stress of the structure. Compared with
the 0 % Co structure, the mechanical properties of the 5 % Co structure
show different characteristics with the change of temperature, the
analysis shows that the yield stress and yield strain of the 5 % Co
structure are lower than that of the 0 % Co structure, while the advan-
tages of flow stress weaken with the increase of temperature. Due to the
differences in mechanical properties of different structures, it is neces-
sary to further analyze the mechanism of their formation, and the effects
of temperature and Co on the evolution of dislocations are introduced.

4. Conclusions
This paper mainly used molecular dynamics simulation to study the
effect of Co and temperature on the tensile mechanical properties of Ni-
based superalloy, and explored from the aspects of mechanical proper-
ties and microstructure. The main conclusions are as follows.
1. The yield stress and yield strain of the same structure decrease with
the increase of temperature. The addition of Co affects the me-
chanical properties of the structure, compared with the 0 % Co
structure, the addition of Co leads to the reduction of yield stress,
yield strain and elastic modulus, however, the flow stress elevates at
300 K–600 K. With the increase of temperature, the magnitude of the
increase in flow stress of the 5 % Co structure is gradually weakened
until the flow stress is lower than the 0 % Co structure at 1200 K.
2. The addition of Co and temperature affect the nucleation of dislo-
cation. In the plastic deformation stage, Shockley and Stair-rod dis-
locations mainly play a role, and the addition of Co at 300 K–600 K
leads to more fixed Stair-rod dislocations, resulting in better flow
stress, the influence of Co on Stair-rod dislocation decreases with the
increase of temperature.
3. The addition of Co also affects the phase structure and dislocation
distribution. It is found that at 300 K–600 K, the 5 % Co structure
produces more layers of lamellar HCP phase and intrudes into the γ ′
phase, and hinders the formation of dislocation loops, resulting in the
decline of its mechanical properties and a large number of obvious
stacking faults enter the γ ′ phase at yield strain, which makes the
stacking fault zone more complete and accelerates the structural
deformation, these trends decrease with increasing temperature.
