1. Introduction
The design concept of high-entropy alloys (HEAs) is proposed in
2004.[1,2] HEAs are composed of ﬁve or more principal elements
with a molar ratio of 5% –35%,[3] and trace elements can be
added. With the continuous research on HEAs by many scholars,
the number of its principal elements has been relaxed from the
initial 5 or more to 3 or more metal elements, [4,5] which provides
a new direction for energetic structural materials. Scholars have
carried out a lot of research on the microstructure and static/
dynamic mechanical properties of HEAs. Zhang et al. [5] studied
the microstructure and mechanical behavior of Hf ZrTiTa 0.53
HEAs at the initial strain rate of 1.0 /C2 10/C0 3–2.2 /C2 103s/C0 1. The
results showed that the alloy displayed thermoplastic instability,
and the deformation is strong localized in adiabatic shear
bands and coinﬂuenced by strain hardening, strain rate strength-
ening, and thermal softening. Hu et al. [6] studied the dynamic
mechanical properties and microstructure evolution of
AlCoCrFeNi2.1 eutectic HEAs at different temperatures, and
found that the yield strength and ﬂow stresses increased with
the increasing of strain rate, but decreased with the increasing
of temperature. Bobbili et al. [7] established
am o d i ﬁed Johnson –Cook model for
FeCoNiCr HEA over a wide range of strain
rates. Hu et al.[8] studied the dynamic behav-
ior and microstructure characterization of
TaNbHf ZrTi HEA at a wide range of strain
rates and temperatures. Zhang et al.[9] stud-
ied the mechanical properties and micro-
structure of the Al 0.3CoCrFeNiTi0.3 HEA
under dynamic compression. In conclusion,
HEAs exhibit remarkable strain hardening,
strain rate sensitivity, and good ductility at
different temperatures.
The application of HEAs in the ﬁeld of
active materials mainly depends on the
energy released by impact reaction. It can
be seen from Figure 1 that the three ele-
ments (Ti, Zr, and Hf ) of Group IVB are
all metal elements with high heat of
reaction.[10–13] In this article, the three elements Ti, Zr, and
Hf are selected as the principal elements. The strength, tough-
ness, and other mechanical properties of HEAs are adjusted by
adding a small amount of Al, Cu, and Ni elements. Table 1 shows
the basic properties of each element.
The phase structure and phase stability of HEAs are deter-
mined by the entropy and enthalpy of mixing. Table 2 shows
the calculation results of mixing entropy and mixing enthalpy
of three HEAs. [14,15] Higher mixing entropy can inhibit the for-
mation of ordered phase and phase separation, and ﬁnally pro-
mote the formation of solid solution phase.
In conclusion, researchers have mainly carried out a lot of
research on the mechanical properties of HEAs at different tem-
peratures and strain rates. However, the research on the in ﬂu-
ence of trace elements on the mechanical properties of HEAs
and the mechanical behavior of HEAs under multiple loading
is still lacking. In this article, TiZrHfAl 0.3, TiZrHfCu 0.3, and
TiZrHfNi0.3 are taken as the research objects, and the effects
of trace element types, strain rates, and multiple loading times
on the dynamic compressive mechanical properties of HEAs are
analyzed.

High-entropy alloys (HEAs) are gradually replacing armor-piercing core materials
such as depleted uranium and tungsten alloys in high strain rate, high temper-
ature, and high pressure service environments due to their excellent mechanical
properties (high strength, high strain rate strengthening effect, and strong shear
self-sharpening characteristics). The dynamic compression performance of TiZrHf-
based HEAs with different elements, different strain rates, and multiple loadings is
tested by the self-constructed separate Hopkinson compression bar experimental
system. The results show that the TiZrHfCu0.3 alloy has the largest elastic modulus
and the strongest deformation resistance. TiZrHfAl 0.3 and TiZrHfCu0.3 show a
certain strain rate effect. The elastic modulus increases with the increase of loading
times. The ultimate strength of TiZrHfAl 0.3 remains unchanged after multiple
loading, while the ultimate strength of TiZrHfCu 0.3 and TiZrHfNi0.3 decreases
gradually.

3. Experimental System and Basic Parameters
3.1. Experimental System
In this article, the split Hopkinson compression bar (SHPB)
experimental system is used to study the dynamic compressive
mechanical properties of HEAs. The SHPB experimental system
mainly includes the impact bar, incident bar, and transmission
bar. The velocity of the impact bar is measured by laser velocim-
etry. Buffer rod and damper are used as the energy absorption
system. The data acquisition system consists of strain gauges,
dynamic strain gauges, and computers with corresponding data
processing functions. The lengths of incident and transmission
bars are 1200 mm, and the steel material is selected. MoS 2
(molybdenum disul ﬁde) is coated on the contact surface to
reduce the friction effect on the end face. In order to eliminate
the dispersion effect in the process of stress wave propagation,
realize the dynamic stress balance in the specimen, and maintain
the constant strain rate deformation of the specimen, a brass
plate is preplaced at the front end of the incident rod as a
waveform shaper. The constructed SHPB experimental system
is shown in Figure 4 .
The experimental loading and testing system is shown in
Figure 5 .

4. Results and Analysis
4.1. Effect of Adding Trace Elements on Dynamic Mechanical
Properties
In order to study the effect of adding different elements on the
mechanical properties of HEAs, the speed of the impact bar
should be controlled to keep it consistent. In the experiments
of no. 1, no. 2, and no. 3, the maximum difference of impact
bar velocity is 5.8%. Figure 7 shows the dynamic compressive
properties of HEAs specimens with different trace elements.
According to the true stress –strain curves obtained from the
experiments, it can be divided into three stages: linear elastic
stage, plastic strengthening stage, and failure stage.
In linear elastic stage, the stress and strain of the three kinds
of HEAs specimens have a linear relationship, which satis ﬁes
Hooke law. It can be seen that the slope of the linear elastic stage
is TiZrHfCu 0.3 > TiZrHfAl0.3 > TiZrHfNi0.3, which indicates
that TiZrHfCu 0.3 has the largest elastic modulus and the stron-
gest deformation resistance, followed by TiZrHfAl 0.3 and
TiZrHfNi0.3. The slope of the linear elastic phase of the
TiZrHfCu0.3 alloy is signi ﬁcantly larger than that of the other
two kinds of alloys. From a microscopic point of view, the added
trace element Cu promotes the bonding strength between the
original atoms.
In the plastic strengthening stage, all three kinds of alloys have
obvious work hardening phenomenon. According to the stress –
strain curves obtained from the experiment, the strengthening
phenomenon of TiZrHfCu 0.3 is the most signi ﬁcant.
In the failure stage, the addition of Al element makes TiZrHf-
based HEAs have stronger plasticity. TiZrHfCu 0.3 and
TiZrHfNi0.3 show a cliff-like drop directly after the stress exceeds
the peak value.
Figure 8 shows the micro morphology of the three specimens
after compression. It can be seen that the specimen is deformed
locally, the grains are elongated and rotated, and the specimen
shows good strength and toughness.

4.3. In ﬂuence of Repeated Loading on Dynamic Mechanical
Properties
Figure 11 shows the true stress –strain curves of the TiZrHfAl 0.3
specimen after 4 times repeated loadings. From the dimensions
of the specimen before each loading in Table 3, it can be seen that
each loading will cause the specimen to deform, and the macro-
scopic performance is that the diameter of the specimen
increases and the length decreases. After 4 times repeated load-
ings, the diameter of the TiZrHfAl0.3 alloy specimen increases by
1.75% and the length dimension decreases by 2.98%. It can be
seen from the trend of the curve that the TiZrHfAl 0.3 alloy has
obvious linear elastic stage, strengthening stage, and failure
stage, and has a certain plasticity. Repeated loading makes the
boundary point between the linear elastic stage and the strength-
ening stage of HEAs more and more obvious. With the increase
of loading times, the elastic modulus increases from 64.5 to
350.4 GPa, and the ampli ﬁcation is 443.3%.

5. Conclusion
The effects of trace element types, strain rates, and multiple load-
ing times on the dynamic compressive mechanical properties of
TiZrHfAl0.3,T i Z r H f C u0.3, and TiZrHfNi 0.3 HEAs are studied.
The main conclusions are as follows: 1) Under single loading,
the TiZrHfCu0.3 has the largest elastic modulus and the strongest
deformation resistance. The strengthening phenomenon of
TiZrHfCu0.3 is the most signi ﬁcant. 2) With the increasing of
impact bar speed, the ultimate strength of TiZrHfAl 0.3 and
TiZrHfCu0.3 specimens increases signi ﬁcantly. The two alloys
show a certain strain rate effect. 3) The elastic modulus increases
with the increase of loading times, and the deformation resistance
of the alloy is signi ﬁcantly improved by repeated loading. The dif-
ference is that the ultimate strength of TiZrHfAl 0.3 remains
unchanged after multiple loading, while the ultimate strength
of TiZrHfCu0.3 and TiZrHfNi0.3 decreases gradually.
