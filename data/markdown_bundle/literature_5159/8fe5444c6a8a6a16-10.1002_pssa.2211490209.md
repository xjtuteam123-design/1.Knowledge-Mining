The effect of copper and zirconium upon the high-temperature mechanical properties and the 
deformation mechanisms of A1-5.7 wt% Zn-2.0 wt% Mg alloys is studied. Results of impression creep 
and tensile tests show that the addition of Cu increases the strength of the material, and strongly 
enhances dynamic recovery during high-temperature deformation. In the case of the ternary AlZnMg 
alloy the main rate-controlling process is viscous glide, but in the alloy containing 1.4 wt% addition 
of Cu the strain rate is controlled by dislocation climb s-' < E < lO-'s-' and 400 "C < 
T < 520 "C). The addition of 0.14 wt% Zr to the AlZnMgCu alloy results in a fine-grained micro- 
structure due to which the material exhibits superplastic properties at high temperatures with grain 
boundary sliding as the dominant process during superplastic deformation. 
1. Introduction 
1.1 General remarks 
The mechanical properties of AlZnMg alloys are strongly influenced by further alloying 
elements being either in solid solution or in the form of precipitates. The effect of alloying 
additions is also significant in the high-temperature region where under certain conditions 
superplastic behaviour may occur. It was shown earlier that AlZnMg alloys can be made 
to exhibit superplasticity by the grain refining effect of zirconium addition [l]. In the AlZnMg 
alloys investigated here above 400 "C the Zn and Mg atoms are in solid solution whereas 
a part of the Cu and most of the Zr atoms constitute second-phase particles (e. g. Al,CuMg, 
A1,Zr) which are stable even at these relatively high temperatures [2]. The fine dispersion 
of the AI,Zr precipitates ensures the fine-grained and relatively stable microstructure which 
is necessary for the achievement of structural superplasticity.

energy of the base metal. Detailed investigations performed on A-type materials have 
shown that in these alloys the rate-controlling process is viscous glide due to the presence 
of solute atom atmospheres around the dislocations [8, 91. The substructure consists of a 
relatively uniform distribution of dislocations without any evidence of defined subgrains. 
Due to the viscous glide the activation energy of the deformation process is expected to be 
in agreement with that for interdiffusion of solute atoms.

1.2 Superplastic materials 
Superplastic materials can be deformed up to several hundred or a thousand per cent strain 
in tension. The superplastic deformation takes place at high temperature (above half the 
absolute melting temperature) and at low strain rate (between and lo-’ sC’). It is 
well known that the curve of the In 0-ln i plot is sigmoidal and consists of three parts with 
a low value of n, in the intermediate region 11, and high values of n, in region I and 111. 
The real superplastic processes with extremely high elongation belong to region 11, where 
n, varies between 1.5 and 3 [l, 131. The superplastic behaviour is characterized in general 
by the inverse of the stress exponent, which is the strain rate sensitivity, 
The experimental results show that the maximum elongation increases with increasing 
values of the m parameter. A deformation process is generally expected to be superplastic 
if m > 0.3. Outside the optimal strain rate range (i.e. in regions I and III), m becomes less

In the present paper the effect of Cu and Zr additions on the high-temperature mechanical 
properties and the deformation mechanisms of AlZnMg alloys are studied. Furthermore, 
some aspects characterizing the superplasticity of the Zr-containing alloy were also 
investigated. 
2. Experimental Details 
The chemical compositions of the alloys investigated are shown in Table 1. The alloys were 
prepared from 99.99% purity aluminium. After casting the samples were homogenized for 
8 h at 470 "C in air, and then hot extruded to sheets of 10 x 40 mm2 cross section. The 
starting temperature of the hot extrusion was 380 5 "C. For solution treatment the 
specimens were annealed at 470 "C for 30 min and water-quenched to room temperature. 
After this treatment fully recrystallized materials were obtained. It was shown previously 
[17] that alloys A, B, and C with no Zr addition have very similar coarse-grained 
microstructure with about 100 pm mean grain size. In constrast to this the microstructure 
in alloy D is extremely fine-grained, with an average grain size of less than 10 pm. The 
developoment of this microstructure can be clearly attributed to the grain refining effect of 
the zirconium. 
The high-temperature mechanical properties were studied at first by impression creep 
tests and on the basis of the results obtained from these measurements complementary 
measurements were made by tensile testing. The method of creep measurements with the 
impression creep tester was described in detail previously [18, 191. During the impression 
creep test a flat-ended punch is pressed into the surface of the sample. On applying a 
constant load (F) - after an initial transient - the punch penetrates with a constant 
velocity (v) into the sample. To compare the results of impression creep and uniaxial tensile

3.2. Tensile tests 
Tensile tests were primarily performed in the temperature and strain rate ranges where on 
the basis of the impression creep measurements the behaviour of alloy D was expected to 
be superplastic. Fig. 4 shows the stress-strain curves obtained at 515 "C with various initial 
strain rates for the four alloys investigated. It can be seen that in the case of alloys A, B, 
and C at each strain rate the flow stress increases with increasing Cu content, the total 
elongation, however, remains constant independently of the strain rates and the Cu content. 
This is in agreement with the observation that the values of the macroscopic parameters 
(n, Q) characterizing the creep process are also independent of the amount of Cu additions. 
In contrast to this, in the case of alloy D the total elongation is strongly changing with 
strain rate, which is a common feature of superplastic materials [13]. While at the lowest 
strain rate the total elongation was larger than 300%, at the highest rate the sample 
elongated only 60%. Fig. 5 shows a 300% elongated sample with no sign of necking which 
is a clear evidence for the superplastic behaviour of alloy D.

The true stress-true strain curves obtained at 515 "C (Fig. 4), where alloys A, B, and C are 
solid solutions, show that rapid strengthening occurs only at the early stages of deformation.

results show that for all alloys investigated after- an initial stage strain hardening and

by dynamic recrystallization [29 to 311. To study the origin of the softening process TEM 
investigations were also made. These investigations are also necessary for studying the 
deformation mechanisms of the alloys investigated.

As we have seen (Fig. 7) the total elongation of samples of alloy D decreases with increasing 
strain rate. Fig. 13 shows the fracture surfaces of the samples of Fig. 7 (the numbering helps

place mainly by grain boundary sliding, the samples can be expected to break along grain 
boundaries. The SEM picture of the fracture surface of sample 1 (Fig. 14) clearly shows the 
failure along grain boundaries. Our results agree well with the established theory that grain 
boundary sliding is the main mechanism of superplastic deformation [32, 331. 
4. Conclusions 
1. The addition of Cu increases considerably the high-temperature strength of the AlZnMg 
alloys studied here, and enhances the dynamic recovery process. 
2. In the pure ternary AlZnMg alloy (without any Cu and Zr addition) the main 
rate-controlling process in the high-temperature (400 "C 5 T 5 520°C) deformation is 
viscous glide in which the solute atom atmospheres around the dislocations play an 
important role, while in the alloy with high Cu content the strain rate is controlled by 
dislocation climb. 
3. With the addition of Zr to the alloy containing also a 1.4% addition of Cu a very 
fine-grained structure is obtained which is stable even at high temperatures. This caused 
the deformation of the alloy to be superplastic in the temperature range of 400 to 515 "C 
at strain rates of 5 x 10 -6 to 2.5 x 
4. Metallographic investigation of the fracture surface of the samples of alloy D indicated 
that during superplastic deformation grain boundary sliding is the main mechanism of the 
deformation process.
