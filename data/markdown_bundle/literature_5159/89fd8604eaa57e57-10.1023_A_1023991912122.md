EFFECT OF ALLOYING OF CHROMOALUMOSILICIDE COATINGS
ON THEIR HIGH-TEMPERATURE STRENGTH
I. A. Basalai 1 and L. F. Pavlovskaya 1
Translated from Metallovedenie i Termicheskaya Obrabotka Metallov, No. 1, pp. 24 – 27, January, 2003.
The effect of alloying of chromoalumosilicide coatings on steel 45 with molybdenum, manganese, titanium,
and tungsten aimed at increasing their high-temperature strength is considered. Microscopic x-ray spectral
analysis and Auger spectral analysis are used to determine the presence of each alloying element and its con-
centration in the coating.
INTRODUCTION
Analysis of compositions of high-temperature steels and
alloys shows that their main operational properties can be
substantially enhanced by alloying them with tungsten, mo-
lybdenum, titanium, and other elements in addition to the of-
ten used chromium, aluminum, and silicon.
Data on systematic research into alloying of high-tem-
perature alloys are quite scarce and some works [1 – 4] only
mention the positive influence of extra additives of some re-
fractory elements on the protective properties of coatings.
Calorizing and chromocalorizing techniques in combina-
tion with the introduction of manganese present certain inte -
rest [1, 2]. In the case of volume alloying of alloys, manga -
nese lowers the harmful effect of sulfur, and its introduction
into a heat-resistant calorized or chromocalorized coating
raises its resistance to oxidation including sulfide corrosion.
Some elements are introduced into coatings with the aim
of raising their protective properties by creating a barrier
layer. For example, the introduction of tungsten into calo -
rizing mixtures [1] substantially reduces the intensity of the
diffusion processes that occur between the coating and the
substrate and cause exhaustion of the protective properties of
the coating. This is caused by the fact that the region between
the external zone of the coating and the substrate is enriched
with tungsten during annealing. The atoms of the latter have
a low mobility, and it becomes a barrier when the coating
serves at a high temperature.
In order to increase the corrosion resistance of parts of
gas turbine engines with chromium coatings, the latter are al-
loyed with a refractory metal simultaneously with REM. As
a result, the coating acquires a form describable as MCrAl +
REM + refractory metal (Mo, W, Nb) [4].
In this connection it is interesting to study the increase in
the high-temperature strength of carbon steel 45 with a
chromoalumosilicide coating due to its additional alloying
with tungsten, molybdenum, titanium, and manganese.
METHODS OF STUDY
The method of deposition of a chromoalumosilicide
coating is presented in detail in [3]. The alloying is per-
formed by adding oxides of appropriate elements into the
saturating mixture with optimum composition [5]. The ther-
mal diffusion saturation lasts for 6 h at a temperature of
950°C.
We tested the coatings for high-temperature strength in
the oxidizing atmosphere of the furnace at 900°C placing the
specimens into porcelain crucibles preliminarily calcinated
to a constant mass. The high-temperature strength was evalu-

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Analyzing the dependences presented in Fig. 1, we will
see that the introduction of alloying additives into the satu -
rating mixture increases the thickness of the obtained layers
and positively influences the high-temperature strength of
the coatings. The thickness of the layer is greatest when the
tungsten and titanium oxides are contained in the mixture in
an amount of up to 3%. In the case of the introduction of
manganese and molybdenum oxides, the maximum is less
manifested and lies in a range of 3 – 6%. Further increase in
the proportion of alloying additives leads to a decrease in the
thickness of the layer, and at an amount of 12% it differs lit -
tle from the initial thickness.

which is provided by its alloying with tungsten and forma -
tion of aluminide spinel of the FeAlO 3 type in the testing
process.
CONCLUSIONS
1. Additional introduction of tungsten, titanium, manga -
nese, and molybdenum into the saturating mixture for
chromoalumosiliconizing causes alloying of the obtained
coatings with the corresponding elements. For example,
upon the introduction of 3% WO
3 the surface layer contains
0.3% tungsten.
2. The elements improving the high-temperature strength
can be arranged in the following order: W /c174Ti /c174
Mn /c174Mo. The coatings have maximum high-temperature
strength, when the oxides of the mentioned elements are in-
troduced into the saturating mixture in an amount of 3%; in
this case the high-temperature strength of the chromo-
alumosilicide coatings increases by a factor of 2.5 – 3.
3. The high-temperature strength of alloyed chromo -
alumosilicide coatings is determined by the degree of alloy -
ing with this or that element rather than by the thickness of
the coatings.
