1. Introduction 
Developing novel high-temperature materials with higher melting 
points than conventional nickel or cobalt-based superalloys are required 
to meet the demands for greater gas turbine efficiency for future gen -
erations of advanced propulsion systems [ 1 , 2 ]. The emergence of 
high-entropy alloys (HEAs) or compositionally complex alloys (CCAs) 
[ 3 , 4 ] brings about new thoughts and a vast compositional space for alloy 
designing. Some prototype HEAs [ 5 – 7 ] can achieve higher strength than 
nickel-based superalloys at elevated temperatures. Among the devel -
oped HEAs, eutectic high entropy alloys [ 8 – 13 ] (EHEAs) are quite 
eye-catching because they have the advantage of both HEAs and eutectic 
alloys, including excellent castability, fine and uniform lamellar mi -
crostructures, high strength, and good ductility [ 14 ]. Extensive efforts 
have proved that 3d transition metals based EHEAs containing 
face-centered cubic (FCC) solid solutions and intermetallic phases, such 
as L1
2 
[ 15 ], B2 [ 8 , 15 ], and Laves phase [ 16 , 17 ], can maintain a good 
combination of strength and ductility and impressive strength and 
thermal stability at elevated temperatures. Nevertheless, they cannot 
resist temperatures higher than 1000 
◦
C, due to the lack of refractory 
elements with higher melting points. Under the design philosophy of 
HEAs, appropriately introducing intermetallics into refractory HEAs to 
achieve decent properties at elevated temperatures is being increasingly 
attractive. 
Among all the refractory elements, vanadium and its alloys have 
attracted increasing attention for the lowest density (6 g/cm
3
) in the 
refractory element family, plus its high melting point (1910 7degC), 
relatively high strength at mediated temperatures (600 – 800 
◦
C) and 
abundant natural resources on earth [ 18 ]. The efforts of developing 
V-based alloys, especially those vanadium solid solution (V
ss

V – Ti – Cr series alloys, were mainly focused on structural materials for 
fusion reactors [ 19 , 20 ], aircraft, rocket, and atomic engineering [ 21 ]. 
Vanadium is very sensitive to impurities and its ductility can be greatly 
improved by extreme purification from interstitial impurities by vacuum
