Abstract 
 A new refractory high entropy alloy (RHEA) with no n-equiatomic composition of 
W10 Mo 27 Cr 21 Ti 22 Al 20 was designed based on empirical rules and CALPHAD approach. 
Calculated results showed that W 10 Mo 27 Cr 21 Ti 22 Al 20 RHEA had lower density and higher 
melting temperature than that of equiatomic alloy. The designed alloy was prepared by vacuum 
arc melting then the crystal structure, microstruct ure, chemical composition, density, hardness 
and compressive strength had been evaluated. The al loy exhibited a BCC solid solution as major 
phase with typical dendritic microstructures. The e xperimental results confirmed calculated 
results, suggesting a BCC solid solution phase. The density of the alloy was 7.48 g/cm 3, which 
was approximately close to the calculated value, 7. 73 g/cm 3. However, the measured hardness 
value (511 kg/mm 2) was significantly higher than the calculated valu e (125 kg/mm 2), indicating 
the effect of solid solution strengthening. This alloy had compressive yield strengths of 1245MPa 
at room temperature, 510MPa at 600°C, 201MPa at 100 0°C and 105MPa at1200°C. 
Compressive strain of the alloy was limited at room temperature, but it increased signiﬁcantly to 
above 60% at 1000°C and 1200°C. Comparing with four conventional superalloys Inconel 718, 
Waspaloy, Hastaloy X and Haynes 230, the studied RH EA shows lower density and superior 
high-temperature yield strength.

1. Introduction 
High entropy alloys (HEA) are defined as solid solu tion alloys that contain at least five principal 
elements, in which all the atomic concentrations ar e between 5% and 35%. HEAs have 
significantly higher mixing entropy than convention al alloys, which increases the stability of 
solid solution compared to intermetallic phases [1–3]. 
The mixing entropy of the alloy is maximized when i ts constituent elements are in equiatomic 
ratio. Hence, major studies in this field are focus ed on the design of HEAs containing elements 
with equal or near-equal atomic ratios [4–7]. Howev er, an increasing number of studies have 
revealed that formation of solid solution phase in HEAs does not depend solely on the 
maximization of mixing entropy through equiatomic r atios of elements [8,9]. In addition to 
mixing entropy, ΔSmix , some other empirical parameters have expressed to predict the formation 
of solid solutions phase in HEAs. The most common parameters are: the mixing enthalpy, ΔHmix , 
the atomic-size difference, δ, and the relative contributions of the mixing enth alpy and the 
mixing entropy, Ω [10–14]. Considering these parameters, the solid s olution phase can also be 
formed in non-equiatomic systems due to the contrib ution of parameters additional to the ΔSmix . 
Therefore, optimizing the composition based on non- equiatomic systems seems to be necessary 
to achieve new solid solution phase HEAs with desired properties. 
One of the most important classes of HEAs is refrac tory high entropy alloys (RHEAs) based on 
refractory metals such as W, Ta, Mo and Nb with BCC structure [15–17]. RHEAs can be 
suitable for high temperature applications due to t heir high melting temperature. However, high 
density and poor high temperature oxidation resista nce are the two main problems for the use of 
refractory metals in these alloys [18–21]. 
Several studies have been done to reduce the densit y and oxidation of HEAs by adding or 
replacing other elements such as aluminum and titan ium [18,19,22–28]. However, the optimal 
composition of the RHEAs containing these elements, so that the solid solution phase remains 
stable while reducing the density, has not been studied.

In the present study, the composition of W-Mo-Cr-Ti -Al based Refractory high entropy alloys 
was optimized to achieve a solid solution phase wit h a minimum density and maximum melting 
temperature. Non-equiatomic compositions can have a great potential for designing a large 
number of new HEAs with desired properties which wo uld enable to overcome the limitations of 
HEAs design concept. On the other hand, the CALPHAD (CALculation of PHAse Diagram) 
method is also used as one of the most important me thods for predicting the phase stability in 
several HEAs [29–33]. In this regard, combined with experimental results, the CALPHAD 
method was used to compare and validate the predict ion of solid solution phase versus the 
empirical parameters. In the following, the propert ies (density, hardness and strength) of the 
designed alloy were studied.

In this research, the composition of the W-Mo-Cr-Ti -Al based alloys was optimized to achieve 
following three main targets simultaneously: 
- Formation of a solid solution phase. 
- Minimum density (less than 8 g/cm 3, which is corresponded to the average density of N i-
base superalloys) [48]. 
- Maximum melting temperature (more than 1350°C, whic h is corresponded to the average 
melting temperature of Ni-based superalloys) [48]. 
For this purpose, using MATLAB software, all compos itions of W-Mo-Cr-Ti-Al quinary alloy 
system in the range of 5-35 at% with steps of 1 at% were obtained and for each of these 
compositions, the parameters of Ω, δ, ρ and T m (Equations 2-6) were calculated. By applying the 
conditions Ω ≥ 1.1, δ ≤ 6.6%, T m = max and ρ = min, the alloy composition that has these 
conditions was finally selected as the optimal allo y. CALPHAD method was used to validate the 
empirical results in predicting the solid solution phase. For this purpose, calculated mole fraction 
of stable phases was determined in the temperature range of 2500°C to 100°C using JmatPro 
software (v.7.0) [44,49–52] with Ni-based superallo ys database. Although Ni-based superalloys 
databases were initially developed for Ni-based sup eralloys, they still cover many other elements used in 
the composition of refractory HEAs (including the e lements used in our composition) 
[14,31,32,44,49,53–56]. 
2.2. Materials and characterization 
The selected alloy W
10 Mo 27 Cr 21 Ti 22 Al 20 was prepared by vacuum arc melting (VAM). High 
purity molten Ti was used as a getter for residual oxygen, nitrogen and hydrogen in the high 
vacuum chamber. All five powdered elements with a p urity of over 99.5% were compacted in 
the form of pellets. At first, the pellets of eleme nts Al, Ti and Cr were melted and the ingot of 
these elements was obtained. Then the ingot of W an d Mo was obtained similarly and added to 
first ingot. Finally, both ingots were remelted four times to ensure the homogeneity of the alloy. 
Crystal structure and phase identification were cha racterized using X-ray diffraction method 
(XRD, EXPLORER GNR analytical instrument group) wit h Cu Ka ( λ = 0.15406 nm) radiation. 
The diffraction pattern was collected over a 2 θ range from 20° to 90° with a step size of 0.01° 
and step time of 2 sec/step. The microstructure and the chemical compositions of the alloy were 
identified using a Field Emission Scanning Electron Microscope (FE-SEM, TESCAN-MIRA3) 
equipped with backscattering electron (BSE) and ene rgy dispersive x-ray spectroscopy (EDS,

SAMx) detector. For both XRD and SEM, the samples c ut from the center part of ingot were 
ground with SiC abrasive papers to 2000 grit, then polished with 1 μm diamond paste, and 
followed by ultrasonic cleaning in ethanol and distilled water. 
The density of the alloy was determined by Archimed es' law. Vickers microhardness was 
measured according to ASTM E384-16 using a Vickers microhardness tester (BUEHLER) under 
a load of 500 g for 20 s. Cylindrical samples (3 mm in diameter and 5 mm in height) for

Compression tests were conducted at room temperatur e (RT), 600, 1000 and 1200°C in a 
Zwick/Roell (Z250) mechanical testing machine equip ped with Zwick contact extensometer 
under initial stain rate of 0.001/s. For elevated t emperature tests, under argon atmosphere, the 
samples were heated at a heating rate of 20 °C/min, soaked at the test temperature for 10 min and 
then compressed to a height strain of 60% or until fracture. 
 
3. Results and Discussion 
3.1. Design and calculation results 
In the W-Mo-Cr-Ti-Al quinary system, there are a hu ge number of possible alloying 
compositions to study the effect of the composition on the empirical parameters. In this research, 
after determining the optimal alloy composition, co ntour maps were used for demonstrating the 
variations of the empirical parameters with the com position in the W-Mo-Cr-Ti-Al system. On 
the other hand, it is not possible to display the v ariations of a parameter versus the composition 
in a quinary alloy system by 2-D contour diagram. T herefore, the atomic percent of one 
constituent element (e.g. Al) was kept constant and the variations of a parameter versus other 
four elements are plotted. Considering the Al = 20 at%, each element was fixed while the other 
three elements varied within the composition range of 5 to 35 at%. The atomic percent of fixed 
element was 10%, 20%, and 30%, corresponding to low , medium, and high-concentration, 
respectively.

4. Conclusion 
Non-equiatomic RHEA, W10 Mo 27 Cr 21 Ti 22 Al 20, was designed as optimum composition to achieve 
a solid solution phase with a minimum density and m aximum melting temperature. This alloy 
was prepared by VAM technique and then the phase formation, microstructure, density and 
mechanical properties were studied. The obtained results can be summarized as follows: 
(1) This alloy had lower density and higher melting temperature than that of equiatomic alloy. 
(2) The XRD result showed the BCC solid solution as the major phase with minor Laves phase, 
which was in good agreement with the results of empirical parameters and CALPHAD. 
(3) The alloy had a typical dendritic structure consist s of dendrites enriched with higher-
melting-temperature elements (W and Mo), interdendr ites enriched with other elements and 
the Laves phase enriched with Cr and Ti inside the interdendrite regions. 
(4) The measured lattice parameter and density of the a lloy (3.114 Å and 7.48 g/cm 3) were 
approximately close to the calculated values. Howev er, the measured hardness was found to 
be 511 kg/mm 2 which was signiﬁcantly higher (about four times) t han the calculated 
hardness value.

(5) The alloy exhibited high yield strengths of 1245, 5 10, 201 and 105 at RT, 600°C, 1000°C 
and 1200°C, respectively and no fracture occurred a t high temperature (1000°C and 
1200°C).

Highlights 
- The composition optimization was performed on the W-Mo-Cr-Ti-Al alloy system to 
achieve minimum ρ and maximum Tm. 
- A new non-equiatomic RHEA, W 10 Mo 27 Cr 21 Ti 22 Al 20, was designed as optimal 
composition. 
- The CALPHAD results, in agreement with the experimental results, confirmed the 
formation of BCC solid solution phase. 
- The measured hardness was about four times the calculated hardness of the alloy. 
- The studied refractory HEA has lower density and higher high-temperature yield strength 
than conventional superalloys .
