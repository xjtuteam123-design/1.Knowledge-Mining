Abstract 
 
The mechanical properties of refractory high entropy alloys (RHEAs) strongly depend 
on their phase structures. In this work, the phase stability of a BCC TiNbTa0.5ZrAl0.5 
refractory high entropy alloy subjected to thermomechanical processing was 
evaluated, and the effects of phase decomposition on room/high temperature 
mechanical properties were quantitatively studied. It was found that, the 
thermomechanical processing at 800 °C and 1200 °C leads to phase decomposition in

the TiNbTa0.5ZrAl0.5 alloy. The phase decomposition is caused by the rapid rising of 
free energy of the primary BCC phase. The effect of the precipitates on room 
temperature strength is determined by the competition between the increasing in 
precipitation strengthening and the decreasing in solid solution strengthening . But at 
high temperatures (800-1200 °C), the phase decomposition causes significant 
reduction in strength, mainly due to the grain boundary sliding and the decreasing in 
solid solution strengthening.

1. Introduction 
High entropy alloys (HEAs) consist of multiple principal elements in nearly 
equal atomic-ratio, and usually present simple solid solution phase [1, 2]. Such extended 
solubility may help to avoid the formation of harmful intermetallic phases and 
enhance the mechanical properties such as strength and ductility [2]. Recently, the 
development of refractory high entropy alloys (RHEAs) has attracted much interest s 
due to their outstanding performance at high tempera tures[3, 4]. These alloys are 
primarily composed of high -melting-point elements, typically from group 4 -6 in the 
periodic table, to ensure high s trength at elevated temperature s, along with other 
elements such as Al, to balance some properties (oxidation resistance and low 
density)[5, 6]. Despite the complex composition, RHEAs usually have a single BCC 
structure. For example, the typical WMoTaNb RHEA with BCC structure has high 
strength at temperature above 1000 °C. A potential application for RHEAs is 
aerospace materials. However, four essential properties are desirable for such 
application, i.e. mechanical properties, oxidation resistance, density and phase 
stability[5]. Considering the high density of WMoTaNb alloy (13.8 g cm-3), Senkov et 
al.[7] designed a new TiNbTa0.5ZrAlMo0.5 RHEA with density of 7.4 g cm-3 and 
superior mechanical properties, which has attracted extensive attention . However, we 
found that Mo element causes poor oxidation resistance for the alloy at high 
temperatures. By removing Mo element, we further found that TiNbTa0.5ZrAlx alloys 
show much better oxidation resistance and also have good mechanical properties [8].

To date, there are many studies on improving the mechanical properties and oxidation 
resistance, either by changing the chemical compositions or by tuning the 
microstructures, but few has focused on the phase stability of RHEAs[9, 10]. 
Usually, phase transformation is slow in HEAs due to its sluggish diffusion 
effect[11]. It needs a long annealing time to observe phase decomposition in RHEA s. 
For example, Yurchenko et al.[12, 13] found that annealing at 800 °C for 100 h leads to 
the formation of Nb 2Al-type σ-phase and C15 Laves phase in AlCr xNbTiV and 
NbTiVZr, respectively. But, it is reported that therm omechanical processing (TMP) 
can stimulate phase transformation and induce precipitation in HEAs [14]. RHEAs 
containing low-density elements such as Al, Ti, and Cr, are more likely to have phase 
transformations[15, 16]. For example, Schuh et al. [15] found that TiZrNbHfTa alloy 
decomposes into a BCC (Nb, Ta)-rich phase and HCP (Zr, Hf)-rich precipitates after TMP at 
intermediate temperatures . Senkov et al. [16, 17] found that AlMo 0.5NbTa0.5TiZr alloy 
and its variants could form a disordered BCC matrix and ordered B2 precipitates after 
TMP at 1400 °C. However, the mechanism of the phase transformations was not 
mentioned in their studies. So far, there are three main methods for evaluating the 
phase equilibri um of HEAs, i.e. first principle method, CALPHAD and empirical 
parameters. Empirical parameters are most widely applied to evaluate the structural 
stability and the phase formation of HEAs. But, the prediction by empirical 
parameters is ess entially for the phase equilibri um at high temperatures, where the 
mixing-entropy effect could be fully utilized to enhance the formation of solid 
solutions[18]. Hence, the empirical prediction may be weak at low or intermediate 
temperatures. For instance, phase transformations were found in TiNbTa0.5ZrAl0.5 and 
Hf0.5Nb0.5Ta0.5Ti1.5Zr alloys after TMP, breaking out of the empirical criterions [19, 20]. 
Therefore, the phase stability of RHEAs (especially t hose subjected to TMP) needs to 
be further studied. 
On the other hand, phase stability has significant effects on the mechanical 
properties of RHEAs. Yurchenko et al.[13] found that the phase decomposition and the 
precipitation of intermetallic phases make the Al -Cr-Nb-Ti-V-Zr HEAs brittle. Schuh 
et al.[15] found that the decomposition of the primary BCC phase in TiZrNbHfTa alloy

produces HCP precipitates, and further leading to a high yield strength but a dramatic 
reduction in ductility at room temperature. Stepanov et al.[21] and Schuh et al.[22] found 
that the precipitates can remarkably improve the hardness of the HfNbTaTiZr and 
AlTiVNb alloys. Generally, precipitation of second phase can enhance the strength of 
RHEAs. But , the ductility usually decreases obviously because of the strong 
impediment for dislocation gliding caused by precipitates. Nevertheless, Zhou et al.[23] 
found that the solid solution strengthening is also influenced by precipitation s in 
HEAs, since the precipitation /phase decomposition could decrease the lattice 
distortion in the solid solution p hase. Further, they found the contribution of 
precipitation to strength is non-monotonic. Senkov et al.[24] also found that the tensile 
strength of a phase decomposed HfNbTaTiZr alloy (with BCC precipitates) is lower 
than its single phase counterpart. In summar y, the effects of precipitation on 
mechanical properties of RHEAs may be complex, and so far are not fully understood. 
Therefore, in this work, a TiNbTa0.5ZrAl0.5 RHEA was prepared by powder metallurgy 
(PM) and subjected to TMP at 1500 °C, 1200 °C and 800 °C. The phase equilibrium 
and precipitation behavior of the RHEA at different temperatures were studied , in 
addition, the effects of phase decomposition on both room and high temperature 
compression properties were discussed. 
2. Experimental 
Elemental powders with purities above 9 9.5% and average particle sizes of 18 -42 
μm were used to prepare the TiNbTa0.5ZrAl0.5 alloy through PM method. The powders 
were blended under argon atmosphere, and then underwent cold isostatic pressing and 
vacuum sintering. The details of preparation process have been reported in our 
previous work [19]. The as-sintered TiNbTa 0.5ZrAl0.5 alloy has a single BCC 
structure[19]. Cylindrical samples with a diameter of 30 mm and a height of 45 mm 
were machined from the as-sintered compacts, and then encapsulated in stainless steel 
cans to avoid severe oxidation and potential fracture during hot forging. The samples 
were then hot forged at 1500 °C, 1200 °C and 800 °C with a total height reduction of 
50%, and subsequently annealed at the co rresponding forging temperature for 30min 
(hereinafter refer to as HEA1500, HEA1200 and HEA800, respectively ). After the

TMP treatments, the stainless steel covering was removed by an electric spark cutting 
machine. 
Compression tests at room temperature were conducted in air on a hydraulic 
testing machine (Instron 8802), while high temperature tests were conducted in 
vacuum ( 5×10-3 Pa) on a dynamic thermal simulation machine (Gleeble -3000). 
Cylindrical specimens used for compression tests were cut from the TMP samples 
with axial direction parallel to the forging direction. The dimension of the specimens 
is 6 mm in diameter and 9 mm in height. All the compression tests were performed at 
a constant strain rate of 0.001 s-1. The specimen surfaces were mechanically polished 
and lubricated by graphite to reduce the friction between the specimen and the 
punches. During high temperature tests, the specimen was heated to the testing 
temperature with a heating rate of 10 °C/s, and soaked at the temperature for 2 min. 
After compression, the deformed specimen was quenched in water to keep the 
microstructural characteristics. The phase analys is was performed by using X-ray 
diffraction (XRD, Advance D8 ) with Cu Kα radiation. Microstructures were observed 
by using a scanning electron microscope (SEM, Helios Nanolab G3 UC) equipped 
with a backscatter electron (BSE) detector and an energy dispersiv e X -ray 
spectroscopy (EDS). The elemental distribution was analyzed by ED S. The details of 
microstructures were studied by using a transmission electron microscope (TEM, 
Titan G2 60-300). Specimens for TEM were prepared by using focused ion beam (FIB) 
apparatus. The micro-hardness was measured by an IBIS Nano-indentation tester.

4. Discussion 
4.1. Phase decomposition behavior 
Though most of the reported RHEAs have single BCC phase structure, they were 
mainly in as-cast status or after the homogenization at high temperatures, in which the 
high entropy effect could help stabilize the solid solution phase[26]. At relative lower 
temperatures, phase decomposition could take place due to the decrease of high 
entropy effect. I n the present work, the TiNbTa 0.5ZrAl0.5 RHEA exhibits different 
phase decomposition behavior , which is dependent on the TMP temperature. When 
subjecting to TMP at 1500 °C, the alloy keeps a single BCC structure. When 
subjecting to TMP at 1200 °C, the primary BCC phase decomposes into a BCC (Ti, Nb, 
Ta)-rich matrix and HCP (Zr, Al)-rich precipitates. When subjecting to TMP at 800 °C, the 
BCC phase turns to decompose into a BCCZr-rich matrix and BCC(Nb, Ta)-rich precipitates. 
The thermodynamic stability of the solid solution phase is associated with Gibbs free 
energy. According to the basic relation, ΔGmix = ΔHmix − TΔSmix, the effect of entropy 
becomes the main factor as the temperature is a multiplier of entropy. And thus, the 
free energy of the alloy with high entropy increases more rapidly than those with 
relative lower entropy, which makes the primary solid solution phase instable at low 
or intermediate temperatures. To evaluate the phase stability of the presen t 
TiNbTa0.5ZrAl0.5 alloy, the total free energy was considered as the sum of the free

phase constitution s, which may be metastable at the present TMP status (especially 
for the HEA800). That means, though HEA800 have BCC + BCC2 phase structure, it

annealing at 800 °C. In our previous work [31], we found that, the TiNbTa 0.5ZrAl0.5 
alloy with BCC + HCP structure could maintain HCP (Zr, Al)-rich phase after annealing at 
800℃, indicating the HCP phase is stable at 800 °C. 
It should be noted that the hot forging also affected the phase decomposition 
behavior. On the one hand, the forging led to the generation of deformation heat, 
which could influence the total free energy. The deformation heat is significant when

## Page 13

the strain rate is high[32]. In this study, the strain rate during hot forging is about 0.1 s-1. 
The temperature rising caused by deformation heat, determined by the Goetz’s 
model[32], is about 36 -158 °C at forging temperatures of 800 -1500 °C. The 
temperature rising helps stabilize the primary solid solution phase. However, since the 
temperature rising is much lower than the forging temperature, the effect of 
deformation heating on free energy should be li mited. On the other hand, the hot 
forging can stimulate phase decomposition in HEAs [14, 33]. Firstly, during the hot 
forging, the dislocation density increased rapidly. The composition fluctuation caused 
by dislocation segregation may induce the decline of local configurational entropy 
and stimulate the destabilization of the primary solid solution phase [14]. Secondly, 
grains were refined after the forging, resulting in a large number of grain boundaries. 
The grain boundaries serve as fast diffusion pathways and preferential nucleation sites 
for the formation of new phases [33]. Thirdly, the hot forging led to high interfacial 
energy and strain energy. The stored energies also stimulate phase transformation in 
HEAs. Note that the precipitates in HEA800 show different characteristics in 
intergranular and intragranular regions. This may be due t o the earlier nucleation and 
faster growth rate of the precipitates in grain boundaries , caused by the decline of 
local configurational entropy and the stimulation of atomic motion . Therefore, the 
intergranular precipitates are coarser than the intragranular precipitates, further 
verifying that the precipitation can be promoted by hot deformation. 
4.2. Room temperature mechanical properties 
For conventional alloys, precipitation strengthening is a very important 
strengthening mechanism. Basically, precipitation leads to improvement in strength 
but sacrifice in ductility. During plastic deformation, precipitates act as barriers for 
dislocation motion either through dislocation by -pass mechanism or particle shearing 
mechanism. The impediment of dislocation motion causes increasing in dislocation 
density, resulting in higher strength and lower ductility. However, for HEAs, the 
effects of precipitation on mechanical properties may be multiple. On the one hand, 
the precipitat es cause precipitation hardening , which is similar to the conventional 
alloys and can lead to remarkable enhancement in strength [34, 35]. On the other hand, 
Journal Pre-proof

## Page 14

the precipitation /phase decomposition may reduce the lattice d istortion, and further 
causing decreasing in solid solution strengthening. Therefore, the effect s of 
precipitation on strength of HEAs should be the competition of precipitation 
strengthening and solid solution strengthening. In this work, the multiple impacts of 
precipitation were found obvious in the phase-decomposed TiNbTa0.5ZrAl0.5 alloys, as 
shown in Fig. 9(a). The precipitation of HCP phase (HEA1200) leads to higher 
strength, while the precipitation of BCC phase (HEA800) leads to lower on e,

4.3. High temperature mechanical properties 
So far, the effects of precipitation/phase decomposition on mechanical properties 
of RHEAs were mostly studied at room temperature [42, 43], but the impacts at high 
temperatures are also important. At high temperatures, grain boundary sliding can 
strongly affect the strength of RHEAs, since it causes significant reduction in flow 
stress during high temperature deformation [44, 45]. Generally, grain boundary sliding 
occurs as a dominant deformation mechanism when strain -rate sensitivity is larger 
Journal Pre-proof

## Page 17

than 0.3. Rajeshwar et al. [45, 46] found that , TiNbTaZrHf RHEA deformed at 
1000-1200 °C and 0.0001 -0.01 s-1 has a high strain -rate sensitivity of 0.33. Fine 
grains were found around grain boundaries. Grain boundary sliding was demonstrated 
to be the dominant deformation mechanism in the fine -grained region. The flow 
behavior of the present TiNbTa0.5ZrAl0.5 RHEA is similar to the TiNbTaZrHf RHEA . 
The deformed microstructures both contain fine grains with grain size of ~1μm on the 
grain boundaries , which can act as a lubricant layer for grain boundary sliding . 
Besides, the porosity in the grain boundaries (Fig. 11(b)), indicates an intensive grain 
boundary deformation[46]. Since the microstructures of HEA1200 and HEA800 are 
much finer than HEA1500, the grain boundary sliding should be stronger, resulting in 
low high-temperature strength. Besides, the effect of precipitates on high temperature 
deformation should be considered. Usually, rigid, fine and dispersive precipitates 
could improve high temperature strength, because the precipitates can help pin grain 
boundary, making it difficult to slide [47]. However, as for the present HEA1200, the 
HCP precipitates show limited pinning eff ect for grain boundary sliding. The phase 
decomposition produced extensive fine grains surrounding the HCP precipitates (see 
Fig. 11(d)), which act as lubricant layer between the precipitates and the matrix, 
making the grain boundary sliding easy. This could also account for the relative low 
strength of HEA1200 at high temperatures. Moreover , solid solution strengthening 
may also have an effect on high temperature strength of HEAs. Senkov et al.[44] found 
that, in a Al 10Nb15Ta5Ti30Zr40 RHEA, the solid solution strengthening contribute s 56% 
to the yield strength at 800 °C, while this ratio increases to 83% at 1200 °C. In 
RHEAs, the configurational entropy may reduce significantly when precipitation or 
phase decomposition occur, and further may cause decreasing in the solid solution 
strengthening[36]. In this work, HEA1500 maintains almost the single BCC phase, but, 
the precipitations of HCP (Zr, Al)-rich phase in HEA1200 and BCC (Nb, Ta)-rich phase in 
HEA800 occur, and thus lowering the solid solution strengthening . This could also 
account for the decreased yield strength in HEA1200 and HEA800. 
5. Conclusions 
(1) TMP at intermediate temperatures leads to phase decomposition in 
Journal Pre-proof

## Page 18

TiNbTa0.5ZrAl0.5 RHEA. The primary BCC phase decomposes into a BCC (Ti, Nb, 
Ta)-rich matrix and HCP(Zr, Al)-rich precipitates at 1200 °C, which turns to a BCCZr-rich 
matrix and BCC (Nb, Ta)-rich precipitates at 800 °C. The phase decomposition is 
caused by the rapid raising in free energy of the primary BCC phase at 
intermediate temperatures. The hard HCP prec ipitates make the HEA1200 
stronger but more brittle (240 MPa increasing in yield strength and 4% 
decreasing in plasticity), while the soft BCC precipitates make the HEA800 softer 
but more ductile (190 MPa decreasing in yield strength and 12% increasing in 
plasticity). 
(2) The decomposition of the BCC phase induces precipitation strengthening but 
causes decreasing in solid solution strengthening. Under the competition of the 
precipitation strengthening and the solid solution strengthening , HEA1200 
exhibits higher strength, while HEA800 shows lower one compared with 
HEA1500. 
(3) At high temperatures, the grain boundary sliding and the decreasing in solid 
solution strengthening caused by the phase decomposition are responsible for the 
low strength of HEA1200 and HEA800.
