Modulating the prestrain history to optimize strength and ductility in
CoCrFeMnNi high-entropy alloy

CoCrFeMnNi high-entropy alloy (HEA) exhibits excellent combination of strength and ductility, but low yield
strength. In order to ameliorate the mechanical properties, prestrain was applied in this work. The HEA
prestrained at 77 K possesses higher yield strength and uniform elongation than the HEA prestrained at 293 K,
indicating that the trade-off relationship between strength and ductility can be broken by modulating the
prestrain history. Furthermore, the yield point phenomenon was disappeared after prestrained at 77 K. This
can be related to the density and distribution of dislocations as imposed in the prestrain process at 293 K and
77 K.

The strong desire for materials with high strength and good ductility
in industrial applications is driving the development of new concepts in
alloy design. In recent years, a new type of equiatomic or near equiatomic
multicomponent alloys named as high-entropy alloys (HEAs) intrigue a
new strategy in developing alloys [1–4]. Due to the ‘high-entropy effect’,
some simple solid solutions with face-centered cubic (FCC), body-
centered cubic (BCC), or hexagonal close-packed (HCP) structures, are
usually formed in HEAs [ 5–10]. Among the HEA systems, the HEAs
with FCC structure were widely studied because of the attractive proper-
ties, such as dynamic properties, fracture toughness and corrosion resis-
tance [ 11–15]. However, the yield strength (YS) of HEAs with FCC
structure is insuf ﬁciently strong (below ∼400 MPa at 293 K [ 12]),
which hinders the wide practical application of the materials.
In order to increase the YS of the HEAs with FCC structure, many ef-
forts were conducted, such as grain re ﬁnement, precipitation and het-
erogeneous structure [ 16–21]. The YS of CoCrFeMnNi HEA increased
with decreasing grain size, achieving an increase of ~200 MPa when
the grain size was decreased from 155 to 4.4 μm[ 16]. By introducing
high-content ductile coherent nanoprecipitates in FCC-based HEAs, a
high tensile strength of 1.9 GPa and a good ductility were achieved in
the Al
0.5Cr0.9FeNi2.5V0.2 HEA [19]. A heterostructured Fe29Ni29Co28Cu7Ti7
alloy with a multi-modal grain size distribution exhibits excellent ductil-
ity and high work-hardening ability [ 20]. In our previous work, a fully
recrystallized ultraﬁne-grained (UFG; grain size d:1 0 0n m b d b 1 μm)
structure was introduced in the CoCrFeMnNi HEA, and a high YS of
888 MPa with a uniform elongation (UE) 21% was obtained [17,22].
Besides the above-mentioned strategies, imposing prestrain is efﬁ-
cient to strengthen materials [ 23–25]. However, while prestrain ef ﬁ-
ciently enhances the YS, ultimate tensile strength (UTS) and high-
cycle fatigue strength, it also deteriorates the ductility [23,25]. Actually,
the mechanical properties of alloys depend on their internal microstruc-
tures and external working conditions. In the previous work, a partially
recrystallized HEA with limited UE at 293 K can exhibit considerable UE
and high YS at 77 K [ 26]. Inspired by the improvement of mechanical
properties at 77 K, here we report a method to enhance the YS and
avoid the deterioration of ductility of CoCrFeMnNi HEA by imposing
prestrain at 77 K. The CoCrFeMnNi HEA showed ultra-high YS of
1.07 GPa with a considerable UE of 15.3% at 293 K.
CoCrFeMnNi HEAs were prepared by magnetic levitation melting
technique. The cast ingot was ﬁrstly hot forged into rods with 30 mm
in diameter, and was further cold rolled into sheets 1 mm in thickness.
Then the UFG specimens were obtained by annealing at 998 K for 5 min
(named as ORI). Details of the processing procedures were reported in
the previous work [ 17]. Tensile specimens with a gauge length of
10 mm, a width of 2 mm and a thickness of 1 mm were ﬁrstly
prestrained on an Instron 5982 testing machine at a crosshead speed

of 10% at room temperature (named as PRT) and liquid nitrogen temper-

specimens were ground using a 2000 grit SiC polishing paper and further
tensile tested with a crosshead speed of 0.6 mm/min at 293 K until frac-

channeling contrast imaging (ECCI) component. A step size of 40 nm
was used during the EBSD measurement. Transmission electron micros-
copy (TEM) characterization was conducted on an FEI Tecnai F20. TEM
foils were cut from the tensile specimens by spark-cutting machine
and ground to a thickness of∼50 μm. Then the foils were further thinned
using a twin-jet electropolishing method by Tenupole-5 in a solution of
70% methanol, 20% glycerine and 10% perchloric acid, and the working
voltage and temperature are 20 V and 253 K.
The ORI HEA with fully recrystallized structure was fabricated in this
study, which exhibits an average grain size of 650 nm, as shown in
Fig. S1. The recrystallized UFG structure is characterized as homoge-
neously equiaxed grains, low-density dislocations and high fraction of
high-angle grain boundaries (HAGBs) [ 17]. The high YS of 800 MPa
was gained in the ORI HEA at 293 K. In order to further improve the
YS of the HEA, a prestrain of 10% was applied to HEA specimens at
293 K and 77 K, respectively, and the stress-strain curves were given
in Fig. 1(a). A yield point phenomenon was observed at both tempera-
tures, which were suggested to be induced by the lack of mobile
dislocations and difﬁcult activation of dislocation glide in the fully re-
crystallized UFG structure [27]. The stress-strain curves were shown in
Fig. 1(b) and the tensile properties are summarized in Table 1. A yield
point phenomenon was observed in the PRT HEA, but this phenomenon
disappears in the PLN HEA. Furthermore, the PLN HEA shows enhanced
strength and ductility in comparison to the PRT HEA, indicating that the
prestrain history can impact the yielding behavior and subsequent
strain-hardening behavior signiﬁcantly. Here, an exceptional YS above
the order of 1 GPa along with a considerable UE of 15.3% is magniﬁcent
in CoCrFeMnNi HEAs at 293 K. Fig. 1(c) demonstrates the tensile true
stress-strain curves of the PLN and PRT HEAs before necking. The corre-
sponding strain-hardening curves are shown inFig. 1(d). It is generally
acknowledged that the strain-hardening capacity of the prestrained
materials is limited [28]. In this study, the PLN HEA exhibits enhanced
strain-hardening capability compared to the PRT HEA, leading to a syn-
chronous increase in strength and ductility. Furthermore, the strain-
hardening rate decreases at ﬁrst and then rises sharply in the PRT
HEA, which could not be observed in the PLN HEA. This can be related
to the yield point phenomenon, which occurred in the PRT HEA while
disappeared in the PLN HEA.
The grain boundary (GB) map, kernel average misorientation (KAM)
and corresponding misorientation angle distributions of both PRT and
PLN HEAs are shown in Fig. 2. A large number of annealing twins were
found in the PRT and PLN HEAs. Moreover, a slightly higher fraction of
low-angle grain boundaries (LAGBs) is formed in PLN HEA than that in
PRT HEA, as shown in Fig. 2(e). Local misorientations in Fig. 2(c) and
(d) are closely associated with the geometrically necessary dislocations
(GNDs), which are formed to accommodate the lattice distortion caused
by the mismatch of active slip systems among neighboring regions of in-
dividual grains [29]. A high density of GNDs was observed near the GBs,

action of dislocations are activated in the grains after applying a strain of
10% on the ORI HEA, i.e. PRT and PLN HEAs, leading to an increase of the
YS relative to the unstrained state. Furthermore, compared to PRT HEA,
the distribution of GNDs was more uniform in the PLN HEA, and the PLN
HEA also showed a higher peak KAM value (KAM = 0.35) than that of
the PRT HEA (KAM = 0.28), as shown in Fig. 2(f). However, due to the
resolution limit of EBSD, the veryﬁne microstructures (i.e. deformation
twins) cannot be clearly characterized. Defect patterns in prestrained
HEAs were further characterized by TEM, as shown in Fig. S2. In the
PRT HEA, the dislocation density increases remarkably in contrast to
the ORI HEA. No deformation twin could be detected, and the deforma-
tion is fully accommodated by dislocation glide, as shown in Fig. S2
(a) and (b). In contrast, the ECCI image reveals that some deformation
twins (yellow arrows) appeared in the PLN HEA, as shown in Fig. S2
(c). However, most grains are still featured with high-density disloca-
tions. In general, the occurrence of deformation twinning is related to
the ﬂow stress and critical twinning stress [22]. According to the calcu-
lated critical twinning stress of ~1.35 GPa of CoCrFeMnNi HEA with
grain size of 650 nm [30], deformation twins could be activated at an en-
gineering strain of 10% (ﬂow stress of 1.41 GPa) at 77 K.
Typical microstructures of the PRT and PLN HEAs after tensile tests at
293 K are summarized in Fig. 3 . Dislocation interaction and tangled
structure in the grains were observed in PRT HEA, as shown in Fig. 3
(a). This high-density dislocation storage can be attributed to the re-
strained dislocation recovery and planar dislocation slip modes in this
HEA with low SFE [ 22]. Some dislocation cells (green arrows) could
also be detected in the annealing twins, as shown in Fig. 3(b) and (c).
The strain-hardening in the PRT HEA mainly originated from the ef ﬁ-
cient accommodation of dislocations. In Fig. 3(d), profuse dislocations
and several deformation twins (yellow arrows) can be observed in the
ECCI image of PLN HEA. More dislocations accumulated in the grains
in PLN HEA compared to the PRT HEA due to the temperature effect,
as shown in Fig. 3(b) and (e). The above-mentioned EBSD and TEM im-
ages indicate that dislocations play a signi ﬁcant role during the defor-
mation process. According to the calculation in the Supplementary
data, the densities of the prestored dislocations in the PRT and PLN
HEAs are 2.56 × 10 15 m−2 and 3.39 × 10 15 m−2, respectively, which
matches well with the KAM values in Fig. 2(f). Furthermore, the dislo-
cation densities of the PLN and PRT HEA after the tensile tests are
17.7 × 10 15 m−2 and 7.76 × 10 15 m−2, respectively, indicating that
the high-density and homogeneous distributed dislocations in PLN
HEA can induce stronger accommodation of dislocations and resul-
tant strain-hardening rate during the tensile test at 293 K.
Interestingly, the obvious yield point phenomenon occurred in the
ORI sample was weakened in the PRT sample, and disappeared in the
PLN sample. This could be explained as follows: The recrystallized UFG
grains are clean inside and nearly no dislocations could be detected
[17]. In that case, high stress is required to activate dislocations, then a
lower stress is enough to glide these activated dislocations, exhibiting
the yield point phenomenon [31]. In the PRT HEA, some prestored mo-
bile dislocations can be activated at the beginning of tensile loading,

Effects of the prestrain history on the strength and ductility in
CoCrFeMnNi HEA are shown in Fig. 4(a). In comparison with the ORI
HEA, apparent trade-off relationship between strength and ductility is
induced by the prestrain treatments at 293 K and 77 K. However, the
routes are totally different. As a result, both the YS and UE of the PLN
HEA are larger than the PRT HEA, leading to the optimized strength
and ductility by simply modulating the prestrain history. The data of
YS versus UE of CoCrFeMnNi HEAs collected from the previous and the
present results are plotted together in Fig. 4 (b). It is found that the
PLN HEA exhibits one of the highest YS (1.07 GPa) among all reported
CoCrFeMnNi HEAs while retaining a good UE (15.3%). Therefore, it is a
feasible way to modulate the prestrain history to optimize the mechan-
ical properties in order to meet the strong desire for materials with high
strength and good ductility in industrial applications. Recently, multi-
phase HEAs have been designed to explore superior mechanical
properties [37,38]. The strategy of prestrain at 77 K could be applica-
ble to these multiphase HEAs since the strength and ductility of FCC,
BCC and HCP alloys could be improved with decreasing temperature
[39].
In summary, the present study reveals that the trade-off relationship
of YS and UE of CoCrFeMnNi HEA can be substantially broken by prop-
erly modulating the prestrain history. The PLN HEA prestrained at
77 K possesses high YS of 1.07 GPa and UE of 15.3%, which are larger
than the YS of 1 GPa and UE of 10.3% of the PRT HEA prestrained at
293 K. In essence, imposing prestrain at 77 K introduces higher density
and more uniform distribution of dislocations than imposing prestrain
at 293 K, leading to an ef ﬁcient capture of dislocations and resultant
strong strain-hardening capability during the tensile test process. Fur-
thermore, the yield point phenomenon disappeared in the PLN HEA
due to the prestored high-density dislocations. The current work intro-
duced a feasible strategy to optimize the mechanical properties by mod-
ulating the prestrain history to alter the dislocation distribution, which
impact signiﬁcantly on the yielding and subsequent strain-hardening
behavior.
