properties of refractory NbTaWMo high-entropy alloys

ABSTRACT
To improve the high-temperature strength and decrease the density of
NbTaWMo alloys, addition of light element Si producing the second-phase
silicide is employed. Refractory NbTaWMoSi x (x = 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75) high-en-
tropy alloys are produced by spark plasma sintering. The phase evolution,
microstructure, compressive mechanical properties, and high-temperature
hardness are investigated in this study. It reveals that there is only a disordered
body-centered cubic (BCC) phase in the matrix NbTaWMo alloy. After adding
the Si element, NbTaWMoSi x alloys demonstrate the presence of multiphase
structure: disordered BCC phase and silicide phase. As the content of Si is
increased, the proportion of silicide is increased, which improves the hardness
and strength. The addition of Si at certain concentration ( x = 0.25 and 0.5) has
positive effect on ductility of the alloys. The alloys all demonstrate brittle frac-
ture due to the brittle phase of BCC and silicide phase. The high-temperature
hardness of NbTaWMoSi x alloys is increased with the addition of Si, but the
same alloy presents a slightly decreasing phenomenon as the temperature
improves.
Introduction
With the rapid development of gas turbines and
airplane engines technologies, new advanced mate-
rials with excellent mechanical properties remain to
be explored urgently to meet with high-temperature
applications in future. Comparing the conventional
alloy materials, high-entropy alloys (HEAs) with
excellent mechanical properties and superior ele-
vated temperature resistance were put forward [ 1–3].
HEAs easily form simple solid solution [ 4–6], which
is applied into the refractory metal V, Zr, Nb, Mo, Hf,
Ta, W, Re with high melting temperature to produce
refractory HEAs with particular properties at ele-
vated temperatures [ 7, 8]. Several refractory HEA
systems have been successfully developed, which
includes NbTiZr-based alloys [ 9], AlCrNbTiVZr [ 10],
ZrNbTiVHf [11], Hf0.5Nb0.5Ta0.5Ti1.5Zr [12], HfMo0.5-
NbTiV0.5Six [13], TiZrNbMoV [ 14], etc. These refrac-
tory HEAs exhibit high strength and hardness, good

In addition, most reported refractory HEAs have a
single-phase body-centered cubic (BCC) structure
[11, 18]. Although the body-centered cubic (BCC)
phase possesses high strength, novel refractory HEAs
with extensive strength are to be developed to
respond the critical demand. Accounting for the
limitation of BCC phase strengthening, to improve
the strength of refractory HEAs, other strengthening
mechanisms including solid solution [ 19] and the
second-phase strengthening [ 20, 21] should be
employed. Inspired by the investigations of niobium
silicide-based alloys [ 22–25], Nb-Si-based composites
possess a balance of several important properties,
including low density, high melting points, low-
temperature ductility/toughness, high-temperature
strength/creep resistance, microstructural stability,
and oxidation resistance, which can be applied in
refractory HEAs to improve strength through Nb-Si
compounds. At the same time, a moderate volume
fraction of the second phase also contributes to the
properties of alloys.
To date, there are many experiment techniques to
synthesis refractory HEAs, and arc melting is the
most widely used method [ 26, 27]. But composition
segregation, inhomogeneous dendrite microstruc-
ture, and limited shape and size are the shortcomings
of the alloys by arc melting. However, spark plasma
sintering (SPS) is a ﬁeld-assisted sintering technique,
which is the combination of plasma activation,
resistance heating, and hot-pressed sintering [ 28, 29].
The microstructure of the HEAs alloys by SPS has
high destiny, is homogenous, and shows no compo-
sition segregation.
In view of these aspects, we add the Si into the
NbTaWMo alloys to fabricate NbTaWMoSi
x (x value
in molar ratio, x = 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75) refractory high-
entropy alloys with different Si content ranging
between 6 and 16 at.% in the present study. The
combination of the BCC phase and intermetallic sili-
cide phase improves the strength and decreases the
density. The inﬂuence of different Si content on the
phase constitutes, microstructure evolution, and
mechanical characterization are examined. From the
investigation of different addition of Si in the
NbTaWMoSi
x, it is suggested that the materials pos-
sess better microstructure and properties, which
contributes to further design the composition.
Materials and methods
The bulk NbTaWMoSi x refractory HEAs were pre-
pared by spark plasma sintering (SPS). The raw ele-
ments Nb, Ta, W, Mo and Si had purities higher than
99.9 wt%. Planetary mixer was carried out in plastic
vials at a speed of 150 rad/min for up to 10 h with a
1:1 ball-to-powder ratio. In order to fabricate rounded
specimens, the mixed raw HEA powders were ﬁlled
in a graphite die and sintered in a SPS-20 T-10 fur-
nace (Shanghai Chen Hua Technology Co., China)
under vacuum. The samples were heated from room
temperature (RT) to 1420 /C176C with a sintering pressure
of 30 MPa at a heating rate of 50 /C176C/min for 10 min,
and then were sintered at 1700 /C176C for 20 min, fol-
lowed by furnace cooling.
The microstructure and crystal structure were
analyzed by X-ray diffraction (XRD, D/MAX-2400)
and scanning electron microscope (SEM, JSM-5600)
equipped with back scatter electron (BSE) detectors
and energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS). The
density of the alloy was measured by the Archi-
medes’ method. The compressive tests at room tem-
perature were performed on a CMT5202 materials
testing machine (Shenzhen Sans Material Test
Instrument Co., China) with a strain rate of
1.6 9 10
-3 s-1. The compressive specimens with
nominal dimension of U 3m m 9 6 mm were cut
from the SPSed samples. The high-temperature
compressive tests were conducted on Gleeble 3500
Test Machine, and the dimensions of the compressive
specimens were U 8m m 9 10 mm. Both the com-
pressive tests were carried out at least three times,
and the average values were reported. High-tem-
perature hardness test were conducted at 200 /C176C,
400 /C176C, 600 /C176C and 800 /C176C by HTV-PHS30 Archi-
medes Britain.

Results and discussion
Phase constitutes
Figure 1 shows the typical X-ray diffraction patterns
of as-mixed NbTaWMoSi x HEAs powders for 10 h.
The NbTaWMo powder includes diffraction patterns
of all alloying element Nb, Ta, W, and Mo. By adding
the Si element in the NbTaWMo matrix, new
diffraction peaks of Si are observed, and the intensity
of Si element increased with the more addition of Si.
There is no other element diffraction identiﬁed,
which indicates that the mixed powers are without
impurities. In addition, the mixed powders did not
occur mechanical alloying during mixing process.
Figure 2 shows the X-ray diffraction patterns of the
SPSed NbTaWMoSi
x alloys. In the alloy NbTaWMo,
only a single BCC phase is observed. However, as the
Si added into the matrix alloy, the results demon-
strate the presence of multiphase structure: disor-
dered BCC phase and silicide phase. In the
NbTaWMoSi
0.25 alloys, alongside with the reﬂections
from BCC lattice, weaker peaks of silicide phase are
observed. As the content of Si is increased to x = 0.5
and x = 0.75, signiﬁcant silicide phase and BCC
phase are conﬁrmed. The volume friction of silicide
increases with the increase in Si which contributes to
the intensity of silicide phase. However, in the
NbTaWMoSi
x alloys, silicide phase is lower than that
of the BCC phase, which indicates that the matrix
may play a dominate role over the silicide phase in
the alloys [20]. There no Si peak has been identiﬁed in
the NbTaWMoSi
x alloys from Fig. 2, which indicates
that a complete reaction has occurred. Based on the
Bragg’s law, the lattice constants of the peaks of the
NbTaWMoSi
x matrix are calculated accurately: 3.231,
3.227, 3.227 and 3.212 A˚, respectively, which result in
the peaks of the matrix BCC phase slightly shifting
rightward with the increase in Si. The reduction in
lattice constant mainly attribute to the consumption
of relatively big radius of Nb and Ta atoms [ 14].

Mechanical properties
Compressive engineering stress vs. engineering
strain curves for the NbTaWMoSi x alloys at room
temperature are shown in Fig. 4, and typical com-
pression mechanical properties are present in
Table 3. The NbTaWMo alloy shows a high yield
stress of 1217 MPa, which is higher than the reported
NbTaWMo alloys by vacuum arc melting [ 17]. As the
content of Si keeps on increasing from x = 0.25 to
x = 0.75, the yield strengths of refractory HEAs rise to
1826, 1883, and 2483 MPa, respectively. Apparently,
the strength of the NbTaWMoSi
x alloys improves
signiﬁcantly with the addition of Si element. From the
microstructure analysis, the volume of silicide
increases with the Si content. As we known, Nb
5Si3
intermetallic compound has excellent speciﬁc
mechanical properties, which indicates that the sili-
cide, especially Nb
5Si3 compound, has a positive
effect on the strength improvement. The silicide
segments the matrix and possesses a strong con-
straint on it [ 13]. In the process of deformation, the
dislocation movement is inhibited by the silicide and
piles at the interfaces, which largely enhances the
strength. Another strengthening mechanism is solid
solution hardening of matrix BCC phase. As the
increase in the Si addition, the Si consumes the Nb
and Ta elements forming the silicide, which decrea-
ses the lattice parameters of matrix.
Figure 5 shows the compressive stress–strain
curve of NbTaWMoSi
0.25 HEA at 1200 /C176C. The
NbTaWMoSi0.25 HEA has excellent yield stress of
926 MPa and high fracture strain of 13.11% at
1200 /C176C. The silicide phase has positive effect on the
increase in yield strength at high temperature.
Compared with the results at RT, the yield stress
exhibits a decrease, but the fracture strain increases,
which are due to the high-temperature softening.
The fracture strain of the HEAs decreases when the
Si content increases from 0.25 to 0.75. But the fracture
strain of the NbTaWMoSi
0.25 and NbTaWMoSi 0.5
alloys are higher than the NbTaWMo alloy, which are
attribute to the microstructures and components of
the HEAs. Firstly, in the sintering process, Si element
has a lower melting temperature of 1414 /C176C, and the
liquid phase sintering occurs above 1414 /C176C which
enhances reaction between powders and increases
the density by improving the diffusion rate [ 30].
Therefore, compared to the NbTaWMo alloy, the
microstructures of NbTaWMoSi
x eliminate the pores
and other defects. The microstructures of the
NbTaWMoSi
x are homogeneous and pyknotic which
resolve the stress concentration and the mechanical
properties improvement. Another reason may be the
large interfacial strength between BCC phase and
silicide phase [ 23, 24]. But silicide content has its
drawback, which has low fracture toughness and
strain at room temperature [ 20]. As the volume fric-
tion of silicide increases, local deformation is not
coordinated, which result in the stress concentration
and reduce the ductility.
The facture surfaces of NbTaWMoSi
x alloys after
bending test are shown in Fig. 6. They all demon-
strate typical brittle cleavage fracture due to the fact
that planer facets and riverlike patterns can be iden-
tiﬁed in fracture surfaces which are the typical
cleavage fracture morphology [ 4]. There are indica-
tions that pores appear in fracture, but it is not the
main reason that initiates fracture. In the NbTaWMo
alloy, BCC phase has its intrinsic brittle feature which
is the main reason of fracture. As to the NbTaWMoSi
x
(x = 0.25, 0.5, 0.75) alloys, not only BCC phase but
also Nb5Si3 phase effects the brittle fracture.
Density and high-temperature hardness
The densities of the produced refractory NbTaWMoSix
(x = 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75) alloys are 13.44, 12.92, 12.65 and
12.23 g/cm3, respectively. Element Si has lower den-
sity than other refractory element Nb, Ta W and Mo.
Thus Si addition has a promoting effect on the den-
sities decrease which is one way to optimize the
density of refractory HEAs.

Conclusion
In this study, the refractory NbTaWMoSi x high-en-
tropy alloys were successfully produced by SPS from
the mixed metal powder. The microstructure and
mechanical properties were characterized and dis-
cussed. Based on obtained results and analysis, the
conclusions are obtained as follows:
1. The NbTaWMo alloy comprised only one BCC
phase, while the NbTaWMoSi
x (x = 0.25, 0.5, 0.75)
were composed of a disordered BCC phase and a
silicide phase (Nb, Ta)
5Si3, and the volume
friction of silicide phase increased with the
increase in Si element.
2. The HEAs possessed excellent strength and
hardness as a result of the BCC phase and silicide
phase, and the strength improved as the addition
of Si. On the other hand, the plasticity of the
alloys decreased with more silicide phase which
resulted in the brittle fracture.
3. The yield strength of NbTaWMoSi
0.25 alloy was
926 MPa at 1200 /C176C. The high-temperature hard-
ness of NbTaWMoSi x alloys from RT to 800 /C176C
increased with more addition of Si although the
hardness of the same alloy decreased slightly
when the temperature was increasing. In addi-
tion, the densities of NbTaWMoSi
x alloys were
decreased.
Combined with the microstructures, compressive
mechanical properties, ductility, and high temperature
properties, NbTaWMoSi
0.5 was the optimized alloy in
the NbTaWMoSix alloys for further investigation.
