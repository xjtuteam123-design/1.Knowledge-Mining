HIGH-TEMPERATURE DUCTILITY OF NICKEL ALLOYS TYPE 
KhN55MBYu WITH A HIGH IRON CONTENT 
N. A. Sorokina and V. I. Gal'tsova UDC 669.018.8:620.172.251.225 
In order to prepare stamped and welded structures which operate in the temperature range from 750 to 
-253°C there is use of high-strength dispersion-hardening nickel alloys KhN55MBYu (EP666) and 
KhN58MB YuD (F~K61) which exhibit resistance to hot crack formation during welding and cracking of welded 
joints during deformation. Alloys of this O'pe have a high level of strength (af = 1300 N/mm 2 at 20°C) and 
toughness at co, ogenic tetnperatures, and they, have high resistance to plastic deformation in the temperature 
range for hot pressing especially in the cast condition. In order to find the optimum forging and stamping 
schedules for these alloys features of the change in their structure and properties in relation to the forming 
temperature have been studied. 
Three test alloys melted in the experimental plant at TsNIIChERMET were studied (see Table 1). 
The strengthening mechanism for the test alloys is different. Alloy 1 strengthens as a result of precipitation during aging 
of intermetallic ,,/'-phase type Ni3(Nb, A1) in an amount up to 7%. The structural transformations in alloy 2 are more complex. 
As can be seen from the curves presented in Fig. 1" in alloy 2 three phases are precipitated from supersaturated ,y-solid 
solution: 13-phase (Ni3Nb) with a rhombic lattice, stable ",/'-phase with a FCC-lattice, and metastable tetragonal -),"-phase. 
Precipitates of equilibrium i3-phase have a lamellar shape (Fig. 2a), y'-phase is spherical in shape (Fig. 2b), and y"-phase is 
disk-shaped. 
The limit for /3-phase precipitation from y-solid solution with short exposures corresponds to -900°C. With an 
increase in aging time to 50 h the region for 13-phase existence expands to 1000°C. 
The temperature range for hot working of alloy 1 is in the single-phase -),-region which excludes the possibility of 
obtaining a microduplex structure. In alloy 2 it is possible to obtain a microduplex structure in the two-phase "),+B-region if 
prolonged soaking is performed at 900°C followed by hot forming at this temperature. However, the rate of B-phase dissolution 
is quite high: the two-phase structure obtained as a result of treatment at 900°C is retained with heating to 1000°C only for 
lh. 
It is established (Fig. 3) that with heating above 1000°C the ductility characteristics of cast alloy 2 are markedly 
reduced. The ductility of alloy 1 increases uniformly with heating to 1200°C. The strength level for alloy 1 is higher than for 
alloy 2, especially in the temperature range 900-1100°C. 
In order to study the high-temperature properties of alloys turned ingots 60-65 mm in diameter were formed in a 
horizontal press with a force of 15000 kN with reduction n = 5. The heating temperature for ingots before pressing was varied 
from I000 to 1120°C. Ingots were worked in the cast condition and after heat treatment by the schedule: stepwise annealing 
at 1150°C for 3 h, cooling in the furnace to 900°C, soaking for 1 h, and cooling at a rate Vcooi = 30°C/h.

Determination of the quantitative characteristics of the difference in metal grain size, i.e., the coefficient of variation, 
showed that after pressing ingots in the original condition at 1120 and 1000°C this index is 0.92 and 0.70 respectively. After 
heat treatment and pressing of ingots the coefficient of variation is reduced to 0.85 and 0.55 respectively. 
The average grain size of alloys after pressing depends to a greater extent on the working temperature than on the ingot 
condition. The least average grain size dg = 10-12 #m occurs for alloys after heat treatment and pressing at 1000°C. 
Alloy mechanical properties were studied after rolling cast billets 25 mm thick into sheet 6 mm thick at 1040°C (e > 
70%). Rolled sheets were heated to 850°C (alloy 1) and 930°C (alloy 2) with soaking for 1 h and water cooling. The 
temperatures 930 and 850°C correspond to the start of -y'- and/3-phase precipitation and they were chosen by proceeding from 
the structural diagram for phase transformations (see Fig. 1). 
For alloy 1 (3,'-phase dissolution temperature -900°C) an increase in hardness from 190 to 220 HV is observed after 
soaking at 850°C for 2 h. As a result of soaking alloys at these temperatures there is formation of a single-phase V-solid 
solution structure in a condition of advanced precipitation of the strengthening phases Ni 3 (Nb, AI) Ni3Nb, which were fixed 
by water cooling. Specimens heat treated by these schedules were tensile tested at a rate of I0-1 sec-1 with ttest = 800-1200°C 
and with soaking for 1 h (Fig. 7). 
At 900-950°C the ductility of alloy 2 increases to 65 = 280-290%, which is accompanied by a reduction in of to 38-46 
N/mm 2. Here the average grain size dg was 8-10/~m, particles of 3/'-phase were not observed, and the ")'-solid solution is 
recrystallized. Apparently soaking of metal given considerable working at the temperature for advanced precipitation of excess 
phase promotes an increase in dislocation density and acceleration of their rebuilding during high-temperature tests as a result 
of which generation and growth of new grains is facilitated. 
The nature of the temperature dependence for the ductility of alloy 1 is the same as for alloy 2 but the maximum 65 
is displaced in the direction of higher temperatures. The strength level for alloy 1 is higher than for alloy 2 over the whole test 
temperature range.

CONCLUSIONS 
1. Heat treatment of ingots of alloy types KhN55MBYu and KhN58MBYuD with the use of a homogenizing anneal 
before working markedly reduces the difference in grain size for the 3,-solid solution which promotes an increase in ductility. 
2. An increase in alloy KhN58MBYuD of the Nb/Al ratio leads to an increase in the amount of 3,"-phase and an 
increase in its dissolution temperature to 1100°C; alloy working in the two-phase 3' + 3""-region promotes preparation of a 
superplastic condition. 
3. A high level of high-temperature ductility for commercial alloy KhN58MBYuD (I~K61) is achieved during hot 
forming at 1040-1050°C with a degree of 70%.
