abstract
Fine-grained tungsten (W) alloys with excellent properties were prepared by using advanced spark
plasma sintering (SPS) and combining with Mo and Co multi-element alloy strengthening technology.
The relative density of W alloy increases from 90.68% to 96.22% when it is sintered at a low temperature
range of 1000 /C14 Ce1225 /C14 C. The W alloy prepared at a low sintering temperature exhibits high hardness
and low bending strength. As sintering temperature increases, the hardness of the alloy gradually de-
creases and the bending strength increases. The simultaneous addition of Mo/Co elements can decrease
the liquid-phase sintering temperature of alloys. The prepared W alloy is close to full densi ﬁcation and
the W grains are nearly spherical when sintering temperature is increased to 1250 /C14 C. This observation
reveals the characteristics of suf ﬁcient liquid-phase sintering. The hardness and bending strength of the
alloys at 1250 /C14 C are simultaneously increased relative to those sintered at 1225 /C14 C because of the
sufﬁcient liquid-phase sintering and Mo eCo co-strengthening. Therefore, these alloys exhibit optimum
mechanical properties. The ductile tearing characteristics of binding phase can be seen obviously on the
alloy fracture surface.

1. Introduction
The W eNieFe alloy is commonly used in tungsten (W) alloy
materials. The liquid-phase and solid-phase temperature range of
the W eNieFe alloy is extremely narrow and can lower the pre-
cipitation probability of alloy brittle phase. Thus, the alloy pos-
sesses good tensile strength and toughness and is widely used in
the national defense and civil industry [1e3]. Multi-alloying tech-
nology is an important approach to improve the mechanical
properties of W alloy, especially alloy strengthening [4e9].
Improving the mechanical properties by adding alloying elements
to re ﬁne the W grains is one of the important development di-
rections and research focus regarding W alloys. Several reports
have investigated the addition of a single alloying element. For
example, the addition of a certain amount of molybdenum (Mo)
powder to W eNieFe mixed powders can enhance the hardness,
yield strength, and ultimate tensile strength of the W alloy.
However, the elongation, section shrinkage, and density of this
alloy exhibit varying degrees of decline [10,1 1]. By contrast, the
addition of cobalt (Co) to the W eNieFe alloys results in slightly
ﬁner grain sizes, which may improve the mechanical properties of
the W eNieFe alloys [12]. Thus, the addition of a single alloying
element in W alloy can usually improve the performance only in a
particular aspect. Combining the characteristics of strengthening W
alloy through a single alloying element and developing the research
on co-strengthening W alloy through two or more alloying ele-
ments present one of the important directions for improving the
comprehensive mechanical properties of W alloys.
At present, the main sintering process for the preparation of
ﬁne-grained W alloys is one of the following methods: vacuum
sintering, hot pressing sintering, microwave sintering, and spark
plasma sintering (SPS). Among these methods, SPS is a type of
advanced sintering method that had developed extremely rapidly
in recent years [13,14]. SPS is different from conventional sintering
method or improved conventional sintering method. This unique
discharge-activated sintering method can reduce the sintering
temperature of W alloys and shorten sintering time. Its sintering
temperature is lower and the soaking time is shorter than that of

2. Experimental
The starting powders included W, Mo, Ni, Fe, and Co powders.
The raw powders had a purity (wt.%) higher than 99.5% and a
particle size approximately 1 e3 mm. The powders were mixed at a
mass ratio of 88: 2: 6: 2.5: 1.5 (W: Mo: Ni: Fe: Co). A certain amount
of starting powders was weighed, placed in a stainless steel jar with
a ball-to-powder weight ratio of 10:1, and mixed at 220 rpm in a
LEBM equipment. The material type of milling balls was WC-
cemented carbide balls, and the size of balls was F4mm and
F8mm. After 20 h of blending, a virtually uniform mixture of raw
materials was obtained. About the size and shape of ball-milled
powders, in our previous study, LEBM only plays a role in
mixture of starting powders, and the SEM images of mixed pow-
ders before and after LEBM showed that the particle size of W
powders had no signi ﬁcant change after LEBM [15]. The mixed
powders were ﬁlled in a graphite mold, and eventually sintered by
SPS. The sintering equipment utilized in the experiment was the
SPS LABOXTM-3010 K sintering system. The mixed powders were
sintered for 8 min under a pressure of 50 MPa at 1000 e1250 /C14 C and
a heating rate of 100 /C14 C/min. The samples were cooled to room
temperature and retrieved from the furnace.
The density of W-2Mo-6Ni-2.5Fe-1.5Co alloys was measured

alloys, as well as its bending fracture morphology.
3. Results and discussion
3.1. Effect of sintering temperature on the relative density of W
alloys
The relative density of W-2Mo-6Ni-2.5Fe-1.5Co alloys sintered
by SPS at different temperatures is shown in Fig. 1. The W alloy
sintered at a low temperature of 1000 /C14 C can rapidly undergo
densiﬁcation, and its relative density is 90.68%. At 1 100 /C14 C, the
relative density reaches 93.91% compared with the temperature of
about 1400 /C14 C of signi ﬁcant densi ﬁcation for traditional H 2 atmo-
sphere sintering, and the temperature decreases to approach
300 /C14 C. With the increase in sintering temperature, the relative
density of the alloys shows a consistently increased trend, and it
reaches 96.22% at 1225 /C14 C. Beyond 1225 /C14 C, the relative density
increases markedly, and at the ﬁnal sintering temperature of
1250 /C14 C, the relative density of W alloy is 98.93% and approximates
full densiﬁcation.
From the change trend of relative density in sintered alloys, the
method involving doping elements Mo and Co and sintering by SPS
can markedly enhance the densiﬁcation of W alloys and achieve full
densiﬁcation of alloys at lower sintering temperature. By contrast,
the W alloys, which possess a single Mo-doping, exhibit the
maximum relative density of only 96.42% [16]. As can be seen
through the simultaneous doping of Mo and Co, the ﬁne-grained W
alloys can achieve rapid densi ﬁcation and full densi ﬁcation in the
lower temperature range.
3.2. Microstructural characteristics of W alloys sintered at different
temperatures
Fig. 2 shows the phase changes in the alloys with sintering
temperatures range of 1000 e1225 /C14 C. Below a lower temperature,
the W phase is dominant in all the alloys and supplemented by NiW
and Ni2W4C phases. Here, the occurrence of the Ni 2W4C phase may
be related to the diffusion of carbon derived from graphite die [16].
With sintering temperature increasing from 1000 /C14 Ct o1 1 0 0/C14 C, the
diffraction peak intensities of Ni 2W4C phase reaches the highest
value. However, the Ni 2W4C diffraction peaks almost completely
disappear at above 1 100 /C14 C. The Ni 2W4C is brittle phase and its
disappearance is in favor of improving bending strength of the W
alloys.

We2Moe7Nie3Fe alloy without the added Co only partially un-
dergoes liquid-phase sintering at this temperature. Compared with
the addition of single Mo element, the W grains of 1250 /C14 C sintering
samples by the simultaneous addition of Mo and Co to W, Ni, and Fe
powders become nearly spherical in shape. It means that the liquid
phase sintering was conducted more fully. It can conclude that the
addition of Mo and Co elements simultaneously in WeNieFe mixed
powders can reduce the liquid-phase sintering temperature of W
alloys to a certain extent. On the other hand, in the present study,
the sintering time is 8 min. If the sintering time was further pro-
longed, this will cause the further growth of W grains, which is
probably disadvantageous to the performance of materials. When
the time of liquid phase sintering is increased, although the diffu-
sion of atoms is more fully, the W grains will grow up. To improve
the density of the alloy, the best method is to increase the sintering
pressure, rather than to prolong the liquid phase sintering time.
3.3. Mechanical properties and fracture morphology of the W alloys
Fig. 4 shows the effect of sintering temperature on the Rockwell
hardness and bending strength of W-2Mo-6Ni-2.5Fe-1.5Co alloys.
At the sintering temperature of 1000 /C14 C, the hardness of the alloy is
the highest at 76.14 HRA. During the increase in sintering temper-
ature to 1225 /C14 C, the hardness of the alloy shows a decreasing
trend. Then, the hardness begins to increase and reach 71.43 HRA at
1250 /C14 C. Different from the change trend of hardness, from 1 100/C14 C,
bending strength increases rapidly with the increase in sintering
temperature. The change is extremely rapid from the lowest value
of 595.0 MPa at 1 100
/C14 C to the highest value of 1040.5 MPa at
1250 /C14 C. Fig. 3 illustrates that the alloys are in the solid-phase
sintering or liquid esolid sintering stage below 1225 /C14 C. Thus, the
prepared W alloys exhibit high hardness and low bending strength.
As sintering temperature increases below 1225 /C14 C, the hardness
and bending strength display different variation trends ( Fig. 4 ).
Thus, the W-2Mo-6Ni-2.5Fe-1.5Co alloys are characterized by high
hardness and high strength, which cannot be obtained at 1000 /C14 C-
1225 /C14 C. As sintering temperature is increased from 1225 /C14 Ct o
1250 /C14 C, the alloy undergoes suf ﬁcient liquid-phase sintering,
although sintering temperature is increased by 25 /C14 C. Meanwhile,
there also exists the Mo eCo-co-strengthening effect. These phe-
nomena result in a higher bending strength of the alloy sintered at
1250 /C14 C than at 1225 /C14 C. The hardness of the alloy is also improved
to some extent. Therefore, the W-2Mo-6Ni-2.5Fe-1.5Co alloy
exhibiting the most comprehensive mechanical properties is ob-
tained through short-time liquid phase sintering at 1250 /C14 C.
The change trend of the mechanical properties of the alloys is
closely related to the microstructural characteristics. Fig. 5 shows
the bending fracture morphology of the W-2Mo-6Ni-2.5Fe-1.5Co
alloy prepared by LEBM-assisted SPS. In general, the ﬁne-grained W
alloy has four kinds of fracture modes, namely, cleavage fracture,
peel-off of the W grain/matrix interface, along-the-interface frac-
ture of W/W grains, and ductile tearing of the binding phase. For
the 1000 /C14 C sintered samples, the densi ﬁcation of the W-2Mo-6Ni-
2.5Fe-1.5Co alloy is higher, reaching 90.68% ( Fig. 1 ), and the
porosity of alloy is smaller ( Fig. 5 a). At this temperature, the frac-
ture method mainly involves peeling off of W grains; thus, the
bending strength is lower. When the sample is sintered at 1 100 /C14 C
(Fig. 5b), the densiﬁcation degree of the alloy slightly increases, and
the fracture mode mainly occurs along-the-interface fracture of the
W/W grains. Although the liquid phase shows a certain increase,
the ductile tearing of the binding phase is hardly observed in the
fracture plane. Meanwhile, the micropores in the alloy are mark-
edly increased, thus decreasing the bending strength. With the
increase in sintering temperature from 1200
/C14 C to 1225 /C14 C( Fig. 5 c
and d), the fracture mode of ductile tearing of the binding phase
begins to appear in addition to the along-the-interface fracture of
W/W grains. The bending strength shows a sustained increase
trend overall. Some parts of the W grains in the alloy sintered at
1250 /C14 C( Fig. 5e) show an evident cleavage fracture, and the ductile
tearing of the binding phase is very prominent. Thus, the bending
strength of the alloy is further increased. Our previous

4. Conclusions
In this study, W e2Moe6Nie2.5Fee1.5Co alloys with simulta-
neous doping of Mo and Co elements in W eNieFe powders were
prepared by low-energy ball milling-assisted SPS method. The effect
of sintering temperature on the microstructure and mechanical
properties of W alloys was studied in detail. During SPS, the ﬁne-
grained W alloys could achieve fast densi ﬁcation at a lower sinter-
ing temperature. The inhibition of W grain growth is most evident at
below 1 100/C14 C. The low-temperature solid-state sintering or liquid-
esolid sintering of SPS occurs at 1000 /C14 C-1225 /C14 C, and the strength
and hardness of the alloy cannot be simultaneously improved. The
We2Moe6Nie2.5Fee1.5Co alloy undergoes suf ﬁcient liquid-phase
sintering and exhibits a signi ﬁcant increase in density at 1250 /C14 C.
The W grains are nearly spherical, and the strength and hardness are
simultaneously improved through suf ﬁcient liquid-phase sintering
and MoeCo-co-strengthening effect at 1250 /C14 C. The comprehensive
mechanical properties of the alloy were improved signiﬁcantly.
