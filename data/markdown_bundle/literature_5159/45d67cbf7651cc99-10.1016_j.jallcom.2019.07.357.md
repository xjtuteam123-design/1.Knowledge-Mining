Abstract 
Single-phase equiatomic CoCrFeMnNi high-entropy all oy (HEA) specimens with 
three different grain sizes (0.65 
µm, 2.1 µm and 105 µm) were processed by cold 
rolling and annealing treatment. Tensile properties were investigated over a broad 
temperature range from 77 K to 873 K. Superior stre ngth-ductility balance can be 
achieved by refining grain size and decreasing temp erature. The Hall-Petch 
relationship was well fitted, and the fitting param eters have a negative relation with 
temperature. The thermal and athermal contributions to the yield strength were 
revealed. This work has unveiled the superior mecha nical property of HEAs at 
cryogenic temperatures. 
 
Keywords: High-entropy alloy; Grain size; Temperature; Hall-P etch relationship; 
Mechanical properties.

1. Introduction 
In recent years, high-entropy alloys (HEAs) named b y Yeh et al. have been drawn 
much attention of the materials engineers due to th eir promising properties [1-6]. It 
has been found that these alloys generally form sim ple solid solutions with a single 
phase or dual phase [7-12]. Among various HEAs, the equiatomic CoCrFeMnNi HEA 
developed by Cantor et al., also named as Cantor al loy, was the extensively studied 
HEA because of the intriguing characteristics, such as exceptional cry ogenic 
mechanical properties and fracture toughness, enhanced creep resistance and 
superplasticity [2, 13-19]. However, due to the intrinsic characteristic of face-centered 
cubic (FCC) structure, the HEA exhibits insufficien t yield strength, which is usually 
below ∼400 MPa at 293 K [2]. Many efforts were conducted by materials scientists to 
improve the yield strength, such as grain refinemen t strengthening, precipitation 
strengthening and solid solution strengthening [15, 20-24]. Among various methods, 
grain refinement via nanostructuring is feasible to induce dramatic strengthening 
effect [25-27]. 
The strengthening mechanism of grain refinement is resulted from the grain 
boundaries as obstacles during the dislocations sli p process. For polycrystalline 
materials, the grain size strengthening could be ca lculated by the Hall-Petch relation 
[28, 29]. The yield strength ( σYS ) is related to the average grain size ( d) by the

2. Experimental procedures 
The as-cast ingot was prepared using magnetic levit ation melting technique, then 
solution-treated for 2 h at 1373 K. The melting process was the same as that described 
earlier [22]. Subsequently, the HEA ingot was hot forged at 1273 K into a rod with 30 
mm in diameter, and the rod was also cold rolled in to sheets with a 2.5 mm thickness. 
The sheets were annealed for 30 min at 948 K and 10 73 K to obtain specimens with 
ultrafine-grained (UFG) and fine-grained (FG) micro structures. The coarse-grained 
(CG) specimens were produced by annealing the forged rod at 1473 K for 1 h. 
The microstructure evolution was characterized using scanning electron microscope 
(SEM, LEO Supra 35) equipped with an electron chann eling contrast imaging (ECCI) 
component and transmission electron microscopy (TEM , FEI Tecnai F20). The grain 
sizes of the specimens with different annealing tre atment states were determined by 
electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD). The EBSD m easurements were performed 
with step sizes of 0.04, 0.1 and 5 
µm for the UFG, FG and CG HEAs, respectively. A 
linear intercept method was used to calculate the g rain sizes, including the high-angle 
grain boundaries (HAGBs) and twin boundaries (TBs). 
The flat dog-bone specimens used for tensile test h ad gauge length of 10 mm. 
Tensile properties were evaluated on an Instron 598 2 testing machine at 77 K, 203 K 
and 293 K with a strain rate of 10 −3 s−1. The tensile experiments of high temperature 
(573 K and 873 K) were conducted on an Instron 8801 testing machine with the same 
strain rate.

3.2. Mechanical properties 
Fig. 2 shows the tensile response for the UFG, FG a nd CG HEAs at different 
temperatures. A decrease in temperature led to an i ncrease of the yield strength ( σYS ) 
and ultimate tensile strength ( σUTS ) for all the HEAs, reaching the highest values of 
σYS and σUTS at 77 K, as shown in Fig. 3. An increase in the un iform elongation ( εUE ) 
was also observed in UFG and FG HEAs with the decre ase of the temperature, while 
εUE for the CG HEAs remains almost constant when the d eformation temperature 
varied in the range of 873 K and 293 K, and an incr ease occurred subsequently when 
the deformation temperature decreased to 77 K. The synchronous improvement of the 
strength and elongation with the decrease of temper ature can be related to the 
transition of twinning behavior and restrained disl ocation recovery, and detailed 
discussion can be found in the previous work [33].

3.3. Microstructural evolution 
 In order to clarify deformation mechanisms of HEA s with different grain sizes at 
various temperatures, ECCI technique was applied to characterize the microstructures 
of UFG, FG and CG HEAs, as presented in Fig. 7. A large number of dislocations and 
abundant deformation twins were detected in the UFG HEAs fractured at 77 K, as 
shown in Fig. 7(a1). These nanosized deformation tw ins are further verified from the 
TEM images (see inset in Fig. 7(a1)). At 293 K, num erous dislocations are observed, 
and dislocation cells (see inset in Fig. 7(a2)) can be detected in the grain, while no 
deformation twins can be observed due to the insuff icient flow stress [33]. At 873 K, 
loose dislocations are accommodated in UFG grains, which are still clean after tensile 
test due to the early necking. Note that the unifor m deformation area was 
characterized. In addition, no obvious grain growt h occurred in the UFG specimen 
since the grain size is comparable with the undefor med state, as illustrated in Fig.8. 
Furthermore, the dynamic recovery was enhanced and the dislocation density after 
deformation decreased as a result, leading to a dec line in flow stress, and similar 
results were reported in a TWIP steel [40]. For FG HEAs, as displayed in Fig. 7(b1), 
numerous deformation twins were activated and the t win-twin interaction was 
captured at 77 K, as delineated by the arrows. As t emperature increases from 77 K to 
293 K, deformation twins can still be detected, as marked by the arrows in Fig. 7(b2), 
while the quantity is relatively low to that at 77 K. At 873 K (Fig. 7(b3)), no 
deformation twins can be detected, and dislocation glide dominates the deformation of 
FG HEA. For CG HEAs, deformation twins dominate the deformation mechanism, 
when specimens deformed at cryogenic and room tempe rature. In contrast, when the 
temperature increased to 873 K, the number of defor mation twins declined and can 
only be detected in some grains. 
It has been reported that the grain size plays an i mportant role on the twinning 
behavior [41, 42]. Deformation twins would be diffi cultly activate with refining 
grains owing to high critical stress [42]. Hence, d eformation twins could be detected 
in FG and CG HEAs, while they can rarely be detected in the UFG HEAs at 293 K, as

4. Conclusions 
UFG (d=0.65 
µm), FG ( d=2.1 µm) and CG ( d=105 µm) CoCrFeMnNi HEAs with 
fully recrystallized microstructures were fabricate d. Temperature dependencies of 
Hall-Petch relation and deformation mechanism were investigated over a broad 
temperature range from 77 to 873 K. The important c onclusions from the 
experimental data are: 
(1) With decreasing temperatures from 873 K to 77 K , σYS and σUTS of UFG, FG, 
and CG CoCrFeMnNi HEAs are improved, and the deform ation mechanism 
transform from dislocation gliding to deformation twinning. 
(2) The Hall-Petch relationship can be well described f rom 77 K to 873 K. The 
Hall-Petch slope and friction stress of CoCrFeMnNi HEAs have a negative relation

with temperature. 
(3) Thermal contribution σth and athermal contribution σath of σYS were unveiled. 
σath and d follow a Hall-Petch type relationship, while σth is strongly related to T as 
well as d. 
(4) Superior strength and ductility can be achieved by decreasing d and T. The 
ultrahigh σYS of the UFG HEA is originated from a harmonized coalescence of σth and 
σath .
