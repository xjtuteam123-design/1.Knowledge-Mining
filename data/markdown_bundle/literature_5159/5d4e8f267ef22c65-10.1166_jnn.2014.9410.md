1. INTRODUCTION
Ti and Ti alloys have been widely used for biomedical
ﬁelds because they have excellent bio-compatibility, high
speciﬁc strength, and low elastic modulus.
1–4 Among sev-
eral properties of Ti alloys, Y oung’s modulus has been
recognized as being important for alloys used in the
replacement of hip joints because insufﬁcient load trans-
fer from the artiﬁcial bone to the adjacent remodeling
bone may results in bone resorption and eventual loos-
ening of the prosthetic device. This phenomenon, known
as stress-shielding, has been found to be associated with
large differences in stiffness between artiﬁcial and natural
bones, which are partly explained by analogous behav-
ior of their Y oung’s modulus.
5 In addition to low Y oung’s
modulus, alloys used for hip joints should have high
strength because high complex stresses including com-
pression, bending and torsion are applied to the stem
during walking, running, and exercising. Recently, sev-
eral Ti alloys with very low Y oung’s modulus have been
developed.
However, these newly developed Ti alloys have poorer
tensile properties than Ti-6Al-4V alloys.
6/commaori7 In previous, a
(Ti-35%Nb)-4%Sn alloy with very low Y oung’s modulus
of less than 45 GPa was developed by Hanada et al.

In this work, therefore, we tried to investigate an effect
of cross–roll rolling on tensile properties and Y oung’s

4. CONCLUSION
A (Ti-35mass%Nb)-4mass%Sn alloy was cross–roll rolled
with a reduction ratio of 70% in which the roll axes are
tilted by ± 5
/compBaway from the transverse direction of the
rolled sample and then aged at 250 /compBC for 2 h. Y oung’s
modulus and tensile strength showed a strong relation-
ship with the stress-induced martensite /SLalpha
′′texture and
microstructure features. Cross–roll rolling was an effec-
tive method to increase yield strength but it resulted in
an increase of Y oung’s modulus, simultaneously. Yield
and tensile strength increased by a low temperature age-
ing at 250
/compBC for 2 h. An increase of yield and tensile
strength by a low temperature ageing was due to /SLomegaprecip-
itation hardening. In particular, a higher yield and tensile
strength of the aged 70% cross–roll rolled sample than the
aged 70% conventionally rolled one indirectly indicated
that the aged 70% cross–roll rolled sample would have
ﬁner microstructure than the aged 70% conventionally
rolled one.
