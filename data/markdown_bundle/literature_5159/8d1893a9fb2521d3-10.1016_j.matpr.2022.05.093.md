abstract
Nb-W and Nb-Mo exhibit unrestrained solubility in liquid and solid phase, leading to inﬂuence the
mechanical properties of Nb. In the present work 90Nb-10 W (alloy A) and 90Nb-10Mo (alloy B) (in
weight%) are synthesized by mechanical alloying for 10 h and consolidated by conventional pressureless
sintering in argon atmosphere at 1450 /C176C with 2 h of soaking time. The effect of compaction pressure
(375 MPa, 500 MPa, 625 MPa) on densiﬁcation before sintering and densiﬁcation, hardness after sintering
of the alloys is also investigated. Plastic-assisted deformation is more in alloy B against alloy A which
enhances the densiﬁcation during sintering. Elemental mapping and line scanning result of sintered alloy
A show the incidence of W in Nb phase, suggesting profound inter-diffusion of W/Nb. Maximum % rela-
tive sintered density of 68.89% is achieved in alloy B at 500 MPa pressure. Alloy A exhibits maximum
hardness of 3.11 GPa at 625 MPa compared to 2.55 GPa hardness for alloy B compacted at identical com-
paction pressure. The wear resistance of alloy B is comparatively higher than alloy A, both compacted at
500 MPa pressure.

1. Introduction
Nb alloys are extensively used in high temperature aerospace
application due to high melting point (2477 /C176C), appreciable creep
behavior [1,2]. The density of Nb is also low (8.57 gm/cm 3) com-
pared to other refractory metals such as W, Ta, Mo and it also exhi-
bit decent formability. However, several Nb alloys such as C-103,
C-3009 contains costly elements as Hf, Ta, Zr which increases the
processing cost [3]. Therefore alloying with low cost metals such
as W ( /C2444 $/Kg) and Mo ( /C2466 $/Kg) [4] can improve the hardness
and wear resistance of Nb alloys. Literature evidences the fabrica-
tion of Nb-W-Mo alloys by vacuum arc remelting technique [5],
however melting process is not cost effective due to involvement
of high temperature. The paper also report coarser grain size of
Nb-3.0Mo and Nb-3.0 W-1.5Mo (in weight %) is 35.2 lm,
29.6 lm respectively [5]. No intermetallic forms in Nb-W and
Nb-Mo alloy suggesting formation of BCC solid solution. During
conventional pressureless sintering the applied load has a signiﬁ-
cant impact on the particle deformation, particle–particle contact
and density of the sample which inﬂuences the microstructure
and mechanical properties after sintering. Report suggests that
increased compaction pressure reduces the porosity and improves
the Young’s Modulus, Yield strength of the Ti [6]. During higher
compaction pressure the number of powder particle contact and
contact area increases which reduces the porosity and welding of
contact improves the strength of powder compact [7]. Addition
of Mo, W in Nb alloy can enhance the mechanical properties by
solid-solution strengthening effect. Liu et al. have fabricated Nb-
Ti-Ta alloy consisting porous structure by sponge impregnation
and sintering process [8]. Yang et al. have developed Nb-5 W-
2Mo-1Zr alloy by electron beam selective melting [9]. Tappin
et al. have also fabricated Nb-10-25at.%Al-20-40at.%V alloy by
transferred-arc plasma melting process and reported the presence
of ductile B2 and brittle A15 phases and the Al/V control the vol-
ume fraction of the phases [10]. Homogenization at 1500 /C176C shows
reduced ductility owing to higher presence of A15 phase compared
to cast structure [10]. Nb-25Ti-2Cr-2Al-16Si (at.%) alloy has also
synthesized by mechanical alloying and plasma spheroidization
and evidences the formation of evenly distributed Nb solid solution
along with Nb 5Si3, Nb3Si phases after plasma spheroidization pro-
cess [11]. Powder metallurgical fabrication of Nb based alloys can
reduce the processing temperature compared to traditional
melting-casting route. During pressureless sintering load applied

2. Materials and methods
Nb, W, Mo powders (commercially procured) are mechanically
alloyed in a high energy ball mill (mill speed: 300 rpm, milling
time: 10 h, ball to powder weight ratio: 10:1, grinding balls: tung-
sten carbide, process control agent: toluene). XRD, SEM of the pow-
ders obtained during milling is carried out. The 10 h mechanically
alloyed powders [90Nb-10 W (alloy A) and 90Nb-10Mo (alloy B)]
are compacted in a uniaxial press by applying 375 MPa,
500 MPa, 625 MPa pressure and after pressure application 5 min
time is provided for even spreading of pressure. The compacted
pellets of alloy A, alloy B is represented as A1, A2, A3 and B1, B2,
B3 at 375 MPa, 500 MPa, 625 MPa respectively. The compacted
alloys are subjected to green density measurement (density before
sintering) by Archimedes’ principle. The cylindrical pellets after
compaction are sintered in tunnel furnace with argon atmosphere
at 1450 /C176C with 2 h of holding time (at 1450 /C176C). The sintered sam-
ples are investigated by XRD, FESEM, line scanning (for phase dis-
tribution) as well as sintered density, hardness (10 gf load), ball on
plate wear testing (at 25 rpm, 20 N load, time: 15 min, wear track
diameter: 4 mm, diamond indenter) has also been carried out.
3. Results and discussion
3.1. Phase analysis of mechanically alloyed powders
Nb forms substitutional solid solution with W and Mo due to sim-
ilar crystal structure, %difference in atomic radius of 2.53%, 4.04%
between Nb-W and Nb-Mo (less than 15% for formation of substitu-
tional solid solution), and the elements possesses BCC crystal struc-
ture. The electronegativity difference is 0.76 for Nb-W and 0.56 for
Nb-Mo which may induce restriction against complete solubility.
The XRD patterns of alloy A and alloy B at 0 h, 1 h, 5 h, 10 h of mechan-
ical alloying is presented inFig. 1(a, b). The patterns shows reduction
in intensity and broadening of Nb (1 0 0), Nb (2 0 0), Nb (2 1 1) peaks
with increase in milling time. The phenomenon indicates reduction
in crystallite size, generation of lattice strain during mechanical
alloying due to defects generation which triggers the diffusion
[12]. Both the alloys show additional WC phase evolution at 5 h
and 10 h of milling which is attributed to the wear effect from WC
grinding balls. The extent of peak ﬂattening is different at different
plane such as Nb (2 0 0) peak intensity is less and peak ﬂattening is
signiﬁcant against Nb (1 1 0) for both the alloys. The intensity of W
and Mo in alloy A and alloy B also reduces suggesting reduction in
path for diffusion in Nb lattice. Formation of Nb-W and Nb-Mo solid
solution occurs at 10 h of milling. The milling time is constrained for
10 h to limit the contamination. The peak position (2 h)o fN b( 1 1 0 )
reduces from 45.0946/C176at 0 h to 45.0439/C176at 10 h of milling. However,
peak position (2h) of Nb (2 0 0) increases from 65.6919/C176to 65.7746/C176
at 0 h and 10 h of milling. The shifting of peak refers to Nb lattice dis-
tortion. Shifting of peak towards higher angle denotes reduction in
lattice parameter of Nb (atomic radius: 0.198 nm) by diffusion of
lower atomic radius of W (0.193 nm) and Mo (0.190 nm). The lattice
expansion caused by the stress assisted deformation during
mechanical alloying contributes in peak shifting to lower angle.
The broadening of Nb peaks for alloy A and alloy B is illustrated in
Fig. 1 (c, d). It is evident that d spacing and broadening increases
and decreases throughout the milling process and plane wise varia-
tion is also observed. The particle deformation, fracturing in
mechanical alloying is not homogeneous which also inﬂuences the
interparticle spacing and broadening. The broadening of Nb (1 1 0)
and Nb (2 0 0) is higher in alloy A compared to alloy B at 10 h of
milling suggest higher propensity of fracturing due to presence of
brittle W. The d spacing at different plane also exhibits disparity
among alloy A and alloy B. The d spacing of Nb is less for alloy A with
respect to (1 0 0) and (2 0 0) plane but increases for (2 1 1) plane com-
pared to alloy B at 10 h of milling. Reduction in d spacing of Nb peaks
indicates reduction in volume of unit cell and diffusion of W and Mo
in Nb lattice for solid solution formation [13].

3.5. Hardness study
The variation of microhardness with respect to different inden-
tation points for A1, A2, A3 and B1, B2, B3 is presented in Fig. 8(a)
and (b) respectively. The ﬂuctuations of hardness are due to varia-
tion in composition at different regions as displayed in Fig. 6(a, b).
The measured hardness value is also contributed by the concentra-
tion of harder W phase and grain size of Nb. High concentration of
W in Nb phase and ﬁne Nb grain size results in high hardness. The
microhardness increases with increase in compaction pressure for
all alloys. Though maximum sintered density is evident in B2, max-
imum hardness of A3 is attributed to dual effect of reﬁned particle
size and high hardness of W. The low hardness value of the Nb
alloys is due to presence of high porosity. Report illustrates that
Nb-10 W alloy fabricated by arc melting possesses hardness of
220 HV (2.158 GPa) which is lower compared to maximum hard-
ness value of Nb-10 W processed by mechanical alloying and con-
ventional pressureless sintering [22].
3.6. Wear study
The variation of wear depth with sliding distance for alloy A2
and B2 is presented in Fig. 9 . Both the variation of composition
and hardness of phases inﬂuence the wear depth. For high porous
material, during wear test the porosity can facilitate cracking, the
load bearing hard particles can be wear out and piled up across
the indenter. The porosity is less in B2 (31.11%) against A2
(42.97%) and although the hardness is higher in A2, still maximum
wear depth is exhibited by A2. During wear test the W incorporates
surface brittleness and therefore may contributes in reduced wear
resistance in A2. Literature evidences that in spite of lower hard-
ness in Nb-5 at.%Ag alloy, the wear rate is reduced compared to
Nb-1 at.%Ag [23]. W grains in Fig. 4 (c) are contiguous and the W/
W interface is quite weak in A2 sample which further can aggra-
vate crack formation. The micrographs of wear tracks in Fig. 10 (a,
b) show that the average wear track width is higher in A2
(728.33 lm) compared to B2 (334 lm). The speciﬁc wear rate
[24] provides comprehension about the wear resistance of materi-
als. The calculated speciﬁc wear rate is higher in A2 (1.03 /C210-2
mm3/N.m) compared to B2 (0.18 /C210-2 mm3/N.m). The accumula-
tion of wear particles and delamination is presented for A2 in
Fig. 10(a). The delamination leads to high material loss and reduces
the wear resistance in A2.
4. Conclusions
Nb-W and Nb-Mo alloys are fabricated by powder metallurgical
method. The particle size progressively reﬁned with increase in
milling time. Compaction pressure of 500 MPa results in maximum
densiﬁcation in Nb-Mo alloy. Presence of WC is evident in the
milled alloys. Higher contiguous grains in Nb-W alloy is observed
at higher compaction pressure (500, 625 MPa). Sintered Nb-W
alloy shows highest hardness compared to Nb-Mo alloy at
625 MPa. Wear study (compaction pressure: 500 MPa) shows
higher wear resistance in Nb-Mo than Nb-W alloy.
