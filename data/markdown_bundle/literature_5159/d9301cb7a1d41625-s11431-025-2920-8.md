Abstract Precipitation-strengthened medium-entropy alloys (MEAs) often display an excellent balance of strength and 
ductility. However, the influence of temperature on their mechanical properties and plastic deformation mechanism requires 
further exploration. This study examines the tensile behavior and plastic deformation mechanisms of the (CoCrNi) 94Ti 3Al 3 
alloy at 298, 77, and 20 K using in-situ neutron diffraction and electron microscopy methods. The tensile property shows a clear 
temperature dependence. Lower temperatures result in improved mechanical performance, as indicated by an increase in yield 
strength, ultimate tensile strength (UTS), and elongation. Specifically, the yield strength increases from 844 to 1152 MPa at 
20 K, while the UTS increases from 1306 to 1848 MPa. Similarly, elongation improves from 25.8% to 33.3% as temperature 
decreases. At lower temperatures, a larger number of dislocations and stacking faults form, enhancing the alloy’s strain 
hardening ability. Notably, dislocations exhibit wavy morphologies at 298 K, whereas planar behavior becomes predominant at 
20 and 77 K. No FCC→HCP phase transformation is detected, a behavior that contrasts with the CoCrNi MEA under 
deformation at 20 K. Additionally, the interaction between dislocations and L1 2 nanoparticles exhibited temperature depen -
dence, suggesting the need for future investigations. This study offers valuable insights for advancing the design of high- 
performance alloys suitable for cryogenic applications.

1 Introduction 
High- and medium-entropy alloys, composed of multi-prin -
cipal metallic elements, have garnered significant interest 
[1–5] owing to their unique properties. Among them, the 
single-phase CoCrNi medium-entropy alloy (MEA) with a 
face-centered cubic (FCC) structure stands out for its ex -
ceptional combination of strength and ductility. Moreover, 
the CoCrNi MEA exhibits a further enhancement of these 
properties at extremely low temperatures [6]
, making it a 
promising candidate for cryogenic applications. This en -
hancement is largely attributed to the reduction in stacking 
fault energy (SFE) as the temperature decreases [6–9]
. The 
lower SFE promotes the activation of mechanical twinning 
over an extended strain range at cryogenic temperatures. The 
tensile properties and SFE of the CoCrNi alloy MEA display 
a notable temperature dependence [6–11]. At room tem
-
perature, the SFE is approximately (18 ± 4) mJ/m 2 [12], but it 
decreases with lower temperatures. SFE plays a critical role 
in influencing plastic deformation in FCC metals. When the 
SFE exceeds 45 mJ/m

dislocations tend to dissociate into Shockley partials with a

from the FCC phase to the hexagonal-close-packed (HCP) 
phase may occur [15–17]. 
Studies have shown that mechanical twinning is prevalent 
during the tensile deformation of CoCrNi MEA at room 
temperature and 77 K. This twinning enhances the strain- 
hardening rate, improving both UTS and tensile elongation, a 
mechanism referred to as the twinning-induced plasticity 
effect. However, at extremely low temperatures, SIMT from 
the FCC to HCP phase is activated, further contributing to 
the strain-hardening rate and improving the alloy’s strength 
and ductility through transformation-induced plasticity 
(TRIP) effects [13–18]. The reduced SFE at low tempera
-
tures amplifies the formation of mechanical twins and phase 
transformations, significantly improving the mechanical 
performance of CoCrNi MEA in cryogenic conditions. 
While FCC single-phase MEAs demonstrate exceptional 
ductility, their strength and wear resistance fall short com -
pared to conventional Ni-based and Co-based alloys, re -
stricting their practical applications. To improve the wear 
resistance, researchers have developed self-lubricating 
composites and alloys with lamellar eutectic microstructures, 
which exhibit excellent mechanical and robust lubrication 
performance [19,20]
. Furthermore, precipitation-strengthened 
MEAs featuring nanosized coherent L1 2-(Co, Ni, Cr) 3(Ti, Al)- 
type precipitates have been introduced to improve strength 
[21–23]. Minor alloying with strong γʹ-phase formers such as 
Ti and Al in CoCrFeNi or CoCrNi MEAs promotes the 
generation of high-density L1 2-nanoparticles. The interface 
between the L1 2 phase and the FCC matrix is either coherent 
or semicoherent, with a very small lattice misfit. Among the 
precipitation-strengthened MEAs, the (CoCrNi) 
94Ti 3Al 3 
alloy stands out as a representative example. Its coherent 
nanoparticles significantly enhance the yield strength and 
UTS of the FCC matrix by approximately 70% and 40%, 
respectively [21,23]. The morphology of the L1 
2 precipitates 
varies based on the local strain environment. Spherical 
particles are typically formed in regions with low local strain 
through homogeneous nucleation, while lamellar-like pre
-
cipitates are preferentially formed in high-strain regions, 
such as grain boundaries, through heterogeneous nucleation 
[21]
. 
Although nanoprecipitates significantly enhance mechan -
ical performance, certain fundamental mechanisms remain 
unsolved. First, the mechanical behavior of precipitation- 
strengthened alloys at cryogenic temperatures requires fur
-
ther investigation. Second, the influence of nanoprecipitates 
on the plastic deformation mechanisms at different tem
-
peratures is not yet fully understood. Third, it is unclear 
whether the strain-induced FCC→HCP martensitic trans
-
formation occurs at extremely low temperatures. To address 
these questions, this study focuses on the tensile properties of 
(CoCrNi) 
94Ti 3Al 3 MEA at 298, 77, and 20 K. The alloy 
microstructure was examined using electron microscopy, and 
the evolution of crystal defects during plastic deformation 
under different temperature conditions was analyzed using 
in-situ neutron diffraction techniques. 
2 Experimental procedure 
The (CoCrNi) 94Ti 3Al 3 alloy, weighing 500 g, was synthe -
sized by vacuum high-frequency induction melting using 
high-purity elements Co, Cr, Ni, Ti, and Al (>99.9 wt.%). To 
ensure chemical homogeneity, the cast ingot was homo -
genized at 1473 K for 5 h in a high-purity Ar atmosphere and 
subsequently forged at the same temperature, reducing its 
cross-section from φ50 mm to 30 mm × 30 mm. Then, the 
alloy was hot-rolled at 1473 K into a 3-mm sheet, cold-rolled 
to a thickness of 1.8 mm, and annealed at 1373 K for 2.5 min 
to develop relatively fine recrystallized grains. Aging at 
1073 K for 1 h, followed by water quenching, promoted the 
formation of small recrystallized grains containing ultrafine 
precipitates. Dog-bone-shaped tensile specimens, with di
-
mensions 1 mm × 2 mm × 10 mm, were prepared by elec -
trical discharge machining. The specimens were 
mechanically ground and polished to achieve mirror-finished 
surfaces before undergoing uniaxial tensile tests at 298, 77, 
and 20 K with a strain rate of 1.5 × 10 −4 s −1. Grain structures 
were characterized using a scanning electron microscope 
(SEM, JSM-7100F, JEOL) equipped with an electron back -
scatter diffraction (EBSD) detector. Orientation imaging was 
conducted using orientation imaging microscopy software 
(version 7.0). Characterization of the precipitates was per
-
formed using a scanning transmission electron microscope 
(STEM, Talos F200X, FEI, and the Titan 3 60-300 Double 
Corrector, Thermo Scientific), both equipped with energy- 
dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) and operated at an 
acceleration voltage of 200 kV . For in situ neutron diffraction 
measurements, the time-of-flight neutron diffractometer at 
BL19 “TAKUMI” in the Materials and Life Science Facility 
at the Japan Proton Accelerator Research Complex (J-PARC) 
was used. Detailed information on instrumentation, mea
-
surements, and data analysis is referenced in previous studies 
[24]
. During experiments, the loading axis was aligned at 
+45° with respect to the incident neutron beam, and dif -
fracted neutrons were collected by axial and radial detectors.

3.2 Tensile performance 
Figure 2(a) presents the uniaxial tensile engineering stress- 
strain curves of (CoCrNi) 94Ti 3Al 3 MEA at temperatures of 
298, 77, and 20 K. The strength and ductility increase as the 
temperature decreases. The yield strength increases from 
844 MPa at 298 K to 1082 MPa at 77 K and then to 
1152 MPa at 20 K. Similarly, UTS increases from 1306 MPa 
at 298 K to 1714 MPa at 77 K and then to 1848 MPa at 20 K. 
Notably, tensile elongation also improves with decreasing 
temperature, increasing from 25.8% at 298 K to 30.5% at 
77 K, and further to 33.3% at 20 K. At 20 K, a serration 
phenomenon was observed, attributed to localized in
-
homogeneous deformation. This serration at cryogenic 
temperatures is likely caused by two factors [26]
: (1) it may 
have a mechanical origin caused by the instability of plastic 
deformation, such as dislocation pileups; (2) it may result 
from thermodynamic instability, where deformation-induced 
temperature rises lead to a drop in flow stress. Figure 2(b) 
shows the true stress-strain curves alongside the strain 
hardening rates. The true stress rises as strain increases (prior 
to necking) owing to strain hardening. The strain hardening 
rates decrease rapidly at low strain levels (true strain <0.05) 
and then decline gradually with increasing strain. The strain 
hardening rates at 77 and 20 K are much higher than those at 
298 K, indicating that dislocation multiplication occurs more 
rapidly at cryogenic temperatures.

resulting in approximately 4 vol.% of the HCP phase being 
formed [27]

4 Conclusion 
The tensile behavior and plastic deformation mechanism of 
the nano-precipitation strengthened (CoCrNi) 94Ti 3Al 3 MEA 
at ambient temperature and cryogenic temperatures were 
investigated using in-situ neutron diffraction and electron 
microscopy. The conclusions are as follows. 
(1) L1 2 nanoparticles with an average size of (11.61 ± 
1.92) nm and σ-phase with an average grain size of (0.90 ± 
0.16) μm were formed in the (CoCrNi) 
94Ti 3Al 3 MEA. The 
σ-phase is mainly distributed along grain boundaries, while 
L1 2 nanoparticles are formed within the grain interior. 
(2) The tensile properties of the (CoCrNi) 94Ti 3Al 3 MEA 
strongly depend on temperature. Both yield strength, ulti -
mate tensile strength, and elongation improve as the tem -
perature decreases. Specifically, the yield strengths are 844, 
1082, and 1152 MPa at 298, 77, and 20 K, respectively. Si -
milarly, the ultimate tensile strengths are 1306, 1714, and 
1848 MPa at 298, 77, and 20 K, respectively. The tensile 
elongation is 25.8% at 298 K, 30.5% at 77 K, and 33.3% at 
20 K. 
(3) A larger number of dislocations and stacking faults are 
formed at 20 and 77 K compared to 298 K, contributing to 
enhanced strain-hardening capacity at cryogenic tempera
-
tures. While dislocations formed at 298 K display wavy 
morphology, they exhibit a planar configuration at 20 and 
77 K. There is no evidence of an FCC→HCP phase trans
-
formation happening in any of the temperatures studied. 
Furthermore, the interaction between dislocations and 
L1 2-nanoparticles depends on temperature, highlighting the 
need for further research on this subject.
