ABSTRACT 
Nb-lean Cu – Cr – Nb alloys have been widely applied in electrical industries as the structural conductive material 
owing to their excellent combination of the mechanical and electrical properties at room temperature. In this 
work, the influence of temperature during fast sintering and post-heat treatment strategies on the precipitate 
evolution and room temperature mechanical properties of the Cu – 3Cr-0.5Nb (wt.%) alloy was investigated. The 
increasing sintering temperature will improve the density and mechanical properties of the alloy without any 
obvious grain growth during this non-equilibrium process, while the post-aging at lower and higher temperatures 
can effectively control the formation and coarsening process of the Cr-based precipitates. Systematic charac -
terization was then conducted on the microstructural evolution, especially the altering precipitates ’ size and 
distribution, in the alloy at both as-sintered and the aged conditions, which is correlated to its room temperature 
tensile properties. Lower aging temperature will induce the nucleation of the new Cr
2
Nb precipitates without 
causing a significant coarsening of the existing precipitates like aging at higher temperature. Therefore, the low- 
temp. aged sample exhibits the highest tensile strength and elongation due to the enhanced Orowan strength -
ening mechanism induced by its unique precipitate microstructure. 
1. Introduction 
Copper-based alloys are a group of promising candidates for engine 
liners, owing to their excellent combination of thermal/electrical con -
ductivity, mechanical properties, and low cost [ 1 , 2 ]. Currently, the 
serving copper alloy systems in aerospace engines are Cu – Cr – Zr, 
Cu – Cr – Ag, Cu – Ag – Zr, as well as the novel Cu – Cr – Nb. Compared to the 
other Cu ternary systems, the Cu – Cr – Nb exhibits advantages in strength, 
creep resistance, and low-cycle fatigue in the service environment [ 3 ], 
which can be explained by the higher melting point of Nb compared to 
Ag, Zr, and Cr [ 4 ]. In addition, Cr and Nb will form an intermetallic 
compound, Cr2Nb, acting as the strengthening phase in this Cu-based 
alloy in both room and elevated temperatures [ 2 , 5 , 6 ]. Therefore, this 
alloy has been widely applied not only in the aerospace, power gener -
ating industries by focusing on the high temperature mechanical prop -
erties, but also in the electronic industrial, the wall of international 
thermonuclear experimental reactor (ITER), liquid rocket engine com -
ponents, which needs this alloy working under room temperature as the 
structural (and conductive) material. 
The main strengthening mechanism in the Cu – Cr – Nb alloy is the 
tough Cr
2
Nb-particle-based-dispersion-strengthening [ 7 ]. These fine 
sized particles have a melting point of 1773 
◦
C, which makes them 
thermally stable during processing and post-treatment [ 3 ]. Therefore, 
over the past decades, they were designed for thermal-mechanical ap -
plications and have been successfully produced by chill block melt 
spinning and conventional gas atomization and powder metallurgy 
[ 8 – 10 ]. Certain representative Cu – Cr – Nb alloy systems like GRCop-84 
or Cu – 8Cr – 4Nb (Cu-8 at.%Cr-4 at.%Nb) and GRCop-42 or 
Cu – 4Cr – 2Nb (Cu-4 at.%Cr-2 at.%Nb) were specifically engineered for 
application in the primary combustion chamber liner of the space shuttle 
main engine and comparable propulsion systems [ 3 , 5 , 11 ]. Seltzman 
et al. has used a high laser power to produce a fully dense GRCop-84

lution, and related room temperature mechanical properties, which are 
crucial when designing novel Nb-lean Cu – Cr – Nb alloy for the 
electrical-mechanical industry [ 14 – 16 ]. 
To solve the aforementioned issue, in our current work, we use fast 
hot pressing sintering (FHPS) to form a fine-grain-sized Nb-lean 
Cu – Cr – Nb alloy. FHPS is an innovative technique that is used to rapidly 
achieve dense sintering and high temperature synthesis by direct current 
heating and axial pressure. It presents the benefits of cost-effectiveness, 
fast heating rates, meticulous and regulated sintering, as well as the 
capability to produce the large size part. A fine-sized Nb-lean Cu – Cr – Nb 
powders were FHPS at varying the external sintering temperature, with 
the densification behavior and properties of these Cu – 3Cr-0.5Nb (wt.%) 
alloy being investigated. The selected samples with the optimum sin -
tering parameters were then subjected to two different post-heat treat -
ment approaches, i.e. low- and high-temperature aging. The evolution 
mechanisms of the precipitates during heat treatments were then 
analyzed and correlated with their room temperature mechanical 
properties. The mechanisms of the Cr-rich precipitates and the enhanced 
tensile stress have been discussed, providing insights into the future 
development of the Nb-lean Cu – Cr – Nb alloys in processing and post- 
treatment. 
2. Methods 
2.1. Sample preparation and heat treatments 
Feedstock Cu – Cr – Nb powder was produced via gas atomization in 
ATO Lab, following the ideal thermodynamically determined composi -
tion of Cu – 3Cr-0.5Nb wt.%. The size of the powder is in the range of 
2.41 μ m – 78.28 μ m, with the average at 31.03 ± 5.21 μ m. The chemical 
composition of powder and sintered samples is listed in Table 1 . 
The powder was put into a cylindrical graphite die with an inner 
diameter of 30.0 mm, and then consolidated by fast hot press sintering 
furnace (FHP-828, Shanghai Ha-teng) in vacuum with a heating rate of 
100 
◦

pressure, and time, are shown in Table 2 . Density of samples after sin -

is related to its properties and was also measured by Archimedes ’ 
principle (bulk density of the sample was measured by Archimedes ’ 
principle, including all the pores and cracks) and summarized in Table 2 
[ 17 – 19 ]. The sintered samples were then subjected to tensile tests with 
the best one selected for the following heat treatments. To produce the

4. Discussion 
4.1. Evolution of the precipitates 
During the sintering process, due to the limited solubility of the Cr in 
the Cu alloy, the Cr atoms will diffuse out of the Cu matrix and form Cr- 
based precipitates, which have been characterized as the Cr-rich and 
Cr
2
Nb precipitates ( Figs. 9 – 10 ) [ 17 , 21 ]. As the sintering temperature 
increases from 850 
◦
C to 950 
◦
C, the average size of these Cr-based 
precipitates decreases from 0.86 μ m to 0.53 μ m by ~38 %, while no 
obvious changes can be detected regarding the volume fraction of the 
precipitates. The average interparticle spacing between the precipitates 
decreases from 0.60 μ m to 0.37 μ m by ~38 % ( Table 4 ), which was 
calculated based on a recent work [ 22 ]. These results indicate more 
widely-distributed precipitates at a higher sintering temperature of 
950 
◦
C ( Fig. 2 b and c). 
Once the Cu – Cr – Nb powders were sintered at 950 
◦
C, the as-sintered 
samples will be aged at two different aging treatments, as mentioned 
before. During the aging treatments, the Nb and Cr solute atoms are 
rejected from the Cu-matrix, facilitating the nucleation and growth of 
the existing Cr
2

5. Conclusion 
A fully dense Nb-lean Cu – Cr – Nb alloy was successfully fabricated 
using fast hot press sintering, with two different aging treatments 
established to tailor the size and distribution of the Cr-based pre -
cipitates. The microstructure of the as-sintered and aged samples, 
especially the evolution of the Cr-rich and Cr
2
Nb precipitates, were 
characterized using XRD, SEM, and TEM. The tensile properties and 
related fractography were then carried out at room temperature and the 
low-temp. aged sample shows the best strength and elongation 
comparing to the as-sintered and high-temp. aged ones, owing to the fine 
and homogeneously distributed precipitates. The conclusions of this 
work are summarized as follows: 
1) Elevating the sintering temperature from 750 to 950 
◦
C causes an 
enhancement of the density, tensile strength, and elongation of the 
Nb-lean Cu – Cr – Nb alloy. The 950 
◦
C sintered sample shows the 
smallest average grain size of 2.23 μ m, the highest ultimate tensile 
strength of 384 MPa and elongation of 45.08 %. 
2) Post-heat treatment will not induce significant changes in grain size, 
which might indicate the pinning effects of the precipitates on the 
grain boundary migration and grain coarsening. Compared to the as- 
sintered and high-temp. aged, the low-temp. aged sample exhibits 
the highest ultimate tensile strength (439 MPa) and elongation 
(46.50 %). Precipitates with a size of ~50 nm are observed in the 
low-temp. aged sample and the interactions between these pre -
cipitates and dislocations produce Orowan strengthening, which is 
the main factor contributing to the increase in strength. The growth 
of Cr
2
Nb and Cr-rich precipitates was noted in the high-temp. aged 
sample, increasing from 0.47 μ m to 1.13 μ m. Such growth will 
reduce the Orowan strengthening, causing a reduction of the tensile 
strength (from 439 to 277 MPa) and the elongation (from 46.5 % to 
38.93 %).
