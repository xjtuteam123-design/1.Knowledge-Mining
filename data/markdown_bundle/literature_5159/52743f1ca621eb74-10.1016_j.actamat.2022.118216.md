a b s t r a c t 
Developing microstructurally stable alloys with the outstanding strength-ductility combination at both 
room temperature and elevated temperatures is highly desirable for advanced structural applications, 
which, however, still remains challenging despite years of efforts. In this study, leveraging on the 
computation-aided thermodynamic modeling, novel high-strength Ni 39.9 Co 20 Fe 30-x Cr x Al 6 Ti 4 B 0.1 ( x = 0, 10, 
15, and 20 at.%) high-entropy alloys (HEAs) were rationally designed, which maintained a stable coherent 
precipitation microstructure when continually substitute Fe with Cr. The structural features, mechanical 
properties, and underlying deformation micro-mechanisms were systematically investigated. We found 
that Cr mostly partitions into the face-centered-cubic (FCC) matrix relative to the L1 2 -type nanoparti- 
cles. The increased Cr concentration substantially decreases the stacking fault energy (SFE) of the ma- 
trix and results in the stacking-fault-prevailed deformation behaviors, thereby producing simultaneously 
enhanced strength and ductility at room temperature. By contrast, when tested at 600 °C, the Cr-free 
HEA suffers from an extremely brittle fracture along grain boundaries caused by environmental oxidation 
damages. Interestingly, with Cr doping up to 20 at.%, such a serve intergranular embrittlement can be 
completely eliminated due to the increased oxidation resistance, leading to a distinct brittle-to-ductile 
transition (from 3.4 to 25.2%) at a high strength level of ∼ 1136 MPa. These ﬁndings will give new in- 
sights into the controllable design of novel high-performance HEAs for achieving target microstructures 
and superior property combinations.

the industrial society. In particular, the development of aerospace, 
petrochemical, and nuclear energy raises an urgent need for alloys 
with outstanding strength-ductility combinations at both room and 
elevated temperatures. Recently, multicomponent alloys, also refers 
to as high-entropy alloys (HEAs), have received considerable inter-

component HEAs are expected to offer a wide compositional space 
for designing novel alloys with superior performances, the pres- 
ence of multiple elements and associated complex chemical inter- 
actions usually lead to the undesired formation of various brittle 
intermetallic phases, in particular at the GBs. For instance, the ele- 
vated addition of Al in HEAs led to the brittle NiAl-rich B2 phase at 
GBs, which in turn, deteriorates their tensile ductility at room tem- 
perature [19] . The minor addition of Nb showed a strong tendency 
to form brittle Nb-enriched Laves, which drastically decreases the 
tensile ductility as well [20] . The excessive addition of Cr will also 
signiﬁcantly promote the formation of hard and brittle σ phases 
[21] . Apparently, achieving pristine “FCC + L1 2 ”m i c r o s t r u c t u r e s to-

however, still remains challenging in the chemically complex HEA 
systems and depends heavily on the precise control of various mul- 
tiple elements. 
In this study, with the aid of thermodynamic calculations, 
we purposefully designed a series of novel L1 2 -strengthened 
Ni 39.9 Co 20 Fe 30-x Cr x Al 6 Ti 4 B 0.1 (at.%) HEAs with varied Cr concentra- 
tions. To stabilize the FCC matrix, the concentrations of Ni and 
Co were tailored to be around 40 and 20 at.%, respectively. Alloy- 
ing additions of Al (6 at.%) and Ti (4 at.%) were introduced here 
to produce the dense L1 2 -type nanoparticles. More importantly, 
to prevent the HEAs from the oxygen damages while maintaining 
the “FCC + L1 2 ”d u a l - p h a s e structure without forming other brit- 
tle phases, Cr was controllably added up to 20 at.%. As compared 
with the Cr-free counterpart, the Ni 39.9 Co 20 Fe 10 Cr 20 Al 6 Ti 4 B 0.1 (at.%) 
HEA exhibits simultaneously enhanced strength-ductility synergy 
at both room temperature and 600 °C. The microstructural evo- 
lutions and mechanical behaviors of these newly designed HEAs 
were systematically studied. Their underlying deformations and 
fracture mechanisms were also carefully analyzed and discussed. 
2. Experimental methods 
2.1. Alloy processing 
Alloy ingots with nominal compositions of 
Ni 39.9 Co 20 Fe 30-x Cr x Al 6 Ti 4 B 0.1 ( x = 0, 10, 15, and 20 in at.%) 
were fabricated by using a vacuum arc-melting furnace under a 
high-purity argon atmosphere. For convenience, the alloys will be 
labeled according to their Cr concentrations, referred to as the 
Cr0, Cr10, Cr15, and Cr20 HEAs, respectively. Micro-alloying of 
boron (0.1 at.%) helps to strengthen GBs due to the segregation 
of boron at the GBs [22] . All ingots were melted at least four 
times and then cast into a copper mold with a dimension of 
50 × 12 × 5 mm 3 . Afterward, all the alloys were homogenized 
at 1150 °C for 2 h to eliminate the elemental segregation during 
solidiﬁcation. Cold rolling with a 65% thickness reduction and 
subsequent static recrystallization at 1150 °C for 2.5 min were 
carried out in sequence, followed by ﬁnal aging at 800 °C for 
12 h. All the heat treatments were conducted in the air condition, 
followed by air cooling to room temperature. 
2.2. Microstructural and elemental characterization 
The as-prepared alloys were systematically characterized in- 
cluding the microstructures and deformation micro-mechanisms. 
The phase constitutions and their characterizations were examined 
using X-ray diffraction (XRD, Bruker D2), scanning electron mi- 
croscope (SEM, Quanta 450) equipped with an electron backscat- 
tered diffraction (EBSD) detector, transmission electron microscope 
(TEM, JEOL 2100F) equipped with an energy-dispersive X-ray spec- 
troscopy (EDX) detector, together with atom probe tomography 
(APT, CAMECA LEAP 50 0 0XR). For the SEM and EBSD tests, spec- 
imens were mechanically grounded and then electrolytically pol- 
ished. For the TEM tests, specimens were mechanically grounded, 
punched, and ion-milled to electron transparency by an ion polish- 
ing system (Gatan 695). Needle-shaped specimens required for APT 
were fabricated by lift-outs and annular milled in an FEI Scios fo- 
cused ion beam/scanning electron microscope (FIB/SEM). The spec- 
imens were analyzed at 70 K in a voltage mode at a pulse repeti- 
tion rate of 200 kHz, a pulse fraction of 20%, and an evaporation 
detection rate of 0.2% atom per pulse. 
2.3. Mechanical characterization 
Dog-bone-shaped samples with a gage length of 12.5 mm and 
a width of 3.2 mm were machined by electro-discharge machin- 
ing. Uniaxial tensile tests were conducted on a Material Testing 
System (MTS, Alliance RT30) tension machine at ambient temper- 
ature with a strain rate of 1 × 10 −3 s −1 . Also, high-temperature 
tests were conducted at 600 °C with the same strain rate. Three 
thermometers were used to ensure the testing temperature can 
be precisely controlled. Moreover, to get rid of oxygen damage, 
the high-temperature tests were conducted in a vacuum condition 
with a vacuum degree of 6 × 10 −3 Pa. At least three specimens 
were tested for each condition, and one representative curve was 
plotted.

3.2. Microstructural features and elemental distributions 
Fig. 2 a 1 , b 1 , c 1 , and d 1 show the EBSD inverse pole ﬁgure 
(IPF) maps of the Cr0 ∼ Cr20 HEAs in the present work, illus- 
trating that all the alloys are fully recrystallized with similar grain 
sizes varying from 67.68 μm to 54.22 μm. Given that the triple 
point junctions of the GBs are often believed to be thermody- 
namically eﬃcient sites for phase nucleation [24] , close-up views 
of those regions were examined by SEM, as shown in Fig. 2 a 2 , 
b 2 , c 2 , and d 2 . Clearly, no measurable detrimental intergranular 
phases at GBs are presented in all these four HEAs. The high- 
magniﬁcation SEM images (see Fig. 2 a 3 , b 3 , c 3 , and d 3 ) of the 
grain interiors show the uniformly distributed spheroidal particles, 
which are further conﬁrmed to be the coherent L1 2 precipitates 
using TEM (see Fig. 2 a 4 , b 4 , c 4 , and d 4 ). Dark-ﬁeld TEM (DF-TEM) 
images acquired from the superlattice reﬂection highlighted by the 
orange circles in the selected-area electron diffraction (SAED) pat- 
terns along the [112]-zone axis clearly verify the ordered struc- 
ture of the L1 2 phase. The experimental results are in high agree- 
ment with the thermodynamic prediction. Interestingly, the parti- 
cle size appears to be reduced by substituting Fe with Cr. Specif- 
ically, the average particle size (in diameter) of the Cr0 HEA can 
be determined to around 41.89 ± 9.83 nm with an average spac- 
ing of 39.70 ± 16.90 nm [25] . By substituting Fe with Cr, the av- 
erage size in the Cr20 HEA is decreased to 25.15 ± 5.92 nm ac- 
companied with a reduced spacing of 13.98 ± 6.02 nm. The de- 
crease in precipitate size might be ascribed to the relatively low 
diffusivity of Cr than that of Fe [ 26 , 27 ]. Additionally, the volume 
fraction is approximately the same (around 38.5%) in those alloys, 
respectively. 
Furthermore, a quantitative compositional analysis based on the 
APT technique was carefully carried out for the Cr20 alloy. The 
corresponding three-dimension reconstructed element maps are 
shown in Fig. 3 a. The elemental partitioning behaviors were quan- 
titatively analyzed in the form of proximity histograms for indi- 
vidual elements (see Fig. 3 b). It is clearly revealed that the Ni, Ti, 
and Al atoms are strongly partitioned into the L1 2 nanoparticles, 
whereas the Co atom ( ∼ 8.2 at.%) is marginally depleted and the 
Cr, Fe atoms ( ∼ 4.8 at.% in total) are largely depleted. Convention- 
ally, for the ordered L1 2 phase with an A 3 B stoichiometry, Ni and 
Co atoms prefer to occupy the A site (the face-centered position of 
the FCC lattice), while the Ti and Al atoms occupy the B site (the 
cube-corner position) of the L1 2 phases [28] . Additionally, for the 
multicomponent Ni-Co-Fe-Cr-Al-Ti system, our previous study indi- 
cated that Cr occupying the B site is energetically more favored as 
compared with Fe [29] . Therefore, in the Cr20 alloy, those nanopar- 
ticles can be determined as the multi-component L1 2 phase with 
a chemical composition of (Ni 63.9 Co 8.20 Fe 2.5 )(Ti 11.5 Al 11.5 Cr 2.4 ). Sim- 
ilar elemental distributions can be also revealed by the STEM- 
EDX maps presented in Fig. 3 c. Noting that no obvious elemen- 
tal segregation was detected near the grain boundary in the Cr20 
HEA. The experimental and predicted chemical compositions of 
Cr0 and Cr20 HEAs are summarized in Table 1 . The chemical 
compositions identiﬁed above also agree well with the CALPHAD 
predictions.

3.3. Mechanical properties and deformation structures at room and 
elevated temperatures 
Evidently, by leveraging of the compositional design based on 
the CALPHAD method, simultaneously enhanced strength-ductility 
combinations can be successfully achieved at both room and in- 
termediate temperatures. As shown in Fig. 4 a, the newly designed 
L1 2 -strengthened Cr0 ∼ Cr20 HEAs exhibit outstanding strength- 
ductility combinations at room temperature. Interestingly, with in- 
creasing the Cr concentration from 0 to 20 at.%, the strength and 
ductility are simultaneously improved. The Cr0 HEA shows a high 
yield strength (YS) of ∼ 820 ± 7 MPa, and an ultimate tensile 
strength (UTS) of ∼ 1190 ± 16 MPa, as well as a large ductility 
of ∼ 22.9 ± 2.0%. As the Cr concentration increases to 20 at.%, the 
YS and the UTS increased to ∼ 875 ± 6 MPa and ∼ 1290 ± 12 MPa, 
respectively, with a large tensile ductility of ∼ 34.5 ± 0.1%. 
Moreover, as shown in Fig. 4 b, the high YS of ∼ 784 ± 6 MPa 
and UTS of ∼ 913 ± 20 MPa are achieved in the Cr0 alloy when 
tested at 600 °C. However, different from the tensile deformation 
at room temperature, the Cr0 alloy turns to be extremely brit- 
tle at 600 °C with a poor ductility of ∼ 3.4 ± 1.1%. Fortunately, 
the tensile elongation can be successively improved by substitut- 
ing Fe with Cr. When the alloy contains 20 at.% Cr, a distinct 
brittle-to-ductile transition at the high YS level can be readily ob- 
served from these HEAs, exhibiting a large tensile elongation up 
to around ∼ 25.2 ± 1.2%. More excitingly, the Cr20 HEA exhibits 
an excellent strain hardening capability, with a high UTS reach- 
ing ∼ 1136 ± 24 MPa at 600 °C. For more clarity, as shown in 
Fig. 4 c, a direct comparison with many other materials including 
HEAs [ 5 , 30–35 ], Co-based superalloy [36] , and several commercial 
Ni-based superalloys [37–41] is plotted in σUTS × ε EL value ver- 
sus yield strength. Apparently, the present Cr20 HEA has an out- 
standing strength-ductility combination ( σUTS × ε EL = 28 GPa % ) at 
600 °C when compared with other high-performance alloys. 
Fig. 5 further presents the EBSD results of the fractured Cr0 
and Cr20 HEAs. As shown in Fig. 5 a 1 –b 2 , when deformed at room 
temperature, the gradient inverse pole ﬁgure (IPF) color maps in- 
dicate the large changes of interior misorientations, and the high 
kernel average misorientation (KAM) values in the vicinity of GBs 
suggest a largely concentrated plastic deformation along them. De- 
spite the high-level stress accumulation, no grain boundary-type 
fractures were detected in both of them, as revealed in Fig. 5 c and 
d. In strong contrast, when deformed at 600 °C, distinctly differ- 
ent deformation and fracture behaviors can be clearly identiﬁed. As 
shown in Fig. 5 e 1–2 , the serious stress localization and associated 
intergranular micro-cracks can be clearly evidenced along GBs in 
the Cr0 alloy. However, for the Cr20 alloy, although being identiﬁed 
with serious stress localization at the grain boundaries, no inter- 
granular cracking occurs, suggesting a superior resistance against 
the intermediate-temperature embrittlement (see Fig. 5 g and h).

4.3. Elimination of oxygen-induced embrittlement by protective 
oxides layer at elevated temperature 
To uncover the origins of intergranular embrittlement in the 
Cr0 HEA at 600 °C, we further performed the tensile test under 
a vacuum condition at the same temperature. As illustrated in 
Fig. 11 , it is surprising to ﬁnd that a considerably recovered ten- 
sile elongation up to ∼ 25% can be achieved. Correspondingly, the 
fracture mode is changed from the brittle intergranular fractures in 
the air into the ductile dimpled fractures under vacuum, seen from 
the right column. This is evidence that the environmental oxygen 
attack should be the main cause of the premature brittle fractures 
at this temperature. Speciﬁcally, the embrittling element of oxygen 
coming from the atmosphere can quickly diffuse along GBs which 
lowers the cohesion of them when tested at 600 °C and leads to 
the easy onset of intergranular cracking [68] . This could be even 
worse when the process is assisted by highly concentrated stress. 
Such a dynamic oxygen embrittlement behavior has also been fre- 
quently observed in many other superalloys and intermetallic com- 
pounds under a similar temperature range, which severely limits 
their practical applications [22] . Fortunately, based on the above 
analysis, the controllable alloying additions of Cr can well defeat 
this problem, which should be reasonably ascribed to the improved 
oxidation resistance. Next, we will focus on revealing the underly- 
ing mechanisms of such a brittle-to-ductile transition by carefully 
characterizing the oxidation behaviors of the Cr0 and Cr20 HEAs. 
The cross sections of the fractured samples deformed at 600 °C 
were carefully examined. For the Cr0 alloy, the oxide layer with 
an average thickness of ∼ 258 nm formed on the sample surface 
can be clearly found, as shown in Fig. 12 a. Analyzed from the EDX 
line spectrums in Fig. 12 b, the oxide scale of the Cr0 HEA can be

vored oxide, the NiO is found to be highly concentrated under-

oxide layer is not able to offer suﬃcient protection against oxy- 
gen attack. Moreover, it is worth noting that there is no protective 
Al 2 O 3 layer formed on the surface of the Cr0 HEA, which can be

5. Conclusions 
In the present work, we have elaborately designed a series of 
L1 2 -strengthened HEAs with various Fe/Cr concentrations based 
on the CALPHAD technique. The phase stability, grain structures, 
mechanical properties, as well as the underlying deformation be- 
haviors and fracture mechanisms have been systematically in- 
vestigated. The coherent multicomponent L1 2 -type nanoparticles 
were controllably introduced without forming other brittle pre- 
cipitates, which enables the superior strength-ductility synergies 
at both room and elevated temperatures. More remarkably, the 
intermediate-temperature embrittlement along GBs can be effec- 
tively defeated by introducing compact protective oxide layers 
through the optimized regulation of Cr doping. Beneﬁting from this 
design, the fracture mode could be changed from the brittle inter- 
granular cleavage to the ductile dimpled transgranular failure, re- 
sulting in a substantial increase of tensile ductility at 600 °C. Taken 
together, our work provides new insights into the controllable al- 
loy design of nanoparticles-strengthened HEAs for achieving well- 
targeted performance enhancement. It is also our belief that moti- 
vated by the present work, more and more novel structural alloys 
with further improved thermal-mechanical properties can be de- 
veloped.
