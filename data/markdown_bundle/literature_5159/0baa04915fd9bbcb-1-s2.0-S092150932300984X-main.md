ABSTRACT 
Achieving a reliable bond between advanced ceramics and high-temperature application alloys for high- 
temperature applications can greatly enhance the operating temperature range of vital components like en -
gines and turbines. In this work, we have achieved success in bonding Yttria-stabilized zirconia (YSZ) ceramics 
with TiAl-base alloys through the use of a novel ceramic-metal joining method called thermoplastic bonding 
(TPB). Compared with traditional brazing methods, TPB requires a lower bonding temperature that is below the 
melting point of the filler. This advantage enables TPB to use fillers with higher melting points, resulting in 
improved high-temperature performance of the joint. Furthermore, TPB substitutes the conventional liquid phase 
bonding method in brazing with a solid phase bonding process. This not only diminishes residual stress on the 
joint but also prevents the generation of fragile oxides, which effectively enhances the strength of the joint. Shear 
tests have demonstrated that TPB joints possess excellent room temperature strength, reaching up to 141 MPa. 
Remarkably, the TPB joint retains remarkable strength, with a value of approximately 84 MPa even when 
exposed to a high temperature of 600 
◦
C. These test results proved that TPB was an effective strategy for suc -
cessfully realizing high-strength and high-temperature resistant metal-ceramic joins. 
1. Introduction 
In the global context, the depletion of fossil fuels and the excessive 
emission of greenhouse gases are two crucial factors impeding human 
progress. In view of these challenges, researchers are diligently striving 
to enhance energy efficiency while simultaneously lowering greenhouse 
gas emissions. This can be done by increasing the operating temperature 
of the engine and turbine [ 1 ]. Currently, superalloys and TiAl-based 
alloys are the common materials used for these applications. However, 
replacing these traditional alloys with advanced ceramics can greatly 
improve the operating temperature of high-temperature components [ 2 , 
3 ]. Advanced ceramic materials possess outstanding mechanical, tribo -
logical, chemical, and physical properties that surpass those of super -
alloys, and can sustain these attributes even at elevated temperatures. 
Nonetheless, their inherent brittleness and rigidity present significant 
challenges in the production of large and intricately shaped components, 
thus hampering their potential applications [ 4 – 6 ]. Joining ceramics 
with metals is an effective method to overcome the above-mentioned 
problems. Therefore, it is of great significance to realize the reliable 
joining between ceramics and metals, especially at high temperatures. 
So far, the principal methods used to achieve the bonding of ceramics 
and metals include solid-phase diffusion bonding (SDB), transient liquid 
phase diffusion bonding (TLP), and vacuum brazing [ 7 – 9 ]. SDB has the 
capability to acquire joints with exceptional high-temperature proper -
ties. However, its intricate process and exceedingly high surface re -
quirements a challenge when implementing it in large-scale production 
[ 10 – 12 ]. The use of TLP solves the problem of strict surface quality re -
quirements of SDB by adding a low melting point intermediate layer to 
wet the base materials. However, in order to make the joint composition 
as uniform as possible, the holding time of this method is often very 
long, which greatly reduces its efficiency in production applications 
[ 13 ]. Brazing is widely regarded as the most commonly used method for 
ceramic-metal joining primarily due to its simplicity, cost-effectiveness, 
and exceptional welding quality [ 14 – 18 ]. However, brazed joints are 
often limited in their high-temperature performance due to the rela -
tively low melting point of brazing filler metals [ 17 – 19 ]. The brazing

In our previous work, we developed a novel joining process called 
TPB and successfully joined dissimilar metals [ 13 , 23 ]. The method uses 
metallic glasses (MG) as an intermediate layer, and utilizes its super -
plastic deformation in the superliquid region to completely fill the rough 
surface of the base materials to achieve the same effect as liquid wetting 
[ 26 – 28 ], and then connect through the atomic diffusion between the 
filler and the base materials. Compared to other joining processes, this 
method has low requirements on the surface of the base materials and 
the holding time is short. Most importantly, the bonding temperature is 
not limited by the melting point of the filler, so high melting point fillers 
such as Ni-based, Fe-based amorphous, etc. can be used, which will 
greatly increase the operating temperature of the joint. In addition, TPB 
uses solid-phase bonding instead of liquid-phase bonding. This not only 
reduces the residual stress experienced by the joint, but also inhibits the 
formation of bulk intermetallic compounds, which contributes to the 
improvement of joint performance [ 13 , 29 – 32 ]. In this work, the TPB 
process was used to join TiAl alloy and YSZ ceramic. TiAl alloys are often 
used in the manufacture of aircraft turbines due to their low density, 
high-temperature resistance and oxidation resistance [ 33 , 34 ]. In order 
to increase the maximum operating temperature of aircraft turbine 
components, YSZ ceramics are usually joined to TiAl alloys as thermal 
insulation [ 35 , 36 ]. At present, the joining of YSZ ceramics and TiAl alloy

non-reactive atoms with ceramics is very difficult [ 41 ]. Therefore, when 
the holding time is too short, the bonding of YSZ ceramic and Ni-Nb 
interlayer is insufficient, and the joint strength is relatively low. In 
this case, cracking occurred at the interface due to the residual stress 
generated by the thermal expansion coefficient mismatch. With the 
extension of the holding time, the interdiffusion between the interme -
diate layer and the base materials intensified, and the microstructure of 
the joint changed significantly. On the YSZ ceramic side, sufficient 
diffusion of elements between the intermediate layer and the YSZ 
ceramic makes the interfacial bonding strength increase and the 
boundary line disappears. Correspondingly, due to the enhanced inter -
diffusion between TiAl alloy and the intermediate layer, zone I (dif -
fusion-reaction zone adjacent to TiAl) become thicker with the 
increasing of holding time. But the zone II will tend to be thinner. This is 
because more Ti, Al elements diffuse into the Ni-Nb intermediate layer, 
making the amorphous crystallization zone II partly transformed into 
the reaction diffusion zone I. It is worth noting that zone II-layer I and 
zone II-layer III which is composed of Nb

3.5. The bonding process of thermoplastic bonding 
A concept physical model of the bonding process was established, as 
shown in Fig. 11 . It can be seen that the bonding was mainly divided into 
two stages: the superplastic deformation stage and the atomic diffusion 
stage. The main purpose of the first stage is to achieve a good physical 
pre-combination of the base materials and the filler, and achieve the 
same effect as the liquid phase wetting through solid phase plastic 
deformation. When the temperature is lower than T
g
, the plastic defor -
mation ability of MG is very poor. Even under the action of pressure, the 
surface of the base materials cannot be filled by MG, and there are a 
large number of cracks and holes at the interface between the base 
materials and MG. As the temperature rises to the superliquid region (T
g
- 
T
x
), the viscosity of MG decreases significantly and the deformability 
increases. Under the action of pressure, MG can completely fill the 
surface of the base materials to achieve good physical pre-bonding. 
Johnson et al. proved that it is difficult for atoms to diffuse in the 
superliquid region [ 44 ]. Therefore, there is no atomic diffusion behavior 
at the interface in stage 1. When the temperature rises above T
x
, the MG 
crystallizes. Interdiffusion of atoms occurs due to the difference in 
atomic concentration between the interlayer and the base materials. It is 
worth noting that since the diffusion rate of Ni atoms is greater than that 
of Nb atoms, Nb-rich regions are formed near the base materials on both 
sides. Due to the more difficult diffusion of atoms in YSZ ceramics, the 
Nb-rich region near the YSZ ceramics is narrow compared to the other 
side. In the subsequent crystallization process, the Nb-rich region 
transforms into Nb
7
Ni
6 
phase, while the intermediate eutectic compo -
sition region transforms into Nb
7
Ni
6
+ NbNi
3 
eutectic phase region. In 
addition, a reliable bond is formed due to interdiffusion, atomic 
bonding, or metallurgical reactions at the base materials and interlayer 
interface. 
4. Conclusion 
YSZ ceramic and TiAl alloy were successfully bonded by utilizing the 
TPB method with a Ni
60
Nb
40 
filler. The joining process is divided into 
two stages: In the first stage, good physical pre-bonding of YSZ ceramics 
and TiAl alloy was achieved through superplastic deformation of Ni-Nb 
metallic glass. In the second stage, the crystallized Ni-Nb eutectic 
diffused with the base materials to form a good metallurgical joining. 
The main conclusions are summarized as follows: 
(1) The plastic deformation ability of MG in the super liquid region is 
greatly improved. Under the action of pressure, the cracks and 
holes on the surface of YSZ ceramics can be completely filled to 
form a good physical pre-combination. 
(2) The formation of the joint microstructure is mainly through two 
modes: the crystallization of MG and the interdiffusion between 
the interlayer and the matrix. The good bonding of the interme -
diate layer and the base materials is achieved by solid phase 
diffusion. 
(3) The shear strength of the joint at room temperature is closely 
related to the microstructural evolution, and reaches the highest 
value of 141 MPa when bonded at 1020 
◦
C for 60min. Since the 
high melting point of Ni
60
Nb
40 
filler used in this work, the joint 
can still maintain high strength at 600 
◦
C, about 84 MPa.
