ABSTRACT
Annealing is a prevalent heat treatment process frequently employed for optimizing alloy properties. Typically, 
increasing annealing temperature gives rise to an improved ductility but a decreased strength. However, this 
work reported a synergistic increase in strength-ductility-wear resistance of Fe
5
Co
5
CrNi
14
Cu
75 
alloy via 
increasing the annealing temperature. Compared with the unannealed specimen, the yield strength of the 
specimen annealed at 1100 
◦
C increased by 33.1 %, the elongation increased by 35.3 %, the coefficient of friction 
decreased by 10.5 %, and the cross-sectional area of wear scars decreased by 33.2 %. Lattice distortion induced 
by annealing contributed to enhancing hardness, which in turn improved the wear resistance, resulting in a 
transition of the wear mode from fatigue wear to abrasive wear.
1. Introduction
In the field of materials science, it is widely accepted that the 
alloying of elements with disparate atomic sizes into a common lattice 
engenders lattice distortion, causing constituent atoms to deviate from 
their intended positions and thereby giving rise to the lattice distortion 
effect [1,2] . The lattice distortion effect induces fluctuations in the 
highly elastic stress/strain field, which has been proven to play an 
important role in dislocation behavior [3] . Furthermore, lattice distor -
tion facilitates the stabilization of thermodynamic non-equilibrium 
states, which in turn reduces the energy barriers for reactive species 
such as electrons and ions [4,5] . Consequently, alloys with distorted 
lattices exhibit a more remarkable array of multifunctional properties 
compared to conventional alloys. For instance, the single BCC phase 
HfNbTaTiV alloy, designed with lattice distortion effects in mind, ex -
hibits a compressive yield strength of 1350 MPa at room temperature, 
coupled with an elongation exceeding 45 % [6] . The FeCoNiCrMn alloy 
(FCC phase), known for its excellent low-temperature fracture toughness 
[7] , has been demonstrated to possess appreciable local lattice distor -
tions [8] . Moreover, the newly emerged (CoNi)
50
(TiZrHf)
50 
alloy 
displayed a pronounced Elinvar effect of non-magnetic origin [9] due to 
severe lattice distortion, rendering it suitable for deployment in complex 
and extreme environments. Such valuable research endeavors have 
significantly heightened attention towards lattice distortion.
Available investigations have demonstrated that severe lattice dis -
tortions can exert high frictional resistance to dislocation motion, pre -
venting dislocation slip and increasing the yield stress [10] . This 
presents a highly promising foundation for material design in multi -
component alloys, as each atom experiences a distinct set of neighboring 
atoms, generating ubiquitous local lattice distortions and stresses. 
Particularly, for the FCC phase alloys with excellent ductility but inferior 
strength, the introduction of pronounced lattice distortions can help to 
increase the yield strength, thus broadening their application fields. In 
the existing studies, lattice distortion has been intensified in the alloys 
through modulation of elemental proportions [11] , or by virtue of the 
addition of larger-sized atoms such as V and Al [12] . For example, Wang 
et al. [13] demonstrated that changing the proportion of Hf element in 
TiNbTaZrHfx could elevate the yield strength to 973.3 MPa (with a Hf 
element proportion of 0.122). Yin et al. [14] substantiated that the 
addition of the V element played a crucial role in enhancing the strength

Annealing is a widely employed heat treatment process in which the 
metal is slowly heated to a preset temperature, held for a sufficient 
period, and then cooled to room temperature at a suitable rate. The 
solubility and atomic diffusion ability of solid-solution elements are 
enhanced at high temperatures, which helps to weaken elemental 
segregation [15] , as well as to relieve residual stresses introduced by 
cold working. In general, an increase in annealing temperature is 
accompanied by a trade-off between improved ductility and decreased 
strength [16,17] . However, available studies have found that the 
increasing annealing temperature promotes phase dissolution [18] , 
which allows atoms of different sizes to crowd into the same lattice, 
exacerbating lattice distortion and enhancing strength. After homoge -

elevated annealing temperatures abnormally led to an increase in

novel approach for synergistically enhancing the strength, hardness, and 
ductility of the alloy.

3.3. Wear performance
The tribological behavior of specimens subjected to different 
annealing temperatures is depicted in Fig. 3 . In the initial stage of the 
coefficient of friction (COF) curves, all curves went through a wear 
period where the COF increased rapidly. This process could be ratio -
nalized in terms of the molecular mechanics perspective [32] , as 
described in Eq. (1) . 
μ = α A / F + β (1) 
where μ is the friction coefficient (equivalent to COF) of the material, α is 
a coefficient that depends on the molecular effect on the surface, A is the 
contact area, F is the nominal load, and β is a mechanical action coef -
ficient. Since α and β could be approximated to remain unchanged when 
the friction environment and the material of the friction pair remained 
the same, the friction coefficient μ showed an approximate positive 
correlation with the contact area. In the initial wear stage, the friction 
partner gradually transitioned from point contact to face contact, which 
brought about a continuous increase in the contact area, further causing 
a continuous increase in the COF curve. Similar conclusions were 
reached by Yang et al. [33] from a Hertzian contact perspective.
The COF was a pivotal parameter in assessing the tribological con -
dition of a material. Generally, the lower the COF of a material under the 
same wear conditions, the better its wear resistance. The COF of the

4.2. Mechanical reinforcement mechanisms
Phase dissolution increased chemical complexity within the solid 
solution, as atoms of varying sizes were forced into the same lattice, 
thereby exacerbating lattice distortion. This lattice distortion raised the 
external force required for dislocation slip, leading to enhanced 
strength. However, whether the strength enhancement induced by 
annealing was entirely due to lattice distortion strengthening remained 
to be determined. It is widely known that the strengthening mechanisms

5. Conclusions
In this work, the synergistic enhancement of strength, hardness, and 
ductility induced by anomalous annealing in the Fe
5
Co
5
CrNi
14
Cu
75 
alloy 
has been reported. As the annealing temperature increased from room 
temperature to 1100
◦
C, the tensile yield strength and elongation were 
enhanced by 33.1 % and 35.3 %, respectively. The elevation in strength 
resulted from exacerbated lattice distortion that enhanced the lattice 
friction stress. The improved ductility originated from the dissolution of 
phases with increasing annealing temperature which reduced the 
interfacial mismatch and relieved the stress concentration. In addition, 
the wear resistance of the Fe
5
Co
5
CrNi
14
Cu
75 
alloy also improved with 
increasing annealing temperature, which was attributed to the enhanced 
hardness induced by lattice distortion. When the annealing temperature 
increased from room temperature to 1100
◦
C, the hardness increased 
from 1.96 GPa to 2.31 GPa, the coefficient of friction decreased from 
0.57 to 0.51, the cross-sectional area of wear scars decreased from 
186.3 µ m

tigue wear to abrasive wear. This work provides an effective strategy for 
exploiting annealing treatment to achieve lattice distortion and phase 
dissolution, which in turn synergistically elevate the strength, hardness, 
and ductility of alloys.
