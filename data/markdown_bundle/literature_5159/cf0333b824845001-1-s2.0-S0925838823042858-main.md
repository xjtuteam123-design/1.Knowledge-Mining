ABSTRACT 
The characteristic of the γ ′ strengthening phase plays a crucial role in the development of high performance 
nickel-based superalloys, which is strongly dependent on the kinetic behavior of γ ′ precipitation. Herein, the 
precipitation kinetics of the nanoscale γ ′ phase in Ni – 10 Al – 8.5 Cr – x Mo at% ( x = 2, 3, and 4) quaternary alloys 
aged at 1073 K are investigated using CALPHAD-assisted phase field simulations. Our calculations show that the 
higher Mo content and lower aging temperatures delay the growth and coarsening kinetics of the γ ′ phase, which 
can be attributed to the decrease of Al chemical mobility. Moreover, although Mo is a γ– stabilized element, 
previous experiments, thermodynamic calculations, and our simulations all indicate that the γ ′ volume fraction 
increases abnormally with increasing Mo concentration in Ni – Al – Cr – Mo quaternary alloys, which is attributed to 
Mo occupying the Al sites of γ ′ -Ni
3
Al and the elemental partitioning behaviors between the γ matrix and the γ ′ 
phase. In addition, Mo is a potential element to reduce the content of Re and W elements in nickel-based su -
peralloys because of its lower density and stronger solid solution strengthening effect in the γ matrix. Our work 
can guide the design and development of lightweight nickel-based superalloys with superior microstructural 
stability and mechanical properties for engineering applications. 
1. Introduction 
Nickel-based superalloys have been widely used as the hot-end 
components in aircraft engines and industrial gas turbines owing to 
their superior microstructural stability and mechanical properties at 
elevated temperatures [1] . Ni, Al, and Cr are the three common elements 
in nickel-based superalloys, and the Ni – Al – Cr alloy is the basic subsys -
tem of commercial nickel-based superalloys [2] . Generally, Ni provides 
the γ matrix, Al promotes the formation and stabilization of the γ ′ phase, 
and Cr strengthens the γ matrix and improves the oxidation and corro -
sion resistance [3,4] . One of the design goals for material scientists is to 
improve the high temperature thermal stability of the γ / γ ′ microstruc -
ture to increase the service life of nickel-based superalloys [5,6] . To 
achieve this goal, large amounts of refractory elements, such as Mo, Ta, 
W, Re, Ru, etc., have been added to the Ni – Al – Cr model superalloys over 
the past few decades to improve the mechanical properties of 
nickel-based superalloys at high temperatures [7 – 10] . Unfortunately, 
the high-density refractory elements Ta, W, and Re have been found to 
segregate most severely during directional solidification due to the 
sluggish elemental diffusivity in the γ matrix, resulting in an inhomo -
geneous composition distribution within the microstructure [11,12] . In 
particular, the microsegregation of Re and W elements could induce the 
formation of solidification defects (e.g. freckles) [13,14] , which weaken 
the mechanical properties of nickel-based superalloys [15 – 17] . 
Besides, the addition of refractory elements has inevitably increased 
the density of nickel-based superalloys, particularly W and Re elements, 
which is not conducive to improving the thrust-to-weight ratio and en -
ergy efficiency of turbine engines. In other words, high density has 
become a bottleneck in the development of advanced nickel-based su -
peralloys. Meanwhile, it is very time- and cost-consuming to reduce the 
microsegregation by optimizing the processing parameters of solid so -
lution heat treatment due to the sluggish diffusivity of the refractory 
elements W and Re [18] . Therefore, the major method to figure out the 
above two problems is to reduce the content of W and Re elements in 
nickel-based superalloys.

determined by the volume fraction, morphology, size, and distribution 
of the γ ′ particles [31,32] . However, the γ ′ particles would inevitably 
grow during the long-term service process at elevated temperatures, 
which reduced the service life of the nickel-based superalloys [33] . 
Herein, we perform CALPHAD-assisted phase field (PF) simulations to 
investigate the effect of Mo addition on the temporal evolution kinetics 
of the γ ′ phase separation process in Ni – 10 Al – 8.5 Cr – x Mo at% ( x = 2, 3, 
and 4) quaternary model superalloys. It is found that the γ ′ coarsening 
rate decreases significantly with increasing Mo concentration. More -
over, the feasibility of reducing the elemental content of W and Re in 
nickel-based superalloys by Mo is discussed in terms of elemental par -
titioning coefficients and solid solution strengthening effects. In addi -
tion, the effect of aging temperatures on the γ ′ precipitation kinetics in 
the Ni – 10 Al – 8.5 Cr – 4 Mo at% alloy is investigated in detail to reveal the 
temperature effect. Further analysis suggests that the supersaturation of 
the γ matrix and atomic diffusion play important roles in the tempera -
ture effect. Our work provides insights into understanding the influence 
of Mo concentrations and aging temperatures on the evolution of γ ′ 
nanostructure and composition in the Ni – 10 Al – 8.5 Cr – x Mo at% ( x = 2, 
3, and 4) model superalloys.

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Evolution kinetics of γ ′ phase in Ni – Al – Cr – Mo alloys with increased 
Mo concentration 
Fig. 1 displays the pseudo-ternary isothermal section phase diagram 
of the Ni-rich corner of the Ni – Al – Cr – Mo system at 1073 K and the 
investigated Ni – 10 Al – 8.5 Cr – x Mo at% ( x = 2, 3, and 4) alloys. The 
equilibrium phase boundaries can be obtained using the Pandat ther -
modynamic software with the PanNi2022 database. As can be seen from 
Fig. 1 , the Ni – 10 Al – 8.5 Cr – x Mo at% ( x = 2, 3, and 4) alloys are located 
in the γ + γ ′ two-phase region, indicating that the investigated alloys 
exist γ→γ ′ phase separation process at 1073 K. The major reasons for 
choosing the Ni – 10 Al – 8.5 Cr – x Mo at% ( x = 2, 3, and 4) alloys are as 
follows: (1) the numerous works have focused on the effect of 2 at% Ta, 
W, and Re additions on the effect of γ ′ precipitation kinetics in Ni – 10 
Al – 8.5 Cr at% alloy aged at 1073 K [51 – 55] ; (2) the topological 
close-packed (TCP) phase inevitably precipitates when the Mo content is 
greater than 6 at% as shown in Fig. 1 , which degrades the mechanical 
properties of alloys [56] ;. 
Fig. 2 illustrates the evolution of γ ′ morphology of Ni – 10 Al – 8.5 Cr – x 
Mo at% ( x = 2, 3, and 4) alloys aged at 1073 K for different aging times. 
It can be observed that the γ ′ morphology is spherical and the γ ′ size 
gradually increases with increasing aging time. Meanwhile, we find that 
the elongated γ ′ precipitates start to appear due to the coalescence be -
tween adjacent γ ′ particles, as marked as yellow dotted circles in Fig. 2 . 
With the extension of aging time, the coalesced γ ′ particles gradually 
grow into a single γ ′ domain. Therefore, in Ni – 10 Al – 8.5 Cr – x Mo at% 
( x = 2, 3 and 4) alloys, it can be concluded that the interconnected γ ′ is a 
transition state during the γ ′ evolution process.

mined by the γ / γ ′ lattice misfit, which is defined as [1,57] .

It is well known that the sign and magnitude of the γ / γ ′ lattice misfit 
is determined by the composition between the γ matrix and the γ ′ phase 
and the aging temperature (see Eqs. (14 – 16) ). Fig. 3 (a) and (b) show the 
calculated lattice parameter and γ / γ ′ lattice misfit as a function of Mo

Fig. 3 (a) that the lattice parameters of the γ ′ phase are larger than the 
lattice parameters of the γ matrix, resulting in a positive γ / γ ′ lattice 
misfit in Ni – 10 Al – 8.5 Cr – x Mo at% ( x = 2, 3, and 4) quaternary model 
superalloys aged at 1073 K. Besides, the γ / γ ′ lattice misfit changes from 
a positive to a negative value as the aging temperature increases as 
shown in Fig. 3 (b), which is attributed to the γ lattice parameter being 
higher than that of the γ ′ phase at high temperature. In addition, it is also 
found that the γ / γ ′ lattice misfit in Ni – Al – Mo alloys decreases with

and a negative value at high Mo content [61] . Meanwhile, the γ / γ ′ lattice

4. Conclusion 
The present work focuses on the effect of Mo addition and aging 
temperatures on the γ ′ precipitation kinetics in Ni – Al – Cr – Mo quaternary 
model superalloys during the isothermal exposure process. Based on the 
simulation and statistical analysis, the main conclusions can be drawn as 
follows: 
(1) The morphology of the γ ′ phase represents the sphere associated 
with a small γ / γ ′ lattice misfit. A higher Mo content can effec -
tively improve the γ / γ ′ thermal stability of Ni – Al – Cr – Mo qua -
ternary model superalloys due to the reduced diffusion 
coefficient of Al and the hindering effect of Cr and Mo atoms. At 
the steady-state coarsening stage, the 3 at% Mo alloy has a higher 
γ ′ coarsening rate compared to the 2 at% Mo alloy, which is 
attributed to the increase in the γ ′ volume fraction. 
(2) Al, Cr, and Mo all preferentially occupy the Al sites of γ ′ -Ni
