ABSTRACT 
Mg – Li – Al (LA143) alloys with high strength and plastic deformability were prepared through a combination of 
heat treatment and multi-directional forging in a channel die (MDFC). The Vickers hardness of the alloy 
depended on the quenching temperature. As the applied equivalent strain was increased, the Vickers hardness 
and yield strength increased. The maximum specific yield strength of the Mg – Li – Al alloy in this study was 263 
kN ⋅ m ⋅ kg
- 1
. The limit of reduction during cold rolling of all MDFCed LA143 samples exceeded 99%. X-ray 
diffraction, field emission scanning electron microscopy, and energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy, revealed that 
the high specific yield strength could be attributed to severe plastic deformation as well as spinodal decompo -
sition of the body-centered cubic phase. Therefore, LA143 alloys with excellent mechanical properties can be 
prepared by heat treatment and severe plastic deformation. 
1. Introduction 
Mg alloys are attracting attention as lightweight structural materials. 
They are classified into two groups based on their crystallographic 
structures: those with hexagonal close-packed (HCP) or body-centered 
cubic (BCC) structures. Mg alloys with the HCP structure have a high 
specific strength; however, these alloys have poor deformability at room 
temperature because of their plastic anisotropy, which is attributed to 
the HCP structure [ 1 – 7 ]. On the other hand, Mg – Li alloys with higher 
than 11 mass% Li content have a BCC structure. These alloys have 
excellent deformability and an ultra-low density; however, their 
strength is significantly lower than that of other lightweight structural 
materials [ 8 – 14 ]. It is difficult to reduce the density of Mg alloys through 
structure control; therefore, strengthening Mg – Li-base alloys with a BCC 
structure is desirable for the development of ultra-lightweight structural 
materials with good mechanical properties such as strength and plastic 
deformability. 
To improve the strength of a material without changing the density, 
grain refinement strengthening is preferred. Grain refinement can be 
attained through various severe plastic deformation (SPD) techniques, 
such as accumulative roll-bonding (ARB), accumulative channel-die 
compression bonding (ACCB), and multi-directional forging (MDF) 
[ 15 – 18 ]. As Mg – Li-base alloys generally have poor resistance to corro -
sion, SPD techniques that bond materials, such as ARB, are not prefer -
able for the structure control of these alloys. On the other hand, MDF 
does not require the bonding of materials. In particular, MDF in a 
channel die (MDFC) is attracting attention as a superior SPD technique 
because it is capable of introducing a larger equivalent strain, as 
compared to MDF, and does not require material bonding [ 19 – 21 ]. 
In previous studies, Mg-11.0 mass%Li-3.3 mass%Al-0.2 mass%Zr-0.6 
mass%Y [ 22 ] and Mg-11.2 mass%Li-0.95 mass%Al-0.43 mass%Zn [ 23 ] 
were prepared by spinodal decomposition of the BCC phase. This 
involved a specific heat-treatment, followed by water-quenching. Kato 
observed that spinodal decomposition of the BCC phase contributes to 
strengthening the materials [ 24 ]. In this study, a high-specific-strength 
LA143 alloy, Mg – 14Li – 3Al (mass%), was prepared by a combination 
of MDFC and spinodal decomposition techniques. The LA143 alloy was 
selected as it contains Al, which was a constituent in previous Mg – Li- -
base alloys that underwent spinodal decomposition [ 22 , 23 ].
