1. Introduction
With the advancement of technology, the performance requirements 
for high-temperature materials in aerospace and other industries have 
become increasingly stringent. For example, the service temperatures of 
high-temperature components such as hypersonic vehicles, aircraft en -
gines, and gas turbines, exceed 1600 ℃ , reaching up to 2200 ℃ [1 – 3] . As 
a result, there is an urgent need for the development of next-generation 
refractory alloys with excellent high-temperature properties (high 
strength at elevated-temperatures). Traditional nickel-based superal -
loys, with relatively low melting points, are limited to service temper -
atures not exceeding 1200 ℃ [4] . Conventional high-melting-point 
refractory alloys, such as solid-solution alloys of tantalum, tungsten, 
molybdenum, and rhenium, suffer from diffusion-induced softening at 
elevated temperatures, leading to insufficient high-temperature 
strength, often below 200 MPa [5,6] . In recent years, compared with 
traditional high-temperature alloys and refractory alloys, high-entropy 
alloys (RHEAs) have exhibited superior room-temperature and 
high-temperature mechanical properties [7,8] . For example, the 
HfMoNbTaTiZr RHEA can still achieve a yield strength of 556 MPa at 
1200 ℃ [7] and retains a compressive fracture strain of 12 % at room 
temperature. However, due to the insufficient melting points and 
high-temperature softening resistance of the main elements of the alloy, 
the high-temperature mechanical properties of such RHEAs deteriorate 
significantly at higher temperatures. Notably, refractory high-entropy 
alloys (RHEAs) with refractory elements as the main components, 
such as NbMoTaW [9] RHEA show exceptional high-temperature 
strength above 1200 ℃ . NbMoTaW RHEA exhibits yield strengths of 
up to 421 MPa at 1400 ℃ and 405 MPa at 1600 ℃ . However, NbMoTaW 
RHEA displayed room-temperature brittleness with a low compressive 
fracture strain of 2.6 %, and NbMoTaW-based RHEAs generally 
exhibited severe room-temperature brittleness [10,11] . Therefore, the 
challenge of further improving the plasticity of NbMoTaW RHEA while 
maintaining its high-temperature strength has become the primary focus 
of current research.
In previous studies, various investigations have concentrated on 
enhancing the plasticity of NbMoTaW RHEAs through the incorporation 
of ductile metal elements [12 – 14] . Wan et al. [12] fabricated the

and C was set as 0.1:1.6:0.4:x, where x = 0.35, 0.5, and 0.7. Metal 
particles and carbide powder raw materials were used to prepare the 
corresponding elements. Metal particles Re, Ta and W with purities

introduced as TaC powder (5 μ m, 99.9 wt%) wrapped in Ta foil (0.1 mm 
thickness) to prevent carbon loss and powder splatter during melting. 
Before melting, the furnace chamber was evacuated to 7 × 10
 4 
Pa and 
filled with high-purity argon gas (99.999 %) to 0.5 MPa as a protective 
gas to ensure an oxygen-free environment. The ingots were re-melted 
eight times (melting current was 500 – 800 A, each melting duration 
was at least 3 min, and each interval was cooled for at least 5 min.) to 
ensure chemical homogeneity. After cooling of the ingot, button shaped 
samples with a 30 mm diameter and 10 mm height with a bright surface 
were obtained. Specimens for the experiments were cut from the ingots 
using a wire electrical discharge machine, and their surfaces were ul -
trasonically cleaned in ethanol before testing.

5. Conclusion
In this study, a series of Re
0.1
Ta
1.6
W
0.6
(TaC)
x 
(x = 0.35, 0.5, 0.7) 
refractory composites were fabricated, and their microstructural char -
acteristics and mechanical performance were investigated. 
(1) The composites consist of a BCC matrix and Ta
2
C carbide phase, 
with microstructural transitions from near-eutectic (x = 0.35, 
hypoeutectic; x = 0.5, slightly hypereutectic) to hypereutectic 
(x = 0.7) as TaC content increases, accompanied by a 
corresponding rise in Ta
2
C volume fraction. All composites 
exhibit a co-continuous 3D network of BCC matrix and Ta
2
C 
phase.
(2) At room temperature, near-eutectic composites (x = 0.35/0.5) 
balance high strength (1428 – 1614 MPa) with ductility 
(9.3 – 10.9 %); hypereutectic (x = 0.7) shows ultra-high strength 
(2015 MPa) but brittle failure from excessive Ta
2
C. At high 
temperatures, all composites retain strengths > 800 MPa at 
1450 ℃ and > 450 MPa at 1750 ℃ , enabled by a co-continuous 
Ta
2
C network suppressing softening.
(3) The strength follows the mixture rule, primarily driven by the 
continuous three-dimensional network of carbide skeletons. The 
room-temperature ductility of the composites originates from the 
plasticity of Ta
2
C and the strain coordination capacity between 
carbides and the BCC phase in the eutectic structure, which fails 
when the Ta
2
C content exceeds the critical value (fully hyper -
eutectic). The co-continuous structure enables synergistic 
strengthening between phases, and the three-dimensional 
network of carbides effectively hinders grain boundary sliding 
of the BCC phase at high temperatures, underpinning its excep -
tional high-temperature performance.
