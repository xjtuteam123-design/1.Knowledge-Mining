ABSTRACT
Fabrication of large-sized Mg alloys with high strength is still challenging through conventional extrusion 
methods. In this work, we proposed a new strategy for achieving ultra-high strength in large-sized Mg alloy 
component via a novel differential-thermal extrusion strategy with a small extrusion ratio. After adopting this 
process, a traditional Mg-11Gd-2Y-1Zn-0.4Zr alloy obtains the exceptional strength-ductility balance, with 
tensile yield strength of ~465 MPa and ductility of ~6.0 %, which outperforms most high-RE-concentration Mg- 
Gd-Y alloys reported previously. As-extruded alloy shows a large proportion (~70 %) of non-dynamic recrys -
tallized (non-DRX) grains with strong texture. Abundant stacking faults (SFs) enriched by RE atoms coupled with 
long-period stacking ordered (LPSO) structures were developed in non-DRXed grains, which contributes to the 
good strength-ductility balance of the alloy. The aging induces a high density of prismatic β’ phases precipitated 
in grains, which further enhances the strength. Note that the vertical distribution of basal SFs/LPSO lamellae and 
prismatic β’ phases hinder dislocation motions more effectively than the single. Our work provides valuable 
insights into the fabrication of large-sized, high-strength Mg alloy extrusions with high RE concentrations.
1. Introduction
Magnesium (Mg) alloys have attracted considerable attention in 
aerospace and automotive fields owing to their remarkable advantages 
in terms of lightweight [1] . In contrast to aluminum (Al) alloys, how -
ever, commercial applications of Mg alloys remain comparatively 
restricted, primarily due to the lower yield strength [1 – 4] . The addition 
of rare earth (RE) elements, particularly Gd and Y, into Mg can induce a 
pronounced age-hardening response. When combined with Zn, the 
long-period stacking ordered (LPSO) phase is created to substantially 
improve mechanical properties [5,6] . Consequently, The 
Mg-Gd-Y-Zn-Zr/Mn alloy system has emerged as particularly promising 
for ultrahigh-strength applications, where trace Zr/Mn additions serve 
as effective grain refiners in as-cast microstructures. For instance, Heng 
et al. [5] reported a high-strength Mg-12.6Gd-1.3Y-0.9Zn-0.5Mn alloy 
processed via hot extrusion and strain aging, with TYS of 475 MPa and 
EL of 4.4 %. Liu et al. [7] using rotary backward extrusion developed 
high-performance Mg-9Gd-4Y-2Zn-0.5Zr (wt%) alloy, with tensile yield 
strength (TYS) of 328 MPa and a high elongation (EL) of 16.5 %. Note 
that these high-strength Mg-Gd-Y series alloys are mostly strengthened 
by fine grains, LPSO, deformation texture and nano-precipitates [1,6,7] .
Grain refinement has been widely recognized as one of the most 
effective strengthening ways in Mg alloys, mostly due to the high Hall- 
Petch coefficients for HCP Mg alloys [5 – 8] . Thermo-mechanical pro -
cessing, such as extrusion, rolling and forging, is an effective approach 
for grain refinement in Mg alloys. As an example, Wan et al. [8] using 
room-temperature rotary swaging fabricated a fully nano-grains in a 
Mg-8Gd-3Y-0.4Zr alloy bar, which achieves an exceptional TYS of 
650 MPa after peak aging. Formation of fine-grain structure depends 
mainly on dynamical recrystallization (DRX) during thermo-mechanical 
processing ( i.e. , hot-extrusion) or severe plastic deformation process, 
while obtaining the fully fine grains in the whole extruded parts needs a 
large extrusion ratio (often more than 20:1) [6 – 8] . Nevertheless, some 
Mg products with a large cross section is difficult to adopt a large 
extrusion ratio during industrial production, due to the limitations of 
forming equipment.
In recent years, such an extrusion process including low-temperature 
small extrusion ratio and differential-thermal extrusion coupled with 
aging can produces high-strength Mg alloy products. Li et al. [2] re -
ported the large cross section of high-strength Mg-13Gd alloy bar with a 
TYS of over 470 MPa via conventional extrusion at 320 º C along with a 
small extrusion ratio of ~4:1. Zhang et al. [3] developed high-strength

actual uses. Low-temperature extrusion of Mg-RE alloys are always of 
low ductility, which is mainly due to the formation of numerous coarse β 
phases that are created by static precipitation during pre-heating and 
dynamic precipitation during extrusion [2,3] . In addition, the relatively 
low preheated temperature will cause a large amount of precipitation, 
which certainly weakens the precipitation strengthening in the ageing 
treatment after the extrusion. In light of this, differential-thermal 
extrusion, where billet is preheated at high temperature while the 
mold is preheated at lower temperature, is developed to eliminate or 
decrease static precipitation during pre-heating. Rong et al. [9] reported 
that differential-thermal extrusion leads to TYS of 380 MPa which is 
338 MPa after isothermal extrusion for the Mg-15Gd-1Zn-0.4Zr alloy. 
Zhang et al. [10] reported that Mg-13.1Gd-1.6Ag-0.4 Zr alloy achieved a 
high TYS of 421 MPa via differential-thermal extrusion, cold rolling and 
ageing, mainly ascribed to the coupled strengthening from the 
bimodal-grained structure and enhanced precipitation. Adopting 
differential-thermal extrusion obtains high-strength Mg-RE series alloy 
extrusions, but they ordinarily design the larger extrusion ratio and 
involve small-sized samples. If differential-thermal extrusion and small 
extrusion ratio are combined, high-strength and large-sized Mg-RE alloy 
extrusions would be expected to be available.
In this work, a traditional Mg-11Gd-2Y-1Zn-0.4Zr (VWZ1121K) alloy 
was processed by differential-thermal extrusion with a small extrusion 
ratio, which aims to achieve both high mechanical properties and large 
size. The temperature of the VWZ1121K alloy billet is heated to 510 º C, 
which corresponds just to its solid solution temperature, effectively 
inhibiting static precipitation during pre-heating. Extrusion dies were 
preheated at 350 
◦

growth. This design not only inhibits static precipitation during pre- 
heating, but also reduces dynamic precipitation during extrusion. As 
expected, as-extruded VWZ1121K sample achieved the good strength- 
ductility balance, and its TYS reaches 460 MPa with an acceptable 
elongation of 6 %.

3. Results
3.1. Mechanical properties
Fig. 1 a shows the age-hardening curve of as-extruded VWZ1121K 
sample. The hardness initially increases rapidly with time, reaching a 
peak-hardness of ~115 HV at 32 h, and then the hardness gently de -
creases and trends a steady state due to over-ageing. The maximum 
hardness increment during aging reaches ~22 HV. For investigate the 
influence of ageing on tensile properties, as-extruded samples aged at 
24, 32 and 90 h would be test by tension, which is referred as under- 
aged, peak-aged and over-aged samples. Fig. 1 b shows tensile stress- 
strain curves of as-extruded and as-aged VWZ1121K samples, and 
their mechanical properties, such as ultimate tensile strength (UTS), TYS 
and EL, are listed in Table 1 . As-extruded sample exhibits a good 
strength-ductility balance, with TYS of 335 MPa and EL of 13.1 %. After 
further ageing, the strength is greatly increased, whereas the ductility is 
reduced, particularly in peak-ageing. The TYS of under-aged, peak-aged 
and over-aged samples is 402 MPa, 465 MPa, 445 MPa, and the corre -
sponding EL is 9.2 %, 6.0 % and 5.8 %, respectively. It is known that 
over-ageing leads to precipitates coarsening, which causes the reduced 
strength and ductility [11] . Also, this also indicates that the alloy is of 
good thermostability, as evidenced by the fact that the strength is 
slightly decreased after over-ageing.
3.2. Microstructure
Fig. 2 a and b shows OM images of as-extruded and peak-aged 
VWZ1121K samples, respectively. The fraction of DRXed grains in as- 
extruded sample is relatively low, only ~30 %, which is possibly 
related to the small extrusion ratio. Low ageing temperature appears to 
have a little effect on the DRX [12] , as the peak-aged specimen main -
tains a comparable DRX fraction (~35 %). Therefore, both samples 
exhibit the typical bimodal grain structure, consisting of fine DRXed 
grains and coarse non-DRXed grains. Here non-DRXed grain was elon -
gated along the ED and can extend up to several hundred microns in 
length. Fig. 2 c and d shows SEM images of as-extruded and as-aged 
VWZ1121K samples, respectively. Numerous lamellar structures can 
be observed within non-DRXed grains, and these stripes are generally 
aligned parallel to the ED, which should be 14H-LPSO precipitates [6, 
13] . Additionally, a lot of bright particles are predominantly distributed 
in DRXed regions. Such particle formation arises from dynamic precip -
itation during hot extrusion, driven by thermodynamic equilibrium re -
quirements under coupled thermal-mechanical conditions [3] . In 
general, these particles were segregated at grain boundaries, which 
hinders grain growth through the Smith-Zener pinning effect, thus 
playing a crucial role in forming fine DRXed grains [4] .

enhanced. To elucidate the underlying mechanisms, the microstructure 
of the peak-aged sample was investigated. No grain growth occurs after 
peak ageing, as shown in Fig. 8 a. This is attributed to solute segregation 
and dynamic precipitates at grain boundaries, which act as effective 
barriers to grain boundary migration [16] . It is consistent with Xu et al. ’ s 
[12] in-situ EBSD study demonstrating that Mg-Gd-Y-Zn-Zr alloys 
exhibit high thermal stability with negligible grain growth during 
ageing at 200

firming the presence of β ′ phase. Notably, the β ′ precipitates are spatially

4. Discussion
As-extruded sample exhibits a good strength-ductility balance, such 
as TYS of 335 MPa and EL of 13.1 %. Further ageing introduces 
numerous prismatic β’ precipitates in the matrix, which leads to a high 
TYS of 465 MPa, although the EL reduces to 6.0 %. Fig. 9 summarizes 
the yield strength in tensile mode a as a function of elongation for 
comparison between the present alloy and other Mg-Gd, Mg-Y, Mg-Al, 
Mg-Zn, and Mg-Sn series wrought alloys [6,16 – 21] . It is significant 
that the strength of our alloys much exceeds those RE-free wrought Mg 
alloys with TYS less than 350 MPa, although our alloys have the low 
ductility. Only a fraction of Mg-Gd series alloys have a TYS over 
~450 MPa, but they contain more than 14 wt%Gd and adopted the 
demanding extrusion process. In terms of strength-ductility balance, the 
under-aged sample is superior to most Mg-Gd-Y-Zn-Zr extrusions alloy, i. 
e. , TYS of 340 MPa and EL of 8.2 % in an extruded 
Mg-9Gd-3Y-1.2Zn-0.5Zr alloy [16] and TYS of 339 MPa and EL of 4 % in 
an extruded Mg-10Gd-3Y-1Zn-0.5Zr alloy [18] . Regarding TYS level, the 
peak-aged sample is also higher than that of most aged Mg-Gd-Y-Zn-Zr 
alloy processed by extrusion or rolling, i.e. , TYS of 372 MPa in 
Mg-11Gd-4.5Y-1Nd-1.5Zn-0.5Zr alloy [19] and TYS of 379 MPa in 
Mg-8.2Gd-3.8Y-1Zn-0.4Zr alloy [20] . Accordingly, we will focus on the 
mechanisms for high ductility in as-extruded sample and high strength 
in peak-aged sample in the following.
4.1. Ductility mechanism
A high density of LPSO and SFs lamellae is generated in non-DRXed 
grains. During plastic deformation, the relatively soft α -Mg matrix 
transfers a portion of the applied load to the stiffer LPSO phase. This 
stress partitioning induces microcrack nucleation at α -Mg/LPSO in -
terfaces, which subsequently triggers kink deformation in the LPSO 
phase. The resulting kink bands impede microcrack propagation, 
thereby enhancing the strain hardening response [22] . After as-extruded 
sample subjected to ~2 % tensile strain, it is observed that the pro -
nounced bending of LPSO lamellae and increased dislocation density 
within their interlamellar regions ( Fig. 10 a and b). The accumulation of 
geometrically necessary dislocations between LPSO lamellae generates 
significant stress concentrations, driving lamellar to bend as a stress 
relaxation mechanism [13] . This deformation mode may also involve 
rotational kinking, facilitated by the avalanche-like formation of basal 
dislocation pairs that enable strain compatibility. Notably, the devel -
opment of well-defined kink bands ( Fig. 10 c) contributes substantially to 
improved ductility. Kim et al. [23] considered this α -Mg/LPSO config -
uration as a dual-phase composite, where rigid LPSO lamellae are 
embedded within a soft α -Mg matrix. Large difference in elastic modulus 
between α -Mg and LPSO phases would lead to a high degree of stress 
concentrations at this phase interface [24] . Thus, the formed stress fields 
at phase interface promote the activation of < c + a > dislocations, 
which facilitate c -axis strain accommodation in the α -Mg matrix [22] . 
Fig. 11 a show TEM image under two-beam condition of g = [0002] 
( Fig. 11 b) to contrast < c + a > dislocations. Obviously, abundant < c 
+ a > dislocations were generated after a tensile strain of ~2 %. Most 
dislocation segments are curved, and thus they are mobile, which pro -

respectively [12] . The soft DRXed grains is more easily deformed than 
hard non-DRXed grains during straining, so the heterogeneous strain

5. Conclusions
In summary, we have successfully produced a large-sized VWZ1121K 
alloy bar using differential-thermal extrusion method with a small 
extrusion ratio. The main conclusions are as follows: 
(1) As-extruded VWZ1121K sample exhibits a superior strength- 
ductility synergy, with the UTS of 393 MPa, TYS of 335 MPa 
and EL of 13.1 %. After peak-ageing, the UTS and TYS of as- 
extruded sample significantly increase to 496 MPa and 
465 MPa, while the EL decreases to 6.0 %.
(2) As-extruded sample features a typical bimodal grain structure 
along with numerous SFs and LPSO phases developed in non- 
DRXed grains. The DRX grain boundaries are segregated by 
Mg
5
RE phases and solutes, which effectively impedes grain 
growth, contributing to the refined DRX grains of ~1 μ m.
(3) The high ductility of the as-extruded sample is primarily attrib -
uted to the presence of thin SFs and LPSO lamellae, which easily 
kink during deformation. This kinking behavior enhances strain 
hardening and mitigates microcrack propagation. Additionally, a 
high proportion of non-DRX grains alleviates strain localization 
during deformation, further benefiting the enhanced ductility.
(4) After aging, a high density of prismatic β’ phases precipitates 
within the grains, leading to significant precipitation hardening 
and contributing to the enhanced TYS. However, profuse β’ 
phases in the matrix deteriorates the ductility. The relative 
perpendicular distribution of β’ phase and SFs/LPSO phases 
creates a closed space, which impedes dislocation motion more 
effectively. These results provide valuable insights into the 
fabrication of large-sized, high-strength Mg alloy extrusions with 
high RE concentrations.
