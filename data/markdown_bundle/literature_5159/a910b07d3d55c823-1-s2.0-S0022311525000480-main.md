ABSTRACT
The high-temperature stability of FeCrAl alloy cladding is crucial in a loss-of-coolant accident. For this purpose, 
microstructural stability and mechanical properties of FeCrAl alloy cladding were investigated during isothermal 
exposure at 800 
◦
C ~1200 
◦
C for times to 100 h FeCrAl alloy claddings exhibited thermally stable γ -fiber 
( < 111 > //ND) oriented grains. The thermal stability temperatures of grains and Laves precipitates were 1000 
◦
C 
and 900 
◦

1. Introduction
FeCrAl alloys are increasingly recognized as viable candidates for 
accident-tolerant fuel cladding in light-water reactors (LWRs) [ 1 – 3 ]. 
Their excellent high-temperature oxidation resistance provides large 
safety margins for LWRs [ 4 – 6 ]. Specifically, FeCrAl cladding acts as an 
effective barrier to separate radioactive fuel and coolant for the safe 
operation of LWRs. However, in a loss-of-coolant accident, the rapid 
increase in fuel temperature causes claddings to increase their temper -
ature sharply even up to 1200 
◦
C [ 7 , 8 ]. As a result, exposure to 
high-temperature conditions would inevitably change the microstruc -
ture of claddings. Microstructural stability is a key factor affecting the 
mechanical properties of FeCrAl alloy claddings.
The microstructure of FeCrAl alloys depends on alloy composition 
and thermomechanical processing history [ 9 – 12 ]. The role of Cr and Al 
in FeCrAl alloys is to improve the high temperature oxidation resistance 
by forming a protective oxide layer [ 2 ]. In addition, Cr and Al also act as 
ferrite stabilizers, maintaining the alloy with a single-phase body- -
centered cubic (BCC) structure to the melting point. Mo and Nb have 
been employed to strengthen the matrix, refine microstructures and 
improve the mechanical properties of the alloy [ 13 ]. During the ther -
momechanical processing of FeCrAl alloy, intermediate precipitates 
were formed due to precipitation effects [ 14 ]. For example, previous 
studies have found that the Fe
2
M-type (M
–
– Mo, Nb) Laves precipitates 
[ 10 – 15 ] were precipitated during FeCrAl alloy cladding fabrication. 
Laves precipitates were considered to result in improved thermal sta -
bility [ 16 , 17 ], high temperature strength [ 18 , 19 ] and creep resistance 
[ 20 ]. However, at elevated temperatures ( ⩾ 800 
◦
C), the instability of 
Laves precipitates may affect the microstructure and mechanical prop -
erties of FeCrAl alloys. The microstructure and mechanical properties of 
FeCrAl alloys have been widely reported mainly on plates with simple 
geometries [ 10 – 12 ], but rarely on claddings. Therefore, it is necessary to 
systematically evaluate the high-temperature stability of FeCrAl alloy 
claddings.
In this work, the microstructure and mechanical properties of FeCrAl 
alloy claddings were investigated after thermal exposure at 800 
◦
C 
~1200 
◦
C. SEM-EBSD was used to characterize microstructure including 
grain size and Laves precipitates. The grain growth kinetics and Laves 
precipitation kinetics were quantified. Hardness and tensile tests were 
used to evaluate the mechanical properties. This work provides a 
reference for evaluating the potential failure risk of FeCrAl alloy clad -
dings at high temperatures.

precipitates. The characterization methods of these microstructural 
characteristics mainly involve EBSD (used to describe grain morphology 
and orientation information qualitatively/quantitatively) and SEM

FeCrAl alloy shows stable γ -fiber at high temperature.
Thermal stability of Laves precipitates is a competition between 
precipitation and dissolution, which is affected by kinetics and ther -
modynamics. The equilibrium phase composition shown in Fig. 2 a re -
veals that the formation of Laves precipitates in FeCrAl alloys is 
temperature dependent. Temperature affects Gibbs free energy and so -
lute solubility. When the temperature is below 1100 
◦
C, the solubility of 
alloys such as Mo and Nb decreases and the Gibbs free energy of the 
system tends to decrease, leading to the precipitation of intermetallic 
compounds Laves. Figs. 2 a and 9 a show that the content of Laves pre -
cipitates increases with decreasing temperature. However, the nucle -
ation, coarsening and even dissolution of Laves precipitates are diffusion 
controlled (i.e. time). Fig. 14 shows that with the increase of aging time, 
the Laves precipitate precipitates, coarsens, and even dissolves. Fig. 9 a 
shows that Laves precipitates exhibits excellent stability at 800 – 900

5. Conclusion
In this work, the microstructural stability of FeCrAl claddings during 
exposure to 800 – 1200 
◦
C for up to 100 h was investigated using EBSD 
and SEM techniques. Thermal stabilization mechanisms are discussed by 
linking microstructure and mechanical properties. From the obtained 
results, the following conclusions can be drawn: 
(1) The FeCrAl alloy cladding shows equiaxed grains of about 10 μ m, 
thermally stable γ -fiber and homogeneously distributed Laves 
precipitates within α -matrix. Exposure to temperatures above 
1000 
◦
C results in coarsening of grains and Laves precipitate.
(2) There is a coherent orientation relationship between the Lave 
precipitates within the matrix and α matrix. Laves precipitates 
preferentially grow at coherent and trifurcated grain boundaries. 
The grain growth kinetics and Lave precipitation kinetics of the 
FeCrAl alloy show a strong temperature dependence.
(3) Thermal exposure at 1100 
◦
C for 10 h results in grain coarsening 
and dissolution of the Laves precipitate, and the yield strength 
and elongation decreased from 497 MPa and 40 % to 441 MPa 
and 26 %, respectively.
