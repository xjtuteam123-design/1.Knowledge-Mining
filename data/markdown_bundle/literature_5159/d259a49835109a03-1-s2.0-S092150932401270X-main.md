ABSTRACT
Shot peening (SP) is a common surface-strengthening technique for improving the fatigue resistance of γ / γ ′ dual- 
phase Ni-based superalloys, yet the coordinated deformation mechanism of γ / γ ′ dual-phase architecture and its 
role in fatigue strengthening remain elusive. In this study, unique graded surface nanostructures were reported 
for the first time in shot peened single-crystal (SX) superalloys that possessed a six-fold fatigue life improvement 
compared to their non-treated counterparts. The graded nanostructures were composed of (i) outermost “ fully 
nanocrystallized zone ” (average grain size ~55 nm) with nearly complete dissolution of γ ′ precipitates, (ii) 
“ transition zone ” with partially fragmented γ ′ precipitates, and (iii) “ partially nanocrystallized γ channel zone ” 
with the emergence of nanograins merely in the γ channels. These observations advance the previous under -
standing that only fully nanocrystallized zone is present in the SP-treated γ / γ ′ SX superalloys. The graded surface 
nanostructures, together with work hardening and compressive residual stress, hindered strain localization and 
delayed crack initiation in the original soft γ matrix channels, thus prolonging the fatigue life. The collective 
outcomes of this work not only provide in-depth insights into the deformation mechanisms in shot-peened γ / γ ′ 
dual-phase superalloys but also can be used to further tune their surface nanostructures and hence mechanical 
properties.
1. Introduction
Ni-based single-crystal (SX) superalloys comprise a high volume 
fraction of ordered γ ′ -Ni
3
(Al, Ti) precipitates that are separated by nar -
row γ channels (disordered austenite matrix) [ 1 ]. Specifically, the 
almost identical lattice parameters and crystal orientation between 
these two phases render the interphase boundary coherent. Such a γ / γ ′ 
dual-phase architecture, coupled with the elimination of grain bound -
aries, endows the alloys with exceptional high-temperature strength. 
This makes SX superalloys ideal for the fabrication of aero-engine tur -
bine blades [ 2 ]. However, under the oscillatory loads and intensive 
centrifugal stresses during operation, fretting contact between the tur -
bine blade and disk leads to a high-stress concentration and renders the 
tenon area of the turbine blade more prone to fatigue damages [ 3 ], 
which may cause premature failure of the turbine blades and even 
aviation accidents [ 4 ].
To improve the fatigue resistance of Ni-based superalloys and other 
kinds of structural materials, various surface hardening approaches, 
such as shot peening (SP) [ 5 – 7 ], laser shock peening [ 8 , 9 ], deep cold 
rolling [ 10 ], and surface mechanical attrition treatment [ 11 ], have been 
employed by imposing high-level plastic strains upon the sample sur -
face. Among these approaches, SP has been used widely in airplane 
engines and serves as the gold standard for fatigue resistance

Generally, the surface work hardening is regulated by the plastically 
deformed near-surface microstructure. To date, a deep understanding of 
surface deformation microstructure (especially surface nano -
crystallization), dominantly governed by dislocation and/or twinning 
activities, has been extensively reached for pure nickel [ 20 , 21 ] and 
solid-solution-hardened superalloys ( e.g. , C-2000 [ 22 ] and C-22HS 
[ 23 ]). Apart from those single-phase alloys, mechanical surface treat -
ments have been also implemented in γ ″ / γ ′ co-precipitation-hardened 
IN718 superalloys to improve the fatigue resistance [ 16 , 24 , 25 ], while 
details of precipitates evolution upon severe plastic deformation have 
not provided yet.
Consequently, the role of precipitates in the formation of surface 
deformation microstructure induced by mechanical treatment is still 
unclear. Especially, in the case of γ / γ ′ dual-phase SX superalloys hard -
ened by high-volume-fraction γ ′ precipitates ( > 60 vol%), the surface 
microstructure responses appear to be more complicated. A few studies 
[ 13 , 26 ] have recently reported that the shot-peened SX superalloy 
contains a work-hardened substrate resulting from abundant 
dislocation-cutting activities and a superficial nanograin layer ( < 1 μ m 
thick) [ 13 , 27 ]. Though these studies provided initial insights into 
deformation microstructure in the work-hardened region of SP-treated 
Ni-based superalloys, the following questions remain open: (i) what 
are the phase-specific responses? (ii) how does the γ / γ ′ dual-phase ar -
chitecture transform into a nanograined structure upon SP treatment? 
and (iii) what is the influence of surface deformation microstructure on 
the fatigue resistance?
Given the framework of rigid γ ′ precipitates embedded in ductile γ 
channels, the two phases with different deformation resistances may 
strain plastically by different amounts of causing stresses [ 28 ]. This 
leads to phase-specific strain concentration, deformation response, and 
orientation change. Thus, a spatially-resolved measurement and direct 
correlation of the phase distribution, crystal defects, and orientation 
distribution, which are closely associated with the mechanical perfor -
mance of Ni-based superalloys are necessitated. Besides, particular 
attention should be paid to the structural integrity of γ / γ ′ interface, 
which correlates with several simultaneously operating strengthening 
mechanisms [ 29 ] and plays a significant role in the microstructural 
evolution subjected to severe plastic deformation [ 30 ].
Consequently, the present work aims to advance the understanding 
of SP-induced deformation microstructure and its contribution to fatigue 
property for a shot-peened γ / γ ′ dual-phase SX superalloy. A combination 
of transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and transmission Kikuchi 
diffraction (TKD) techniques were employed to probe the γ / γ ′ phase- 
specific deformation responses at the nanoscale and establish the γ / γ ′ 
phase-specific deformation maps (with emphasis on lattice distortion/ 
rotation and dislocation configurations).

2.2. Fatigue tests
As illustrated in Fig. 1 c, isothermal fatigue tests were performed for 
both samples with and without SP treatment via a cantilever-type 
rotating-bending fatigue testing machine (QBWP-10000) at 650 
◦

conditions in aero-engines [ 32 ]. To ensure reliable data acquisition, the 
experiments at each condition were repeated three times, and the 
average fatigue life was obtained for each material condition.
2.3. Hardness test
Microhardness tests were performed using a Vickers microhardness 
tester (HV-1000). For each measurement, a 0.2 kgf load was applied to 
the mechanically polished surface of the cylindrical test piece for a 
dwelling time of 15 s. Three lines of indents were made from the shot- 
peened surface towards the deep substrate, and the measurements at 
the same depth were averaged. Note that the first indent was placed 
~20 μ m away from the surface to avoid any potential edge effects. For 
simplicity, the depth direction is defined as y axis, and the shot-peened 
surface is y = 0 in this article. To further evaluate the validity of the 
microhardness result, nanoindentation tests (Hysitron TI 950 Tri -
boIndenter) with a peak load of 8 mN in a load-control mode were 
further performed on the cross-sectional plane to collect the nanohard -
ness profile along the SP direction. Additionally, based on the load- 
displacement curves of each intent, the residual stress as a function of 
depth can be derived.
2.4. Microstructural characterization
The microstructure of the base material, a heat-treated SX ingot, was 
first characterized. A slice of ~500 μ m thick was cut from the ingot with 
its surface normal along [001] direction and then ground into ~80 μ m 
thick. Then a 3 mm diameter disc was punched out and thinned in a 
solution of 8 vol% HClO
4

polishing machine. The specimen was investigated via TEM (FEI Talos- 
F200S) equipped with an energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) 
detector. The crystal orientation of the TEM specimen was analyzed by 
TKD (Bruker Optimus on-axis) with a scan step of 15 nm under the 
scanning electron microscopy (SEM) conditions of 30 kV and 5 nA.
After SP treatment, an overall cross-sectional view of the cylindrical 
test piece was performed to evaluate the plastic deformation distribu -
tion. After etching with 30 vol% orthophosphoric acid in distilled water, 
the γ channels were selectively etched and the γ ′ morphology at different 
depths was revealed using SEM (Zeiss EVO10). The crystal orientation 
information of the same sample was collected using electron back -
scattered diffraction (EBSD, Bruker e-Flash XS). Post-treatment of EBSD 
and TKD data was accomplished by the MTEX package [ 33 ] and a 
custom-designed graphical user interface [ 34 ].
To further scrutinize the near-surface microstructure in the SP- 
treated sample, a thin foil of ~8 μ m thick was lifted out from the SP 
surface by focused ion beam (FIB, FEI Strata 400 S). The deformed 
microstructure was characterized using the same TEM via high-angle 
annular dark-field (HAADF) and bright/dark field (BF/DF) scanning 
TEM (STEM) imaging.
After fatigue failure, the fracture surfaces of both SP- and non-treated 
samples were observed using SEM. To evaluate the fatigue damage 
characteristics, TEM thin foils were lifted out from the tapered surface, 
~30 μ m away from the edge adjacent to the fatigue crack initiation sites 
of both samples, as schemed in Fig. 1 d.

3. Results
3.1. Initial microstructure of non-treated SX base material
The microstructure of the heat-treated SX base material was first 
analyzed. As shown in Fig. 2 a, a typical dual-phase architecture is 
composed of well-shaped cuboidal γ ′ precipitates with an average size of 
~580 nm and γ channels with a mean channel width of ~80 nm. The

➢ Fully nanocrystallized zone
Fig. 7 a – b present microstructural and chemical comparisons be -

4.1.3. Fragmentation and dissolution of γ ′ precipitates
The deformation-induced fragmentation and dissolution (or 
ordering-disordering transition) of γ ′ precipitates have been reported in 
superalloys through mechanical machining [ 52 ] and multiple friction 
stir processing [ 53 ], where dynamic recrystallization occurs due to the 
peak temperature close to or even above γ ′ solvus (~1300 
◦
C). However, 
the temperature rise during SP ( < 200 
◦
C) [ 54 ] is insufficient to fulfill the 
conditions of dynamic recrystallization, hence the fragmentation 
together with structural disordering of γ ′ precipitate should proceed 
differently. In light of the correlation between shear strain profile and 
graded nanostructures, as mentioned, it is inferred that after having 
formed the PN γ CZ, γ ′ precipitates were firstly fragmented into misor -
iented blocks ( i.e. , TZ) by sequentially forming dislocation tangles, 
sub-boundaries, and HAGBs, and finally refined to disordered nano -
crystallites with high misorientations ( i.e. , FNZ). This is similar to the 
Ni
3
Al intermetallic alloys subjected to high-pressure torsion [ 44 ]. It has 
been proposed that the strain-induced disordering of Ni
3
Al can occur by 
the formation of an antiphase boundary (APB) that connects a pair of 
superlattice dislocations a /2 < 110 > [ 55 ]. In this study, though indi -
vidual dislocations cannot be resolved due to the extremely high dislo -
cation density, the above mechanism is believed to be still operative. 
This is because APB shearing commonly governs the deformation 
mechanism below 800 
◦
C for Ni-based superalloys [ 55 ]. Besides, other 
anomalous deformation-induced dissolution mechanisms [ 56 ], such as 
chemical disordering (as a result of migration of solute atoms dragged by 
moving dislocations at room and cryogenic temperatures) might also 
occur during the SP process.
The fragmentation and dissolution of most γ ′ precipitates brought 
forth a nearly homogeneous chemical mixing and a reduced stacking 
fault energy (SFE) [ 57 ], which facilitated the formation of deformation 
twins and SFs ( Fig. 7 e – f). Since grain subdivision in low SFE alloys often 
involves deformation twinning [ 58 ], both twinning and dislocation ac -
tivities were considered to be contributors to grain refinement in the 
FNZ.

Since shot impacts repeatedly induced plastic flow in the sample

dislocation dissociation in γ ′ precipitates [ 2 ] occurred (Stage II). Further 
increases in strain promoted the formation of highly misoriented grains

4.2. Origins of enhanced fatigue life induced by shot peening
The origins of enhancement in fatigue life of SP-treated superalloys 
can be derived from the factors from the macroscopic ( i.e. , compressive 
residual stress and work hardening) and the microscopic scales ( i.e. , 
graded surface nanostructures).
4.2.1. Macroscopic factors
Compressive residual stress can normally mitigate the driving force 
for fatigue crack initiation from the surface [ 16 ]. Under a cyclic load 
condition, however, significant residual stress relaxation occurs because 
the residual stress undergoes frequent interactions with applied loads 
and dynamic self-equilibration [ 60 ]. More importantly, at elevated 
temperatures, dislocation glide, and climbing activities get more active, 
resulting in significant stress relaxation based on a creep-like mecha -
nism [ 61 ]. Consequently, a quantitative assessment of residual stress 
evolution in SP-treated samples before and after fatigue failure was 
performed using the nanoindentation method. As shown in Fig. 14 , the 
depth-dependent residual stress profiles were derived from the nano -
indentation load-displacement curves based on Wang ’ s model [ 37 ]. It is 
assumed that the profile of residual stress σ

relaxation was mainly caused by the compensation of cyclic tensile 
stresses and dislocation recovery/recrystallization ( e.g. , annealing twins 
shown in Fig. 10 ). Despite this, the SP-induced pre-fatigue compressive 
residual stress compensated the fatigue tensile stress level in the case of 
crack initiation/propagation and acted as closure stress in the case of 
crack growth [ 62 ].
Moreover, SP treatment produced a work-hardened surface layer 
with a depth of about 250 μ m ( Fig. 3 a – b). The similar trend between the 
hardness profile and KAM profile ( Fig. 5 d) is attributed to an increased 
dislocation density from the interior to the topmost surface. This pre- 
fatigue deformation hardened the material locally and hindered pre -
mature fatigue failure from the surface region, as seen from the transi -
tion of fatigue crack initiation sites from the surface to a subsurface 
region after SP treatment ( Fig. 4 d).
4.2.2. Microscopic factors
Previous studies have emphasized the positive effect of surface 
nanocrystallization ( i.e. , FNZ) on fatigue resistance [ 6 , 13 ]. For the 
SP-treated SX superalloys, apart from the FNZ (with a thickness < 1 μ m), 
the PN γ CZ and TZ possessing a broader depth range also contributed to 
the surface work hardening and fatigue life improvement. This is 
because strain partitioning and substantial dislocation accumulation 
inside the γ channels ( Fig. 12 d) greatly strengthened the γ phase and 
reduced the strength disparity between the two phases, which benefited 
the fatigue strength improvement for superalloys [ 63 ]. Taking the 
different surface microcrack patterns in post-fatigued samples with -
out/with SP treatment ( Fig. 4 e – f) into account, the improved strength of 
the γ phase was believed as a major contributor to the suppression of 
surface fatigue crack initiation in the SP-treated sample. To verify this, 
the near-surface microcrack pattern in non-SP samples after fatigue 
failure was further investigated using TEM, as shown in Fig. 15 a. These 
microcracks orient close to < 010 > directions. A close-up view of the 
microfracture segment ( Fig. 15 b) indicates that the microcracks are 
mainly confined inside the γ channels or along the γ / γ ′ interface. These 
cracks did not result from FIB damages but were initiated under 
thermo-mechanical conditions during the fatigue testing at 650 
◦
C. The 
amorphous region near the microcracks in Fig. 15 b should be a result of 
oxidation. Detailed analysis can be found in Fig. S1 in the Supplemen -
tary Material. This kind of fatigue microcrack pattern has also reported 
the formation of these microcrack patterns [ 64 , 65 ], originating from the 
localized plastic strain induced by cyclic loading that exceeds the 
strength of soft γ channels. For the SP-treated sample, however, the 
improved strength of γ channels together with graded surface nano -
structures and work-hardened layer suppressed the premature crack 
initiation in the γ channels and shifted the crack initiation site from 
surface to subsurface region, thus effectively suppressing fatigue crack 
nucleation and prolonging the alloy ’ s fatigue life.
Additionally, the three-layer nanostructures were still preserved 
after fatigue testing ( Fig. 10 ). The nanograins with intrinsic thermal 
instability [ 66 ] in the FNZ grew slightly compared to the SP-treated 
sample. In the TZ, the presence of twinned γ ′ precipitates with rela -
tively low-density dislocations and uniform orientations ( Fig. 10 d – e) 
could be attributed to dynamic recrystallization in the condition of 
isothermal fatigue. The stored energy, serving as the driving force for 
recrystallization, was contributed by the high density of dislocations and 
residual stress by SP treatment. While twin boundaries have been 
recognized as preferential fatigue crack initiation sites due to strain 
localization in polycrystalline superalloys [ 67 , 68 ], no such evidence was 
observed in the present SX superalloys. Instead, large plastic deforma -
tion was accommodated by unfragmented γ ′ precipitates at the top of the 
PN γ CZ ( Fig. 10 e). On the other hand, the formation of twins accom -
modated the strains applied by both SP treatment and cyclic loading, 
facilitating the release of surface stress/strain concentration. The ob -
servations in this study provide a road map for possible future work in 
the thermal stability evaluation of SP-induced graded surface nano -

5. Conclusions
This work provides an in-depth understanding of the formation 
mechanism of graded surface nanostructures in an SP-treated γ / γ ′ dual- 
phase SX superalloy and reveals the origins of their influence on the 
alloy ’ s fatigue properties. The key findings are summarized as follows:
(1) The fatigue life of SP-treated SX superalloys was improved by ~6 
times compared to their non-treated counterparts. The improve -
ment of fatigue resistance was attributed to the unique graded 
surface nanostructures, compressive residual stress, and the work 
hardening layer. Particularly, the synergetic combination of these 
factors hindered strain localization and delayed surface crack 
initiation in the γ channels during the fatigue process, in contrast 
to the extensive fatigue microcracks nucleated in the surface of 
the non-treated base materials.
(2) Due to the shear strain gradient generated during the SP process, 
the surface deformation microstructure of γ / γ ′ dual-phase SX 
alloy exhibited three distinct layers, including FNZ (from the 
topmost surface to a maximum depth of ~1 μ m), TZ (~0.6 μ m 
thick), and PN γ CZ (a depth of > 8 μ m). The presence of the latter 
two zones ( i.e. , TZ and PN γ CZ) has been rarely reported before 
and is believed to be unique in the SP-treated dual-phase γ / γ ′ SX 
superalloys.
(3) In the FNZ, nearly disordered γ nanograins originated from 
dislocation slip and twinning activities. In the TZ, γ ′ precipitates 
were partially fragmented into multiple nanograins due to 
intensive dislocation-cutting activities (including superlattice 
dislocation tangling, SSFs, and L-C locks). In the PN γ CZ, phase- 
specific strain partitioning and γ lattice distortion/rotation led 
to an increase in interphase misorientations and the formation of 
nanograins in the γ channels. The strain-induced transition from 
the initial γ / γ ′ dual-phase SX architecture into nearly disordered 
nanograins was finally rationalized.
These findings not only advance the understanding of deformation 
mechanisms in the shot-peened γ ′ -hardened SX superalloys but also help 
to develop severe plastic deformation techniques to further improve the 
fatigue life of engineering materials/components.
