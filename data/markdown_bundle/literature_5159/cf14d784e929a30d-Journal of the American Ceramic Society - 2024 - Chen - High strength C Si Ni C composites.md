Abstract
Silicon‒nickelalloy(Si‒Ni)wasfirstusedforfabricatingcontinuouscarbonfiber
reinforcedceramicmatrixcompositesbyreactionmeltinfiltrationprocess.Based
onSi‒Niphasediagram,66.7at.%Si‒Niwaschosen,whichhasalowerliquidus
temperature of 1115◦C. The C/Si‒Ni‒C composites were infiltrated at tempera-
tureof1270 ◦C‒1390◦C,andthepreparedcompositeconsistsofC,SiC,NiSi,and
NiSi2 phases. The density of the composite is higher than 2.29 g/cm3 and their
openporosityisbelow3%,indicatingthecompositewasinfiltratedcompletelyby
the Si‒Ni alloy. The C/Si‒Ni‒C composite exhibits significantly higher flexural
strengthandfracturetoughnesscomparedtotheC/C‒SiCcompositeinfiltrated
withpuresiliconat1500 ◦C.Specifically,theflexuralstrengthandfracturetough-
nessoftheC/Si‒Ni‒Ccompositeinfiltratedat1390 ◦Cincreaseby140%and300%,
respectively.Theimprovementofmechanicalpropertiescontributedtothelow
reactivity between silicon‒nickel with carbon fibers, and the lower infiltration
temperature, which can reduce degradation of carbon fiber as reinforcement.
Moreover, the NiSi2 and NiSi alloy phases in the C/Si‒Ni‒C composite replace
thebrittlesiliconphaseintheC/C‒SiCcomposite,whichcanobviouslyenhance
thefracturetoughness.

1 INTRODUCTION
Carbon/carbon‒silicon carbide (C/C‒SiC) composites
exhibit exceptional properties, making them ideal for
applications requiring lightweight and high-strength
materials at high temperature.1–4 The reaction melt
infiltration (RMI) is a low cost, near net-size method for
preparing C/C‒SiC composite.5,6 It has a short sintering
time at a high temperature of 1500◦C‒1600◦Ca n di s
suitable for mass production. Compared with alternative
methods such as chemical vapor deposition and polymer
impregnationpyrolysis,7–9 thematerialsproducedbyRMI
hasalowerstrength,mainlyduetoresidualsiliconinthe
composite,whichwillsignificantlyreducethemechanical
propertiesofthecomposites. 10–12 Inaddition,thetemper-
atureofthesiliconreactioninfiltration(RMI-Si)processis
usuallyhigherthanthemeltingpointofsilicon(1414 ◦C)to
promotethepenetrationofsiliconintotheporouscarbon
matrix.Thehighlyreactiveliquidsiliconorpartofsilicon
vapor may destroy the surface structure and interface
of the carbon fiber, leading to the siliconizing of carbon
fibers,seriouslydamagethemechanicalpropertiesofcar-
bonfiber.3,13–15 Thatisanotherreasonforthedegradation
oftheC/C‒SiCcomposite.

In order to reduce the content of free silicon in com-

to lower the infiltration temperature to avoid the ero-

during the RMI process. Liu et al.17 introduced Al into
the silicon, which can reduce the infiltration tempera-
ture to below 1400◦C. However, during the RMI process,
insufficient reactivity occurred, resulting in insufficient
infiltrationofthecomposite. 18RMIofalloyssuchasSi‒Ti 19
and Si‒Y20 have also been studied recently. These results
demonstratedthatthelow-temperatureRMIprocesscould
improve the strength of the composites and reduce the
processingcost.
To overcome the limitations of the RMI-Si process and
enhancethemechanicalpropertiesofC/C-SiCcomposites,
a low-melting point Si‒Ni alloys for reactive infiltration
was developed firstly. Based on the Ni‒Si alloy phase

2 EXPERIMENTALPROCEDURES
2.1 Materialpreparationand
fabrication
The Si powder (5µm, 99.99%, Naiou Nano Technology
Co.) and Ni powder (5µm, 99.99%, Nanhai TOP Metal
Building Material Co.) were weighed at a mass ratio of
49:51 (66.7 at.% Si‒Ni alloy), and mixed with mechanical
stirring for 3 h to ensure the uniformity of the resulting
alloy. Then, mixed powder was then pressed into a mold
under a pressure of 45‒60 MPa to form a sample block
with a diameter of 35 mm and a thickness of 10‒15 mm.
The pressed block was placed in a graphite crucible and
smeltedat1400 ◦Cfor40mininavacuummeltingfurnace
(10 Pa) to obtain Si‒Ni alloy. In order to avoid the con-
sumption of Si powder reacting with graphite crucible, a
layer of graphite paper reacting with silicon is laid in the
crucible.
The2.5DC/CcompositesusedforRMIwasobtainedby
ChaomaTechnologyCo.,withadensityof1.33g/cm 3,an
openporosityof23.10%.Thestructureofthecarbonfiber
preformisshowninFigure 1.Aneedle-punchedquasi-3D
carbonfiberfelt(carbonpreform)wasmadebyrepeatedly
overlapping the layers of 0◦ weftless ply, short-cut fiber
web and 90◦ weftless ply in the X–Y plane with needle
punchinginthe Zdirectionstepbysteptoacertainthick-
ness.ThecarbonfibersusedinthisstudywereT300from
TorayIndustrieswithatensilestrengthof3880MPaanda
tensile modulus of 233 GPa. The fiber volume fraction in
thepreformisabout30%.
The C/Si‒Ni‒C composite was prepared by RMI of the
2.5DC/CcompositewiththeSi‒Nialloy.TheRMIprocess
was carried out in a vacuum furnace (∼10 Pa). The tem-
perature was increased at a rate of 8◦C/min, the reaction
temperature was at 1270◦C, 1310◦C, 1350◦C, and 1390◦C,
respectively. The reaction time was 40 min, followed
by cooling rate of 12◦C/min. The resulting composites
obtained at these different temperatures were labeled as
1270-Si-Ni, 1310-Si-Ni, 1350-Si-Ni, and 1390-Si-Ni, respec-
tively. As a comparative study, C/C‒SiC composite was
prepared by RMI of the same 2.5D C/C composite with
pure silicon at temperature 1500◦C, which named as
1500-Si.

4 CONCLUSIONS
InthisstudySi‒NialloywasusedforRMIprocessatlower
temperature to achieve low porosity and high strength
of the C/Si‒Ni‒C composites. The microstructural anal-
ysis confirmed the successful infiltration of Si‒Ni alloy,
resulting in the formation of SiC phase, NiSi phase, and
NiSi2 phase.Itcaneffectivelyimprovetheflexuralstrength
(409.04MPa)andfracturetoughness(9.63MPam 1/2).The
improvementinmechanicalpropertiesisattributedtothe
very low reactivity between Si‒Ni alloy and carbon fiber,
which retains most of the carbon fiber reinforcement. It
also avoids the formation of SiC at the interface between
the fiber and the matrix, achieving weak interface bond-
ingandfullyrealizingthetougheningeffectssuchasfiber
pulloutanddebonding.Theuniformdistributionofalloy
phases NiSi2 and NiSi promotes effective load transfer,
strengthens the matrix, and expands the crack deflec-
tion path, thereby consuming a large amount of fracture
energy. The results of this study are of great significance
to the design and manufacturing of high-performance
composites, providing the possibility to further optimize
penetrationparameters.
