abstract
In the present investigation, the formation of g-Nb2C in the Nb eZreC alloy and its interrelation with
other Nb2C carbide phases ( a, b) has been studied. The use of synchrotron X-ray diffraction experiments,
crystallographic analysis, high resolution imaging, as well as the determination of the relevant orien-
tation relationship, between the second phase and matrix, has led to a proposed mechanism of g-Nb2C. It
has been shown that the transformation of Nb to g-Nb2C takes place by the occupation of octahedral sites
in the bcc Nb lattice by carbon atoms. The mechanism proposes minimal movement of Nb atoms during
formation of
g-Nb2C. Further, it has been shown that the sequence of phase transformations ( g-Nb2C /
b-Nb2C / a-Nb2C) involves ordering of vacancies whereas the basic lattice in all these three structures
remains essentially same. First principle calculations aimed at calculating the formation energies of these
carbides have revealed that the difference in these energies to be rather low. This rationalized the
observation of a metastable disordered structural phase (
g-Nb2C) in the present study. It has been shown
that the arrangement of the carbon atoms in the g-Nb2C phase leads to the accumulation of strain along
the [0001] direction which is accommodated by the creation of lattice defects. This strain destabilizes the
g-Nb2C phase and leading to its dissolution at temperatures as low as 800 /C14 C.

1. Introduction
Nb based alloys are being actively pursued in high temperature
structural applications due to their high strength to weight ratio
combined with their compatibility with liquid metal environment
[1,2]. In particular, the alloy Nb e1%Zre0.1%C has been successfully
used in high temperature structural nuclear applications. For
example, this alloy has been deployed as a structural material for a
liquid metal (Pbe Bi alloy and Li) loop of a reactor operating at
10 0 0
/C14 C [1,3,4]. The Nb e1%Zre0.1%C alloy essentially derives its
high temperature strength from the formation of various Nb based
carbides in the matrix [5e8]. Unfortunately the same carbides that
strengthen the alloy also make the alloy dif ﬁcult when thermo-
mechanically processed for component fabrication. Earlier at-
tempts have employed temperatures as high as 1600
/C14 C and above
for the processing of this alloy to ensure a suf ﬁciently soft matrix to
enable deformation [9e11 ]. However, such high temperature pro-
cessing demands specialized techniques (such as coatings, appli-
cation of vacuum, etc.) because of the susceptibility of this alloy to
oxidation resistance [12]. In contrast, if it were possible to process
the alloy at lower temperatures, the problems related to oxidation
could be mitigated [13]. The key to successful low temperature
deformation lies in a compromise between two competing condi-
tions: (a) a suf ﬁciently high temperature to enable dissolution of
the metastable Nb
2C carbides and (b) a suf ﬁciently low tempera-
ture to prevent formation of stable Nb based carbides. Such a
possibility has been demonstrated in previous work of some of the
current authors, where the alloy was successfully deformed at
temperature as low as 800
/C14 C [13]. This temperature was selected
on the basis of ﬂow property data which shows a minimum in yield
strength of the alloy at this temperature. This minimum in yield
strength was attributed to the absence of Nb based carbides at this

(a) Although

the Nb eC phase diagram which shows Nb 2C as a stable
phase. One pre-requisite for such a transformation would

nism of which is not clear from the available literature.
(c) The mechanism for the observed signi ﬁcant dissolution of
the carbide phase at the lower temperature of 800 /C14 C needs
to be addressed [13].
(d) Further, such an understanding of the evolution behavior of
various carbides will be very useful to the optimization of the
microstructure through suitable thermo-mechanical
treatment.
In view of these comments, in the present work, detailed
microstructural investigations have been carried out to bring out
the mechanistic aspects of formation of various carbide phases. To
the authors' knowledge, this is the ﬁrst time that such a detailed
study has been carried out. This study is expected to bring out the
formation mechanism and inter-relationship among various allo-
tropes of Nb

2. Experimental procedures
The Nbe1%Zre0.1%C (wt%) alloy was prepared by electron beam
melting technique using pure (99.9%) Nb, Zr and C in the stoi-
chiometric amount. The alloy was remelted several times to ach-
ieve better chemical homogeneity. The composition of the alloy
was veriﬁed by the combustion extraction method for the carbon
content and the inductively coupled plasma method for zirconium
and niobium contents (see Table 2 ). The phase identi ﬁcation was
carried out by analyzing the X-ray diffraction patterns obtained
using a synchrotron X-ray source. Microstructural investigations
were carried out by optical and transmission electron microscopy
(TEM) (JEOL 2000 FX, FEI TITAN 80 e300) techniques. For optical
metallography, the samples were mirror polished and etched with
a mixed acid solution (HNO
3:HF:H2SO4, 1:1:3). TEM samples were
prepared by the ion milling technique. Plasma cleaning of TEM
samples prior to examination using TEM was done to avoid any
contamination. For determining the structure of the carbide phases,
selected area electron diffraction (SAED) patterns were recorded in
TEM with the electron beam parallel to various crystallographic
zones. To understand the morphology of phases, bright ﬁeld (BF)
images were obtained in TEM. X-ray energy dispersive spectros-
copy (XEDS) in the STEM mode with a probe size of z 1e2 nm (spot
size 7) has been used to determine the chemical composition of
different phases in the Nb samples. For crystallographic analysis,
crystal structures were simulated using JEMS (Java electron mi-
croscopy software) and Vesta software's.

3.4. Estimation of relative stabilities using ﬁrst principle
calculations
To determine the stability of Nb 2C carbides in the Nb alloy, ﬁrst-
principles calculations were carried out on disordered carbon-
sublattice ( g-Nb2C (P6 3/mmc)) and ordered carbon-sublattice
structures ( b-Nb2C ðp31mÞ and ( a-Nb2C( Pbcn)) to estimate their
ground state cohesive properties. To model g-Nb2C structure with
disorder in the carbon sublattice, a 2 /C2 2 /C2 2 supercell of Nb2C2 was
employed and 50% of carbon atoms were removed in a random
fashion to mimmic the disordered arrangement of carbon atoms.
The calibration of various approximations in the DFT calculations
were done by calculating ground state cohesive properties (lattice
parameters and cohesive energies) of elemental Nb (bcc) and C
(graphite). The cohesive energies are calculated by subtracting the
corresponding atomic contribution from the total energies. The
optimized lattice parameter and cohesive energy for Nb(bcc) were
calculated to be 3.3105 Å and /C0 7.465 eV/atom, respectively, which
are in good agreement with experimental values of 3.3004 Å
and /C0 7.483 eV/atom [33]. Similarly, for C (graphite), the calculated
values of lattice parameters (a ¼ 2.4732 Å, c ¼ 6.7360 Å) and
cohesive energy ( /C0 7.397 eV/atom) are in good agreement with the
experimental values (a ¼ 2.4612 Å, c ¼ 6.7090 Å and /C0 7.379 eV/
atom) [33]. It must be mentioned here that the cohesive energies
are with respect to the true ground state electronic con ﬁgurations
of Nb and C atoms, respectively.

4. Discussion
In the present study, two highlights can be summarized as
follows:
(i) Diffraction patterns and the HRTEM (high resolution TEM)
images were consistent with that of a hexagonal Nb
2C
structure, and the OR between the carbide and the matrix
determined as ½111/C138 Nb k½ 1123/C138 Nb2 C , ½001/C138 Nb k½ 1120/C138 Nb2 C
and ð011ÞNb kð 0111ÞNb2 C . This OR appears to be speci ﬁc, as
all the composite SAD patterns of carbide needles with the
matrix phase could be successfully indexed.
(ii) Chemical composition analysis of several carbide needles
showed that Zr concentration in the carbide phase was
negligible. This observation is not unexpected as at high
temperature, Zr has high solid solubility in the Nb matrix but
during cooling it is not partitioned to the carbides due to
insufﬁcient time. The lattice parameter of the matrix phase
determined from XRD patterns was 0.3418 nm which is
nearly ~3.5% larger than that of pure Nb (a ¼ 0.3303 nm).
Such variations in lattice parameter also provide evidence of
the presence of Zr and C in the matrix phase. In short, the
Nb
2C precipitates in the present study are considered as a
binary phase of Nb and C for all practical purposes.

Nb2C. Crystallographic information of all the Nb 2C phases are given

5. Conclusions
In the present study, the formation mechanism of Nb 2C carbide
phase in the Nb e1%Zre0.1%C alloy was studied, and the following
conclusions can be drawn.
1. Detailed crystallographic analysis of the structures of Nb and g-
Nb2C has revealed that the transformation of the Nb lattice to
the Nb 2C lattice has taken place by the occupation of carbon
atoms at octahedral sites in bcc Nb lattice. This transformation
involves small movement of Nb atoms.
2. The presence of a speci ﬁc orientation relationship between the
Nb and Nb
2C carbide phase
(½111/C138 Nb k½ 1123/C138 Nb2 C ; ð011ÞNb kð 0111ÞNb2 C Þ has been estab-
lished. HREM analysis showed the presence of coherent inter-
face between Nb and Nb 2C carbide phases.
3. Lattice strain calculations have shown that the strain due to the
occupancy of carbon atoms at octahedral sites would produce
strain mainly along the [0001] direction of the Nb
2C lattice. This
strain can also assist in dissolution of the g-Nb2C phase at a
relatively lower temperature.
4. The structural relationship among disordered and ordered Nb 2C
carbides suggest that disordered g-Nb2C has a close structural
relationship with ordered b and a-Nb2C. Small differences in
energy of formation among these carbides allow the formation
of g-Nb2C beyond its phase ﬁeld.
