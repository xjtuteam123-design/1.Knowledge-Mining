ABSTRACT
High-performance titanium alloy fasteners play an important role in the aerospace industry as important general
basic parts. This work developed a high-elastic-modulus Ti-6.2Al-4.26Mo-1.33Mn alloy with good balance of
strength and plasticity through regulating its primary equiaxed α ( α
p
) phase with solution aging treatment. The
influence of α
p
on the tensile properties and low-cycle fatigue (LCF) behavior of the alloy was investigated
systematically, and the crack propagation mechanism of fatigue fracture of titanium alloy was revealed. The
results show that with increasing solution temperature, the content of α
p
phase decreases from 43.6 % to 15.1 %
in the range of 825
◦
C – 925
◦
C, and the LCF life is increased by almost 2 times. However, the size of α
p
phase is
almost unchanged with a diameter of about 2.3 μ m. With the increase in solution temperature, the tensile
strength of the alloy is improved, while the plasticity decreases gradually. At the solution temperature of 875
◦
C,
the ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of the alloy is 1350 MPa, which is 18.6 % higher than the as-forged alloy, with
an elongation (El) of > 7 %. Moreover, the fatigue performance is also significantly improved, which is associated
with the reduced α
p
phase that was characterized to act as crack initiations and crack propagation path.
1. Introduction
Titanium alloys have been widely applied to aerospace, marine en-
gineering and biomedical fields due to their low density, high specific
strength and excellent corrosion resistance and biocompatibility [ 1 – 3 ].
Especially in the aerospace field, titanium alloy has good benefits to
reducing the engine weight, improving the thrust-to-weight ratio and
improving the performance and life of aircraft parts. As an important
connector, fasteners play an important role in the aviation industry.
With the development of lightweight equipment, more and more aero-
space fasteners favor titanium alloy materials, which significantly re-
duces aircraft weight. For example, the use of titanium fasteners on the
US C-5A military transport aircraft results in a mass reduction of about
4500 kg; the replacement of steel with titanium in the fasteners of the
Boeing 747 for civil aircraft leads to a mass reduction of 1814 kg; and the
use of 940 kg of BT16 titanium fasteners on the Russian Tu-204 aircraft
causes a mass reduction of 688 kg [ 4 ]. However, the elastic modulus of
titanium alloy is usually 105 GPa, which is about half of that of steel.
Such a lower elastic modulus makes titanium alloy weak in resisting
external deformation when it is used as a fastener to connect different
parts, and is prone to deformation and loosening, resulting in aircraft
crashes. Not only for fasteners, but also for titanium structural compo-
nents designed for stiffness, the lower elastic modulus value makes it
necessary to increase the wall thickness or reinforce the structure to
meet the requirements of use. This results in thick and bulky parts,
which undermines the weight-saving advantages of titanium alloys over
other materials [ 5 , 6 ]. Therefore, improving the elastic modulus of tita-
nium alloys has become the focus of research. For instance, Ye et al. [ 7 ]
studied the phase stability and modulus of Ti

ciples. The results show that the stability of each phase is roughly related
to the unit volume and bulk modulus of the phase. As strong β stable

In engineering applications, titanium alloy fasteners are often
required to be used in harsh environments and subjected to cyclic
external loads, which may cause fatigue cracks and fractures in a short
period of time and thus cause damage to the components [ 11 ]. There-
fore, the study of low-cycle fatigue (LCF) behavior of titanium alloys is
also a hot issue in current research [ 12 – 15 ]. For example, Saleh and
Margolin [ 16 , 17 ] have compared the LCF life of Widmansta ¨atten

The manufacturing process of fasteners includes forging, heat treat-
ment, machining, wire rolling and other steps. As a connector con-
necting different parts, it is required to have good tensile strength and
certain plasticity for forging and subsequent processing and forming.
Solution aging treatment is often used for microstructure control in ti-
tanium alloy production. By using the phase transformation of titanium
alloy during heating and cooling, the desired microstructure can be
obtained, and the best strength-plasticity matching of the alloy can be
realized [ 20 , 21 ]. According to the report of Shekhar et al. [ 22 ], ( α + β )
solution aging state have higher strength and plasticity than β phase
region solution aging state. Zhang et al. [ 23 ] have pointed out that so-
lution treatment can affect the phase fraction, especially the β phase
fraction, and improve the strength of dual-phase titanium alloy by
30~50 %.
In our previous work, the most widely used Ti – 6Al – 4V(TC4) tita-
nium alloy was used as a reference, and Mo and Mn elements with
stronger β phase stability were used to replace V elements [ 7 ]. By
introducing stronger solution strengthening effect, the strength and
elastic modulus of titanium alloy were improved, and a TiAlMoMn ti-
tanium alloy was developed. The elastic modulus of this alloy reaches
126 GPa, which is 15 % higher than that of TC4 titanium alloy (110
GPa). In this paper, the previously developed TiAlMoMn alloy was used
as the raw material. The α
p
content of the alloy was regulated by solution
aging treatment, and the equiaxed microstructure with different α
p
volume fraction was obtained. The effects of solution temperature on the
volume fraction of α
p
, tensile properties and LCF behavior of titanium
alloy were systematically studied, and the relationship between α
p
and
LCF strength was investigated. The mechanism of LCF crack initiation
and propagation was revealed. The results exhibited that the
solution-aging treated titanium alloy possessed LCF performance
together with high strength and toughness.

4.2. Effect of solution temperature on the tensile properties
In Fig. 7 , it can be found that the change in the elastic modulus of the
alloy after solution aging treatment is small. It has been shown that the
elastic modulus of titanium alloy is closely related to the ratio of phases
in the microstructure, and the elastic modulus of common phases in ti-
tanium alloys has the following relationship: E( ω ) > E( α ) > E( α ′ ) > E
( α ") > E( β ) [ 37 – 39 ]. When the solution treatment of titanium alloy is
performed, due to the rapid cooling rate, martensitic shear occurs at
high temperature β phase, generating α ′ and α " phase, and even ω phase,
and the elastic modulus will be greatly changed. However, because the
α ′ , α " and ω phases in the β
t
are unstable phases, after 550
◦

phases will be re-transformed into α and β phases, so the elastic modulus
will return to the value before heat treatment. In addition, α
s

precipitate in the β
t
after aging, resulting in precipitation strengthening,
so the hardness of the sample will increase with the increase of solution
temperature.

4.3. LCF fracture mechanism analysis
In two-phase titanium alloy, the fatigue properties are strongly
influenced by the morphology and arrangement of α and β phases,
including β grain size, the thickness of α lamellar and the size of cluster.
The location of crack nucleation depends on both the microstructure
factor and the response to cyclic strain. The hcp α phase is more brittle
than the bcc β phase, so the slip begins at the β phase first, and the
dislocation accumulates at the α / β phase boundary or the primary α
grain boundary. In fact, the cumulative amount of strain required for
crack formation in α is usually lower than that in β phase, so the large
primary α grains in the microstructure may be the “ weak ” site for in-
ternal crack initiation. Moreover, long α / β lamellar grain boundaries
can also accumulate a large number of dislocations, which promotes
crack initiation [ 43 ]. Therefore, whether the slip, cleavage or two
mechanisms together lead to the final fracture of the material, the pri-
mary α
p
phase is a weak fatigue area, and the higher its content, the
worse the fatigue performance.
