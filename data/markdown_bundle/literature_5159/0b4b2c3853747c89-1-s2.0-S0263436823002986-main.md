Joining C/C composite in a few seconds using refectory high entropy alloy 
fillers via spark plasma sintering 
Xincheng Wang, Li Fu

ABSTRACT 
C/C composites were flash joined with ZrHfNbTa and TiZrHfTa refectory high entropy alloy fillers using spark 
plasma sintering within a short time frame of 16 s. The influence of different fillers on the joint morphology, 
shear strength at room temperature, and fracture behavior was investigated. The results indicated that the 
ZrHfNbTa filler completely transformed into carbide after flash SPS joining for 16 s, while the TiZrHfTa filler 
exhibited a (Ti-Zr-Hf-Ta)C phase, along with a small amount of Ti
–
Ta rich alloy phase in the central area. The 
shear strengths of the obtained C/C composite joints using ZrHfNbTa and TiZrHfTa fillers at room temperature 
were close, with value of 16.0 ± 2.4 MPa and 14.1 ± 2.0 MPa, respectively. This work introduces a novel and 
flexible processing approach for joining high temperature resistant materials. 
1. Introduction 
Owning to the low density, low linear expansion coefficient, strong 
thermal and electrical conductivities and excellent high temperature 
properties, carbon fiber reinforced carbon composites (C/C composites) 
have been widely used in the fields of aerospace and nuclear applica -
tions [ 1 – 4 ]. In general, the effective joining of C/C composites is crucial 
for the successful fabrication of complex structures. 
Several joining techniques, such as brazing [ 5 , 6 ] and diffusion 
bonding [ 7 ], have been developed for joining C/C composites either 
with themselves or with metals due to their simplicity, high joint 
strength, low cost-effectiveness and flexibility in adapting to complex 
shapes. However, despite their advantages, most joints exhibit limited 
serviceability at high temperatures, as the fillers and interlayers tend to 
soften at elevated temperatures, causing joint failure. 
High entropy alloys (HEAs), also known as multi-principal element 
alloys (MPEAs), as a new alloy design concept, has been developed 
greatly since it was proposed by Yeh [ 8 ] and Cantor [ 9 ] in 2004. HEAs 
exhibit many attractive properties such as high resistance to irradiation 
[ 10 ] and oxidation [ 11 ]. Of particular interest are refractory high en -
tropy alloys (RHEAs), which feature a single bcc structure and demon -
strate outstanding structural stability at high temperatures, as well as a 
unique combination of high tensile strength and plasticity [ 12 , 13 ]. 
Drawing inspiration from the HEA concept, a series of notable discov -
eries has been made with high entropy carbides (HECs), which show 
many promising properties, such as high hardness [ 14 ], excellent high- 
temperature flexural strength [ 15 ], oxidation [ 16 ] and ablation resis -
tance [ 17 ]. Based on the previous research on the interfacial reaction 
between RHEA foil and graphite [ 18 ], it is of great possibility to join C/C 
composites using the conversion from HEAs to HECs via the reaction 
between the HEAs and carbon. 
In recent years, there are many reports on the joining of high tem -
perature resistant materials like C/SiC [ 19 ] and YSZ [ 20 ] using spark 
plasma sintering (SPS), as well as flash joining based on flash spark 
plasma sintering (FSPS). This is due to the unique ability of SPS to 
achieve ultra-high bonding temperatures and heating rates, while also 
enabling the application of an electric field to accelerate self-diffusion 
and promote the migration of ions through the joining interface [ 21 ]. 
In the present work, C/C composites were joined using single-phase 
ZrHfNbTa and TiZrHfTa refectory high entropy alloy fillers in 16 s via 
flash SPS joining with rapid heating (over 100 
◦
C/s) and cooling rate. 
The resulting microstructure evolution, shear strength at room tem -
perature, and fracture behavior of each joint were investigated.

The phase composition and microstructure of the joints, as well as 
the fracture surfaces after mechanical testing, were analyzed using a 
scanning electron microscope (SEM, Tescan Clara GMH) equipped with 
energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS), X-Ray diffraction (XRD, Co

microscope (STEM, FEI, Themis Z), where the samples were fabricated 
by focused ion beam sectioning (FIB, Helios G4 CX). 
The shear strength of the joints was measured at room temperature 
using a universal testing machine (INSTRON 3882) with a constant 
speed of 0.5 mm/min and a single lap offset set-up. Four samples were 
tested to obtain an average value for the shear strength. As a comparison 
group for the shear strength testing, a set of joints was made using pure 
Ti foil (100 μ m thick). 
3. Results and discussion 
Fig. 1 (a) and (c) present the SEM BSE images of the polished cross 
sections of the C/C-ZrHfNbTa-C/C joint and C/C-TiZrHfTa-C/C joint, 
respectively, which were produced using flash SPS joining in 16 s. 
Moreover, Fig. 1 (b) and (d) display the magnified images of the brazed 
seams of these two joints, respectively. In summary, the morphology of 
the joints obtained using ZrHfNbTa and TiZrHfTa RHEA fillers were 
similar, and no observable defects, such as voids and cracks, were 
detected at either joint interface, indicating that it is feasible to join C/C 
composites with ZrHfNbTa and TiZrHfTa filler within a 16-s timeframe.

However, upon closer inspection of the C/C-TiZrHfTa-C/C joint 
structure, it was observed that a small quantity of a bright second phase 
was present in only a few central regions of the brazed seam, as depicted 
in Fig. 3 (a). To further investigate the microstructure and composition of 
this second phase, STEM analysis was conducted. As demonstrated by 
the EDS maps presented in Fig. 3 (b), it was verified that the second 
phase was abundant in Ti
–
Ta. Additionally, the high-resolution STEM 
high-angle annular dark-field (HAADF) image ( Fig. 3 (d)) and corre -
sponding fast Fourier transform (FFT) pattern ( Fig. 3 (e)) further indicate 
that the second phase had a bcc structure. Following the principle of the 
high-resolution STEM imaging, the brightness contrast of an image is 
proportional to the atomic number. Therefore, Fig. 3 (d) suggests that 
atoms were uniformly dispersed with no significant contrast variation, 
indicating the formation of a homogeneous solid solution. Fig. 3 (f) and 
(g) further confirm that the (Ti-Zr-Hf-Ta)C HEC matrix formed in the 
joint was a homogeneous solid solution phase with a fcc rock salt 
structure, which was in agreement with the XRD analysis. Notably, there 
was no significant atomic partition at the phase boundary, as there was 
no constant change at the phase boundary ( Fig. 3 (c)). Based on the 
above analysis, it can be determined that the second phase present in the 
C/C-TiZrHfTa-C/C joint was a Ti
–
Ta rich solid solution phase with a bcc 
structure, which was likely due to the incomplete reaction of the 
TiZrHfTa filler with the C/C composite substrates. As previously noted, 
Zr and Hf possess greater carbon affinity than Ti and Ta, causing their 
segregation towards the filler-C/C interface to form Zr
–
Hf rich carbide 
at the beginning of joining process. This resulted in residual unreacted 
Ti
–
Ta rich alloy in the central region of the brazed seam. As the SPS 
heating process continued, more alloy was carbonized, and the formed 
carbide gradually homogenize. However, due to the rapid flash SPS 
process, the TiZrHfTa filler ( bcc structure) was unable to fully carbonize, 
leading to a small amount of residual second phase in the final joint 
microstructure. In contrast, no second phase was found in the C/C- 
ZrHfNbTa-C/C joint. A reasonable explanation for this is that during 
the formation process of the joints, C atoms continue to diffuse into the 
filler and form carbides, so the C self-diffusion rate is closely related to 
the formation of joints. Given that the atomic radius of Ti (1.429 Å) is 
the smallest among these five elements, the lattice parameter of the 
carbide formed near the central area of the C/C-TiZrHfTa-C/C joint was 
smaller than that near the central area of the C/C-ZrHfNbTa-C/C joint, 
resulting in a smaller tetrahedral site. The C self-diffusion process is 
thought to be a two-step process, where the C atom can occupy an empty 
tetrahedral site before migrating into a nearest neighbor C-vacancy 
[ 23 , 24 ]. Thus, C diffused more readily into the central region of the 
ZrHfNbTa filler, avoiding the occurrence of a second phase.

ZrHfNbTa and TiZrHfTa RHEA fillers via flash SPS in a short duration of 
16 s. During the flash SPS process, the melted fillers infiltrated into the 
C/C composite substrates, leading to a significant shrinkage of the
