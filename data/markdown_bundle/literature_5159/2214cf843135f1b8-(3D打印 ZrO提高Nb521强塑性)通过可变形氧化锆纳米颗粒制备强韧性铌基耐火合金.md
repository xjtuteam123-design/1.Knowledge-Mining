ABSTRACT 
The use of dispersed precipitates is an effective method for strengthening metals, but it typically results in a 
reduction in ductility. A potential way to overcome this strength-ductility trade-off is through the incorporation 
of coherent nanoscale deformable precipitates. Here, we present an approach of designing and fabricating a 
niobium-based (Nb-5W-2Mo-1Zr, Nb521) alloy with copiously and semi-coherently dispersed nanoscale zirconia 
(ZrO
2
) precipitates through laser powder bed fusion followed by heat treating. The intergranular ZrO
2 
pre -
cipitates at grain boundaries underwent tetragonal-to-monoclinic martensitic phase transformation and defor -
mation twinning, while the intragranular ZrO
2 
precipitates within the matrix experienced deformation twinning 
and substantial plastic deformation during tension. The combination of transformation induced plasticity (TRIP) 
and twinning induced plasticity (TWIP) from the nanoscale ZrO
2 
precipitates provides sufficient capacity to 
accommodate strain and relieve stress concentration. Additionally, the intragranular ZrO
2 
precipitates effectively 
piled up incoming dislocations from the Nb matrix to form high-density dislocation networks during tension, 
which interacted with each other via the Orowan mechanism and finally resulted in its significant plastic 
deformation. These mechanisms contribute to the exceptional tensile properties of the Nb521 alloy, including an 
ultimate strength of 628.7 ± 2.3 MPa and a ductility of 17.8 ± 0.6% with a superior work-hardening capacity 
(~230 MPa), achieving a record of strength and ductility combination for the Nb521 alloys fabricated by ad -
ditive manufacturing to date. This strategy represents a promising approach for designing dispersion- 
strengthened refractory alloys with excellent synergy of strength and ductility. 
1. Introduction 
Niobium (Nb)-based alloys, including Nb-5W-2Mo-1Zr (Nb521) and 
Nb-10Hf-1Ti (C103), are widely used in the aerospace fields such as 
propulsion systems, due to their superior high-temperature mechanical 
properties, low density among refractory alloys, and excellent corrosion 
resistance [ 1 – 5 ]. These properties make Nb521 an attractive choice for 
use as a new generation of high-temperature structural material [ 6 – 8 ]. 
Recently, additive manufacturing (AM) has emerged as a cost-effective 
and efficient method for fabricating complex and lightweight Nb- 
based parts [ 9 – 11 ], as compared to conventional manufacturing routes. 
Nb521 alloys have been produced using additive manufacturing 
techniques such as laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) [ 11 ], electron beam 
melting (EBM) [ 12 , 13 ] and laser melting deposition (LMD) [ 14 ]. How -
ever, the extreme conditions of the AM process, including rapid cooling

tion [ 16 , 17 ]. This can result in ultrafine grains and high densities of 
dislocations in the additive manufactured (AMed) parts. The ultrafine 
grains, precipitates (e.g., (Nb, Zr)C or Nb

Nb521 alloys [ 12 – 14 ]), and dislocations in the AMed Nb521 alloys all 
contribute to a higher yield strength (higher than 100 – 150 MPa) 
[ 11 , 13 ], as compared to the cast-and-wrought Nb521 parts [ 7 , 13 , 14 ]. 
On the other hand, the increased density of grain boundaries (GBs), 
heterogeneous interfaces between the carbide precipitates and Nb ma -
trix, and high-density dislocations present in the AMed Nb521 alloys can 
lead to significant stress concentration, resulting in an early onset of 
plastic instability and premature failure (showing brittle fracture for the 
parts made by LPBF and LMD [ 14 ], and ~ 10% of ductility lower for the 
EBM-made parts than that of the cast-and-wrought parts [ 7 , 13 , 14 ]). To 
address this issue, post treatment processes such as annealing under 
vacuum or argon atmosphere [ 14 , 18 – 20 ] and hot isostatic pressing 
(HIP) [ 11 , 18 , 19 ] have been used to reduce the internal stress remained 
in the Nb-based alloys caused by the AM process and improve the 
ductility, but these treatments often lead to a ~ 100 MPa decrease in 
strength. As a result, achieving an excellent combination of both 
strength and ductility in the AMed Nb521 alloys remains a challenge to 
date. 
In a previous study, we reported on the fabrication of the Nb521 
alloy using LPBF with quasi-spherical hydride-dehydride (HDH) pow -
ders modified by jet milling as the raw feedstock [ 11 ]. The use of HDH 
Nb521 powders not only reduces the powder cost significantly but also 
generates secondary nanoscale zirconia (ZrO
2
) precipitates rather than 
carbide particles, unlike spherical powders as commonly used in other 
studies [ 12 – 14 ]. This is due to the higher oxygen content (~ 1600 ppm) 
of the jet-milled HDH Nb521 powders by comparing with the atomized 
powders [ 12 – 14 ], which is thermodynamically favored to react with 
zirconium to form nanoscale ZrO
2 
precipitates and suppress the carbide 
precipitation [ 11 , 21 , 22 ]. ZrO
2 
is an ionically bonded ceramic that can 
undergo plastic deformation (when in the form of single crystal) through 
phase transformation and stress-induced deformation twinning [ 23 – 28 ]. 
As compared to the intrinsically brittle carbide precipitates by using the 
atomized powders, the generated single-crystalline ZrO
2 
particles in our 
previous study [ 11 ] are expected to demonstrate a much better 
deformability that provides an excellent ductility. Conversely, the 
generated ZrO
2 
precipitates in Ref. [ 11 ] did not present an acceptable 
deformability, and the as-made Nb521 alloy demonstrated an unsatis -
factory fracture elongation ( < 6%). This unexpected result [ 11 ] is 
possibly caused by the remained large internal stress, and the achieved 
ultrafine particle size of ZrO
2 
(below 30 nm) that is unfavorable for the 
phase transformation and deformation twinning due to the high acti -
vation energy during deformation [ 26 ]. The work in Ref. [ 11 ] focused 
on the precipitation behavior, and tailoring of size and distribution of 
ZrO
2 
particles, while still lacking of underlying deformation mechanism, 
causing the unsatisfied ductility of the as-made Nb521 alloy. 
Here, we present an approach for in situ incorporation of semi- 
coherent, copious and homogeneously dispersed nanoscale single- 
crystalline ZrO
2 
particles into the Nb521 alloy through LPBF followed 
by vacuum heat treating, achieving superior combination of strength 
and ductility. In the current study, plastic deformation of single- 
crystalline ZrO
2 
particles (with a mean size over 60 nm) occurred 
through phase transformation and deformation twinning during stress 
loading. Moreover, the semi-coherent interface between the ZrO
2 
par -
ticles and Nb matrix can also accommodate strain and transmit 
incoming dislocations from the matrix [ 29 – 31 ]. Both factors theoreti -
cally contribute to the material ’ s ductility. However, the deformation 
mechanism of the ZrO
2 
particles e.g., deformation twinning and phase 
transformation, and their effect on the mechanical properties of the 
LPBF-made (LPBFed) Nb521 alloy after heat treating are still not well 
understood. These investigations are of great importance, and beneficial 
to optimize the microstructure and mechanical properties of the Nb521 
alloy by tuning the size, population, and spatial distribution of the ZrO
2 
particles. 
Bearing this in mind, the microstructures and tensile properties of 
the as-fabricated Nb521 alloy were characterized in detail in this study. 
An emphasis was placed on the phase transformation and deformation 
mechanisms of the in situ generated single-crystalline ZrO
2 
nano -
particles, and associated strengthening and ductilizing mechanisms of 
the as-fabricated Nb521 alloy. Our strategy offers a promising pathway 
for producing dispersion-strengthened refractory alloys with excellent 
mechanical properties. 
2. Experimental 
2.1. Powder feedstock 
HDH Nb521 powders modified by jet milling were used as the raw 
feedstock for the LPBF process. The detailed information of the powders 
can be found in Ref. [ 11 ]. The powders have a quasi-spherical 
morphology as shown in Fig. 1 (a), with a particle flowability of 38.6 
± 0.6 s/50g and an average particle size (D
50

representative chemical composition of the powders is listed in Table 1 , 
which shows an oxygen content of 0.13 wt%. The particle size distri -

3000, UK), and the interstitial elemental (O, N, H) content was measured 
with an inert gas fusion analytical instrument (Leco-Tch600, USA). The 
alloying elemental content (W, Mo, Zr) was determined using an ICP 
analyzer (Agilent ICPMS7800, USA). 
2.2. Laser powder bed fusion and heat treatment 
The Nb521 specimens were fabricated using an LPBF machine (SLM 
Solutions 125HL, Germany) equipped with a 400 W IPG fiber laser. The 
substrate was preheated to 200 
◦

and a hatch distance of 0.10 mm, respectively. The scanning strategy 
was the stripe of zigzag pattern with a stripe length of 10 mm and a 
rotation of 67
◦

1 × 10

3.2. Tensile properties 
The representative engineering tensile stress-strain curve of the as- 
fabricated Nb521 alloy is shown in Fig. 4 (a). The achieved room- 
temperature ultimate tensile strength (UTS) and fracture strain of the 
alloy are 628.7 ± 2.3 MPa and 17.8 ± 0.6%, respectively. It is inter -
esting to note that the engineering stress slightly decreases when the 
engineering strain reaches ~ 5.5% (see Fig. 4 (a) and S3), which may be 
due to the sudden activation of specific deformation mechanism. The 
true stress-strain and work-hardening rate curves, presented in Fig. 4 (b), 
reveal a significant plastic strain accompanied by work hardening. The 
as-fabricated Nb521 specimen exhibits a high work-hardening capacity 
(~ 230 MPa, σ
YS
/ σ
UTS 
= 0.63, YS: yield strength) and excellent uniform 
elongation (from ~ 1% true strain to fracture), which would guarantee a 
significant safety margin before fracture in structural applications. The 
high uniform elongation correlates to a steady and progressive work- 
hardening procedure, including the increase of dislocation slip resis -
tance and synergistic deformation between the precipitates and matrix. 
The responsible strengthening and ductilizing mechanisms will be dis -
cussed in the following section. The tensile performance is exceptional 
compared to the AMed and cast-and-wrought Nb521 alloys and AMed 
C103 alloys reported in the literature, as shown in Fig. 4 (c), achieving a 
record of strength and ductility combination for the AMed Nb521 alloys 
to date ( Fig. 4 (c)). 
The fracture surface of the specimen after tension exhibits a domi -
nant ductile feature, as implied by numerous fine dimples with slight 
cleavage appearance, as shown in Fig. 4 (d) and (e). In particular, the 
amplified feature in Fig. 4 (f) shows a high density of nanoscale particles 
embedded in the fine dimples, which are likely secondary ZrO
2 
pre -
cipitates detached from the Nb matrix after tensile fracture.

4. Discussion 
4.1. Evolution of crystallographic structure of ZrO
2 
ZrO
2 
is a well-studied ceramic material with a range of desirable 
properties, including high strength, high operating temperature, excel -
lent oxidation and corrosion resistance, and unique shape memory effect 
[ 26 – 28 ]. At equilibrium, three different crystal structures are typically 
observed for ZrO
2 
at different temperature ranges [ 36 ]: (i) monoclinic 
m-ZrO
2
, stable up to 1443 K; (ii) tetragonal t-ZrO
2
, metastable at room 
temperature and stable up to 2625 K; and (iii) cubic c-ZrO
2
, stable from 
2625 K to its melting point. The tetragonal t-ZrO
2 
can transform to the 
monoclinic m-ZrO
2 
through either stress or thermal activity, a process 
known as martensitic phase transformation, which is often accompanied 
by the reorientation of twin laths in the microstructure [ 23 , 27 , 40 ]. The 
phase composition and twinning behavior of ZrO
2
, in turn, can greatly 
influence the mechanical properties of ZrO
2 
and materials containing it, 
such as the as-fabricated Nb521 alloy in this study. Therefore, it is 
crucial to understand the effects of thermal and stress activity on the 
evolution of crystallographic structure of ZrO
2 
with associated 
mechanisms.

4.2. Strengthening and ductilizing mechanisms 
4.2.1. Strengthening 
The Nb521 alloy produced by LPBF in this work has a high density of 
ZrO
2 
(with a volume fraction of ~ 3.1%) evenly distributed throughout 
the Nb matrix ( Fig. 2 ), instead of carbides that strengthen the Nb521 
alloys manufactured by traditional technologies [ 12 – 14 ]. The carbides 
have been suppressed during rapid cooling in the LPBF process [ 11 ]. 
Upon tensile loading, the localized stress triggered the martensitic 
transformation, by which the intergranular metastable t-ZrO
2 
phase 
transformed into the m-ZrO
2 
phase (see Figs. 5 and 7 ), resulting in the 
phase transformation induced toughening effect [ 48 ], and subsequently 
enhances the strength and fracture toughness of the material [ 23 ]. 
Furthermore, the typical dispersion strengthening caused by the ZrO
2 
precipitates embedded in the matrix can be seen in the microstructures 
of the specimens after tensile fracture, as shown in Fig. 8 . Fig. 8 (a) shows 
that the Nb521 specimen after tension contains networks with high 
densities of dislocations, which formed around the ZrO
2 
precipitates 
(highlighted by arrows in the corresponding DF-STEM image of Fig. 8 
(b)). As shown in the enlarged image in Fig. 8 (c), a large number of 
tangled dislocations formed cell structures around the precipitates, 
effectively piling up the dislocations. With increasing the stress, the 
semi-coherent interface between the ZrO
2 
precipitates and Nb matrix 
generates long-range stresses that effectively inhibit the dislocation slip, 
resulting in the dislocations interacting with precipitates through the 
Orowan bowing mechanism and even plastically deforming the ZrO
2 
precipitates (see Figs. 5, 6 and 7 ). Besides, this dislocation-precipitate

4.2.2. Ductilizing 
The as-fabricated Nb521 alloy exhibits a remarkable strength of 
628.7 MPa and a uniform elongation of up to 17.8% (Fig. S3(a)), su -
perior to the conventional Nb521 and C103 alloys made by AM ( Fig. 4 
(c)). Unlike the brittle ZrC precipitates with the covalent bonding [ 53 ] in 
the conventional Nb521 alloys, the plastically deformable ZrO
2 
pre -
cipitates in this study ( Figs. 5, 6 and 7 ) show a different deformation 
behavior under stress. The presence of martensitic tetragonal-to- 
monoclinic transformation in ZrO
2 
( Figs. 5 and 7 ) can be controlled to 
provide plasticity during tension, improving the ductility through 
transformation induced plasticity (TRIP) [ 23 ]. It is important to note 
that the ZrO
2 
precipitates in this study are all nanoscale single- 
crystalline particles with the mean size over 60 nm (much larger than 
those in the LPBFed specimen, Fig. S2(a) vs. Fig. S2(b)), which signifi -
cantly reduces the energy barrier to trigger the phase transformation 
and favors the continuously plastic deformation of ZrO
2 
(transformation 
mismatch stresses may cause cracks in polycrystal ZrO
2 
[ 28 ]). The 
deformation of nanoscale ZrO
2 
precipitates ( Figs. 5 and 7 ) begins when 
the stress of surrounding matrix has increased significantly through 
work hardening, allowing the stress to reach a level sufficient to impose 
critical stress on the ZrO
2 
precipitates. The progressive transformation 
and twinning of ZrO
2 
during tension ( Figs. 5 and 7 ) can accommodate 
incoming strains, effectively relieving the stress concentration caused by 
dislocation pile-up and leading to prolonged uniform elongation and 
enhanced work hardening due to the synthetical effect of TWIP and TRIP 
[ 52 , 54 ]. In this sense, the in situ synthesized nanoscale single-crystalline 
ZrO
2 
precipitates in this study exhibit steady synergistic deformation 
with the Nb matrix, which is mainly responsible for strengthening 
without sacrificing ductility. 
Dislocations within Nb grains also exhibit a great influence on the 
tensile ductility of the Nb521 alloy. After tensile fracture, we analyzed 
the dislocation feature in a Nb grain under the two-beam condition (with 
the same zone axis of z = [ 11 1 ] and different g vectors of [ 110 ] , [ 01 1 ] and 
[ 101 ] ), as presented in Fig. 9 . We randomly selected 30 dislocations and 
highlighted them by solid lines. Based on the extinction conditions 
(dislocations are invisible when g
→

→
= 0), the dislocations are indi -
cated by different colors in terms of Burgers vector. Among these dis -
locations in Fig. 9 , the majority dislocations are determined as the mix of 
edge-and-screw and edge types, while few ones belong to the screw 
typed dislocations as highlighted by white arrows. It indicates that non- 
screw dislocations are dominant in the deformed Nb521 specimen, 
which also benefits the ductility due to the intrinsically high mobility of 
non-screw dislocations in the BCC-structured metals e.g., Nb [ 55 – 58 ]. 
Besides, the heat treatment enables the stress relief and thus benefits the 
final ductility, as compared to the LPBFed specimen. All above are 
responsible for the strong and ductile Nb521 alloy. 
The size, population, and spatial distribution of nanoscale ZrO
2 
precipitates can be adjusted through controlling the LPBF processing 
parameters and heat-treating process to optimize the properties of the 
Nb521 alloy in the future. The introduction of semi-coherent nanoscale 
deformable dispersions, as demonstrated in this work, provides a new 
approach for designing high-performance refractory metals for a wide 
range of applications.
