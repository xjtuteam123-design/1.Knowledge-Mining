abstract
The microstructure, creep resistance, and high-temperature shear strength of the Mg-5Sn, Mg-5Sn-1.4Ca,
and Mg-5Sn-0.4Sb alloys were studied in the as-cast and aged conditions. Impression creep tests were
performed at 150 and 200
o C and under punching stresses in the range 250-500 MPa. The shear yield stress
(SYS) and ultimate shear strength (USS) were measured by the shear punch test (SPT) in the temperature
range 25-250
o C. The results showed that creep resistance and shear strength of the base alloy were
signiﬁcantly enhanced with the addition of Sb and Ca. This was attributed to the concurrent formation of
Mg
2 Sn particles with the more thermally stable CaMgSn or Mg3 Sb2 phases which strengthen both matrix
and grain boundaries during deformation in the investigated system. Furthermore, mechanical properties
and creep resistance of the aged alloys were much better than those of the as-cast materials, due to the
higher volume fraction of Mg
2Sn, Mg 3 Sb2 , and CaMgSn precipitates distributed homogeneously in the
microstructure of the aged materials.

1. Introduction
Magnesium alloys have great potential for high performance
structural applications due to the unique combination of low
density, high speciﬁc strength, superior damping capacity and
good castability. The most commonly used magnesium alloys are
those based on the Mg-Al system [1,2]. Although Al signiﬁcantly
increases castability, discontinuous precipitation of the Mg
17Al12
/H9252-phase signiﬁcantly decreases the high temperature strength of
these alloys, and thus the application of the aluminum-containing
Mg alloys is often limited to temperatures below 125
◦C [2–4].
Consequently, improving the elevated-temperature properties has
become a critical issue for possible applications of the cast Mg
alloys in hot components. In this connection, the high tempera-
ture properties of the Mg-Sn-based cast alloys have attracted some
attentions [5–8]. It has been shown that the formation of ﬁne ther-
mally stable Mg
2Sn particles is the main strengthening mechanism
in these alloys.
To further improve the mechanical properties and creep resis-
tance of the Mg-Sn alloys, different alloying elements such as RE,
Zn, Si, Ca, and Sb have been used as the ternary alloying ele-
ments to form the new thermally stable intermetallics. Among
these elements, Ca has found a widespread acceptance due to
its low cost and its inherent ability to improve mechanical prop-

Enhanced age hardening response of Mg-Sn alloys has been inves-

2. Experimental procedure
2.1. Materials and processing
The three alloys used in this investigation were based on Mg-
5 wt.% Sn with 0.4 Sb and 1.4 wt.% Ca additions. High purity Mg, Sn,
Ca and Sb were used to prepare the alloys. Melting was carried
out in an electrical furnace under the Foseco Magrex 36 cover-
ing ﬂux to protect molten magnesium from oxidation. The melt
was held at 750
◦C for 20 min and mechanically stirred for 2 min
before Sb or Ca was added to it. Another 20 min were allowed to
ensure a homogenous composition and to settle the oxides and
other contaminants before pouring the melt into a permanent mold
preheated to 150
◦C. Pouring was accomplished by a tilt-casting
technique in order to minimize the melt turbulence and the quick
emergence of entrapped air. The cast bars had dimensions of 12
mm × 20 mm × 180 mm, from which 4 and 1 mm thick slices were
cut by an electrodischarge wire-cut machine for structural charac-
terization, impression creep, and mechanical testing.
To study the effect of precipitation hardening on the mechan-
ical behavior of the material, some of the samples were solution
treated at 480
◦C for 22 h in a resistance furnace under an argon
atmosphere, before being water quenched at room temperature.
For precipitation hardening, samples were aged at 210
◦C for
24 h. Both cast and aged samples were studied by scanning elec-
tron microscopy (SEM) and optical microscopy to examine the
microstructure of the alloys. The specimens were polished with
0.3-/H9262m alumina paste, followed by polishing on an abrasive-free
microcloth. Etching was carried out using a 5% nitric acid and 95%
ethyl alcohol solution at room temperature. The X-ray diffraction
(XRD) and energy-dispersive X-ray (EDS) analysis were carried out
on selected samples to identify the phases.
2.2. Impression creep tests
The 4-mm thick slices were tested in an impression tester. An
MTS universal tensile testing machine equipped with a three-zone
split furnace was used to perform constant-load impression tests
in the air atmosphere. A ﬂat-ended cylindrical punch 2 mm in
diameter was mounted in a holder positioned in the center of the
vertical loading bar. The specimen was located on an anvil below
the loading bar; the assembly of the specimen and the indenter
was accommodated by the split furnace. Impression creep mea-
surements were made on each sample at 150 and 200
◦C and under
punch stresses in the range 250 to 500 MPa for dwell times up to
3600 s. After application of the load, the impression depth was mea-
sured automatically as a function of time by the machine; the data
were acquired by a computer.
2.3. Mechanical property measurements
In order to study the effects of Ca and Sb additions and
microstructural evolution on the mechanical properties of the cast
and aged alloys, the shear strength of the alloys was assessed by
the SPT in the temperature range 25-250
◦C. The 1-mm thick slices
of the as-cast and aged materials were ground to a thickness of
0.6 mm, from which disks of 10 mm in diameter were punched
for the SPT. A shear punch ﬁxture with a 3.175 mm diameter ﬂat
cylindrical punch and 3.225 mm diameter receiving-hole was used
for this experiment. Shear punch tests were performed at different
temperatures using a screw driven MTS material testing system
equipped with a three-zone split furnace with a load cell of 20 kN
capacity and a crosshead speed of 0.25 mm/min. After application
of the load, the applied load P was measured automatically as a
function of punch displacement; the data were acquired by a com-
puter so as to determine the shear stress of the tested materials

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructural observations
Fig. 1 a-c show the optical microstructure of the investigated
alloys in the as-cast condition. As can be seen, the coarse dendritic
structure of the Mg-5Sn alloy in the as-cast condition is generally
reﬁned after Ca and Sb addition. This dendritic structure, how-
ever, is totally removed after the long solutionizing treatment, as
exhibited in Fig. 1d-f. For the Mg-5Sn base alloy, shown in Fig. 1d,
the microstructure consists of large Mg grains with small spheri-
cal particles, which are mostly located in the grain boundaries of
the aged material. A comparison of the Mg-5Sn-1.4Ca microstruc-
tures in the as-cast and aged conditions in Fig. 1 b and e reveals
that the structure has been reﬁned and the volume fraction of the
second phases has signiﬁcantly increased after aging. In the Sb-
containing alloy, illustrated in Fig. 1 c and f, aging has resulted in
a rather coarse grain structure with a uniform distribution of ﬁne
precipitated throughout the matrix.
The XRD patterns of the binary base alloy in the as-cast and
aged conditions, exhibited in Fig. 2, indicate that /H9251-Mg and Mg
2Sn
are the only constituents of the Mg-5Sn alloy. It is also evident
that the intensity of the Mg
2Sn peaks increases after aging, the
results which are in agreement with those reported by Wei et al.
[8]. Examination of the XRD patterns for the ternary Ca-containing
and Sb-bearing materials shows some new peaks corresponding
respectively to the CaMgSn and Mg
3Sb2 intermetallic phases. It
can be inferred that, the intensity of CaMgSn, Mg 3Sb2, and Mg 2Sn
peaks increased notably by the aging process. As it will be dis-
cussed later, these intermetallic compounds would enhance the
high-temperature properties of the material. It is worth noting that
neither the binary Mg-Ca compounds such as Mg
2Ca, nor the Sn-Ca
intermetallics have been detected in the Mg-5Sn-1.4Ca alloy. Our
results are in agreement with those reported for similar alloys by
Hort et al. [5].
Fig. 3 shows the SEM micrographs of the Mg-5Sn, Mg-5Sn-1.4Ca,
and Mg-5Sn-0.4Sb alloys in the as-cast and aging-treated condi-
tions at the same magniﬁcation. The microstructure of the cast
Mg-5Sn alloy consists of the primary /H9251-Mg matrix and the lamel-
lar eutectic structure at the grain boundaries, as shown in Fig. 3 a.

lamellar eutectic structure in the cast condition consists of /H9251-Mg

higher volume fraction of the Mg 2Sn phase in the aged material is
in consistent with the XRD results. The microstructure of the cast

cast Mg-5Sn alloy, the binary Mg

and EDS results ( Table 1 ), aging treatment increases the volume

Due to the localized nature of the impression creep test and SPT,
care must be taken to consider the impact of the comparable sizes of
the punch, defects, grains, and other microstructural constituents.
This is likely to cause signiﬁcant scatter, depending on where the
punching is made, and how many grains and other constituents
contribute to the obtained depth-time and load–displacement data.
If, for example, only a few grains contribute, the data will become
dependent on crystallographic orientation. The same is true for
the microstructural inhomogeneities and defects which may be
located beneath the punch, affecting the materials resistance to
compressive and shear deformation. This is why the majority of
the SPT studies have concentrated on wrought materials having
very homogeneous defect-free microstructures [20]. In the present
work, however, at least three separate measurements were taken at
random places on the surface of the impression creep specimens for
each condition and the depth–time curves were almost identical.
The same is true for the SPT, in which the load-displacement curves
of three different samples were almost matching with little scat-
ter. This may imply that, due to the use of a tilt-casting method, a
reﬁned defect-free microstructure has been resulted, and hence the
impression and shear punch test data bear a high level of reliability.

4. Conclusions
The microstructural evolution, creep resistance, and high-
temperature shear deformation of Mg-5Sn with 1.4% Ca and 0.4%
Sb additions were investigated in the as-cast and aged conditions.
The microstructure of Mg-5Sn alloy in the as-cast condition con-
sists of Mg
2Sn intermetallic phase in /H9251-Mg matrix. The separate
addition of 1.4% Ca and 0.4% Sb improved the creep resistance
and mechanical properties of the Mg-5Sn alloy, mainly due to the
respective formation of the thermally stable CaMgSn and Mg
3Sb2
intermetallic particles. The enhanced creep resistance and mechan-
ical properties of the material in the aged condition are related
to the dispersive distribution of Mg
2Sn with either CaMgSn or
Mg3Sb2 phases in the /H9251-Mg matrix. These thermally stable par-
ticles strengthen both grains and grain boundaries and oppose
both recovery and recrystallization processes during deforma-
tion processes at elevated temperatures, decreasing the minimum
creep rate and increasing the strength of the material. The high-
est creep resistance and shear strength of the Sb-containing
