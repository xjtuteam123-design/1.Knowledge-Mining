ABSTRACT
The strengthening phases in Ni-based superalloys are the key structures for providing excellent high-temperature 
mechanical properties, and a current lack of understanding of the very initial step-to-step formation mechanisms 
for these phases hinders their applications in advanced manufacturing. Here we report the initial formation 
mechanism of the γ’ phase in the GH4099 Ni-based superalloy, which differs from the reported classical and non- 
classical nucleation theories. Based on the experimental observations, preferable bondings between γ’ elements 
will induce the Al-Al and Ti-Ti clustering, acting as the starting mechanism in the chemical homogeneous su -
peralloy. This spinodal-like chemical modulation causes the uphill diffusion of the γ’ elements into the clusters 
and reduces the energy barrier of the chemical ordering of the γ’ phase in them. This transitional initial for -
mation mechanism of the strengthening phase in this alloy will provide a foundamental theory to design the 
novel superalloys for advanced manufacturing and processing.
Ni-based superalloys are widely used as the primary material of 
structural components in demanding environments, such as aerospace 
turbo engines, nuclear reactors, and modern power plants, due to their 
exceptional microstructural stability and oxidation/corrosion resistance 
at elevated temperatures [ 1 – 3 ]. For γ’ phase strengthened nickel-based 
superalloys, the precipitating process of the strengthening phase will 
affect both their manufacturing (especially additive manufacturing, 
welding etc.) and post-heat treatment strategies, which might cause the 
degradation of the superalloy components ’ performance in an aggres -
sive service environment [ 4 , 5 ]. Thus, understanding the formation and 
coarsening mechanisms of the γ’ phase in the Ni-based superalloys is 
crucial for designing/modifying superalloy systems and upgrading cur -
rent superalloy parts in both scientific and technological aspects. 
Although the coarsening/growth of the γ’ phase has been intensively 
discussed [ 6 – 8 ], the initial formation mechanism remains unclear.
The initial precipitation of the γ’ phase has been discussed among 
simple binary Ni-Al systems [ 9 – 12 ], ternary Ni-Al-Ti or Ni-Al-Cr systems 
[ 13 – 18 ], and multi-component Ni-based alloy systems [ 19 , 20 ] with 
inconsistent formation mechanisms of the γ’ phase being proposed. 
Generally, there are two different initial formation mechanisms of the γ’ 
phase in the Ni-based superalloys, which are spinodal decomposition 
and chemical ordering [ 21 – 23 ]. These mechanisms are the two 
competing and mutually exclusive processes in disordered solid solu -
tions [ 24 , 25 ], usually sololy being observed in a single Ni-based alloy 
system [ 11 , 26 – 29 ]. However, uphill diffusion at the interfaces was 
detected for most elements of Ni-based superalloy, with the chemical 
ordering of the γ’ phase ’ s L1
2 
structure at the γ’ elements enriched re -
gion, providing strong evidence of a combined chemical ordering and 
spinodal decomposition mechanism [ 30 , 31 ]. However, the interrela -
tionship between these two formation mechanisms of the γ’ phase in the 
Ni-based superalloy remains unclear, especialy during the very initial 
formation process. Therefore, further investigation is necessary to unveil 
the formation mechanisms and the interaction between the spinodal 
decomposition and chemical ordering mechanisms during the very 
initial γ’ phase formation process in the Ni-based superalloy, particu -
larly within the elements homogeneously distributed matrix of addi -
tively manufactured samples.

In summary, this work provides an atomic-level characterization and 
kinetic-thermodynamic explanation of the initial formation mechanism 
of the ordered γ ′ phase from the disordered γ matrix in GH4099 Ni-based 
superalloy. Unlike the classical and non-classical nucleation theory, the 
electron microscope and MC/MD investigation clearly identified the 
disordered clusters of the γ ′ elements, Al and Ti, as the initial formation 
mechanism before chemical ordering during the short-term aging, 
which is spinodal-like-chemical-modulation process induced by the 
atomic preferable bonding and separation. With increasing aging time, 
chemical ordering and continuous uphill diffusion of the γ ′ elements 
from the matrix to the clustering regions are revealed, elucidating the 
initial formation-growth process of γ ′ phase. The increasing Al concen -
tration in the clustering region reduces the thermodynamic energy 
barrier of the γ to γ ′ transition, facilitating the chemical ordering and 
formation of the ordered γ ′ phase in the Al-Ti clusters, which might serve 
as a theoretical foundation for optimizing the precipitation in the 
superalloys.
