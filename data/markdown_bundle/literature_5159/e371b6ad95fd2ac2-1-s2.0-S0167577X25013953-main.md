ABSTRACT
Conventional Ti6Al4V(Ti64) alloys struggle to balance strength and ductility under dynamic loading, limiting 
their use in extreme conditions. Herein, a Ti64-FeCoNiCrMo heterostructured alloy was fabricated via selective 
laser melting with FeCoNiCrMo high-entropy alloy (HEA) additions, aiming to enhance dynamic mechanical 
performance. The alloy incorporated HEA as β -phase stabilizers, achieved microstructural heterogeneity to 
enhanced strain rate sensitivity and energy dissipation. Split Hopkinson Pressure Bar test results revealed strain 
rate hardening, with peak stress reaching approximately 2100 MPa at 3500 s
 1 
and 20 
◦
C, as well as temperature 
softening, with a 25 – 30 % reduction in strength at 300 
◦
C. XRD revealed β → α ’ transformation, dislocation 
tangles, and grain refinement, enhancement of ductility and energy dissipation. Heterostructure enabled syn -
ergistic strain partitioning with reduced strength-ductility trade-offs. This study demonstrates the potential of 
HEA-reinforced Ti64 alloy for aerospace and marine applications requiring impact resistance.
1. Introduction
The Ti6Al4V(Ti64) alloy is a well-known engineering material 
due to its excellent mechanical properties, corrosion resistance, 
biocompatibility, and lightweight [ 1 ]. Owning to these properties, it 
is extensively employed in aerospace, marine, and automotive ap -
plications. However, it faces many challenges under dynamic stress 
conditions, i.e., high-speed impact, explosive blast, or ballistic 
incident. Ti64 exhibits minimal strain rate hardening and is sus -
ceptible to adiabatic shear banding (ASB), leading to failure [ 2 ]. 
Despite extensive research, achieving balance between strength and 
ductility in Ti64 alloys under dynamic loading remains challenge.
Selective laser melting (SLM) is able to produce Ti64 but has 
difficulty achieving strength-ductility balance [ 3 ]. To solve this 
problem, a technique was created to control the synthesis as well as 
composition of transition-induced plasticity (TRIP) titanium alloys 
by employing mix powders that contain β -phase stabilizer. This 
method utilized the fast solidification and in-situ alloying properties 
of SLM to form heterostructures in titanium alloys [ 4 ]. In our pre -
vious research [ 3 ], high entropy alloy of FeCoNiCrMo was incor -
porated into the Ti64 alloy matrix and prepared by SLM, finally 
heterostructured Ti64/FeCoNiCrMo with excellent strength 1366 
MPa and ductility 8.7 %, was achieved. The dynamic mechanical 
properties of Ti64 have been extensively researched [ 5 ]. However, 
the dynamic mechanical properties of Ti64/FeCoNiCrMo alloy have 
never been reported.
In this study, the dynamic compressive characteristics of SLM pro -
cessed Ti64-FeCoNiCrMo heterogeneous alloy was investigated under 
high strain rate impacting load. Dynamic compressive tests conducted at 
varying strain rates (1500 – 3500 s
 1
) and temperatures (20 – 300 
◦
C) 
revealed significant strain rate hardening and temperature-induced 
softening effects, both of which play a crucial role in influencing the 
material ’ s deformation behavior.

3 . Results and discussion
X-ray diffraction (XRD) was employed to study phase evolution 
in the FeCoNiCrMo-Ti64 composite under mechanical stress and 
varying temperature/strain rate conditions. In Fig. 2 (a), the XRD 
patterns of Ti64 and FeCoNiCrMo powders show characteristic 
peaks for the α / α ′ -phase for the Ti64 powder. The FeCoNiCrMo alloy 
exhibits peaks attributed to its FCC-phase. The mixed Ti64-3.0 wt% 
FeCoNiCrMo powder displays peaks from both the α / α ′ -phase and 
the FCC-phase, with the Ti64 peaks being more prominent, sug -

4. Conclusion
This study demonstrates that incorporating FeCoNiCrMo HEA into 
Ti64 via selective laser melting significantly enhances dynamic 
mechanical properties. At 20 
◦
C, peak stress increases by 18 – 22 % 
(~2100 MPa) due to dislocation entanglement under high strain rates. 
However, at elevated temperatures (150 – 300 
◦
C), flow stress decreases 
by 25 – 30 % due to thermal softening. Enhanced β → α ′ transformation, 
grain refinement (2 – 5 µ m) and complex dislocation networks contribute 
to higher energy absorption and delayed shear banding. The alloy 
transitions from strain hardening at 20 
◦
C to uniform plasticity at 
300 
◦
C, which offers improved impact resistance for aerospace and 
marine applications under thermomechanical conditions.
