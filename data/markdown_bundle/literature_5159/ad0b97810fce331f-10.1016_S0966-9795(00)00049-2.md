Abstract
E/C128ects of alloying elements on the compression deformation behavior of single crystals of MoSi2 have been investigated in the
temperature range from room temperature to 1500 C. The alloying elements studied include V, Cr, Nb and Al that form a C40
disilicide with Si and W and Re that form a C11 b disilicide with Si. The addition of Al is found to decrease the yield strength of
MoSi2 at all temperatures while the additions of V, Cr and Nb are found to decrease the yield strength at low temperatures (below
800C) and to increase the yield strength at high temperatures (above 1300 C). In contrast, the additions of W and Re (C11 b for-
mers) are found to increase the yield strength at all temperatures. Of the ternary elements investigated, Al and Nb seem to be the
most e/C128ective in improving the low-temperature deformability of MoSi 2 while Re and Nb seem to be the most e/C128ective in
improving the high-temperature strength, judging from the observed yield strength. # 2000 Elsevier Science Ltd. All rights
reserved.

1. Introduction
MoSi2 with the C11 b structure has received a great
deal of attention as a candidate for structural materials
to be used in oxidizing environments at temperatures
higher than the upper limit for Ni-base superalloys
because of its high melting temperature, relatively low
density, good oxidation resistance and high thermal
conductivity [1–3]. However, monolithic MoSi
2 exhibits
only a modest value of fracture toughness at low tem-
peratures and inadequate strength at high temperatures
[1–4]. Thus, many of recent studies on the development
of MoSi
2-based alloys have focused on improving these
poor mechanical properties through forming composites
with ceramics and with other silicides [1,4,5]. These
properties have recently been reported to be signiﬁcantly
improved in composites formed with Si
3N4 and SiC
[6,7]. However, the volume fraction of Si 3N4 and SiC
ceramic reinforcements in these MoSi 2-composites gen-
erally exceeds 50% [6,7]. Further improvements in
mechanical properties of these composites will be
achieved if those of the MoSi 2 matrix phase are
improved. The present study was undertaken to achieve
this by alloying additions to MoSi
2.
Transition-metal atoms that form disilicides with tet-
ragonal C11 b, hexagonal C40 and orthorhombic C54
structures are considered as alloying elements to MoSi 2.
These three structures commonly possess (pseudo-)
hexagonally arranged TMSi
2 layers shown in Fig. 1 and
di/C128er from each other only in the stacking sequence of
these TMSi
2 layers; the C11 b, C40 and C54 structures
are based on the AB, ABC and ADBC stacking of these
layers, respectively. W and Re have been known to form
a C11b disilicide with Si and they are believed to form a
complete C11 b solid-solution with MoSi 2, although
recent studies have indicated that the disilicide formed
with Re is an o/C128-stoichiometric (defective) one for-
mulated to be ReSi
1.75 having a monoclinic crystal
structure [8,9]. The details of our crystal structure
assessment for ReSi
1.75 as well as phase equilibria in the
MoSi2–ReSi1.75 pseudobinary system will be published
elsewhere. Large amounts of alloying additions are
possible for these alloying elements, and high-tempera-
ture strength is expected to be improved through a

V, Cr, Nb and Ta have been known to form a C40

from the C11 b to the C40 structures by substituting it
for Si. Of the ﬁve slip systems identiﬁed to be operative

500C. 1/2<111> dislocations of this slip system are
reported to dissociate into two identical 1/4<111>

ing across the fault is ABC and resembles the stacking
of (0001) in the C40 structure. Hence, the addition of
elements that form a C40 disilicide may cause the energy
di/C128erence between C11

so that the energy of the stacking fault would also be
decreased, although the solid-solubility of these alloying
elements in MoSi

ited to the level of a few atomic% [5]. From this point of
view, we may expect that the deformability of MoSi
2 at
low temperatures increases upon alloying with elements
that form a C40 disilicide. This is consistent with the
theoretical predictions by Waghmare et al. [14] that V
and Nb may enhance the ductility of MoSi
2. Indeed,
Harada et al. [10] have reported that that room-tem-
perature hardness of MoSi 2 polycrystals decreases upon
alloying with Cr, Nb, Ta and Al and similar observa-
tions were made for Al-bearing MoSi
2 polycrystals by
Costa e Silva et al. [16] and Peralta et al. [17] Compres-
sion deformation experiments made so far on ternary
MoSi
2 single crystals containing these elements have
focused attention to the high-temperature deformation
behavior. Umakoshi et al. [18] have found that the yield
stress of MoSi
2 increases upon allying with Cr above
1100C. A similar observation was made by Evans et al.
[19] for Nb-bearing MoSi 2 at 1400C. However, since
these compression experiments were made only at high
temperatures above 1100
C, almost nothing is know
about the low-temperature strength and deformability
of these ternary MoSi
2 single crystals.
In the present study, we have chosen V, Cr, Nb and
Al that form a disilicide with the C40 structure and W
and Re that form a disilicide with the C11 b structure as
alloying elements to MoSi 2, and investigated the defor-
mation behavior of single crystals of MoSi 2 containing
these elements in a wide temperature range from room
temperature to 1500
C. The crystal orientations investi-
gated were the [0 15 1] orientation, in which slip on
{110}<111> is operative, and the [001] orientation, in
which the highest strength is obtained at high tempera-
tures for binary MoSi
2. [3]

3.1. Deformation behavior
The yield stresses obtained for binary and V-, Cr-,
Nb- and Al-bearing ternary MoSi 2 single crystals with
the [0 15 1] orientation are plotted in Fig. 2 as a function
of temperature. The yield stresses plotted in Fig. 2 cor-
respond to the ﬂow stress values at 0.2% strain in
stress–strain curves such as those shown in Fig. 3. As
seen in Fig. 2, plastic deformation is possible even at
room temperature for binary and all ternary single
crystals. The overall yield stress–temperature trend is
the same for all single crystals; the yield stress decreases
with increasing temperature at low and high tempera-
tures, while at intermediate temperatures it increases
anomalously with increasing temperature.
In the low temperature range (below 800
C), as
reported in our previous paper on binary MoSi 2 [3], a
change in the decreasing rate of yield stress occurs
around 400
C. This is related to a change in slip sys-
tems; slip on {013}<331> and {110}<111> occur,
respectively, below and above the transition tempera-
ture (Fig. 4). The temperature for the slip system tran-
sition varies with alloying element. The transition
temperature is 400–500
C for binary, V- and Cr-bearing
ternary MoSi 2 and it is lowered to 300-400 C for Nb-
bearing MoSi 2 and to 200–300C for Al-bearing MoSi 2.
In the low temperature range, the yield stress of all
ternary MoSi 2 is lower than that of binary MoSi 2 for
both slip on {110}<111> and slip on {013}<331>.
This is a clear indication of deformability enhancement
upon alloying MoSi
2 with elements forming a C40 dis-
ilicide. When compared to binary MoSi 2, the extent of
the decrease in yield stress is signiﬁcant for Nb- and Al-
bearing ternary MoSi
2 with the largest decrease in yield
stress being observed for Nb-bearing MoSi 2 for both
slip systems. In this paper, we will restrict ourselves to
the investigation on slip on {110}<111>, and the
results of our investigation for slip on {013}<331> will
be published elsewhere.
In the intermediate temperature range where the yield
stress anomalously increases with temperature, a ser-
rated ﬂow is usually observed to occur in the stress–
strain curves for binary and all ternary MoSi
2 (Fig. 3).
The anomaly has been attributed to the so-called Por-
tevin–Le Chatelier e/C128ect in which interaction between
dislocations and point defects (impurities and/or vacan-
cies) plays a role [3]. The extent of the anomaly as well
as the anomalous temperature range vary with alloying
elements. The anomalous temperature range is 800–
1000
C for binary and Nb-bearing MoSi 2, 700–1100C
for Cr-bearing MoSi 2 and 700–1000C for V- and Al-
bearing MoSi 2, respectively. When compared to the
binary counterpart, the extent of anomaly tends to
increase for the Cr-bearing ternary while it tends to
decrease for the Nb-bearing ternary and any clear cor-
relation cannot be found between the extent of anomaly
and the amplitude of serration in stress–strain curves.
In the high temperature range (above 1300
C), the
yield stress for all ternary MoSi 2 except for the Al-
bearing one is higher than that for the binary counter-
part. Al-bearing MoSi 2 exhibits a signiﬁcantly lower
yield stress than the binary counterpart both at low and
high temperatures. Cr-bearing MoSi
2 exhibits a large
increase in yield stress at high temperatures. However,
the largest increase in yield stress at high temperatures is
observed for Nb-bearing MoSi
2 which shows the lowest
yield stress at low temperatures. The yield stress for Nb-
bearing MoSi 2 is 2–3 times higher than that for binary
MoSi2 at temperatures between 1300 and 1500 C. This
is consistent with the result obtained by Evans et al.
[19]. These indicate that alloying elements forming a
C40 disilicide, in particular, Nb, are e/C128ective both in
decreasing the yield strength at low temperatures and in
increasing the yield stress at high temperatures.

We now discuss the variation of CRSS for slip on
{110}<111> at low temperatures with ternary alloying
elements. Solid-solution softening occurs upon alloying
MoSi
2 with ternary elements that form a C40 disilicide
while solid-solution hardening occurs upon alloying
with ternary elements that form a C11
b disilicide. Solid-
solution softening has been observed to occur in refrac-
tory b.c.c. metals [26] and semiconductors such as Si
[27] and Ge [28]. The occurrence of solid-solution soft-
ening is usually explained in terms of changes in Peierls
potentials and in dislocation core structures upon
alloying or scavenging e/C128ects where solute atoms sca-
venge impurities from the lattice. We will exclude to
discuss scavenging e/C128ects in this discussion, since we did
not change the amount of alloying additions for a par-
ticular alloying element and did not measure the
amount and species of impurities. The CRSS values for
slip on {110}<111> at 500
C (the lowest temperature
at which this slip is observed for binary and all ternary
MoSi
2) and energies of stacking faults on {110}
obtained for binary and ternary MoSi 2 are listed in
Table 1 together with the atomic radius and shear
modulus for each alloying element. As seen in Table 1,
the observed softening and hardening behaviors upon
alloying can be simply correlated with neither the
atomic radius nor the shear modulus of alloying ele-
ments. However, a good correlation can be found
between the CRSS for slip on {110}<111> at 500
C
and the energy of stacking faults on {110}. As the
stacking fault energy is lowered, the CRSS value tends
to decrease. This indicates that the softening and hard-
ening behaviors are associated with changes in disloca-
tion core structure, which may result from electronic
structure changes occurring upon alloying. In fact, while
as in binary MoSi
2, many 1/2<111> dislocations are
observed to be straight along some crystallographic
directions in W- and Re-bearing MoSi
2 in which solid-
solution hardening is observed, those in the other tern-
ary MoSi
2, in which solid-solution softening is
observed, are seen to be rather curved in most cases.
This view is consistent with the theoretical prediction by
Waghmare et al. [14]. They have calculated disemblit-
tlement parameter, D, expressed as the ratio of the sur-
face energy to the unstable stacking fault energy on
{013} by a ﬁrst principle method and found that alloy-
ing elements with the number of valence electrons less
than Mo tend to increase the disembrittlement para-
meter while those with the number of valence electrons
more than Mo tend to decrease it. They concluded that
the former alloying elements improve the deformability
of MoSi
2 while the latter gives rise to the worsening of
deformability. This is consistent with the present results
that the additions of V and Nb, both of which possess
valence electrons less than Mo, soften MoSi
2 while the
addition of Re, which possesses valence electrons more
than Mo, hardens MoSi
2, although V and Nb do not
necessarily behave similarly as predicted by Waghmare
et al. [14]. Of interest to note here is that this tendency is
opposite to what has been observed for Si [27] and Ge
[28]. The upper yield stress of Si [27] and Ge [28] with

7. Conclusions
1. Additions of Al decreases the yield strength of
MoSi
2 at all temperatures. Thus, Al seems to be
e/C128ective in improving the low-temperature deform-
ability of MoSi2.
2. Alloying elements (V, Cr and Nb) that form a C40
disilicide are e/C128ective both in decreasing the yield
strength at low temperatures and in increasing the
yield stress at high temperatures. This e/C128ect occurs
most signiﬁcantly for Nb.
3. A small amount of W addition does not sig-
niﬁcantly a/C128ect the deformation behavior of
MoSi
2. In contrast, a large amount of W addition
is e/C128ective in increasing the yield strength at high
temperatures, although the deformability at low
temperatures declines.
4. A small amount of Re addition signiﬁcantly
increases the yield strength of MoSi
2. However, at
the same time, the low-temperature deformability
drastically declines.
