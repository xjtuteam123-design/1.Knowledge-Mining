ABSTRACT 
In the present work, novel tungsten heavy alloys (WHAs) have been developed using FeNiCoCrCu high entropy 
alloy (HEA) as binder through conventional sintering process. The composition of the alloy is 90 wt% W-10 wt% 
HEA. FeNiCoCrCu HEA was prepared by mechanical milling of elemental Fe, Ni, Co, Cr, and Cu powders in an 
equiatomic ratio for 60 h. XRD analyses revealed that HEA contains predominant FCC phase along with BCC 
minor phase. In order to evaluate the effectiveness of FeNiCoCrCu HEA as a binder of WHA, in this study, W and 
HEA were mixed in the ratio of 9:1, pressed and sintered at temperatures of 1470 and 1500 
◦
C for dwell time of 1 
h 30 min under hydrogen atmosphere. These sintered samples are mentioned as WHEAs (W-High Entropy Al -
loys). The evolution of phases, microstructures, and mechanical properties was investigated as a function of 
sintering temperature vis- ´a-vis those prepared by mixing of W and Fe, Ni, Co, Cr, Cu in the same proportion as in 
HEA (W-EleMental: WEM). The effect of hydrogen treatment (HyT) prior to the sintering of WHEAs on above 
mentioned characteristics was studied. Structural investigation of WEMs and WHEAs showed high intense W- 
peaks. Micrographs of these sintered samples showed typical liquid phase sintered microstructures having three 
distinct phases: bright W-phase, gray matrix phase, and black Cr-oxide phase. With the increase of sintering 
temperature and introduction of HyT step, the volume fraction of solid W-grain has been increased and W- 
content in the matrix of WHEAs is reduced. Upon sintering, WHEAs yield better densification, higher 
compressive strength, and higher bulk hardness. This increase of strength from WEMs to WHEAs was attributed 
to better densification of WHEAs compared to the former. The maximum values of compressive strength (1912 ±
14 MPa) and bulk hardness (479 ± 16 VHN) were achieved in the case of hydrogen treated W-HEA powder 
sintered at 1500

1. Introduction 
Over the past several decades, tungsten heavy alloys (WHAs) have 
been emerged as the material of choice for applications in various 
strategic sectors due to their unique properties, such as high density, 
high strength, high impact strength and corrosion resistance, etc. [ 1 – 3 ]. 
These combination of properties are achieved due to the attainment of 
adequate densification during liquid phase sintering (LPS) of the WHAs 
having compositions of 88 – 98 wt% W and 12-2 wt% of metallic binders. 
Commonly, Ni based binders such as Ni – Fe, Ni – Cu, Ni – Co, Ni – Fe – Co, 
etc. [ 4 – 7 ] are used to consolidate WHAs. Upon LPS, WHAs show a 
composite microstructure in which BCC W-grains are embedded in the 
Ni-based FCC binder systems. The mechanical properties of sintered 
WHAs depend on various factors, such as processing methods, compo -
sition of WHA systems, selection of binder, sintering conditions, grain 
size of W, solubility of W in the matrix, etc. [ 4 , 8 , 9 ]. Elements like Co,

Mo, Re, Ta, etc. can be added to WHAs in order to restrict the growth of 
W-grains during sintering. As a result, one can observe enhancement of 
mechanical properties in those WHAs [ 7 , 9 – 11 ]. 
Recently, High Entropy Alloys (HEAs) have been used as a filler/ 
reinforcement in various metal matrix composites to improve their 
mechanical properties [ 12 – 17 ] as they exhibit unique characteristics 
such as high strength, strength retention at high temperature, excellent 
corrosion resistance, higher wear and oxidation resistance, etc. [ 18 – 22 ]. 
These remarkable properties in HEAs are derived from four core effects, 
namely, the high entropy effect, the sluggish diffusion effect, the lattice 
distortion effect, and the cocktail effect [ 21 , 23 ]. Prakash et al. [ 12 ] 
developed aluminum metal matrix composites by varying weight frac -
tion of AlCoCrCuFe HEA in the range of 0 – 6 wt% and reported higher 
hardness and higher wear resistance upon incorporation of HEA. Chen 
et al. [ 13 ] fabricated AlCoNiCrFe HEA reinforced Cu matrix composites, 
which resulted in a better strengthening effect than metallic glasses. 
Another report on reinforcing HEA particles in Mg matrix showed a 
significant increase in the strength caused by grain refinement [ 16 ]. 
HEA as binder has also been introduced into W to prepare WHAs [ 24 , 
25 ]. Zhou et al. [ 24 ] produced a composite of 85 wt% W and 15 wt% 
AlCrFeNiV HEA through conventional sintering route. This composite 
exhibited a higher value of ultimate tensile strength (1132 MPa) in 
comparison to that of conventional LPSed WHAs. Satyanarayana et al. 
[ 25 ] investigated the microstructure and mechanical properties in 93 
W/7 wt% FeNiCoCrMn HEA composites as a function of different pro -
cessing routes; namely, conventional sintering, microwave sintering, 
and spark plasma sintering (SPS) and revealed higher compressive 
strength (2041 MPa) in SPS processed samples compared to other 
competing processes. 
In the present work, FeNiCoCrCu HEA is used as a novel binder to 
develop WHAs replacing the conventional metallic binders. The sintered 
WHAs with HEA binder is mentioned as WHEA (W-High Entropy Alloy). 
To the best of the authors ’ knowledge, the effects of sintering temper -
ature on the evolution of phase, structure, microstructure, and me -
chanical properties have never been investigated in WHEA. Also, a 
comparison of above-mentioned characteristics between WHEA and 
those of sintered WEM ( W - E le M ental: W and elements of Fe, Ni, Co, Cr, 
and Cu are in the same proportion as in HEA) has never been established. 
Therefore, evolution of phase, structure, microstructure, and mechani -
cal properties as a function of sintering temperature are investigated in 
both WHEA and WEM cases. When WHAs are processed from milled 
powders, hydrogen treatment (HyT) of as-milled powders is generally 
carried out prior to compaction in order to relieve the internal stresses 
[ 26 , 27 ]. However, when WHAs are processed from blended powders, 
HyT of powders (before compaction) is not always followed [ 4 – 6 , 28 ]. 
Since HEA is processed by high energy milling, it was imperative to 
undertake hydrogen treatment of the milled HEA powder. Therefore, 
attempts have been made to investigate the effect of hydrogen treatment 
(prior to compaction) on mechanical properties in WHEAs. 
2. Materials and methods 
To prepare HEA, elemental powders of Fe (Alfa Aesar, particle size 
< 10 μ m, purity 99.9%), Ni (Alfa Aesar, particle size 3 – 7 μ m, purity 
99.9%), Co (Alfa Aesar, particle size 1.6 μ m, purity 99.8%), Cr (Alfa 
Aesar, particle size < 10 μ m, purity 99.9%), and Cu (Alfa Aesar, particle 
size 3.25 – 4.75 μ m, purity 99.9%) were mixed in equiatomic ratio using a 
turbo mixer (Alphie-3, Hexagon, India) for 1 h and mechanically milled 
up to 60 h in a high energy ball mill (Retsch, PM-100, Germany) at 300 
rpm with a ball to powder ratio of 10:1. High-performance stainless steel 
vials and balls were used as the milling media. Toluene was used as a 
process controlling agent (PCA) to avoid metal oxidation. The as- 
processed material was subjected to characterization by X-ray diffrac -
tion to ensure the formation of HEA. The XRD patterns of 40 and 60 h 
milled FeNiCoCrCu clearly revealed the formation of solid solutions in 
the material (presented in section 3.2 ). In one batch, about 30 g of HEA 
powder is prepared. 
For the preparation of WHEA (90 wt% W-10 wt% HEA), HEA was 
mixed with W powder (Electronica Tough Carb Ltd, particle size 3 – 5 μ m, 
purity = 99.9%) in the weight ratio of 1:9 using a turbo mixer for 1 h and 
ball milled in high energy ball mill for the duration of 10 h at 300 rpm 
with a ball to powder ratio of 10:1. Tungsten carbide vials and balls (7 
mm in diameter) were used as the milling media and toluene was used as 
PCA. A batch of mechanically milled W and HEA powder was heat- 
treated in a horizontal tubular furnace (Therelek, India) for 2 h at

3. Results and discussions 
3.1. Morphology and EDS mappings of starting powders 
Fig. 1 (a-c) show the morphology of as-received elemental W, as- 
prepared FeNiCoCrCu HEA, and 10 h mechanically milled W + HEA 
powders. SEM micrograph of W-powder reveals polygonal/poly-faceted 
particles of 3 – 5 μ m size. HEA obtained by 60 h of mechanical milling 
exhibits particles with irregular morphology having a broad range of 
size distribution (0.2 – 6.5 μ m). It can be seen from Fig. 1 (c) that the W +
HEA mixed powder has become flattened and some cold-weld lumps 
have also been formed as a consequence of mechanical milling. 
Elemental maps of C, N, and O in addition to constituent elements of 
HEA, 10 h milled W + EM, and 10 h milled W + HEA powders are shown 
in Fig. 2 . EDS mapping of HEA (see Fig. 2 (a)) shows homogeneous 
elemental distribution of Fe, Ni, Co, Cr, and Cu. The presence of C and O 
indicates that contamination (C and O) has been induced during milling 
process. EDS mapping of 10 h milled W + EM powder ( Fig. 2 (b)) also 
show the presence of C and O, distributed over the microstructure. The 
distinct locations of elements (W, Fe, Ni, Co, Cr, and Cu) are observed 
owing to no formation of alloy during milling. In the case of 10 h milled 
W + HEA powder ( Fig. 2 (c)), the mapping illustrates W-particles along 
with HEA. HEA particles can be seen as single entity constituting Fe, Ni, 
Co, Cr, and Cu. From Fig. 2 , it is noticed that significant amount of C and 
O has been induced during mechanical milling, while N amounts to 
almost null.

4. Conclusions 
In the present investigation, WHEAs (mechanically milled 90 wt%W- 
10 wt% HEA, HEA: FeNiCoCrCu) have been successfully prepared using 
conventional sintering technique at the temperatures of 1470 and 
1500 
◦
C with a dwell time of 1 h 30 min in hydrogen atmosphere. In 
some cases, hydrogen treatment (HyT) step was also introduced and 
sintering was done at two temperatures as mentioned above. In order to 
compare the evolution of phase, structure and microstructure and their 
effect on mechanical properties to those of WHEAs, W and elemental 
powders of Fe, Ni, Co, Cr, Cu were mixed and mechanically milled for 
10 h and were sintered at similar conditions as of WHEAs, mentioned as 
WEMs. The phase, microstructural evolution in sintered samples were 
systematically investigated. The mechanical properties of the sintered 
samples were evaluated using compressive tests and bulk hardness 
measurements. Based on the experimental findings, the following con -
clusions are drawn: 
(I) Mechanical milling of elemental powders in an equiatomic ratio 
for 60 h resulted in FeNiCoCrCu HEA. Structural investigation of 
HEA confirms the presence of two phases, namely, the predomi -
nant FCC phase (F
major
) and a minor BCC phase (B
minor
). 
(II) Upon sintering, WHEAs offer better densification in comparison 
to WEMs. Increase of the sintering temperature from 1470 to 
1500 
◦
C yields an increase in sintered density in both WEM and 
WHEA cases. HyT step prior to the sintering of WHEAs marginally 
improves the sintered density. 
(III) WEMs, WHEAs, and WHEA-HyTs exhibit a typical liquid-phase 
sintered microstructure having three distinct phases: bright W- 
phase, gray matrix phase, and black Cr-oxide phase. The decrease 
of W-content in the matrix with temperature and HyT step is 
explained by accelerated solution re-precipitation phenomenon 
concomitant with microstructural coarsening and increase in 
solid volume fraction.

strength exhibited by the sintered samples follows the order of

WEMs to WHEAs is contributed by better densification upon 
sintering. Introduction of HyT step aids the sintering process and 
increases grain connectivity. A maximum value of fracture 
strength is noticed for the HyT treated WHEA sample, sintered at 
1500 
◦
C (1912 ± 14 MPa). The bulk hardness of sintered samples 
also follows the same order as of yield strength and fracture 
strength. 
The current work describes the merit of introducing HEA as a binder 
into WHA system to enhance mechanical strength. Both sintering tem -
perature and HyT step are playing crucial roles in controlling the 
microstructure, densification, and mechanical properties. Altogether, 
the current work brings a novel area of research in the development of 
WHAs.
