Abstract 
Electro discharge machining (EDM) is an important manufacturing process for hard -to-cut-materials like nickel 
based alloys, e.g. alloy 718. Its high yield strength at high temperature makes it suitable as material for 
manufacturing of pressure vessels for high pressure / high temperature applications, in particular for 
ammonothermal pressure vessels. This report first summarizes the relationship between microstructure and 
properties of alloy 718 for practitioners. Second, it focuses on the metallographic description of alloy 718 
surfaces proceeded by EDM and its behavior during use as equipment for ammonoacidic systems. 
Furthermore, this article includes the finding of suitable post processing, namely honing, to achieve high 
stability of the surface at high pressure atmosphere. 
Keywords: Ammonothermal crystal growth, electro discharge machining, high pressure equipment, high 
temperature corrosion, nickel base alloy 
1 Introduction 
Importance of manufacturing technology in high pressure / high temperature equipment engineering 
The ammonoacidic process as an example for a high pressure and high temperature process in 
corrosive acidic environment with high scientific and commercial importance. It takes place in vessels 
as shown in Figure 1 [1]. Especially when focusing on up-scaling of the ammonothermal process as 
well as serial production of the apparatus, analysis and optimization of the manufacturing process 
allows a more effective production.

The autoclave is positioned in a heater to enable required growth temperatures. Efficient crystal 
growth requires separated growth and dissolution regions inside the autoclave. Therefore, the 
heater is divided into two zones, distinguished by two different temperatures, typical values of the 
temperature gradient are in the range of Δ𝑇 = 20 … 100 K. For dissolution and transport of 
precursor and associated intermediates the reactor is filled with ammonia and ammonium halides 
like NH4I, NH4Cl or NH4F leading to a solution enriched with NH4
+ ions and highly corrosive I-, Cl- or F- 
ions what is called an ammonoacidic environment [2]. Charging the autoclave with ammonia in 
accordance to the required filling rate leads to the desired system pressure of 𝑝𝑚𝑎𝑥 = 300 MPa if 
the vessel is heated to a process temperature of 𝑇𝑚𝑎𝑥 = 600 °C. Process conditions finally result in 
the following requirements for reactors: 
(1) High surface integrity is first of all important to avoid parasitic crystal growth. Second, it is 
essential to guarantee low maintenance of sealing parts. Moreover, high corrosive ammonothermal 
fluid as well as high internal pressure requires high surface quality for long term stability of the 
reactor. 
(2) Since high internal pressure results in high local stresses in the apparatus, minimized heat 
affected zones (HAZ) are preferable, especially at surfaces proceeded with electro discharge 
machining. 
(3) As indicated in other reports corrosion behavior is not only influenced by surface parameters, 
but also by subsurface properties [3–7]. 
(4) For a stable operation, especially of sealing parts, high dimensional accuracy and low 
manufacturing tolerances are required. 
The fabrication of ammonothermal autoclaves requires different fabrication steps, due to their 
complex design and special features like internal screw threads. The EDM-process is hereby used to 
realize filling pipes and thermocouple feedthroughs at the top and bottom of the autoclave as shown 
in Figure 1. As described below, using EDM in this context is of great advantage, especially compared 
to cutting technologies for hard-to-cut materials like precipitation hardened superalloys. Among 
nickel based superalloys alloy 718 (Inconel © alloy 718/alloy 2.4668) offers high strength up to 
T = 650 °C. E. g. a typical value for yield strength at T = 600 °C is approx. 𝜎𝑦 ≈ 980 MPa [8–11]. It is 
also characterized by good intrinsic corrosion resistance due to the formation of a chrome oxide 
passivation layer [12,13]. Consequently, this material shows high suitability as reactor material for 
ammonothermal synthesis. 
Focus of this report 
There are detailed investigations dealing with the material and several manufacturing processes for 
suitable alloys. Studies that relate these aspects to high pressure / high temperature equipment are 
not available by now. This report bridges this gap for the fabrication process electro discharge 
machining.

changes in particle size of ’’-phase [17]. 
3.2 Alloy 718 as a difficult to cut material 
As mentioned above, alloy 718 in its aged state is categorized as a difficult-to-cut material, its poor 
machinability is caused by the following factors [19–21]: 
(1) Microstructure and strengthening mechanisms of the alloy (see section 3.1) are still present 
at cutting temperatures. 
(2) Carbides, located in grain boundaries, show high abrasive effects regarding the cutting 
inserts. 
(3) Alloy 718 as a superalloy shows high tendency to work harden at machined (sub-) surfaces, 
often resulting in notch wear (see Figure 3). 
(4) Low thermal conductivity in combination with high heat generation during the cutting 
process leads to high temperature at the tool tip in combination with high temperature gradients. 
(5) Chemical modification of work piece and tool, as a consequence of high temperature 
diffusion or oxidation. 
(6) Alloy 718 shows high tendency to the formation of built-up-edges (see Figure 3).

As described in Section 1, there is an increased industrial demand of high temperature alloys like 
alloy 718 due to its excellent mechanical and chemical properties, despite its poor machinability. 
Consequently, research focuses on the machinability of superalloys in the aged state, resulting in an 
enormous progress in productions capabilities. Therefore, advanced manufacturing technologies, like 
electro discharge machining, as non-cutting alternatives become more important. 
3.3 Fundamentals of electro discharge machining 
As described above, conventional cutting processes for nickel based alloys are connected to high tool 
wear and low surface quality [22,23]. Especially for complicated shapes, fine structures as well as 
micro and macro hole drilling advanced machining processes (AMP) like electro discharge machining 
replace conventional cutting techniques [24–27]. In principal, mechanical hole drilling is limited to a 
length-to-diameter-ratio (aspect-ratio) of 20 for commonly used nickel based alloys [22,23]. 
Consequently, to fabricate filling pipes with high aspect ratios and other feedthroughs in apparatus 
engineering EDM shows high potential to avoid tool wear and low surface quality of the work piece. 
For the autoclaves, described above (see figure 1), EDM is used to realize feedthroughs with a 
diameter of 𝑑𝑖 = 3.2 mm and a length of 𝑙𝑖 = 60 mm. For the thermocouple feedthrough a 
diameter of 𝑑𝑖 = 1 mm and a length of 𝑙𝑖 = 30 … 60 mm is required. 
EDM uses a periodic high voltage between tool and work piece, where the tool is acting like an 
electrode and the work piece shows opposite polarity. In the presence of a dielectric fluid, periodic 
short duration discharge between tool and work piece leads to the formation of a plasma channel 
vaporizing material of work piece and tool. Proper tool and process design lead to minimized 
ablation of the tool compared to the work piece. During the process, erosive particles are flushed 
away by the dielectric fluid. A detailed description of the process itself as well as influence of 
different parameters is found elsewhere [28,29]. 
Finding optimized process parameters like composition of the dielectric fluid, pulse parameters or 
electrode gap is part of numerous studies. Consequently, this article focuses on the determination of 
(sub-) surface properties under economic EDM-process parameters. Caused by erosive ablation of 
material rough surfaces and high impurity densities are unavoidable. Surface quality is determined by 
the re-solidified material, which was not flushed away by the dielectric fluid and forms the so called 
white or recast layer. Beyond this layer a heat affected zone (HAZ) is formed, both layers together 
are called the altered material zone (AMZ). White layer is characterized by high hardness, residual 
stresses, cohesion and changed chemical properties. Its exact microscopic structure and composition 
depends on process conditions. But in any case significant amounts of tool material in form of 
impurities are found in the recast layer, since there is also a depletion of the electrode. [28] 
To archive high surface quality as well as high dimensional accuracy, four cuts were applied to form 
the hole. Principal cut (so called rough cut) and first re-shaping cut (so called trim cut) were applied 
to receive geometry with high precision. Following re-shaping cuts (second and third) improve 
surface quality [29]. 
A critical issue for EDM machined surfaces at high pressure components is the high tendency to 
micro-cracking. Drastic heating and subsequent cooling rates in combination with inhomogeneous 
temperature distribution leads to high surface stresses, if they locally exceed ultimate tensile 
strength, crack formation is introduced [28,29]. Crack density and propagation depend on process 
parameters as well as material composition. For alloy 718 as a material with high ductility low 
tendency to crack formation is expected. But no data describing crack parameters for alloy 718 are 
available.

4 Results and discussion 
4.1 (Sub-)surface microstructure of electro discharge machined 
To proof the suitability of EDM for the nickel based alloy 718, EDM-machined samples of this alloy 
(aged state, microstructure described in section 2) were prepared. The possible post-processing 
composition of recast layer, crack propagation and stresses in the HAZ after EDM were investigated 
metallographically to determine the properties of AMZ. 
Material samples with EDM holes were prepared the same way as feedthroughs in the high pressure 
vessels. That means, sinking EDM (HS300E; Maho Hansen) was used for making the base hole. Shape 
making was carried out with wire EDM (AQ537L; Sodick). Process parameters were adjusted to 
commercial conditions, to economic factors and to realize a homogenous surface and low thickness 
of the AMZ. This includes a feed rate of 𝑓 = 0.2 
mm
min, the choice of water as dielectric fluid and an 
electrode made of brass with a diameter of 𝑑𝑒𝑙𝑒𝑐. = 0.25 mm. Principle cut and third re-shaping cut 
were investigated. Figure 8 shows microscope images and scanning electron images of etched 
samples. For a proper illustration of the properties of the recast layer optical and scanning electron 
microscopy was applied. In this way, further changes in the underlying HAZ zone are represented 
detailed. 
Depending on shaping step different structure of the recast layer was found. Optical microscopy 
images lead to a distinctive representation of the recast layer. By statistical reasons three cross 
sections of three different samples were estimated. Selection of images in Figure 4 was motivated by 
showing significant differences between principle and re-shaping cut. Temperature of removed 
material is in a range of approx. T ≈ 20 000 °C [28]. To estimate thermal input in the AMZ, reference 
samples with different temperature load were prepared, as shown in Figure 5. Comparing Figure 4 
and 5, delivers that temperatures higher than T = 1000 °C occur in the HAZ. 
After the third trim step a more uniform surface structure compared to the rough cut appeared. 
Recently, Li et al. focused on wire-EDM to increase surface integrity and obtained similar results 
regarding subsurface properties [29]. Due to the inhomogeneous appearance of both layers, only 
maximum values were defined in the plot of Figure 4. Minimum thickness of the recast layer in both 
cases is 𝑡𝑟𝑒𝑐𝑎𝑠𝑡 ≈ 1 µm. To analyze the HAZ, SEM pictures show a higher contrast and sensitivity. As a 
first result, the thickness of the HAZ also shows strong local variation. This periodic behavior leads to 
completely vanishing occurrence at minima. Moreover, the HAZ is more extended after third trim 
cut. Furthermore, waveform-like deformations in the HAZ became observable, indicating residual 
stresses. Micro indentation measurements in Figure 6 show that hardness is increased in the first 
d ≈ 100 µm below the surface. In contrast, after principle cut a smaller HAZ was found and reduced 
hardness in similar subsurface regions appeared. 
Elemental composition of the recast layer was investigated by Energy Dispersive X-Ray spectroscopy. 
Due to the complex microstructure and composition and the low efficiency of EDX spectroscopy for 
low-Z elements, only semi-quantitative results are achieved. As top view SEM images in Figure 7 
indicate, that surfaces are characterized by two categories. First, parts of the surface are only 
covered with thin recast layer. The second category is characterized by deposited particles of 
different size. The elemental amount of copper and zinc in those particles is higher than on the 
surface layer [26]. However, the particles are depleted in Ni, Cr and Fe content. Compared to the 
original composition of the alloy, additional impurities like carbon or oxygen are found in both areas, 
having their origin in the dielectric fluid. However, Li et al. achieved different surface integrity and 
did not distinguished between particles and surface layer [29]. 
Recast layer is characterized by low ductility and high tensile stresses. In combination with high 
heating and cooling rates during the EDM process, micro crack formation is favored. Ekmekci found

4.2 Corrosion of EDM proceeded surfaces 
This section describes physical and chemical stability of AMZ of EDM-processed surfaces under 
ammonothermal conditions. 
The corrosion tests were carried out in a high pressure vessel also made of alloy 718. Ammonium 
chloride (Sigma-Aldrich; 99.998% based on trace metal) was added to the autoclave to generate the 
corrosive acidic environment. The samples were positioned in the autoclave using a custom made 
rack. After closing the autoclave, it was flanged to the pilot plant. This allows evacuating and purging 
with nitrogen to remove oxygen. After repeating this procedure for three times, the autoclave is 
charged with liquid ammonia (Linde; 99.999 %) to reach the desired filling rate. Heating up the 
complete setup to process temperature of T ≈ 600°C leads to a system pressure of p ≈ 120 MPa. With 
a concentration of 𝑐𝑚𝑜𝑙(𝑁𝐻4𝐶𝑙) ≈ 6 % process parameters typical for ammonoacidic crystal growth 
experiments were achieved [30,31]. To evaluate the effect of surface properties on the corrosion 
behavior an exposure time of one day was chosen. The samples were removed from the autoclave 
after the corrosion test and cleaned in an ultrasonic cleaner to remove residuals of ammonia 
compounds. 
Figure 8 shows that the corrosive interaction of the supercritical fluid with the EDM surface leads to 
significant changes in its morphology. At low magnifications a more rugged surface appears, 
compared to Figure 7 surface particles are smaller. Working with high magnifications (see Figure 9) 
also shows changes in the surface on a smaller scale, resulting in pores and more frequently 
appearing cracks. This means that particles and surface layer are partly dissolved in the fluid. 
Analyzing the composition of the layers shows significant depletion in chrome, copper and tin. 
Moreover, oxygen and carbon residuals from the dielectric fluid were not detectable. However, after 
the corrosion test, residuals from the supercritical fluid, namely chlorine, were found in the recast 
layer. Corrosive modifications in sub-surface layers were not observed in cross sections. 
4.3 Suitable post-processing methods 
As mentioned above, despite its advantages in shaping complex geometries, following drawbacks are 
found: 
 High concentration of impurities (e. g. copper and tin) in white layer. 
 Poor mechanical properties of the HAZ. 
 Micro cracks in white layer. 
In this way post processing for optimized application in high pressure equipment is required. To 
enhance surface quality of holes honing is a promising technique, as shown in Figure 10. For the 
process investigated in this study an overall-infeed of a = 150 µm is sufficient to remove critical areas 
of AMZ. 
As we presented in a previous report, for a further enhancement of corrosion resistance of the high 
pressure equipment, a galvanic noble metal coating is promising technique [32]. As pointed out in

allow higher layer adhesion [33]. 
Moreover, ammonothermal crystal growth is a high purity process, because impurities limit the 
performance of the grown crystals drastically. Therefore, high concentration in the white layer will 
have negative impact on the crystals grown in the reactor. 
5 Conclusion 
Summing up, EDM is a powerful tool for machining of holes in alloy 718, although we did not focus 
on a complete description of all influencing factors, a post processing is necessary due to the 
formation of the white layer. First, the large amounts of copper, tin, oxygen and carbon in the recast 
layer are undesirable for high purity crystal growth. Second, cracks within the white layer could lead 
to fatigue, especially in combination with high internal pressures and residual stresses in subsurface 
area. Third, the recast layer shows low corrosion resistance compared with the unmachined alloy, 
what finally results in an additional impurity source for high purity crystal growth. A post processing 
in form of honing with a minimal infeed of a = 150 µm is proposed.
