ABSTRACT
Cu-0.47Cr-0.16 Nb (wt%) alloys were designed and prepared, and the microstructures of the Cu-Cr-Nb alloy
were investigated using transmission electron microscopy and three-dimension atom probe tomography tech-
nique. After homogenizing at 950 °C for 4 h, cold rolling by 80% reduction, then aging at 450 °C for 30 min, the
micro-hardness, electrical conductivity, tensile strength, yield strength and elongation of the alloy were up to
150 HV, 89.1%IACS, 453 MPa, 443 MPa and 11.4%, respectively. The tensile strength / elongation of the alloy
approached 399 MPa / 22.9% as test at 200 ℃, 334 MPa / 14.8% for 300 ℃, and 282 MPa / 12.3% for 400 ℃,
respectively. The Cr
2Nb phase with an average size of 700 nm and Nb phase with an average size of 500 nm
formed during the solidi ﬁcation process, while the nano-scale Cr-rich phases precipitated from the solid solution
during the aging process. The sizes of Cr 2Nb and Cr-rich phases were close to each other during aging. These
precipitates located in the grain and sub-grain boundary could eﬀ ectively pin the movement of boundary, re-
sulting in a high strength of the alloy at the elevated temperature. The addition of Nb can promote the pre-
cipitation of Cr from the solid solution during aging, thus both strength and electrical conductivity of the alloy
were improved.
1. Introduction
Copper alloys with high strength and high electrical conductivity
(HSHC) have been widely used as the key material in many high-tech
ﬁelds, such as integrated circuit lead frame, high speed railway contact
network cable, heat sink materials for nuclear fusion, and so on [1– 4].
There are mainly two types of HSHC copper alloy according to the
strengthening mechanism: (1) solid solution strengthening copper al-
loys, such as Cu-Sn [4], Cu-Ag [5], Cu-Mg [6,7] alloys, and so on; and
(2) precipitation strengthening copper alloys, such as Cu-Ni-Si [8,9],
Cu-Fe-P [10],and Cu-Cr [11– 13] alloys. The properties of these typical
HSHC copper alloys are listed in Table 1. It is very di ﬃcult to achieve
high strength and high electrical conductivity simultaneously for the
solid solution strengthening copper alloy due to the inﬂ uence of solid
solution atoms in the matrix. The precipitation strengthening copper
alloy often has better mechanical properties than the solid solution
strengthening copper alloy due to the contribution of orowan
strengthening mechanism [8– 13]. Besides, the solid-soluble atoms in
the precipitation strengthening copper alloy can be precipitated from
the matrix during aging, leading to the increase of strength and elec-
trical conductivity of the alloy [8– 13].
Cu-Cr alloy is a typical precipitation strengthening copper alloy, and
it exhibits good comprehensive properties due to the precipitation of Cr
particles during aging. However, the binary Cu-Cr alloy exhibits poor
softening resistance at elevated temperature due to the rapid coarsening
of precipitates [11– 13]. Many researchers have put e ﬀorts to develop
new Cu-Cr alloys with excellent comprehensive properties by: (1)
adding alloying element to improve the resistance to softening at ele-
vated temperature (such as Cu-Cr-Zr, Cu-Cr-Mg and Cu-Cr-Be alloys),
and (2) introducing advanced processing and aging technology to fur-
ther increase the strength, electrical conductivity of the alloys (such as
severe plastic deformation, multi-step aging, and so on). The former one
determines the basic performance of the alloy, while the latter one will
improve the comprehensive properties of the alloy. The element Zr is
usually added into the Cu-Cr alloy to improve the softening resistance
property of Cu-Cr alloy, and the Cu-Cr-Zr alloy has a softening

soluble in solid copper. Cr 2Nb has the C14 or MgZn 2 structure above
1588 °C, and FCC C15 or MgCu 2 structure in the lower temperature
[21]. Normally, the ratio of Cr and Nb can be selected as 2:1 in order to
form Cr 2Nb, and Cr 2Nb hardening phase can re ﬁne the grain size of
copper and further enhance the strength of copper alloy [22]. If the
ratio of Cr to Nb is higher than 2:1, a multi-modal precipitate dis-
tribution of Cr
2Nb precipitates and nano-scale Cr precipitates in the
matrix would play an important role in strengthening.
In this paper, Cu-0.47Cr-0.16 Nb alloy with a high Cr:Nb atomic
ratio was designed to improve the comprehensive properties of the
alloy further, and the microstructure and the properties of the alloy
during heat treatments were studied systematically. The purpose of this
study is to investigate the mechanical properties of the Cu-Cr alloy with
the addition of Nb after di ﬀerent thermo-mechanical treatments at in-
termediate temperature, and this could be expected to guide the design
of Cu-Cr based alloy with good mechanical properties at elevated
temperature.
2. Materials and methods
There is a dramatic di ﬀerence in the melting temperature of Nb
(2469 ℃) and Cu (1084 ℃). In order to gain the homogeneous alloy
melt, Cr-64 wt%Nb master alloy with the melting point of about 1650 ℃
was used. Cu-0.47Cr-0.16 Nb (wt%) alloy was melted in a vacuum in-
duction furnace with the raw materials of electrolytic Cu, pure Cr and
Nb-Cr master alloy. After removing the surface defects, the ingot was
solution-treated at 950 ℃ or 4 h in an Ar atmosphere and then quickly
quenched into water. The solution-treated alloy was then cold rolled at
room temperature with a total reduction of 80% in thickness. Two
diﬀerent treatments were applied on the cold-rolled alloy, which are
listed in Table 2. Samples for optical microscopy observation were
prepared by mechanical grinding and polishing.
To test the hardness and electrical conductivity of the Cu-Cr-Nb
alloy treated after the two processes, the samples were cut by wire
cutting machine with dimensions of 3 cm
×cm × 5 cm. The room
temperature Vickers hardness was measured by a HV-5 micro-Vickers
hardness tester with a load of 2 kg and dwelling time of 15 s. Five
measurements were taken at the di ﬀerent positions on the sample, and
then the average was taken. The electrical conductivity was measured
by a double arms electrical bridge device with a QJ36S DC resistance
tester with at room temperature, and four values were measured for
average. Tensile tests were performed on MTS 8810 mechanical testing
machine with a constant strain rate of 1 mm/min at room temperature,
200 ℃, 300 ℃ and 400 ℃, respectively. Microstructure of the samples
solution-treated at 950 ℃ for 4 h was observed by Quanta 250 ﬁeld
emission scan electron microscopy, and X-ray di ﬀraction (XRD) test was
conducted on Dmax-2500 di ﬀractometer (Rigaku Corporation) with Cu-
Ka radiation, tube voltage of 40 kv, current 250 mA and continuous
scanning mode at the rate of 1°/min. Three-dimension atom probe to-
mography (3DAP), transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and high-
resolution electron microscopy (HREM) were used to characterize the
microstructure of the alloy aged at 450 ℃ for 2.5 min, 30 min and 4 h,
respectively. The 3DAP observation was conducted on LEAP 4000 h
electron spectrometer (CAMECA Co., Gennevilliers, France) applying a
laser pulse repetition rate of 200 kHz, a pulse fraction of 15%, a sample
rate of 0.5% and a temperature of 20 K. The samples for 3DAP ob-
servations had dimensions of 0.5 × 0.5 × 15 mm

by the standard two-step electro-polishing procedure. Transmission
electron microscope observation was performed on Titan G2 60 – 300
spherical aberration correction ﬁeld emission microscope (FEI

diameter of 3 mm and were ﬁrst ground mechanically to a thickness of
around 70 µm and then electro-polished by a Struers Tenpol-5 twin-jet

hardness at a certain temperature, the softening temperature is de-

good softening resistance at elevated temperature.

4. Discussions
There are two typical kinds of aging precipitation sequence of the
Cu-Cr alloys: (1) supersaturated solid solution → nanoscale Cr-rich
cluster → Cr G.P zone coherent → bcc Cr phase → incoherent bcc Cr
phase [25], and (2) supersaturated solid solution → Cr G.P zones → fcc
Cr phase → order fcc Cr phase → bcc Cr phase [26]. The structures of
precipitated Cr phase in the designed Cu-Cr-Nb are the same as those of
other Cu-Cr alloys reported in Ref [24,26,27], which has NW-OR and
KS-OR with the matrix ( Fig. 6 ). With longer aging time, Cr phase with
the KS-OR could grow at the expense of that with the NW-OR [24].
Three types of precipitates have been identi ﬁed in the Cu-Cr-Nb
alloy: the Cr 2Nb Laves phase locates in the grain boundary, Nb-rich
phase precipitates in the matrix, and nanoscale Cr that forms in the
matrix. The high strength of the designed Cu-Cr-Nb alloy are mainly
attributed to the precipitation strengthening ( Fig. 5d and Fig. 6d), sub-
structure strengthening ( Fig. 6a) and dislocation strengthening ( Fig. 4).
The good mechanical and electrical properties of copper alloy, such
as Cu-Cr-Zr, Cu-Al 2O3, Cu-Ni-Be and Cu-Ni-Si alloys, are mainly at-
tributed to a small volume fraction of nanoscale diameter precipitates
or dispersoids that are uniformly dispersing in the matrix [8,32– 34].
These precipitates can be produced by solution annealing and aging, or
powder metallurgy approaches. The precipitation of solid solution
elements increases the electrical conductivity and the nano-scale pre-
cipitate plays an important role of strengthening, which can be ex-
pressed by Orowan-Ashby strengthening mechanism [35,36]:

Cu-Cr alloys have been found to su ﬀer from elevated temperature
embrittlement in the temperature range of 400 – 800 K, which is caused
by the segregation of surface absorbed sulfur element at grain bound-
aries [16– 18]. Sulfur can resolve into the Nb, forming various com-
pounds [19]. Adding Nb into Cu-Cr alloys can eliminate residual sulfur
and improve the ductility of Cu-Cr alloys. Therefore, the segregation of
sulfur at the grain boundary can be removed, and the hot ductility of
Cu-Cr-based alloy can be improved.
5. Conclusion
(1) The designed Cu – 0.47Cr– 0.16 Nb shows excellent comprehensive
properties. After homogenized at 950 °C for 4 h, cold rolled by 80%
reduction, and then aged at 450 °C for 30 min, the micro-hardness,
electrical conductivity, tensile strength, yield strength and elonga-
tion of designed alloy are up to 150 HV, 89.1%IACS, 453 MPa,
443 MPa, 11.4%, respectively. After second cold rolled by 80%
reduction, the tensile strength and yield strength increase to
549 MPa and 492 MPa, respectively.
(2) The 3DAP results show that the element Nb in aged samples dis-
tributes on the surface and inside the Cr phase, which promotes the
precipitation of Cr phase and hinder its coarsen during aging.
(3) The Nb-rich phase and Cr
2Nb phase can not coarsen during the
aging process, e ﬀectively pinning sub-grain boundary. This makes
the Cu-Cr-Nb alloy di ﬃcult to recrystallize during the aging process
and improves its thermal stability. The addition of Nb into Cu-Cr
alloy also improves the elevated temperature ductility of the alloy.
