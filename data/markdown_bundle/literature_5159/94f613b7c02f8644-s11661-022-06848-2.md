ORIGINAL RESEARCH ARTICLE
Mechanistic Modeling of Cyclic Softening in Ni-Based
Superalloys at Elevated Temperatures
KWAI S. CHAN
The cyclic stress–strain response of Ni-based superalloys at elevated temperatures in the
literature has been analyzed and modeled mechanistically to develop a better understanding of
cyclic softening behavior as a function of temperatures. The analysis reveals that at
temperatures below 973 K, cyclic softening in Ni-based superalloys is caused predominantly
by shearing of c¢ precipitates. The strain hardening exponent (n
1) is positive at lower fatigue
cycles, but the strain-hardening exponent (n 2) becomes negative (i.e., strain softening) at higher
fatigue cycles. The onset of cyclic softening appears to correspond to shearing of c¢ precipitates
into two halves. In contrast, cyclic softening at temperatures above 1173 K is partly caused by
thermal recovery of anomalous hardening induced by cross slip of super-dislocations from {111}
planes to {001} planes in the presence of climb-assisted bowing of dislocations around c¢
precipitates, and partly caused by a reduction of forest dislocation density at the c/c¢ interface
due to impinging super-dislocations. As the result of these mechanism changes, the onset of
cyclic softening commences after the ﬁrst fatigue cycle but continues at a slow rate at a
temperature at 1173 K or above. Relations of the strain-hardening exponents on microstruc-
tural variables have been developed and validated against experimental data from the literature.

I. INTRODUCTION
IT is well-established that Ni 3Al compounds [1–4] and
Ni-based superalloys [5,6] exhibit anomalous hardening
that results in an increase in yield and tensile strengths
with increasing temperatures in the range of intermedi-
ate and elevated temperatures as high as about 1033 K.
The origin of anomalous hardening is the occurrence of
cross slip from {111} planes to {001} planes to form
incomplete or complete Kear-Wilsdorf (K-W) locks
[7,8]
that hinder further glides of superdislocations on the
{111} planes. Despite anomalous hardening, Ni-based
superalloys are also known to be susceptible to localized
{111} slip due to shearing of c¢ precipitates, and fracture
along slip bands during monotonic and/or cyclic
loading.
[9]
Several hardening mechanisms are pertinent in
Ni-based superalloys with either a supersolvus or a
subsolvus microstructure.
[10] Besides anomalous hard-
ening, other hardening mechanisms include grain size
hardening of c and c¢,[10] solid solution hardening of c
and c¢,[10] shearing of c¢ by weak-pair coupling [11]
shearing of c¢ by strong-pair coupling, [11] and disloca-
tion bowing around c¢ precipitates.[12,13] These harden-
ing mechanisms have been treated and incorporated in
several physics-based constitutive models [10,14–16] for
predicting the yield strengths of Ni-based superalloys
as functions of temperature and/or microstructure. The
material parameters in these models include the anti-
phase boundary (APB) energy ( c
APB), alloy composi-
tions, the c grain size, as well as the volume fraction and
the size of the primary, secondary, and tertiary c¢
precipitates.[10,14–16] The eﬀects of multimodal precipi-
tate size distributions on the yield strength of Ni-based
superalloys have been modeled by Galindo-Nava
et al.
[17] Anomalous hardening is treated in these models
in terms of an empirical relation of yield strength of
Ni
3Al as a function of temperature. [10,16]
It is a common practice to represent the stress–strain
curves of Ni-based superalloys in terms of the Ram-
berg–Osgood (RO) equation
[18] in which the total strain
(et) is taken to be the sum of the elastic strain ( ee) and the
plastic strain ( ep). The elastic strain is governed by
Hooke¢s law and the plastic strain is related to the ﬂow
stress according to a power-law. The onset of macro-
scopic yielding is not taken at zero plastic strain but is
typically deﬁned as the ﬂow stress at a particular level of
plastic strain oﬀset, e.g., at 0.2 pct plastic strain oﬀset.

superalloys subjected to monotonic loading has revealed

predicted dependence of n 2 on c¢ size has been validated

atures remains to be established.
The objective of this article is to present results of an
investigation focused on mechanistic modeling of cyclic
softening of Ni-based superalloys subjected to fatigue
loading at elevated temperatures. This work extends
previous eﬀorts on modeling cyclic softening mecha-
nisms at room temperature to treat pertinent cyclic
softening mechanisms at elevated temperatures. In
Section A, the cyclic softening behaviors of Ni-based
superalloys at elevated temperatures are highlighted to
identify the occurrence of two distinctly diﬀerent cyclic
softening behaviors: (1) the onset of softening after
many fatigue cycles, and (2) the onset of cyclic softening
after the ﬁrst fatigue cycle. The characteristics and
implications of these two types of cyclic softening
behaviors are investigated in Section B, which shows
the importance of anomalous hardening, climb-assisted
dislocation bowing around c¢ precipitates, and interac-
tions of strong-pair coupled super-dislocations with
forest dislocations located at or near the c/c¢ interface.
Mechanistic modeling of the strain hardening exponents
associated with anomalous hardening in the presence of
dislocation climb and thermal recovery are presented in
Section C and the cyclic softening mechanisms at
elevated temperatures are correlated with relevant
microstructural variables. Section II presents a discus-
sion of the important ﬁndings followed by Conclusions.

B. Onset of Cyclic Softening
A detailed review of the cyclic hardening and soften-
ing data for fatigue of Ni-based superalloys at elevated
temperatures shown in Figures 1 to Figure 6 reveals two
important observations related to the cyclic softening
processes: (1) the onset of cyclic softening occurs after a
number of fatigue cycles in superalloys fatigued within a
temperature range from room temperature to a temper-
ature as high as 1173 K, and (2) the onset of cyclic
softening occurs after the ﬁrst fatigue cycle in superal-
loys fatigued at a temperature that is typically above
1173 K, but can be as low as 973 K. A mixture of the
two cyclic softening behaviors can occur from 973 to
1173 K, depending on individual alloys and strain
amplitudes. The multiple-cycle onset was reported
earlier in superalloys tested at ambient temperature
[36–38]
and it was attributed to shearing of c¢ precipitates into
two halves by to-and-fro dislocations and cyclic slip
during fatigue.
[21,36–38] The c¢ shearing process involves
the propagation of superkinks on the {111} planes of c¢
precipitates. In particular, the shearing process involves
weak-pair coupling in tertiary c¢ precipitates, while it
involves strong-pair coupling in secondary c¢ precipi-
tates that are larger than 30 nm in size. Within the
anomalous hardening region (400 K to 973 K), super-
kink propagation on {111} is often accompanied with
cross slip from the {111} planes to {001} planes, leading
to the formation of immobile complete K-W locks
[7] and
partially mobile incomplete K-W locks. [8] The number
of fatigue cycles required for the onset of cyclic

II. DISCUSSION
One of the signiﬁcant ﬁndings of this study is the
identiﬁcation of two distinctly diﬀerent cyclic softening
mechanisms in Ni-based superalloys fatigued at elevated
temperatures: (1) shearing of c¢ precipitates into two
halves by strong-pair coupled {111} super-dislocations
that result in the onset cyclic softening after multiple
fatigue cycles, and (2) the onset of cyclic softening after
the ﬁrst fatigue cycle by a reduction of forest disloca-
tions due to interactions of strong-pair coupled
super-dislocations with the c/c¢ interface. With regard
of the ﬁrst mechanism, cyclic softening after multiples
fatigue cycles is a characteristic of shearing of c¢
precipitates
[36–38] and it is the same cyclic softening
mechanism observed in Ni-based superalloys fatigued at
ambient temperature.
[21,36–38] Thus, it becomes apparent
that shearing of c¢ precipitates is the predominant cyclic
softening mechanism from ambient temperature to
elevated temperatures as high as 973 K. This ﬁnding is
supported by experimental observation of c¢ shearing
and the formation of localized slip bands in IN 713 LC
from ambient temperature to temperatures as high as
1073 K,
[46] as well as localized slipband formation in the
very high-cycle fatigue (VHCF) regime. [47] In contrast,
cyclic softening after the ﬁrst cycle appears to occur
primarily at temperatures above 1173 K with some
overlaps between 973 and 1173 K. The transition from
the ﬁrst mechanism (softening after multiples cycle) to
the second mechanism (softening after the ﬁrst cycle)
appears to be controlled mainly by temperature with
little or only minor overlaps. Recently, Yang et al.
[48]
performed three-dimensional (3D) discrete dislocation
dynamics (DDD) modeling of c¢ cutting by strong-pair
coupled super-dislocations. They showed that a transi-
tion from an APB fault to a superlattice intrinsic
stacking fault (SISF) can result in a lowering of the
strain-hardening response. At presence, it is unclear
such a transition from APB fault to SISF can lead to
strain softening under monotonic or cyclic loading. In
discussion of the observation of cyclic softening in MD2
SC at 1098 K, Zhang et al.
[30] suggested several potential
cyclic softening mechanisms including: (1) dissolution of
c¢ particles, (2) shearing of c¢ precipitates, (3) develop-
ment of dislocation networks at the c/c¢ interface, and
(4) dislocation annihilation by fatigue and thermal
recovery. Dissolution of c¢ in MD2 was manifested as
rounding of cuboidal c¢ corners,[30] which may be
preceded by shearing oﬀ of the corners by cyclic slip.
Except for the dissolution of c¢ particles, the last three
softening mechanisms are in agreement or consistent
with those identiﬁed in this investigation based on
theoretical considerations.
Shearing of c¢ by strong-pair coupled super-disloca-
tions is particularly important for Ni-based superal-
loys with large c¢ sizes. The presence of anomalous
hardening due to incomplete and complete K-W
locks
[7,8] results in a substantial increase of the c¢
cutting stress over that for strong-pair coupling alone
by a factor of > 4X at 973 K (Figure 13a) and by a
factor of > 2X at 1173 K (Figure 13b). These large
increases in the c¢ cutting stresses, however, do not
prevent or hinder shearing of c¢ from occurrence. On
the other hand, dislocation climb promotes dislocation
bowing around c¢ by reducing the bowing stresses. As
shown in Figure 13, the critical dislocation bowing
stresses are lower than those for shearing of c¢ at
973 K and 1173 K for c¢ sizes greater than 500 nm. In
comparison, dislocation bowing does not occur for c¢
sizes less than 600 nm in the absence of dislocation
climb, as shown in Figure 10. Climb-assisted disloca-
tion bowing of c¢ has been observed by Li et al. [30] in
DD6 SC fatigued at elevated temperatures. Validation
of the dependence of the cyclic softening exponent n 2
on c¢ size is presented in Figure 9, which shows that
the value of n 2 increases with increasing c¢ sizes. In
addition, the n 2 value changes from negative to
positive when the c¢ size exceeds about 560 nm. In
addition to dislocation climb, cross slip on cube planes
may also promote dislocation bowing around c¢
precipitates, which has been modeled by Tinga
et al.
[49]

III. CONCLUSIONS
The conclusions reached as the results of this inves-
tigation are as follows:
1. Two cyclic softening mechanisms are present in
Ni-based superalloys fatigue at elevated tempera-
tures. The first softening mechanism, which occurs
after multiple fatigue cycles, is the result of shearing
of c¢ precipitates into two halves by strong pair
coupled super-dislocations. The second softening
mechanism, which happens after the first cycle, is
likely caused by interaction of strong pair coupled
super-dislocations with the c/c¢ interface.
2. Cyclic softening by shearing of c¢ precipitates after
multiple cycles is the dominant mechanism from
ambient temperature to elevated temperatures as
high as 1173 K, while cyclic softening after the first
cycle occurs at temperatures above 973 K (973 K to
1250 K). A combination of these two cyclic soften-
ing behaviors can occur at temperatures between
973 to 1173 K, depending on individual alloys and
strain amplitudes.
3. Cyclic softening after the first cycle occurs at low
density of superkinks and is likely caused by a
reduction of forest dislocation density due to
interaction of strong-pair coupled super-disloca-
tions with the c/c¢ interface.
4. Anomalous hardening increases the critical stresses
for shearing ofc¢ precipitates at elevated temperatures,
which, in turn, promotes the transition of c¢ shearing
to dislocation bowing around c¢ precipitates.
5. Cyclic hardening, which is characterized by a
positive n
1 value, is enhanced by increasing volume
fractions of primary c¢ (f1) and secondary c¢ (f2).
6. Cyclic softening, which is characterized by a nega-
tive n 2 values, is decreased with increasing c¢ size
and may be suppressed to zero when the c¢ size
exceeds about 500 to 560 nm, at which point
shearing of c¢ is replaced by dislocation bowing of
c¢. The value of n
2 is directly related to the value of
n1 prior to the onset of cyclic softening.
7. Dislocation climb enhances dislocation bowing
around c¢ precipitates and the transition of c¢
shearing to bypass of c¢ precipitates by strong pair
coupled super-dislocations.
8. Mechanistic models of n 1 and n2 developed based on
shearing of c¢ have been validated for selected
Ni-based superalloys from ambient temperature to
elevated temperatures as high as 973 K. Above
973 K, interaction of super-dislocations with the
forest dislocation at the c/c¢ interface induces another
cyclic softening mechanism which n
2 value is rela-
tively independent of temperature above 973 K.
