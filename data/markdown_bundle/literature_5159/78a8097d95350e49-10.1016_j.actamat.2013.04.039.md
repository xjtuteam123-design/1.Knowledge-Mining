Abstract
Systematic characterization of the shape memory properties of a quaternary Ni 45.3–Ti29.7–Hf20–Pd5 (at.%) polycrystalline alloy was
performed in compression after selected aging treatments. Precipitation characteristics were revealed by transmission electron micros-
copy. The eﬀects of aging temperature and time on transformation temperatures, recoverable and residual strains, and temperature
and stress hystereses were determined by diﬀerential scanning calorimetry, constant-load thermal cycling experiments and isothermal
strain cycling (superelasticity) tests. The crystal structure and lattice parameters of the transforming phases were determined from
X-ray diﬀraction analysis. It was revealed that precipitation hardening signiﬁcantly improved the shape memory properties of the
NiTiHfPd alloy. Under optimum aging conditions, shape memory strains of up to 4% under 1 GPa were possible, and superelasticity
experiments resulted in full strain recovery without any plastic deformation, even at stress levels as high as 2 GPa. The NiTiHfPd
polycrystalline alloy exhibited very high damping capacity/absorbed energy (30–34 J cm
/C0 3) and work output (30–35 J cm /C0 3), which were
attributed to the ability to operate at high stress levels without signiﬁcant plastic deformation and to a high mechanical hysteresis
(>900 MPa) at temperatures ranging from 20 /C176Ct o8 0 /C176C.

1. Introduction
Shape memory alloys (SMA) have the unique ability to
recover large deformations upon heating or by removal of
load owing to their capability to undergo thermoelastic
martensitic transformations [1]. Because of this behavior,
there are countless potential and many actual applications
for SMA, including use as actuators, sealing elements, cou-
plers, stents, valves and surgical appliances in the biomed-
ical, aerospace, oil–gas, automotive, robotics and
telecommunication industries [2]. NiTi is the most com-
monly used SMA, since it has good physical and functional
properties, such as high ductility, low density, high corro-
sion resistance and good fatigue life [1,3]. However, it is
also known that transformation temperatures (TT) of NiTi
alloys are usually <100 /C176C, and they tend to exhibit low
strength and poor cyclic stability [4]. Consequently,
changes in stoichiometry leading to precipitation strength-
ening, alloying and thermomechanical treatments have
been used as the primary methods to tailor the shape mem-
ory behavior of NiTi [1,5–7].
Ni-rich NiTi alloys have been studied widely, since
their strength can be increased by the formation of ﬁne
Ni-rich precipitates through aging [8,9]. Even without the
precipitation hardening, NiTi alloys with high Ni content

is not strong enough to observe perfect superelasticity in

and perfect superelastic behavior without plastic

Alloying is another method used to tailor shape memory
behavior, especially TT [1]. Among the many elements
investigated as potential ternary alloying additions to NiTi,
only Hf, Zr, Pd, Au and Pt are known to increase the TT
[2]. Because of the high cost of Pt, Pd and Au, the most
practical alloying additions for developing high-tempera-
ture shape memory alloys are Zr and Hf [2]. But, NiTiHf
alloys suﬀer from brittleness and poor shape memory and
superelastic behavior [12]. Furthermore, the use of quater-
nary alloying additions to overcome these limitations has
not been generally pursued. NiTiHfCu alloys have been
investigated previously [12–16], since Cu additions to NiTi
are known to improve thermal stability, ductility and hys-
teresis [13–16]. However, NiTiHfCu alloys do not exhibit
any substantial beneﬁt, other than good two-way shape
memory eﬀect [16], over the shape memory properties of
NiTiHf alloys. Nb was added to NiTiHf alloys in addition
to Cu, and was found to increase the cold workability and
shape recovery ratio of the NiTiHf SMA, while decreasing
the TT and plastic strain in thermal cycling experiments
under stress levels of 500 MPa [17].
One of the advantages that a high-strength SMA can
oﬀer is high work output, which is crucial in solid-state
actuator applications. SMA-based actuators have addi-
tional advantages over commercial pneumatic/hydraulic
or motor-driven systems, such as higher energy density,
compactness, lightweight and silent operation [18]. One
measure of the capacity of a solid-state actuator is its work
output. As the work output of the material increases, the
required weight or volume decreases, resulting in a more
eﬃcient system. There is an emerging need for actuators
that produce high work output for applications in such
industries as aerospace, biomedical, automotive and
down-hole energy exploration. For example, some actua-
tion applications in civil or military aircrafts involve vari-
able (area or geometry) inlets and nozzles that require up
to 20% shape change [19,20]. Compared with existing
hydraulic actuation systems, solid-state actuators made
out of SMA may work more eﬃciently while occupying
much less space, thus enabling certain aircraft designs.
With suitable TT, high work output SMA may also be used
in biomedical applications such as stronger stents for main
arteries or certain implants. Commercial NiTi alloys have
work output densities of /C24 10 J cm
/C0 3 [21], while NiTiPd
and NiTiPt alloys have work output capabilities on the
order of 6–9 J cm /C0 3 and 13 J cm /C0 3, respectively [2,22].I n
a recent study, the maximum work output of Ni-rich
NiTi-Hf polycrystalline alloys was found to be 18–
20 J cm
/C0 3 [23]. It is clear that alloying signiﬁcantly aﬀects
work output, but further investigation is needed for a bet-
ter understanding of the relationship between composition,
microstructure and work output of NiTi-based alloys.
In addition, because of their energy-absorbing capabili-
ties, SMA could be employed as dampers in many indus-
tries, such as aerospace (in aircraft engines to dampen the
acoustic energy) and construction (in impact damping
devices to counter seismic movements). Damping capacity
is the ability of the material to dissipate energy from a sys-
tem. Hence, for this type of application, high stress hyster-
esis and transformation strain values are desired during
isothermal deformation. In general, the stress hysteresis
of NiTi alloys is /C24 200–400 MPa [1], while it can be
increased to 500–600 MPa with the addition of Nb [24].
It was reported that damping capacities are 16 J cm

composite wires [26]. An alloy that has a damping capacity
similar to NiTiNb and NbTi/NiTi systems but also with
the ability to operate under higher stress levels would be
very beneﬁcial for many damping applications.
During previous studies, the superelastic properties of
NiTiHfPd single crystals, along the [1 1 1] direction, were
determined in compression [27]. Homogenized single crys-
tals were capable of superelastic behavior at temperatures
ranging from /C0 30 /C176Ct o7 0 /C176C under extremely high stress
levels (2.5 GPa) with little or no plastic deformation [27].
Moreover, they had a very large damping capacity
(44 J cm
/C0 3) due to a very large mechanical hysteresis
(>1200 MPa). The eﬀects of aging on the shape memory
properties (e.g. mechanical hysteresis, TT and strength)
of [1 1 1] oriented NiTiHfPd single crystal alloys were also
determined [28]. Results showed that aging improved the
strength of the material during constant-load thermal
cycling as a result of precipitation hardening. The aged
alloy was capable of fully recoverable shape memory strain
up to 2% under 1000 MPa, and almost complete transfor-
mation behavior at stress levels as high as 1500 MPa [28].
The purpose of this study is to investigate the shape
memory and superelastic properties of a quaternary
Ni
45.3–Ti29.7–Hf20–Pd5 (at.%) polycrystalline alloy in com-
pression after selected aging conditions. The eﬀects of
aging temperature and time on the microstructure, TT,
transformation strain, temperature and mechanical (stress)
hystereses, strength levels, work output and damping
capacities were determined by conducting a series of
mechanical experiments on polycrystalline alloys.

3.1. Diﬀerential scanning calorimetry
To determine the eﬀect of aging temperature and time
on TT, DSC tests were conducted after 3 and 5 h of aging
at temperatures ranging from 400 to 900 /C176C. The DSC
curves are shown in Fig. 1 . For each DSC cycle, the endo-
thermic peaks at higher temperatures represent the mar-
tensite to austenite (reverse) transformation upon
heating, and the exothermic peaks at lower temperatures
represent the austenite to martensite (forward) transforma-

to ﬁve thermal cycles were completed to investigate the

Fig. 1 a shows the DSC response of the NiTiHfPd after
aging at selected temperatures between 400 /C176C and 900 /C176C

repeated cycling in the DSC is less for certain aging condi-

of increased thermal stability due to the aging treatments.

4. Discussion
4.1. Transformation temperatures
Changes in the stress-free TT with aging temperature
and time are summarized in Fig. 6 . The TT used in
Fig. 6 and throughout the text were measured from ﬁrst
DSC curves shown in Fig. 1 . The initial drop in TT with
aging at 400 /C176C is attributed to the formation of precipi-
tates with very small size and very short interparticle dis-
tance. Thus, the resistance for martensite nucleation
increases, and formation of martensite requires more
energy change, which in turn requires further undercooling
in the course of the forward transformation [34].
The underlying reason for the increase in TT after the
initial decline can be an increase in the volume fraction
of the precipitates as in NiTi [1,7] and NiTi-based alloys
[35,36], resulting in a change in composition (e.g. Ni deple-
tion in the matrix) of the remaining matrix material. It is
well known that TT are highly composition dependent in
NiTi-based SMA [1]. In nickel-rich NiTi-based alloys, TT
increase by formation of Ni-rich precipitates as in NiTi
[6–9], NiTiPt [37], NiTiPd [38] and NiTiHf [39] alloys,
while precipitation of Ti-rich phases can potentially result
in a decrease in TT, as has been reported in NiTiPd alloys
[35]. Consequently, as the chemical composition of the
matrix changes during precipitate coarsening, the TT are
altered [1,35]. The coherency of the formed precipitates in
the microstructure is another factor that can aﬀect the
TT. The mismatch between matrix and precipitate lattice
parameters may cause internal stresses around precipitates
that can also change the TT [40]. If the internal stress has
the same sign as the applied stress, TT will increase, since
the stress will assist transformation.

precipitate volume fraction as the aging temperature
increases and either approaches or surpasses the precipitate
solvus temperature. This once again results in a Ni-rich

Transformation strains extracted from the constant-

transformation, and thus their presence reduces the trans-

the volume fraction of precipitates in a material, the less
transformation strain would be expected, all other factors
being equal.

transformation strain during the stress-induced transfor-
mation is consistent with the results from the constant-load
thermal cycling tests and can be attributed to the increased
volume fraction of precipitates.

The change from Class I to II transformation behavior

uted to the higher elastic energy storage during thermal
cycling at 1000 MPa compared with lower stress levels.
The higher elastic energy storage can be a result of (i) a
change in twinning properties of the martensite, (ii) a
change in lattice parameters of the transforming phases,
(iii) improved strength of the material, and (iv) back stress
due to the precipitates.
The NiTiHfPd polycrystalline alloy undergoes a B2
(cubic) to B19 0 (monoclinic) phase transformation under
zero stress. However, at stresses as high as 1000 MPa, the
lattice parameters of the parent and martensitic phases
may be subjected to changes that will aﬀect the compatibil-
ity of the transforming phases and twinning properties.
Thus, elastic energy storage might increase during thermal
cycling at 1000 MPa compared with thermal cycling at
lower stresses [50]. However, it is clear that for the as-
extruded sample that the applied stress does not result in
high elastic energy storage, since A
s > Ms for all applied
stress levels. Thus, either applied stress does not increase
the stored elastic energy, or stored elastic energy relaxes
after transformation in the as extruded sample.

In Fig. 11 , the maximum work output levels of various
NiTi-based SMA are shown as a function of their typical
operating temperature range. It is observed that NiTiHfPd
alloys can generate higher work outputs of 32–35 J cm

NiTi alloys can generate work outputs >10–20 J cm /C0 3

between 9 and 15 J cm /C0 3 at temperatures >150 /C176C [22,56].
Beside the high work output, NiTiHfPd polycrystals
have considerable damping capacity, which in simple terms
is the ability to repeatedly disperse unwanted energy from a
system. Damping capacity is related to the mechanical hys-
teresis and transformation strain and can be determined by
calculating the area between the forward and reverse trans-
formations in the superelastic stress–strain curves, as
depicted schematically in Fig. 12 a. High hysteresis and
high transformation strain result in more energy dissipa-
tion from a system. Damping capacities of selected NiTi-
based alloys are compared in Fig. 12 b as a function of
operating stress levels (critical stress for forward
transformation).
The high damping capacity (30–34 J cm /C0 3) of the pres-
ent alloy stems from its outstanding mechanical hysteresis
and good superelastic strain, approaching 4% ( Fig. 5 ). In
related systems, the damping capacity is 16 J cm
/C0 3, 18–
20 J cm /C0 3,3 8 J c m /C0 3 and 54 J cm /C0 3 for NiTi, NiTiHf,
polycrystalline NiTiNb alloys and NbTi/NiTi nanocom-
posites, respectively [23–26]. The NiTiHfPd alloy has capa-
bility similar to polycrystalline NiTiNb alloys, which are
often used in coupling applications, while both polycrystal-
line materials have damping capacity that is less than new
NbTi/NiTi nanocomposites. However, it should be noted
that NiTiHfPd alloys have the ability to operate at much
higher stresses ( /C24 2 GPa) than the NiTiNb and NbTi/NiTi
systems.
In addition to damping applications, dissipation mecha-
nisms due to the martensitic transformation allow SMA to
be used in applications that require wear resistance [57].I n
particular, properties such as high strength, high hardness,
corrosion resistance and the ability to be used with conven-
tional lubricants [58], combined with this superb damping
capacity and wear resistance, open these types of materials
to many tribological and wear-resistant applications,
including bearings and gears [59].
5. Conclusions
In this study, a Ni 45.3Ti29.7Hf20Pd5 (at.%) polycrystalline
shape memory alloy, aged under diﬀerent conditions, was
characterized using various experimental techniques
including DSC, XRD, TEM, thermal cycling under con-
stant load, and isothermal stress cycling tests. Eﬀects of
aging temperature on shape memory eﬀect and superelas-
ticity responses were revealed. Based on the results of test-
ing, the present authors arrived at the following
conclusions.
1. Precipitates with sizes /C24 20–30 nm in length and an inter-
particle distance of /C24 10–30 nm were formed after
550 /C176C–3 h aging. The length of the precipitates varied
from 80 nm to 300 nm with a width of /C24 50 nm and
interparticle distances of 15–150 nm as the aging tem-
perature was increased to 650 /C176C. (0 0 1)
B190 compound
twins were observed in all samples, regardless of thermal
treatment.
2. The strength of the NiTiHfPd alloy depended on aging
temperature. In the as-extruded form, it was possible
to recover strains of /C24 4% during both constant-load
thermal cycles under 1000 MPa and superelastic stress
cycling at stresses up to 2000 MPa. Similar testing of
the material after aging at 550 /C176C for 3 h occurred with-
out measureable plastic deformation, though the result-
ing transformation strains were smaller.
3. When NiTiHfPd was aged at 650 /C176C for 3 h, precipita-
tion hardening was much less eﬀective, and the aged
alloy did not show full shape recovery in superelastic-
ity tests conducted in the temperature range 100–
