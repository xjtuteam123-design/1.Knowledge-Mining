Keywords: AZ61 Mg alloy; Texture; Anisotropy; Tensile properties; Superplasticity. 
Abstract. The texture characteristics and the relationshi p between texture and tensile properties at 
room temperature and superplasticity at high temperatures in a hot extruded AZ61 alloy are 
examined in this paper. After wa rm extrusion, the alloy exhibits a of refined grains microstructure 
and a sharp basal {0001} texture. The tensile properties at room and elevated temperatures in 
extruded with the loading axis oriented at 0 °, 45° and 90° to the extrusion direction are tested. It is 
found that the 45 ° specimens exhibit has lower yield stre ngth and higher ductility. The possible 
causes for mechanical anisotropy are also analyzed based on the Schmid factor calculations. 
Introduction 
Magnesium alloys are emerging as potentially good candidates for numerous applications, 
especially in transportation vehicles or lightwe ight enclosures for 3C (computer, communication 
and consumer electronic) products owing to thei r good properties, such as low density and high 
specific strength [1]. However, the disadvantage of Mg alloys is that they generally exhibit limited 
ductility due to their hcp crystalline structure at room temperature. Therefore, it is required to 
improve their poor workability. In the view of i ndustrial practice, the wa rm-extrusion process is 
one of efficient forming technology to refine gr ain size, which is the key prerequisite for a 
microstructural capable of deformation. Moreover , the warm-extrusion pr ocess would result in a 
significant crystallographic texture, which has an important influence on the mechanical properties 
[2-4]. The presentation for texture components and texture evolutions, as well as the relationship 
between the texture and mechanical properties for hexagonal materials are less reported. The 
present work is to demonstrate the texture char acteristics of warm extruded AZ61 Mg alloy, and 
the influence of texture on the tensile properties and superplasticity.

used in this study. The texture of the tested ma terial was measured by X -ray diffraction using the

deformation and hence the higher ductility. So, it is expected that the yield strength is the lowest

As for the anisotropic s uperplasticity at 300-400℃, it is generally accept ed that grain boundary 
sliding accommodated by dislocation slip is the main mechanism for superplastic deformation [10, 
11]. The mobility of intragranular dislocations also plays an impor tant role in the mechanism. A 
reasonable explanation for the anisotropic superpla sticity in the present textured AZ61 Mg alloy 
would be contributed to the mobility of dislocati ons in the differently oriented samples. Based on 
the Schmid factor analysis on the slip systems, it can be realized that the mobility of dislocations 
in the 45° sample is the highest. This means that the overlap of grains and the stress concentration 
due to the grain boundary sliding can be more r eadily relieved by the movement of high mobility 
of dislocations owing to the highest Schmid factor than that of ot her two samples, at least in the 
initial stage of superplastic deformation for the 45 ° sample, because the texture is little altered by 
the small amount of grain boundary sliding and grai n rotation at this stag e. For this reason, the 
accommodation by dislocation slip with respect to the grain boundary sliding and rotation is 
expected to occur more smoothly, and grai n boundary sliding can continue as the major 
deformation process. This will lead to lower stress concentration at the triple points and less 
occurrences of cavity nucleation, favorable for achieving higher superplastic elongation than other 
two 0° and 90° samples. 
Conclusion 
1. After warm extrusion, the coarse grains in the cast AZ61 Mg alloy were transformed into 
refine equiaxed grain structures ~6.5 µm in size, implying that the strong dynamic 
recrystallization takes place during the warm-extrusion. 
2. The {0002}<10 10> texture is the main component among the sharp basal {0002} textures 
after warm extrusion. 
3. The macroscopic orientation has an important in fluence on the tensile properties at room and 
elevated temperatures. The 45 ° sample exhibits lower tensile strength and higher ductility at 
~25℃, and higher superplastic elongation at 300 ~ 400 ℃, as compared with the 0 ° and 90° 
samples. 
4. The anisotropic behavior would be rationalized in terms of the Schmid factor values and the 
ease of dislocation mobility in various slip systems. Th e results are in good with the 
experimental observations.
