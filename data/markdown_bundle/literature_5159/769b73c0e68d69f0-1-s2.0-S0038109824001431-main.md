1. Introduction 
Titanium (Ti) and Ti-based alloys have been widely utilized in 
various engineering fields and biomedical applications for many years 
because of their ability to resist corrosion, deep hardenability and 
biocompatibility [ 1 – 5 ]. Pure Ti, also referred to as the α phase, remains 
stable in the hexagonal close-packed structure at room temperature. 
However, above the β transus temperature phase, the β phase with a 
body-centered cubic (bcc) structure becomes stable [ 6 ]. The addition of 
different metallic elements in Ti-based alloys significantly influences the 
phase stability [ 7 ], leading to the formation of nonequilibrium phases 
such as the orthorhombic α ", the hexagonal α ′ , and ω martensite phases. 
The concentration of the alloying element, temperature, or pressure 
determines the presence of different phase constitutions, which in turn 
result in various mechanical properties such as strength, modulus, 
ductility, and biocompatibility [ 8 , 9 ]. Nonetheless, the utilization of 
these materials has been hindered due to the inadequate compatibility 
between strength and toughness, as well as the insufficient ductility of 
existing high-strength Ti alloys. For example, Aermet340 alloys with 
Ultra-high strength has not been widely promoted because its fracture 
toughness value is only 37 MPa m
1/2 
[ 10 ]. Meyers et al. proposed that 
homogeneous nanocrystalline metals exhibit ultra-high strengths over 1 
GPa, but that comes with diminishing (e.g., less than 5 %) ductility [ 11 ]. 
Currently, β -Ti alloys with elements that stabilize the β phase have 
gained significant attention due to their unique mechanical properties, 
alongside the fact that the β phase can be stabilized by incorporating 
numerous non-toxic transition metal elements [ 12 , 13 ]. Consequently, it 
is imperative to investigate the effect of substitutional atomic on the 
mechanical properties of binary Ti-based alloys to solve this current 
issue. 
Numerous studies have shown that the phase transformation and 
mechanical properties of binary Ti-M alloys are significantly influenced 
by the content of substitutional alloying elements M. The addition of 
strong stabilizers in Ti – Nb and Ti – Ni alloys, such as alloying compo -
nents, has been extensively examined and has demonstrated the in -
duction of martensitic transformation, resulting in superelastic
