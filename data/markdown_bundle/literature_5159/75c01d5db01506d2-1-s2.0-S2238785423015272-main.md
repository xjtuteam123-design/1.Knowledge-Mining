abstract
The microstructural features of long period stacking ordered (LPSO) phase in Mg e(11-x)Gd
exYe1Zne0.3Zr (wt.%) alloys were optimized by adjusting Gd/Y ratios to10:1, 8:3, 6:5, 4:7
and 2:9, respectively. Superior high-temperature strength was successfully obtained in the
extruded 6Gde5Y alloy with ultimate tensile strength (UTS) values of 338 MPa and 286 MPa
at 250
/C14 C and 300 /C14 C, respectively. In the as-cast alloys, with the decrease of Gd/Y ratio, the
type of intermetallic compound gradually changed from (Mg, Zn) 3(Gd, Y) (W) to LPSO phase.
After homogenization, the W phase was completely converted to LPSO phase except for the
10Gde1Y alloy. Importantly, the block-shaped LPSO phases with the smallest size and the
abundant lamellar LPSO phases were formed in the 6Gd e5Y alloy, which signiﬁcantly
inﬂuenced the microstructures of the extruded alloy. Firstly, the small sized block-shaped
LPSO phase before extrusion provided favorable conditions for the formation of thin plate-
shaped LPSO phase at DRXed regions, which effectively hindered the grain boundary
migration at high temperature due to superior thermal stability. Secondly, the profuse
lamellar LPSO phases prior to extrusion contributed to the highest density of low angle
grain boundaries (LAGBs) and residual dislocations in the extruded 6Gd e5Y alloy, which
obstructed dislocation slip at elevated temperatures. In addition, the abundant lamellar
LPSO phase also caused the ﬁne DRXed grain size and strong basal texture to enhance the
high-temperature strength of the extruded 6Gd e5Y alloy.

1. Introduction
Under the advocacy of energy saving and emission reduction,
magnesium (Mg) alloys are considered to have great potential
for broad applications in automotive, aerospace and other
ﬁelds due to their low density, high speciﬁc strength and
stiffness [1e3]. However, the wider application of Mg alloys is
limited due to their moderate strength and poor heat resis-
tance [ 4]. For example, although the commercial AZ91, AM50
and AM60 alloys have suitable room temperature (RT)
strength, their high temperature properties are dramatically
deteriorated due to the existence of thermally unstable
Mg
17Al12 phase [5]. As a result, the design of high-strength and
heat-resistant Mg alloys plays a crucial role in the future
development of Mg alloys.
In recent years, Mg-rare earth (RE)-Zn alloys with LPSO
phase have received much attention due to their superior
comprehensive properties in terms of strength-plasticity
trade-off and heat resistance [ 6e8]. On the one hand,
the LPSO phase has a higher elastic modulus and hardness
than the Mg matrix, which contributes to its high strength
[9]. On the other hand, the lamellar LPSO phase can sub-
stantially increase the critical resolved shear stress (CRSS) of
basal slip and promote the activation of non-basal slip,
thus leading to increased plasticity [ 10]. Kawamura et al. [ 11]
prepared an Mg eYeZn alloy with ultra-high strength by
powder metallurgy and extrusion, which showed a high
yield strength (YS) of 610 MPa resulting from the tiny LPSO
phase and nano-sized grains. Liu et al. [ 8] successfully
developed an Mg eGdeYeZneMn alloy with an UTS of
538 MPa accompanied with good elongation of 13.1%,
which was originated from the b’ precipitates and lamellar
LPSO phase.
In Mg eREeZn systems, alloying design is a straightfor-
ward and feasible way to further improve their mechanical
properties. For example, Yang et al. [ 12] studied the effect of
Mg
3YZn6 (I), Mg3Y2Zn3 (W) and Mg 12ZnY (LPSO) phases on the
tensile properties of Mg eYeZn alloys by changing the con-
tents of Y and Zn. Jiang et al. [ 13] successfully improved the
elongation (EL) of Mg e10Zne6Ye0.5Zr alloy by replacing part
of Gd with Y element. Wang et al. [ 14] regulated the charac-
teristics of second phase in the Mg eGdeYeZneZr alloy by
changing the Zn/(Gd þ Y) ratio to 0.14, 0.21 and 0.25,
respectively. The results showed that the alloy with Zn/
(Gd þ Y) ratio of 0.21 exhibited the best YS and EL at RT.
Generally, the investigations of modulating the RT mechan-
ical properties of Mg eREeZn alloys by varying the RE con-
tents have received a great deal of attention [ 15e17].
However, the effect of different Gd/Y ratios on the high-
temperature properties of Mg eGdeYeZneZr alloy has al-
ways been neglected.
In present study, the microstructural features of LPSO
phase were tailored by adjusting the Gd/Y ratios of Mg-(11- x)
GdexYe1Zne0.3Zr (wt.%) alloys to achieve superior high-
temperature mechanical properties. By revealing the re-
lationships between the microstructures and strengthening
mechanisms, these ﬁndings could offer a theoretical basis for
the rapid application of high-strength heat-resistant Mg
alloys.
2. Experimental procedures
MgeGdeYeZneZr alloys with different Gd/Y ratios (10:1, 8:3,
6:5, 4:7 and 2:9) and constant total RE content of 11 wt% were
designed. The ingots were prepared in an electrical furnace
with pure Mg (99.99%), Mg e30Gd (wt.%), Mg e30Y (wt.%),
Mge30Zr (wt.%) master alloys and pure Zn (99.99%) under the
protection of mixed SF
6 (1 vol %) and CO 2 (99 vol %) atmo-
spheres using traditional gravity casting. Pure Zn, Mg e30Gd
and Mge30Y master alloys were added at 740 /C14 C after the pure
Mg was completely melted. The Mg e30Zr master alloy was
then added after the melt temperature was raised to 780 /C14 C.
After complete melting, the melt was manually stirred for
5 min and holding for 20 min. Finally, the melt was poured
into a steel mould preheated to 200
/C14 C for solidiﬁcation. The
actual compositions of these ingots were measured using X-
ray ﬂuorescence spectrometer (XRF-1800CCDE), the corre-
sponding results are shown in Table 1 . For convenience, the
studied alloys were simpliﬁed as 10Gd e1Y, 8Gde3Y, 6Gde5Y,
4Gde7Y and 2Gde9Y alloys, respectively. The cast ingots were
homogenized at 510
/C14 C for 12 h in a resistance furnace. After
homogenization, these ingots were preheated at 410 /C14 C for
30 min and then extruded at a ram speed of 0.5 mm s /C0 1 with
an extrusion ratio of 7.11:1. These extrusion rods were
quenched into water at ambient temperature.
Tensile samples which have a gauge length of 18 mm, a
thickness of 2 mm, and a width of 3 mm were cut along the
extrusion direction (ED). Tensile tests were carried out on a
CMT6305-300 KN test machine at RT, 250
/C14 C and 300 /C14 C,
respectively. The strain rate was set as 10 /C0 3/s. All tensile tests
were repeated for three times to guarantee the accuracy of the
measured results. Before performing tensile testing at
elevated temperatures, the specimens were heated and held
for 20 min to equilibrate the testing temperature.
The microstructures of the samples were obtained using
optical microscopy (OM, ZEISS Axiovert 40 MAT), scanning
electron microscopy (SEM, Tescan Vega 3 LMH) equipped with
a backscattered electron spectrometer (BSE), a transmission
electron microscopy (TEM, FEI Talos F200S)) and a high angle
annular dark ﬁeld scanning TEM (HAADF-STEM) operating at
200 kV. The texture was measured by another SEM (JEOL JSM-
7800F) equipped with an HKL-Electron backscatter diffraction
(EBSD) system. The step size of EBSD characterization was set
at 0.7 mm. The acquired EBSD data were further analyzed using
HKL Chanel 5 software. The phase constitutions were detec-
ted by an X-ray diffractometer (XRD, D/MAX-2500PC). The
phase diagram simulation was calculated by Pandat software.
The area fraction of secondary phases was evaluated using
Image-Pro Plus software.

4. Discussion
4.1. Microstructural evolutions before extrusion
In this study, with the increase of Gd/Y ratio, the W phase is
gradually substituted by the LPSO phase in the as-cast
MgeGdeYeZneZr alloys. Gd and Y have a strong inﬂuence
on the types of intermediate compounds in the Mg eREeZn
system alloys. It is widely known that the W phase could be
produced during solidiﬁcation and convert to LPSO phase after
annealing in the Mg eGdeZn alloy [ 34]. On the contrary, the
LPSO phase was produced directly in the cast Mg eYeZn alloy
[35,36]. In addition, some researchers reported that the as-cast
MgeGdeYeZneZr alloys with Zn/(Gd þ Y) ratios ranging from
0.14 to 0.25 have W or LPSO phases [ 14]. In our work, the Zn/
(Gd þ Y) ratios between 0.13 and 0.2 are within its range, which
is therefore consistent with the previous report. Combining
the above analysis, W is the dominant phase with high Gd and

and abundant lamellar LPSO phases were formed in the ho-
mogenized 6Gd e5Y alloy. Such microstructural features of
LPSO phase are closely related to its area fraction of the sec-
ondary phase in the as-cast state. Zhou et al. [ 39] pointed out
that with the extension of homogenization time in Mg-Gd-Y-
Zn-Zr alloy, the size of block-shaped LPSO phase gradually
decreased and the lamellar LPSO phase disappeared ﬁrstly
and then precipitated. Therefore, the block-shaped LPSO
phase was preferentially dissolved and its size reduced since
6Gde5Y alloy has minimum area fraction of secondary phase.
Meanwhile, the dissolution of block-shaped LPSO phase leads
to an increase of the RE/Zn content in the Mg matrix. Upon
heat treatment their atomic diffusion rate increases remark-
edly, which is beneﬁcial for the precipitation of lamellar LPSO
phase [ 20]. Therefore, the abundance of RE/Zn atoms in the
matrix accounts for the large number of lamellar LPSO phase
in the 6Gd e5Y alloy.
4.2. Microstructural evolutions after extrusion
In 10Gde1Y alloy some small W particles are intersecting with
LPSO segments, implying that the W phase is gradually
transformed into LPSO phase during extrusion ( Fig. 5 (a)).
Firstly, the extrusion temperature of 410
/C14 C favors the gener-
ation of LPSO phase as predicted by phase diagram. Secondly,
the crushing of coarse W phase into small-sized one during
extrusion may provide favorable conditions for the trans-
formation of LPSO phase [ 38]. Normally, a single W phase
could transform into an LPSO phase with one certain orien-
tation. However, in this study, the LPSO segments consisting
of many different orientations was observed (yellow lines,
Fig. 5 ), suggesting that they were originally converted from
multiple broken W particles that were crushed during extru-
sion process.
The 10Gde1Y, 8Gde3Y, 6Gde5Y and 4Gde7Y alloys consist
of ﬁne DRXed and unDRXed grains elongated along the ED.
Low extrusion ratio (7.11:1) and extrusion temperature (410
/C14 C)
usually promote the creation of bimodal microstructures [ 40].
In addition, the 10Gd e1Y alloy has a large grain size and
minimal grain boundaries prior to extrusion. Therefore, the
nucleation sites for DRXed grains are reduced, which results
in a minimum DRXed ratio of about 31.2%. In contrast, the
2Gde9Y alloy has a nearly fully DRXed microstructure, which
stems from its large area fraction of block-shaped LPSO phase.
It promotes the nucleation of DRXed grains through particle-
stimulated nucleation (PSN) mechanism [ 39]. It is worth
mentioning that massive lamellar LPSO phases before extru-
sion can effectively hinder the growth of DRXed grains [ 41],
making the smallest grain size in the 6Gd e5Y alloy.
It is noticed that the homogenized 6Gd e5Y alloy has the
smallest block-shaped LPSO phase, which leads to the for-
mation of thin plate-shaped LPSO phase. As mentioned earlier
that no precipitates were formed at DRXed region in 10Gd e1Y
alloy ( Fig. 5 (d)), while nano-sized thin plate-shaped LPSO
phase appears at the grain boundary of 6Gd e5Y alloy
(Fig. 6 (c)). Assuming that the LPSO phase is dynamically
precipitated during extrusion, the intragranular lamellar LPSO
phase and the intergranular LPSO phase should have the same
orientation. Because the block-shaped LPSO phase is formed
by periodic stacking of intragranular lamellar LPSO phase/SFs
through a ledge growth mechanism [ 20]. But red dotted circle
shows that they do not have the same orientation ( Fig. 6(c)). In
addition, some thin plate-shaped LPSO phases are composed
of LPSO fragments with different orientations (yellow dotted
circles). Therefore, another explanation should be responsible
for the formation of this thin plate-shaped LPSO phase.

small-sized block-shaped LPSO phase. Moreover, such small
thin-plate LPSO phase has excellent thermal stability to
inhibit the growth of DRXed grains [ 7,45], which accounts for
the smallest size of DRXed grains in the 6Gd e5Y alloy.
In addition to the smallest block-shaped LPSO phase,
abundant lamellar LPSO phases prior to extrusion can also

the extrusion process, the lamellar LPSO phase underwent

deformation is an important way to coordinate deformation
due to the limited slip systems in HCP crystal structure [ 46].
Some researchers reported that dipole-pair dislocations are
generated at the kink interface. Dislocations with the same
directions keep accumulating, thus leading to an increased
kink angle in the early stage of extrusion [ 47]. Therefore, the
number of kink interfaces and rotation angles are positively
correlated with dislocation densities [24]. With the continuous
accumulation of dislocations, a fan-shaped kink was ﬁnally
formed. In other words, the kinked interface of the lamellar
LPSO phase is also the LAGBs. In addition, the fan-shaped kink
is believed to be generated under multiple stresses such as
extrusion and the involvement of non-basal dislocation slip

grains. Depending on the g*b ¼ 0( g: diffraction vector; b:

the CRSS of basal slip and contribute to the activation of the
non-basal slip in the Mg matrix [ 50]. For 10Gd e1Y alloy, the
lamellar LPSO phase is dynamically precipitated during pre-
heating prior to hot extrusion [ 19]. In contrast, the 6Gd e5Y
alloy already has a large number of lamellar LPSO phases
before extrusion. This plays an essential role in generating the
highest proportion of LAGBs and the largest KAM values
compared to other alloys. Consequently, the extruded 6Gde5Y
alloy has large quantities of lamellar LPSO kinks, LAGBs, and
residual dislocations.

4.3. Strengthening mechanism at room and elevated
temperatures
By optimizing the morphology of LPSO phase, the 6Gd e5Y
alloy has the best heat resistance compared to other alloys. In
the following sections, the mechanical properties of
MgeGdeYeZneZr alloys with different Gd/Y ratios are dis-
cussed with respect to their differences in the secondary
phases, grain sizes, textures and dislocation densities.
For 10Gde1Y alloy, it has a connected W phase and block-
shaped LPSO phase distribution along the ED. The high
hardness of the LPSO phase commonly causes stress con-
centration during tensile process [ 19,29], while the brittle W
phase could be crack sites to deteriorate the strength and
plasticity [56]. For other alloys, the size and amount of block-
shaped LPSO phase play an essential role in the improvement
of mechanical properties. In comparison, the smallest size of
the block-shaped LPSO phase in the 6Gd e5Y alloy is the most
favorable for mechanical properties. Meanwhile, the thin
plate-shaped LPSO and b phases with excellent thermal sta-
bility in the DRXed regions could hinder grain boundary
migration by increasing the Zener pinning force [ 57]. Espe-
cially at high temperatures, the pinning of grain boundaries is
extremely critical when a large amount of grain boundary
slips exist [ 58]. Except for the block-shaped LPSO phase,
intragranular lamellar LPSO phase is equally important in
affecting the mechanical properties. Chen et al. [59] illustrated
that the basal lamellar LPSO phase has a weak strengthening
effect because its hindrance to basal dislocation slip is minor
during RT deformation. Nonetheless, the deformation mech-
anism will change signiﬁcantly at high temperatures. On the
one hand, the CRSS of non-basal slip decreases signiﬁcantly
with the increasing temperature, which leads to the opening

DRXed grains can effectively prevent dislocation movement

temperatures and deteriorate the performance. However, the
thin plate-shaped LPSO phase with outstanding heat resis-

improve the high-temperature performance by reinforcing
the grain boundaries. However, the grain boundary sliding in
unDRXed grain is not easily to be activated due to its large
grain size. Therefore, within the unDRXed grains, dislocation
slip may be the main deformation mechanism at 300
/C14 C. The
basal lamellar LPSO phase can effectively impede dislocation
slip, especially non-basal dislocation. When the accumulation
of dislocations reaches the maximum amount, cracks and
fractures would occur inside the unDRXed grains. This is the
key reason for the strong heat resistance of the 6Gd e5Y alloy
with high density of lamellar LPSO phases.
The texture intensity of <10
10 >//ED in the 6Gde5Y alloy is
the highest among all the alloys, and their tensile direction is
perpendicular to the c-axis in many grains. This orientation
relationship is not suitable for the activation of basal slip,
which contributes to high texture strengthening. The 2Gd e9Y
alloy with <0001>//ED also serves as hard direction for the
activation of basal slip. However, although both alloys have a
hard orientation, the <0001>//ED orientation is more condu-
cive to the formation of tensile twins [ 62]. On the other hand,
the large grain size of 2Gd e9Y alloy also facilitates the pro-
duction of twins [ 63]. It is well documented that twinning is a
critical mechanism to coordinate the plastic deformation, but
twin tends to be the location of crack initiation and thus de-
teriorates the mechanical properties both at room and high
temperatures [64,65].
The 2Gd e9Y alloy with fully DRXed microstructure has a
large number of HAGBs. However, the 6Gd e5Y alloy has the
largest amount of LAGBs and residual dislocations. LAGB is a
low-energy region in polycrystals, which could effectively
obstruct the dislocation movement [ 33]. Meanwhile, the large
number of residual dislocations inside unDRXed grains can
also improve the mechanical properties by obstructing dislo-
cations both at room and high temperatures. Therefore, the
most residual dislocations and LAGBs in the 6Gde5Y alloy play
a key role in the enhancement of the mechanical strength.
As for the ductility, all alloys increases monotonously with
the increase of tensile temperatures. It is well acknowledged
that the basal slip and twinning are the major deformation
modes of Mg alloys at low temperatures [ 66]. As the temper-
ature increases, the CRSS values of prismatic and pyramidal
systems decrease, and grain boundary tend to soften [ 67].
Therefore, the various deformation modes were activated that
can increase the EL values. It is previously reported that the
most dominant deformation mechanism is the activation of
non-basal slip on the block-shaped LPSO phase during RT
tensile testing. Therefore, the ﬁne block-shaped LPSO phase in
the 6Gd e5Y alloy plays an essential role in maintaining a
moderate EL. However, the coarse block-shaped LPSO phase in
2Gde9Y alloy is more prone to crack and therefore it has poor
RT plasticity. In addition, the bimodal microstructure with
coarse unDRXed grains and ﬁne DRXed grains could increase
both the strength and EL [68]. At high temperatures, non-basal
slip and grain boundary slip are easily to be activated. The
coarse unDRX grains with abundant lamellar LPSO phases can
effectively prevent non-basal dislocations, which contribute
to the excellent high-temperature strength. However, when
the amount of dislocation accumulation reaches the limit,
cracks would be generated inside the unDRXed grains and
thus affecting the EL.
5. Conclusions
In this work, the LPSO phase was tailored in Mg e(11-x) Gd- x
Ye1Zne0.3Zr (wt.%) alloys with different Gd/Y ratios to ach-
ieve optimum high-temperature mechanical properties. Their
mechanical properties were systematically revealed with
respect to their microstructures at room and high tempera-
tures. The main conclusions are drawn as follows.
(1) With the decrease of Gd/Y, the W phase in the as-cast
alloy gradually changed to the LPSO phase. After ho-
mogenization, the W phase was converted to LPSO
phase except for the 10Gd e1Y alloy. Notably, the
6Gde5Y alloy has the smallest size of LPSO phase and
the largest number of lamellar LPSO phases.
(2) After extrusion, the 10Gd e1Y, 8Gd e3Y, 6Gd e5Y,
4Gde7Y alloys which consisted of ﬁne DRXed and
coarse unDRXed grains, have a typical basal ﬁber
texture with <10
10 >kED. However, the 2Gd e9Y alloy
exhibited a fully DRXed microstructure with large grain
size and an unusual texture component of <0001>kED.
(3) The DRXed regions of the 6Gd e5Y alloy produced
several thin plate-shaped LPSO phases due to the frag-
mentation during extrusion. It also showed the most
LAGBs and residual dislocations.
(4) The 6Gd e5Y alloy displayed the best mechanical prop-
erties at RT, 250
/C14 C and 300 /C14 C with UTS values of
359 MPa, 338 MPa and 286 MPa, respectively. The high
strength was mainly attributed to the thin plate-shaped
LPSO phase, ﬁne size DRXed grains, texture strength-
ening and profuse LAGBs and residual dislocations.
