Effect of carburization (Pyrolysis furnace tube main failure factor) 
on the microstructure and properties of HPNb alloy tube
Jingfeng Guo1, Tieshan Cao1, Congqian Cheng1, Xianming Meng2, JieZhao1*
1. School of Materials Science and Engineering, Dalian University of Technology, Dalian 116024, China

Abstract:
The structure characteristics and mechanical properties of as-cast and carburized HPNb alloy tubes 
were investigated in this paper. Through magnetism test by the vibrating sample magnetometer, we find 
that the paramagnetic structure of as-cast HPNb alloy tubes has changed into ferromagnetic structure 
after carburization. Electron back-scattered diffraction (EBSD) and X-ray diffraction (XRD) analyses 
show that the M 23C6 type carbide transformed into M 7C3 type carbide in carburization zone. Due to the 
coarser primary carbides and blocky-shaped secondary carbides after carburization, the ductility and 
toughness of the ethylene cracking furnace tubes are very low and become brittle. At high temperature, 
the tensile strength and yield strength of carburized tube are higher than those of as-cast tube, but the 
tensile elongation is lower. Owing to the microstructure degradation, the creep strength of HPNb alloy 
decreased after carburization. Hence, the carburization also could lead to the decrease of the remnant life 
of the pyrolysis furnace tubes. 
Keywords: Carburization, Carbide, HPNb alloy, Ferromagnetic structure, Creep strength.
1.Introduction：
The traditional industrial process for producing ethylene is based on the thermal cracking of a 
mixture of hydrocarbons in a pyrolysis furnace at temperature ranges from 1173K to1373K [1]. Ethylene

## Page 3

pyrolysis furnace, which is composed of centrifugal casting heat-resistant steel tubes, is the critical 
component of petrochemical systems. Ethylene pyrolysis furnace tube alloys should have excellent creep 
strength, superior oxidation and carburization resistance at high temperature. Heat-resistant cast steel 
HPNb alloy is suitable to be used in cracking furnaces for its superior high-temperature creep strength 
and good oxidation and carburization resistance [2,3]. The heat-resistant cast steel is usually designed for 
a normal life time of 100,000h (11.4years) on the basis of API 530. However, their design life, varies 
from 30,000 to 180,000 h, is not expected to be equal to the actual life depending on the actual operating 
conditions[4]. Overheating [5], oxidization and carburization [6-10], creep and fatigue [11] are main factors 
contributing to ethylene cracking furnace tubes premature failure. 
Naphtha and ethane feed to produce ethylene are almost exclusively carried out in steam crackers 
operating around 1173~1373K [9]. So the ethylene cracking reaction releases free carbon during the 
cracking process, together with the characteristics of the fluids present, and lead to carbon diffusion into 
the tube walls from the internal surface towards the external one, resulting in carburization [12]. This 
detrimental effect leads to metallurgical alterations and brittleness, affecting the reliability of the tubes 
and reducing their service life. At first, the M7C3 type carbide of HPNb alloy change into M23C6 carbides 
after service [13]. Then, the metallurgical alteration of ethylene pyrolysis furnace tube after carburization 
is that the M 23C6 carbide transforms into M 7C3 type carbide in carburized zone at temperature ranges 
from 1123 K to1373 K[7,9,14]. The carbide transformation and carbide precipitation result in a decrease of 
the Cr concentration in the matrix. When the chromium content of the matrix decreased to 15%, it’s on 
the boundary between ferromagnetic and paramagnetic; the chromium reached to 11%, the entire matrix 
should be ferromagnetic at room temperature; while the Chromium of the matrix above 18%, the matrix 
will be paramagnetic [15,16]. The HPNb austenitic alloys when not carburized are paramagnetic, but with

## Page 4

the increase of carburization, they gradually become ferromagnetic owing to a decrease in the Cr 
concentration in the matrix[17-19]. 
Not only the ferromagnetic structure transformation of ethylene cracking furnace tube materials 
occurs after carburization, but also the mechanical properties tend to be a decline. Carburization is usually 
considered to be one of the major factor of damaging ethylene pyrolysis furnace tubes [8,14,20-22]. Many 
researchers have simulated the effect of carburization on the damage of ethylene cracking furnace tubes. 
Carburization accelerated the damage process dramatically, and contributed to the loss of ductility [20,23]. 
The failure probability of ethylene cracking furnace tube under interaction of creep and carburization is 
larger than that under creep, pointed out by Wenhe Wang [24]. As a consequence of precipitation of 
carbides due to carburization, the ductility and creep strength of furnace tube are decreased [25]. 
Carburization caused a great deal of failure of pyrolysis furnace tubes. Hence, carburization is considered 
to be one of the typical failures in ethylene pyrolysis furnace tubes[26].
 Studying the microstructure and mechanical properties of HPNb alloy after carburization is an 
important reference on evaluating the material grades and assessing remaining life duration of ethylene 
pyrolysis furnace[25,27,28]. Many researchers have studied the carbides transformation in the furnace tube 
after carburization, but there are few studies on the mechanical properties of carburized furnace tube. 
This present work focused on analysing the structure characteristics and its influence on the mechanical 
properties of HPNb alloy tubes after carburization, and assess the effect of carburization on the pyrolysis 
furnace tube damage. So microstructure observation, magnetism tests, tensile tests, high-temperature 
tensile tests and creep tests were conducted on the carburized ethylene pyrolysis furnace tubes in this 
paper to evaluate microstructure and mechanical properties.

2. Materials and experiments
Pyrolysis furnace tubes were exposed temperatures in the 1223~1373 K with flowing naphtha at a 
total pressure close to 0.5 MPa. To study the effect of carburization on metallurgical alteration and 
mechanical properties, the carburized furnace tube is selected from an ethylene pyrolysis furnace after a 
five-year service. The thickness of the furnace tube is equal to 5.8mm. The chemical compositions of the 
tubes are listed in Table 1, which are tested by Optical Emission Spectrometer (Q8 MAGELLAN). Figure 
1 shows the sample position of the furnace tube. Due to the carburization, the carbon content of 
carburized tube is higher.
Fig.1The schematic diagram of samples
In order to test the magnetism and the carburized depth of the ethylene pyrolysis furnace tube, the 
saturation magnetization was carried out by a vibrating sample magnetometer (VSM: JDM-13) through 
the tube thickness. In order to investigate microstructure, the specimens were grounded with silicon 
carbides papers from 200 to 2000 grit and polished by woollen cloth with 0.1μm diamond paste. The 
specimens, which were used for observation of microstructure, were electrolytically etched in a solution 
of oxalic acid (wt. 10%) at 5 V, and 3 A for 10 s. The microstructure was characterized by scanning 
electron microscopy (SEM: SUPARR 55).
EBSD and XRD analyses were performed in order to identify the phases present in the serviced 
tube. After polishing with diamond paste down to 1μm, the specimens were further polished with a 
0.03μm colloidal silica suspension for 6 h. EBSD measurements were carried out with a field emission

was 0.5μm.
Tensile tests of standard specimens (ISO 6892-1:2009 MOD) with a gauge length of 35 mm were 
carried out at room temperature with a strain rate of 2.5×10 -4/s[29]. Tensile tests of standard specimens 
(ISO 6892-2:2011) at elevated temperature were carried out at 1173 K (ASTM A608-2014) [30,31]. To 
evaluate the creep strength at high temperature, uniaxial creep tests in tension of standard specimens 
(ISO 204:2009) were conducted at 1173 K, 55 MPa and 1273 K, 25 MPa[32].

3. Results and discussions
3.1 Structure change
As-cast HPNb alloy is paramagnetic and has an austenitic microstructure. After carburization, the 
carbon diffuses into the austenitic matrix, precipitating as M 7C3 and M 23C6 carbides, and such carbide 
precipitation brings about a decrease in the Cr concentration in the matrix. So, with the increase of 
carburization, the tubes gradually become ferromagnetic [16,18,19,33]. To evaluate the carburized depth and 
structure changes of pyrolysis furnace tubes, we can check the magnetism through the thickness of tubes.
In order to investigate magnetism of the furnace tube after carburization ， the saturation 
magnetization of the tube was tested across the thickness. Figure 2 shows the profile of the saturation 
magnetization across the thickness of carburized tube. The result reveals that the saturation magnetization 
of carburized tube is higher than that of as-cast tube, especially at inner wall and outer wall(Fig.2). That’s

## Page 7

because of the oxidation occurred both at inner and outer surface of the ethylene pyrolysis furnace tubes 
[34,35]. The saturation magnetization of carburized tube is more than 20 emu/g. The result shows that the 
structure of carburized tube has transformed into ferromagnetic structure. Carburization occurred 
throughout the thickness of the furnace tube. After carburization, the paramagnetic structure of the 
ethylene pyrolysis furnace tubes have changed into ferromagnetic structure.

3.2 Microstructure 
Fig 3 shows the microstructure of as-cast tube and carburized tube. The microstructure of as-cast 
HPNb alloy consists of a cellular γ-matrix and the skeleton shape eutectic carbides: the eutectic carbide 
is lamellae on the dendrite boundaries and γ-matrix in grains, which is a typical microstructure of as-cast 
materials of alloys with high chromium and nickel contents [6,36](Fig.3(a)). After carburization, the 
primary eutectic carbides coarsened and formed a continuous network, the secondary carbides 
precipitated and grew into blocky shape in dendrite interior (Fig.3(b)). The primary carbides of 
carburized tube with a width of approximately 10 μm precipitated on the dendrite boundaries in the γ-
matrix, and the blocky-shape precipitates of the secondary carbide with a mean size of 10μm distributed 
in dendrite interior(Fig.3(b)). There are many microcracks in the carburized tube, which will reduce the

## Page 8

mechanical properties. The microcracks appear at the boundary of carbides and the interface between 
carbide and matrix in different directions. There are some creep voids, which are signs of early creep 
damage in the carburized tube. The creep voids usually appear at the interface between carbides and 
matrix, and could reduce the creep strength. The carburized tube becomes brittle owing to the coarser 
primary carbides with a continuous network, blocky-shape secondary carbides and abundant microcracks.

After carburization, not only the paramagnetic structure changed into ferromagnetic, but also the 
condition of carbide phases varied. Due to the free carbon diffusion into the alloy, the M 23C6 carbides 
would transform into M 7C3 type carbide after carburization [7,9,40]. Figure 5 shows the carbides 
transformation of the carburized tube. There are three different zones in the carburized tube. In the inner 
region of the carburized tube, the M23C6 carbides were replaced by carbon-rich M7C3 carbides(Fig.5(a)). 
In the interface of carburization zone, many M23C6 carbides transformed into M7C3 carbides, but part of 
M23C6 carbides did not change at the edge of carbides(Fig.5(b)). There are some G phases mixed with 
M23C6 carbide in this zone(Fig.5(b)). In the outer part of the carburized tube, the microstructure is similar

## Page 10

to that of as-cast tube after aging (Fig.5(c)). The free carbon diffuses from inner surface of furnace tube 
to outer surface in service, so the carbon content of furnace tube gradually decreases from inner to outer 
wall. And the M7C3 carbides only occupy a small proportion. The main second phases are M23C6 carbides 
and G phases in the outer zone of carburized tube.
Figure 6 shows X-ray diffraction analysis of carburized furnace tube. The XRD analysis also show 
that the carbon-rich M7C3 carbides replaced the M23C6 carbides in the carburized furnace tube. And, the 
second phase Nb3Ni2Si formed in the carburized furnace tube.

3.3 Tensile and impact tests at room temperature
The mechanical properties measured by tensile tests and Charpy impact tests at room temperature 
are summarized in table 2. The carburized tube tensile tests demonstrated that the tensile strength has 
decreased from 530MPa to 360MPa, yield strength increased from 240MPa to 350MPa and elongation 
decreased from 18.5% to 1.7%. The absorption energy has decreased from 21J/cm 2 to 3J/cm 2. The 
ductility and toughness of carburized tube are very low. The mechanical properties of furnace tube at 
room temperature were decreased due to carburization. The plasticity and toughness of the cracking 
furnace material are exhausted after service.

The carbides are brittle materials with high hardness. After carburization, the size and volume 
fraction of carbides in carburized tube became bigger than as-cast tube (Fig.3). The ductility and 
toughness of the carburized tube decreased because of the coarser primary carbides, blocky-shape 
secondary carbides, abundant microcracks and creep voids. The mechanical properties of furnace tube at 
room temperature were decreased due to carburization. The carburized tube became brittle with lower 
ductility and toughness than as-cast tube. The brittle furnace tube material would lead to a premature 
failure. A sudden failure of the pyrolysis furnace tube would be occurred by the poor ductility and 
toughness, which is the tragic service failures for ethylene production.
The fracture surfaces of the tensile specimens and Charpy impact specimens of as-cast tube and 
carburized tube are shown in Fig.7. The as-cast tube and carburized tube specimens are all interdendritic 
fracture. Through the fracture, Figure 7 shows the dendritic morphology of the HPNb alloy tube.

However, through a detailed analysis of the fracture surfaces, it is find that there is a big difference 
between the fracture of the as-cast furnace tube and the carburized furnace tube. There are some cleavage 
fan pattern and tough dimple in the as-cast tube tensile high-magnification fracture morphology 
(Fig.7(a)). High-magnification fracture morphology of tensile specimens of carburized tube are cleavage 
fracture with fan pattern, which is indicative of brittle fracture (Fig.7(b)). High-magnification fracture

pattern. But there are more second microcracks on the entire fracture surface of carburized tube than as-
cast tube(Fig.7(c) and (d)). The differences of fracture morphology between as-cast and carburized tube 
also reveals that the ductility and toughness of carburized tube are lower than as-cast tube.

3.4 Tensile test at elevated temperature
Fig 8 shows the stress-strain curves of specimens of as-cast tube and carburized tube at 1173K. The 
tensile strength and yield strength of as-cast tube are 120MPa and 108MPa, which below the strengths 
of carburized tube. The flow stress of carburized tube increased and reached to 200Mpa, and its tensile 
elongation was approximately 16% which is lower than that of as-cast tube (42.4%). The tensile strength 
of carburized furnace tube at elevated temperature is higher than as-cast furnace tube. And the plasticity 
of the cracking furnace material at high temperature is decreased very quickly after carburization.

The coarser primary carbides at grain boundary and blocky-shape secondary carbides as the high-
temperature reinforce phases could improve the tensile strength and yield strength of carburized tube at 
elevated temperature. But the coarser primary carbides with a continuous network and abundant 
microcracks could reduce the ductility of carburized tube at high temperature. So the tensile elongation 
of carburized tube at high temperature is very low. The coarser primary carbides and blocky-shape 
secondary carbides have a significant effect on the room and high temperature tensile strength of the

HPNb alloy, which could reduce the room temperature tensile strength and improve the elevated 
temperature tensile strength. Carbides are brittle materials, so a great deal of big carbides can decrease 
the ductility of HPNb alloy at room and high temperature.
3.5 Creep test at elevated temperature
Fig 8 shows the creep strength of as-cast HPNb alloy tube and carburized HPNb alloy tube. The 
creep strength of as-cast tube is higher than the minimum rupture strength, while the creep strength of 
carburized tube (two test data) is below it. Typical creep strain and creep strain rate curves of as-cast 
tube and carburized tube at 1173K are respectively plotted in Figures 9(b) and(c). The creep rupture time 
of as-cast tube is longer than that of carburized tube at the 1173K, though the stress of as-cast tube 
(60MPa) is higher than carburized tube(55MPa). The as-cast tube and carburized tube exhibit an 
extended secondary or steady creep with significantly lower minimum creep rates in Fig.9(b) and (c). 
The secondary creep life in carburized tube accounted for more than 70 pct of its total creep life. These 
clearly show that the limited extent of primary creep and the protracted secondary stage. According to 
the results above, the creep strength of as-cast HPNb alloy is higher than carburized tube at high 
temperature. Figure 8(c) shows that the minimum creep rate of carburized tube is higher than as-cast 
tube, and the rupture time is shorter. It suggests that the carburized tube is apt to failure at service. 
These results show that the creep strength of carburized tube at high temperature is lower than as-
cast tube. That’s because the primary carbides of as-cast tube are skeleton shape at the grain boundary, 
and the primary carbides of carburized tube coarsened and formed a continuous network. The 
microcracks and creep voids appeared in the carburized tube also reduced the creep strength. The grain 
boundary twists and turns can prevent grain boundary slip and crack propagation, so the creep strength 
of as-cast tube with skeleton shape carbides is higher than the carburized tube with continuous network 
carbides. In general, with the grain boundary carbides coverage increases, the creep strength increases.

However, the carbides with a continuous network at grain boundary are beneficial to crack propagation 
and play a negative effect on the creep strength. And, there are many microcracks and creep voids 
appeared near the carbides. Though the carbides at grain boundary of the carburized tube are bigger and 
more than as-cast tube, the creep strength is still lower than as-cast tube.

According to results above, the paramagnetic structure of ethylene pyrolysis furnace tube changed 
into ferromagnetic after carburization. Meanwhile, the M 23C6 carbides were replaced by carbon-rich 
M7C3 carbides in the carburization zone. The primary carbides of carburized HPNb alloy tube with a 
continuous network and many blocky-shape secondary carbides can lead to the decrease of ductility 
and toughness and embrittlement of materials. There are many microcracks and creep voids in the 
total carbides of the ethylene pyrolysis furnace tube after carburization, which is a sign of early creep

## Page 16

damage. The material embrittlement and early onset of creep damage would lead to a premature failure 
of ethylene pyrolysis furnace tubes.
4. Conclusion 
The microstructural characterization and mechanical property degradation of the pyrolysis furnace 
tube after carburization were studied in this research. The brittleness of furnace tube, abundant 
microcracks and creep voids decreased the remnant life of the pyrolysis furnace tube. The following 
conclusions can be drawn:
(1) The paramagnetic structure of HPNb alloy changed into ferromagnetic structure after carburization.
(2) The primary carbides of HPNb alloy after carburization coarsened and formed a continuous network 
secondary carbides precipitated and grew into blocky shape in the dendrite interior. The M23C6 carbides 
would be replaced by carbon-rich M7C3 carbides in the carburization zone.
(3) The ductility and toughness of HPNb alloy at room temperature were very low and the furnace 
tubes became brittle after carburization.
(4) The tensile and yield strength of HPNb alloy tube increased and the ductility decreased at high 
temperature after carburization. The high temperature creep strength of HPNb alloy tube also decreased 
after carburization.
