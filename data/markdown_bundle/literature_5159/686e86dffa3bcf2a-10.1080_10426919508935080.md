Abstract 
INCONEL" alloy 617 is a solid solution, nickel-chromium-cobalt- 
molybdenum alloy with an exceptional combination of high-temperature strength, 
oxidation and carburization resistance and thermal stability. In this paper, the 
performance of alloy 617 in actual and simulated gas turbine environments will 
be presented. In addition some mechanical property data will be included to 
show the stability of the alloy after exposure to high temperatures for an extended 
period of time. For comparison the data obtained on such alloys as INCO' alloy 
HX, Haynes alloys HS188", 214" and 230" are also reported. (INCONEL and 
INCO are trademarks of the Inco family of companies. HS188, 214 and 230 
trademarks of Haynes International).

The room temperature tensile 
properties (RTT) of alloy 617 were 
measured in the transverse direction and 
are shown in Table 4. The results shown

rolled and solution annealed condition.

2.7 Low Cycle Fatigue and Stress 
Rupture Strength 
Figure 9 shows the effect of grain size 
on the fatigue strength of alloy 617. Table 
7 shows the fatigue data for stress 
controlled tension-tension experiments. It 
is clear that finer grain sizes improve the 
fatigue strength. However, as operation 
temperatures increase for better efficiency 
of gas turbines, an optimization of stress 
rupture and fatigue strength is essential . 
Grain sizes finer than 6 may have excellent 
fatigue strength, but may have poor creep 
strength. By the same token, a grain size 
of 0 or I may have excellent stress rupture

theearly stages of the alloy's development. 
However, coarse grain sizes result in lower 
fatigue strength as indicated by the trend 
in Figure 9.

3.0 Summary 
In this paper the outstanding 
oxidation, carburization and burner rig 
oxidation/sulfidation resistance of alloy 
61 7 was described and found to be superior 
when compared with other alloys such as 
HX, 188, 214 and 230 that are used in 
similar applications. Alloy 617 has been 
successfully used in hot gas path 
applications in aerospace and land based 
gas turbines for over two decades. 
To cope with the increasing inlet 
temperatures of advanced gas turbines, 
slight modifications in composition and 
extensive thermomechanical processing 
studies have been carried out to produce 
alloy 617 with an ASTM grain size of 3 
to 6. An ASRvl grain size of 3 to 6 gives 
the alloy good fatigue strength without 
compromising creep strength. This grain 
refinement ha:; also resulted in good room 
temperature strength and ductility. The 
combination of grain size, room
