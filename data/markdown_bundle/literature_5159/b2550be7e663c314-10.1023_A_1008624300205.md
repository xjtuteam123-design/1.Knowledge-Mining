Microstructure Development and Property
Improvement in Mill Products Produced from Ingot
Metallurgy and Powder Metallurgy Ni-Based
Superalloys of the Same Chemical Composition
O.KH. FATKULLIN, V .I. YERIOMENKO, O.N. VLASOVA AND N.N. KORNEYEVA
All-Russia Institute of Light Alloys, Gorbunov St., 2, Moscow 121596, Russia
Abstract. Despite projected inroads from competing materials Ni-based superalloys remain the material of
choice in the hot sections of advanced gas turbine engines. In this paper microstructural development and im-
provement of mechanical properties in mill products produced by ingot metallurgy (IM) and powder metallurgy
(PM) techniques are discussed. Particular attention is given to approaches to improvement in ingot ductility. Ther-
momechanical treatment schemes used during forging of a complex-alloyed Ni-based superalloy are recommended.
It is demonstrated that the PM technique used for production of this complex-alloyed superalloy is a promising
approach. On the basis of the studies carried out the salient features of the structure of material produced both by
both IM and PM techniques have been deﬁned. The mechanical properties of two versions of an advanced Ni-base
superalloy produced by IM and PM techniques are discussed. The best combination of mechanical properties are
exhibited by a PM material with a necklace structure; and this latter structure is superior to a ﬁne grained structure
for IM product also.
Keywords: ﬁne grained structure, ingot metallurgy (IM) alloys, isostatic homogenization treatment, mechanical
properties, “necklace” structure, powder metallurgy (PM) alloys, thermomechanical treatment

Introduction
Despite the promise of new materials (ﬁgure 1) [1] such as ceramics and intermetallics in
advanced gas turbine engines (ﬁgure 2) [2] superalloys are the materials of choice; and
are likely to remain so for the foreseeable future. Thus enhancement in behavior of the
superalloys has been a world-wide goal.
In this paper we report on recent work in Russia designated to enhance the perfor-
mance of a family of indigenous superalloys, with particular attention given to short-time
strength and stress-rupture strength of these complex-alloyed Ni-base superalloys. The

Speciﬁcally in the present paper we will report on studies undertaken to develop thermo-
mechanical treatment (TMT) schemes for the production of mill products of an advanced

The paper deals with a Ni-Cr-Co-W-Mo-Nb-Al-Ti alloy containing microadditions of B,
La, Mg, Ce, and other elements produced by the two techniques of IM and PM followed
by subsequent deformation. The alloy contains 45% of the strengthening γ
′ phase, the
temperature of complete dissolution of theγ′-phase (Tcdγ′ ) is 1170◦C.
The main problem which occurs in production of highly-alloyed superalloy semipro-
ducts is their poor ductility. In the case of IM alloys, this is caused by structural and
chemical inhomogeneities in the ingot; while in the case the PM alloys it results from
formation of microporosity in zones of large shear stresses localization, occurring during
deformation.
The following concepts formed the basis for development of the technological scheme
adopted for production of mill products from the IM and PM alloys, speciﬁcally development
of “tailor-made” structures:
1. Use of retained strain hardening effects during ﬁnal heat treatment.
2. Attainment of cooling rates during quenching in a particular range to ensure formation
ofγ
′-phase precipitates of the desired dispersion.
3. In the case of IM alloys, formation of a homogeneous billet structure using special
methods for reduction of the microchemical inhomogeneity of the ingot.
Material and experimental procedure
The IM materials used in this study was produced by vacuum-induction melting (VIM)
followed by vacuum-arc melting (V AR). To improve microchemical and structural homo-
geneity of the billets studied, an isostatic homogenization treatment (IHT) [3] and repeated
deformation with intermediate annealing was employed. Before deformation the ingots were
subjected to the IHT treatment at a pressure of 150–160 MPa. The deformation process was
carried out on a hydraulic press.
Powders produced by melt atomization followed by screening to a size fraction of−200
+ 50µm were used as the initial material for the PM alloy studies. PM compacts produced
by HIP’ing at a temperature of 1180–1190
◦C were then isothermally forged on a vacuum
press unit.
The subsequent thermomechanical treatment of the alloy produced by the two techniques
included modiﬁcations of the deformation processes at temperatures in the two-phase ﬁeld
and recrystallization annealings. This was then be followed by quenching from a tempera-
ture well below T
cdγ′ for the alloy to prevent further recrystallization. Table 1 shows TMT
and heat treatment (HT) conditions for the alloy under study.

Results
After repeated deformation at a temperature in the two-phase ﬁeld 70
◦C below Tcdγ′ fol-
lowed by an intermediate recrystallization annealings at Tcdγ′ and a ﬁnal deformation of
30% at a temperature 80 ◦C below Tcdγ′ a non-recrystallized structure is formed in the
IM alloy. This structure contains deformed grains in the range 150–250 µm with some
evidence of the initial stages of recrystallization. When the material is heated prior to
quenching at a temperature 100
◦C below Tcdγ′ , a necklace structure is formed, and the
degree of recrystallization increases to 5–15%; with the recrystallized grains 5–6 µm
in size.

non-homogeneity of this alloy is caused by the nonuniformity of the deformation processes
in various regions of the billet resulting from a heterogeneous ingot structure. In the ingot
there are coarse polygonized grains inside dendritic cells, and these grains show a low

ultraﬁne 10–20 nm spherical particles. Grains with precipitates of the ultraﬁne γ′ phase
have a polygonized structure. These regions apparently do not recrystallize, a result of the
chemical composition in these regions.
Metallographic examination of the PM alloy shows elongated deformed grains after
deformation of the material. The initial γ grains, of 50–80µm grain size, can be detected
due to the presence of coarseγ
′ phase precipitates and discrete ﬁne recrystallized grains at
boundaries.
Electron microscope examination of the as-deformed PM alloys shows that they have
mainly a polygonized structure, and regions exhibiting an increased dislocation density.
The polygonized structure is characterized by a subgrain size of 0.3–10 µm. Along with
the polygonized structure there are also regions where ﬁne recrystallized γ andγ
′ grains
can be observed, with the γ′ grains being much ﬁner (0.3–0.5 µm) than the γ grains
(1.0–1.5µm). Precipitates of the coarse γ′ phase particles can be observed at boundaries
of recrystallized grains and polygonized grains (ﬁgure 4(a)).
Heat treatment at a temperature 80◦C below Tcdγ′ followed by quenching and ageing
results in an increase in the amount of recrystallization. The recrystallized grains increase
in size to some extent, up to 3 µm, in comparison with those observed in the alloy after
deformation. The polygonized structure becomes better deﬁned (ﬁgure 4(b)), and a partially
recrystallized necklace-type structure can be observed.
Heat treatment of the PM alloy, followed by quenching from a temperature 10
◦C above
Tcdγ′ and ageing results in formation of a ﬁne-grained recrystallized structure, with a grain
size of 30–50µm.
The microstructural features of the IM and PM alloy described above strongly inﬂuence
the mechanical properties (Table 2). The PM material which has the necklace structure
shows the best combination of high strength and high-temperature strength, adequate duc-
tility, and no notch sensitivity. The IM alloy with a similar structure shows lower values of
strength and high-temperature strength.

Discussion
The TMT schemes studied include variations in the deformation operations carried out at
temperature in the two-phase ﬁeld, and recrystallization annealing followed by quenching
from temperature well below T
cdγ′ ; the latter to prevent an increased amount of recrystal-
lization. The TMT and subsequent heat treatment used result in formation of different ﬁnal
structures in the mill products studied. Two types of the “tailor-made” structures, namely
the necklace and UFG structures, formed in the MILL products studied are of great practical
interest.
According to the results of the studies, formation of the necklace structure depends on
the following factors:
(1) The thermomechanical treatment parameters at the ﬁnal stage of deformation.
(2) The temperature of the last recrystallization annealing before ﬁnal deformation.
(3) The temperature of heating prior to quenching.
The ﬁrst factor deﬁnes the type of structure formed before quenching, the second item
dictates the size of the deformed non-recrystallized grains, and the third parameter promotes
formation of a perfect polygonized structure.
Table 1 shows that in the case of the IM alloy, the TMT includes an increased number
of recrystallization annealing operations and deformation operations, alternating with each
other, than practiced for the PM alloy. This procedure was used to decrease the chemical
inhomogeneity inherited from the ingot.
The above analysis of the structural parameters and mechanical properties demonstrates
that improved mechanical property levels are obtained in material with a necklace structure
in comparison to the ﬁne-grained material.

uct, in which high strength and high-temperature are obtained in combination with no notch
sensitivity, can be explained as follows. The PM alloy has a homogeneous polygonized

Conclusions
This work demonstrated that a thorough knowledge of the important microstructural
features in controlling mechanical properties, in combination with carefully controlled pro-
cessing, can lead to improved properties in a highly alloyed complex NI-based superalloy.
Speciﬁcally:
1. The highest level of mechanical properties are shown in the PM material with a necklace
structure.
2. The necklace structure demonstrates enhanced material behavior over the ﬁne-grained
structure for both IM and PM alloys.
