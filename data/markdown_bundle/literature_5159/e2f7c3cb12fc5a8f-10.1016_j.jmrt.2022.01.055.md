abstract
The AlCrFe 2Ni2(MoNb)x multiphase high entropy alloys were designed, prepared and
characterized. The synergistic effect of Mo and Nb alloying on microstructures and me-
chanical properties of AlCrFe
2Ni2-based high entropy alloy were investigated. By syner-
gistic alloying with Mo and Nb, the volume fraction of the BCC phase increased, and a novel
Laves phase with hexagonal close-packed(HCP) structure was formed. With the increase of
Mo and Nb content, the alloys dramatically transformed from equiaxed grain with multiple
phases to dendrites with the spinodal decomposition structure of the A2 phase and B2
phase, as well as inter-dendrites with the eutectic structure of the FCC phase and Laves
phase. Furthermore, the solidiﬁcation behavior of the alloys varied considerably as the
increase of Mo and Nb content. The coexisting solidiﬁcation microstructure of the eutectic
structure and spinodal decomposition structure in the AlCrFe
2Ni2(MoNb)0.3, AlCrFe 2Ni2
(MoNb)0.5 and AlCrFe 2Ni2(MoNb)0.7 alloys have hardly been observed in other HEAs. The
increase of Mo and Nb elements tends to segregate in the front of the liquid esolid inter-
face, which hinders the growth of the FCC phase and leads to the formation of ﬁne rod-like
morphology. The synergistic effect of the ﬁne grain strengthening and the solid solution
strengthening resulted in high yield strength and fracture strength of 878 MPa and
2830 MPa, maintaining high plastic strain of 43.7%. Further increasing the content of Mo
and Nb would lead to the increase of brittle Laves phase, with the second phase
strengthening becoming the dominant strengthening mechanism, resulting in an increase
in yield strength to 1549 MPa and a decrease in plastic strain to 8.6%. The investigation
results would provide a guide for strengthening the macroscopic mechanical properties of
eutectic high entropy alloys through synergistic alloying effect.

1. Introduction
Traditional alloys are composed of one or two principal ele-
ments, and other minor metallic or nonmetallic elements are
generally added in order to improve the properties, such as
copper, iron, aluminum, etc. To break the design strategy of
traditional alloys, a few years ago, high entropy alloys (HEAs)
were proposed by Yeh et al. and Cantor et al. and have
attracted extensive research attention [ 1,2]. HEAs are initially
deﬁned as consisting of at least ﬁve principal elements with
equal or near-equal atomic ratios and show comprehensive
properties such as high strength and hardness, outstanding
corrosion, and wear resistance. These properties are closely
related to the high-entropy effect, lattice distortion effect,
sluggish diffusion effects and cocktail effect [ 1,3e6]. There-
fore, HEAs can be used as high temperature structural mate-
rials, corrosion resistant materials, wear resistant tools and
die materials.
According to the previous studies, most high entropy alloys
are dominated with solid solution phase. Some alloys with
single body-centered cubic (BCC) solid solution phase exhibit
high strength, but the plasticity is not high enough to meet the
expected application requirements. The alloys with single
face-centered cubic (FCC) phase have excellent plasticity but
relatively low strength. It has been reported that HEAs with
dual-phase or multi-phase can present dramatic plasticity
and strength through the reasonable design of composition
[4,7]. What 's more, most of the as-cast single-phase HEAs
exhibit chemical inhomogeneity and poor castability. There-
fore, casting large-scale HEAs (kilogram scale) is a great
challenge.
To compromise the strength, plasticity, and castability, Lu
et al. [ 8] ﬁrst proposed a novel HEAs, eutectic high entropy
alloys (EHEAs). AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 EHEA with excellent properties
at room temperature and high temperature was designed. The
fracture strength of AlCoCrFeNi 2.1 alloy can reach to 1.2 GPa,
and the fracture strain was 22.8%. Subsequently,
AleCoeCreFeeNi system alloys have received extensive
attention. AlCoCrFeNiTi
x [9], AlCoCrFeNiMo x [10],
AlCoCrFeNiCx [7], AlCoCrFeNiSi x [11] alloys have been inves-
tigated one after another. Although the above EHEAs exhibit
outstanding mechanical properties, most alloys contain the
costly Co element, which greatly limits their industrial appli-
cation. Furthermore, it is found that some HEAs without Co
element can also have excellent properties through adding
other alloying elements [ 12]. As a result, exploring Co-free
EHEAs with excellent properties and low cost is of great sig-
niﬁcance. On the basis of this design concept, four-component
AlCrFeNi
2 [13], Al0.9CrFeNi [14], AlCrFe2Ni2 [12] and Al1.3CrFeNi
[15] alloys were designed.
Dong et al. [ 16] have reported AlCrFeNiMo x alloys from
x ¼ 0t ox ¼ 1. With the increase of Mo content, the primary
phase transformed from the BCC phase to the sigma phase.
The compressive strength went up to 3.22 GPa, and the plastic
strain was up to 28.7%. Zhang et al. [ 17] have investigated the
effect of Nb content on properties of FeNiCoCrTi
0.5Nbx alloy
coating, and the FeNiCoCrTi 0.5Nb0.5 coating showed the best
wear resistance with a hardness of up to 852.5 HV.
In previous studies, the synergistic effect of two metallic
elements on the solidiﬁcation path of the alloy has been less
investigated. In this paper, we ﬁrst removed the costly Co
element and selected AlCrFe
2Ni2 as the matrix alloy, which
reduced the cost of the alloy and improved the industrial
application value of the alloy. Mo and Nb content was chosen
as addition elements. On the one hand, the addition of Mo and
Nb with large atomic size can lead to severe lattice distortion
in the high entropy alloy, resulting in a substantial solid

2. Experimental procedures
The purity of the raw material used in this paper is above
99.9%. AlCrFe
2Ni2(MoNb)x alloys (x ¼ 0, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5 and 0.7, and
the alloys are denoted by A00, A01, A03, A05 and A07, corre-
spondingly), are prepared to investigate the evolution of mi-
crostructures and properties. The samples are polished and
observed with SEM equipped with back-scattered electron
(BSE). The compositions of different phases are analyzed by
the SEM operated at energy dispersive spectrometer (EDS)
mode. The crystal structure is identiﬁed by an X-ray diffrac-
tometer (XRD, with Cu eK a radiation). The scanning rate is 5
deg/min, and the scanning range is from 30
/C14 to 100 /C14 . The
volume fractions of different phases are estimated using the
Image-Pro Plus software. The compressive test at room tem-
perature is conducted at the compression loading speed of
1.2 mm/min using MTS-Exceed 40 Universal tester. The
specimens with a diameter of 4 mm and a length of 6 mm are
obtained from alloy ingots by electron-spark wire-electrode
cutting. For the nanoindentation tests, the sample surface is
polished using electrolytic polishing at 38 V for 15 s in a
mixture of 90% glacial acetic and 10% perchloric acid. The
mechanical behavior of the HEAs is investigated using nano-
indentation (Micro Materials Ltd, Nanotest Vantage Alfa) with
a berkovich tip (radius 50 nm) at room temperature. The
indenter is held for 10 s with maximum loads 15 mN, and the
loading rate is 0.5 mN/s under constant load. The
loadedisplacement data is analyzed using Oliver-Pharr
method [ 19]. The tests are carried out until thermal drift re-
duces to 0.05 nm/s, and thermal drift correction is performed
at 10% of the maximum load. The Poisson 's ratio of alloys is
settled as 0.3. At least ﬁve tests are conducted at each testing
condition to ensure the reliability of the results [ 20].

Combined with the phase identiﬁcation, composition
analysis, and microstructure analysis, the solidiﬁcation
behavior of the AlCrFe
2Ni2(MoNb)x alloys can be assumed as
follows. In the A00 and A01 alloys, the conventional Wid-
manst€atten microstructure is formed by the diffusive growth
of FCC plates into the parent B2 phase grains. The FCC plates,
which originate at the B2 grain boundary, grow into parallel
distributed aggregates with a lamellar morphology and form a
pattern of intersecting plates. The growth of the Wid-
manst€atten microstructure requires a small undercooling
below the B2/FCC phase transformation temperature, so the
growth of the FCC phase occurs prior to the spinodal decom-
position of the B2 parent phase [28]. The remaining B2 phase is
located among the FCC plates and subsequently decomposes
spinodally into the A2 phase and the B2 phase. UVM was ﬁrst
reported by Dong et al. [ 12], but the solidiﬁcation formation
principle is unclear. Due to its ultraﬁne structure and layered
appearance, some authors have believed that it may be the
product of eutectic reaction. It has also been suggested that
the spinodal decomposition of the parent B2 phase triggers
the growth of the FCC phase, and the relevant transformation
pathway is B2 / FCC þ B2 þ A2. The morphology of the UVM
is peculiar in which the adjacent FCC phase is intertwined
with almost equal volume fraction of the spinodal decompo-
sition structure. In particular, in the A01 alloy, the added Mo
and Nb elements are easily repelled to the front of the
liquidesolid interface owing to the poor mutual solubility with
other elements during solidiﬁcation, which induces the in-
crease of the undercooling of the alloy and hinders the growth
of the FCC phase. It can be inferred that the addition of a small
amount of Mo and Nb content plays a role in grain reﬁnement.
The microstructure of the A03 alloy changes from equiaxed
crystal to dendrite crystal. In the solidiﬁcation, the B2 parent
phase is ﬁrst formed in the grain, and then the mixed struc-
ture of the FCC phase and the Laves phase precipitates at the
grain boundaries. When the temperature reaches the desired
temperature, spinodal decomposition occurs in the grains,
forming a biphasic spinodal decomposition structure with
periodic alternating arrangements. The A05 and A07 alloys
show typical dendrite morphologies. The Al, Ni-rich B2 phase
precipitates ﬁrst in the dendrites, so more Cr, Fe, and Nb ele-
ments are assigned to the remaining liquid phase, forming a
eutectic structure of Fe, Cr-rich FCC phase and Fe, Nb-rich
Laves phase in the inter-dendrites. During continuous cool-
ing, spinodal decomposition occurs in the dendrites (seen
Fig. 4 (gej)). It is noteworthy that in the A05 alloy, when the

phases differ signiﬁcantly, their segregation in the front of the
liquidesolid interface of the FCC phase would hinder the
continued growth of the FCC phase. Due to the bridging

increase of plasticity of the alloy. As the Mo and Nb content
increases from 0.1 to 0.7, the yield strength continues to
improve signiﬁcantly from 878 MPa to 1549 MPa, but simul-
taneously the plasticity decreases sharply from 43.7% to 8.6%.
The phenomenon is due to the high solid solubility of Mo and
Nb in the AlCrFe
2Ni2 alloy, which can lead to the precipitation
of the Laves phase with HCP structure at the grain boundaries.
During the deformation of the alloy, the interaction between
the Laves phase and dislocations impedes the dislocation
motion, which improves the deformation resistance of the
alloy with the effect of the second phase strengthening. The
slip system of the HCP structure is less than that of the FCC
structure, so the plasticity of the Laves phase is worse than
that of the FCC phase.

that the A01 alloy has excellent room-temperature mechani-

ties of different phases needs to be further explored.

4. Conclusion
In summary, The AlCrFe2Ni2(MoNb)x (x ¼ 0, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5 and 0.7)
multi-phase HEAs were designed and investigated. The in-
ﬂuence of Mo and Nb addition on the microstructures and
mechanical properties of the HEAs can be concluded as
follows:
I. There are FCC phase, A2 phase and B2 phase in the
AlCrFe2Ni2 alloy. The addition of Mo and Nb leads to the
transformation of microstructure from multiphase
grains to dendrites with the spinodal decomposition
structure of the A2 phase and B2 phase, as well as inter-
dendrites with a eutectic structure of the FCC phase and
Laves phase.
II. The coexisting solidiﬁcation microstructure of eutectic
structure and spinodal decomposition structure in the
A03, A05 and A07 alloys have hardly ever been observed
in other HEAs or even in conventional alloys. The added
Mo and Nb elements are easily repelled to the front of
the liquidesolid interface, which hinders the growth of
the FCC phase and forms a ﬁne rod-like morphology.
