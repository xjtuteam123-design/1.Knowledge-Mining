ABSTRACT 
In this work, we introduce a microstructure with high-density nano solute clusters by rolling at 100 
◦
C to effi -
ciently fabricate a high-strength ZK60 alloy plate. High yield strength of 336 MPa is achieved. The rolling process 
at 100 
◦
C facilitates the formation of high-density dislocations and nano substructures, which causes the for -
mation of high-density nano Zn solute clusters. The high strength is mainly attributed to the formation of high- 
density nano solute clusters, dislocations, and nano substructures. The finding of nano clusters shed light on 
designing high-strength Mg alloys, and the applied conventional rolling can be readily implemented in industrial 
production. 
1. Introduction 
Mg alloys have attracted worldwide attention for their low density 
and high specific strength [1,2] . Nonetheless, the relatively low strength 
limits their applications. The strengthening of Mg alloys commonly 
dependents on grain boundaries, dislocations, texture, and precipitates 
[3,4] . Recently, low-temperature processing is reported to facilitate to 
achieve high-strength Mg alloys by introducing a microstructure with 
strong texture, ultra-fine dynamically recrystallized (DRXed) grains, 
high-density dislocations, and solute clusters [5 – 8] . Therefore, low- 
temperature processing is suggested to be an efficient way to develop 
high-strength Mg alloys. ZK60 alloy is a commercially wrought Mg al -
loys that is widely accepted to have good ductility, which is favourable 
to low-temperature processing [9 – 11] . Wang et al. fabricated ZK60 alloy 
via a five-pass rolling at 200 
◦
C with a thickness reduction of 31 % per 
pass, which exhibits YS of 244 MPa, and elongation of 17.5 %, respec -
tively [9] . Regrettably, the existence of nano clusters is rarely mentioned 
and the strength was not so impressively high. In this work, a micro -
structure with high-density nano solute clusters is successfully intro -
duced in a ZK60 alloy by rolling at 100 
◦
C, and prominently high 
strength is achieved. The formation mechanism of high-density nano 
solute clusters and the related strengthening mechanisms are fully 
discussed. 
2. Experimental procedures 
A commercial extruded ZK60 (Mg-5.86Zn-0.54Zr, wt.%) alloy was 
employed. The samples with a length of 50 mm, a width of 20 mm, and a 
thickness of 3 mm were cut from extrusion bar and homogenized at 
400 
◦
C for 24 h. The rolling direction was parallel to extrusion direction. 
The as-homogenized samples were preheated at 200 
◦
C and 100 
◦
C for 
20 min and then rolled with thickness reduction of 70 % and 40 %, 
respectively. Based on our previous work, the rolling temperature of 
200 
◦
C and 100 
◦
C are chosen for facilitating the formation of high- 
density solute clusters and precipitates, respectively. The roller was 
heated to rolling temperatures for reducing temperature loss. The 
sample rolled at 200 
◦
C and 100 
◦
C are labelled as R200 and R100, 
respectively. Tensile samples were cut along rolling direction (RD). 
Uniaxial tensile tests were carried out with a strain rate of 10
-3 
s
 1 
at 
room temperature along RD. The microstructure was characterized by a 
FEI Nova 400 scanning electron microscope with Oxford HKL Channel 5 
electron backscattered diffraction (EBSD) detector with step size of 0.3 
µ m, JEOL JEM-2100F transmission electron microscope (TEM) operated 
at 200 kV, and Cs-corrected FEI Titan G2 60 – 300 ChemiSTEM operated 
at 300 kV.

3. Results 
Fig. 1 a shows the stress – strain curves of rolled samples. R100 sample 
exhibits a YS of 336 MPa, an UTS of 375 MPa, and an elongation of 5 %, 
while R200 sample shows a YS of 207 MPa, an UTS of 319 MPa, and an 
elongation of 17 %. Fig. 1 b shows that the YS of R100 sample is superior 
to the ZK alloys processed by rolling [9 – 18] . 
Fig. 2 shows the EBSD results. In R200 sample, the volume fraction 
and size of DRXed grains are 89 % and 1.4 µ m , respectively ( Fig. 2 a). 
There are almost no twins can be observed ( Fig. 2 b and c). The kernel 
average misorientation (KAM) map ( Fig. 2 d) shows that dislocation 
density in R200 sample is low. The pole figure ( Fig. 2 e) exhibits R200 
sample has typical basal texture of rolled ZK60 alloy with maximum 
multiple of uniform distribution (MUD) of 10. For R100 sample, the 
volume fraction and size of DRXed grains are 13 % and 0.5 µ m, 
respectively ( Fig. 2 f). Some twins can be observed in elongated grains 
( Fig. 2 g and h). The KAM map ( Fig. 2 i) shows that of dislocation density 
for R100 sample is higher than R200 sample. The pole figure ( Fig. 2 j) 
exhibits R100 sample has MUD of 12. 
The TEM images for R200 sample are shown in Fig. 3 a-c. The pre -
cipitates form mainly in elongated grains ( Fig. 3 a and b). The average 
size of the precipitates is 53 nm. From corresponding Fast-Fourier 
Transform (FFT) patterns ( Fig. 3 c), the precipitate is [1 1 0]-oriented 
C14 type MgZn
2 
Laves phase [19] . The TEM images for R100 sample 
are shown in Fig. 3 d-k. The number density and size of precipitates in 
R100 sample are less than R200 sample ( Fig. 3 d-e). The average size of 
precipitates is 24 nm ( Fig. 3 f). From the FFT patterns ( Fig. 3 g), the 
precipitates are also [1 1 0]-oriented C14 type MgZn
2 
Laves phase. The 
diffraction rings ( Fig. 3 h) prove that many nano substructures exist in 
R100 sample. Energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) mapping 
( Fig. 3 i-k) shows distribution of Zn and Zr atoms in elongated grains. The 
Zr atoms are only enriched in the precipitates. The Zn atoms enriched in 
the precipitates and segregate on dislocations ( Fig. 3 j). 
Fig. 4 a-c shows that Zn atoms segregated on dislocations, whereas Zr 
atoms show random distribution. The diffraction pattern for elongated 
grains shows satellite reflection spots ( Fig. 4 d), indicating that high- 
density Zn solute clusters exist in elongated grains. In comparison, sat -
ellite reflection spots are hard to be observed in the diffraction pattern 
for DRXed grains ( Fig. 4 e), which means that the clusters are mainly 
formed in elongated grains. To show more details of the nano clusters, 
HAADF-STEM imaging ( Fig. 4 f-h) and HRTEM imaging ( Fig. 4 i-k) were 
performed along < 0001 > zone axis. High-density rod-shape and block- 
shape clusters can be observed in grains. The rod-shape clusters have an 
average length and width of 10 nm and 0.5 nm, respectively. The block- 
shape clusters exhibit an average size of 2 nm and distribute randomly in 
elongated grains. 
4. Discussion 
The low rolling temperature of R100 sample inhibits the recovery of 
dislocation and DRX, which results in the formation of a high-volume 
fraction of elongated grains with high-density dislocations and nano 
substructures, which facilitate the segregation of Zn atoms [6,15] . On 
the other hand, the low rolling temperature of R100 sample limits the 
diffusion rate of Zn atoms. Therefore, Zn atoms can only diffuse to dis -
locations and nano substructures nearby and then form high-density 
solute clusters ( Fig. 4 a-e). 
Compared with R100 sample, R200 sample has higher volume 
fraction of DRXed grains, which has stronger strengthening effect from 
grain boundaries. Because the MUD of texture in R200 sample is close to 
R100 sample, it is reasonable to suggest that texture strengthening effect 
should be similar. However, the YS and UTS of the R100 sample are 129 
MPa and 56 MPa higher than that of R200 sample, respectively. It is 
reasonable to conclude that the strong strengthening effect caused by 
high-density dislocations, nano substructures, and nano clusters can 
compensate the loss in strengthening effect from grain boundaries, and 
precipitates. Especially, the high-density solute clusters can provide 
strong dragging effect on the dislocation motion, which plays crucial 
role in enhancing the strength. Differently, there is no clusters in R200 
sample because of a limited number of dislocations for segregation. 
Therefore, the strength of R200 sample depends mainly on grain 
boundaries strengthening and precipitation strengthening, and the high 
strength of R100 sample should be mainly attributed to high-density 
nano clusters, dislocations, and nano substructures. 
5. Conclusions 
In summary, a high-strength ZK60 alloy plate is successfully fabri -
cated by introducing a microstructure with high-density nano Zn solute 
clusters. The high YS of 336 MPa and UTS of 375 MPa are achieved. The 
high-density dislocations and nano substructures in R100 sample facil -
itate the segregation of Zn atoms, which results in the formation of high- 
density nano solute clusters. The strong strengthening effect caused by 
high-density dislocations, nano substructures, and nano clusters can 
compensate the loss in the strengthening effect from grain boundaries 
and precipitates.
