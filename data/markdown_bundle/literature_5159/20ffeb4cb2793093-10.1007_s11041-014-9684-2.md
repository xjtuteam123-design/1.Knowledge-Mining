DEVELOPMENT OF ION-PLASMA REFRACTORY METALLIC LAYERS
OF HEAT-INSULATING COATINGS FOR COOLED TURBINE ROTOR BLADES
S. A. Budinovskii, 1 S. A. Muboyadzhyan, 1 A. M. Gayamov, 1 and P. V . Matveev1
Translated from Metallovedenie i Termicheskaya Obrabotka Metallov, No. 12, pp. 16 – 21, December, 2013.
Alloys ZhS32 and ZhS36 with heat-protection coatings (HPC) consisting of external ceramic layers and inter-
nal refractory metallic layers are studied. The coated alloys are tested for heat fastness and high-temperature
strength at 1100 – 1150°C. The HPC with a metallic layer of the (Ni – Cr – Al – Hf) + Al system are shown to
be more advantageous than similar HPC based on an SPD-2 + VSDP-16 commercial refractory coating. The
effect of the HPC on the characteristics of high-temperature strength and fatigue resistance of alloys ZhS32
and ZhS36 is investigated.
Key words: heat-protection coatings, heat-resistant ion-plasma coatings, refractory castable nickel
alloys.
INTRODUCTION
Deposition of heat-protection coatings (HPC) onto cooled
rotor blades of gas turbine engines is the most effective
method for raising their technical characteristics including
the service life and the temperature of the working gas of the
turbine. Such coatings consist of an external ceramic layer
(CL) and an internal metallic layer (ML). The ceramic layer
has a low thermal conductivity and should provide consider -
able lowering of the temperature on the surface of the blade
tongue as compared to the temperature of the turbine gas.
The internal metallic layer protects the surface of the tongue
from high-temperature oxidation [1, 2, etc.].
However, the thirty-year experience of operation of HPC
with zirconia CL stabilized by yttrium oxide (ZrO
2 –7 %Y 2O3
denoted YSZ in what follows) has shown some problems
that require solution for further development of gas turbine
engineering [3]. An analysis of many published experimental
data on active HPC of type Me – Cr – Al – Y + YSZ and
PtAl + YSZ performed in [4] has shown that the intensity of
some processes on the CL/c47ML interface grows with the tem-
perature, and this results in chipping of the CL under cyclic
or isothermal heating, namely, after a hold of 100 – 150 h at
1200°C, after a hold of 400 – 500 h at 1050°C, and after a
hold of 1000 h at 1000°C.
Oxidation of the surface of the ML, which yields metal-
lic oxides, primarily of aluminum, results in growth of nor-
mal stresses and detachment of the CL [5]. Growth in the
stresses on the CL/c47ML interface is connected with the devel-
opment of a number of processes such as sintering of the ce-
ramics, which increases its modulus of elasticity, lowering of
the strength of cohesion between the ceramics and the metal
due to changes in its chemical composition at high tempera -
tures, and erosion damage by the incident gas flow. When the
oxide film grows to a thickness of 8 – 10 mm, the ceramic
layer on the ceramics /c47metal interface breaks. The breakage
of the CL is accelerated by the presence of stress concentra -
tors on the surface of the ML in the form of surface rough -
ness and contamination and under the action of plastic defor-
mation of the aluminide ML under thermocycling that yields
wavelike structures on the surface when the phase composi -
tion of the layer changes ( /c98/c171/c103 /c162+ /c103). The main driving
force of all the mentioned processes is the high temperature.
Therefore, the development of new ceramic materials with
low thermal conductivity (below 1 W /c47(m /c215K)) is expedient
not only for raising the heat-protection effect on the surface
of turbine rotor blades but also for prolonging the service life
of the HPC.

to be promising materials for HPC. Such zirconates have a
thermal conductivity of about 1 W /c47(m /c215K) and a working

At the same time, studies aimed at advancing the compo-
sitions and structures of the metallic layers of HPC are virtu-
ally lacking. This seems to be connected with the fact that
both in Russia and in other countries ML are commonly pro-
duced from commercial refractory aluminide coatings for
turbine rotor blades without allowance for the special fea -
tures of operation of heat-protection coatings as multilayer
systems.
The requirements on the ML of HPC differ noticeably
from the requirements on refractory coatings. The ML should
not only protect the surface from oxidation but also serve a
maximally durable barrier for detachment of the CL. At high
temperatures the diffusion interaction between the ML and
the protected alloy results in penetration of the refractory
metals into the layer. Formation of oxides of refractory me -
tals on the surface of ML increases the thickness and disturbs
the continuity of the “gluing” film of Al
2O3. Therefore, in
addition to such important properties as refractoriness and
heat resistance, the ML should exhibit a low rate of oxidation
and a low thermal coefficient of linear expansion for lower -
ing of the thermal stresses on the CL /c47ML interface. From
this standpoint a nickel monoaluminide alloyed with plati -
num, hafnium, zirconium, and chromium and having an al -
most stoichiometric composition looks promising for creat -
ing ML [8].
The aim of the present work was to develop refractory
metallic layers for heat-protection coatings for cooled rotor
blades of aircraft gas turbine engines from refractory nickel
alloys.
METHODS OF STUDY
We studied specimens with single-crystal structure with
orientation /c225001/c241fabricated from refractory nickel alloys
ZhS32 and ZhS36.
Due to the possibility of detachment of the external ce-
ramic layer we chose the composition and the structure for
the internal metallic layer of the HPC bearing in mind that it
should preserve its protective properties for a long enough
time at the temperatures to which the HPC is heated. Other-
wise, fracture and detachment may cause catastrophic failure
of the tongue of the rotor blade. In this connection, we stu-
died ML with a structure similar to that of combined conden-
sation-diffusion coatings including commercial SDP-2 +
VSDP-16 compositions that preserve their protective proper-
ties up to a temperature of 1200°C [9] and are used to protect
turbine blades of civil and military aircraft engines. The coat-
ings are deposited in two stages. In the first stage the surface
of the part is coated with a layer of the nickel alloy and in the
second stage with a layer of the aluminum alloy; then both
layers are subjected to a heat treatment in vacuum.
The high refractoriness of two-layer condensation-diffu -
sion coatings in the range of up to 1200°C is explainable by
the special features of the phase structure of the layers. The
external layer of the coating based on alloyed nickel monoalu-
minide (/c98-phase) possesses a high high-temperature strength.
The internal condensed layer represented by a multi -
component nickel alloy of the Ni – Cr – Al – Y system and
having a complex phase composition (a nickel-base /c103-solid
solution, a /c103/c162-phase based on Ni
3Al, and a chromium-base
/c97-solid solution) hinders penetration of the refractory ele -
ments from the matrix into the layer of the /c98-phase in the
process of formation of the coating and provides its high
thermal stability during the subsequent high-temperature ex -
posure. Together, the internal and external layers raise the
protective properties of the ‘refractory alloy – refractory
coating’ composition above the level of the known aluminide
coatings including platinum-aluminide ones.

All the ML had a thickness of 80 – 90 /c109m and were ob-

The external (ceramic) layers of the HPC 40 – 45 /c109m

(cathodes) 100 /c180440 mm in size from zirconium alloys of

The composition of the target alloy was determined by the
type of the ceramics used for depositing the CL of the HPC.
The facility contained four pairs of planar magnetrons. Each
pair of magnetron evaporators was fed by an inverter source
with parameters 600 V , 30 A and a dual switch that changed
the polarity of the feeding voltage from one magnetron to the
other (opposite) at a frequency of 40 kHz. Such switching
provided stable operation of the magnetrons, the surface of
the target cathodes of which was oxidized during sputtering
in an oxygen-argon mixture.
The ML (without ceramics) were tested for the high-tem-
perature strength in accordance with the GOST 6130–71
Standard for specimens 25.4 mm in diameter and 3 mm
thick. The high-temperature strength of the ML was evalu -
ated in terms of the specific change in the mass [ /c68m/c47S
(g/c47m2 ), where /c68m is the mean arithmetic value of the change
in the mass in a test of five specimens with coatings of the
same type and S is the area of the specimen].
Tests of the HPC for heat resistance under the conditions
of cyclic heating were also performed for disk specimens on
a laboratory bench at 1100 and 1150°C. A test cycle included
heating and holding of the specimen at the tested temperature
for 50 min and subsequent cooling at room temperature for
10 min to /c163200°C.
The characteristics of long-term strength were deter -
mined with the help of a ZST2/c473-VIÉT device in accordance
with the GOST 10145 Standard at 1000°C for up to 500 h;
the fatigue resistance was determined with the help of an
MVI-611M testing machine in accordance with the
GOST 25.502 standard at 900°C for 2 /c180107 cycles.

strength computations in design of gas turbine engines. The

this characteristic. However, approximation of the experi -
mental data obtained by logarithmic lines of the trends shows
that after more than 107 cycles the fatigue limit of the alloys
with HPC tends to decrease under the chosen test conditions
(Fig. 5). It should be noted that the decrease is less obvious
for alloy ZhS32, because the tests of the specimens without
ceramic layer but with a sublayer of ML-2 + Al give higher
results than the tests of the uncoated specimens (Fig. 5 a ).
This means that the characteristics of fatigue resistance of
the compositions studied can be raised by improving the
structure of the HPC, in particular, by choosing an optimum
proportion of the thicknesses of the internal metallic layer
and the external ceramic layer with allowance for the thick-
ness of the wall of the actual gas turbine engine blade [12].
CONCLUSIONS
1. We have studied the heat fastness and heat resistance
of heat-protective coatings with refractory internal metallic
layers of the Ni – Cr – Al – Hf + Al system and external ce -
ramic layers of the Zr – Y – (La, Gd, Pr, Dy) – O system.
2. The heat-protective coating of ML-2 (80 /c109m )+A l
(60 g/c47m
2 )+( Z r–Y–G d )–O( 4 3 /c109m) is more than thrice
superior to standard SDP-2 (80 /c109m) + VSDP-16 (75 g/c47m2 )+
( Z r–Y–G d )–O( 4 3 /c109m) coatings with respect to the num-
ber of cycles before detachment of ceramic layer after heat -
ing to from 1100 to 1150°C and subsequent air cooling.
3. Deposition of a protective coating of ML-2 (80 /c109m) +
Al (60 g/c47m2 )+( Z r–Y–G d )–O( 4 3 /c109m) does not lower
the guaranteed characteristics of long-term strength of alloys
ZhS32 and ZhS36 at 1000°C for a test base of 500 h and
lowers by 3 – 5% their fatigue limit at 900°C after
2 /c18010
7 cycles, which can be corrected by optimizing the
proportion of the thicknesses of the metallic and ceramic
layers of the coating.
