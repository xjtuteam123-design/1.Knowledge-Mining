ABSTRACT
Mo is a key solid solution strengthening element that improves the mechanical 
properties of Ni-based superalloys, but the partitioning behavior of Mo remains 
controversial. Herein, we uncover the effect of Mo addition on the precipitation 
kinetics of the γ′ phase in Ni–(17− x) Al–x Mo at.% ( x = 2, 3, 4, and 5) alloys by 
directly coupling accurate thermodynamic and kinetic descriptions with a phase-
field model. Our simulations show that the addition of Mo would decrease the γ′ 
coarsening rate due to the decrease in the diffusion rate and the γ/γ′ lattice misfit. 
Besides, the partitioning behavior of Mo is reversed from the γ′ phase ( K𝛾�∕𝛾
Mo > 1 ) 
into the γ matrix ( K𝛾�∕𝛾
Mo < 1 ) with increasing Mo concentration, which is attributed 
to the solubility limit of Mo in the γ′ phase as well as a decrease in the γ/γ′ lat-
tice misfit. Meanwhile, Al and Mo preferentially occupy the Al sublattice site of 
the γ′–Ni3Al and form the Ni3(Al, Mo) phase in Ni–Al–Mo ternary model alloys. 
Furthermore, the solid solution strengthening effect increases with increasing 
Mo concentration because of an increase in the Mo concentration in the γ matrix. 
Our results provide valuable insights into understanding the effect of Mo addi-
tion to Ni-based superalloys on the coarsening kinetics of γ′ phase and elemental 
partitioning behavior.
Introduction
Ni-based superalloys have been widely used as the 
key hot-end components in aircraft engines due to 
their excellent microstructural stability and mechani-
cal properties at elevated temperatures [1 ]. Optimum 
mechanical properties can be achieved by microstruc-
tural modification, which is fundamentally deter-
mined by the composition of Ni-based superalloys. 
In the last few decades, significant amounts of refrac-
tory elements, including Mo, W, Ta, and Re, have been 
added to the Ni matrix to improve the microstructural

stability and mechanical properties of Ni-based super-
alloys [2 ]. Among these alloying elements, Re is the 
most effective alloying element for improving the 
mechanical properties of Ni-based superalloys due to 
its high melting point, low diffusion coefficient, and 
high partitioning ratio in the γ matrix [3 –5]. Unfor-
tunately, Re is a scarce strategic element and is sub-
ject to speculation in the financial markets, resulting 
in potentially high alloying costs [6 ]. In addition, the 
addition of higher levels of Re can cause other prob-
lems, including poor oxidation resistance [7] and brit-
tleness due to the formation of brittle topological close-
packed (TCP) phases [8]. Therefore, the development 
of low-Re and Re-free superalloys without compro -
mising the original properties of the alloy has been the 
subject of considerable research interest [9 ].
It has been reported that Mo is an important 
strengthening element with a lower price and density 
than Re, which could be a potential substitute for Re 
[10–13]. Besides, it has been demonstrated that the 1.6 
wt.% of Mo is required to replace 1 wt.% of Re in the γ 
matrix phase to achieve the same solid solution hard-
ening efficiency [3 ]. Meanwhile, the addition of Mo 
can reduce the microsegregation of W and Re [14– 16] 
and increase the partitioning ratio of Re in the γ matrix 
[17–19]. In addition, it is well-known that the morphol-
ogy, size, volume fraction, and distribution of the γ′ 
phase influence the strength and creep properties of 
Ni-based superalloys [20]. Unfortunately, the γ′ coars-
ening would degrade the mechanical properties of 
alloys during the long-term service process [21, 22]. It 
has been reported that the addition of Mo can slightly 
reduce the γ′ phase coarsening rate in Co-based alloys 
due to the reduction in the γ/γ′ lattice misfit [23 , 24]. 
However, the addition of Mo can increase the coarsen-
ing rate of the γ′ phase in Ni-based superalloys due to 
the increase in diffusion rate [25]. Therefore, it is essen-
tial to study the effect of Mo addition on the coarsen-
ing kinetics of the γ′ phase in Ni-based superalloys.
Particularly, the creep resistance of Ni-based 
superalloys depends strongly on the γ/γ′ lattice mis-
fit. For instance, the rupture life at 1373 K/137 MPa of 
TMS-138 and TMS-75(+ Ru) superalloys is tr = 412 h 
and tr = 143 h, respectively, which is attributed to the 
different lattice misfit in the former (  /u1D6FF= 0.33% ) and 
the latter (  /u1D6FF= 0.16% ) [26– 28]. Besides, the previous 
literature has shown that the addition of Mo facili-
tates the formation of a dense and regular interfa-
cial dislocation network due to the larger γ/γ′ lattice 
misfit, thereby hindering dislocation motion and 
improving the mechanical properties of Ni-based 
superalloys [18, 26, 29, 30]. It is well-known that the 
γ/γ′ lattice misfit is determined by the elemental par-
titioning behavior between the γ matrix and the γ′ 
phase. However, the partitioning behavior of Mo in 
Ni–Al–Mo ternary model superalloys remains con-
troversial. For instance, Mo is preferentially enriched 
in the γ′ phase in Ni–16 Al–1.7 Mo at.% model super-
alloy [31, 32]. On the contrary, Mo is preferentially 
partitioned into the γ matrix in Ni–15 Al–5 Mo at.% 
alloy [33] and Ni–6.5 Al–9.9 Mo at. % model superal-
loys [34]. Therefore, it is indispensable to unveil the 
physical origin of the partitioning behavior of Mo in 
Ni–Al–Mo ternary model superalloys.
In this study, the CALPHAD-assisted phase-field 
simulations are carried out to uncover the effect of 
Mo addition on the precipitation kinetics of the γ′ 
phase in Ni–(17−x ) Al–x Mo at.% (x  = 2, 3, 4, and 5) 
ternary model superalloys aged at 1173 K. Mean-
while, the thermodynamic and kinetic database 
was used to obtain accurate free energy and diffu-
sion mobility. Our simulations show that the addi-
tion of Mo induces a lower γ′ coarsening rate due to 
the lower diffusion rate and the γ/γ′ lattice misfit. 
Besides, the partitioning behavior of Mo is related to 
the Mo concentration, with Mo tending to partition 
from the γ′ phase to the γ matrix with increasing Mo 
concentration. Further analysis shows that the rever-
sal of the partitioning behavior of Mo is due to the 
solubility limit of Mo in the γ′ phase and the reduc-
tion of the γ/γ′ lattice misfit. Our work is valuable 
for the design of novel low-Re Ni-based superalloys 
with superior microstructural stability and mechani-
cal properties.

1. The γ′ morphology is the cubic shape in Ni–(17-x) 
Al–x Mo at.% (x  = 2, 3, 4, and 5) alloys due to the 
larger γ/γ′ lattice misfit (0.2% ~ 0.5%). The decrease 
in γ′ coarsening rate with increasing Mo concentra-
tion is believed to be due to the combined effect of 
lower diffusivity and lower γ/γ′ lattice misfit.
2. In Ni–Al–Mo ternary model superalloys, the par-
titioning behavior of Mo depends on the Mo con-
centration, with Mo preferentially enriched in the 
γ′ phase at low concentrations and in the γ matrix 
at high concentrations, resulting from the solubil-
ity limit of Mo in the γ′ phase and the reduction 
of the γ/γ′ lattice misfit. Meanwhile, Mo preferen-
tially occupies the Al site of γ′-Ni3Al in Ni–Al–Mo 
ternary model superalloys.
3. The volume fraction of the γ′ phase decreases with 
increasing Mo concentration in Ni–(17-x) Al–x Mo 
at.% (x  = 2, 3, 4, and 5) alloys, which is ascribed 
to the change in elemental partitioning behavior 
between the γ matrix and the γ′ phase.
4. The solid solution strengthening effect of the γ 
matrix gradually increases with increasing Mo 
concentration in Ni–Al–Mo ternary model super-
alloys due to more Mo partitions to the γ matrix.
