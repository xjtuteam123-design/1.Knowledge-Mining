ABSTRACT 
A preferentially-aligned lamellar structure in TNM alloy with enhanced strength-ductility synergy is successfully 
obtained by texturing the high-temperature α phase through hot extrusion. The lamellar orientations of the 
resultant lamellar colonies inherit the texture feature of the extruded high-temperature α phase ({0001} basal 
fiber texture), exhibiting a preferential distribution with 60% of the lamellar traces {0001} α /{111} γ nearly 
parallel to the extrusion direction (ED). The influence of the microstructural variables on the deformation 
mechanisms and fracture mechanisms are elucidated by microstructural characterization and crystallographic 
analysis. Results show that when the lamellar trace is parallel to the tensile direction (TD//ED), twinning and 
stacking faults in the γ laths are favorable to be activated. The origin of the enhanced strength-ductility synergy is 
attributed to the combined effects of twinning, stacking faults and its induced dislocation slip, as well as the extra 
interaction stresses introduced by the multiple deformation mechanisms and the α
2
/ γ interfaces. This study can 
provide a novel strategy for microstructural regulation and design of high-performance β -solidifying TiAl alloys. 
1. Introduction 
TiAl alloys have been deemed an optimal substitute for superalloy 
turbine blades in aerospace and automotive engine since it possesses 
early half the density of superalloys and better overall performance at 
650~800 
◦
C [ 1 – 3 ]. Especially, the new β -solidifying TiAl alloy, TNM 
alloy with the nominal component of Ti-43.5Al – 4Nb – 1Mo-0.1B (at. %), 
has been used for turbine blades in the PW1100G ™ engines [ 4 , 5 ]. 
Considering the structural characteristics and the load distribution in 
service environment, the blades will show outstanding performance if 
TiAl alloy component exhibits a lamellar structure with the lamellae 
aligned along the radial direction of blade body [ 6 , 7 ]. It derives from the 
fact that TiAl alloys have a good balance of strength and plasticity when 
the lamellar orientation is parallel to the loading axis [ 8 , 9 ]. To improve 
the overall performance and popularize the application of β -solidifying 
TiAl alloys, the development of a technique to optimize the lamellar 
structure, i.e., lamellar orientation control, has been of continuous 
concern. 
Conventionally, the method to control lamellar orientation is direc -
tional solidification process [ 10 – 12 ]. The main difficulty in the lamellar

contribute to better design strategy of high-performance β -solidifying 
TiAl alloys.

4. Discussions 
4.1. Origin of enhanced strength-ductility synergy 
The mechanical property of TNM alloy is the result of the operation 
of deformation mechanisms, which are affected by the lamellar structure 
features, such as the colony size, lamellar spacing, lamellar orientation, 
and the volume fraction of different phases. As demonstrated in section 
3.1 , compared with the microstructure of the HTed TNM alloy, the 
microstructure characteristics of the EHTed TNM alloy are reflected in 
the three aspects: i) the increased volume fraction of the γ blocks; ii) the 
slightly refined but inhomogeneous colony size; iii) the preferred 
lamellar orientation. In the following, the interplay between these 
microstructural features and the mechanical properties are discussed. 
From the point of view of the γ blocks, the main deformation 
mechanism is twinning during the tensile process, as seen in Fig. 7 . The 
twin formation can relieve the local stress concentration induced by the 
incompatible inelastic strains, inhibit the crack formation, and increase 
the deformation of the material before fracture [ 35 , 36 ], however, which 
in turn lead to a reduction in the strength of the alloy. 
Generally speaking, the reduction in colony size is beneficial to the 
strength and ductility of TiAl alloy at room temperature [ 37 – 39 ]. This is 
due to the fact that as the grain size decreases, the volume of defects such

aggregated in the γ laths ( Fig. 9 ). The shear directions of twins and 
stacking faults are inclined to the lamella interfaces with a certain angle, 
thus the lamellar interfaces have a strong hindering effect on the shear 
deformation of twinning and stacking faults, which lead to the existence 
of high shear deformation in front of the shear band. The local stress 
concentrated near lamellar interfaces could activate other slip systems

is inclined or perpendicular to the TD, the shear direction generated by 
the dislocation motion is consistent with the lamellar orientation, as 
shown in Fig. 11 (b). Once the stress concentration generates micro -
cracks at the lamella boundaries, the microcracks will not be hindered 
by the lamellar interface, so they can rapidly propagate forward with 
little resistance. In this case, as soon as microcracks are formed, the 
specimen will break up quickly. Consequently, it can be concluded that 
the preferred lamellar orientation paralleling to the TD of the EHTed 
TNM alloy is the main reason for the improvement of its strength- 
ductility synergy.

5. Conclusions 
The nearly lamellar-structured TNM alloy with preferred orientation 
was successfully obtained by texturing the high-temperature α phase 
through hot extrusion, showing enhanced strength-ductility synergy. 
The main conclusions are summarized as follows: 
(1) The high-temperature α phase in the TNM alloy exhibit a basal 
fiber texture {0001}//ED after extruded at 1290 
◦
C with the 
extrusion ratio of 7.11. Accordingly, the lamellar orientations of 
the resultant lamellar colonies inherit the texture feature of the 
as-extruded high-temperature α phase, showing a preferential 
distribution with 60% of the lamellar traces {0001} α /{111} γ 
nearly parallel to the ED. This proves that it is a feasible strategy 
for lamellar structure optimization by regulating the high- 
temperature α phase. 
(2) Compared with the HTed TNM alloy, the strength and ductility of 
the EHTed TNM alloy at RT and high temperature have been 
significantly improved. The UTS, YS and El at room temperature 
are 930 ± 4.2 MPa, 868 ± 24.0 MPa and 1.0 ± 0.02%, respectively. 
At 800 
◦

and the El increase to 6.7 ± 0.21%. 
(3) The origin of enhanced strength-ductility synergy for the EHTed 
TNM alloy is mainly attributed to the preferred lamellar orien -
tation. The γ laths in the lamellar colonies with their lamellar 
interfaces nearly parallel to TD are more conducive to the acti -
