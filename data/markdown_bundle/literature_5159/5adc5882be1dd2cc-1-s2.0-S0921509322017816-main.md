ABSTRACT 
The microstructure and tensile behavior of GH3535 and 2 wt% niobium (Nb) modified GH3535 alloys at room 
temperature (RT) and 750 
◦
C were systematically investigated. It is found that Nb element is distributed in both 
matrix and M
6
C carbide, while the type and content of carbide in GH3535 alloy are barely affected by Nb 
element. The tensile properties at RT and 750 
◦
C are improved by adding Nb element, which can be ascribed to 
the synergistic effect of the solid solution strengthening, and nano twins strengthening. Additionally, they will be 
improved by grain refinement at RT. The strengths of both alloys at 750 
◦
C are lower than that of RT, which is 
related to the softening caused by dislocations climbing and softening of grain boundaries at high temperature. 
1. Introduction 
The Ni – 16Mo – 7Cr-based superalloy GH3535 (UNS N10003 alloy), 
has been used as the primary structural material in the molten salt 
reactor (MSR) due to its superior mechanical properties at high tem -
peratures, excellent resistance to high-temperature oxidation, molten 
fluoride salts, and neutron irradiation [ 1 – 3 ]. The typical microstructure 
of GH3535 alloy, a solid solution nickel-based superalloy, consists of 
austenite γ matrix and M
6
C carbide [ 4 ]. Because of the continued in -
crease in MSR ’ s operating power, the performance of key structural 
materials, especially the mechanical properties and resistance to 
neutron irradiation, must be enhanced to fulfill increasing service 
requirements. 
Composition optimization is an effective method to improve the 
properties of superalloys. Niobium (Nb) element is a widely-used addi -
tive element in wrought nickel-based superalloys. The resistance to 
irradiation and mechanical properties of alloys can be improved by the 
addition of Nb element by promoting Ni
3
Nb- γ " phase or NbC carbide 
[ 5 – 7 ]. The most notable example is the Inconel 718 alloy containing ~5 
wt% Nb, whose γ " phase is beneficial to the comprehensive properties 
[ 8 , 9 ]. The researchers of Oak Ridge National Laboratory carried out a 
series of studies to increase the resistance to irradiation in UNS N10003 
alloy [ 7 , 10 ]. It was found that the resistance to irradiation of UNS 
N10003 alloy was increased by decreasing Mo content to 12 wt% and 
adding 2 wt% Nb. They speculated that the dispersed distributed MC 
carbide can effectively trap the helium atoms to increase the resistance 
to irradiation [ 7 ]. 
However, as the main solid-solution strengthener in UNS N10003 
alloy, more Mo content should be a blessing for mechanical perfor -
mance, but unfortunately, the influence of Nb element on UNS N10003 
alloy with a high concentration (16 wt%) of Mo element has not been 
reported. Whether the microstructure (grain size, carbide type, etc.) and 
the properties (mechanical, resistance to irradiation, etc.) of the alloy 
can be affected in UNS N10003 alloy by only adding Nb element is still 
unclear. Therefore, it is necessary to investigate the effect of the Nb 
element on the microstructure and mechanical property of UNS N10003 
alloy (with 16 wt% Mo), especially, to clarify the micro mechanism of 
the effect of Nb element. It is essential to understand the role of the Nb 
element, which can provide a theoretical fundamental for the compo -
sition optimization and irradiation resistance improvement of UNS 
N10003 alloy. 
In this work, the tensile experiments of GH3535 and 2 wt% Nb 
modified GH3535 (Nb-GH3535) alloys were performed at room tem -
perature (RT) and 750 
◦
C. The microstructures and tensile behavior of 
two alloys are analyzed by the scanning electron microscope (SEM) and 
transmission electron microscopy (TEM). The effect of Nb element on 
the microstructures and tensile behavior of alloys were discussed.

2. Materials and experimental methods 
The as-cast master GH3535 alloy was melted by vacuum induction 
melting and vacuum self-consuming re-melting. 2 wt% Nb was added 
into the master GH3535 alloy and re-melted in vacuum arc melting. The 
nominal compositions of the two alloys are listed in Table 1 . The ingots 
were rolled to plates with 4 mm thickness at 1200 
◦
C. Finally, solution 
treatments for the two alloys were performed at 1200 
◦
C for 0.5 h fol -
lowed by water cooling. 
The tensile specimens were machined with a “ dog-bone ” shape by 
wire-cut electrical discharge machining. The dimension of the tensile 
specimen is 1 mm thickness, 3 mm gage width, and 12 mm gage length. 
The specimens were mechanically ground with 2000# SiC sandpapers. 
The uniaxial tensile experiments were performed at a constant strain 
rate of 8.3 × 10
 4 
s
 1 
in a uniaxial tensile testing machine (Zwick/Roell 
Z100) at RT and 750 
◦
C. At least three specimens for each alloy were 
used for the tensile experiments to minimize experimental errors. The 
stress of 0.2% strain is used to determine the yield strength, and the 
ultimate tensile strength is taken from the maximum stress. 
The samples for optical microscope (OM) and SEM were etched in a 
copper sulfate solution for 30 s. The characteristics of alloy grains were 
obtained using a Zeiss Axio Imager M2m OM. The statistics of grain sizes 
and area percentages of precipitates were measured using Adobe Pho -
toshop and Image-Pro Plus software, and three images for each sample 
were measured to obtain more accurate results. The microstructure and 
fracture morphology were observed using Zeiss Merlin Compact SEM 
equipped energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) with the second -
ary electron mode at an accelerating voltage of 15 kV. The X-ray 
diffractometry (XRD) patterns were measured by Bruker D8 X-ray 
analyzer. The measurements were carried out using copper radiation ( λ 
= 1.542 Å) at the step of 0.02

The Jade software was used to fit the XRD data. 
The areas of tensile specimens for TEM samples were selected to be 
around 3 mm away from the fracture surface, and the sample for 
obtaining the Burgers vector of dislocations was selected to be around 6 
mm away from the fracture surface. The TEM foils were mechanically 
ground and polished to a thickness of 50 μ m, and then the foils were 
thinned by twin-jet polishing at 20

examined using Tecnai G2 F20 TEM at an accelerating voltage of 200 
kV. The EDS experiments were performed at STEM model, and the EDS 
data were quanti fi ed through Cliff-Lorimer analysis. 
3. Results 
3.1. The initial microstructure of two alloys 
Fig. 1 (a and b) are the OM images of the microstructures of GH3535 
and Nb-GH3535 alloys after heat treatment. The equiaxed grains can be 
observed in the two alloys. The distribution statistics of grain sizes are 
exhibited in the insets of Fig. 1 (a and b), and the average grain sizes of 
GH3535 and Nb-GH3535 alloys are 66 μ m and 42 μ m, respectively, 
indicating grain sizes reduce with the addition of Nb element. The SEM 
images of the microstructure of GH3535 and Nb-GH3535 alloys after 
heat treatment are shown in Fig. 1 (c and d). Some annealing twins with 
micron size in width and many precipitates with polygonal shape were 
observed in the two alloys. The area percentages of precipitates in 
GH3535 and Nb-GH3535 alloys are evaluated as 5.42% and 6.04%, and 
the average sizes of precipitates in GH3535 and Nb-GH3535 alloys are 
measured as 2.08 μ m and 2.29 μ m, respectively, indicating the addition 
of Nb has a barely significant effect on the content of precipitate. Fig. 1 
(e) is the XRD patterns of two alloys after heat treatment. The two alloys 
have similar characteristic diffraction peaks of γ matrix, as marked by 
blue triangle. Additionally, the weak diffraction peaks marked by blue 
stars are found in two alloys, which are identified to belong to the M
6
C 
carbide. 
The chemical compositions of γ matrix and precipitates of two alloys 
are analyzed by EDS and the results are listed in Table 2 . It exhibits that 
the precipitate of GH3535 alloy is rich in Mo and C elements, and that of 
Nb-GH3535 alloy is rich in Mo, C and Nb elements. 1.90 wt% and 4.66 
wt% Nb are detected in the matrix and precipitate of Nb-GH3535 alloy. 
It shows that Nb element is distributed in both of the γ matrix and 
precipitate. 
To further investigate the effect of Nb on the type and structure of 
precipitate of GH3535 alloy. The microstructures of precipitates in two 
alloys were analyzed by TEM. Fig. 2 (a – c) exhibits the bright field image

GH3535 alloy. The precipitate is polygonal shaped, which is consistent 
with the SEM result in Fig. 1 (c), which can be identified to be the FCC 
structured M
6
C carbide with [1-1-2] and [-103] zone axis by the SAED 
patterns. The TEM bright field image and SAED patterns of precipitate of 
Nb-GH3535 alloy are displayed in Fig. 2 (d – f). Similar to GH3535 alloy, 
the shape of the precipitate of Nb-GH3535 alloy is also polygonal. The 
zone axes of SAED patterns are identified to be M
6
C with [111] and [01- 
1], respectively. These TEM results demonstrate that the precipitates of 
the two alloys are both M
6
C carbides, indicating the carbide type in 
GH3535 alloy is barely affected by the addition of Nb element.

4. Discussion 
Tensile experiments of GH3535 and Nb-GH3535 alloys were 
performed at RT and 750 
◦
C. The microstructures and tensile behavior of 
two alloys are investigated. It is found that the tensile properties in -
crease with the addition of Nb element at two temperatures, and the 
strengths of the two alloys at 750 
◦
C are lower than those of at RT. As a 
result of the strong association between the microstructure and prop -
erties of alloys, the relationship between the microstructure and prop -
erties of the two alloys at various temperatures is explored below. 
4.1. The effect of Nb element on the tensile properties 
The main strengthening mechanisms of nickel-based superalloys 
include solid solution strengthening, grain refinement strengthening, 
precipitation strengthening and deformation strengthening [ 17 – 19 ]. 
The effects of Nb on various strengthening mechanisms are discussed by 
investigating the influence of Nb on the microstructure of GH3535 alloy.

According to the calculation above, the value of increment by the 
effect of Nb on the solid solution, grain refinement, carbide, nano twins, 
and stacking faults strengthening are quantified as 15 MPa, 24 MPa, 
0.02 MPa, 20 MPa, and 3 MPa, respectively, indicating that the main 
strengthening mechanism by adding of Nb element in GH3535 alloy is 
the solid solution, grain refinement, and nano twins strengthening.

opposite burgers vector encounter [ 27 ], which can reduce the disloca -
tion density, leading to the softening of alloys. Additionally, grain 
boundaries are easy to slide and migrate at high temperatures [ 28 , 29 ],

temperatures. Therefore, the strengths of two alloys at 750

4.3. The prospect of modified GH3535 alloy with high Mo content 
The yield strength and tensile strength of GH3535 alloy are 297 and 
800 MPa, respectively ( Table 3 ), while those of Ni – 12Mo – 7Cr-based 
alloy reported in the literature are 251 and 703 MPa, respectively [ 44 ]. 
It indicates that, in terms of mechanical properties, Ni – 16Mo – 7Cr-based 
alloy has better development potential, which is confirmed by the 
excellent mechanical properties of Nb-GH3535 alloy in this work. 
Therefore, in future work, the resistance to irradiation of Nb-GH3535 
alloy would be further studied to evaluate the application potential of 
modified Ni – 16Mo – 7Cr-based alloy in MSR. 
5. Conclusion 
The effect of Nb element on the tensile properties and deformation 
mechanisms of GH3535 alloy is investigated. The following conclusions 
can be obtained: 
(1) The type and content of carbide in GH3535 alloy are barely 
affected by the addition of Nb element. 
(2) The yield and ultimate tensile strengths of GH3535 alloy can be 
improved by adding Nb element at RT and 750 
◦
C. 
(3) The plastic deformation mechanism of GH3535 alloy at RT is 
dominated by the dislocation slipping, while that of Nb-GH3535 
alloy at RT is stacking fault and nano twins, besides dislocation 
slipping. Compared with RT, dislocations climbing and formation 
of dislocation networks are found in both alloys at 750 
◦
C. 
(4) The synthetic synergistic effect of various strengthening mecha -
nisms, including solid solution strengthening, grain refinement 
strengthening, and nano twins strengthening, is the reason for 
strength improvement by adding of Nb element. 
(5) The strengths of GH3535 and Nb-GH3535 alloys at 750 
◦
C are 
lower than those at RT, which is related to the softening caused 
by dislocations climbing. The serrations behavior of the stress- 
strain curve is observed at 750 
◦
C for two alloys.
