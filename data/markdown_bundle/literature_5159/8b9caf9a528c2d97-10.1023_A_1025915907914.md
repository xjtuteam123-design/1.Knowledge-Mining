Physical and mechanical properties of high-strength nickel alloy ÉK61-ID and stainless maraging steel
ÉK49-VD with reduced carbon content, which are used for fabrication of metallic seals of detachable joints
(DJ) of re-usable liquid-propellant engines (LPE), are studied at cryogenic temperatures (20 – 293 K). Tests of
DJ simulators with seals from these materials and subsequent field tests of LPE confirm the high operational
properties of the structures.
INTRODUCTION
The working medium in the production of powerful reus-
able LPE is liquid hydrogen (20 K) or liquid oxygen (77 K),
which creates problems with choosing appropriate materials
for detachable joints.
It is known [1 – 6] that metallic alloys of the austenitic
class are preferable for operation at cryogenic temperatures.
However, the high level of operational pressure in the piping
and units of LPE, which creates high operational stresses, re-
quires alloys with enhanced mechanical properties. The high
strength in them should be combined with high ductility and
fracture toughness at low temperatures and with low sensi -
tivity to stress concentrators.
In order to provide reliable high-cycle operation of DJ
and the requisite level of tightness (leakages not exceeding

ties (thermal conductivity, coefficient of linear expansion)
are also important for providing the appropriate tightness.
The Énergomash RPA traditionally uses nickel-base al-
loys for the piping and units operating in oxygen in any tem-
perature range (including the cryogenic one). Elastic seals of
DJ operating in liquid oxygen are produced from high-
strength nickel alloy ÉK61-ID. Seals and flange joints oper -
ating in liquid hydrogen are produced from specially de -
signed corrosion-resistant maraging steels with reduced car -
bon content. Steels of this class have high strength characte -
ristics ( /c115

hanced fracture toughness due to the cobalt additive that de -
creases the cold-shortness threshold. Conventional marten -
site steels with comparable strength characteristics are em -
brittled at a temperature below 180 K (– 93°C).
Low-carbon maraging steels are well weldable and do
not require heat treatment after the welding in contrast to the
austenitic-martensitic steels of type SN2A (07Kh16N6) with
the same strength level. In addition, the structure of low-car -
bon maraging steels has a twice-lower amount of retained
austenite than the austenitic-martensitic steels. For this rea -
son they are less susceptible to hydrogen charging in the pro-
cess of deposition of tightening galvanic coatings on elastic
seals. The high-strength low-carbon stainless steel ÉK49-VD
is widely used for operation at cryogenic temperatures up to
20 K (liquid hydrogen).

CONCLUSIONS
1. High-strength alloy ÉK61-ID serving at 293 – 77 K
and maraging steel ÉK49-VD serving at 293 – 20 K are not
embrittled, are insensitive to stress concentrators, and have
high strength and ductility characteristics.
2. The elastic characteristics (modulus of elasticity,
modulus of rigidity) of alloy ÉK61-ID and steel ÉK49-VD
increase from 4 to 10% with decrease in temperature to 80 K.
The Poisson coefficient remains virtually constant.
