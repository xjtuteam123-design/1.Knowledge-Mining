abstract
Nb–16 at.% Si bulk alloys were prepared by uniaxial hot pressing from mechanically alloyed powder
mixtures. It is shown that the addition of appropriate absolute ethyl alcohol signiﬁcantly restrained
the agglomeration of particles and accelerated the milling process. Moreover, besides the solid solu-
tion of Si into Nb lattice, signiﬁcant iron contamination dissolved into Nb to develop another Nb solid
solution after a long milling time. Sintering results indicate that, with sintering temperature increasing
from 1200 to 1500
◦ C, densities and mechanical properties of sintered alloys increased and the mor-
phology of constituent phases (niobium solid solution, Nb 5Si3 and Nb3Si in micron dimension) changed
from irregular shape to nearly equiaxed one. However, as for the material hot pressed at 1600 ◦C, the
microstructure became coarser and the properties were not improved because of the weakening of
ﬁne-grain contribution.

1. Introduction
The improvement of aircraft engines and gas turbines requires
advanced high temperature structural materials, for operation at
temperatures above 1100
◦C. Niobium silicide based alloys have
been studied as potential candidate for use at elevated temper-
ature [1–3], because of their high melting point, low density
and excellent mechanical properties at high temperature [4–6].
The microstructures of these alloys consist of Nbss, Nb
3Si and/or
Nb5Si3 silicides, and the silicides offer reasonable high temperature
strength. However, niobium silicides have low fracture toughness
at room temperature and poor deformability at high temperature,
and the drawbacks hinder the practical applications of niobium
silicide based alloys. Some reports have shown that the fracture
toughness of these alloys was enhanced by incorporating a ductile
Nb phase to hard deformable niobium silicides [7,8], i.e., ductile
phase toughening. Besides, alloying with metal elements such as
Cr, Hf, Ti and Al to form Nb–Ti–Si [9,10], Nb–Ti–Si–Al [9,10] and
Nb–Ti–Hf–Cr–Al–Si [11], has resulted in signiﬁcant improvement
of fracture toughness of Nb–Si alloys. In addition, a recent study[12]
found that hot extrusion of multiphase Nb–Si–Fe in situ compos-
ite could also improve the fracture toughness. To date, researchers
have successfully fabricated niobium silicide based composites by
electron beam melting [1], vacuum arc melting [3], directional
solidiﬁcation [13], and spark plasma sintering[14]. However, Nb–Si
alloys fabricated by the above-mentioned casting methods possess
component segregation and coarse microstructure. Accordingly,

Amancherla et al. used MICRESS to simulate the microstructural
evolution during solidiﬁcation of a binary Nb–16 at.% Si alloy [15].
As compared with the arc-melted counterparts, Nb–Si alloys fabri-
cated by spark plasma sintering owned ﬁne grains because of the
short holding time. Until recently, few reports have exhibited the
microstructure and mechanical properties of Nb–Si alloys based on
a Nb–Si–Ti ternary system by reactive hot-pressing sintering [16],
and the synthesis of niobium silicides by ball milling of powder
mixtures with different Si content of Nb–Si binary system [17–19].
However, rare report has been available on the inﬂuence of con-
trol agent on the mechanical alloying of Nb–Si powders, and the
effect of processing parameters and routes of hot-pressing sin-
tering on the microstructure and mechanical properties of Nb–Si
alloys.
In the present study, Nb–16 at.% Si alloys were fabricated by uni-
axially hot pressing from mechanical alloyed compound powders
and absolute ethyl alcohol was used as control agent during milling.
We aimed to investigate the evolution of mechanical alloyed pow-
ders and proper sintering parameters for fabricating dense alloys.
2. Experimental procedures
Element powders of Nb (99.9 wt.% purity, <200 mesh) and Si (99 wt.% purity,
<200 mesh) were used as starting materials. The powders were blended in appropri-
ate amounts to give the nominal composition of 84 at.% Nb and 16 at.% Si. Mechanical
alloying was carried out at room temperature in a planetary ball-milling machine
(QM-BP) with stainless steel vials and bearing steel balls. The vial was loaded with
a powder mixture of 50 g. During milling, the ball-to-powder weight ratio and rota-
tion speed were maintained at 15:1 and 250 rpm, respectively. In order to prevent
oxygen contamination, the handling of powders was done in a glove-box with argon
protection, and the vial was also sealed under argon before milling. 20 and 150 ml
absolute ethyl alcohol were used, respectively, to examine the effect of control agent
on the progress of mechanical alloying process. During milling, powder samples

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Morphological evolution of MA powders

with 20 ml absolute ethyl alcohol are shown in Fig. 1 . After 3 h

powders still remained irregular. After 12 h milling, the particles
became much ﬁner and no agglomeration were found. This indi-
cates that work-hardening was at work at this stage and absolute
ethyl alcohol played an effective role in the reduction of fresh sur-
face contacting. The average size of particles was reduced to about
5 /H9262m after 24 h milling. At this stage, work-hardening increased

the case of the ball milling with 20 ml absolute ethyl alcohol, pow-

underwent various deformations, such as forging and shearing, by
which particles were deformed into blocks and ﬂakes. With respect
to the milling process with 150 ml absolute ethyl alcohol, after

3.2. X-ray diffraction analysis of MA powders
The XRD patterns of Nb–16Si powders milled with 20 and 150 ml
absolute ethyl alcohol for different milling times are presented in
Fig. 3. In general, with the milling time increasing, a similar behavior
was noted in both of the milling processes, i.e., the broadening and
intensity reducing of Nb peaks. The peak broadening is attributed
to the grain reﬁnement and increasing lattice strains by virtue of
severe plastic deformation of particles. In addition, the positions of
Nb peaks slightly moved towards the low angle side and Si peaks
reduced in intensities during milling. The results suggest that the
lattice parameter of Nb increased and a Nb solid solution formed
with Si dissolving into Nb lattice. The solid solution is thought
to be interstitial in view of the well-known fact that the atomic
radium of Si (1.46 Å) is smaller than that of Nb (2.08 Å). Further-
more, at the beginning of milling process with 150 ml absolute
ethyl alcohol, Si peaks were barely identiﬁed. It may be ascribed
to the coverage of fractured Si particles by ﬂat particles of Nb at
this milling stage. Zhou and Rao [20] have raised the same prob-
able cause for the absence of Si peaks after 5 h milling in Ti–Al–Si
system.
Moreover, it is noticeable that new satellite peaks appeared
on the left of primary Nb peaks after a period of milling time in
both of the milling processes, and the secondary peaks got obvi-
ous with milling proceeding. The result suggests that two niobium
solid solutions with different lattice parameters coexisted in the
compound powders. Similar behavior was once observed in the
mechanical alloying of Nb–20V–15Al [21] due to the presence of
vanadium in the Nb solid solution. In the milling process with 20 ml
absolute ethyl alcohol, the primary and subsequent solid solutions
were labeled as NbI and NbII, respectively. In the other milling pro-
cess, they were labeled as NbIII and NbIV, respectively. It indicates
that the lattice parameter of subsequent solid solution was larger
than that of primary ones, because the diffraction angles of satellite
peaks were lower than those of primary peaks. Such result may be
associated with the iron contamination which was introduced to
the materials from milling containers and grinding mediums dur-
ing milling. XRF analysis shows that 7.78 and 8.97 at.% of Fe existed
in compound powders milled for 45 and 55 h with 150 ml absolute
ethyl alcohol, respectively. Since the atomic radium of Fe (1.72 Å)
is much greater than that of Si, it is predicated that the subsequent
solid solution was enriched with Fe besides Si. Because of that, the
lattice parameter of Nb solid solution was increased. In addition, for
the milling process with 20 ml absolute ethyl alcohol, the iron con-
tamination was restrained to some extent with powders tending
to adhere to the vial and balls. Accordingly, the secondary peaks
appeared later in the milling process with 20 ml absolute ethyl
alcohol than that with 150 ml absolute ethyl alcohol.

temperature ascending, while slightly increased with the holding
time prolonged. The relative density reached over 99% for the alloys

◦C or less, the
properties displayed similar tendency that they enhanced sharply
with the sintering temperature increasing. According to Ryske-

porosity of material by an exponential relation ( /ESC

and fracture strength was attributed to the weakening of ﬁne-grain
strengthening and toughening.

4. Conclusions
Bulk Nb–16Si alloys were uniaxially hot-pressed from mechan-
ical alloyed powders. The obtained results are summarized as
follows.
1. The reduction in average particle size was much quicker in
the milling process with 20 ml absolute ethyl alcohol than that
with 150 ml. The collision between particles and grinding balls
decreased and the contact of fresh surfaces was restrained in the
process with 150 ml absolute ethyl alcohol.
2. A portion of Si atoms dissolved into Nb lattices to form the
interstitial solid solution during milling. Besides, another Nb
solid solution with larger lattice parameter formed after a longer
milling time in both of the processes, owing to the introduction
of iron contamination from the milling containers and grinding
mediums.
3. Densities of bulk Nb–16Si alloys increased signiﬁcantly with the
sintering temperature increasing and the relative density of the
alloy hot pressed at 1500
◦C reached over 99%. With sintering
temperature increasing from 1200 to 1500 ◦C, the morphology
of constituent phases (niobium solid solution, Nb 5Si3 and Nb3Si
in micron dimension) changed from irregular shape to nearly
equiaxed one. The microstructure became much coarser when
the sintering temperature increasing from 1500 to 1600
◦C.
4. Vickers hardness, fracture toughness, ﬂexural strength and
elastic modulus of bulk Nb–16Si alloys all were signiﬁcantly
improved with the sintering temperature increasing from 1200
