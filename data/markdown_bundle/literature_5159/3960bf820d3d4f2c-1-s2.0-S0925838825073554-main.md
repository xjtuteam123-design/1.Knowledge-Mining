1. Introduction
In the pursuit of high strength of metallic materials, high entropy 
alloys (HEAs), a new class of metallic materials, have attracted extensive 
attention. HEAs are defined as alloys containing five or more principal 
elements in equal or near-equimolar ratios [1] . Different from tradi -
tional materials, the composition of HEAs is complex, and the constit -
uent element atoms are distributed in the lattice position randomly and 
disorderly. The high conformational entropy of HEAs contributes to the 
reduction of the Gibbs free energy in these alloys, and thus retarding the 
formation of intermetallics and stabilizing the single solid solution phase 
[2,3] . Among HEAs, RHEAs, which are composed of refractory metal 
elements, exhibit high strength at elevated temperatures, and thus are 
considered as promising materials for high-temperature applications 
such as shafts and turbines for aeroengines [4,5] .
In 2010, Senkov et al. [6] prepared a coarse-grained (CG) RHEA, 
VNbMoTaW, with a high yield strength of 477 MPa at a high tempera -
ture of 1873 K. It should be interesting to study if the strength of 
VNbMoTaW at room and elevated temperatures can be further 
improved. One of the effective strategies for strengthening metallic 
materials is to refine their grain size. Using mechanical alloying and 
high-pressure/high-temperature consolidation techniques, we have 
prepared a NC VNbMoTaW with an average grain size of 30 nm [7] . This 
NC RHEA has high hardness of 11.4 GPa, which is approximately twice 
that of its CG counterpart. Meanwhile, refining the grain size of metallic 
materials to nano scales can induce superplasticity - the ability of ma -
terials to exhibit extensive neck-free elongations during deformation at 
elevated temperatures of above 40 % melting temperature [8] . The su -
perplastic behavior makes it possible to be deformed with large uniform 
strains at relatively low stresses [8] , resulting in considerable savings 
costs in the forming process [9] .
Recent developments in RHEAs have revealed several challenges 
associated with high grain boundary (GB) density [10 – 12] , including 
poor thermal stability [12] , inadequate creep resistance [13] , and 
embrittlement caused by impurity segregation [14] . Oxygen contami -
nation during processing is particularly detrimental, as oxygen atoms

spurred innovation in grain boundary engineering through selective 
doping, where understanding the distinct roles of segregating elements 
is crucial. The selection of doping elements (e.g., B, C, O) is critical, as 
they compete for segregation sites and differentially alter GB properties. 
Oxygen segregation typically forms strong directional bonds with metal 
atoms at GBs, which localizes electrons and weakens metallic bonds, 
leading to significant GB embrittlement [15] . In contrast, boron segre -
gation can enhance GB cohesion and suppress oxygen-induced embrit -
tlement in some RHEAs [15] . However, its effect is system-dependent 
and may not suffice to enhance high-temperature stability or creep 
resistance. Segregation of C at the GBs has recently emerged as a supe -
rior multifunctional strategy. With a higher diffusion coefficient and 
lower segregation energy than oxygen, carbon readily outcompetes ox -
ygen for GB sites [15] . Upon segregation, it forms metallic-like bonds 
with neighboring metal atoms, enhancing GB cohesion and deform -
ability without embrittlement [15] . Moreover, carbon doping uniquely 
stabilizes the microstructure by significantly lowering GB energy via 
segregation [16] . Recent advances in other material systems reinforce 
the concept of tailoring GB properties via solute segregation. Liu et al. 
[17] used atom probe tomography to obtain direct atomic-scale evi -
dence of carbon segregation at GBs in NbTiZr RHEA. Segregation of Mo 
to GBs in nanocrystalline (NC) Ni effectively lowers the specific GB 
energy, thereby enhancing creep resistance [18] . Moreover, studies 
have underscored the frequently overlooked role of GB thickness in 
mechanical behavior, showing that atomic-level GB engineering can 
overcome the traditional strength-ductility trade-off [19] .
In the present study, we have used a GB engineering approach, i.e., 
the segregation of C atoms at the GBs of bulk NC (VNbMoTaW)

overcome the challenges of NC HEAs: inferior capability of plastic 
deformation, weak creep resistance, and low thermal stability.

opment Co., Ltd.). To prevent the oxidation of the milled powders, the 
mechanical alloying and pressing were performed in a glove box filled 
with argon. The compacts were inserted into a pure h-BN crucible and 
then mounted in a graphite furnace. The high-pressure sintering was 
carried out in a CS-IB type cubic-anvil apparatus (Guilin Guiye Ma -
chinery Co., Ltd.). Two-step sintering method was used, that is, first the 
pressure was increased to 4 GPa, then the temperature was raised to 
1273 K and held for about 3 s, after that, the temperature was lowered to 
1173 K within 1 min and held for 30 min. Finally, the specimens were 
cooled down to room temperature in air. The obtained specimen was a 
cylinder with a diameter of about 8 mm and a height of about 9 mm. For 
comparison, two bulk NC VNbMoTaW with an average gran size of 
30 ± 3 nm and 67 ± 17 nm were also prepared, as described in our 
previous studies [7,13] .

4.5. Synergistic effects of carbon segregation
Collectively, our results demonstrate that carbon segregation at grain 
boundaries exerts multifaceted, synergistic effects on the mechanical 
properties, thermal stability, and creep resistance of the NC (VNbMo -
TaW) ₉₈ C ₂ RHEA. Lower grain-boundary energy provides the thermody -
namic origin of its excellent thermal stability; high grain-boundary 
cohesion constitutes the structural basis of this reduced energy; and the 
increased diffusion activation energy acts as the kinetic bottleneck for 
grain-boundary migration. Together, these three factors form an 
"energy – structure – kinetics" closed loop in the NC (VNbMoTaW) ₉₈ C ₂ 
RHEA. All improvements are intrinsically coupled through carbon 
segregation, which modifies grain-boundary structure and energetics. 
The enhanced cohesion directly boosts room-temperature plasticity and 
fracture strain, while simultaneously suppressing grain-boundary- 
mediated deformation at elevated temperatures, thereby preserving 
strength and improving creep resistance. Fig. 14 schematically sum -
marizes how segregation of C concurrently enhances the strength, 
thermal stability and creep resistance of the NC (VNbMoTaW) ₉₈ C ₂ 
RHEA. The illustration juxtaposes the deformation/failure modes of the 
undoped alloy with those of the carbon-doped material. In the undoped 
condition ( Fig. 14 a), oxygen impurities preferentially occupy grain 
boundaries (GBs), thereby weakening interatomic cohesion and pro -
moting brittle, intergranular fracture. The resultant high GB energy 
provides a strong thermodynamic driving force for rapid grain growth at 
elevated temperatures. In addition, GBs serve as rapid diffusion paths 
and easy sliding planes, so the creep rate remains high and is governed 
by a low apparent activation energy for GB self-diffusion. For the 
carbon-doped (VNbMoTaW) ₉₈ C ₂ alloy ( Fig. 14 b), carbon atoms ener -
getically replace oxygen at GBs, markedly enhancing cohesion and 
allowing extensive plasticity by inhibiting crack initiation and propa -
gation. Thermodynamically, the segregation of C lowers GB energy, 
drastically curtailing the driving force for grain growth and imparting 
superior thermal stability. Kinetically, nanometre-sized M ₂ C 
precipitates-intrinsically formed through carbon addition-pin GBs and 
impede dislocation motion. The synergistic reduction in GB energy 
together with Zener pinning elevates the apparent activation energy for 
GB diffusion, thereby strongly suppressing Coble creep. Consequently, 
carbon segregation functions as a unified design strategy that concur -
rently overcomes the major limitations of NC RHEAs — brittleness, 
thermal instability and poor creep resistance — by fundamentally 
tailoring grain boundary structure and lowering the GB energy.
