High temperature deformation behaviors of a high Nb
containing TiAl alloy
J.P. Lin*, X.J. Xu, Y .L. Wang, S.F. He, Y . Zhang, X.P. Song, G.L. Chen
State Key Laboratory for Advanced Metals and Materials, University of Science and Technology Beijing, Beijing 100083, PR China
Available online 28 November 2006
Abstract
In the present paper, high temperature tensile and creep behaviors of Ti e45Ale9(Nb,W,B,Y) alloy with duplex (DP) microstructure were
investigated. In addition to tensile tests at 815 /C14 C and a strain rate range of 1 /C2 10/C0 4 s/C0 1/C0 1 /C2 10/C0 3 s/C0 1 and tensile, creep tests at 760 /C14 C
and 815 /C14 C under the stress of 180 MPa, the microstructure evolutions during tensile and creep tests were studied. The results show that
high temperature high Nb containing TiAl alloy with DP microstructure has a good balance between ductility and strength and intermediate
creep resistance. The tensile properties have the strain rate dependence, and ultimate tensile strength (UTS) and yield strength (YS) vs. strain
rate obey a single-logarithm linear relationship. Minimum creep rate is affected by the test temperature and stress. Using loading change ex-
periment a stress exponent of 4.3 is determined. DP microstructure is unstable after long-term exposure at high temperatures, and the spheroid-
ization of lamella and recrystallization along grain boundaries occur during the high temperature deformation. It is assumed that the diffusion-
assisted climb of dislocations might be the controlling mechanism at the minimum creep rate stage.

1. Introduction
High Nb containing TiAl alloys have attracted more and
more attention in recent years. Many valuable conclusions, in-
cluding oxidation resistance [1], phase relationships and phase
diagram construction [2], microstructure evolution [3], rela-
tionships of microstructures and properties [4] and workability
at high temperatures [5], have been reported previously. It is
worth noting that the TiAl alloys with Al content ranging
from 45 to 49 at.% and Nb content ranging from 8 to10 at.%
exhibit excellent balanced properties. They have the optimum
content of Nb for oxidation resistance at 900
/C14 C [1,6], and ex-
hibit superior strength at both room and high temperatures [5]
and creep strength at high temperatures [7]. Therefore, the
high Nb containing TiAl alloys mentioned above are more
suitable for higher temperature application than conventional
or so-called ‘‘second generation’’g-TiAl alloys. It is known
that the high Nb containing TiAl alloy with DP microstructure
has both high ductility at room temperature and high strength
at high temperatures. However, evaluation of the high temper-
ature mechanical behaviors including both tensile and creep
behaviors is limited as yet. This paper gives a preliminary
study on the tensile and creep behaviors of a high Nb contain-
ing TiAl with a DP microstructure, including experimental
data as well as microstructural changes occurring under these
conditions.

4. Discussion
4.1. Effects of elements and microstructure on the high
temperature properties
At high temperatures, high Nb containing TiAl alloy with
a DP microstructure has a good balance between ductility
and strength. The main reason for the higher high temperature
strength comes from the Nb and W additions, which are found
to strengthen but also embrittle the g phase. The strengthening
of Nb can be attributed to the increase of the thermal resis-
tance stress and the elastic interaction with forest dislocation
of the dislocations in g phase [11]. The strengthening effect
of W addition is thought to be associated with lamellar width
reﬁnement and solid solution, which is induced by W, that im-
pedes elevated temperature dislocation movement [12]. The
high ductility is attributed to the small grain size and the strain
compatibility of the lamellar colonies and the g grains. Grain
boundary sliding and migration also play an important role.
However, the creep resistance of the alloy with the DP
microstructure is inferior to that of the similar alloy with FL
microstructure but superior to that of the conventional TiAl
alloy with the similar microstructures. The minimum creep
rates of the alloy studied were at least an order of magnitude
lower than those of conventional TiAl with a DP microstruc-
ture. This is also because the high Nb and minor W additions
decrease the climb rates of dislocations of the alloy. But at the
same test condition the minimum creep rates obtained by the
present work are an order of magnitude higher than those ob-
tained by others for similar alloy with an FL microstructure,
and the creep strains prior to fracture for the current study
are much higher than those obtained from the similar alloy
with an FL microstructure too [7]. These are mainly attributed
to the existence of the equiaxed g grain. For the full lamellar
microstructure the interfacial boundaries can act to inhibit the
motion of dislocations and can block the generation of dislo-
cations from the interfaces [13]. For the DP microstructure g
grains are preferentially deformed during creep, which causes
dynamic recrystallization. Even a small volume fraction of g
grains causes substantially less creep resistance [14]. The
mechanism will be discussed afterwards.

4.3. The mechanism of tensile and creep deformations
During creep or tension at low strain rates, the alloy is
susceptible to colony (or grain) boundary sliding (GBS),
which leads to a high elongation. As the elongation in-
creases, the shape of a
2 grain remains essentially unchanged
with a sharp and straight boundary, and the boride is hard to
deform at high temperatures, but the g grain is soft com-
pared with the a
2 and boride. Thus, the misﬁt strain of
these phases, accompanied by the strain incompatibility of
lamellar colonies and g grain, results in many cavities
formed not only at g/lamella and g/g boundaries, but also
at g/a2 and g/boride boundaries under extensive deforma-
tion. At the ﬁnal stage of deformation intensive pore forma-
tion occurs, ultimately leading to fracture.
Various deformation mechanisms dominate the high tem-
perature deformation. The special aspect of high temperature
deformation is that the effect of diffusion is pronounced.
The dislocations can climb as well as glide so that recovery
due to dislocation rearrangement and pair annihilation proceed
quickly. For polycrystalline metals, recrystallization, grain
boundary sliding and migration also occur. The controlling
mechanisms vary with the test conditions.
During tension at 1 /C2 10
/C0 3 s/C0 1, the deformation rate is suf-
ﬁciently high, and the effect of diffusion on deformation may
be small. At the decreased strain rate, the dislocation climb
process frequently accompanies the grain matrix deformation,
because the dislocation pile-ups at grain boundaries can climb
easily at high temperatures, thereby relieving the stress
concentration. The strain rate dependence of ﬂow stress is con-
sidered always to occur when a dislocation can surmount ob-
stacles by a thermally activated process, so the UTS (YS) vs. _3
data obey a single-logarithm linear relationship. This agrees
with work reported previously [16]. The grain boundary slid-
ing and migration also occur, showing the necking and grain
elongation.
During the initial stage of creep deformation, dislocations
are generated and multiplied; bowing and tangling of the dis-
locations contribute to work hardening of the material. Al-
though dislocation climb also occurs, the aforementioned
features are the dominant microstructural changes during the
initial stage of creep deformation, decreasing the creep strain
rate to a minimum value. It has been assumed that a stress ex-
ponent in the range of 3 e5 indicates a creep process, which is
controlled by dislocation climb [9]. Thus, from the stress ex-
ponent obtained by the present study, diffusion-assisted climb
of dislocations is the possible mechanism that controls the
creep process. But the n interpreted is based on the view
that a strain hardening process is balanced by a time softening
process, which results in a dynamically steady microstructure.
However, these will not affect the second-stage creep, espe-
cially the alloy with DP microstructure. It is clear that a consid-
erable part of the creep life is spent in the tertiary creep region.
This is because the high temperature and the stress eventually
lead to microstructural degradation in the form of spheroidiza-
tion of the lamella and recrystallization of equiaxed g and a
2
grains along the colony boundaries. This, in turn, enhances the
grain boundaries’ sliding, leading to an early onset of the ter-
tiary creep.
