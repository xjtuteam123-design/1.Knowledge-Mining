The present work is devoted to the effect of modification of high-temperature alloys ZhS6U-VI and 
ZhS3DK-VI with hafnium on their properties and structure. Alloys ZhS6U-VI and ZhS3DK-VI were molten 
in a vacuum induction furnace and modified with hafnium in an amount of 0 - 1.5% (as calculated) using a 
nickel-hafnium alloying composition. It is shown that hafnium alloying diminishes the sizes and amount of 
the brittle component in a fracture and the distance between the second-order dendrite axes and changes the 
morphology of the carbides. With growth of the hafnium content the mechanical properties of both alloys and 
the high-temperature strength of alloy ZhS3DK-VI at room temperature increase. 
The alloys were melted by the method of remelting a 
measured preform in a UPPF-3M vacuum induction furnace 
in a crucible with a basic lining and a capacity of 7.5 kg. A 
weighed portion of a hafnium-nickel alloying composition of 
fraction i- 3 mm was introduced into the melt heated to 
1600~ 1.5 min before discharging. The composition pro-

ZhS3DK-VI. Blocks of finger specimens were fabricated by

In studying the dendrite structure of the alloys we estab- 
lished that the distance between the second-order axes of the 
dendrites decreases considerably with growth of the hafnium

promote growth of the crystallization rate of the alloys. 
A metallographic study showed that hafnium modifica- 
tion of the alloys promotes a change in the morphology of 
the MC carbide, which has the form of "Chinese hiero- 
glyphs" in alloy ZhS6U-VI and polyhedra in alloy 
ZhS3DK-VI, into a carbide with a virtually equiaxiai globu- 
lar morphology (Fig. 3). It is known [1] that carbides in the 
form of Chinese hieroglyphs are represented by a solid solu- 
tion based on (Ti, Nb)C. As compared to globular carbides 
they contain a large amount of titanium and tungsten. This 
form of carbides is characterized by a high energy of phase 
separation.

The effect of small hafnium additives on the change in 
the form of carbide seems to be caused by the high car- 
bide-forming capacity of this element (several times higher 
than that of titanium [2]). 
For example, even a 0.5% additive of Hf stimulated 
breaking of carbide branches in the form of Chinese 
hieroglyphs into more compact particles characterized by a 
smaller length-to-width ratio. Introduction of 1.2- 1.5% Hf 
(as calculated) causes globularization of virtually all carbides. 
An electron-microscopic study of the morphology of the 
principal hardening ),'-phase in dendrite axes showed that 
modification of both alloys with hafnium does not affect sub- 
stantially its sizes or shape. An increase in the hafnium con- 
centration in the alloys causes growth of the amount of the 
eutectic (Fig. 4). This is manifested the more obviously in al- 
loy ZhS3DK-VI.

CONCLUSIONS 
1. Modification of alloys ZhS6U-VI and ZhS3DK-VI 
with hafnium diminishes the proportion of the brittle compo- 
nent in fractures and the distance between the second-order 
axes in dendrites. Hafnium additives promote an increase in 
the crystallization rate of the alloys.
