ABSTRACT
The fourth generation of Ni-based single crystal superalloys (Ni-SXs), such as DD91, play a crucial role in 
aerospace and gas turbines due to their excellent performance at high temperature. To enhance the oxidation 
resistance and high-temperature performance of Ni-SXs, the laser shock peening (LSP) treatment was employed 
to introduce the dislocations in their surface, which act as diffusion channels for metal atoms and promote the 
formation of dense protective oxide layer during oxidation. In this study, XRD, XPS, SEM and TEM analyses were 
conducted on the cross-section of Ni-SXs to compare the microstructure and oxidation behavior before and after 
the LSP treatment. The results indicate that surface engineering via the LSP treatment significantly alters the 
dislocations status of γ / γ ՛ phases, consequently influencing the surface oxide layer and oxidation resistance of Ni- 
SXs. Additionally, the oxidation mechanism of Ni-SXs after the LSP treatment was studied. The understanding 
developed in this work could be beneficial for the design of the alloys with enhanced oxidation resistance.
1. Introduction
Nickel (Ni) based single crystal superalloys (Ni-SXs) have the 
excellent mechanical properties, oxidation resistance, and creep prop -
erties at high temperatures, which are widely used in hot-end compo -
nents, such as aerospace and turbine [ 1 – 5 ]. Such excellent performance 
of Ni-SXs is attributed to the combination of γ phase with a face-centered 
cubic (FCC) crystal structure, and γ ՛ phases with a FCC-based Ll
2
-ordered 
structure [ 6 – 8 ]. Owing to the strengthening strategy of γ ՛ phases, it ex -
hibits excellent thermo-mechanical properties. As the key parts in en -
gines, it works in a high-temperature environment, and suffers from 
high-temperature oxidation and corrosion. During high-temperature 
service, the failure of Ni-SXs by high-temperature oxidation will 
threaten the safe operation of the engines [ 9 ]. Therefore, a large number 
of researchers are committed to enhancing the oxidation resistance of 
Ni-SXs [ 10 – 12 ].
In fact, most previous researches are focused on the coating tech -
nologies and alloying processes of Ni-SXs [ 13 – 15 ]. Unfortunately, the 
introduction of alloying elements inevitably leads to an increase in the 
density and cost of the materials [ 10 , 16 – 19 ]. Thus, it is urgent to 
develop a non-alloying method to improve the oxidation resistance of 
Ni-SXs. Here, the formation of a protective oxide layer is facilitated by 
introducing dislocations on the surface of the Ni-based superalloy that 
act as diffusion channels for the metal atoms, thereby enhancing the 
oxidation resistance of the materials [ 20 ]. For instance, Du et al. re -
ported that the introduction of dislocations into Ni-SXs can also enhance 
the oxidation resistance through forming diffusion channels for antiox -
idant elements [ 21 ]. Ren et al. found that the oxidation resistance of 
nickel alloys can be effectively improved by introducing dislocations 
into the alloys by pre-deformation [ 22 ]. Therefore, it is feasible to 
enhance the oxidation resistance of Ni-SXs by introducing dislocations.
Based on the aforementioned researches, this study proposes 
applying laser shock peening (LSP) to Ni-SXs surfaces to regulate the 
dislocation density, thereby enhancing their high-temperature oxidation 
resistance. Here, the LSP treatment is an attractive technology for sur -
face modification, and plays an important role in enhancing mechanical 
properties through the formation of large compressive stresses [ 23 – 26 ].

2. Experimental procedure
2.1. Materials and process
The LSP experiments were implemented through Q-switched Nd: 
YAG laser (SIA, CAS, Shenyang, China) with wavelength of 1064 nm, 
laser energy of 9 J, pulsed width of 15 ns, laser beam spot diameter of 3 
mm and repetition frequency of 1 Hz. The black tape with thickness of 
250 μ m bought from Nitto Denko Corporation (Osaki, Tokyo, Japan) 
was selected as the absorbing protective layer to absorb laser energy and 
protect the target surface from laser thermal ablation. Running water 
layer about 1 – 1.5 mm was applied onto the black tape as the transparent 
constrain layer to increase the peak pressure of laser-induced plasma 
shock waves. The overlap rate with 50 % and the LSP path with a zigzag 
were selected (as shown in Fig. 1 ).

4. Conclusions
In this work, the LSP treatment was employed on Ni-SXs to achieve 
exceptional high-temperature performance. The microstructural evolu -
tion and oxidation behavior of Ni-SXs treated by LSP were investigated. 
The main conclusions are drawn as follows. 
(1) Compared to the untreated Ni-SXs, LSP treatment simultaneously 
reduces the γ ՛ phase width, oxide layer thickness and suppresses 
the γ ՛ phase coarsening during thermal exposure while enhancing 
the oxidation resistance.
(2) There are two layers of oxide films in the Ni-SXs after the 
oxidation, namely the Ni, Co-rich outer oxide layer formed 
adjacent to the environment and Al, Ta-rich inner oxide layer 
developed near the subsurface layer. Compared to untreated Ni- 
SXs, fewer cracks distribute on the Ni, Co-rich outer oxide layer 
of LSP-treated Ni-SXs.
(3) The LSP treatment in Ni-SXs is beneficial for the generation of 
dislocations, which provides channels for diffusion of metal ele -
ments and imposes a drag effect on the dislocation movement. 
Therefore, the dense protective oxide layer is formed in the LSP- 
treated Ni-SXs after oxidation, which hinders the oxidation 
behavior and suppresses the formation of cracks.
