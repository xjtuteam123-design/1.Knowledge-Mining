The development of mechanical alloying (MA)-oxide dispersion strengthened (ODS) heat-resistant ferritic
alloys of Fe-12%Cr with W, Ti and Y 203 additions were carried out. Fe-12%Cr alloys with 3%W, O,4%Ti
and 0.25% Y 203 additions showed a much finer and more uniform dispersion of oxide particles among the
alloy system studied. Nano-sized oxides dispersed in the alloys suppress the grain growth during annealing
at a high temperature and resulted in the remarkable improvement of creep strength. The oxide phase was
identified as a complex oxide type of Y-Ti-O.
Keywords: heat-resistant ferritic alloys, mechanical alloying(MA), oxide dispersion strengthened (ODS), high
temperature strength, creep, nano-sized oxides
1. INTRODUCTION
In recent years, the development of high-temperature
structural material for ultra-super critical pressure power
plant and nuclear reactor material (nuclear fast-breeder reac­
tor and fusion first wall applications) for the improvement of
energy efficiency in order to solve problems such as deple­
tion of energy resource, global warming, and environmental
preservation has been carried out around the globe [1-3].
In the past, austenitic heat-resistant steels have been used
for structural materials at high temperature. Though used
extensively, it was found that after using it at a temperature
for a long time, the steels tend to generate problems in ther­
mal stress and fatigue failure which can be related to their
low thermal conductivity and high expansion coefficient. In
an attempt to solve the problem, many studies have been
done on the ferritic alloys with high thermal conductivity
and a low expansion coefficient.
And, results from the studies showed that in order for the
alloys to be used at higher temperatures, it is necessary to
improve their high temperature strength [4-8].
Oxide dispersion strengthened (ODS) ferritic alloys pro­
duced by mechanical alloying (MA) have been found to be
effective with increasingly high temperature strength. ODS
steels are materials with excellent high temperature strength
by dispersion strengthening of the oxide particles and solid
solution hardening. Though precipitation hardening is simi­
lar to particle dispersion hardening, the hardening effect of
the precipitator formed by precipitation behavior decreases
because of thermal stability factors of precipitator such as:
re-solution, coarsening, etc., which occur as a result of being
used at high temperatures for long durations of time. Com­
paratively, the oxide in a Fe-base alloy does not change in
size or state of dispersion even after high-temperature long
duration use, and it is also possible for it to maintain an
excellent high-temperature strength near the melting point.
In the previous studies it was found that at temperature
ranging from 297 K to 1173 K, the MA-ODS alloys with the
addition of Y203 showed higher strength than those of the
alloys with additions of Ab03, zr0 2, Si02 and MgO [1].
In this study, oxide dispersion strengthened Fe-12% Cr
base alloys with an addition of Y203 and elements having
higher melting temperature W, Ti made by MA, have been
attempted by investigating their creep strength at high tem­
perature and microstructure.

creep deformation behavior is affected by average size, for­
mation site, chemical composition of precipitates and dis­
tance between precipitates. Hence, it is very important to
analyze the relationship between the microstructure and
creep deformation.

growth, revealing that the process of recrystallization and
retardation of grain growth were slow due to the fine dis­

4. CONCLUSION
Oxide dispersion strengthened Fe-12% Cr base alloys with
the addition of Y203 and elements having higher a melting
temperature, W, Ti made by MA have been developed by
investigating their creep strength and microstructure.
The experimental results are summarized as follows:
It was found by observing in TEM and APFIM that most
oxides of the Fe-12% Cr base alloys with the addition of
3%W, O.4%Ti and 0.25% Y203 (12YWT) were dispersed
uniformly with nanosized complex oxide forms, much fmer
than those ofFe-12% Cr base alloys with 0.25% Y203(12YI)
and those with 3%W, 0.25% Y203(12YW).1t was also found
that matrix microstructures of annealed 12Yl and 12YW
specimens were recrystallized, followed by grain growth,
but the microstructures of 12YWT specimens were partially
recrystallized and retarded in grain growth.
The reason for the highest value in high-temperature creep
strength of 12YWT specimens among the materials (12Yl,
12YW and 12YWT) is mainly attributed to the fine complex
oxides dispersed in the matrix of 12YWT specimens and
their suppression of dislocation migration during creep. In
addition, the fine oxides affected the recrystallization behav-
ior, that is, partial recrystallization and retardation of grain
growth. As a result, fme grain was formed during annealing.
This also contributed to increasing the high-temperature
creep strength of the 12YWT specimens.
