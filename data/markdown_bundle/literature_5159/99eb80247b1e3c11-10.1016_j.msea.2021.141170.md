ABSTRACT 
Considering the lower cost of Nb and its chemical similarity to Ta, this work investigated the effect of complete 
replacement of Ta by Nb (in at.% basis) in MAR-M246 Ni-based superalloy for the automotive turbocharger 
applications. Conventional and Nb-modified polycrystalline MAR-M246 superalloys were prepared through 
vacuum induction melting and investment casting from commercial raw-materials. Creep tests were performed 
on the as-cast alloys in air, at 800 
◦

◦
C (150/200/ 
225 MPa). Additional creep tests were carried out at 900 
◦

effects of oxidation on the creep behavior of the alloys. Both alloys presented similar creep behavior at 800 
◦

1. Introduction 
Nickel based superalloys have been widely used for structural ap -
plications at high temperatures due to their mechanical strength, 
toughness, and environmental resistance at elevated temperatures [ 1 , 2 ]. 
The exceptional properties result from the combination of chemical 
composition and microstructural features, tailored to either avoid or 
mitigate the effects of high temperature on the mechanical properties as 
well as corrosion/oxidation resistance [ 3 ]. 
MAR-M246 is a well-known conventionally cast Ni-based superalloy 
designed for applications at high temperatures in aero-engines as well as 
in automotive turbochargers. In the latter, operating temperatures 
normally varies from 800 
◦
C (diesel engine) to 1000 
◦
C (gasoline en -
gine), combined with high centrifugal loads for components such as 
impellers. Thus, long term creep resistance is one of the main 
requirements the alloy must present. On the other hand, MAR-M246 
presents 1.5 wt% of the high cost element Ta in its chemical composi -
tion [ 4 , 5 ]. In the aspect of cost efficiency of the material, replacement of 
such expensive alloying element by a low-cost element without severe 
property reduction is highly desired. 
Nb and Ta are refractory elements used in polycrystalline Ni-base 
superalloys for similar purposes, to strengthen γ
′
precipitates and to 
form MC type carbides for higher transverse ductility [ 6 ]. Moreover, 
these elements increase the incipient melting and solidus temperatures, 
favoring conditions for heat treatments involving solutioning/homoge -
nization [ 7 – 9 ]. Noting the chemical similarities of Nb and Ta, Costa et al. 
[ 10 ] evaluated the effects of replacing Ta by Nb (at.% basis) in the phase 
transformation temperatures, microstructure and mechanical properties 
of Nb-modified MAR-M247, an alloy similar to MAR-M246. According 
to their results, although both conventional and Nb-modified alloys

between Ta and Nb, and especially the lower cost and density of Nb, this 
investigation aimed to further evaluate the effects of complete Ta

Ni-based superalloy. Creep tests were carried out in air at 800, 900,

2. Experimental procedures 
Conventional and Nb-modified polycrystalline MAR-M246 superal -
loys were prepared through vacuum induction melting (VIM) from 
commercial raw-materials and investment cast into pre-heated ceramic 
molds in the form of cylindrical rods of approximately 14 mm diameter 
× 130 mm length. Table 1 shows the nominal chemical compositions of 
the alloys in wt.%, noting that both Ta and Nb contents are the same in 
at. % basis. 
Cylindrical samples were machined out from the central part of the 
as-cast rods by electron discharge machining (EDM) and then mechan -
ically machined to produce creep specimens with the gauge dimensions 
near φ 4 × 14 mm and a total length of 42 mm. Heat treatment was not 
applied to the specimens since turbocharger turbine blades/impellers 
are used directly from the as-cast state. Before each creep test, the cy -
lindrical surface layer (~0.1 mm in depth) of the specimens ’ gauge 
section was removed by low stress mechanical polishing (finished by 
P4000 European standard SiC abrasive paper) to remove residual 
scratches and stresses inherited from machining processes. 
Creep tests were performed in air, using a resistive heating furnace, 
at 800 
◦
C (475/500/550 MPa), 900 
◦
C (300/350/400 MPa) and 1000 
◦
C 
(150/200/225 MPa) with ± 1 
◦
C accuracy under constant applied load. 
The actual gauge section diameter of each individual creep specimen, 
after polishing, was measured and used to calculate the applying 
weights. A soak time of 3 h was applied to ensure thermal stability along 
the loading line and to eliminate any residual stresses from the 
machining/polishing step. The reader is referred to a previous article for 
further experimental details regarding these creep tests [ 17 ]. Additional 
creep tests were carried out at 900 
◦
C/350 MPa using radiant heating 
furnace in high vacuum conditions (below 1.0 × 10
 6 
mbar) to evaluate 
the effect of oxidation on the creep behavior of both alloys. After creep 
tests, the specimens were longitudinally cut by EDM to avoid any sig -
nificant microstructural damage. In addition, oxidation tests at 1000 
◦
C 
for 30 min were performed in air, for both alloys, without any external 
applied load. 
Microstructural changes occurring during the creep tests were veri -
fied with the aid of scanning electron microscopy (SEM) using back -
scattered electron (BSE) imaging mode under 15 kV in a Hitachi 
TM3000 SEM equipped with an Oxford Energy Dispersive X-ray spec -
trometer (EDS) calibrated with electronic standards. Fracture surface 
characterization of selected crept samples was performed using a Zeiss 
EVO MA15 SEM in secondary electrons (SE) and BSE imaging modes. 
Metallographic preparation of all samples in this investigation were 
performed by conventional route: hot mounting in bakelite resin; 
grinding with SiC paper (#320, #600, #800, and #1200), polishing 
with colloidal alumina suspension (1 μ m); etching using aqua regia (1/3 
HNO
3 
+ 2/3 HCl, vol. parts) for 5 s to reveal microstructural details.

4. Discussion 
According to the results presented in this study, both conventional 
and Nb-modified Mar-M246 as-cast alloys presented similar micro -
structural features ( Figs. 1 and 2 ) in terms of existing phases, which 
should be related to the minor chemical composition variation between 
them as well as the chemical similarities between Nb and Ta. Such 
similarities have been predicted by thermodynamic simulations in a 
previous investigation [ 18 ] as well as in this study, as shown as Fig. 3 , 
and finally proven by the experimental results ( Figs. 1 and 2 , Table 2 ). In 
addition, it agrees with the evidences highlighted by other authors that 
the total replacement of Ta by Nb does not significantly influence the 
quantitative contents of the elements in the phases they participate 
[ 30 – 32 ]. However, pronounced difference appeared in creep perfor -
mances from the present study. This section discusses the mechanisms 
underlying the creep behaviors of the experimental alloys. 
4.1. Oxidation induced intergranular creep damage 
One important aspect which has contributed significantly to the 
creep rupture life of both alloys is their oxidation behavior. Oxidation 
damage has been reported in the studies of MAR-M247 and M200 + Hf 
alloys, and it has been shown that carbides connected to the surface are 
preferentially oxidized and become crack initiation sites [ 15 , 17 , 33 , 34 ]. 
The results from this investigation, shown in Fig. 10 , suggest that car -
bides could be already cracked during the soak time prior to the load 
application. Considering the carbides located at the grain boundaries in 
the interdendritic regions, these cracked/oxidized carbides allow oxy -
gen diffusion along grain boundaries, especially those transverse to the 
applied load direction. Evidences that cracked carbides at the surface 
could control the minimum creep rate in this environment are available 
in references [ 15 , 24 , 35 – 38 ]. Indeed, all creep tested specimens in this 
work presented predominantly intergranular fracture, as indicated in 
Fig. 6 . Borides found in the initial microstructure also act similarly to 
carbides, preferably form oxides and can be a source of secondary cracks 
[ 37 , 38 ].

oxide scales consisting of an inner Al

4.2. Bulk microstructural changes during the creep tests 
From our results, there is a competition/synergy between the inter -
nal damages and the oxidation damages which has already been re -
ported in the literature [ 40 ]. Sources of internal creep damage are 
discussed in this section. 
It is known that micro-segregation occurs during the dendritic 
growth in the conventional castings made of Ni-base superalloys [ 41 ]. 
As presented previously ( Table 2 ), it was noted that W and Co still re -
mains segregated to the dendrite core for all tested specimens after the 
creep tests, whilst γ
′
-forming elements such as Al, Ti, Nb/Ta remains 
segregated to the interdendritic region. These results are in accordance 
with the segregation patterns after thermal exposure of similar alloys 
[ 26 ]. Thus, the presence of segregation after creep tests suggests that the 
temperature and the time of exposure during all tests were not enough to 
eliminate the initial segregation pattern from the as-cast alloys. In 
addition to the thermal expansion differences between carbides/borides 
and γ / γ
′
phases, this segregation may cause thermal expansion 
mismatch between dendritic and interdendritic regions, resulting in 
stress concentration at the dendrite/interdendritic interface region [ 42 ]. 
To achieve the microstructural stability in these regions, dislocations are 
generated to alleviate these stresses. As an alternative to reduce these 
internal stresses, solution heat treatments have been used to homogenize 
the alloy and annihilate some dislocations [ 26 , 43 ]. 
It was observed in some creep specimens tested at 900 
◦
C and 
1000 
◦
C the presence of needle-like precipitates in the interdendritic 
region and connected to some carbides ( Fig. 11 (c, e, f). These pre -
cipitates are assumed to be μ phase [ 27 , 29 ] and their formation is 
favored by the enrichment of Cr, W, and Mo in those regions by a 
stress-induced diffusion of these elements via dislocations, a phenome -
non known as pipe diffusion. Co and Cr are known to segregate at dis -
locations during deformation at high temperatures [ 33 ] as well as W and 
Mo ( γ and μ phase formers) [ 27 , 34 ]. Assuming that dislocation density 
increases faster at interdendritic regions, enrichment of such μ phase 
formers elements would result in a higher driving force for precipitation 
and growth of μ phase during deformation at high temperatures [ 27 , 33 ]. 
In addition, μ phase can also nucleate from M
6
C carbides due to crystal 
structure similarities and chemical composition of these phases [ 44 ]. 
There are several reports in the literature informing μ phase precipitates 
in the interdendritic regions of Ni-based superalloys with composition 
similar to the conventional or Nb-modified MAR-M246, when exposed 
at 900 – 1000 
◦
C temperature range [ 26 , 27 , 44 , 45 ]. 
In addition to TCP precipitation, MC decomposition, that may 
decrease the creep properties of the alloys, was also observed in the 
creep tested specimens. This decomposition releases C into the matrix 
which subsequently reacts with either Cr to form M
23
C
6 
or with W and 
Mo to form M
6
C. This decomposition could also result in a local 
enrichment of W, Co, Cr, and Mo in the surrounding area of MC carbides 
to form TCP phases, which is the mechanism of μ phase formation from 
MC carbides [ 27 – 29 , 33 , 44 ]. The precipitation of these new carbides and 
the μ phase (see Table 3 ) creates a depleted zone of γ -matrix strength -
ening elements, reducing γ -strength and increasing the proportions of 
γ
′
-formers within this zone. Thus, γ
′
thin film (near 10 μ m thickness) is 
created around these precipitates as evidenced in Fig. 11 (c,f). Due to the 
lower strength of these regions caused by weakness of γ matrix and the 
brittleness of the minor phase precipitates (carbides and μ phase), cracks 
can initiate directly from the precipitates or at the interface between the 
precipitates and γ / γ
′
substrate ( Fig. 11 (c)). TCP phases (such as μ phase) 
and carbides are indeed known to be stress concentrators, leading to a 
local disturbance of the γ / γ
′
microstructure in their vicinity and possible 
recrystallization [ 15 , 46 , 47 ]. Moreover, this morphology further en -
hances the crack propagation from the surface layer as these precipitates 
are mostly distributed in the interdendritic region, close or connected to 
grain boundaries. This crack path is clearly proven by the fractographies 
shown in Fig. 6 . 
4.3. Reasons for the lower creep rupture life of Nb-modified alloy 
According to the results of this investigation, it is clear that the 
conventional MAR-M246 alloy has better creep properties compared to 
the Nb-modified version, particularly at T > 800 
◦
C. In other words, the 
complete replacement of Ta by Nb has direct impact on the creep 
properties of the alloys. This direct comparison of two materials at the 
same creep conditions in air and vacuum is very valuable in the scope of 
alloy design and application design that considers cost/property trade- 
off. In this study, several aspects have been discussed to explain the 
creep damaging mechanism involved in these alloys. 
Among all aspects, the oxidation induced damage is the most pro -
nounced one. For specimens tested in air, there is larger γ
′
-depleted zone 
beneath the oxide scale in the case of Nb-modified alloy. The larger 
γ
′
-depleted zones are likely due to the higher internal diffusivity and/or 
lower adhesiveness of oxide scale in the Nb-modified alloy [ 48 ]. An 
increase of γ
′
-depleted zone means a reduction of deformation resistance 
in this region, since γ
′
particles block the dislocations movement. 
Assuming that this γ
′
-depleted zone has no load bearing capability, one 
could calculate an increased effective stress compared to the initially 
applied stress. The typical increases in effective stress taking into ac -
count such a loss of load bearing section are in the 2 – 5 MPa range at 
1000 
◦
C, 2 – 8 MPa range at 900 
◦
C and 2 – 4 MPa range at 800 
◦
C for both 
alloys. These increases in effective stresses are far too low to be able to 
account for the difference in creep resistance between both alloys, ac -
cording the Larson Miller diagram presented in Fig. 5 . However, the

comparison to the one obtained for specimens tested in air, there is still a 
gap in creep lifetime of the two experimental alloys. The decreased creep 
life gap in vacuum is the clear evidence that Nb-modified alloy has 
higher oxidation sensitivity during high temperature creep. At the same 
time, this creep life difference in vacuum informs that bulk micro -
structure of the Nb-modified alloy is less resistant to creep loading 
compared to the conventional alloy, probably due to the higher internal 
diffusivity of elements, as discussed above. 
As the temperature increases, it can be assumed that oxidation of 
surface layer will be more active. This explains the increasing deviation 
of creep life between conventional and Nb-modified alloys with the 
increasing temperature. In addition, longer creep life is expected for any 
kind of alloy if the applied stress decreases. However, the creep life 
difference at 1000 
◦

stress) is relatively smaller than at other stress levels at the same tem -

important part in the creep damaging mechanisms at this condition. The 
temperature around 1000 – 1100 
◦

of TCP phase. With increasing internal diffusion of the elements at such 
high temperature, microstructure degradation occurs much faster and 
more creep damage is possibly accumulated within the bulk at this lower 
stress condition than other creep conditions. Because of chemical simi -
larity between Ta and Nb as well as their initial microstructures, 
enhanced degradation in the bulk microstructure has probably affected 
evenly both alloys in the same duration. The conventional MAR-M246 
was exposed in the creep loading condition for much longer time, 
which means more microstructure degradation occurred for the con -
ventional alloy, and consequently decreased the creep life difference 
with the Nb-modified alloy. Even if the Nb-modified alloy is more prone

5. Conclusions 
Taking advantage of cost effectiveness and chemical similarity of Nb 
to Ta, this work investigated the creep behavior of MAR-M246 Ni-based 
superalloy with complete replacement of Ta by Nb (in at. % basis) in its 
chemical composition. Creep tests were performed on both conventional 
and Nb-modified MAR-M246 alloys in the 800 – 1000 
◦
C temperature 
range in air under several applied stress conditions. Creep tests in vac -
uum and non-loading oxidation tests were also performed to verify 
possible oxidation effects on the creep behavior. The following conclu -
sions can be written from the presented results: 
• The conventional and the Nb-modified alloys have similar initial 
microstructure in terms of primary dendrite arm spacing, grain size, 
intrinsic phases ( γ matrix, γ
′
precipitates, γ / γ
′
-eutectics, carbides, 
and borides), and precipitate sizes. EDS analyses showed that the 
distribution of alloying elements in both alloys are similar except for 
Ta and Nb. 
• The Nb-modified alloy presented shorter creep life for all testing 
conditions at 900 and 1000 
◦
C, however, it was comparable at 800 
◦
C 
with lower applied stress of 475 MPa. Oxidation induced creep 
damages were more pronounced in the Nb-modified alloy compared 
to the conventional one. Transverse crack initiation from oxidized 
carbides; oxidized and expanded carbides creating local strain field 
and triggering recrystallization; and penetration of γ
′
-depleted layer 
through oxidized grain boundary were observed as oxidation 
induced damages. 
• Both the conventional and the Nb-modified alloys showed improved 
creep properties in the creep tests performed in vacuum. Oxidized 
carbide showed volume expansion and crack after non-loading 
oxidation at 1000 
◦
C. Oxidation induced creep damages are proven 
to be starting from soaking period and playing important role in the 
creep damage mechanism of both experimental alloys. 
• TCP precipitation and carbide decomposition were observed in both 
alloys, but the Nb-modified alloy is more prone to these micro -
structural degradation during high-temperature exposure compared 
to the conventional alloy. This is probably due to the higher internal 
diffusivity of elements in the Nb-modified alloy. At lower tempera -
tures, such as 800 
◦
C/475 MPa, creep properties of both tested alloys 
were similar. It is likely due to the fact that precipitate shearing is 
dominant over the diffusion related creep damages at this 
temperature.
