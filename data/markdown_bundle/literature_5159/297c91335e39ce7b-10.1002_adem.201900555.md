1. Introduction 
Superplasticity of crystalline (metallic, ceramic, composite) materials is one of the favorite 
subjects that Prof. Terence G. Langdon is most responsible for bringing int o the scientific 
mainstream. His first works on superplasticity published in 1977 [1, 2], then Prof. Langdon has 
authored over hundred publications on this subject, including review articles [3-5] actually 
developing very important insights about the nature of this phenomenon. As a n active 
member and Chairman (1991 -1997) of the International Committee on Superplasticity he 
gave an impulse to encourage the studies of superplasticity in leading laboratories worldwide. 
His earlier scientific results include the first experimental evidence for the occurrence of 
cavitation in superplastic flow [1] and set a world record of >5000% elongation in a lead -tin 
alloy [2]: this exciting result was listed for several years in the “Guinness Book of Records”. It 
is well known that superplasticity is associated with grain boundary sliding (GBS ) and with 
attempts to determine the role of GBS. Prof. Langdon undertook the first experimental 
measurements of the grain boundary sliding contributions from intercrystalline and interpha se 
boundaries in a superplastic alloy [6]. When later he returned to the problem of grain boundary 
behavior, he developed a theoretical model of GBS, that combined both creep and 
superplastic conditions [7].

2. Materials and Experimental Methods 
In this work aluminum alloy of the 7xxx series was used as the material for study with 
chemical composition as follows: Al -4.8Zn-1.2Mg-0.14Zr (wt. %). Disc -shaped samples 
(diameter 20 mm, thickness 1.4 mm) were cut out from the extruded rods for SPD processing 
using high pressure torsion (HPT). Following the previous studies [12, 13 ] the alloy was 
homogenized at 470 C for 1 hour and water-quenched immediately before HPT processing. 
The samples were subsequently processed by HPT for ten revolutions at room temperature 
(RT) under a hydraulic pressure of 6 GPa and at a ram rotation speed of 1 rpm. As was shown 
in earlier works [14, 15], such HPT processing conditions for aluminum alloys of the 7xxx series 
allows forming a uniform UFG structure and achieving very high values of strength. 
All investigations on the microstructure and mechanical properties of the alloy were 
performed near the half radius of the disc samples. 
A number of initial samples were subjected to conventional treatment Т 6 that is recommended 
for achieving maximum hardening in the 7xxx s eries alloys [16]. This treatment included a 
solution heat treatment at 470 °С for within 2 hours followed by water quenching to introduce 
a supersaturated solid solution and aging at 120 °С for 24 h .

3. Results 
3.1. Grain refinement and thermal stability 
HPT processing of the alloy Al-4.8Zn-1.2Mg-0.14Zr at RT led to the formation of the UFG 
structure, as shown in Figure 1a. Quantitative analysis showed that the mean grain size was 
17613 nm. As is seen from the selected electron diffraction pattern (SAED) (Fig ure 1а ), the 
grains are separated by boundaries with mostly high-angle misorientations. The noted features 
of the UFG structure are characteristic of many aluminum alloys subjected to HPT processing 
at similar regimes [14, 17]. TEM images of the UFG structure could not reveal the formation of 
dispersed particles of secondary phases, nano -sized clusters and/or atom segregations of 
alloying elements. Such nanostructural features were only recently identified in the 7xxx 
series alloys processed by HPT under similar conditions, using more precise methods of 
scanning transmission electron microscopy (STEM) and 3 -D atom probe tomography (APT) 
[17, 18]. Obviously, such nano -sized elements are also present in the microstructure of the HPT 
processed supersaturated Al-4.8Zn-1.2Mg-0.14Zr alloy, but their observation requires the use 
of higher resolution systems . It is well known that the presence of nano -sized particles, 
clusters and grain -boundary segregations in the UFG structure makes it possible to achieve a 
unique level of strength by additional hardening mechanisms associated with these 
nanostructural features characterizing SPD-processed alloys [14, 17, 18]. This explains the fact 
that а fter HPT at RT the microhardness , HV=1867 of the UFG alloy Al-4.8Zn-1.2Mg-
0.14Zr exceeded by ~ 80% the microhardness, (HV=1025) of the sample after conventional 
heat treatment T6 (Figure 2).

structure became more significant with a rise in annealing temperature (Figure 1 b,c). After

conventionally T6-treated samples are shown in Figure 3a. Experimental results show that the 
strength characteristics (YS = 631 10 MPa and UTS = 678 5 MPa) of the HPT-processed 
samples are significantly higher than that after conventional heat treatment T6 (YS = 3979 MPa,

sample, which is comparable to the high strength of commercial alloy 7075 processed by 
high-pressure sliding (HPS) [22], is obviously related not only to the formation of a 
homogeneous UFG structure, but it is also possible d ue to the formation of nanoclusters and 
atom segregations of the main alloying elements in the aluminum matrix [12, 18]. According to 
the data in [14, 18], the contribution of these nano -sized elements may reach more than 15% of 
the total hardening. 
At the same time, the increase in strength of the material after HPT is accompanied by a 
noticeable decrease in ductility, especially in uniform elongation (from 10 to only 1.5%) as 
compared to the coarse-grained alloy after T6 treatment (Figure 3a). Such change in ductility of 
the UFG materials under tension at RT is typical for many aluminum alloys subjected to SPD[23-26]. 
However, it should be noted that total elongation to failure (δ = 7.8±0.4%) demonstrated by the 
UFG alloy in a high strength state is acceptable for its practical application.

Conclusion 
1. The application of HPT processing contributes to the formation of homogeneous 
ultrafine-grained structure in the alloy Al -Zn-Mg-Zr under study with a mean grain 
size of ~170 nm that remains sufficiently stable up to the temperatures of 120 -150°C. 
After annealing at higher temperatures, a slight grain growth occurs and the 
appearance of nano-sized precipitates of second phase 15 -30 nm in size is observed in 
the structure. 
2. The investigated UFG alloy demonstrates a very high strength at room temperature 
(σUTS higher than 670 MPa) and, at a temperature of 120 -150°C, it exhibits typical 
superplastic behavior but maintains a high-strength state under normal conditions after 
superplastic deformation. 
3. The superplastic behavior of the UFG alloy at such low temperatures may be caused 
by the presence of grain -boundary zinc segregations that activate the development of 
grain boundary sliding and contribute to the exhibition of the superplasticity effect.

Graphical abstract 
 
In this work, the ultrafine -grained (UFG) 7xxx series alloy Al -4.8Zn-1.2Mg-0.14Zr 
demonstrates superplasticity at unusually low temperatures of 120 -170°C while maintaining 
its high-strength state. The UFG structure was formed by high pressure torsion (HPT) at room 
temperature (RT), which leads to considerable increase in the strength characteristics by ~ 
60% compared to the material after conventional heat treatment T6. It was found that the 
UFG alloy exhibits thermostability when testing or annealing up to 170°C. Deforming by 
tensile test at strain rate of 10 –4 s–1, the elongation to failure at 120 and 170°C exceeded 250 
and 500%, respectively, whereas the strain rate sensitivity reached 0.45, which is typical value 
characterizing superplastic deformation. After superplastic deformation, the strength 
characteristics of the UFG alloy at RT maintained 25 -50% higher than that after conventional 
treatment T6. The origin of such superior behavior of the UFG alloy is discussed.
