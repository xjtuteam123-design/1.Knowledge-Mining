Abstract 
In this study, Ti-Al-Nb, Ti-Ni-Nb and Ni-Cr-Nb system alloys were designed and 
incorporated in order to construct a gradient structure at the surface of the joined 
Ti3Al base material. And the Ti 3Al-based alloy and Ni -based superalloy were 
successfully joined together using gas tungsten arc (GTA) welding technology. The 
microstructure evolution, mechanical properties and fractured behaviors of the joints 
were investigated. The gradient structure remarkably decreased the formation 
tendency of brittle phases within the joints compared with a single filler alloy and thus 
improved the joint strength effectively. The average room-temperature tensile strength 
of the Ti3Al/In718 dissimilar joint reached 353 MPa, and the strength value at 873 K 
was 245 MPa. At the Ti-Ni-Nb/Ni-Cr-Nb interface, some Ni3(Nb, Ti)+(Nb, Ti)Cr2 and 
TiNi3 phases were detected in the Ti-Ni-Nb matrix. It was believed that their presence 
decreased the room -temperature strength of the Ti -Ni-Nb alloy but improved its 
high-temperature strength. 
Keywords: Ti3Al; Ni-based superalloy ; dissimilar welding ; gradient structure ; 
microstructure; mechanical property.

1 Introduction 
The newly developed Ti3Al-based alloy , such as Ti-24Al-11Nb (at.%) [1] , 
Ti-24Al-17Nb (at.%) [2], Ti-13.4Al-21.2Nb (wt.%) [3], and so on, has many desirable 
properties including low density, superior strength, high modulus, and good 
performance at elevated temperatures, which make it suitable and very attractive as 
aerospace materials [4]. At present, Ti 3Al-based alloy is inclined to be designed and 
adopted as one of the candidate materials for heat-resistance components in advanced 
aero-engines [5, 6]. Apparently, the utility of this material for engineering applications 
needs the joining technolog ies of both Ti 3Al-based alloy to itself and Ti 3Al-based 
alloy to other materials. 
For the joining of Ti 3Al-based alloy to itself, the weldability proble m has been 
basically solved through choosing appropriate joining processes and sound joints can 
be obtained [2, 7, 8 ]. Considering the dissimilar joining between Ti 3Al-based alloy 
and other materials, the researches mainly focus on welding Ti3Al to Ti-based alloys 
and some advances have been achieved [7]. For example, electron beam welding of 
Ti3Al-based alloy to TC11 alloy and laser beam welding of Ti 3Al to TC4 alloy were 
investigated by Tan et al. and Lei et al. respectively, and the joint strengths at room 
temperature were equal to those of the joined Ti-based alloys [9, 10]. 
But in fact, the dissimilar joining between Ti 3Al-based alloy and Ni -based alloy 
is much more attractive for engineering applications , because this type of dissimilar 
material combination could not only greatly reduce the weight of aero-engine parts, 
but also make full use of the high -temperature property advantages of the two alloys. 
For example, Ti3Al-based alloy can be used to manufacture the outer case of advanced 
aero-engine compressor and its connection with the mounting edge usually made of 
Inconel 718 superalloy will need the dissimilar joining technology. Furthermore, the 
joining of the active cooling structure and the mounting seat in a erospace scramjet 
engine also needs the welding of Ti3Al to Ni-based wrought superalloy. However, so 
far, few researches have been reported concerning the joining of the dissimilar 
materials. 
Joining dissimilar materials such as Ti3Al-based alloy and Ni-based superalloy is

It should be noted that , the joining of Ti3Al-based alloy and Ni -based superalloy 
is not only an extrem ely difficult technology problem but also a key scientific issue. 
Undoubtedly, to achieve a sound dissimilar joint it becomes very important to depress 
the formation of interfacial brittle phases and to ensure good metallurgical quality at 
the joining interface. 
2 Metallurgical basis for the joining of Ti3Al-based alloy to Ni-based superalloy 
In the previous experimental study, a number of filler materials have been tried 
to join a Ti3Al-based alloy and a Ni-based superalloy to investigate the individual 
metallurgical effect of each element, and the corresponding results can be summarized 
as follows: 
(1) Each one of Ni -based superalloy, stainless steel, pure Ni, pure Cu and Ni-Cu 
solid solution alloy could form good metallurgical combination with the joined 
Ni-based superalloy, but exhibits poor compatibility with the joined Ti3Al substrate. 
When welded with Ni -based superalloy, stainless steel and pure Cu as filler 
materials, the joints all cracked at the interface between the Ti3Al and the weld 
immediately after welding . And when welded with pure Ni, the joint strength was 
only 17 MPa. The joints made with CuNi 18-22 (at.%) filler alloy gave a tensile strength 
of 163 MPa [20], and though Ni62-68Cu (at.%) filler increased the joint strength to 242

Ni3Nb and Ni6Nb7 were generated at the interface. Furthermore, the residual pure Nb 
interlayer within the joint would be harmful to its mechanical performance at elevated 
temperatures. 
In all these researches, preheating processes were necessary prior to the 
dissimilar welding and the joint strength showed relatively low values. Therefore, it is 
extremely difficult and even impossible to find a single filler material that shows good 
metallurgical compatibility concurrently with the two base alloys and thus to achieve 
a strong joint. 
Therefore, i n this work, an attempt was made to join Ti 3Al-based alloy to 
Ni-based superalloy through constructing a gradient structure at the surface of the 
joined Ti 3Al substrate before the dissimilar welding. Using gas tungsten arc ( GTA) 
welding technology, several intermediate alloys with different chemical compositions 
were designed and built up at the surface of the Ti 3Al-based alloy layer by layer, so 
that the composition could present a gradient transition from the Ti3Al-based alloy to 
the Ni-based superalloy in this multi-layered structure. Subsequently GTA welding 
between the surface layer of the gradient structure and the Ni-based superalloy w as

3.3 Welding experiments 
Alloy ingots with nominal compositions of TiAl 12-21Nb25-34, TiNi36-45Nb14-22, 
TiNi46-56Nb7-13 and NiCr14-22Nb5-10 were prepared by arc melting the mixtures of the 
pure elements in a Ti -gettered high purified Ar atmosphere. Bars with cross section 
dimensions of 1×2 mm2 were machined from the ingots and cleaned . They were used 
as the filler alloys in the subsequent welding experiment s. The diameter of the used 
NiCr-based filler wire is 2 mm.

The preparation of the gradient stru cture was then carried out. Using GTA 
welding technology, the filler alloys were built up at the surface of the joined Ti3Al 
base alloy layer by layer in the sequence of TiAl 12-21Nb25-34, TiNi36-45Nb14-22,

alloy were GTA welded using the NiCr -based filler alloy with a welding speed of 
70~110 mm/min and a current of 45 ~65 A DC. To prevent oxidation during the 
welding process, flowing argon shielding was applied to the weld bead with flow rates 
of 15 L/min for the top, 20 L/min for the tail and 8 L/min for the bottom surfaces, 
respectively. 
3.4 Microstructure observation, phase analysis and mechanical property tests 
Microstructure evolution of the Ti 3Al/Ni-based superalloy joint was investigated 
using a scanning electron microscopy (SEM) equipped with an X -ray energy 
dispersive spectroscopy (EDS). A software named Java -based materials properties 
software V7.0 (JMatPro 7.0) was used to do thermodynamic calculation of the 
non-equilibrium solidification process during welding, to investigate the phas es that 
might be produced in the joint. 
Microhardness values of various regions within the joint were measured using a 
Vickers hardness tester with a load of 200 gf and load time of 15 s. At least 5 
indentations were performed at each region. Transverse te nsile tests were conducted 
to evaluate the joint strengths at room temperature and at 873 K , respectively. The 
fracture behaviors of the joints were studied by SEM , EDS and X-ray diffraction 
(XRD).

In this study, the Ti 3Al/In718 joint strength has been apparently increased by 
designing and constructing a gradient structure at the surface of the joined Ti3Al base 
alloy before the final dissimilar welding . The design and the fabrication of the 
gradient structure was based on the concept of functionally gradient materials (FGM)

variation but also the material system change of the graded layers.

strength at elevated temperatures could be enhanced by optimizing the chemical 
compositions of the Ti-Ni-Nb filler alloys or choosing other suitable alloy systems to 
substitute for Ti-Ni-Nb. Certainly, the basic principle is to ensure the interface 
metallurgical quality among the layers in the gradient-structured weld of the joint. 
5 Conclusions 
In this study, Ti-Al-Nb, Ti-Ni-Nb, Ni-Cr-Nb system alloys and NiCr -based filler 
alloy were incorporated into Ti 3Al-based alloy/ Ni-based superalloy joint so that a 
gradient structure was constructed within the dissimilar joint. And the Ti 3Al-based 
alloy and In718 alloy has been successfully joined together using GTA welding 
technology. The conclusions are summarized as follows: 
(1) The formation tendency of brittle phases in the j oint has been greatly 
decreased. The average room -temperature tensile strength of the joint has been 
enhanced to 353 MPa, and the strength value at 873 K is 245 MPa , indicating that up 
to 69% of the room-temperature strength can be maintained. Furthermore, no 
preheating process was needed prior to the welding process. 
(2) At the Ti -Al-Nb/Ti-Ni-Nb interface the following phases were detected:

Ti-Ni-Nb phase, (Nb, Ti) solid solution, Ti 2Ni and TiNi intermetall ics, as well as 
eutectic microstructure composed of B2-TiNi and bcc-(Nb, Ti). 
 (3) At the Ti -Ni-Nb/Ni-Cr-Nb interface , some Ni 3(Nb, Ti)+(Nb, Ti)Cr 2 and 
TiNi3 phases are scatteringly distributed in the Ti-Ni-Nb matrix. The presence of 
Ni3(Nb, Ti)+(Nb, Ti)Cr 2 and TiNi 3 decreased the room -temperature strength of the 
Ti-Ni-Nb ternary alloy but improved its high-temperature strength. 
(4) In the dissimilar joint, the Ti -Al-Nb/Ti-Ni-Nb and Ti -Ni-Nb/Ni-Cr-Nb 
interfaces show relatively higher microhardness values than other regions. When 
tested at room temperature the Ti3Al/In718 joint fractured at the layer “C” within the 
Ti-Ni-Nb/Ni-Cr-Nb interface zone (Figure 6) . However, the strength of the whole 
Ti3Al/In718 joint at 873 K was dependent on the Ti-Ni-Nb alloy itself.
