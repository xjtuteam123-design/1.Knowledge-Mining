ABSTRACT
A gradient extrusion method with varying parameters (extrusion temperature, speed, gradient) was employed to 
design different lamellar heterostructures in Mg alloys and then to investigate their effects on the mechanical 
property. The heterostructures consists primarily of fine dynamic recrystallization (DRXed) grains and three non- 
recrystallized coarse grain types (Type A: fine shorty, Type B: elongated, Type C: large). The findings showed that 
elevated extrusion speed and temperature resulted in a reduction in the ratio of coarse grains, whereas the 
proportion of coarse grains initially decreased and then increased with rise in extrusion gradient. The lamellar 
heterostructured alloy containing a lower content of Type C and a higher content of Type A with more uniform 
distribution would possess higher strength. The ultimate tensile strength (UTS) and yield strength (YS) of the 
heterostructured material reached 320 MPa and 259 MPa, showing a significant increase of 94.7 % and 25 % 
compared to the equiaxed structured material. This alloy was extruded at 400 
◦
C, maintaining a speed of 2 mm/s 
with extrusion gradient: T1, resulting in heterostructures with a total coarse grain content of 8.1 %, (comprising 
80 % of Type A, 20 % of Type B, and 0 % of Type C) The loading-unloading-reloading (LUR) tests elucidated that 
the high strength of lamellar alloy came from increased hetero-deformation induced (HDI) stress. Furthermore, 
observation of the fracture surface morphology revealed coarse-fine grain hinders further crack propagation. This 
study systematically optimized the configuration of heterogeneous structures, providing meaningful insights to 
fabricate high-performance lamellar heterogeneous structure alloys.
1. Introduction
Magnesium alloys, renowned for their exceptional lightweight 
characteristics, superior specific strength, and impressive electromag -
netic shielding capabilities, are extensively utilized in sectors including 
automotive, aerospace, and 3C electronics [ 1 – 3 ]. However, the chal -
lenge of significantly enhancing the strength of materials while main -
taining their ductility remains an unresolved issue, particularly in fields 
requiring lightweight and high-strength materials, such as automotive 
manufacturing [ 4 , 5 ]. In recent years, research has found that hetero -
geneous structured magnesium alloys can maintain good ductility while 
simultaneously enhancing strength [ 6 – 10 ]. Nevertheless, the systematic 
optimization of heterogeneous structures and the production of 
high-performance magnesium alloys through process parameter 
adjustments continue to pose technical challenges.
Scientists have thoroughly investigated the composition of different 
heterogeneous structures, such as gradient structures [ 11 , 12 ], lamellar 
structures [ 13 – 15 ],bimodal structures [ 16 ], and dual-phase structures 
[ 17 ], to accomplish an equilibrium of enhanced strength and ductility. 
Wu et al. [ 12 ] obtained gradient structured steel plates through surface 
mechanical attrition treatment. The gradient structured samples 
exhibited a yield strength 2.6 times that of coarse-grained copper, while 
maintaining comparable elongation. As indicated by Liu et al. [ 18 ], a 
heterogeneous lamellar structure into nickel, significantly enhancing its 
mechanical properties (tensile strength increased from 385.6 MPa to 
694.8 MPa, while elongation remained at 12 %). Additionally, Wang 
et al. [ 19 ] developed a bimodal structure in ultrafine-grained pure 
copper through cold rolling and short-time annealing, achieving UTS

Past research points out the enhanced mechanical properties of het -

heterogeneous interfaces and the hardening process during plastic

response of heterogeneous interfaces during deformation. The design of 
heterogeneous structures requires maximizing HDI to achieve optimal 
mechanical properties [ 15 , 24 ]. This requires comprehending how the 
arrangement, volume fraction, and inherent properties of the compo -
nent areas influence HDI enhancement. The best advantageous structure 
for inducing HDI strengthening and HDI strain hardening so far is the 
heterogeneous lamellar structure, which is important to mention [ 8 , 15 ].
The Mg-Y-Sn alloy has been a research hotspot for many years. 
Adding Y significantly refines the grain structure, enhancing the 
strength and hardness of alloy. Furthermore, adding Y elements to Mg 
not only weakens the strong basal texture of magnesium but also pro -
motes the activation of non-basal slip, resulting in Mg-rare earth alloys 
with high plasticity and good formability [ 25 ]. Additionally, Y has a 
high solubility in Mg, leading to the formation of high-density, finely 
dispersed coherent precipitates, which significantly contribute to pre -
cipitation strengthening [ 26 ]. Wang et al. [ 27 ] and Zhao et al. [ 28 ] 
investigated the mechanical properties of Mg-Sn-Y alloys and pointed 
out that the addition of Sn and Y contributes to the formation of Mg ₂ Sn, 
Sn ₃ Y ₅ , or MgSnY phases, thereby enhancing the room-temperature 
strength Moreover, our previous research [ 26 ], has shown that the 
gradient extrusion technique can lead to the development of a diverse 
lamellar structure in Mg-Y-Sn alloys, significantly enhancing strength 
while maintaining ductility. Therefore, to further reduce costs, we chose 
to decrease the Y and Sn content for further investigation. Nevertheless, 
a comprehensive overview regarding the impact of lamellar features is 
currently missing, such as corase type, volume fraction, size, distribution 
of coarse grain, on mechanical properties of materials with a lamellar 
heterogeneous structure. Therefore, in this study, we employed the 
gradient extrusion method previously proposed by our research group to 
prepare heterogeneous Mg-5Y-1Sn structures. The region without dy -
namic recrystallization (Un-DRXed) was considered as the coarse grain 
layer, while the recrystallized region was considered as the fine grain 
layer. By altering extrusion parameters to influence the DRXed process, 
different proportions, distributions, and types of coarse grains in 
Mg-5Y-1Sn alloy were obtained. We conducted a comprehensive anal -
ysis of how various process parameters impact the lamellar micro -
structure of the Mg-5Y-1Sn alloy, identifying different types, 
distribution, and proportion of coarse grains, Then we studied the 
impact of these factors on mechanical property, and summarized the 
Mg-5Y-1Sn lamellar heterogeneous structure with the best mechanical 
property. In addition, the deformation behavior at the fracture region of 
lamellar heterogeneous and homogeneous samples was observed, and 
the contribution of HDI stress in both samples was quantitatively eval -
uated through LUR tests.

The increase in extrusion temperature provides sufficient thermal 
energy for atoms to overcome energy barriers, facilitating the slip and 
interaction of dislocations. Additionally, the increased atomic diffusion 
rate promotes the rearrangement of dislocations, resulting in larger 
recrystallized grain sizes, which is consistent with theoretical expecta -
tions based on the formula [ 37 – 39 ]. Conversely, an escalation in 
extrusion speed yields a larger actual recrystallized grain size, deviating 
from the anticipated trend of Z. This observed phenomenon aligns with 
the outcomes reported in the studies conducted by Liu and Nie [ 35 , 40 ,
41 ]. Higher extrusion speeds typically result in higher strain rates, 
which increase the generation and accumulation of dislocations within 
the material. During rapid deformation, dislocation density rises 
quickly, leading to the dislocation density required for the formation of 
recrystallized grains. Thus, the elevation in extrusion speed results in the 
generation of significant deformation heat during extrusion. And the 
elevated temperature accelerates the diffusion of atoms across grain 
boundaries, fostering grain growth.
In addition to modifications to extrusion parameters can influence 
both the recrystallization ratio and grain size of the alloys, the uneven 
distribution of the broken second phases along the extrusion direction 
may also be one of the reasons for the appearance of different grain 
structures. Via the particle-stimulated nucleation (PSN) mechanism, an 
profusion of micron-sized second-phases can induce DRXed in the alloys 
[ 34 , 42 ]. In the course of deformation process around these second-phase 
particles, stress concentration occurs, leading to an augmented dislo -
cation density. Subsequently, dislocation motion and annihilation give 
rise to the formation of LAGBs in these deformed regions. These LAGBs 
are regarded as potential nucleation sites for DRXed in the extrusion 
process [ 34 ]. Besides, studies suggest that the second phase may impede 
the migration of LAGBs, preventing the rise in subgrain size and the 
misorientation angle between them, thereby impeding the continuous 
DRXed [ 29 ].
In short, the microstructural evolution of the alloy under varying 
extrusion parameters is intricately influenced by extrusion temperature, 
speed, and gradient.

heterogeneous structures
Fig. 10 (a) illustrates the distribution of total coarse grains and the 
proportions of three distinct types of coarse grains across diverse 
extrusion parameters, while Fig. 10 (b) delineates the corresponding 
mechanical characteristics. Examining Fig. 10 (a), the T400S4T1 exhibits 
the lowest proportion of coarse grains at 0.4 %. Its microstructure pri -
marily comprises uniform equiaxed grains, thereby deviating from a 
typical lamellar structure. Additionally, this alloy records the lowest YS 
and lower UTS among all parameters, measuring 133 MPa and 255 MPa, 
respectively. On the other hand, the T400S1T1 demonstrates the 
maximum proportion of coarse grains, reaching up to 23 %. Its corre -
sponding YS stands at 231 MPa, while UTS is at 266 MPa, accompanied 
by an elongation rate of 8 %. The T400S2T1 with the most favorable 
mechanical property under default parameters exhibits a coarse grain 
proportion of 8.1 %. This highlights that a higher proportion of coarse 
grains does not necessarily correlate with improved mechanical 
properties.
Furthermore, it is noteworthy that the coarse grain proportion of T0 
is 10.5 %, lower than T2 at 18.7 %. However, the YS and UTS of 
T400S2T0 are lower than those of T400S2T2. These observations sug -
gest that the relationship between the coarse grain ratio and the me -
chanical property of the alloy is more intricate than a simple direct 
proportional correlation. Research conducted previously has revealed 
that the exceptional mechanical property of the heterostructured alloy 
stems from the generation of HDI and work hardening [ 6 , 8 ]. The pre -
vious work have pointed that a critical strategy in achieving an optimal 
strength-ductility balance involves creating a heterogeneous configu -
ration characterized by varied ratios and distributions [ 14 , 21 , 22 ]. 
Therefore, conducting a statistical analysis of the proportions and dis -
tributions of different types of coarse grains holds meaningful 
implications.

coarse grains. These cracks propagate toward the fine-grain regions, and 
due to significant stress concentration, small cracks begin to emerge at

5. Conclusions
This research utilized extrusion process to optimize the lamellar 
heterogeneous structure of Mg-5Y-1Sn alloy. A systematic analysis was 
conducted on the influence of coarse grain type, proportion, and dis -
tribution concerning the mechanical properties of the alloy at RT, 
culminating in the characterization of lamellar heterogeneous structure 
in Mg-Y-Sn alloys with the best mechanical properties. The main find -
ings are outlined as follows: 
(1) Increasing extrusion temperature and speed leads to larger fine 
grain size and lower coarse grain proportion. As extrusion 
gradient elevates, the fraction of coarse grains increases at first 
and then diminishes. Additionally, the texture distribution in the 
coarse grain regions of lamellar alloys resembles the overall 
structure, exhibiting a fiber texture, while the fine grain structure 
tends to show rare earth textures.
(2) The increase in extrusion temperature, speed, and gradient 
initially enhances mechanical properties but subsequently leads 
to a decline. The T400S2T1 has the most comprehensive me -
chanical property (UTS of 320 MPa, YS of 259 MPa, and an 
elongation of 12 %). It contains 8.1 % of the total coarse grains, 
which include 80 % of Type A, 20 % of Type B, and 0 % of Type C, 
and demonstrates.
(3) The quantity of Type C exhibits a clear negative correlation with 
both the UTS and YS of the alloy. The content of Type C signifi -
cantly influences the lamellar spacing of all coarse grains. A 
reduction in the content of Type C, coupled with a higher pro -
portion of Type A, leads to a more uniform distribution of coarse 
grains in all regions, which is advantageous for boosting the 
strength of alloy.
(4) It is noted that in the failure process of lamellar heterogeneous 
alloy, internal cracks within coarse grains continuously propa -
gate until they are impeded at the coarse-fine grain boundaries. 
This initiates small cracks in the fine-grain regions near the 
interface, progressively expanding and ultimately culminating in 
fracture. Furthermore, LUR comparative experiments confirmed 
that lamellar heterogeneous materials possess superior back 
stress strengthening.
