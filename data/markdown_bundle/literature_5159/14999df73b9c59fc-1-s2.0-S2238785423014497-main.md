abstract
In this work, a desirable combination of mechanical properties is achieved at 550 /C14 Cb y
tailoring the boundary microstructure between the primary a phase ( ap) and the trans-
formed b structure (btrans) in a dual-phase Ti-6242s titanium alloy. The ap/btrans boundary is
displaced by a transition region that consists of b nano-plates or nanoprecipitates gradu-
ally penetrating into the ap in a particular semi-equiaxed microstructure (S-ES). The results
show that the ap/btrans boundary, where strain concentration easily occurs during defor-
mation in the equiaxed microstructure (ES), induces recrystallization softening in the a
phase. However, the transition region in the S-ES alleviates the strain localization and thus
effectively inhibits the recrystallization softening that occurred in the a phase with
concentrated strain. These b plates/precipitates in this region in turn enable an appropriate
accumulation of dislocations. Signiﬁcant improvement in the high-temperature strength,
~240 MPa for the yield strength, is obtained for the S-ES relative to the ES. This work
provides a new strategy for achieving outstanding strength at high temperature by
designing special boundary architecture in dual-phase titanium alloys.

and high-temperature strength along with excellent process-
ability and are widely used in the aerospace industry for parts
such as aircraft load-bearing components and gas turbine
engine compressor disks [ 1e3]. Generally, a better combina-
tion of strength and ductility at both room temperature and
high temperature is achieved with the equiaxed structure
consisting of the primary a phase ( a
p) and lamellar trans-
formed b structure (btrans)[ 4e6], which is applicable in severe

2. Experimental procedures
The experimental material was rolled and annealed in the Ti-
6242s alloy, and its chemical composition was determined by
X-ray ﬂuorescence (Shimadzu XRF-1800), as shown in Table 1.
The b transition temperature (T
b) of this alloy was approxi-
mately 1006 /C14 C, as determined with a DIL402C thermal
dilatometer.
Samples of the Ti-6242s alloy measuring 410 /C2 90 mm were
solution treated at 950 /C14 C for 1 h and cooled by water (marked
as S0). Some of the samples were heated to temperature near
the b phase transition point by induction heating and held
there for 12 s (marked as S12) and 14 s (marked as S14). Finally,
S0, S12 and S14 were aged at 580
/C14 C for 8 h and air cooled. The
heat treatment procedures are illustrated in Fig. (1) . All sam-
ples were subjected to hot tensile tests at 550 /C14 C with an MTS
Landmark electrohydraulic servo-test system. To ensure the
reliability of the experimental data, three experiments were
conducted for each sample. Prior to the tensile test, the sam-
ple was heated to the desired test temperature at a rate of
10
/C14 C/s for a dwell time of 5 min to eliminate the temperature
gradient. The strain rate was 5.5 /C2 10/C0 4 s/C0 1, and the sample
size is shown in Fig. (2).
Optical microscopy (OM), scanning electron microscopy
(SEM), electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD), and trans-
mission electron microscopy (TEM) were used to characterize
the microstructures. Image-Pro Plus 6.0 software was used to
quantify the contents of the a
p and btrans. Before microstruc-
tural observations, metallographic polishing was performed,
followed by etching in Kroll 's reagent (10% HF, 30% HNO 3, and
70% H 2O) for 60 s. EBSD (Oxford C nano) analyses combined
with ﬁeld emission scanning electron microscopy (FESEM)
were used to characterize the initial and deformed micro-
structures with step length of 100 nm. The EBSD samples were
prepared by mechanical grinding and argon-ion polishing.
Channel 5 software was used for processing the EBSD data to
analyze the kernel average misorientation (KAM) and recrys-
tallization distribution of the samples. An FEI Talos F200X
ﬁeld-emission TEM system was used to observe the micro-
structure near the fracture site of the specimen.

3.2. Tensile properties at 550 /C14 C
The engineering stress ‒strain curves determined for the S0,
S12, and S14 samples at 550 /C14 C are shown in Fig. 4(a). Fig. 4(b)
shows the corresponding yield strength (YS), ultimate tensile
strength (UTS) and elongation (EL). The S0 sample has the
highest elongation; however, its high-temperature strength is
signiﬁcantly lower than those of the S12 and S14 samples. The
yield strength of the S12 sample is increased to 889 MPa with
an increment of 241 MPa relative to the 648 MPa YS of the S0
sample. Moreover, the tensile strength increases from
864 MPa to 1039 MPa. The yield strength and tensile strength
increase by 37% and 20%, respectively. In addition, the elon-
gation decreases from 17.8% to 12.91%, while the differences
in the a
p contents for the S0 and S12 samples are approxi-
mately 3%. Although the S14 sample with an ap content of
38.60% exhibits higher strength than the S0 sample, its
strength and ductility are lower than those of the S12 sample.
Due to the excessive insertion of lamellae into the a
p region,
the deformation behavior of S14 is similar to that of the
lamellar structure. That is, many dislocations cannot accu-
mulate in the microstructure during deformation, and the
effect of strain hardening is reduced. Additionally, the
lamellae structure exhibits low strain capacity, and cracks
appears earlier, resulting in lower plasticity [ 13,14,28]. In this
work, the S12 sample achieves the optimal strength-ductility
synergy, and higher contents of the lamellar structure are
unfavorable to the high-temperature mechanical properties.

4. Conclusions
In this work, a comparative investigation is performed to
examine the mechanical properties of Ti-6242s alloys at 550
/C14 C
with two representative microstructures, i.e., the equiaxed
and semi-equiaxed microstructures. The main conclusions
are as follows.
(1) Signiﬁcant improvement in the high-temperature
strength, ~240 MPa for the yield strength, is obtained
in the semi-equiaxed S12 sample with a slight loss of
ductility, ~5% for elongation.
(2) Dynamic recrystallization occurred in the a phase
adjacent to the a
p/btrans boundaries, where strain
concentration occurs under tension and mainly ac-
counts for the decreased strength in the equiaxed S0
sample.
(3) In the semi-equiaxed S12 sample, however, the ap/btrans
boundaries are displaced by the transition region that
consists of b nano-plates gradually grown into ap and b
nano-precipitates with a gradient dispersion in the ap.
This largely alleviates strain localization and effectively
inhibits recrystallization softening, whereas both nano-
precipitates and nano-plates contribute to an appro-
priate accumulation of dislocations. Both factors in-
crease the high-temperature strength.
