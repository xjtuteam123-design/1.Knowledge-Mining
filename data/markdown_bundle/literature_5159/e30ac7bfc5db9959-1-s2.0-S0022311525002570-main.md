ABSTRACT
The application of W alloys is greatly hindered by the low-temperature brittleness and recrystallization 
embrittlement. In this work, hot-rolled W alloys with enhanced mechanical properties and thermal stability were 
fabricated by the dispersion of nanoscale potassium (K) bubbles and ZrC particles. The K bubbles and ZrC 
particles strengthened W (WZCK) alloy is ductile with a tensile strength of 960 MPa at 150 
◦
C, while the K-doped 
W (WK) alloy is brittle. WZCK exhibits both higher strength and lower ductile-brittle transition temperature 
(DBTT) than the WK, and the recrystallization temperature is ~1600 
◦
C. After annealing at 1600 
◦
C for 1 h, the 
tensile strength of WZCK at 500 
◦
C is 460 MPa, which is over 40 % higher than that of WK, and the total 
elongation is 26 %. Nanosized ZrC particles and K bubbles dispersed in W significantly improve the strength and 
high temperature stability of the alloys, while ZrC particles can also react with impurity oxygen to form Zr-C-O or 
ZrO
2 
particles and suppress low-temperature brittleness.
1. Introduction
Tungsten (W) and W-based alloys have been considered promising 
candidates for plasma-facing materials (PFMs) in future fusion reactors 
due to their high melting temperature, high thermal conductivity, high 
energy threshold for sputtering and low tritium retention [ 1 – 5 ]. How -
ever, W also exhibits several disadvantages such as room-temperature 
brittleness [ 6 ], recrystallization brittleness [ 7 ], and 
irradiation-induced brittleness [ 8 , 9 ]. The low fracture toughness at 
room temperature due to high ductile-to-brittle transition temperature 
(DBTT) greatly hinders their application. At high temperatures, grain 
coarsening by recrystallization causes the increase in the DBTT and the 
degradation in strength and toughness.
The dispersion of small amounts of thermally-stable oxide (La

example, ultra-fine grained W-(0.3 – 0.7)%TiC alloys prepared by me -
chanical alloying and hot isostatic pressing exhibit high three-point 
bending fracture strength of 1.6 – 2 GPa, while the hot plastically 
deformed W-1.1 %TiC exhibits a fracture strength of 4.4 GPa [ 13 , 14 ].

lower DBTT than pure W [ 30 – 32 ]. Due to the high thermal conductivity,

reducing the DBTT, and trace K bubbles improving the strength and 
thermal stability, it is expected that the addition of ZrC particles and 
potassium into W alloy may further improve the mechanical properties. 
Besides, since both W-ZrC and W-K alloys showed enhanced irradiation 
resistance, the W alloys strengthened by ZrC particles and K bubbles may 
also have improved irradiation resistance.
In this work, ZrC nanoparticles and trace potassium bubbles 
strengthened W alloy plates with high strength, low DBTT and enhanced 
thermal stability were fabricated by powder metallurgy and hot rolling. 
The microstructure, mechanical properties, thermal stability and the 
strengthening mechanism of the W-K-ZrC alloys were investigated.
2. Experimental
2.1. Materials preparation
Commercial K-doped W powders with an average particle size of 3.3 
μ m and nano-sized ZrC powders (average particle size ~50 nm, purity 
> 99 %) were used as starting materials. The chemical compositions of 
the K-doped W powders were listed in Table 1 . The potassium content in 
the K-doped W powders is 105 wt. ppm. The K-doped W alloy plates with 
0.5 wt% ZrC addition (designated as WZCK) were fabricated by powder 
metallurgy method, and K-doped W (designated as WK) plates were 
fabricated by the same process for comparison. The K-doped W powders 
and ZrC particles were mixed and ball-milled in a planetary ball mill for 
4 h in argon atmosphere with a rotation speed of 240 rpm and a ball-to- 
powder weight ratio of 8:1. WC/Co balls and mortars were used to 
minimize the impurity contamination. The ball-milled powders were 
subsequently compacted into a green compact by cold isostatic pressing 
and then sintered at 2050 
◦
C for 4 h in flowing dry hydrogen. The sin -
tered materials were hot-rolled at 1600 
◦
C with a total thickness 
reduction of about 70 %. After hot rolling, tungsten plates with a 
thickness of about 6.0 mm were obtained. The consolidation and hot- 
rolling of the tungsten alloys were carried out at ATTL Advanced Ma -
terials Co., Ltd. The density of hot-rolled specimens was measured by the 
Archimedes methods and the relative density was calculated based on 
the theoretical density of W (19.3 g/cm
3
) and ZrC (6.73 g/cm
3
). The 
measured relative density of WK and WZCK is 99.9 % and 99.8 %, 
respectively.
2.2. Microstructure characterization
The tungsten samples were polished and etched for metallographic 
characterization. Electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD, Oxford In -

–

and size distribution. The morphology of tensile fracture surfaces and 
the thermal loaded surfaces were characterized using a field-emission 
scanning electron microscope (FE-SEM SU8020 Hitachi). The micro -
structure of the W alloys was characterized by using a JEOL JEM- 
2000FX transmission electron microscope (TEM). TEM samples were 
prepared by using a Struers Tenupol-5 twin-jet electropolisher in 2 %

America) and Inductively Coupled Plasma Atomic Emission Spectrom -
eter (ICP-AES, Agilent Technologies, 5100 model). The chemical 
composition of WK and WZCK alloys is shown in Table 2 .

shown in Fig. 8 . In WK, nanoscale K bubbles with the size of 10 – 300 nm 
were distributed in the grains and grain boundaries ( Fig. 8 a). It can be 
seen that more than 70 % of K bubbles are smaller than 40 nm as 
indicated by the hollow yellow arrows. Fig. 8 b shows nanoscale K 
bubbles pinned grain boundaries and dislocations, which can greatly 
increase tensile strength and recrystallization temperature [ 39 ]. While 
in the WZCK, both ZrC nanoparticles and nanoscale K bubbles were 
observed, as shown in Fig. 8 c. These highly dispersed nanoparticles and 
K bubbles in WZCK can significantly refine grains, and improve the 
strength and thermal stability of the materials. In Fig. 8 d, the dispersoids 
in WZCK were characterized by EDS. Both K and O signals were detected 
in the smallest dispersoids (~10 nm), which could be identified as the K 
bubbles. It can be seen that K bubbles are mainly distributed in grain 
interior. Some larger particles consisting of Zr, C, O elements or Zr, O 
elements were also found. The results suggest that some ZrC particles 
were transformed into Zr-C-O particles (40 – 70 nm) and larger ZrO

nanoscale ZrC particles were encapsulated in the K bubbles during the 
sintering process. These abundant second-phase particles in grains are 
beneficial to improve the strength and thermal stability of alloys.

4.2. Mechanisms for enhanced mechanical properties
Fig. 10 is the schematic diagram showing the distribution of nano -
scale K bubbles and ZrC particles in WK and WZCK. Nanoscale K bub -
bles, similar to ZrC particles, can also pin grain boundaries and hinder 
the motion of dislocations, as shown in Figs. 8 b and 10 a. Some studies 
reported that K-bubbles tend to distribute at grain boundaries along the 
RD direction during the hot-rolling process, which can inhibit the lateral 
movement of grain boundaries to achieve good resistance to recrystal -
lization [ 45 , 46 ]. Besides, the good deformation ability of K-bubbles 
makes the alloy have remarkable ductility. X. Ma et al. reported that K 
bubbles exist in the form of liquid/gas phase at 63 
◦
C - 760 
◦
C [ 44 ]. 
Therefore, spherical K-bubbles would be continuously stretched during 
high-temperature tensile testing until the width of the rod-shaped bub -
bles is less than the critical size [ 47 ].
Different with K bubbles, ZrC nanoparticles have very high hardness 
and high melting temperature [ 16 , 37 , 40 ]. As compared with WK, the 
ZrC nanoparticles in WZCK further refined the tungsten grains ( Fig. 10 b) 
and improved the strength and thermal stability. ZrC nanoparticles and 
W matrix with small lattice mismatch can form coherent or 
semi-coherent interfaces, which are beneficial to inhibit particles 
coarsening and enhance interfacial bonding [ 13 , 17 ]. Besides, ZrC 
nanoparticles as oxygen getter can react with impurity oxygen to form 
Zr-C-O and ZrO
2 
particles and suppress the brittle intergranular fracture, 
leading to decreased DBTT [ 16 ]. The above results show that the high 
strength, low DBTT and high thermal stability of the WZCK could be 
attributed to the synergistic effects of nanoscale K bubbles and ZrC 
particles.
5. Conclusions
Nanoscale K bubbles and ZrC particles strengthened W (WZCK) 
plates with high strength, low DBTT (100 – 150 
◦
C) and high thermal 
stability were fabricated by powder metallurgy and hot rolling. The ef -
fects of ZrC nanoparticle and potassium bubbles on the microstructure, 
mechanical properties and thermal stability of W alloys were studied. 
The main results can be summarized as follows: 
(1) WZCK exhibits both higher strength and lower DBTT as compared 
with WK. WK is brittle at 150 
◦
C, while WZCK is ductile with a 
high tensile strength of 960 MPa. At 200 
◦
C, WZCK exhibits a high 
tensile strength of 883 MPa and a total elongation of 13.4 %, 
much higher than that of WK (702 MPa and 2.4 %).
(2) WZCK shows high thermal stability with a recrystallization 
temperature of ~1600 
◦
C, and maintains relatively fine grains 
and high strength even after annealing at 1600 
◦
C. The 1600 
◦
C 
annealed WZCK exhibits a high tensile strength of 460 MPa at 500 
◦
C, which is over 40 % higher than that of WK (319 MPa).
(3) The dispersed nanoscale K bubbles and ZrC particles in WZCK can 
significantly refine grains, and improve the strength and thermal 
stability of W alloys. ZrC nanoparticles can also react with im -
purity oxygen to alleviate the detrimental effects of oxygen on 
grain boundaries, thereby reducing the DBTT of the alloy. Be -
sides, the high fraction LAGBs introduced by hot-rolling can also 
improve the strength while maintain good ductility. Therefore, 
the synergistic effects of nanoscale K bubbles, ZrC particles 
together with the high fraction LAGBs lead to the high strength, 
low DBTT and high thermal stability of WZCK.
