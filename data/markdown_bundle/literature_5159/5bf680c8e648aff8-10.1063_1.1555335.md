Advanced power systems require soft magnetic materials with a combination of high saturation
magnetization and high creep resistance. When the Fe–Co–V alloy laminate is used in a rotor at
high temperatures ~500–600 °C! coupled with very high rpm, signiﬁcant creep occurs, which
destroys the device integrity. Since grain boundary slide is predominantly responsible for creep
deformation in the Fe–Co–V alloy at temperature higher than ;430 °C, the approach in this study
was to reduce the volume fraction of the grain boundaries by making a Fe–Co–V alloy with very
large grains. Very large grains, up to mm range, were readily obtained after small cold deformation
of ;3% followed by a normal recrystallization anneal. Fe–Co–V alloy with large grains displays
lower coercivity and higher permeability than the commercial Fe–Co–V alloy. Even though its
yield strength at 600 °C is lower than the commercial Fe–Co–V , the creep strains of the Fe–Co–V
alloy with large grains are only 1/10–1/2 of that for the commercial alloy in the initial and middle
periods of the creep test performed at 600 °C under 150 MPa. At the end of the test before the
specimens were broken, the Fe–Co–V alloy with large grains showed an accelerated creep strain
rate. Better balancing the high-temperature yield strength and creep resistance by creating the
optimum grain size would further improve the high-temperature creep resistance of the Fe–Co–V
alloy. © 2003 American Institute of Physics. @DOI: 10.1063/1.1555335#
I. INTRODUCTION
Advanced power systems require soft magnetic materials
with a combination of high saturation magnetization and
high creep resistance.

For these reasons, increasing the grain size in the Fe–Co or
Fe–Co–V alloy is an appropriate approach to improving
their high-temperature ~500–600 °C! creep resistance with-
out sacriﬁcing their magnetic properties.

Generally speaking, it is not easy to grow grains much
larger than the sheet thickness by anneal. In this study, com-
mercial Hiperco 50 alloy sheets were ﬁrst annealed at 800 °C
for 1–3 h in a high vacuum. Following anneal, they were

lowed by a normal recrystallization anneal at 800–850 °C for
1–6 h in a high vacuum or dry H

recrystallization anneal, very large grains up to the mm range
can be obtained.
Magnetic and mechanical properties of the large-grain
Fe–Co–V alloy sheets and commercial Hiperco 50 alloy
sheets were compared. The room-temperature magnetic
properties were determined using a MI SMT 600 hysteresi-
graph. The yield and tensile strengths at 600 °C were deter-
mined using an Instron Model 4505 tensile test machine, and
the creep tests were performed using a Satec Model D testing
facility at 600 °C under 120 and 150 MPa, respectively.
III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
A. Grain size
It has been demonstrated in this study that the applica-
tion of a small amount of cold deformation ~;3%! followed
by a normal recrystallization anneal is a proper way of ob-
taining very large grains in a Fe–Co–V alloy. Figure 1
shows the microstructure of a Hiperco 50 alloy sheet cold
rolled with a 3% thickness reduction followed by a recrys-
tallization anneal at 850 °C for 6 h.
B. Magnetic properties
The Fe–Co–V alloy with large grains displays lower
coercivity, higher permeability, and higher induction as com-
pared with the commercial Fe–Co–V alloy with the same
composition as shown in Fig. 2.
