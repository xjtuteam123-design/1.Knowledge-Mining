Effect of nano Y2O3 dispersion on thermal, microstructure, mechanical and 
high temperature oxidation behavior of mechanically alloyed W-Ni-Mo-Ti

Abstract: The present research deals with the fabrication of mechanically alloyed W based 
alloys with nominal composition of W 75Ni10Mo10Ti5 (alloy A), W 74Ni10Mo10Ti5(Y2O3)1 
(alloy B), W73Ni10Mo10Ti5(Y2O3)2 (alloy C) (all in wt.%) by compaction at 500 MPa pressure for 
5 min and conventional pressureless Ar injected sintering at 1500C for 2 h . The phase 
evolution, microstructure, thermal characteristics, mechanical and high temperature behavior of 
the mechanically alloyed powders and sintered alloys has been studied by X-ray diffraction 
(XRD), Scanning electron microscopy (SEM), High resolution transmission electron microscopy 
(HRTEM), Energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) and Differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) . 
Alloy C shows minimum crystallite size and maximum lattice strain, dislocation density of 
18.6 nm, 0.54%, 36.71 ×1016/m2 respectively at 20 h of milling as compared to other alloys. The 
activation energy for recrystallization decreases with increase in Y 2O3 dispersion. The residual 
stress also enhances with increased Y 2O3 content. Alloy C exhibit s improved relative si ntered 
density, hardness and elongation of 92.1%, 7.22 GPa, 12.73% respectively and appreciable wear 
resistance, high temperature oxidation resistance at 1000 °C whereas maximum compressive 
strength of 1.91 GPa has been recorded in alloy B as compared to other alloys. Most interestingly 
the ductility also enhances with increase in Y2O3 dispersion. 
Keywords: W-Ni-Mo-Ti alloy; Oxide dispersion strengthening ; Hardness; Strength; Ductility; 
Oxidation.

1. Introduction 
Several unique properties of W such as high melting point (3410 C), excellent mechanical 
strength at elevated temperature, highest density (19.3 g/ cm3), hardness of 9.8 GPa, high tensile 
elastic modulus (411 GPa at 20 C), high thermal conductivity (155 W/m. K at 20ºC) , low 
thermal expansion coefficient, low sputtering yield and low tritium retention facilitates the 
application in kinetic energy penetrators (KEP), aviation counterweights, plasma facing material 
(PFM) in nuclear reactor [1-4]. However high ductile-brittle transition temperature ( DBTT) 
(200-500°C) of W [4] is associated with irradiation-effected embrittlement, recrystallization 
embrittlement [5] and poor oxidation resistance [4] owing to the formation of porous and volatile 
WO3 which limits the high temperature application of W as a structural material. Application of 
W based alloys in KEP requires enhanced high temperature strength and ductility (6 -8% 
minimum) as the penetrator head is exposed to high temperature (~2000°C), strain rate (10 6 s−1) 
and hydrostatic pressure (2 –6 GPa) [6]. Synthesis of nanostructured alloy prior to consolidation 
is a potential approach to reduce the consolidation temperature [7, 8] as compared to 
consolidation of W based alloys at higher than 2700°C temperature from commercial micron size 
powders [9, 10]. Presence of higher grain boundary area in nanostructured alloys and addition of 
dispersoids act as sink for the defects incorporated during radiation [11]. 
Addition of Mo facilitates easy alloying with W , as W -Mo alloys exhibit enhanced solid 
solubility [12] and improved strength, hardness and ductility [13,14]. Ni contributes reduction of 
Peierls stress followed by improvement of ductility [1 5] and also liquid phase sintering at the 
sintering temperature used in our present investigation (1500°C) which promotes the 
densification and mechanical properties as discussed in our earlier literature [16]. 
Ti addition is beneficial to r educe the sintering temperature, grain refinement of W and improve 
the densification [17-19] which aids in enhancing strength and hardness of W based alloys. 
However higher content of Ti addition is not attractive due to high DBTT of Ti [19] which 
lowers the ductility. Y2O3 is a favorable dispersoid to improve the densification during sintering 
and hardness of W alloys as compared to La2O3, HfO2 [20]. However Liu et al. have reported 
that Y2O3 dispersion in W -Zr alloy improves the high temperature strength with lowering of 
ductility [2 1]. Higher content of Y 2O3 addition declines the mechanical properties (strength, 
ductility) and high temperature applicability of the alloys owing to wide difference of melting 
point between W and Y2O3 (2439°C) [22, 23].

Increase in the oxidation resistance of Ni-20Cr alloys at 1000 -1200°C with increase in Y 2O3 
addition has reported by Michels [24]. Recent literatures report that Y2O3 is a candidate 
dispersoid to improve the oxidation resistance of NiCr, NiCrAl, FeCrAl alloys [25, 26]. Lawn et 
al. has reported that higher Y2O3 addition (2%) is effective to achieve enhanced oxidation 
resistance in ODS chromium [27] whereas lower Y2O3 addition (0.17%) to improve the oxidation 
resistance of Fe-Cr-Al alloy has been reported by Nickel and Quadakkers [28]. Patra et al. [ 29] 
and Telu [ 30] et al. have illustrated similar improvement of oxidation resistance in Y2O3 
dispersed W-Ni-Mo and W -Cr alloys respectively. According to recent report sintering in Ar 
environment improves the %relative sintered density and grain refinement as compared to H 2 
[31]. 
Several literatures report the microstructure, mechanical behavior of W based alloy with varying 
Y2O3 content [ 17, 23, 32]. However microstructure -strength-ductility correlation and high 
temperature oxidation resistance with increasing Y 2O3 dispersion in W based alloys has not been 
thoroughly investigated. Present investigation focuses on the effect of varying Y 2O3 dispersion 
(0 wt.%, 1 wt.%, 2 wt.%) on the microstructure, densification , mechanical and high temperature 
behavior of the consolidated W-Ni-Mo-Ti alloys. 
2. Materials and Method 
2.1. Synthesis of mechanically alloyed powders 
Elemental powders of W, Ni, Mo, Y 2O3 (purity 99.5%, Sigma Aldrich ), Ti ( purity 99.5%, 
Himedia) with 100 -150 μm particle size of W, Ni, Mo, Ti and <50 nm for Y 2O3 has been 
mechanically alloyed in a high energy planetary ball mill (Fritsch, P5 ) for 20 h . The milling 
container has been filled (1/3 rd by volume) with toluene to inhibit agglomeration and 
contamination of powder particles from atmosphere and grinding media . Chrome steel ball of 
10 mm diameter has been used as grinding medium with mill speed and ball to powder weight 
ratio (BPR) of 300 rpm and 10:1 respectively. The milled powders have been collected from the 
container after 1, 5, 10, 15 and 20 h for further investigation. The nominal composition of the 
alloys is provided in table 1. 
A high resolution X -ray diffractometer (Rigaku Japan, Ultima IV) with Cu-kα radiation 
(λ=1.541874 Å), step size of 0.05 ° and scan rate 10°/min has been employed to study the X-ray 
diffraction pattern (XRD) at various milling time to identify the phase evolution of the milled 
powders supported by JCPDS data bank [33]. The peak position and broadening of the XRD

3.1.1.2. Crystallite size and lattice strain analysis 
Fig. 5(a) and (b) displays the variation of crystallite size of W and induced lattice strain with 
increase in alloy s A through C respectively. The crystallite size and lattice strain has been 
evaluated by Williamson–Hall equation as described in previous literature [ 29]. Reduction in 
crystallite size and enhancement of lattice strain with progress of milling is evident for all the 
alloys. Minimum crystallite size of 18.6 nm and maximum lattice strain of 0.54% is achieved in 
alloy C (2 wt.% Y2O3) as compared to alloy A (0 wt.% Y2O3) (crystallite size : 37.9 nm, lattice 
strain : 0.36%) and alloy B (1 wt.% Y2O3) (crystallite size : 28.1 nm, lattice strain : 0.52%) at 20 
h of milling. The results are attributed to increased nano Y 2O3 addition which promotes the 
higher crystallite size reduction due to enhanced surface energy. The crystallite size of W in 
alloy B is marginally less due to the presence of Ti as compared to previously reported crystallite 
size of W in W -Ni-Mo-1%Y2O3 [29]. It is evident that Y 2O3 addition in nanometer size is 
effective in considerable reduction in crystallite size. Similar behavior of substantial crystallite 
size reduction of Cu by incorporation of nano Al 2O3 has been reported by Sheibani et al . [48]. 
According to literature evidence finer Y 2O3 particles are difficult to be fractured [ 49] and 
therefore it only contributes enough surface energy for fracturing of coarser W particles. The rate

3.1.1.3. Dislocation density and Lattice parameter study 
Fig. 6(a) and (b) shows the variation of dislocation density and lattice parameter of W in alloys A 
through C respectively with increase in milling time. It is evident from Fig. 6(a) that dislocation 
density increases with increase in milli ng time and Y 2O3 content. The rate of increase in 
dislocation density drops beyond 10 h of milling in alloy A and alloy B. Such a drop is attributed 
to enhancement of Peierls-Nabarro stress (required stress for dislocation movement) with 
increased crystallite size reduction [5 0]. Increased nano -Y2O3 content strongly inhibits the 
dislocation motion and results in accumulation of dislocations. Alloy C shows sharp increase in 
dislocation density (36.71×1016/m2) as compared to alloy A (12.01×1016/m2) and alloy B 
(23.39×1016/m2) at 20 h of milling. The atomic diffusion of Y 2O3 in W matrix increases with 
increased Y 2O3 addition which can act as source for formation and storage of dislocation and 
increase the strain hardening rate [51]. Higher surface energy with increased dispersion also 
relaxes the Peierls-Nabarro stress to some extend and facilitates further dislocation generation. 
The trend of variation of dislocation density is also supported by substantial drop of crystallite 
size at 20 h of milling in alloy C (Fig. 5(a)). The increased dislocation density in alloy C 
contributes significant enthalpy which extent the solubility limit of solute elements (Mo, Ni, Ti) 
in W lattice. 
The lattice parameter of W in alloy s A through C incre ases with increase in milling time and 
higher Y2O3 dispersion as shown in Fig. 6(b). W lattice shows expansion of 0.08%, 0.09% and 
0.14% at 10 h of milling followed by contraction of 0.05%, 0.12%, 0.18% for alloy A, alloy B, 
alloy C respectively at 20 h of milling. The expansion of W lattice in all the alloys is due to 
expansion stresses owing to inter -dipolar repulsion at surface [5 2] as well as grain boundary 
vacancy generated stress field [5 3]. Similar tendency of extension of Nb lattice in mec hanically 
milled Nb has been earlier reported by Pabi et al. [5 4]. W (110) diffraction peak in alloy C shift 
from 47.1548° at 0 h to 47.1030° at 10 h and 47.1909° at 20 h of milling. The shifting of 
diffraction peak to lower angle indicates increase in W lattice parameter whereas movement of 
peak to high angle at higher milling time designates amorphization of milled powder [55].

3.1.1.4. Microstructure of mechanically alloyed powders 
SEM image of Fig. 7 to Fig. 9 shows the change in powder particle morphology and size for 
alloys A through C respectively at different milling time. The powder particles are subjected to 
intense impact force due to ball -powder-ball collision. The brittle W, Ti particles are 
predominantly fractured as compared to comparative ductile Ni, Mo which undergoes flattening 
followed by fragmentation at higher milling time. The Y 2O3 particles are also entangled at the 
particle interfaces with the progress of milling . The particles for all the alloys before th e start of 
milling are irregular. Some particles are transformed to flake shap e morphology at 10 h of 
milling as evi dent from inset image of Fig. 7(c) as well as from Fig. 8(c) and Fig. 9 (c). The 
particles are finally changed to fine size at 20 h of milling. The reduction in the interparticle 
distance between W and other solute elements with increase in milling time leads to alloying. 
Fig. 10 shows the particle size distribution in alloys A through C at 20 h of milling. Wide particle 
size distribution (both finer and coarser particles) is mostly evident in alloy B and alloy C as 
compared to alloy A. Minimum particle size of 1.13 µm has been achieved in alloy C at 20 h of 
milling. Alloy B shows higher tendency of cold weldi ng as compared to alloy C which reduces 
the degree of alloying. Similar behavior is also supported by the XRD pattern of alloy B (Fig. 3) 
and alloy C (Fig. 4). The particle size in the mechanically alloyed powder significantly 
influences the particle rearr angement, packing during compaction which also effect the relative 
density prior to sintering and after sintered condition. Recent literature suggests that finer particle 
shows increased interparticle friction as compared to coarser particles [ 57]. Enhanced 
interparticle friction also related with increased compaction pressure and reduced particle -
particle contact [ 58] to achieve a specific relative green density (density before sintering) . 
Therefore comparatively wider particle size distribution can be effective to improve the 
compactibility and sintered density at reduced pressure.

3.2.1.2. Microstructure study of Consolidated W based alloys 
The microstructure of the consolidated alloy s A through C observed under SEM is presented in 
Fig. 16(a-c). The bright phase indicates W matrix where the gray phase is Mo based matrix. The 
dark phases represents MoNi and Ni 3Ti intermetallics in all the alloys formed during sintering 
along W/W or W/Mo interfaces . The resultant melting point of W -Ni-Mo-Ti-Y2O3 alloy drops 
with the presence of low melting constituents (Ni and Ti). The reduction in the particle size of Ni 
(melting point: 1455°C) and Ti (melting point: 1668°C) with the progress of milling facilitates 
the liquid phase formation during heating cycle of sintering. The liquid phase is converted to Ni 
and Ti matrix and W -Mo phase with the start of cooling cycle. Ni and Ti matrix phase reacts 
with W-Mo phase during cooling and precipitated as MoNi and Ni 3Ti intermetallic phase. In the 
process grain boundary porosities are fully or partially sealed by the liquid phase. The formation 
of intermetallic along the weaker W/W grain boundary reduces the continuous area fraction of 
adjacent W grains (contiguity) and improves the strength of the matrix . Ti has a strong reactivity 
at elevated temperature which gives rise to TiO 2 formation [6 4], whereas W, Mo also forms 
brittle WO3, MoO 3 oxides [29]. Therefore to prevent undue newer oxides formation , the argon

3.2.1.3. Densification study of Consolidated W based alloys 
Fig. 1 8 shows the variation of %relative sintered density with increase in Y 2O3 addition for 
alloy A (0 wt.% % Y2O3), alloy B (1 wt.% Y2O3) and alloy C (2 wt.% Y2O3). It is evident that

and alloy B (91.0%). Recent report shows that compaction of W nanostructured powder is 
challenging with 1 GPa pressure owing to enhanced surface area, s trength and brittleness issues 
of W which leads to low relative density of compact prior to sintering [ 69]. Therefore high 
sintering temperature and time is required to enhance the sintering kinetics of compacts. Wang et 
al. have reported enhanced green and sintered density of mechanically alloyed W powder with

comparatively ductile powders such as Mo, Ni than W , Ti can result in wide particle distribution 
in mechanically alloyed powder. The comparatively finer W, Ti, Y 2O3 particles may get trapped 
inside the voids of coarser Ni, Mo particles and contribute in enhanced packing during 
compaction [58]. The improved densification in high Y 2O3 added sintered alloys is attributed to 
refined crystallite size, enhanced surface energy and grain boundary energy of W which 
facilitates faster diffusion (mass transfer along the neck region of W/solute atoms) and chemical 
activation for liquid phase sintering (MoNi, Ni3Ti phase formation) with respect to pore filling.

Therefore it indicates that increased Y 2O3 dispersion eases the interdiffusion between W/Y 2O3 
and facilitates (Y -W) oxide formation which also results in enhanced pore sealing and 
densification in sintered alloy C as compared to alloy A and alloy B.

3.2.1.8. Oxidation study 
Fig. 28 shows the plot between weight change per unit cross sectional area and time of exposure

as compared to alloy A and alloy B which indicates hindrance in oxide film growth with increase 
in Y 2O3 addition. The initial increase in the weight change or oxidatio n for all the alloys is 
attributed to the formation of WO 3, MoO3, TiO2 oxides (Fig. 29(a-c)). The WO 3, MoO3 oxides 
are comparatively porous and volatile than TiO2. According to literature evidence the activation 
energy for diffusion of oxygen in W, Mo, Ti is 80 kJ/mol, 82.4 kJ/mol and 140.1 kJ/mol 
respectively [87, 88]. Therefore W and Mo readily form oxides as compared to Ti. The vapor 
pressure of all the oxides increases with increase in temperature, however the extent of 
vaporization of MoO 3 is substa ntially higher at about 1000 °C as compared to WO 3 and TiO 2 
[89]. Recent literature also reports that severe oxidation of Mo occurs at about 725°C, much 
below than the present oxidation temperature (1000°C) and above 801°C probability of liquid 
oxide format ion and volatility of oxides intensely increases [8 7]. During oxidation , MoO3 
dissociates into MoO 2 and highly reactive nascent oxygen. However only MoO 2 is detected 
instead of MoO 3, as evident from XRD pattern of all oxidized sample s (Fig. 29(a-c)). This is 
attributed to the reduction in volatilization of MoO3 by formation of more stable NiMoO 4 and 
rest Mo based oxide is identified as MoO 2. No major weight change per unit area is observed 
beyond 2 h of oxidation owing to the formation of comparative dense and stable WO 2, W 5O14, 
NiWO4, NiTiO 3 oxide formation. The standard free energy (ΔG°) of formation for WO3, TiO2 
and NiO at 1000ºC is - 349.3 KJ mol-1, -688.69 KJ mol-1, -192.2 KJ mol-1 respectively [90-92].

4. Conclusions 
Fabrication of nanostructured W-Ni-Mo-Ti with various content of Y 2O3 dispersion (0 wt.%, 
1 wt.%, 2 wt.%) has been carried out by mechanical alloying and conventional sintering. 
Increased solubility of Mo, Ni, Ti and Y2O3 in W lattice is detected with the progress of milling 
and increase in Y 2O3 dispersion. Minimum crystallite size and maximum lattice s train, 
dislocation density of 18.6 nm, 0.54%, 36.71×1016/m2 has been achieved in 
W73Ni10Mo10Ti5(Y2O3)2 alloy at 20 h of milling . TEM investigation of 20 h milled 
W73Ni10Mo10Ti5(Y2O3)2 alloy reveals intersecting diffraction fringes at matrix -oxide interface 
which suggest bowing of dislocation over dispersed Y 2O3 particles. The activation energy for 
recrystallization in W73Ni10Mo10Ti5(Y2O3)2 alloy is reduced as compared to 
W74Ni10Mo10Ti5(Y2O3)1 owing to ease in bowing of grain boundary. Increase grain bimodality is 
observed in sintered W 73Ni10Mo10Ti5(Y2O3)2 alloy due to reduction in population of Y 2O3 
pinning particles at the interface. Maximum sintered density of 92.1% is recorded in 
W73Ni10Mo10Ti5(Y2O3)2 alloy. The hardness, %elongation and wear resistance increases whereas 
compressive strength decreases with enhancement of Y 2O3 addition owing to reduction in grain 
boundary pinning and increase in fraction of coarser grains. Maximum hardness, %elongation of 
7.22 GPa, 12.73% is measured in W73Ni10Mo10Ti5(Y2O3)2 alloy whereas W74Ni10Mo10Ti5(Y2O3)1 
alloy exhibits maximum compressive strength of 1.91 GPa. High temperature oxidation study 
displays that enhanced oxidation resistance is achieved in Y 2O3 dispersed alloys as compared to 
non Y2O3 dispersed alloy due to continuous and stable NiWO4, NiMoO4, NiTiO 3, Y2WO6, 
Y2MoO6, Y2TiO5 oxide scale formation. Present investigation suggest that 2 wt.% Y2O3 addition 
provides superior protection at 1000°C for 10 h of exp osure time as compared to other alloys. 
The prime novelty of present research work is enhancement of ductility with reduction in 
strength (optimized strength -ductility combination) and elevated temperature oxidation

Highlights 
 Nanostructured W -Ni-Mo-Ti-(0, 1.0, 2.0 wt.% Y2O3) alloys are fabricated by mechanical 
alloying and conventional sintering. 
 Increase in Y2O3 dispersion considerably reduces the activation energy for recrystallization. 
 Enhanced grain bimodality is achieved in W-Ni-Mo-Ti-2 wt.% Y2O3 alloy. 
 The density, hardness, wear resistance and ductility increases and strength decreases with 
increase in Y2O3 addition. 
 W-Ni-Mo-Ti-2 wt.% Y2O3 alloy exhibit superior resistance against oxidation at 1000°C as 
compared other alloys.
