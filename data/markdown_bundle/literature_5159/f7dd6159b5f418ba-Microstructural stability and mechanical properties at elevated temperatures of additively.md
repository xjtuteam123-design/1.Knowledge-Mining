ABSTRACT
The stabilities of microstructures and mechanical properties are noteworthy problems for additively manufac -
tured (AMed) TiAl alloys because of their high-temperature service condition, which deserves to be investigated. 
In this study, we utilized the approach of direct laser deposition to fabricate bulk TiAl alloy which possessed 
superior room-temperature mechanical property. Although the AMed alloys exhibited a reduced microstructural 
stability at elevated temperature, they performed favorable strengths at elevated temperatures. The enhanced 
strength was considered to result from the significant microstructural refinement and the bimodal hetero -
structure. This research demonstrates a feasibility of additive manufacturing TiAl alloys for high-temperature 
service.
1. Introduction
Intermetallic TiAl alloys, exhibiting low density, high specific yield 
strength, and satisfactory creep and oxidation resistance at elevated 
temperatures, are considered as one of the most promising candidates to 
substitute Ni-based superalloys at elevated temperatures [1] . Additive 
manufacturing (AM) is a rapidly developing technology to innovate the 
fabricating manners for metals and alloys including TiAl alloys [2] . Its 
various processing parameters can provide diverse combinations to 
flexibly tailor and optimize the microstructures and mechanical prop -
erties. Investigations focused on additively manufactured (AMed) TiAl 
alloys have been conducted for more than a decade, and their corre -
sponding room-temperature microstructures have been intensively 
characterized [3 – 10] . Specific microstructural features could occur in 
these AMed TiAl alloys, e.g., alternating layered microstructures [4,5] , 
gradient microstructures [6] , and exceptional phase constitutions [7,8] . 
Moreover, improved strength, fluctuant hardness and anisotropic 
deformation behaviors, are also reported [9,10] . However, the unique 
microstructural features and mechanical behaviors were considered to 
result from the cyclic rapid heating/cooling characteristics, and they 
severely deviated from the equilibrium state, which brings uncertainties 
for the performance at elevated temperatures. Hitherto, few studies have 
investigated the microstructures and mechanical properties of AMed 
TiAl alloys at elevated temperature, and the corresponding differences 
between AMed and casted alloys are still under veil. Hence, studies 
deserve to be conducted to develop a comprehensive understanding of 
the microstructural evolution and mechanical properties of AMed TiAl 
alloys at elevated temperatures.
In the present study, a high-performance Ti-48Al-2Cr-2Nb alloys 
dominated by a convoluted lamellar microstructure were fabricated by 
the approach of direct laser deposition (DLD). The as-deposited alloys 
not only have superior strength and improved ductility at room tem -
perature, but also show favorable strengths at elevated temperatures. 
The microstructural stabilities and related strengthening mechanisms 
were discussed.

3. Results

At room temperature, the volume fraction of equiaxed γ grain in 
AMed alloy is only 2.3 %, which can hardly contribute to the strength; 
the additional strength resulting from the heterostructure results are 
also negligible [21] . Therefore, the strength of the AMed alloy is 
determined by the lamellar microstructure. Compared with the casted

refined from hundreds of micrometers to tens micrometers and the 
lamellae were thinned from more than 200 hundred nanometers to only 
tens nanometers. Thus, the refinement of both colonies and lamellae are 
considered to be the determining factor in increasing the yield strength 
for AMed sample.

5. Conclusions
In this study, we investigated the microstructure and mechanical 
properties of AMed Ti-48Al-2Cr-2Nb alloy at both room and elevated 
temperature. Convoluted α
2
/ γ lamellar microstructure with small col -
onies and thin lamellae was the main room-temperature feature. When 
temperature was elevated, more granular γ grains would nucleate in the 
lamellar microstructure, and they would grow coarse with increasing 
temperature. The reduced microstructural stability is attributed to the 
large deviation from the equilibrium and the higher strain energy. Even 
though, the AMed samples performed superior strength at both room 
and high temperatures. The enhancement of high-temperature yield 
strength is related to the microstructural refinement and heterostructure 
that result from the microstructural destabilization at elevated 
temperatures.
