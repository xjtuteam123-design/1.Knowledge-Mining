Nb-16Si-25Ti-8Hf-2Cr-2Al alloy powder was prepared by electrode induction gas atomization (EIGA). The
Nb alloy ingot, which was prepared by vacuum arc melting and was subjected to solid solution treatment,
was composed of a (Nb, Ti) solid solution and (Nb, Ti, Hf)
5Si3. The grain size of each phase was larger than
50 lm. The ingot was gas-atomized using the EIGA process and sieved to the range of 45–75 lm. The

solution, (Nb, Ti, Hf)
5Si3, and (Nb, Ti, Hf) 3Si, and the silicide grain size was smaller than 5 lm.

1. Introduction
Niobium silicide-based alloys have excellent strength and resis-
tance to ultrahigh temperatures [1]. The temperature limit of Ni-
based superalloys to apply the industry without failure is known
to be 1150 /C176C [2], while Nb alloys maintain a high strength at
higher temperatures of 1200–1350 /C176C [3,4]. In addition, since the
density of Nb alloys is lower than that of Ni-based superalloys,
the miniaturization of the gas turbine system could be achieved
by adopting Nb alloys for fabricating turbine blades [5]. Therefore,
niobium silicide-based alloys have been considered as alternatives
to Ni-based superalloys for ultrahigh-temperature applications.
Among niobium silicide-based alloys, Nb-Si-Ti-Hf-Cr-Al multi-
component alloys have been receiving considerable attention due
to the good balance between mechanical properties and oxidation
resistance at high temperatures [6]. The representative composi-
tion of the multicomponent Nb alloy is Nb-16Si-25Ti-8Hf-2Cr-
2Al [5], and it is composed of the Nb solid solution and Nb 5Si3
phases. The Nb 5Si3 phase improves high-temperature creep resis-
tance as well as oxidation resistance by forming a protective SiO 2
layer on the surface [7]. However, since the Nb alloy has low
machinability and fracture toughness at room temperature due
to the existence of the silicide phase [8,9], thus fabrication of com-
ponents to use the Nb alloy by a conventional method such as
forming and cutting is difﬁcult.
Recently, near-net shape fabrication techniques such as addi-
tive manufacturing and power injection molding have been inten-
sively studied because they can directly fabricate components
having complex shapes [10]. By adopting these techniques, the
amount of machining could be reduced signiﬁcantly. For these
techniques, powders with spherical particles are needed to realize
good ﬂuidity and high packing density [11,12]. However, the suit-
ability of these methods for the fabrication of niobium silicide-
based alloy powder with spherical particles has not yet been
studied.
Spherical metal powder particles are usually fabricated by vac-
uum induction gas atomization (VIGA), which involves a ceramic
crucible such as an alumina crucible [13]. However, niobium
silicide-based alloys cannot be fabricated by VIGA due to their very
high melting temperature. Therefore, in this study, we fabricated
spherical Nb-16Si-25Ti-8Hf-2Cr-2Al alloy powder particles by
electrode induction gas atomization (EIGA) [14,15], in which the
metal melt is not in contact with the ceramic during the process.
2. Materials and methods
The initial material subjected to EIGA was an ingot with a chem-
ical composition of Nb-16Si-25Ti-8Hf-2Cr-2Al (in at%). The ingot
was cast to the size of U50 /C2H150 mm by vacuum arc melting.
The raw materials for casting were Nb, Si, Ti, Hf, Cr, and Al granules
with purities of more than 99.99 wt%. After casting, the ingot was
annealed at 1600 /C176C for 20 h in a high-purity argon atmosphere for
solid solution treatment. The chemical concentration of the ingot
was analyzed by inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometer,

solution phase and the bright region corresponded to the silicide
phase. The clear contrast appears to be due to the high concentra-
tion of Hf in silicides, as indicated in Fig. 1 (c).

Hf-Cr-Al alloys [6]. In a case of the powder, an additional phase,
Nb3Si, was observed in the XRD pattern. As shown in Fig. 1 ,N b

(Nb, Ti, Hf) 3Si. To obtain the weight percentage of each phase in
each sample, Rietveld reﬁnement is used. The percentages of (Nb,

43.3%, respectively, which is similar to previous results [5]. In the
powder, the percentages of (Nb, Ti) solid solution, (Nb, Ti, Hf) 5Si3,
and (Nb, Ti, Hf) 3Si were 45.2%, 35.8%, and 19.0%, respectively.

4. Conclusions
A Nb-16Si-25Ti-8Hf-2Cr-2Al (in at%) ingot composed of (Nb, Ti)
solid solution and (Nb, Ti, Hf) 5Si3 phases was used. When EIGA was
carried out, a powder with spherical particles was manufactured,
and the size of the silicide grains decreased by 10 times compared
with that of the ingot grains. The atomized powder was composed
of (Nb, Ti) solid solution, (Nb, Ti, Hf) 5Si3, and (Nb, Ti, Hf) 3Si phases.
The residual (Nb, Ti, Hf) 3Si phase was formed due to the rapid cool-
ing during the solidiﬁcation in the EIGA process.
