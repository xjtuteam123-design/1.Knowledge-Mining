abstract
The effect of Si 3N4 addition on the mechanical properties and microstructure of Ti –Al–Zr–Sn based near-
α Ti alloys has been systematically investigated at both room temperature and high temperature
(650 1C). Minor Mo and Ta ( β stabilizing elements) together with Si 3N4 addition have signi ﬁcantly
improved the compressive yielding strength at both room temperature and 650 1C. The strengthening
effect was mainly due to the precipitation of TiN, α2-Ti3Al and the retained β phase. High compressive
yielding strength together with pronounced ductility can be obtained by only adding minor Si 3N4 into
Ti–Al–Zr–Sn alloys. The enhanced ductility is probably due to the depression of the brittle α2
intermetallic phase, which did not precipitate out even after long time ageing (750 1C for 500 h).

1. Introduction
Titanium and its alloys are widely used for aerospace applica-
tions because of their exceptional properties of strength, speci ﬁc
strength, low-thermal expansion, corrosion resistance, workability
and weldability [1–4]. The maximum usable temperature range of
commercial α type or near- α Ti alloy is up to 600 1C. The Ti –Al
intermetallic compound is now applied above 700 1C [5–7]. But
due to the poor ductility and fracture toughness at room tempera-
ture, Ti –Al intermetallic is very sensitive to micro crack and other
superﬁcial defects [8]. Also the fatigue crack growth resistance was
signiﬁcantly inferior to commercial high temperature Ti alloys [9].
The processing cost to make components is very high compared to
commercial high temperature Ti alloys and Ni- based superalloys.
For components in the temperature range 600 –900 1C, heavy Ni-
based superalloys are being used. No Ti alloys can be used between
600 1C and 700 1C where Ti intermetallics can be used for the
application of current high temperature Ti alloys for aero engine
components. Numerous numbers of research works has been
carried out to develop new Ti alloys to use in the temperature
above 600 1C [7,10–13]. However, a suitable strengthening addi-
tive to increase the maximum usable temperature range of Ti alloy
has not been identi ﬁed.
The high temperature Ti alloys, which has been applied at
about 500 –600 1C, are a typical near- α Ti–Al–Sn–Zr based alloys,
containing α stabilizing element like Al and neutral stabilizing
elements (Sn, Zr, Si) with a small fraction of β stabilizing elements
like Mo, Ni or Ta [14–18]. One of the problems in using near- α Ti
alloys in the temperature range above 500 1C is the precipitation of
α2-Ti3Al intermetallic compound phase, since the embrittlement
will happen if the precipitate coarsening occurs [19]. In general, it
is considered that the precipitation of α2 will easily occur when the
Al-equivalent value is greater than 9 [5]. It has also been reported
that even in the alloys with the Al-equivalent values less than 9,
the α2 will precipitate out after long time exposure at high
temperature [10,20]. The coarsening of such precipitates will cause
embrittlement and degrade the mechanical properties during
service [3].
The materials for a long term application at high temperature
need a stable microstructure at a wide temperature range. Since
α-Ti phase is the main phase of the near- α type high temperature
alloys, thus, the α phase stability is the most important issue for
developing a new high temperature near- α type Ti alloy. The
effects of impurities or interstitial elements such as C, N and O on
the α-Ti phase stability have been intensively studied [21,22]. They
tend to stabilize the α-phase and make it dif ﬁcult to transform into
β-Ti or ω-Ti [21]. With the addition of these elements in high
temperature Ti alloys, the α-Ti matrix phase is mainly strength-
ened via these elements, as an interstitial element in hexagonal
α-Ti structure, except the formation of some alloying compounds
with these impurity elements. Ti and Al are known to have a
strong reaction with these elements, however, it is not very clear
that the behavior of these elements with the impurities in high
temperature Ti-alloys.
Effect of nitrogen addition into Ti alloy has been investigated
by many researchers [23,24]. It has been reported that nitrogen

In the present study, the effect of Si 3N4 addition on the

effect of Si addition in Ti –Al–Zr–Sn based near- α alloy has also
been investigated. Long time ageing has been carried out on the
Si3N4 added Ti –Al–Zr–Sn alloys, which is free of any β stabilizing
elements and the stability of mechanical properties and micro-
structure has been investigated.
2. Experimental procedures
The ingot composition is listed in Table 1 . Two Ti –6.7Al–4Zr–
0.5Ta–0.5Mo–2Sn–xSi alloy ingots with different Si content

Each ingot was melted six times to ensure the compositional
homogeneity. The ingots named as A1, A2, A3 and A4 in Table 1
were solution treated at 1 100 1C for 2 h and air cooled to room
temperature, followed by ageing at 750 1C for 4 h and air cooled to
room temperature. The ingot A5 was cut into three parts. Different
heat treatments were performed for each part. For the ﬁrst part,
solution treatment at 1 100 1C for 2 h and air cooled to room
temperature, followed by ageing at 750 1C for 4 h (ingot desig-
nated as A5). For the second part, solution treatment at 1 100 1C for
2 h and air cooled to room temperature, followed by ageing at
750 1C for 4 h, and it was aged again at 750 1C for 96 h ( ingot
designated as A5-LTA). For the third part, solution treatment at
110 01C for 2 h and air cooled to room temperature, followed by
ageing at 750 1C for 4 h, and again aged at 750 1C for 496 h (ingot
designated as A5-VLTA).
Hardness test was carried out using micro Vickers hardness
tester with a load of 300 gf. For each sample, the hardness value
was measured at 10 points with spacing of 2 mm. The average
value of the 10 points is used for the representation of the
hardness. Compression test at room temperature and 650 1Cw a s
carried out at a strain rate of 5 /C2 10/C0 3/s using a cylindrical
specimen with a diameter of 3 mm and a height of 6 mm, in
Shimadzu testing machine equipped with split type electric
furnace. High temperature compression test was carried out inside
the furnace, maintained at 650 1C until the test ﬁnished. The
specimens were held at 650 1C for 10 min before the compression
test. To check the reproducibility of compression test results, three
samples were tested for each condition.
Microstructural characterizations were carried out using X-ray
diffraction (XRD), optical microscope (OM), scanning electron
microscope (SEM) and transmission electron microscope (TEM).
The XRD measurement was carried out on RINT2500 X-ray diff-
ractometer using Cu K α radiation operated at 40 kV and 300 mA.
The specimens for OM observation and SEM were mechanically
polished on emery papers of GRIT 180 to 2000 grades. Final
polishing was carried out on sylvet cloth, mounted on a smooth
rotating polishing wheel using water suspensions of alumina
powder of 0.5 μm. Polished samples were etched with a solution
of 2% HF þ6% HNO 3 þ92% distilled water (volume %) at room
temperature.
SEM observation was carried out on JEOL 7100 SEM equipped
with energy dispersed spectroscopy (EDS). TEM observations were
carried out on JEOL 2000FX TEM and JEOL 2100F TEM. The
specimens for TEM observation were prepared by electrolytic
polishing of thin discs of ∅3 mm, punched from thin sections of
/C24 50 μm thickness. Electro polishing was carried out in an electro-

methanol at 20 V, using a twin jet polisher. The temperature of
the electrolyte was maintained at /C0 40 1Ct o –30 1C, using liquid
nitrogen, throughout the process of electro thinning.

3.3. The hardening or strengthening mechanism with the Si and/or
Si3N4 addition
In Ti –Al–Zr–Sn based alloy with Si (alloy A1 and A2), the
increase in hardness and compressive yielding strength with an
increase in Si content was due to the precipitation hardening effect
of silicide. With an increase in Si content from 0.05 wt% to 0.3 wt%,
silicide formed at the lath boundaries as shown in Fig. 5 (d). These
silicides acts as a barrier for dislocation motion [10], and signi ﬁ-
cantly increased the hardness and compressive yielding strength.
The higher hardness and compressive yielding strength in the A3
and A4 alloys with Si 3N4 might be explained based on the
precipitation hardening effect of TiN and retained β phase. Due
to the addition of Si 3N4, TiN precipitate dispersed in the alloy (as
shown in Fig. 4 (b)). TiN is a hard phase, produces an appreciable
strengthening effect by acting as a barrier for dislocation motion
[25–30]. With an increase in Si 3N4, the volume fraction of TiN has
increased as mentioned in Section 3.2 , which result in an increase
in hardness and compressive yielding strength, accompanied with
loss in ductility.

gested that the near α-Ti alloy (Ti–Al–Zr–Sn with minor addition of
Si3N4) exhibits improved mechanical properties and stable micro-
structure at high temperature.
4. Conclusions
In the present study the effect of Si 3N4 addition on the
mechanical properties and microstructure of Ti –Al–Zr–Sn based
near-α Ti alloys has been investigated.
1. In the Ti –Al–Zr–Sn based near- α Ti alloy with β stabilizing
elements, Si 3N4 addition enhances the hardness, compressive
yielding strength both at room temperature and high tempera-
ture. The increase in hardness and strength was due to the
precipitation hardening effect of TiN, silicide, Ti 3Al and the
retained β phase.
2. In the alloys with Si 3N4 and β stabilizing elements, more α2
precipitates were formed, due to the enrichment of more Al in
the matrix, which makes the alloy much brittle.
3. In the Ti –Al–Sn–Zr alloys with minor Si 3N4 addition and with-
out β stabilizers, no retained β phase was detected. Also no α2
was detected even after long time ageing at 750 1C for 500 h.
The hardness and compressive yielding strength of this alloy
was higher than that of conventional near- α Ti alloy, without
much loss in ductility.
4. The strengthening effect in the Ti –Al–Sn–Zr alloys with minor
Si3N4 addition and without β stabilizers was probably due to
the nitrogen atoms solid-solutioned in α-Ti matrix phase, since
no obvious nitride compound was detected. And also due to the
presence of ﬁne stable Zr –Si compounds at the lath boundaries.
