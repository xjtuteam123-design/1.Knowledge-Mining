INTRODUCTION
Castable refractory nickel alloys VZhL12U, ZhS6U,
ZhS6F [1 – 5], IN100, MAR-M200, MAR-M246,
TRW-NASA VIA [6 – 8] developed in the 1965 – 1970 still
remain the main materials fabricated by the method of
equiaxed crystallization for polycrystalline blades of aircraft
engine gas turbines. These alloys have a multicomponent al -
loying system (Table 1), which provides precipitation hard -
ening of a /c103-matrix with particles of a /c103/c162-phase in an amount
of 55 – 65 wt.% and particles of MC carbides based on Ti,
Nb and Ta monocarbides. The grain boundaries of the alloys
are reinforced with carbides and microadditions of boron
and zirconium. Some properties of the alloys are presented in
Table 2. However, the alloys of this type are susceptible to
solid-phase reactions yielding Me
6C carbides and tcp-phases
in long-term operation at a temperature of up to 1050°C,
which shortens their life. The factors promoting formation of
these undesirable phases include poorly balanced alloying,
which may be defined by parameter /c68E
2 [9, 10] (Table 1). In
addition, some of the alloys mentioned have a low ductility
(Table 2), which seems by be explainable by a “font” mor -
phology of the MC carbides forming at a temperature
30 – 40°C lower than the liquidus one and arranged in the
interdendritic regions of the casting in the form of a net [11].
This makes elevation of the ductility characteristics at pre-
served or elevated high-temperature strength-to-weight ratio
a priority direction for advancing refractory alloys of equi-
axed crystallization.
The most substantial progress in the field of castable re-
fractory nickel alloys for polycrystalline blades with equi-
axed structure has been attained upon introduction of rhe -
nium into the alloying systems [12 – 14]. We should spe -
cially mention a refractory nickel alloy CM681 (Table 1)
used as a material for casting blades with polycrystalline
equiaxed and/c47or columnar grain structure [12].
We should recall that rhenium has been used as an alloy -
ing element in the middle of the 1980s for creation of indus -
trial refractory nickel alloy ZhS32 for casting turbine blades
with columnar grain structure by the method of directed
crystallization.
The aim of the present work was to design and study a
new-generation castable polycrystalline refractory nickel al -
loy VZhL22 with 2% rhenium with equiaxed crystallization,
which possesses a high phase stability and elevated charac -
teristics of strength, ductility and high-temperature strength-
to-weight ratio.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Microstructure. The obtained polycrystalline castings of
specimens of the alloy VZhL22 had a dendritic-cellular
structure typical for cast refractory nickel alloys of equiaxed
crystallization (Fig. 1a ). Carbide particles of different shapes
were contained in interdendritic regions of the /c103/c47/c103/c162matrix
and over grain boundaries (Fig. 1 b ). By the data of the x-ray
spectrum microanalysis, these particles were identified as
precipitates of a MC carbide based on TaC. The same regions
contain coarse precipitates of /c103/c162-phase in an amount of
5 vol.% (Fig. 1 c ) formed by a eutectic and/c47or peritectic reac-
tion [22] during solidification of the remnants of the
interdendritic melt. The particles of the /c103/c162-phase formed dur -
ing cooling of the solid alloy in the process of decomposition
of the matrix /c103-solid solution have different shapes and sizes
in the dendrites and interdendritic regions; the particles be -
tween the dendrites are much coarser (Fig. 1 d and e). It is ob-
vious that the structural inhomogeneity of the alloy after the
casting is caused by segregation (microsegregation) of the al -
loying elements within a dendrite cell in the crystallization
process. The dendritic segregation is characterized quantita -
tively by the segregation coefficient [23]; for an ith alloying

The laws of the process of dendritic segregation are de -
termined [23] by the form of the solidus and liquidus sur -
faces in phase diagrams. An alloying element lowering the
solidus temperature of a nickel-base binary alloy enriches the
interdendritic regions; otherwise, it concentrates in dendrite
arms. Only tungsten and rhenium of the whole of the alloy-
ing set exert a noticeable positive action on the solidus tem-
perature of refractory nickel alloys. The effect of cobalt is
not substantial, and all the other elements (Al, Cr, Ta, Nb, Ti,
V , Mo, Hf, Zr, C) lower the solidus temperature of nickel al-
loys [25].
Thus, the laws of dendritic segregation in complexly al-
loyed VZhL22 are the same as in binary nickel systems.
As a result of heat treatment, dendritic segregation of all
the alloying elements in VZhL22 except tungsten and rhe-
nium, the segregation coefficients of which fall to –1.1 (in
the absolute value), can be eliminated virtually fully. The
particles of the /c103/c162-phase in the /c103-matrix of the dendrites and
in the interdendritic regions acquire an almost cubic shape,
and their average size is about about 0.5 /c109m (Fig. 2 a ). The
fraction of the precipitates of the eutectic (peritectic) /c103/c162-phase
decreases from 5.3 to 4 vol. % as a result of partial dissolu -
tion. The MC carbide phase does not change substantially
(Fig. 2b ). We have not detected M 6C carbides and tcp-phases
in the structure of the alloy.

2. To improve the phase stability, to lower the density
and to raise the long-term strength, the total content of the
/c103-stabilizing (Cr, Co, W, Mo, Re) and /c103/c162-forming (Al, Ti, Ta)

3. In the process of fabrication of polycrystalline castings
of specimens of the alloy by the method of equiaxed crystal-
lization, the alloying elements are segregated, which results
in their nonuniform distribution over the dendrite cells, i.e.,
Ti, Ta, Al, Mo and Cr enrich the interdendritic regions, while
W, Re and Co are concentrated in the dendrite arms. The re-
sulting castings have a dendritic-cellular structure repre-
sented by /c103/c47/c103/c162dendrites with precipitates of MC-carbide and
/c103/c162-eutectic (peritectic) phases arranged between the dendrites
in the /c103/c47/c103/c162matrix. Homogenizing annealing at 1280°C low -
ers the segregation coefficients of Ti, Ta, Al, Mo, Cr and Co
to an equilibrium value of /c1771; complete homogenization of
W and Re us not attained.
