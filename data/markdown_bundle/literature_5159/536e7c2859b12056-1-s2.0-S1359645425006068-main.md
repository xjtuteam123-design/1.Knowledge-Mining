ABSTRACT
The development of materials that exhibit enhanced strength-ductility synergy across a broad temperature 
spectrum is a non-stop pursuit for material scientists. It challenges the conventional paradigm of materials 
designed for optimal performance within a limited temperature range. In this study, we implement a combi -
national approach by integrating the high-entropy principles of a metastable Fe-based metallic matrix with 
negative mixing enthalpy strategies for intermetallic precipitates, alongside phase engineering techniques for 
multicomponent ceramic dispersions, to fabricate a novel multiphase high-entropy alloy with a nominal 
composition of Fe-28.2Ni-17Co-11Al-2.5Ta-0.5C-0.04B (at. %). The composite microstructural design leverages 
various strengthening mechanisms across scales, such as dislocation slip, deformation twinning, and martensite 
phase transformation at high strength levels, thereby achieving a remarkable tensile strength over 1.83 GPa and 
an exceptional tensile elongation of 20.6 % at room temperature. Furthermore, it demonstrates an impressive 
yield strength of 583 MPa at 1073 K, substantially broadening its applicability at elevated temperatures 
compared to traditional ferrous alloys. The strategic integration of diverse deformation mechanisms through 
multiphase design not only enhances material performance under extreme conditions but also opens new ave -
nues for the application of advanced materials in demanding environments.
Introduction
In extreme application environments such as aerospace, marine en -
gineering, deep space exploration and the nuclear industry, ultrastrong 
metallic materials are crucial for fabricating primary load-bearing 
components. These materials must possess exceptional tensile strength 
and acceptable ductility when exposed to extreme loadings and tem -
peratures[ 1 , 2 ]. Conventional metallic materials are usually constrained 
by a narrow operational temperature range and sub-optimal overall 
performance, restricting their ability to meet the escalating demands of 
harsh and extreme conditions. The innovative paradigm of complex 
alloy design based on the high entropy and negative mixing enthalpy 
principles is revolutionizing traditional notions; accordingly, this 
approach is promising for developing ultrastrong and ductile alloys that 
can withstand extreme conditions[ 3 , 4 ]. Moreover, it is becoming more 
evident that multiphase compositionally complex alloys (MCCAs) are 
surpassing single-phase high-entropy alloys (HEAs) at the forefront of 
research[ 5 , 6 ], as they can integrate various hardening and deformation 
mechanisms to strengthen these materials[ 7 , 8 ].

Inspired by these physical metallurgy principles, a complex phase 
engineering strategy was designed to stimulate different strengthening 
mechanisms in a meticulously engineered alloy (Fe-28.2Ni-17Co-11Al- 
2.5Ta-0.5C-0.04B, at. %) based on the paradigm of multi-principal 
component alloy design. In this study, the alloy is named FeNi -
CoAlTaCB alloy. The complex alloy features a desirable hierarchical 
microstructure characterized by the FCC matrix, LCO distributed in the 
matrix, dispersed intermetallic compounds (B2 precipitate), and multi- 
element borocarbide (TaBC for short) across various length scales. 
Based on this design, the SFE of the FCC matrix can reach the optimal 
critical value, allowing the alloy to trigger different deformation 
mechanisms, including dislocation slip, deformation twinning, and 
martensite phase transformation at high strength levels. This alloy was 
fabricated through levitation melting, then further utilized the benefits 
of the dynamic Hall-Petch effect through multiple-step rolling defor -
mation[ 23 , 24 ].
2. Materials and methods
2.1. Calculation of phase diagram
The Gibbs free energy (G), entropy (S) and enthalpy (H) of mixing 
defined by statistical mechanics were calculated to assess the thermo -
dynamic stability of solid solution structures formed by various com -
ponents. Additionally, further parameter calculations were performed to 
determine the atomic size difference ( δ ), valence electron concentration 
(VEC), and computational parameters ( Ω ). The characteristic parame -
ters of this alloy were calculated based on CALPHAD simulation results 
for the designed alloy. The equilibrium phase diagram and martensitic 
transformation of the alloy system were evaluated using Thermo-calc 
and JMatpro software.
2.2. Material preparation
A 500 g ingot of this alloy with a nominal composition of Fe-28.2Ni- 
17Co-11Al-2.5Ta-0.5C-0.04B (atomic per cent, at. %) alloy was syn -
thesized in an induction furnace using pure iron, nickel, cobalt, 
aluminum, tantalum, carbon and boron ( > 99.9 %) under argon 
atmosphere. The ingot was turned over and remelted five times to 
reduce compositional inhomogeneity and then dropped into a cylin -
drical alloy column with a diameter of 50 mm. The as-cast alloy un -
derwent homogenization at 1573 K for 3 h, subsequently followed by 
water quenching. The homogenized plate was subsequently hot rolled at 
1523 K to attain a 30 % reduction in thickness, followed by cold rolling 
to achieve a further 90 % thickness reduction. The cold-rolled sheet was 
then annealed at 1473 K for 10 min, prior to quenching in water. The 
sample was stored at low temperature in liquid nitrogen for 48 h, 
removed and allowed to return to room temperature. It was then 
annealed at 873 K for 2 h and then quenched again in water.

Further enhancing strength while maintaining ductility has been a 
primary objective for researchers. It has been demonstrated that twin -
ning can be induced in metallic materials, such as steels, single 
component alloys and high-entropy alloys, through mechanisms 
including the reduction of stacking fault energy, high strain rate loading 
(via impact loading), or low-temperature deformation [ 24 , 40 , 42 , 48 ]. 
Austenite steels and single-FCC phase HEAs are characterized by their 
moderate-to-low SFE, a feature also applicable to the FCC matrix of this 
alloy whose calculated SFE is about 60 mJ/ m
 2
, (see Supplementary 
Materials for details), which favors TWIP once the flow stress in the 
twinning direction reaches its critical limit. The TWIP mechanism im -
parts moderate strength and high ductility to this alloy, which is 
consistent with various manganese-containing steels and 
multi-component alloys [ 20 ]. This alloy possesses an appropriate SFE 
and C interstitials distort the lattice, leading to a significant increase in 
dislocation density at higher strains and triggers the formation of 
deformation twinning. As illustrated in Fig. 4e , twinning threads tra -
verse the FCC phase grains within the crystal and terminate at the grain 
boundaries.
Deformation-induced twins enhance the strength and toughness of 
alloys via multiple synergistic mechanisms: (1) dynamic Hall-Petch Ef -
fect: Twin boundaries act as barriers that effectively subdivide grains 
into finer subdomains, thereby enhancing strength ( σ ∝ d
-1/2
). (2)
Dislocation mean free path restriction: twin boundaries impede dislo -
cation glide, forcing dislocations to accumulate within twin lamellae 
and necessitating higher stress for further slip. Both mechanisms allow 
alloys to achieve high strength while preserving ductility, effectively 
addressing the conventional strength-ductility trade-off [ 17 , 23 , 36 , 40 ,
42 ].
The elastic modulus mismatch across the twin boundary is a conse -
quence of variations in crystal orientation, which leads to further 
pinning of dislocations by TBs and restricts the movement of disloca -
tions[ 23 ]. Ultimately, the TWIP effect during this deformation stage, as 
depicted in Fig. 3b , significantly enhances the strain-hardening capacity, 
resulting in superior tensile ductility even at ultrahigh strength levels. 
Following the occurrence of twinning and its induced TWIP effect, 
stress-induced martensite with a BCC structure is observed at a strain 
level of 15 %, which leads to TRIP.
The phase map presented in Fig. 4c indicates that the metastable FCC 
phase undergoes a deformation-induced martensitic transformation 
(FCC- γ → BCC- α ’ ) as the predominant deformation mechanism at high

5. Conclusion
In this study, the results of the simulated phase diagram design and 
thermodynamic parameter calculations show that the feasibility of 
constructing a well-coordinated multi-phase microstructure within a 
metastable system. By precisely tailoring the composition and spatial 
distribution of each phase, the synergistic interactions among different 
phase structures can be fully exploited, aiming to strengthen the mate -
rials at different temperatures. As such, we propose a synergistic alloy 
design and microstructural control strategy that integrates multiple 
strengthening mechanisms acting sequentially to improve the mechan -
ical performance of the designed alloy over a wide temperature range. 
Specifically, multi-principal element alloying is employed to reduce the 
SFE of the FCC matrix, enabling TWIP and TRIP to provide significant 
plastic deformation capability at low to medium temperatures. The 
incorporation of multiple strengtheners, including chemical short-range 
orders, intermetallic compounds and nanoprecipitates of this alloy 
allowed for the establishment of a high-strength foundation, while the 
synergistic deformation of the different phases led to a highly beneficial 
dynamic strain – stress partitioning effect. These sequential activations of 
deformation mechanisms and the impressive mechanical response 
confirm the success of this methodology of simultaneously achieving 
greatly improved strength (from massive solid solution strengthening 
and the increased interface density) and ductility (from dislocation- 
plasticity, TWIP and TRIP) at room temperature.
Additionally, by applying the negative mixing enthalpy design 
principle, local chemical heterogeneities and long-range ordered inter -
metallic precipitates can form during solidification and subsequent heat 
treatment, substantially enhancing the alloy ’ s strength, especially at 
high temperatures. The incorporation of ultra-high melting point from 
high-hardness transition metal borocarbides further boost high- 
temperature performance. Moreover, micrometer-scale ordered inter -
metallic precipitates and transition metal borocarbides can distribute 
along grain boundaries, playing a crucial role in stabilizing the micro -
structure by impeding grain boundary migration at elevated tempera -
tures. Even in severe environments at 1073 K, this alloy could become 
the high-temperature engineering components ’ favor for its good per -
formance due to the appearance of L1
2 
nanoprecipitates. We anticipate 
that the strategy presented here will be applicable to other systems, 
including both conventional and high-entropy alloys.
