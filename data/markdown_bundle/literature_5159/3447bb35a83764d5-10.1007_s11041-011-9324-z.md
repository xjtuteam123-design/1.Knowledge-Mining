A STUDY OF PROPERTIES OF HIGH-STRENGTH MAGNESIUM ALLOY
O FT H E M g–Y–G d–Z r SYSTEM

Translated from Metallovedenie i Termicheskaya Obrabotka Metallov, No. 12, pp. 15 – 18, December, 2010.
The structure and properties of a magnesium alloy of the Mg–Y–G d–Z r system are studied. The kinetics of
decomposition of the magnesium-base supersaturated solid solution is studied at 175, 200, 225, and 250°C
and the mechanical properties of the alloy are determined at room and elevated (to 350°C) temperatures. A
hot-pressed plate after aging for maximum hardening of the alloy exhibits high strength parameters both at
room and elevated (to 300°C) temperatures.
Key words: magnesium alloys, decomposition of solid solution, mechanical properties, structure.
INTRODUCTION
Magnesium-base alloys are used as light structural mate-
rials in applications where it is important to lower the intrin-
sic weight of the structure. Alloying of magnesium with
rare-earth metals raises its strength properties at room and el-
evated temperatures. Different rare-earth metals act on mag -
nesium differently [1]. The highest effect is attained in the
case of joint alloying of magnesium with two or more spe -
cific rare-earth metals. The alloy studied in the present work
contained two such metals, i.e., yttrium and gadolinium.
These rare-earth metals form wide ranges of solid solution
with magnesium, which explains the considerable growth in
the strength properties of magnesium upon their introduc -
tion. In the ternary M g–Y–G d system only the phases of
the Mg – Y and Mg – Gd binary systems are in equilibrium
with the magnesium solid solution and form long-range solid
solutions with each other [2]. The joint solubility of yttrium
and gadolinium in solid magnesium decreases upon decrease
in the temperature, which implies the possibility of harden -
ing of magnesium alloys with yttrium and gadolinium by ag-
ing as a result of decomposition of the supersaturated magne-
sium solid solution [2]. It is known that in magnesium alloys
containing rare-earth metals high strength properties are at -
tained at elevated temperatures, which allows us to expect
high strength properties in alloys of the Mg–Y–G d system
until up to 300°C.
In the present work we studied the structure and proper-
ties of an alloy of the M g–Y–G d–Z r system after hot
pressing and aging.
METHODS OF STUDY
We studied a magnesium alloy with 4.7% Y and 4.6% Gd.
In addition to yttrium and gadolinium we introduced 0.3%
zirconium into the composition for refining the grains in cast
condition [3].
The alloy was melted in a furnace with inert atmosphere
and cast into a round ingot by the semi-continuous method.
The ingot was subjected to hot pressing for a plate with cross
section of 200 /c18040 mm2 at deformation degree of about 90%
(draw ratio 13). We studied the behavior of the alloy during
aging, which was expected to cause decomposition of the
magnesium-base supersaturated solid solution, the mechani -
cal properties in tensile tests, and the microstructure.
The decomposition of the supersaturated solid solution
was studied by measuring the resistivity and the hardness.
Aging was performed at 175, 200, 225, and 250°C for up to
128 h. The resistivity was measured by a compensation
method with the help of an R348 low-resistance dc potenti -
ometer. The hardness was measured by the Brinell method at
a load of 2500 N and ball diameter of 5 mm. Indents were
obtained in the middle of the plate in a plane parallel to its

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
The alloy was aged right after hot pressing (without
treatment for solid solution by quenching after heating) with
allowance for the results of our earlier studies [1], which
have shown that a magnesium solid solution containing yt-
trium and gadolinium is highly stable in cooling.
Figure 1 presents the variation of the hardness and of the
resistivity of the alloy in the aging process. The behavior of
the hardening curves is typical for magnesium alloys with
rare-earth metals of the yttrium subgroup. It is characterized
by the presence of an incubation period (about 10 h) in the
initial aging stage and marked growth in the hardness to a
maximum upon increase in the aging time. The hardness cor-
responding to maximums on the isothermal curves decreases
upon growth in the aging temperature. The highest hardens
(108 – 117 HB ) is attained after aging at 200°C for 16 –
128 h. The resistivity in the initial aging stage (about 10 h)
varies inconsiderably and falls abruptly upon growth in the
aging time, which reflects depletion of the magnesium solid
solution. According to the results of the measurements of the
resistivity, decomposition of the magnesium-base solid solu-
tion at 250°C occurs slower than at 200°C, which implies the
possibility of retrogression in alloys of the Mg–Y–G ds y s -
tem [4]. The considerable decomposition of the magnesium
solid solution in the alloy during aging confirms that the for-
mer is supersaturated enough due to cooling of the plate in
air after hot pressing.
The mechanical properties of the alloy were determined
after aging by three regimes, i.e., 225°C, 24 h; 200°C, 24 h;
and 200°C, 64 h, chosen with allowance for the results of the
measurement of the hardness in aging, because these regimes
provided substantial hardening. The data on the mechanical
properties obtained in the tensile tests at room temperature
are presented in Table 1.
These data show that the strength properties of the alloy
increase considerably after aging at simultaneous decrease in
the ductility. After aging at 200°C the strength properties are
higher than after aging at 225°C, but the ductility is lower
though its level is still acceptable for magnesium alloys.
Growth in the aging time at 200°C from 24 to 64 h increases
somewhat the strength level of the alloy at room temperature
but lowers the ductility. We should note the high strength
level of the alloy after aging at 200°C for 24 and 64 h.

CONCLUSIONS
1. Alloy Mg – 4.7% Y – 4.6% Gd – 0.3% Zr hardens in
aging after hot pressing with 90% deformation (draw ratio
13). The degree of the hardening is the highest after aging at
200°C for 24 – 64 h. In this case the strength characteristics

2. The strength properties of the alloy decrease upon
growth in the test temperature but remain at a high level for
magnesium alloys until up to 250 – 300°C.
