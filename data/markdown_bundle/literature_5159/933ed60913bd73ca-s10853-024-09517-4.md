ABSTRACT
Ni-based wrought superalloys are a type of superalloy produced through casting-
forging processes, and usually their high-temperature performance is strongly 
influenced by microstructure and deformation mechanism. The typical micro-
structure of Ni-based wrought superalloys after heat treatments comprises equi-
axed grains, numerous annealed twins, carbides within the grain interior and/or 
at grain boundaries, and spherical γ′ precipitates (typically comprises around 20% 
of their volume). The common deformation mechanisms in Ni-based wrought 
superalloys mainly include anti-phase boundary formed by paired dislocations 
shearing γ′ phase, stacking fault formed by single dislocation shearing γ′ phase, 
dislocation bypassing γ′ phase (precipitation-strengthened) and dislocation 
entanglement, dislocation networks (solid solution strengthened). In this paper, 
the microstructure and deformation mechanism of tensile and creep in Ni-based 
wrought superalloys for Advanced ultra-supercritical (A-USC) power plants are 
reviewed, for the two prominent areas of current research, Ni–Fe-based wrought 
superalloys exhibit superior processability and cost-effectiveness compared to Ni–
Co-based wrought superalloys, thereby offering a more advantageous solution.

The performance of Ni-based wrought superalloys

these elements enhance high-temperature strength, 
they introduce complexities in terms of alloy com-

temperature strength continues to rise, the degree of 
alloying has surged significantly. This increased alloy-

Research has unveiled the critical role of the γ′

[15]. However, reaching a volume fraction exceeding 
50% poses manufacturing challenges for Ni-based 
wrought superalloys, affecting their deformation 
behavior [16]. This predicament underscores the inher-
ent tension between strengthening and deformability. 
Understanding these challenges and seeking solutions 
is imperative to unlock the full potential of Ni-based 
wrought superalloys.
It is well-recognized that an alloy’s microstructure 
determines its deformation mechanism, and this, in 
turn, significantly influences the alloy’s performance 
characteristics and service environment. Previous 
studies have delved into the deformation mechanisms 
of Ni-based wrought superalloys, but these investiga-
tions have largely been confined to specific superal-
loys under certain conditions. For instance, Sun et al. 
[17] explored GH2984 and found that, as temperature 
increased, the size of γ′ precipitates decreased, leading 
to a change in the dominant tensile deformation mech-
anism from Orowan looping to precipitate shearing. 
Wang et al. [18] investigated the impact of precipitate 
size on the tensile deformation mechanism in Nimonic 
263 at 500 °C, revealing changes in deformation mech-
anisms as precipitate size increased. Hayes et al. [19] 
delved into the creep behavior of a solution-strength-
ened Ni-based superalloy (Nimonic75), and their find-
ings showcased dislocation climb and grain boundary 
diffusion occurring concurrently during creep defor-
mation. Cui et al. [20–22] focused on the mechanism 
of creep deformation in a precipitation-strengthened 
Ni-based superalloy, observing dislocation shearing in 
γ′ at low creep temperatures, transforming into dislo-
cation climb and anti-phase boundary (APB) shearing 
in γ′ as temperature increased. Lastly, Hou et al. [23] 
examined the creep properties of a Ni-based superal-
loy, revealing the operative mechanisms of precipitate 
shearing, including stacking fault (SF) formation and 
APB creation, at high stress levels, while dislocation 
climb dominated at low stress.
To harness the full potential of Ni-based wrought 
superalloys and comprehensively understand their 
application in A-USC power plants, an exhaustive 
review of the microstructure and deformation mecha-
nism of existing Ni-based wrought superalloys is para-
mount. This review aims to elucidate the deformation 
behavior of these superalloys under varying condi-
tions, thereby providing a roadmap for the design 
of Ni-based wrought superalloys with enhanced 
temperature-bearing capacity. This holistic approach 
seeks to strike a balance between high-temperature 
strength and processability, essential for the contin-
ued advancement of coal-fired power plants and their 
efficiency. The intersection of materials science, manu-
facturing processes, and high-temperature application 
requirements becomes the focal point in this quest for 
innovation.
Microstructure of Ni‑based wrought 
superalloys
The typical microstructure characteristics 
of Ni‑based wrought superalloys for A‑USC 
power plants
The microstructure of Ni-based wrought superalloys 
plays a pivotal role in shaping their mechanical prop-
erties and performance. Figure 2 illustrates the typi-
cal microstructures of Ni-based wrought superalloys 
after heat treatment. Notably, these microstructures 
reveal equiaxed grains and an abundance of anneal-
ing twins within the grains, both of which contribute 
significantly to the alloys’ strength. Additionally, the 
presence of irregularly-shaped MC carbides is evident, 
both within the grain interior and at grain bounda-
ries, while discrete M23C6 carbides are primarily dis -
tributed along grain boundaries. The uniform pre-
cipitation of continuous carbides on grain boundaries 
serves as a robust deterrent to grain boundary sliding,

spherical γ′ precipitates are homogeneously dispersed

The role of grain boundaries in determining the 
mechanical properties of superalloys is of paramount 
importance. Grain boundaries act as obstacles to the 
motion of dislocations by disrupting the continuity 
of slip between adjacent grains. Consequently, refin-
ing the grains can enhance the overall strength of the 
superalloy. Grain size is a critical factor in this con-
text since it determines the number of grain bounda-
ries present. However, it is essential to acknowledge 
that the strength of grain boundaries diminishes with 
increasing temperature. Therefore, while finer grains 
may be advantageous for certain mechanical prop-
erties, they are less conducive to high-temperature 
strength. Research studies have shown that the creep 
properties of superalloys are closely linked to grain 
size. Larger grain sizes tend to offer superior creep 
resistance. Consequently, to strike an optimal balance 
between various mechanical properties, the average 
grain size in Ni-based wrought superalloys for A-USC 
power plants is typically controlled within the range 
of about 50–100 μm.
To bolster the strength of grain boundaries, espe-
cially in the context of elevated temperatures, it is 
a common practice to introduce grain boundary 
strengthening elements such as C, B, and Zr. These 
elements play a crucial role in increasing the strength 
of grain boundaries, thus enhancing the overall per-
formance of the superalloy. Carbides that precipitate 
at grain boundaries effectively "pin" grain boundaries, 
inhibiting grain boundary slip and promoting the 
material’s strength. The type and morphology of these 
carbides hold significant implications for the mechani-
cal properties of the superalloy [27–29]. M23C6 carbide 
is primarily rich in Cr elements, and it has a complex 
FCC structure (see Fig. 3a) that can solidly dissolve 
elements such as W, Mo, Fe, and others. The fine and 
discrete nature of M23C6 carbides is particularly ben-
eficial for high-temperature tensile and creep strength. 
MC carbide is another carbide commonly observed 
in many superalloys, as it directly precipitates from 
the liquid phase at high temperatures. This carbide 
is typically rich in strong carbide-forming elements 
such as Ti, Nb, Ta, W, Mo, and Hf, which have FCC 
structure (where WC and MoC are HCP structure, see 
Fig. 3b, c). The morphology of MC carbide can vary,

The γ′ phase, a strengthening phase in Ni-based 
wrought superalloys, assumes particular significance 
in understanding the material’s mechanical behavior. 
This phase forms as a coherent precipitate with an L12 
structure (see Fig. 4b) and is typically enriched with 
elements such as Al, Ti, and Nb, as shown in Fig. 4c. 
Extensive research has affirmed that a higher volume 
fraction of the γ′ phase results in superior perfor-
mance, particularly in high-temperature applications 
[15, 16]. However, it is pertinent to acknowledge the 
practical constraints of manufacturing, which limit the 
volume fraction of the γ′ phase to around 20% in Ni-
based wrought superalloys designed for A-USC power 
plants (refer to Table 1).
In the realm of superalloys, there is the potential 
for additional phases to be present, especially in aged 
states or under specific conditions. These phases may 
include topologically close-packed (TCP) phases 
like μ, σ, and Laves phases. However, it is essential 
to highlight that the compositions of superalloys are 
typically designed to deter the formation of these com-
pounds rather than promote them. The presence of 
additional phases can introduce undesirable mechani-
cal properties and is thus carefully managed in super-
alloy design and production.
In conclusion, the microstructure of Ni-based 
wrought superalloys is a multifaceted domain that sig-
nificantly influences their mechanical properties and, 
by extension, their suitability for high-temperature 
applications like A-USC power plants. The interplay 
between grain size, grain boundaries, strengthening 
elements, carbides, and the γ′ phase is central to the 
mechanical behavior of these materials. Achieving 
a delicate balance among these factors is the key to 
producing superalloys that can withstand the rigors 
of high-temperature environments while delivering 
optimal performance. The pursuit of the ideal micro-
structure remains a core endeavor in materials sci-
ence, where advances continue to shape the future 
of high-temperature materials for critical industrial 
applications.

One noteworthy revelation from extensive research 
is that the grain size of Ni-based wrought superalloys 
tends to remain relatively stable during extended ther-

significant advantage, as grain boundaries play a piv-
otal role in determining the mechanical properties of 
superalloys. Maintaining the size and stability of MC 
carbides is equally essential. This stability is attributed 
to the high (Nb/Ti)/Mo and Nb/Ti atomic ratios, which 
confer excellent thermal stability upon these carbides 
[26, 33–38]. Furthermore, M23C6 carbides, which are 
discrete and typically distributed at grain bounda-

ally increase in content with prolonged exposure. 
Importantly, the increase in M23C6 carbides eventually 
reaches a thermodynamic equilibrium state.
A substantial change that transpires during thermal 
exposure is the coarsening of the γ′ phase. Despite this 
change, the morphology of the γ′ phase in Ni-based 
wrought superalloys remains largely unchanged and 
typically retains a spherical form, as depicted in Fig. 5. 
While coarsening is a recognized phenomenon, it’s 
crucial to consider that Ni-based wrought superalloys 
tend to have a low content of the γ′ phase. As such, the 
changes in γ′ morphology, while noteworthy, do not 
significantly compromise the overall stability of the 
superalloy microstructure.
Extensive research has been dedicated to under -
standing the mechanisms underlying the coarsening of 
the γ′ phase in Ni-based superalloys. It is well-estab-
lished that coarsening is a deleterious process, leading 
to a degradation of the mechanical properties of super-
alloys [39– 42]. This change in the γ ′ phase is driven 
by the minimization of the system’s total free energy, 
which includes factors such as interfacial energy and 
elastic energy. To achieve a state of minimal energy, 
smaller γ′ precipitates tend to coarsen during thermal 
exposure. This process, known as Ostwald ripening, 
is a well-acknowledged phenomenon in materials sci-
ence. It is characterized by the growth of larger pre-
cipitates at the expense of smaller ones, reducing the 
interfacial free energy and enhancing the overall sta-
bility of the material [39, 43–49].

superalloys align with the classical theory developed 
by Lifshitz, Slyozov, and Wagner, collectively known 
as the LSW theory [50, 51]. This theory is founded 
on the Gibbs–Thomson theorem, which asserts that

Tensile performance
Superalloys are a remarkable class of materials known 
for their exceptional performance under extreme con-

exception. These alloys exhibit a complex relationship 
between temperature and yield strength. As depicted

Creep damage
Creep deformation is not solely about the gradual 
reshaping of materials; it also entails damage accu -
mulation over time. This damage is particularly pro-
nounced in materials subjected to high-temperature 
and high-stress creep conditions, and its under-
standing is integral to ensuring the reliability and 
longevity of these materials. Several microscopic 
mechanisms contribute to this damage, including the 
nucleation and growth of grain boundary cavities, 
dynamic changes in the sub-grain microstructure, 
the multiplication of mobile dislocation density, sol-
ute depletion due to precipitation, and the growth of 
creep-benign particles [126–129]. Among these mech-
anisms, the formation of cavities is the most common 
and well-studied form of creep damage. Cavities are 
deleterious in multiple ways, as they serve as stress 
concentration sites and diminish the load-bearing 
cross-section of the material. These cavities, which 
often preferentially form on grain boundaries, can 
eventually grow into cracks along these boundaries, 
leading to fracture in the later stages of creep life. 
It’s important to recognize that these cavities are not 
random in their formation but often occur at specific 
locations.
The formation of cavities during creep deforma-
tion is intricate and can occur in various ways. For 
example, cavities may initiate at the intersection of 
slip bands, where sliding along a grain boundary is 
impeded by an irregularity or rigid particle. They 
can also form at the junction of three grains. Notably, 
boundaries inclined at a 45° angle to the tensile axis 
are particularly susceptible to intense sliding. Sun

The transformation of carbides
Ni-based wrought superalloys are remarkable materi-
als that find extensive use in environments character-
ized by high temperatures and stresses. These condi-
tions instigate a series of microstructural changes in 
the alloys, including the coarsening of the γ′ phase, 
the formation of secondary M23C6 carbides along 
grain boundaries, and the decomposition of MC car-
bides. These transformations occur irrespective of the 
alloy’s chemical composition or grain size [71, 131, 
132]. Interestingly, the fine M23C6 carbides that form 
as a byproduct of MC decomposition play a crucial 
role in preventing crack germination during creep [98, 
133]. This suggests that the accumulated creep damage 
at grain boundaries could be mitigated through the 
decomposition of MC carbides, rather than initiating 
cracks at the interface of grain boundary carbides.

Summary and outlook
Ni-based wrought superalloys are a class of super-
alloy produced through casting-forging processes, 
and usually their high-temperature performance is 
strongly influenced by microstructure. This paper 
reviews the microstructure and deformation mech-
anism of Ni-based wrought superalloys in A-USC 
power plants. The main conclusions are as follows:
(1) The typical microstructure of Ni-based wrought 
superalloys after heat treatments comprises equi-
axed grains, numerous annealed twins, carbides 
within the grain interior and/or at grain bounda-
ries, and spherical γ′ precipitates. Notably, the γ′ 
phase of these superalloys, which are employed 
in A-USC power plants, typically comprises 
around 20% of their volume.
(2) In solid solution strengthened Ni-based wrought 
superalloys, the deformation mechanism is dis-
location entanglement within γ phase and near 
grain boundaries. As the temperature increases, 
SF and dislocation loops form, and at high tem-
peratures, the deformation mechanism typically 
involves a dense network of dislocations.
(3) The common deformation mechanisms in precip-
itation-strengthened Ni-based wrought superal-
loys mainly include APB formed by paired dislo-
cations shearing the γ′ phase, SF formed by single 
dislocation shearing γ′ phase, and dislocation 
bypassing the γ′ phase.
(4) For tensile deformation, as the tensile temperature 
and size of γ′ phase increase, the dominant defor-
mation mechanism gradually transitions from 
dislocation shearing γ′ phase (APB formed by 
paired dislocations shearing or/and SF formed by 
single dislocation shearing) to dislocated bypass-
ing γ′ phase (Orowan mechanism or/and climb -
ing).
(5) For creep deformation, the primary deformation 
mechanism is the APB formed by paired disloca-
tions shearing the γ′ phase at low temperatures 
and high stresses. At low temperatures and low 
stresses, SF formed by single dislocation shear-
ing γ′ phase instead. At high temperatures, the 
creep deformation mechanism involves thermally 
activated dislocation climbing and dislocation 
bypassing the γ′ phase.
In addition to the microstructure and deformation 
mechanism, the fracture mechanism and processing 
difficulty are also crucial for the application of Ni-
based wrought superalloys in A-USC power plants. 
The relative change of grain boundary and intracrys-
talline strength with service temperature and time 
can significantly impact the service life of superalloys. 
Although the deformation mechanism of Ni-based 
wrought superalloys has been reviewed in this paper, 
it is limited to the conditions of the intended service 
environment, with a lack of information on the defor-
mation mechanism in the actual service environment 
and under processing conditions. Some scholars have 
reviewed that superalloys may exhibit brittleness [140] 
and the Portevin–Le Chatelier effect [141, 142] under 
service temperature conditions.
The research and production of highly alloyed and 
refractory superalloys present a global challenge. For 
these highly alloyed superalloys, the relationship 
between composition, processing, microstructure, 
and properties is complex and intricately tied to pro-
duction conditions. Therefore, for Ni-based wrought 
superalloys, workability must be considered, particu-
larly for A-USC components, which are often large. 
It is desirable for the strengthening mechanism of 
alloys to be effective under service conditions but fail 
under processing conditions. Therefore, studying the 
deformation mechanism of the alloy during process -
ing is both necessary and extremely important. For the 
two prominent areas of current research, Ni–Fe-based 
wrought superalloys exhibit superior processability 
and cost-effectiveness compared to their Ni–Co-based 
counterparts, thereby offering a more advantageous 
solution.
