Abstract:
The use of dispersed precipitates is an effective method for strengthening metals, but it
typically results in a reduction in ductility. A potential way to overcome this strength-
ductility trade-off is through the incorporation of coherent nanoscale deformable
precipitates. Here, we present an approach of designing and fabricating a niobium-
based (Nb-5W-2Mo-1Zr, Nb521) alloy with copiously and coherently dispersed
nanoscale zirconia (ZrO2) precipitates through laser powder bed fusion followed by
heat treating. The intergranular ZrO2 precipitates at grain boundaries underwent
tetragonal-to-monoclinic martensitic phase transformation and deformation twinning,
while the intragranular ZrO2 precipitates within the matrix experienced deformation
twinning and substantial plastic deformation during tension. The combination of
transformation induced plasticity (TRIP) and twinning induced plasticity (TWIP) from
the nanoscale ZrO2 precipitates provides sufficient capacity to accommodate strain
and relieve stress concentration. Additionally, the intragranular ZrO2 precipitates
effectively piled up incoming dislocations from the Nb matrix to form high-density
dislocation networks during tension, which interacted with each other via the Orowan
mechanism and finally resulted in its significant plastic deformation. These
mechanisms contribute to the exceptional tensile properties of the Nb521 alloy,
including an ultimate strength of 516.8±4.0 MPa and a ductility of 18.6±0.3% with a
superior work-hardening capacity (~200 MPa), achieving a record of strength and
ductility combination for the Nb521 alloys fabricated by additive manufacturing to date.
This strategy represents a promising approach for designing dispersion-strengthened
refractory alloys with excellent synergy of strength and ductility.

results in a reduction in ductility. A potential way to overcome this strength -ductility trade-off

twinning and substantial plastic deformation during tension. The combination of transformation 
induced plasticity (TRIP) and twinning induced plasticity (TWIP) from the nanoscale ZrO 2 
precipitates provides sufficient capacity to accommodate strain and relieve stress concentration.

1. Introduction 
Niobium (Nb)-based alloys, including Nb-5W-2Mo-1Zr (Nb521) and Nb-10Hf-1Ti (C103), are 
widely used in the aerospace fields such as propulsion systems, due to their superior high -
temperature mechanical properties, low density, and excellent corrosion resistance [1-5]. These 
properties make Nb521 an attractive choice for use as a new generation of high -temperature 
structural material [6-8]. Recently, additive manufacturing (AM) has emerged as a cost -
effective and efficient method for fabricating complex and lightweight Nb-based parts [9-11], 
as compared to conventional manufacturing routes. 
Nb521 alloys have been produced using additive manufacturing techniques such as laser 
powder bed fusion (LPBF) [11], electron beam melting (EBM) [12, 13] and laser melting 
deposition (LMD) [14]. However, the extreme conditions of the AM process, including rapid 
cooling (104~106 K/s [15]) and large thermal gradients (102~104 K/mm [15]), can lead to high 
levels of thermal stress and nonequilibrium solidification [16, 17]. This can result in ultrafine 
grains and high densities of dislocations in the additive manufactured (AMed) parts. The 
ultrafine grains, precipitates (e.g., (Nb, Zr)C or Nb2C commonly found in the Nb521 alloys [12-
14]), and dislocations in the AMed Nb521 alloys all contribute to a higher yield strength

interfaces between the carbide precipitates and Nb matrix, and high-density dislocations present 
in the AMed Nb521 alloys can lead to significant stress concentration, resulting in an early 
onset of plastic instability and premature failure (showing brittle fracture for the parts made by 
LPBF and LMD [14], and ~10 % of ductility lower for the EBM-made parts than that of the

annealing under vacuum or argon atmosphere [14, 18-20] and hot isostatic pressing (HIP) [11, 
18, 19] have been used to reduce the internal stress remained in the Nb-based alloys caused by 
the AM process and improve the ductility, but these treatments often lead to a ~100 MPa 
decrease in strength. As a result, achieving a n excellent combination of both str ength and 
ductility in the AMed Nb521 alloys remains a challenge to date. 
In a previous study, we reported on the fabrication of the Nb521 alloy using LPBF with quasi-
spherical hydride-dehydride (HDH) powders prepared by jet milling as the raw feedstock [11]. 
The use of HDH Nb521 powders not only reduces the powder cost significantly but also 
generates secondary nanoscale zirconia (ZrO2) precipitates rather than carbide particles, unlike 
spherical powders as commonly used in other studies [12-14]. This is due to the higher oxygen 
content (~1600 ppm) of the jet-milled HDH Nb521 powders by comparing with the atomized 
powders [12-14], which is thermodynamically favored to react with zirconium to form 
nanoscale Zr O2 precipitates and suppress the carbide precipi tation [11, 21, 22] . ZrO2 is an 
ionically bonded ceramic that can undergo plastic deformation (when in the form of single 
crystal) through phase transformation and stress-induced deformation twinning [23-28]. As 
compared to the intrinsically brittle carbide precipitates by using the atomized powders, the 
generated single -crystalline ZrO2 particles in our previous study [11] are expected to 
demonstrate a much better deformability that provides an excellent ducti lity. Conversely, the 
generated ZrO2 precipitates in Ref. [11] did not present an acceptable deformability, and the as-
made Nb521 alloy demonstrated an unsatisfactory fracture elongation (<6 %). This unexpected 
result [11] is possibly caused by the remained large internal stress, and the achieved ultrafine

Ref. [11] focused on the precipitation behavior , and tailoring of size and distribution of ZrO2

ductility of the as-made Nb521 alloy. 
Here, we present an approach for in situ incorporation of coherent, copious and homogeneously 
dispersed nanoscale single -crystalline ZrO 2 particles into the Nb521 alloy through LPBF 
followed by vacuum heat treating, achieving superior combination of strength and ductility. In 
the current study, plastic deformation of single-crystalline ZrO2 particles (with a mean size over 
60 nm) occurred through phase transformation and deformation twinning during stress loading. 
Moreover, the coherent interface between the ZrO2 particles and Nb matrix can also 
accommodate strain and transmit incoming dislocations from the matrix [29-31]. Both factors 
theoretically contribute to the material’s ductility. However, the deformation mechanism of the 
ZrO2 particles e.g., deformation twinning and phase transformation, and their effect on the 
mechanical properties of the LPBF-made (LPBFed) Nb521 alloy after heat treating are still not 
well understood. These investigations are of great importance , and beneficial to optimize the 
microstructure and mechanical properties of the Nb521 alloy by tuning the size, population, 
and spatial distribution of the ZrO2 particles. 
Bearing this in mind, the microstructures and tensile properties of the as-fabricated Nb521 alloy 
were characterized in detail in this study. An emphasis was placed on the phase transformation 
and deformation mechanisms of the in situ generated single-crystalline ZrO2 nanoparticles, and 
associated strengthening and ductilizing mechanisms of the as -fabricated Nb521 alloy. Our 
strategy offers a promising pathway for producing dispersion -strengthened refractory alloys 
with excellent mechanical properties.

2.2 Laser powder bed fusion and heat treatment 
The Nb521 specimens were fabricated using an LPBF machine (SLM Solutions 125HL, 
Germany) equipped with a 400 W IPG fiber laser. The substrate was preheat ed to 200 ℃ in a 
flowing argon atmosphere before processing. The processing parameters included a powder 
layer thickness of 30 μm, a laser power of 210 W, a scanning speed of 800 mm/s, and a hatch 
distance of 0.10 mm, respectively. The scanning strategy was the stripe of zigzag pattern with 
a stripe length of 10 mm and a rotation of 67° after each layer [32]. Specimens cut from the 
substrate were then heat treated in a vacuum furnace under a vacuum degree of ~1×10-3 Pa at 
1400 ℃ for 1 hour to relieve internal stress. All analyses herein after were performed on the 
heat-treated (HTed, also referred to as the as-fabricated) specimens if not specified.

specimens were characterized by scanning electron microscopy (SEM, JSM -SU8100, Japan)

2.4 Tension test 
The as -fabricated Nb521 sheets perpendicular to the bui lding direction (BD) were cut into 
tensile specimens with a cross section area of 2.0×2.0 mm 2 using an electric arc wire cutter . 
Tensile properties were determined on a universal electronic tensile testing machine (WDW -
10E, P.R. China) using an extensometer with a gauge length of 10 mm under a strain rate of 0.4 
mm/min at room temperature i.e., ~20 ℃. Three specimens were tested to e nsure repeatability 
for each experiment, and average tensile data was reported . The work -hardening rate was 
obtained by differentiating the true tensile stress (σ T) over the true tensile strain (ε T) using the 
equation θ = dσT / dεT. The fracture morphology of the specimens after tension was examined 
using SEM. To study the deformation mechanism of the ZrO2 nanoparticles, interrupted tensile 
tests were conducted at different stopping strains , and the deformed specimens cut from the 
middle section were then analyzed using TEM.

spherical nano-sized precipitates/particles are homogeneously dispersed throughout the grain

patterns in Fig. 2(e) confirm that this nanoparticle is the [100] orientated monoclinic zirconia

m-ZrO2 precipitate and ma trix is well bonded with a low lattice mismatch. In this study, the

periodically spaced misfit dislocations (Fig. 2(f)).

4.1 Evolution of crystallographic structure of ZrO2 
ZrO2 is a well -studied ceramic material with a range of desirable properties, including high 
strength, high operating temperature, excellent oxidation and corrosion resistance, and unique 
shape memory effect [26-28]. At equilibrium, three different crystal structures are typically 
observed for ZrO 2 at different temperature ranges [36]: (i) monoclinic m-ZrO2, stable up to 
1443 K; (ii) tetragonal t-ZrO2, metastable at room temperature and stable up to 2625 K; and (iii) 
cubic c-ZrO2, stable from 2625 K to its melting point. The tetragonal t -ZrO2 can transform to 
the monoclinic m-ZrO2 through either stress or thermal activity, a process known as martensitic 
phase transformation, which is often accom panied by the reorientation of twin laths in the 
microstructure [23, 27, 40]. The phase composition and twinning behavior of ZrO2, in turn, can 
greatly influence the mechanical properties of ZrO2 and materials containing it, such as the as-
fabricated Nb521 alloy in this study. Therefore, it is crucial to understand the effects of thermal 
and stress activity on the evolution of crystallographic structure of ZrO2 with associated 
mechanisms. 
4.1.1 Effect of heat treatment 
The microstructure of the intergranular ZrO2 particles in the Nb521 alloy after heat treating is 
complex, as shown in Fig. 3. The particles are consisted of both tetragonal t -ZrO2 and 
monoclinic m-ZrO2 phases, nanotwins as well as SFs. However, only metastable tetragonal t-
ZrO2 phase can be found in the inter-and-intragranular ZrO 2 particles in the LPBFed Nb521 
alloy before heat treat ing (see Fig. S2 and the result in Ref. [11]). During heat treating, these 
metastable t-ZrO2 particles grew through Ostwald ripening [43], and started to transform into 
the stable m-ZrO2 phase when temperature dropped below the martensitic transformation 
temperature upon cooling. Based on the results in Fig. 3, Fig. S3 illustrates the schematic of the 
dual-phase crystallographic structure within an individual intergranular ZrO2 particle. Fig. S3(a) 
shows the atomic arrangement of the martensitic transformation from tetragonal t -ZrO2 to 
monoclinic m-ZrO2 (structure II) as well as the (001)[100] twin in the m-ZrO2 phase (structure 
I) along the [11̅0]t and [010] m zone axis. This finding is consistent with Refs. [26, 41, 42] ,

toughening effect [48], and subsequently enhances the strength and fracture toughness of the 
material [23]. Furthermore, the typical dispersion strengthening caused by the ZrO2 precipitates

4.2.2 Ductilizing 
The as-fabricated N b521 alloy exhibits a uniform elongation of up to 18. 6 % (Fig. 4(a)), 
surpassing the conventional Nb521 and C103 alloys made by AM (Fig. 4(c)). Unlike the brittle 
ZrC precipitates with the covalent bonding [53] in the conventional Nb521 alloys, the 
plastically deformable ZrO 2 precipitates in this study (Figs. 5, 6 and 7) show a different 
deformation behavior under stress. The presence of martensitic tetragonal -to-monoclinic 
transformation in ZrO 2 (Figs. 5 and 7) can be controlled to provide plasticity during tension, 
improving the ductility through transformation induced plasticity (TRIP) [23]. It is important 
to note that the ZrO2 precipitates in this study are all nanoscale single-crystalline particles with 
the mean size over 60 nm (much larger than those in the LPBFed specimen, Fig. S1(a) vs. Fig. 
S1(b)), which significantly reduces the energy barrier to trigger the phase transformation and 
favors the continuously plastic deformation of ZrO2 (transformation mismatch stresses may 
cause cracks in polycrystal ZrO2 [28]). The deformation of nanoscale ZrO 2 precipitates (Figs. 
5, 6 and 7) begins when the stress of surrounding matrix has increased significantly through 
work hardening, allowing the str ess to reach a level sufficient to impose critical stress on the 
ZrO2 precipitates. The progressive transformation and twinning of ZrO2 during tension (Figs. 5 
and 7) can accommodate incoming strains, effectively relieving the stress concentration caused 
by dislocation pile -up and leading to prolonged uniform elongation and enhanced work 
hardening due to the synthetical effect of TWIP and TRIP [52, 54]. In this sense, the in situ 
synthesized nanoscale single-crystalline ZrO 2 precipitates in this study exhibit steady 
synergistic deformation with the Nb matrix. Besides, the heat treatment enables the stress relief 
and thus benefits the final ductility, as compared to the LPBFed specimen. All above are 
responsible for the strong and ductile Nb521 alloy. 
The size, population, and spatial distribution of nano scale ZrO2 precipitates can be adjusted 
through controlling the LPBF processing parameters and heat -treating process to optimize the 
properties of the Nb521 alloy in the future. The introduction of coherent nanoscale deformable 
dispersions, as demonstrated in this work, provides a new approach for designing high -
performance refractory metals for a wide range of applications.

dispersion of coherent nanoscale ZrO 2 precipitates fabricated by LPBF followed by heat 
treating using cost-effective jet-milled powders. This resulted in superior strength and ductility 
for the Nb521 alloy. The microstructures and t ensile properties of the Nb521 specimens were 
systematically analyzed, and the associated strengthening and ductilizing mechanisms were 
investigated in detail. Main findings of this study can be drawn as follows. 
1. The in situ synthesized ZrO 2 precipitates were homogeneously and coherently distributed 
either in the Nb grain matrix or at GBs, with the average grain sizes of ~60 nm and ~2 37 nm 
for the intragranular and intergranular precipitates, respectively. 
2. The intergranular ZrO2 precipitates in the as-fabricated Nb521 alloy contain both tetragonal 
and monoclinic phases with minor (002)[100] annealing twins, while the intragranular ZrO 2 
precipitates exhibit the single monoclinic phase with good coherency with the matrix. 
3. Martensitic phase transformation from t -ZrO2 to m-ZrO2 and deformation twinning of m -
ZrO2 are the dominant deformation mechanism for the intergranular ZrO2 precipitates after 
tension. On the other hand, the intragranular ZrO2 precipitates underwent deformation twinning 
of (100)[001] and significant plastic deformation with the shape changed from quasi-spherical 
to lamellar after tension. 
4. The stress-induced effects of TRIP and TWIP in the ZrO 2 nanoparticles help accommodate 
strain and relieve stress concentration during tension. The intragranular ZrO2 precipitates in the 
Nb matrix also effectively piled up dislocations, enhancing work hardening. 
5. The as-fabricated Nb521 alloy using the low-cost HDH powders achieved outstanding room-
temperature tensile properties of 51 6.8±4.0 MPa ultimate strength and 18. 6±0.3 % uniform 
elongation with an excellent work -hardening capacity (~200 MPa) , superior to previously 
reported AMed Nb521 alloys using more expensive atomized powders.
