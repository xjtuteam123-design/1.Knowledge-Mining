1. Introduction
The development of engineering fields such as polar navigation space 
exploration, liquefied natural gas and superconducting technologies has 
driven significant demand for metallic materials with superior cryogenic 
mechanical properties [ 1 – 3 ]. Notably, multi-principal element alloys 
(MPEAs) with a single-phase face-centered cubic (FCC) structure exhibit 
remarkable potential for cryogenic applications, demonstrating superior 
ductility coupled with outstanding fracture resistance [ 4 ]. In contrast to 
conventional alloys, which are predominantly based on a single prin -
cipal element with minor alloying additions for property optimization, 
MPEAs are characterized by the incorporation of at least three major 
elements in equiatomic or near-equiatomic ratios [ 5 , 6 ]. Due to their 
unique compositional characteristics, MPEAs primarily form single- 
phase or multiphase solid solutions owing to the high entropy effects 
[ 7 ]. These alloys have attracted considerable research attention owing 
to their distinctive atomic configurations and exceptional mechanical 
performance [ 8 , 9 ]. However, the mechanical properties of single-phase 
FCC alloys are inherently constrained by the matrix characteristics. 
Specifically, the lattice distortion fluctuations in FCC structures can only 
provide weak Labusch-type pinning effects on dislocation motion 
[ 10 , 11 ], resulting in insufficient yield strength which significantly limits 
their engineering applicability. Consequently, it is imperative to intro -
duce additional strengthening mechanisms, such as grain refinement 
strengthening, precipitation strengthening, and heterogeneous structure 
strengthening, to further enhance the comprehensive mechanical 
properties of these alloys.
The strength and ductility of a material are fundamental to its me -
chanical performance. However, most metallurgical strengthening 
mechanisms inevitably lead to ductility loss, well-known as the strength- 
ductility trade-off dilemma [ 12 ]. The ductile – brittle transition is 
particularly pronounced in structural materials dominated by

In addition to Ti, Al also exhibits strong interactions with transition 
elements, playing a crucial role in the regulation and refinement of 
microstructures. In CrFeNi alloy system, the promotion mechanism of Al 
addition on BCC phase precipitation within the FCC matrix is mainly 
driven by the thermodynamic interactions between Al and Ni/Fe phases, 
which exhibit a more negative mixing enthalpy compared to the in -
teractions between Al and Cr [ 31 ]. Therefore, the stronger affinity be -
tween Al, Ni and Fe leads to the stability of these elements during heat 
treatment, while the Cr-rich parts within the matrix tend to segregate 
and form the BCC phase. Meanwhile, the addition of Al can also change 
the grain size and distribution. On one hand, the addition of Al promotes 
the formation of the BCC precipitation phase playing a role similar to 
nucleation sites, inhibits the growth of grains during recrystallization 
process [ 32 , 33 ]. This precipitation-induced grain refinement is consis -
tent with the Zener-Smith model [ 34 ]. On the other hand, from the 
perspective of solidification, Al enrichment in the nucleation zones 
promotes the formation of a supercooled zone at the liquid – solid 
interface, further contributing to grain refinement [ 35 ]. This dual 
mechanism has been widely reported in various alloys, underscoring the 
significant role of Al in microstructural control. Consequently, varia -
tions in Al content markedly affect the grain morphology and phase 
constitution, thereby providing a theoretical framework and technical 
reference for the rational design and microstructural optimization of 
MPEAs.

By comparing the tensile mechanical properties at room temperature 
and low temperature, the strength of the alloy significantly increases as 
the temperature decreases. This enhancement is primarily attributed to 
the increased hindrance of dislocation movement at lower temperatures, 
which results in a more pronounced work-hardening phenomenon 
during deformation [ 38 ]. However, the elongation of the alloy does not 
present a clear trend, due to the ductile – brittle transition of the phases 
within the alloy and the general reduction in ductility when the tem -
perature decreases. Interestingly, the SFE of the alloy decreases with 
temperature reduction, facilitating the initiation of deformation twin -
ning, which can improve the ductility of the alloy. This finding is 
consistent with the report by Wu et al., which demonstrated that high- 
entropy alloys can enhance both strength and toughness through 
deformation twinning at cryogenic temperatures, thereby promoting 
their potential for applications under extreme service conditions [ 39 ]. 
These observations suggest a complex interplay between strengthening 
and toughening mechanisms, which will be further explored in the 
following sections to provide a deeper understanding of the mechanical 
behavior of these alloys under different temperature conditions.
