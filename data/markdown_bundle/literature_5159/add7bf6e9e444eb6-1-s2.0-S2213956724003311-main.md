Strengthening of Mg-Li alloy dominated by continuously hardened Mg 
phase during room temperature rolling

Abstract 
The process of room temperature rolling is a straightforward and efﬁcient method for producing high strength Mg-Li alloys, but the 
underlying strengthening mechanism remains unclear. In this study, we successfully enhanced the tensile properties of a novel dual-phase Mg- 
Li alloy through room temperature rolling, with a remarkable yield strength of 201 MPa and an elongation-to-failure of 14%. Microhardness 
testing was conducted to evaluate the contribution of the Mg- and Li- phases to the improvement in strength. The results demonstrate that the 
hardness of Mg-phase reaches 60 HV , which is signiﬁcantly higher than the 49 HV observed in Li-phase, indicating that the Mg-phase after 
rolling plays a pivotal role in enhancing material strength. The presence of a high density of dislocations stored in the Mg-phase emerges 
as the dominant factor contributing to improved strength in Mg-Li alloys. In-situ compression testing reveals that ⟨ c + a ⟩ slip activation 
and twinning-induced slip serve as internal mechanisms for continuous deformation and hardening within the Mg-phase. Despite numerous 
precipitated Mg-phase particles within the Li-phase matrix, the hardness analysis reveals minimal strain-induced phase transformation effects 
on the overall strength of the Al-free and Zn-free Mg-Li alloy. These ﬁndings provide valuable insights for designing and fabricating 
high-strength dual-phase Mg-Li alloys.

1. Introduction 
Due to the ultra-low density, ultra-high speciﬁc strength, 
and excellent magnetic shielding and damping properties, Mg- 
Li alloys have been successfully applied in the ﬁelds of au- 
tomation, aerospace, and 3C products [ 1-3 ]. The microstruc- 
ture of the Mg-Li alloy is inﬂuenced by the lithium content, 
which in turn determines the ultimate mechanical properties 
of the material. When the lithium content is below 5.7%, the

effective in enhancing the strength of Mg-Li alloys and there- 
fore in expanding their applications. In particular, the severe 
plastic deformation process can not only eliminate casting de- 
fects and reﬁne grains, but also simultaneously enhance the 
strength and ductility of Mg-Li alloys [ 16 ]. Among various 
severe plastic deformation processes for Mg-Li alloys, rolling 
has gained increasing popularity among researchers due to its 
simplicity and wide applicability. 
Although rolling is a conventional processing technique, 
it has been extensively employed to enhance the mechani- 
cal properties of Mg-Li alloys [ 17 , 18 ]. Rolling can induce 
internal stress or facilitate dynamic recrystallization within 
the alloy, thereby effectively increasing dislocation density 
and reﬁning grain structure [ 19–21 ]. Moreover, for Mg-Li 
alloys containing second phases, rolling also contributes to 
the fragmentation of these phases, resulting in a more dis- 
persed distribution and signiﬁcantly improving mechanical 
properties [ 22–24 ]. Additionally, by modifying the rolling 
process, control over texture intensity in Mg-Li alloys can 
be achieved to further enhance their mechanical properties 
[ 4 , 25–27 ]. For instance, weakening the basal texture can im- 
prove the plasticity of magnesium alloys. In short, the mi- 
crostructure of the dual-phase Mg-Li alloy can be effec- 
tively regulated through adjusting various rolling parame- 
ters, leading to enhanced mechanical properties of the mate- 
rial. Considering the low strength and excellent machinability 
of dual-phase Mg-Li alloys, room temperature or cryogenic 
rolling processes have been recently adopted to optimize their 
properties [ 28–30 ]. 
However, it is worth noting that the dual-phase Mg-Li al- 
loy contains α-Mg and β-Li, which possess distinct intrinsic 
structures. This implies that different strengthening mecha- 
nisms may be employed for α-Mg and β-Li during the rolling 
process. The ﬁndings suggest a signiﬁcantly higher probabil- 
ity of dynamic recrystallization activation in the β-Li com- 
pared to the α-Mg under identical deformation conditions, 
indicating an increased likelihood of heterostructure forma- 
tion in the Mg-Li alloy [ 19 , 31 ]. Furthermore, strain induced 
precipitation, characterized by the appearance of numerous 
precipitated phases during rolling, can also impact the me- 
chanical properties of Mg-Li alloys. Yang et al.’s research 
revealed that after rolling, MgLi2 Al and Mg2 LiZn3 phases 
were precipitated within the β-Li, resulting in partial con- 
sumption of solid solution atoms aluminum and zinc and sub- 
sequently reducing the hardness of the β-Li [ 32 ]. Guo et al.’s 
researches have revealed that the precipitation of α-Mg can 
occur in the β-Li matrix of dual-phase Mg-Li alloys during 
room temperature rolling [ 33 , 34 ]. This phenomenon of strain- 
induced phase transformation is now being widely recognized 
as a crucial factor contributing to the reinforcement of Mg-Li 
alloys [ 26 , 33 ]. These studies have demonstrated that rolling 
will induce distinct microstructure evolution in the α-Mg and 
β-Li. Therefore, it is necessary to study the strengthening 
mechanisms of the α-Mg and β-Li respectively to deepen the 
understanding of Mg-Li alloys. 
In addition, dispersion strengthening plays a pivotal role 
in enhancing the strength of Mg-Li alloy. Besides the con- 
ventional precipitated phases containing aluminum and zinc, 
rare earth element-containing second phases and precipitated 
α-Mg are also present in Mg-Li alloys, leading to the co- 
existence of diverse precipitated phases within Mg-Li alloys 
[ 35–37 ]. Hence, comprehending the contribution of each 
strengthening mechanism is imperative for advancing new 
Mg-Li alloys. For instance, Li et al. [ 38 ] observed the precip- 
itation of α-Mg within β-Li during the natural aging process 
of the dual-phase Mg-6% Li alloy. The microhardness results 
indicated that continuous precipitation and growth of α-Mg 
led to a decrease in the microhardness of β-Li. These ﬁnd- 
ings seem contradictory to Guo’s conclusion, raising ques- 
tions about whether precipitated α-Mg can actually enhance 
the strength of Mg-Li alloys or not. If not, what underly- 
ing mechanisms are responsible for strengthening rolled dual- 
phase Mg-Li alloys? These highlights lingering uncertain- 
ties regarding the strengthening mechanisms in second phase- 
contained Mg-Li alloys and calls for further comprehensive 
research. 
In this study, a high-strength Mg-8Li-1La alloy was fab- 
ricated via room temperature rolling, with a yield strength 
of 201 MPa and an elongation to failure of 14%. As no 
aluminum and zinc were incorporated, the inﬂuence of the 
metastable Mg-Li-Al phase and Zn-solid solution on the me- 
chanical properties was eliminated. The hardening effect in- 
duced by the room temperature rolling process on both α-Mg 
and β-Li was evaluated using hardness tests. Speciﬁcally, this 
study discussed the role of the α-Mg in strengthening the ma- 
terial and reveal internal factors contributing to its continuous 
hardening. Additionally, the effect of the β-Li on the strength- 
ening was brieﬂy explored. The research provides valuable 
insights for achieving high-strength and high-toughness dual- 
phase Mg-Li alloys through room temperature rolling pro- 
cesses. 
2. Experiments 
2.1. Sample preparation 
The initial material utilized in this study is an Mg-8Li- 
1La ingot. The ingot was prepared by melting commercially 
pure magnesium, lithium, and lanthanum base metals in an 
argon-protected vacuum melting furnace, followed by casting 
and cooling. Three plates with a thickness of 15 mm were 
cut out from the casted material using a wire-cut electrical 
discharge machining (WCEDM). Subsequently, the plates un- 
derwent solid solution treatment at a temperature of 300 °C 
for 10 h in a vacuum heat treatment furnace under a vac- 
uum of < 10 Pa, followed by water quenching, as shown in 
Fig. 1a . The plate after solid solution treatment was desig- 
nated as MgLi-SS. The remaining two plates were selected 
for subsequent room temperature rolling process, which con- 
sists of two and three passes with each pass imposing a true 
strain of 20%, as shown in Fig. 1b . For comparison, the rolled 
material is referred to as Rolling-40% and Rolling-60%, re- 
spectively.

2.3. Mechanical testing 
The quasi-static tensile properties of the samples were 
examined using a uniaxial tensile testing machine at room 
temperature with a constant stretching rate of 0.5 mm/min. 
The schematic diagram of the tensile specimens is shown in 
Fig. 1d . The plate dog-bone specimens with gauge dimen- 
sions of about 10.0 mm (length) × 1.6 mm × 1.0 mm were 
fabricated through WCEDM. Each type sample was tested at 
least three times under the same conditions to ensure the ac- 
curacy of the data. To simulate the rolling process of Mg-Li 
alloy at room temperature, an in-situ compression test was

the sample underwent electrolytic polishing in order to reveal 
its microstructure. Since no extensometer is conﬁgured, the 
displacement of the ﬁxture is used to estimate the strain of the 
sample in the in-situ test. The sample preparation process for 
the hardness test is the same as that mentioned above for the 
FE-SEM test. Vickers hardness measurements for α-Mg and 
β-Li were conducted using a HUAYIN microhardness tester 
with a load of 10 gf and a holding time of 10 s. To ensure

3.2. Mechanical properties 
The true stress-strain curves of MgLi-SS, Rolling-40% and 
Rolling-60% are displayed in Fig. 6a . The comparison re- 
veals that, although the strain hardening ability of the mate- 
rial is suppressed after room temperature rolling in contrast to 
MgLi-SS, there is a signiﬁcant enhancement in strength. The 
yield and ultimate tensile strengths of MgLi-SS are around 
91 MPa and 134.7 MPa, respectively. After rolling, the yield 
and ultimate tensile strengths of the alloy, i.e., Rolling-40%, 
increase up to about 147.3 MPa and 156.7 MPa, while main-

recrystallized grains within the microstructure. This is be-

tion consumption, thereby enhances material’s work harden-

4. Discussion 
4.1. Plastic deformation mechanism of α-Mg 
The in-situ compression test facilitates a comprehensive 
understanding of the deformation mechanism of α-Mg during 
room temperature rolling. Fig. 8a presents the in-situ com- 
pressive stress-strain curve, while the SEM image inside il- 
lustrates the overall sample morphology after compression. 
The compression process is divided into three stages based 
on strain: 0% strain, 15% strain, and 23% strain. Prior to 
compression deformation ( Fig. 8b ), α-Mg grains exhibit a 
ﬂat morphology with only a few embedded second phases. 
At 15% strain ( Fig. 8c ), original smooth α-Mg grains become 
ﬂuctuated with lenticular twins and slip traces observed within 
them. As strain reaches 23% ( Fig. 8d ), both twin formations 
and slip traces continue to increase along with a signiﬁcant 
appearance of slip traces within twins. The slip trace was 
further analyzed with reference to EBSD results. Fig. 9 a rep- 
resents the actual slip trace in the crystal, Fig. 9 b corresponds 
to the EBSD map of the grain, and Fig. 9 c displays all pos- 
sible slip systems. The slip mode of α-Mg is determined by 
comparing it with the direction of each slip trace obtained 
through EBSD analysis. When there are two slip modes with 
similar direction, the ﬁnal slip system is determined based on 
which one has a larger Schmid factor. Fig. 9 d presents a mon- 
tage comprising several SEM images. Through this method, 
the speciﬁc slip system activated within each grain was iden- 
tiﬁed. A signiﬁcant number of non-basal slips were found to 
be active, particularly pyramidal < a > and pyramidal ⟨ c + a ⟩ 
slips appear to be dominant in this region. The activation of 
pyramidal ⟨ c + a ⟩ slips indicates that grains can deform along 
the c-axis direction, suggesting that deformation of α-Mg can 
better accommodate global strain. 
Twinning is also a signiﬁcant mechanism for plastic defor- 
mation in α-Mg, as illustrated in Fig. 8d . This implies that 
the addition of lithium not only facilitates the activation of 
non-basal slip but also does not suppress twin formation. For 
grains with limited initiation of slip systems, twinning is con- 
tinuously activated to coordinate plastic deformation. Fig.10 
displays the EBSD map of the microstructure after loading, 
revealing that tensile twins play a dominant role in the defor- 
mation process. Moreover, these deformation twins serve as 
crucial sites for accommodating dislocation slip. It has been 
reported that the {11¯2 2 } and {10¯1 2 } twins in HCP metals ex-

4.3. Effect of β-Li on strength 
During the rolling process of dual-phase Mg-Li alloys, 
strain-induced phase transformation occurring in β-Li is con- 
sidered a crucial mechanism for material strengthening. In 
this study, the precipitation of α-Mg in the β-Li was success- 
fully achieved through room temperature rolling. The micro- 
hardness test results of both phases indicate that the hardness 
increase in β-Li is comparatively smaller than that observed 
in α-Mg. Considering the results of strength contribution of 
α-Mg to the material, it can be inferred that the strength 
contribution of β-Li occupies a secondary position. In other 
words, the effect of precipitated α-Mg on strengthening β-Li 
is limited. Additionally, plastic deformation generates numer- 
ous dislocations which facilitate lithium and magnesium atom 
diffusion leading to precipitation and growth of α-Mg within 
β-Li [ 61 ]. And this precipitated α-Mg undoubtedly hinders 
dislocation slip and alters deformation behavior in β-Li. Cer- 
tainly, further investigation may be required regarding its im- 
pact on mechanical properties. In summary, this study pro-

5. Conclusion 
In this study, a dual-phase Mg-Li alloy with high strength 
and moderate ductility was prepared. The mechanical proper- 
ties and microstructure of the magnesium phase and lithium 
phase were analyzed separately, revealing the main strength- 
ening mechanism of the material after room temperature 
rolling. The following conclusions can be drawn: 
(1) After room temperature rolling, the yield strength of the 
Mg-Li alloy increased from 91 MPa to 201 MPa. The 
hardness of the magnesium phase increased by 40%, 
while that of the lithium phase increased by 20%. The 
hardening of the magnesium phase during the room 
temperature rolling process is primarily responsible for 
enhancing the strength of the dual-phase Mg-Li alloy. 
(2) The hardening behavior of the magnesium phase is 
mainly attributed to an increase in dislocation density 
caused by activation of numerous non-basal slip systems 
and further slip activation through twinning. 
(3) Although precipitated magnesium phases are present 
within the lithium phase, their contribution to overall 
strengthening is minimal. This suggests that in the dual- 
phase Mg-Li alloys without aluminum or zinc, it is 
difﬁcult for harden lithium phase by room temperature 
rolling.
