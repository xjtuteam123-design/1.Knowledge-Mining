abstract
Non-equiatomic Co 17. 5Cr12.5Fe55Ni10Mo5 (Mo5) and Co 18Cr12.5Fe55Ni7Mo7.5 (Mo7.5) medium-entropy al-
loys were synthesized by vacuum induction melting, cold rolling, and subsequent annealing treatment at
various temperatures (900 e1200
/C14 C) and they were investigated to exploit the precipitation strength-
ening in addition to solid solution strengthening of alloys. The effect of annealing temperature and Mo
content on the microstructure and mechanical properties are systematically analyzed. From the micro-
structural analysis, a Mo-rich
m phase is observed in the face-centered cubic (fcc) matrix. Increasing the
Mo content and low annealing temperature enhance the formation of m phase, which is consistent with
the thermodynamic calculation results. The formation of m phase effectively enhances the strength of the
Mo7.5 alloy by precipitation strengthening, and suppression of grain growth and recrystallization by
Zener pinning effect. These lead to superior combination of tensile strength as high as 1 100 MPa and
large ductility. Our results provide insights not only into
m-phase strengthening of fcc-structured alloys,
but also into the future development of high-performance MEAs.

1. Introduction
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) and medium-entropy alloys (MEAs),
consisting of multi-elements in an equiatomic or near-equiatomic
composition, have attracted increasing attention due to their un-
usual properties compared with conventional alloys [ 1e5]. As a
result of the high con ﬁgurational entropy, these alloys tend to form
a single-phase solid solution rather than intermetallic compounds.
Since the ﬁrst paper of HEAs by Yeh et al. [ 6], numerous HEAs and
MEAs have been proposed with respect to their crystal structures,
such as fcc-centered cubic (fcc), body-centered cubic (bcc), and
hexagonal close-packed (hcp) [ 7e10]. Among them, fcc-structured
HEAs and MEAs, such as CoCrFeMnNi and CoCrNi alloys, have come
to the fore as candidate materials for cryogenic applications
because of their outstanding combination of tensile properties and
fracture toughness even down to the liquid nitrogen temperature.
However, they show relatively low yield and tensile strength at
room temperature. Motivated by this weak point of fcc single phase
alloys, extensive efforts have been made to effectively improve the
room-temperature tensile properties of fcc-structured alloys, such
as solid-solution hardening [ 11], grain re ﬁnement [ 12], deforma-
tion strengthening [ 13], and metastability-engineering strategy
[14,15]. These efforts suggest that careful manipulation of alloying
elements and composition can provide optimized microstructure
for the desired properties. Along with these attempts, it has
recently been found that non-equiatomic composition of some
multicomponent alloy systems also exhibits a single fcc structure in
spite of its relatively low con ﬁgurational entropy [ 12,16]. Further-
more, well-tuned compositional variations from equiatomic to
non-equiatomic alloy system can lead to improved mechanical
properties, as suggested by Tasan et al. [ 16]. Zhou et al. [ 17]h a v e
studied the relationship between con ﬁgurational entropy and
composition of n-element multicomponent alloy system with one
matrix element (n ¼ 2e10). They observed that not only equiatomic
ternary or quaternary alloys but also many non-equiatomic multi-
component alloys can be regarded as MEAs due to their con ﬁgu-
rational entropy between 1 R and 1.5R (R is the gas constant).
As an alternative method for strengthening alloys, an appro-
priate compositional modi ﬁcation can lead to precipitation
strengthening through the formation of secondary precipitates. The

Some researchers have studied the microstructure and me-
chanical properties of Mo-containing alloys such as VNbMoTaW
[21], AlCoCrCuFeNiMo x [22], CoCrFeNiMo x [23]. Majority of the
earlier investigations have focused on the phase evolution and
mechanical properties of Mo-containing alloys. For example, the
addition of Mo into the CoCrFeNi alloys led to the formation of s
and m phases due to limited solubility of Mo in the fcc matrix and
modiﬁes the mechanical properties of CoCrFeNiMo x [23]. Ming
et al. found that tailoring the thermal-mechanical processing en-
ables the evolvement of fcc-structured Mo-added alloys to nano-
scale
m phase precipitated fcc solid solution [ 24]. But, the effect of
precipitates on recrystallization and grain growth behavior com-
bined with the evolution of tensile properties with respect to the
annealing temperature has not been reported so far in Mo-added
alloys.
In this work, the mechanical properties and microstructural
evolution of cold-rolled and subsequently annealed
Co22.5þx/C0 yCr12.5Fe55Ni10/C0 xMoy alloys were studied. The purpose of
this study was to investigate the effect of precipitates on micro-
structure evolution combined with mechanical properties of Mo-
added alloys. Working toward this aim, the Co-Cr-Fe-Ni-Mo alloy
system was chosen, which can exhibit the formation of m phase in
the fcc matrix upon heat treatment [ 23,24]. In the present alloy
system, the inexpensive Fe element was increased up to 55 at%,
while high-cost alloying elements Co and Ni were decreased for
cost-effectiveness. The Cr is of high value for its high corrosion
resistance. However, many previous studies have reported the Cr-
rich s phase in HEAs, and higher Cr concentration may lead to
the increase in the volume fraction of the s phase in the CoCr-
FeMnNi system [25]. Therefore, 12.5 at% of Cr was selected. The Mo
content was adjusted not only to promote solid solution and pre-
cipitation strengthening effects but also to investigate the effect of
precipitates on microstructure evolution. Hence, we selected the
two non-equiatomic CoCrFeNiMo alloys with compositions of
Co17. 5Cr12.5Fe55Ni10Mo5 and Co 18Cr12.5Fe55Ni7Mo7.5 (denoted by
Mo5 and Mo7.5, respectively). The con ﬁgurational entropy of the
Mo5 and Mo7.5 alloys were ~1.27 R. Therefore, the present alloys
can be regarded as MEAs. The fraction of equilibrium phases
existing at the temperatures ranging from 400 /C14 C to 1600 /C14 Cw e r e
determined by thermodynamic calculations. With the increased
Mo additions, the thermodynamic calculation results show that the
forming temperature range of m phase increases, i.e., Mo element
enhances the formation of m phase. Based on the equilibrium phase
distribution results, the microstructure of the annealed specimens
was varied by controlling the annealing treatment and the role of
precipitates was investigated in detail.
2. Experimental methods
The ingots with nominal compositions of Co
17.5Cr12.5Fe55-
Ni10Mo5 and Co 18Cr12.5Fe55Ni7Mo7.5 were casted in a vacuum in-
duction melting equipment (MC100V, Indutherm, Walzbachtal-
Wossingen, Germany) under an Ar atmosphere using pure metals
(purity higher than 99.9%). The obtained rectangular ingots with
the dimensions of 100 /C2 35 /C2 8m m3 were homogenized at 1250 /C14 C
for 6 h, pickled in a 20% HCl to remove scales and contaminants, and
milled to a thickness of 7 mm. For further grain re ﬁnement, the
pickled ingots were rolled at room temperature with a thickness
reduction ratio of ~79% (from 7 to 1.5 mm). The rolled sheets were
annealed at temperatures in the range between 900 and 1200 /C14 C for
60 min, and subsequently water-quenched.
The plate-type sub-sized tensile specimens with a gauge length
of 6.4 mm, a gauge width of 2.5 mm, and a thickness of 1.5 mm were
cut in the rolling direction. The mechanical properties of all sam-
ples subjected to annealing treatment between 900 and 1200 /C14 C for
60 min were tested in tension at an engineering strain rate of
10/C0 3 s/C0 1 using a universal testing machine (Instron 1361, Instron
Corp, Canton, MA, USA) with a vision strain gage system (ARAMIS
v6.1, GOM Optical Measuring Techniques, Germany). Three samples
were tested for each condition to con ﬁrm the reproducibility of
results, and the representative data were obtained by averaging
three values at each data point and determining the standard
deviation.
The cold-rolled and annealed specimens were mechanically
polished and then electro-polished using a 92% CH 3COOH þ8%
HClO4 solution at an operating voltage of 40 V. Phase present in
both the Mo5 and Mo7.5 alloys were identi ﬁed by X-ray diffraction
(XRD) using a RIGAKU D/MAX-2500 X-ray diffractometer with an
incident beam of Cu K a radiation of 0.15406 nm with a step scan-
ning rate of 2 deg/min. Electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD)
measurements were performed with a ﬁeld emission scanning
electron microscope (FE-SEM) equipped with a TSL EBSD system.
The EBSD data were interpreted using orientation imaging micro-
scopy (OIM) collection software (TSL OIM Analysis 7). The SEM
analysis was conducted using a high-resolution FE-SEM (JSM-
7800F PRIME, JEOL) equipped with back-scattered electron (BSE)
detector. The samples for transmission electron microscopy (TEM)
were produced by dual-beam focused ion beam (FIB, FEI Helios
Nano-Lab™). Scanning-TEM (STEM) imaging and energy-dispersive
spectroscopy (EDS) in STEM mode were obtained with a JEOL 2100F
analytical TEM system equipped with an aberration corrector
operated at an acceleration voltage of 200 kV.

4. Discussion
The aim of this study is to analyze the impact of the precipitates
on a fcc-structured non-equiatomic MEAs. The emphasis is placed
on the microstructural changes (with respect to annealing tem-
peratures after cold rolling) and their impact on the mechanical
properties of the present alloys. Microstructural analysis revealed
that the present alloys are composed of m phase at the grain
boundaries and within fcc grains. Previous studies reported the
effect of Mo addition on the microstructures and properties in
CoCrFeNiMox [23,32], Al 0.5CoCrFeNiMox [33], and AlCrFeNiMo x al-
loys [ 34]. The results showed the formation of a second phase,
whose volume fraction increases with increasing the Mo content.
More speci ﬁcally, most alloys in the previous studies showed the
formation of m phase, together with Cr-rich s phase with a tetrag-
onal structure.
The present Mo5 and Mo7.5 alloys, on the other hand, show no
trace of s phase when the cold-rolled specimens were annealed at
900e1200 /C14 C for 1 h (Fig. 2). The thermodynamic calculation results
also show that the primary crystalline phase is fcc phase, and the m
phase begins to form as the temperature decreases under

attribute this to the enhanced solid solution strengthening by
further addition of Mo content in the Mo7.5 alloy. Mo has the
largest metallic radius among the ﬁve elements in the present alloy
system, and it is known that the increase in the Mo content in the
fcc phase leads to the enhanced solid solution strengthening effect
[23,24].
The further addition of Mo also facilitates the formation of m
phase and greatly hardens the Mo7.5 alloy without accompanying
serious embrittlement. In addition, the increase in the fraction of
m-
phase particles with the decrease in annealing temperature, leads
to the improved strength. The hardness of the m-phase particles is
extremely high in the order of 15 GPa [ 23]. These hard particles
precipitated at boundaries and those in the ductile fcc matrix are
not plastically sheared by gliding dislocation during plastic defor-
mation [ 24]. Thus, they act as a strong barrier for dislocation mo-
tion, leading to the precipitation strengthening. The BSE-SEM
analysis in Fig. 6 shows that the m-phase particles are essentially
ﬁne and discrete with no trace of a continuous network. According
to Liu et al. [ 23], the discrete particles, formed from the supersat-
urated fcc matrix during annealing, were much ﬁner because of the
slow diffusion process in multicomponent alloys. We note that
numerous microcracks are nucleated in associated with these
particles, while the propagation of cracks is effectively blunted by
the ductile fcc matrix, leading to the ductile dimple fracture ( Fig. 9).
Therefore, the improved strengths with comparable ductility loss is
closely associated with the nature of particle distributions and the
ductile characteristics of the fcc phase.

perature also enhances the strength of the material. Fig. 10 presents
the variation in mean grain size of the Mo5 and Mo7.5 alloys as a
function of annealing temperatures. In the Mo5 alloy, the higher the
temperature, the larger the grain size. Corresponding to the
decrease in grain size, the yield strength increases, which is
consistent with the Hall-Petch relation. Meanwhile, the grain size
of the Mo7.5 alloy is maintained at ~5

5. Conclusions
In the present study, non-equiatomic CoCrFeNiMo MEAs
(Co17.5Cr12.5Fe55Ni8Mo5 and Co 18Cr12.5Fe55Ni7Mo7.5, in at%) were
investigated after cold rolling and subsequent annealing treatment
at different temperatures to elucidate the effect of the micro-
structural evolution on mechanical properties. From the XRD and
TEM analyses, the introduced alloys are composed of fcc and m
phase. The m phase is found to be a Mo-rich phase. The micro-
structural evolution shows that the further addition of Mo en-
hances the formation of m phase. In addition, the fraction of m-
precipitates in the fcc matrix increases with the decrease in
annealing temperature, which is in good agreement with the
thermodynamic calculation results. In the Mo7.5 alloy, the forma-
tion of m-phase particles acts as obstacles for grain boundary
migration, which leads to the suppression of the recrystallization
and grain growth owing to Zener pinning, The SEM analysis shows
that the propagation of cracks associated with particles is effec-
tively blunted by the ductile fcc matrix, leading to the ductile
dimple fracture. The combination of various strengthening mech-
anisms leads to exceptional room-temperature tensile properties.
Our results are important not only for understanding the
strengthening effect by the
m-phase formation, but also for the
future development of high-performance alloys.
