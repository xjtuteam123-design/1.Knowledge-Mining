Remarkable strength of CoCrFeNi high-entropy alloy wires at cryogenic
and elevated temperatures

CoCrFeNi high-entropy alloy wires containing nano-sized twins were produced by heavy cold-drawing process.
The alloy wires exhibit high tensile yield strength of 1.2 GPa, as well as a considerably high percentage elongation
(i.e., ductility) of 13.6% at 223 K. Such cryogenic and room temperature properties were found to result from the
blockage of dislocation glide by primary and secondary nano-scale twin boundaries and, to less extent, by other
dislocations. The high level of strength remained with the increase of temperature up to 923 K. The deformation
mechanism of the cold-drawn wires was observed to be governed by dislocation slip and dynamic recovery at
elevated temperatures.

Abundant grain boundaries (GBs) inﬁne grain alloys serve as obsta-
cles to dislocation motion [1–3]. Grain reﬁnement is thus an attractive
way in improving the strength of engineering alloys [4]. However, the
GB increment by grain size reduction has limits. Most nano-crystalline
metals with high strength are very brittle, with a tensile ductility of typ-
ically less than a few percent [5]. Such strength-ductility trade-off [6]
has hindered the development of engineering alloys for many years.
On the other hand, twin boundaries (TBs), a special type of boundaries,
are also effective in blocking dislocation motions[7]. TB strengthening is
therefore similar to GB strengthening governed by Hall-Petch relation
[8]. Some studies showed that the strengthening effect of TBs is surpris-
ingly strong when the twin thickness was reduced down to the nano-
meter scale [9–10].T h i si sb e c a u s et h e‘actual’ grain size is determined
by nano-twin boundary spacing [11–12]. In addition, unlike that the
high density of GBs in nano-crystalline metals signi ﬁcantly reduces
the ductility, nano-twins could accommodate plastic straining[13].
For the nanotwin-governed strengthening, the higher critical stress-
es are required to carry plastic deformation across the twin bundles
other than individual twins, the strengthening effect is ampli ﬁed (e.g.,
N13%) [14]. It is closely related to the twin thickness of twin bundles,
which drops with the decrease of stacking fault energy (SFE). The
twin thickness of the nano-twins in copper generated by dynamic
plastic deformation at liquid nitrogen temperature is ~ 40 nm [15],
while in the 316 L austenitic stainless steel processed by dynamic plastic
deformation at high strain rate (up to 10
3 s−1) it is ~ 23 nm [16]. Al-
though the high density nano-twinned structure can be created, such
cumbersome preparation processes are undesirable for practical appli-
cation. Recently, HEAs, consisting of multiple principal elements, have
broken the shackles applied to alloy design for decades[20–21],p r o v i d -

ties [22–24]. Bearing this in mind, CoCrFeNi system was chosen to
address the issue raised above, because of its low stacking fault energy
(SFE) (31.7 mJ/m
2) that promotes deformation twins [17].I na d d i t i o n ,
the alloy has a FCC structure, rendering it potentially ductile over a
wide temperature range (i.e., from cryogenic to elevated temperatures)
[18]. Moreover, the alloy has potential for applications involving high
strain rates such as impact loading [19].
Here we report the generation of nano-scale twins in CoCrFeNi HEA
wires by cold-drawing at room temperature without high strain rates.
For the ﬁrst time, a very high tensile strength combined with a consid-
erable tensile ductility was obtained, using simple, yet effective process-
ing method, in the CoCrFeNi HEA wires over a wide temperature range
(223–923 K).

Ti-gettered high-purity argon atmosphere. The alloy cast was ﬂipped
and re-melted ﬁve times to minimize the inhomogeneity. After homog-
enizing, hot forging, annealing and turning, the HEA wires (9.3 mm in

The XRD patterns of the cross-section of the CoCrFeNi wires are
shown in Fig. 1 a. A single-phase bearing face-centered cubic (FCC)
structure was detected. The patterns of the wires show a well-devel-
oped 〈111〉 texture aid a 〈100〉 texture. The grain orientation and
[001] inverse pole ﬁgure map obtained from the longitudinal section
of the HEA wires are shown in Fig. 1 b. The texture is consistent with
the results revealed by the XRD patterns. Based on the EBSD results,
the average grain size was calculated and found to be ~ 4.1μm. TEM ob-
servations show that numerous lamellar structures resulted from the
formation of nano-TBs in the micron-sized grains. The twins are very
ﬁne (ranging from a few to tens of nanometers, as seen in Fig. 2 ),
while the TBs were found to be 38.9° 〈110〉 type.
According to the nano-indentation results inFig. 3a, the hardness of
microcrystalline CoCrFeNi HEA wires with nano-twins has been greatly
improved (4.57 ± 0.29 GPa) from that of recrystallized HEA (2.71 ±
0.13 GPa). Interestingly, the HEA wires showed a good combination of
high strength and ductility over a wide range of temperatures, as dem-
onstrated in Fig. 3b. At 293 K, the yield strength is 1107 MPa, while the
percent elongation is 12.6%. At 223 K, the yield strength increased to
1224 MPa, while the elongation to failure remained unchanged
(13.6%). With the increase of the test temperature to 923 K, the percent
elongation increased markedly up to 27.1%, accompanied by a modest
drop of the yield strength (837 MPa). The formation of the high density
of TBs greatly reduced the actual grain size. According to the Hall-Petch
relation [25,26], resulting grain reﬁnement is to a great extent responsi-
ble for enhanced yield strength of the alloy. Notably, strain hardening
phenomena are absent from the stress-strain curves (Fig. 3b). Such un-
usual mechanical response was also observed in nano-twinned metals
with pre-existing dislocations[27–29], which was attributed to the sup-
pression of dislocation glide by the high density of TBs [28].
The grains with different orientations show different deformation
mechanism which could be evaluated by comparing the Schmid factors
for mechanical twinning and dislocation slip. Under tensile conditions,
the Schmid factor for the dislocation slip (0.27) in 〈111〉 oriented
grains is smaller than that for mechanical twinning (0.31) [30], while
in grains with other orientation the factor for deformation twinning is
smaller than that for dislocation slip. In light of this condition, the
〈111〉 texture developed in the CoCrFeNi HEA wires promoted greatly
the formation of deformation twins during the cold-drawing. The low
SFE also made the twins with nanoscale thickness (e.g., 2.8 nm) possi-
ble. Of particular interest is the birth of nano-twins during cold drawing
at room temperature without high strain rate, which served as a barrier
to dislocation glide. As shown in Fig. 2,
nano-twins were clustered to-
gether in the form of bundles. The formation of deformation twins
could lead to dynamic Hall-Petch effect [31–32]. As the TBs became
narrower, the effective grain size was reduced and the dislocation mo-
tion became more difﬁcult. Strong interactions between twins and dis-
locations would cause the generation and accumulation of a high
density of sessile dislocations within the twins, making the strength in-
crease further. The high dislocation in-take also increased the critical
shear stress required to induce plastic deformation across the twins[8].

yield strength and substantial percent elongation over a wide tempera-
ture range. Fig. 4 shows the TEM images of the nano-twins in the HEAs
after tensile deformation. The temperature inﬂuences the deformation
mechanisms of the HEAs with nano-twins. It can be observed that the

to create secondary deformation twins. That could lead to a strong dy-

fact, dislocation slip and deformation twinning are two competitive
mechanisms that govern plastic deformation. Similar to traditional al-
loys [33–34], there exists a critical stress that needs to be overcome in
order to activate secondary mechanical twinning upon further plastic
straining of the HEAs with nano-twins. From room to cryogenic temper-
atures, the dynamic recovery was suppressed and the yield strength in-
creased. Boundaries of the primary and secondary deformation twins
differ structurally from coherent growth TBs or annealing TBs [13].
High-density partial dislocations exist along deformation TBs (as
shown in Fig. 4), of which the excess energy is higher than that of coher-
ent TBs. When the test temperature increased, the dynamic recovery in-
creased and the yield strength decreased as a result. It became difﬁcult
for the secondary mechanical twins to emerge. The primary nano-
twins could still exist at elevated temperatures, and they provided effec-
tive resistance to permanent deformation during tensile testing at ele-
vated temperatures. The remarkable strengthening effect as observed
in the HEA wires apparently originated from strong TB-dislocation in-
teractions that differ fundamentally from the GB-dislocation interac-
tions in conventional alloys (e.g., coarse- or nano-grained alloys) [13].
Continuum crystal plasticity model [35], built upon the observation of
deformation in nano-twinned Cu, sheds light on the possibility of softer
resistance to plastic ﬂow within a small region (a few atomic layers
wide) of high density dislocations centered around the TBs. The model
predicts an increase in strength for nano-twinned alloys with ﬁner
TBs, and also exposed a desirable trend that the presence of nano-
twins would improve ductility, which is consistent with ﬁndings in
this work. The strengthening effect of nano-twins was found to span
from cryogenic to elevated temperatures. In so doing, it distinguishes
the HEA wires from currently commercially available products (e.g.,
pearlite or ferrite wires), meaning that the HEA wires have great poten-
tial for safety-critical, load-bearing applications over a wider tempera-
ture range. The further studies are however still needed to alleviate
strength-ductility trade-off in the developed wires.
In summary, CoCrFeNi high-entropy alloy wires with deformation
nano-twins were produced by heavy cold-drawing process. The alloy
wires exhibit high tensile yield strength of 1.1 GPa and a considerable
percent elongation of 12.6% at 293 K. Unlike deformation-induced
grain reﬁnement, a high level of strength was retained when the wires
were tested at a high temperature of 923 K. The strengthening effect
was further enhanced by lowering the test temperature without sacri-
ﬁce in the tensile ductility. The dominant deformation mechanism
