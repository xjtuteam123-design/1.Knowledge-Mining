Simultaneously enhanced strength and ductility of Mg-Zn-Zr-Ca alloy
with fully recrystallized ultraﬁne grained structures

Fully recrystallized ultraﬁne grained (UFG) Mg-Zn-Zr-Ca alloy was successfully fabricated by a process including
high pressure torsion (HPT) and subsequent rapid annealing treatment. After the HPT by one rotation, a nano-
crystalline (NC) structure with an average grain size of about 100 nm and deformed characteristics was obtained.
After subsequent annealing at 300 °C and 400 °C for 1 min, fully recrystallized UFG structures with average grain
sizes of 0.77 μm and 0.98 μm were obtained, respectively. Room temperature tensile test revealed that the UFG
Mg alloy exhibited simultaneously enhanced strength and ductility compared with those of the coarse grained
counterpart.

DuctilityIn recent years, Magnesium (Mg) alloys have attracted extensive at-
tention due to their low density and high speci ﬁc mechanical proper-
ties. They are expected to be applied in transportation and aerospace
industries, leading to signi ﬁcant weight reduction [1–5]. However,
their anisotropic hexagonal close-packed (HCP) crystal structure pro-
vides only a limited number of independent slip systems, resulting in
relatively low ductility as well as poor formability in Mg alloys at
room temperature [6].
Grain reﬁnement is considered as one of the most promising ways
for optimizing ductility without sacri ﬁcing strength in metallic mate-
rials [7,8]. To achieving ultraﬁne grained (UFG) structures, many strate-
gies have been developed. Among them, severe plastic deformation
(SPD) processes, such as equal channel angular pressing (ECAP), accu-
mulative roll bonding (ARB) and high pressure torsion (HPT), have al-
ready been proven as effective approaches for achieving signi ﬁcant
grain reﬁnement [9]. A survey of available scienti ﬁc literatures for Mg
alloys demonstrated that SPD was mostly applied by ECAP at relatively
high temperatures to avoid cracking[10–12].H o w e v e r ,d u et ot h ee f f e c t
of recovery and recrystallization at elevated temperatures, the mini-
mum grain sizes of the ECAP-processed Mg alloys are usually around
several micrometers. For example, Kulyasova et al. [10] reported that
an AM60 Mg alloy with an average grain size of ~ 2μm could be obtained
after 10 passes ECAP at 210 °C.
In contrast, according to the principle of HPT, high hydrostatic pres-
sure is applied to the disc-shaped specimen during torsion, so that un-
desirable cracking can be avoided even at room temperature [13].
Very recently, the HPT has been started to be used for fabricating bulk
nanocrystalline (NC) or UFG Mg alloys. However, due to the high densi-
ty of remained lattice defects, the HPT processed NC/UFG Mg alloys did
not show good ductility. Thus, their mechanical properties were charac-
terized only by simple hardness test[14,15]. Such a limited ductility due
to deformation characteristics in microstructures is a common issue in
as-SPD processed NC/UFG materials [16]. In order to overcome the
above de ﬁciency of HPT processed Mg alloys, a process including
room temperature HPT and subsequent rapid annealing treatment is
proposed in the present study. Mg-Zn-Zr-Ca (ZKX600) alloy is selected
for investigating the idea, because this alloy contains high density of
thermally-stable nano-precipitates even in as-cast state [17].T h e
nano-precipitates would be effective for controlling the grain size dur-
ing thermo-mechanical processes.

shaped specimens with a diameter of 10 mm and thickness of
0.80 mm sliced from the ingot were provided for the HPT process. The
HPT was conducted at room temperature under a pressure of 6 GPa

Mechanical properties of the specimens was characterized by
Vickers hardness test and uniaxial tensile test. Vickers hardness test
was performed at a load of 980 mN and a dwell time of 10 s using a
SHIMADZU HMV-1T hardness tester. For the tensile test, small-sized
tensile specimens with a gage length of 2 mm and a gage cross section
of 1 mm × 0.5 mm were cut from the HPT processed discs so that the
center of the gage part coincided with the position at a radial distance
of 3.0 mm from the center. Tensile tests were carried out at a quasi-
static strain rate of 8.3 × 10
−4 s−1. The displacement of the gage section
was precisely measured by a CCD video camera extensometer and the
strain was calculated by a digital image correlation (DIC) technique
using a Vic-2D software [18]. For each processing condition, three ten-
sile specimens were tensile tested and the results showed good
reproducibility.
The microstructures of the specimens were observed by transmis-
sion electron microscopy (TEM) using JEOL 2010 operated at 200 kV
on the sections parallel to the rotation axis in the HPT at a radial position
of 3.0 mm from the center of the HPT discs. The areas on the sections
perpendicular to the radial direction at a distance of 3.0 mm from the
center of the annealed specimens were characterized by scanning elec-
tron microscopy (SEM) using JSM 7100F equipped with an electron
backscattering diffraction (EBSD) system. The grain size was measured
by linear interception method on the obtained EBSD maps.

Tensile stress-strain curves of several representative specimens are
presented in Fig. 4 (a). For the as-ST specimen (grain size of 57 μm),
the yield strength, ultimate tensile strength, uniform elongation and
total elongation are 107 ± 4 MPa, 256 ± 5 MPa, 13.9 ± 0.5% and
16.5 ± 0.7%, respectively. After the HPT process by 360° rotation, the
yield strength was greatly enhanced to 311 ± 3 MPa. However, the ten-
sile ductility (total elongation) greatly dropped to 1.2 ± 0.1%. This is a
typical mechanical behavior of as-HPT processed materials in not only
Mg alloys but also other metals. After annealing at 300 °C for 1 min, it
was found that the yield strength and tensile strength of the specimen
having the mean grain size of 0.77 μm were 235 ± 2 MPa and 328 ±
3 MPa, respectively. The tensile strength is comparable to that of the
as-HPT specimen. The uniform elongation and total elongation signiﬁ-
cantly recovered to 20.5 ± 1.0% and 26.1 ± 1.4%, respectively. That is,
by making the microstructure a fully recrystallized UFG, the tensile duc-
tility could be remarkably regained without sacri ﬁcing the tensile
strength. When the annealing temperature was increased to 400 °C,
the yield strength, tensile strength, uniform elongation and total elon-
gation decreased slightly to 213 ± 3 MPa, 317 ± 5 MPa, 19.3 ± 1.2%
and 21.8 ± 1.5%, respectively. It has been reported previously that the
T6 treated ZKX600 alloy (i.e., twin roll cast, hot rolled by 50% thickness
reduction and peak aged) shows a tensile strength of 321 MPa with an
uniform tensile elongation of about 7.5% [17]. The present UFG
ZKX600 specimens with fully recrystallized UFG structures possess
comparable strength and almost three times tensile ductility as those
of the alloy at T6 state.
By changing the annealing temperatures and holding time, fully re-

size, with a slope of 123.8 MPa·m

in the UFG specimens should be mainly attributed to the grain boundary

In summary, fully recrystallized UFG ZKX600 alloy specimens having
sub-micrometer mean grain sizes (0.77μm and 0.98 μm) were ﬁrst fab-
ricated by annealing heavily deformed specimens under appropriate
conditions. Finely dispersedβ′ phase played a critical role to inhibit mi-
crostructure coarsening. It was found from the room temperature ten-
sile test that simultaneously enhanced strength and ductility could be
realized in the fully recrystallized UFG specimens, which was attributed
to the grain boundary strengthening and enough strain-hardening abil-
ity regained by recrystallization. The present result would develop a
possibility of high strength Mg alloys with good formability.
