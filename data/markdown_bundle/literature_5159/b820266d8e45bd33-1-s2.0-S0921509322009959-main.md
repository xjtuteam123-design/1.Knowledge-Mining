ABSTRACT 
In this work, by equipping the high-entropy concept we designed a γ / γ
′
-type high-entropy alloy with high Fe 
content (25 at.%), and further alloyed Nb to evaluate its microstructural stability and mechanical responses. We 
found that after 850 
◦
C annealing, 1.5 at.% Nb addition can completely suppress the discontinuous precipitation 
(DP) reaction. The high γ / γ
′
structural stability even after one month ageing at 800 
◦
C is believed to be originated 
from strong partitioning of Nb to γ
′
. The onset of continuous inter-mass-flow of Fe and Nb across the γ / γ
′
interface further enhances the γ
′
ripening resistance. The increased tensile strength in the Nb-alloyed HEA across 
a wide temperature range (23 – 900 
◦
C) is because of the increased volume fraction and anti-phase boundary 
energy of γ
′
. The corresponding lowered ductility is then attributed to the absence of the DP colony that not only 
acts as a stress buffer zone but also introduces grain boundary serration to withstand intergranular cracking. 
1. Introduction 
Iron is one of the cheapest and most widely used transition metal 
elements in the world, for example, various types of steels. However 
when facing thermal exposure, the low structural stability of iron-based 
solid-solution (available for martensitic transformations [ 1 – 3 ]), and the 
high risk to form detrimental intermetallics by interacting with re -
fractory elements that are beneficial to temperature capability, e.g., Nb 
[ 4 , 5 ], address serious doubts on putting iron into the alloying strategy 
for high-temperature structural alloys. Actually except for the class of 
NiFe-based wrought superalloys (e.g., Inconel and Incoloy series) that 
are sustainable typically below 900 
◦
C, iron is rigidly excluded in almost 
all commercial Ni-/Co-based cast superalloys [ 6 ]. The absence of iron in 
these superalloys indeed results in superhigh heat-resistance, yet as we 
cannot ignore, at the expense of raw cost. Realizing a balance between 
the high-temperature performance and the cost has long been one tough 
question in the past decades, which is crucial and important in both 
scientific and engineering perspective. 
Recently, a new alloy design philosophy, namely high-entropy alloy 
(HEA), or compositional complex alloy (CCA), emerged and encourag -
ingly challenges the tradition by the subversive concept of, multi- 
principal [ 7 – 9 ]. The facts that the increased mixing entropy stabilizes 
solid-solution phases at elevated temperatures, as well as that the 
enhanced local lattice distortion leads to decent solid-solution 
strengthening, seem paving a new way towards new-generation heat- -
tolerance materials. Efforts have been paid along this direction. Starting 
from the Cantor alloy system and further co-alloying Al and Ti in it, 
distributed nano coherent γ
′
precipitates are introduced hence both 
room and elevated temperature strength are profoundly arisen [ 10 – 12 ]. 
This scheme soon leads to numerous variants of γ / γ
′
type HEAs, most of 
which contain near equiatomic percentage of iron [ 13 – 15 ]. In partic -
ular, the (FeCoNi)
84
Ti
7
Al
7 
HEA shows the most extraordinary room 
temperature tensile properties with yield strength over 1 GPa while 
fracture strain of ~50% [ 13 ]. However, the high-temperature perfor -
mance, including structural stability and high-temperature strength, of 
these heavily iron-contained HEAs still remains unsatisfactory [ 16 , 17 ]. 
Along this line, high-entropy (HE) superalloys, a new HEA branch, 
has been proposed and developed. Currently, these HE superalloys show

4. Discussion 
In the following, we will try to first understand the effect of minor Nb 
alloying on DP reaction in the current HEA system. In general, DP re -
action is directly related to GB precipitation and GB migration [ 38 , 39 ]. 
The former term can be tuned by the thermal stability of continuous 
precipitates and the diffusion kinetics of the precipitate formers, while 
the later term is determined by the difference between the driving force, 
including total surface energy and strain energy, and the resisting force, 
mainly originated from solute drag [ 40 ] and Zener drag [ 33 ]. It is 
already mentioned in Fig. 1 i and Appendix Fig. S1 that, Nb strongly 
partitions to γ
′
and further makes the dissolving of the continuously 
precipitated γ
′
harder. Nb is also known as a sluggish diffuser, which 
slows down the redistribution process during DP reaction. The driving 
force for GB migration is not easy to clarify however, the resisting force 
is certainly increased due to Nb alloying. Firstly, the few scattered 
Nb-rich GB particles address plausible Zener dragging force against GB 
migration, as supported by the observation in the prolong-aged TAN1.5 
HEA in Fig. 2 b. Secondly, although not observed here, Nb is prone to GB 
segregation as suggested in another HEA system [ 37 ], which will 
generate additional solute dragging force. In short, Nb addition inhibits 
both the heterogeneous GB precipitation and the migration of GB, 
together contributing to the near-fully suppressed DP reaction in the 
TAN1.5 HEA.

At last we discuss the intergranular cracking and the relative duc -
tilizing origins. Before that we recall that the environmental attacking 
mainly by hydrogen and oxygen, has long been the Achilles heel for 
superlattice structural materials, especially at intermediate tempera -
tures [ 44 – 46 ]. Among them, oxygen is somehow more general func -
tioning, with an important mechanism known as stress assisted grain 
boundary oxidation (SAGBO) [ 47 , 48 ]. This is also true for alloys con -
taining high fraction of superlattice phases, e.g., Ni-based superalloys 
[ 49 , 50 ] and of no doubt, γ / γ
′
type HEAs [ 16 , 17 , 51 ] as involved in this 
study. Yang et al. [ 16 ] mentioned that the appearance of DP colony 
degrades the GB cohesion and results in crack initiation at the abutting 
GB. However as they also pointed out, the blocky fragile Huesler phase 
forms simultaneously during DP and plays an equally important role. 
Thus, it is hard to distinguish each own contribution to the GB embrit -
tlement. Chang et al. [ 51 ] simply attributed the poor ductility at 
mid-temperatures to the easy de-bonding along the DP-associated GBs in 
another γ / γ
′
type HEA, but they neglected that the environment attack 
shall be the root reason no matter DP occurs or not. 
Based on the current observations, we discuss three possible ductil -
izing origins in TAN0 with respect to TAN1.5. Firstly, Nb interacts with 
diffusing O along the GBs and causes SAGBO. Actually in similar Ni- 
alloys 718 and 718plus, the role of Nb on SAGBO is controversial. 
Early findings suggested that the severe GB decohension during sus -
tained loading might result from the formation of Nb – O films and/or 
oxidization of Nb-carbide along GBs [ 52 – 54 ]. More recently by using 
state-of-the-art APT and TEM, the occurrence of SAGBO is instead 
related to the diffusion of Cr and Ni [ 55 ]. Here, no clear evidences of 
Nb-oxide films nor even the Nb segregation at the GBs are found. 
Although Nb-rich precipitates indeed exist near the GBs, the amount is 
too low for consideration. These suggest that Nb doesn ’ t essentially, or 
directly, contribute to SAGBO in our HEA, agreeing with the later 
viewpoint. Notice that the onset of Nb – O interaction cannot be exclu -
sively ruled out, especially for the GBs near the very sample surface 
contacting environmental atmosphere. Nevertheless, it is reasonable to 
propose that the Nb – O interaction at the GBs is not mainly responsible 
for SAGBO hence not the origin for the catastrophic ductility lose in 
TAN1.5 at intermediate temperatures. 
Secondly, no brittle phase is formed during the DP reaction. The high 
γ / γ
′
coherency endows good strain transferring across the interface, 
while the multiple slip systems in the FCC-based crystalline ensures the 
easy passage of dislocations within not only γ but also γ
′
, as supported by 
the observation in Fig. 3 a. Therefore, the DP colony can act as a micron- 
scale buffer zone for the dislocation accumulation near the GBs, and thus 
average the local stress level. This is probably true, as shown in Fig. 3 c 
where numerous dislocations are stopped and arrested at the long γ / γ
′
lamellae interfaces when heading to the GB. This buffering effect is 
similar yet not the same to the case reported by Yang et al. [ 56 ], where 
they introduced a nano-scale disordered layer at the GBs in an ordered 
alloy to facilitate dislocation penetration across GBs. 
Thirdly, the GBs in TAN0 are to some degree serrated as a result of 
the DP reaction, as more clearly described in Appendix Fig. S2 . Such 
serration phenomena has been widely studied especially in poly -
crystalline Ni-based superalloys [ 57 , 58 ], which is generally agreed to 
benefit high temperature fatigue resistance, creep life and even tensile 
ductility since they yield resistance to GB sliding and retard the cavities 
coalescence at the GBs [ 57 ]. This is certainly similar in our TAN0 HEA, 
and step-on investigations will be carried out in the near future.
