1. Introduction
Ferromagnetic NiFeGa-based Heusler alloys that undergo thermo -
elastic martensitic L2
1
(B2)-10 M/14 M-L1
0 
transformations are of great 
practical interest for the design of sensors, power drives, manipulators, 
and solid-state refrigerators because they can convert thermal, stress, 
and magnetic field energy into mechanical work and vice versa [1 – 3] . 
Today, these single crystals are well-studied and are known to have 
unique functional properties, such as the shape memory effect (SME), 
two-way SME, elastocaloric effect, rubble-like behavior, and super -
elasticity (SE) [1 – 5] .
The main problems hindering the widespread practical use of these 
multifunctional materials are the high cost, limited size, and the diffi -
culty of obtaining and processing single crystals. However, poly -
crystalline alloys cannot be used. NiFeGa-based polycrystals are brittle 
and cannot be cold-rolled to the required shape and uniform grain 
structure due to the stress-induced martensitic transformation from the 
L2
1
(B2)-phase to L1
0
-martensite below 673 K [6,7] . Thus, cold-rolling 
deformation occurs through the L2
1
(B2)-L1
0 
martensitic trans -
formation and further elastic and plastic deformation of L1
0
-martensite.
L1
0
-martensite is characterized by high anisotropy of elastic and 
strength properties. The yield strength of L1
0
-martensite depends 
strongly on the initial crystallographic orientation of the L2
1
(B2)-phase 
and ranges from 400 to 1500 MPa [7,8] . Therefore, the low-strength 
grains (for example, those oriented along < 123 >
B2
, < 011 >
B2
, and 
< 012 >
B2

0
-martensite, whereas the 
high-strength grains (for example, those oriented along < 001 >
B2 
and 
< 115 >
B2

1500 MPa. Consequently, brittle fracture is observed due to the in -
compatibility of the elastic and plastic deformation of the neighboring 
grains. Indeed, Ref. [9] presents the compression flow curves for poly -
crystalline NiFeGa alloys at room temperature: the total strain before 
fracture does not exceed 15 % – 25 %.
At temperatures above 673 K, the martensite formation stresses 
reach the yield stress of the high-temperature L2
1
(B2)-phase. The tem -
perature above which only the high-temperature phase will be deformed 
is denoted as M
d

high-temperature L2
1

compression is weakly dependent on the orientation and is 
950 – 1050 MPa at 700 – 750 K > M

at high temperatures due to the plastic deformation of the 
L2
1
(B2)-phase. Indeed, Biswas et al. have demonstrated the possibility 
of hot rolling at 1223 – 1273 K [10] .
However, detailed studies of the temperature dependence of the 
L2
1
(B2)-austenite yield strength and plastic flow curves during high- 
temperature deformation on NiFeGa alloys have not been conducted. 
Such studies are needed to select rolling regimes and predict the pa -
rameters of the microstructure formed during the hot rolling process. 
Several plastic flow curves have been obtained for NiFeGa alloys [11] at 
1123 – 1323 K and for a multicomponent NiFeMnGaCo alloy used as the 
core in a shell of ferritic carbon steel at 873 – 1273 K. The microstructural 
changes during high-temperature tests have not been studied, and the

In this type of study, the following factors are important. First, the 
secondary phase particles with γ’ - and γ -structures are precipitated at 
temperatures above 700 K in NiFeGa alloys [1,11,12] . These particles 
change the chemical composition and strength properties of the matrix. 
No studies have been performed on the precipitating processes during 
the plastic deformation of NiFeGa alloys: it is unknown whether the 
particles deform or change their shape with increasing size and volume 
fraction. Second, as shown in Refs. [13,14] , microalloying with 
0.1 – 0.6 at% boron reduces the grain size and improves ductility and 
strength. Boron strengthens grain boundaries and can influence the 
phase precipitation by the diffusion of substitution atoms. Therefore, it 
is promising to study the effect of boron microalloying on the plasticity 
of the L2

of sample fracture during the deformation process. The measurement 
errors of strain ± 0.1 %, temperature ± 1 K, and stress ± 2 MPa were 
considered during the data analysis.

irreversible strain was 7 % – 10 %. The microstructure study was con -
ducted on thin foils prepared by jet polishing on a Tenupol-5 electrolytic 
thinning machine using transmission electron microscope (TEM) 
“ Hitachi HT-7700 ” of the Krasnoyarsk regional collective user center SB

The slip system is determined by many factors: low elastic energy of 
dislocation formation, easy slip (low slip parameter or other mobility 
criteria), an interplay between the dislocation mobility and its energy, 
and ordering energy [26,27] . A variety of mechanisms for blocking the 
< 111 > slip and the transition to < 010 > have been described in the 
literature: for example, a decrease in the mobility of < 111 > super -
dislocations and pinning of paired 1/2 < 111 > superdislocations, which 
is facilitated by splitting with increasing temperature [26] . A possible 
mechanism for the occurrence of < 010 > slip observed in this work is as 
follows: in alloys with a high ordering energy and a high stacking fault 
energy, two single 1/2 < 111 > dislocations with the narrow antiphase 
boundary between them can combine into one dislocation with a double 
Burgers vector < 111 > . Such a dislocation is unstable and, as a result of 
the dislocation reaction, it transformed into three sliding < 010 > dislo -
cations, which do not produce antiphase boundaries in the B2 super -
structure [26] .
A strong decrease in the yield strength, more than tenfold, was 
discovered in NiFeGa(B) alloys with increased temperature ( Figs. 2, 9 ). 
A significant decrease in the yield strength by 2 – 5 times at elevated 
temperatures is typical of many B2 alloys [28 – 30] . Ref. [26] provides 
the temperature dependence of the critical resolved shear stress for the 
(hk0)[001] and (110)[010] slip systems in the AgMg B2-alloy. The 
stresses were reduced to less than 20 MPa with increased temperature. 
The authors of [26,31] have associated the decrease in the yield stresses 
in B2 alloys with the influence of thermally activated processes on the 
movement of the < 010 > dislocations: a combination of slipping and 
crawling of the < 010 > dislocations and the thermally activated move -
ment of the screw components of the < 010 > superdislocations along the 
Peierls relief, which is widely used to describe the deformation of bcc 
alloys. Furthermore, this is associated with the nature of the interactions 
between dislocations and atomic defects associated with deviations from 
the stoichiometric composition.
