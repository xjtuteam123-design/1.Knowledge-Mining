ABSTRACT
This study systematically investigates the effect of niobium content on the microstructural evolution and me -
chanical properties of a precipitation-hardened nickel-based superalloy. The results demonstrate that increasing 
Nb content promotes grain refinement through δ -phase precipitation at grain boundaries, while simultaneously 
reducing recrystallization degree and increasing dislocation density. Solution heat treatment leads to full 
recrystallization and grain coarsening, whereas subsequent aging treatment facilitates precipitation of γ ″ and δ 
phases. Mechanical properties reveal a clear strength-ductility trade-off with varying Nb content, accompanied 
by a transition in fracture mode from fully ductile to mixed ductile-brittle. The 3 Nb alloy exhibits the most 
balanced combination of strength and ductility, offering valuable insights for alloy design and processing 
optimization.
1. Introduction
Owing to their outstanding mechanical performance and corrosion 
resistance, nickel-based superalloys have become indispensable mate -
rials for critical applications in aerospace, nuclear engineering, and 
petrochemical sectors [1 – 4] . Alloy 625 Plus (UNS N07716) exhibits a 
distinctive combination of advantageous features from both IN 625 
(UNS N06625) and IN 718 (UNS N07718) alloys. Optimized chemical 
composition enables Alloy 625 Plus to develop the γ ″ phase precipitates 
during brief aging treatments, attaining IN 718-equivalent strength 
while preserving IN 625-grade corrosion resistance [5] . These excep -
tional characteristics render it particularly suitable for extreme service 
conditions involving elevated temperatures, high pressures, and 
aggressive corrosive environments. The growing industrial demand for 
enhanced performance has created urgent requirements for advanced 
alloys simultaneously possessing superior strength and corrosion resis -
tance [6] .
Niobium (Nb) enhances alloy mechanical performance via coordi -
nated mechanisms including solid-solution strengthening, precipitation 
hardening, and grain refinement. When dissolved in the γ matrix, Nb 
atoms induce lattice strain fields that effectively impede dislocation 
motion, thereby improving strength [7,8] . As the predominant 
strengthening phase in Alloy 625 Plus, the γ ″ phase precipitation is 
strongly promoted by Nb addition [9] . Furthermore, serving as the 
primary constituent of NbC, Laves phase, and δ phase, Nb significantly 
influences grain size control. Liu et al. [10] demonstrated that Nb 
addition effectively refined grain structure in IN 718 alloy and accel -
erated the γ ″ and δ phases precipitation during aging, yielding 
direct-aged specimens with exceptional tensile strength (1598.3 MPa) 
and microhardness (543.2 HV
0.2
). Shi et al. [11] verified that Nb refines 
solidification microstructure in single-crystal superalloys, consequently 
enhancing stress-rupture lifetime. Wise et al. [12] revealed that 
high-Nb-content nickel-based alloys exhibit superior thermal stability 
and more rapid age-hardening kinetics compared to IN 718. Liu et al. 
[13] additionally confirmed that Nb alloying improves both 
room-temperature and high-temperature (750
◦

GH3535 alloy. Remarkably, Wang et al. [14] reported a 382 % 
improvement in fracture life for Fe-Ni-based alloys through optimized 
Nb addition.
Nevertheless, comprehensive investigations addressing Nb content 
effects on microstructural evolution and mechanical behavior in Alloy 
625 Plus are still lacking. The sensitivity of γ ″ strengthening phase for -
mation to Nb content, which directly influences mechanical perfor -

2. Experimental
2.1. Material and processing
The materials investigated in this work were Alloy 625 Plus with 
varying Nb contents; the main compositions are listed in Table 1 . It 
should be noted that the standard Nb content requirement for Alloy 625 
Plus ranges from 2.75 to 4.0 wt%, with commercial alloys typically 
containing approximately 3.4 wt% Nb. To investigate the effects of Nb 
concentrations beyond this range — specifically low (2.06 wt%) and high 
(4.52 wt%) contents — on microstructure and properties, three alloys 
with varying Nb levels were prepared and designated as 2 Nb, 3 Nb, and 
4 Nb. The alloys were prepared as φ 100 mm ingots via vacuum induc -
tion melting (VIM), which were subsequently forged into rods with a 
diameter of φ 42 mm. All specimens were cut from the rods at the mid- 
radius (1/2 R ) position.
To maximize the precipitation of γ ′ / γ ′ ′ phases ， and avoid excessive 
coarsening, the recommended solution treatment followed by a two-step 
aging process was selected in accordance with standard ASTM B805. 
Solution-treated (S) samples were heated to 1038

held for 1 h, and subsequently air-cooled to room temperature. Subse -
quently, the solution-treated samples underwent an aging treatment. 
The aging treatment involved holding at 732

at 55

S denotes the solution-treated state, and SA denotes the solution-treated 
+ aged state. The tensile properties were evaluated using uniaxial tensile 
tests conducted at room temperature (25

of 32 mm (length) × 4 mm (width) × 2 mm (thickness).

During plastic deformation, the coarse initial grains are fragmented 
into refined deformation microstructures. To accommodate the imposed 
plastic strain, persistent activation of dislocation sources results in a 
substantial increase in intragranular dislocation density. Through 
organized rearrangement and annihilation, dislocations self-assemble 
into dislocation walls or cells, wherein accumulated dislocations pro -
gressively transform into low-angle grain boundaries (LAGBs, GOS: 
2 – 15
◦
). A portion of LAGBs transitions into HAGBs via absorption of 
supplementary dislocations. Regions adjacent to grain boundaries typi -
cally display more pronounced dislocation pile-ups. When the disloca -
tion density attains the critical value for recrystallization, recrystallized 
grains nucleate and grow through consumption of substantial disloca -
tion density. Nb confers significant solid-solution strengthening in su -
peralloys [16] . An increase in Nb content significantly enhances the 
deformation resistance during thermomechanical processing, conse -
quently resulting in accelerated dislocation accumulation rates [17] . 
The 2 Nb alloy, owing to its reduced deformation resistance, more 
readily attains the critical strain required for dynamic recrystallization 
(DRX) during forging. As Nb content increases, the elevated deformation 
resistance delays the attainment of the critical strain necessary for 
recrystallization initiation. Incomplete recrystallization leads to inade -
quate dissipation of stored dislocation energy. Consequently, as the Nb

During solution heat treatment, the as-forged microstructure un -
dergoes near-complete transformation to recrystallized grains via ther -

via grain boundary migration and assimilation of fine deformation 
substructures [18] , culminating in reduction of both dislocation density 
and LAGBs fraction to minimal levels. Notably, the as-forged micro -
structure of high-Nb-content alloys exhibits higher dislocation density 
and greater stored deformation energy, which supplies additional ther -
modynamic driving force for subsequent recrystallization and grain 
growth, thereby accelerating grain coarsening [19] . Therefore, there is a 
trend of abnormal growth of small forged structures in these alloys.
Fig. 4 presents the SEM microstructures of the alloys under different 
processing conditions. Twins (indicated by red arrows) are markedly 
pronounced in the heat-treated microstructures, which aligns with the 
EBSD analysis. Distinct inclusions (denoted by white circles) are 
observable in the as-forged microstructures. Fine-scale inclusions serve 
dual functions: they pin grain boundaries to provide preferential 
nucleation sites for recrystallized grains, and simultaneously hinder 
grain boundary migration to suppress grain growth [20,21] . Notably, 
copious δ phase precipitates (indicated by yellow arrows in Fig. 4 (c)) 
nucleate at grain boundaries in the as-forged 4 Nb alloy sample. During 
the forging process, the Nb content in the 4 Nb alloy exceeds the solu -
bility limit in the γ matrix. Combined with strain-induced effects, this 
promotes the nucleation of δ -phase precipitates at grain boundaries. In 
contrast, the Nb contents in the 2 Nb and 3 Nb alloys remain below the 
critical value required for δ -phase precipitation, thus preventing the 
formation of δ -phase. These intergranular δ phase precipitates exhibit 
potent pinning effects that effectively impede grain boundary migration 
and promote grain refinement [22 – 24] . This mechanism represents one 
of the critical factors governing the development of ultrafine-grained 
microstructure in the as-forged 4 Nb alloy. This is attributed to Nb ’ s 
dual roles as a vital solid-solution strengthening element and a potent δ 
phase former. During solution treatment at 1038
◦
C (which exceeds the δ 
phase solvus temperature), the elimination of the δ phase pinning effects 
leads to accelerated grain coarsening in the 4 Nb alloy. During the 
two-step aging treatment, the precipitation of δ -phase at grain bound -
aries effectively pinned these boundaries, thereby inhibiting grain 
coarsening throughout the aging process. In the 2 Nb alloy, the low Nb 
content resulted in negligible δ -phase precipitation, which was barely 
detectable. Consequently, compared to the 3 Nb and 4 Nb alloys, the 
2 Nb alloy exhibited significantly more pronounced grain coarsening, 
even though still smaller than them.

3.2. Effect of Nb content on mechanical properties
Fig. 6 displays the room temperature tensile properties of alloys 
under various processing conditions, with corresponding quantitative 
data compiled in Table 2 . Fig. 6 (a) shows the engineering stress-strain 
curves for all studied alloys. Both yield strength (YS) and ultimate ten -
sile strength (UTS) increased markedly with rising Nb content. Owing to 
pronounced grain refinement strengthening, the as-forged 4 Nb alloy 
exhibited substantially enhanced strength (YS: 858.0 MPa, UTS: 
1135.5 MPa). However, the high fraction of unrecrystallized deforma -
tion structures and elevated density of retained dislocations reduced the 
elongation (EL) to 26.7 %. Solution-treated alloys exhibited comparably 
low strength levels but demonstrated significantly enhanced ductility 
(EL: > 60 %). The aged samples revealed substantial performance vari -
ations. The 4 Nb alloy attained peak strength levels (YS: 1144.5 MPa, 
UTS: 1365.1 MPa) but exhibited the lowest elongation (EL: 19.0 %). 
Conversely, the 2 Nb alloy displayed the lowest strength (YS: 
490.8 MPa, UTS: 913.7 MPa) yet superior elongation (EL: 62.4 %). 
These pronounced property variations primarily stem from the 
strengthening contribution of the γ ″ precipitates formed during aging 
[27] . The strong coherency between fine γ ″ precipitates and the γ matrix 
imposes substantial obstacles to dislocation motion, thereby markedly 
increasing strength [28] . Nevertheless, intense precipitate-dislocation 
interactions create additional barriers that facilitate crack nucleation 
and propagation [29] . Consequently, while higher Nb contents signifi -
cantly enhance strength, the concomitant ductility reduction increases 
susceptibility to brittle fracture. Fig. 6 (b) compares the ultimate tensile 
strengths of the aged alloys in this study with literature data for 625 Plus 
[5] , IN 718 [30 – 39] , and IN 625 [15,40 – 49] alloys. Although the 
strength level of the 2 Nb alloy is comparable to that of IN 625 alloy, it 
exhibits higher elongation. The strength of the 4 Nb alloy is particularly 
remarkable, reaching levels equivalent to high-performance IN 718 
alloy, while still maintaining considerable ductility. Meanwhile, the 
3 Nb alloy exhibited comparable behavior to conventional Alloy 625 
Plus, achieving an optimal strength-ductility balance.

within dimples, consistent with the mechanism of void initiation at 
microstructural discontinuities such as inclusions, phase boundaries, or 
grain boundaries, followed by dimple growth [50] . Extensive dimple 
formation signifies superior plastic deformation capability. Fracture 
surfaces of 3-SA and 4-SA samples revealed mixed ductile-brittle failure 
modes. The 3-SA fracture surface contained limited cleavage facets and

Fig. 8 presents the EBSD analyses of regions adjacent to tensile 
fractures along the loading direction. Grains exhibited substantial 
plastic deformation, with pronounced dislocation pile-ups occurring at 
both grain boundaries and twin boundaries. This observation confirms 
that annealing twins effectively obstruct dislocation motion, contrib -
uting to strength enhancement. Compared to the pre-tensile state, the 
density of GNDs increased significantly, with notable variations 
observed across different samples. In general, finer grain sizes, a higher 
density of precipitates, and greater extent of deformation contribute to 
more pronounced dislocation accumulation. Interestingly, the 4-F sam -
ple demonstrated a distinct post-tensile microstructure. As depicted in 
Fig. 8 (c), a characteristic banded structure composed of alternating fine- 
grained and coarse-grained layers oriented along the tensile axis was 
observed. Coarse grains within the black-dashed box in Fig. 8 (c) were 
further analyzed at higher magnification in Fig. 8 (j). Six distinct loca -
tions within these grains were selected for local crystallographic orien -
tation analysis. Points 1, 4, and 6 exhibited small orientation deviations, 
confirming they belong to the same grain. Conversely, points 2, 3, and 5 
displayed significant orientation differences. These misorientations 
result from stress-induced intragranular lattice rotation, which facili -
tates strain accommodation by optimizing crystallographic orientation 
for slip deformation. Such plastic deformation necessitates coordinated 
strain accommodation across neighboring grains to maintain interfacial 
cohesion and structural continuity [51] . The high-density deformation 
substructure present in the 4-F sample provided substantial strain en -
ergy to drive intragranular rotation [52] . Grains with compatible ori -
entations coalesced to form extended banded structures aligned with the 
tensile axis. Meanwhile, fine grains exhibiting significant orientation 
mismatches failed to accommodate strain via lattice rotation, forming 
localized bands separating coarse-grained regions.
Fig. 9 presents a comparison of {111} pole figures along the loading 
direction near the fracture surface before and after tensile deformation. 
The low maximum texture intensity in the pre-tensile state indicates the 
absence of a strong preferred orientation. In contrast, the high maximum 
intensity in the post-tensile state, with its peak located at the intersec -
tion of the RD and the circumference, demonstrates the development of 
a relatively strong {111}//RD fiber texture. This observation indicates 
that the < 111 > directions of most grains rotated and became parallel 
to the RD, as well as the tensile direction, during deformation. From the 
slip characteristics of face-centered cubic (FCC) crystals, the 
< 111 > orientation is known to be a hard orientation where slip system 
activation is difficult. Consequently, during tensile deformation, the 
orientations of multiple grains tend to cluster near the 
< 111 > direction, resulting in the formation of this pronounced 
texture.
4. Conclusions
This study systematically investigated the influence of Nb content on 
microstructural evolution and mechanical properties of Alloy 625 Plus. 
The main findings are summarized as follows:
(1) Increasing Nb content significantly refined the grain size in as- 
forged samples. The geometrically necessary dislocation density 
( ρ
GNDs
) increased from 1.32 × 10 ¹ ⁴ m ⁻ ² to 4.66 × 10 ¹ ⁴ m ⁻ ², while the 
recrystallized fraction decreased from 84.7 % to 5.4 %. Post-heat 
treatment substantially reduced ρ
GNDs
, promoted near-complete 
recrystallization, and increased the twin boundary density exceeding 
60 %.
(2) Ellipsoidal γ ″ precipitates (~20 nm) were discernibly dispersed 
within the γ matrix of 3 Nb and 4 Nb alloys, whereas no γ ″ phase was 
observed in the 2 Nb alloy.
(3) Both yield strength and ultimate tensile strength increased pro -
gressively with Nb content. The precipitation strengthening through γ ″ / γ 
coherency and dislocation interactions significantly enhanced strength 
while retaining acceptable elongation.
(4) Fracture mode transitioned from ductile-dominant to mixed 
ductile-brittle characteristics with increasing Nb content.
