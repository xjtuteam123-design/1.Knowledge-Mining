ABSTRACT
Achieving high strength-ductility synergy has been a significant challenge in Mg alloys. In this study, we have 
found that adding traces of Sn can effectively improve ductility and maintain high strength in Mg alloys. The 
results indicate that the peripheral area of Mg-0.4Al-0.3Ca-0.2Mn-0.1Sn alloy exhibits a yield strength (YS) of 
344 MPa, an ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of 382 MPa, and a high fracture elongation (FE) of 14.6 % after 
rotary swaging (RS) process. High strength is caused by the combined effects of grain refinement, abundant 
dislocations, and nano-scaled precipitated phases, with grain refinement being the most dominant factor. 
Average grain sizes are estimated to be 1.82 μ m in the central area and 1.78 μ m in the peripheral area, 
respectively. The introduction of trace amounts of Sn facilitates the formation of CaMgSn precipitates with 
nanometer size, which can enhance strength by pinning dislocation. The superior ductility is further attributed to 
the suppression of basal < a > slips, and the activation of prismatic < a > and pyramidal < c + a > slips. 
Furthermore, adding traces of Sn notably reduces the maximum intensity of the {0001} texture from 35.65 mrd 
to 15.42 mrd, thereby improving the ductility of the alloy. These findings offer novel insights into the devel -
opment of high-performance, low-weight, and low-cost Mg alloys for industrial applications.
1. Introduction
Mg alloys are characterized by low density, high specific stiffness and 
high specific strength, which have enabled their widespread application 
in aerospace, automobile and communication [1 – 6] . Despite the 
recognized importance of Mg alloys, their industrial applications remain 
restricted to some extent. One of the primary reasons is the inherent 
difficulty in achieving a high strength-ductility synergy in conventional 
Mg alloys [7 – 11] .
To address this challenge, researchers have explored alloying with 
rare earth (RE) elements including cerium (Ce), yttrium (Y), dysprosium 
(Dy), and gadolinium (Gd) [12,13] . For instance, the WE54 
(Mg-5.3Y-3.5RE-0.5Zr) alloy exhibited an ultimate tensile strength 
(UTS) of ~ 390 MPa and an elongation of ~ 10.5 % after 
room-temperature rotary swaging (RS) [14] . However, while RE addi -
tions can enhance strength, they often compromise ductility [15] . 
Moreover, the scarcity and high cost of RE elements pose significant 
barriers to their large-scale industrial adoption. To reduce material 
costs, intensive research has shifted toward RE-free Mg alloys over the 
past decades, including Mg-Ca, Mg-Al and Mg-Mn alloy systems. 
Recently, Yu et al. [16] prepared the Mg-Zn-Sn and Mg-Zn-Sn-0.2Ca 
scaffolds via infiltration casting using 3D-printed Ti templates and 
achieved the accurate control of the pore structure. The results indicated 
that the Ca changed the secondary phases from the Mg

Mg-3Al alloys enhanced stretch formability by activating prismatic < a >

Furthermore, activation of pyramidal < c + a > slips facilitate the for -

RE-free alloys exhibit comprehensive mechanical properties comparable 
to those of RE-containing alloys while offering cost advantages. Notably, 
recent studies on RE-free Mg alloys have yielded promising results. For 
example, a novel Mg-4.1Al-0.6Mn-0.5Zn-0.4Ca alloy processed by RS

Mg-3.8Al-1.1Sn-0.4Ca alloy processed by differential-thermal asym -
metric extrusion (DAE) displayed a YS of ~ 290 MPa and a FE of 18.2 % 
[25] , wherein uniform fine grains facilitated the strength-ductility 
balance.
In addition to alloying design, plastic deformation techniques are 
critical for optimizing mechanical properties in low-alloyed Mg alloys. 
Severe plastic deformation (SPD) methods such as high-pressure torsion 
(HPT), multi-directional forging (MDF), and accumulative roll bonding 
(ARB) have been employed to refine grains [13,26 – 28] . However, these 
techniques suffer from high costs, low efficiency, and restricted sample 
sizes to some extent. In contrast, RS is a cost-effective and scalable in -
dustrial process capable of achieving strength-ductility synergy through 
ultrafine grain formation even at moderate strain levels [29,30] . For 
example, a RSed Mg-Mn-based alloy exhibited an UTS of 400 MPa and a 
FE of 10 % [31] , with strengthening mechanisms elucidated by EBSD 
and TEM as fine grains, dislocations, and nano-scaled Al-Mn pre -
cipitates. Similarly, an Mg-6Li-3Al-0.4Ce alloy processed by RS achieved 
an UTS of 411 MPa and a FE of 11 %, with strengthening contributions 
from dislocations, stacking faults, and twins [32] . An ultra-light 
Mg-4Li-3Al-2Zn alloy were prepared by the low-strain swaging defor -
mation. Numerous twins and stacking faults in the coarse grains 
significantly improved the strength of alloy [33] .
In this study, we systematically investigate the effects of trace Sn 
additions (x = 0, 0.1 wt%) on the strength-ductility synergy of Mg-0.4Al- 
0.3Ca-0.2Mn-xSn alloys processed via hot extrusion followed by room- 
temperature RS. The base alloy (Mg-0.4Al-0.3Ca-0.2Mn) is denoted as 
AXM000, and the Sn-containing alloy (Mg-0.4Al-0.3Ca-0.2Mn-0.1Sn) as 
AXMT0000. Our findings provide novel insights into the development of 
lightweight, cost-effective Mg alloys with optimized mechanical prop -
erties for industrial applications.

3.2. Mechanical properties
Tensile tests were employed at room temperature to evaluate me -
chanical properties of the peripheral areas and central areas of the 
AXM000-xSn (x = 0, 0.1 wt%) alloys after the RS process. The engi -
neering stress-strain curves are shown in Fig. 8 , and the corresponding 
tensile properties are summarized in Table 2 . Intriguingly, the central 
area of the AXM000 alloy shows an extremely low FE of only 3.6 %, 
while the peripheral area exhibits relatively a high YS of 317 MPa. For 
both alloys, the YS and FE are higher in the peripheral area. The UTS for 
two kinds of alloys is approximately equal, but the FE of the AXM000 
alloy is notably lower (10.0 %). The peripheral area of the AXMT0000 
alloy shows significantly improved mechanical properties, with a higher 
YS (344 MPa), UTS (382 MPa), and FE (14.6 %).
Fig. 9 shows fractographs of the alloys after tensile tests. Cracks and 
tear ridges, pointed by red arrows and yellow circles in Fig. 9 (a) and (b), 
suggest that brittle fracture occurred in the AXM000 alloy. In contrast, 
the AXMT0000 alloy exhibits cleavage planes and few dimples in the 
central area, while much dimples are found in the peripheral area, as 
shown in Fig. 9 (c) and (d). Furthermore, numerous spherical particles 
are also found at the bottom of dimples, highlighted by yellow arrows. 
Therefore, these results confirm that ductility is significantly better in 
peripheral areas of the AXMT0000 alloy. As shown in Fig. 10 , the pe -
ripheral area of the AXMT0000 alloy exhibits superior strength-ductility 
synergy compared with other Mg alloys reported in the literature.
4. Discussion
4.1. Microstructural evolution
According to the statistical results in Fig. 4 , the average grain sizes of 
the AXM000-xSn (x = 0, 0.1 wt%) alloys after RS at room temperature 
are consistently around 2 μ m. The average size of grains in the periph -
eral area is notably smaller than that of the central area. This is because 
the applied strains during RS are distributed in a gradient from pe -
ripheral area to the central area along the radial direction. As a result, 
the higher effective strains in peripheral area result in the grains more 
fully refined [52] . Additionally, the addition of Ca to Mg-Al alloy can 
also lead to finer grains [7] .

reason for this difference is that the DRX was more suppressed during 
the hot extruding process due to the addition of traces of Sn, leading to a 
lower DXRed grains fraction. Additionally, we observe in Fig. 12 that 
paralleled dislocations with high density formed during RS (pointed by 
yellow arrows). These dislocations got entangled, forming LAGBs 
composed of dislocation arrays, which facilitate the transformation sub- 
structured grains into refined grains. These LAGBs can be regarded as 
sites for dislocation nucleation and emit new dislocations [53] . In the 
peripheral area, the proportion of LAGBs is much higher (frequency of 
28.51 %), as shown in Fig. 11 . With these sites, inner strain can be 
relaxed, thus enhanced the dislocation-multiplication capability [15] . 
Further plastic deformation induced the addition of storage of disloca -
tion in LAGBs in this way, thus the size of grain is smaller [42,54,55] . 
Similarly, the addition of Ca also promoted accumulation of the pyra -
midal < c + a > dislocations and these dislocations transformed into 
LAGBs [7] . These grain boundaries retarded the movement of disloca -
tions and caused dislocation accumulation. Dislocations migrated from 
the high density to low density. Therefore, more coarse grains were 
broken into smaller ones and finally achieved grain refinement.
Mg alloys with the hexagonal close-packed structure exhibit poor 
ductility due to the limited number of slip systems at room temperature 
[8] . In this current study, average SF for basal < a > slip is significantly 
below that of the prismatic < a > and pyramidal < c + a > slips, as shown 
in Fig. 6 (c) and (d). This important observation implies that the basal 
< a > slip was suppressed to some extent, while the prismatic < a > and 
pyramidal < c + a > slips was easily activated in the AXMT0000 alloy. 
The more activated non-basal < a > slip, the better deformability for 
alloy. Our important findings reveal that the considerable activated 
non-basal slips provide sufficient slip systems for deformation, thereby 
changing deformation behavior of the alloy with the addition of Sn.

also made texture intensity weaken through providing the nucleation 
site for the DRXed grains, while also improving ductility in the 
AXMT0000 alloy [34,59] .

central area in Fig. 4 (a) and (c). The high-density dislocations accu -
mulate boundary between the unDRXed grains and finer grains, which 
cause the strain incompatibility. The load generated stress at the coarse 
unDRXed grains, resulting in the local stress concentration at the in -
terfaces between coarse and fine grains [60] . The low volume fraction of 
fine grains cannot accommodate the high-density dislocations trans -
ferred from the coarse grains, leading to premature crack initiation and 
rapid propagation, finally resulting in premature fail and deterioration 
of material mechanical properties.
In addition, numerous nano-scaled CaMgSn phases can effectively 
enhance ductility, due to the enhancement of the strain hardening 
exponent (SHE) [61] . Specifically, a better strain hardening exponent 
correlates with improved ductility [62] . The swaged AXMT0000 alloy 
with a SHE of about 2.03 exhibits a higher ductility in the case of the 
swaged AXM000 alloy with a SHE of about 1.34, which is consistent 
with the results of tensile tests. In this study, the AXMT0000 alloy ex -
hibits better ductility compared with AXM000 alloy under the same 
deformed conditions. High ductility is attributable to that the addition of 
Sn, which decreased basal texture intensity, and facilitated to activate 
non-basal < a > and pyramidal < c + a > slips. During the process of DRX, 
many dislocations annihilated, and there are almost no dislocations 
within the DRXed grains, whereas there are lots of dislocations within 
the unDRXed grains. In previous literature, appropriate alloying is 
beneficial to enhance the fraction of < c + a > dislocation cross-slip [63] . 
The Sn element can decrease the energy barriers for dislocation in 
prismatic and basal slips to enhance ductility in Mg-Al alloys [8,20] . 
However, the effects of varying Sn content on the mechanical perfor -
mances for low-alloyed Mg alloy remain unclear in this study, thereby it 
needs to determine the optimal Sn content and further explore the un -
derlying mechanisms of strength-ductility synergy in the future 
research.
