ABSTRACT 
Advanced intermetallic TiAl alloys have attracted considerable attention as a high-temperature structural ma -
terial in aerospace and automobile sectors owing to their low density, elevated temperature strength, superior 
creep, and oxidation resistance. The present study is focused on assessing the effect of alloy composition, heat 
treatment and microstructural variation on the bulk and small-scale deformation behavior of commercial grade 
2nd generation and newly developed 3rd generation TiAl alloys at 25 
◦
C (298 K), 300 
◦
C (573 K), and 500 
◦
C 
(773 K). Twinning is noted to be the dominant deformation mechanism irrespective of the temperature. The 
highest yield strength and hardness are noted for the as-cast 3rd generation TiAl alloy owing to the finer 
microstructure and compositional variation. Heat treatment however modifies the TiAl microstructure signifi -
cantly resulting to unique bilamellar-biglobular (BLBG) microstructure, devoid of the brittle β
0 
phase. Conse -
quently, nano twins- and micro twins-induced enhanced deformability is noted for such microstructure, as 
compared to the as-cast counterparts. Interestingly, an anomalous increase in strength with an increase in 
temperature is observed for the studied alloys owing to the presence of high-density twins and Kear-Wilsdorf 
locks. Most importantly, a temperature dependant hardness anomaly is noted in the TiAl alloys that opens 
new avenues for further research in nanoscale deformation. This piece of research potentially strengthens the 
understanding of the role of constituent phases and temperature in the deformation behavior of complex TiAl 
alloys. Twin-induced deformation mechanism noted for the novel BLBG microstructure, can also be valid for 
different generations of TiAl alloys to achieve enhanced deformability that paves the way for the development of 
next-generation material. 
1. Introduction 
Researchers worldwide are rigorously working towards achieving a 
common goal: energy conservation and environmental protection. In the 
case of aviation and automobile industries, this can only be made 
feasible by manufacturing lightweight engines and machine parts that 
can drastically decrease fuel consumption, thereby making the parts eco- 
friendly and economical. TiAl intermetallic alloys have attracted 
considerable attention in this regard owing to their low density, excel -
lent specific strength, superior oxidation resistance, and high- 
temperature mechanical properties, including fatigue and creep resis -
tance [ 1 , 2 ]. As a result, TiAl-based alloys are widely acknowledged as 
one of the most promising candidates to replace conventionally used Ni- 
based superalloys [ 3 ]. Over the past few decades, different generations 
of TiAl alloys have been developed, and in fact, a 2nd generation Ti- 
48Al-2Nb-2Cr alloy has been successfully used in the General electric 
aero-engines (GEnx-1B) of Boeing 787 Dreamliner [ 4 ]. However, 
inherent brittleness along with microstructural instability limit the 
capability and widespread usage of 2nd generation TiAl alloys. With the 
development of next-generation engines, increasing requirements have

studies have confirmed that the macroscopic properties, especially the 
yield strength of TiAl alloys, are strongly temperature-dependent 
[ 21 , 22 ]. In fact, such alloys tend to show an anomalous increase in 
strength with an increase in temperature. Such a direct relationship 
between yield strength and temperature is popularly known as yield

explored thoroughly for the TiAl systems. To generate further under -

It is also apparent from the literature that there appears to be a 
dearth of research on the behavior of these advanced TiAl intermetallic 
alloys at elevated temperatures using nanomechanical characterization. 
Both bulk scale compression and small-scale indentation exhibit simil -
itude in terms of assessing the mechanical behavior of the material 
under the application of compressive load. However, nanoindentation 
possesses the advantages of rapid acquisition and precise evaluation of 
the hardness. Most importantly, during indentation, multi-axial stress 
state exists beneath the indenter, whereas uniaxial stress state prevails 
during compression [ 25 ]. Thus, the indentation technique offers the 
scope to closely simulate the scenario during the service conditions 
where the components mostly experience stress multiaxiality. Consid -
ering this, nanoindentation has been widely acknowledged in the 
characterization of nanoscale mechanical properties of metallic systems 
[ 26 ]. Such an experimental technique would be even more appropriate 
when carried out at elevated temperatures, thereby replicating the ser -
vice temperature for the materials. It is well established that activation 
of dislocation source and various dislocation activities like slip, cross- 
slip, and twinning are significantly temperature dependent. As a mat -
ter of fact, the onset of plasticity beneath the indenter and its physical 
origin is noted to be highly temperature sensitive. In this regard, it is 
noteworthy that high-temperature instrumented indentation has been 
successfully used for various materials systems like Al
–
Cu [ 27 ], Mg 
[ 28 ], Zr [ 29 ], Al [ 30 ], and Ni-based superalloys [ 31 ]. In contrast, the use 
of elevated temperature indentation for the TiAl system is very limited 
and almost negligible, as per the knowledge of the authors. To gain a 
detailed insight about the role of microstructures (as-cast versus BLBG) 
in affecting the localized deformation behavior of 2nd and 3rd genera -
tion TiAl alloys at both room and intermediate temperature, nano -
indentations are performed on the studied alloys. Furthermore, the 
activity of YSA, best represented as hardness anomaly (HA) at the small- 
scale is also verified. Most importantly, this study also gives us a scope to 
directly compare the influence of loading scale and state on the room to 
intermediate temperature properties of a prior and recent generation of 
TiAl alloys. 
To address the dual objective, in the present work, we report a sys -
tematic analysis of the evolution in microstructure, constituent phases, 
deformation mechanism, etc., from room temperature, i.e., ~25

assessed. Anomalous strength, as well as hardness dependence with 
operational temperature, is explored. The detailed temperature depen -
dant deformation mechanisms for different generations of TiAl alloys, 
assessed through this study, can strengthen the framework for compre -
hending the anomalous temperature dependence of mechanical perfor -
mance and provide increased applicability for the alloy system.

2.4. Microstructural characterization 
For microstructural investigation, specimens of dimension 10 mm ×
10 cm × 5 mm were cut from both as-cast and heat-treated TiAl-2Nb and 
TiAl-7.5Nb alloys. Additionally, the deformed specimens were sectioned 
parallel to the compression axis for microstructural analysis. The con -
ventional metallographic polishing technique was used to prepare all 
the specimens in the pre- and post-deformed condition. Further, etching 
was done using Kroll ’ s reagent. A field emission scanning electron mi -
croscope ( FESEM Zeiss Merlin ) was used for microstructural analysis. 
Electron back scattered diffraction ( EBSD ) analysis was further per -
formed to confirm the phases present in the alloys with the help of 
FESEM ( Carl Zeiss, Model: model: Auriga Compact Crossbeam FIB-FEG ). 
For this purpose, following the standard metallographic polishing, 
electropolishing was done using an electrolyte having methanol, 
butoxyethanol, and perchloric acid in a ratio of 10:6:1. Post processing 
of EBSD data analysis was done using TSL-OIM software. Specimens for 
atom probe tomography ( APT ) characterization were prepared using a 
focused ion beam ( FIB ) in a HELIOS Dual Beam SEM . APT was carried out 
with the help of 3D atom probe ( 3DAP ) CAMECA LEAP 5000 XR 
equipment, operated at 60 K with a pulse frequency of 200 kHz and laser 
energy of 30 pJ. IVAS (Version 3.8.2) application software from 
CAMECA was used for the reconstruction and analysis of the 3DAP data. 
Further, the deformation behavior following compression was 
analyzed at a finer scale with the help of a transmission electron mi -
croscope ( TEM FEI TECNAI G2 20S-TWIN ). For TEM , specimens were 
first mechanically thinned down to ~50 μ m. Following this, 3 mm discs 
were punched out of the respective specimens, which were further 
subjected to ion-milling. Notably, all the microstructural examinations 
of the deformed specimens through SEM and TEM were performed at the 
mid-thickness region to avoid the friction-affected zone. An optical 
microscope (Carl Zeiss Axio Vert.A1) was also used to capture the indent 
impression following the indentation at 25 
◦

representative TEM-BF image of TiAl-7.5Nb-AC alloy deformed at

C (773 K) is shown in Fig. 9 (a). The corresponding SAED pattern is 
also presented in the inset of Fig. 9 (b). The prominent cross-slip 
(marked with an arrow) of dislocations can be seen from the 
corresponding TEM image. It is noteworthy that the dissociation of 
[10 1] superdislocation leads to the formation of anti-phase boundary 
(APB) in TiAl system [ 55 ]. The screw dislocations are more likely to 
cross-slip from octahedral {111} slip planes to cubic {100} planes owing 
to a drop in APB energy [ 56 ]. Subsequently, the cross-slipped disloca -
tions possess the ability to drive back onto parallel {111} octahedral 
planes. Conversely, the APB gets immobilized on the {100} planes [ 57 ].

• Heat treated counterparts of the TiAl-2Nb and TiAl-7.5Nb alloys with 
bilamellar-biglobular (BLBG) microstructure are noted to have lower 
compressive yield and peak strengths. As per the inverse relationship 
between strength and ductility, better strain to failure values is noted 
for the TiAl-2Nb-HT and TiAl-7.5Nb-HT alloys as compared to their 
as-cast counterparts. Increased ability to twin, promoted by micro -
structural modification is found to be prime reason behind such 
observation. 
• Hardness variation follows the similar trend as that of the 
compressive behavior of the TiAl alloys. Interestingly, an anomaly in 
hardness with increase in temperature owing to the presence of Kear- 
Wilsdorf locks is noted for TiAl-2Nb-AC, TiAl-2Nb-HT and TiAl- 
7.5Nb-HT alloys. However, a random fluctuation in hardness is 
observed for TiAl-7.5Nb-AC alloy system, possibly owing to the 
complex interplay between applied stress triaxiality along with 
microstructural evolution and compositional variation for the alloy.
