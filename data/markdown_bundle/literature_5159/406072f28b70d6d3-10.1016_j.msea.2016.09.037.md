Abstract: 
Equal channel angular pressing(ECAP) as a common severe plastic deformation 
(SPD) procedure has been extensively explored in Mg alloys to improve their 
mechanical properties. But due to the hot processing temperature and texture 
softening, the strength of ECAP processed Mg alloys was found to be relatively low. 
In this work, high strength and good ductility were achieved in ZK60 magnesium 
alloy by a combination of grain refinem ent strengthening and precipitate hardening 
through the use of heat treatment and equal channel angular pressing (ECAP) . Due to 
the pre-solution heat treatment , fine metastable phase particles (MgZn2) precipitated 
during the severe plastic deformation, which improved the work hardening of the 
alloy and increased the strength and ductility simultaneously . When the ECAP 
processing temperature was reduced gradually, the grains were further refined to ~500 
nm and more MgZn 2 particles precipitated hierarchically. Therefore, there was a 
notable increase in the yield strength and ultimate tensile strength, while the ductility 
was almost retained. 
Keywords: Mg alloy; ECAP; solution heat treatment; precipitate; strength 
 
1. Introduction 
Magnesium alloys have attracted increasing attention in automotive, aerospace

## Page 3

2 
 
and electronics industries due to their low density and high specific strength [1, 2]. 
However, the wide application of Mg a lloys has been substantially restricted due to 
their relatively low strength and poor ductility. Their hexagonal close -packed (hcp) 
crystal structure only provides limited number of slip systems at room temperature, 
which consequently lead to the poor workability and formability. Therefore, 
magnesium alloys are usually mechanically processed at temperatures higher than 
200˚C[3, 4], but this high temperature directly lead to grain growth and thus low 
strength. 
Severe plastic deformation (SPD) is a recognized method that has excellent grain 
refining capability in bulk metals , capable of improving both strength and ductility. 
Equal channel angular pressing (ECAP) [5, 6] as a common SPD procedure has been 
extensively explored in Mg alloys to improve the mechanical properties [7-10]. The 
room temperature ductility of AZ31 [11, 12] and AZ61[13] Mg alloy improved up to 
~50% by ECAP, which was more than twice that of the conventionally extruded 
magnesium alloys. In order to improve the formability and avoid cracking, Mg alloys 
are generally processed by ECAP at relatively high temperatures , usually higher than 
300˚C which can hinder the strength of the alloy . And the specific texture with a high 
Schmidt factor could also lead to some strength losses [14-16]. The reported strengths 
of some Mg alloys processed by ECAP [13, 17] were not increased effectively. The 
relatively big grain size s and low dislocation density caused by the high processing 
temperature have been found to be responsible for the relatively low strength . Some 
researchers have tried to either reduce the processing temperature of ECAP[18], or 
combine extrusion and ECAP [19, 20] with the aim of producing finer grains and 
increasing the strength of the alloys . But as temperature decreases, the formability of 
the alloys becomes poor which makes it necessary for careful control of the process. 
For precipitate hardening magnesium alloys, in addition to the grain refinement 
strengthening, precipitation hardening is another approach to strengthen the alloys. 
Temperature has also been an important factor that affects the precipitation behavior 
of these alloys. By combining heat treatment with mechanical processing, fine grained

structure containing fine homogenously dispersed metastable phase or second phase 
precipitates can be fabricated. In addition to the grain refinement strengthening, the 
fine dispersed particles supplies extra precipitate strengthening effect which is capable 
of improving the work hardening rate of the alloy thus becoming an advantage for 
both the strength and ductility [21]. Such thermomechanical procedure had been 
adopted in some aluminum alloys. By pre -solution heat treatment , ECAP and low 
temperature aging procedure, t he yield strength of 2024 Al alloy was imp roved up to 
~630 MPa and the ductility was higher than that of the alloy processed by ECAP 
only[22]. By similar thermal treatment and cryo -rolling, both the strength and 
ductility of 7075 Al [21] and 2024 Al[23] alloys were simultaneously improved. 
However, t here are still few studies on the precipitate hardening effect in the 
severely deformed magnesium alloy s. Kim et al. achieved high strength and good 
ductility in an AZ91 (Mg-9Al-1Zn) magnesium alloy by high -ratio differential speed 
rolling (HRDSR) and low -temperature aging [24]. Compared with the sample 
processed by HRDSR alone, the yield strength and ultimate tensile strength were 
increased by 13.3% and 13.9%, respectively. We combined ECAP with pre -solution 
and post aging treatment in AZ91 alloy, and it also rendered higher strengths without 
ductility loss by the hierarchical precipitates [25]. The good strengthening effect was 
partly due to the high aluminum component (~9%) , and thus high volume fraction of 
hard precipitates. In addition to Mg -Al alloy, Mg-Zn alloys are another common 
series that have precipitate hardening effect. At present work, we combined 
pre-solution heat treatment with low temperature ECAP in order to achieve high 
strength and good ductility in ZK60 Mg alloy . The effect s of the heat treatment and 
deformation temperature on the microstructure and precipitate behavior w ere also 
discussed. 
2. Experimental procedure 
The as-cast ZK60 Mg alloy with a composition of Mg –6.08Zn–0.56Zr (wt. %) 
was used in this study. In order to fit the die used in the ECAP process, the ingot

## Page 5

4 
 
samples were first cut into billets with dimension of 20 mm  20 mm  45mm. Before 
ECAP, the samples were pre-solution heat treated at 430˚C for 16h and quenched to 
room temperature in water. Then the pre-solution heat treated samples were processed 
by ECAP for 8 and 12 passes . Rotational die ECAP setup [26, 27] was used for the 
ECAP process, which had two equal square channels intersecting each other at 90˚. 
Before ECAP processing, the die was first heated to 300 ˚C, after which the sample 
was inserted into the die and annealed at 300 ˚C for 15 min. During ECAP processing, 
the temperature was kept controlled at ~300˚C. The above procedure will be 
designated as SHT+ECAP hereafter. The pre-solution heat treated sample s were also 
processed by ECAP at lower temperature. The sample was firstly processed by ECAP 
for 4 passes at 250 ˚C . Since the magnesium alloy would have better ductility and 
formability after ECAP, the processing temperature was decreased to 200 ˚C for the 
next 2 passes of ECAP. At last, continual reduc e of the temperature to 150 ˚C and 
ECAP process the sample for another 2 passes . The total passes of thi s low 
temperature was 8, after which it was difficult to continue the ECAP process for more 
passes at lower temperature without causing fractures in the sample . In order to 
reduce the inner strain, after the 8 passes of low temperature ECAP, the sample was 
annealed at 100˚C for 1h. Such procedure will be designated as LT ECAP hereafter. 
For comparison, the ingot ZK60 samples were also directly processed by ECAP for 8 
and 12 passes at 300˚C. 
After ECAP processing, tensile tests were conducted for all the samples at room 
temperature with a strain rate of 5×10-4 s-1. Dog-bone shaped specimens were used for 
tensile testing, which were cut parallel to the longitudinal axis and the flow plane of 
the ECAP processed billet [28]. The dimension of the tensile specimen was 6 mm in 
gauge length, 1.0mm in thickness and 2mm in width. Microstructures and precipitate 
morphologies were examined by the optical microscopy (OM) and transmission 
electron microscopy (TEM). Before OM observati on, the samples were polished and 
etched with an acetic-picral solution (3g picric, 20 ml acetic acid, 20 ml water, and 50 
ml ethanol). The TEM samples were prepared by jet polishing with a solution of 2%

perchloric ethanol at -20ºC. TEM observations were carried out with a Tecnai G2 
transmission electron microscope operated at 200KV . X -ray Diffraction (XRD) 
analyses were used to investigate the precipitate behavior and phases existing in the 
samples at different processed states. 
3. Results and discussion 
3.1 Mechanical properties 
Fig.1 (a) shows the typical tensile true stress-strain curves of the ZK60 alloy 
processed by ECAP and different heat-treating schemes. The comparisons of the yield 
strength, ultimate tensile strength and elongation to failure are demonstrated in Fig. 
1(b). It can be seen that the ingot sample processed by ECAP directly has the lowest 
strength. After 8 passes of ECAP, the yield strength and ultimate strength were 
193MPa and 265MPa respectively, and the elongation to failure was 19%. By 
pre-solution heat treatment and ECAP, the samples had a higher strain hardening rate, 
which brought advantages to both the strength and ductility . Although t he yield 
strength (184MPa) of SHT+ECAP 8 passes samples were similar to that of the ECAP 
processed samples without pre -solution heat treatment , the ultimate strength and 
ductility were much higher. The ultimate tensile strength of the SHT+ECAP 8 passes 
sample was increased to 342MPa and the ductility was up to 19.9%. Meanwhile, the 
ultimate tensile strength and ductility were also increased with increasing ECAP 
passes. More passes of ECAP yielded higher strengths and ductility. Comparing with 
the SHT+ECAP 8 passes sample, the SHT+ECAP 12 passes sample had higher 
ultimate strength (353MPa), and better ductility, 25 .2%. There were similar 
phenomena in the ingot samples. 
When the processing temperature was reduced to 250˚C and the alloy processed 
by ECAP for 4 passes after solution heat treatment , the yield strength (189MPa) was 
still similar to other samples processed at 300˚C, but due to the pre -solution heat 
treatment, the ultimate strength (311Mpa) was higher. The LT ECAP 4 passes sample 
still had a lower ultimate tensile strength and ductility than that of SHT+ECAP

samples, which may be caused by the less passes of ECAP . When the passes of LT 
ECAP increased to 8, both the stren gth and ductility were improved evidently. It got 
the highest yield strength (2 60MPa) and ultimate tensile strength (371MPa) amongst 
all the processed samples. The yield strength and ultimate tensile strength w ere 
improved by ~37% and ~ 40% respectively, as compared to the ECAP 8 passes 
sample. And most importantly, there was almost no loss in the ductility compared to 
that of the ECAP 8 passes sample and SHT+ECAP 8 passes sample, 18.5%, although 
it was lower than that of samples processed by ECAP for 12 passes.

3.2 Microstructure 
Fig. 2 shows opti cal micrographs of the ori ginal as -cast, the directly ECAP 
processed, the solution heat treated, and the SHT+ECAP alloys. The as -cast ZK60 
alloy had a typical dendritic structure which was composed of α magnesium matrix, 
the eutectic phase and second phase particles. The average grain size of the original 
as-cast ZK60 alloy was ~150μm. After solution heat treatment at 430 ˚C for 16 h, 
most of the eutectic and second phase particles were dissolved in the matrix, and the 
grain size grew to ~200μm. When the as -cast ZK60 alloys were processed by ECAP 
for 8 and 12 passes directly, the grains were refined obviously, as show in Fig. 2 (b) 
and (c), but the grains were still not homogenous. Both the fine grains with average 
size of 2 μm and coarse grains with average size of ~10μm coexisted in the 
SHT+ECAP 12 passes sample . The eutectic and second phase particles were broken 
up and spread along the grain boundaries. When the alloys were pre -solution heat 
treated and then processed by ECAP, the grains were refined gradually. And with 
increasing the passes to 12, the grains became finer and more homogenous than that 
of the ECAPed alone alloys . The averag e grain size of the SHT+ECAP 12 passes 
alloy was ~3μm. Some fine second phase particles could also be seen inside the grains 
and along the grain boundaries, which precipitated during the hot ECAP processing.

Although the pre-solution heat treatment improved the structure homogeneity of 
the ECAP processed alloy, the grain size s were still not very fine. The processing 
temperature is important for magnesium alloys since it influences the deformed 
microstructure and precipitate behavior , so low temperature ECAP was conducted in 
this present work. In order to improve the formability of the alloy at low temperature, 
the processing temperature was reduced gradually. Fig. 3 shows the optical 
micrographs of ZK60 alloys after different passes of low temperature ECAP. After 
the first 4 passes of ECAP , it presented a bimodal structure elongated along the shear 
direction with both coarse and fine grains. The strain accumulated heterogeneously in 
grains with different orientation which led to the inhomogeneous grain refinement. 
The average size of the fine grains was ~5μm. With the number of ECAP passes 
increased to 6, the coarse grains were refined and the average grain size was 
continuously refined to ~2μm in diameter. Only a few of the coarse grains were left 
with size of ~20μm. After further reduction in the processing temperature and increase 
in the ECAP passes to 8, a homogeneous microstructure was obtained with super-fine 
gains inside, < 1μm. Comparing with the microstructure of the samples processed at 
300˚C for 12 passes , the 8 passes of low temperature ECAP sample rendered a much 
finer microstructure.

3.3 Precipitate behavior 
Because of the pre-solution heat treatment, precipitation occurred in the solut ion 
treated ZK60 alloys simultaneously with the severe deformation. Although there are 
different types of Mg -Zn(Zr) precipitates existing in the Mg-Zn alloys[29, 30], when 
aged isothermally at 120 -260˚C, the transition phase β1′ and β2′ are considered as the 
main strengthening precipitates phase in Mg -Zn alloys [30-32]. Both β1′ and β2′ 
metastable phase have a hexagonal structure as MgZn2[32, 33]. The transition phase 
β1′ forms as thin long rod with its long axis parallel to the c axis [0001] of the α-Mg 
matrix, and the transition phase β2′ are plate-shaped lying on the basal plane (0001) of 
α-Mg matrix[33]. Fig. 5 shows the morphology of the pr ecipitates in the SHT+ECAP 
12 passes and LT ECAP 8 passes samples. Different from the β1′ and β2′ phase, the 
relatively big precipitate particles were spherical shaped with diameter from dozens to 
~200 nanometers, as indicated by arrows in Fig. 5(a) and (c). Such spherical shaped 
precipitates were also demonstrated in other hot extruded or pre -strain Mg-Zn alloys 
and they were found more effective than the rod-like precipitates for strengthening the 
alloys[34, 35]. In the SHT+ECAP 12 passes sample ，Fig. 5(a), there are fewer big 
spherical particles distributed around the grain boundaries and most of the particles 
were smaller than 100 nm. But When the ECAP processing temperature was gradually 
reduced, as shown in Fig. 5(c), the precipitate fraction was obviously increased. High

volume fraction of spherical precipitates with different size coexisted in the LT ECAP 
8 passes sample , which suggested that the metastable phase particles precipitated 
hierarchically and grew bigger with the gradually reduced processing temperature. 
Fig. 5(b) and (d) shows a closer look at the morphology of the particles inside 
the grains of SHT+ECAP 12 passes and LT ECAP 8 passes samples with high 
magnification. A large number of short rod -like or blocky precipitates are seen 
distributed densely inside the grains ，which are indicated by arrows and circles, 
respectively. These fine particles can be classified as the typical β1′ phase in Mg -Zn 
alloys aligned parallel to [0001] α . However, different from the long rod-like β1′ phase 
formed during the static aging with the aspect ratios (length to di ameter) up to 40 [29], 
the aspect ratios of these fine rod-like precipitates found in the ECAP processed ZK60 
alloys were much lower. Due to the severe deformation, t he short rod-like precipitate 
did not tend to grow along the longitudinal axis during ECAP processing. The average 
length of the rod-like precipitate was 30nm~50nm, and the diameter was only ~10nm. 
Blocky or round-like precipitates could also be seen with similar or a little bit bigger 
diameter. They might have evolved from the short rod -like particles, and then grew 
bigger to the big spherical particles.

4. Discussion 
The present investigation revealed that by appropriate ECAP thermomechanical 
processing, a synergy of high strength and good ductility could be attained in ZK60 
magnesium alloy. The influence of pre -solution heat treatment and processing 
temperature will be discussed in the context of the microstructure homogeneity, grain 
size and the precipitate evolution. 
 As shown in the microstructure observation, pre -solution heat treatment made 
more homogeneous microstructure in the ECAP processed ZK60 alloys compared 
with their counterpart without pre -solution heat treatment . Most of the eutectic and 
second phase particles dissolved by pre-solution heat treatment, but which were the 
hardening or strengthening particles during ECAP processing in the alloy without 
pre-solution heat treatment. Therefore, the grain refining mechanism during ECAP 
differs in the alloys with and without pre -solution heat treatment. In the sample 
without pre -solution heat treatment, dislocations were first hindered and tangled 
around the hard eutectic and second phase particles at the early stag e of ECAP, and 
then dynamic recovery and recrystallization were activated inhomogeneously. But 
for the pre -solution heat treated sample, due to the absence of hard particles in the 
homogenized alloy, the distribution of dislocations and strain were more 
homogeneous which induced continuous dynamic recry stallization and a more 
homogeneous microstructure. 
Although the ECAP processed alloys with pre -solution heat treatment had 
homogenous microstructure, the grains were not much finer than their counterpart 
without pre -solution heat treatment . The main reason was that dynamic

## Page 12

11 
 
recrystallization readily occurred in the magnesium alloys accompanied with the hot 
deformation process. The restricted grain refinement also reflected in the mechanical 
properties. Except for the LT ECAP 8 passes sample, all the yield strength s of the 
ECAP processed samples were similar, although the ductility was improved by the 
pre-solution heat treatment. Reducing the processing temperature of ECAP could 
suppress the dynamic recovery and recrystallization and then further refined the 
grains, but the ductility of the magnesium alloy would become poor. In this study, in 
order to avoid reducing the formability of the alloy suddenly, the processing 
temperature was gradually reduced with the ECAP passes , and then it would take 
advantages from the prior ECAP at higher temperature that improved the formability 
of the alloy at lower temperature . Just as expected, the grains of the ZK60 al loy were 
successfully further refined to ultra-fine size by 8 passes of LT ECAP, and accordingly, 
there was a clear improvement in the strength, especially the yield strength. 
Another influence of the pre -solution heat treatment and processing temperature 
on the microstructure was the precipitate behavior. By pre -solution heat treatment, 
spherical and dense nano-sized metastable phase particles precipitated simultaneously 
with the ECAP processing. Different from the rod -like or plate particles precipitated 
in static isothermal aging, spherical shape particles were formed, which were more 
energetically favorable when precipitated during continuous ECAP deformation[36]. 
The first precipitated particles played a role as obstacles to dislocation movement 
during ECAP. As deformation proceeded, more and more dislocations ac cumulated 
around particles. It supplied abundant nucleus for dynamic recrystallization. Therefore, 
such spherical shape particles were usually found around the grain/subgrain 
boundaries and tended to grow bigger . As the processing temperature was gradually 
reduced, the lower temperature as the main driver of the precipitate nucleation and 
growth directly led to more and bigger particles in the LT ECAP 8 passes sample. 
Furthermore, dynamic recovery was suppressed in the alloy that led to an increase in 
dislocation density and subgrains. Therefore, except for the decreasing processing 
temperature that impelled more metastable phase particles, the increased dislocations

12 
 
and subgrain boundaries supplied abundant nucleuses that also accelerated the 
precipitation. A large amount of metastable phase particles precipitated hierarchically 
in the alloy with increasing the ECAP passes. The increased amount and bigger size of 
metastable phase particles supplied additional precipitate hardening . The particles 
spread densely inside the grains also supplied precipitate hardening effect . Fig. 7 
shows the high density of dislocations that were blocked and tangled around the 
nanosized short-rod or blocky metastable phase particles in the SHT+ECAP 12 passes 
and LT ECAP 8 passes sa mples. Such fine metastable phase particles effectively 
pinned down the basal dislocations, and non-basal cross slips could be seen activated 
by the fine particles in Fig. 7 (a). It certainly increased the work hardening of the alloy 
and had advantages on both the strength and ductility. The ultimate strength and 
ductility were improved simultaneously by the pre -solution heat treatment and hot 
ECAP processing. When the processing temperature was gradually reduced, the finer 
grain size and the increased metastable phase particles supplied synergic fine grain 
and precipitate hardening, which certainly rendered higher strength of the LT ECAP 8 
passes sample.

5. Conclusion 
In this work, high strength and good ductility were achieved in ZK60 magnesium 
alloy by combining heat treatment with ECAP. Pre-solution heat treatment , as the 
critical step, promoted a large amount of metastable phase particles which precipitated 
in the fine structure during ECAP. There were two kinds of particles, sub-micrometer 
sized spherical and nano short-rod precipitates, with the same composition MgZn 2. It 
improved the work hardening of the alloy effectively and increased the strength and

## Page 14

13 
 
ductility simultaneously. By reducing the ECAP processing temperature gradually, the 
grains were further refined to ~ 500 nm and more particles p recipitated hierarchically. 
It rendered a notable increase in the yield strength and ultimate tensile strength, 
without much loss in the ductility.
