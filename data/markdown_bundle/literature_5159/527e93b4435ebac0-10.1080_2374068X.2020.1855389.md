ABSTRACT
Inconel alloys are used for high temperature applications due to 
their excellent properties at elevated temperatures. The joining of 
Inconel alloys with other alloys is challenging due to the formation 
of cracks and interface microstructural segregation of alloying ele -
ments. In the present investigation Inconel 718 alloy was joined 
with high tensile steel using laser welding. In order to attain the 
quality joints, welding parameters were studied and identified their 
range. The microstructural characteristics of the welds at the fusion 
zone and heat-affected zone were analyzed. The microhardness 
variations across the fusion zone and other zones were observed. 
The mechanical properties of the joints were evaluated and found 
that the strength of the joints was related to laser power. The 
elemental analysis across the fusion zone was revealed the forma -
tion of segregation of the elements which has an influence on the 
strength of the joints.

1. Introduction
Alloy 718 (Inconel 718) is a nickel based superalloy having inimitable properties of 
excellent resistance to high temperature environments, creep resistance and hot corro -
sion. It is extensively used in nuclear, gas turbines chemical industries and power plants 
[ 1 , 2 ]. On the other hand, the weight of the superalloys is quite higher than the structural 
steels and other major structural materials. It will increase the cost and weight of the 
components in aerospace and the collecting tanks positioned at the highest height in 
chemical industries. To condense the weight of the components, the amount of alloy 718 
usage has been reduced by replacing it with other materials which have nearly similar 
properties. Also, some of the components were prepared by various parts which are made 
with different materials were needs to be joined mandatorily. Therefore, the parts were 
integrated into a component consisting of complex design and improves the design 
functionality [ 3–6 ]. Hence it is desirable to weld the dissimilar materials in the manu -
facturing industries to make the complex products and technology.

Joining of dissimilar materials is incessantly demanding in the manufacturing indus -
tries to produce the multi component products with the complex shapes and advanced 
design models. Whereas, the differences in dissimilar materials properties such as 
chemical and mechanical, the joining between them is quite difficult [ 7–11 ]. In particular, 
the dissimilar joining using fusion welding methods causes several issues in the welds and 
difficult to produce the successful joints [ 12–14 ]. Whereas, the solid state welding 
methods can join the dissimilar materials without much problems compared to fusion 
welding methods. There are many incompatible materials were joined successfully with -
out any problems using solid state welding methods [ 15–21 ]. In order to control the grain 
boundary precipitates in dissimilar joints of Inconel 718 and ATI718Plus solid state 
welding methods were attempted. The studies were identified that the amount of grain 
boundary precipitates reduction and its consequence on the enhancement of mechanical 
properties of the welded joints [ 22 ]. Along with that, the studies on the thermal stability 
of the phases such as γ ′ and γ ″ , and the stress influence on precipitation kinetics and their 
effect at various environments identified. In general, the δ phase exists along the grain 
boundaries as a precipitates in Inconel 718 alloy, but it can be changes by the addition of 
other alloying elements such as Al, Nb, Ti. The main grain boundary precipitate is γ 
phase in the ATI718 plus alloy instead of δ phase. But it is also identified that the presence 
of both δ + γ precipitates can exists in the interwoven layers. The δ phase nucleates and 
transforms to γ phase at a temperature range of 990 ºC-1020 ºC at the grain boundaries. 
Along with the solves temperature, the addition of Nb element leads to the δ precipitate 
nucleates intergranular to γ ″ phase [ 22 ]. Some of the studies were conducted in different 
way that applying the aging treatment for the dissimilar friction welds between Inconel 
718 and EN24 to release the stresses and enhance the mechanical properties of the welds. 
The welds were exhibited the low strength and high toughness for the as-welded joints, 
whereas the heat treated welds led to decline in toughness and increase in strength [ 23 ]. 
Inconel 718 was joined with titanium alloys which is critical combination to produce 
successful joint using fusion welding methods by diffusion bonding. The interface 
microstructures and joint mechanical properties were obtained through higher proper -
ties than the fusion welds [ 24 ]. Some other dissimilar combinations between the steel to 
titanium, steel to aluminum and titanium to aluminum were joined effectively using solid 
state welding methods, usually, they are not feasible to weld by arc welding methods 
[ 25–31 ]. Using solid state welding methods thin plates and dissimilar combinations with 
low temperature can be welded with high joint strength [ 32–43 ]. The dissimilar combi -
nation of Ti and stainless steel attractive for various applications but it is not using 
regularly due to the presence of harmful brittle intermetallic compounds. There are 
several elements in the stainless steel such as Fe, Ni and Cr, which can form any phases 
with Ti and deteriorate the mechanical strength of the joints. As per the phase diagram of 
these elements, there are several intermetallic compounds possible to generate such as 
NiTi, FeTi, CrTi types and all are brittle and harmful to the joint properties [ 3 ].

The dissimilar combination of Inconel 718 and high tensile steel is mostly used for 
various applications of high temperature environments. Based on the applications and 
the quality, the welded joints and their performance should be strong enough to sustain 
the working environments. Therefore, the welding processes are the main source of 
producing such high quality welds. Laser welding is one of the major Page 3 Deleted: 
source to produce high quality welds and can control the welding defects in the joints. In 
the present study, laser welding was used to produce dissimilar welds between Inconel 
718 and high tensile steel. The feasibility of the laser welding to obtain high quality welds 
and the welding parameters were studied extensively. The welds were characterised to 
observe the microstructural changes and the formation of defects along with the weld 
interface, fusion zone and the heat affected zones. The mechanical properties of the joints 
were evaluated and determined the major influencing parameter for the dissimilar 
combination of Inconel 718 to high tensile steel. The fractography analysis was con -

2. Experimental procedure
In this present study, high tensile steel (AISI4140) and Inconel 718 are used as 
a dissimilar combination with the thickness of 3 mm. The dimensions of the specimens 
150 mm length and 60 mm wide were cut and joined using butt joint configuration. Laser 
welding was performed for the selected dissimilar combination in a butt joint configura -
tion using pulsed Nd: YAG laser welding process. The welding parameters of travel speed 
5 mm/min to 9 mm/min and laser power up to 2 kW wasused to produce the joints. The 
range of values were determined based on the trail experiments for the dissimilar 
combinations. During welding, Ar was used as a shielding gas to protect the weld pool

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructural characterization
The laser welding of butt welds were produced using different welding parameters. The 
selection of welding parameters is important to make a stronger welds and obtain a good 
microstructural characteristics. However, it is quite challenging to identify the proper 
welding parameters for the dissimilar combinations. The given heat input and thermal 
stresses are needs to be balanced for both the materials. If the elemental composition and 
number of elements are varies in large difference leads to the formation of much amount 
of stresses and phases in the welds. The precipitates combined with Nb, Al, Ti and Cr in 
the Inconel 718 welds used to form along with the grain boundaries. However, if the weld 
metal composition varies with the addition of other material due to the dissimilar 
welding, the physical properties of the welds entirely different from the pure Inconel 
welds. The high tensile steel have major elements of Mo and Cr along with the Fe which 
are common elements in the Inconel 718 substrate. Many of these changes were influence 
by elemental composition and the heat input in the welds. The amount of heat in the 
welds were provided by the welding parameters. The selection of parameters and the 
controlling heat input into the welds is necessary to obtain defect free welds. There are 
many combination of parameters with a large range of values studied extensively to 
identify the proper combination of parameters for a dissimilar combination of steel to 
Inconel 718 welded joints. The inappropriate combination of welding conditions were 
produced the welds with depression shape on the face of the weld. Whereas some of the 
combinations produced the convex shape of the weld bead appearance at the centre of the 
welds. The macrostructure of the welds exhibited the presence of weld face and root 
adequately and the width of the each zones. The welds produced under optimal condi -
tions were exhibited flat face bead appearance and the cross section of the welds showed 
the formation of any defects. Moreover, the welds were completely free from the presence 
of cracks either in the fusion zone and the heat affected zone. The thermal conductivity of 
the each material is different and the high tensile steel experienced to distribute the 
thermal heat such a large distance from the weld centre compared to Inconel 718. The 
distributed heat in the substrates towards base metal from the fusion zone makes changes 
in the microstructure. However, indirectly it may also effect the mechanical properties of

3.2. Mechanical properties
The mechanical properties of the joints were evaluated by microhardness and tensile tests. 
Figure 4 presents the microhardness profile and mapping of the welds. Microhardness 
profile taken across the cross section of the welds of different zones as shown in the Figure 
4(a ). The peak hardness of 510 HV was identified at the HAZ of the Inconel 718, where the 
precipitates enriched and spreads densely. The small amount of hardness raised in the 
fusion zone over the base metal and other zones is due to the grain boundary precipitates 
[ 44 ]. Whereas, the highest hardness on the high tensile steel is due to the formation of 
martensite along the HAZ region. The amount of heat spreads towards the base metal 
during the cooling cycle, hence the HAZ region experienced to a long duration of heat 
accumulation lead to the microstructure change in the HAZ of the high tensile steel. As 
shown in the microstructures (see Figure 1 ), there is no evidence of precipitates in the high 
tensile steel side confirmed by hardness profile. Figure 4(b ) illustrates the hardness 
mapping of the fusion zone with the distribution of hardness variations within the fusion 
zone. There is a small amount of hardness changed with the grains in the fusion zone and
the distribution of precipitates. The highest hardness region resembles the enrichment of 
precipitates at the boundaries of the small grain regions. Whereas, the hardness variation also 
depends on the presence of elements in the precipitates and denser distribution of the 
precipitates. The increase in hardness in the mapping and the hardness profile shows that the 
strength of the welds slightly enhanced due to the presence of precipitates. The tensile test

4. Conclusions
The dissimilar materials of Inconel 718 and high tensile steel are joined successfully using 
laser welding. The effect of laser power on the strength of the joints studied in detailed. 
The following conclusions are drawn from the experimental results.
(1) The welding parameters combination was determined for the dissimilar combina -
tion of laser welding to obtain the high strength joints.
(2) Microstructural characteristics were observed with the different features on the 
Inconel 718 side with the presence of precipitates and the formation of martensite 
on the high tensile steel.
(3) EDS and EPMA analysis identified the interdiffusion of the elements along the 
grain boundaries and the presence of γ ″ strengthening precipitates.
(4) Microhardness increased towards the HAZ on both the substrates owing to the 
presence of precipitates on Inconel 718 side and the martensite on the high tensile 
steel side.
(5) The highest strength of 672 MPa was obtained for the joints at a laser power of 
1.5 kW and its trend falling down with the further increasing of the laser power.
(6) The fractography analysis investigated the ductile mode of failure occurred with 
the small amount of brittle regions.
