ABSTRACT
Controlling the texture and grain size of Fe-Cr-Al alloys is essential for enhancing mechanical properties. Here, 
the effect of Nb on the texture evolution and the anisotropy of mechanical properties in Fe-Cr-Al alloys were 
investigated. Compared to the annealed Fe-Cr-Al alloy, the yield strength of the annealed Fe-Cr-Al-Nb alloy 
increased from 430 MPa to 540 MPa with nearly identical rolling and transverse tensile yield strengths. 
Transmission electron microscope results indicate that the Nb could form Fe
2
Nb particles with an average radius 
of 50 nm in matrix of the annealed Fe-Cr-Al-Nb alloy. The electron backscatter diffraction results indicate that 
the grains of the Fe-Cr-Al-Nb alloy are significantly refined by Fe
2
Nb particles, and the α -fiber and γ -fiber textures 
are weakened, promoting texture randomization. The strengthening calculations indicate that compared to 
annealed Fe-Cr-Al alloys, the increase in the yield strength of the annealed Fe-Cr-Al-Nb alloy is attributed, in 
sequence, to dislocation strengthening, Orowan strengthening by particles and grain boundary strengthening. As 
a result, the annealed Fe-Cr-Al-Nb alloy achieves an increase in yield strength while reducing anisotropy. This 
finding provides insights for the development of Fe-Cr-Al alloys.
1. Introduction
The Fe-Cr-Al alloys exhibit promising prospects for utilization as 
nuclear reactor fuel cladding materials owing to their cost-effectiveness, 
superior thermal conductivity, and outstanding resistance to high- 
temperature steam oxidation [ 1 – 3 ]. In order to improve neutron econ -
omy, it is crucial to decrease the weight of seamless Fe-Cr-Al fuel clad -
ding tubes, with specified outer diameter and wall thickness 
requirements [ 4 , 5 ]. Yamamoto et al. managed to manufacture seamless 
Fe-13Cr-5.2Al-0.05Y-2Mo steel tubes using the drawing method. How -
ever, the resulting cladding tubes exhibited a coarse grain structure and 
did not meet the required dimensional accuracy standards [ 6 ]. Coarse 
grains in FeCrAl alloys not only raise the risk of premature failure in the 
production of seamless thin-walled tubes for fuel cladding in light water 
reactors [ 4 ], but also have a detrimental impact on the mechanical 
properties of the final tubes, including their strength, fracture 
toughness, and resistance to irradiation [ 7 ]. In addition, the textures 
resulting from deformation and recrystallization are vital in influencing 
the alloy ’ s yield strength, heat resistance, creep performance in reactors, 
fatigue life, resistance to stress corrosion cracking, hydride orientation, 
and its behavior under irradiation-induced growth. For example, the 
high-Cr ferritic ODS steel, due to the presence of a strong texture, ex -
hibits poor transverse mechanical properties compared to its longitu -
dinal properties. In the application of Fe-Cr-Al alloys, internal pressure 
creep caused by the accumulation of fission gases inside the tube leads to 
severe stress in the hoop direction, which may limit the use of Fe-Cr-Al 
alloys [ 8 ]. Hence, the inherent mechanical characteristics of Fe-Cr-Al 
alloy and the specific geometric configuration of light water reactor 
fuel cladding present considerable obstacles in the design of alloy tubes.

the concentrations of Cr (approximately 10 wt%) and Al (around 5 wt%) 
in Fe-Cr-Al alloys have been designed. This fine-tuning is aimed at

increasing the grain boundary density and weakening the texture. [ 24 ]. 
Therefore, Nb is a candidate element for improving the anisotropy of 
Fe-Cr-Al alloys.
Nb was introduced into Fe-Cr-Al alloys to develop a new type of Fe- 
Cr-Al alloy in this work. The Nb element forms submicron-sized Fe
2

cipitation strengthening. The submicron-sized phases delay grain 
growth and significantly weaken the texture, achieving the dual goals of 
increased strength and reduced anisotropy. A detailed examination was 
conducted to analyze the microstructural modifications and mechanical 
properties of Fe-Cr-Al-Nb alloys after undergoing deformation and 
annealing processes. The study also examined the role of Nb in influ -
encing the microstructure and anisotropy.

3. Results
3.1. Mechanical properties
After hot rolling and annealing, the mechanical properties in the 
rolling and transverse directions of Fe-Cr-Al alloy and Fe-Cr-Al-Nb alloy 
are depicted in Fig. 1 , with detailed data summarized in Table 2 . 
Following hot rolling, the Fe-Cr-Al alloy displays a yield strength of 590 
MPa, a tensile strength of 720 MPa, and an elongation of 16 % in the 
rolling direction. Conversely, in the transverse direction, the yield 
strength is 740 MPa, the tensile strength is 825 MPa, and the elongation 
is 12 %. These results highlight notable differences in mechanical 
properties between the two directions, indicating pronounced anisot -
ropy in the hot-rolled Fe-Cr-Al alloy. With the incorporation of Nb, the 
hot-rolled Fe-Cr-Al-Nb alloy demonstrates a yield strength of 774 MPa, a 
tensile strength of 879 MPa, and an elongation of 14 % in the rolling 
direction. Conversely, in the transverse direction, the yield strength is 
874 MPa, the tensile strength is 936 MPa, and the elongation is 10 %. 
The results show that the differences in yield strength and tensile 
strength between the two directions are reduced, indicating that the 
addition of Nb decreases anisotropy while enhancing yield strength and 
tensile strength (see Table 2 ).
After annealing, the Fe-Cr-Al alloy exhibits the yield strengths of 430 
MPa and 420 MPa, tensile strengths of 789 MPa and 744 MPa, and 
elongations of 45 % and 35 % in the rolling and transverse directions, 
respectively. The Fe-Cr-Al-Nb alloy, after annealing, shows yield 
strengths of 540 MPa and 520 MPa in the both directions, with tensile 
strengths of 885 MPa and 896 MPa, and elongations of 38 % and 33 %, 
respectively. These results indicate that after annealing, the yield 
strengths of both alloys are similar in different directions, and the yield 
strength and tensile strength of the Fe-Cr-Al-Nb alloy are significantly 
higher than those of the Fe-Cr-Al alloy. This results further confirms that 
the addition of Nb element significantly enhances tensile strength and 
yield strength while maintaining isotropy.

undergoes full recrystallization, resulting in an average grain size of 54 
μ m. On the other hand, the Fe-Cr-Al-Nb alloy retains recrystallization, 
displaying an average grain size of 10 μ m. This indicates that the

4. Discussion
4.1. Particle-induced grain recrystallization and texture randomization
The anisotropic behavior of polycrystalline materials is affected by 
both single-crystal anisotropy and the grain orientation distribution [ 26 ,
27 ]. Numerous studies have shown that particles can promote recrys -
tallization during thermal deformation or annealing processes [ 28 , 29 ]. 
Therefore, this section discusses the recrystallization behavior of grains 
influenced by Fe
2
Nb particles and how they affect the texture. The 
behavior of particles promoting recrystallization is analyzed through 
TEM( Fig. 6 ). In the hot-rolled Fe-Cr-Al alloy (without particles), there 
are numerous subgrains and dislocation cells. After annealing, due to the 
absence of nanoscale pinning, the grains migrate and grow rapidly 
( Fig. 6 b). After the addition of Nb, Fe
2
Nb particles are located at the 
subgrain boundaries ( Fig. 6 c), and a significant accumulation of

5. Conclusion
A systematic study was conducted on the effects of Nb on the 
recrystallization behavior, texture, and mechanical anisotropy of Fe-Cr- 
Al-Nb alloys. Based on this, the strengthening mechanism of the Fe
2
Nb 
phase and its role in weakening the texture were discussed. The con -
clusions can be summarized as follows. 
(1) The newly-developed annealed Fe-Cr-Al-Nb alloy involved the 
Fe
2
Nb particles has yield strengths of 520 MPa and 500 MPa in 
the both directions, and elongations of 38 % and 33 %, respec -
tively. In contrast, the annealed Fe-Cr-Al alloy has yield strengths 
of 430 MPa and 420 MPa in the both directions, and elongations 
of 45 % and 35 %, respectively. More importantly, the addition of 
Nb significantly reduces the mechanical anisotropy of Fe-Cr-Al 
alloys while enhancing their yield strength.
(2) The addition of Nb results in the formation of Fe
2
Nb particles 
with an average size of 50 nm in matrix, which promotes grain 
nucleation and inhibits grain growth during hot rolling defor -
mation and subsequent annealing, ultimately promoting texture 
randomization and grain refinement. Fe
2
Nb particles refines the 
grain size from 54 μ m to 10 μ m. The randomization of texture 
promotes the reduction of anisotropy in the mechanical proper -
ties of Fe-Cr-Al-Nb alloys.
(3) In Fe-Cr-Al-Nb alloys, the yield strength is a result of dislocation 
strengthening, grain boundary strengthening, Orowan strength -
ening, and solution strengthening. Compared to annealed Fe-Cr- 
Al alloys, the order of yield strength increment is: dislocation 
strengthening > Orowan strengthening by particles > grain 
boundary strengthening.
