Keywords: Intermetallic, Nb5Si3, Mechanical property, Phase diagram, Fracture Toughness 
 
Abstract. Microstructure and mechanical property at room temperature and at 1773 K of Nb-Si based 
refractory intermetallic alloys were investigated in terms of compression and fracture toughness test. 
Mo and V were chosen as ternary alloying elements because of their high melting points, atomic sizes 
smaller than Nb. Both ternary alloying elements we re found to have a significant role in modifying 
the microstructure from dispersed structure to eutectic-like structure in Nb solid solution/Nb 5Si3 
intermetallic composites. The 0.2% offset yield strength at room temperature increased with 
increasing content of ternary elements in Nb solid solution and volume fraction of Nb 5Si3. At 1773 
K, Mo addition has a positive role in increasing the yield strength. On the other hand, V addition has 
a role in decreasing the yield strength. The fracture toughness of ternary alloys was superior to binary 
alloys. Details will be discussed in correlation w ith ternary alloying, volume fraction of constituent 
phase, and the microstructure. 
Introduction 
Niobium silicide (Nb 5Si3) based refractory alloys have a high potential for high temperature 
structural applications because Nb 5Si3 has a high melting point (2527 K), high creep resistance at 
temperatures higher than 1473 K, and low density (7.1 g/cm3). Therefore, these alloys can be one of 
the good candidate materials to be used at temp eratures above 1473 K as a replacement for Ni-base 
superalloys whose attainable maximum temperature is around 1273 K. Since the monolithic Nb 5Si3 
phase has been found to be very brittle at ambient temperatures, Nb 5Si3-based multiphase alloy 
systems containing a ductile niobium solid solution (Nbss) phase would be of a technological interest 
in the field of high temperature structural materials. Considering the inherent difficulty in ductilizing 
this silicide, it is essential that an incorpora tion of the ductile phase into the brittle silicide is 
necessarily required in order to improve the room temperature deformability and fracture toughness. 
Thus, high temperature strength can be held by th e silicide, and low temperature deformability and 
fracture toughness can be enhanced by the incorporated ductile phase. From the viewpoints of phase 
formation behavior and physical property such as melting point and atomic size, we chose Mo and V 
as ternary alloying elements in the present a lloy system. We aim at understanding the alloying 
behavior of molybdenum and vanadium on phase formation, the crystal structure of constituents, the 
mechanical property at 1773 K, and fracture toughness at room temperature.

Experimental procedure 
The purities of elemental materials were 99.9 mass% Nb, 99.999 mass% Si, 99.9 mass% Mo, and 
99.9 mass% V, respectively. Alloy buttons of 30 mm in diameter were produced by arc melting on a 
water-cooled copper hearth under an argon gas atmosphere with a non-consumable tungsten 
electrode. The ingots were re-melted at least five times for chemical homogeneity. The ingots were 
then heat treated under an argon atmosphere at 1773 K for 100 h followed by rapid cooling to room 
temperature. The nominal compositions for all of the samples studied here are listed in Table 1. X-ray 
diffraction (XRD) analysis was conducted on bulk samples to identify the constituent phases. 
Compression test specimens with 2.5 mm x 2.5 mm cross-section and 6 mm height were prepared by 
EDM and then mechanically polished. The compression test was carried out using an Instron model 
8500 mechanical testing machine at 1773 K in Ar atmo sphere and at an initial strain rate of 3 x 10
-4 
s-1. The fracture toughness test was performed on 3-point bending specimen prepared by EDM. 
Results and discussion 
Figs. 1(a) and (b) show the isothermal sections of ternary Nb-Si-Mo and Nb-Si-V phase diagrams 
determined at 1973 K. It can be observed that bot h phase diagrams have similar feature from the 
viewpoint of phase form ation behavior. The Nb 5Si3 intermetallic phase is extended along 
pseudo-binary lines between Nb 5Si3 and V 5Si3 or Mo 5Si3 without having a solubility range, 
indicating ternary alloying elements V and Mo substitute for Nb sites. The Nb solid solution phase for 
both alloy systems was found to be extended along Nb-V and Nb-Mo phase lines. From the behavior 
of tie-line formation, it is clear that ternary alloying elements V and Mo are more dominantly 
partitioned to the Nb ss phase than Nb 5Si3 intermetallic phase within the composition range 
investigated.

The lightly and darkly contrasted regions in Fig. 2 correspond to Nbss and Nb5Si3, respectively. A 
dispersed feature consisting of a small Nb ss particles and Nb 5Si3 matrix phase is characterized in 
binary Nb-18Si. With an addition of ternary a lloying elements Mo(Fig. 2(b)) and V(Fig.2(c)), a 
drastic change in the microstructure--from disperse d structure to eutectic-like structure-- was found 
over the whole microstructure. No discernable ch ange in volume fraction ratio was found for three 
kinds of alloys indicating that the volume fraction of the present Nb ss/ Nb5Si3 composites has not a 
dependence of ternary alloying elements V and Mo. This result is well consistent with the formation 
of ternary phase diagrams as shown in Figs. 1(a) and (b). 
Fig. 3 shows the 0.2% offset yield stress plotted as a 
function of content of tern ary alloying element. All 
samples were tested at 1773 K under argon atmosphere. 
Concerning the Nb-18Si- xV alloys, 0.2% offset yield 
stress decreased continuously with increasing V content, 
while a complex strength behavior is observed in the 
Nb-18Si-xMo alloys. With increasing Mo content to 
5at%, 0.2% offset yield stress decreased slightly and 
then increased rapidly with an additional increase to 
15at% and finally decreased slightly with increasing 
content to 25at%. The continuous decrease in yield 
stress in Nb-18Si- xV alloys can be related with solid 
solution softening at 1773 K due to the increase in V 
content in Nb solid solution phase which may cause to 
accelerated diffusional process. In Nb-18Si-xMo alloys, 
a completely different feature appeared to display a 
different strengthening mechanism compared to 
Nb-18Si-xV alloys. The decrease in yield stress with 
an addition of 5at%Mo can be explained by two 
competitive effects; (1) positive effect to increase the 
yield stress in terms of solid solution strengthening due to Mo addition, (2) negative effect to decrease 
the yield stress by microstructural change from disp ersed structure to eutectic-like structure. Thus, 
the latter effect may play a dominant role in decreasing the yield stress with an addition of 5at%Mo. 
A rapid increase in yield stress from 5 to 15at% Mo can be closely associated with the effect of solid 
solution strengthening due to the difference in elastic modulus between Mo and Nb. A slight decrease 
in yield stress from 15 to 25at%Mo may be ascribed to phase transformation from α-Nb
5Si3 to 
β-Nb5Si3 since α-Nb5Si3 has been known to exhibit higher strength than β-Nb5Si3 in the prior study 
[1-2]. 
Fig. 4 shows crack propagation behavior for binary Nb-18Si(a) and Nb-18Si-15Mo(b) alloys. In 
Nb-18Si alloy, crack propagates straightly without showing toughening mechanisms such as crack 
deflection, crack bridging, crack a rrest and re-nucleation. In contrast, crack deflection and crack 
bridging are frequently observed to take into account the higher fracture toughness of Nb-18Si-15Mo 
alloy than Nb-18Si alloy.

Fig. 5 shows room temperature fracture toughness plo tted as a function of the content of ternary 
alloying element in Nb-18Si- xMo or xV alloys. In Nb-18Si- xV alloys, fracture toughness increased 
rapidly up to 5at%V and then increased gradually. The sharp increase at 5at%V may be explained by 
microstructural change from dispersed to eutectic-like structure leading to crack deflection and crack 
bridging, and solid solution strengthening of Nb ss phase. In Nb-18Si-xMo alloys, similarly, fracture 
toughness increased rapidly up to 5at%Mo and then decreased with increasing Mo content. The 
decrease in fracture toughness from 5 to 25at%Mo can be ascribed to the solid solution of Mo in Nb
ss. 
Note that the DBTT(ductile brittle transition temperature) of Mo is about 366K which temperature is 
quite higher than that of Nb. Therefore the DBTT of Nbss phase would be increased with increasing 
Mo content. While, the rapid increase of fracture toughness at 5at%Mo can be explained as described 
for Nb-18Si-5V alloy. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Summary 
Microstructure, mechanical property, and room te mperature fracture toughness were investigated in 
an attempt to understand the effect of ternary alloying elements V and Mo in Nb-Si based refractory 
alloy system. The dispersed structure shown in bi nary Nb-18Si alloy is changed to eutectic-like 
structure with an addition of ternary alloying element V or Mo. The eutectic-like structure appeared 
to be favorable to the room temperature fracture toughness, but not to the high temperature strength at 
1773 K. At the given volume fraction, 0.2% offset yield stress at 1773 K increased with increasing 
Mo content. On the contrary, 0.2% offset yi eld stress decreased with increasing V content. 
Microstructural modification and solid solution strengthening can enhance room temperature fracture 
toughness. From the viewpoints of room temp erature fracture toughness and high temperature 
strength at 1773 K, it is suggested that an addition of less than 5at% ternary alloying elements V or 
Mo is effective in improving room temperature fracture toughness with a large consumption of high 
temperature strength.
