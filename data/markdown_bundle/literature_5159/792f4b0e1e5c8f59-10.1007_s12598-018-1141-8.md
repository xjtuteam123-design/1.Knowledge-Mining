Microstructure and room temperature fracture toughness of
Nb–Si-based alloys with Sr addition
Yong-Lin Huang, Li-Na Jia* , Bin Kong, Yue-Ling Guo, Na Wang
Received: 17 November 2017 / Revised: 14 January 2018 / Accepted: 14 August 2018
/C211The Nonferrous Metals Society of China and Springer-Verlag GmbH Germany, part of Springer Nature 2018
Abstract Nb–Si-based alloys show great potential to sur-
pass the widely used Ni-based superalloys. The element Sr
is widely applied in aluminum and magnesium alloys, but
reports about the effects of Sr on Nb–Si-based alloys are
quite rare. So, Nb–Si-based alloys with nominal composi-
tions of Nb–15Si–24Ti–4Cr–2Al–2Hf–0/0.05/0.15Sr (at%)
were prepared by directional solidiﬁcation and heat treat-
ment. The microstructural characterization and room tem-
perature fracture toughness of Nb–Si-based alloy were
systematically investigated. Results show that all these
alloys consist of Nb 5Si3 phase embedded within Nb solid
solution (Nbss) matrix. The Nb 5Si3 phase becomes reﬁned
and more discontinuous after adding minor Sr. As for the
fracture toughness, 0.05 at% and 0.15 at% Sr additions do
not cause signiﬁcant change. The discontinuous and reﬁn-
ing mechanism of Sr element was studied, and the analysis
of toughness decreasing with Sr addition reveals that the
size of Nb ss phase plays a crucial role in determining the
fracture toughness.
Keywords Nb–Si alloy; Reﬁnement; Discontinuous;
Fracture toughness; Nb ss
1 Introduction
Nb–Si-based alloys have several advantages, such as high
melting point, relatively low density, high stiffness and
excellent strength at elevated temperature, over Ni-based
single-crystal superalloys [1, 2]. Accordingly, Nb–Si-based
alloys show great promise to meet the required properties
for high-temperature structural applications [ 3–5]. Nb–Si-
based alloys are usually characterized by intermetallic
compounds embedded in Nb solid solution (Nb ss) matrix
[6, 7]. The in situ alloys are toughened by Nb ss at room
temperature, and the intermetallic phase Nb 5Si3 provides
high-temperature strength and creep resistance [ 4, 8–11].
However, the poor room temperature fracture toughness
and low ductility of Nb–Si system alloys still need further
improvement for practical application [ 11, 12].
The enhancement of mechanical properties is achieved
mainly through adding alloying elements, such as Ti, Hf,
Zr [ 13–15], B [ 16], V [ 17], or developing advanced pro-
cessing technologies [ 18, 19], e.g., directional solidiﬁca-
tion, spark plasma sintering, vapor deposition, selective
laser melting. Ti and Hf are beneﬁcial to improve fracture
resistance by reducing Peierls–Nabarro (P–N) barrier
energy. Besides, Ti and Hf are the only elements that do
not increase the brittle-to-ductile transition temperature
(BDTT) [ 20]. Cr and Al are undesirable for fracture
toughness and tensile ductility since these two alloying
elements promote cleavage fracture [6]. But the addition of
Cr and Al is indispensable for the oxidation resistance
[21, 22]. In addition to alloying, suitable fabrication
methods are also helpful for the mechanical properties. For
example, the unidirectional Nb ss/Nb5Si3 solidiﬁcation
microstructure can raise the fracture toughness [ 23–26].
Sr has excellent modiﬁcation efﬁciency and is widely
used in aluminum [ 27–29] and magnesium alloys [ 30, 31].
Zhao et al. [ 29] investigated the effect of Sr-containing
grain reﬁner on the reﬁning performance of A356 alloy and
found that the reﬁner can promote grain reﬁning efﬁciency.
Cheng et al. [ 31] also reported that Sr can enhance grain
reﬁnement efﬁciency in AZ31 magnesium alloys. There are

2 Experimental
According to Sr content, three ingots with nominal com-
positions of Nb–15Si–24Ti–4Cr–2Al–2Hf–(0/0.05/0.15) Sr
(at%) were marked as AC0, AC1 and AC2. The purities of
the starting raw materials were 99.870 wt% Nb,
99.999 wt% Si, 99.760 wt% Ti, 99.980 wt% Cr,
99.990 wt% Al and 99.900 wt% Hf. Sr was introduced by
adding Al–30Sr (wt%) intermediate alloy since Sr can
easily react with H
2O and O 2 when exposed to air [ 32].
Each ingot weighed 1500 g. These ingots were prepared by
non-consumable arc melting under the protection of argon
in a water-cooled copper crucible and were then re-melted
at least four times to ensure the chemical homogeneity. The
arc-melted ingots were cut into rods with a diameter of
13 mm by electro-discharge machining (EDM) and then
ground to remove the oxidized surface. Directional solid-
iﬁcation operations were carried in a high-vacuum liquid–
metal-cooled furnace ﬁlled with high-purity Ar. After
holding at 1980 /C176C for 20 min, the directionally solidiﬁed
(DS) rods were withdrawn with a rate of 500 lm/C1s
-1 and
subsequently quenched into a liquid Ga–In–Sn bath. These
DS rods were marked as DS0, DS1 and DS2 (for AC0,
AC1 and AC2, respectively). DS rods were then heat-
treated at 1450 /C176C for 12 h in a high-vacuum heat treat-
ment furnace, and the heat-treated rods were marked as
HT0, HT1 and HT2. Also, alloys with the same composi-
tions of Nb
ss phase were prepared. Each weighed 600 g and
was then heat-treated at 1450 /C176C for 12 h.
Samples for microstructural observation and mechanical
property test were cut from the same position in each HT
rod or heat-treated solid solution ingot by EDM. The
constituent phases were identiﬁed by X-ray diffractometer
(XRD, D/max 2500) with Cu K a radiation. Backscatter
electron (BSE) images were taken by electron probe micro-
analyzer (EPMA, JEOL JXA-8100), and the chemical
compositions of each phase were analyzed by the wave-
dispersive spectroscope (WDS) attached to EPMA. The
microstructure was further studied using a transmission
electron microscope (TEM, JEM-2100). The thickness of
Nb
ss and Nb 5Si3 phase was quantitatively analyzed by
Image-Pro Plus 6.0 software. Fracture toughness ( KQ) was
determined by three-point bending tests using the single-
edge U-notched specimens with dimensions of 30 mm 9
6m m 9 3 mm. The U-shaped notch was introduced
perpendicular to the direction of unidirectional solidiﬁca-
tion by EDM. The tests were executed with a span of
24 mm on a hydraulic servo machine (SANS 5105) with a
compaction head loading rate of 0.1 mm /C1min
-1 at room
temperature. Secondary electron (SE) images were taken
by scanning electron microscope (SEM, JEOL JSM-6010)
to obtain the fracture surface of three-point bending
samples.

Early works [ 38–40] suggest that the reﬁned
microstructure would beneﬁt the fracture toughness of the
Nb–Si-based alloys. Discontinuous Nb 5Si3 phase in Nb ss
matrix is helpful to the improvement in fracture toughness
since Nbss phase would possess higher plastic deformation
capability with less restriction. However, after adding
minor Sr, the ﬁner microstructure with more isolated and
discontinuous Nb
5Si3 phases does not obtain a higher
fracture toughness. The following section will explain the
reasons in detail.
3.4 Possible mechanisms of Sr addition on
microstructure of Nb–Si-based alloys
The solute atoms may incorporate into the solvent crystal
lattice in substitutional type or interstitial type. Several
factors affect the dissolution degree: similar atomic radii
(the difference is less than ± 15%), same crystal structure,
similar electronegativity, and valency [ 41]. However, for
the atomic radii among Sr, Nb, and other alloying addi-
tions, the difference is obvious, as shown in Table 2.
Moreover, the crystal type of Sr (fcc) is different from the
crystal lattice of Nb ss or Nb 5Si3 phase. Thus, the solid
solubility of Sr in Nb ss matrix and Nb 5Si3 phase is rela-
tively low, and Sr is inclined to concentrate on the solid/
liquid interface during solidiﬁcation.
The enrichment of Sr in the liquid phase ahead of the
growing interface will affect the microstructure in the
following two aspects: (1) the enriched Sr solute can
restrict the element diffusion and continuous grain growth
by preferential absorption on the grain surface [ 42–45]. Li
et al. [ 46] also reported the similar condition that Ca
aggregations on the boundary restrict the grain growth.
And the restricting effect can provide time for more crystal
nuclei to be formed. (2) Surface active element Sr can
improve the wettability and reduce the liquid surface ten-
sion since Sr atoms absorbed on the growing crystals
change the solid/liquid interfacial energy, which would
enhance the nucleation rate [ 42].
These mechanisms discussed above can interpret that
Nb
5Si3 phase of HT1 and HT2 alloys distributes more
discontinuous than that of HT0 alloy. Nb 5Si3 phase is not
easier to grow continuously due to the restricting effect.
Alongside restriction, some more nucleation sites emerge,
which lead the Nb 5Si3 phase to become discontinuous and
isolated with each other. After heat treatment, all alloys
remain in the similar morphology that Nb 5Si3 exists at the
continuous matrix, except for the different isolation
degrees. The reﬁned microstructure can be explained by
higher nucleation rate and more nucleation sites after
adding Sr. Figure 8 illustrates the effect of Sr during
solidiﬁcation, in which the blue points represent Sr
aggregation.
There are some possible reasons to explain the cause of
less dark phase in Sr-containing alloys. One explanation
could be that the enrichment of Sr discourages the segre-
gation of Hf and Ti, and then, these alloying elements have
better uniform distribution among more nucleation sites.
Another possible reason is that Sr increases the Gibbs free
energy of c-(Ti, Nb)
5Si3 phase, which lowers its precipi-
tation tendency. The conclusions of this section still need
further research and conﬁrmation.
3.5 Reasons for decreased fracture toughness of Nb–
Si-based alloys with Sr addition
Brittle intermetallic compound Nb5Si3 can be toughened by
introducing ductile phase Nb, and thus, the in situ Nb/
Nb
5Si3 alloy is formed. With the addition of alloying ele-
ments, mechanical properties can be further improved.
Previous works indicated that several factors inﬂuence the
fracture toughness. Many researchers [ 11, 47] noted that a
high level of plastic constraint caused by Nb
5Si3 reduces
the fracture toughness. Davidson et al. [ 47] point out that
the size, shape, content and toughness of the matrix and
intermetallic compound, and the interfacial strength
between matrix and intermetallic compound determine the
fracture toughness. Other studies [ 48–52] also reported
these factors, and the discussion revolves around them.
According to Refs. [ 25, 52, 53], the decreased thickness
of Nb
ss results in a low fracture toughness since ﬁne Nb ss
particles are not so resistant to crack propagation. A rela-
tively large size of Nb
ss is favorable to crack bridges,
deﬂection, and arresting, while the brittle phase Nb 5Si3
with large dimensions can break easily. From the statistical

fracture process. So, in HT0 alloy, the existence of sec-
ondary cracks can beneﬁt the fracture toughness.
The Nb
ss phase imparts ductility and toughness by
plastic stretching, crack bridging, and interface separating
[55–57]. The experiment for comparing the fracture
toughness of the Nb ss matrix in HT0 and HT1 alloys is
designed. Average compositions of Nb ss phase are shown
in Table 1. As seen in Fig. 9, the fracture toughness of Nbss
in HT1 alloy is as good as that in HT0 alloy or even better.
Minor dissolved Sr atoms may play the solid solution
strengthening effect. As a result, the change in chemical
compositions of Nbss is not the primary factor to determine
the fracture toughness in this study.
A reﬁned microstructure leads to more Nb ss/Nb5Si3
interfaces in HT1 and HT2 alloys, meaning that there
would be more chances for interface separation, but the
interface decohesion mechanism does not work since no
microcracks are observed in Figs. 5 and 6. Although
Nb5Si3 phase is more isolated (the more isolated and dis-
continuous, the less constraint), HT1 and HT2 alloys do not
possess high fracture toughness as expected. It implies that
a less plastic constraint caused by Nb
5Si3 phase in HT1 and
HT2 alloys does not compensate for the decreased fracture
toughness.
4 Conclusion
The effect of minor Sr addition on microstructure and room
temperature fracture toughness of Nb–15Si–24Ti–4Cr–
2Al–2Hf alloy was investigated. Several conclusions can
be induced from this study.
Sr is inclined to enrich at the liquid/solid interface. The
restricting effect and more nucleation sites caused by Sr
addition will promote Nb
5Si3 phase to be reﬁned and
reduce the continuity of Nb 5Si3, which leads the average
thickness of Nb ss and Nb5Si3 phase to decrease. The inhi-
bition of c-Nb5Si3 phase formation is also found in Sr-
containing alloys. Room temperature fracture toughness of
Nb–15Si–24Ti–4Cr–2Al–2Hf alloy decreases after adding
minor Sr. The thickness of Nb
ss phase plays a crucial role
in determining the alloy’s toughness. Moreover, Nb ss
contributes more to the toughness than Nb 5Si3. The lack of
c-Nb5Si3 phase leads less energy to be absorbed, which
may not help the toughness of Sr-containing alloys. Some
factors, like the toughness of Nb ss matrix, the interfacial
area between Nbss and Nb5Si3 and the continuity of Nb5Si3
phase, are not the main reasons.
