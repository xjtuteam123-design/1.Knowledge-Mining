abstract
The microstructural evolution and room-temperature mechanical properties of Fe 50Al50 /C0 nNbn alloys
(n¼ 1, 3, 5, 7, and 9 at%) were investigated after solidi ﬁcation and subsequent heat treatment. For all the
compositions, the (Fe, Al) 2Nb Laves phase formed because of the incomplete solid solubility of Nb in the
Fe–Al-based phases and tended to develop an eutectic mixture with the Fe –Al-based phase. According to
the results of EDS analysis and microstructural investigations, the Nb concentration of the eutectic
composition was 9 at%, and the solid solubility of Nb in the B2-type Fe –Al-based phase was 3 at%. In
addition, the eutectic phase transition temperature was approximately 1265 °C. Compared with the as-
cast state, all the heat-treated alloys exhibited ultrahigh compressive strength and considerably in-
creased compressive fracture strains. The heat-treated hypoeutectic Fe 50Al47Nb3 alloy exhibited the
highest compressive strength and fracture strain of 3.02 GPa and 33.1%, respectively, and the eutectic
Fe
50Al41Nb9 alloy exhibited the lowest compressive strength and fracture strain of 2.66 GPa and 21.8%,
respectively, because of the absence of the comparably softer Fe –Al-based primary dendrites. The su-
perior mechanical properties of the heat-treated alloys were attributed to the bimodal distribution of the
microstructure, structural incoherency between the crystalline phases, and elimination of solidi ﬁcation
artifacts and lattice defects.

1. Introduction
Iron aluminides are considered suitable candidates for struc-
tural applications at high temperatures because of their out-
standing intermediate temperature strength and corrosion and
oxidation resistance [1,2]. In addition, their low density, relatively
high melting point, and low cost are the main advantages of these
materials over many commercial Fe-based alloys and stainless
steels [1–11 ]. However, their poor ductility and brittleness at room
temperature signi ﬁcantly hinder their fabricability and potential
applications, similar to other intermetallic compounds [4,8–14].
Doping iron aluminides with ternary alloying additions and ther-
mal treatments appear to be effective methods to increase their
room-temperature ductility and toughness [4,7,9,11 ].
However, ternary alloying element additions have generally
resulted in either enhanced plasticity with reduced strength for
solid-solution strengtheners [5,7,15–17] or improved strength
with reduced plasticity for precipitation hardeners [18–23].
Therefore, the development of an alloy that provides the best
combination of mechanical properties with large plasticity and
high strength at room temperature is desired. Fine-scaled eutectic
alloys are potential candidates that exhibit extraordinary me-
chanical properties at ambient temperatures because of their bi-
modal microstructure, which consists of relatively soft micro-
meter-scale dendrites and a hard ﬁne-scaled eutectic mixture.
These features are responsible for high plasticity and high
strength, respectively [24–29]. Among Fe –Al-based eutectic alloys,
Fe–Al–Nb alloys with such a bimodal solidi ﬁcation microstructure
are particularly attractive as the Fe –Al–Nb ternary phase diagram
contains several eutectic points as a result of the deep eutectic
valley between the C14 (Fe, Al)
2Nb Laves phase and A2 ( α-Fe) and
B2 FeAl phases [30–34].
Park et al. [24] reported that the as-cast hypoeutectic
Fe54Al40Nb6 alloy exhibits a combination of a compressive strength
of 2.24 GPa and 12% plasticity. In another study, these researchers
also reported an as-cast hypoeutectic Fe 56Al40Nb4 alloy exhibiting
a compressive strength of 2.32 GPa and plasticity of 1 1% [25].

that heat treatment might further improve the room-temperature
mechanical properties of Fe –Al–Nb alloys. In this study, we report

2. Experimental
The Fe
50Al50/C0 nNbn alloys (n ¼ 1, 3, 5, 7, and 9 at%) were pre-
pared by arc-melting using a non-consumable tungsten electrode
and a water-cooled copper tray in a high-purity argon atmosphere.
Samples of 10 –15 g were produced using high-purity constituent
elements (Fe ¼99.97 wt%, Al ¼ 99.9 wt%, and Nb ¼99.6 wt% purity).
To achieve higher composition homogeneity, the alloys were
melted several times in a zirconium-gettered argon atmosphere.
The weight loss during the arc-melting process was less than
0.5 wt%. Fe
50Al50/C0 nNbn alloy cylindrical rods with diameters of
3 mm and lengths of 150 mm were fabricated via suction casting
from the arc-melted ingots. The rods were homogenized at 900 °C
for 24 h in encapsulated and argon- ﬁlled quartz tubes followed by
an annealing heat treatment at 400 °C for 168 h.
X-ray diffraction (XRD) analyses were conducted using a
Rigaku D/Max-2200 PC diffractometer with Cu-K α radiation
(λ¼1.54056 Å) and an x-ray source operating voltage of 40 kV to
identify the phases present in the samples. The typical XRD scans
were performed in a 2 θ range of 25–100° and a scanning rate of 2 °/
min. The microstructures were examined using scanning electron
microscopy (SEM, JEOL JSM-6400 and FEI Nova Nano 430 ﬁeld
emission gun (FEG) coupled with energy-dispersive X-ray spec-
trometry (EDS)). The SEM samples were prepared using standard
metallographic techniques and etched with a solution containing
68 ml of glycerin, 16 ml of 70% HNO
3, and 16 ml of 40% HF. The
relative amounts (volume fraction) of the phases observed in the
metallographic examinations were calculated based on the sys-
tematic manual point count procedure described in ASTM E562
standards.
Differential Scanning Calorimetry (DSC) measurements were
performed with Setaram SETSYS 16/18 high temperature thermal
analyzer at a heating rate of 10 °C/min under constant argon ﬂow.
Prior to DSC measurements, the instrument was calibrated for a
wide range of temperatures and scanning rates by using high
purity standard elements of Al, Zn, Pb, Ag, Au and Ni. The accuracy
of the temperature calibration was found to be 71 °C.
The room temperature mechanical properties of as-cast and
heat-treated Fe
50Al50 /C0 nNbn alloys were investigated by means of
compression tests using an Instron 5582 universal testing machine
at a strain rate of 10 /C0 4 s/C0 1 according to ASTM E9-09 standard
(Standard Test Methods of Compression Testing of Metallic Mate-
rials at Room Temperature). Cylindrical specimens of 3 mm dia-
meter and 4.5 mm length were cut by spark erosion and any sur-
face layers were removed by grinding prior to testing. The yield
stress was determined as 0.2% proof stress in uniaxial
compression. Eight separate compressive stress-strain data were
averaged for each composition and condition. Samples having
deviations due to the artifacts such as voids and cracks were
excluded.
3. Results and discussions
3.1. Microstructure and phase analysis
The typical microstructures of the as-cast and heat-treated
Fe50Al50/C0 nNbn alloys are shown in Fig. 1. The microstructure of the
Fe50Al50/C0 nNbn (0o no9) alloys consisted of primary Fe –Al-based
dendrites and interdendritic eutectic regions ( Fig. 1 (a)–(d)). The
volume fraction of the eutectic increased with increasing Nb
content up to 9 at% Nb ( Table 1 ), at which point the eutectic
mixture underwent a phase instability, leading to the develop-
ment of an ultra ﬁne eutectic morphology free of primarily solidi-
ﬁed dendrites ( Fig. 1 (e)). Even a small addition of Nb (i.e., 1 at%)
into the B2-type ordered FeAl intermetallic compound resulted in
the formation of the C14-type (Fe, Al)
2Nb Laves phase (space
group 194, P63/mmc), which is one of the main constituent phases
of the eutectic microstructure with B2-type Fe –Al-based phase
(space group 221, Pm ̅3m), as determined by the XRD analyses
(Fig. 2). The relative intensity of the (Fe, Al) 2Nb peaks continuously
increased with increasing Nb content. This ﬁnding indicates that
the volume fraction of the (Fe, Al) 2Nb Laves phase increased,
whereas the volume fraction of the Fe –Al-based phase decreased
in the Fe 50Al50/C0 nNbn alloys with increasing Nb content. Thermal
analysis measurements of the heat-treated Fe 50Al50/C0 nNbn alloys
indicated that the endothermic reaction peak associated with the
B22A2 order-disorder phase transition observed in the binary
FeAl intermetallic compound was only present for the 1 at% Nb
addition ( Fig. 3 ). The appearance of this peak for the Fe 50Al49Nb1
composition clearly indicates the presence of a B2-type ordered
structure after annealing [35,36]. However, this peak eventually
vanished for increased Nb contents (n 41) because of the strong
eutectic reaction that occurred at approximately 1265 °C. More-
over, the other endothermic peak related to the liquidus ap-
proached the eutectic reaction peak and ﬁnally disappeared at
9 at% Nb, which implies that this composition represents the eu-
tectic composition of the alloy under consideration.
In alloys containing less than 5 at% Nb, the eutectic mixture
was distributed along the boundaries of the Fe –Al-based dendrites
as a hard skeleton. The microstructures of the hypoeutectic
Fe
50Al45Nb5 and Fe 50Al43Nb7 alloys were composed of a cellular
eutectic structure and Fe –Al-based dendrites. The amount of eu-
tectic cell boundaries of Fe 50Al43Nb7 alloy is distinctly higher than
that of Fe 50Al45Nb5 alloy. The eutectic cell boundaries are the last
solidiﬁcation regions and have the coarsest structure (i.e highest
lamellar spacing). For this reason, they seem to have different
contrast ( Fig. 1 (d)). The main difference between the cell center
and cell boundary is the thickness of the alternating layers. Within
the eutectic cell, the interlamellar spacing of (Fe, Al)
2Nb and Fe –Al
near the cell center was ﬁner than that in the cell boundary. For
this reason, they seem to have different contrast ( Fig. 1(d)). High-
magniﬁcation SEM investigations ( Fig. 4 ) of the eutectic for these
two compositions revealed that the eutectic structure was mainly
composed of a lamellar or rod-like (Fe, Al)
2Nb Laves phase (white).

3.2. Compression tests and fractographic analysis
Fig. 5 presents the room-temperature compressive stress –strain
curves of the investigated Fe 50Al50/C0 nNbn alloys. Compressive
stress–strain curves for the as-cast and heat-treated FeAl inter-
metallic compound are also provided for comparison. The un-
doped FeAl intermetallic compound exhibited limited compressive
plasticity and an ultrahigh yield strength. It is well known that
stoichiometric FeAl is the most brittle composition among Fe –Al-
based alloys with the lowest ductility and highest yield strength
[38–41]. The room-temperature brittleness of the FeAl inter-
metallic compound is directly related to the thermal vacancy
concentration; theoretical and experimental studies have de-
monstrated that the concentration of thermal vacancies increases
with increasing Al content and reaches a maximum near the
stoichiometric composition [42–45]. Long-time annealing at
400 °C eliminates thermal vacancies and softens the FeAl inter-
metallic compound [8]. Heat treatment reduces the compressive
yield strength from 1449 to 673 MPa and increases the compres-
sive fracture strain from 6.8% to 23.6% ( Table 3 ). Moreover, the
fracture mode also changed from intergranular decohesion to
transgranular cleavage after annealing ( Fig. 6 (a)).

room temperature. Casting ﬂaws including pores are believed to
act as crack nucleation sites [46] and lead to specimen failure
along the free surfaces of dendrites and spaces between den-

at room temperature [47]. Although the pore formation is mainly
attributed to the solidi ﬁcation shrinkage that arises from the
temperature difference between the solidus and liquidus/eutectic
temperatures [48,49], it appears likely that the volume fraction of
the eutectic liquid also affects the formation of the pores. If
the amount of eutectic liquid is suf ﬁcient, the eutectic liquid
tends to heal the pores during solidi ﬁcation [50]. For the as-cast
Fe50Al47Nb3 and Fe 50Al45Nb5 compositions, the solidi ﬁcation
temperature range was large, and the volume fraction of the
eutectic was small ( o 30%). Thus, the pore formation sensitivity
was higher for these two compositions.
When the amount of Nb reached 7 or 9 at%, the compressive
strength and room-temperature compressive plasticity con-
siderably increased. The higher volume fraction of the hard and
brittle (Fe, Al) 2Nb Laves phase and the length scale of the eutectic
mixture were responsible for the enhanced compressive strength.
However, the reasonably higher compressive plasticity of these
compositions despite the higher volume fraction of the hard Laves
phase compared with the Fe 50Al47Nb3 and Fe 50Al45Nb5 alloys was
mainly attributed to the absence of casting or solidi ﬁcation arti-
facts. The fracture surfaces of the as-cast near-eutectic Fe 50Al43Nb7
and eutectic Fe 50Al41Nb9 alloys revealed the presence of smooth
cleavage in the Fe–Al-based matrix and interface decohesion at the
(Fe–Al)/(Fe, Al) 2Nb ﬁbrous eutectics ( Fig. 6 (e) and (f)). Slight de-
bonding along the (Fe –Al)/(Fe, Al) 2Nb interface may play an im-
portant role in the improvement of the compressive ductility be-
cause the (Fe, Al) 2Nb Laves phase is unavoidably subjected to some
deformation during the pulling out of ﬁbers [51]. Ashby et al. [52]
reported that interface decohesion has a signi ﬁcant effect on the
improvement of fracture toughness, and Li et al. [37] noted that
interface decohesion plays an important role in increasing plasti-
city. Thus, the improved mechanical properties of the Fe 50Al43Nb7
and Fe 50Al41Nb9 alloys can be explained by the above micro-
structural and fractographic observations. Moreover, the me-
chanical properties of the 7 and 9 at% Nb containing as-cast alloys
were comparable to those for previously reported ternary Fe –Al–
Nb alloys [24, 25]. For example, Park et al. [24] reported that the
hypoeutectic Fe 54Al40Nb6 alloy exhibits a yield strength, com-
pressive strength, and plastic strain of 1.25 GPa, 2.24 GPa, and 12%,
respectively. These researchers also reported [25] that the hy-
poeutectic Fe56Al40Nb4 alloy exhibits a yield strength, compressive
strength, and plastic strain of 1.12 GPa, 2.32 GPa, and 1 1%,
respectively.

compressive plasticity [54,55]. The B2-type Fe –Al-based phase
(a¼0.29152 nm) and C14-type hexagonal (Fe, Al) 2Nb Laves phase
(a¼0.4947 nm and c ¼0.804 nm) [32] have large structural and
lattice parameter differences, leading to important structural
misﬁt.
Compared with other ternary intermetallic-phase-forming
elements (Zr, Hf, Ta, or Ti), Nb has the best positive effect on room-
temperature mechanical properties, especially for the ductility of
Fe–Al intermetallics. Among these transition elements, Nb, Ta, and
Ti exhibit relatively larger solid solubility in Fe –Al alloys compared
with Zr or Hf [56]. Thus, the volume fraction of the hard and brittle
ternary intermetallic phase is lower for Nb, Ta, and Ti additions.
Although Nb, Ta, and Ti have similar solid solubilities, Ta and Ti
have detrimental effects on the room-temperature ductility of Fe –
Al-based alloys in contrast to the positive effect of Nb addition
[20,29]. For the following reasons, Nb-containing Fe –Al inter-
metallics show good potential as an engineering material for
structural applications: (i) Nb has a solid-solution-strengthening
effect because of its limited but suf ﬁcient solid solubility [57], (ii)
strong work hardening is observed during plastic deformation,
and (iii) the room-temperature ductility is enhanced because of
their duplex structure.
4. Conclusions
The microstructure, room-temperature mechanical properties,
and deformation mechanisms of Fe 50Al50 /C0 nNbn alloys (n ¼1, 3, 5,
7, and 9 at%) were investigated after solidi ﬁcation and subsequent
heat treatment, and the following conclusions can be drawn:
/C15 All the investigated ternary alloys consisted of a relatively softer
Fe–Al-based phase and hard (Fe, Al) 2Nb Laves phase. The vo-
lume fraction of the Laves phase increased with increasing Nb
content.
/C15 Based on the results of EDS analysis and microstructural in-
vestigations, the Nb solubility of the Fe –Al-based phase and
eutectic composition were 3 and 9 at% Nb, respectively.
/C15 Among the as-cast alloys, the near-eutectic Fe 50Al43Nb7 and
eutectic Fe 50Al41Nb9 alloys exhibited higher compressive
strength and reasonable compressive fracture strain.
/C15 The room-temperature mechanical properties of the
Fe
50Al50 /C0 nNbn alloys were signi ﬁcantly improved after heat
treatment. All the heat-treated ternary alloys exhibited ultra-
high compressive strength and highly enhanced compressive
fracture strain.
/C15 Among the heat-treated alloys, the hypoeutectic Fe 50Al47Nb3
alloy exhibited the highest compressive strength and fracture
strain of 3.02 GPa and 33.1%, respectively. The eutectic
Fe50Al41Nb9 alloy exhibited the lowest compressive strength
and fracture strain of 2.66 GPa and 21.8%, respectively, because
of the absence of the relatively softer Fe –Al-based primary
dendrites.
/C15 The improved compressive plasticity of the heat-treated alloys
can be understood based on the elimination of solidi ﬁcation
artifacts and lattice defects, bimodal distribution of the micro-
structure, and structural incoherency between the crystalline
phases.
This paper shows promising properties of the Fe-Al-Nb alloys
for structural applications. It is predicted that the room tempera-
ture mechanical properties of these alloys can be further enhanced
by C or B addition which will result in the formation of carbide or
boride particles in the microstructure.
