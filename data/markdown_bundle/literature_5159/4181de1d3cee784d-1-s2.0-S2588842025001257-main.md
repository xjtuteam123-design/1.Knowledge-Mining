ABSTRACT
Understanding the mechanisms controlling topologically close-packed (TCP) phase nucleation and growth re -
mains crucial yet unclear for designing advanced Ni-based superalloys, which are widely needed for low-CO
2
- 
emitting advanced gas turbines. This study delivers the first combined atomic-scale and thermodynamic inves -
tigation of σ phase evolution, establishing a novel “ interfacial defect-composition-structure ” matching model for 
the morphological evolution of TCP phase. Atomic-resolution observation revealed unprecedented atomic mis -
arrangement appeared at the interface between the habitant planes of σ and γ ʹ phase near the interfacial ledges, 
creating coherent transition zones that accelerated σ phase coarsening. These nanoscale structural anomalies 
provided both compositional and structural similarity between the σ phase and γ ʹ phase, thereby facilitating the 
coarsening of the σ phase. Theoretical calculations further confirm the thermodynamic stability for such inter -
facial configuration formation, thus thermodynamically favoring coarsening kinetics. Crucially, stacking fault 
energy (SFE) of the γ ʹ phase is established as the governing parameter controlling coarsening kinetics. External 
stress further accelerates coarsening by promoting similar defect formation. These findings proposed 
composition-based SFE control as a strategic pathway to design degradation-resistant superalloys.
1. Introduction
In new-generation Ni-based single crystal superalloys (Ni-based SCs), 
the emergence of topologically close-packed phases (TCP phases) has 
garnered significant research interest recently [ 1 – 4 ]. Commonly 
observed TCP phases in SCs include the tetragonal σ phase, rhombohe -
dral μ phase and orthorhombic P phase [ 5 ], among which the σ phase 
frequently appears at mediate temperatures, acting as a precursor of μ 
phase and P phase [ 5 – 7 ]. Moreover, the precipitation of tetragonal σ 
phase has been widely documented across different alloy systems 
including steel, high entropy alloys and other binary/tertiary model 
alloys [ 8 – 11 ], thereby highlighting its significance in phase studies. The 
formation of TCP phases preferentially absorbs critical strengthening 
elements in the SCs including Re, W and Mo, causing significant 
degradation of high-temperature mechanical properties [ 12 – 14 ]. 
Consequently, deciphering the nucleation and growth mechanism of 
TCP phases is crucial in advanced SCs.
Currently, significant research efforts have been devoted to eluci -
dating the nucleation and growth mechanisms of TCP phases. Current 
understanding attributes the TCP phase nucleation primarily to the 
composition segregation along the γ ʹ / γ interface or crystal defects (i.e. 
dislocations and stacking faults) and the composition fluctuation in both 
γ ʹ and γ phase during heat exposure [ 3 , 15 – 17 ]. Characteristically, 
common TCP phases possess needle-like, lath-like, plate-like or blocky

morphologies after sufficient growth [ 18 – 20 ]. Therefore, the morpho -
logical evolution of TCP phases involves two aspects: longitudinal 
extension (for instance, needle-like and acicular shape) and lateral 
thickening (i.e., coarsening). Previous studies demonstrate that TCP 
particle elongation and morphological evolution are primarily driven by 
interfacial energy and lattice misfit minimization at TCP/matrix in -
terfaces [ 18 , 19 , 21 , 22 ]. The large-scale planar faults with refractory 
elemental segregation could also facilitate the growth of the TCP plate 
along the fault [ 17 ]. However, on the other hand, the mechanisms 
governing the coarsening and thickening kinetics remain remarkably 
understudied.
Conventional theory pointed out that the coarsening behavior is 
determined by the optimization of total interfacial energy [ 23 ]. In terms 
of microstructural evolution, the coarsening of TCP phases relies on the 
interfacial ledges and steps, which serve as preferential atomic attach -
ment sites for the growth process [ 24 – 26 ]. Moreover, the formation of 
interfacial ledges and steps was thermodynamically driven by the 
interfacial energy minimization [ 27 ]. Nevertheless, the exact coarsening 
mechanism and model of the TCP phase are still lacking in investigation, 
which brings the following factors to determine: 1) interfacial 
ledge-mediated microstructural evolution dynamics. 2) possible factors 
governing the coarsening kinetics of TCP particles. Coarsened TCP 
particles exhibit progressive loss of interfacial coherency, causing 
microcracks along the interface between the TCP phase and the matrix, 
leading to the final failure [ 16 , 28 ]. Hence, it is crucial to establish a 
comprehensive framework for the coarsening mechanism of TCP phases.
This study carried out the first combined atomic-scale experiment/ 
thermodynamic theory methodology to decode σ phase coarsening 
mechanisms through interface-specific analysis. Our integrated high- 
resolution scanning transmission electron microscopy (HR-STEM)/ 
density functional theory (DFT) approach revealed that a stacking fault- 
like atomic misarrangement occurred at the interface adjacent to the 
interfacial ledge. This phenomenon established the structural and 
elemental similarity between the γ ʹ phase and σ phase, facilitating the 
transformation from the γ ʹ phase to σ phase and accelerating the 
coarsening and thickening of σ phase. The energetic evolution induced 
by the structural evolution has also been quantitatively analyzed by 
first-principles calculations. By correlating atomic alignments with 
thermodynamic analysis, the long-standing issue of TCP growth kinetics 
in the γ ʹ phase was explored in detail. These multiscale findings estab -
lished a new “ interfacial defect-composition-structure ” matching model 
of TCP phase evolution, providing critical insights for predictive 
microstructure control in advanced superalloys.

3.2. The structural and elemental similarity
The structural similarity between the atomic misarrangement at the 
interface and the structure of the σ phase was analyzed in detail in 
Fig. 3 a – b. Such atomic-scale commensurability serves to reduce the 
activation energy for phase transformation, thereby facilitating the 
phase transformation process thermodynamically [ 32 , 38 ]. In Fig. 1 , the 
orientation relationship between the σ phase and γ ʹ phase was 
σ [-110]// γ ʹ [110] and σ (001)// γ ʹ (1 – 11). Therefore, the crystallographic 
similarity of the γ ʹ phase and σ phase on both sides of the interface was 
analyzed, viewing from the direction both parallel ( Fig. 3 a) and 
perpendicular ( Fig. 3 b) to the interface. In Fig. 3 a, atomic-resolution 
imaging revealed that the misarrangement region in the γ ʹ (1 – 11) 
exhibited well lattice correspondence with σ (001), which were marked 
by blue circles and green circles. On the other hand, the structural 
synchronicity persisted on γ ʹ (1 – 11) and σ (001) in Fig. 3 b when viewed 
along the interface-normal direction. It appeared that the formation of 
the atomic misarrangement in Fig. 2 emerged as a structural mediator 
between the γ ʹ phase and σ phase, establishing local crystallographic 
continuity that bridges the γ ʹ / σ interface and facilitates phase 
transformation.
The EDS results at the interface with high-resolution images were 
exhibited in Fig. 3 c – e. The structure and the inverse fast Fourier trans -
formation (FFT) image verified the existence of an atomic misarrange -
ment at the interface near an interfacial ledge. The EDS mapping results 
revealed the elemental segregation, such as slight segregation of Re and 
Mo compared to the γ ʹ phase away from the interface, near the atomic 
misarrangement at the interface. Notably, Z-contrast variations in 
Fig. 3 c and e in the γ ʹ phase near the interfacial ledge and the core region 
of the γ ʹ phase also hinted the enhanced refractory element concentra -
tions near the interface. The refractory element atoms tend to segregate 
along the crystal defects such as dislocation lines and stacking faults, 
which is energetically favorable [ 39 ]. The elemental segregation 
behavior along a stacking fault in the γ ʹ phase in A4 alloy was further 
validated in Fig.S4 , explaining the rationality of the slight elemental 
segregation at the atomic misarrangement which was similar to the 
stacking fault.
Furthermore, Fig. 3 f – h revealed the nanoscale chemical mapping of 
TCP phase peripheries by using APT methods. Thickness variations in 
the TCP plate correlated with the interfacial ledges [ 25 ] (rough location 
was marked by the yellow arrow in Fig. 3 h). The contents of 
TCP-forming elements, such as Re, Mo and W, near the interface ledge 
were higher than that inside the γ ʹ phase, confirming the EDS results in 
Fig. 3 c – e. Additionally, no segregation of TCP forming elements at the 
interface on the TCP side. Such phenomenon was different from the 
elemental depletion at the plain interface [ 15 , 40 ], indicating that the 
elemental depletion did not exist neat the ledges and the absence of 
depletion zones enabled continuous growth with less kinetic limitations 
from long-range diffusion.

4.2. The effects of stacking fault energy on the coarsening of TCP particle
Structurally analogous to γ ʹ phase stacking faults, the interfacial 
disarrangement suggests a potential coupling between stacking fault 
energy (SFE) and TCP particle coarsening kinetics. Comparative 
compositional analysis of the γ ʹ phase in A1 and A2 alloy in Fig.S5
revealed that the contents of Ta, Mo and Re were higher in A2, while the 
contents of W and Cr were slightly higher in A1 alloy. The SFE in the γ ʹ 
phase of the A1 alloy was slightly lower than that in A2 according to the 
analysis in Supplementary Note 1, which was consistent with the 
accelerated TCP coarsening in A1 alloy.
Systematic validation of TCP coarsening patterns was extended to A3 
and A4 alloys with different SFE, as experimentally validated in Fig. 6 a. 
The SFE in the γ ʹ phase is reverse proportional to the width of the 
stacking faults [ 53 ], indicating that the SFE in the γ ʹ phase was lower in 
A4 alloy. Moreover, higher amounts of blocky and rod-like TCP phases 
revealed that the coarsening of TCP particles was more significant in A4 
alloy. It was noteworthy that the refractory element contents in the 
investigated alloys were different, which was a crucial factor influencing 
the coarsening and growing behavior. Therefore, to exclude the effects 
of the contents of refractory elements and emphasize the role of the 
stacking fault energy, the thickness or width of TCP particles was 
measured in different alloy systems and compared in Fig. 7 b. 
Cross-system analysis of TCP particle dimensions revealed a universal 
anti-correlation between SFE (calculated via JMatPro) and coarsening 
propensity of TCP phases. Elevating the SFE of the γ ʹ phase through 
strategic alloying (e.g., optimizing Mo, Ta, Cr content) might emerge as 
a promising strategy to suppress defect formation at the TCP/ γ ʹ interface 
and impede TCP coarsening, thereby enhancing microstructural

4.4. The coarsening mechanisms facilitated by the atomic misarrangement
Fig. 8 a schematically illustrated the mechanisms of the coarsening 
behavior aided by the interfacial atomic misarrangement. The atomic 
rearrangement process was elucidated through sequential analysis in 
Fig. 8 b – d. First, the atomic misarrangement formed at the region of the 
plane 1 and 2 ( Fig. 8 c), initiated by the strain induced by the interfacial 
ledges (The ledges were not shown in Fig. 8 c). The planar fault area 
attracted TCP-forming elements to further provide the chemical driving 
force for the TCP phase transformation. Moreover, crystallographic 
analysis of the ledge-adjacent interface in Fig. 8 d revealed progressive 
structural evolution from the crystal plane 2 in the γ ʹ phase to the σ (002) 
plane. Possible mechanistic interpretations of such transformation 
pathway and structural analysis were in the Supplementary Note 2 and 
Fig.S9 . The γ ʹ to σ phase transition initiated upon achieving structural 
and compositional similarity between γ ʹ and σ phases, ultimately 
resulting in σ phase plate thickening ( Fig. 8 d).
σ phase is considered as a precursor of the precipitation of μ and P 
phase, especially at elevated temperatures where μ and P phase exhibit 
superior thermodynamic stability [ 5 ]. Our study particularly addressed 
the coarsening process of the σ phase. The formation of interfacial 
atomic misarrangements should not promote the transformation of the 
TCP phase, but facilitate the thickening and coarsening of the σ phase. 
The phase transformation of TCP phases is typically assisted by the de -
fects inside the TCP particle [ 7 , 63 ]. The direct coarsening kinetics of μ 
and P phase particles might be different, which requires more experi -
ments and calculations. According to our findings, the coarsening of TCP 
particles might be related to the defect energy in the γ ʹ phase (i.e., 
stacking fault energy or anti-phase boundary energy). Therefore, alloy 
design may inhibit TCP particle coarsening by elevating the stacking 
fault energy in the γ ʹ phase, such as moderately decreasing the contents 
of Co and Cr, suppressing the formation of defects adjacent to the 
interface and retarding the coarsening of TCP particles.
5. Conclusion
In summary, the coarsening mechanism facilitated by the interfacial 
atomic misarrangement near the interfacial ledges was investigated, 
establishing a defect-mediated interfacial configuration as the key 
mechanism governing TCP phase coarsening in Ni-based superalloys. 
This study has revealed the following insights. 
(1) Defect-mediated σ / γ ʹ interfacial restructuring were first experi -
mentally identified, where an atomic misarrangement near the 
interfacial ledges created a TCP-similar structure with matching 
structural configurations and resembled elemental segregation. 
This interfacial structure feature aided the morphological evo -
lution of σ phase in the γ ʹ phase.
(2) Quantitative validation via DFT calculation indicated that the 
formation of such atomic misarrangement led to the decrease of 
interfacial systematic energy, which was thermodynamically 
favorable to the coarsening process.
(3) Stacking fault energy was innovatively recognized as a governing 
parameter determining the TCP particle coarsening kinetics.
(4) External stresses might aid the coarsening of TCP particles at 
certain temperatures and stresses.
(5) An atomic coarsening model of TCP particle induced by the 
interfacial atomic misarrangement was established based on 
high-resolution observation and theoretical calculation.
These findings fundamentally redefine our understanding of TCP 
phase coarsening dynamics, proposing a novel alloy design paradigm. 
By strategically modulating SFE and interfacial defect through compo -
sition optimization, a revolutionary approach to develop degradation- 
resistant superalloys for next-generation clean energy systems could 
be developed.
