ABSTRACT 
Only few medium: and high-strength aluminium alloys can be processed by Laser Powder Bed Fusion without 
forming solidification cracks. This constraint limits the diffusion of this technology in many industrial fields, 
including aerospace and motorsport sectors. In this study, a novel high-strength aluminium alloy for Laser 
Powder Bed Fusion was designed and its solidification behavior, microstructure and mechanical performance 
were investigated. The results were compared with those achieved by processing the high-strength A20X alloy 
processed with the same technology. The alloy was designed based on the chemical composition of the widely 
used 2618 Al alloy, a conventional high strength Al – Cu – Mg alloy for high temperature applications. The 
chemical composition of the 2618 alloy was modified by adding Ti and B, which form TiB
2 
particles that act as 
nuclei for the solidification of primary α -Al grains. The resulting microstructure made of equiaxed grains 
revealed resistant to hot cracking. A20X and 2618-modified alloys produced with optimized parameters featured 
relative densities higher than 99,7% and crack-free microstructures. The A20X-T6 showed yield strength and 
ultimate tensile strength of 428 MPa and 485 MPa, respectively, while the modified 2618-T6 revealed upper 
yield strength and ultimate tensile strength of 370 MPa and 468 MPa, respectively. The two alloys also showed a 
remarkably high strength at 150 
◦
C and 250 
◦
C, exceeding the typical strength values of the 2618 alloy produced 
by forging. 
1. Introduction 
Additive Manufacturing (AM) is believed to be a game-changer 
technology in the manufacturing industry due to its ability to produce 
complex-shape components with small amount of waste. Several AM 
technologies have been developed during recent years, the most wide -
spread for prototypes and small series production is the Laser Powder 
Bed Fusion (L-PBF) [ 1 ]. In L-PBF systems, a metallic powder bed is 
selectively melted by a laser beam, involving formation and rapid so -
lidification of tiny melt pools. Nowadays, although there is a large de -
mand for high-performance Al alloys with different properties, the types 
of available powder for L-PBF is still limited. Indeed, most of standard 
wrought Al alloys that are conventionally used for high-demanding 
applications are poorly processable by L-PBF. In particular, the alloys 
belonging to the 2xxx, 6xxx and 7xxx series have severe susceptibility to 
hot cracking [ 2 – 4 ]. 
Hot cracking is a metallurgical phenomenon that can occur in cast -
ing, welding and additive manufacturing of several metallic alloys. 
Specifically, during final stage of solidification, the thermal shrinkage 
promotes the build-up of tensile stresses in the semi-solid alloy. This 
phenomenon can cause the nucleation and consequent propagation of 
cracks along grain boundaries [ 5 ]. 
For a wider diffusion of beam-based additive manufacturing tech -
nologies, the design and development of high-strength Al alloys with 
good L-PBF processability is considered as one of the main challenges to 
overcome [ 6 ]. 
Several studies about the design of high-strength Al alloys having 
good processability by L-PBF have been recently published. In our pre -
vious work, we added Ti and B to the chemical composition of the EN 
AW 2618 alloy to obtain a crack-free fully-dense material with enhanced 
mechanical properties at high temperature [ 7 ]. In addition, some of the 
authors of this work, showed that the promotion of an eutectic reaction 
at the end of the solidification of the EN AW 7068 Al alloy leads to a 
significant reduction of hot cracks [ 8 ]. Montero Sistiaga et al. tailored 
the chemical composition of the EN AW 7075 Al alloy by alloying with 4 
wt% Si, improving the L-PBF processability of the material [ 4 ]. Zhang 
et al. investigated the effect of the addition of Zr on the solidification 
behavior of an Al – Cu – Mg alloy processed by L-PBF. They showed that Zr

2xxx Al alloy with improved L-PBF processability. Specifically, in the 
present work we are presenting the results achieved with a novel Al alloy 
for high temperature applications with reduced amount of Ti and B, 
namely 2,3 wt% and 0,8 wt% respectively. This study was driven by the 
fact that high amount of Ti and B in Al alloys is responsible for a number 
of drawbacks during the gas atomization of powder, including nozzle 
clogging. Thus, a lower content of such alloying elements eases the 
powder production and guarantees a more robust and reproducible 
process. The tensile properties of the novel alloy at room and high 
temperatures were compared with those previously published on the EN 
AW 2618 with higher content of Ti and B and with original results ob -
tained by testing the A20X, a commercial Al alloy for high temperature 
application with good L-PBF processability. Simulations of the solidifi -
cation curves of the alloys were computed by the Scheil method and the 
produced microstructures were investigated by electron microscopy and 
X-ray diffraction. The response of the alloys to artificial aging was 
investigated starting from the solution treated and as built conditions.

4. Discussion 
The main goal of the manuscript is the investigation of the effect of 
TiB
2 
particles to promote heterogeneous nucleation of grains and to 
avoid crack formation in Al – Cu – Mg alloys, usually very prone to solid -
ification cracking when welded or subjected to L-PBF processes [ 2 , 7 , 9 ], 
and to investigate in depth the metallurgical phenomena related to the 
two novel alloys. We believe that slight differences in relative density 
(99,76% vs. 99,85% for the 2618Ti/B-L and A20X alloy, respectively) 
should not have a strong effect on alloy response to heat treatments and 
on final mechanical (static) properties. Moreover, the T6 heat treatment 
route that includes a solution treatment at high temperature should 
reduce/eliminate the influence of different process parameters on final 
material properties.

leading to the capability of the Al – Si alloys to better respond to direct 
aging.

The addition of Ti and B positively affects the L-PBF processability of 
the 2618 Al alloy. TiB

followed artificial aging.
