abstract
A ferritic-austenitic duplex matrix alloy strengthened by NiAl and nanosized oxides was developed by
the combination of computational-aided alloy design and the master alloy approach. The master alloy
route promotes the solid solution and the diffusion-homogenization of Mo in the matrix. The small
lattice mis ﬁt (0.42%) between a-Fe matrix and b' precipitates stabilizes the coherent interface by
lowering the elastic strain energy, favouring near-spherical particle morphology and reducing the driving
force for competitive coarsening. The duplex matrix microstructure contains mainly submicron-sized
(0.2e0.8 mm) ferritic grains with a large volume fraction of b' precipitates and a small amount of
coarse grained austenite. A high population of oxide dispersion with particle size of 10 e30 nm distrib-
uted uniformly in the matrix. The alloy exhibits enhanced strength and improved ductility both at room
temperature and elevated temperature. The compressive strength of the heat treated sample is as high as
2053 MPa, 964 MPa and 672 MPa at room temperature, 650
/C14 C and 700 /C14 C, respectively. The compressive
ductility (strain to failure) of the alloy is about 12% at room temperature.

1. Introduction
Intermetallic compound (NiAl-type) strengthened ferritic alloys
offer advantages with respect to high-temperature strength, ther-
mal conductivity, thermal expansion, oxidation resistance and low-
cost alloying elements [ 1e3]. A lot of work has been undertaken to
study the methods to improve the stability of NiAl precipitates
(designated as bʹ), and thus increase the high temperature me-
chanical properties of the ferritic alloys with the aim to extend the
application temperature of ferritic alloys to above 600 /C14 C[ 4,5]. The
ferritic alloys strengthened by B2 order NiAl phase has demon-
strated that high volume fraction of b' phase has low coarsening
rate [6,7]. However, it still suffers from the lack of high temperature
creep strength with further increase of service temperature [ 8,9].
In order to further improve the mechanical properties of ferritic
alloys, several multiphase strengthening strategies were devel-
oped, such as NiAl/Ni 2AlTi [ 10e12], NiAl/Ni 2AlMn [ 13], NiAl/Cu
[14,15], NiAl/laves phase [16,17], NiAl/Ni3Ti [18] and NiAl/nanosized
oxides [19]. Among those advanced ferritic alloys, oxide dispersion
strengthened (ODS) alloys are promising materials with a potential
to be used at elevated temperatures due to the addition of
extremely thermally stable oxide particle dispersion into the
ferritic matrix [ 20,21]. Therefore, the ferritic alloys which are
strengthened on one hand by ﬁne dispersions of nanosized oxides
and on the other hand by intermetallic NiAl phase offer potential
for a signi ﬁcant strength increment at enhanced operating tem-
peratures. It is expected that b' phase contributes to the enhance-
ment of intermediate temperature strength by mechanisms such as
the creation of antiphase boundaries that effectively impede
dislocation motion, while the oxide dispersion would have added
strengthening effect on the existing ferritic ODS alloys at higher
temperature [22].
ODS alloy can be fabricated by mechanical alloying [22], reactive
ball milling [ 23], reactive spark plasma sintering [ 24,25] and
combustion synthesis [ 26]. Among those preparation methods,
mechanical alloying is the most widely used technique to fabricate
ODS alloys, which is a lengthy process in order to achieve fully solid
solution of alloying elements in the matrix. It is demonstrated in
our previous work that a lot of Mo-rich phase remained in the
microstructure due to the much lower diffusivity of Mo in a-Fe, and

excessive intake of impurity oxygen and the change of precipitation
behavior of b' precipitates increase with prolonging milling time
due to the high afﬁnity of oxygen by the active elements (Al and Cr).
In view of above problems, master alloy method is utilized. The
master alloy technique is a signi ﬁcantly cost-bene ﬁt process, in
which the master alloy powders and the metal matrix powder are
blended together and sintered to form the alloy with homogenous
composition. The potential advantages of adopting the master alloy
approach can be seen from the aspects of promoting the diffusion of
alloying elements, controlling impurity oxygen and improving
homogenization of alloying elements. Previous work have proved
that master alloy method is an effective technique for the prepa-
ration of high performance low alloy steels [27], stainless steels [28]
and superalloys [ 29,30]. Up till now, there has been little reports
about the fabrication of ODS alloys by master alloy route.
The relative low ductility limit the application of the ferritic
alloy with a high population of b' phase [ 31,32]. Recently, an
attractive viewpoint of use the austenite as a secondary phase in
ferritic matrix can result in optimization of the strength and
ductility due to the occurrence of transformation-induced plasticity
[33] or twinning-induced plasticity [34]. So it may provide a worthy
prospective to balance the strength and the ductility of the high
performance ferritic alloy.
In this work, a ODS alloy with ferritic-austenitic duplex matrix
and NiAl precipitation was designed. With the aim to explore the
possibility of balance the strength and the ductility of NiAl-
strengthened ferritic ODS alloy by inducing the austenite as a
secondary phase. A computational-aided alloy design approach
was employed to guide the alloy design, and the alloy was fabri-
cated via master alloy approach. Phase constitutions, microstruc-
tural characteristics and mechanical property of the alloy were
characterized by experimental studies. This information would be
valuable not only to the design of alloying composition, but also to
the microstructural control.
2. Computational-aided alloy design
The purpose of this work is to obtain a ferritic-austenite dual-
phase ODS alloy with high volume fraction of NiAl-type (
b0) phase.
The studied alloy is based on the Fe eNieAleCreMo system. Cr is
mostly distributed in the ferritic phase, and 10 wt% of Cr was added
for oxidation resistance. The alloying of Mo is for the purpose of
solid-solution strengthening of the ferrite matrix. Moreover, the
lattice parameters of the body-centered-cubic (BCC) a-Fe and B2-
ordered NiAl phase are 0.28665 nm and 0.28864 nm respectively
and the lattice mis ﬁt between a-Fe and B2-ordered NiAl is about
0.69% [ 32]. The lattice parameter disregistry between matrix and
precipitate can be reduced by proper addition content of Mo [ 4].
The Mo content in the alloy is set to be 3.4 wt%.
The contents of Ni and Al determines the number of NiAl-type
(b0) phase and the ductility of ferritic alloys. Teng and Liu et al.
[31] reported that the ductility of FBB series alloys (with 10 wt%Ni
and 3-10 wt%Al) were signi ﬁcant in ﬂuenced by Al contents:
Reducing the Al concentration from 10% to 3% improves the failure
bending strain from 0.5% to more than 5%. Stallybrass and Sauthoff
[32] reported that the Fe-10 wt%Cr-21.5 wt%Ni-12.5 wt%Al alloys
which have with a high volume fraction (0.37) of b0 phase exhibited
a bending ductility less than 1.5% even at temperatures higher than
10 0 0/C14 C. It seems like that higher levels of (Ni þ Al) can offer higher
volume fraction of NiAl-type ( b0) phase and provide enhanced
mechanical properties at elevated temperature. Furthermore, the
contents of Ni and Al in ﬂuence phase constitutions of the matrix
signiﬁcantly. It is well known that Ni is an austenite stabilizing
element, on the contrary, Al can stabilize ferrite. Austenite may
dissolve some of the Ni elements, reducing the volume fraction of
NiAl-type ( b0) phase for precipitation strengthening. On the other
hand, a certain volume fraction of highly dispersed austenite may
be bene ﬁcial for plasticity and fracture toughness [ 35]. Therefore,
the proportion of Ni and Al determine the microstructure and the
mechanical property of the ferritic alloy.
In order to clarify the in ﬂuence of Ni and Al on the micro-
structure of the alloy, thermodynamic calculations of the phase
diagrams were employed to design the phase constitutions of the
alloy. The calculated isothermal section of pseudo-ternary
FeeNieAl phase diagrams with the addition of 10 wt% Cr and
3.4 wt% Mo for 1200, 1 100, 900, 800 /C14 C are shown in Fig. 1(a)-(d),
respectively. According to Stallybrass and Sauthoff's work, a high
volume fraction of b0 phase(0.37) in ferritic alloy contributes greatly
to the high temperature performance [36]. Therefore, the content of
Ni is set to be 21.5 wt%. It can be seen isoconcentration line (red
dash line) from Fig. 1 (a) that phase constitutions of alloys were
inﬂuenced by Al content signi ﬁcantly. As the Al content increases,
the equilibrium phase constitution of the alloys is pure g-Fe, (aþg),
pure a,( aþb0), respectively. Although the amount of b0 increases
with the Al content, the alloy should be a pure ferrite at a sol-
utionizing temperature of 1200 /C14 C. In order to get pure ferrite at
solutionizing temperature, the Al content of the alloy can be read
from Fig. 1(a), should from 8 wt% to 1 1 wt%. Considering the high
volume fraction of b0 phase and the compositional deviation during
material preparation, this study set content of Al to be 10 wt%.
Based on all above design guidance, the ﬁnal optimal compo-
sition is derived as Fe-10 wt%Cr-21.5 wt%Ni-10 wt%Al-3.4 wt%Mo-
0.3 wt%Y2O3. The red dot in Fig. 1 indicated the nominal composi-
tion of the alloy. It can be noticed that when temperature decreased
to 1100/C14 C (see in Fig. 1 (b)), the studied alloy consists of a-Fe phase
and NiAl ( b0) phase, which indicate that b0 phase precipitates from
the a-Fe phase as the temperature decreasing. Fig. 1(c)e(d) show
that the alloy is consist with a-Fe, g-Fe and NiAl (b0) phase at 800 /C14 C
and 900 /C14 C, which indicated that some of the ferrite transformed to
austenite. It is expected that NiAl-strengthened ferritic-austenitic
dual-phase ODS alloy can be obtained after a proper heat
treatment.
Fig. 2 shows the calculated equilibrium phase diagram of the Fe-
10 wt%Cr-21.5 wt%Ni-10 wt%Al-3.4 wt%Mo alloy. It is seen that the
initial melting temperature of the master alloy is as high as 1380 /C14 C.
The phase constitution of the alloy is only ferrite from 1 136 /C14 Ct o
1380 /C14 C. NiAl( b0) phase precipitate from ferritic matrix when the
temperature below 1 136 /C14 C and the fraction of NiAl( b0) phase in-
creases with decreasing temperature. It is noted that austenite is
formed at 1040 /C14 C and transforms to ferrite at 630 /C14 C and the alloy
has peak austenite content (~24 wt%) at 853 /C14 C. It is also can be seen
from Fig. 3 that Cr and Mo rich bcc phase generate below 630 /C14 C,
which will reduce the mechanical property. In order to obtain the
dispersed austenite in the ferritic matrix and to suppress the for-
mation of Cr and Mo rich phase, we quench the alloy by water after
aging isothermally at 700 /C14 C.

4. Results and discussion
4.1. Master alloy powder
Master alloy is the concentrated source of alloying elements. The
aim of the use of master alloy is to promote the alloying elements
homogenization and to prevent excess oxygen contamination, but
not to enhance densi ﬁcation through transient liquid phase sin-
tering. The partially melted master alloy is undesirable because it
adversely affect the re ﬁnement of oxide dispersion. The designed
master alloy contains Al, Cr and Mo, and the molar ratio of these
alloying elements is the same as that studied ferritic-austenitic
duplex matrix ODS alloy. Fig. 3 shows the calculated phase dia-
gram of the Fe-20.62 wt%Al-20.62 wt%Cr-7.01 wt%Mo master alloy.
It is seen that the master alloy consists with a-Fe and FeAl at room
temperature. The initial melting temperature of the master alloy is
as high as 1300 /C14 C, which makes it suitable for solid phase sintering.
The master alloy consists only a-Fe at the sintering temperature of
10 0 0/C14 C, indicating that the alloying elements solid solute in a-Fe
matrix.
Fig. 4 shows the backscattered electron (BSE) imaging of the
master alloy ingot. The dark regions (marked by A) contains higher
content of Al (31.7 wt%) and the grey regions (marked by B) is rich
in Cr (20.4 wt%) and Mo (7.23 wt%). These regions are correspond to
FeeAl and a-Fe phases, respectively.
Fig. 5 shows XRD spectrum of the master alloy powder. The
diffraction peaks around 44.64/C14 , 65.02/C14 and 82.33/C14 correspond to a-
Fe and the fundamental FeAl lines (h þ k þ l ¼ even). The diffraction
peaks around 30.82 /C14 , 54.86 /C14 and 73.01/C14 originate from supperlat-
tice lines (h þ k þ l ¼ odd), and these superlattice lines suggest that
ordered B2 body-centered cubic structure (bcc) FeAl phase was
formed in master alloy ingot [ 37]. Combined BSE, XRD and
computational phase diagram results, it is con ﬁrmed that the
master alloy powder is consisted with a-Fe and FeAl phases.
The high concentration of alloying elements in the matrix makes
the master alloy brittle, which can be crushed into ﬁne powder. The
morphology of the prepared master alloy powder is shown in Fig. 6.
The master alloy powder exhibits polyhedral morphology due to
mechanical crushing. The average particle size of the master alloy
powder was about 10 e80 mm, and the oxygen content of the
powder is 800 ppm.

temperature.
The room temperature compressive fracture morphology of the
ferritic-austenitic duplex matrix ODS alloy is presented in Fig. 14.I t
can be seen from Fig. 14(a) and (b) that the fracture surface consists
of quasi-cleavage facets and dimple-like structures which indicated
that plastic deformation occurred before fracture. Both NiAl type
(b') precipitation phase and nano-sized oxides is effective to
strengthen alloy by impeding the motion of the dislocations.
However, the pilling up of dislocations at the phase boundary be-
tween matrix and second phases promote the generation of critical
cracks, which induced fracture of alloys. It is seen from Fig. 14 (b)
and (c) that some particles still remains on the fracture surface.
That's might be the main reason for the fracture of the alloy at room
temperature. However, the contribution and mechanism of
austenite toughening need to be further investigated.
5. Conclusions
The ODSþbʹ strengthened ferritic-austenitic duplex matrix alloy
was fabricated via a master alloy approach for high temperature
application. The microstructures and compressive properties of the
alloy were examined. The conclusions drawn in this study are given
below:
(1) A high strength ODS þbʹ strengthened ferritic-austenitic
duplex matrix alloy was developed in the
FeeCreNieAleMo alloy system through a computational-
aided design approach.
(2) Master alloy approach proves to a pronounced effect on a
composition homogenization of the NiAl-strengthened
ferritic-austenitic duplex matrix ODS alloy.
(3) Microstructural characterization results indicated that
ferritic-austenitic duplex matrix alloy with large volume
fractions of precipitated intermetallic NiAl and dispersed
nanosized oxide particles was obtained. The near spherical
NiAl precipitation phase with a mean size of around 80 nm
dispersed in the ferrite region. The small oxide dispersion
dispersed in the matrix with a particle size of 10 e30 nm.
(4) By inducing nanosized oxide dispersion and austenite phase,
the ODS þbʹ strengthened ferritic-austenitic duplex matrix
alloy exhibits enhanced strength and improved ductility both
at room temperature and elevated temperature. The
compressive strength of the heat treated sample is as high as
2053 MPa, 964 MPa and 672 MPa at room temperature,
650 /C14 C and 700 /C14 C, respectively. The three-point-bending
ductility (strain to failure) of the alloy is about 4.6% room
temperature.
