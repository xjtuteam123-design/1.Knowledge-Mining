Keywords: Cr 7C3, Cr 3C2, compressive strength, hardness, wear resistance, thermal conductivity 
 
Abstract. Ni 3Al-based alloys possess good oxidation resistance, moderate room and high 
temperature strength and ductility. Introduction of Cr-carbide particles through a solidification route 
is attempted to provide higher hardness and wear resistance. The mechanical and physical 
properties are measured at room temperature for several alloys with various carbon concentrations 
up to 2.0 wt.%. Hardness and wear resistance as well as compressive strength increase with 
increasing carbon concentration, while bend ductility decreases. Adhesion between carbides and 
matrix phase is good, and cracks propagate mainly through carbides. The crystallographic 
orientation relationships between constituent phases are also attempted using electron 
back-scattering diffraction (EBSD) technique. The thermal conductivity is found to be less sensitive 
to the alloy composition. 
Introduction 
 Ni 3Al-based alloys have been investigated in views of materials science as well as 
engineering applications. It is because these alloys show a positive temperature dependence of 
strength [1, 2], often called an anomalous deformation behavior, and also show a good oxidation 
resistance due to a large amount of Al. As the micro-alloying of boron improves ductility 
dramatically [3,4], the alloys have been attempted to apply for practical uses such as hot rolling for 
the steel making [5] or a carrier in the furnace for heat treatment [6]. 
 For the alloy development, strength and hardness is primarily improved by applying 
solution hardening and dispersion hardening mechanisms through the microstructural control. In 
Ni3Al alloys several attempts have been reported by introducing Cr 23 C6-type carbides and other 
neighboring intermetallic phases such as B2-NiBe phase [7,8]. In order to develop high strength 
alloys for the substitution of high speed steels and cermets, carbide dispersion is a promising 
method. In the previous study Ni 3Al-based IC218 alloy was modified by addition of carbon to 
introduce various kinds of carbides, and the equilibrium 
phase relationship and the solidification paths were 
revealed [9]. In the present study, micro-vickers 
hardness, compressive strength and bend ductility at 
room temperature are investigated with alloys 
containing various amount of carbon ranging up to 2.0 
wt.% to introduce different amount of carbides such as 
Cr 7 C3 type and/or Cr 3C2 type carbides. The thermal 
conductivity which is important for high temperature 
applications was also investigated.

Experimental Procedures 
 The nominal compositions of alloys are listed in Table 1. The alloys in the present study 
are based on IC218 alloy with different amount of carbon. Small amount of boron was also added to 
improve their room temperature ductility. All of the specimens were prepared by conventional 
melting and casting method heated by induction heater in an Ar atmosphere. Details are described 
elsewhere [9]. Microstructure observation by SEM (Scanning Electron Microscopy) was carried out 
on polished samples. 
 All the tests were conducted at room temperature. Micro-vickers hardness tests were 
carried out with a load of 300 g for 30 s. The compression specimens with 3 x 3 x 6 mm 3 in 
dimension were cut from alloy ingots by a wheel cutter and formed by polishing with emery papers. 
An Instron-type testing machine was used for compression tests with an initial strain rate of 2 x 10 -4 
s-1. Bend ductility was examined with samples having dimensions of about 2 mm in thickness and 6 
mm in width. Surfaces of samples were prepared by polishing with emery papers up to #1200. 
Four-point bending tests with point distance of 7 mm were performed with a cross-head speed of 
0.1 mm/min. The wear specimens with 5 x 5 x 10 mm 3 in dimension were subjected to a 
Block-on-Disc type wear tester under the rotating movement (a sliding velocity: 1 m/s, an initial 
applied stress: 1 MPa, a sliding distance: 1 km) with a high carbon tool steel counterdisc (SKD11, 
12Cr-Mo-V , 460HV). The investigation of the crystallographic orientation relationships between 
constituent phases are also attempted using a field emission SEM and electron back-scattering 
diffraction (EBSD) technique. For the measurement of thermal conductivity of samples, a laser 
flash method was employed with a specimen size of about 10 mm in diameter and 1 mm in 
thickness. 
Results and Discussion 
 Figure 1 shows the microstructure of alloys. Grains of alloys except for Alloy A are 
surrounded by dark carbides, and the grains are composed of bright and gray regions, each of which 
corresponds to Ni solid solution γ and Ni 3Al γ’, respectively. According to the previous study [9] the 
carbide phase in Alloy B is mainly Cr 7 C3, while the carbides in Alloys C and D are mainly Cr 3C2. 
The grain size of matrix phase and dispersed carbides ranges 100 - 200 µm and 5 - 20 µm, 
respectively, while large carbides are found in Alloy D. The amount of carbides seemingly increases 
with increasing the carbon content. 
Figure 2 shows the distribution of micro-vickers hardness of alloys. It is obvious that with 
increasing carbon concentration, fraction of high hardness values, i.e., numbers of the position 
which show high hardness, increases as was reported by Tian et al.[7]. High values for 
micro-vickers hardness may correspond to the carbides because the size of indentation is

1000 and 1400 HV , and the values for Cr 23 C6 and Cr 7 C3 are reported to be 1000 HV and 1600 HV ,

almost the same level in Alloys C and D. 
This may imply the composition and/or 
microstructure of the metallic matrix among 
the present alloys are almost identical. 
Figure 3 shows the carbon 
concentration dependence of the 0.2% flow 
stress of alloys. The stress increases from 
300 MPa to 900 MPa by carbon addition. 
The strength of Alloy A is about half of the 
reported value for IC218 [12], due to the 
difference of grain size. On the other hand, 
the present results (Alloys A and B) are 
higher than those of Ni-19Al-4Cr(at.%) and 
Ni-19Al-4Cr- 0.5C(at.%) alloys by Tian et al. 
[7], respectively. The carbon concentration 
dependence of 0.2% flow stress is much 
larger than that of the hardness value. The 
increase in the strength is also attributed to 
the strengthening by carbide dispersion. 
However, the uniform plastic deformation of 
metallic grains during compression test 
might be suppressed by neighboring carbides 
in the manner of the metal-matrix composite 
with hard particles. 
 Figure 4 indicates the bend ductility 
as a function of carbon concentration of 
alloys. Bending ductility was estimated by a 
curvature of the bend specimen at fracture. 
Alloy A, the carbide-free alloy, shows an 
excellent ductility caused by an addition of 
boron. Alloys B and C are less ductile but 
still show some amount of ductility. Alloy D 
has almost no ductility. The bend ductility 
decreases with increasing carbon 
concentration. Figure 5 shows the 
microstructure of the tension side of the bend 
specimens near the fracture edge. It is clear 
that cracks propagate within the carbides, 
and the inter-phase cracking between 
carbides and matrix are scarcely observed.

Figure 6 shows the carbon concentration 
dependence of the wear resistance of alloys, 
together with the result of Inconel 625. The 
weight-loss decreases with increasing carbon

weight-loss mainly depends on the amount of 
carbide [13], and the similar tendency is also

Figure 7 shows the thermal conductivity 
of alloys. It is obvious that the thermal conductivity 
is less sensitive to the alloy composition and the 
amount of carbides. These values are relatively 
higher than those for conventional stainless steels 
and other tool steels. 
Concluding remarks 
 The mechanical and physical properties 
such as compressive strength, bend ductility, wear 
resistance and thermal conductivity are measured at 
room temperature for several Ni 3Al-based alloys 
with various carbon concentrations up to 2.0 wt.%. 
It was found that the hardness and wear resistance 
as well as compressive strength increase with 
increasing carbon concentration, while bend 
ductility decreases. Cracks propagate mainly 
through carbides, which implies good adhesion 
between carbides and matrix phase.
