ABSTRACT 
The high content of alloying elements in Ti alloys and high-strength steels, combined with the requirement for 
quenching and tempering heat treatment during the fabrication process of the clad plate, leads to complex 
interfacial products and poor bonding properties. An Nb interlayer was introduced to enhance the mechanical 
properties of Ti alloy/steel clad plates. This work investigated the effect of Nb interlayers with initial thicknesses 
of 10, 25, and 50 μ m on the evolution of interfacial products and bonding properties. The results indicated that 
when the interlayer was thin (10 μ m thick), the diffusion of Ti, Fe, and C in the matrix and clad materials could 
not be completely inhibited due to the uneven distribution of the Nb interlayer at the interface. TiC, NbC, Nb
2
C, 
and Fe
2
Nb were detected at the Nb/steel interface. With the thickness of the interlayer increased (reaching 25 μ m 
or 50 μ m), the influence of uneven deformation on the interlayer was weakened, and the Nb/steel interface was 
composed of Nb
2
C and Fe
2
Nb. The tensile shear test indicated that the fracture occurred at the Nb/steel interface 
and the Nb interlayer. The plasticity of the micrometer Nb interlayer decreased with the increase in interlayer 
thickness. Therefore, more cracks and brittle fracture characteristics were observed in the fracture surface of the 
50 μ m thick Nb interlayer sample. The clad interface with a 25 μ m thick Nb interlayer exhibited the highest 
interfacial shear strength of 295 MPa due to the few intermetallic compounds (IMCs) at the Nb/Steel interface 
and the excellent plasticity of the Nb interlayer. 
1. Introduction 
Ti/steel clad plates combine the lightweight and corrosion-resistant 
characteristics of Ti with the good formability and weldability of steel 
[ 1 , 2 ]. At present, Ti/steel clad plates are widely used in the petro -
chemical industry, marine equipment, and the nuclear industry. In 
addition, Ti alloy/steel clad plates, by utilizing duplex Ti alloy with 
higher strength than pure Ti as the clad material, make them more 
suitable for use in complex marine corrosion environments accompanied 
by strong wind and waves. At present, the preparation methods for 
Ti/steel clad plates include explosive welding, diffusion bonding, and 
rolling cladding [ 3 – 5 ]. Among them, rolling cladding, with its simple 
operation, low production cost, and high efficiency, has become the 
most extensively used method for fabricating clad plates [ 6 ]. 
The type and morphology of IMCs significantly influence the 
strength of the bonding at the interface [ 7 ]. In the bonding process, the 
Ti/steel clad interfacial products mainly include TiC, Fe
2
Ti, and FeTi 
[ 8 ]. These interfacial products possess high hardness and melting points, 
which are not conducive to bonding at the interface [ 9 ]. Among these, 
TiC has the lowest formation energy and standard Gibbs free energy 
( Δ G
θ
), leading to its initial appearance at the clad interface and subse -
quently inhibiting the formation of Fe – Ti IMCs [ 10 ]. In addition, 
compared with pure Ti/steel clad interface, because of the addition of 
Al, V, and other elements in Ti alloy/steel clad interface. It increased the 
phase transformation point of Ti in the clad material, and the micro -
structure of the clad material changed from single-phase to dual-phase, 
which led to a more complex interface reaction [ 11 ]. 
Adding an interlayer to the clad interface could effectively prevent 
direct contact and reaction at the Ti/steel interface and holds promise 
for significantly enhancing the interfacial properties of the Ti/steel 
bonding [ 12 – 14 ]. Interlayers such as Nb, Ni, V, and Fe are favored by 
many scholars for their excellent physical properties and the lower 
brittleness of the interfacial products formed with the base and clad 
materials [ 15 – 18 ]. Thus, they are used to relieve the interfacial stress 
concentration and control the interfacial products. In our previous 
research, Wang et al. [ 19 ] found that the hardness and brittleness of 
IMCs formed at the Nb/steel interface are lower than that of Fe – Ti IMCs, 
thus significantly enhancing the bonding strength at the clad interface.

Furthermore, Nb has excellent corrosion resistance, and its inclusion 
will not reduce the corrosion resistance of the Ti/steel clad plate [ 20 ]. 
However, the high cost of interlayer materials means that extensive use 
in preparing clad plates will increase their use costs. The thickness of the 
interlayer plays a crucial role in determining the type and strength of the 
interfacial products [ 21 , 22 ]. Selecting an appropriate thickness for the 
interlayer improves interfacial properties, conserves scarce metal re -
sources, and reduces material use costs. However, current researchers 
have mainly focused on the influence of various interlayer materials on 
the rolled clad interface, with few studies reported on the impact of the 
thickness of the interlayer on the interface. 
Consequently, this paper aims to study the effects of various thick -
nesses of Nb interlayers on the Ti – 6Al – 4V/EH690 rolled clad plates. The 
interface morphology, elemental diffusion, and phase composition of Nb 
interlayer with different thicknesses were characterized. Moreover, the 
IMCs, mechanical properties, and fracture behavior of different clad 
interfaces were compared and analyzed. Based on the results mentioned 
above, an analysis was conducted on the critical/optimum thickness of 
the interlayer to enhance mechanical properties and the reaction and 
fracture mechanisms of the clad interface. Based on the results 
mentioned above, we analyzed the optimum thickness of the interlayer 
to enhance mechanical properties, as well as the reaction and fracture 
mechanisms of the clad interface. 
2. Experimental materials and procedure 
In this experiment, Ti – 6Al – 4V alloy with dimensions of 100 × 80 ×
10 mm and EH690 steel with dimensions of 140 × 100 × 25 mm were 
adopted. The chemical compositions of these two materials are pre -
sented in Table 1 . Pure niobium (99.99 at. %) with the same length and 
width as Ti – 6Al – 4V and initial thicknesses of 10, 25, and 50 μ m were 
used as the interlayer material. In this paper, clad plates with these three 
interlayer thicknesses are referred to as 10Nb, 25Nb, and 50Nb samples, 
respectively. 
Fig. 1 (a) summarizes the whole hot working process of fabricating

shown in Fig. 1 (b). Firstly, the surface of the EH690, Ti – 6Al – 4V, and Nb 
interlayer is polished to remove the surface oxides and grease. Secondly, 
as shown in Fig. 1 (b), the blank was assembled, and the electron beam 
was welded (EBW) in a vacuum chamber at 5 × 10

the mechanical properties of the EH690, the clad plate was water-cooled

The tensile shear test samples were cut along the rolling direction 
(RD) with the size of 50 mm × 10 mm × 4 mm (RD × transverse di -

teristics of the shear interfaces were observed by X-ray diffractometer

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Microstructures and element diffusion of Ti alloy/steel clad interface 
with Nb interlayers of different thicknesses 
As shown in Fig. 4 (a – c), interfaces with Nb interlayers of varying 
thicknesses all achieved metallurgical bonding without any obvious 
defects. In three different thickness Nb interlayer clad plates, the EH690

Ti – 6Al – 4V side presented a dual-phase structure of α + β -Ti. During the 
rolling process, the deformation resistance of the Nb interlayer was 
significantly lower than that of the EH690 and Ti – 6Al – 4V, leading to an 
uneven thickness in the Nb interlayer [ 19 ]. The thickness of the Nb 
interlayer of 10Nb, 25Nb, and 50Nb samples after rolling is about 2.5, 
5.5, and 9 μ m, respectively. The interface of 10Nb was severely 
deformed locally, with the thinnest part of the Nb interlayer being 
almost discontinuous. EDS analysis was conducted on the clad interface 
of the Nb interlayer with different thicknesses, and the chemical

interface, further analysis of the microstructure composition and dis -

deformation led to the formation of dislocation entanglement and 
dislocation walls, indicating significant deformation at the Nb/steel

the existence of TiC, Nb
2

2
Nb as four types of IMCs at the 
Nb/steel interface. Due to the more complex and ordered lattice struc -
ture of IMCs, they often pose a greater hindrance to dislocation motion 
[ 34 ]. In polycrystals, dislocations move within the grains. When 
encountering an interface, the dislocations could not easily slip from one 
grain to another due to different orientations, leading to the accumu -
lation of dislocations at the grain boundaries. Therefore, a variety of fine 
IMCs crystals at the Nb/steel interface strongly hindered the dislocation 
movement. The existence of (Nb, β -Ti) solid solution was confirmed at 
the Ti/Nb interface through SAD4. A solid solution enrichment layer was 
formed near the interface in the Ti alloy, and the α -Ti phase existed in 
the Ti alloy matrix far away from the interface, which was consistent 
with the EBSD results.

4. Conclusions 
The Ti–6Al–4V/EH690 clad plates with Nb interlayer with initial 
thicknesses of 10, 25, and 50 μ m were prepared by vacuum hot rolling. 
The effects of different Nb interlayer thicknesses on the microstructure 
evolution and room-temperature mechanical properties of the clad 
interface were analyzed. The main conclusions were summarized as 
follows. 
(1) Ti alloy/steel clad plate with different thicknesses of Nb inter -
layer achieved good metallurgical bonding, and (β-Ti, Nb) solid 
solution existed at Ti/Nb interface. The interlayer thickness of the 
10Nb sample was not enough to prevent the diffusion of elements 
on both sides of the interface, and TiC, NbC, Nb
2
C, and Fe
2
Nb 
appeared at the Nb/steel interface. The interlayer thickness in the 
25Nb and 50Nb samples was adequate to block the diffusion of Ti 
in Ti alloy to the Nb/steel interface, which is composed of Nb
2
C 
and Fe
2
Nb. 
(2) For the Ti alloy/steel clad plates, the selection of the interlayer 
thickness should primarily aim to fully hinder the diffusion of 
elements from both sides of the interface in order to avoid the 
formation of an excessive variety of IMCs. For the 10Nb sample, 
there are many kinds of IMCs at the Nb/steel interface. During the 
shearing process, brittle fracture occurred both at the interface of 
Nb/steel and Nb interlayer, and the average interfacial shear 
strength was only 231 MPa. The interfacial shear strength of the 
25Nb sample and 50Nb sample increased to 295 MPa and 276 
MPa, respectively, due to the reduction in the types of IMCs. 
(3) The inferior plasticity of the Nb interlayer is a secondary factor 
that weakens the interfacial bonding strength. Compared to the 
50Nb sample, the 25Nb sample exhibited fewer brittle fracture 
characteristics and secondary cracks during the shear fracture 
process, benefiting from the superior plasticity of the Nb 
interlayer.
