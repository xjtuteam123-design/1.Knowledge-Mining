Abstract
The mechanical data of novel alloys based on the pseudo-ternary system Ni 3Al–Ni3Ti–Ni3V was assessed. This alloy system exhibits
dual multi-phase microstructures and was described in part I of this paper [Acta Mater 2005:53]. The mechanical characterization was
performed on as-cast and hot-worked material. The new alloys show extremely high yield stresses with good temperature retention, when
compared with conventional nickel-based alloys. Over a broad temperature range fracture preferentially occurred along grain bound-
aries. The compressive creep test conducted at temperatures between 1150 and 1250 K showed the presence of threshold stress and also
extremely low creep rates when compared with the conventional Ni-based superalloys with dispersed oxide particles. The obtained results
are promising for the development of a new type of high-temperature structural material.

1. Introduction
The geometrically close packed (gcp) Ni 3X structures
(phases) combined with group IIIb and IVb elements
(e.g., Al, Si and Sn), and also IVa and Va transition metals
(e.g., Ti, V and Nb) have attractive properties as high tem-
perature structural and chemical materials. For examples,
the alloys Ni
3Al [1–4],N i 3Si [5,6],N i 3Sn [7],N i 3Ti [8,9],
Ni3V [10], and Ni 3Nb [9,11,12] exhibit an anomalous
increase of strength with increasing temperature. Ni 3Al
and Ni 3Si show good corrosion resistance at intermediate
temperatures and superior oxidation resistance at high
temperatures. However, it is well known that monolithic
intermetallic compounds have some universal drawbacks,
such as poor ductility at room temperature and low (creep)
strength at high temperature. In the companion paper (Part
I) [13], it was shown that the intermetallic alloys based on
the Ni
3Al–Ni3Ti–Ni3V pseudo-ternary alloy system have a
dual multi-phase microstructures composed of Ni 3Al (L12),
Ni3V (D0 22) and Ni 3Ti (D0 24) phases, accompanied with
good interfacial coherency and structural stability upon
high-temperature exposure.
In part II, the mechanical properties of these new alloys
will be assessed over a wide temperature interval utilizing
compression and tensile tests. It will be shown that the
observed mechanical properties are superior to those of
the conventional Ni-base superalloys.

annealed at 1273 K for 7 days (a), and at 1373 K for 2 days
(b). The previous study reported that alloys annealed at
1273 K are composed of the featureless eutectoid micro-
structure (i.e., only the /C212lower/C213multi-phase microstructure)
consisting of (L1
2 +D 022), while alloys annealed at 1373 K
are composed of the dual multi-phase microstructure [13]
although two phases of L1 2 and D0 22 in their /C212lower/C213
multi-phase microstructures are not so well developed (or
separated). It is well documented that the alloys Ni 3Al
(L12) [1–4],N i 3Ti (D0 24) [8,9] and Ni 3V (D0 22) [10] exhibit
an anomalous increase of yield strength with temperature.
Similar behavior was also observed for all the multi-phase
alloys investigated here. However, the yield strength levels
of the multi-phase intermetallic alloys were substantially
higher than that of Ni
3Al (L1 2) [1–4],N i 3Ti (D0 24) [8,9]
and Ni3V (D022) [10] binary alloys. The high yield strength
of the multi-phase intermetallic alloys is maintained at up
to 800–1000 K, followed by a decrease at temperatures
above 1000 K. It should be noted that the yield stress of
the alloys with dual multi-phase microstructures
(Fig. 3 (b)) is signiﬁcantly higher than that of alloys with
the /C212lower/C213multi-phase microstructure ( Fig. 3 (a)). This
result suggests that the dual multi-phase microstructure is
very useful to strengthen the multi-phase intermetallic
alloys. In a wide range of temperatures, the measured yield
strength is higher in alloys with a large area fraction of the
eutectoid microstructure (i.e., the /C212lower/C213multi-phase
microstructure, e.g., alloys 7 and 10) than in the other
alloys.

3.3. Tensile test
Figs. 5–7 show tensile strength and tensile elongation as
a function of test temperature for the alloys 10HF,
10HF + B and 12HF, respectively. Alloy 10HF did not
show macroscopic yielding and subsequent plastic elonga-
tion, and consequently resulted in a consistently low tensile
(fracture) strength at whole temperatures ( Fig. 5 ). On the
other hand, alloys 10HF + B and 12HF showed macro-
scopic yielding and subsequent plastic elongation at almost
all test temperatures.
For alloy 10HF + B with a grain size of 130 lm
(Fig. 6 (a)), the yield strength and the tensile strength were
relatively high at low temperature, and showed a broad
maximum at intermediate temperature (around 1000 K),
followed by a decrease at temperature beyond about
1000 K. The tensile elongation ranged between 1% and
3% at room temperature, and remained up to 1200 K. In
order to investigate the eﬀect of the grain size on the yield
stress, this alloy was also tested in the as-forged condition,
which is characterized by a very ﬁne grain size of 21 lm.
The results are shown in Fig. 6 (b). Accordingly, the grain
reﬁnement results in the further strengthening particularly
at low temperature and larger tensile elongation at temper-
ature beyond about 1200 K. The occurrence of such a ten-
sile ductility in the wide range of temperatures is attributed
to the eﬀect of the boron doping in addition to a speciﬁc
dual multi-phase microstructure. Fig. 8 shows the change
of stress versus strain curves with temperature for alloy
10HF + B. Depending on temperature, largely diﬀerent
stress versus strain curves were observed. At low tempera-
tures, this alloy shows certain amount of plastic deforma-
tion with a large strain-hardening rate after yielding. At
temperatures around a peak temperature (i.e., 1000 K),
moderate plastic deformation with a relatively low strain-
hardening rate followed by rupture was observed. At high
temperatures (beyond 1200 K), the samples exhibited
steady-state ﬂow after yielding, indicating high plastic
deformability.

4. Discussion
4.1. Strength properties
The present dual multi-phase intermetallic alloys, which
are composed of L1
2 and D0 22 phases (or L1 2,D 0 22 and
D024 phases), exhibit a yield strength anomaly, as shown
in Figs. 3–8 . This result is closely related to the fact that
alloys Ni 3Al (L1 2), Ni 3V (D0 22) and Ni 3Ti (D0 24) display
the positive temperature dependence of the ﬂow (or yield)
strength. It has been repeatedly reported that the positive
temperature dependence of the ﬂow strength occurs in
Ni
3Al (L1 2) and related intermetallic compounds [1–4],
and was attributed to the Kear–Wilsdorf (K–W) locking
mechanism, which arises from cross-slip of dissociated
screw dislocations from the {1 1 1} to {0 0 1} planes [1–3].
Recently, Umakoshi et al. group intensively investigated
the plastic ﬂow behavior of several Ni
3V (D0 22) [10] and
Ni3Ti (D0 24) single crystals [8,9]. The yield strength anom-
aly observed in Ni 3V (D0 22)a n dN i 3Ti (D0 24) could be
attributed to a dislocation mechanism similar to the K–
W locking operating in L1 2 structures. It was suggested
that the anti-phase boundary (APB) energy is highly
anisotropic, concerning {1 1 1} and {0 0 1} planes of the
L1
2 structure, and (0 0 0 1) and f01 /C2210 g planes in D0 24
structure. This anisotropy promotes the thermally acti-
vated cross-slip of the dissociated screw dislocation (e.g.,
1/2Æ011 æ dislocation in L1
2 structure and 1 =3h2 /C221 /C2210 i dislo-
cation in D0 24 structure).
At a wide range of temperatures, the yield strength (or
the ﬂow strength) of the present dual multi-phase interme-
tallic alloys was close to (or beyond) 1 GPa, as shown in
Fig. 13 . Such a high strength is closely associated with
the ﬁne and coherent dual multi-phase microstructure.
Large interfacial strengthening is expected when a disloca-
tion passes through the interface between the /C212upper/C213and
/C212lower/C213microstructures at the micron size, and also through
the interface between the L1
2 and D0 22 phases within the
/C212lower/C213multi-phase microstructure in a sub-micron size.
Although the relevant strengthening mechanism is not
clear at the moment, it is suggested that it involves the fol-
lowing processes: (i) generation of interfacial dislocations
during the dislocation propagation event, (ii) transforma-
tion of the dislocation core during intersection of the inter-
face according to the change of the crystal structure, or (iii)
intersection of the network of the misﬁt dislocations situ-
ated at the interface.

5. Summary
The high-temperature mechanical properties of the
intermetallic alloys based on the Ni
3Al–Ni3Ti–Ni3V
pseudo-ternary alloy system were investigated, focusing
on the low Ti content region where the dual multi-phase
microstructures are developed. Using arc-melted and hot-
forged materials, the following results were obtained from
the present study.
(1) The yield strength of the dual multi-phase intermetal-
lic alloys increased up to relatively high temperatures
and was extremely high over the whole range of test
temperatures.
(2) A signiﬁcant tensile ductility was observed for hot-
forged materials doped with boron. Intergranular
fracture was observed over a wide temperature range.
(3) Extremely low creep rates were observed between
1150 and 1250 K, when compared with conventional
Ni-based superalloys. Also, the threshold stress was
observed below which the materials did not creep.
(4) Excellent creep strength and tensile ductility observed
in the present intermetallic alloys were attributed to
the dual multi-phase microstructures that are com-
posed of the L1
2 and D0 22 phases, connected by
highly coherent interfaces.
(5) The obtained results are promising for the develop-
ment of a new type of high-temperature structural
material.
