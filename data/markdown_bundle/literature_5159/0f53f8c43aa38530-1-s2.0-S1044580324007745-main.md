1. Introduction
In the last decade, refractory high entropy alloys (RHEAs) have been 
extensively studied due to their excellent high-temperature stability and 
mechanical properties [ 1 – 3 ]. RHEAs are usually composed of a single 
phase BCC/B2 structure or a multi-phase structure including a BCC/B2 
phase and intermetallic compounds, and these RHEAs usually have a 
remarkably stability and high strength at high temperature [ 2 , 4 , 5 ]. For 
example, Tseng et al. [ 6 ] found that the HfMoTaTiZr RHEAs with a 
single BCC phase exhibited the attractive compressive yield strength 
(404 MPa) at 1200 
◦
C. The slow diffusion effects and high melting of 
component element were the main reasons for the RHEAs to maintain 
high strength at elevated temperature. Unfortunately, the further 
development of TiZrHf-based RHEAs is significantly hindered due to 
their low strength at room temperature. For instance, the tensile yield 
strength of TiZrHfNb alloy is merely 720 MPa [ 7 ].
It has been demonstrated that forming the lamellar eutectic structure 
with alternating soft and hard regions is an efficient way to improve 
strength [ 8 – 10 ]. Moreover, the eutectic high entropy alloys (EHEAs) 
with very fine lamellar structures can obtain high strength through the 
grain boundary strengthening. Yurchenko et al. [ 11 ] studied the linkage 
between the microstructure and compressive properties of the

To forming complete eutectic structure in the RHEAs, the empirical 
parameter prediction and numerous repeated experiments need to be 
explored for the various alloys. How to design the elemental constituent 
to gain the complete eutectic structures in RHEAs is still a difficult

chromium (Cr) is selected as the alloying element according to the three 
aspects. First, Cr has a smaller atomic radius than that of Ti, Zr and Hf. 
The large difference of atomic size makes severe lattice distortion and 
the charge transformation effect in the alloy, and the decrease of mutual 
solubility between atoms in the alloy is more likely to make the phase 
separation [ 14 , 15 ]. Second, Cr has a larger valence electron concen -

effect and relative valence effect in terms of Hume-Rothery criterion that 
the bonding between the principal elements in the alloy can be deter -

cation can be analyzed [ 16 ]. Third, Cr elements with higher 
electronegativity than that of Ti, Zr and Hf elements are more likely to 
lose their extranuclear electrons, thus promoting the generation of 
intermetallic compounds in the alloy [ 17 – 19 ]. Through the above 
analysis, it is considered as the potential element of Cr to improve the 
strength of TiZrHf-based RHEAs at room temperature due to the second 
phase precipitating.

approached to optimize TiZrHfCr RHEAs composition. It can be found

these, intermetallic compounds have a high melting point and strength 
due to their strong covalent bonding [ 47 ]. Therefore, Cr element is
