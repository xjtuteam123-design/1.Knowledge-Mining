1. Introduction 
Al – Li alloys possess the advantages of relatively lower density and 
higher specific stiffness when compared with conventional Al and Fe 
based alloys [ 1 – 3 ]. It is well known that every 1 wt % of Li added to an 
Al matrix can reduces its density by 3% and increases its elastic modulus 
by 6% [ 4 , 5 ]. Currently, the research works are mainly focused on the 
wrought Al – Li alloys [ 6 , 7 ]. Nevertheless, the limit content of Li addition 
and the anisotropy brought into the alloy are obstacles that may restrict 
the wide application of wrought Al – Li alloys. Comparing to wrought 
Al – Li alloys, cast Al – Li alloys contain higher Li content and exhibit 
competitiveness in terms of density, modulus and castability [ 8 ]. Hence, 
this would meet the increasing requirements for weight reduction crit -
ical, stiffness enhancement and more complex structures in aerospace, 
weapons and military industries. 
Zhang developed a sand cast Al – 2Li – 2Cu-0.5Mg-0.2Zr-0.2Sc alloy 
which obtained excellent room temperature mechanical properties [ 9 ], 
and then investigated the influence of heat treatment on microstructure 
and mechanical properties [ 10 ]. It has been reported that the alloy was 
strengthened by an age-hardening treatment through a precipitation 
strengthening mechanism [ 11 ]. With the evolvement of the aerospace 
industry, metal structural materials have been subjected to extraordi -
nary high temperature requirements. For some special structural parts, 
their service temperature is at 200 – 300 
◦
C condition. ZL205A alloy, as 
an aerospace material, the tensile strength at room temperature and

the researches on the high-temperature performance of Al – Li alloys are 
mostly focused on wrought Al – Li alloys. Zheng investigated the influ -

transition of fracture from typical quasi-cleavage below 200

4. Discussion 
4.1. Effects of temperature on the high temperature mechanical properties 
The widths of δ ′ -PFZ in different temperature of PA state are given in 
Fig. 10 , at the process of aging, δ ′ -PFZ with a certain width is formed 
near the grain boundaries, and coarsened δ ′ phases may also be formed. 
δ ′ -PFZ is softer than the matrix, which is accompanied with a weak 
hindering effect on dislocation movement, crack initiation and propa -
gation. Therefore, dislocations tend to accumulate near grain bound -
aries during plastic deformation [ 17 , 18 ]. Once the crack is generated, it 
tends to expand in δ ′ -PFZ, and promotes the intergranular fracture of the 
alloy, which is pernicious for the toughness of the alloy [ 19 ].

4.3. Fracture mechanism under high temperature tensile 
The fracture modes of materials are related to type, size, distribution 
and number density of precipitated phases. Under tensile loading, when 
dislocation slip occurs in the grain, the dispersed precipitates interact 
with the dislocation, resulting in blocking or pinning of the dislocation, 
thereby strengthening the alloy [ 33 ]. The different fracture modes are 
shown in Fig. 16 . 
For the PA alloys, at 175 
◦
C, the internal cracking is mainly due to 
coplanar deformation and local slip caused by the coplanar precipitated 
δ ′ phase, leading to early failure of grain boundaries [ 34 ], then it 
propagates within the crystal. The crack propagation diagram is shown 
in Fig. 16 (a), the alloy cracks from the grain boundary, showing the 
characteristics of intergranular and quasi-cleavage fracture. At 200 
◦
C, 
cracks start and propagate at the precipitate-matrix interface, while new 
cracks would be generated by further disintegration and breaking of 
precipitates [ 35 ]. This is confirmed by Fig. 7 (b), which shows that the 
fracture mode is intergranular fracture with broken precipitates at the 
grain boundaries. With the increase in temperature, under the action of 
external force, recrystallization occurs in the cast alloy at 200 
◦
C, as 
shown in Fig. 16 (c). Grain refinement occurs, that is, the distance be -
tween the dislocation source and the grain boundary declines, which 
may lead to the difficulty of transferring plastic deformation between 
two continuous grains, thus cracks will inevitably appear at the grain 
boundary, thereby increasing the strength. Following the increase of 
tensile temperature, the decline rate of strength intragranular exceeds 
the grain boundary [ 36 ], which not only leads to the destruction of grain 
boundary resistance to dislocation slip, but also contributes to GBS or 
grain boundary deformation (GBD) [ 37 ]. The fracture morphology of 
recrystallization is relatively flat, which is consistent with Fig. 7 (b), 
perpendicular to the tensile direction, and the main fracture is inter -
granular fracture. 
Fig. 16 (d) – (e) depict the dissolution of δ ′ phase and the coarsening of 
T
1 
and the broadening of δ ′ -PFZ with increasing temperature. The 
fracture mechanism at high temperature can be explained mainly from 
two aspects: microstructure evolution and grain size variation. On the 
one hand, with the elevated temperature, the alloy gets soften, the grain 
boundary strength reduces, as a result, the elongation increases. On the 
other hand, recrystallization also occurs in cast alloys under applied 
stress tensile and high temperature conditions which enhances the 
tensile strength.

5. Conclusion 
In this paper, the high temperature mechanical behavior of cast 
Al – 2Li – 2Cu-0.5Mg-0.15Sc-0.15Zr-0.05Ti alloy under different temper -
atures and different aging states were investigated, and the variations of 
mechanical behavior and fracture mechanism under high temperature 
tensile were discussed on basis of microstructural evolution from two 
aspects of precipitates and grain size. The main conclusions are drawn as 
follows: 
(1) The alloy showed a response to aging hardening, and the peak is 
attained at 175 
◦
C for 32 h with a value of 176 HV, followed by a 
successive decline as the aging time prolonged. At room tem -
perature, when the time of potentization was extended from 8 h 
to 64 h, the YS of the alloy increased from 311 MPa to 483 MPa, 
and the elongation decreased from 17.6% to 2.7%. 
(2) For PA alloys, at high temperature, when the temperature 
increased from 175 
◦
C to 225 
◦
C, the YS decreased from 357 MPa 
to 255 MPa, and the elongation decreased from 7.5% to 3.2% at 
200 
◦
C and then increased to 9.8% at 255 
◦
C. Under high tem -
perature stretching, the sliding of the grain boundaries occurred, 
resulting in a decline in strength, while the plasticity decreased at 
200 
◦
C because the δ ′ -PFZ plays a dominant role, while at 225 
◦
C 
the dynamic recrystallization improved the plasticity of alloy. 
(3) With the prolongation of aging time, the YS decreased from 273 
MPa in UA state to 255 MPa in PA state, and then increased to 
302 MPa in OA state. The elongation increased from 7.1% to 
9.8% first and then decreased to 4.4%. The decline in the strength 
of the PA alloy is due to that the broadening of the δ ′ -PFZ plays a 
major role, whereas the enhance in the strength of the OA alloy is 
on account of the fact that the main factor affecting the strength 
in this stage is the grain size. 
(4) After artificial aging process, the mechanical properties of alloy 
are dominated by δ ′ , T
1 
and S ′ precipitates. The δ ′ -PFZ half-width 
gradually widens with rising temperature, and the broadening in 
δ ′ -PFZ width reduces the strength of the alloy. At high tempera -
tures, recrystallization occurs, leading to grain refinement, 
resulting in an enhancement of the plasticity of the alloy.
