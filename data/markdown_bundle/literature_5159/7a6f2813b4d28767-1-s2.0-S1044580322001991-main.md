basic Ti

1. Introduction 
Laser-based direct energy deposition (L-DED) is a newly developed 
additive manufacturing technique, which is capable of producing almost 
any geometric and complex part through a layer-by-layer manufacturing 
strategy [ 1 , 2 ]. Compared to the traditional manufacturing technology, 
this technology has many advantages, namely a high material utilization 
ratio, a lower time-to-market, no limit on component size and geome -
tries, low manufacturing cost and a near-net-shape production without 
needing expensive molds, etc. [ 3 – 5 ]. Until now, there are many studies 
on L-DED titanium alloys, most of which are related to the Ti-6Al-4 V 
alloy with ( α + β ) structure resulting from its excellent mechanical 
properties [ 6 – 8 ]. However, the high cooling rates and extremely large 
temperature gradients in the L-DED process often lead to the formation 
of exclusively columnar grains, so mechanical properties exhibit unde -
sirable anisotropy [ 9 , 10 ]. Process optimization experiments have shown 
that it is difficult to promote equiaxed growth of titanium grains by 
controlling processing parameters of L-DED. Moreover, the alloy is very 
sensitive to metallurgical defects [ 11 – 13 ]. If the L-DED process is not 
properly controlled, gas pores, inadequate fusion and cracks are easily 
produced. Compared with the ( α + β )-type titanium alloys, β -type tita -
nium alloys have an excellent match of mechanical and welding prop -
erties, which makes them ideal for industrial applications [ 14 – 16 ]. But 
the problem with them is that the uncontrolled micro-segregation, 
produced by the wide solidification interval and high cooling rate, 
which may induce the local precipitation of the second phase in further 
multiple thermal cycles, leading to an inhomogeneous microstructure 
[ 17 – 19 ]. 
As an ideal L-DED alloy, it should have the following basic charac -
teristics: a) High structure stability and weak crystallographic texture to 
obtain homogeneous microstructure and isotropous properties. b) 
Excellent forming properties (including low defect sensitivity, good 
liquid-state fluidity, and weak micro-segregation, etc.) to meet the 
technical requirements of L-DED. c) Good comprehensive properties to 
achieve a noble balance of mechanical, chemical, and tribological 
properties, etc. [ 20 , 21 ]. In this respect, the Ti

good candidate since it has a single melting temperature, which gives it 
high structure stability and good formability. But a considerable 
disadvantage of this alloy is its insufficient strength (747 MPa) [ 22 , 23 ].

precipitation of α solid solution along grain boundaries, but also

solution to restrict easy motion of dislocations. It is important to note 
that the amount of V should be strictly limited, since an increased V 
addition enlarges the solidification interval, causing deterioration in 
formability [ 24 , 25 ]. In contrast, Nb has little effect on the solidification 
interval and plays the function of refining grain and improving corrosion 
resistance [ 26 , 27 ].

3. Experimental procedure 
Table 1 lists the compositions used for L-DED feedstocks. The 
elemental powders of Ti (50 – 120 μ m, 99.95%), Zr (25 – 100 μ m, 
99.95%), V (50 – 120 μ m, 99.90%), and Nb (50 – 120 μ m, 99.90%) were 
purchased from the Changsha Tijo Metals. Preliminary experiments 
showed that milling rotation speed and ball milling time were two 
important parameters affecting the uniformity of the mixed powders. A 
combination of rapid milling rotation speed and long ball-milling time 
makes powders cold weld, which will decrease the solid fluidity of 
powders during feeding. While a combination of low milling rotation 
speed and short ball-milling time makes the powders difficult to mix 
uniformly. Based on this, the optimal milling parameter was chosen with 
a rotation speed of 200 r/min for 48 h to ensure the uniformity of the 
mixed powders. Fig. 2 shows the typical SEM images of element powder 
and the mixed powders after milling. Combining with the SEM images 
and elemental mapping distribution of the mixed powders shown in 
Fig. 2 (e-i), it can be observed that the powder particles with irregular 
shapes of Zr, V, and Nb are uniformly dispersed in the voids of spherical 
Ti powders. The substrate materials were Ti plates with the size of 60 
mm × 50 mm × 10 mm. Before the L-DED experiment, the surface of the 
substrates was grounded by sandpaper and cleaned with acetone and 
ethanol. 
The L-DED experiment was performed on the LDM-800 additive 
manufacturing system equipped with a 6 KW fiber laser in an argon 
atmosphere, as shown in Fig. 3 . The laser beam diameter and overlap 
rate were fixed at 4 mm and 50%, respectively. The laser power, scan -
ning velocity, and powder feed rate were varied to investigate the op -
timum conditions. By analyzing the variation of microstructure and 
formability with processing parameters ( Table 2 ), the final parameters 
were chosen: laser power 2.2 kW, scanning velocity 10 mm/s, laser 
diameter 4 mm, powder feed rate 5.4 g/min, and overlap rate 50%. After 
17 layers of deposition, rectangular specimens with dimensions of 35 
mm × 25 mm × 20 mm were built. 
As-deposited specimens were sectioned by electrical discharge 
machining along the direction perpendicular to the laser scanning di -
rection, and then metallographically prepared using successively finer 
grinding paper and given a final polish with diamond paste for micro -
structure analysis. Constituent phases were identified by using an 
Empyrean X-ray diffractometer at 40 kV and 40 mA. A Zeiss Supra 55

5. Conclusions 
New Ti-Zr-V-Nb alloys were designed from a binary Ti
–
Zr congruent 
composition alloyed with constant V content and varying Nb contents, 
and then deposited by laser-based direct energy deposition. The 
microstructure and properties of the as-deposited alloys were system -
atically studied, and the key findings are summarized as follows: 
1) All as-deposited alloys are composed of near-equiaxed β grains with 
multiple orientations. The difference is that as the Nb content in -
creases, the lattice parameter of the phase enlarges and its grain size 
gradually decreases. 
2) The increasing Nb content enhances hardness, strength, and tribo -
logical properties of as-deposited alloys but reduces ductility. The 
corrosion resistance in HCl solution is an increasing function of Nb 
content, while surface roughness gradually decreases with Nb con -
tent increasing to 2.35 at.%, and subsequently increases. 
3) The optimized alloy with 3.91 at.% Nb exhibits outstanding advan -
tages in hardness, strength, wear and corrosion resistance, but it has 
a slight decrease in ductility and formability compared with
