ABSTRACT 
In this paper, CoCrCu
1.2
FeNi high entropy alloy (HEA) was prepared by vacuum suspension melting. After 50 % 
cold-deformation, recrystallization annealing experiments involving the annealing temperatures of 650 – 950 ℃ 
and the annealing times of 30 – 120 min were carried out on the cold-deformed HEA. The microstructure and 
properties of the alloy were compared before and after annealing. The results showed that with the extension of 
annealing time and temperature, the microstructure of the alloy was gradually uniform and coarsening occurred 
after spheroidization. In addition, fusion and engulfment occurred in the microstructure and the microstructure 
size decreased first and then increased. Both the fracture strength and fracture strain of the alloy increased first 
and then decreased. The alloy had the best compression performance at 850 ℃ /60 min. Compared with the alloy 
after cold-deformation, the fracture strength was increased by 79.3 %, the fracture strain was increased by more 
than 1 time, and the comprehensive mechanical properties were the best at 750 – 850 ℃ . The average hardness 
decreased with the increase of temperature and annealing time, and the initial recrystallization temperature of 
the alloy was determined to be about 650 ℃ . The formation of annealing twins refined the microstructure and 
improved the properties of the alloy. 
1. Introduction 
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) are a solid solution alloy composed of 
five or more elements in accordance with the equal atomic ratio or close 
to the equal atomic ratio, and the atomic ratio of the principal elements 
is between 5 % and 35 % [1 – 3] , which was proposed by Ye et al. in 2004 
[4,5] . HEAs are also named as multi-element alloys, multi-principal al -
loys, complex alloys, high-concentration complex alloys, multi-element 
alloys, etc. The elements are generally selected in the central region of 
the periodic table, mainly the metal elements of the third, fourth and 
fifth periods of group B. According to the solid solution theory of alloys, 
the main elements of HEAs are mostly selected as those with similar 
densities, little difference in melting points, similar electronegativity 
and complementary atomic radius [6] . Therefore, the mixing entropy of 
HEA is relatively high, which will generally form single-phase or 
multiphase solid solutions of FCC and BCC, and it is difficult to appear 
intermetallic compounds or other complex ordered phases. Due to its 
unique crystal structure characteristics, HEAs exhibit many excellent 
properties compared with traditional alloys such as high strength and 
hardness, high work hardening, significant wear and corrosion 
resistance, excellent oxidation resistance and high temperature stability 
[7 – 13] . At present, HEAs are mainly prepared by vacuum arc melting 
and other methods. After compression at room temperature, the grain 
size almost maintains the same, but the grain orientation changes to a 
certain extent, which may be due to the joint effects of dislocation slip in 
the grain. After plastic deformation, the HEAs appear work hardening. 
At the same time, there are still many defects in the internal structure, 
such as dislocations, stress concentration and so on. The annealing 
treatment can also eliminate the internal structure defects of the HEAs, 
make the internal structure of the alloy more uniform, and improve the 
performance of the alloy [14 – 20] .

FCC phase, and phase separation phenomenon exists [21,22] . Wang

studied the effect of different annealing temperatures on the micro -
structure and mechanical properties of high-entropy alloys. The results

2. Experimental procedures 
In this experiment, the pure metal with molar ratio of Co, Cr, Cu, Fe, 
Ni = 1:1:1.2:1:1 (99.9 % purity) was used as the experimental raw 
material. The original ingot of CoCrCu
1.2
FeNi HEA was prepared by 5 
five-times vacuum suspension melting. The cylinder sample of φ 16 × 25 
mm was cut from the ingot, and then the cold-deformation experiment 
with 50 % deformation was carried out via the hydraulic press of 2000 
kN. The recrystallization annealing experiment was carried out on the 
sample after the experiment. The recrystallization annealing process 
was selected as follows: annealed at 650 ℃ , 750 ℃ , 850 ℃ and 950 ℃ 
for 30 min, 60 min, 90 min and 120 min, respectively, and then cooled 
with the furnace. 
In order to explore the influence of recrystallization annealing pro -
cess on the microstructure and properties of cold-deformed CoCrCu
1.2 -
FeNi HEA, as shown in Fig. 1 , samples were taken from the central 
plastic deformation area of the cold-deformed sample for microstructure 
and properties analysis. Olympus GX71 optical microscope was used to 
observe and analyze the microstructure of the alloy before and after heat 
treatment. Tecnai G2 F30 field emission high resolution transmission 
electron microscope was used to observe the dislocation morphology of 
recrystallized annealed specimens and analyze the phase qualitatively. 
For TEM sample preparation, foil sample with a diameter of 3 mm was 
mechanically thinned out, then ion polished by Gatan PIPS system to 
create electron transparent region. HVS-1000A Vickers hardness tester 
and Instron 5569 electronic universal testing machine were used to test 
the hardness and mechanical properties of the recrystallized annealed 
specimens.

commonly used methods to improve the alloy properties. All elements in

the alloy, which could play the role of solid solution strengthening, and 
then improved the strength of the alloy. Fine grain strengthening could 
improve the strength of the alloy and increased its plastic deformation 
ability, so that the hardness of the annealed state was greatly improved 
compared with the as-cast state.

4. Conclusions 
In this paper, the recrystallization annealing of cold-deformed 
CoCrCu
1.2
FeNi HEA was carried out at the temperatures of 
650 – 950 ℃ , and the annealing time was 30 – 120 min. The microstruc -
ture and properties of the alloy were analyzed by metallographic 
microstructure analysis and room temperature compression experiment. 
The following conclusions are drawn: 
(1) As holding time increased, the fiber structure was gradually 
uniform, spheroidized and coarsened. Best compressive property 
was achieved at 850 ℃ /60 min, the fracture strength and strain 
reached 2011.5 MPa, 60.1 %. The fracture strength increased by 
79.3 % and the fracture strain increased by more than 1 time. 
(2) As annealing temperature increased, the microstructure changed 
from dendrite to equiaxed grains and then to coarsened non- 
equiaxed grains. With annealing temperature increasing, the 
fracture strength and strain increased first and then decreased, 
optimized mechanical properties was obtained at 750 – 850 ℃ . 
(3) The average hardness decreased as annealing temperature 
increased, which was consistent with the change trend of yield 
strength. The initial recrystallization temperature of the 50 % 
cold-deformed CoCrCu
1.2
FeNi alloy was about 650 ℃ . 
(4) After recrystallization and annealing, dislocation wall, disloca -
tion cell structure and annealing twins occurs. The formation of 
twins could lead to higher properties of the alloy.
