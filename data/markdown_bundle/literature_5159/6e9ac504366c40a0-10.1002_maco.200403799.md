Corrosion resistance of the high-strength filler
metal NiCr20Fe14Mo11WN (alloy 50 PLUS)
U. Brill*
The Nb-free NiCr20Fe14Mo11WN alloy has been developed as
a new filler metal which combines high strength and excellent cor-
rosion resistance in wet environments at ambient temperatures.
The alloy is characterized by exceptional resistance to pitting and
crevice corrosion, no sensitiveness to chloride induced stress cor-
rosion cracking and shows high mechanical properties, e.g. 0.2 %-
yield strength.
Because the alloy is practically free of stable nitride formers like
niobium, titanium etc., no nitride formation during welding of super
austenitic, duplex and super duplex steels is possible, which proves
the alloy to be superior to alloy 625.1 Introduction
Super austenitic, duplex and super duplex stainless steels
gain increasing market shares because of their exeptional
combination of corrosion resistance in salt water and various
mineral acids and attractive mechanical properties at ambient
temperatures, e.g. 0.2%-yield strength which is commonly
used as a design value.
Matching filler metals for the above mentioned alloys are
available but suffer decreased corrosion resistance because of
micro and macro segregations in the cast microstructure of the
weld metal thus resulting in chromium and molybdenum de-
nuded areas.
Therefore it is necessary to apply an overalloyed filler me-
tal to achieve at last the same corrosion resistance. NiCrMo-
filler metals provide satisfactory corrosion resistance, but can-
not beware the high mechanical properties of the parent metal
in the as welded condition at ambient temperature.
To take care of this need alloy 50 PLUS has been devel-
oped. This alloy is characterized by:
l a high general and local (pitting and crevice) corrosion re-
sistance in various organic and mineral acids, caustic solu-
tions and fused salts,
l high mechanical strength and appropriate ductility at am-
bient temperature up to 550 8C,
l good weldability and fabricability,
l resonable price/property ratio.
Because of almost universal applications for super austeni-
tic, duplex and super duplex stainless steels, alloy 50 PLUS
might reduce hand stocking of various different filler metals,
which might generates high costs, logistical problems and ac-
celerated damper of mix up [1, 2].

3 Welding
As a criterion of weldability, testing of the hot cracking be-
haviour in the Modified Varestraint Test (MVT) of the Bun-
desanstalt fu¨r Materialforschung und -pru ¨fung (BAM) has
proven to be of increasing importance for materials consid-
ered as filler metals. In such a test a specimen (100 /C2 40
/C2 10 mm) is melted with a GTAW torch under defined con-
ditions over a specific length and mechanically bent during
this procedure [7, 8]. The total length of the cracks visible
on the surface at a magnification of 25 /C2 is then determined
as a function of the applied bending strain and serves as a mea-
sure of the sensitivity to hot cracking. The results for different
heat inputs are shown in Fig. 1. It can be seen that in this test
alloy 50 PLUS exhibits for 7.5 kJ/cm heat input the lowest
sensitivity to hot cracking compared to the alloys 625 and
825 welded with the same heat input. Even with a heat input
of 14.5 kJ/cm which is almost the double alloy 50 PLUS is still
on the transient line which limits the “low sensitivity to hot
cracking” region.

4 Corrosion resistance of welded joints
4.1 Corrosion resistance of SS 926 welded joints with alloy
50 PLUS filler metal
Three samples of SS 926 which were GTAW and GMAW
welded with the new filler metal were tested according to
ASTM G-28/A, ASTM G-48/C and in Green Death Solution
(7% H
2SO4, 3% HCl, 1% CuCl 2, 1% FeCl 3 /C2 6H 2O), which
are typical screening tests for an alloy’s resistance against lo-
calized corrosion.
In order to establish the critical pitting temperature (CPT),
the welded specimens were immersed into the above men-
tioned solution, starting at 40 8C. After 24 hours each, the tem-
perature was increased by 5 8C. The CPT is the temperature at
which initial pitting becomes visible on the surface (the depth
of the pits are defined to be greater than 50 lm). A comparison
to the unwelded material showed that the GTAW/GMAW
welded samples of alloy 926 exhibited a similar pitting corro-
sion resistance with respect to scatter, with a slightly better
performance of the GTAW welded specimens in comparison
to GMAW welded samples (Table 3). Corrosion rates obtained
for ASTM G-28/A showed the GTAW welded joints even su-
perior to the parent metal whereas the GMAW welded speci-
mens are slightly worse than the parent metal.

of the welded joints the 0.2%-yield strength values as typical

6 Summary
The new Nb-free NiCr20Fe14Mo11WN filler metal (alloy
50 PLUS) has been developed to combine high strength at am-
bient temperature and excellent corrosion resistance.
Corrosion tests performed according to ASTM G-28/A,
ASTM G-48/C and in Green Death Solution showed the
welded joints at least comparable to the parent metal.
The mechanical properties of the welded joints of the alloys
926, 24 and SS 318 LN are superior to the results achieved for
their parent metals, while the extraordinary high 0.2%-yield
strength of SS 327 super duplex stainless steel could not be
realized.
The filler metal is easily weldable and can be used for weld-
ing all high nitrogen stainless steels.
