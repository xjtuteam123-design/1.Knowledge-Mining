Composites

Keywords: Silicides, Composites, Microstructure, Property 
Abstract. The microstructures, mechanical properties and oxidation resistance of the refractory 
Nb-silicide-based composites have been investigated by scanning electron microscopy (SEM), X-ray 
diffraction (XRD), compression tests and high-temperature oxidation e xperiments. The results 
showed that 1773K/100h heat-treatment was an optimum processing for acquiri ng equilibrium Nb 
solid solution and silicides microstructure. In the binary Nb-Si system, the microstructure consisted of 
continuous Nb 5Si 3 equilibrium matrix and dispersed Nb particles, while in the the 
Nb-Ti-Cr-Al-Si-(Hf) multicomponent system, there are two typical microstructures, i.e., a two-phase 
structure of β (Nb solid solution)+D8 1 Nb 5Si 3-type silicide in the alloys with the Si+Al content 
(15at.% and 6at.%, respectively), and a three-phase structure of β+D8 1 Nb 5Si 3-type + D8 8 Ti 5Si 3-type 
silicides in the alloys with lower Si+Al content (10at.% and 8at.%, respectively). The results of 
compression tests showed that all alloys display high strength at both room and high temperatures, 
only a slight decrease in compression properties occured for Nb-Ti-Cr-Al-Si alloys, comparing to the 
binary Nb-Si in-situ composites. This type of alloys possesses good high temperature strengths up to 
at least 1473K. The results of high-temperature oxidation experiments showed that the oxidation rates 
of the alloys with Ti, Cr, Al and Hf addition were at least one order of magnitude lower than those of 
the Nb-Si binary alloys. 
Introduction 
Advanced aeronautical engines require the materials with high tempe rature capability and 
reasonable balance of properties such as low temperature toughness, high temperature strength, creep 
resistance and oxidation resistance. Nb/Nb 5Si 3 in-situ composites [1-4] may have the potential of 
meeting the high temperature requirements of advanced turbine engines. The potential increased 
temperature capability of Nb/Nb 5Si 3 in-situ composites is estimated as nearly 200ºC for both bulk 
average temperature and maximum surface temperature as compare d to the current single-crystal 
superalloys [5, 6]. The demonstrated rupture performance of the Nb/Nb 5Si 3 in-situ composites 
compares well to the second- and third- generation single-crystal superalloys. However, the use of the 
Nb/Nb 5Si 3 in-situ composites at surface temperatures much higher than 1370ºC[7, 8] will require 
further substantial increases in oxidation resistance for both the N b/Nb 5Si 3 in-situ composites and 
protective coatings. The density target for the Nb/Nb 5Si 3 in-situ composites is <6.8g/cm 3, and a 
density of 7.2g/cm 3 has already been demonstrated for a tough, strong Nb/Nb 5Si 3 in-situ composites 
[9]. This target is at least 25% lower than densities of third-g eneration single-crystal superalloys. In 
this paper, the process, microstructure and mechanical properties of r efractory Nb-silicides-based 
composites have been studied.

In order to acquire the equilibrium Nb+Nb 5Si 3 two-phase microstructure, the heat-treatment at 
1773K was carried out. Fig. 2 showed the effects of annealing time on t he microstructures of the 
Nb-10at.% Si alloy. With the increase of heat-treatment time, the Nb 3Si matrix transformed to Nb 5Si 3 
phase gradually via a eutectoid reaction, Nb 3Si → Nb + Nb 5Si 3. After 1773K/100h heat-treatment, the 
microstructure of Nb-10at.% Si alloy consisted of the continuous Nb 5Si 3 equilibrium matrix and the 
dispersed Nb particles, while no remarkable growth of Nb particles occurred, which suggested that 
1773K/100h annealing is an optimum condition.

Conclusions 
1. The 1773K/100h annealing is necessary for acquiring the equilibrium micros tructure, i.e., Nb 
solid solution for toughening and Nb silicides for strenghtening. 
2. The Nb-Ti-Cr-Al-Si-(Hf) alloys consisted of two typical micr ostructures, i.e., two-phase 
structure of β+D8 1 Nb 5Si 3-type silicide in the alloys with the higher Si+Al content and 
three-phase structure of β+D8 1 Nb 5Si 3-type + D8 8 Ti 5Si 3-type silicides in the alloys with the 
lower Si+Al content. Hf mainly dissolved into Nb silicides. 
3. The alloys represented high strength at both room and high temperature, only a slight decrease in 
room temperature compression properties occured for Nb-Ti-Cr-Al-Si alloys, comparing to the 
Nb-Si binary alloys. This type of alloys possessed good high temperature strengths up to at least 
1473K. 
4. Compared to the Nb-Si binary alloys, multicomponent alloying can improve the high temperature 
oxidation resistance by at least one order of magnitude.
