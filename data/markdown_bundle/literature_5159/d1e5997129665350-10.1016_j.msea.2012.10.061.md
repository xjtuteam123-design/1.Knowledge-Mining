abstract
Zirconium (Zr)-based bulk metallic glass possesses the highest potential as a structural material among
metallic glasses. Although Zr-based bulk metallic glass exhibits extremely high strength, its potential
application has been restricted by a number of issues, such as fragility, small size, difﬁcult fabrication
into different shapes and poisonous beryllium content, among others. In this paper, a Zr-based crystal
alloy with comparable strength and higher plasticity than Zr-based bulk metallic glass is presented. The
proposed Zr-based alloy has a tensile strength greater than 1600 MPa. That value is comparable to the
1500 MPa to 2000 MPa strength of Zr-based bulk metallic glasses (BMGs). The ductility in terms of
elongation reached 6.2%; at the same time, the 1400 MPa tensile strength was retained. This
phenomenon is not possible for Zr-based BMGs. XRD results show that the proposed ultrahigh-
strength Zr-based crystal alloy has two-phase structures: an hcp-structured
a phase and a bcc-
structured b phase. The forged specimen exhibits a typical basket-weave microstructure, which is
characterised by the interlaced plate a phase separated from the b phase matrix. Fine, short bar-shaped
a phases precipitated along the original b grain boundary together with ultraﬁne dot-shaped a phases
that presented inside the original b grain when the ageing temperature was between 500 1C and 525 1C.
As the ageing temperature increased, the dot-shaped a phase grew into plate shapes, decreasing the
material’s strength and increasing its plasticity. The ultraﬁne dot-shaped and short bar-shaped a
phases in the original b phase matrix are the main strengthening mechanisms of the ultrahigh-strength
Zr-based crystal alloy.

1. Introduction
Zirconium (Zr) and Zr-based alloys have many desirable
properties such as small thermal neutron absorption cross-
section and good corrosion resistance. These materials have been
widely used in the nuclear industry and in applications that
require high chemical corrosion resistance. Zr is a potential
structural material; however, the strength of this metal (approxi-
mately 300 MPa) is insufﬁcient to meet the requirements of
structural materials.
Amorphisation, a process utilising rapid cooling and liquid
structure retention, is a somewhat effective measure for improv-
ing the mechanical properties of alloys [1]. As potential structural
materials, Zr-based bulk metallic glasses (BMGs) have been
extensively studied because of their strength of over 2 GPa [2,3],
nearly ﬁve times than that of pure Zr. These BMGs also possess
other excellent properties such as high hardness, good wear
resistance, and excellent anti-corrosion performance. However,
the industrialisation of Zr-based BMGs is restricted by its almost
zero tensile ductility [4,5] and other serious weaknesses such as
narrow composition range [6], small size, and limited shapes (rod,
plate, strip or other simple shapes). Although the preparation of
amorphous composites remarkably improves plasticity [5,7],
other drawbacks of Zr-based BMG such as size and shape limita-
tions are still evident in these composites. Therefore, the indus-
trialisation of Zr-based BMGs is confronted with many difﬁculties
and requires prolonged research.
The addition of alloying elements into a host metal or alloy to
regulate its structure and transformation characteristics [8,9] has
been demonstrated to result in very good strengthening effects
via solid solution, ageing, precipitation, and ﬁne grain strengthen-
ing [10,11]. The macro-plastic deformation of crystalline metal
alloys through the directional migration of a large number of
atoms under an external force results in excellent processability,
which provides a solid foundation for industrialisation. According
to previous reports [12,13], the addition of small amounts of Sn

2. Experimental
Sponge zirconium (Zr þHf Z99.5 wt. %), sponge titanium
(99.7 wt. %), industrial pure aluminium (99.5 wt. %) and vanadium
(99.9 wt. %) were used to prepare alloys with a weight percent
composition of 47Zr-45Ti-5Al-3 V (identiﬁed as 47Zr). The alloy
was melted three times by a ZHT-001 type vacuum consumable
electro-arc furnace to ensure uniform chemical composition.
Multiple breakdowns were performed after holding at 1050 1C
above
b transit temperature for 1.5 h to fully break the coarse
grain. The ﬁnal heat forging was carried out after holding at
930 1C for 1.5 h, and the ingot was forged into 43 mm diameter
bars. The bars were sectioned into 10 mm /C2 10 mm /C2 70 mm heat
treatment specimens. The heat treatments were performance in a
SK-G06143 type tubular vacuum heat treatment furnace with a
protective argon atmosphere. Plate specimens with a gage length
of 15 mm, gage width of 3 mm, and gage thickness of 2 mm were
prepared for the tensile tests.
Tensile tests were performed o n an Instron mechanical test
system at room temperature and a strain rate of 5 /C2 10/C0 4 s/C0 1.
The strain during the entire testing process was monitored using an
extensometer with a 12.5 mm gage length. The phase structure was
examined by x-ray diffraction (XRD) using CuKa radiation. An optical
microscope (OM) and a transmissi on electron microscope (TEM)
were used for microstructure examination. TEM specimens were
prepared by twin-jet electrochemical polishing in a solution of 10%
perchloric acid and 90% methanol at 13 V and 238 K.
3. Results and discussion
Fig. 1 shows the tensile properties of Zr alloys, namely, Zr-
based BMGs, conventional Zr alloys (Zr-4 and Zr-2.5Nb alloys),
pure Zr, and the fabricated 47Zr alloy. The stress–strain curves of
the 47Zr alloy after solution and age treatments in Fig. 1 (a) show
excellent tensile properties, from 1376 MPa to 1638 MPa for
strength and from 1.5% to 7.6% for ductility in terms of elongation.
The examined Zr-based crystal alloy also has a high strength of
1638 MPa while aged at 500 1C for 4 h. The distinct tensile
ductility value with elongation is approximately 1.5%, as shown
in the stress–strain curve in Fig. 1 (a). The ductility in elongation
can reach 6.2%, and, at the same time, the tensile strength can
remain above 1400 MPa while aged at 575 1C for 4 h. According to
previous studies [4,16–19], the tensile strengths of Zr-based
BMGs range from 1500 MPa to 2000 MPa, and their ductilities,
as shown in a block diagram ﬁlled by slanting lines in Fig. 1(b), are
below 0.25% in elongation. For example, although the tensile
strength of Vit 1 Zr-based BMG was as high as 1840 MPa, the
tensile ductility in terms of elongation was only 0.2%. The
strength of the examined Zr-based alloy is comparable to that
of Zr-based BMGs. At the same time, the ductility of the former far
exceeds that of Zr-based BMGs. Furthermore, the remarkable
strengthening effect is clearly shown in Fig. 1(b), which compares
the strength of the examined Zr alloy with the existing Zr-4 and
Zr-2.5Nb alloys that are typically used in the nuclear industry.

4. Conclusions
Compared to the BMGs, the mechanical properties of crystal
alloys can be widely adjusted for the usage requirements, and most
of the weaknesses obstructing the development of BMGs can be
resolved in crystal alloys. A crystal Zr-based alloy with composition
of 47Zr-45Ti-5Al-3V is described in this paper. The strength of the
47Zr alloy reached 1638 MPa, comparable to most Zr-based BMGs.
Its ductility in terms of elongation increased to 6.2%, while its tensile
strength remained over 1400 MPa, which is not possible for Zr-based
BMGs. The ultrahigh-strength Zr-based alloy is composed of two a
and b ductile solid solution phases, ensuring good ductility. The
microstructure of the Zr alloy can a lso be adjusted through various
heat treatments to regulate mechanical properties. Overall, the
fabricated Zr alloy is a potential structural material.
