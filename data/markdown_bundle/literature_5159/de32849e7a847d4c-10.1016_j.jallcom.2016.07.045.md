Abstract 
The superplastic properties of two high strength al uminium alloys are investigated. The first 
alloy contains fine, coherent Al 
3(Zr,Sc) particles, and the second alloy contains bo th fine, coherent 
Al 3Zr particles and coarse, eutectic Al 3Ni particles. Due to its fine, stable grain structure, the alloy 
containing both Al 3Zr and Al 3Ni particles exhibits significantly better superpla stic properties. The 
superplastic behaviour in tension under both consta nt and initial strain rates was compared, and the 
effective activation energy of superplastic deforma tion was calculated. The alloy containing both fine 
and coarse particles exhibit 1100%-1200% elongation at strain rates of 1×10 -2 s -1 - 1×10 -1 s -1 and 
temperature of 440 °C.

(10

temperature stability [1, 2]. Superplasticity may b e used for superplastic forming, which is an effici ent

Superplastic forming at very high strain rates (gre ater than 1×10 –2 s-1) significantly improves 
productivity, which leads to increased economic and technical efficiency [4]. Conventional wrought

Annealing after thermomechanical processing with la rge plastic deformations is required to 
recrystallize and refine the grains to achieve supe rplasticity in aluminium alloys [1, 7]. Methods bas ed

parameters [8, 9]) are used to form fine-grained st ructures that are stable at high temperature. The 
presence of particles significantly affects the dis location structure and grain and sub-grain sizes du ring 
recrystallization and superplastic deformation [10-14]. Coarse particles (larger than 0.75-1 µm [15, 16]) 
promote particle stimulated nucleation (PSN), which may result in a fine-grained, recrystallised 
microstructure [12,16-17]. Fine particles (smaller than 0.1 µm) may create a non-recrystallised 
structure at the beginning of the superplastic defo rmation process (Supral 100 [18, 19], AA8090 [20]) 
and may inhibit grain growth due to the Zener pinni ng effect [11, 21]. The addition of both Zr and Sc 
significantly improves the superplasticity and the strength of Al-based alloys [22-24]. Al 3Sc, Al 3(Sc,Zr) 
and Al 3Zr dispersoids in the 5-10 nm size range prevent re crystallization upon heating to the 
superplastic deformation temperature [18, 25-27]. T hus, the misorientation angle between subgrains 
increases and a fine-grained microstructure with hi gh-angle boundaries develops during superplastic 
deformation due to a dynamic recrystallization proc ess. Unfortunately, the addition of just 0.2% Sc, 
which is rare and expensive, increases the cost of the sheets by a factor of 5-10 compared with 
conventional Al-based alloys. The significant increases in cost limit the use of Sc–containing materials. 
However, there have been several recent efforts to replace Sc with other elements in superplastic 
materials. Our research focuses on the comparison of the superplastic behaviour and the microstructural 
evolution of two high-strength aluminium alloys: a Sc-containing alloy with fine particles and a Sc-free 
alloy containing both fine Al 3Zr and coarse Al 3Ni particles.

4. Discussion 
4.1. Superplastic behaviour and effective activation energy 
The shapes of the stress-strain curves for both alloys are different. Alloy B exhibits a stable and 
uniform flow during superplastic deformation withou t significant work hardening or softening after 
100%-150% strain. A decrease of the stress values d uring the initial stage of deformation may be a 
result of dynamic recrystallization. For Sc-contain ing alloy A, the stress values decrease during 
superplastic deformation, which may be a result of continuous dynamic recrystallization and Al 3(Zr,Sc) 
particle coarsening. 
The experimental strain rate sensitivity exponents are 0.38-0.47 for alloy A and 0.45-0.47 for 
alloy B. These values are close to 0.5, which is a typical value for fine-structure superplasticity [1 ]. 
Grain boundary sliding (GBS) is the predominant deformation mechanism during superplastic flow for 
most fine-grained materials with strain rate expone nts of approximately 0.5 and an activation energy 
which is either equal to the activation energy for lattice diffusion (Q L) or to the activation energy for 
grain-boundary diffusion (Q GB ) [1,49]. In alloy A, the effective activation ener gy for superplastic 
deformation is similar to the energy for grain boun dary self-diffusion of Al (Q = 85 kJ/mol ≈ QGB ). In 
alloy B, the effective activation energy for superp lastic deformation is similar to the lattice self-
diffusion activation energy of Al (Q = 142 kJ/mol ≈ QL) at strain values of less than 200% and is near 
the energy of grain boundary self-diffusion at larg er strains of 300% and 400%. As a result, the value s

4.2. Evolution of grain structure and analysis of the dislocation structure 
Increased dynamics in the recrystallised volume dur ing superplastic deformation indicates that 
dynamic recrystallization occurs very quickly in al loy B possibly due to particle stimulated nucleatio n 
(PSN) and dynamic recrystallization is slow in alloy A due to a low volume fraction of coarse particles. 
The highly deformed zones around the coarse particl es form during cold rolling and stimulate the 
recrystallization process during superplastic defor mation in alloy B. As a result, in alloy A, the flo w 
stress decreases during superplastic deformation as the strain increases due to continuous dynamic 
recrystallization. In alloy B, stable flow occurs without any significant softening after 100-200% strain 
because of fast PSN and dynamic recrystallization d uring the initial stages of deformation and the 
stable grain structure at large strains. 
It is most probable that less cavity in alloy B is the result of a finer grain size. In addition, 
the GBS begins to dominate after 200% deformation, which is the most common cause of porosity [51] 
TEM analysis of the microstructure confirmed that t he coarse 1.5 µm Al 
3Ni particles provide 
nucleation sites for new grains and a larger number of nuclei for recrystallization due to the PSN. Ne w 
grains are observed near the eutectic Al 3Ni particles in alloy B. In alloy A, no such effect is observed. 
The results of the microstructural analysis and the superplastic behaviour studies of the alloys 
can be summarized. Both the Al 3(Zr,Sc) dispersoids in alloy A and the Al 3Zr dispersoids in alloy B 
partially inhibit the recrystallization process during heating to the superplastic deformation temperature

5. Conclusion 
 
The aluminium alloy with a bimodal particle size di stribution (coarse Al 3Ni and fine Al 3Zr) 
exhibits finer and more stable grains at superplast ic deformation compared with the analogous alloy 
containing only fine Al 3(Sc,Zr) particles. 
A fine grain structure with the grain size of 2.2±0 .1 µm of the alloy with the bimodal particle 
size distribution is the result of extensive recrys tallisation during the first 200% of superplastic 
deformation; the recrystallisation volume increases from 0.30 to 0.82 as the strain increases from 0 t o 
200% at a temperature of 440 °C and a strain rate of 1×10 -2 s-1. The alloy exhibits a stable, uniform flow 
after strains of 200% without softening. In contrast, recrystallised grain size is 3.7±0.3 µm, the vol ume 
fraction of the recrystallised grains increases fro m 0.1 only to 0.5 as the strain increases from 0 to 
200% in the alloy containing only fine precipitates and significant softening occurs at superplastic 
deformation. 
As a result, the experimental alloy containing Ni a nd Zr exhibits 450%-850% of elongation at 
constant strain rates ranging from 10 -2 to 10 -1 s -1, whereas the alloy with Zr and Sc exhibits 300%-
600% elongation at significantly lower constant str ain rates of 10 -3 - 10 -2 s-1, and no superplasticity is 
observed at higher strain rates. 
The elongation to failure in the tensile tests with initial strain rates is 1.4-2.0 times larger in both 
alloys compared to in the tensile tests with consta nt strain rates. Elongations of 1200% and 1100% at 
initial strain rates of 1×10 -2 s-1 and 1×10 -1 s-1, respectively, are achieved in the alloy with the bimodal 
particle size distribution.
