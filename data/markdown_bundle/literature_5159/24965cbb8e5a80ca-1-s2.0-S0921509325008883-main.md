ABSTRACT
In this study, we systematically explored the effect of Ta, Mo, and V on the structure, mechanical properties, and 
oxidation behaviour of a single-phase body-centred cubic (bcc) NbTiZr alloy. Alloying with Ta or Mo in con -
centrations of 10, 25, or 40 at% retained the single-phase bcc structure, yet led to elemental segregations, with 
the formation of Ta/Mo-rich dendrites. Among the V-containing alloys, the (NbTiZr)

were thoroughly examined through a comparison of experimental and computational data.
1. Introduction
Complex concentrated alloys (CCAs), which are a more broad defi -
nition of high/medium-entropy alloys (HEAs/MEAs), represent a new 
class of alloys composed of multiple elements. Due to a flexibility of 
compositional space, CCAs can achieve excellent properties, thereby 
attracting huge attention for different potential applications. Specif -
ically, CCAs based on refractory elements (RCCAs) are considered as 
promising candidates for the aerospace sector, including aircraft and 
hypersonic vehicles [ 1 – 15 ].
RCCAs with a single-phase body-centred cubic (bcc) structure gain 
notable interest owing to their exceptional high-temperature mechani -
cal properties [ 16 – 23 ]. Among these RCCAs, the NbMoTaW alloy stands 
out for its remarkable strength at 1600 
◦
C, with a yield strength, σ
YS
, of 
405 MPa [ 16 ]. However, its practical application is constrained by a low 
room-temperature plasticity ( < 2 %) and a high density ( > 13 g/cm
3
). In 
contrast, another single-phase bcc alloy, NbTiZr, offers a more balanced 
mechanical properties ( σ
YS 
= 975 MPa and ε > 50 % in compression [ 24 ] 
or σ
YS 
= 630 MPa and ε > 20 % in tension [ 25 ]), along with a relatively 
low density (6.34 g/cm
3

suffers from rapid softening at elevated temperatures and susceptibility 
to pesting oxidation [ 26 ].
One way to improve the performance of RCCAs is the increasing 
number of components [ 9 , 27 , 28 ]. This approach has been verified for 
the Nb-Ti-Zr-Cr alloy system [ 26 ] and found to be most successful in 
single-phase Nb-Ti-Zr compositions. For them, simultaneously higher

The efficient development of (R)CCAs requires the use of computa -

In recent years, approaches based on machine learning (ML) algorithms 
that can achieve high prediction accuracy of the mechanical properties 
[ 40 – 44 ] and oxidation resistance [ 45 – 48 ] for both single- and 
multi-phase alloys have been developed. However, the validity of these 
ML techniques is yet to be experimentally verified for different con -
centrations of certain elements.
Therefore, the present study pursued two main goals: (i) to explore 
the effect of Ta, Mo, and V added in concentrations of 10 – 40 at% on the 
structure, compression mechanical properties, and oxidation behaviour 
of the NbTiZr alloy, and (ii) to compare experimental properties with ML 
predictions to estimate the credibility of ML approaches.

4. Discussion
4.1. Effect of alloying on the structure
First, we would like to analyse the effect of chemical composition on 
the phase structure of the alloys studied, mostly using the CALPHAD 
( Fig. 8 ). The “ base ” NbTiZr alloy has a relatively narrow crystallisation 
range (1824-1690 
◦
C) and stable single-phase bcc structure, with a 
probable precipitation of a secondary Nb-rich bcc phase at 694 
◦
C.
Additions of Ta ( Fig. 8 a) result in the following changes (i): a gradual 
increase in both solidification temperature ( Table S2 ) and interval; (ii) a 
gradual increase of the solvus temperature of a secondary Zr-rich bcc 
phase; (iii) a possible precipitation of a hcp phase at low temperatures. 
Mo additions have the similar effects ( Fig. 8 b), with some differences: (i) 
the solvus temperature of the secondary Zr-rich bcc phase reaches 
maximum at ~18 at% of Mo, and then decreases slightly; (ii) in addition 
to the hcp phase, the precipitation of a Laves phase at a high Mo content 
is expected. The effect of V is very different ( Fig. 8 c). First, V additions 
are predicted to decrease the solidification temperature, yet have a 
negligible effect on the solidification interval. Second, the solvus tem -
perature of the secondary Nb-rich bcc phase remains constant until V 
content reaches ~10 at% and then increases gradually.
CALPHAD modelling provides valuable insights into the phase for -
mation in the alloys studied. For instance, the similar behaviour of the 
Ta- and Mo-containing alloys during solidification is expected from the 
phase diagrams ( Fig. 8 ), which agrees with the experimental data 
( Figs. 1 and 2 ). The formation of dendritic segregations in these alloys is 
primarily caused by an increased solidification range, together with the 
repulsive interactions between some elements, as indicated by the pos -
itive mixing enthalpies ( Table 6 ). Note that, although in the present 
study single-phase bcc structure was found in the as-cast Ta- and Mo- 
containing alloys, it was not stable at certain temperatures. Indeed, 
the phase decomposition was found in the alloys with 25 and 40 at% of 
Ta or Mo as a response to compression at 1000 
◦

the relevance of CALPHAD predictions for a wide temperature interval.
Meanwhile, the phase formation in the V-containing alloys differed 
greatly. For these alloys, the solidification range is expected to be rela -

moreover, phase separation accompanied by the emergence of three

4.2. Effect of alloying on the mechanical properties
As the alloys studied mostly have the solid solution structures in the 
as-cast state, it is anticipated that their mechanical strength will be 
governed by solid solution hardening [ 55 ]. The solid solution hardening 
by the element i [ 56 , 57 ] is proportional to the atomic size ( ϵ
α

G

atomic radii and shear moduli of the constitutive elements are pre -

expected to cause the most significant lattice distortions in the NbTiZr 
alloy. When Ta, Mo, or V are added to the NbTiZr alloy, Ta and, espe -
cially, Mo should have a strong solid solution hardening due to signifi -
cantly higher shear moduli. These expectations correlate well with the 
experimental findings, where Mo has the strongest solid solution hard -
ening effect, and V - the weakest ( Fig. 5 ).

4.3. Effect of alloying on the oxidation behaviour
Our results demonstrated that the alloying with Ta, Mo, and V 
aggravated the oxidation resistance of the NbTiZr alloy at relatively long
