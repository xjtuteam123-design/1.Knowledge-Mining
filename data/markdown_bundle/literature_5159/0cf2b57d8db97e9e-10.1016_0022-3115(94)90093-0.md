Effect of fast-neutron irradiation on tensile properties 
of precipitation-hardened Cu-Cr-Zr alloy 
P. Fenici a, D.J. Boerman a, G.P. Tartaglia a, J.D. Elen b 
a Commission of the European Communities, Joint Research Centre, Institute for Advanced Materials, 21020, Ispra, Varese, Italy

Abstract 
High-strength, high-conductivity copper alloys are suitable heat-sink materials for the divertor of magnetically 
confined fusion reactors subject to high thermal loads. 
Tensile specimens of a commercial high-strength Cu, Cr, Zr alloy were irradiated in the HFR reactor in Petten at 
150 and 250°C. The total accumulated damage (10 dpa) and the damage production rate (3.3 x lo-’ dpa/s) 
correspond to the envisaged operating requirements of the ITER divertor. Tensile testing after irradiation was 
performed in the LMA hot laboratory at JRC Ispra, at a single constant strain rate of 2.9 X 10e4/s. 
At 155°C an increase of yield strength (YS) was measured and a decrease of uniform elongation (UE) from 5% to 
virtually zero. Persisting high total elongation (TE) values of about 9% indicate that the ductile fracture mode is not 
affected. Irradiation hardening at 255°C is less pronounced. 
In the light of this study and of published results, the temperature of 275°C is proposed as a conservative 
temperature limit for use of fully precipitation hardened Cu-Cr-Zr (Mg) alloys to exclude irradiation softening. 
1. Introduction 
Testing of a commercial high-strength Cu-0.65% 
Cr-O.O8%Zr alloy started at JRC in support of an early 
divertor design study [ll. Further developments in these 
studies for NET have been described by Boutard [2,3]. 
The basic concept for a divertor collector plate is 
having a water-cooled heat-sink covered by a layer of 
low-sputtering, low-Z and heat-resistant material as 
arniour against incoming plasma particles. Because high 
thermal conductivity is the most important require- 
ment for a heat sink material, general attention for 
possibilities to strengthen pure copper with a fine 
dispersion of second phases came naturally. There are 
two venues: oxide dispersion hardening (ODH) and 
precipitation hardening (PH). The first way is a devel- 
opment in powder metallurgy, using internal oxidation 
of a dilute Cu-Al alloy in powder form as the critical 
step to obtain ultimately a small enough Al,O, parti- 
cle size. The second is solid-state precipitation of inter- 
metallic compounds or elements from solution-an- 
nealed and quenched alloys. The Cu-Cr-Zr alloy of 
our present work is a well established commercial 
product of this type. Other similar products may con- 
tain a trace of Mg, used to get an oxygen-free melt. 
They are included in our references to other irradia- 
tion experiments. Eventually joining of armour to 
heat-sink will impose limitations. High-temperature 
brazing to graphite tiles, for instance, would soften PH 
copper alloys. However such considerations may be 
premature, since design studies are still in evolution. A 
new concept of a gas-cooled pumped divertor for ITER 
has recently been proposed [4]. 
A wide range of experimental copper alloys have 
been irradiated in the FFIF reactor at 415°C to 150 
dpa, including three varieties of ODH Cu-Al and four 
of PH Cu-Cr-Zr-(Mg) [5,6]. Both groups are equally 
good in swelling resistance - swelling ranging from 
zero to about 4% - but the ODH alloys show better 
strength retention at a level of about 400 MPa com- 
pared with softening of the PH alloys to 150 MPa. 
Softening of PH Cu-Cr-Zr-(Mg) has also been ob- 
served in other irradiations at lower temperatures down 
to 300°C [7-111. On the other side of the temperature

2. Material characterization 
The material of nominal composition Cu-0.65%Cr- 
O.O8%Zr is a commercial product of Kabelmetal 
(Osnabruck, Germany) under trade name ELBRO- 
DUR HF. It was procured in cold-worked and precipi- 
tation-hardened condition. The production route was: 
solution annealing at 1000°C after extrusion to 45 mm, 
cold drawing to 25 mm (44% diameter reduction) and 
tempered for one hour at 460°C to reach an optimal 
condition of room-temperature yield strength and duc- 
tility, respectively about 500 MPa and 20%. The actual 
chemical analysis is given in Table 1. The composition 
is within the DIN 17666 specification. 
Its microstructure has been described earlier in 
sufficient detail [12,13]. The grain size is approximately 
35 pm. A small population of relatively large (micron 
range) second-phase particles is attributed to segrega- 
tion of Cr and Cu,Zr, at ingot solidification. Strength- 
ening and delay of recrystallization are due to a high 
density, small size (nm range) and, from TEM strain 
contrast, apparently coherent Cu-Zr precipitation of 
unresolved composition. 
The temperature dependence of the tensile proper- 
ties has been reported separately [13]. Comparison 
with earlier tests on hard-drawn OFHC copper [14] in 
Fig. 1, shows the magnitude of both beneficial effects. 
The strength is increased by about 60 MPa. The soften- 
ing temperature is shifted from 250 to 5WC, just 
above the applied optimal precipitation hardening tem- 
perature. For pure copper it is directly followed by 
rapid dynamic recovery and recrystallization - during 
the typically short elevated temperature dwell time of a 
tensile test - as is evident from the loss of strength and 
large elongation to rupture. The more gradual decline 
for the alloy between 500 and 650°C is attributed to 
dynamic coarsening, overaging, of the precipitate, be- 
coming less effective in dislocation pinning, which again 
is opposing cold-work recovery and recrystallization. 
3. Experimental 
18 tensile specimens (4 mm diameter, gauge length 
20 mm> were irradiated in the HFR reactor at Petten. 
Samples were distributed in two sample holders loaded 
in the Siena capsule, a device with spectrum tailoring 
features offering the possibility to irradiate 22 sample 
holders simultaneously and separately controlled. Both 
sample holders were irradiated up to 10 dpa, one at 
15O”C, the second at 250°C. The capsule was loaded in 
the core position C5 allowing a damage rate of about 
3.3 x 10e7 dpa/s. This value corresponds to the oper- 
ating requirements for the ITER divertor, as reported 
by Boutard [2]. 
Samples were kept at constant temperature by ad- 
justing the composition of the helium-neon gas mix- 
ture flowing in the gaps between samples and irradia- 
tion channels. Moreover a vertical displacement unit 
allowed the sample columns to be moved, in order to 
follow the shift of the neutron fluence rate curve due 
to reactor operation. Five thermocouples per sample 
holder, fixed at the midpoint of every other sample in 
the stack, continuously monitored the actual sample 
temperatures. Details on the irradiation history, statis- 
tical evaluation of the temperature distribution during 
the irradiation are given in the irradiation report by 
Tartaglia [ 151.

of JRC Ispra. A single constant strain rate of 2.9 x

5. Discussion 
Thin miniature samples of the same ELBRODUR 
HF batch have been irradiated earlier to 0.2 dpa at 
295°C in a 18 MeV proton beam of the MC-40 cy- 
clotron at JRC-Ispra, for a first assessment of the 
stability of the Cr-Zr precipitation strengthening un- 
der damage production rates relevant to fusion reac- 
tors. A 53% decrease of YS was measured [16]. There 
were at that time some results available from fast 
neutron irradiations. Irradiations in the FFTF and 
EBR-2 sodium-cooled fast reactors at 385-450°C to 
15-16 dpa produced in similar materials a decrease in 
YS of about 34% and of about 12% for an interrnedi- 
ate dose point of 3 dpa [7,8]. Another alloy of the same 
composition was irradiated to 4 dpa in the BR-2 reac- 
tor at computed temperatures of 150 and 300°C result- 
ing respectively in 4% increase and 3% decrease of YS 
[9]. Compared with these reactor irradiation results, 
the appreciable softening after the cyclotron irradia- 
tion was surprising, considering its low dpa level.

6. Conclusions 
Tensile specimens of a commercial precipitation- 
hardened Cu-Cr-Zr alloy were irradiated in the HFR 
reactor to 10 dpa at 155°C and 255°C. The damage and 
damage production rate correspond to the 14 MeV 
neutron exposure envisaged in NET divertor design 
studies. Post-irradiation experiments were performed 
at irradiation temperature. For 155°C an increase of 
YS was measured from 423 MPa to 526 MPa and a 
decrease of UE from 5% to virtually zero. Persisting 
high TE values of about 9% indicate that the ductile 
fracture mode is not affected. Irradiation hardening at 
255°C is less pronounced. YS did increase from 383 
MPa to 417 MPA while UE actually improved from 
2% to 4%. 
The separation between irradiation hardening at 
the lower end of the irradiation temperature scale and 
irradiation softening at the upper end is demonstrated 
and compared with published results from other 
sources. The present HFR irradiations are shown to 
narrow the information gap. 275°C is proposed as a 
conservative temperature limit for use of fully precipi- 
tation-hardened Cu-Cr-Zr-(Mg) alloys in order to 
exclude irradiation softening.
