Abstract
The Mg-6.0Gd-1.2Cu-1.2Zr (wt%) alloy was designed, then processed by solution treatment, as well
as the following single-aging and double-aging treatments respectively. The mechanical behavior of
the alloys at ambient temperature and 150°C was evaluated. The results indicate that the ambient
temperature yield strength of the alloy is slightly reduced after solution treatment but is enhanced after
following single-aging and double-aging treatments. At 150°C, the yield strength of the as-cast alloy
decreases by 18.0%, and those of the heat-treated alloys are reduced from 1.2% to 17.7% as compared
to those at ambient temperature. Aging treatment results in higher yield strength decrease at elevated
temperature than solution treatment.
1. Introduction
Magnesium (Mg) and its alloys exhibit high speciﬁc strength and speciﬁc stiffness among the engineering
structural materials [1– 5]. Their unique characteristic of low density plays a crucial role in additional weight
reductions, leading to fuel economy[6, 7]. And thus, Mg alloys have been continuously studied for the potential
applications of the various industries, such as aerospace, automotive, high-speed train, and other applications
[8– 10]. However, the absolute strength of the Mg alloys at ambient temperature has been proven to be poor as
compared to that of traditional structural materials, and their strength loss at elevated temperatures was still
undesirable [11, 12]. These performances could not meet the actual requirements during the service process.
Therefore, more attention should be paid to enhancing the elevated temperature mechanical properties of Mg
alloys.
It has been conﬁrmed that Cu-containing Mg alloys showed good mechanical properties at room
temperature [13– 19]. The addition of Cu element contributed to grain reﬁnement, and Mg-Zn-Cu-Zr alloy with
relatively low content of Cu exhibited high tensile strength, however, the deteriorated mechanical properties
were obtained with high addition of Cu[15]. The positive inﬂuences of Cu on the grain size reduction and
strengthening phase formation were also veriﬁed in ZK60 alloy[16, 17], Mg-3Zn-1Y alloy[18], and Mg-8Sn-
2Zn alloy [19]. Mg-RE (RE is short for rare earth) based alloys have attracted increasingly attention recently
owing to the modiﬁed tensile strength, ductility and hardness etc, and the fundamental reasons of that could be
the grain reﬁnement, solid solution strengthening and precipitation strengthening caused by the addition of RE
elements [20– 28]. Research has found that a serious lattice distortion was generated owing to the dissolution of
Gd into theα-Mg matrix, and hence Mg-Gd-Y-Nd-Zr alloy showed increased yield strength. In addition, this
alloy under aging state maintained high ultimate tensile strength and yield strength even at elevated temperature
(300 °
C) [22]. The presence of Gd element was also proven to be an effective method to enhance high
temperature deformation resistance[25]. Pourbahari Bet al [26] have reported that Gd element in the as-cast
Mg-6Gd-1Zn alloy was partially substituted with Al element, therefore, mechanical properties were enhanced
since the types and morphology of intermetallic compound were changed, and grain size of the matrix was
reﬁned. This result meant that Mg-RE alloys may also be strengthened by the appropriate addition of other
alloying elements [29– 33]. For instance, Zn element added in the Mg-10Gd-3Y-0.5Zr alloy played a crucial role

Solution treatment and aging treatment are common methods to enhance the mechanical properties of Mg
alloys remarkably [39– 41]. Our previous studies have found that solution treatment played a signiﬁcant role in
modifying the microstructures of Mg alloys, especially in controlling volume fraction and distribution of the
LPSO structure. It was summarized that the LPSO structure increasedﬁrst, then decreased and vanishedﬁnally
with increasing the solution temperature[38, 42, 43]. However, the mechanical properties variation has not
been clearly illustrated. Zhuet al [44] introduced double-aging treatment and concluded thatﬁne precipitates
with high density and homogeneously distribution were formed after this treatment, and hence the tensile
strength of Mg-6Zn-4Al-1Cu alloy was signiﬁcantly improved. This result was also consistent with the Mg-
2.2Sn-0.5Zn, Mg-4.5Zn-4.5Sn-2Al and Mg-6Zn-4Al-1Sn alloys reported by Sasakiet al [45], Zhang et al [46] and
Zhu et al [47] respectively. Based on the above references, the multi-steps aging treatment could be carried out
for better understanding of its effect on the mechanical behavior of LPSO structure containing Mg alloys.
In this work, Mg-6.0Gd-1.2Cu-1.2Zr(wt%) alloy was designed from perspective of the presence of the LPSO
structure. Solution treatment accompanied with single-aging treatment and double-aging treatment
respectively were performed on the as-cast alloy. The microstructural evolution and mechanical behavior of the
alloy at ambient and elevated temperatures were studied.
2. Experimental
The Mg-6.0Gd-1.2Cu-1.2Zr (wt%) alloy was prepared by gravity casting, using pure Mg(99.99%), Mg-30Gd
(wt%), Mg-30Cu (wt%) and Mg-30Zr (wt%) master alloys. These raw materials were melted under a protective
atmosphere of mixed CO2 and SF6 gas. After mechanical stirred and maintained at 720°C for 20 min, the
molten metals were poured into the molds. The speciﬁc preparation process was referred to the literature[38].
The ingots were solution treated at 350°C and 440 °C under a protective SO2 atmosphere in order to keep and
eliminate LPSO structure[38]. Single-aging treatment and double-aging treatment were then performed on the
solution-treated alloys. Speciﬁcally, the heat treatment process parameters and the corresponding labels of the
alloys were listed in table1.
The specimens for microstructural observation and mechanical properties characterization were cut by wire
cut electrical discharge machining. The investigated specimens were ground progressively and polished using
2.5 μm diamond paste, and then etched by 4 vol% nitric acid in ethanol. An optical microscopy(OM,
OLYMPLUS GX51) was utilized to characterize the microstructures of the alloys. And the microstructures with
high magniﬁcation and local chemical compositions of the alloys were analyzed by a scanning electron
microscope (SEM, Zeiss Merlin Compact) equipped with an energy dispersive spectrometer(EDS, Oxford Atec
X-max 50).
Nano hardness of different phases was measured by a nano indentation(Agilent G200) at room temperature,
following the maximum normal force of 25 mN and the duration time of 10 s. Three hardness values of each
phase were obtained to determine the average value. Tensile properties of the alloys at elevated temperature were
evaluated by an electronic material testing machine(Zwick/Roell, Z030TH) equipped with a heating chamber,
and then compared with those tested at ambient temperature. The elevated temperature was applied to be
150 °C referred to the literatures[48– 50]. Prior to tensile test at 150°C, each specimen was held for 5 min to
ensure its uniform heat. Three tensile samples with a dimension of 55 mm×15 mm×2 mm were tested for

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Microstructure evolution
The optical microstructures of the Mg-6.0Gd-1.2Cu-1.2Zr alloy under different processes are shown inﬁgure 1.
For all alloys,α-Mg matrix and eutectic phase are detected. The lamellar LPSO structure, located at the outer
edge of theα-Mg grains and showed various orientations in different grains, is observed in Alloys A-D. The
volume fraction of the LPSO structure increases after being solution-treated at 350°C for 12 h and then vanishes
at 440 °C for 12 h, as presented inﬁgures 1(a), (b) and (e) respectively. This result is similar to the previous work
in Mg-Gd-Zn-Zr and Mg-Gd-Y-Zn-Ca-Zr alloys[42, 43]. Compared with the alloy only solution treated at
350 °C, the LPSO structure undergoes a negligible variation in its volume fraction, while it seems that this
structure in the single-aging and double-aging alloys exhibit more obvious lamellar structure. The eutectic phase
generates dramatic decrease generally after solution treatment at 440°C and the following aging treatments.
Numerous particles distribute in the vicinity of the grain boundaries and the eutectic phase. The grain size of the
alloys increases after various heat treatment processes.
Figure 2 presents the SEM images of Mg-6.0Gd-1.2Cu-1.2Zr alloy under different heat treatment processes.
The lamellar LPSO structure is clear in Alloy A, B, C and D. After solution treated at 350°C and aging
treatments, the volume fraction of LPSO structure seems to be increased as compared to that of the as-cast alloy.
Nevertheless, for the Alloy E, F and G, the LPSO structure disappears. Theα-Mg matrix and eutectic phase
surrounding by massive tiny particles are recognized as the main phases of these alloys. Furthermore, it is
indicated that the eutectic phase decreases with increasing the solution temperature. The corresponding EDS
mapping results of various alloys are also shown inﬁgure 2. Zr element is distributed homogenously for the
alloys, consistent with the previous work[38]. The eutectic phase is identiﬁed to be rich in Gd and Cu elements,
and the LPSO exhibits lower Gd and Cu concentrations than the eutectic phase. The aging treated alloys seem to
exhibit higher Gd and Cu contents in the LPSO structure. However, there are not obvious variations of Gd and
Cu within theα-Mg matrix according to the mapping results.
At lower solution temperature, the volume fraction of the LPSO structure increases, while after solution
treatment at 440 °C, the LPSO structure eliminates, along with reduction of eutectic phase. The formation of the
LPSO has been proven to be ascribed to the satisfaction of composition order and stacking order, as well as the
thermodynamic and dynamic requirements[51, 52]. According to the SEM images and the corresponding EDS
mapping results, it is found that in Alloy B, C and D, the Cu concentration increases near the eutectic phase
where the LPSO structure is more easily formed, and slight variation of Gd is also observed fromﬁgure 2.L i et al
[53] have reported that when the composition requirement of LPSO structure formation was achieved, this
structure was detected at the grain boundaries. Therefore, the increased LPSO structure in this work can be
explained by the adequate Cu and Gd concentration in vicinity of the eutectic phase and grain boundaries. With
increasing the solution temperature, Gd element is further dissolved into theα
-Mg matrix, and the LPSO
vanishes [38]. Massive tiny particles rich in Gd are distributed dispersedly along grain boundaries in the Alloys
E-G, indicating dissolution of the eutectic phase, since the higher solution temperature was performed on these

4. Conclusions
(1) The as-cast Mg-6.0Gd-1.2Cu-1.2Zr alloy consists of the α-Mg matrix, eutectic phase and LPSO structure.
The LPSO structure increasesﬁrst and then vanishes with increasing solution temperature. The eutectic
phase decreases and the grain size increases after heat treatment. The aging treatments show no obvious
modiﬁcation on the solution-treated alloys.
(2) Nano hardness of different phases in Mg-6.0Gd-1.2Zn-1.2Zr alloy obeys the following order: α-Mg
matrix<LPSO structure<eutectic phase.
(3) Solution treatment at 350°C and 440 °C does not improve yield strength and ultimate tensile strength of the
as-cast alloy at ambient temperature. Nevertheless, the YS is enhanced by 8.3% to 16.6% after the following
aging treatments.
(4) 82.0%– 98.8% yield strength retention of the alloy under various conditions is detected at 150 °Ca s
compared to the yield strength at room temperature. The lowest decreasing rate of yield strength is found
after solution treatments, and the highest yield strength value is detected in the single-aging treated alloys.
(5) The mixing features of cleavage planes, tear ridges and dimples dominate the tensile fractures of the alloys at
room temperature, while at 150°C, dimples are mainly detected. The characteristic of transgranular
fracture and intergranular fracture is found from the microstructures of fractures.
