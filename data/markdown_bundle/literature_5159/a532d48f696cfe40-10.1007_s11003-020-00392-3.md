SELECTION OF THE COMPOSITION OF A MULTICOMPONENT ALLOY OF 
THE Nb–Ti–Al–X SYSTEM WITH HIGH-TEMPERATURE STRENGTH, 
HEAT RESISTANCE, AND TECHNOLOGICAL EFFICIENCY 
M. P. Brodnikovs’kyi,1 T. L. Kuznetsova, 1,2 
O. A. Rokytska,1 and Yu. Yu. Zubets 1 UDC 620.17/18, 669.018.4.672 
We establish t he physicochemical conditions required for the creation of multicomponent Nb –Ti–Al–X 
(X – Cr, Zr, Mo, Sr) niobium alloys with improved characteristics of high-temperature strength, heat r e-
sistance, and technological efficien cy. The conclusions are based on the relationship between the phys i-
cal properties of alloying elements, enthalpy of mixing, compositions of alloys, and the diagrams of state 
of the components with specific features of their crystallization caused by alloying. 
Keywords: multicomponent niobium alloys, high -entropy alloys, physicochemical conditions, specific 
high-temperature strength, heat resistance, technological efficiency. 
According to their physico mechanical and technological properties, niobium alloy s prove to be very prom-
ising for nuclear engineering, rocket-building, chemical apparatus building, etc. In order to improve the operat-
ing characteristics of gas drive s of the system of control over the flight of space rocket carriers, it is necessary to 
develop multicomponent alloys ba sed on niobium. 
By u sing the Nb –Ti–Al system as bas ic, it is possible to develop refractory alloys w hose specific weight 
does not exceed 7 g/cm3 . Moreover, as a result of additional alloying, it is possible to decrease the interval of 
crystallization (Ti, Mo, Zr), to enhance the solid-solution (Ti, Mo, Zr) and disper sion hardening (Al, Cr, Zr ), and 
to improve the heat and corrosion resistances (Al, Ti, Cr, Si). Due to the increase in the entropy of mixing, 
the multicomponent alloys give new possibilities for the formation of structure and mechanical properties. 
The high -entropy al loys (HEA ) characterized by elevated operating characteristics are regarded as promising 
materials for satisfying the indi cated technological requirements. At present , numerous HEA are investigated. 
However, these investigations are mainly devoted to the analysis of the regularities of influence of physical 
characteristics of the components of alloys (size s of atoms, chemic al activity, enthalpy of mixing, electron co n-
centration, etc.) on the properties of the produced HEA . 
The indicated conditions are necessary but not sufficient to guarantee the durability of simple solid solutions 
with bcc, fcc, and hcp lattices [1, 2]. Note that o nly HEA with certain chemical composition may form diso r-
dered solid solutions [3]. In the other alloys with negative enthalpy of mixing, high concentrations of the compo-
nents, and high rates of crystallization, one may observe the formation of various ordered systems corresponding 
to the structure of short-range order ing of metals and alloys in the liquid state at temperatures close to the mel t-
ing point [4]. In turn, the rate of crystallization depends not only on the cooling rate of the melt but also on

Therefore, all attempts to preserve the disordered solid soluti ons in high-entropy alloys at superhigh cooling

In view of the specific features of the chemical composition s of promising multicomponent niobium alloys 
of the Nb–Ti–Al system and the requirements imposed on their operating characteristics, it is insufficient to deter-

ment of these alloys, it is necessary to additionally predict the specific features of the process of crystallization 
and possible inhomogeneit ies of the chemical composition of crystallites with regard for the physicochemical 
characteristics of the components of binary or ternary diagrams of state of these systems. 
In what follows, we study physico chemical conditions required for the creation of multicomponent niobium 
alloys of the Nb– Ti–Al system with elevated high-temperature strength, heat resistance, and technological eff i-
ciency based on the correlation between the atomic physical properties of their elements correspond ing to the 
approaches used for the formation of high-entropy alloys. 
Materials and Procedure 
The experimental samples of alloys of the Nb –Ti–Al system alloyed with Cr, Zr, Mo, and Si were smelted 
in an updated MIFI 9-3 vacuum-arc melting installation in argon with the use of copper pitted crucibles 50 mm in 
diameter with water cooling. To guarantee the required chemical homogeneity of the composition, the billet was 
remelted 4–5 times by turning it over in the crucible. As a result, we obtained ingots 0.1–0.2 kg in weight from 
high-purity niobium of Nb -1 grade, iodinated zirconium and titanium, electrolytic chromium of ERKh grade, 
and A00 high-purity aluminum . Since the alloy includes volatile c omponents (chromium and alum inum), it was 
smelted in the atmosphere of high -purity argon under an excess pressure of (2−4) ⋅104 Pa . 
The chemical compositions of alloys were studied by the method of emissive spectral analysis in an ISP-30 
installation. The degree of homogeneity of the ingots was tested with the help of a probe from 3 to 35 µm2 in 
area by using a Camebax SX-50 X-ray diffraction microanalyzer. For metallographic studies of the structure s of 
cast, defo rmed, and thermally treated alloys, we used a MIM -7 microscope. The tests for heat resistance were 
carried out in a resistance electric furnace at 1000°C in the atmosphere of quiet air with periodic (after 2; 5; 7, 
and 10 h) weighing. The ranges of crystallization and the phase transformations of alloys were studied in a VDTA-
8M3 installation (differential thermal analysis of alloys).

According to the theory of short- range ordered structures of metals and alloys in the liquid state at temper a-
tures close to the melting point [4], it is possible to predict the specific featur es of the process of crystallization 
and the inhomogeneity of the chemical composition s of crystals of multicomponent alloys, including high -
entropy alloys, by using binary or te rnary diagrams of their state. 
For high-entropy alloys of the 16Al –16Cr–16Ti–16Zr–36Nb system, the values of pairwise enthalpy of mix-
ing for its binary alloys vary from 2 (for Ti –Nb) down to – 30 (for Al– Ti) and – 44 kJ/mole (for Al –Zr). 
Therefore, the formation of TiAl and ZrAl phases is probable. As follows from the diagrams of state, the binary 
alloys of this system are characterized by wide ranges of crystallization: Cr–Zr (260°C), Al–Zr (250°C), Nb–Zr 
(150°C), Al–Nb (100 °C), Zr–Si (230 °C), Nb–Si (280 °C), and Ti–Si (170 °C). In addition, the system con-
tains fusible eutectics, such as Al–Si (577°C), Cr–Zr (1332°C), Ti–Si (1350°C), Ti–Cr (1410°C), and Al–Zr 
(1490°C), and continuous solid solutions with the maximum initial temperatures of crystallization [Ti– Nb 
(2200°C) and Nb–Zr (2300°C)]. This reveals a high probability of the existence of broad intervals for the initi-
ation and termination of the process and, hence, for the formation of dendrite structure s and segregation of some 
components of the alloy. These observations confirm the results of differential thermal analysis of the alloy and its 
chemical composition. 
In our studies, we revealed dendrite structures (Fig. 1a) and the presence of a significant liquation (up to 
20–30%). It was established that the uniformity of crystallization of the components in the central parts of den-
drites and on the ir periphery is determined not by the melting points of the components but by the thermodynam-
ic parameters of their interaction [4]. As important factors of crystallization, we can mention the thermal charac-
teristics of the alloys. Thus, we arrive at the ambiguity in the interpretation of the influence of cooling rate, which 
is not always proportional to the rate of crystallization, on the stability of the bcc, fcc, and hcp lattices of simple 
solid solutions of high-entropy alloys [6]. 
In view the specific features of the thermal properties of the components of alloys , namely, the abnor-
mal decrease in the heat-conduction coefficient of niobium in the process of cooling from 102 (at 240°C) to 
53 W⋅ m–1⋅ K–1 (at 20 °C), relatively low heat conduction of zirconium [λT = (25–31) W⋅ m–1⋅ K –1] and titani-
um [(24–20) W⋅ m–1⋅ K –1], and elevated heat capacities of titanium [C p = (0.51–0.85) kJ ⋅ kg–1⋅ K –1] and alumi-
num [(0.90–1.15) kJ ⋅ kg–1⋅ K –1)], the dependence of the process of crystallization of these alloys on the cooling 
rate becomes weak er. 
By the differential thermal analysis, it can be shown that the interval of crystallization of a high -entropy al-
loy of the Nb –Ti–Al system alloyed with Cr, Zr, Mo, and Si is equal to ∼ 300°C, which significantly exceeds 
the limits admissible for the technological cast alloys. The broad ranges of c rystallization lead to the fo r-
mation of dendrite structure s and serve as causes of chemical inhomogeneity. They also promote the initiation of 
microcracks and the appearance of the intercrystalline porosity, which may negatively affect the plastic characteris-
tics of alloys, and serves as a factor responsible for the presence of large numbers of defects in cast semifinished 
products. 
By using the results of investigations, we managed to choose (both qualitatively and quantitatively ) the 
components required for the production of niobium alloys with elevated high-temperature strength, heat r e-
sistance, and technological efficiency. Our analysis was based on the relationship between the physical prope r-
ties of elements of the alloys, enthalpy of mixing, their c ompositions, diagrams of state, and physico chemical 
and thermal properties of alloys of the Nb –Ti–Al system doped with Cr, Zr, Mo, and Si.
