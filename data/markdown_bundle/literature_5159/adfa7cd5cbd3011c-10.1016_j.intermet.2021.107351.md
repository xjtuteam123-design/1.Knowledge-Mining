1. Introduction 
Ni-based superalloys have excellent high temperature strength, 
outstanding superior corrosion resistance and favorable workability, 
and are widely used in aeronautic engines and steam turbines [ 1 , 2 ]. As a 
typical precipitation-strengthened Ni-based superalloy, GH4169 is 
considered to be an ideal material for turbine blades and disks [ 3 , 4 ]. 
However, the density of GH4169 superalloy is intolerably high. Arbi -
trary utilization of GH4169 superalloy will increase the weight of 
components, reduce work efficiency and require enormous fossil fuel 
consumption. TiAl based alloys have attracted many researchers ’ 
attention because of their high temperature strength, superior oxidation 
resistance and low density, etc. The application of TiAl turbine blades in 
GE ’ s commercial aircraft has inspired the development of TiAl alloys in 
the aerospace industries [ 5 , 6 ]. The addition of high Nb content in TiAl 
alloys can refine the microstructures, benefit the oxidation resistance 
and enhance the high temperature strength. In recent years, consider -
able efforts for accelerating the application of high Nb – TiAl alloys have 
been made. Joining has been found to be an effective way to use the 
advantages of both the Ni-based superalloys and high Nb – TiAl alloys. 
The utilized technology for joining two alloys contains arc welding 
[ 7 ], laser welding [ 8 ] and friction welding [ 9 ], all the joints are obtained 
by melting the substrate. Due to the high sensitivity of TiAl-based alloys 
to cracking and high brittleness at low temperatures, these fusion 
welding technologies are inapplicable for joining with other alloys or 
itself. It is reported that satisfactory joint can be obtained by diffusion 
bonding [ 10 , 11 ] or brazing [ 12 , 13 ]. The diffusion bonding, however, is 
not efficient enough, and the extensive industrial application is limited 
by multiple factors such as technology, welding processes. Brazing is an 
appropriate technique for bonding the TiAl-based alloy and the Ni-based 
superalloy due to its convenience, low cost, flexible shape and size of 
joint, and satisfactory joint-quality. Sequeiros et al. [ 14 ] reported that 
the brazing of γ -TiAl alloy and Inconel 718 Ni-based superalloy using 
Ag-27.5Cu-12.5In-1.25Ti (wt. %) filler metal. The hardness of the joint 
decreased from the periphery to the center, and the shear strength 
reached 228 MPa at room temperature. Ren et al. [ 15 ] fabricated the 
Ag-32.6Cu-21.5Pd (at. %) filler metal and employed it in brazing the 
Ti

2. Experimental 
The base alloys used in this experiment were high Nb – TiAl alloys and 
GH4169 superalloys. Table 1 presents the chemical composition of the 
base alloys. Pure Nb (99.9%), Al (99.9%) and Ti (99.9%) were used to 
prepare high Nb – TiAl alloy by induction melting technique. During the 
melting process, electromagnetic stirring was utilized to ensure a ho -
mogeneous composition of the alloy ingots when all raw materials were 
fully melted. The microstructure of the high Nb – TiAl alloy is shown in 
Fig. 1 (a), displaying γ grains and ( α
2

mercial GH4169 alloy was purchased from Beijing Jinxin Co., Ltd, which 
mainly consists of γ and δ phase, as shown in Fig. 1 (b). Amorphous 
brazing foils with a melting point of 860 
◦
C were fabricated by the single 
roller melt-spinning technique. The nominal composition is about 
Cu41.8Ti30.2Zr19.8Ni8.2 (wt. %). To obtain alloy ingots of homoge -
neous composition, all the raw materials used for the fabrication of the 
amorphous filler were first melted five times in an argon atmosphere. 
The ingots were cut into Φ 8 × 10 mm and placed in a quartz tube to be 
remelted in an argon atmosphere. The melted alloy was rapidly cooled 
by ejecting it onto a Cu roller rotating at 40 m/s. The amorphous filler 
ribbons with a width of about 2 mm and a thickness of about 30 μ m were 
prepared. 
The high Nb – TiAl alloy and GH4169 alloy were 4 × 4 × 4 mm
3 
and 
10 × 10 × 4 mm
3 
in dimensions respectively. The surfaces were 
grounded to 2000 grit with SiC sandpaper. Ultrasonic cleaning of the 
substrate and brazing foils were carried out in anhydrous ethanol for 10 
min. All samples were assembled into the structure shown in Fig. 2 (a). 
Mo blocks were used to maintain proper contact between the samples. 
Brazing experiments were conducted in a vacuum furnace with a vac -
uum level of about 2.0 × 10
 3

◦

binder, then heated to the brazing temperature for 10 min. After 
brazing, the samples were cooled to room temperature at 5 
◦

The microstructure of the high Nb – TiAl/GH4169 joint was charac -
terized using scanning electron microscopy (SEM) affiliated with energy 
dispersive spectroscopy (EDS). The standard samples were used to cor -
rect for atomic number effects (Z), absorption (A) and fluorescence (F) to 
improve the accuracy of chemical composition analysis. Phase identifi -
cation in the joint was carried out by the X-ray diffractometer (XRD). 
The shear strength of the joint was evaluated with an electronic uni -
versal testing machine at a constant speed of 0.5 mm/min. During shear 
testing, at least five joints were measured to obtain an average value. 
The schematic diagram of the shear test is depicted in Fig. 2 (b). The 
morphology and path of fracture were characterized by SEM after the 
shear test.
