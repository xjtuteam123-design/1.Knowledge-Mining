Abstract
A novel Nb–V–Ce multi-microalloyed low-alloy cast steel for offshore platform nodes was investigated to achieve the
properties similar to X80 pipeline steel, including high yield strength and low-temperature impact energy. The experi-
mental results demonstrate that the addition of elements such as Nb, V and Ce can markedly improve the strength of the
low-alloy cast steel. Maintaining a constant level of elements such as Nb, V and Ce, while reducing the content of Si to
0.28 wt.%, leads to substantial enhancements in impact energy at -40 /C176C, meeting the mechanical properties criteria of
X80 pipeline steel. The excellent mechanical properties of the multi-microalloyed low-alloy cast steel result from the
reason that the multi-addition of microalloying elements reﬁnes the grains and facilitates the formation of nanoprecipitates
like NbC. Moreover, decreasing Si content can enhance the recovery of martensitic laths in cast steel during the tempering
process, reducing stress from dislocation movement and improving plasticity.

1 Introduction
Ocean platforms play a pivotal role in exploring and uti-
lizing marine resources, with platform nodes serving as
vital components. Presently, offshore platform nodes are
predominantly produced by welding [ 1]. However, welded
nodes present various drawbacks, like elevated stress levels
during welding, vulnerability to cracking in localized stress
concentration areas within welded parts and platform
structures, and subsequent reduction in the pressure-bear-
ing capacity of components [ 1]. In contrast, cast nodes
offer numerous advantages over welded nodes. Through
thoughtful size and shape design, cast nodes can effectively
reduce stress concentrations and boost fatigue strength
[2, 3]. Furthermore, they exhibit superior toughness in the
thickness direction, preventing layer tearing risks during
welding [3]. Cast nodes are also more cost-efﬁcient, posi-
tioning them as the preferred option for future ocean
platform joints [ 2–4]. The excellent strength, low-temper-
ature impact energy, and weldability are essential consid-
erations for offshore platform casting nodes [ 5]. However,
certain unavoidable minor defects, like shrinkage and
inclusions, may occur during the production of casting
nodes, potentially impacting their performance [ 6]. Hence,
optimizing the strength and low-temperature impact energy
of the cast steel is crucial to offset the inherent casting
defects that may compromise their mechanical properties.
This aspect has emerged as a pivotal factor in the industrial
advancement of offshore platform casting steel nodes.
Microalloying with Nb, V and rare earth (RE) is an
effective approach to enhance the overall properties of
materials, widely adopted in the steel rolling industry and
certain casting steel applications. Najaﬁ et al. [ 7] demon-
strated that the addition of 0.09 wt.% V or 0.04 wt.% Nb to
low-carbon cast steel led to signiﬁcant enhancements in
yield strength and tensile strength, coupled with a total
elongation of 20%–25%, primarily attributed to nanopre-
cipitates within the steel. Meanwhile, Gao et al. [ 8]
observed that introducing 0.044 wt.% Nb in microalloyed
cast steel raised the tensile strength to 350 MPa and yield
strength to 520 MPa, compared to 290 and 485 MPa in Nb-
free cast steel, primarily due to grain reﬁnement and the

3 Results
3.1 Microstructure characterization
Figure 2 shows OM micrographs of cast steels with various
compositions. The grain size of 0.6Si–0.04Nb–0.05V steel
is notably smaller than that of 0.6Si–0Nb–0V steel
(Fig. 2a, b). When Si content decreases from 0.63 to 0.28
wt.%, the grain size of 0.3Si–0.04Nb–0.05V steel aligns
closely with that of 0.6Si–0.04Nb–0.05V cast steel
(Fig. 2c). Comparison based on grain size grades reveals
that the addition of microalloying elements elevates the
grain size from grade 7 to grade 8. It is noteworthy that the
co-addition of microalloying elements results in a signiﬁ-
cant decrease in the proportion of grains larger than 40 lm,
dropping from 75.00% to 6.17% (Fig. 2d, e). This shift
suggests that microalloying elements play a crucial role in
promoting grain reﬁnement, while the impact of Si on grain
size is minimal (Fig. 2e, f). The microstructure of 0.6Si–
0Nb–0V cast steel consists of tempered martensite and a
minor amount of ferrite (Fig. 2g). With the addition of 0.04
wt.% Nb, 0.05 wt.% V and 0.02 wt.% Ce, the width of
martensite lath in 0.6Si–0.04Nb–0.05V steel decreases
(Fig. 2h). Upon reducing Si content to 0.28 wt.%, the
microstructure of 0.3Si–0.04Nb–0.05V steel retains tem-
pered martensite and block ferrite, with the tempered
martensite displaying a shift toward equiaxed grains
(Fig. 2i). This suggests that the tempering stability of
martensite is signiﬁcantly impacted by the silicon content.
Figure 3 shows scanning electron microscope (SEM)
micrographs of cast steels with various compositions.
Nanoprecipitates are observed at the boundaries of

addition, with the addition of microalloying elements, the
width of martensitic lath also decreases, which is consistent
with the trend of grain size in Fig. 2. Statistical analysis is
carried out to evaluate the size distribution and content of
nanoprecipitates (Fig. 3g–j). The addition of microalloying
elements leads to a noticeable increase in the proportion of
small-sized nanoprecipitates (Fig. 3g, h). When the content
of microalloying elements remains constant, and Si content

4.1 Thermodynamics of nanoprecipitates
Figure 7 shows the equilibrium phase fraction diagrams of
different steels. According to the general principles of
precipitation formation, nanoprecipitates must be formed
within the solid phase region. Therefore, in this study, we
refer to the precipitates formed in the solid phase as
nanoprecipitates. When no microalloying elements are
added, the main nanoprecipitate is M
7C3. However, when
the co-addition of Nb, V and Ce elements is added, in
addition to M 7C3, a certain amount of NbC, V(C, N) and
(Mo, V)C are also observed (Fig. 7a, b). This ﬁnding
suggests that the combined addition of microalloying ele-
ments enhances the formation of nanoprecipitates, which
aligns with the experimental results shown in Fig. 3j.
When Si content is reduced to 0.28 wt.%, there is no sig-
niﬁcant change in the type, precipitation temperature, or
quantity of nanoprecipitates. This indicates that, from a
thermodynamic perspective, variations in Si content do not
signiﬁcantly inﬂuence the precipitation of nanoprecipitates.
The thermodynamic calculations show that the equilib-
rium precipitates consist of M
7C3, (Mo, V)C, V(C, N) and
NbC, along with a minor presence of M 23C6. However, the
observed actual samples only show M 23C6 and NbC pre-
cipitates, with V largely concentrated in M23C6 precipitates
(Fig. 5k). This discrepancy can be attributed to the fact that
under equilibrium conditions, atoms have sufﬁcient time
for diffusion, enabling the nucleation and growth of pre-
cipitates. However, the non-equilibrium state of the actual
phase transition involves ﬂuctuating temperatures or brief
isothermal periods. Consequently, some precipitates that
would form under equilibrium conditions lack the neces-
sary time to generate. Therefore, certain precipitates pre-
sent in the equilibrium state are absent in real samples.

sulfur than iron, leading to the formation of rare earth
inclusions that can purify molten steel and improve inclu-

0.04Nb–0.05V steels revealed distinctive characteristics. In

dominant, accompanied by a minor presence of Al
2O3,

xAlyOz–

RE elements can effectively modify inclusions, diminish-

inclusions such as CeAlO

reduction in silicon content undermines its inhibitory effect

sizes under constant temperature and time.

yield strength of 0.6Si–0Nb–0V steel is higher than the
experimental value. The small discrepancy may be
attributable to two reasons. On the one hand, nanoprecip-
itates are considered to be spherical in the models to sim-
plify calculations; however, many of them are actually in
plate-like shape. Irregular plate-shaped particles are
expected to produce more resistance to plastic ﬂow as
compared to smooth spherical particles [ 35]. On the other
hand, the inﬂuence of inclusion morphology on strength
was not considered in the calculation of this model. With
the same inclusions content, Ce element can modify
inclusions by reﬁning and spheroidizing them, thereby
enhancing mechanical properties. The absence of Ce ele-
ment in 0.6Si–0Nb–0V steel may lead to measured strength
lower than the theoretical value, whereas alloys like 0.6Si–
0.04Nb–0.05V and 0.3Si–0.04Nb–0.05V steels, when
incorporated with rare earth elements, may exhibit higher
measured strength than the theoretical value.
The addition of microalloying elements and the opti-
mization of Si lead to a signiﬁcant increase in strength,
while also inﬂuencing the low-temperature impact tough-
ness of low-alloy cast steel, which initially decreases and
then increases. This is mainly attributed to two reasons. On
the one hand, compared to 0.6Si–0Nb–0V steel, 0.6Si–
0.04Nb–0.05V steel increases the number of nanoprecipi-
tates, reduces their size and enhances the precipitation
strengthening effect (Figs. 3 and 12). The increase in
strength by precipitation strengthening may reduce tough-
ness. However, the inclusion modiﬁcation and grain size
reﬁnement resulting from the addition of Nb, V and Ce can
contribute to an improvement in low-temperature tough-
ness, and their enhancement of low-temperature toughness
can be less than the toughness loss caused by precipitation
strengthening. On the other hand, in comparison with
0.6Si–0.04Nb–0.05V steel, the reduced Si content in 0.3Si–
0.04Nb–0.05V steel promotes the decomposition of brittle,
metastable martensite into ferrite and carbide during tem-
pering, thereby enhancing low-temperature toughness.

1. The multi-microalloying elements signiﬁcantly
enhance the yield strength and tensile strength of cast
steel. When the amounts of microalloying elements
remain constant and Si is reduced to 0.28 wt.%, both
the yield strength and impact energy of the cast steel
