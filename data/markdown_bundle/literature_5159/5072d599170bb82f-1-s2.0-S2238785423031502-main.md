1. Introduction 
Nb – Si based alloys have been considered as potential candidates for 
future advanced turbine blade materials in replace of currently widely 
used Ni-base superalloys due to high melting point( > 1700 
◦
C), rela -
tively lower densities(6.6 – 7.2 g/cm
3
), significant high temperature 
strength and creep resistance [ 1 – 3 ]. TiAl based alloys regarded as 
another promising high temperature structure materials are promi -
nently characterized as high specific strength with the service temper -
ature lower than 900 
◦
C [ 4 , 5 ]. What ’ s more, the recent developed high 
niobium β - γ TiAl alloy(HNBG) has good deformation capacity at high 
temperature and can be forged without can [ 6 , 7 ]. Targeted at their 
different serving temperatures, the two kinds of alloys can be joined 
together as hot-section components with temperature gradient in 
aero-engines [ 8 ]. As a result, the advantages of both can be fully made 
use of in the engineering application. 
Until now, investigations on Nb – Si based alloys have focused almost 
entirely on the fabrication process and microstructure optimization [ 9 ]. 
Nevertheless, there is scarcely report on the joining of this kind of alloy 
with dissimilar materials. Diffusion bonding is often used for high 
quality joints between dissimilar materials for the elimination of 
segregation, crack and distortion stresses frequently present in the 
fusion welding [ 10 ]. Especially for the dissimilar joining of intermetallic 
compounds, such as TiAl with Ti

In the present study, the direct joining of Nb – Si to HNBG alloys was 
conducted by spark plasma sintering (SPS) in order to simplify the 
process. Due to the existence of electrical migration effect during the 
joining by SPS, the atom diffusion ability across the joint is enhanced 
and thus lower bonding temperature and shorter bonding time are used 
[ 14 ]. The microstructure and shear strength of the joint were investi -
gated, and the interfacial bonding mechanism was also discussed.

3.4. Joint diffusion bonding mechanism of HNBG/Nb – Si alloys 
Fig. 10 schematically displays the joint diffusion bonding mechanism 
of HNBG/Nb – Si alloys. For the asperities on the mating surface, there 
exist many pores in the joint with mating surfaces initially contacting, as 
shown in Fig. 10 (a). Afterwards with the action of temperature and 
pressure, the deformation of surface asperities takes place which leads to 
some pores close in the joint. Subsequently, the interdiffusion of ele -
ments occurs across the joint under the concentration gradient, as dis -
played in Fig. 10 (b). On the side of HNBG alloy, more Al atoms diffuse 
into Nb – Si alloy compared with Ti atoms due to larger diffusion rate 
resulting from the Al atom radius smaller than Ti, so that HNBG alloy 
near the joint is rich in Ti but poor in Al. At the same time, Nb and Si 
atoms from Nb – Si alloy diffuse slowly into into HNBG alloy due to high 
bond energy of Nb – Si and Si – Si bonds along with large atomic radius of 
Nb atom. As a result, γ -TiAl phase firstly transformed into α
