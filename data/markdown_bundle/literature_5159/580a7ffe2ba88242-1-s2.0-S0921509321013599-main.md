ABSTRACT 
In this work, the Ti
2
AlNb/TiAl laminated composite is successfully fabricated by hot pressing combined with the 
foil-foil metallurgy. The microstructure and the orientation relationship of the phases in the interface region, the 
distribution of strain and hardness along the interface, and the stress releasing mechanisms by the plastic 
deformation accompanied with two types of dislocation sources near the interface are detailly discussed. The 
composite presents an obvious yield phenomenon in contrast with the TiAl alloy and shows an improved tensile 
strength at room temperature. Further, an enhanced strength-ductility synergy at high temperature has been 
achieved. The synergetic deformation ability between the interface and the base alloys, deformation behaviors, 
and the effects of the laminated structure on crack distribution and morphology are also carefully investigated. 
The excellent mechanical performances of the composite are attributed to the outstanding metallurgical interface 
bonding, the introducing of ductile Ti
2
AlNb alloy into the laminated composite and the well-coordinated 
deformation ability of the laminated structure. The presented findings provide a new structural design strat -
egy for property optimization of TiAl alloys. 
1. Introduction 
TiAl alloys are considered to be the most promising lightweight en -
gineering materials for high-temperature applications in aerospace and 
automobile industries due to their low weight, high specific strength and 
excellent high-temperature creep resistance [ 1 – 4 ]. However, the 
intrinsic brittleness of TiAl alloy leads to its poor ductility and fracture 
toughness, which have seriously limited their further commercial 
development [ 5 , 6 ]. Inspired by the nacre structure, the laminated 
structures exhibit excellent mechanical properties including high 
toughness with outstanding strength-ductility synergy [ 7 – 10 ]. Recently, 
architecting TiAl-based laminated composite is becoming a new strategy 
to improve the mechanical performances of TiAl alloys at room tem -
perature. Ti (alloy)/TiAl laminated composites are prepared by hot-pack 
rolling and vacuum hot pressing [ 11 – 14 ]. These studies show that the 
strength and fracture toughness of TiAl alloys can be effectively 
enhanced. However, there are still two problems to be delt with. (i) The 
mismatch of high-temperature oxidation resistance. The 
high-temperature oxidation resistance of Ti alloys (below 500 
◦
C [ 15 ]) is 
much lower than that of TiAl alloys (above 750 
◦
C [ 16 , 17 ]). (ii) The 
mismatch of creep resistance at high temperature. TiAl alloys have much 
better high-temperature creep resistance (above 750 
◦
C [ 18 ]) than Ti 
alloys (below 600 
◦
C [ 19 , 20 ]). The mismatch of oxidation resistance and 
creep resistance at high temperature will deteriorate the structural sta -
bility of TiAl-based laminated components at elevated temperature. 
Ti

resistance at high temperature with the service temperature up to 700 
◦
C 
[ 21 , 22 ], which is close to the service temperature of TiAl alloys. 
Meanwhile, Ti

strength, good toughness and ductility [ 23 ]. Apparently, in terms of 
engineering application in the future, the Ti
2

candidates for constructing TiAl/Ti

previous work, we have studied the fracture toughness of the

5. Conclusions 
In summary, we have successfully fabricated the Ti
2
AlNb/TiAl 
laminated composite by hot pressing combined with the foil metallurgy. 
The interface structures, mechanical properties and the deformation 
behaviors are carefully investigated. The major findings in this study are 
summarized as follows. 
1) The interface is divided into region I and region II. Compared with 
the coherent interface between the Ti
2
AlNb base alloy and the region 
II, α
2 
in region II is incoherent with the γ in TiAl base alloy and the 
interface is at a low level of stress-strain state. Two types of dislo -
cation sources formed in the TiAl base alloy play important roles in 
releasing the stress at the interface. 
2) In the as-prepared composite, strain hardening is caused by the 
dislocation pile-ups in the γ -TiAl near the interface. The composite 
behaves an obvious yield phenomenon and an improved strength at 
room temperature. For the high-temperature tensile performance, 
the composite achieves an enhanced strength-ductility synergy. 
3) Strong interface bonding and well-coordinated deformation ability 
provide the composite with the brilliant mechanical properties. The 
structural strengthening at room temperature is originated from the 
back stress strengthening. The dislocation slip, twinning and the 
solid-state phase transformation are the main deformation mecha -
nisms at high temperature. 
4) Crack nucleation occurs in the α
