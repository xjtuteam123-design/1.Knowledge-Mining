Fabrication of Ir–Nb alloys with addition of Ni or Al by pulse
electric current sintering
C. Huang *, Y. Yamabe-Mitarai, H. Harada
National Institute for Materials Science (NIMS), Sengen 1-2-1, Tsukuba, Ibaraki 305-0047, Japan
Received 31 August 2001; accepted 30 January 2002
Abstract
Ir–Nb alloys were fabricated via a pulse electric current sintering (PECS) process with addition of Ni or Al from powder
mixtures. A sintering temperature of 1800 /C176C and a holding time of 4 h were used. Relative densities of sintered sample exceeded
90% theoretical density. Phases and microstructures were analyzed. The inﬂuence of Ni and Al on the microstructure was compared.
Metal loss during sintering and the existence of pores were the main problems found in this study.

1. Introduction
As the demand for high-temperature materials in the
aerospace industry continues to increase, more and more
attention is given to searching for new alloys with higher
temperature capability than the conventional high-tem-
perature material, typically, Ni-based superalloys. Irid-
ium in the platinum group metals has excellent potential
to become aerospace materials because of its relatively
high melting temperature, excellent oxidation, and good
corrosion resistance. Our studies on Ir-based alloys,
including binary alloys, ternary alloys, and quaternary
alloys, indicated that these alloys, termed as Ir-based
refractory superalloys [1], have an fcc/L1
2 coherent struc-
ture, as do Ni-based superalloys, but a higher melting
point and superior high-temperature strength. Among
the Ir-based refractory superalloys investigated, the Ir–
Nb system is more noticeable because niobium has the
lowest density of all the refractory metals (8.58 g/cm
3)
[2] and a high melting temperature (2496 /C176C). However,
one of the major problems in the application of these
alloys in the polycrystalline form is their poor ductility
[3]. Rapid solidiﬁcation processes have been found to
introduce a number of transformations into the micro-
structure, such as ﬁned grain size, improved uniformity
of the microstructure, and so on, resulting in improved
ductility [4]. Pulse electric current sintering (PECS), a
novel process, is receiving increased attention by virtues
of its short sintering time and eﬀective densiﬁcation of
some diﬃcult powder systems at lower sin-tering tem-
peratures.
In our previous studies, binary Ir–Nb alloys were ﬁrst
tried by PECS [3] using sintering times from several
minutes to 1 h and a sintering temperature of about
1800 /C176C. However, segregation of Ir and Nb was ob-
served, indicating that the reaction of Ir and Nb was not
ﬁnished. Then, three other Ir–Nb alloys, one binary
alloy and two ternary alloys with 1% addition of Ni or
Al, were tested by PECS [5] using a longer sintering time
of 4 h under a 1800 /C176C sintering temperature. Ni or Al,
with their relatively low melting points, can form a liq-
uid phase during the sintering process. Both the ability
of the liquid to ﬂow and the surface energy released
during the melting of powder Ni or Al were expected
to allow the sintering reaction to progress. The results
indicated that prolonging the sintering time promoted
the reaction and that the addition of Al had an eﬀect
on the elimination of pores. However, an examination of
the microstructure did not reveal clear evidence of the
formation of enough liquid phase during the PECS
process because of the small content of Ni or Al, and the
microstructures of samples with addition of Ni or Al
showed no obvious improvement compared with the
samples without additions.

The aim of the present work is to further investigate
the eﬀect of Ni or Al on the densiﬁcation of Ir–Nb alloys
using a PECS process. Four Ir–Nb alloys with addition
of more Ni or Al were fabricated. For comparison, two
ternary Ir–Nb alloys with 1% Ni or Al that had been
previously tested are also presented in this paper.
2. Experimental
Six samples (5 g each) were prepared by mixing pure
elemental powders of Ir (purity over 99.9 wt.%, grain
size /C245 lm, porous); Nb (purity 99.9 wt.%, grain size
/C2475 lm, particle); and Al (grain size < 75 lm, particle)
or Ni (purity 99.8%, grain size < 45 lm, porous). The
grain sizes of Ir and Nb were measured under scanning
electron microscope (SEM), and those of Ni and Al were
provided by company. These samples were divided into
two series, the Ni-series with Ni addition and the Al-
series with Al addition. The nominal compositions of
the sample are listed in Table 1. The powder mixtures
were milled on a rotation machine for 24 h and then
poured into a graphite die and sintered in a PECS fur-
nace under pressure of about 40 MPa in vacuum con-
dition of /C240:5 /C210
/C02 Torr. The maximum sintering
temperature was 1800 /C176C, and the holding time was 4 h.
A detailed description of the PECS process can be found
in another study by our group [5]. Al05(P) and Ni10(P)
were pre-pressured under 332.7 MPa for 1 min before
being placed into the furnace. The heating-up conditions
of the two series of samples were diﬀerent, as plotted in
Fig. 1. The heating rate was slower around the melting
point of Ni or Al. The temperature, displacement of the
powder compact, and pressure on it were monitored at
the same time by a connected computer.
The size of the specimens after sintering was about 15
mm in diameter and 1 mm or so in height. Specimens
were ground to remove the graphite contamination on
the surface and examined by X-ray diﬀraction (XRD)
analysis. The diameter and thickness were measured by
micrometer to calculate the bulk density. Then, the
specimens were polished and etched with a solution of
5% HCl in ethyl alcohol for microstructure observation
using a SEM. The compositions of the constituent phase
were measured by energy-dispersive analysis X-ray spec-
troscopy (EDX) equipped with SEM. Some sintered
specimens were analyzed by the ﬂuorescence X-ray
method to determine their real composition.

Another important phenomenon was that when
the samples were taken out of the PECS furnace after
sintering, some metals were observed attached to the
graphite column surface, which suggests that some
metals were squeezed out of the graphite die during the
sintering process and then solidiﬁed on the graphite
column when the temperature went down. This phe-
nomenon was found in all these samples.
3.2. Density
The relative densities of sintered samples are listed in
Table 2. The real compositions of sintered sample Ni01
and Al01 were examined by the ﬂuorescence X-ray
method. The relative density values of these two sam-
ples, calculated by the real composition, are also listed in
this table. Fluorescence X-ray results revealed that the
real composition of the samples changed from the
normal composition. This meant that the constituents of
squeezed metals, which included more Nb than that
found in the normal composition, were not uniform. For
Al-series samples, the relative density values, which were
calculated from the ratio between the bulk density and
the nominal density, were over 100%. The relative den-
sity results indicated that Al was more eﬀective for the
density increasing than Ni.

Comparing the eﬀect of the addition of Ni and the
addition of Al, one obvious phenomenon was that the
coarsening was more serious in Ni-series samples than in
Al-series samples, as shown in Fig. 4(c)–(f). Considering
the microstructures of Ni01 and Ni10, we could con-

clear. One possibility was that some reaction happened
between Al and Ir or Nb after Al was melted and, thus,
less Al liquid remained to join the later sintering process.
However, the density was higher in the Al-series sam-
ples, and the number of small pores was larger in the Ni-
series samples. This is maybe attributed to the powder
pattern of Ni that was porous [4].
Two pre-pressured samples, Al05(P) and Ni10(P), did
not show any superior characteristics to the Al10 and
Ni10 samples, which were not pre-pressured. On the
contrary, X-ray results suggested that the pre-pressuring
process hindered the reaction between Ir and Nb. More-
over, the expected eﬀect on the elimination of pores
could not be seen on microstructure observation. The
strip-like pores in Ni10(P) was believed to be caused by
pre-pressuring. When the sample was pre-pressured,
some areas were compressed tightly, preventing the
liquid of Ni or Al to ﬂow through the powder compact

3.5. Some points on PECS for Ir-based refractory
superalloys
The addition of a certain amount of Ni and Al, in-
troducing the liquid phase during sintering, accelerated
the densiﬁcation of the powder mixtures and uniformed
the microstructure. PECS process is a novel method for
powder’s densiﬁcation, the densiﬁcation mechanism has
not been clearly understood, particularly for the powder

compact with liquid phase during sintering. However,
this study indicated that liquid phase in PECS is also
beneﬁcial for microstructure improvement, as that in
normal liquid phase sintering which involves three basic
processes, namely: (a) particle rearrangement and ﬂuid
ﬂow; (b) solution and re-precipitation; and (c) coales-
cence [10]. However, accompanied with advantage of
liquid phase sintering, coarsening is a drawback that
should be controlled.
The most remarkable advantages of PECS over
conventional methods are its short sintering time and
lower sintering temperature. However, in this work, a
long sintering time of 4 h and a high sintering temper-
ature of 1800 /C176C were used. Our previous studies indi-
cated that PECS, as noted at the beginning of the paper
needed a longer sintering time for Ir–Nb binary alloys
sintered. Accordingly, a sintering time of 4 h was applied
in this work. From the results mentioned above, adding
Ni or Al accelerated the sintering process. The sintering
time of 4 h could be shortened. Although the sintering
temperature of 1800 /C176C was high, it was lower than
melting point of Ir or Nb by more than 600 /C176C. At this
temperature the reaction of Ir and Nb in the samples
with higher content addition elements Ni or Al was al-
most ﬁnished, showing strong phase peaks. A lower
sintering temperature should be tried in future studies.
In this study the most valuable result was that the
reaction of Ir and Nb by PECS was considerably im-
proved with more addition of Ni or Al at 1800/C176C, form-
ing an L1
2 or fcc phase. This suggested to us the use
of an alternative approach for obtaining Ir-based re-
fractory superalloys with addition of a certain amount
of elements by PECS. Comparing the microstructure
of sample with addition of 1% of elements and that of
samples with a higher percentage of added elements, it
was observed that the addition of Ni or Al also had an
eﬀect on the homogenization of the microstructure as
a result of the formation of the liquid phase. However,
two major problems remain to be solved. The ﬁrst one is
the loss of metals during sintering, which was believed to
be the reason for the heterogeneous melting within the
powders. The second one is the existence of pores. Un-
less these problems are overcome, it will not be possible
to test the mechanical properties further.
Many factors aﬀect the sintering process. The main
factors are the sintering conditions, such as temperature
and time. The next is the quality of powders, including
powder size and distribution, shape, and surface energy.
For the powder mixtures, milling is also very important.
The shrinking sequence is found to be sensitive to
the heating temperature and the holding time at sintering
temperature. Accordingly, in order to decrease the
number of pores, special attention should be paid to
adjusting the sintering condition in the future work.
Moreover, some attention should be paid to the im-
provement of powder quality and milling condition in
order to control the loss of metal, particularly that of Nb.
4. Summary
Ir–Nb alloys were fabricated by a PECS process with
addition of Ni or Al at sintering temperature of 1800/C176C,
at which the reaction between Ir and Nb in the samples
with more addition of Ni or Al was almost ﬁnished. The
relative densities of all samples were over 90%.
The L1
2 phase was detected in Ni10 and Ni10(P), and
an fcc as well as an L12 phase were detected in Al01, Al10,
Al05(P), and Ni01. In Ni01 and all Al-series samples,
Some blocks with higher content of Nb were found.
The addition of Ni or Al to Ir–Nb systems accelerates
the densiﬁcation of Ir–Nb alloys. Coarsening was ob-
vious in samples with Ni addition of 10 at.%.
Pre-pressuring was believed to be detrimental to the
reaction of Ir and Nb and had no advantage for the
elimination of pores by PECS.
In order to make progress in this area, it would be
necessary to overcome two problems, namely the loss of
metal and the existence of pores.
