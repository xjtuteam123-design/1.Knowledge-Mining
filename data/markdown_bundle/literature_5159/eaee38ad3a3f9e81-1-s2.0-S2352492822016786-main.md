ABSTRACT 
In situ enhancement of alloy properties via additive manufacturing is a convenient and promising approach that 
is attracting increasing attention. Herein, we overcame the insufficient strength of Ti-10 Nb alloy used in or -
thopedic implants by introducing an Ar + N
2 
reactive atmosphere during selective laser melting. The mechanical 
properties, phase compositions, microstructures, and biocompatibility of the Ti-10 Nb alloys formed under the 
reactive atmospheres containing 3 vol% and 5 vol% N
2 
were investigated. Scanning electron microscope, elec -
tron backscatter diffraction, and transmission electron microscope were employed to investigate the mechanism 
of N on the mechanical property evolution of Ti-10 Nb. The biocompatibility of the Ti-10 Nb alloys fabricated 
under different N
2 
concentrations was investigated using in vitro cell proliferation and adhesion assays, with 
pure Ti used as a reference. The Ti-10 Nb alloy displayed a tensile strength of 855 MPa, yield strength of 763 
MPa, an elongation of 21.6%, and elastic modulus of 72 GPa when the N
2 
fraction was 3 vol%. The as-printed Ti- 
10 Nb alloy exhibited good biocompatibility, and the introduction of N promoted the proliferation of MG-63 
cells. This study paves the way for the synthesis of high-strength and low modulus biomedical Ti alloys by 
reactive additive manufacturing. 
1. Introduction 
Ti and Ti alloys are widely used in orthopedic implants because they 
display superior mechanical properties, corrosion resistance, and 
biocompatibility compared with those of stainless steel and Co-Cr-based 
alloys [1,2] . Therefore, they are successfully used in dentistry and bone 
fixation plates, in addition to the earliest total hip arthroplasties [3 – 5] . 
Nevertheless, the elastic moduli of α -type pure Ti (~105 GPa) and 
several conventional Ti alloys, such as α + β -type Ti6Al4V (~110 GPa), 
are higher than those of human bones (cortical bone: 15 – 25 GPa [6] , 
cancellous bone: 0.05 – 3 GPa [7] ), which will cause stress shielding ef -
fect and, finally, lead to bone resorption and the failure of the orthopedic 
implant. Moreover, Ti6Al4V contains toxic V [8] and harmful Al, the 
accumulation of which will damage the human nervous system, causing 
Alzheimer ’ s disease [9 – 11] . Therefore, developing a novel type of Ti 
alloy with a lower elastic modulus and superior biocompatibility is 
critical. Compared with those of α - and α + β -type Ti alloys, β -type Ti 
alloys display better biocompatibility and lower elastic moduli (40 – 80 
GPa, [12,13] ), as they contain non-toxic β stabilizers, such as Nb, Mo, 
Ta, Sn, and Fe [14] . Additionally, Mo equivalency (Mo
eq
) is generally 
used to describe the contributions of the β stabilizers to the stability of β 
phase in β -type alloys [15] , as expressed by Eq. (1) :

Recent studies reported progress in designing and processing β -type 
Ti alloys, including binary Ti-Mo [16] and Ti-Nb [17] , ternary Ti-Ta-Zr 
[18] and Ti-Nb-Sn [19] , and quaternary Ti-Nb-Ta-Zr [20] series. Most 
research focuses on a high β stabilizer content to improve the stability of 
β phase. However, upon increasing the β stabilizer content, problems 
such as element segregation and increased density and raw material 
costs may occur. In contrast, binary Ti-10 Nb (wt%) contains a low Nb 
content and exhibits a low density (4.76 g/cm
3
) and elastic modulus 
(69 – 80 GPa, [21,22] ) and non-toxic property, rendering it a candidate 
use in orthopedic applications. However, similar to numerous β -type Ti 
alloys, the Ti-10 Nb alloy exhibits an insufficient strength (500 – 600 
MPa, [21,22] ) compared to that of Ti6Al4V (1000 – 1200 MPa). Ortho -
pedic implants are generally made to thin-walled or porous structures to 
reduce implant mass and favor cell and tissue adherence on the im -
plants. When the implants are made into thin-walled or porous struc -
tures, they may fail prematurely due to insufficient strength and 
load-bearing capacity. Therefore, developing a novel type of β -Ti alloy 
with high strength, low elastic modulus, and good biocompatibility is 
crucial. 
Various studies show that introducing small amounts of interstitial 
elements, such as O and N, may effectively improve the strength of Ti 
and Ti alloys via solid solution strengthening [23] . Additionally, grain 
refinement occurs when the concentration of interstitial atoms, such as 
O and N, is increased [24] . The radius of the N atom is 0.71 Å, which is 
similar to that of octahedral interstitial α -Ti (0.61 Å [25] ), and thus, it 
may occur at this position to cause solid solution strengthening. As for 
β -Ti, the radius of the octahedral interstitial in the < 110 > direction is 
0.91 Å, and thus, the N atom may also occur at this position to induce 
solid solution strengthening. 
Issariyapat et al. [26 – 29] mixed pure Ti and Ti-(N) powders to obtain 
raw powders with different N concentrations to increase the strength of 
pure Ti from 400 to approximately 1000 MPa. Yokota et al. [30] 
improved the strength of a β -type Ti-35Ta (at%) alloy from 700 to 1160 
MPa via O solid solution by mixing TiO
2 
and Ti-35Ta powders. These 
studies pre-mixed the raw powder and then used selective laser melting 
(SLM) or spark plasma sintering and hot extrusion to prepare the alloys, 
which preparation process is complicated. Wang et al. [31] initially used 
Ar + xN
2 
(x = 3, 5, 7, and 10 vol%) reactive gas as the shielding at -
mosphere during SLM and conveniently increased the ultimate tensile 
strength of pure Ti from 700 to approximately 1000 MPa while main -
taining a desirable elongation of 17.1%. Subsequently, Liu et al. [32] 
used an Ar + 5 vol% N
2 
reactive atmosphere to improve the ultimate 
tensile strength of the Ti6Al4V alloy from 1240 to 1408 MPa via SLM in 
situ forming. The direct use of Ar + N
2 
mixed gas during SLM to 
strengthen the β -type Ti alloy has not been reported. 
Additive manufacturing (AM) is attracting increasing attention 
because it exhibits the advantages of fast forming, high forming accu -
racy, and customization and enables the preparation of metallic com -
ponents with complex shapes. AM is widely used in the fields of 
aerospace, medical implants, automobiles, and metal jewelry [33,34] . 
As an AM technology, SLM is widely applied in manufacturing metal 
components [35] . When employing SLM to prepare metal implants, the 
bone to be operated on is first visualized via computed tomography (CT) 
scanning, and then the 3D reconstruction and repair of the bone are 
conducted theoretically, using a computer. Subsequently, doctors 
should rehearse the implant surgery and optimize the implant model. 
The implant then be printed via SLM and implanted into the patient after 
post-processing. Most significantly, compared with traditional process -
ing technologies, such as casting, SLM can realize customization and 
effectively improve the success of the operation. 
Herein, a near- β -type Ti-10 Nb (wt%) alloy with a low Young ’ s 
modulus and superior strength was prepared via SLM. The mechanical 
properties of Ti-10 Nb were easily improved in situ after N
2

microstructure, phase composition, and mechanical performance with 
N

mechanism within the near- β -type Ti-10 Nb alloy was further studied. 
Additionally, the biocompatibility of the Ti-10 Nb alloys fabricated 
under different N
2 
concentrations, with pure Ti as a control group, were 
further investigated via cell proliferation and initial cell adhesion in 
vitro studies.

3. Results 
3.1. SLM process and relative density 
Based on the previous parameter investigation, a higher relative 
density was obtained when the layer thickness and hatching distance 
were 0.04 and 0.12 mm, respectively. In this study, laser power ranged 
from 90 W to 270 W, scanning speed ranged from 400 mm/s to 
700 mm/s were tuned to further explore the optimal fabrication pa -
rameters. The relative densities of the as-printed Ti-10 Nb are shown in 
Fig. 3 (a). The highest density is 99.18% when the laser power and 
scanning speed are 265 W and 700 mm/s, respectively (denoted E1), 
with few pores observed in the Optical Microscope (OM), as shown in 
Fig. 3 (b), and the width of molten pool is 109 µ m. Under this optimal 
processing parameter, Ti-10 Nb spinal implant models with dimensions 
of 50 × 41 × 23 mm were printed, as shown in Fig. 4 . The chamber in 
the implant model is designed to facilitate powder leakage and reduce 
weight. 
Due to the coarse particle size of Ti-10 Nb powders (39.3 µ m) and the 
high melting point of Nb (2468
◦
C), unmelted powders occur in the as- 
printed alloys, forming holes and lowering the relative density when the 
laser power and scanning speed are low. When the laser power and 
scanning speed are increased, the energy density may be increased to a 
suitable range (E = 70 – 90 J/mm
3
), the powders can be completely 
melted, and the pores may be reduced, so that high relative density was 
obtained. However, when the laser power and energy density are too 
high, the laser causes metal evaporation and plasma formation. Metal 
evaporation leads to the formation of vapor cavities, and their collapse 
would form keyholes, resulting in a decreased relative density [38] .

Pure Ti exhibits an α / β phase transition temperature of 882
◦
C. 
Below this temperature, pure Ti will undergo a phase transition from 
stable β phase of BBC structure to α phase of HCP structure. As a β -phase 
stabilizing element, Nb element can reduce the α + β / β phase transition 
temperature of Ti [39] . The central temperature of the molten pool 
generated at the laser focus during SLM can rapidly melt the Ti-10 Nb 
powder, which then undergoes rapid cooling process. The rapid β -to- α 
phase transition would occur when the Ti-10 Nb alloy is rapidly cooled 
from the β phase. As a result, non-diffusion transformation α ’ martensite 
generates. As the lattice structures of the α and α ’ martensite phases are 
very similar (HCP structure), distinguishing them accurately using 
general phase characterization methods, such as XRD, is challenging. 
Typically, the length-to-width ratio of α ’ martensite grain in Ti alloys is 
10:1 or greater [40,41] . The distinguish feature is that α ’ grains cut-off at 
the original β grain boundaries and display acicular morphology. EBSD 
may be used in determining the differences in microstructure 
morphology between the α and α ’ grains.

The introduction of N

refinement in the Ti-10 Nb alloy. (1) Firstly, the violent reaction be -
tween N
2

severe spattering and sparks observed during printing process. Finally, 
under the violent stirring of the molten pool, the original coarse grains 
broken into fine grains at high temperature. (2) Secondly, the non- 
stoichiometric nitrides (TixN) initially formed during the reaction be -
tween N
2 
and Ti-10 Nb could provide new nucleation sites promoting 
the nucleation of α / α ’ phase during cooling. During the scanning of laser 
melted in the freshly printed layer in the Ti-10 Nb matrix, microdroplets 
with high specific surface areas generally absorb higher concentrations 
of N to form nitrides such as Ti-N and TixN [31] . Under the subsequent 
continuous laser scanning, the as-printed material cannot be further 
contacted with nitrogen. Meanwhile, under continuous thermal cycling 
and remelting, the nitrides redissolved and decomposed in the matrix. 
(3) Thirdly, the pinning effect of N at the grain boundaries impeded their 
migration, which reduced the growth rates of the grains and, finally, led 
to grain refinement in Ti-10 Nb.

5. Conclusions 
In this study, commercial biomedical Ti-10 Nb alloy was synthesized 
by selective laser melting under application of reactive atmospheres. 
Due to the in-situ alloying of the Ti-10 Nb alloy by N, the significant 
improvement in strength of about 25% compared with the samples 
synthesized under pure Ar is observed. At the same time, the ductility of 
the samples remains in the application relevant range. Additionally, the 
low elastic modulus and good biocompatibility offering the opportunity 
to use N-reinforced biomedical Ti-10 Nb alloy for the manufacturing of 
orthopedic implants. This study paves the way to use in-situ reactive 
processing of additively manufactured metallic materials for achieving 
improved strength performance. The main conclusions of this study are: 
(1) The Ti-10 Nb alloys with low elastic moduli and excellent me -
chanical properties were successfully synthesized by SLM under 
reactive Ar + N
2 
atmosphere. After adding 3 vol% N
2 
to the 
shielding atmosphere, the yield tensile strength of Ti-10 Nb 
increased from 627 MPa to 741 MPa, while maintaining an 
excellent elongation of 21.59%. Upon increasing the N
2 
concen -
tration to 5 vol%, the ultimate tensile strength is above 905 MPa, 
while the sample maintained an application relevant elongation 
of 12.1%. 
(2) The introduction of N from the processing atmosphere causes 
grain refinement within Ti-10 Nb and leads to an expansion of the 
Ti-10 Nb lattice. The microstructures of Ti-10 Nb-xN (x = 3 and 
5 vol%) consisted almost of α ’ martensite, and no nitrides were 
detected by the XRD and TEM. At low N
2 
fraction (3 vol%), the 
improvement in the strength of the Ti-10 Nb alloy was primarily 
due to grain refinement and solid solution strengthening. When 
the N
2 
fraction is increased to 5 vol%, the solid solution 
strengthening by N becomes dominant strengthening mechanism. 
(3) The Ti-10 Nb alloy with a low β stabilizer content displayed a low 
elastic modulus, which is comparable with the most stable β -type 
Ti alloys that contain high contents of high-cost Ta and Nb. The 
introduction of low N content exhibits neutral effect on the elastic 
modulus of the Ti-10 Nb alloy, although it promotes the stabili -
zation of α phase. The elastic modulus of the N-reinforced Ti- 
10 Nb alloy is in the range of 70 – 80 GPa. 
(4) The current Ti-10 Nb alloy synthesized via the reactive additive 
manufacturing displayed better biocompatibility compared with 
pure Ti. Furthermore, the introduction of N into the Ti-10 Nb 
alloy improves cell proliferation.
