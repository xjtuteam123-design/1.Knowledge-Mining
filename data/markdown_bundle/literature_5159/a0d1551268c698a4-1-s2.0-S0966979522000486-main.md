ABSTRACT 
The recently emerged CoCrNi-based medium-entropy alloys (MEAs)/high-entropy alloys (HEAs) have a good 
combination of high strength and ductility. However, there are still challenges for further increasing their me -
chanical properties by finding optimal composition with simple processing. In this investigation, we revisited the 
commercial superalloys MP35N and MP159 with similar composition and entropy to CoCrNi-based MEAs/HEAs 
to find inspirations for designing CoCrNi-based MEAs. After a simple thermomechanical process, MP35N/MP159 
can achieve a high tensile strength over 2.2 GPa at ambient temperature, better than a majority of present 
CoCrNi-based MEAs/HEAs. At intermediate temperature tensile tests, the aged MP159 also has a considerable 
tensile strength (1.8 GPa) and tensile elongation (12.5%). The underlying strengthening factors of aged MP35N/ 
MP159 were investigated by transmission electron microscope. It indicated that precipitation strengthening, 
solid solution strengthening, and work-hardening could be introduced in CoCrNi-based MEAs through alloying, 
and simple thermomechanical treatments to significantly improve the performance. The present findings and 
perspectives provide useful enlightenment for developing ultra-strong CoCrNi-based MEAs/HEAs. 
1. Introduction 
Alloys with multiprincipal elements are the frontier of metallic ma -
terials since the proposal of high-entropy alloys (HEAs). Very recently, it 
was found that the CoCrNi medium-entropy alloys (MEAs) have better 
mechanical performances than the HEAs of CoCrFeMnNi and CoCrFeNi, 
including fracture toughness at low temperatures, friction stress, and 
dynamic shear performance [ 1 – 3 ]. In the consideration of low yield 
strength in the single face-centered cubic (fcc) CoCrNi [ 4 ], several 
strategies have been reported to improve the strength of fcc MEAs 
through solid solution strengthening, precipitation strengthening, and 
architecting gradient hierarchical grains through thermomechanical 
treatment [ 5 – 8 ]. After cold rolling and annealing at 700 
◦
C, the CoCrNi 
has a good combination of excellent tensile strength (1 GPa) and uni -
form elongation (28%) [ 7 ]. Furthermore, after recrystallization and 
high-pressure torsion, the grain size of CoCrNi can be reduced to 30 – 50 
nm, inducing an excellent yield strength of 1.8 GPa and uniform elon -
gation of 4% [ 9 ]. Besides the thermomechanical treatment, alloying has 
also been explored to increase the mechanical properties of CoCrNi 
MEAs. The additions of 7.5 at% Al or 3 at.% Mo increases the yield 
strength by 70% or 131%, respectively [ 10 , 11 ]. Moreover, through the 
precipitation hardening effect, the yield strength and tensile strength 
increases by 70% and 44% respectively and maintains a good ductility of 
45% [ 6 , 10 ]. 
Pursuing high-strength CoCrNi-based MEAs through the simple 
process and alloying is still a hot research topic, but with several chal -
lenges. The first one is the wide composition range with dozens of 
optional alloying elements in the alloy design. Alloys with different 
compositions will have different corresponding optimal processings. The 
second challenge is the feasibility of industrialization for wide applica -
tions. For example, high-pressure torsion and cryorolling can only be 
used for the preparation of small samples. The industrial application also 
requires simple processings. To resolve these challenges, commercial 
alloys with similar compositions could provide valuable clues for the 
development of CoCrNi-based MEAs. 
Here, we focus on two candidates, superalloys of MP35N and MP159. 
Through a large number of experiments, Smith et al. developed the 
MP35N alloy, and the subsequent MP159 alloy with excellent perfor -
mance was developed by Slaney [ 12 ]. The MP35N and MP159 are 
Co-based superalloys with different alloying elements. These two alloys

Combine with the observations described earlier, the secondary 
hardening effect produced by aging [ 32 ] is dependent on the in -

precipitation. For fcc alloys like MP35N with low stacking fault energy, 
either deformation twins or deformation-induced ε -hcp phases may 
form [ 33 ]. Both the deformation twins and the ε -hcp phase are caused by 
stacking faults on the {111} close-packed surface of the fcc matrix, but 
the stacking sequence is different [ 34 ]. The SAED pattern of CRA MP35N 
shown in Fig. 5 (e) exhibits the fcc matrix with smaller superlattices 
reflection in the 1/3 (1 11) direction, which corresponds to ε -hcp. The DF 
image determines the existence of hcp platelets. Besides, TEM results 
indicate that a large number of dislocations and other defects caused by 
cold deformation have not been eliminated during the aging. The 
HRTEM image shown in Fig. 5 (c) exhibits a large number of nano-scale 
stacking faults in the CRA MP35N.

By revisiting MP35N and MP159 superalloys, we come up with some 
strategies for the design of CoCrNi-based MEAs. In the CoCrNi, solid 
solution strengthening can be introduced by adding Mo, W, and Nb. The 
Mo, W, and Nb can both strengthen the γ and γ’ phase by solid solution, 
respectively. Nb has a better solid solution effect than Mo and W since 
the atomic radius of Nb is larger than that of Mo and W. More signifi -
cantly, precipitation strengthening can be introduced by adding Ti, Al, 
Nb, Mo, etc. Ti, Al, and Nb play the role of solid solution strengthening 
and precipitation strengthening at the same time. A small amount of Ti, 
Al dissolves into the fcc matrix through the solid solution, and super -
saturated Ti, Al forms the γ
′
phase, which increases the strengthening 
effect and can preserve intermediate temperature strength. The Nb 
atoms can promote the precipitation of the γ
′
phase by substituting the 
Al, Ti in the γ
′
phase. Recently, there are studies on solution strength -
ening and precipitation strengthening of CoCrNi-based MEAs. and some 
of them have achieved considerable improvement [ 5 , 6 , 35 – 37 ]. How -
ever, the combined strengthening by solid solution with Mo, W, and Nb 
and precipitation with Ti, Al, Ta is still absent. Accordingly, the strength 
of most reported precipitation-strengthened CoCrNi-based MEAs is still 
lower than MP35N/MP159. 
Moreover, the Suzuki segregation is another interesting phenomenon 
to be considered in the strengthening of CoCrNi-based MEAs. The Suzuki 
segregation only causes any measurable strengthening when there is a 
high enough density of dislocations for the stacking faults to be suffi -
ciently close to overlap [ 23 ]. When aging the cold-rolled MP alloys, 
isolated dislocations experience the Suzuki mechanism and become 
more extended by absorbing Cr, Mo atoms, and desorbing Ni atoms to 
stabilize the ε -hcp structure [ 23 ]. Prior cold rolling of MP35N and 
MP159 provides the required dislocation density. The stacking faults in 
the fcc structure are partially composed of ε -hcp structure layers [ 38 ] 
which gradually thickens due to the interaction between dislocations. 
Fine dispersed ε -hcp platelets can improve the strength of the alloys. 
Furthermore, Mo, Nb, additions can easily segregate to stacking faults 
and slip bands during aging and further decrease the stacking fault en -
ergy [ 26 ]. Therefore, for CoCrNi-based MEAs, they inherently have low 
stacking fault energy, the Suzuki segregation of Mo, Nb atoms could be 
utilized for tailoring the microstructure and phase during the deforma -
tion and aging. Up to now, there are limited examples of introducing the 
Suzuki effect in CoCrNi-based MEAs [ 39 ]. The Suzuki segregation needs 
more attention in the CoCrNi-based MEAs.

5. Conclusion 
In this study, we revisited the MP35N and MP159 superalloys for 
designing the ultra-strong CoCrNi MEAs. The microstructure and me -
chanical properties were investigated. The possible strengthening 
mechanism for ultra-high strength was discussed. The main findings and 
perspectives are as follows: 
(1) The compositions of MP35N and MP159 are similar to CoCrNi- 
based MEAs/HEAs, and the configurational entropy already 
falls in the range of MEAs/HEAs. The study of MP35N/MP159 
superalloys has significance for the design of CoCrNi MEAs. 
(2) At ambient temperature, both MP35N and MP159 have a tensile 
strength of 2.2 GPa, better than a majority of CoCrNi-based 
MEAs/HEAs. At intermediate temperature 600 
◦
C and 650 
◦
C, 
both MP35N and MP159 also have an excellent combination of 
strength and ductility. 
(3) The strengthening factors of aged MP35N include stacking faults, 
dislocations, and ε -hcp phase. For the aged MP159, the stacking 
faults, dislocations, and γ
′
phase contributed to the high strength. 
(4) Precipitation strengthening, solid solution strengthening, and 
work-hardening can be introduced in CoCrNi MEAs easily 
through alloying and simple thermomechanical treatments, 
which are more economically friendly and easy to achieve than 
high-cost methods like high-pressure torsion. 
Through the combination of the various strategies mentioned above, 
it is hopeful to realize the CoCrNi-based medium-entropy alloys with 
ideal comprehensive performance.
