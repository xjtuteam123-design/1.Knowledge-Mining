Composition formula of Al-TMs high-entropy alloys 
derived by cluster-plus-glue-atoms model and its 
experimental verification
*Tian-yu Liu, Kun Shi, Jun Zhao, Shi-bing Liu, Hong-yu Liu, Bo-liang Liu, Xin-min Mei, Zhao-sheng Ning, 
and Xiao-ming Chen
State Key Laboratory of Light Alloy Casting Technology for High-end Equipment, Shenyang Research Institute of Foundry Co., Ltd., 
Shenyang 110022, China
Abstract: High-entropy alloys (HEAs) are based on solid solutions which 
characterized by chemical short-range ordering (CSRO), but there is no accurate 
structural tool to address CSRO characteristic, which obstacles precise composition 
design for HEAs. In this study, based on the cluster-plus-glue-atoms model, the 
composition law of Al-TMs (TMs, transition metals) HEAs with BCC and FCC structures 
was revealed. In BCC structure, with five elements in equi-atomic ratio, the composition 
formula of Al-TMs HEAs can be expressed by a cluster formula [Al-M 14]Al3, and in 
non equi-atomic ratio can be expressed by [Al-M 14]Al, where M is the average atom 
of the TMs. But in FCC structure, both Al-TMs HEAs with five elements in equi-
atomic and non equi-atomic ratios can be expressed by a cluster formula [Al-M 12]Al. 
To confirm the effectiveness of cluster formula for Al-TMs, two alloys were designed, 
[Al-Ti4V3Nb4Mo3]Mo and [Al-Ti3V3Nb4Mo4]Al. Results show that the [Al-Ti 3V3Nb4Mo4]Al
alloy has both higher strength and higher plastic deformation at room temperature. 
Besides, [Al-Ti3V3Nb4Mo4]Al alloy shows slower soften effect at 800 ºC, contributed to its 
higher strength. By substituting Ta for some of Mo, the strength of [Al-Ti3V3Nb4Mo3Ta]Al
at room temperature and high temperature drastically decreases, suggesting that Ta 
element deteriorates the properties of Al-TMs alloys. 
Keywords: Al-TMs high-entropy alloys; composition design; short-range order; 
cluster-plus-glue-atom model

1 Introduction
Owing to the limitation of the alloy melting point, the performance of traditional high-
temperature alloys, such as Ni-based superalloys, has reached the limit in high temperature 
condition. But, new equipment needs to be able to operate at much higher temperature, thus 
it is urgently to explore novel alloys. Recently, high-entropy alloy (HEAs), which usually 
contain more than four elements in equi-atomic or nearly equi-atomic ratios, have attracted 
widely attention due to their high yield strength 
[1]
, superior high temperature strength 
[2]
, 
high fracture toughness 
[3]
, high corrosion resistance 
[4]
, high impact toughness 
[5-6]
, etc. 
Currently, two types of HEAs have the most prospective for use at high temperatures: (1) 
HEAs consisting of 3D-transition metals (TMs HEAs) Co, Cr, Cu, Fe, Mn, Ni, Ti and V , but 
their high-temperature properties are inferior to Ni-based superalloys; (2) HEAs containing 
refractory elements, which are called refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs), exhibiting 
superior high-temperature performance than the commercial Ni-based superalloys 
[7]
.

and high strength. Xu et al. 
[8]

in molar percentage) alloys with a single disordered BCC phase, and found that with the

Researchers 
[11, 12]
 confirmed the existence of the chemical 
short-range order (CSRO) in HEAs, which plays an important 
role in strength and plasticity. Therefore, it is a good idea for 
composition design of HEAs via CSRO. The cluster-plus-
glue-atom model, proposed by Dong et al. 
[13]
, is an advanced 
structure model to describe CSRO. In this model, the SRO 
structure is dissociated into a 1st-neighbor coordination 
polyhedral cluster and a glue atom part that are situated outside 
the cluster part at the 2nd-neighbor and beyond. Then, the 
structure can be expressed by a cluster formula [cluster]glue x, 
where x is the number of glue atoms 
[14]
. The model has been 
successfully used as an effective alloy composition design 
method in HEAs 
[15-17]
.
In low-density RHEAs, Al and Ti play an important role 
in reducing the density. Besides, due to the strong interaction 
between Al and Ti, the microstructure is unstable. Therefore, 
in this study, a series of novel Al-Ti-V-Nb-Mo-Ta system 
alloys were designed based on the cluster-plus-glue-atom 
model, and the evolution of microstructure and change of 
mechanical properties with different contents of Al and Ti 
were investigated. Compared to V , Nb and Mo, Ta has a 
higher melting point, which is beneficial to improve the high 
temperature performance. Therefore, the effect of Ta element 
on the microstructure and properties of high-entropy alloy was 
also studied by partially replacing Mo with Ta.

2.1 Chemical formula of Al-TMs with BCC 
structure
Some Al-TMs HEAs are derived from the high-temperature 
BCC phase, and some even retain the single BCC structure at 
room temperature. Table 2 lists some typical Al-TMs HEAs 
with BCC structure. There are three steps to analyze the 
composition law of Al-TMs HEAs by the cluster-plus-glue-
atoms model. Firstly, M is assigned to the shell positions in 
the cluster formula. According to the cluster-plus-glue-atom 
model for BCC solid solutions, the shell positions cover 14 
atoms, as shown in Fig. 1(a). Secondly, the number of Al (x) 
is calculated by setting the total number of solvents to be 14, 
according to the equation of M/Al=14/x . Finally, the total 
number of atoms, Z , of cluster formula equals to 14 plus x , 
as shown in Table 2. It can be seen that the number of Z is 
mainly concentrated in 16 and 18, indicating that the cluster 
is the CN14 coordination polyhedron, and the number of glue 
atoms between clusters is either 1 or 3, as shown in Fig. 2.
Furthermore, for the Al-TMs HEAs with five elements in 
equi-atomic ratio, the composition formula can be expressed 
by a cluster formula [Al-M
14]Al3, and in non equi-atomic 
ratios can be expressed by [Al-M 14]Al.

 Al-TMs with FCC structure
Some Al-TMs HEAs are derived from 
the high-temperature FCC phase, and 
some even retain the single FCC structure 
at room temperature. Table 3 lists 
some typical Al-TMs HEAs with FCC

are three steps to analyze the composition

to the shell positions in the cluster

glue-atom model for FCC solid solutions, 
the shell positions cover 12 atoms, as 
shown in Fig. 1(c). Secondly, the number 
of Al is calculated by setting the total 
number of solvents to be 12, according to 
the equation of M/Al=12/ x. Finally, the 
total number, Z, of cluster formula equals 
12 plus x. The number of atoms Z in the 
cluster formula is calculated, as shown in 
Table 3. It can be seen that the number of 
Z is mainly concentrated in 14, indicating 
that the cluster is the CN12 coordination 
polyhedron, and the number of glue atoms 
between clusters is either 1 or 3, as shown 
in Fig. 3(a). The composition formula of 
FCC Al-TMs HEAs with five elements in 
equi-atomic and non equi-atomic ratios 
can be expressed by a cluster formula 
[Al-M12]Al.

Figure 4 shows the XRD diffraction patterns for designed 
Al-TMs HEAs. It is clearly that there is only BCC phase in 
the three alloys, indicating that changes of Al and Ta contents 
have little effect on the phase constitution. In these alloys, 
which contain more than five elements in nearly equi-atomic 
ratios, the entropy of mixing (ΔS
mix) is significantly improved 
compared to traditional solid solution alloys. Then, according 
to the equation of ΔG
mix=ΔHmix-ΔSmix 
[24]
, the ΔGmix (the Gibbs 
free energy of mixing) is decreased, which is beneficial to 
improve the stability of solid solution.
Figure 5 shows the microstructures via OM images. It can 
be seen that the microstructure of all the [Al-Ti
4V3Nb4Mo3]Mo,
[Al-Ti3V3Nb4Mo4]Al and [Al-Ti3V3Nb4Mo3Ta]Al alloys consists of

Figure 6(a) shows the compressive true stress-true strain 
curves obtained at room temperature. The [Al-Ti4V3Nb4Mo3]Mo 
and [Al-Ti3V3Nb4Mo4]Al alloys display high yield compressive 
strength of 889 MPa and 985 MPa, and good plastic 
deformation of 19% and 24%, respectively. Compared to the 
alloy based on the cluster formula of [Al-Ti
4V3Nb4Mo3]Mo, the 
[Al-Ti3V3Nb4Mo4]Al alloy, which satisfies the cluster formula 
of HEAs, shows higher strength and plastic deformation. When 
replacing some Mo with Ta based on the [Al-Ti
3V3Nb4Mo3Ta]Al
cluster formula, the strength and plastic deformation of the 
alloy are sharply decreased which may be ascribed to the work 
hardening capacity. Figure 6(b) exhibits the curves of work 
hardening rate at room temperature, showing the multi-stage 
work hardening behavior. In Stage A, the work hardening rate 
dramatically decreases because the alloys undergo elastoplastic 
transition 
[25-27]
. Furthermore, the curve of work hardening 
rate of [Al-Ti 4V3Nb 4Mo 3]Mo alloy is nearly parallel to 
[Al-Ti 3V3Nb4Mo4]Al, indicating their evolutional rate of 
dislocation density is at the same level during the initial 
beginning of plastic deformation 
[28]
. However, the sharp drop 
of work hardening rate is suppressed in Stage B, where the 
work hardening rate slightly increases with the increasing 
strain, which is beneficial to improve plastic deformation.

4 Conclusions
The composition law of Al-TMs (TMs, transition metals) 
HEAs with BCC and FCC structures was revealed based on 
the cluster-plus-glue-atoms model. The main conclusions from 
the present work could be summarized as follows:
(1) The composition formula of Al-TMs HEAs with BCC and 
FCC structure can be expressed by cluster formula [Al-M
14]Al
and [Al-M12]Al, respectively, where M is the average atom of 
the TMs.
(2) The [Al-Ti 3V3Nb4Mo4]Al alloy has both high strength 
and high plastic deformation at room temperature. Besides, 
compared to [Al-Ti
4V3Nb4Mo3]Mo and [Al-Ti3V3Nb4Mo3Ta]Al,
[Al-Ti 3V3Nb4Mo4]Al alloy shows a slower soften effect at 
800 ºC, resulting in a higher strength. The results suggest the
