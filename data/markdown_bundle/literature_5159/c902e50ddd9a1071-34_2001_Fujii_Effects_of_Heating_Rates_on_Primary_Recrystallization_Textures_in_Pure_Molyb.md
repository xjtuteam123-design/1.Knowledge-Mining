SYNOPSIS
In order to control the primary recrystallization textures of pure molybdenum (pure Mo) and molybdenum-0.5mass% 
Ti-0.1%Zr-0.5%C alloy (TZM alloy) sheets, the effects of heating rates during annealing on the development of 
recrystallization texture were investigated by means of X-ray diffraction and line broadening techniques . 
Recrystallization at a rapid heating rate produces a texture which is substantially similar to the rolling texture in both 
pure Mo and TZM alloy sheets, i.e. a ({100} <011>+{111} <uvw>+{211} <011>) type texture. While recrystallzation 
at a slow heating rate produces a texture which is significantly different from the rolling texture in both sheets, i.e. a 
strong {111} <112> type texture. Changes in the primary recrystallization textures of both molybdenum sheets 
which were brought about at different heating rates are considered mainly to arise as a result of the difference in the 
orientation dependence of degree of recovery for the deformed fine structures (potential nuclei of recrystallization).
KEY WORDS
pure molybdenum, TZM alloy, primary recrystallizatin texture, heating rate
1 Introduction
It is well known that the primary recrystallization texture 
developed on annealing a rolled material is controlled to a 
large extent by the rolling texture and is often very different 
from its parent texture. In fact, the primary recrystallization 
textures, which are significantly different from rolling ones 
in the cases of iron and low carbon steel sheets, have been 
shown by a large number of papers.
Considering the textures developed in rolled and 
annealed molybdenum and its alloy sheets which have been 
presented by a few investigators, it is thought that the 
primary recrystallization textures of polycrystalline 
molybdenum and its alloy are substantially the same as the 
rolling ones and are qualitatively consistent with those 
found in other b.c.c refractory metals such as W and Nb 
having high melting points. It seems , however, that no 
attempts have been made to change the rolling textures into 
other recrystallized textures different from those produced 
by the conventional heat treatment . In the present work, 
therefore, the effects of heating rates during annealing upon 
the development of primary recrystallization textures of 
pure Mo and TZM alloy sheets were investigated. In this 
experiment, pure Mo and TZM alloy were adopted as the 
representative refractory metals with high melting points . 
Especially, TZM alloy, a precipitation strengthening alloy ,
was used to examine the effects of precipitates such as TiC, 
Mo2C and ZrC on rolling textures.
2 Experimental
A sintered pure Mo and a Arc-Melted TZM alloy sheets 
used in this investigation had been hot-rolled from 20 to 2 mm 
in thickness with a finishing temperature of about 1273 K 
in H2 atmosphere. The rectangular specimens 35 x 35 x 
2 mm3 were cut from the hot rolled sheets and then annealed 
at 1773 K for 1 h (3.6 Ks) in a vacuum of less than 10-7 
Torr, through rapid heating in order to accomplish a 
complete recrystallization and hopefully to obtain random-
oriented initial grains. Metallographic examination 
indicated that the annealed pure Mo and TZM alloy 
specimens exhibited uniformly fine-grained structure with 
average grain size of 30µm and 35µm, respectively. 
Electron microscopic observation also indicated that the 
annealed TZM alloy specimen had a very few precipitates, 
presumed to be TiC, Mo2C and ZrC, which were scattered 
at the grain boundary or inside grain. Both specimens were 
rolled up to 80% with a reduction of 2% per pass at 473 K 
Annealing was performed in two ways. In rapid heating, 
specimens were put into the tube furnace which was kept 
at 1573 K In this case, heating rate was 13000 K/h (3.6 K/s).

4 Conclusion
Studies were undertaken to examine the influences of 
heating rates during annealing on the development of 
primary recrystallization textures in pure Mo and TZM alloy 
sheets. It is concluded that the heating rate has a major 
effect on the development of primary recrystallization 
texture of pure Mo and TZM alloy as follows.
(1) In the case of slow heating rate during annealing, a 
 strong {1111 <112> type texture, which is significantly 
different from the rolling texture, is formed in both 
 sheets.
(2) In the case of rapid heating rate, a weak ({100} <011>
+{111} <uvw>+{211} <011>) type texture, which is 
 substantially similar to the rolling texture, is developed 
 in both sheets.
