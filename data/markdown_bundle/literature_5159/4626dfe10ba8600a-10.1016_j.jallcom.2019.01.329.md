Abstract 
The Ni-Mo-Cr system is critical for the design of n ickel-based superalloys. This system 
stabilizes different topologically close packed (TC P) phases which precipitate in many 
of the commercially used superalloys. A diffusion m ultiple approach was applied to 
map the phase diagram of the ternary system in a hi ghly efficient manner. Two 
isothermal sections of the Ni-Mo-Cr system at 1100° C and 1250°C were constructed 
from the results obtained from diffusion multiples using scanning electron microscopy 
(SEM), energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) a nd electron backscatter 
diffraction (EBSD). At both temperatures two TCP ph ases were observed, namely the 
tetragonal σ- and the orthorhombic P-phase. In the range of thi s study the σ-phase was 
found to be widely stable towards the Cr-poor side, whereas the P-phase was detected 
on the Ni-rich side. The phases were characterized mechanically by microhardness

1. Introduction 
The development of gas turbine engines is strongly governed by new achievements in 
materials engineering. The performance and efficien cy of such turbines is closely 
related to the turbine inlet temperature and thus t he maximum operating temperature of 
the turbine blades, which are mostly made of Ni-bas ed superalloys [1,2]. Consequently 
Ni-based superalloys passed various generations of development over more than 7 
decades. Research and development were concentrated in large part on enhancing their 
working temperature. Latest improvements in the hig h temperature creep and rupture 
properties have been accomplished primarily by addi ng increasing amounts of 
refractory elements [3-6]. Modern Ni-based superalloys are alloyed with W, Mo and Re, 
for instance, as they are known to be efficient solid solution strengtheners [7,8]. 
The different generations of Ni-based superalloys c an be easily distinguished by the 
content of Re and Ru. Whereas the first generation alloys contained neither of both

However, the useful concentrations of all refractor y elements are limited as they 
promote the formation of a phase family known as to pologically close packed (TCP)

difficulties in alloy design, which arise from mult iple facts. First the solubility of the 
elements W, Mo, Re and Cr is much lower in the inte rmetallic γʹ-phase (Ni 3Al) than in 
the γ-matrix [10], which results in an accumulation of t he refractories in the matrix. 
Moreover this effect is intensified by the large vo lume fraction of the γʹ-phase in 
modern alloys of up to 70%.

that the regular 
γ-γʹ-structure is thermodynamically unstable. Just slow kinetics in terms 
of nucleation and growth transformation help to pre vent TCP precipitation during the 
life of a Ni-based component [7,11-14]. The main di sadvantage brought about by TCP 
formation is the associated degradation in mechanic al properties caused by two main 
mechanisms. On the one hand the elements primarily forming TCPs are Ni, Cr, Mo, Co, 
W and Re, which are also the elements with the high est solid solution strengthening 
effect. Consequently, TCP formation and the matrix’ depletion of solid solution 
strengtheners are inseparable. On the other hand TC P phases are very brittle [15] and

2.1. Material and experimental procedure 
Manufacturing of diffusion multiples by encapsulating cast 
In this investigation a new manufacturing process f or diffusion multiples was used, 
which was developed by advancing the idea of Zhao e t al. [21-23] and was introduced 
in [24]. Elements with a purity of at least 99,95 w t.% were molten using an arc furnace. 
Thus ingots of the pure elements Ni, Re, Ru and W a s well as of a Mo-Cr solid solution 
(Mo80Cr20 at.%) were prepared. They were machined i nto cuboidal shapes with 
dimensions of 3 ·5·15 mm 3 by electro discharge machining (EDM). Use of the f lat 
grinding technique ensured proper contact and remov al of the re-cast layer of the EDM 
process. The individual rods were ground to a final surface finish of a roughness 
average R a lower than 0,3 µm. It has to be ensured that after isothermal annealing the 
initial concentration remains in a far enough dista nce as compared to the diffusion 
length. 
For each multiple three or four specimens were enca psulated by a two-step casting 
process. For this purpose, a vacuum induction furna ce working at <10 
-2 Pa was used. 
For the first casting campaign nickel melts were poured into a cylindrical copper mould. 
The different rods were placed at the bottom of the mould, fixed in position by clamps 
also made out of copper and with half of their leng th protruding. During subsequent 
cooling the shrinkage leads to a firm contact betwe en the specimens. The nickel-shells 
were completed from the opposite side by placing th e half-encapsulated diffusion 
multiples upside down into ceramic shell moulds of the same size and preheating them 
to 1450°C. This guarantees excellent quality of the joints when pouring molten nickel,

The thickness of the interdiffusion zone after cast -encapsulation (~15 min exposure to

achieved by intensively analysing four diffusion multiples in total, after several attempts 
(15) to optimize the processing route.

Isothermal annealing 
The assembled multiples were annealed at temperatur es of interest in order to enable 
diffusion of each material into the other. A very i mportant feature of the diffusion 
multiples encapsulated by casting is that they do n ot require further precautions against 
oxidation of the specimens’ interfaces, which disti nguishes the technique from earlier 
approaches [21-23,25,26].

One half of the samples was annealed at 1250°C for 100 h. For the other half of samples 
the applied temperature profile consists of two par ts, as described in [21-23]. First the 
samples were heated up to 1200°C and kept at this t emperature for 4 h to initialize an

expands and thermodynamic equilibrium at the interf ace is achieved. Subsequently to 
the heat treatment, the samples were quenched in wa ter in order to freeze in the high-
temperature state and to hinder further diffusion.

4. Discussion 
4.1. TCP formation and phase stabilities in ternary system 
Interdiffusion zones of pure Ni and a Mo80Cr20 (at.%) solid solution at temperatures of 
1100°C and 1250°C lead to the formation of two TCP phases, namely 
σ- and P-phase. 
Fig. 2, 3 and 6 show the sequence of phases typically formed during annealing, which is 
the Mo-Cr solid solution followed by the σ- and the P-phase, with increasing Ni content. 
Preceding literature results regarding the ternary Ni-Mo-Cr system are focused on the 
Ni-rich part of the system [18-20]. The investigati on of the continuous composition 
range from pure Ni to the binary Mo-Cr system is a novelty. Consequently it is the first 
time the σ-phase was detected in the Mo-rich part of the tern ary system with a Cr 
content of only 8 at.%. 
In the SEM-images of the interdiffusion zones – esp ecially the EBSD phase map 
(Fig. 3) – interfaces of bcc- and 
σ-phase as well as interfaces of bcc- and P-phase can be 
found. A three-phase equilibrium shows up. Herewith , the existence of a three-phase 
region of bcc-, σ- and P-phase in the phase diagrams is proven. Rela tive phase regions

4.2. Mechanical characterization of TCPs 
As both TCP phases show only a slight dependence of composition on temperature it 
can be expected that the same applies to their mech anical properties. The results of the 
hardness values confirm this assumption not showing a significant dependence on 
annealing temperature. The difference between both temperatures is 534 and 
432 N/mm 
2 for σ- and P-phase, respectively. 
To our knowledge σ- and P-phase have never been characterised mechani cally as 
material of this dimension (>10 µm). With layers of both phases in the interdiffusion 
zones being at least 20 µm in width it is fair to c onsider them as bulk in microhardness 
measurements. Therefore, a classification of the re sults is critical. σ- and P-phase are 
reported to have values of 435 HV and 450 HV, respe ctively [20] or even less, namely 
350 HV [31]. However, hardness values of TCP phase s from literature are generally 
taken from precipitates in alloys or multi-phase sp ecimens, i.e. very hard precipitates 
embedded in a notably softer matrix. Consequently, hardness values of TCP phases 
have been drastically underestimated and are much m ore reliable taken from larger 
volumes, as given in the present work.

σ- and P-phase, were characterized regarding their m aximum 
solubility of Re, W and Ru. Three trends can be derived from the results of Fig. 7 and 8. 
First, the solubility of the examined elements is h igher in P- than in σ-phase. Moreover, 
there is a temperature dependence, which applies to both phases. The maximum

5. Conclusion 
The following conclusions can be drawn from the present work: 
(i) Phase equilibria of the ternary Ni-Mo-Cr system at temperatures of 1100°C and 
1250°C have been investigated. Two types of TCP pha ses were observed in the

## Page 21

MANUSCRIPT 
ACCEPTED
ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT
- 20 - 
 
interdiffusion zones of pure Ni and a Mo-Cr solid s olution, namely σ- and 
P-phase. 
(ii) Using EDS and EBSD analyses of the present wor k and data from literature 
sections of the phase diagrams have been mapped at both temperatures. The 
investigations reveal that the σ-phase is widely stable towards the Cr-poor side of 
the ternary system, which has not been reported in literature yet. 
(iii) Mechanical characterization of the TCP phases was carried out by microhardness 
measurements. Very high values in the range of 8,5 to 9,2 kN/mm 2 were found for 
the two TCP phases. 
(iv) Investigations of quaternary systems, includin g the elements Re, W, and Ru, 
reveal that both TCP phases have a high solubility limit for Ru (>5 at.%) and 
lower limits for Re (<10 at.%). This contradicts fi ndings of Ni-based superalloys, 
where TCPs are generally observed with higher Re (> 15 at.%) and lower Ru 
content (<5 at.%). 
(v) The presented multiple approach applied in TCP phase containing systems 
brought new findings regarding the stability of the σ-phase, which will provide a 
suitable experimental base for physically proper ex tensions of thermodynamic 
descriptions to multi-component Ni-based superalloy s databases, and
