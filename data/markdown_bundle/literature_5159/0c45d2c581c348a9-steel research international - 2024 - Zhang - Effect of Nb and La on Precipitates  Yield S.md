Effect of Nb and La on Precipitates, Yield Strength and
Toughness of FeCrAl Alloy
Huai Zhang, Chengbin Shi,* and Yiwa Luo*
1. Introduction
The Fukushima nuclear accident in 2011 exposed the insufﬁcient
ability of traditional zirconium alloy cladding materials to resist
accidents. The reason is that under accident conditions, the tem-
perature of the reactor core rapidly rises to over 1000 °C, and zir-
conium alloy reacts with water vapor to produce a large amount
of H 2 through the reaction Zr þ 2H2O ! ZrO2 þ 2H2", leading
to hydrogen explosion. [1– 3] Therefore, it is urgent to develop
accident-tolerant fuel (ATF) cladding materials. ATF cladding
materials not only require good resistance to neutron irradiation
and corrosion but also excellent resistance to high-temperature
steam oxidation.[4,5] Traditional austenitic stainless steel, ferrite/
martensite (F/M) steel, zirconium alloy, and titanium alloy are
difﬁcult to meet the requirements of high-temperature steam
oxidation resistance above 1000 °C. [6– 8] FeCrAl alloy is regarded
as a promising material for light water reactor fuel cladding,
owing to its exceptional resistance to oxidation at elevated tem-
peratures and its impressive radiation performance. [8,9] Due to
the high Al content, the FeCrAl alloy maintains excellent
oxidation resistance in a steam environ-
ment at 1400 °C. [10,11] Once the water loss
accident occurs, the ATF cladding material
can be subjected to temperatures exceeding
1000 °C. However, FeCrAl alloy exhibits a
single ferrite matrix, with rapid grain
growth and signi ﬁcant embrittlement at
temperatures above 700 °C. [12,13] The pre-
cipitation of dispersed second phase in
the FeCrAl alloy matrix is employed to
effectively pin grain boundaries and inhibit
grain coarsening, thereby enhancing the
mechanical properties.
Several studies have been conducted on
the precipitate of FeCrAl alloy after
microalloying.[13– 21] Sun et al.[13] found that
FeCrAl alloy with Nb or Mo additions
obtained ﬁne and uniform recrystallized
microstructure after annealing at 650–900 °C,
and the texture of Nb-containing alloy is
much weaker than that of Mo-containing
alloy. Gussev et al.[14] demonstrated a signiﬁcant reduction in grain
size (70– 80%) and an increase in yield strength (98 MPa) of FeCrAl
alloy with the addition of 1 wt% TiC. Massey et al.[15] reported that
the addition of Zr would compete with Al in the matrix to formﬁne
composite oxides, which is beneﬁcial for improvingthe mechanical
properties of FeCrAl alloy. Rare earth has a unique extranuclear
electronic structure, which can play a role in modifying inclusions,
puriﬁcation of liquid steel, and microalloying.[16,17] Hiroki et al.[18]

FeCrAl alloy is considered a promising material for light water reactor fuel
cladding due to its excellent elevated temperature oxidation resistance and
radiation performance. The effects of 0.21 wt% Nb or 0.23 wt% La additions on
the microstructure, second phase, yield strength and toughness of FeCrAl alloy
are studied. The morphology and number fraction of inclusions in FeCrAl alloys
are analyzed. The addition of 0.21 wt% Nb in FeCrAl alloy promotes the pre-
cipitation of nanoscale (Fe,Cr) 2(Nb,Ti) Laves phase and re ﬁning grain size. The
addition of La-modi ﬁed Al2O3 and MgO·Al2O3 inclusions to La 2O3 and LaS, and
La2O3 is favorable to promote the heterogeneous nucleation of α-Fe. The fracture
mode of the FeCrAl alloy with 0.21 wt% Nb or 0.23 wt% La transitions from
dimple fracture and quasi-cleavage fracture to dimple fracture, in comparison
with Nb-free or La-free FeCrAl alloy. Nb and La additions improve the yield
strength and toughness of FeCrAl alloy. The addition of Nb exhibits a more
pronounced strengthening effect on FeCrAl alloy compared with the addition
of La.

2. Experimental Section
2.1. Sample Preparation
The chemical compositions of the FeCrAl alloys with 0.21 wt%
Nb or 0.23 wt% La produced by vacuum induction melting are
shown in Table 1 . As-cast FeCrAl alloys were named after the
chemical compositions, in which Fe, Cr, and Al elements were
abbreviated as FCA. The specimens were cut from the as-cast
alloy using an electric spark wire-cutting machine for microstruc-
ture characterization, impact, and tensile tests.
2.2. Microstructure Characterization
After grinding and polishing, the alloy specimens were etched
with aqua regia solution. The microstructures of the alloys were
observed by optical microscope (OM, DM4 M, Leica, Germany)
and scanning electron microscope (SEM, FEI Quanta-250, FEI
Corporation, Hillsboro, OR, USA). The grain size was obtained
by measuring ﬁfteen randomly selected OM images, which was
completed by Image-Pro Plus 6.0 software (line intercept method
for measuring grain size). An automated SEM instrument
(EVO18, ZEISS) equipped with energy dispersive spectrometer
(X-MaxN, Oxford Instruments) (INCA, Oxford Instruments)
was employed to measure the type and quantity of the inclusions.
The polished cross-section of 100 /C2 100 μm was analyzed in all
three alloys. The type of Laves phase was identi ﬁed by transmis-
sion electron microscopy (JEM-2100 F).
2.3. Impact Toughness Testing
The Charpy impact tests were performed at /C0 40, /C0 20, 0, 20, 40,
and 60 °C, respectively. The specimens were cut into a size of
55 /C2 10 /C2 10 mm with a V-notch on one side according to
GB/T 229-2020. Five specimens were tested at each temperature,
and both the highest and lowest values were excluded when
calculating the average of the data.
2.4. Tensile Test
Tensile tests were carried out at room temperature using a
Quasar 10 electronic tensile testing machine. Tensile specimens
with a gauge length of 15 mm and a diameter of 5 mm were pre-
pared according to GB/T 228.1-2021. Tensile tests were carried
out at a strain rate of 0.1 mm min /C0 1. The value of yield strength
and elongation presented in this work is an average of three
measurements.
3. Results
3.1. Effect of Nb and La on the Microstructure of FeCrAl Alloy
Figure 1 illustrates OM micrographs for the microstructure of
the as-cast FeCrAl alloys. The average grain sizes for FCA,
FCA-Nb, and FCA-La are 64.7, 32.5, and 50.3 μm, respectively
(Figure 2 ). Compared with other alloys, the FCA-Nb exhibits
the smallest average grain size and a more homogeneous distri-
bution of grain size.
The morphology of inclusions and precipitates in FeCrAl
alloys is illustrated in Figure 3 . The size of inclusions in FCA

the main components of Fe, Nb, Cr, and Ti within the grains
(Figure 3f ). The nanoscale precipitate is identi ﬁed as (Fe,Cr) 2
(Nb,Ti) Laves phase with a size of about 300 nm by energy dis-
persive spectrometer detection and electron diffraction pattern

4. Discussion
4.1. Thermodynamic Consideration of Nb Addition to
FeCrAl Alloy
According to the composition of the FeCrAl-Nb alloy shown
in Table 1, the equilibrium phase diagram was calculated
with Thermal-Calc software (TCFE8). Comparing Figure 8a with
Figure 8b, the (Fe,Cr)2(Nb,Ti) Laves phase in the FCA-Nb alloy is
preferentially precipitated. The precipitation of Laves phase con-
sumes a large amount of Cr, which inhibits the precipitation of
Cr23C6. The interface energy between the M 23C6 and the matrix
is higher than that of the Laves phase, which is unfavorable for
the tensile strength of the alloy. The precipitation of nanoscale
Laves phase is bene ﬁcial to improve the strength of the alloy.
During prolonged exposure to elevated temperature, the coarsen-
ing of precipitates is an inevitable occurrence in heat-resistant
alloys. The coarsening rate of Laves phase is lower, maintaining
a nanoscale size even during a prolonged ageing process of up to
3000 h.[25] The Laves phase can maintain a signi ﬁcant strength-
ening effect during the service of the alloy.

depends on two aspects: a) the high liquidus temperature of
inclusion, which provides a heterogeneous nucleation core,

phase.[26,27] Upon the incorporation of La, the average size of
inclusions decreases, and the size range is 1 – 2 μm. Since the
binding energy between La and O is larger than that between

as a suitable core for heterogeneous nucleation. The calculation

5. Conclusions
The effects of Nb and La additions on the microstructure, second
phase, and mechanical properties of FeCrAl alloy were ascer-
tained. The main conclusions are as follows: 1) The quantity
of Ti(C,N) inclusions observed in FeCrAl alloy with 0.21 wt%
Nb is signi ﬁcantly reduced, with the predominant precipitate
being (Fe,Cr) 2(Nb,Ti). 2) Compared with FeCrAl alloy, the yield
strength of FeCrAl alloy with 0.21 wt% Nb increased by
95.06 MPa, and the impact energy at 0 °C increased by 102.3 J.
The addition of Nb promoted the precipitation of nanoscale
(Fe,Cr)2(Nb,Ti) Laves phase, re ﬁning grain size and improving
grain size uniformity. 3) The addition of La-modi ﬁed Al 2O3
and MgO ·Al2O3 inclusions to La 2O3 and LaS inclusions. The
