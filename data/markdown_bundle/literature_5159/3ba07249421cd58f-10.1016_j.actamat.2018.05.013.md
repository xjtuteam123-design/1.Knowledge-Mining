Abstract 15 
The deformation responses at 77 and 293 K of a FeCo NiCr high-entropy alloy, 16 
produced by a powder metallurgy route, are investig ated using in situ neutron 17 
diffraction and correlative transmission electron m icroscopy. The strength and 18 
ductility of the alloy are significant improved at cryogenic temperatures. The true 19 
ultimate tensile strength and total elongation increased from 980 MPa and 45% at 293 20 
K to 1725 MPa and 55% at 77K, respectively. The evo lutions of lattice strain, 21 
stacking fault probability, and dislocation density were determined via quantifying the 22 
in situ neutron diffraction measurements. The results demo nstrate that the alloy has a 23 
much higher tendency to form stacking faults and me chanical twins as the 24 
deformation temperature drops, which is due to the decrease of stacking fault energy 25 
(estimated to be 32.5 mJ/m 2 and 13 mJ/m 2 at 293 and 77 K, respectively). The 26 
increased volume faction of nano-twins and twin-twi n intersections, formed during 27 
cryogenic temperature deformation, has been confirm ed by transmission electron 28 
microscopy analysis. The enhanced strength and duct ility at cryogenic temperatures 29 
can be attributed to the increased density of dislo cations and nano-twins. The findings 30 
provide a fundamental understanding of underlying g overning mechanistic 31 
mechanisms for the twinning induced plasticity in high entropy alloys, paving the way 32 
for the development of new alloys with superb resistance to cryogenic environments. 33 
Keywords : high entropy alloy, deformation twinning, neutron diffraction, cryogenic 34 
deformation, stacking fault energy 35

1. Introduction 37 
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) were first introduced in 2004 [1, 2], which aimed to 38 
maximise the configuration entropy to form a single phase microstructure via 39 
combining four or more principle elements in equimo lar or near equimolar ratios. The 40 
high configuration entropy, sluggish diffusion, coc ktail effect and large lattice 41 
distortion lead to their promising properties such as high strength, excellent ductility, 42 
and superior fracture toughness [3-8]. 43 
One type of HEA and its variants, based on five 3d transition elements (Fe, Co, Cr, 44 
Mn, Ni), can form a single phase face-centered-cubi c (fcc) structure, displaying an 45 
excellent combination of high strength, ductility a nd fracture toughness at both room 46 
and cryogenic temperatures. Their mechanical proper ties improve significantly with 47 
decreasing deformation temperatures [3-7]. We termed this group of HEA as tHEA to 48 
distinguish them from other high entropy alloys (su ch as multi-phase HEAs [8]). 49 
tHEAs have attracted significant interests for cryo genic applications, such as liquid 50 
nitrogen storage and transportation of liquid gas from offshore. 51 
Many microstructural studies [3-7], on strained or failed tensile specimens, have been 52 
carried out, confirming the occurrence of nano-twin ning during low temperature 53 
deformation of tHEAs. Hence twinning induced plasti city is attributed to the tHEAs’ 54 
superb mechanical performance. Twinning induced plasticity has been at the forefront 55 
of research recently as it is one of the micro-mech anistic mechanisms that can 56 
overcome the strength-ductility trade-off, which ha s been demonstrated by fcc metals 57

The deformation mechanisms of fcc metals and alloys are strongly related to the 60 
temperature- and composition- dependent stacking fa ult energy (SFE) [12]. With the 61 
drop of SFE, the deformation mechanisms change from dislocation glide alone, to 62 
dislocation glide and mechanical twinning, to dislo cation glide and martensite 63 
transformation [13-15]. This has been demonstrated in many fcc metals and alloys, 64 
including austenite steels [16], TRIP and TWIP stee ls [3,4], and Cu-Al alloys [17] . 65 
The SFE of the tHEAs has been measured or calculate d to be 10-40 mJ/m 2 at room 66 
temperature [18-20]. In this SFE range, deformation twinning is usually activated, 67 
which has been confirmed in tHEAs by a few experime ntal studies [3-7]. Recently, 68 
martensite phase transformation have been observed when tHEAs were deformed 69 
under hydrostatic high-pressure conditions [21, 22] at both room and high 70 
temperatures, demonstrating that the fcc tHEAs can be destabilized into hcp phase at 71 
high pressure (e.g. over 14 GPa [21]). 72 
It is known that the SFE will drop as the temperatu re decreases, hence at cryogenic 73 
temperatures, the formation of shear bands, deforma tion twins and stacking faults can 74 
be enhanced [6, 23]. Simulations using first-princi ples methods [24, 25] confirm the 75 
temperature dependent behaviour of SFE and some predict that the SFE of tHEAs can 76 
even be negative at cryogenic temperatures [19, 26- 28]. To validate the simulated 77 
results, the SFE of tHEAs at cryogenic temperatures need to be determined 78

3.3 Stacking fault probability 233

relationship between twins and stacking faults. Upo n deformation, passage of 235

nucleuses; in the end larger twins can be formed by passage of more partials on 239 
neighbouring (111) planes [43]. For alloys with sta cking fault between 18<SFE<45 240
