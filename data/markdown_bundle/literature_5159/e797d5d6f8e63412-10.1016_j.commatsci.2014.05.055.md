abstract
Microstructural evolution in single crystal Ni-based superalloys is investigated by the phase ﬁeld
simulation. During creep of the alloys, the morphology of the c0 phase changes from the cuboidal shape
to the rafted one and the rafted structure is collapsed in the later stage of creep. It is understood that
rafting is caused by the strain energy resulting from
c/c0 coherent interface. In this study, we focused
on the decrease of interfacial coherency in creep condition and investigated the dependence of morpho-
logical evolution on it. Creep strain, misﬁt strain and gradient energy coefﬁcient are changed with time in
a two dimensional (2D) phase ﬁeld simulation to express the
c/c0 interfacial transition from coherent to
semi-coherent in creep condition. The semi-coherent interface model recreates the two types of
microstructural evolution of the rafted structure to collapse observed in CMSX-4 and NKH71 superalloys.
Furthermore, creep strain rate, instantaneous strain and time of starting the interfacial transition from
coherent state to semi-coherent state decide substantially the morphology in the later stage of creep.
These morphological evolutions can be explained by the relationship between the amount of creep strain
and degree of coherency.

1. Introduction
Single crystal (SC) Ni-based superalloys are used for turbine
blades in gas turbines, aircraft engines etc. due to their superior
high strength properties at high temperatures than other metallic
alloys. Microstructures of the superalloys consist of the c and c0
phases. The cuboidal c0 phase precipitated in the c matrix coarsens
and arranges along the h100 i crystallographic directions during
aging. The creep property of the SC alloys is related closely to their
microstructural stability. In the early stage of creep, the plate
shape c0 phase becomes normal to the stress direction. The plate
like c and c0 phases are stacked alternately in the microstructure
and is called the rafted structure. In the later stage of creep, the raf-
ted structure is found to collapse resulting in decreasing the creep
strength. For example, this morphological evolution is recognized
in practical SC Ni-based superalloys such as CMSX-4 [1] and
NKH71 [2]. Some main differences e.g. microstructure and chemi-
cal parameters of CMSX-4 and NKH71 alloys are shown in Fig. 1
and Table 1 respectively. NKH71 alloy contains no Re and
12.2 wt% Cr, whereas CMSX-4 has 3 wt% Re and 6.5 wt% Cr. As Re
is very expensive, NKH71 alloy should be cheaper than CMSX-4
alloy, although CMSX-4 is being commercially used now. These
alloys show difference in the morphological evolution with respect
to the manner of the collapse of the rafted structure. In CMSX-4,
the c0 phase locally coarsens towards the direction parallel to the
applied stress direction and the c phase is isolated [1], whereas
in NKH71, the rafted structure transforms into a wavy structure
[2]. Furthermore, in NKH71, the rafted structure is formed in the
early stage of creep compared to CMSX-4 and its creep strength
is better than CMSX-4 at the later stage of creep [3] due to the
excellent stability of the rafted structure in NKH71. However, the
mechanism why the rafted structure collapses in two different
ways in these two superalloys is not clear yet. Since the creep
strength of the superalloys originates from the stability of the raf-
ted structure and accurate lifetime prediction of high temperature
component is of considerable technological importance, it is
important to reveal the phenomenon in the morphological evolu-
tion of superalloys.
Recently, the phase ﬁeld method has been applied for simulat-
ing microstructural evolution in SC Ni-based superalloys [6–14].
The phase ﬁeld approach describes spatial and temporal evolution
of the ﬁeld by solving the evolution equations. The microstructural
evolution can be simulated under the condition of reducing the

3.2. Semi-coherent interface model and coherent interface model
In this model, the transition from coherent to semi-coherent in
the c/c0 interface is expressed by decreasing the absolute value of
misﬁt strain in Eq. (14) and increasing gradient energy coefﬁcient
in Eq. (8). These correspond to the fact that dislocations introduced
during creep reduce misﬁt strain and that incoherent interfacial
energy density is larger than coherent one. This model represents
the transition from coherent to semi-coherent in the c/c0 interface
and simulates systematic morphological change of the c0 phase in
nickel-based superalloys during creep with a wide temporal range.
In general, it is hard to separate the elastic interfacial energy
and the chemical interfacial energy, both of which compose the
interfacial energy between the matrix and precipitates in alloys.
In nickel based superalloys, the elastic interfacial energy is an
important factor in the formation of cubic shape of the c0 phase.
On the other hand, the rafted phenomenon is the result of cubic
symmetry of the eigen strain originated from lattice mismatch
between the
c and c0 phase changes into tetragonal symmetry. This
is attributed to the plastic strain caused by creep stress. As the
plastic strain increases i.e., the interfacial dislocation density
increases, relaxation of the elastic strain occurs. As a result, the
chemical interfacial energy plays an important role in total interfa-
cial energy, which makes round shape of the c0 phase. In this study,
in order to make clear the main factor for the morphological
change of the c0 phase during wide temporal range from the
formation of the rafted structure to the collapse of it consistently,
a simple but substantial model suitable for the phase ﬁeld simula-
tion is newly constructed.

main factors causing the difference in the morphological evolution
between CMSX-4 and NKH71.
3.3. Characteristic features of the morphological evolution in semi-
coherent interface model
First, in order to investigate the effect of creep strain rate on the
morphological evolution, the simulations using the semi-coherent
interface model are performed by varying the creep strain rate as
(a) 4.0 /C2 10/C0 8, (b) 5.5 /C2 10/C0 8 and (c) 7.0 /C2 10/C0 8; the changing rate
of misﬁt strain and gradient energy coefﬁcient are the same as
shown in Fig. 2 . Fig. 6 shows the results of the simulation at
t/C3 = 200,000. The amount of the creep strain introduced at
t/C3 = 200,000 is (a) 0.008, (b) 0.011 and (c) 0.014. The width of c
phase channel becomes narrow and the morphology of the c0 phase
becomes wavy with increasing the creep strain rate. This means if
large amount of strain still remains during morphological
evolution before the transition from coherent to semi-coherent
interface, the morphological evolution occurs so as to release the
creep strain instead of the reduction of interfacial energy. As a
result, the morphology of the c0 phase becomes wavy. In fact, the
wavy structure as shown in Fig. 6 (c) is observed in the later stage
of creep in NKH71 alloy [2,3].

wavy microstructure that observed in NKH71 alloy. Furthermore,
the degree of semi-coherency depends on the amount of creep
strain. Thus, in order to predict the morphological evolution of
the c0 phase quantitatively, it is important to make clear the rela-
tion between the amount of creep strain and the degree of
coherency.
4. Conclusions
To make clear the inﬂuencing factors on the collapse of rafted
structure in SC Ni-based superalloys during creep condition, the
morphological evolution of the microstructure with creep defor-
mation is studied using the phase ﬁeld simulation. In this model,
the interfacial transition from coherent into semi-coherent is
expressed as the time change of misﬁt strain and gradient energy
coefﬁcient. In addition, the creep strain increases at a constant rate
with simulation time. By simulating this model, the dependence of
morphological evolution on creep strain rate, instantaneous strain
and the time when interface starts to change into semi-coherent
are examined and the results obtained are summarized below:
(1) The morphological evolution of collapse of the rafted struc-
ture observed in CMSX-4 is reproduced by using the semi-
coherent interface model where the time change of misﬁt

(2) The morphological evolution from rafted structure into
wavy structure is reproduced by using the coherent interface
model where misﬁt strain and gradient energy coefﬁcient

(3) In semi-coherent interface model, the morphology of
collapse of the rafted structure transforms into a wavy struc-

(4) The amount of creep strain at the starting time of
semi-coherency causes the difference in the morphological
evolution in the later stage of creep. Initially less creep strain
leads to round shape
c phase observed in CMSX-4 alloy and
more creep strain results in wavy microstructure observed
in NKH71 alloy in the later stage of creep.
(5) Clear understanding of the relation between the amount of
creep strain and degree of coherency is important in order
to predict the morphological evolution of c and c0 phases
quantitatively in Ni-based superalloys.
