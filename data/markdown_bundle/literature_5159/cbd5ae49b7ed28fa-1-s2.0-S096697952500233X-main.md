ABSTRACT
The body-centered tetragonal (BCT) metastable phases are usually observed during deformation and rarely after 
heat treatment. Here, we developed a novel dual-phase high-entropy alloy (HEA) featuring about 10 vol% blocky 
BCT and 90 vol% FCC phases by high temperature treatment. Our designed HEA exhibits a good 
strength – ductility balance, accompanied by a special fracture mode. This work not only advances the under -
standing of the intergranular cracking behaviors but also offers a strategy to develop new duplex HEAs with good 
work hardening.
1. Introduction
High entropy alloys (HEAs) are a novel class of alloys that are 
different from conventional alloys with one or two primary elements 
and have attracted growing attention due to their desirable mechanical 
properties [ 1 – 7 ]. HEAs are usually found to solidify into structures of 
single-phase solid solutions such as single-phase face center cubic (FCC) 
or body center cubic (BCC) structures even when they alloy various 
primary elements [ 8 – 12 ]. Nevertheless, these single-phase HEAs seem 
difficult to obtain a good combination between high strength and large 
ductility, such as FCC-structured HEAs generally have large ductility but 
low strength, whereas BCC-structured HEAs generally display high 
strength but relatively low ductility [ 9 , 10 ].
To address this issue, a class of HEAs with dual-phase structures has 
been developed to enhance mechanical properties, leading to a favor -
able strength-ductility combination [ 13 – 19 ]. Previous studies suggest 
that a dual-phase structure consisting of FCC(L1
2
)/BCC(B2) phases can 
be acquired by adding Al to HEAs with a single FCC structure under a 
certain range [ 20 , 21 ]. These dual-phase HEAs can incorporate the ad -
vantages of FCC and BCC phases. For example, Lu et al. reported the 
eutectic alloy idea and developed an AlCoCrFeNi

Recently, body-centered tetragonal (BCT) metastable phase has

The EBSD inverse pole figure (IPF, Fig. 4a1 ) displays slightly

Additionally, the average kernel average misorientation (KAM) value, as 
an indication of local misorientation, which is generally associated with 
the density of geometrically necessary dislocations (GNDs) during the 
deformation of HEAs [ 33 ]. The KAM mapping demonstrated that the 
local stress was concentrated primarily close to the grain boundaries and 
phase boundaries (see Fig. 4a3 ), which can be related to the beginning of 
grain-boundary sliding aided by slip and the accumulation of GNDs 
nearby [ 34 ]. As the strain increased to 9 %, multiple large cracks were 
detected along with grain boundaries close to the fractured end (see 
Fig. 4b1-b2 ). The KAM mapping revealed that large local misorientation 
was detected near the grain boundaries and phase boundaries, which 
can be correlated with the heavy accumulation of GNDs at the vicinity of 
them [ 35 ]. These grain boundaries and extensive block phases around 
grain boundaries can be considered as preferred paths or locations for 
the initiation of multiple cracks. Moreover, owing to their comparatively 
greater energies and activities, the high-angle grain boundaries (HAGBs,

nucleation of multiple cracks. Once the cracks start to initiate, they will 
extend rapidly along these phase boundaries and grain boundaries,

Recently, Cao et al. prepared the heterogenous columnar-grained HEAs 
[ 34 ]. Different from the common equiaxed grained counterpart HEA 
design idea, they demonstrated a novel grain boundary design approach 
to increase fractions of LAGBs effectively. This enhanced the ability to 
reduce the connectivity of HAGBs, and also helped to inhibit the 
nucleation and propagation of cracks. Therefore, we believe that further 
efforts will be adopted for such alloys to grain boundary engineering and 
second phase refinement in the future.

mode with little necking at ambient temperature. Such a pronounced 
transition without necking obtained here was mainly attributed to the 
presence of large-size block phases around HAGBs, which greatly 
enhance the barriers for dislocation slipping against FCC/BCT phase 
boundaries and consequently promote the nucleation and propagation 
of cracks along with phase boundaries. This finding not only advances 
the understanding of the intergranular cracking behaviors but also offers 
a strategy to develop new duplex HEAs with good work hardening.
