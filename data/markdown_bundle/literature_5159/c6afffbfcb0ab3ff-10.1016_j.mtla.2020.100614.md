Abstract 
A high -strength metastable -titanium alloy, Ti -5Al-5Mo-5V-3Cr-0.5Fe (wt.%) was 
deformed at room temperature and characterized using scanning and transmission 
electron microscopy. A novel {10 9 3}<331>  type deformation twining mode ( 19a) is 
reported for the first time, and crystallographic analysis was carried -out to elucidate the 
origin of such high -index deformation twins. The initially -solutionized alloy contains 
nano-scale ⍵ phase, which is also subsequently distributed within the twin s. 
Additionally, a thin nano -scale ⍵ layer forms along the twin boundaries, and two 
variants of ⍺ martensite phase form inside the deformation twins. The current work 
opens up new prospects for improving the ductili ty of high -strength Ti alloys by 
inducing the twinning and transformation induced plasticity effects 
.

Introduction 
 
Titanium and its alloys are tremendously important structural materials for a wide 
variety of commercial applications in industries ranging from biomedical to aerospace 
[1,2]. Careful microstructural design via alloying or thermo -mechanical processing has 
allowed for alloys with exceptional specific -strength, high toughness and/or good 
corrosion resistance. In general for -Ti alloys, a combination of refined  precipitate 
distribution, for high strength, coupled with transformation induced plasticity (TRIP) 
and/or twinning induced plasticity (TWIP), for improved ductility and strain 
hardenability, is highly desired and the goal of many studies [3]. In practice, such a 
combination of properties is difficult to achieve for -Ti alloys with relatively higher 
alloying content due to the influence of the  matrix stability on the deformation mode 
[4]. This is captured by the ̅̅̅̅- ̅̅̅̅̅ diagram, where an increase of matrix phase stability

## Page 3

(i.e.,  stabilizing content) , translates to a shif t from deformation twinning (DT) to 
dislocation slip (DS) [4]. Additionally, as the content of  solutes increases, the 
martensite (orthorhombic structured ) starting temperature (M s) is reduced to below 
room temperature and TRIP is essentially suppressed[4]. Based on this knowledge, most 
novel alloys are designed such that their compositions are located at the transition 
region between DT and DS of the ̅̅̅̅- ̅̅̅̅̅ diagram, where TWIP and TRIP are ac tive 
and impart good ductility at reasonable strength levels [5,6]. Although successful, t his 
design philosophy constrains research to a very narrow compositional range and 
excludes many existing alloys with great properties. 
 
DT in -Ti alloys occurs via either {112}<111> and/or {332}<113>  type twins, 
where the former is thought to proceed through the collective glide of 1/6<111> partial 
dislocations of the same Burgers vector on {112} planes [7–10], while the latter is 
proposed t o nucleate from  martensite during deformation [7,11–14]. Additionally, 
there have been reports on stress -induced  martensite [15,16], stress-induced ⍵ phase 
[17,18], and a combination of these [19–22]. Although much research has been focused 
towards understanding these deformation mechanisms, their exact origins, how the alloy 
composition influences them, and their interactions with DS, nano -scale second phase 
particles, and each other are still either unknown or highly debated. Moreo ver, Zhang et 
al. [23] recently reported a new DT mode, {5 8 11}<135> , as part of multimodal 
twinning mechanism that allowed for simultaneous improvement in strength and 
ductility of Ti -18Zr-13Mo (wt.%). The occurrence of such twinning mode warrants 
further (more detailed) investigation into the deformation mechanisms of more classical 
high-strength -Ti alloys. Extending the TWIP/TRIP range toward the precipitation 
region of the ̅̅̅̅- ̅̅̅̅̅ diagram will expand the compositional search space for new 
alloys, meanwhile elucida ting the deformation mechanisms in those regions will allow 
us to successfully leverage them, possibly in combination with precipitation, and to 
design alloys with improved performance and targeted properties, such as high strength 
with great ductility. 
 
Ti-5Al-5V-5Mo-3Cr-0.5Fe (wt.%, Ti -5553) is a metastable - commercial aerospace 
alloy utilized for the manufacture of landing gear and airframe components due to its 
high specific -strength and toughness [2]. Traditionally, this is a precipitation 
strengthened alloy, placing it in the dislocation slip deformation mode ( +) region of 
the ̅̅̅̅- ̅̅̅̅̅ diagram, away from the TRIP/TWIP regions [5], as shown in Figure 1 . As 
such, there are currently no reports of twin formation during deformation of Ti -5553, 
which would otherwise be advantageous in terms of improving ductility and strain 
hardenability. On the other hand, many studies have reported a variety of thermo -
mechanical processing routines that can refine the  precipitation to various degrees 
[24], and have characterized and explored the effect of different nano-scale precipitates, 
such as ⍵, O, and O on the  precipitation kinetics [25]. Achieving the higher strength 
levels, however, leads to very limited ductility of the alloy [26], thereby limiting its 
usefulness to certain applications. In these cases, achieving TWIP/TRIP would be very 
beneficial. In this work, we report the structure of Ti -5553 from b efore to after 
deformation (as-quenched state and cold -rolled to 10% reduction). Then, we focus on 
the characterization of the hierarchical deformation structure using scanning electron 
microscopy (SEM), and transmission electron microscopy (TEM).

Materials and Experimental Procedures 
 
A slab of Ti-5553 15 cm in diameter and 3 cm in height was cut from a forging obtained 
from BaoTi Group Ltd. Its composition was measured by inductively coupled plasma 
optical emission spectroscopy (ICP -OES), and LECO analysis for the light elements; 
provided in Table 1. Samples measuring 10mm ⨉ 7mm ⨉ 5mm were excised from the 
center of the forging, e ncapsulated in quartz tubes back -filled with Ar, and solution 
treated at 1000ºC for 30 mins (T  ~855ºC [1]) follo wed by water quenching. The 
samples were subsequently cold rolled to 10% strain at room temperature in 2 passes. 
After conventional sample preparation to 0.06μm colloidal silica, the microstructures 
were characterized in backscattered electron imaging mode (BSE) using a FEI Apreo 
field-emission gun SEM, equipped with an electron backscattered diffraction (EBSD) 
detector. TEM foils were prepared along specific orientations using a dual-beam 
focused ion beam (DB-FIB), FEI Helios Nanolab 600 system, and the Ga+ ion beam 
damage of the foil surfaces was further minimized using low voltage Ar + ion milling in 
a Fischione Nanomill TM Model 1040. A FEI Tecnai30 microscope operating at 300 kV 
was used for selected area diffraction (SAD) patterns and dark field images.

Results and Discussion 
 
Fig. 2(a-c) shows the undeformed, starting microstructure of Ti -5553 at different length 
scales. The average grain size after the solution heat treatment was ~270 µm, where the 
grains were devoid of any twins or martensite, as shown in Fig. 1(a). In the grain 
interior, a TEM -SAD pattern along the [110]  zone axis reveals the characteristic 
reflections belonging to the BCC matrix . Additional reflections can be seen at 1/3 and 
2/3 {112}  belonging to ⍵ phase, as well as reflections at ½ {112}  for the 
orthorhombic O' phase, Fig. 2(b). A corresponding dark-field (DF) image was generated 
using the reflections in the red circle, revealing a random distribution of these nano -
scale phases within the grain interior, Fig. 2(c). The structure of these phases in Ti-5553 
has been investigate d in detail by HAADF -STEM and atom probe tomography and 
reported in our previous work [25].

similar) sample. The darker-contrast regions inside the twin and parts of the parent -twin 
interface could not be indexed, signifying that the lattice of these regions is not BCC (or 
⍺-HCP). A pole figure was constructed with the data from the orientation map of the 
twin and parent grain and is shown in Fig. 3(f). As highlighted by the red circle, the 
common invariant twinning planes appear to be {10 9 3}.

To further confirm the crystallographic analysis of the DT mode in this alloy and gain 
insight of the structure of the twin and its interaction with the pre -existing nano-scale 
phases, DF-TEM imaging was performed; the results are presented in Fig 4. A bright 
field (BF) many-beam image of the twinned region is shown in Fig. 4(a), where the red 
dashed-lines are slightly offset from the twin/parent boundaries for visual aid. A SAD 
pattern along the [110] zone axis obtained from a twin/parent interface region is shown 
in Fig 4(d), revealing a ~26º rotation between two sets of BCC lattice reflections. 
Although the {10 9 3}  reflections are forbidden in the <110>  zone axes, the above 
measured misorientation between the twin and matrix is in close agreement wit h the 
predicted misorientation, 26.53°, by the crystallographic analysis, and also consistent 
with the EBSD measurement. Thus, this study provides the first experimental data 
showing the presence of the novel {10 9 3}<331>  twinning mode, which is unique i n 
metastable -titanium alloys and is in agreement with the crystallographic analysis. DF 
imaging of the respective reflections of each BCC lattice allows for independent 
visualization of the parent grain and twin, as shown in Fig. 4(b-c), respectively. 
Interestingly, large areas within the twin appear completely dark, Fig. 4(b), suggesting 
the existence of an additional phase in those regions. Performing SAD of the twin and 
parent grain reveals the presence of orthorhombic structured  only within the twi n, 
Fig. 4(e), and ⍵ phase in the twin and parent grain, Fig 4(f).

imaging was performed with their respective reflections and shown in Figure 5. The DF

is currently underway.

To illustrate the complex hierarchical nature of the deformed microstructure, a 
schematic is presented in Fig. 6. Initially the microstructure consists of nano -scale ⍵, 
and is devoid of any ⍺ precipitation, twinning or martensite. Upon deformation via cold 
rolling, {10 9 3}<331>  twins form, and both the parent grain and the newly -formed 
twins still contain the nano-scale ⍵. Additionally, two variants of ⍺ martensite form 
within the twins. Variant 1 corresponds to thick laths, while variant 2 is secondary 
smaller laths contained within variant 1 and thin lines. It can be speculated that due to 
the aggressive strain rate, the configuration of variant 2 likely reduce la ttice strains 
caused by the initial formation of the thick martensitic laths (variant 1), but this needs to 
be further confirmed by experimental evidence. Meanwhile a thin layer of ⍵ phase 
forms along the twin/parent interface, which also serves to reduce lattice strain by 
accommodating interfacial stress, and has been reported to form at {112}<111> twin 
boundaries [9].

While there are no reports of deformation twinning in Ti-5553, deformation-induced  
in single -phase Ti -5553 has been previously reported for samples undergoing high 
pressure torsion or uniaxial compression [31]. Barriobero-Vila et al. [32] studied the  
formation kinetics during slow strain -rate compression of Ti -5553 by in -situ hig h 
energy x-ray diffraction, and observed its formation upon yielding and a non -complete 
reversion upon unloading. The authors suggested that the ̅̅̅̅- ̅̅̅̅̅ diagram, Fig. 1, 
should be modified to extend the martensite formation boundaries to include Ti -5553, 
and given the results of this study, it could be argued that the limits of the twinning 
region of the diagram should also be adjusted to include Ti -5553. Furthermore, it has 
recently been reported that isothermal ⍵ phase can suppress the {332}<113>  twinning 
and martensitic transformation in Ti-25Nb-0.7Ta-2Zr [3], while nano-scale O' phase can 
contribute to the formation of different ⍺' martensite variants in Ti-4Al-4Fe-0.25Si-0.1O 
[33,34]. These studies demonstrate that the deformation of -stabilised Ti alloys cannot 
be over -simplified and additional factors influencing their deformation mode, such as 
grain size, initial microstructure, strain -rate, etc., need to be further studied to fully 
comprehend and appreciate the complex nature of def ormation. Cold rolling imposes a 
more complex stress state on the material compared to uniaxial deformation, and has 
been shown to result in the formation of large amount of  and considerable 
refinement of the  grains thereafter in Ti -10V-2Fe-3Al [35]. Ghaderi et al. [36] 
reported strong texture formation during 40% cold rolling of -annealed Ti -5553, 
however, they did not report any twin formation. The {112}<111> and/or 
{332}<113> type twins are the most frequently observed de formation twinning modes 
for -Ti, however, the present study unambiguously identified a higher-index twinning 
mode in Ti -5553. The analysis shows that the recently -reported {5 8 11}<135>  
twinning in Ref. [23], with a misorientation of 34.0º, and the {10 9 3}<331>  twinning 
reported in this study, with misorientation of 26.53º, are unique high order twinning 
systems in -Ti alloys. With respect to the coincidence lattice sites (CSL) description, 
these two boundaries correspond to a Σ35a and a Σ19a boundaries, respectively. Even 
though the crystallographic analysis reveals that these are also possible DT modes in -
Ti alloys , further investigation is needed to gain insight into what determines the 
twinning mode preference. 
 
Conclusion

## Page 12

In summary, a novel {10 9 3}<331>  type deformation twining mode (Σ19a) has been 
observed for the very first time in a -solutionized and cold -rolled metastable- 
titanium alloy, Ti -5553, using scanning electron microscopy and transmission electron 
microscopy. Results from both EBSD and TEM -SAD show a misorientation of ~26º 
between the parent phase and the deformation twin, consistent with the characteristics 
of {10 9 3}<331>  twin. A nano -scale thin layer of ⍵ phase at the twin boundary has 
been characterized together with two variants of  phase in the interior of the 
deformation twin. The observation of deformation twin and deformation induced 
martensite in the highly -stabilized Ti-5553 alloy indicates that the c riterion for TWIP 
and TRIP effect in titanium alloys should be re-visited. Besides the widely-used ̅̅̅̅- ̅̅̅̅̅ 
diagram, there are other factors that need to be taken into consideration for the future 
design of high -performance titanium alloys. Notably, the c urrent work provides a new 
design strategy to improve the overall performance of commercial, high -strength -
titanium alloy by inducing the TWIP and TRIP effect.
