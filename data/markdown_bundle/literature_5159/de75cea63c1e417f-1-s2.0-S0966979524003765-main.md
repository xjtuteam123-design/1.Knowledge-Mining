ABSTRACT
Novel nano-multilayer foils composed of Ni/Al-Al-FeCo/CrNi filler metals were developed for brazing 
Al
0.1
CoCrFeNi alloys. These Ni/Al-FeCo/CrNi filler metals, designed using a high-entropy concept, were inte -
grated to increase the mixing entropy and form solid solution structures. This strategic integration was aimed at 
preventing the formation of intermetallic compounds. The microstructural evolution and shear strength of the 
brazed joints were investigated by varying the holding time and brazing temperature. The results demonstrated 
the formation of a defect-free brazing joint, marked by the emergence of a solid solution structure resulting from 
the higher mixing entropy. With increasing temperature, the contribution of the solid-solution phase to the 
strengthening effect on the joint was more significant. The maximum shear strengths of the brazed joints were 
308.5 MPa and 292.8 MPa at room temperature and 800 
◦
C, respectively. Furthermore, the extended holding 
time and increased brazing temperature produced a steady increase in the shear strength of the joint with a 
corresponding transformation in the fracture mechanism from cleavage to ductility. This study introduces a novel 
process for brazing Al
0.1
CoCrFeNi and offers a technological paradigm for manufacturing industry at a high 
temperature (800 
◦
C).
1. Introduction
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) [ 1 , 2 ] have been widely utilized as al -
ternatives to high-temperature structural materials in the defense, nu -
clear, and aerospace sectors owing to their exceptional mechanical 
performance, heat resistance [ 3 ], high-temperature strength [ 4 ], and 
corrosion resistance [ 5 ]. These alloys are prepared using five or more 
principal elements that are present in equiatomic or unequiatomic 
proportions, with each component ranging from 5 % to 35 % [ 6 ]. The 
wide-ranging alloying composition and higher mixing entropy of HEAs 
contribute to their excellent mechanical performance compared to su -
peralloys. For instance, Al
0.1
CoCrFeNi [ 7 ], an unequiatomic HEA, 
exhibited exceptional mechanical properties across a range of temper -
atures, including room, cryogenic, and high temperatures [ 8 ]. This 
finding was attributed to its superior phase stability over an extended 
temperature range. In addition, Al
0.1
CoCrFeNi exhibited 
precipitation-strengthening capability and resistance to oxidation at 
high temperatures, making it a potential replacement for superalloys in 
aeroengines [ 9 ]. Consequently, HEAs have emerged as practical solu -
tions for meeting the rising demand in service conditions in various 
sectors, such as the nuclear, turbine, marine, and aircraft industries. 
Therefore, Al
0.1
CoCrFeNi is a promising high-performance material for 
advanced engineering applications [ 10 ].
In response to the increased competition in material processing and 
manufacturing, large structural and mechanical machinery are being 
redesigned to prevent expensive casting and machining. This redesign 
aimed to eliminate the need to transport these components from the 
suppliers to the manufacturing industry. A notable innovation in this 
context is the manufacturing of substructures and subsequent welding 
using advanced welding technologies. Therefore, the exploitation of 
dependable joining of the Al
0.1
CoCrFeNi alloy is critical for engineering 
applications. Several joining methods have been implemented for 
dependable joining of Al
0.1
CoCrFeNi alloys, including gas tungsten arc 
welding (GTAW) [ 11 ], laser beam welding (LBW) [ 12 ], rotary friction 
welding (RFW) [ 13 ], friction stir welding(FSW) [ 14 ], and diffusion 
bonding [ 15 ]. For the GTAW of HEAs, grain coarsening in the weld and

unique advantages for joining similar, dissimilar, and complex joints

exceptional brazing properties. However, a representative

formation of brittle compounds can be prevented by employing an 
appropriate intermediate filler layer. HEAs fillers have demonstrated 
significant potential for inhibiting the formation of harmful interme -

destroyed. To address these challenges, the utilization of 
nano-multilayer foils could be useful because they exhibit a melting 
point reduction effect and are integrated into the HEA synthesis process, 
thereby substantially lowering the preparation temperature of HEAs 
through the use of nano-multilayer films. The utilization of 
nano-multilayer films presents a promising avenue for further 
enhancing the preparation and procedure temperatures of HEAs 
[ 22 – 24 ]. Zhao et al. [ 23 ] produced nanocrystalline HEAs (FeCoNiCrCu) 
by designing equally layered nanotwinned Cu/HEA (FeCoCrNi) crys -
talline/crystalline nanolaminates with coherent crystalline/crystalline 
interfaces.
Brazing with a nano-multilayer film filler metal featuring a high- 
entropy design is a feasible process for achieving reliable bonding be -
tween alloys or ceramics at lower temperatures in air. Wu et al. [ 25 ] 
used self-propagation brazing to connect 304 stainless steel and 
Al
0.1
CoCrFerNi HEAs using nano-multilayer films and an Al filler. Wu 
et al. [ 26 ] also reported Inconel 625 alloy and Al
0.1
CoCrFerNi HEAs 
brazed joints with nano-multilayer films and Al fillers during 
self-propagation brazing. The formation of Fe-Al and Ni-Al IMCs was 
restrained and a solid-solution phase was formed. As the temperature 
increased, the morphology of the solid-solution phase changed from a 
single BCC layer to a dual-phase FCC + BCC layer, which increased the 
joint strength. The above investigation reveals that the formation of the 
solid-solution phase can improve joint properties during 
low-temperature brazing of dissimilar metals with nano-multilayer films 
and Al fillers. However, there are few reports on the properties of 
Al
0.1
CoCrFeNi brazed joints without IMCs and on the preparation of 
joints, particularly the interface, free of IMCs.

under the protection of an argon atmosphere and remelted at least 
four times for uniformity. The Al
0.l

microstructures and X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of the Al

followed by polishing with diamond polishing agent (W0.5, Ra 
(0.030 – 0.015 μ m)) to remove pollutants and oxidation film. The speci -
mens were ultrasonically cleaned in acetone for 10 min to remove grease

Al (99.99 % purity), and Ni (99.99 % purity). The RNMFs exhibited a 
total thickness of approximately 10.58 μ m and contained 9 Ni/Al bilayer 
repetitions with thicknesses of 708 nm Al and 468 nm Ni. Thus, the 
overall composition of the RNMFs is approximately AlNi ( Fig. 2 (c-1)).

4. Conclusion
We developed a novel brazing method for joining Al
0.1

HEAs using a multilayer Ni/Al-Al-FeCo/CrNi filler metal. To enhance 
the brazing structure and optimize the process parameters, we investi -
gated the formation and homogenization of Al
0.1
CoCrFeNi joints to 
understand the interfacial structures and phase compositions during 
brazing. 
(1) A pioneering brazing approach, named the “ high-entropy brazed 
joint, ” effectively united Al
0.1

resulting joint exhibited a typical microstructure comprising

0.1
CoCrFeNi 
HEAs.
(2) The use of NiCr/FeCo HEA-NMFs maximized the mixing entropy 
of the Al

◦
