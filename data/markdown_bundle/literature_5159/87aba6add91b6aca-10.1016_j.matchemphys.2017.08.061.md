Abstract
The elastic parameters and ideal tensile strength in the [001] direction for the body-centered cubic 
solid solution phase of FeCrCoMnAl x (0.6 ≤ x ≤ 1.5) high-entropy alloys are determined using 
first-principle alloy theory. Based on the estimated theoretical Curie temperatures, all alloys 
considered here are predicted to order ferromagnetically at room temperature. The mechanical 
behaviors are analyzed through the single crystal and polycrystalline elastic moduli, Pugh ratio, 
and Debye temperature by making use of a series of phenomenological models. High ideal tensile 
strength is found for the equi-atomic FeCrCoMnAl system, and the intrinsic strength increases 
with decreasing Al content.
Keywords: High-entropy alloys; Mechanical performance; First-principle calculations.

1. Introduction
 High-entropy alloys (HEAs), named by Yeh et al. in 2004 [ 1], have been considered as a 
promising new class of engineering materials in recent years [ 2-7]. Unlike traditional alloys with 
one or two major elements, HEAs are often composed of four or more elements in equal or nearly 
equal concentrations. Despite containing multiple components, HEAs tend to form simple yet 
chemically disordered solid solution phase rather than complex intermetallic structures. For 
example, HEAs consisting of late 3d transition elements usually crystalize in a face-centered cubic 
(fcc) phase, and the refractory HEAs in the body-centered cubic (bcc) phase [ 8, 9]. Besides the 
interesting structure, HEAs also exhibit a wide range of attractive properties, such as high hardness 
[10], excellent strength and ductility [ 11], outstanding thermal stability [ 12], as well as good 
resistances to wear, corrosion and oxidation [ 13-15]. Many important features, including high-
entropy, sluggish diffusion, severe lattice distortion and the cocktail effect, have been revealed in 
HEAs, which are responsible for their unique microstructure and special properties [5-7].
 Recently, Marshal et al. [ 16] introduced a non-refractory single bcc solid solution phase 
HEA, i.e., FeCrCoMnAl, which displays hardness (Vickers) value around 6.6 GPa. This value is 
~22% higher than other Al containing bcc HEAs such as FeCrCoNiAl [17]. To further enhance or 
tailor the properties of the HEAs for special applications, systematic understanding of the 
mechanical performance remains critical. So far, very few theoretical investigations devoted to 
this question have been published, which may be attributed to the complexity of the problem 
related to the chemical and magnetic disorder in connection with the multicomponent nature of the 
HEAs. In this work, we focus on the effect of Al addition on the mechanical properties of the 
FeCrCoMnAlx HEAs. Our tool is based on first-principle alloy theory, which was found to have 
the necessary accuracy and predictive power to reveal various parameters and mechanisms in such 
complex multicomponent magnetic alloys. We study several physical parameters which today are 
directly accessible from ab initio calculations: equilibrium structure, elastic constants, elastic 
moduli, Debye temperature, and ideal tensile strength. These parameters in turn are used as input 
data in a series of phenomenological models commonly used in literature for discussing and 
predicting the mechanical properties of metals and alloys. Our results reveal the effect of Al 
addition to the base alloy on the above physical parameters and the derived mechanical properties.

2. Theoretic methods
 All calculations presented here were performed using the exact muffin-tin orbitals (EMTO) 
method [18, 19], in combination with the coherent potential approximation (CPA) [ 20-22]. The 
EMTO method is an improved screened Korringa-Kohn-Rostocker method [ 23], where the exact 
single-electron potential is represented by large overlapping potential spheres. Inside these spheres 
the potential is spherically symmetric and constant between the spheres. It was shown that within 
overlapping spheres, one describes more accurately the crystal potential compared to the 
traditional muffin-tin or atomic-sphere approximations [ 18, 24]. Further details about the EMTO 
method and its self-consistent implementation can be found in previous work [18, 19, 22, 23, 25]. 
The CPA represents one of the most efficient alloy theory for the electronic structure calculations 
in random solid solutions [ 19-22, 26, 27]. As a single-site approximation, the CPA neglects the 
short-range order and local lattice relaxation effects. Nevertheless, the EMTO-CPA approach can 
accurately trace energy change related to lattice distortion in complex alloys [ 28, 29]. Also it has 
been applied previously to study the phase stability, magnetic behavior and mechanical 
performance of other HEAs [30-35].
 Here, we used the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) exchange-correlation approximation to 
obtain the charge density and total energy [36, 37]. The Kohn-Shan equations were solved within 
the scalar-relativistic approximation and soft-core scheme. The Green’s function was calculated 
for 16 complex energy point distributed around the valence states. The muffin-tin basis set 
included s, p, d and f orbitals. The Brillouin zone integrations were performed on 21×21×21 k-
points mesh. The paramagnetic state was modeled with the disordered local moment (DLM) 
approximation [38]. All calculations were performed for completely disordered solid solutions, 
considering static lattices and neglecting electronic and magnetic thermal excitations.

3.4. Tensile properties
 The ideal tensile strength (ITS) is the maximum attainable stress of a perfect single crystal 
and an intrinsic property of a material. It can provide insight into the correlation between chemical 
bonding and crystal symmetry, and has been accepted as a mechanical parameter for the design of 
high performance materials [50-52]. In the theory of fracture [52], for example, the local stress for 
nucleation of a cleavage crack should overcome the ITS. This strength has also been used to 
determine whether a pristine material is intrinsically brittle/ductile depending on failure modes 
under tension [53, 54]. In addition, the ITS is relevant in situations where dislocation activity is 
suppressed, such as in Ti-Nb binary alloys [ 55], or for systems with very low defect density [ 56, 
57] such as whiskers, nanowires and nanopillars. For a bcc crystal, the previous theoretical studies 
[58-60] and symmetry considerations [ 61] have identified the [001] direction as the weakest

4. Conclusions
 Using first-principle alloy theory, we put forward a comprehensive study of the mechanical 
performance for the FeCrCoMnAl x (0.6 ≤ x ≤ 1.5) HEAs. Within the considered composition 
range, the bcc structure is found to have lower energy than the fcc and hcp structures for both 
ferromagnetic and paramagnetic states. The calculated lattice parameters for the bcc phase agree

well with the available experimental data. The Curie temperature estimated via the mean field 
approach varies from 513 K to 413 K when x changes from 0.6 to 1.5 in the bcc lattice, indicating 
that the present alloys adopt the ferromagnetic state at room temperature. Next the composition-
dependent single crystal and polycrystalline elastic moduli calculated for the ferromagnetic state 
are used to analyze the mechanical performance including the brittle/ductile behavior. The ideal 
tensile strength in the [001] direction for the equi-atomic ferromagnetic FeCrCoMnAl alloy is 
predicted to be 7.7 GPa at a maximum strain around 9 %. The intrinsic strength can further be 
increased by decreasing the Al concentration. The present calculations serve as a guide for the 
design and optimization of alternative high strength materials based on HEAs.
