ABSTRACT
In this letter, MoNbTaTiC
x
(MNTTC
x
) alloys with a good combination of room-temperature fracture strain and 
high-temperature (1473 K) strength were successfully prepared by vacuum arc melting. The alloy is composed of 
the BCC matrix phase and the FCC carbide phase. With the increase of C content, the morphology changed from 
hypoeutectic to eutectic to hypereutectic finally. The eutectic structures ensure the good comprehensive me -
chanical properties of the fully eutectic MNTTC
0.5 
alloy by second-phase strengthening and grain refining, which 
has a fracture strain of 13.4 % at room temperature and a yield strength of 496 MPa at 1473 K, surpassing many 
of the reported refractory high-entropy alloys.
1. Introduction
Refractory high entropy alloys (RHEAs) are receiving increasing 
attention due to their unique behavior across a wide temperature range. 
Solid solution strengthening provides the greatest contribution to su -
perior performance at high temperatures due to severe lattice distortion. 
However, this strengthening effect rapidly weakened with increasing 
temperature [1] . Thus, second-phase strengthening is a method to 
compensate for the weakness of solid solution strengthening by intro -
ducing carbide [2] , silicide [3] , nitride [4] , etc. Nearly all robust RHEAs 
lack ductility at room temperature, including MoNbTaW [5] , suggesting 
careful design is needed.
Eutectic RHEAs are a potential candidate for combining deformation 
ability with high-temperature strength. This is due to grain refinement, a 
higher volume fraction of the strengthening phase, and stronger inter -
face inhibiting dislocation gliding [6,7] . In this study, a novel eutectic 
RHEA alloy was designed and successfully synthesized by vacuum arc 
melting. The evolution of the microstructure and mechanical properties 
were discussed with C content varying. With the contribution of second- 
phase strengthening and grain refining from the eutectic microstructure, 
the alloys show good comprehensive mechanical properties.

mainly contributed by the more prominent second phase strengthening

grain boundaries are detrimental for deformation ability especially at 
room temperature, thus the room-temperature fracture strains decrease 
quickly as the C content increases (x = 0.1 – 0.4). Eutectic morphology 
with an alternate lamellar structure is proved to be an effective way to 
achieve good combination of ductility and strength at the same time,

bonding, and various interfaces which could accommodate more dislo -

4. Conclusions
In this study, MNTTCx alloys were successfully prepared by vacuum 
arc melting. The microstructures changed from hypoeutectic to eutectic 
and then to hypereutectic as the C content increased. The eutectic 
lamellar is composed of BCC phase and FCC MC-type carbide phase. 
Carbide and eutectic morphology are important factors for achieving a 
good combination of room-temperature deformability and high- 
temperature strength. MNTTC
0.5
, with its eutectic morphology, was 
able to reach a yield strength of 496 MPa at 1473 K, while maintaining a 
fracture strain of 13.4 % at room temperature. This surpasses the per -
formance of many typical RHEAs.
