Microstructure and mechanical properties in
Ni45?4Mn39?5In13?1Gd2 alloy with high

G. F. Dong *1,2, H. J. Zhang 1, X. L. Zhang 1, H. B. Wang 3, J. H. Sui 4 and W. Cai 4
A Heusler Ni 45?4Mn39?5In13?1Gd2 alloy with high transformation temperature has been obtained by
substituting 2 at-%Gd for Mn in a ternary Ni 45?4Mn41?5In13?1 ferromagnetic shape memory alloy. It
is shown that the microstructure of Ni 45?4Mn39?5In13?1Gd2 alloy consists of a matrix and a Gd rich
phase. The Ni 45?4Mn39?5In13?1Gd2 alloy exhibits a martensitic transformation start temperature of
726 K, and the transformation hysteresis is Af2Ms5148uC. At room temperature, non-modulated
martensite of tetragonal L1 0 structure is observed in Ni 45?4Mn39?5In13?1Gd2 alloy. In addition, it is
revealed that the addition of Gd significantly enhances the compressive strength and improves
the ductility of Ni 45?4Mn39?5In13?1Gd2 alloy.
Keywords: Ni45?4Mn39?5In13?1Gd2 alloy, Martensitic transformation, Compressive strength, Microstructure
Introduction
Ferromagnetic shape memory alloys (FSMAs) exhibit
ferromagnetic and shape memory effect simultaneously,
in which the shape memory effects can be controlled by
temperature and stress as well as by magnetic ﬁeld.
Among the FSMAs, Ni–Mn–Ga is the most studied
alloy with 10% giant magnetic ﬁeld induced strain.
1,2
However, the polycrystalline severe brittleness and lower
output stress hinder its practical application. Therefore,
the exploitation of the candidate for Ni–Mn–Ga
becomes a primary trend. Recently, Sutou et al.
3
reported a new type of FSMA in Ni–Mn–X (X 5In, Sn
and Sb) alloy systems, where the magnetisation of the
martensite (M) phase is considerably smaller than that
of the parent (P) phase. Ni–Mn–In and Ni–Mn–Sn
based alloys have received much attention as promising
materials for magnetic devices, magnetic actuators,
4,5
magnetocaloric cooling devices, 6,7 etc., as well as the
NiMnGa conventional FSMA. 8–10 The output stress in
Ni–Mn–In based alloys is two orders of magnitude
higher compared with that in Ni–Mn–Ga alloys.
Therefore, the present study strides one step forward
on the practical applications of magnetic actuation
shape memory alloy (SMA) due to Ni–Mn–In based
alloys.
11 However, Ni–Mn–In alloys exhibiting extreme
brittleness, low strength and the requirement of high mag-
netic ﬁeld have greatly limited its practical applications.
Therefore, reducing the magnetic ﬁeld intensity threshold
of Ni–Mn based alloys and improving the mechanical
properties of alloys have become a priority in the
development of Ni–Mn based alloys.
Heavy rare earth Gd is ferromagnetic and has the
highest Curie temperature among the rare earth elements.
As we all know, the addition of Gd will greatly affect
the martensitic transformation temperatures, mechani-
cal properties and magnetic properties of Ni–Mn–Ga
alloys.
12–14 On the other hand, some researches reported
that the Gd element has giant magnetoresistance effect
and improves the mechanic al properties of alloys.
Consequently, we try to substitute Gd for Mn in Ni–
Mn–In alloy to improve the mechanical properties and
enhance the magnetocaloric effect. However, little
information about the addition of Gd to Ni–Mn–In
alloy has been found up to date. In the present paper, we
investigate the effect of Gd doping on the mechani-
cal property of Ni–Mn–In alloy for the ﬁrst time.
Moreover, our results show that by adding 2 at-%Gd in
Ni
45?4Mn39?5In13?1 alloy, a signiﬁcant improvement in the
mechanical properties of the alloy is achieved. Thus, the
purpose of the present work is to investigate the effect of
the substitution of Gd for Mn on the microstructure,
phase transformation behaviour and mechanical proper-
ties of polycrystalline Ni
45?4Mn39?5In13?1Gd2 alloy. The
results show that Gd doping can signiﬁcantly enhance the
compressive strength and improve the ductility of Ni–
Mn–In alloy. At the same time, the substitution of Gd for
Mn in a polycrystalline Ni
45?4Mn41?5In13?1 alloy notably
increased the martensitic transformation temperature and
transformation hysteresis; the M
s of the alloy with 2
at-%Gd was up to 726 K and still had a tendency to
increase. The excellent ductility and higher martensitic
transformation temperature make these alloys more
attractive for practical appl ications. Therefore, it is
necessary to study the inﬂuence of the increased Gd

Experimental
The alloy Ni 45?4Mn39?5In13?1Gd2, with nominal compo-
sition, was prepared with high purity elements by
melting four times in an arc melting furnace under an
argon atmosphere. The melted alloys were casted into a
chilled copper mould to obtain a master rod with
dimensions of 10 mm in diameter and 70 mm in length.
The master rod was sealed in a quartz tube under a
vacuum, then annealed at 900 uC for 12 h and quenched
into ice water for homogeneity. The phase transforma-
tion temperatures are determined by Perkin-Elmer
diamond differential scanning calorimetry (DSC), and
the rate of heating and cooling is 20 uC min
21. X-ray
diffraction (XRD) measurements were performed as a
means of crystal structure determination using a Rigaku
D/Max-rB with Cu K
a radiation. The microstructures of
the alloys were studied by an MX2600FE scanning
electron microscope equipped with an X-ray energy
dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) analysis system. Com-
press test was conducted at ambient temperature on
an Instron-1186 model machine at a strain rate of
0?05 mm s
21.
Results and discussion
Figure 1 a and b shows the backscattered electron images
of the Ni 45?4Mn41?5In13?1 and Ni 45?4Mn39?5In13?1Gd2
alloys. It is evident that the addition of Gd markedly
changed the microstructure and also reduced obviously
the grain size of the ternary Ni 45?4Mn41?5In13?1 alloy. It is
well known that the microstructure of Ni 45?4Mn41?5In13?1
alloy is monophase, whereas the microstructure of the
Ni45?4Mn39?5In13?1Gd2 alloy consists of the matrix (black
area) and the second phase (white area). Because the
scattering amplitude of the backscattering electron is
proportional to the atomic number of the individual
element, the content of Gd in the white second phase is
higher than that in the matrix. A similar phenomenon
has been reported in Ni

compositions of both the matrix and the second phase of
the experimental alloys measured by EDS qualitative
analysis are listed in Table 1. It can be seen that the solid
solubility of Gd in the matrix is very low. The Gd
addition resulted in the formation of the Gd rich phase
along the grain boundaries or in the grains. As shown in
Table 1, the Gd rich phase consists of Ni, Mn, In and

The reason for the increase in martensitic transforma-
tion temperatures in the Ni 45?4Mn39?5In13?1Gd2 alloy
may be explained as follows. The EDS results show that
the composition in the matrix is changed due to the
existence of the Gd rich phase, as shown in Table 1. The
content of Mn in the matrix is increased, and the Ni
content is almost unchanged, whereas the decrease in Ga
content occurs due to the substitution of Gd for Ga.
This implies that the Mn/Ga ratio increases when the
content of Ni appears to be approximately unchanged,
which can be accounted for the increase in martensitic
transformation temperatures. This is consistent with the
results obtained by Cai et al. and Jiang et al.
15,22 They
found that the partial substitution of Ga by Mn at
constant Ni concentration results in an increase in
martensitic transformation temperatures. In addition,
the thermal hystereses of both alloys are calculated as
the difference between reverse martensitic transforma-
tion ﬁnish temperature and martensitic transformation
start temperature. It appears that thermal hysteresis is
enlarged by the addition of Gd. Wang et al. reported
that the thermal hysteresis in NiMnGa alloys origi-
nates from the friction of phase boundary motion and is
only a small part of the latent heat of martensitic
transformation.
23 Thus, it is speculated that the addition
of 2 at-%Gd to the ternary Ni 45?4Mn41?5In13?1 alloy
increases the friction losses of phase boundary motion.

As mentioned above, the microstructure reveals that
the grains are reﬁned obviously with increasing Gd
content. The reﬁnement of the grains means the increase
in grain boundaries. Because grain boundaries hinder
effectively the plastic deformation, the movement of
dislocation and the propagation of the cracks, the
increase in fracture strength is mainly due to the
reﬁnement of the grains. In addition, rare earth element
can improve the purity and strength of the grain
boundaries by means of reducing the content of some
impure elements, which may be accounted for the
improvement of the mechanical properties. Further
investigation about the effect of Gd addition on the
mechanical properties is in progress.
To clarify the fracture mechanism and investigate the
nucleation and propagation of cracks in Ni
45?4Mn41?5In13?1
and Ni 45?4Mn39?5In13?1Gd2 alloys, SEM observations of
the fracture surfaces were performed, as shown in Fig. 4.
The fracture type of the undoped Ni 45?4Mn41?5In13?1 alloy
is intergranular crack, as shown in Fig. 4 a. Then, it
gradually becomes transgranular cleavage fracture after
being doped with Gd. As shown in Fig. 4b, the pulling out
of the precipitated second phase on the fracture is clearly
observed. The fracture morphology change is attributed to
the strengthening of grain boundary with increasing Gd
content. Compressive stress–strain curves and fracture
patterns at room temperature show that the doping of Gd
has successfully improved th e ductility and increased the
strength of the Ni–Mn–In intermetallics.
Conclusions
The microstructure of the Ni 45?4Mn41?5In13?1 alloy is
notably altered by the addition of Gd. The microstructure
of the Ni
45?4Mn41?5In13?1 alloy is cubic L2 1 structure and
that of Ni 45?4Mn39?5In13?1Gd2 with non-modulated mar-
tensite of tetragonal L1 0 structure at room temperature.
One-step thermoelastic martensitic transformation occurs
in this quaternary alloy. It can be seen that the marten-
sitic and reverse transformation temperatures of the
Ni
45?4Mn39?5In13?1Gd2 alloy increase compared with
that of Ni 45?4Mn41?5In13?1 alloy. It is found that Gd
addition can signiﬁcantly enhance the compressive
strength and improve the ductility of Ni 45?4Mn41?5In13?1
alloy. A maximum compressive strength of 2065 MPa
with a fracture strain of 9 ?32% is obtained in the
Ni45?4Mn39?5In13?1Gd2 samples, which is ,1700 MPa
higher than that of the Ni 45?4Mn41?5In13?1 alloy, the
highest compressive strength up to date compared with
alloys of other element alloying.
