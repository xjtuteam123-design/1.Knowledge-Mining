abstract
Refractory high-entropy alloys (HEAs) usually exhibit high strength but limited ductility. Here, we report
high strength ( /C24 1334 MPa) and large ductility ( /C24 18.8%) in nitrogen-doped TiZrNbTa HEAs. The
microstructural evolution and the corresponding mechanical properties of the as-cast/annealed
nitrogen-doped HEAs were investigated. All as-cast (TiZrNbTa)
100-xNx (x = 0, 0.3, 0.6) HEAs exhibit a
single-phase body-centred cubic structure. Interstitial strengthening contributes to the enhanced
strength. The improved ductility of the 0.6 at.% nitrogen-doped HEA originates from the promoted planar
dislocation slip inside grains. Spinodal decomposition and grain boundary (Zr, N)-enriched precipitates
emerge when the nitrogen content is 0.9 at.%, which results in signiﬁcant embrittlement. Isothermal
annealing of the 0.6 at.% nitrogen-doped HEA at 800 /C176C to 1200 /C176C was conducted to further improve
the mechanical performance. The complicated precipitates and grain boundary impurity segregation in
the alloys annealed at 800 /C176C and 1200 /C176C lead to deteriorated strength and ductility. However, in the
alloy annealed at 1000 /C176C, a slight increase in both strength and ductility appears due to solid solution
strengthening, in situ grain reﬁnement and promoted dislocation cross slip, which is induced by the
coherent boundaries. This work provides a promising conduit to approach high strength and ductility
in HEAs.

1. Introduction
As the most important structural materials, we have been pur-
suing higher strength and better ductility in metallic materials for
thousands of years. The improvement of strength is generally
achieved by increasing the resistance to dislocation motion [1].
These stagnant dislocations will accumulate to cause stress con-
centration and induce the formation of cracks, leading to the sacri-
ﬁce of ductility. A variety of strategies have been used to develop
advanced metallic materials with high strength and large ductility,
such as ultrastrong steels with high-density Ni(Al, Fe)-ordered
nanoprecipitates [2] and medium manganese steels with meta-
stable austenite grains and high dislocation density [3]. Recently,
high-entropy alloys (HEAs) have attracted much attention due to
their unique atomic-scale microstructures and subsequent unusual
mechanical properties [4–8]. Designing HEAs with high strength
and ductility plays an essential role in their structural applications.
HEAs are multiprincipal alloys that consist of at least four con-
stituent elements [9]. Due to their high mixing entropy, HEAs usu-
ally form simple solid solutions [10,11]. HEAs with high strength
and ductility can be obtained by tuning the complexity and
microstructure [12]. For example, (1) replacing Mn in CrMnFeCoNi
with Pd results in composition ﬂuctuations in the range of 1–3 nm
[7]. These composition ﬂuctuations bring nanoscale alternating
tensile and compressive strain ﬁelds in the matrix, which gives rise
to the dislocation pinning effect and cross-slip. Thus, the CrPdFe-
CoNi HEA has a higher yield strength than the CrMnFeCoNi HEA
without sacriﬁcing the strain hardening capacity and ductility.
(2) Doping 0.5 at.% interstitial carbon atoms in Fe
50Mn30Co10Cr10
HEA with transformation-induced plasticity can modify the phase
stability and stacking fault energy and then stimulate the
twinning-induced plasticity and transformation-induced plasticity
effects simultaneously [13]. Hence, alloying carbon doubles the
tensile strength at no expense of ductility. (3) Introducing 2.0 at.
% oxygen into the TiZrHfNb HEA leads to the formation of ordered
oxygen complexes [6]. The ordered oxygen complexes change the
dislocation shear mode from plane slip to wavy slip, promote dou-
ble cross-slip and thus cause dislocation multiplication through
the formation of Frank-Read sources. Accordingly, the tensile
strength and elongation of the (TiZrHfNb)
98O2 HEA are increased
by 49% and 95%, respectively, compared to those of the TiZrHfNb
base alloy. Therefore, achieving high strength and ductility in HEAs
is quite possible.
Interstitial nitrogen in HEAs produces a good strength-ductility
synergy. Nieh et al. [14] proposed that doping nitrogen not only
has a strong effect on lattice expansion but also enhances the
elastic modulus and the critical shear stress for incipient plastic-
ity. Alloying nitrogen could also be used for the accommodation
of partial dislocation-induced defects (i.e., extended stacking
faults),
e-martensites, deformation twins and phase transforma-
tions in Fe 50Mn25Cr15Co10 HEAs [15]. In the 1.6 at.% nitrogen-
doped Fe 50Mn25Cr15Co10 HEA, the yield strength is 602 MPa,
and the ductility is as high as 80%. Li et al. [16] reported that
the addition of nitrogen to Fe 50Mn30Co10Cr10 HEA facilitates the
formation of deformation twins, leading to the plastic deforma-
tion mode changing from transformation-induced plasticity to
twinning-induced plasticity. As a result, the strength and elonga-
tion of the nitrogen-doped Fe 50Mn30Co10Cr10 are simultaneously
increased by 156 MPa and 9.10%, respectively. Song et al. found
that the introduction of nitrogen and heterogeneous hierarchical
structures contribute to the exceptional combination of tensile
strength (853 MPa) and elongation (34%) in FeCoNiCr HEA [17].
Nitrogen could also exist in the form of nitrides in the HEA
matrix, which causes signiﬁcant embrittlement [18,19]. It should
be pointed out that most of the reported nitrogen-doped HEAs
are in the as-cast state. Few studies have concentrated on the
effect of nitrogen content and heat treatment on the microstruc-
tures and mechanical properties of HEAs.
TiZrNbTa HEA exhibits decent tensile strength and ductility
[20,21], and the solubilities of nitrogen in individual Ti, Zr, Nb
and Ta metals are relatively high (>5.0 at.%), as shown in the binary
phase diagrams (see Fig. S1 ). Here, we report a series of TiZrNbTa
HEAs doped with different contents of nitrogen (0.3–0.9 at.%).
The microstructures and mechanical behaviors of the nitrogen-
doped alloys were investigated. We realize a synchronized increase
in the strength and ductility of the TiZrNbTa HEA by doping 0.6 at.%
nitrogen. Interstitial solid solution strengthening contributes to the
enhanced strength, and the promoted planar dislocation slip inside
grains is conducive to the improved ductility. Importantly, there is
a slight increase in both strength and ductility in the 0.6 at.%
nitrogen-doped TiZrNbTa HEA after annealing at 1000 /C176C. The syn-
ergistic effects of solid solution strengthening, in situ grain reﬁne-
ment and dislocation cross slip provide high mechanical
performance. This work builds towards a new paradigm for design-
ing high strength and ductile HEAs.
2. Experimental methods
Ingots with nominal compositions of (TiZrNbTa) 100-xNx (x =0 ,
0.3, 0.6 and 0.9) were prepared by arc melting a mixture of the con-
stituent pure metals (purity > 99.9 wt%) and TiN (99.9 wt%, 50 nm
in diameter) in a Ti-gettered high-purity argon atmosphere. All the
arc melting parameters were similar and the ingots were remelted
at least eight times to ensure chemical homogeneity. The detailed
parameters were described in the Part 2 of Supplementary Mate-
rial. According to the nitrogen content, the as-cast nitrogen-
doped alloys are referred to as N0.3, N0.6 and N0.9 HEAs, respec-
tively. The as-cast N0.6 ingots were sealed in quartz tubes ﬁlled
with high-purity argon, annealed at 800 /C176C, 1000 /C176C and 1200 /C176C
for 24 h, and ﬁnally quenched in water. For simplicity, the N0.6
HEAs annealed at 800 /C176C, 1000 /C176C and 1200 /C176C are referred to as
N0.6–800, N0.6–1000 and N0.6–1200 HEAs, respectively. All the
samples for test were obtained from the concentric annulus area,
which was 14 mm away from the center, as shown in Fig. S2 .
The crystalline structures were determined by X-ray diffraction
(XRD; Rigaku UltimaIV). Microstructure characterization was per-
formed by scanning electron microscopy (SEM; TESCAN MIRA3)
equipped with an electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) system
as well as energy dispersive spectrometry (EDS) and transmission
electron microscopy (TEM, FEI Titan Cubled Themis G2 300).
Tensile tests on dog-bone-shaped specimens with a thickness of
1 mm, whose gauge length and width were 18 mm and 4 mm,
respectively, were conducted at room temperature with an Instron
8801 Tensile Tester at a strain rate of 10
/C0 3 s/C0 1. At least three tensile
measurements of each sample were taken to obtain the average
values of the mechanical properties.

4. Discussion
4.1. Effect of nitrogen on the microstructures of as-cast TiZrNbTa HEA
As mentioned above, all as-cast (TiZrNbTa) 100-xNx HEAs exhibit
a BCC solid solution structure. Such a small number of nitrogen
atoms can dissolve in the BCC lattices and occupy the interstitial
sites randomly. Thus, the lattice of the (TiZrNbTa) 100-xNx HEAs con-
tinuously expands with increasing nitrogen content, and the
diffraction peaks shift towards low angles, as shown in Fig. 1 .
However, the addition of nitrogen decreases the phase stability
of the TiZrNbTa system. First, the more nitrogen atoms dissolved,
the more pronounced lattice distortion was in the TiZrNbTa HEA,
as depicted by the increase in the FWHM of the diffraction peaks
(Fig. 1 b) and local misorientation ( Fig. 3 e). The increased lattice
distortion leads to a stronger strain ﬁeld and then decreases the
lattice stability. Second, the introduction of nitrogen changes
the atomic size difference d and Allen electronegativity
difference DvAllen of the alloy system. As shown in Table 3 ,i n
(TiZrNbTa)100-xNx alloys, when x /C21 0.6, d and DvAllen reach 7.00–
8.10% and 10.30–11.80%, respectively, which are both beyond the
single solid solution region, according to the proposed phase for-
mation rules for HEAs [11,33–35]. Finally, the intrinsic characteris-
tics of the components of the TiZrNbTa HEA promote the formation
of spinodal decomposition. Spinodal decomposition is a kind of
spontaneous exsolution decomposition of supersaturated solid
solutions through uphill component diffusion. In the TiZrNbTa
HEA, Ti/Zr and Ta/Nb come from the ⅣB and ⅤB groups, respec-
tively. Elements that belong to the same periodic group generally
have similar properties, including valence electron concentration
and crystal structure, as listed in Table S1. Thus, elements from
the same group are usually inﬁnitely miscible (mixing enthalpy
DHmix = 0), while those from different groups are ﬁnitely miscible
(DHmix > 0). As seen from DHmix of Ti, Zr, Nb, Ta and N (Table S2), a

4.2. Effect of nitrogen on the mechanical behaviors of as-cast TiZrNbTa
HEA
The differences among the microstructures of (TiZrNbTa) 100-xNx
HEAs induced by doping nitrogen contribute to the variation in the
mechanical properties.
The well-ﬁtted strengthening of the (TiZrNbTa) 100-xNx HEAs
with two solid solution strengthening models ( Fig. 5b) and the nor-
mal strengthening coefﬁcients demonstrate that the strengthening
in the current alloys is indeed interstitial. Namely, the solid solu-
tion strengthening induced by interstitial nitrogen atoms is the
dominant strengthening mechanism of (TiZrNbTa) 100-xNx HEAs.
However, the content of nitrogen dissolved in the BCC lattice is
lower than 0.9 at.% due to spinodal decomposition and grain
boundary segregation. Therefore, there must be other strengthen-
ing mechanisms that contribute to the yield strength increment
in the N0.9 HEA. Actually, the basket-weave fabrics and the precip-
itates would also retard dislocation motion and then lead to second
phase strengthening.
Generally, the enhancement of strength comes at the expense of
the ductility in most metals, referred to as the so-called strength-
ductility trade-off. However, this trade-off can be overcome in
(TiZrNbTa)100-xNx HEAs ( x /C20 0.6). For the base TiZrNbTa HEA, the
active dislocation motion around the grain boundaries leads to
limited ductility and intergranular fracture [20]. In the nitrogen-
doped TiZrNbTa HEAs, the distribution of interstitial nitrogen
atoms is homogeneous when the nitrogen content is lower than
0.6 at.%. On the one hand, alloying nitrogen would result in severe
lattice distortion and a large strain ﬁeld in solid solutions [43,44].
On the other hand, the binding enthalpy between nitrogen atoms
and dislocations is high [45]. Both scenarios would hinder disloca-
tion motion. Thus, doping with N could form many dispersed pin-
ning sites in the grain interior [46–48]. The pinning effect not only
increases the dislocation motion barrier but also stimulates dislo-
cation multiplication [7]. Moreover, such a pinning effect promotes
planar dislocation motion inside grain interiors, as displayed in
Fig. 6 b and Fig. 7 . The behaviour is similar to that observed in Ti-
O alloys [49]. Then, the inhomogeneous deformation near grain
boundaries is suppressed, which effectively alleviates strain local-
ization. Therefore, the ductility of (TiZrNbTa) 100-xNx HEAs increases
with nitrogen doping ( x /C20 0.6).
Overall, doping nitrogen surmounts the strength-ductility
trade-off in the TiZrNbTa HEA, giving the N0.6 HEA a good combi-
nation of strength and ductility, as indicated by the hollow red star
in Fig. 15. When the nitrogen content is 0.9 at.%, ordered nanopre-
cipitates ( Fig. 4 c-4 g and Fig. 6 d) and the basket-weave fabrics of
spinodal decomposition ( Fig. 4h-4j) appear in the grain boundaries
and matrix, respectively. Dislocations would pile up at the bound-
aries with high misorientation and distortion energy, resulting in
brittle intergranular fracture ( Fig. 6 c) and the cliff fall of ductility
in N0.9 HEA ( Fig. 5 a).

4.4. Effect of annealing temperature on the mechanical behaviors of
N0.6 HEA
Compared with the as-cast N0.6 HEA, the resistance for disloca-
tion slip from lattice distortion is reduced in N0.6–800 HEA due to
the dissolution of nitrogen atoms. Moreover, the precipitates
around the grain boundaries cannot effectively impede dislocation
movements, which mainly determine the yield strength. However,
grain boundary precipitates would result in dislocation pile-up and
stress concentration, which deteriorate the ductility. Hence, the
yield strength and elongation of N0.6–800 HEA are both lower than
those of as-cast N0.6 HEA.
When the annealing temperature rises to 1000 /C176C, submicron
coherent precipitates are dispersed in the matrix. Interstitial nitro-
gen atoms and coherent precipitates are beneﬁcial to the mechan-
ical properties. On the one hand, the solid solution strengthening
induced by interstitial nitrogen atoms, precipitation hardening
(Fig. 14 a), and grain boundary strengthening caused by in situ
grain reﬁnement ( Fig. 14 c) contributes to the enhancement of
strength. On the other hand, the coherent boundaries and lattice
distortion do not easily cause dislocation pile-ups and the resultant
stress concentration ( Fig. 14b). Owing to the relatively low misori-
entation of the coherent boundaries, dislocation cross slip prevails
(Fig. 14 d) [61,62]. Therefore, N0.6–1000 HEA also exhibits good
ductility ( /C24 18.8%) (see Fig. 12 ).
For N0.6–1200 HEA, its grain size (156.06 lm) is more than
twice that of N0.6–1000 HEA (67.44 lm). Such grain coarsening
signiﬁcantly deteriorates the strength and ductility of N0.6–1200
HEA [63,64]. Moreover, the precipitates inside the grain disappear
(Fig. 9f), but impurity segregation at the grain boundaries emerges
(Fig. 11c and 11d). Therefore, N0.6–1200 HEA exhibits remarkable
intergranular brittle fracture (see Fig. 13 c).
From the above analyses, we can obtain high strength and duc-
tility in N0.6 HEA by introducing coherent precipitates. The simul-
taneous increase in strength and ductility causes N0.6–1000 HEA
to have a good combination of mechanical properties, as indicated
by the brownish red star in Fig. 15 .
5. Conclusions
In the present study, we report a combination of high strength
and ductility in TiZrNbTa HEAs achieved by doping nitrogen and
subsequent annealing. The main conclusions could be obtained
as follows:
1. The as-cast (TiZrNbTa) 100-xNx (x = 0, 0.3, 0.6 and 0.9) HEAs are
all solid solutions with BCC structures. With increasing nitrogen
content, the phase stability decreases, and the driving force for ele-
mental separation is enhanced. Then, element segregation and
dendrites form with nitrogen doping. Spinodal decomposition
and grain boundary precipitation emerge in N0.9 HEA. The pro-
nounced lattice distortion induced by nitrogen doping leads to
the promoted sluggish diffusion effect and subsequent dislocation
pipe effect. For the as-cast (TiZrNbTa) 100-xNx HEAs, the grain size
ﬁrst decreases with increasing nitrogen content ( x /C20 0.6) and
increases with 0.9 at.% nitrogen doping.
2. The yield strength and elongation are simultaneously
increased by 18% and 32%, respectively, by doping 0.6 at.% nitrogen
in the as-cast TiZrNbTa HEA. Interstitial solid solution strengthen-
ing contributes to the enhanced strength. The improved ductility
originates from the promoted planar dislocation slip inside grains.
For N0.9 HEA, the formation of grain boundary precipitates leads to
poor ductility and brittle intergranular fracture.
3. Annealing would contribute to the destabilization of
(TiZrNbTa)
100-xNx HEAs. N0.6 HEA annealed at 800 /C176C exhibits dual
BCC phases and complex precipitates with various sizes and
shapes. With increasing annealing temperature, the solid solubility
of nitrogen atoms increases; submicron coherent precipitates are
dispersed in the N0.6–1000 HEA, and a single BCC phase appears
in the N0.6–1200 HEA.
4. The yield strength and ductility of N0.6–1000 HEA are
1238 MPa and 18.8%, respectively. The high strength stems from
interstitial strengthening, precipitation strengthening and grain
boundary strengthening. The high ductility is attributed to the pro-
moted cross slip. However, for N0.6–800 HEA, the formation of pre-
cipitates weakens the interstitial strengthening effect and leads to
stress concentration, which deteriorates the strength and ductility.
The intergranular brittle fracture of N0.6–1200 HEA contributes to
grain coarsening and grain boundary impurity segregation.
