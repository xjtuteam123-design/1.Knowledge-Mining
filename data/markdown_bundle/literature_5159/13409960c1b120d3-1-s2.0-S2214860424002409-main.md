ABSTRACT 
Intermetallic TiAl alloys have found applications in the aerospace and automotive fields. Nevertheless, to satisfy 
the increasingly complex environmental requirements, these lightweight alloys must be improved particularly in 
strength and ductility. Additive manufacturing (AM) exhibits a rapid solidification rate and may strengthen 
intermetallic TiAl alloys. In this study, an additively manufactured (AMed) Ti-48Al-8 Nb alloy with fine form -
ability was successfully fabricated by optimizing the power used during directed energy deposition. The pref -
erential crystal plane for the obtained alloy is {111} γ due to its lowest surface energy, and the presence of the 
thermal gradient during the AM process results in the alloy growing along the building direction. The TiAl alloy 
AMed at 500 W delivers an excellent tensile strength of 880 MPa that is 1.71 times higher than that of its as-cast 
counterpart, as well as elongation of 0.7% at room temperature. The superior properties of this alloy are due to 
the formation of numerous deformation twins during tensile deformation at room temperature that enables twin 
intersections to produce high-density nano-twins while contributing to stress release. Moreover, the AMed TiAl 
alloy exhibits outstanding high-temperature strength retention performance. 
1. Introduction 
Obtaining a balanced relationship between weight, ductility, and 
strength in metallic materials is a persistent goal of materials research. 
Because materials with low weight, high strength, and high ductility can 
enable truly astounding technological advances in a range of fields such 
as in the automotive and aerospace industries. The development of 
structural materials with suitable properties at elevated temperatures is 
crucial for core components, such as gas turbine wheels, turbine blades, 
and exhaust valves [1,2] . TiAl intermetallic alloys are advanced 
high-temperature (HT) structural materials that are attractive for 
various applications owing to their low density (~4.2 g/cm
3
), high 
specific strength and specific modulus, and superior oxidation and creep 
resistance [3,4] . With the increasing demand for HT operation, TiAl 
alloys are gradually replacing heavy Ni-based superalloys, providing a 
50% reduction of weight in turbine engines [5,6] . As the 
third-generation TiAl alloys, high-Nb TiAl alloys feature a higher yield 
strength and service temperature that satisfy next-generation aircraft 
engine requirements [7 – 9] . However, much improvement is still 
necessary to enable their large-scale industrial applications, and in 
particular, their inherently brittle nature and poor formability at room 
temperature (RT) are the main challenges that must be overcome to 
achieve their widespread use [10,11] . Fundamentally, techniques must 
be urgently developed to further strengthen TiAl-based alloys. 
TiAl alloys are usually fabricated via casting, and their inherent 
coarse lamellar microstructures limit their mechanical properties and 
subsequent processing [12] . Additive manufacturing (AM) is a highly 
prospective emerging technology to fabricate metal parts [13,14] that 
enables the production of parts with complex geometries from 
difficult-to-process metals [15] . Compared with conventional technol -
ogies, fine microstructures are always obtained in laser AM (LAM) due to 
the rapid solidification, resulting in enhanced mechanical properties of 
the components [16] . Furthermore, AM shows significant advantages by 
saving production time and avoiding material waste [17] . Directed en -
ergy deposition (DED) is an extensively developed AM technology that 
has been used to repair and remanufacture worn parts, fabricate special

2. Materials and methods 
The diameters of high Nb-TiAl alloy powders ranging from 40 to 120 
μ m were prepared using electrode induction melting gas atomization 
(EIGA) from a Ti-48Al-8 Nb ingot (Xi ’ an Sailong AM Technologies Co., 
Ltd). Firstly, the Ti-48Al-8 Nb ingot was placed in the atomization 
equipment and melted by induction through the electrode. Meanwhile, 
the current generated from the electrode produces a plasma on the TiAl 
alloy surface, and a high-speed flow of argon gas was directed through 
the plasma region. The TiAl alloy vapor is rapidly stretched and cooled 
to form micrometer-high Nb-TiAl alloy particles, as shown in Fig. 1 a. 
The chemical compositions of the prealloyed powders were conducted 
by using an inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometer (ICP-MS). As 
listed in Table 1 , the actual and nominal chemical composition had 
almost no difference. The powders were first vacuum-dried for 24 h in a 
150 
◦
C oven. 
The TiAl alloy single-track walls were fabricated in an Ar chamber by 
a back-and-forth scanning strategy, and using a DED system equipped 
with a Yb fiber laser. During the DED process, the pressure for Ar needed 
to be kept constant, the concentration of oxygen needed to be below 
50 ppm to prevent melt pool oxidation, and a Ti-6Al-4 V alloy needed to 
act as the deposition substrate. The DED parameters were as follows: LP 
of 400 – 1000 W, laser beam diameter at the melt pool of 3 mm, laser- 
scanning speed of 12 mm/s, powder feed rate of 1.2 r/min, and layer 
thickness of 0.5 mm. Moreover, to reveal the transient temperature field 
during the DED process, a single-track Ti-48Al-8 Nb alloy theoretical 
model with 40 layers was built. The model was simulated by FEM cal -
culations using ANSYS with Mesh SOLID70. The addition of alloys was 
simulated via the element birth and death technique. The assumptions 
for simplifying the calculations and the initial and boundary conditions 
were the same as in our previous study [34] .

some sub-micron pores caused by the Ar atmosphere used in the powder

3.4. Tensile properties and fracture analysis 
As shown in Figs. 2 and 3 , the α
2
/ γ or γ / γ lamellar are prone to form 
the orientation along the horizontal direction during prepared high Nb- 
TiAl via the DED process. When the loading direction is perpendicular to 
lamellar orientation, the normal stress is exerted between the lamellar 
during the tensile process, causing the crack to propagate along the 
lamellar interfaces. Once the crack is formed, it can spread rapidly and 
cause a fracture, which is the main reason for the relatively low ultimate 
strength along the BD. However, when the loading direction is parallel 
to the lamellar orientation, the normal stress exerted on the lamellar 
interfaces is lower, and the crack is difficult to propagate along the 
lamellar interfaces. When the applied stress is increased to a higher 
level, the cracks cross the α
2
/ γ or γ / γ lamellar structures. Since the in -
terfaces strongly block the propagation of cracks across lamellar, the 
fracture resistance is greatly improved. During this process, more slip 
and twin deformation systems are generated, which eventually have 
higher ultimate strength and strain. As confirmed by the study of the 
anisotropic tensile behavior of AMed Ti-47Al-2Cr-2 Nb by the DED 
process [49] . As shown in Fig. S1a , the tensile property along the hori -
zontal direction ( θ = 0
◦

the substrate) is superior to that of θ = 45
◦
and θ = 90
◦

above analysis, here, the specimen ’ s tensile direction parallel to the 
substrate was selected to study the influence of various LPs on tensile 
properties and fracture behavior. 
The RT tensile properties of the AMed Ti-48Al-8 Nb alloy were 
measured at various LPs, and the results are shown in Fig. 8 a. The tensile 
properties show a decreasing tendency as the LP increases from 500 W 
to 1000 W. However, the significant forming deficiencies of the TiAl 
alloy AMed at 400 W result in a failure to prepare tensile specimens 
( Fig. 1 g), and thus no stress-strain curve for the sample AMed at 400 W 
is presented in Fig. 8 a. The optimal ultimate tensile strength (UTS, 
880 MPa) and elongation ( δ , 0.7%) are obtained at 500 W, and the UTS 
of this sample is 1.71 times higher than that of the cast sample 
(515 MPa). The average RT tensile strength along the BD delivers a 
lower tensile strength of 755 MPa and no plasticity for the sample AMed 
at 500 W ( Fig. S1b ), which is in agreement with the previous report 
[49] . The Ti-48Al-8 Nb alloy AMed at 1000 W exhibits a poor tensile 
property owing to large grain size and larger lamellar spacing. By 
contrast, the UTS and δ for the alloy AMed at 500 W in our study are 
vastly superior to those of the alloys gained from traditional methods as

4.2. Mechanical behavior and deformation mechanisms 
Unlike in the TiAl alloys fabricated by other AM and conventional 
methods, the goal of simultaneously improving the tensile strength and 
RT plasticity is achieved in the DED-prepared Ti-48Al-8 Nb alloy 
( Fig. 8 b). The origin of the strength, plasticity, and damage tolerance as 
well as that of the differences in the properties between the alloys AMed 
at 500 and 1000 W was analyzed via SEM-BSE ( Fig. 11 ). The crack path 
of the sample at 500 W is tortuous and shows evidence of crack 
dispersion, deflection, and branching ( Fig. 11 a – d), suggesting a higher 
damage tolerance. For the crack dispersion, the stress spread is hindered 
by the fine lamellar colony, contributing to the reduction in the crack 
driving force. However, the presence of a few dispersed cracks at 
1000 W indicates that the crack fracture is more brittle and has no 
tearing feature ( Fig. 11 e), signifying its lower damage tolerance. Several 
scattered tiny cracks are distributed between the lamellae ( Fig. 11 f – h), 
suggesting that higher LP (1000 W) promotes the formation of the 
coarse lamellar colony and weakens interlamellar strength, which is 
consistent with the results presented in Fig. 8 g and h. The typical Hall- 
Petch relationship [54] indicates that a material with a smaller lamellar 
colony size is expected to show higher strength. For the AMed high 
Nb-TiAl alloy, a higher cooling rate plays a crucial role in refining the 
microstructure ( Fig. 4 a), so grain refinement is one of the reasons for the 
enhanced strength. Another origin of the strength enhancement is the 
released stress, which benefits from the smaller interlamellar spacing 
and the randomly oriented lamellar colony. Moreover, increased dam -
age tolerance further contributes to strength, which results from the 
augmented crack deflection and branching changing the crack propa -
gation path and increasing the effective length of crack propagation, 
finally assimilating more energy and delaying alloy fracture. 
Furthermore, EBSD and TEM experiments were performed to reveal 
the deformation-strengthening mechanism. The tensile fracture for the 
alloy AMed at 1000 W occurs in the elastic stage ( Fig. 8 a), and almost no 
deformation twins are generated in the lamellar colonies ( Fig. 12 a). By 
contrast, numerous γ twins form in the equiaxed γ phase and lamellar 
colony regions ( Fig. 12 b – d) because plastic deformation occurs at RT in 
the tensile sample obtained at 500 W. In particular, the twin structure 
exists in the γ lamellae and stops at the intersection of the α

phase and enable the easy formation of intrinsic twins in the lamellar 
colony [70] . The γ -TiAl phase exhibits a typical {111} < 110 >

Numerous slip lines appear in the equiaxed γ phase ( Fig. 13 a), 
conducting RT tension in the Ti-48Al-8 Nb AMed at 500 W; this suggests 
that the equiaxed γ phase plays a vital role in tuning the strength and 
plasticity during deformation. The slip bands form end-to-end in the 
bulk and lamella ( Fig. 13 a), indicating the continuity of slip during the 
tensile deformation, and contributing to continuous stress release and 
avoiding stress concentration. Importantly, the strain between the 
lamellar colony and equiaxed γ phases is extremely symmetrical during 
the RT tension. The deformation due to the slip system operation thus 
can be coordinated via the equiaxed γ grains among the lamellar col -
onies, preventing tensile fracture. Finally, the high Nb-TiAl alloy AMed 
at 500 W exhibits a relatively high RT elongation. Twin boundaries 
multiply in the equiaxed γ phase as the applied load is increased, 
forming localized high-density twins ( Fig. 13 b and c) and enhancing the 
applied load, thus delaying crack generation and strengthening the TiAl 
alloys. In summary, two deformation mechanisms emerge in the AMed 
Ti-48Al-8 Nb alloy, namely dislocation-induced slip and twin-induced 
twins. At the initial deformed stage, the dislocations require a small 
critical stress to be activated, and they play the main role in the high Nb- 
TiAl alloy deformation. The stacked dislocations induce stress concen -
tration to reach the critical stress of twins, thus generating twins [71] . 
Twins enable materials to undergo continued and stable plastic defor -
mation while the coherent twin boundaries hinder dislocation move -
ment and further strengthen the alloy. Thus, as described previously, the 
Ti-48Al-8 Nb alloy AMed at 500 W exhibits better plasticity owing to the 
synergistic effects of the slip and twins on the plastic deformation. 
The large dislocations present around the γ -nanotwin layer are 
trapped in the subgrains ( Fig. 13 d and e), implying that the dislocations 
moving toward the twins on both sides are hindered by the twin 
boundaries. Furthermore, the dislocations distributed in parallel are 
formed due to the dislocation-dislocation and dislocation-boundary 
stresses ( Fig. 13 e), facilitating increased dislocation storage capacity 
and reinforcement in the high Nb-TiAl alloy matrix. Typically, the twins 
generated in the deformation process can change several crystal orien -
tations and affect the dislocation movement [72] . Moreover, 
high-density twins can prevent dislocation movement while allowing 
dislocations to propagate and suppress their pile-up [73] . Hence, two 
possible results can be inferred from the twins interacting with dislo -
cations. On one hand, the changed crystal orientation is in favor of 
dislocation slip and enables dislocations to pass through the twins. The 
high-density coherent twin boundaries offer usable channels to dislo -
cation glide and accommodate vast dislocations [74] , eventually facili -
tating further plastic deformation and improved plasticity of the alloy. 
On the other hand, the twin-changed orientation hinders dislocation 
slip, impeding dislocation movement or inducing dislocation locks 
formed from the dislocation decomposition reaction, further hindering 
dislocation movement [75] . Meanwhile, numerous Shockley partial 
dislocations and SFs are generated in the γ phase, and they can act as 
effective heterogeneous nucleation sites for deformation twins. The 
entangled dislocations form a dislocation block and are conducive to 
increasing the strength of the TiAl alloy. In conclusion, the interaction of 
dislocations and twins plays a positive effect in the strengthening and 
toughening of the TiAl alloy while preventing high-stress concentration. 
Meanwhile, high-density nano twins exhibit superior thermal stability, 
suggesting that materials with such a microstructure can be operated at 
HTs [76] .

5. Conclusions 
In this study, a high Nb-containing TiAl alloy was successfully 
fabricated via the DED process using prealloyed Ti-48Al-8 Nb powders 
as the precursors, and the reason for how the LP affects the micro -
structure and mechanical properties of prepared alloys was revealed. 
The growth mechanism of the AMed TiAl alloy single-wall along {111} γ 
crystal plane was elucidated via a combination of FEM and DFT calcu -
lations. Meanwhile, the mechanical behavior and strengthening mech -
anisms of the AMed Ti-48Al-8 Nb alloy arising from twin evolution were 
demonstrated. The main conclusions are as follows: 
(1) The optimal LP for AMed Ti-48Al-8 Nb alloy single-wall is 500 W, 
enabling excellent formability. Insufficient energy input by a 
smaller LP (400 W) induces clear sunken defects on both single- 
wall sides, whereas the use of a large LP (1000 W) melts the 
powders excessively and causes bulges and collapse. 
(2) The AMed TiAl alloy always comprises γ and α
2 
phases regardless 
of the LP used in the deposition process. During the DED process, 
microstructures change from equiaxed to columnar structure 
along the BD as the increased LP gives rise to an increased heat 
flow gradient. Importantly, the {111} γ crystal plane in γ -TiAl 
exhibits the lowest DFT-calculated surface energy, and the pres -
ence of the thermal gradient enables it to grow along the BD. 
(3) A high Nb-containing TiAl alloy AMed at 500 W exhibits an 
excellent RT tensile strength of 880 MPa and elongation of 0.7%. 
The obtained strength is 1.71 times higher than that of the as-cast 
Ti-48Al-8 Nb alloy. Moreover, an excellent tensile strength of 
737 MPa and 594 MPa at 800 ℃ and 900 ℃ can be received in 
high Nb-TiAl alloy AMed at 500 W, respectively, outperforming 
the as-cast alloy and previously reported AMed and traditional 
methods prepared TiAl alloy. Most importantly, the DED high-Nb 
TiAl alloy AMed at 500 W delivers outstanding HT strength 
retention performances. 
(4) Tensile deformation enables multiscale twins of the γ -twin 
lamella, γ -twin subgrain, and γ -nanotwin layer to segment γ 
lamellae. The generated high-density nano twins and twin 
boundaries give rise to a boundary-strengthening effect to 
enhance the alloy strength and a stress-release effect to increase 
elongation. Finally, a high Nb-containing TiAl alloy AMed at 
500 W exhibits excellent mechanical properties at RT.
