Introduction
We suggested “refractory superalloys” (1) based on Ir or Rh with fcc and L1 2 two-phase coherent
structures. The refractory superalloys are potentially useful at ultra-high temperatures at which Ni-based
superalloys cannot be used. We have investigated the compression strength behavior and deformation
mechanism of Ir-based refractory superalloys (1– 4).
The microstructure and strength behavior of Ir-Nb and Ir-Zr alloys were investigated in our
preliminary results, which demonstrates that the precipitation hardening of the Ir-Zr alloy is higher than
that of the Ir-Nb alloy (5). Cuboidal precipitates and plate-like precipitates form in Ir-Nb and Ir-Zr
alloys respectively (6). In the Ir-Zr alloy, a semi-coherent structure with misfit dislocations at the
interface also exists in addition to the coherent structure. The precipitate shape difference is attributed
to the lattice misfit between the fcc matrix and the L1
2 precipitates. It is also shown that the deformation
of the Ir-Zr alloy progresses as a result of precipitates shearing (6). The deformation mode of the Ir-Nb
alloy is suggested as being a bypassing mechanism (7).
To be used as high-temperature materials, materials must have microstructure stability. When the
lattice misfit is large, the coherent interface has high coherent strain energy. To reduce interfacial
energy, misfit dislocations are induced at the interface. Thus, we sometimes observe a semi-coherent
structure in the Ir-Zr alloy. This shows that the coherent structure is unstable in alloys with a large
lattice misfit. If microstructure stability is low, this will make the creep property poor because, in creep
conditions, materials must stand up to at high temperature for long time. In this study, we investigated
the microstructure stability of the Ir-Zr alloy by heat treatment at high temperature. Precipitate shape
depended on the heat-treatment temperature. Creep behavior was investigated in Ir-Nb and Ir-Zr alloys
with different shape precipitates.
Experimental Procedure
Three kinds of alloys, the Ir-17at% Nb and Ir-12 and 15at% Zr alloys, were prepared as 30g button
ingots by arc melting in an argon atmosphere. According to the phase diagram, Ir-17at% Nb and
Ir-12at% Zr include 50% L1
2 precipitates in volume fraction (8). These compositions are located in the
fcc and L1 2 two-phase region. Cylindrical samples that were 3 mm in diameter and 6 mm high were
cut from Ir-15at% Zr alloys and heat-treated at temperatures between 1500 and 1900 °C for up to 24
hours to investigate microstructure stability. Before the creep test, a heat treatment at 1800°C for 24
hours was carried out for Ir-Nb alloys. Heat treatments at 1200 °C for 10 hours and at 1900°C for 4

Results
Microstructure Stability
In our previous study, a discontinuous coarsened lamellar structure (DCLS) was formed on grain
boundaries and grew into grains after fine plate-like precipitates are formed in the Ir-15at% Zr alloy
during heat treatment at 1200°C (4,5). In the DCLS, the lamellar spacing was about 2–5
mm, and each
phase was not strictly plate-shaped; however, the orientation relationship between the fcc and L1 2
phases was the same as in the coherent structure, that is, {111} fcc//{111}L12, [100]fcc//[100]L12. Thus,
the interface of the fcc and L1 2 phases was found to be coherent or semi-coherent at the atomic scale.
We distinguished the DCLS from the coherent structure made of plate-like precipitates because the
DCLS is formed after the coherent structure and replaces the coherent structure. The growth rate was
slow at 1200°C, and the DCLS grew about 10
mm from the grain boundary after a heat treatment for
168 hours (5). In this study, DCLS growth was investigated by several heat treatments. The growth area
of the DCLS (the bright contrast region around the grain boundary) was about 10 –20
mm after heat
treatment at 1500°C for 24 hours (Fig. 1a). However, at 1800°C, the DCLS area reached 20 mm after
one hour of heat treatment (Fig. 1b). After 24 hours of heat treatment at 1800°C, the entire sample area
was covered by the DCLS (Fig. 1c). At 1900°C, DCLS growth was fast, and the DCLS covered the
entire sample area after only 4 hours of heat treatment (Fig. 1d).
On the other hand, no great difference in macroscopic structure was observed in the Ir-Nb during
heat treatment at 1200°C and 1800°C (7). At 1800°C, cuboidal precipitates were still formed although
precipitate size increased from 100 nm at 1200°C to 300 nm at 1800°C (7).

Creep Behavior
Three kinds of samples, the Ir-17at% Nb with cuboidal precipitates heat-treated at 1800°C for 24 hours,
the Ir-12at% Zr with plate-like precipitates heat-treated at 1200°C for 10 hours, and the Ir-12at% Zr
alloy with the DCLS heat-treated at 1900°C for 4 hours, were chosen for the compression creep test.
The creep strain curve as a function of time is shown in Fig. 2. The alloys with cuboidal and plate-like
precipitates showed small instantaneous strain during the initial stage. After this instantaneous strain,
strain increased gradually at a constant rate. In other words, the slope of the creep curve was constant
during creep. On the other hand, the Ir-Zr alloy with the DCLS did not show instantaneous strain
clearly, and the strain increased gradually with increasing time. After 300 hours, the strain of the Ir-Nb
was about 1.2%. This was the smallest strain among the three samples. In the Ir-Zr alloys, the strain of

Discussion
Discontinuous coarsening shows that coherent plate-like precipitates are not stable above 1500°C as a
result of a large lattice misfit of the Ir-Zr alloy. Precipitate shape depends on interfacial and elastic
energies. It is well known that, in Ni-base superalloys, precipitates are surrounded by {100} planes with
low elastic energy although the interfacial plane with minimum energy is {111} in the fcc structure (9).
The lattice misfit also affects precipitate shape. When the lattice misfit is large, a twofold symmetric
shape (for example, plate) is preferable than a fourfold symmetric shape (for example, cube) to reduce
total energy (10). However, if the lattice misfit is large and there is no misfit dislocation at interface,
the coherency strain energy is high at the interface. Thus, a semi-coherent structure in which precipitates
are surrounded by considerable misfit dislocations is also formed. In addition to the semi-coherent
structure, discontinuous coarsening occurs, and coarsened lamellae grow into grains during heat
treatment. Similar behavior was observed in the Ti-Al alloy, in which a fine fully lamellar structure is
formed first, then discontinuous coarsening occurs at grain boundaries to reduce interfacial energy, and
finally the discontinuous coarsened lamellar structure covers whole sample (11). Coarsening of a
plate-like precipitate to a sphere is difficult; thus, discontinuous coarsening from the grain boundary
often occurs, and coarse lamellae finally replace fine lamellae or plate-like precipitates.
After the creep test at 1500°C for 300 hours, cuboidal precipitates kept their coherent shapes in the
Ir-Nb alloy, demonstrating that cuboidal precipitates are stable at 1500°C even under stress. On the
other hand, some of the coherent structures made of plate-like precipitates changed to the DCLS in the
Ir-Zr alloy. This microstructure stability gives the Ir-Nb alloy higher resistance for creep deformation
than Ir-Zr alloy, even plate-like precipitates attributed to have strong precipitation hardening in Ir-Zr
alloy. A coherent structure with a suitable lattice misfit is desirable to obtain both high precipitation
hardening and strong resistance to creep deformation.
The creep curve shows that instantaneous strain exists during the initial creep in the Ir-Nb and Ir-Zr
alloys with cuboidal or plate-like precipitates due to strain hardening. Strain hardening is more
significant with the decreasing lamellar spacing in the fully lamellar structure in Ti-Al because the
interface becomes an obstacle for the movement of dislocation in a lamellar structure (12). The fine
precipitates in Ir-Nb and Ir-Zr alloys play similar roles as the lamellar structure in a fully lamellar
structure in Ti-Al. Thus, many dislocations are introduced for the initial stage of creep. On the other
hand, creep strain increased gradually in the sample with DCLS. This shows that strain hardening does
not occur in the DCLS because coarse lamellar spacing is not an obstacle for the dislocation movement.
From the above discussion, the largest creep strain of the Ir-Zr alloy with the DCLS can be
explained. In the DCLS, dislocations can move in the coarse lamellae more easily than in other
microstructures. On the other hand, the microstructure of the Ir-Nb alloy was stable, and fine cuboidal
precipitates remained. Thus, the smallest creep strain was observed in the Ir-Nb alloy. The creep strain
of the Ir-Zr alloy with plate-like precipitates was almost equivalent to that of the Ir-Nb alloy until 150
hours. After 150 hours, however, the creep strain of Ir-Zr increased more than that of Ir-Nb as a result
of the growth of the DCLS. The area replaced by the DCLS can deform more easily than the area with
plate-like precipitates. After 150 hours, the DCLS became more effective.
Conclusion
The compression creep behavior of the Ir-Nb alloy with cuboidal precipitates, the Ir-Zr alloy with
plate-like precipitates, and the Ir-Zr alloy with a discontinuous coarsened lamellar structure (DCLS)
was investigated at 1500°C under 137MPa. Primary and secondary creep were observed during 300
hours. The largest strain about 3.2% was shown in the Ir-Zr with the DCLS. The smallest strain, of
