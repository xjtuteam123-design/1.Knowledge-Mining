EFFECT OF SILICIDE CERAMIC COATINGS
ON THE HIGH-TEMPERATURE STRENGTH AND DUCTILITY
OF NIOBIUM ALLOY OF THE N b–W–M o–Z r SYSTEM
V . V . Bukhanovskii1
Translated from Metallovedenie i Termicheskaya Obrabotka Metallov, No. 2, pp. 29 – 34, February, 2004.
The characteristics of short-term strength and ductility of niobium alloy 5VMTs of the Nb–W–M o–Z rs y s -
tem and a composite from alloy 5VMTs with a silicide ceramic coating are studied in vacuum, in an inert me-
dium, and in air within a temperature range of 290 – 2070 K. The kinetics of the nucleation and evolution of
damage in the protective coating and in the matrix is investigated. The values of ultimate plastic strain at
which the composite preserves the bearing capacity in high-temperature aggressive and oxidizing gas media
are determined.
INTRODUCTION
Successful use of niobium and its alloys in structures
operating in high-temperature oxidizing and gas media de-
pends considerably on the development of reliable protective
coatings. This class of material is primarily protected by re-
fractory coatings from ceramics based on molybdenum and
hafnium disilicides. The process of deposition of a coating
may substantially influence the structure of the material and
its mechanical properties [1 – 4].
We will present experimental data on the strength, ducti-
lity, and bearing capacity of a composite “niobium alloy
5VMTs + silicide ceramic coating” under short-term static
loading in a temperature range of 290 – 2070 K. The results
of the study were compared with the mechanical characteris-
tics of the matrix obtained for specimens cut from bars and
rolled sheets from alloy 5VMTs. We studied the effect of
heat treatment in a mode imitating the temperature cycle of
deposition of the coating on the strength and ductility of the
alloy.
METHODS OF STUDY
Alloy 5VMTs (grade 5V2MTs-2) of the N b–W–
Mo – Zr system belongs to low-alloy high-temperature nio -
bium alloys with solid solution hardening [1, 2]. Due to the
optimum content of alloying elements it possesses a unique
combination of physicomechanical and technological charac-
teristics and combines high refractoriness and low-tempera-
ture ductility with good weldability and mechanical pro-
cessibility. The alloy was fabricated by the method of va-
cuum arc melting with subsequent hot deformation for bars
60 mm in diameter and sheets 1–2m m thick. In the con -
cluding stage of fabrication the alloy was subjected to 2-h
recrystallization annealing at 1670 K. In the as-delivered
state the sheets were isotropic and their fine-grained structure
had homogeneous polyhedral grains 25 – 30/c109m in size [5].
The ceramic coating was formed on the functional part of
a specimen by double immersion into a slip suspension con -
sisting of 40% molybdenum and 60% silicide mixture bear -
ing 80% molybdenum silicides (MoSi
2 and Mo 5Si2 ) and
20% hafnium dicilicide HfSi 2 and subsequent low-tempera -
ture and high-temperature annealing and vacuum diffusion
siliconizing in silicon vapors at 1470 K for 15 h with an acti-
vator. The specimens were annealed for 30 min at 1670 K af-
ter the deposition of the first layer of slip and for 15 min at
2230 K after the second deposition of a silicide ceramic mix-
ture of the same composition. The thickness of the coating
was 100 – 120 /c109m.2

The kinetics of the nucleation and evolution of damage
in the composite was studied on flat specimens of a special
shape (Fig. 1). The silicide ceramic coating was removed
from one of the side surfaces of the functional part of these
specimens, and a polished section was prepared for metallo -

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
The mechanical properties of niobium alloy 5VMTs
and of the “5VMTs – silicide ceramic coating” compos-
ite are presented in Tables 1 and 2 and in Fig. 2 with al-
lowance for the statistical parameters of scattering of the
test data. The tables present the mechanical characteris-
tics of the alloy and of the composite obtained in vacuum and
in an inert medium for cylindrical specimens cut from bars
60 mm in diameter in the initial condition and after heat
treatment in the mode of deposition of the silicide ceramic
coating. Figure 2 presents the mechanical properties of alloy
5VMTs with silicide ceramic coating and without it for flat
specimens cut from sheets 1–2m m thick and tested in vac -
uum, in an inert medium, and in air.
The temperature dependences of the mechanical proper -
ties of alloy 5VMTs are characterized by nonmonotomic
variation of strength and ductility characteristics, which is
typical for most titanium alloys. The curves have extrema
within 770 – 1270 K due to the process of dynamic strain ag-
ing connected with blocking of the motion of dislocations by
interstitial impurity atoms [11]. The tensile diagrams of the
alloy in the given temperature range have a sawtooth nature
that indicates nonmonotonic evolution of plastic strain [5].
Note that the extrema are the most manifested for /c115
r and /c100u
and are virtually absent on the temperature dependences of
/c115pr (Fig. 2). Maximum scattering of mechanical characteris -
tics is observed for alloy 5VMTs at 1070 – 1520 K; mini -
mum scattering occurs at room temperature. The intensifica -
tion of the scattering of mechanical properties in this temper-
ature range is connected with the processes of dynamic strain
aging and with the thermally activated physicochemical
transformations in the alloy, in particular, with segregation of
particles of the second phase [2].

Heat treatment of alloy 5VMTs in the mode of deposition
of silicide ceramic coating softens the alloy by 5 – 10% at
290 – 1770 K and raises its strength characteristics by the
same value at higher temperatures. Simultaneously, the duc-
tility decreases by a factor of 1.3 – 2.0 in the entire range of
studied temperatures (see Table 1). The fracture behavior of
the alloy changes, especially in the high-temperature range,
from the ductile intragrain fracture in the state as delivered to
ductile intergrain fracture after three-stage high-temperature
annealing (see Fig. 3).
The effect of heat treatment on the mechanical properties
and fracture behavior of alloy 5VMTs can be explained by
the growth of grains to 250 – 350 /c109m due to the processes of
collective recrystallization and by the increase in the percent-
age of gaseous interstitial impurities that penetrate by diffu -
sion from the protective atmosphere of the furnace, espe -
cially in boundary zones of crystallites [2].
Comparing the characteristics of strength and ductility of
the alloy and of the composite we established the following.
As a rule, the strength characteristics of the composite
decrease relative to the material of the matrix by 10 – 40%
virtually in the whole of the studied temperature range. The
strength of coated specimens is lower that that of uncoated
specimens subjected to stage heat treatment in the mentioned
mode. However, at 1770 – 2020 K the strength characteris -
tics of the composite obtained on flat specimens are
15 – 20% higher on the average than the strength characteris-
tics of the sheet material.
Deposition of silicide ceramic coatings affects the ducti -
lity of the niobium alloy to an even greater degree. Spe -
cifically, the elongation of the composite tested in an inert
medium decreases by a factor of 1.5 – 2 relative to that of al-
loy 5VNTs; in air tests the elongation decreases by a factor
of 3 – 4. The uniform elongation /c100

capacity of the material for strain hardening, for alloy
5VMTs with silicide ceramic coating at 290 and 1270 K is
1.5 – 3.5 times lower than for the uncoated alloy, whereas at

The results of our experiments show that when the
5VMTs alloy is coated with a silicide ceramic coating, the
mechanical characteristics of the substrate are equally influ -
enced by the concurrent heat treatment and by the physico -
chemical processes in the diffusion zone, in particular, the
diffusion of silicon from the surface layers of the coating into
the metal [12].
The results of the study of damage of the “alloy 5VMTs –
silicide ceramic coating” composite due to high-temperature
extension show the following. In the initial condition, the
coating bears numerous pores of a round shape from 2.5 to
12 /c109m in size and individual cracks up to 25 /c109m long. The
pores are chiefly concentrated near the external surface of
the composite and are virtually absent on the side of the ma -
trix. As a rule, the matrix and the boundary zone have no
pores and cracks (see Fig. 4a). Under high-temperature load-
ing in the elastic region we did not discover noticeable
changes in the coating and in the matrix.
In the process of plastic deformation at 1770 K the
cracks present in the coating propagate and pass into the ma-
trix at a certain deformation stage; the formation and growth
of new cracks is accompanied by curing of old ones (see
Fig. 4b – d ). It should be noted that cracks might develop
from defects on the surface of the composite as well as from
defects lying in the depth of the coating. At some deforma -
tion stages they may open and go to the surface (Fig. 4 d )o r
be cured on the side of the external surface of the composite.
In the latter case a crack presents the highest danger, because
it is not visible on the surface of the part but can propagate
into the matrix.

In our opinion, the heat treatment concurrent to the pro-
cess of deposition of silicide ceramic coating and the high-
temperature plastic deformation of the composite in the pro-
cess of operation, especially in aggressive and oxidizing me-
dia, present substantial danger from the standpoint of embrit-
tlement of the material and lowering of its strength even for
the high-strength alloy like 5VMTs. In this connection, the
presence of cracks in the material composed of alloy 5VMTs
and silicide ceramic coating, which penetrate the matrix, is
an impermissible defect.
Such cracks occur in individual zones of the composite
even in the initial stage of elastoplastic loading. For example,
in zone E of a polished section (see Fig. 1b ) a hair crack ob-
served after plastic deformation with degree /c101= 0.7% pene-
trated into the matrix to a depth of 10 /c109m, though it was in -
visible on the external surface of the composite (see Fig. 5a).
In later loading stages it developed steadily both towards the
external surface of the silicide ceramic coating and towards
the matrix. After high-temperature deformation of the speci -
men to degree /c101= 1.3%, the crack penetrated into the matrix
by 25 /c109m. Its width was 5 /c109m in the coating and 2 – 2.5 /c109m
in the matrix. At the tip of the crack we observed a plastic
zone formed in the process of penetration of the crack into
the depth of the material. After deformation with /c101= 3.9%
the crack penetrated the material to another 5 /c109m and wid -
ened to 18 /c109m in the coating and to 7.5 /c109m in the matrix (see
Fig. 5b). The tip of the crack blunted and acquired a round
shape with radius 4 /c109m, which indicated the high relaxation
capacity of the niobium alloy.
At higher degrees of plastic deformation exceeding 5%
numerous cracks in the composite were accompanied by de -
tachment of the coating from the matrix. At a temperature of
2070 K, which is 50 K higher than the upper boundary of the
operating temperature range of the composite, the silicide ce-
ramic coating was partially fused.
The obtained data speak in favor of the suggested
method for studying damage of the composite. It makes it
possible to follow the kinetics of appearance and develop-
ment of fracture sources on one specimen and demonstrates
the degree of their danger in different stages of deformation.
In addition, the method shows the relation between damage
of the composite and the level of plastic strain, i.e., a quantity
used in engineering design. Specifically, it becomes obvious
that plastic strain /c101> 0.5% in parts and structural elements
fabricated from alloy 5VMTs with silicide ceramic coatings,
which serve under a high temperature, are impermissible.
The disadvantage of the method consists in its applicability
only in protective media and unsuitability for tests in oxidiz -
ing media.
CONCLUSIONS
1. Heat treatment concurrent to the process of deposition
of silicide ceramic coatings softens niobium alloy 5VMTs by
5 – 10% for temperatures ranging from room one to 1770 K
and raises its strength characteristics by the same value at
higher temperatures. The ductility of annealed alloy de -
creases by a factor of 1.3 – 2 in the entire studied tempera -
ture range.
2. Deposition of silicide ceramic protective coatings de -
creases the characteristics of short-term strength of alloy
5VMTs by 10 – 40% in virtually the whole of the studied
temperature range. The ductile properties of the composite
are 1.5 – 4 times lower than those of the matrix.
