Mathematical Model for High Temperature 
Properties of Cast Nickel Superalloys with high 
Content of Gamma Prime Phase 
A. Efimov 
This investigation relates to structural cast superalloys with 
improved oxidation resistance in the temperature range of 
1100-1200°C. It was tried to clarify the role of heavy metal 
elements on mechanical properties of an alloy in the system 
Ni-l7at% Al-3at% Cr-Mo-Ta-W. Solidus temperture, yield strength 
at 20"C, ultimate tensile strength at 1200°C of polycrystal alloys 
and creep-rupture life at 1000°C and 1200°C of single crystals 
(001) were investigated by means of experimental design and 
regression analysis techniques. It was determined that solidus 
temperature do not depend from Wcontent,yield strength at 20°C 
and ultimate tensile strength at 1200°C strongly depend onTa and 
W contents. The properties sharply increase with simultaneous 
adding of Ta+W. At 1200°C Mo content is a critical factor. The 
influence of alloying withTa and Won the creep resistance of single 
crystals was examined. With increase of test temperature depen- 
dence of creep resistance from refractory additives appears to 
increase, but interval of an optimum alloying narrows. 
1 Introduction 
By now a lot of experimental data has been collected and 
compiled on effects of ternary additions on yield strength of 
compaund Ni3AI both in singlecrystalline and polycrystal- 
line forms [l]. However, there have been only a few works 
on the creep properties of these materials [l, 21. At the 
same time there is a distinct group of cast nickel-base 
superalloys with 17-18 at% Al, whose mechanical proper- 
ties are mostly related with ones of monolithic Ni3Al phase. 
These alloys were created after a series of successive efforts 
to develop one phase Ni3Al-base material for elevated 
temperature structural applications. However successful 
were the alloys with lesser content of aluminium (17-18 
at%), but with heterogeneous y'ly structure.They include: 
NA-188 DS, NGTE-RAE tentative alloys [3],VKNA-4 and 
its derivatives [4] where y'-phase volume fraction being in 
exes of 80 ~01%. 
The alloys are used in turbine-vanes and some lower- 
stressed parts in hot sections of fourth generation gas 
turbines. One route to improve creep resistance above 
1100°C is a ballanced alloying by refractory metals substi- 
tuting for aluminium simultaneously with limiting or exep- 
tion of melting point supressing elements. 
2 Materials and Procedures 
The aim of the present work is to find statistical 
dependences of high temperature properties for Ni3Al-base 
alloys from refractory metals content. Firstly the role of 
Mo,Ta and Won solidus temperature and tensile strength of 
polycrystalline alloys was determined. Experimental 
design and regression analysis techniques [5] were used for 
approximations of the dependent variables of interest. 
Because the dependencies a priori are asumed to be of 
second order the fractional 33 factorial design with three 
levels for three alloying elements was used. In base 
composition containing 17 at% aluminium and 3 at% 
chromium the alloying elements limits varied so: 0,5-2,5 
%Mo, 0,5-2,5 %Ta and 0,5-2,5 %W. Table 1 lists matrix of 
selected plan of experiment, chemical composition of the 
alloys tested and mean values of solidus temperature, room 
temperture (RT) 0.2% yield- and 1200°C ultimate tensile 
strength (UTS). 
Alloys were induction melted and cast into ceramic 
molds. As-cast samples were annealed for five hours at 
1200 "C. Single crystal bars were manufactured from com- 
positions with nominal molybdenum content of 2 at% .The 
16mm dia and 160 mm long bars were directionally 
solidified at 10 mdmin by the withdrawal process with a 
seed crystal incorporated in the shell mold. Temperature 
gradient was 70 Klcm. Single crystal bars had orientations 
within 10" of (001). Solidus temperature data were 
obtained by differential thermal analysis at a heating rate of 
5 K/min. Standard round tensile specimens with gauge

For polycrystalline alloys Ni3(Al,X) it has been reported 
that the solid solution hardening rate dddx is mostly 
controlled by the lattice misfit [7]. Lattice parameter of the 
compound Ni3(Al,X) where X stands for the elements 
substituted for the aluminium depends liniarly from ternary 
addition at RTand 1000°C [8]. Dependences of yield stress 
shown in Fig. 2 a and b are linear for elements W and Mo, as 
well as in Fig. 3a for W and are well in accord with the 
lattice parameter change. However the effects of Mo on 
UTS at 1200 "C and Ta on RT yield stress is nonlinear. This 
may be connected with latent variables which are not 
present in the Eq. 2 and 3 but responsible for deviation of 
stoichiometry, amount of second y phase and for misfit. 
More work needs to be done in this direction.
