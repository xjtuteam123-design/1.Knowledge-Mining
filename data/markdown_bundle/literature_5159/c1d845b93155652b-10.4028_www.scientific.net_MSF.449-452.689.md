Abstract. A new high strength titanium alloy system with low cost alloyi ng elements, such as Al, 
Fe, has been recently developed. In present study the expensive V was replaced with Fe, and Si was 
added from 0 to 7.5wt.%. The effect of Fe and Si on the microstructur e and tensile properties of Ti-
6Al-4Fe-xSi (x=0, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75wt.%) alloys was investigated. The room and high 
temperature mechanical properties of Ti-6Al-4Fe alloys were bet ter than those of the Ti-6Al-4V. It 
was mainly due to the phase boundary strengthening at ambient and high tem perature. The strength 
and elongation of the developed alloys depended upon the Si contents. The Si el ements made the 
grain boundary and colony size fine, and increased the strength of the devel oped alloys by solid 
solution and precipitation hardening. The tensile strength variation with the Si contents at room 
temperature and 400oC, and at 450oC and 500oC showed a similar behavior, respectively. 
Introduction 
As the demand for cost reduction of titanium alloys increases, a c onsiderable number of studies 
have recently been made on design of low cost titanium alloy. One of the methods is to use 
inexpensive alloying elements instead of the expensive alloying elem ents such as V , Mo. Two 
alloys developed by Timet, low cost beta or LCB(6.8Mo-4.5Fe-1.5Al) and Ti62S (6Al-2Fe-0.1Si), 
are the examples[1,2]. The former alloy is designed to use low cost ferro-molybdenum to produce a 
near beta alloy capable of being aged to very high strengths. Ti62S, which is a substitute for Ti-6Al-
4V , includes Fe as the beta stabilizer. Silicon is generally added in titanium base alloys with a range 
from 0.08 to 0.5wt% to improve the high temperature mechanical propertie s and the oxidation 
resistance of the alloys[3,4]. The solubility of silicon in titani um is low; a maximum of 0.47wt% in 
alpha phase is normally quoted[5]. Solubility decreases with decrea sing temperature in alpha 
titanium. The solubility and the amount of beta stabilizer in the a lloy have influence on the 
structure and properties of silicon containing alloys. However, if mor e than 1wt%Si is added to the 
titanium alloy, the improvement of the mechanical properties is substantially reduced because of the 
coarse titanium silicides[6~8]. 
Ti-Al-Fe-Si alloy series have been recently developed through the pre liminary study for 
development of a new titanium alloy having low cost and high strength. Alt hough a lot of studies 
have been made on silicon effect in titanium alloys, little is known about the Si effect in Ti-Al-Fe-Si 
alloys[9,10]. The present study is to investigate the tensile properties and microstructures associated 
with Si content in Ti-Al-Fe-Si alloys.

 Experimental Procedures 
A series of quaternary alloys based on the ternary composition Ti-6A l-4Fe (wt.%), in which 
silicon was substituted for titanium at levels up to 0.75wt%, has bee n examined. The master alloys 
of the Ti-6Al-4Fe-xSi were melted and cast into 3kg ingots with a size of 50mm in diameter and 
150mm in length using ISM(Induction Skull Melting) furnace, followed by hot is ostatic pressing at 
900°C, 100MPa for 2 hours. Specimens were etched using a Kroll solution for examination by 
optical microscopy. For the evaluation of mechanical properties, tensile test was carried out for each 
alloy at R.T., 400
 
, 450
 
, 500
 
, respectively. The gage section size of the test specimens w as 
commonly 6.25mm in diameter and 25mm in gage length. Precipitates and fracture surfaces were 
observed using a scanning electron microscope (SEM) after the test s. Ti-6Al-4V alloy was also 
prepared by the same method to compare the properties of Ti-6Al-4Fe-xSi alloys.

shown in Fig. 4. The tensile strengths increased linearly up to 400

silicon. At 450 °C and at 500 °C, however, the ultimate tensile strengths increased with silic on 
content up to 0.25wt% and then maintained almost constant in silicon content over 0.25%. The 
elongation of the alloy showed slight decrease at the elevated tempe rature. It is believed that the 
interfaces between matrix and the precipitates worked as the dis location sinks at higher than 450
 
. 
The elongation decreased suddenly when silicon levels were above 0.25wt%. The tensile strengths 
of the alloys were superior to those of the Ti-6Al-4V at room and el evated temperatures up to 
500°C. The tensile strength of the Ti-6Al-4Fe, not containing Si, was higher than that of the Ti-6Al-
4V alloy at room and elevated temperature. It was due to the / phase boundary, which was 
increased by the strong  stabilizer Fe. The increase in the tensile strength of the s ilicon bearing 
alloys is dominantly attributed to the microstructural modification by the Si solid solution at the 
range from 0 to 0.25 Si, and the microstructural modification and the titanium silicide precipitates at 
the range from 0.25 to 0.5 Si.

Conclusion 
Tensile properties of Ti-6Al-4Fe-xSi alloy were superior to those of Ti-6Al-4V . As the silicon 
was added to the Ti-Al-Fe-Si system alloys, the grain size be came refined. The Si solid solution is 
more effective in refining the grain size than the precipitates. The dominant strengthening 
mechanism of the Si-bearing alloys was the microstructural modifi cation by the Si solid solution at 
the range from 0 to 0.25wt%, and the microstructural modification and t he precipitation 
strengthening at the range from 0.25 to 0.75wt%. Coarse silicides in T i-Al-Fe-Si alloys containing 
over 0.75wt.% Si had a harmful effect on mechanical properties.
