ABSTRACT
Powder Plasma Arc Additive Manufacturing (PPA-AM) technique has tremendous potential for the practical
application in medium-entropy alloys (MEAs). In this study, we investigated the effect of heat treatment on
microstructural evolution, mechanical properties, and the work hardening behavior in the PPA-AM processed
(CoCrNi)
90
Al
5
Ti
5
MEA. The results show that the As-built specimen is a single-phase FCC structure, displaying
< 001 > strong FCC texture in the deposition direction. The stacking faults (SFs) and dislocations were observed
in the alloy, which formed the stacking fault networks and the Lomer-Cottrell locks structure. The plasticity of
the alloy increased significantly after the high-temperature heat treatment, which can be attributed to the
modifications of the microstructure, such as the weakening of the texture strength and the reduction of the
stresses. Low-temperature heat treatment decreases the density of dislocations in the alloy, but promotes the
generation of the co-lattice phase. The evolution of the dislocation density, texture strength, and precipitates
significantly influenced the strain hardening behavior and mechanical properties. The tensile results showed that
the strength and plasticity of the samples were increased after two-step heat treatment. The yield strength, ul-
timate tensile strength, and elongation to failure were 733 MPa, 1080 MPa, and 22.3%, respectively, which were
16.2%, 20.3%, and 15.5% higher than the As-built samples. This work elucidates the underlying mechanisms of
heat treatment on the microstructure and mechanical properties of the alloy.
1. Introduction
Compared to conventional alloys with single or double primary el-
ements, high-entropy alloys (HEAs) and medium-entropy alloys (MEAs)
have broken the traditional alloy design strategies through mixing
multiple principal components [ 1 – 4 ]. Such unique compositions lead to
high conformational entropy of the alloys, which tend to form simple
solid solution phases, such as face-centered cubic (FCC), body-centered
cubic (BCC) and hexagonal-close-packed (HCP), rather than complex
metallic compounds [ 5 , 6 ]. Apart from the high entropy effect, the
inherent properties of HEAs/MEAs are lattice distortion, slow diffusion
and cocktail effects [ 7 , 8 ]. Those four effects make HEAs/MEAs tend to
have excellent properties such as high strength [ 9 , 10 ], superior wear
resistance [ 11 , 12 ] and excellent corrosion resistance [ 13 , 14 ]. However,
for face-centered-cubic (FCC) single-phase HEAs/MEAs such as CoCrNi
MEA, they have excessive ductility and low strength, which limit their
practical engineering applications.
Currently, vacuum arc melting and casting are the common methods
to prepare bulk samples of HEAs/MEAs [ 15 , 16 ]. Yoshida et al. studied
the Hall-Petch relationship and friction stresses of CoCrNi MEA [ 17 ].
Agustianingrum et al. investigated the effect of Al on the microstructure
and mechanical properties of the CoCrNi MEA by cold rolling [ 18 ].
However, these samples are often difficult to apply directly to engi-
neering practice owing to metallic elemental segregation and sample
size limitations. Additive manufacturing (AM) technology has been
rapidly developed to offer a novel processing option for the preparation
of bulk HEAs/MEAs. AM is a direct forming manufacturing method by
stacking melted raw materials layer by layer under Computer-aided
design (CAD). The main technology includes Selective Laser Melting
(SLM) [ 19 , 20 ], Laser-Direct Energy Deposition (L-DED) [ 21 , 22 ], Selec-
tive Electron Beam Melting (SEBM) [ 23 ] and Powder Plasma Arc Ad-
ditive Manufacturing (PPA-AM) [ 24 , 25 ]. Among them, the preparation
of HEAs/MEAs by using PPA-AM has attracted widespread attention
from academia and industry owing to the advantages of high deposition

study have an excellent combination of strength and plasticity, which is
significant for the preparation of bulk MEAs by PPA-AM technique and
promotes the MEAs in engineering applications.
2. Material and methods
2.1. PPA-AM process and subsequent heat treatments
In this work, gas atomized Co, Cr, Ni, Al and Ti elemental powders
( > 99.5% purity) with particle sizes in the range of 53 – 150 μ m were
used. To improve the homogeneity of the metal powders, the weight
ratio of powders to Al
2

3

ball mill (YXQM12L, MITR Ltd, Hunan, China) with the speed of 100 r/
min for 8 h, subsequently vacuum dried in an oven for 100 min. When
blending, enough argon gas should be filled into the grinding tank as
blending protective gas to avoid oxidization of the metal powders. The
MEA specimens were printed using an PPA-AM equipment (DML-
VO3AD, Duomu Company, Shanghai, China), as shown in Fig. 1 (a). The
following processing parameters were used: deposition current 110 A,
scanning speed 25 mm/min, interlayer waiting time 4 min, torch to
sample distance 10 – 12 mm, powder feeding gas (high purity Ar) flow
2.5 L/min and powder feeding rotor speed 20 r/min.
As shown in Fig. 1 (b), the first group of experimental samples were

3.1. Microstructures
Fig. 2 shows the XRD patterns and lattice parameters of the FCC
phase for as-built and different heat treatment states. According to the
XRD patterns ( Fig. 2 (a)), all the MEA samples showed the FCC phase
structure. However, no significant second-phase diffraction peaks were
observed in the heat-treated MEAs, which could be attributed to the
coherence of the second phase with the FCC matrix or the low propor-
tion of the second phase [ 33 ]. Fig. 2 (b) shows the enlarged view of the
FCC

changed the Bragg diffraction peak angle of the MEAs. By Bragg ’ s law

AT-700 are 3.5828 Å, 3.5785 Å, 3.5796 Å and 3.5818 Å, respectively

substantial residual stresses in the MEA samples, and the
high-temperature heat treatment alleviates such defects, causing the
decrease of the lattice parameter [ 29 , 34 ]. In addition, the lower tem-
perature heat treatment promoted the formation of second-phase
nanoparticles in the FCC matrix, which also decreased the lattice
parameter of MEAs [ 35 , 36 ].
Fig. 3 shows the IPF maps for the As-built and heat-treated MEA
samples, which were detected in the YOZ plane. The IPF maps show that
all MEAs have columnar FCC crystals along < 001 > . It is due to the layer-
by-layer deposition characteristics of the AM process, which has the
largest thermal gradient in the deposition direction. And the FCC phase
tends to form columnar crystals along the direction of the highest tem-
perature [ 36 , 37 ]. In addition, to further understand the texture features
for the alloys, the pole figures (PFs) and inverse pole figures (IPFs) are
shown in Fig. 4 . It is clear that all the MEAs have a strong {100} < 001 >
texture, but the MEAs exhibit different texture strength. For As-built
samples the texture strength is remarkably stronger than the HT-1200
samples, and lower than AT-700 and HT-AT. The high-temperature
heat treatment reduced microstructural defects and induced grain re-
covery in MEAs, making the microstructure more uniform and weak-
ening the textural strength. Meanwhile, the lower annealing heat
treatment promoted the coherent ordered L1

The TEM maps for As-built and heat-treated samples are shown in
Fig. 5 . As shown in Fig. 5 (a – c), the as-built sample has a large number of
stacking faults (SFs) and dislocations. Interestingly, the stacking fault
network and Lomer-Cottrell lock structures were formed between the
two slipping planes in the alloy. In addition, selected area electron
diffraction (SAED) showed that the only FCC phase in the As-built
specimen and no second phase was generated, which was consistent
with the XRD data. PPA-AM has the largest thermal gradient in the BD,
and the layer-by-layer deposition undergoes repeated thermal cycling

residual stresses and some lattice defects in the alloy [ 34 ]. The

dislocations and SFs, as well as the Lomer-Cottrell lock structure [ 3 , 4 ,
40 ]. In addition, the rapid cooling of the AM process prevents the for-
mation of the second phase in the alloy [ 33 , 41 ]. Through twice heat
treatments, the HT-AT produced annealing twins, and the dislocations
and SFs were significantly reduced as shown in Fig. 5 (e and f). It is
shown that heat treatment has a tremendous impact on the micro-
structure of the alloys. To better describe the changes of microstructure
during heat treatment, the HT-1200 and AT-700 specimens were char-

3.3. Strain hardening behavior
The heat treatment can effectively change the microstructure of the
alloys (e.g., dislocations, precipitated phases), which improves the
strength and plasticity of the alloys. To further elaborate the effect of
heat treatment on the mechanical properties of the alloys, the work-
hardening curve was plotted as in Fig. 7 . The work-hardening
behavior of the alloys can be divided into three stages. The strain
hardening rate of the alloys can be roughly divided into three stages. In
Stage I, the elastoplastic transition stage, the work-hardening rate of
alloys decreases with increasing strain. During Stage II, the hardening
stage, the alloys have a sustained strain hardening rate; In Stage III, the
strain hardening rate decreases again with true stress values beyond
UTS.
The microstructural transformation diagrams of the alloys are
demonstrated as shown in Fig. 8 . In stage I, the strain hardening rates of
the MEAs all show the decreasing trend, but the drop rate is different for
each alloy. Among them, the strain hardening rates of As-built, HT-AT
and HT-1200 decreased slowly, which could be attributed to the high
dislocation densities within the MEAs, and these pre-existing storage
dislocations and stacking faults hindered the dislocation slip during the
tensile processes and effectively slowed down the decreasing trend of
the alloys [ 43 , 44 ]. In contrast, the precipitated phase generated in
AT-700 promoted crack extension during tensile processes, leading to a
rapid decrease in stage I [ 45 ]. The grain misorientation and size distri-
bution of the alloys are shown in Fig. 9 . For stage II, the change of strain
hardening rates decreases and the alloys show sustained strain hard-
ening behavior. Among them, the work-hardening curves of As-built,
HT-AT and AT-700 samples show secondary hardening, while

4. Conclusion
In this work, the effect of heat treatment on the microstructure and
mechanical properties of (CoCrNi)
90
Al
5
Ti
5
MEAs fabricated by powder
plasma arc additive manufacturing (PPA-AM) was investigated. The
main conclusions are summarized as follows.
(1) The unheat-treated As-built specimen is the single-phase FCC
structure with a large number of SFs and dislocations. In addition,
the stacking fault network and Lomer-Cottrell lock structures
were formed. After the appropriate heat treatment process, the
second precipitation phase was generated in the AT-700 alloy,
which effectively improved the strength of the alloy. The high
temperature heat treatment can effectively modify the structural
defects caused by the AM process and enhance the plasticity of
the alloys.
(2) The alloys show the {100} < 001 > strong FCC texture in the
deposition direction, but the texture strength differs among the
alloys. The high-temperature heat treatment effectively alleviates
the structure defects caused by additive manufacturing and
weaken the texture strength; the low-temperature heat treatment
promotes the generation of the co-lattice phase, which enhances
the texture strength of the alloys.
(3) Compared with the As-built samples, the strength and plasticity
of the HT-AT samples were improved. The yield strength, ulti-
mate tensile strength, and elongation to failure were 733 MPa
1080 MPa, and 22.3%, which were increased by 16.2%, 20.3%,
and 15.5%, respectively.
