ABSTRACT 
Inherent strength-plasticity tradeoff and unfavorable anti-wear capacity of FCC-structured high-entropy alloys 
(HEAs) bottlenecks their applications in advanced industries. This work controllably deployed an enhanced 
heterostructure and graded grain distribution in the single FCC-phase HEA model according to the concept of 
selective solid solution, which contributed to the ultrahigh compressive strength at appreciable plasticity levels 
for allowing worn surface to accommodate tribological loading. Simultaneously, the multiscale FCC-coupled 
architecture with compositional fluctuation effectively inhibits high temperature softening and activates the 
in-situ tribochemistry protection of the duplex glaze-layer and recrystallized subsurface, reaching low wear 
between room-temperature and 800 
◦
C. Our alloying strategy offers a novel avenue for further governing 
mechanical-tribological combined properties in applicable metallic systems. 
1. Introduction 
Enhancing the mechanical and tribological properties of advanced 
tribo-systems is an imperative demand in various modern industrial 
applications, such as power stations, transportation sectors and aero -
nautic systems [1 – 3] . In general, strong and deformable materials are 
desirable for reducing the contact area/stress and providing homoge -
neous plastic deformation during the low-to-moderate temperature 
wear process, contributing to increased anti-wear capacity [1,4] . What 
is more, at elevated temperatures, the surface exposed to wear tends to 
be abraded and delaminated rapidly due to thermal softening and ad -
hesive transfer between the tribo-pairs [5,6] . Under such circumstances, 
the wear-resistance of materials can be facilitated when introducing 
favorable tribo-oxidation to generate reactive interfacial protection, e. 
g., by forming a compacted protective glaze [3,7] . However, intrinsic 
strength-plasticity tradeoff and challenges for extremely manipulating 
constituents to optimize wear response still exist for conventional 
metallic materials based on single-principal-element alloying concept. 
Consequently, the design of high-performance components is hampered 
by precisely organizing the composition and structure of materials to 
accommodate such a variable and extreme environmental interplay. 
Recently, the emergence of high/medium-entropy alloys (HEAs/ 
MEAs), which contain multicomponent solid-solutions instead of com -
plex intermetallics, has been considered to have the potential to over -
come the aforementioned dilemma [8] . In particular, the single-phase 
Co-Cr-(Fe)-Ni system tuned by alloying is proved promising for 
approaching the optimum combination of mechanical-tribological 
properties [9,10] . This alloy system is developed by configuring a ho -
mogenous FCC matrix phase that responsible for plasticity and 
work-hardening with coherent heterostructures as reinforced phases to 
hinder global deformation [11] . Moreover, the peculiar “ cocktail effect ” 
of the HEAs provides a broad compositional space for its alloy design 
[12] . The specific solutal effect for controlling tribochemical behavior 
can effectively accelerate the formation of the glaze-layer, obtaining a 
low wear level [9,13] . For instance, Verma et al. [10] reported that Cu 
aggregates at grain boundaries (GBs) of the CoCrFeNi system and acts 
twofold, by inducing grain refinement to increase the microhardness 
and promoting the self-organization of lubricating CuO-glaze to alle -
viate wear between room-temperature (RT) and 600 
◦
C. Our recent 
investigation [5] found that a proper (Al, Ti)-rich heterostructure not 
only contributes to an excellent strength-plasticity balance relative to 
the Co-Cr-Ni MEA without a heterostructure but also to the densification

2. Experimental procedures 
The bulk AC and AT were produced by mechanical alloying (MA, 
FRITSCH-P5) combined with spark plasma sintering (SPS, LABOX- 
3010KF). The initial HEA powders were synthesized by MA between 
elemental powders (purity > 99.9%, particle size < 45 µ m), and the 
entire process was operated utilizing a high-energy ball-milling appa -
ratus protected by argon at 250 r/min. Thirty-hour dry milling was used 
to achieve a pre-solid solution between the elements, while six-hour wet 
milling with the control agent (ethanol) acted as the grain-refining 
process. The obtained powders were consolidated by SPS at 1100 
◦
C 
under a preset pressure of 40 MPa in vacuum. Crystal structures of tested 
HEAs were illustrated by XRD measurements (EMPYREAN, 
Netherlands). Scanning electron microscopy (SEM, ZEISS-Sigma-300) 
with energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) and a backscatter electron 
(BSE) detector were performed for microstructural and compositional 
identification. The grain morphology and orientation distribution were 
investigated via electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD, Nordly-Max3) 
equipped with Oxford-Channel5 collection software. The microstruc -
ture and phase composition of the as-fabricated HEAs were further 
examined using a transmission electron microscopy (TEM, JEM-F200) 
coupled with selected area electron diffraction (SAED).

3.2. Influence of solutal effect on mechanical properties 
Fig. 3 a,b presents the compressive properties of our designed HEAs 
compared with those of other high-performance HEAs ( Table S2 for 
detailed information). Significantly, AC and AT HEAs exhibit a 
remarkable strength-plasticity balance of 1805 MPa × 24% and 
2314 MPa × 13% (yield-strength × strain-to-failure), respectively. 
Distinctly, although our HEAs comprise a single-FCC phase with heter -
ostructures, their strength levels not only surpass those of other single- 
FCC structured HEAs [30,31] but also surpass those of strengthened 
HEAs with unique microstructures, including dual/multiphase HEAs 
[25,32 – 34] , refractory HEAs [35] , eutectic HEAs [36,37] and nano -
crystalline HEAs [20] . To emphasize the effects of compositional 
fluctuations and heterogeneous configurations of crystallographic 
plastic-strains due to selective solid solution on the strength enhance -
ment, various strengthening mechanisms are considered. First, the 
addition of Cu/Ti solute atoms contributes to solid-solution hardening. 
Since the atomic size of Cu/Ti (especially Ti) is larger than that of matrix 
elements (Co-Cr-Ni), the symmetric compressive stress field would in -
crease at the substitution site when the solute atoms were dissolved, 
which could cause lattice distortion and local compositional fluctuation 
that hinder dislocation movement [23,35,38] . Furthermore, the elec -
tronegativity difference between Ti atoms and matrix elements boosts 
the interaction energy between fluctuating atomic-level pressure and 
dislocation, thereby further intensifying the resultant solid-solution 
hardening [8] . Second, Al/Cr-rich oxidic precipitates provide the ma -
trix a strong bond strength and generate extra precipitation strength -
ening, which alleviates the stress concentration during elastic 
deformation depending on the Orowan bypass model [5] . Third, the 
graded grain induces the dynamic evolution of inhomogeneous defor -
mation and strain gradient during loading, resulting in 
dislocation/back-stress strengthening and conspicuous work-hardening 
capacity [26,39] . Because of the bimodal grain size distribution, the 
yield strength of grains accordingly exhibits a graded distribution. Fine 
grains begin to deform plastically, while ultrafine grains remain elastic, 
causing deformation incompatibility that requires the storage of kine -
matic GNDs at the hetero-interfaces. Subsequently, the accumulated 
GNDs and hierarchical plastic-strain can substantially strengthen the 
relatively soft fine grains through two types of mechanisms: severe
