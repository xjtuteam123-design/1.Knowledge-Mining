abstract
A glassy phase with glass transition (GT) was formed in a wide B content range of 12 e31 at%
for (Co0.75Cr0.125Mo0.125)100-xBx alloys. The appearance of GT at the low metalloid content of
12%B among Co-, Fe- and Ni-based alloys is the ﬁrst, in contrast to the absence of GT for
(Fe
0.75Cr0.125Mo0.125)88B12 and (Ni0.75Cr0.125Mo0.125)88B12 alloys. The Co-based 12%B glassy alloy
crystallized through unique three stages, i.e., glass (G) / [G þ hcp þ fcc] / [G þ fcc] /
[fcc þ M23B6]. The appearance of GT is due to the necessity of long-range rearrangement of
constituent elements for the simultaneous precipitation of hcp and fcc phases from glassy
phase. The glass transition temperature, crystallization temperature, Vickers hardness (Hv)
and Young 's modulus for the Co-based glassy alloys increase linearly with increasing B
content and reach 904 K, 946 K, 1566 and 188 GPa, respectively, at 31%B. The good bending
plasticity is retained up to 29%B. The crystallization of the 27 e29%B alloy occurs through
G / [G þ M
23B6] / [M23B6 þ M2B] and the ultrahigh Hv above 2000 is attained for the
metastable [G þ M23B6] phase state. The glassy alloy rod with a diameter of 1 mm was
prepared for the 27%B alloy and exhibited ultrahigh yield strength of 4700 MPa, plastic strain
of 1% and high oxidation resistance up to about 1200 K. The Co-based bulk glassy alloy with
simultaneously high strength, ultrahigh hardness and high oxidation resistance, is prom-
ising for the future high hardness and heat-resistance materials.

1. Introduction
Academic and engineering attention to multicomponent alloys
has been increasing steadily for the last several decades since
the ﬁrst success of forming a bulk glassy alloy in a multicom-
ponent system by copper mold casting in 1990 [ 1‒3]. It is well
known that the multicomponent metastable alloys were
formed in the order of cast bulk metallic glasses (BMGs) [ 1‒3],
BMG with equiatomic ﬁve components [ 4], melt-spun fcc solid
solution alloy ribbon with equiatomic ﬁve components and
sputtered deposited bcc thin ﬁlms with equiatomic or near
equiatomic ﬁve components named with high entropy alloy
(HEA) [ 5,6], and then cast [HE-BMG þ crystalline] composite
alloy [ 7]. Recently, there are a number of experimental and
calculated data on the structure, thermal stability, mechanical
properties and corrosion resistance for HE-BMGs [8‒12]a n dH E
crystalline (fcc, bcc, fccþ bcc, hcp) alloys [13e16]. When we pay
attention to ultrahigh yield strength and hardness, little is
known about the ultrahigh strength materials with yield
strength above 4500 MPa. It is known that Zr-based BMGs in
ZreAleNieCu base system have gained wide application ﬁelds
[17,18]. Considering the further wide application of BMGs, it is
important to develop an ultrahigh strength material in Fe-, Co-
and Ni-based alloys. It has been reported that ultrahigh yield
strength above 5 GPa is obtained for BMGs in Co eFeeTaeB
system and their strength is the highest among all kinds of
BMGs in conventional metallic base system at that time [ 19].
Much later, Co eTaeB BMGs with ultrahigh yield strength of
5190e5630 MPa and distinct plastic strain of 0.5e1.5% were also
synthesized [20]. Subsequently, a series of BMGs with ultrahigh
strength has been reported successively for the last ﬁve years,
e.g., IreNieTae(B) [21], OseCoeTaeB[ 22] and CoeTaeBeSi [23].
As we know, there has been a signiﬁcantly increasing demand
on precise die press mold which can be used in the warm
pressing treatment for BMGs. The BMG with ultrahigh strength,
high glass transition temperature ( T
g), high crystallization
temperature ( Tx) and good oxidation resistance is a good
candidate material. However, the above-described new alloys
always contain Ta element, which will result in ultrahigh
melting temperature and high cost, and prevent the practical
use of the BMGs as precise die press mold as well as high
strength and high wear resistant materials. The recent syn-
thesis of a different type refractory Mo eCoeB BMGs with high
thermal stability shows the possibility of developing Ta-free
BMGs [ 24]. If we can synthesize a new BMG with the similar
ultrahigh level strength in Ta-free Fe-, Co- or Ni-based alloy
system, the new ultrahigh strength BMG is expected to be used
widely as a novel precise die press mold and high-elevated
temperature strength materials.
We have studied a series of multicomponent type alloys in
(Fe,Co,Ni)
1-x-y(Cr,Mo)xBy systems and reported that the high B-
containing alloys exhibit rather high hardness and strength in
an annealed crystallization state [25,26]. The hardness of their
multicomponent glassy alloys is in the range of 1000 e1500,
which is considerably lower than that for Co eFeeTaeB BMGs
and the lower hardness values have been presumed to result
from the dissolution of Fe and Ni elements, though the
multicomponent effect is expected to be favorable for the in-
crease in hardness. Here we examined the possibility of
synthesizing a new BMG with ultrahigh yield strength and
hardness in Ta-free Co eCreMoeB multicomponent alloys in
comparison with Fe eCreMoeB and Ni eCreMoeB alloys. This
paper presents the formation, structure, thermal stability,
crystallization behavior, mechanical properties and oxidation
resistance of multicomponent Co eCreMoeB glassy alloys in
melt-spun ribbon and cast rod forms. Besides, the features of
their properties are investigated through the comparison
among the similar three Co eCreMoeB, Fe eCreMoeB and
NieCreMoeB alloys.
2. Experimental procedure
Multicomponent alloy ingots were prepared by arc melting the
mixture of pure Co, Fe, Ni, Cr and Mo metals with purities
above 99.8 mass% and crystal B metalloid with a purity of 99.9
mass% in an argon atmosphere. Bulky rod specimens with
diameters of 1 e2 mm and a lengths of about 30 e50 mm were
prepared by the ejection copper mold casting method. Alloy
ribbons with a cross section of about 0.025 * 1.0 mm
2 were also
prepared by the melt spinning method. Amorphous structure
was evaluated by X-ray diffraction (XRD) and transmission
electron microscopy (TEM). Thermal stability associated with
glass transition, supercooled liquid region and crystallization
was examined by differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) at a
heating rate of 0.67 K/s. Crystallized structure was examined
by XRD and TEM. Mechanical properties of Young 's modulus,
yield strength, fracture strength and plastic and fracture
strains were measured at room temperature under a uniaxial
compressive deformation mode with an Instron-type testing
machine. The gauge dimension of the sample was 1 mm in
diameter and 2 mm in height and the initial strain rate was 5 *
10
/C0 4 s/C0 1. Hardness was measured by a Vickers hardness tester
with a load of 0.98 kN. Plasticity was evaluated using a simple
bend-test. The degree of ductility was determined by
measuring the radius of curvature at which fracture occurred
in the bend test. The strain on the outer surface required for
fracture ( ε
f ) is evaluated by εf ¼ t
L/C0 t, where L is the distance
between parallel plates at fracture and t is the thickness of the
ribbon specimen. When the εf is 1.0, the ribbon sample is
deﬁned as “ductile” or “good plasticity”, while the ribbon with
εf below 1.0 is presented as “brittle”. Deformation marking and
fracture surface appearance of the tested specimens were
observed with an optical microscope and a scanning electron
microscope (SEM). Corrosion behavior was examined using a
potentiostaticmeter (Gamry Interface 1000) at 298 K in 3.5
mass% NaCl solution. The oxidation behavior in air was also
evaluated by a thermal gravity analyzer (TGA/DSC1) at a
heating rate of 0.67 K/s.

of the ﬁnal-stage exothermic peak is about 1050 K for the Co-

amorphous phase to complete crystallization. The highly

well as the extremely high offset crystallization temperature

resulting from strong bonding nature among their constituent

With the aim of clarifying the ﬁrst-stage crystallization
structure, the TEM data of the (Co
0.75Cr0.125Mo0.125)88B12 alloy
annealed for 1.8 ks at 814 K are shown in Fig. 2. It is notable in
Fig. 2 (a) that two kinds of ﬁne precipitates with different
morphologies are dispersed homogeneously. The selected-
area electron diffraction (SAED) pattern ( Fig. 2 (b)) indicates
that their precipitates are composed of hcp and fcc phases. As
shown in Fig. 2 (c, d), the hcp phase, as the main precipitation
phase, has a nearly spherical morphology with a size of about
25 nm, while the fcc phase has a heterogeneous needle-like
morphology with a width of about 10 nm and a length of
about 20 e40 nm and includes a high density of planar faults.
The highly heterogeneous morphology for the fcc phase
seems to be the ﬁrst, and the ease of the generation of planar
faults is thought to be the origin for the highly heterogeneous
growth mode. The HAADF-STEM micrograph and corre-
sponding energy dispersive X-ray (EDX) maps of metallic-
elements are shown in Fig. 2 (e). The nanocrystal regions
consist of mainly a hcp phase with enriched Co and lower Cr
and Mo contents, indicating that the three elements are par-
titioned among the nano-hcp, nano-fcc and residual glassy
phases. The distinct component redistribution among the
three phases is inconsistent with almost all previous data
where the ﬁrst-stage nanocrystalline phase has nearly the
same nominal alloy composition [ 11,26]. The simultaneous
precipitation of two nanophases with different crystal struc-
tures, different morphologies and different alloy composi-
tions is presumably because the ﬁrst-stage precipitation
reaction occurs in the supercooled liquid with high atomic
mobility and low viscosity. It is thus concluded that the
unique ﬁrst-stage crystallization mode for the Co-based alloy
is attributed to the appearance of supercooled liquid region
before crystallization through the retardation of the two-
phase decomposition even at the low metalloid composition.
This result also implies that the suppression of more long-
range atomic rearrangement required for the further prog-
ress in crystallization is difﬁcult in the supercooled liquid
region.

Although there are a number of crystallization examples
where two phases precipitate almost simultaneously from
amorphous or glassy phase [ 34,35], their two phases always
contain at least one compound phase. In the present 12%B
alloy, the simultaneous precipitation of only simple metallic

crystallization mode. Even for this crystallization mode, the
progress in the crystallization reaction requires the long-
range atomic rearrangements of homogeneously mixed
glassy phase to [hcp þ fcc] phases. The necessity seems to
suppress the easy precipitation of one simple metal phase,
resulting in the appearance of glass transition. Here, it is
important to pay attention to the alloy compositions among

Fig. 2 , the hcp phase is enriched with Co element, while the
residual glassy phase is characterized by the lower Co and

glass transition even for the low 12%B content glassy alloy. In

primary mixed [hcp þ fcc] phases with different compositions
for the appearance of glass transition. On the other hand, it
may be said that the simultaneous precipitation of the hcp
and fcc phases with different alloy compositions is possible
only from the supercooled liquid region with high atomic
mobility and low viscosity. There have been hardly reports on
such a two-phase simple metal crystallization mode for
amorphous and glassy alloys reported to date. The unique-

rable precipitation potential of B-free hcp and fcc phases
through the preferential strong atomic bonding of Cr eB and
MoeB pairs.

4.4. Ultrahigh strength for Co eCreMoeB bulk metallic
glass
It is important to develop ultrahigh strength materials with
fracture strength above 5000 MPa for the alloys without
expensive elements because the development is expected to
affect signiﬁcantly basic science as well as engineering ma-
terial ﬁeld. When we look at the ultrahigh fracture strength
material on the basis of a number of previous papers, one can
recognize one group of bulk metallic glasses in Co eFeeTaeB
[19], Co eTaeMoeB[ 30], Co eTaeB[ 20], Ir eNieTae(B) [ 21],
OseCoeTaeB[ 22], and Co eTaeBeSi [ 23] systems. For
instance, the Co
43Fe20Ta3.5B31.5 glassy rod of 2 mm in diameter
exhibits high compressive fracture strength of 5185 MPa under
a uniaxial compressive deformation mode at room tempera-
ture. This alloy was reported in 2003 [ 19], but there have been
few data on the synthesis of ultrahigh strength metallic ma-
terials with fracture strength exceeding 5000 MPa in glassy,
nanocrystalline and ordinary crystalline states for the con-
ventional Fe, Co and Ni metallic base systems. The ultrahigh
strength of the Co eFeeTaeB base bulk glassy alloys has been
presumed to originate from the medium-range ordered
atomic conﬁgurations including icosahedral-like local atomic
arrangements. Later, it has been reported that the bulk glassy
alloys have a local atomic conﬁguration which is similar to
that for the complex cubic (Co, Fe, Ta)
23B6 compound [46]. This
information suggests that a new alloy searching on the basis
of M
23B6 type compound structure enables the ﬁnding of a
new ultrahigh strength metallic material in glassy and nano-
crystalline states. Considering that the compressive strength
is 4700 MPa for the present (Co, Cr, Mo)
73B27 alloy and
5185 MPa for Co 43Fe20Ta3.5B31.5, the difference is presumably
due to the difference in B content. The alloy design to Co-
based glassy alloys with high bulk glass-forming ability and
good bending plasticity in the higher B content range seems to
enable the formations of a new bulk glassy alloy with ultra-
high strength above 5000 MPa for engineering important Fe
and Co-based alloy systems as well as of a new nano-
composite [G þ M
23B6] alloy with ultrahigh Hv values above
2000. Finally, it is summarized that the present Co-based BMG
has unique advantages of high resistance to elevated tem-
perature oxidation in air and high corrosion resistance to
highly corrosive chemical solutions, in addition to the ultra-
high yield strength with distinct plastic strain.
