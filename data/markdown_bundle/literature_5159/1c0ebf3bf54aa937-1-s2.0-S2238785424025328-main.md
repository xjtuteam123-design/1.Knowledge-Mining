ABSTRACT
Microstructure evolution and first-principles calculations were adopted to explore alloying elements Sn and Y 
proportion influence on room- and elevated-temperature mechanical properties of as-cast Mg – Sn – Y alloy in the 
present study. The results show that the room- and elevated-temperature compression yield strength and ulti -
mate compression strength of Mg – 1Sn-yY alloy simultaneously improves with Y addition from 0.5 wt% to 2 wt%. 
Decreasing Sn to 0.5 wt% in Mg – 1Sn – 2Y alloy, Mg-0.5Sn – 2Y alloy yields comparable room-temperature strength 
to Mg – 1Sn-1.5Y alloy with similar alloying element addition. Notably, the elevated-temperature strength of Mg- 
0.5Sn – 2Y alloy is even greater than that of counterpart Mg – 1Sn-1.5Y alloy and achieves increment by ~42 MPa 
at 200 
◦
C, 48 MPa at 250 
◦
C, and 29 MPa at 300 
◦
C, respectively. Combined with microstructure analysis and 
first-principles calculations, the mechanism behind this is mainly attributed to increased Y solubility in the 
matrix. Sn content reduction leads to the MgSnY ternary phase (4.97%) in Mg – 1Sn-1.5Y alloy being replaced by 
the low-content Sn
3
Y
5 
phase (2.30%) so as to high Y solid solution in the Mg-0.5Sn – 2Y alloy. Raised Y solubility 
is more favorable to decrease cohesive energy and increase bulk modulus, shear modulus and Young ’ s modulus, 
which avails enhancing intrinsic cohesive strength and deformation resistance of alloy other than Sn solubility. 
Thus, improvement of cohesive strength and deformation resistance by Y solubility promotes Mg-0.5Sn – 2Y alloy 
to endure high loading at elevated temperatures.
1. Introduction
As the automobile, rail transit, and aerospace industries experience 
unprecedented growth, the appetite for environmentally benign, light -
weight materials with superior performance characteristics is intensi -
fying. Magnesium (Mg) alloys, renowned for low density and high 
specific strength, have emerged as a pivotal focus of research endeavors 
in alloy exploration [ 1 – 5 ]. Several Mg alloy series have been success -
fully developed, such as Mg-Al [ 4 – 8 ] and Mg – Zn [ 9 – 12 ] alloys, due to 
the satisfactory comprehensive combination of mechanical properties at 
room temperature (RT). However, dramatically decreased strength at 
elevated temperatures limits their further applications in severe condi -
tions [ 13 ]. For instance, the tensile strength of as-cast Mg – 9Al (wt.%) 
alloy at RT decreased from 184 MPa to 148 MPa at 150 
◦

strength of as-cast Mg – 4Zn (wt.%) alloy sharply declined by 61.8% at

◦

from 294 MPa to 167 MPa [ 11 ]. So, it is significant to design and develop 
novel Mg alloys with excellent mechanical properties at RT and elevated 
temperatures for engineering requirements.

3. Results
3.1. Mechanical properties
Fig. 2 shows the compression engineering stress-strain curves of as- 
cast Mg – 1Sn-(0.5, 1.5, 2 wt%) Y and Mg-0.5Sn – 2Y (wt.%) alloys at RT 
and elevated temperature, respectively. The compressive yield strength 
(CYS) and ultimate compressive strength (UCS) are shown in Fig. 3 . As 
seen in Fig. 2 (a), Mg – 1Sn-0.5Y alloy exhibits a long processing softening 
stage at elevated temperature. With Y content increased from 0.5 wt% to 
2 wt%, the elevated temperature softening is suppressed, when the Sn 
content hold on 1 wt%. Both CYS and UCS are simultaneously improved 
with Y addition regardless of RT and elevated temperatures. Compared 
to Mg – 1Sn-0.5Y alloy, the increment of CYS of Mg – 1Sn – 2Y alloy reaches 
~17 MPa, 28 MPa, 20 MPa, and 23 MPa from 25

In addition, although CYS and UCS of identical alloy gradually

Interestingly, the Mg-0.5Sn – 2Y alloy exhibits a significant

4. Discussion
4.1. Strength depends on solid solubility and second phase
Based on the above analysis, when the Sn content is 1 wt%, Y 
addition increased to be 2 wt% improves the compression strength of as- 
cast Mg – Sn – Y alloy at RT and elevated temperature. When Sn content is 
reduced to be 0.5 wt% in the basis of Mg – 1Sn – 2Y alloy, as-cast Mg- 
0.5Sn – 2Y exhibits comparable RT strength with Mg – 1Sn-1.5Y alloy and 
further enhanced elevated-temperature compression strength than 
Mg – 1Sn – 2Y alloy. It is acknowledged that the mechanical properties of 
alloys are affected by the solid solution atoms, grain size, and second 
phase. Because all alloys are in an as-cast state, alloys present compar -
atively coarse grain size, which slightly affects strength.
Therefore, a doubled increase of MgSnY ternary phase (4.97%) in 
Mg – 1Sn-1.5Y alloy might be the main reason for improved strength, 
compared to Mg – 1Sn-0.5Y alloy (2.75%). While Y continually increases 
to be 2 wt% in Mg – 1Sn – 2Y alloy, the fraction and type of the second 
phase are basically unchanged in comparison to Mg – 1Sn-1.5Y alloy. The 
Y solubility in Mg matrix increases to 0.3 at.% in Mg – 1Sn – 2Y alloy,

4.2. Cohesive energy transformation in Mg-xSn-yY solid solution
To thoroughly explore the influence of Sn and Y solutes on the me -
chanical performances, five Mg-xSn-yY solid solutions models according 
to the atomic ratio of Sn and Y, are designed. Corresponding cohesive 
energy, describing the strength of the binding force between atoms, is 
predicted. As is shown in Fig. 7 , the cohesive energy decreases from 
 1.57 eV/atom to 1.66 eV/atom, where the Y atomic ratio increases 
from 1 to 4 in Mg – 1Sn-yY solid solution. Sn atomic ratio up to 4, the 
cohesive energy decreases to 1.65 eV/atom in Mg-xSn-1Y solid solu -
tion. In comparison, the differential of cohesive energy of 0.09 eV/atom 
in Mg – 1Sn-yY solid solution is higher by 13% than that of Mg-xSn-1Y 
solid solution. The amplitude of variation in the cohesive energy con -
cerning Y atoms is stronger than that of Sn. Therefore, improved Y solid 
solubility in Mg – Sn – Y alloy is more facilitated to strengthen the intrinsic 
properties of the alloy and raise bonding force to withstand external 
loading. The related discussion will be shown in section 4.4 on 
strengthening mechanisms. This consisted of experiments of superior 
elevated-temperature strength in Mg-0.5Sn – 2Y alloy with high Y solu -
bility, other than comparable Mg-1.5Sn – 1Y alloy and Mg – 2Sn – 1Y alloy.
4.3. Mechanical stability and moduli variation in Mg-xSn-yY solid 
solution
Furthermore, the mechanical parameters, including bulk modulus 
(B), shear modulus (G), and Young ’ s modulus (E) of the five Mg-xSn-yY 
solid solutions, are evaluated, as shown in Fig. 8 . It appears that the bulk 
modulus slightly increases with Y from 35.43 GPa to 38.41 GPa, whereas 
that nearly maintains unchanged with Sn addition. Similarly, increased 
Y contributes to the enhancement of shear modulus and Young ’ s 
modulus. Both the shear modulus and Young ’ s modulus of 1Sn4Y solid 
solution are enhanced by around 14% higher than those of 1Sn1Y. This 
improved modulus by the Y solute is more likely to promote the defor -
mation resistance of the alloy. In contrast, Sn addition leads to the ~4% 
deduction of shear modulus and Young ’ s modulus. It signifies that added 
Sn solute in Mg – Sn – Y alloy shows the disadvantage of deformation 
resistance improvement. The reason might be ascribed to the change of 
nearest-neighbor distance from Mg and valence orbit. In the reported 
investigations, the short nearest-neighbor distance from Mg was sug -
gested to weaken the lattice expansion and s-p hybridization is stronger 
than s-s hybridization [ 41 ]. Short nearest-neighbor distance and strong 
s-p hybridization between Mg and Y are more favorable for modulus 
improvement than that of Sn.
