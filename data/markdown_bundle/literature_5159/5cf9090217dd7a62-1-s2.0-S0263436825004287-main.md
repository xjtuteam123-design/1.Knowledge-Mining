ABSTRACT
Advancements in science and technology have led to increasingly stringent performance requirements for ma -
terials, and traditional alloys cannot satisfy the demands of modern applications. Refractory high-entropy alloys 
(RHEAs) composed of W and Mo have the potential to become the next generation of high-performance mate -
rials. In this study, a W35Nb25Mo15Ta5Ti10Ni10 alloy was fabricated using laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) and 
subsequently annealed. Under rapid solidification conditions, the LPBFed alloy exhibited a supersaturated solid 
solution of Ti elements, while the precipitation of secondary phases effectively suppressed crack defects. The 
average grain size of the LPBFed alloy was less than 5 μ m, and its yield strength exceeded 2050 MPa. The average 
grain size did not increase after heat treatment. Element diffusion led to the formation of a semi-coherent Ni
4
Ti
3 
phase with the matrix phase. This caused compositional fluctuations within the matrix phase, increasing the 
plasticity of the alloy. The annealed alloy exhibited a room-temperature compressive ultimate strength that 
exceeded 3.1 GPa, with a fracture strain increase of 266.7 %. The heat treatment method, which induced 
compositional fluctuations through intergranular and intragranular element diffusion, synergistically improved 
the ultimate strength and plasticity of the alloy. Thus, this study presented a new approach for the synergistic 
enhancement of ultimate strength and toughness in brittle alloys.
1. Introduction
Unlike traditional alloys, high-entropy alloys (HEA) are formed by 
the solid solution of five or more elements as the main constituents [ 1 ]. 
HEAs exhibit thermodynamic high-entropy, structural lattice distortion, 
kinetic sluggish diffusion, and performance “ cocktail ” effects, which 
afford them advantages such as high temperature resistance, high 
strength, high hardness, corrosion resistance, wear resistance, oxidation 
resistance, and radiation resistance [ 2 – 8 ]. Refractory high-entropy al -
loys (RHEA) composed of elements with high melting points, such as W 
and Mo, are considered key materials for future research and develop -
ment in the aerospace and defense industries [ 9 – 11 ].
In 2010, Senkov designed two RHEAs (WNbMoTa and WNbMoTaV); 
the hardness of the WNbMoTaV alloy reached 5250 MPa [ 10 ]. Although 
RHEAs with a high content of W and Mo elements exhibit excellent high- 
temperature properties, they suffer from poor room-temperature 
plasticity. For example, the NbMoTaWV alloy has a compressive 
strength of 405 MPa at 1600 
◦
C; however, its room-temperature 
compressive plasticity is only ~2 % [ 11 ]. The plasticity of the alloy 
can be improved by adding elements with low melting points or 
reducing the content of W and Mo elements; however, the improvement 
remains limited [ 12 ]. Most RHEAs are formed using arc melting [ 13 , 14 ], 
which suffers from compositional segregation and long development 
cycles. Further, secondary processing is difficult because of their 
extremely high melting points and hardness values, which makes it 
challenging to form large-sized, complex-structured parts. The poor 
room-temperature plasticity and difficulty in processing and forming 
severely hinder the development of RHEA materials.
The laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) additive manufacturing tech -
nology is a point-by-point digital forming process that uses high-energy 
laser beams and can shape components with high melting points and 
complex structures [ 15 , 16 ]. RHEAs composed of multiple elements

3.2. Mechanical property
Fig. 6 shows the room temperature compressive testing curves for the 
LPBFed and annealed W35Nb25Mo15Ta5Ti10Ni10 alloys; the test re -
sults are listed in Table 3 . The LPBFed alloy has the yield strength, ul -
timate strength, and fracture strain for the LPBFed and annealed alloys 
are 2068 ± 43 MPa, 2386 ± 126 MPa, and 7.8 ± 0.7 % and 1671 ± 28 
MPa, 3119 ± 49 MPa, and 28.6 ± 1.6 %, respectively. Although the 
yield strength of the alloy decreases by 19.2 % after heat treatment, the 
ultimate strength and fracture strain increases by 31.0 % and 266.7 %, 
respectively. Thus, the ultimate strength and plasticity of the alloy are 
significantly enhanced. As reported in previous studies, arc melting is 
the predominantly used forming method for RHEAs, with a few studies 
employing additive manufacturing [ 12 , 28 – 30 ], and even fewer using 
hot rolling [ 31 ]. This is because RHEAs typically exhibit poor plasticity 
and high processing difficulty, especially RHEAs containing C [ 32 , 33 ] 
and those with both W and Mo. The room temperature compressive yield 
strength of the LPBFed W35Nb25Mo15Ta5Ti10Ni10 alloy is superior to 
that of other RHEAs [ 11 , 12 , 28 – 48 ]. After heat treatment, the ultimate 
strength reaches 3.1 GPa, which demonstrates an excellent combination 
of strength and plasticity, achieving a simultaneous improvement in 
both ultimate strength and plasticity.

5. Conclusions
This study proposes a heat treatment strategy that leverages 
elemental fluctuations in the matrix phase and the formation of a semi- 
coherent Ni
4
Ti
3 
grain boundary phase, enhancing both the ultimate 
strength and plasticity of RHEAs fabricated via LPBF without altering 
the grain size. Owing to the extremely high temperature gradient in 
LPBF, the LPBFed alloy supersaturates the Ti element in the matrix 
phases, resulting in a grain size of less than 5 μ m. Simultaneously, the 
precipitation of the grain boundary phase helps to repair crack defects in 
the matrix. The alloy exhibits a yield strength of 2068 ± 43 MPa, ulti -
mate strength of 2386 ± 126 MPa, and fracture strain of 7.8 ± 0.7 %. 
After heat treatment, the grain boundary density is reduced without 
changing the average grain size, facilitating dislocation slip and 
improving plasticity. Precipitation of the Ni
4
Ti
3 
phase promotes Ni 
diffusion from the matrix to the grain boundary phase, reducing lattice 
distortion. The semi-coherent interface between Ni ₄ Ti ₃ and the matrix 
strengthens the interface and aids dislocation transfer, thereby 
enhancing plasticity. First-principles calculations confirm that the 
plasticity of the matrix phase in the annealed alloy increased compared 
to that of the LPBFed alloy. The ultimate strength of the annealed alloy 
exceeds 3.1 GPa, the ultimate strength increased by 31.0 % and the 
fracture strain increased by 266.7 %. This heat treatment, which induces 
intergranular and intragranular elemental diffusion, successfully en -
hances both ultimate strength and plasticity, offering a new strategy for 
improving the strength and toughness of brittle alloys.
