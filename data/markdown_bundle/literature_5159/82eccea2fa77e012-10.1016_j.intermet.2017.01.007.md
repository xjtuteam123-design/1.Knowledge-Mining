Effect of Ti additions on mechanical properties of NbMoTaW and

abstract
With Ti addition in a NbMoTaW alloy, a new refractory high entropy alloy (HEA) of TiNbMoTaW was
developed. It has been found that this HEA possesses single body-centered cubic (BCC) phase structure,
and could sustain the phase structure even after annealing at 1200 /C14 C for 24 h. In comparison with the
mechanical properties of the NbMoTaW and VNbMoTaW HEAs, the room-temperature yield strengths
and compressive plastic strains of the TiNbMoTaW and TiVNbMoTaW HEAs are signi ﬁcantly enhanced. In
particular, the TiNbMoTaW and TiVNbMoTaW HEAs also show very promising high temperature
strength. Their yield strengths are as high as ~586 and ~659 MPa at 1200
/C14 C, respectively. The present
results indicate that Ti addition is bene ﬁcial to the strength and the compressive ductility of the re-
fractory HEAs at room-temperature. It is suggested that the present refractory HEAs possess the po-
tentials in applications of high temperature structure materials.

1. Introduction
Containing ﬁve or more components in equimolar or near-
equimolar ratios, HEAs, ﬁrstly named by J.W. Yeh in 2004 [1],
have been considered as a new class of advanced materials in recent
years. Due to a combination of high entropy, lattice distortion,
sluggish diffusion and Cocktail effects [2,3], HEAs attract lots of
attention and hold promises for a variety of applications, such as
high strength alloys [4e18], corrosion resistant alloys [19e22],
wear resistant alloys [23e25], and diffusion barriers [26,27].
Among their different purposes, the most attractive one for
HEAs is that they could be served as high strength alloys at elevated
temperature. As already con ﬁrmed in the work of Senkov and his
colleagues, the refractory HEAs possess higher strengths than those
of widely used superalloys such as Inconel 718 and Haynes 230 at
high temperature ( >1000 /C14 C), which is attributed to their high
melting temperature and solid solution strengthening [5].I n
response to the need for high-temperature load-bearing materials,
several refractory HEA systems with a single-phase body-centered-
cubic (bcc) solid solution structure, including Nb-Ta-Mo-W
[5,28,29], Nb-Ti-Zr [4,6,8,30e35] and Nb-Ti-V [7,15], have been
successfully developed.
Among these reported refractory HEAs, the NbMoTaW and
VNbMoTaW HEAs have received the most attentions and been
extensively studied due to that they possess the yield strength
higher than 500 MPa at 1200 /C14 C [4,6e8,33,36]. However, the
NbMoTaW and VNbMoTaW HEAs are brittle (showing very limited
compressive plasticity of about 2% at room temperature [5]), which
would limit their broad applications. Thus, improving their
ductility is very important and meaningful.
It is well known that alloying is an effective method to improve
mechanical properties of alloys[37]. Here, alloying method has been
used to enhance the plasticity of the NbMoTaW and VNbMoTaW
HEAs. Titanium and their alloys possess high speciﬁc strength, good
ductility and excellent high temperature properties. Therefore, Ti
additions in the NbMoTaW and VNbMoTaW HEAs might induce
unique alloying effect in improving their mechanical properties. In
addition to an earlier reported refractory high entropy alloy
TiVNbMoTaW [29], a new refractory high entropy alloys of TiNb-
MoTaW was developed in the present work. The effect of Ti addi-
tions on their structural stability and mechanical properties was
investigated at both room temperature and elevated temperature.

Based on the calculation, the obtained values of the parameters
for the TiNbMoTaW HEA are: U ¼ 13.0, g ¼ 1.079 and VEC ¼ 5.2. It is
known that U > 1.1 and g < 1.175 is required for solid solution
formation and BCC phases are more stable when VEC<6.87
[38e40]. Consequently, the structure of the TiNbMoTaW HEA is
supposed to be a stable BCC solid solution phase. Our experimental
results indeed agree well with the prediction.
Meanwhile, the as-cast TiVNbTaMoW HEA was also con ﬁrmed
to possess a single BCC phase with a lattice constant of 3.212 Å by
XRD analysis ( Fig. 1 b), which coincides with the reported result
[29]. Similarly, the initial single BCC phase structure is retained
even after the alloy was annealed at 1200 /C14 C for 24 h ( Fig. 1b). The
peaks are shifted to high diffraction angles a little bit by yielding a
lattice constant of 3.209 Å, indicating a structural relaxation
induced by the annealing treatment. The result implies that this
HEA possesses high-temperature structural stability as well. For-
mation of stable single BCC phases was also reported in the
NbMoTaW and VNbMoTaW HEAs [5]. Therefore, additions of Ti are
supposed to play no detrimental effect on the structural stability of
these refractory HEAs at elevated temperature.
The micro-hardness of the two as-cast and annealed alloys was
measured by a MH-3 micro-Vickers hardness tester. The hardness
of the as-cast TiNbMoTaW HEA is 498.7 ± 8.3 HV, while it increases
to 510.9 ± 5.7 HV after annealing at 1200
/C14 C for 24 h. Meanwhile,
the hardness values are 510.3 ± 6.0 HV and 522.3 ± 1 1.2 HV for the
TiVNbMoTaW HEA in the as-cast and annealed states, respectively.

indicates that structure relaxation of refractory HEAs resulted by
annealing doesn't result in softening. Similar phenomenon was also
been observed in an AlNbTiV HEA [7]. The hardness of the TiVNb-
MoTaW HEA is slightly larger than that of the TiNbMoTaW HEA,

The two alloys exhibit good mechanical properties, which are
beneﬁcial for the improvement of the strength of the crystalline
materials due to their relatively high strength and high solid so-

4. Conclusions
With Ti additions, a new refractory TiNbMoTaW high entropy
alloy with a single BCC phase structure has been developed. The Ti-
containing HEAs possess high thermal stability and sustain a BCC
phase even when annealed at 1200 /C14 C for 24 h. Owing to the Ti
alloying in the NbMoTaW and VNbMoTaW HEAs, the TiNbMoTaW
and TiVNbTaMoW HEAs show enhanced strength and ductility. The
room-temperature yield strengths of the TiNbMoTaW and TiVNb-
MoTaW HEAs are 1343 MPa and 1515 MPa, respectively. Mean-
while, their compressive plastic strains at room temperature are
above 10%. The effect of Ti additions on the mechanical properties
of these HEAs can be interpreted in terms of a solid solution
hardening model. It has been found that both the TiNbMoTaW and
TiVNbTaMoW HEAs exhibit yield strengths higher than 550 MPa at
1200 /C14 C. The high-temperature mechanical performance of these
two HEAs makes them to be potential materials for high-
temperature applications.
