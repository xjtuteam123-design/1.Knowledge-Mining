1. Introduction 
Niobium (Nb) alloys have the lowest density among the refractory- 
element based components (Mo, Nb, Ta, W, etc.), and are widely used 
in the aerospace and nuclear industries due to the significant mechanical 
properties, outstanding radiation resistance and suitable machinability 
[ 1 – 3 ]. However, the development of Generation IV nuclear system aims 
to enhance its safety, economical efficiency and recycling of the fuel, 
which raises the higher demands for nuclear materials in terms of the 
mechanical and other functional properties [ 4 , 5 ]. 
The nuclear components serving in reactor core generally fail for the 
irradiation swelling or irradiation hardening according to the common 
view [ 6 – 8 ]. To evaluate the irradiation resistance of these materials, ion 
irradiations, i.e. self-ions and inert gas ions, are implanted with different 
dose and temperature to simulate the neutron irradiation in reactor [ 9 ]. 
The ions implanted in the materials will produce a large number of 
defects including interstitials and vacancies. With accumulation of these 
defects, dislocation loops are formed and then they become the obstacles 
for dislocation slipping, leading to the irradiation hardening accompa -
nied by the loss of ductility and the embrittlement [ 10 ]. In addition, the 
inert gas atoms (He, Ar, Kr, Xe) are insoluble in materials, and hence 
they will tend to precipitate in the formation of bubbles [ 11 , 12 ]. The 
formation mechanisms of bubbles have been widely investigated 
including the nucleation via defect aggregation and growth mechanism 
[ 13 ]. Some vacancies cluster with gas atoms and then several clusters 
gather to form bubbles. They could grow further by the migration of Xe 
atoms and vacancies or by absorbing other bubbles via Ostwald ripening 
[ 14 , 15 ].

4. Discussion 
4.1. Swelling induced by Xe bubbles 
The neutron irradiation will cause not only cascade collision but also 
transmutation, and Xe element is the most typical one [ 47 ]. Irradiation 
induced bubble swelling in the fission reactor is mainly due to the 
accumulation of inert gases produced by transmutation [ 48 , 49 ]. 
Therefore, Xe ion irradiation in present NbMoZrCrAlB alloy can simulate 
cascade collision and inert gas diffusion under neutron irradiation 
environment, simultaneously. For both the pure Nb and NbMoZrCrAlB 
alloys irradiated at 350 
◦
C, there are almost no Xe bubbles formed in the 
BCC matrix phase ( Fig. 5 ). Chen et al. [ 50 ] proposed that the bubble 
formation is mainly controlled by gas atom diffusion. In this medium 
temperature regime, the diffusion mechanism of Xe in NbMoZrCrAlB 
alloy is basically the same as pure Nb and their diffusion activation 
energies are comparable. Consequently, both the pure Nb and 
NbMoZrCrAlB alloy in this temperature show few Xe bubbles accumu -
lation. Effect of the alloying elements, Mo, Zr, Cr and Al, on swelling of 
present Nb alloy would not be significant. 
At elevated temperature, the diffusion activity in pure Nb becomes 
more active than NbMoZrCrAlB alloy. To evaluate the swelling resis -
tance of pure Nb and NbMoZrCrAlB alloy, the volume swelling ratio 
caused by Xe bubbles at 750 
◦

posed a simple method to calculate the bubble swelling, according to Eq.
