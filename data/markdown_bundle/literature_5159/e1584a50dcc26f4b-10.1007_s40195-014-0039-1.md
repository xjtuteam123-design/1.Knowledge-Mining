Effect of Mo Addition on the Microstructure and Properties
of TiNiNb Alloy
Haichang Jiang • Ying Chen • Dan Yang • Hongding Sun • Jianmin Zeng • Lijian Rong
Received: 24 November 2013 / Revised: 9 January 2014 / Published online: 14 March 2014
/C211The Chinese Society for Metals and Springer-Verlag Berlin Heidelberg 2014
Abstract The inﬂuence of Mo addition on the microstructure and properties of TiNiNb alloy with 4.5 at.% Nb has been
investigated systemically. The experimental results indicated that the uniform distribution of Mo depresses the appearance
of coarse b-Nb particles at the grain boundaries and short stripped texture consisting of abundant ﬁne disperse Nb-rich
particles appears around the grain boundaries. The yield strength of the alloy was enhanced from 450 to 600 MPa due to
the solution strengthening of Nb and Mo and the elongation reached 18% when the Mo content is 0.5 at.%. At the same
time, the shape memory effect of the alloy also is improved signiﬁcantly by the Mo addition. The maximum recoverable
strain of the alloy with 0.5 at.% Mo is near 8% and has reached the high level of Ni–Ti binary alloys. This novel high-
strength alloy is promising to be used for high pressure tube and the macro-scale coupling with higher-quality
requirements.
KEY WORDS: Shape memory alloy; Yield strength; Recoverable strain; Pipe coupling
1 Introduction
As a kind of NiTi-based shape memory alloy, NiTiNb alloy
with a nominal composition of Ni
47Ti44Nb9 have received
considerable attention for their remarkable transformation
hysteresis expansion in the past decades, and it has been
extensively used as coupling and fasteners of the tubes [ 1–
7]. With the development of industrial technique, the
pressure of the tube is gradually increased from 21 to
37 MPa in some special application ﬁeld, which brought
forward the high request of the mechanical properties of
the NiTiNb alloy. The mechanical properties of existing
Ni
47Ti44Nb9 alloys already cannot satisfy the practical
requirements of the high pressure tube.
Recently, some researches have been carried out to
improve the mechanical properties of TiNiNb alloy. Zhao
et al. [ 8] studied the NiTiNb alloys with 4.5 at.% Nb and
indicated that adding Nb can effectively improve the yield
stress of NiTiNb shape memory alloys. He et al. [ 9] found
that Ti 46.9Ni50.1Nb3 alloy has better shape memory effect
than Ti 44Ni47Nb9 and exhibits enough wide transform
temperature hysteresis after deformation. Chen et al. [ 10]
investigated the TiNiNb alloy with different Nb contents
and found the Ni
49.8Ti45.2Nb5.0 alloy shows a better
mechanical property. However, all the above investigations
only focus on the NiTiNb alloy with different Nb contents.
In fact, the addition of Zr or Mo element also is an effective
way to elevate mechanical properties of NiTi-based alloy.
Liu et al. [ 11] found that the Zr addition may improve the
mechanical properties of TiNiNb alloy. At the same time, it
has been found that the workability accompanying yield
strength and fracture strength of a NiTi(Nb) alloy are also
improved by Mo additions [12–15]. Solution of Nb in NiTi
matrix is found to play a key role in strengthening NiTiNb
alloys. In this paper, the Mo element was added into the

2 Experimental
In order to avoid the inﬂuence of the testing temperature on the
mechanical properties, the transformation temperatures of the
samples in this investigation were designed to be as close as
possible. According to the previous experimental results about
the effect of Mo and Nb elements on the phase transformation
temperature [ 16, 17], the Ni
49.8-1.3xTi45.7?0.3xNb4.5Mox
(x = 0, 0.5, 1.0, and 1.5) alloys were prepared from
99.96 wt% Ni, 99.8 wt% Ti, and 99.7 wt% Nb in a WCE300
tungsten electrode arc furnace with water-cooled copper
crucible. Buttons of approximately 60 g were melted six
times, and then homogenized in quartz capsules at 1,153 K for
2 h. The specimens for casting microstructure observation
were directly spark-cut from the buttons after homogeniza-
tion. The buttons were hot-rolled to 2 mm thickness sheets at
1,143 K, and then solution treated at 1,133 K for 40 min
followed by water quenching. The specimens for the follow-
ing experiments were taken from the sheets. Microstructure
observation was performed on a scanning electron microscope
(SEM) with EDX. The phase transformation behavior was
measured by differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) using a
PerkinElmer Diamond DSC in a helium atmosphere with a
heating/cooling rate of 10 K/min. In the experiments, the
specimens were heated ﬁrst to a complete austenitizing tem-
perature before the heating ? cooling ? heating cycle was
performed. The tensile tests were carried out at room tem-
perature. The measurement of the shape memory effect is
through loading the tensile sample to different pre-strain at
77 K, and then reheated to 473 K.

kind of TiNiNbMo alloy with high damping capacity [ 12].
It is common to understand the addition of Mo will
decrease the solution of Nb in the primary NiTi phase and
usually leads to the precipitations of b-Nb particles from
the grain boundary because Mo owns the similar atomic
structure to Nb. However, the content of Mo dissolved in
the dispersed b-Nb phases is close to that in the matrix and
enriched in coarse b-Nb phases, which indicate that the Mo
distribution in the matrix will inhibit the diffusion of Nb
into the grain boundary and depress the formation of the
coarse b-Nb particles at the grain boundary.

3.3 Mechanical Properties
The stress–strain curves of the Ni 49.8-1.3xTi45.7?0.3x
Nb4.5Mox alloys at ambient temperature are shown in
Fig. 4. It can be seen that with increasing of Mo content,
both the yield and the rupture strengths increase, while the
elongation decreases gradually. When the Mo addition is
0.5 at.%, the yield strength of TiNiNb alloy is elevated
from 510 to 640 MPa and the elongation is 18%. With the
further increase of Mo content, the increment of the yield
strength is not very notable, but the elongation decreased
sharply. In Fig. 1, the addition of Mo in TiNiNb alloy will
involve the precipitation of ﬁne and dispersed Nb particles
in the grains, and it plays an effect of dispersion
strengthening. However, with increasing of Mo content, the
amount of the large size b-Nb particles in the grain
boundaries is increased, which is harmful to the elongation

3.4 Shape Memory Effect
The shape memory effect of Ni
49.8-1.3xTi45.7?0.3xNb4.5Mox
(x = 0, 0.5, and 1.5) alloys was measured, and the rela-
tionship between recoverable strain and the pre-deforma-
tion strain is shown in Fig. 6a. It can be seen that the
recoverable strain of the alloys increased gradually with
pre-deform strain increasing. At the same time, the alloy
with doping of 0.5 at.% Mo shows the higher recoverable
strain than the other alloys. However, the recovery rate of
NiTiNb(Mo) alloys is decreased with pre-deform strain
increasing (Fig. 6b). It should be pointed out that the
Ni
49.15Ti45.85Nb4.5Mo0.5 alloy owns the excellent shape
memory effect. In the same pre-deformation, the recover-
able residual strain and the recovery stain are much higher
than the others. More importantly, the maximum recover-
able residual strain is about 8%, which is close to the level
of Ni–Ti binary alloy. This novel high-strength alloy with
excellent shape memory effect is promising to be used for
high pressure tube and the macro-scale coupling with
higher-pressure requirements.
4 Conclusions
(1) The addition of Mo into the TiNiNb alloy with low
Nb content will induce the precipitation of ﬁne and
dispersed b-Nb particles from the primary TiNi
phase.
(2) No R transformation was induced by the addition of
Mo and there is only one step B2 $ B19
0 transfor-
mation during heating and cooling process in the
TiNiNbMo alloys with different Mo contents.
(3) The suitable doping of Mo will elevate the mechan-
ical properties of TiNiNb alloys at the room temper-
ature. The yield strength of TiNiNb alloy with
0.5 at.% Mo was improved obviously due to the
solution strengthening of Mo and the precipitation of
ﬁne and dispersed b-Nb particles. However, the
overdose of Mo is harmful to the elongation of
TiNiNb alloys.
(4) The addition of Mo will improve the shape memory
effect of TiNiNb alloy. The maximum complete
recovery strain of TiNiNbMo alloy with 0.5 at.% Mo
is about 8%, which is close to the level of Ni–Ti
binary alloy.
