abstract
Mechanical properties and textures of the ultraﬁne grained (UFG) Mg–3Al–1Zn (AZ31) alloy with a mean
grain size of 1/H9262m produced by high-ratio differential speed rolling were investigated. The resulting mate-
rial exhibited high strength and relatively high ductility at ambient temperature. The high strength was
attributed to grain-size and texture strengthening, while the high ductility was attributed to suppres-
sion of inhomogeneous twinning and increased strain-rate-sensitivity. The rolling temperature and the
amount of shear strain accumulated during HRDSR affected the basal texture intensity and the rotation
angle of the basal poles. Bimodal grain-size distribution obtained by annealing the UFG AZ31 at 573 K for
a short time period resulted in considerable improvement of uniform elongation.

1. Introduction
Magnesium alloys have potential as structural materials due
to their high speciﬁc properties. Grain reﬁnement in magnesium
alloys by thermo-mechanical processing has been widely studied
in the recent past to enhance their mechanical properties. Conven-
tional processes such as extrusion [1,2] and rolling [3,4] produced
the grain sizes between 3 and 20 /H9262m. To get ﬁner microstructures,
severe plastic deformation (SPD) techniques have been used and
equal channel angular pressing is the representative SPD process. In
the case of AZ31 alloy, various grain sizes (1–5 /H9262m) were reported
depending on processing temperature [5–7]. It has been demon-
strated by Kim et al. [8,9] that high-ratio differential speed rolling
(HRDSR) is quite effective in achieving very ﬁne grained microstruc-
ture in magnesium alloys in sheet form by inducing high shear
straining during the rolling process for a large thickness reduction
through a single rolling pass. Very recently, we proposed a novel
method of fabricating the ultraﬁne-grained (UFG) AZ31 sheets with
a mean grain size of 1 /H9262m by applying HRDSR under the condi-
tion that a cold sheet was severely deformed in hot rolls [10]. The
idea behind this trial was that grain-reﬁning efﬁciency increases
as the Zener–Hollomon parameter under hot working increases.
Proper control of the working parameters such as roll speed, roll-
speed ratio and roll temperature is, however, critical in maximizing

In the present paper, texture characteristics of the UFG AZ31

examined. The obtained results were compared with the data of
the AZ31 alloys processed by other methods.
2. Material and methods
A cold AZ31 sheet with 2 mm thickness was subjected to the hot
rolls maintained at 473 K. The diameters of the upper and lower
rolls were identical (400 mm) and the ratio of upper to lower roll
speed was set at 3. Rolling was conducted under non-lubricated
condition. A total thickness reduction ratio of 73% was attained
in two step rolling [10]. The same rolling direction was kept in
the second pass. In the ﬁrst pass, the sheet was rolled to a thick-
ness of 1.65 mm (called as the FP sheet, hereafter). The rolled sheet
was cooled and then rolled again to the ﬁnal thickness of 0.55 mm
(called as the SP sheet, hereafter). For comparison, a sample pre-
heated at 473 K was deformed in the hot rolls at the same roll-speed
ratio to obtain the similar thickness reduction (68%) through a sin-
gle rolling pass (called as the Preheated sheet, hereafter). A sample
preheated at 473 K was also subjected to the HRDSR in the cold rolls
for the similar thickness reduction, but serious cracking occurred
during the rolling. The SP sheet was annealed at 473 K and 573 K for
5 min and 20 min, respectively, in order to examine the annealing
effect on microstructure and ductility.

3. Results and discussion
Fig. 1 (a)–(c) shows the optical microstructures of the as-
received, the FP and the SP sheets, respectively. Signiﬁcant grain
reﬁnement is obvious after the second rolling. The SP sheet reveals
a homogeneous and highly reﬁned microstructure consisting of
equiaxed and recrystallized grains. Fig. 2(a) and (b) shows the OIM
maps of the FP and the SP sheets, respectively. The FP sheet (a mean
grain size, d,o f4 . 8 /H9262m) shows the generation of twinning dur-
ing rolling. The blue-colored region in the OIM maps indicates the
twin-involved area, while the red color depicts the basal texture
component, i.e. ⟨0001 ⟩//ND. These two different colored regions
have a misorientation of ∼86
◦ ⟨11 − 20⟩, implying that the twins
have the typical orientation relationship of {10 ¯12 } tension twin. It
is obtained from examination of the twin orientation relationship
that the volume fraction of tension twin is much higher than those
of compression and double twins (0.27 vs. 0.003 vs. 0.005). The SP
sheet exhibits d =1 /H9262m and a strong basal texture. Few twins are
found in the microstructure of the SP sheet. The Preheated AZ31
(Fig. 2(c)) with d = 5.53 /H9262m also possesses few twins.
Fig. 3 shows the computer simulation results.Fig. 3(a) shows the
variation of effective strain with processing time in the three dif-
ferent cases: sheet (473 K)/rolls (473 K), sheet (298 K)/rolls (473 K)
and sheet (473 K)/rolls (298 K). In all the three cases, effective strain
reaches to the level of ∼3.6, which is comparable to that obtained
by ECAP after 3–4 passes. The maximum effective strain rates are
∼11 s
−1 (Fig. 3(b)). Rapid temperature rise takes place in the second
case where the cold sheet and hot rolls are used (Fig. 3(c)). Temper-
ature of the sheet becomes identical to that in the ﬁrst case (631 K)
near the exit. In the third case where the cold rolls are used, temper-
ature of the sheet slightly decreases due to heat loss to the rolls and
then increases due to heat generation by friction and severe plas-
tic deformation. The temperature of the sheet is lower by 50–55 K
than that for the ﬁrst and the second cases. Fig. 3(d) shows the tem-
perature compensated strain rate parameter (i.e., Zener–Hollomon
Z parameter) as a function of effective strain. In calculating Z, the
activation energy Q for Mg lattice diffusion is 135 kJ/mol [13]. Dur-
ing the rolling process, the Z values of the second case are always
larger compared to the ﬁrst case, though the difference decreases
as deformation proceeds. This explains why a smaller grain size
was obtained in the second case, since the recrystallized grain size
would decrease as the Z value increases. In the third case, the Z val-
ues are consistently larger (by a factor of 10) compared to the other
two cases at the effective strain >0.38. It was shown previously that
damage could be formulated as a function of the Zener–Holloman
parameter in Mg alloys [14,15]. According to the result, probability
of failure increases as theZ value increases. Therefore, the third case
is most vulnerable to cracking during deformation. Fig. 3 (d) may
be used as a basic guideline for determining the optimum HRDSR
conditions for maximizing grain reﬁning efﬁciency with avoiding
cracking, once additional data for many other cases with different
temperature combinations are accumulated by experiments.

middle layers in the Preheated sheet as well as in the SP sheet. The
strengthening of the basal texture after HRDSR is different from the
report by Huang et al.[16] where signiﬁcant weakening of basal tex-
ture was resulted after differential speed rolling at the speed ratio
of 1.167. This result is also in contrast to the previous observation
where the maximum intensity decreased after HRDSR [8]. Dynamic
recrystallization (DRX) mechanism may depend on the initial tex-
ture intensity. Rotational DRX may take place in Mg alloys with an
initially strong basal texture, resulting in texture weakening after
rolling since recrystallization can give rise to a large reorientation of
the texture development, while continuous DRX (CDRX) may take
place in Mg alloys with an initially weak basal texture, resulting
in texture strengthening after rolling since plastic deformation by
basal slip and tension twinning leads to basal texture [17]. The ini-
tial texture intensity of the AZ31 used in this study is relatively low
(7.5) compared to the two referred cases where the texture weak-
ening took place (11.7–35 [16] and 17 [8]). It is feasible, therefore,
that the basal texture strengthening during the current HRDSR is
resulted from the predominant operation of CDRX. Nevertheless,
grain boundary sliding or/and grain rotation, which occur(s) more
readily at a higher temperature, seem to affect the resultant tex-
ture. This is because the texture intensity of the Preheated sheet is
lower than that of the SP sheet.

After annealing the SP sheet at 473 K for 5 min, the post-
uniform elongation was increased with little change in the strength
and the uniform elongation. This result may show the effect of
residual stress relief by annealing. Grain size increased to 1.7 /H9262m
after annealing at the same temperature for 20 min ( Fig. 7 (c)).
As the result, strength decreased by ∼30 MPa. After 573 K for a
short time of 5 min, on the other hand, strength largely dropped
to the level of the as-received one, but uniform elongation was
greatly improved. Its uniform elongation is even larger than
that of the as-received one. The strain hardening exponent mea-
sured from the true stress–true strain curve of the annealed
alloy (0.24) is indeed larger than that measured from the as-
received material (0.18). During the annealing at 573 K for 5 min,
the maximum intensities of basal ﬁber texture at s = −9 and
s = 9 were reduced to 22 and 15, respectively. These values are,
however, still notably larger than that the intensity (7.5) of the
as-received one, implying that the high ductility of the annealed
SP sheet can’t be explained in terms of texture. The bimodal grain-
size distribution obtained after the annealing at 573 K ( Fig. 7 (d))
may be the reason of the enhanced ductility since the bimodal
grain-size distribution is known to enhance the strain harden-
ing by promoting dislocation activity in larger grains [22]. The
ductility improvement may be resulted when the bimodal-grain-
size distribution effect surpasses the negative effect of strong
basal texture on ductility. Production of microstructures with a

4. Conclusions
1. The ultraﬁne grained AZ31 alloy with the mean grain size of 1/H9262m
could be produced by feeding the cold AZ31 sheet to the hot rolls
set at the speed ratio of 3 for a thickness reduction of 73% through
the two-step rolling processes.
2. The analysis of texture characteristics suggested that the grain
reﬁning during the HRDSR process proceeded by continuous
dynamic recrystallization. The basal texture intensity and the
rotation of basal angles depended on amount of shear deforma-
tion and rolling temperature.
3. Grain-size strengthening and basal-texture strength-
ening contribute to the high strength achieved after
HRDSR.
4. The ultraﬁne grained AZ31 showed a relatively large post-
uniform elongation compared to the as-recieved one due to
increased strain rate sensitivity exponent and suppressoin of
inhomogeneous twinning.
5. By annealing the ultraﬁne grained AZ31 properly, a bimodal
grain size distribution could be obtained, leading to signiﬁcant
improvement of uniform elongation.
