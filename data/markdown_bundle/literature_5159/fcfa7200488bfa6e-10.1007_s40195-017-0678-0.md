1 Introduction
Ni-based single-crystal superalloys are widely used in aero-
engines and industrial gas turbines for their superior
mechanical properties at elevated temperatures [ 1–3]. The
major alloying elements of the ﬁrst-generation Ni-based
single-crystal superalloys, being Re-free, are mainly Al, Ti,
W, Ta, Mo, Cr, Nb, V and Co. Among them, Co is a
solution-strengthening element [ 4] that improves the
microstructural stability [ 5], decreases the stacking fault
energy [ 6] and reduces the precipitation tendency of
topologically close-packed (TCP) phase [ 7]. The formation
of TCP is suppressed by promoting the diffusion process
with more Co additions [ 5]. It was also shown that Co plays
a role similar to Ru for inducing the reverse partitioning of
the alloying elements and for improving the resistance to
the TCP formation [ 8]. However, the third-generation Ni-
based single-crystal superalloys, CMSX-10, with low Co
concentrations, also maintain an acceptable alloy stability
[9], signifying that multi-element alloying effects are quite
complex as Co alloying necessarily involves composition
changes of other elements. In fact, the Co alloying is not
always essential for practical use because Co-containing
superalloys are very sensitive to tensile orientation [ 10].
Due to the increasing Co content, strongly segregating
elements such as Ta and Re exhibit a signiﬁcant decrease in

Recently, in order to describe the chemical short-range-
order structure in solid solutions, we developed a new
cluster-plus-glue-atom approach. The major merit of this
model is the proposition of chemical composition formulas

bridge linking the chemical short-range-order structure and
the alloy composition. We were also able to evaluate the c

this approach [ 32].
In the study, the cluster-plus-glue-atom model and the
relevant composition formula for Ni-based single-crystal
superalloys are ﬁrst proposed. The general formula is then
used as the atomic structural platform for the composition
design of alloys with different Co contents. Then, the
designed alloy series are fabricated into Ni-based single-
crystal superalloys and are characterized for their struc-
tures, thermal stabilities and high-temperature capabilities.
2 General Composition Formula for Ni-Based
Single-Crystal Superalloys
The basic idea of the cluster-plus-glue-atom model is to
derive a short-range-order structural unit with high struc-
tural stability as resulted from the chemical difference
between many alloying elements and the solvent Ni.
Since Ni-based single-crystal superalloys are based on
face-centered cubic (FCC) lattice (including the L1
2-c0
phase which is a superstructure based on FCC), the cluster-
based model identiﬁes a structural unit consisting of a
nearest-neighbor cuboctahedron cluster and some glue
atoms situated outside the cluster, expressed by a so-called
cluster formula [cuboctahedron](glue atoms)
x [18]. Here
the cuboctahedron cluster with coordinate number of 12 is
centered by the major solute Al and nearest-neighbored by
solvent Ni and its alike ( Ni, to represent the average sol-

the FCC-c solid solution matrix and the L1
2-c0 precipitation
phase elements.

4 Results and Discussion
4.1 Structural Stability During Long-Term Aging
The microstructural morphologies after the standard heat
treatments and longer term aging as observed by SEM
(Fig. 2) all show typical c0 cuboidal precipitates. The mean
size (denoted by ‘ ‘ a’’ [5]) of the c0 was measured by
quantitative metallography. For the mean size of the c0 after
standard heat treatment, at least 100 particles were mea-
sured and the value of the mean size a was calculated by
a = (a1a2)0.5, where a1 and a2 are the length and width in a
c0 particle, respectively.
The coarsening of the c0 particles is followed during the
long-term aging at 900 /C176C for up to 1000 h as shown in
Fig. 2, too. After 300-h aging, c0 shows smooth cuboidal
shapes but rafting is not initiated as the particles are basi-
cally separated. After 500-h aging, rafting occurs already
as adjacent particles start to merge. After 1000-h aging, the
coalescence of adjacent c
0 particles is nearly accomplished
and exhibits typical raft structures.

4.2 Creep Performance
The creep rupture lifetime as illustrated in Fig. 4 shows
obvious decreasing tendency as a function of the Co con-
tents. The 9.43 Co alloy survives the longest creep rupture
life (about 350 h) at 1050 /C176C/120 MPa while the 15.71 Co
alloy has the shortest creep rupture life (about 100 h). In
this paper, the level of Co is above 9 wt%, as already
known [ 8] sufﬁcient amounts of Co (for instance
12.5–20 wt%) depress the c0 solvus temperature, accelerate
particle coarsening at high temperatures and reduce the
creep resistance.
Here, the cluster-plus-glue-atom formula for single-
crystal superalloys always includes 1 center atom, 12 shell
atoms and 3 glue atoms, where solvent Ni is all located at
the shell sites. Since Co replaces Ni, it should be consid-
ered as a solvent similar element that interacts with other
solute elements such as Al and Cr (Fig. 5). The weakened
creep resistance is also caused by the fact that, as compared
to Ni, Co has smaller negative enthalpies with other
alloying elements. The enthalpies of mixing between Co
and (Al, Ti, Ta, Cr, Mo, W) are, respectively, - 19, - 28,
- 24, - 4, - 5 and - 1 kJ/mol, and those between Ni and
(Al, Ti, Ta, Cr, Mo, W) are, respectively, - 22, - 35,
- 29, - 7, - 7 and - 3 kJ/mol [ 33]. The tendency of
creep rupture life shows that the content of Co should not
exceed 9.43 wt% Co (below 1.5 Co in the proposed
formula).

surfaces after the creep test. In the condition of 1050 /C176C/

formulate Ni-based single-crystal superalloys as [Al–
