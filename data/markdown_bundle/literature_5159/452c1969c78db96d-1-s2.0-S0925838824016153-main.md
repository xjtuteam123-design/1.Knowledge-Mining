ABSTRACT 
In numerous engineering applications, the occurrence of highly rapid loading conditions, coupled with signifi -
cant temperature rise, is prevalent. Under these harsh conditions, shear localization is a prominent material 
failure mechanism, which may result in catastrophic failure. Generally, materials possessing higher strength, 
limited work hardening rate, and lower thermal conductivity are more prone to mechanical instability. However, 
in the current study, a cryo-rolled CoCrNi alloy, characterized by much higher strength and limited work 
hardening, shows remarkable room and high-temperature mechanical performance under impact loading except 
at one temperature domain~600 
◦
C where this material displays mechanical instability by exhibiting adiabetic 
shearband. To shed light and understand this abnormal mechanical response, an in-depth microstructural study 
revealed that the ASB formation is closely linked to the recrystallization behavior of this alloy that is invariantly 
related to the stored energy of cold work, test temperature, and strain rate. Uniquely, the amplified nano- 
twinning activity at high strain rate and critical temperature promoted the fragmentation of grains and facili -
tated recrystallization, respectively. Once the recrystallization is completed, the alloy regains work hardening 
ability even at very high test temperatures, reaching 900 
◦
C due to the deformation of the newly recrystallized 
matrix. The present work is valuable in delineating the alloy ’ s application domain for rigorous impact and crash- 
worthiness applications, as there exists a dearth of literature on this material for dynamic scenarios. 
1. Introduction 
The research and exploration for materials that inherit exceptional 
mechanical performance is a swift journey where emerging materials 
demand unremitting and heightened focus. Amongst the new era of 
materials, medium and high entropy alloys (conventionally termed 
multicomponent alloys) have attracted tremendous attention from the 
scientific community since their birth in 2004 [1,2] . Multicomponent 
alloys such as CoCrFeNiMn, CoCrFeNi, and CoCrNi inherit low stacking 
fault energies (SFE) in the range of 30 ± 4 – 22 ± 4 mJ/m2 [3 – 5] , respec -
tively. The SFE is a dominant contributor towards controlling the 
deformation behavior of materials; specifically, low SFE enhances pre -
potency towards forming deformation twins [6] . This, in turn, increases 
strengthening [7,8] , work hardening capability [9] , and flow stress 
values with excellent ductility [10] . Besides SFE, two other factors, 
strain rate and temperature, can also significantly alter the deformation 
mechanisms and, hence, the microstructure of materials. 
It is well known that at high strain rates and impact-related sce -
narios, a prominent deformation mode is localized shearing or band 
formation [11,12] that is triggered due to the occurrence of concen -
trated shear strain [13] . This causes softening inside the material at 
specific points, resulting in sudden strength degradation with ultimate 
catastrophic failures [14,15] or adiabatic shear band formation. 
Generally, a shear band is a narrow, severely deformed region where the 
microstructure differs entirely from the surrounding matrix. The 
evolved microstructure has traditionally been proposed to be caused by

concentrated deformation always occurs before temperature rises in the 
adiabatic shear band (ASBs) [18] . This suggests that the microstructure 
within the localized band undergoes severe deformation first, followed 
by ‘heat treatment ’ with elevated temperatures, and finally, recrystal -

ASBs are frequently observed in materials possessing low thermal 
conductivity [20] , low strain hardening capability, and higher thermal 
softening rate [17] . The lower heat capacity, thermal conductivity, and 
reduced work hardening may promote the formation of ASB [21] . 
Experimental evidence shows that materials processed by severe plastic 
deformation (SPD) methods generally showcase limited work hardening 
capability. The limited work hardening can be attributed to the high 
dislocation densities following SPD method, which reduces the capacity 
for accumulating defects during subsequent high-strain rate deforma -
tion. In such a scenario, the slope of the stress-strain curve reaches zero, 
or even catastrophic instability [22] . Besides these factors, the stored 
energy of cold work has been reported as a potential cause of ASB for -
mation [23,24] . The initial microstructure, including grain size, is 
another factor. For example, ultrafine-grained (UFG) titanium shows 
altered ASB properties and susceptibility compared to the 
course-grained counterpart under impact loading [25] . Dynamic 
compression experiments on UFG titanium produced by equal channel 
angular pressing [26] showed that the reduced strain hardening and 
strain rate sensitivity in fine-grained material promoted the formation of 
ASB. The initial crystalline phase also plays a pivotal role in initiating 
ASB, and materials having hexagonal closed packed (HCP) and 
body-centered cubic (BCC), including BCC iron, tungsten, and tantalum 
[27] , show extended prepotency towards shear localization. Inclusions 
like carbides, their size, and density have been reported to cause shear 
bands and change the prepotency of ASB formation [28] . 
From the above debate, it can be summarized that materials with 
HCP/BCC crystalline structure, UFG, and highly cold worked are more 
prone to ASB initiation under dynamic loading conditions. CoCrNi is a 
renowned tertiary, single-phase FCC alloy spotlighted for its excellent 
mechanical performance [29 – 31] . This alloy has lower SFE ~22 mJ/K 
[3,32] that roots to control its deformation by twinning dominant 
mechanism, specifically at cryogenic temperature [33] . CoCrNi is 
notably famous for its exceptional cryogenic fracture toughness [34] , 
excellent shear resistance [35] (even under forced shear experiments at 
cryogenic temperatures), and outstanding elevated tensile strength and 
ductility [33,36] . These exceptional mechanical attributes make this 
alloy a favorite candidate for low-temperature applications [37] . 
Another area of applications that holds equal importance is the impact 
scenarios coupled with temperature rises, such as the automotive in -
dustry [38] (car crash incidents), anti-armor vehicles (ballistic events), 
and aerospace (high energy absorption), where strain rates may

strain hardening by changing the deformation mechanisms and, finally, 
will affect the structural integrity of components [39] . To find the uti -
lization of CoCrNi for high-temperature impact applications, a thorough 
understanding of mechanical behavior and related deformation mech -
anisms is critical under narrated harsh impact conditions coupled with 
extreme temperatures. However, in-depth literature reveals that no 
available research outlines the performance of CoCrNi under the 
coupled effect of high temperature and dynamic strain rate. Also, the 
room temperature dynamic response of this alloy has been investigated 
scarcely. This renders a stringent need to investigate the mechanical 
behavior and underlying deformation mechanisms to extend the future 
utilization of this superb alloy in the engineering industry. 
Considering the vital need, the current work investigates the dy -

transition temperature of 600

as a critical temperature for ASB formation. Interestingly, the material 
exhibits no abnormality below or above the critical temperature. 
Employing careful microstructural examination using EBSD, SEM, and 
TEM, the factors and reasons for the formation of ASB are analyzed and 
discussed. In addition, supplementary thermal analysis using differential 
scanning calorimetry (DSC) and ex-situ heat treatment was used to 
further insight into the alloy ’ s recrystallization behavior and micro -
structural evolution. The current study provides valuable insight into a 
fascinating microstructural evolution for the cryo-rolled medium en -
tropy alloy under harsh conditions of elevated temperature and strain 
rate, which, to date, is a scarcely evaluated research topic for multi- 
principal alloys. Also, the processing conditions/parameters and appli -
cation domain can be assessed for better utilization of this alloy in 
advanced applications. 
2. Methods & experimental details 
The medium entropy alloy CoCrNi with equimolar composition was 
prepared by a vacuum magnetic levitation furnace using raw materials 
of purity of more than 99.95 %. The cast-homogenized (CH) material 
was cryo-rolled to 50 % reduction and then annealed at 900
◦
C for 
60 mins to produce a fully recrystallized microstructure. Subsequently, 
cryo-rolled to 30 % reduction to induce sufficient cold work energy, and 
the material was stress relieved at a lower temperature of 400 
◦
C for 
30 min. Hereafter, this material was termed as M-80. Compression 
specimens of 3.25 mm × 3.50 mm × 3.50 mm were cut using electric 
discharge machining from the central region of the rolled plates. Before 
testing, all the samples were mechanically polished to remove any sur -
face imperfections. Uniaxial compression tests under dynamic strain rate 
(~ 4000/s) were performed in the temperature range of 20 
◦

The high-temperature tests were performed on a modified split Hop -

compaction facility. More details can be found in [40] .

exceptionally good work-hardening ability, whereas the work-

3.3. Microstructural evolution after dynamic compression

underlying deformation mechanisms play a pivotal role in controlling 
the material ’ s mechanical response and microstructural evolution. 
Nevertheless, these mechanisms are complex and become more multi -
faceted when deformation occurs under severe/harsh conditions due to 
the activation of some non-generic modes or mechanisms during the 
progression of plasticity. However, a thorough analysis of evolved 
microstructure can provide an understanding, to the maximum extent, 
of the mechanical behavior of the material. Considering the same, TEM 
characterization was applied for microstructural analysis and to inter -
pret the flow behavior under dynamic loading. The respective micro -

4. Conclusion 
The current work investigated the coupled effect of dynamic strain 
rate and elevated temperature on the compression behavior of CoCrNi 
alloy. The study ’ s insights show a quite complex microstructural evo -
lution linked to the dynamic deformation of this alloy. It can be 
concluded that; 
a) The thermomechanical processing profoundly changed the usual 
mechanical response of the alloy at a critical high temperature of 600 
◦
C, where the material becomes mechanically unstable and forms an 
adiabatic shear band. 
b) Surprisingly, even with diminished work hardening capability, this 
instability is not shown at any other test temperature. Contrarily, the 
material regains its lost work-hardening capability at higher test 
temperatures. 
c) The cold work resulted in heterogeneous regions of defects within 
the matrix. The areas with elevated localized stress preferentially 
deformed, leading to the formation of micro shear bands. These 
micro shear bands subsequently coalesced into macro shear bands. 
d) Once the shear band is developed in a narrow region, the adjacent 
areas are under higher deformation. To accommodate this increased 
deformation, secondary and tertiary twins were generated. These 
complex twin structures subdivided the surrounding matrix into 
refined blocks. These refined blocks transformed into nanograins of 
random misorientation. This nucleation of nanograins widened the 
shear band.
