ABSTRACT
Ti
2
AlNb-based alloys have attracted extensive interest for aerospace applications as they exhibit high specific 
strength, reasonable room-temperature ductility, acceptable fracture toughness, and excellent creep resistance. 
However, insufficient high-temperature strength and poor oxidation resistance narrow the service temperature 
range, as well as forming/reheat cracks during the fabrication process impede their engineering applications. The 
current paper reviews the design of Ti
2
AlNb-based alloys, including Al/Nb ratios, single/multiple elements, and 
the contribution of first-principles calculations. At the same time, Ti
2
AlNb matrix composites are discussed that 
reinforced by ceramic particles, ceramic fibers/whiskers, carbon nanomaterials, and so on, which improve the 
properties accordingly. This review highlights the effects and contributions of alloying elements and re -
inforcements on the phase transformation, microstructural evolution, and properties of Ti
2
AlNb-based alloys, 
with the aim of better understanding the relationships of composition-microstructure-property. The insights 
gained from this overview can pave the way to design and develop lightweight Ti
2
AlNb-based alloys for aero -
space applications.
1. Introduction
There is an ever-increasing demand for the development of aero -
space components towards thermodynamic efficiency. Therefore, the 
aerospace industry has been seeking the development of alternative 
high-temperature materials that meet such requirements as light weight, 
high strength at room and elevated temperature, excellent creep resis -
tance, good antioxidation, and acceptable low-temperature damage 
tolerance [1] . The lightweight Ti-Al intermetallics such as α
2

3

γ -TiAl, and δ -TiAl

structure materials owing to their low density, high specific strength, 
and excellent high-temperature properties compared to the current used 
Ni-based alloys [2 – 5] . Encouragingly, a cast Ti-48Al-2Nb-2Cr alloy, 
namely 4822 alloy, has been introduced into service in commercial 
aircraft engines as a new lightweight low-pressure turbine blade (LPTB) 
material successfully in 2011 [6] . However, further applications in the 
aerospace field were limited by its poor ductility, low fracture tough -
ness, and insufficient high-temperature properties [7,8] .
The orthorhombic titanium aluminides based on the intermetallic 
compound Ti
2
AlNb were developed from Ti

in 1988 [9] . Ti

accordingly exhibit better room-temperature ductility and fracture 
toughness than α

2

alloys can reduce weight by nearly 40 % without sacrificing mechanical 
strength compared to the Ni-based superalloys. Consequently, 
Ti
2
AlNb-based alloys, known as O-phase alloys, exhibit reasonable 
room-temperature ductility, acceptable fracture toughness, high 
strength-mass ratio, and good creep resistance, showing a balanced set 
of room and elevated temperature properties [10 – 12] . Despite decades 
of development, there are still several issues that must be urgently 
addressed, including insufficient high-temperature strength and poor 
oxidation resistance, as well as forming/reheat cracks during the fabri -
cation process.

2. Fundamental characteristics of Ti
2
AlNb-based alloys
Based on the varying ratios of Ti, Al, and Nb elements, O-phase alloys 
with a general chemical composition of Ti-(18 %~30 %)Al-(12.5 % 
~30 %)Nb (atomic fraction) can be classified into two generations [13] . 
The first-generation O-phase alloys contain less than 25 at% of Nb and 
are primarily composed of β /B2, α
2
, and O phase, such as Ti-25Al-17Nb 
[14,15] , Ti-21Al-22Nb [16,17] , and Ti-22Al-23Nb [18,19] . On the other 
hand, the second-generation O-phase alloys feature a minimum Nb 
content of 25 at% and predominately consist of β /B2 and O phase, 
including Ti-22Al-25Nb [20 – 23] and Ti-22Al-27Nb [24 – 26] . Extensive 
studies have been conducted by scholars on these second-generation 
Ti
2
AlNb-based alloys due to their excellent balance of performance.

well as tend to decompose into β phase below 750 ℃ [27,28] . Generally, 
the B2 →β phase transformation is influenced by the content of Al, Nb,

slip systems compared to α

compromise the ductility of Ti

2.4. Application challenges
The main driving force behind the rapid development of aerospace 
materials is cost reduction through weight reduction and service life 
extension of aerospace parts/structures [60] . Designing lightweight 
aerospace components using materials with enhanced mechanical 
properties can improve fuel efficiency, increase payload capacity, and 
extend flight range, thereby directly reducing operating costs [61] . 
Ti
2
AlNb-based alloys have attracted extensive interest for aerospace 
applications because they exhibit low density, high specific strength, 
acceptable room temperature plasticity and fracture toughness, and 
excellent creep resistance. Despite this, there are still several issues to be 
addressed in the development of Ti
2
AllNb-based alloys for aerospace 
applications.
The tensile strength of Ti
2
AlNb-based alloys decreases sharply as the 
temperature rises, as shown in Fig. 6 , with a more pronounced decrease 
observed above 650 ℃ [49,62 – 65] . It is an undeniable fact that the loss 
of mechanical integrity at elevated temperatures adversely shortens the 
service life of Ti
2
AlNb-based alloys in aerospace applications. As is 
widely known, atoms become more active as input energy increases with 
rising temperature, leading to weakened interactions between them and 
a decrease in strength. More importantly, O phase transforms into cubic 
β /B2 phase, which is more stable in terms of structure and energy. This 
transformation in microstructure also results in the formation of internal 
stress, caused by the difference of volume change in each phase, 
including β /B2 phase, O phase, as well as α

phase. Generally, the 
reduction in the content of O phase with an excellent high-temperature 
strengthening effect, is attributed to the phase transformation of 
O →β /B2 that sharply drops the strength of Ti

temperatures higher than 650 ℃ .

3.3. Multi-element alloying
There are currently extensive studies on single-element alloyed

Mo- and Fe-modified alloy possessing higher 0.2 % yield strength and

alloy to develop new Ti

Ti-22Al-25Nb-1.5Mo-2.0Zr alloy showed corresponding values of 
1091 MPa, 1184 MPa, and 3.21 %, respectively. The results presented in 
this paper suggest that a high Al/Nb ratio is required for excellent 
oxidation resistance associated with additions of Mo, Zr, and Si to attain 
improved mechanical properties over a wide range of temperatures.

alloyed Ti

3.4. First-principles calculations for alloying design
It is well known that computational simulation and experimental 
measurement are two common methods that are widely adopted to 
discover and design material [109] . Generally speaking, experimental 
measurement, usually includes material preparation, microstructure 
analysis, and properties measurement. Alternatively, computational 
simulation, ranging from electronic structure calculation based on 
different theoretical models containing density functional theory, mo -
lecular dynamics, Monte Carlo techniques, and the phase-field method 
to continuum macroscopic approaches, is another approach expected to 
efficiently reduce the time and cost of developing new materials [110] .
Therefore, first-principles calculations have also been applied in the

4.3. Carbon nanomaterials reinforcement
Conventional micron-sized reinforcements have been utilized to 
improve the strength of metals, however, their ductility is sacrificed at a 
cost [132,133] . In contrast, metal matrix composites reinforced by 
nano-sized fillers exhibit excellent strength and ductility, while 
increased creep resistance and fatigue life [134,135] . Compared with 
particles, whiskers, and fibers used as reinforcements, low-dimensional 
nanomaterials such as carbon nanotubes, graphene, boron nitride 
nanotubes, boron nitride nanosheets, MXene, and metal dichalcoge -
nides, have emerged as effective nano-sized fillers for developing metal 
matrix nanocomposites to overcome the performance limitations of 
conventional materials [136] . Among various reinforcements, the 
choice of using carbon allotropes such as carbon nanotubes or graphene 
nanoplates seems to be an attractive option because they exhibit 
excellent mechanical properties, low density, and enhanced wear 
resistance [137,138] , which can satisfy further specific strength and 
reduction in weight of Ti
2
AlNb-based alloys allowing for energy-saving 
in aerospace applications.
Carbon nanotubes (CNTs), first reported by lijima in 1991 [139] , 
have received significant attention due to their extraordinary mechan -
ical properties. Zhang et al. [140] discussed the effect of adding different 
amounts of carbon nanotube (CNT) into Ti-22Al-25Nb alloy to evaluate 
the changes in microstructure and hardness, as illustrated in Fig. 25 . The 
carbon atoms were predominantly dissolved in this alloy at 0.6 wt% 
CNT due to the low solubility of carbon in α -Ti. However, carbon tended 
to precipitate as TiC when the CNT content increased to 2.0 wt%, which 
accelerated the transformation of α
2
+ B2 → O. The addition of CNT 
improved the hardness of the composites, which was attributed to the 
refinement of the acicular O phase, the precipitation of fine O laths, and 
the solution hardening of the carbon. The width and content of O phase 
in composites remained unchanged by further increasing the CNT con -
tent, therefore, the enhancement in hardness was related to the pre -
cipitation hardening of carbides.
In addition, the TiC phase generated by the in-situ reaction was 
found in graphene/Ti
2

2

with different content graphene nanoplates (GNPs) prepared by high- 
energy ball milling and spark plasma sintering, were investigated by 
Wang et al. [141] . As seen from XRD and SEM results a small number of 
new phase TiC formed at the grain boundaries by in situ reaction be -
tween GNPs and Ti-22Al-25Nb alloy, and the remaining GNPs retained 
their original layer structures. Compared with the unreinforced alloy, 
the ultimate compressive strength of 0.2 wt%GNPs/ Ti-22Al-25Nb 
composite was increased by 13.35 % and exceeded 1500 MPa. Based 
on strengthening mechanisms analysis, it was found that the improve -

enhance the tribological properties of Ti

4.4. Other reinforcement
Soft metallic silver with a face-centered cubic structure can cause an 
intergranular slip. Most importantly, its critical shear stress is very 
small, measuring 0.588 MPa [143,144] . Therefore, silver (Ag) was 
selected as reinforcement to enhance the tribological properties of 
Ti
2
AlNb-based alloys, achieving improvements in the reliability and 
service life of aerospace parts. The tribological behavior of Ti
2
AlNb 
matrix composites containing different concentrations of Ag was fabri -
cated by ball milling and spark plasma sintering was analyzed by Wang 
et al. [145] . Compared to Ti-22Al-25Nb alloy, the coefficient of friction 
and wear rate of 15 wt%Ag/Ti-22Al-25Nb composite at room temper -
ature were decreased by approximately 42 % and 46 %. The silver-rich 
friction film formed on the friction surface under compression stress was 
regarded as the main anti-friction and anti-wear factors, and the wear 
mechanism was transformed from abrasive wear to adhesive wear and 
micro-plowing, as shown in Fig. 27 . Moreover, Gao et al. [144] found 
that the addition of Ag significantly reduced the high-temperature 
friction and wear rate of Ti-22Al-25Nb alloy. However, the compres -
sive performance was deteriorated due to the softer Ag-rich areas acting 
as crack initiation when compressed.

5. Summary and outlook
Regarding the challenges currently encountered by Ti
2
AlNb-based 
alloys in aerospace applications, including limited high-temperature 
strength, poor oxidation resistance, and forming/reheat cracks during 
fabrication processes, it would be important to find out which type of 
alloying elements and reinforcements are compatible.
On the alloying side, designing high-performance Ti
2
AlNb-based 
alloys by combining the advantages of different periodic elements, such 
as high-temperature strengthening element (Ta, W), microstructure- 
refining element (Y, Mo Ta, W), antioxidation element (Zr), as well as 
deformation-contributing element (Mo). The effects of alloying elements 
on phase transformation, phase stability, and microstructure evolution 
deserve special consideration, as they play a crucial role in the in-depth 
study of high-temperature properties. At the same time, it is necessary to 
control impurities elements such as Fe, Ni, O, and N in Ti
2
AlNb-based 
alloys to achieve better performance and extended service life. In 
addition, first-principles calculations are also employed to design and 
develop the alloyed Ti
2
AlNb alloys with enhanced mechanical proper -
ties and excellent oxidation resistance, thereby minimizing development 
time and costs.
On the composites side, alloying involves optimizing the composi -
tion and properties of the substrate Ti
2
AlNb-based alloys. A diverse of 
fillers such as ceramic particles/fibers/whiskers, carbon nanomaterials, 
and other reinforcements (e.g., silver particles and titanium alloys) can 
be adapted for strengthening Ti
2
AlNb-based alloys. Fiber-reinforced 
composites with extensive attraction exhibit superior strengthening ef -
fects, however, their mechanical anisotropy, higher costs, and complex 
fabrication process are still significant concerns. Based on the current 
results, ceramic particle reinforcement is a suitable choice for Ti
2
AlNb 
matrix composites due to their appropriate comprehensive properties, 
lower costs, and simpler preparation process. Meanwhile, controlling 
reinforcement content and distribution, as well as interfacial reactions, 
are another key factor for the further improvement of Ti
2
AlNb matrix 
composites. In conclusion, the parallel advances on both sides will lead 
to the development of Ti
2
AlNb-based alloys with excellent comprehen -
sive properties for a wide range of aerospace applications.
To date, much efforts have been devoted to the composition design of 
Ti
2
AlNb-based alloys for their application in the aerospace field. How -
ever, there remain major challenges and potential areas of improvement 
that must be addressed. Future research on Ti
2
AlNb-based alloys should 
be given more attention. Firstly, the design of high-performance 
Ti
2
AlNb-based alloys should incorporate high-melting β -phase stabiliz -
ing elements such as Ta, deformation-contributing elements including 
Mo, and others with appropriate additional contents. In addition, it is 
not a good idea to decrease the content of Al or Nb as they play a crucial 
role in determining the mechanical, oxidation, and creep performance. 
In contrast, alloying elements substitute Ti in available alloying while 
maintaining Al and Nb content may be a promising approach. Secondly, 
ceramic particles, particularly those at the nanoscale, have great po -
tential for aerospace applications, but their distribution needs to be 
given special consideration. Typically, in situ composites exhibit better 
properties compared to those strengthened by external reinforcements, 
while there is limited research on in situ Ti
2
AlNb matrix composites. 
Thirdly, computational simulations, including density functional theory 
(DFT) calculations and molecular dynamics (MD) simulations, provide 
insights into tailoring microstructural equilibrium and optimizing 
comprehensive properties. Specifically, DFT calculations have great 
potential to analyze phase transition processes, while MD simulations 
offer the capability to decode interfacial behavior, jointly advancing the 
development of high-performance Ti
2
AlNb-based alloys and composites. 
Lastly, additive manufacturing is a promising fabrication considering 
the complexity of aerospace structural components. It is necessary to 
optimize the preparation parameters and heat treatment schedule to 
stabilize the microstructure and improve performance due to the un -
satisfactory properties of AMed Ti
2
AlNb-based alloys.
