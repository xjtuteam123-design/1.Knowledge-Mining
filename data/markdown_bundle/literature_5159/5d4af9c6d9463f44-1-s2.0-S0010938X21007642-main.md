ABSTRACT 
The effect of crystal orientation on the hot corrosion behaviors of Ni-based single-crystal (SC) superalloys 
exposed to mixtures of 90 wt% Na
2
SO
4 
+ 10 wt% NaCl at 750 
◦
C for 200 h were studied using the “ deposit 
recoat ” method. The results showed that the mass gains of the specimens with different orientations increased in 
the order of (210) < (100) < (110). The (210)-oriented specimen exhibited superior hot corrosion resistance, 
which resulted in the lowest mass gain. First-principles calculations indicated that the origin of the orientation- 
dependent hot corrosion resistance was the different diffusivities of active cations in differently oriented alloys. 
1. Introduction 
Ni-based single-crystal (SC) superalloys are widely used in the hot- 
section parts of advanced airplane engines owing to their outstanding 
thermo-mechanical performance and oxidation resistance at high tem -
peratures [1,2] . Its outstanding performance is mainly derived from the 
intrinsic two-phase microstructure with a high-volume fraction of γ
′
precipitates that coherently embedded in a γ matrix. Because Ni-based 
SC superalloys can withstand extreme and complex service conditions, 
including high-temperature environments containing sulfur, sodium, 
and chloride; hot corrosion can reduce the high-temperature strength 
and service life [3 – 7] . In addition, various methods have been proposed 
to protect superalloys from hot corrosion, such as protective coatings 
[8 – 12] and surface treatments [2,5,13] . However, hot corrosion remains 
a long-term challenge to their performance and functional lifetime 
under aggressive environments. 
Consequently, the hot corrosion behavior of superalloys has been 
thoroughly investigated in previous studies [7,14] . Although protective 
oxide scales form during the incubation stage of hot corrosion, acting as 
a barrier between the metal and salt, it has also been reported that 
molten salts, such as Na
2
SO
4 
and NaCl, accelerate the hot corrosion and 
oxidation of superalloys [14] . In addition, in most cases, chlorine can 
prevent the formation of protective oxide layers, resulting in enhanced 
attack in oxidizing environments. For example, Mansfeld et al. [15] 
concluded that NaCl in the molten state attacked along the grain 
boundaries, diffused into the alloy and degraded it, leading to severe hot 
corrosion. In addition, NaCl played a significant role by decreasing the 
melting point of eutectic mixtures with Na
2
SO
4 
to 620 
◦
C and acceler -
ating the corrosion process. Gurrappa [16] reported the enhanced 
corrosion of IN718 superalloys in 90/10 (w/w) Na
2
SO
4
/NaCl at 900 
◦
C 
and found that Mo and Fe were also susceptible to hot corrosion. 
Previous studies [17 – 20] have investigated the effects of crystal 
orientation on the fatigue, creep, and oxidation resistance of superal -
loys. However, the relationship between orientation and hot corrosion 
has seldom been reported. In fact, a research by Montero et al. [5] 
showed that there is a strong dependence of hot corrosion resistance on 
orientation for Type I hot corrosion. The correlations between orienta -
tion and corrosion resistance of Ni-based SC superalloys in aqueous 
solutions have also been observed [21] . Ni-based SC superalloys 
exhibited apparent crystal anisotropy [22] . The effect of crystal

4. Discussion 
This study found that Ni-based SC superalloys with different orien -
tations exhibited different hot corrosion behaviors when exposed to a 
Na
2
SO
4
/NaCl (90/10 w/w) environment at 750
◦
C. The mass gains of 
the differently oriented specimens increased in the order of (210) 
< (100) < (110). The specimen with the (210) orientation had less mass 
gain and spallation, thinner corrosion product scale, and shallower in -

(100) and (110) orientations. The specimen with the (110) orientation 
had many acicular clusters on the surface and the deepest internal at -
tacks, and showed phase interface attacks, resulting in severe corrosion. 
The superior hot corrosion resistance of the (210) orientation provides 
useful insights. The possible mechanisms responsible for the superior 
hot corrosion resistance of the (210)-oriented specimen are discussed in 
the following section.

4.2. Effect of crystal orientation on hot corrosion 
According to the mass gain results and the cross-sectional morphol -
ogies, the hot corrosion resistance of the differently oriented specimens 
decreased in the order of (110) > (100) > (210). To determine the cause 
of the orientation-dependent hot corrosion behavior, it is important to 
trace its origin, which is decisive for the corrosive tendency of various 
oriented specimens. The dependence of the corrosion behavior on the 
crystal orientation can be explained by the atomic packing density 
(APD) of the alloys [45] . In general, a higher APD leads to higher atomic 
coordination and stronger atomic bonding, indicating that the surface is 
more difficult to corrode [21] . Schematic diagrams of the structures of 
the bulk unit cell of the (100), (210), and (110) orientations in the 
surface layer of Ni-based SC superalloys are shown in Fig. 12 . The 
Ni-based SC superalloys are face-centered cubic structures, and the 
corresponding APD values for each orientation are listed in Table 4 . The 
APD values of the differently oriented specimens decreased in the order 
of (100) > (110) > (210), demonstrating that the hot corrosive tendency 
of the differently oriented specimens decreased in the order of (210) 
> (110) > (100). Apparently, the corrosive tendency of the (110)-ori -
ented specimen was higher than that of the (100)-oriented specimen, 
which agrees with the experimental results demonstrating severe 
corrosion of the former compared to the latter. However, the APD order 
reveals that the (210)-oriented specimen had the highest hot corrosive 
tendency among the differently oriented specimens. This is contrary to 
our experimental results. Previous studies [21,45] have pointed out that 
the corrosion behavior of superalloys with different orientations can be 
attributed to the difference in APD values. It should be noted that the 
reported results were in aqueous solutions, whereas the hot corrosion 
process is more complicated. Therefore, the orientation-dependent hot 
corrosive tendency of Ni-based SC superalloys cannot be completely 
explained based on the surface APD, regardless of the hot corrosive 
condition.
