ABSTRACT
Developing new materials with high strength and ductility, low modulus and high biocompatibility is a con-
tinuing demand in the ﬁeld of surgical implants. Inspired by the high-entropy design philosophy, two medium
entropy alloys (MEAs), i.e. equiatomic TiZrHf and equi-weight Ti 40Zr20Hf10Nb20Ta10 were designed and their
mechanical properties and biocompatibility were assessed. Both the single-phase hexagonal close-packed (HCP)
structured TiZrHf alloy and the single-phase body-centered cubic (BCC) structured Ti
40Zr20Hf10Nb20Ta10 alloy
show high strength-ductility combinations close to commercial Ti-6Al-4V wrought alloy and remarkably lower
young's modulus than commercial pure titanium (CP-Ti) and Ti-6Al-4V. From the aspects of adhesion, pro-
liferation, toxicity and related gene expression of human gingival ﬁbroblasts (HGFs), the Ti 40Zr20Hf10Nb20Ta10
alloy exhibits distinctively better biocompatibility than that of CP-Ti while the TiZrHf shows only slightly better
biocompatibility as compared with CP-Ti. These results indicate that these two ductile MEAs are potential
candidates for dental application.
1. Introduction
Titanium and its alloys are considered the most reliable choice for
orthopedics and dental implants materials with great biocompatibility,
superior mechanical properties and corrosion resistance among the
metallic materials used in the biomedical ﬁeld [ 1]. In the past few
decades, dental implants have become a common tool in oral and
maxillofacial surgery for the reconstruction of oral function and aes-
thetics [ 2]. In special clinical situations, however, the small-diameter
implants are necessary for single tooth replacement or implant in a
narrow edentulous ridge. Thus, it is worth developing alloys compar-
able in strength to Ti-6Al-4V and also at least as biocompatible as pure
Ti. The adhesion of ﬁbroblasts and epithelial cells to the transmucosal
component of the implant to form a barrier which is essential to the
early healing and long-lasting function of dental implants [ 3,4]. It is
well known that surface features, including topography and physico-
chemical properties, are foremost important in determining the
response of tissues [ 5,6]. Unremitting e ﬀorts have been made to pro-
mote tissue healing for early loading via modifying the implant mate-
rials in recent decades.
The biocompatible Nb, Ta and Zr can be utilized as the alloy matrix
for biomedical application [ 7] in addition to serving as the constituent
elements of Ti [ 8–12]. Niobium-based Nb-28Ta-3.5W-1.3Zr alloy [ 13]
and zirconium-based Zr-xNb alloys [ 14] as a stent and spinal ﬁxation
rods, respectively, can reduce the magnetic resonance image artifact. Ta
exhibits good biocompatibility and excellent corrosion resistance even
in acid medium and has been employed in the dental and orthopedic
areas [ 7].
Hf has rarely been used in the clinic although its biocompatibility
has been evaluated in several studies. Matsuno et al. [ 15] suggested
that the Hf has excellent biocompatibility and no dissolution was de-
tected in both soft and hard tissue. The binary Ti –xHf alloys exhibit
excellent corrosion resistance within the simulated oral environment
[16]. Besides, increasing Hf content gently reduces the dynamic Young's

Recently, the high-entropy alloys (HEAs) or multiple-principal-ele-
ment alloys (MPEAs) concept [ 20], i.e., mixing elements in equal or
near-equal atomic ratio, provide new possibilities for materials design.
Under this design strategy, remarkably increasing strength can be
achieved only by solid-solution strengthening without resort to de-
formation or precipitation hardening. The equiatomic [ 21,22] and non-
equiatomic [ 23,24] TiZrNbTaMo alloys have been developed as me-
tallic biomaterials. This equiatomic alloy exhibits good biocompat-
ibility and excellent corrosion resistance, together with high yield
strength and young's modulus, which o ﬀers an opportunity for ortho-
pedic application [ 21,22]. However, the low ductility of these high
entropy alloys limits their widespread application.
The prototypical equiatomic TiZrHfNbTa alloy composed of bio-
compatible constituent elements [ 7,15,25] exhibits an excellent
strength-ductility combination [ 26,27]. The addition of Hf can increase
the lattice distortion of Ti-Zr-Hf-Nb-Ta HEA considering its relatively
large atomic size [ 28], which could contribute to solid-solution hard-
ening [ 20]. More importantly, Hf e ﬀectively reduces the valence elec-
tron concentration (VEC) and improves the intrinsic ductility of BCC
refractory HEAs [ 29,30].
These outstanding alloying e ﬀect of Hf motivate us to explore the Hf
containing multicomponent alloys with high strength-ductility synergy
for dental implants. In this work, considering the polymorphic char-
acter (BCC →HCP) of Ti, Zr and Hf elements, two Hf-containing alloys
with di ﬀerent crystal structures were designed to comparatively study
the mechanical properties, corrosion resistance and biocompatibility.
Fibroblasts are considered the preferred cells to form a collagen-rich
connective tissue when repopulating the wound after the placement of a
dental implant [ 31]. In vitro study was performed to compare the
biocompatibility of the newly developed alloys with CP-Ti using human
gingival ﬁbroblasts (HGFs). These comparative biocompatibility eva-
luations include cell cytoskeletons, cell proliferation, cell cytotoxicity
and related gene expression.
2. Materials and methods
2.1. Alloys design and preparation
The CALPHAD modeling was used to predict the phase evolution of
HEAs using the TCNI8 database supplied by Thermo-Calc ™. The alloys
with a nominal composition of TiZrHf and Ti
40Zr20Hf10Nb20Ta10 were
prepared by arc-melting using pure Ti (99.99 wt%), Zr (99.95 wt%), Hf
(99.95 wt%), Nb (99.95 wt%) and Ta (99.95 wt%) under a Ti-gettered
argon atmosphere. The button ingots were remelted at least ﬁve times
to achieve chemical homogeneity, and then drop-cast into a copper
mold with dimensions of 10 mm × 10 mm × 60 mm.
2.2. Microstructural characterization
The crystal structures of these two alloys were identi ﬁed by X-ray
diﬀraction (XRD). The XRD tests were performed on the equipment of
Bruker D8 ADVANCE Da Vinci using Cu-K
α radiation (λ = 0.154 nm) at
the 2θ range of 20– 100° with the scanning rate of 2°/min. The micro-
structure, elements distribution and crystal orientation maps were
analyzed with Mira3 Field Emission scanning electron microscope
(SEM) equipped with backscatter electron (BSE), energy dispersive
spectroscopy (EDS) and electron backscattered di ﬀraction (EBSD) de-
tectors. The SEM equipment was operated under 20KV condition during
data acquisition of BSE, EDS and EBSD. Samples for SEM analyses were
mechanically and electrochemically polished with a solution of 10%
HClO
4 and 90% CH 4O, at −35 °C and 20 V for 15 s. Foils for trans-
mission electron microscopy (TEM) analyses were mechanical polishing
followed by twin-jet electro-polishing using the same solution as above
at 20 V around −40 °C. TEM investigations were carried out with JEM
2100F.
2.3. Mechanical properties test
The microhardness measurements were conducted with SCTMC HV-
1000 microhardness tester under a load of 1 kN and 15 s dwell times.
Ten indents were measured for each sample to give the average value.
Tensile tests were conducted on a Zwick/Roell Z020 machine with an
extensometer at an engineering strain rate of 1.0 × 10
−3 s−1 at room
temperature. The dog-bone shaped specimens with a gauge length of
10 mm (3.4 mm in gauge section width and 1.4 mm in thickness) were
machined from the drop-cast ingot and mechanically ground. Both al-
loys for tensile tests were performed at least in triplicate.
2.4. Electrochemical test
To assess the corrosion resistance of these two developed alloys,
potentiodynamic polarization was performed using an electrochemical
station (CHI 760E, CH Instruments Inc., Shanghai) within a three-
electrode cell. The 3.5 wt% NaCl solution was used for the electro-
chemical tests. Mechanically polished alloy specimens with an exposed
area of 1 cm
2 were used as working electrodes. A saturated calomel
electrode (SCE) and a 2 cm 2 Pt sheet were used as reference electrode
and counter electrode, respectively. Before polarization tests, the spe-
cimens were immersed in 3.5 wt% NaCl solution about 2 h to reach a
relatively stable open circuit potential (OCP). The potentiodynamic
polarization tests were performed at potential range from −0.8 V to
+2.0 V vs OCP at the rate of 1 mV/s. To ensure the reproducibility of
the results, each tests were repeated at least three times.
2.5. Sample preparation and cell cultures
The smooth discs with the dimension of Φ13×1.4 mm were ma-
chined from alloy buttons and divided into three groups as follows: 1)
CP-Ti (control); 2) TiZrHf alloy; 3) Ti
40Zr20Hf10Nb20Ta10 alloy. To get
the same roughness, all samples were mechanically polished using SiC
waterproof papers up to #2000. Then cleaned ultrasonically in acetone,
ethanol, and ultrapure water sequentially, ﬁnally dried in an ambient
atmosphere.
Human gingival ﬁbroblasts (HGFs) were obtained from medically
and periodontally healthy male and female patients (18 –60 years)
during the course of implant surgery using a punch. The Independent
Ethics Committee of Shanghai Ninth People's Hospital a ﬃliated to
Shanghai Jiao Tong University, School of Medicine approved the re-
search protocol and each donator signed the written informed consent.
The gingival connective tissues were cut into 2 –3m m
2-sized pieces and
placed in Dulbecco's Modi ﬁed Eagle's Medium (DMEM; HyClone, South
Logan, UT, USA) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Gibco, USA)
and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (Gibco BRL, Life Technologies, UK).
HGFs were cultured and the culture media was changed every 48 –72 h.
The cells were passaged with 0.25% trypsin/trypsin-ethylenediamine-
tetraacetic acid (EDTA; Thermo Fisher Scienti ﬁc) when they reached
90% con ﬂuence, and cells from passages 2– 4 were used.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Alloy design and microstructure characterization
The thermodynamically empirical parameters listed in Table 2 were
used to roughly estimate the phase formation of the two medium en-
tropy alloys. Because the constituent elements are all located in the IVB
group, the VEC keeps constant at 4 for TiZrHf alloy, which promotes the
formation of a single HCP phase. To decrease the melting temperature
and density, an equi-weight mixing strategy (Ti-19.9Zr-19.4Hf-20.2Nb-
19.7Ta, wt%) was used to obtain the Ti
40Zr20Hf10Nb20Ta10 alloy. The
single and ductile BCC phase is expected since the VEC of
Ti
40Zr20Hf10Nb20Ta10 alloy increased to 4.3 [ 30]. The mixing enthalpy
ΔHmix, atomic size di ﬀerences δ, and parameter Ω reﬂecting the com-
bined e ﬀect of ΔSmix and ΔHmix satisfy the criteria for the formation of
solid solution phase [ 32]. Based on the empirical parameters calculated
above, the HCP and BCC solid solution phase can be predicted for
TiZrHf and Ti
40Zr20Hf10Nb20Ta10 alloys, respectively.
To more accurately predict the phase formation, thermodynamic
phase diagram calculations were conducted. Fig. 1(a) and (b) illustrates
the volume fraction of phases vs. temperature from 200 °C to above the
melting point. For TiZrHf alloy, the BCC phase is stable at high-tem-
perature ranging from 972 °C to its solidus temperature. In the range of
872 °C to 972 °C, the polymorphic transformation from BCC to HCP
occurred upon cooling. The single HCP phase is stable below this
temperature range, which is consistent with the VEC parameters cal-
culated above. As illustrated in Fig. 1(b), single BCC zone of “equi-
weight” Ti
40Zr20Hf10Nb20Ta10 alloy (solid line) is broadened than that
of extensively studied equiatomic TiZrHfNbTa alloy (dotted line).
Probably due to the sluggish di ﬀusion in high-entropy alloys, this high-
temperature BCC phase of Ti 20Zr20Hf20Nb20Ta20 alloy can be stabilized
at room temperature although the phase decomposition would occur at
long-term aging [ 33,34]. Owing to the signi ﬁcantly lower BCC desta-
bilizing temperature (775 °C) of Ti
40Zr20Hf10Nb20Ta10 alloy, a single
BCC phase could be possibly retained at room temperature.
Fig. 2(a) and (b) shows the X-ray di ﬀraction results of as-cast TiZrHf
and Ti 40Zr20Hf10Nb20Ta10 alloy, respectively. The single HCP phase is
identiﬁed with its lattice parameters a = 0.3148 nm, c = 0.4982 nm
for equiatomic TiZrHf alloy. For Ti 40Zr20Hf10Nb20Ta10 alloy, the single
BCC phase is con ﬁrmed with its lattice parameter a = 0.3381 nm.
According to the Vegard's law ( amix = ∑ ciai)[ 26,28], the theoretical
lattice parameters can be obtained a = 0.3126 nm, c = 0.4961 nm for
TiZrHf, and a = 0.3373 nm for Ti
40Zr20Hf10Nb20Ta10 alloy. The con-
sistency of lattice parameters between XRD and theoretical results in-
dicates that the constituent elements are randomly mixed in solid so-
lution phases.

3.3. Electrochemical corrosion behavior
Fig. 7 displays the polarization behavior of newly developed TiZrHf
and Ti 40Zr20Hf10Nb20Ta10 alloys together with the control CP-Ti in
3.5 wt% NaCl solution. And the parameters of the potentiodynamic
polarization for these alloys are inserted in Fig. 7. Similar to CP-Ti,
Ti40Zr20Hf10Nb20Ta10 alloy shows typical passive behavior with the
increasing potential. For Ti 40Zr20Hf10Nb20Ta10 alloy, the corrosion
potential Ecorr displaced toward noble values and the corrosion current
density icorr is distinctively lower compare to the corresponding results
of the CP-Ti. These indicate a remarkably higher corrosion resistance of
Ti40Zr20Hf10Nb20Ta10 alloy than CP-Ti. Additionally, the passivation
current ip for Ti 40Zr20Hf10Nb20Ta10 alloy is 4.01 ± 0.75 μA/cm2,
which is near one third that of the CP-Ti. This obviously decreased ip,
indicates the easier passivation of the Ti 40Zr20Hf10Nb20Ta10 alloy and
the more stable passive ﬁlm formed on the surface of the sample. For
TiZrHf alloy, the corrosion potential Ecorr is more positive than that of
CP-Ti. And both the corrosion current density icorr and the passivation
current ip are decreased. These parameters indicate the higher corrosion
resistance of TiZrHf than CP-Ti. The abrupt increase of current density
at about 0.4 V (SCE) for TiZrHf implies that the passive ﬁlm broke down
and resulted in the corrosion initiation.
For Zr and Zr alloys, the passive ﬁlm is considered to be not very
stable and pitting easily occurs in chloride medium [ 41]. In Fig. 5, the
{1011} twins were detected in microstructure which derived from the β
to α' martensitic transformation. And these non-equilibrium α' mar-
tensite are usually considered to decrease the corrosion resistance of the
alloy [ 42]. In short, the pitting corrosion behavior might be derived
from its chemical constituent or the martensite microstructure, which
needs to be further studied. In contrast, with the addition of Nb and Ta
elements, the excellent corrosion resistance is achieved in
Ti
40Zr20Hf10Nb20Ta10 alloy.

4. Conclusions
In this study, two novel medium entropy alloys (single HCP TiZrHf
and single BCC Ti 40Zr20Hf10Nb20Ta10 alloys) with high strength-ducti-
lity combination and low-modulus were successfully developed for
biomedical application. In view of mechanical properties, HCP struc-
tured TiZrHf alloy is more attractive owing to its higher yield strength
and strain hardening than Ti 40Zr20Hf10Nb20Ta10 alloy. However, the
obvious pitting corrosion behavior in 3.5 wt% NaCl solution will hinder
the biomedical application of TiZrHf alloy. In contrast, for single BCC
structured Ti
40Zr20Hf10Nb20Ta10 alloy, the corrosion resistance is
distinctly improved compared with CP-Ti and TiZrHf alloy. The bio-
compatibility results show that HGFs spread more readily on TiZrHf
and Ti 40Zr20Hf10Nb20Ta10 surface, especially on Ti 40Zr20Hf10Nb20Ta10
surface. None of the two newly developed alloys shows cytotoxicity to
HGFs. Also, TiZrHf and Ti
40Zr20Hf10Nb20Ta10 alloys improve the pro-
liferation of HGFs and the gene expression of several adhesion factors
instead of tending to ﬁbrinolytic or proinﬂ ammatory situations when
compared with CP-Ti. All these results show that Ti
40Zr20Hf10Nb20Ta10
alloy is much more e ﬀective to improve the biocompatibility than
TiZrHf alloy. In summary, this preliminary work demonstrates the
possibility of using the high-entropy design philosophy to develop
dental implants by considering tensile properties, corrosion resistance
and biocompatibility.
