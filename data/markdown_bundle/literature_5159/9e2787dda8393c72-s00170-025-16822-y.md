Abstract
CoCrFeNiMn high-entropy alloy (HEA) produces a large number of burrs and leads to rapid tool wear by conventional 
milling due to its high plasticity and strength. An icing clamp was used to fix the workpiece to achieve low-temperature 
milling of HEA, thus improving the cutting performance of HEA. The influence of low-temperature milling parameters on 
the burr length, burr morphology and chip morphology were analyzed, and the wear mechanism of the tool under the two 
conditions of low-temperature milling and room-temperature milling was compared and analyzed. The findings reveal that a 
low-temperature environment significantly reduces burr size by suppressing plastic flow and promoting brittle fracture during 
chip formation. The primary tool wear mechanisms during high-entropy alloy milling include adhesive and abrasive wear. 
Low-temperature conditions effectively inhibit adhesive wear, thereby protecting the tool coating and extending tool life.
Keywords High-entropy alloys · Low-temperature milling · Burr morphology · Chip morphology · Tool wear
1 Introduction
High-entropy alloys have some excellent properties that tra-
ditional alloys cannot match [1 –4], such as high strength, 
high hardness, high wear resistance and corrosion resistance, 
which make HEAs have a wide range of promising applica-
tions in various fields, and the application areas of HEAs 
are shown in Fig.  1. However, the excellent mechanical 
properties of high-entropy alloys make it exceptionally ardu-
ous to cut this material.
CoCrFeNiMn high entropy alloy (HEA), as one of the 
many high-entropy alloys, releases a large amount of plas-
tic heat during cutting due to its high plasticity [5 ], which 
results in more burrs and increased tool wear. Studies have 
shown that burrs have a significant impact in industry as 
they interfere with the performance and functionality of the 
workpiece and can only be removed by time-consuming 
and expensive deburring processes, so it is crucial to know 
how to inhibit the growth of burrs. Nowadays, cutting flu-
ids are mostly used to reduce the temperature during the 
cutting process and improve the cutting performance of 
the material, thus reducing the burr size and improving the 
machining accuracy. However, cutting fluids are expensive 
to manufacture and have serious problems such as polluting 
the environment and endangering the health of operators 
in the process of using them. This study proposes to solve 
the cutting problem of difficult-to-machine materials by 
using an icing clamp to fix the workpiece and achieve low-
temperature milling of HEA. The cryogenic environment 
can have a significant effect on the physicochemical proper-
ties of materials, such as changing the hardness, toughness 
and brittleness of the materials, which in turn optimizes the

3.2 Theoretical analysis of burr formation process 
in slot milling
In metal cutting, due to the plastic deformation of the 
material, when the tool cuts away from the workpiece, 
in its edges, corners, ribs and other locations will often 
remain beyond the design size of the workpiece raised or 
depressed part of this part of the machining by-products 
known as cutting burrs [18, 19]. Figure  8 shows the burr 
formation process, through the oblique angle shear theory 
can be equivalent to the milling process converted to two-
dimensional cutting form [20]. Figure  8a for the ideal state 
of the metal cutting process, Fig.  8b for the burr forma-
tion process, the formation of the burr and the Ⅳ -defor-
mation zone (negative shear zone) is closely related to 
the generation of when the tool does not reach the end of 
the workpiece, due to the workpiece stiffness is not good, 
insufficient support, the cutting layer of the material below 
the imbalance of the force: the first is the stress over the 
elasticity limit triggered by the elastic–plastic deforma-
tion, and then the accumulation of shear stresses to pro -
mote the shear slip. In the present study, the burr size is 
characterized by the burr length h, as illustrated in Fig.  8c.
Gillespie [21], based on the burr formation mechanism, 
classified the burrs produced by plastic deformation of 
materials in cutting operations into four main categories: 
Poisson burrs, rollover burrs, tearing burrs and cutoff 
burrs, see Fig.  9.

4 Conclusions
This paper investigates the effect of room-temperature 
milling and low-temperature milling work on the cutting 
performance of HEA and analyses the evolution of chips, 
burrs and tool wear. This study reveals the material removal 
mechanisms and tool wear characteristics of HEA under 
Low-temperature conditions, providing insights into opti-
mizing machining efficiency and surface quality. In future 
academic exploration, it is recommended to combine ultra-
sonic vibration-assisted technology with low-temperature 
milling, construct a new-type composite machining system, 
and provide theoretical support and technical approaches 
for improving the machining quality and efficiency of

(2) The low-temperature environment reduces the size
