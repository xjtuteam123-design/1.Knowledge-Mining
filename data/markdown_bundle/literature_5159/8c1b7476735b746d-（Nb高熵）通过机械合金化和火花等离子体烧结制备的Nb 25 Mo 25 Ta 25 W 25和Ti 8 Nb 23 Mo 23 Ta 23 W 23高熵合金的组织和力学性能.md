ABSTRACT
In the present work, Nb 25Mo25Ta25W25 and Ti 8Nb23Mo23Ta23W23 refractory high entropy alloys (HEAs) were
successfully prepared by mechanical alloying (MA) and spark plasma sintering (SPS) technologies. With the aid
of ball milling, the stabilized single-phase BCC nano-crystalline structures can be obtained with the average
crystallite size of about 10 nm. After the SPS process, the grain size of both alloys is less than 1 µm. The com-
pressive yield strength, fracture strain and Vickers hardness of bulk Nb
25Mo25Ta25W25 HEA are 2460 MPa,
16.8% and 7.78 GPa, respectively, which are remarkably superior to the same alloy prepared by the vacuum arc-
melting (VAM) method. The addition of Ti can further promote the compressive properties in the
Ti8Nb23Mo23Ta23W23 HEA, where the fracture strain and peak compressive stress surprisingly increased by
56.5% and 10.7%, respectively.
1. Introduction
Over the past few decades, ‘high entropy alloy ’ (HEA), which was
proposed by Yeh [1,2], attracts increasing attentions in the research
ﬁeld of advanced metal matrix materials. From a thermodynamic point
of view, the high mixing entropy of HEA induced by the equiatomic or
near-equiatomic con ﬁguration of 4 or 5 principal elements leads to the
trend to form single-phase solid solution instead of intermetallic com-
pounds [3]. Therefore, it is highly practical and e ﬃcient to modify the
physical and mechanical properties of HEA with the alloying or the
substitution of principal elements considering the requirement of ap-
plication, such as high strength and ductility, favorable oxidation re-
sistance at high temperature [4].
In recent years, the refractory HEAs were explored to show potential
applications for the high temperature aerospace structures, such as
Nb
25Mo25Ta25W25,V 20Nb20Mo20Ta20W20,A l 21.9Nb32Ta9Ti26.7Zr10.3,
and Al 5.2Nb23.4Ta13.2Ti27.7V4.3Zr26.2 alloys [5,6]. Nevertheless, the
common defect of refractory HEAs is their low formability at room
temperature, since the matrix is mainly composed of the single-phase
BCC solid-solution phase that possesses a smaller interplanar spacing
and higher lattice friction for dislocation motion in the atomic scale,
comparing with FCC structure [7].
In order to improve the ductility, heat treatment and composition
adjustment can be adopted [8]. Wen et al. [7] found that a structural
transformation from BCC to FCC occurred at aging temperature of
918 K in the AlCoCrCuFeNi HEA and the plastic strain increased to 27%
after aging at 1273 K. Senkov et al. reported that the substitution of
heavy elements (W, Mo and V) with the lighter ones (Hf, Zr and Ti)
considerably decreased the alloy density and improved the fracture
strain [9]. Yang et al. prepared a series of novel (NbTiVTa)
(100-x)Alx
refractory HEAs and also found that as Al content increased to 25 at%,
the yield strength increased to 1330 MPa and the compressive strain
exceeded 50% [10].
It is well known that grain re ﬁnement can simultaneously improve
the strength and toughness of alloys. In this paper, we utilized me-
chanical alloying (MA) with spark plasma sintering (SPS) to prepare
ultraﬁne-grained Nb
25Mo25Ta25W25 bulk HEA. The alloying behavior
and phase evolution during the ball-milling process were systematically
studied, as well as the investigation on the microstructures and me-
chanical properties of the sintered HEA. Another aspect, literatures
reported that the ductility of Mo and W-containing alloys increased
with the decrease of average valence electron concentration (VEC)
[11– 15]. Considering that the VEC of Nb
25Mo25Ta25W25 HEA decreases
from 5.5 to 5.38 with the addition of 8 at% of Ti, a novel
Ti
8Nb23Mo23Ta23W23 HEA was designed to explore the e ﬀects of Ti
element on the structure stability and mechanical properties.

2. Materials and methods
In this study, Nb 25Mo25Ta25W25 and Ti8Nb23Mo23Ta23W23 (Table 1)
were prepared by pure elemental powders (higher than 99.5%). The
raw powders were milled in a planetary ball milling (QM-DY4, China)
with tungsten carbide grinding media under high purity argon atmo-
sphere. The ball-to-powder weight ratio was 15:1 and the milling speed
was 400 rpm. The acetone was used as a process-controlling agent to
avoid excessive cold welding. The milled powders were subsequently
dried in vacuum for at least 24 h at 373 K, and then sintered by Spark
Plasma Sintering System (SE-607, Germany) at 1873 K for 8 min with
an axial pressure of 35 MPa under vacuum.
The phase compositions of the milled powders and sintered samples
were analyzed by X-ray di ﬀractometer (XRD, Bruker D8 DISCOVER)
with a Cu-Ka radiation. Microstructures of the alloys were observed
using scanning electron microscopy equipped with a backscatter elec-
tron (BSE) detector (SEM, Sirion 200 FEI) and transmission electron
microscope (TEM) Tecnai G2 20 (FEI Company, Hillsboro, USA)
equipped with an EDS detector. The compressive properties of samples
at room temperature were tested by the electronic universal testing
machine (CMT5105, SANS, China) at a strain rate of 10
−3 s−1.
Hardness measurement was conducted using a Digital Micro Hardness
Tester FM-700 Vickers hardness instrument under a load of 500 gf.

3.1.2. Microstructure
Fig. 2 shows that the matrices of both Nb
25Mo25Ta25W25 and
Ti8Nb23Mo23Ta23W23 alloys remain single-phase BCC structure after the
SPS process. In addition, some carbide impurities are identi ﬁed, which
might be inevitably introduced during the MA and SPS process. Ac-
cording to the refs. [21– 23], the mechanism of the formation of these
carbides and the unknown phases is still unclear, thus, further studies
should be carried to understand the inoculation process of impurities
during the MA and SPS process and their e ﬀects on the microstructures
and properties.
The EBSD and SEM images of sintered Nb
25Mo25Ta25W25 and
Ti8Nb23Mo23Ta23W23 bulk HEAs are presented in Fig. 3, respectively.
According to Fig. 3(a), the average grain size of the Nb 25Mo25Ta25W25
alloy is of 0.88 µm, which is obviously ﬁner than that prepared by VAM
[5]. Furthermore, the grain size of Ti 8Nb23Mo23Ta23W23 alloy can be
reduced to 0.62 µm. After sintering, the grain size and the degree of
crystallization of both alloys obviously increase compared to the as-
milled powders [23].
The TEM images and electrical di ﬀraction patterns of the selective
areas of Nb
25Mo25Ta25W25 and Ti8Nb23Mo23Ta23W23 bulk HEAs, shown
in Fig. 4 and Fig. 5 respectively, indicate that the microstructure of both
HEAs is consisted of BCC matrix phase and BCC precipitated phase. The
composition analysis results of these phases were listed in Table 3. The
matrix in Nb 25Mo25Ta25W25 alloy is rich in elements of W and Mo,
while the precipitated phase has an enrichment of Ta and Nb elements.
Moreover, the matrix of Ti 8Nb23Mo23Ta23W23 HEA is also rich in W and
Ta elements and its lattice constant increases from 3.15 Å to 3.19 Å,
compared with Nb 25Mo25Ta25W25 alloy. The increase of the lattice
constant of Ti 8Nb23Mo23Ta23W23 alloy is because the Ti has the largest
atom radius compared with other elements [14]. It can be clearly seen
that W element is mainly concentrated in the matrix phase where Ti
element is deﬁ cient. In contrast, Ti element in the precipitates is closer
to the nominal composition. The similar results were also reported in
other Ti-contained HEAs that Ti is prone to segregate in the inter-
dendrite regions and precipitated compounds [14,18]. In addition, a
few nano-size Ti particles also distribute at the grain boundaries
(Fig. 5), which may e ﬀectively suppress the intergranular fracture and
improve the ductility.

4. Conclusions
(1) Nb
25Mo25Ta25W25 and Ti 8Nb23Mo23Ta23W23 HEAs are successfully
prepared via the MA and SPS technologies. With a 60 h ball-milling
process, the as-milled powders for both HEAs are characterized by
the nano-crystalline structures with an average crystallite size of
about 10 nm.
(2) After the SPS process, the average grain sizes of the
Nb25Mo25Ta25W25 and Ti 8Nb23Mo23Ta23W23 HEAs increase to
0.88 µm and 0.62 µm, respectively. Meanwhile, some carbide im-
purities are identi ﬁed in the bulk samples.
(3) The compressive yield stress, peak stress and fracture strain of
Nb
25Mo25Ta25W25 HEA are 2460 MPa, 3016 MPa and 16.8%, re-
spectively, which are remarkably superior to the same alloy pre-
pared by the VAM method.
(4) The addition of Ti can further improve the compressive properties
in the Ti
8Nb23Mo23Ta23W23 HEA, where the fracture strain and
peak compressive stress surprisingly increased by 56.5% and
10.7%, respectively.
