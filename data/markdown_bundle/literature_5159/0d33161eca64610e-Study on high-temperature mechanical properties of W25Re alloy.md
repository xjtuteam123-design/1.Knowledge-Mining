Abstract
Tungsten rhenium alloy is widely used in aerospace, weapon electronics, and other high-temperature
ﬁelds. W25Re alloy bars were prepared by powder metallurgy combined with the rolling deformation
process. The mechanical properties of W25Re alloy were studied. The results show that rolled W25Re
alloy bars have excellent mechanical properties. The tensile strength at room temperature is up to
1650 MPa and the elongation is 15%. As temperature increases, the tensile strength of W25Re alloy
decreases rapidly, and the elongationﬁrst increases and then decreases. However, at 2000°C, the
tensile strength of more than 200 MPa and elongation of 16% can still be maintained. The fracture
morphology shows that the dimples disappear and dynamic recrystallization occurs at 1600°C and
that the grain grows further at 2000°C. The analysis shows that with increasing temperature, the
increasing grain size and decreasing grain adhesion lead to decreasing strength and plasticity at
2000 °C.
1. Introduction
Tungsten rhenium alloy has the advantages of high melting point, high hardness, high strength, high plasticity,
high recrystallization temperature, high resistivity, high thermoelectric potential value and thermoelectric
potential rate, low electron escape work, low plastic brittle transition temperature, good fatigue resistance, and
thermal shock resistance. It is one of the best structural materials in theﬁeld of high-temperature material. It is
widely used in aerospace engineering, nuclear power, medical treatment, weapons, electronics, and otherﬁelds
[1– 6].
There are two main preparation methods of tungsten rhenium alloy: powder metallurgy and smelting. The
melting method includes vacuum arc melting and vacuum electron beam melting. Vacuum arc melting is a
method of melting metals and alloys in a vacuum environment by using arc heat. Electron beam melting is a
vacuum melting method that converts the kinetic energy of high-speed electron beams into heat energy as a heat
source in a high vacuum. The disadvantages of the vacuum arc melting method are that the melted metal is in the
molten state for a short time and has poor degassing capacity. The advantage is that it can obtain large-size
ingots, involves simple operation, and is suitable for large-scale production. The advantages of the electron beam
melting method are that it obtains ingots with very low impurity content and that the melting speed is easy to
control. The disadvantages are large metal loss and low yield[1]. Ingots made by arc melting and electron beam
melting have a columnar crystal structure, so the billet must be extrudedﬁrst to break the columnar crystal into
ﬁne crystals. This greatly improves the mechanical properties of the alloy and further processability, but the
method’s high production cost limits its wide application.
The preparation process of powder metallurgy involves the preparation of tungsten rhenium alloy powder,
followed by molding(molding or cold isostatic pressing), and then by high-temperature sintering(vertical
melting and medium frequency induction sintering), which is followed by hot deformation processing
(extrusion, forging, rolling, etc). The preparation methods of tungsten rhenium alloy powder include solid-solid
methods, solid-liquid methods, and liquid-liquid methods. The composition, distribution, and uniformity of

The preparation of tungsten rhenium alloy by powder metallurgy can avoid component segregation, and the
microstructure and grains areﬁne [5, 7]. The obtained alloy has a uniform microstructure, stable properties,
simple processing, and low cost. Therefore, the powder metallurgy method is usually used to prepare tungsten
rhenium alloy in the production process.
Due to the high price of rhenium, tungsten rhenium alloy is generally made into wire. The general processing
method involves vertical melting, bar rotary forging, and wire drawing, but there is less research on the
processing technology and performance of large-scale high-performance structural parts. With the
development of high-temperature application technology, the demand for large-scale high-performance
tungsten rhenium alloy structural parts is becoming more and more urgent. At present, the research on tungsten
rhenium alloy mainly focuses on low rhenium plus carbide/oxide, [8– 11] while there is less research on high
rhenium tungsten alloy[12], but the deformation processing of low rhenium carbide tungsten alloy is difﬁcult,
while high rhenium tungsten alloy is not only easy to carry out deformation processing but also has higher high-
temperature strength. Therefore, In this paper, the W25Re alloy bar was prepared by powder metallurgy
combined with the rolling deformation process, and its high-temperature tensile properties were studied. A
large size and high-performance tungsten rhenium alloy bar were prepared.
2. Experimental materials and methods
2.1. Blank preparation
In the test, tungsten powder and ground high-purity rhenium acid ammonia were proportioned according to
the composition ratio of tungsten rhenium alloy and fully mixed in a three-dimensional mixer. W25Re powder
was prepared after hydrogen reduction at 400°C and 850°C. The prepared powder was pressed by cold isostatic
pressing equipment under 200 MPa of pressure, which was maintained for 10 min to prepare the compact. It
was then sintered in a medium frequency induction furnace, according to the sintering system, at 2300°C for
4 h. The density of aj54 mm billet, measured by the Archimedes drainage method, was 18.30 g cm
−3. The
sintered blank was heated to 1550°C in a hydrogen-protected molybdenum wire furnace and kept hot for
40 min. After rolling on 12 stands on Type 55ﬂat and vertical roll mill, the total deformation was 75%. This
yielded a j27 mm alloy rolled bar blank. Finally, the bar blank was annealed at 1100°C and for one hour to
eliminate stress.
2.2. Test method
Metallographic samples were cut from the sintered and annealed materials to observe the microstructure.j5 *
5 mm metallographic samples of theﬁred billet were cut at the end of the billet. 6* 6 * 10 mm metallographic
samples were cut from annealed material along the rolling direction. After grinding and polishing of the
metallographic sample, the sample was corroded by solution with a 50 ml corrosive agent mixed with 6 g
potassium ferricyanide, 4 g sodium hydroxide, and water. The microstructure of sintered and annealed samples
was observed on an OLYMPUS GX51 metallographic microscope, and the average grain size of the sintered state
was measured using the cut-off point method with Image-Pro software. On the annealed W25Re alloy rolled bar
blank, tensile samples were taken at the outer circle, 1/2 R, and center along the rolling direction. The schematic
diagram of sampling position and tensile samples are shown inﬁgure 1 and 2, respectively.
Room temperature and high-temperature tensile tests were carried out on an MTS E45.105 electronic
universal testing machine and a ZKWD-50 vacuum high-temperature electronic universal testing machine,
respectively, with a tensile speed of 2 mm min
−1. The experimental temperatures were room temperature, 1000,
1300, 1600, 1800, and 2000°C. Three tensile specimens were cut at each experimental temperature for tensile
testing. The tensile strength and elongation of samples at different temperatures were measured, and the results
of the three samples at each temperature were averaged to characterize their room temperature and high-
temperature mechanical properties. The tensile fracture morphology was observed using an FEI Nova Nano
SEM 450 high-resolutionﬁeld emission scanning electron microscope.

3.2. Mechanical properties
The macroscopic mechanical properties of materials are the main basis for the selection of materials in the
design of various engineering structures. This test mainly tested the hardness,ﬂexural strength, and tensile
strength of W25Re alloy.
Hardness is an important performance index for comparing various metallic materials. It can be understood
as the ability of materials to resist residual deformation and hard objects pressed into their surface. Hardness can
also be expressed as the ability of materials to resist elastic deformation, plastic deformation, or failure. Due to
the ‘rhenium effect’, the Vickers hardness of tungsten rhenium alloy is nearly 50% higher than that of pure
tungsten. According to GB/t4340 1-2009 =Vickers hardness test of metals Part 1: Test methods?, Vickers
hardness tests of tungsten rhenium alloy were carried out. The results compared with pure tungsten are shown
in table 1.
Tensile strength is the maximum tensile stress that a material can bear before tensile fracture, which reﬂects
the fracture resistance of the material. The high-temperature tensile stress-strain curve of W25Re alloy is shown
in ﬁgure 5. It can be seen from theﬁgure that at the initial stage of strain, the stress of the W25Re sample increases
rapidly with the increase of strain. During this stage, the sample is effectively undergoing elastic deformation.
The stress increases slowly to a peak value with the strain and decreases slowly after reaching the peak. As
temperature increases, the strain required for the sample to reach the peak stress decreases gradually. The results
show that the higher the temperature, the easier the sample is to recrystallize.
It can be seen from the tensile test data inﬁgure 6 that the rolled tungsten rhenium alloy has excellent tensile
strength and elongation after a fracture. The tensile strength at room temperature is as high as 1650 MPa and the
elongation after fracture is 15%. Even at 2000°C, the tensile strength is still more than 200 MPa, and the
elongation after fracture is over 16%. The excellent mechanical properties of rolled W25Re alloy show great
future potential in high-temperature environments.

process is dynamic recovery. Work hardening and dynamic recovery occurred in W25Re alloy at 1000°C and
1300 °C, so a large number of dimples were observed in the fracture.

It can be seen fromﬁgure 8 that the fracture at room temperature is mainly transgranular because the
strength of the grain boundary at room temperature is much higher than that in the grain. Thus, the crack will
expand inside the grain rather than along the grain boundary. However, when the temperature rises, the grain
boundary is weakened. Cracks that originally propagated along the grain boundary instead expand at the grain
boundary. Coupled with the segregation of impurity elements at the grain boundary, the alloy begins to undergo
intergranular fracture. As shown inﬁgures 9 and 10, W25Re alloy has an intergranular fracture at 1000°C and
1300 °C, and a large number of dimples appear on the fracture surface. The toughness continues to improve.
It can be seen from the mechanical property test results inﬁgure 6 that when the temperature rises to
2000 °C, the strength and toughness decrease signiﬁcantly, which is mainly related to the grain boundary
adhesion. The grain boundary is relatively loose relative to the arrangement of atoms in the crystal, which is a
solid interface material[14, 15]. Briant and Messmer[16– 18] ﬁrst revealed the electronic states and binding
properties of grain boundaries by quantum mechanical methods. With the increase of temperature and the
inﬂuence of thermal movement, the charge in the atom will transfer, weakening the chemical bond between
metal atoms. The weakened chemical bond is easy to break under the action of external force. Therefore, the
atomic binding force decreases gradually with the increase of temperature, and the grain grows continuously
with the increase of temperature, which is consistent with the fracture morphology of W25Re at 2000°C. This is
the reason why the elongation decreases signiﬁcantly at 2000°C. However, compared with pure tungsten, the
grain size is still small, so W25Re still has a strength of 200 MPa and an elongation of 16% at 2000°C.
4. Conclusion and prospects
Through the trial production of W25Re alloy bars and the analysis of their mechanical properties and fracture
morphology, the following conclusions are drawn:
(1) Rolled W25Re alloy bar has excellent mechanical properties. The tensile strength at room temperature is up
to 1650 MPa and the elongation after fracture is 15%. Although the tensile strength of W25Re alloy
decreases rapidly with increasing temperature, the tensile strength still reaches more than 200 MPa at
2000 °C, and the elongation after fracture remains at 16%. With increasing temperature, the elongation
after fracture of W25Re alloyﬁrst increases then decreases.
(2) The results of fracture analysis show that when the temperature is less than 1300 °C, dimples gradually
appear on the fracture surface. With increasing temperature, the dimple area, size, and depth increase.
When the temperature continues to rise to 1600°C, the dimples disappear, the grains recrystallize and the
recrystallized grain size is small. When the temperature is about 2000°C, the grains grow further, and the
plasticity decreases, though it remains slightly above that found at room temperature.
(3) W25Re alloy has excellent high-temperature mechanical properties. Research on second phase particle
strengthening can further improve the comprehensive mechanical properties of the alloy and expand the
high-temperature applications of W-Re alloy.
