ABSTRACT 
In this study, an AlSi10Cu4Mg alloy was prepared by selective laser melting (SLM) using a mixture of pre-alloyed 
AlSi10Mg and elemental Cu powders, followed by aging at 180 ℃ for 2 h. The high-temperature tensile prop -
erties of both the SLMed and SLM-aged samples were tested in the temperature range of 200 – 400 ℃ , and the 
microstructure changes during deformation were compared. At the temperature of 200 ℃ , the SLMed sample 
exhibits outstanding tensile properties with an ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of 327 ± 2 MPa and an elongation 
(EL) of 25.5 ± 0.5 %. This is mainly related to the strain hardening that caused by the accumulation of anomalous 
dislocations near the high-volume fraction of Si nano-particles within the grains. For the SLM-aged sample, the 
aging treatment is found to further increase the strength, yielding a UTS of 381 ± 3 MPa. The metastable tran -
sition of the Al
2
Cu phase indirectly promotes the precipitation of the nano-sized Si phase during the aging 
process. As the testing temperature increases, the strength increment of the SLM-aged sample gradually di -
minishes, while the elongation increment increases. Due to the limited intrinsic thermal stability of Si nano- 
particles and θ ′ phase, the coarsening of the precipitates can weaken the strengthening effect. The dynamic 
recrystallization process can be accelerated by the aging treatment, thereby helping to improve the elongation. 
1. Introduction 
Selective laser melting (SLM) is an important additive manufacturing 
(AM) technology that has gained increasing prominence in recent years 
to produce aluminum alloys with superior mechanical properties [1 – 3] . 
Sub-eutectic AlSi10Mg alloy is extensively used in industries such as 
aerospace and automotive due to its excellent corrosion resistance, low 
thermal expansion, and high specific strength [4] . SLMed AlSi10Mg 
alloy has good formability and mechanical properties at room temper -
ature (i.e. relative density of 99.4 %, ultimate tensile strength of 
471.3 MPa and elongation of 9.6 % [5] ). However, previous studies have 
shown that coarsening of Si particles at high temperatures can nega -
tively affect the mechanical properties of the SLMed AlSi10Mg alloy [1, 
6 – 8] . Consequently, the main application of the SLMed AlSi10Mg alloy 
is generally limited to environments below 150 ℃ [9] . The continuous 
advancement of today ’ s industry places greater demands on the latest 
iteration of aluminum alloys, including enhanced heat resistance, faster 
forming, and greater strength [10] . Improving the heat resistance of the 
SLMed AlSi10Mg alloy is therefore is crucial to broaden its applicability 
in high-temperature environments. 
In recent years, several methods have been employed to enhance the 
high-temperature mechanical properties of aluminum (Al) alloys, such 
as adding nanoparticles, alloying, and heat treatment optimization 
[12 – 15] . Unfortunately, the reinforcement effect achieved through 
these methods remains somewhat limited. Copper (Cu) has emerged as a 
particularly promising element for improving the high-temperature 
properties of Al alloys due to its low price and its susceptibility to the 
formation of a wide range of heat-resistant nanoparticles by precipita -
tion hardening techniques [11,16 – 19] . These precipitates reported in 
previous studies include T phase (Al

were thoroughly discussed, which can provide insight for optimizing the 
high-temperature mechanical properties of AlSi10Mg alloy. 
2. Experimental procedures 
The gas atomized AlSi10Mg alloy powders and fine pure Cu powders 
with particle sizes of 25 – 55 μ m and 1 – 3 μ m, respectively, were used in 
this study. The chemical composition of the AlSi10Mg alloy powders is 
as follows: Al-10.2 Si-0.31 Mg-0.06 Fe (wt%). The blended powders 
consist of 96 wt% AlSi10Mg alloy powders and 4 wt% Cu powders. To 
ensure homogeneous mixing and sufficient fluidity, the two powders 
were loaded into a three-dimensional mixer (JXHY-2 L) with a stirring 
speed of 65 r/min and a total time of 70 min, which is the best mixing 
parameters for optimizing the stirring speed and mixing time in our 
previous work. Fig. 1 shows the morphology of the blended powders.

AlSi10Mg alloy powders. The block samples were fabricated using an 
EPLUS-3D-M150 metal printer in an argon atmosphere (oxygen content

3.2. High-temperature tensile property and fracture surface 
Fig. 4 a shows the high-temperature engineering stress-strain curves 
of both the SLMed and SLM-aged samples in the temperature range of 
200 – 400 ℃ . At 200 ℃ , the SLMed sample exhibits a yield strength (YS) 
of 259 ± 1 MPa, an ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of 325 ± 2 MPa, and an 
elongation (EL) of 25.5 ± 0.2 %. The sample that underwent aging shows 
a notable 24.32 % increase in YS to 322 ± 1 MPa and a 17.23 % increase 
in UTS to 381 ± 4 MPa. However, there is a corresponding 34.5 % 
reduction in EL, as shown in Fig. 4 b. The strain hardening of both 
samples gradually decreases as the testing temperature rises. The 
strength increment of the SLM-aged sample also decreases while the 
elongation increment increases. It is evident that aging has a plasticizing 
effect without compromising strength at elevated temperatures. In 
Fig. 4 c, a summary of the high-temperature tensile properties of various 
AM Al alloys and several typical cast Al alloys is presented [1,2,38 – 46] . 
The UTS of the SLM-aged sample at 200 ℃ is much higher than that of 
other Al alloys which are all below 300 MPa. Overall, the current sam -
ples show superior performance at testing temperatures ranging from 
200 to 300 ℃ . 
Fig. 4 d-k displays the fracture morphology of the samples at various 
testing temperatures. Both the SLMed and SLM-aged samples exhibit 
ductile fractures at all testing temperatures. At 400 ℃ , larger equiaxial 
dimples appear, accompanied by the presence of a small number of 
micron-sized Si particles within the dimples (details are highlighted by 
the red circles). This observation suggests stress concentration around 
the Si particles, rendering the cracks more susceptible to propagation 
and connection [47 – 49,51] . 
3.3. Correlation between microstructure and high-temperature tensile 
property 
3.3.1. Tested at 200 ℃ 
The deformed microstructures near the fracture for both the SLMed 
and SLM-aged samples tested at 200 ℃ are given in Fig. 5 . In both 
samples, the eutectic networks are disrupted, and micron-sized Si par -
ticles are observed to precipitate. Fig. 6 a and d show the corresponding 
IPF maps of the deformed microstructures. The average grain size of the 
SLMed-aged sample is relatively small ( Fig. 6 g). Geometrically neces -
sary dislocations (GNDs) exhibit inhomogeneous distribution during hot 
deformation, as shown in Fig. 6 b and e. The high density of GNDs in the 
grain interiors of both samples indicates a significant accumulation of 
internal stress. However, previous reports have suggested that regions 
with high GND density are typically found near interfaces rather than 
within grain interiors [53] . In the plastic deformation of polycrystalline 
alloys, when the misorientation of some neighboring grains is not suit -
able for glide of dislocations, the moving dislocations will be difficult to 
traverse at the interfaces, and the blocking stresses cause the grains to 
rotate, and the altered misorientation allows the deformation to be 
further transmitted [52] . Similarly, in Al-Si alloys, due to the different 
elastic moduli of the α (Al) and Si phases, the interactions between dis -
locations and Si nano-particles in the grain interiors will also lead to 
relative rotation of Si particles with respect to the Al matrix during hot 
deformation, and hence the GNDs will be generated at the grain interiors 
to accommodate this deformation [53] . Specifically, the formation of 
GNDs is attributed to the uneven strain distribution. During deforma -
tion, the softer domains in the vicinity of Si nano-particles in the grain 
interiors undergo greater plastic strains, creating a long-range stress 
field known as back stress. With the development of the back stress, 
forward stress induced by the plugging of the GNDs appear in the hard 
domains. The two forces partially offset each other during the defor -
mation process to maintain the stress equilibrium [54] . On the other 
hand, this unique heterogeneous structure not only increases the critical 
resolved shear stress for dislocation slip near Si nano-particles, but also 
serves as an effective and sustainable source of dislocations at higher 
stresses [55] . As Orowan rings accumulate in the grain interiors, Si 
nano-particles will also further reduce the spacing, making it increas -
ingly challenging for dislocations between neighboring Si particles to be 
dislodged [56] . The strain hardening observed at this temperature may 
be due to the anomalous pile-up of GNDs near the high-volume fraction

orientation between the particles and the matrix grains prompts GNDs

onset of slip is often accompanied by grain rotation, which leads to the 
distribution of aggregates of individual grain orientations near one or 
some orientation positions, resulting in the formation of different types 
of texture [55] . Fig. 7 a and b show the Orientation Distribution Function

its rheological direction), and the S {123} < 634 > texture content in -

attributed to grain boundary migration, and the activation of slip sys -
tems in the {110} plane facilitates the substable transition of Cube ori -

samples are affected by various factors, including the motion of GNDs 
within grain interiors ( Fig. 6 ) and the migration of grain boundaries

The rheological curves demonstrate that both samples exhibit sig -

Cu-rich Si particles precipitate and coarsen, displaying distinctive 
morphology (confirmed by the corresponding EDS maps revealing 
bright white Cu-rich Si particles). The Si particles in the SLM-aged

and the formation of DRX nuclei in turn reduces the dislocation density.

the sample. Thus, the aging treatment facilitates the DRX grain nucle -

the elongation of the sample by accelerating the DRX process.

4. Summary 
In this study, the high-temperature tensile properties of both SLMed 
and SLM-aged AlSi10Cu4Mg alloys were tested within the temperature 
range of 200 – 400 ℃ , and the microstructural changes during defor -
mation was analyzed. The following conclusions were drawn: 
(1) The SLMed alloy exhibits a distinctive eutectic network. After 
aging treatment, the Si nano-particles and Cu-rich phases are 
diffusely distributed in the matrix. 
(2) At 200 ℃ , the SLMed alloy exhibits excellent high-temperature 
tensile properties (UTS 327 ± 2 MPa, El 25.5 ± 0.5 %), and the 
aging treatment further increases the strength (UTS 381 ± 3 MPa). 
However, as the test temperature increases, the strain hardening 
phenomenon gradually diminishes in both alloys, and the 
strength increment of the SLM-aged sample also gradually 
decreases. 
(3) The accumulation of anomalous dislocations in the vicinity of the 
high volume fraction of Si nano-particles in the grain interiors 
promotes the development of GNDs, contributing to strain 
hardening at 200 
◦
C. This unique heterogeneous structure not 
only increases the critical resolved shear stress for dislocation slip 
in the vicinity of Si nano-particles, but also makes it an effective 
and sustainable source of dislocations at higher stresses. The 
strength of the alloys is further improved by the aging treatment 
due to the substable transition of the Al
2
Cu phase and the pre -
cipitation of the Si nano-particles. 
(4) The SLMed alloy is found to have predominantly Cube {001} <
100 > , Copper {112} < 111 > , and P {011} < 211 > texture types. 
While in the SLM-aged alloy, the content of the S {123} < 634 >
texture notably increases, becoming one of the main texture 
types. 
(5) The strengthening effect weakens at higher testing temperatures 
due to the coarsening of the Si nano-particles and θ ′ phases. 
Additionally, the α -Al matrix exhibits softening characteristics of 
CDRX. The DRX process is accelerated by the aging treatment, 
resulting in an increased elongation.
