abstract
A new micromechanical model based on the equivalent inclusion method has been developed to study
the directional coarsening behavior in Ni-based superalloy single crystals. The von Mises stress, elastic
strain energy density and hydrostatic pressure in different matrix channels are calculated when the
external stress is applied along the [0 0 1], [1 1 0] and [1 1 1] directions, respectively. The calculated results
are used to predict the directional coarsening behavior and raft morphology. The effects of the misﬁt and
the external stress on the rafting behavior are carefully discussed.

1. Introduction
Ni-based superalloys have been an inevitable material for the
blades and vane applications in gas turbines, owing to their excel-
lent creep resistance behavior and mechanical properties. It is
strengthened by a high volume fraction of hard cubical c0 precipi-
tates embedded coherently in a softer c matrix. When superalloys
are subjected to creep at high temperatures, typically above
1123 K, the c0 particles coarsen directionally to form rafts generally
aligned along the [0 0 1] direction. The direction and morphology of
these rafts depend on several parameters such as the signs of the
misﬁt between c and c
0 phases and applied creep stress [1,2].
Directional coarsening of c0 precipitates under the inﬂuence of
an applied stress was ﬁrst studied in detail by Tien and Copley
[2]. Subsequently, the directional coarsening behavior and its inﬂu-
ence on the creep resistance of superalloys have been widely ob-
served by experiments [3–5]. Moreover, some theoretical models
[6–10] have been proposed to describe the directional coarsening
behavior at high temperatures. For example, Bufﬁere and Ignat
[9] proposed a dislocation-based criterion model, which suggested
a possibly microscopic mechanism of coarsening involving disloca-
tions. Yue et al. [10] developed a rafting prediction criterion based
on the relief of the interfacial energy density by dislocation gener-
ation at c=c
0 interface of Ni-superalloys under multiaxial stresses.
In addition, Socrate and Parks [11] developed a general methodol-
ogy in the framework of the ﬁnite element method to study direc-
tional coarsening of c
0 precipitates in Ni-based superalloys. Besides,
the stresses and strain energy density distributions in Ni-based
superalloys by ﬁnite element computations have been applied for
describing and predicting the raft morphology [12,13].
Furthermore, we notice that the micromechanical model has
been successfully applied to describe the rafting that occurs in
the Ni-based superalloys. The inclusion theory with anisotropic
elasticity proposed by Ratel et al. [14] has been used to evaluate
the stress state and the associated elastic energy caused by a
change in the shape of the c
0 precipitates, and predict correctly
the directional coarsening of the c0 precipitates and evolution mor-
phology in the case of a creep stress applied along the [0 0 1] direc-
tion. Generally, the equivalent inclusion method is limited to
application for a material system in which the precipitates exist
with a low volume fraction in an isolated style [15]. However,
the current Ni-based superalloys contain c0 precipitates with a very
high volume fraction. Thus in the present paper, we will develop a
new micromechanical model based on Eshelby’s equivalent inclu-
sion theory to predict the rafting behavior. The cubical inclusion
embedded in the c matrix is considered in our model, which is sim-
ilar to the actual structure in the Ni-based superalloys. The effect of
the misﬁt (positive or negative), the external loads (tensile or com-
pressive) and the creep stress direction (along the [0 0 1], [1 1 0] and
[1 1 1] directions, respectively) on the directional coarsening and
rafting behavior will be carefully discussed.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. The effect of the misﬁt
Fig. 2 shows the variation of the von Mises stress (Fig. 2 a), elas-
tic strain energy density ( Fig. 2b) and hydrostatic pressure (Fig. 2 c)
in the inclusions with the external tensile stress. Two different
cases with misﬁts d ¼/C0 0:38% and d ¼ 0:56% are selected. When
the external stress is zero, we can ﬁnd clearly in Fig. 2 that all these
three values in inclusion I equal to which in inclusion II for both the
negative and positive misﬁt cases. But the values of the von Mises
stress and elastic strain energy density with a positive misﬁt
d ¼ 0:56% are lager than those with a negative misﬁt
d ¼/C0 0:38%. The hydrostatic pressure is tensile for a positive misﬁt
d ¼ 0:56%, and is compressive for a negative misﬁt d ¼/C0 0:38%.
When a tensile stress is applied, the von Mises stress, elastic
strain energy density and hydrostatic pressure both in inclusion I
(horizontal matrix channel) and inclusion II (vertical matrix chan-
nel) change with the external stress. For example, when a tensile
stress is applied to a negative misﬁt alloy ðd ¼/C0 0:38%Þ along the
[0 0 1] direction, we can observe in Fig. 2a and b that the von Mises
stress and elastic strain energy density increase in inclusion I, but
decrease in inclusion II with the external tensile stress increasing.
Thus the von Mises stress and elastic strain energy density in inclu-
sion I become larger than those in inclusion II. In Fig. 2 c, we ob-
serve that the hydrostatic compressive pressure increase with
the external tensile stress increasing, and the absolute value of it
in inclusion I is lager than that in inclusion II. Moreover, when a
tensile stress is applied to a positive misﬁt alloy ðd ¼ 0:56%Þ along
the [0 0 1] direction, the situation is the reverse.
Due to the higher von Mises stress and elastic strain energy
density in
inclusion I when a tensile stress is applied to a negative
misﬁt alloy, the mixed dislocations would generate and distribute
predominantly in that region. As a consequence, the width of inclu-
sion I would increase, while the width of inclusion II decreases. The
rafting direction is vertical to the [0 0 1] stress axes (horizontal ma-
trix channel). Contrarily, when a tensile stress is applied to an alloy
with a positive misﬁt, the width of inclusion I decrease, while
width of the inclusion II increases. The rafting direction is parallel
to the [0 0 1] stress axes (vertical matrix channel).
Furthermore, we ﬁnd that the compressive stress in inclusion I
is larger than that in inclusion II when an external tensile stress is
applied to a negative misﬁt superalloy. This results in a larger con-
traction of the crystal lattice in inclusion I. Therefore, a part of the
Cr and Co atoms with smaller atomic radii which is originally in the
matrix phase will diffuse from inclusion II to inclusion I. Mean-
while, the elements Al and Ta with larger atomic radii originally
in the precipitate phase will diffuse to the regions with bigger lat-
tice parameters. As a consequence, the width of inclusion I would
increase during the creep deformation, while the width of inclu-
sion II decreases. We can surmise that the rafting direction is ver-
tical to the [0 0 1] stress axes (horizontal matrix channel) in this
condition. Contrarily, for a positive misﬁt superalloy, the resulting
rafting direction is parallel to the [0 0 1] stress axes (vertical matrix
channel).
3.2. The effect of the external stress (tensile or compressive)
In this section, we discuss the variation of the von Mises stress,
elastic strain energy density and hydrostatic pressure when an
external tensile or compressive stress is applied. We ﬁnd that the
application of the external stress (tensile or compressive) leads
to differential values of the von Mises stress, elastic strain energy
density and hydrostatic pressure both in inclusion I and inclusion
II. In Section 3.1, we have discussed the case when a tensile stress
is applied to a negative misﬁt alloy along the [0 0 1] direction. How-
ever, when a compressive stress is applied, the situation is the re-
verse. As shown in Fig. 3 a and b, the von Mises stress and elastic
strain energy density in inclusion I decrease with the compressive
stress increasing, and increases in inclusion II. Thus the von Mises
stress and elastic strain energy density in the inclusion II are larger
than those in inclusion I when a compressive stress is applied.
Moreover, the hydrostatic pressure of inclusion I and inclusion II
both increase with the external stress increasing, and the values
of the compressive stress in inclusion II are always larger than
those in inclusion I. Based on above results, we can predict that
when an external tensile stress is applied for a negative misﬁt
superalloy, the width of inclusion I would increase due to the high-
er von Mises stress, elastic strain energy density and hydrostatic
pressure in inclusion I. Meanwhile, the width of inclusion II de-
creases. The rafting direction is vertical to the [0 0 1] stress axes
(horizontal matrix channel). However, when a compressive stress
is applied, the width of inclusion II would increase, while the width
of inclusion I decrease. The rafting direction is parallel to the [0 0 1]
stress axes (vertical matrix channel).

values of the von Mises stress, elastic strain energy density and

However, we notice that the application of the external tensile
stress along the [1 1 1] direction leads to identical values of the von
Mises stress, elastic strain energy density and hydrostatic pressure
in inclusion I and inclusion II. As seen in Fig. 5 a–c when a tensile
stress is applied along the [1 1 1] direction, the values of the von
Mises stress, elastic strain energy density in inclusion I and inclu-
sion II almost keep invariant with the external stress increasing;
the hydrostatic pressure in inclusion I and inclusion II decrease
with the external stress increasing. Moreover, these three values
of inclusion I and inclusion II are always equal when the external
tensile stress is applied along the [1 1 1] direction.
Moreover, we observe a non-linear dependence of the von
Mises and the hydrostatic stress on the external stress as shown
in Figs. 2, 3 and 5 . As we have known that the model is based on
linear theory of elasticity, a linear relationship between external
load and averaged stresses is expected. This non-linear behavior
is closely related with characteristics of the lattice misﬁt. For
example, the misﬁt stress in inclusion II (the vertical matrix) is
compressive for a negative misﬁt alloy. When the external tensile
stress is applied along [0 0 1] direction, the misﬁt stress in inclusion
II and external stress weaken each other. At ﬁrst, the von Mises
stress decreases with the external tensile stress increasing. After
the external stress completely overcomes the impact of misﬁt
stress, the von Mises stress will increase with the external tensile
stress increasing. Therefore, a non-linear relationship between
the von Mises stress and external stress is observed in inclusion II.
From above results, we can conclude that the application of the
external stresses along the [0 0 1] and [1 1 0] directions lead to dif-
ferential values of the von Mises stress, elastic strain energy den-
sity and hydrostatic pressure in inclusion I and inclusion II. It
causes the directional coarsening in Ni-based superalloys. The ori-
entation of the directional coarsening depends on the sign of the
misﬁt and the type of the external stress (tensile or compressive).
When an external stress along [1 1 1] direction is applied, the calcu-
lated values in inclusion I are always equals to those in inclusion II.
Therefore, there is no preferential direction of coarsening. The raft-
ing is difﬁcult to occur when an external stress is applied along the
[1 1 1] direction. These results are in good agreement with the pre-
diction of the dislocation model proposed by Bufﬁere and Ignat [9]
and a rafting prediction criterion developed by Yue et al. [10].
4. Conclusions
We have developed a new micromechanical model based on the
equivalent inclusion method to study the directional coarsening
behavior in Ni-based superalloy single crystals. In this model, the
cubical c
0 precipitate with the volume fraction of 70% is embedded
in the c matrix, which is similar to the actual structure in the Ni-
based superalloys. Then we take the c0 precipitate as the matrix,
and the c matrix as the inclusion in our calculation. The von Mises
stress, elastic strain energy density and hydrostatic pressure of the
inclusions (matrix of original material) are calculated when the
external stresses are applied along the [0 0 1], [1 1 0] and [1 1 1]
directions, respectively.
The calculated results indicate that the directional coarsening of
c0 precipitates occurs when the external stress is applied along the
[0 0 1] and [1 1 0] directions, respectively. The rafting direction de-
pends on the sign of the misﬁt (positive or negative) and the type
of the external stress (tensile or compressive). However, the direc-
tional coarsening become difﬁcult to occur when the external
stress is applied along the [1 1 1] direction. This result agrees well
with previous theoretical predictions and experimental
observations.
