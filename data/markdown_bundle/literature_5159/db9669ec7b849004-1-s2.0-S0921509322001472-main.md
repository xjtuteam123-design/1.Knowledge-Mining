ABSTRACT 
To investigate the effect of the γ
′
phase morphology of cobalt-based superalloys on the plastic deformation 
behavior of the superalloys, Co-8.8Al-9.8W-2X (X = Ta, Ni) alloys were prepared to obtain superalloys with 
different morphologies of γ
′
phases. The microstructure and room temperature compression behavior of super -
alloys were studied by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM). The 
results showed that the γ
′
phase particles of 2Ta alloy are cubic, while that of 2Ni alloy is spherical. The 
compressive strength of 2Ta and 2Ni alloys are almost the same, but the hardness of 2Ta alloy is higher than that 
of 2Ni alloy, while the plastic deformability is significantly worse. 2Ta alloy exhibits quasi-cleavage fracture 
during compression, while 2Ni alloy showed a cleavage fracture, and amount of slip bands were found easily on 
the fracture surface of 2Ni alloy. The deformations of 2Ni alloy and 2Ta alloy were dominated by dislocations 
shearing γ
′
phase particles. The γ
′
phase of the 2Ni alloy underwent rafting, while that of 2Ta alloy did not 
change significantly. 
1. Introduction 
Traditional Co-base superalloys mainly depend on solid solution 
hardening and carbide strengthening, so their high-temperature 
strength and temperature-bearing capacity are lower than Ni-base su -
peralloys strengthened by γ
′
-Ni
3
Al phase with L1
2 
structure, which 
limits its application under high temperature conditions. In 2006, Sato 
et al. [ 1 – 3 ] discovered a new γ
′

3

structure of γ

system. Since the solidus and liquidus of Co-base superalloys are about 
100 – 150 
◦

Co-base superalloys for Ni-base superalloys is promoted. 
In the further investigation of the novel Co-base superalloys, it is 
found that similar to the Ni-based superalloys, the lattice misfit between 
γ and γ
′
is the driving force for the growth and coarsening of the γ
′
phase 
particles, and can control the morphology of the γ
′
phase. In Co – Al – W- 
based superalloys, lattice dislocations will increase with increasing W 
content (within 10 at.%) and the morphology of the γ
′
phase is main -
tained as cubic [ 5 , 6 ]. On the contrary, when the W content exceeds 10 
at.%, the lattice mismatch starts to decrease and the γ
′
phase starts to 
spheroidize. Domestic and foreign scholars have found that the addition 
of Ni, Mo and Cr will reduce the content of W in γ
′
phase [ 2 , 7 – 11 ], so the 
lattice misfit is decreased and the γ
′
phase transform from cubic to 
spherical. The addition of Cr will even destroy the γ / γ
′
two-phase 
structure, while the addition of Ta and Ti will replace Al or W in γ
′

morphology highly cubic. At present, the research on the plastic defor -
mation of Co-base superalloys focuses on the influence of the addition of 
alloying elements on the creep and tensile properties of superalloys. The 
yield strength and creep resistance of superalloys were improved by 
doping Cr, Ta and Ti [ 12 , 13 , 16 – 18 ], while the addition of Mo and Ce 
would reduce the yield strength and elongation of superalloys [ 19 – 21 ]. 
In the current research of superalloys deformation, the shear process of 
dislocations could be stopped at the γ / γ
′
interface 4]. From room tem -
perature to 900 
◦
C, the deformation is dominated by dislocations 
shearing γ

◦

dislocations bypassing γ

particles [ 22 ]. During the γ
′
phase was sheared 
process, planar faults, such as antiphase boundaries (APBs) and super -
lattice intrinsic stacking faults (SISFs) and extrinsic stacking faults 
(SESFs), or complex stacking faults (CSFs) and twins, can also form and 
exert great influence on the deformation behavior [ 23 ].

2. Experimental 
The experimental alloy is Co-8.8Al-9.8W-2X (X = Ta, Ni) and the 
nominal and actual compositions of the experimental alloy are listed in 
Table 1 . The alloy ingots were prepared from powder in VHF1 vacuum 
non-consumable arc furnace (protected by Ar). The ingots, encapsulated 
in quartz tubes back-filled with Ar gas, were solution-treated at 1300 
◦
C 
for 4h in a vacuum (water cooling), and then, the alloy ingots were aged 
at 900 
◦
C for 50h (air cooling). The γ / γ
′
microstructure and fracture 
surface of the alloy samples after heat treatment were analyzed using a 
scanning electron microscope (SEM). X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis 
was performed on the bulk samples to identify the phase constitutions. 
The microhardness of the alloy was measured by HXZ-100 microhard -
ness tester, and the average value of 15 points of each alloy were 
measured and was taken as the microhardness. The compression tests 
were performed on a WDW-100D universal testing machine. The 
compression samples were the cylindrical( Ф 4 × 8 mm) sample and the 
compression rate was 0.001s
 1
. The average value of five tests was taken 
as the compressive strength of each combination of alloys. After 
compression deformation, the alloy samples were cut into discs with 
~30 μ m in thickness before final thinning by twin jet polishing at 30 
◦
C 
in a solution of 10% perchloric acid and 90% ethanol (vol%). A FEI Talos 
F200 transmission electron microscope (TEM) was used for dislocations 
analysis. 
3. Results 
3.1. Compositions of alloys 
Table 1 shows the chemical composition of the superalloy samples, 
which were detected and analyzed by ICP-AES. For the convenience of

This may be caused by the burning loss and volatilization of Al in the

3.3. Mechanical properties 
Fig. 3 shows the ultimate strength and microhardness of Co-8.8Al- 
9.8W, 2Ta and 2Ni alloys. All three alloys maintain a high level of 
compressive strength, with the maximum difference not exceeding 5%, 
indicating that the morphology of the γ
′
phase has almost no effect on 
the compressive strength of the alloy. By comparing the hardness of the 
alloys, it was found that the 2Ta alloy with a highly cubic γ
′

significantly harder than the 2Ni alloy with a spherical γ
′
phase. The 
previous investigation [ 24 ] found that the higher the volume fraction 
and cuboidal degree of the γ

Fig. 4 shows the engineering stress-strain curves of Co-8.8Al-9.8W, 2Ta 
and 2Ni alloys. In the initial stage of deformation, the alloy undergoes 
obvious elastic deformation and the stress-strain curve of Co-8.8Al-9.8W 
alloy shows an obvious inflection point at b, the yield point, which is 
about 4%. The yield point of the 2Ni alloy is about 2.5%, while the 2Ta 
alloy does not show an obvious yield point. In addition, the 
Co-8.8Al-9.8W alloy fractured at a strain of 39.9%, the 2Ni alloy frac -
tured at 41.7%, and the 2Ta alloy was the least plastic, fracturing at a 
strain of only 36.7%. This indicates that the 2Ni alloy with a spherical γ
′
phase also shows significantly more plastic deformability than the 2Ta 
alloy with a highly cubic γ
′
phase.

4. Discussion 
The lattice misfit of γ / γ
′
is the driving force for the growth and 
coarsening of γ
′
particles, which is the main factor controlling the 
morphology of γ
′
phase [ 30 ]. Ta as a γ
′
phase forming element can in -
crease the lattice misfit and lead to the precipitation of cubic γ
′
phase 
[ 31 ]. In contrast, the addition of Ni leads to a decrease of W in the γ
′
phase, which is the main reason for the decrease of lattice mismatch and 
the transformation of the γ
′
phase from cubic to spherical [ 32 , 33 ]. It has 
been reported that the hardness of the alloy is closely related to the 
volume fraction and cubicity of the γ
′
phase [ 24 ], so the hardness of the 
2Ta alloy with cubic γ
′
phase is significantly higher than that of the 2Ni 
alloy with spherical γ
′
phase. The plastic deformability of the alloy, 
however, shows a trend opposite to the hardness, the 2Ta alloy with 
cubic γ
′
phase having less plasticity compared to the 2Ni alloy with 
spherical γ
′
phase. The secondary phases are brittle and hard, which 
cause the increase in hardness and decrease in plasticity of the super -
alloys. Ni can inhibit the formation of secondary phases [ 34 ], while Ta 
promotes the precipitation of secondary phases [ 27 , 28 ], which may be 
one of the reasons for the higher hardness and lower plasticity of the 2Ta 
alloy compared to the 2Ni alloy.

5. Conclusion 
Different morphologies of γ
′
phase were prepared by doping Ta and 
Ni to Co-8.8Al-9.8W alloy, and by studying the strength, hardness and 
dislocation morphology in deformation of the alloy, it was found that: 
1 The γ
′
phase of the 2Ta alloy is highly cubic and with a higher volume 
fraction of 85.4%, while the γ
′
phase of the 2Ni alloy is spherical with 
volume fraction of 80.5%. 
2 The compressive strength of 2Ta and 2Ni alloys are almost the same, 
but the hardness of 2Ta alloy is higher than that of 2Ni alloy, while 
the plastic deformability is significantly worse. 
3 During room temperature compression, the 2Ta alloy exhibits quasi- 
dissociative fracture and the 2Ni alloy is dissociative fracture, and 
amount of slip bands were found easily on the fracture surface of 2Ni 
alloy. 
4 The deformations of 2Ni alloy and 2Ta alloy are dominated by dis -
