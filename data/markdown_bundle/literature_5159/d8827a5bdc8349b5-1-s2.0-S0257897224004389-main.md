ABSTRACT 
In this study, Co-Cr-Ni-W alloy parts were fabricated on the surface of 304 stainless steel (SS) substrate by laser 
directed energy deposition (LDED). Microstructure, microhardness and tribological behavior of as-deposited 
samples and 750 
◦
C – 950 
◦

and martensite transformation from γ -Co (FCC) to ε -Co (HCP) occurred in high temperature. The changes of 
microstructure caused by aging treatment could increase the microhardness and change wear mechanism. 
Tensile property of Co-Cr-Ni-W alloy at 750

sliding structure under high temperature, leading to high tensile strength. The main fracture mode of Co-Cr-Ni-W

1. Introduction 
Some working components need to work at high temperature envi -
ronment (piercing plugs and rollers in producing steel pipes, etc.) under 
stress impact condition in industrial production, and these components 
need strong mechanical properties under high temperature to ensure the 
security and stability. Components can be prepared quickly and effi -
ciently by using the additive manufacturing (AM) technology, which has 
been applied in recent years [ 1 – 3 ]. Laser directed energy deposition 
(LDED) is a kind of additive manufacturing technology. It uses high 
energy laser beam to melt high-property material and deposit the ma -
terial to form deposition layers. Material for deposition can be metal, 
alloy, ceramic or metal matrix composites, and can be in the form of 
powder or wire. This technology is now being applied on various areas 
such as aerospace, automobile, tool industry, and also being used to 
repair damaged surfaces [ 4 , 5 ]. 
Cobalt-based superalloys is a class of alloy which have good prop -
erties such as high temperature resistance, wear resistance, corrosion 
resistance, etc. [ 6 – 8 ]. These alloys can work under the environment of

Two main systems of cobalt-based superalloys are Stellite series, the 
main elements are Co, Cr, W [ 10 – 12 ] and biomedical alloy, the main 
elements are Co, Cr, Mo [ 13 , 14 ]. Cobalt-based alloys have face-centered 
cubic (FCC) structure at room temperature ( γ -Co), martensitic trans -

3.2. Microstructure 
Fig. 4 shows the morphologies of as-deposited samples. Planer grains 
appear at the melting interface. In upper zone, the grains gradually turn 
into cellular grain, columnar dendrites and equiaxed grain. The main 
factor of grain change is the ratio of temperature gradient (G) and 
cooling rate (R) [ 38 ]. Once the laser beam irradiates the powder, heat 
exchange occurs at the interface. The temperature difference is 
extremely large, and the cooling rate is the lowest near to zero, hence the 
G/R is approximately infinite. In this way, planer grains generate on the 
interface ( Fig. 4 a). For the heat accumulation in the process, tempera -
ture gradient decreases, and cooling rate increases. Constitutional 
undercooling zone appears at the cooling interface, making grains grew 
along the largest gradient direction (the direction perpendicular to the 
interface), facilitating the growth of some cellular grain and columnar 
dendrites ( Fig. 4 a and b). In the middle of LDED part, as G/R further 
reduces, crystal growth orientations keep the same with the direction of 
temperature gradient, gradually incline toward preferred orientation of 
γ -Co (100) and (200) [ 19 ]. The columnar dendrites are large, long grains 
with uniform orientation ( Fig. 4 c). On the top of LDED part, as G/R 
become close to zero, the direction of heat conduction become random, 
the crystal orientation is not judged by preferred orientation, and the 
formed grain is mainly cellular grain and equiaxed grain in smaller size

3.6. High temperature tensile property 
Fig. 15 shows the stress-strain curves at high temperature and the 
results are summarized in Tables 6 and 7 . It is noticed that under the 
same temperature, the ultimate tensile stress and yield stress of Co-Cr- 
Ni-W alloy are significantly higher than those of high-speed steel, and 
tensile property of two materials are both decrease with the temperature 
increasing. When the samples are stretched at high temperature, the 
isothermal martensitic transformation and strain-induced martensitic 
transformation occurs at the stretching area simultaneously [ 63 ]. The 
Shockley partial dislocations on (111) plane starts to move, making γ 
phase (with more slip systems) gradually replaced by ε phase martensite 
(with less slip systems). Stacking fault energy decrease, and martensitic 
blocks the slip on (111) plane, reduced the plastic deformation ability of 
the alloy. Besides, with temperature increasing, the carbides precipi -
tated on interdendritic areas increase and the size of carbides also in -
creases as shown in Fig. 16 . This phenomenon was similar to the 
microstructural evaluation of the aging-treated samples as mentioned 
above. According to the research by Henhoeffer et al. [ 64 ], when hard 
phases appear in cobalt-based alloys, the tensile strength of the alloy will 
decrease. The precipitated carbides can cause stress concentration, 
leading to local high stress and becoming the source of cracks. Yama -
naka et al. [ 13 ] reported that the yield strength of Co-Cr-Mo alloy after 
hot forging can be improved by grain refinement. Dislocations at γ -Co 
decompose into Schockley partial dislocation under tensile strain, which 
induces the formation of ε phase and affects the elongation. 
Fig. 17 shows the SEM image of the tensile fracture for Co-Cr-Ni-W 
alloy. It is noticed that the main fracture mode of Co-Cr-Ni-W alloy is 
brittle cleavage fracture. Mass of cleavage steps can be found on the 
fracture, and the carbide precipitations at interdendritic areas can be 
seen on cleavage planes. Stress concentration caused by small-size car -
bide makes samples easier to crack around the precipitations [ 65 ]. The 
FCC-metals are less prone to brittle fracture. Since HCP-structure ε phase 
increases, the dislocations are harder to slip, so that the fracture surface 
of the samples exhibits brittle fracture. The mechanism of fracture is 
shown in Fig. 19 . A small degree of necking can be observed at 750 
◦
C, 
which is not obvious at higher temperatures. This phenomenon might 
due to the increase of martensite [ 66 ], the elongation was inhibited. 
Fig. 18 shows the SEM images of the tensile fracture for high-speed 
steel. The main fracture mode was ductile fracture. Comparing to Co- 
Cr-Ni-W alloy, the tensile strength of high-speed steel was obviously 
lower, and necking was easier to occur. The solid solution alloy elements 
are low in the matrix, so it is easier to deform under stress. As dislocation 
density decreases at high temperature, grains are easier to be elongated 
under stress. In this way, samples deform sharply, and fracture at the 
precipitated carbide particles. When the test is at 950 
◦

fracture caused by strain concentration caused by carbide precipitated at
