ABSTRACT 
In this study, four Fe – 13Cr – 4Al alloys with different Nb contents (0 – 1.5 wt%) were prepared by using vacuum 
induction melting, forging, hot rolling, and cold rolling. The effects of annealing temperature (600 
◦
C – 1100 
◦
C) 
and the Nb contents on the microstructure, textures as well as tensile properties were investigated. The 
dispersion of fine Fe
2
Nb-type Laves phase particles was observed in the BCC matrix and along grain boundaries. 
These particles effectively pinned dislocations and boundaries, resulting in stabilizing the microstructure, 
delaying the recrystallization temperatures and refining the recrystallized grains. The Laves phase particles were 
also able to retard the nucleation and growth of the γ fiber grains, therefore Nb-containing alloys had weak 
recrystallized γ fibers, which was beneficial to improve the processing properties of Fe – 13Cr – 4Al alloys. As the 
annealing temperature increased, the strengths decreased while the elongation increased. Because of the effects 
of solid solution strengthening, precipitation strengthening and fine grain strengthening produced by the addi -
tion of Nb element, the strength and hardness of the alloys increased with the increase of Nb content. 
Comprehensively considering the microstructure and mechanical properties, the suitable addition of Nb content 
was 1 wt%, and the optimum annealing treatment after cold-rolling with 50% thickness reduction was at 700 
◦
C 
for 30 min. 
1. Introduction 
In the post-Fukushima era, the cladding materials used in light-water 
reactors (LWRs) need to be further developed to improve the safety and 
reliability in severe water-loss accident scenarios. Recently, FeCrAl al -
loys, exhibiting excellent oxidation resistance under high-temperature 
water vapor environment and good mechanical properties, have 
become one of the most promising candidate materials for accident 
tolerance fuel (ATF) cladding materials [ 1 – 3 ]. 
The addition of Al in FeCrAl alloys can form a stable Al
2
O
3 
scale on 
the alloy surface [ 4 , 5 ], protecting the matrix from oxidation. The high 
amount of Cr also plays a key role in stabilizing the protective layers [ 6 ]. 
However, the high content of Cr can result in deterioration of the 
toughness of alloys because of the precipitation of Cr-rich α ʹ phase [ 7 – 9 ]. 
Similarly, the content of Al also affects the processability of FeCrAl al -
loys [ 10 ]. Therefore, the contents of Cr and Al should be strictly 
controlled. Mo and Nb have been used to strengthen the alloys by 
solid-solution strengthening [ 11 – 13 ]. With the formation of Fe
2
Nb-type 
Laves phase precipitates, the addition of Nb can stabilize the recovered 
and recrystallized microstructures [ 12 , 13 ]. It is also reported that the 
addition of Y can retard the growth of the Al
2
O
3 
film on the surface of 
the alloys at elevated temperatures and improve the bonding force be -
tween the oxide film and the substrate, thereby improving the oxidation 
resistance of FeCrAl alloys at elevated temperatures [ 4 ]. The Fe – 13C -
r-4.5Al investigated by several scholars [ 14 , 15 ] demonstrates a good 
balance between the composition and mechanical properties. Thus, 
FeCrAl alloys based on this composition have considerable development 
potential. 
However, the processing performance of FeCrAl alloys is not satis -
factory due to containing high contents of Cr and Al, which enables the 
matrix retain a single bcc phase from room temperature to the melting 
temperature, so that the grains cannot be refined by phase trans -
formation. Additionally, FeCrAl alloys are prone to brittle fracture and 
difficult to be welded due to high Cr and Al content. It is well known that

Currently, FeCrAl cladding alloys have some problems in actual 
production. For example, the application of FeCrAl cladding alloys in 
nuclear plants must be the form of ultrathin-wall seamless tubes in order 
to improve the neutron economy. This is because FeCrAl alloys have 
higher thermal neutron absorption cross-section than the conventional 
Zr-based alloys. However, the preparation of ultrathin-wall seamless 
tubes of FeCrAl alloys via cold-rolling is difficult since the poor pro -
cessing performance of the alloys as mentioned before. Besides that, 
during the hot processing of FeCrAl alloys, the stability of grain size is 
also very important because it will affect the cold forming performance 
of FeCrAl alloy tubes. As mentioned before, it is essential to investigate 
the effect of annealing treatment on the cold-rolled Fe – 13Cr – 4Al alloys. 
Sun et al. [ 11 ] suggested that the annealing time of FeCrAl alloy tubes 
should be less than 1 h in order to facilitate the actual production. In the 
actual manufacturing process, annealing is a time-consuming and costly 
procedure. Therefore, from the perspective of manufacturing, the 
shorter time is better. According to the relationship between time and 
recrystallized grain size, the longer the annealing time is, the larger the 
grain size is. Hence, we choose the annealing time as 30 min. Based these 
actual problems, a series of Fe – 13Cr – 4Al alloys with different Nb con -
tents were fabricated in this study. The alloys were annealed at various 
temperatures for 30 min, followed by air cooling to room temperature. 
The effects of annealing temperatures on the final microstructure, 
texture and tensile properties had been investigated and discussed. This 
study will provide guidance and optimization for the actual parameter 
and properties optimization of its production. 
2. Experimental

denoted as 0Nb, 0.5Nb, 1Nb and 1.5Nb. Their chemical compositions 
are listed in Table 1 . Four Fe – 13Cr – 4Al alloy ingots were made by

were homogenized at 1100 
◦

electrical discharge machining (EDM). These small samples were

◦

an interval of 50

3. Results 
3.1. Microstructure of deformed and annealed samples 
Fig. 2 displays the representative inverse pole figure (IPF) color maps 
of as-rolled specimens along the normal direction (ND) of the sheets. The 
color key for IPF maps is included on the right. As shown in Fig. 2 , after 
cold rolling with ~50% thickness reduction, two strong deformation 
textures were found in Fe – 13Cr – 4Al alloys: γ fiber (i.e., < 111 > //ND, 
blue in IPF maps) and < 100 > //ND fiber (red in IPF maps). The 
deformed band structure was gradually refined with the increase of Nb 
content. 
Fig. 3 shows the EBSD grain maps of as-rolled Fe – 13Cr – 4Al alloys. 
For all the specimens, elongated grains were observed along the RD, and 
many fine subgrains were also observed within the elongated grains. 
With the increase of Nb content, the width of elongated grains gradually 
reduced. Fig. 4 shows the relationship between widths of the elongated 
grains and average subgrain sizes with Nb content. As can be seen, with 
the increase of Nb content, the widths of elongated grains and average 
subgrain sizes gradually decreased. The widths of the elongated grains 
for the 0Nb, 0.5Nb, 1Nb, and 1.5Nb specimens were approximately 70 
μ m, 40 μ m, 20 μ m, and 10 μ m, respectively. The subgrains within the 
deformed grains were gradually refined from 10.5 μ m to 2.3 μ m and the 
number of subgrains increased in proportion to the Nb content. These 
subgrains can be formed during plastic deformation. Under the action of 
shear stress, a large number of dislocations generated from the dislo -
cation source move along the slip plane, and these dislocations will 
encounter various obstacles, such as grain boundaries, second phase 
particles, and cut steps, etc. that hinder the movement of the disloca -
tions, causing dislocations tangling. In this case, regions with low 
dislocation density separated by high-density entangled dislocations 
appear in the metal, and these regions are deformed subgrains.

3.4. Mechanical properties of the deformed and annealed samples 
Figs. 15 and 16 shows the room temperature tensile engineering 
stress-strain curves of the Fe – 13Cr – 4Al alloy specimens. The yield 
strength (YS), ultimate tensile strength (UTL), and total elongation (TL) 
of the as-rolled and as-annealed specimens are summarized in Table 3 . It 
can be clearly observed from Fig. 15 that with increasing annealing 
temperatures, the YS and UTS of the Fe – 13Cr – 4Al alloys decreased,

strength-ductility trade-off. For example, the cold-rolled sheet of the 
1Nb alloy had the maximum YS of 1013.8 MPa and UTS of 1038.1 MPa. 
This indicates that the 1Nb alloy experienced a very small uniform 
deformation after reaching the YS. The stress immediately dropped, as 
shown in Fig. 15 (c), and hence, its TL was only 13.1%. When the alloys 
were annealed at higher temperatures, the strength significantly 
decreased and the ductility was dramatically improved. For instance, 
after annealing at 700 
◦

decreased. The YS and UTS were 652.7 MPa and 817.4 MPa, respec -

4.2. Texture evolution 
As shown in Figs. 2 and 13 , after cold rolling with a large amount of 
deformation, strong textures were observed in the alloys. Three repre -
sentative deformation textures were found in cold-rolled sheets, namely 
α , γ , and < 100 > //ND fibers. These deformation textures had also been 
found in other FeCrAl alloys [ 12 , 13 , 15 ], which are typical deformation 
textures in low carbon ferritic steels [ 20 – 22 ]. Textures not only strongly 
influence the working performance of the material, but also have a 
significant impact on the service behavior. This is because the properties 
and phenomena, such as yield strength, creep strength, fatigue, and 
stress corrosion cracking, are all related to the texture [ 23 ]. As shown in 
Fig. 14 (a), strong γ and < 100 > //ND fibers were observed in the 
cold-rolled Nb-free alloy, which is in good agreement with other 
research findings [ 11 , 13 ]. However, with the increase of the Nb content, 
the strength of the γ and < 100 > //ND fibers decreased first and then 
increased, as shown in Fig. 14 (b) – (d). The deformation textures were 
modified during the recrystallization process. Recrystallization textures 
are related to the alloy composition and processing conditions [ 24 , 25 ]. 
A notable characteristic in the Nb-free Fe – 13Cr – 4Al alloy was the for -
mation of a strong recrystallized γ fiber with an area fraction of 0.506, as 
demonstrated in Fig. 14 (e). This has often been observed in low-carbon 
ferritic stainless steels [ 20 , 21 , 24 , 26 ], possibly due to preferential 
nucleation or grain growth. Several researchers have also suggested that 
the elastic storage energy ( V ) after deformation is related to the grain 
orientation (along the ND direction), which follows the relationship:

of the alloys, which can make it easier to adapt to the applied strain.

Traditional bcc alloys generally show a large increase in strength due 
to cold working and a significant softening effect due to recrystallization 
annealing [ 29 ]. As shown in Fig. 15 , the cold-rolled sheets had a higher 
strength than the as-annealed ones, but the ductility was poor. The 
reason was that a large number of dislocations were generated by 
cold-rolling, which entangled with each other, forming dislocation cells. 
In addition, cold-rolling refined the grains, generating a large number of 
subgrain boundaries. Therefore, due to dislocation strengthening and 
grain boundary strengthening, the strength and hardness were high, but 
the ductility was poor. However, the dislocation density significantly 
decreased due to slip or climb during the annealing process, and hence, 
the dislocation strengthening effect weakened. Additionally, annealing 
also improved ductility [ 30 ]. Hence, the strength of the as-annealed 
alloys decreased, while the ductility was improved. In general, the 
strength reduced with increase of the grain size, and the relationship 
between the two variables agreed with the classical Hall-Petch rela -
tionship [ 31 , 32 ]. Hansen [ 33 ] pointed out that the larger grains in the 
recrystallized microstructure show a weaker Hall-Petch strengthening

5. Conclusions 
In this study, four Fe – 13Cr – 4Al alloys with different Nb contents 
were prepared. The alloys were subjected to cold rolling and annealing 
at different temperatures for 30 min. The microstructure, textures, and 
mechanical properties of the as-rolled and as-annealed alloys were 
studied. Based on the experimental results, the following conclusions 
can be drawn: 
(1) The addition of the Nb element could form finely dispersed 
Fe
2
Nb-type Laves phase particles along the grain boundaries and 
in the matrix. These particles can effectively pin the grain 
boundaries, resulting in stabilizing the microstructure, delaying 
the recrystallization temperature and refining the recrystallized 
grains. 
(2) Strong deformation textures (i.e., α , γ , and < 100 > //ND fibers) 
were observed in the cold-rolled Fe – 13Cr – 4Al alloys. The tex -
tures were significantly modified after the alloys underwent full 
recrystallization: the strength of the γ fiber in the Nb-free alloy 
was enhanced, but the γ fibers in the Nb-containing alloys 
weakened. The relatively weak textures were beneficial to 
improve the isotropic properties of the alloys, enhancing the 
processing performance of Nb-containing Fe – 13Cr – 4Al alloys. 
(3) Due to the addition of the Nb element, solid solution strength -
ening, precipitation strengthening, and fine grain strengthening 
were produced. Therefore, the strength and hardness of the alloys 
increased with increase of the Nb content. 
(4) By comprehensively considering the microstructure evolution 
and mechanical properties, the addition of Nb content was 
beneficial for improving the performance, thermal stability, and 
processability of the alloys. The suitable Nb addition amount was 
1 wt%, and the optimum annealing treatment after cold-rolling 
with 50% thickness reduction was at 700 
◦
C for 30 min.
