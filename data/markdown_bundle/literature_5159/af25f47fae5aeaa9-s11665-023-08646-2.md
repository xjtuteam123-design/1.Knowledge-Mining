ORIGINAL RESEARCH ARTICLE
Effect of Annealing Temperature on Microstructure
and Mechanical Properties of Fe 50Mn25Co10Cr10Ti5 High
Entropy Alloy
Qian Zhang, Ke Yang, Weidong Yuan, Yong Dong, Beibei Jiang, Chuanqiang Li, and Zhengrong Zhang
Submitted: 5 May 2023 / Revised: 9 August 2023 / Accepted: 10 August 2023 / Published online: 25 August 2023
To explore wear-resistant and high-strength materials, Fe 50Mn25Co10Cr10Ti5 high entropy alloy with non-
equal atomic ratio was designed and prepared. The inﬂuence of annealing temperature on wear and
mechanical properties was researched. The results show that the as-cast Fe 50Mn25Co10Cr10Ti5 alloy pre-
sents hypereutectic microstructure which is composed of primary Laves phases and eutectic structure with
FCC plus Laves phases. The main wear mechanism is contained fatigue wear, oxidation wear and abrasive
wear. The microstructure and wear mechanism of the alloy have not changed signiﬁcantly after annealing.
With the increasing annealing temperature, the hardness and volume wear loss of the alloy present a
tendency of going up ﬁrst and then going down. The friction coefﬁcient has not changed signiﬁcantly after
annealing 900 and 950 /C176C, while the alloy has increased signiﬁcantly after annealing 1000 /C176C. In addition,
the mechanical properties of Fe
50Mn25Co10Cr10Ti5 alloy have not signiﬁcantly improved after annealing
900 and 950 /C176C, while the compression rate and fracture strength have increased from 8.3 to 11.2% and
1493.2 to 1710.2 MPa, respectively, after annealing 1000 /C176C. The research results could provide experi-
mental references to modify the wear and mechanical performance by using the annealing process.
Keywords high entropy alloys, mechanical properties,
microstructures, wear
1. Introduction
Traditional alloys based on one main element and supple-
mented by other trace elements limit the further application of
metal materials (Ref 1). In 2004, Yeh et al. put forward a new
concept of alloy design. The alloys, named high entropy alloys
(HEAs), contain at least ﬁve main alloy elements, and each
range from 5 to 35% (Ref 2). The HEAs are nowadays a new
class of materials with four typical core effects: high entropy
effect, lattice distortion effect, slow diffusion effect and cocktail
effect during the solidiﬁcation and microstructure formation,
which lead to the excellent properties, such as high strength,
high hardness and excellent wear resistance (Ref 3-7).
Among the previous researched, the face-centered cubic
(FCC) structured HEAs have good ductility but limited strength
and hardness (Ref 8, 9). The Laves phase is well known as a
typical close-packed hexagonal hard phase (Ref 10). The
addition of Ti element is beneﬁcial for the strength and
hardness improvement by facilitating the formation of hard
Laves phase and perhaps modify the wear and mechanical
performance (Ref 11, 12). In addition, it is also worth noting
that the too high content of Ti may signiﬁcantly reduce ductility
(Ref 11).
Cui et al. researched the effect of Ti on microstructures and
mechanical properties of high entropy alloys based on
CoFeMnNi system. Results showed both the CoFeMnNi and
CoFeMnNiTi
0.25 alloys exhibit a simple face-centered cubic
(FCC) solid-solution structure, whereas the CoFeMnNiTi 0.5 and
CoFeMnNiTi0.75 alloys exhibit a dual-phase structure consist-
ing of FCC and Laves phase, which reveals that the Ti is the
forming element of Laves phase (Ref 13). Wu et al. researched
the wear behaviors of AlCrFeCoNi and AlCrFeCoNiTi
0.5 high
entropy alloys on Si 3N4 balls in 3.5 wt.% NaCl and HCl
solutions and analyzed the effect of Ti element addition on the
wear. Results showed that the AlCrFeCoNiTi
0.5 exhibited better
corrosion and corrosive wear resistance, due to the addition of
Ti element (Ref 14).
In addition, the Fe 50Mn30Co10Cr10 alloys have been exten-
sively studied in recent years, which exhibited both FCC and
HCP structure as well as mechanical properties superior to the
traditional single-phase HEAs (Ref 15). Chen et al. investigated
the feasibility of improving the corrosion resistance of
Fe
50Mn30Co10Cr10 DP-HEAs through the application of a
cerium conversion coating to its surface (Ref 16). Liu et al.
investigated the characteristics of thermal- and strain-induced e-
martensite in Fe 50Mn30Co10Cr10 multi-component alloy, focus-
ing on the grain size effect (Ref 17). However, the research on
the wear of Fe 50Mn30Co10Cr10-based alloy is lacking.
In this study, the Fe 50Mn30Co10Cr10 high entropy alloy was
used as the matrix to design a new type of wear-resistant and
high-strength materials, Fe
50Mn25Co10Cr10Ti5 high entropy
alloy with non-equal atomic ratio. In addition, annealing
treatment is expected to improve these defects such as

2. Experimental Procedures
2.1 Material Preparation
The non-equimolar Fe 50Mn25Co10Cr10Ti5 HEA was batched
with pure Fe, Mn, Co, Cr and Ti metal blocks (the purity of raw
materials is more than 99.9 wt.%) and melted by vacuum
induction melting. The raw materials are washed by ultrasonic
and alcohol before being put into the furnace, and then, the
vacuum pressure reached to 2 Pa, backﬁlled argon (Ar) to
0.05 MPa. Finally, the melting and casting were performed
with the 0.1 atm by backﬁlled to argon (Ar). After the raw
material is completely melted at 1520 /C176C for 15 min, the
molten metal is poured into the graphite crucible
(u50 9 150 mm) coated with zirconia, followed by air cooling
to room temperature. The tested specimen was obtained from
the ingot by wire cutting. The dimensions of metallographic
and wear samples are u10 9 10 mm and u30 9 2 mm,
respectively. The annealing specimens were heated at 900,
950 and 1000 /C176C for 6 h and subsequently quenched in water,
respectively.
2.2 Microstructure Observation
The as-cast and annealed samples were ground, polished,
and etched with 8% solution of nitric acid alcohol, before the
microstructures were observed under an optical microscope
(DMi8C, Leica) and a scanning electron microscope (SEM,
SU8010). The chemical compositions of different regions and
elements mapping were analyzed by SEM equipped with
energy-dispersive spectrometry (EDS). In addition, the crys-
talline structure was identiﬁed with an x-ray diffractometer
(XRD, EMPYREAN with Cu radiation target) at a scanning
rate of 8 /C176min
/C01 in the 2 h scanning range from 20 /C176to 100 /C176.
2.3 Wear and Mechanical Properties Tests
Microhardness was tested by digital hardness tester (HV-
1000) with applied load 1000 gf for 15 s. Five random dots
were taken on each sample, and the average hardness was
adopted. The wear resistance test was conducted in a ball plate
abrasion testing machine (WTM-2E). The wear samples were
grinded by Si
3N4 ceramic ball at the speed of 200 r/min, wear
radius 5 mm and the load of 1000 g for 1500 s. In addition, the
worn surface and wear volume were observed and calculated by
scanning electron microscopy (SU8010) and laser confocal
microscope (OLS4100). Cylindrical specimens with a diameter
of 5 mm and a height of 10 mm were cut from the master ingot
by wire electrode cutting for room temperature compression
test on the Universal Testing Machine (AGS-X Plus 100 KN)
with an initial strain rate of 10
/C03 s/C01. All compression tests
were repeated three times to obtain reproducible compressed
data, and then, the compression fracture morphology was
observed by SEM (SU8010).
3. Results and Discussion
3.1 Microstructure and Phase Constitution
of Fe 50Mn25Co10Cr10Ti5
Figure 1 shows the XRD patterns of the as-cast and annealed
Fe50Mn25Co10Cr10Ti5 high entropy alloys, revealing that the
alloys in different states are composed of FCC and Laves
phases, which is due to the Ti element is Laves phase forming
element in this system, resulting in the Laves phase formed
after the Fe
50Mn30Co10Cr10 alloy was used as the matrix to
replace a part of Mn elements with Ti elements in this work
(Ref 13). Specially, both Laves and HCP phase are topolog-
ically close packed, which is consistent with previous research
results that the as-cast Fe
50Mn30Co10Cr10 alloy is composed of
FCC and HCP phase (Ref 19, 20). In addition, the phase
constitution of the alloys has not changed signiﬁcantly after
annealing, indicating the Fe 50Mn25Co10Cr10Ti5 HEA has good
high-temperature stability; meanwhile, the phase decomposi-
tion and transformation have not appeared after annealing,
which is consistent with the high-temperature stability one of
the characteristics the high entropy alloys. Compared with the
XRD pattern of the as-cast alloy, the intensity of the FCC phase
and Laves phase diffraction peak of the alloy is obviously
increased after annealing; meanwhile, the half width of the
Laves diffraction peak is slightly wider after annealing
1000 /C176C, which is probably due to the grains of Laves thinner.
On the whole, although the phase constitution has not changed
after annealing, the inﬂuence on grains morphologies of
Fe
50Mn25Co10Cr10Ti5 HEA is obvious.
The micrographs of the as-cast and annealed Fe 50Mn25-
Co10Cr10Ti5 HEAs are shown in Fig. 2. Figure 2(a) and (b)
shows the different size grains of the as-cast Fe 50Mn25-
Co10Cr10Ti5 HEA contained blocky and lamellar structures,
which are preliminarily judged as the primary Laves and
eutectic structure that is composed of FCC and Laves phases.
Similar eutectic morphology could be found in previous
researches (Ref 21-23). The micrographs of the annealed
Fe
50Mn25Co10Cr10Ti5 HEAs are shown in Fig. 2(c), (d), (e), (f),
(g) and (h), showing the phase constitution of the alloys has not
changed after annealing, which is consistent with the XRD
patterns. However, the gains size and fraction of Laves and
FCC phases have changed obviously after annealing, which
probably further affects the wear and mechanical properties of
the alloys. Compared with the micrographs of the as-cast alloy,
the fraction of primary Laves phase has increased signiﬁcantly
after annealing 900 /C176C, while has decreased slightly after
annealing 950 and 1000 /C176C. In addition, it is also worth noting
that the grain boundary has widened after annealing 950 /C176C,
resulting in the grains size of the alloy is relatively smaller.
Energy dispersive spectrometer (EDS) was used to further
analyze the chemical constitution of different phases in the as-
cast Fe
50Mn25Co10Cr10Ti5 HEA, and the results are shown in
Table 1. The results show that the elements of Cr, Co and Mn
are uniformly distributed in primary Laves, eutectic FCC and
eutectic Laves phases; meanwhile, compared with the gaps of
lamellar structure, the content of Fe element in block and

3.2 Wear Properties of Fe 50Mn25Co10Cr10Ti5
The results of the friction coefﬁcients and wear volume loss
by different test parameters and annealing temperatures are
shown in Fig. 3, showing signiﬁcant impact on wear properties.
The effects of different loads and speeds on wear properties
have been researched, as shown in Fig. 3(a). A kind of oxide
ﬁlm with self-lubricating effect may be formed during the wear
process, which is demonstrated in the following EDS of wear
surface section. With the sliding speed increases, the oxide ﬁlm
is probably more easily detached during the wear process,
resulting in a relatively weakened self-lubricating effect and an
increase in friction coefﬁcient, as shown in ‘‘A1’’ and ‘‘A2’’ in
Fig. 3(a). Moreover, with the sliding speed further increases,
this oxide ﬁlm is probably quickly detached, while a large
number of wear debris may be generated during the process,
which is reported in the following wear morphology section,
thus regaining a strong self-lubricating effect, as shown in
‘‘A3’’ in Fig. 3(a); meanwhile, the wear process has become
extremely unstably, resulting in signiﬁcant ﬂuctuations in the
wear coefﬁcient curve and the wear volume loss curve show a
trend of ﬁrst increasing and then decreasing, as shown in
Fig. 3(b). With the load increases, the oxide ﬁlm and wear
debris may jointly enhance the self-lubricating effect, present-
ing better wear reduction effect and lower volume wear loss, as
shown in ‘‘A1’’ and ‘‘B’’ in Fig. 3(a) and (b). When the load is
too large and the speed is constant, the stability in the testing
process probably has been effect, and the synergistic enhance-
ment effect of the oxide ﬁlm and wear debris also has been
destroyed during the stage of severe wear, resulting in
instability of friction coefﬁcients and an increase in wear
volume loss, as shown in ‘‘C’’ in Fig. 3(a) and (b). Conse-
quently, the wear properties are signiﬁcantly affected by
different test parameters.
Subsequently, a set of parameters (‘‘B’’) with relatively
stable and positive test results was selected to research the
effects of different annealing temperatures on wear properties at
constant loads and sliding speeds. The relation of wear
coefﬁcient with sliding time of the as-cast and annealed
Fe
50Mn25Co10Cr10Ti5 HEAs is shown in Fig. 3(c). The stability
of wear coefﬁcient has not changed signiﬁcantly after annealing
900 /C176C, while has changed signiﬁcantly after annealing 950
and 1000 /C176C. According to the micrographs of the as-cast and
annealed Fe 50Mn25Co10Cr10Ti5 HEAs are shown in Fig. 2,
although the microstructure and wear mechanism of Fe 50Mn25-
Co10Cr10Ti5 have not changed signiﬁcantly after annealing, the
fraction of primary phase and FCC phase has changed
obviously, which probably further affects the wear properties
of the alloy. Fig. 3(d) shows the wear volume loss and hardness
of the as-cast and annealed Fe
50Mn25Co10Cr10Ti5 HEAs. With
the increasing annealing temperature, the hardness and volume
wear of the Fe 50Mn25Co10Cr10Ti5 HEAs present a tendency of
going up ﬁrst and then going down. Compared with the as-cast

4. Conclusion
In this study, the Fe 50Mn25Co10Cr10Ti5 high entropy alloy
was prepared by vacuum arc melting, and the phase constitu-
tion, microstructures, wear and mechanical properties were
investigated.
1) The as-cast Fe 50Mn25Co10Cr10Ti5 alloy presents hypereu-
tectic microstructure, which is mainly composed of
coarse primary Laves phase plus eutectic structure con-
taining FCC phase and Laves phase, and the phase con-
stitution has not changed after annealing.
2) The wear properties are signiﬁcantly affected by different
test parameters. The wear mechanism of the as-cast alloy
is mainly fatigue wear, accompanied by a mixed wear
mechanism of abrasive wear and oxidation wear, and the
main wear mechanism of the Fe
50Mn25Co10Cr10Ti5 alloy
has not changed signiﬁcantly after annealing. With the
increasing annealing temperature, the fracture strength of
the alloys has increased, however, the compression ratio
decreased signiﬁcantly after annealing 900 /C176C, while re-
mained almost not changed after annealing 950 /C176C. The
