ABSTRACT 
In order to obtain a TiAl-based alloy that can be well hot rolled at lower temperature and simultaneously have 
good mechanical properties, several (Nb,Fe) co-alloyed TiAl-based alloys were designed and studied in this 
paper. The results showed that the Ti-44Al-6 Nb-2Fe-0.1B alloy met the design requirements. The Ti-44Al-6 Nb- 
2Fe-0.1B alloy with package could be well hot rolled at a relatively lower temperature of 1180 
◦
C. However, if 
there was no package, all these designed alloys could not be well hot rolled due to the severe surface temperature 
drop and the “ hard ” processing method of rolling. Before hot rolling, the Ti-44Al-6 Nb-2Fe-0.1B alloy was 
composed of (B2 + γ ) blocky morphology. After hot rolling and annealing, it exhibited the fully recrystallized 
(B2 + γ ) blocky morphology. The hot rolled Ti-44Al-6 Nb-2Fe-0.1B alloy also had the best compression property at 
room temperature, including compression strength of 2214 ± 34 MPa and fracture strain of 41.0 % ± 0.4 %. But its 
compression fracture strength started to become lower than that of Ti-44Al-8 Nb-0.1B alloy at 700 
◦
C. It indicated 
that there was the temperature range between room temperature and 700 
◦
C, when the compression strength of 
Ti-44Al-6 Nb-2Fe-0.1B alloy was higher than that of Ti-44Al-8 Nb-0.1B alloy. 
1. Introduction 
TiAl-based alloys are the excellent light-weight and high-strength 
high temperature structural materials [1 – 3 ] . Like other alloys, the hot 
deformation processing is always the effective processing method for 
them. Especially the rolling, it has higher preparation efficiency, and the 
rolled alloy sheet has the higher utilization when it is made into various 
workpieces [4 – 6 ] . However, as the intermetallics, TiAl-based alloys 
have the very strong intrinsic brittleness. This makes the alloys difficult 
to rolling, limiting their wide application. 
In order to solve the problem, researchers naturally thought of 
adding β -stabilizers into TiAl-based alloys, such as Nb, Mo, V, Cr, Zr, W 
[7,8 ] . These β -stabilizers can make the B2 phase form, and the B2 phase 
will transform into the soft phase at high temperature, improving the hot 
deformation processing capability of the alloys. For example, Niu [9] 
designed and prepared Ti-44Al-6 V-3 Nb-0.3Y alloy (at%, Ti for the 
remainder, the same below). The alloy ingot was canned forged at

850 MPa at 700

Fe is the strongest β -stabilizer [15] . The addition of a small amount of 
Fe can simultaneously form a large amount of B2 phase and maintain the 
low-density advantage of the alloy. Therefore, Fe can be expected the 
candidate to fundamentally improve the hot deformation processing 
capability of the alloy. As a structural material, besides the hot defor -
mation processing capability, the mechanical properties after the hot 
deformation processing should also be considered. Previous studies have 
shown that Nb alloying [16 – 19] has the significant and irreplaceable 
role in improving the mechanical properties of TiAl-based alloys. 
Therefore, considering comprehensively, this study would choose (Nb, 
Fe) co-alloying, in which the addition of Fe was mainly expected to 
further improve the hot deformation processing capability of the alloy, 
and the addition of Nb was mainly expected to play its beneficial role in 
mechanical properties. In our previous study, we had designed several 
(Nb,Fe) co-alloyed TiAl alloys. And, through the thermal simulation 
study, it was found that these designed alloys had excellent hot defor -
mation processing capability [20 , 21] . Therefore, in this study, we would 
continue to use those (Nb,Fe) co-alloyed alloys. In order to cater to the 
hot deformation processing, 0.1 at% B [22 – 24] was added into each 
alloy. Therefore, these alloys used here were Ti-44Al-8 Nb-0.1B (for 
comparative analysis), Ti-44Al-6 Nb-2Fe-0.1B, Ti-44Al-2 Nb-2Fe-0.1B 
and Ti-44Al-4 Nb-0.5Fe-0.1B, respectively. We would directly roll these 
alloy ingots, and challenge the rolling at a much lower temperature of 
1180 
◦
C with and without package. The processing effect of rolled alloys 
was investigated. Microstructures of the alloys before and after rolling 
were compared and analyzed. The compression properties of the rolled 
alloys at different temperatures were studied. And, the fracture 
morphology of the compression sample at RT were investigated. Finally, 
it was found that the Ti-44Al-6 Nb-2Fe-0.1B alloy had both excellent hot 
deformation processing capability and good compression property. 
2. Experimental 
Four master ingots respectively with the composition of Ti-44Al- 
8 Nb-0.1B, Ti-44Al-6 Nb-2Fe-0.1B, Ti-44Al-2 Nb-2Fe-0.1B and Ti-44Al- 
4 Nb-0.5Fe-0.1B were prepared by vacuum consumable melting method. 
From each master ingot, two 25 mm × 25 mm × 80 mm cuboid billets 
were cut, and they were for the hot rolling without and with package, 
respectively. The package material was 304 stainless steel. The billet 
with package had the size of 30 mm × 30 mm × 90 mm. Fig. 1 shows the 
four alloy billets with package and without package. 
The rolling was carried out on the 400-type two-roll asynchronous 
hot rolling experimental machine with the roll diameter of 430 mm. The 
rolling temperature was 1180
◦
C. The billet was firstly heated to 1180
◦
C 
in the furnace, and then held at this temperature for 30 mins before 
rolling. The total engineering deformation of the rolling was 50 %, and 
the samples with or without package were both subjected to 5 passes of 
rolling. For the billet with package, each pass was rolled down by 3 mm, 
and finally rolled to the thickness of 15 mm. For the billet without 
package, each pass was rolled down by 2.5 mm, and finally rolled to the 
thickness of 12.5 mm. After rolling, removing the package, the thickness 
of the rolled billets originally with and without the package was found to 
be almost the same. Before the next pass of rolling, the billets needed to 
be re-heated to 1180
◦
C and held for 10 min. The rolling speed was 
0.8 m/s. After the final pass of rolling, the billets were air-cooled. And 
then, the rolled billets were annealed at 1200
◦
C for 90 min. 
Cylinder samples with the size of diameter 6 mm and height 9 mm 
were cut from the rolled billets for compression property testing. The 
height direction of the compression sample was parallel with the rolling 
direction. The compression property testing was carried out on 
WDW100-type material universal testing machine. The testing temper -
atures were RT, 700
◦
C, 750
◦
C and 800
◦
C, respectively. The compres -
sion speed was 0.5 mm/min. For the case that the fracture occurred 
during the compression testing, the experiment would be automatically 
stopped, and for the case that no fracture occurred, the experiment 
would be manually stopped when the compression displacement was 
8 mm. For the testing at 700
◦
C, 750
◦
C and 800
◦
C, the compression 
strength of down 4 mm, i.e., 44.4 % engineering strain, was used for 
analysis. Because it was found that when the compression distance was 
more than about 4 mm, the bottom size of the compression sample 
began to increase, leading to the abnormal and untrue rise of the 
compression strength. 
The microstructure of the four alloys before and after hot rolling 
were investigated by using scanning electron microscope (SEM) and 
transmission electron microscope (TEM). The phase was determined by 
selected area electron diffraction patterns (SADPs) of TEM. For prepar -
ing the SEM samples, the alloy was first subjected to standard metallo -
graphic treatment, and then subjected to light corrosion by using the 
solution of 95 % H
2
O and 5 % HF (volume ratio). For preparing the TEM 
samples, the alloy was firstly cut into 500- μ m-thick thin piece; and then, 
the thin piece was ground to about 100- μ m thickness; next, the 100- μ m- 
thick thin piece was punched into the 3-mm-diameter small thin piece; 
finally, the small thin piece was twin jet electro-polished in the etched 
liquid at about 20
◦
C until 20 % proportion of hole appeared on it. The 
etched liquid was composed of 60 vol% methanol, 35 vol% butyl 
alcohol and 5 vol% perchloric acid. In addition, the content of B2 phase 
after rolling and annealing were counted by using Image-Pro-Plus 6.0 
software. And, the fracture of the RT compression samples was inves -
tigated by using the SEM secondary electron mode.

Fig. 3 shows the rolled billets of the four alloys. Unfortunately, the 
alloy billets without package cannot be well hot rolled, and all these 
alloy billets are broken during the hot rolling. It can be seen that, no 
matter how good the hot deformation processing capability of the TiAl- 
based alloy is, the hot rolling without the package cannot be successful. 
That is because, the hot processing of brittle TiAl-based alloys is very 
sensitive to temperature. Without a package, the surface temperature of 
the alloys largely drops during rolling that will be lower than the ductile- 
brittle transition temperature. Moreover, the rolling processing itself is a 
relatively “ hard ” processing method. Therefore, the cracking will first 
appear on the billet surface during the rolling, and then the cracking will 
pass from the surface to the core quickly, eventually leading to the 
fragmentation of the hot rolled billet. In all these alloy billets with 
package, the Ti-44Al-6 Nb-2Fe-0.1B alloy billet has a good rolling effect 
at the lower temperature of 1180

package, the inner of the rolled billet is almost complete, and no surface 
cracks can be found. However, for other alloy billets, the rolling effect is 
much worse as shown in Fig. 3 (a,c,d) , where the obvious crack can be 
seen. Especially, for the Ti-44Al-4 Nb-0.5Fe-0.1B alloy, its rolling effect 
is the worst, and cracks are all over the rolled billet, making it impossible 
to cut out the cylinder compression samples.

bility, which can achieve the successful low-temperature hot rolling. 
Moreover, its compression property is good, which is better than the 
high-Nb TiAl-based alloy Ti-44Al-8 Nb-0.1B at a relatively lower tem -
perature (RT to maybe about 600 
◦
C). Therefore, Ti-44Al-6 Nb-2Fe-0.1B 
alloy is a perfect candidate both with excellent hot deformation pro -
cessing capability and better mechanical property that is suitable for use 
at relatively lower temperature. 
4. Conclusions 
In this paper, the high-Nb TiAl-based alloys and newly designed (Nb, 
Fe) co-alloyed TiAl-based alloys were hot rolled at a relatively lower 
temperature of 1180 
◦
C, the compression property of the rolled billets 
was tested, and the microstructure before and after rolling was studied. 
The following conclusions could be drawn: 
(1) Only the Ti-44Al-6 Nb-2Fe-0.1B alloy with package can be well 
hot rolled at the relatively lower temperature of 1180 
◦
C. How -
ever, if there is no package, all the four alloys, i.e., Ti-44Al-8 Nb- 
0.1B, Ti-44Al-6 Nb-2Fe-0.1B, Ti-44Al-2 Nb-2Fe-0.1B and Ti- 
44Al-4 Nb-0.5Fe-0.1B, cannot be well hot rolled at 1180 
◦
C due to 
the severe surface temperature drop and the “ hard ” processing 
method of rolling. 
(2) Before hot rolling, the Ti-44Al-8 Nb-0.1B alloy is composed of 
( α
2
+ γ ) lamella colonies with B2 phase in it and γ phase around 
the colonies. The Ti-44Al-6 Nb-2Fe-0.1B alloy is composed of 
(B2 + γ ) blocky morphology. The Ti-44Al-2 Nb-2Fe-0.1B alloy is 
composed of amount of (B2 + γ ) blocky morphology and a few 
( α
2
+ γ ) lamella colonies. The Ti-44Al-4 Nb-0.5Fe-0.1B alloy is 
composed of less (B2 + γ ) blocky morphology and amount of 
( α
2
+ γ ) lamella colonies. 
(3) After hot rolling and annealing, the Ti-44Al-8 Nb-0.1B alloy is 
composed of less recrystallized (B2 + γ ) blocky morphology and 
amount of ( α
2
+ γ ) lamella colonies. The Ti-44Al-6 Nb-2Fe-0.1B 
alloy has the most fully recrystallized (B2 + γ ) blocky morphology. 
The Ti-44Al-2 Nb-2Fe-0.1B alloy has similar microstructure with 
Ti-44Al-6 Nb-2Fe-0.1B alloy, but its recrystallization degree is 
relatively lower. The Ti-44Al-4 Nb-0.5Fe-0.1B alloy is composed 
of recrystallized (B2 + γ ) blocky morphology and amount of 
( α
2
+ γ ) lamella colonies. 
(4) The hot rolled Ti-44Al-6 Nb-2Fe-0.1B alloy has the best 
compression property at RT, including compression strength of 
2214 MPa ± 34 MPa and fracture strain of 41.0 % ± 0.4 %. But its 
compression fracture strength starts to become a little lower than 
that of Ti-44Al-8 Nb-0.1B alloy at 700 
◦
C. It indicates that there is 
the temperature range between RT and 700 
◦
C, when the 
compression strength of Ti-44Al-6 Nb-2Fe-0.1B alloy is higher 
than that of Ti-44Al-8 Nb-0.1B alloy.
