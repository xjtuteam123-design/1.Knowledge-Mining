ABSTRACT 
It is known that inhibiting rapid decline of strain hardening is crucial in enhancing uniform ductility under 
uniaxial tension stress for metallic materials with high yield stress. In this study, a high-mechanical-response Ni- 
based alloy was introduced by fabricating a dual-heterogeneous structure allowing to provide improved strain- 
hardening capability at both ambient and cryogenic temperatures. Especially at cryogenic temperature, an 
exceptional strength-ductility synergy with yield strength of 1.85 GPa and ductility of 24% can be achieved, 
accompanied by a distinctive five-stage strain-hardening response. In such a case, deformation twinning behavior 
was activated successfully to assist the strain hardening in the Ni base alloy although precipitation-hardening 
effect was available. Our work sheds light on the strain hardening effect assisted by twinning previously un -
expected in coherent precipitation-hardened Ni base alloys, and hence represents a promising route for 
improving uniform ductility of Ni base alloys with high yield strength. 
1. Introduction 
Urgent demand of high-performance engineering materials to keep 
up with the rapid progress in aerospace, marine shipbuilding and some 
low-temperature industry (such as the gas industry) has encouraged 
researches either in exploring new materials or excavating potential of 
existed conventional materials. Of these demands, the materials with 
high yield strength and considerable uniform ductility are highly desired 
for the fastening pieces(the equipments used in vacuum condition) at 
both ambient (293 K) and cryogenic (77 K) temperatures [ 1 , 2 ]. As one of 
the promising candidates, Ni base alloys with the face-centered-cubic 
(FCC) structure have been explored to obtain superior combination of 
yield stress-uniform ductility. However, the FCC-structured materials 
usually display inferior strengthening-toughening ability compared with 
their counterparts, such as super-strong steels owing to the shortage of 
maraging ability [ 3 – 5 ]. Indeed, the typical strength of wrought Inconel 
718 Ni base superalloy is fallen in the range from 1200 to 1500 MPa 
when acceptable ductility is obtained [ 6 , 7 ]. 
Recent investigations have shown that, to prepare heterogeneous 
structure (HS) combined with L1
2
-type ordered precipitates has been 
accepted as a promising way in fabricating Ni-contained materials with 
sound strength-ductility synergy [ 8 – 14 ]. It can provide “ back stress 
hardening ” and “ coherent precipitation hardening (PH) ” simulta -
neously, both are beneficial for the alloys to achieve improved strength 
response without causing the loss of ductility in an unacceptable degree. 
Nonetheless, it is discovered that the twin-induced-plasticity (TWIP) or 
transformation-induced-plasticity (TRIP) effects have been seriously 
hindered owing to the homogeneous distribution of coherent L1
2
-type 
ordered precipitates in these Ni-contained materials [ 8 – 16 ]. The inhi -
bition of TWIP or TRIP effects can be understood by two factors: (1) 
L1
2
-type ordered phase usually has a high stacking fault energy, which is 
on the level of about 200 mJ/m
2
; (2) An extremely high stress is needed 
to induce the nucleation of twins in the narrow regions with the 
magnitude of dozens nanometer of FCC matrix [ 8 – 16 ]. However, it has 
been demonstrated that in order to obtain considerable uniform 
ductility, assistant strengthening-toughening routine, i.e., either TRIP or 
TWIP effect is still necessary to sustain the strain hardening ability under 
high yield stress (gigapascal-level) conditions [ 15 – 18 ]. 
Relevant researches have showed that the occurrence of TWIP or 
TRIP effect is controlled mainly by the magnitude of their stacking fault

tried to be disclosed by characterizing the mechanical response and

available at the interior of FCC regions during the deformation process 
at 77K for the Ni base alloy with heterogeneous structure. It is known 
that during the deformation at 77K, the thermal activation of the normal 
dislocations is suppressed. Instead, the activation of dislocations de -
pends largely on the local stress intensity [ 28 , 30 ]. In this present study, 
owing to its heterogeneous structure, the higher applied stresses and the 
severely heterogeneous deformation at 77 K lead to larger variations of 
internal stresses, at least more non-uniform than the case deformed at 
293 K. In some regions, the local stress intensity is higher than that other 
regions. This forms structural basis for activating the partial dislocations 
during the deformation process. The higher local stress intensity will 
result in a larger difference in the resolved shear stress between the 
leading and trailing partials with different Schmid factors, which will 
reduce the probability of the trailing partial and favor twin nucleation 
that requires partials with the same Burgers vector as for the leading 
partial. As a result, twinning behavior becomes kinetically available [ 27 , 
28 , 30 ]. Similar phenomenon has been observed in nanocrystalline Ni 
and Ni base alloys with high SFEs deformed at 77 K [ 28 , 30 , 45 , 47 ]. Here, 
it is shown that by employing the advantages of “ heterogeneous defor -
mation ” , the partial dislocations mediated plasticity (PDMPs) can also 
be activated in the dual-heterogeneous-structure Ni base alloy with high 
SFEs deformed at 77 K.
