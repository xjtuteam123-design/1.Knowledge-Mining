The center of phase diagram, relatively high-disordered region, often has few alloy systems available, es- 
pecially for traditional ternary alloy systems. Here, we develop a novel Zr 50 Ti 35 Nb 15 alloy near the center 
of phase diagram, as a body-centered cubic (bcc) solid solution phase, which displays a unique combi- 
nation of Young’s modulus of 62 GPa, yield strength of 657 MPa, tensile ductility of 21.9%, and excellent 
corrosion resistance ( E corr = 422 mV, I pit = 0.336 μA/cm 2 ). The excellent mechanical properties of this alloy 
are related to dislocation slip and deformation twins. This result also proves that the disordered structure 
is valuable for traditional alloy systems.

Zr-based alloys are considered as the most promising materials 
in the engineering and biomedical applications because of its ex- 
cellent in-vivo performance, acceptable mechanical properties, and 
great corrosion resistance [1–5] . In general, the most commonly 
used Zr-based alloys in the past decades are mainly hexagonal 
closed-packed (hcp) structure, which are also referred to α-type 
Zr-based alloys. Although, the α-type Zr-based alloys display 
excellent mechanical performances and processing properties [1] . 
There are also some performances that need to be improved for 
enabling their practical applications as biomedical and engineering 
components, such as corrosion resistance and Young’s modulus. 
With reference to current researches, these issues can be solved 
by body-centered cubic (bcc) structured Zr-based alloys (namely, 
β-type Zr-based alloys), especially in terms of mechanical per- 
formances [6] . Moreover, in recent years, many new alloys with 
promising properties are likely to be discovered near the centers 
(as opposed to the corners) of phase diagrams, which no longer 
contain a single major component, but multiple major elements 
and form a concentrated solid-solution structure [7 , 8] . Therefore, 
it is interesting and imperative to explore disordered Zr alloys 
with bcc structure which concurrently possess good performances. 
Based on the previous high-throughput studies [9] , a disordered 
Zr 50 Ti 35 Nb 15 alloy with bcc structure was developed in this work. 
As we all know, disordered materials that derive their properties 
from multiple principal elements, generally form a concentrated

Ingot with a composition of Zr 50 Ti 35 Nb 15 (in at%) was pre- 
pared by arc-melting a mixture of the metallic elements with 
purity higher than 99.95 wt% in a Ti-gettered high-purity ar- 
gon atmosphere. The ingot was re-melted at least six times to 
ensure chemical homogeneity and eventually drop-cast into rect- 
angular cross-section copper molds with a dimension of 10 mm 
(width) × 10 mm (thickness) × 75 mm (length). In parallel, the 
ingot cuts into sheets with 3 mm thickness along the longitu- 
dinal section, followed by a cold-rolling with 50% reduction to 
1.5 mm thickness. Recrystallization annealing was then carried out 
at 0.5 T m ( T m = 1884 K). The phase structure of the alloy was 
conducted using X-ray diffraction (XRD, BRUKERD8 Discover) with 
Cu K α radiation. The continuous scanning mode was used to scan 
in the range 10–100 ° at a scanning rate of 4 °/min. Microstructures 
and morphologies were characterized by a Zeiss Supra55 ﬁled 
emission scanning electron microscopy (SEM) equipped with 
electron back-scattering diffraction (EBSD) spectrometer. The EBSD

As shown in Fig. 1 a, the Ti–Zr–Nb phase diagram can be 
roughly divided into the ordered alloys region (green) near the 
corners and relatively disordered alloys region (orange) near 
the center. Ordered alloys near the corners are referred to as 
Zr-based, Ti-based, Nb-based alloys respectively, which derive 
their properties from a single major component. This target alloy 
composition with high-mixing entropy was developed near the 
center of Ti–Zr–Nb phase diagram, as indicated by star symbol 
in Fig. 1 a. The EBSD image taken from the cross-section of the 
as-cast bar shows a typical casting structure, which contains both 
equiaxed and columnar grains with grainsize ranging from 10 to 
1100 μm. From the metallographic morphologies, it was found that 
the as-cast microstructure of Zr 50 Ti 35 Nb 15 alloy is very uniform 
and without any dendrite segregation. As shown in Fig. 1 c, the 
XRD pattern indicates that the Zr 50 Ti 35 Nb 15 alloy has a single 
BCC solid solution structure. TEM bright ﬁeld image and the 
corresponding selected area electron diffraction (SAED) pattern 
of the Zr 50 Ti 35 Nb 15 alloy evidence no other crystalline phases or 
precipitates in the BCC matrix, further identifying that the alloy 
has a single BCC solid solution structure, as shown in Fig. 1 d. 
The stress-strain curves were measured by uniaxial, dog-bone- 
shaped tensile specimen at room temperature, as shown in Fig. 2 a. 
The present alloy exhibits high strength ranging from 612 to 
810 MPa, together with signiﬁcant tensile ductility ( > 10%). The as- 
cast Zr 50 Ti 35 Nb 15 alloy exhibits a yield strength of 657 MPa to- 
gether with a signiﬁcant tensile ductility of ~22%. After cold rolling, 
the yield strength reached 810 MPa, and the tensile ductility re- 
mained at 10%. For the alloy subjected to recrystallization anneal- 
ing, the plasticity is signiﬁcantly improved to 26% and the tensile 
strength is comparable to that of the as-cast alloy. The recrystal- 
lization images characterized by EBSD are shown in the internal 
diagram of Fig. 2 a ( Ⅰ , Ⅱ and Ⅲ ). Complete recrystallization with 
a crystallinity of 96% was achieved in the Zr 50 Ti 35 Nb 15 alloy. The 
grains are remarkably reﬁned, and the grain size is generally less 
than 100 μm. Furthermore, pole ﬁgures also indicate that complete 
recrystallization carried out in the Zr 50 Ti 35 Nb 15 alloy without any 
preferred orientation, as shown in Fig. 2 a- Ⅲ . The Young’s modulus 
obtained by linear ﬁtting the slope of the elastic part in Fig. 2 a is 
estimated to be ~62 GPa.

The detailed discussion of fracture mechanism is performed for 
the as-cast Zr 50 Ti 35 Nb 15 alloy. The excellent plasticity is associated 
with fully ductile fracture, with a pronounced stretch-zone at crack 
initiation. From the physical diagram, it can be observed that the 
alloy was fractured in the form of slip separation with obvious 
plastic deformation, as shown in Fig. 3 a. The SEM images of the 
fracture surfaces are shown in Fig. 3 b, in low and high magniﬁca- 
tion. The fracture surface is mainly covered by plastic tear ridges as 
well as large-sized dimples, indicating numerous large-angle grain 
boundaries and severe local misorientation, in particular at grain 
boundaries [17 , 18] . Moreover, Zone II shows the slip bands of the 
wave pattern. Many micropores have also been observed in the slip 
bands, which absorb energy during the deformation process to en- 
sure high yield and excellent ductility of the Zr 50 Ti 35 Nb 15 alloy. 
The grain maps of the fracture were conﬁrmed by EBSD, as 
shown in Fig. 3 c. The mechanical twins appear in the shape of 
nano-scale lenticulars, most of which originate from the grain 
boundaries and terminate inside the crystal. The mechanical twin- 
ning promotes the new slip systems to start, thus ensuring the 
continuous plastic deformation. In comparison with the as-cast 
grains, the grains near the tensile fracture are severely deformed 
and accompanied by grain fragmentation, which is caused by 
the interaction of dislocation slip and mechanical twinning. The 
speciﬁc grain size is shown in the normal distribution statistics 
in Fig. 3 c, and the maximum radius is 130 μm. From the TEM 
images, dislocation dipoles produced by dislocation cross-slip and 
the stress-induced deformation twins are clearly observed in the 
as-cast Zr 50 Ti 35 Nb 15 alloy, as shown in Fig. 3 d–f. Normally, the 
motion of dislocations is patterned with two prevalent types: 
planar slip and wavy slip [19] . Continuous planar propagation of 
dislocations often prevails in fcc sturcutred alloys, whereas plas- 
ticity in bcc strucutred alloys is strongly inﬂuenced by frequent 
cross-slip owing to the similarly close packed (110) and (112) slip 
planes, resulting in wavy slip patterns [20 , 21] . Moreover, high 
concentration of each component in disordered strucutres causes 
inhomogeneous element distributions, which could strongly inﬂu- 
ence the deformation mechanisms and mechanical properties [22] . 
Fluctuations in composition inside the crystal lattice makes the 
dislocations more prone to plugging, which further leads to the 
occurrence of cross-slip of the material. These observations suggest 
the interaction of cross-slip and deformation twins resulting in the 
stable plastic deformation, and dynamic grain reﬁnement due to 
the interaction of dislocation and twins also ensures such excellent 
mechanial properties. 
Excellent corrosion resistance is also essential for the practical 
applications. Fig. 4 shows the potentiodynamic polarization curves 
of Zr 50 Ti 35 Nb 15 alloy in PBS solution at room temperature. The 
corrosion potential ( E corr ) and passive current density ( I pass ) of the 
alloy are −0.422 V and 0.336 μA/cm 2 , respectively. Compared with 
several traditional alloys [18] , Zr 50 Ti 35 Nb 15 alloy display a relative 
higher E corr value together with the lowest I pass value, as shown 
in Fig. 4 . In comparison, electrochemical property of Zr 50 Ti 35 Nb 15 
alloy is remarkably superior to that of 316 L SS and CoCrMo alloy 
and comparable to that of Ti6Al4V alloy examined in PBS. It is 
noteworthy that this alloy did not exhibit pitting or transpassi- 
vation at potentials up to 1.5 V sce . Generally, pitting corrosion 
presents a potential failure hazard to the biomedical materials. 
Brieﬂy, preliminary evaluations indicate that the developed alloy 
has excellent corrosion resistance to meet the requirements of 
practical applications.

valuable, and contribute to the discovery of promising materials in

In summary, a medium-entropy alloy Zr 50 Ti 35 Nb 15 with a 
disordered bcc structure has been developed. Disordered solid- 
solution structure allows the alloy possessing excellent mechanical 
properties, and the underlying mechanisms are related to dis- 
location slip together with deformation twins. Furthermore, the 
Zr 50 Ti 35 Nb 15 alloy has a unique combination of high corrosion 
potential and low passive current density in PBS soultion, ex- 
hibiting good corrosion resistance. The outstanding mechanical 
performances and corrosion resistance of this alloy make it a po- 
tential material for engineering and biomedical applications. This 
result also proves that the concentrated solid-solution strucutre 
is valuable for traditional alloy systems, and contribute to the 
discovery of promising materials on the basic of conventional 
alloy systems. We suggest that the concept of disordered medium- 
entropy alloys also can be applicable to a wide range of other alloy 
classes.
