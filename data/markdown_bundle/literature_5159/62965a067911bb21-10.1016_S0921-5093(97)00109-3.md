Mechanical properties of rapidly solidified Al-Ti-Fe, Al-Cu-Fe and 
Al-Fe-Cu-Ti based alloys extruded from their atomized powders 
J.Q. Guo *, N.S. Kazama 
Semfai Institute of Materials Science and Technology, YKK Corporation, 38 Akaishi, Miyagi 98133, Japan 
Received 15 December 1996; received in revised form 5 April 1997 
Abstract 
Mechanical properties and heat-resistant properties of rapidly solidified Al-T&Fe, Al-Cu-Fe and Al-Fe-Cu-Ti based alloys 
extruded from their atomized powders are investigated. These alloys have tensile strength in the range of 700-880 MPa, good 
elongation and large Young’s modulus at room temperature. At high temperature they show excellent heat-resistant properties. 
Some of them have the heat-resistant tensile strength as high as 350 MPa at 573 K. The phase structures of these alloys are 
analyzed and the origin of heat-resistance is discussed. 0 1997 Elsevier Science S.A. 
Key~vds: Al-X-Fe; Al-Cu-Fe; Al-Fe-Cu-Ti; Alloys; Properties 
1. Introduction 
Rapid solidification can enhance and improve me- 
chanical properties of alloys by producing new phases, 
supersaturated solid solution and refining the mi- 
crostructures of the alloys. Powder metallurgy is an 
effective method to get high strength bulk materials. 
Many new materials have been developed by rapid 
solidification and powder metallurgy method. When 
materials are used under high temperature conditions it 
is required that the materials have good heat-resistant 
properties. Among Al alloys Al-Fe based alloys show 
good heat-resistant properties. How to enhance the 
heat-resistant properties of Al-Fe based alloys has been 
an important subject. Through research on rapidly 
solidified high strength Al-Ni based alloys [l-4], it is 
found that Ti, Zr and rare earth elements can inten- 
sively strengthen Al-Ni based alloys and Cu can im- 
prove the elongation of the alloys. To check whether 
Zr, Ti, Cu and rare earth elements can also improve 
mechanical properties of Al-Fe based alloys, the me- 
chanical properties and phase structures of rapidly soli- 
dified Al-Ti-Fe, Al-Cu-Fe and Al-Fe-Cu-Ti alloys 
containing Zr and Ce are studied in the present work.

2. Experimental methods 
We choose Al-Ti-Fe, Al-Cu-Fe and Al-Fe-Cu-Ti 
based alloys as experimental examples. These alloys 
were prepared from pure elements and gas atomized 
into powders in an argon atmosphere. The purity is 
99.9 wt.% for Al, Fe, Cu, Zr, Ti, W, Cr and 99 wt.% for 
Ce. The atomized powders with diameters less than 26 
pm were sealed in an aluminum capsule cylinder and 
were extruded to form a bulk with a diameter of about 
8 mm at an extrusion ratio of 1O:l. The extrusion 
temperature was 673 K. The mechanical properties of 
the extruded bulk alloys were measured by an Instron- 
type tensile testing apparatus at room temperature, 573, 
623 and 673 K, respectively. The high temperature 
mechanical properties were measured after the alloy 
had been heated to the objective temperature and kept 
for 3.6 1~s. The thermal stability of these extruded alloys 
was evaluated by differential scanning calorimetry 
(DSC) of MAC Science DSC-3100. The heating rate is 
0.33 K/s. On basis of the DSC results, the extruded 
alloys were annealed at different temperatures for 3.6 
ks. The crystal structures and microstructures of the 
as-extruded and annealed alloys were examined by X- 
ray diffractometry (XRD) of MAC Science Ml 8XHF 
and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) of JEOL 
JEM-2010F. The scanning speed of XRD is 2”/min and 
the target of Cu is used.

3. Mechanical properties 
The composition and room temperature mechanical 
properties of Al-Ti-Fe, Al-Cu-Fe and Al-Fe-Cu-Ti 
based alloys extruded from their atomized powders 
are shown in Table 1. The table indicates that all the 
alloys have high tensile strength more than 700 MPa. 
Compared with Al-Fe-Cu-Ti and Al-Ti-Fe alloys, Cu- 
rich Al-Q-Fe alloys show larger elongation. This 
proves that Cu plays an important role to contribute 
Al-based alloys to large elongation. It is known by 
comparing the mechanical properties of

the addition of Zr and Ce to the alloys can enhance 
their tensile strength when contents of Al and Fe are 
the same in the alloys.

4. Phase structures and microstructures 
To make sure why these alloys have such good 
mechanical properties and heat-resistant properties, the 
phase structures of the extruded Al-Ti-Fe, Al-Cu-Fe 
and Al-Fe-Cu-Ti based alloys were analyzed by X-ray 
diffraction. The identified phases are listed in Table 3. 
As shown in Fig. 1 the Al-Ti-Fe-Cr alloy is composed 
of u-Al, D022-Al,Ti, metastable Ll,-Al,Ti and a new 
orthorhombic phase. Lattice parameters of the new 
orthorhombic phase were calculated to be a = 0.8837, 
b = 0.7493 and c = 0.6484 nm on basis of X-ray diffrac- 
tion data. 
~~ To check whether or not the metastable Ll,-A1,Ti 
and the new orthorhombic phase exist in the alloy, 
selected area diffraction (SAD) patterns were taken 
from the phases of the alloy by TEM. The SAD 
patterns shown in Fig. 2 prove the existence of these 
phases in the extruded Al,,,,Ti,Fe,,,Cr, alloy. Because 
Ll,-A1,Ti is a metastable phase, at high temperature 
the Ll,-A1,Ti phase will transform into the equilibrium 
D022-A1,Ti phase. On the other hand, the thermal 
stability of the new orthorhombic phase is unknown. 
The thermal stability of the two phases should have an 
effect on the heat-resistant properties of the alloy. 
Therefore, it is necessary to investigate the thermal 
stability of the extruded alloy. The DSC analysis on the 
alloy was carried out at heating rate of 0.33 K/s. As 
shown in Fig. 3, there is an exothermic peak at 782 K 
on the DSC curve. This means that some phase trans- 
formation happens at the temperature. Based on X-ray 
diffraction results shown in Fig. 4 it is identified that 
the Ll,-Al,Ti phase keeps stable below 773 K and 
disappears after annealed at temperature above 773 K 
and the new orthorhombic phase remains up to 873 K. 
This means that at temperature near 782 K the Ll,- 
Al,Ti phase transfers to the stable D022-A1,Ti phase. 
Because the phase transformation temperature is 
around 782 K it is considered that an Al,,,,Ti,Fe,,Cr, 
alloy will have stable heat-resistance properties below

5. Summary 
Al-X-Fe, Al-Cu-Fe and Al-Fe-Cu-Ti based alloys 
extruded from their atomized powders have good 
room temperature mechanical properties and excellent 
heat-resistant properties. Some of them show the high 
temperature tensile strength as high as 350 MPa at 
573 K. The coexistence of Fe, Cu, Ti, Ce and Zr 
causes formation of a-Al, Al,,Fe, Al,,Fe,Cu, 
Al,Cu,Fe, Ll,-Al,Ti(Zr) and the Al-Cu-Fe-Ce-Zr cu- 
bic phase in the Al-Fe-Cu-Ti-Ce-Zr alloys. These 
phases contribute to good room temperature mechani- 
cal properties and excellent heat-resistant properties of 
these alloys.
