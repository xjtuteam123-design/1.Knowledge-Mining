ORIGINAL RESEARCH ARTICLE
Achieving Synchronous Improvement in Mechanical
Properties and Softening Resistance of Cu-Cr Alloys
by Introducing Ta
Ningyu Li, Shizhi Yu, Mingyang Li, Wenhao Yu, Renyi Jian, Yachao Yu, Xinyu Wang, and Yongqin Chang
Submitted: 23 April 2024 / Revised: 21 June 2024 / Accepted: 3 July 2024 / Published online: 20 December 2024
Cu-0.6 at.%Cr (Cu-Cr) and Cu-0.6 at.%Cr-0.6 at.%Ta (Cu-Cr-Ta) alloys were prepared by mechanical
alloying combined with spark plasm sintering. The effective reﬁnement of the microstructure along with the
signiﬁcantly increased dislocation density in Cu-Cr alloy is achieved through the introduction of Ta. Most
precipitates transform from Cr precipitates into Cr
2Ta Laves phases with semi-coherent interfacial
structure, which provides the effective inhibition effect to dislocation movement and grain coarsening, along
with signiﬁcantly enhanced mechanical properties. Compared with Cu-Cr alloy, 25% and 24% enhance-
ment for the yield strength (413 MPa) and ultimate tensile strength (497 MPa) is realized by Cu-Cr-Ta
alloy along with the acceptable elongation of 13.1%. It is worth noting that the signiﬁcant improvement for
high-temperature mechanical properties is also realized through the introduction of Ta, showing the 130%
and 124% enhancement of yield strength and ultimate tensile strength at 450 /C176C, respectively. The soft-
ening temperature of Cu-Cr-Ta alloy reaches 1015 /C176C, which is 16% higher than that of Cu-Cr alloy
(876 /C176C). This study provides an effective approach to improve the mechanical properties and softening
resistance of the traditional Cu-Cr alloys under the elevated temperature environment, which has a great
potential application in the ﬁeld of future fusion reactor.

1. Introduction
Cu-based binary alloys have been considered as the
favorable candidate in several areas, for example, the electro-
magnetic shielding materials, lead frames, electrical connectors,
the divertor heat sink of fusion reactors, and so on, due to their
excellent electrical conductivity, thermal conductivity, and
mechanical properties (Ref 1-5). Precipitation strengthened
copper alloys, especially for Cu-Cr alloys, exhibit the relatively
good comprehensive properties such as high strength and high
thermal conductivity (Ref 1, 6), which has been used as heat
sink materials for International Thermonuclear Energy Reactor
(ITER). With the development of the fusion reactor, more work
has been carried out to study new structural materials (Ref 7-
11), and more demanding requirements such as higher strength
and higher thermal stability have been put forward to meet the
service for Demonstration Reactor (DEMO) ( /C2420 MW/m
2)
(Ref 12, 13) compared with ITER ( /C2410 MW/m 2) (Ref 14).
However, due to the severe coarsening and re-dissolving of Cr
precipitates (Ref 15, 16), Cu-Cr alloys always show the poor
mechanical properties at high temperatures (exceeds 300 /C176C),
along with the weak softening resistance, which seriously limits
the application of Cu-Cr alloys under the elevated temperature
environment. Therefore, it is necessary to effectively improve
the mechanical properties and softening resistance of Cu-Cr
alloys to meet the requirements for DEMO.
To improve the mechanical properties of Cu-Cr alloys,
introducing trace amount of alloying elements or nanoparticles
into Cu matrix has been regarded as an effective way. Wang
et al. (Ref 17) effectively enhanced the hardness and ultimate
tensile strength of Cu-Cr alloys by adding trace amount of Y
and found that Y element effectively suppresses the coarsening
of Cr precipitates and inhibits the softening of matrix. Fernee
et al. (Ref 18) introduced small amount of Fe into Cu-Cr alloys
to promote Fe-rich precipitates to effectively stabilize the
microstructure, resulting in the improved strength and softening
resistance. Based on the previous research, although some work
on enhancing the strength and improving the softening
resistance of Cu-Cr alloys has been carried out, it is hard to
effectively enhance the strength while maintaining the high
softening resistance, and the high-temperature mechanical
properties of Cu-Cr alloys was few reported. Thus, to ensure
the safe operation of materials, more works on enhancing the
mechanical properties and softening resistance of Cu-Cr alloys
need to be carried out.

zero solubility within Cu lattice (Ref 19), and Ta precipitates
exhibit the excellent coarsening resistance even annealing at

on microstructure, mechanical properties at room and elevated

related mechanisms were also discussed.
2. Experimental Details
2.1 Materials and Preparation
Pure Cu powders (99.8%, average diameter of 48 lm), Cr
powders (99.9%, average diameter of 48 lm), and Ta powders
(99.9%, average diameter of 45 lm) were used as initial
materials. To avoid oxidation, all the powders were weighted in
the glove box under argon atmosphere. Cu powders and 0.6
at% Cr powders were placed in the stainless steel tank
containing stainless steel balls, and the ball milling was
conducted at 350 rpm for 40 h with the ball-to-material weight
ratio of 10:1. The obtained alloyed Cu-0.6 at.%Cr alloy
powders were named as Cu-Cr powders. Meanwhile, Cu-0.6
at.%Cr-0.6 at.%Ta powders were prepared by the same ball
milling parameters, and the obtained alloyed powders were
named as Cu-Cr-Ta powders. Spark plasm sintering was then
utilized to consolidate Cu-Cr and Cu-Cr-Ta powders at 850 /C176C
under 50 MPa for 7 min, followed by hot rolling with the
reduction of 70%. Before rolling, the alloys were held at
850 /C176C for 1 h. Finally, all the alloys were annealed at 350 /C176C
for 1 h to remove the residual stress.
2.2 Characterization and Measurement of Properties
The morphologies and size of ball-milled Cu-Cr and Cu-Cr-
Ta powders were observed by ﬁeld-emission type scanning
electron microscope (SEM, LEO1450). X-ray diffraction
(XRD) equipment with Cu K a radiation was utilized to analyze
the phase compositions of the alloys. Electron back-scattered
diffraction (EBSD) was adopted to study the characteristics of
grains and grain boundaries with Auger electron spectrometer
(AES, ULV AC-PHI710). The microstructure and precipitate
characteristics were studied by transmission electron micro-
scopy (TEM, JEM-2100F) with the accelerating voltage of
300 kV . The foils with the diameter of 3 mm were mechan-
ically thinned to /C24100 lm followed by ion milling for EBSD
and TEM observation. The hardness of the samples was tested
by a Vickers microhardness tester (401MVDTM) with the load
of 200 g for 15 s. The dog-bone-shaped specimens with the
gauge length of 10 mm and the cross section of 3 mm 9 1.5
mm cut along the rolling direction were used to test the tensile
mechanical property at room temperature by CMT4105 testing
machine with the test rate of 0.3 mm/min, and the 10 mm
extensometer was utilized to monitor the strain. The elevated
temperature tensile property of alloys was tested at 450 /C176Cb y
LE5105 testing machine with the test rate of 0.15 mm/min, and
the dog-bone-shaped specimens with the gauge length of 5 mm
and the cross section of 1 mm 9 0.8 mm were cut along the
rolling direction. The strain was monitored by the 5 mm
extensometer.

3.2 Mechanical Properties and Softening Resistance
Figure 8(a) shows the hardness values of two alloys, which
is 133 HV and 177 HV for Cu-Cr alloy and Cu-Cr-Ta alloy,
respectively, and 33% increment is achieved by introducing Ta.
The tensile engineering stress-strain curves of two alloys
measured at room temperature are exhibited in Fig. 8(b). The
yield strength (YS), ultimate tensile strength (UTS), and total
elongation (EL) of Cu-Cr alloy are 331 MPa, 402 MPa, and
28.9%, respectively. With the introduction of Ta, The YS and
UTS of the alloy reach to 413 MPa and 497 MPa, respectively,
along with the acceptable EL of 13.1%. Compared with Cu-Cr
alloy, due to the increased dislocation density and the
signiﬁcantly reﬁned grains induced by the introduction of Ta,
the dislocation strengthening and grain boundary strengthening
effects play the signiﬁcant role in strengthening the matrix.
What/C213s more, the formation of Cr
2Ta Laves phase obviously
overcomes the problems of severe coarsening and re-dissolving
of Cr precipitates, and the ﬁne and dispersed Cr
2Ta Laves
phases with semi-coherent interface further signiﬁcantly
enhance the inhibition effect to the movement of dislocations.
In addition, the addition of Ta inevitably introduces some Ta
nanoclusters with (semi-)coherent interface (Ref 30), which is
also beneﬁcial for improving the mechanical properties of
alloys (Ref 31). Thus, the signiﬁcant enhancement to the
strength contributed by the second phases is achieved. The
detailed values of YS, UTS, and EL of Cu-Cr and Cu-Cr-Ta
alloys are summarized in Fig. 8(c). 25% and 24% increment are
successfully achieved for YS and UTS with the addition of Ta,
which distinctly reveals that the obvious improvement in
mechanical properties of Cu-Cr alloys through the introduction
of Ta, and ﬁnally, the excellent comprehensive tensile property
is obtained for Cu-Cr-Ta alloy.
To further investigate the effect of Ta addition to mechanical
properties of Cu-Cr alloy under elevated temperature, the test of
tensile properties under 450 /C176C was conducted, and the tensile
engineering stress-strain curves are exhibited in Fig. 9(a). The
YS and UTS of Cu-Cr alloy are 56 and 70 MPa, respectively,
showing the relatively low strength on account of weak

Cu-Cr-Ta alloy exhibits the exceptional softening resistance
achieved by the introduction of Ta mainly attributed to the
signiﬁcantly improved microstructural stability induced by both
improved kinetic and thermodynamic stability (Ref 20). The
ﬁne and dispersed Cr
2Ta Laves phases and small amount of Ta
nanoclusters are regarded as the essential roles played in
inhibiting the dislocation gliding and grain coarsening even the
temperatures close to the T
m of Cu (Ref 32), especially for the
overcoming of the problem of the deterioration of mechanical
properties caused by the severe coarsening and re-dissolving of
Cr precipitates, which effectively improves the kinetic stability
of the alloys. Meanwhile, with the introduction of Ta, a small
part of Ta atoms segregated at grain boundaries are beneﬁcial to
reduce the grain boundary energy (Ref 33). In addition, a large
amount of HGBs and twin boundaries (Fig. 5c) with low grain
boundary energy in the alloy also play the important roles in
reducing the grain boundary energy of the alloy system and
blocking the dislocation gliding. Thus, the thermodynamic
stability of the alloy system is improved to some degree. The
signiﬁcantly improved microstructural stability is successfully
achieved by Cu-Cr-Ta alloy, and high strength and high
softening resistance are realized under the high-temperature
environment.
4. Conclusion
In this work, Cu-0.6 at.%Cr and Cu-0.6 at.%Cr-0.6 at.%Ta
alloys were prepared by mechanical alloying combined with
spark plasm sintering followed by hot rolling. The microstruc-
tures, mechanical properties, and softening resistance were
investigated in detail, and the main conclusions can be drawn as
follows:
(1) Work hardening degree of Cu-Cr alloy signiﬁcantly in-
creases due to the increased dislocation density after
introducing Ta, and the effective reﬁnement effect of
grains is further achieved. Meanwhile, the signiﬁcant
reﬁnement of precipitates is also achieved through the
introduction of Ta.
(2) The precipitates mostly transform from Cr precipitates
to Cr
2Ta Laves phases with semi-coherent interfacial
structures after introducing Ta into Cu-Cr alloy, which
provides the signiﬁcant inhibition effect to the disloca-
tion movement and grain coarsening.
(3) The signiﬁcantly enhanced strength is realized beneﬁted
from the improved dislocation strengthening and grain
boundary strengthening effects in Cu-Cr-Ta alloy. 33%,
25%, and 24% increment are achieved for hardness,
YS (331 MPa for Cu-Cr, 413 MPa for Cu-Cr-Ta) and
UTS (402 MPa for Cu-Cr, 497 MPa for Cu-Cr-Ta),
respectively, and 130% and 124% enhancement of YS
(56 MPa for Cu-Cr, 129 MPa for Cu-Cr-Ta) and UTS
(70 MPa for Cu-Cr, 157 MPa for Cu-Cr-Ta) are
achieved at 450 /C176C.
(4) The softening resistance of Cu-Cr alloy is signiﬁcantly

of Cu-Cr-Ta alloy is much higher than those of Cu-Cr al-

(5) The challenge of performance deterioration of Cu-Cr al-

is effectively overcome, and the great potential applica-
tion of Cu-Cr-Ta alloy in the ﬁeld of future fusion reac-
