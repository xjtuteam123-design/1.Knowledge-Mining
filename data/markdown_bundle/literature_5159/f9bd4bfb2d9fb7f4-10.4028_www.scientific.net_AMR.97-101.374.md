Hot Deformation and Processing Maps of a High Strength Alloy Steel 
Y.C. Lin a and Ge Liu b 
School of Mechanical and Electrical Engineering, Central South University, Changsha 410083, 
China 
 ayclin@mail.csu.edu.cn, b303458154@qq.com 
Keywords: Low alloy steel; Hot deformation; Processing map; Microstructural evolution 
Abstract. The hot deformation behaviors of a high strength low alloy steel have been studied using 
the processing map technique, which is based on the variations of efficiency of power dissipation with 
forming temperatures and strain rates. The results show that: (1) The average grain size of the 
deformed alloy steel increases with the increase of forming temperatures and decreases with the 
increase of strain rates. (2) The efficiency of power dissipation increases as the forming temperature is 
increased. However, the efficiency of power dissipation changes with strain rates in the form of bulgy 
parabola, and the maximum value exists at the strain rate of 0.1 -1 s . (3) A domain for reasonable 
dynamic recrystallization (DRX) exists in the temperature range of (1050–1150)℃ and strain rate 
range of (0.01–3)-1 s , with its peak efficiency of 32% at about 1140 °C and 0.23 -1 s , which are the 
optimum hot working parameters for the studied high strength low alloy steel. 
Introduction 
Bulk metal working processes are often carried out at elevated temperatures where the occurrence of 
simultaneous hardening and softening processes are both significantly affected by the forming 
temperatures and strain rates. The materials are subjected to complex time, strain, strain rate, and 
temperature histories in industrial forming processes. On one hand, a given combination of 
thermo-mechanical parameters yields a particular metallurgical phenomenon (microstructural 
evolution). On the other hand, microstructural changes of the metal in turn affect the mechanical 
characteristics of the metal such as the flow stress, and hence influence the forming process. An 
understanding of the material flow behaviors during hot working is essential for the optimization of 
the intrinsic workability and control of microstructural evolution [1-7]. 
The principle for the processing maps is the dynamic materials model based on fundamental 
properties of continuum mechanics of large plastic flow, physical system modeling, and irreversible 
thermodynamics. Essentially, the work-piece undergoing hot deformation can be considered as a 
dissipater of power. A superimposition of the instability map over the power dissipation map gives a 
processing map, which reveals the deterministic domains where individual microstructural processes 
occur and the limiting conditions for the regimes of flow instability. By processing under conditions 
of highest efficiency in the “safe” domains and by avoiding the regimes of flow instabilities, the 
intrinsic workability of the studied material may be optimized and microstructural control may be 
achieved [7-10]. 
In this study, the hot deformation behaviors of a high strength low alloy steel were investigate by 
processing map technique based on the variations of efficiency of power dissipation with forming 
temperature and strain rate. Finally, the optimized hot working process was obtained.

Results and discussion 
Typical processing maps generated in the temperature range of 850–1150 
℃ and strain rate range of 
0.01–50-1 s at a strain of 60% are shown in Fig. 1 and Fig. 2. The contours represent constant 
efficiencies of power dissipation expressed in percent. The shade areas denote the unsafe domains 
which are not suitable for bulk metal processing and should be avoided. Obviously, the domain with 
its peak power dissipation efficiency at about 1140℃ and 0.23 -1 s , and the peak efficiency arrives at 
32%. The domain observed in the processing map is generally identified by correlating the efficiency 
variation with temperature and strain rate. The microstructural changes that occur in the deformed 
specimens also give evidence regarding the particular mechanism dominating in the domain. On the 
basis of the hot deformed microstructural observations, the domain in the processing map can be 
interpreted in terms of DRX, and the full recrystallization region can be identified in the processing 
map at a strain of 0.6. The power dissipation map reveals that a main domain for reasonable dynamic 
recrystallization (DRX) exists in the temperature range of (1050–1150) ℃ and strain rate range of 
(0.01–3) -1 s , which are the optimum hot working parameters.

At a higher forming temperature, grain growth takes place during deformation and the equiaxed 
grain structures are obtained. The higher the forming temperature, the larger the dynamic 
recrystallisation degree. However, at the relatively low forming temperature (850 ℃ ), the elongated 
grains with serrations developed in the grain boundaries were observed, indicating that the dynamic 
recrystallization occurs in evidence during the hot compression deformation, and the small grains 
located along the grain boundaries of larger grains are typical of “necklace” type dynamic 
recrystallisation, which indicate the dynamic recrystallisation is not complete. With the increase of 
strain rate, dynamic recovery rate decreases. Meanwhile, the dislocation generation rate, the 
dislocation density and nucleation sites increase in the deformed microstructure. Then, for the case of 
high strain rate, there are more deformation energies stored in the deformed block. Therefore, it is

Conclusion 
Hot compression tests were performed on a high strength low alloy steel. The power dissipation 
efficiency and instability parameters were evaluated and processing map was made. The optimum 
domains for dynamic recrystallization occur in the temperature range of 1050-1150 ℃ and strain rate 
range of 0.01-3-1 s , with its peak efficiency of 32% is identified as optimum working regions.
