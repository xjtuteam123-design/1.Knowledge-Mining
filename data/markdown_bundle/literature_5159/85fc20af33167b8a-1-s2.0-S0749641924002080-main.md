ABSTRACT 
Additively manufacturing alloys by a selective laser melting (SLM) usually generates large tem -
perature gradients and rapidly cooling, which enables a refined microstructure, an elemental 
segregation and high-density dislocations network to achieve an excellent strength-ductility 
synergy. In this study, the SLM fabricates FeCoNiAlTi high-entropy alloys (HEAs) with a 
cellular structure composed of high-density dislocations network and elemental segregation, 
which results in a noteworthy combination of a yield strength and a significant uniform plastic 
elongation. Strengthening mechanism and deformation behavior of SLM-prepared FeCoNiAlTi 
HEAs are investigated by a transmission electron microscopy in combination with an in-situ 
neutron diffraction technique. The results demonstrate that the high strength is mainly derived 
from cellular structure strengthening, which accounted for over 64 % of the yield strength. The 
cellular structure ’ s capability to alleviate severe stress concentrations can facilitate deformation 
homogenization, and break a strength-ductility trade-off. This study provides essential insights 
into the underlying mechanisms governing the strength and ductility of additively manufactured 
HEAs. 
1. Introduction 
High-entropy alloys (HEAs) is believed to be a great promise novel structural material, which are composed of multiple (five or 
more) elements with atomic percentages ranging from 5 to 35 % for each constituent element ( Lu et al., 2020 ; Yeh et al., 2004 ; Zhang 
et al., 2014 ). The specific chemical composition of HEAs leads to an increase in the mixed conformational entropy ( > 1.61 R , where R is 
Avogadro constant), thereby aiding in overcoming the enthalpy of compound formation, which facilitates formations of stable 
solid-solution phases ( Lu et al., 2020 ; Tsai and Yeh, 2014 ). A high-entropy effect, together with a slow diffusion, a lattice distortion and 
a cocktail effect, are called four effects of HEAs ( George et al., 2019 ; Xin et al., 2020 ). The excellent mechanical properties exhibited by

plasticity cannot be quantitatively assessed by TEM( An et al., 2023 ). 
In conclusion, a characterization of the dislocations in AM samples is of importance to explore the structural origins responsible for 
the unprecedented mechanical properties s. Due to a high penetrating ability in most alloys, neutron beam can be used to investigate 
the average microstructural response of alloys based on a neutron diffraction (ND) technique ( Bacon and Thewlis, 1949 ; Liu et al., 
2020 ). The dislocation induced elastic field can alter local lattice spacing, which can be manifested in broadening of ND peak ( Ung ´ar 
et al., 1989 ). Diffraction line profile analysis (LPA) can quantitatively determine dislocation density ( ρ ) and evaluate 
dislocation-arrangement parameters (M) based on the ND peak ( Ungar et al., 1998 ; Wilkens, 1970 ). Furthermore, the ND is a 
nondestructive test that allows to in-situ study a lattice strain, dislocation, a stacking lamellar dislocation, twinning, a phase trans -
formation and a weave evolution during alloy stretching ( Bae et al., 2019 ; Cai et al., 2017 ; Diao et al., 2019 ; Kim et al., 2021 ; Lam et al., 
2022 ; Lee et al., 2023 ). 
In this study, we prepared a FeCoNiAlTi HEA with cellular structure by selective-laser melting (SLM), which has excellent ductility 
and strength. Utilizing ND and TEM for calculating the dislocation density of the alloy and characterizing elemental segregation 
confirmed that its unique cellular structure serves as the principal strengthening mechanism. Meanwhile, in-situ ND and TEM 
demonstrated the stabilization of the cellular structure, which explains the material ’ s excellent ductility. The results obtained deepen 
the understanding of strengthening mechanism and deformation behavior of cellular structure in additively manufactured HEAs, 
contributing to the design and fabrication of high-performance HEAs by SLM.

2.2. Microstructural characterization 
The microstructure, chemical composition and particle size distribution of metal powders were analyzed in a scanning electron 
microscope (SEM) (Hitachi SU-1510), a plasma optical emission spectrometer (ICP-OES, 7300DV) and a laser particle size analyzer 
(Bettersize 3000). The microstructures of alloys were characterized by an optical Microscope (OM, Zeiss Axiolab 5), a SEM (Zeiss 
Gemini300) with an electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) (Oxford Symmetry S2). The accelerating voltage for EBSD measurements 
was 20 kV with a step size of 0.8 μ m. The HEAs were mechanically polished, and finally polished in a vibratory polisher (VIBROTECH 
300) or by etching with aqua regia (10 vol. %). 
The undeformed and deformed HEAs were characterized in a transmission electron microscope (TEM) (Talos F200X G2) equipped 
with a windowless four-probe Super-X super-energy spectrometry detector. The TEM specimens were firstly mechanically ground to a 
thickness of 50 μ m, and then thinned in an electrolytic double spray thinning apparatus (TJ-100SE) with 10 % perchloric acid alcohol 
as the double spraying solution. 
2.3. In-situ neutron diffraction 
Neutron diffraction (ND) experiments were performed at a General-Purpose Powder Diffractometer (GPPD) in the China Spallation 
Neutron Source (CSNS). The GPPD used the time-of-flight method to collect sample information, which was equipped with two ± 90
◦
detector sets to collect the diffraction information of HEAs ( Chen et al., 2018 ). Neutron diffraction experiments were performed on 
HEA powders, SLM-FCNAT, H-FCN, CR-FCN and strain-free standard Si powders mounted in TiZr cans. Instrument parameters, e.g., 
primary and secondary flight path distances, and a calibration of instrument peak spreading effect were determined by measuring Si 
specimens. 
In-situ stretching experiments were conducted in a BIOWAS stretching machine equipped with GPPD. The stretching sample with a 
thickness of 3 mm, a width of 4 mm and a pitch segment length of 32 mm was mounted vertically on the diffractometer ’ s stretching 
machine. The stretching direction of sample was perpendicular to the direction of incident neutron beam, and the pitch segment was 
irradiated by an incident neutron beam with an area of 8 × 20 mm. The slit size of fine collimator receiving the diffracted neutron beam 
was 2 mm. A constant-load control mode with a stepwise loading sequence was used during the measurement of diffraction pattern. A 
measurement time of neutron diffraction data was 60 min at each stress level to obtain good statistical diffraction data. 
2.4. Analysis of diffraction data 
Diffraction peak profile can include microstructural information, such as lattice parameter, phase fraction, dislocation density and 
dislocation arrangement. Full-spectrum fitting the neutron diffraction data by a Rietveld method in a GSAS software can provide the 
lattice parameters and phase fractions.

3.1. Initial microstructure 
Compared with conventional preparation methods, the alloy prepared by additive manufacturing has a unique microstructure ( Mu 
et al., 2022 ; Xu et al., 2023 ). The plane perpendicular to the 3D-printing building direction (BD) displays a discernible laser scanning 
trajectory, in which neighboring strip laser fusion channels form an angle of 67
◦

( Fig. 2(a) ). The sub-crystalline structures within the strip laser scan tracks exhibit a well-defined alignment ( Fig. 2(b) ). The grains 
present an equiaxed morphology in the BD with an average grain size of 12.1 ± ± 0.5 μ m. A notable orientation preference is observed, 
which is that most of grains exhibit a growth along a (100) direction ( Fig. 2(c) ). Due to a thermally residual stress during the SLM 
preparation, a localized distortions and high densities of sub-grain boundaries with dislocation angles of 2

The EBSD result shows that the volume fraction of FCC matrix phase approaches 99 %, and the precipitated phase of BCC structure is 
less than 1 %, which is basically consistent with the phase composition of the powder. The grains in the H-FCN and CR-FCN exhibit a 
sub-equiaxed morphology without any apparent preferential orientation ( Fig. 2(e) and ( F )). 
A dark field scanning transmission microscopy (DF-STEM) image shows that the dislocation configuration in the SLM-FCNAT is 
significantly different from that prepared by the casting alloys, exhibiting a hexagonal structure of dislocation cells with an average 
size of about 0.7 μ m ( Ji et al., 2022 ; Liu et al., 2018 ). HDDs are interwoven to form cell walls at the sub-crystalline boundaries. The 
dislocation density at grain interiors is relatively low. Additionally, a small quantity of L2
1 
phase precipitates with a size of 
approximately 80 nm can be observed at the cell walls ( Fig. 3(a) ). An inherent locally heating/cooling thermal cycle created during 
laser cyclic scanning must introduce residual stresses, which may cause that the dislocations proliferate and self-adapt into cells if the 
residual stress is high enough to initiate a plastic deformation ( Birnbaun et al., 2019 ; Kong et al., 2020 ; Wang et al., 2020 ). Fig. 3(b) 
displays a STEM image and a corresponding energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) analysis of the cellular structure, revealing 
notable elemental segregation at the cell walls. This segregation is primarily characterized to be an enrichment of Ti, an absence of Fe, 
and a presence of a small amount of Al-rich oxide within the cell interior. This elemental segregation is resulted from that a solvent 
repels a low-solubility solute at the solid-liquid interface, and accumulates at the solid/liquid interface ( Sames et al., 2016 ). The HDDs 
network and elemental segregation together form the cellular structure in SLM-FCNAT. The L2
1 
phase precipitates are harder than the 
FCC phase substrate, and can impede the movement of dislocations as does the enrichment of Ti at the sub-grain boundaries 
( Choudhuri et al., 2015 ; Yang et al., 2018 ).

4.1.1. Solid solution strengthening 
In generally, an evaluation of the effect of solid-solution strengthening is based on dilute-solution alloys, especially in binary 
systems. However, the terms “ solute ” and “ solvent ” lose their conventional meaning for HEAs, which are commonly known as 
“ concentrated solid solution ” systems. How to evaluate or even define the exact contribution of solid solution strengthening in HEAs

4.2. Plastic deformation 
The SLM-FCNAT HEA has the very high ductility, which presumably stem from the stable cellular structure. To investigate the 
plastic behavior of SLM-FCNAT HEA, the fractured samples were characterized by TEM. Compared to that in the undeformed samples, 
the dislocation density of fractured HEA is increased significantly. The HDDs network does not disappear, which proves that the 
cellular structure has a good structural stability during the plastic deformation ( Fig. 11(a) ). The dislocations can organize to be 
dislocation arrays by slip and multiplication ( Fig. 11(b) ). No twinning or stacking faults (SFs) are observed after fracture ( Fig. 11(b) ).

relatively high SFEs (~ 70 mJ/m

5. Conclusion 
In summary, utilizing the characteristics of ultra-fast cooling rate during the SLM technique, we have successfully synthesized the 
SLM-FCNAT HEA with a unique microstructure, i.e., the cellular structure composed of the HDDs network and elemental segregation. 
This cellular structure leads to a very excellent combination of strength and ductility. The SLM-FCNAT HEA contains HDDs network, 
generated during the SLM preparation process, with a density of 14.1 × 10
14 
m
2 
that is similar to the value (15.0 × 10
14 
m
2

concentrate at the sub-grain boundary to form the cellular structure accompanied by the elemental segregation. The dislocation 
densities at the cell interiors and cell walls are different considerably, which are 8.6 × 1014 m2 and 63.6 × 1014 m2, respectively. The 
cellular structure, consisting of dislocation and compositional segregation, in the SLM-FCNAT HEA give a significant contribution to 
the yield strength. Cellular structure strengthening contributes 508 MPa and plays a major role in the four strengthening mechanisms 
of the SLM-FCNAT HEA, which accounts for about 80 % of strengthening effect. With plastic straining, the cellular structure can 
alleviate the severe stress concentration and facilitate the homogeneous deformation. The pinning effect due to the solute atoms and 
the L2
1 
phase at the sub-grain boundary effectively stabilize the cellular structure. In-situ ND investigations reveal the structural 
stability of cellular structure even at a high plastic flow stress. The cellular structure cause that our SLM-FCNAT can not only has the 
high work-hardening capability and deformation stability but also obtain the extremely high strength under the premise of a high 
uniform elongation.
