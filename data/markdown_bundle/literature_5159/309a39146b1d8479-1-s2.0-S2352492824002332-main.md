ABSTRACT 
β -Ti-Nb alloys have drawn widespread attention on account of their excellent mechanical properties, phase 
stability, and chemical performance. In this study, first-principles calculations are utilized to examine how the 
non-metallic element O affects the stability and mechanical characteristics of β -Ti
3
Nb. The most remarkable 
result of the change from the lattice constants is that O induces α ” phase structure. The computation results of the 
elastic modulus and phonon spectrum confirm the structural and dynamical stability of all the Ti-Nb-O solid 
solutions. In addition, the ideal tensile and shear strength in various directions are predicted by simulating 
tensile and shear deformations. The results indicate that the theoretical strength of β -Ti
3
Nb is improved when 
maintaining O concentration within a certain range. Particularly, the 20 at% Ti-Nb-O system shows extraordi -
nary shear strength and tensile strength. Finally, the bonding characteristics of the Ti-Nb-O systems under stress 
loading are analyzed by electronic structure. The results in this work have great significance for the design and 
application of Ti-Nb alloys. 
1. Introduction 
Titanium alloys have been extensively applied in aerospace, bio -
logical materials, and marine fields owing to their superior mechanical 
properties, such as low density, good heat resistance, high strength, and 
great corrosion resistance [1 – 4] . Compared with steels, the mass of Ti 
alloys is only 60% of that at the same strength. Currently, Ti alloys 
containing transition metal (TM) elements such as V, Ta, Mo, W, and Nb 
have been extensively studied [5 – 8] . Particularly, Nb element has 
excellent biocompatibility and plays a significant role in the decrease of 
Young ’ s modulus [9] . Therefore, Ti-Nb alloys have attracted great in -
terest because of their superior corrosion resistance, high ductility, and 
good biocompatibility. 
Recently, widespread researches have been conducted on Ti-Nb al -
loys [10 – 15] . Han et al. [2] discovered that the corrosion resistance of 
Ti-Nb alloys is better than that of commercially pure Ti (cp-Ti) in the 
electrochemical experiments. Moreover, extensive studies have found 
no toxicity with the addition of Nb element, and Ti-Nb alloys have 
superelasticity and outstanding shape memory phenomenon. Guo et al. 
[11] added Sn in β -TiNb and discovered that the addition of Sn would 
inhibit the transformation of the β phase and improve the stability of 
β -TiNb. With the increasing Sn content, the Young ’ s modulus of TiNb 
alloys had little change while water-quenched TiNb alloys showed lower 
Young ’ s moduli. The study revealed that the β -Ti
3
Nb alloy, whose 
chemical composition is close to Ti-25 at%Nb, exhibited low Young ’ s 
modulus [16 – 18] . It has been confirmed that the addition of metal or 
non-metallic solute atoms has a significant impact on the mechanical 
behavior of Ti alloys [19] , for instance, the addition of TM elements 
enhances the stability of β phase [5] while nonmetallic elements such as 
C, N, and O are known as solid solution reinforcements in Ti alloys. 
Especially O concentration has an intensive impact on the plasticity and 
elastic anisotropy of Ti alloys [20 – 24] . Introducing oxygen into the α -Ti 
alloys can produce a significant strengthening effect, and oxygen addi -
tion can dramatically improve their elasticity, strength, and ductility 
properties. In the manufacturing of β -Ti-Nb-O alloys, O is commonly 
introduced in the form of TiO
2
. Consequently, it may lead to uneven 
distribution of O in the Ti-Nb-O alloys after the O diffuses into the Ti-Nb 
matrix, resulting in different mechanical properties of the Ti-Nb alloys. 
Therefore, an in-depth exploration of the influence of non-metallic atom 
O on the comprehensive mechanical properties of Ti-Nb-O system, as 
well as a detailed understanding of the potential factors affecting per -
formance and their inherent connections, are of great significance for 
the design of Ti-Nb alloys under different service conditions. Never -
theless, the influence of O on the mechanical properties of β -Ti
3
Nb lacks

stronger hybridization between the d-orbital of Ti, the d-orbital of Nb,

increase, and interaction stabilizes the structure, which means that the 
performance of Ti-Nb alloys are enhanced by increasing O content.

3.3. Ideal tensile strengths 
Exploring tensile stress-strain responses is crucial for a fuller un -
derstanding of the mechanical properties of materials, which can usually 
be evaluated through theoretical strength [42 – 48] . Therefore, in order to 
explore the influence of O atoms on the mechanical properties of 
β -Ti
3
Nb crystals, the ideal tensile strength and ideal shear strength of 
Ti-Nb system and all the Ti-Nb-O systems with different O concentra -
tions in different directions were calculated. In this work, we mainly 
studied three representative crystallization directions: [001], [100], and 
[111], as well as four independent sliding systems: (001) [010], (010) 
[100], (100) [001], and (100) [010]. 
As shown in Fig. 6 , the ideal tensile strength and stress-strain 
response process of the Ti-Nb-O system were first investigated in the 
[001], [100], and [111] directions. It is observed that there are signif -
icant differences in stress-strain responses among the three tensile di -
rections, indicating that the tensile deformation is anisotropic in three 
directions. The weakest tensile strength in the [001] direction indicates 
that the system is most prone to fracture along this direction, which is 
similar to other materials with body centered cubic structures [42] . And 
the stress-strain curves shapes of the directional system in [111] are 
similar, demonstrating the optimal tensile performance. Fig. 6 (a) shows 
the tensile stress-strain curves in [001] direction. The stress of Ti-Nb-O 
solid solutions with β -Ti

3.4. Ideal shear strengths 
Subsequently, we calculated the shear stress-strain curves of the Ti- 
Nb system and Ti-Nb-O system with different O concentrations along 
four slip directions (001) [010], (010) [100], (100) [001], and (100) 
[010], as shown in Fig. 7 . Meanwhile, Table 3 summarizes the ideal 
tensile strength, ideal shear strength, and corresponding critical strain of 
all systems along different deformation directions. Firstly, it can be seen 
that the stress of all systems along the four slip directions gradually rises 
with increasing strain from Fig. 7 , and reaches maximum value, grad -
ually starting to decrease. It is observed that both the Ti-Nb system and 
Ti-Nb-O systems along the (100) [010] sliding direction exhibit excel -
lent shear resistance, and the shear strength of Ti-Nb system and solid 
solution system with O content of 6 at%, 11 at%, 20 at%, and 27 at% are 
5.60, 10.51, 11.20, 16.85, and 16.41 GPa, respectively, and the corre -
sponding strains are 0.26, 0.33, 0.29, 0.42, 0.37. It can be found that the 
shear strength of four solid solution systems with different O content all 
rise, and the critical strains also increase accordingly. In addition, the 
shear strength of all systems along the (100) [001] direction is the 
weakest, indicating that this direction is the most prone to slip under 
external action. In particular, it can be found that the 20 at% Ti-Nb-O 
system has excellent shear strength and tensile strength under four 
slip systems, manifesting that the 20 at% Ti-Nb-O system has excellent 
mechanical properties.

elastic constants and phonon spectrum. Furthermore, the effect of O
