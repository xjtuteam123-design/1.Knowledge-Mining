1. Introduction
Considering the energy-efficient improvement and great engineering 
safety factors, the fulfilment of high strength and elongation in metals is 
a vital timeless pursuit for material science. Unfortunately, almost all 
materials typically suffer from a strength-ductility trade-off limitation 
over a broad operating temperature range, which has sparked a wave of 
study in materials science [ 1 – 3 ]. Recently, several special approaches, 
such as engineered heterogeneous nanostructures [ 4 – 8 ], multiple effects 
of non-metallic elements in alloys [ 9 – 11 ], have been of great importance 
to break the strength-ductility trade-off. For example, it was found that 
the interstitial solid solution of boron in an eutectic high entropy alloy 
(EHEA) brought significant phase boundaries (PBs), grain boundaries 
(GBs), and precipitation strengthening effect, which achieved synergis -
tic improvement of plasticity and strength [ 10 ].
Admittedly, Nb-doping is one of the most common means to 
dramatically improve the mechanical properties in Fe-based or Ni-based 
alloys [ 12 – 16 ], and several mechanisms have been suggested to explain 
its potential impact. First of all, the classical theory shows Nb-doping are 
prone to significant lattice distortion owing to their large atomic size 
differences, which impedes the movement of dislocations during plastic 
deformation. However, Liu et al. emphasized the over-doping of Nb may 
destabilized the matrix and promote the emergence of the Nb-enriched 
Laves phases, thus causing the alloy to undergo a distinct ductile-to- 
brittle transition [ 17 ]. Secondly, Nb addition can delay the growth of 
the grain boundaries by the interaction of Nb element and grain 
boundary, thereby refining the grains of alloys. Thirdly, Cardoso et al. 
indicates the sensitivity characteristics of microstructures induced by Nb 
element [ 16 ]. Wang et al. displayed that minor Nb adjunction in Ni- 
based superalloy encouraged the transition from of granular and thin 
rod-like M
2
C carbides formed at the matrix and grain boundaries into 
needle shaped MC carbides [ 18 ]. However, the previous studies mainly 
concentrated in the role of Nb on the room temperature mechanical 
properties of single-phase alloys and solid solution strengthened alloys, 
rather than FCC/BCC/B2 MPEAs. The impacts of Nb addition on the 
high-temperature mechanical properties and deformation behavior in 
FCC/BCC/B2 MPEAs need to be explored.

3. Results and discussions
The XRD pattern in Fig. 1 (a) reveals the presence of FCC, BCC and the 
ordered B2 phases in of 0Nb and 0.5Nb alloys. Similar to most literature 
reports, the addition of Nb caused a slight left shift, as shown in Fig. 1 (b) 
that was an enlarged XRD image ranging from 48 to 53 degree, indi -
cating an increase in the lattice constants and crystal plane spacing ac -
cording to the Bragg ’ s law. In addition, all diffraction peak intensity 
gradually increased with Nb-doping.
The primitive microstructures and phase distribution were carefully 
investigated by multiscale microscopic characterization, as shown in 
Fig. 2 . There is no real distinction between the observed BSE 
morphology of the 0Nb and 0.5Nb MPEA as shown in Fig. 2 (a). It can be 
clearly observed that the 0Nb and 0.5Nb MPEA both exhibits the fea -
tures of extensive B2 particles precipitated in the BCC matrix and bulk 
lath-like FCC phase embedded on the BCC grain boundary. However, 
distinctly different from the 0Nb, the designed 0.5Nb MPEA shows the 
increased volume fraction of BCC phases from 30.9 % to 40.9 % (see the 
EBSD phase-index map in Fig. 2 (b)). Although similar phase trans -
formation induced by Nb addition was unusually common in steels, the 
complex electronic field environment in FeNiCrAl based alloys made it 
easier to form intermediate phases [ 22 ]. The inverse pole figure (IPF) 
maps of the 0Nb and 0.5Nb MPEA shows a relatively random crystal -
lographic texture (seen Fig. 2 (c)). In addition, the average sizes of the 
BCC and FCC phases according to the statistics of size distribution were 
2.59, 3.79, 2.85 and 3.68 μ m in the 0Nb and 0.5Nb MPEA, respectively, 
as can be ascertained from Table 1 , which indicates the addition of Nb 
do not produce the effect of grain refinement in this study.
