Abstract
The high-temperature strength and deformation behavior of c/c0 two-phase Co–Al–W-base alloys have been studied with polycrys-
talline and single-crystal materials. The ternary, quaternary and higher-order alloys containing Ta, Cr and/or Re exhibit ﬂow stress
anomalies above 873 K due to slip of pairs of 1/2 h110 i superpartial dislocations on {0 0 1} planes, in addition to {1 1 1} planes, in
the c
0 precipitates. Compression tests on the single-crystal specimens reveal a true anomalous peak temperature of 1073 K for both
ternary and Ta-containing quaternary alloys. Above the peak, the ternary alloy exhibits a rapid decrease in strength with temperature,
as 1/2h110 i dislocations bypass the c
0 precipitates without signiﬁcant shearing. Conversely, the Ta-containing quaternary alloy sustains
strength to higher temperatures due to the activation of 1/3 h112 i partial dislocation slip that introduces a high density of stacking faults
in the c0 precipitates.

1. Introduction
The development of superalloys has been driven by the
demand to increase the operating temperatures of gas tur-
bines in power plants and aircraft engines. Among various
classes of superalloys, including Fe-base, Co-base and Ni-
base superalloys, Ni-base superalloys strengthened with
the L1
2 compound (c0 phase) possess the highest heat resis-
tance. For the highest-temperature applications, they are
solidiﬁed into a single-crystal form, and alloyed to achieve
a high volume fraction of the c
0 precipitates, good solid-
solution strengthening of the c and c0 phases along with
environmental resistance. The superior strength of c/c0
Ni-base superalloys at elevated temperature can be attrib-
uted to a high volume fraction of regularly aligned coher-
ent c
0 precipitates with a cuboidal shape that inhibit the
dislocation glide in the c channels [1,2]. In the most
advanced Ni-base single-crystal superalloys, the volume
fraction of the c0 phase reaches approximately 70%.
Recently, a stable ternary Co 3(Al, W) phase with L1 2
structure was discovered by Sato et al. [3]. The c0-Co3
(Al, W) phase thermodynamically equilibrates with c-Co
(fcc) solid-solution phase in the Co–Al–W ternary alloy
system at 1173 K. In addition, the small misﬁt between
the c and c
0 phases permits the formation of a coherent
two-phase microstructure where submicron cuboidal c0
precipitates align regularly in a continuous matrix phase.
This is morphologically identical to the microstructure of
c/c
0 two-phase Ni-base superalloys. Conventional Co-base
superalloys lack eﬀective precipitation strengthening by
intermetallic compounds, and depend on solid-solution ele-
ments and precipitation of low volume fraction of carbides
for their strength. Despite their inferior strength compared
to the Ni-base superalloys, Co-base superalloys generally
oﬀer better oxidation, corrosion and wear resistance, and
have therefore been utilized in components where environ-
mental resistance is required, such as gas turbine vanes,

To explore the potential mechanical properties of this
new alloy system, the temperature dependence of the
0.2% ﬂow stress of the Co–Al–W ternary and Ta-contain-
ing quaternary alloys has been investigated [5]. These
polycrystalline alloys exhibit anomalous temperature
dependence above 873 K, and have peak strengths at
973 K and 1073 K, respectively. Due to the ﬂow stress
anomaly, the Ta-containing alloy exhibits strength compa-
rable to a commercial polycrystalline Ni-base superalloy at
1173 K. Transmission electron microscopy studies on
deformed specimens indicated that the activation of multi-
ple slip modes is responsible for the occurrence of the ﬂow
stress anomalies in the Ta-containing alloy [5].
In this study, polycrystalline and single-crystal Co–Al–
W-base alloys containing additional alloying elements were
used to obtain further understanding of the deformation
behavior and strengthening mechanisms in this new c/c
0
system. The temperature dependence of the strength was
examined in compression, and detailed examination of dis-
location substructure was conducted by transmission elec-
tron microscopy. The role of alloying elements and future
approaches for alloy improvement are discussed.
2. Experimental procedures
The nominal compositions of polycrystalline and single-
crystal alloys are listed in Table 1. Ternary alloys are here-
after referred to with reference to their W concentration,
and the others are referred to according to their quaternary
element concentration or elements added to the ternary
system, as listed in Table 1 . Two ternary alloy composi-
tions were chosen based on the reported phase diagram
[3] to vary the volume fractions of the c

fractions are low in the 9W alloy and high in the 11W alloy.
Ta was added to improve the strength and stability of the c

phase [3,5]. Re was chosen to enhance the solid-solution
strength of the c matrix phase [4]. The combined addition
of Cr and Ta aimed to improve both strength and environ-

The polycrystalline alloys were melted as 65 g button
ingots using a Crystalox induction levitation melting sys-
tem under an Ar back-ﬁlled atmosphere after evacuation.
Pure 99.9% Co and 99.99% Al pellets and a combination
of compressed high-purity elemental powders of 99.5%
Co, 99.95% W, 99.9% Ta, 99.99% Re and 99.95% Cr were
utilized. These alloys except for CrTa were homogenized at
1623 K for 43.2 ks. CrTa was homogenized at 1573 K for
21.6 ks. Following this, the 2Ta alloy was aged at 1273 K
for 360 ks, and the others were aged at 1173 K for
540 ks. For these heat treatments, the alloys were sealed
in quartz capsules, which were argon back-ﬁlled after
evacuation.
For single-crystal material preparation, polycrystalline
ingots of 11W and 2Ta alloys 2.5 kg in weight were fabri-
cated by Sophisticated Alloys Inc., Butler, PA, USA. These
ingots were directionally solidiﬁed as cylindrical bars (19

3.2. Temperature dependence of the ﬂow stress
The 0.2% ﬂow stress for each of the polycrystalline
alloys is plotted as a function of temperature in Fig. 3 a.
For comparison, the 0.2% yield stresses of conventional
Ni-base cast and superalloy (MarM247), sheet Ni-base
superalloy (IN617), cast Co-base superalloy (MarM302)
and wrought Co-base superalloy (Haynes 188) are also
included [7]. All examined alloys exhibited a similar tem-
perature dependence of the ﬂow stress with three regimes
of behavior. The ﬁrst stage is the normal decrease of ﬂow
stress with temperature up to 873 K. The second stage dis-
plays anomalous positive temperature dependence, and the
third stage again exhibits negative temperature depen-
dence. The ternary alloys, 9W and 11W, exhibit almost
equivalent strength, regardless of the varying volume frac-
tions of the c
0 precipitates. They have a ﬂow stress of about
520 MPa at room temperature. With increasing tempera-
ture, these alloys show a minimum of 420 MPa at 873 K,
and then show a maximum of about 460 MPa at 973 K,
followed by a rapid decrease with temperature. The 2Re
exhibits the same trend as the ternary alloys, but the
strength level is 150–200 MPa lower, probably due to the
formation of the c +C o
3W two-phase layer along the grain
boundaries (Fig. 1d). On the other hand, the Ta-containing
alloys, 2Ta and CrTa, exhibit an extended anomalous
strengthening stage. These alloys possess similar strength
up to 1073 K. The peak temperature is located at 1073 K
and the ﬂow stress reaches close to 580 MPa. Above the
peak, the 2Ta alloy exhibited a slight decrease in the ﬂow
stress up to 1173 K, followed by a rapid decrease to
430 MPa at 1210 K. In the CrTa alloy, the onset of the
third stage occurs above 1073 K. It is apparent that addi-
tions of Ta signiﬁcantly improve the high-temperature
strength in the Co–Al–W alloy system. The earlier onset
of the third stage in CrTa compared with 2Ta can be attrib-
uted to the lower c
0 solvus temperature. Compared with the
conventional superalloys, the ternary alloys exhibit almost
same strength as cast Co-base superalloys. The strength of
the Ta-containing alloys exceeds the conventional Co-base
cast superalloys above 973 K due to the large ﬂow stress
anomalies. Importantly, the ﬂow stress of 2Ta is compara-
ble to that of the Ni-base superalloy above 1173 K.

3.3. Dislocation substructures of polycrystalline 2Ta alloy
The inverse dependence of the ﬂow stress on tempera-
ture and the appearance of the peak in the ﬂow stress at ele-
vated temperatures are phenomena observed in L1
2 and
other intermetallic compounds [8–16] and also in c/c0 Ni-
base superalloys [7,8,16–19]. It is generally understood that
the anomalous strengthening in L1 2 compounds is caused
by pinning of cross-slipped screw segments of superdisloca-
tions from the octahedral {1 1 1} plane to the cube {1 0 0}
plane, which is driven by elastic anisotropy and/or lower
anti-phase boundary (APB) energy on {0 0 1} planes
[8,13–16,20–22]. In order to understand the origin of the
ﬂow stress anomalies and strengthening mechanisms of
the Co–Al–W-base alloys, dislocation substructures were
examined in detail in the polycrystalline 2Ta alloy speci-
mens after compression tests.
Fig. 4 shows general features of the dislocation substruc-
tures observed in 2Ta after deformation in compression at
873, 1073 and 1163 K, which correspond to temperatures
below, near-peak and above the peak anomalous ﬂow
stress. At 873 K ( Fig. 4 a) and 1073 K ( Fig. 4 b), disloca-
tions are present preferentially in the c matrix channels that
contain hyperﬁne c
0 precipitates. Additionally, some dislo-
cations are trapped within the c0 precipitates. Stacking
faults extending across the precipitates are occasionally
observed, and they are more frequent at 1073 K than at
873 K. Fig. 4 c is taken from the specimen deformed at
1163 K. The observation from the [1 0 1] direction reveals
a high density of planar faults on two {1 1 1} planes. These
faults are stacking faults that extend across the entire diam-
eter of the c
0 precipitates. Thus, stacking faults become the
dominant defects in the precipitates with increasing tem-
perature from 1073 to 1163 K.

4. Discussion
Nickel-base alloys exhibit a unique combination of high-
temperature deformation mechanisms that to date have not
been duplicated in any higher melting point materials that
might displace superalloys from technologically important
systems such as aircraft engines and power-generation tur-
bines. Nickel-base alloys have a high resistance to deforma-
tion at high fractions of their melting temperature due to
the diﬃculty of shearing the ordered L1
2 precipitates at
moderate stresses and strain rates [17]. However, since
the precipitates may eventually be sheared by superdisloca-
tions, as the stresses rise to high levels at crack tips or under
high rate (foreign object damage) conditions, this class of
materials maintains a reasonably high level of ductility
and toughness, preventing catastrophic failure. Higher-
temperature refractory alloys and intermetallic com-
pounds, with generally high strengths and accompanying
high resistances to dislocation motion, do not generally dis-
play these characteristics and thus have not experienced
widespread engineering application.
The cobalt-based materials examined here, in many
important respects, possess similar high-temperature defor-
mation attributes compared to nickel-base superalloys.
Deformation is initiated by glide of 1/2 h110 i dislocations
in the fcc c matrix channels. As the stresses rise, the precip-
itates begin to deform by glide of h110 i superdislocations
on octahedral and cube planes or by motion of 1/3 h112 i
dislocations across the precipitates, with accompanying
stacking-fault formation. However, important diﬀerences
in the deformation modes exist in the Ta-containing ver-
sions of these Co-base materials at temperatures above
1073 K. Stacking fault formation in the L1
2 precipitates
above the peak temperature results in very high work hard-
ening rates, giving these Co-base materials potentially
higher strengths compared to Ni-base materials at the high-
est end of the temperature spectrum. A more detailed
understanding of the partitioning of the Ta between the
matrix and precipitate phases is needed to further exploit
this unusual deformation characteristic. Nevertheless, this
present study suggests that further alloying of these mate-
rials may result in Co-base alloys with properties superior
to Ni-base alloys above 1073 K. For this reason, it is worth
considering several issues important to alloying for further
improvements in the properties of these materials.
Compared with the Ni-base superalloys, the Co–Al–W
alloys have higher liquidus temperatures by more than
50 K [6] as is shown in Table 2 . This suggests that there
is a possibility for greater temperature capability compared
to Ni-base alloys. However, the c
0 solvus temperatures of
the Co–Al–W-base alloys are lower by more than 100 K.
The thermal stability of the c
0 phase is key to improving
the high-temperature strength of c/c0 alloys. In addition,
the shearing mechanisms of the c0 phase at elevated temper-
ature were important for sustaining the strength above the
anomalous peak temperature. Based on the comparison of
the dislocation substructures of single-crystal 2Ta and 11W
(Figs. 8 and 9 ), an addition of Ta seems to reduce the stak-
ing fault energy of the c
0 phase. The stacking-fault energy is
closely related to the diﬀerence in bulk free energy of face-
centered cubic (fcc) and hexagonal close packed (hcp)
phases and the magnetic surface energy [28,29]. Therefore,
additions of elements that can increase the c0 solvus

wide c single-phase region due to the relatively high liqui-

could permit high-temperature processing operations, sug-

initially evolved from the Ni–Al–Cr system, the Co–Al–
W-base alloys are still in their early stages if development
and have high potential for improvement.
5. Summary
1. An addition of Ta to Co–Al–W alloys is eﬀective for sta-
bilization of the c
0 phase, while an addition of Cr
decreases the c0 solvus temperature. An addition of Re
does not aﬀect the transformation temperatures.
2. The Co–Al–W-base c/c0 two-phase alloys exhibit the
ﬂow stress anomalies above 873 K due to activation of
paired 1/2 h110 i dislocation slip on both octahedral
and cube planes in the c
0 precipitates.
3. The anomalous peak temperature of the ternary and Ta-
containing alloys is at 1073 K, although the polycrystal-
line ternary alloy showed a peak at 973 K due to the
grain boundary brittleness.
4. The Ta-containing alloy sustains its strength above the
peak temperature due to the activation of 1/3 h112 i par-
tial dislocations that shear the c0 precipitates and form a
high density of stacking faults in the precipitates. On the
other hand, the ternary alloy shows a rapid decrease in
the ﬂow stress above the peak temperature, due to
1/2h110 i dislocations by-passing the precipitates.
