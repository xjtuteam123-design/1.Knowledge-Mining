Severe operating conditions of high-temperature nickel alloys require the development of new regimes of heat 
treatment that would improve the short-term mechanical properties, impact strength, and structural stability of 
the alloys without worsening their high-temperature characteristics. The authors suggest a method of heat 
treatment with decelerated cooling in the hardening process. 
The results published in the last ten years in domestic and 
foreign literature in the field of increasing the high-tempera- 
ture strength and especially the plasticity of nickel alloys 
have mainly been obtained by realizing composite regimes of 
heat treatment [1 - 5]. 
The authors of [6, 7] have developed and recommended 
multistep regimes of heat treatment of nickel alloys in order 
to attain the maximum possible stabilization of the structure. 
A multistep regime of heat treatment consists in that aider 
hardening in air several sequential aging regimes follow each 
other at a gradually decreasing temperature with intermediate 
cooling in air. In each aging step the forming metal structure 
undergoes various stages: changes in the size and shape of the 
grains, the amount and distribution of intermetallic and car- 
bide phases, and intergrain boundaries. In the opinion of the 
authors, such heat treatment makes it possible to increase the 
deformability of nickel alloys in the range of operating tem- 
peratures. Our work in the same direction on the investigation 
of the three industrial high-alloy nickel alloys 1~P-957, 
I~P-800, and I~K-78 (Table 1), used in the production of large 
blades of stationary gas turbine installations [8 - 10], has not

CONCLUSIONS 
1. The optimum combination of the strength and plastic 
properties of nickel alloys in a wide temperature range is ob- 
tained due to the maximum possible volume fraction of the 
strengthening y'-phase in their structure, the bimodality of 
this phase, and the formation of crooked (teeth-shaped) grain 
boundaries. 
2, Crooked grain boundaries are formed in nickel alloys 
in the process of two-stage cooling from the quenching tem- 
perature: at a rate of 1.5 - 5°C/rain in the first stage and in air 
in the second stage. Then the alloys are subjected to final ag- 
ing. The formation of such grain boundaries is associated 
with the segregation of the strengthening y'-phase in the form 
of coarse particles with incorporations of fine discrete car- 
bides MC, M6C , and M23C 6.
