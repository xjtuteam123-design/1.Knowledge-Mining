abstract
A NiAl–Cr(Mo)–Hf eutectic alloy was prepared by suction-casting technique and subsequently hot
isostatic pressing treatment. Microstructure and mechanical tests were performed and the results
revealed that the suction-cast alloy possessed ﬁne NiAl/Cr(Mo) lamellar, large area fraction of eutectic
cell and semi-continuously distributed Ni
2AlHf phase at the cell boundaries. After the HIP treatment, the
Ni2AlHf particles became ﬁne and distributed evenly in the alloy. Moreover, some of the Ni 2AlHf particles
along the eutectic cell boundaries were transformed into Hf solid solution phase. Compared with the
conventionally cast alloy, the room-temperature compressive ductility and strength of the suction-cast
alloy attained signiﬁcant improvement. In addition, the room-temperature ductility and elevated
temperature strength of suction-cast alloy were markedly enhanced by HIP treatment.

1. Introduction
NiAl has received much attention as a potential high-tempera-
ture structural material because of its many advantages, but its poor
high-temperature strength and low room-temperature ductility
and fracture toughness limit its industry applications [1,2]. In order
to conquer these shortcomings many alloys were developed based
on the NiAl. NiAl–28Cr–6Mo (NiAl–Cr(Mo) for short) eutectic alloy
was regarded as one of the most hopeful alloys due to its relative
good room-temperature (RT) and high-temperature mechanical
properties [3–5]. In order to improve the high-temperature
strength of the NiAl–Cr(Mo) eutectic alloy further, Hafnium (Hf)
was introduced in the alloy. However, the Ni
2AlHf strengthened
phases formed along the eutectic cell boundaries weakened the
room-temperature fracture toughness and compressive ductility of
the alloy severely [6–8].
For NiAl–Cr(Mo)–Hf eutectic alloy with Ni
2AlHf strengthening
precipitates, a feasible way to improve its ductility is to reduce the
micro-segregation of Ni 2AlHf phases. Solidiﬁcation at high cooling
rate and high temperature–high pressure treatments are antici-
pated to attain these purposes. As a popular method to fabricate
bulk amorphous [9,10], suction casting can obtain a relatively high
cooling rate of about 50 w 10

[1 1,12]have reported that the rapid solidiﬁcation can improve the
mechanical properties of the NiAl-based alloy signiﬁcantly.
However, few investigations have been done on the effect of hot
isostatic pressing (HIP) treatment on the rapid solidiﬁed NiAl–
Cr(Mo)–Hf eutectic alloy. Therefore in the present paper the
microstructure and mechanical properties of suction-cast NiAl–
28Cr–5.7Mo–0.3Hf alloy with and without HIP treatment were
investigated.

3. Results and discussions
3.1. Microstructure characteristics
The microstructure of the conventionally cast NiAl–Cr(Mo)–Hf
eutectic alloy (as shown in Fig. 1(a)) is composed of gray eutectic
cell, black coarse NiAl phase and white phase which is mainly
distributed at the eutectic cell boundaries. According to the former
research [6] the white phase should be Ni 2AlHf Heusler phase. In
the eutectic cell, black NiAl and gray Cr(Mo) plates exhibited
a radially emanating pattern from the cell interior to its boundaries.
Coarser Cr(Mo) rods, primary NiAl phase and white Ni
2AlHf phase
are mainly distributed at the intercellular zone. By close observa-
tion (Fig. 1b), there are many Cr(Mo) precipitates in the NiAl phase
and NiAl precipitations in the Cr(Mo) phase in the conventionally
cast alloy. The typical microstructure of the suction-cast NiAl–
Cr(Mo)–Hf eutectic alloy is quite different from that of the
conventionally cast alloy, as shown in Fig. 1(c) and (d). The eutectic
cell, NiAl/Cr(Mo) lamellar, primary NiAl phase and Ni
2AlHf
precipitates become ﬁne, and the Ni 2AlHf precipitates are semi-
continuously distributed along the eutectic cell boundaries. In
addition, the amount of primary NiAl phase in the suction-cast
alloy increases, compared with that of the conventionally cast alloy.
TEM observations show that there are some ﬁne Cr(Mo) precipi-
tates with size of 5–10 nm in the primary NiAl phase, as shown in
Fig. 1 (e). Moreover high-density network dislocations along the
NiAl/Cr(Mo) phase interface and ﬁne NiAl and Cr(Mo) precipitates
are observed in the suction-casting alloy, as shown in Fig. 1 (f).
According to the report of Hein et al. [13], such high-density
interface dislocations are beneﬁcial for improving the strength of
super alloy single crystals.
The microstructure of the suction-cast alloy changes after HIP
treatment, as shown in Fig. 2(a). The local degeneration of NiAl and
Cr(Mo) lamellar near the eutectic cell boundary occurs and the
eutectic morphology coarsens signiﬁcantly. The distribution of
the Ni
2AlHf phase at the eutectic cell boundary becomes ﬁner and
the volume fraction of Ni 2AlHf phase at the intercellular region
decreases. TEM investigations reveal that many ﬁne Ni 2AlHf parti-
cles precipitate at NiAl/Cr(Mo) phase interface inside eutectic cell, as
shown in Fig. 2 (b). The TEM observations also revealed Hf solid
solution (Hfss) precipitates with hexagon or irregular shape, as
shown in Fig. 2 (c) and (d). The EDS test on the Hfss ﬁnds that it
contains more Cr elements, compared with Ni
2AlHf phase. The
observations on the primary NiAl phase show that the Cr(Mo)
precipitates tend to be coarse (as shown inFig. 2(e)), which suggests
that Cr and Mo elements solid solved in the NiAl phase precipitate
during HIP treatment. In addition, the TEM observations show that
some dislocations extend into the NiAl phase in the HIP-treated
suction-cast alloy, while almost no mobile dislocation exist in the
Cr(Mo) lamellar, as shown in Fig. 2(f). It is suggested that a certain
deformation occurred in the NiAl phase during the HIP process.
3.2. Compressive properties
The true stress–true strain compressive curves at RT and yield
stresses at different temperatures and strain rate of the conven-
tionally cast, suction-cast and HIP-treated suction-cast alloys are
shown in Fig. 3(a) and (b), respectively. It can be seen that the alloys
fabricated by different technologies have the similar stress–strain
curves, which exhibit continuous work hardening after yield, as
shown in Fig. 3 (a). The plastic strain in compression and yield
strength of the suction-cast alloy are about 16% and 1450 MPa
respectively, which are signiﬁcantly improved, compared with the
conventionally cast alloy. It means that reﬁnement of the initial cast
microstructure by suction casting is beneﬁcial for improving the
plastic strain in compression and yield strength of the alloy. After
the HIP treatment, the RT yield strength of the HIP-treated suction-
cast alloy decreases, but the plastic strain in compression of the
HIP-treated suction-cast alloy improved by about 22%, which is
more than three times of the conventionally cast alloy. The yield
strength of alloys at various test conditions is shown in Fig. 3 (b).
With the increase of strain rate, the yield stresses of all the alloys
increase. Though the yield strengths of the suction-cast and
conventionally cast alloys are almost the same at 1273 K, the yield
strength of the suction-cast alloy at 1373 K is slightly higher at all
strain rates. The HIP treatment improves the elevated temperature
strength of the suction-cast alloy at 1273 K and 1373 K signiﬁcantly.
For example, HIP treatment increases the yield strength of the
suction-cast alloy at 1273 K from 388 MPa to 512 MPa. The
improvement of the mechanical properties of the suction-cast alloy
with and without HIP treatment should be attributed to the
microstructure evolution. The reﬁnement of the NiAl/Cr(Mo)
lamellar and reducing segregation of Ni
2AlHf phase are all helpful
to the RT compressive properties. Though the ﬁne NiAl/Cr(Mo)
lamellar are not beneﬁcial for improving the high-temperature

3.3. Microhardness
The results of microhardness measurements on primary NiAl
phase and NiAl/Cr(Mo) lamellar in the conventionally cast alloy,
suction-cast alloy with and without HIP treatment are shown in
Table 1. It is clear that the hardness of both primary NiAl phase and
NiAl/Cr(Mo) lamellar of the suction-cast alloy increases signiﬁ-
cantly. Generally, the hardness of NiAl/Cr(Mo) lamellar is higher
than that of the NiAl phase in NiAl–Cr(Mo) eutectic alloys. However,
the hardness of primary NiAl phase in the suction-cast alloy even
exceeded that of NiAl/Cr(Mo) lamellar in the conventionally cast
alloy. The increased hardness for the suction-cast alloy can be
attributed to the extended solid solution and ﬁne microstructure
induced by high cooling rate. The large amount of Cr(Mo) precipi-
tates in primary NiAl phase resulted by the HIP treatment increases
its hardness, while the more precipitates in the NiAl and Cr(Mo)
phases improve the hardness of NiAl/Cr(Mo) lamellar further.
4. Conclusions
1. The suction-casting reﬁnes the eutectic cell and NiAl/Cr(Mo)
lamellar signiﬁcantly and reduces the micro-segregation of the
Ni
2AlHf phases. After HIP treatment, part of NiAl/Cr(Mo)
eutectic lamellar becomes coarse and some of the Ni 2AlHf
particles along the eutectic cell boundaries are transformed
into Hf solid solution phase.
2. The suction-casting technique and subsequent HIP treatment
improve the plastic strains in compression and strength of the
NiAl–Cr(Mo)–Hf eutectic alloy at room temperature signiﬁ-
cantly, which can be mainly attributed to the ﬁne microstruc-
ture and well distributed Ni
2AlHf and Hf solid solution phases.
3. The elevated temperature compressive properties of the
suction-cast alloy almost have no improvement, compared
with the conventionally cast alloy. However the HIP treatment
improves its elevated temperature strength signiﬁcantly, which
should be ascribed to the coarseness of NiAl/Cr(Mo) eutectic
lamellar, precipitation strengthening and well distributed
Ni
2AlHf phases.
