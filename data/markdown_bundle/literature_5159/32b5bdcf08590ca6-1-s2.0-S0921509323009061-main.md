1. Introduction 
Refractory high-entropy alloys (RHEAs), regarded as up-and-coming 
high temperature materials, are facing a comprehensive breakthrough in 
density and mechanical properties [ 1 – 5 ]. Classical WMoNbTa and 
WMoNbTaV RHEAs outperformed the Nickel-based superalloys ( i.e. , 
Inconel 718 and Haynes 230) at T > 800 
◦
C, even retained excellent 
strength up to 1600 
◦

cost/density design, simply focusing on strength enhancement could not 
meet practical applications. 
One approach to tackle this bottleneck is to introduce nonmetal- 
metal reinforced phases, e.g. , silicides [ 8 , 9 ], carbides [ 10 , 11 ], borides 
[ 12 ], etc ., which could contribute positively to strength and oxidation 
resistance improvement [ 8 , 9 ]. To the best of our knowledge, 
Nb – Si/Nb – Ti – Si based in-situ composites are candidates as the turbine 
blades and the nozzle materials in aero-engine propulsion systems, with 
their attractive high temperature performance and low density [ 13 , 14 ]; 
while subject to inadequate toughness at room temperature and poor 
oxidation resistance. Therefore, we proposed here to combine the Nb – Si 
based alloy idea to design RHEAs with silicide intermetallic compounds.

4.3. Deformation mechanism 
Generally, high stacking fault energy (SFE) metals favor the dislo -
cation slip or cross-slip, and prevent twinning in most of cases; while 
low-SFE metals are deformed via planar glide, extended stacking fault 
development and twinning, benefitted from the large splitting of perfect 
dislocations into partials [ 37 ]. Indeed, no deformation twins appear in 
either our as-cast or heated-treated specimens, and plastic deformation 
is mainly based on slip, which is associated with the high SFE of 
BCC-based system. In addition, the composite glide with screw character 
confirms the presence of cross-slip at 800
