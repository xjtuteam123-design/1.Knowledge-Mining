Site preference of refractory elements in Ni-based single-crystal 
Superalloys alloying with Ru: from first principles 
Jianjun Cui 1, a , Fei Sun 1,b and Jianxin Zhang 1,c 
1 Key Laboratory for Liquid-Solid Structural Evolution and Processing of Materials (Ministry of 
Education), Shandong University, Jinan 250061, China 
axcui@sdu.edu.cn, bsunfei1147@hotmail.com, c jianxin@sdu.edu.cn 
Keywords: Site preference; Ruthenium (Ru); superalloys; Elec tronic structure; Atomic structure; 
Computer simulation. 
 
Abstract. A first principles calculation method was used to investigate the site preference of 
Ruthenium (Ru) at the γ/γ′ interface in Ni-based si ngle-crystal superalloys. The calculation results 
show that the addition of Ru can decrease the total energy and the binding energy of γ/γ′ interface, 
which may result in an improved microstructure stab ility of Ni-based single-crystal superalloys. 
Moreover, by calculation, it is also found that Ru can stabilize both γ and γ′ phases and have a 
preference for Ni site at the coherent γ/γ′ interfa ce. When Ru substitutes the central Ni at the γ/γ′ 
interface, a reverse partitioning of W, Re and Cr occurs; while the partitioning behavior of Mo is not 
affected. The influence of Ru on the partitioning behavior of W, Re and Cr in γ′-Ni 
3Al was studied by 
Dmol3 calculation as well. The calculation results show that W, Re and Cr have a preference for Ni 
site in γ′- Ni 3Al with Ru alloying. When Ru substitutes the central Ni atom, the site preference of W, 
Re and Cr varies accordingly. Furthermore, electronic structure analysis of γ/γ′ interface and γ′-Ni 3Al 
in terms of Mulliken population and partial density of states (PDOS) was performed to understand the 
alloying mechanism of Ru in Ni-based single-crystal superalloys. The results show that the 
strengthening effect of Ru alloying is mainly due to the reduction in binding energy of Ru as well as a 
p-orbital hybridization between Ru and the host atoms. 
Introduction 
The Ni-based single-crystal superalloys have made s ubstantial contributions to the performance of 
aircraft engines and turbine blades because of their high creep strength and low crack-growth rates at 
high temperatures [1-8]. There are two primary phas es present in such superalloys, where the 
precipitate γ′ phase (based on L12 Ni 
3Al) is coherently embedded between narrow channels of γ phase 
(based on fcc Ni). Generally speaking, it is γ′ pha se that is responsible for the high temperature 
strength of the Ni-based single-crystal superalloys . This is the reason, at least in part, why most 
researches have been focused on the alloying effect of refractory elements in the γ′ phase [9-20]. 
Modern Ni-based superalloys usually contain more than ten alloying elements. These elements, such 
as W, Re and Cr, are effective in improving the sol ution strengthening effect and the elevated 
temperature strength of Ni-based superalloys [21, 22]. The refractory elements content has increased 
from 14 wt. % for first-generation (CMSX-2) superalloys to greater than 20 wt. % for third-generation 
(CMSX-10) superalloys[23]. In particular, the addit ion of Ru has generated research interest as a 
promising element, due to its significant contribut ions to an enhanced microstructure stability and 
high-temperature mechanical properties of Ni-based single-crystal superalloys [24, 25]. Recently, 
several different theoretical methods have been used to elucidate the alloying mechanisms controlling 
the mechanical properties of Ni-based single-crystal superalloys [19, 26-29]. The alloying effect has 
been studied in terms of the electronic structure c alculation and atomic structure determination. In 
spite of extensive investigations, some issues still remain unresolved. Ru is a typical element added in 
the fourth-generation superalloys; Ru is also proven to be an effective element that can suppress the 
formation of topologically close-packed (TCP) phase s in superalloys [30-32]. However, the 
strengthening mechanism of Ru in superalloys is still unclear, especially at the level of the electronic

Method and computational model 
Calculations were performed using Dmol3 method based on the density functional theory. The Dmol3 
method is widely used as an approach for determination of electronic structures and analysis of orbital 
electron occupations. The Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (P BE) parametrization of the generalized 
gradient approximation (GGA) [35] was used for the electron exchange and correlation. The 
convergence criterion for the charge density of self-consistent iterations was set to 10 
-6, which allows 
the binding energy to converge to 10 -6 Ry. Because of the magnetic nature of the Ni-based superalloys, 
our calculations are spin-restricted (without spin polarization). 
Two 48-atom supercells were employed in our calculations in which the center of a supercell is either 
Al or Ni, as shown in Fig. 1(a) and (b). An upper Ni 3Al region and a lower Ni region represent the γ′ 
and γ phases respectively. Since both phases have a cubic lattice with similar lattice parameters, the γ′ 
phase would precipitate in a cube-cube orientation relationship with the γ matrix, forming a complete 
coherent γ/γ′ interface. This coherent interface was used to show the site preference of PGMs atoms 
(Ru, Rh, Pd, Os, Ir and Pt) at the γ/γ′ interface, which is placed at the center of a supercell, occupying 
either a Ni or Al site. Furthermore, the 2×2×3 unit cells were constructed to calculate the effect of Ru 
on the partitioning behavior of W, Re, Cr and Mo across the coherent γ/γ′ interface. The central atoms 
labeled by “1” are substituted for Ru. The Ni sites labeled by “2” and “3” are substituted for W, Re, Cr 
and Mo. The atoms “2” and “3” are located in γ and γ′ phases, respectively. The total energy ( Et) and 
the binding energy ( Eb) are used in our present work to study the site pr eference of platinum group 
metals (PGMs) at the coherent γ/γ′ interface, especially on the alloying effect of Ru. Eb is defined as:

Results and discussion 
Influence of Ru on the partitioning behavior of refractory elements W, Re, Cr and Mo. Usually, 
refractory elements W, Re, Cr and Mo are added in N i-based single-crystal superalloys to form a 
strengthening solid-solution phase. The partitionin g behaviors of these refractory elements also 
influence the strength and microstructure stability of superalloys. Recent experimental results show 
that Ru can reverse the partitioning behavior of refractory elements between γ phase and γ′ phase in a 
manner termed ‘reverse partitioning’, i.e., refract ory elements W, Re, Cr, Mo, segregate to the γ′ 
phase instead of γ phase [24,37]. In order for us to better understand the role of Ru, we calculated the 
binding energies of the supercells with Ni and Ru atoms centered at coherent interface, 
simultaneously with W, Re, Cr and Mo atoms placed in the γ phase and γ′ phase respectively. The Ni 
atom labeled by “2” in γ phase is substituted for W , Re, Cr and Mo, respectively. This substitution 
also applies to the Ni atom labeled by “3” in γ′ phase. The calculated binding energies are presented in

by Volek et al. [38]. It is necessary to obtain a b etter understanding of the effect of Ru on the 
partitioning behavior by electronic structure calculations.

Site preference of W, Re and Cr in pure γ′-Ni 3Al. In order to stabilize the microstructure, there 
are limits to the concentrations of alloying elements in that too many of them are tend to promote the 
formation of TCP precipitates. The partitioning behaviors of these refractory elements also influence 
the strength and microstructure stability of supera lloys. The site labeled by 2 and 3 in the γ′-Ni 3Al 
model shown in Fig.2 (a) and (b) are adopted as the substitution sites for W, Re and Cr, respectively. 
In order to study the partitioning behaviors of W, Re and Cr, the binding energy and the total energy 
meet this aim. As seen in Table 2, we present the t otal energy and the binding energy of pure 
supercells and supercells with the substitution of W, Re and Cr atoms. For the supercells in which the 
center in either a Ni or Al, we can determine the site preference of W, Re and Cr in pure γ′- Ni 3Al. It is 
clear seen that, in comparison with the pure superc ells, the binding energy and the total energy of 
supercells containing W, Re and Cr are lower. When a W (or Re) atom occupies an Al site, the 
binding energy and the total energy decrease by as much as 5.48eV (5.45eV) and 3.58eV (4.00eV), 
respectively. When a W (or Re) atom occupies a Ni s ite, the binding energy and the total energy of 
Al-centered supercell is both lower than those of the corresponding Ni-centered supercell. For the Cr 
addition, the binding energy and the total energy of the Ni-centered supercell are the lowest when Cr 
occupies a Ni site. These results imply that both W , Re and Cr can stabilize the γ′- Ni 3Al phase. 
Moreover, W and Re have a site preference for the N i site in Al-centered supercell. However, Cr 
exhibits a Ni preference in Ni-centered supercell.

Mulliken orbital population and partial density of states . Mulliken orbital population can 
explain quantitatively a charge transfer between electronic orbitals as well as a charge accumulation 
or depletion at atomic sites [53]. Table 4 lists the electron occupation numbers in valence orbitals of 
the system containing Re atom. It can be seen from this table that the 5 d and 6 p orbitals gain charges, 
while the 6 s orbital loses charges. Note that the number of val ence electrons of a Re atom increases 
with Ru. For Ni, charge transfer occurs from 3 d orbital and 4 s orbital to 4 p orbital; for Ru, also, charge 
transfer occurs from 4 d orbital and 5 s orbital to 5 p orbital. Such charge transfer behavior 
demonstrates that there is a strong orbital hybridization in Ni, Re and Ru. When Ru substitutes Ni (1), 
both Re (2) atom and Re (3) atom gain approximately 0.6e charge. In particular, a charge transfer 
occurs in Re atom from 6 s orbital to 5 d orbital and 6 p orbital to compensate for the charge depletions 
at Re (2) atom and Re(3) atom. Moreover, Ru atom itself gains a 0.3e charge, which transfers from Ru 
4d orbital and 5 s orbital to Ru 5 p orbital, resulting in Re p-orbital/Ru p-orbital hybridization and an 
enhanced binding across the γ/γ′ interface. Based on the above discussion, the alloying strengthening 
mechanism of Ru in superalloys can be attributed to the formation of direct bonding between Ru atom 
and host atoms of superalloys. Table 5 lists the electron occupation numbers in the valence orbitals for 
Re in γ′ phase with and without Ru additions, respectively. Take Re as an example, it is found that for 
all the cases of Re, the 6 s orbital gains charge, while 5 d and 6 p orbitals lose charge. For Ni, charge 
transfer occurs from 3 d orbital and 4 s orbital to 4 p orbital; for Ru, also, charge transfer occurs from 4 d 
orbital and 5 s orbital to 5p orbital. Such charge transfer behavi or demonstrates that there is a strong 
orbital hybridization in Ni, Re and Ru. When Ru substitutes Ni (1), both Re (2) atom and Re (3) atom 
gain less than 1e charge. Moreover, Ru atom itself gains approximately 0.2e charge, which transfers 
from Ru 4 d orbital and 5 s orbital to Ru 5 p orbital, resulting in Re p-orbital/Ru p-orbital hybridization 
and an enhanced binding across the γ/γ′ interface. Based on the above discussion, the alloying 
strengthening mechanism of Ru in superalloys can be attributed to the formation of direct bonding 
between Ru atom and host atoms of superalloys.

Besides the Mulliken orbital population, we also ca lculated the PDOS to further elucidate the 
interaction between Re atom and Ru atom. The calcul ation results of PDOS are presented in Fig. 3 
and Fig. 4. The feature of PDOS indicates that there is a strong interaction between Re atom and Ru 
atom through p-p hybridization, which can be seen from the resonant hybridization peaks labeled by 
the dashed lines in Fig. 3 and Fig. 4. Less p-p orbital hybridization peaks indicate that the bonding of 
interface with Re in γ phase are weaker than that of interface with Re in γ′ phase. Hence, the stability 
of γ/γ′ interface can be increased when Re stays in the γ′ phase. Moreover, less p-p orbital 
hybridization peaks indicate that the bonding of Re with Ru in the supercell corresponding to Fig. 2 (a) 
are weaker than that of Ru in the supercell corresponding to Fig. 2 (b). Hence, the stability of γ′-Ni
3Al 
can be increased when Ru occupies the central atom in Ni-centered γ′-Ni 3Al. These results provide 
additional insights into the alloying effects of Ru and Re in superalloys.

Conclusions 
The atomic site preference of PGMs at the γ/γ′ inte rface in Ni-based single-crystal superalloys is 
studied by Dmol3 calculation method. The calculatio n results show that PGMs atoms prefer to 
occupy a Ni site at coherent γ/γ′ interface. When PGMs replace the central atom of the γ/γ′ interface, 
the total energy and binding energy decreased. Ru remarkably affects the partitioning behavior of W, 
Re and Cr between γ phase and γ′ phase. However, Ru has no effect on the partitioning behavior of 
Mo. The atomic site preference of W, Re and Cr in γ ′-Ni 
3Al is also studied by Dmol3 calculation 
method. The calculation results present that W, Re and Cr prefer to occupy a Ni site in alloying with 
Ru. When W, Re and Cr replace the central atom of t he γ′-Ni 3Al, the reduction in total energy and 
binding energy results in the improvement of microstructure stability. Ru remarkably affects the site 
preference of W, Re and Cr in γ′-Ni 3Al. Our calculation results of binding energy, Mull iken 
population and PDOS consistently elucidate the stre ngthening mechanism of Ru in Ni-based 
single-crystal superalloys.
