ABSTRACT 
Single crystal (SC) Ni-based superalloys undergo microstructural degradations such as rafting and coarsening due 
to the extremely harsh service environment, which consequently introduce the challenge in inelastic behaviours 
prediction when considering the time-varying microstructural state. Within this content, a thermodynamically 
microstructural state-based constitutive model for SC Ni-based superalloys is proposed at elevated temperature. 
A rafting state variable satisfying the positive dissipation is determined and then is introduced into the crystal 
plasticity framework to depict the inelastic deformation of SC Ni-based superalloys with different rafting mi -
crostructures. The interconnection between rafting and constitutive law is achieved by altering the internal state 
variables (ISVs) through a series of micromechanical mechanisms rather than through continuum damage me -
chanics. Specially, a novel microstructure-based back stress model is proposed considering the morphology and 
size variation of the γ’ / γ structure on the basis of dislocation wall theory. The capability of the model is validated 
by the stress-strain responses and the microstructural rafting behaviours by coupling the thermodynamic rafting 
model. Finally, an application case of a centre-hole component made by a SC Ni-based superalloy is provided to 
highlight the capability of the model for the analysis of inelastic deformation combined with inhomogeneous 
microstructural rafting for engineering components. The proposed framework not only gives inherent under -
standing on the interaction between microstructural rafting and constitutive law by means of microphysical 
mechanisms, but also provides a powerful tool for analysing rafting and inelastic deformation of complex 
structures. 
1. Introduction 
Hot-section components such as the turbine blades and vanes of aero- 
engines (AE) and industrial gas turbines (IGT) are preferentially made 
by single crystal (SC) Ni-based superalloys [6 , 55] . These alloys own 
excellent fatigue, creep and thermomechanical properties at elevated 
temperature because of the high volume fraction of the strengthening 
phase, i.e. γ’ precipitates, in a cubic shape to impede the motion of 
dislocations as shown in Fig. 1 (a). Due to the extremely harsh circum -
stances, the microstructure of SC Ni-based superalloys degrades rather 
than remains steady where rafting is the most common and important 
case [15 , 40 , 69] . Notably, rafting changes the shape, size and distribu -
tion of the γ’ / γ microstructures, which further alters the deformation 
behaviours and mechanical properties of SC Ni-based superalloys [10 , 
12 , 19 , 20 , 33 , 51] . Therefore, quantitatively capturing the inherent effect 
of rafting on the mechanical behaviours at high temperature is signifi -
cantly important for the inelastic deformation prediction and life man -
agement of hot-section components made by SC Ni-based superalloys. 
Extensively experimental, numerical and theoretical investigations 
on rafting of SC Ni-based superalloys have been carried out. Earlier 
studies have demonstrated that rafting is an atomic diffusion process at 
high temperature where the direction of the applied stress, sign of the 
γ’ / γ misfit and difference in the elastic constants between the individual 
phases are decisive for the rafting orientation [26 , 49 , 53 , 69] . Generally, 
the γ’ / γ structure with a negative misfit produces a needle-like 
morphology parallel to the applied stress (P-type rafting) under a

hot issue for the safety service and reliability design of the components 
made by of SC Ni-based superalloys. 
Regarding the aspects of the altering of mechanical properties, the 
impacts of rafting and isotropic coarsening on deformation behaviours 
are most pronounced on yield strength and subsequently hardening 
behaviours once plastic deformation occurred. At high temperature, the 
decrease in the Orowan stress induced by the γ matrix widening during 
rafting is the main reason for the reduction of the yield stress [39 , 76 , 80] . 
Under cyclic loading, a lower stress amplitude at rafting and coarsening 
states is observed compared with the virgin material [20 , 22] . Fan et al. 
[19] have reported that the coarsening microstructure promotes the 
accumulation of ratchetting strain under the stress-control regime. Some 
interesting results also show that the stress state of the degraded mate -
rial converges to that of the virgin material once stabilized under low 
cycle fatigue (LCF) conditions [33 , 46] . The degradation of microstruc -
ture brings new challenges to the constitutive modelling of the SC 
Ni-bases superallys without doubt, where the main issue is how to give a 
quantitative description and consider the time and loading-related 
microstructural rafting in constitutive theory. 
To date, attempts have been made for microstructure-sensitive 
constitutive modelling of Ni-based superalloys for tensile, cyclic [28 , 
63] , fatigue-creep interactions [57] with a stable microstructure. The 
models that account for the effects of microstructure evolution on the 
constitutive response mainly concentrate on the 
isothermal/non-isothermal creep deformation [11 , 18 , 23 , 27 , 38 , 43 , 36 , 
37 , 44] . Three approaches that link microstructure rafting with the 
constitutive theory have been adopted now: 
i The Orowan stress related to the γ matrix width plays an important 
role in reflecting the influence of isotropic coarsening and rafting on 
the slip resistance; 
ii The microstructural features such as the γ’ volume fraction and γ 
matrix width are introduced into the hardening laws as phenome -
nological correction coefficients, which consequently alters the 
hardening/softening results; 
iii A microstructure-related viscous overstress is introduced into the 
damage evolution on the basis of the continuum damage mechanics 
(CDM), which allows the creep damage to consider the change in 
microstructure. 
However, fewer models account for the effects of microstructure 
evolution on the constitutive response for both long and short-term 
deformation regimes simultaneously [13 , 32 , 39] . le Graverend et al. 
[39] introduce the Orowan mechanism and proposed a new back stress 
that is modified by variation in the γ’ volume fraction with temperature 
to modelling the deformation behaviours of SC Ni-based superalloys for 
very high temperature and overheating. Desmorat et al. [13] gives a 
tensorial thermodynamic framework to account for rafting behaviour on 
the deformation of SC Ni-based superalloys through the change of the 
Orowan stress at elevated temperature. This model is validated by ten -
sile and creep responses along different crystal orientations whereas the 
ability to describe cyclic deformation needs further verification due to 
the lack of the back stress. Kirka and Neu [32] introduce a 
microstructure-sensitive variable to the internal state variables (ISVs), 
whereas the microstructural degradation model does not considering the 
effects of the inelastic deformation. However, these models reflect the 
effects of microstructural rafting mainly through the Orowan mecha -
nism and phenomenological modifications for ISVs such as back stress 
and slip resistance, rather than presenting the inherent micro mecha -
nisms. Therefore, it is still necessary to develop constitutive models that 
can predict the evolution of long/short-term deformation responses 
caused by microstructure rafting considering the microphysical mech -
anism, in which the coupling between constitutive behaviours and 
microstructure degradation needs to be addressed in a self-consistent 
framework. 
The main aim of the present work is to develop a thermodynamically 
microstructural state-based constitutive model for SC Ni-based super -
alloys that can capture the effect of rafting on both long/short-term 
deformation behaviours at high temperature. The present paper is 
structured as follows: Section 2 gives a thermodynamic state variable of 
rafting from our previous work [41] that only concentrates on the

The main contribution of this paper is to construct a thermody -

high temperature that quantitatively considers the role of microstruc -
tural rafting. The interaction of rafting and constitutive behaviour is 
achieved through changes in the dislocation motion mechanism caused 
by the geometrical changes of the γ’/γ structures at the microscopic 
level, which alters the Orowan stress and mean free path of dislocations
