Abstract
Molybdenum boron silicides containing between 18 and 31 vol.% α-Mo are prepared by reactive hot-pressing from Mo–Si–B powder mixtures.
Their microstructures and mechanical properties are investigated. The multiphase alloys consist of Mo 3Si, Mo5SiB2 and discontinuous α-Mo. The
results demonstrate that the volume fraction of α-Mo and B content exerts a significant effect on the flexural strength and fracture toughness of the
multiphase alloys.

3. Results and discussion
Fig. 2 shows the XRD patterns of the three alloys after hot-
pressing sintered at 1600 °C for 2 h. It is evident that the three
diffraction patterns are almost identical as all of them show
peaks from the compounds of Mo
3Si and Mo 5SiB2 and the
remaining Mo with no peaks detected from other phases. As
anticipated, the products are in good agreement with the three phase
fields of Mo(ss) +T

However, the amount of Mo3Si and Mo5SiB2 compounds increases
whereas that of the remaining Mo phase decreases with the
increasing boron content since the peak intensity from Mo is
lowered.
Fig. 3 illustrates the microstructures of polished and etched
specimens of the three alloys. In general, complete densification
occurs during the hot-pressing process, resulting in dense micro-
structures for the Mo –15Si–5B and Mo –15Si–10B alloys.
Comparatively, the microstructures are refined with increasing
B content owing to the inhibition of grain growth by the
molybdenum boron silicides. Moreover, the formed Mo
3Si and
Mo5SiB2 particles are embedded homogeneously throughout the
discontinuous Mo matrices. However, numerous pores are

individual α-Mo particles [15]. Therefore, it is completely pos-
sible to improve the mechanical properties based on further
optimization of the composition and processing parameters.
The SEM micrographs of fracture surfaces after bending
tests ( Fig. 4 ) show evidence for limited ductility and plastic
deformation of α-Mo phase in the alloys. Generally, all the three
alloys fail in a brittle manner. The failure takes place via a mixed
mode of intragranular and intergranular fracture. Again, some
pores are observed on the fracture surface of the Mo –15Si–15B
alloy.
4. Conclusion
The Mo –Mo
3Si–Mo5SiB2 multiphase alloys are fabricated
by reactive hot-pressing of the Mo –Si–B powder mixtures. The
volume fraction of remaining α-Mo varies between 18 and 31%
depending on B content. The Mo–15Si–5B alloy exhibits a fully
dense microstructure and shows the highest flexural strength and
fracture toughness among the three alloys. The toughening effect
against the monolithic molybdenum silicide is attributed to the
ductile α-Mo phase. However, the Mo –15Si–15B alloy shows
the lowest values in both flexural strength and fracture
toughness, due to the existence of some pores and the low
content of α-Mo.
