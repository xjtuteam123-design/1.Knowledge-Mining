1. Introduction 
High entropy alloys (HEAs), also known as multi-principal element 
alloys, are produced by using at least four principal elements with 
equimolar or near-equimolar compositions, such that the configura -
tional mixing entropy increases and the free energy of the stable solid 
solution phase decreases despite the initial expectation of brittle com -
plex multi-phase materials [1] . A decade ago, the refractory HEAs 
(RHEAs) have been introduced by Senkov et al. to meet the increasing 
demand for alloys that can operate at elevated temperatures [2] . These 
alloys have attracted significant attention owing to their outstanding 
thermo-mechanical and chemical properties stemming from their 
simple-structure solid solution phases, such as high wear resistance, 
good oxidation and corrosion resistances, structural stability, and high 
strength at elevated temperatures. In contrast to their conventional 
counterparts, the RHEAs exhibit relatively high strength both at lower 
and elevated temperatures, where the latter is essential for load-bearing 
capability for components and structures used in aerospace industry [3] . 
Despite the aforementioned remarkable properties, various chal -
lenges exist that need to be addressed prior to the utility of RHEAs in 
applications. First of all, since many of the reported RHEAs have a body 
centered cubic (BCC) structure, lack of room temperature ductility is 
expected, which is a concern for formability. Fortunately, several clues 
to enhance the ductility of RHEAs have been suggested. For instance, it 
has been shown that that the face centered cubic (FCC) structure be -
comes predominant with increasing valence electron concentration 
(VEC) [4,5] , implying that a better formability can be attained by 
adjusting the alloy content to ensure a higher VEC. Another issue that a 
majority of the RHEAs suffers from is the fall in yield strength beyond

A third major drawback is the difficulty of designing a new compo -
sition. Specifically, even a slight alteration of the molar fraction of ele -
ments can have significant impact on the strength and ductility of the 
alloy. In general, adding subgroup V and VI elements improves strength 
but reduces ductility, whereas adding subgroup IV elements enhances 
ductility [6] . Moreover, the compositional space of the RHEAs is 
appreciably wider as compared to other alloys since the RHEAs can be 
formed by a vast number of metallic elements. Therefore, a classical 
trial-and-error approach requires excessive time and resources. Alter -
natively, the assessment of the current literature data may shed light on 
the design approach. One method to take advantage of available data is 
employing machine learning (ML) algorithms to predict new promising 
compositions. In recent years, the material scientists have started to 
benefit from ML algorithms in the design of new alloys. In particular, ML 
models have already been employed to predict the phase structures and 
mechanical properties of HEAs [7 – 11] . To illustrate, artificial neural 
network (ANN) was utilized in a study to classify solid solution and 
intermetallic compound phases [7] . In another example, ML tools were 
successfully implemented to search for an Al-Co-Cr-Cu-Fe-Ni system 
with the highest hardness, and 10 % higher hardness was achieved as 
compared to previous experimental efforts [9] . 
The successful examples of the implementation of artificial 
intelligence (AI) and ML methods in designing HEAs and other classes of 
alloys, such as shape memory alloys [12,13] , has encouraged this 
research. The object was to benefit from these techniques to overcome 
the aforementioned drawbacks in RHEA design and predict novel RHEAs 
that exhibit high strength at elevated temperatures and good room- 
temperature ductility. For this purpose, four datasets were generated 
by mining the data available in literature, containing room temperature 
strength, high temperature strength, room temperature ductility and 
hardness.

2. Computational details 
2.1. Dataset preparation 
The data mining is of utmost importance for the construction of a 
capable ML algorithm, and relevancy of the data is as critical as the 
number of data instances for a successful prediction. Hence, the 
compositional space considered in this study was restricted to Nb-Ti-Zr- 
Ta-V-Mo-Hf-Al-W-Cr-Si system by taking into account previously re -
ported RHEAs ( Fig. 1 ). To overcome the uncertainties raised due to 
experimentation, the authors collected data of materials produced only 
by casting and arc-melting, following HIP and homogenization treat -
ments (where present). Data produced with other methods, such as 
elemental P/M method or isochronal annealing were excluded. 
Manufacturing and treatment conditions (time and temperature) were 
included in the dataset. This way, the uncertainty in the ML models was 
minimized. Consequently, a dataset containing 370 data instances was 
created [14 – 66] , which included molar fractions of 11 elements, infor -
mation about the alloy manufacturing technique and thermomechanical 
treatments, the temperature at which the experiment was conducted, 
the hardness, strength, and fracture strain of the alloy as a measure of 
ductility. Since the last three properties were not available for each 
instance, the dataset was re-grouped into 4 new training sets: room 
temperature (RT) strength, high temperature (HT) strength, ductility 
and hardness training sets, containing 149, 370, 131 and 75 instances, 
respectively. The corresponding datasets can be found in the Supple -
mentary Material , as well as on the open access platform Github 
(https://github.com/AysuCatal/Dataset_file). 
Considering the vital role of ML features in uncovering the unseen 
relationships within the material dataset, six additional fundamental 
features were added to the dataset. The first one is the valence electron 
concentration (VEC), an indicator of the electronic structure of the 
RHEA. The relation between VEC and phase stability has been demon -
strated although the mechanism behind this phenomenon is not clearly 
understood yet. In particular, as the VEC increases, the stable phase 
alters from BCC to FCC structure, in other words, from relatively brittle 
to more ductile. The VEC of the RHEA is calculated by the following

The second model adopted in this study was the ANN due to its 
proven potential in designing new alloys as demonstrated in previous 
works [8,73] . Tensorflow framework was employed in Python, which 
allows to control the number of hidden layers and neurons. These were 
assessed by trying different numbers systematically and a model with 2 
hidden layers was chosen. In order to prevent overfitting, ridge regu -
larization was utilized and 5-fold cross validation (CV) was applied. The 
MSE was selected as an indicator of the model ’ s score. The regulariza -
tion parameter was determined experimentally. 
Finally, RF, which is an ensemble model constituted by numerous 
decision trees, was utilized. In RF, the output is determined from the 
outcomes of many decision trees; its reliability comes from the crowd. 
The RF model utilized in this study consisted of 100 decision trees and 
the MSE was adopted as the cost function. To tune the model, the 
minimum number of instances in each node and the maximum number 
of features considered for the best split were adopted as selection 
criteria. 
In order to optimize the RHEA compositions, a synthetic matrix 
constituted by various new compositions was created and trained with 
the defined models. The synthetic matrix was also transformed by the 
aforementioned yeo-johnson method. The molar fractions of the 
elements were restricted to 0.04 – 0.36, 0.06 – 0.44, 0.03 – 0.37, 0 – 0.59, 
and 0 – 0.54 for Nb, Ti, Zr, Ta, and V, respectively, in accordance with 
available data to avoid additional uncertainty. Specifically, different 
results in literature bring about some uncertainty due to the variety of 
experiments conducted. However, the authors would like to emphasize 
that the aim of ML is not to find the perfect fit for every single experi -
mental data in the dataset, but to create a generalized curve to explain 
most of the data as good as possible. Thus, this generalization of the 
model already includes uncertainty among the dataset itself. The Mo 
content (at.%) was determined by subtracting the total molar fraction 
content of Nb, Ti, Zr, Ta, and V from 1. The other elements in the 
training sets were not considered since they are insignificant in content 
as compared to these elements. For each element, the molar fractions 
were increased by 0.02 while the rest (except the at.% content of Mo) 
were kept constant to be able to check a wide range of compositions in 
the desired range. The as-cast state was selected for predictions and the 
other features were calculated from the molar fractions. The condition of

It should also be noted that the dataset size has a crucial impact on 
the performance of a ML model and limits the prediction range. How -
ever, considering the main benefit of using ML in materials science, 
namely notably enhancing the time and financial efficiency of designing 
new materials, ML models can be of significant help in identifying the 
target alloy composition and/or the several alloy compositions that can 
satisfy the demanded performance, even in the case of small datasets. 
Nevertheless, every single new material designed with this method will 
be included in the dataset and help improve the accuracy of further 
predictions, which is another strategy adopted by many alloy design 
studies incorporating ML. Furthermore, one should note that refractory 
high entropy alloys constitute a novel class of materials, and thus, there 
is currently very limited amount of data available on these alloys. 
Therefore, the current study should be considered as work to build up 
on, such that the results are expected to help with the down selection of 
candidate alloys and refinement of the procedure as more work is car -
ried out along the same line in the near future. 
In the current study, the authors kept the synthetic dataset in a 
certain range to overcome any extrapolation the model could make, 
such that the synthetic dataset contains only certain elements within 
certain atomic ratio range. The number of instances in the dataset is 
quite large (822827), but this is due to the sensitive search, such that the 
compositional space search was carried out with very small increments. 
Specifically, the change in the molar fractions of compositions in this 
dataset is as small as 0.02 per element, which resulted in a large search 
space.

Moreover, the ML algorithm processes the available data from the

the experiments do not always yield the ideal morphology predicted by 
thermophysical calculations or theory since success of the experiments 
depends on how controlled the manufacturing is, especially how well 
the constituent elements are mixed, which might be quite determinant 
in the case of prediction of intermetallics. Even though it might be 
possible to obtain a good solid solution with the approach, it might be 
necessary to optimize of process parameters to be able to predict the 
morphology to a full extent, including presence and distribution of 
intermetallic phases, which however, remained beyond the scope of the 
current study but should be incorporated in future work. 
The second important issue is associated with the material design by 
machine learning. Even though the experimental validation analysis 
presented herein demonstrated that the three RHEAs designed by ML 
performed remarkably, one should not forget that the analysis depends 
on the dataset, in addition to the models and features chosen. In the case 
of RHEAs, the available data is scarce, yet the current design effort 
showed remarkable success. However, with increasing number of 
available data in literature, the success of the current model will surely 
increase. In addition, validation experiments rely solely on the manu -
factured material, and considering the aforementioned difficulties with 
the manufacturing of such complex alloys, incorporation of 
manufacturing parameters into the ML model could bring about 
improved accuracy. Nevertheless, the current RHEA design effort uti -
lizing ML has demonstrated that novel alloy design by ML can open new 
venues for applications that demand very high performance with less 
experimental effort and high efficiency and accuracy. 
6. Conclusions 
In this study, a new design strategy with the assistance of machine 
learning (ML) was adopted to optimize mechanical properties of re -
fractory high entropy alloys (RHEAs). Artificial neural network, random 
forest and support vector regression models were utilized to train the 
datasets constructed from experimental results reported in the litera -
ture. The strength, hardness and ductility of unsearched compositions 
were predicted via designed models to obtain an optimal alloy.
