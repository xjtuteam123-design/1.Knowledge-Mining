abstract
By means of high resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM) and high-angle annular dark-
ﬁeld image technique (HAADF), morphological of plate-shaped r phase and interfacial characteristics
between plate-shaped r phase and c phase in Ni-based single crystal superalloys have been studied.
On the basis of HRTEM observations, an atomic structural interface between r phase and c phase with
a step has been proposed. r Phase has the relationship of [0 0 1] c//[1 1/C222]r,( /C22220 )c//(/C22110 )r, (/C222 /C2220 )c//
(1 1 1)r;[ 0 1 1 ]c//[1 1 0]r,( 1 /C2211 )c//(0 0/C221)r with the c phase. The compositional characteristics of the r
phase which are rich in more heavy elements, such as W, Re, Cr and Ru, has also been clearly revealed
by the HAADF imaging technique and Energy Dispersive X-Ray Spectroscopy (EDS) mapping analysis.

1. Introduction
The superior high temperature properties of Ni-based single
crystal superalloys make them the best candidates for use in jet
engines and the blades in aerospace turbine engines [1–4]. To im-
prove the high temperature strength, solid solution strengthening
elements have been added to later generation superalloys. Unfor-
tunately, the formation of topologically close-packed (TCP) phases
depletes the matrix of these elements in long-term exposures at
elevated temperatures. Furthermore, the precipitates also serve
as damage accumulation sites. They can initiate or accelerate crack
propagation and cause serious deterioration in creep strength [5,6].
In the past decade, researchers highly concerned about the alloy
element distribution, which primarily involves solidiﬁcation segre-
gation (interdendrite area and dendrite arm areas) and aging
precipitation (c phase and c0 phase). However, there is no system-
atic study of the distribution of chemical elements and its interac-
tion with the formation of TCP phases at the nanometer and atomic
scale. As previously reported, Rae et al. have proposed that all
alloys can form r phase, in some cases as a meta-stable intermedi-
ate. The r phase acted as nucleation site of other TCP phases, such
as l phase and P phase [7]. In addition, Sims has conﬁrmed that r
phase is commonly precipitated from the c matrix. Due to its
inherent brittleness and plate-like morphology, r plate acts as
many crack initiation sites and reduces high temperature mechan-
ical properties of superalloys [8]. A detailed understanding of r
phase and its interactions with matrix has the vital signiﬁcance
to the design of superalloys. To the authors’ best knowledge, the
report on elemental and interfacial characteristics of r phase and
matrix cannot be found up to now.
As well known, high-resolution electron microscopy has been
an important tool to reveal small precipitates in superalloys. By
using the high resolution image, the visual image of interface
structure can be acquired. In combination with the composition
analysis information can further clarify the atomic structure of
the interface, chemical bonding, defect structure, etc. On the other
hand, the high-angle annular dark-ﬁeld image technique (HAADF)
imaging technique is another powerful mean to reveal the
real-space structure of an alloy phase in atomic scale. Unlike the
contrast in a high resolution transmission electron microscopy
(HRTEM) image which varys depending on imaging conditions,
that in a HAADF image is chemically sensitive and its intensity is
directly in proportion to the atomic numbers of constitutional ele-
ments in the alloy phase. Energy Dispersive X-Ray Spectroscopy
(EDS) is commonly coupled with TEM to observe the microstruc-
ture of the material and to analyze micro-section component. In
the present work, we present substantial microscopic information
of the interface between r phase and c matrix. The elemental dis-
tribution and interfacial characteristics of r phase in Ni-based

2. Experimental procedure
In this study, the experimental alloy was directionally solidiﬁed into cylindrical
single crystal bars with 14 mm in diameter and 150 mm in length. The composi-
tions were determined by an atomic absorption spectroscope as listed in Table 1 .
Specimens were sectioned from the solution-heat-treated single crystals and
carefully prepared for the standard samples with 5 mm in diameter and 25 mm
in length to carry on stress rupture test. TEM slices were mechanically thinned
down to a thickness of approximately 50
lm. Discs of 3 mm in diameter were
punched out of the thin sheets and with ion thinning ﬁnally. Microstructural evo-
lution observations were carried out with Tecnai T20 TEM and JEOL 2010F HRTEM
under an acceleration voltage of 200 kV and a point resolution of 0.19 nm. EDS anal-
ysis was used for the chemical identiﬁcation of precipitated phases and evaluation
of alloying elements segregation characteristic at the interface of
r phase and c ma-
trix in the superalloys.
3. Results
3.1. Nucleation and morphology of the r phase
As the solidiﬁcation proceeds, some alloying elements accumu-
late to the dendrite core, while some other alloying elements tend
to accumulate in the interdendritic liquid. This nonequilibrium
solidiﬁcation process will result in microsegregation between den-
drite core and interdendritic region [9]. Due to the low diffusivities
of alloying elements in Ni-based single crystal superalloys at ele-
vated temperatures, solidiﬁcation and segregation behaviors lead
to the formation of TCP phases in the dendrite core [5,10]. There
has been some researches claimed that the TCP precipitates ﬁrst
formed at regions associated with the dendrite core and eventually
spread into interdendritic regions and covered the entire micro-
structure [7,11]. The r phase is one of the most common TCP
phases in Ni-based single crystal superalloys. It starts to nucleate
at dendritic arms because the long time exposure alleviates the
segregation of the metal elements W, Mo, Cr and Co and makes
them diffuse toward the dendritic cores [12].
Fig. 1 shows the TEM micrograph and the selected area diffrac-
tion patterns (SADPs) of the r phase and c matrix. It is well known
that excessive content of alloying elements in c phase will increase
the Nv (phase control electron vacancy number) value of c phase,
which in turn will favor the precipitation of TCP phases during
thermo exposure. r phase will readily precipitate from the c ma-
trix at solid state and seriously degrades superalloys mechanical
properties due to its plate-like morphology and high refractory
alloying containing [8]. The typical morphology of r phase is
shown in Fig. 1 (a). Large-size r phase was observed with plate-
shaped morphology. The circumference of the r phase was also
found to be c phase, which suggests that there is possible phase
transformation for both phases. Moreover, the two distinctive
phase transformation behavior happened during thermal expo-
sure. The SADPs of the r phase and c matrix can be seen in
Fig. 1 (b). It may be understood according to the SAD analysis that
the exact crystallographic orientation relationships between r
phase and c matrix are listed as follows:
[0 0 1]c//[1 1/C222]r,( /C22220 )c//(/C22110 )r,( /C222 /C2220 )c//(1 1 1)r
[0 1 1]c//[110]r,( 1 /C2211 )c//(0 0/C221)r
Fortunately, the r phase had regular platelet morphology and
deﬁnite orientation relationship with c phase, which indicated that
it precipitated from c phase surrounding the remaining liquid.

loys is reduced. It was found that lower creep rupture strength and
ductility was associated with higher weight fractions of r phase,

3.2. Component analysis of r phase
Fig. 2(a) shows one HAADF image of r/c interfaces in the crept
Ni-based single crystal superalloys, and bright contrast can be ob-
served at interfaces. Element mapping was carried out across inter-
faces corresponding to the area indicated by the rectangle frame in
Fig. 2 (a), and mapping images of Al, Co, Cr, Ni, Re, Ru, Ta and W
are shown in Fig. 2 (b–i), respectively. From the HAADF image as
shown in Fig. 2 (a), r phase is composed of a higher concentration
of heavy elements compared with other areas because of its bright-
er image contrast. Enrichment of W, Re, Cr and Ru can be observed
clearly in
r phase, especially W and Re, which is consistent with
that in Refs. [7,9]. As the primary element of construction of ma-
trix, Ni is more prone to stay in c phase to keep the matrix struc-
ture stability. In addition, there is no obverse preference for Al, Co
and Ta elements, because of they distribute evenly across the r/c
interfaces.
3.3. Interface structure of r phase and c phase
Because the lattice parameters of the r phase and c phase are
not identical, the stress ﬁeld resulting from the lattice misﬁt will
be created. Obviously, the interface with the stress ﬁeld is unstable.
Fig. 3 (b) shows high-resolution TEM micrographs of the inter-
face structure between r phase (lower left) and c phase(upper
right) corresponding to the area of the rectangle in Fig. 3 (a). It is
indicated that the combination of r/c interface appears very well.
Fig. 3(c) shows an atomic scale image of the interface structure en-
larged from the white rectangle frame in Fig. 3 (b). Due to the
crystallographic orientation of sample is [0 0 1] zone axes and the
rotation angle of the specimen holder is X =±3 5 /C176, Y =±3 0 /C176, the
r/c interface observed by HRTEM is not parallel to the electron
beam (edge-on). Thus, the clarity of the interface is not enough
emphasis (sharpening). Importantly, a two-atomic-layer step
(marked by dotted line) on the r/c interface is shown in
Fig. 3 (c). From the atomic image of interface between r phase
and c phase, it is clear that r phase has the relationship of
[0 0 1]c//[1 1/C222]r;( /C222 20)c//(/C221 10)r and ( /C222 /C2220 )c//(1 1 1)r with the c
phase. The orientation relationship between r phase and c phase
shown here is consistent with the SDAP as shown in Fig. 1 (b).
Since direct observation shows that a two-atomic-layer step on
the r/c interface ( Fig. 4 (a)), a schematic illustration of the inter-
face, as shown in Fig. 4 (b), is aiming to present a mechanism for
the formation of the two-atomic-layer step. The balls shown in
Fig. 4(b) do not indicate the true size of atoms. Some of them show
the projection along respective crystal zone axis. The interface
(1 /C2211 )c//(0 0/C221)r is of special signiﬁcance. This particular orienta-
tion relationship involves two close packed planes, perpendicular
to which the variation of the local density of atoms in the largest
making this interface also the most strongly pinned [15]. The crys-
tallographic structure of r phase is known to be tetragonal with 30
atoms per unit cell and parameters a = 0.88 nm, c = 0.45 nm [16].
While, c matrix has the face-centered cubic (fcc) structure with a
lattice parameter of 0.35 nm [17]. In virtue of the crystallographic
orientation, the two-atomic-layer step formation provides the best
lattices match of two distinct crystal structures. Moreover, the
best-ﬁt goal positions will share the same constituent atoms as
their common interface atoms, as indicated in Fig. 4(b). In addition,
the minimum of the displacement of atoms and elastic-strain
energy of the interface are also primarily caused by the combina-
tion mode of two-atomic-layer step. To maintain the optimal

4. Conclusions
In the present work, morphological and interfacial characteris-
tics of r phase and r/c interface in Ni-based single crystal super-
alloys have been investigated by HRTEM. The r phase reveals
plate-shaped morphology and precipitates from the c matrix due
to the segregation of alloying elements. The combination of r/c
interface appears very well, and a two-atomic-layer step is shown
on the
r/c interface. In addition, r phase has the orientation rela-
tionship of [0 0 1] c//[1 1/C222]r,( /C22220 )c//(/C22110 )r,( /C222 /C2220 )c//(1 1 1)r;
[0 1 1]c//[1 1 0]r,( 1 /C2211 )c//(0 0/C221)r with the c phase. The composi-
tional characteristic of the r phase has also been clearly revealed
by HAADF techniques and EDS mapping analysis. W, Re, Cr and
Ru partition preferentially to r phase, and Ni stays at in c phase
to keep the matrix structure stability. Al, Co and Ta distribute
evenly across the r/c interfaces and appear no obverse preference.
