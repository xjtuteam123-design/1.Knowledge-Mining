Design of Novel High‑Strength Ni‑Based Superalloys Using 
a Data‑Driven Approach

Abstract Designing compositions to improve yield 
strength for Ni-based superalloys is a vital operation on 
aeroengines to improve thrust-weight ratio. A data-driven 
approach by combining machine learning and theory-phys-
ical models is proposed to design high-strength Ni-based 
superalloys. Using machine learning to select 12 critical 
features to build predicting model, the design space hav -
ing 372,763 alloys then is determined. The theory-physical 
models on matrix stability, precipitate stability and the sol-
vus temperature of γ′ are used to select microstructure. The 
Gaussian process model is built to predict yield strength, 
determining Ni-15Co-15Cr-4Mo-4Al-3Ti as designed alloy. 
Experimental tests verify the designed alloy having the fea-
tures of microstructure stability, the 1142 °C solvus tem -
perature of γ′, and the yield strength of 657 MPa at 850 °C. 
To still further improve yield strength, the strengthening 
mechanisms on precipitate shearing and coherency strain 
are discussed in detail. A feasible measure is that scales 
down the particle diameter of secondary γ′ through increas-
ing the cooling rate of solution treatment. The data-driven 
superalloy-design way can be used as a typical case of big 
data technology accelerating alloy design. This work tak -
ing data-driven concept has a vital significance on avoiding 
excessive trial-and-error waste to save costs.
Keywords Ni-based superalloys · Data-driven design · 
Machine learning · Precipitate strengthening · Alloy design
1 Introduction
Ni-based superalloys are widely applied into aircraft engines 
due to an excellent high-temperature performance on the 
yield strength, creep resistance, fatigue resistance and oxi-
dized stability [1 ]. The coherent microstructure that is 
composed by the FCC-structure γ-matrix phase with the 
 L12-structure γ ′-precipitate phase can effectively hinder 
dislocation motivation[2 ], wherein the yield strength of 
superalloy is regarded as the critical metric to evaluate the 
resistance of plastic deformation to keep the size stability[3]. 
Owing to the high-performance aircraft having an urgent 
requirement that engine needs to have a high thrust-weight 
ratio[4 ], the stress loading into turbine discs increases 
thereto [5 ]. Therefore, the improvement of yield strength 
has always been the focus of attention. However, having 8 
elements with 1 wt.% resolution from 1 to 10wt.% will exist 
 108 alloys. The trial-and-error tests by scanning the com-
position space are unpassable due to the huge depletion of 
cost and time [6].
To develop novel superalloys, the computational design 
was applied, involving the phase computation (PHACOMP) 
[7] and the new PHACOMP [8 ]. However, an obstacle 
still exists between phases and temperature; furtherly, in

3 Results
3.1 ML Model and Design Optimization
The ML model accuracies are shown in Fig.  4a, with GP 
having the highest accuracy. The extrapolation assessment 
for GP is shown in Fig. 4b. The selection result of maximal 
information subset is shown in Fig.  5. The features were 
ranked using GRA, as shown in Fig.  5a. Inputting ranked 
features into GP one by one, the maximal information subset 
has 12 features, as shown in Fig. 5b.
For Ni-Co-Cr-Mo-Al-Ti-C system, the Co concentra -
tion adopted 1–23 wt.%, with 8–23 wt.% Cr, 1–9 wt.% Mo, 
1–9 wt.% Al, and 1–9 wt.% Ti. Then, C is temporarily not 
considered with 0. The resolution was 1 wt.%. The aim 
temperature in ML model adopted 850 °C corresponding 
to service temperature. Total alloy number is 372,763. The 
statistical numbers were counted and plotted using histo-
gram, as shown in Fig.  6. In Fig.  6a, the yield strengths 
of alloys are clustered in the range of 800 to 1600 MPa. 
The Md in the range of 0.88 to 1.12 accounts for a large 
proportion, as shown in Fig.  6b. In Fig.  6c, Reta decreases 
progressively from 0 to 5.0. The γ′ solvus temperature of 
most alloys is above 1080 °C, as shown in Fig. 6d. The dis-
tributions on Md, Reta and γ′ solvus temperature are shown 
in Fig.  7. Overall, yield strength is lower in design region 
than in Md > 0.915 eV, as shown in Fig.  7a. In Fig.  7b, the 
tendency of yield strength is ascending with Reta increasing, 
for which the reason is Ti improves the anti-phase boundary 
energy (APBE). Figure  7c shows that both vertex and bot-
tom are in Tsolvus < 1170 °C. Based on the design strategies 
to select the design space, a selected alloy with Ni-15Co-
15Cr-4Mo-4Al-3Ti was determined.

5 Conclusion
A data-driven approach on designing novel high-strength Ni-
based superalloys is proposed. The designed alloy Ni-15Co-
15Cr-4Mo-4Al-3Ti is optimized from 372,763 alloys, which 
demonstrates efficiency with avoiding trial-and-error waste.
