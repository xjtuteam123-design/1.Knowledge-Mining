ABSTRACT
Enhanced fuel efficiency and engine performance have driven interest in Co-based superalloys reinforced by 
coherent γ ′ dispersed in the matrix with low stacking fault energy (SFE). However, poor microstructure stability 
due to thermodynamically unstable γ ′ phases limits their application. A (multi-component diffusion multiple) 
MCDM approach was employed to efficiently explore composition-microstructure-property relationships of the 
novel Co – Ti – V – Ni alloys in the present work. Two alloy groups (A: high Ni, B: low Ni) with varying V/Ti ratios 
were designed, homogenized, and analyzed via EPMA, FE-SEM, in situ XRD, TEM, and mechanical testing. Re -
sults revealed that γ ′ phase stability decreased with increasing Ni, contrasting prior studies where Ni improved γ ′ 
stability in Al-containing systems. In situ XRD demonstrated temperature-dependent γ / γ ′ lattice misfit, with 
Group A exhibiting higher misfits than Group B, and the lattice misfit decreased with rising V/Ti ratio. Alloy 4# 
showed anomalous strength increase at 750 
◦
C, attributed to dislocation cross-slip. The deformation mechanisms 
of the alloy exhibit a temperature-dependent evolution: at room temperature, the matrix primarily undergoes 
deformation through SFs, Lomer-Cottrell locks (LCs), and HCP phase formation. As the temperature increases to 
750 
◦
C, deformation twins progressively dominate in the matrix. However, upon further heating to 800 
◦
C, the 
deformation behavior reverts to a combination of SFs and LC locks as the primary mechanisms. SFE calculations 
indicated a high-temperature chemical segregation-assisted SF formation. This work highlights the efficiency of 
MCDM in alloy design and underscores the prominent roles of V in optimizing Co-based superalloys, providing 
insights for future development of thermally stable, high-performance materials.
1. Introduction
Nickel-based superalloys have been widely employed in extreme 
environments characterized by high temperatures, pressures, and hot 
corrosion due to their exceptional mechanical and thermal properties 
[ 1 – 7 ]. However, the ongoing pursuit of enhanced fuel efficiency and 
thrust-to-weight ratios in turbine engines has shifted research focus to -
ward cobalt-based alternatives. Cobalt offers intrinsic advantages over 
nickel, including a higher melting point and improved hot-corrosion 
resistance [ 1 , 2 , 8 – 10 ]. Recent advancements have led to the 
development of novel γ׳strengthened Co-based superalloys showing the 
coherent precipitation mechanism similar to that of Ni-based superal -
loys [ 11 – 31 ]. Furthermore, these alloys demonstrate promising 
high-temperature performance, attributed to their positive lattice misfit 
and low stacking fault energy (SFE) in the matrix, which contributes to 
superior creep and fatigue resistance [ 32 – 36 ].
Despite these merits, most emerging Co-based systems, such as 
Co – Al – W, Co – Ta – V, Co – Nb – V, Co – Al – Ta, Co – Al – Nb, and 
Co – Al – Mo – Nb, suffer from inadequate microstructural stability, pri -
marily due to the thermodynamic instability of their γ ׳phase [ 16 , 21 , 22 ,

design the detrimental-phase-free alloys with optimized properties [ 54 ]. 
The synergistic effects of Ni and V are systematically explored to 
advance next-generation Co-based superalloys.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Composition-dependent hardness
Fig. 2 (a) presents the back-scattered electron (BSE) image of the 
dual-stage heat-treated Ni/12Ti/15V MCDM, highlighting the target 
analysis area. White circles denote the locations selected for composi -

were approximated using a polynomial fitting method. Notably, no 
deleterious phases, commonly observed in refractory-rich MCDMs

4. Conclusions
In the present work, the CoTiVNi alloys were investigated experi -
mentally. The discoveries underscore the critical roles of V/Ti ratio and 
solute segregation in tailoring microstructure stability and mechanical 
performance of novel Co-based superalloys, providing a roadmap for 
optimizing high-temperature alloys via compositionally graded design 
strategies. The details are as follows: 
(1) Efficient alloy design via MCDM : The multi-component diffu -
sion multiple (MCDM) approach successfully established a broad 
composition gradient in the Ni/Co12Ti/Co5Ti15V system, 
enabling systematic exploration of composition-microstructure- 
hardness relationships. The dual-step heat treatment and pre -
cise processing (e.g., HIP, cold rolling, static recrystallization) 
yielded uniform microstructures, confirming MCDM ’ s efficacy in 
accelerating alloy design for γ ′ -strengthened Co-based 
superalloys.
