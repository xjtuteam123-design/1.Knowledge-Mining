Wear mechanisms of coated carbide tools during high‑speed face 
milling of  Ti2AlNb intermetallic alloys
Xin Wang1 · Biao Zhao1 · Wenfeng Ding1 · Jiahao Song1 · Hai Li2
Received: 22 September 2023 / Accepted: 25 October 2023 / Published online: 13 November 2023 
© The Author(s), under exclusive licence to Springer-Verlag London Ltd., part of Springer Nature 2023
Abstract
Ti2AlNb intermetallic alloy is currently the most promising lightweight high-temperature resistant material in the aerospace 
industry, owing to its high specific strength, favourable room temperature plasticity, outstanding temperature strength, and 
creep resistance. High speed cutting technology is characterized by low cutting force, minimal thermal deformation, high 
material machining efficiency and precision, which is widely used in machining TiAl intermetallic alloys and associated key 
components to ensure superior machined surface quality and dimensional accuracy. However, there are serious tool wear 
during high speed cutting of Ti2AlNb intermetallic alloys because of its high specific strength and temperature strength, 
and the tool wear mechanism is unknown. In this paper, the high-speed face milling trials of Ti2AlNb intermetallic alloys 
are performed to investigate tool wear evolutions and wear mechanisms. More specifically, tool wear morphologies, tool tip 
breakage, machined surface roughness, and cutting forces are investigated in detail. Results indicate that high-speed face 
milling results in severe tool wears, leading to a rapid increase in cutting forces and machined surface roughness during the 
severe wear stages. The tool's service life is limited to 228 s due to tool tip breakage resulted from coating delamination, 
cracking, and mechanical impact. Tool wear morphologies encompass tool rake face and flank face wear, exhibiting typical 
characteristics of coating delamination and microchipping. Adhesive wear and oxidation wear are the primary mechanisms 
of tool wear, occurring on both the rake face and flank face. The experimental results can provide a reference for high speed 
machining of TiAl material and promote the application of the material.
Keywords Ti 2AlNb intermetallic alloy · High-speed face milling · Wear characteristics · Wear mechanism
1 Introduction
Ti2AlNb intermetallic alloy, as a novel TiAl material, has 
emerged as one of the most promising new materials in 
manufacturing key components for the aerospace indus-
try due to its exceptional mechanical properties, includ-
ing high-temperature strength and specific strength [ 1–6]. 
However, the high cutting force and temperature have to 
be confronted during the machining process, giving rise to 
significant challenges, including low machining efficiency, 
severe tool wear, and poor surface quality [7 –12]. In this 
case, the application and promotion of Ti2AlNb intermetallic 
alloys in high efficiency and precision fabrication of aero-
space components are significantly impeded. Therefore, it is 
crucially important to develop a new manufacturing method 
for Ti2AlNb intermetallic alloys that can balance high effi-
ciency and precision simultaneously.
High-speed cutting technology is characterized by low 
cutting force, minimal thermal deformation, high machin-
ing efficiency, and superior surface quality [13–16]. It finds 
extensive application in the machining of TiAl intermetallic 
alloys and their critical components in aerospace [17– 20]. 
However, high-speed cutting can produce higher tempera-
tures and exacerbate tool wear [21–24]. Tool wear is affected 
by many factors, such as cutting force, cutting temperature, 
cooling conditions, tool materials, etc. [25– 30]. It plays a 
crucial role in achieving high-quality machining, particularly 
during high-speed cutting processes involving TiAl inter -
metallic alloys, which attracts more attention from scholars 
[31–33]. Ma et al. [19] carried out high-speed side milling 
of Ti2AlNb intermetallic alloy and conducted a study on

accompanied by noticeable chipping on both the rake face 
and flank face of the tool, as well as crack formation, coat-
ing delamination, material adhesion, among others. Klocke 
et al. [34] conducted an experimental study on high-speed 
turning of TiAl intermetallic alloy under various lubrica-
tion conditions. The tool service life was limited when using 
minimum quantity lubrication, whereas it could be extended 
by implementing cryogenic lubrication with liquid nitrogen. 
Hood et al. [35] investigated the effects of varying cutting 
speeds on cutting force, cutting temperature, and tool wear 
morphologies during high-speed face milling of TiAl inter-
metallic alloy. The findings suggested a positive correlation 
between cutting speed and both cutting temperature and tool 
wear severity, with diffusion wear and adhesion wear being 
the primary mechanisms of tool degradation. Ren et al. [36] 
performed an optimization experiment on tool performance 
for high-speed machining of intermetallic alloys, with the 
objective of maximizing tool service life. The primary 
factors taken into consideration were the tool type and its 
structural parameters. The study revealed that an increase 
in rake angle and clearance angle of the tool led to a wider 
flank wear width, while an increase in spiral angle resulted 
in reduced tool wear. Bai et al. [37] preformed cutting trials 
on Ti2AlNb intermetallic alloys using a carbide-coated tool 
to investigate the influence of cutting parameters on tool 
life. An increase in cutting speed did not significantly affect 
the change in cutting force, but it did lead to a significant 
rise in cutting temperature, resulting in reduced tool life. 
Benedicto et al. [38] conducted tool wear experiments and 
revealed that the primary mechanisms of tool wear were 
abrasive and adhesive. Anwar et al. [ 20] conducted com-
parative cutting experiments using both multi-coated and 
uncoated inserts, and found that the latter reduced cutting 
force and prolonged tool life compared to the former. The 
thermal shielding effect of coating materials led to high tem-
perature accumulation in the cutting area and severe tool 
wear. Although extensive research has been conducted on 
tool wear during the cutting of TiAl intermetallic alloys, 
there lacks experimental data supporting in-depth analysis 
of the tool wear mechanism during high-speed face milling 
of Ti2AlNb intermetallic alloys. Therefore, it is imperative 
to investigate tool wear behaviors during high-speed face 
milling of Ti2AlNb intermetallic alloys.
In this paper, the tool wear experiments with coated car -
bide tools are conducted during high-speed face milling of 
 Ti2AlNb intermetallic alloys. The experimental condition 
and scheme for high-speed face milling trials are described 
in Sect. 2. Subsequently, the tool wear evolution, tool wear 
morphologies, tool wear mechanisms, tool tip breakage and 
the influence of tool wear on machined surface roughness 
and milling force are analyzed in Sect.  3. Finally, the main 
findings are summarized in last section.

3 Results and discussions
3.1 Tool wear Evolution
Tool wear evolution reflects the wear process of the coated 
carbide tool during the whole machining process. In this 
section, images of tool wear at different machining times 
were collected by 3D confocal microscope, and the wear 
evolution of rake face and flank face was analyzed. The 
flank wear mainly focuses on the change of the wear 
band width, while the rake face wear pays attention to the 
change of coating delamination area.
3.1.1 Flank face wear evolution
The measurement of flank wear on a tool is a crucial factor 
in evaluating its overall wear and tear. Typically, the width 
of the flank wear serves as an indicator for when a tool has 
reached its failure point [39]. To better understand the evo-
lution of high-speed face milling on Ti2AlNb intermetallic 
alloy, three-dimensional confocal microscopy was used to 
observe images of the flank face at various stages through-
out machining until tool failure occurred. Simultaneously, 
measurements were taken to determine the width of the 
flank wear. The tool life criteria in this paper are as fol-
lows: (i) the tool tip is broken; (ii) the wear width of flank 
face reached 0.2 mm [31, 40, 41]. In the initial stage of 
wear, the tool's cutting edge is sharp and the contact area 
between the tool surface and machined surface is small, 
resulting in high compressive stress and a rapid rate of 
tool wear. Subsequently, the tool enters a steady state of 
wear where its width increases slowly over time. Finally, 
due to large cutting forces and temperatures, the rate of 
tool wear rapidly accelerates leading to severe wear and 
eventual failure [42]. The tool life of high-speed face mill-
ing Ti2AlNb intermetallic alloy is only 228 s, with a cor -
responding machining length of 1.6 m, indicating severe 
tool wear. To ensure the quality of the machined surface, 
it is necessary to replace the tool before reaching the stage 
of severe wear during milling (Fig.  2).

3.2 Tool wear morphologies
The rake face and flank face of the cutter rub against chips 
and workpieces, resulting in tool wear. After the machin-
ing, the rake face and flank face were carefully observed 
by SEM, and typical phenomena were found and analyzed.
3.2.1 Rake face wear morphologies
In high-speed face milling, the friction between the rake 
face and the chip generates high pressure and temperature 
[43]. Subsequently, SEM is employed to detect tool wear 
morphologies, revealing typical characteristics. Figure  4 
illustrates coating delamination and micro chipping on 
the rake face during high-speed face milling of Ti2AlNb 
intermetallic alloy. The tool coating adjacent to the cutting 
edge exhibits delamination at multiple locations (Fig.  4a). 
This is attributed to the high cutting speed and resultant 
generation of significant heat during high-speed milling. 
The difference in thermal conductivity between the tool 
matrix and coating material induces thermal stress, which 
renders the tool coating susceptible to cracking. The coat -
ing material is subjected to periodic mechanical impact 
loads, which can result in delamination of the coating [44]. 
The micro chipping observed in Fig.  4b is a small local 
notch on the cutting edge that forms under the compre-
hensive influence of multiple factors. The reason can be 
concluded that the tool is exposed to strong mechanical 
and thermal influences during intermittent milling in the 
machining process, owing to the poor thermal conductivity 
of Ti2AlNb material, and hardness and brittleness of the 
tool’s material [45]. In addition, significant material adhe-
sion was observed on the rake face, which was caused by 
the friction between the cutter and the chips. The chemical 
composition of the material was analyzed in Sect.  3.3.1.
3.2.2 Flank wear morphologies
During high-speed face milling of Ti2AlNb materials, 
the tool flank face experiences strong friction with the 
machined surface, leading to flank wear [ 46]. Figure  5a 
shows delamination of the tool coating on the flank face, 
which is smaller in area than that on the rake face (Fig. 4a). 
The primary indications are spotty delamination, which 
poses a challenge to detect. Additionally, micro chipping 
is also present but less severe than that of the rake face 
(Fig. 4b). This can be attributed to the fact that the tool's 
rake face experiences both mechanical impact and friction 
compared to its flank face. The intense mechanical impact 
on the tool's rake face increases the likelihood of micro 
chipping and coating delamination. Similarly, the mate-
rial adhesion phenomenon was also observed on the flank 
face of the cutter due to the friction between the flank face 
and the machined surface. Compared with the rake face, 
the material adhesion was more serious and the adhesion 
area was larger.

3.3.1 Adhesive wear
Tool wear is influenced by various factors, including 
machining parameters, cooling conditions, and workpiece 
materials. Previous studies have utilized SEM and EDS 
for analyzing tool wear mechanisms [47]. Prior to these 
analyses, the AlTiN-coated carbide end mill undergoes 
ultrasonic vibration cleaning in an alcohol container. 
Figure  6 displays the detection results of the end mill, 
revealing conspicuous material adhesion on both the tool's 
rake and flank faces. EDS element analysis identified a 
significant amount of Nb element in the adhesive material 
(Point B, Point D), which is primarily present in the 
workpiece rather than the tool itself (Point A, Point C). 
Therefore, the comparison of elements confirms that the 
adhesive material is identical to the workpiece material. 
Consequently, it can be inferred that adhesive wear occurs 
during milling processes due to bonding between tool and 
workpiece materials. Ti2AlNb intermetallic alloys contain 
high levels of Ti element, which exhibit strong chemical 
activity and affinity with tool materials. Under high 
temperature and pressure conditions in the cutting zone, 
 Ti2AlNb material easily adheres to the tool surface when the 
distance between the tool and workpiece materials reaches 
interatomic distance. During milling, this bonded Ti2AlNb is 
torn due to relative motion and friction between the tool and 
workpiece, causing simultaneous removal of material particles 
from the tool matrix and resulting in adhesive wear [48].

3.3.2 Oxidative wear
During high-speed cutting of difficult-to-cut materials, 
the cutting temperature is elevated. The Co, W and other 
elements in carbide tool materials react with O in the air 
to form cobalt oxide and tungsten oxide, which are easily 
carried away by chips or friction. As a result, tool wear is 
accelerated [48]. Based on the detection results shown in 
Fig. 7, O element was detected at both the tool rake face 
and flank face, in addition to the elements of tool matrix 
material and workpiece material. The atomic percentage 
of O at point E was found to be 42% (Fig.  7a), while that 
at point F was measured as 14.8% (Fig.  7b), indicating 
oxidation reactions have occurred at both locations.
3.4 Tool tip breakage
Carbide tools are prone to damage during intermittent 
machining. The tool tip breakage was observed during 
high-speed face milling of Ti2AlNb intermetallic alloys, 
and the process of tip breakage was recorded. Subse-
quently, the modes of tool tip breakage were observed 
and analyzed. Finally, the chemical wear of tool tip was 
investigated.
3.4.1 Tool tip breakage evolution
Tool breakage is a common occurrence during the milling 
process with carbide tools. In high-speed face milling of 
 Ti2AlNb intermetallic alloy, tool tip breakage can occur. 
Figure 8 illustrates the evolution of the tool tip, including 
the tool tip itself, rank face, flank face and three-dimensional 
model of tool tip breakage.
At the onset of face milling test (L = 0.04 m), no signifi-
cant alteration is observed at the tool tip. As the milling 
length increases (L = 0.4 m), micro-chipping becomes appar-
ent at the tool tip, while remaining undetectable from the 
tool flank face. Chipping occurs at a milling length of 0.8 m, 
followed by tool tip breakage (L = 1.6 m) with a significant 
pit observed on the tool rake face and tool tip breakage noted 
on the flank face.
3.4.2 Tool tip breakage modes
To comprehensively detect the tool tip breakage modes, 
the surface micro-morphology of all four tips were 
observed from both rake and flank faces. Figures  9 and 10 
reveal typical characteristics such as coating delamination, 
cracks, and breakages. Coating delamination is primar -

in tool tip failure under mechanical impact [31]. In addi-

detection at the tool tip where Nb and O were detected in 
multiple locations. Material adhesion and oxidation reac-
tions are also responsible for tool tip breakage.
3.5 Effect of tool wear on milling forces 
and machined surface morphology
Tool wear is a crucial factor that impacts both milling force 
and machined surface roughness [49, 50]. Figure 11 plots the 
milling forces at various stages, as well as the force signal 
during the tool's steady wear stage (L = 0.8 m). The milling 
force is the average force in three directions, which is measured 
by three times. As the machining length increases, so does the 
corresponding increase in milling force due to gradual tool 
wear. When the milling length is less than 0.4 m, the tool enters 
the initial wear stage and experiences a small maximum milling 
force of 6.14 N, after which there is a rapid increase in milling 
force until tool failure occurs. The maximum milling force 
reaches 24.81 N, representing an increase of over three times.

4 Conclusions
In this study, the tool wear characteristics of high-speed 
face milling of Ti2AlNb intermetallic alloy are investi-
gated comprehensively because current research lacks 
experimental data supporting in-depth analysis of the 
tool wear mechanism during high-speed face milling. The 
experimental results can provide a reference for high speed 
machining of TiAl material and promote the application 
of the material. In particular, the evolution, morphologies 
and mechanisms of tool wear as well as the occurrence 
of tool tip breakage are analyzed in detail. Furthermore, 
the influence of tool wear on milling force and machined 
surface roughness is thoroughly explored. Based on the 
above findings, several conclusions can be drawn:
