alloys mainly consist of niobium-based solid solutions (Nbss) and
silicides (Nb 5Si3,N b 3Si, etc.), where the Nbss provides room tem-
perature fracture toughness as the matrix and the brittle silicides
provide the strength at high temperatures [3,5–7].
Nb-Si binary alloys exhibit a poor fracture toughness at room
temperatures[8,9] and an inadequate oxidation resistance at high
temperatures[10–15]. Therefore, researchers introduced Ti, Hf to
increase the fracture toughness at room temperatures and add
Cr, Al to improve the oxidation resistance, thus Nb-Si-Ti based
multi-alloys (Nb-Si-Ti-Cr-Al-Hf) were developed [3,16–19]. Based
on this alloy system, most studies recently focused on improving
the room temperature properties further by the regulation of solid-
iﬁcation process [20–26] and the multi-component alloying. The
plasticity and fracture toughness at room temperatures have been
greatly improved up to 20 MPa /C1 m
1/2 by different alloying elements,
such as Y, V, Hf, B and Er [27–35]. Besides these elements, as a
skeleton key to improve many aspects at the same time, element
Zr has been widely studied. It has been proved that Zr can improve
the strength, plasticity, toughness at room temperature and the
oxidation resistance[36–39]. Wang et al. [37] reported that the sub-
stitution of Zr for Hf in Nb-Si-Ti based multi-alloys reﬁned the
microstructure and improved the room temperature toughness.
Sankar et al. [36] found that the addition of 4 at.% Zr signiﬁcantly
improved the plasticity of Nb-Si alloys at room temperatures,
and the plastic strain of compression increased from less than
0.5 % to near 2.5 %. They also revealed that Zr accelerated the dis-
sociation kinetics of Nb 3Si phases during solidiﬁcation leading to
the economy of heat treatment time and energy [40]. Therefore,
Zr plays an important role in the microstructural controls,
improvements of room temperature properties and process simpli-
ﬁcations of Nb-Si-Ti based multi-alloys.
Through the addition of alloying elements such as Zr, the low
toughness at room temperatures of Nb-Si-Ti based multi-alloys
can be well solved. However, it also leads to a more complex alloy
system and phases with new deformation behavior at high temper-
atures. But the systematic studies about high temperature proper-
ties still focused on the early simple Nb-Si based alloys [41,42]. The
effects of new elements on high temperature properties have not
been studied systematically. These lack of researches about high
temperature properties seriously limits the development and prac-
tical application of alloys. Furthermore, due to the complex crystal
structure of silicides, such as D8 1 for a-Nb5Si3 and D88 for c-Nb5Si3,
the research ﬁeld of deformation behavior at high temperatures of
Nb-Si-Ti based multi-alloys is still in a relatively backward state
compared to other superalloys. In some other intermetallic sys-
tems, researchers have made great efforts to explore deformation
mechanisms and slip systems at high temperatures. For instance,
Ni
3Al ( c0) has been reported with a stacking-fault-related slip sys-
tem < 112 > f111g [43–47] and a super-dislocation slip system
< 110 > f111g [45,48–51]. Tang et al. [48] revealed a sequence of
c0 rafts shearing events with the evolution of tertiary creep: indi-
vidual screw dislocations, antiphase boundary-coupled dislocation
pairs, and superlattice intrinsic stacking faults. They proposed that
the softening mechanism during the tertiary creep of nickel-based
superalloys at high temperatures and low stresses is the emer-
gence of new super-dislocation types with higher mobility rather
than the density rise of a single type of super-dislocation produced
during the later secondary creep stage [48]. These discoveries are
very helpful to the study of high temperature deformation and
development of nickel-based superalloys. There are also some
studies about dislocation movements in TiAl that promote the
development of TiAl based alloys [52–56]. However, as for silicides,
there is little study about the dislocation characterization in a-
Nb5Si3[57]. The high temperature deformation and dislocations
in Nb-Si-Ti based multi-alloys, especially in c-Nb5Si3 are still
unclear. In recent years, some studies began to explore the soften-
ing mechanism during the compressive deformation at high tem-
perature, including recovery and recrystallization [58,59]. But it is
still necessary to explore the dislocation movements in silicides
and further determine the deformation mechanism of alloys at
high temperatures. This will be very conducive to the design of
compositions and microstructures.
In the present work, a series of Nb-15Si-24Ti-4Cr-2Al-2Hf-xZr
(x = 0; 1; 3; 5 at.%) multicomponent alloy bars were produced by
directional solidiﬁcations. The effect of Zr on the microstructure
and tensile strength at 1250 /C176C of the alloys was investigated.
The mechanism of deformation and fracture at high temperature
was discussed, including the work hardening and dynamic soften-
ing. More importantly, new super-dislocations in c-Nb5Si3 were
revealed with a slip system for the ﬁrst time. Finally, we put for-
ward constructive suggestions on microstructural designs.
2. Experimental
2.1. Material preparation
The master alloy ingots were prepared by vacuum non-
consumable arc-melting, with different nominal compositions of
Nb-15Si-24Ti-4Cr-2Al-2Hf-xZr (x = 0; 1; 3; 5 at.%). Then master
alloy rods were cut from the ingot by electro-discharge machining
(EDM). Before directional solidiﬁcations (DS), the rods were
machined by a lathe to remove the oxidized surface and cleaned
with ethanol. Then the rods were put into a Y
2O3 crucible, which
was nominally 18 mm in outside diameter, 15 mm in inside diam-
eter and 220 mm in length.
The directional solidiﬁcations were carried out in a Bridgman
furnace. The furnace chamber was evacuated to a vacuum level
of 5.0 /C2 10/C0 3 Pa. Then high purity (99.99 vol%) argon was backﬁlled
into the chamber to minimize the contamination and volatiliza-
tion. The melt temperature was set at 1980 /C176C and the directional
solidiﬁcations were designed at a drawing rate of 500 lm/s with a
Ga-In-Sn coolant. After the directional solidiﬁcations, the heat
treatment was carried out in a high vacuum heat treatment fur-
nace at 1450 /C176C for 12 h. Then the sample bars were cooled down
to room temperature in the furnace. These samples with different
nominal contents of Zr were marked as base alloys (0 at.% Zr), 1Zr
alloys (1 at.% Zr), 3Zr alloys (3 at.% Zr) and 5Zr alloys (5 at.% Zr),
respectively.
2.2. Mechanical testing
Plate-shaped specimens with a gauge length of 30 mm and a
cross section of 3 /C2 2m m2 were used for tensile tests by a univer-
sal testing machine (HT-9102, Hung Ta Instrument Co., ltd.) with a
vacuum high temperature environmental chamber. The chamber
was evacuated to a high vacuum (<5.0 /C2 10/C0 3 Pa). Then the spec-
imens were heated to 1250 /C176C and held for 30 min before the tests.
The tensile tests were conducted at a constant axial speed of
0.6 mm/min. The loading direction was parallel to the solidiﬁcation
direction. For each composition of the alloy, the tensile strengths of
three samples were averaged to a statistical result.
2.3. Microstructural characterization
Microstructures of different alloys were observed using an elec-
tron probe micro-analyzer (EPMA; JEOL JXA-8100) in the back-
scatter electron mode (BSE) with energy dispersive spectroscope
(EDS). The phase proportions were measured by the Image-
pro6.0 software, combining the microstructures of a longitudinal
section with a cross section. Before the observation, the samples

3. Results
3.1. Microstructures of Nb-Si-Ti based multi-alloys with Zr additions
The microstructures of different alloys in the longitudinal sec-
tion are shown in Fig. 1 with black arrows indicating the direction
of solidiﬁcation. The light gray regions are niobium solid solutions
(Nbss), the gray regions are a-Nb5Si3 phases and the dark regions
represent c-Nb5Si3 phases as indicated by red arrows in Fig. 1 .I t
can be noticed that the addition of Zr reﬁnes the directional
microstructures and the ﬁnest microstructure can be obtained in
the 5Zr alloy as shown in Fig. 1(d). Some ﬁne eutectic cells appear
in the microstructures of 3Zr and 5Zr alloys as indicated by green
arrows. In addition, there are few micro-cracks in silicides of the
base, 1Zr and 3Zr alloy. But the 5Zr alloy exhibits a dense
microstructure without any cracks. Table 1 presents the volume
fraction of phases in different alloys and the content of Zr in differ-
ent phases measured by EDS. It is obvious that with the addition of
Zr, the amount of c-Nb5Si3 increases from 1.5 vol% to 45.8 vol%
while the amount of a-Nb5Si3 decreases. When the content of Zr
reaches to 5 at.%, a-Nb5Si3 completely disappear which is also con-
ﬁrmed by XRD. In all alloys with the Zr addition, most of Zr is seg-
regated in silicides, and the Nbss contains little Zr. Compared to a-
Nb5Si3, Zr prefers more to dissolve in c-Nb5Si3 and its content
increases greatly from 0 to 9.84 at.% with the addition of Zr. There-
fore, it can be inferred that Zr promote the formation of c-Nb5Si3
instead of a-Nb5Si3.This is mainly because that Zr and Nb atoms
have similar atomic radii and the Zr 5Si3 exhibits a similar crystal
structure to c-Nb5Si3 (D88). Zr can be dissolved in c-Nb5Si3 by sub-
stituting Nb atoms and form a substitutional solid solution to pro-
mote the formation of c-Nb5Si3. The similar substitutional solid
solution (Hf 5Si3) was also reported in Nb-Si-Ti based multi-alloys,
which also stabilizes c-Nb5Si3[60–64].
3.2. Stress-strain behavior at 1250 /C176C
In order to characterize the deformation and fracture behavior
at high temperature, tensile tests were carried out at 1250 /C176C.
Fig. 2(a) presents the engineering stress-strain plots of the investi-
gated alloys. Fig. 2(b) shows the fracture samples after tensile tests
at 1250 /C176C. The base alloy exhibits a brittle fracture with the high-
est tensile strength and the least plastic deformation only about
0.9 % elongation. Under the conditions of approximate strain rates

arc melting is also reported to be close to 300 MPa [58,65]. The

strengths reach the maximum values as shown in Fig. 2 (b). After
the necking, the alloys containing Zr present signiﬁcant ﬂowing
deformations with a stress ﬂuctuation resulting from a balance of
work hardening and dynamic softening. With the increase of Zr
content, the plasticity of alloys is gradually improved. The 5Zr alloy
shows the highest plasticity about 21.0 % elongation as compared
to the alloys of other compositions. It is noteworthy that the ﬂow-

4. Discussion
4.1. Obstructions of deformation in 3Zr alloys
According to the EBSD results as shown in Fig. 5, it is conﬁrmed
that the DRV in Nbss and c-Nb5Si3 is the main softening mecha-
nism of 3Zr alloy during tensile deformation at 1250 /C176C. More
importantly, the a-Nb5Si3 do not take part in deformation or recov-
ery during the tensile deformation. It plays an effective barrier to
prevent the further deformation and work hardening which is pro-
vided by Nbss and c-Nb5Si3.
According to the observation of 3Zr samples at different strains
as shown in Fig. 6, it can be concluded that the fracture of 3Zr alloy
originates from the cracking of silicides, which occurs at a strain of
nearly 17 % in the later stage of the tensile tests. It is different from
the results obtained by Tang and Guo [58,59], where the deforma-
tion and failure processes of Nb-Si-Ti based alloy were investigated
at a temperature range of 1350–1440 /C176C. In their study, the initial
softening mechanism was mainly attributed to the propagation of
cracks in c-Nb5Si3, which is not observed in the initial deformation
of 3Zr alloy in our work. This indicates that the addition of Zr
slightly improve the plasticity of c-Nb5Si3 and enables the DRV
in c-Nb5Si3 at 1250 /C176C although no signiﬁcant morphological
deformation observed. Fig. 12 shows a series of schematic dia-
grams revealing the deformation and fracture of 3Zr tensile sam-
ples at high temperature. A continuous Nbss phase embedded
with rod-like silicides is used to simplify the microstructure of
3Zr alloy as shown in Fig. 12 (a). The gray, blue, and green regions
represent the Nbss, c-Nb5Si3 and a-Nb5Si3, respectively. There are
some micro-cracks in the silicides. At the stage of elastic and uni-
form plastic deformation (strain < 3 %) as shown in Fig. 12 (b), the
sample is elongated along the tensile direction, and the cross-
sectional area decreases uniformly. The plastic deformation at this
stage can be attributed to the deformation of Nbss and c-Nb5Si3.

4.3. The effects of Zr addition
The alloys with low Zr additions exhibit brittle fractures, espe-
cially the base alloy. It exhibits a high tensile strength remarkably
at high temperatures by the load-carrying capacity of the a-Nb5Si3.
But it also limits the deformation of Nbss leading to a weak work
hardening and a catastrophic brittle fracture. The addition of Zr
increases the amount of c-Nb5Si3 and decreases the amount of a-
Nb5Si3 as shown in Table 1 . Compared to the a-Nb5Si3, the c-
Nb5Si3 represents a lower strength and a better plasticity. How-
ever, it still cannot provide an effective work hardening in 1Zr
and 3Zr alloys. It is because the a-Nb5Si3 hinder the deformation
of Nbss and c-Nb5Si3, resulting in local stress concentrations. Neck-
ing with slight nonuniform work hardening tends to occur in these
areas. Therefore, the low addition of Zr (1Zr and 3Zr alloys) causes
necking and the decrease of tensile strength.
However, when the content of Zr reaches 5 at.%, there is a com-
pletely different situation. The alloy exhibits a ﬁne microstructure
without any a-Nb5Si3. A new orientation relationship and coherent
interfaces formed between Nbss and c-Nb5Si3 which promote a
cooperative deformation and provide a signiﬁcant work hardening.
As a result, the 5Zr alloy represents an excellent plasticity due to
the DRV and DRX at high temperatures. And compared to other
low-Zr alloys, the 5Zr alloy exhibits the least drop of tensile
strength. It shows the best comprehensive property of the plastic-
ity and the tensile strength. Therefore, the double-edged effects of
Zr addition are revealed. By introducing c-Nb5Si3 to achieve the
movement of super-dislocations, Zr improves plasticity and work
hardening of Nb-Si-Ti based multi-alloys at high temperatures.
Although the strength of the alloy decreases ﬁrst with a small
amount of Zr addition, this is because the decrease of a-Nb5Si3
reduces the load carrying capacity, while the remaining a-Nb5Si3
limits the deformation and causes insufﬁcient work hardening.
But in the 5Zr alloy, the work hardening caused by cooperative
deformation of Nbss and
c-Nb5Si3 exceeds the adverse effects
caused by the disappearance of a-Nb5Si3, so the strength re-

This work investigates the effect of Zr element on the
microstructure and tensile deformation at 1250 /C176C of the direc-

Nb5Si3 and c-Nb5Si3 phases with different proportions, and thus
reveals a new super-dislocation type and slip system in c phases.
