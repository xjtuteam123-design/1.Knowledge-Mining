Abstract 
A pre-deformation process was employed for a TiAl a lloy via high-temperature torsion, in which 
the stability of the constituent phases was tailore d, resulting in enhanced hardening capability and 
ductility via a gradient microstructure. A sample w ith a pre-torsion of 360° exhibited a yield 
strength of 475 MPa and an ultimate tensile strength of 592 MPa, with a tensile ductility of 47% at 
850 °C. The tensile properties were significantly e nhanced compared with the as-forged sample, 
which exhibited values of 395 MPa, 494 MPa, and 4.6 %, respectively. The physical mechanisms 
for the significant enhancement of the mechanical p roperty of the TiAl alloys were studied 
in-depth via of transmission electron microscopy, e lectron-backscattered diffraction, and 
high-energy X-ray diffraction techniques. The high strength is mainly attributed to the twin

1. Introduction 
The pursuit of an ideal balance between lightness, strength, and ductility in metallic materials has 
been a long-standing and central focus for material scientists and engineers. Intermetallics of 
γ-based TiAl alloys are of special interest owing to their low density (3.9-4.2 g/cm 
3), superior 
creep properties, and oxidation resistance at elevated temperatures [1, 2]. TiAl alloys have already 
been used for decades in components such as gas tur bine blades, turbine wheels, and exhaust 
valves [3-6]. The demand for TiAl alloys with excel lent high-temperature performance has been 
fueled by the successful substitution of heavy Ni-b ased superalloys, which can result in a weight 
reduction up to 50% in turbine engines [7-10]. High -Nb-containing TiAl alloys, as the third 
generation of TiAl alloys, have a higher yield stre ngth and service temperature compared with

previous generations of TiAl alloys, and meet the d emands of next-generation aircraft engines 
[11-13]. However, the main obstacles that limit the ir wide applications include their inherently 
brittle nature and the lack of an effective process ing method to achieve satisfactory mechanical 
performance at temperatures above 750 °C. The well- known TiAl polysynthetic twinned (PST) 
single crystals with well-aligned crystal orientati ons have been investigated by material scientists 
for decades [14-15]. As reported by Chen et al. [11 ], a Ti–45Al–8Nb (at. %) alloy with 
well-aligned 0°-orientation PST single crystals was successfully realized; exhibited a yield 
strength of 637 MPa and fracture elongation of 8.1% at 900 °C. However, in the case of 
polycrystalline TiAl alloys, available methods to i mprove the mechanical properties at high 
temperatures have scarcely been reported. 
Gradient designs are widely used in engineering systems, such as biological materials [16, 17] and 
structural materials [18-21], and are used to promo te plastic deformation via deformation-induced 
transformation, and to prevent premature failure. R ecently, gradient microstructures with either 
grain size or twin spacing have been reported to po ssess high strength as well as ductility in 
high-Mn steels, aluminide alloys, and copper [19, 2 1-25]. The formation of an ultrafine-grained 
structure can decrease the brittle-ductile transiti on temperature in hard deformed materials, and 
thus may solve the problems associated with some Ti Al alloys, such as the service temperature, 
which is also in the temperature range of the britt le-ductile transition [26, 27]. Moreover, 
interfaces in microstructures play a crucial role i n enhancing plasticity. The number of interfaces 
and their spacing can tailor the deformability of t he material. Our previous work [28] 
demonstrates that the density of twins increased wi th the radial distance from the center of 180° 
torsional sample, and the γ grain is recrystallized at the twin-twin junctions.

The rapid development of the synchrotron-based high -energy X-ray diffraction (HE-XRD) 
technique in recent years has proven to be a powerful tool for characterizing the strain partitioning 
among constituent phases and measuring the strain d istribution based on the phases of 
polycrystalline materials [29-33]. This technique h as been widely used to characterize 
high-entropy alloys [30], TRIP steels [31], and titanium alloys [34]; however, TiAl alloys have not 
been systematically investigated using HE-XRD. In a ddition, the distribution of strains/stresses 
and the evolution of the microstructure during mech anical deformation have been scarcely 
reported. It is essential to understand the changes that occur in the microstructure and the 
distribution of strains/stresses among the constitu ent phases during plastic deformation, because 
poor microstructure stability and performance relia bility seriously limit the applications of TiAl 
alloys, especially at service temperatures [35, 36]. Characterization methods, such as transmission 
electron microscopy (TEM), scanning electron micros copy (SEM), and electron- backscattered 
diffraction (EBSD) are also powerful tools for inve stigating the deformation mechanisms and 
microstructure changes of engineering materials. Th ese tools are complementary techniques to 
study plastic deformation, and can facilitate a better understanding of such mechanisms in material 
samples. 
In this work, we attempt to explore the possibility of strengthening and toughening using the 
gradient twin structure, and the evolution of the m icrostructure induced by the high-temperature 
torsion of a high-Nb-containing TiAl alloy. The cor responding microstructure and load 
partitioning among the constituent phases are then carefully studied with a focus on torsion and 
tension, using EBSD, TEM, and HE-XRD. The ultimate goal is to provide new insights into the 
development of polycrystalline intermetallic materials and to further improve the understanding of

2. Experimental procedure 
A high-Nb-containing TiAl alloy with a nominal composition of Ti-45Al-8.5Nb-0.2W-0.2B-0.02Y 
(at. %) in this study was prepared using a plasma a rc cold-hearth process, in Shanghai, China. A 
part of the ingot with dimensions of Φ100 mm × 150 mm was canned by 304 stainless steel t ubes 
and thereafter forged at 1250 °C via a two-step for ging process [37]. The preparation of the 
torsional samples is described in our previous work [28]. Two torsional angles, 180° and 360°, 
were selected for the pre-torsion tension tests. Af ter pre-torsion deformation, square collets of the 
sample were machined into threads; thus, samples wi th a gauge section of diameter 5 mm, length 
35 mm, and total length 71 mm were prepared accordi ng to the standard GB/T 4338-2006 
(metallic materials-tensile testing at elevated temperature) [38] for the tensile tests. The specimen 
geometries for the torsion and pre-torsional tensio n are shown in Fig. 1a and b. The tensile tests 
were conducted using a WDW-50 universal material testing system at 850 °C in laboratory air. 
The microstructure was examined by a field-emission Zeiss Supra 55 SEM instrument combined 
with EBSD analyses. In Fig. 2, the microstructure o f the as-forged high Nb-containing TiAl alloy 
is shown after heat treatment for 48 h at 1250 °C. Samples for SEM-EBSD observations and thin 
foils for TEM observations after torsion and pre-torsion tension tests were cut perpendicular to the 
tensile loading axis from the gauge length by elect ron discharge machining. The accumulated 
EBSD data were post-treated with Channel 5 TM software. TEM investigations were performed on 
a Tecnai G 2 F30 instrument operated at 200 kV . The hardness along the radial direction was tested 
using an MH-6 microhardness machine with an indentation load of 300 g and dwelling time of 10 
s. Twelve independent measurements were performed at each tested position (error bars: standard

3. Results and discussion 
3.1 Tensile properties of high-Nb-containing TiAl alloy 
Two steps were used to forge the high-Nb-containing TiAl alloy with a nominal composition of 
Ti-45Al-8.5Nb-0.2W-0.2B-0.02Y (at. %), which was se lected in the present study. A 
microstructure consisting of α
2/γ lamellae, γ grains, and the B2 ( ωo) phase was obtained. For a 
nominal torsion of 180° and 360°, the tests were interrupted, in which case the sample did not fail. 
The twin volume fractions and recrystallization fra ctions were calculated as the average value, 
from five of the EBSD maps for each position, as shown in Figs. 4a and 4b. The results of the twin 
volume fraction showed a gradient density of the mechanical twins along the radial direction from 
the rim to the center of the samples after torsion and pre-torsional tension. Fig. 4c shows the 
variation of hardness in the radial direction. Firstly, at the center region ( r/R = 0), the twin volume 
fraction was almost the same for the sample of tors ion 180° as compared to the as-forged sample, 
whereas it increased after a torsional deformation of 360°. This result is consistent with the results 
for recrystallization fraction and hardness. At r/R = 0.5 and 1, a larger deformation resulted in a

Hence, the twin volume fractions, recrystallization fractions, and hardness in a sample of torsion

was analyzed to evaluate the evolution of lattice d efects, i.e., dislocations and/or twins, and/or the 
reduction of the scattering size of the phase, afte r torsional and pre-torsional tensile deformation, 
which resulted from inhomogeneous deformation. With regards to the ω
o phase, which is 
nano-sized in the investigated high-Nb-containing T iAl alloy, it has been reported by Williams et 
al. [42] that dislocation loops were formed around some of the ωo phases with clear co-existence 
of ωo phase areas, indicating inhomogeneous deformation of the ωo phase after the tension 
deformation at room temperature. Moreover, the grai n size can also cause peak broadening, and 
hence, the increase of FWHM of {202} ωo was partly caused by its nano-size. The FWHM of

3.4 Fracture behavior after torsional and pre-torsional tensile deformation 
Fig. 9 shows the fracture surfaces of the failed to rsional sample deformed at the angle of 1505° 
[28]. There are two different regions, i.e. part A and B; part A is the region at the rim of the 
torsional sample, where the morphology of the fract ure surface is too indistinct to identify the 
fracture propagation due to the large shear strain in this region. However, stretched pores can 
easily be observed, indicating that the fracture me chanism in this region was a microvoid 
coalescence fracture mode; part B is the center reg ion of the sample, which presented a cleavage 
fracture mode. This torsional fracture behavior rea sonably affected the pre-torsional tensile 
behavior. As shown in Fig. 10, the as-forged high-N b-containing TiAl alloy tensile-deformed at 
850 °C showed a ductile fracture with a large numbe r of pores, whereas the tensile sample with a 
pre-torsion of 360° exhibited a mixture fracture mode combining pores and cleavage fracture. The 
pre-torsional tensile behavior mainly resulted from the microstructure of the sample after torsion. 
In Fig. 11, the microstructures after torsional def ormation of 360° and pre-torsional tensile 
deformation of 360° at the position r/R = 0.5 are s hown. Pores were formed at the γ and/or the B2 
(ωo) grain boundaries during torsional deformation. Th erefore, during the tensile deformation, the 
formation of pores continued, and the existing pore s were stretched and combined to form cracks 
preferentially along the γ and/or the B2 ( ωo) grain boundaries, which resulted in the mixture 
fracture mode.

The high-temperature tensile property of high-Nb-co ntaining TiAl alloy was successfully 
enhanced via a gradient twin structure. The strength and ductility of the as-forged TiAl alloy were 
494 MPa and 4.6%, respectively, which were enhanced to 557 MPa and 28%, respectively, by a 
pre-torsion of 180°, and to 592 MPa and 47.0%, respectively, by a pre-torsion of 360°. The tensile 
strength and ductility are simultaneously enhanced by the pre-torsional deformation. The high 
strength is mainly attributed to the twin structure formed during torsion, while high ductility 
correlates to the recrystallization of γ the phase at the twin-twin sections and the load p artitioning 
regulated by a hierarchical microstructure. 
The correlation between the micro-mechanical behavi or and the macro-mechanical properties of 
high Nb-containing TiAl alloy was studied using HE-XRD, in combination with EBSD and TEM. 
The γ phase and ω
o phase were subjected to tensile micro-strains alon g LD after the pre-torsional 
and tensile deformation, while the α2 phase experienced compressive micro-strains along the LD 
after torsion and tensile micro-strains along LD af ter torsion. Besides, when the tensile 
micro-strains act along both the LD and TD in the γ phase of the pre-torsional sample, the 
properties of the TiAl alloy can be improved further.
