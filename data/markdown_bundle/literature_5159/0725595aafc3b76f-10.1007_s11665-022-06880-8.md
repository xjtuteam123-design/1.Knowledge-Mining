TECHNICAL ARTICLE
Effects of Annealing Treatment on Microstructure
and Tensile Properties of Ti-48Al-2Cr-2Nb-5Ta Alloy
by Laser Additive Manufacturing
Zhan-qi Liu, Gui-li Yin, and Yan Zhang
Submitted: 8 October 2021 / Revised: 6 February 2022 / Accepted: 4 March 2022
Effects of annealing treatment on the microstructure, phase composition, texture, and tensile properties of
Ti-48Al-2Cr-2Nb-5Ta alloy prepared by laser additive manufacturing were studied systematically. Results
show that the microstructure of both as-deposited and annealed alloys is composed of a2(Ti3Al), c(TiAl),
and b/B2 (b ordered to B2) phases. With the increase in annealing temperature, the microstructure changes
signiﬁcantly, and the content of b/B2 phase in lamellar clusters decreases gradually. However, the content of
b/B2 phase between lamellar clusters increases, and its size increases. Compared with the as-deposited alloy,
the content of b/B2 phase in the annealed alloy increases signiﬁcantly. With the increase in annealing
temperature, the c(111) orientation texture strength of the annealing alloy decreases gradually. The results
of tensile properties show that the tensile strength of as-deposited alloy is higher than that of annealed alloy
at room temperature and high temperature. However, the elongation of Ti-48Al-2Cr-2Nb-5Ta alloy sample
annealed at 1260 /C176C/1h/furnace cooling (FC) secondary cycle is the highest at room temperature and 750
/C176C, and its values are 2.2 and 3.4%, respectively.
Keywords annealing treatment, laser additive manufacturing
(LAM), tensile properties, c-TiAl alloy, b/B2 phase
1. Introduction
Ti-Al alloys, usually c-TiAl alloy, are a kind of lightweight
structural materials suitable for aerospace. They are more
widely used in high temperature, mainly due to their low
density, high speciﬁc strength, and good oxidation resistance.
(Ref 1-4). Recently, c-TiAl alloy has been successfully used in
commercial aircraft engines as low-pressure turbine blades and
is expected to be a potential substitute for nickel-based
superalloys at temperatures between 600–800 /C176 C (Ref 5-7).
Although c-TiAl alloy has excellent properties, its poor
ductility at room temperature and hot deformation at high
temperature makes it difﬁcult for traditional manufacturing
technology to manufacture structures with complex geometry,
which limits its further practical application (Ref 8). Compared
with the traditional casting technology, the microstructure of
TiAl alloy prepared by LAD has smaller size, higher mechan-
ical properties, and can be used to prepare parts with more
complex shapes.
This material is mainly used in aerospace engine blades. The
purpose of adding Ta element is to improve the high-
temperature tensile properties of TiAl alloy. Generally speak-
ing, annealing treatment is considered as an important post-
treatment technology to adjust the microstructure, texture, and
mechanical properties of c-TiAl alloy (Ref 9-11). Schwaighofer
et al. (Ref 12) carried out various annealing treatments on c-
TiAl intermetallics and obtained the ﬁnal microstructure with
balanced mechanical properties, which has sufﬁcient tensile
ductility at room temperature and high creep strength at high
temperature. Rottinghaus et al. (Ref 13) carried out several
groups of annealing treatments on Ti-43.5Al-4Nb-1Mo-0.1B
alloy made by laser hardening, and systematically studied the
effects of annealing temperature, holding time, and cooling
mode on the microstructure. Liu et al. (Ref 14) carried out
directional annealing treatment of Ti-44Al alloy for 1, 4, and 6
times at 1750K to obtain directional structure and study its
microstructure evolution. The results show that with the
increase in the number of cycles, the grain size of the annealed
Ti-44Al alloy increases obviously, the grain width increases,
and the number of grains decreases.
Chen et al. (Ref 15) used cyclic directional annealing to
improve the microstructure and mechanical properties of Ti-
44Al-6Nb-1Cr directionally solidiﬁed alloy. The results show
that with the increase in the number of cyclic directional heat
treatment (DHT), the thickness of lamellar phase increases, and
the mechanical properties decrease gradually. The best tensile
strength is 636MPa and the elongation is 2.28%. Therefore,
annealing treatment plays an important role in improving
microstructure and mechanical properties.
In order to obtain a suitable microstructure and mechanical
properties, the effect of annealing on microstructure and tensile
properties was studied systematically. In this study, Ti-48Al-
2Cr-2Nb-5Ta (at.%) alloy was prepared by LAM technology.

3.4 Mechanical Properties of As-deposited and Annealed
Samples
Fig. 14 shows the room temperature and high-temperature
tensile properties of as-deposited and different annealed
samples. Compared with Ti48Al2Cr2Nb alloy (408MPa,
313MPa, 283MPa) (Ref 19), the tensile properties of
Ti48Al2Cr2Nb-5Ta alloy are obviously improved at room
temperature, 750 /C176 C, and 850 /C176 C. Obviously, the annealing
temperature and cyclic treatment have great inﬂuence on the
tensile properties of the alloy at room temperature, 750 /C176 C, and
850/C176 C. The original lamellar structure of the alloy changes
obviously after annealing at different temperatures, including
the size of lamellar clusters, the precipitation and coarsening of
equiaxed c crystals at grain boundaries, and the precipitation
and coarsening of massive b/B2 phase. Therefore, annealing
and cyclic treatment will have a great impact on the mechanical
properties of the alloy, and its mechanical properties need to be
further studied.

2Nb-5Ta alloy at room temperature are shown in Fig. 14(a). It
can be seen from the curve that the tensile curves do not yield

of the alloy is not signiﬁcantly improved after annealing.

48Al-2Cr-2Nb-5Ta alloy at 750 /C176 C show a certain yield
phenomenon before fracture, indicating that the alloys have

can be seen from the results that the tensile strength of the as-
deposited alloy is the highest at 750 /C176 C, and the tensile strength
of the annealed alloy at 1320 /C176 C/2h/FC is the lowest, which is
469MPa. Compared with the alloy annealed at 1260 /C176 C/2h/FC,
the tensile strength of the alloy annealed at 1260 /C176 C/1h/FC
decreased from 559MPa to 525MPa, and the elongation
increased from 2.8 to 3.4%. This is mainly due to the increase
in the number of equiaxed grains in the alloy after cyclic
treatment, compared with the lamellar structure. Dislocations
are easier to slip in equiaxed grains, so the tensile strength of
the alloy decreases slightly and the elongation increases
obviously.
The tensile strength values of as-deposited alloy and
different annealed alloys at 850 /C176 C are 538, 486, 482, 448,
438, 455 and 437MPa, respectively, and their elongations are
13.0, 14.0, 7.8, 4.7, 5.3, 11.5, and 3.5%, respectively
(Fig. 14(e)). It can be seen from the experimental results that
with the increase of annealing temperature, the tensile strength
of the alloy decreases gradually, the elongation increases
obviously, and the maximum value reaches 14.0%. Moreover,
the change of tensile strength has a good gradual downward
trend. This is mainly due to the weakening of the brittleness
effect of b/B2 phase on the mechanical properties of the alloy
during tension at 850 /C176 C (Ref 20).

in a serious decrease in its strength and plasticity. In summary,

4. Conclusions
In this paper, the effects of annealing treatment on the
microstructure and tensile properties of Ti-48Al-2Cr-2Nb-5Ta
alloy at room temperature and high temperature were studied
systematically. The experimental conclusions are as follows:
(1) The phase composition in the annealed Ti-48Al-2Cr-
2Nb-5Ta alloy remains unchanged, which is composed
of c, a
2, and b/B2 phases. With the increase in anneal-
ing temperature, the content of b/B2 phase distribution
in lamellar clusters decreases sharply. However, the con-
tent and size of b/B2 phase distribution at the grain
boundaries between lamellar clusters increase sharply. In
addition, the size of equiaxed c grains precipitated at
the grain boundary grows slightly.
(2) Compared with the as-deposited alloys, the tensile
strength of the annealed alloys decreased to varying
degrees, and the elongation of most of the annealed al-
loys also decreased. However, under the condition of
secondary annealing at 1260 /C176 C/1h/FC, the elongation of
the alloy increased by 1.2 and 1.5 times at room temper-
ature and 750 /C176 C, respectively.
