abstract
Insight in phase equilibria is very valuable for the design of Co-based superalloys that have the potential
of 50 /C14 C higher temperature capability than Ni-based superalloys. The Co-rich part of the Co-Al-X (X ¼ W,
Mo, Nb, Ni, Ta) ternary systems is especially important and was thus investigated using two sets of
diffusion multiples (Ni-Co-CoAl-Cr-Mo-Nb-Ta-W and Ni-Co-NiAl-Si-Zr). Isothermal sections of the Co-Al-
W and Co-Al-Nb systems at 900
/C14 C as well as the Co-Al-Mo, Co-Al-Ni and Co-Al-Ta systems at both 900 /C14 C
and 800 /C14 C were constructed from the results of scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and electron probe
microanalysis (EPMA). The data can be used as experimental input to future thermodynamic assessments
of the Co-Al-X ternary systems in order to improve the relevant thermodynamic databases for the design
and development of Co-based superalloys.

1. Introduction
Nickel-based superalloys have been widely used as critical
structural components in aircraft, marine, and industrial gas tur-
bines as well as other challenging environments because of their
exceptional combination of properties such as high temperature
strength, toughness, and resistance to mechanical and chemical
degradation at elevated temperatures [1,2]. The L1 2 structured g0-
Ni3Al phase precipitated from the disordered g-fcc matrix is the
major contributor to the excellent high-temperature mechanical
performance of Ni-based superalloys. Traditional Co-based alloys
have their inherent advantages such as higher melting point and
superior hot corrosion resistance and thermal fatigue resistance [3],
but they have relatively limited strength at very high temperatures
due to the lack of comparable
g0 precipitation strengthening,
relying on only less effective solid solution and carbide precipitate
strengthening.
Sato et al. [4] discovered in 2006 that a ternary g0-Co3(Al,W)
compound with a L12 structure in the Co-rich corner of the Co-Al-W
ternary system is stable at elevated temperatures; and they
observed a coherent g/g0 microstructure analogous to the Ni-based
systems. Their observations promoted worldwide research on the
potential of Co-based superalloys as a replacement of Ni-based
superalloys for even higher temperature capabilities.
Previous studies on the thermodynamic phase equilibria of the
Co-Al-W-based systems suggest that the solvus temperature of the
g0-Co3(Al,W) phase is still low (below 1000 /C14 C) [4e6]. The g0 solvus
temperature increases with higher Al and W contents [7e9]. Sub-
sequently, the partitioning behavior of additional alloying elements
between the g and the g0 phases and the alloying effects on the
solvus temperature and the g0 phase volume fraction in the Co-Al-
W-based alloys have been investigated both experimentally and
computationally. The elements Mo, Nb and Ta preferentially
partition to the g0 phase, which signi ﬁcantly increases the solvus
temperature as well as the volume fraction of the g0 phase
[7,8,10e18]. Shinagawa et al. [19,20] found that the g0 phase

More insight in phase equilibria in the Co-Al-X (X ¼ W, Mo, Nb,
Ni, Ta) ternary systems, especially the Co-rich part, will be needed
and very valuable to better understand the effects of additional
alloying elements on the phase stability in Co-based superalloys. A
systematic experimental study of the phase equilibria of the Co-Al-
X systems at 900 /C14 C and 800 /C14 C was thus carried out to collect more
experimental data as input to future thermodynamic assessments
of the Co-Al-X ternary systems. Such assessments will improve the
reliability of thermodynamic databases for the design of advanced
Co-based superalloys.
2. Experimental method
A diffusion multiple [39e42] is an assembly of several pieces of
metals or alloys with interfacial contact in a designed geometry to
contain several diffusion couples and triples in one sample. The
basis of the diffusion-multiple approach was explained in details
elsewhere [41,42]. After diffusion annealing at an elevated tem-
perature for an extended duration of time, solid solutions and
intermetallic compounds form by interdiffusion. Local equilibria at
the phase interfaces [43], revealed by compositional analysis from
EPMA data, are employed to obtain the tie-line information and
thus to build equilibrium phase diagrams. In the present study, the
diffusion-multiple approach was employed to determine the
isothermal sections of the Co-Al-X (X ¼ W, Mo, Nb, Ni, Ta) ternary
systems at both 900 /C14 C and 800 /C14 C.
Two Co-CoAl-Cr-Mo-Nb-Ni-Ta-W diffusion multiples were
made of the same geometry, as shown schematically in Fig. 1 (a).
The starting pure elements of Co, Al, Cr, Mo, Nb, Ni, Ta and W were
all with a purity of 99.99% (wt.%). The stoichiometric CoAl was
produced by arc-melting in argon atmosphere, and the arc-melting
was repeated for ﬁve times to ensure homogeneity. The ingot was
then encapsulated in an evacuated quartz tube back ﬁlled with 1/5
atmospheric pressure argon, and homogenized at 1200 /C14 C for 48 h
followed by furnace cooling. All parts for the diffusion multiples
were cut by electrical discharge machining (EDM). The dimensions
of the pieces are shown in Fig. 1(a). Each diffusion multiple includes
four CoAl bricks and two Co bricks of dimensions 3 /C2 5 /C2 25 mm,
two Co pieces of dimensions 2 /C2 5 /C2 25 mm, one Cr bar and one Mo
bar of dimensions 1 /C2 5 /C2 25 mm, and one Nb, one Ta, and one W
plate of dimensions 1 /C2 10 /C2 25 mm. A pure Ni cartridge with a
10 /C2 15 mm rectangular opening was cut from a Ni cylinder with a
diameter of 25 mm. After cutting, the surfaces of all the metal
pieces were ground to 1200 grit SiC sand papers to obtain a ﬂat
surface ﬁnish. All of the pieces were ultrasonically cleaned in
alcohol before being placed into the Ni cartridges. Two Ni caps of
3 mm in thickness were then electron beam welded in vacuum onto
the top and bottom of each assembled piece. The samples under-
went a hot isostatic pressing (HIP) run at 950 /C14 C and 310 MPa for 8 h

was employed to analyze the phases that have formed during the
annealing heat treatments. Composition pro ﬁles were obtained
from quantitative EPMA across the phase interfaces on a JEOL JXA-

probe current of 20 nA. The step size of each EPMA line-scan is

highlighted in Fig. 1. Results for the other systems contained in the
diffusion multiples will be reported separately.
3. Results and discussion
During the long-term diffusion annealing, extensive interdiffu-
sion among Co, Al and X (X ¼ W, Mo, Nb, Ni, Ta) took place, and all
equilibrium phases formed by interdiffusion processes. By per-
forming EPMA line-scans across the phase interfaces, many con-
centration pro ﬁles were obtained. The local equilibrium
information (tie-line) was extracted by plotting the composition
proﬁle in both composition-distance and composition-composition
formats. The detailed procedure of such analysis can be referred to
Ref. [42]. All the experimental data on tie-lines and phase bound-
aries obtained from the current study are tabulated in the Sup-
plementary Information.

ternary system that were observed from two tri-junctions in the
same diffusion multiple sample, it is postulated that an invariant
reaction ( g þ m þ Co3W) þ (g þ b þ m) /

at a temperature above but very close to 900 /C14 C, leading to very
similar Gibbs free energies of these equilibria. Any slight change in
local conditions might lead to different equilibria. More detailed
study is necessary in the future to con ﬁrm this postulation. In other

3.2. Isothermal sections of Co-Al-Mo at 900 /C14 C and 800 /C14 C
Existing experimental phase equilibrium information on the Co-
Al-Mo system in the literature is scarce and two isothermal sections
of the ternary phase diagram at 900 /C14 C and 800 /C14 C were established
from this study for the ﬁrst time. Fig. 4 (a) is an SEM BSE image
taken from the Co-CoAl-Mo tri-junction region of the diffusion
multiple shown in Fig. 1(a) that was annealed at 900 /C14 C for 1010 h.
The 900 /C14 C isothermal section obtained from the present study is
shown in Fig. 4 (b).
Phase boundaries of the g, b, m and Co3Mo phases, as well as two
three-phase regions, g þ b þ Co3Mo and b þ m þ Co3Mo can be
clearly deﬁned from the EPMA results. The solubility of Mo in the g
phase is relative high, up to ~8 at.% Mo. The solubility of Al in the
Co3Mo and m phases is quite low, up to ~3 at.% Al and ~6 at.% Al
respectively, predominately replacing Co while the Mo contents
essentially keep constant. Similar results for the 800 /C14 C - 2200 h
sample are shown in Fig. 5. The general phase equilibrium relations
remain similar as those at 900 /C14 C with some slight changes of
solubility due to the 100 /C14 C temperature difference.

4. Summary
Two Ni-Co-CoAl-Cr-Mo-Nb-Ta-W diffusion multiples were
fabricated and annealed at 900 /C14 C and 800 /C14 C for 1010 h and 2200 h,
respectively. The other Co-Ni-NiAl-Si-Zr diffusion multiple
annealed at 900 /C14 C for 580 h was also employed to obtain more data
for the Co-Al-Ni 900 /C14 C isothermal section. EPMA line-scans across
the phase interfaces in both the Co-CoAl-X (X ¼ W, Mo, Nb, Ni, Ta)
and Co-Ni-NiAl tri-junctions in the diffusion multiples provided
local equilibrium information to extract the two-phase equilibrium
tie-lines. A large amount of tie-lines was obtained to establish the
isothermal sections of the Co-rich part of the Co-Al-W and Co-Al-
Nb ternary phase diagrams at 900 /C14 C as well as the Co-Al-Mo, Co-
Al-Ni and Co-Al-Ta systems at both 900 /C14 C and 800 /C14 C. The
isothermal sections of the Co-Al-Mo and Co-Al-Ta systems at both
900 /C14 C and 800 /C14 C as well as the isothermal section of the Co-Al-Ni
system at 800 /C14 C are the ﬁrst such phase diagrams experimentally
established. The isothermal sections of the Co-Al-X systems will be
very valuable for better understanding the alloying effects of W,
Mo, Nb, Ni and Ta in the Co-based superalloys. These experimental
isothermal sections will be essential input to future thermody-
namic assessments of the Co-Al-X (X ¼ W, Mo, Nb, Ni, Ta) ternary
