abstract
As a precipitation-hardening nickel-based superalloy, Inconel 718 has decent corrosion resistance, high
strength at ambient temperature, excellent creep and fatigue strengths at high temperature. In this work,
high performance Inconel 718 alloy was fabricated by cold spray process. The microstructure evolution,
hardness, adhesive strength and tensile strength were investigated systematically on the cold-sprayed
Inconel 718 in as-sprayed and heat-treated conditions. The N
2-processed Inconel 718 samples demon-
strate a decreasing porosity with the increasing gas pressure, whereas the fully dense coating was ob-
tained by using He gas. Consistent with the particle velocity measurement, higher adhesive and tensile
strength were obtained at higher pressure of N
2 propelling gas. Through the heat treatment at 990 /C14C for
4 h, the adhesive strength of N 2 and He-processed Inconel 718 reached as high as 435.4 and 899.4 MPa,
which is comparable with the tensile strength of bulk material. The microstructure and EBSD exami-
nation indicate the disappearance of particle boundaries after the diffusion during heat treatment. Be-
sides, the He-processed Inconel 718 samples show a signi ﬁcantly improved ductility and ultimate
strength comparable and even higher than those of bulk material. The results show that the high-
performance Inconel 718 alloys can be fabricated by CS process for the application of damage repair
and additive manufacturing.

1. Introduction
During the recent decades, various additive manufacturing (AM)
techniques have been developed to fabricate the metallic compo-
nent in net-shape or near net-shape [ 1e3]. Due to the distinctive
advantages over traditional subtractive manufacturing techniques,
AM has been widely applied in medical implants, automobiles,
aircrafts and military [ 1,2,4]. Generally, based on the fabrication
principle, the AM technologies can be classi ﬁed into the categories
of powder-bed fusion and direct deposition. In the powder-bed
based methods, the high-energy sources such as laser or electron
beam are used to selectively melt or sinter the powder bed ac-
cording to the CAD model, the following rapid solidi ﬁcation can
build the designed component. For the moment, the methods like
selective laser melting (SLM) [ 5,6] and electron beam melting
(EBM) [ 7] are mostly applied for the AM of metallic component.
Alternatively, the AM methods based on direct deposition are
attracting increasing attention due to its unique advantages.
As an emerging AM technique, the cold spray (CS) or known as
cold gas dynamic spray (CGDS) has drawn increasing attentions
since its invention in the 1980's at the Institute of Theoretical and
Applied Mechanics in former Soviet Union [ 8,9]. In the CS process,
feedstock particles are injected into a supersonic jet of compressed
gas through a de-Laval nozzle and accelerated to a high velocity
(300e1200 m/s) [10]. Due to low temperature of processing gas, the
particle deposition in CS takes place through the intensive plastic
deformation upon impact in solid state at a temperature well below
the melting point [ 11,12]. Such unique ‘cold’ feature of CS process

can effectively avoid the unwanted oxidation, degradation and
phase change in the as-sprayed coating. Apart from the excellent
electrical, thermal and mechanical properties, CS coating can be
extremely dense with high adhesive and cohesive strength [13e16].
Based on its high fabrication rate and low processing temperature,
CS has been widely recognized as an effective additive
manufacturing technique [ 11,17]. Based on the low temperature
features, CS is suitable for the deposition of temperature-sensitive
materials such as copper [ 18], aluminum [ 19], titanium [ 20] and
also high entropy alloy [ 21]. Besides, the characteristics of CS pro-
cess and the properties of CS deposits make it perfectly suitable as a
dimensional damage repair method [ 22,23]. For example, the key
parts of engine or gas turbine that consists of high-performance
alloys like Ti6Al4V and Inconel may experience serious degrada-
tion and damage owing to the high-temperature cycle, crack for-
mation and oxidation. Thus, the CS can provide an alternative
method for the reparation of such high-performance alloys with
excellent mechanical properties.
As a precipitation-hardening nickel-based superalloy, Inconel
718 possesses decent corrosion resistance, high strength at ambient
temperature, excellent creep and fatigue strengths at high tem-
perature [24e27]. Thus, Inconel 718 alloy has been widely used in
extreme environments such as the gas turbine blades, combustors,
nuclear reactor and aerospace sections [ 26,28]. Furthermore, heat
treatment was frequently conducted on the Inconel 718 samples to
adjust the microstructure and improve the mechanical properties
[29,30]. Recently, many efforts have been made to fabricate the
Inconel 718 alloys by the cold spraying additive manufacturing.
Bagherifard et al. [ 31,32] compared the microstructure character-
istics and mechanical properties of CS Inconel 718 with that of SLM.
The results show that the with high fabrication ef ﬁciency, CS
Inconel 718 demonstrates comparable mechanical properties
including tensile strength and fatigue strength with bulk material
and SLM samples. Wong et al. [ 33] studied the particle impact ve-
locity and post heat-treatment on the microstructure and me-
chanical properties of CS Inconel 718. The results show that an
elongation of 24% can be obtained at higher particle velocity and
heat treated at 1250
/C14C for 1 h. Sun [34] used experiments and ﬁnite
element analysis to study the deposition behavior of Inconel 718
particles onto Inconel 718 substrates with different surface condi-
tions. The results revealed that a polished substrate surface could
provide a higher adhesion strength promoted by adiabatic shear
instability and the formation of material jetting. Singh et al. [ 35]
studied the effects of substrate roughness and spray angle on the
deposition behavior of Inconel 718 coating. Lower surface rough-
ness can lead to the coating defects like cracks and peeling-off,
while the spray angle strongly affects the deposition ef ﬁciency.
Luo et al. [ 36] used the novel in-situ shot peening assisted cold
spray system to deposit fully dense deposits through the in-situ
micro-forging induced by the mixed big-sized stainless-steel par-
ticles. The resultant deposits show largely decreased porosity 0.26%
in as-sprayed conditions, and post heat treatment exhibits a high
tensile strength and ductile manner.
For the moment, there is an increasing trend of studies focusing
on the CS deposition of Inconel 718 alloys. Due to the high hardness
and low deformability of feedstock particle, the fabrication of high-
performance by CS generally requires high-end working parame-
ters. In the present work, Inconel 718 was deposited by cold spray
process with different propelling gases of nitrogen and helium. The
particle velocities were measured experimentally, and the deposi-
tion behavior of single Inconel 718 splats were observed. The ad-
hesive strength of Inconel 718 coatings on Inconel 718 substrate
was obtained using a novel pull-out test. The microstructures,
hardness and tensile strength of CS Inconel 718 deposits were
investigated. Besides, the microstructure and mechanical
properties of CS Inconel 718 after heat treatment were also
investigated.
2. Experimental procedure
2.1. Feedstock powder and cold spray process
A commercial Inconel 718 particle (Plasma Giken Co., Ltd., Japan)
was used as feedstock powder for cold spray process in this work.
As surface morphology is shown in Fig. 1(a), the Inconel 718 par-
ticles exhibit perfect spherical shape. The powder size distribution
was characterized by a laser diffraction particle size analyzer
(Seishin Trading Co., Ltd., Kobe, Japan). As shown in Fig. 1 , the
volume distributions of particle diameter are shown in Fig. 1(b). It
can be seen that the powder diameter ranges from 8 to 50
mm, and
the volume average diameter is about 22 mm. In this study, a
commercial high-pressure CS system of PCS-1000 (Plasma Giken
Co., Ltd., Japan) was used to fabricate the Inconel 718 coatings. An
optimized convergence-divergence nozzle was used to achieve
better particle acceleration, and water-cooling system was
employed to alleviate nozzle clogging. As shown in Table 1 , the
nitrogen gas with pressures of 3, 5 and 7 MPa and temperatures of
600, 800 and 1000 /C14C was used as propelling gas. It is worth noting
that such higher working parameters at 7 MPa and 1000 /C14Cw a s
ﬁrst used in this work to maximize the mechanical properties of CS
Inconel 718 coating. Besides, the propelling gas of helium with a
pressure of 3 MPa and a temperature of 1000 /C14C was also used in
this work. The detailed parametric conditions were given in Table 1.
To further improve the mechanical properties, the as-sprayed
Inconel 718 samples for microstructure observation and mechani-
cal properties test were heat treated at 990 /C14C for 4 h in argon at-
mosphere followed by furnace-cooling. The heat treatment
conditions were determined to achieve a balance between strength
and ductility based on the related studies on CS Inconel 718
[31e33,36]. For the convenience of reference, the as-sprayed
samples are designated as AS, while the heat-treated ones as HT.
2.2. Measurement of in- ﬂight particle velocity and numerical
simulation
The in-ﬂight particle velocity distribution at different deposition
conditions of cols spray process was measured at the center line of
the gas ﬂow by using CSM Evolution system (Tecnar Automation
Ltd., St-Bruno, Qu /C19ebec, Canada) [ 37]. The substrate was removed
during the particle velocity measurement process. Owing to the
low temperature of the particles in CS process, the radiation in-
tensity emitted from the in- ﬂight particles is too weak to be
detected by the optical sensor. Therefore, a high-power diode laser
was equipped in the CSM Evolution system to beam the in- ﬂight
particles. By detecting the monochromatic light scattered by par-
ticles, the velocity of particles can be measured by CSM Evolution
system. In this study, the velocity measurements were taken at a
point of 30 mm away from the nozzle exit. Following the velocity
measurement, the ﬁnite element analysis (FEA) study by the
commercial software ABAQUS with the Lagrangian algorithm and
Dynamic-Explicit procedure was carried out to study the defor-
mation behavior of Inconel 718 particle onto Inconel 718 substrate.
A simpli ﬁed 3D-symmetric model was used to reduce the compu-
tational resource. A particle with a diameter of 22
mm was used
according to the experimental conditions. A Johnson-Cook plas-
ticity model accounting the strain, strain rate hardening as well as
thermal softening was applied to describe the material deformation
under high-velocity impact [ 38]. The contact process between
particle and substrate was implemented by the surface-to-surface
penalty contact algorithm available in ABAQUS. The material

2.3. Microstructure characterization and mechanical properties
To better understand the bonding behavior, single Inconel 718
splats were deposited on Inconel 718 substrates. The morphologies
of the splats were examined by an emission scanning electron
microscope (SEM) (JSM-5200LV, JEOL, Japan). Cross sections of as-
sprayed coatings were prepared vertically to their surfaces by a
conventional mechanical polishing method with SiC papers and
diamond suspensions. The mirror-polished cross-sections were
examined with a digital optical microscope (OM) (VHX-900, Key-
ence, Japan). Electron back scattered diffraction (EBSD) examina-
tion were conducted at electron energy of 25 KeV, and with a step
size of 100 nm using the FEI-Sirion SEM equipped with a TSL-OIM
EBSD camera. EBSD samples were the prepared and polished by
standard metallographic techniques were further processed using
an ion milling system (Hitachi IM4000). The micro-hardness of
coatings was tested using a FM-700 tester (Future-Tech. Crop), at a
load of 300 g and a dwell time of 15 s.
In the conventional adhesion strength test for thermal spray
coatings, an epoxy resin adhesive is commonly employed to glue
the sample with a non-coated rod [ 39]. However, the resultant
adhesion strength by this testing method is tremendously
restricted by the strength of glue, which is usually no more than
70 MPa. Thus, this method is no longer applicable to measure the
high-strength coatings. In this study, a novel test method was
employed to measure the bonding strength of CS Inconel 718
coatings on Inconel 718 substrates. As the schematic diagram is
shown in Fig. 2 (a), a thick coating is ﬁrstly deposited on a cylinder
rod. As shown in Fig. 2 (b), a gauge section is then machined on the
as-deposition sample to facilitate the plastic deformation during
pull-out test. Finally, the adhesion sample was pull-out directly by
the special jig as shown in Fig. 2 (c), and the ﬁnal failure can occur at
the gauge area. Similar with the tensile test, the novel adhesion test
can rule out the in ﬂuence of glue layer. The adhesion test was
conducted on both the as-sprayed and heat-treated CS Inconel 718
samples. Thus, the potential applications of CS as the damage repair
or restoration can be evaluated by the proposed novel adhesion
test.
Besides, bulk Inconel 718 coatings were fabricated by CS tech-
nique using propelling gases of He (3 MPa, 1000
/C14C) and N 2 (5 MPa,
10 0 0/C14C). The tensile samples were then machined according to the
dimensions given in Fig. 2 (d) and the ASTM E8 standard [ 40]. The
tensile properties of as-sprayed and heat-treated coatings were
measured with the tensile testing equipment (AG-X (100 KN),
Shimadzu Co., Ltd. Japan). Two cameras were equipped with the
tensile machine to measure the extension of the specimen within
the gage length during tensile test. The tensile test was conducted
on both as-sprayed and heat-treated Inconel 718 coatings. The
fracture morphologies of tensile samples were observed by using
the SEM to reveal the cohesive bonding and failure mode.
3. Results and discussions
3.1. Particle velocity
The measured particle velocity distributions at N 2 gas pressure
of 5 MPa and various temperatures are shown in Fig. 3 (a). The
velocity distribution was found to behave as a gaussian function,
and the velocity values range from 400 to 1200 m/s. It is obviously
that the particle velocity increases at a higher gas temperature,
which is in good agreement with previous studies [ 10]. Based on
the particle velocity distribution, the average velocity values as a
function of propelling N
2 gas pressure and temperature were
depicted in Fig. 3 (b). It can be found that both the temperature and
pressure have a positive effect on the acceleration behavior of
Inconel 718 particles. The average velocity of Inconel 718 particles
reaches as high as 830 m/s using the N 2 with a pressure of 7 MPa
and a temperature of 1000 /C14C. Besides, by using He as propelling gas
(3 MPa and 1000 /C14C), the average particle velocity prior to impact
reaches as high as 1001 m/s, which is signi ﬁcantly higher than
those of N 2 propelling gas. Due to the smaller molecular weight
than air and nitrogen, helium can reach a higher gas velocity [ 10]. It
has been widely accepted that particle velocity is a key factor to
determine the properties of cold-sprayed coating [ 9,10]. A higher
impact velocity can bene ﬁt the plastic deformation of deposited
particle, improve cohesive and adhesive bonding and eliminate the
interior defects.

3.4. Microstructure observation
From Fig. 8 (a) and (b), the AS Inconel 718 coatings deposited by
N2 (7 MPa, 1000 /C14C) and He (3 MPa, 1000 /C14C) exhibit a typical
dendritic morphology in the interior of the deformed particles,
which are marked by the white arrows. Such retained dendrites can
be attributed to the insuf ﬁcient plastic deformation of the inner
areas of Inconel 718 particles during deposition. At the inter-
particle boundaries, the breaking dendritic structure can be
observed in both cases, where the plastic deformation is more se-
vere. Irregular pores can be observed at the particle boundaries for
both AS Inconel 718 coatings, which were formed due to the
insufﬁcient particle deformation during deposition. Furthermore,
the etched microstructures of Inconel 718 coatings after heat
treatment were given in Fig. 8 (c) and (d). It is obvious that the
particle boundaries and dendrites are dissolved and healed out
after the heat treatment. The signi ﬁcant enhancement in the
diffusion and recrystallization occurring during heat treatment
makes the dendritic microstructure to dissolve and disappear into
the matrix of the deposit. Besides, due to the signi ﬁcant grain
growth during the heat treatment [ 13], the irregular and elongated
pores were transformed to spherical ones, which are marked by the
red arrows in Fig. 8 (c) and (d).
To understand the deformation and orientation of the grain, the
EBSD investigations were made on the CS Inconel 718 coatings, and
the IPF maps are given in Fig. 9 . As the N 2-AS sample is given in
Fig. 9 (b), the inhomogeneous grain size deformation can be clearly
observed in both AS coatings. A large quantity of nanosized grains
can be seen in the deformed particle, while the interior area re-
mains the larger grains with original equiaxed shapes. As for the
He-AS sample, more signi ﬁcant grain re ﬁnement can be found at a
higher particle impact velocity by using He as propelling gas. By
applying the heat treatment at 990 /C14C for 4 h, evident grain growth
can be clearly observed as a result of static recrystallization (see
Fig. 9 (c) and (d)). The ultra- ﬁne grains were replaced by the
equiaxed ones after HT for both N 2 and HT samples. The Kernel
average misorientation (KAM) maps of the CS Inconel 718 coatings
are then given in Fig. 10 to examine the evolution of strain stress
and dislocation density [ 44,45]. As shown in Fig. 10 (a) and (b), the
concentration of high KAM values indicates the high dislocation
density within the deformed grains in the N 2-AS and He-AS Inconel
718 coatings. Such phenomena exhibit the generation of high strain
stress as a result of serious plastic deformation during the high
velocity impact in CS process. As a contrast, the KAM values are
evidently decreased in the HT conditions in Fig. 10 (c) and (d), in-
dicates the decreased dislocation density resulting from grain
growth and static recrystallization [ 46]. Meanwhile, the heat
treatment can further promote the metallurgical bonding through
the induced diffusion.

the eliminated work hardening caused by grain growth and strain

inﬂuence of porosity and interior defects on the microhardness of

3.6. Adhesive strength
As an important mechanical property, adhesive strength can be
used to evaluate the potential of cold spray in the application of
damage repair. However, there's no existing literature reporting the
adhesive strength of CS Inconel 718 coating. In this work, the ad-
hesive test of cold-sprayed Inconel 718 coatings on Inconel 718
substrate is conducted at different deposition conditions, and the
strength variation is shown in Fig. 12. The propelling gas of N 2 was
used at a temperature of 1000 /C14C and pressures of 3, 5 and 7 MPa,
and He was used at 1000 /C14C and 3 MPa. As for the N2-AS Inconel 718
samples, the adhesive strength increases from 73.1 to 132.6 MPa as
the working gas pressure rises from 3 to 7 MPa. According to the
single splat morphology (see Fig. 4 ), the enhanced plastic defor-
mation of both particle and substrate at higher particle impact
velocity can result in enhanced mechanical interlocking and better
adhesive bonding with the substrate. At the same time, the serve
plastic deformation can also lead to a better cleaning effect on the
original oxide ﬁlm on impact particle at higher propelling gas
pressure. Higher fraction of fresh metal exposure can promote the
formation of metallurgical bonding between coating/substrate

4. Conclusions
As an emerging additive manufacturing method, cold spray
technique has been widely applied in the near net-shape fabrica-
tion and repair of damaged components. In this study, high-
performance Inconel 718 coatings were prepared with high-
pressure cold spray system by using different propelling gas types
of N2 and He. In- ﬂight particle velocity of Inconel 718 particles was
measured experimentally. The microstructures, adhesive and ten-
sile strength of Inconel 718 coating were investigated systemati-
cally. The conclusions can be drawn as following.
The results revealed that the in- ﬂight particle velocity increased
with the increase of working gas temperature or pressure. The
propelling gas of He can result in a higher particle acceleration
effect. Single splat observation shows that higher particle velocity
can generate more deformation with the substrates and particles,
consequently, particles embedded into the substrates more deeply.
Porosity value of N 2-processed Inconel 718 coatings was gradually
reduced at a higher propelling gas pressure. However, the porosity
of 1.84% was still remained in the CS Inconel 718 coating even at a
N2 pressure of 7 MPa. With the He as propelling gas, almost fully
dense Inconel 718 was fabricated with a porosity of 0.21%.
The novel adhesion test shows that the higher strength value
was obtained at higher propelling gas pressure. The adhesive
strength of N 2-processed Inconel 718 can reach 132.6 MPa at the
gas pressure of 7 MPa, and then was further improved to 582 MPa
after heat treatment at 990 /C14C for 4 h. With He as propelling gas,
high adhesive strength of 435.4 MPa was obtained at as-sprayed
condition, which was increased to 899.4 MPa after heat treat-
ment. The CS-fabricated Inconel 718 show the potential as an
effective method to repair or restore defective parts due to great
improvement with the bonding strength.
Both the as-sprayed Inconel 718 coatings deposited by N 2 and
He exhibit typical brittle behavior in tensile test, with elongation
value below 0.6%. He-AS Inconel 718 coatings show much higher
UTS value than that of N 2 due to the extensive plastic deformation,
enhanced inter-particle bonding and lower porosity. After heat
treatment, signi ﬁcant improvements were achieved for the UTS,
elongation and Young's Modulus of CS Inconel 718 coatings. The
fracture morphology shows that extensive dimple features were
formed after heat treatment as a sign of metallurgical bonding. As a
summary, the present study presents the application of CS in the
additive manufacturing of high-quality Inconel 718 component. In
the future work, the post treatments such as HIP and heat treat-
ment will be carried out to further improve the mechanical prop-
erties of CS-fabricated Inconel 718 coatings.
