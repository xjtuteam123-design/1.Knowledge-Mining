A cost-effective eutectic high entropy alloy with an
excellent strength eductility combination designed
by VEC criterion

abstract
Due to the excellent combination of strength and ductility, eutectic high entropy alloys
(EHEAs) are of great interests to the researchers. Here, a cobalt free cost-effective eutectic
high entropy alloy was designed by VEC criterion. The cobalt element in AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 alloy
is replaced by Fe and Ni in equal proportions to maintain VEC value unchanged. The
designed AlCrFe 1.5Ni2.6 alloy exhibited a eutectic microstructure composed of FCC (L1 2)
phase and BCC (B2) phase. The room temperature mechanical properties of the designed
alloy were comparable to those of AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 EHEA and better than those of a majority
of as-cast high entropy alloys. An excellent combination of strength and ductility origi-
nates from the hetero-interface strengthening and slip continuity across the Kurdjumov-
Sachs (K eS) interface. The present work proves the VEC criterion could successfully be
used to design the cost-effective eutectic high entropy alloy and the designed alloy has a
great potential in engineering applications.

1. Introduction
Owing to the appealing mechanical and physical properties,
such as the excellent strength eductility combination [ 1e5],
outstanding yield strength at elevated temperatures [ 6e9],
high corrosion resistance and wear resistance [ 10e14] and
excellent oxidation resistance [ 15e17], high entropy alloys
(HEAs) have attracted widespread attentions from materials
researchers. Due to the high conﬁguration entropy in HEAs to
stabilize solid solutions, the most reported HEAs are in the
form of a single-phase solid solution, e.g., single-phase face-
centered cubic (FCC) HEAs and single-phase body-centered
cubic (BCC) HEAs. Generally speaking, the FCC HEAs have high
ductility but poor strength, while the BCC HEAs have enough
strength but limited ductility. Simultaneously, most HEAs,
especially at a large scale (kilogram scale), do not possess good
castability and exhibit considerable elemental segregation in

[1,2,18]. To address the above problems of HEAs in engineering
applications, the design concept of eutectic high entropy al-
loys (EHEAs) was proposed by Lu et al. [ 1] in 2014. EHEAs refer
to use the eutectic alloy concept to design HEAs with the
composite structure, aiming at good castability of eutectic
alloys and resolving the strength and ductility trade-off
dilemma. AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 as the ﬁrst reported EHEA, possesses
excellent mechanical properties in a wide temperature range
and great seawater corrosion resistance, which could be
candidate material for propeller blades [ 1,2].
Though AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 EHEA exhibits excellent compre-
hensive performances, but the high content of Co element in
alloy limits its industrial applications to some extent. Using
traditional trial-and-error method to obtain Co free EHEA is
time and ﬁnancial resources consuming process, so it is
necessary to use empirical parameters to assist in the design
of eutectic high-entropy alloys. In the past years, several
physical parameters like mixing enthalpy ( DH
mix), mixing
entropy ( DSmix), atomic size difference ( d), U (deﬁned as
TmDSmix/|DHmix|) and valance electron concentration (VEC)
were adopted to predict the phase constituents of HEAs
[19,20]. Among all the physical parameters, VEC has been
proved to be an effective criterion to predict and control the
constituent phases in HEAs [ 20e26]. VEC ¼ P

for a pure element. Generally, FCC phases are stable at higher
VEC ( >8) and instead BCC phases are stable at lower VEC
(<6.87), if 6.87 <VEC<8, alloys are prone to form the FCC þBCC
dual-phase structure [20]. Through analyzing the VEC features
of intermetallic alloys, He et al. [ 21] proposed to use an overall

the microstructure of the EHEAs and control a good combi-
nation of strength and ductility for EHEAs. Liang et al. [ 23]
combined VEC and average d-orbital energy level (M

rameters together to design corrosion-resistant non-equal-
molar CoCrFeMoNi HEAs with a single phase and the corro-
sion resistance of designed alloys was competitive to that of
Hastelloy C276. Yang et al. [24] revisited the empirical VEC rule
with the high-throughput CALPHAD approach in the
AleCoeCreFeeNi system, and the investigation showed that
more than 90% compositions have BCC structures when 5.7
<VEC<7.2 and 100% FCC structures are observed when
VEC>8.4. Vikram et al. [ 25] developed a new cobalt base nano-
lamellar eutectic high entropy alloy based on VEC and the
newly developed AlCrFeNiCo
1.9 alloy showed noteworthy
mechanical properties both at room temperature and high
temperature, indicating that controlling the VEC value of alloy
to get eutectic microstructure is feasible. Wu et al. [ 26] un-
covered the eutectic formation in the Al eCoeCreFeeNi HEA
systems by data mining with machine learning (ML), and
found that Al is the critical element for the eutectic formation
and Cr is the strongly associated element with Al, while Fe, Co,
Ni can be considered as a whole. Furthermore, when the Al
and Cr contents are ﬁxed, the FCC fraction will change with
the changes of the average VEC of Fe, Co and Ni. In other
words, the phase fractions in the Al eCoeCreFeeNi HEA will
be unchanged theoretically if Al, Cr contents and the average
VEC of Fe, Co, Ni are ﬁxed.
The VEC values of Fe, Co, Ni elements are 8, 9 and 10,
respectively. Undoubtedly, the VEC value of alloy will remain
unchanged if the cobalt element in AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 are replaced
by Fe and Ni in equal proportions, further, it could be antici-
pated that the cobalt free AlCrFe
1.5Ni2.6 HEA will have similar
eutectic microstructure like AlCoCrFeNi 2.1 EHEA. Therefore,
the microstructure, mechanical properties and deformation
mechanism of cobalt free AlCrFe
1.5Ni2.6 EHEA were studied
thoroughly in this work and the effectiveness of VEC criterion
to design EHEA was tested.
2. Materials and methods
A large scale ingot (~6 kg) of the AlCrFe 1.5Ni2.6 alloy was pre-
pared by vacuum induction melting and the purity of the
alloying elements was above 99.5%. The ingot was re-melted
twice to ensure the chemical homogeneity. The phase con-
stituent of the as-cast AlCrFe
1.5Ni2.6 alloy was characterized by
D/MAX-2500PC X-ray diffractometer (XRD) with a Cu target
operated at 45 kV and 200 mA. The scanning electron micro-
scope (SEM, TESCAN MIRA) equipped with an electron back-
scatter diffraction (EBSD) system of Oxford Instruments was
used to investigate the microstructure, fractography, phases
distribution and interface orientation relationship (OR) of
alloy. The samples for EBSD investigations were prepared by
mechanical polishing followed by vibration polishing. The
phase compositions were determined by electron probe micro
analyzer (EPMA, SHIMADZU EPMA-1610). High magniﬁcation
microstructure was observed by transmission electron mi-
croscopy (TEM Talos F200C). The TEM samples were electro-
polished in a solution of 10% perchloric acid and 90%
ethanol at /C0 24
/C14 C using a twin-jet polishing system and then
thinned on a GATAN Model 695 machine. The dog-bone sha-
ped tensile specimens with a gauge length of 10 mm and
cross-section dimension of 3 mm /C2 1.2 mm were taking from
casting ingot by electric discharged machining. Prior to tensile
tests, the specimens were grinded with SiC sandpaper up to
2000 grit. Tensile tests were performed using Instron 8801
testing machine at a strain rate of 10
/C0 3 s/C0 1. Three tensile
specimens were measured to obtain reliable results. To
elucidate the origins of high strength and high ductility in the
as-cast AlCrFe
1.5Ni2.6 alloy, the dislocations of interrupted
tensile specimen at strain level of 5% and fracture specimen
were investigated by Tecnai G2 20 and JEM-2100F TEM.
Furthermore, the OR between adjacent phases of fracture
specimen was also studied by EBSD.

[25,28].

The tensile stress estrain curves of the as-cast AlCrFe 1.5-
Ni2.6 alloy are shown in Fig. 3 (a). The average yield strength,
ultimate strength and elongation are measured to be 522 MPa,
1028 MPa and 18.3%, respectively, corresponding to 550 MPa,
1187 MPa and 16.9% in the true stress estrain curve. The strain
hardening rate against true strain curve of the as-cast alloy is
presented in the inset illustration with three distinct stages. In
the ﬁrst stage, the strain hardening rate drops quickly with
increasing strain. Then, the work hardening rate almost re-
mains unchanged in the second stage, which is prerequisite
for the excellent tensile ductility. Finally, the strain hardening
rate sharply falls until tensile failure in the third stage. A
similar three-stage work hardening behavior described above
is observed in other HEAs [33e36]. The yield strength, ultimate
strength and elongation of AlCoCrFeNi
2.1 alloy are reported to
be about 545 MPa, 1100 MPa and 17%, respectively [ 2].
Compared with the mechanical properties of AlCoCrFeNi 2.1
alloy, AlCrFe 1.5Ni2.6 alloy exhibits relative lower strength and
higher ductility due to the higher volume fraction of FCC (L1 2)
phase. At the same time, Fig. 3 (b) shows ultimate strength -
ultimate elongation map of the reported as-cast EHEAs, it can
be seen that the designed AlCrFe
1.5Ni2.6 alloy possesses more
excellent combination of strength and ductility compared
with the reported as-cast EHEAs, showing a good prospect in
engineering applications.
To reveal the microstructural origins of the excellent
combination of strength and ductility in the as-cast
AlCrFe
1.5Ni2.6 alloy, the dislocations morphology at different
strain level were characterized by TEM. At strain level of 5%, a
number of dislocations are observed in the soft FCC (L1 2)
phase, while few planar dislocations could be observed inside
the hard BCC (B2) phase, as shown in Fig. 4 (a-c). Simulta-
neously, hetero-phase interfaces are strong obstacles of the
slip transfer through the interfaces, thus resulting in the for-
mation of dislocations pile-up at the interfaces (highlighted by
arrow in Fig. 4 ). Due to the different crystal structure, the
critical resolved stress for onset of dislocation slip in two
phases are different. Generally, the soft FCC (L1
2) phase is
more prone to starting plastic deformation through the planar
slip of the 1/2 <110> dislocation on the {111} planes [ 27,28,35],
while the dislocation is hard to nucleate and slip in the BCC
(B2) phase. Attributable to the deformation incompatibilities
between soft and hard phases, strain gradients will exist in
the soft FCC (L1
2) phase near lamellar interfaces and
geometrically necessary dislocations (GNDs) will generate
near the interfaces (highlighted by arrow in Fig. 4 (c)), which
will induce hetero-deformation induced (HDI) hardening
[39,40]. A plenty of investigations have demonstrated that HDI
hardening of heterostructure materials could break off the
strength and ductility trade-off dilemma [ 40].
With the tensile strain increasing to fracture (~18.3%), a large
amount of dislocations are observed in both FCC (L1
2) and BCC
(B2) phases, indicating a fast multiplication of dislocations in
both phases. As shown in Fig. 4(d-f), the dislocation density in
the FCC (L1 2) phase is much higher than that in the BCC (B2)
phases. It can be noticed that two kinds of {111} slip traces are
observed to intersect with each other in the FCC (L1
2) phase,
demonstrating that the secondary slip plane is activated in FCC

4. Conclusions
In summary, a cobalt free cost-effective AlCrFe 1.5Ni2.6 EHEA
was successfully designed by VEC criterion and studied. The
alloy is composed of FCC (L1
2) and BCC (B2) phases and the
volume fraction of two phases are about 72% and 28%,
respectively. The as-cast AlCrFe
1.5Ni2.6 EHEA presents yield
strength of ~522 MPa, ultimate strength of ~1028 MPa and
ultimate elongation of ~18.3% in engineering stress estrain
condition at room temperature, showing an excellent combi-
nation of strength and ductility. The mechanical properties of
the designed alloy are found to be superior to a majority of
reported as-cast EHEAs. The origins of an excellent combina-
tion of strength and ductility in the as-cast AlCrFe
1.5Ni2.6 alloy
can be summarized as follows: On the one hand, the hetero-
interfaces and in-situ precipitations serve as a strong obsta-
cles of dislocations movement, which will induce HDI hard-
ening and promote the strain hardening ability of the alloy,
thus the as-cast AlCrFe
1.5Ni2.6 alloy exhibited high strength;
On the other hand, the special K eS type OR between adjacent
phases favors the slip transmission cross the interfaces,
which will promote the co-deformation ability of the alloy,
therefore the designed alloy showed high ductility. The pre-
sent work proves that the VEC criterion can be used to predict
and control the constituent phases in HEAs and the designed
Co free EHEA shows a good prospect in the engineering
applications.
