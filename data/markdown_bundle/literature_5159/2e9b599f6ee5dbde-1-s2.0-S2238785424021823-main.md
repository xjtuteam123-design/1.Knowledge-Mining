ABSTRACT
The AlN particles reinforced Mg-10Gd-3Y – 1Zn (GWZ) composites were prepared by mixing AlN/Al particles 
using ball milling followed by stirring casting method. The influence of AlN/Al particles on the microstructural 
evolution and high-temperature mechanical properties of GWZ alloys were revealed and discussed. With AlN/Al 
particles added into the as-cast alloys, the Mg
3
RE phase was transformed into lamellar LPSO phase, while the 
content of Al
2
RE and LPSO phase increased. Since the Al
2
RE and AlN were effective nucleation sites for α -Mg, the 
grain size of AlN/Al-GWZ composites was reduced obviously from 126.1 to 39.8 μ m. After homogenization, due 
to the coherent lattice matching relationships between 18R-LPSO phase and Al
2
RE or AlN particles, the 18R- 
LPSO phase nucleated on the Al
2
RE and AlN particles. After ageing treatment, the 2AlN/Al-GWZ composite 
showed the optimum mechanical properties at 250 
◦
C, with the yield strength, ultimate tensile strength and 
elongation of 247.1 MPa, 265.5 MPa and 4.0%, respectively. The strengthening effect was primarily originated 
from the AlN particles, Al
2
RE and LPSO phases in the 2AlN/Al-GWZ composite. Besides, the composites can 
achieve the enhancement of high-temperature strength without the sacrifice of plasticity due to the coordinated 
deformation of AlN particles as well as the refinement of grains.
1. Introduction
Magnesium (Mg) alloy is currently the lightest metal structural ma -
terial with the low density of 1.73 g/cm
3
, which shows broad applica -
tion prospects in automotive lightweight fields [ 1 – 5 ]. Driven by the 
demand for energy conservation and emission reduction, many impor -
tant fields have put forward high requirements for the high-temperature 
strength of Mg alloys, such as engine gearboxes or cylinder heads, which 
are usually used at 120 – 300 
◦
C and 35 – 70 MPa [ 6 ]. However, due to the 
low thermal stability of Mg
17
Al
12 
phase, the heat resistance of 
commercially used AZ (Mg – Al – Zn) alloy is relatively poor. When the 
temperature is higher than 120 
◦
C, the coarsening of Mg
17
Al
12 
phase and 
softening of grain boundary make the strength of AZ series alloys 
decrease seriously [ 7 , 8 ]. Hence, it is necessary to develop the heat 
resistant Mg alloy with excellent high-temperature stability at the 
temperature over 200 
◦
C.
Recently, the strength of Mg alloys at high temperatures is improved 
generally by inhibiting the generation of Mg
17
Al
12 
phase and/or pro -
moting the precipitation of thermally stable phases. Rare earth elements 
(RE) are considered to be particularly effective and important to 
enhance the heat resistance of Mg alloys [ 9 ]. For example, the Mg
24
Y
5
, 
Mg
5
Gd and Mg
3
Gd compounds can be formed by the combination of RE 
and Mg, which have high melting points and can remain stable at 
200-350 
◦
C [ 10 – 13 ]. In addition, Zn element can also combine with Mg, 
Gd and Y atoms to form a thermally stable phase with 
long-period-stacking-ordered (LPSO) structure. It usually has two forms 
of 14H-LPSO and 18R-LPSO phases, which can inhibit the dislocations 
and pin the grain boundary simultaneously. Thus, the strength of Mg 
alloys containing LPSO phase are significantly improved at 200 – 300 
◦
C 
[ 14 – 18 ].

However, it is inadequate to improve the heat resistance simply by 
the second phase strengthening, which tends to easily dynamically 
precipitate or dissolve at elevated temperatures. To further enhance the 
high-temperature strength, researchers added ceramic particles into Mg 
alloys, which would not dynamically precipitate or dissolve in the Mg 
matrix during long-term thermal exposure [ 19 – 21 ]. The commonly used 
ceramic particles, such as TiC, AlN, SiC, TiB
2 
etc. [ 22 – 29 ], have low 
thermal expansion coefficient, high hardness and high melting points. 
Among them, AlN is a promising and effective reinforcing particle to 
fabricate Mg-based composites. With the hexagonal close-packed crystal 
structure similar to Mg, AlN is the heterogeneous nucleation site to 
significantly refine the α -Mg grains [ 30 , 31 ]. Fu et al. added AlN particles 
to Mg – Al alloy and refined the grains from 450 to 120 μ m [ 31 ]. Besides, 
AlN particle can maintain thermal stability with the melting point up to 
2200 
◦
C. Cao et al. added 1 wt% AlN particles into AZ91D alloy by 
stirring casting and the UTS was enhanced from 135 MPa to 170 MPa at 
200 
◦
C [ 19 ].
Although the particle reinforced Mg composites show excellent me -
chanical properties, the key factor limiting its wide application is the 
particle agglomeration. Researchers have developed many techniques to 
avoid the particle clusters. Katsarou et al. added 1 wt% AlN nano -
particles in Mg-Nd-Gd-Zr-Zn alloy by ultrasonic stirring, which 
successfully dispersed particles and increased the creep resistance of the 
composite at 240 
◦
C [ 32 ]. Yang et al. realized the uniform dispersion of 
AlN particles in Elektron21 alloy using the high shearing dispersion 
technique. The heat-resistance of the composites was significantly 
improved at elevated temperature [ 20 , 33 ]. Furthermore, the agglom -
eration of particles can also be reduced by ball milling before casting. 
For example, the ball milling of metal powders (such as Al, Mg) and 
ceramic powder can realize the pre-dispersion of ceramic particles and 
improve their wettability, thereby optimizing the dispersion of particles 
[ 34 ]. Sun et al. mixed TiB
2 
with Mg and Al powders by ball milling to 
obtain the Mg/TiB
2 
and Al/TiB
2 
particles, respectively. They found that 
the particles were successfully dispersed in Mg – 10Li – 3Al alloy by stir -
ring casting [ 35 ]. Chen et al. mixed 10 wt% AlN particles with Al powder 
and prepared Mg – Al/AlN composites. They revealed that the mechani -
cal properties were enhanced with AlN particles [ 36 , 37 ]. Hence, the 
pre-mixture of metal and ceramic particles before casting is a feasible 
way to promote the uniform distribution of particles.
In this work,the ceramic AlN particles and Mg-Gd-Y-Zn alloy were 
selected as the reinforcements and matrix to prepare the composites. 
The AlN and Al powders were pre-mixed by ball milling to achieve the 
optimum distribution of AlN particles. The effect of AlN particles on the 
microstructural evolution was systematically investigated, and the

2. Experimental methods
2.1. Preparation of materials
The matrix alloy is Mg-10Gd-3Y – 1Zn (simplified as GWZ), which was 
melted by pure Mg ingot (99.99%), pure Zn particles (99.99%), Mg-30 
wt% Gd and Mg-30 wt% Y master alloys. All the ingots were purchased 
from Ganzhou Weiran New Material Co., Ltd. The AlN particles were 
used as reinforcements. Before casting, the AlN/Al mixed particles were 
prepared by ball milling of AlN (~5 μ m) and Al particles (~10 μ m), 
which were purchased from Shanghai Xiangtian Nanomaterials Co., Ltd 
( Fig. 1 (a and b)). After mixture, the small AlN particles adhere firmly to 
large Al particles ( Fig. 1 (c and d)), thereby successfully achieving the 
pre-dispersion of AlN particles.
The contents of AlN particles were designed as 0, 1 wt%, 2 wt%, 3 wt 
%, and the content of pure Al powder was 0.8 wt%. Therefore, the mass 
ratio of AlN/Al was 0 : 0.8, 1 : 0.8, 2 : 0.8, 3 : 0.8, respectively. These 
designed alloy and composites are simplified as Al-GWZ, 1AlN/Al-GWZ, 
2AlN/Al-GWZ, 3AlN/Al-GWZ, respectively. During ball milling, the 
ball-to-powder ratio is 10: 1 (wt.%), the rotation speed is 100 rpm and 
the milling time is 240 min ( Fig. 2 (a)). The matrix alloys were preheated 
at 200 
◦
C and added to the crucible at 730 
◦
C with the atmosphere of 99 
vol% CO
2 
+ 1 vol% SF
6 
until alloys were completely melted. When the 
melt temperature was reduced to 630 
◦
C, the mixed AlN/Al particles 
were introduced into the melt and mechanically stirred at the frequently 
variable speeds of 200 – 900 rpm, until the melt temperature was grad -
ually cooled down to the semi-solid temperature (610 – 615 
◦
C) ( Fig. 2
(b)). [ 38 ]. Then, the melt was heated to 650 
◦
C and water-cooled rapidly 
after 30 s of mechanical stirring. Subsequently, all materials were heat 
treated at 510 
◦
C for 10 h with aging treatment at 200 
◦
C for 64 h ( Fig. 2
(c)). Meanwhile, the GWZ and Al-GWZ alloy were fabricated following 
the same process for comparison.
2.2. Microstructure characterizations
The compositions of as-cast ingots were identified by inductively 
coupled plasma optical emission spectrometer (ICP-OES, Optima 8000, 
Table 1 ). The actual compositions of the matrix are close to Mg-10Gd- 
3Y – 1Zn, and the Al element in the composites has an obvious content 
gradient due to a slight burning loss. The metallographic samples were 
etched with picric acid and observed by optical microscopy (OM, Axi -
overt 40 MAT). The grain size was measured by Nano Measurer software 
based on optical micrographs. The identification of phase was analyzed 
by X-ray diffraction (XRD, PANalytical X ’ Pert Powder). The micro -
structure was analyzed through scanning electron microscopy (SEM, 
Tescan Vega 3 LMH) and energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS). The 
TEM samples for aging precipitates observation were made by me -
chanical thinning and ion-beam thinning (Gatan 695). The samples were 
mechanically thinned to 50 μ m and then thinned by ion-beam thinning 
for 5 min at 5 KeV, 4.5 KeV, 4 KeV and 3 KeV, respectively. The TEM 
samples with the size of 5 × 3 μ m for the observation of interface be -
tween AlN and matrix were acquired by focused ion beam (FIB, Thermo 
Scientific Helios 5 UX). All the samples were observed using trans -
mission electron microscopy (TEM, FEI Talos F200X) at 200 kV.
2.3. Mechanical properties test
The hardness of the material was tested by Vickers microhardness 
test machine (HXD-1000TM) with a load of 100 g F and a holding time of 
15 s. The results were calculated based on the average values of at least 6 
repetitive tests to ensure dependability. The tensile properties were 
tested on CMT5305-300 KN tensile machine at 250 
◦
C and the rate of 1 
mm/min. The tensile samples with the dog bone-shape were taken from 
as-cast and aged alloys. The gauge length, thickness and width of 
specimens are 18 mm, 2 mm and 3 mm, respectively. Before testing, all 
samples were pre-heated at 250 
◦
C and kept for 20 min. Each test was 
repeated three times to ensure repeatability.
2.4. Theoretical calculation
The phase diagrams of Mg-10Gd-3Y – 1Zn- x Al alloys ( x = 0 – 3 wt %) 
were calculated using the Pandat software with the PanMg2023 data -
base. The crystallographic data of the Al
2
RE, α -Mg, AlN, 18R-LPSO and 
Mg
5
RE phases were obtained from the Springer Materials website and 
derived from VESTA and Material Studio software. The Gibbs free en -
ergy of phases was calculated by VASP software based on the density 
functional theory [ 39 ]. The projector augmented wave method was used 
with the generalized gradient approximation exchange correlation 
function. The cut-off energy for Al
2
RE, 18R-LPSO and Mg
3
RE/Mg
5
RE 
was 500 ev, 550 ev and 600 ev respectively. The accurate system energy 
was obtained by Gaussian smearing method with the σ of 0.2eV. The 
ground state energy and lattice parameters of the system were obtained 
by fitting the state equation. The Brillouin zone was sampled according 
to the Monkhorst-Pack scheme, and the K-point density of all systems 
was tested to ensure that the system energy error was within 1 
emv/atom.

4.2.3. The plasticity of the composites
Generally, the increase of strength is usually accompanied by the 
sacrifice of plasticity. However, it is worth noting that the addition of 
AlN/Al particles increases the strength without sacrificing the plasticity 
of the composites. The main reason is relevant to the refinement of grain 
size as well as the coordinated deformation of AlN particle. Firstly, the 
grains are obviously refined by the AlN/Al particles in the GWZ alloys. 
The small grains can alleviate the local stress concentration during 
deformation and result in better plasticity before fracture [ 54 ]. Sec -
ondly, although ceramic particles are frequently considered to be 
non-deformable, some dislocations in ceramic particles can still be 
activated at high temperatures. In Fig. 15 (a and b), plenty of dislocations 
are observed inside the AlN particles using TEM, indicating that AlN 
particles can coordinate the plastic deformation at elevated tempera -
tures. Such phenomenon was also reported by other researchers, they 
proposed that AlN ceramic particles showed deformation behaviors 
similar to metals. Guo et al. [ 59 ] conducted compression experiments on 
micron-sized AlN single crystals, and found that the {2-1-12} < 11 – 23 >
and {1 – 100} < 11 – 20 > slip systems were initiated in the AlN by TEM. 
Cheng et al. [ 60 ] also observed several dislocation slips in AlN single 
crystal by nano-indentation, and confirmed that {2-1-12} < 11 – 23 > and 
{1 – 100} < 11 – 20 > are the main slip systems of AlN particles. This 
brittle-to-ductile transition of ceramic is actually induced by the size of 
sample, and the size of AlN ceramics that can exhibit plastic deformation 
is considered to be 2.5 – 5.8 μ m [ 59 ]. In the AlN/Al-GWZ composites, the 
diameters of AlN are about 5 μ m, so it is reasonable for AlN particles to 
exhibit the dislocation slips during the tensile test. Meanwhile, it is also 
found that some dislocations are effectively hindered by AlN particle 
( Fig. 15 (c and d)), demonstrating that AlN particle is in favor of 
obstructing dislocations during deformation. In summary, the AlN par -
ticles can not only increase the strength of GWZ alloys by hindering 
dislocations, but also coordinate deformation by activating their own 
dislocations at high temperature.
5. Conclusions
In this work, the AlN/Al particles were successfully added into the 
GWZ alloys by stir casting with ball-milling. The microstructure of AlN 
reinforced composites was optimized and the mechanical properties at 
250 
◦
C were significantly improved. The primary results are summarized 
as follows:
(1) With the addition of AlN/Al particles to the as-cast alloy, the 
Mg
3
RE phase was gradually transformed into lamellar LPSO 
phase, and the amount of Al
2
RE and LPSO phase increased. Since 
the Al
2
RE and AlN are nucleation sites for α -Mg, the grain size of 
AlN reinforced composite was reduced from 126.1 μ m to 39.8 μ m.
(2) After homogenization, 18R-LPSO phases were formed on the 
Al
2
RE and AlN particles, because Al
2
RE and AlN particles were 
effective nucleation sites for 18R-LPSO phase.
(3) After ageing treatment, the 2AlN/Al-GWZ composite shows the 
optimum mechanical properties at 250 
◦
C, with the YS, UTS and 
EL of 247.1 MPa, 265.5 MPa and 4.0%, respectively. Its 
strengthening effect was primarily originated from the AlN par -
ticles, Al
2
RE and LPSO phases, which can effectively hinder the 
propagation of cracks and avoid premature fracture during 
plastic deformation.
(4) Due to the refinement of grains and the coordinated deformation 
of AlN particles via dislocations, the stress concentration of AlN/ 
Al-GWZ composites can be alleviated during deformation, thus 
avoiding the sacrifice of plasticity.
