ABSTRACT 
In this study, the effects of oxygen addition on the phase formation and mechanical behavior of martensitic Ti- 
Nb-O alloys with relatively low Nb contents were evaluated. The oxygen content did not change the phases 
formed after solution followed by water quenching and samples ’ microstructures were mainly composed of 
orthorhombic α
′′
martensite. The presence of retained β and ω
ath 
phases was identified in alloys with Nb content 
equal or higher than 20 wt%. Hardness and compressive strength increased with oxygen addition, whereas the 
Nb content had a minor influence. The Young ’ s modulus was found to be significantly affected by Nb but not by 
oxygen. Accordingly, it was possible to increase the mechanical strength of the alloys while maintaining their 
elastic moduli at a low level. 
1. Introduction 
Reasonable biocompatibility, high mechanical strength, reduced 
elastic modulus, and good corrosion resistance are features that make Ti 
alloys highly employed in implants for repair and replacement of hard 
tissues in medical and dental practice [1,2] . Despite the current success of 
Ti-based alloys, there is still an intense search for improvement of their 
properties, primarily focused on combining high strength with low 
Young ’ s modulus, which can favor the design of biomedical devices with 
reduced rigidity and thus decreasing bone loss caused by stress shielding 
[3] . The addition of Nb to Ti can be a good strategy targeting reduction of 
the Young ’ s modulus. Such reduction is strongly dependent on Nb con -
centration and formed phases. Martensitic Ti-Nb alloys exhibit a mini -
mum elastic modulus around 15 wt% of Nb that is comparable to the 
minimum values obtained for their β -metastable counterparts [4] . How -
ever, fewer studies are available on martensitic alloys than β -metastable 
alloys [5] , even considering that Nb is expensive and employing lower 
amounts of Nb leads to lower densities and melting temperatures. 
Furthermore, the mechanical performance of Ti alloys is greatly 
affected by the interstitial oxygen content. The improvement of me -
chanical strength with oxygen addition while keeping the Young ’ s 
modulus at a low threshold has already been reported for binary Ti-Nb 
[6] and other Ti-Nb-based alloys [7] . This means that oxygen can in -
crease the strength/Young ’ s modulus ratio, but it comes at expense of 
ductility. Nonetheless, oxygen additions around 0.40 wt% still allowed 
a sufficient plastic deformation [6,7] . Despite these interesting results, 
there is a lack of similar investigations involving martensitic Ti-Nb 
alloys with relatively low Nb contents. Therefore, this study aims to 
evaluate the effects of oxygen content on the phase formation and 
mechanical properties of Ti-Nb-O alloys.

3. Results and discussion 
The ST/WQ alloys exhibited very similar microstructural features, all 
exhibiting typical martensite plates/laths, as shown, for instance, by 
SEM-SE micrographs of the Ti-15Nb-0.15O and Ti-22.5Nb-0.40O sam -
ples ( Fig. 1 a and b, respectively). XRD measurements ( Fig. 1 c and d) 
confirmed that the microstructures consisted predominantly of ortho -
rhombic α
′′
martensite. This means that the transition from hexagonal α ’ 
martensite to α
′′
occurs at a Nb content lower than 15 wt%. In agreement 
with ours results, the transition took place close to 14 wt% of Nb in the 
investigation performed by B ¨onisch et al. [8] . As can be seen in Fig. 1 d, 
peaks associated with retained β phase and consequently with ω
ath 
phase 
could be found in the Ti-22.5Nb-0.40O alloy. The presence of these 
phases was further investigated by TEM ( Fig. 2 ). In these investigations, 
the β and ω
ath 
phases were only found in samples of the Ti-20Nb-xO and 
Ti-22.5Nb-xO alloys. TEM bright-field images and corresponding 
selected area diffraction (SAD) patterns of Ti-20Nb-0.15O and Ti-22.5- 
Nb-0.40O are shown in Fig. 2 a and b, respectively. In both cases, it 
was possible to observe diffraction spots that correspond to the β , ω
ath
, 
and α
′′
phases. The orientation relationship among these phases was 
given by β [011]// α
′′
[001]// ω [11 20]. Furthermore, two crystallo -
graphic variants of the martensitic phase were observed. Although β 
phase was not found in samples with lower Nb contents, it does not 
necessarily mean that no β phase was retained, since XRD measurements 
can fail in detecting phases with very low volume fractions, and TEM 
foils are small so low-fraction phases that are not evenly distributed are 
difficult to be detected. Based on the obtained results, the oxygen con -
tent did not appear to have influenced the type of the phases formed. 
Nonetheless, it could have had some effect on their volumetric fractions. 
Oxygen is known to suppress α
′′
phase by decreasing the martensite 
starting temperature [6] , leading to a higher fraction of retained β . 
However, we could not evaluate this with the current experimental set. 
Fig. 3 a exhibits the obtained Vickers hardness (mean and standard 
deviation) as a function of composition, and Table 1 presents the mul -
tiple comparisons among these means. Statistical analysis showed that 
both the Nb and oxygen contents had a significant effect on hardness, 
but, as can be seen, the effect of oxygen was much more pronounced. 
The results of the compression tests were in good agreement with the 
hardness measurements. Fig. 3 b and c show typical compressive 
stress – strain curves. In Fig. 3 b the curves were grouped according to the 
Nb content, whereas the curves shown in Fig. 3 c were grouped according 
to the oxygen content. It is clearly seen that the compressive strength 
increased with oxygen addition ( Fig. 3 b), whereas small or no relevant 
changes were caused by the amount of Nb ( Fig. 3 c). It is important to 
mention that the yield point could not be unambiguously determined 
because these alloys yield continuously without marking a clear elastic 
to plastic transition, which is a usual behavior observed in martensitic 
Ti-Nb alloys [5] . Although oxygen was able to increase the mechanical 
strength, it also decreased the ductility of these martensitic Ti-Nb alloys. 
Nonetheless, they still exhibited a good plasticity under compression.

Fig. 3 d and Table 1 present the results obtained for the Young ’ s 
modulus. Statistical analysis showed that only the Nb content had a 
significant effect on the elastic constant of the studied alloys. Although no 
significant difference was observed among means of Young ’ s modulus of 
alloys with different oxygens levels and the same Nb content, several 
studies reported that oxygen caused the Young ’ s modulus to slightly in -
crease [6,7,9 – 11] . The alloys with 15 wt% of Nb exhibited the lowest 
elastic moduli, around 59 GPa, which is close to 65 GPa reported by 
Aleixo et al. [12] . As the Nb content increased, the elastic modulus also 
increased up to approximately 79 GPa for the Ti-20Nb-xO alloys, while 
no significant difference was observed among the latter and Ti-22.5Nb- 
xO alloys. The increasing trend in modulus within this compositional 
range was expected, as showed by the results of B ¨o nisch et al. [5] also 
plotted in Fig. 3 d. As Nb concentration increases, more β phase is retained 
after rapid cooling and consequently more ω
ath 
particles are formed, 
raising the Young ’ s modulus. Therefore, the Ti-15Nb alloys are the most 
promising for biomedical applications. Their mechanical strength can be 
increased with oxygen addition due to solid-solution strengthening 
without significantly changing the elastic modulus, which was the 
minimum observed. Fig. 3 e compares these alloys with Ti-6Al-4V and Ti- 
Cp compressed under the same conditions [13] . It is possible to observe 
that their compressive behavior is similar or even superior to these 
commonly employed biomedical metals. 
4. Conclusions 
The microstructures of martensitic Ti-Nb-O alloys were mainly 
composed of orthorhombic α
′′
phase. Retained β and ω
ath 
phases were 
only found in Ti-20Nb and Ti-22.5Nb samples. The oxygen content did 
not affect the type of phases formed. Both the Nb and oxygen contents 
had a significant effect on hardness and compressive strength, but the 
effect of oxygen was much more pronounced; hardness and strength 
increased with oxygen addition, whereas smaller or no relevant changes 
were caused by Nb. The oxygen content was not found to have a sig -
nificant effect on Young ’ s modulus. Therefore, the mechanical strength 
could be increased while the elastic modulus was maintained low, pri -
marily for the Ti-15Nb alloys, which exhibited the lowest elastic moduli 
(around 59 GPa).
