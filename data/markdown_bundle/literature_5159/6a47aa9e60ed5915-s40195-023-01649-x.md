Abstract
The equiatomic TiNbZrTaHf alloy was successfully rolled at room temperature with the reduction of ~ 85%. The microstruc-
ture and tensile properties were investigated after cold working and annealing. It was determined that the recrystallization 
temperature of the TiNbZrTaHf alloy between 850 and 900 °C. Complete recrystallization and normal grain growth occurred, 
the high stability of single phase was maintained after annealing at 1000, 1200, and 1400 °C. But the precipitated phase 
appeared after long term annealing, as seen after 500 h at 1000 °C. After cold working, the tensile strength and the elonga-
tion of TiNbZrTaHf were 1137 MPa and 25.1%, respectively. The annealed alloy has a high tensile strength (σb = 863 MPa) 
and ductility (εe = 28.5%). Moreover, the oxidation of TiNbZrTaHf alloy at elevated temperatures has a significant impact on 
its mechanical properties. The poor oxidation resistance of TiNbZrTaHf can accelerate tensile failure by inducing fractures 
at grain boundaries.
Keywords Refractory high entropy alloy · Cold rolling · Recrystallization · Oxidation resistance · Mechanical properties
1 Introduction
With the accelerated growth of the aerospace and gas tur -
bine industries, refractory high entropy alloys (RHEAs) 
are receiving increased attention due to their exceptional 
strength at temperatures as high as 1600 °C [1 , 2]. The 
design of RHEAs is based on refractory elements (Ti, Zr, 
Hf, V, Nb, Ta, Cr, Mo, and W) and they possess high melt-
ing points and excellent mechanical properties. RHEAs have 
the common characteristics of high entropy alloy, including 
high entropy effect, sluggish diffusion, severe lattice distor-
tion, and cocktail effect [3 , 4]. The high entropy effect can 
enhance the mutual solubility between the constituent ele-
ments of RHEAs and improve the mechanical properties [5, 
6]. In addition, RHEAs are expected to form a simple phase 
with good thermal stability [7 ]. On account of the nature 
of the alloying elements, most RHEAs have a bcc crystal 
structure, which may also contain secondary intermetallic 
phases such as B2 and/or Laves [8, 9].
Usually, RHEAs demonstrate high strength at both room 
and elevated temperatures, but low ductility has limited their 
potential applications [ 2, 10–12]. Among them, the equia -
tomic TiNbZrTaHf alloy with a single-phase bcc structure 
is highly ductile and exhibits excellent high-temperature 
(HT) strength, thereby exhibiting exceptional properties 
[13–17]. Senkov et al. [18, 19] were the first to devise this 
material, which exhibited a fracture stress of 1000 MPa and 
an astounding ductility of over 50% at room temperature 
(RT). To eliminate the effect of solidification segregation 
on the mechanical properties of as-cast TiNbZrTaHf alloy 
[16], homogenization treatment and/or heated working are 
required. Cold rolling and annealing can further modify 
the properties of TiNbZrTaHf alloy [20– 22]. The alloy 
can be cold rolled to greater than 85% reduction, and its 
yield strength in tensile is greater than 1300 MPa [20]. The

Also, previous studies have proved that TiNbZrTaHf

alloy were examined at RT, 800 and 900 °C.
2 Material and Methods
The equiatomic TiNbZrTaHf alloy was prepared by vac-
uum-arc melting in an atmosphere with high purity argon. 
The button ingot was flipped and remelted five times to 
homogenize the chemical composition and microstructure 
of solidification. The dimensions of the TiNbZrTaHf ingot 
are ~ 12 mm in thickness, 70 mm in length and 30 mm in 
width. The as-cast ingot was rolled at RT with a one-time 
thickness reduction of ~ 85% and the ultimate thickness of 
1.8 mm. From the cold-rolled sheet, specimens measur -
ing 5 mm × 8 mm × 1.8 mm were cut and encapsulated in 
quartz tubes for heat treatment in a muffle furnace (STM-20-
17, SAFTherm, China). The heating rate is 15 K/min, and 
annealed at 1000, 1200 and 1400 °C followed by air cooling. 
In situ observation was performed using a high-temperature 
laser scanning confocal microscope. In a high purity argon 
environment, the polished specimen (size: Φ7 × 1.8 mm) was 
heated to 1400 °C from RT at a heating rate of ~ 280 K/min 
and hold at the temperature for 1 h. Microscopy images were 
in-situ obtained per ~ 1 s during the experiment.
The crystal structure was characterized by X-ray diffrac-
tometry (XRD, D8 Advance, Bruker, Germany) with Cu 
Kα radiation, and the 2 /u1D703 range of 20°–90°. The polished 
microstructures were analyzed using a scanning electron 
microscope (SEM, Hitachi SU8010) equipped with back -
scatter-electron (BSE), energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy 
(EDS), and electron-backscatter diffraction (EBSD) detec-
tors, and analyzed using Channel 5 software.
Uniaxial tensile tests were performed for characterizing 
the strength and ductility. From the cold-rolled TiNbZrTaHf 
sheet, electrical discharge machining was used to cut dog-
bone-shaped tensile specimens. The gauge section of tensile 
samples has dimensions of 5 mm in length, 2 mm in width, 
and 1.8 mm in thickness, and the tensile axis coincides with 
the rolling orientation. The tensile mechanical properties 
of the as-cast, cold-rolled and annealed (1200 °C for 1 h) 
alloys were tested at RT, 800 and 900 °C. The tensile tests 
were conducted using a universal testing machine (D series, 
Sinotest, China) at a rate of 5 × 10–3 mm/s, corresponding 
to an initial strain rate of 10–3  s−1. The fracture morphology 
of the tensile alloys was observed under SEM.

3.2.1 Grain Growth at Different Annealing Parameters
The Microstructures and grain diameter distribution histo-
gram for 85% cold-rolled TiNbZrTaHf alloy annealing at 
1000 °C for 1 h, 4 h, 24 h and 500 h are shown in Fig.  3. 
After annealed for 1, 4, and 24 h, fully-recrystallized micro-
structures with a single-phase BCC structure were formed. 
New fine recrystallized grains were formed after annealing 
at 1000 °C for 1 h and the recrystallization process was pro-
moted by the extensive deformation during cold rolling. The 
average grain size grows with annealing time, with recrys-
tallized grains growing from 22 to 152 μm as the annealing 
time increases from 1 to 500 h. The statistics of the boundary 
character in Fig.  3 demonstrate that just below 10% of the 
boundaries are LABs that had not transformed into HABs. 
The total area of low-angle grain boundaries is reduced dur-
ing annealing due to the occurrence of recovery and recrys-
tallization. The TiNbZrTaHf alloy maintained high stabil-
ity at 1000 °C because of its sluggish diffusion refractory 
elements, but the precipitated phase appeared after a long 
time, as seen in the results of 500 h annealing (discussion in 
Sect. 4.3) [29]. Based on XRD and EDS analysis, the pre-
cipitated phase was identified at a hcp structure and enriched 
in Zr and Hf [24].
After 85% cold rolling, inverse pole figures revealed 
〈101〉 parallel to the rolling direction (RD), 〈111〉 and a 
small fraction of 〈114〉 parallel to the normal direction 
(ND) (Fig.  4a). Consequently, the primary texture compo-
nents consisted of the {111} 〈011〉 γ-fiber and the feeble 
{100} 〈011〉 ε-fiber. The texture of 〈101〉 ‖RD decreased 
with the increase of annealing time, as shown in Fig.  4. 
After annealing at 1000 °C, the deformation texture of 
the cold-rolled alloy was weakened owing to recrystal-
lization [20, 21]. The recrystallized fibers were identified 
as 〈111〉 and 〈100〉 paralleling to the normal direction (ND) 
after annealing.
Figure  5 presents the EBSD images of TiNbZrTaHf 
alloy annealing at 1200 and 1400 °C for 1 and 4 h. The 
cold-rolled alloy recrystallized completely to form equi-
axed grains with the average grain diameters were 51 
and 187 μm after annealing at 1200 °C for 1 h and 4 h. 
At 1400 °C annealing, the average grain size is 135 and 
695 μm for 1 h and 4 h, respectively. The growth rate 
of recrystallized grains at 1400 °C is significantly higher 
than that at 1000 and 1200 °C. It can be concluded that 
grain growth is extremely sensitive to annealing tempera -
ture. The TiNbZrTaHf alloy maintained a bcc single-phase 
structure after annealing at 1200 and 1400 °C for 1 h and 
4 h.
3.2.2 In‑Situ Observation of Recrystallization and Grain 
Growth
The recrystallization and grain growth of TiNbZrTaHf 
alloy were in-situ observed using a laser high tempera -
ture confocal microscope during the annealing at 1400 °C. 
Figure  6 shows the in-situ images at 22, 1200, 1300 and 
1400 °C during continuous heating at ~ 280 K/min−1. With 
the increasing of temperature, the surface of the alloy grad-
ually becomes rough due to the action of thermal stress. As 
shown in Fig.  6b, when heating to 1200 °C, small recrys-
tallized grains appeared in local regions, and the average 
grain size is about 11.5 μm. At 1300 °C (Fig.  6c), the 
recrystallization occurred almost at the same time in the 
whole area. Under an enhanced driving for recrystalliza-
tion, the process of recrystallization was very rapid. When 
the temperature reached to 1400 °C (Fig.  6d), the recrys-
tallization was fully completed along with a grain growth. 
The average grain size coarsened to 29.1 μm.
The in-situ observation of annealing for 1 h is dis -
played in Fig.  7. The grain growth was achieved by the 
mechanism of Ostwald ripening and continuous migration 
of grain boundaries [30]. To reduce the energy of grain 
boundaries, grains tended to form equiaxed morphology 
and the boundary angle was 120°. The growth was fast at 
the beginning of recrystallization and then slowed down 
with the extension of holding time. The average grain 
diameter is 211.2 μm after annealing for 1 h at 1400 °C.
As shown in Fig.  8a, when held at 1400 °C for 22 min, 
some dendritic precipitates appeared at grain boundaries 
and extended to the grain interior. Figure  8a–f indicates 
that the precipitates, gradually covered the whole grain. 
With increasing holding time, the needle-like precipi-
tates decompose into the matrix phase again. Compared 
the microstructures after holding at 30 min (Fig.  7e) and 
60 min (Fig.  7f), the precipitates had a pinning effect on 
grain boundaries, which resulted in hindering the migra-
tion of grain boundaries [30]. The composition after 
annealing was detected by EDS, and the distribution of 
elements in the recrystallized alloy was uniform (Fig.  8g). 
The appearance and disappearance of the unstable needle-
like precipitates during in-situ observation did not change 
the chemical composition after annealing, but the reason 
is still unknown.

3.3 Tensile Properties
3.3.1 Room Temperature Tensile Properties
Tensile properties are of critical significance for the applica-
tion in of RHEAs [31]. The RT tensile stress–strain curves 
of the as-cast, cold-rolled and annealed alloys are shown 
in Fig.  9. The mechanical properties are summarized in 
Table  1. For the as-cast condition, the yield stress (σ 0.2) 
and the tensile strength (σ b) were 639 MPa and 668 MPa, 
respectively. Caused by the increase of dislocation density 
and work hardening, cold rolling directly resulted in a con-
siderable increase of σ 0.2 and σ b, which reached 1089 and 
1137 MPa, respectively [32]. Correspondingly, the alloy still 
exhibited well-balanced fracture elongation, which exceeded 
25%.
The σ0.2 after annealing at 1200 °C for 1 h decreased to 
803 MPa and the σ b was 863 MPa, but the elongation (ε e) 
increased to 28.5%, suggesting an excellent tensile plasticity 
at room temperature. The EBSD result in Fig. 5a proved that 
the alloy maintains a single-phase structure and completely 
recrystallizes to equiaxed grains after annealing at 1200 °C 
for 1 h [33]. The crystal defects in the recrystallized alloy 
were eliminated and the dislocation density was significantly 
reduced.
Figure 10 presents the fracture appearance after RT ten-
sile tests. The as-cast, cold-rolled and annealed alloys show 
a mixed transgranular-ductile fracture morphology. The 
fracture surface is consisted of dimples and slip bands, and 
there are amounts of microvoids on the fractured as-cast 
alloy, which might be produced in solidification (Fig.  10a). 
Then the microvoids would be decreased after cold roll -
ing. Figure  10b, c demonstrates that ductile dimples and 
slip bands dominate the fracture. Generally, most RHEAs 
often display low toughness, but TiNbZrTaHf high-entropy 
alloy possesses both high strength and excellent ductility at 
room temperature after cold rolling [2, 34–37]. Some larger 
size dimples appeared after annealing at 1200 °C for 1 h 
(Fig. 10d), indicating an increased plasticity caused by the 
grain refinement. The dimples and slip bands indicate that 
the tensile fracture of TiNbZrTaHf alloy is governed by the 
mechanism of microvoids nucleation and aggregation, and 
dislocation slip mainly contributes to the increase of plastic 
deformation [38].

stored energy that the degree of dynamic recrystallization

4.3 Precipitations at High Temperature
Figure 15 shows the BSE microstructures of TiNbZrTaHf 
alloy after heat treatment at 1000 and 1200 °C for 500 h. The 
composition analysis of the corresponding microstructures 
with different morphologies is listed in Table  2.
The as-cast and cold-rolled alloys were composed of 
a single phase without precipitation. After annealing at 
1000 °C for 500 h, some black precipitates appeared in the 
matrix and at grain boundaries with a block or long strip 
shape (Fig.  15a). Zr and Hf elements are enriched in the 
black precipitates. As investigated by Thermal-Calc mod-
eling and experiment, the equiatomic TiNbZrTaHf favora-
bly decomposes into Nb, Ta-rich and Zr, Hf-rich phases at 
1000 °C [47]. The content of Ti, Nb and Ta in the matrix was 
high, indicating composition segregation at local region after 
annealing at 1000 °C. The phase distribution obtained by 
EBSD shows that the hcp phase is concentrated at the grain 
boundaries. It can be speculated that the precipitated phase 
that appeared after annealing is an hcp phase composed 
of Zr and Hf combined with the XRD results of Fig.  3g. 
According to the phase distribution (Fig. 15c), 6.13% of the 
hcp phase is present, and the precipitates tend to nucleate on 
grain boundaries and disperse heterogeneously in the grain 
interior. Owing to the low diffusion abilities of the refrac-
tory atoms, the bcc solid solution in TiNbZrTaHf alloys is 
stabilized at low temperatures, while at elevated temperature 
induced lattice distortion occurs and the bcc-to-hcp phase 
transformation [48, 49].
The microstructure of TiNbZrTaHf alloy after 1200 °C 
annealing for 500 h has a large number of precipitates. EDS 
analysis reveals that it is an oxygen-rich region. The oxy -
gen diffused very fast at 1200 °C and formed oxide phases 
quickly. The oxidation of the TiNbZrTaHf alloy significantly 
was aggravated by enhancing temperature [50]. Meanwhile, 
amounts of needlelike precipitates, black and gray globu-
lar precipitates were observed inside grains, and some pre-
cipitates with a bunched morphology were formed at grain 
boundaries. The dark area is rich in Ti and O with a few 
other metal elements. The grey precipitates have higher 
oxygen and contains an appreciable amount of Zr, Nb, and 
Hf. The oxide of (Ta, Zr, Hf) O grew from the grain bound-
ary into the matrix and presented a needlelike morphology 
(Fig. 15d). Nb and Ta enriched in the matrix (Region E), and 
the measured composition was close to the grain boundary.
Based on the composition analysis in Fig.  15e, the oxi-
dation products are formed by Ti, Zr, Hf, as proposed in 
[51, 52]. Group IV transition elements (Ti, Zr, Hf) are pref-
erentially oxidized comparing with the group V elements 
(Nb, Ta) due to their relatively low formation energy. The 
X-ray pattern after annealing at 1200 °C for 500 h is shown 
in Fig.  15f. The matrix and precipitates contain a mixture 
of various oxide phases. The formation of mixed oxides

the poor oxidation resistance at high temperatures [50]. The 
cracks fast developed at the grain boundaries because of the 
oxygen enrichment after annealing [54]. The poor oxidation 
resistance of refractory high entropy alloy at HT can pro-
mote crack propagation and accelerate oxidation along grain 
boundaries.

5 Conclusions
A single-phase BCC TiNbZrTaHf refractory high entropy 
alloy was produced by vacuum arc-melting, and successfully 
cold rolled to 85% thickness reduction once, recrystallization 
behavior was studied, the results are summarized as follows:
1. With the increase of annealing time, the texture 
of 〈101〉‖RD decreased. After annealing, the recrystal-
lized fibers were identified as 〈111〉‖ND, 〈100〉‖ND. 
The recrystallization temperature of the TiNbZrTaHf 
alloy was explored to between 850 and 900 °C. Anneal-
ing at 1000 °C for 500 h revealed precipitation of Zr and 
Hf rich particles with an hcp crystal structure which is 
concentrated at the grain boundary.
2. After cold-rolling and annealing, the tensile strength and 
ductility of as-cast alloys are effectively enhanced. The 
dynamic recrystallization of the as-cast and cold-rolled 
alloys occurs at 800 and 900 °C tensile. The oxidation 
of TiNbZrTaHf alloy at HT has a significant effect on its 
elevated temperature mechanical properties.
3. Due to the weak oxidation resistance of TiNbZrTaHf at 
high temperatures, the alloy develops a porous micro-
structure and cracks at the grain boundary. A continuous 
oxide layer was formed after annealing, and the oxygen 
was enriched at grain boundaries. The inferior oxida-
tion resistance of TiNbZrTaHf at HT may be ascribed to 
grain boundaries providing a short-circuit pathway for 
enhanced oxygen diffusion and accelerated oxidation.
