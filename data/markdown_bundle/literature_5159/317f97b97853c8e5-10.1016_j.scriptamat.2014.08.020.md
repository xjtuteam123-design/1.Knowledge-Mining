Development of alloys with high strength at elevated temperatures by tuning
the bimodal microstructure in the Al–Cu–Ni eutectic system
C.S. Tiwary, ⇑ S. Kashyap and K. Chattopadhyay
Department of Materials Engineering, Indian Institute of Science, Bangalore 560012, Karnataka, India
Received 2 June 2014; revised 13 August 2014; accepted 15 August 2014
Available online 2 September 2014
The development of high-strength aluminum alloys that can operate at 250 /C176C and beyond remains a challenge to the materials community. In
this paper we report preliminary development of nanostructural Al–Cu–Ni ternary alloys containing a-Al, binary Al 2Cu and ternary Al 7Cu4Ni
intermetallics. The alloys exhibits fracture strength of /C24 1 GPa with /C24 9% fracture strain at room temperature. At 300 /C176C, the alloy retains the high
strength. The reasons for such signiﬁcant mechanical properties are rationalized by unraveling the roles and response of various microstructural
features.

Future materials for the aerospace and automotive
industries must have a high material index (strength/den-
sity). Structural materials with a high material index will
decrease vehicle weight, hence improving fuel eﬃciency
and reducing cost [1,2]. Among structural alloys, Al-based
alloys have attracted the most attention for lightweight
structural components due to their four times lower density
as compared to steel. However, their yield strengths are
also three times lower than that of steel, hindering their
application as a replacement for steel in room-temperature
applications (<300 /C176C). Hence eﬀorts are being made to
develop Al-based alloys with enhanced strength at room
temperature as well as at elevated temperatures (<400 /C176C)
[3–5]. Most of the reported Al-based state-of-the-art com-
mercial alloys are precipitation or dispersion hardened, giv-
ing rise to maximum strengths of /C24 400 MPa [1–5]. In order
to improve the strength further, composites, and in partic-
ular those having eutectic structures, have been explored
[6–9]. The Al–Si eutectic is one of the most sought after
in situ composites in the domain of aluminum alloys. How-
ever, its strength is limited to /C24 500 MPa at room tempera-
ture, due to the poor interface between Al and Si [10].
Hence, for development of Al-based alloys intended for
high-temperature applications, the focus is on producing
alloys with a high volume fraction of intermetallic rein-
forcement using in situ grown metal–intermetallic compos-
ites. Al–Al
2Cu, Al–Al 3Ni and Al–Al 3Sc are among the
most intensively studied in situ eutectic composites. How-
ever, the structural applications of these alloys are limited
due to their poor room-temperature ductility [11–13].
Non-equilibrium processing provides an opportunity to
produce materials with a bimodal microstructure having a
nanometer length scale containing a high volume fraction
of intermetallic phases. Such alloys can exhibit high
strength and reasonably high ductility at room tempera-
ture. In a recent report it was shown that Al–Cu–Si alloy
with a bimodal distribution of eutectic colonies exhibited
a fracture strength of 1.1 ± 0.1 GPa and a compressive
plastic strain of 11 ± 2% at room temperature [13].
In this paper we report the synthesis, through chilled
copper mold suction casting, of a multiphase alloy having
an ultraﬁne microstructure with a distribution of phases
at diﬀerent length scales in Al-Cu-Ni system. The new ter-
nary Al alloy containing eutectic exhibits exceptional
mechanical properties both at room and elevated tempera-
tures up to 300 /C176C.
To prepare the alloy, nominal amounts of Al (99.9%
pure) Cu (99.9% pure) and Ni (99.9%) elemental metals
to yield the ternary composition of Al–10.5 at.% Cu–1.5
at.% Ni were melted and suction cast under an argon
atmosphere in an arc melting furnace [18,19,21–23].I n
the phase diagram this corresponds to a ternary eutectic

compositions of the phases were obtained by ﬁeld emission
gun (FEG) electron probe microanalysis (EPMA; JEOL
JXA-8530F). Further conﬁrmation of the phases present
was obtained by transmission electron microscopy (TEM;

The compressive properties of 3 mm suction-cast binary
and ternary alloys are further compared as shown in
Figure 2 f. A large fracture strain (9%) could be observed
in the case of ternary alloy as compared to binary alloy
(3%). The large ductility can be related to the diversions
of cracks due to the presence of well-separated hard ternary
intermetallics ( Fig. 2 d). This is absent in binary alloy
(Fig. 2 e). As shown in Figure 2 e, the cracks responsible
for fracture of the binary eutectic initiate and propagate
through colony boundary. In the case of ternary alloy,
the hard ternary phase acts as barrier to crack growth. It
leads to an increase in fracture strain and results in an
improvement in ultimate strength and plasticity.

modulus between binary and ternary alloys. The elastic
modulus value of suction-cast alloy was computed using a
linear multiple regression analysis following Hafez et al.

Figure 3 e,f shows SEM images of fracture samples
tested at 150 and 300 /C176C. The fracture at 150 /C176C is similar
to fracture at room temperature ( Fig. 3 e). The microstruc-
ture not only reveals a colony of eutectic but also estab-
lishes that the crack propagation is hindered by s phase.
Figure 3 f shows fracture at 300 /C176C. The softening of Al
and fracture of intermetallic lamellae can be seen. The
growth of the crack tip is again hindered by high-tempera-
ture stable s intermetallic phase that gives rise to high
strength at high temperature.
The mechanical behavior of the Al–Al 2Cu eutectic under
compression as well as tension at elevated temperature (up
to 500 /C176C) has been studied by several groups [24–29].
These studies indicated a remarkable softening at high tem-
perature of the eutectic alloy. The high elongation during
test above 350 /C176C reveals the softening of Al
2Cu [24–25].
The microstructure study as reported by Butcher et al.
[26] shows coarsening of the eutectic. Work by Cantor
et al. [11,12] clearly explains the mechanical behavior of
Al–Al
2Cu at elevated temperature. In order to understand
the eﬀect of temperature on microstructure, and hence on
mechanical properties, a comparison plot of yield stress
as a function of temperature is presented in Figure 4 a.
The decrease in strength value as a function of temperature
for both alloys are similar. However, we observe a constant
diﬀerence in strength between the two studies with higher
strength for our alloy, which can be either due to length
scale diﬀerence between the two alloys or due to stable s
phase. The microstructure of the sample at 300 /C176C is shown
in Figure 4 b. The size of the ternary s phase remains the
same. On the other hand, the length scales as well as mor-
phology of eutectic have changed to a partially granular
morphology at elevated temperature. In order to quantify
the length scale change, the thickness of individual Al–
Al
2Cu lamellae for our alloy has been plotted as a function
of temperature ( Fig. 4 c). The plot shows an increasing
trend from 50 nm to /C24 200 nm. The stress calculated for dif-
ferent length scales using the stress and length scale relation
from previous work [18,19] is compared with the current
work ( Fig. 4 d). The trends are very diﬀerent to that
observed in our experiments. This conﬁrms that the change
in the morphology of the lamellae is the major reason for
the decrease of strength. Figure 4d also shows the schemat-
ics of the morphology as a function of temperature to illus-
trate this. The retained strength is due to stable ternary s
phase.
The bimodal size distribution of intermetallic phase in
rapidly solidiﬁed eutectic alloy gives rise to an enhance-
ment in strength and ductility both under compressive
and tensile conditions. The nanometer-scale eutectic gives
rise to strength while the hard third phase plays a crucial
role in diverting cracks and hence increasing fracture
strain. Thus the architecture of the phases developed in
our alloy provides a real opportunity to develop strong
high-temperature aluminum alloys.
