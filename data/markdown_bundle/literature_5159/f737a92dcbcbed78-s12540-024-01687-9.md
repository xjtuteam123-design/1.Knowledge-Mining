Abstract
The AlCrFe2NiCuMox multiphase high entropy alloys were designed, prepared and characterized. The effect of Mo addition 
on the microstructure evolution and mechanical properties of AlCrFe2NiCuMox high entropy alloys was investigated. The 
microstructure of AlCrFe2NiCuMox alloy consists of B2 + BCC + FCC + σ phases with a typical dendritic morphology. The 
addition of the Mo element promotes the generation of the σ phase in the alloy, while the B2 phase is gradually replaced 
by the σ phase. The hardness test and room temperature compression test results show that AlCrFe2NiCuMo0.6 alloy has a 
compressive rupture strength of 2030 MPa, a yield strength of 1462 MPa, a compressive strain limit of 18.18%, and a hard-
ness of 488 HV, which has good comprehensive mechanical properties. With the increase of Mo element addition, the BCC 
solid solution phase and the ordered B2 phase separated in the alloy, and the σ phase precipitate from the BCC solid solution 
phase, and the mechanical properties of the alloy become worse at room temperature.
Keywords High entropy alloys · Multiphase materials · Microstructure · Mechanical properties
1 Introduction
High entropy alloys (HEAs) also called multi-principal 
components alloys have attracted widespread attention since 
proposed by Yeh [1 ] and Cantor [2 ] in 2004. Most of the 
reported high entropy alloys are prepared by melting, and 
the casting ability of high entropy alloys are poor, resulting 
in severe component segregation, and large size high entropy 
alloy ingot is very difficult to prepare, which seriously hin-
ders the industrial application of high entropy alloys. To 
solve these problems, Lu et al. [3 ] designed eutectic high 
entropy alloys (EHEAs) and gained wide attention. EHEAS 
have better mobility, which prevents the formation of den-
drites that hinder the flow of liquid during solidification, 
thus improving the casting performance, and the constant 
temperature transition reduces the casting defects. Eutectic 
solidification allows for a wide range of microstructure to 
be obtained, and eutectic microstructure have unique advan-
tages for high temperature use [4].
In the existing research on EHEAs, the microstructure 
and properties of AlCrFe2Nix high entropy alloy system have 
been extensively studied [5–10]. Moreover, the mechanical 
properties can be further improved by adding of suitable ele-
ments. Thereinto, Cu element favors precipitation strength-
ening in HEAs [11]. It was observed that the addition of Cu 
element in CoCrFeMoNiCu x HEAs increased the strength 
due to its segregation to the interdendritic region and by the 
formation of Cu rich precipitates without the loss in ductility 
[12–14]. Furthermore, the addition of Cu element can lead 
to the refinement of the grain size of HEAs [15]. In addition 
to the miscible phase systems, Cu element tend to form vari-
ous phase-separated structures with most metals by conven-
tional bimetallic synthesis methods [16]. When designing 
high entropy alloys, it has been discovered that adding Cu 
element also results in the phase separation of Cu-rich phase. 
According to the study of FeNiMnCuxAl0.1Ti0.1 HEA, the 
addition of Cu element is conducive to the separation of the 
FCC phase, and the yield strength and tensile strength of the

in the alloy strengthening mechanism are investigated by

optimal additions of Mo element to achieve the best overall 
mechanical properties.
2 Materials and Methods
AlCrFe2NiCuMo x (x = 0.6, 0.8, 1.0, 1.2 and 1.4) (simply 
referred to as Mo0.6, Mo0.8, Mo1.0, Mo1.2, Mo1.4) high entropy 
alloys were produced by arc melting. The purity of all raw 
materials is higher than 99.6 wt%. The designed composi-
tions of five high entropy alloys were listed in Table  1. All 
raw materials were put into the copper crucible in order of 
melting point from low to high. Before melting ingots, the 
pure titanium material was firstly melted to absorb oxygen 
in the furnace chamber. Then, all ingots were melted for five 
times to ensure the homogeneous chemical compositions. 
The ingots were cut by wire cutting for the following micro-
structure observation and properties testing. The following 
investigated samples were taken from the middle part of the 
ingots with the uniform microstructure. The phase consti -
tutes were detected using an X-ray diffractometer. The scan-
ning angle (θ) was from 20° to 90°. The microstructure and 
the chemical compositions were analyzed using scanning 
electron microscope (SEM) and the SEM-equipped with the 
energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS). Compressive tests 
were conducted with the Instron 5569 testing machine at 
room temperature in air. The constant strain rate was set as 
 10–3  s−1. The compressive samples were cylindrical and their 
size was Φ 4 × 6 mm.

3.3 Mechanical Property Analysis
Figure  4 shows the Vicker hardness of AlCrFe2NiCuMox 
(x = 0.6, 0.8, 1.0, 1.2, 1.4). It can be seen from Fig.  4 and 
Table 3 that when x = 1.2, the microhardness value is the 
largest, up to 640 HV, which is 31% higher than 488 HV 
of Mo0.6.
It can be found from Fig. 4 that when 0.6 < x < 1.2, with 
the continuous increase of Mo element content in the alloy, 
the microhardness of the alloy shows an increasing trend 
due to the significant difference between the atomic radius 
size of Mo element and that of other elements. Most of the 
added Mo element is dissolved in the BCC solid solution 
phase, resulting in serious lattice distortion, enhancing the 
solution strengthening of the alloy. At this time, the solu-
tion strengthening is the primary strengthening mechanism 
in the alloy, meanwhile, Mo elements above the solution 
limit will combine with other elements in the alloy, causing 
a phase transition of BCC solid solution phase and σ phase 
precipitation. The second phase strengthening effect in the 
alloy is increased, which further increases the hardness of 
the alloy. When the content of Mo element exceeds x = 1.0, 
the increasing trend of alloy hardness slows down. This is 
because the addition of Mo element reaches the solid solu-
bility limit, and the solid solution strengthening effect no 
longer increases. At this time, the second phase strengthen-
ing becomes the main mechanism for the increase of alloy 
hardness. However, when x > 1.2, the hardness of the alloy 
shows a sharp downward trend. According to the data in 
Table 4 when x = 1.4, the volume fraction of the interden-
dritic region increases due to the change in the phase com-
position ratio in the alloy, i.e., the volume fraction of the 
FCC phase. The FCC phase belongs to the soft phase, and 
the increase of the FCC phase content will reduce the hard-
ness of the alloy to some extent. The Vickers hardness of this 
alloy decreases to 591 HV, which is 8 percent lower than the 
highest value of 640 HV.

4 Conclusion
This paper systematically studies the microstructure and 
mechanical properties of AlCrFe2NiCuMo x (x = 0.6, 0.8, 
1.0, 1.2, 1.4) series high-entropy alloys.
1. The microstructure of the AlCrFe2NiCuMox (x = 0.6, 
0.8, 1.0, 1.2, 1.4) eutectic high-entropy alloy consist of 
BCC + B2 + FCC + σ phases. The alloy microstructure 
presents a typical dendrite morphology, the microstruc-
ture of the dendritic region consists of B2 phase, BCC 
solid solution phase and its transformation precipitated 
σ phase, and the interdenditic region is composed of an 
irregular eutectic structure of ordered B2 phase and FCC 
phase.
2. The addition of Mo elements increases the volume frac-
tion of the interdendrite region and increases the content 
of the FCC phase, enabling the separation of the BCC 
solid solution phase and the ordered B2 phase.
3. Increasing Mo content intensifies the precipitation of the 
σ phase in the BCC solid solution and causes the matrix 
phase of the dendrite to change from BCC phase to σ 
phase. In addition, it is speculated that the addition of
