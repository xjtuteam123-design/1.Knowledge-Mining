ABSTRACT 
The role of micro-twins in the high-temperature deformation of superalloys is crucial and influenced by pre -
cipitates and deformation conditions. This study investigates the deformation mechanisms in a Ni-Co-based 
superalloy with γ ′ precipitates varying from 31 to 122 nm under two compression conditions at 760 
◦
C. The 
deformation mechanism exhibits a close correlation with the size of γ ′ precipitates under 5 % stain, while the 
formation of micro-twins is relatively limited. Microtwinning becomes the predominant deformation mode when 
compressed to 20 %. The micro-twins reach the peak intensity for the alloy with 47 nm-sized γ ′ precipitate. The 
optimizing of γ ′ precipitate size can significantly enhance the formation of micro-twins in Ni-Co-based super -
alloys. This strategy of modulating the deformation mechanism through precipitate size regulation offers valu -
able insights for the design of advanced alloys. 
1. Introduction 
Ni-Co-based superalloys are extensively employed in high- 
temperature applications like aero engines and industrial gas turbines, 
offering superior strength and robust resistance to creep, corrosion, and 
oxidation [1] . The addition of Co enhances their flexibility and high- 
temperature processing capabilities, while simultaneously reducing 
stacking fault energy (SFE) [2] . The low SFE introduces a crucial 
deformation mechanism, micro-twin (MT). MTs and SFs can form stable 
boundary networks, contributing positively to strength and plasticity 
[3,4] . Additionally, γ ′ precipitates in Ni-Co-based superalloys play a 
crucial role, as a strong correlation exists among γ ′ sizes, deformation 
mechanisms, and mechanical properties [5,6] . However, limited 
research has been conducted on the relationship between γ ′ sizes and 
deformation mechanisms, particularly MTs. Further investigation is still 
required. 
In this work, we examined the interaction between the γ ′ phase and 
dislocations in a newly developed Ni-Co-based superalloy with varying 
γ ′ sizes after compression at 760 
◦
C, utilizing transmission electron mi -
croscopy (TEM). Ultimately, we identified the crucial influence of γ ′ 
precipitate size on the formation of micro-twins under different strain 
levels. The elucidated mechanism provides foundational insights for 
enhancing the microstructure and mechanical properties of such alloys.

CRSS of dislocation loops becomes even lower. Consequently, disloca -
tions are more inclined to bypass the γ ′ rather than cutting into it to form 
SFs. The change results in a decrease in SF density, ultimately leading to 
a reduction in the number of MTs. 
5. Conclusion 
In this study, TEM analysis revealed a correlation among the γ ′ size, 
strains, and deformation mechanisms in a novel Ni-Co-based superalloy 
subjected to intermediate-temperature compression. At 5 % strain, as γ ′ 
size increases, the deformation mechanisms transition from dislocation 
coupling pairs to SFs and dislocation loops. Conversely, at 20 % strain, 
the predominant deformation mechanism across all samples is MTs. The 
size and density of MTs exhibit an initial increase and then decrease with 
the increasing γ ′ size. Throughout both strain stages, as the strain in -
tensifies, the capability to generate high-density SFs improves, and the 
accumulation of defects becomes sufficient to facilitate the formation of 
MTs.
