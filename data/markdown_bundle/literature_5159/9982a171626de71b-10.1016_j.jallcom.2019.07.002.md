Abstract : Three Ni-base powder metallurgy superalloys have been developed 
recently, and tensile tests at temperatures ranging from room temperature (RT) to 
815°C were conducted on them. The results conformed their excellent tensile 
properties, in comparison with several other existe d polycrystalline superalloys. In 
this work, by means of microstructure characterizat ion, thermal dynamic calculations, 
and theoretical modeling, different strengthening m echanisms including precipitation 
strengthening, grain boundary strengthening, solid solution strengthening, and 
Orowan strengthening, were found to contribute to t he yield strength in different 
degrees, which would help to further enhance the te nsile properties of these alloys 
through composition design and processing optimization thereafter. 
Keywords: Powder metallurgy superalloys; tensile properties; strengthening 
mechanism; composition design; processing optimization.

1. Introduction 
Nickel-base superalloys with excellent comprehensiv e properties at elevated 
temperatures play paramount roles in the aircraft e ngines, wherein polycrystalline 
superalloys are thought ideal materials as turbine disks [1-5]. To develop a new 
nickel-base superalloy, several mechanical properti es including tensile strength, creep 
resistance, fatigue crack resistance, and damage to lerance should be comprehensively 
considered [6-8]. In terms of the tensile property, nickel-base disk superalloys with 
yield strength over 1000 MPa and ultimate tensile s trength excessing 1200 MPa at 
service temperature are generally required to preve nt yielding and burst during 
rotating [9, 10]. 
With the development of computational materials sci ence, many scientists have 
tried to predict the mechanical properties prior to experiments based on data science, 
empirical and physic-based models, which could sign ificantly improve the efficiency 
of discovering novel superalloys [9, 11-15]. Genera lly, the enhanced yield strength in 
polycrystalline superalloys mainly derives from three strengthening contributions, i.e., 
grain boundary, solid solution in γ matrix, and precipitation strengthening [2, 16, 17 ]. 
To realize abovementioned mechanisms, compositional design and processing 
optimization are two efficient methods to boost the mechanical strength [18-24]. For 
example, Reed et al. [25] proposed the strength mer it index Mstrength , which integrates 
the anti-phase boundary energy (APBE) and the volum e fraction of γ′ based on 
hardening theory, to obtain a desirable precipitate strengthening during composition 
design; Collins and Stone [4] used a modelling appr oach to optimize the solution and 
aging heat treatments to maximize yield strength at 600 - 700 °C. However, less work 
has quantitatively studied how the different streng thening mechanisms contribute to 
the tensile strength of newly developed polycrystal line superalloys and give feedback 
to the thereafter composition and processing optimization. 
By combing several composition-property models and calculation of phase 
diagrams, three promising polycrystalline superallo ys were recently designed,

2. Materials and methods 
 The nominal compositions of three P/M superalloys are listed at table. 1. These 
P/M superalloys are prepared by casting, vacuum ind uction gas atomization, hot 
extrusion, and thereafter heat treatment. Specifically, vacuum inducing melting (VIM) 
was firstly adopted to produce the master alloys wi th different ingredients, then the 
powders within 150 µm were obtained by argon atomization and screening. Prior to 
hot extrusion at 1130°C with area reduction of 16:1 , the powders were loaded into in 
a stainless steel in diameter around 100 mm and deg assed at 400°C. Finally, the 
consolidated billets were heat treatment at 1100°C for 2 hours and air cooled, 
followed by aging treatment at 750°C for 24 hours. 
In order to perform EBSD experiment and detect the initial grain size of the P/M 
superalloys, the samples were sectioned along extru sion direction, polished by 
abrasive papers and 50 nm colloidal silica, followe d by vibration polishing. After 
EBSD detection, the data were analyzed via HKL Chan nel5 software. The equivalent 
grain size, defined as the diameter of a circle wit h identical area of the measured 
grain, was calculated by 2 A / π , wherein A was the area of a specific grain.

4. Conclusions 
 In summary, this work concerns the tensile propert ies of three newly developed 
P/M superalloys, and four different mechanisms cont ributing to the yield strength at 
ambient temperature are analyzed. Accordingly, foll owing conclusions can be 
reached: 
1. 
 The excellent tensile properties are detected by te nsile tests at room and elevated 
temperatures, as yield stresses (YS) and ultimate t ensile strengths (UTS) at room 
temperature of all the three designed P/M superallo ys excess 1250 MPa and 1650 
MPa respectively, and one of them owns YS at room t emperature approximating 
to 1800 MPa. In comparing with other fine-grained p olycrystalline superalloys, 
the new P/M superalloys also present superior YS an d UTS at test temperatures 
of RT, 650°C, 750°C, and 815°C. 
2. As the temperature increases from RT to 815°C, the fracture modes changes from 
ductile to brittle ones, based on the observation o f fractographies. Intergranular 
fracture plays dominating roles at temperature over 750°C, which contributes to 
the drops of the ductility. Thereafter, coarsened g rains realized by heat treatment 
are preferred at that high temperature. 
3. According to the composition and microstructure bas ed theoretical modeling, the 
precipitation strengthening makes the largest contr ibution to the yield strength at 
room temperature, which can be further improved by composition and processing 
optimization. Grain boundary strengthening and soli d solution are other two 
major factors enhancing the YS at room temperature, while the Orowan 
strengthening makes negligible contribution. As the temperature increases, the 
strength of the tested polycrystalline alloys drops gradually, which is caused by 
the weakened strengthening effects.
