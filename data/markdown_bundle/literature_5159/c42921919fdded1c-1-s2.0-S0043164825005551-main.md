ABSTRACT
CoCrW alloy is widely used in the field of aerospace, naval and nuclear industries as high-temperature moving 
components. For most Co-based alloys, the high-temperature mechanical properties are largely influenced by 
carbide. However, its effect on high-temperature tribological behavior is still unknown. Herein, B
4
C-reinforced 
CoCrW high temperature self-lubricating alloy (1B) was designed and prepared by spark plasma sintering for the 
probable application as high-temperature bearing sleeves of aero-engine. During the sintering process, hetero -
geneous growth of carbide takes place on the basis of B
4
C accompanied by diffusion of B into chromium carbide. 
Compared with the pure CoCrW alloy, the shape and distribution of carbides are changed in 1B, thus improved 
mechanical properties are obtained with yield strength of 1740 MPa, compressive strength of 2535 MPa and 
ultimate plasticity strain of 32 %, thereby rendering the enhancement of tribological properties against Si
3
N
4 
counterpart at room temperature. As temperature elevated, the wear resistance of 1B alloy is always better than 
that of 0B alloy and tribo-oxide film appears preferentially on the worn surface of 1B alloy compared to 0B alloy 
at 450 
◦
C. At 700 
◦
C, the large lath carbides provide sufficient strength to avoid fatigue wear of worn surface. 
Oxide debris upon the subsurface layer are sintered readily and remained stable for a long sliding time, which 
leads to the formation of a glaze layer and reduces frictional coefficient to 0.25. Simultaneously, a continuous 
Cr
2
O
3 
layer is formed under the glaze layer that avoids severe internal oxidation. So, the 1B alloy achieves 
perfect compatibility between tribological properties and oxidation resistance at 700 
◦
C.
1. Introduction
CoCrW alloy (Stellite alloy), as a traditional commercial cobalt-based 
alloy, is known for its excellent comprehensive performance which is 
characterized by high hardness, good compressive strength-plasticity 
matching and excellent wear resistance [ 1 – 5 ], and is widely used in 
the field of aerospace, naval and nuclear industry [ 6 – 8 ]. As Co-rich 
oxides possess favorable tribo-sintering properties, oxide debris on the 
worn surface of many Co-based alloys can be easily compacted and

2. Experimental
2.1. Materials preparation
Commercial CoCrW powders (45 μ m, Nangong Leigong Alloy Ma -

B

raw materials. Original powders were mixed through the method of high 
energy ball milling for 10 h, with the ball to powder mass ratio of 10:1. 
Thereafter, the mixed powders were sintered by spark plasma sintering 
(FCT HPD 250, Germany) with the parameters determined as follows: 
heating rate 25 
◦

◦

sintered alloys with different contents of B

4

4

alloys were cut into φ 6 × 9 mm and 32 × 5 × 4.5 mm for compression 
test and tribological test, respectively. The surfaces of samples were 
sanded with 400 #, 1000 # and 2000 # SiC sandpapers and then pol -
ished until free of scratches.
2.2. Mechanical measurement
Hardness was measured by Vickers indenter (Buehler Micromet 5114 
Mitutoyo Co., Japan) with a load of 300 g and a dwell time of 10 s. ASTM 
E92-82 provides testing standards for hardness measurement. Each 
sample was tested at least eight times and the average hardness was 
taken. Compressive properties test was carried out by a universal testing 
machine (WDW-100, Changchun Test Machine Co. Ltd., China) with a 
down speed of 0.1 mm/min. To ensure the test accuracy, three samples 
of each alloy were tested separately. To be noted, the ultimate plastic 
strain should be calculated by the following equation [ 27 ]: 
ε = ( H 0 H 1 ) / H × 100% (1) 
where H 0 and H 1 were the heights of the sample before and after test.
2.3. Tribological test
This work is an academic research based on the industrial demand 
for high-temperature self-lubricating alloys with high strength in aero- 
engine bearing sleeves over a wide range of temperatures (a maximum 
operational temperature of 700 
◦
C). Considering the ineffectiveness of 
liquid lubricants over 500 
◦

wear tests from room temperature to 700

America) to ensure operational consistency in lubricant-free scenarios. A 
Si

as the counterpart due to its excellent thermal stability and chemical 
inertness at elevated temperatures. The sliding wear tests were carried 
out at a load of 10 N, a speed of 24 mm/s and a scar length of 6 mm for 5 
and 30 min. With regards to the selection of load and speed, the 
experimental parameters of the PM series bearing sleeves with inside 
diameter of 6.35 mm (PM212, PM304 and PM400) that have been made 
public by NASA were applied as a reference, with a maximum contact 
stress of 24.1 MPa and a speed of 19.939 mm/s [ 29 , 30 ]. To better 
evaluate the tribological performance of as-sintered alloys and yet 
accelerate the wear process, more rigorous experimental parameters 
were selected here: load of 10 N (maximum contact stress 1043 MPa) 
and speed of 24 mm/s (reciprocating frequency of 2 Hz). Furthermore, 
in order to focus on the microstructure-properties relationships rather 
than parametric optimization, the fixed parameters of sliding wear tests 
were applied. Each sample was tested at least three times to ensure the 
reproducibility. Worn profiles, 3D worn scar contour maps and wear

process of tribo-oxide film against high-temperature softening. When

counterparts. When the temperature is high enough that the tribo-oxide

wear mechanism induced by increasing temperature can be observed 
through variation in the profile at the periphery of the wear tracks.
