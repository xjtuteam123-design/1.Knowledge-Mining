HIGH TEMPERATURE MECHANICAL PROPERTIES OF PURE AND ALLOYED GROUP 5 METALS IN VIEW 
OF THEIR APPLICATION AS TRITIUM PERMEATION MATERIALS
Takuya Nagasaka1, Takeo Muroga1, Akio Sagara1, Hiroshi Yukawa2, Tomonori Nambu3
1National Institute for Fusion Science, 322-6, Oroshi, Toki, Gifu 509-5292, Japan, nagasaka@nifs.ac.jp
2Graduate School and School of Engineering, Nagoya University, Furo, Chikusa, Nagoya 464-8603, Japan
3Suzuka National College of Technology, Shiroko, Suzuka, Mie 510-0294, Japan
Tensile and creep strength of pure V, Nb, Ta, and 
Nb-9W alloy were examined at 600 °C to assess their
applicability as hydrogen permeation material for the 
recovery of tritium from the high temperature liquid 
breeder blanket of a fusion reactor. The Nb-9W alloy 
required a comparatively higher annealing temperature to 
achieve good ductility. Based on tensile strength results, it 
is estimated that a permeation wall thickness of 0.1 mm is 
achievable. According to creep rate of Nb-9W alloy, 
design stress maybe decreased below 1/3 of yield stress.
I. INTRODUCTION
In the liquid breeder blanket systems, one of key 
issues is the tritium extraction from the breeder materials, 
such as molten salt Flibe, liquid Li and Pb-Li. Several
mechanisms are proposed as an extraction method, such 
as hydrogen permeation through metal wall and so on
1, 2.
Pd-25 wt%Ag (Pd-25Ag) alloy is practical for the metal 
wall permeation system because of its high hydrogen 
permeation rate
3. In addition, group 5 metals, such as V, 
Nb and Ta, and their alloys are potential high hydrogen 
permeation materials
4-6. However, they have only been 
investigated at temperatures up to 400 oC and not at the 
temperatures anticipated for tritium recovery from the 
liquid breeders. The tritium recovery system should be 
operated at higher temperature, to keep the breeder 
temperature high, leading to high thermal efficiency of 
the blanket system. A molten salt loop system, 
Operational Recovery Of Separated Hydrogen and Heat 
Inquiry – 1 (Orosh
2i-1)7, has been installed in National 
Institute for Fusion Science, and requires 600 oC for 
operation temperature. At higher temperature, lifetime of 
these materials is determined by corrosion rate and 
mechanical properties. The purpose of the present study is 
to evaluate the mechanical properties of the candidate
materials, and their applicability to high temperature 
operation.
II. EXPERIMENTAL
Materials used were pure V, Nb, Ta, and Nb-9 wt% 
W (Nb-9W) alloy, listed in TABLE I. Nb-9W alloy has 
recently been developed as a hydrogen-embrittlement
resistant permeation material. Embrittlement resistance 
was achieved by a reduction of hydrogen solubility 5.P d -
25Ag alloy, the current reference hydrogen permeation 
material, was also prepared for the tests. All originally 1 
mm thick sheets were cold-rolled into 0.25 mm thick foils. 
From these sheets small tensile specimens with gauge 
dimensions of 5 x 1.2 x 0.25 mm were punched. The 
specimens were annealed at 600~1100
oC for 1 hr in a 
vacuum. Vickers microhardness tests were performed at 
room temperature (RT) with a load of 500 g for 30 s. 
Specimens for tensile tests and creep tests were annealed 
at 800 
oC. Only Nb-9W was also annealed at 1000 °C. 
Tensile tests were conducted at RT under the air and at 
600 °C under a vacuum of about 1 x 10-4 Pa. Creep tests 
were carried out at 600 oC with a load of 50~240 MPa in 
the vacuum. Fracture surface after the tensile tests was 
observed with scanning electron microscope (SEM).

III. RESULTS
Figure 1 shows the hardness of the hydrogen 
permeation materials after cold rolling and annealing. 
Hardness of all the materials at as-rolled condition was 
higher than those for annealed condition, because of work 
hardening by cold rolling. Considerable recovery of the 
hardness was observed at 700, 800 and 900 
oC for pure 
Nb, V and Ta, respectively. Pd-25Ag alloy exhibited large 
recovery at 700 oC. Above the recovery temperature, 
hardness was relatively stable, while Nb-9W alloy 
showed continuous recovery of the hardness in the 
temperature range from 700 to 1100 
oC. Generally, 
materials should be annealed at about 200 oC higher than 
operation temperature. Assuming 600 oC is the operation 
temperature for the hydrogen permeation materials, 800 
oC was selected as the annealing temperature. Hardness of 
pure V, Nb and Pd-25Ag alloy was similar to each other 
at 800 oC, while Ta kept the hardness for as-rolled 
condition. Nb-9W alloy was hardest among the alloys. In 
order to investigate the effect of further softening by high 
temperature annealing, Nb-9W alloy was annealed also at
1000 oC.
Figure 2 depicts stress-strain curves of hydrogen 
permeation materials. Pure V, Nb, Ta and Pd-25Ag alloy 
showed large work hardening beyond the yield point and 
large elongation. Nb-9W alloy showed less work 
hardening and elongation after annealing at 800 
oC, while 
they were improved by annealing at 1000 oC. Figure 3
presents the tensile parameters obtained from Fig. 2. The 
harder materials indicated in Fig. 1, such as Ta and Nb-
9W alloy, exhibited higher yield stress and ultimate 
tensile stress at both RT and 600 
oC.
Figure 4 exhibits typical fracture surfaces of the 
hydrogen permeation materials after tensile tests. Pure V, 
Nb and Ta indicated almost no fracture surface because of 
their large deformation at both RT and 600 
oC as shown 
in Fig. 4 (a). Pd-25Ag alloy indicated similar behavior at 
RT (Fig. 4 (b)), while larger fracture surface appeared at 
600
oC (Fig. 4 (e)). The fracture surfaces of Nb-9W alloy 
annealed at 800 or 1000 °C were tested at RT and found 
to be similar (see Figs. 4 (c) and (d)). When specimens 
were annealed at 800 °C and there after tested at 600 °C a 
different surface with less deformation became apparent 
(see Fig. 4 (f)). Figure 5 shows the reduction of area 
obtained from the analyses on the fracture surfaces.
Reduction of area for the specimens except Pd-25Ag alloy 
and Nb-9W alloy tested at 600 
oC was larger than 90 %, 
whereas that for the alloys was relatively smaller. Figure
6 presents fracture surface with high magnification.

dimple pattern as shown in Figs. 6 (a) and (c). Part of 
fracture surface for Nb-9W alloy annealed at 800 oC was

IV. DISCUSSION
Softening of cold rolled pure metals shown in Fig. 1 is 
probably caused by recovery of dislocations,
recrystallization and grain growth 8. Hardness and tensile 
strength of pure Ta was found to be higher than that of 
pure V and Nb. It is thought that pure Ta is of higher 
hardness because of its resista nce to dislocation recovery 
and recrystallisation. Structural materials are usually 
annealed at 200 
oC or higher temperature than their 
operation temperature, in order to avoid aging at the 
operation temperature. Operation at 600
oC for Orosh 2i-1
probably requires annealing at 800 oC, therefore pure Ta 
is more suitable for controlling strength by cold rolling.

system are both solid solution type, the softening of Pd-

Figure 8 compares the hoop stress (a = r) with 1/3 of yield 
stress for the hydrogen permeation materials at 600 
oC,
assuming r = 5 mm, P 1 = 0.5 or 1 MPa, P 2 = 0 MPa 
(vacuum). The cross points give the minimum limit for 
tube wall thickness. Figure 8 indicates that 0.1~0.3 mm is 
enough for wall thickness of pure V and Nb. Pure Ta and 
Nb-9W alloy can reduce the requirement to around 
0.03~0.07 mm. On the other hand, the creep rate for 1/3 
of yield stress of Nb-9W, 120 MPa, is equivalent to 9.4 % 
deformation per year as mentioned for Fig. 7. This 
deformation is comparable to total elongation in tensile 
test at 600 
oC. This suggests that Nb-9W alloy maybe
fractured by creep after one year exposure to 1/3 yield 
stress. The minimum limit of wall thickness would be 
smaller than 1/3 of yield stress, if longer lifetime than one 
year is required. Creep strength becomes more important 
than tensile strength at higher temperature, and must be 
investigated further for the hydrogen permeation materials. 
However, the wall thickness is not only determined by the 
mechanical properties but also by the hydrogen
permeability and corrosion rate in liquid breeder materials. 
In view of this, hydrogen permeation studies and 
corrosion measurements are needed at temperatures of the 
order of 600 °C or above to provide the data base for the 
development of a tritium recovery process from the liquid 
blanket of a fusion reactor.
V. CONCLUSION
High temperature tensile and creep strength were 
examined for group 5 metals, such as pure V, Nb, Ta, and 
Nb-9W alloy in comparison to the reference hydrogen 
permeation material, Pd-25Ag. Pure Ta and Nb-9W alloy 
could be stronger than pure V, Nb and Pd-25Ag alloy at 
600 
oC. Based on the 1/3 of yield strength, design of 
tritium permeation tube wall is possible around 0.1 mm
with a liquid breeder pressure of 0.5~1 MPa. According to 
creep rate of Nb-9W alloy, design stress maybe decreased 
below 1/3 of yield stress, therefore further investigation of 
the creep properties of potential hydrogen permeation 
materials is needed.
