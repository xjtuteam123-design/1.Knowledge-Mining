ABSTRACT 
To develop ultrahigh-strength and lightweight structural materials, the temperature dependence of the defor -
mation behavior of Ti/FeTi eutectic alloys was examined using compression tests. An extremely high yield stress 
of ~2.3 GPa was obtained at room temperature accompanied by ~5 % of plastic strain by the alignment of a 
“ flower-like ” lamellar microstructure. High stress was maintained up to 400 
◦
C; however, the yield stress 
considerably decreased to ~0.8 GPa at 600 
◦
C. Shear deformation was observed at room temperature. However, 
at high temperatures, at 600 
◦
C and above, the formation of a deformation kink band was observed for the first 
time as the deformation mechanism, and this resulted in a rapid decrease in yield stress. The size of the kink 
bands was governed by the diameter of the aligned lamellar grains, which was termed the colony size. Large kink 
bands were formed in the alloy containing coarse colonies, resulting in the buckling of the specimen. The 
introduction of primary β -Ti grains in the eutectic microstructure improved the ductility at low temperatures, 
which was only accompanied by the marginal decrease in yield stress to ~1.9 GPa. Furthermore, the high- 
temperature strength of the hypoeutectic alloy at 600 
◦
C showed a higher value of ~1.0 GPa compared to 
that in eutectic alloys. The refinement of the colony microstructure of the hypoeutectic alloy suppressed the 
formation of kink bands. The experimental results suggest that microstructural control is extremely important for 
improving the high-temperature strength of Ti/FeTi eutectic alloys by increasing the formation stress of the kink 
bands. 
1. Introduction 
The demand for the development of high-strength materials to 
overcome the social problems related to global warming has been 
growing. Titanium alloys are one such candidate because of their high 
specific strength and corrosion resistance. Previous studies on ultra- 
high-strength titanium alloys have focused on Ti – Fe alloys [ 1 – 8 ], 
especially the eutectic composition of Ti/FeTi, that is, approximately 
Ti – 32.5 mass%Fe. For the binary alloys, the development of nanoscale 
lamellae comprising β -Ti and B2 – FeTi and a resultant room-temperature 
compressive strength of ~1.4 – 2 GPa have been reported [ 2 , 3 , 5 , 7 , 8 ]. To 
broaden the application of high-strength titanium alloys, their use above 
room temperature is desired, for example, in turbine blades. However, 
the variation in the strength of Ti/FeTi eutectic alloys with temperature 
remains unknown, which is investigated in this study. 
In addition, we recently focused on lamellar microstructured alloys 
to induce the formation of a deformation kink band [ 9 – 12 ]. A kink band 
is a deformation band in which dislocations are aligned along a direction 
perpendicular to their slip planes [ 13 – 17 ]. Studies on the development 
of high-strength Mg alloys have shown that the long-period stacking 
ordered (LPSO) phase induces the formation of a kink band [ 18 – 20 ] and 
contributes to an increase in strength [ 21 – 24 ]. This phenomenon is 
known as kink-band strengthening. The reason for inducing kink-band 
formation in the LPSO phase is its unique crystal structure, in which a 
soft Mg layer and hard (Y, Zn)-containing layer are alternately stacked 
[ 25 , 26 ]. Based on this finding, lamellar microstructured alloys 
comprising soft and hard phases, called mille-feuille microstructures, 
are expected to induce the formation of kink bands by restricting slips 
that cross the lamellar interface. This was confirmed in Mg/Mg

been made on the formation of kink bands in β -Ti single-phase alloys 
[ 27 – 30 ] and α / β two-phase alloys [ 31 – 34 ]; however, these studies are 
still limited. No studies have been conducted on the formation of kink

2. Experimental procedure 
Two ingots with different compositions, Ti – 30.0 mass%Fe and 
Ti – 32.5 mass%Fe, were prepared via arc melting in an Ar-gas atmo -
sphere. The compositions corresponded to Ti-26.9 at.%Fe and Ti-29.2 at. 
%Fe, respectively. The Ti – 32.5 mass%Fe corresponded to the eutectic 
composition in the Ti – Fe binary-phase diagram [ 35 ]; thus, the Ti – 30.0 
mass%Fe is a hypoeutectic composition in which the formation of pri -
mary Ti grains is expected. The prepared ingots were button-like with a 
diameter of ~25 mm and height of ~14 mm. For the Ti – 32.5 mass%Fe 
alloy, a larger rod-like ingot with a length of ~120 mm and height of 
~16 mm was also prepared to examine the variation in the micro -
structure with the ingot size. Hereafter, these alloys are referred to as the 
small ingot eutectic (SI-eutectic) and large ingot eutectic (LI-eutectic) 
alloys. The microstructure was observed using optical microscopy (OM) 
with Nomarski interference contrast and scanning electron microscopy 
(SEM). Powder X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis and energy-dispersive 
X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) in the SEM were used to examine the con -
stituent phases in the alloys. In addition to EDS, electron backscatter 
diffraction (EBSD)-pattern analysis was conducted to examine the 
crystal-orientation relationships between the constituent phases. The 
mechanical properties of the alloys were investigated using compression 
tests. Rectangular specimens with dimensions of ~1.2 mm × 1.2 mm ×
3.0 mm and ~1.5 mm × 1.5 mm × 4.5 mm were cut via electrodischarge 
machining. The loading direction was set parallel to the heat-flow di -
rection in the melt ingots, as will be described later. Compression tests 
were conducted in the temperature range from room temperature (RT) 
to 800

deformation microstructure was analyzed using SEM-EBSD. In some 
parts of the deformation microstructure, a three-dimensional sectional

4. Discussion 
4.1. Control of the formation behavior of kink band by microstructure 
In this study, the formation of kink bands in Ti-based eutectic alloys 
was investigated. The introduction of a deformation band was reported 
in some previous studies [ 1 , 4 – 7 ]; however, these were not kink bands, 
and the proposed formation mechanism was different from that of kink 
bands. Maity et al. argued that the formation of a shear band was 
induced by compressive deformation, resulting in the segmentation of 
lamellae [ 7 ]. This may correspond to the traces of shear deformation 
observed at low temperatures between RT and 400 
◦
C in this study, 
shown in Fig. 9 . However, the shear deformation band did not develop 
into a large deformation band. The formation of kink bands was 
observed in deformation at high temperatures at 600

4.2. Formation mechanism of the kink band in Ti/FeTi mille-feuille 
material 
The arbitrary variation in the crystal-rotation angle shown in Fig. 12 
indicates that the deformation bands in the Ti/FeTi eutectic alloys 
formed at high temperatures are deformation kink bands. Yamasaki 
et al. reported that, in general, the rotation axis in the kink band in the 
“ single-phase material ” is considered to be the direction perpendicular 
to both the slip-plane normal and Burgers vector of the dislocation that 
forms the kink-band boundary [ 15 , 16 ]. The operative deformation 
mode in β -Ti varies depending on its β -phase stability, and the domi -
nantly operative deformation mode in β -Ti alloy contained a large 
amount of the β -phase stabilizing element; in the present case Fe, is 
known to be the dislocation slip with a Burgers vector of < 111 > [ 41 , 
42 ]. The slip plane of the < 111 > dislocation in bcc materials, including 
β -Ti, is not fixed to {10 1}, but it can be widely changed between {10 1} 
and {11 2} on the < 111 > -zone axis via the microscopic cross slip 
[ 43 – 45 ]. However, in B2 – FeTi, the operative deformation mode was 
previously reported to be a {110} < 001 > slip [ 46 , 47 ]. That is, although 
the crystal structures were similar in bcc-Ti and B2 – FeTi, the Burgers 
vectors of the dislocations are different, and the slip plane is fixed in 
FeTi. Note that in the crystal-orientation map of the kink band shown in 
Fig. 11 , discontinuous variations in crystal orientation were measured in 
β -Ti; however, the crystal orientations were rather continuous in FeTi 
around the boundary region of the kink bands. This suggests that the 
distinct kink-band boundary, i.e., the array of dislocations perpendicular

5. Conclusions 
In this study, the temperature dependence of the deformation 
behavior of Ti/FeTi eutectic alloys was examined using compression 
tests. The results are summarized as follows: 
[1] For the Ti/FeTi eutectic alloy with a microstructure aligned along 
the loading direction, the yield stress showed an extremely high 
value of ~2.3 GPa at RT accompanied by ~5 % plastic strain. The 
high stress was maintained up to 400 
◦
C, but the yield stress 
sharply decreased above 400 
◦
C. 
[2] During deformation at 600 
◦
C and above, deformation kink bands 
were formed in the lamellar-microstructure region instead of 
shear deformation across the rod-like microstructure. This 
resulted in a significant decrease in the yield stress. The size of the 
kink bands was affected by the colony-grain diameter in the alloy. 
In the alloy with large colonies, large kink bands were locally 
formed at the edge of the specimen, resulting in buckling. In al -
loys containing smaller colonies, the formation of tiny kink bands 
was induced, leading to more homogeneous deformation. 
[3] In the hypoeutectic alloy with primary β -Ti grains, the yield stress 
at RT and 400 
◦
C was marginally lower than that in the eutectic 
alloy; however, the yield stress exhibited a higher value of ~1.0 
GPa at 600 
◦
C because of the suppression of kink-band formation 
by primary β -Ti grains. Based on the results achieved for hypo -
eutectic alloys, the reduction in colony diameter with an increase 
in the formation stress of the kink band is the next target for 
improving the high-temperature strength of Ti/FeTi eutectic 
alloys.
