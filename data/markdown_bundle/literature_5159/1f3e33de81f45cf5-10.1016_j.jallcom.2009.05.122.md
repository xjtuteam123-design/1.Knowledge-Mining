abstract
Ni–Nb-based binary and ternary metallic glass wires with superior bending ductility and extremely high
tensile strength exceeding 2 GPa were prepared by the arc-melt-type melt-extraction method. Continuous
metallic glass wires without a thick oxide layer and surface roughness were obtained from Ni
60Nb20M20
(M = Ti, Zr, Hf, V, Nb, and Ta) alloys. However, an amorphous phase was not obtained in Ni 60Nb20M20
(M = Cr, Fe, Co, Cu, Al, Si, and Sn) alloys; further, a continuous metallic crystalline wire was formed only
from the Ni–Nb–Fe alloy. In Ni–Nb based alloys, amorphous phase formation is favorable for the formation
of continuous metallic wires by the arc-melt-type melt-extraction method.

1. Introduction
Metallic glasses are known to exhibit superior mechanical prop-
erties such as high tensile strength and large elastic elongation limit
of approximately 2% [1,2]. In order to effectively utilize their supe-
rior strength, it is preferable to prepare wire-shaped samples of
metallic glasses with a circular cross section and a highly smooth
surface without any roughness (such as that of a mirror). Metal-

2. Experimental procedures
We prepared Ni60Nb20M20 (M = Y, Ti, Zr, Hf, V, Nb, Ta, Cr, Fe, Co, Cu, Al, Si, and Sn)
alloys in order to examine the difference between the ability of metallic amorphous
and non-amorphous phase alloys to form melt-extracted wires. Master ingots of Ni-
Nb-based alloys were prepared by arc-melting a mixture of pure Ni, Nb, Y, Ti, Zr, Hf,
V, Nb, Ta, Cr, Fe, Co, Cu, Al, Si, and Sn (purity >99.9%) by the conventional arc-melt
technique under highly puriﬁed Ar atmosphere. The rapidly solidiﬁed wires were
prepared by the arc-melt-type melt-extraction method from the ingots.Fig. 1 shows
the schematic illustration (a) and photograph (b) of the experimental apparatus
(Nissin-Gikken, NEV-AT3). The edge angle of the Cu roll (diameter 200 mm) was
ﬁxed at an angle of 60
◦. The rotation speed of the roll was ﬁxed to 33.3 turns s −1
(2000 rpm) and the circumferential velocity of the Cu roll was 21 ms −1. A molten
pool of the mother alloy was maintained on a water-cooled copper-mold during its
extraction under Ar atmosphere. In this apparatus, the position of the Cu roll could
be controlled. The molten mother alloy was extracted by moving down the rotating
Cu roll. The features of this apparatus are summarized in other studies [24,25]. The
conditions for the preparation of the Ni–Nb-based melt-extracted wires are similar
to those of Zr-based metallic glass wires in a previous study[25]. The formation of an
amorphous phase was conﬁrmed by X-ray diffraction (XRD) using Cu-K/H9251radiation,
differential scanning calorimetry (DSC), and differential thermal analysis (DTA) at
heating rates of 0.67 K s
−1 and 0.33 K s−1, respectively. The surface morphology was
examined by optical microscopy (OM) and scanning electron microscopy (SEM). The
tensile strength of the wire specimens was measured using an Instron-type testing
machine. The wire diameter was approximately 100/H9262m, and the gauge length was
ﬁxed to 10 mm—approximately 100 times the wire diameter. The test wires were
bonded to a specially designed paper frame with Aron-Alpha adhesive. The wires
were pulled at a strain rate of 4.2 × 10
−4 s−1 in air. Ductility of the test wires was
evaluated by a simple bending test. Ductility implies the ability of the wire to bend
through 180
◦ without fracture.

3.2. Surface morphology and degree of circularity in
melt-extracted wires
Fig. 5shows the appearance of the outer surface of Ni
60Nb20Zr20
amorphous and Ni60Nb20M20 (M = Cr, Fe, Co, Cu, Al, Si, and Sn) crys-
talline wires. Melt-extracted metallic glass wires have a smooth
surface and no distinct roughness, and such features can be con-
ﬁrmed in Ni
60Nb20M20 (M = Ti, Hf, V, Nb, and Ta) alloys. In contrast,
a ﬂuctuation in diameter indicated by black arrows can be observed
in the melt-extracted crystalline wires. The degree of ﬂuctuation
in diameter is very sensitive to its constituent element. Previous
papers have reported the origin of the ﬂuctuation in diameter in
melt-extracted oxide ﬁbers in detail[26,27]; the effects of viscosity
and surface tension are considered important material parameters.
The discussions can be considered to be applicable to the metallic
material considered in the present study. The viscosity and surface
tension of the melt might be highly sensitive to the constituent ele-
ment, resulting in the dependence of the ﬂuctuation in diameter
on the constituent element. The degree of ﬂuctuation in diame-
ter in Ni
60Nb20M20 (M = Cr, Co, Cu, and Sn) alloys, from which only
ﬂake- and/or powder-like specimens were obtained by the melt-
extraction method, was observed to be larger than that observed in
the other alloys. The column “Fluctuation in diameter” in Table 1
summarizes the diameter ﬂuctuations in the melt-extracted wires.
In order to investigate the surface morphology of melt-extracted
wires in detail, we carried out SEM observations. The obtained
results are shown in Fig. 6. The superior smooth surface without
any roughness, observed in Fig. 6(a), can be conﬁrmed by not only
OM but also SEM observations in the melt-extracted Ni
60Nb20Zr20
metallic glass wires. Further, no signiﬁcant change of contrast corre-
sponding to crystalline precipitates was observed in Ni 60Nb20M20
(M = Ti, Zr, Hf, V, Nb, and Ta) alloys. However, the contrast corre-
sponding to the crystalline precipitates was observed in all the
melt-extracted crystalline wires, as shown in Fig. 6(b). The crys-
tallization of extracted-melt occurs in Ni
60Nb20M20 (M = Cr, Fe, Co,
Cu, Al, Si, and Sn) alloys, resulting in surface roughness and ﬂuc-
tuations in the diameter. The surface morphology depends on the
constituent elements, which is in good agreement with the differ-
ence in phase selection with constituent elements, as shown in
Fig. 3(b). Both phase selection and solidiﬁcation mode are highly
sensitive to the constituent elements in Ni–Nb-based alloys, thus
resulting in the formation of various surface morphologies in melt-
extracted crystalline wires. A highly smooth surface without any
roughness, a mirror surface, is obtained only in the case of metallic
glass wires drawn from Ni
60Nb20M20 (M = Ti, Zr, Hf, V, Nb, and Ta)
alloys. In a conventional steel cord prepared by the wire-drawing
process (Fig. 6(b)), the trace of metallic ﬂow along the die can be
observed. The surface roughness of wires sometimes acts as an ori-
gin of the destruction of a surface crack. The melt-extracted metallic
glass wires show a highly smooth surface without any initiation site
of cracks and fractures. The melt-extraction method is highly effec-
tive for obtaining metallic glass wires with highly smooth surface;
this cannot be achieved by the conventional metal plastic forming
process in Ni–Nb-based alloys.

3.3. Mechanical properties of melt-extracted metallic glass wires
Mechanical properties of melt-extracted Ni–Nb-based metallic
glass wires were investigated by performing a tensile test in air.
In this study, the tensile stress was evaluated under the assump-
tion that the cross section of melt-extracted metallic glass wires
shows perfect circular morphology and that there is no ﬂuctua-
tion in diameter. The elongation of the wires was measured by
determining the displacement of a crosshead and not using a strain
gage. In this case, the elongation may be slightly higher than the
net value, namely, over-estimated value; moreover, the absolute
value of elastic elongation cannot be evaluated[25,29]. Fig. 8shows

4. Discussion
The formation of an amorphous phase is known to be an impor-
tant factor for continuous melt-extracted metallic wire formation
[24–26]. The discontinuous change in volume due to crystallization
destabilizes the shape of extracted melt, resulting in the breaking up
of the continuous wire shape melt.Fig. 5(b) shows a typical example
of the crack of the melt-extracted wire due to the crystallization of
melt, indicated by black arrows, in Ni
60Nb20Co20 alloy.Table 1sum-
marizes the relationship between the amorphous phase formation
and continuous melt-extracted wire formation. The amorphous
phase formation is favorable for the formation of a melt-extracted
wire of a Ni–Nb-based alloy. The amorphous phase formation cri-
terion, which is similar to that for Zr-based alloys, is also applicable
to Ni–Nb-based alloys [24,25].
In the present study, the amorphous phase formation can be con-
ﬁrmed in the Ni–Nb–M alloy whose M-element belongs to group 4A
(M = Ti, Zr, and Hf) or 5A (M = V, Nb, and Ta) among Ni
60Nb20M20
(M = Y, Ti, Zr, Hf, V, Nb, Ta, Cr, Fe, Co, Cu, Al, Si, and Sn) alloys, as
shown in Table 3. Chemical mixing enthalpy plays an important
role in amorphous phase formation during liquid quenching, and
the values are shown in Table 3 [31,32]. The Ni–Nb–M alloy whose
M-element belongs to group 4A or 5A shows a large negative value,
resulting in the formation of an amorphous phase during melt-
extraction. Ni–Nb–Al and Ni–Nb–Si alloys also show large negative

5. Conclusion
Ni–Nb-based metallic glass wires were prepared by the arc-
melting type melt-extraction method, and their characteristics
were studied. The following conclusions were drawn:
(1) Amorphous phase formation was observed in Ni
60Nb20M20
(M = Ti, Zr, Hf, V, Nb, and Ta) alloys, while fully crystallized
structures were formed in the melt-extracted specimen of
Ni
60Nb20M20 (M = Cr, Fe, Co, Cu, Al, Si, and Sn) alloys.
(2) Amorphous phase formation is favorable for the formation of
continuous melt-extracted wires. Continuous metallic amor-
phous single phase wires can be drawn from Ni
60Nb20M20
(M = Ti, Zr, Nb, and Ta) alloys.
(3) Ni 60Nb20M20 (M = Ti, Zr, Nb, and Ta) metallic glass wires simul-
taneously show negligible ﬂuctuation in diameter, a mirror
surface without any initiation site of cracks and fractures, high
roundness of around 100, high tensile strength of the order of
2 GPa, large elastic elongation, and superior bending ductility.
