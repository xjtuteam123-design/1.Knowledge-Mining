STRUCTURE, CHEMICAL COMPOSITION, AND PHASE COMPOSITION
OF INTERMETALLIC ALLOY VKNA-1V AFTER HIGH-TEMPERATURE
HEAT TREATMENT AND PROCESS HEATING
O. A. Bazyleva, 1 Yu. A. Bondarenko, 1 G . I. Morozova,1 and O. B. Timofeeva 1
Translated from Metallovedenie i Termicheskaya Obrabotka Metallov,N o .5 ,p p .3–6 ,M a y ,2014.
Single-crystal preforms from intermetallic alloy VKNA-IV with different crystallographic orientations (CGO)
are studied. The mode of heat treatment is shown to affect the microstructure of the preforms, ultimate rupture
strength, yield strength, and long-term strength (the time before failure). Process heatings used in soldering
also affect these properties.
Key words: VKNA, single-crystal preforms, crystallographic orientation (CGO), microstructure,
Ni3Al intermetallic alloy, strength characteristics, long-term strength, soldering.
INTRODUCTION
The structural materials of gas-turbine engines behave
differently from one another at temperatures above 0.6 Tmg ,
their specific behavior depending on the base of the material
— iron, cobalt, nickel, a ceramic, or elements with a high
melting point. This difference is related to aspects of the
chemical bonds in the materials and the structural and phase
transformations that take place [1]. The intermetallic com -
pound Ni
3Al has unidirectional covalent bonds, so it is ther -
mally stable and has an ordered structure up to the melting
point of the alloy [2, 3].
The character of the effect of alloying on the strength of
the intermetallide (nickel aluminide) depends on the sub -
lattice (Ni and/c47or Al) in which the alloying element (AE) is
located and on the deviation of the initial and alloyed compo-
sitions from the stoichiometric composition. These factors
can change not only the degree of distortion of the crystalline
lattice and the average valence of the metals in the
intermetallide but also the degree to which the given compo-
sition of the solid solution is close to the limit of solubility of
the AE in nickel aluminide [4, 5].
Domestic alloys based on Ni
3Al are designed so that they
contain not only /c103/c162-Ni3Al but up to 5 – 10 vol.% of a /c103-solid
solution which is based on nickel with a disordered fcc lat-
tice. This solid solution serves as the tough component of the
alloy, ensuring that it has good low-temperature ductility
characteristics [4, 5].
In order to be able to use cast intermetallic Ni
3Al-based
superalloy VKNA-1V in future engines PS90A2 and PD14
[6] and determine the safe life of parts and components made
of it, a study was made of the structure, phase composition,
and chemical composition of VKNA-1V after it underwent
high-temperature heat treatment in different regimes and
casting defects were repaired by soldering.

The chemical composition of alloy VKNA-1V was de -
termined by the following methods: the contents of the main
alloying elements (Al, Cr, Mo, W, Ti) and impurities (Fe, Si,
P) were evaluated by spectral analysis on an ARL4460 spec-
trometer, while the amounts of C, S, O, and N were deter -
mined on a TC-600 gas analyzer (Table 1) [8].
Computer-controlled high-gradient crystallization unit
UVNS-5 was used to cast monocrystalline semifinished
products of intermetallic alloy VKNA-1V for heat treatment
and use in the repair of casting defects by soldering. The
CGO of the semifinished products is inherited by mono-
crystalline dummy bars that serve as a substrate for growing
a single crystal from the liquid phase. To do this, before re-
fining was begun, high-melting monocrystalline dummy bars
with the prescribed /c225111/c241CGO were placed in a 16-mm-
diam. cylindrical ceramic mold containing starting cones.
The apex of the cones included cavities to accommodate the
bars. The bars were obtained by cutting them out from cylin-
drical monocrystalline 8-mm-diameter semifinished products
at a certain orientation. The semifinished products, made of
an Ni – W alloy, had previously been cast on a UVNES-4
unit by the same technology used in [9] to obtain
monocrystalline semifinished products from an intermetallic
alloy. After being cut, all of the bars were etched and sub -
jected to x-ray study on a DRON diffractometer in order to
check their orientation. Dummy bars that deviated from the
prescribed direction by no more than 5° were used in the in -
vestigation.
The process of casting monocrystalline semifinished
products of the intermetallic alloy on a UVNS-5 unit consists
of the following: ceramic molds containing dummy bars hav-
ing the requisite CGO during heating are placed in a preheat-
ing furnace — a region which has a temperature field that is
constant over its height and that corresponds to the tempera -
ture range above the liquidus temperature of the intermetallic
alloy but lower than the liquidus temperature of the dummy-
bar alloy. The measured bar-shaped (charge-grade) semifi -
nished product of the Ni
3Al alloy was melted in an induction
furnace, poured into ceramic molds, and allowed to undergo
directional crystallization by moving the melt-bearing mold
from the heating zone through a heat shield and into a cool -
ing zone. The mold was moved as rapidly as possible until it
was maximally immersed in the coolant (molten tin)
[10, 11].
The macrostructure of the resulting semifinished pro -
ducts was determined by etching in a specially prepared mix-
ture of concentrated hydrochloric acid and hydrogen per -
oxide. The monocrystalline semifinished products were dis -
patched for x-ray examination after visual inspection. To do
this, the part of the casting that adhered to the dummy bar
was cut from each monocrystalline semifinished product per-
pendicular to the main axis of the casting and was subjected
to deep etching until the dendritic structure was clearly re -
vealed. X-ray photographs were taken of the starting cones
on transverse sections with the use of a DRON-3 diffrac-
tometer and the method that was developed.
The semifinished products that were deemed to be usable
were determined from an evaluation of the x-ray photo-
graphs of the specimens. The specimens were judged to be
usable if the required crystallographic direction of the corre-
sponding starting cone deviated from the vertical axis of the
semifinished product by an angle á no greater than 10°, i.e.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
The microstructure of alloy VKNA-1V in the initial state
has a dendritic-cellular structure (Fig. 1 a and b ). The sec -
tions between the dendrites contain coarse particles of the
/c103/c162-phase (Fig. 1 c and d ). The particles of the intermetallic
/c103/c162-phase are surrounded by thin intermediate layers of a
/c103-solid solution. The particles are finer on the axes of the
dendrites and are triangular (for the /c225111/c241orientation) and
cubic (for the /c225001/c241orientation) in form (Fig. 1e and f ).
Studies performed to choose regimes for homogenizing
annealing in order to eliminate stresses created by the casting
operation showed that the phase composition of intermetallic
alloy VKNA-1V after annealing in the chosen regimes re -
mained nearly constant (Table 2). The only change was in the
ratio of the concentrations of the phase — there was an in -
crease in the content of the interaxial /c103/c162-phase. Figure 2
shows the microstructure of the alloy after homogenizing an-
nealing within the range 1200 – 1300°C. It is apparent that
the structural changes which are seen after annealing at
1250°C are connected mainly with processes that occur in
the intermediate layers of the /c103-solid solution. This phase is
of variable composition, with the atoms of the different ele-
ments in the phase being located in the crystalline lattice of
nickel. The increase in the amount of /c103-solid solution that is
present, the decrease in its stability, and the precipitation of a
highly dispersed fraction of the /c103/c162-phase after annealing at
1270 – 1300°C weaken the alloy and reduce the time to fail -
ure in stress-rupture tests (Table 3).

The tensile tests showed that the intermetallic alloy re-
tains its original mechanical properties even after lengthy an-
nealing at 1250°C (Table 4). This result can be attributed to
the ordered structure of alloys of the given type and the con-
sequent low diffusional mobility of the atoms of the alloying
elements over the volume of the alloy at high temperatures
[15, 16].
We tried removing residual compressive stresses (from
–100 to –370 MPa) formed in the surface as a result of ma -
chining during the specimens’ preparation by using different
annealing regimes: at temperatures of 800, 900, 1000, 1100,
1150, and 1200°C for 1, 2, 5, and 10 h. The residual stresses
were measured after the annealing operation and it was de -
termined that complete relaxation of the stresses in the alloy
takes place after annealing for1ha t1 150°C.
A microstructural analysis was performed on semifin -
ished products of the Ni3Al-based intermetallic alloy that had
been heat-treated to remove machining-induced stresses by
the following regime: holding at 1150°C for 1 h and cooling
in air. The analysis showed that there were no significant
changes in the microstructure of alloy VKNA-1V after an-
nealing at 1150°C (Fig. 3). There was some growth and co-
alescence of particles of the /c103/c162-phase on the axes of the den-
drites and in the regions between the dendrites (Fig. 3 c,
e, g, i ).
The effect of the process heatings used in soldering ope -
rations was studied by microstructural analysis of the alloy.
Heating at 1280±5°C for 30 min and subsequent vacuum an-
nealing at 1150°C for 30 min showed that the structure of the
alloy is thermally stable. Such heating results in partial co -
alescence and elongation of particles of the /c103/c162-phase on the
axes of the dendrites and between the dendrites, and finely
dispersed particles of the /c103/c162-phase are formed in the interme-
diate layers of the /c103-solid solution (Fig. 4).

CONCLUSIONS
1. The structure of alloy VKNA-1V is highly stable at
service temperatures up to 1250°C.
2. Analyzing the microstructure of alloy VKNA-1V after
homogenizing annealing makes it possible to determine the
service temperature of parts made of the alloy based on its
structural-phase composition.
