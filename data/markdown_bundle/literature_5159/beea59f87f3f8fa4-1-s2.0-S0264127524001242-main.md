1. Introduction 
The composition of traditional alloys were mainly based on one or 
two principle elements with addition of tracer elements to attain the 
desired properties, and from this perspective the excellent structural 
materials i-e., ultra-high strength steels and super alloys were formed 
[1 – 3] . However, these alloys have some limitations in microstructure 
and properties due to the presence of one or two principle elements [4] ; 
for example, the applications of these alloys are limited due to high work 
hardening and low thermal conductivity, and these alloys are prone to 
cracks [5 – 8] . High entropy alloys (HEAs) is the new generation of alloys, 
which changed the traditional rules adopted for an alloy design. High 
entropy alloys (HEAs) mainly contain at least five principle elements 
and make the complex concentrated systems, which have potential in -
dustrial applications due to their outstanding mechanical properties 
[9 – 13] . However, the strength increment in HEAs is also related to the 
ductility loss and vice-versa. The FCC structured HEAs mainly have low 
strength, and the different approaches have been adopted by materials 
researchers to overcome this dilemma. The strength increment can be 
attained through different strengthening mechanisms including, grain 
refinement, precipitation strengthening, dislocation strengthening and 
severe plastic deformation [14 – 19] , while the ductility of HEAs could be 
improved through nano-scale defect heterogeneity of extensive stacking 
faults (SFs), dislocations, transformation induced plasticity (TRIP), and 
twining induced plasticity (TWIP) effects [20 – 23] . 
The chemistry changes of FCC-HEAs through alloying with Nb, C, Ti, 
Al, and Mo elements led to the microstructural heterogeneities which 
further increased the strength of alloys through precipitation strength -
ening [24 – 27] . Therefore, precipitation strengthening can overcome the 
dilemma of low strength in FCC HEAs. The strength-ductility combina -
tion of Ni
2

element, which was attributed to the formation of Multiple SFs, Lomer- 
Cottrell locks, the refinement of topological close packed (TCP) sigma 
( σ ) and lave phases [20] . The nano-sized σ particles possessed a high 
hardness, which pinned the dislocation slip and increased the hardness 
of the CoCrFeNiMo

3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Mechanical properties 
The tensile test was conducted for two HEAs at room temperature, 
and engineering stress – strain curve was plotted as shown in Fig. 1 . Both 
of these HEAs exhibit the excellent mechanical properties especially 
NbC reinfored HEA, which has a yield strength (YS) and tensile strength 
(TS) of 493 MPa and 863 MPa, respectively with the total elongation of 
58 % as shown in Fig. 1 a. A balanced strength and ductility combination 
is achieved in this precipitation strengthen HEA with TWIP effect. In 
reminder of this paper, the NbC reinforced HEA is taken as the research 
object, and deformed specimens with strain of 10 %, 20 %, 30 %, and full 
fractured were observed by EBSD and TEM to investigate the underlying 
deformation mechanism. The detailed analysis is further elaborated in 
the next section. Fig. 1 b compares the room temperature tensile strength 
versus elongation of NbC reinforced HEA with various FCC structured 
NbC containing and precipitation strengthen HEAs 
[4,20,24,26,27,33 – 40] . The strength increment is mainly accompanied 
with ductility loss and vice-versa for most of these HEAs, and it is 
obvious that the studied HEA significantly breaks the strength-ductility 
trade-off.

accumulation of high density dislocations around the NbC precipitates

entanglement suggests that the dislocation slip is mainly hindered by

deformation twin. Moreover, the distribution of precipitates inside the 
FCC matrix and at GBs can trigger the strain hardening mechanism. The 
precipitate at GBs leads to a high stress concentration in its adjacent 
grains and then induces the deformation twins [62] .
