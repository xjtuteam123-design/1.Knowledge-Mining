Abstract: 13 
In this paper, the CrMnFeCoNi high entropy alloy ha s been processed by electopulsing-assisted 14 
ultrasonic surface rolling. The strength of the hig h-entropy alloy has been greatly improved while 15 
it still has good plasticity, attributing to the ap proximate gradient structure can be formed by 16 
ultrasonic rolling, and the plastic deformation can be adjusted by electric pulse. 17

Introduction 22 
CrMnFeCoNi HEA is a face centered cubic (FCC) chemi cal disorder solid solution and has good 23 
mechanical properties at extremely low temperatures [1]. HEAs have also been reported to 24 
exhibited excellent performance in corrosion resistance, oxidation resistance, electrical properties, 25 
magnetic properties, radiation resistance [2-6]. However, the low tensile strength (usually less than 26 
500 MPa) of CrMnFeCoNi HEA at room temperature limi ts its application to a certain extent. 27 
Without changing the composition of its components, it is usually treated by severe deformation. 28 
However, the plasticity is seriously reduced [7-8]. 29 
 30 
The gradient structure can avoid the local strain c oncentration effectively and make the metal 31 
show good plasticity with the increase of strength [9]. At present, the methods of preparing 32 
gradient structure in metal by plastic deformation mainly include surface mechanical attrition 33 
treatment (SMAT), surface mechanical grinding treat ment (SMGT), and surface mechanical 34 
rolling treatment (SMRT) [10]. Although the deformation of the sample after the SMRT process is 35 
not as serious as the first two treatment, the surf ace quality of the sample is very good. It is a 36 
treatment with industrial application prospects. In order to improve the deformation of the sample 37 
after SMRT, ultrasonic and electrical pulses were i ntroduced into the mechanical rolling process. 38 
Ultrasonic softening effect [11] and electroplastic effect [12] produced by ultrasonic waves and 39 
electric pulses can make metal deformation more sev ere and the gradient layer deeper [13-15]. 40 
The previous study revealed that gradient structure can be formed in the metal surface during 41 
ultrasonic rolling process efficiently, and electric pulse can regulate plastic deformation due to its 42

thermal effect and non-thermal effect [16]. Thus, to improve the strength and control the plasticity 1 
of HEA, HEA was processed by electopulsing-assisted ultrasonic surface rolling process 2 
(EP-USRP) in this study and its microstructure and mechanical properties were studied. 3

Experimental 5 
Five elements with 99.9% purity of Cr, Mn, Fe, Co a nd Ni with equimolar ratio were melted in 6 
vacuum electromagnetic induction melting furnace. Then the as-cast HEA was heated to 800  for 7 
30 minutes and hot-rolled (deformation 50%). Finall y, annealed at 1000  for 2 hours and cooled 8 
to room temperature in air. Ultrasonic surface roll ing process (USRP) and EP-USRP are 9 
performed on a self-assembly platform (see Figure 1 ). The parameters of ultrasonic rolling 10 
equipment are as follows: the frequency is 30 kHz, the amplitude is 6 μm, and the diameter of 11 
cemented carbide ball is 14 mm. The processing para meters are as follows: the rolling line speed 12 
is 15 m/min, the feed rate is 15mm/min, the static force is 800 N and the rolling pass is 20. Pulse 13 
power parameters are as follows: the frequency is 3 00 Hz, the duration is 70 μs, the 14 
root-mean-square current density is 3×10 7 A/m 2, the amplitude current density is 2.8×10 8 A/m 2, 15 
the processing temperature is 50 °C. The lubricating oil is mixed with kerosene in th e proportion 16 
of 4:1 for cooling and lubrication [16]. 17

Severe plastic deformation treatments have been con ducted on the single-phase FCC metal (such 20 
as Al, Cu and Ni) to improve its strength or hardne ss [20-22]. Generally, previous studies have 21 
explained this phenomenon according to the Hall–Pet ch relationship. The severe deformation 22 
normally results in grain refinement and these addi tional grain boundaries can effectively block 23 
the dislocation movement and increase the yield str ength or hardness of the material [20, 22]. In 24 
current study, the strengthen mechanisms of HEA processed by USRP/ EP-USRP are similar with 25 
theirs. After severe plastic deformation, the HEAs maintain the FCC phase (see Fig. 4(b)), which 26 
suggesting that there is no phase transformation an d phase transformation strengthening is not the 27 
strengthening mechanisms in USRP/ EP-USRP HEAs. Mea nwhile, as shown in TEM results (see 28 
Fig. 3(c)-(f)), significant grain refinement has be en observed in both the USRP/ EP-USRP HEAs, 29 
which indicating that the higher strength of USRP/ EP-USRP HEAs is mainly resulted by grain 30 
refinement. More interestingly, comparing the micro structure of USRP with EP-USRP HEAs, 31 
different grain refinement approaches are observed, i.e., grains in USRP and EP-USRP HEAs are 32 
mainly subdivided by nano-twin boundaries and dislocation boundaries, respectively. Therefore, it 33

deformation mode and produce the extra strength for USRP HEA, while the grain refinement by 2 
dislocation boundaries contributes to the higher strength of EP-USRP HEA. 3

It has been widely reported that dislocation glide is the main deformation mode of HEA deformed 9 
at normal or low strain rate (such as regular tensi on or compression using normal strain rate) at 10 
room temperature [20, 23]. A more systematic study by G. Laplanche et al. [23] showed that 11 
deformation occurs by dislocation plasticity up to much higher strains and nanotwins were 12 
observed in ~25% tension sample at strain rate of 10 −3 s−1 at ambient temperature. In this case, the 13 
existence of twinning in the surface layer of USRP sample may partly owing to the strain of the 14 
region near to the surface is high enough to form n anotwin. More importantly, previous studies 15 
[24-27] had well established that twining was promo ted by the lower temperature or higher strain 16 
rate and resulted in twinning in FCC metals which c ould not twin because of their relatively high 17 
SFEs. The other important reason for the formation of nanotwins in the USRP samples should be 18 
attributed to the high strain rate, strain velocity and strain gradient of the ultrasonic rolling [28] 19 
which can also promote the twinning. Moreover, in t his study, EP-USRP shows different grain 20 
refinement mechanism compared with USRP, i.e., that is dislocation boundaries strengthening 21 
mechanism. Maybe it’s because of the electric pulse , the HEA will produce instantaneous high 22 
temperature [29] and electro-plasticity [30] under the action of thermal effect and non-thermal 23

## Page 8

effect. On one hand, the instantaneous high temperature reduces the width of the stacking fault and 1 
increases the stacking fault energy. Meanwhile, it is difficult to produce twins and easier for 2 
dislocations to slip. As a result, the dislocation motion become the main form of plastic 3 
deformation. On the other hand, according to the th eory of electrical impulse electron wind [29] 4 
and thermal activation theory [31], the directional movement of electrons provides an additional 5 
force to the dislocation, which promotes the direct ional alignment of dislocations and forms the 6 
dislocation boundaries. The parallel-arrangement di slocations in EP-USRP HEA (yellow box in 7 
Fig. 3(f)) may be direct evidence of these theories. 8 
 9 
In addition, strength–ductility trade-off has been a long-standing dilemma in the metals. As 10 
expected, the ductility of HEA was decreased dramat ically after USRP processed and it was 11 
necked when tension to only ~2.5%. This was mainly because the dislocation movement can be 12 
severely suppressed by the high-density nano-twinning boundaries and stress around the twins are 13 
easier to increase to a critical value and thus ind uce necking and fracture. At present, the 14 
mechanism of the special phenomena of metal materia ls under the action of ultrasonic wave has 15 
not been determined. The following are the views of this paper. When the ultrasonic wave acts on 16 
the metal, the relaxation process will occur in the metal. On the microscopic level, it can 17 
accelerate the thermal movement of electrons outside the nucleus, and on the macroscopic level, it 18 
is showed as energy absorption. Under the joint act ion of the mechanical force, which may be 19 
produced by ultrasonic high frequency vibration, th e change of electronic motion, the external 20 
mechanical force, and etc., the microstructure and mechanical properties of metal are affected. It 21 
has been reported that, under the action of high-fr equency ultrasonic waves, metal materials will 22 
have a softening effect [11], due to the force in N D and auxiliary energy generated by ultrasonic 23 
high-frequency vibration. With the increase of ultr asonic amplitude, the grains are gradually 24 
elongated in the slip direction. In addition, dislo cations can overcome obstacles more easily and 25 
reduce flow stress. The softening effect is mainly manifested in the flow stress during the 26 
processing. [32] When the ultrasonic wave is remove d, the metal returns to the original strength 27 
level.[11] But this effect is very limited, so the USRP HEA sh ows obvious ductility loss. The 28 
improvement of surface hardness of USRP-HEA is mainly due to the refinement of surface grains 29 
and the increase of grain boundaries. Fortunately, with the assistant of electropulsing, EP-USRP 30 
HEA showed a better comprehensive mechanical property with high strength and medium ductility. 31 
This is because the spacing of the dislocation boun daries is larger than the spacing of the twins 32 
and thus dislocation can move for a longer distance and has higher mobility [33], and meanwhile, 33 
the stress of the movable dislocations can be relea sed when they interact with the dislocation 34 
boundaries. Under the joint action of pulse current , ultrasonic wave and rolling on high entropy 35 
alloy, a large number of nano twin grain boundaries disappear, a large number of dislocation 36 
boundaries are generated, and nano crystalline mate rials with approximate gradient are formed, 37 
thus the purpose of controlling the plasticity and toughness of high entropy alloy is achieved. How 38 
the pulse current and ultrasonic wave affect the extranuclear electron motion of high entropy alloy 39 
elements is still needed to be further studied. Bes ides, the elongation increase in EP-USRP HEA 40 
can also be attributed to the electroplastic effect and/or the thermal effect of the electrical curren t. 41 
According to the relevant simulation results, the h eat is concentrated in the contact area between 42 
the tool head and the sample, and the temperature c hanges with the waveform of the electrical 43 
pulse. In a waveform (several microseconds), the temperature rises sharply from room temperature 44

## Page 9

to about 400 , and then drops sharply to room temperature [29]. Similarly, in other 1 
single-principal alloys, such as 304 stainless stee l, the electroplastic effect has also been reported 2 
to improve the ductile [13]. But in EP-USRP of 304 stainless steel, the transformation from 3 
γ-austenite to α-martensite is accelerated by electric pulse. 4

Conclusions 16 
In summary, this study describes the nano surface t reatment of CrMnFeCoNi HEA by EP-USRP 17 
technology. It is shown that, for the first time, t he approximate gradient nanocrystalline material 18 
can be formed in the surface of HEA. The results sh ow that the HEA treated by EP-USRP 19 
produces a large number of defined location grain b oundaries. The distance between location 20 
boundaries is larger than that between twins and it has high mobility. The tensile strength of the 21 
HEA at room temperature is greatly improved by this processing method, and its plasticity can be 22 
effectively controlled. At the same time, this pape r also reveals the mechanism of this 23 
phenomenon in essence. 24
