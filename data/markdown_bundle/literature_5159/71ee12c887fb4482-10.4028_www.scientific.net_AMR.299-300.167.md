Microstructure and High Temperature Mechanical Property of 
Directionally Solidified NiAl-Cr(Mo)-W/Nb Alloy 
Yihui Qi 1,a , Shengnan Ma 1,b and Jianting Guo 2,c 
1School of Materials Science and Engineering, Liaoning University of Technology, Jinzhou 121001 
China; 
2Institute of Metal Research, Chinese Academy of Sciences, Shenyang 110016, China 
aqiyihui65@163.com, bdama312@126.com, cjtguo@imr.ac.cn 
Keywords: NiAl; Directional solidification; Microstructure; Mechanical property 
Abstract. The microstructure, high temperature tensile behavior of the DS NiAl-Cr(Mo)-W/Nb 
alloy have been investigated. The transverse microstructure of the alloy consists typically of 
eutectic colonies of NiAl and Cr(Mo) phase with white phases segregating at the cell boundaries. 
The white phase is possibly Cr(Mo) phase containing large amount of Nb and W elements. The 
longtitudinal microstructure of the alloy is lamellar with the direction basically parallel to the 
directional solidification direction. The tensile yield strength and ultimate strength of the DS 
NiAl-Cr(Mo)-W/Nb alloy are much higher than that of the general cast NiAl-Cr(Mo)-W/Nb alloy, 
and the elongation of the alloy is also higher than that of the general cast alloy. The fracture is 
debonding along phase boundary and cleavage. 
Introduction 
NiAl-based alloy possesses a number of promising properties that make it an attractive 
replacement material for superalloys, however, the low ductility and toughness is a major limiting 
factor as an engineering materials. The investigations that NiAl-refractory metal pseudo-binary 
eutectic alloy were prepared by directional solidification process have attracted a great of attention 
because the fracture toughness was improved [1-6]. Up to now, the NiAl-Mo and NiAl-Cr alloys 
with fiber-like microstructure or the NiAl-Cr(Mo) alloy with lamellar microstructure can be 
obtained by the strictly controlling process of directional solidification, and these kinds of alloys 
possess a better balance between high temperature strength and low temperature toughness[7]. 
However, the strength of these pseudo-binary eutectic alloys are still lower than that of the 
superalloy[1]. It is now well established that transition element addition to NiAl can improve the 
creep-resistance by solid-solution effect and precipitate hardening through the formation of Ni 2AlX 
(β′) Heusler phase precipitates, where X=Ti, Hf, and Zr[8-12]. Therefore, in order to further 
improve the high temperature strength and low temperature toughness of NiAl-based alloys, a 
NiAl-28Cr-5Mo-0.5W-0.5Nb alloy, which was based on the NiAl-28Cr-6Mo with small amount of 
W and Nb elements substituting for Mo element, was prepared by directional solidification process. 
This paper aimed at studying microstructure and high temperature mechanical property of the 
directionally solidified NiAl-Cr(Mo)-W/Nb alloy. 
Experimental procedure 
The NiAl-28Cr-5Mo-0.5W-0.5Nb (at%, denoted as NiAl-Cr(Mo)-W/Nb) alloy was prepared by 
induction melting in vacuum from starting materials of 99.9% Ni, 99.9% Al, 99.5%Cr, 99.5% Mo, 
99.0%W and 99.0% Nb, the cast rod is 36mm in diameter. The directional solidification was 
performed in vacuum at a pulling rate of 5mm/min, using a modified Bridgman method with the 
temperature gradient of 70-80K/cm through the solid and liquid interface. The ingot is 16mm in 
diameter and 150mm in length. Tensile specimens with a gauge section of 2mm×2.5mm×16mm 
were electro-discharge machined, with the length parallel to the direction of directional 
solidification. Tensile tests of DS and cast alloy were performed at the SHIMADZU AG-250KNE 
machine. Specimens for microstructure observation were electropolished in a solution containing

Results and discussion 
Microstructure of the DS NiAl-Cr(Mo)-W/Nb alloy 
Fig.1(a) is the transverse microstructure of the DS NiAl-Cr(Mo)-W/Nb alloy, which is typically 
eutectic colonies of NiAl and Cr(Mo) phase. The eutectic colony is chrysanthemum-like, and the 
size is about 100-200 µm. Fig.1(b) is the longtitudinal microstructure of the DS alloy, which is 
lamellar, i.e. the lamellar Cr(Mo) phases (grey white) distribute along the direction of the 
directional solidification, but in the center section of the sample, the lamellar direction is slightly 
deviated from the direction of the directional solidification because of the solidification process. 
The white phases which was indicated “1” in Fig.1(a) and “2” in Fig.1(b) segerate non-continuously 
in the cell boundary. The EDS result of the white phase indicated “1” in Fig.1(a) is as follow(at.%): 
Al8.40, Cr36.58, Ni21.8, Nb24.79, Mo7.25, W1.18. The detailed analyses indicate that the white 
phases are possibly the Cr(Mo) phases containing large amount of Nb and W elements. It remains to 
be investigated that the white phase is solid solution or compound of Cr, Mo, Nb, W elements. The 
white phase is not Heusler phase ，and also not Laves phase. Detailed microstructure observation 
shows that the fine Cr(Mo) particles precipitate in the NiAl matrix and the fine NiAl particles also 
precipitate in the Cr(Mo) phases.

Tensile property of the DS NiAl-Cr(Mo)-W/Nb alloy 
The high temperature tensile property of the DS NiAl-Cr(Mo)-W/Nb alloy is shown in Table 1. 
The yield strength and ultimate strength of the DS NiAl-Cr(Mo)-W/Nb alloy are much higher than 
that of the general as-cast NiAl-Cr(Mo)-W/Nb alloy, the increasement is over 50%. The elongation 
of the DS alloy is not generally large, but higher than that of the cast alloy at 1253K and 1273K 
under the strain rate of 1.04×10 -3s-1. The strength of the DS NiAl-Cr(Mo)-W/Nb alloy decrease 
generally accompanied with increasing tensile temperature or descending strain rate. At the same 
strain rate, elongation of the DS NiAl-Cr(Mo)-W/Nb alloy is not dependent on the test temperature, 
the reason is not clear . For the comparison, the high temperature tensile properties of the HIPed 
NiAl-33.5Cr-0.5Zr[13] and the DS NiAl-Cr(Mo)-1Hf[14] alloys are also shown in Table 1. At the 
same testing condition, the yield strength and ultimate strength of the DS NiAl-Cr(Mo)-W/Nb alloy 
are higher than that of the HIPed NiAl-33.5Cr-0.5Zr alloy, but the elongation is apparently low. The 
strength of the DS NiAl-Cr(Mo)-W/Nb alloy is lower than that of the DS NiAl-Cr(Mo)-1Hf alloy at 
1253K and 1273K, while is higher than that of the DS NiAl-Cr(Mo)-1Hf alloy at 1373K.

Conclusions 
(1). The transverse microstructure of the DS NiAl-Cr(Mo)-W/Nb alloy is typically eutectic colonies 
of NiAl and Cr(Mo) phase. The eutectic colony is chrysanthemum-like, and the eutectic colony size 
is about 100-200 µm. The white phases which are possibly the Cr(Mo) phases containing large 
amount of Nb and W elements distribute non-continuously in the cell boundaries. The longtitudinal 
microstructure is lamellar with Cr(Mo) phases distributing along the direction of the solidification. 
(2). The tensile yield strength and ultimate strength of the DS NiAl-Cr(Mo)-W/Nb alloy are much 
higher than that of the general cast NiAl-Cr(Mo)-W/Nb alloy, the increasement is over 50%. The 
fracture is debonding along phase boundary and cleavage.
