Ni–Co-Based Wrought Superalloys
Containing High W —Microstructure Design
for a Balance of Properties
Akane Suzuki, Steve J. Buresh, Richard DiDomizio,
Scott M. Oppenheimer, Soumya Nag, Ian M. Spinelli,
P. R. Subramanian, Stephen G. Pope, and Jon C. Schaeffer
Abstract
Ni–Co-based c + c′ superalloys were explored in a wide
range of Co/Ni ratios for designing a wrought superalloy
with a combination of superior high temperature strength
and environmental resistance at temperatures above
927 °C (1700 °F). While alloys with high Co/Ni ratios
possess superior creep resistance, reductions of the Co/Ni
ratio and additions of Fe and B are effective in improving
ductility in the intermediate temperature range and
hold-time low cycle fatigue resistance. A formation of
ﬁne, discrete Co
2W Laves precipitates covering a large
fraction of grain boundaries is responsible for the
improvements. The experimental alloys exhibited excel-
lent oxidation resistance at 982 °C (1800 °F) by forming
a protective alumina scale during cyclic exposure.

Introduction
High temperature capable alloys are key enablers for
improving the ef ﬁciency of gas turbines by allowing oper-
ations at higher temperatures with reduced cooling require-
ments. This study focuses on the development of a new high
temperature sheet alloy for applications above 927 °C
(1700 °F), aiming to exceed the capability of Haynes 282
alloy by 56 °C (100 °F) in both mechanical and environ-
mental properties. Some of the conventionally available
alloys, such as Ren é 41 and Waspaloy, possess creep tem-
perature capabilities higher than that of Haynes 282 [ 1, 2].
However, these alloys lack high temperature environmental
resistance [ 3]. On the other hand, more environmentally
resistant alloys, such as Haynes 188, do not possess suf ﬁ-
cient high temperature mechanical capabilities [ 1]. A new
alloy design approach is required to identify a unique alloy
composition space for achieving the desired combination of
properties.
In our previous study, we identi ﬁed a Co –Ni-based alloy
(Co–30Ni–9.5Cr–4.4Al–13.2W–6.9Ta–0.05C–0.005B–
0.05La, wt.%) with high temperature creep resistance com-
parable to that of conventional equiaxed cast Ni-based
superalloys. The alloy also exhibited superior oxidation
resistance at temperatures up to 1093 °C due to its ability to
form a protective a-alumina (Al
2O3) scale [ 4–6]. The alloy
composition was based on the Co –Al–W ternary alloys
strengthened with c′-Co3(Al,W) precipitates [ 7], where
additions of Ni and Ta were made to improve the stability of
the c′ phase and an addition of Cr was made to promote the
formation of an alumina scale under an oxidizing environ-
ment [ 6, 8]. The alloy was thermo-mechanically processible
and hot-rolled into sheet form using its wide c single-phase
window between its relatively high solidus (1339 °C) and
low c′ solvus (1090 °C) temperatures, which were inherited
from the Co –Al–W ternary system. Despite its attractive
properties, this Co –Ni-based alloy lacked ductility in the
intermediate temperature range, which led to an unaccept-
able fatigue crack growth behavior. The failure was pre-
dominantly intergranular, which indicated insuf ﬁcient grain
boundary strength for the grain interiors strengthened by the
c′ precipitates.
In this study, Ni –Co-based alloys with a wide range of
Co/Ni ratios were explored to design a new high temperature
capable sheet alloy that has a combination of ductility and
resistance to creep, hold-time low cycle fatigue (LCF),

Experimental Procedure
A list of the alloys examined in this study is shown in
Table 1. The ﬁrst six alloys were chosen for evaluating the
effects of the Co/Ni ratio in combination with additions of
Fe. The Co/Ni ratio (at.%/at.%) was varied from 1.19 to
0.47. Based on the alloy NF0 with the Co/Ni ratio 0.86, an
addition of 4 wt.% Fe was made in the alloy NF1 by sub-
stituting Co. The Co/Ni ratio was further reduced in the
alloys NF2 and NF3 by replacing Co with Ni. In the latter
four alloys, a wide range of B additions up to 0.125 wt.%
were made based on the alloys NF2 and NF3. These
experimental alloy chemistries were aimed to have the c′
solvus temperatures and the c′ volume fractions comparable
to those of the alloy N0 using thermodynamic predictions by
Thermo-Calc software. Estimated densities of these alloys
range between 8.8 and 9.1 g/cm
3.
Ingots weighing about 2 kg were made by vacuum
induction melting. The c′ solvus temperatures measured by
differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) were between 1080
and 1100 °C, which were signi ﬁcantly higher than that of
Haynes 282 (997 °C) [ 2]. After homogenization at 1200 °C,
each ingot was hot-rolled to plates approximately 15 and
8 mm thick with a 10% reduction per pass. Intermediate
annealing treatments were applied between the passes at
super-solvus temperatures. The plates were solutioned at
temperatures between 1100 and 1200 °C for 1 h, then aged
at 1000 °C for 10 h, followed by furnace cooling.
Microstructural observations were performed by optical
microscopy (OM) and scanning electron microscopy (SEM).
Electron backscattered diffraction (EBSD) and electron dis-
persive spectroscopy (EDS) were used for phase
identiﬁcation. The c′ volume fractions measured from SEM
images after the solution and aging heat treatments were
approximately 40% in all the alloys examined.
Round bar specimens were machined for tensile, creep
and hold-time LCF tests, and compact tension specimens
were prepared for fatigue crack growth tests. Tensile tests
were performed at temperatures between room temperature
and 982 °C at a strain rate 3.3 /C210
−3 s−1. Creep rupture life
was compared at 927 °C. Hold-time LCF tests were con-
ducted isothermally at 927 °C under a strain-controlled
condition with two-minute hold at a peak compressive strain
(A = −1). A cyclic oxidation test was carried out in a
bottom-loading furnace with a thermal cycle consisting of a
50-minute hold at 982 °C followed by a ten-minute of forced
air cooling outside the furnace. Weight change was period-
ically measured up to 500 cycles.
Results and Discussion
Microstructures
Backscattered electron (BSE) images of the examined alloys
are shown in Fig. 1. After the solution and aging heat
treatments, all alloys exhibited intragranular spherical c′
precipitates 200 –300 nm in diameter (Fig. 1, insets) with
grain size between 20 and 60 lm. The alloys with high
Co/Ni ratios (Fig. 1a–c) showed relatively low amounts of
inter- and intragranular Ta-rich carbides and W-rich borides.
A very small amount of Laves (Co
2W) phase was con ﬁrmed
along the grain boundaries in the alloy N0 by transmission
electron microscopy (TEM). The presence of the
Laves-Co
2W phase is not con ﬁrmed as an equilibrium phase
in the Co –W binary [ 9] and the Co –Al–W[ 10–12] ternary
systems, but the phase was previously observed in other Co –
Ni–Cr–W-based alloys such as L-605 and HS188 [ 13].

Mechanical Properties
Tensile properties of the experimental alloys were compared
at two temperatures, 760 and 871 °C. Figure 3 shows 0.2%
yield strength and elongation as functions of the Co/Ni ratio
and the B content. Reductions in the Co/Ni ratio and addi-
tions of Fe signi ﬁcantly improved tensile elongation at
871 °C from less than 3% to over 20% (Fig. 3c) while
maintaining the 0.2% yield strength at a relatively constant
level (Fig. 3a). In contrast, the elongation at 760 °C
remained at a low level with a minimal improvement in
alloys with lower Co/Ni ratios. A larger addition of B to the
alloy NF2 (Co/Ni ratio = 0.59) proved effective in further
improving the elongation at both 871 and 760 °C without
changing the 0.2% yield strength. The 0.2% yield strength of
these alloys was approximately 300 and 100 MPa greater
than that of Haynes 282 [ 2] at 760 and 871 °C, respectively.
The alloy with 0.125 wt.% B (NF2B3) showed elongation
over 30% at 871 °C and 10% at 760 °C (Fig. 3d). On the
other hand, a large addition of B to the alloy NF3 (Co/Ni
ratio = 0.47) did not improve the elongation. Based on these
results, the presence of relatively large amount of the inter-
granular precipitates appears to improve the elongation at
871 °C (ex. NF2, NF3, and NF2B3). However, at 760 °C,
an improved elongation was only achieved in NF2B3 in
which the intergranular precipitates were predominantly
Laves phase instead of a mixture of Laves and l phases.
Creep and hold-time LCF test results at 927 °C are
summarized in Fig. 4 as functions of the Co/Ni ratio and the
B content. Creep rupture life and hold-time LCF life showed
opposite dependencies on the Co/Ni ratio; longer creep life
was achieved in alloys with higher Co/Ni ratio (Fig. 4a),
while longer hold-time LCF life was obtained in alloys with
lower Co/Ni ratio (Fig. 4c). Minor improvements in both
creep and hold-time LCF life were observed in high B
containing alloys (Figs. 4b and d). Compared with Haynes
282, these experimental alloys showed improved creep and
hold-time LCF resistance. The creep temperature capability
was greater by 28 –83 °C (50 –150 °F) based on a compar-

cross sections of the fatigue crack growth specimens tested

alloys N1 and NF1, crack propagation was mostly inter-
granular, and it led to accelerated crack growth. On the other

Grain boundary coverage with the W-rich precipitates
was measured using image analyses in alloys NF0, NF1,
NF2, and NF2 with variation of B contents up to 1700 ppm
to understand the effects of the size and amount of the
precipitates on the mechanical properties. The grain
boundaries, excluding twin boundaries, were segmented to
0.2 lm wide bands, and the area fraction of the grain
boundary phase was measured within the bands. Grain
boundary phase lineal density was calculated by dividing the
total length of the grain boundaries by the number of grain
boundary precipitates in the bands. Figure 6 shows the
results. A reduction in the Co/Ni ratio from 0.86 (NF0) to
0.59 (NF2) resulted in increases in both parameters by
approximately 6 times (Fig. 6). By increasing the levels of B
to NF2, the grain boundary phase fraction showed a further
increase in a range between 0.08 and 0.15 wt.% B. The grain
boundary lineal phase density also showed an increase with
additions of B greater than 0.05 wt.%, followed by a
decrease beyond 0.15 wt.%. Figure 7 shows tensile elon-
gation plotted against the grain boundary phase fraction
(Fig. 7a) and the grain boundary phase lineal density
(Fig. 7b). These plots clearly show that improved tensile
elongation was achieved in alloys with larger grain boundary
coverage with ﬁner precipitates at both 760 and 871 °C.
Therefore, it is critical to optimize the levels of alloying
elements to form a high-volume fraction of ﬁne, discrete
grain boundary precipitates which are necessary for
achieving improved fracture resistance of grain boundaries.

Summary
Ni–Co-based c + c′ superalloys containing high W were
explored in a wide range of Co/Ni ratios for designing a
wrought superalloy with a combination of superior high
temperature strength and environmental resistance at tem-
peratures above 927 °C. A balance of mechanical properties
can be achieved by optimizing the phase, morphology and
amount of grain boundary precipitates using the Co/Ni ratio,
Fe and B as variables. Increasing the Co/Ni ratio results in
superior creep resistance. Reductions of the Co/Ni ratio and
additions of Fe and B are effective in improving ductility in
the intermediate temperature range and hold-time low cycle
fatigue resistance. A formation of ﬁne, discrete Co
2W Laves
precipitates covering a large fraction of grain boundaries is
responsible for the improvements. Excellent oxidation
resistance was demonstrated in the experimental alloys at
982 °C due to the protective alumina scale formation during
high temperature exposure.
