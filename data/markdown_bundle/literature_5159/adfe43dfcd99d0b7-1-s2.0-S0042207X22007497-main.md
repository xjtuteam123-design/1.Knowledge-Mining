ABSTRACT 
Novel homogeneous and compact W–Ta-Re alloys with different concentrations of Ta were fabricated by spark 
plasma sintering. The effect of Ta concentration on the microstructure and mechanical properties of W-xTa-10 wt 
%Re (x = 0, 10, 20, and 30 wt%) alloys was studied. The single body centered cubic (BCC) phase W–Ta-Re matrix 
is formed by solid solution, and the grains are effectively refined with the addition of Ta. Attributing to the grain 
refinement and solution strengthening, the alloys exhibit excellent strength and good ductility. The average grain 
size reduces to 6.7 ± 2.5 μm after the addition of 30 wt% Ta, whereas it is 17.3 ± 5.5 μm for the W–Re matrix 
without the addition of Ta. In addition, its yield strength increased to 1248.8 ± 13.2 MPa, an enhancement by 
~183%. The ultimate compressive strength and strain rate for alloys are greater than ~2940 MPa and ~42%, 
respectively. With increasing Ta content, the matrix deteriorates because of hard oxides, causing transgranular 
cleavage fracture. The preferred orientation of grains along the compression direction is < 111> and <001>
after deformation. The deformed structure showed several short curved and entangled dislocations, guaranteeing 
better mechanical properties. This research is of great significance for the strengthening and toughening of 
tungsten materials. 
1. Introduction 
Tungsten is extensively used in modern industry, national defense, 
and high technological fields attributing to its high density, high 
strength, high hardness, good electrical and thermal conductivity [1–3]. 
Besides, it is also considered as a candidate for plasma facing wall ma -
terials owing to its low sputtering yield and low neutron activation in 
fusion environments [4–6]. However, its low temperature brittleness, 
recrystallization brittleness and irradiation brittleness as well as high 
ductile-brittle transition temperature (DBTT) [7,8] have greatly limited 
its use in some demanding applications. The current reports on tungsten 
materials mainly focus on reducing the intrinsic brittleness and 
improving mechanical properties [9], and the effectiveness of alloying 
with other alloys has also been established. 
Over the years, many alloying elements such as niobium, vanadium, 
rhenium, tantalum, and titanium have been investigated [10–12]. A 
large number of researchers reported that alloying with Re improved the 
dislocation mobility of ½<111> [13,14]. Romaner et al. also showed 
that the addition of Re can transform the screw dislocations from 
symmetrical to asymmetric structure, and then reduce the Peierls stress 
and shear resistance based on Density Functional Theory (DFT) [15]. 
Compared to pure W, W–Re alloys exhibit better ductility and fracture 
toughness, and a small amount of Re significantly reduces the DBTT of 
the materials [16–18]. Kappacher et al. reported a decrease in the 
activation energy in W-10 wt% Re alloy through nanoindentation ex -
periments [19]. However, the addition of Re has a limited effect on 
improving the strength of materials. 
The present researches about solid solution tungsten-based materials 
mainly concentrate on binary alloys. Whether it can create an amazing 
superimposed effect when multiple elements interact, such as traditional 
steel materials and new high-entropy alloys [20,21], remains to be 
studied. Ta has high toughness, low activation energy, and the same 
crystal structure as tungsten and it can also achieve an infinite solid 
solution, therefore, W–Ta alloys have also been extensively studied 
recently [22–25]. Liu et al. calculated that the Pugh’s shear-to-bulk 
modulus ratio (G/B) value decreased rapidly with high Ta concentra -
tion in solid solution, indicating the increase in ductility [26]. Experi -
mental studies indicate that the addition of Ta can help to refine the

Based on the synergistic effect of Ta and Re, the formation of a ho -

optimize the microstructure and mechanical properties of tungsten- 
based materials. Based on previous research [19], in this study,

behavior of ternary or even multi-component tungsten systems can be 
different from that of binary alloys. The prospects of structural materials 
and fusion applications in the future are of great practical significance. 
Spark plasma sintering (SPS) has the peculiarity of fast heating rate, 
short sintering time and rapid cooling [29]. Therefore, in this study, the 
novel W–Ta-Re alloys were fabricated by SPS to rapidly achieve 
compactness and homogeneity. The effect of Ta concentration on the 
microstructure and mechanical properties was investigated. The 
deformed microstructure and strengthening mechanism of the alloys are 
also discussed.

2.2. Experimental 
Different compositions of W, Ta and Re powders were initially mixed 
by three-dimensional mixing machine for 10 h in an atmosphere of 
argon to prevent oxidation. The morphology of the mixed powder is 
shown in Fig. 1d, indicating uniform distribution of powder. Then, the 
mixed powder was placed in cylindrical graphite dies with a diameter of 
15 mm for pre-pressing. Finally, they were consolidated in vacuum in a 
SPS apparatus (LABOX-325R, Japan). The axial pressure of the mixed 
powder was 40 MPa, and it was heated to 2000 
◦C at a heating rate of 
100 ◦C/min, and then dwelled at sintering temperature for 10 min. Fig. 2 
shows the precise processing diagram for compression and microstruc -
ture specimens. To avoid the impact of surface carburization, the sam -
ples were taken from the center of the sintered alloy. 
2.3. Characterization 
The microstructure of commercial powder, the mixed powder, sin -
tered alloys, and fracture of samples were characterized by Scanning 
electron microscopy (FEI quanta 250 FEG). The phase analysis of alloys 
was characterized by X-ray diffraction (Brooke company advance D8). 
The EBSD images of original sintered and deformed samples were 
measured by cold field emission scanning electron microscopy (Regulus 
8230). The actual density of sintered alloys was measured by the 
Archimedes drainage method. The compression properties of the alloy 
were investigated using a universal testing machine (Instron 3369, 
America). The Vickers hardness of samples was measured by micro -
hardness machine (Shimadzu HMV-2T). The TEM images of the samples 
were obtained by spherical aberration corrected transmission electron 
microscopy (Titan G2).

3.2. Microstructure characterization 
Fig. 4 shows the inverse pole figure (IPF) maps of sintered alloys with 
different compositions, indicating that the shapes of most grains are

3.3. Mechanical properties 
Fig. 8a shows the compressive stress-strain curve for W–Ta-Re alloys 
at room temperature. The magnified view of local area clearly shows 
that the yield strength of the alloy obviously increases with increasing 
concentration of Ta. The alloy with 30 wt% Ta content exhibited a yield 
strength of 1248.8 ± 13.2 MPa, increase by ~183% compared to that of 
Ta0 alloy, indicating that Ta has a certain strengthening effect on the 
materials, most probably attributing to the combined effect of lattice 
distortion and grain refinement while forming solid solution with more 
Ta atoms. A summary of detailed performance parameters of the sam
-
ples is listed in Table 5. Moreover, the strain hardening rate of sintered 
alloys is generally high. Due to the large atomic radius of Ta, lattice 
distortion occurred after solid solution, which made it easier for a large 
number of dislocations to proliferate and gradually become entangled 
when the alloy was axially compressed, promoting the plastic defor -
mation resistance accordingly. As illustrated in Fig. 8a, the alloys con-
taining 10 wt% and 20 wt% Ta maintained good ductility and did not 
fracture after compression, while Ta30 alloy fractured at a strain of 
~42%, revealing that the alloys within a certain amount of Ta exhibited 
both good ductility and strength. The fracture morphology in Fig. 9

3.4. Microstructure analysis after deformation 
The microstructure of the alloys after compressive deformation was 
analyzed to illustrate the strengthening and toughening mechanism. 
Ta10 alloy has less hard oxides and exhibits good properties; therefore, 
it was taken as an example to discuss. 
Fig. 10a shows the Inverse pole figure (IPF) image of Ta10 alloy from 
the cross-section surface after compression at room temperature, indi
-

long strip shape after deformation, and the average grain width reduce 
to 3.7 ± 1.0 
μm, as indicated in Fig. 10b. The main slip direction of W is

these slip directions. Therefore, the grains have an obvious preferred

rate forging bulk pure W [34].

grains. This result is consistent with the results of EBSD analysis before 
and shows that the deformation coordination ability of matrix is strong.

4. Conclusions 
In conclusion, novel W-xTa-10 wt%Re (x = 0, 10, 20, and 30 wt%) 
alloys were prepared by SPS. The effect of Ta concentration on the 
microstructure and mechanical properties of the sintered alloy and the 
microstructure of the alloy after compression deformation at room 
temperature were analyzed. The key conclusions of this study are 
summarized as follows: 
1. W-xTa-10 wt%Re (x = 0, 10, 20, and 30 wt%) alloys present a stable 
single-phase BCC structure through thermodynamic calculation and 
experiments. The addition of Ta significantly refines the grain size 
because Ta has lower atomic diffusion activation energy than W, 
providing more nucleation sites during the formation of sintered 
necks. 
2. The solid dissolved processes of Ta atoms cause lattice distortion. 
With increased Ta content, the strength and hardness of the alloys 
increase continuously affected by solution strengthening and grain 
refinement mechanism, and the strength is improved by ~183% 
compared without the addition of Ta. When the content of Ta ex
-
ceeds 30 wt%, excessive hard oxides lead to the fracture of the alloy 
due to their poor interfacial bonding with the matrix. 
3. The microstructure of the alloy after deformation shows obvious 
preferred orientation. During the process of deformation, the density 
of dislocation and the interaction between the dislocations increase, 
contributing to the improvement of strength. The solute atoms alter 
the local interatomic bonding strength, and a large number of screw 
dislocations and cross-slip activate, maintaining good ductility. 
Therefore, the introduction of Ta and Re provide alloys excellent 
mechanical properties consequently.
