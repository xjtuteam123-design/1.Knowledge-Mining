ABSTRACT
The strain field near the γ / γ ′ interface has a significant influence on the morphology evolution and the me -
chanical properties of Ni-based single crystal superalloys. The present work precisely determines the strain field 
near the γ / γ ′ interface in a Ni-Al binary model single crystal superalloy by a combined usage of convergent beam 
electron diffraction (CBED) and high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM) method. At positions 
slightly away from the γ / γ ′ interface, the variation of lattice is sensitively measured by using standard CBED 
method. Within the range of about 10 nm close to the γ / γ ′ interface, which is difficult to obtain a clear HOLZ 
pattern through traditional CBED solely, by using the CBED-calibrated HRTEM results, the complete lattice 
distortion and strain field are obtained. There is compressive strain in the γ ′ phase and tensile strain in the γ 
phase, and the lattice strain affects a range as long as 80 nm near γ / γ ′ interface. The strain distribution in the two 
phases is asymmetric, with the strain field in the γ phase obviously larger than in the γ ′ phase. Our results provide 
important fundamental data for understands on the relationship between microstructure evolution and prop -
erties of Ni-based single crystal superalloys.
1. Introduction
Nickel-based single crystal superalloys are widely used for manu -
facture of aero-engine turbine blades because of their excellent me -
chanical properties under high temperature [ 1 , 2 ]. The typical 
microstructure of nickel-based single crystal superalloys is the ordered 
L1
2 
structure γ ′ phases coherently precipitated in the face-centered cubic 
(FCC) γ matrix [ 3 ]. To bridge the gap between the lattice constants of the 
two phases [ 4 ], the phase with a higher lattice constant is compressed, 
while the phase with a smaller lattice constant is pulled. The strain field 
near the γ / γ ′ interface has a significant influence on the morphology 
evolution during service and the mechanical properties [ 5 – 7 ]. Some 
researchers found that the lattice mismatch had a strong effect on the 
morphology of γ ′ phase, where the spherical morphology would trans -
form into cubic when the lattice mismatch increased [ 8 , 9 ]. Tien et al. 
found that the interfacial strain status would strongly affect the rafting 
behavior of Udimet-700 nickel-based single crystal superalloy. With the 
existence of negative mismatch, the N type rafting which stands for the 
rafting was occurred normal to the load direction would occur in the 
superalloy under tensile stress and P(Parallel) type rafting would occur 
under compressive stress. However, if positive mismatch was in the 
alloy, the rafting behavior was reversed [ 10 ]. Other researchers sug -
gested that superalloys with higher lattice mismatch performed better in 
creep resistance [ 11 – 13 ]. Zhang J X et al. found that the TMS-138 
commercial superalloy with a relatively high negative mismatch 
exhibited a better creep resistance than TMS-75 superalloy with a lower 
mismatch. The high lattice mismatch can enhance the formation of fine 
dislocation networks inhibiting the dislocation cut into γ ′ precipitates, 
while small mismatch can promote the dislocation glide in early stage of 
creep [ 12 , 13 ]. Therefore, it is of great significance to study the lattice 
distortion and strain distribution near γ / γ ′ interface in single crystal 
superalloys.

X-ray diffraction, neutron diffraction, and convergent beam electron 
diffraction (CBED) are methods that can be selected to measure the 
lattice changes and obtain the lattice strain in crystallized materials 
[ 14 – 20 ]. In contrast with the other two methods, CBED can accurately 
measure the local strain since the extremely small spot size of the 
electron beam as low as nanometer scale. For example, Grossmann et al. 
found the thermal strain in the opposite direction to the initial co -
herency strains in aged nickel-based single crystal superalloys [ 21 ]. Lu Z 
et al. found a significant difference in elastic strain between horizontal 
and vertical γ channels in single crystal nickel-based superalloys during 
tensile creep [ 22 ]. They attribute the formation of rafting to the reversed 
elastic strain condition in horizontal γ channels from compressive to 
tensile. Moreover, they found that the uniformly distributed strain field 
changes after deformation, which may lead to the coarsening of γ ′ phase. 
Using CBED method, the lattice stain change at different positions can be 
experimentally obtained through multi-point measurement.
In the present work, the lattice near the γ / γ ′ interface in the Ni-Al 
binary model single crystal superalloy is measured by combined using 
of CBED and high-resolution transmission electron microscopy 
(HRTEM) methods. With the combination, the limit of the standard 
CBED method which the severely distorted local lattice cannot provide 
enough periodicity to form clear experimental diffraction patterns can 
be overcome and the complete information of lattice distortion and 
strain field near the γ / γ ′ interface is obtained. Our results provide 
important fundamental data for understanding the relationship between 
microstructure evolution and properties in the service process.

High-order Laue zone (HOLZ) line patterns near the γ / γ ′ interface are 
acquired using a FEI Talos F200X transmission electron microscope, 
with the spot size of the convergent beam of about 2.5 nm. HOLZ pattern 
simulations are carried out on MacTempasX software. The HRTEM im -

electron microscope at an acceleration voltage of 300 kV.

The location of the HOLZ lines in the central bright-field diffraction 
disc is influenced by the wavelength λ of the incident electron beam and 
the lattice constants a , b and c of the material [ 24 ]. Once the electron 
beam wavelength λ is determined, the variation of HOLZ patterns can 
indicate the lattice distortion. Usually, there is a certain difference be -
tween the nominal acceleration voltage and the actual acceleration 
voltage of electron microscope. Therefore, the actual acceleration 
voltage of the electron microscope must be accurately measured before 
CBED experiments. In this work, the acceleration voltage of transmission 
electron microscope used in CBED tests is corrected to 199.50 kV. More 
details are given in our previous paper [ 25 ].

3. Results
3.1. Lattice constants of γ ′ phase and γ phase without distortion
Fig. 3 gives the HOLZ patterns under the [114] zone axis at points X 
and Y shown in Fig. 1 . Both patterns are symmetrical, indicating no 
tetragonal distortion occurrence. Fig. 4 gives a series of simulated HOLZ 
patterns of FCC structures with lattice constants from 0.3530 nm to 
0.3570 nm. The length of line segment AB is sensitive to the change of 
lattice constant. By calculating the ratio of AB ’ s length and the diameter 
of the diffraction disk, and comparing the experimental and simulated 
patterns, the lattice constant of Ni-Al binary model single crystal su -
peralloy is measured to be 0.3561 nm for γ ′ phase and 0.3553 nm for γ 
phase. In addition, we have also measured lattice constants of Ni-Al 
binary model superalloy at some other randomly selected positions to 
validate this result. As shown in Table 1 , the results fluctuate yet 
consistent with the above result. Therefore, the selected region is 
representative for further characterization.

4. Discussion
Since only a relatively weak angular distortion appears in the CBED 
results shown in Section 3.2 , only the distortion of the lattice spacing is 
further considered in the present study. The small angular distortions 
are not taken into further accounts. Combining the measurement results 
of CBED and HRTEM, a lattice strain distribution near γ / γ ′ interface is 
given in Fig. 10 (a). The strain is not symmetrically distributed, and the 
width of strain field in γ phase is obviously higher than that in the γ ′ 
phase.
Fig. 10 (b) shows a two-dimensional schematic diagram of lattice 
plane near the γ / γ ′ interface. The γ / γ ′ interface is parallel to the di -
rections of a (named as [100]) and c (named as [001]) and perpendic -
ular to the direction of b (named as [010]). Although there is a difference 
in the lattice constants between γ ′ phase and γ phase, the atoms near the 
coherent interface have to match with each other, which leads to this 
deformation of lattice. The lattice parameters a and c in the γ phase are 
increased by the tensile stresses, while a and c in the γ ′ phase are reduced 
by compressive stresses. The forced matching gives the peak strain at the 
phase interface, and the lattice strain decreases with the increasing of

Srinivasan et al. came up with the definition of γ / γ ′ interface width, 
which the interface is not only the boundary of the order-disorder 
transformation between the two phases, but also a transition area in 
the chemical composition. By combining Cs-corrected high-resolution 
STEM and 3DAPT tomography, they found the interface width in Rene 
88DT commercial superalloy to be around 2 nm thick, both on the order- 
disorder aspect and the chemical aspect [ 31 , 32 ]. Zhang H et al. inves -
tigated the γ / γ ′ interface in second generation single crystal superalloy 
CMSX-4 by performing precise intensity ratio analysis on HRTEM re -
sults, and found a similar result that the interface width is around 2 nm 
[ 33 ]. However, in the present work, CBED results shows exactly that the 
strain field can spread into the two phases to a much larger range. This 
gives another insight to the form of the phase interface. The distorted 
interface may have an effect on releasing stress under loads from parallel 
direction, which may have a positive effect on the elastic properties of 
the alloy. Moreover, as previous researches showed, the distorted 
interface can enhance the formation of dislocation network which is 
vital for creep resistance of the alloy. The long range of distorted lattice 
distribution can provide increased number of sites for dislocation 
nucleation.
In particular, we find that the strain gradient is lower closer to the 
γ / γ ′ interface, which is even smaller than the strain gradient in remote 
areas. In our consideration, it is speculated that the variation of elements 
reconciles strains in this area due to the influence of composition tran -
sition near the phase interface. Based on the lattice strain distribution 
near the γ / γ ′ interface obtained in this paper, a more accurate atomic 
structure model of the interface area can be built later.
5. Conclusions
Based on an accurate measurement of acceleration voltage of 
transmission electron microscope, the lattice distortion and strain field 
near the γ / γ ′ interface in a Ni-Al binary model single crystal superalloy 
are accurately characterized based on CBED technology. In the area 
within 10 nm to the γ / γ ′ interface where HOLZ patterns cannot be ob -
tained, we make a supplementary analysis through HRTEM images. The 
following conclusions can be summarized. 
(1) The changes of characteristic lines in HOLZ pattern reflect that 
the lattices of the two phases are distorted near the γ / γ ′ interface 
due to the lattice mismatch. There is compressive strain in the γ ′ 
phase and tensile strain in the γ phase. The influence range of 
lattice mismatch stress is as long as 80 nm.
