In the past two decades, multi-principal element alloys (MPEAs) 
(also called medium/high-entropy alloys) have been gaining increasing 
interest. The most investigated face-centered cubic (FCC) single-phase 
MPEAs are the CoNi-based MPEAs (such as CoCrNi, CoNiV and CoCr -
FeMnNi alloys) due to their excellent ductility, fatigue and fracture 
toughness [1 – 7] , as well as good oxidation and wear resistance [8] . 
However, due to the single-phase, the yield strength of FCC MPEAs (e.g., 
~360 MPa for CoCrNi [3] ) is often inadequate at room temperature 
(RT), limiting their wide applications. Therefore, many dedicated efforts 
have been put on further improving strength of CoNi-based MPEAs, such 
as by introducing secondary phase (e.g., B2 phase and L1
2 
phase by 
adding Al and Ti elements [ 9 , 10 ]) and by tailoring heterogeneous mi -
crostructures [ 11 , 12 ]. However, these methods may cause dramatic 
sacrifice of in-serve properties (such as ductility and fatigue) due to 
localized cracking from the interface between phases or heterogeneous 
structures [ 13 , 14 ]. 
In contrast, solute atoms and grain boundaries are two potent me -
diums that can be used to strengthen FCC MPEAs without sacrificing 
much ductility. For instance, Zhang et al. [15] designed Al/Ta co-doped 
CoCrNi MPEA with ~600 MPa yield strength and ~50 % ductility at RT. 
Recently, by introducing 8 % Mo content, Jang et al. [16] developed 
CoNi-based MPEAs with ~686 MPa yield strength and sufficient 
ductility. By further increasing the amount of Mo content (to 15 %) and 
utilizing additional high-pressure torsion (HPT) treatment, the yield 
strength is increased to ~816 MPa [16] . The addition of Mo content was 
reported to introduce severe lattice distortion and reduce stacking fault 
energy (that promotes grain boundary strengthening) [16] . Apart from 
the Mo, the addition of 3 % refractory W was also reported to signifi -
cantly improve the strength of CoCrNi alloy. However, it remains un -
clear whether the synergistic addition of refractory Mo and W could 
further improve the combination of mechanical properties of 
CoNi-based MPEAs.
