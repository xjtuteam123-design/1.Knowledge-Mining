ABSTRACT: 
The microstructure evolu tion of bi -crystal samples during directional solidification 
was characterized to understand the effect of secondary dendrite orientation on the 
competitive growth of converging dendrites of Ni -based superalloys. The results 
showed that the secondary dendrite orientation effected obviously the needed critical 
withdrawal rate in which the bi-crystal coexisted during competitive growth. And, the 
needed lowest critical withdrawal rate was obtained when the secondary dendrite 
orientation was perpendicular to th e grain boundary of bi -crystal samples. When the 
secondary dendrite orientation was increased to 45°, the needed critical withdrawal 
rate for competitive growth was largest. Because the secondary dendrite orientation 
effected the spatial arrangement of den drites with respect to the grain boundary in 
three dimensions space, the insertion between unfavorably oriented dendrites and 
favorably oriented dendrites and the corresponding lateral motion were believed as 
the main mechanism for overgrowth based on the experimental investigation.

1. Introduction 
With the development of aviation engines and industrial gas turbines, the Ni -based 
single crystal superalloys has become the preferred materials of turbine engine blades 
due to the good high temperature strength and creep resistance [1]. <001> direction is 
confirmed as a fast -growing dendritic orientation and a preferred orientation with the 
most favorable mechanical properties compared with other orientations in Ni -based 
single crystal superalloys [2, 3]. Therefore, the directional solidification technology 
assisted by grain selection method is widely used to produce turbine blades of 
Ni-based single crystal superalloys. For the grain selection in starter block, the 
competitive growth is a key to obtain well-orientated single crystal superalloys during 
directional solidification [4,5], therefore, it is a dominating factor to determine the 
quality of the single crystal superalloys. Understanding for the microstructure 
evolution during the competitive growth is of extreme importance. 
A generally accep ted model for competitive growth is proposed by Walton and 
Chalmers in 1959 [6], which schematically summarized by Rappaz et al. using the 
transparent organic film experiment in 1994 [7,8]. This model believed that the 
dendrite tip of UO grain impinged upo n the side of FO dendrite at the converging 
grain boundary and then it was stopped. Since FO grain did not develop new dendrites 
at the grain boundary and the dendrites in UO grain could not overgrow the dendrites 
in FO grain so that the grain boundary was parallel to the dendrites in FO grain. In 
2008, Zhou et al. found that the UO grain could overgrow the FO grain at the lower

withdrawal rate while the FO grain overgrew the UO grain at the higher withdrawal 
rate [9][10][11]. It is clear that the withdrawa l rate effects on the competitive growth 
between UO and FO dendrites and there may be a needed critical withdrawal rate for 
competitive growth. Steinbach found that a stable growth solution should be 
established and the band primary spacing effected the un dercooling of dendrite tip 
when the growth of dendrites array got into steady state [12]. In 2012, Li et al. 
showed that unusual overgrowth only occurred when the spacing between the FO 
grain boundary dendrite and its immediate FO neighbor decreased to a c ertain level 
through lateral motion [13][14]. In 2017, Tourret et al. believed that the grain 
boundary orientation selection depended strongly on secondary dendrite orientation 
(SDO), namely the azimuthal angles of rotation of the dendrites around their pr imary 
axis, during thin -sample directional solidification of converging dendrites [15]. 
However, it should be noted that these analyses above for competitive growth are 
mainly built in two dimension (2D) or quasi -2D behavior, the competitive growth in 
metal bulk materials is actually more complex. 
As we all know, the primary dendrite orientation and the SDO are randomly 
distributed in three dimension (3D) space, therefore, this complex competitive growth 
process cannot be fully described by these models abo ve established in 2D. 
Unfortunately, many studies only focused on the effect of primary dendrite orientation 
on the competitive growth [16][17][18], little attention was paid on the SDO. Actually, 
the SDO played also an important role in complex competitive growth. Recently, Liu 
et al. found that the competitive growth depended on secondary dendrites

perpendicular to the grain boundary of bi -crystal samples, rather than primary 
dendrites parallel to the thermal gradient as generally recognized in Ni -based

unclear in the case of converging dendrites. 
Therefore, this work focused on the effect of SDO on competitive growth of 
converging dendrites in Ni -based bi -crystal samples using a needed critical 
withdrawal rate as a criterion, and further try to explore the mechanism of competitive 
growth in metal bulk materials.

3.2 Microstructure observation at longitudinal section in two dimension plane 
In order to observe the competitive growth process along the directional solidification 
direction, some quenched microstructures of the longitudinal section with different

was concluded that the insertion should affect the competitive growth in converging 
bi-crystal and it was unfavorable for the survival of FO dendrite s when SDO of FO

4. Discussion 
4.1 Mechanism of competitive growth of converging dendrites 
Based on the experimental results, it may be concluded that the competitive growth 
of converging dendrites actually took place in a 3-D space not in the 2-D longitudinal 
plane as described in previous models. Competitive gr ain growth mainly depended 
on block from dendrites related with the position of dendrite tip according to 
previous models in 2 -D space. However, UO dendrites could insert into the gap of 
FO dendrites in the metal bulk materials. Therefore, the block from dendrites was not 
the determinedly factor during competitive growth of converging dendrites. In fact, 
in order to obtain a more reasonable space distribution for a stable state, the primary 
dendrites arrangement were well -proportioned in 3 -D plane at grain boundary to 
avoid facing each other. Therefore, Fig. 6 showed a schematic diagram for the 
competitive growth of dendrites in 3 -D space, where a UO dendrite B2 inserted into 
the gap of FO dendrites. However, not all arrangement of dendrites at grain boundary 
were staggered as shown by dendrites A1 and B1 in Fig. 6, where UO and FO 
dendrites were arranged face to face. When UO dendrite B1 grew approaching to FO 
dendrite A1, lateral motion of A1 underwent two stages during competitive growth 
according to the r esults of simulation [13,20]. Firstly, FO dendrite A1 moved 
laterally towards UO dendrite B1 as A1 was far away from B1. Secondly, FO 
dendrite A1 moved laterally to depart from UO dendrite B1 when they were too

lateral motion of FO dendrite A1, resulting in the lateral motion of A1 towards FO

move forward to FO grain, the density of dendrites array would increase while the 
dendrite spacing would get smaller near the grain boundary. In order to adjust 
dendrite spacing to the range of primary dendrite spacing for a stable state, some 
dendrites would be eliminated to increase dendrite spacing [21]. The UO grain 
boundary dendrites could be replaced by their UO neighbors during competitive

4.2 Effect of secondary dendrites orientation 
As analyzed in section 4.1, the insertion between U O and FO dendrites and the 
corresponding lateral motion were believed as the main mechanism for competitive 
growth by changing dendrite spacing near the grain boundary. In order to adjust 
dendrite spacing to a stable range, the development of new dendrite or the 
elimination of existing dendrite would occur resulting in overgrowth during 
competitive growth. The 3 -D spatial arrangement of dendrites with respect to the 
grain boundary was related with SDO, resulting in the difference of dendrites 
insertion and overgrowth, as shown in Fig. 4.

5. Conclusions 
The effect of second dendrite orientations on the competitive converging grain growth 
of Ni-based bi-crystal superalloys were investigated. With the increased of the second
