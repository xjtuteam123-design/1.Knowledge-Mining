1. Introduction 
High entropy alloy (HEA) and their derivative medium entropy alloy 
(MEA), which comprise multi-principal metallic elements, have attrac -
ted extensive attention in the past decade [ 1 – 3 ]. Hereinto, the 
face-centered cubic (fcc) phase CoCrFeMnNi HEA and the CoCrNi MEA 
are the most attractive, because of their exceptional combination of 
strength and ductility over a wide range of temperatures [ 2 – 5 ]. How -
ever, the relatively low strength compared with the conventional 
fcc-metals such as Nickel-based alloys and Cobalt-based alloys is the 
main drawback restricting the practical applications of the HEAs and 
MEAs. Nowadays, several methods were utilized for improving their 
strength. The regulation of stacking fault energy (SFE) is one of the 
methods [ 6 – 10 ], in which the mechanical twinning and fcc → hcp 
(hexagonal close-packed) martensitic transformation are promoted by 
reducing the SFE. Both of them contribute to the increment in strain 
hardening rates and tensile strength, known as the twinning-induced 
plasticity (TWIP) effect and transformation-induced plasticity (TRIP) 
effect [ 7 – 12 ]. However, the precipitate-free TWIP or TRIP HEAs and 
MEAs often exhibit inadequate yield strength, which is another impor -
tant performance index of materials. 
As is known, precipitation-strengthening and grain-refinement are 
two of the effective methods for enhancing the yield strength of metals 
and alloys. Quite recently, a series of nanosized coherent L1
2
-(Co, Ni, 
Cr)
3
(Ti, Al)-type precipitates-strengthened MEAs were proposed 
[ 13 – 16 ]. The minor-addition of strong γ
′
-phase formers (Ti, Al) into the 
CoCrFeNi or CoCrNi MEAs leads to the formation of highly-coherent 
intermetallic L1
2
-nanoparticles, which could increase the yield 
strength and ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of the matrix by ~ 70% and 
~40% respectively [ 13 , 14 ]. The spherical L1
2

extraordinary thermal stability even at 800 
◦
C due to their small inter -
facial energy and the sluggish diffusion of elements in the system, where 
the coarsening rate is much lower than that in the conventional Ni-based 
alloys [ 17 , 18 ]. At a temperature above 850 
◦
C, the precipitates will be 
re-dissolved into the matrix forming a single fcc-phase [ 19 ]. It was re -
ported that the particles could be formed via either homogenous or 
heterogeneous nucleation, where the latter mechanism is preferred in 
the region with a high lattice strain such as grain boundaries [ 13 ]. The 
dynamic recrystallization and grain refinement have been recently 
investigated by hot compression [ 19 ]. However, the influence of grain 
refinement on the precipitation characteristics and mechanical proper -
ties of the MEAs has not been clarified. 
On the other hand, rolling is one of the widely utilized severe plastic 
deformation techniques for fabricating ultrafine-grained/ 
nanostructured metals and alloys with high strength [ 20 – 23 ]. The 
decrease of rolling temperature aids the storage of dislocations and leads 
to an enhancement of grain refinement, owing to the suppression of 
thermally activated recovery of dislocations [ 24 – 26 ]. Furthermore, a

4. Discussion 
4.1. The influence of pre-strain on the annealed grain structures and 
precipitates 
In the present study, the grains were refined and homogenized at a 
larger pre-strain (75% rolling) compared with that at a small pre-strain 
(25% and 50% rolling). The grains were refined mainly by recrystalli -
zation during aging at 800 
◦
C for 2 h, which is a process driven by the 
deformation-induced stored energy. The stored energy is almost linearly 
proportional to dislocation density, i.e., the prior strain level [ 35 ]. Thus, 
a higher strain could lead to a larger amount of stored energy, i. e, 
driving force for the recrystallization. The recrystallization is progressed 
by nucleation and growth of the new grains. According to the laws of 
recrystallization [ 36 , 37 ], the recrystallized grain sizes are primarily 
dependent on prior strain levels, which are smaller for a large amount of 
pre-deformation and insensitive to the aging temperature. It implies that 
the number of nuclei or the nucleation rate rather than the growth rate is 
more significantly affected by the pre-strain, in which a larger amount of 
nuclei per unit volume will be provided after pre-strain to a higher level 
resulting in a smaller recrystallized grain size [ 38 ].

On the other hand, the pre-straining-introduced stored energy not 
only stimulated the recrystallization but also promoted the formation of

The precipitation depends on both thermodynamical and kinetical 
mechanisms. According to the phase transformation theories [ 48 – 50 ], 
the homogeneously coherent nucleation and the subsequent growth 
process could not relieve the strain energy associated with dislocations. 
However, the dislocations could promote the incoherent nucleation of 
precipitates, in which the elimination of dislocations or the interaction 
between the dislocations and precipitates reduces the energy barrier and 
the transformation volume strain energy of nucleation [ 29 – 31 , 49 , 50 ]. 
The dislocation strain energy eliminated by the nuclei provides the 
nucleation-required free energy, which is inversely proportional to the 
radial distance ( l ) from the nuclei to dislocation lines [ 48 ]. Therefore, 
the incoherent nucleation preferred to occur close to the dislocations.

5. Conclusions 
In the present study, the mechanical property of the representative 
precipitation-strengthened (CoCrNi)
94
Ti
3
Al
3 
medium entropy alloy was 
significantly enhanced by pre-straining followed by aging. The influence 
of pre-strain on the grain structure and the precipitate characteristic 
were investigated and discussed. The tensile properties at room tem -
perature were measured and compared. The following conclusions were 
drawn from the present study: 
1. The grains were refined and homogenized by cold/cryo pre-rolling 
and subsequent aging, and a larger pre-strain results in a more 
refined grain structure. Decreasing the pre-rolling temperature from 
25 
◦
C to 50 
◦
C has a slight effect on the aged grain structures. The 
grain was refined to ~2 μ m by a 75% pre-rolling and subsequent 
aging at 800 
◦
C for 2 h. 
2. Abundant heterogeneous precipitates were formed by aging the pre- 
strained alloys, in which the incoherent σ -phase and the coherent 
L1
2
-phase were formed concurrently. The pre-strain-introduced 
dislocations promoted heterogeneous precipitation. It is quite 
different from the aging of dislocation-free (CoCrNi)
94
Ti
3
Al
3 
alloy, in 
which only the L1
2
-type precipitates were obtained. 
3. Both the yield strength and ultimate tensile strength were improved 
significantly, owing to the grain refinement and abundant hetero -
geneous precipitates. A larger pre-strain and a lower pre-deformation 
temperature contribute to the increment of strength more efficiently. 
The yield strength and ultimate tensile strength were increased from 
397 MPa to 890 MPa (precipitation-free) to 1431 MPa and 1683 MPa 
(multiple-precipitates), respectively. The present study sheds light 
on improving the mechanical properties by concurrently refining 
grains and promoting precipitation in the precipitation-strengthened 
alloys. 
Impact statement 
We demonstrated a novel strategy for notably improving the strength 
of precipitation-strengthened high- and medium-entropy alloys, ach -
ieved by producing ultrafine grains containing abundant heterogeneous 
precipitates.
