ABSTRACT
Lightweight high-entropy alloys (LWHEAs) present new opportunities for exploring innovative, low-cost mate -
rials with a high strength-to-weight ratio, thanks to their expansive alloy-design space. This study introduces the 
newly designed AlMg
x
TiSnZn HEAs to examine the relationship between microstructure, phase stability, and 
functional properties (including mechanical, thermal, and electrochemical properties alongside oxidation 
resistance). These alloys were produced by mechanically mixing them in a high-energy ball mill (HEBM), fol -
lowed by heating with spark plasma sintering (SPS). Thermodynamic calculations were utilized to ascertain the 
phase structure. XRD studies revealed a predominant ordered BCC phase with minor peaks indicating a 
tetragonal structure. The correlation between microhardness (H
v
) and fracture strength ( σ
f
) with Mg content 
indicates that the HEA sample featuring the highest microhardness concurrently exhibits the highest fracture 
strength (AlMgTiSnZn). This AlMgTiSnZn HEA sample, noted for its high corrosion resistance and distinct 
properties (such as favorable over-potential, low corrosion current density (I
corr
), and minimal corrosion rate), is 
particularly promising for high-temperature applications.
1. Introduction
Lately, High Entropy Alloys (HEAs), a fascinating class of materials, 
have garnered significant attention for their outstanding structural & 
functional properties [ 1 , 2 ]. The distinct ’ cocktail effect ’ in HEAs, among 
their core design principles, is achieved via high configurational entropy 
[ 3 ]. This entropy aids in stabilizing a uniform solid solution structure. 
There has been extensive research on single-phase alloys. Yet, studies on 
the simultaneous precipitation of intermetallic and solid solution phases 
in multiphase HEAs remain somewhat scarce. Nevertheless, research 
over the past five years reveals that HEAs with two or multiple phases 
often show superior characteristics or performance in certain applica -
tions compared to their single-phase counterparts. Such enhancement is 
primarily due to the dispersed secondary-phase particles within the 
HEAs, which markedly improve the mechanical properties of these 
materials. As industries, especially the energy sector, confront the 
challenge of operating at increasingly higher temperatures, traditional 
nickel-based alloys are proving insufficient [ 4 – 7 ]. Consequently, there is 
a pivotal shift towards developing new HEA types incorporating light -
weight elements like magnesium, aluminum, titanium & lithium.

2. Materials and methods
Due to the boiling point of magnesium being lower than the melting 
point of some other constituent elements in selected HEAs, melting 
techniques are unsuitable for synthesizing these HEAs. Consequently, 
we synthesized the AlMgxTiSnZn ( x = 0, 0.5, 1, and 1.5 mol) alloys 
using high-energy ball milling (HEBM). We utilized the MBM-07 plan -
etary ball mill to mill highly pure powders of Al (99.7 %), Mg (99.8 %), 
Sn (99 %), Ti (99.6 %), and Zn (99.8 %) in 250 ml tungsten carbide vials 
under inert conditions (5 % H2 & 95 % Ar). The process parameters 
included a rotation speed of 570 rpm, a ball-to-powder weight ratio of 
20:1, and a milling duration of 25 h. Toluene was used to avoid oxida -
tion during the milling process. The as-milled powders of AlTiSnZn (Mg 
free) HEAs were placed in a graphite die with an internal diameter of 10 
mm. These were sintered using SPS at 520

the crystallite size and phases present. For TEM sample preparation, 
sintered HEAs were ultrasonicated in ethanol for about 30 min. 
Agglomerated particles were allowed to settle for 15 min. Then, a

study. Compression tests were done on cylindrical specimens (20 mm ×
10 mm). Each test was repeated three times to ensure reproducibility 
using a compression testing machine (Shimadzu AGS-x 300 kN) at about 
1 mm/min strain rate. For corrosion tests, we used an RST500F appa -
ratus with electrochemical polarization (voltage range: 1 to + 1 V at a 
sweep rate of roughly 0.05 mV/s). The solution used was NaCl.

composition design process for HEAs [ 11 – 15 ].

4. Conclusion
Following the sintering process, the XRD patterns for all produced 
AlMg
x
TiSnZn high entropy alloys (HEAs) reveal a predominantly or -
dered BCC phase. This is accompanied by some minor tetragonal 
structure peaks. When magnesium is added in optimal amounts (up to 
1.0 mol%), new intermetallic phases like Mg
2
Sn and MgZn
2 
start to form 
alongside the existing BCC phase. These additions introduce more 
disorder, as clearly evidenced by SEM image analysis. Furthermore, the 
regularity of the BCC structure starts to improve due to the random 
distribution at the ‘Ti ’ lattice atomic sites. Within the experimental 
range, the AlMgTiSnZn alloy ( x = 1.0) displays a significant endo -
thermic peak at 837 
◦
C. This peak is primarily linked to the disordered 
BCC phase and the ideal proportion of the intermetallic Mg
2
Zn phase 
indicating superior thermal stability. Investigations on microhardness & 
compression performance show that the AlMgTiSnZn HEA sample with 
the highest microhardness (479 Hv) also exhibits the best fracture 
strength (677 MPa) coupled with strong corrosion resistance. This is 
corroborated by PDP curves which display a broad Nyquist semi-circle, 
demonstrating a favorable structure-property relationship for applica -
tions requiring lightweight materials.
