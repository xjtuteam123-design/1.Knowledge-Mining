The tensile behaviors of polycrystalline Ni-base superalloys have been studied in the temperature range
of
 25–980 ◦ C. Anomalous increase of yield strength was observed in precipitation hardened superalloys
at
 intermediate temperature. The alloy with high /CR′ volume fraction showed a remarkable increase of
yield
 strength at intermediate temperature. A peak of yield strength was observed in the alloy with low
/CR′ volume fraction at intermediate temperature while solid solution strengthened alloys did not have
such
 peak. Abrupt decrease of ductility in the intermediate temperature regime was observed not only
in
 the /CR′ strengthened superalloys but also in the solid solution strengthened superalloy. This result
implies
 that /CR′precipitation is not a substantial cause for the occurrence of the ductility minimum in the
superalloys.
 It was found that twinning was an important deformation mechanism of the superalloys at
intermediate
 temperature where ductility was abnormally low. Deformation twins formed easily in the
superalloys
 whose reduction of stacking fault energy was high regardless of strengthening mechanisms
because
 alloys with low stacking fault energy was prone to extend stacking faults.

1. Introduction
It is expected that the strength of metals and alloys normally
decreases when temperature increases owing to the thermally
assisted movement of dislocations. Many superalloys, however,
show anomalous tensile behavior, that is, ﬂow stress increases with
increasing temperature in the intermediate temperature regime
[1–3]. It has been understood that this anomalous tensile behav-
ior is attributed to L12 type ordered /CR′ phase in the superalloys
[4–6]. The deformation mechanism at intermediate temperature
was explained to be activated cross slip of moving dislocations
from the {1 1 1} primary slip plane to low energy {1 0 0} cube plane
which acts as a sessile plane in L12 type crystal structure [7–9]. The
dominant deformation mechanisms of superalloys are: shearing of
/CR′ by pair of a/2⟨1 1 0⟩ dislocations at low temperature, stacking
fault formation by the movement of a/3⟨2 1 1⟩ partial dislocations
at intermediate temperature, and dislocation by-pass around /CR′at
high temperature [10,11].
Generally, crystals have high ductility at high temperature.
However, elongation decreases abruptly with increasing temper-
ature for /CR′ particle strengthened superalloys in the intermediate
temperature region [12]. This mid range ductility minimum or

2.
 Experimental procedure
Three kinds of wrought superalloys and cast superalloy
CM247LC were prepared for the analysis. The equiaxed poly-
crystal CM247LC specimens of 13 mm in diameter were vacuum
investment cast followed by the standard heat treatment; solu-
tion treatment at 1260 ◦C for 2 h + 1st aging at 1079 ◦C for 4 h + 2nd
aging at 871 ◦C for 20 h. Wrought superalloys Haynes 230, Alloy 263,
and Hastelloy X specimens were cut from the commercially avail-
able hot rolled plates. Alloy 263 specimens were subjected to aging
treatment at 800 ◦C for 8 h followed by air cooling, which is stan-
dard heat treatment to precipitate ﬁne spherical /CR′particles in the
matrix. Other two wrought superalloys were tested in the solution
heated condition. The chemical compositions of the superalloys are
listed in Table 1.
Tensile tests were carried out in air at 25–980 ◦C with strain rate
of 2 × 10−3 s−1 at 25 ◦C and 8 × 10−4 s−1 at temperature above 25 ◦C.

3.
 Results and discussion
3.1. Initial microstructures
The initial microstructures of the superalloys are shown in Fig. 1.
Solution heat treated and aged CM247LC has still /CR//CR′ eutectic in
grain boundaries and interdendritic regions. Discrete M23C6 parti-
cles precipitated at the expense of dissolution of MC embedded
on the grain boundaries. Small M23C6 carbides precipitated on
grain boundary and annealing twin boundary during aging treat-
ment at 800 ◦C in Alloy 263 (Fig. 1b). Large M6C particles formed
within grain interior and on grain boundary while very small M23C6
carbides precipitated along grain boundary during cooling from
solution heat treated temperature in Haynes 230 and Hastelloy X
(Fig. 1c and d). Shape and size of /CR′ particles in the precipitation
hardened superalloys are shown in Fig. 2. The volume percent and
average size of cuboidal /CR′ were 65 and 0.5 /H9262m, respectively, in
CM247LC. About 10 vol% of spherical /CR′ with 15–20 nm diameter
formed in Alloy 263.
3.2.
 Tensile properties
Tensile properties of the superalloys at various temperatures
are shown in Fig. 3. Yield strength was determined using a 0.2%
offset strain criterion. Both yield strength (YS) and ultimate tensile
strength (UTS) show similar temperature dependence in CM247LC.
YS decreases slightly with increasing temperature up to 650 ◦C, and
UTS is almost constant at this temperature range. The YS and UTS
reach at the peak value at 760 ◦C, and then they rapidly decrease
above this temperature. Elongation increases slightly with temper-
ature between room temperature and 600 ◦C. The abrupt decrease
of elongation occurs at 760 ◦C and 870 ◦C, and then it increases
with temperature. Temperature dependence of yield behavior of
CM247LC is similar to that of other cast superalloys with high
/CR′ volume fraction [13]. Variation of yield strength and tensile
strength with temperature in Alloy 263 is different from each other.
Yield strength decreases slightly with increasing temperature up
to 540 ◦C, and then it comes to the peak value at 760 ◦C. Tensile
strength decreases gradually with increasing temperature. Elon-
gation decreases with increasing temperature at and above 540 ◦C
and it reaches to the minimum value at 760 ◦C. Tensile strength
of Haynes 230 decreases with increasing temperature while yield

It is well known that the anomalous yield behavior, positive
temperature dependence of ﬂow stress at intermediate tempera-
ture, arises from L12 type ordered /CR′phase in the superalloys [4–6].
The deformation behaviors of the ordered alloys can be strongly
affected by the state of the ordered phase because it has a signif-
icant effect on the slip process and nature of dislocations of the
alloys [9]. The alloy with high /CR′volume fraction shows a remark-
able increase of yield strength at intermediate temperature. The YS
of CM247LC has prominent peak at 760 ◦C and that of Alloy 263 also
shows a peak while the solid solution strengthened superalloys,
Haynes 230 and Hastelloy X, do not have such peak at intermediate
temperature. It is found that the precipitation of /CR′ particle does
not have a substantial effect on the occurrence of the ductility min-
imum

well at intermediate temperature on the elongation curves. Solid
solution strengthened alloy, Haynes 230, also has a ductility mini-
mum, which is similar to /CR′strengthened Alloy 263. However, the
other solid solution strengthened alloy, Hastelloy X, does not have
the ductility minimum.
3.3.
 Deformation structure after tensile testing
Microstructures of CM247LC after tensile testing at various
temperatures are shown in Fig. 4. The predominant deformation
mechanism is /CR′ shearing by pairs of a/2⟨1 1 0⟩ dislocations at
650 ◦C. It has been generally accepted that the most part of dislo-
cations observed below the peak temperature are a/2⟨1 1 0⟩ type
which glide on {1 1 1} plane [7,14]. Even though the deforma-
tion structure is inhomogeneous and intense slip bands shear both
/CR matrix and /CR′ particle, elongation is relatively high at 650 ◦C.
Dislocation density becomes high at /CR//CR′ interface and many dis-
locations shear /CR′particle at 760 ◦C and 870 ◦C. Stacking faults and
deformation twins are observed at these low ductility tempera-
tures regimes. Kear and Wilsdorf [15] explained the peak strength

Microstructures of Alloy 263 after tensile testing at various tem-

Slip is planar and the dislocations are concentrated in intense
slip bands at 25 ◦C and 540 ◦C in Haynes 230, as shown in Fig. 6.
High density of dislocation is localized to slip bands, but elonga-
tions at low temperature are comparatively high. Microtwinning
mechanism operates during tensile deformation at 760 ◦C. Haynes
230 also shows formation of microtwins at the ductility minimum
temperature like the /CR′particle strengthened alloys. Elongation has
minimum value at 760 ◦C although dislocation density in matrix
between deformation twins is lower than those at low tempera-
tures. The superalloys which have been reported to show ductility
minimum at intermediate temperature contain high volume frac-
tion of /CR′particle. It should be noted that Haynes 230 has a ductility
minimum accompanied by operation of microtwinning at inter-
mediate temperature although it is a solid solution strengthened
superalloy. Dislocation network forms during tensile testing at
870 ◦C. The dislocation networks consist of primarily two types
of dislocations [12] and they are results of combined reaction of
slip and climb process [17,18]. High elongation at this tempera-
ture
 could be attributed to low dislocation density and thermally
assisted climb.
No slip band is observed, but cell-like dislocation structure
formed in Hastelloy X testing at 760 ◦C (Fig. 7a) where the formation
of microtwin was found to be a principal deformation mechanism
in other superalloys. The homogeneous distribution of dislocations
with small loops is observed as shown in Fig. 7b. The presence of dis-
location loops and tangles indicates that dislocation climb operates
at this temperature [19]. At 870 ◦C, it is observed that dislocations
rearrange to form dislocation network as shown in Fig. 7c, which
implies that dislocation climb is a signiﬁcant deformation mecha-
nism at this temperature.
3.4.
 Stacking fault energy and formation of microtwin
Anomalous yield behaviors are observed in both /CR′particle hard-
ened and solid solution strengthened superalloys. Inhomogeneous
deformation with intense slip band occurs at low temperature.
Thermally assisted dislocation climb becomes signiﬁcant deforma-
tion mechanism at high temperature in all alloys. Formation of
microtwin prevails over slip and climb of dislocations in CM247LC,
Alloy 263 and Haynes 230 at intermediate temperature at which
elongation has its minimum value. Hastelloy X does not show an
abrupt decrease of elongation at intermediate temperature, which
is consequent on the absence of microtwin.
Formation of microtwin has been analyzed in terms of stacking
fault energy of each alloy in order to explain ductility minimum
behavior. Xie et al. [20] proposed Eq. (1) to estimate the reduction
of stacking fault energy by the addition of elements into the Ni-base
alloys.

Ni–Fe binary alloy was almost constant up to 20 at.% Fe content, so
reduction of stacking fault energy caused by Fe addition could be
negligible.
Two /CR′ hardened superalloys CM247LC and Alloy 263 which
have high values of /Delta1/CRs show the ductility minimum and defor-
mation twined microstructure at intermediate temperature. Solid
solution strengthened superalloy, Haynes 230, which is also char-
acterized by well developed microtwin and abruptly decrease of
elongation at intermediate temperature has high values of /Delta1/CRs.
However, solid solution alloy, Hastelloy X, which has relatively low
/Delta1/CRs value, reveals continuous increase of elongation with increas-
ing temperature without the twin formation.
Many models to explain formation of deformation twin have
been proposed. Knowles and Chen [24] reported that initiation
and propagation of twin was associated with successive shear of
{1 1 1} plane by a/3⟨1 1 2⟩ twinning partial dislocations which cre-
ated
 a super-extrinsic stacking fault (SESF). They proposed that
pole mechanism should be operated in order to form deformation
twin. Vergnol and Grilhe [25] calculated energetic cost for the for-
mation of a twin under tension and compression conditions. They
proposed that the extrinsic stacking faults were always the most
favorable for twinning occurrence. Venables [26] suggested that
there was relationship between the twin stress and stacking fault
energy in Cu–Al alloys. Twin propagation is more difﬁcult stage
for alloys with low stacking fault energy, whereas twin nucleation
is more difﬁcult for alloys with high stacking fault energy. Kolbe
[27] and Kovarik et al. [28] suggested atomic reordering process
within the /CR′precipitate in order for a twin structure to originate
from the sequential passage of a/6⟨1 1 2⟩ partials. Reordering pro-
cess initiates at temperatures lower than those required for general
climb of dislocation, and is a conservative process involving diffu-
sion within atomic distance. Therefore, formation of twin occurs at
intermediate temperature. Even though there are many theoretical
and experimental approaches to elucidate twinning, the formation
mechanism is not yet fully understood. However, it should be noted
that dissociation of perfect dislocation into the partial dislocations
which are separated by stacking fault is required in order to form
twin. It is known that alloys with low stacking fault energy are
prone to extend stacking fault. In this study, it has been found that
deformation twins are observed in the superalloys of which /Delta1/CRs
is high due to the addition of elements regardless of /CR′ precipita-
tion. Further studies are needed to explain the reason why ductility
minimum with twined microstructure is observed at intermediate
temperature in all of the alloys.

4. Conclusion
Remarkable increase of yield strength is observed at intermedi-
ate temperature in the cast superalloy CM247LC which has high
volume fraction of /CR′ particle. Precipitation hardened wrought
superalloy 263 also has a peak of yield strength on the tensile
curve while solid solution strengthened superalloys, Haynes230
and Hastelloy X, did not show such peak strength at interme-
diate temperature. Precipitation of /CR′ particle does not have a
substantial effect on the occurrence of the ductility minimum of
the superalloys. Abrupt decrease of elongation in the intermedi-
ate temperature regime is observed not only in the /CR′ hardened
superalloys but also in the solid solution strengthened superalloy.
Inhomogeneous microstructure with intensive slip bands is
observed at specimens tested under the temperature of peak
strength. Thermally assisted dislocation climb processes are
commonly operating at high temperature. Microtwinning is an out-
standing deformation mechanism of the superalloy at intermediate
temperature where the ductility minimum occurs. It is found that
deformation twins tend to form in the superalloys whose reduc-
tion of stacking fault energy is high regardless of strengthening
mechanisms.
