Morphology evolution and crystallographic feature of
Nb nanowire in an in-situ NiTi-Nb nanocomposite
Zhen-Yang Liu, Fang-Min Guo, Zhong-Qiang Wang,
Li-Shan Cui*
Received: 26 May 2014 / Revised: 16 June 2014 / Accepted: 26 June 2014 / Published online: 1 August 2014
/C211The Nonferrous Metals Society of China and Springer-Verlag Berlin Heidelberg 2014
Abstract In–situ NiTi shape memory alloy–Nb nanowire
nanocomposite was revealed to exhibit exceptional
mechanical properties. The properties of high strength and
ultra-large elastic strains are closely related with the high
performance of in situ-formed Nb nanowire reinforce-
ments. In this study, the morphology evolution and crys-
tallographic feature of Nb nanowires in NiTi shape
memory alloy matrix during cold drawing were investi-
gated by means of transmission electron microscopy
(TEM). It is revealed that, as a result of the severe work
hardening and amorphization of NiTi matrix, the cold work
reduction of nanowire reinforcements can exceed that of
the entire composite. The irregular-shaped Nb nanowires
are reshaped to straight and smooth nanowires during cold
drawing process. By means of TEM nanobeam diffraction,
it is revealed that the in situ-formed Nb nanowire in such
top-down processed NiTi–Nb composite keeps single
crystalline over 1,000 nm at length direction. As a conse-
quence, the aspect ratio of nanowire reinforcements in NiTi
matrix exceeds 25:1.
Keywords Deep drawing; Nanowire; Metal matrix
composite; TEM
1 Introduction
Both computational and experimental studies reveal that
metallic nanowires can exhibit ultra-large elastic strains
and ultra-high strength, whose values are several times
larger than those of their bulk form [1–4]. Given this, it is a
profound designing strategy that incorporates nanowires
into metal matrix to harness the exceptional mechanical
properties of the nanowires in bulk forms [5–9]. NiTi shape
memory draws considerable attentions, when acting as
functional metal matrix in the fabrication of novel com-
posite materials [10]. Recently, our group reported a novel
NiTi shape memory alloy-based Nb nanowire-reinforced
nanocomposite. The composite exhibits ultra-large elastic
strains and ultra-high strength [ 11], which was derived
from the exceptional mechanical performance of the Nb
nanowire reinforcements. In NiTi–Nb composite system,
Nb nanowires were in-situ formed during the severe plastic
deformation process of wire drawing. Such method was
also employed in the fabrication of binary Cu–Ag, Cu–Nb
[7, 12, 13], and ternary Cu–Cr–Ag [14] composite systems.
The microstructure evolution and morphology of ﬁla-
ment reinforcements in pure metal (Cu)-based composites
were studied extensively [ 7, 12–14]. During cold drawing
processing of the Cu-based composite system, both matrix
and reinforcements deform in a dislocation-mediated
plastic manner and keep crystalline states. During cold
deformation processing, NiTi shape memory alloy exhibits
various deformation mechanisms. With respect to different
deformation strainings, NiTi may undergo stress-induced
B2 to B19
0 phase transformation and plastic deformation,
leading to severe strain hardening and ultimately amorph-
ization [15, 16]. The knowledge on the microstructure and
morphology evolution of nanowire reinforcements in such
alloy matrix during cold drawing was limited.

Since the NiTi–Nb nanocomposite represents a unique
metallurgical system, their exceptional mechanical prop-
erties are closely related with the high performance of Nb
nanowire reinforcements and show great potentials in
practical applications [ 17]. It is important to establish a
knowledge base for the microstructure and morphology
evolution of metallic nanowire reinforcements in alloy
matrix during cold working. This work was focused on the
morphology and microstructure characterization of Nb
nanowires during the cold drawing process when embed-
ded in NiTi shape memory alloy matrix.
2 Experimental
An ingot with nominal composition of Ti41Ni39Nb20
(at%) was prepared by means of vacuum induction melting.
Then, the ingot was hot forged at 1,223 K into a rod of
16 mm in diameter and further hot drawn at 1,023 K into a
thick wire of 2 mm in diameter. The hot drawn wire was
cold drawn into thin wire of 0.5 mm in diameter at room
temperature with intermediate annealing at 973 K. In order
to achieve maximized cold work reduction of the com-
posite, the 0.5 mm wire was ﬁnally annealed at 1,023 K for
150 s and then cold drawn into wires of 0.38 mm in
diameter and ultimately 0.26 mm in diameter, respectively.
The cold work reduction was calculated as (1 - A/
A
0) 9 100 %, in which A0 is the initial cross-section area
of the wire and A is the ﬁnal cross-section area of the wire.
According to the calculation, the cold work reductions of
0.38 and 0.26 mm cold drawn wire were about 42 % and
73 %, respectively. The samples used for transmission
electron microscope (TEM) observations were taken from
the cold drawn 0.38 and 0.26 mm wire, respectively. The
TEM samples were ﬁrst thinned to *40 lm in thickness
by grinding, followed by ion polishing to below 100 nm in
thickness. The microstructure of the sample was studied
using a FEI Tecnai G20 high-resolution transmission
electron microscope (HRTEM).
3 Results and discussion
Figure 1 is the TEM study of 0.5 mm composite wire heat
treated at 1,023 K for 150 s. Figure 1a is the TEM-high
angle annular dark ﬁeld (HAADF) image of the longitu-
dinal view of the wire, in which the bright white contrasts
are Nb nanowires and the axial direction of the wire is
indicated by arrows. It is seen that Nb nanowires exhibit
two types of different morphologies. For one type denoted
as ‘‘1’’, the nanowires are long and continuous at length
direction, but the sides of the nanowire are irregular. For
another denoted as ‘‘2’’, Nb is very short at length direction
and exhibits sphere-like morphology. Figure 1b is the
corresponding selected area electron diffraction (SAED)
pattern. It is seen that, the h110i Nb diffraction is highly
concentrated at axial direction, indicating the strong h110i
wire texture of Nb. The texture is developed from the
previous hot drawing and cold drawing process, which is
typical for heavily drawn metals with body-centered cubic
structure [18, 19]. The irregular morphology indicates that
Nb nanowires are spheroidized under intermediate heat
treatment at 1,023 K for 150 s.

which makes the aspect ratio of single crystalline Nb
nanowire in NiTi–Nb nanocomposite exceed 25:1. The
lattice strain of Nb nanowires in composite during tensile
deformation is maximally as high as 6.5 % [ 11]. It is
suggested that such high strength of the nanowires is
derived from the perfect alignment in composite as well as
their intrinsic single-crystalline feature, which is in accor-
dance with the reported mechanical properties of free-
standing single-crystalline nanowires [ 5–9].
4 Conclusion
In this study, it was investigated the morphology evolution
of Nb nanowire and crystallographic feature of Nb nano-
wire by means of TEM. It is revealed that the intermediate
heat treatment during cold drawing process of the NiTi–Nb
composite causes spheroidization to Nb nanowires. In case,
Nb nanowires are deformed in NiTi alloy matrix during
cold drawing, and the cold work reduction of nanowire
exceeds that of entire composite wire. Cold deformation
reshapes the morphology of the Nb nanowire, and the
in situ-formed Nb nanowire in such top-down processed
NiTi–Nb composite exhibits single crystalline with aspect
ratio exceeding 25:1.
