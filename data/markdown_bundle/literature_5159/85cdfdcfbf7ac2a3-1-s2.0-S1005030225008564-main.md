a b s t r a c t 
Many precipitation-strengthened materials for elevated-temperature applications encounter substantial 
challenges with intermediate-temperature embrittlement, which is often associated with the formation 
of soft precipitate-free zones (PFZs) near grain boundaries (GBs). This research employs a novel double- 
aging strategy to trigger nanoprecipitation in the original PFZs, which establishes a dual-scale nanopre- 
cipitate distribution in an L12 -strengthened Co-Cr-Fe-Ni-Al-Ti high-entropy alloy (HEA) with a composi- 
tion of Co22.8 Cr18.0 Fe22.8 Ni27.4 Al5 Ti4 (at. %). The microstructure comprises unimodal nanoparticles in the 
original PFZs near GBs and a bimodal distribution of nanoparticles in grain interiors. The resulting alloy 
exhibits an exceptional yield strength of 780 MPa and an elongation of 35% at 650 °C. The formation of 
nanoparticles in the original PFZs enhances the strength and work hardening of PFZs, which decreases 
the strength mismatch between the grain interiors and GBs, thereby alleviating the strain localization at 
GBs. This mechanism not only promotes the coordinated deformation between PFZs and grain interiors 
but also facilitates the GB sliding, thereby remarkably enhancing the plasticity of the alloy. This approach 
holds promise for applications in various types of alloys and stands as an eﬃcient method for addressing 
the intermediate-temperature embrittlement in high-temperature alloys.

1. Introduction 
In polycrystalline materials, the interaction between disloca- 
tions and grain boundaries (GBs) is one of the principal factors 
determining their mechanical properties [ 1 , 2 ]. It is generally un- 
derstood that increasing the dislocation slip distance can enhance 
the pile-up stress of planar slip on GBs [ 3 ], and grain sizes pose 
a critical role in impeding the dislocation slip [ 4 ]. Thus, reducing 
the grain size can effectively alleviate the stress concentration at 
GBs during deformation and promote the coordinated deformation 
among grains [ 5 , 6 ]. However, the GB strength of many metallic 
materials exhibits a notable weakening at intermediate tempera- 
tures, leading to intergranular embrittlement [ 7–11 ]. Intermediate- 
temperature embrittlement commonly arises from factors such as

In addition to aforementioned factors, the formation of 
precipitate-free zones (PFZs) near the GB, commonly observed 
in precipitation-strengthened alloys, is another key contributor 
to intermediate-temperature embrittlement [ 17 , 18 ]. This concern 
spans a broad range of alloys, including Ni- and Co-based super- 
alloys, heat-resistant steels, and high-entropy alloys (HEAs) [ 8–
10 , 19–21 ]. The accumulation of plastic damage within the PFZs 
during early deformation stages often leads to the formation of mi- 
croscopic voids. Intergranular fracture can occur if signiﬁcant void 
coalescences take place within the PFZs before the onset of the 
global plastic deformation. At certain situations, plastic deforma- 
tion in the soft PFZs may trigger work-hardening through disloca- 
tion multiplications, which would facilitate the coordinated defor- 
mation among grains, thereby yielding exceptional plasticity. For

example, Unwin et al. [ 22 ] reported that plastic deformation in- 
duces strain localization in the PFZs, which leads to the strain con- 
centration within the PFZs in the initial deformation. This mech- 
anism can effectively enhance the work hardening of PFZs and 
trigger global yielding before extensive void coalescence, thereby 
suppressing the intergranular embrittlement of alloys. Therefore, 
achieving a high work-hardening rate in PFZs and minimizing the 
difference between the intragranular and intergranular regions in 
strength is crucial. Re -introducing nanoparticles into PFZs is bene- 
ﬁcial for elevating the strength of PFZs and promoting work hard- 
ening [ 23–27 ]. It should be pointed out that a high particle den- 
sity can increase the void susceptibility, leading to premature inter- 
granular cracking, while a low density may result in a signiﬁcant 
strength disparity between the PFZs and grain interiors [ 28 , 29 ]. 
Therefore, tailoring a reasonable density of particles, a stable work 
hardening rate in PFZs, and an appropriate PFZs width are crucial 
for facilitating the coordinated deformation of the alloys [ 30 ]. It 
should be pointed out that modulating these factors to optimize 
mechanical properties poses a key challenge. Niu et al. [ 31 ] and 
Zhao et al. [ 32 ] found that GB segregation and precipitation are 
as key drivers of the PFZ formation. During aging, solute atoms 
segregate to GBs driven by the Gibbs adsorption isotherm, which 
promotes the formation of GB precipitates. This process depletes 
solutes near the GBs, leading to the development of PFZs. The 
mechanisms of GB precipitation vary with temperature and depend 
on intricate thermodynamic and kinetic factors, which are not yet 
fully understood. A more detailed understanding of these factors is 
crucial for delicately tuning PFZ characteristics and improving me- 
chanical properties [ 33 ]. 
In this paper, we propose a two-step progressive aging ap- 
proach to enhance the intergranular embrittlement resistance of 
precipitation-strengthened alloys. The ﬁrst-step aging at high tem- 
peratures can induce the precipitation of coarse-sized precipitates 
at both GBs and grain interiors. The GB precipitation consumes 
the solutes signiﬁcantly, which creates PFZs near the GBs. Subse- 
quently, the second-step aging at low temperatures promotes the 
precipitation of nano-sized precipitates in the original PFZs and 
also in the grain interior. The two-step treatment results in the 
bimodal distribution of precipitate sizes in the grain interior, i.e., 
coarse ones formed at high temperatures and ﬁne ones formed 
during the low temperature aging. In the PFZs, only small-sized 
nanoparticles are formed during the low-temperature aging, while 
the coarse-sized precipitates at the GBs are retained during the 
second-step aging process. Speciﬁcally, an L12 -strengthened HEA 
(Co22.8 Cr18.0 Fe22.8 Ni27.4 Al5 Ti4 , at. %) was chosen as the model mate- 
rial due to its wide temperature range for nanophase precipitation. 
Through the two-step aging steps, we have established a dual-scale 
precipitation distribution extending from GBs to grain interiors in 
the L12 -strengthened HEA. Intriguingly, the double-aged alloy ex- 
hibits both high strength and plasticity at intermediate tempera- 
tures, with an elongation at fracture exceeding 30 % while main- 
taining a tensile yield strength of 780 MPa at 650 °C. This ap- 
proach can be applied in many other precipitation-strengthened 
alloys. The ﬁndings provide new insights into addressing the 
intermediate-temperature embrittlement of high-temperature al- 
loys. 
2. Materials and methods 
2.1. Materials preparation 
An alloy with a nominal composition of Co22.8 Cr18.0 Fe22.8 
Ni27.4 Al5 Ti4 (at. %) was chosen as the model material due to its 
wide temperature range for nanophase precipitation. The equilib- 
rium phase diagram (Fig. S1 in Supplementary Information) indi- 
cates that the alloy maintains a single-phase face-centered cubic 
(fcc) structure above 1017 °C and precipitates the L12 ( γ′ ) phase 
within the temperature range of 740–1017 °C. This wide precip- 
itation window facilitates the controlled formation of nanoscale 
precipitates, enabling the dual-scale nanoprecipitate distribution 
achieved in this study. The alloy ingot was prepared using a vac- 
uum induction melting furnace under a high-purity argon atmo- 
sphere. The molten alloy was poured into a mold to form a 25-kg 
ingot. The as-cast ingot was homogenized at 1200 °C for 5 h (fol- 
lowed by air cooling) to eliminate chemical segregation and pro- 
mote microstructural uniformity. Subsequently, the ingot was sub- 
jected to multiple forging passes at 1180 °C to reﬁne the grain 
structure and achieve uniformity throughout the ingot. Speciﬁcally, 
the ingot was rapidly reheated to 1180 °C (within 10 min) and held 
for 1 h before hot forging at 1180 °C. The forging was performed 
in three deformation cycles involving repeated upsetting and draw- 
ing to facilitate uniform plastic ﬂow and to eliminate potential in- 
ternal defects. Before each cycle, the samples were preheated at 
1180 °C for 15 min. The ingot was processed from an original di- 
ameter of 80 mm to a ﬁnal diameter of 25 mm, followed by air 
cooling. 
After forging, a two-step aging treatment was applied to tailor 
the nanoprecipitate distribution. The ﬁrst-step aging temperature 
was selected to promote solute segregation at grain boundaries 
and stimulate grain boundary phase precipitation, thereby inducing 
the formation of PFZs. Yang et al. [ 8 ] showed that elevated temper- 
atures ( ≥ 800 °C) aging favors the precipitation of L21 -type phase 
at GBs. Taking these factors into consideration, 950 °C was cho- 
sen as the ﬁrst-step aging temperature. This temperature resides 
within the overlapping precipitation range for both L21 and L12 
phases, thereby facilitating L21 precipitation at GBs and L12 precip- 
itation within grain interiors, while simultaneously promoting PFZ 
formation. The second-step aging temperature of 750 °C was de- 
termined based on previous ﬁndings [ 34 , 35 ] and thermodynamic 
calculations, which indicated that this condition optimizes the L12 
precipitate volume fraction ( ∼30 %). Additionally, a controlled slow 
cooling rate of 4 °C/min from the ﬁrst to the second aging step was 
adopted to facilitate spherical nanoparticles while avoiding cellular 
precipitates, enabling targeted nanoprecipitation in these regions. 
Accordingly, the forged bars were aged at 950 °C for 2 h to facili- 
tate the segregation of solute atoms at GBs, followed by slow cool- 
ing to 750 °C. The second aging step at 750 °C was maintained for 
100 h, followed by air cooling, to promote further precipitation of 
nanophases in the grain interiors and original PFZs, establishing a 
dual-scale nanoprecipitation conﬁguration. Two reference samples 
(R1 and R2) were fabricated for comparison. These two samples 
were subjected to the same homogenization and forging processes. 
For the R1 alloy, the forged sample was aged at 750 °C and then 
air-cooled. For the R2 alloy, the forged sample was solutionized at 
1100 °C for 1 h, water-quenched, and subsequently aged at 750 °C 
for 100 h, followed by air cooling. 
2.2. Mechanical tests 
To evaluate the mechanical properties of the alloy, room- and 
intermediate-temperature tensile tests were performed using a 
Z100 universal tensile testing machine, which is equipped with a 
laser extensometer and has a load capacity of 100 kN. The system 
supports tensile testing across a wide temperature range (room 
temperature to 10 0 0 °C). All tests were performed at a constant 
strain rate of 3 × 10–4 s–1 . Dog-bone-shaped specimens with a 
gauge length of 15 mm and a diameter of 3 mm were machined 
from the aged alloy bars and carefully polished. For intermediate- 
temperature testing, the specimens were placed in a furnace before 
heating and heated at a rate of 50 °C/min. Once the target temper- 
ature was reached, the specimens were held for 10 min to ensure 
uniform thermal distribution before loading. Tensile deformation

2.3. Microstructural characterization 
Crystal structures were analyzed via X-ray diffraction (XRD) us- 
ing a Bruker AXS D8 Advance X-ray monochromator with Cu K α
radiation, operating at a scanning rate of 1.0 °/min across the 2 θ
range of 20 °–100 °. Microstructural analysis was carried out us- 
ing scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and electron backscatter 
diffraction (EBSD, with a step size of 0.2 μm). SEM samples were 
polished to a smooth ﬁnish and etched in a 70 % aqua regia so- 
lution for 40 s to reveal microstructural details. EBSD measure- 
ments were performed on mechanically polished samples, which 
were further electro-polished in a 10 % oxalic acid solution at 
a voltage of 20 V and a current of 0.1 A. Transmission elec- 
tron microscopy (TEM) characterization was performed on an FEI 
200X ﬁeld-emission microscope operated at 200 kV to examine 
the microstructure evolution at the nanoscale. TEM samples were 
thinned to less than 200 nm using a dual-jet polishing system 
in a solution of 8 vol. % perchloric acid and 92 vol. % ethanol at 
−25 °C, ensuring minimal damage to the samples during prepara- 
tion. 
3. Results 
3.1. Microstructures 
Representative SEM and EBSD images of the alloy in different 
conditions are displayed in Fig. 1 . In the as-forged state ( Fig. 1 (a, 
b)), the alloy exhibits an equiaxed grain structure with an average

TEM results indicate that these particles are of Ni3 (Al, Ti)-type. 
The results clearly show that the ﬁrst-step leads to the forma- 
tion of PFZs, and the second-step aging triggers the formation of 
nanoparticles in the original PFZs and induces the formation of 
a bimodal precipitate microstructure by further precipitation. To- 
gether, these two steps result in the development of the dual-scale 
nanoprecipitate structure.

4. Discussion 
The double-aged alloy exhibits an excellent combination of 
intermediate-temperature strength and plasticity, which can be at- 
tributed to the unique nanoprecipitate conﬁguration, i.e., a dual- 
scale precipitation distribution extending from GBs to grain interi- 
ors. In the following sections, we discuss the formation mechanism 
of dual-scale precipitation and its inﬂuence on the strengthening 
and deformation mechanisms. 
4.1. Formation mechanism of the dual-scale precipitation 
The microstructure characterization indicates that the two-step 
aging plays a pivotal role in establishing the dual-scale distribu- 
tion of nanoprecipitates in the alloy. Speciﬁcally, the development 
of PFZs during the high-temperature pre-aging is a key step in de- 
veloping the dual-scale precipitation conﬁguration. Generally, the 
GB segregation and precipitation are key factors driving the forma- 
tion of PFZs. To further explore the underlying mechanisms, STEM 
analysis was conducted on the L21 precipitates at GBs ( Fig. 5 ). The 
STEM results reveal that PFZs form around the L21 phase, indicat- 
ing a strong correlation between these behaviors.

The GB segregation of Ti/Al and the formation of L21 phase dur-

culations conﬁrm that Ti and Al segregate to the GBs during the 
950 °C aging, resulting in a depletion of these solute atoms in the 
surrounding regions. Due to the sluggish diffraction effect of HEAs 
[ 56 , 57 ], the solute atoms in these depleted zones cannot be quickly 
replenished, preventing the nanoparticle precipitation and leading 
to the formation of PFZs. However, the grain interiors remain su- 
persaturated with solutes, enabling nanoparticle precipitation dur- 
ing aging at 950 °C. This helps balance the composition between 
the PFZs and grain interiors, reducing the chemical potential gradi- 
ent. When the alloy undergoes the second-step aging at 750 °C, the 
increased supersaturation of Ti and Al further drives the precipita- 
tion of nanoparticles in both the PFZs and grain interiors. Addition- 
ally, previously formed L12 nanoparticles continue to grow, with 
the size increasing from approximately 57 nm to 110 nm. Fig. 6 vi- 
sualizes this series of complex transformations, including the for- 
mation of new nanoparticles and the growth of existing ones, re- 
sults in a dual-scale nanoprecipitation conﬁguration within the al- 
loy following the two-step aging process.

ing during the early stage of plastic deformation. 
As the deformation proceeds, the dislocations that pile up at 
the interface between the PFZs and grain interiors migrate towards 
the grain interiors, which facilitates the global plastic deformation 
and effectively averts the intergranular fracture. This favorable de- 
formation behavior can be attributed to two primary factors. First, 
the formation of nanoparticles in the original PFZs enhances the 
strength and work hardening of PFZs, which decreases the strength 
mismatch between the grain interiors and PFZs, thereby alleviating 
the strain localization near GBs. This controlled microstructure re- 
duces the susceptibility to void formation without compromising 
the strength, thereby averting early failure during deformation. In 
addition, the PFZs with a width of ∼200 nm facilitates the dislo- 
cation slip, which allows the stress concentration zone to expand 
from a narrow region (a few nanometers in width) to a wide re- 
gion (tens or even hundreds of nanometers in width), which sig- 
niﬁcantly increases the space for dislocation activities and the tol- 
erance of void coalescence. As the strain accumulates, dislocations 
pile up at the interface between the original PFZs and grain inte- 
riors. With increasing interfacial stresses, the dislocations migrate 
towards the grain interiors, which alleviates the stress concentra- 
tion near the GBs and facilitates the global plastic deformation, ul- 
timately yielding an excellent plasticity. Second, the dynamic re- 
crystallization during deformation promotes the GB sliding (GBS), 
which is likely another signiﬁcant factor contributing to the ex- 
traordinary plasticity of the double-aged alloy. As noted by Watan-

the relative motion of grains to coordinate the overall deformation 
process. This process leads to changes in the linking pattern be-

or even X-junctions ( Fig. 11 (a)). The GBS can result in the transfor- 
mation of grains from equiaxed to rhombic shapes, thereby form-

5. Conclusions 
In this study, a two-step progressive aging approach was em- 
ployed to establish a precipitation conﬁguration with dual-scale 
nanoprecipitates extending from GBs to grain interiors. This strat- 
egy not only improves the yield strength but also effectively sup- 
presses the embrittlement at 650 °C, achieving an excellent com- 
bination of high strength and plasticity. The main conclusions are 
drawn as follows: 
(1) The ﬁrst-step aging at 950 °C induces the Ti and Al segrega- 
tion at GBs, resulting in the solute depletion and the for- 
mation of PFZs. Meanwhile the grain interiors remain su- 
persaturated, allowing for the nanoparticle precipitation. The 
second-step at 750 °C drives the formation of new nanopar- 
ticles in both PFZs and grain interiors and promotes the 
growth of the existing ones, leading to a dual-scale nano- 
precipitation conﬁguration. 
(2) Strengthening modeling reveals that the solid solution 
strengthening, Hall-Petch strengthening, and nanoprecipita- 
tion strengthening of the double-aged alloy do not change 
signiﬁcantly with increased temperature. However, a sig- 
niﬁcant reduction in lattice friction stress (Peierls-Nabarro 
stress) accounts primarily for the reduced strength at 650 °C 
compared to that at room temperature. 
(3) The strong strain localization at GBs is identiﬁed as the 
key factor causing embrittlement of the reference alloys at 
650 °C. In contrast, the original PFZs in the double-aged al- 
loy provide space for dislocation activity, increasing toler- 
ance for void coalescence and reducing stress concentration 
near GBs. Additionally, the precipitates within the PFZs en- 
hance the work hardening. As the deformation progresses, 
dislocations migrate from the original PFZs into grain in- 
teriors, which alleviates stress concentrations and enables 
coordinated deformation, thereby overcoming intermediate- 
temperature embrittlement. 
(4) GBS is another critical mechanism that facilitates the 
sustained deformation and suppresses the intermediate- 
temperature embrittlement. Early-stage deformation gener- 
ates numerous dislocations in the original PFZs, which pro- 
motes the dynamic recrystallization. This recrystallization 
enhances the GBS, enabling the alloy to resist embrittlement 
and achieve superior plasticity at 650 °C.
