Ta-containing NiAl-base alloys with the Laves phase TaNiAl with Cl4 structure, 
which were studied in Part I of this study, were modified by alloying with Cr and 
were studied with respect to constitution, thermal properties, deformation beha- 
viour at ambient and high temperatures-including elastic deformation, brittle- 
to-ductile transition, fatigue and creep-and corrosion as a function of alloy 
composition and microstructure. It was found that an NiAl alloy with about 
2.5 at% Ta and 7.5 at% Cr-IP75 for short-is advantageous for application at 
high temperatures above 1000°C in view of high hot strength and creep resis- 
tance, tolerable brittleness at room temperature and excellent corrosion and 
thermoshock resistance at temperatures up to 1350°C. Small alloying additions of 
MO, Nb, or W may be used for further property optimisation. The obtained 
alloys are promising for applications at temperatures above those of the Ni-base 
superalloys. 0 1998 Elsevier Science Limited. All rights reserved 
Keywords: A. nickel aluminides based on NiAl, Laves phases, B. mechanical 
properties at ambient temperature, mechanical properties at high temperatures. 
1. INTRODUCTION 
In Part I of this study NiAl-base alloys with vari- 
ous amounts of Ta to produce the Laves phase 
TaNiAl with Cl4 structure as strengthening second 
phase were studied with respect to the deformation 
behaviour in compression and bending at ambient 
and high temperatures-including elastic deforma- 
tion and creep-as a function of alloy composition 
and microstructure.’ It was found that NiAl-Ta 
alloys with the strengthening Laves phase TaNiAl 
are a promising base for materials developments 
aiming at structural applications at 1200°C. Part II 
concentrated on the effects of further alloying on 
constitution and deformation behaviour of the 
NiAl-Ta alloys with Laves phase.2 On the basis of 
the obtained results, NiAl-Ta-Cr alloys were 
selected as most promising for further study and 
development and this is subject of the present Part 
III. The final Part IV refers to effects of processing. 
Some selected results have been reported earlier.3-5

3.3 Flow stress 
Cr in solid solution affects the flow stress of NiAl 
only slightly, which is in contrast to the effect of Ta 
(Fig. 5). Cr as additional precipitate phase in a 
NiAl-Ta alloy with Laves phase leads to distinct 
softening above 500°C as is visible in Fig. 6. It is 
noted that the flow-stress curve for the NiAl-Ta- 
Cr alloy extends to room temperature which indi- 
cates an improved plastic deformability compared 
with the NiAl-Ta alloy without Cr. It is further 
noted that an abrupt drop of flow stress was not 
observed above 1100°C for this alloy. This is in 
contrast to the behaviour of the Ni-base ODS 
superalloy MA6000, which is also shown in Fig. 6. 
Figure 7(a) illustrates the effect of the continuous 
replacement of Ta by Cr in the NiAl-Ta alloy T-10 
in detail. It can be seen that the substitution of Ta 
by Cr reduces the flow stress, but only for Cr/Ta 
ratios up to O-25. For higher fractions of Cr the 
flow stress is more or less constant. A closer 
inspection of the microstructure by TEM shows 
that the fine Cr precipitate particles are effective 
obstacles for dislocation movement as is visualised 
by Fig. 8. Obviously the hardening effect of the fine 
particles counterbalances the effect of the reduction 
of the volume fraction of Laves phase by the 
reduction of the Ta fraction. 
Apart from the Cr/Ta ratio the flow stress is 
controlled by the Ni/Al ratio. Figure 7(b) shows 
that deviations from the Ni/Al ratio 1, which cor-

3.7 Corrosion and thermal shock behaviour 
Figure 17 illustrates the oxidation behaviour of the 
NiAl-Ta-Cr alloy TC-2.5-7.5 in a He-02 atmos- 
phere and compares it with that of the ternary 
NiAl-Ta alloy T-5 without Cr. It can be seen that 
the oxidation is more or less parabolic and the rate 
for the alloy with Cr is distinctly lower than that 
for the alloy without Cr. The data analysis revealed 
a parabolic oxidation rate constant of the order of 
3.10-‘4 g2cm-4 SK’ at 1 lOO”C, which is more than a 
magnitude lower than the rate constant for NiAl- 
Ta of the order of 5.10-i3 g2cme4 s-l at 1100°C 
and is even below the scatter band of the parabolic 
rate constants for binary NiAl as given in Ref. 15. 
Pesting behaviour was not observed. 
NiAl-Ta-Cr alloys also show a high sulphida- 
tion resistance. Figure 18 compares the sulphida- 
tion behaviour of the alloy TC-2.5-7.5 in cyclic 
tests at 850°C with that of the commercial Ni-base 
alloys Nicrofer5520Co-Alloy 617, which excels by 
its oxidation and carburisation resistance as well as 
strength up to 1 lOO”C, and Nicrofer3220H-Alloy 
800H, which is a suitable material in oxidising/ 
carburising atmospheres with sulphur.i6 Internal 
and external corrosion was observed for the NiAl- 
Ta-Cr alloy with formation of a thin adherent 
scale with thickness in the range 2-4,~m. 
Obviously the NiAl-Ta-Cr alloy TC-2.5-7.5 com-

3.8 Effects of further alloying 
After having selected the composition of alloy TC- 
2.5-7.5 with short designation IP75 as most 
advantageous quaternary composition, various 
additional alloying additions were checked for fur- 
ther optimisation. Observed flow stresses are 
shown in Table 4 for alloys with Fe, MO, or Nb. It 
can be seen that the used alloying additions pro- 
duce flow stress increases only at 1000°C. At higher 
temperatures and lower deformation rates this 
effect vanishes, and even weakening was observed 
at the lowest rate at 1100°C. Fe seems to produce 
increased embrittlement in these alloys since the 
alloy TCF-2.5-7.5-10 with 10% Fe fractured 
before reaching 0.2% plastic strain even at lOOO”C, 
which is in contrast to the other alloys. It is noted 
that Fe reduces the corrosion resistance of the TC- 
2.5-7.5 type alloys. 
A positive effect on the brittle-to-ductile transi- 
tion temperature BDTT was observed for the small 
alloying additions of MO and Nb with reductions 
of up to 100°C. A similar effect was obtained with 
alloying of 1 at% W which also increased the flow 
stress between room temperature and 1000°C. Si, 
which is expected to improve the corrosion resis- 
tance, produced ductility improvements whereas 
the flow stress was not affected according to pre- 
liminary results.
