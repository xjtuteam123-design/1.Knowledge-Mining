abstract
Bulk Ti–Fe–(In, Nb) composites with high strength (w 2350 /C6 50 MPa) and reasonably good ductility
(plastic strain w4.5 /C6 0.5%) have been produced in the form of 2 mm as-cast rods. Analysis of the
microstructure by X-ray diffractometry, scanning electron microscopy and transmission electron
microscopy indicates that it consisted of b-Ti dendrites as a primary phase and Ti (Fe, In and/or Nb)
(B2) þ b-Ti (A2) as eutectic phase. Modiﬁcation of atomic structure, degree of eutectic structure reﬁne-
ment and volume fraction of the constituent phases with the addition of In and Nb are the crucial factors
in enhancing the mechanical properties of Ti–Fe–(In, Nb) composites.

1. Introduction
In recent years, one of the key topics in the development of new
metallic alloys is to develop the nano-/ultraﬁne-grained metallic
materials with simultaneously improved strength and room
temperature plasticity [1–18]. Among these metallic materials
developed so far, Ti-based alloys are known as one of the potential
lightweight engineering materials for industrial applications due to
their excellent mechanical properties (yield strength w 1000 MPa
and ductility w 10%) and good corrosion resistance [19]. Recently,
Ti-based metallic glasses [20–30] and bimodal nano-/ultraﬁne
composites [4,5,31–35] have been developed with the aim to
enhance the level of the strength. In the former case, they should
either contain a toxic constituent element of beryllium or have
a low Ti concentration (<50 at.%) for enhancement of glass forming
ability. Without addition of Be, the glass forming ability is signiﬁ-
cantly deteriorated. The Be-free Ti-based bulk metallic glasses
(BMGs) usually exhibit a high fracture strength exceeding
w2000 MPa, but very limited plasticity (w 0.5%) [20–23]. The Be-
containing Ti-based BMGs exhibit a larger plasticity of about 1–5%,
but with relatively lower fracture strength of w1750 MPa [24–26].
On the other hand, high strength Ti-based bimodal nano-/ultraﬁne
composites display much higher fracture strength ( w2000–
2600 MPa) and larger plasticity ( >2%) compared with the metallic
glasses. Recently, it has been shown that the Ti
60Cu14Ni22Sn4Nb10
alloy consisting of micron scale bcc b-Ti dendrites in nanostructure
matrix exhibits a high strength of 2400 MPa and a plastic strain to
fracture of 14.5% [31]. The enhanced plasticity has been attributed
to the microscale ductile crystalline phases which can toughen the
nanostructured materials. It has been also shown that addition of
Nb as a bcc
b-Ti stabilizer is effective in enhancing plasticity since
the bcc b-Ti phase exhibits better ductility than the hcp a-Ti
phase. Quite recently Zhang et al. [33] have reported that
Ti63.375Fe34.125Sn2.5 alloy exhibits high strength of 2650 MPa and
a plasticity of 12.5%. Recently, even in binary Ti–Fe alloys, Louz-
guine et al. [4] have shown that the hypereutectic Ti 65Fe35 alloy,
consisting of primary Ti–Fe phase and Ti–Fe þ b-Ti eutectic phase
exhibits better mechanical properties (strength w 2200 MPa,
plasticity w 6.7%) than hypoeutectic or eutectic alloys. Moreover, it
is interesting to note that addition of Co in hypereutectic Ti–Fe alloy
results in the enhancement of properties [5], although the addition
of B, Cr, Mn and Ni signiﬁcantly deteriorates the plasticity of the
Ti
65Fe35 alloy [36]. Das et al. [35] have also reported that the
addition of bigger size atom Sn can change the growth mode of
eutectic phases, resulting in the improvement of mechanical
properties [32]. Therefore, it can be noticed that substitution of
a constituent element in binary eutectic that can either change the
growth mode of eutectic phases, or produce a composite consisting

2. Experimental procedure
Master alloys with nominal composition of Ti
70.5Fe29.5,
Ti68Fe28.5In3.5 and Ti66Fe27.5In3.5Nb3 were prepared from elemental
pieces with purity /C21 99.99% by arc melting under a Ti gettered argon
atmosphere in a water-cooled copper crucible. Rods with 2 mm
diameter were suction cast from the master alloy ingots into
a copper mold under Ar atmosphere. The structural features of the
cross-section of the rods were examined by X-ray diffraction (XRD,
Rigaku CN2301) using monochromatic CuK a radiation. The
microstructures of the cross-sectional surfaces were characterized
using a scanning electron microscopy (SEM; Hitachi S2700) and
a transmission electron microscope (TEM; JEOL 2010F 2000FX)
coupled with energy-dispersive X-ray analysis. Thin foils for TEM
were prepared by an ion milling method (Gatan, Model 600). The
room temperature mechanical properties of as-cast specimens
were evaluated by uniaxial compression tests with a strain rate of

4. Discussion
It is well established that a high cooling rate and addition of
bigger size atom promotes the formation of ﬁne-grained micro-
structures by suppressing the grain growth and obstructing the
diffusion characteristics, thus resulting in an improvement of the
strength [40–45]. Elliott et al. [46] has reported that the reduced
eutectic lamellar spacing down to an ultraﬁne (or nanometer) scale
can offer a similar high level strength as for polycrystalline material
with nanometer-scale grain size or bulk metallic glass. In the
present investigation, in situ formed bulk ultraﬁne eutectic matrix
with embedded micron sized dendrites has been synthesized
through suction casting at a relatively higher cooling rate (w10
2 K/s)
than in conventional casting techniques (10 /C0 1–10/C0 2 K/s) [47]. The
microstructural investigations reveal that the average lamellar
spacing in the present eutectic structures decreases from 600 nm
(mention in results) for binary eutectic to 120 nm for In and Nb
substituted Ti–Fe alloy. The dendrite reinforced eutectic composites
Ti68Fe28.5In3.5 and Ti 66Fe27 .5In3.5Nb3 show signiﬁcantly increased
plasticity and fracture strength along with high yield strength. In the
present alloy, the addition of small amount of In in Ti 70.5Fe29.5 alloy
leads to a more reﬁned eutectic matrix by reducing the lamellar
spacing w225 nm. On the other hand, Nb addition together with In
increases signiﬁcantly the volume fraction of
b-Ti dendrites as Nb is
supposed to be b phase stabilizer and also it renders the eutectic
growth more sluggish which favors the further reﬁnement of
lamellar spacing. The volume fraction of dendrite is estimated to be
8% in Ti
68Fe28.5In3.5 and 27% in Ti 66Fe27 .5In3.5Nb3 alloy. Thus it
appears that different microstructure scale and volume fractions of
the primary
b-Ti dendrites and the eutectic matrix are crucial for the
strength and plasticity (shown inFig. 4). To a ﬁrst approximation, the
mechanical properties of composites can be explained by a simple
rule of mixtures [48] as it is often considered for composites (when
neglecting complex interactions between the different constituent
phases)
Pc ¼ PdVd þ PeVe
where Pc is the property of the composite, Pd and Pe are the prop-
erties of the dendrite and eutectic matrix, respectively and Vd and
Ve are the volume fraction of dendrites and eutectic matrix,
respectively. In our present alloys, b-Ti (A2) phase is the soft phase,
while the ﬁne eutectic matrix is the hard phase. Thus it appears that
increased volume fraction of soft
b-Ti dendrite phase is responsible
for the enhanced plasticity. However, on the other hand, high
strength of the hard matrix comes from ﬁne eutectic structure.
Moreover, high content of solutes (shown in Table 1 ) leads to
solution hardening. Thus, possibly ﬁne eutectic matrix is
strengthening phase. It is important to mention here that Hanlon
et al. [49] recently studied the fatigue response of nanocrystalline
and ultraﬁne grain metals and alloys. They observed that grain

work can be correlated with extension of solubility during cooling
as it has been reported that by splat cooling of the liquid at
extremely high cooling rate of 10
7–108 Ks /C0 1, b-Ti solutions up to
35 at.% Fe and TiFe from 35 to 50 at.% Fe was produced [51].
Furthermore, as shown in Fig. 1, there is only about 6% difference
in the lattice parameters of b-Ti and Ti (Fe, In) phases. From this
result, one can expect formation of a semi-coherent interface
between these phases. This leads to lower coherency stresses
compared with the alloy prepared under an equilibrium condition
providing a larger difference in the lattice parameters of the b-Ti
and Ti (Fe, In) phases owing to the lower solute content.
Thus, enhanced fracture strength and work hardening capability
of Ti 68Fe28.5In3.5 and Ti 66Fe27 .5In3.5Nb3 alloys may be correlated
with internal atomic structure of the b-Ti (Fe, In and/or Nb) solid
solution, i.e. solids solution hardening by substitution of atoms
with different atomic radii. Also reﬁnement of eutectic structure by
reducing eutectic lamellar spacing generates more interfaces of
Ti–Fe (In) and
b-Ti phases per unit volume by the addition of small
amount of In and then Nb. The increase of Ti–Fe (In) and b-Ti
interface per unit volume might be accentuating the role in
strengthening the alloys. Sun et al. [34] reported interfacial dislo-
cation networks between the two phases in eutectic alloy playing
an important role in the increment of strength.
5. Conclusion
The experimental results obtained during present investigation
of Ti–Fe–(In, Nb) alloys indicate that In acts as a reﬁnement of
eutectic structure while Nb stabilizes the ductile
b-Ti phase. The
small amount of In and Nb addition in the Ti 70.5Fe29.5 alloy exhibits
higher yield strength over 2000 MPa with enhanced plasticity in
comparison to binary eutectic Ti 70.5Fe29.5 alloy. The yield strength
observed in Ti 66Fe27 .5In3.5Nb3 alloy is slightly higher than Ti-based
BMGs [20–22]. Moreover, present composite consisting of primary
dendrites and ultraﬁne eutectic in Ti 66Fe27.5In3.5Nb3 exhibits
z575 MPa higher yield strength than the Ti 60Cu14Ni22Sn4Nb10
alloy [31] and &ap75 MPa higher yield strength than UFG Ti–Fe-
based bimodal composites [4,5,33,34]. The enhanced properties are
mainly attributed to large volume fraction of b-Ti dendrites and
reﬁnement of eutectic structure by reducing interlamellar spacing
with the addition of In and then Nb.
