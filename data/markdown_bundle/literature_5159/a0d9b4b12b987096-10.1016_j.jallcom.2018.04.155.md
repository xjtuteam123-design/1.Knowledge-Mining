Abstract: 
 Mo-ZrO 2 alloy plate was prepared by combing hydrothermal s ynthesis and powder 
metallurgy, and its microstructure, fracture and me chanical properties after annealing 
at different temperatures were analyzed. Compared w ith pure molybdenum, 
maximum hardness, tensile strength and yield streng th of the alloy plate at room 
temperature were enhanced by 20%, 32% and 54%, resp ectively. The plasticity 
increased initially and then decreased with recover y and recrystallization. The 
strength and reduction of area of alloy annealed at 1,400°C were 25% and 100% 
higher than those of pure molybdenum, respectively. ZrO 2 in the alloy demonstrated 
an obvious hindering effect on recrystallization, w hich could effectively improve 
recrystallization strength and recrystallization plasticity of the recrystallized plate. 
Keywords : Microstructure; ZrO 2 particles; recrystallization 
1 Introduction 
 Molybdenum is widely used in petroleum, chemical, aerospace, military industries 
due to its high strength, low thermal expansion coe fficient, and excellent thermal 
conductivity. However, molybdenum has a body-centered cubic struc ture and 
relatively less sliding system than other structure s, which have brought it higher 
brittleness and more deformation processing difficu lty [1-3] . Moreover, the 
recrystallization temperature of molybdenum is lowe r than its neighboring metals, 
which can limit its usability and processability ab ove 1000 °C [1-5] . Thus, alloying 
elements or oxides are usually added into molybdenu m to improve its processability, 
recrystallization temperature and high-temperature strength. The most common 
molybdenum alloys include Mo-0.5Ti alloy, Mo-0.1Zr alloy and TZM alloy [2-7] . 
 The addition of alloying elements could improve al loy properties under both room 
and high temperature obviously [3-5] . Meanwhile, relevant researches have shown that 
alloying elements could enhance the recrystallizati on temperature and high 
temperature plasticity of the alloy as well [6-8]. Generally, in powder metallurgy

2 Materials and methods 
(NH 4)2Mo 4O13 /circle22H 2O and Zr(NO 3)4/circle23H 2O were used as raw materials. 
Molybdenum alloy, precursor powders of ZrO 2 and MoO 3 were prepared through 
hydrothermal methods, respectively [8-10] . The mixed powders of ZrO 2 and MoO 3 can 
be obtained by calcination. Then composite powders of Mo and ZrO 2 were prepared 
by two stage reduction processes. Mo-ZrO 2 composites were fabricated through 
powder metallurgy technology in which the pressure value was set at 280 MPa and 
held for 5 min (by cold and static pressing (CIP)). Subsequently, the samples were 
rolled to a thickness of 1 mm under different condi tions, such as hot rolling at 1400 o 
C, warm rolling at 1000 oC, cold rolling and process annealing. The plastic 
deformation of each sample was above 95%. The tensi le specimens were cut from 
these resulted plates and annealed at different temperatures (800 oC, 1000 oC, 1100 oC, 
1200 oC, 1300 oC) in a medium frequency furnace for 1 hour, and then cooled to room 
temperature. 
 The density of alloy was investigated by Archimed es method with drainage. 
Scanning electron microscope (SEM, VEGA-3 SBH) coup led with energy dispersive 
spectrometer (EDS) was employed to observe the micr ostructures and fracture 
behaviors of the samples before and after tension f racture tests. The second phases 
appeared in molybdenum alloy were analyzed by Trans mission electron microscope 
(TEM, JEM-2100). The tensile tests were conducted o n a precision universal testing 
machine (SHIMADZU AG-I250KN) with the tensile rate of 2×10 -4 m/min. 
Microhardness of the alloys was measured by a micro hardness tester (MH-3). Figure

 Microstructures of the alloys in Figure 2 showed that alloy grains varied

 TEM observations of alloy plates were shown in Fi gure 3. The circular particles of 
about 100 nm were ZrO 2. The high-resolution experiment was carried out on the 
junction between the near-spherical particles and t he matrix on upper right of in the 
figure, with the diffraction patterns calibrated. A s a result, the white particles were 
determined to be stable t-ZrO 2 surrounded by molybdenum matrix. 
 Combination effect of the second phase and the ma trix directly affected 
enhancement effect, while combination degree was ma inly dependent on crystal 
structure of the enhanced phase. Only when the latt ice structure parameters of the 
second phase and the matrix were similar or even id entical, these two would form a 
strong bonding interface and thus bring them streng thening effect [5,7,10] . On the 
contrary, if there was a great difference between t heir lattice structure parameters, it 
would be difficult to form good bonding surface, an d hardly produce enhanced effect. 
The lattice parameters (a = b = 3.598 nm, c = 5.152 nm) of tetragonal phase zirconia 
were close to those (a = b = c = 3.147 nm) of molyb denum, therefore zirconium and 
molybdenum were likely to form good bonding interfa ce. It could be seen from 
high-resolution zirconium-molybdenum phase interfac e that zirconia and 
molybdenum in molybdenum matrix had good combinatio n. Phase calibration was 
carried out for their phase boundaries, which were Mo (000, 110, 101, 211) and ZrO 2 
(000, 002, 101, 103).

between actual density and theoretical density decr eased as the amount of pores was 
greatly reduced during rolling process. Actual dens ity of the rolled plate was close to 
its theoretical density, due to the densification caused by pressure processing.

3.2 Microstructure and properties of Mo/ZrO2 alloy after annealing 
 Microstructure of the annealed alloy was characte rized. Two groups of samples, 
pure molybdenum and alloy with 1.0% ZrO 2, were selected for microstructural 
analysis. Figure 8 showed microstructure of the ann ealed pure molybdenum plate. At 
the temperatures of 800°C and below 800°C, alloy mi crostructures almost had no 
change, but exhibited strip structure. However, the strip widths were broadened to a 
certain extent, and the alloy performance has under gone great changes close to the 
pre-deformation value. This was recovery process of the alloy, in which the atoms 
slightly migrated and the grains remained original state after deformation processing, 
but the work hardening phenomenon of the material w as alleviated. The dislocation 
density in the deformation substructure caused by d eformation was reduced [7]. As 
annealing temperature rose upon 1,000 °C, the grain structure widening of the matrix 
became more obvious, and the length-diameter ratio of grains decreased, with some

equiaxed crystals appearing in some regions. At this point pure molybdenum plate had 
been recrystallized, and began to show new strain-f ree grains that gradually grew up 
until the original deformation structure was comple tely replaced [10] . The performance 
of the alloy would also undergo significant changes . The process was driven by 
residual deformation stored energy [7,10] . 
The lattice types of metal before and after recryst allization had no changes. As 
temperature increased to 1100°C, grains in the micr ostructure wholly changed into 
equiaxed crystals, and the recrystallization proces s of pure molybdenum ended [10] . 
Then as the temperature further rose to 1,300 °C, some new grains in the alloy merged 
adjacent new ones through grain boundary migration, grew up, and eventually formed 
larger grains [7,10] . This process was called grain growth, and complet ely driven by 
grain boundary energy [10] . The grain boundary energy per unit volume of the alloy 
during grain growth continuously decreased [10] . In pure molybdenum plate, the 
dimension and shape distribution state of grains ba sically remained the same, and 
grains normally grew up. But in doped alloy, the di spersed hard particles formed 
because the second phase could be the first priorit y to prevent normal growth of the 
grain, which was called abnormal growth [10] . 
 Annealing microstructures of Mo-1.0% wtZrO 2 alloy plate were shown in Figure 9. 
As the annealing temperature increased, alloy micro structure underwent 
recovery-recrystallization-grain growth process as well. Alloy recrystallization and 
growth were restrained by the addition of ZrO 2 particles, and its microstructure grew 
obviously at 1100°C. Due to the hindering effect of ZrO 2, the directionality of grain 
growth was different and stronger than that of pure molybdenum with more uniform 
dimension, shape and size. Obviously, the grains in annealed microstructures of the 
alloy after the addition of ZrO 2 were different. It could be found that the alloy 
microstructure still had obvious stripe structure u p to 1200 °C, and disappeared at 
1,300°C, which was completely replaced by equiaxed crystals. The addition of ZrO 2 
particles had obviously inhibited grain growth and increased recrystallization 
temperature.

With increased temperature, the hardness of molybde num and molybdenum alloy 
began to decrease. The hardness of molybdenum alloy overall added ZrO

was still higher than that of pure molybdenum. When the addition amount of ZrO 2 
increased, alloy hardness was enhanced at any annealing temperature.

 The strength and plasticity of molybdenum plate a nd molybdenum alloy plate after 
annealing were investigated. Strength values of the alloy after annealing were shown 
in Figure 11. With the increase of annealing temper ature, alloy plasticity exhibited a 
declined trend, and decreased rapidly after 1000 °C , which was caused by 
recrystallization of the plate. The addition of zir conium oxide in the alloy plate could 
effectively retard grain growth during recrystalliz ation, thus enhancing strength and 
plasticity of the alloy. It could also be seen from the figure that the strength of 
molybdenum alloy with zirconia content of 1.5% afte r annealing at 1300°C reached 
500 MPa, and was about 25% higher than that of pure molybdenum. That could 
greatly improve the application range and service l ife of the materials. Meanwhile, 
alloy plasticity increased initially with the rise of annealing temperature, and then 
decreased until the alloy recrystallized. The plast icity of pure molybdenum was 
optimal at 1000 °C, i.e., recrystallization tempera ture of pure molybdenum plate was 
about 1000 °C. But after the addition of zirconia, the plasticity of the alloy within 
1000 °C decreased with increased addition amount of zirconia. Above 1000 °C, the 
ductility of the alloy was higher than that of pure molybdenum, and the plasticity of 
the alloy with higher addition amount increased wit h enhanced addition amount, and 
reached the peak at 1100°C. Recrystallization tempe rature of the alloy with addition 
content of above 1.0% was improved obviously to abo ut 1100 °C. The plasticity of 
the sample with maximum addition amount of 1.5% at 1100 °C was optimum, with 
the plastic deformation capacity of about 20%. As t he temperature increased, the 
plasticity of the alloy decreased, but the plastici ty of the alloy to which zirconia was 
added declined slowly. After annealing at 1300 °C, the alloy still remained about 10% 
of pristine plasticity, which was 2 times higher th an that of pure molybdenum, and 
indicated very good high-temperature processing performance.

second phase was avoided. The purpose to improve th e processing performance, 
recrystallization temperature and recrystallization strength of the alloy was achieved.

recrystallization of the alloys. The ZrO 2 can effectively improve the recrystallization
