ABSTRACT 
Conventional precipitation-hardened aluminum alloys experience severe mechanical degradation 
above 200 °C due to precipitate coarsening and dissolution. This study presents a novel Al–Fe-Mg- 
Zr eutectic alloy fabricated via laser powder bed fusion, achieving crack-free fabrication with 99.8% 
relative density, ultrafine grains, and a heterogeneous cellular-lamellar microstructure. Direct aging 
(400 °C/4 h) enhances yield strength (YS) to 376.1 MPa (72.6% increase) and ultimate tensile 
strength to 405.8 MPa (30.7% increase) compared to the as-printed condition, while reducing 
ductility to 11.1%. The strengthening mechanisms arise from grain boundary strengthening, 
precipitation strengthening via coherent L1
2
-Al
3
Zr nanoscale dispersoids, dislocation 
strengthening, and effective load transfer through submicron-scale Al–Fe intermetallic phases. 
High-temperature tensile testing reveals excellent mechanical stability, retaining a YS of 124 
MPa at 350 °C. After prolonged thermal exposure (400 °C/100 h), the alloy maintains good 
mechanical properties, demonstrating excellent heat resistance due to the low coarsening rate 
of Al
13
Fe
4 
(0.98 nm
3
/s) and sustained Al
3
Zr precipitates. The dual-scale strengthening strategy 
significantly advances developing high-strength and thermally stable Al alloys for high- 
performance applications.

1. Introduction
Conventional heat-resistant aluminum (Al) alloys have 
predominantly relied on precipitation-hardened Al-Cu 
and eutectic Al–Si systems [ 1–6 ]. However, these alloys 
suffer from significant degradation of mechanical prop -
erties above 200 °C, primarily due to the dissolution 
and coarsening of strengthening precipitates. To miti -
gate precipitate growth at elevated temperatures, a 
practical strategy involves alloying with elements (Fe, 
Ni, Cr, Ce, and La) characterised by low solubility and 
diffusion coefficients, leading to the formation of 
stable high-melting-point intermetallic compounds [ 7 ]. 
Based on this strategy, Al–Fe based alloys such as Al– 
Fe-Mn [ 8 ], Al–Fe-Ni [ 9 ], Al–Fe-Si-(Cu) [ 10 , 11 ], and Al–Fe- 
V-Si [ 12 , 13 ] have been developed since the late twenti -
eth century. Nevertheless, the use of common low- 
cooling-rate manufacturing methods has restricted the 
mechanical enhancement of Al–Fe alloys, confining 
their application largely to thermal conductivity 
materials. This limitation falls short of meeting the 
high-performance demands for lightweight, heat-resist -
ant materials required for long-term service in aerospace 
and automotive sectors.
Laser powder bed fusion (LPBF), characterised by its 
inherently high cooling rate (10
6

7 
K/s) [ 14 , 15 ], has 
driven major advancements in Al–Fe alloy processing. 
This rapid solidification leads to significant microstruc -
tural refinement and increases the solubility of alloying 
elements in the Al matrix, resulting in improved mech -
anical properties and thermal stability. The refinement 
of intermetallic phases, coupled with enhanced solubi -
lity, provides dual benefits of higher strength and 
better resistance to high-temperature degradation. For 
instance, the LPBF-fabricated Al-2Fe alloy exhibits a

2.2 Sample preparation and mechanical testing
In the present work, gas-atomised spherical powders with 
particle size distribution of 30–63 µm were the raw 
material ( Figure 2 ). The chemical compositions of the 
powder and printed bulk were tested by inductively 
coupled plasma atomic emission spectroscopy (ICP-AES), 
as presented in Table 1 . An Al-1.87Fe-1.02Mg-1.03Zr 
(wt.%) alloy, hereafter referred to as Al–Fe-Mg-Zr, was 
fabricated using an SLM 125HL system. The volumetric 
energy density (VED) is defined as VED = P/(h * t * v), 
where P represents laser power (W ), h denotes hatching 
distance (µm), t indicates layer thickness (µm), and v 
stands for scanning speed (mm/s). Rectangular samples 
with dimensions of 10 mm × 10 mm × 8 mm were used 
for parameter optimisation. Relative density measure -
ments were obtained using Archimedes’ drainage 
method. The processing parameters are as follows: 200– 
380 W of laser power, 600–2600 mm/s of scanning 
speed, 100 µm of hatching distance, 30 µm of layer thick -
ness. The tensile samples with gauge dimensions of 
11.5 mm × 3.5 mm × 1 mm were obtained with long 
axes perpendicular to the building direction (BD). Direct 
aging refers to artificially aging the as-fabricated material 
without a separate solution heat treatment, taking advan -
tage of the supersaturated solid solution formed during 
rapid solidification. Accordingly, to investigate the precipi -
tation behaviour, direct aging treatments were performed 
at 300, 400 and 500 °C for 4 h, respectively. To assess heat 
resistance, long-term thermal exposure of 100 h was con -
ducted at 300 and 400 °C, respectively.
Microhardness tests was carried out under a 300 g 
load for 10 s in accordance with ASTM E384-08a. 
Room-temperature uniaxial tensile tests were carried 
out on an MTS Alliance machine at a strain rate of

3. Results
3.1. Densification behaviour
Figure 3 provides a comprehensive view of the densifica -
tion behaviour in LPBF Al–Fe-Mg-Zr alloy under various 
process parameters, offering insights into the interplay 
between VED, relative density, porosity, and hardness. 
In Figure 3 a, surface morphologies differ when scanning 
speed and laser power are varied. At lower power com -
bined with higher scanning speeds – specifically 200 W 
at 1800 mm/s or faster, and 290 W at speeds above 
2200 mm/s – insufficient energy input gives rise to 
lack-of-fusion defects. These can be identified as irregu -
larly shaped pores that compromise the relative density. 
As corroborated by Figure 3 b, these samples show high 
porosity, signifying that an inadequate VED translates 
directly into poor densification. Conversely, raising VED 
within a moderate range of approximately 47–87 J/ 
mm
3 
substantially enhances relative density and 
reduces porosity, reflecting a positive correlation 
between VED and densification quality. However, exces -
sive VED produce counterproductive results: in cases 
where laser power is as high as 380 W coupled with rela -
tively low scanning speeds of 600–1000 mm/s, overheat -
ing induces keyhole defects marked by near-circular 
pores. Such defects not only diminish relative density 
but also have the potential to weaken the final com -
ponent. Turning to Figure 3 c, the Vickers hardness 
data across different parameter sets shows a more 
subtle relationship with VED. Generally, hardness is 
more closely related to factors such as cooling rate, 
elemental evaporation, and resultant phase distri -
butions, which may overshadow the direct influence of 
VED. However, hardness typically exhibits a certain 
degree of positive correlation with material strength. 
Based on the analyses above, a relative density of 
99.8% and a peak hardness of 134.1 HV were achieved 
at 380 W and 1800 mm/s (70.4 J/mm
3
), identifying this 
parameter combination as the optimal processing con -
dition. This parameter set achieves the best balance 
between energy input and heat management, yielding 
both minimal porosity and notably high strength. 
While other combinations, such as 380 W and 2200 
mm/s, also achieve decent hardness, their relative 
density is slightly lower. Consequently, the 380 W and 
1800 mm/s condition was chosen as the final printing 
parameters for subsequent microstructure characteris -
ation and mechanical testing.

3.3 Mechanical properties
3.3.1 Room-temperature tensile properties

aging strengthening and robust thermal stability as dis -

fabrication with 99.8% relative density, characterised 
by ultrafine grains (∼2 µm) alongside distinct cellular 
and lamellar eutectic morphologies resulting from 
rapid solidification dynamics and solute redistribution.
