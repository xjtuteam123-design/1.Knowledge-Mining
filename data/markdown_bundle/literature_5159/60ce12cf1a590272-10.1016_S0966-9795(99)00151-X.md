Microstructure and tensile properties of orthorhombic

Abstract
Microstructure and tensile properties of orthorhombic Ti–Al–Nb–Ta alloys have been studied. In order to optimize ductility and
strength of the orthorhombic alloys with the nominal compositions of Ti–22Al–23Nb–3Ta and Ti–22Al–20Nb–7Ta, various thermo-
mechanical processing steps were implemented as part of the processing route. With a special heat treatment before rolling to obtain
a ﬁne and homogeneous rolled microstructure, the rolled microstructure resulted in a good combination of high tensile yield
strength and good ductility of the alloys through available solution and age treatments. The duplex microstructure with equiaxed a
2/O
particles and ﬁne O phase laths in a B2 matrix, deforming in a2+B2+O phase ﬁeld and treating in O+B2 phase ﬁeld, possesses the
highest tensile properties. The R.T. yield strength and ductility of the Ti–22Al–20Nb–7Ta alloy are 1200 MPa, and 9.8% respectively.
The yield strength and ductility values of 970 MPa and 14% were also maintained at elevated temperature (650 C). # 2000 Elsevier

1. Introduction
The ductility and toughness of Ti3Al base alloys can be
improved by the addition of Nb [1,2]. In exploring the
e/C128ect of niobium on phase transformations and mechan-
ical properties in Ti
3Al base alloy, Banerjee [3] identiﬁed a
ternary intermetallic based on the stoichiometry Ti2AlNb
which was designated as the O phase on the basis of its
orthorhombic structure with the CmCm symmetry. Since
then, the new alloy family, ordered orthorhombic
Ti
2AlNb based alloys, has received considerable attention
during the past decade. The alloys of this family exhibited
higher yield strength, fracture toughness and creep resis-
tance than other alloys based on Ti
3Al [4,5]. For example,
one of the alloys, the Ti–22Al–27Nb alloy, showed a
R.T. tensile yield strength of 1290 MPa and 3.5% elon-
gation, which contained the microstructure of Widman-
statten O+B2 phase. However, the phase equilibria and
microstructural evolution are complicated in the orthor-
hombic alloys. The volume fraction, size, and morphology
of the constituent phases O, B2 and a
2 phases are depen-
dent on the thermal processing and the heat treatment
history [6,7]. Thus, it is necessary to further explore the
microstructure–mechanical property relationship and to
optimize the microstructure to improve the ductility and
strength of the orthorhombic alloys. Although the
e/C128ects of V, Mo and Si [8,9] on mechanical properties of
orthorhombic alloy were reported, it is not clear about
the e/C128ect of Ta on the microstructure and mechanical
properties. In this present work, the microstructure and
tensile properties of orthorhombic alloys in the Ti–Al–
Nb–Ta alloy system were studied to determine whether
substituting part of Nb by Ta can improve the tensile
properties of orthorhombic alloys. Di/C128erent thermo-
mechanical processing steps (TMP) were applied to opti-
mize the microstructure, and thereby, improve the
ductility and strength of the alloys. The inﬂuence of the
di/C128erent parameters during TMP on microstructure and
tensile properties were observed and analyzed.

4. Conclusions
1. Microstructuctural optimization enables us to
obtain a good combination of high strength and
good ductility of orthorhombic alloys in the Ti–
Al–Nb–Ta alloy system both at room temperature
and at elevated temperatures.
2. With heat treatment prior to deformation, thermo-
machanical processing in thea
2+B2+O and O+B2
phase ﬁelds can improve e/C128ectively the tensile
properties in O phase alloy. The R.T. yield strength
and ductility of the Ti–22Al–20Nb–7Ta alloy up
to 1200 MPa and 9.8% are achieved. The yield
strength and ductility values of 970 MPa and 14%
are also maintained at elevated temperature (650
C).
3. The addition of Ta substituted for part of Nb can
improve the strength and ductility of the orthor-
hombic alloy.
