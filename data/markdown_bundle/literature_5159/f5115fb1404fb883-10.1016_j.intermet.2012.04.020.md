abstract
New Co-based alloys containing a L1 2 reinforcement phase display exceptional high-temperature
properties. Early research has shown that the quaternary alloy Co-8.8Al-9.8W-2Ta (at.%) has a high-
temperature strength comparable to single-crystal Ni-based superalloys above 1200 K. Associated
with high strength is an unusual high density of intrinsic stacking faults within the
g
0
precipitates. In this
work, Density Functional Theory, the Axial Next Nearest Neighbor Ising model and Special Quasi-random
Structures have been used to calculate the stacking fault energy of L1
2-Co3(Al,W) and the effect of small
Ta additions on the stacking fault energy. The model predicts a superlattice intrinsic stacking fault energy
of 90e93 mJ/m
2, which increases up to 30% when one Ta atom is substituted on the Al/W sub-lattice. This
effect can be explained by considering d-band effects resulting from the addition of Ta.

2. Theory
2.1. Axial next nearest neighbor Ising model (ANNNI)
The calculation of DESISF of the g
0
phase of Co-based superalloys
using DFT is complicated due to the fact that the second sub-lattice
is randomly populated by Al and W. The supercell approach, the
most common approach to calculate the fault energies of a crystal
from ab initio, usually requires relatively large supercells in order to
obtain reliable results. These large supercells would need to be
even larger in order to accurately resemble the random distribution
of Al and W on the second sub-lattice in all directions. Therefore,
using supercell approaches to calculate
DESISF of L1 2-Co3(Al,W) is
problematic due to prohibitive computational times. A solution to
this problem is provided by the axial next nearest neighbor Ising
(ANNNI) model [14].

3. Methodology
SQSs for the L12 and D019 crystal structures were obtained using
the ATAT code [24e27]. Structures containing 32 atoms (i.e.
Co24Al4W4) were deemed suf ﬁcient to accurately replicate
a random distribution of Al and W on the second sub-lattice [8].
Two SQSs were selected for each crystal structure, matching the
correlation functions of an ideal random distribution of Al and W
atoms up to 4th neighboring 2nd sub-lattice atoms. The correlation
functions of the selected SQSs are listed in Table 1 and the lattice
vectors and atomic positions are listed in Table 2.
All the DFT calculations were performed using the Vienna Ab
initio Simulation Package (VASP) [28,29]. The Projector Augmented
Wave (PAW) method [30,31] was used to describe the electron eion
interactions. The spin-polarized Generalized Gradient Approxima-
tion (GGA) parameterized by Perdew, Burke and Ernzerhof [32] was
used as an approximation of the exchange-correlation functional.
Plane waves have been included up to 300 eV, which was deemed
sufﬁcient for accurate convergence. For large supercells, the
projector operators were evaluated in real space. The Monkhorst-
Pack scheme was used to de ﬁne the k-points and high accuracy
was ensured by running calculations with a k-point density higher
than 5 $10
5 k-points/Å/C0 3. The electronic self-consistent loops were
considered converged after the change in total energy fell below
10

on all the ions fell below 10 /C0 3 eV/Å.
Despite its limitations, the ANNNI model is deemed suitable to
provide a qualitative assessment of the effect of Ta on the DESISF of
L12-Co3(Al,W). In this case, the DESISF of the L1 2 phase can be
calculated by obtaining the total energies of the L1 2 crystal struc-
ture, for which the superlattice stacking order is the same as fcc,
and the D0 19 crystal structure, for which the superlattice stacking
order is the same as hcp. When calculating the total energies for the
DESISF calculations, the total energies of the D0 19 SQSs were
obtained ﬁxing the volume to be equivalent to the relaxed volume
of the corresponding L1 2 SQS, allowing only the ions to relax to
their lowest-energy positions [21].
4. Results
4.1. Validation of SQSs
Each SQS obtained from ATAT can be used to obtain two distinct
total energies, since the positions of the Al and W atoms can be
inverted. As a result, four formation energies could be calculated for
L12-Co3(Al,W), averaging /C0 0.126 eV/atom. Similarly, four formation
energies could be calculated for D0 19-Co3(Al,W),
averaging /C0 0.1 10 eV/atom. These formation energies were calcu-
lated using hcp-Co, fcc-Al and bcc-W as reference states. The small
scatter in formation energy suggests that the SQSs obtained from
ATAT reliably replicate the random distribution of Al and W on the
second sub-lattice of these two crystal structures. Furthermore, it
must be pointed out the excellent agreement between the results
shown here and the work of Jiang [8], who calculated the formation
energies to be /C0 0.125 eV/atom and /C0 0.1 18 eV/atom for L1 2-
Co3(Al,W) and D0 19-Co3(Al,W) respectively.
4.2. SISF energy of L1 2-Co3(Al,W)
The DESISF of L1 2-Co3(Al,W) is calculated using Equation (9),
along with the total energies of the SQSs obtained from ATAT. As
described in Section 3, the total energies of the D0 19 SQSs were
calculated at a volume equivalent to the ground state volume of the
L12 SQSs. This means that four distinct values of DESISF can be
obtained from these four SQSs, since the energies of each of the
D0
19 SQSs can be evaluated after making their volume equivalent to
either of the L12 SQSs. All permutations lead to a DESISF in the range
89e93 mJ/m 2. These values can be compared to the DESISF of L1 2-
Ni3Al, which is the g
0
phase in Ni-based superalloys. A survey of the
literature reveals values of DESISF ranging from 40 to 147 mJ/m 2 as
calculated with a variety of methods based on ab initio simulations
[33e36]. The large scatter in the literature is due to differences in
the methodologies used to obtain these results. Using the ANNNI
model, the
DESISF of Ni3Al was found to be 53 mJ/m2. Calculating the
DESISF of Ni 3Al via the same method serves two purposes. First, it
can be observed that the value of DESISF obtained falls within the
range of values obtained from previous studies, suggesting that the
ANNNI model can be used reliably to predict
DESISF. Second, the
value of DESISF for Ni 3Al obtained with the ANNNI model can be
directly compared to the value of DESISF for Co 3(Al,W). It can be
observed that the DESISF of L12-Co3(Al,W) is w75% higher than the
DESISF of L12-Ni3Al.

4.3. Effect of Ta on the SISFE of L1 2-Co3(Al,W)
Next, the effect of Ta on DESISF of L12-Co3(Al,W) was investigated.
The generation of a separate set of SQSs with varying amounts of Al,
W and Ta on the second sub-lattice would be desirable. However,
obtaining these structures with Pk;m comparable to those of an
ideal random arrangement of Al, W and Ta atoms is impractical due
to computational constraints. Indeed the SQSs would need to be
much larger in size, exploding the amount of computational time
required to iteratively check all possible arrangements of the atoms
on the second sub-lattice.
Instead, as a ﬁrst step, the effects of Ta on the total energies of
the same set of 32-atom SQSs used above. In the 32-atom super-
cells, the substitution of an Al or W atom with a Ta atom would lead
to a Ta concentration of w3 at.%, which is roughly equivalent to the
expected concentration of Ta in the
g
0
phase of the alloy studied by
Suzuki and Pollock [2]. The substitution with a Ta atom raises two
concerns. First, each of the 8 sites of the second sub-lattice is
inequivalent. This is because, although each site is surrounded by
12 Co nearest neighbors, higher order neighbors are different at
each site. Therefore, we expect small differences in total energy
when the substitutional Ta atom is located at different sub-lattice
sites within supercells of equal composition. Second, the substitu-
tion with Ta will lead to different concentrations of Al and W,
yielding 32-atom supercells with formula Co

a W atom. As a result, this study is expected to yield the effect of Ta
of DESISF when used as a substitute for either Al or W.

(or any of the 4 W sites). This leads to 4 supercells of L1 2-Co24Al3-
W4Ta1 and 4 supercells of L1 2-Co24Al4W3Ta1. The spread in total
energy values for each of these sets of supercells is 0.099 eV when
Ta substitutes an Al atom and 0.013 eV when Ta substitutes a W
atom in these 32-atom SQSs. This behavior is mirrored for the D0 19
SQSs. Consequently, a larger range in the predicted SISF energies is
expected due to this inequivalence of each sub-lattice site. It can be
noted that the scatter in total energy values obtained when Ta is
substituting Al is larger than when Ta is substituting W. This may be
explained by considering both the larger size difference between Ta
and Al versus Ta and W and the different electronic con ﬁguration
between Ta and Al.

5. Discussion
A previous study has demonstrated a strong positive in ﬂuence
of Ta additions to Co
3(Al,W) on the high-temperature yield strength
[2]. The authors observed a high work hardening rate and an
unusually high superlattice intrinsic stacking fault density after
high-temperature deformation at 1270 K in Ta-containing samples
when compared to the simple ternary alloy (see Fig. 3 ) [2]. The
activation of the a=3h112if111g slip system in the
g
0
precipitates at
higher temperatures, which results in the observed superlattice
intrinsic stacking faults, is linked to the high-temperature strengths
observed in single-crystal Ni- and Co-based superalloys [2]. The
authors suggested that a higher density of SISFs in Ta-containing
alloys could be caused by additions of Ta lowering the
DESISF of
the g
0
phase in Co-based superalloys [2]. This study has employed
ﬁrst-principles calculations to examine this hypothesis.
According to the results shown in Section 4, Ta additions are
expected to increase the DESISF of the g
0
phase of Co-based super-
alloys, and it would then be expected that precipitate shearing by
a=3h112i superlattice partial dislocations would be in ﬂuenced by
Ta additions. This is a direct consequence of the fact that Ta addi-
tions lower the formation energy of the L1
2 phase relative to the
D019 phase (see Equation (9)). This is expected since experimentally
Ta is added to promote the formation and stability of the g
0
precipitates [1,2].
Since Ta additions do not lower the DESISF of the g
0
phase, the
anomalous work hardening effect generated by Ta must not be
directly due to the presence of SISFs within the
g
0
precipitates.
However, a higher DESISF would require a higher shear stress for
initial penetration of dislocations into the Ta-containing L1 2 phase
compared to the simple Co3(Al,W) ternary g
0
phase. Examination of
the TEM micrographs in Fig. 3 also reveals that the volume fraction
of g

phase is considerably higher in the Ta-containing alloy. This is
expected, since the volume fraction of g

precipitates strongly
depends on composition and deformation tests were carried out at
temperatures close to the
g

L12-Co3(Al,W) phase is only metastable at 0 K, as it is expected
to decompose into hcp-Co, D019-Co3W and B 2-CoAl.

g

be caused by changes in average valence electron concentra-
