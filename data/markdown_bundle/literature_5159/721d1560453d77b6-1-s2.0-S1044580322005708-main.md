ABSTRACT 
A low-cost rare earth Mg-5Y-Ni alloy containing LPSO phase was fabricated via multi-pass rolling in this work, 
and strength and ductility were enhanced based on dislocation regulation and grain refinement at various rolling 
temperatures. The alloy was designed to contain about 9.6% lamellar 18R-LPSO phase after homogenization. All 
alloys were completely recrystallized after rolling, and the alloy rolled at 450 
◦
C (450R) achieved the finest 
recrystallized grains with an average size of 3.97 μ m. The {0002} basal texture intensity decreased and average 
Schmid factors of non-basal slips increase with increasing rolling temperature. 450R alloy exhibits the highest 
strength and that rolled at 500 
◦
C (500R) exhibits the best strength-ductility synergy and work hardening 
capability. Accumulation of dislocations and stacking faults promotes uniform and complete recrystallization. 
Dislocation and grain refinement strengthening effects provide most strength increments. High-density dislo -
cations promote dynamic recovery process during tensile deformation and results in a rapidly decrease in stage I 
and a low level in stage III of work hardening rates. Grain refinement induced by dislocation accumulation, 
dynamic recovery process promoted by high-density dislocations and high dislocation activity of non-basal slips 
improve the ductility. This work illustrates the feasibility of dislocation regulation to synergistically enhance the 
strength and ductility of Mg alloys. 
1. Introduction 
Mg-rare earth (RE)-based alloys, as the lightest metallic structural 
materials, have attracted substantial attention for decades due to their 
excellent mechanical properties at room and elevated temperatures, 
which can be attributed to the significant solid solution strengthening by 
solute clusters, precipitation strengthening by several RE-containing 
compounds and texture weakening by deformation accommodation 
[ 1 – 4 ]. Y, Gd and Sm elements have higher solid solubility and lower 
cost, which can enhance precipitation strengthening effect and reduce 
the preparation cost of high strength Mg-RE alloys [ 5 – 7 ]. Tong et al. [ 5 ] 
fabricated a high-strength Mg-8.2Gd-3.8Y-1.0Zn-0.4Zr alloy with an 
ultra-high yield strength (YS) of 417 MPa and moderate elongation (EL) 
of 12.9% by decreased-temperature multi-directional forging. However, 
the higher strength of alloys means that the ductility could be inevitably 
sacrificed [ 8 , 9 ]. Disappointedly, due to the limited slip systems of Mg 
alloys with hexagonally close-packed (hcp) structure, this conflict could 
be more serious [ 10 ]. 
One strategy is to screen effective RE-containing strengthening 
phases which could coordinate plastic deformation so that the ductility 
of the alloy is not seriously damaged. Among all RE-containing 
strengthening phases, long period stacking ordered (LPSO) phases are 
considered to be with extremely high strength and good ductility [ 11 ]. 
In recent years, numerous high-performance Mg-RE alloys have been 
developed by the addition of Zn and Ni elements, which can induce the 
formation of various LPSO phases [ 12 – 16 ]. Su et al. [ 17 ] developed an 
ultrahigh strength Mg-12Gd-2Y-1Zn-Mn alloy containing 14H-LPSO 
phase with an ultimate tensile strength (UTS) of 509 MPa by hot 
extrusion and ageing. Compared with Mg-Y-Zn alloys containing LPSO 
phases, Mg-Y-Ni alloys are also considered to possess potentially

4. Discussion 
4.1. Recrystallization mechanisms and texture evolution 
As observed in Fig. 2 , completely recrystallized microstructure can 
be obtained after multi-pass rolling. It is known that DRX process should 
occur when the strain reaches a critical level. However, during the multi- 
pass rolling process, the reduction amount of single pass is lower than 
20%, which can hardly activate the nucleation of recrystallized grains. 
For further investigating the recrystallization mechanism, it is necessary 
to study the microstructure evolution of rolled alloys under lower 
reduction. Fig. 10 shows the SEM and TEM images of as-rolled Mg-5Y-Ni 
alloy with a thickness-reduction of about 30% at 500 
◦
C (3 passes). 
Lamellar LPSO phases are distributed in parallel, which indicates that 
these LPSO phases are intragranular phases in the same coarse grain, 
implying that the coarse grain has not been recrystallized at this 
reduction ( Fig. 10 a). Moreover, intragranular staggered traces can be 
observed as well, which demonstrates serious lattice distortion occurs in 
the coarse grains ( Fig. 10 b). Further TEM observation results show that a 
large number of dislocations arrange in parallel to form a dislocation 
wall and a larger distortion area around the dislocations ( Fig. 10 c), and 
the dislocations are parallel to neither (0002) nor ( 11 20 ) planes ac -
cording to the SAED pattern, which suggests these dislocations belong to 
non-basal slips. Such piled-up dislocations do not appear near the grain 
boundaries and LPSO/Mg interfaces but in grains. Besides, large

dislocation density reaches a critical level. 
4.2. Strengthening mechanisms 
The strengthening effects in Mg alloys can be attributed to second 
phase, solid solution, grain refinement and dislocations. In this investi -
gation, no other precipitates have been found in as-rolled alloys and the 
18R-LPSO phase remains a bulk morphology with a larger size, which 
implies that the solid solution atoms (Y) and second phases (mainly 18R- 
LPSO phase) have little change before and during hot rolling at various 
rolling temperatures. Therefore, the solid solution strengthening 
contribution of Y atoms and dispersed strengthening of LPSO phases 
could be considered to be similar in the three as-rolled Mg-5Y-Ni alloys, 
and the difference of YS among the three as-rolled alloys is mainly 
related to grain size and dislocation density.

4.3. Work-hardening behavior and ductility enhancement 
Previous studies have discussed the effects of grain size, texture and 
dislocation densities on work hardening behaviors. At the beginning of 
deformation (stage I), dislocation proliferation dominates plastic 
deformation, and when the new dislocations meet the original disloca -
tions, the opposite sign dislocations will annihilate and dynamic re -
covery (DRV) process occurs, resulting in the decrease of θ value [ 52 ]. 
Due to the high dislocation density in the rolled alloy ( > 2 × 10
14 
m
 2
), 
the DRV process can be accelerated, and θ value rapidly decreases. Be -
sides, the alloy with finer grains contains more grain boundaries, which 
can profit the proliferation and annihilation of dislocations. The grains 
have recrystallized in the rolled Mg-5Y-Ni alloys during rolling in this 
work, which introduces much more grain boundaries, which facilitates 
the DRV process as well. As the deformation continues, the proliferation 
and annihilation of dislocations reach a balance state and the θ value in 
stage III remains a remarkably low level until the end of deformation due 
to the highly efficient DRV process. 
According to the above analyses, the ductility variation of all as- 
rolled alloys can be understood. First of all, grain refinement can bet -
ter support grain boundary sliding which contributes to additional 
plastic deformation and adjust slip orientation. Besides, 400R and 450R 
alloys have smaller recrystallized grains, while the dislocation density is 
higher than those of other alloys, which causes that the two alloys still 
remain a high stress level when the work hardening and DRV process 
reach balance. However, the dislocation density in 400R alloy is also 
high, the dislocation density near the grain boundaries is significantly 
higher than that in grains, which may lead to grain boundary brittleness 
and weaken the ductility. Simultaneously, stronger basal texture and 
lower SFs of non-basal slips in 400R and 450R alloys limit the contin -
uation of dislocation slips, and the fracture occurs more easily. By 
contrary, 500R alloy can profit continuous dislocation slips owing to 
lower density of dislocations, weaker texture and higher activity of non- 
basal during deformation. As a result, 500R alloy can obtain higher UTS 
and H
c 
value due to the relatively lower YS. 
In all, accumulation of dislocations and stacking faults during multi- 
pass rolling and immediate annealing promotes uniform and complete 
DRX process, which provides significant grain refinement. High-density 
dislocations and grain refinement achieve the main strength increments 
for as-rolled alloys. During tensile deformation, grain refinement 
induced by dislocation accumulation, DRV process promoted by high- 
density dislocations and high dislocation activity of non-basal slips 
improve the ductility. The results suggest that dislocation regulation and 
grain refinement can synergistically enhance the strength and ductility 
of Mg-5Y-Ni alloy. 
5. Conclusions 
In this work, a low-cost rare earth Mg-5Y-Ni alloy containing LPSO 
phase was fabricated via multi-pass rolling, and strength and ductility 
was enhanced based on dislocation regulation and grain refinement at 
various rolling temperatures. Recrystallization mechanisms, strength -
ening effects, work hardening and ductility enhancement were analyzed 
as well. The results drawn from this work were summarized as follows: 
(1) About 9.6% lamellar 18R-LPSO phase and few Mg

all as-rolled Mg-5Y-Ni alloys are mainly composed of uniform 
recrystallized grains, and 450R alloy achieved the finest recrys -
tallized grains with an average size of 3.97 μ m. Basal texture 
intensity decreases and SFs of non-basal slips increase with 
increasing rolling temperature. Dislocations and stacking faults 
are accumulated during multi-pass rolling and immediate 
annealing, which promotes uniform and complete 
recrystallization.
