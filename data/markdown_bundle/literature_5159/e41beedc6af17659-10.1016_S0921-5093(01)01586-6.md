Design of Laves phase strengthened ferritic heat resisting steels in
the Fe–Cr–Nb(–Ni) system
Keisuke Yamamoto *, Yoshisato Kimura, Fu-Gao Wei, Yoshinao Mishima
Department of Materials Science and Engineering , Tokyo Institute of Technology , 4259 Nagatsuta, Midori-ku, Yokohama 226-8502, Japan
Abstract
A new class of heat resisting ferritic steels is investigated in the Fe–Cr–Nb and Fe–Cr–Nb–Ni systems, in which the major
strengthener is the Fe 2Nb Laves phase. Ferritic steels strengthened by Laves phase are expected to show excellent high
temperature strength, while the brittleness of Laves phase may lower the toughness of the alloy. The /p97-Fe/Fe2Nb two-phase
microstructure is selected to improve mechanical properties through changing volume fraction and morphology of Laves phase.
Effects of Nb and Ni contents on the microstructure and mechanical properties of the alloys, ﬁxed at 10 at.% Cr, have been
systematically investigated. Mechanical properties were evaluated by tensile tests conducted at room temperature, 873 and 973 K.
The tensile test revealed that the room temperature ductility decreases with increasing Nb content. The alloys with 1.0–1.5 at.%
Nb are found to exhibit a good balance between room temperature ductility and high temperature strength. © 2002 Elsevier

1. Introduction
High Cr ferritic heat resisting steels are widely used
in power generating plants for high creep resistance and
superior oxidation resistance. These steels based on
tempered martensite matrix are strengthened by solu-
tion hardening of such elements as Mo, V, Nb, Ti and
by precipitation hardening of such phases as MC car-
bides and MX carbonitrides. Recently, utilization of
more complex precipitation involving intermetallic
Laves phase has drawn a growing interest for the
enhancement of creep strength [1].
The Laves phase is an intermetallic compound of
A
2B-type with topologically close packed (TCP) struc-
ture and is expected to have high temperature strength.
Ohta et al. found that the increase in the creep rupture
strength by the addition of Mo, W and Ti in 2.25Cr
ferritic steels was due to the precipitation of the Laves
phases (Fe
2Mo, Fe2W and Fe 2Ti) [2]. Additionally it is
suggested that ﬁne precipitation of Fe 2W Laves phase
during creep under service conditions improves creep
resistance in recently developed ferritic steels with W
additions [3]. It has been pointed out, however, that the
precipitation of the Laves phase reduced toughness of
the alloy particularly at ambient temperature.
The present authors have made an effort on the
development of a new class of ferritic heat resisting
steels, in which the Laves phase is solely the precipita-
tion strengthener in the ferrite matrix. In ternary Fe–
Cr–Nb system, it is possible to produce two-phase
alloys of /p97-Fe/Fe
2Nb. In the ternary system at a Cr
concentration of 10 at.%, /p97-Fe/Fe2Nb is stable up to
high temperature. With additions of an austenite for-
mer Ni, the quaternary Fe–Cr–Nb–Ni system has a
/p103–/p97transformation. By utilizing the /p103–/p97transforma-
tion and by controlling the transformation temperature
through adjustment of Ni content, we could modify the
substructure of the /p97-Fe matrix through which the
morphology of the Fe
2Nb Laves phase may be varied.
The objective of the present work is to evaluate the
microstructure and mechanical properties of /p97-Fe/
Fe2Nb two phase alloys in Fe–Cr–Nb and Fe–Cr–
Nb–Ni systems.* Corresponding author.

2. Experimental procedure
Nominal compositions of the alloys used in the
present study are shown in Table 1. The alloys were
prepared by induction melting in an alumina crucible
and pouring the molten material into a mold in an
argon atmosphere. The ingots of 300 g (30 mm in
diameter and 50 mm high) were then heated at 1373 K
and hot rolled from 30 to 6 mm plates. Finishing
temperature for the rolling process was higher than
1073 K.
The /p103–/p97transformation temperatures for several al-
loys were determined by dilatometer. The cylindrical
specimens, with a diameter of 3 mm and a height of 10
mm, were machined from the plates. Specimens were
heated to 1273 K for 2 h, held for 5 min and cooled at
a rate of /p580.8 K s
−1.
Microstructures of as-rolled alloys and those an-
nealed at 1023 K for 1 h were characterized by optical
microscopy (OM), scanning electron microscopy (SEM)
and transmission electron microscopy (TEM). The
specimens for OM and SEM were mechanically pol-
ished and then etched in a picric acid solution. Thin foil
specimens for TEM examination were obtained by elec-
trodischarge machining 3 mm discs and then twin-jet
electropolished using a mixture of 10% perchoric acid
and 90% methanol at /p58223 K. They were examined by
a JEOL LEM-2011 transmission electron microscope
operating at 200 kV.
Specimens for ﬂat tensile tests (15 mm in gage length,
3 mm width and 1 mm thickness) were prepared by
electrodischarge machining from as-rolled plate. Tensile
tests of as-rolled specimens and annealed specimens at
1023 K for 1 h were performed under the strain rate
1.1 ×10
−4 s−1 at room temperature, and at 873 and
973 K in a vacuum of /p5810−4 torr. Tensile direction
was parallel to the rolling direction.
