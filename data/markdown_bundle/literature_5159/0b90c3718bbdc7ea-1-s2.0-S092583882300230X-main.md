1. Introduction
Tun gsten (W) alloy s are typical two-phase alloy s that are com -
posed of tungsten particles and a matrix phase. The toughne ss of the 
alloy is supported by the matrix phase, which is composed of Ni, Fe , 
Cu, Co, and other components [1–3] . Tungst en alloy s ha ve the ad -
v antage s of high stren gth, toughness, and density , and thus, tungsten 
alloy s are widely used in adv anced weapons and national defense 
fields [4] .
In pr evious studies, it has been found that adding rhenium (R e) 
[5,6] , mol ybdenum (Mo) [7] or other elements can impro ve the 
strength of tungst en alloy s. Ho wev er , it w eakens the interfacial 
bonding of tungst en alloy s, and this significantly reduces the 
toughness of tungst en alloy s [8] . Finding suitable additional ele -
ments has become a hot re search topic for impro ving the stren gth of 
tungsten alloy s without decreasi ng their toughness.
T antalum (T a) has a high melting point (2996 °C), high density 
(1 6.6 g/cm
3

cubic (BCC) structure as W and an atomic radiu s that is similar to 
that of W ; these char acteristics mean that T a and W can be mix ed to 
form an infinite solid solution [9] . Man y studies ha ve demonstrat ed 
that adding T a can form an orde red orientation, which can effec -
tiv ely impro ve the brittleness of tungst en particles [1 0, 1 1] . Adding T a 
can also greatl y impro ve the stren gth of tungst en alloy s ( ∼ 1 025 MPa)

use in tungst en alloy s.
How ever , the higher sintering temper ature (1 450 °C) causes Ni- 
T a intermetallics (IMC) to gro w rap idly and destro y the matrix phase 
[1 3] , and this decreases the alloy tou ghness. Previous studies ha ve
