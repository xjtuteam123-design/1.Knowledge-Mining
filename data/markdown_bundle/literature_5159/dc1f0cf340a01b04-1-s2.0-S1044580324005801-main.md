ABSTRACT
γ’ -precipitate strengthened cobalt-based superalloys show promise for higher temperature-capability over nickel-
based superalloys due to higher melting temperatures. This study investigates a multicomponent CoNi-based
superalloy with composition of 54Co-30Ni-9Al-1Ta-4Ti-2-W (at.%) in terms of elemental partitioning and site
occupancy. Atom probe tomography (APT) reveals preferential partitioning of Ni, Al, Ti, Ta and W to the
γ’ -precipitate and Co to the γ -matrix. Aberration-corrected high-angle annular dark-field scanning transmission
electron microscopy (HAADF-STEM) and energy dispersive spectrometer (EDS) along the [001] zone axis of
γ’ -precipitate with L1
2
structure indicate that Co, Ni substitute at A-sites, whereas Al, Ti, Ta, W prefer the B-sites.
Density functional theory (DFT) calculations further confirm the results of partitioning behaviors and site
preferences. Clarifying elemental partitioning and site occupancy provides valuable insights into alloy design
principles for advanced CoNi-based superalloys.
1. Introduction
The novel γ’ -strengthened cobalt-based superalloys not only inherit
the excellent environmental resistance of conventional cobalt-based
alloys (oxidation and corrosion resistance), but also possess melting
temperatures of 50 – 150
◦
C higher than nickel-based superalloys [ 1 , 2 ].
They are therefore expected to break the temperature-capability
bottleneck of superalloys. However, the metastable γ’ -Co
3
(Al,W) phase
with L1
2
structure in Co-Al-W alloys eventually decomposes into detri-
mental phases like Co
3
W and CoAl, imposing substantial constraints on
the development and application of γ’ -strengthened cobalt-based alloys
[ 3 , 4 ].
Alloying additions like Ni, Ti, Ta, Nb, Mo and W can effectively
stabilize the γ’ -precipitate to varying extents [ 5,6 ]. Specifically, the
addition of Ni widens the γ / γ’ two-phase region in Co-Al-W alloys,
improving the alloying content and enhancing the microstructural sta-
bility of Co-Al-W-based superalloys [ 7 , 8 ]. This finding throws light on
the development of cobalt-based alloys. Currently, 25 – 35 at.% Ni is
typically incorporated in γ’ -strengthened multi-component cobalt-based
superalloys to improve overall high-temperature capabilities, leading to
the development of CoNi-based superalloys. The development of multi-
component CoNi-based superalloys presents an effective solution to
address the issues of microstructural stability and high density
associated with γ’ -precipitate strengthened cobalt-based alloys, and
further enhancing high-temperature performance [ 2 , 9 ]. However, as
alloying levels increase, the role of alloying elements becomes complex
and challenging to predict. This complexity poses new challenges for
alloy design in the pursuit of enhanced high-temperature performance
[ 2 , 5 ].
The partitioning and site occupancy of alloying elements in γ -matrix
and γ’ -precipitate are the fundamentals of superalloys, which directly
influence the thermodynamic stability of γ’ -precipitate, and lattice
misfit, dislocation dissociation, antiphase boundaries, and dislocation
motion during deformation [ 9 ]. Previous investigations into the parti-
tioning behaviors and site occupancy of alloying elements in cobalt-
based alloys have predominantly utilized model systems such as Co-
Al-W-X and Co-Ti-X, employing atom probe tomography and first-
principles calculations [ 10 – 13 ]. However, extrapolating these results
to multicomponent alloys may be challenging due to complex interac-
tion effects.
Aberration-corrected high-angle annular dark-field scanning trans-
mission electron microscopy (HAADF-STEM) allows direct visualization
of atomic occupancy [ 14 ], while atom probe tomography (APT) pro-
vides 3D atom-by-atom analysis of chemistry [ 15 ]. By integrating these
advanced techniques of aberration-corrected STEM, APT, and first-
principles calculations, alloying principles can be elucidated for CoNi-

The differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) measurement was per-

γ’ -precipitates were determined with the image analysis software Image
J ( https://imagej.nih.gov/ij/ ). Measurements were based on at least 400
particles. Detailed methodologies for calculating the volume fraction are
available in the Ref [ 16 ]. Aberration-corrected HAADF-STEM imaging
and EDS (Energy Dispersive Spectrometer) mapping were performed
using an FEI Themis Z microscope equipped with a Super-X EDS system
to investigate atomic occupancy. Specimens preparation for trans-
mission electron microscopy (TEM) involves selecting a representative
portion of the material, cutting it into small pieces, and mechanical
grinding to 50 μ m thickness, punching into 3 mm diameter disks, and
then further thinning by ion milling at ambient temperature. Final in-
spection ensures the desired thickness and absence of defects before
imaging in the TEM. Lattice parameters and misfit were measured by
high-resolution X-ray diffraction using a Bruker D8 diffractometer, the
scanning is set to 3 s/step at the range of 42
◦
-45
◦
, the peak shape of
Gaussian is assumed for fitting. Atom probe tomography (APT) was
conducted using a CAMECA LEAP ® 4000X HR to obtain 3D re-
constructions and compositional distributions, with data analysis per-
formed in IVAS 3.6.12. APT specimens preparation requires mounting
the sample onto a needle-shaped substrate, followed by preliminary
thinning and preparation of a sharp needle tip based on a standard
focused ion beam (FIB) procedure. First-principles DFT (density func-
tional theory) calculations were carried out using the VASP (Vienna ab
initio simulation package) package to elucidate alloying mechanisms,
with a PBE-GGA pseudopotential [ 17 , 18 ], K-points of 6 × 6 × 1 and cut-
off energy of 400 eV. The convergence tolerance was 10 – 5 eV/atom
[ 19 ].

Typically, the distribution and partitioning behaviors of alloying
elements in the γ / γ’ phases significantly influence the microstructure
and high-temperature mechanical properties of alloys. Exploring other
multi-component Co-based alloys or even the ternary base system Co-Al-
W could provide additional insights into partitioning behavior can
enhance the breadth and depth of the discussion and analysis of alloying
effect on the Co-based superalloys. A comprehensive comparison is
therefore made between the elemental partitioning in the Co-Al-W-X
model alloys and multi-component CoNi-based superalloys, as shown
in Fig. 2 . The partitioning coefficient K

conversely, when K < 1, the alloying elements partition to the γ -matrix.
It can be seen that partitioning behaviors were not significant for
alloying elements in the Co-Al-W-X alloys, exemplified by partitioning
coefficient of Al approaching 1. The addition of Ni enhances the parti-
tioning behaviors of alloying elements in CoNi-based superalloys. For
instance, the partitioning coefficient of Al can reach approximately 2.
Another noteworthy element is W, an important γ’ -precipitate stabilizer
exhibiting more pronounced partitioning to γ’ -precipitate in Co-Al-W
alloys [ 2 , 5 ]. The solubility of W in the γ -matrix can be enhanced by
Ni addition [ 7 ], the partitioning coefficient of W therefore declines with
Ni addition. Finally, the present results generally fall within the range of
results for CoNi-based superalloys.
The addition of Ni, along with other alloying elements, synergisti-
cally increases the γ’ -precipitate field in multicomponent Co-based su-
peralloys. For example, substituting 30% of Co with Ni in a Co-10Al-
5Mo-2Nb alloy elevates the γ’ -precipitate dissolution temperature by

incorporation of 30% Ni has become the standard configuration in the
development of most Co-based superalloys. This is also the most sig-
nificant distinguishing feature between multi-component Co-based su-
peralloys and model alloys.

Typically, atomic site occupancy in intermetallic compounds is pri-
marily influenced by magnetic interactions and strain energy resulting
from the incorporation of atoms with varying sizes into the lattice
[ 37 – 39 ]. Table 2 lists the atomic radii, electronic structures, and site
occupancy of these elements in the γ’ -precipitate. The atomic radii of Co
and Ni are approximately 0.126 and 0.125 nm, respectively, while
atomic radii of alloying elements like Al and Ti exceed 0.14 nm. This size
disparity indicates that smaller atoms (Co, Ni) preferentially occupy A-
sites, and larger atoms occupy B-sites, consistent with the prediction
based on atomic size effects [ 14 ]. The electronic structure of alloying
elements is further considered [ 40 ], elements such as Al, Ti, Ta, and W,
with d-bands less than half-filled (d-electron count less than 5) pre-
dominantly occupy B-sites. Whereas elements like Co and Ni, with d-
bands greater than half-filled, exhibit a preference for A-sites. Therefore,
the atomic structure of alloying elements is crucial in determining their
site occupancy behavior. This should be further considered in predicting
lattice parameters, misfits, and local chemical ordering in the alloys.
Superalloys rely heavily on the dissolution temperature of γ’ -pre-
cipitates and the coherency of the γ / γ’ interface to determine their
microstructural features and mechanical properties. These aspects are
intricately linked to how alloying elements distribute and occupy posi-
tions within the γ matrix and γ’ phases. A comprehensive understanding
of alloy performance can be achieved by simultaneously considering the
distribution and site preference characteristics of alloying elements.
Especially for the solvus temperature of the γ’ -precipitates and the co-
herency of the γ / γ’ interface. Alloying elements such as W, Ti, and Ta,
which predominantly occupy B-sites in γ’ -precipitates, play a crucial
role in enhancing the thermodynamic stability of γ’ -precipitates. This
enhancement is evidenced by the increased solvus temperature and
volume fraction of γ’ -precipitates upon their addition [ 2 , 5 ]. Notably, Ta,
known for its effective precipitation strengthening, significantly elevates
both the solvus temperature and the γ / γ’ interfacial misfit due to its
pronounced partitioning behavior and its occupancy of B-sites in
γ’ -precipitates (see Fig. 2 and Fig. 5 ). However, the addition of Ni, which
predominantly occupies the A-sites, may not significantly affect the
solvus temperature and volume fraction of γ’ -precipitates [ 7 ]. In addi-
tion to the impact on microstructural stability, the distribution and
occupation of alloying elements also influence the lattice parameters of
γ -matrix and γ’ -precipitates, thereby altering the misfit. Previous studies
have shown that W, Ti, and Ta increase the misfit, while the addition of
Ni reduces it [ 7 , 21 , 40 ]. Regarding mechanical properties, the incorpo-
ration of W, Ti, and Ta tends to enhance mechanical performance to
varying degrees [ 41 , 42 ]. DFT calculations suggest that when these ele-
ments occupy B-sites, they increase the antiphase boundary (APB) en-
ergy of γ’ -precipitates, thereby strengthening resistance to dislocation
cutting and enhancing the overall strengthening effect [ 43 , 44 ]. This
dual impact on thermodynamic stability and mechanical properties
underscores the critical role of alloying element distribution and site
occupancy in optimizing the performance of γ’ -strengthened

4. Summary
In this study, the CoNi-based alloys exhibit a γ / γ’ coherent two-phase
microstructure with a misfit of 0.44%, and the volume fraction of the
γ’ -precipitate is 48%. The APT results show that the alloying elements
Ni, Al, Ti, Ta, and W are more enriched in the γ’ -precipitate, whereas Co
is more partitioned to the γ -matrix. HAADF-STEM imaging and EDS
mapping further clarify site occupancy of γ’ -precipitates with L1
2
A
3
B
structure, Co and Ni substitute A-sites while Al, Ti, Ta, and W occupy the
B- sites and these results are confirmed by First Principle calculations.
Clarifying the distribution behavior and site occupancy of elements in
cobalt-based alloys can clarify the alloying principle and provide
compositional design guidelines for advanced superalloys.
