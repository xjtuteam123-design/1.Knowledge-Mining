The element Re is known to be a very potent strengthener concerning the creep properties of
Ni-base superalloys. In this paper the in ﬂuence of Re on the properties of new c9-strengthened
Co-base superalloys is addressed. Atom probe tomography reveals that Re partitions preferentially
to the c phase, but not as pronounced as in ni-base superalloys. Nanoindentation and micro-pillar
compression tests of the c9 phase indicate an increase of the hardness and the critical resolved
shear stress caused by a considerable concentration of Re in the c9 phase. Creep investigations
show that the positive effect of Re is by far not as pronounced as in Ni-base superalloys. Several
effects, which can contribute to this behavior, such as the lower Re concentration in c and hence
a slightly reduced effective diffusion coef ﬁcient, a smaller diffusion barrier of Re in Co compared
to Ni, a slightly lower lattice mis ﬁt and c9 volume fraction of the Re-containing alloy, are
discussed.
I. INTRODUCTION
Co-base superalloys strengthened by coherently em-
bedded Co 3(Al,W) c9 precipitates were discovered in
2006 in the ternary system Co –Al–W.1 This novel
material class with a c/c9 microstructure similar to
Ni-base superalloys has the potential for high temperature
applications. The strength and creep properties of Co –
Al–W-based superalloys are comparable to wrought
2 and
ﬁrst generation cast 3–6 Ni-base superalloys. In Ni-base
superalloys refractory elements, especially Re, are added
to increase the high temperature capability and creep
properties.
7,8 Re acts as a strong solid solution strength-
ening element in the c phase9,10 since it strongly
partitions to the matrix phase. 11–14 Furthermore, Re is
the slowest diffusing element in Ni-base superalloys and
thus of great importance for retarding diffusion controlled
creep processes.
15–18 Therefore, Re would be a very
interesting alloying element also for Co-base superalloys
if it behaves there in a similar manner. Recently,
Neumeier et al.
19 published diffusion coef ﬁcients for
Re and other elements in fcc Co. They found that Re is
also the slowest diffusing element in fcc Co among the
elements typically added to superalloys. The diffusivity
of the transition metal solutes is typically lower than in Ni
alloys. These ﬁndings suggest that Re could also play
a signi ﬁcant role for improving the creep resistance of
Co-base superalloys. However, so far there are no
investigations on the partitioning behavior of Re in c/c9
Co-base superalloys. Furthermore it is not clear how Re
inﬂuences the mechanical properties of the c and c9 phase
and the creep properties. Suzuki et al.
3 investigated the
mechanical properties of a Re containing Co –8.8Al–
9.8W–2Re alloy. They did not observe any increase in
the compression strength up to 800 °C compared to a Re-
free alloy. The main topic of this study is to clarify if the
addition of Re to Co-base superalloys has a similar
beneﬁcial impact on the mechanical properties as it has
for Ni-base superalloys.

III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
The microstructures observed after different heat treat-
ments are shown in Fig. 1. All samples exhibit a typical
c/c9 microstructure, where the c9 phase appears in bright
contrast as probed in backscattered electron mode (BSE).
The c9 volume fraction (see Table II) determined by
the lever rule from APT measurements after standard heat
treatment and 1050 °C/5 h show that in both cases the
ERBOCo-Re0 has a slightly higher c9 volume fraction.
The c9 volume fraction determined by digital image
analysis using ImageJ is slightly higher but shows
a similar trend. The morphology of the c9 precipitates
at high temperatures changes from rather cuboidal [see
Figs. 1(e) and 1(g)] to more round-shaped precipitates
[see Figs. 1(f) and 1(h)] through the addition of Re. This
implies that the lattice mis ﬁt and thus the coherency
stresses are reduced by the addition of Re. The c9 solvus
temperature was determined to approximately 1150 °C
for both alloys. The coarsened microstructure after slow
cooling from 1200 to 1050 °C is characterized by large c9
precipitates with a size of up to 10 lm [see Figs. 1(i) and
1(j)]. This coarsened microstructure allows to mill micro-
pillars from c9 precipitates, hence it is not necessary to
produce a single phase c9 alloy with a different alloy
composition. Due to the formation of secondary c9
precipitates [see Figs. 1(i) and 1(j)] in the matrix channels
the nanomechanical tests were performed only on the c9
phase, since the mechanical properties of the matrix
phase would have been in ﬂuenced by an additional
precipitation hardening.

The results from nanoindentation testing closely cor-
relate with the micro-pillar experiments which show an
increased critical resolved shear stress (CRSS) due to the
addition of Re. For compression along the [100] loading
direction, the deformation is expected to operate on the
{111}h110i slip system (see deformed pillar in Fig. 3)
and thus the CRSS for this slip system is determined.
To ﬁnally evaluate the performance at high temperatures,
the creep properties need to be considered. During creep the
microstructure of both alloys changes and c/c9 rafts form
perpendicular to the compressive stress axis [see Fig. 4(b)].
This indicates a positive lattice misﬁta tt h et e s tt e m p e r a t u r e
of 1050 °C
6 which is different to most Ni-base superalloys

inversion takes place in both alloys so that the former c9

Both ERBOCo alloys show similar creep curves; however,
after reaching the minimum creep rate [see Fig. 4(a)], the
Re-free samples show an accelerated softening compared to
the Re-containing samples, which may be due to a faster
coarsening of the microstructure of the Re-free sample due

low strains. This observation indicates that most of the
deformation takes place primarily in the c phase at the
chosen creep conditions. Hence, this argument is not
considered to be the reason for the observed results.
(iv) Due to the enrichment of Re in the c phase of Ni-
base superalloys its lattice parameter increases, giving
rise to a higher absolute value of the negative lattice
misﬁt. For the same reason, the positive lattice mis ﬁti n
Co-base superalloys is reduced. As a consequence, the
morphology of the c9 precipitates in the alloy ERBOCo-
Re0 is more cuboidal, whereas in ERBOCo-Re1 more
rounded precipitates form [see Figs. 1(e) and 1(f)]. It is
conceivable that these roundish precipitates in combina-
tion with the smaller coherency stresses could reduce the
creep strength as well.
Furthermore, the positive mis ﬁt produces tensile
stresses which would probably accelerate diffusion
whereas the compressive coherency stresses in the matrix
of Ni-base alloys would even retard it.
41 This effect might
counteract the slow diffusivity of Re in Co.
(v) The microstructural analysis and APT results indicate
that the c9 volume fraction of the Re-containing sample is
slightly lower, which could counterbalance a strengthening
effect of Re. However, this in ﬂuence alone is quite small
and cannot explain the large difference of the minimum
creep rate between Co-and Ni-base superalloys.
IV. CONCLUSIONS
Creep experiments show that Re only leads to a mar-
ginal improvement of the creep resistance in Co-base
superalloys compared to multinary Ni-base superalloys.
This is at least partly caused by the lower partitioning of
Re to the c phase as shown by APT measurements. In
consequence the effective diffusion coef ﬁcient is only
slightly reduced by Re and thus the minimum creep rate
of the Re-containing alloy should only be slightly lower
than that of the Re-free alloy. Further direct and indirect
effects such as the smaller diffusion barrier of Re in Co,
a slightly lower lattice mis ﬁt of the Re-containing alloy
and a slightly lower c9 volume fraction could contribute
to this effect too. As shown by nanoindentation and
micro-pillar compression tests, the hardness as well as the
CRSS of the c9 phase is increased due to a signi ﬁcant
solubility of Re in the c9 phase.
These observations suggest that Re is less bene ﬁcial in
Co-base superalloys than in Ni-base superalloys, where
Re signi ﬁcantly improves the high-temperature creep
properties.
