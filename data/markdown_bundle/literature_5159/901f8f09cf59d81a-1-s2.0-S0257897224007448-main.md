ABSTRACT
With the increasing speed of high-speed train, the performance requirements of brake disc friction surface
become more and more strict, so it is necessary to study the performance of brake disc friction surface of high-
speed train. This study successfully prepared Stellite 21 alloy coatings by laser cladding with the addition of Nb
elements. EDS, XRD, SEM, EBSD and mechanical properties test methods were used to investigate the effect of Nb
on the microstructure, microhardness, micro-shear properties and wear resistance of Stellite 21 alloy coating.
The results indicate that the addition of Nb prevents the continuous growth of columnar grains in the Stellite 21
alloy coating, resulting in more short and small columnar grains, dendritic grains, and cellular grains. The Stellite
21 alloy coating is mainly composed of γ -Co, with a small amount of M
23
C
6
carbides precipitated. The addition of
Nb induces martensitic phase transformation ( γ -Co → ε -Co) and the precipitation of Laves strengthening phase in
the coating, which increases with increasing Nb content. Particularly, when the Nb content reaches 5 %, the ε -Co
content reaches approximately 5.9 %. The hardness and shear strength of the coating increase with the increasing
of Nb addition, which is mainly attributed to the solid solution strengthening and precipitation strengthening of
the Stellite 21 alloy coating caused by Nb addition, which increases the hardness of the coating by about 21.4 %,
thereby improving the wear resistance of the coating. However, it also leads to a decrease in the plasticity of the
coating. Nevertheless, compared to the alloy coating without Nb, the shear strength and wear resistance of the
coating are increased by approximately 31.5 % and 49 %, respectively. The study demonstrates that the addition
of Nb plays an important role in improving the shear performance, hardness, and wear resistance of Stellite21
alloy coatings.
1. Introduction
In recent years, with the rapid development of society, the speed of
high-speed trains has increased significantly, especially with the
development of high-speed railways in China, which has attracted global
attention. However, for high-speed trains to run fast, they also need to
stop efficiently. The brake disc, as one of the key components of the train
braking system, has drawn attention from various sectors of society and
researchers [ 1 – 3 ]. However, the commonly used 24CrNiMo steel brake
disc is already struggling to meet the demands of higher speeds [ 4 ],
despite its high strength, toughness, wear resistance, and thermal fa-
tigue resistance [ 5 ]. Directly scrapping the brake discs would result in
significant economic losses. Therefore, applying a wear-resistant layer
on the friction surface of the brake disc is of great significance.
Among the various repair technologies, laser cladding (LC) stands
out due to its high efficiency, low heat input, minimal deformation, low
dilution rate, and high bond strength with the base material [ 6 – 8 ]. It can
form a layer with higher hardness, thereby improving wear resistance
[ 9 – 12 ]. Additionally, LC allows for the adjustment of powder compo-
sition ratios through mechanical mixing and gas atomization methods,
enabling precise control over the microstructure and mechanical prop-
erties (such as wear resistance) of formed specimens. Therefore,
applying a wear-resistant layer on the surface of the 24CrNiMo brake
disc through laser cladding is a cost-effective solution to extend its
service life.
Due to the insufficient high-temperature performance of Cr-Mo-V
and Cr-Ni-Mo-V steels [ 13 , 14 ], nickel-based alloys and cobalt-based
alloys have been considered as alternative materials for coating

As a widely used alloying element, Nb plays a crucial role in the
mechanical properties of alloys, such as wear resistance [ 30 – 32 ]. For
example, Qi et al. studied the effect of different Nb contents on the
strength and toughness of NiCrMo weld metal and found that adding Nb
improved strength but had an adverse effect on toughness [ 33 ]. Some
researchers have also found that Nb addition can prevent the propaga-
tion of microcracks in FeCo alloys [ 34 ]. Additionally, Jiang et al. re-
ported that Nb is a key factor influencing the comprehensive
performance of rebar, and adding Nb can simultaneously enhance the
hardness, yield strength, and tensile strength of rebar [ 35 ]. Jaroslav

there is currently limited research on the impact of alloying elements on
the wear resistance of cobalt-based alloy coatings, especially regarding
the influence of Nb on the microstructure, shear properties, and wear
resistance of Stellite 21 alloy coatings. Although some scholars have
investigated the significant impact of Nb on alloy microstructure and
mechanical properties, most studies focus on aluminum or magnesium
alloys [ 36 – 38 ].
Against this background, the aim of this work is to investigate the
effect of Nb on the microstructure, microhardness distribution, micro-
shear performance, and wear properties of Stellite 21 alloy coatings.
Analyzing the evolution of the microstructure of Stellite 21 alloy coat-
ings with Nb, as well as the wear mechanism of Stellite 21 coatings
affected by Nb, provides data support and theoretical basis for
improving the wear performance of friction surfaces of high-speed train
brake discs.
2. Materials and methods
2.1. Experimental materials and preparation
The powder materials used in this study were provided by H ¨ogan ¨as
AB, Sweden, including Stellite 21 alloy powder and pure Nb powder
provided by Changsha Tijo Metal Material Co., Ltd., China. The purities
are all 99.9 wt%, and the particle sizes range from 45 to 105 μ m. The
Stellite 21 alloy powder is spherical and atomized, whereas the pure Nb
powder is irregular and blocky. The SEM morphology of the powders is
shown in Fig. 1 . Pure Nb powder was mechanically mixed with Stellite
21 at concentrations of 1 %, 3 %, and 5 % (wt%), and the equivalent
chemical compositions are shown in Table 1 . In the following sections,
the coatings with 0 %, 1 %, 3 %, and 5 % Nb added to Stellite 21 are
referred to as S21, S21Nb1, S21Nb3, and S21Nb5, respectively. All
powders were mechanically mixed in a planetary ball mill (YXQM-2 L)
at 200 rpm for 3 h. The LC process was conducted on an IPG YLS-12000
laser system, comprising a 12.0 kW fiber laser, a coaxial laser cladding
head with a focal length of 15 mm (using a spot size of 4.5 mm), a
cooling system, powder feeder, and a robotic arm. Pure argon gas
(99.999 %) was used as the powder carrier and protective gas. The
substrate material was 24CrNiMo steel, with chemical compositions
listed in Table 1 . The LC deposition equipment and process are illus-
trated in Fig. 2 , and the summarized LC process parameters are pre-
sented in Table 2 . To meet the size requirements of the samples, three
layers were deposited orthogonally to each other to minimize internal
stress, forming a coating thickness of approximately 4 – 5 mm. The
orthogonal deposition involved depositing subsequent layers perpen-
dicular to the preceding layer, as shown schematically in Fig. 2 (d).
2.2. Microscopic characterization
Microstructural examination was conducted using a field emission
scanning electron microscope (FE-SEM; FEI, XL30S-FEG) at an acceler-
ating voltage of 20 kV. X-ray diffraction (XRD) was performed using a
Pert-Pro MPD diffractometer to determine the phase composition of the
coatings. Energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS; Oxford, X-MaxN)
was utilized to determine the chemical compositions of the phases.
Electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) was carried out using FE-SEM
(JEOL, JSM-7800F) with a 50 nm scanning step (Oxford, Nod-
lysNano). Prior to XRD and SEM examinations, the microstructure
samples were ground, mirror-polished, and etched with aqua regia
solution.

hardness of alloys [ 46 – 50 ]. It is indicated that the significant increase in
hardness is due to the addition of Nb to S21, which, under LC process

resistance of coatings. Under dry friction conditions, the wear resistance
of materials is often closely related to their hardness; higher hardness
corresponds to stronger wear resistance. Moreover, the wear resistance
is also related to the hardness of the reinforcing phase when present.

the wear specimen, causing larger or more particles to detach. This leads

During the coating wear process, subjected to the vertical downward
load, cracks initially appear on the surface of the specimen when the
hardness and strength of the coating are low. With the prolongation of
wear time, the cracks enlarge, gradually forming furrows that increase in
size, and abrasive particles begin to detach from the coating surface. As
more particles detach from the coating surface, the wear becomes more
severe. When the coating has high hardness and contains strengthening
phases, under the same load, the appearance of similar furrows and
abrasive particles requires a longer time due to the presence of the
higher hardness strengthening phase. Therefore, under the same load
and wear time, the “ Stellite 21 + Nb ” coating with higher hardness and
the presence of Laves phase exhibits better wear resistance.
