1. Introduction 
Refractory high entropy alloys (RHEAs) are widely considered as 
promising high-temperature materials with the potential to overcome 
temperature limitations of Ni-based superalloys [ 1 – 4 ]. Numerous works 
have demonstrated superior specific high-temperature strength of 
RHEAs [ 3 , 5 – 12 ], and some of them can have promising oxidation 
resistance [ 3 , 13 , 14 ]. Most RHEAs have a body-centered cubic (bcc, A2)- 
based structure with the possible presence of secondary phases; the most 
frequent are the Laves and B2 (ordered bcc) phases [ 3 ]. The co-existence 
of the bcc and B2 phases allows to generate superalloy-like structures 
with a high fraction of cuboidal or plate-like precipitates in the matrix 
[ 15 – 18 ]. Such a structure can result in appreciably high strength at 
temperatures up to ~1000 
◦
C [ 16 ]. However, the bcc + B2 RHEAs often 
have poor ductility caused by the B2 ordered matrix (while the pre -
cipitates are disordered bcc) [ 15 , 19 ]. Another drawback of such alloys is 
associated with poor thermal stability of the bcc + B2 structure: an 
exposure at potential usage temperatures results in the precipitation of 
additional phases and rapid microstructure degradation [ 20 , 21 ]. 
Recently, an alternative approach for the development of the bcc +
B2 RHEAs based on ductile B2 compounds, like CoHf, as the matrix 
phase reinforced by hard bcc phase (like binary NbMo) was proposed 
[ 22 , 23 ]. The (HfCo)

structure demonstrated a nice combination of strength and ductility in a 
wide temperature range due to the development of dislocation activity

4. Conclusions 
In this work, the effect of long-term (700 h) annealing at 1000 
◦
C on 
the structure and mechanical properties of the (HfCo)
90
(NbMo)
10 
and 
(HfCo)
75
(NbMo)
25 
alloys were examined, and following conclusions 
were drawn: 
1) Similarly to the as-cast condition, the annealed alloys were 
composed of the (Co, Hf)-rich B2 and (Nb, Mo)-rich bcc phases. The 
phase stability agreed reasonably with the Thermo-Calc predictions. 
The response to annealing was associated with the precipitation of 
nanoscale bcc / B2 particles inside the B2 / bcc phases in the 
(HfCo)
90
(NbMo)
10 
alloy. In addition, the partitioning of Nb and Mo 
led to an unexpected formation of the Nb-rich and Mo-rich bcc 
phases. The B2 precipitates in the Nb-rich bcc phase were coarser 
possibly due to higher lattice misfit. 
2) Annealing resulted in noticeable strengthening of both alloys 
accompanied with some decrease in ductility. For example, the room 
temperature yield strength of the (HfCo)
90
(NbMo)
10 
alloy increased 
from 640 to 1340 MPa, while ductility decreased from 15 to 4%. The 
strength increment was mostly attributed to the Orowan hardening 
of the B2 phase by the bcc precipitates.
