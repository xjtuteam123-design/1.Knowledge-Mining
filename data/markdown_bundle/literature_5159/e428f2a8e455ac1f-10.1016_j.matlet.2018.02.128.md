abstract
In contrast to other structural alloys, yield strength of nickel base superalloys either remains constant or
increases with temperature up to 700–800 /C176C making them more suitable for high temperature structural
applications. However, the increase in yield strength is usually accompanied by a decrease in ductility.
The results of the present investigation describe the substurural evolution in a directionally solidiﬁed
nickel base superalloy CM 247 DS LC during tensile deformation, where ductility is not impaired even
when yield strength is highest (at 750 /C176C). Extensive TEM studies offer plausible dislocation based mech-
anisms for retention of high ductility at 750 /C176C. The highest yield strength is attributed to the presence of
intersecting faults along with partials bounded by anti-phase boundary. However, high ductility is due to
the formation of SISF inside
c0 precipitates. These mechanisms provide necessary impetus for further
alloy development.

1. Introduction
Nickel base superalloys are microstructurally designed for use
in high temperature structural applications such as in the hot sec-
tions of aircraft gas turbine engines [1,2]. The main feature of their
microstructure is the dispersion of precipitates that have ordered
L12 crystal structure and are coherently embedded in a solid solu-
tion strengthened c matrix [2]. Due to presence of these ordered
precipitates, c0, nickel base superalloys exhibit typical tensile beha-
viour known as yield strength anomaly (YSA), which consists of an
increasing or constant value of yield strength (YS) over a range of
temperatures [3–4]. It is usually observed that increase in yield
strength owing to YSA is accompanied by decrease in ductility of
the superalloys, and the reasons for reduced ductility are different
for different alloys [3–6].
However, in the present study it is observed that the alloy CM
247 DS LC exhibits enhanced ductility at 750 /C176C, where the yield
strength of the alloy is highest. Such type of strange behaviour,
where both high yield strength and high ductility occurs together
is termed as strength-ductility paradox. CM 247 DS LC is a direc-
tionally solidiﬁed (DS) nickel base superalloy used for manufactur-
ing of turbine blades and vanes. This alloy has multiphase
microstructure consisting of c0 precipitates embedded in c matrix,
carbides, and c-c0 eutectics [7]. In the present investigation, the
micromechanism of tensile deformation of alloy CM 247 DS LC at
750 /C176C is studied and an attempt has been made to understand
the strength-ductility paradox behaviour based on the sub struc-
tural evolution. This study will be beneﬁcial for future alloy devel-
opment and improving the mechanical behaviour of alloy at
elevated temperatures.

3. Results and discussion
The representative engineering stress–strain ( S-e) curves
obtained from the tensile tests in the temperature range 25–955
/C176C are shown in Fig. 1 a. Signiﬁcant strain hardening is observed
at 750 /C176C. Variation of yield strength ( SY), tensile strength ( SU),
and % total elongation, ef, within the temperature regime studied
is shown in Fig. 1 b. The SY and SU values marginally increase till
650 /C176C, reach a peak at 750 /C176C and then decrease with further
increase in temperature. Like many other nickel base superalloys,
YSA is observed in this alloy. However, one striking feature of this
observation is that the ef, value keeps on increasing with tempera-
ture even in the temperature bracket of anomalous yield strength.

As the microstructure of specimen deformed at 750 /C176C mainly
consists of SSFs and APB bounded partial dislocations, therefore,
it is essential to understand the mechanisms responsible for their
formation.
Generally, in nickel base superalloys the dislocations shear the
precipitates with Burger vectors a/2 h110 i, a/6 h112 i, and a/3
h112 i, produce anti-phase boundaries (APB), complex fault (CF),
and super-lattice intrinsic/extrinsic stacking faults (SISF/SESFs)
respectively, within
c0.
Many dislocation models are available in the open domain to
explain the formation of SSFs inside c0 precipitates. Two such
prominent models, one proposed by Kear et al. [10] and the other
by Condat et al. [11] are described here. In Condat’s model, SISF/
SESFs are formed by the shearing of c0 -precipitate by a/2[1 1 0] dis-
location which creates APB inside c0 and Shockley partials a/6[1 1
2] are subsequently nucleated at c/c0 interfaces. According to Con-
dat et al. following dislocation reaction leads to the formation of
SISF
Kear et al. [10] proposed that, based on the APB energy, the a h1
10 i super dislocation would dissociate into two identical super
partials a/2 h110 i at c/c0 interface which may react in the matrix
channel to form a partial of Burger vector a/2 h112 i. This partial
further dissociates into a/3 h112 i superpartials which shears the
precipitate and a a/6 h112 i Shockley partial that remains pinned
at the interface. According to Kear et al. following dislocation reac-
tion leads to the formation of SISF
From the above discussion it is clear that the shear mechanism
leading to the SSFs formation can be distinguished from the nature
of the Burgers vectors associated with the stacking faults, provided
leading dislocation of a/3 h112 i can be imaged. The two Burgers
vectors are parallel in Kear’s model, whereas they are at 60/C176in Con-
dat’s model. Despite the low energy associated with SISF forma-
tion, the dissociation in accordance to Eq. (2) may not be
favoured as the larger Burger vector of superpartials inevitably
has higher energy and require additional energy to trigger the reac-
tion in Eq. (2). However, it has been reported that, negative misﬁts
are generated in many nickel base superalloys [12] with increase in
operating temperature due to the high thermal expansion coefﬁ-
cient value of
c matrix as compared to c0 precipitate. This misﬁt
strain is considerable at YSA temperature regime and might have
supplied the additional energy required for dislocation dissociation
at c-c0 interfaces. The Shockley partials a/6 h112 i left at interface
reduces the negative misﬁt strain and makes the above dislocation
reaction feasible.
Therefore, from the sequence of dislocation dissociation reac-
tions described in Eq.2 and the ﬁndings in the present investigation
(Figs. 2 and 3 ) it is established that the faults formed inside c0 pre-
cipitates are consistent with those described by Kear et al. The
retention of ductility at 750 /C176C is attributed to the formation of
SISFs, which relieves the strain localization at the c-c0 interface,
hence delayed the fracture. However, presence of intersecting iso-
lated SSFs along with partials bounded by APB is responsible for
strain hardening and highest yield strength at 750 /C176C.
4. Conclusions
In summary, nickel base superalloy CM 247 DS LC exhibits YSA
behaviour and yield strength maxima occur at 750 /C176C. However,
unlike in other superalloys, ductility keeps on increasing with tem-
perature even in the temperature bracket of anomalous yield
strength. Yield strength anomaly is attributed to the formation of
partial dislocations bounded by APB and existence of intersecting
SSFs inside c0 precipitates. However, higher ductility at 750 /C176Ci s
conﬁrmed to be due to the formation of SISFs according to Kear’s
model.
