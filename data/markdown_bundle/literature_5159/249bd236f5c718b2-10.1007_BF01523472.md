Refractory metals and alloys are finding wider applications as structural materials for 
assemblies and units of machines operating at high temperature. As is well known, tempera- 
ture has a marked effect on the mechanical properties of the majority of refractory metals, 
connected with complex physicochemical transformations occurring in the metals with a change 
in the temperature. Considering that the majority of structural elements during operation 
work under complex loading conditions, from a practical viewpoint it is important to have 
available the temperature dependences for the mechanical properties of refractory metals with 
a complex stress state

two of its alloys (carbide-strengthened alloy 4605 [i] and alloy TsM6) under biaxial tension 
conditions in the temperature range 293-1800~ with the aim of establishing failure mecha- 
nisms and grounds for using known strength conditions for describing the limiting state of the 
test materials at high temperature.

Widespread use of aluminum alloys in the structures of modern technology is due to the 
quite extensive study of their strength properties not only witll static, but also with high 
dynamic loads. This is indicated by the considerable number of experimental studies devoted 
to spalling failure of aluminum and its alloys [1-20]. These studies may be provisionally 
subdivided into two categories: i) for determination of critical loading levels leading to 
initiation of spalling damage in a material or to macroscopic material delamination [3, 5, 8, 
10-14, 17, 18, 21]; 2) for material failure with very high shock or explosive loads which of- 
ten markedly exceed critical levels [I, 2, 4, 6, 7, 9, 19, 20]. Results of the first cate- 
gory are more interesting in a scientific sense and may be used in dynamic calculations for 
structures, but the results of the second category may be used in practice for explosive ex- 
periments, and also in designing certain specific production operations such as welding, 
strengthening, and explosive forming. Separate from this it is possible to note studies in 
which data are given for the effect of temperature on spalling failure of aluminum alloys [2, 
15-18, 22]. 
Results of experiments are partly given in [17, 18, 20], where critical loading levels 
are determined corresponding to macroscopic material delamination. Also given there are 
schemes for carrying out tests for evaluating mechanical shock loading conditions. A further 
stage of this study is examination of the loading level corresponding to initiation in test 
materials of spalling microdamage and clarification of the nature of spa]ling failure for ma- 
terials over a wide temperature range (--196-600~ 
We now note certain features connected with obtaining experimental results. Specimens 
of test materials were cut from bar in the as-supplied condition. Specimen thickness for alu- 
minum ADI and alloy DI6 was 20 mm, and for alloy AMg6 it was i0 mm. Mechanical shock loading 
was accomplished by impact of an aluminum plate 4 mm thick, accelerated by explosion of a 
sheet of explosive charge. After loading specimens were cut along the axis, the cut surface 
was polished, etched, and studied under a microscope at a magnification up to I000. This 
made it possible to compare the known impact velocity and test temperature with the degree 
of material spalling damage.
