bulk. There have been cases where the action of the solder 
broke the structure into separate blocks [ 1, 2]. 
It is known [2] that the embrittlement of a solid body in 
contact with liquid melt depends on mechanical, metallurgi- 
cal, and chemical factors. 
In addition, the liquid metal medium can cause a change 
in the fracture toughness and the initial activation energy of 
fracture in solid bodies plus affect the rate of nucleation and 
propagation of cracks. 
The isolation of copper at the crack tip in alloys indicates 
that the low-melting elements present in the alloys in an in- 
considerable amount and having a higher affinity for copper 
than for silver cause its selective deposition on the surfaces 
of the crack just like nickel (the alloy base). This feature 
made researchers think that copper makes an additional con- 
tribution to the formation of a liquid low-melting phase in 
the melt of the copper-silver solder, which fills the crack. 
The aim of the present study consisted of studying the 
dependence of the properties of soldered materials on the 
temperature, the heating rate, the scale factor, the stress con- 
centrators, the grain size, the hold time under the action of 
the Cu- Ag solder melt, the thickness and kind of barrier 
coating, the solid-state copper and silver, and hydrogen. 
In addition, we studied the influence of the molten solder 
on the value of the initial activation energy of fracture, the 
stress parameter, the fracture rate, and the fracture toughness.

CONCLUSIONS 
1. High-temperature steels and alloys fracture in solder- 
ing by a brittle mechanism (this is the most typical for dis- 
persion-hardening alloys). 
2. An elevated sensitivity of dispersion-hardening alloys 
to the action of molten solder is observed in a specific tempe- 
rature range where their plasticity falls abruptly upon heating. 
3. In soldering structures by copper-silver solders, such 
factors as the multiphase structure, the grain size 
d > 0.1 ram, the duration of contact with the melt, the hydro- 
gen content exceeding 3  10 4 wt.%, the low-melting ele- 
ments Pb, S, and Sn, the mechanical strength concentrators 
(sealing flanges), and the growth of the deformation rate en- 
hance the embrittlement of the materials, whereas a barrier 
coating possessing a chemical affinity for the solder weakens 
it. The scale factor virtually does not affect the degree of 
embrittlement. 
4. Upon the interaction between the material and the 
molten melt, the overstress and the rate of fracture become 
enhanced and the activation energy and the fracture tough- 
ness diminish. These changes are more typical for disper- 
sion-hardening alloys than for steels and homogeneous alloys.
