ABSTRACT 
Previous study indicated developing a bimodal (micron/submicron) structured α -Mo matrix could improve 
fracture toughness of the fine-grained Mo-12Si-8.5B-0.57 wt% La
2
O
3 
alloy greatly without sacrificing strength 
dramatically at room temperature. In this work, the effect of bimodal α -Mo structure on the compressive 
behavior of this alloy at 1000 – 1400 
◦
C was investigated. At 1000 
◦
C, because micron α -Mo regions weakened 
grain boundary strengthening effect, the compressive strength and yield strength of bimodal alloy were lower 
than those of fine-grained alloy. But at 1200 
◦
C and 1400 
◦
C, the compressive strength and yield strength of 
bimodal alloy were higher since the micron α -Mo regions exhibited less grain boundary sliding deformation. 
Especially at 1400 
◦
C, the obvious work hardening induced by the large plastic deformation of micron α -Mo 
regions was an extra contribution to the strength. Additionally, the intragranular and intergranular nano-scale 
La
2
O
3 
particles could strengthen alloy by inhibiting dislocation slip and pinning grain boundaries. Moreover, 
this bimodal alloy exhibited good dynamic softening resistance at 1200 – 1400 
◦
C because the micron α -Mo re -
gions preferentially occurred plastic deformation and dynamic recrystallization, and then the occurrence of 
dynamic recovery and recrystallization of submicron α -Mo, Mo
3
Si and Mo
5
SiB

regions was delayed. With 
increasing temperature, the occurrence of plastic deformation and dynamic recovery of submicron Mo
3
Si and 
Mo

occur compared to the submicron α -Mo regions in the bimodal alloy. 
1. Introduction 
Recent developments in the fields of power generation and aerospace 
industry have led to increased demand for gas turbine systems for higher 
temperature applications in order to improve energy efficiency [ 1 , 2 ]. 
Ni-base superalloys as the currently used material of turbine blades can 
withstand temperatures up to about 1150 
◦
C, which has been close to 
90% of their melting temperature [ 3 ]. The further development of Ni- 
base superalloys has been limited by their melting point. Thus, synthe -
sizing the new-type ultra-high temperature structural materials as al -
ternatives to Ni-based superalloys has been an attracting problem. Mo- 
Si-B alloys comprised of α -Mo, Mo
3
Si (A15) and Mo
5
SiB
2 
(T2) are 
promising substitutes due to their high melting points (above 2000 
◦
C), 
superior high-temperature mechanical properties and outstanding 
oxidation resistance [ 4 , 5 ]. Especially, when the Mo-Si-B alloy formed a 
fine-grained (FG) microstructure, the oxidation resistance of alloy was 
further improved by accelerating the growth of a protective borosilicate 
(SiO
2
-B
2
O
3
) scale [ 6 , 7 ]. However, poor room-temperature ductility and 
fracture toughness (~9 MPa ⋅ m
1/2
) of FG Mo-Si-B alloy limited its 
practical applications primarily owing to the limited dislocation storage 
capability in the fine grains [ 8 , 9 ]. 
Developing a bimodal microstructure (i.e. micron-sized coarse grains 
distributed in a nanocrystalline (NC) or ultrafine-grained (UFG) matrix) 
could improve ductility of NC or UFG metal materials without much loss 
of their strengths [ 10 ]. The coarse-grained (CG) grains contributed to 
high ductility whereas the NC or UFG grains provided high strength 
[ 11 , 12 ]. In our previous study, a series of FG Mo-12Si-8.5B-0.57 wt% 
La
2
O
3 
alloys with bimodal FG (submicron)/CG (micron) α -Mo grains 
were fabricated by multi-step ball milling and hot pressing sintering 
[ 13 ]. The fracture toughness of alloys was improved significantly and 
attained to 11.3 – 12.5 MPa ⋅ m
1/2
, meanwhile the compression strength 
was not decreased dramatically. 
Possessing superior high-temperature mechanical properties is also 
crucial to the refractory Mo-Si-B alloys as structural materials. Some

studies about the high-temperature creep properties and deformation 
behaviors of Mo-Si-B alloys have been reported. Jain et al. [ 14 ] found 
that small levels of Si and B in solid solution in Mo promoted the increase 
of compressive strength at 1000 – 1200 
◦
C owing to the solid solution 
strengthening effect of Si and B. In addition, Alur et al. [ 15 ] confirmed 
that a two-phase Mo-6.1Si-7.9B alloy ( α -Mo + 38 vol% of T2) exhibited 
superior compressive strength relative to their TZM alloys counterpart at 
1000 – 1400 
◦
C. The α -Mo matrix was soft and readily deformable at high 
temperatures. The T2 was beneficial to providing physical obstruction to 
slip, increasing the flow stress of alloys. Besides, with increasing 
deformation temperature from 1000 
◦
C to 1400 
◦
C, the strength and 
work-hardening rate of α -Mo matrix decreased and the T2 particles 
transformed elastic to plastic gradually at lower strain rate. Moreover, 
recrystallization could occur in the α -Mo matrix while the T2 either 
cracked or deformed plastically depending on the deformation temper -
ature and strain rate. 
Addition of the active elements or the second phase could improve 
the high-temperature creep properties of Mo-Si-B alloys. Hochmuth 
et al. [ 16 ] reported that the addition of 1 at.% Zr could decrease the 
creep rate of Mo-9Si-8B alloy by one order of magnitude. The addition of 
Zr prevented the formation of SiO
2 
on the grain boundaries, thereby 
enhancing grain boundary strength. Moreover, the nanometer second 
phase Mo
2
Zr/ZrO
2 
particles strengthened the α -Mo matrix by pinning 
dislocations. Schneibel et al. [ 17 ] reported the addition of Nb not only 
improved the creep strength of Mo-12Si-8.5B alloy at 1200 – 1400 
◦
C but 
also increased the dependence of creep strength on the temperature 
obviously. But, alloying with W didn't attain the good strengthening 
effect as alloying with Nb. 
In metal matrix materials, microstructural size usually affects the 
high-temperature deformation behavior. The changes of size and num -
ber of grains and grain boundaries influence atom diffusion and dislo -
cation slipping. Schneibel et al. [ 17 ] found that, compared to the CG 
microstructural alloy, the FG Mo-16.8Si-8.4B alloy exhibited higher 
strength at 1200 
◦
C, but presented lower strength at 1400 
◦
C. At low 
temperature, the Hall-Petch hardening might be a strengthening factor 
for the FG alloy, but at high temperature the effect of the interfaces 
sliding became important, thereby a high density of high diffusivity 
interfaces caused the decrease of strength of FG alloy. Besides, the 
plasticity of α -Mo was restrained by surrounding Mo
3
Si/T2 particles, 
thus microstructures containing isolated α -Mo particles were stronger 
than microstructures with a continuous α -Mo matrix [ 17 ]. 
The high-temperature deformation behavior of Mo-Si-B alloys is the 
result of combined action of three phases ( α -Mo, Mo
3
Si and T2). Because 
the three phases possessed different crystal structures and mechanical 
properties at high temperatures, the microstructural evolution in the 
deformation and dynamic recrystallization process of alloy is very 
complex. Although there have been some studies about the deformation 
and dynamic recrystallization behaviors of Mo-Si-B alloy [ 18 – 21 ], 
further investigations on the evolutions of grain structure, substructure, 
and grain boundaries of three phases should be carried out. What's more, 
the effect of the whole microstructure size on the creep behavior of Mo- 
Si-B alloys has been investigated, but the effect of the grain size of in -
dividual phase on the creep behavior of alloys hasn't been explored. 
Especially, the α -Mo, controlling mainly deformation behavior of alloy, 
the effect of its grain size on the deformation behavior of alloy should be 
studied systematacially. In addition, in the alloy microstructure with a 
bimodal structured α -Mo, the relationship of deformation behaviors 
between the CG α -Mo regions and the FG α -Mo regions, between the CG 
α -Mo regions and the FG intermetallics regions, and between the FG 
α -Mo regions and the FG intermetallics regions should be analyzed 
deeply, which is instrumental in the understanding of deformed and 
dynamic recrystallized mechanisms of Mo-Si-B alloys. 
In this study, isothermal compression creep tests of an FG (unimodal) 
and a bimodal Mo-12Si-8.5B-0.57 wt% La
2
O
3 
alloys were performed 
under the deformation temperature range of 1000 – 1400 
◦
C. Micro -
structure of the samples was characterized before and after the 
compression tests by electron back scatter diffraction (EBSD) and 
transmission electron microscopy (TEM). The effect of bimodal α -Mo 
structure on deformation behavior of alloys was investigated at different 
temperatures. The interaction between La
2
O
3 
and dislocation or grain 
boundary was analyzed. The deformed and dynamic recrystallized 
mechanisms of alloys were discussed.

3.2. Compressive deformation behaviors 
Fig. 3 shows the true stress ( δ
t
)-strain ( ε
t
) curves of alloys in the 
compression tests at 1000 
◦
C, 1200 
◦
C and 1400 
◦
C. The flow stress of FG 
and bimodal alloys first increases with the increasement of strain and 
reaches the peak stress, and then gradually decreases to either fracture 
or a relatively steady state at 1000 – 1400 
◦
C. The morphological features 
of the flow stress-strain curves are resulted from the competitive rela -
tionship between work hardening, dynamic recovery and dynamic 
recrystallization. At the initial deformation stage, because of the dislo -
cation generation and multiplication, the work hardening effect is 
obvious and the flow stress rapidly increases [ 22 , 23 ]. Once the 
increased dislocation density exceeds a critical value, the dynamic re -
covery and dynamic recrystallization occurs. The nucleation and growth 
of dynamic recrystallized grains can induce the annihilation of dislo -
cation, decreasing the work hardening rate. Hence, with the increase of 
strain, the dynamic recovery and dynamic recrystallization gradually 
strengthens, reducing the increasing rate of stress, and finally the stress 
gradually reaches the peak value. After reaching the peak stress, the 
obvious dynamic softening is induced by dynamic recovery and 
recrystallization [ 24 , 25 ]. When the work hardening and dynamic soft -
ening attains a dynamic balance, a relatively steady-state flow behavior 
will be obtained [ 26 , 27 ]. 
The flow behaviors of the FG and bimodal alloys are strongly sen -
sitive to the deformation temperature. With the deformation tempera -
ture increases, the peak stress of alloys decreases. This is because that 
the higher deformation temperature results in a higher softening rate. At 
1000 
◦
C ( Fig. 3 (a)), the FG and bimodal alloys reach the peak stress 
when the strain is 14 – 15.4%, but they occur brittle fracture when the 
strain is about 20%. At 1200 
◦
C ( Fig. 3 (b)), the FG and bimodal alloys 
reach the peak stress when the strain is 8.2 – 8.3%, and then the flow 
stress decreases gradually. However, the dynamic softening resistance of 
bimodal alloy is stronger, the decreasing rate of stress is lower. At 
1400 
◦
C ( Fig. 3 (c)), the FG and bimodal alloys don't reach the peak stress 
rapidly after passing the yield point, and they reach the peak stress when 
the strains are about 15% and 10%, respectively. Compared to 1200 
◦
C, 
the peak stress of two alloys remains for a longer strain before obvious 
decreasing, showing good dynamic softening resistance at 1400 
◦
C. 
Besides, at 1200 
◦
C and 1400 
◦
C, the bimodal alloy exhibits the stronger 
work-hardening compared with the FG alloy, and these two alloys don't 
fracture when the strains reach to 30%. 
Figs. 4 (a) and 4 (b) show the compressive strength and yield strength 
of alloys at different temperatures. At 1000

4. Discussion 
The hot deformation behavior of Mo-Si-B alloys at 1000-1400 
◦
C is a 
thermal activation process, which consists of the deformation of grain 
itself and the sliding of grain boundary [ 29 ]. At 1000 
◦
C, the compres -
sive strength and yield strength of FG alloy are higher than those of 
bimodal alloy. Compared to deformation at 1200 
◦
C and 1400 
◦
C, the 
thermal activation effect of alloys is weaker at 1000 
◦
C, grain boundary 
sliding is relatively difficult and the grain boundaries can hinder dislo -
cation slip effectively. Therefore, for the FG alloy containing more grain 
boundaries, when the dislocations move to the grain boundaries during 
deformation, the grain boundaries will impede dislocation movement 
and strengthen alloy. However, because of the presence of CG α -Mo 
regions (~16.8 vol%) in the bimodal alloy, the bimodal alloy contains 
less grain boundaries and the effect of grain boundaries strengthening is 
weaker compared to the FG alloy. In addition, when the compressive 
strains of FG and bimodal alloys are only 20%, these two alloys occur 
brittle fracture at 1000 
◦
C. Although the grain boundary strengthening 
effect is obvious, the dislocations pile-up at the grain boundaries will 
cause stress concentration at the interface, resulting in the formation 
and propagation of cracks at the grain boundaries, and then the brittle 
fracture of alloys will occur. In the bimodal alloy, because the grain 
boundary sliding of the CG α -Mo regions is less, the CG α -Mo grains tend 
to occur deformation and induce the movement of dislocations within 
the grains. It testifies to some extent that the deformation of bimodal 
alloy starts from the CG α -Mo regions, in other words, the CG α -Mo re -
gions store deformation energy first and are easier to induce dynamic 
recovery compared to the FG α -Mo, Mo
3
Si and T2 regions. The FG α -Mo 
regions are easier to occur deformation compared to the FG Mo
3
Si and 
T2 regions. But, the CG/FG α -Mo, Mo
3
Si and T2 regions can't occur 
obvious dynamic recrystallization owing to the low fracture strain. 
At 1200 – 1400 
◦
C, the compressive strength and yield strength of 
bimodal alloy are higher than those of FG alloy owing to the presence of 
CG α -Mo regions. Compared to 1000 
◦
C, the thermal activation effect 
improves obviously, and the glutinousness at grain boundaries increases 
at 1200 – 1400 
◦
C [ 30 ]. Under the action of shear stress component along
