abstract
Mechanical and fatigue properties of ternary CoTi based single crystals containing 2% and 5% Al was
investigated. It was shown that in these two alloys the addition of Al improve the strength of CoTi at
all tested temperatures, but by increasing Al content the peak temperature shifted to lower temperature
and the effect of anomalous strengthening was weakened. The number of cycles to failure in fatigue test
for CoTi (2 at.% Al) single crystals showed a minimum, at high temperatures. This phenomenon seems to
be due to the activation of new slip systems. Also by increasing Al, the fatigue limit at constant temper-
ature can be changed. It was shown that Al addition is not effective in improving fatigue life at low tem-
peratures but it can improve it at high temperatures.

1. Introduction
Intermetallic compounds are of interest as candidates for high
temperature structural materials. In these compounds different
atomic species occupy different atomic sites forming the ordered
structures and the strong bonds between different atoms produce
attractive properties such as high melting point, anomalous
strengthening, superior shear modulus and high stability against
chemical environment. The anomalous strengthening was ob-
served in FeCo [1], CuZn and FeAl [2] deformed by h111 i slip. In
addition CoTi [3,4], CoHf [3,5,6] and CoZr [5–7] which form
strongly ordered B 2 structure and deform by h001 i slip, showed
the anomalous strengthening at high temperatures. It has been re-
ported that CoTi shows considerable ductility even at room tem-
perature. At the same time the alloy demonstrated better than
expected fatigue strength [8]. In single crystalline form CoTi
showed strong resistance against fatigue failure. The effect of com-
position on mechanical properties such as anomalous strengthen-
ing, and fatigue life also have been studied; for example yield
stress of single crystals of the off-stoichiometric CoTi alloy
(49.6 at.% Ti) showed higher values than those of the stoichiome-
tric CoTi alloys [4]. Among all phases in the Co–Ti–Al systems, B
2
and L21 have fundamentally the same bcc unit cell, but have differ-
ent atomic conﬁgurations. The L2 1-type Co2TiAl intermetallic com-
pound as well as the B 2-type CoAl intermetallic compound is
attractive for high temperature structural applications because of
high-melting point, low density and excellent corrosion and oxida-
tion resistance [9].
Since a large amount of Al addition to CoTi produces Co 2AlTi
with the L2 1 structure, Al addition may be effective in strengthen-
ing and induce strong ordering of B 2 structure in CoTi. Co 2TiAl and
Ni2AlTi with L2 1 structure are known to improve creep resistance
of NiAl and CoTi based compounds at high temperatures [10].
It has been reported that the L2 1-type Co2TiAl phase forms via a
continuous ordering from the B 2 phase to the L2 1 phase, or is in
equilibrium with two B 2-type CoTi and CoAl phases, respectively,
depending on alloy composition and temperature [11,12].
For the polycrystalline alloys composed of the L2 1 microstruc-
ture with high Al concentrations (12–24 at.% Al), the solid solution
strengthening has been reported as a dominant parameter and the
antiphase domain strengthening as an additional parameter affect-
ing the high-temperature strength [9].
In this paper, effects of Al addition on mechanical properties of
CoTi alloys was examined focussing on temperature dependence of
yield stress and fatigue resistance using single crystals.

3. Results
3.1. Plastic deformation behaviour in compression
3.1.1. Stress–strain curves at various temperatures
Stress–strain curves of ternary CoTi containing 5 and 2 at.% Al
deformed at various temperatures are shown in Figs. 1 and 2 ,
respectively.
The stress drop and frequency of serration depend strongly on
deformation temperature, but at high temperatures, the serration
disappears and smooth stress–strain curves are observed. The ser-
ration is closely related to inhomogeneous deformation and
discontinuous motion of dislocations. At the intermediate temper-
ature, solute atoms segregate near the dislocation core and form a
dragging atmosphere around the moving dislocations. As a result
of pinning and unpinning effect of dislocations from the atmo-
sphere, serration appears. When the deformation temperature is
high enough for diffusion of solute atoms, the serration disappears.
Considering the high temperature activation of
h001 i-type dislocations and the h011 i-type dislocations in both
the B
2-type CoTi(Al) [13] and L2 1-type Co 2TiAl single crystals
[14], the observed solid solution strengthening has been attributed
to the interaction of such type of disordering atoms with the
h001 i-type dislocation or the h011 i-type dislocation [9].
Addition of Al in CoTi increases the dragging effect for the mo-
tion of dislocations and induces inhomogeneous deformation. In
each temperature plastic strain showed considerable decrease with
increasing Al content.
3.1.2. Temperature dependence of yield stress
Addition of Al is very effective in improving strength of CoTi at
all tested temperatures as shown in Fig. 3 . The yield stress in-
creases about 100 MPa for every percent of Al addition. Anomalous
strengthening occurs in ternary CoTi–Al single crystals although
the anomalous strengthening rate is moderated with increasing
Al content. Al addition shifts the peak temperature of anomalous
strengthening from 973 K to 873 K. It has been reported that for
the alloys composed of the L2
1 microstructure, the solid solution
strengthening is a dominant parameter affecting the high-
temperature strength, and the antiphase domain strengthening is
an additional parameter affecting the high-temperature strength
[9].
Even above the peak temperature, ternary CoTi–Al compounds
maintains high strength and they are favourable for high tempera-
ture structural materials.
3.1.3. Slip marking and operating slip systems in fatigue
In CoTi(5 at.% Al) single crystals fatigued at 773 K, no signiﬁcant
slip traces were observed even near the fractured position, at low
applied stress. Only at high applied stress ( Dr = 400 MPa), ﬁne slip
traces on (1 1 0) planes were observed ( Fig. 4 ). Slip traces in Co-
Ti(2 at.% Al) were fairly uniformly distributed and straight along
(1 1 0) plane. They became ﬁne and secondary slip was activated
at high temperature ( Fig. 5 ).

Although addition of Al strengthens CoTi in compression, an in-

of fatigue property of ternary CoTi–Al crystals.
3.4. Fracture behaviour
Fig. 9 shows fractographs of CoTi-2 at.% Al single crystals fati-
gued at Dr = 270 MPa and at various temperatures. At 573 K stria-
tions are observed to be aligned in a straight line and microcracks
propagate along a direction perpendicular to the striation direc-
tion. The fatigue life was less than 10,000 cycles. With increasing
temperature, cracks were propagated and fatigue life was slightly
shortened. At 773 K, where a minimum fatigue life was obtained,
the same inclined steps were observed but the straight lines disap-
peared. At higher temperatures, river like structure was formed.
The width of crack propagated regions increased with temperature
and fatigue life was slightly improved.
In this alloy the number of cycles to fracture at 773 K decreased
with increasing applied stress. At Dr = 250 MPa where the fatigue
life was long, there was no preferential path for fatigue crack
growth and the crack path showed zigzag motion as shown in
Fig. 10.A t Dr = 290 MPa, privilege of one type of crack propagation
direction occurred and the fatigue life was shortened.

4. Discussion
4.1. Effect of Al addition on cyclic hardening and fatigue life
In general, an increase in strength of alloys is known to induce
improvement of fatigue property. However, although addition of Al
in CoTi strongly increased the yield stress at all tested temperature,
fatigue strength of ternary CoTi-2 at.% Al and CoTi-5 at.% Al single
crystals was not necessarily improved in comparison with binary
CoTi single crystals. Binary CoTi and ternary CoTi–Al compounds
are dominantly deformed by {1 1 0} h001 i slip ( Figs. 12 [8] , 4
and 5 ). This is shown in Figs. 12 [8] , 4 and 5 for binary, (5 at.%
Al) and (2 at.% Al) CoTi compounds respectively. In binary CoTi,
h001 i dislocations easily cross slip on the other orthogonal
{1 1 0} planes resulting in {h k 0} non-crystallographic plane-slip,
at high temperatures and high applied stress. Frequent cross slip
of h001 i screw dislocations produced numerous dislocation di-
poles and debris which induced strong cyclic hardening [8].O n
the other hand, ternary CoTi–Al compounds show planar {1 1 0}
slip even at high temperatures. Since h001 i screw dislocations
are required to perform the to-and-fro motion on the same
{1 1 0} planes during cyclic hardening, dislocation reaction and
the formation of highly tangled dislocations hardly occur. In fact,
cyclic hardening was moderated by addition of Al. However, Al
addition was not effective in improving fatigue life of ternary
CoTi–Al compounds. Fatigue life is known to be closely related to
the crack initiation and propagation under cyclic loading. Although
a microcrack initiates in the initial stage of fatigue life in conven-
tional alloys, initiation of the microcrack does not directly connect
to failure but the crack propagation is believed to control the fati-
gue life. Therefore, the propagation rate of the crack should be sup-
pressed by alloying and microstructural design for improvement of
fatigue life in the alloys. On the other hand, once a microcrack ini-
tiates in intermetallic compounds under cyclic loading, it easily
develops into macrocrack in a short period resulting in fracture
of specimen. In ternary CoTi–Al compounds, cyclic hardening was
not accelerated by Al addition but the slip traces were inhomoge-
neously distributed. Coarse slip bands may act as a nucleation site
of microcrack. Addition of Al induces an increase in yield stress of
CoTi but reduces the ductility. At low applied stress, long fatigue
life was obtained in CoTi-2 at.% Al single crystals because of high
yield stress, while fatigue life was shortened at high applied stress.
A microcrack may initiate in an early stage of cyclic loading in
CoTi-2 at.% Al under high applied stress. Since the microcrack

4.2. Improvement in strength, ductility and fatigue life
Various methods were challenged for improvement of strength
and ductility in intermetallic compounds. Grain reﬁning, micro-
structural control such as lamellae and duplex phase [15], suppres-
sion of impurity atoms such as oxygen, sulphur and carbon [16]
and addition of small amount of interstitial atoms such as B in
Ni3Al are effective [17]. Another typical example to make a mul-
ti-phase is in the case of TiAl alloys in which a ﬁne lamellar micro-
structure consisting of the L1 0 TiAl and the D0 19 Ti3Al provides a
few to several room temperature tensile ductility [18]. Similar
attempts have been found in L1 2 Ni3Al by introducing the B 2 NiAl
[19] and in the B 2 (Co,Ni)Al by introducing the L1 2 (Co,Ni)3Al
[20]. In these efforts it has been shown that the strength is im-
proved but a dramatic improvement in room temperature ductility
is not accompanied which by itself is not enough to consider this
class of materials for practical structural applications.
Development of fabrication method to receive low lattice de-
fects and favourable microstructure was performed such as direc-
tional solidiﬁcation, mechanical alloying, hot isostatic pressing
(HIP) method and special casting [21–23]. Controlling the grain
boundary character to obtain high frequencies of low angle and
coincidence boundaries with low R values has been reported
[24] to improve grain boundary embrittlement in Ni 3Al
polycrystals.
In general, B 2 compounds with high antiphase boundary (APB)
energy hardly deform at low temperatures and are extremely brit-
tle at room temperature. The poor ductility is considered to come
from the intrinsic brittleness due to the difﬁculty of slip deforma-
tion in the B 2 ordered structure [25]. It has been reported [4,13]
that for CoTi intermetallic compound, the poor ductility at room
temperature is possibly due to the facts that the active slip system
at low temperatures is {1 1 0} h001 i and therefore the number of
slip systems guaranteeing the von Mises’ criterion is not satisﬁed.
In addition, the stress driving dislocations is very high and the mul-
tiplication of h001 i dislocations may be difﬁcult. However, if
introducing mobile dislocations into the material, there is a possi-
bility that ductility at low temperatures can be improved [26,27].
Most interest to develop NiAl and CoAl has been paid to im-
prove ductility and fracture toughness at low temperatures. How-
ever present CoTi single crystals can easily deform and good
ductility is obtained even at room temperature.
Since CoTi shows anomalous strengthening with a maximum
peak around 973 K it is favoured for high temperature structural
materials. However, fatigue resistance does not show similar tem-
perature dependence of yield stress. The fatigue life decreases

5. Conclusions
Effect of Al addition to CoTi single crystals was explained focus-
sing on anomalous strengthening, cyclic deformation and fatigue
life, and the following conclusions were reached:
(1) Addition of Al is effective in improving strength of CoTi at all
tested temperatures. Anomalous strengthening occurs in
binary CoTi and ternary CoTi–Al compounds. Al addition
shifts the anomalous strengthening peak temperature from
973 K to 873 K.
(2) Al addition is not effective in improving fatigue life at low
temperatures. In ternary CoTi–Al compounds, fatigue life is
controlled by initiation of microcrack because of their brit-
tleness. The brittle fracture is more accelerated in CoTi-
5 at.% Al single crystals.
(3) Fatigue life of CoTi rapidly decreases at high temperatures,
while Al addition improves fatigue life at high temperatures.
