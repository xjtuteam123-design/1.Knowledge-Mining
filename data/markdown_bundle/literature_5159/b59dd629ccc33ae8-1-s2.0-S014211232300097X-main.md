ABSTRACT 
The purpose of this paper is to investigate the effect of different diffusion-resistance bond coats on the fatigue 
performance of second-generation single crystal superalloys. The β -(Ni,Pt)Al coating with Re-base diffusion 
barrier decreased the fatigue performance of the substrate, while the γ
′
coating improved the fatigue perfor -
mance. Oxidation is the most important factor leading to fatigue failure of the bond coat at high temperature. 
Stress concentration due to the diffusion barrier and TCP accelerated the β -(Ni,Pt)Al coating failure. Solution of 
refractory elements is of significant importance for the γ
′
coating to resist plastic deformation and prevent fatigue 
crack propagation. 
1. Introduction 
Ni-based single crystal (SX) superalloys, due to their excellent ther -
mal stability and mechanical properties, have been extensively applied 
to manufacture rotating components in advanced aero- and land- based 
gas turbine engines [1,2] . In order to meet the requirements of 
increasing power efficiency and reducing exhaust emissions, turbine 
blades made of SX superalloys must operate at higher inlet temperature. 
Protective coatings, used either alone or as a bond coat for thermal 
barrier coatings (TBCs), are required to avoid high temperature oxida -
tion damage to the superalloy [3] . Upon exposure at operational tem -
peratures, a thermally grown oxide (TGO), predominately exclusive and 
intact α -Al
2
O
3 
scale, develops on protective coatings. Pt-modified alu -
minide coatings have been shown to possess much superior high tem -
perature oxidation resistance compared to the unmodified ones [4] . The 
presence of Pt in the coating can promote the selective oxidation and 
adhesion of α -Al
2
O
3 
scale [5] , improve the stability of alumina and 
decrease the oxidation rate [6] . 
In contrast to the good oxidation resistance, at high temperatures, 
metallic bond coats usually cause interdiffusion of elements due to sig -
nificant concentration differences between the coating and the super -
alloy (e.g. Al and Ni), which can lead to the formation of harmful 
topologically-close-packed (TCP) phases and rapid growth of the 
secondary reaction zone (SRZ) [7] . The inward diffusion accelerates the 
depletion of Al in the bond coat and the formation of SRZ degrades 
mechanical properties of the superalloy. In order to resolve the inter -
diffusion problem, bond coat with a diffusion barrier (DB) are currently 
being explored [8 – 10] . The Re-base diffusion barrier can effectively 
inhibit the elemental interdiffusion during the isothermal oxidation at 
high temperature. The thickness of SRZ and the quantity of TCP 
precipitated phases in the superalloy were significantly decreased [8] . In 
addition, recent studies have found that bond coats having a γ - γ
′
structure alleviate interdiffusion with the superalloy [11 – 14] . The 
former shows a three-layer structure consisting of an outer layer, a 
diffusion barrier and the interdiffusion zone (IDZ), while the latter ex -
hibits a relatively simple structure. 
Despite their excellent diffusion and oxidation resistance, bond coats 
can affect the mechanical properties of the superalloy substrate [15 – 18] . 
Micro-voids formed due to the interdiffusion of elements, and the coa -
lescence of micro-voids was the main reason for the formation of the 
fatigue crack [15] . The Pt-Al bond coat had a positive effect on the fa -
tigue behavior at 800 
◦
C. The fine grains in the IDZ effectively disperse 
the stress concentration of the crack tip under low stress. Under high 
stress, Al diffusion into the substrate and the rafting γ
′
formed near the 
bond coat, thus inhibiting crack propagation [16] . The generation of 
brittle TCP particles may be the main cause of lifetime degradation due

For oxidation-fatigue conditions, both internal oxide intrusion into 
the material [32] and local plastic strain near the crack tip [33] promote 
fatigue crack growth. However, when the crack reaches the interior of 
the single crystal superalloy, local plastic strain is not observed on both 
sides and tip of the crack, as indicated by KAM maps ( Fig. 10 d). 
Oxidation becomes the main dynamic of fatigue crack growth [34] . The 
effect of oxidation is twofold, and whether it affects fatigue life depends 
on temperature, stress state, fatigue cycle and the inherent oxidation 
resistance of the material [35] . 
3.6. Evolution of microstructure and structure of β bond coat 
Fig. 11 shows the cross-sectional microstructure evolution of the β 
coat during high temperature fatigue test at 325 MPa. Oxidation is still 
the main feature of this process. The brittle aluminum oxide was 
generated on the surface and grain boundaries of the bond coat, which 
could easily crack under cyclic stress, as displayed in Fig. 11 b and c. 
Before the fatigue experiments, the numerous Re-rich particles were 
dispersed in the upper region of the DB layer. As the high temperature 
fatigue experiments proceeded, the tiny rhenium-rich particles in the 
upper layers began to coalesce together. The phase boundary free energy 
tends to decrease so that the small Re rich grains dissolve and the large 
Re rich grains grow, which is a common phenomenon at high temper -
ature [8] . As a result, the thickness of the diffusion barrier was reduced 
from 12.1 µ m to 7.0 µ m , as shown in Fig. 11 a-c. Due to the high melting 
point of the σ -phase [36] and its extremely low solubility in the β -NiAl 
and γ
′
-Ni
3
Al phases, the diffusion barrier has high stability at high 
temperatures. Thus, the DB layer exhibited continuity and denseness at 
the substrate/coating interface. The Re-base diffusion barrier could 
inhibit interdiffusion of Al in the coating with Ni in the substrate, which 
helps to maintain the stability of the coating microstructure and avoid 
degradation of the mechanical properties of the superalloy [8] . How -
ever, the Re-base diffusion barrier has a deleterious effect on mechanical 
properties by causing intergranular embrittlement. A microcrack is 
observed in the early stage of the test shown in Fig. 11 b. In addition, the 
thickness of the interdiffusion zone and needle-like TCP phases in the 
secondary reaction zone (SRZ) increases, as shown in Fig. 11 d-f. The 
formation of the interdiffusion zone is detrimental to the the mechanical 
properties of superalloys [37] . The needle-like TCP phase in the sec -
ondary reaction zone decreases the ductility and creep resistance of the 
single crystal superalloy due to initiation of microcracks at the tip of 
brittle intermetallic precipitates [38] . In the present work, exactly the 
same experimental results were obtained, i.e., microcracks appeared in 
the interdiffusion zone ( Fig. 11 c). 
For the β coating sample after fatigue test at 350 MPa, representative 
cross-sectional EBSD maps are shown in Fig. 12 . Grains in the diffusion 
barrier contained high misorientation, see Fig. 12 b. The highly local 
deformation can develop to small fatigue cracks. Higher values of 
misorientation in the outer layer and the interdiffusion zone are asso -
ciated with grain boundaries. The darker areas of the bond contrast map 
represent defects, which are unidentified aluminum oxides. This means 
that oxidation occurs preferentially at grain boundaries of the coating.

of the two coating samples is not the same. In order to clarify the fatigue 
crack initiation mechanism of both Pt-modified aluminum coated sam -
ples, the following discussion will be made from the perspective of the 
microstructure and coating thickness.

Second, σ -phase as the main composition of the diffusion barrier is

β -phase have different Burgers vectors, and both the high-angle and low-

is difficult for dislocations to cross the phase boundary. TCP phases act 
as obstacles against dislocation movement in a slip system. Dislocations 
pile up at the TCP phase boundary, as shown in the inset of Fig. 13 f. The 
dislocation pile-up causes the strong stress concentrations at the 
σ -phase. The σ -phase relieves stress concentration through stacking 
fault. However, when the deformation exceeds the capacity of the 
σ -phase, the cracking will happen and act as the fatigue crack initiation 
of the bond coat, as shown in Fig. 13 f.

5. Conclusions 
In this study, different bond coats were deposited on single crystal 
superalloys. The fatigue behaviors of the β coating sample and the γ
′
coating sample were investigated at 900 
◦
C. The fatigue crack initiation 
and propagation mechanism in the bond coat have also been discussed. 
Based on the experimental results, the following conclusions can be 
drawn: 
(1) The β -(Ni,Pt)Al coating with Re-base diffusion barrier decreased 
the fatigue performance of the substrate, while the γ
′
coating 
improved the fatigue performance. The fatigue strength at 10
7 
cycles of bare single crystal superalloys, the β coating sample and 
the γ
′
coating sample is 337.5 MPa, 325 MPa, and 350 MPa, 
respectively. 
(2) Casting pores are responsible for fatigue crack initiation for bare 
superalloys. Bond coats changed the fatigue crack initiation 
mechanism of single crystal superalloys. Oxidation of the bond 
coats became the main factor of failure under high temperature 
cyclic loading condition. 
(3) The diffusion barrier and the interdiffusion zone in β coating 
sample had a negative effect on fatigue performance due to the 
brittle precipitation phase. With the selective oxidation of 
aluminum, solution of refractory elements is of significant 
importance for the γ
′
coating to resist plastic deformation and 
prevent fatigue crack propagation.
