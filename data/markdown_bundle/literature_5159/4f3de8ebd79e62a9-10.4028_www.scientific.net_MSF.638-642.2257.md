Role of Rhenium in Single Crystal Ni-based Superalloys 
Tao Jin a, Wenzhen Wang b, Xiaofeng Sun c, Zhuangqi Hu d 
(Institute of Metal Research, Chinese Academy of Sciences, Shenyang 110016, China) 
atjin@imr.ac.cn, bw_zhwang@hotmail.com, cxfsun@imr.ac.cn, dzqhu@imr.ac.cn 
Keyword: Re, creep rupture property, dislocation, single crystal superalloy 
Abstract. The effect of rhenium on the microstructure and mechanical properties of single crystal 
superalloys with a nominal composition of Ni-3Cr-12Co-1Mo-6W-6Al-8Ta-0.1Hf-(0, 2, 4) Re has 
been studied. With the rise of rhenium content, the size of as-cast γ′ particles becomes smaller. 
Rhenium addition elevates the incipient melting temperature and slows down the solid solution 
process. Even after 2000h prolonged aging both at 950°C and 1050°C, no topologically 
close-packed phase precipitation is found in the three alloys with Re content up to 4%. Re retards 
the γ′ coarsening and is beneficial to improving the stress rupture life. The deformation mechanisms 
together with the dislocation configuration have been studied and discussed. 
Introduction 
Single crystal Ni-based superalloy is widely used as the blades and vanes in advanced 
aeroengines and gas turbines. Recent development tendency for aeroengines is to further enhance 
the efficiency by increasing the turbine inlet temperature, which demand the superalloys have 
superior elevated temperature properties, especially creep resistance [1,2]. The rhenium (Re) 
alloying in single crystal superalloys is a powerful way to heighten strength [3-9]. But the 
systematical researches about the influences of Re with other elements being the same on as-cast 
structure, incipient melting point, heat treatment process, evolution of microstructures during 
long-term ageing, creep rupture life and deformation mechanism are not many. In addition, with the 
rise of Re content in single crystal superalloys, the deleterious phases such as TCP (topologically 
close-packed phases) [10-13] and SRZ (secondary reaction zones) [14-16] will precipitate, resulting 
in the degradation of creep properties. Meanwhile, Re addition causes some drawbacks, for instance, 
increases in cost and density [3]. Lowering Re content without degrading the mechanical properties 
is an attractive topic. Therefore, much attentions still should be put on the effects of Re. In order to 
explore the roles of Re in single crystal superalloys, three alloys with the nominal composition of 
Ni-3Cr-12Co-1Mo-6W-6Al-8Ta-0.1Hf-(0, 2, 4) Re are investigated. 
Materials and Experimentals 
The [001] orientation single crystal bars were produced by means of crystal selection method. 
The samples with a size of Φ 16mm × 10mm were cut out of the bars for observing microstructures 
and proceeding heat treatments. Samples for SEM (scanning electron microscopy) were etched with 
5g copper sulfate, 25ml hydrochloric acid and 20ml water which dissolved γ′ or electrolyzed in a 
solution of 2.5g citric acid, 2.5g ammonium sulfate and 300ml water which dissolved γ . The heat 
treated bars were machined into specimens with a gauge length of Φ 5mm × 25mm to carry out the 
creep rupture tests along [001]. Thin slices normal to [001] direction were sectioned out of the 
fractured samples and prepared the TEM (transmission electron microscopy) specimens by 
conventional techniques. The SEM and TEM observations were performed on JMS-6301F SEM 
and TECNAI 20 TEM respectively.

Results and Discussions 
Effect of Re on microstructure. The as-cast 
γ′ morphology is shown in Fig. 1. With the increase of 
Re content, at both dendrite core and interdendritic region, the as-cast γ′ size reduces. The growth of 
γ′ particles is a diffusion-controlled process, and the reduction of γ′ size with increasing Re content 
suggests that Re hinders diffusion.

The incipient melting point and the heat treatment process is indicated in Table 1. It can be seen 
that the addition of 2 wt.% Re does not heighten the incipient melting point, while 4 wt.% Re 
elevates that significantly. Although the incipient melting point is equivalent, during the solid 
solution treatment, the 2Re alloy requires higher temperature and longer time than 0Re alloy 
revealing the impeding effect of Re on diffusion. 4Re alloy has highest solid solution temperature 
and longest time showing that Re slows down solid solution process.

superior mechanical properties when γ′ is cuboidal and with the edge length of 0.4~0.5 µ m [17-19].

moderate size. If the cuboidal γ′ is forced to obtain, its edge length will exceed 1µ m causing adverse

During long-term ageing at 950 and 1050 ℃, Re retards the coarsening of γ′ strengthening phase 
by reducing the diffusion coefficient. Meanwhile, there is no precipitation of deleterious phases for 
the three alloys, even for alloy containing 4wt.% Re and after 2000h thermal exposure, which is 
attributed to the contribution of 12wt.% Co. Co enhances diffusion coefficient in superalloys and 
suppresses the precipitation of deleterious phases. The details have been formulated in our 
previously published paper [6]. 
Effects of Re on mechanical properties. The creep rupture tests under 1010 ℃/248MPa are carried 
out on the heat treated samples and the results are shown in Table 2. Compared with the life of alloy 
free of Re, that of 2Re alloy increases slightly, while that of 4Re alloy heightens significantly.

The dislocation configurations of 2Re and 4Re alloy interrupted at 1h, 5h and fractured are 
indicated in Fig. 4. Before creep rupture test, γ′ is cuboidal and coherent with γ matrix in 4Re alloy, 
and the width of matrix channel is nearly identical. Under external stress, {111} <110> slip system 
operates simultaneously at many matrix channels leading to the increase of strain. Dislocations 
glide in matrix then deposit on γ /γ′ interface. When the deposited dislocations shear γ′ particles, 
there is one force pushing dislocations into γ′ . This force is defined as: F= Φ /r 0, where Φ is line 
tension, and r 0 is the radius of curvature [17]. F rises with the degradation of r 0. At the apex and 
edge of γ′ cube, the radius of curvature r 0 is smallest resulting in the large driving force. Hence the 
dislocations cut into γ′ particle from the apex and edge (Fig. 4 (a)). With the proceeding of creep, 
another {111} <110> slip system operates then interacts with the former slip system causing the 
formation of networks of dislocations at γ /γ′ interface (Fig. 4 (b)). The dense networks of 
dislocations can hinder the further movement of dislocations. In addition, Re partitions into γ matrix 
and lowers the γ /γ′ misfit value, which is beneficial to developing perfect rafts. γ′ rafts can impede 
the glide and climb of dislocations. Consequently, the strain has not enhanced until the end of 
secondary creep (Fig. 3 (a)). 
However, for 2Re alloy, the spherical γ′ particles induce various γ channel width. According to 
Orowan’s theory [20], dislocations generate and glide at wide channel, resulting in the 
heterogeneous distribution of dislocations and the lower strain at the initiating stage (Fig. 3 (b)). At 
the dislocations concentrated region, dislocations shear γ′ particles while at other areas, the γ′

The alloy containing 4wt.% Re has superior creep rupture property under 1010°C/248MPa and 
may be a potential candidate for the third generation single crystal superalloys. Creep rupture tests 
under other conditions are carried out for this alloy. Fig. 5 shows the comparison of creep rupture 
lives between present 4Re alloy and CMSX-4 [21], CMSX-10 [22], the second and third generation 
single crystal superalloy, respectively. Fig. 5 indicates the lives of present alloy are significantly 
higher than those of CMSX-4, and slightly higher than those of CMSX-10. The stress rupture level 
of 4Re alloy has achieved or exceeded that of the third generation single crystal superalloys, but the 
Re content is low thus degrade the cost. Taking full advantage of the effects of Re and other 
elements and the interactions among them may explain for the cause.

Conclusions 
From above analyses, some conclusions can be drawn: 
(1) With the increase of Re content, the size of as-cast 
γ′ reduces. 
(2) Addition of 2wt.% Re does not heighten the incipient melting point, while 4wt.% Re 
enhances that significantly. 
(3) Re slows down the solid solution process and raises the primary ageing temperature. 
(4) During 2000h thermal exposure at 950 and 1050°C, no deleterious phases can be observed, 
and Re retards γ′ coarsening. Both of them can be explained by lowering of Re diffusion 
coefficient. 
(5) Re is beneficial to improving creep rupture life of single crystal superalloys. 
(6) The alloy containing 4wt.% Re can be used as a third generation single crystal superalloy.
